Injectivity of spherical mean on Métivier Group
Rupak Kumar Dalai and R. K. Srivastava

Abstract
. In this article, we study the injectivity of the spherical mean for continuous functions on the Métivier group. The spherical mean is injective for f ( z , . ) ∈ L p ( ℝ m ) , 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 f(z,.)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),~1\leq p\leq 2 with tempered growth in z z variable. This result is also true for a class of functions in L p ​ ( ℂ n ) , 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),\,1\leq p\leq\infty without tempered growth. Further, we obtain a two-radii theorem for functions on the Métivier group, which are tempered in z z variable and periodic in the centre variable.

1.
Introduction

In integral geometry, it is an interesting question to know if the average
of a continuous function
f
over all translates of a surface can determine
f.

Particularly, when does the operator
f
into
f\ast\mu_{r}
turn out to be injective,
where
\mu_{r}
is the normalised surface measure on
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|=r\}.

In general, the answer to this is negative, since there are non-trivial bounded
continuous functions, e.g. Bessel functions
\varphi
for which
\varphi\ast\mu_{r}=0,

when
r
is a zero of the Bessel function.
The injectivity of the spherical mean is an ever interesting question and
studied by several authors including
[
1
,
5
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
. Thangavelu
[
10
]

has shown that the one radius theorem is true for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when

1\leq p\leq 2n/(n-1),
by exploiting the spectral decomposition of the Laplacian.

The above question (one radius theorem) was also considered for the Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.
Indeed, in
[
10
]
it is
shown that if
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n}),1\leq p<\infty,
then
f\ast\mu=0
implies
f=0,

where
\mu
is a compactly supported rotation invariant probability measure
with no mass at the centre. The proof of this result is based on a summability
result due to Strichartz
[
7
]
for sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}.

Although, in the Métivier group, denoted by
G\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},
the analogue to summability result
[
7
]

is yet to settle due to appearance of a multi-parameter singular integral due to
higher dimensional centre, whose kernel need not be a Calderón-Zygmund kernel.
However, we show that the mean operator
f
into
f\ast\mu
is
injective when
f(z,.)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),1\leq p\leq 2
and
f
is of tempered
growth in
z
variable.
This result is obtained employ the simplified

\lambda
-twisted spherical mean on the Métivier group, which we
introduced in
[
2
]
and the special Hermite
expansion as discussed in Section
3
. Moreover, when
\mu=\mu_{r},

we prove one radius theorem for continuous functions
f
when

f(z,.)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),1\leq p\leq 2
and

f^{\lambda}(z)e^{\frac{1}{4}\left|J_{\lambda}z_{\lambda}\right|^{2}}
is in

L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),1\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty.

At the end, we observed a two radii theorem for tempered continuous
functions in
z
variable and
2\pi
-periodic in
t
variable.

It is known that the symplectic bilinear form appears in the group action of
the Métivier group is far from
U(n)
-invariance, due to its higher
dimensional centre, the
\lambda
-twisted spherical cannot be radialised
as in the case of the Heisenberg group. However, it is elliptical
up to a rotation
[
2
]
. We connect this elliptical mean to the
twisted spherical mean of a Lie group having
3n
-dimensional step two
nilpotent Lie algebra. This fact unfolds many tools for studying
the spherical mean in the Métivier group. We obtain the spectral
decomposition for
L^{2}
-functions in terms of
eigenfunctions of sub-Laplacian on this particular Lie group.
This reduction eases towards proving an analogue of one radius
theorem on the general Métivier group.

2.
Preliminaries and Auxiliary results

Let
G
be a connected, simply connected Lie group with real step two nilpotent
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
has the orthogonal decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{z},
where
\mathfrak{z}
is the centre.
Since
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent, the exponential map

\exp:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow G
is surjective. Hence
G
is parameterised
by
\mathfrak{g}
and endowed with the exponential coordinates. We identify

X+T\in\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
with
\exp(X+T)
and denote it by

(X,T)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Since
[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}]\subseteq\mathfrak{z}
and
[\mathfrak{b},[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}]]=0
, by the
Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, the group law on
G
expressed as

(X,T)(Y,S)=(X+Y,T+S+\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]),

where
X,Y\in\mathfrak{b}
and
T,S\in\mathfrak{z}
.
For
\omega\in\mathfrak{z}^{\ast},
consider the skew-symmetric bilinear form

B_{\omega}
on
\mathfrak{b}
by
B_{\omega}(X,Y)=\omega\left([X,Y]\right).

Then
B_{\omega}
is called a non-degenerate bilinear form when

r_{\omega}=\left\{X\in\mathfrak{b}:B_{\omega}(X,Y)=0,~\forall~Y\in\mathfrak{b}\right\}
is trivial.

We say group
G
is Métivier group if
B_{\omega}
is
non-degenerate for all nonzero
\omega\in\mathfrak{z}^{\ast}.
In such cases, the dimension of

\mathfrak{b}
is even, say
d=2n.

Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{2n}
and
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}
be orthonormal bases for
\mathfrak{b}

and
\mathfrak{z},
respectively. Since
[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}]\subseteq\mathfrak{z},

there exist scalars
U_{j,l}^{(k)}
such that

[B_{j},B_{l}]=\sum_{k=1}^{m}U_{j,l}^{(k)}Z_{k},\quad\mbox{where }1\leq j,l\leq 2n\mbox{ and }1\leq k\leq m.

For
1\leq k\leq m,
define
{2n\times 2n}
skew-symmetric matrices by

U^{(k)}=(U_{j,l}^{(k)}).
Then the group law for the Métivier group can precisely
be expressed as

(2.1)

\displaystyle(x,t)\cdot(\xi,\tau)=\binom{x_{i}+\xi_{i},~i=1,\ldots,2n}{~t_{j}+\tau_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\langle x,U^{(j)}\xi\rangle,~j=1,\ldots,m},

where
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Left-invariant vector fields for the Lie algebra of the Métivier group
G
computed as

X_{j}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(x_{l}U_{l,j}^{(k)}+x_{n+l}U_{n+l,j}^{(k)}\right)\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}},

X_{n+j}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{n+j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(x_{l}U_{l,n+j}^{(k)}+x_{n+l}U_{n+l,n+j}^{(k)}\right)\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}},

and
T_{k}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}},
where

(x,t)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n},t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},~j=1,\ldots,n
and
k=1,\ldots,m.

As
U^{(k)}
’s are skew-symmetric, we obtain the following commutation relations

[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{m}U_{i,j}^{(k)}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}},\quad[X_{n+i},X_{n+j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{m}U_{n+i,n+j}^{(k)}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}},\quad\text{ for }~i,j=1,\ldots,n.

Since
U^{(1)},\ldots,U^{(m)}
are linearly independent, the dimension of the space
spanned by
\left\{(U_{i,j}^{(1)},\ldots,U_{i,j}^{(m)}):~i,j=1,\ldots,n\right\}
will be
m.

Let
\mu_{r}
be the normalised surface measure on
\{(x,0):|x|=r\}\subset G.

Then the spherical mean of a function
f\in L^{1}(G)
is defined as

(2.2)

f\ast\mu_{r}(x,t)=\int_{|\xi|=r}f\left((x,t)\cdot(-\xi,0)\right)d\mu_{r}(\xi).

Denote
\mathbb{R}^{l}_{\ast}=\mathbb{R}^{l}\setminus\{0\},~l\in\mathbb{N}.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\ast},
let
f^{\lambda}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x,t)e^{i\lambda\cdot t}dt
be the inverse Fourier transform
of
f
in
t
variable, then

\displaystyle(f\ast\mu_{r})^{\lambda}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f\ast\mu_{r}(x,t)e^{i\lambda\cdot t}dt=\int_{|\xi|=r}f^{\lambda}(x-\xi)e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\langle x,U^{(j)}\xi\rangle}d\mu_{r}(\xi).

Let us define the
\lambda
-twisted spherical mean of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
by

(2.3)

f\times_{\lambda}\mu_{r}(x)=\int_{|\xi|=r}f(x-\xi)e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\langle x,U^{(j)}\xi\rangle}d\mu_{r}(\xi).

Then the spherical mean
f\ast\mu_{r}
on the Métivier group
G
can be studied by

\lambda
-twisted spherical mean
f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu_{r}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\ast},
the skew-symmetric matrix

V_{\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}U^{(j)}
is non-singular
[
4
]
.
Let
u_{1}\pm iv_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\pm iv_{n}
be the eigenvectors of
V_{\lambda}

with corresponding eigenvalues
\pm i\mu_{\lambda,1},\ldots,\pm i\mu_{\lambda,n}

satisfying
\mu_{\lambda,1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{\lambda,n}>0.
Define

A_{\lambda}=\left(\sqrt{2}~v_{1},\ldots,\sqrt{2}~v_{n},\sqrt{2}~u_{1},\ldots,\sqrt{2}~u_{n}\right).
Then

A_{\lambda}
is an orthogonal matrix that satisfies
V_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}=A_{\lambda}U_{\lambda},

where

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0_{n}&-J_{\lambda}\\
J_{\lambda}&0_{n}\end{array}\right)

with
J_{\lambda}=\text{diag}(\mu_{\lambda,1},\ldots,\mu_{\lambda,n})
and
0_{n}

is zero matrix of order
n.
In view of (
2
), we have

(2.6)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\langle x,U^{(j)}\xi\rangle=\langle x,V_{\lambda}\xi\rangle=\langle A_{\lambda}^{t}x,U_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}^{t}\xi\rangle,

where
A_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}^{t}=I.
For

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n},
we write

z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(x_{1}+ix_{n+1},\ldots,x_{n}+ix_{2n})
and say
z

be the complexification of
x.
Thus, after complexifying (
2.6
), we get

(2.7)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\text{Re}\,\left(z\cdot\overline{U^{(j)}w}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{\lambda,j}\text{ Im}\left((z_{\lambda})_{j}\cdot(\bar{w}_{\lambda})_{j}\right),

where
z_{\lambda}
and
w_{\lambda}
are complexification of
A_{\lambda}^{t}x
and

A_{\lambda}^{t}\xi
respectively. The following lemma would rationalise the
\lambda
-twisted
spherical mean (
2.3
) to a simpler mean.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
2
]

Let
f_{\lambda}(x)=f(A_{\lambda}x)
and
z,\tilde{z}_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be
the complexification of
x,A_{\lambda}x\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
respectively. Then

f\times_{\lambda}\mu_{r}(\tilde{z}_{\lambda})=f_{\lambda}\tilde{\times}_{\lambda}\mu_{r}(z),

where

(2.8)

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}\tilde{\times}_{\lambda}~\mu_{r}(z)=\int_{|w|=r}~f_{\lambda}(z-w)~e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{\lambda,j}\text{ Im}\left(z_{j}\cdot\,\bar{w}_{j}\right)}~d\mu_{r}(w).

We say
f_{\lambda}\tilde{\times}_{\lambda}~\mu_{r}
as modified
\lambda
-twisted spherical mean.

3.
Twisted spherical mean and spectral decomposition

In this section, we perceive that there is a Lie group with real
3n
-dimensional
step two nilpotent Lie algebra whose twisted spherical mean close with

f_{\lambda}\tilde{\times}_{\lambda}~\mu_{r}.
We look for eigenfunctions of sub-Laplacian
on this particular group, and via that, obtain the spectral decomposition for
L^{2}
-functions.
We derive some auxiliary results related to the special Hermite functions.

Consider the group
\tilde{G}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

\left\{(x,y,t):x,y,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}

equipped with the group law

(x,y,t)\cdot(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{\prime}\cdot y-y^{\prime}\cdot x)\right).

The group
\tilde{G}
is a
3n
-dimensional Métivier group with a basis of left-invariant
vector fields

(3.1)

\displaystyle X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}},\quad Y_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}-\frac{1}{2}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}}\quad\mbox{and }T_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}},

where
j=1,\dots,n.
The sub-Laplacian on
\tilde{G}
is

\mathcal{L}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(X_{j}^{2}+Y_{j}^{2}\right).

For each
{{\lambda^{\prime}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\ast},
we can see that the operator

\pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x,y,t)
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

(3.2)

\pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x,y,t)\phi(\xi)=e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}t_{j}+i\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j}+\frac{1}{2}x_{j}y_{j})}\phi(\xi+y)

are all possible irreducible unitary representation of
\tilde{G},

where
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
If
\pi_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z)=\pi_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z,0)
, then

\pi_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z,t)=e^{i{\lambda^{\prime}}t}\pi_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z).
Identifying
\tilde{G}

with
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},
let
L_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}
be the operator defined by

\mathcal{L}\left(e^{i{\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot t}f(z)\right)=e^{i{\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot t}L_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}f(z),

where
z=x+iy.

Then
L_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}
can precisely be expressed as

(3.3)

L_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}=-\Delta_{z}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}^{2}|z_{j}|^{2}+i\mathcal{N}_{\lambda^{\prime}},\quad\mbox{where }\mathcal{N}_{\lambda^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}\left(x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}-y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right).

Let
f\in L^{1}(\tilde{G})
and
f^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(z,t)e^{i{\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot t}dt

be the inverse Fourier transform of
f
in the
t
variable. Then, for this particular
group
\tilde{G}
the
{\lambda^{\prime}}
-twisted spherical mean can be explicitly calculated by

(3.4)

f^{\lambda^{\prime}}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mu_{r}(z)=\int_{|w|=r}f^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z-w)e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}\text{ Im}\left(z_{j}\cdot\,\bar{w}_{j}\right)}d\mu_{r}(w).

Similarly, if
f,g\in L^{1}(\tilde{G}),
then we can also define the
{\lambda^{\prime}}
-twisted convolution as

(3.5)

f^{\lambda^{\prime}}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}g^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z-w)g^{\lambda^{\prime}}(w)e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}Im(z_{j}.\bar{w}_{j})}dw.

Remark 3.1
.

For any
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\ast},m\geq 2,
the modified

{\lambda}
-twisted spherical mean (
2.8
) coincides with the

{\lambda^{\prime}}
-twisted spherical mean (
3.4
), where

\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and each of its coordinate can be
identified with the imaginary part of an eigenvalue of
V_{\lambda}.

Therefore, studying the injectivity of spherical mean on an
arbitrary Métivier group
G,
it is enough to consider
spherical mean on
\tilde{G}.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},
let
\Phi_{\alpha}(x)=\Pi_{j=1}^{n}h_{\alpha_{j}}\left(x_{j}\right),
where
h_{\alpha_{j}}
are normalised
Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}.
Then
\Phi_{\alpha}
is an eigenfunction
of Hermite operator
H=-\Delta+|x|^{2}
with eigenvalue
(2|\alpha|+n).

For more details, see
[
12
]
. Moreover, for

{{\lambda^{\prime}}}=({{\lambda^{\prime}}_{1}},\ldots,{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{n}})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

if we define

\Psi_{\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|^{\frac{1}{4}}h_{\alpha_{j}}\left(\sqrt{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|}x_{j}\right),

then
\Psi_{\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
are the eigenfunctions of the elliptic Hermite operator

H_{\lambda^{\prime}}=-\Delta+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}({{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}x_{j})^{2}

with eigenvalues
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(2\alpha_{j}+1)|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|.

Thus,

L_{\lambda^{\prime}}\left(\pi_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z)\Psi_{\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}},\Psi_{\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(2\alpha_{j}+1\right)|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|\left(\pi_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z)\Psi_{\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}},\Psi_{\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right).

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},
define the function as

\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z)=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sqrt{\frac{{|{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}|}}{2\pi}}\right)\left(\pi_{{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z)\Psi_{\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}},\Psi_{\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right).

Then
\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
are eigenfunctions of the operator

-\Delta_{z}+\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}^{2}|z_{j}|^{2}.

The set
\{\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}:\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}\}
form a complete orthonormal
set for
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Next, we come up with some identities for
\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}

which can be derived by a suitable change of variables in the special
Hermite function.

Recall that the Laguerre function
\varphi^{n-1}_{k}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by

\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(z)=L_{k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{4}|z|^{2}},

where
L_{k}^{n-1}
are Laguerre polynomials of type
(n-1).
For
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},
define

\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(z)=\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(\sqrt{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}|}}z),

where the notation
\sqrt{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}|}}z
is fixed by

(3.6)

\sqrt{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}}|}z:=\left(\sqrt{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{1}}|}z_{1},\ldots,\sqrt{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{n}}|}z_{n}\right).

As similar to special Hermite function,
\Psi_{\alpha\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
can be expressed in terms of Laguerre functions as

(3.7)

\Psi_{\alpha\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\Pi_{j=1}^{n}L_{\alpha_{j}}^{0}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}z_{j}\right|^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}z_{j}\right|^{2}}.

Then we can derive the formula

(3.8)

\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sqrt{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|}\right)\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\Psi_{\alpha\alpha}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(z).

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),
then in view of (
3.8
), and the completeness of

\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
’s in
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

f
will satisfy the identity

\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\sum_{\beta}\left(f,\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(z-w)e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}Im(z_{j}.\bar{w}_{j})}dw.

The right-hand side is simply
\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|}{2\pi}\right)\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}f(z)
and
\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}f(z)=f\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(z),
we have

(3.9)

f(z)=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(z).

The next proposition shows that the
{\lambda^{\prime}}
-twisted spherical mean of
\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}

will satisfy the following functional relation.

Proposition 3.2
.

Denote
\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(r)=\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(w)

for
|w|=r.
Then

(3.10)

\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mu_{r}(z)=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}}\right)\frac{k!(n-1)!}{(k+n-1)!}\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(z)\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(r).

Proof.

From (
[
8
]
, Theorem 2.1), it is known that the twisted spherical mean of

\varphi_{k}^{n-1}
with respect to the Heisenberg group can be written as

(3.11)

\int_{|w|=r}\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(z-w)e^{\frac{i}{2}Im\left(z\cdot\bar{w}\right)}d\mu_{r}(w)=\frac{k!(n-1)!}{(k+n-1)!}\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(z)\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(r).

Now, by a change of variable, we can rewrite

(3.12)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mu_{r}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{|w|=r}\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}(z-w)e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}\text{ Im}\left(z_{j}\cdot\,\bar{w}_{j}\right)}d\mu_{r}(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{|w|=r}\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}}z-\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}}w)e^{\frac{i}{2}Im\left(\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}}z\cdot\overline{\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}}w}\right)}d\mu_{r}(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{|\sqrt[-1]{{\lambda^{\prime}}}w|=r}\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(z^{\prime}-w)e^{\frac{i}{2}Im\left(z^{\prime}\cdot\bar{w}\right)}d\mu_{r}(w),

where
z^{\prime}=\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}}z.
By a suitable change of variable in (
3.11
),
we can write the above equation (
3.12
) as

\displaystyle\int_{|\sqrt[-1]{{\lambda^{\prime}}}w|=r}\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(z^{\prime}-w)e^{\frac{i}{2}Im\left(z^{\prime}\cdot\bar{w}\right)}d\mu_{r}(w)=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}}\right)\frac{k!(n-1)!}{(k+n-1)!}\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(z^{\prime})\varphi_{k}^{n-1}(\sqrt{{\lambda^{\prime}}}w).

Hence the identity (
3.10
) is followed.

Let
m=\left(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.
A function
f
on

\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called
m
-homogeneous if it satisfies

f(e^{i\theta}z)=f(e^{i\theta_{1}}z_{1},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{n}}z_{n})=e^{im\cdot\theta}f(z),
where
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}).

For a function
g
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
define
m
-radialization
R_{m}g
by

(3.13)

R_{m}g(z)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{[0,2\pi)^{n}}g\left(e^{i\theta}z\right)e^{-im\cdot\theta}d\theta.

Then
R_{m}f
is
m
-homogeneous and we have

(3.14)

f\left(z\right)=\sum_{m}R_{m}f(z)e^{im\cdot\theta}.

The series in the right-hand side of (
3.14
) converges
in the topology of Schwartz class function
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\right),

see
[
10
]
.

Since
\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{{\lambda^{\prime}}}
is
(\beta-\alpha)
-homogeneous, we can see that

\left(f,\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{{\lambda^{\prime}}}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(z)\overline{\Psi_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}(z)dz

is nonzero only when
\beta=\alpha+m.
Thus, if
f
is
m
-homogeneous we can write

(3.15)

f\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{n-1}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|{{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}}|}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{|\beta|=k}\left(f,\Psi_{\beta-m\,\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\Psi_{\beta-m\,\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}.

In
[
10
]
, it has been proved that for the Heisenberg group
the special Hermite series of an m-homogeneous
function converges in the topology of
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\right).

By imitating the prove in this case, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

lf
f
is a Schwartz class function and
m
-homogeneous, then
the series (
3.9
) of
f
converges in the topology of

\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\right).

4.
Spherical mean on the Métivier Group

This section deals with the injectivity of spherical mean
f\ast\mu
, where
\mu\in X_{P}(G),

the space of compactly supported rotation invariant probability measure with no mass at the
centre of Métivier group
G.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p_{i}\leq 2
for
i=1,2.
Let
f\in C(G)
be such that
f(z,\cdot)\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and

f^{\lambda}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
for a.e.
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\ast}.
If
f
satisfies

f\ast\mu=0
for some
\mu\in X_{P}(G),
then
f=0.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\ast},
let
f^{\lambda}
and
\mu^{\lambda}

be the partial Fourier transform of
f
and
\mu
in
t
variable, respectively.
Then applying
\lambda
-twisted convolution, we get
f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu^{\lambda}=0.

Since
\mu\in X_{P}(G),
by Choquet’s Theorem we get

(4.1)

f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu^{\lambda}=\int_{E}f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu_{r}\,dM,

where
E=\{\mu_{r}\},\,\mu_{r}
is the normalised surface measure on the sphere of radius
r

centred at the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{n},
and
M
is the measure on
E.
For more details,
refer to
[
6
]
. From Lemma
2.1
, we can rewrite (
4.1
) using the modified

\lambda
-twisted spherical mean as

(4.2)

f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu^{\lambda}=\int_{E}(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}~\tilde{\times}_{\lambda}~\mu_{r}\,dM,

where
(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}(x)=f^{\lambda}(A_{\lambda}x)
defined as in Lemma
2.1
.
For a fixed
\lambda,
and considering Remark
3.1
, we can write

(4.3)

(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}~\tilde{\times}_{\lambda}~\mu_{r}=(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mu_{r}

for some
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.
In the right-hand side,

\lambda^{\prime}
-twisted spherical mean is with respect to
\tilde{G}
as
defined by (
3.4
). By an appropiate approximation identity, we may assume that

(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
Then applying the spectral decomposition
(
3.9
),
(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}
can be expressed in terms of
\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}

as

(4.4)

(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1},

where the series converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
Now, it is enough to prove
that each spectral projection
(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}~\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}=0.

From (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{E}(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mu_{r}\,dM=0.

In view of Proposition (
3.2
), we get

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu^{\lambda}\left(\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\right)(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}=0,

where

\mu^{\lambda}\left(\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\mu^{\lambda}=\int_{E}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}dM.

Since for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

\mu^{\lambda}\left(\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\right)

vanishes only for countable many values of
\lambda.
Hence

(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}=0
implies

(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}~\tilde{\times}_{\lambda}~\mu_{r}=0
for a.e.

\lambda
and each
k.
Thus,
(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}=0
for a.e.
\lambda,
which concludes
f=0.

∎

In the following result we relax the integrability condition of
f^{\lambda}
in the
z

variable with tempered growth with help of some approximation lemmas from Section
3
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f
be a continuous function on
G
with
f(z,\cdot)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

1\leq p\leq 2

and
f^{\lambda}
has tempered growth in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for a.e.
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\ast}.

If
f
satisfies
f\ast\mu=0
for some
\mu\in X_{P}(G),
then
f=0.

Proof.

Since
f
is integrable in the second variable, applying
\lambda
-twisted
convolution on
f\ast\mu=0,
we get
f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu^{\lambda}=0
for
a.e.
\lambda.
Hence we claim
f^{\lambda}=0
for almost all
\lambda.

But tempered growth of
f^{\lambda}
reduces to show that

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f^{\lambda}(z)g(z)dz=0

for every
g
in
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\right).
Since
g
admits an

m
-radialization expansion, we can replace both
g
and
f^{\lambda}
with
their
m
-radialization. Therefore, it is enough to consider

(4.5)

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}R_{m}f^{\lambda}(z)g(z)dz=0

for all
m
-homogeneous
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\right).

If we fix
\lambda,
then there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
as in
(
4.3
), and by Lemma
3.3
we can reciprocate
g
with

g\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}
in (
4.5
).
Hence it is enough to examine

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}R_{m}f^{\lambda}(z)g\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(z)dz=0

for every
k,
which is equivalent to

\int\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}f^{\lambda}(z)g(z)dz=0.

From (
3.15
) it is clear that
\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}f^{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
And we also have

\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu^{\lambda}=0.

Using Proposition
4.1
we conclude that
\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}f^{\lambda}=0.
This proves the theorem.
∎

In the previous results we have considered
\mu\in X_{P}(G).
In the
following result, we replace
\mu
by
\mu_{r},
the normalised surface measure
on
\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z|=r\},
which demands more decay in
z
variable.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
f
be a continuous function on
G
such that
f(z,\cdot)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

1\leq p\leq 2

and
f^{\lambda}(z)e^{\frac{1}{4}\left|J_{\lambda}z_{\lambda}\right|^{2}}
is in

L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),1\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty
for a.e.
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\ast}.
If
f
satisfies
f*\mu_{r}=0

for some
r>0,
then
f=0
.

Proof.

Applying
\lambda
-twisted convolution on

f*\mu_{r}=0,
we get
f^{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}\mu_{r}=0
for
a.e.
\lambda.
For a fix
\lambda,
then there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}

as in (
4.3
), and using Lemma
2.1
it follows that

(4.6)

(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mu_{r}=0.

Further, taking the
\lambda^{\prime}
-twisted convolution of equation (
4.6
) and

\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1},
and using Proposition
3.2
we get

\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(r)(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Since the zero sets of Laguerre polynomials are disjoint,

\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(r)\neq 0
for all
k
except one, say
k=l.

That is,
(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}=0

for all
k\neq l.
Hence we get
(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}=C(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{l,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}
for some nonzero constant
C.
Since

R_{m}(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is
m
-homogeneous, in view of (
3.15
) we get

R_{m}(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}=CR_{m}(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\times_{\lambda}^{\prime}\vartheta_{l,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}=C\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}|}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{|\beta|=l}\left((f^{\lambda})_{\lambda},\Psi_{\beta-m\,\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\Psi_{\beta-m\,\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}.

Replacing
R_{m}(f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}
with
R_{m}((f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left|{\lambda^{\prime}}z\right|^{2}})
we have

R_{m}((f^{\lambda})_{\lambda}\,e^{\frac{1}{4}\left|{\lambda^{\prime}}z\right|^{2}})=C\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}|}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{|\beta|=l}\left((f^{\lambda})_{\lambda},\Psi_{\beta-m\,\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\Psi_{\beta-m\,\beta}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\,e^{\frac{1}{4}\left|{\lambda^{\prime}}z\right|^{2}}.

By the hypothesis
f^{\lambda}(z)e^{\frac{1}{4}\left|J_{\lambda}z_{\lambda}\right|^{2}}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),
it follows that left-hand side is in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

which makes
f=0
since the right-hand side is a polynomial.
∎

We now prove a version of the two radii theorem for the class of tempered
continuous functions on the Métivier group, which are periodic in the
centre variable.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f
be a tempered continuous function in
z
and
2\pi
-periodic in the centre variable of
G.

If
f
satisfies
f\ast\mu_{r_{i}}=0,~i=1,2,
then
f=0
as long as

(i)

\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{r_{2}^{2}}
is not a quotient of zeros of Laguerre polynomials

L_{k}^{n-1}
for any
k
.

(ii)

\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}
is not a quotient of zeros of Bessel functions
J_{n-1}
.

Proof.

For
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},
define the
l
th Fourier coefficient of
f
by

f^{l}(z)=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}f(z,t)e^{il\cdot t}dt.

It follows by Lemma
3.3
that
f^{l}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Further, taking the
l
-twisted spherical mean of
f\ast\mu_{r_{i}}=0

and uniqueness of the Fourier series, we get
f^{l}\times_{l}\mu_{r_{i}}=0

for
i=1,2.
Let us fix
l\neq 0,
then using Lemma
2.1
and Equation (
4.3
) we can write

(f^{l})_{l}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mu_{r_{i}}=0~\text{ for some }\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\text{ and }i=1,2,

where
(f^{l})_{l}=f^{l}(A_{l}x)
as defined in Lemma
2.1
. Then using Proposition
3.2
we get

\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(r_{i})(f^{l})_{l}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(z)=0
for
i=1,2.
Since for each
k,
either

\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(r_{1})\neq 0
or
\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(r_{2})\neq 0,

we have
(f^{l})_{l}\times_{\lambda^{\prime}}\vartheta_{k,\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}=0.
Hence
(f^{l})_{l}=0.

When
l=0,
the
l
-twisted spherical mean conditions
f\ast\mu_{r_{i}}=0,~i=1,2

led to two radii theorem on
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
Hence each Fourier coefficient
of
f
is zero, and thus
f=0.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

Since the twisted spherical mean of a Métivier group is coherence
with a
3n
-dimensional Métivier group, we could show one radius theorem for

f\in L^{p}(G),~1\leq p\leq 2.
However, when we approach as similar to the Heisenberg group

[
10
]
for
f\in L^{p}(G),~2<p<\infty,
based on a summability result, we seek
L^{p}
-
boundedness of a multi-parameter singular integral,
whose kernel may fail to be a Calderón-Zygmund kernel.

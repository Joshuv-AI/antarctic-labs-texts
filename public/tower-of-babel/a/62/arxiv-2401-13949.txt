Asymptotic decay for the Chern-Simons-Higgs equations
Dongyi Wei, Shiwu Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the long time asymptotic behaviors for solutions to the Chern-Simons-Higgs equation with a pure power defocusing nonlinearity. We obtain quantitative inverse polynomial time decay for the potential energy for all data with finite conformal energy. Consequently, the solution decays in time in the pointwise sense for all power. We also show that for sufficiently large power the solution decays as quickly as linear waves. Key ingredients for the proof include vector field method, conformal compactification and the geometric bilinear trace theorem for null hypersurface developed by Klainerman-Rodnianski.

Let

To state our main result, for

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k,\gamma}=\sum\limits_{l\leq k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|x|)^{\gamma+2l}(|\bar{D}^{l+1}\phi_{0}|^{2}+|\bar{D}^{l}\phi_{1}|^{2})+(1+|x|)^{\gamma}|\phi_{0}|^{p+1}dx.

Our first result is the time decay of the potential energy.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the Cauchy problem to the Chern-Simons-Higgs equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
with a pure power defocusing nonlinearity and finite initial conformal energy
\mathcal{E}_{0,2}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\phi(t,x)|^{p+1}dx\leq C\mathcal{E}_{0,2}(1+t)^{-\min\{\frac{p-1}{2},2\}},\quad\forall t\geq 0

for some constant
C
depending only on
p
.

Remark 1.1
.

A weaker decay estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\phi(t,x)|^{p+1}dx\leq C\mathcal{E}_{0,2}(1+t)^{3-p},\quad p<5,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

had been obtained by Huh in
[
24

, based on the method developed by Pecher in
[
18
]
for the study of time decay for the solution with vanishing connection field

The above time decay of the potential energy together with a type of logarithmic Sobolev embedding is sufficient to conclude the pointwise decay estimates for the solution.

Theorem 2
.

If the initial data
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
for the Chern-Simons-Higgs system (
1

\mathcal{E}_{0,2}

\mathcal{E}_{1,0}

satisfies the rough pointwise decay estimates for all

|\phi(t,x)|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{p-1}{8}}\sqrt{\ln(2+t)},\quad\forall t\geq 0

with some constant
C
depending on
p

\mathcal{E}_{0,2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1,0}
.

The above rough decay estimate is far from optimal. However the proof also implies that the solution decays faster away from the light cone. Moreover for sufficiently large

, we can show that the solution decays as fast as linear waves in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that the initial data
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
to the system (
1

\{|x|\leq 1\}

|\phi(t,x)|\leq C(1+t+|x|)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall t\geq 0

for some constant
C
depending only on
p
,
\mathcal{E}_{0,2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1,0}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Compact support assumption on the initial data is merely for simplicity. Same result holds for data bounded in some weighted energy space. For general data, inspired by the work

Remark 1.3

For the flat case when
A=0

The proof for the potential energy decay, that is the main theorem
1
, is similar to that in
[
12
]
by the authors for the defocusing semilinear wave equations. For the Chern-Simons-Higgs system studied here, the key observation is that the error term

X^{\nu}F_{\mu\nu}J[\phi]^{\mu}=X^{\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\gamma}J[\phi]^{\gamma}J[\phi]^{\mu}

arising from the connection field
A
for any multiplier
X
actually vanishes as
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\gamma}
is skew symmetric. This in particular indicates that any multiplier that can be used for the flat case (with vanishing connection field
A
) also works for the general Chern-Simons-Higgs equation. For readers’ interest, we briefly review the ideas for the proof.

Instead of using the conformal Killing vector field, which is of particular importance for the superconformal case (see discussions for example in
[
47
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
24
]
), non-spherical symmetric vector field

\displaystyle X=(x_{2}^{2}+(t-x_{1})^{2}+1)\partial_{t}+(x_{2}^{2}-(t-x_{1})^{2})\partial_{1}+2(t-x_{1})x_{2}\partial_{2}

will be applied to the exterior region
\{t\leq x_{1}\}
. This shows the weighted energy estimate through the null hyperplane
\{t=x_{1}\}

\int_{t=x_{1}\geq 0}x_{2}^{2}|(D_{t}+D_{1})\phi|^{2}(t,t,x_{2})+|D_{2}\phi(t,t,x_{2})|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi(t,t,x_{2})|^{p+1}dtdx_{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

Then in the interior region, take the multiplier

\displaystyle X=u_{1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\partial_{t}-\partial_{1})+u_{1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}-2}x_{2}^{2}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{1})+2u_{1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}-1}x_{2}\partial_{2},\quad u_{1}=t-x_{1}+1

and the region
\{t\geq x_{1}\}
. By using the above weighted energy bound on the null hyperplane
\{t=x_{1}\}
(as boundary of the chosen region), one then can obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq t}u_{1}^{\frac{p-5}{2}}(x_{2}^{2}+u_{1}^{2})|\phi(t,x)|^{p+1}dx\leq C\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

Since we only study the solution in the future
t\geq 0
, restricting the integral to the half plane
\{x_{1}\leq 0\}
, the above estimate then implies that

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq 0}(1+t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\phi(t,x)|^{p+1}dx\leq C\mathcal{E}_{0,2}

since
u_{1}=t-x_{1}+1
. The potential energy decay then follows by symmetry
x_{1}
to
-x_{1}
.

For the rough pointwise decay estimate for the solution, we rely on the logarithmic Sobolev embedding as in the flat case. However, the new difficulty for the Chern-Simons-Higgs equation is the polynomial growth bound for the first order energy, that is the
H^{2}
bound for the solution. To control the first order energy, one needs to commute the equation with derivatives. A typical nonlinear term arising from the connection field
A
is of the form

F_{\nu\mu}D^{\nu}\phi=\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}D^{\nu}\phi\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}\phi})\approx|\phi||D\phi|^{2},

which seems not possible to be controlled by using the standard Sobolev embedding. The idea is to rely on a type of Strichartz estimate for linear half wave equation. See Lemme
3.4
for more details. Now to obtain necessary dispersive estimate for
D\phi
, we can write the Chern-Simons-Higgs equation as first order hyperbolic system for
D\phi
under Coulomb gauge condition. We emphasize here that the gauge condition is only used at this point. By using Riesz transform together with the potential energy and weighted energy decay estimates for the solution, we can show that

\displaystyle\|D\phi\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}\lesssim 1,\quad\forall T\geq 0.

This dispersive estimate is sufficient to control the above nonlinearity. Indeed by using Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation for Besov space and the energy conservation, we can bound that

\displaystyle\||D\phi|^{2}|\phi|\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D\phi\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s}}^{\frac{2+2\epsilon}{2+\epsilon}}\|DD\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2s(1+\epsilon)}{2+\epsilon}}.

Here
\epsilon
is a small positive constant and
s
is nonnegative such that
s(1+\epsilon)<1
. By doing the standard energy estimate for
D\phi
, we then can show that the first order energy grows at most polynomially in time

\displaystyle\|DD\phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1+(1+t)^{\frac{2+\epsilon}{2+\epsilon-2s(1+\epsilon)}}.

Finally to improve the pointwise decay estimate for the solution for larger power of
p
, we rely on the conformal symmetry of the system

\phi\mapsto(1+t+|x|)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+t-|x|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi,\quad A\mapsto A

and the proof for the main theorem

is reduced to showing that solution to the system

\Box_{A}\phi=\Lambda^{\frac{5-p}{2}}|\phi|^{p-1}\phi,\quad F_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu\gamma}J[\phi]^{\gamma},\quad\Lambda=(1-t+|x|)(1-t-|x|)

on the backward cone
B=\{t+|(x_{1},x_{2})|\leq 1\}
with compactly supported initial data is uniformly bounded up to the tip point. However to better make use of the geometric Kirchoff-Sobolev paramatrix, we lift the above equation to space dimension three by simply assuming that
A
and
\phi
is independent of the third variable
x_{3}
. The cone
B
will be changed to
\{t+|(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|\leq 1\}
accordingly. For any fixed point
q=(t_{0},x_{0})
, the solution
\phi
at
q
can be represented as follows

\begin{split}4\pi|\phi(q)|&=I.D.+\iint_{\mathcal{N}^{-}(q)}\langle\widetilde{\mbox{$\Delta\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}}_{A}h-i\widetilde{\rho}h,\phi\rangle\widetilde{r}d\widetilde{r}d\widetilde{\omega}-\iint_{\mathcal{N}^{-}(q)}\langle h,\Box_{A}\phi\rangle\widetilde{r}d\widetilde{r}d\widetilde{\omega},\end{split}

where
h
is the function on the backward light cone
\mathcal{N}^{-}(q)=\{t-t_{0}+|x-x_{0}|=0\}
such that

\displaystyle D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}h=0,\quad h(q)=|\phi(q)|^{-1}\phi(q).

The tilde components are those associated to the coordinates centered at the point
q
.
The first term relies only on the initial data which is clearly uniformly bounded. The third term is the pure power nonlinearity which could be controlled in a similar way as the flat case in
[
12
]
. The most difficult term is the second one arising from the connection field
A
.
Like the Yang-Mills-Higgs system studied in
[
48
]
, commuting the transport equation for
h
with angular derivatives, one first can obtain the bound

\displaystyle|\tilde{r}^{2}(\mbox{$\Delta\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}_{A}h-i\tilde{\rho}h)|

\displaystyle\leq|\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\tilde{s}^{2}\partial^{\mu}F(\tilde{\underline{L}},\partial_{\mu})ds|+\big(\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}F(\tilde{\underline{L}},\tilde{\Omega}_{jk})ds\big)^{2}.

Now the first term is easier since

\displaystyle|\partial^{\mu}F(\tilde{\underline{L}},\partial_{\mu})|\lesssim|\phi|^{6}+|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi|^{2}+|\tilde{\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}}\phi|^{2},

which after integration over the unit sphere can be bounded by using the standard energy conservation (recall that we have lifted the problem to space dimension three) through the backward light cone
\mathcal{N}^{-}(q)
. The second term involves the Maxwell field
F
, for which the energy estimate is absent for solution to the Chern-Simons-Higgs equation. This is in vast contrast to the Maxwell-Klein-Gordon system
[
51
]
for which the energy controls both the scalar field and the Maxwell field. Now we first bound that

\displaystyle\big(\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}F(\tilde{\underline{L}},\tilde{\Omega}_{jk})ds\big)^{2}\lesssim\big(\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\tilde{s}\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi})ds\big)^{2}+\big(\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}|\tilde{x}_{3}|(|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi|+|\tilde{\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}}\phi|)|\phi|ds\big)^{2}.

Although the rough bound for the solution
\phi
may blow up on the boundary, the key observation is that it can only go to infinity at
\tilde{x}_{3}=0
. This structure allows us to control the second term by using the rough pointwise estimate for
\phi
obtained in Thoerem
2
.

It then remains to control the first term for which we make use of the geometric bilinear trace theorem developed by Klainerman-Rodnianski in
[
33
]
(see Theorem
4.1
in the last section). Roughly speaking, the backward light cone
\mathcal{N}^{-}(q)
is null hypersurface which can be written as
[0,\tilde{r}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Note that
\tilde{\underline{L}}
is the tangential derivative. Then the bilinear trace theorem states that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f\cdot D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}gds\right)^{2}d\tilde{\omega}\lesssim\mathcal{N}_{1}(f)\mathcal{N}_{1}(g),

in which
\mathcal{N}_{1}(f)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}f|^{2}+|\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}f|^{2}dsd\omega
. This is closely related to the energy flux through the null hypersurface. Then a simple scaling argument could lead to the desired bound.

The paper is organized as follows: In section 2, we apply the new vector field introduced by the authors in
[
12
]
to obtain time decay for the potential energy. Then in section 3, we show rough pointwise decay estimate for the solution. The main new difficulty is to establish the polynomial growth of the second order energy, which we rely on Strichartz estimate. The last section is devoted to the improvement of pointwise decay estimate for the solution for larger power of
p
. This is realized by using conformal transformation and geometric Kirchoff-Sobolev paramatrix for geometric wave equations.

2
Potential energy decay

As in the flat case when the connection field
A
vanishes, the starting point of the decay mechanism of the solution to this nonlinear system is the time decay of the potential energy.

Proposition 2.1
.

For solution
\phi
to the nonlinear equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
, we have the time decay of the potential energy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\phi(t,x)|^{p+1}(1+t+|x|)^{\min\{\frac{p-1}{2},2\}}dx\leq C\mathcal{E}_{0,2},\quad\forall t\geq 0

for some constant
C
depending only on
p
.

Remark 1
.

Same time decay for the superconformal case when
p\geq 5
and a weaker decay estimate for the case when
3\leq p\leq 5
had been shown by Huh in
[
24
]
. The method relies on the conformal energy identity used for the flat case by Pecher in
[
18
]
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of this proposition, which is similar to that in
[
12
]
. Without loss of generality, we only study the solution in the future, that is,
t\geq 0
. For simplicity, in this section
A\lesssim B
stands for
A\leq CB
for some constant
C
depending only on
p
.

The proof relies on the vector field method originally introduced by Klainerman in
[
32
]
, which
turns out to be quite robust for treating such geometric nonlinear wave equations.
Define the energy momentum tensor for the scalar field
\phi

\displaystyle T[\phi]_{\mu\nu}=\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D_{\nu}\phi\rangle-\frac{1}{2}m_{\mu\nu}(\langle D^{\gamma}\phi,D_{\gamma}\phi\rangle+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}),

where
\langle a,b\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(a\bar{b}+\bar{a}b)
means the inner product for complex numbers
a
and
b
. We can compute that

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}T[\phi]_{\mu\nu}=\langle\Box_{A}\phi-|\phi|^{p-1}\phi,D_{\nu}\phi\rangle+\langle D^{\gamma}\phi,iF_{\gamma\nu}\phi\rangle=\langle\Box_{A}\phi-|\phi|^{p-1}\phi,D_{\nu}\phi\rangle-F_{\gamma\nu}J^{\gamma}[\phi].

Here recall that
J_{\mu}[\phi]=\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D_{\mu}\phi})
is the current defined in the equation (
1
).
For any vector field
X
and any function
\chi
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
, denote the current

P^{X,\chi}_{\mu}[\phi]=T[\phi]_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\chi\cdot|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\chi\partial_{\mu}|\phi|^{2}.

We can compute that

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}P^{X,\chi}_{\mu}[\phi]=

\displaystyle T[\phi]^{\mu\nu}\pi^{X}_{\mu\nu}+\langle\Box_{A}\phi-|\phi|^{p-1}\phi,D_{X}\phi\rangle-X^{\nu}F_{\gamma\nu}J^{\gamma}[\phi]-\frac{1}{2}\Box\chi\cdot|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle+\chi(\langle\phi,\Box_{A}\phi\rangle+\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle),

in which
(\pi^{X})^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial^{\mu}X^{\nu}+\partial^{\nu}X^{\mu})
is the deformation tensor of the metric
m
along the vector field
X
.
For any domain
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
, by using Stokes’ formula, we have the energy identity

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}=\iint_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle T[\phi]^{\mu\nu}\pi^{X}_{\mu\nu}+\langle\Box_{A}\phi-|\phi|^{p-1}\phi,D_{X}\phi\rangle-X^{\nu}F_{\gamma\nu}J^{\gamma}[\phi]

(2)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Box\chi\cdot|\phi|^{2}+\chi(\langle\phi,\Box_{A}\phi\rangle+\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle)d\textnormal{vol},

where
i_{Z}d\textnormal{vol}
is the contraction of the vector field
Z
with the volume form
d\textnormal{vol}
and
\Box
is the wave operator corresponding to the flat metric
m
in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
.

For solution to the Chern-Simons-Higgs equation (
1
), we first note that

\displaystyle\langle\Box_{A}\phi-|\phi|^{p-1}\phi,D_{X}\phi\rangle-X^{\nu}F_{\gamma\nu}J^{\gamma}[\phi]+\chi(\langle\phi,\Box_{A}\phi\rangle+\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle)

\displaystyle=-X^{\nu}\varepsilon_{\gamma\nu\mu}J^{\mu}[\phi]J^{\gamma}[\phi]+\chi(|\phi|^{p+1}+\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle)

\displaystyle=\chi(|\phi|^{p+1}+\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle)

as
\varepsilon_{\gamma\nu\mu}
is skew symmetric.

In space dimension two for the case when
1<p\leq 5
, instead of taking regions bounded by null cones, consider the region

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\{(t,x_{1},x_{2})|0\leq t\leq\min\{x_{1},T\}\}

bounded by the hyperplane
\{t=x_{1}\}
, the initial hypersurface
\{t=0\}
and the constant
t
-slice
\{t=T\}
for any positive constant
T
. In the exterior region, first take the vector field
X=\partial_{t}
and the function
\chi=0
. Since
\partial_{t}
is Killing, the deformation tensor vanishes
\pi^{X}=0
. In particular, the bulk term on the right hand side of the energy identity (
2
) vanishes and hence we derive the classical energy conservation. To compute the boundary integrals, define the null coordinates and frame

u_{1}=t-x_{1}+1,\quad v_{1}=t+x_{1},\quad L_{1}=2\partial_{v_{1}}=\partial_{t}+\partial_{1},\quad\underline{L}_{1}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{1}.

Recall the volume form

d\textnormal{vol}=dtdx=dtdx_{1}dx_{2}=\frac{1}{2}du_{1}dv_{1}dx_{2}.

On the null hyperplane
\{t-x_{1}=0\}
, we have

\displaystyle i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])^{u_{1}}dv_{1}dx_{2}=-(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])_{v_{1}}2dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-(\langle D_{t}\phi,(D_{t}+D_{1})\phi\rangle-\frac{1}{2}(-|D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|D_{1}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}))dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})dtdx_{2}.

On the constant
t
-slice, we compute that

\displaystyle i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}=

\displaystyle(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])^{0}dx=-T[\phi]_{00}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(|D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|D_{1}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})dx.

We hence derive the classical energy conservation in the exterior region

\displaystyle\int_{0\leq t\leq T}(|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}+\int_{x_{1}\geq T}(|D\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(T,x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{1}\geq 0}(|D\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(0,x)dx.

Here
D\phi
is short for
(D_{t}\phi,D_{1}\phi,D_{2}\phi)
.
By letting
T\to+\infty
, it then holds that

\displaystyle\int_{t\geq 0}(|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0,0}.

(3)

Similarly take
X=\partial_{t}
,
\chi=0
and
\mathcal{D}=[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the energy identity (
2
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|D\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(t,x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|D\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(0,x)dx\leq\mathcal{E}_{0,0}.

(4)

Next we choose the vector field
X
and the function
\chi
to be

\displaystyle X=(x_{2}^{2}+(t-x_{1})^{2})\partial_{t}+(x_{2}^{2}-(t-x_{1})^{2})\partial_{1}+2(t-x_{1})x_{2}\partial_{2},\quad\chi=t-x_{1}.

We can compute the deformation tensor

\pi^{X}_{\mu\nu}=2(t-x_{1})m_{\mu\nu}

and it is obvious that

\partial_{t}\chi=1,\quad(\partial_{t}+\partial_{1})\chi=0,\quad\Box\chi=0.

Here recall that
m_{\mu\nu}
is the flat Minkowski metric on
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
. For such choice of vector field, the bulk term on the right hand side of the energy identity (
2
) becomes

\displaystyle T[\phi]^{\mu\nu}\pi^{X}_{\mu\nu}+\chi(\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle+|\phi|^{p+1})

\displaystyle=2\chi\left(\langle D_{\gamma}\phi,D^{\gamma}\phi\rangle-\frac{3}{2}(\langle D^{\gamma}\phi,D_{\gamma}\phi\rangle+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})\right)+\chi(\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle+|\phi|^{p+1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{6(t-x_{1})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}+(t-x_{1})|\phi|^{p+1}=-\frac{(5-p)(t-x_{1})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}.

This implies that the bulk integral is nonnegative restricted to the exterior region when
t\leq x_{1}
for the subconformal case
p\leq 5
.

For the boundary integrals on the left hand side of the energy identity (
2
) with the above chosen vector field
X
and function
\chi
, similarly on the null hyperplane
\{t-x_{1}=0\}
, we have

\displaystyle i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}

\displaystyle=-(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])_{L_{1}}dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-(T[\phi]_{L_{1}\nu}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}L_{1}\chi|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\chi\cdot L_{1}|\phi|^{2})dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-T[\phi]_{L_{1}X}dtdx_{2}=-x_{2}^{2}T[\phi]_{L_{1}L_{1}}dtdx_{2}=-x_{2}^{2}|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}dtdx_{2}.

Here we note that on the null hyperplane
\{t-x_{1}=0\}
it holds that

L_{1}\chi=0,\quad\chi=0,\quad X=x_{2}^{2}L_{1}.

Similarly on the constant
t
-slice
\{t=0\}
or
\{t=T\}
, we have

\displaystyle i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}=

\displaystyle(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])^{0}dx=-(T[\phi]_{0\nu}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\chi|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\chi\cdot\partial_{t}|\phi|^{2})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big((x_{2}^{2}+(t-x_{1})^{2})(|D\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})-|\phi|^{2}+(t-x_{1})\partial_{t}|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+2\langle D_{t}\phi,(x_{2}^{2}-(t-x_{1})^{2})D_{1}\phi+2(t-x_{1})x_{2}D_{2}\phi\rangle\big)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+(t-x_{1})D_{2}\phi|^{2}+|(t-x_{1})(D_{t}\phi-D_{1}\phi)+x_{2}D_{2}\phi+\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2(x_{2}^{2}+(t-x_{1})^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}+\partial_{1}((t-x_{1})|\phi|^{2})-\partial_{2}(x_{2}|\phi|^{2}))dx.

Since the initial data decay sufficiently fast, integration by parts on the hypersurface

\{(t,x_{1},x_{2})|t=t,x_{1}\geq 0\}

leads to

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\geq t}(\partial_{1}((x_{1}-t)|\phi|^{2})+\partial_{2}(x_{2}|\phi|^{2}))(t,x)dx=0.

Combining the above computations, in view of the energy identity (
2
), we obtain the following energy identity

\displaystyle 2\iint_{0\leq t\leq\min(x_{1},T)}\frac{(5-p)(x_{1}-t)}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}d\textnormal{vol}+2\int_{0\leq t\leq T}x_{2}^{2}|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{x_{1}\geq T}|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi-(T-x_{1})D_{2}\phi|^{2}+|(T-x_{1})D_{\underline{L}_{1}}\phi+D_{2}(x_{2}\phi)|^{2}+\frac{2(x_{2}^{2}+(T-x_{1})^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{1}\geq t}(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi-x_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}+|-x_{1}D_{\underline{L}_{1}}\phi+D_{2}(x_{2}\phi)|^{2}+\frac{2(x_{2}^{2}-x_{1}^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})dx,

from which we obtain the weighted energy flux bound

\displaystyle\int_{t\geq 0}x_{2}^{2}|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}

(5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{x_{1}\geq 0}|x_{2}(\phi_{1}+D_{1}\phi_{0})-x_{1}D_{2}\phi_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}(D_{1}\phi_{0}-\phi_{1})+x_{2}D_{2}\phi_{0}+\phi_{0}|^{2}+\frac{2|x|^{2}}{p+1}|\phi_{0}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\{t=0\}}(1+|x|^{2})(|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{D}\phi_{0}|^{2}+|\phi_{0}|^{p+1})dx=\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

Here recall that the implicit constant relies only on
p
and the integral of
|\phi_{0}|^{2}
can be controlled by using a type of Hardy’s inequality.

Finally in the interior region
\{t\geq x_{1}\}
, for the vector field
X
and the function
\chi

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=u_{1}^{q}(\partial_{t}-\partial_{1})+u_{1}^{q-2}x_{2}^{2}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{1})+2u_{1}^{q-1}x_{2}\partial_{2},

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=u_{1}^{q-1},\quad u_{1}=t-x_{1}+1,\quad q=\frac{1}{2}(p-1),

we can compute the covariant derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla_{L_{1}}X=0,\quad

\displaystyle\nabla_{\underline{L}_{1}}X=2qu_{1}^{q-1}\underline{L}_{1}+2(q-2)u_{1}^{q-3}x_{2}^{2}L_{1}+4(q-1)u_{1}^{q-2}x_{2}\partial_{2},

\displaystyle\nabla_{2}X=2u_{1}^{q-2}x_{2}L_{1}+2u_{1}^{q-1}\partial_{2}

in terms of the null frame
\{L_{1},\underline{L}_{1},\partial_{2}\}
.
Therefore we have the nonvanishing components of the deformation tensor
\pi_{\mu\nu}^{X}

\displaystyle\pi^{X}_{L_{1}\underline{L}_{1}}

\displaystyle=-2qu_{1}^{q-1},\quad\pi^{X}_{\underline{L}_{1}\underline{L}_{1}}=-4(q-2)u_{1}^{q-3}x_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle\pi^{X}_{\underline{L}_{1}\partial_{2}}

\displaystyle=2(q-2)u_{1}^{q-2}x_{2},\quad\pi^{X}_{\partial_{2}\partial_{2}}=2u_{1}^{q-1}.

Note
\Box\chi=\Box u_{1}^{q-1}=0
. The bulk term on the right hand side of the energy identity (
2
) is of the form

\displaystyle T[\phi]^{\mu\nu}\pi^{X}_{\mu\nu}+\chi(\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle+|\phi|^{p+1})-\frac{1}{2}\Box\chi|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle=-qu_{1}^{q-1}(|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})+2u_{1}^{q-1}(|D_{2}\phi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\langle D^{\mu}\phi,D_{\mu}\phi\rangle-\frac{1}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})

\displaystyle\quad-(q-2)u_{1}^{q-3}x_{2}^{2}|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}-2(q-2)u_{1}^{q-2}\langle x_{2}D_{2}\phi,D_{L_{1}}\phi\rangle+u_{1}^{q-1}\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle+u_{1}^{q-1}|\phi|^{p+1}

\displaystyle=(2-q)u_{1}^{q-3}|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+u_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}.

This shows that for the subconformal case when
p\leq 5
, the bulk term is nonnegative as
q=\frac{p-1}{2}\leq 2
.

Apply the energy identity (
2
) to the above chosen vector field
X
and function
\chi
for the region
\mathcal{D}=\{\max(x_{1},0)\leq t\leq T\}
. For the boundary integrals on the left hand side, on the null hyperplane
\{t-x_{1}=0\}
,
note that

\chi=u_{1}=1,\quad X=\underline{L}_{1}+x_{2}^{2}L_{1}+2x_{2}\partial_{2}.

Therefore we can compute that

\displaystyle i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}

\displaystyle=-(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])_{L_{1}}dtdx_{2}=-(T[\phi]_{L_{1}\nu}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}L_{1}\chi|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\chi\cdot L_{1}|\phi|^{2})dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-(x_{2}^{2}T[\phi]_{L_{1}L_{1}}+T[\phi]_{L_{1}\underline{L}_{1}}+2x_{2}T[\phi]_{L_{1}\partial_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}L_{1}|\phi|^{2})dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-(x_{2}^{2}|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}+2\langle x_{2}D_{2}\phi,D_{L_{1}}\phi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}L_{1}|\phi|^{2})dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}+\frac{1}{2}L_{1}|\phi|^{2})dtdx_{2}.

On the part of the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\{t-x_{1}=0\}
, integration by parts again, we see that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\{t-x_{1}=0\}}L_{1}|\phi|^{2}dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\{t-x_{1}=0\}}\partial_{v_{1}}|\phi|^{2}dv_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\int_{t=T=x_{1}}|\phi(T,T,x_{2})|^{2}dx_{2}-\int_{t=0=x_{1}}|\phi(0,0,x_{2})|^{2}dx_{2}).

Similarly on the constant
t
-slice, we have

\displaystyle i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}=

\displaystyle(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])^{0}dx=-(T[\phi]_{0\nu}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\chi|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\chi\cdot\partial_{t}|\phi|^{2})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{1}^{q-2}\Big((x_{2}^{2}+u_{1}^{2})(|D\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})-(q-1)|\phi|^{2}+u_{1}\partial_{t}|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2(x_{2}^{2}-u_{1}^{2})\langle D_{t}\phi,D_{1}\phi\rangle+4u_{1}x_{2}\langle D_{t}\phi,D_{2}\phi\rangle\Big)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{1}^{q-2}\Big(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+u_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}+|u_{1}D_{\underline{L}_{1}}\phi+D_{2}(x_{2}\phi)|^{2}+\frac{2(x_{2}^{2}+u_{1}^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}\Big)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\partial_{1}(u_{1}^{q-1}|\phi|^{2})-\partial_{2}(x_{2}u_{1}^{q-2}|\phi|^{2})\Big)dx.

On the part of the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\{t=\tau\}
(
\tau=0
or
T
), integration by parts again, we can show that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\{t=\tau\}}\Big(\partial_{1}(u_{1}^{q-1}|\phi|^{2})-\partial_{2}(x_{2}u_{1}^{q-2}|\phi|^{2})\Big)dx=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\{t=\tau\}}|\phi|^{2}(\tau,\tau,x_{2})dx_{2}.

Here we always use the fact that the solution decays sufficiently fast at spatial infinity (one could also take compact regions to approximate the region
\mathcal{D}
). These computations show that

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq t}(\partial_{1}(u_{1}^{q-1}\phi^{2})-\partial_{2}(x_{2}u_{1}^{q-2}\phi^{2}))dx\Big|_{t=0}^{t=T}=\int_{0\leq t\leq T}(L_{1}|\phi|^{2})(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}.

In other words, those terms on the boundary without a definite sign are canceled.
Therefore combining the above computations, we obtain the energy identity

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq T}u_{1}^{q-2}(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+u_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}+|u_{1}D_{\underline{L}_{1}}\phi+D_{2}(x_{2}\phi)|^{2}+\frac{2(x_{2}^{2}+u_{1}^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(T,x)dx

\displaystyle+2\iint_{\mathcal{D}}(2-q)u_{1}^{q-3}|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+u_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}d\textnormal{vol}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0\leq t\leq T}(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{x_{1}\leq 0}u_{1}^{q-2}(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+u_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}+|u_{1}D_{\underline{L}_{1}}\phi+D_{2}(x_{2}\phi)|^{2}+\frac{2(x_{2}^{2}+u_{1}^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(0,x)dx.

Recall that on the initial hypersurface
\{t=0\}
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq 0}u_{1}^{q-2}(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+u_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}+|u_{1}D_{\underline{L}_{1}}\phi+D_{2}(x_{2}\phi)|^{2}+\frac{2(x_{2}^{2}+u_{1}^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(0,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

In view of the previous estimates (
5
) and (
3
), we can bound the weighted energy flux through the null hyperplane

\displaystyle\int_{0\leq t\leq T}(|x_{2}D_{L_{1}}\phi+D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0\leq t\leq T}(x_{2}^{2}|D_{L_{1}}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})(t,t,x_{2})dtdx_{2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

Since
q=\frac{1}{2}(p-1)\leq 2
, we therefore obtain the weighted potential energy
estimate

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq T}\frac{2(u_{1}^{q}+u_{1}^{q-2}x_{2}^{2})|\phi|^{p+1}}{p+1}dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2},\quad\forall T\geq 0.

Recall that
u_{1}=t-x_{1}+1
. Restricting the above integral to the region
\{x_{1}\leq 0\}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq 0}(t+1)^{q}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x_{1}\leq t}u_{1}^{q}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2},

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq t\leq r}(1+2r)^{q-2}x_{2}^{2}|\phi|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x_{1}\leq t}u_{1}^{q-2}x_{2}^{2}|\phi|^{p+1}dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

Here
r=|x|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}
.
This in particular shows that

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\leq 0}(1+t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx+\int_{x_{1}\leq 0,r\geq t}(1+r)^{\frac{p-1}{2}-2}x_{2}^{2}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

By symmetry (or changing variable
x_{1}\rightarrow-x_{1}
), we also have

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}\geq 0}(1+t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx+\int_{x_{1}\geq 0,r\geq t}(1+r)^{\frac{p-1}{2}-2}x_{2}^{2}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2},

which together with the previous estimate implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx+\int_{r\geq t}(1+r)^{\frac{p-1}{2}-2}x_{2}^{2}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

Now by symmetry again with
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
, we also have

\displaystyle\int_{r\geq t}(1+r)^{\frac{p-1}{2}-2}x_{1}^{2}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

These estimates are sufficient to show the weighted potential energy estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+t+r)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

In particular the proposition holds for the subconformal case when
p\leq 5
.

For the superconformal case when
p\geq 5
, we can directly use the conformal Killing vector field

\displaystyle X=(t^{2}+r^{2})\partial_{t}+2tr\partial_{r},\quad\chi=t.

We can compute the deformation tensor

\displaystyle\pi^{X}_{\mu\nu}=2tm_{\mu\nu}.

Thus for the bulk integral, we can demonstrate that

\displaystyle T[\phi]^{\mu\nu}\pi^{X}_{\mu\nu}+\chi(\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle+|\phi|^{p+1})-\frac{1}{2}\Box\chi|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle=2t\Big(\langle D^{\mu}\phi,D_{\mu}\phi\rangle-\frac{3}{2}(\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})\Big)+t(\langle D_{\mu}\phi,D^{\mu}\phi\rangle+|\phi|^{p+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-5)t}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}.

In particular the bulk integral on the right hand side of the energy identity (
2
) is nonnegative for the superconformal case
p\geq 5
with the above conformal Killing vector field.

Next for the boundary integrals for the region
\mathcal{D}
bounded by the constant
t
-slice
\{t=T\}
and the initial hypersurface
\{t=0\}
, we first have

\displaystyle i_{P^{X,\chi}[\phi]}d\textnormal{vol}=(P^{X,\chi}[\phi])^{0}dx=-(T[\phi]_{0\nu}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\chi|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\chi\cdot\partial_{t}|\phi|^{2})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Big((t^{2}+r^{2})(|D\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1})-|\phi|^{2}+t\partial_{t}|\phi|^{2}+4tx_{1}\langle D_{t}\phi,D_{1}\phi\rangle+4tx_{2}\langle D_{t}\phi,D_{2}\phi\rangle\Big)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{2(t^{2}+r^{2})}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}+|tD_{t}\phi+x_{1}D_{1}\phi+x_{2}D_{2}\phi+\phi|^{2}+|tD_{1}\phi+x_{1}D_{t}\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+|tD_{2}\phi+x_{2}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|x_{2}D_{1}\phi-x_{1}D_{2}\phi|^{2}-\partial_{1}(x_{1}|\phi|^{2})-\partial_{2}(x_{2}|\phi|^{2})\Big)dx.

On the constant
t
-slice
\{t=\tau\}
, note that

\displaystyle\int_{t=\tau}\partial_{1}(x_{1}|\phi|^{2})+\partial_{2}(x_{2}|\phi|^{2})dx=0

as long as the solution decays suitably fast at spatial infinity. We then obtain the conformal energy identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(p-5)t}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}d\textnormal{vol}+\int_{t=T}\frac{t^{2}+r^{2}}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}+Q[\phi]dx=\int_{t=0}\frac{t^{2}+r^{2}}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}+Q[\phi]dx,

(6)

in which

\displaystyle Q[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\Big(|D_{S}\phi+\phi|^{2}+|tD_{1}\phi+x_{1}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|tD_{2}\phi+x_{2}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|D_{\Omega}\phi|^{2}\Big).

(7)

Here
S=t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}
is the scaling vector field and
\Omega=x_{1}\partial_{2}-x_{2}\partial_{1}
is the angular momentum vector field.
Since
p\geq 5
and
Q[\phi]\geq 0
, in view of the standard energy conservation (
4
), we in particular derive that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(t^{2}+r^{2}+1)|\phi|^{p+1}(t,x)dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+r^{2})|\phi|^{p+1}(0,x)+Q[\phi](0,x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}.

We thus finished the proof for Proposition
2.1
.

3
Rough pointwise decay estimate for all
p

As we have pointed previously, the decay mechanism of the solution is the potential energy decay obtained in the previous section. This section is devoted to showing the pointwise decay estimates for the solution by using a type of Brézis-Gallouet-Wainger (see for example in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
) inequality. Although the solution decays faster for larger
p
as discussed in the introduction, in view of the potential energy decay in Proposition
2.1
, the solution decays at most like linear wave for the superconformal case when
p\geq 5
. In other words, the case when
p\geq 5
is similar to that for the case when
p=5
. For simplicity, we only discuss the situation for the subconformal case
p\leq 5
. In this section we derive a rough decay estimate for the solution for all
1<p\leq 5
.

One difficulty in space dimension two is the failure of the classical Sobolev embedding for the
L^{\infty}
bound by the standard energy. This can be compensated by using a type of logarithmic Sobolev inequality. In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let

B_{R}=\{x:|x|\leq R\},\quad B_{R}^{c}=\{x:|x|>R\}.

We first recall the Brézis-Gallouet-Wainger logarithmic Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 3.1
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{3/4})}^{2}(1+\ln\frac{\|u\|_{H^{2}(B_{3/4})}}{\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{3/4})}}),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{c})}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{5/6}^{c})}^{2}(1+\ln\frac{\|u\|_{H^{2}(B_{5/6}^{c})}}{\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{5/6}^{c})}}),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\{\frac{1}{2}\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{2}\})}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|(u,\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left(1+\ln\frac{\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\|(u,\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\right).

Here
\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}u=r^{-1}\Omega u=r^{-1}(x_{1}\partial_{2}-x_{2}\partial_{1})u
.

The proof is essentially based on the classical logarithmic Sobolev inequality originally introduced by Brézis-Gallouet-Wainger. The detailed proof for the above lemma could be found for example in
[
12
]
.

However to adapt the above Sobolev inequality to the covariant derivatives of the solution studied in this paper, we also need a refined version of the above logarithmic Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}>0
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ln\frac{\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}}.

Proof.

For completeness we give a proof for this lemma. Let
\hat{u}
be the Fourier transform

\hat{u}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx.

By Fourier inverse transform, we see that

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\|\hat{u}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|\xi|^{4}+2a|\xi|^{2}+b)|\hat{u}|^{2}d\xi\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|\xi|^{4}+2a|\xi|^{2}+b)^{-1}d\xi\right).

By the assumption, it is obvious that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}>0
. Let

a=1+\frac{\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}},\quad b=\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality implies that

b\leq a-1\leq\frac{a^{2}}{4}.

In particular we can compute that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|\xi|^{4}+2a|\xi|^{2}+b)^{-1}d\xi=\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}+2ar+b}dr=\pi\frac{1}{2\sqrt{a^{2}-b}}\ln\frac{a+\sqrt{a^{2}-b}}{a-\sqrt{a^{2}-b}}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{3}a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2a|\nabla u|^{2}+b|u|^{2}dx\cdot\ln\frac{4a^{2}}{b}

\displaystyle\leq 6\sqrt{3}\pi\ln\frac{\|u\|_{H^{2}}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

The Lemma holds.
∎

To apply the above logarithmic Sobolev inequality, we need higher order weighted energy bound for the solution, which heavily relies on the potential energy decay obtained in the previous section.

Proposition 3.1
.

For the case when
1<p\leq 5
, the solution
\phi
to (
1
) verifies the following weighted energy bound

\displaystyle(1+t)^{2}\|\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|(1+|t-r|)\bar{D}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathcal{E}_{0,2}(1+t)^{\frac{5-p}{2}}

for some constant
C_{p}
depending on
p
. Here recall that
\bar{D}\phi=(D_{1}\phi,D_{2}\phi)
and
\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}=r^{-1}D_{\Omega}
.

Proof.

The above weighted energy estimate relies on the conformal energy identity (
6
) which also holds for the subconformal case
1<p\leq 5
. In view of the potential energy decay in Proposition
2.1
, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{t^{2}+r^{2}}{p+1}|\phi(t,x)|^{p+1}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(|D_{S}\phi+\phi|^{2}+|tD_{1}\phi+x_{1}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|tD_{2}\phi+x_{2}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|D_{\Omega}\phi|^{2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{t^{2}+r^{2}}{p+1}|\phi(0,x)|^{p+1}+Q[\phi](0,x)dx+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(5-p)s}{p+1}|\phi(s,x)|^{p+1}dxds

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}+\mathcal{E}_{0,2}\int_{0}^{t}s(1+s)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}ds

(8)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}(1+s)^{\frac{5-p}{2}}.

Here we still use the notation
A\lesssim B
to be short for
A\leq CB
for some constant
C
relying only on
p
.

First note that for small
t\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{1}\|D_{t}\phi(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0,2}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0,0}}\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0,2}}

by using the standard energy conservation. We remark here that the initial
L^{2}
bound for the solution in terms of the conformal energy is a direct consequence of a type of Hardy’s inequality.

Then for large
t\geq 1
, note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(D_{S}\phi+\phi)\cdot\phi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(tD_{t}\phi+x_{1}D_{1}\phi+x_{2}D_{2}\phi)\cdot\phi dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}t\partial_{t}|\phi|^{2}+x_{1}\partial_{1}|\phi|^{2}+x_{2}\partial_{2}|\phi|^{2}+2|\phi|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\phi|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{1}(x_{1}|\phi|^{2})+\partial_{2}(x_{2}|\phi|^{2})dx

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\phi|^{2}dx.

Combined with the previous conformal energy bound, we in particular obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{t}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|(D_{S}\phi+\phi)(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0,2}}(1+t)^{\frac{5-p}{4}},

from which we have

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi(1)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{1}^{t}\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds\quad

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0,2}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0,2}}\int_{1}^{t}s^{-1}(s+1)^{\frac{5-p}{4}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0,2}}(t+1)^{\frac{5-p}{4}},\quad\forall t\geq 1.

Therefore the
L^{2}
bound for the solution holds.

Next we use the bound for
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
to control the first order weighted energy, which relies on the following observation

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(tD_{1}\phi+x_{1}D_{t}\phi)+\omega_{2}(tD_{2}\phi+x_{2}D_{t}\phi)

\displaystyle=tD_{r}\phi+rD_{t}\phi,

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(tD_{1}\phi+x_{1}D_{t}\phi)-\omega_{1}(tD_{2}\phi+x_{2}D_{t}\phi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{t}{r}D_{\Omega}\phi,\quad\omega_{i}=\frac{x_{i}}{r},\quad i=1,2.

Note that

D_{1}=\omega_{1}D_{r}-\omega_{2}r^{-1}D_{\Omega},\quad D_{2}=\omega_{2}D_{r}+\omega_{1}r^{-1}D_{\Omega}.

We therefore can bound that

\displaystyle(1+\frac{1+t^{2}}{r^{2}})|D_{\Omega}\phi|^{2}+(1+|t-r|^{2})(|D_{1}\phi|^{2}+|D_{2}\phi|^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\frac{1+t^{2}}{r^{2}})|D_{\Omega}\phi|^{2}+(1+|t-r|^{2})(|D_{r}\phi|^{2}+|D_{r^{-1}\Omega}\phi|^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim|\bar{D}\phi|^{2}+(1+\frac{t^{2}}{r^{2}})|D_{\Omega}\phi|^{2}+|\frac{t}{t+r}(tD_{r}\phi+rD_{t}\phi)-\frac{r}{t+r}(tD_{t}\phi+rD_{r}\phi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\bar{D}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}+|D_{S}\phi+\phi|^{2}+|tD_{1}\phi+x_{1}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|tD_{2}\phi+x_{2}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|D_{\Omega}\phi|^{2}.

Now by using the bound (
8
) and the standard energy conservation, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+t^{2}+r^{2})|D_{r^{-1}\Omega}\phi|^{2}+(1+|t-r|^{2})|\bar{D}\phi|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\bar{D}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}+|D_{S}\phi+\phi|^{2}+|tD_{1}\phi+x_{1}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|tD_{2}\phi+x_{2}D_{t}\phi|^{2}+|D_{\Omega}\phi|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0,2}(t+1)^{\frac{5-p}{2}}.

We thus finished the proof for the proposition.
∎

To apply the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, we also need to control the growth of the second order energy. It suffices to show that the second order energy grows at most polynomially in terms of time. Unlike the flat case in
[
12
]
, commuting the equation with covariant derivative may introduce new nonlinear terms. In fact we can compute that

\displaystyle\Box_{A}D_{\mu}\phi=[\Box_{A},D_{\mu}]\phi+D_{\mu}\Box_{A}\phi=2iF_{\nu\mu}D^{\nu}\phi+i\partial^{\nu}F_{\nu\mu}\phi+D_{\mu}(|\phi|^{p-1}\phi).

Since

F_{\nu\mu}=\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}J^{\gamma}[\phi]=\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}\phi}),

we then can show that

\displaystyle\partial^{\nu}F_{\nu\mu}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(D^{\nu}\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}\phi})+\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\nu}D^{\gamma}\phi})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(D^{\nu}\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}\phi})+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\nu}D^{\gamma}\phi}-\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}D^{\nu}\phi})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(D^{\nu}\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}\phi})+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{iF^{\nu\gamma}\phi})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}\Im(D^{\nu}\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}\phi})-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}|\phi|^{2}F^{\nu\gamma}.

In general the first term does not vanish as both
\varepsilon_{\nu\mu\gamma}
and
\Im(D^{\nu}\phi\cdot\overline{D^{\gamma}\phi})
are skew symmetric. It seems that this new type of nonlinearity arising from commutation could not be bounded by using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. To control this term, we rely on a type of Strichartz estimate.

To establish the necessary Strichartz estimate used in this paper, let’s review some standard definitions in harmonic analysis. For sufficiently smooth function
f
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\widehat{f}=\mathcal{F}f
the Fourier transform of
f
. For function
P
defined on
\mathbb{R}
, the differential operator
P(|\nabla|)f
is given through the relation

P(|\nabla|)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(P(|\xi|)\widehat{f}(\xi)).

Define the homogeneous weighted Sobolev norm

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}=\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L^{r}}.

The standard Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}=\dot{H}_{2}^{s}
is the special case when
r=2
.
We also need to use the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}
. Recall the Littlewood–Paley projection

P_{k}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((\varphi(\xi/2^{k})-\varphi(\xi/2^{k-1}))\widehat{f}(\xi))

for some fixed cut-off function
\varphi
, which equals to
1
when
|x|\leq 1
and vanishes when
|x|>2
. Then the Besov norm is given by

\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r,q}^{s}}=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{kqs}\|P_{k}f\|_{L_{x}^{r}}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Obviously the above definition shows that

\dot{B}_{2,2}^{\rho}=\dot{H}_{2}^{\rho}=\dot{H}^{\rho},\quad\dot{H}_{r}^{0}=L^{r}

since the norms are equivalent.

We recall the following embedding

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{\rho}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r,2}^{\rho}},\quad 2\leq r<\infty,

(9)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r,2}^{\rho}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{\rho}},\quad 1<r\leq 2,

(10)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r_{1},s}^{\rho_{1}}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r_{2},s}^{\rho_{2}}},\quad 1\leq r_{2}\leq r_{1}\leq\infty,\ \frac{n}{r_{2}}-\frac{n}{r_{1}}=\rho_{2}-\rho_{1},

(11)

which have been shown for example in
[
16
]
. In particular for the case when
1<r<2
and
n=2
, we have the following trace inequality

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1-2/r}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{1-2/r}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r,2}^{0}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{r}}.

(12)

Moreover by using Hölder inequality, we also have the following type of Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r,s}^{\rho}}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r_{1},s}^{\rho_{1}}}^{\theta}\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{r_{2},s}^{\rho_{2}}}^{1-\theta},\quad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{r_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{r_{2}},\ \rho=\theta\rho_{1}+(1-\theta)\rho_{2},\ \theta\in(0,1).

(13)

Now let’s recall the classical Strichartz estimate in space dimension
2
for the half wave equation.

Lemma 3.3

(Strichartz Estimate)
.

Assume that
2\leq q,\rho\leq\infty
such that

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{\rho}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Let
b=1-\frac{2}{\rho}-\frac{1}{q}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for function
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\exp(\pm it|\nabla|)f\|_{L_{t}^{q}\dot{B}_{\rho,2}^{-s}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{b-s}}.

See proof for example in
[
16
]
or
[
31
]
. Based on the classical Strichartz estimate, we first establish a type of Strichartz estimate for linear half wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
\frac{8}{5}<r<2
and
\frac{2}{r}-\frac{1}{4}<s<1
. Let
u(t,x)
be solution to the linear half wave equation

(\partial_{t}\pm i|\nabla|)u=f

in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
.
Then there exists a constant
C
, independent of
f
and
T
, such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}).

Here the
L^{r}
norm and
\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s}
is taken with respect to the variable
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

By Duhamel’s formula we have

\displaystyle u(T+t)=\exp(\mp it|\nabla|)u(T)+\int_{0}^{t}\exp(\mp i(t-s)|\nabla|)f(T+s)ds=:u_{1}(T+t)+u_{2}(T+t).

Apply Lemma
3.3
with

\rho=\infty,\quad b=s,\quad q=\frac{1}{1-s}.

The assumption on
r
and
s
shows that
q>4
. Then by using
Hölder inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L_{t}^{q}\dot{B}_{\rho,2}^{-s}}=\|\exp(it|\nabla|)u(T)\|_{L_{t}^{q}\dot{B}_{\rho,2}^{-s}}\leq C\|u(T)\|_{L^{2}}.

To bound
u_{2}
, we still rely on the Lemma
3.3
with

\rho=\infty,\quad b=s+1-\frac{2}{r},\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{2}{r}-s.

Since
r<2
and
s<1
, we have
0<\frac{2}{r}-s<\frac{1}{4}
, which indicates that
q>4
.
Hence by using Hölder inequality and the embedding (
12
), we can estimate that

\displaystyle\|\exp(\mp i(t-s)|\nabla|)f(T+s)\|_{L^{4}([s,1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\exp(\mp i(t-s)|\nabla|)f(T+s)\|_{L_{t}^{q}\dot{B}_{\rho,2}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f(T+s)\|_{\dot{H}^{b-s}}=C\|f(T+s)\|_{\dot{H}^{1-2/r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f(T+s)\|_{L^{r}},\quad 0<s<1.

Then Minkowski inequality leads to

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(T)\|_{L^{2}}+C\|f\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}).

This completes the proof for the Lemma.
∎

Next we adapt the above Strichartz estimate for the half wave equation to the linear transport equation for the scalar field under Coulomb gauge.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u=(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})
be defined on
\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
. Denote

f_{0}=\partial_{t}u_{0}-\partial_{1}u_{1}-\partial_{2}u_{2},\quad f_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}u_{\mu},\quad\mu,\nu=0,1,2.

Assume that
\frac{8}{5}<r<2
and
\frac{2}{r}-\frac{1}{4}<s<1
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|(f_{0},f_{01},f_{02},f_{12})\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}).

for some constant
C
which is independent of
T
and
u
.

Proof.

To reduce this lemma to the previous lemma, we rely on the Riesz transform

R_{j}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(-i\xi_{j}|\xi|^{-1}\widehat{f}(\xi)),j=1,2.

In particular by definition we have

|\nabla|R_{j}f=-\partial_{j}f,\quad R_{1}^{2}f+R_{2}^{2}f=-f.

Moreover since the operators
R_{1},R_{2},|\nabla|,\partial_{1},\partial_{2}
are commutative, we deduce that

R_{1}\partial_{1}f+R_{2}\partial_{2}f=|\nabla|f.

Regarding the Riesz transform
R_{j}
, it is bounded on
L^{r}
for
1<r<\infty
(see for example chapter one in
[
44
]
). As the Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}
is homogeneous, the Riesz transform is also bounded on it for
1\leq p\leq\infty
(it suffices to consider the case when
\hat{f}
is supported on
|\xi|\in[\frac{1}{2},2]
).

Now let

h_{1}=R_{1}u_{1}+R_{2}u_{2},\quad h_{2}=-R_{1}u_{2}+R_{2}u_{1}.

Then we get

\displaystyle u_{1}=-R_{1}h_{1}-R_{2}h_{2},

\displaystyle\quad u_{2}=-R_{2}h_{1}+R_{1}h_{2},

\displaystyle|\nabla|h_{1}=-\partial_{1}u_{1}-\partial_{2}u_{2},

\displaystyle\quad|\nabla|h_{2}=\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1}=f_{12}.

In particular, we derive that

\partial_{t}u_{0}+|\nabla|h_{1}=f_{0}.

On the other hand, we compute that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{1}=R_{1}\partial_{t}u_{1}+R_{2}\partial_{t}u_{2}=R_{1}(\partial_{1}u_{0}+f_{01})+R_{2}(\partial_{2}u_{0}+f_{02})=|\nabla|u_{0}+f_{3},

in which we denote
f_{3}=R_{1}f_{01}+R_{2}f_{02}.
Therefore we conclude that

(\partial_{t}\mp i|\nabla|)(u_{0}\pm ih_{1})=f_{0}\pm if_{3}.

Lemma
3.4
then implies that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\pm ih_{1}\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\pm ih_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|f_{0}\pm if_{3}\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}).

Since the Riesz transforms
R_{1},R_{2}
are bounded on
L^{2}
and
L^{r}
, we therefore conclude that

\displaystyle\|(u_{0},h_{1})\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|(u_{0},h_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|(f_{0},f_{3})\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|(u_{0},R_{1}u_{1}+R_{2}u_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|(f_{0},R_{1}f_{01}+R_{2}f_{02})\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|(f_{0},f_{01},f_{02})\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}).

Now it remains to estimate
h_{2}
. Recall that
|\nabla|h_{2}=f_{12}
. By using the embedding (
12
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|h_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2/r}}=\||\nabla|h_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-2/r}}=\|f_{12}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-2/r}}\leq C\|f_{12}\|_{L^{r}}.

Since the Riesz transforms
R_{1},R_{2}
are bounded on
L^{2}
, we then conclude that

\displaystyle\|h_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}=\|-R_{1}u_{2}+R_{2}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}\leq C\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}.

The assumptions on
r
and
s
show that

0<1-s<1-(\frac{2}{r}-\frac{1}{4})=\frac{5}{4}-\frac{2}{r}<2-\frac{2}{r}.

Hence by using the embedding (
11
), (
13
) and (
10
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|h_{2}\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s}}\leq C\|h_{2}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{1-s}}\leq C\|h_{2}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{0}}^{1-\theta}\|h_{2}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{2-2/r}}^{\theta}\leq C\|h_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}\|h_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2/r}}^{\theta},

in which

0<\theta=\frac{1-s}{2-\frac{2}{r}}<\frac{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{2}{r}}{2-\frac{2}{r}}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Then by using Hölder inequality we can show that

\displaystyle\|h_{2}\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\leq\|h_{2}\|_{L^{1/\theta}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|h_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}^{1-\theta}\|h_{2}\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];\dot{H}^{2-2/r})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}^{1-\theta}\|f_{12}\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}([T,T+1];L^{2})}+\|f_{12}\|_{L^{1}([T,T+1];L^{r})}).

Finally as
u_{1}=-R_{1}h_{1}-R_{2}h_{2},

u_{2}=-R_{2}h_{1}+R_{1}h_{2}
and
R_{1},R_{2}
are bounded on
\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u_{0},h_{1},h_{2})\|_{L^{4}([T,T+1];\dot{B}_{\infty,2}^{-s})}

For the geometric nonlinear equation studied here, there are extra complicated nonlinear terms when applying the Poisson paramatrix as we have already seen for the growth of higher order energy.

Alternatively, we make use of the conformal invariance structure of the system (

can be sufficiently small by taking

In the following, we will also allow the implicit constant in

for the Cauchy problem to the original system (

4.1
Conformal transformation

In view of finite speed of propagation, the solution is support in
\{t+2\geq|x|\}
.
Define the hyperboloid

\mathbb{H}:=\left\{(t,x)|(t^{*})^{2}-|x|^{2}=t^{*}\right\},\quad t^{*}=t+2

and the map

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}:(t,x)\longmapsto\left(-\frac{t^{*}}{(t^{*})^{2}-|x|^{2}}+1,\quad\frac{x}{(t^{*})^{2}-|x|^{2}}\right)

\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
to itself. We are in particular interested in the region inclosed by the hyperboloid
\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}:=\left\{(t,x)|(t^{*})^{2}-|x|^{2}\geq t^{*}\right\}.

The image of

under the above map

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{D})=\left\{(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})|\quad\tilde{t}+|\tilde{x}|<1,\quad\tilde{t}\geq 0\right\}.

For solution
(\phi,A)
to the Chern-Simons-Higgs equation (
1

(\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{D}),\tilde{g}=d(\mathbf{\Phi}^{-1})^{*}m)

Let

\Lambda(t,x)=(t^{*})^{2}-|x|^{2},\quad\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})=\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(\mathbf{\Phi}^{-1}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})),\quad\tilde{A}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})=A(\mathbf{\Phi}^{-1}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})).

Here we emphasize that as a 1-form

is invariant under the change of coordinates.
We need to find the equations for
\tilde{\phi}
,
\tilde{A}
on
\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{D})
. In

\tilde{g}=\Lambda^{-2}m.

Hence the Christoffel symbols for the metric
\tilde{g}
under the coordinates system

are

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{l}

\displaystyle=-\Lambda^{-1}(\delta_{\mu}^{\ l}\partial_{\nu}\Lambda+\delta_{\nu}^{\ l}\partial_{\mu}\Lambda-m_{\mu\nu}\partial^{l}\Lambda).

For the covariant derivative, still under the coordinates
(t,x)
on
\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{D})
, we compute that

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mu}\tilde{\phi}=\partial_{\mu}(\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)+iA_{\mu}\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi=\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}D_{\mu}\phi+\partial_{\mu}\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi.

The covariant wave operator verifies

\displaystyle\Box_{\tilde{A}}\tilde{\phi}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{2}m^{\mu\nu}\tilde{D}_{\mu}\tilde{D}_{\nu}\tilde{\phi}

Theorem 4.1

(Geometric bilinear trace theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the conic cone
\{(t,x)|t+|x|=1,0\leq t\}
and
A
a 1-form in
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\int_{|\omega|=1}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}(f\cdot D_{\underline{L}}g)(s,s\omega)ds\Big)^{2}d\omega\leq C\mathcal{N}_{1}(f)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{1}(g)

for any sufficiently smooth function
f,g
on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
.
Here the energy
\mathcal{N}_{1}(f)
is defined for any
0\leq\tau\leq 1

\mathcal{N}_{\tau}(f)=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{|\omega|=1}\tau(|D_{\underline{L}}f|^{2}+|\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}f|^{2})+\tau^{-1}|f|^{2}dsd\omega

under the null frame
\{L=\partial_{t}+\partial_{r},\quad\underline{L}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{r},e_{1},e_{2}\}
and
D
is the covariant derivative associated to the connection field
A
.

See Theorem 4.1 in
[
33
]
. We emphasize here that the original version of Theorem 4.1 in
[
33
]
is much stronger and applies to the more general setting of Lorentzian manifolds. The connection field
A
plays the geometric role of a Lorentzian metric.

To apply the above theorem, consider the cone
\mathcal{N}^{-}(q)\cap\{t_{0}-\tilde{r}\leq t\leq t_{0}\}
. Scaling implies that

\displaystyle\int_{|\omega|=1}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}(f\cdot D_{\underline{L}}g)(s,s\omega)ds\Big)^{2}d\omega\leq C\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{r}}(f)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{r}}(g).

Note that

s^{2}\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi})=\Im((s\phi)\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}(s\phi)}).

In view of Proposition
4.1
, we can bound that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{r}}(s\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\int_{|\tilde{\omega}|=1}\tilde{r}(|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}(s\phi)|^{2}+|\tilde{\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}}(s\phi)|^{2})+\tilde{r}^{-1}|s\phi|^{2}dsd\tilde{\omega}

\displaystyle=\tilde{r}\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\int_{|\tilde{\omega}|=1}(|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi|^{2}+|\tilde{\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}}\phi|^{2})s^{2}dsd\tilde{\omega}+\tilde{r}^{-1}\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\int_{|\tilde{\omega}|=1}|\phi|^{2}s^{2}dsd\tilde{\omega}+\tilde{r}^{2}\int_{|\tilde{\omega}|=1}|\phi|^{2}d\tilde{\omega}|_{s=\tilde{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{r}\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\int_{|\tilde{\omega}|=1}(|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi|^{2}+|\tilde{\mbox{$D\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{p+1})s^{2}dsd\tilde{\omega}+\tilde{r}\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\int_{|\tilde{\omega}|=1}|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi|^{2}s^{2}dsd\tilde{\omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{r}\sqrt{\tilde{r}}.

The second last step follows from a type of Hardy’s inequality by controlling
\phi
with its derivative
|D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi|
and the value on the sphere
s=\tilde{r}
.

Now denote

G(\tilde{r},\tilde{\omega})=\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}\Im((s\phi)\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}(s\phi)})ds.

In particular by using the rough bound (
23
), we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\tilde{r}\rightarrow 0}\tilde{r}^{-1}G(\tilde{r},\tilde{\omega})=0.

Then the above argument implies that

\|G(\tilde{r},\tilde{\omega})\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{\omega}}}\lesssim\tilde{r}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Taking the derivative in in terms of
\tilde{r}
, we derive that

s^{-1}\partial_{s}G(s,\tilde{\omega})=s^{-1}\Im((s\phi)\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}(s\phi)})=s\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi}).

Integrate in terms of
s
from
0
to
\tilde{r}
. We can estimate that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}s\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi})ds=\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}s^{-1}\partial_{s}G(s,\tilde{\omega})ds=\tilde{r}^{-1}G(\tilde{r},\tilde{\omega})+\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}G(s,\tilde{\omega})s^{-2}ds.

We therefore can show that

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}s\Im(\phi\cdot\overline{D_{\tilde{\underline{L}}}\phi})ds\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{\omega}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{r}^{-1}G(\tilde{r},\tilde{\omega})\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{\omega}}}+\|\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}s^{-2}G(s,\tilde{\omega})ds\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{\omega}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{r}^{-1}\tilde{r}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}s^{-2}\|G(s,\tilde{\omega})\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{\omega}}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{r}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{0}^{\tilde{r}}s^{-2}s^{\frac{3}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\tilde{r}}.

Combining the estimates (
27
), (
28
), (
29
) and (
30
), we have shown that

\displaystyle\int_{S_{(t_{0}-\tilde{r},x_{0})(\tilde{r})}}|\tilde{r}^{2}(\mbox{$\Delta\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}_{A}h-i\tilde{\rho}h)|d\tilde{\omega}\lesssim\sqrt{\tilde{r}}+\tilde{r}^{\frac{p-3-2\epsilon_{1}}{4}}+\tilde{r}\lesssim\tilde{r}^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Here recall that
p>4
and
p-3-2\epsilon_{1}>1
.

Note that on the cone
\mathcal{N}^{-}(q)
it holds that

\displaystyle 1-t_{0}+|x_{0}|\leq 1-(t_{0}-s)+|x_{0}+s\tilde{\omega}|=1-t+r.

From the representation formula (
24
) and the bound (
26
), we then obtain that

\begin{split}&(1-t_{0}+|x_{0}|)^{\frac{5+2\epsilon-p}{2}}|\phi(t_{0},x_{0})|^{p_{1}^{\prime}}\\
&\lesssim 1+\Big|\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{|\tilde{\omega}|=1}|\widetilde{\mbox{$\Delta\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}}_{A}h-i\widetilde{\rho}|\mathcal{M}(t_{0}-\tilde{r})^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{r}d\widetilde{r}d\widetilde{\omega}\Big|^{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathcal{M}(t_{0}-\tilde{r})\tilde{r}^{-1+\epsilon}d\tilde{r}\\
&\lesssim 1+\Big|\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathcal{M}(t_{0}-\tilde{r})^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{r}^{-\frac{3}{4}}d\widetilde{r}\Big|^{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathcal{M}(t_{0}-\tilde{r})\tilde{r}^{-1+\epsilon}d\tilde{r}\\
&\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathcal{M}(t_{0}-\tilde{r})\tilde{r}^{-1+\epsilon}d\tilde{r}.\end{split}

For fixed
t_{0}
, take supreme for
|x_{0}|\leq 1-t_{0}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{M}(t)
, we then obtain that

\mathcal{M}(t_{0})\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathcal{M}(t_{0}-\tilde{r})\tilde{r}^{-1+\epsilon}d\tilde{r},

which implies the uniform bound

\mathcal{M}(t_{0})\lesssim 1,\quad\forall t_{0}\leq 1

by using the following variant of
Gronwall’s inequality.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be a positive continuous function on
[0,1]
. If there exist positive constants
A
,
B
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
so that for all
t_{0}\in[0,1]
it holds that

\displaystyle f(t_{0})\leq A+B\int_{0}^{t_{0}}s^{-\gamma}f(t_{0}-s)ds.

Then, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
B
and
\gamma
so that

\displaystyle f(t)\leq CA,\quad\forall 0\leq t\leq 1.

See Lemma 2.2 in
[
48
]
.

Homotopy equivalences and Grothendieck duality over rings with finite Gorenstein weak global dimension
†

Abstract




Abstract:

Let
R
be a ring with Gwgldim
(R)<\infty
. We obtain a triangle-equivalence

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})

which restricts to a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})

\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
. This class of rings includes, among others, (left) Gorenstein rings, Ding-Chen rings and the more general Gorenstein
n
-coherent rings (
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},n\geq 2
).
As application, we establish some triangle-equivalences of Grothendieck duality over Ding-Chen rings and Gorenstein
n
-coherent rings.

Keywords:
(relative) Gorenstein rings; homotopy category of (Gorenstein) projective modules; (relative) derived category; triangle equivalence; duality pairs;
Grothendieck duality.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
: 18G25, 18E65; 16E50; 18G80; 18G35.

1.
Introduction

Gorenstein rings were introduced by Iwanaga in
[
37
]
.
They play an important role in (Gorenstein) homological algebra, representation theory of groups and algebras, etc.
As a generalization of Gorenstein rings,
Ding-Chen rings
were initially introduced by Ding and
Chen in
[
20
]
under the name “
m
-FC rings” and renamed by Gillespie in
[
31
]
.
To be more general, Wang, Liu, and Yang introduced
Gorenstein

n
-
coherent rings
in
[
52
]
.
It is defined as a two-sided
n
-coherent ring
R
such that all FP
n
-injective left and right
R
-modules have finite flat dimension at most
m
for some integer
m\geq 0
(see Definition
4.10
).
One motivation for introducing such rings is to give a negative answer to a question posed by Gillespie in
[
33
]
.

Another generalization of Gorenstein rings is the so-called
left Gorenstein ring
, introduced by Beligiannis
[
5
]
. It is defined as a ring
R
such that all projective left
R
-modules have finite injective dimension and all injective left
R
-modules have finite projective dimension. This ring is just a ring whose left Gorenstein global dimension, Ggldim
(-)
, is finite (see, for example,
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
). In addition, by
[
5
, Corollary 6.11]
, a ring is Gorenstein if and only if it is two-sided noetherian and left Gorenstein. One important feature about left Gorenstein rings is that they provide canonical triangle equivalences between well-known homotopy categories. As usual, denote by
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})
) the homotopy category of Gorenstein projective (resp. Gorenstein injective) left
R
-modules.
Its homotopy subcategory consisting of projective (resp. injective) left
R
-modules is denoted by
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
).
We also denote by
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
) the homotopy subcategory of
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
) consisting of exact complexes of projective (resp. injective) left
R
-modules.
The study of compactness of
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
and
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
and triangle equivalence between them was initiated in 2005 by Krause
[
40
]
and Jørgensen
[
39
]
, and has been extensively investigated during the last years by Iyengar, Krause, Neeman, Chen, Št’ovíček, Asadollahi, Hafezi, Salarian, Positselski and Gillespie
[
38
,
42
,
43
,
13
,
48
,
1
,
33
,
45
]
. Now the relation between such homotopy categories and left Gorenstein rings is made explicit in the following result due to Chen

Theorem 1.1
.

(=
[
13
, Theorem B]
).

Let
R
be a left Gorenstein ring.
Then there is a triangle-equivalence

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})

which restricts to a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj}).

Another classical Gorensteing homological global invariant is the Gorenstein weak global dimension of a ring
R
, Gwgldim
(R)
. It is a refinement of the usual weak global dimension of
R
, and it is closely related to Ggldim
(R)
.
Recently, we have used different methods to prove that Gwgldim
(R)\leq
Ggldim
(R)
in
[
53
, Theorem 3.7]
and in
[
16
, Theorem 5.13]
. In other words, any left Gorenstein ring admits finite Gorenstein weak global dimension.
On the other hand, it is shown in Remark
2.6
that a ring
R
is of Gwgldim
(R)<\infty
if and only if all injective left and right
R
-modules have finite flat dimension.
Thus, it is easy to see that any Gorenstein
n
-coherent ring
R
(in particular, any Gorenstein and Ding-Chen ring) admits Gwgldim
(R)<\infty
.

Our main result substantially improves Theorem
1.1
by relaxing the assumption of the finiteness of the Gorenstein global dimension to the weak one. Notice that there are examples of rings with infinite Gorenstein global dimension but finite Gorenstein weak global dimension (see Example
4.13
).

Theorem 1.2
.

(=Theorem
4.7
).

Let
R
be a ring with
\mathrm{Gwgldim}(R)<\infty
.
Then there is a triangle-equivalence

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})

which restricts to a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj}).

We also refer to
[
13
]
to note the following: it is not clear, just using Theorem
1.1
and its proof there, that over a
left
Gorenstein ring
R
whether or not the following hold:

(1) the triangle equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})

can restrict to a triangle-equivalence

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj});\quad\text{and}

(2) the triangle equivalences involving the corresponding homotopy categories of
right

R
-modules hold true as well.

With our proof of Theorem
1.2
, we give an explicit construction of the equivalent functor between
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})
and
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})
, which guarantees that the answer to (1) is affirmative (see Corollary
4.9
).
On the other hand, it is known from
[
15
, Corollary 2.5]
that the notion of Gorenstein weak global dimension of rings is left-right symmetric. Hence the answer to (2) is also affirmative (see Corollary
4.9
).

The application of Theorem
1.2
is twofold. One is to obtain a triangle equivalence which relates the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
and the the subcategory of flat modules whenever
R
is a flat-typed
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A})
-Gorenstein ring with respect to a bi-complete duality pair
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A})
(see Theorem
6.6
for the result and see Definitions 2.1-2.3 for the corresponding notions).
Moreover, we show that the equivalent triangulated categories
are compactly generated under some mild conditions (see Theorem
6.10
).

The other is to obtain some triangle equivalences involving Grothendieck duality over Ding-Chen rings and more general Gorenstein
n
-coherent rings.
Let us give a simple background of Grothendieck duality and describe our work in more detail.

Grothendieck duality is a classical subject which can go back 1958s. Roughly speaking, it is a statement concerning the existence of a right adjoint to
the “direct image with compact support” functor between derived categories of sheaves or modules.
For a ring
R
, over the years many people investigated Grothendieck duality for derived categories of
R
-modules by providing the following insights:

(
GD1
)

R
has a dualizing complex.

(
GD2
)

There is a triangle-equivalence
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{mod})^{op}\simeq\mathbf{D}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{mod}),

where the right category is the bounded derived category of finitely presented left
R
-modules and the left one is the opposite category of the bounded derived category of finitely presented right
R
-modules;

(
GD3
)

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
and
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
are triangulated equivalent.

According to
[
42
, Introduction]
, we know that, over a left noetherian and right coherent ring
R
such that all flat left
R
-modules have finite projective dimension, there are implications

(\textbf{GD1})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD3})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD2}).

In Remark
7.1
we note that the assumptions on
R
to fulfill these implications can be relaxed to coherent rings
R
such that every FP-injective left
R
-module has finite injective dimension.
Also, for a left noetherian and right coherent ring
R
, Theorem
1.1
and its corollary
[
13
, Corollary C]
show that the above implications remain true if we replace
(\textbf{GD1})
with “
R
is a left Gorenstein ring”. We note in Remark
7.4
that the left noetherian assumption for
R
can be relaxed to
left coherent
.

Motivated by these results, if we apply Theorem
1.2
to Ding-Chen rings, we obtain that, over a left and right coherent ring
R
, the above implications still hold true if we replace
(\textbf{GD1})
with “
R
is a Ding-Chen ring” (see Corollary
7.3
).

Let
R
be a left noetherian ring. Denote by
\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R
-proj) the bounded homotopy category of finitely presented and projective left
R
-modules and
let
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R
-mod) be as above. The Verdier quotient triangulated category

\mathbf{D}_{sg}(R):=\mathbf{D}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{mod})/\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{proj})

is known as the
singularity category
of left
R
-modules in the literature.
This Verdier quotient was introduced primarily by Buchweitz in
[
12
]
under the name “stable derived category” and renamed by Orlov in
[
44
]
.
On the one hand, by Buchweitz’s theorem (see
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
) singularity categories are related with Gorenstein projective modules.
On the other hand, using Krause’s recollement,
Krause
[
40
, Corollary 5.4]
proved that the homotopy category
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
is compactly generated
with a triangle equivalence

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})^{c}\simeq\mathbf{D}_{sg}(R),

where
\mathcal{T}^{c}
denotes the triangulated subcategory of compact objects of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
.

Motivated by
[
40
, Corollary 5.4]
and applying Theorem
1.2
to Ding-Chen rings and left Gorenstein rings,
we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

(=Corollary
7.9
).

Let
R
be a left and right coherent ring. Consider the following conditions

(
GD1
′
)

R
is Ding-Chen or left Gorenstein;

(
GD2
′
)

There is a triangle-equivalence
\mathbf{D}_{sg}(R^{op})^{op}\simeq\mathbf{D}_{sg}(R),

where the left category is the opposite category of the singularity category of right
R
-modules;

(
GD3
′
)

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
and
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
are triangulated equivalent.

Then there are implications

(\textbf{GD1${}^{\prime}$})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD3${}^{\prime}$})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD2${}^{\prime}$}).

Finally, the above two results for Ding-Chen rings have both a version for Gorenstein
n
-coherent rings (see Corollary
7.2
and Theorem
7.7
).

2.1.
Cotorsion pairs.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an abelian category and
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
be subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
.
A pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is called a
cotorsion pair
if

\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{X}=

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}
. Here
\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=

\{C\in\mathcal{C}~|~\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,C)=0,\forall X\in\mathcal{X}\}
, and
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}
can be defined dually.
A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is said to be
hereditary
if Ext
{}^{m}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=0
for all
X\in\mathcal{X}
,
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
and
m\geq 1
.
A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is said to be
cogenerated~by~a~set
if there is a set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
of objects in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
.
A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is called
complete
if
for any object
C
of
\mathcal{C}
, there are short exact
sequences
0\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
and
0\rightarrow C\rightarrow Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
in
\mathcal{C}
with

X,X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
and
Y,Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}
.
A triple
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Y})
is called a
cotorsion triple
if the two pairs
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z})
and
(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Y})
are cotorsion pairs.
We say that a cotorsion triple
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Y})
is
complete
(resp.
hereditary
)
if the two cotosion pairs
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z})
and
(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Y})
are complete (resp. hereditary).

Given
C\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{X}\subseteq

\mathcal{C}
, a
special
\mathcal{X}
-preenvelope
of
C
is defined as a monic
morphism
\alpha:C\to X
with
X\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mbox{\rm Coker}\alpha\in{{}^{\perp}\mathcal{X}}
.
A subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
injectively coresolving
if it is closed under extensions and cokernels of monic morphisms, and contains all injective objects of
\mathcal{C}
.
Dually, we have the definitions of

special

\mathcal{X}
-
precover
and that
\mathcal{X}
is
projectively resolving
.

We say that a subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{C}
is
thick
if
\mathcal{X}
is closed under direct summands and such that if any two out of three of the terms in
a short exact sequence are in
\mathcal{X}
, then so is the third.

If
\mathcal{C}
has enough projectives and enough injectives, then we say that a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
of subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
is a
projective cotorsion pair
(resp.
injective cotorsion pair
) if
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
forms a complete cotorsion pair such that
\mathcal{Y}
(resp.
\mathcal{X}
) is thick and
\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{Y}
coincides with the subcategory of all projective (resp. injective) objects. Equivalently, if
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
forms a hereditary complete cotorsion pair such that
\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{Y}
coincides with the subcategory of all projective (resp. injective) objects (see
[
29
, Definition 3.4]
and
[
29
, Propositions 3.6 and 3.7]
).

2.2.
Duality pairs and relative Gorenstein rings

As usual, we write

(_{R}M)^{+}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(_{R}M,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
for a left
R
-module
{}_{R}M
.
The following definitions can be found in

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a ring.

(1) A
duality pair
over
R
is a pair
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})
, where
{}_{R}\mathcal{L}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}_{R}
) is a subcategory consisting of left (resp. right)

R
-modules, subject to the following two conditions:

(i)
{}_{R}L\in~_{R}\mathcal{L}
if and only if
(_{R}L)^{+}\in~\mathcal{A}_{R}
, and

(ii)
\mathcal{A}_{R}

(2) A duality pair
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})
over

(co)product-closed

{}_{R}\mathcal{L}
is closed under arbitrary (co)products.

(3) A duality pair
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})
over
R

(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})

{}_{R}\mathcal{L}

(4) A duality pair
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})
over

if
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})

over

(_{R^{op}}\mathcal{A},\mathcal{L}_{R^{op}})

R^{op}
.

(5) A duality pair
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})
over
R
is called
complete
if
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})

(6) A duality pair
(_{R}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}_{R})
over

is called

if

is a complete duality pair over

such that the pair
(^{\perp}\mathcal{A}_{R},\mathcal{A}_{R})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair in

-Mod which is

Next we consider the Gorenstein analogue notions.

Definition 2.8
.

(1) According to
[
47
, Theorem 5.6]
, for any ring
R
, there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}},~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
in
R
-Mod.
So one has the notion of dg-
{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}
complexes.
We say that a complex
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is
dg
-
Gorenstein injective
if
G^{\bullet}
is dg-
{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}
.
In addition, a morphism
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\to~_{R}G^{\bullet}
is called
dg
-
Gorenstein injective resolution

of the complex
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
if it is a dg-
{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}
resolution of
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
,
that is,
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\to~_{R}G^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphism with
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
dg-Gorenstein injective.

(2) Let
R
be a ring such that there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}})
in
R
-Mod. Then one gets the notion of dg-
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}
complexes.
We say that a complex
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is
dg
-
Gorenstein projective
if
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is dg-
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}
.
In addition, a morphism
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}\to~_{R}X^{\bullet}
is called
dg
-
Gorenstein projective resolution

of the complex
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
if it is a dg-
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}
resolution of
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
,
that is,
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}\to~_{R}X^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphism with
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
dg-Gorenstein projective.

2.6.
Gorenstein derived categories

As mentioned above, we denote by
\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
the derived category of all left
R
-modules.
Gao and Zhang
[
26
]
introduced the notion of Gorenstein derived categories.
These notions can be unified as the following:

Definition 2.9
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
,
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
be subcategories of
R
-Mod which contain
{}_{R}\mathcal{P}
and
{}_{R}\mathcal{I}
respectively.

(1)

A complex
{}_{R}C^{\bullet}
in
Ch
(R\text{-Mod})
is called
right
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
-acyclic
(resp.
left
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
-acyclic
) if
{}_{R}C^{\bullet}
is
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(_{R}\mathcal{X},-)
-exact. (resp.
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(-,~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
-exact).

(2)

A morphism
f^{\bullet}:~_{R}C^{\bullet}\rightarrow~_{R}D^{\bullet}
in
Ch
(R\text{-Mod})
is said to be a
right
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
-quasi-isomorphism
(resp.
left
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
-quasi-isomorphism
)
if Hom
{}_{R}(_{R}X,f^{\bullet})
is a quasi-isomorphism for all
{}_{R}X\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}
(resp. Hom
{}_{R}(f^{\bullet},~_{R}Y)
is a quasi-isomorphism for all
{}_{R}Y\in~_{R}\mathcal{Y}
).

(3)

Denote by
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}
(resp.
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac}
) the subcategory of
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

consisting of all right
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
-acyclic (resp. left
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
-acyclic) complexes. So
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})
and
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})

mean the the corresponding homotopy subcategories respectively. Note that such homotopy subcategories are both a thick subcategory of
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.
Now, the relative derived category with respect to
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
(resp.
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
),
denoted by
\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
(resp.
\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
), is defined as the following Verdier quotient triangulated category:

\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})~(\mathrm{resp}.\quad\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac})).

If we take the pair
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
as
(_{R}\mathcal{P},~_{R}\mathcal{I})
, then the relative derived categories with respect to
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
and
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
,
coincide with each other, which are exactly the usual derived category of
R
-Mod,
\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.

If we take the pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
as
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
, then the relative derived categories with respect to
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
and
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
respectively, are called
Gorenstein projective
and
Gorenstein injective

derived category
of
R
-Mod, respectively.
We denote such relative derived categories by
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{GP}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
and
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{GI}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
respectively.
As noted in
[
26
]
, in contrast to the classic situation,
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{GP}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
and
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{GI}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
are not the same, in general.

3.
Realizing relative derived categories as homotopy categories.

As mentioned above, we denote by
\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{I})
) the subcategory of
Ch
(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
consisting of all
dg-projective (resp. dg-injective) complexes. Hence,
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{P}))
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{P}))
) means the homotopy subcategory
consisting of all dg-projective (resp. dg-injective) complexes.

It is well known that there are triangle equivalences

\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{P}))\simeq\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{I}))

for any ring
R
.
This realizes the usual derived categories as homotopy categories.
In this section, we will consider the result for relative derived categories.
Our aim is to obtain some torsion pairs.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
its triangulated subcategory. The pair
(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2})
is said to be a
torsion pair

if the following are satisfied

(TP1)

\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(T_{1},T_{2})=0
for all
T_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
and all
T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
.

(TP2)

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}
, there is a distinguished triangle

T_{1}\to T\to T_{2}\to\Sigma T_{1}

with
T_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
, where
\Sigma
denotes the autofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
.

Remark 3.2
.

According to
[
6
, Chapter I, Definitions 2.1 and 2.5, and Proposition 2.6]
, the notion in Definition
3.1
should be named as a “hereditary torsion pair”.
We simply call it a “torsion pair”.

As shown in the following lemma, torsion pairs are an important tool to obtain some triangle-equivalences.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2})
be a torsion pair of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
. Then the following
composite functors

F_{1}:\mathcal{T}_{1}\overset{\mathrm{inc}_{1}}{\to}\mathcal{T}\overset{Q_{2}}{\to}\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{T}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad F_{2}:\mathcal{T}_{2}\overset{\mathrm{inc}_{2}}{\to}\mathcal{T}\overset{Q_{1}}{\to}\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{T}_{1}

are triangle equivalent, where
\mathrm{inc}_{1}
and
\mathrm{inc}_{2}
mean the canonical inclusion functors respectively, and
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
denote the canonical quotient functors respectively.

Proof.

The result is well-known. For the left-hand triangle equivalence,
one can see the proof of (i)
\Rightarrow
(vi) in
[
6
, Chapter I, Proposition 2.6]
, and the right-hand one is a dual.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2})
be a torsion pair of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}

and
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be the equivalent functors in Lemma
3.3
. We write
F^{\prime}_{1}
and
F^{\prime}_{2}
as their quasi-inverses respectively.
According to the proof of (i)
\Rightarrow
(vi) and its dual in
[
6
, Chapter I, Proposition 2.6]
, we know that the rule of
F^{\prime}_{1}
and
F^{\prime}_{2}
for objects are as follows:

F^{\prime}_{1}(Y)=T_{2}^{Y},\forall Y\in\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{T}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad F^{\prime}_{2}(X)=T_{2}^{X},\forall X\in\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{T}_{1},

where
T_{2}^{Y}
and
T_{1}^{X}
come from the distinguished triangles

T^{Y}_{1}\to Y\to T_{2}^{Y}\to\Sigma T^{Y}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad T^{X}_{1}\to X\to T^{X}_{2}\to\Sigma T^{X}_{1}

which are guaranteed by (TP2).

In order to obtain our desired torsion pairs, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then every complex in
\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}}
is right
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
-acyclic.

Proof.

Let

{}_{R}X^{\bullet}=\cdots\to~_{R}X^{k-1}\overset{d^{k-1}}{\to}~_{R}X^{k}\overset{d^{k}}{\to}~_{R}X^{k+1}\to\cdots

be a complex in
\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}}
.
Then by the definition,
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
is exact and each cycle module
\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k}}
is in
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
. Now we decompose
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
into short exact sequences

{}_{R}X^{\bullet}_{k}=0\to\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k}}\to~_{R}X^{k}\overset{d^{k}}{\to}\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k+1}}\to 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(_{R}X^{\prime},\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k}})=0
for all
{}_{R}X^{\prime}\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}
and all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
as each
\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{K}}\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
.
This implies that each
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}_{k}
is Hom
{}_{R}(_{R}\mathcal{X},-)
-exact,
and then so is
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
. It follows that
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
is right
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
-acyclic.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then every complex in
\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})\cap~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}
is contractible.

Proof.

We let

{}_{R}X^{\bullet}=\cdots\to~_{R}X^{k-1}\overset{d^{k-1}}{\to}~_{R}X^{k}\overset{d^{k}}{\to}~_{R}X^{k+1}\to\cdots

be a complex in
\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})\cap~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let us consider the complex

0\to\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k}}\to~_{R}X^{k}\overset{d^{k}}{\to}\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k+1}}\to 0.

Note that any complex in
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-rac}
is exact as
{}_{R}\mathcal{P}\subseteq~_{R}\mathcal{X}
,
and hence, by
[
34
, Theorem 3.12]
one has

{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})\cap~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-rac}\subseteq\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}}\cap~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-rac}.

It follows that, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the complex
0\to\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k}}\to~_{R}X^{k}\overset{d^{k}}{\to}\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k+1}}\to 0
is a short exact sequence in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
with
\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k+1}}\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}
.
Thus, the identity morphism of
\mbox{\rm Ker}{d^{k+1}}
factors through
d^{k}
. That is,
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
is an exact complex with each
d^{k}
a split epic morphism, and so,
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
is contractible.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{K}(R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod})}(_{R}X^{\bullet},~_{R}Y^{\bullet})=0
for all
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})
and all
{}_{R}Y^{\bullet}\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}
.

Proof.

Let
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})
,
{}_{R}Y^{\bullet}\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}
and consider a morphism
f^{\bullet}:~_{R}X^{\bullet}\to~_{R}Y^{\bullet}
.
Since
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp})
is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, it,
applying
[
55
, Theorem 3.5]
, induces a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X}),\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}})
in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.
In particular, there is a short exact sequence in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

0\to~_{R}K^{\bullet}\to~_{R}G^{\bullet}\overset{\alpha^{\bullet}}{\to}~_{R}Y^{\bullet}\to 0

with
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})
and
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}\in\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}}
.
Note that
\alpha^{\bullet}:~_{R}G^{\bullet}\to_{R}Y^{\bullet}
is a special
\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})
-precover of
{}_{R}Y^{\bullet}
,
there is a morphism
\beta^{\bullet}:~_{R}X^{\bullet}\to~_{R}G^{\bullet}
such that
f^{\bullet}=\alpha^{\bullet}\beta^{\bullet}
.

By Lemma
3.5
one gets
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}
. It follows that
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is in
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}
since so is
{}_{R}Y^{\bullet}
, and hence,
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})\cap~_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-rac}
.
Thus, Lemma
3.6
yields that
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is is contractible.
Consequently, the morphism
\alpha^{\bullet}
is null-homotopic and so is
f^{\bullet}=\alpha^{\bullet}\beta^{\bullet}
. That is,
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{K}(R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod})}(_{R}X^{\bullet},~_{R}Y^{\bullet})=0
.
∎

The next three results are the dual of Lemmas
3.5
-
3.7
respectively.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then every complex in
\widetilde{{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}}}
is left
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
-acyclic.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then every complex in
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac}\cap\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y})
is contractible.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{K}(R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod})}(_{R}X^{\bullet},~_{R}Y^{\bullet})=0
for all
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\in~_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac}
and all
{}_{R}Y^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y})
.

Now we can give some torsion pairs of
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then there is a torsion pair
(\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})),\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac}))

in
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.

Proof.

We must check that the pair
(\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})),\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-rac}))
satisfies the conditions (TP1) and (TP2) in Definition
3.1
.
(TP1) holds by Lemma
3.7
. To see (TP2), let
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}\in

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.
As mentioned above,
[
55
, Theorem 3.5]
allows us to have a short exact sequence in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

0\to~_{R}K^{\bullet}\to~_{R}G^{\bullet}\overset{\alpha^{\bullet}}{\to}~_{R}M^{\bullet}\to 0

with
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})
and
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}\in\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}}
.
Now we consider the distinguished triangle in
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

{}_{R}G^{\bullet}\overset{\alpha^{\bullet}}{\to}~_{R}M^{\bullet}\to\mathrm{Cone}(\alpha^{\bullet})\to\Sigma_{R}G^{\bullet}

where
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is in
\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})
.
For any left
R
-module
{}_{R}X\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}
, viewed it as a complex, it is in
~\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})
, and hence,
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(_{R}X,~_{R}K^{\bullet})=\mathcal{H}om(_{R}X,~_{R}K^{\bullet})

is exact. It follows that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(_{R}X,\alpha^{\bullet})
is a quasi-isomorphism for all
{}_{R}X\in~_{R}\mathcal{X}
, and so
\alpha^{\bullet}
is a right
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
-quasi-isomorphism.
This implies that
\mathrm{Cone}(\alpha^{\bullet})\in\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})
and then (TP2) follows.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}
a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then there is a torsion pair
(\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac}),\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y})))

in
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.

Proof.

By an argument dual to Proposition
3.11
.
∎

Note that
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
and
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.
Now applying Lemma
3.3
to the torsion pairs from Propositions
3.11
and
3.12
, one can obtain the following result, which realizes the relative derived category
\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
(resp.
\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
) as a homotopy subcategory.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{Y}
subcategories of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.

(1)

If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp})
in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then there is a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X}))\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.

(2)

If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then there is a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}))\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{}_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{Y}
subcategories of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
If there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion triple
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=~^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, then there are triangle-equivalences

\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X}))\simeq\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y})).

Proof.

Note that
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
and
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.
Since
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=~^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
is a complete and hereditary cotorsion triple in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
, one has
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})=\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac})

by
[
13
, Propositions 2.6 and 2.2]
, and hence
\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
.
At the same time, the triangle-equivalences
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X}))\simeq\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
and
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}))\simeq\mathbf{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
holds by Theorem
3.13
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Let
R
be a ring such that
(_{R}\mathcal{X},~_{R}\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=~^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}},~_{R}\mathcal{Y})
is a complete and hereditary cotorsion triple in

R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
. Then Corollary
3.14
shows a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X}))\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}))
.
We denote by
H
and
H^{\prime}
the triangle-equivalent functor
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X}))\to\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}))
and its quasi-inverse.

According to Lemma
3.3
and Remark
3.4
,
H
and
H^{\prime}
can be be described as follows:

\begin{aligned} &H:\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X}))\overset{\mathrm{inc}_{\mathcal{X}}}{\to}\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})\overset{Q_{\mathcal{X}}}{\to}\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac})\overset{F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\rightarrow}\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}))\\
&H^{\prime}:\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}))\overset{\mathrm{inc}_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\to}\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})\overset{Q_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\to}\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{Y}\text{-}\mathrm{lac})=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})/\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{X}\text{-}\mathrm{rac})\overset{F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}}{\rightarrow}\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{X})).\end{aligned}

Hence, the rule of
H
and
H^{\prime}
for objects are as follows

\begin{aligned} &H:~_{R}A^{\bullet}\mapsto~_{R}B^{\bullet},\text{where}~_{R}B^{\bullet}~\text{is a dg-}{{}_{R}\mathcal{Y}}~\text{resolution of}~_{R}A^{\bullet};\\
&H^{\prime}:~_{R}N^{\bullet}\mapsto~_{R}M^{\bullet},\text{where}~_{R}M^{\bullet}~~\text{is a dg-}{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}}~\text{resolution of}~_{R}N^{\bullet}.\end{aligned}

Recall from
[
19
]
that a ring
R
is
left virtually Gorenstein
if
~{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}=~^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}
.
We denote by
{}_{R}\mathcal{PGF}
the subcategory of
R
-Mod consisting of all projectively coresolved Gorenstein flat modules.
Here, a left
R
-module
{}_{R}M
is said to be a
projectively coresolved Gorenstein flat

[
47
]

if there exists an
F
-totally acyclic complex
{}_{R}P^{\bullet}
of projective left
R
-modules
such that
{}_{R}M\cong Z^{0}(_{R}P^{\bullet})
.

Let
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
Following
[
47
]
,
the
definable closure
of
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
, denoted by
\overline{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}}
,
is the smallest subcategory which is definable and contains
{}_{R}\mathcal{X}
.
Here we say that
\overline{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}}
is
definable
if
\overline{{}_{R}\mathcal{X}}
is closed under pure submodules, direct limits and direct products.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
R
be a left virtually Gorenstein ring. Then we have the equality
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}=~_{R}\mathcal{PGF}
. In particular, there is a projective cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}}).

Proof.

Assume that
R
is a left virtually Gorenstein ring.
Note from
[
47
, Theorem 5.6 and its proof]
that, for any ring
R
, the subcategory
{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}
is closed under direct limits.
Therefore, as
R
is left virtually Gorenstein, the subcategory
{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}}
is closed under direct limits.
On the other hand, we know from
[
18
, Corollary 3.4(1)]
that the pair
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}})
forms a cotorsion pair (over any ring).
Thus,
[
46
, Theorem 6.1]
gives that the subcategory
{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}}
is definable. In particular, one gets
\overline{{}_{R}R}\subseteq~{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}}
.
So
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}=~_{R}\mathcal{PGF}
by
[
53
, Lemma 3.3]
, and hence the cotorsion pair

(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}})=(_{R}\mathcal{PGF},~{{}_{R}\mathcal{PGF}^{\perp}})

is complete and hereditary by
[
47
, Theorem 4.9]
. It is further projective by
[
29
, Theorem 5.4]
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

A classical fact in algebra is that projective modules are always flat. However, it is still unknown
whether or not “all Gorenstein projective left
R
-modules are Gorenstein flat” holds over an arbitrary ring
R
.
According to Proposition
3.16
, we know that all Gorenstein projective left
R
-modules are Gorenstein flat whenever
R
is left virtually Gorenstein, since
projectively coresolved Gorenstein flat modules are always Gorenstein flat.
Thus, to ensure that the result holds, the statements “Suppose that
R
is a left virtually Gorenstein ring satisfying
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}\subseteq~_{R}\mathcal{GF}
” and “Suppose that
R
is a virtually Gorenstein ring satisfying
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}\subseteq~_{R}\mathcal{GF}
and
\mathcal{GP}_{R}\subseteq~\mathcal{GF}_{R}
” from
[
19
, Theorems B and C]
can be restated as “Suppose that
R
is a left virtually Gorenstein ring ” and “Suppose that
R
is a virtually Gorenstein ring ”, respectively.

We end this section with some examples.

Example 3.18
.

(1) Let
R
be any ring. There is a complete and hereditary triple cotorsion given by
(_{R}\mathcal{P},R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod},~_{R}\mathcal{I})
in

So Corollary

holds true for any ring

.

(2)
Let

in

.
And hence, there is complete and hereditary cotorsion triple

in

, since, for any ring

,

(see
[

, Theorem 5.6]
).
It follows from Corollary

that there are triangle-equivalences

Here

(resp.

(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
consisting of all

&F^{\prime}:~_{R}N^{\bullet}\mapsto_{R}M^{\bullet},\text{where}~_{R}M^{\bullet}~\text{is a dg-Gorenstein projective resolution of}~_{R}N^{\bullet}.\end{aligned}

4.
Applying to rings with finite Gorenstein weak global dimension

According to
[
19
, Theorem A]
, we know that any ring with finite Gorenstein weak global dimension is both left and right virtually Gorenstein.
In this section, we will give some characterizations of a ring with finite Gorenstein weak global dimension and obtain some homotopic equivalences over such a ring.

In what follows, we denote by
\mathcal{F}^{\wedge}
the subcategory consisting of all left or right
R
-modules with finite flat dimension
and by
\mathcal{F}_{m}
the subcategory consisting of all left or right
R
-modules with finite flat dimension at most
m
for an integer
m\geq 0
;
by
\mbox{\rm sfli}(R)
the supremum of the flat dimensions of all injective left
R
-modules;
by
\mathrm{FFD}(R)
the left finitistic flat dimension of
R
, which is
defined as the supremum of the flat dimensions of those left
R
-modules that have finite flat dimension. The following result is immediate from
[
16
, Proposition 1.9]
, by taking
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=(_{R}\mathcal{F},_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\perp})
, and using
[
35
, Theorem 4.1.3]
. For the benefit of the reader, we opt for including a more independent argument below.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m
be a nonnegative integer. Then the following are equivalent for any
R
:

(1)

\max\{\mbox{\rm sfli}(R),\mathrm{FFD}(R)\}\leq m
.

(2)

The pair
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
is an injective cotorsion pair in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.

(3)

The pair
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp})
is an injective cotorsion pair in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3). Assume
\max\{\mbox{\rm sfli}(R),\mathrm{FFD}(R)\}\leq m
. Due to
[
35
, Theorem 4.1.3]
, the pair
(\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~{\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp})
is a complete hereditary cotorsion pair in
R
-Mod.
Thus, it is enough to prove
{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}\cap~{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp}=~_{R}\mathcal{I}
by
[
29
, Proposition 3.6]
.
Note that
{}_{R}\mathcal{I}\subseteq~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}\cap~{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp}

as
{}_{R}\mathcal{I}\subseteq~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}

by the assumption that
\mbox{\rm sfli}(R)\leq m
.
Conversely, let
{}_{R}M\in~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}\cap~{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}{{}^{\perp}}
.
Consider the short exact sequence

0\to~_{R}M\to~_{R}I\to~_{R}N\to 0

of
R
-modules
with
{}_{R}I
injective.
Since
{}_{R}I
and
{}_{R}M
have finite flat dimension, so has
{}_{R}N
.
The assumption
\mathrm{FFD}(R)\leq m
yields that
{}_{R}N
is in
{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
, and so one has
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(_{R}N,~_{R}M)=0
.
Thus the above exact sequence is split, and hence
{}_{R}M
is injective.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2). Suppose that
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp})
is an injective cotorsion pair in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
Then
{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp}\subseteq~_{R}\mathcal{GI}
by
[
29
, Theorem 5.2]
.
On the other hand,
[
47
, Theorem 5.6]
tells us that
(^{\perp}\mathcal{GI},\mathcal{GI})
is a complete cotorsion pair, it is injective by
[
29
, Theorem 5.2]
.
It follows from
[
33
, Corollary 3.3]
that
{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}\subseteq~{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp}
.
So
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
=
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}^{\perp})
is an injective cotorsion pair in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). Assume that
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
is an injective cotorsion pair in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
To see (1), first let
{}_{R}I
be any injective left
R
-module.
Using the completeness of the cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
,
one gets a short exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\to~_{R}G\to~_{R}F\to~_{R}I\to 0

with
{}_{R}F\in~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
and
{}_{R}G\in_{R}\mathcal{GI}
.
The above exact sequence is split as
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(_{R}I,~_{R}G)=0
. Hence
\mbox{\rm fd}(_{R}I)\leq\mbox{\rm fd}(_{R}F)\leq m
.
It follows that
\mbox{\rm sfli}(R)\leq m
.

Now, let
M
be an
R
-module with
\mbox{\rm fd}_{R}(M)<\infty
.
Then
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(_{R}M,~_{R}G)=0
for any
G\in~_{R}\mathcal{GI}
by
[
33
, Corollary 3.3]
as
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
is an injective cotorsion pair in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
.
This yields that
{}_{R}M\in~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
, and so one has
\mbox{\rm FFD}(R)\leq m
.
∎

Given a left
R
-module
{}_{R}M
(viewed it as a complex), according to
[
14
]
, its Gorenstein flat-cotorsion dimension, denoted by
\mbox{\rm Gfcd}(_{R}M)
, is defined as follows

\mbox{\rm Gfcd}(_{R}M)=\sup\left\{m\in\mathbb{Z}~\begin{array}[]{ |l}_{R}W^{\bullet}~\text{is a dg-flat-cotorsion resolution}~\text{of}~_{R}M~\text{with}~\mbox{\rm H}^{i}(_{R}W^{\bullet})=0\\
\text{for all}~i<m~\text{and with}~\mathrm{C}^{m}(_{R}W^{\bullet})~\text{Gorenstein flat-cotorsion}.\end{array}\right\}

Here a
dg-flat-cotorsion resolution
of a complex
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
is a dg-flat resolution of
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
with cotorsion components.
And a left
R
-module
{}_{R}N
is called
Gorenstein flat-cotorsion
if there is a
totally acyclic complex of flat-cotorsion

left
R
-modules
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
, that is,
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is a complex of flat-cotorsion left
R
-modules with
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(_{R}G^{\bullet},~_{R}H)
and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(_{R}H,~_{R}G^{\bullet})
acyclic
for any flat-cotorsion left
R
-module
{}_{R}H
, such that
{}_{R}N=\mbox{\rm Z}^{0}(_{R}G^{\bullet})
.
According to
[
14
, Theorem 5.7]
, we know that
\mbox{\rm Gfcd}(-)
is a refinement of
\mbox{\rm Gfd}(-)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
m
be a nonnegative integer. Then the following are equivalent for any
R
:

(1)

R
is a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)\leq m
.

(2)

R
is a left virtually Gorenstein ring with
\sup\{\mbox{\rm Gfcd}(_{R}M)~\mid~_{R}M\in R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}\}\leq m
.

(3)

There is a complete and hereditary cotorsion triple
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
.

(4)

There is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m})
.

(5)

There is an equality
{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}=~_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) follows from
[
19
, Theorem A]
and
[
16
, Fact 3.1]
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(5). Suppose that
R
is a left virtually Gorenstein ring with
\sup\{\mbox{\rm Gfcd}(_{R}M)~\mid~_{R}M\in R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}\}\leq m
.
By
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
, the assumption
\sup\{\mbox{\rm Gfcd}(_{R}M)~\mid~_{R}M\in R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}\}\leq m
is equivalent to
\max\{\mbox{\rm sfli}(R),\mathrm{FFD}(R)\}\leq m
.
Hence, there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}~,~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
in
R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}
by Lemma
4.1
.
Now, since
R
is left virtually Gorenstein, we get that

{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}=~{{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}}=~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3) holds by
[
54
, Lemmas 3.2 and 2.1]
and (3)
\Rightarrow
(4)
\Rightarrow
(5) are clear.

(5)
\Rightarrow
(1). Assume that
R
is a ring with an equality
{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}=~_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}
. Then according to
[
18
, Corollary 3.4(1)]
, the pair
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m})
forms a cotorsion pair. In particular, the class
~{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
is closed under direct products. It is certainly closed under direct limits and, by
[
23
, Lemma 9.1.4]
, it is also closed under pure submodules. Hence, the class
~{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
is definable. Then, as
~{}_{R}\mathcal{F}\subseteq~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
, we follow that

~\overline{{}_{R}R}=~\overline{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}}\subseteq~\overline{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}=~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}=~_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}.

Thus,
[
47
, Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 4.5]
(or
[
53
, Lemma 3.3]
) yields
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}=~_{R}\mathcal{PGF}
, and therefore
(_{R}\mathcal{PGF},~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m})
is a cotorsion pair.
It is complete (and hereditary) by
[
47
, Theorem 4.9]
.
Now, for any left
R
-module
{}_{R}M
, there is a short exact sequence of left
R
-modules

0\to~_{R}M\to~_{R}F\to~_{R}G\to 0

with
{}_{R}F\in~_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
and
{}_{R}G\in~_{R}\mathcal{PGF}
.
Therefore, one concludes from
[
54
, Lemma 2.2]
that
\mbox{\rm Gfd}(_{R}M)\leq m
, and so
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)\leq m
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

It is not known whether the invariants
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)
and
\sup\{\mbox{\rm Gfcd}(_{R}M)~\mid~_{R}M\in R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod}\}
always agree for an arbitrary ring
R
. By
[
16
, Section 5]
they coincide over a large class of rings. Theorem
4.2
adds another class of rings for which the two invariants are the same: left virtually Gorenstein rings.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
R
be a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. By Theorem
4.2
and
[
54
, Lemma 2.1]
(see also
[
54
, Lemma 3.2]
), there are complete and hereditary cotorsion pairs

(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\wedge})
and
(_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\wedge},~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
in
R
-Mod.
By using
[
29
, Theorems 5.2 and 5.4]
, such cotorsion pairs are further projective and injective respectively.
Now the induced cotorsion pairs
(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP}),\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\wedge}})
and
(\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\wedge}},~\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GI}))
in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

are projective and injective respectively by
[

Let
R
be any ring. As already mentioned, by
[

, Theorem 5.6]
, there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}},~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
in
R
-Mod.
So we have the notion of dg-Gorenstein injective complex (see Definition
2.7
). As usual, we denote by
{}_{R}\mathcal{C}ot

the subcategory of
R
-Mod consisting of all
cotorsion
left
R
-modules
{}_{R}M

(that is,
{}_{R}M\in~_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\perp}

(_{R}\mathcal{C}ot)
denotes the subcategory of

(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
consisting of all cotorsion modules in our sense. The next result shows that dg-Gorenstein injective complexes are particularly simple over rings of finite Gorenstein weak global dimension.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
be a a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. Then the following are equivalent for any
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
:

(1)

{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
is dg-Gorenstein injective.

(2)

{}_{R}M^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})
.

(3)

Each
{}_{R}M^{i}
is Gorenstein injective.

Proof.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) follows from
[
56
, Proposition 2.8]
and (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is clear.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). Suppose that
R
is a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)=m<\infty
.
Let
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})
. Then
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(_{R}\mathcal{C}ot)
by
[
48
]
.
Due to Remark
4.4
there exists a short exact sequence in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

0\to~_{R}M^{\bullet}\to~_{R}T^{\bullet}\to~_{R}N^{\bullet}\to 0

with
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GI})
and
{}_{R}N^{\bullet}\in\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}
.
Note that
{}_{R}N^{\bullet}
is in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(_{R}\mathcal{C}ot)
since
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
and
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}
are so. Now
{}_{R}N^{\bullet}\in\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}\cap\mbox{\rm Ch}(_{R}\mathcal{C}ot)
is contractible by
[
2
, Theorem 5.2(2)]
. Thus,
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
and
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}
are homotopically equivalent, and hence
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
is dg-Gorenstein injective.
∎

As mentioned in Remark
4.4
, there is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\wedge})
whenever
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. Now one has the notion of dg-Gorenstein projective complexes (see Definition
2.7
) in this case. As in the previous case, dg-Gorenstein projective complexes can be characterized as follows:

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
R
be a a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. Then the following are equivalent for any

:

(1)

{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
is dg-Gorenstein projective.

(2)

{}_{R}M^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})
.

(3)

Each
{}_{R}M^{i}
is Gorenstein projective.

Proof.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) follows from
[
56
, Theorem 2.2]
and (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is obvious.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). Assume that
R
is a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)=m<\infty
.
Let
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})
.
By Remark
4.4
there exists a short exact sequence in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

0\to~_{R}K^{\bullet}\to~_{R}T^{\bullet}\to~_{R}M^{\bullet}\to 0

with
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP})
and
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}\in\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}
.
Since both
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
and
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}
belong to
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})
, one gets
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})
, and hence
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\cap\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}}
.
From Theorem
4.2
we have that
{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}=~_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}
. This implies that each

. Therefore,
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}
is an acyclic complex in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
with cycle modules in
{}_{R}\mathcal{F}_{m}
.
Then, it is easy to check that
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}
is an acyclic complex in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
with cycle modules in
{}_{R}\mathcal{F}
. Now
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}
is contractible by a result
[

, Theorem 2.5]
due to Benson and Goodearl (see also
[

, Corollary 3.6(2)]
). Thus,
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
and
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}
are homotopically equivalent, and so
{}_{R}M^{\bullet}
is dg-Gorenstein projective.∎

Now, we are in a position to state and prove our main result. For a ring
R
, recall that
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm Proj}\text{-}R)
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm Inj}\text{-}R)
,
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm GProj}\text{-}R)
and
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm GInj}\text{-}R)
) denotes
the homotopy category of all projective (resp. injective, Gorenstein projective and Gorenstein injective) right

-modules,
and that their homotopy subcategory consisting of all exact complexes will be denoted by
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mbox{\rm Proj}\text{-}R)
,
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mbox{\rm Inj}\text{-}R)
,
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mbox{\rm GProj}\text{-}R)
and
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mbox{\rm GInj}\text{-}R)

respectively.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
R
be a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
.
Then there is a triangle-equivalence

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})

\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})

which restricts to a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj}).

Proof.

Suppose that
R
is a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. Then it is a left (and right) virtually Gorenstein ring with a complete and hereditary triple
(_{R}\mathcal{GP},~_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\wedge},~_{R}\mathcal{GI})
by Theorem
4.2
.
So Example
3.18
(2) tells us the following triangle-equivalences

\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP}))\simeq\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{GP}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})\simeq\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{GI}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GI})).

Using Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
, there are equalities

\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP}))=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GI}))=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj}).

Thus, we have a triangle-equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})
.
Furthermore, if we denote by
F:\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})\to\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})=

\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP}))\to\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GI}))
and
F^{\prime}:\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GInj})\to\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm GProj})=

\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GI}))\to\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP}))
the associate equivalent functor respectively,
then the rules of
F
and
F^{\prime}
on objects are defined as follows (see Example
3.18
(2)):

\begin{aligned} &F:~_{R}X^{\bullet}\mapsto~_{R}Y^{\bullet},\text{where}~_{R}Y^{\bullet}~\text{is a dg-Gorenstein injective resolution of}~_{R}X^{\bullet};\\
&F^{\prime}:~_{R}N^{\bullet}\mapsto~_{R}M^{\bullet},\text{where}~_{R}M^{\bullet}~\text{is a dg-Gorenstein projective resolution of}~_{R}N^{\bullet}.\end{aligned}

To see the restricted triangle-equivalence, it suffices to show the essential image of
F
(resp.
F^{\prime}
) is contained in
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
).
For this, let
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
(resp.
{}_{R}N^{\bullet}
) be any complex in
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
).
Then by Remark
4.4
, there are short exact sequences in
\mbox{\rm Ch}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})

0\to~_{R}X^{\bullet}\overset{\alpha^{\bullet}}{\to}~_{R}G^{\bullet}\to~_{R}C^{\bullet}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad 0\to~_{R}K^{\bullet}\to~_{R}H^{\bullet}\overset{\beta^{\bullet}}{\to}~_{R}N^{\bullet}\to 0

with
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GI})
,
{}_{R}H^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm dg}(_{R}\mathcal{GP})
and
{}_{R}C^{\bullet},~_{R}K^{\bullet}\in~\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{F}^{\wedge}}
.
Note that
\alpha^{\bullet}
and
\beta^{\bullet}
are quasi-isomorphisms as
{}_{R}C^{\bullet}
and
{}_{R}K^{\bullet}
are exact complexes, and hence,
{}_{R}G^{\bullet}
is a dg-Gorenstein injective resolution of
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}

and
~{}_{R}H^{\bullet}
is a dg-Gorenstein projective resolution of
{}_{R}N^{\bullet}
.
Since each
{}_{R}C^{k}
is in
{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}
and each
{}_{R}X^{k}
is projective (of course in
{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}{\mathcal{GI}}}
),
and that each
{}_{R}K^{k}
is in
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}
and each
{}_{R}N^{k}
is injective (of course in
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}
),
we conclude that
{}_{R}G^{k}\in~_{R}\mathcal{GI}\cap~{{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}}=~_{R}\mathcal{I}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}

and that
{}_{R}H^{k}\in{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}}\cap~{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}^{\perp}}=~_{R}\mathcal{P}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore,
F(_{R}X^{\bullet})=~_{R}G^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
and
F^{\prime}(_{R}N^{\bullet})=~_{R}H^{\bullet}\in\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
, as desired.
∎

5.
Homotopy equivalence on totally acyclic complexes

Let
R
be a ring with finite Gorenstein weak global dimension.
This section is devoted to give a link between
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})

and
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
, the homotopy categories of totally acyclic complexes of
projective left
R
-modules and totally acyclic complexes of injective left
R
-modules.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
R
be a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. Then there are equalities

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj}).

Proof.

Using
[
54
, Theorems 2.3 and 2.6]
, we get that any exact complex of projective (injective) left
R
-modules is totally acyclic.
In other words, the containments

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\supseteq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})\supseteq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})

hold true. On the other hand, the converse containments are trivial.
∎

Now we can give a link between
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})

and
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
R
be a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. Then there are triangle equivalences

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj}).

Proof.

The equalities follows from Lemma
5.1
and the triangle equivalence from Remark
4.8
.
∎

It is well known that over an arbitrary ring
R
the subcategory
{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}
(resp.,
{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}
) is a Frobenius category with projective-injective objects projective (resp. injective) left
R
-modules.
Hence, the stable categories
\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}}
and
\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}
are triangulated categories.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
R
be a ring with
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
. Then there is a triangle equivalence

\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}}\simeq\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}.

Proof.

According to
[
40
, Proposition 7.2 and its dual]
, we know that there are triangle equivalences

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})\simeq\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}.

Now Proposition
5.2
applies.
∎

We end this section with the next corollary.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
R
be a left Gorenstein ring or a Gorenstein
n
-coherent ring for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then there are triangle equivalences

\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GP}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})\simeq\underline{{}_{R}\mathcal{GI}}.

and

\underline{\mathcal{GP}_{R}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(\mbox{\rm Proj}\text{-}R)=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mbox{\rm Proj}\text{-}R)\simeq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mbox{\rm Inj}\text{-}R)=\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{tac}}(\mbox{\rm Inj}\text{-}R)\simeq\underline{\mathcal{GI}_{R}}.

Proof.

By the proofs of Corollaries
4.9
and
4.11
, we know that
\mbox{\rm G-wgldim}(R)<\infty
.
Now the triangle equivalences hold by Proposition
5.2
and
[
40
, Proposition 7.2 and its dual]
.
∎

Lemma 6.1

(R\text{-Mod})

(R\text{-Mod})

{}_{R}Y^{\bullet}

Now we prove that

Proposition 6.5

Conversely, let

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(_{R}M,~\mbox{\rm Z}^{k}(_{R}T^{\bullet}))\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathrm{Ch}(R\text{-Mod})}^{\hskip 8.19447pt1}(\mathrm{S}^{k}(_{R}M),~_{R}T^{\bullet})=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{K}(R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod})}(\mathrm{S}^{k}(_{R}M),~_{R}T^{\bullet})=0

for all
{}_{R}M\in~_{R}\mathcal{S}
and all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
\mbox{\rm Z}^{k}(_{R}T^{\bullet})\in~_{R}\mathcal{A}
. Now
\mbox{\rm Z}^{k}(_{R}T^{\bullet})\in~^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{A}}\cap{{}_{R}\mathcal{A}}
since
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}\in~\widetilde{{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{A}}}
.
Thus,
{}_{R}T^{\bullet}
is a contractible complex, which is a zero object in
\mbox{\rm K}(\widetilde{{}^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{A}}}\cap\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{A}})
, of course is a zero object in
\mbox{\rm K}(\mbox{\rm dg}(^{\perp}{{}_{R}\mathcal{A}})\cap\mbox{\rm Ch}(_{R}\mathcal{A}))
, and hence is a zero object in
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{A}\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
by Lemma
6.3
.
Notice further that the above short exact sequence
({\ddagger})
is degree-wise split as each component module of
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
is injective. So, we have an isomorphism
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\cong~_{R}A^{\bullet}
in
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{A}\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
.
Consequently, any morphism
f^{\bullet}:~_{R}X^{\bullet}\to~_{R}X^{\bullet}
is null-homotopic since
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}\cong~_{R}A^{\bullet}\in~\widetilde{{}_{R}\mathcal{A}}
. It follows that
{}_{R}X^{\bullet}
is a contractible complex, which is a zero object in
\mathrm{K}(_{R}\mathcal{A}\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
.
Therefore,
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{A}\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
is generated by the set
{}_{R}\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}
of compact objects.
∎

7.

Let
S
be a
left coherent ring and
R
be a right coherent ring.
A complex
{}_{R}D_{S}^{{}^{\bullet}}
of
R

S

is said to be a
dualizing complex

[
45
]
for the rings
R
and
S
if the following
conditions are satisfied:

(DC1)

all component modules of
{}_{R}D_{S}^{{}^{\bullet}}
are FP-injective left
R
-modules and FP-injective right

S
-modules;

(DC2)

all homology modules of
{}_{R}D_{S}^{{}^{\bullet}}
are finitely presented left
R
-modules and finitely presented right
S
-modules;

(DC3)

the homothety maps
R\to\mathcal{H}om_{S^{op}}(_{R}D_{S}^{\bullet},~_{R}D_{S}^{\bullet})

S\to\mathcal{H}om_{R}(_{R}D_{S}^{\bullet},~_{R}D_{S}^{\bullet})

quasi-isomorphisms of DG-rings.

This notion coincides with the notion of “dualizing complex” in
[
17
, Definition 1.1]
when
R
and
S
are left and right noetherian, respectively.
In addition, we say that a left coherent ring
R

has a dualizing complex

{}_{R}D_{R}^{\bullet}
is a dualizing complex for the rings
R
and

Grothendieck duality is a classical subject which can go back 1958s. Roughly speaking, it is a statement concerning the existence of a right adjoint to
the “direct image with compact support” functor between derived categories of sheaves or modules.
For a ring
R
, over the years many people investigated Grothendieck duality for derived categories of
R
-modules by providing the following insights:

(
GD1
)

R
has a duality complex.

(
GD2
)

There is a triangle-equivalence
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{mod})^{op}\simeq\mathbf{D}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{mod}),

where the right category is the bounded derived category of finitely presented
R
-modules and the left one is the opposite category of the bounded derived category of finitely presented right
R
-modules;

(
GD3
)

There is a triangulated equivalence
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
.

According to
[
42
, Introduction]
, we know that, for a left noetherian and right coherent ring such that all flat left
R
-modules have finite projective dimension, there are implications

(\textbf{GD1})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD3})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD2}).

As shown in the next remark, the condition on the ring such that the above implications
hold can be relaxed.

Remark 7.1

be a two-sided coherent ring

It is well-known that
\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})
is compactly generated.
In addition,
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
) is compactly generated since
R
is left and right coherent (see
[
42
, Proposition 7.14]
and
[
48
, Corollary 6.13]
).
Now, by an argument used in the proof of
[
40
, Proposition 5.3]
, one has
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
and
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
) are compactly generated
with the following triangle-equivalences

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})^{\text{c}}/\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})^{\text{c}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})^{\text{c}}/\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})^{\text{c}}.

On one hand, using the equality
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{A}c\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
and
[
51
, Theorem 8(2)]
,
one gets that

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mathbf{D}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{mod}).

Hence,

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})^{\text{c}}/\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})^{\text{c}}=\mathbf{D}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{mod})/\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{proj})

as
\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{proj})
.
On the other hand, using the compact generation of
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
and
[
51
, Theorem 8(2)]
, one obtains that

\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mathbf{D}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{mod})^{op}.

Whence,

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})^{\text{c}}/\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})^{\text{c}}=\mathbf{D}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{mod})^{op}/\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{proj})^{op}

as Lemma
7.5
tells us that
\mathbf{D}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Mod})^{\text{c}}\simeq\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{proj})^{op}
.
Consequently, the triangle-equivalence in (2) holds by the assumption
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})\simeq\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
which can restrict to compact objects.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

By the proof of Theorem
7.7
, one can see that
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mbox{\rm ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
) is compactly generated whenever
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
(resp.
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
) is so.

The Verdier quotient triangulated category
\mathbf{D}_{sg}(R):=\mathbf{D}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{mod})/\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{proj})
was primarily introduced and studied by
Buchweitz in
[
12
]
under the name “stable derived category” over noetherian rings. It was renamed by Orlov in
[
44
]

and is known then as
singularity category
in the literature.
We end the paper with the next corollary, which can be viewed as a singularity category version of Corollary
7.3
and Remark
7.4
.

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
R
be a left and right coherent ring. Consider the following conditions

(
GD1
′
)

R
is Ding-Chen, or
R
is left Gorenstein (that is
\mbox{\rm G-gldim}(R)<\infty
);

(
GD2
′
)

There is a triangle-equivalence
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{mod})/\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R^{op}\text{-}\mathrm{proj})^{op}\simeq\mathbf{D}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{mod})/\mbox{\rm K}^{b}(R\text{-}\mathrm{proj}),

where the left category is the opposite category of the singularity category of right
R
-modules;

(
GD3
′
)

\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
and
\mbox{\rm K}_{\mathrm{ac}}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
are triangle equivalent.

Then one has implications

(\textbf{GD1${}^{\prime}$})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD3${}^{\prime}$})\Longrightarrow(\textbf{GD2${}^{\prime}$}).

Proof.

Suppose that
R
is left and right coherent. Then
\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Proj})
is compactly generated and there is an equality
\mbox{\rm K}(_{R}\mathcal{A}c\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})=\mbox{\rm K}(R\text{-}\mbox{\rm Inj})
(see
[
42
, Proposition 7.14]
and
[
33
, Remark in p.109]
). In addition, we know from
[
30
, Theorem 3.21]
that the two subcategories
R^{op}\text{-mod}
and
R^{op}\text{-tmod}
(resp.
R\text{-mod}
and
R\text{-tmod}
)
coincide. Now the implications hold by Theorem
7.7
.
∎

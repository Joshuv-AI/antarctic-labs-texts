Remark 1.6

Remark 1.7

In

l considered the existence of clustered solutions for the mass critical two dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation

2.
Approximate solutions

The purpose of this section is to give
C^{1}
estimates of Green’s function
G_{K}
and construct approximate solutions to (
1.13

The expansion of Green’s function
G_{K}
of the operator
-\text{div}(K(x)\nabla\cdot)
with 0-Dirichlet condition plays an essential role in our analysis.
Let
G_{K}(x,y)
be the Green’s function of
-\text{div}(K(x)\nabla\cdot)
with 0-Dirichlet condition in
\Omega
, that is, solutions of the following linear elliptic problem:

\begin{cases}-\text{div}(K(x)\nabla u)=f,\ \ &\Omega,\\
u=0,\ \ &\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(2.1)

can be expressed by
u(x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{K}(x,y)f(y)dy
for
x\in\Omega.

In
[
9
]
we have obtained
C^{0}
-asymptotic expansion of
G_{K}(x,y)
. We need to use
C^{1}
-asymptotic expansion of
G_{K}(x,y)
in the present paper.

Lemma 2.1

(lemma 3.1,
[
9
]
)
.

For
y\in\Omega
, let

be the unique positive-definite matrix satisfying

(T_{y})^{-1}(T_{y})^{-t}=K(y).

Then there exists a function
\bar{S}_{K}\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\Omega\times\Omega)
for any

, such that

G_{K}(x,y)=\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)+\bar{S}_{K}(x,y),\ \ \forall\ x,y\in\Omega.

Now for
i,j=1,2
, we denote
T_{ij}=(T_{y})_{ij}=(T(y))_{ij}
the component of row
i
, column
j
of the matrix
T_{y}
. The following lemma gives the
C^{1}
-asymptotic expansion of

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Then there holds

\bar{S}_{K}(x,y)=-F_{1,y}(x)-F_{2,y}(x)+\bar{H}_{1}(x,y)\ \ \ \ \forall\ x,y\in\Omega,

It follows from Lemma

Proof.

Let
y\in\Omega
be fixed. In the following, we always denote
T_{ij}=(T_{y})_{ij}
the component of row
i
, column
j
of the matrix
T_{y}
for
i,j=1,2
. From Lemma
2.1
, the regular part
\bar{S}_{K}(x,y)
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\text{div}\left(K(x)\nabla\bar{S}_{K}(x,y)\right)=\text{div}\left(\left(K(x)-K(y)\right)\nabla\left(\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right)\right)\ &\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\bar{S}_{K}(x,y)=-\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\ &\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2.5)

This implies that

\begin{split}-\text{div}\left(K(x)\nabla\bar{S}_{K}(x,y)\right)=&\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}K_{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}\left(\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right)\\
&+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left(\left(K_{ij}(x)-K_{ij}(y)\right)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}\left(\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right)\right)\\
=&:A_{1}+A_{2}.\end{split}

(2.6)

As for
A_{1}
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we denote
J(x)=-\frac{1}{8\pi}|x|^{2}\ln|x|
. Then
\Delta\left(J(x-y)\right)=\Gamma(x-y)-\frac{1}{2\pi}.

Using transformation of coordinates, one computes directly that

\text{div}\left(K(y)\nabla J(T_{y}(x-y))\right)=\Gamma(T_{y}(x-y))-\frac{1}{2\pi},

from which we deduce,

\text{div}\left(K(y)\nabla\partial_{x_{j}}\left(J(T_{y}(x-y))\right)\right)=\partial_{x_{j}}\left(\Gamma(T_{y}(x-y))\right).

We define for
x\in\Omega

\begin{split}F_{1,y}(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}K_{ij}(y)\cdot\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\partial_{x_{j}}\left(J\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right),\end{split}

(2.7)

then one has

\text{div}\left(K(y)\nabla F_{1,y}(x)\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}K_{ij}(y)\partial_{x_{j}}\left(\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right).

(2.8)

As for
A_{2}
, using Taylor’s expansion we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left(K_{ij}(x)-K_{ij}(y)\right)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\left(\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right)\\
=\sum_{\alpha,i,j=1}^{2}\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}&\partial_{x_{\alpha}}K_{ij}(y)(x-y)_{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}\left(\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right)+\phi_{y}(x),\end{split}

where
\phi_{y}(\cdot)\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all

Since

\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}\Gamma(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{2x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{4}}\right),

where
\delta_{i,j}=1
for
i=j
and
\delta_{i,j}=0
for
i\neq j
, we have

\begin{split}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}\left(\Gamma(T_{y}(x-y))\right)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m,n=1}^{2}T_{mj}T_{ni}\left(\frac{\delta_{m,n}}{|T_{y}(x-y)|^{2}}-\frac{2\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)_{m}\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)_{n}}{|T_{y}(x-y)|^{4}}\right).\end{split}

(2.10)

Taking (
2.10
) into (
2.9
), we get

\begin{split}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left(K_{ij}(x)-K_{ij}(y)\right)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\left(\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}\Gamma\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)\right)\\

-\frac{1}{2\pi}T_{mj}T_{ni}&\left(\frac{\delta_{m,n}}{|T_{y}(x-y)|^{2}}-\frac{2\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)_{m}\left(T_{y}(x-y)\right)_{n}}{|T_{y}(x-y)|^{4}}\right)+\phi_{y}(x).\end{split}

(2.11)

Note that

\frac{x^{p}}{|x|^{4}}=-\frac{1}{8}\Delta\left(\frac{x^{p}}{|x|^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{8}\frac{\Delta x^{p}}{|x|^{2}}\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ |p|=3,

(2.12)

where

is the multi-index and

. From (

), it is not hard to check that for

\begin{cases}\frac{x_{m}}{|x|^{2}}&=\Delta\left(\frac{1}{2}x_{m}\ln|x|\right),\\

\begin{cases}\frac{\left(T_{y}\left(x-y\right)\right)_{m}}{|T_{y}\left(x-y\right)|^{2}}&=\text{div}\left(K(y)\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{y}\left(x-y\right)\right)_{m}\ln|T_{y}\left(x-y\right)|\right)\right),\\

∎

(2.17)

3.

Let us consider the following equation

-\Delta w=w^{p}_{+},\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

(3.1)

The unique

\phi^{\prime}(1)\ln|x|,\ \ &|x|>1.\end{cases}

By the classical theory for elliptic equations,

for any

-\Delta v-pw^{p-1}_{+}v=0,\ \ v\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(3.2)

Clearly,
\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{h}}

(h=1,2)
are solutions of (
3.2

[
12

(see also
[
6

Proposition 3.1

(Non-degeneracy)
.

w

span\left\{\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{2}}\right\}.

Let
\eta
be a smooth truncation function satisfying

supp(\eta)\subseteq B_{1}(0),\ \ 0\leq\eta\leq 1\ \text{in}\ B_{1}(0),\ \ \eta\equiv 1\ \text{in}\ B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0).

Define
\eta_{i}(x)=\eta\left((x-z_{i})|\ln\varepsilon|^{M+1}\right)

Denote

F_{\delta,Z}=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\mid\int_{\Omega}u\left(\eta_{j}\frac{\partial V_{\delta,Z,j}}{\partial x_{h}}\right)=0,\ \ \forall j=1,\cdots,m,\ h=1,2\right\},

(3.3)

(3.4)

So
F_{\delta,Z}
and

are co-dimensional
2m

For any

Q_{\delta}:L^{p}\to F_{\delta,Z}

Q_{\delta}u:=u-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{2}b_{j,h}\left(-\delta^{2}\text{div}\left(K(z_{j})\nabla\frac{\partial V_{\delta,z_{j},\hat{q}_{\delta,j}}}{\partial x_{h}}\right)\right),

(3.5)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{2}b_{j,h}\int_{\Omega}\left(-\delta^{2}\text{div}\left(K(z_{j})\nabla\frac{\partial V_{\delta,z_{j},\hat{q}_{\delta,j}}}{\partial x_{h}}\right)\right)\left(\eta_{i}\frac{\partial V_{\delta,Z,i}}{\partial x_{\hbar}}\right)=\int_{\Omega}u\left(\eta_{i}\frac{\partial V_{\delta,Z,i}}{\partial x_{\hbar}}\right)

(3.6)

for

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&\left(-\delta^{2}\text{div}\left(K(z_{j})\nabla\frac{\partial V_{\delta,z_{j},\hat{q}_{\delta,j}}}{\partial x_{h}}\right)\right)\left(\eta_{i}\frac{\partial V_{\delta,Z,i}}{\partial x_{\hbar}}\right)\\

(3.7)

where
\delta_{i,j}=1
if

and
\delta_{i,j}=0

M_{i}
are

.
Note that for

in

The linearized operator of (

The following lemma gives estimates of the linear operator

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist

and

\begin{split}||R_{\delta}(\omega)||_{L^{p}}=||(V_{\delta,Z}+\omega-q)^{p}_{+}-(V_{\delta,Z}-q)^{p}_{+}-p(V_{\delta,Z}-q)^{p-1}_{+}\omega||_{L^{p}}\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{p-2}}||\omega||_{L^{\infty}}^{2}.\end{split}

Hence we get

\begin{split}||T_{\delta}(\omega)||_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{p}}|\ln\varepsilon|^{p-1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}+\gamma}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{p}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{p-2}}||\omega||_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2-\theta_{0}}}.\end{split}

(4.2)

\mathcal{N}
to
\mathcal{N}

\leq&C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{p}}|\ln\varepsilon|^{p-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\frac{||\omega_{1}||_{L^{\infty}}+||\omega_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{p-2}}\right)||\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2}||\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}
.
Note that by Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
,

\begin{split}K_{\delta}(Z)=&\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\pi\delta^{2}}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(z_{j})-\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}\frac{2\pi^{2}\delta^{2}q(z_{i})q(z_{j})\sqrt{\det K(z_{i})}\sqrt{\det K(z_{j})}}{\left(\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}}G_{K}(z_{i},z_{j})\\
&+O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).\end{split}

(6.2)

We have

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
\delta
sufficiently small, the following maximization problem

\max\limits_{Z\in\overline{\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}}}K_{\delta}(Z)

has a solution
Z_{\delta}\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}.

Proof.

Clearly
K_{\delta}
has a maximizer in
\overline{\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}}
.
Let
Z_{\delta}=(z_{1,\delta},\cdots,z_{m,\delta})\in\overline{\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}}
be a maximizer. It suffices to prove that
Z_{\delta}\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}.
We choose a test function

z_{j}^{0}=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\ln\varepsilon|}}\hat{z}_{j}^{0},

where
\hat{z}_{j}^{0}=\left(\cos\frac{(j-1)\pi}{m},\sin\frac{(j-1)\pi}{m}\right)
,
j=1,\cdots,m
, form a
m
-regular polygon with radius 1 in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then it is easy to see that
(z_{1}^{0},\cdots,z_{m}^{0})\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}
since
|z_{j}^{0}-z_{i}^{0}|\geq C|\ln\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq|\ln\varepsilon|^{-M}
. Using Lemma
2.1
, (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), one computes directly that

\begin{split}\max\limits_{Z\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}}K_{\delta}(Z)\geq&K_{\delta}((z_{1}^{0},\cdots,z_{m}^{0}))\\
\geq&\frac{m\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})}{|\ln\varepsilon|}-\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}\frac{\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(z_{j}^{0})}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\ln\frac{1}{|z_{i}^{0}-z_{j}^{0}|}+O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)\\
\geq&\frac{m\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})}{|\ln\varepsilon|}-\frac{m(m-1)\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})}{2|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\ln|\ln\varepsilon|+O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).\end{split}

(6.3)

We assume that
(z_{1,\delta},\cdots,z_{m,\delta})\in\partial\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}
. There are two possibilities: either there exists a
j_{0}
such that
z_{j_{0},\delta}\in\partial B_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0}),
in which case,
q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(z_{j_{0},\delta})\leq q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})-\sigma_{0}
for some
\sigma_{0}>0
; or there exists
i_{0}\neq j_{0}
such that
|z_{i_{0},\delta}-z_{j_{0},\delta}|=|\ln\varepsilon|^{-M}.

In the first case, we have

\begin{split}\max\limits_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}}K_{\delta}\leq&\frac{\pi\delta^{2}\left(mq^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})-\sigma_{0}\right)}{|\ln\varepsilon|}+O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}\ln|\ln\varepsilon|}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\right),\end{split}

(6.4)

which contradicts (
6.3
) for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. This also shows that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(z_{j_{0},\delta})=q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})
. By assumptions, we have
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}z_{j_{0},\delta}=x_{0}.

In the second case, by (
6.2
) we have

\begin{split}\max\limits_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}}K_{\delta}\leq&\frac{m\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})}{|\ln\varepsilon|}-\frac{\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(z_{j_{0},\delta})}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\ln\frac{1}{|z_{j_{0},\delta}-z_{j_{0},\delta}|}+O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)\\
\leq&\frac{m\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})}{|\ln\varepsilon|}-\frac{M\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(z_{j_{0},\delta})}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\ln|\ln\varepsilon|+O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).\end{split}

(6.5)

Combining (
6.3
) with (
6.5
), we get

\frac{M\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(z_{j_{0},\delta})}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\ln|\ln\varepsilon|\leq\frac{m(m-1)\pi\delta^{2}q^{2}\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})}{2|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\ln|\ln\varepsilon|.

This clearly contradicts with the choice of
M=m^{2}+1
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Thus we get
Z_{\delta}\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

From Lemma
6.1
, we know that for
\delta>0
sufficiently small, there exists
Z_{\delta}=(z_{1,\delta},\cdots,z_{m,\delta})
being a critical point of
K_{\delta}(Z)
in
\Lambda_{\varepsilon,m}
and as
\delta\to 0
,

(z_{1,\delta},\cdots,z_{m,\delta})\to(x_{0},\cdots,x_{0}).

Lemma
5.1
then guarantees that
v_{\delta}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}V_{\delta,Z,j}+\omega_{\delta,Z}
is a clustered solution to (
1.13
).

Let
u_{\varepsilon}=|\ln\varepsilon|v_{\delta}
and
\delta=\varepsilon|\ln\varepsilon|^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}
, then
u_{\varepsilon}
is a solution to (
1.12
). Define
A_{\varepsilon,i}=\{u_{\varepsilon}>q\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\}\cap B_{|\ln\varepsilon|^{-M-1}}(z_{i,\delta})
. From Lemma
2.5
, there exist
R_{1},R_{2}>0
such that

B_{R_{1}\varepsilon}(z_{i,\delta})\subseteq A_{\varepsilon,i}\subseteq B_{R_{2}\varepsilon}(z_{i,\delta}).

It remains to calculate the limiting value of
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-q\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p}_{+}dx.
We have

Lemma 6.2
.

There holds for
i=1,\cdots,m

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{B_{|\ln\varepsilon|^{-M-1}}(z_{i,\delta})}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-q\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p}_{+}dx=2\pi q\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0}).

As a consequence,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-q\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p}_{+}dx=2\pi mq\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0}).

Proof.

It follows from (
2.27
), (
2.29
) and Proposition
4.1
that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}&\int_{B_{|\ln\varepsilon|^{-M-1}}(z_{i,\delta})}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-q\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p}_{+}dx=\frac{|\ln\varepsilon|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{B_{|\ln\varepsilon|^{-M-1}}(z_{i,\delta})}(w_{\delta}-q)^{p}_{+}dx\\
&=\frac{|\ln\varepsilon|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{B_{Ls_{\delta,i}}(z_{i,\delta})}\left(V_{\delta,z_{i,\delta},\hat{q}_{\delta,i}}(x)-\hat{q}_{\delta,i}+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)\right)^{p}_{+}dx\\
&=\frac{|\ln\varepsilon|}{\delta^{2}}\delta^{2}|\phi^{\prime}(1)|^{p-1}\left(\frac{\ln\frac{1}{s_{\delta,i}}}{\hat{q}_{\delta,i}}\right)^{p-1}|\phi^{\prime}(1)|^{-p}\left(\frac{\ln\frac{1}{s_{\delta,i}}}{\hat{q}_{\delta,i}}\right)^{-p}\sqrt{\det K(z_{i,\delta})}\cdot 2\pi|\phi^{\prime}(1)|+o(1)\\
&\to 2\pi q\sqrt{\det K}(x_{0})\ \ \ \ \text{as}\ \delta\to 0.\end{split}

∎

The rest of properties of
u_{\varepsilon}
can be easily deduced from the decomposition of

in (

) and we finish the proof of Theorem

.

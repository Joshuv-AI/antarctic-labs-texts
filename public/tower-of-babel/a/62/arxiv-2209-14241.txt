Remark 1

Similar to ’ratio’, we can define it, the cross-ratio, also as

c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=[(B-D)(A-D)^{-1}][(A-C)(B-C)^{-1}],

(or all combination of product of this 4-factors)
the results would be similar,
but the obtained point will always be different for each case

[(B-D)(A-D)^{-1}][(A-C)(B-C)^{-1}]\neq\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right]\left[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)\right].

and

[(B-D)(A-C)][(A-D)^{-1}(B-C)^{-1}]\neq\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right]\left[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)\right].

also for the other cases, we would have a difference for each pair, found for the cross ratio, according to any definition we take.
We are keeping our definition.

A,B,C,D
are distinct points in a
\ell^{OI}-
line, in Desargues affine plane, then

c_{r}(-A,-B;-C,-D)=c_{r}(A,B;D,C)

Proof.

From cross-ratio definition
4
, we have

\displaystyle c_{r}(-A,-B;-C,-D)

\displaystyle=[(-A-(-C))^{-1}(-B-(-C))][(-B-(-D))^{-1}(-A-(-D))]

\displaystyle=[(-A+C)^{-1}(-B+C)][(-B+D)^{-1}(-A+D)]

\displaystyle=[([-I](A-C))^{-1}[-I](B-C)][([-I](B-D))^{-1}[-I](A-D)]

\displaystyle=[(A-C)^{-1}[-I]^{-1}[-I](B-C)][(B-D)^{-1}[-I]^{-1}[-I](A-D)]

\displaystyle=[(A-C)^{-1}[-I][-I](B-C)][(B-D)^{-1}[-I][-I](A-D)]

\displaystyle=[(A-C)^{-1}(B-C)][(B-D)^{-1}(A-D)]

\displaystyle=c_{r}(A,B;D,C)

From skew fields properties we have that
(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}
and
ab\neq ba
,
[-I]^{-1}=-I
, and
[-I][-I]=I.

∎

Theorem 3
.

If
A,B,C,D
are distinct points in a line, in Desargues affine plane, then

c_{r}^{-1}(A,B;C,D)=c_{r}(A,B;D,C)

Proof.

From cross-ratio Definition
4
, have

\displaystyle c_{r}^{-1}(A,B;C,D)

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right]\left[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)\right]\right\}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)\right]^{-1}\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=[(A-C)^{-1}(B-C)][(B-D)^{-1}(A-D)]

\displaystyle=c_{r}(A,B;D,C)

∎

Theorem 4
.

For 4 co-linear points
A,B,C,D
in a line
\ell^{OI}
in the Desargues affine plane, the cross-ratio satisfies the equation,

c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

Proof.

From the definition
4
we have
c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right]\left[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)\right]
, and the points of this line forms a skew-field, therefore, we have association property:

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=c_{r}(A,B;C,D)

\displaystyle=[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)][(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)]

\displaystyle\text{(since this factor are elements of skew field, so have the associative property)}

\displaystyle=\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)(B-C)^{-1}\right](A-C)

So, the point
R
, is,

R=\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)(B-C)^{-1}\right](A-C)

multiply in the right side by side with
(A-C)^{-1}
, and have

R\cdot(A-C)^{-1}=(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)(B-C)^{-1}

now multiply in the right side by side with
(B-C)
, and have

(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)=\left(R\cdot(A-C)^{-1}\right)(B-C)

multiply side by side with
(A-B)^{-1}
, and have,

\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right](A-B)^{-1}=\left[R\cdot(A-C)^{-1}(B-C)\right](A-B)^{-1}

Transform the left side of this equation as

\displaystyle\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B+A-A-D)\right](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left[(A-D)^{-1}([A-D]-[A-B])\right](A-B)^{-1}

rewrite it, and have

\displaystyle\left[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)\right](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=[(A-D)^{-1}([A-D]-[A-B])](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=[(A-D)^{-1}[A-D]-(A-D)^{-1}[A-B]](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=[I-(A-D)^{-1}[A-B]](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}[A-B](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}

So, have

(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}=\left[R(A-C)^{-1}(B-C)\right](A-B)^{-1}

In the same way as above, (always bearing in mind that the points of a line of Desargues affine planes form a skew-field related to the addition and multiplication of the points, on this line, and the properties that satisfy a skew-field) we do the following transformations.

First we have the associative property for the multiplication of points on a line,

\left[R(A-C)^{-1}(B-C)\right](A-B)^{-1}=R\left[(A-C)^{-1}(B-C)(A-B)^{-1}\right]

Now we transform the expression

\displaystyle\left[(A-C)^{-1}(B-C)(A-B)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[(A-C)^{-1}(B+A-A-C)(A-B)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[(A-C)^{-1}([A-C]-[A-B])(A-B)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=[I-(A-C)^{-1}[A-B]](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}[A-B](A-B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}

So, have

(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}=R\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]

Hence

R=\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

so,

c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}.

∎

Theorem 5
.

If
A,B,C,D
are distinct points in a line, in Desargues affine plane and
I
is unital point for multiplications of points in same line, then

I-c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=c_{r}(A,C;B,D)

Proof.

Let’s start the calculations, using the result of the theorem

I-\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}=

=\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right\}\left[\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-B)^{-1}\right\}\right]^{-1}

write,

I=\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

so,

\displaystyle I-c_{r}(A,B;C,D)

\displaystyle=I-\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle-\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right]\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]-\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left\{-(A-C)^{-1}+(A-D)^{-1}\right\}\left[(A-B)^{-1}-(A-C)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=(-I)\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right\}\left[(-I)\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-B)^{-1}\right\}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=(-I)\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right\}\left[\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-B)^{-1}\right\}\right]^{-1}(-I)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-D)^{-1}\right\}\left[\left\{(A-C)^{-1}-(A-B)^{-1}\right\}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=c_{r}(A,C;B,D)

from skew-field properties, we have
(-I)^{-1}=-I
and
(-I)(-I)=I

∎

Theorem 6
.

A,B,C,D
are distinct points in a line, in Desargues affine plane and
I
is unitary point for multiplications of points in same line, then,

(a):

c_{r}(A,D;B,C)=I-c_{r}^{-1}(A,B;C,D)

(b):

c_{r}(A,C;D,B)=[I-c_{r}(A,B;C,D)]^{-1}

(c):

c_{r}(A,D;C,B)=[c_{r}(A,B;C,D)-I]^{-1}c_{r}(A,B;C,D)

Proof.

(a) In theorem
3
we have prove that
c_{r}^{-1}(A,B;C,D)=c_{r}(A,B;D,C)
, and from theorem

, have that

. So, we have prove that

3
have that

(c) At this point we will prove that:

From point (a), we prove that
c_{r}(A,D;C,B)=I-c_{r}^{-1}(A,B;C,D),
and from theorem
3
have that
c_{r}(A,D;C,B)=c_{r}^{-1}(A,D;B,C)
. So, we have that

c_{r}(A,D;C,B)=[I-c_{r}^{-1}(A,B;C,D)]^{-1}

Mark the cross-ratios point

, and rewrite. So we have to prove that the equation holds,

[I-R^{-1}]^{-1}=[R-I]^{-1}R,

remember that the points are points of
\ell^{OI}-
line, in Desargues affine planes, and can also be thought of as elements of skew-fields
K=(\ell^{OI},+,\cdot)
, therefore, we can make algebraic transformations, allowed for skew-fields, and we have

\displaystyle\left[I-R^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=[R-I]^{-1}R

\displaystyle\text{right with $R^{-1}$)}

Theorem 7
.

If
A,B,C,D
are distinct points, and different from zero-point
O
, in a line, in Desargues affine plane and
I
is unitary point for multiplications of points in same line, have,

c_{r}(A^{-1},B^{-1};C^{-1},D^{-1})=A\cdot c_{r}(A,B;C,D)\cdot A^{-1}

Proof.

From cross-ratio definition
4
, we have,

c_{r}(A^{-1},B^{-1};C^{-1},D^{-1})=[(A^{-1}-D^{-1})^{-1}(B^{-1}-D^{-1})][(B^{-1}-C^{-1})(A^{-1}-C^{-1})]

Points
A,B,C,D
and
A^{-1},B^{-1},C^{-1},D^{-1}
, are points of
\ell^{OI}-
line in Desargues affine plane, so are and elements of the skew field
K=(\ell^{OI},+,\cdot)
. First we prove that, for tow elements
X,Y
in a skew field
K
, we have that
X^{-1}-Y^{-1}=Y^{-1}(Y-X)X^{-1}
. Indeed

\displaystyle Y^{-1}(Y-X)X^{-1}

\displaystyle=[Y^{-1}(Y-X)]X^{-1}

\displaystyle=(Y^{-1}Y-Y^{-1}X)X^{-1}

\displaystyle=(I-Y^{-1}X)X^{-1}

\displaystyle=IX^{-1}-Y^{-1}(XX^{-1})

\displaystyle=X^{-1}-Y^{-1}I

\displaystyle=X^{-1}-Y^{-1}.

We use this result in the calculation of
c_{r}(A^{-1},B^{-1};C^{-1},D^{-1})

\displaystyle c_{r}(A^{-1},B^{-1};C^{-1},D^{-1})

\displaystyle=[(A^{-1}-D^{-1})^{-1}(B^{-1}-D^{-1})]

Theorem 9
.

If
A,B,C,D\in\ell^{OI}
are distinct points in a line,
in Desargues affine plane and
I
is unital point
for multiplications of points in same line, then equation

c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=c_{r}(B,A;D,C)

it’s true, if

(a):

points
A,B,C,D
are in ’center of skew-field’
z[K]
;

(b):

ratio-points
r(A,B;C)
are in ’center of skew-field’;

(c):

ratio-point
r(B,A;D)
are in ’center of skew-field’;

(d):

ratio-point
r(A,B;D)
is in centaralizer of point
r(A,B;C)
, or vice versa.

Proof.

(a) If points
A,B,C,D\in z[K]
, we have that,

A-D,B-D,B-C,A-C\in z[K]

and

(A-D)^{-1},(B-D)^{-1},(B-C)^{-1},(A-C)^{-1}\in z[K]

also the production is commutative. Hence,

[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)]\cdot[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)]=[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)]\cdot[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)]

so,

c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=c_{r}(B,A;D,C).

(b) If ratio-points
r(A,B;C)
are in ’center of skew-field’, we have that,

X\cdot r(A,B;C)=r(A,B;C)\cdot X,\quad\forall X\in K\text{(so for all points $X\in\ell^{OI}$)}

so the equation is also true for the ratio-point
r(B,A;D)
, and have,

\displaystyle r(A,B;C)\cdot r(B,A;D)

\displaystyle=r(B,A;D)\cdot r(A,B;C)

\displaystyle[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)]\cdot[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)]

\displaystyle=[(A-D)^{-1}(B-D)]\cdot[(B-C)^{-1}(A-C)]

\displaystyle c_{r}(B,A;D,C)

\displaystyle=c_{r}(A,B;C,D).

(c) in the same way, as in case (b).

(d) The Centralizer
\mathcal{C}_{K}(r(A,B;C))=\{Y\in K|Y\cdot r(A,B;C)=r(A,B;C)\cdot Y\}
, and we have that,
r(B,A;D)\in\mathcal{C}_{K}(r(A,B;C))
, so we have,

r(B,A;D)\cdot r(A,B;C)=r(A,B;C)\cdot r(B,A;D)

so,

c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=c_{r}(B,A;D,C),

in the same way, it is proved that if
r(A,B;C)\in\mathcal{C}_{K}(r(B,A;D))
, then
c_{r}(A,B;C,D)=c_{r}(B,A;D,C).

∎

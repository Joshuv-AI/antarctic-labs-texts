Abstract
. This paper is devoted to investigate the extinction and propagation properties of solutions to the graph Laplacian parabolic problems with Kpp type or Allen-Cahn type forcing terms on graphs. To this end, we establish the (strong) maximum principle and the upper and lower solutions method for parabolic and elliptic problems on graphs. The stability of equilibrium solutions is studied by constructing suitable upper and lower solutions. Moreover, we give an example and numerical experiments to demonstrate one of our main results.

We show that now the solvability of the initial-boundary value problem for graphs using the method of separation of variables.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{\Omega}u(x,t)+cu(x,t)=\tilde{G}(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t\in(0,T),\\
{\mathcal{B}u(x,t)=g(x,t),\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\quad t\in[0,T),}\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega\end{array}\right.

admits a unique solution

\mathcal{B}u(x,t)=u

For

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\Omega}}v(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{y\in\overline{\Omega}}\left(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\right)\frac{\omega_{yx}}{\mu(x)}

where

h_{j}(t)=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}H(x,t)\phi_{j}(x)\mu(x)=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}\left[\tilde{G}(x,t)+\sum\limits_{y\in\partial{\Omega}}g(y,t)\frac{\omega_{yx}}{\mu(x)}\right]\phi_{j}(x)\mu(x)

for
j=1,\cdots,n
. Then we substitute the above expansions into (
3.3
) to deduce that

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left[v_{j}^{\prime}(t)+v_{j}\lambda_{j}+cv_{j}-h_{j}(t)\right]\phi_{j}(x)=0,

and

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}[v_{j}(0)-u_{0}^{j}]\phi_{j}(x)=0

for
j=1,\cdots,n
. It follows from Corollary
2.2
(2) that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{j}^{\prime}(t)+v_{j}\lambda_{j}+cv_{j}=h_{j}(t),~t\in(0,T),\\
v_{j}(0)=u_{0}^{j}\end{array}\right.

for
j=1,\cdots,n
. Thus, we deduce that

v_{j}(t)=e^{-(\lambda_{j}+c)t}(u_{0}^{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\lambda_{j}+c)s}h_{j}(s)ds),~j=1,\cdots,n.

Therefore, we see that

v(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(\lambda_{j}+c)t}(u_{0}^{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\lambda_{j}+c)s}h_{j}(s)ds)\phi_{j}(x),~x\in\Omega,~t\in(0,T).

Due to (
3.2
), we conclude that

(3.4)

u(x,t)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(\lambda_{j}+c)t}[u_{0}^{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\lambda_{j}+c)s}h_{j}(s)ds]\phi_{j}(x),~x\in\Omega,~t\in(0,T),\\
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(\lambda_{j}+c)t}[u_{0}^{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\lambda_{j}+c)s}h_{j}(s)ds]\phi_{j}(x)+g(x,t),~x\in\partial\Omega,~t\in(0,T).\end{cases}

A simple calculation shows that the function defined by (
3.4
) on
\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T)
gives a solution of equation (
3.1
).

In the other case of
\mathcal{B}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}
. For the host graph
\overline{\Omega}
, let
N
denote the number of vertices of the graph
\overline{\Omega}
and
u:\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
be a solution in
C^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T))\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T))
of (
3.1
). Clearly, there exists a function
I(x,t)\in C^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T))
such that for any given
t\in(0,T)
,

\int_{\Omega}I(x,t)d\mu=\int_{\partial\Omega}g(x,t)d\mu
(If

, we take
I\equiv 0
).
For any fixed
t\in(0,T)
, by a similar argument as in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.8]
, we see that the following problem

\begin{cases}\Delta_{\Omega}\Phi(x)=I(x,t)&x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial_{\Omega}n}(x)=g(x,t),&x\in\partial{\Omega}\end{cases}

admits a solution
\hat{u}(x,t)\in C^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T))
.

Set

{v(x,t)}:=u(x,t)-\hat{u}(x,t)~x\in\bar{\Omega},~t\in[0,T).\\

By straightforward calculations, we deduce that, for
x\in\Omega
,

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{\Omega}v(x,t)+cv(x,t)=:H(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t\in(0,T),\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial_{\Omega}n}(x,t)=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\quad t\in[0,T),\\
v(x,0)={u}_{0}(x)-\hat{u}(x,0):=\psi(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
H(x,t):=\tilde{G}(x,t)-\hat{u}_{t}(x,t)+\Delta_{{\Omega}}\hat{u}(x,t)-c\hat{u}(x,t).

Recall that
K_{j}
and
\Phi_{j}(x)

(j=1,2,\cdots,n)
are the eigenpairs satisfy the eigenvalue problem (
2.4
). We consider the following expansions

v(x,t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}(t)\Phi_{j}(x),H(x,t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}(t)\Phi_{j}(x),\psi(x)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}\Phi_{j}(x).

where

v_{j}(t)=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}v(x,t)\Phi_{j}(x)\mu(x),\psi_{j}=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}\psi(x)\Phi_{j}(x)\mu(x),

h_{j}(t)=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}H(x,t)\Phi_{j}(x)\mu(x)=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}\left[G(x,t)-\hat{u}_{t}(x,t)+\Delta_{{\Omega}}\hat{u}(x,t)-c\hat{u}(x,t)\right]\Phi_{j}(x)\mu(x).

Substituting the above expansions into (

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}[v_{j}^{\prime}(t)+v_{j}K_{j}+cv_{j}-h_{j}(t)]\Phi_{j}(x)=0,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}[v_{j}(0)-\psi_{j}]\Phi_{j}(x)=0.

In view of the properties of

in Proposition
2.3
, we deduce that

v_{j}^{\prime}(t)+v_{j}K_{j}+cv_{j}-h_{j}(t)=0,~t\in(0,T),v_{j}(0)-\psi_{j}=0,~j=1,\dots,n.

Thus, we see that

v_{j}(t)=e^{-(K_{j}+c)t}[\psi_{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(K_{j}+c)s}h_{j}(s)ds],~j=1,\cdots,n.

Therefore, we conclude that

v(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(K_{j}+c)t}[\psi_{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(K_{j}+c)s}h_{j}(s)ds]\Phi_{j}(x),~x\in\Omega,~t\in[0,T).

It follows that

u(x,t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(K_{j}+c)t}[\psi_{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(K_{j}+c)s}h_{j}(s)ds]\Phi_{j}(x)+\hat{u}(x,t),~x\in\bar{\Omega},~t\in[0,T).

A simple calculation shows that the function

defined by (
3.1
) on
\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T)
gives a solution of equation (
3.1
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In Theorem

Next, we establish the existence and uniqueness for the Cauchy problem on graphs.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph,
M\in\mathbb{R}
,
h\in C^{0}\left(V\times[0,T)\right)\cap L^{1}\left(V\times(0,T)\right)
and
\psi:V\to\mathbb{R}
be given. Then the following Cauchy problem

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{V}u(x,t)+Mu(x,t)={h}(x,t),\quad x\in V,\quad t\in(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=\psi(x),\quad x\in V\end{array}\right.

admits a unique solution
u(x,t)\in C^{0}(V\times[0,T))
.

Proof.

Let
N
denote the number of vertices of
V
and
u(x,t)
be a solution to (
3.6
).
We consider the following expansions

(3.7)

u(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}u_{j}(t)\Psi_{j}(x),~h(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}h_{j}(t)\Psi_{j}(x),~\psi(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\psi_{j}(t)\Psi_{j}(x),

where

u_{j}(t)=\sum\limits_{x\in V}u(x,t)\Psi_{j}(x)\mu(x),~h_{j}(t)=\sum\limits_{x\in V}h(x,t)\Psi_{j}(x)\mu(x),~\psi_{j}=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\psi(x)\Psi_{j}(x)\mu(x).

Substituting (
3.7
) into (
3.6
), we conclude that

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}\left[u_{j}^{\prime}(t)+u_{j}\mu_{j}+Mu_{j}-h_{j}(t)\right]\Psi_{j}(x)=0,\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}[u_{j}(0)-\psi_{j}]\Psi_{j}(x)=0.

By Proposition
2.1
(2), we know that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{j}^{\prime}(t)+u_{j}\mu_{j}+Mu_{j}=h_{j}(t),~t\in(0,T),\\
u_{j}(0)=\psi_{j}\end{array}\right.

\text{for}~j=0,1,\cdots,N-1.
Thus, we obtain

u_{j}(t)=e^{-(\mu_{j}+M)t}(\psi_{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\mu_{j}+M)s}h_{j}(s)ds),~j=0,1,\cdots,N-1.

Therefore,

(3.8)

u(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{-(\mu_{j}+M)t}\left[\psi_{j}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\mu_{j}+M)s}h_{j}(s)ds\right]\Psi_{j}(x).

Furthermore, it is easy to see that
u(x,t)
in (
3.8
) satisfies the Cauchy problem (
3.6
).
∎

3.2.
Maximum principle for parabolic problems

This section develops the maximum principle for elliptic equations and parabolic equations on finite connected weighted graphs.

3.2.1.
The parabolic equation with initial-boundary value

This subsection is devoted to establish the maximum principle for parabolic equations with boundary conditions on graphs.

Theorem 3.4
.

(Maximum principle)

Let
\Omega
be a subgraph of a host graph
G=(V,E)
with
\partial\Omega\not=\emptyset
. For any
T>0
, we assume that
v(x,t)

\in C^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T])\cap C^{0}(\Omega\times[0,T])
satisfies

\begin{cases}v_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{\Omega}v(x,t)-k(x,t)v(x,t)\geq 0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
v(x,0)\geq 0,&x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{A}v(x,t)\geq 0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\end{cases}

where
k(x,t)
is bounded on
\Omega\times(0,T]
and
\mathcal{A}v=\alpha(x,t)v+\beta(x,t)\frac{\partial v}{\partial_{\Omega}n}
,
\alpha\geq 0
,
\beta\geq 0
, and
\alpha+\beta>0
on
\Omega\times[0,T]
. Then

(3.9)

v(x,t)\geq 0~\text{on}~\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T].

Proof.

Set

\mathcal{L}v(x,t)=v_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{\Omega}v(x,t)-k(x,t)v(x,t).

It’s clear that
\mathcal{L}v\geq 0~\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T].
As
k(x,t)
is bounded in
\Omega\times(0,T]
, we can find a positive number
l
such that
l>k(x,t)
for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T]
.

Define
\psi=ve^{-lt}
. It gives that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}v(x,t)

\displaystyle=v_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{\Omega}v(x,t)-k(x,t)v(x,t)

\displaystyle=e^{lt}(\psi_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}\psi-(k-l)\psi)

\displaystyle=:e^{lt}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x,t).

Recall that
v(x,t)
belongs to
C^{0}(\Omega\times[0,T])
, then we have
{\psi(x,t)\in C^{0}(\Omega\times[0,T])}
. Therefore, we can find
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega\times[0,T]
so that

(3.10)

\psi(x_{0},t_{0})=\min\limits_{\Omega\times[0,T]}\psi.

Next, we assert that
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0
. Suppose by way of contradiction that
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<0
.
If
t_{0}=0
, it’s clear that
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})=v(x_{0},0)<0
. This is a contradiction.
For
t_{0}\in(0,T]
, then we use
k(x_{0},t_{0})<l
and
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<0
to obtain that

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<\psi_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})-\Delta_{\Omega}\psi(x_{0},t_{0}).

It’s obviously that
\psi_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq 0
. Then, we have

(3.11)

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<-\Delta_{\Omega}\psi(x_{0},t_{0}).

For any
y\in\partial\Omega
, we use

\left(\alpha\psi+\beta\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial_{\Omega}n}\right)\bigg|_{(y,t_{0})}=e^{-lt_{0}}\left(\alpha v+\beta\frac{\partial v}{\partial_{\Omega}n}\right)\bigg|_{(y,t_{0})}\geq 0,

to conclude that
\psi(y,t_{0})\geq\psi(x_{0},t_{0})
for all
y\in\partial{\Omega}
. This implies that

(3.12)

\Delta_{\Omega}\psi\left(x_{0},t_{0}\right)=\frac{1}{\mu\left(x_{0}\right)}\sum_{y:y\sim x_{0}}\omega_{x_{0}y}\left(\psi\left(y,t_{0}\right)-\psi\left(x_{0},t_{0}\right)\right)\geq 0.

Note that
\mathcal{L}v\geq 0
in
\Omega\times(0,T]
, we see that
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi\geq 0
in
\Omega\times(0,T]
.
On the other hand, it follows from (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) that

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<0.

This is impossible.
Thus, we can conclude that
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0
and hence that
\psi(x,t)\geq 0
for
x\in\Omega
and
t\in(0,T]
by (
3.10
).

If there exists
(x_{1},t_{1})\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T]
such that
\psi(x_{1},t_{1})<0
,
then we have

\left(\alpha\psi+\beta\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial_{\Omega}n}\right)(x_{1},t_{1})=\left(\alpha\psi\right)(x_{1},t_{1})+\beta(x_{1},t_{1})\frac{1}{\mu(x_{1})}\sum_{z\in\Omega}(\psi(x_{1},t_{1})-\psi(z,t_{1}))\omega_{x_{1}z}<0,

which is a contradiction. Thus, we obtain
{u(x,t)\geq 0}
for
x\in\bar{\Omega}
and
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

(Strong maximum principle)
Suppose that
u\in C^{1}({\Omega}\times(0,T])\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T])
satisfies

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}u+k(x,t)u\geq 0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\mathcal{B}u(x,t)\geq 0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geqslant,\not\equiv 0,&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

and
k(x,t)
is bounded function for
x\in\Omega
and
t\in[0,T]
, where
\mathcal{B}u=u
or
\mathcal{B}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}
. Then if
\mathcal{B}u=u
, then
u(x,t)>0
for
x\in{\Omega}
and
t\in(0,T]
and if
\mathcal{B}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}
, then
u(x,t)>0
for
x\in\bar{\Omega}
and
t\in(0,T]
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.4
, we obtain
u(x,t)\geq 0
on
\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]
. As the boundness of
k(x,t)
, we can find a constant
\bar{k}>0
such that
k\leq\bar{k}
. It follows that

0\leq u_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}u+ku\leq u_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}u+\bar{k}u

in
\Omega\times[0,T]
.
By Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
, the problem

\begin{cases}v_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}v+\bar{k}v=0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\mathcal{B}v(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
v(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega\end{cases}

admits a unique solution
v\geq 0
on
\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T]
. It follows that

\displaystyle v_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}v+kv

\displaystyle\leq v_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}v+\bar{k}v=0~\text{in}~\Omega\times[0,T].

Setting
W=u-v
, we find that
W
satisfies

\begin{cases}W_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}W+\bar{k}W\geq 0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\mathcal{B}W(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
W(x,0)=0,&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

Then we apply Theorem
3.4
to conclude that
W\geq 0
on
\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]
. It’s easily seen that
u\geq v
on
\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]
.

Consider the transformation
w=e^{\bar{k}t}v
, then
w
satisfies

\begin{cases}w_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}w=0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\mathcal{B}w(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
w(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geqslant,\not\equiv 0,&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

Denote
n=|\Omega|
. For the Dirichlet boundary
\mathcal{B}u=u
,
it follows from Theorem
3.1
that

(3.13)

w(x,t)=\sum\limits_{y\in\Omega}P(x,y,t)u_{0}(y)~\text{for}~x\in\bar{\Omega}~\text{and}~t\in[0,T],

where
P(x,y,t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y)\mu(y)
. It is easy to check that

P_{t}=\Delta_{\Omega,x}P,

where

\Delta_{\Omega,x}P(x,y,t)=\sum_{z\in\bar{\Omega}}[P(z,y,t)-P(x,y,t)]\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)}.

Combining Theorem
3.4
and

P(x,y,0)=\delta_{x}(y)=\begin{cases}1,x=y,\\
0,x\not=y\end{cases}\text{for}\;x,y\in\Omega,

we have
P(x,y,t)\geq 0
for
t\in[0,T]
and
x,y\in\Omega
.

Now,we shall show

(3.14)

P(x,y,t)>0~\text{ for}~x,y\in\Omega~\text{ and}~0<t\leq T

by using proof by contradiction. Otherwise, there exists
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
and
t_{0}\in(0,T]
such that
P(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=0
. It’s easily seen that

0=P_{t}(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=\Delta_{\Omega,x}P(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\geq 0.

and hence
P(x,y_{0},t_{0})=P(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=0

\forall x\in\Omega:x\sim x_{0}
. Note that

\Omega
is connected, for any
z\in\Omega
, we can find
x_{j}(j=1,\cdots,n)
such that
x_{1}\sim x_{2}\sim\cdots\sim x_{n}\sim z
. It now follows from the discussion inductively that
P(z,y_{0},t_{0})=0
, which implies that

P(y_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{j}t_{0}}\phi_{j}^{2}(y_{0})\mu(y_{0})=0.

Therefore, we see that

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{j}^{2}(y_{0})\mu(y_{0})=0
which is a contradiction with Corollary
2.2
(4).
Combining (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and
u_{0}\geq,\not\equiv 0
, we obtain

w(x,t)>0
for
x\in\Omega
and
t\in(0,T]
. It follows that
v(x,t)>0
for
x\in\Omega
and
t\in(0,T]
. Recalling that
u\geq v
on
\Omega\times[0,T]
, we see that
u(x,t)>0
for
x\in\Omega
and
t\in(0,T]
.

Next, we remain to prove the conclusion holds with the Neumman boundary
\mathcal{B}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}.

Applying Theorem
3.1
, we see that

w(x,t)=\sum\limits_{y\in\Omega}Q(t,x,y)u_{0}(y),

where
Q(t,x,y)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}e^{-K_{j}t}\Phi_{j}(x)\Phi_{j}(y)\mu(y)
. It is easy to check that

Q_{t}=\Delta_{\Omega,x}Q,

where

\Delta_{\Omega,x}Q(t,x,y)=\sum_{z\in\bar{\Omega}}[Q(t,z,y)-Q(t,x,y)]\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)}.

Due to

Q(0,x,y)=\delta_{x}(y)=\begin{cases}1,x=y,\\
0,x\not=y,\end{cases}\text{for}\;x,y\in\Omega,

we use the similar approach to that in the case that
\mathcal{B}u=u
to deduce that
Q(t,x,y)>0
for
x,y\in\Omega
and
0<t\leq T
. It follows that
w(x,t)>0
for
x\in\Omega
and

which implies that

(3.15)

u(x,t)>0~\text{ on}~\Omega\times(0,T].

If there exists
x_{0}\in\partial{\Omega}
and

such that

, then

0=\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}(x_{0},t_{0})=\sum_{y\in\bar{\Omega}}(u(x_{0},t_{0})-u(y,t_{0}))\frac{\omega_{x_{0}y}}{\mu(x_{0})}\leq 0.

Therefore, we have

u(y,t_{0})=u(x_{0},t_{0})=0~\forall y\in{\Omega}:~y\sim x_{0},

which contradicts to (
3.15
). Thus, we obtain

u(x,t)>0~\text{for}~x\in\bar{\Omega}~\text{and}~t\in(0,T].

∎

3.2.2.
The parabolic equation with initial value

This subsection is devoted to establish the maximum principle for parabolic equations under the condition that there is no boundary condition on graphs.

Theorem 3.6
.

(Maximum principle)

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For any
T>0
, we assume that
v(x,t)

\in C^{1}(V\times[0,T])
, which satisfies

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v(x,t)-{\Delta_{V}}v(x,t)-k(x,t)v(x,t)\geq 0,&(x,t)\in V\times(0,T],\\
v(x,0)\geq 0,&x\in V,\end{cases}

where
k(x,t)
is bounded on
V\times[0,T]
. Then

v(x,t)\geq 0~\text{on}~V\times[0,T].

Proof.

Denote

\mathcal{L}v(x,t)=v_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{\Omega}v(x,t)-k(x,t)v(x,t).

It’s easily seen that
\mathcal{L}v\geq 0~\text{in}~V\times(0,T].
We observe that
k(x,t)
is bounded in
V\times(0,T]
, then there exists a positive constant
l
such that
l>k(x,t)
for all
(x,t)\in V\times(0,T]
.

If we set
\psi=ve^{-lt}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}v(x,t)

\displaystyle=v_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{\Omega}v(x,t)-k(x,t)v(x,t)

\displaystyle=e^{lt}(\psi_{t}-\Delta_{\Omega}\psi-(k-l)\psi)

\displaystyle=:e^{lt}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x,t).

We use
\mathcal{L}v\geq 0
in
V\times(0,T]
easily to deduce that
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi\geq 0
in
V\times(0,T]
.
Recalling that
v(x,t)\in C^{1}(V\times[0,T])
, we have
\psi(x,t)\in C^{1}(V\times[0,T])
. Then we can find
(x_{0},t_{0})\in V\times[0,T]
such that

(3.16)

\psi(x_{0},t_{0})=\min\limits_{V\times[0,T]}\psi.

Now, we are going to prove that
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0
. Arguing indirectly, we assume that
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<0
.
If
t_{0}=0
, we have
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})=v(x_{0},0)\geq 0
. This is a contradiction.
For
t_{0}\in(0,T]
, we use
k(x_{0},t_{0})<l
and
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<0
to deduce that

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<\psi_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})-\Delta_{\Omega}\psi(x_{0},t_{0}).

In this situation, we observe that
\psi_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq 0
. Then, we have

(3.17)

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<-\Delta_{\Omega}\psi(x_{0},t_{0}).

It follows from (
3.16
) that

-\Delta_{{\Omega}}\psi(x_{0},t_{0})=-\sum_{y:y\sim x_{0}}(\psi(y,t_{0})-\psi(x_{0},t_{0}))\frac{\omega_{x_{0}y}}{\mu(x_{0})}\leq 0.

Moreover, we apply (
3.17
)
to deduce that

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi(x_{0},t_{0})<0.

This contradicts with
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi\geq 0
in
V\times(0,T]
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\psi(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0
and hence that
\psi(x,t)\geq 0
on
V\times[0,T]
. This implies that
v\geq 0
on
V\times[0,T]
. ∎

Theorem 3.7
.

(Strong maximum principle)

Assume that
u(x,t)\in C^{1}(V\times[0,T])
satisfies

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta_{{V}}u+k(x,t)u\geq 0,~(x,t)\in V\times(0,T],\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq,\not\equiv 0,~x\in V,\end{cases}

where
k
is bounded on
V\times[0,T]
.
Then we have
u(x,t)>0
for
x\in V
and
t\in(0,T]
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.6
that
u(x,t)\geq 0
on
V\times[0,T]
. Thanks to
k(x,t)
is bounded, we can find
\bar{k}>0
such that
k\leq\bar{k}
. Using Theorems
3.3
and
3.6
, the linear parabolic problem

\begin{cases}v_{t}-\Delta_{{V}}v+\bar{k}v=0,~(x,t)\in V\times[0,T],\\
v(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~x\in V.\end{cases}

admits a unique solution
v\geq 0
on
V\times[0,T]
.
Obviously,

v_{t}-\Delta_{V}v+kv\leq v_{t}-\Delta_{V}v+\bar{k}v=0

in
V\times(0,T]
.

Denote
W=u-v
. Then we find that
W
satisfies

\begin{cases}W_{t}-\Delta_{V}W+kW\geq 0,&(x,t)\in{V}\times(0,T],\\
W(x,0)=0,&x\in{V}.\end{cases}

By Theorem
3.6
, we deduce that
w\geq 0
on
V\times[0,T]
, and hence that

(3.18)

u(x,t)\geq v(x,t)~\text{on}~V\times[0,T].

Defining
w=ve^{\bar{k}t}
; then we know that
w
is the solution of

\begin{cases}w_{t}-\Delta_{V}w=0,~x\in V,~t\in[0,T],\\
w(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~x\in V.\end{cases}

and

(3.19)

w(x,t)=\sum\limits_{y\in V}p(x,y,t)u_{0}(y)

by Theorem
3.3
, where
p(x,y,t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{-\mu_{j}t}\Psi_{j}(x)\Psi_{j}(y)\mu(y)
is called the heat kernel. It is easy to check that

p_{t}=\Delta_{\Omega,x}p,

where

\Delta_{\Omega,x}p(x,y,t)=\sum_{z\in\bar{\Omega}}[p(z,y,t)-p(x,y,t)]\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)}.

It’s known that

p(x,y,0)=\delta_{x}(y)=\begin{cases}1,x=y,\\
0,x\not=y\end{cases}\text{for}\;x,y\in V

Moreover, we apply Theorem
3.6
to obtain
p(x,y,t)\geq 0
for
t\in[0,T]
and
x,y\in V
.

Now, we shall claim that

(3.20)

p(x,y,t)>0~\text{ for}~x,y\in V~\text{ and}~0<t\leq T.

Otherwise, there exists
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
and
t_{0}\in(0,T]
such that
p(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=0
. Thus we deduce that

0=p_{t}(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=\Delta_{\Omega,x}p(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\geq 0.

Therefore,
p(x,y_{0},t_{0})=p(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=0

\forall x\in V:x\sim x_{0}
. As the connectivity of

V
, for any
z\in V
, we can find
x_{j}(j=1,\cdots,n)
such that
x_{1}\sim x_{2}\sim\cdots\sim x_{n}\sim z
. By the discussion inductively, we see that
p(z,y_{0},t_{0})=0
. This implies that

p(y_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{-\mu_{j}t_{0}}\Psi_{j}^{2}(y_{0})\mu(y_{0})=0.

Therefore, we see that

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}\Psi_{j}^{2}(y_{0})\mu(y_{0})=0.
This is a contradiction with Proposition
2.1
.

Thanks to
u_{0}\geq,\not\equiv 0
, by (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), we deduce that

w(x,t)>0
for
x\in V
and
t\in(0,T]
. It follows that
v(x,t)>0
for
x\in V
and
t\in(0,T]
. In view of (
3.18
), we see that
u(x,t)>0
for
x\in V
and
t\in(0,T]
.
∎

3.3.
Maximum principle for elliptic problems

In this section, we establish the maximum principle for elliptic equations on graphs.

3.3.1.
The elliptic problems with boundary

Theorem 3.8
.

(Maximum principle)

Suppose that
u
satisfies

-{\Delta_{\Omega}u}-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u+c(x)u\geq 0\text{~in~}\Omega,~\mathcal{B}u\geq 0~\text{on}~\partial{\Omega}.

where
b(x)\geq 0
and
c(x)>0
for all
x\in V
.
Then we have
u\geq 0
for all
x\in\bar{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Let
u(x_{0})=\min\limits_{\bar{x\in\Omega}}u(x)
. We shall prove that
u(x_{0})\geq 0
. Suppose by way of contradiction that
u(x_{0})<0
.
If
x_{0}\in\Omega
, then we see that

\displaystyle-\Delta_{{\Omega}}u(x_{0})-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u(x_{0})

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{y:y\sim x_{0}}(u(y)-u(x_{0}))\frac{\omega_{yx_{0}}}{\mu(x_{0})}-\sum_{y\in\bar{\Omega}}b(y)\left(u(y)-u(x_{0})\right)\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{x_{0}y}}{2\mu(x_{0})}}\leq 0.

Thus, we have

\left(-{\Delta_{\Omega}}u-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u+c(x)u\right)\big|_{x=x_{0}}<0,

which is a contradiction.

For
x_{0}\in\partial{\Omega}
, we first consider the case of
\mathcal{B}u=u
, we deduce that
u(x_{0})<0
, which contradicts to
u\geq 0
on
\partial{\Omega}
.
However, for the case of
\mathcal{B}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}
, we see that

0\leq\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}(x_{0})=\sum\limits_{y\in\bar{\Omega}:y\sim x_{0}}(u(x_{0})-u(y))\frac{\omega_{x_{0}y}}{\mu(x_{0})}\leq 0.

This implies that
u(y)=u(x_{0})
for all
y\in\Omega
satisfying
y\sim x_{0}
. As
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, we can find
z\in\Omega:z\sim x_{0}
such that
u(z)=u(x_{0})<0
. By a similar argument as above (the case that
x_{0}\in\Omega
), we obtain a contradiction.
Therefore, we deduce that
u(x_{0})\geq 0
and hence that
u(x)\geq 0

\forall x\in\bar{\Omega}
.
∎

3.3.2.
The elliptic problems without boundary

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume that
u
satisfies
-\Delta_{V}u-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u+c(x)u\geq 0
on
V
, where
b(x)\geq 0
and
c(x)>0
for all
x\in V
. Then
u\geq 0
for all
x\in V
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
x_{0}\in V
such that
u(x_{0})=\min\limits_{V}u<0
. Then we see that

-\Delta_{V}u(x_{0})-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u(x_{0})+c(x_{0})u(x_{0})<0,

which is a contradiction.
∎

4.
Upper and lower solutions method

In this section, we develop the upper and lower solutions method for parabolic equations and elliptic equations on finite connected weighted graphs.

4.1.
The upper and lower solutions method for parabolic problems

4.1.1.

In this subsection, we introduce the upper and lower solutions method for the problem (

) on graphs.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose that

Theorem 4.1
.

(Ordering of upper and lower solutions)

Let
\bar{u}
,
\underline{u}
be an upper and a lower solution of (
1.10
), respectively. Denote
\sigma=\inf\limits_{\Omega_{T}}\min\{\bar{u},\underline{u}\}

f
satisfies the Lipschitz condition in

|f(x,t,u)-f(x,t,v)|\leq M|u-v|,~\forall~(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T],~u,v\in[\sigma,\omega],

then
\bar{u}\geq\underline{u}
in
\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]
. Moreover,
\bar{u}>\underline{u}
in
\Omega\times(0,T]
if
\bar{u}(x,0)\not\equiv\underline{u}(x,0)
.

Proof.

Setting

\hat{c}(x,t)=\begin{cases}M,~\text{if}~\bar{u}(x,t)\geq\underline{u}(x,t),\\
-M,~\text{if}~\bar{u}(x,t)<\underline{u}(x,t),\end{cases}

and
u=\bar{u}-\underline{u}
. Then
u
satisfies

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}u+\hat{c}(x,t)u(x,t)\geq 0,~~&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\mathcal{B}u{\geq}0,~&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u(x,0)\geq 0,~&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\hat{c}(x,t)
is bounded. It follows from Theorem
3.4
that
u(x,t)\geq 0
for
x\in\bar{\Omega}
and
t\in[0,T]
. Furthermore, if
\bar{u}(x,0)\not\equiv\underline{u}(x,0)
, then
u(x,0)\not\equiv 0
,
x\in\Omega
. Thus, we apply Theorem
3.5
to deduce that

u(x,t)>0~\text{in}~\Omega\times(0,T].

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\overline{u}
,
\underline{u}
be an upper and a lower solution of (
1.10
), respectively,

,

satisfies the Lipschitz condition in

for all

, then (
1.10

satisfying
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
on
\overline{\Omega}_{T}
, where
\overline{\Omega}_{T}:=\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T],\;\underline{\eta}=\min\limits_{\overline{\Omega}_{T}}\min\{\overline{u},\underline{u}\}

\overline{\eta}=\max\limits_{\overline{\Omega}_{T}}\max\{\overline{u},\underline{u}\}

Proof.

If we define

then for any given

, we see that

. Combining this with (

admits a unique solution

.

Define

3.4

on
\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]

Now, we assert that

obtain that

3.4
to deduce that

.

Next, we can define

,
f
satisfies the Lipschitz condition in
u\in[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]
, i.e., there exists a positive constant
M
such that

(4.8)

|f(x,t,u)-f(x,t,v)|\leq M|u-v|

for all
(x,t)\in V\times(0,T],~u,v\in[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]
, then (
1.8
) admits a unique solution
u\in C^{1}(V\times[0,T])
satisfying
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
on
V\times[0,T]
, where
\underline{\eta}=\min\limits_{V\times[0,T]}\min\{\overline{u},\underline{u}\}
and
\overline{\eta}=\max\limits_{V\times[0,T]}\max\{\overline{u},\underline{u}\}
.

Proof.

Define

<\underline{u},\bar{u}>=\{u\in C^{0}(V\times[0,T])|{\underline{u}\leq u\leq\bar{u}}~\text{in}~V\times[0,T]\}.

For any given
v\in<\underline{u},\bar{u}>
, we apply (
4.7
) to obtain that

H(x,t):=f(x,t,v(x,t))+Mv(x,t)\in C^{0}(V\times[0,T]).

It follows from Theorem
3.3
that the linear problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{V}u(x,t)+Mu(x,t)=H(x,t),~x\in V,~t\in(0,T],\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in V\end{array}\right.

admits a unique solution
u(x,t)
.

We now define
\mathcal{F}:<\underline{u},\bar{u}>\to X_{T}:=C^{0}(V\times[0,T])
by
u=\mathcal{F}v
and

\underline{u}_{0}=\underline{u},~\underline{u}_{k}=\mathcal{F}\underline{u}_{k-1},~\bar{u}_{0}=\bar{u},~\bar{u}_{k}=\mathcal{F}\bar{u}_{k-1},~k=1,2,\cdots.

Next, we claim that

(4.9)

\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\underline{u}_{k}\leq\bar{u}_{k}\leq\cdots\leq\bar{u}_{1}\leq\bar{u}~\text{on}~V\text{~for ~all~}k\geq 1.

For any
u,v\in<\underline{u},\bar{u}>
satisfying
u\leq v
on
V\times[0,T]
. Let
w_{1}=\mathcal{F}u
,
w_{2}=\mathcal{F}v
and
w=w_{2}-w_{1}
. Then we can find
w
satisfies the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w_{t}-\Delta_{V}w+Mw=f(x,t,v)-f(x,t,u)+M(v-u)\geq 0~in~V\times(0,T],\\
w(x,0)=0~\text{ on}~V.\end{array}\right.

It follows from Theorem
3.6
that
w\geq 0
on
V\times[0,T]
. This implies that
w_{1}=\mathcal{F}u\leq\mathcal{F}v=w_{2}
on
V\times[0,T]
. By Theorem
4.3
, we have
\bar{u}\geq\underline{u}
in
V\times[0,T]
. It’s easily seen that
\underline{u}_{k}\leq\bar{u}_{k}
in
{V}\times(0,T]
for any integer
k\geq 1
by induction.

We shall show that
\underline{u}_{k+1}\geq\underline{u}_{k}
on
V\times[0,T]
for all
k\geq 1
by induction. Let
z=\underline{u}_{1}-\underline{u}
; then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{V}z(x,t)+Mz\geq 0,~x\in V,~t\in(0,T],\\
z(x,0)=0,\quad x\in V.\end{array}\right.

In view of Theorem
3.6
, we see that
\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{1}
on
V\times[0,T]
.
We assume that
\underline{u}_{k+1}\geq\underline{u}_{k}
on
V\times[0,T]
. Denote
U:=\underline{u}_{k+2}-\underline{u}_{k+1}
. Then we apply Theorem
3.6

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}U_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{V}U(x,t)+MU(x,t)=f(x,t,\underline{u}_{k+1})-f(x,t,\underline{u}_{k})+M(\underline{u}_{k+1}-\underline{u}_{k})\geq 0\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad in~V\times(0,T],\\
U(x,0)\geq 0~\text{ on}~V.\end{array}\right.

to conclude that
\underline{u}_{k+2}\geq\underline{u}_{k+1}
on
V\times[0,T]
.
Therefore, by induction, we see that
\underline{u}_{k+1}\geq\underline{u}_{k}
on
V\times[0,T]
for all
k\geq 1
. Similarly, since
\bar{u}_{k}
satisfies

(4.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(\bar{u}_{k})_{t}-\Delta_{V}\bar{u}_{k}+M\bar{u}_{k}=f(x,t,\bar{u}_{k-1})+M\bar{u}_{k-1}(x,t),~x\in V,~t\in(0,T],\\
\bar{u}_{k}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in V.\end{array}\right.

We argument similar to those shown above imply via induction that

\bar{u}_{k+1}\leq\bar{u}_{k}
on
V\times[0,T]
for all
k\geq 1
.
Thus, (
4.9
) holds.

Therefore, we can define

\underline{u}(x,t):=\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\underline{u}_{k}(x,t),~\bar{u}(x,t):=\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\bar{u}_{k}(x,t).

For any given
x\in V
and any
(t_{1},t_{2})\subset[0,T]
, it follows from the first equality in (
4.10
) that

(4.11)

\bar{u}_{k}(x,t_{2})-\bar{u}_{k}(x,t_{1})=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\Delta_{{V}}\bar{u}_{k}-M\bar{u}_{k}+f(x,t,\bar{u}_{k-1})+M\bar{u}_{k-1}\right)dt.

As
k\to+\infty
in (
4.11
),
\overline{u}
satisfies

\bar{u}(x,t_{2})-\bar{u}(x,t_{1})=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\Delta_{{V}}\bar{u}-M\bar{u}+f(x,t,\bar{u})+M\bar{u}~\right)dt.

This implies that

\bar{u}_{t}-\Delta_{V}\bar{u}+M\bar{u}=f(x,t,\bar{u})+M\bar{u}(x,t)~\text{for}~x\in V,~t\in[0,T].

As
k\to\infty
in the second equality of (
4.10
), we obtain

\bar{u}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~x\in V.

In other word,
\bar{u}\in C^{1}(V\times[0,T])
satisfies (
1.8
). Similarly,
\underline{u}\in C^{1}(V\times[0,T])
satisfies (
1.8
).
∎

4.2.
The upper and lower solutions method for elliptic problems

4.2.1.
The elliptic equation with boundary

In this subsection, we establish the upper and lower solutions method for the problem (
1.11
).

Define

H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega}):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)|u(x)=0~\text{for}~x\in\partial{\Omega}\}

and

||u||_{\bar{\Omega}}:=\left(\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~\forall~u\in H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega}).

It is easy to check that
\left(H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega}),\|\cdot\|_{\bar{\Omega}}\right)
is a Banach space.

Definition 4.3
.

A function
u:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
is called an upper (a lower) solution of (
1.11
), if

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}u\geq(\leq)~f(x,u),~&x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}[u]\geq(\leq)~\phi(x),~&x\in\partial{\Omega}.\end{cases}

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\bar{u}
and
\underline{u}
be an upper and a lower solution of (
1.11
) respectively and
\phi:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
be given. Denote
\underline{c}:=\min\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\underline{u}
,
\overline{c}:=\max\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\bar{u}
. Suppose that
\underline{u}\leq\bar{u}
on
\bar{\Omega}
and that there exists constant
M>0
such that

(4.12)

f(x,u)-f(x,v)\geq-M(u-v)~\forall x\in\overline{\Omega},~u,v\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}].

Then there exist sequences
\{\underline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
and
\{\overline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
such that
\{\underline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
is monotone nondecreasing with respect to
m
,
\{\overline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
is monotone nonincreasing with respect to
m
,

\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{m}\leq\overline{u}_{m}\leq\overline{u}~\forall m\geq 1,

and

\underline{u}_{m}\to\tilde{u},~\overline{u}_{m}\to\hat{u}~\text{as}~m\to+\infty,

where
\tilde{u}
and
\hat{u}
are the minimal and maximal solutions of (
1.11
) in the following sense: if
u
is a solution to (
1.11
) satisfying
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\bar{u}
on
\bar{\Omega}
, then
\tilde{u}\leq u\leq\hat{u}
on
\bar{\Omega}
.

Proof.

For any function
v\in<\underline{u},\bar{u}>:=\{v\in R\bar{\Omega}~|~\underline{u}(x)\leq v(x)\leq\bar{u}(x)~\text{on}~\bar{\Omega}\}
. Considering the following problem

(4.13)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}u+Mu=f(x,v)+Mv,~x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}u=\phi,~x\in\partial{\Omega}.\end{cases}

Now, we are going to prove that (
4.13
) has a unique solution. We first deal with the case that
\mathcal{B}u=u
.
Suppose that
u
satisfies (
4.13
).
Let
w=u-\phi
; then
w
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}w+Mw=f(x,v)+Mv-M\phi+\Delta_{{\Omega}}\phi=:g(x),~&x\in\Omega,\\
w=0,&x\in\partial{\Omega}.\end{cases}

If we define

(4.14)

B(u,v):=\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\left(\Gamma(u,v)+Muv\right)d\mu,~u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega}).

It follows from Cauchy Schwartz inequality and H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle|B(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\left[\sum_{y\in\bar{\Omega}:y\sim x}\frac{\omega_{xy}}{2\mu(x)}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x))\right]d\mu+M\left(\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}u^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}v^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}[\Gamma(u,u)]^{\frac{1}{2}}[\Gamma(v,v)]^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu+M||u||_{L^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}||v||_{L^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\bar{\Omega}}|\nabla v|^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+M||u||_{L^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}||v||_{L^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq(1+M)\left[\left(\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\bar{\Omega}}|\nabla v|^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+||u||_{L^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}||v||_{L^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+M)\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega})}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega})}.

In the light of (
4.14
) , we have

\displaystyle B(u,u)\geq\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Mu^{2}\right)d\mu\geq\min\{1,M\}\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu=\min\{1,M\}\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega})}.

It is easy to check that
B
is bilinear. Then, by the Lax-Milgram theorem, for any function
g:

\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique function
U\in H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
such that
B(U,v)=\int_{\bar{\Omega}}gvd\mu~\text{~for~any~}v\in H_{0}^{1}(\bar{\Omega}).

This is equivalent to

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}U+MU=g~\text{in}~\Omega,\\
U=0~\text{ on}~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

That is to say, (
4.13
) admits a unique solution
U
.

In the following, we consider the case that
\mathcal{B}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}
. Clearly, there exists a function
I(x)
such that

-\int\limits_{\Omega}I(x)d\mu=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\phi(x)d\mu.

Then, we use
[
10
, Theorem 3.8]
to conclude that the elliptic problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\Omega}Z(x)=I(x)&x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial Z}{\partial_{\Omega}n}(x)=\phi(x),&x\in\partial{\Omega}\end{cases}

admits a unique solution
Z(x)
.
Suppose that
u
satisfies (
4.13
). Let
w=u-Z
. It’s easy to check that
w
satisfies

(4.15)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\Omega}w(x)+Mw=f(x,v)+Mv-I-MZ=:F(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial w}{\partial_{\Omega}n}(x)=0,&x\in\partial{\Omega}.\end{cases}

We now consider the following expansions

w(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\Phi_{i}(x),~F(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}\Phi_{i}(x),~x\in\Omega,

where

c_{i}=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}w(x)\Phi_{i}(x),F_{i}=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega}F(x)\Phi_{i}(x).

As we substitute these expansions into (
4.15
), then it follows that

c_{i}=\frac{F_{i}}{K_{i}+M},~i=1,\cdots,n.

Therefore, we can conclude that

(4.16)

w(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{F_{i}(x)}{K_{i}+M}\Phi_{i}(x)

is a solution of (
4.15
). Applying Theorems
3.8
, we deduce that
w(x)
defined by (
4.16
) is the unique solution to (
4.15
). Thus
Z=u-w
is the unique solution to (
4.13
).
So that we can define an operator
T
by
u=Tv
.

Next, we first show that
T
is monotone. For any
v,w\in<\underline{u},\bar{u}>
and
v\leq w
. Let
z=Tw
,
u=Tv
; then
h=z-u
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}h+Mh=\left[f(x,w)-f(x,v)\right]+M(w-v)\geq 0,~&x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}h=0,&x\in\partial{\Omega}.\end{cases}

We use Theorem
3.8
to deduce that
h\geq 0
on
\bar{\Omega}
and hence that

u=Tv\leq z=Tw.

Define

\underline{u}_{1}=T\underline{u},~\underline{u}_{m+1}=T\underline{u}_{m},\overline{u}_{1}=T\overline{u},~\overline{u}_{m+1}=T\overline{u}_{m}

for
m=1,2,3,\cdots
. It follows from the monotonicity of
T
to deduce that
\underline{u}_{1}\leq\overline{u}_{1}
. Denote
v=\overline{u}-\overline{u}_{1}
. Then
v
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}v+Mv\geq f(x,\bar{u})+M\bar{u}-[f(x,\bar{u})+M\bar{u}]=0,~x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}v\geq 0,~x\in\partial{\Omega}.\end{cases}

Due to Theorem
3.8
, we obtain that

v\geq 0~\text{ on}~\bar{\Omega},

i.e,
\bar{u}\geq\bar{u}_{1}
. Similarly, we can show that
\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{1}
on
\bar{\Omega}
. Thus, by induction, we have

\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\underline{u}_{m}\leq\overline{u}_{m}\leq\cdots\leq\overline{u}_{1}\leq\bar{u}~\text{ on}~\bar{\Omega}

for all
m\geq 1
.

Therefore, we can define

\tilde{u}(x):=\lim\limits_{m\to+\infty}\underline{u}_{m}(x)

and

\hat{u}(x):=\lim\limits_{m\to+\infty}\overline{u}_{m}(x).

Letting
m\to+\infty
in the following elliptic problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{{\Omega}}+M)\underline{u}_{m+1}=M\underline{u}_{m}+f(x,\underline{u}_{m}),~x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}\underline{u}_{m+1}=\phi,~x\in\partial{\Omega}.\end{cases}

we obtain

\tilde{u}=T\tilde{u},~\hat{u}=T\hat{u}.

That is to say,
\tilde{u}
and
\hat{u}
are solutions of (
1.11
). Set
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
be a solution of (
1.11
). Then we have
u=Tu
. In view of
T
is nondecreasing, we see that

\underline{u}_{1}=T\underline{u}\leq Tu=u\leq T\overline{u}=\overline{u}_{1},

and thus, by induction,

\underline{u}_{m}=T\underline{u}_{m-1}\leq Tu=u\leq T\overline{u}_{m-1}=\overline{u}_{m}~\text{on}~\bar{\Omega}~\text{ for ~all~}m\geq 1.

As
m\to\infty
in (
4.2.1
), we obtain that

\tilde{u}\leq u\leq\hat{u}~\text{on}~\bar{\Omega}.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

Theorem
4.5
still holds with the assumption (
4.12
) replaced with

|f(x,u)-f(x,v)|\leq M|u-v|.

4.2.2.
The elliptic equation without boundary

In this subsection, we establish the upper and lower solutions method for the problem (
1.9
).

Definition 4.4
.

A function
u
is called an upper (a lower) solution of (
1.9
), if

-\Delta_{{V}}u-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u+c(x)u\geq(\leq)~f(x,u),~x\in V.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\bar{u}
and
\underline{u}
be an upper and a lower solution of (
1.9
) respectively. Denote
\underline{c}:=\min\limits_{V}\underline{u}
,
\overline{c}:=\max\limits_{V}\bar{u}
. Suppose that
\underline{u}\leq\bar{u}
on
V
and
b(x)\geq 0
on
V
and that there exists constant
M^{{}^{\prime}}>0
such that

(4.17)

|f(x,u)-f(x,v)|\leq M^{{}^{\prime}}|u-v|~\forall x\in V,~u,v\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}].

Then there exist sequences
\{\underline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
and
\{\overline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
such that
\{\underline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
is monotone nondecreasing with respect to
m
,
\{\overline{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{+\infty}
is monotone nonincreasing with respect to
m
,

\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{m}\leq\overline{u}_{m}\leq\overline{u}~\forall m\geq 1,

and

\underline{u}_{m}\to\tilde{u},~\overline{u}_{m}\to\hat{u}~\text{as}~m\to+\infty,

where
\tilde{u}
and
\hat{u}
are the minimal and maximal solutions of (
1.9
) in the following sense: if
u
is a solution to (
1.9
) satisfying
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\bar{u}
on
\bar{\Omega}
, then
\tilde{u}\leq u\leq\hat{u}
on
\bar{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Define

<\underline{u},\bar{u}>:=\{u\in RV|\underline{u}\leq u\leq\bar{u}~\text{ on}~V\}.

For any
v\in<\underline{u},\bar{u}>
, we choose a sufficiently large
M>M^{\prime}
such that
M+c_{0}-\frac{|\vec{b}|^{2}}{2}>1
with
c_{0}=\min\limits_{{V}}c(x)
and consider the following problem

(4.18)

-\Delta_{{V}}u-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u+c(x)u+Mu=f(x,v)+Mv,~x\in V.

Denote
E(x)=M+c(x)
. If we define a bilinear form
B[\cdot,\cdot]
by

B(u,w):=\int\limits_{V}\left[\Gamma(u,w)-\left(\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u\right)w+Euw\right]d\mu

for
~u,w\in H^{1}(V)
.
Then we use Cauchy Schwartz inequality and H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality to obtain that

\displaystyle|B(u,w)|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{V}\left[\Gamma(u,w)+\sqrt{\sum_{x\in V}b^{2}(x)}|\nabla u||w|+E|u||w|\right]d\mu

\displaystyle=||\nabla u||_{L^{2}(V)}||\nabla w||_{L^{2}(V)}+\sqrt{\sum_{x\in V}b^{2}(x)}||\nabla u||_{L^{2}(V)}||w||_{L^{2}(V)}+E||u||_{L^{2}(V)}||w||_{L^{2}(V)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+E+\sqrt{\sum_{x\in V}b^{2}(x)}\right)\|u\|_{H^{1}(V)}\|w\|_{H^{1}(V)}.

It’s obviously that

B(u,u)=\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Eu^{2}-u\vec{b}\cdot\nabla u\right)d\mu.

If we choose
\epsilon
small enough so that
\epsilon|b|=\frac{1}{2}
, by Cauchy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle B(u,u)

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Eu^{2}-|\vec{b}|\nabla u||u|\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+Eu^{2}\right)d\mu-|\vec{b}|\left(\int_{V}\epsilon|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}u^{2}d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{V}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\left(M+c_{0}-\frac{|\vec{b}|}{4\epsilon}\right)u^{2}\right]d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\frac{1}{2},M+c_{0}-\frac{|\vec{b}|^{2}}{2}\}\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu.

Denote
F(x):=f(x,v)+Mv
, we use Lax-Milgram theorem to find a unique function
u\in H^{1}(V)
such that

B(u,g)=\int\limits_{V}Fgd\mu\;\forall g\in H^{1}(V).

This is equivalent to
the problem (
4.18
) admits a unique solution
u
. Now, we can define an operator
T
by
u=Tv
and show that
T
is monotone. For any
v,w\in<\underline{u},\bar{u}>
satisfying
v\leq w
. Let
z=Tw
,
u=Tv
; then by (
4.17
), we can find
h=z-u
satisfies

(4.19)

-\Delta_{V}h-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla h+\left(c(x)+M\right)h=\left[f(x,w)-f(x,v)\right]+M(w-v)\geq 0,~x\in V.

It follows from Theorem
3.9
that
h\geq 0
on
V
and hence that

u=Tv\leq Tw=z.

Define

\underline{u}_{1}=T\underline{u},~\underline{u}_{m+1}=T\underline{u}_{m},\overline{u}_{1}=T\overline{u},~\overline{u}_{m+1}=T\overline{u}_{m}

for
m=1,2,3,\cdots
. By the monotonicity of
T
,
\underline{u}_{1}\leq\overline{u}_{1}
. Setting
v=\overline{u}-\overline{u}_{1}
; then we can find
v
satisfies

-\Delta_{V}v-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla v+\left(c(x)+M\right)v\geq f(x,\bar{u})+M\bar{u}-[f(x,\bar{u})+M\bar{u}]=0,~x\in V.

Applying Theorem
3.9
, we have

v\geq 0~\text{on}~V,

i.e,
\bar{u}\geq\bar{u}_{1}
on
V
. Similarly,
\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{1}
on
V
. Furthermore, by induction, we have

\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\underline{u}_{m}\leq\overline{u}_{m}\leq\cdots\leq\overline{u}_{1}\leq\bar{u}~\text{on}~V

for all
m\geq 1
.

Therefore, we can define

\tilde{u}(x):=\lim\limits_{m\to+\infty}\underline{u}_{m}(x)

and

\hat{u}(x):=\lim\limits_{m\to+\infty}\overline{u}_{m}(x).

Considering the elliptic problem

(-\Delta_{{V}}-\vec{b}\cdot\nabla+c(x)+M)\underline{u}_{m+1}=M\underline{u}_{m}+f(x,\underline{u}_{m}),

we obtain

\tilde{u}=T\tilde{u},~\hat{u}=T\hat{u}
as
m\to+\infty
.
Thus,
\tilde{u}
and
\hat{u}
are solutions of (
1.9
). Let
u\in<\underline{u},\bar{u}>
be a solution of (
1.9
); it’s obvious that
u
satisfies
u=Tu
. Since
T
is nondecreasing, we see that

\underline{u}_{1}=T\underline{u}\leq Tu=u\leq T\overline{u}=\overline{u}_{1},

and thus, by induction,

(4.20)

\underline{u}_{m}=T\underline{u}_{m-1}\leq Tu=u\leq T\overline{u}_{m-1}=\overline{u}_{m}~\text{on}~V

for all
m\geq 2
.
Letting
m\to\infty
in (
4.20
), we deduce that

\tilde{u}\leq u\leq\hat{u}~\text{on}~V.

∎

5.1.1.

In this subsection, we investigate the monotonicity and convergence of solutions of the problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}u=f(x,u),&x\in\Omega,\quad 0<t\lesssim T,\\

and
\varphi:\Omega:\to\mathbb{R}
are given functions and

.

In the following, the monotonicity of solutions to (
5.1

\varphi
is a subsolution or supersolution to (

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
f
satisfies (
4.1
) for any
\sigma,\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. If the initial data
\varphi
of (
5.1

Proof.

We only deal with the case that
\varphi
is a subsolution of (
1.11

\varphi
is a supersolution of (
1.11

\varphi
is a lower solution of (
5.1
). Applying Theorem
4.1
and the locally Lipschitz continuous of
f
in

, we deduce that
u(x,t)\geq\varphi(x)
for

and
0\leq t\lesssim T
. For
0<\epsilon<T
, it’s easy to check that
v(x,t)=u(x,\epsilon+t)
is a solution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}v_{t}-\Delta_{{\Omega}}v=f(x,v),&x\in\Omega,&0<t\lesssim T-\epsilon,\\
\mathcal{B}v=g(x),&x\in\partial\Omega,&0<t\lesssim T-\epsilon,\\

It follows from Theorem

that

,

.
∎

Next, we study the long time behavior of solutions of (

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that (
5.1
) admits a solution

Proof.

Note that

is monotone with respect to
t

(5.3)

v(x)=\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}u(x,t)~\text{for}~x\in\bar{\Omega}.

For any given function
\varphi(x):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and any

5.1

\varphi

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{t}\varphi-\varphi\Delta_{{\Omega}}u\right)d\mu dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u)\varphi}{T}d\mu dt.

In view of (

Taking

∎

Equivariant K K -theory of even-dimensional complex quadrics
Bidhan Paul

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to describe the torus equivariant K K -ring of even-dimensional complex quadrics by studying the graph equivariant K K -theory of their corresponding GKM graphs. This involves providing a presentation for its graph equivariant K K - ring in terms of generators and relations. This parallels the description of the equivariant cohomology ring of even-dimensional complex quadrics due to Kuroki.

1.
Introduction

A GKM manifold is an
equivariantly formal
manifold
M^{2k}
endowed with an action of a compact torus
T:=(S^{1})^{n}
. This action satisfies the condition that the set of
0
and
1
-dimensional orbits have the structure of a graph. This class of objects was initially introduced by Goresky, Kottwitz, and MacPherson in
[
6
]
as a class of algebraic varieties (GKM derives from their initials).

Inspired by their work, Guillemin and Zara
[
9
]
introduced the concept of an
abstract GKM graph
as an abstract graph with edges labeled by vectors in the dual of Lie algebra of
T
. In particular, an abstract GKM graph is a combinatorial counterpart of GKM manifolds and helps to study various topological and geometric properties of GKM manifolds (see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
11
]
) using combinatorial properties of GKM graph. On the other hand, abstract GKM graphs themselves have gathered attention beyond their geometric motivations (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
14
,
17
,
20
]
). These concepts gave rise to a rich field in mathematics commonly referred to as
GKM theory
. This theory has been explored from both geometric and combinatorial perspectives and has found applications in various areas, such as representation theory (e.g
[
2
]
).

In
[
6
]
, Goresky, Kottwitz, and MacPherson showed that the equivariant cohomology ring
H_{T}(M)
of a GKM pair
(M,\penalty\ T)
can be computed by studying the graph equivariant cohomology of the associated GKM graph
(\Gamma,\alpha)
. Later, Knutson and Rosu proved the much harder part
[
18
, Theorem A.4, Corollary A.5]
that the results remain true for the equivariant
K
- theory ring
K_{T}(M)
as well (see, (
2.4
)). It is noteworthy that although Knutson and Rosu primarily focused on equivariant
K
-theory with complex coefficients in
[
18
]
, the validity of (
2.4
) persists with integer coefficients due to the works of Vezzosi and Vistoli
[
19
, Corollary 5.11]
, which is an algebraic analogue of
[
18
, Theorem A.4]
(this fact is also remarked in
[
18
, p.447]
).

A complex quadric in dimension
2n
is defined to be the zero locus of a non-degenerate quadratic form in
2n+2
complex variables and can be represented as

Q_{2n}:=\Big\{[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2n+2}]\in\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum_{j=1}^{n+1}x_{i}x_{2n+3-i}=0\Big\}.

There exists the natural
T^{n+1}
- action on
Q_{2n}

(1.1)

[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2n+2}]\cdot(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1}):=[x_{1}t_{1}:\cdots:x_{n+1}t_{n+1}:t_{n+1}^{-1}x_{n+2}:\cdots:t_{1}^{-1}x_{2n+2}].

where
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\in T^{n+1}
. Since,
Q_{2n}
is diffeomorphic with the Grassmannian
\mathbb{R}_{2,2n}\cong SO(2n+2)/(SO(2n)\times SO(2))
of oriented 2-planes through the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2n+2}
, the above action is equivalent to a maximal torus
T^{n+1}
- action which is induced by restricting the transitive
SO(2n+2)
- action. Further, since
SO(2n)\times SO(2)\subset SO(2n+2)
is a subgroup of maximal rank (i.e
T^{n+1}
is also a maximal torus of
SO(2n+2)
), the set of
0
and
1
-dimensional orbits of
T^{n+1}
- action have the structure of a graph (see,
[
7
]
). Therefore, the GKM graph of
Q_{2n}
with
T^{n+1}
- action (
1.1
) can be determined by labeling the edges with tangential representations. Note that, the torus action in (
1.1
) is not effective as it has a non-trivial kernel
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\pm 1\}
. Alternatively, one can consider the
T^{n+1}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\penalty\ (\cong T^{n+1})
-action on
Q_{2n}
, which is effective (see, Section
3
).

In
[
15
,
16
]
, H. Lai studied the ordinary cohomology ring (over
\mathbb{Z}
) and homotopy groups of even-dimensional complex quadrics
Q_{2n}
explicitly. Since
H^{odd}(Q_{2n})=0
by
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
,
Q_{2n}
is an equivariantly formal GKM manifold (see,
[
6
]
). Therefore, the equivariant
K
-theory
K_{T^{n+1}}(Q_{2n})
(resp. equivariant cohomology
H_{T^{n+1}}(Q_{2n})
) of the effective
T^{n+1}
-action can be determined from the graph equivariant
K
- theory (resp. graph equivariant cohomology) of its GKM graph .

Recently, in
[
13
]
, S. Kuroki studied the GKM graph of
Q_{2n}
under the effective
T^{n+1}
- action and computed the graph equivariant cohomology of their GKM graphs. In particular, the author has provided a ring structure for the equivariant cohomology, presenting it in the form of generators and relations.

Our main aim in this paper is to determine the equivariant
K
- theory of the effective
T^{n+1}
- action on
Q_{2n}
by explicitly describing its generators and relations in terms of the GKM graphs. This parallels the description of the equivariant cohomology ring of even-dimensional complex quadrics due to Kuroki
[
13
]
.

This article is structured as follows :

In Section
2
, we provide an overview of fundamental concepts related to GKM manifolds, integral GKM graphs, and their

-ring. Moving on to Section
3
, we define even-dimensional complex quadrics

and present the associated GKM graph

-action.

In Section

and

of

and establish four types of relations among them. Additionally, we explore various properties of

and
\Delta_{P}

5.1

2.1.
An integral GKM graph and its
K
- ring

Let
\Gamma=({\mathcal{V}},\penalty\ {\mathcal{E}})
be a regular graph of degree

and

are the set of vertices and the set of oriented edges respectively.
For an oriented edge

, we define

(resp,

(resp,

to be the edge with direction opposite to

{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
be the set of edges whose initial vertex is
p
.

Definition 2.1

(Connection)
.

Let
e=(pq)\in{\mathcal{E}}
be an oriented edge. A bijection
\nabla_{e}:{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{q}
with
{\nabla_{e}}(e)=\overline{e}
is said to be a connection along
e
. Furthermore, we define a

on graph
\Gamma
to be a collection
\nabla:=\{\nabla_{e}\}_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}
satisfying
\nabla_{e}^{-1}=\nabla_{\overline{e}}
for every
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
.

Let
(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
be the weight lattice of torus
T
.

Definition 2.2

(Integral axial function)
.

Let
\nabla
be a connection on graph
\Gamma
. A map
\alpha:{\mathcal{E}}\to(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
is called a
\nabla
-compatible integral axial function
on
\Gamma
if it satisfies the following conditions :

(1)

\alpha(\overline{e})=-\alpha(e)
for all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
.

(2)

the vectors
\{\alpha(e)\,:\,e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\}
are pairwise linearly independent for all
p\in{\mathcal{V}}
.

(3)

for every edge
e=(pq)
and every
e^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
, there exists
m(e,e^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}
depending on
e,\,e^{\prime}
such that

\alpha(\nabla_{e}(e^{\prime}))-\alpha(e^{\prime})=m(e,e^{\prime})\alpha(e).

An
integral axial function
on
\Gamma
is a
\nabla
-compatible integral axial function for some connection
\nabla
on
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.3

(Integral GKM graph)
.

An integral GKM graph is a pair
(\Gamma,\alpha,\nabla)
or
(\Gamma,\alpha)
(if
\nabla
is obviously determined) which consists of a graph
\Gamma=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
and an integral axial function
\alpha:{\mathcal{E}}\to(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
.

Remark 2.4
.

For an integral GKM graph
(\Gamma,\alpha,\nabla)
, if every three-tuple of elements in
\alpha({\mathcal{E}}_{p})
is linearly independent for all vertices

, then
\nabla
is uniquely determined (see,
[
17
, p.453]
,
[

).

Let

be the representation ring of

which can be identified with the ring of finite sums

\sum_{k=1}^{l}m_{k}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\alpha_{k}}

for
m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha_{k}\in(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
. Let

be the ring of maps from the set of vertices to the representation ring of

.

Definition 2.5

(

- ring of an integral GKM graph)
.

An element

is said to be a

- class if for every edge

,

(2.2)

f(x)-f(y)=\beta(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\alpha(e)})

for some

.
Observe that the collection of all

-classes forms a subring of

, known as the
K
- ring
of

and denoted by

(see,
[
8
]
for further details).

Note that

has a

- module structure which is induced by the injective homomorphism
i:R(T)\to K_{\alpha}(\Gamma)
, where the image of

, denoted as

, is defined by

(2.3)

i(x)(v)=x\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all $v\in{\mathcal{V}}$.}

2.2.
K
-ring of GKM graphs induced from GKM manifolds

For a given GKM manifold
(M,T)
, one can define a graph
\Gamma_{M}:=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
with vertices
{\mathcal{V}}=M^{T}
. For
p,\,q\in M^{T}
, there is an oriented edge

if and only if there exists a
T
-invariant embedded 2-sphere

containing
p
and
q
as north and south poles respectively. The edges are labeled by a function
\alpha_{M}:{\mathcal{E}}\to(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
, known as

, which takes an edge
e=(pq)\in{\mathcal{E}}
to the weight of the isotropy representation
T_{p}S^{2}_{e}
of
T
.

Further, since
(M,T)
is a GKM manifold, a
connection

\nabla
can be canonically defined (see, Remark
2.4
) such that the above
\alpha_{M}:{\mathcal{E}}\to(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
is an integral axial function. In particular, the pair

is an integral GKM graph associated to the GKM manifold
(M,T)
(see,
[
9
, Section 1.1]
for further details).

Remark 2.6
.

Knutson and Rosu
[
18

proved that, for a GKM graph
(\Gamma_{M},\alpha_{M})
associated to the GKM pair
(M,T)
, the following isomorphisim holds

(2.4)

K_{T}(M)\simeq K_{\alpha_{M}}(\Gamma_{M}),

where

is the

- equivariant

- ring of

Knutson and Rosu have studied equivariant

- theory with complex coeffecients in
[

]

as well due to the works of Vezzosi and Vistoli
[

[

, Theorem A.4]
(this fact is also remarked in
[

Here, we provide an overview of why the GKM pair

Remark 2.7
.

For

, consider a 1-parameter subgroup

3.
Even dimensional complex quadrics and its GKM graph

In this section we define even dimensional complex quadrics and briefly recall their GKM graphs from
[
13
]
. We broadly follow the notation and conventions from that article.

Definition 3.1
.

An even dimensional complex quadrics is defined to be the following manifold

Q_{2n}:=\Big\{[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2n+2}]\in\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum_{j=1}^{n+1}x_{i}x_{2n+3-i}=0\Big\}.

There exists a natural action of
T^{n+1}
on
Q_{2n}
:

Q_{2n}\times T^{n+1}\longrightarrow Q_{2n}

(3.5)

[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2n+2}]\cdot(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1}):=[x_{1}t_{1}:\cdots:x_{n+1}t_{n+1}:t_{n+1}^{-1}x_{n+2}:\cdots:t_{1}^{-1}x_{2n+2}].

Note that,
Q_{2n}\cong SO(2n+2)/(SO(2n)\times SO(2))
. Hence the above action is equivalent to a maximal torus
T^{n+1}
- action (of
SO(2n+2)
), induced by restricting the

SO(2n+2)
- action by left multiplication.
Additionally,
T^{n+1}
is also a maximal torus of
SO(2n)\times SO(2)
. Therefore from
[
7
]
, the fixed points and one-dimensional orbits under
T^{n+1}
- action have the structure of a graph.

Since, the above action (
3.5
) is not effective due to its non-trivial kernel
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\pm 1\}
, we consider
T^{n+1}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\penalty\ (\simeq T^{n+1})
- action on
Q_{2n}
. In particular, we obtain the GKM graph of
Q_{2n}
(denoted by
\mathscr{Q}_{2n})
by labeling edges with tangential representations on (effective)
T^{n+1}
-fixed points.

Remark 3.2
.

Throughout the paper, we consider the cohomology ring

(3.6)

H^{*}(BT^{n+1})\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}].

The generators

k=1,\ldots,n+1
, has degree 2 and can be regarded as the
k
-th projection
\rho_{k}:T^{n+1}\to S^{1}
. In particular, we commonly use the following equivalences :

H^{2}(BT^{n+1})\cong(\mathfrak{t}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}\cong\mbox{Hom}(T^{n+1},S^{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}.

Further, we consider the representation ring of

as the following Laurent polynomial ring

(3.7)

R(T^{n+1})\cong\mathbb{Z}[y_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,y_{n+1}^{\pm 1}],

generated by 1-dimensional representations
y_{k}=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}x_{k}},\penalty\ y_{k}^{-1}=e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}x_{k}}

Remark 3.3

0=f(1)=h_{1}(1)+0+\cdots+0.

Hence,
h_{1}=0
.
Further, by using
h_{1}=0
in (
5.40
) and, for
({1}-M_{2})(2)=0
, we have

0=f(2)=h_{2}({1}-M_{1})(2)+0+\cdots+0.

Notice that
h_{2}(2),(1-M_{1})(2)\in\mathbb{Z}[M_{i},M_{i}^{-1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ i\in I_{2}]
(see, (
5.39
)) and
({1}-M_{1})(2)\not=0
. Hence
h_{2}(2)=0
since
\mathbb{Z}[M_{i},M_{i}^{-1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ i\in I_{2}]
is an integral domain. Therefore,
h_{2}=0
.

By following the similar arguements for
i=3,\ldots,n+1
, one can have

h_{3}=\cdots=h_{n+1}=0.

Therefore,

(5.42)

\begin{split}f=h_{n+2}\Delta_{\{n+2,\ldots,2n+2\}}+\cdots+h_{2n+1}\Delta_{\{2n+1,2n+2\}}+h_{2n+2}\Delta_{\{2n+2\}}+\mathfrak{I}\end{split}

Claim 2:

h_{i}=0
for all
n+2\leq i\leq 2n+2
.

From (
5.42
), we have the following for the vertex
n+2\in{\mathcal{V}}_{2n}
.

0=f(n+2)=h_{n+2}\Delta_{\{n+2,\ldots,2n+2\}}(n+2)+h_{n+3}\Delta_{\{n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}}(n+2)+\cdots+h_{2n+2}\Delta_{\{2n+2\}}(n+2).

Since

\begin{split}\Delta_{\{n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}}&(n+2)=\cdots=\Delta_{\{2n+2\}}(n+2)=0,\\
&\Delta_{\{n+2,\ldots,2n+2\}}(n+2)\not=0,\end{split}

we have
h_{n+2}=0
, by the similar reason as above. Iterating the similar arguements for other vertices
n+3,\ldots,2n+2\in{\mathcal{V}}_{2n}
, we have
h_{i}=0
for all
n+3\leq i\leq 2n+2
.

Therefore
f=0
, which shows the injectivity of
\Phi
.

Hence, the lemma follows.
∎

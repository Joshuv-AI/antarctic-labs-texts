Classification for positive singular solutions to critical sixth order equations Thanks: This research is partially supported by São Paulo Research Foundation (FAPESP) #2020/07566-3 and #2021/15139-0 and Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC)
João Henrique Andrade, Juncheng Wei

Abstract
. We classify entire positive singular solutions to a family of critical sixth order equations in the punctured space with a non-removable singularity at the origin. More precisely, we show that when the origin is a non-removable singularity, solutions are given by a singular radial factor times a periodic solution to a sixth order IVP with constant coefficients. On the technical level, we combine integral sliding methods and qualitative analysis of ODEs, based on a conservation of energy result, to perform a topological two-parameter shooting technique. We first use the integral representation of our equation to run a moving spheres technique, which proves that solutions are radially symmetric with respect to the origin. Thus, in Emden–Fowler coordinates, we can reduce our problem to the study of an sixth order autonomous ODE with constant coefficients. The main heuristics behind our arguments is that since all the indicial roots of the ODE operator are positive, it can be decomposed into the composition of three second order operators satisfying a comparison principle. This allows us to define a Hamiltonian energy which is conserved along solutions, from which we extract their qualitative properties, such as uniqueness, boundedness, asymptotic behavior, and classification.

into three second order operators.

Proposition 2.7
.

The following decomposition holds

(2.5)

P^{3}_{\rm rad}=L_{\lambda_{1}}\circ L_{\lambda_{2}}\circ L_{\lambda_{3}},

where

(2.6)

\lambda_{1}=\frac{n-6}{2},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{n-2}{2},\quad{\rm and}\quad\lambda_{3}=\frac{n+2}{2}.

Proof.

Initially, recall that for
q>6-n
, the following formula holds in the sense of distributions,

(-\Delta)^{3}(|x|^{q})=\prod_{j=0}^{3}(q-2j)\prod_{j=1}^{3}(q-2j+n)|x|^{q-6}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

which implies that
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\{1,\dots,6\}}\subset C^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is a basis for the space of radial solutions to the homogeneous tri-harmonic equation, where
u_{j}(|x|)=|x|^{q_{j}}
with
q_{j}=2(j-1)
if
j=1,2,3
and
2(j-m)-n
if

.
In other terms,
(-\Delta)^{3}u_{j}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
for any

Hence, under the Emden–Fowler change of variables, we have that
\{v^{\pm}_{1},v^{\pm}_{2},v^{\pm}_{3}\}\subset C^{6}(\mathbb{R})
, where
v^{\pm}_{j}(t)=e^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}t}
with
\lambda_{j}=q_{j}-\gamma_{n}
for
j=1,2,3
, forms a basis for the space of solutions to (

O
6

), that is,
\{v^{\pm}_{1},v^{\pm}_{2},v^{\pm}_{3}\}\subset C^{6}(\mathbb{R})
is linearly independent and
P^{3}_{\rm rad}v^{\pm}_{1}=P^{3}_{\rm rad}v^{\pm}_{2}=P^{3}_{\rm rad}v^{\pm}_{3}=0
in
\mathbb{R}
.
Then, the set of indicial roots of
\mathcal{I}(P^{3}_{\rm rad})=\{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{1}},\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{2}},\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{3}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
From this, it is straightforward to check that (

) holds.
∎

Conservation of energy

In this section, we find a quantity that is conserved along solutions to (

Let us start with the classical definition of Hamiltonian energy for a solution

Definition 2.8

)

Remark 2.11

For any

(3.1)

|D^{(j)}\eta_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon^{-j}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(u)\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(-\Delta)^{3}\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x.

such that

\left|(-\Delta)^{3}\xi_{\varepsilon}\right|\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon^{-6}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-6}{2}}\chi_{\{\varepsilon\leqslant|x|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\}}=C_{1}\varepsilon^{-6}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-6}{n+6}}\chi_{\{\varepsilon\leqslant|x|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\}},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(-\Delta)^{3}\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon^{-6}\int_{\{\varepsilon\leqslant|x|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\}}u\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-6}{n+6}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant\tilde{C}_{1}\varepsilon^{-6}\varepsilon^{\frac{12n}{n+6}}\left(\int_{\{\varepsilon\leqslant|x|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\}}f(u)\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n-6}{n+6}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\hat{C}_{1}\left(\int_{\{\varepsilon\leqslant|x|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\}}f(u)\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n-6}{n+6}}.

Thus, it follows

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(u)\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\hat{C}_{1}\left(\int_{\{\varepsilon\leqslant|x|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\}}f(u)\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n-6}{n+6}},

from which one can find a constant

(independent of
\varepsilon

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(u)\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C_{2}.

Now letting

For any nonnegative
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

P

6
,
∞

(3.2)

3.2.

We show that positive singular solutions to (

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in C^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})

6

∞

Proceeding similarly to Lemma

Let

6
,

\displaystyle 0\geqslant-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{\varepsilon}\phi f(u)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta^{2}\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}\phi\right)\Delta u\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta u\left(\Delta^{2}\phi\eta_{\varepsilon}+\varsigma^{\varepsilon}\right)\mathrm{d}x,

Integral moving spheres method

We prove the main result of this subsection, namely, the radial symmetry of the blow-up limit solutions.

Lemma 3.6

The following lemma states that solutions to (

Lemma 4.3

Let

Initially, by transforming

In what follows, we have two cases to study:

Case 1.

Next, we show that the other possibility cannot happen.

Case 2.

\mathcal{Z}_{+}(v)
is unbounded.

Now, since

, there exists an

such that

for all

In addition, because

Let

O
6

Initially, notice that by interchanging

Case 1:

\ell_{+}\in[0,\infty)\setminus\{0,a^{*}_{n}\}

By assumption, we have

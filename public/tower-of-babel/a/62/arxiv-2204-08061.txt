Positive solutions of quasilinear elliptic equations with Fuchsian potentials in Wolff class
Ratan Kr. Giri, Yehuda Pinchover

Abstract
. Using Harnack’s inequality and a scaling argument we study Liouville-type theorems and the asymptotic behaviour of positive solutions near an isolated singular point ζ ∈ ∂ Ω ∪ { ∞ } \zeta\in\partial\Omega\cup\{\infty\} for the quasilinear elliptic equation − div ( | ∇ u | A p − 2 A ∇ u ) + V | u | p − 2 u = 0 in Ω , -\text{div}(|\nabla u|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla u)+V|u|^{p-2}u=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega, where Ω \Omega is a domain in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , 1 < p < d 1<p<d , and A = ( a i ​ j ) ∈ L loc ∞ ​ ( Ω , ℝ d × d ) A=(a_{ij})\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}) is a symmetric and locally uniformly positive definite matrix. It is assumed that the potential V V belongs to a certain Wolff class and has a generalized Fuchsian-type singularity at an isolated point ζ ∈ ∂ Ω ∪ { ∞ } \zeta\in\partial\Omega\cup\{\infty\} .

1.
Introduction

This paper studies positive Liouville-type theorems and removable singularity theorems for the
(p,A)
-Laplacian type elliptic equation

(1.1)

Q(u)=Q_{p,A,V}(u):=-\Delta_{p,A}(u)+V|u|^{p-2}u=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega

near an isolated singular point
\zeta\in\partial\Omega\cup\{\infty\}
. Here
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 2)
with boundary
\partial\Omega
,
1<p<d
,
V
is a real valued potential belonging to a certain Wolff space
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
(see Definition
2.8
), and

\Delta_{p,A}(u):=\text{div}(|\nabla u|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla u)

is the
(p,A)
-Laplacian, where
A=(a_{ij})\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a symmetric and locally uniformly positive definite matrix valued function, and

|\xi|_{A}^{2}=|\xi|_{A(x)}^{2}:=A(x)\xi\cdot\xi=\!\!\!\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\;\;\;\;\;x\in\Omega,\;\xi=(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d})\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Liouville theorems and asymptotic behaviours of positive solutions of second-order quasilinear elliptic operators near an
isolated singular point

\zeta\in\partial\Omega\cup\{\infty\}
was studied extensively in the past few decades, see
[
8
,
9
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
25
,
28
]
and references therein. In particular, we mention the work of Frass and Pinchover
[
8
]
, where Liouville theorems and removable singularity theorems for positive solutions of (
1.1
) have been obtained for the entire range
1\!<\!p\!<\!\infty
under the assumptions that
A
is the identity matrix, and
V\!\in\!L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
has a
pointwise Fuchsian-type singularity at

\zeta\!\in\!\{0,\infty\}
, namely,

|V(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{p}}\qquad\mbox{near }\zeta.

Furthermore, in the same paper and in
[
9
]
, the asymptotic behaviour of the
quotient
of two positive solutions near the singular point
\zeta
has been established. The outcomes in
[
8
,
9
]
extend to the quasilinear case results obtained in
[
19
, and references therein]
for positive classical solutions of a second-order
linear
elliptic operators in a non-divergence form.

Recently, based on criticality theory for
Q_{p,A,V}
with
V
in a local Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
(see Definition
2.2
) established in
[
20
]
, the aforementioned results were further extended in
[
11
]
to the case where
A=(a_{ij})\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a symmetric and locally uniformly positive definite matrix valued function, and
V
belongs to
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
which has a generalized Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
. More precisely, let
\mathcal{A}_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:R/2\leq|x|<3R/2\}
.
V\in M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
is said to have a
generalized Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
if for every relative punctured neighbourhood
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
of
\zeta
, there exists a positive constant
C
and
R_{0}>0
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\||x|^{p-{d}/{q}}\,V\|_{M^{q}(p;\mathcal{A}_{R}\cap\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C&\text{if }p\neq d,\\[5.69054pt]
\|V\|_{M^{q}(d;\mathcal{A}_{R}\cap\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C&\text{if }p=d,\end{cases}

for all
0<R<R_{0}
if
\zeta=0
, and
R>R_{0}
if
\zeta=\infty
.

The results in
[
11
]
covers the full range of
1<p<\infty
as in
[
8
,
9
]
. Note that for
p>d
,
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)=L^{1}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
, which is mainly the weakest assumption for the well definiteness of weak solutions, and for ensuring the local Harnack inequality and the Hölder continuity of weak solutions of the equation
Q(u)=0
.

On the other and, by Lemma
2.4
and remarks
2.6
and
2.9
, the local Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
for
1<p<d
is a proper subset of the local Stummel-Kato class
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
(see Definition
2.3
), which in turn is a proper subset of the local Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
(see Definition
2.8
). It is important to remark here that when
1<p<d
, the assumption that
V\in M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
is the weakest to ensure the local Hölder continuity of weak solutions of (
1.1
)
[
20
]
. That is, if
V\in K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
or
V\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, then weak solutions of (
1.1
) may not be Hölder continuous, see
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
24
]
for counter examples.

The purpose of this paper is to extend the results obtained in
[
8
,
9
,
11
]
for
1<p<d
. We study the asymptotic behaviour of positive weak solutions near a Fuchsian-type singular point
\zeta
, and prove removable singularity theorems for positive solutions of (
1.1
) when the potential
V
lies in a local Wolff space and having a Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
(see Definition
4.1
). More precisely, after establishing the needful parts of criticality theory for
Q_{p,A,V}
with
V\!\in\!\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, we study the uniqueness (up to a multiplicative constant) of certain positive weak solutions and the asymptotic behaviour of the quotients of two positive weak solutions of the quasilinear elliptic equation (
1.1
) near the singular point
\zeta
. Our main tool is a uniform Harnack inequality and a scaling argument using the quasi-invariance of (
1.1
) under the map
x\mapsto Rx
, where
x\in\Omega
and
R>0
. The novelty of this work lies in the fact that
V
is assumed to be in the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
which implies that solutions of (
1.1
) are not necessarily Hölder continuous. Another difficulty arises since
\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)
is not a Banach space for
p>2
for subdomains
\omega\Subset\Omega
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall various function spaces such as Morrey, Kato and Wolff spaces, and provide basic results related to these spaces. Section
3
is devoted to criticality theory for the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
with
V
in the local Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
. In particular, we prove the corresponding Harnack convergence principle, weak maximum, and weak comparison principles. Finally, in Section
4
, we introduce the notion of a Fuchsian-type singularity, discuss some related notions, and prove the main results of the paper. In particular, we present a uniform Harnack inequality and a ratio limit theorem for positive solutions defined near the singular point
\zeta
. Finally, we obtain a positive Liouville-type theorem under the further assumption that
Q
has a
weak
Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
.

2.
Morrey, Kato and Wolff spaces

We begin with some standard notation. Let
\Omega
be a domain (i.e., a nonempty open connected set) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. We denote the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
r>0
centered at
x
by
B_{r}(x)
. Similarly,
S_{r}(x):=\partial B_{r}(x)
denotes the sphere of radius
r>0
centered at
x
, and we set
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
,
S_{r}:=S_{r}(0)
. Further, denote by
B_{r}^{*}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B_{r}}
and
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
the corresponding exterior domains.

Let
f,g\in C(\Omega)
be two positive functions. By
f\asymp g
in
\Omega
, we mean that there exists positive constant
C
such that

C^{-1}g(x)\leq f(x)\leq Cg(x)\quad\text{ for all }\,x\in\Omega.

We write
\Omega_{1}\Subset\Omega_{2}
if
\Omega_{2}
is open and
\overline{\Omega}_{1}
is compact (proper) subset of
\Omega_{2}
. By a
compact exhaustion
of a domain
\Omega
, we mean a sequence
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of smooth, relatively
compact domains in
\Omega
such that
\Omega_{1}\neq\emptyset
,
\Omega_{i}\Subset\Omega_{i+1}
, and

\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{i}=\Omega
.
Finally, throughout the paper,
C
refers to a positive constant which may vary from line to line.

In this section we introduce various function spaces such as Morrey, Kato and Wolff spaces, and discuss the relationships between them.

2.1.
Morrey spaces

We recall certain classes of Morrey spaces.

Definition 2.1

(Morrey spaces)
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, for
q\in[1,\infty]
, we say that
f
belongs to the local Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(\Omega)
if for any
\omega\Subset\Omega

\|f\|_{M^{q}(\omega)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\omega\\
0<r<\text{diam}(\omega)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r^{d/q^{\prime}}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}(y)}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

where
q^{\prime}\!=\!q/(q\!-\!1)
is the Hölder conjugate exponent of
q
. An application of Hölder inequality implies that
L^{q}_{\loc}(\Omega)\!\subsetneq\!M^{q}_{\loc}(\Omega)\!\subsetneq\!L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
for any
q\!\in\!(1,\infty)
.

Next we recall a special local Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
which depends on the underlying exponent
1<p<\infty
.

Definition 2.2

(Local Morrey spaces)
.

Let

M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega):=\begin{cases}M^{q}_{\loc}(\Omega)\mbox{ with }q>d/p&\mbox{ if }p<d,\\
L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)&\mbox{ if }p>d,\end{cases}

while for
p=d
, a function
f\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is said to belong to the Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(d;\Omega)
if for some
q>d
and any
\omega\Subset\Omega

\|f\|_{M^{q}(d;\omega)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\omega\\
0<r<\text{diam}(\omega)\end{subarray}}\varphi_{q}(r)\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}(y)}|f|\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

where
\varphi_{q}(r)\!:=\!\log^{q/d^{\prime}}\!\!\left(\!\!\frac{\text{diam}(\omega)}{r}\!\!\right)\!
(see
[
18
, Theorem 1.94]
, and references therein).

2.2.
Stummel-Kato class

Next, we introduce the
Stummel-Kato
class of locally integrable functions for
1<p<d
.

Definition 2.3

(
Stummel-Kato class)
.

Let
1<p<d
. We say that
V\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
belongs to the class
\bar{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
if for any
\omega\Subset\Omega

\eta_{V,\omega}(r):=\sup_{x\in\omega}\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}(x)}\frac{|V(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-p}}\>\!\mathrm{d}y<\infty\qquad\forall\,0<r<\diam(\omega).

A function
V\in\bar{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is said to belong to the local Stummel-Kato class
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
if for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
we have
\displaystyle{\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\eta_{V,\omega}(r)=0}
. (see for example,
[
7
, Definition 1.1]
for
p=2
, and
[
24
, Definition 2.1]
for
1<p<d
). If
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
we write
\eta_{V}(r):=\eta_{V,\mathbb{R}^{d}}(r)
.

We call
\eta_{V,\omega}(r)
the
Stummel-Kato modulus
of
V
in
\omega
. It is known that
\eta_{V,\omega}(r)
is a nondecreasing continuous function of
r
with the doubling property
[
24
]
, i.e., there exists constant
\mathcal{K}\in(0,1)
such that

\mathcal{K}\eta_{V,\omega}(2r)\leq\eta_{V,\omega}(r)\qquad\forall\,r>0.

The constant
\mathcal{K}
is called the doubling constant of
\eta_{V,\omega}
. The Stummel-Kato class
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is equipped with the seminorms given by

\|V\|_{K^{p}(\omega)}:=\eta_{V,\omega}(\diam(\omega)),

for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, and therefore,
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is a Fréchet space. In fact, for any
\omega\Subset\Omega
and a fixed
0<r<\diam(\omega)
,
\eta_{V,\omega}(r)
defines a semi-norm in
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
and
\eta_{V,\omega}(r)\leq\|V\|_{K^{p}(\omega)}
.

When
p=2
,
K^{2}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is the classical Kato class which was introduced by Kato
[
13
]
to study the self-adjointness of Schrödinger operators. Aizenman and Simon
[
1
]
for
A=I
, and later Chiarenza et al.
[
5
]
established for
p=2
a local Harnack inequality and the continuity of solutions for the Schrödinger-type equation
-\mathrm{div}(A\nabla u)+Vu=0
, where
A
is a symmetric matrix such that
A\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is locally uniformly elliptic, and
V

belongs to the
Stummel-Kato
class. For
1<p<d
, the higher order
Stummel-Kato
class potentials can be traced back to Davies and Hinz work
[
6
]
. In general, the study of higher-order Schrödinger operators with potentials in
Stummel-Kato class

K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
turns out to be more difficult than in the case
p=2
. More details about the
Stummel-Kato
class and related theory in PDEs can be found for example in Rasgusa and Zamboni
[
24
]
, Zamboni
[
29
]
, Zheng and Yao
[
30
]
, and in references therein.

Lemma
2.4
below asserts that for
1<p<d
, the local Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
is a (proper) subset of
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
. For a proof see
[
18
, Lemma 1.29]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1<p<d
and
f\in M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
with
q>d/p
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for
0<r<\diam(\omega)

\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}(x)}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-p}}\>\!\mathrm{d}y\leq Cr^{p-d/q}\|f\|_{M^{q}(\omega)}.

We next give a subclass of
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
in which each
Stummel-Kato modulus

\eta_{V,\omega}(r)
of
V\in K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
satisfies a particular integral condition near zero.

Definition 2.5

(See
[
24
,
29
]
)
.

Let
1<p<d
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and let
\theta:={p}/{(\sigma p^{\prime}+p)}
, so,
0<1-\theta=\frac{\sigma}{\sigma+p-1}<1
. A function
V\in K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is said to belong to the class
\tilde{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
if there exists
\delta>0
such that

\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\eta_{V}^{1-\theta}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\>\!\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Remark 2.6
.

Clearly,
\tilde{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)\subset K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)\subset\bar{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
. Also, it can be seen that for
1<p<d
the local Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
is in fact, a proper subspace of the class
\tilde{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
. Indeed, if
f\in M^{q}(p;\omega)
, then by Lemma
2.4
, we have
\eta_{V}^{1-\theta}(s)\leq Cs^{(p-d/q)(1-\theta)}\|f\|^{1-\theta}_{M^{q}(\omega)}
. Hence for
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{1}{t}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\eta_{V}^{1-\theta}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\!\!\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\|f\|^{(1-\theta)/p}_{M^{q}(\omega)}\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}s^{(p-d/q)(1-\theta)-1}ds\right)^{1/p}\!\!\!\!\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=C\|f\|^{(1-\theta)/p}_{M^{q}(\omega)}\int_{0}^{\delta}t^{\frac{(p-d/q)(1-\theta)}{p}-1}\>\!\mathrm{d}t=C\|f\|^{(1-\theta)/p}_{M^{q}(\omega)}\delta^{\frac{(p-d/q)(1-\theta)}{p}}<\infty.

The next result is an uncertainty-type inequality due to Ragusa and Zamboni
[
24
, Corollaries 2.6, 2.7]
(see also
[
29
, Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.2]
).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
1<p<d
,
0<\theta<1
as above, and
\omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that a function
V\in K^{p}(\omega)
satisfies

\int_{0}^{\varrho}\frac{\eta_{V}^{1-\theta}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty\qquad\mbox{for some $\varrho>0$},

and let

\Phi(r):=C(d)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\eta_{V}^{1-\theta}(t)}{t}\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

Then

(i).

There exists a constant
C(d,p)
such that

\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}}|V(x)||u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(d,p)\Phi(r)\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

for every
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(w)
with
\mbox{\rm supp}\,(u)\subseteq\omega\cap B_{r}
.

(ii).

For any
\delta>0
the following inequality holds true for any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega)

\int_{\omega}|V(x)||u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta\int_{\omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C_{1}(\diam(\omega))\delta}{\left[\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{\delta}{C(d,p,\eta_{V})}\right)\right]^{d+p}}\int_{\omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using a standard density argument one can replace in Theorem
2.7
the space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega)
with
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
. Theorem
2.7
is an extension of the Morrey-Adams theorem to the Kato class (see for example
[
20
,
11
]
and references therein, where the potential
V
is assumed to be in the local Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
).
Using Theorem
2.7
and the Moser iteration technique, Ragusa and Zamboni
[
24
]
proved the local boundedness of weak solutions and the validity of Harnack inequality for nonnegative weak solutions of the equation
-\Delta_{p}u+V|u|^{p-2}u=0
for
V\in\tilde{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
. Hence, as a consequence of the Harnack inequality, the local continuity of weak solutions is obtained (see
[
24
, Theorem 6.1]
). Note that if the potential
V
lies in the Morrey space
M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
with
1<p<d
, then weak solutions are in fact Hölder continuous (see Malý and Ziemer
[
18
, Theorem 4.11]
and also Di Fazio
[
7
]
).

2.3.
Wolff class

In this subsection, we introduce another space of locally integrable functions for
1<p<d
which is known as the
Wolff class
.

Definition 2.8

(Wolff class)
.

Let
1<p<d
. We say that
f\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
belongs to the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
if for every
\omega\Subset\Omega

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}W_{f}(r):=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\left(\sup_{x\in\omega}\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-p}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{s}\right)=0.

We call
W_{f}(r)

the Wolff modulus of

f
. Note that we should consider
d\geq 2
when
1<p<2
, and
d\geq 3
when
p=2
. For
p=2
, by
[
18
, Lemma 1.27]
we have

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{s^{d-1}}\left(\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{(d-2)}\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}(x)}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Thus,
\mathfrak{W}^{2}_{\loc}(\Omega)=K^{2}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, i.e., for
p=2
the Wolff space
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
is in fact the standard Kato class. The following remark gives a relationship between the Kato class
K^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
and the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
for
p\in(1,d)
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
1<p<d
and
r>0
be sufficiently small. Then

\displaystyle W_{f}(r)

\displaystyle\!=\!\sup_{x\in\omega}\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-p}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\>\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle\!\leq\!\sup_{x\in\omega}\!\int_{0}^{r}\!\!\left[\!\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}\!\!\frac{|f(y)|}{|x\!-\!y|^{d-p}}\!\,\mathrm{d}y\!\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\!\!\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{s}\!\leq\!\int_{0}^{r}\!\frac{\eta_{f,\omega}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(s)}{s}\!\,\mathrm{d}s\!\leq\!\int_{0}^{r}\!\frac{\eta_{f,\omega}^{1-\theta}(s)}{s}\!\,\mathrm{d}s,

since

1-\theta=\frac{\sigma}{\sigma+p-1}<\frac{1}{p-1}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{with }0<\sigma<1.

This implies that if
f\in K_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
and for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\eta_{f,\omega}^{1-\theta}(s)}{s}\>\!\mathrm{d}s<\infty
, then
f
is in the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.10
.

For
1<p\leq 2
and
0<r\leq\diam(\omega)
, the expression

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)}:=W_{f}^{p-1}(r)=\left[\sup_{x\in\omega}\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-p}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\>\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}\right]^{p-1}

defines a norm on the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)
. Moreover,
(\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega),\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

We will show only the triangle inequality. Since
1<p\leq 2
implies
\frac{1}{p-1}\geq 1
, the Minkowski inequality implies that

\displaystyle\|f_{1}+f_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)}=\left[\sup_{x\in\omega}\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-p}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f_{1}(y)+f_{2}(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\>\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}\right]^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\omega}\left[\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-1}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f_{1}(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{s^{d-1}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f_{2}(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\>\!\mathrm{d}s\right]^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\omega}\left[\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-1}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f_{1}(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\!\,\mathrm{d}s\right]^{p-1}

\displaystyle+\sup_{x\in\omega}\left[\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-1}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f_{2}(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\!\!\>\!\mathrm{d}s\right]^{p-1}=\|f_{1}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)}+\|f_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)}.

Following
[
3

, we infer that the Wolff space
\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)
with the norm

is a Banach space for
1<p\leq 2
.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

For

, the expression

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)}:=W_{f}^{p-1}(r)=\left[\sup_{x\in\omega}\int_{0}^{r}\left[\frac{1}{s^{d-p}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{s}(x)}|f(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\>\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}\right]^{p-1}

.

Proof.

If

. Using the same argument as in the proof of Lemma

(2.1)

\|f_{1}+f_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)}\leq 2^{p-2}\bigg(\|f_{1}\|_{\mathfrak{W}_{p}(\omega)}+\|f_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)}\bigg),

where we use the following two inequalities with

∎

Theorem 2.14

(Morrey-Adams type theorem)
.

3.
Criticality theory for
Q_{p,A,V}
with potential in Wolff class

The present section is devoted to criticality theory for weak positive solutions of the equation (
1.1
) with potentials in the local Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
.

3.1.
The setting

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain, and fix
1<p<d
. Throughout the paper we assume that the operator
Q=Q_{p,A,V}
satisfies the following local regularity assumptions.

Assumptions 3.1
.

•

A\!=\!(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}\!\in\!L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a symmetric matrix valued function.

•

A
is locally uniformly elliptic in
\Omega
, that is, for any
\omega\Subset\Omega
there exists
\Theta_{\omega}>0
such that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x\in\omega

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt(\Theta_{\omega})^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{i}^{2}\leq|\xi|_{A(x)}^{2}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Theta_{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{i}^{2}.

•

V\in\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
is a real valued function.

By Assumptions
3.1
, the function
\xi\mapsto|\xi|_{A(x)}^{p}
is
strictly convex
a.e.
x\in\Omega
. At some points, we assume that
|\xi|_{A}^{p}
is locally strong convexity.

Assumption 3.2
.

We say that
|\xi|_{A}^{p}
is a locally strong convex function with respect to
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
if there exists
\bar{p}\geq p
such that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
there exists a positive constant
C_{\omega}(\bar{p})
such that

|\xi|_{A}^{p}-|\eta|_{A}^{p}-p|\eta|_{A}^{p-2}A(x)\eta\cdot(\xi-\eta)\geq C_{\omega}(\bar{p})|\xi-\eta|_{A}^{\bar{p}}\quad\forall\,\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and a.e. }x\in\omega.

In fact, by
[
21
, Lemma 3.4]
and
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
, there exists a constant
C(p)>0
such that for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
\eta\neq 0
if
p<2
) and a.e.
x\in\omega
,

(3.1)

|\xi|^{p}_{A}-|\eta|^{p}_{A}-p|\eta|^{p-2}_{A}A(x)\eta\cdot(\xi-\eta)\geq C(p)[\xi,\eta]_{p,A},

where

[\xi,\eta]_{p,A}\triangleq\begin{cases}|\xi-\eta|_{A}^{p}&\text{if~$p\geq 2$},\\
|\xi-\eta|_{A}^{2}(|\xi|_{A}+|\eta|_{A})^{p-2}&\text{if~$1<p<2$}.\end{cases}

Definition 3.3
.

A function
v
is said to be a (weak) solution of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
if
v\in W_{\loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
v
satisfies

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla v|^{p-2}_{A}A\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi+V|v|^{p-2}v\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

We say the
v\in W_{\loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a supersolution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
, if the integral in (
3.2
) is nonnegative for every nonnegative test function
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. A function
v
is a subsolution of (
1.1
) if
-v
is supersolution of (
1.1
).

Theorem
2.12
implies that the integral in (
3.2
) is well defined. Note that (
1.1
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation associated with the energy functional

\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(\varphi)=\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(\varphi;\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\varphi|^{p}_{A}+V|\varphi|^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Throughout the paper we write
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\geq 0
in
\Omega
if
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

3.2.
Harnack convergence principle

In this subsection we prove the Harnack convergence principle. First we recall the local Harnack inequality for nonnegative weak solutions of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
for
1<p<d
proved in
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
. We also refer to
[
26
, Theorem 2.2]
, where the local Harnack inequality of nonnegative solutions is proved for certain quasilinear equations with coefficients in Wolff class. The Harnack inequality of positive solutions is further extended in
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
for a certain class of divergence-type elliptic equations with nonstandard growth conditions and coefficients from appropriate Wolff classes.

Theorem 3.4

(Local Harnack inequality)
.

Let
1<p<d
and
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\omega\Subset\Omega
. Then under Assumptions
3.1
, any nonnegative solution
v
of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
satisfies

\sup_{\omega^{\prime}}v\leq C\inf_{\omega^{\prime}}v,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only
d,p,\Theta_{\omega}
,
W_{V}(\diam(\omega))
and
\mbox{\rm dist}\,(\omega^{\prime},\omega)
, but not on
v
.

We recall the weak Harnack inequality which is valid for nonnegative supersolutions of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
. For the proof see
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 3.5

(Weak Harnack inequality)
.

Let
1<p<d
and
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\omega\Subset\Omega
. Then under Assumptions
3.1
, for every
0<q<\frac{d(p-1)}{d-p}
any nonnegative supersolution
v
of the equation of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
satisfies

\|v\|_{L^{q}(\omega^{\prime})}\leq C\inf_{\omega^{\prime}}v,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
d
,
p
,
q
,
\Theta_{\omega}
,
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\omega^{\prime})
,
\mbox{\rm dist}\,(\omega^{\prime},\omega)
and
W_{V}(\diam(\omega))
. Here
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\omega^{\prime})
is the Lebesgue measure of
\omega^{\prime}
.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.4
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.6

(Local continuity)
.

Let
1<p<d
. Then under Assumptions
3.1
, any solution
v
of (
1.1
) is continuous and has the following bound on its modulus of continuity: if
B_{r_{0}}:=B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, then there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending on
\beta
, and positive constants
C=C\big(d,p,\Theta_{B_{r_{0}}},W_{V}(r_{0})\big)
,
\gamma=\gamma\big(\>\!\mathrm{d},p,\Theta_{B_{r_{0}}},W_{V}(r_{0})\big)
, such that

\underset{B_{r}}{\osc}\,v\leq C\left[\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\alpha}\underset{B_{r_{0}}}{\osc}\,v+\gamma W_{V}(r_{0}^{1-\beta}r^{\beta})\right]\quad\forall\,0<r<r_{0}.

Proof.

Since the solution
v
of (
1.1
) is locally bounded
[
2
, Theorem 3.5]
, hence
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{0}})}<\infty
. For
0<r<r_{0}
, define

M(r):=\sup_{B_{r}}v,\quad m(r):=\inf_{B_{r}}v,\quad\tau(r):=\underset{B_{r}}{\osc}\,v=M(r)-m(r).

Following the arguments in
[
18
, Theorem 4.11]
or in
[
10
, Theorem 8.22]
, one obtains that there exists a positive constant
\vartheta=\vartheta(d,p,\Theta_{B_{r_{0}}},W_{V}(r_{0}))\in(0,1)
, such that

\tau\bigg(\frac{r}{2}\bigg)\leq\vartheta\tau(r)+\gamma W_{V}(r)\qquad\forall\,0<r<r_{0},

where
\gamma>0
is a constant depending on
\>\!\mathrm{d},p,\Theta_{B_{r_{0}}},W_{V}(r_{0})
(see
[
17
, Corollary 1.5.]
for
p(x)\equiv p
).

Then by applying
[
18
, Lemma 4.12]
, or
[
10
, Lemma 8.23]
, it follows that for any
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exist a positive constant
C=C(\vartheta)>0
and

such that

\tau(r)\leq C\bigg[\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\alpha}\tau(r_{0})+\gamma W_{V}(r_{0}^{1-\beta}r^{\beta})\bigg]\qquad\forall\,0<r<r_{0}.

This implies the continuity of solutions since
W_{V}(r)\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

When the potential
V
lies in the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
or in the Stummel-Kato class
\tilde{K}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, one should not expect Hölder continuity of solutions of (

) (see for example,
[
5
,
24
]
).

The following version of the Harnack convergence principle will be used several times throughout the paper.

Theorem 3.8

(Harnack convergence principle)
.

Let
\{\Omega_{i}\}
be a compact exhaustion of the domain
\Omega
. Suppose that
\{A_{i}\}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a sequence of symmetric and locally uniformly positive definite matrices such that their local ellipticity constants are uniformly bounded away from
0
, and
\{A_{i}\}
converges weakly in
L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
to
\mathbb{A}\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfying Assumptions
3.1
. Furthermore, assume that

(i)

for
1<p\leq 2
,
\{V_{i}\}\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\Omega_{i})
converges weakly in the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
to
\mathbb{V}\in\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
,

(ii)

for
p>2
,
\{V_{i}\}\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\Omega_{i})
converges with respect to the quasinorm of the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
to
\mathbb{V}\in\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
v_{i}
be a positive (continuous) solution of
Q_{p,A_{i},V_{i}}(v)=0
in
\Omega_{i}
with
v_{i}(x_{0})=1
, where
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}
is a fixed reference point.

Then, up to a subsequence,
\{v_{i}\}
converges weakly in
W^{1,p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
and also locally uniformly to a positive solution
v
of the equation
Q_{p,\mathbb{A},\mathbb{V}}(v)=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

(i) Assume first that
1<p\leq 2
. So,
\{V_{i}\}\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\Omega_{i})
converges weakly in the Wolff class
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
to
\mathbb{V}\in\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
. The sequence
\{A_{i}\}
of matrices is locally uniformly elliptic and converges in the weak topology of
L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, so
\|A_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{\omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}\leq C
for every
\tilde{\omega}\Subset\Omega
. Therefore, the matrices
A_{i}
are locally uniformly positive definite matrices which are uniformly bounded in every
\tilde{\omega}\Subset\Omega
except for a set of measure zero.

Since
v_{i}
is a positive weak solution of the equation
Q_{p,A_{i},V_{i}}(v)=0
in
\Omega_{i}
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{i}}|\nabla v_{i}|_{A_{i}}^{p-2}A_{i}\nabla v_{i}\cdot\nabla u\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{i}}V_{i}v_{i}^{p-1}u\,\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\text{for all}\,\,u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{i}).

Fixing a natural number
k
and
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{k})
, we may plug
v_{i}|u|^{p}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{k})
,
i\geq k
, as a test function in the above equality to obtain

\|\,|\nabla v_{i}|_{A_{i}}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})}\leq p\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla v_{i}|_{A_{i}}^{p-1}|u|^{p-1}v_{i}|\nabla u|_{A_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{k}}|V_{i}|v_{i}^{p}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Applying Young’s inequality
pab\!\leq\!\varepsilon a^{p^{\prime}}\!+\![(p\!-\!1)/\varepsilon]^{p-1}b^{p}
,
\varepsilon\!\in\!(0,1)
, with
a\!=\!|\nabla v_{i}|_{A_{i}}^{p-1}|u|^{p-1}
and
b\!=\!v_{i}|\nabla u|_{A_{i}}
on the first term, and Morrey-Adams theorem (Theorem
2.13
) for the second term of the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\||\nabla v_{i}|_{A_{i}}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p-1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\!\!\|v_{i}|\nabla u|_{A_{i}}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})}+\delta\|\nabla(v_{i}u)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{\delta}\|v_{i}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})},

where
C_{\delta}=C\big(d,p,\delta,W_{V_{i}}(\diam(\Omega_{k+1}))\big)
.
Since the sequence
\{A_{i}\}
is locally uniformly elliptic and bounded a.e., therefore, using the inequality

\|\nabla(v_{i}u)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\|v_{i}\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\nabla v_{i}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),

we obtain the following estimates for every
i\geq k
and any

\displaystyle\left((1\!-\!\varepsilon)C_{\Omega_{k}}^{p}\!-\!2^{p-1}\delta C_{\Omega_{k}}^{-p}\right)\!\||\nabla v_{i}|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\!\!\left(\!\frac{p\!-\!1}{\varepsilon}\!\right)^{p\!-\!1}C^{-p}_{\Omega_{k}}\!+\!2^{p-1}\delta\!\right)\!\|v_{i}|\nabla u|\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})}\!\!+\!C_{\delta}\|v_{i}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})}.

Pick an arbitrary subdomain
\omega\Subset\Omega
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{0}\in\omega
. Now for a subdomain
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
with
\omega\Subset\omega^{\prime}
, there exists
k\geq 1
such that
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega_{k}
. Let
0<\delta<(1-\varepsilon)2^{1-p}C^{2p}_{\Omega_{k}}
and specialize
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{k})
such that

(3.4)

\text{supp}\{u\}\subset\omega^{\prime},\,0\leq u\leq 1,\,\,u=1\,\text{in}\,\omega,\text{ and }|\nabla u|\leq\frac{1}{\text{dist}(\omega,\partial\omega^{\prime})}\,\,\text{in}\,\,\omega^{\prime}.

Our assumption on the weak convergence of
\{V_{i}\}
in
\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
implies that
\{W_{V_{i}}(\diam(\omega^{\prime}))\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
. Hence, by the local Harnack inequality, the sequence of solutions
\{v_{i}\}
is bounded in

. Also, by Corollary
3.6
, the solutions
\{v_{i}\}
have a common modulus of continuity in
\omega

\{v_{i}\}
converges uniformly in
\omega
. Moreover, using

as in (

\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|v_{i}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq C\big(d,p,\varepsilon,\delta,\text{dist}(\omega,\partial\omega^{\prime}),C_{\Omega_{k}},W_{V_{i}}(\diam(\Omega_{k+1}))\big),

for every

. This shows that the sequence
\{v_{i}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
. Hence, up to a subsequence,
\{v_{i}\}
converges weakly to a nonnegative function
v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\omega)
with

. Since up to subsequence,
\{v_{i}\}
converges uniformly in
\omega

\displaystyle v_{i}\rightarrow v\quad\text{ uniformly in }\omega,\quad\text{ and }\nabla v_{i}\rightharpoonup\nabla v\,\,\text{in}\,L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Claim:

v
is a weak solution of
Q_{\mathbb{A},p,\mathbb{V}}(u)=0
in
\tilde{\omega}\Subset\omega
. Indeed, using the uniform convergence of
\{v_{i}\}
to
v
in
\omega
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\tilde{\omega}}(V_{i}v_{i}^{p-1}\varphi-\mathbb{V}v^{p-1}\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tilde{\omega}}|V_{i}|\,|v_{i}^{p-1}-v^{p-1}|\,|\varphi|\,\mathrm{d}x+\left|\int_{\tilde{\omega}}(V_{i}-\mathbb{V})v^{p-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{i}-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{\omega})}\int_{\tilde{\omega}}|V_{i}|\,\mathrm{d}x+\left|\int_{\tilde{\omega}}(V_{i}-\mathbb{V})v^{p-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{\omega}).

Since
\{W_{V_{i}}(\diam(\omega^{\prime}))\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
, the sequence
\{V_{i}\}
is bounded in
L_{\loc}^{1}(\Omega)
(see Corollary
2.15
). Thus, the first term of the above inequality converges to zero, while the second term converges to zero by our assumption on the weak convergence of
\{V_{i}\}
to
\mathbb{V}
.
Therefore, from (
3.5
), we get

\int_{\tilde{\omega}}V_{i}v_{i}^{p-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\mathbb{V}v^{p-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{\omega}).

On the other hand, the sequence
\{A_{i}\}
of matrices converges weakly in
L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
to
\mathbb{A}\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\nabla v_{i}\rightharpoonup\nabla v\,\,\text{in}\,L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, consequently, by
[
11
, Proposition 2.7]
it follows that

|\nabla v_{i}|^{p-2}_{A_{i}}A_{i}\nabla v_{i}\rightharpoonup_{i\rightarrow\infty}|\nabla v|^{p-2}_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbb{A}\nabla v\,\,\,\text{in}\,L^{p^{\prime}}(\tilde{\omega};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Thus, the claim is proved.

(ii) Assume now that
p>2
. In this case, we only need to show that

(3.6)

\int_{\tilde{\omega}}V_{i}v_{i}^{p-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\mathbb{V}v^{p-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{\omega}),

since the rest of the proof follows as in (i). Obviously,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\!\!(V_{i}v_{i}^{p-1}u\!-\!\mathbb{V}v^{p-1}u)\!\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\!\leq\!\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\!\!|V_{i}\!-\!\mathbb{V}||v_{i}|^{p-1}\!|u|\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!+\!\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\!\!|\mathbb{V}||v_{i}^{p-1}\!-\!v^{p-1}||u|\!\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq C\!\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\!\!|V_{i}-\mathbb{V}||u|\,\mathrm{d}x+\!\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\!|\mathbb{V}||v_{i}^{p-1}-v^{p-1}||u|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Clearly,
\mathbb{V}v_{i}^{p-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}v^{p-1}
pointwise in
\tilde{\omega}
, and by the uniform bound of
\{v_{i}\}
in
L^{\infty}(\tilde{\omega})
,
|\mathbb{V}v_{i}^{p-1}|\leq C|\mathbb{V}|
, where
C
is independent of
i
. Since
|\mathbb{V}|\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)\subset L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, the dominated convergence theorem implies that

\int_{\omega}|\mathbb{V}|\,|v_{i}^{p-1}-v^{p-1}|\,|u|\,\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow 0\qquad\forall u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{\omega}).

Moreover,
V_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}
in the quasinorm of
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, and
\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
(Corollary
2.15
), it follows that the first integral of the right-hand side of (
3.7
) converges to
0
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, (
3.6
) follows.
∎

3.3.
The Dirichlet problem

The present subsection is devoted to the existence of a solution of the nonhomogeneous Dirichlet problem (
3.11
) in a bounded Lipschitz subdomain. For a uniqueness result, see Theorem
3.26
. We begin with a lemma concerning the weakly lower semicontinuity and coerciveness of a functional
J
associated with this Dirichlet problem.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
1<p<d
, and
\omega\Subset\tilde{\omega}\Subset\omega^{\prime}\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\omega
is a Lipschitz domain. Assume that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega^{\prime})
. Define a functional
J:W^{1,p}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
by

J[u]:=\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u;\omega)-\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega}|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\omega}V|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then:

(i)

The functional
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
.

(ii)

Moreover, if
V\geq 0
, then for any
g\in W^{1,p}(\omega)
the functional
J
is coercive in

S_{g}:=\{u\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\mid u=g\,\,\,\mbox{on }\partial\omega\mbox{ in the trace sense}\}.

Proof.

(i). Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
. Consequently,
\{\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)}\}
is bounded. Since
W^{1,p}(\omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\omega)
, it follows that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
L^{p}(\omega)
and
u_{n}\rightarrow u
a.e. in
\omega
.

Let
\delta>0
. Using Minkowski’s inequality and Theorem
2.14
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\omega}V^{\pm}|u_{n}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}-\left(\int_{\omega}V^{\pm}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\int_{\omega}V^{\pm}|u_{n}-u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\delta\|\nabla(u_{n}-u)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C(d,p,\delta,\|V^{\pm}\|^{1/(p-1)}_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega^{\prime})})\|u_{n}-u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{1/p}\left(C+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+C(d,p,\delta,\|V^{\pm}\|^{1/(p-1)}_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega^{\prime})})\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)},

where
C=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)}
. This implies that

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\sup}\int_{\omega}V^{\pm}|u_{n}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\omega}V^{\pm}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x
.
On the other hand, Fatou’s lemma implies

\int_{\omega}V^{\pm}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\inf}\int_{\omega}V^{\pm}|u_{n}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x
. Therefore,

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\int_{\omega}V|u_{n}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega}V|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

In addition, the weak lower semicontinuity of the gradient term is immediate since the mapping
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{p}_{A(x)}
is convex. Thus,

\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u)\leq\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u_{n}).

Similarly, for the last term of the functional
J
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}^{\pm}|u_{n}|\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}^{\pm}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|\mathcal{G}^{\pm}\|^{1/p^{\prime}}_{L^{1}(\omega)}\left(\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}^{\pm}|u_{n}-u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{G}^{\pm}\|^{1/p^{\prime}}_{L^{1}(\omega)}\!\!\left[\delta^{1/p}\left(\!C\!\!+\!\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\!\right)\!\!+\!C(d,p,\delta,\|\mathcal{G}^{\pm}\|^{1/(p-1)}_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega^{\prime})})\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\!\right]\!.

This shows that
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\sup}\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}^{\pm}|u_{n}|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}^{\pm}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x.

On the other hand, by Fatou’s lemma we have

\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}^{\pm}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\inf}\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}^{\pm}|u_{n}|\,\mathrm{d}x.
Thus,

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}|u_{n}|\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x,

and part (i) is proved.

(ii) Fix a number
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and suppose
u\in S_{g}
such that
J[u]\leq t
. In order to prove that the functional
J
is coercive in
S_{g}
, it is enough to show that
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)}\leq C
, where the positive constant
C

is independent of
u
.

Since
V\geq 0
in
\omega
and
J[u]\leq t
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq t+\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq t+\|\mathcal{G}\|^{1/p^{\prime}}_{L^{1}(\omega)}\left(\int_{\omega}|\mathcal{G}||u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}\leq t+C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)},

where the last inequality follows from the Morrey-Adams theorem (Theorem
2.14
), and the constant
C
depends only on
d,p,\delta,\omega,\omega^{\prime},\|\mathcal{G}\|^{1/(p-1)}_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega^{\prime})}
and
\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}
. Since
A
is locally uniformly elliptic, (
3.8
) implies

(3.9)

\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)},

where the positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are independent of
u
. Since
u=g
on
\partial\omega
, it follows
u-g\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
. Furthermore, by the Poincaré inequality in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
, the uniform ellipticity of
A
and inequality (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-g\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq C_{p}\|\nabla(u-g)\|_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})})+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})})+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{p}}{\Theta_{\omega}}\left(\Big(\int_{\omega}|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/p}+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{p}}{\Theta_{\omega}}\left(\left(t+C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)}\right)^{1/p}+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\omega)},

where the constant
C_{p}
depends only on
d,\omega
and
C
as in (
3.8
). Therefore,

(3.10)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}^{p}\leq C_{3}+C_{4}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)},

where
C_{3}
,
C_{4}
are positive numbers independent of
u
. Thus, from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) we get

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)}^{p}\leq C_{5}+C_{6}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)},

where the positive constants
C_{5}
and
C_{6}
are independent of
u
. Since
p>1
the latter inequality implies that there exists
C>0
(independent of
u
) such that
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)}\leq C
.
∎

Consequently, we have:

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume
1<p<d
and let
\omega\Subset\Omega
be any Lipschitz subdomain. Let
A
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and let
V,\mathcal{G}\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, where
V,\mathcal{G}\geq 0
a.e. in
\omega
, and let
g\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
be nonnegative.
Then there exists a nonnegative solution
v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
to the Dirichlet problem

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u)=\mathcal{G}&\mbox{ in }\omega,\\
u=g&\mbox{ on }\partial\omega.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider the functional
I:W^{1,p}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
defined by

I[u]:=\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u;\omega)-\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}u\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega}|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\omega}V|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\omega}\mathcal{G}u\,\mathrm{d}x.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{g}=\{u\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\mid u\!=\!g\mbox{ on }\partial\omega\}
be such that

I[u_{n}]\searrow m:=\underset{u\in S_{g}}{\inf}I[u]
. Since
g\geq 0
, we have
\{|u_{n}|\}\subset S_{g}
. Now, consider the functional
J:W^{1,p}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
defined as in Lemma
3.9
. Since
\mathcal{G}\geq 0
, it follows that
J[u]\leq I(u)
and
m\leq I[|u_{n}|]=J[u_{n}]\leq I[u_{n}]
. This implies that
J[u_{n}]\rightarrow m
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and
m=\underset{u\in S_{g}}{\inf}J[u]
.

By Lemma
3.9
, the functional
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous and also coercive in
S_{g}
. So, in light of Mazur’s theorem, and since
J
is even, there exists a nonnegative
u\in S_{g}
such that
J[u]=m
. Also we have
J[u]=I[u]=m
. Thus,
u
is a minimizer of the functional
I
in
S_{g}
, and hence, a solution of problem (
3.11
).
∎

3.4.
Principal eigenvalue and the maximum principle

In this subsection we recall the definition of a principal eigenvalue of the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
satisfying Assumptions
3.1
, and discuss its properties.

Definition 3.11

(Eigenvalue)
.

Let
\omega\Subset\Omega
be a bounded domain.

1. A real number
\Lambda
is said to be an eigenvalue with an eigenfunction
v
of the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u)=\Lambda|u|^{p-2}u&\mbox{ in }\omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\omega,\end{cases}

if
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)\setminus\{0\}
and
v
satisfies

\int_{\omega}|\nabla v|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\omega}V|v|^{p-2}v\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x=\Lambda\int_{\omega}|v|^{p-2}v\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega).

2. A principal eigenvalue of the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem (
3.12
) is an eigenvalue of (
3.12
) with a nonnegative eigenfunction.

The following theorem on the existence of principal eigenvalue extends the result in
[
20
, Thorem 3.9]
, where the potential
V
is assumed to be in the Morrey space
M^{q}(p;\omega)
. The proof follows the same arguments as in
[
20
, Thorem 3.9]
(using the Morrey-Adams theorem for
V\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)
), and therefore it is omitted.

Theorem 3.12
.

Assume that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
. Then
Q_{p,A,V}
on a subdomain
\omega\Subset\Omega
admits a unique principal eigenvalue, denoted by
\Lambda_{1}
. Moreover,
\Lambda_{1}
is given by the Rayleigh-Ritz variational formula

\Lambda_{1}:=\Lambda_{1}(Q_{p,A,V};\omega)=\Lambda_{1}(\omega)=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u;\omega)}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)}}.

Furthermore, the principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}
is simple and isolated.

We recall a generalized Picone identity which implies the strict monotonicity of
\Lambda_{1}(Q_{p,A,V};\omega)
as as a function of subdomains
\omega\Subset\Omega
. For the proof of Picone identity see
[
21
, Lemma 3.1]
and Remark 3.2 therein.

Lemma 3.13

(Picone identity)
.

Let
v>0,u\geq 0
be in
W_{\loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
uv^{-1}\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, and assume that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
. For
x\in\Omega
define

(3.13)

L_{A}(u,v)(x):=|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}+(p-1)\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p}}|\nabla v|_{A}^{p}-p\frac{u^{p-1}}{v^{p-1}}\nabla u\cdot A(x)\nabla v|\nabla v|_{A}^{p-2},

and

(3.14)

R_{A}(u,v)(x):=|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}-\nabla\left(\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\cdot A(x)\nabla v|\nabla v|_{A}^{p-2}.

Then

L_{A}(u,v)(x)=R_{A}(u,v)(x)\geq 0\quad\mbox{a.e. }\,x\in\Omega.

Moreover,
L_{A}(u,v)=0
a.e. in
\Omega
if and only if
u=cv
in
\Omega
for some constant
c\geq 0
.

Using this Picone identity we have the following theorem on the positivity of principal eigenvalue which implies that if
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then
\Lambda_{1}(\omega)>0
for any bounded subdomain
\omega\Subset\Omega
. This extends the result obtained in
[
21
, Lemma 5.1]
for potential
V\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\omega_{1}\Subset\omega_{2}\Subset\Omega
be Lipschitz subdomains. Suppose that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\geq 0
in
\omega_{2}
. Then
\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})>\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})\geq 0
.

Proof.

From the Rayleigh-Ritz variational formula, we get
\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})\geq 0
. Let
\phi_{i}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\omega_{i})
be the normalized principal eigenfunctions corresponding to the principal eigenvalues
\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{i})
,
i=1,2
. By the Harnack inequality,
\phi_{i}
does not vanish in
\omega_{i}
for
i=1,2
, and hence
\frac{\phi_{1}}{\phi_{2}}\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\omega_{1})
. Now consider a nonnegative minimizing sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega_{1})
that converges to
\phi_{1}
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\omega_{1})
. Then by using the Picone identity (Lemma
3.13
) we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{2}}L_{A}(\varphi_{n},\phi_{2})\>\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega_{1}}L_{A}(\varphi_{n},\phi_{2})\>\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega_{1}}R_{A}(\varphi_{n},\phi_{2})\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega_{1}}|\nabla\varphi_{n}|_{A}^{p}\>\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\omega_{1}}\nabla\left(\frac{\varphi_{n}^{p}}{\phi_{2}^{p-1}}\right)\cdot A(x)\nabla\phi_{2}|\nabla\phi_{2}|_{A}^{p-2}\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega_{1}}|\nabla\varphi_{n}|_{A}^{p}\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\omega_{1}}V\varphi_{n}^{p}\>\!\mathrm{d}x-\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})\int_{\omega_{1}}\varphi_{n}^{p}\>\!\mathrm{d}x,

where we have used
\frac{\varphi_{n}^{p}}{\phi_{2}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega_{2})
as a test function in weak formulation of
(
3.12
)
in
\omega_{2}
. Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
and using Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\omega_{1}}L_{A}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\omega_{1}}L_{A}(\varphi_{n},\phi_{2})\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\left(\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})-\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})\right)\int_{\omega_{1}}\varphi_{1}^{p}\>\!\mathrm{d}x.

This implies that
\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})\geq\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})
. If
\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})=\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})
, then
L_{A}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=0
a.e. in
\omega_{1}
. Therefore, Lemma
3.13
, implies
\phi_{2}|_{\omega_{1}}=c\phi_{1}
for some
c>0
. But by the Harnack inequality
\phi_{2}>0
on
\partial\omega_{1}
which contradicts the fact that
\phi_{1}=0
on
\partial\omega_{1}
in the trace sense. Thus,
\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})>\Lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})
.
∎

The positivity of the principal eigenvalue in a bounded subdomain implies the generalized maximum principle for
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\geq 0
.

Theorem 3.15

(Maximum principle)
.

Suppose that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and assume that
\mathcal{Q}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Let
\omega\Subset\Omega
be a subdomain. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

The principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}
of
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\omega
is positive.

(ii)

The weak maximum principle for
Q_{p,A,V}
holds true in
\omega
: If
v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)
is a solution of

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{p,A,V}(u)&=\mathcal{G}\quad\mbox{in }\omega,\\
u&\geq 0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\omega,\end{split}

where
\mathcal{G}\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\omega)
is nonnegative, then
v\geq 0
in
\omega
.

(iii)

The strong maximum principle for
Q_{p,A,V}
holds true in
\omega
: a solution
v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)
of (
3.15
) is either
v=0
or
v>0
in
\omega
.

Proof.

\mbox{(i)}\Rightarrow\mbox{(ii)}
: Let
v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)
be a solution of (
3.15
). Then choosing
v^{-}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
as a test function in the weak formulation of (
3.15
) we have

\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(v^{-};\omega)=\int_{\{x\in\omega:v(x)<0\}}\mathcal{G}v\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\mathcal{G}\geq 0
it follows that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(v^{-};\omega)\leq 0
. If
v^{-}\neq 0
, then
\Lambda_{1}\leq 0
, which contradicts (i). Thus,
v^{-}=0
a.e. in
\omega
and, hence
v\geq 0
a.e. in
\omega
.

\mbox{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mbox{(iii)}
: Since
\mathcal{G}\geq 0
, it follows that
v
is a nonnegative supersolution of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
. The weak Harnack inequality (Theorem
3.5
) implies that
v>0
in
\omega
.

\mbox{(iii)}\Rightarrow\mbox{(i)}
: Suppose that the principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}\leq 0
and let
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
be its principal eigenfunction. Then
u:=-v
is a supersolution of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
with
u=0
on
\partial\omega
. Since
u\neq 0
, the strong maximum principle implies that
u>0
which is a contradiction. Thus,
\Lambda_{1}>0
.
∎

Next we prove the existence and uniqueness for the Dirichlet problem.

Theorem 3.16
.

Assume that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and let
\omega\Subset\omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
be subdomains. Suppose that
\Lambda_{1}
of
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\omega
is positive. Then for
0\leq g\!\in\!L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)
there exists a unique positive solution in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
of

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u)=g&\mbox{ in }\omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\omega.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider the functional

J[u]:=\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u;\omega)-\int_{\omega}gu\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega).

Using Theorem
2.13
, and following the same steps as in Lemma
3.9
(i), we obtain that the functional
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
is weakly lower semicontinuous in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
. On the other hand, since
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)
, the functional
I(u):=\int_{\omega}gu\,\mathrm{d}x
is a bounded linear functional in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
. Therefore, the functional
J[u]
is weakly lower semicontinuous in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
.

We claim that the functional
J[u]
is coercive in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
. Assume that
J[u]\leq t
. Since
\Lambda_{1}>0
hence we have

\Lambda_{1}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u;\omega)\quad\forall u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega).

Then by applying Hölder inequality, we get

\Lambda_{1}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq J[u]+\int_{\omega}gu\,\mathrm{d}x\leq t+\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}.

This inequality implies that

(3.17)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq m:=\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{t+\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)}}{\Lambda_{1}}\right)^{1/{(p-1)}}\right\}.

On the other hand, applying Hölder’s inequality and Morrey-Adams theorem (Theorem
2.13
) in the inequality
J[u]\leq t
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq t+\int_{\omega}gu\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\omega}|V||u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq t+\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}+\delta\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{\delta}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)},

where
C_{\delta}=C(d,p,\delta,\omega,\omega^{\prime},\|V\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega^{\prime})})
. Then, (
3.17
), (
3.18
) and Assumptions
3.1
imply

\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq(\Theta_{\omega}^{-p}-\delta)^{-1}(t+\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)}m+C_{\delta}m^{p}),

for
\delta<\Theta_{\omega}
. This together with (
3.17
) implies that
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega)}\leq C
, where the constant
C>0
is independent of
u
.

Thus, the functional
J[u]
is weakly lower semicontinuous and coercive in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
. Consequently, the functional has a minimizer in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
and the corresponding Dirichlet problem has a nonnegative solution
v_{1}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
. Since
\Lambda_{1}>0
by the strong maximum principle (Theorem
3.15
), the solution
v_{1}
is either
0
or positive in
\omega
.

Uniqueness:
Let
v_{i}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
,
i=1,2
, be two solutions of (
3.16
). If
v_{1}=0
, then
g=0
in
\omega
, and then
v_{2}
is a principal eigenvalue with eigenvalue
0
, but in light of the uniqueness of the principal eigenvalue, this contradicts the assumption that
\Lambda_{1}>0
.

Thus,
v_{i}>0
in
\omega
for
i=1,2
. Consider
v_{i,h}:=v_{i}+h
, where
h
is a positive constant and
i=1,2
. Taking
\varphi_{1,h}:=\frac{v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p}}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
as a test function in the definition of
v_{1}
being a solution of the equation (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\!(v_{1,h}^{p}\!-\!v_{2,h}^{p})|\nabla\log v_{1,h}|^{p}_{A}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!

\displaystyle-\!p\!\int_{\omega}\!\!v_{2,h}^{p}|\nabla\log v_{1,h}|^{p-2}_{A}\!A\nabla\!\log v_{1,h}\!\cdot\!\nabla\!\log(\frac{v_{2,h}}{v_{1,h}})\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\frac{(g-Vv_{1}^{p-1})(v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p})}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}.

Similarly, using
\varphi_{2,h}=\frac{v_{2,h}^{p}-v_{1,h}^{p}}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
as a test function in the definition of
v_{2}
being a solution of the Dirichlet problem (
3.16
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\!(v_{2,h}^{p}\!-\!v_{1,h}^{p})|\nabla\!\log v_{2,h}|^{p}_{A}\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\!-\!p\!\int_{\omega}\!\!v_{1,h}^{p}|\nabla\log v_{2,h}|^{p-2}_{A}\!A\nabla\!\log v_{2,h}\!\cdot\!\nabla\!\log(\frac{v_{1,h}}{v_{2,h}})\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\frac{(g-Vv_{2}^{p-1})(v_{2,h}^{p}-v_{1,h}^{p})}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}.

Adding the above two equations, we reach at

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\!v_{1,h}^{p}\!\left(\!|\nabla\!\log\!v_{1,h}|^{p}_{A}\!-\!|\nabla\!\log\!v_{2,h}|^{p}_{A}\!-\!p|\nabla\log v_{2,h}|^{p-2}_{A}\!A\nabla\!\log v_{2,h}\!\cdot\!\nabla\!\log(\frac{v_{1,h}}{v_{2,h}})\!\right)\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\!\!\int_{\omega}\!\!v_{2,h}^{p}\!\!\left(\!|\nabla\!\log\!v_{2,h}|^{p}_{A}\!-\!|\nabla\!\log\!v_{1,h}|^{p}_{A}\!-\!p\nabla\!\log v_{1,h}|^{p-2}_{A}\!A\nabla\!\log\!v_{1,h}\!\cdot\!\nabla\!\log(\frac{v_{2,h}}{v_{1,h}})\!\right)\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,=\int_{\omega}\left[g\left(\frac{1}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)-V\left(\frac{v_{1}^{p-1}}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{v_{2}^{p-1}}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)\right](v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x

Using the convexity inequality (
3.1
)
we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\left[g\left(\frac{1}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)-V\left(\frac{v_{1}^{p-1}}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{v_{2}^{p-1}}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)\right](v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq C(p)\,\times

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\!\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\begin{cases}\!\!(v_{1,h}^{p}\!+\!v_{2,h}^{p})|\nabla\log(\frac{v_{1,h}}{v_{2,h}})|_{A}^{p}\qquad\qquad p\in[2,\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\!(v_{1,h}^{p}\!+\!v_{2,h}^{p})|\nabla\!\log(\frac{v_{1,h}}{v_{2,h}})|_{A}^{2}\!\left(|\nabla\!\log\!v_{1,h}|_{A}\!+\!|\nabla\!\log\!v_{2,h}|_{A}\!\right)^{p-2}\,\ p\!\in\!(1,2).\end{cases}

This implies that

0\!\geq\!\int_{\omega}\!\!g\left(\frac{1}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)(v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p})\!\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\!\!\int_{\omega}\!V\!\!\left(\frac{v_{1}^{p-1}}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{v_{2}^{p-1}}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)(v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p})\!\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
v_{i,h}\rightarrow v_{i}
as
h\rightarrow 0
for
i=1,2
, it follows that the integrand of the integral of the right hand side above converges to
0
a.e. in
\omega
. Also,

\left|V\!\!\left(\frac{v_{1}^{p-1}}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{v_{2}^{p-1}}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)(v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p})\right|\leq 2|V|(\left(v_{1}+1)^{p}+(v_{2}+1)^{p}\right)\in L^{1}(\omega).

Therefore, by the dominated converges theorem, it follows that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{\omega}g\left(\frac{1}{v_{1,h}^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{v_{2,h}^{p-1}}\right)(v_{1,h}^{p}-v_{2,h}^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

This together with the Fatou lemma implies that

0=\int_{\omega}\!\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\begin{cases}\!\!(v_{1}^{p}\!+\!v_{2}^{p})|\nabla\log(\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}})|_{A}^{p}&p\in[2,\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\!(v_{1}^{p}\!+\!v_{2}^{p})|\nabla\!\log(\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}})|_{A}^{2}\!\left(|\nabla\!\log\!v_{1}|_{A}\!+\!|\nabla\!\log\!v_{2}|_{A}\!\right)^{p-2}&\ p\!\in\!(1,2).\end{cases}

Thus,
v_{1}=v_{2}
a.e. in
\omega
.
∎

3.5.
Agmon-Allegretto-Piepenbrink-type theorem

The next result, known as Agmon-Allegretto-Piepenbrink (AAP)-type theorem, asserts that the nonnegativity of the functional
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is equivalent to the existence of a weak positive solution or supersolution of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
. This result generalizes the AAP Theorem
4.3
obtained in
[
20
]
for a potential
V\in M^{q}_{\loc}(p;\Omega)
(see
[
20
]
for the history of AAP theorem).

Theorem 3.17

(AAP-type theorem)
.

Suppose that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\geq 0
in
\Omega
;

(ii)

the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
admits a positive solution;

(iii)

the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
admits a positive supersolution.

Proof.

\mbox{(i)}\Rightarrow\mbox{(ii)}
: Fix a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
and consider a sequence
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of Lipschitz compact exhaustion
\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\omega_{i}=\Omega
such that
x_{0}\in\omega_{1}
, and consider the following Dirichlet problem for
i\geq 2

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{p,A,V+1/i}(u)&=f_{i}\quad\mbox{ in }\omega_{i},\\
u&=0\quad\mbox{ on }\partial\omega_{i},\end{split}

where
f_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega_{i}\setminus\bar{\omega}_{i-1})\setminus\{0\}
and
f_{i}\geq 0
. By (i), we have

\Lambda_{1}=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega_{i})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V+1/i}(u;\omega_{i})}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega_{i})}}\geq\frac{1}{i}\quad\mbox{for all }i\geq 1.

This together with Theorem
3.16
implies that the Dirichlet problem (
3.19
) admits a positive solution
v_{i}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega_{i})
. Set
\omega^{\prime}_{i}=\omega_{i-1}
. As
\mbox{\rm supp}\,(f_{i})\subset\omega_{i}\setminus\bar{\omega^{\prime}}_{i}
, we get for
i\geq 2

\int_{\omega^{\prime}_{i}}|\nabla v_{i}|_{A}A\nabla v_{i}\cdot\nabla u\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\omega^{\prime}_{i}}\left(V+\frac{1}{i}\right)v_{i}^{p-1}u\,\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\forall u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega^{\prime}_{i}).

By Theorem
3.6
,
v_{i}\in C(\omega^{\prime}_{i})
for all
i\geq 2
. Normalize
f_{i}
to get
v_{i}(x_{0})=1
for all
i\geq 2
. The Harnack convergence principle (Theorem
3.8
) with
A_{i}=A
and
V_{i}=V+{1}/{i}
, implies that, up to a subsequence,
v_{i}\to v
, where
v
is a positive solution of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
.

\mbox{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mbox{(iii)}
is trivial.

\mbox{(iii)}\Rightarrow\mbox{(i)}
: Let
\tilde{v}\in W_{\loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a positive supersolution of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
. The weak Harnack inequality (Theorem
3.5
) implies that
1/\tilde{v}\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
. For any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, choose
|\varphi|^{p}\tilde{v}^{1-p}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as a test function and denote
T:=-|\nabla\log\tilde{v}|_{A}^{p-2}\nabla\log\tilde{v}
, then

(p-1)\int_{\Omega}|T|_{A}^{p^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq p\int_{\Omega}|T|_{A}|\varphi|^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|_{A}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}V|\varphi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using the Young’s inequality:
pab\leq(p-1)a^{p^{\prime}}+b^{p}
with
a=|T|_{A}|\varphi|^{p-1}
and
b=|\nabla\varphi|_{A}
, we obtain that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
∎

3.6.
Null-sequence and ground state

In this subsection we recall the notion of a null-sequence and a ground state corresponding to the functional
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
. We show that if
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
admits a ground state
\Phi
in
\Omega
, then
\Phi
is the unique (up to a multiplicative constant) positive supersolution of
{Q}_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
. Moreover, in a bounded domain, the positive principal eigenfunction is in fact a ground sate if
A
and
V
satisfy assumptions
3.1
, and
3.2
.

Definition 3.18

(Null-sequence)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. A sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset W_{c}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is said to be a null-sequence of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
if

(a)

u_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(b)

there exists an open set
K\!\Subset\!\Omega
such that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(K)}\!=\!1
for all
n\!\in\!\mathbb{N}
;

(c)

\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(u_{n})=0}
.

Definition 3.19

(Ground state)
.

A positive
\Phi\in W^{1,p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is an (Agmon) ground state of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
if
\Phi
is an
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
limit of a null-sequence.

Lemma 3.20
.

Assume that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\!\geq\!0
in
\Omega
, where
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
. If
p<2
assume further that Assumption
3.2
is satisfied. Suppose that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
admits a null-sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset W_{c}^{1,p}(\Omega)
in
\Omega
. Let
v
be a positive supersolution of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)\!=\!0
in
\Omega
, and consider the sequence
\{w_{n}\!:=\!u_{n}/v\}
. Then
\{w_{n}\}
is bounded in
W_{\loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, and
\nabla w_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Fix
K\Subset\Omega
such that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(K)}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
K\Subset\omega\Subset\Omega
be a Lipschitz subdomain. By the Minkowski inequality we have

\|w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leq\|w_{n}-\langle w_{n}\rangle_{K}\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}+\langle w_{n}\rangle_{K}(\mathcal{L}^{d}(\omega))^{1/p},

where
\langle w_{n}\rangle_{K}=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
. Applying the Poincaré inequality to the first term, and the weak Harnack inequality (Theorem
3.5
) to the second term of the right hand side of the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,p,K,\omega)\|\nabla w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{\inf_{K}v}\langle u_{n}\rangle_{K}(\mathcal{L}^{d}(\omega))^{1/p}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq C(d,p,K,\omega)\|\nabla w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{\inf_{K}v}\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(\omega)}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(K)}\right)^{1/p},

where in the second term of the latter inequality, we used the Hölder inequality, and
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(K)}=1
.
Let

I(v,w_{k}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C(\bar{p})\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}v^{\bar{p}}|\nabla w_{k}|_{A}^{\bar{p}}\,\mathrm{d}x}&1\leq p<2,\\[8.53581pt]
C(p)\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}v^{p}|\nabla w_{k}|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x}&p\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

where
\bar{p}\geq p
, and
C(p)
and
C(\bar{p})
are the constants in (
3.2
) and (
3.1
), respectively. Using (
3.2
) and (
3.1
) to obtain

(3.21)

\displaystyle I(v,w_{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}w_{k}^{p}|\nabla v|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}v|\nabla v|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla v\!\cdot\!\nabla(w_{k}^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla v\!\cdot\!\nabla(w_{k}^{p}v)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
v
is a positive supersolution, we get

(3.22)

I(v,w_{k})\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}Vu_{k}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=Q_{A,p,V}[u_{k}].

Using the definition of
I
, the weak Harnack inequality, (and for
p\leq 2
use also Hölder inequality), we obtain from (
3.22
) that

(3.23)

c_{\omega}\!\int_{\omega}\!|\nabla w_{k}|^{p}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\leq\!C(p)\int_{\Omega}v^{p}|\nabla w_{k}|^{p}_{A}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\leq\!\mathcal{Q}_{A,p,V}[u_{k}]\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }k\rightarrow\infty,

where
c_{\omega}>0
is a positive constant. By the weak compactness of
W^{1,p}(\omega)
, we infer that (up to a subsequence)

(3.24)

\nabla w_{k}\rightarrow 0\qquad\mbox{in }L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

By (
3.20
) and (
3.23
), we have that
w_{k}
is bounded in
W_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(\omega)
.
∎

Remark 3.21
.

In Lemma
3.20
, for
p<2
we may replace Assumption
3.2
with the assumption that the gradient of the positive supersolution
v
is locally bounded (see the proof of Proposition 4.11 in
[
20
]
).

As a consequence of this lemma, we have the following uniqueness result:

Theorem 3.22
.

Assume that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\!\geq\!0
in
\Omega
, where
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and if
p<2
assume further that Assumption
3.2
is satisfied. Then any null-sequence of the functional
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
converges, both in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
and a.e. in
\Omega
to a unique (up to a multiplicative constant) positive supersolution (which is in fact a solution) of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)\!=\!0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By the AAP theorem (Theorem
3.17
), the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
admits a positive solution
v\in W_{\loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and a positive supersolution
\tilde{v}\in W_{\loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
\{u_{n}\}
is a null-sequence of the functional
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
. Consider
w_{n}:=u_{n}/\tilde{v}
. Lemma
3.20
implies that
\nabla w_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem implies that up to a subsequence (still denoted by
\{w_{n}\}
),
w_{n}\rightarrow c
in
W^{1,p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
for a constant
c\geq 0
. Since
\tilde{v}
is locally bounded and bounded away from zero,
u_{n}\rightarrow c\tilde{v}
a.e. in
\Omega
and also in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
(up to a subsequence). In particular,
c=\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{-1}>0
. It follows that any null-sequence
\{u_{n}\}
converges (up to a positive multiplicative constant) to the same positive supersolution
\tilde{v}
. Since the positive solution
v
is also a positive supersolution, we conclude that
\tilde{v}=Cv
for some
C>0
, and thus
\tilde{v}
is also the unique positive solution of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
.
∎

Lemma 3.23
.

Suppose that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
. Let
\omega\Subset\Omega
be a subdomain. Then, up to a multiplicative constant, the positive principal eigenfunction of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
in
\omega
is a ground state of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V-\Lambda_{1}}
in
\omega
.

Proof.

Let
v_{1}
be the positive principal eigenfunction of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}
in
\omega
with principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}
. Fix
K\Subset\omega
such that
C_{K}:=\|v_{1}\|_{L^{p}(K)}>0
. Then the sequence
\{C^{-1}_{K}v_{1}\}
is a null-sequence and,
C^{-1}_{K}v_{1}
is a ground state of the functional
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V-\Lambda_{1}}
in
\omega
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
3.22
, we have:

Theorem 3.24
.

Let
\omega\Subset\Omega
be a subdomain. Assume that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and if
p<2
assume further that Assumption
3.2
is satisfied. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

the principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}>0
;

(ii)

the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
admits a strict positive supersolution in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\omega)
;

(iii)

the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
admits a strict positive supersolution in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
.

Proof.

\text{ (i) }\Rightarrow\text{ (ii) }
: Assume that the principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}>0
with a principal eigenvalue
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\omega)
. Then by the strong maximum principle,
v
is a strict positive supersolution of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
.

\text{ (ii) }\Rightarrow\text{ (iii) }
: This is immediate.

\text{ (iii) }\Rightarrow\text{ (i) }
: Suppose that the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
admits a strict positive supersolution in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
. By the AAP theorem (Theorem
3.17
),
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}(\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega)
. This implies that the principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}\geq 0
. Now if
\Lambda_{1}=0
, then in light of Theorem
3.22
and Lemma
3.23
, the associated principal eigenfunction is the unique (up to a multiplicative constant) positive supersolution of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
. This contradicts our assumption that the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\omega
admits a strict positive supersolution in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
. Therefore,
\Lambda_{1}>0
.
∎

3.7.
Weak comparison principle

The first result in this subsection is a simple version of the weak comparison principle between sub and supersolutions of the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
when the potential
V\in\mathfrak{W}_{\loc}^{p}(\Omega)
is nonnegative. We omit the proof since it is quite standard and follows exactly as in
[
20
, Lemma 5.1]
, where the potential is assumed to be in
M^{q}(p;\omega)
.

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
1<p<d
, and
\omega\Subset\Omega
be a Lipschitz subdomain. Suppose that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
,
\mathcal{G}\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, and
V\geq 0
in
\omega
. Let
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
be respectively a subsolution and a supersolution of the problem

Q_{p,A,V}(u)=\mathcal{G}\quad\mbox{in }\omega.

If
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. on
\partial\omega
in the trace sense, then
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. in
\omega
.

Theorem 3.26
.

Let
1<p<d
and
\omega\Subset\Omega
be a Lipschitz subdomain. Suppose that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
and
g\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, where
g\geq 0
. Let
f,u_{1},u_{2}\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
, where
f\geq 0
a.e. in
\omega
and

\displaystyle Q_{p,A,V}(u_{1})

\displaystyle\leq g\leq Q_{p,A,V}(u_{2})\quad\mbox{in }\omega,

\displaystyle u_{1}

\displaystyle\leq f\leq u_{2}\quad\mbox{on }\partial\omega,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq u_{1}\leq u_{2}\quad\mbox{in }\omega.

Then there exists a nonnegative solution
u\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
of the Dirichlet problem

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u)=g\quad&\mbox{ in }\omega,\\
u=f\quad&\mbox{ on }\partial\omega,\end{cases}

such that
u_{1}\leq u\leq u_{2}
a.e. in
\omega
. Moreover, if
f>0
a.e. on
\partial\omega
, then the solution
u
of the Dirichlet problem (
3.25
) is unique.

Proof.

Existence:

Define the set

S:=\{v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega}):0\leq u_{1}\leq v\leq u_{2}\,\,\mbox{in }\omega\}.

For
v\in S
, consider the function
\mathcal{G}(x,v):=g(x)+2V^{-}(x)v^{p-1}(x)
,
x\in\omega
. Since
0\leq v\in C(\bar{\omega})
, it follows that
\mathcal{G}\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)
and also
\mathcal{G}\geq 0
a.e. in
\omega
. For
v\in S
consider the following Dirichlet problem

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,|V|}(u)=\mathcal{G}(x,v)\quad&\mbox{ in }\omega,\\
u=f\qquad&\mbox{ on }\partial\omega.\end{cases}

By Theorem
3.10
, the Dirichlet problem (
3.26
) admits a solution
u\!\in\!W^{1,p}(\omega)
. Moreover, the solution is unique. Indeed, if
u
and
\tilde{u}
are solutions of the above problem, then we have

\displaystyle Q_{p,A,|V|}(u)=\mathcal{G}(x,v)=Q_{p,A,|V|}(\tilde{u})

\displaystyle\mbox{in }\omega,

\displaystyle u=f=\tilde{u}

\displaystyle\mbox{on }\partial\omega.

By applying Lemma
3.25
with
u
as a subsolution and
\tilde{u}
as a supersolution, we get
u\leq\tilde{u}
in
\omega
. By interchanging the roles of
u
and
\tilde{u}
, it follows that
\tilde{u}\leq u
in
\omega
. Hence,
u=\tilde{u}
in
\omega
.

Define a map
\psi:S\rightarrow W^{1,p}(\omega)
by
\psi(v)=u
, where
u
is a solution of the problem (
3.26
). The mapping
\psi
is well-defined in the set
S
and by using Lemma
3.25
, it can be seen that the map
\psi
is monotone. Let
v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
be a subsolution of the Dirichlet problem (
3.25
). Then

Q_{p,A,|V|}(v)=Q_{p,A,V}(v)+\mathcal{G}(x,v)-g(x)\leq\mathcal{G}(x,v)

in the weak sense, and hence
v
is a subsolution of the problem (
3.26
). Also, by the definition of
\psi
,
\psi(v)
is a solution of (
3.26
). By Lemma
3.25
, we have
v\leq\psi(v)
a.e. in
\omega
. Moreover,

\displaystyle Q_{p,A,V}(\psi(v))

\displaystyle=Q_{p,A,|V|}(\psi(v))-\mathcal{G}(x,\psi(v))+g(x)

\displaystyle=2V^{-}(|v|^{p-2}v-|\psi(v)|^{p-2}\psi(v))+g(x)\leq g(x)\quad\mbox{in }\,\omega,

in the weak sense. This shows that if
v
is a subsolution of the Dirichlet (
3.25
), then
\psi(v)
is also a subsolution of (
3.25
) with
v\leq\psi(v)
a.e. in
\omega
. Similarly, it can be seen that if
v\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
be a supersolution of (
3.25
), then
\psi(v)
also a supersolution of (
3.25
) with
\psi(v)\leq v
a.e. in
\omega
.

Now define the following two sequences

\underaccent{\bar}{v}_{0}:=u_{1},\quad\underaccent{\bar}{v}_{n}:=\psi(\underaccent{\bar}{v}_{n-1})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\bar{v}_{0}:=u_{2},\quad\bar{v}_{n}:=\psi(\bar{v}_{n-1}).

From the above arguments, the sequences
\underaccent{\bar}{v}_{n}\nearrow\underaccent{\bar}{v}
and
\bar{v}_{n}\searrow\bar{v}
for every
x\in\omega
. Also due to Brezis-Lieb Lemma
[
14
, Theorem 1.9]
,
both the sequences converges in
L^{p}(\omega)
. By using a similar argument as in Theorem
3.8
, it can be seen that
\underaccent{\bar}{v}
and
\bar{v}
are fixed point of
\psi
and both are solutions of the Dirichlet problem (
3.25
) with
u_{1}\leq\underaccent{\bar}{v}\leq\bar{v}\leq u_{2}
in
\omega
.

Uniqueness:
Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
be positive solutions of (
3.25
) with
f>0
a.e. on
\partial\omega
. Then using
\varphi_{1}=\frac{v_{1}^{p}-v_{2}^{p}}{v_{1}^{p-1}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
as a test function in the definition of
v_{1}
being a solution of (
3.25
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}(v_{1}^{p}-v_{2}^{p})|\nabla\log v_{1}|^{p}_{A}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-p\int_{\omega}v_{2}^{p}|\nabla\log v_{1}|^{p-2}_{A}A\nabla\log v_{1}\cdot\nabla\log(\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\frac{(g-Vv_{1}^{p-1})(v_{1}^{p}-v_{2}^{p})}{v_{1}^{p-1}}.

Similarly, using
\varphi_{2}=\frac{v_{2}^{p}-v_{1}^{p}}{v_{2}^{p-1}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega)
as a test function in the definition of
v_{2}
being a solution of the Dirichlet (
3.25
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}(v_{2}^{p}-v_{1}^{p})|\nabla\log v_{2}|^{p}_{A}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-p\int_{\omega}v_{1}^{p}|\nabla\log v_{2}|^{p-2}_{A}A\nabla\log v_{2}\cdot\nabla\log(\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\frac{(g-Vv_{2}^{p-1})(v_{2}^{p}-v_{1}^{p})}{v_{2}^{p-1}}.

Adding the above two equations, we reach at

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}v_{1}^{p}\left(|\nabla\log v_{1}|^{p}_{A}-|\nabla\log v_{2}|^{p}_{A}-p|\nabla\log v_{2}|^{p-2}_{A}A\nabla\log v_{2}\cdot\nabla\log(\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\omega}v_{2}^{p}\left(|\nabla\log v_{2}|^{p}_{A}-|\nabla\log v_{1}|^{p}_{A}-p\nabla\log v_{1}|^{p-2}_{A}A\nabla\log v_{1}\cdot\nabla\log(\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,=\int_{\omega}g\left(\frac{1}{v_{1}^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{v_{2}^{p-1}}\right)(v_{1}^{p}-v_{2}^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0

Now,(
3.1
) implies that

\displaystyle 0\!\geq\!C(p)\!\!\!\int_{\omega}\!\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\begin{cases}(v_{1}^{p}+v_{2}^{p})|\nabla\log(\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}})|_{A}^{p}\qquad\mbox{ if }p\geq 2,\\[5.69054pt]
\!(v_{1}^{p}\!+\!v_{2}^{p})|\nabla\log(\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}})|_{A}^{2}\left(|\nabla\log v_{1}|_{A}\!+\!|\nabla\log v_{2}|_{A}\right)^{p-2}\mbox{ if }1\!<p\!<\!2.\end{cases}

Consequently,
v_{2}\nabla v_{1}=v_{1}\nabla v_{2}
a.e. in
\omega
. Hence, there is a constant
c
such that
v_{1}=cv_{2}
a.e. in
\omega
. Since
v_{1}=v_{2}=f
on
\partial\omega
, we infer that
v_{1}=v_{2}
in
\bar{\omega}
. Therefore, the Dirichlet problem (
3.25
) admits a unique solution.
∎

Theorem 3.27

(Weak Comparison Principle)
.

Let
\omega\Subset\Omega
. Assume that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
, and
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that
v_{1},v_{2}\in W^{1,p}(\omega)\cap C(\bar{\omega})
satisfy the following inequalities

\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(v_{2})=g\,\,\text{in}\,\,\omega,\\
v_{2}>0\,\,\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\omega,\end{cases}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(v_{1})\leq Q_{p,A,V}(v_{2})\,\,\text{in}\,\,\omega,\\
v_{1}\leq v_{2}\,\,\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\omega.\end{cases}

where
g\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\omega)
with
g\geq 0
a.e. in
\omega
. Then
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. in
\omega
.

Proof.

By the assumption on
v_{2}
, we have that
v_{2}
is a supersolution of equation (
1.1
) in
\omega
and
v_{2}>0
on
\partial\omega
. Hence, by the strong maximum principle we have
v_{2}>0
in
\bar{\omega}
. Define
c:=\max\{1,\frac{\max_{\bar{\omega}}v_{1}}{\min_{\bar{\omega}}v_{2}}\}
. Then
v_{1}\leq cv_{2}
in
\bar{\omega}
. Now consider the following boundary value problem

(3.27)

\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u)=g\,\,\text{in}\,\,\omega,\\
u=v_{2}\,\,\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\omega.\end{cases}

By our assumption and the choice of the constant
c
, it follows that
cv_{2}
is a supersolution of the problem (
3.27
) with
v_{1}\leq v_{2}\leq cv_{2}
on
\partial\omega
. Also note that
v_{1}
is a subsolution of the problem (
3.27
). By applying Theorem
3.26
with
u_{1}=v_{1}
and
u_{2}=cv_{2}
, we have a solution
v
of (
3.27
) such that
v_{1}\leq v\leq cv_{2}
in
\omega
with
v=v_{2}
on
\partial\omega
. Again by the strong maximum principle, we have
v>0
in
\bar{\omega}
. Now, the uniqueness of the boundary value problem (
3.27
) (Theorem
3.26
) implies that
v=v_{2}
. Therefore,
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. in
\omega
.
∎

4.
Fuchsian-type singularity and positive Liouville-type theorem

The present section is devoted to the study of positive Liouville-type theorems, Picard-type principles, and removable singularity theorems for the equation

(4.1)

Q_{p,A,V}(u)=-\Delta_{p,A}(u)+V|u|^{p-2}u=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

where the potential
V\in\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
has an isolated Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
which belongs to the (ideal) boundary of
\Omega
. Since we permit the domain
\Omega
to be unbounded and the singular point
\zeta=\infty
, it is worthy to consider the one-point compactification
\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\cup\{\infty\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We denote by
\hat{\Omega}
the closure of
\Omega
in
\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Throughout this section, we assume that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Note that under this assumption the weak comparison principle in bounded subdomains is valid when
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
.

For
R>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}_{R}
the annuls given by

\mathcal{A}_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid{R}/{2}\leq|x|<{3R}/{2}\}.

The dilated domain
\Omega/R
of
\Omega
is defined by

\Omega/R:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,x=R^{-1}y,\,\text{where}\,y\in\Omega\}.

We assume that the singular point
\zeta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}
is an isolated component of
\partial\hat{\Omega}
and
\zeta
is either equal to
0
or
\infty
. We are interested in the behavior of positive solutions of (
4.1
) near
\zeta
. In fact, we may assume that
\Omega
is one of the followings domains:
B_{r}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}
for some
r>0
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.

With some abuse of notation, we write

x\to\zeta\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;\begin{cases}x\to 0&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}\,\mbox{ and }\zeta=0,\\
x\to\infty&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}\,\mbox{ and }\zeta=\infty.\end{cases}

Definition 4.1

(Fuchsian-type singularity)
.

Let
1<p<d
, and
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
. Let
\zeta\in\{0,\infty\}
be an isolated point of
\partial\hat{\Omega}
. Then we say that the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
has a Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
if there exists a punctured neighbourhood
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
of
\zeta
such that

(i)

The matrix
A
is bounded and uniformly elliptic in
\Omega^{\prime}
, i.e., there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(4.2)

C^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq|\xi|_{A(x)}^{2}\leq C|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall x\in\Omega^{\prime}\mbox{ and }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(ii)

There exists a positive constant
C
and
R_{0}>0
such that

(4.3)

\|V\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{R}\cap\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C

for all
0<R<R_{0}
if
\zeta=0
, and
R>R_{0}
if
\zeta=\infty
.

A set
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
is said to be an essential set with respect to the singular point
\zeta
if there exist
a\in(0,1)
,
b\in(1,\infty)
, and a sequence
\{R_{n}\}
of positive numbers converging to
\zeta
such that

\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n},\quad\text{where}\,\,\mathcal{A}_{n}=\{x\in\Omega:aR_{n}<|x|<bR_{n}\}.

Similar to the linear case as in
[
19
]
or for the quasi-linear cases as in
[
8
,
11
]
, it is enough to assume that inequalities (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) hold only on an essential set
\mathcal{A}
with respect to the singular point
\zeta
, i.e., there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
n
) such that

\mathrm{C}^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq|\xi|_{A(x)}^{2}\leq C|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{A}\mbox{ and }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mbox{ and }\quad\|V\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{n})}\leq C,

for some essential set
\mathcal{A}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n}
of
\zeta
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
1<p<d
, and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Consider the
(p,A)
-Laplacian with the Hardy potential
V(x)=\lambda|x|^{-p}
, and the equation

(4.4)

-\Delta_{p,A}(u)-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{p}}|v|^{p-2}v=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

where the matrix
A
satisfies (
4.2
) outside a compact set in
\Omega
. We assert that equation (
4.4
) has Fuchsian-type singularity both at
\zeta=0
and
\zeta=\infty
.

Let us check that (
4.3
) is satisfied near
\zeta=0
. It is enough to show that for sufficiently small
R>0
, (
4.3
) is fulfilled over the annular set
\mathcal{A}_{R}=\{x\in\Omega:R/2\leq|x|<3R/2\}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|V\|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{R})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{R}}\int_{0}^{\diam(\mathcal{A}_{R})}\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d-1}{p-1}}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R}\cap B_{r}(x)}|y|^{-p}\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\>\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{R}}\frac{1}{R^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\int_{0}^{3R}\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d-1}{p-1}}}r^{\frac{d}{p-1}}\>\!\mathrm{d}r\leq C\frac{1}{R^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}R^{\frac{p}{p-1}}=C<\infty.

Note that the constant
C>0
is independent of
R
. Hence, (
4.4
) has a Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta=0
. The case
\zeta=\infty
follows similarly for large enough
R>0
.

Next, we present a dilation process using the quasi-invariance of (
4.1
) under the scaling
x\mapsto Rx
, where
R>0
. For
R>0
, let
A_{R}
and
V_{R}
be the scaled matrix and the scaled potential defined by

A_{R}(x):=A(Rx),\quad V_{R}(x):=R^{p}V(Rx)\qquad x\in\Omega/R.

If
u
is a solution of (
1.1
), then for any
R>0
, the function
u_{R}(x):=u(Rx)
is a solution of the equation

-\Delta_{p,A_{R}}(u_{R})+V_{R}(x)|u_{R}|^{p-2}u_{R}=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega/R.

Now take an annular subset
\mathcal{A}_{R}:=(B_{3R/2}\setminus\bar{B}_{R/2})\cap\Omega
. Since
\zeta
is an
isolated singular point of
\partial\hat{\Omega}
, it follows that for
R
‘near’
\zeta
, the set
\mathcal{A}_{R}/R
is a fixed annular set
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Moreover, for such
R
we have

\displaystyle\|V_{R}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{R}/R)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{R}/R}\int_{0}^{\diam(\mathcal{A}_{R}/R)}\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d-1}{p-1}}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R}/R\cap B_{r}(x)}\!\!\!R^{p}|V(Ry)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\!\!\!\!\>\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{R}/R}\int_{0}^{\diam(\mathcal{A}_{R}/R)}\frac{R^{\frac{p-d}{p-1}}}{r^{\frac{d-1}{p-1}}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R}\cap B_{Rr}(Rx)}|V(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\!\!\!\>\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\sup_{Rx\in\mathcal{A}_{R}}\int_{0}^{\diam(\mathcal{A}_{R})}\frac{R^{\frac{p-d}{p-1}}\cdot R^{\frac{d-1}{p-1}}}{(Rr)^{\frac{d-1}{p-1}}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R}\cap B_{Rr}(Rx)}|V(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\!\!\!\>\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\!\!\sup_{Rx\in\mathcal{A}_{R}}\int_{0}^{\diam(\mathcal{A}_{R})}\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{(Rr)^{\frac{d-1}{p-1}}}\!\left[\!\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R}\cap B_{Rr}(Rx)}\!\!\!|\!V(y)|\>\!\mathrm{d}y\!\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\!\!\!\!\>\!\mathrm{d}(Rr)\!=\!\|V\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Thus, if
Q_{p,A,V}
has a Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
, then

(4.5)

\|V_{R}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})}=\|V_{R}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{R}/R)}=\|V\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{R})}\leq C,

where
C>0
is a positive constant independent of
R
.

The limiting dilation process is defined as follows. Let
\{R_{n}\}
be a sequence of positive numbers satisfying
R_{n}\rightarrow\zeta
, and set
Y:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Omega/{R_{n}}
. Clearly,
Y=(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
\zeta=0
or
\zeta=\infty
, and assume that

(4.6)

\displaystyle A_{R_{n}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}\quad\mbox{in the weak topology of }L^{\infty}_{\loc}(Y,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\mbox{and }

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}V_{R_{n}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}\quad\mbox{in the weak toplogy of }\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(Y)\mbox{ if }1<p\leq 2,\\
V_{R_{n}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}\quad\mbox{with resect to the quasinorm of }\mathfrak{W}^{p}_{\loc}(Y)\mbox{ if }p>2,\end{cases}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Define the
limiting dilated equation
with respect to (
4.1
) and the sequence
\{R_{n}\}
(which satisfies the conditions (
4.6
) and (
4.7
)) by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\{R_{n}\}}(Q)(w)=\mathcal{D}^{\{R_{n}\}}(Q_{p,A,V})(w):=Q_{p,\mathbb{A},\mathbb{V}}(w)

(4.8)

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{p,\mathbb{A}}(w)+\mathbb{V}|w|^{p-2}w=0\quad\mbox{on }Y.

Next, we show that the property of having a Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
is preserved under the limiting dilation process
Q_{p,A,V}\mapsto\mathcal{D}^{\{R_{n}\}}(Q)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
1<p<d
and assume that
A
and
V
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
. Suppose that (
4.1
) has a Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta\in\{0,\infty\}\subset\partial\hat{\Omega}
. Then for any sequence
R_{n}\rightarrow\zeta
which satisfies conditions (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), the corresponding limiting dilated equation
\mathcal{D}^{\{R_{n}\}}(Q)(w)=0
in
Y
has Fuchsian-type singularity at
\zeta
.

Proof.

By our assumption, there exists an essential set
\mathcal{A}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{n}=\{x\in\Omega:aR_{n}<|x|<bR_{n}\}
and a constant
C>0
such that

\|V\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{n})}\leq C,\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

We claim that

\|\mathbb{V}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{n})}\leq C,\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since for each
n
,
\mathcal{A}_{n}/R_{n}
is a fixed annular set
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\{x\in\Omega:a<|x|<b\}
, we have for any fixed
k
sufficiently large:

\displaystyle\!\|\mathbb{V}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}/R_{k})}=\|\mathbb{V}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})}\!\leq\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\inf}\|V_{R_{n}}\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{n}/R_{n})}\!\!=\!\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\inf}\|V\|_{\mathfrak{W}^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{n})}\!\!\leq\!\!C.

where

, while for

\mathrm{C}^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq|\xi|_{\mathbb{A}(x)}^{2}\leq C|\xi|^{2}
for all

the lemma follows.

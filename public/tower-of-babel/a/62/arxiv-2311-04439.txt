A Geometric Extension of the Itô-Wentzell and Kunita’s Formulas
Aythami Bethencourt de León 1, 2, ∗, So Takao 1, 3

Abstract
. We extend the Itô-Wentzell formula for the evolution along a continuous semimartingale of a time-dependent stochastic field driven by a continuous semimartingale to tensor field-valued stochastic processes on manifolds. More concretely, we investigate how the pull-back (respectively, the push-forward) by a stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms of a time-dependent stochastic tensor field driven by a continuous semimartingale evolves with time, deriving it under suitable regularity conditions. We call this result the Kunita-Itô-Wentzell (KIW) formula for the advection of tensor-valued stochastic processes. Equations of this nature bear significance in stochastic fluid dynamics and well-posedness by noise problems, facilitating the development of certain geometric extensions within existing theories. Key words: Itô-Wentzell formula; stochastic processes on manifolds; tensor-valued semimartingales; Lie transport equation.

2.1.
Hölder spaces on Euclidean domains

Let
\alpha\in(0,1).
We say that a Borel-measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is (locally)
\alpha
-Hölder continuous if for any compact subset
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(7)

\displaystyle[f]_{\alpha,W}:=\sup_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in W}\frac{|f(\boldsymbol{x})-f(\boldsymbol{y})|}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

We denote the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions by
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
which is a Fréchet space endowed with the seminorm (
7
).
This definition can be straightforwardly extended to functions of multiple components, in which case we employ the notation
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m}).
For
k\in\mathbb{N},
we define
C^{k+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the space of functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
having continuous derivatives of order
i=0,\ldots,k,
and
k
-th derivatives of class
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Again, this definition can easily be extended to vector-valued functions. As before,
C^{k+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
defines a Fréchet space, whose corresponding seminorm is given by

(8)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{f}]_{k+\alpha,W}:=\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in W}|\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})|+\sum_{1\leq|\boldsymbol{v}|<k}\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in W}|D^{\boldsymbol{v}}\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})|+\sum_{|\boldsymbol{v}|=k}\sup_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in W}\frac{|D^{\boldsymbol{v}}\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})-D^{\boldsymbol{v}}\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{y})|}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|^{\alpha}}.

2.2.
Hölder sections of tensor bundles over manifolds

Definition 2.1

For

The above definition can be checked to be chart-independent, owing to the smoothness of transition maps between charts.
We denote the Fréchet space of

.

C^{k}

2.3.
Some operations defined on tensor fields

Pull-back and push-forward.

Let
\varphi:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
be a smooth mapping between manifolds. The push-forward
\varphi_{*}X
of a vector field
X\in\Gamma(TQ)
with respect to
\varphi
is a vector field on
Q^{\prime}
whose expression in local coordinates is given by

\displaystyle(\varphi_{*}X)^{i}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\boldsymbol{x})):=X^{j}(\boldsymbol{x})\frac{\partial\varphi^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}(\boldsymbol{x}).

Similarly, the pull-back
\varphi^{*}\alpha
of a one-form
\alpha\in\Gamma(T^{*}Q^{\prime})
with respect to
\varphi
is a one-form on
Q
whose local expression is given by

\displaystyle(\varphi^{*}\alpha)_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\alpha_{j}(\boldsymbol{\varphi(x)})\frac{\partial\varphi^{j}}{\partial x^{i}}(\boldsymbol{x}).

Furthermore, when
\varphi
is a diffeomorphism, one can also define the pull-back of a vector field
X\in\Gamma(TQ^{\prime})
as
\varphi^{*}X:=(\varphi^{-1})_{*}X\in\Gamma(TQ)
, and the push-forward of a one-form
\alpha\in T^{*}Q
as
\varphi_{*}\alpha:=(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\alpha\in\Gamma(T^{*}Q^{\prime})
.
We can check that these definitions are indeed independent of charts.

This notion can also be extended to arbitrary
(r,s)
-tensor fields. Indeed, for a smooth tensor field

we define its pull-back
\varphi^{*}K

\displaystyle(\varphi^{*}K)(x)(\alpha^{1}(x),\ldots,\alpha^{r}(x),v_{1}(x),\ldots,v_{s}(x))

12
) than the formula for the push-forward (
19
). This is natural since in the latter formula, the Lie derivatives appear outside the push-forward, so at least three spatial derivatives of the flow are required to make sense of equation (
19
). In the former however, only one derivative is necessary as the Lie derivatives appear inside the pull-back.
Hence, equations (
18
) and (
19
) are not strictly counterparts of one another, as one might expect. That is, they cannot be viewed as equivalent statements, where one is merely derived from the other by replacing
\phi_{t}
with its inverse.

•

Our main results (Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
) are stated up to a stopping time since on a general manifold, the flow of (
11
) under Assumption
2
does not necessarily admit a global solution. We can however get global-in-time statements under stronger conditions. For example, when the manifold
Q
is compact, then (
11
) admits a global flow if the drift and diffusion vector fields satisfy Assumption
2
(see Proposition
2.5
). Alternatively, when
Q
admits a Riemannian structure, then we have a well-defined notion of “global Hölder spaces”
C_{b}^{\alpha}(T^{(r,s)}Q)
on tensor fields over
Q
, which is a Banach space of tensor fields complete under the norm

(36)

\displaystyle\|K\|_{\alpha}:=\sup_{x,y\in Q}\frac{|K(x)-\tau_{y\rightarrow x}K(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}},

where
d(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the geodesic distance and
\tau_{y\rightarrow x}
denotes parallel transport of tensors from point
y
to
x
along the geodesic.
Assuming that the vector fields are globally Hölder, we can also guarantee the existence of a global-in-time solution almost surely.

We conclude by noting that the results we established in this paper could be potentially useful in the following applications, which we plan to explore in future works:

•

Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
can be applied in stochastic fluid dynamics to derive stochastic equations of motion on manifolds (e.g. the sphere) from first principles using conservation laws. Indeed, we can generalise the applications established in Section 4 of
[
1
]
on the Euclidean space to statements on general manifolds. Furthermore, conservation laws for new types of objects appearing naturally in physics (such as vector fields or one-form densities) can be included.

•

Our results may be useful in the field of well-posedness/regularisation by noise, where the classic Itô-Wentzell formula plays a prominent role. For example, in the work
[
12
]
, the Itô-Wentzell formula is used as a key tool to establish uniqueness of solutions to the transport equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
perturbed by transport noise. Indeed, the generalised KIW formulas we derive here could be useful for proving similar statements in more general settings, such as when the transport equation is defined over manifolds, or when transport of general tensors (instead of scalars, or a tuple of scalars as considered in
[
12
]
) are considered. For more concrete details, we refer the readers to Section 5 of
[
12
]
.

Appendix A
Time extension

In this appendix, we discuss how to extend the time window in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, so that the main result holds for all
t\in[0,\tau(x))
, where
\tau(x)
is the maximal existence stopping time of the flow
\phi_{t}(x)
. To this end,
we follow the argument in
[
18
]
, Lemma 4.8.2, which proves the existence of a unique maximal solution of SDEs defined on manifolds under Assumption
2
. The argument can be used to extend the time window of any result involving the flow
\phi_{t}
, up to its maximal time of existence.

The idea of the argument is as follows. First, note that under Assumption
1
,
Q
is metrizable by the Urysohn metrization theorem
[
5
]
. Hence, we can define a distance function
d:Q\times Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
and treat
Q
as a metric space, whose metric topology coincides with the manifold topology.
Now consider three countable families
(U_{l})_{l=1}^{\infty},(V_{l})_{l=1}^{\infty},(W_{l})_{l=1}^{\infty}
of open neighbourhoods of
Q
, with the following properties:

(1)

For all
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
U_{l},V_{l},W_{l}
are open balls with a common centre and radii of
3r,2r,r
respectively, for arbitrary
r>0
.

(2)

(W_{l})_{l=1}^{\infty}
is an open cover for
Q
. That is, for any
x\in Q,
there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\in W_{l}
.

Since
(W_{l})_{l=1}^{\infty}
is an open cover, we set
x\in W_{1}
without loss of generality. Next, we extract a sub-family of open neighbourhoods
(U_{l_{m}})_{m=1}^{\infty},(V_{l_{m}})_{m=1}^{\infty},(W_{l_{m}})_{m=1}^{\infty}
and a sequence of stopping times
(\tau_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
by applying the argument presented in Algorithm
1
, which we expressed in pseudocode format for ease of presentation.

Initialisation:
l_{1}=1
,
x_{1}=x
,
\tau_{1}=0
.

for

m=1,2,3,\ldots

do

(1)

Restrict to the local chart
U_{l_{m}}
and prove “result” as a statement on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
until the stopping time
\tau_{m+1}(x_{m}):=\inf\{t>0:\phi_{\tau_{m},t}(x_{m})\notin V_{l_{m}}\},
which we denote by
\tau_{m+1}
for simplicity.

(2)

Verify that “result” is independent of this choice of chart.

(3)

Choose
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi_{\tau_{m},\tau_{m+1}}(x_{m})\in W_{m^{\prime}}\backslash\bigcup_{\alpha<m^{\prime}}W_{\alpha}
, which is always possible since
(W_{l})_{l=1}^{\infty}
is an open cover of
Q
.

(4)

Set
l_{m+1}=m^{\prime}
and
x_{m+1}=\phi_{\tau_{m},\tau_{m+1}}(x_{m})
. Note that since
x_{m+1}\in U_{l_{m}}\cap U_{l_{m+1}}
, we can return to step 1.

end

for

Algorithm 1

Argument to extend the time window of a “result” involving the flow
\phi_{t}
.

In plain language, this algorithm allows us to construct a path

U_{l_{1}}\cup U_{l_{2}}\cup\cdots
for
\phi_{t}(x)
to traverse (i.e., for each
t
there exists
m
such that
\phi_{t}(x)\in U_{l_{m}}
), such that on each
U_{l_{m}}
, the “result” reduces to a statement in the Euclidean space. Moreover, in
[
18
, Lemma 4.8.2]
, Kunita shows that under such construction, the maximal time of existence
\tau(x)
for
\phi_{t}(x)
can be computed as

(37)

\displaystyle\tau(x)=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{M}\tau_{m+1}(x_{m}).

Hence, if we can iterate the algorithm indefinitely, then the result holds for all
t\in[0,\tau(x)).

Coming back to our proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have already shown that in the first iteration (
m=1
), steps 1 and 2 in Algorithm
1
hold for any
x_{1}=x\in Q
, where we replace “result” by the statement that the KIW formula for tensor-valued processes (
18
) holds (recall that in our proof, the chart was chosen arbitrarily, hence it also holds for the specific chart
U_{1}
given in the algorithm). Clearly, steps 3 and 4 hold automatically, which takes us to the next iteration
m=2
. Note that our proof of Theorem
3.1
still holds by replacing
\phi_{t}
with
\phi_{s,t}
for any
0<s\leq t
(i.e., the argument does not depend on the initial time of the flow) and due to the arbitrariness of the spatial variable
x_{m}\in Q
, we can verify steps 1 and 2 again for the updated
x_{m},\tau_{m}
. By induction, the loop continues indefinitely. This concludes our proof that (
18
) holds for all
t\in[0,\tau(x))
.

Optimal control of diffusion equation with missing data governed by Dirichlet fractional Laplacian
J-D. Djida, P.F. Soh, G. Mophou

Abstract
We consider an optimal control problem of diffusion equation with missing data governed by the fractional Laplacian with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on an arbitrary interaction domain disjoint from the domain of the state equation. We assume that the unknown initial condition belongs to an appropriate space of infinite dimension, the so-called space of uncertainties. The key tools we used in order to characterize the optimal control is the no-regret and low-regret control developed by J.L Lions.

We assume that we do not know when the phenomenon began. To solve this problem we use the notion of no-regret and least regret control

As far as we know this concept of optimal control has not been used to study evolution equation with missing initial data involving fractional Laplacian operators. As the low regret control is obtained by relaxing the cost associated to the no-regret control, we obtain that the low-regret control converge towards the no-regret control that we characterize with an optimality system.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we start by introducing some spaces and their norms which will be used throughout the paper. Then we provide some elementary properties of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
that we will need.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N},\,N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
with Lipschitz boundary. For any
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, we recall that the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is defined as follows:

W^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in L^{p}(\Omega):\;\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dx~dy<\infty\right\}.

It is endowed with the natural norm

\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p}\;dx+\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dx~dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

we set

W_{0}^{s,2}(\overline{\Omega}):=\left\{w\in W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\;w=0\;\mbox{ on }\;\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\}.

If we denote by
\mathbb{D}(\Omega)
the space of continuously infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\Omega
, then
\mathbb{D}(\Omega)
is dense in
W_{0}^{s,2}(\overline{\Omega})
because the boundary of
\Omega
is Lipschitz and continuous ( see
[
15
]
).
We then denote by
W^{-s,2}(\overline{\Omega})=\left(W^{s,2}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\right)^{\prime}
, the dual of the Hilbert space
W^{s,p}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
.

We recall the following continuous embedding of fractional Sobolev spaces.

1)

if
0<s\leq s^{\prime}<1
,

W^{s^{\prime},2}(\overline{\Omega})\hookrightarrow W^{s,2}(\overline{\Omega}).

Hence there exists a constant
C(N,s)\geq 1
such that, for any
w\in W^{s^{\prime},2}(\Omega)
:

\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,2}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C(N,s)\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s^{\prime},2}(\overline{\Omega})}.

2)

for
0<s<1
,

W_{0}^{s,2}(\overline{\Omega})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{-s,2}(\overline{\Omega}).

Remark 2.1

Set

(2.1)

W(0,T):=\displaystyle\left\{\rho\,\big|\,\,\rho\in L^{2}\left((0,T);W^{s,2}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\right),\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho\in L^{2}\left((0,T);W^{-s,2}(\overline{\Omega})\right)\right\}.

Then from
[
20
, Theorem 10]
, we have
W(0,T)\subset\mathcal{C}\left([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\right)

Next we recall the definition of the fractional nonlocal operators that we are interested in this work, the fractional laplacian in the integral formulation.

Let us consider the following space

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{w:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\;\mbox{ measurable},\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|w(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{N+2s}}\;dx<\infty\right\}.

For
w\in\mathcal{L}_{s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and for a positive small enough constant
\varepsilon
we set

(-\Delta)_{\varepsilon}^{s}w(x):=C_{N,s}\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\;|x-y|>\varepsilon\}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;dy,\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
C_{N,s}
is a normalization constant, given by

C_{N,s}:=\frac{s2^{2s}\Gamma\left(\frac{2s+N}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)}.

The
fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{s}
of
w\in\mathcal{L}_{s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined by the following singular integral:

(2.2)

(-\Delta)^{s}w(x)=C_{N,s}\,\mbox{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;dy=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}(-\Delta)_{\varepsilon}^{s}w(x),\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

provided that the limit exists.

For more details on the fractional Laplace operator we refer to
[
4
,
7
,
11
,
17
,
42
,
41
]
and references therein.

For
w\in W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we recall the
nonlocal normal derivative
\mathcal{N}_{s}
given by

(2.3)

\mathcal{N}_{s}w(x):=C_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;dy,\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

The following result is taken from
[
18
, Lemma 3.2]
and
[
40
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Lemma 2.2

The operator
\mathcal{N}_{s}
maps
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
into
W_{\rm loc}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
, where

W_{\rm loc}^{s,2}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega):~w\varphi\in W^{s,2}(\Omega),\quad\forall~~\varphi\in\mathbb{D}(\Omega)\right\}

It follows from Lemma
2.2
, that if
w\in W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
\mathcal{N}_{s}w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
.

We thus have the integration by parts formula.

Proposition 2.3

Let
w\in W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
(-\Delta)^{s}w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then for every
v\in W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the identity

(2.4)

\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(w(x)-w(y)\right)\left(v(x)-v(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}~dx~dy=\int_{\Omega}v(-\Delta)^{s}w~dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v\mathcal{N}_{s}w~dx,

holds, where

\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}=(\Omega\times\Omega)\cup(\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega))\cup((\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega).

Moreover, if
w=0
in
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
then (
2.4
) can be rewritten as

(2.5)

\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left(w(x)-w(y)\right)\left(v(x)-v(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}~dx~dy=\int_{\Omega}v(-\Delta)^{s}w\;dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v\mathcal{N}_{s}w~dx.

Consider the following nonlocal fractional diffusion equation:

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}p+(-\Delta)^{s}p&=f&\textrm{ in }Q,\\
p&=0&\textrm{ on }\Xi,\\
p(0,x)&=p^{0}&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
0<s<1,

f\in L^{2}(Q)
,
p^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\Xi:=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T)
, for
T>0
. The associated definition of weak solutions of (
2.6
) with finite energy is given as follows.

Definition 2.4

We say that
p\in W(0,T)
is a finite energy solution to problem (
2.6
), if the identity

(2.7)

\begin{array}[]{llllll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}w~\partial_{t}p~dx~dt+\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(p(x,t)-p(y,t))(w(x,t)-w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx~dy~dt=\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}fv~dx~dt,\end{array}

holds, for any
w\in L^{2}((0,T);W_{0}^{s,2}(\overline{\Omega}))
.

Thanks to the continuous imbedding of
L^{2}(Q)
into
L^{2}((0,T);W^{-s,2}(\overline{\Omega}))
, we have the following results
[
20
]
.

Proposition 2.5

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(Q)
, then for any
p^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
problem (
2.6
) has a unique finite energy solution in
W(0,T)
.

Remark 2.6

Since
\displaystyle\partial_{t}p\in L^{2}((0,T);W^{-s,2}(\overline{\Omega}))
, the first integral in (
2.7
) should be written

\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\partial_{t}p,w\right\rangle_{W^{-s,2}(\overline{\Omega}),W^{s,2}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})}dt.

But using an appropriate change of variable and a cut-off argument, we can prove as in
[
2
]
that if
p
is a unique finite energy solution of (
2.6
) then
\displaystyle\partial_{t}p\in L^{2}(Q)
. Moreover we have the following estimate

(2.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}p\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(s,N,T)\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\left\|p^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

(2.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|p\right\|_{W^{s,2}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(s,N,T)\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\left\|p^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

where
C(s,N,T)>0
is a positive constant depending on
s,N
and
T
.

Remark 2.7

From the Remark
2.6
, we have that
p
is solution of

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}p+(-\Delta)^{s}p&=0&\textrm{ in }Q,\\
p&=0&\textrm{ on }\Xi,\\
p(0,x)&=0&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\end{array}\right.

with
0<s<1
is identically zero.

Remark 2.8

By a change of variable
t\mapsto T-t
, equation (
2.6
) can be rewritten as

(2.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{p}+(-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{p}&=\tilde{f}&\textrm{ in }Q,\\

where
0<s<1,

\tilde{p}=p(x,T-t),

\tilde{f}=f(T-t)
. Therefore,
\tilde{p}
is a finite energy solution to problem (
2.11
), if the identity

(2.12)

\begin{array}[]{llllll}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}w\partial_{t}\tilde{p}dx~dt+\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(\tilde{p}(x,t)-\tilde{p}(y,t))(w(x,t)-w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx~dy~dt=\\

holds, for any

. Moreover, if

, then for any

) has a unique finite energy solution in

and the following estimates holds:

(2.13)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}\tilde{p}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq

(2.14)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{p}\right\|_{W^{s,2}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})}

3
Formulation of the problem

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let also
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N},\,N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
with Lipschitz boundary.
For any time
T>0
we set
Q=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
,
\Xi=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T)
and we consider the following nonlocal fractional diffusion equation:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}q+(-\Delta)^{s}q&=f+v&\textrm{ in }Q,\\
q&=0&\textrm{ on }\Xi,\\
q(0,x)&=g&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
f
in
L^{2}(Q),
the control function
v
in
L^{2}(Q)
. The function
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is unknown. Under the above assumption on the data, it has been shown that from Proposition
2.5
the problem (
3.1
) admits a solution
q(v,g)=q(x,t;v,g)\in W(0,T)
that depends on the control
v
and on the missing initial data
g
. Since we want to bring the state
q
solution of (
3.1
) to a desired state
z_{d}\in L^{2}(Q)
, we consider the cost function

J(v,g)=\left\|q(v,g)-z_{d}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\aleph\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)},

where
\aleph>0
. Observing that the optimal control problem

\inf_{v\in\mathscr{U}_{ad}}J(v,g),\qquad\forall~g\in L^{2}(Q),

has no sense, we are interested for any
\gamma>0
in the following inf-sup problem:

(3.2)

\inf_{v\in\mathscr{U}_{ad}}\sup_{g\in L^{2}(\Omega)}\left[J_{\gamma}(v,g)-J_{\gamma}(0,g)\right],

where

(3.3)

J_{\gamma}(v,g)=\left\|q(v,g)-z_{d}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\aleph\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}-\gamma\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This problem is called low-regret control problem. It was introduced by J. L. Lions
[
21
]
. This concept is an extension of the no-regret control also introduced by Lions which is stated as follows: find
v\in L^{2}(Q)
solution of

(3.4)

\inf_{v\in\mathscr{U}_{ad}}\sup_{g\in L^{2}(\Omega)}\left[J(v,g)-J(0,g)\right].

Actually, the control we are looking for is the no-regret control. But this control problem is in general difficult to characterize. That is the reason why we start by studying the low-regret problem (
3.2
) which is easy to characterize. Then we will show that when
\gamma
tends zero this control tends to the no-regret control. Finally we will obtain an optimality system for the no-regret control as a limit of the optimality system of the low-regret control.

4
Study of inf-sup problem

The aim of this section is to solve inf-sup problem given by the problem (
3.2
). Let
q(v,g):=q(x,t;v,g)\in W(0,T)
be solution of (
3.1
). Consider
q(v,0):=q(x,t;v,0)
,
q(0,g):=q(x,t;0,g)
and
q(0,0):=q(x,t;0,0)
respectively solutions of

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}q(v,0)+(-\Delta)^{s}q(v,0)&=f+v&\textrm{ in }Q,\\
q(v,0)&=0&\textrm{ on }\Xi,\\
q(0;v,0)&=0&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}q(0,g)+(-\Delta)^{s}q(0,g)&=f{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}+v}&\textrm{ in }Q,\\
q(0,g)&=0&\textrm{ on }\Xi,\\
q(0;0,g)&=g&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}q(0,0)+(-\Delta)^{s}q(0,0)&=f{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}+v}&\textrm{ in }Q,\\
q(0,0)&=0&\textrm{ on }\Xi,\\
q(0;0,0)&=0&\textrm{ in }\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Thus, according to the assumption on the data,
q(v,0)
,
q(0,g)
and
q(0,0)
belong to
W(0,T)
and we have the following result.

Lemma 4.1

For any
v\in L^{2}(Q)
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

(4.4)

\begin{array}[]{llll}J_{\gamma}(v,g)-J_{\gamma}(0,g)&=&J_{\gamma}(v,0)-J_{\gamma}(0,0)\\
&+&\displaystyle 2\int_{Q}\left[q(0,g)-q(0,0)\right]\left[q(v,0)-q(0,0)\right]dx~dt-\gamma\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\end{array}

where
J_{\gamma}
is the functional defined in (
3.3
),
q(v,0)
,
q(0,g)
,
q(0,0)
are respectively solutions of (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).

Proof.

The proof of this lemma relies on simple computations. Indeed we first observe that one can decompose
q(v,g)
as

(4.5)

q(v,g)=q(v,0)+q(0,g)-q(0,0),

so that replacing
q(v,g)
by its expression in (
3.3
) yields

(4.6)

J_{\gamma}(v,g)=\left\|\left(q(v,0)-z_{d}\right)+\left(q(0,g)-z_{d}\right)-\left(q(0,0)-z_{d}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\aleph\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}-\gamma\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

After some computations and identifications of some of the terms such as

\begin{array}[]{rll}J_{\gamma}(v,0)&=&\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left[q(v,0)-z_{d}\right]\left[q(v,0)-z_{d}\right]dt~dx+\aleph\parallel v\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)},\\
J_{\gamma}(0,g)&=&\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left[q(0,g)-z_{d}\right]\left[q(v,0)-z_{d}\right]dt~dx-\gamma\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\\
J_{\gamma}(0,0)&=&\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left[q(0,0)-z_{d}\right]\left[q(0,0)-z_{d}\right]dt~dx,\end{array}

its comes that (
4.6
) takes the form

\begin{array}[]{rll}J_{\gamma}(v,g)&=&J_{\gamma}(v,0)+J_{\gamma}(0,g)-J_{\gamma}(0,0)\\
&+&\displaystyle 2\int_{Q}\left(q(v,0)-q(0,0)\right)\left(q(0,g)-q(0,0)\right)dx~dt-\gamma\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

which is the desired result.

\Box

From Lemma
4.1
, we prove the following result.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\gamma>0.
Then problem (
3.2
) is equivalent to the following problem: find
u^{\gamma}\in L^{2}(Q)
solution to the optimal control:

(4.7)

\inf_{v\in L^{2}(Q)}J^{\gamma}(v),

where

(4.8)

J^{\gamma}(v)=J_{\gamma}(v,0)-J_{\gamma}(0,0)+\frac{1}{\gamma}\|\xi(0;v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

with
J_{\gamma}
given by (
3.3
) and
\xi(v)=\xi(x,t;v)\in W(0,T)
, solution of

(4.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}-\partial_{t}\xi(v)+(-\Delta)^{s}\xi(v)&=&q(v,0)-q(0,0)&\textrm{in}&Q,\\
\xi(v)&=&0&\textrm{on}&\Xi,\\
\xi(x,T;v)&=&0&\textrm{in}&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Proof.
From the fact that
q(v,0)-q(0,0)\in L^{2}(Q)
, Proposition
2.5
allows to say that there exists a unique
\xi(v)\in W(0,T)
solution to the problem (
4.9
). Moreover there exists
C(s,N,T)>0
such that the following estimates hold:

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\xi(v)\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,T);W_{0}^{s,2}(\overline{\Omega})\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(s,N,T)\left\|q(v,0)-q(0,0)\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)},

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\partial_{t}\xi(v)\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,T);W_{0}^{-s,2}(\overline{\Omega})\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(s,N,T)\left\|q(v,0)-q(0,0)\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}.

Next we set
z(g):=z=q(0,g)-q(0,0)
. Then in view of (
4.2
) and (
4.3
),
z
verifies

(4.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}z(g)+(-\Delta)^{s}z(g)&=&0&\textrm{in}&Q,\\
z(g)&=&0&\textrm{on}&\Xi,\\
z(T,v)&=&g&\textrm{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

As
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that (

\rho\in\mathbb{D}\left(\Omega\times(0,T)\right)
and integrate by parts, we get

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\phi^{\gamma}\left(-\partial_{t}\rho+(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\right)~dx~dt-\int_{Q}(q^{\gamma}-z_{d})\rho~dx~dt=\displaystyle\int_{Q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\psi^{\gamma}\rho~dx~dt.

Passing this latter inequality at the limit when
\gamma\to 0
while using (
4.80
) and (
4.63
), we obtain that

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\int_{Q}\phi\left(-\partial_{t}\rho+(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\right)~dx~dt-\int_{Q}(q-z_{d})\rho~dx~dt&=&\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to 0}\int_{Q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\psi^{\gamma}\rho~dx~dt,\\
\forall\rho\in\mathbb{D}\left(\Omega\times(0,T)\right),\end{array}

which by integrating by parts again gives

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\int_{Q}\rho\left(\partial_{t}\phi+(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\right)~dx~dt-\int_{Q}(q-z_{d})\rho~dx~dt&=&\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to 0}\int_{Q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\psi^{\gamma}\rho~dx~dt,\\
\forall\rho\in\mathbb{D}\left(\Omega\times(0,T)\right).\end{array}

Hence we deduce that there there exists
\tau_{2}
such that

\partial_{t}\phi+(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=q-z_{d}+\tau_{2}\in Q,

and it follows from second and third equations in (
4.49
) that
\phi
satisfies (
4.60
).
Now, in view of (
4.65
) we have that

\xi^{\gamma}(0)\rightarrow\xi(0;u)=0\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega).

As a consequence, we have that
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g\xi(x,0;u)~dx=0,
from which according to Remark
4.3
means that
u
is solution of the no-regret control problem (
3.4
).

\Box

5
Conclusion

The optimal control of diffusion equation with missing data is an important result in the theory of control of PDEs, and its fractional counterpart should have no less signiﬁcance in the theory of control of fractional PDEs. We discussed about the optimal control of diffusion equation with missing data governed by Dirichlet fractional Laplacian. We proved that if the control acts on
Q
then we can characterise the limit of the optimal control problem (
3.2
) by optimality system given by (
4.57
)-(
4.61
).

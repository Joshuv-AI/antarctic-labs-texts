Basic background

Let
\mathfrak{L}:\mathbb{R}\left[x\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(5)

A sequence

\mathfrak{L}\left[p_{k}p_{n}\right]=h_{n}\delta_{k,n},\quad k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad h_{n}\neq 0,

sequence
. Notice that it is unique with the convention that the leading
coefficient is a positive real number. In such a case, the linear functional
is said to be

, If

for every

then the
linear functional is said to be

.

Let’s denote by

the sequence of

polynomials, orthogonal with respect to
\mathfrak{L}
.
From (

\mathfrak{L}\left[xP_{k}P_{n}\right]=0,\quad k\neq n,n\pm 1,

and therefore the polynomials

satisfy the

xP_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+\beta_{n}P_{n}(x)+\gamma_{n}P_{n-1}(x),n\geq 1,

with initial values
P_{0}(x)=1,

P_{1}(x)=x-\beta_{0}.
The coefficients

\beta_{n}=\frac{\mathfrak{L}\left[xP_{n}^{2}\right]}{h_{n}},\quad\gamma_{n}=\frac{\mathfrak{L}\left[xP_{n}P_{n-1}\right]}{h_{n-1}},\quad n\geq 1,

with initial values

\beta_{0}=\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{0}}.

(9)

Note that using (

h_{n}=\mathfrak{L}\left[x^{n}P_{n}\right]=\mathfrak{L}\left[xP_{n}P_{n-1}\right]=\gamma_{n}h_{n-1},n\geq 1,

\gamma_{n}=\frac{h_{n}}{h_{n-1}},\quad n\geq 1.

Pearson equation

Proposition 3

Let

Proof.
Let

We have

\displaystyle L\left[\partial_{x}\left(\phi p\right)\right]={\displaystyle\int\limits_{-z}^{z}}\partial_{x}\left(\phi p\right)e^{-x^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\left[\phi\left(x\right)p\left(x\right)e^{-x^{2}}\right]_{-z}^{z}-{\displaystyle\int\limits_{-z}^{z}}-2x\phi\left(x\right)p\left(x\right)e^{-x^{2}}dx=L\left[2x\phi p\right],

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\frac{u_{n+k}(z)}{t^{2n+1}}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq k}}\frac{u_{n}(z)}{t^{2n-2k+1}}=t^{2k}\left(S-{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k-1}}\frac{u_{n}(z)}{t^{2n+1}}\right).

\displaystyle-2z^{2}t^{2}\left(S-\frac{u_{0}(z)}{t}\right)-z^{2}t\partial_{t}S=0,

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}\left(2n+3\right)\frac{u_{n+1}(z)}{t^{2n+1}}=t^{2}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}}\left(2n+1\right)\frac{u_{n}(z)}{t^{2n+1}}=t^{2}\left(-t\partial_{t}S-\frac{u_{0}(z)}{t}\right).

4
Laguerre-Freud equations

The (generally nonlinear) equations satisfied by the coefficients of the
three-term recurrence relation (
7
) are known in the literature as

Laguerre-Freud equations
(see
[
4
]
). They can be consider
discrete analogues of the Painlevé equations
[
19
]
.

First of all, we will find a second order nonlinear difference equation that
the parameters of the three term recurrence relation satisfy.

Theorem 9

The coefficients
\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)
satisfy the Laguerre-Freud
equation

\frac{z^{2}}{2}=\gamma_{n}\left(\gamma_{n-1}+\gamma_{n}-z^{2}+\frac{1}{2}-n\right)-\gamma_{n+1}\left(\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n+2}-z^{2}-n-\frac{3}{2}\right).

(38)

Proof.
Taking
p(x)=P_{n}P_{n+1}
in (
18
), we get

L\left[\partial_{x}\left(\phi P_{n}P_{n+1}\right)\right]=L\left[\psi P_{n}P_{n+1}\right].

(39)

The left hand side gives

L\left[\partial_{x}\left(\phi P_{n}P_{n+1}\right)\right]=2L\left[xP_{n}P_{n+1}\right]+L\left[(x^{2}-z^{2})\left(P_{n+1}\partial_{x}P_{n}+P_{n}\partial_{x}P_{n+1}\right)\right]

and using (
6
), we have

\displaystyle 2L\left[xP_{n}P_{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=2h_{n+1},\quad L\left[\left(x^{2}-z^{2}\right)P_{n+1}\partial_{x}P_{n}\right]=nh_{n+1},

\displaystyle L\left[-z^{2}P_{n}\partial_{x}P_{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=-z^{2}\left(n+1\right)h_{n}.

From (
4
), we see that

x^{2}P_{n}=P_{n+2}+\left(\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}\right)P_{n}+\gamma_{n-1}\gamma_{n}P_{n-2},

(40)

and using (
15
) we get

\partial_{x}P_{n+1}=\left(n+1\right)P_{n}+\left[2c_{n+1}-\left(n+1\right)\gamma_{n}\right]P_{n-2}+O\left(x^{n-4}\right).

Hence,

L\left[x^{2}P_{n}\partial_{x}P_{n+1}\right]=\left(n+1\right)\left(\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}\right)h_{n}+\left[2c_{n+1}-\left(n+1\right)\gamma_{n}\right]\gamma_{n-1}\gamma_{n}h_{n-2}.

Using (
10
), we conclude that

L\left[x^{2}P_{n}\partial_{x}P_{n+1}\right]=\left(n+1\right)\left(\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}\right)h_{n}+\left[2c_{n+1}-\left(n+1\right)\gamma_{n}\right]h_{n},

and therefore

L\left[\partial_{x}\left(\phi P_{n}P_{n+1}\right)\right]=\left[\left(2n+3\right)\gamma_{n+1}+2c_{n+1}-\left(n+1\right)z^{2}\right]h_{n}.

The right hand side in (
39
) reads

L\left[\psi P_{n}P_{n+1}\right]=L\left[2x(x^{2}-z^{2})P_{n}P_{n+1}\right],

and since (
4
) gives

x^{3}P_{n}=P_{n+3}+\left(\gamma_{n+2}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}\right)P_{n+1}

+\gamma_{n}\left(\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n-1}\right)P_{n-1}+\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}\gamma_{n-2}P_{n-3},

(41)

we have

L\left[\psi P_{n}P_{n+1}\right]=2\left(\gamma_{n+2}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}\right)h_{n+1}-2z^{2}h_{n+1}

or, using (
10
),

L\left[\psi P_{n}P_{n+1}\right]=2\gamma_{n+1}\left(\gamma_{n+2}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}-z^{2}\right)h_{n}.

Thus,

\left(2n+3\right)\gamma_{n+1}+2c_{n+1}-\left(n+1\right)z^{2}=2\gamma_{n+1}\left(\gamma_{n+2}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}-z^{2}\right),

(42)

and shifting
n\rightarrow n-1,

\left(2n+1\right)\gamma_{n}+2c_{n}-nz^{2}=2\gamma_{n}\left(\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n-1}-z^{2}\right).

(43)

Subtracting (
43
) from (
42
) and using (
13
), we obtain

\left(2n+3\right)\gamma_{n+1}-\left(2n-1\right)\gamma_{n}-z^{2}=2\gamma_{n+1}\left(\gamma_{n+2}+\gamma_{n+1}-z^{2}\right)-2\gamma_{n}\left(\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n-1}-z^{2}\right)

and the result follows.

Remark 10

Theorem 11

The coefficients
\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)

From (

P_{n}^{2}\left(z;z\right)e^{-z^{2}}=\frac{\left(2n+1\right)h_{n}\left(z\right)-2\left[h_{n+1}\left(z\right)+\gamma_{n}^{2}\left(z\right)h_{n-1}\left(z\right)\right]}{2z}.

5
Structure relation and differential equation

Semiclassical polynomials (with respect to the derivative operator) are

holonomic functions
[
13
]
, meaning that they are solutions
of a linear ODE with polynomial coefficients. In this section, we shall find
the differential equation (in
x)
satisfied by
P_{n}\left(x;z\right).

5.1
Structure relation

One of the basic properties of semiclassical polynomials with respect to the
derivative operator, is a relation between
\partial_{x}P_{n}
and
P_{n}

[
10
]
. For the polynomials
P_{n}\left(x;z\right),
we have the
following result.

Theorem 17

The polynomials
P_{n}\left(x;z\right)
satisfy the
differential-recurrence relation

\phi\left(x;z\right)\partial_{x}P_{n+1}=\left(n+1\right)P_{n+2}+\lambda_{n}P_{n}+\tau_{n}P_{n-2},

(59)

where

\lambda_{n}\left(z\right)=\left[2\left(\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n+2}-z^{2}-1\right)-n\right]\gamma_{n+1},

(60)

and

\tau_{n}\left(z\right)=2\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}.

(61)

Proof.
If

\phi\left(x;z\right)\partial_{x}P_{n+1}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+2}}d_{n,k}\left(z\right)P_{k},

(62)

then using (
6
) and (
11
), we have

h_{k}d_{n,k}=L\left[\phi\partial_{x}P_{n+1}P_{k}\right]=0,\quad n+1\equiv k\ \operatorname{mod}\left(2\right),

(63)

since
L
is symmetric and
\phi
is an even polynomial of
x.
Using
(
19
), we get

\displaystyle h_{k}d_{n,k}

\displaystyle=L\left[\phi\partial_{x}\left(P_{n+1}P_{k}\right)\right]-L\left[\phi P_{n+1}\partial_{x}P_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=L\left[2x\left(\phi-1\right)P_{n+1}P_{k}\right]-L\left[\phi P_{n+1}\partial_{x}P_{k}\right].

Since the polynomials
P_{n}
are orthogonal, we conclude that
d_{n,k}=0
for

0\leq k<n-2,
and because we are working with monic polynomials, we see from
(
62
) that
d_{n,n+2}=n+1.
Hence, we obtain (
59
).

Using (
40
) and (
41
), we get

\displaystyle x\left(\phi-1\right)P_{k}

\displaystyle=\left[\allowbreak x^{3}-\left(z^{2}+1\right)x\right]P_{k}=P_{k+3}+\gamma_{k}\gamma_{k-1}\gamma_{k-2}P_{k-3}

\displaystyle+\left[\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k+1}+\gamma_{k+2}-\left(z^{2}+1\right)\right]P_{k+1}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{k}\left[\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k+1}+\gamma_{k-1}-\left(z^{2}+1\right)\right]P_{k-1}.

Using (
6
), it follows that

\displaystyle L\left[2x\left(\phi-1\right)P_{n+1}P_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n+2}-\left(z^{2}+1\right)\right]h_{n+1},

\displaystyle L\left[2x\left(\phi-1\right)P_{n+1}P_{n-2}\right]

\displaystyle=h_{n+1},

and since
\phi\partial_{x}P_{k}=kP_{k+1}\left(x\right)+O\left(x^{k}\right),
we see that

L\left[\phi P_{n+1}\partial_{x}P_{n}\right]=nh_{n+1},\quad L\left[\phi P_{n+1}\partial_{x}P_{n-2}\right]=0.

Hence, we conclude that

h_{n}\lambda_{n}=2\left[\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n+2}-\left(z^{2}+1\right)\right]h_{n+1}-nh_{n+1},\quad h_{n-2}\tau_{n}=2h_{n+1},

and using (
10
) we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=2\left[\left(\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{n+2}\right)-\left(z^{2}+1\right)-\frac{n}{2}\right]\gamma_{n+1},

\displaystyle\tau_{n}

\displaystyle=2\frac{h_{n+1}}{h_{n-2}}=2\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}.

5.2
Differential equation

We will now obtain a lowering operator acting on the variable
x
for

P_{n}\left(x,z\right).

Proposition 18

Let the operator
U_{n}
be defined by

U_{n}=A_{n}\left(x;z\right)\partial_{x}-B_{n}\left(x;z\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where

A_{n}\left(x;z\right)=\frac{\phi\left(x;z\right)}{2\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)C_{n}\left(x;z\right)},\quad B_{n}\left(x;z\right)=\frac{n-2\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)}{2\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)C_{n}\left(x;z\right)}x,

(64)

and

C_{n}\left(x;z\right)=\phi\left(x;z\right)+\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)+\gamma_{n+1}\left(z\right)-n-\frac{1}{2}.

(65)

Then,

U_{n}P_{n}=P_{n-1},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(66)

Proof.
If we use (
40
) in (
59
), then we have

\displaystyle\phi\partial_{x}P_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left(n+1\right)P_{n+2}+\lambda_{n}P_{n}+\tau_{n}\frac{\left[x^{2}-\left(\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n+1}\right)\right]P_{n}-P_{n+2}}{\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(n+1-\frac{\tau_{n}}{\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}}\right)P_{n+2}+\left[\lambda_{n}+\frac{\tau_{n}\left(x^{2}-\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}}\right]P_{n}.

From (
4
), we see that

\left[\phi\partial_{x}-\left(n+1-\frac{\tau_{n}}{\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}}\right)x\right]P_{n+1}=\left[\lambda_{n}+\tau_{n}\frac{x^{2}-\gamma_{n}}{\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n-1}}-\left(n+1\right)\gamma_{n+1}\right]P_{n}.

(67)

Using (

and the solution with initial condition
\alpha_{0,k}\left(x\right)=0
is

\alpha_{n,1}\left(x\right)=-x^{n-2}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}}\eta_{i,1}\left(x\right)=-\frac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2\left(2n-1\right)}x^{n-2}.

Setting
k=2
in (
95
) we get

\alpha_{n+1,2}\left(x\right)-x\alpha_{n,2}\left(x\right)=-\eta_{n,1}\alpha_{n-1,1}\left(x\right)-\eta_{n,2}x^{n-1},

and therefore

\alpha_{n,2}\left(x\right)=-x^{n-1}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}}\left[x^{-i}\eta_{i,1}\alpha_{i-1,1}\left(x\right)+\frac{\eta_{i,2}}{x}\right].

Using (
90
) and (
96
), we obtain (
97
).

8
Conclusions

We have defined the family of truncated Hermite polynomials
P_{n}\left(x;z\right)
, orthogonal with respect to the linear functional

L\left[p\right]={\displaystyle\int\limits_{-z}^{z}}p\left(x\right)e^{-x^{2}}dx,\quad p\in\mathbb{R}\left[x\right],\quad z>0.

Such a linear functional satisfies the Pearson equation

L\left[\phi\partial_{x}p\right]=L\left[2x\left(\phi-1\right)p\right],\quad\phi\left(x;z\right)=x^{2}-z^{2}.

We related
P_{n}\left(x;z\right)
to the Hermite and Rys polynomials, and
studied the sequence
P_{n}\left(x;z\right)
as semiclassical polynomials
of class
2
. The expansion of
\phi\partial_{x}P_{n}
in the
\left\{P_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 0}
basis (structure relation), an asymptotic
approximation (for large
n)
, a lowering operator, second order ODE (in
x),

and power series (in
z)
for
P_{n}\left(x;z\right)
are given.

We obtained a second order linear recurrence for the moments, as well as a
differential-recurrence equation in terms of the variable
z
. An asymptotic
approximation for the moments (as
z\rightarrow\infty)
is obtained.
Differential equations (in
t
and
z
, respectively) for the Stieltjes
function
S\left(t;z\right)
of the moments associated with
L
are deduced.

We also got nonlinear recurrences (Laguerre-Freud equations) and a nonlinear
ODE that the parameters
\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)
satisfy. As a
consequence, an asymptotic approximation (for large
n)
, a
differential-recurrence equation, and a power series for the coefficients

\gamma_{n}\left(z\right)
in the recurrence relation of
P_{n}\left(x;z\right)
are obtained.

We plan to continue our research on these polynomials in order to obtain
asymptotic expansions for
P_{n}\left(x;z\right)
as
n\rightarrow\infty,

z\rightarrow\infty
as well as when both
n,z\rightarrow\infty

simultaneously. One should be able to obtain the well known asymptotic
approximations for the Hermite polynomials in the last case.

Finally, we will deal with the analysis of truncated Laguerre polynomials, as
well as other families of truncated semiclassical polynomials.

1.
Introduction

The continuity of attractors with respect to perturbations is a growing subject that has called the attention of many mathematicians over the years.
In the literature, the study of the robustness of attractors is usually divided into two main features:
upper semicontinuity
and
lower semicontinuity
.

Roughly speaking, upper semicontinuity ensures that the unperturbed attractor remains stable when the perturbation is well-behaved. On the other hand, lower semicontinuity means that the unperturbed attractor does not undergo a collapse or degeneration. The first property is typically expected due to the convergence of the perturbed dynamical system under a small perturbation. However, obtaining a result on lower semicontinuity is considerably more challenging, as it necessitates specific structural assumptions about the dynamics within the unperturbed attractor. Moreover, characterizing the structure of attractors is a crucial step towards achieving a result on lower semicontinuity.

In the case of an autonomous dynamical system (nonlinear semigroup) subject to an autonomous perturbation, only a structural assumption for the limit dynamical system is necessary. This means that only the limit global attractor needs to be gradient, and all the equilibrium points of the limit semigroup are hyperbolic. For further details, refer to
[
17
]
, where the authors established an abstract result and also explored applications to partial differential equations. In other words, no additional characterization was needed for the perturbed problem.

Later, in
[
7
]
, the authors explored a scenario in which the perturbed dynamical system is non-autonomous, while the associated limit dynamical system is autonomous. In this context, the attractor is defined as the union of unstable manifolds around hyperbolic equilibrium points. Furthermore, through the introduction of a non-autonomous term into an autonomous equation, they proved how the concept of a hyperbolic equilibrium point (in the autonomous case) turns to the concept of a hyperbolic global solution for evolution processes (within the non-autonomous framework). Additionally, they established the continuity of local unstable and stable manifolds in relation to perturbations.

For non-autonomous problems under regular perturbation, the work
[
8
]
focuses on the property of exponential dichotomy for non-autonomous linear equations. It provides more general results that enable the treatment of cases where the unperturbed pullback attractor is given as the closure of the union of unstable manifolds around an infinite collection of hyperbolic time-dependent global solutions.

Regarding applications of the abstract results in the literature, we refer to
[
5
]
, where the authors obtained the continuity of pullback attractors at
\epsilon=0
for a non-autonomous strongly damped wave equation, given by the following formulation

u_{tt}-\Delta u-\gamma(t)\Delta u_{t}+\beta_{\epsilon}(t)u_{t}=f(u),

in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where the time-dependent terms
\gamma(t)
and
\beta_{\epsilon}(t)
are continuously differentiable in
\mathbb{R}
satisfying some uniform boundedness conditions, and the nonlinear term
f
has appropriate growth restrictions.

Meanwhile, in
[
25
]
, the author studied the lower semicontinuity of the family of pullback attractors for the following singularly non-autonomous plate equation

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+a_{\epsilon}(t,x)u_{t}+(-\Delta)u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{2}u+\lambda u=f(u),&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u=\Delta u=0,&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

with respect to the damping coefficient
a_{\epsilon}
, where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\lambda>0
, and
f
is a suitable nonlinearity. This type of problem is related to models of vibrations in elastic systems.

In this paper, we aim to investigate the robustness of the family of pullback attractors associated with a non-autonomous coupled system of strongly damped wave equations, as examined in
[
4
]
. This system is defined by the following evolution equations:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}+a_{\epsilon}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}=f(u),&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}-a_{\epsilon}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}=0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
u=v=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\end{cases}

with initial conditions

u(\tau,x)=u_{0}(x),\ u_{t}(\tau,x)=u_{1}(x),\ v(\tau,x)=v_{0}(x),\ v_{t}(\tau,x)=v_{1}(x),\ x\in\Omega,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},

where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, with the boundary
\partial\Omega
assumed to be regular enough,
\eta>0
is a constant,
a_{\epsilon}
is a Hölder continuous function satisfying uniform boundedness conditions, and
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a dissipative nonlinearity with a subcritical growth restriction.

The problem (
1.1
) is a non-autonomous modified version of the so called
Klein-Gordon-Zakharov
(KGZ) system.
In the autonomous case with space dimension
N=3
, it is well known that the KGZ system arises to describe the interaction between a Langmuir wave and an ion acoustic wave in a plasma, see
[
3
,
13
,
23
]
and the references therein.

In
[
4
]
, the authors used the theory of uniform sectorial operators to establish the local and global well-posedness of the problem (
1.1
) in the product space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
.
By proving that the nonlinear evolution process, associated to the global solution of (
1.1
), is a compact map, it was established the existence of pullback attractors for the problem (
1.1
).
Using energy estimates and progressive increases of regularity, the authors were able to improve the regularity of the pullback attractors. Lastly, under a suitable assumption on the functional parameter
a_{\epsilon}(t)
, they obtained the upper semicontinuity of the family of pullback attractors at
\epsilon=0
.

In this work, we study the limit system (
\epsilon=0
) associated with (
1.1
) in order to obtain a characterization for the unperturbed pullback attractor in terms of the unstable manifolds around equilibrium points, assuming that all of them are hyperbolic for the limit problem. With the aid of a Lyapunov Functional, we prove that any bounded global solution in the limit pullback attractor is forward and backward asymptotic to equilibria.
We also study the linearized system associated with (
1.1
) in order to prove a result on exponential dichotomy under perturbation, see Proposition
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
.
Moreover, thanks to abstract general results on lower semicontinuity from
[
5
,
7
,
8
]
, we obtain the lower semicontinuity of pullback attractors at
\epsilon=0
. Consequently, we achieve the continuity of the family of pullback attractors at
\epsilon=0
, concluding in this way our study of robustness for the problem (
1.1
).

It is important to mention that, in our system (
1.1
), the coupling terms

a_{\epsilon}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}\quad\mbox{and}\quad-a_{\epsilon}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}

have the same order, in the sense of fractional powers, as well as the strong damping terms
\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}
and

\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}
.
This scenario generated a situation where we were not able to apply
[
18
, Theorem 7.6.11]
directly in order to obtain our result on exponential dichotomy for the problem (
1.1
).
For more details, see Section
5
, in particular Lemma
5.2
, Proposition
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
, where we had to carry out an analysis that is not straightforward, and then we apply
[
18
, Theorem 7.6.10]
to conclude our proof on exponential dichotomy. In fact, there were several difficulties that we had to overcome, and to the best of our knowledge, the analysis presented in this work is novel up to the present moment. The reader may compare our results with those presented in
[
5
]
and
[
25
]
, for instance. It is worth mentioning that if we replace the coupling terms
a_{\epsilon}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}
and

-a_{\epsilon}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}
in system (
1.1
), respectively, by

a_{\epsilon}(t)v_{t}
and
-a_{\epsilon}(t)u_{t}
, then one may proceed exactly as in
[
5
]
in order to obtain the continuity of the family of pullback attractors at
\epsilon=0
.

Structure of the limit problem

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}+a_{0}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}=f(u),&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}-a_{0}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}=0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
u=v=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
(u(\tau),u_{t}(\tau),v(\tau),v_{t}(\tau))=(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1}),\end{cases}

which is associated to the problem (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) with parameter
\epsilon=0
. Firstly, we will prove a result on asymptotic stability for solutions of (
3.1
) starting in the pullback attractor. Thereafter, this will be used to show that the limit pullback attractor
\{\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
can be written as the union of the unstable manifolds around equilibria.

From now on, we shall assume that
\frac{N-1}{N-2}\leq\rho<\frac{N}{N-2}
. Recall from Theorem
2.5
that this condition ensures that
\overline{\bigcup\limits_{\epsilon\in[0,1]}\bigcup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}(t)}
is compact in
Y_{0}
.

3.1.
Asymptotic stability of solutions

Recall that
a_{\epsilon}
is
(\beta,C)
-Hölder continuous for each
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, with
0<\beta\leq 1
(see (
2.8
)). By Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem, for each fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
, given a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have

(3.2)

\{a_{n}(t)=a_{0}(t_{n}+t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

has a convergent subsequence (which we denote by the same notation) such that

a_{n}(t)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{\infty}(t)

uniformly for
t
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Note that the limit function
a_{\infty}
is also bounded as in (
2.7
). Thus, we may consider the following problems

(3.3)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}+a_{n}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}=f(u),&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}-a_{n}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}=0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
u=v=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
(u(\tau),u_{t}(\tau),v(\tau),v_{t}(\tau))=(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1})\in Y_{0},\end{cases}

and

(3.4)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}+a_{\infty}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}=f(u),&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}-a_{\infty}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}=0,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
u=v=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(\tau,\infty),\\
(u(\tau),u_{t}(\tau),v(\tau),v_{t}(\tau))=(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1})\in Y_{0},\end{cases}

with global solutions given, respectively, by
W^{n}(t)=(u^{n}(t),u_{t}^{n}(t),v^{n}(t),v_{t}^{n}(t))
and
W^{\infty}(t)=(u^{\infty}(t),u_{t}^{\infty}(t),v^{\infty}(t),v_{t}^{\infty}(t))
,
t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\{\mathbb{A}_{n}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}

be the pullback attractors associated with the evolution problems (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
W^{n}(\cdot)=(u^{n}(\cdot),u_{t}^{n}(\cdot),v^{n}(\cdot),v_{t}^{n}(\cdot))
and
W^{\infty}(\cdot)=(u^{\infty}(\cdot),u_{t}^{\infty}(\cdot),v^{\infty}(\cdot),v_{t}^{\infty}(\cdot))
are the global solutions associated to the problems (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively, starting in their pullback attractors, then

\|W^{n}(t)-W^{\infty}(t)\|_{Y_{0}}^{2}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0

in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

If we denote
u=u^{n}-u^{\infty}
and
v=v^{n}-v^{\infty}
, then from systems (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we are able to obtain the following system

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}+a_{n}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}^{n}-a_{\infty}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}^{\infty}=f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty}),\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}-a_{n}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}^{n}+a_{\infty}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}^{\infty}=0,\end{cases}

for all
t>\tau
and
x\in\Omega
, which can be rewritten in the following way

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}+a_{n}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}+[a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)](-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}^{\infty}=f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty}),\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+\eta(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}-a_{n}(t)(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}+[a_{\infty}(t)-a_{n}(t)](-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}^{\infty}=0,\end{cases}

for all
t>\tau
and
x\in\Omega
. Taking the inner product of the first equation in the previous system with
u_{t}
in
X
, and also the inner product of the second equation with
v_{t}
in
X
, we get

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx+\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\\
&+a_{n}(t)\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t},u_{t}\rangle_{X}+[a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)]\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}^{\infty},u_{t}\rangle_{X}\\
&=\langle f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty}),u_{t}\rangle_{X}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx+\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\\
&-a_{n}(t)\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t},v_{t}\rangle_{X}+[a_{\infty}(t)-a_{n}(t)]\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}^{\infty},v_{t}\rangle_{X}=0,\end{split}

respectively. Adding up these two last equations, it follows that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx\right)\\
&\quad+\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+[a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)]\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}^{\infty},u_{t}\rangle_{X}\\
&\quad+[a_{\infty}(t)-a_{n}(t)]\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}^{\infty},v_{t}\rangle_{X}\\
&=\int_{\Omega}[f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty})]u_{t}\,dx,\end{split}

which leads to

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|u\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{X}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\right)\\
&=-2\eta\left(\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\right)+2[a_{\infty}(t)-a_{n}(t)]\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t}^{\infty},u_{t}\rangle_{X}\\
&\quad+2[a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)]\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t}^{\infty},v_{t}\rangle_{X}+2\int_{\Omega}[f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty})]u_{t}\,dx\\
&\leq 2|a_{\infty}(t)-a_{n}(t)|\|v_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}\left(\|u_{t}^{n}\|_{X}+\|u_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X}\right)\\
&\quad+2|a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)|\|u_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}\left(\|v_{t}^{n}\|_{X}+\|v_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X}\right)+2\int_{\Omega}|(f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty}))u_{t}|\,dx.\end{split}

From the regularity theorem of the pullback attractor (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
), there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, which is independent of both the parameter
\epsilon
and
t
, such that

\|u_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}},\,\|v_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}\leq C_{1}.

Moreover, due to the theorem on existence of global solution, it follows that the norms

\|u_{t}^{n}\|_{X},\,\|u_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X},\,\|v_{t}^{n}\|_{X},\,\|v_{t}^{\infty}\|_{X}

are all bounded and this boundedness depends only on the initial data (see the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
).
Thus, it turns out that we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|u\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{X}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq C^{\prime}|a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)|+2\int_{\Omega}|(f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty}))u_{t}|\,dx,\end{split}

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
.

Now, let us deal with the integral term
\int_{\Omega}|(f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty}))u_{t}|\,dx
. In fact, using the Mean Value Theorem, there exists
\sigma\in(0,1)
such that

|f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty})|=|f^{\prime}(\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty})|\,|u^{n}-u^{\infty}|=|f^{\prime}(\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty})|\,|u|.

On the other hand, since
\frac{\rho-1}{2\rho}+\frac{1}{2\rho}+\frac{1}{2}=1
, then we apply Hölder inequality to obtain

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|(f(u^{n})-f(u^{\infty}))u_{t}|\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|f^{\prime}(\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty})|\,|u|\,|u_{t}|\,dx\\
&\leq\|f^{\prime}(\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty})\|_{L^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

The embedding
X^{1/\penalty 2}\hookrightarrow L^{2\rho}(\Omega)
holds as
\rho\in\left[\frac{N-1}{N-2},\frac{N}{N-2}\right)
. Thus, we can use the growth condition (
2.11
) to obtain a bound for the norm

\|f^{\prime}(\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty})\|_{L^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}(\Omega)}

as follows

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\|f^{\prime}(\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty})\|_{L^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}[c(1+|\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty}|^{\rho-1})]^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\rho-1}{2\rho}}\\
&\leq c_{1}\left(|\Omega|^{\frac{\rho-1}{2\rho}}+\|\sigma u^{n}+(1-\sigma)u^{\infty}\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}^{\rho-1}\right)\\
&\leq c_{2}\left(1+\|u^{n}\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}^{\rho-1}+\|u^{\infty}\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}^{\rho-1}\right)\\
&\leq c_{3}\left(1+\|u^{n}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{\rho-1}+\|u^{\infty}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{\rho-1}\right)\leq C_{0},\end{split}

where the norms
\|u^{n}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}
and
\|u^{\infty}\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}
are bounded by the theorem on existence of global solution, and this boundedness depends only on the initial data (see the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Thus, combining the estimates (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|u\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{X}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq C^{\prime}|a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)|+2C_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}|a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)|+2\hat{C}\|u\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}\|u_{t}\|_{X}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}|a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)|+\hat{C}\left(\|u\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{X}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\right),\end{split}

that is,

\frac{d}{dt}G(t)\leq C^{\prime\prime}|a_{n}(t)-a_{\infty}(t)|+C^{\prime\prime}G(t),\quad t>\tau,

where
C^{\prime\prime}=\max\{C^{\prime},\hat{C}\}>0
and

G(t)=\|u(t)\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{X}^{2}+\|u_{t}(t)\|_{X}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}(t)\|_{X}^{2}.

Since

(u^{n}(\tau),u_{t}^{n}(\tau),v^{n}(\tau),v_{t}^{n}(\tau))=(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1})=(u^{\infty}(\tau),u_{t}^{\infty}(\tau),v^{\infty}(\tau),v_{t}^{\infty}(\tau)),

u=u^{n}-u^{\infty}
and
v=v^{n}-v^{\infty}
, then
G(\tau)=0
. With this, we can integrate the inequality

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\left(G(s)e^{-C^{\prime\prime}(s-\tau)}\right)&\leq C^{\prime\prime}|a_{n}(s)-a_{\infty}(s)|e^{-C^{\prime\prime}(s-\tau)},\quad\tau\leq s\leq t,\\
\end{split}

from
\tau
to
t
in order to obtain

G(t)e^{-C^{\prime\prime}(t-\tau)}\leq-\|a_{n}-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}([\tau,t])}e^{-C^{\prime\prime}(t-\tau)}+\|a_{n}-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}([\tau,t])},

that is,

G(t)\leq-\|a_{n}-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}([\tau,t])}+\|a_{n}-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}([\tau,t])}e^{C^{\prime\prime}(t-\tau)}.

Hence, as
a_{n}(s)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{\infty}(s)
uniformly for
s
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, it follows that

\begin{split}\|W^{n}(t)-W^{\infty}(t)\|_{Y_{0}}^{2}&=\|(u(t),u_{t}(t),v(t),v_{t}(t))\|_{Y_{0}}^{2}\\
&=\|u(t)\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|u_{t}(t)\|_{X}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}(t)\|_{X}^{2}\\
&\leq G(t)\leq\|a_{n}-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}([\tau,t])}e^{C^{\prime\prime}(t-\tau)}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0,\end{split}

in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Next, we aim to prove that if
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to Y_{0}
is a bounded global solution of the problem (
3.1
) and
\xi^{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\to Y_{0}
is a bounded global solution of the problem (
3.3
),
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\xi(t_{n}+t)=\xi^{n}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. For that, we exhibit some auxiliary results.

According to
[
4
, Proposition 4.1]
, for each fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)
defined in (
2.4
)-(
2.5
) is maximal accretive (i.e.,
-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)
is dissipative and
R(I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))=Y_{0}
).
By the Lumer-Phillips Theorem (
[
22
, Theorem 4.3]
), we have
-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions in
Y_{0}
given by
\{e^{-\tau\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)}:\tau\geq 0\}
, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
. Consequently, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, it follows by
[
22
, Corollary 3.6]
that
\rho(-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))\supset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Re}(\lambda)>0\}
and

(3.8)

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}(\lambda)},

whenever
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm Re}(\lambda)>0
.

On the other hand, as proved in
[
4
, Theorem 4.7]
, for each fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)
satisfies

(3.9)

|\beta|\|(i\beta I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq M,

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, where
M=1+2\left(2\eta+\frac{2(a_{1}^{2}+1)}{\eta}+4\right)+\frac{2a_{1}+2}{\eta}>0
is independent of
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
\mathcal{C}>0
, which is independent of
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, such that

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{C}}{1+|\lambda|}
uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1],
for all
\lambda
in a sector
\Gamma
whose angle is greater than
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
. By the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 4.3]
, there exists a constant
N>0
, independent of
t
and
\epsilon
, such that
\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq N
. Thus,
\|\lambda\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\frac{1}{2}
provided
|\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{2N}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Therefore,
(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}
exists for
|\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{2N}
and

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\|(I+\lambda\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq 2N.

Consequently,

(3.10)

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda|},

whenever
|\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{2N}
.

Now, let
\varphi\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
be such that
\tan(\varphi)=4M
, where
M>0
comes from (
3.9
). Set
\Gamma_{1}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\colon|\lambda|\geq\frac{1}{2N}\;\text{and}\;|arg(\lambda)|\leq\varphi\}
.

Claim 1:

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\dfrac{\frac{2}{1-\sin(\varphi)}}{|\lambda|}
for all
\lambda\in\Gamma_{1}
.

Indeed, let
\lambda\in\Gamma_{1}
. Then

|\lambda|\leq|{\rm Re}(\lambda)|+|{\rm Im}(\lambda)|\leq|{\rm Re}(\lambda)|+\sin(\varphi)|\lambda|.

Consequently, it follows by (
3.8
) that

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\frac{1}{{\rm Re}(\lambda)}\leq\frac{\frac{2}{1-\sin(\varphi)}}{|\lambda|}.

Define
\Gamma_{2}=\bigcup_{t\neq 0}\{\mu\in\mathbb{C}\colon|\mu-it|<\frac{|t|}{2M}\}
.

Claim 2:
If
\mu\in\Gamma_{2}
, then
\|(\mu I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\dfrac{2M+1}{|\mu|}
.

In fact, let
\mu\in\Gamma_{2}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\neq 0
, be such that
|\mu-it|<\frac{|t|}{2M}
. Using (
3.9
), we deduce

\begin{split}\|(\mu-it)(itI+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}&=|\mu-it|\|(itI+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}<\frac{1}{2}\end{split}

and, thanks to the Neumann series, we obtain

\|[(\mu-it)(itI+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}+I]^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq 2.

Now, using (
3.9
) again, we get

\begin{split}\|(\mu I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}&=\|[(\mu-it)(itI+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}+I]^{-1}(itI+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\leq\frac{2M}{|t|}\leq\left(\frac{|\mu-it|}{|t|}+1\right)\frac{2M}{|\mu|}<\frac{2M+1}{|\mu|}.\end{split}

Since
\partial\{\lambda\in\Gamma_{1}:|arg(\lambda)|=\varphi\}=\left\{\left(\frac{|t|}{4M},t\right):t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset\Gamma_{2}
, it follows by (
3.10
), Claim 1 and Claim 2 that we have construct a sector
\Gamma
whose angle is greater than
\frac{\pi}{2}
such that

(3.11)

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\frac{\tilde{C}}{|\lambda|},

where
\tilde{C}=\max\{2M+1,\frac{2}{1-\sin(\varphi)}\}>0
is independent of
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
.

Lastly, for a given
\lambda\in\Gamma
, we have

(3.12)

\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq 1+|\lambda|\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq 1+\tilde{C},

which together with the uniform boundedness of
\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}
, we conclude that

(3.13)

\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq K,

for some constant
K>0
independent of
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
.

From (
3.11
) and (
3.13
),

(1+|\lambda|)\|(\lambda I+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\tilde{C}+K:=\mathcal{C},

for all
\lambda\in\Gamma
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Since the estimates (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) do not depend on
\epsilon
, then there exist
\delta>0
and
K>0
such that:

(i)

\|e^{-\tau\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq Ke^{-\delta\tau}
, for all
\tau\geq 0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)e^{-\tau\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\dfrac{Ke^{-\delta\tau}}{\tau}
, for all
\tau>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
T>0
, there are constants
C>0
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, both independent of
T
and
\epsilon
, such that

\|[\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(s)]\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq C|t-s|^{\beta}

for every
t,s,\tau\in[-T,T]
. In particular,
\|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq 1+2a_{1}
, for all
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, where the constant
a_{1}
comes from (
2.7
).

Proof.

Let
T>0
be fixed and arbitrary, and let
t,s,\tau\in[-T,T]
.
Since

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(s)\,\!\!=\!\!\,[a_{\epsilon}(t)-a_{\epsilon}(s)]\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&A^{1/\penalty 2}\\
0&0&0&0\\

\begin{split}\left\|[\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(s)]\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(\tau)\begin{bmatrix}u\\

w\\

for every

,

).
Hence,

for every

and the result is proved.
∎

Proposition 3.5

Let

Proof.

Consider the homogeneous problems

Let
\{L(t,s)\colon t\geq s\in\mathbb{R}\}
and

Proposition 3.7
.

The evolution system (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) admits a Lyapunov functional.

Proof.

Let
(u(t),u_{t}(t),v(t),v_{t}(t))
be the global solution of the problem (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) in
Y_{0}
. Multiplying the first equation in (
2.1
) by
u_{t}
, and the second by
v_{t}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx+\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\\
&+a_{\epsilon}(t)\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}v_{t},u_{t}\rangle_{X}=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u}f(s)\,dsdx\end{split}

and

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx+\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}-a_{\epsilon}(t)\langle(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 2}u_{t},v_{t}\rangle_{X}=0,

for all
t>\tau
, which combined leads to

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{X}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}-2\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u}f(s)\,dsdx\right)\\
&=-\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}-\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}\end{split}

for all
t>\tau
.

Thus, we may define an energy functional in the phase space
Y_{0}
through the map
\mathcal{E}\colon Y_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.18)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(\phi,\varphi,\psi,\Phi)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\phi\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{X}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{X}^{2}\right)\\
&-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\phi}f(s)\,dsdx,\end{split}

for
(\phi,\varphi,\psi,\Phi)\in Y_{0}
. By (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), it follows that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(u(t),u_{t}(t),v(t),v_{t}(t))=-\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}-\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}

for all
t>\tau
and, therefore, the energy functional
\mathcal{E}
is monotone decreasing along solutions.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{E}
is a constant function with respect to
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then it follows from (
3.17
) that
-\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}\|_{X}^{2}-\eta\|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}\|_{X}^{2}=0
. Consequently,
|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}u_{t}|^{2}=|(-\Delta)^{1/\penalty 4}v_{t}|^{2}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\Omega
, i.e.,
u_{t}=0\mbox{ and }v_{t}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\Omega
.
Consequently, the global solutions where the energy functional
\mathcal{E}
is constant in time
t\in\mathbb{R}
must belong to the set of equilibria
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
(set of time independent solutions). This proves that
\mathcal{E}\colon Y_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined in (
3.18
) is a Lyapunov functional for the evolution system (
2.1
)-(
2.2
).
∎

In the next result, we consider the unstable manifold around an equilibria
e_{i}^{\ast}
,
1\leq i\leq r
, given by

W^{u}(e_{i}^{\ast})=\{(t,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}\times Y_{0}:\text{ there is a global solution }\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X\text{ such that }

\xi(t)=\zeta\text{ and }\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}dist(\xi(\tau),e_{i}^{\ast})=0\}

and its section at time

Theorem 3.8
.

The limit pullback attractor
\{\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, associated to the problem (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) with
\epsilon=0
, can be written as

\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}W^{u}(e_{i}^{\ast})(t)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

At first place, we will prove that any bounded global solution in the pullback attractor
\{\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is both forwards and backwards asymptotic to some equilibria in
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
. Indeed, let
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to Y_{0}
be a bounded global solution of the problem (
3.1
) such that
\xi(t)\in\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)
is compact (see (
2.14
)) and the energy functional
\mathcal{E}
is monotone decreasing along solutions, there exist

\ell_{i},\ell_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.19)

\ell_{i}\xleftarrow{t\rightarrow-\infty}\mathcal{E}(\xi(t+s))\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow+\infty}\ell_{j}

for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a sequence such that
t_{n}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}+\infty
. We may assume without loss of generality that

a_{0}(t_{n}+s)=a_{n}(s)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{\infty}(s)

uniformly for
s
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Consider problem (
3.3
) with this function
a_{n}
. By Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.5
, if

\xi^{n}(\cdot)=(u^{n}(\cdot),u_{t}^{n}(\cdot),v^{n}(\cdot),v_{t}^{n}(\cdot))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\xi^{\infty}(\cdot)=(u^{\infty}(\cdot),u_{t}^{\infty}(\cdot),v^{\infty}(\cdot),v_{t}^{\infty}(\cdot))

are global solutions associated with the problems (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively, then we have

(3.20)

\xi(t_{n}+t)=\xi^{n}(t)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\xi^{\infty}(t)\quad\mbox{in}\quad Y_{0}

in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. By (
3.19
) and (
3.20
),

\mathcal{E}(\xi^{\infty}(t))=\ell_{j}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the global solutions where the energy functional
\mathcal{E}
is constant in
t\in\mathbb{R}
are the equilibria in
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
, there exists an equilibrium point
e_{k}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
, for some
1\leq k\leq r
, such that
\xi^{\infty}(\cdot)\equiv e_{k}^{\ast}
. Hence,

\xi(t_{n}+t)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow+\infty}e_{k}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\quad\mbox{in}\quad Y_{0}

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, we can take a sequence

\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
t_{n}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}-\infty
and prove that there exists an equilibrium point
e_{p}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
, for some
1\leq p\leq r
, such that

\xi(t_{n}+t)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}e_{p}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\quad\mbox{in}\quad Y_{0}

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
.

Now, we argue that this assertion does not depend on the particular choices of subsequences, and then we can conclude our result. Indeed, suppose that there are sequences
t_{n},s_{n}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\infty
such that

\xi(t_{n})\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}e_{i}^{\ast}\neq e_{j}^{\ast}\xleftarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\xi(s_{n}).

Reindexing these sequences, if necessary, we can assume without loss of generality that
t_{n+1}>s_{n}>t_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\delta=\|e_{i}^{\ast}-e_{j}^{\ast}\|_{Y_{0}}>0
. There is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\xi(t_{n})\in B(e_{i}^{\ast},\frac{\delta}{3})
and
\xi(s_{n})\in B(e_{j}^{\ast},\frac{\delta}{3})
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Let
\tau_{n}\in(t_{n},s_{n})
be such that
\xi(\tau_{n})\notin B(e_{j}^{\ast},\frac{\delta}{3})\cup B(e_{i}^{\ast},\frac{\delta}{3})
, for
n\geq n_{0}
. Thus,
\tau_{n}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\infty
and, taking subsequences if necessary, we also have

a_{0}(\tau_{n}+s)=a_{n}(s)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{a}(s)

uniformly for
s
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Repeating the previous argument, we obtain

\xi(\tau_{n}+t)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\xi}(t)

in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, where
\tilde{\xi}
is the global solution of the problem (
3.4
) with
\bar{a}
replaced by
\tilde{a}
, with

\tilde{\xi}(\cdot)\equiv e_{m}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}.

Since
\xi(\tau_{n})\notin B(e_{j}^{\ast},\frac{\delta}{3})\cup B(e_{i}^{\ast},\frac{\delta}{3})
, for
n\geq n_{0}
, we conclude that

e_{m}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\backslash\{e_{i}^{\ast},e_{j}^{\ast}\}.

Continuing with the previous argument, we obtain a contradiction due to the fact that there are only finitely many equilibria in
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
. Hence, we can write the pullback attractor
\{\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
as

\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}W^{u}(e_{i}^{\ast})(t)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Consider the linearization of the problem (

)-(

) around an equilibrium point

\tilde{u}_{t}\\
\tilde{v}\\
\tilde{v}_{t}\\
\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{F}(\tilde{W})=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
h(\tilde{u})\\

0\\

We may write
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(t)=\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)+B
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(t)

Proposition 4.1
.

The operator
B
, defined in (
4.3
), satisfies

\|B\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq c\left(1+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq r}\|e_{i}^{\ast}\|_{Y_{0}}^{\rho-1}\right),

where
c>0
is a constant.

Proof.

For a given
\vartheta=\begin{bmatrix}u&v&w&z\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in Y_{0}
and recalling that

e_{j}^{\ast}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{j}^{\ast}&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast},
we have

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\|B\vartheta\|_{Y_{0}}\,\!\!=\!\!\,\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-f^{\prime}(u_{j}^{\ast})u\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{Y_{0}}=\|f^{\prime}(u_{j}^{\ast})u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\|f^{\prime}(u_{j}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{c}\|f^{\prime}(u_{j}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{X^{1/\penalty 2}}\\
&\leq\tilde{c}\|f^{\prime}(u_{j}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}(\Omega)}\|\vartheta\|_{Y_{0}},\end{split}

where we have used the Hölder inequality with
\frac{\rho-1}{2\rho}+\frac{1}{2\rho}=\frac{1}{2}
, and the embedding
X^{1/\penalty 2}\hookrightarrow L^{2\rho}(\Omega)
(as condition
\frac{N-1}{N-2}\leq\rho<\frac{N}{N-2}
holds). On the other hand, we can use the growth condition (
2.11
) to obtain

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\|f^{\prime}(u_{j}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}[c(1+|u_{j}^{\ast}|^{\rho-1})]^{\frac{2\rho}{\rho-1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\rho-1}{2\rho}}\\
&\leq c_{1}\left(|\Omega|+\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}^{\ast}|^{2\rho}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\rho-1}{2\rho}}\\
&\leq c_{2}\left(|\Omega|^{\frac{\rho-1}{2\rho}}+\|u_{j}^{\ast}\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}^{\rho-1}\right)\leq c_{3}\left(1+\|u_{j}^{\ast}\|_{L^{2\rho}(\Omega)}^{\rho-1}\right)\\

\begin{split}\|e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}x\|_{Y_{1}}&=\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}x\|_{Y_{0}}\leq M_{0}(s-\tau)^{-1}e^{-\omega(s-\tau)}\|x\|_{Y_{0}}\end{split}

for every
x\in Y_{0}
, where the constants
M_{0},\omega>0
are both independent of the parameters
t
and
\epsilon
, that is,

(5.2)

\|e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0},Y_{1})}\leq M_{0}(s-\tau)^{-1}e^{-\omega(s-\tau)}.

Using Propositions
4.3
and
4.6
, condition (
2.9
) and (
5.2
) the result is complete.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

For
\epsilon\in[0,1],
let

\{\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}

be the linear evolution process in
Y_{0}
corresponding to the linearized problem (
4.1
). Then, for each
t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Proof.

According to
[
10
]
, the process
\{\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
can be written as

\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)=e^{-(t-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}\,ds,\quad t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R},

for each
\epsilon\in[0,1],
where
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)
is given as in (
4.2
).
At first place, we have

(5.3)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}&\leq\|e^{-(t-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(t-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&+\Big\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}\,ds\\
&\quad\quad-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\Big\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}.\end{split}

By Proposition
4.6
, we have

(5.4)

\|e^{-(t-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(t-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Note that the difference between the integrals in (

) can be written in the following way

(5.5)

\small\begin{split}&\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}\,ds-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\\
&=\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)](e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)})\,ds\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{\tau}^{t}(\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s))[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds.\end{split}

Now, we are going to analyze each one of the integrals that appear in (
5.5
), following the order listed below

(A)

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds
;

(B)

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}(\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s))[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds
;

(C)

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)](e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)})\,ds.

In fact, for item (A), let us denote

\hat{A}\,\!\!=\!\!\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&A^{1/\penalty 2}\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&-A^{1/\penalty 2}&0&0\end{bmatrix}

and then note that

\begin{split}&\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\\
&=\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[a_{\epsilon}(\tau)-a_{\epsilon}(s)]\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[a_{0}(\tau)-a_{0}(s)]\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\\
&=[a_{\epsilon}(\tau)-a_{0}(\tau)]\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds+\int_{\tau}^{t}[a_{0}(s)-a_{\epsilon}(s)]\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\end{split}

which leads to

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right.\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\left.-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\leq\left\|[a_{\epsilon}(\tau)-a_{0}(\tau)]\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\quad+\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}[a_{0}(s)-a_{\epsilon}(s)]\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&\leq|a_{\epsilon}(\tau)-a_{0}(\tau)|\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}|a_{0}(s)-a_{\epsilon}(s)|\|\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds\\
&\leq\|a_{\epsilon}-a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds\\
&\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|a_{0}-a_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds\\
&=2\|a_{\epsilon}-a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\hat{A}\,e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}0,\end{split}

which proves that

(5.6)

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right.\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\left.-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}0.\end{split}

Next, in order to deal with item (B), we write

\begin{split}&\|(\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s))[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\leq\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\|[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\times\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{-1}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\end{split}

and then using the estimates

\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\tilde{C}(s-\tau)^{-1},\,s-\tau>0,\,\tau\in\mathbb{R},

for some constant
\tilde{C}>0,

(5.7)

\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{-1}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq 1+\|a_{\epsilon}-a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\,\tau\in\mathbb{R},

and Proposition
5.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}(\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s))[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\|(\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s))[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\|[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}^{-1}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}

\displaystyle\times\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{-1}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}C|\tau-s|^{\beta}\left(1+\|a_{\epsilon}-a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\tilde{C}(s-\tau)^{-1}\,ds

(5.8)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\tilde{C}}\left(1+\|a_{\epsilon}-a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}|\tau-s|^{\beta}(s-\tau)^{-1}\,ds

\displaystyle=\tilde{\tilde{C}}\left(1+\|a_{\epsilon}-a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\int_{\tau}^{t}|\tau-s|^{\beta-1}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds.

Now, for the integral on item (C), we write

(5.9)

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)](e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)})\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)](e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)})\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds\\
&\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{1},Y_{0})}\|e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0},Y_{1})}\,ds\end{split}

Now, according to
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
, the process
\{\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
satisfies

\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq M,\quad t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R},

with constant
M>0
being independent of the time

and also of the parameter
\epsilon
. With this, we can combine (
5.9
), Lemma
5.2
, Proposition
5.1
, and the Dominated Convergence Theorem in order to obtain

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)](e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)})\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&\leq\tilde{M}\int_{\tau}^{t}|\tau-s|^{\beta}\|e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0},Y_{1})}\,ds\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}0,\end{split}

which yields

(5.10)

\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)](e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)})\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}0.

Finally, combining (
5.3
), (
5.5
) and (
5.8
), we can write

\small\begin{split}&\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\leq\|e^{-(t-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(t-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&+\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(s)]e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)}\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\\
&+\tilde{\tilde{C}}\left(1+\|a_{\epsilon}-a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\int_{\tau}^{t}|\tau-s|^{\beta-1}\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\,ds\\
&+\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,s)[\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(s)](e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}(\tau)}-e^{-(s-\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\tau)})\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\end{split}

and then, using (

\|\tilde{L}_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)-\tilde{L}_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{0})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

∎

Appendix

A.1.
Evolution processes and pullback attractors

Let
(Z,d)
be a metric space and
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
be the set of all natural numbers. An
evolution process
acting in
Z
is a two-parameter family
\{S(t,\tau):t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
of maps from
Z
into itself such that:

\bullet

S(t,t)=I
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
(
I
is the identity operator in
Z
),

\bullet

S(t,\tau)=S(t,s)S(s,\tau)
for all
t\geq s\geq\tau
, and

\bullet

the map
\{(t,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t\geq\tau\}\times Z\ni(t,\tau,x)\mapsto S(t,\tau)x\in Z
is continuous.

For a given set
B\subset Z
,

S(t,\tau)B=\{S(t,\tau)x\colon x\in B\}

is the image of
B
under
\{S(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
. If
Z
is a Banach space and
\{S(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathcal{L}(Z)
, we refer to this process as a
linear evolution process
.

The
Hausdorff semidistance
between two nonempty subsets
A
and
B
of
Z
is given by

{\rm d_{H}}(A,B)=\sup\limits_{a\in A}\inf\limits_{b\in B}d(a,b)

and the
symmetric Hausdorff distance
is given by

{\rm dist_{H}}(A,B)=\max\{{\rm d_{H}}(A,B),{\rm d_{H}}(B,A)\}.

Definition A.1
.

A family
\{\mathbb{A}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
of compact subsets of
Z
is a
pullback attractor
for an evolution process
\{S(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\{\mathbb{A}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is invariant, that is,
S(t,\tau)\mathbb{A}(\tau)=\mathbb{A}(t)
for all
t\geq\tau
,

(ii)

\{\mathbb{A}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
pullback attracts bounded subsets of
Z
, that is,

\lim\limits_{\tau\to-\infty}{\rm d_{H}}(S(t,\tau)B,\mathbb{A}(t))=0

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and every bounded subset
B
of
Z
, and

(iii)

\{\mathbb{A}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the minimal family of closed sets satisfying property
(ii)
.

A general result concerning the existence of pullback attractors states that, if an evolution process is pullback strongly bounded dissipative and pullback asymptotically compact, then it has a pullback attractor (see
[
9
, Theorem 2.23]
). The theory of pullback attractors for nonlinear evolution processes is covered with details in
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
.

Definition A.2
.

A
global solution
for an evolution process
\{S(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
in
Z
is a function
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to Z
such that
S(t,\tau)\xi(\tau)=\xi(t)
for all
t\geq\tau
.

Definition A.3
.

An
equilibrium point

\xi^{\ast}\in Z
for an evolution process
\{S(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
in
Z
is a time independent global solution, that is,
S(t,\tau)\xi^{\ast}=\xi^{\ast}
for all
t\geq\tau
.

Definition A.4
.

Let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a family of subsets of
Z
indexed on a metric space
\Lambda
. The family

\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is said to be:

(i)

upper semicontinuous as

\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
(or at
\lambda_{0}
) if
\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}{\rm d_{H}}(A_{\lambda},A_{\lambda_{0}})=0
;

(ii)

lower semicontinuous as

\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
(or at
\lambda_{0}
) if
\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}{\rm d_{H}}(A_{\lambda_{0}},A_{\lambda})=0
;

(iii)

continuous as

\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
(or at
\lambda_{0}
) if it is both upper and lower semicontinuous as
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
.

In the next two definitions, we consider a linear evolution process
\{L(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
acting on a Banach space
X
.

Definition A.5
.

A linear evolution process
\{L(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
in a Banach space
X
admits an
exponential dichotomy
with projection

\{P(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
, exponent
\omega>0
and constant
M\geq 1
, if:

(i)

P(t)L(t,\tau)=L(t,\tau)P(\tau)
for all
t\geq\tau
;

(ii)

the restriction of
L(t,\tau)
to
R(P(\tau))
is an isomorphism from
R(P(\tau))
onto
R(P(t))
, where
R(P(t))
denotes the range of the operator
P(t)
; and

(iii)

\|L(t,\tau)(I-P(\tau))\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Me^{-\omega(t-\tau)},\quad t\geq\tau,

\|L(t,\tau)P(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Me^{\omega(t-\tau)},\quad t\leq\tau
.

Remark A.6
.

In Definition
A.5
, item
(ii)
, we denote the inverse of
L(t,\tau)
by

L(\tau,t)
.

Definition A.7
.

A global solution
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
for a linear evolution process

\{L(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
in a Banach space
X
is said to be

hyperbolic
, if
\{L(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
has an exponential dichotomy in the sense of Definition
A.5
.

A.1.
An abstract result on lower semicontinuity of pullback attractors

Let
X
be a Banach space,
\mathcal{B}\colon D(\mathcal{B})\subset X\to X
be the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup

\{e^{\mathcal{B}t}\colon t\geq 0\}\subset\mathcal{L}(X),

y_{0}\in X

and consider the following abstract semilinear problem

(A.1)

\begin{cases}y_{t}=\mathcal{B}y+f_{0}(t,y),&t>\tau,\\
y(\tau)=y_{0},&\tau\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

and its associated regular perturbation, given by

(A.2)

\begin{cases}y_{t}=\mathcal{B}y+f_{\epsilon}(t,y),&t>\tau,\quad\epsilon\in[0,1],\\
y(\tau)=y_{0},&\tau\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

If we assume that, for each
\epsilon\in[0,1],
the nonlinearity
f_{\epsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
is continuous and Lipschitz continuous in the second variable, uniformly on bounded subsets of
X
, then the problems (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) are locally well-posed. Assuming that, for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
y_{0}\in X
, the solution
t\mapsto S_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)y_{0}
of (
A.2
) is defined in
[\tau,\infty),
we write

S_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)y_{0}=e^{\mathcal{B}(t-\tau)}y_{0}+\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\mathcal{B}(t-s)}f_{\epsilon}(s,S_{\epsilon}(s,\tau)y_{0})\,ds.

The family
\{S_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\},\epsilon\in[0,1],
consists of nonlinear evolution processes.

If
\xi_{\epsilon}^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{R}\to X,\epsilon\in[0,1],
is a global solution of (
A.2
), then we consider the linearization of (
A.2
) around
\xi_{\epsilon}^{\ast}
given by

(A.3)

\begin{cases}z_{t}=\mathcal{B}z+(f_{\epsilon})_{z}(t,\xi_{\epsilon}^{\ast}(t))z,\\
z(\tau)=z_{0},\end{cases}

and the evolution process
\{U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\},\epsilon\in[0,1],
given by

U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)z_{0}=e^{\mathcal{B}(t-\tau)}z_{0}+\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\mathcal{B}(t-s)}(f_{\epsilon})_{z}(s,\xi_{\epsilon}^{\ast}(s))U_{\epsilon}(s,\tau)z_{0}\,ds.

Definition A.1
.

The linearized problem (
A.3
) admits an

exponential dichotomy
with exponent
\omega>0
and constant
M\geq 1
, if there exists a family of projections

\{\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)

such that:

(i)

\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(t)U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)=U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(\tau)
for all
t\geq\tau
;

(ii)

U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon R(\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(\tau))\to R(\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(t))
is an isomorphism, with inverse

U_{\epsilon}(\tau,t)\colon R(\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(t))\to R(\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(\tau))\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\geq\tau;

(iii)

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)(I-\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(\tau))\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Me^{-\omega(t-\tau)},\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\geq\tau,

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Me^{\omega(t-\tau)},\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\leq\tau.

Definition A.2
.

A global solution
\xi_{\epsilon}^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{R}\to X,\epsilon\in[0,1],
of (
A.2
) is said to be
hyperbolic
if the evolution process

\{U_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}

associated to (
A.3
) has an exponential dichotomy in the sense of Definition
A.1
.

The following result concerns the lower semicontinuity of a family of pullback attractors and it was stated in
[
5
, Theorem 6.2]
.
We also refer to
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
for a more general result.

Theorem A.3
.

Let
\{S_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\},\epsilon\in[0,1],
be the family of nonlinear evolution processes associated to (
A.2
) in
X
and assume that, for
[\tau,t]\subset\mathbb{R},

\|S_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)x-S_{0}(t,\tau)x\|_{X}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

for any
x
in a compact subset of
X
. Suppose that, for each
\epsilon\in[0,1],
the process
\{S_{\epsilon}(t,\tau)\colon t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
has a pullback attractor
\{\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
in
X
such that, for
\epsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small,

\overline{\bigcup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bigcup\limits_{\epsilon\in[0,\epsilon_{0}]}\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}(t)}\mbox{ is compact in }X.

Moreover, assume that there exists a finite collection
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}=\{e_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,e_{r}^{\ast}\}
of equilibrium points, all of them hyperbolic for the limit problem (
A.1
), such that the limit pullback attractor
\{\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the union of their unstable manifolds, that is,

\mathbb{A}_{0}(t)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}W^{u}(e_{i}^{\ast})(t)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
W^{u}(e_{i}^{\ast})(t)=\{\zeta\in X:\text{ there is a global solution }\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X\text{ such that }\xi(t)=\zeta\text{ and }\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}dist(\xi(\tau),e_{i}^{\ast})=0\}
. Further, assume that for each
e_{j}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
:

\bullet

given
\delta>0
, there exists
\epsilon_{j,\delta}>0
such that, for all
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{j,\delta}
there is a global hyperbolic solution
\xi_{j,\epsilon}
of (
A.2
) that satisfies

\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\xi_{j,\epsilon}(t)-e_{j}^{\ast}\|_{X}<\delta,

\bullet

the local unstable manifold of
\xi_{j,\epsilon}
behaves continuously as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, that is,

{\rm dist_{H}}\left(W_{0,{\rm loc}}^{u}(e_{j}^{\ast}),W_{\epsilon,{\rm loc}}^{u}(\xi_{j,\epsilon})\right)\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

where
{\rm dist_{H}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the symmetric Hausdorff distance and
W_{{\rm loc}}^{u}(\cdot)=W^{u}(\cdot)\cap B_{X}(\cdot,\rho),
with
\rho>0
.

Then the family

\{\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R},\epsilon\in[0,\epsilon_{0}]\}

is lower semicontinuous at
\epsilon=0
, that is,

{\rm d_{H}}(\mathbb{A}_{0}(t),\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}(t))\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

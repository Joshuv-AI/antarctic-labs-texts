Violation of Diagonal Non-Invasiveness: A Hallmark of Non-Classical Memory Effects
Adrián A. Budini

Abstract
An operational (measurement based) scheme that connects measurement invasiveness and the presence of memory effects in open quantum systems is defined. Its underlying theoretical basis relies on a non-invasive measurability of (memoryless) quantum Markovian dynamics when the corresponding observable is diagonal in the same basis as the system density matrix. In contrast, violations of this property can be assigned to intrinsic non-classical system memory effects. Related conditions for violation of Leggett-Garg inequality due to quantum memory effects emerge from this perspective. The developed approach applies to open quantum dynamics whose time-evolution is derived from a full unitary (microscopic) description, stochastic Hamiltonian dynamics, as well as for a broad class of non-unitary system-environment models (bipartite Lindblad equations).

Measurement invasiveness

In operational approaches, memory effects can be witnessed with (a minimum

Consequently, here measurement invasiveness is defined from the same

Their joint probability is denoted as

(1)

Alternatively, one can perform only two measurements at times

and

which defines the joint probability

For quantum
systems, measurement invasiveness implies that
P_{2}(z,x)\neq P_{3}(z,x).

In order to quantify this disagreement, we use the probability distance

I\equiv\sum\nolimits_{zx}|P_{3}(z,x)-P_{2}(z,x)|.

For classical systems

III
Markovian dynamics

In the present approach a dynamics is
defined

density matrix propagator, denoted as

(3)

Similarly, when performing three measurements it follows

\frac{P_{3}(z,y,x)}{P_{1}(x)}=\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(E_{z}\Lambda_{t+\tau,t}[\rho_{y}])\ \mathrm{Tr}_{s}(E_{y}\Lambda_{t,0}[\rho_{x}]).

In both cases,
P_{1}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(E_{x}\rho_{0}),
where
\rho_{0}

is the initial system state.
\mathrm{Tr}(\bullet)
is the trace operation.

\{E_{m}\}
and
\{\rho_{m}\}

(m=x,y,z)
are the (positive) measurement
operators and system post-measurement states respectively

;

Hence, both

and

are the projectors associated to
each observable spectral representation.

Under the assumptions considered [e.g. (arbitrary) projective measurements
and underlying

-
e
propagators that fulfill a semigroup property

A
)], the Markovian property of the system

equivalent
to Markovianity in probability (see

B

P_{3}(z,y,x)=P_{2}(z|y)P_{2}(y|x)P_{1}(x),
where

P(b|a)
denotes the conditional probability of
b

a.
Departure
from this condition (at least for some set of consecutive measurement

P_{3}(z,y,x)\neq P_{2}(z|y)P_{2}(y|x)P_{1}(x),

non-Markovian. We notice that this operational (measurement based)
definition of memory effects has not associated any criteria of classicality
or quantumness. This distinction is introduced over the basis of the
following properties.

Diagonal non-invasiveness

From Eq. (
3

This property defines the DNI of Markovian dynamics

x

The DNI property of Markovian dynamics implies that, at the intermediate

unaffected by the intermediate measurement (invasiveness is always avoided

VI
Conclusions

A deep relation between measurement invasiveness and the presence of memory
effects in open quantum systems has been found. The established relation is
always valid for stochastic Hamiltonian models, unitary
s
-
e
dynamics,
and all (non-unitary) bipartite Lindblad models which do not fulfill
superclassicality
super
. The relation was established over the basis
of an operational (measurement based) scheme. It relies on performing three
consecutive system measurement processes, where the intermediate observable
must to commutates with the pre-measurement state. Using the arbitrariness
of the first and last measurement processes, we concluded that all of these
non-Markovian dynamics are intrinsically modified by a measurement process
even when the corresponding observable commutates with the system state.
This violation of DNI due to memory effects disappears when the dynamics
approaches a memoryless (operationally defined) Markovian regime.

Violations of DNI determine the non-classical character of the memory
effects, while the validity of DNI for arbitrary first and last measurement
processes define superclassicality. In stretched relation, Markovian
dynamics always obeys LGI if the pairs of measurement processes (involved in
its definition) commutate with the pre-measurement system state. Under these
conditions, violations of LGI can also be attributed to the presence of
non-classical memory effects. Nevertheless, the operational approach based
on performing three measurement processes provides a deeper discernment of
this characteristic.

The studied models support the main conclusions. The examples also allowed
us to demonstrate that memory effects can drive a transition in the validity
of LGI. Most of studied dynamics could be implemented in different
experimental photonic platforms. For example, in the schemes of Refs.
hefei
;
rio
the intermediate measurement-basis associated to violation of
DNI could be determined from standard tomographic techniques.

Underlying system-environment dynamics

The developed results rely on assuming that the underlying
s
-
e

by any measurement process performed over the bipartite arrangement.

In the case of

\Lambda_{t,t^{\prime}}^{st},

\Lambda_{t+\tau,t^{\prime}}^{st}=\Lambda_{t+\tau,t}^{st}\Lambda_{t,t^{\prime}}^{st},

Specifically, it is possible to write

where

\Lambda_{t,0}[\rho]=\overline{\Lambda_{t,0}^{st}}[\rho].

Alternatively, we consider
completely positive bipartite
s
-
e

dynamics
where the the underlying bipartite propagator fulfills

\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t^{\prime}}=\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}\mathcal{G}_{t,t^{\prime}}.

(21)

In this case, it is possible to write explicitly
\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}=\exp\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\mathcal{L}_{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}.
For

\mathcal{L}_{t^{\prime}}[\bullet]=-i[H_{T},\bullet],
where
H_{T}
is the total
s
-
e
Hamiltonian
breuerbook
. Alternatively, a

\mathcal{L}_{t^{\prime}}[\bullet]=-i[H_{se},\bullet]+\sum_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha}\bullet V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}-(1/2)\{V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}V_{\alpha},\bullet\}_{+}).
In both cases, unitary and

Appendix B
Equivalent definitions of Markovianity

The Markovian property of quantum dynamics can be defined from the system
propagator or complementarily from the outcome probabilities. Here, we show
that both definitions are equivalent when considering the
s
-
e
dynamics
considered in the manuscript [Eqs. (
20
) and (
21

Markovianity in propagator
(
\Lambda
-Markovianity) is defined by
the property

\Lambda_{t+\tau,t}[\rho_{\{t,0\}}]\overset{\Lambda}{=}\Lambda_{t+\tau,t}[\rho_{t}],

(22)

which implies that the (system) propagator
\Lambda
is independent of the
previous system history, here denoted as
\rho_{\{t,0\}}.
When the history
does not involve any measurement,
\rho_{\{t,0\}}=\Lambda_{t,0}[\rho_{0}],
the divisibility of the dynamics follows straightforwardly,
\Lambda_{t+\tau,0}=\Lambda_{t+\tau,t}\Lambda_{t,0}.
In general, the
inverse implication is not true
QRT
. On the other hand,
Markovianity in probability
(

-Markovianity) is defined by the property

P_{3}(z,y,x)\overset{p}{=}P_{2}(z|y)P_{2}(y|x)P_{1}(x),

(23)

which in turn must be valid when considering
arbitrary
(projective)
measurement processes performed at each stage.

It is straightforward to prove that [see Eq. (
4
)]

\Lambda\text{-Markovianity}\Rightarrow p\text{-Markovianity.}

(24)

Furthermore, the inverse implication

\Lambda\text{-Markovianity}\Leftarrow p\text{-Markovianity,}

(25)

is also valid. The explicit demonstration of this last result is developed
below.

From Bayes rule it is always possible to write

P_{3}(z,y,x)=P_{3}(z|y,x)P_{2}(y|x)P_{1}(x).

(26)

Consequently,
p
-Markovianity [Eq. (
23
)] implies that

P_{3}(z|y,x)\overset{p}{=}P_{2}(z|y).

(27)

Using this condition if follows that
\Lambda
-Markovianity must be
fulfilled. The explicit demonstration of this affirmation is performed in a
separate way for stochastic Hamiltonian models and CP bipartite
s
-
e

dynamics.

Using that

\Lambda_{t+\tau,0}[\rho]\overset{DNI}{=}\sum_{c}\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}[\Pi_{t}^{c}\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(\Pi_{t}^{c}\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}])]).

(57)

Here,
\rho
denotes an arbitrary initial system state, while
\{\Pi_{t}^{c}\}
follows from
\rho_{t}=\Lambda_{t,0}[\rho]=\sum_{c}\Pi_{t}^{c}p_{t}^{c}.
On the other hand, this equality must be valid for
arbitrary initial system states
\rho.

Given that the underlying
bipartite
propagator is divisible [Eq. (
21
)] it follows that

\Lambda_{t+\tau,0}[\rho]=\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}[\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}]]).

(58)

Under the replacing
\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}]\rightarrow\mathrm{I}_{s}\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}]\mathrm{I}_{s},

where the identity matrix is written as
\mathrm{I}_{s}=\sum_{c}\Pi_{t}^{c}=\sum_{c}|c_{t}\rangle\langle c_{t}|,
the previous equation leads
to

\begin{array}[]{c}\Lambda_{t+\tau,0}[\rho]=\sum_{c}\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}[\Pi_{t}^{c}\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(\Pi_{t}^{c}\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}])])\\
\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,\tilde{c}\\
c\neq\tilde{c}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}[\Pi_{t}^{c\tilde{c}}\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(\Pi_{t}^{\tilde{c}c}\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}])]),\end{array}

(59)

where
\Pi_{t}^{c\tilde{c}}=|c_{t}\rangle\langle\tilde{c}_{t}|.
By
comparing this expression with Eq. (
57
) it follows that DNI can
equivalently be expressed as

0\overset{DNI}{=}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,\tilde{c}\\
c\neq\tilde{c}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}[\Pi_{t}^{c\tilde{c}}\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(\Pi_{t}^{\tilde{c}c}\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}])]),

(60)

where
\rho
is an arbitrary system state.

C.2.1
Markovian solution

The DNI condition Eq. (
60
) is always satisfied if the
bipartite propagator satisfies

\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\mathcal{G}_{t+\tau,t}[\rho\otimes\sigma])=\Lambda_{t+\tau,t}[\rho]\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\sigma).

(61)

In fact, by definition of the projectors
\Pi_{t}^{\tilde{c}c},
the
equality

\mathrm{Tr}_{se}(\Pi_{t}^{\tilde{c}c}\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}])=0,

(62)

is always satisfied. Eq. (
61
) expresses that the system
propagator does not depends on the previous system history. Thus, it
guarantees the Markovianity of the reduced system dynamics.

C.2.2
Superclassical non-Markovian dynamics

In contrast to the stochastic Hamiltonian case, here it is possible to
define a specific class of quantum non-Markovian dynamics that fulfill DNI.
They are termed as superclassical dynamics
super
. These kind of
evolutions only emerges when considering a non-unitary (Lindblad)
s
-
e

dynamics. They are briefly described below.

Solutions with vanishing quantum discord
: Eq. (
60
)
is identically satisfied if one assume that

0=\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(\Pi_{t}^{\tilde{c}c}\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}]),\ \ \ \ \ c\neq\tilde{c}.

(63)

This condition implies that, at an arbitrary time
t,
the bipartite
propagator can be written as

\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho\otimes\sigma_{0}]=\sum_{c}|c_{t}\rangle\langle c_{t}|\otimes\sigma_{t}^{(c)}=\sum_{c}\Pi_{t}^{c}\otimes\sigma_{t}^{(c)},

(64)

where
\{\sigma_{t}^{(c)}\}
are environment states. Thus, in this case the
bipartite evolution,
independently of the system initial condition
,
does not generate any quantum discord.

Solutions with non-vanishing quantum discord:
When the condition (
63
) is not fulfilled [equivalently Eq. (
64
)
is not fulfilled] the bipartite evolution generates quantum discord. Even in
this case it is possible to satisfy the DNI condition (
60
).
When this situation occurs, the non-Markovian system dynamics induced by the
reservoir is always equivalent to the system dynamics induced in absence of
quantum discord generation. This conclusion follows straightforwardly from
Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
). This property
expresses that, when DNI is fulfilled, the
s
-
e
correlations that lead to
discord generation do not affect the system propagator.

C.2.3
Unitary system-environment models are incompatible with
superclassicality

Here we show that superclassicality does not arises when the underlying
s
-
e
dynamics is unitary, that is, when

\rho_{t}^{se}=\mathcal{G}_{t,0}[\rho_{0}\otimes\sigma_{0}]=U_{t,0}^{se}(\rho_{0}\otimes\sigma_{0})U_{t,0}^{\dagger se},

(65)

where
U_{t,t_{0}}^{se}\equiv\exp[-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}dt^{\prime}H_{T}(t^{\prime})].
Contrarily to dissipative models, unitary
s
-
e
dynamics
always
leads to quantum discord generation, neither
fulfills the DNI condition Eq. (
60
). These results are
demonstrated below.

The condition of null discord generation, Eq. (
63
), here
can be written as

0=\langle c_{t}|U_{t,0}^{se}(\rho\otimes\sigma_{0})U_{t,0}^{\dagger se}|\tilde{c}_{t}\rangle,\ \ \ \ \ c\neq\tilde{c}.

(66)

On the other hand, the basis of states
\{|c_{t}\rangle\}
and
\{|c_{0}\rangle\},
where the system state is a diagonal matrix at an
arbitrary time
t
and at
t=0
respectively, are always related by a
unitary transformation,

|c_{t}\rangle=\exp[-i\mathbb{H}_{t}]|c_{0}\rangle.

(67)

In general, the Hermitian operator
\mathbb{H}_{t}
depends on both the
total Hamiltonian and the
s
-
e
initial state. Nevertheless, it does not
depend on the states
\{|c_{0}\rangle\}.
Taking an arbitrary system
initial condition with the structure
\rho_{0}=|\bar{c}_{0}\rangle\langle\bar{c}_{0}|,
where
|\bar{c}_{0}\rangle\in\{|c_{0}\rangle\},
the null
quantum discord condition Eq. (
66
) can be rewritten as

0=\langle c_{0}|e^{+i\mathbb{H}_{t}}U_{t,0}^{se}|\bar{c}_{0}\rangle\sigma_{0}\langle\bar{c}_{0}|U_{t,0}^{\dagger se}e^{-i\mathbb{H}_{t}}|\tilde{c}_{0}\rangle.

(68)

This equation, which must be valid for arbitrary basis
\{|c_{0}\rangle\},

is
inconsistent
if the propagator
U_{t,0}^{se}
intrinsically
couples the system and the environment. In fact, the equality
\langle c_{0}|e^{-i\mathbb{H}_{t}}U_{t,0}^{se}|c_{0}^{\prime}\rangle=0
is
consistently satisfied only when the total Hamiltonian
H_{T}=H_{s}+H_{e}+H_{se}
does not introduce any
s
-
e
coupling,
H_{se}=0.

The unavoidable discord generation induced by unitary
s
-
e
interactions
also
forbids
the fulfilment of the DNI condition Eq. (
60
). In fact, Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
) imply that when DNI is fulfilled the system propagator in
absence or presence of discord generation is the same, the former propagator
being inexistent. A technical demonstration of this impossibility is
provided below.

Using that
\mathrm{I}_{s}=\sum_{s}|s\rangle\langle s|=\sum_{\tilde{s}}|\tilde{s}\rangle\langle\tilde{s}|,
where
\{|s\rangle\}
and
\{|\tilde{s}\rangle\}
are arbitrary complete basis in the system Hilbert space, the
DNI condition Eq. (
60
), with the unitary propagator (
65
), assumes the structure

0\overset{DNI}{=}\sum_{s,\tilde{s}}|s\rangle\langle\tilde{s}|\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,\tilde{c}\\
c\neq\tilde{c}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\langle s|U_{t+\tau,t}^{se}|c_{t}\rangle\sigma_{t}^{c\tilde{c}}\langle\tilde{c}_{t}|U_{t+\tau,t}^{\dagger se}|\tilde{s}\rangle),

(69)

where
\sigma_{t}^{c\tilde{c}}\equiv\langle c_{t}|U_{t,0}^{se}(\rho\otimes\sigma_{0})U_{t,0}^{\dagger se}|\tilde{c}_{t}\rangle\neq 0.

Taking into account that
\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(\sigma_{t}^{c\tilde{c}})=0,

given the arbitrariness of the basis
\{|s\rangle\}
and
\{|\tilde{s}\rangle\},
it follows that a sufficient condition for fulfilling Eq. (
69
) is

\langle\tilde{c}_{t}|U_{t+\tau,t}^{\dagger se}|\tilde{s}\rangle\langle s|U_{t+\tau,t}^{se}|c_{t}\rangle\overset{DNI}{\simeq}\mathrm{I}_{e},\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall(|s\rangle,|\tilde{s}\rangle,|c_{t}\rangle,|\tilde{c}_{t}\rangle).

(70)

Nevertheless, this proportionality can only be consistently fulfilled when
the system and the environment are not coupled by the interaction
Hamiltonian.

In general, introducing in Eq. (
69
) the explicit expression for
\sigma_{t}^{c\tilde{c}},
taking
\rho_{0}=|\bar{c}_{0}\rangle\langle\bar{c}_{0}|,
where
|\bar{c}_{0}\rangle
is an arbitrary system state,
using the arbitrariness of the basis
\{|s\rangle\}
and
\{|\tilde{s}\rangle\},
it follows the condition

0\overset{DNI}{=}\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(R_{s,\tilde{s},\bar{c}_{0}}\sigma_{0}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall(|s\rangle,|\tilde{s}\rangle,|c_{0}\rangle),

(71)

where the environment operator
R_{s,\tilde{s},\bar{c}_{0}}=R
is

R=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,\tilde{c}\\
c\neq\tilde{c}\end{subarray}}\langle\bar{c}_{0}|U_{t,0}^{\dagger se}|\tilde{c}_{t}\rangle\langle\tilde{c}_{t}|U_{t+\tau,t}^{\dagger se}|\tilde{s}\rangle\langle s|U_{t+\tau,t}^{se}|c_{t}\rangle\langle c_{t}|U_{t,0}^{se}|\bar{c}_{0}\rangle.

(72)

Given that each factor corresponds to the bipartite propagator projected on
an arbitrary system state, the DNI condition can only be fulfilled when
there is not any
s
-
e
coupling.

We conclude that unitary bipartite evolutions are incompatible with
superclassicality. Equivalently, for unitary
s
-
e
models DNI can only be
satisfied in a Markovian limit. The corresponding condition [Eq. (
61
)] only applies when a Born-Markov approximation
breuerbook
applies, even in the interval between measurement processes.

A survey on deformations, cohomologies and homotopies of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras
Yunhe Sheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we review deformation, cohomology and homotopy theories of relative Rota-Baxter ( 𝖱𝖡 \mathsf{RB} ) Lie algebras, which have attracted quite much interest recently. Using Voronov’s higher derived brackets, one can obtain an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are relative 𝖱𝖡 \mathsf{RB} Lie algebras. Then using the twisting method, one can obtain the L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra that controls deformations of a relative 𝖱𝖡 \mathsf{RB} Lie algebra. Meanwhile, the cohomologies of relative 𝖱𝖡 \mathsf{RB} Lie algebras can also be defined with the help of the twisted L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra. Using the controlling algebra approach, one can also introduce the notion of homotopy relative 𝖱𝖡 \mathsf{RB} Lie algebras with close connection to pre-Lie ∞ -algebras. Finally, we briefly review deformation, cohomology and homotopy theories of relative 𝖱𝖡 \mathsf{RB} Lie algebras of nonzero weights.



1.
Introduction

The concept of Rota-Baxter (
\mathsf{RB}
) operators on associative algebras was
introduced by G. Baxter
[
6
]
in his study of
fluctuation theory in probability. Recently it has found many
applications, including Connes-Kreimer’s
[
11
]
algebraic
approach to the renormalization in perturbative quantum field
theory.
\mathsf{RB}
operators lead to the splitting of
operads
[
3
,
48
]
, and are closely related to quasisymmetric functions and Hopf algebras
[
18
,
59
]
. Recently the relationship between
\mathsf{RB}
operators and double Poisson algebras were studied in
[
25
]
.
In the Lie algebra context, a
\mathsf{RB}
operator was introduced independently in the 1980s as the
operator form of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. For further details on

\mathsf{RB}
operators, see
[
28
,
29
]
. To better understand the classical Yang-Baxter equation and
related integrable systems, the more general notion of a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator (which was called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator in the original literature)
on a Lie algebra was introduced by Kupershmidt
[
36
]
. Relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators provide solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the semidirect product Lie algebra and give rise to pre-Lie algebras
[
2
]
.

The concept of a formal deformation of an algebraic structure began with the seminal
work of Gerstenhaber
[
22
,
23
]
for associative
algebras. Nijenhuis and Richardson extended this study to Lie algebras

[
46
,
47
]
. See
[
26
]
for more details about the deformation theories of various algebraic structures. More generally, deformation theory
for algebras over quadratic operads was developed by Balavoine
[
4
]
. For more
general operads we refer the reader to
[
34
,
41
,
44
]
, and the references therein. There is a well known slogan, often attributed to Deligne, Drinfeld and Kontsevich:
every reasonable deformation theory is controlled by a differential graded Lie algebra, determined up to quasi-isomorphism
. This slogan has been made into a rigorous theorem by Lurie and Pridham, cf.
[
42
,
49
]
, and a recent simple treatment in
[
27
]
.
It is also meaningful to deform
maps
compatible with given algebraic structures. Recently, the deformation theory of morphisms was developed in
[
7
,
20
,
21
]
and the deformation theory of diagrams of algebras was studied in
[
5
,
19
]
using the minimal model of operads and the method of derived brackets
[
35
,
43
,
57
]
.
Sometimes a differential graded Lie algebra up to quasi-isomorphism controlling a deformation theory manifests itself naturally as an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
. This often happens when one tries to deform several algebraic structures as well as a compatibility relation between them, such as diagrams of algebras mentioned above.

A classical approach for studying a mathematical structure is associating invariants to it. Prominent among these are cohomological invariants, or simply cohomology, of various types of algebras. Cohomology controls deformations and extension problems of the corresponding algebraic structures. Cohomology theories of various kinds of algebras have been developed and studied in
[
10
,
22
,
31
,
32
]
. More recently these classical constructions have been extended to strong homotopy (or infinity) versions of the algebras, cf. for example
[
30
]
.

Homotopy invariant algebraic structures play a prominent role in modern mathematical physics.
Historically, the first such structure was that of an
A_{\infty}
-algebra introduced by Stasheff
in his study of based loop spaces
[
52
]
.
Relevant later developments include the work of Lada and Stasheff
[
37
,
53
]
about

L_{\infty}
-algebras in mathematical physics
and the work of Chapoton and Livernet
[
9
]
about
pre-Lie
∞
-algebras. Strong homotopy (or infinity-) versions of a large class of algebraic
structures were studied in the context of
operads in
[
41
,
45
]
.

Due to the importance of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie and associative algebras, the studies of corresponding deformation, cohomology and homotopy theories attract much interest recently. The first step toward such a study was given in
[
54
]
, where the deformation and cohomology theories of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators were established and applications were given to study deformations and cohomologies of skew-symmetric
r
-matrices. See also the survey article
[
55
]
for more details. Then in
[
39
]
, applying Voronov’s higher derived brackets
[
57
]
, the controlling algebra of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras (a relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra consists of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
and a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
) was constructed, which turns out to be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Then using the twisting method via Maurer-Cartan elements given in
[
24
]
, one obtain a twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs simultaneous deformations of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras. Using the
l_{1}
in the twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra, one can define the cohomology of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras. Finally, one can define homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators via Maurer-Cartan characterization. Voronov’s higher derived brackets and the controlling algebras of homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras were studied more intrinsically in
[
40
]
via the functorial approach. Note that the aforementioned relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators are of weight 0, and deformation, cohomology and homotopy theories of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators and relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras of nonzero weights were further studied in
[
8
,
14
,
15
,
33
,
56
]
.

In the associative algebra context, deformations, cohomologies and homotopies of relative
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras of weight 0 were studied in
[
12
,
13
]
. Deformations and cohomologies of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators of nonzero weights were studied in
[
14
]
. Independently, deformations, cohomologies and homotopies of
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras of nonzero weights were studied in
[
58
]
. In particular, it was shown in
[
58
]
that the operad governing homotopy
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras is a minimal model of the operad of
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras. Note that due to the nonhomogeneous
relations, the operad of
\mathsf{RB}
algebras are not quadratic, and not covered by the Koszul duality theory. In
[
17
]
, Dotsenko and Khoroshkin gave a detailed study of the operad of
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras, and note that it is very difficult to give explicit formulas for differentials in the free resolutions. So it is still curious to give the homotopy theory of
\mathsf{RB}
algebras using the purely operadic approach.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the main tools which will be used frequently: the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket and higher derived brackets. In Section
3
, we survey the deformation theory of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras. Given vector spaces
\mathfrak{g}
and
V
, first using Voronov’s higher derived brackets one obtains an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, whose Maurer-Cartan elements are relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra structures on
\mathfrak{g}
and
V
. Then given a relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra, applying the twisting theory via Maurer-Cartan elements, one obtains a twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra governs deformations of the relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra.
In Section
4
, we survey the cohomology theory of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras. Using the
l_{1}
in the above twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra, one can define the cohomology of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras.
Moreover, there is a long exact sequence of cohomology groups linking the cohomology of
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pairs introduced in
[
1
]
, the cohomology of
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators introduced in
[
54
]
and the cohomology of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras. The above general framework has two important special cases:
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras and triangular Lie bialgebras. In Section
4.1
, one can apply the above general framework to introduce the cohomology of
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras. In Section
4.2
, one can apply the above general framework to introduce the cohomology of triangular Lie bialgebras.
In Section
5
, we survey homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras that obtained through the Maurer-Cartan approach. In Section
6
, we briefly survey deformation, cohomology and homotopy theories of
\mathsf{RB}
Lie and associative algebras of nonzero weights.

2.
The Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket and higher derived brackets

In this section, we recall the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket and higher derived brackets which are the main tools in later sections.

2.1.
The Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. For all
n\geq 0
, set
C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}):=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).
Consider the graded vector space

C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

Then
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the
Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket

[
46
,
47
]

(1)

\displaystyle[P,Q]_{\mathsf{NR}}=P\bar{\circ}Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\bar{\circ}P,\,\,\,\,\forall P\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

is a graded Lie algebra, where
P\bar{\circ}Q\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

(2)

\displaystyle(P\bar{\circ}Q)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(q+1,p)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P(Q(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(q+1)}),x_{\sigma(q+2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p+q+1)}).

Here
\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}
denote the set of
(i,n-i)
-shuffles. Recall that a permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-shuffle
if
\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)
. If
i=0
or
n
, we assume
\sigma={\rm{Id}}
. The notion of an
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})
-shuffle and the set
\mathbb{S}_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})}
are defined analogously.
For
\mu\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle[\mu,\mu]_{\mathsf{NR}}(x,y,z)=2(\mu\bar{\circ}\mu)(x,y,z)=2\Big(\mu(\mu(x,y),z)+\mu(\mu(y,z),x)+\mu(\mu(z,x),y)\Big).

Thus,
\mu
defines a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
[\mu,\mu]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
be a Lie algebra. Define the set of
0
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
to be
0
, and define the set of
n
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
to be

\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g}):=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=C^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\quad n\geq 1.

The
Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator

\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the adjoint representation is defined by

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}f=(-1)^{n-1}[\mu,f]_{\mathsf{NR}},\quad\forall f\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g}).

The resulting cohomology is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be two vector spaces and elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
will be denoted by
x,y,z,x_{i}
and elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
will be denoted by
u,v,w,v_{i}
. For a multilinear map
f:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we define
\hat{f}\in C^{k+l-1}\big(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\big)
by

\displaystyle\hat{f}\big((x_{1},v_{1}),\cdots,(x_{k+l},v_{k+l})\big):=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(k,l)}}(-1)^{\tau}\Big(f(x_{\tau(1)},\cdots,x_{\tau(k)},v_{\tau(k+1)},\cdots,v_{\tau(k+l)}),0\Big).

Similarly, for
f:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, we define
\hat{f}\in C^{k+l-1}\big(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\big)
by

\displaystyle\hat{f}\big((x_{1},v_{1}),\cdots,(x_{k+l},v_{k+l})\big):=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(k,l)}}(-1)^{\tau}\Big(0,f(x_{\tau(1)},\cdots,x_{\tau(k)},v_{\tau(k+1)},\cdots,v_{\tau(k+l)})\Big).

The linear map
\hat{f}
is called a
lift
of
f
.
Define
\mathfrak{g}^{k,l}:=\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.
The vector space
\wedge^{n}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is isomorphic to the direct sum of
\mathfrak{g}^{k,l},~k+l=n
.

Definition 2.1
.

A linear map
f\in\mathrm{Hom}\big(\wedge^{k+l+1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}),\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\big)
has a
bidegree

k|l
, which is denoted by
||f||=k|l
, if
f
satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

If
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{k+1,l}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and if
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{k,l+1}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{2};

(ii)

In all the other cases
f(X)=0.

We denote the set of homogeneous linear maps of bidegree
k|l
by
C^{k|l}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
.

It is clear that this gives a well-defined bigrading on the vector space
\mathrm{Hom}\big(\wedge^{k+l+1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}),\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\big)
.
We have
k+l\geq 0,~k,l\geq-1
because
k+l+1\geq 1
and
k+1,~l+1\geq 0
.

The following lemmas are very important in later studies.

Lemma 2.2
.

The Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket on
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is compatible with the bigrading. More precisely, if
||f||=k_{f}|l_{f}
,
||g||=k_{g}|l_{g}
, then
||[f,g]_{\mathsf{NR}}||=(k_{f}+k_{g})|(l_{f}+l_{g}).

Proof.

It follows from direct computation.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In later studies, the subspaces
C^{k|0}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
and
C^{-1|l}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
will be frequently used. By the above lift map, one has the following isomorphisms:

(4)

\displaystyle C^{k|0}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{2}),

(5)

\displaystyle C^{-1|l}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}).

Lemma 2.4
.

If
||f||=(-1)|k
and
||g||=(-1)|l
, then
[f,g]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0.
Consequently,
\oplus_{l=1}^{+\infty}C^{-1|l}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is an abelian subalgebra of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

2.2.
L_{\infty}
-algebras and higher derived brackets

The notion of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra was introduced by Stasheff in
[
53
]
. See
[
37
,
38
]
for more details.

Let
V=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{k}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space. We will denote by
\mathsf{S}(V)
the
symmetric algebra
of
V
. That is,

\mathsf{S}(V):=\mathsf{T}(V)/I,

where
\mathsf{T}(V)
is the tensor algebra and
I
is the
2
-sided ideal of
\mathsf{T}(V)
generated by all homogeneous elements of the form

x\otimes y-(-1)^{xy}y\otimes x.

We will write
\mathsf{S}(V)=\oplus_{i=0}^{+\infty}\mathsf{S}^{i}(V)
. Moreover, we denote the reduced symmetric algebra by
\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V):=\oplus_{i=1}^{+\infty}\mathsf{S}^{i}(V)
. Denote the product of homogeneous elements
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in V
in
\mathsf{S}^{n}(V)
by
v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n}
. The degree of
v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n}
is by definition the sum of the degrees of
v_{i}
. For a permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in V
, the
Koszul sign

\varepsilon(\sigma)=\varepsilon(\sigma;v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in\{-1,1\}
is defined by

\displaystyle v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n}=\varepsilon(\sigma;v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})v_{\sigma(1)}\odot\cdots\odot v_{\sigma(n)}.

The
desuspension operator

s^{-1}
changes the grading of
V
according to the rule
(s^{-1}V)^{i}:=V^{i+1}
. The degree
-1
map
s^{-1}:V\,\rightarrow\,s^{-1}V
is defined by sending
v\in V
to its copy
s^{-1}v\in s^{-1}V
.

Definition 2.5
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{k}
equipped with a collection
(k\geq 1)
of linear maps
l_{k}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
of degree
1
with the property that, for any homogeneous elements
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

(i)

(graded symmetry)
for every
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
,

\displaystyle l_{n}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n-1)},x_{\sigma(n)})=\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n}),

(ii)

(generalized Jacobi identity)
for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}}\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0.

Definition 2.6
.

An element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{0}
is called a
Maurer-Cartan element
of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if
\alpha
satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}(\alpha,\cdots,\alpha)=0.

Let
\alpha
be a Maurer-Cartan element. Define
l_{k}^{\alpha}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}

(k\geq 1)
by

(7)

\displaystyle l_{k}^{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{n!}l_{k+n}(\underbrace{\alpha,\cdots,\alpha}_{n},x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}).

Remark 2.7
.

To ensure the convergence of the series appearing in the definition of Maurer-Cartan elements and Maurer-Cartan twistings above, one need the
L_{\infty}
-algebra being filtered given by Dolgushev and Rogers in
[
16
]
, or weakly filtered given in
[
39
]
. Since all the
L_{\infty}
-algebras under consideration in the sequel satisfy the weakly filtered condition, so we will not mention this point anymore.

The following result is given by Getzler in
[
24
, Section 4]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

With the above notation,
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, obtained from
\mathfrak{g}
by twisting with the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha
. Moreover,
\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
if and only if
\alpha^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
.

One method for constructing explicit
L_{\infty}
-algebras is given by Voronov’s higher derived brackets
[
57
]
. Let us recall this construction.

Definition 2.9
.

A
V
-data consists of a quadruple
(L,H,P,\Delta)
where

\bullet

(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a graded Lie algebra,

\bullet

H
is an abelian graded Lie subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
,

\bullet

P:L\,\rightarrow\,L
is a projection, that is
P\circ P=P
, whose image is
H
and kernel is a graded Lie subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
,

\bullet

\Delta
is an element in
\ker(P)^{1}
such that
[\Delta,\Delta]=0
.

Theorem 2.10
.

(
[
57
,
20
]
)

Let
(L,H,P,\Delta)
be a
V
-data. Then the graded vector space
s^{-1}L\oplus H
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where nontrivial
l_{k}
are given by

\displaystyle l_{1}(s^{-1}x,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-s^{-1}[\Delta,x],P(x+[\Delta,a])),

\displaystyle l_{2}(s^{-1}x,s^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{x}s^{-1}[x,y],

\displaystyle l_{k}(s^{-1}x,a_{1},\cdots,a_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[x,a_{1}],a_{2}]\cdots,a_{k-1}],\quad k\geq 2,

(8)

\displaystyle l_{k}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k-1},a_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}]\cdots,a_{k}],\quad k\geq 2.

Here
a,a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}
are homogeneous elements of
H
and
x,y
are homogeneous elements of
L
.

Moreover, if
L^{\prime}
is a graded Lie subalgebra of
L
that satisfies
[\Delta,L^{\prime}]\subset L^{\prime}
, then
s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus H
is an
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra of the above
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}L\oplus H,\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
.

3.
Deformations of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras

In this section, first we use Voronov’s higher derived brackets to construct the
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra structures. Then using the twisting method, one obtains the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that controls simultaneous deformations of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras.

Definition 3.1
.

A
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair
, denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},\mu;\rho)
, consists of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu=[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
of
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
.

Note that
\mu+\rho\in C^{1|0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
. Moreover, the fact that
\mu
is a Lie bracket and
\rho
is a representation is equivalent to that

[\mu+\rho,\mu+\rho]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0.

We now recall the notion of a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator.

Definition 3.2
.

(i)

A linear operator
T:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called a
\mathsf{RB}
operator
if

(9)

[T(x),T(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big([T(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,T(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover, a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with a
\mathsf{RB}
operator
T
is
called a
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra
, which is denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
.

(ii)

A
relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra
is a triple
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\rho,T)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}};\rho)
is a
\mathsf{Lie}\mathsf{Rep}
pair and
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
, i.e.

(10)

[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big(\rho(Tu)(v)-\rho(Tv)(u)\big),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

Note that a
\mathsf{RB}
operator on a Lie algebra is a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator with respect to the adjoint representation.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
V
be two vector spaces. Then one has a graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
. This graded Lie algebra gives rise to a V-data, and an
L_{\infty}
-algebra naturally.

Proposition 3.3
.

One has a
V
-data
(L,H,P,\Delta)
as follows:

\bullet

the graded Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is given by
\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}}\big);

\bullet

the abelian graded Lie subalgebra
H
is given by

(11)

H:=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{-1|(n+1)}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}V,\mathfrak{g});

\bullet

P:L\,\rightarrow\,L
is the projection onto the subspace
H;

\bullet

\Delta=0
.

Consequently, one obtains an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}L\oplus H,\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
, where
l_{k}
are given by

\displaystyle l_{1}(s^{-1}Q,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(Q),

\displaystyle l_{2}(s^{-1}Q,s^{-1}Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{Q}s^{-1}[Q,Q^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}},

\displaystyle l_{k}(s^{-1}Q,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[\cdots[Q,\theta_{1}]_{\mathsf{NR}},\cdots,\theta_{k-1}]_{\mathsf{NR}},

for homogeneous elements
\theta,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1}\in H
, homogeneous elements
Q,Q^{\prime}\in L
and all the other possible combinations vanish.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
,
H
is an abelian subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Since
P
is the projection onto
H
, it is obvious that
P\circ P=P
. It is also straightforward to see that the kernel of
P
is a graded Lie subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
. Thus
(L,H,P,\Delta=0)
is a V-data.

The other conclusions follows immediately from Theorem
2.10
.
∎

By Lemma
2.2
, one obtains that

(12)

L^{\prime}=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n|0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\quad\mbox{where}\quad C^{n|0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)

is a graded Lie subalgebra of
\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}}\big)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

With the above notation,
(s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus H,\{l_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where
l_{k}
are given by

\displaystyle l_{2}(s^{-1}Q,s^{-1}Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{Q}s^{-1}[Q,Q^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}},

\displaystyle l_{k}(s^{-1}Q,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[\cdots[Q,\theta_{1}]_{\mathsf{NR}},\cdots,\theta_{k-1}]_{\mathsf{NR}},

for homogeneous elements
\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1}\in H
, homogeneous elements
Q,Q^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
, and all the other possible combinations vanish.

Now we are ready to formulate the main result in this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
V
be two vector spaces,
\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
and

. Then
((\mathfrak{g},\mu),\rho,T)
is a relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra if and only if
(s^{-1}\pi,T)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus H,\{l_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty})
given in Corollary
3.4
, where
\pi=\mu+\rho\in C^{1|0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)

Proof.

Let
(s^{-1}\pi,T)
be a Maurer-Cartan element of
(s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus H,\{l_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty})
. By Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle||[\pi,T]_{\mathsf{NR}}||=0|1,\quad||[[\pi,T]_{\mathsf{NR}},T]_{\mathsf{NR}}||=-1|2,\quad[[[\pi,T]_{\mathsf{NR}},T]_{\mathsf{NR}},T]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0.

Then, by Corollary
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle(0,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}\Big((s^{-1}\pi,T),\cdots,(s^{-1}\pi,T)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2!}l_{2}\Big((s^{-1}\pi,T),(s^{-1}\pi,T)\Big)+\frac{1}{3!}l_{3}\Big((s^{-1}\pi,T),(s^{-1}\pi,T),(s^{-1}\pi,T)\Big)

\displaystyle\Big(-s^{-1}\frac{1}{2}[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{NR}},\frac{1}{2}[[\pi,T]_{\mathsf{NR}},T]_{\mathsf{NR}}\Big).

Thus, we obtain
~[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
and

which implies that

is a Lie algebra,
(V;\rho)
is its representation and

is a relative
\mathsf{RB}

∎

Let

be a relative
\mathsf{RB}

, we obtain that

.
Now we are ready to give the

.

With the above notation, one has the twisted

\mathsf{RB}

4.
Cohomologies of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras

In this section, we survey the cohomology of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras. In particular, one can define the cohomology of
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras and the cohomology of triangular Lie bialgebras using this general framework.

One can define the cohomology of a relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra using the twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra given in Theorem
3.6
.

Let
((\mathfrak{g},\mu),\rho,T)
be a relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra. Define the set of
0
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)
to be
0
, and define
the set of
1
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)
to be
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(V)
. For
n\geq 2
, define the space of
n
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho)\oplus\mathfrak{C}^{n}(T)=C^{(n-1)|0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)\oplus C^{-1|(n-1)}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)\Big)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}V,\mathfrak{g}).

Define the
coboundary operator

\mathcal{D}:\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)
by

(13)

\mathcal{D}(f,\theta)=(-1)^{n-2}\big(-[\pi,f]_{\mathsf{NR}},[[\pi,T]_{\mathsf{NR}},\theta]_{\mathsf{NR}}+\frac{1}{n!}\underbrace{[\cdots[[}_{n}f,T]_{\mathsf{NR}},T]_{\mathsf{NR}},\cdots,T]_{\mathsf{NR}}\big),

where
\pi=\mu+\rho,~f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
and
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}V,\mathfrak{g}).

Theorem 4.1
.

With the above notation,
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T),\mathcal{D})
is a cochain complex, i.e.
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{D}=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6
,
\big(s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus H,\{l_{k}^{(s^{-1}\pi,T)}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty}\big)
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where
\pi=\mu+\rho
,
H
and
L^{\prime}
are given by (
11
) and (
12
) respectively. For any
(f,\theta)\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)
, one has
(s^{-1}f,\theta)\in(s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus H)^{n-2}
. By (
13
), one deduces that

\mathcal{D}(f,\theta)=(-1)^{n-2}l_{1}^{(s^{-1}\pi,T)}(s^{-1}f,\theta).

Thus,
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T),\mathcal{D})
is a cochain complex.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T),\mathcal{D})
is called the
cohomology of the relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra

((\mathfrak{g},\mu),\rho,T)
. We denote its
n
-th cohomology group by
\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)
.

Define a linear operator
h_{T}:\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\rho)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n+1}(T)
by

(14)

\displaystyle h_{T}f:=(-1)^{n-2}\frac{1}{n!}\underbrace{[\cdots[[}_{n}f,T]_{\mathsf{NR}},T]_{\mathsf{NR}},\cdots,T]_{\mathsf{NR}}.

More precisely,

(15)

\displaystyle(h_{T}f)(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}f_{\mathfrak{g}}(Tv_{1},\cdots,Tv_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}Tf_{V}\big(Tv_{1},\cdots,Tv_{i-1},Tv_{i+1},\cdots,Tv_{n},v_{i}\big),

where
f=(f_{\mathfrak{g}},f_{V}),
and
f_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~f_{V}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes V,V)
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in V.

By (
13
) and (
14
), the coboundary operator can be written as

where

is given by

(17)

\partial f:=(-1)^{n-1}[{\mu}+{\rho},{f}]_{\mathsf{NR}}.

and
\delta:\mathfrak{C}^{n}(T)\to\mathfrak{C}^{n+1}(T)
is given by

(18)

\displaystyle\delta\theta=(-1)^{n}[[\pi,T]_{\mathsf{NR}},\theta]_{\mathsf{NR}}.

The formula of the coboundary operator
\mathcal{D}
can be well-explained by the following diagram:

\scriptstyle{h_{T}}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 16.09991pt\partial}

Since

.

Let

\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra. Define the set of
0
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{0}_{{\rm RB}}(\mathfrak{g},T)
to be
0
, and define
the set of

-cochains

to be
\mathfrak{C}^{1}_{{\rm RB}}(\mathfrak{g},T):=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

n
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{{\rm RB}}(\mathfrak{g},T)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\rm RB}(\mathfrak{g},T):=\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{C}^{n}(T)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Im}^{n}(\mathfrak{i}),\mathcal{D})
is a subcomplex of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad},T),\mathcal{D})

\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\mathrm{ad},T)
.

\mathfrak{p}:\mathrm{Im}^{n}(\mathfrak{i})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\rm RB}(\mathfrak{g},T)

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\rm RB}(f,\theta)=\Big({\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}}f,\delta\theta+\Omega f\Big),\quad\forall f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle(\Omega f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=(-1)^{n}\Big(f(Tx_{1},\cdots,Tx_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}Tf(Tx_{1},\cdots,Tx_{i-1},x_{i},Tx_{i+1},\cdots,Tx_{n})\Big).

4.2.
Cohomology of triangular Lie bialgebras

In this subsection, all vector spaces are assumed to be finite-dimensional. We survey the cohomology of triangular Lie bialgebras, which is defined with the help of the general cohomological framework for relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras.

Recall that a Lie bialgebra is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped
with a Lie algebra structure

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and a Lie coalgebra
structure
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
such that
\delta

is a 1-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
.
The Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
in a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

naturally extends to the
Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{SN}}
on
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}
. More precisely, one has

~[x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{p},y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{q}]_{\mathsf{SN}}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq p\atop 1\leq j\leq q}(-1)^{i+j}[x_{i},y_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\wedge x_{1}\wedge\cdots\hat{x}_{i}\cdots\wedge x_{p}\wedge y_{1}\wedge\cdots\hat{y}_{j}\cdots\wedge y_{q}.

An element
r\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is called a
skew-symmetric
r
-matrix

[
50
]
if it satisfies the
classical Yang-Baxter equation

[r,r]_{\mathsf{SN}}=0
.
It is well known
[
36
]
that
r
satisfies the classical Yang-Baxter
equation if and only if
r^{\sharp}
is a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator on
\mathfrak{g}

with respect to the coadjoint representation,
where
r^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is defined by
\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi),\eta\rangle=\langle r,\xi\wedge\eta\rangle
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Let
r
be a skew-symmetric
r
-matrix. Define

\delta_{r}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
by

\delta_{r}(x)=[x,r]_{\mathsf{SN}},
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\delta_{r})
is a Lie bialgebra, which is
called a
triangular Lie bialgebra
. From now on, denote a triangular Lie bialgebra by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},r)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
r\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
a skew-symmetric
r
-matrix. Define the set of 0-cochains and 1-cochains to be zero and define the set of
k
-cochains to be
\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}
. Define
\mathrm{d}_{r}:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}
by

(20)

\mathrm{d}_{r}\chi=[r,\chi]_{\mathsf{SN}},\quad\forall\chi\in\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\mathrm{d}_{r}^{2}=0.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}^{k}(r)
the corresponding
k
-th cohomology group,
called the
k
-th cohomology group of the skew-symmetric

r
-matrix
r
.

For any
k\geq 1
, define
\Psi:\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g})
by

(21)

\langle\Psi(\chi)(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{k}),\xi_{k+1}\rangle=\langle\chi,\xi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_{k}\wedge\xi_{k+1}\rangle,\quad\forall\chi\in\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

By
[
54
, Theorem 7.7]
, we have

(22)

\Psi(\mathrm{d}_{r}\chi)=\delta(\Psi(\chi)),\quad\forall\chi\in\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}.

Thus
(\mathrm{Im}(\Psi),\delta)
is a subcomplex of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{k}\mathfrak{C}^{k}(r^{\sharp}),\delta)
associated to the relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
r^{\sharp}
, where
\mathrm{Im}(\Psi):=\oplus_{k}\{\Psi(\chi)|\forall\chi\in\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\}
and
\delta
is the coboundary operator given by (
18
) for the relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
r^{\sharp}
.

In the following, we survey the cohomology of a triangular Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},r)
. Define the set of
0
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)
to be
0
, and define
the set of
1
-cochains to be
\mathfrak{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r):=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. For
n\geq 2
, define the space of
n
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r):=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}.

Define the embedding
\mathfrak{i}:\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad}^{*},r^{\sharp})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g})
by

\mathfrak{i}(f,\chi)=(f,f^{\star},\Psi(\chi)),\quad\forall f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\chi\in\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},

where
f^{\star}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is defined by

(23)

\displaystyle\langle f^{\star}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},\xi),x_{n}\rangle=-\langle\xi,f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n})\rangle.

Denote by
\mathrm{Im}^{n}(\mathfrak{i})
the image of
\mathfrak{i}
, i.e.
\mathrm{Im}^{n}(\mathfrak{i}):=\{\mathfrak{i}(f,\chi)|\forall(f,\chi)\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)\}
.
It was proved in
[
39
]
that
(\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Im}^{n}(\mathfrak{i}),\mathcal{D})
is a subcomplex of the cochain complex
(\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad}^{*},r^{\sharp}),\mathcal{D})
associated to the relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\mathrm{ad}^{*},r^{\sharp})
.

Define the projection
\mathfrak{p}:\mathrm{Im}^{n}(\mathfrak{i})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)
by

\mathfrak{p}(f,f^{\star},\theta)=(f,\theta^{\flat}),\quad\forall f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\theta\in\{\Psi(\chi)|\forall\chi\in\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\},

where
\theta^{\flat}\in\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}
is defined by
\langle\theta^{\flat},\xi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_{n}\rangle=\langle\theta(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1}),\xi_{n}\rangle.

Define the
coboundary operator

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}:\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)
for a triangular Lie bialgebra by

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}=\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathcal{D}\circ\mathfrak{i}.

Theorem 4.8
.

The map
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}
is a coboundary operator, i.e.
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}=0
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{i}\circ\mathfrak{p}={\rm{Id}}
when restricting on the image of
\mathfrak{i}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}=\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathcal{D}\circ\mathfrak{i}\circ\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathcal{D}\circ\mathfrak{i}=\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{D}\circ\mathfrak{i}=0,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},r)
be a triangular Lie bialgebra. The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r),\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}})
is called the
cohomology of the triangular Lie bialgebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},r)
. Denote the
n
-th cohomology group by
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)
.

Now we give the precise formula for the coboundary operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}
. By the definition of
\mathfrak{i}
,
\mathfrak{p}
,
\mathcal{D}
and (
22
), one has

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(f,\chi)=\Big({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathsf{CE}}f,\Theta f+\mathrm{d}_{r}\chi\Big),\qquad\forall f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\chi\in\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},

where
\mathrm{d}_{r}
is given by (
20
) and
\Theta:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\Theta f=\Psi^{-1}(h_{r^{\sharp}}(f,f^{\star})).

More precisely,

(25)

\displaystyle\langle\Theta f,\xi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_{n+1}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\langle\xi_{i},f(r^{\sharp}(\xi_{1}),\cdots,r^{\sharp}(\xi_{i-1}),r^{\sharp}(\xi_{i+1}),\cdots,r^{\sharp}(\xi_{n+1}))\rangle,

for all
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Remark 4.10
.

One can use the cohomology theory developed here to study infinitesimal deformations. More precisely, the cohomology groups
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T),~\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\rm RB}(\mathfrak{g},T),~\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{TLB}}(\mathfrak{g},r)
classify infinitesimal deformations of the relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rho,T)
, the
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},T)
and the triangular Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},r)
respectively.

5.
Homotopies of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras

In this section, we survey the notion of a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra, which consists of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, its representation and a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator. Homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators can be characterized as Maurer-Cartan elements in a certain
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Denote by
\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),V)
the space of degree
n
linear maps from the graded vector space
\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V)=\oplus_{i=1}^{+\infty}\mathsf{S}^{i}(V)
to the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V
. Obviously, an element
f\in\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),V)
is the sum of
f_{i}:\mathsf{S}^{i}(V)\,\rightarrow\,V
. We will write
f=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}f_{i}
.
Set
C^{n}(V,V):=\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),V)
and

C^{*}(V,V):=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{n}(V,V).

As the graded version of the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket given in
[
46
,
47
]
, the
graded Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}}
on the graded vector space
C^{*}(V,V)
is given
by

(26)

\displaystyle[f,g]_{\mathsf{NR}}:=f\bar{\circ}g-(-1)^{mn}g\bar{\circ}f,\,\,\,\,\forall f=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}f_{i}\in C^{m}(V,V),~g=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}g_{j}\in C^{n}(V,V),

where
f\bar{\circ}g\in C^{m+n}(V,V)
is defined by

(27)

\displaystyle f\bar{\circ}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}f_{i}\Big)\bar{\circ}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}g_{j}\Big):=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\Big(\sum_{i+j=k+1}f_{i}\bar{\circ}g_{j}\Big),

while
f_{i}\bar{\circ}g_{j}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathsf{S}^{i+j-1}(V),V)
is defined by

(28)

\displaystyle(f_{i}\bar{\circ}g_{j})(v_{1},\cdots,v_{i+j-1}):=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(j,i-1)}}\varepsilon(\sigma)f_{i}(g_{j}(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(j)}),v_{\sigma(j+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(i+j-1)}).

The following result is well-known and, in fact, can be taken as a definition of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Theorem 5.1
.

With the above notation,
(C^{*}(V,V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
is a graded Lie algebra. Its Maurer-Cartan elements
\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}l_{k}
are the
L_{\infty}
-algebra structures on
V
.

Definition 5.2
.

(
[
38
]
)
A
representation
of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
on a graded vector space
V
consists of linear maps
\rho_{k}:\mathsf{S}^{k-1}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes V\,\rightarrow\,V
,
k\geq 1
, of degree
1
with the property that, for any homogeneous elements
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i-1)}}\varepsilon(\sigma)\rho_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n-1)},v)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n-i,i-1)}}\varepsilon(\sigma)(-1)^{x_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+x_{\sigma(n-i)}}\rho_{n-i+1}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n-i)},\rho_{i}(x_{\sigma(n-i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n-1)},v))=0.

Let
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
be a representation of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
. There is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
given by

\displaystyle l_{k}\big((x_{1},v_{1}),\cdots,(x_{k},v_{k})\big):=\big(l_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}),\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{x_{i}(x_{i+1}+\cdots+x_{k})}\rho_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{k},v_{i})\big).

This
L_{\infty}
-algebra is called the

semidirect product
of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
and
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
, and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V
.

Definition 5.3
.

(i)

Let
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
be a representation of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
. A degree
0
element
T=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}T_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),\mathfrak{g})
with
T_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathsf{S}^{k}(V),\mathfrak{g})
is called a
homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
on an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
with respect to the representation
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
if the following equalities hold for all
p\geq 1
and all homogeneous elements
v_{1},\cdots,v_{p}\in V
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=t\atop 1\leq t\leq p-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{m},1,p-1-t)}}\frac{\varepsilon(\sigma)}{m!}\cdot

\displaystyle T_{p-t}\Big(\rho_{m+1}\Big(T_{k_{1}}\big(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k_{1})}\big),\cdots,T_{k_{m}}\big(v_{\sigma(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m-1}+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(t)}\big),v_{\sigma(t+1)}\Big),v_{\sigma(t+2)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(p)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}=p}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{n})}}\frac{\varepsilon(\sigma)}{n!}l_{n}\Big(T_{k_{1}}\big(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k_{1})}\big),\cdots,T_{k_{n}}\big(v_{\sigma(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n-1}+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(p)}\big)\Big).

(ii)

A
homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra
is a triple
\big((\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty}),\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty},\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty}\big)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra,
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on a graded vector space
V
and
T=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}T_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),\mathfrak{g})
is a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator.

A homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator on an
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a generalization of an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator on a Lie
2
-algebra introduced in
[
51
]
.

A representation of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra will give rise to a V-data as well as an
L_{\infty}
-algebra that characterize homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators as
\mathsf{MC}
elements.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
. Then the following quadruple forms a V-data:

\bullet

the graded Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is given by
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
;

\bullet

the abelian graded Lie subalgebra
H
is given by
H:=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),\mathfrak{g});

\bullet

P:L\,\rightarrow\,L
is the projection onto the subspace
H
;

\bullet

\Delta=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(l_{k}+\rho_{k})
.

Consequently,
(H,\{\mathfrak{l}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where
\mathfrak{l}_{k}
is given by
(
8
).

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
,
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
is a graded Lie algebra. Moreover, by (
28
),
\mathrm{Im}P=H
is an abelian graded Lie subalgebra and
\ker P
is a graded Lie subalgebra. Since
\Delta=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(l_{k}+\rho_{k})
is the semidirect product
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
, one has
[\Delta,\Delta]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
and
P(\Delta)=0
. Thus
(L,H,P,\Delta)
is a V-data. Hence by Theorem
2.10
, one obtains the higher derived brackets
\{{\mathfrak{l}_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty}
on the abelian graded Lie subalgebra
H
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

With the above notation, a degree
0
element
T=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}T_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),\mathfrak{g})
is a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator on
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
with respect to the representation
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
if and only if
T=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}T_{k}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(H,\{{\mathfrak{l}_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
39
, Theorem 5.10]
.
∎

A homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator naturally gives rise to an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
V
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
T=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}T_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\bar{\mathsf{S}}(V),\mathfrak{g})
be a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator on
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
with respect to the representation
(V,\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
.

(i)

e^{[\cdot,T]_{\mathsf{NR}}}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(l_{k}+\rho_{k})\Big)

;

(ii)

there is an

given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}_{t+1}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{t+1})=\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=t}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{m},1)}}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon(\sigma)}{m!}\rho_{m+1}\Big(T_{k_{1}}\big(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k_{1})}\big),\cdots,T_{k_{m}}\big(v_{\sigma(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m-1}+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(t)}\big),v_{\sigma(t+1)}\Big);

(iii)

T
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra homomorphism from the

-algebra
(V,\{\mathfrak{l}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
to
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
.

Proof.

See the proof of
[

, Proposition 5.11]
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

In the classical case, a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator induces a pre-Lie algebra
[
2
]
. Now a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator also induces a pre-Lie
∞
-algebra, which was introduced in
[
9
]
. See
[
39
, Section 5.2]
for details.

Remark 5.8
.

Dotsenko and Khoroshkin studied the homotopy of
\mathsf{RB}
operators on associative algebras in
[
17
]
using the operadic approach, and noted that “in general compact formulas
are yet to be found”. For
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras, one encounters a similarly challenging situation. Nevertheless, we use the controlling algebra and Maurer-Cartan approach to give the concrete formulas of homotopy
\mathsf{RB}

6.
Deformations, cohomologies and homotopies of
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras of nonzero weights

Note that there is a more general notion of relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
, and the relative
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras studied in previous sections are of weight 0. In this section, we briefly review recent developments of deformations, cohomologies and homotopies of
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a Lie algebra
homomorphism, which is called an
action
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie
algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. A linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator of weight
\lambda
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect
to
(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
if

(29)

[T(u),T(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\Big(\phi(T(u))v-\phi(T(v))u+\lambda[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

In particular, if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}
and the action is the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, then
T
is called a
\mathsf{RB}
operator of weight
\lambda
. A
\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
is a Lie algebra equipped with a
\mathsf{RB}
operator of weight
\lambda
.

In
[
56
]
, the notion of a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator of weight
\lambda
on a symmetric Lie algebra was introduced using the controlling algebra approach, which was the first step toward the definition of a homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator of weight
\lambda
on an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. As a byproduct, the controlling algebra of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators of weight
\lambda
was given in
[
56
, Corollary 2.17]
. More precisely,
let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
. Then
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d})
is a differential graded Lie algebra, where the differential
\mathrm{d}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}g)(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{n+i+j-1}g(\lambda[v_{i},v_{j}]_{\mathfrak{h}},v_{1},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,\hat{v}_{j},\cdots,v_{n+1}),

for all
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{h}
, and the graded Lie bracket

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\times\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m+n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})

is given by

\displaystyle\left\llbracket g_{1},g_{2}\right\rrbracket(v_{1},\cdots,v_{m+n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,1,n-1)}}(-1)^{1+\sigma}g_{1}\Big(\phi\big(g_{2}(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m)})\big)v_{\sigma(m+1)},v_{\sigma(m+2)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m+n)}\Big)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,1,m-1)}}(-1)^{mn+\sigma}g_{2}\Big(\phi\big(g_{1}(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(n)})\big)v_{\sigma(n+1)},v_{\sigma(n+2)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m+n)}\Big)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}(-1)^{1+mn+\sigma}[g_{1}(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(n)}),g_{2}(v_{\sigma(n+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{\mathfrak{g}},

for all
g_{1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),~g_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{m+n}\in\mathfrak{h}.

Moreover,
a linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator of weight
\lambda
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the action
\phi
if and only if
T
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the above differential graded Lie algebra.

As soon as one has the above controlling algebra of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators of weight
\lambda
, one can obtain immediately the differential graded Lie algebra that controls deformations of a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
T
of weight
\lambda
using the twisted differential
\mathrm{d}_{T}:=\mathrm{d}+\left\llbracket T,\cdot\right\rrbracket
. Meanwhile, one can also define the cohomology of a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators
T
of weight
\lambda
using the twisted differential
\mathrm{d}_{T}.
See
[
14
]
for details. Note that in
[
14
]
, the controlling algebra of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators of weight
\lambda
on associative algebras were constructed parallelly.

Before
[
14
]
, the cohomologies of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators of weight
1
on Lie algebras were given in
[
33
]
using a different approach. Namely a relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
of weight
1
induces a new Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
and a representation
\theta:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
of
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{T}
and
\theta
are given by

(30)

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(T(u))v-\phi(T(v))u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(31)

\displaystyle\theta(u)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\phi(x)u)+[T(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

The Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
\theta
is taken to be the cohomology of the relative
\mathsf{RB}
operator
T
. In the same paper, the cohomologies of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators of weight
1
on Lie groups were also introduced and the classical Van Est map was extended to the context of cohomologies of relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators on Lie groups and Lie algebras.

Using Voronov’s higher derived brackets
[
57
]
, Caseiro and Nunes da Costa succeed in defining homotopy relative
\mathsf{RB}
operators of weight 1 on
L_{\infty}
-algebras with respect to
L_{\infty}
-actions
[
8
]
, generalized some results in
[
39
]
and
[
56
]
. In the associative algebra context,
Wang and Zhou studied homotopy
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras of weight
\lambda
in
[
58
]
and showed that the operad governing
homotopy
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras is a minimal model of the operad of
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras. The cohomologies of
\mathsf{RB}
associative algebras of weight
\lambda

\mathsf{RB}
Lie algebras of weight

[

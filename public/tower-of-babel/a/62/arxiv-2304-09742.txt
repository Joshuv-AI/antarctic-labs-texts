Diophantine stability for elliptic curves on average
Anwesh Ray, Tom Weston

Abstract
. Let K K be a number field and ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 be a prime number. Mazur and Rubin introduced the notion of diophantine stability for a variety X / K X_{/K} at a prime ℓ \ell . We show that there is a positive density set of elliptic curves E / ℚ E_{/\mathbb{Q}} of rank 1 1 , such that E / K E_{/K} is diophantine stable at ℓ \ell . This result has implications to Hilbert’s tenth problem for number rings.



1.
Introduction

Let
V
be a variety over a number field
K
and let
\ell
be a prime number. Mazur and Rubin introduced the notion of diophantine stability. Given a number field extension
L/K
, the variety
V_{/K}
is
diophantine stable
in
L
if
V(L)=V(K)
. For a prime
\ell
it is said that
V
is
\ell
-diophantine stable
over
K
if for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and finite set of primes
\Sigma
of
K
, there are infinitely many cyclic extensions
L/K
of degree
\ell^{n}
, such that

(1)

all primes
v\in\Sigma
are completely split in
L
,

(2)

V(L)=V(K)
.

Theorem 1.1

(Mazur-Rubin
[
MRL18
]
, Theorem 1.2)
.

Let
A_{/K}
be a simple abelian variety for which all geometric endomorphisms are defined over
K
. Then, there is a set of prime numbers
S
of positive density such that
A
is
\ell
-diophantine stable for all
\ell\in S
.

When specialized to elliptic curves, this result has significant consequences to Hilbert’s tenth problem for number rings.

Corollary 1.2

(Mazur-Rubin
[
MRL18
]
, Corollary 1.6)
.

For every prime
\ell
, there are uncountably many pairwise non-isomorphic
totally real fields
L
of
algebraic numbers in
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
over which the following two statements both hold:

(1)

There is a diophantine definition of
\mathbb{Z}
in the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{L}
of
L
.
In particular, Hilbert’s Tenth Problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{L}
;
i.e., there does not exist an algorithm to determine whether a polynomial
(in many variables) with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
has a solution in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

(2)

There exists a first-order definition of the ring
\mathbb{Z}
in
L
.
The first-order theory for such fields
L
is undecidable.

1.1.
Main results

We study statistical questions from a different perspective. Any elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
is isomorphic to a unique curve of the form

E=E_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that for all primes
p
, either
p^{4}\nmid A
or
p^{6}\nmid B
, cf.
[
Duk97
, p. 814, l.-5]
. Such a Weierstrass equation is minimal and the (naive) height of
E
is defined as follows

H(E)=H(E_{A,B})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}.

Here, we follow the convention in
loc. cit.
, it also customary define the height to be
\operatorname{max}\{4|A|^{3},27B^{2}\}
, however, this is only a matter of convention.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{C}(X)
those with height
\leq X^{6}
. As is well known,

\#\mathcal{C}(X)=C_{1}X^{5}+O(X^{3}),

where
C_{1}=\frac{4}{\zeta(10)}
, cf.
[
Bru92
, Lemma 4.3]
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subset of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Set
\mathcal{S}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{C}(X)=\{E\in\mathcal{S}\mid H(E)\leq X^{6}\}
. The density of
\mathcal{S}
is given by

\delta(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)},

provided the limit exists.

In any case, we can define the upper and lower densities by

\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}

and

\underline{\delta}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}

respectively. We prove the following result.

Theorem A

(Theorem
2.3
)
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number and let
K
be a number field. Then, the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
that are
\ell
-diophantine stable over
K
has density
1
.

We recall a result of Bhargava and Skinner, which will be shown to have consequences for Hilbert’s tenth problem over number rings.

Theorem 1.3

(Bhargava-Skinner
[
BS14
]
)
.

The upper density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with rank
1
is positive.

Theorem B

(Theorem
2.5
)
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number and let
K
be a number field. Then, the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which

(1)

E_{/K}
is
\ell
diophantine stable,

(2)

\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})=1

has positive upper density.

Theorem C
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number, let
K
be a number field,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\Sigma
be a finite set of primes of
K
. Assume that
\mathbb{Z}
is a diophantine subset of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and consequently, Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Then, there are infinitely many degree
\ell^{n}
cyclic extensions
L/K
in which the primes of
\Sigma
are completely split, such that
\mathbb{Z}
is diophantine in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

The key point is that the above result holds for all primes
\ell\geq 5
. It follows from results of Shlapentokh and Mazur-Rubin that the above assertion holds for a density
1
set of primes
\ell
.

1.2.
Organization

Including the introduction, the article consists of four sections. In section
2
, we recall a criterion of Mazur and Rubin for diophantine stability, cf. Theorem
2.2
. For elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, this criterion can be verified if the field cut out by the residual representation satisfies some additional conditions, cf. Proposition
2.8
. Density results are proven in section
3
. It is shown that the conditions of Theorem
2.2
are satisfied for a set of elliptic curves of density

. Theorems

, which is seen to follow from Theorem

2.
Diophantine stability

2.1.
Diophantine stability for elliptic curves

We review the notion of diophantine stability for an elliptic curve. Throughout, we shall fix a number field
K
and a prime number
\ell\geq 5
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we say that an extension
L
of
K
is a

\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
-extension if it is a Galois extension of
K
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. We recall the notion of diophantine stability, due to Mazur and Rubin
[
MRL18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
L/K
be a field extension and let
E_{/K}
be an elliptic curve. Then,
E_{/K}
is said to be diophantine stable in
L
if
E(L)=E(K)
. It is said that
E_{/K}
is diophantine stable at
\ell
if for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and every finite set of primes
\Sigma
of
K
, there are infinitely many
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
-extensions
L/K
such that

(1)

E(L)=E(K)
,

(2)

all primes in
\Sigma
are completely split in
L
.

Given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, we say that
(E,K,\ell)
satisfies (DS) if
E_{/K}
is diophantine stable at
\ell
.

Given a number field
K
, set
\operatorname{G}_{K}
to denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
. Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve and denote by
E[\ell]
, the
\ell
-torsion group

E[\ell]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})\right).

Set
\bar{\rho}_{E}=\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
to denote the Galois representation on
E[\ell]
. Set
\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell])
to be the field cut out by
E[\ell]
, i.e.,
\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell])\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{\operatorname{ker}\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}}
.

We recall a criterion for diophantine stability, specialized to rational elliptic curves without complex multiplication.

Theorem 2.2

(Mazur-Rubin)
.

Let
K
be a number field,
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
an elliptic curve without complex multiplication, and
\ell\geq 3
a prime number. Assume that the following conditions hold

(1)

E[\ell]
is an irreducible
\operatorname{G}_{K}
-module,

(2)

H^{1}(K(E[\ell])/K,E[\ell])=0
,

(3)

there is no abelian extension of degree
\ell
of
K(\mu_{\ell})
contained in
K(E[\ell])
,

(4)

there is
\tau_{0}\in\operatorname{G}_{K(\mu_{\ell})}
such that
E[\ell]/(\tau_{0}-1)E[\ell]=0
,

(5)

there is
\tau_{1}\in\operatorname{G}_{K(\mu_{\ell})}
such that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\left(E[\ell]/(\tau_{1}-1)E[\ell]\right)=1
.

Then,
E_{/K}
is diophantine stable at
\ell
.

Proof.

The result follows from
[
MRL18
, Theorem 9.21]
.
∎

In the next section, we prove the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number and let
K
be a number field. Then, the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which the conditions of Theorem
2.2
are satisfied has density
1
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{K,\ell}
be the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that

(1)

\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})=1
,

(2)

(E,K,\ell)
satisfies (DS).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number and
K
be any number field. Then,
\mathcal{S}_{K,\ell}
has positive upper density.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
1.3
.
∎

2.2.
A criterion for diophantine stability in terms of the residual image

In the remainder of this section, we shall introduce some further notation and establish a criterion for the conditions of Theorem
2.2
to be satisfied. We fix
(K,\ell)
where
K
is a number field and
\ell\geq 5
is a prime number. We let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve and recall that

\bar{\rho}_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

denotes the representation on
E[\ell]
. Set
\mathbf{1}
to denote the identity element of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
and
\langle-\mathbf{1}\rangle
the subgroup of order
2
generated by
-\mathbf{1}
. We set
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}{\langle-\mathbf{1}\rangle}
and denote by

\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

the homomorphism which is the composite of
\bar{\rho}_{E}
with the natural quotient map. The determinant character
\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{E}
is the mod-
\ell
cyclotomic character
\chi_{\ell}
, and thus the kernel of
\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{E}
is
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})}
. Note that
\langle-\mathbf{1}\rangle
lies in the kernel of the determinant map, and hence,
\operatorname{det}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
factors through
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Note that the restriction of
\bar{\rho}_{E}
and
\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}
to
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})}
gives rise to homomorphisms

\bar{\rho}_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})}\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
and
\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})}\rightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
respectively. Galois
[
Gal46
, p. 412]
, in a letter to Chevalier showed that when
\ell\geq 5
, the group
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is simple. This property shall prove to be considerably useful in establishing our results.

Given a number field
F
, a finite group
\mathcal{G}
, and a homomorphism
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
, we let
F(\varrho)
denote the field
\bar{F}^{\operatorname{ker}\varrho}
. We identify the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F(\varrho)/F)
with the image of
\varrho
. We refer to
F(\varrho)
as the extension of
F
cut out by
\varrho
. In particular, when
\varrho
is surjective,
F(\varrho)
is a
\mathcal{G}
-extension of
F
. With respect to the above notation, we set
F(\bar{\rho}_{E})
(resp.
F(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})
) to be the extension of
F
cut out by
\bar{\rho}_{E}
(resp.
\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}
). We shall also write
F(E[\ell])
to denote the field
F(\bar{\rho}_{E})
. Let
G_{E}
(resp.
G_{E}^{\prime}
) denote the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E})/\mathbb{Q})
(resp.
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/\mathbb{Q})
). Set
D\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E})/\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}))
and identify
G_{E}^{\prime}
with
G_{E}/D
. The residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{E}
induces an injection

\bar{\rho}_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{E}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}),

and
D
lies in the kernel of the determinant character

(2.1)

\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{E}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}.

The character
G_{E}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{E}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
(
2.1
) thus factors through the quotient
G_{E}^{\prime}
. Let
H_{E}
(resp.
\bar{H}_{E}
) denote the kernel of
G_{E}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{E}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
(resp.
G_{E}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{E}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
). Note that
D
is contained in
H_{E}
and
\bar{H}_{E}=H_{E}/D
. We find that
H_{E}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E})/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}))
, while
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
is contained
\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})
and
\bar{H}_{E}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}))
. Set
\tilde{K}
to be the Galois closure of
K
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{K,\ell}
be the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}
is surjective,

(2)

\tilde{K}(\mu_{\ell})
does not contain
\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})
.

Note that if
\bar{\rho}^{\prime}_{E}
is surjective, then the image of
\bar{\rho}_{E}
is not solvable. In particular,
E
does not have complex multiplication.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
E\in\mathcal{T}_{K,\ell}
,

\operatorname{Gal}(K(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/K(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}).

Proof.

Note that since
\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}
is surjective, we find that
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. On the other hand, since
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is simple, and
\tilde{K}(\mu_{\ell})
does not contain
\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})
, it follows that
\tilde{K}(\mu_{\ell})\cap\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
. This implies that
K(\mu_{\ell})\cap\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell})
and therefore we conclude that

\operatorname{Gal}(K(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/K(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}).

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

For every elliptic curve
E\in\mathcal{T}_{K,\ell}
,
(E,K,\ell)
satisfies (DS).

Proof.

It suffices to show that the five conditions of Theorem
2.2
are satisfied. Note that Lemma
2.7
asserts that
\operatorname{Gal}(K(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/K(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

(1)

Since
\operatorname{Gal}(K(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/K(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
,
E[\ell]
is irreducible as a
\operatorname{G}_{K}
-module.

(2)

This part follows from
[
LW17
]
or
[
PS21
, Lemma 2.2]
. We summarize the argument here. Let
G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
denote the image of
\bar{\rho}_{E|\operatorname{G}_{K}}
. Note that since
\operatorname{Gal}(K(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/K(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
,
G
contains an element of order
\ell
. Clearly,
G
is not contained in a Borel subgroup. It follows from
[
Sut16
, Proposition 3.1]
that
G
contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. In particular,
G
contains the negative identity
-\mathbf{1}
. Identify
G
with
\operatorname{Gal}(K(E[\ell])/K)
and let
\Delta
be the subgroup of
G
generated by
-\mathbf{1}
. Inflation-restriction yields an exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(G/\Delta,E[\ell]^{\Delta})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,E[\ell])\rightarrow H^{1}(\Delta,E[\ell]).

Since
E[\ell]^{\Delta}=0
, it follows that
H^{1}(G/\Delta,E[\ell]^{\Delta})=0
. Since
D
has order
2
, its order is coprime to that of
E[\ell]
and we find that
H^{1}(\Delta,E[\ell])=0
. Therefore, we conclude from the above exact sequence that
H^{1}(G,E[\ell])=0
.

(3)

Since
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is simple,
\operatorname{Gal}(K(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})/K(\mu_{\ell}))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
and the degree
[K(E[\ell])\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})]
is prime to
\ell
, it follows that there is no abelian extension of degree
\ell
of
K(\mu_{\ell})
contained in
K(E[\ell])
.

(4)

Since
\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}(\operatorname{G}_{K(\mu_{\ell})})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, it is clear that there is a diagonal element
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&\\
&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\bar{\rho}_{E}(\operatorname{G}_{K(\mu_{\ell})})
, such that
a\neq\pm 1
. Let
\tau_{0}\in\operatorname{G}_{K(\mu_{\ell})}
be chosen so that
\bar{\rho}(\tau_{0})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&\\
&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}}\right)
. Then, it follows that
E[\ell]/(\tau_{0}-1)E[\ell]=0
.

(5)

It is easy to see that
\bar{\rho}_{E}(\operatorname{G}_{K(\mu_{\ell})})
contains a unipotent element
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&b\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
, with
b\neq 0
. Let
\tau_{1}\in\operatorname{G}_{K(\mu_{\ell})}
be such that
\bar{\rho}(\tau_{1})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&b\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
, we find that

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\left(E[\ell]/(\tau_{1}-1)E[\ell]\right)=1.

∎

3.
Density results

Recall from Definition
2.6
that
\mathcal{T}_{K,\ell}
is the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}
is surjective,

(2)

\tilde{K}(\mu_{\ell})
does not contain
\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime})
.

According to Proposition
2.8
, for every elliptic curve
E\in\mathcal{T}_{K,\ell}
,
(E,K,\ell)
satisfies (DS). We prove that the set of elliptic curves
\mathcal{T}_{K,\ell}
has density
1
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\prime}
be the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}
is surjective. We shall assume throughout this section that
\ell\geq 5
. Note that the elliptic curves
E\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\prime}
do not have complex multiplication. Duke
[
Duk97
]
showed that the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\bar{\rho}_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is surjective has density
1
. In particular, it follows from Duke’s result that the set
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\prime}
has density
1
. The result proven in
loc. cit.
is much stronger. A prime
\ell
is an
exceptional prime
of an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
if the residual representation at
\ell
is not surjective. It is proven that a density
1
set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
have no exceptional primes. An elliptic curve
E_{/Q}
is said to be a
Serre curve
if the image of its associated adelic Galois representation has index
2
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Jones
[
Jon10
]
proves that the set of Serre curves has density
1
.

Let
A_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve and let
\mathcal{T}_{A}
be the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\bar{\rho}_{E}^{\prime}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{A}^{\prime}
. Given an elliptic curve
E
, we write
N_{E}
for its conductor. At a prime
p
, let
\operatorname{G}_{p}
denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\sigma_{p}\in\operatorname{G}_{p}
be a lift of the Frobenius element. For
p\nmid N_{E}\ell
,

\operatorname{trace}\bar{\rho}_{E}(\sigma_{p})\equiv a_{p}(E)\pmod{\ell};

set
t_{p}(E)\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
to denote
\operatorname{trace}\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{p})
. For
E\in\mathcal{T}_{A}
, we find that

(3.1)

t_{p}(E)=\pm t_{p}(A)

for all primes
p\nmid N_{E}N_{A}\ell
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
and let
(t_{1},t_{2},d)\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{3}
be such that
d\neq 0
. Let
\pi(X,d,\ell)
be the number of prime numbers
p\leq X
such that

Proposition 3.3

4.
Hilbert’s tenth problem

In this section, we prove Theorem
C
. Let us first recall the notion of an integrally diophantine extension of number fields, in the sense of
[
Shl07
, Section 1.2]
.
Let
A
be a commutative ring with identity, and
A^{n}
be the free
A
-module of rank
n
, consisting of tuples
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
with entries in
A
. Let
m
and
n
be positive integers and let
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\in A^{m}
. Denote by
(a,b)\in A^{n+m}
the tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{m})
. Given a finite set of polynomials,
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
, we set

\mathcal{F}(a;F_{1},\dots,F_{k})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{b\in A^{m}\mid F_{i}(a,b)=0\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,k\}.

Definition 4.1
.

A subset
S
of
A^{n}
is a
diophantine subset
of
A^{n}
if for some
m\geq 1
, there are polynomials
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\in A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
such that
S
consists of all
a\in A^{n}
for which the set
\mathcal{F}(a;F_{1},\dots,F_{k})
is nonempty.

Definition 4.2
.

An extension of number fields
L/K
is said to be
integrally diophantine
if
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a diophantine subset of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

Let
L^{\prime},L
and
K
be number fields such that

L^{\prime}\supseteq L\supseteq K.

Suppose that
L^{\prime}/L
and
L/K
are integrally diophantine extensions. Then, it is a well known fact that
L^{\prime}/K
is an integrally diophantine extension. Indeed, this is a special case of
[
Shl07
, Theorem 2.1.15]
.

We recall a conjecture of Denef and Lipchitz
[
DL78
]
.

Conjecture 4.3

(Denef-Lipchitz)
.

For any number field
L
,
L/\mathbb{Q}
is an integrally diophantine extension.

The celebrated result of Matiyasevich proves that Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer over
\mathbb{Z}
. If
L
is a number field for which Conjecture
4.3
holds, then Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. The Conjecture
4.3
above
is known to hold for various families of number fields
L
.

•

The conjecture holds when
L
is either totally real or a quadratic extension of a totally real number field (cf.
[
Den80
,
DL78
]
),

•

L
has exactly one complex place (cf.
[
Phe88
,
Shl89
,
Vid
]
),

•

L/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian (cf.
[
SS89
]
),

•

[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=4
,
L
is not totally real and
L/\mathbb{Q}
has a proper intermediate field (cf.
[
DL78
]
).

Poonen
[
Poo02
]
, Cornelissen-Pheidas-Zahidi
[
CPZ05
]
and Shlapentokh
[
Shl08
]
subsequently established new criteria for Conjecture
4.3
to hold, stated in terms of the stability of the Mordell-Weil rank of an elliptic curve. The following criterion of Shlapentokh relates diophantine stability for positive rank elliptic curves to Hilbert’s tenth problem for number rings.

Theorem 4.4

(Shlapentokh)
.

Let
L/K
be an extension of number fields and suppose that there exists an elliptic curve

such that
\operatorname{rank}E(L)=\operatorname{rank}E(K)>0
. Then,
L/K
is integrally diophantine. In particular, if Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then, Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

Proof of Theorem

By Theorem

∎

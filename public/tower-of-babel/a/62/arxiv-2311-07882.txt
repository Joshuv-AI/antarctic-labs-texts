Nonreciprocal transport in U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge theory of high- T c T_{c} cuprates
Taekoo Oh, Naoto Nagaosa

Abstract
The nature of the charge carriers in high- T c T_{c} cuprates is an essential issue to reveal their novel physical properties and the mechanism of their superconductivity. However, the experimental probes and the theoretical analysis have been mostly restricted to the linear responses. On the other hand, recent observations of Rashba-type spin-orbit coupling (SOC) on the surface of high- T c T_{c} cuprates imply the possible nonlinear and nonreciprocal transport phenomena under in-plane magnetic fields. In this paper, we study the nonreciprocal transport properties on the surface of cuprates by employing a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge theory framework, where the electrons are considered to be fractionalized. Our investigation highlights the intricate variations in nonreciprocal transport with respect to temperatures and dopings. First, it reveals contrasting behavior of nonreciprocity in each normal phase. Second, it discerns the tendencies between underdoped and overdoped superconducting states and their paraconductivity. The complex behaviors of nonreciprocal transport originate from the spinful and spinless nature of the charge carriers and their kinetic energy scales. These findings pave a new avenue to explore the electronic states in high- T c T_{c} cuprates in terms of nonreciprocal transport phenomena.

Each phase of normal states shows contrasting behavior of nonreciprocal transport, as for magnitude and dependence on

These observations are elucidated by the distinct characteristics of the carrier type and its kinetic energy.

Accordingly, the magnitude of nonreciprocal transport in normal states is intricately linked to the kinetic energy of carriers, while its dependence on

Similarly, the overdoped SC and its vicinity shows a sharp increase in nonreciprocal transports compared to the underdoped SC and its vicinity, owing to the carrier type and the fermion pairing energies.

Discussion.—

Nonreciprocal transport phenomena in high-
T_{c}
cuprates are chiefly influenced by two factors: the kinetic energy and the type of carriers.
As the kinetic energy increases, the magnitude of linear conductivity becomes more prominent, so the nonreciprocal transport diminishes.
On the other hand, considering the spinlessness and indirect Zeeman effect of holons, nonreciprocal transport of holons will be small due to the weak symmetry breaking.
Specifically, in normal states, the magnitude of nonreciprocity
\gamma
is large in AF, intermediate in PG and SM, and small in FL, because the carriers gain more kinetic energy at a higher doping.
Upon the kinetic energy, the drop of
\gamma
at the transition from AF to PG phases can be attributed to the fact that the carrier changes from electron to holon.
On the other hand,
\gamma
drops significantly at the transition from SM to FL despite electron carriers in FL phase, since the kinetic energy increases more rapidly.

Moreover, the disparity in energy scales of symmetry breaking of carriers dictates the dependence of
\gamma
on
T
and
\delta
.
In FL phase, the symmetry breaking energy scale of
h
and
\lambda_{SO}
remains constant with changes in
T
and
\delta
, leading to a reduction in
\gamma
as
T
decreases and
\delta
increases due to kinetic energy.
Similarly, in AF phase, upon
h
and
\lambda_{SO}
, the symmetry breaking is governed by the magnetization which decreases as
T
increases and
\delta
decreases.
This results in the enhancement in
\gamma
as
T
increases, but
\gamma
still reduces in increasing
\delta
because of the kinetic energy.
In contrast, in the PG and SM phases, the symmetry breaking is attributed to the energy scale of
\lambda_{SO}\xi_{y}
. This increases as
T
decreases and
\delta
increases, resulting in the enhancement of
\gamma
[see SI for details].

In the superconducting (SC) phase and its vicinity, where the system is regarded as a boson condensate, the energy scale of symmetry breaking assumes paramount importance.
The breaking of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}
symmetries is attributed to
h
and
\lambda_{SO}

in electronic systems, whereas it is linked to
\lambda_{SO}\xi_{y}
in holonic systems. Notably, since

h\sim 1~meV
and
\lambda_{SO}\sim 10~meV
are much larger than
\lambda_{SO}\xi_{y}\sim 1~\mu eV
, the symmetry breaking is strong in the overdoped region.
Additionally, as the energy scale is introduced by the electron pairing
T_{c}\lesssim 70~K
, both
\gamma
for paraconductivity and the quality factor for the SC diode effect significantly enhance in the overdoped region.

In light of the theoretical analysis depicted in Figure
3
, we propose several predictions for experimental exploration.
Discontinuities, suggested by the theoretical computations, might be replaced by a smooth but rapid change in reality, owing to the inherent approximations of the mean-field theory.
Accordingly, we envisage distinctive nonmonotonous
\gamma
profiles, which begins with a
U
-shape in underdoped region and ends with a
\lambda
-shape in overdoped region as a function of
\delta
.
Moreover, the spinon pairing reduces the kinetic energy of holons, PG has a slightly larger nonreciprocity than SM. This might result in a weak two-hump shape for
\gamma
in
\delta
on optimally doped region.
Close to
T_{c}
, the surge in
\gamma
due to paraconductivity is expected to be more pronounced in the overdoped region compared to the underdoped region.
In the SC phase, the rapid escalation of
Q
in the SC diode effect driven by additional doping, is also foreseen.
Lastly, we address a caveat that our argument is not applicable to the bulk 3D system, since we only consider the 2D surface.

Our extensive theoretical examination underscores the intriguing aspects of surface nonreciprocal transport in diverse phases of high-
T_{c}
cuprates and offers practical insights for future investigations.
From the MCA in the AF phase to the nonlinear paraconductivity and SC diode effect in the SC phase, these findings illuminate the intricate behavior of high-
T_{c}
cuprates.
We are confident that these practical expectations will pave the way for pioneering discoveries in cuprates, serving as valuable tools in advancing the field of high-
T_{c}
superconductors.

Appendix A
The free energies of superconductors

A.1
Paraconductivity and SC diode effect

We here describe the Ginzburg-Landau free energies and the nonreciprocal transports for superconductor phase.
The basic form of free energy is

\displaystyle F=\int_{k}(\eta(k)|\phi_{k}|^{2}+\nu(k)|\phi_{k}|^{4}).

(32)

Using this free energy, we obtain the nonreciprocity
\gamma
by computing the paraconductivity, and the quality factor for SC diode effect. The current from paraconductivity is defined as:

\displaystyle J_{x}=\frac{2T_{c}}{V\Gamma}\sum_{k}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}j_{x}(t)\exp(-\f{2}{\Gamma} \int_{t'}^{t} dt'' \eta(t'')).

(33)

Here,
\Gamma
is the coefficient in the time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau equation,
V
is the volume of system,
\eta(t)=\eta(\vec{k}-2e\vec{E}t)
, and
j_{x}(t)\equiv-\partial\eta(t)/\partial A_{x}
.

On the other hand, the supercurrent is computed by derivative of the integral kernel of the free energy with respect to momentum,

\displaystyle J(k)=-2e[\eta^{\prime}(k)|\phi_{k0}|^{2}+\nu^{\prime}(k)|\phi_{k0}|^{4}].

(34)

Here,
\phi_{k0}
is the order parameter at the energy minimum for momentum
k
.
The critical currents
I_{c+}
and
-I_{c-}
are found at the maximum of
J(k)
for positive momentum and the minimum for negative momentum, respectively.
The quality factor is
Q=2(I_{c+}-I_{c-})/(I_{c+}+I_{c-})
.

A.2
Underdoped

For underdoped case, the holonic transport is dominant. Because the holon is bosonic, the superconductor arises when the holonic condensation occurs. The order parameter for bose condensation is
\langle b\rangle
, where
b
is the boson operator. Accordingly, the free energy for holonic superconductor is simply

\displaystyle F_{1}=\int_{k}(\alpha_{1}+\epsilon_{k})|\phi_{k}|^{2}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}|\phi_{k}|^{4}.

(35)

Here,
\phi_{k}
is the order parameter,
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{0}T_{c}\epsilon
and
\beta_{1}
are conventional Ginzburg-Landau order parameters,
\alpha_{0}\sim H_{cB}^{2}/8\pi\sim J\delta^{2}
,
\epsilon=(T-T_{c})/T_{c}
, and
\epsilon_{k}
is the holonic energy.
From Eq.
14
, we approximate
\epsilon_{k}
up to the third order of momentum near
\Gamma
point,
\epsilon_{k}=ak^{2}-\lambda(k_{x}-k_{x}^{3}/6)
, where
a\sim t\chi
,
\lambda\sim\lambda_{SO}\xi_{y}
.

Using Eq.
33
, the current from paraconductivity is given by

\displaystyle J=\frac{e^{2}}{16\epsilon}E-\frac{e^{3}\pi}{1536T_{c}\epsilon^{2}}\frac{\lambda}{a}E^{2}.

(36)

Thus, the nonreciprocity is

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{\pi\lambda}{6aheT_{c}W}.

(37)

The quality factor from SC diode effect, on the other hand,
is given by

\displaystyle Q=\frac{\sqrt{-\alpha_{1}}\lambda}{3\sqrt{3}a^{3/2}}.

(38)

When we apply
t\sim 3,\chi\sim 1,\lambda\sim 0.1,\xi_{y}\sim 10^{-2}-10^{-1}h,T_{c}\sim 10^{-2}
,
\alpha_{0}\sim J\delta^{2},
and
\delta\sim 0.1
, we got
\gamma\sim 10-100~T^{-1}A^{-1}
and
Q\sim 10^{-9}-10^{-8}h\sqrt{-\epsilon}
.

A.3
Overdoped

For overdoped case, the electronic transport is dominant. The superconductor arises when the Cooper pair is formed. The order parameter here is
\langle cc\rangle
, where
c
is the fermionic operator. Hence, the free energy could be obtained by computing the bubble diagrams.

Here, while the SC diode effect comes from Rashba spin-orbit coupling, the nonlinear paraconductivity is attributed to the parity mixing by
\mathcal{P}
symmetry breaking. Therefore, we employ two different free energies as for the temperature range.

Basically, we suppose that the Hamiltonian in spinor basis is
H_{0}=\sum_{k}c_{k\alpha}^{\dagger}H_{k,\alpha\beta}c_{k\beta}
, where

\displaystyle H_{k}=\xi_{k}+a(k_{x}\sigma_{y}-k_{y}\sigma_{x})-\vec{h}\cdot\vec{\sigma}.

(39)

Here,
\xi_{k}=k^{2}/2m-\mu
. Comparing with Eq.
11
, we could let
m^{-1}\sim J\chi,a\sim D\chi
. The energy dispersion is

\displaystyle E_{kA}=\xi_{k}+A\sqrt{(ak_{x}-h)^{2}+(ak_{y})^{2}},

(40)

where
A=\pm 1
.

When
T\gtrsim T_{c}
, in order to consider the parity mixing, we include the following electron interactions in spinor basis:

\displaystyle H_{int}^{g}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime}}V^{g}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime})c_{k,\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k,\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k^{\prime},\downarrow}c_{k^{\prime},\uparrow}

\displaystyle H_{int}^{u}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2V}\sum_{\vec{k}\vec{k}^{\prime}}V^{u}_{ij}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime})(i\sigma_{i}\sigma_{2})_{\alpha\beta}(i\sigma_{j}\sigma_{2})^{\dagger}_{\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle\times c_{k\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{-k\beta}^{\dagger}c_{-k^{\prime}\gamma}c_{-k^{\prime}\delta},

(41)

Here,
V_{g}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime})=-V_{g}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})(k_{x}^{\prime 2}-k_{y}^{\prime 2})/2k^{2}k^{\prime 2}
and
V^{u}_{ij}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime})=V_{u}g_{i}(\vec{k})g_{j}(\vec{k}^{\prime})
, where
\vec{g}(\vec{k})=(-k_{y},k_{x})/k
.

By transforming the Hamiltonians to the band representation and perform the calculation for bubble diagram, we acquire the free energy.

\displaystyle\int_{k}\sum_{AB}\psi_{Ak}^{*}[g^{-1}_{AB}+L_{Ak}\delta_{AB}]\psi_{Bk}.

\displaystyle-N_{A}(S_{1}(T)-K(T)k^{2}+AR(T)hk_{x}).

(43)

Also,

\displaystyle S_{1}(T)=

\displaystyle\log\frac{2e^{\gamma_{E}}E_{c}}{\pi T}\approx S_{1}(T_{c})-\epsilon,

\displaystyle K(T)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}S_{3}(T)\frac{(E_{R}+\mu)}{2m},

\displaystyle R(T)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}S_{3}(T)\sqrt{\frac{E_{R}+\mu}{2m}},\text{ and}

\displaystyle S_{3}(T)=

\displaystyle\frac{7\zeta(3)}{4\pi^{2}T^{2}}.

r_{t}=\frac{2V_{u}}{V_{g}+V_{u}}

\displaystyle\times[\{L_{+k}(N_{S}(k)+N_{U}(k))+L_{-k}(N_{U}(k)-N_{S}(k))\}^{2}

\displaystyle-4N_{U}^{2}(k)S_{1}^{2}(T_{c})].

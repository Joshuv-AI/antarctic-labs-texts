Near-Optimal Estimation of Linear Functionals with Log-Concave Observation Errors
Simon Foucart, Grigoris Paouris — Texas A&M University

Abstract
This note addresses the question of optimally estimating a linear functional of an object acquired through linear observations corrupted by random noise, where optimality pertains to a worst-case setting tied to a symmetric, convex, and closed model set containing the object. It complements the article “Statistical Estimation and Optimal Recovery” published in the Annals of Statistics in 1994. There, Donoho showed (among other things) that, for Gaussian noise, linear maps provide near-optimal estimation schemes relatively to a performance measure relevant in Statistical Estimation. Here, we advocate for a different performance measure arguably more relevant in Optimal Recovery. We show that, relatively to this new measure, linear maps still provide near-optimal estimation schemes even if the noise is merely log-concave. Our arguments, which make a connection to the deterministic noise situation and bypass properties specific to the Gaussian case, offer an alternative to parts of Donoho’s proof.



Key words and phrases:
Optimal recovery, Statistical estimation, Log-concavity, Minimax problems.

AMS classification:
41A65, 62C20, 90C47.

Introduction

In this note, we take a second look at the Optimal Recovery problem when random observation errors are present.
As a very brief reminder,
we recall that the Optimal Recovery problem consists in recovering an object
f
—typically a function—from observational data
y_{i}=\lambda_{i}(f)
—typically point evaluations—in a way that is worst-case optimal or near-optimal relatively to a model set
\mathcal{K}
.
Here, the difference with this standard scenario is that the observations
y_{i}
are corrupted with random additive errors
e_{i}
,
so that
y_{i}=\lambda_{i}(f)+e_{i}
.
Thus, the situation is as follows:
an element
f
from a Banach space
F
is partially known through:

•

some
a priori
information:
f
belongs to a subset
\mathcal{K}
of
F
, i.e.,

f\in\mathcal{K},

where
\mathcal{K}
is called the model set;

•

some
a posteriori
information:
f
is inaccurately observed through the actions of some linear functionals
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in F^{*}
, i.e.,

y_{i}=\lambda_{i}(f)+e_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.

This is summarized as
y=\Lambda f+e
, where the linear map
\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called the observation map.
Here,
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a random vector.

When estimating
f
, or merely a quantity of interest
Q(f)
taking values in some Banach space
Z
,
we simply apply a so-called recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to Z
to the available observation vector
y=\Lambda f+e
.
The performance of this recovery map could be assessed,
for some index
p\in[1,\infty]
, via the global recovery error

(1)

{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}(\Delta)=\bigg(\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbb{E}\big[\|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f+e)\|_{Z}^{p}\big]\bigg)^{1/p}.

We appended a superscript “
{\rm se}
” because
this choice is favored in Statistical Estimation,
see e.g. the article
[
3
]
, which contains the classical result being complemented by this note.
However, we prefer to assess the performance of a recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to Z
via another global recovery error, namely

(2)

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{p}(\Delta)=\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f+e)\|_{Z}^{p}\bigg]\bigg)^{1/p}.

We appended a superscript “
{\rm or}
” because we believe
that this choice is better suited to a worst-case perspective,
hence more relevant in Optimal Recovery.
Indeed, suppose that
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}(\Delta)
and
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{p}(\Delta)
are small,
say bounded by some
\theta

Markov’s inequality in conjunction with
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}(\Delta)^{p}\leq\theta^{p}

\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K},\quad\mathbb{P}\bigg[\|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f+e)\|_{Z}\leq\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon}\bigg]\geq 1-\varepsilon^{p},

while Markov’s inequality in conjunction with
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{p}(\Delta)^{p}\leq\theta^{p}
would naturally yield the statement

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f+e)\|_{Z}\leq\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon}\mbox{ for all }f\in\mathcal{K}\bigg]\geq 1-\varepsilon^{p}.

Of course, in the absence of observation errors (
e=0
),
these two notions coincide and reduce to a quantity which is independent of
p
,
namely to the global worst-case error (aka distortion)

{\rm gwce}(\Delta)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f)\|_{Z}.

In this case, if the model set is symmetric and convex
(i.e., if
-\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}
and
(1/2)\mathcal{K}+(1/2)\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
)
and if

is a linear functional,
a classical result of Smolyak (see
[
1

or
[

states that “linear recovery maps are optimal”,
meaning that there exists a linear map
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\rm gwce}(\Delta_{\rm lin})=\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}}{\rm gwce}(\Delta).

In the presence of Gaussian observation errors,
the previously mentioned seminal work
[
3
]
of Donoho implies that,
although not yielding genuine optimality anymore,
“linear recovery maps are still near-optimal”.
Precisely, for

{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\leq\kappa\times\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}}{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}(\Delta),

As a matter of fact,

In this note,

Relevant examples include vectors with independent entries distributed according to the Gaussian, Laplace, or uniform distribution.

.
Precisely, we show (Theorem

for any

,
if
\mathcal{K}
is symmetric

is a linear functional,
and if

there exists a linear map

Background Information

2.1
Properties of log-concave random vectors

A probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called log-concave if,
for all compact subsets
\mathcal{C}_{0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{1}
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and all
\tau\in[0,1]
,

\mu((1-\tau)\mathcal{C}_{0}+\tau\mathcal{C}_{1})\geq\mu(\mathcal{C}_{0})^{1-\tau}\mu(\mathcal{C}_{1})^{\tau}.

A result of C. Borell ensures that a log-concave probability measure—provided it is not supported on a subspace—satisfies
\mu(\mathcal{C})=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\pi(x)dx
,
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
for some integrable function
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{m}~\to~\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
-\ln(\pi):\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is a convex function.
We refer to
[
2
]
for the properties of log-concavity listed below.

A random vector
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called log-concave if it is distributed according to a probability measure which is log-concave.
The following fact about log-concave random vectors,
known as Borell’s lemma,
will be useful later.

Lemma 1
.

Let
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a log-concave random vector and let
|\cdot|
be a seminorm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Then, for any
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
,

\big(\mathbb{E}[|e|^{q}]\big)^{1/q}\leq C\frac{q}{p}\big(\mathbb{E}[|e|^{p}]\big)^{1/p},

where the absolute constant
C
can be taken as
C={\mathrm{e}}
.

Another useful fact for us is that, if
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a mean-zero log-concave random vector with covariance matrix
\mathbb{E}[ee^{\top}]=\sigma^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{m}
and if
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an
\ell_{2}
-normalized vector,
then
\xi=\langle u,e\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
is a mean-zero log-concave random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}
.
We will also rely on the following property of log-concave random variables.
The result is not new,
but we could not pinpoint the exact statement in the literature.
So, for the reader’s convenience,
we provide a proof inspired by an argument of Milman and Pajor from
[
9
]
.
An extension to log-concave random vectors follows from results of
[
7
, Section 5]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the probability density function of a mean-zero log-concave random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}
.
Then

\pi(x)\geq\frac{\delta}{\sigma},\qquad\mbox{whenever}\qquad|x|\leq\gamma\,\sigma,

where the constants
\delta
and
\gamma
can be taken as
\delta=1/(2\sqrt{3}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2})
and
\gamma=1/(5{\mathrm{e}}
).

Proof.

The argument makes crucial use of a two-sided estimate for
\pi(0)
, namely

(3)

\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}\,{\mathrm{e}}\,\sigma}\leq\pi(0)\leq\frac{3}{\sigma},

which goes back to Hensley
[
6
]
(see also
[
9
, Section 2.5]
,
[
2
]
, or
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
).
There, the result is stated for a symmetric random variable.
The nonsymmetric case, which follows by applying a result of Fradelizi (
[
2
, Theorem 2.2.2]
), appears in
[
2
]
, see Theorem 2.2.3 and Lemma 2.2.4.

We shall now prove that
\pi(\pm\gamma\sigma)\geq{\mathrm{e}}^{-1}\pi(0)
.
This will imply that,
for any
x\in[-\gamma\sigma,\gamma\sigma]
written as
x=(1-\tau)\times(-\gamma\sigma)+\tau\times(\gamma\sigma)
for some
\tau\in[0,1]
,
we have
\pi(x)\geq\pi(-\gamma\sigma)^{1-\tau}\pi(\gamma\sigma)^{\tau}\geq{\mathrm{e}}^{-1}\pi(0)
, yielding the announced inequality

\pi(x)\geq\delta/\sigma
with
\delta=1/(2\sqrt{3}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2})
by invoking (
3
).
So let us assume on the contrary that one of
\pi(-\gamma\sigma)
or
\pi(-\gamma\sigma)
is smaller than
{\mathrm{e}}^{-1}\pi(0)
,
e.g. that
\pi(\gamma\sigma)<{\mathrm{e}}^{-1}\pi(0)
.
Then, for
x\geq\gamma\sigma
,

{\mathrm{e}}^{-1}\pi(0)>\pi(\gamma\sigma)=\pi\bigg(\Big(1-\frac{\gamma\sigma}{x}\Big)\times 0+\frac{\gamma\sigma}{x}\times x\bigg)\geq\pi(0)^{1-\gamma\sigma/x}\pi(x)^{\gamma\sigma/x}.

Rearranging the latter, we deduce that
\pi(x)<\pi(0){\mathrm{e}}^{-x/(\gamma\sigma)}
for
x\geq\gamma\sigma
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma\sigma}^{\infty}x\pi(x)dx

\displaystyle<\pi(0)\int_{\gamma\sigma}^{\infty}x{\mathrm{e}}^{-x/(\gamma\sigma)}dx=\pi(0)\Big[-\gamma\sigma(x+\gamma\sigma){\mathrm{e}}^{-x/(\gamma\sigma)}\Big]_{\gamma\sigma}^{\infty}=\pi(0)\,2(\gamma\sigma)^{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{{\mathrm{e}}}\gamma^{2}\sigma,

where the last step utilized (
3
).
Moreover, we also have

\int_{0}^{\gamma\sigma}x\pi(x)dx\leq\gamma\sigma\int_{0}^{\gamma\sigma}\pi(x)dx\leq\gamma\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\pi(x)dx=\gamma\sigma.

Adding these two inequalities, then using the mean-zero property and Lemma
1
,
we obtain

\Big(1+\frac{6\gamma}{{\mathrm{e}}}\Big)\gamma\sigma>\int_{0}^{\infty}x\pi(x)dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|\pi(x)dx\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{2{\mathrm{e}}}\bigg[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2}\pi(x)dx\bigg]^{1/2}=\frac{1}{4{\mathrm{e}}}\sigma.

We derive the desired contradiction
as soon as
\gamma
is small enough so that

(1+6\gamma/{\mathrm{e}})\gamma<1/(4{\mathrm{e}})
,
which occurs with our choice
\gamma=1/(5{\mathrm{e}})
.
∎

2.2
Comparison of the two notions of global recovery error

In this subsection,
we compare the notions of global recovery error introduced in (
1

2

The results are stated right below and proved shortly afterwards.
We note that they are valid when

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

Let
\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

F

\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to Z

{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}(\Delta_{\rm lin})

Before proving these two statements, we point out a key consequence mentioned in the introduction:

—as established for Gaussian observation errors in

]
—then it is automatically acquired for all

Here is a precise statement.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an observation map defined on a Banach space
F
,

be a symmetric model set, and let

When estimating a linear map

\kappa\geq 1

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\\

We just have to consider a linear map

.
This implies that

{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{q}(\Delta_{\rm lin})^{q}\leq 2^{q-1}\Big({\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})^{q}+(C\,q)^{q}{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})^{q}\Big)\leq 2^{q}(C\,q)^{q}{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})^{q},

which is the required inequality (
5
) with
D_{q}=2\,C\,q
.
∎

Remark
.

As revealed in the above proof,
all notions
{\rm ge}_{p}^{\rm se/or}(\Delta_{\rm lin})
of global recovery error
for linear maps
\Delta_{\rm lin}
are comparable to the maximum (or sum)
of the noiseless global worst-case error
{\rm gwce}(\Delta_{\rm lin})=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\|Q(f)-\Delta_{\rm lin}(\Lambda f)\|_{Z}

and the expected noise
\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta_{\rm lin}e\|_{Z}]
.

2.3
Optimal estimation with deterministic observation errors

Throughout this subsection,
it is assumed that the quantity of interest is a linear functional,
in short that
Q\in F^{*}
.
As for the model set
\mathcal{K}
, it is assumed to be symmetric and convex,
so it can be thought of in terms of its Minkowski functional (aka gauge function)

|f|_{\mathcal{K}}=\inf\{t>0:f\in t\,\mathcal{K}\},\qquad f\in F,

recalling that
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{K}}:F\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
is a seminorm in the present situation.
Moreover,
we take notice of the equivalence
f\in\mathcal{K}\Leftrightarrow|f|_{\mathcal{K}}\leq 1
when the set
\mathcal{K}
is furthermore closed in the norm of
F
.

In the accurate setting (where there is no observation error),
we have already pointed out that “linear recovery maps are optimal”.
This remains true in the presence of observation errors modeled deterministically via the assumption that
e\in\mathcal{E}

for some symmetric and convex subset
\mathcal{E}
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The relevant example in this note is
\mathcal{E}=\{e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\|e\|_{2}\leq\sigma\}
,
for which the Minkowski functional is given by
|e|_{\mathcal{E}}=\|e\|_{2}/\sigma
,
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The precise optimality result reads as follows
(the statements in (
10
) are somewhat present in
[
4
]
, but for a specific model set based on approximability).

Proposition 6
.

Let
Q:F\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear functional.
If the sets
\mathcal{K}\subseteq F
and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
are symmetric, convex, and closed,
then

(8)

\displaystyle\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}}\,\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K},\,e\in\mathcal{E}}\big|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f+e)\big|

\displaystyle=\min_{\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\,{\rm linear}}\,\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K},\,e\in\mathcal{E}}\big|Q(f)-\Delta_{\rm lin}(\Lambda f+e)\big|

(9)

\displaystyle=\min_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\bigg\{\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg|\Big(Q-\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\lambda_{i}\Big)f\bigg|+\sup_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\Big|\langle a,e\rangle\Big|\bigg\}

(10)

\displaystyle{=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{K},\,\Lambda h\in\mathcal{E}}\big|Q(h)\big|}=\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|Q(h)|}{\max\{|h|_{\mathcal{K}},|\Lambda h|_{\mathcal{E}}\}}.

Proof.

The optimality of linear recovery maps expressed by (
8
) is well known and follows from a simple reduction, recalled here, to the accurate setting.
Namely, for any
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we interpret the global worst-case error as

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K},\,e\in\mathcal{E}}|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f+e)|=\sup_{(f,e)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}\big|\widetilde{Q}\big((f,e)\big)-\Delta\big(\widetilde{\Lambda}\big((f,e)\big)\big)\big|,

where the extended quantity of interest
\widetilde{Q}:F\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is the linear functional defined by
\widetilde{Q}\big((f,e)\big)=Q(f)

and the extended observation map
\widetilde{\Lambda}:F\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the linear map defined by
\widetilde{\Lambda}\big((f,e)\big)=\Lambda f+e
.
Since the extended model set
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}=\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{E}
is symmetric and convex,
the classical result of Smolyak about optimality of linear maps applies and justifies the equality (
8
).
The equality (
9
) is obtained by writing any linear recovery map from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
\mathbb{R}
as

\Delta_{\rm lin}=\langle a,\cdot\rangle
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and by minimizing over
a
(with some simple manipulations in the mix).
The first equality in (
10
) is also a consequence of Smolyak’s result,
since it contains (see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem 9.3]
) the fact that the minimal global worst-case error equals the so-called null error, which is

{\sup_{(h,e)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}},\,(h,e)\in\ker\widetilde{\Lambda}}\big|\widetilde{Q}\big((h,e)\big)\big|=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{K},\,e\in\mathcal{E},\,\Lambda h+e=0}\big|Q(h)\big|=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{K},\,\Lambda h\in\mathcal{E}}\big|Q(h)\big|.}

It remains to notice that
[h\in\mathcal{K}\mbox{ and }\Lambda h\in\mathcal{E}]\Leftrightarrow\max\{|h|_{\mathcal{K}},|\Lambda h|_{\mathcal{E}}\}\leq 1

and exploit homogeneity to arrive at the second equality in (
10
).
∎

Remark
.

Making sense of (
10
) implicitly requires that
\max\{|h|_{\mathcal{K}},|\Lambda h|_{\mathcal{E}}\}>0
whenever
h\in F\setminus\{0\}
.
This is actually a common assumption in Optimal Recovery,
at least when
\mathcal{E}
is a ball relative to some norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
If this assumption was violated,
any recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
would have infinite global worst-case errors,
so the problem would not even be contemplated in the first place!
Indeed, suppose that we could find a nonzero
h\in F
such that
\|\Lambda h\|=0
and
|h|_{\mathcal{K}}=0
,
meaning that
\Lambda h=0
and that
(1/t)h\in\mathcal{K}
for all
t>0
.
Then, fixing
f_{0}\in\mathcal{K}
and defining
f_{x}=f_{0}+xh
for any
x>0
,
we notice that
\Lambda f_{x}=\Lambda f_{0}

and that
f_{x}\in\mathcal{K}
—this is because
(1-\varepsilon)f_{0}+\varepsilon(x/\varepsilon)h
belongs to
\mathcal{K}
as a convex combination of elements from
\mathcal{K}
, and hence its limit when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
,
i.e.,
f_{0}+xh=f_{x}
, belongs to
\mathcal{K}
.
In this case,
the global recovery error
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)
, say,
cannot be finite independently of
Q\in F^{*}
,
since

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{x>0}\big|Q(f_{x})-\Delta(\Lambda f_{x}+e)\big|\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{x>0}\big|Q(f_{0})+xQ(h)-\Delta(\Lambda f_{0}+e)\big|\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{x>0}\Big(x|Q(h)|-\big|Q(f_{0})-\Delta(\Lambda f_{0}+e)\big|\Big)\bigg]=\sup_{x>0}\;x|Q(h)|-\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Q(f_{0})-\Delta(\Lambda f_{0}+e)\big|\Big].

The latter is certainly infinite for those linear functionals
Q\in F^{*}
such that
Q(h)\not=0
.

3
The One-Dimensional Lower Bound

This section is devoted to the simplest setting of all, namely:

F=\mathbb{R}
,
f\in\mathcal{K}=[-\tau,\tau]
,

m=1
,
y=cf+\xi\in\mathbb{R}

with a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and a mean-zero random variable
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
Q(f)=bf
with
b\in\mathbb{R}
.
The global recovery errors of a map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
then reduce, for
1\leq p<\infty
, to

(11)

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}(\Delta)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|bf-\Delta(cf+\xi)\big|^{p}\bigg],

(12)

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{p}(\Delta)^{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\big|bf-\Delta(cf+\xi)\big|^{p}\bigg].

When
\xi
is log-concave,
we shall show that “linear recovery maps are near-optimal”:
for
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}
,
this is expected since we intend to establish this fact in a more general setting;
for
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}
, it may seem more surprising.
The result for
{\rm ge}^{\rm se/or}_{p}
with
p\geq 1
follows from
the result for
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{p}
with
p=1
, as explained in Subsection
2.2
,
and the latter is a consequence of an upper bound for the infimum of
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})
when
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a linear map (Lemma
7
below)
and of a lower bound for
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)
when
\Delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary map
(Lemma
8
below).
This lower bound is in fact an essential step towards the main result.

Lemma 7
.

In the simplest setting,
if
\xi
is a mean-zero random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}
, then

\inf_{\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\,{\rm linear}}{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\leq\inf_{\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\,{\rm linear}}{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta_{\rm lin})=\frac{|b|\tau\sigma}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}\tau^{2}}}\asymp\frac{|b|}{|c|}\min\{\sigma,|c|\tau\}.

Proof.

The leftmost inequality follows from
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\leq{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta_{\rm lin})
, see Proposition
3
.
The rightmost comparison follows from the two-sided estimate
\max\{\sigma,|c|\tau\}\leq\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}\tau^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}\max\{\sigma,|c|\tau\}
and some straightforward manipulations.
For the middle equality,
representing the action of
\Delta_{\rm lin}
as the multiplication by some
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta_{\rm lin})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big((b-ac)f-a\xi)\big)^{2}\Big]=\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\mathbb{E}\Big[((b-ac)f)^{2}-2(b-ac)fa\xi+(a\xi)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\big[((b-ac)f)^{2}+a^{2}\sigma^{2}\big]=(b-ac)^{2}\tau^{2}+a^{2}\sigma^{2}=(c^{2}\tau^{2}+\sigma^{2})a^{2}-2bc\tau^{2}a+b^{2}\tau^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}\tau^{2}}a-\frac{bc\tau^{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}\tau^{2}}}\bigg)^{2}-\frac{b^{2}c^{2}\tau^{4}}{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}\tau^{2}}+b^{2}\tau^{2}\geq\frac{b^{2}\tau^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}\tau^{2}},

with equality possible for the choice
a=bc\tau^{2}/(\sigma^{2}+c^{2}\tau^{2})
.
This justifies the value of the infimum of
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta_{\rm lin})
over all linear maps
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

In the simplest setting,
if
\xi
is a mean-zero log-concave random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}
, then,
for any
\Delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)\geq{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)\geq\alpha\frac{|b|}{|c|}\min\{\sigma,|c|\tau\},

where the constant
\alpha
can be taken as
\alpha={1/(100\sqrt{3}{\mathrm{e}}^{4})}
.

Proof.

Since
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)\geq{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)
in general,
see Proposition
3
, it suffices to lower-bound
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)
, which takes the form

{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)=\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|bf-\Delta(cf+x)|\pi(x)dx,

where
\pi
is the probability density function of the log-concave distribution.
According to Lemma
2
, it satisfies
\pi(x)\geq\delta/\sigma
whenever
|x|\leq\gamma\sigma
,
which implies that

{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)\geq\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\int_{-\gamma\sigma}^{\gamma\sigma}|bf-\Delta(cf+x)|dx.

Let us introduce the quantity
\nu=\gamma\min\{\sigma,|c|\tau\}/(2|c|)
,
so that
|c|\nu\leq\gamma\sigma/2
and
\nu\leq\gamma\tau/2\leq\tau
,
ensuring that
\pm\nu\in[-\tau,\tau]
.
We obtain

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\int_{-\gamma\sigma}^{\gamma\sigma}|\pm b\nu-\Delta(\pm c\nu+x)|dx=\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\int_{-\gamma\sigma\pm c\nu}^{\gamma\sigma\pm c\nu}|\pm b\nu-\Delta(y)|dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\int_{-\gamma\sigma+|c|\nu}^{\gamma\sigma-|c|\nu}|\pm b\nu-\Delta(y)|dy\geq\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\int_{-\gamma\sigma/2}^{\gamma\sigma/2}|\pm b\nu-\Delta(y)|dy.

In turn, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\int_{-\gamma\sigma/2}^{\gamma\sigma/2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}|b\nu-\Delta(y)|+\frac{1}{2}|-b\nu-\Delta(y)|\bigg)dy\geq\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\int_{-\gamma\sigma/2}^{\gamma\sigma/2}|b|\nu dy=\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\gamma\sigma|b|\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\gamma^{2}}{2}\frac{|b|}{|c|}\min\{\sigma,|c|\tau\},

which is the desired inequality with
\alpha=\delta\gamma^{2}/2={1/(100\sqrt{3}{\mathrm{e}}^{4})}
.
∎

We finish this section by emphasizing that near-optimality of linear recovery maps does not apply
to all types of distributions for the random observation errors,
even in the simplest setting.
Namely, we prove that a Rademacher distribution scaled to have variance
\sigma^{2}
leads to near-optimality for
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}
, say, if and only if
\sigma\leq|c|\tau
,
so near-optimality of linear recovery maps is invalid for large noise level.
This is due to
\inf_{\Delta_{\rm lin}}{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\asymp(|b|/|c|)\min\{\sigma,c|\tau|\}
(Lemma
7
)
and to the result below.

Proposition 9
.

In the simplest setting,
if
\xi
is the mean-zero random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}
defined by
\mathbb{P}[\xi=-\sigma]=\mathbb{P}[\xi=+\sigma]=1/2
,
then

\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}}{\rm ge}^{\rm se/or}_{2}(\Delta)\left\{\begin{matrix}\geq\dfrac{|b|}{|c|}\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2}}&\mbox{ if }\sigma\leq|c|\tau,\\
&\\
=0&\mbox{ if }\sigma>|c|\tau.\end{matrix}\right.

Proof.

Case 1:
\sigma\leq|c|\tau
.

Since
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{2}(\Delta)\geq{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta)
in general, see Proposition
3
,
we only need to establish the lower bound on
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta)
for an arbitrary
\Delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Here,

{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta)^{2}=\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\big|bf-\Delta(cf+\sigma)\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big|bf-\Delta(cf-\sigma)\big|^{2}\Bigg).

Fixing some
y
in the interval
[-|c|\tau+\sigma,|c|\tau-\sigma]
,
which is nonempty in this case,
we consider
f_{-}=(y-\sigma)/c\in[-\tau,\tau]
, so that
cf_{-}+\sigma=y

and let
f_{+}=(y+\sigma)/c\in[-\tau,\tau]
, so that
cf_{+}-\sigma=y
.
We obtain

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\max_{\pm}\frac{1}{2}\big|bf_{\pm}-\Delta(y)\big|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\big|bf_{-}-\Delta(y)\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big|bf_{+}-\Delta(y)\big|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8}\Big(\big|bf_{-}-\Delta(y)\big|+\big|bf_{+}-\Delta(y)\big|\Big)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{8}\big|b(f_{-}-f_{+})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2c^{2}}.

Case 2:
\sigma>|c|\tau
.

In view of
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{2}(\Delta)\leq{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{2}(\Delta)
again, we only need to establish that
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{2}(\Delta)=0
for an appropriately chosen recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
This map is defined by

\Delta(y)=\left\{\begin{matrix}\dfrac{b}{c}(y-\sigma)&\mbox{ if }y>0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{b}{c}(y+\sigma)&\mbox{ if }y<0.\end{matrix}\right.

Keeping in mind that

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{2}(\Delta)^{{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\big|bf-\Delta(cf+\sigma)\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{f\in[-\tau,\tau]}\big|bf-\Delta(cf-\sigma)\big|^{2},

we notice that, for any
f\in[-\tau,\tau]
, we have
cf+\sigma>0
and
cf-\sigma<0
,
so that
\Delta(cf+\sigma)=bf
and
\Delta(cf-\sigma)=bf
.
This immediately implies that
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{2}(\Delta)=0
.
∎

4
The Main Result

This section finalizes the full justification of this note’s message,
namely that “linear recovery maps are near-optimal” for the estimation of a linear functional with log-concave observation errors relatively to the unconventional global recovery error
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}
.
The result is formally stated below.

Theorem 10
.

Let
Q:F\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear functional.
If the model set
\mathcal{K}\subseteq F
is symmetric, convex, and closed and if
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a mean-zero log-concave random vector with invertible covariance matrix,
then there exists a linear map
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that,
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
,

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{p}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\leq{\kappa_{p}}\times\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}}{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{p}(\Delta)

for some constant
\kappa_{p}
depending only on
p
.

In what remains, we may and do assume that the invertible covariance matrix is of the form
\mathbb{E}\big[ee^{\top}\big]=\sigma^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{m}
.
Indeed, as a positive definite matrix,
it can be written as
\mathbb{E}\big[ee^{\top}\big]=MM^{\top}

for some invertible matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
.
Then we can convert the global errors of a recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,
given the observation map
\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
into the global errors of the recovery map
\widetilde{\Delta}=\Delta\circ M:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,
given the observation map
\widetilde{\Lambda}=M^{-1}\circ\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
by virtue of the identity
\Delta(\Lambda f+e)=\widetilde{\Delta}(\widetilde{\Lambda}f+\widetilde{e})
.
Here,
\widetilde{e}:=M^{-1}e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is still a mean-zero log-concave random vector (log-concavity is preserved under linear transformations) and, importantly,
its covariance matrix is
\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{e}\widetilde{e}^{\top}\big]={M^{-1}\mathbb{E}\big[ee^{\top}\big](M^{\top})^{-1}=M^{-1}(MM^{\top})(M^{\top})^{-1}}=\mathrm{Id}_{m}
.
Thus, the near-optimality result for the original problem reduces to the near-optimality result for the converted problem,
whose covariance matrix is (a multiple of) the identity.

For
p=1
, the result in the latter case is a consequence of an upper bound for the infimum of
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})
when
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is a linear map (Lemma
11
below)
and of a lower bound for
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)
when
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary map
(Lemma
12
below).
For
p\geq 1
, the result follows from Propositions
3
and
4
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
Q:F\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear functional.
If the model set
\mathcal{K}\subseteq F
is symmetric, convex, and closed and if
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a mean-zero random vector with covariance matrix
\mathbb{E}\big[ee^{\top}\big]=\sigma^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{m}
,
then there exists a linear map
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\leq\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|Q(h)|}{\max\{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}/\sigma,|h|_{\mathcal{K}}\}}.

Proof.

Given a linear map
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, according to (
7
), we have

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\big|(Q-\Delta_{\rm lin}\Lambda)f\big|+\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta_{\rm lin}e|\big].

In view of
\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{\rm lin}e|]\leq(\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{\rm lin}e|^{2}])^{1/2}

and writing
\Delta_{\rm lin}=\langle a,\cdot\rangle
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
we arrive at

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg|\bigg(Q-\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\lambda_{i}\bigg)f\bigg|+\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\langle a,e\rangle^{2}\big]\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg|\bigg(Q-\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\lambda_{i}\bigg)f\bigg|+\sigma\|a\|_{2}.

The minimum over
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of the latter coincides with the quantity (
9
) appearing in Proposition
6
with
\mathcal{E}=\{e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\|e\|_{2}\leq\sigma\}
,
i.e., with the minimal global worst-case error over
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{E}
.
As such, it also equals (
10
).
All in all, we have found a linear map
\Delta_{\rm lin}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta_{\rm lin})\leq\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|Q(h)|}{\max\{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}/\sigma,|h|_{\mathcal{K}}\}},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
Q:F\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear functional.
If the model set
\mathcal{K}\subseteq F
is symmetric, convex, and closed and if
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a mean-zero log-concave random vector with covariance matrix
\mathbb{E}\big[ee^{\top}\big]=\sigma^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{m}
,
then, for any recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)\geq\kappa_{1}\times\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|Q(h)|}{\max\{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}/\sigma,|h|_{\mathcal{K}}\}},

where the constant
\kappa_{1}
can be taken as
\kappa_{1}={1/(100\sqrt{3}{\mathrm{e}}^{4})}
.

Proof.

We start by recalling the expression

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\big|Q(f)-\Delta(\Lambda f+e)\big|\bigg].

We decompose
f\in F
as a (unnormalized) direction
h\in F\setminus\{0\}
and a magnitude
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
so that
f=th
.
We set aside the cases
\Lambda h=0
and
|h|_{\mathcal{K}}=0
for now.
Noticing the equivalence
f\in\mathcal{K}\Leftrightarrow|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}
,
we can write

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}\big|Q(h)t-\Delta((\Lambda h)t+e)\big|\bigg]

(13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}\Big|Q(h)t-\Delta\Big(\frac{\Lambda h}{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}}(\|\Lambda h\|_{2}t+\xi)+e_{\perp}\Big)\Big|\bigg],

after having decomposed
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
as
e=\xi\,\Lambda h/\|\Lambda h\|_{2}+e_{\perp}
,
where
\xi=\langle\Lambda h/\|\Lambda h\|_{2},e\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
is a mean-zero log-concave random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}

and
e_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a random vector orthogonal to
\Lambda h
.
From
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}(\cdot)]\geq\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\mathbb{E}[(\cdot)]
, we obtain

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)\geq\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}E_{h}
, where

E_{h}=\mathbb{E}_{e_{\perp}}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\bigg[\sup_{|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}\big|Q(h)t-\widetilde{\Delta}_{e_{\perp}}\big(\|\Lambda h\|_{2}t+\xi\big)\big|\,\Big|\,e_{\perp}\bigg]\bigg]

for some appropriately defined map
\widetilde{\Delta}_{e_{\perp}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Fixing
e_{\perp}
,
the inner expectation can be interpreted as the one-dimensional recovery error
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\widetilde{\Delta}_{e_{\perp}})
given in (
12
).
Thus, according to Lemma
8
, it can be lower-bounded as

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\widetilde{\Delta}_{e_{\perp}})\geq\alpha\,\frac{|Q(h)|}{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}}\min\Big\{\sigma,\frac{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}}{|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}\Big\}=\alpha\,\frac{|Q(h)|}{\max\{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}/\sigma,|h|_{\mathcal{K}}\}}.

This lower bound being independent of
e_{\perp}
,
it remains a lower bound for
E_{h}
itself.
We can therefore conclude that

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)\geq\alpha\,\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|Q(h)|}{\max\{\|\Lambda h\|_{2}/\sigma,|h|_{\mathcal{K}}\}}.

This is, in the generic case,
the desired inequality with
\kappa_{1}
equal to the constant
\alpha
from Lemma
8
.
It remains to deal with the set-aside cases.
Consider first the case
\Lambda h=0
, which enforces
|h|_{\mathcal{K}}>0
.
The identity (
13
) is then replaced by

{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}\big|Q(h)t-\Delta e\big|\bigg]\geq\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}|Q(h)||t|=\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|Q(h)|}{|h|_{\mathcal{K}}},

where the above inequality used the fact that,
whenever
h\in F\setminus\{0\}
and
|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}
,
the double-supremum is at least
\max_{\pm}|Q(\pm h)t-\Delta e|=|Q(h)||t|+|\Delta e|\geq|Q(h)||t|
.
Thus, the desired inequality is even valid with
\kappa_{1}=1
in this situation.
Consider next the case
|h|_{\mathcal{K}}=0
,
which implies that
f=th\in\mathcal{K}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
also enforces
\Lambda h\not=0
.
Then the lower bound
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)\geq\sup_{h\in F\setminus\{0\}}E_{h}
still holds with any
\tau>0
replacing
1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}
,
and in particular with
\tau>0
large enough so that
\min\{\sigma,\|\Lambda h\|_{2}\,\tau\}=\sigma
.
Thus, resorting to Lemma
8
yields
E_{h}\geq\alpha\,(|Q(h)|/\|\Lambda h\|_{2})\sigma
,
which reduces to the desired inequality with
\kappa_{1}=\alpha
in this situation.
∎

Remark
.

As already mentioned,
the previous argument is easily adapted to retrieve the result of
[
3
]

for Gaussian observation errors,
which essentially boils down to establishing the above lower bound for
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)
instead of
{\rm ge}^{\rm or}_{1}(\Delta)
.
We would first express
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)
as in (
13
)
but with expectation and suprema interchanged.
Then, the benefit of the Gaussian case lies in the independence of
\xi
and
e_{\perp}
,
so that we can write

\displaystyle{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{h\in F}\sup_{|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{e_{\perp}}\bigg[\big|Q(h)t-\widetilde{\Delta}_{e_{\perp}}\big(\|\Lambda h\|_{2}t+\xi\big)\big|\bigg]\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{h\in F}\sup_{|t|\leq 1/|h|_{\mathcal{K}}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\bigg[\Big|Q(h)t-\mathbb{E}_{e_{\perp}}\Big[\widetilde{\Delta}_{e_{\perp}}\big(\|\Lambda h\|_{2}t+\xi\big)\Big]\Big|\bigg]

and invoke the one-dimensional lower bound on
{\rm ge}^{\rm se}_{1}(\widehat{\Delta})
from Lemma
8
for the map
\widehat{\Delta}=\mathbb{E}_{e_{\perp}}\big[\widetilde{\Delta}_{e_{\perp}}(\cdot)\big]

Remark
.

In closing, we point out that our arguments do not just translate into an existence result.
Indeed, the proof of Lemma
11
reveals that a near-optimal recovery map is provided by a recovery map which is genuinely optimal, albeit with respect to observation errors modeled deterministically via
\mathcal{E}=\{e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\|e\|_{2}\leq\sigma\}
.
The latter has the form of a linear functional
\langle a^{\sharp},\cdot\rangle
,
where
a^{\sharp}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a minimizer of the convex program (
9
).
This program is solvable in many practical situations, including,
as described in
[
4
]
,
the approximability model sets defined for some finite-dimensional linear subspace
\mathcal{V}
of
F
and some parameter
\varepsilon>0
by

\mathcal{K}=\{f\in F:{\rm dist}_{F}(f,\mathcal{V})\leq\varepsilon\}.

We remark that this model set is symmetric, convex, and closed, but not bounded.

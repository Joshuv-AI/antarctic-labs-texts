Uniqueness when the L p L_{p} curvature is close to be a constant for p ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) p\in[0,1)
Károly J. Böröczky, Christos Saroglou

Abstract
For fixed positive integer n n , p ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) p\in[0,1) , a ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) a\in(0,1) , we prove that if a function g : 𝕊 n − 1 → ℝ g:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R} is sufficiently close to 1, in the C a C^{a} sense, then there exists a unique convex body K K whose L p L_{p} curvature function equals g g . This was previously established for n = 3 n=3 , p = 0 p=0 by Chen, Feng, Liu [ 10 ] and in the symmetric case by Chen, Huang, Li, Liu [ 12 ] . Related, we show that if p = 0 p=0 and n = 4 n=4 or n ≤ 3 n\leq 3 and p ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) p\in[0,1) , and the L p L_{p} curvature function g g of a (sufficiently regular, containing the origin) convex body K K satisfies λ − 1 ≤ g ≤ λ \lambda^{-1}\leq g\leq\lambda , for some λ > 1 \lambda>1 , then max x ∈ 𝕊 n − 1 ⁡ h K ​ ( x ) ≤ C ⁡ ( p , λ ) \max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{K}(x)\leq C(p,\lambda) , for some constant C ⁡ ( p , λ ) > 0 C(p,\lambda)>0 that depends only on p p and λ \lambda . This also extends a result from Chen, Feng, Liu [ 10 ] . Along the way, we obtain a result, that might be of independent interest, concerning the question of when the support of the L p L_{p} surface area measure is lower dimensional. Finally, we establish a strong non-uniqueness result for the L p L_{p} -Minkowksi problem, for − n < p < 0 -n<p<0 .

, and the surface area measure

, and hence

tends weakly to

. We deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\det\Phi_{m}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(x)\|\Phi_{m}^{-1}x\|^{-n-p}d\,{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(x)h_{K^{\prime}_{m}}^{1-p}(x)f_{K^{\prime}_{m}}(x)\,d{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(x)

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(x)\,dS_{p,SK_{\infty}}(x).

Let
O
be any orthogonal map that fixes the elements of
H^{\perp}
. Then, according to (
35
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi\circ O^{t})(x)\,dS_{p,SK_{\infty}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\det\Phi_{m}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(O^{t}x)\|\Phi_{m}^{-1}x\|^{-n-p}\,d{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\det\Phi_{m}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(y)\|\Phi_{m}^{-1}Oy\|^{-n-p}\,d{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\det\Phi_{m}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(y)\|\Phi_{m}^{-1}y\|^{-n-p}\,d{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(x)\,dS_{p,SK_{\infty}}(x).

Consequently,

, for any such

and for any Borel set

in
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Therefore, since

is concentrated in

to the class of Borel subsets of

, and hence it is a multiple of the spherical Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap H
. However (recall that

), by
[

, this is a contradiction, completing the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.10

Proof of Theorem

.

Consider the map

Then,

at

Notice that

. Thus, by the inverse function theorem for Banach spaces, there exists an

of the constant function 1, such that for

implies
u_{1}=u_{2}
.
In particular, there exists a
C^{2}
-neighbourhood
N
of 1, such that if
h_{K},h_{L}\in N
, and
S_{p,K}=S_{p,L}
, then
K=L
. Thus, it suffices to prove that there exists
\varepsilon>0
, such that if
\|g-1\|_{C^{a}}<\varepsilon
and
dS_{p,K}=gdx
, then
h_{K}\in N
.

Assume not. Then, there exists a sequence
\{K_{m}\}
of convex bodies, such that

\|h_{K_{m}}^{1-p}f_{K_{m}}-1\|_{C^{a}}\to 0,\qquad\textnormal{as }m\to\infty,

(36)

but
h_{K_{m}}\not\in N
, for all

. The latter can be written as

\|h_{K_{m}}-1\|_{C^{2}}>\eta_{0},

(37)

for some fixed
\eta_{0}>0

It follows from Proposition
3.1
that
\{K_{m}\}
is a bounded sequence.

\{K_{m}\}
converges to some compact convex set

. Notice that

Notice that

converges weakly to

Proposition 4.2
.

If for some

and for some

is negative, then, there exists

, with

to the class of Borel subsets of

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap H

Before proving

4.2

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\mu
is a finite non-trivial Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that there exists a constant
A>0
, with the property that

\mu(V_{1})\leq A\mu({\rm cl}\,V_{2})

for any two open spherical caps
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
of the same diameter, then,
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
{{\cal H}^{n-1}}
such that its density function can be chosen to be bounded and bounded away from zero.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
\varepsilon>0
, set
U(x,\varepsilon):=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\|x-y\|<\varepsilon\}
. It is well known that there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
, that depends only on
n
(actually
C_{0}=2^{n-1}
works), such that

{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(U(x,2\varepsilon))\leq C_{0}{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(x,\varepsilon).

(39)

For
\varepsilon>0
, fix a maximal set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, with the
property that the spherical caps
U(x_{1},2\varepsilon),\dots,U(x_{N},2\varepsilon)
are disjoint, and hence

\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}U(x_{j},4\varepsilon)=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Set
V_{j}:=U(x_{j},\varepsilon)
,
j=1,\dots,N
. Then,
{\rm cl}\,V_{1},\dots,{\rm cl}\,V_{N}
are disjoint, and we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu({\rm cl}\,V_{j})\leq\mu(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(U(x_{j},4\varepsilon))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}^{2}\frac{\mu(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(V_{j})=B\sum_{j=1}^{N}{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(V_{j})

for
B=C_{0}^{2}\frac{\mu(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}
.
Therefore, there exists
t\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, such that

\mu({\rm cl}\,V_{t})\leq B{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(V_{t}).

It follows by our assumption that for any open spherical cap
V
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, of radius
\varepsilon
, it holds

\mu(V)\leq A\mu({\rm cl}\,V_{t})\leq A\cdot B\,{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(V).

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, it follows from
Vitali’s Covering Lemma, the regularity of Hausdorff measure and the doubling property (
39
)
that
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
{{\cal H}^{n-1}}
having bounded density.

Next, we suppose that the essential infimum of the density of
\mu
is zero, and seek a contradiction. Then, for each
\eta>0
, there exists a point
x_{\eta}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, such that the limit
\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\mu(U(x_{\eta},\delta)/{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(U(x_{\eta},\delta))
exists and is less than
\eta/(2A)
. It follows that if
\delta
is sufficiently small, then

\mu(U(x_{\eta},\delta)<\frac{\eta}{A}\cdot{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(U(x_{\eta},\delta)).

Consequently, using again the assumption of Lemma
4.3
and the absolute continuity of
\mu
(established previously), we conclude that for any
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, it holds

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(U(y,\delta))}{{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(U(y,\delta))}<\eta.

Since
\eta
is arbitrary, we arrive at

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(U(y,\delta))}{{{\cal H}^{n-1}}(U(y,\delta))}=0,\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

As
\mu
is absolute continuous, we deduce that
\mu\equiv 0
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof of the Lemma
4.3
.
∎

5
Severe non-uniqueness for

and the proof of Theorem

For the proof of Theorem

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
L\in{\cal K}_{o}^{n}
, such that
o\in{\rm int}\,L
and
p<0
. Then, for
x\in L
,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L-x}^{p}(u)\,dS_{p,L}(u)=\inf_{y\in L}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L-y}^{p}(u)\,dS_{p,L}(u),

Since
h_{L-x}(y)=h_{L}(y)-\langle y,x\rangle
, the function

F:L\ni x\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L-x}^{p}(u)\,dS_{p,L}(u)

, we have

\langle\nabla F(x),z\rangle=-p\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle z,u\rangle h_{L-x}(u)^{p-1}h_{L}(u)^{1-p}\,dS_{L}(u),

and hence
\nabla F(o)=o
follows from the fact that the barycentre of
S_{L}
is at the origin. We deduce from the strict convexity of
F
that
x=o
is the unique minimum point of
F
.

1.6
.

Define the functional

J_{p,\mu}(M):=\inf_{x\in M}\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{M-x}^{p}(u)\,d\mu(u),\qquad M\in{\cal K}_{o}^{n}.

If
{\cal K}={\cal K}_{s}^{n}
and

is even, it follows easily from Lemma

If, in addition,

It is known (see

and

in

can be chosen so that

S_{p,M_{0}}=\mu.

(42)

Notice that if

(43)

This is trivial to verify, if
{\cal K}={\cal K}_{s}^{n}
and follows immediately from Lemma

Claim 1.
Let
K\in{\cal K}_{+}
, with

and

, it holds

then

We show first that if (

Setting

\displaystyle V(K)^{(n-p)/n}V(L^{\prime})^{p/n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V(K)^{(n-p)/n}V(L)^{p/n}<J_{p}(K,L)=J_{p}(K,L^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L^{\prime}}^{p}(u)\,dS_{p,K}(u)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{h_{K}(u)}{h_{L^{\prime}}(u)}\right)^{-p}h_{K}(u)\,dS_{K}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{h_{K}(u)}{h_{L^{\prime}}(u)}\right)^{-q}h_{K}(u)\,dS_{K}\right)^{p/q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{K}(u)\,dS_{K}\right)^{(q-p)/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{q}(K,L^{\prime})^{p/q}V(K)^{(q-p)/q}.

Readily, this gives (
45
) and our claim is proved.
∎

To finish with the proof of Theorem

, for

It follows by Claim 1 that it suffices to prove that the set of convex bodies

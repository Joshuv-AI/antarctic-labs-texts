Deformations of symplectic groupoids
Cristian Camilo Cárdenas, João Nuno Mestre, Ivan Struchiner

Abstract
. We describe the deformation cohomology of a symplectic groupoid, and use it to study deformations via Moser path methods, proving a symplectic groupoid version of the Moser Theorem. Our construction uses the deformation cohomologies of Lie groupoids and of multiplicative forms, and we prove that in the symplectic case, deformation cohomology of both the underlying groupoid and of the symplectic groupoid have de Rham models in terms of differential forms. We use the de Rham model, which is intimately connected to the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff double complex, to compute deformation cohomology in several examples. We compute it for proper symplectic groupoids using vanishing results; alternatively, for groupoids satisfying homological 2-connectedness conditions we compute it using a simple spectral sequence. Finally, without making assumptions on the topology, we constructed a map relating differentiable and deformation cohomology of the underlying Lie groupoid of a symplectic groupoid, and related it to its Lie algebroid counterpart via van Est maps.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study deformations of a symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, constructing the deformation cohomology
H_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
controlling them. Any deformation of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
gives rise to a deformation class
[\eta]
in the cohomology, which can be used in Moser path methods. We carry out computations for the cohomology in examples, comparing it with cohomologies associated to related objects.

Symplectic groupoids were introduced by Karasëv
[
47
]
, Weinstein
[
72
]
and Zakrzewski
[
75
]
, motivated by the search for a quantization procedure for Poisson structures. They are first of all Lie groupoids, objects introduced by Ehresmann in the 1950’s
[
36
]
, which generalize Lie groups and which, among other uses, permit the unified study of differential geometric objects such as foliations or Lie group actions. A Lie groupoid equipped with a compatible, i.e.,
multiplicative
symplectic structure, becomes well adapted to the study of Poisson structures. Poisson structures are Lie algebra structures on the algebra of smooth functions on a manifold, which in addition are biderivations of the associative algebra structure. Although these Lie algebras are infinite dimensional, they are on the infinitesimal side of a rich Lie theory, with symplectic groupoids (which are finite dimensional!) playing the part of the corresponding global object.

In analogy with the relation between Lie groups and Lie algebras, any symplectic groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows M
induces a Poisson structure
\pi
on its space of units
M
, such that the Lie algebroid of
{\mathcal{G}}
is naturally isomorphic to the cotangent algebroid
(T^{*}M)_{\pi}
associated to the Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
. Not all Poisson structures arise in this way, but for the ones which do, called
integrable
, it was shown in in
[
62
]
that there is always a natural symplectic groupoid structure on the source
1
-connected integration
{\mathcal{G}}
of
(T^{*}M)_{\pi}
. The symplectic groupoid structure on
{\mathcal{G}}
was also obtained by an infinite dimensional reduction procedure in
[
15
]
, and the characterization of which Poisson manifolds are integrable was finally settled in
[
20
]
.

The survey
[
73
]
gives an account of Poisson geometry at the early stages of development of symplectic groupoids, and already then they feature prominently in the results and the problems presented, some of them now settled, some still open.

First and foremost (from our specific viewpoint) symplectic groupoids are useful in the study of the Poisson geometry of the base, both in its local and global properties.
But we should mention that symplectic groupoids have also feature in a variety of other treatments, for example in geometric quantization
[
43
]
, deformation quantization (cf. e.g.
[
9
,
15
]
), the study of the geometry of varieties appearing in Poisson-Lie theory (for example
[
53
]
) and of moduli spaces of flat connections (e.g.
[
51
,
71
]
).

Particulary motivating for this work, was the role played by symplectic groupoids, both in the study of local and of global Poisson geometry, via Moser path arguments, also known as “the Moser trick”. We mention how it came to influence our work. Starting with the famous Moser Theorem, the Moser trick has been used to prove many rigidity and normal form results in symplectic geometry, and related topics (see e.g.
[
11
]
), by reducing deformation problems to cohomological conditions. It was first used in the context of Lie groupoids, to our knowledge, by Weinstein
[
74
]
, in the pursuit of linearization theorems for proper Lie groupoids. Next, in the geometric proof of Conn’s Theorem of
[
21
]
, a
Poisson
version of the Moser Theorem was used, together with symplectic groupoids that solved the cohomological conditions. Further, similar techniques were used around leaves of a Poisson manifold
[
27
]
. These techniques eventually led to the geometric proof of the linearization of proper groupoids around orbits
[
29
]
, and to the development of the deformation cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})
of an arbitrary Lie groupoid in
[
25
]
.

Recent developments related to deformation theory for Lie groupoids include a rigidity result for foliations
[
32
]
, the study of deformations of vector bundles over Lie groupoids
[
49
]
, and of morphisms of Lie groupoids
[
12
]
. Specific classes of deformations of Lie groupoids (deformations to the normal cone) are also of importance in noncommutative geometry (see e.g.
[
30
,
31
]
).

Our work brings this new understanding on deformations of Lie groupoids full circle back to Poisson geometry, as exemplified by a symplectic groupoid version of the classical Moser Theorem (detailed further in Sections 7 and 8).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
be a deformation of a compact symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathcal{G}})
and let
[\eta]
be the deformation class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
associated to it. Then, the deformation
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
is trivial if and only if
[\eta]
is exact.

This result also leads to local descriptions of moduli spaces of multiplicative symplectic forms on a compact Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
, given by an analogue of a Kodaira-Spencer map (See Theorem
7.8
for a more precise and detailed version).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
be a compact symplectic groupoid, and denote by
\mathcal{S}({{\mathcal{G}}})
the space of multiplicative symplectic structures on
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Then there is a neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of

in
\mathcal{S}({{\mathcal{G}}})
and a neighbourhood

of

\kappa:\ \mathcal{U}\big/\sim\ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{1:1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \mathcal{V}\subset H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega),

sending the equivalence class of

to

. The equivalence relation is such that
\omega_{1}\sim\omega_{2}
if
\omega_{1}+{\varepsilon}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})
is a trivial family of multiplicative forms for all

.

The key insight in order to construct the deformation cohomology

, where the cohomological equations of the Moser Theorem naturally live, was that the pair of a Lie groupoid and an extra structure on it is more manageable when interpreted as a diagram. Then the new tools on deformations of morphisms of Lie groupoids from
[
12
]
could be used.

Moreover, in the specific case where the extra structure is symplectic, there is a description in terms of differential forms on the nerve

of
{\mathcal{G}}
, of

, and of

of the symplectic groupoid

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
be a symplectic groupoid. Then
\omega^{b}
induces a quasi-isomorphism

C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\sim\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)}).

The deformation complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)

is isomorphic to the mapping cone of the de Rham differential

-d_{dR}:\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})\to\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)}).

In order to effectively use a symplectic groupoid to study the Poisson structure on the base, it is desirable to have a good description of the symplectic groupoid, or at least to know some of its properties. There are conditions to characterize whether a Poisson manifold is integrable
[
20
]
and if it is, there is an infinite-dimensional reduction procedure for the construction of the source
1
-connected integration
[
15
]
. Nonetheless, constructing a concrete integration is often still challenging.

Fortunately, symplectic groupoids for several classes of Poisson manifolds have been described, usually immediately bringing about new insight about the Poisson structures they integrate; we mention a few of them. Some of the simplest cases are symplectic manifolds, the zero Poisson structure and linear Poisson structures on vector spaces and vector bundles
[
17
]
. Even for the zero Poisson structure, symplectic groupoids may provide rich geometry in the form of integral affine structures, as shown recently in
[
23
,
24
]
. Integrations for any Poisson-Lie group are described in
[
52
,
54
]
, and these and other symplectic groupoids also have interpretations in terms of moduli spaces of flat connections, for example in
[
51
,
71
]
. Integrations of Poisson fibrations are studied in
[
4
]
; integrations of Log-symplectic manifolds are studied using blow-up and gluing techniques in
[
42
]
; a description of the integration of neighborhoods of Poisson transversal submanifolds is given in
[
38
]
; more examples appear in relation to the theory of Poisson-Lie groups - for example, symplectic leaves of double Bruhat cells are symplectic groupoids studied in
[
53
]
; and this list is far from complete.

We carried out computations for the deformation cohomology in a few of these examples. Most computable examples
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
are typically of one of two kinds: either we assumed some sort of compactness condition, or vanishing of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}({\mathcal{G}})
and
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}({\mathcal{G}})
. Clearly we cannot ask for both vanishing
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}({\mathcal{G}})
and compactness, because we deal with symplectic groupoids. In fact, even examples of compact symplectic groupoids with the more reasonable condition of having simply connected source fibres are very hard to find
[
24
,
63
,
77
]
.

Asking for the symplectic groupoid to be proper (a compactness-type condition), we obtained the following result (See Section 6 for more a detailed version of it), and applied it for linear Poisson and zero Poisson structures of proper type.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
be a proper symplectic groupoid, and let
\nu
denote the normal distribution to the symplectic leaves. Then there is a 7-term exact sequence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to\Gamma(\nu^{*})^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\to

\displaystyle\to\Gamma(\nu)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\omega)\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\to 0

and
H^{k}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\omega)
for
k>2
.

In particular, expressed in terms of the de Rham model,

H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\omega)/d(H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})).

With the help of some simple spectral sequence arguments we also provide descriptions of the deformation cohomology of an arbitrary symplectic groupoid. Those descriptions, however, become much simpler in the case of proper symplectic groupoids, as detailed in Sections
6.5
and
6.6
.

If on the other hand we ask for moderately strong topological conditions - vanishing of first and second de Rham cohomologies, we see a very close relation between deformation cohomology of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
and Poisson cohomology
H_{\pi}(M)
of the base. Poisson cohomology of
(M,\pi)
is computed by the complex

of multivector fields on

, with differential

, i.e., given by the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket with

, and in general it is hard to compute. We refer to
[
66
]
for an account of techniques that can be used to compute it in different classes of Poisson manifolds, as well as for the very recent developments in
loc. cit.

Call a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
homologically
2
-connected if all the spaces
{\mathcal{G}}^{(n)}
of its nerve, as well as its source-fibres, are homologically
2
-connected.
In this situation we obtained the following result (Section 9 contains precise and improved versions).

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a homologically
2
-connected symplectic groupoid.
Then

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{0}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong H^{0}_{\pi}(M)/\mathbb{R},\ \ \ H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong H^{1}_{\pi}(M)\ \ \ H_{\mathrm{def}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong H^{2}_{\pi}(M),

and there is an injective map

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{3}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\to H^{3}_{\pi}(M).

Although the conditions do sound restrictive, we focus in Section 9 on some situations in which they are satisfied, for example in the context of linear Poisson structures, Poisson-Lie groups, and cotangent VB-groupoids.

We would like to make some final remarks.
We believe that the description of the deformation complex of a symplectic groupoid is important by itself, because this work should serve as prototype. It opens the road to similar studies for other structures on groupoids, which should shed light on deformation theoretic properties of objects described by them. We expect that similar techniques can be used, but revealing different phenomena, for multiplicative contact, Poisson, complex structures, foliations, etc.

The theory developed in this paper should have a version for local and formal symplectic groupoids, likely using the version of the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff for local Lie groupoids
[
50
]
. In this direction, it would be interesting to study the classes of deformations of formal symplectic groupoids
[
14
]
induced by star products arising from quantization, or pairs of star products, as in the works of
[
9
]
and
[
45
,
46
]
. We expect that local and formal versions automatically satisfy many of the topological assumptions we used in computations, bringing the theory closer to the deformations of the underlying Poisson structures.

Finally, all the work developed in this paper is intimately connected with the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff double complex
[
2
]
, therefore lending itself to generalizations towards twisted presymplectic, and higher (shifted) (pre)symplectic groupoids.

Outline of the paper

In Section 2 we recall needed background material about Lie groupoid cohomology and deformations, VB-groupoids, multiplicative forms and symplectic groupoids, and Poisson manifolds.

In Section 3 we introduce deformations and equivalence of deformations of symplectic groupoids, and provide examples of trivial and non-trivial deformations.

In Section 4 we explain how the symplectic form of a symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
permits a description of deformation cohomology of
{\mathcal{G}}
in terms of differential forms.

In Section 5 we come to the central object of this paper, the deformation cohomology
H_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
of a symplectic groupoid. We explain how to construct it out of the deformation cohomologies of the
{\mathcal{G}}
and of
\omega
. We also prove that there is a de Rham model for it, in which cochains are differential forms, used for the computations in the rest of the paper.

In Section 6 we carry out the first computations of deformation cohomology, for symplectic manifolds, and proper symplectic groupoids; we then specialize to zero Poisson structures of proper type and linear Poisson structures of proper type. Using simple spectral sequence arguments we present some explicit computations for general symplectic groupoids.

In sections 7 and 8 we use the deformation cohomology to prove symplectic groupoid versions of the Moser theorem, first for specific (source-constant), and then for general deformations. We also study consequences moduli spaces of multiplicative symplectic forms.

In Section 9 we use another spectral sequence to carry out more computations of deformation cohomology, this time for symplectic groupoids for which the nerve has vanishing first and second de Rham cohomology. The relation with Poisson cohomology is very close in these cases; we include computations for linear Poisson structures, the zero Poisson structure, Poisson-Lie groups, and cotangent groupoids.

in Section 10 we study, now without topological assumptions on
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, relations between the differentiable and deformation cohomology of
{\mathcal{G}}
, the Poisson cohomology of the base, and the deformation cohomology of the corresponding algebroid. We do so by providing a map
i_{\mathcal{G}}:H^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}})\to H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})
and proving that it is related by van Est maps to the map
i:H^{\bullet}_{\pi}(M)\to H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(A)
of
[
26
]
.

Finally, we collect in an appendix the needed material on double structures, used to prove the Theorem of Section 10.

2.1.
Deformations of Lie groupoids

More details on the notions in this section can be found in
[
25
]
, where they are discussed in detail.

Definition 2.1
.

A
family of Lie groupoids
over a manifold
B
consists of a Lie groupoid
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}{\rightrightarrows}\tilde{M}
, together with a submersion
\textbf{p}:\tilde{M}\to B
, such that
\textbf{p}\circ\tilde{s}=\textbf{p}\circ\tilde{t}
.

In other words, a family of Lie groupoids over
B
consists of a submersive Lie groupoid map
\textbf{p}:(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}{\rightrightarrows}\tilde{M})\to(B{\rightrightarrows}B)
, from
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
to the unit groupoid of
B
. We denote by
{\mathcal{G}}_{b}{\rightrightarrows}M_{b}
the fibre of
p
over
b\in B
, i.e.
(\textbf{p}\circ\tilde{s})^{-1}(b){\rightrightarrows}\textbf{p}^{-1}(b)
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
equivalence between families

\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\prime}
over
B
is given by a Lie groupoid isomorphism
F:\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}\to\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}}
such that
\textbf{p}^{\prime}\circ F=\textbf{p}
.
A family is said to be
trivial
if it is equivalent to the product family given by

(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}{\rightrightarrows}\tilde{M})\times(B{\rightrightarrows}B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textbf{p}=pr_{B}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(B{\rightrightarrows}B).

Definition 2.3
.

A
deformation of a Lie groupoid

{\mathcal{G}}
is a family of Lie groupoids
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}{\rightrightarrows}\tilde{M}
over an open interval
I
containing zero, such that
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
is equal to
{\mathcal{G}}
.

An
equivalence between deformations

\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
is given by an equivalence of families
F:\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{|_{J}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{|_{J}}^{\prime}
such that
F_{0}:=F_{|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}}}=id_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}}
, where
J\subset I\cap I^{\prime}
is an open interval containing zero, and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{|_{J}}
denotes the restriction of the family
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
to the interval
J
.

Definition 2.4
.

A deformation
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
for which
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}={\mathcal{G}}\times I
as smooth manifolds is called a
strict
deformation.

It is called an
s
-constant
deformation if additionally the source map is left constant,
i.e.
,
s_{\varepsilon}=s_{0}
for all
{\varepsilon}\in I
.

Remark 2.5
.

A deformation of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
determines a collection
\{{\mathcal{G}}_{\epsilon}\}
of Lie groupoids parametrized by
\epsilon\in I
(the fibres of the family), varying smoothly with respect to
\epsilon
in the sense that they fit as fibres of a submersion. If the deformation is strict then the
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
are equal as manifolds, and only the structural maps change.

Accordingly, we often use denote a deformation of
{\mathcal{G}}
simply by
\{{\mathcal{G}}_{\epsilon}\}
, keeping in mind that there is a specified family that the members of this family fit into.

Similarly, we often denote an equivalence
F:\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\prime}
of deformations by the associated family of maps
F_{\epsilon}:{\mathcal{G}}_{\epsilon}\to{\mathcal{G}}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
.

2.2.
The nerve of a Lie groupoid and differentiable cohomology

Let
{\mathcal{G}}{\rightrightarrows}M
be a Lie groupoid and denote its space of strings of
k
composable arrows by

{\mathcal{G}}^{(k)}=\{(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}):s(g_{i})=t(g_{i+1})\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq k-1\},

and let
{\mathcal{G}}^{(0)}=M
.
The nerve of
{\mathcal{G}}
is the simplicial manifold
{\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)}
for which the space of
k
-simplices is
{\mathcal{G}}^{(k)}
, the face maps are
d_{i}:{\mathcal{G}}^{(n)}\to{\mathcal{G}}^{(n-1)}
given by

d_{i}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=\begin{cases}(g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})&\text{ if }i=0\\
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n})&\text{ if }1\leq i\leq n-1\\
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1})&\text{ if }i=n,\end{cases}

and degeneracies
s_{i}:{\mathcal{G}}^{(n)}\to{\mathcal{G}}^{(n+1)}
are given by inserting the appropriate unit in the
i
-th position,
s_{i}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1},1_{,}g_{i},\ldots,g_{n})
, for
1\leq i\leq n+1
.

Smooth functions on the nerve form a cosimplicial space, out of which we can construct the cochain complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{diff}}({\mathcal{G}})
(also denoted just by
C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}})
) computing the
differentiable cohomology
of
{\mathcal{G}}
, denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{diff}}({\mathcal{G}})
. Explicitly, its spaces of cochains are
C^{k}_{\mathrm{diff}}({\mathcal{G}})=C^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k)})
, and the differentials are given by

\delta=\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{i}d_{i}^{*}:C^{k}_{\mathrm{diff}}({\mathcal{G}})\to C^{k+1}_{\mathrm{diff}}({\mathcal{G}}).

The subcomplex of
normalized
cochains consists of those cochains
c\in C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{diff}}({\mathcal{G}})
which vanish on all degeneracies, i.e.,
s_{i}^{*}c=0
for all
i
; it is quasi-isomorphic to
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{diff}}({\mathcal{G}})
.

More generally,
k
-forms on the nerve of
{\mathcal{G}}
together with a differential
\delta
defined by the same formula form the cochain complex
\Omega^{k}({\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet})
. These complexes form the lines of the
Bott-Shulman-Stasheff double complex

\left(\Omega^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)}),\delta,d\right)

[
2
]
; the horizontal differentials
\delta:\Omega^{q}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})\longrightarrow\Omega^{q}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet+1)})
are determined by the simplicial structure of the nerve of
{\mathcal{G}}
, as described above, and vertical differentials
d:\Omega^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}^{(p)})\longrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet+1}({\mathcal{G}}^{(p)})
are given by the de Rham differential of forms.

2.3.
The deformation complex of a Lie groupoid

We recall the definition from
[
25
]
of the deformation complex of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
.
To that end, let
\bar{m}
denote the division map of
{\mathcal{G}}
, defined as

\bar{m}(p,q)=pq^{-1},\text{ for all }p,q\in{\mathcal{G}}\ \text{ such that }\ s(p)=s(q).

Definition 2.6
.

The deformation complex
(C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}),\delta)
of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
, whose cohomology is denoted
H_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}})
, is defined as follows. For
k\geq 1
, the
k
-cochains
c\in C_{\mathrm{def}}^{k}(\mathcal{G})
are the smooth maps

c:{\mathcal{G}}^{(k)}\longrightarrow T{\mathcal{G}},\ \ (g_{1},\ldots,g_{k})\mapsto c(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k})\in T_{g_{1}}{\mathcal{G}},

which are
s
-projectable in the sense that

ds\circ c(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k})

does not depend on
g_{1}
.

The differential of
c\in C_{\mathrm{def}}^{k}(\mathcal{G})
is defined by

\displaystyle(\delta c)(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k+1})=

\displaystyle-d\bar{m}(c(g_{1}g_{2},\ldots,g_{k+1}),c(g_{2},\ldots,g_{k+1}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(-1)^{i}c(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{k+1})+(-1)^{k+1}c(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}).

For
k=0
,
C_{\mathrm{def}}^{0}(\mathcal{G}):=\Gamma(A)
and the differential of
\alpha\in\Gamma(A)
is defined by

\delta(\alpha)=\overrightarrow{\alpha}+\overleftarrow{\alpha}\in C_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\mathcal{G}),

where
\overrightarrow{\alpha}
and
\overleftarrow{\alpha}
are the right-invariant and the left-invariant vector fields on
{\mathcal{G}}
induced by
\alpha
.

Note that, to interpret the case
k=0
, one way to think of a section of

is as a map
c:{\mathcal{G}}^{(0)}=M\longrightarrow T{\mathcal{G}}
, with
c(1_{x})=c_{x}\in T_{1_{x}}{\mathcal{G}}
, such that
ds\circ c_{x}=0_{x}
.

2.4.
The deformation class of a deformation of Lie groupoids

We recall the definition from
[
25
]
of the deformation class of a deformation
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}{\rightrightarrows}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textbf{p}}}{{\to}}I
of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
.

If
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
is an
s
-constant deformation, then the expression

(1)

\xi(g,h):=\left.\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\right|_{{\varepsilon}=0}\bar{m}_{\varepsilon}(gh,h)

defines a
2
-cochain in deformation cohomology. It is a cocycle and its cohomology class only depends on the equivalence class of the deformation
[
25
, Lemma 5.3]
. Moreover, If we let
\tilde{\xi}=-\delta(\frac{\partial}{\partial{\varepsilon}})\in C_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}})
, then
\xi=\tilde{\xi}_{|{\mathcal{G}}_{0}}

[
25
, Prop. 5.7]
.

The expression (
1
) no longer makes sense if the deformation
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
is not
s
-constant, but defining
\xi
in terms of
\tilde{\xi}
is still possible. For that, we need an appropriate analogue for the vector field
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\varepsilon}}
on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}{\rightrightarrows}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textbf{p}}}{{\to}}I
be a deformation of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
. A transverse vector field for
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
is a vector field
\tilde{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}})
which is
s
-projectable to a vector field
\tilde{V}\in\mathfrak{X}(\tilde{M})
, which is in turn
p
-projectable to
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\varepsilon}}\in\mathfrak{X}(I)
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
25
]
, Prop. 5.12)
.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
be a deformation of
{\mathcal{G}}
. Then

(1)

There exist transverse vector fields for
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
;

(2)

For
\tilde{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}})
transverse, the restriction of
\delta\tilde{X}
to
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
defines a cocycle
\xi_{0}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})
;

(3)

The cohomology class of

does not depend on the choice of
\tilde{X}
.

Definition 2.9
.

The resulting cohomology class
[\xi_{0}]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})
is called the deformation class associated to the deformation
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
of

.

Again, the deformation class is invariant under equivalence of deformations, and as remarked in
[

Note that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
can also be seen as a deformation of its other fibres

[\xi_{\varepsilon}]=[\delta\tilde{X}_{|{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}]
.

2.5.
Deformations of Lie groupoid morphisms and diagrams

We recall some background on deformations of Lie groupoid morphisms, which are studied in detail in
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
F:{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{H}
be a morphism of Lie groupoids. A
deformation of the morphism
F
is a smooth map
\tilde{F}:{\mathcal{G}}\times I\to\mathcal{H}
such that
\tilde{F}(\cdot,0)=F
and for each
\epsilon\in I
, the map
F_{\epsilon}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is a morphism.

More generally, and this will be useful in our study, one can at once deform the whole diagram
F:{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{H}
, which for simplicity we denote by
F
as well.

Definition 2.11
.

A
deformation of the diagram
F
consists of a triple
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\tilde{F},\tilde{\mathcal{H}})
, where
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
are deformations of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathcal{H}
, and the map
\tilde{F}:\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is a Lie groupoid morphism sending
{\mathcal{G}}_{\epsilon}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
, such that
F_{0}:=\tilde{F}_{|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}}}=F
.

Note that the requirement that
\tilde{F}
is a morphism implies in particular that each
F_{\epsilon}:=\tilde{F}_{|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\epsilon}}}:{\mathcal{G}}_{\epsilon}\to\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
is a Lie groupoid morphism as well.

For the purposes of this paper we will be interested in simultaneous deformations of the Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
and the morphism
F
, or in other words, deformations of the diagram
F
where
\mathcal{H}
is kept fixed.

2.6.
The deformation complex of a Lie groupoid morphism

The deformation complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(F)
of a Lie groupoid morphism
F:{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{H}
was briefly described in
[
25
]
and further explored in
[
12
]
. It has the following description, similar to that of
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})
.
For
k\geq 1
,

C_{\mathrm{def}}^{k}(F)=\left\{c:{\mathcal{G}}^{(k)}\to T\mathcal{H}\ \middle|\ c(g_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\in T_{F(g_{1})}\mathcal{H}\ \text{and}\ c\ \text{is}\ s_{\mathcal{H}}-\text{projectable}\right\},

where
c
being

s_{\mathcal{H}}-
projectable means that
ds_{\mathcal{H}}\circ c(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k})
is independent of
g_{1}
.

The differential of
c
is defined by the same formula as the differential for
C^{*}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})
(see Definition
2.6
), except using the division
d\bar{m}_{\mathcal{H}}
of
T\mathcal{H}
instead of the division
d\bar{m}
of
T{\mathcal{G}}
.

In degree 0, we have
C_{\mathrm{def}}^{0}(F)=\Gamma({F^{*}A_{\mathcal{H}}})
, and
\delta\alpha=\overrightarrow{\alpha}+\overleftarrow{\alpha}\in C_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(F)
, where
\overrightarrow{\alpha}(g)=dR_{F(g)}(\alpha_{t(g)})
and
\overleftarrow{\alpha}(g)=dL_{F(g)}(di(\alpha_{s(g)}))
.

There is a natural cochain complex map
F_{*}:C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\to C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(F)
, given by
F_{*}c(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}):=dF_{g_{1}}(c(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}))
.

Analogously, for example, to the cases of deformations of morphisms of associative algebras
[
39
]
, of Lie algebras
[
37
]
, and of deformations of holomorphic maps (see
[
44
]
and references therein), it is shown in
[
12
]
that simultaneous deformations of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
F
are controlled by the mapping cone complex of
F_{*}
,

\text{Cone}(F_{*})^{n}=C^{n}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\oplus C^{n-1}_{\mathrm{def}}(F),\ \ \delta(c,Y)=(\delta c,F_{*}c-\delta Y).

VB-groupoids can be thought of as groupoid objects in the category of vector bundles. They provide alternative ways to look at the representation theory and the deformation theory of Lie groupoids (See
[

]
,
[

We review some basics on VB-groupoids which will be useful in the following sections. For more details we refer to

]

[

]
and
[

Definition 2.12
.

A

(\Gamma,E,{\mathcal{G}},M)
consists of two Lie groupoids together with two vector bundle structures as in the diagram

(2)

Remark 2.13

The requirement that

Example 2.14
.

(Tangent VB-groupoid)

Given a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows M

Remark 2.15
.

Note that in the previous example one has the following short exact sequences of vector bundles over

(3)

s^{*}(A_{\mathcal{G}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-l\circ di}}{{\longrightarrow}}T{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(dt)^{!}}}{{\longrightarrow}}t^{*}(TM)

t^{*}(A_{\mathcal{G}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}T{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ds)^{!}}}{{\longrightarrow}}s^{*}(TM)

where

.

(Cotangent groupoid)
As noticed in

\left\langle\tilde{s}(\alpha_{g}),a_{s(g)}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\alpha_{g},l_{g}\circ di(a)\right\rangle

With multiplication determined by

VB-groupoid cohomology

Let
\Gamma
be a VB-groupoid. The differentiable complex of

given by the fibrewise linear cochains of
\Gamma
.

The VB-groupoid complex

of
\Gamma
(
[

which takes into account simultaneously the Lie groupoid and vector bundle structures of

c\in C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\Gamma)
for which

(1)

c(0_{g_{1}},\gamma_{g_{2}},...,\gamma_{g_{k}})=0
,

(2)

c(0_{g}\cdot\gamma_{g_{1}},\gamma_{g_{2}},...,\gamma_{g_{k}})=c(\gamma_{g_{1}},\gamma_{g_{2}},...,\gamma_{g_{k}})
.

This complex turns out to compute the cohomology of the base groupoid with coefficients in a representation up to homotopy. For the case of the tangent VB-groupoid it yields an interpretation of the adjoint representation (
[
41
]
). We will make use of the following fact.

Proposition 2.17

(
[
41
]
, Prop. 5.5, Thm. 5.6)
.

The deformation complex of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
is isomorphic to the VB-groupoid complex of its cotangent VB-groupoid,
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{VB}}(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}})
. The isomorphism is given by
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\longrightarrow C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{VB}}(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}})
,
c\mapsto c^{\prime}
with

c^{\prime}(\eta_{g_{1}},...,\eta_{g_{k}})=\left\langle\eta_{g_{1}},c(g_{1},...,g_{k})\right\rangle.

We recall also the following result of Cabrera and Drummond, that will let us substitute the VB-complex by the complex of linear cochains for most purposes.

Lemma 2.18

(
[
10
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

Let
(\Gamma,E,{\mathcal{G}},M)
be a VB-groupoid. The inclusion
\iota:C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{VB}}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\Gamma)
induces an isomorphism of right
H^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}})
-modules in cohomology.

2.8.
Multiplicative forms, symplectic groupoids, and Poisson manifolds

Given a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
, a differential
k
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}({\mathcal{G}})
is called
multiplicative
if it satisfies

m^{*}\alpha=pr_{1}^{*}\alpha+pr_{2}^{*}\alpha,

where
m
is the multiplication of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
pr_{1},pr_{2}:{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}\to{\mathcal{G}}
are the two projections. We denote by
\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{mult}}({\mathcal{G}})\subset\Omega^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}})
the subcomplex of multiplicative forms on
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Remark 2.19
.

Multiplicativity can be expressed in yet a few other equivalent ways, some of which will be useful in this study. By definition, multiplicativity of
\alpha
amounts to
\delta\alpha=0
, where
\delta=pr_{1}^{*}-m^{*}+pr_{2}^{*}
is the horizontal differential of the first column in the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff double complex (cf. Section
2.2
).

Moreover, note that to a
k
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}({\mathcal{G}})
one can associate the map

\hat{\alpha}:\oplus^{k}_{\mathcal{G}}T{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R},

given by contraction of
\alpha
with tangent vectors. The map
\hat{\alpha}
is skewsymmetric and multilinear (with respect to the linear structure of
\oplus^{k}_{\mathcal{G}}T{\mathcal{G}}
over
{\mathcal{G}}
). We can now determine multiplicativity of
\alpha
in terms of
\hat{\alpha}
.

Lemma 2.20
.

[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
The form
\alpha
is multiplicative if and only if the map
\hat{\alpha}
is a groupoid morphism (where the groupoid structure on
\oplus^{k}_{\mathcal{G}}T{\mathcal{G}}
is the natural extension of the structure of
T{\mathcal{G}}
, and
\mathbb{R}
is seen as an additive group).

A
symplectic groupoid
is a pair
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, where
{\mathcal{G}}
is a Lie groupoid and
\omega
is a multiplicative symplectic form on
{\mathcal{G}}
.
As seen above this can be interpreted as having a pair of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
and a symplectic form
\omega
on
{\mathcal{G}}
for which
\hat{\omega}:\oplus^{2}_{\mathcal{G}}T{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a groupoid morphism. This point of view will be useful, because it lets us draw from the study of deformations of Lie groupoid morphisms
[
12
]
in order to study deformations of symplectic groupoids.

Symplectic groupoids are very closely related to Poisson manifolds. Recall that a
Poisson structure
on a manifold
M
is a Lie bracket

\{\cdot,\cdot\}:C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)

which is a derivation in each entry, i.e. it satisfies the Leibniz identity

\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+g\{f,h\}.

Being a biderivation, it can be seen as a bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}(TM))
.
The pair
(M,\pi)
is called a
Poisson manifold
.

Any Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
gives rise to a Lie algebroid structure on the bundle
T^{*}M\to M
, called the
cotangent Lie algebroid
. The anchor
\pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\to TM
is given by contraction with the Poisson bivector,
\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)=\pi(\alpha,\cdot)
, while the Lie bracket on
\Omega^{1}(M)
is defined by

[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi}=\mathcal{L}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)}\beta-\mathcal{L}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\beta)}\alpha-d(\pi(\alpha,\beta)).

Returning to symplectic groupoids, let us recall a few of the many notable properties they satisfy (see for example
[
17
]
for more).
Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
be a symplectic groupoid over
M
. Then

(1)

The dimension of
{\mathcal{G}}
is twice the dimension of
M
.

(2)

M
(embedded via the unit map) is a Lagrangian submanifold of
{\mathcal{G}}
. This is a consequence of the multiplicativity of
\omega
and of the relation between the dimensions.

(3)

Let
A
be the Lie algebroid of
{\mathcal{G}}
. There is a natural splitting at the units

T{\mathcal{G}}_{|M}=A\oplus TM,

and since
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
is symplectic, it follows from the dimension relation that
A
and
TM
have the same rank. Moreover, the fact that
M
is Lagrangian, together with the splitting
T{\mathcal{G}}_{|M}=A\oplus TM
, implies that
\omega:\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
induces a non-degenerate pairing
TM\oplus A\to\mathbb{R}
, and hence an isomorphism
\omega^{\flat}_{|TM}:TM\to A^{*}
. Its dual map
-\omega^{\flat}_{|A}
induces an isomorphism
A\cong T^{*}M
.

(4)

The anchor of
A
, viewed via the identification above as a map
T^{*}M\to TM
, is anti-selfadjoint, giving us a bivector
\pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
. Moreover,
\pi
is in fact Poisson, and the induced algebroid structure on
T^{*}M
is that of the cotangent algebroid of
(M,\pi)
.

A Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
is called integrable if there is a symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
over
M
inducing the Poisson structure on the base. In that case, as mentioned above,
{\mathcal{G}}
integrates the cotangent Lie algebroid
T^{*}M
. There is a converse to this statement.

Theorem 2.21

(
[
62
]
, Thm. 5.2)
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold such that the cotangent Lie algebroid
T^{*}M
is integrable. Then the source
1
-connected integration of
T^{*}M
admits a natural multiplicative symplectic structure, which induces the Poisson structure
\pi
on
M
.

Let us mention a few examples of integrable Poisson manifolds, and of symplectic groupoids integrating them.

Symplectic manifolds

Any symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
can be seen as a Poisson manifold, with the Poisson bivector being given by the inverse of the symplectic form,
\pi=\omega^{-1}
. The pair groupoid
M\times M\rightrightarrows M
, equipped with the symplectic form
pr_{2}^{*}\omega-pr_{1}^{*}\omega
is a symplectic groupoid integrating
\pi
. The source
1
-connected symplectic groupoid integrating
(M,\omega)
is the symplectic fundamental groupoid
(\Pi_{1}(M),s^{*}\omega-t^{*}\omega)
.

Any other
s
-connected Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
integrating
T^{*}M
lies between these two extremes, as there are groupoid submersions
\Pi_{1}(M)\to{\mathcal{G}}\to M\times M
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{G}}
is a symplectic groupoid when equipped with the form
s^{*}\omega-t^{*}\omega
.

Zero Poisson structure

Let
(M,0)
be a manifold equipped with the zero Poisson structure, meaning that the Poisson bracket of any two functions is zero. The source
1
-connected symplectic groupoid integrating
(M,0)
is the cotangent bundle of
M
, with the canonical symplectic structure
(T^{*}M,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})
. The source and target maps of
T^{*}M
are equal to the vector bundle projection, and the groupoid multiplication is the fibrewise addition.

Linear Poisson manifolds

A linear Poisson structure on a vector space
V
is one for which the Poisson bracket of linear functions is again linear. This happens if and only if there is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that
V=\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and the Poisson bracket is the Kirillov-Konstant-Souriau bracket, which extends the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
(seen as the space of linear functions on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
) to all smooth functions on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. We denote the corresponding Poisson bivector by
\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Explicitly,

\{f,g\}(\eta):=\langle\eta,[d_{\eta}f,d_{\eta}g]_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle,\ \ \forall\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},\ f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}).

The
s
-connected symplectic groupoids integrating the linear Poisson manifold
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are of the form
(T^{*}G,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})
, for any connected Lie group
G
integrating
\mathfrak{g}
. The groupoid structure of
T^{*}G
is that of the cotangent groupoid of
G
, as explained in Example
2.16
.

The source 1-connected symplectic groupoid integrating
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\mathfrak{g}})
is the cotangent groupoid
(T^{*}G,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})
of the
1
-connected group
G
integrating
\mathfrak{g}
.

The cotangent groupoid
T^{*}G
is also isomorphic to the action groupoid
G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}
of the coadjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. An isomorphism is given by the trivialization of the cotangent bundle via right translations,
T^{*}G\cong G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Cotangent VB-groupoids

Generalizing the previous case, Lie algebroid structures on a vector bundle
A
are in
1:1
correspondence with Poisson structures on its dual
A^{*}
which are linear along the fibres
[
18
]
. By that we mean a Poisson structure such that the Poisson bracket of two fibrewise linear functions on
A^{*}
is linear, and the bracket of a fibrewise linear function with a basic function is basic, and the bracket of two basic functions is zero.

Given any Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows M
with Lie algebroid
A=\mathrm{Lie}({\mathcal{G}})
, the cotangent Lie groupoid
T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows A^{*}
(Example
2.16
) is a symplectic groupoid integrating
A^{*}
. The source 1-connected integration of
(A^{*},\pi_{\mathrm{lin}})
is the cotangent groupoid
(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathrm{can}})
of the source
1
-connected groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
integrating
A
.

3.
Deformations of symplectic groupoids

We now come to the notion of a deformation of a symplectic groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
. It naturally consists of an underlying deformation of Lie groupoids of
\mathcal{G}
(Definition
2.3
), with a compatible deformation of the multiplicative symplectic form
\omega
. For simplicity let us start with strict deformations.

Definition 3.1
.

(Strict deformations of symplectic groupoids)

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
be a symplectic groupoid, let
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}=({\mathcal{G}},\bar{m}_{{\varepsilon}})
be a strict deformation of
{\mathcal{G}}
and let
\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}({\mathcal{G}})
be a deformation of
\omega
by symplectic forms. The family
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
is called a
strict deformation
of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
if each
({\mathcal{G}}_{{\varepsilon}},\omega_{\varepsilon})
is a symplectic groupoid.

Similarly, we define an

s
-constant deformation of symplectic groupoids
as one for which the underlying Lie groupoid deformation is
s
-constant.

An
s
-constant deformation of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
such that
(\bar{m}_{{\varepsilon}},\omega_{{\varepsilon}})=(\bar{m},\omega)
for all
{\varepsilon}
is called a
constant deformation
of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
.

Examples of deformations can be easily constructed (at least) in two simple ways.

Examples 3.2
.

Consider
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
a symplectic groupoid with
(M,\pi)
the Poisson structure induced on the base
M
.

(1)

(Diffeomorphisms) Let
\phi_{\varepsilon}:{\mathcal{G}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}

The deformation complex of a (symplectic) groupoid, revisited

We revisit the deformation complex of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
in the case that
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
is a symplectic groupoid. We will use the symplectic form

Step 1

Recall that the deformation complex

Step 2

Step 3

Step 4

For each integer

5.
The deformation complex of a symplectic groupoid

Our approach to find the deformation complex of a symplectic groupoid is to construct it out of the deformation complex of a Lie groupoid
[
25
]
(taking also into account the discussion of Section
4
), and the deformation complex for multiplicative forms, introduced in
[
12
]
.

In section
7
we will study the resulting complex, looking at cocycles and coboundaries related to deformations and equivalences, confirming that this approach leads indeed to the correct complex.

Deformations of symplectic groupoids in terms of morphisms

Let
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
be a deformation of the symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
. The first step is to consider the associated morphism
\hat{\omega}:\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
, described in Remark
2.19
. Doing so,

({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
can be replaced by the simultaneous deformation
(\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\hat{\omega}_{\varepsilon})
of the Lie groupoid
\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}
and of the morphism
\hat{\omega}
.

Note that this is a very particular type of simultaneous deformation of a Lie groupoid and a morphism having that groupoid as domain. The deformation of
\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}
is of the form
\bigoplus^{2}T({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon})
and the deformation of
\hat{\omega}
is by (closed, non-degenerate) bilinear and skewsymmetric morphisms
\hat{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
. Let us then first examine these separate pieces.

Deformations of multiplicative 2-forms

Let us first describe the deformation complex
\hat{C}_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)
of a multiplicative 2-form
\omega
, defined in
[
12
]
. It is the subcomplex of
bilinear
and
skewsymmetric
cochains of the deformation complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(\hat{\omega})
of the associated Lie groupoid morphism
\hat{\omega}
. We remark that our notation differs from the one in
[
12
]
, where the deformation complex
\hat{C}_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)
was denoted simply by
C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)
. We reserve the latter notation for the deformation complex of a
closed
multiplicative form
\omega
.

As shown in
[
12
]
, it is isomorphic to the complex
\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
of 2-forms on the nerve of
{\mathcal{G}}
, and it can also be identified with the subcomplex
C^{\bullet}(\bigoplus^{2}_{p_{\mathcal{G}}}T{\mathcal{G}})_{\mathrm{2-lin,\,sk}}
of 2-linear skewsymmetric cochains of the differentiable complex of the groupoid
\bigoplus^{2}_{p_{\mathcal{G}}}T{\mathcal{G}}
.
As remarked in
[
12
]
, a deformation
\omega_{\varepsilon}
of
\omega
induces a family of cocycles
\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\hat{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
in
\hat{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
.

In our setting,
\omega
is closed and we are interested in deformations of it via closed multiplicative forms. The deformation complex to consider is then the subcomplex
C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)\subset\hat{C}_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)
, corresponding to closed
2
-forms on the nerve of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Tangent lifts of deformation cochains

For any Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
, the
tangent lift
of deformation cochains, described in
[
49
]
, is the inclusion of cochain complexes
T_{\mathrm{def}}:C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\to C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(T{\mathcal{G}})
determined by composing the canonical involution (also called canonical flip, cf.
[
64
, Section 8.13]
)
J_{\mathcal{G}}:TT{\mathcal{G}}\to TT{\mathcal{G}}
and the differential
d
of a smooth function:

T_{\mathrm{def}}c:=J_{\mathcal{G}}\circ dc.

We also call tangent lift to its natural extension
\oplus^{2}T_{\mathrm{def}}:C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\to C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(\oplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}})
defined as
\oplus^{2}T_{\mathrm{def}}(c):=\oplus^{2}(T_{\mathrm{def}}c),
of which we will make use.

As remarked in
[
12
,
49
]
, the tangent lift includes the deformation cocycles of
{\mathcal{G}}
into the deformation complex of
\oplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}
, as cocycles associated to deformations of the form
\bigoplus^{2}T({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon})
.

The deformation complex

We come back to the idea that deformations of symplectic groupoids can be seen as particular simultaneous deformations of
\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}
, of the form
\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
, and of a morphism
\hat{\omega}
, via morphisms associated to (closed, non-degenerate) multiplicative forms.

In this situation, we can use the idea from
[
12
]
of using the mapping cone construction to control simultaneous deformations.
As described in Section
2.6
,
\hat{\omega}
induces a map
\hat{\omega}_{*}:C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\bigoplus^{2}T{\mathcal{G}})\to C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\hat{\omega})
between deformation complexes. In fact, the bilinearity and skewsymmetry of
\omega
imply, by direct computation, that its image is contained in the subcomplex
C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)
.

Definition 5.1
.

The
deformation complex of the symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, denoted by
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, is the mapping cone complex of the map

\hat{\omega}_{*}\circ\oplus^{2}T_{\mathrm{def}}:C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\to C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega).

.

Considering deformations of symplectic groupoids as simultaneous deformations of a morphism of Lie groupoids and of its domain, the work of
[
12
]

∎

Corollary 5.4
.

The deformation complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
of the symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
is isomorphic to the mapping cone of the de Rham differential

-d_{dR}:\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})\to\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)}).

Explicitly, according to our convention for the mapping cone complex, the differential
D:\Omega_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})\oplus\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet-1)})\to\Omega_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet+1)})\oplus\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
is given by

(\zeta,\omega)\mapsto(\delta\zeta,-d_{dR}\zeta-\delta\omega).

We call this complex the
de Rham model for deformation cohomology
of the symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
.

Remark 5.5
.

Notice that
d_{dR}:\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})\to\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
is a part of the vertical differential of the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff double complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)}),\delta,d_{dR})
. This allows us to identify the deformation complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
with the total complex of a sub-double complex
B^{\bullet,\bullet}({\mathcal{G}})\subset\Omega^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
. This sub-double complex consists of the subset
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
of the second line
\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
and of the subset
\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
of the third line
\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
of the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff double complex (and it is zero in all other bidegrees). In fact, the map

\varphi:\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(n)})\oplus\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}}^{(n-1)})\to Tot^{n+1}(B^{\bullet,\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}))

defined by

(\zeta,\omega)\mapsto(\zeta,(-1)^{n}\omega)

gives us the isomorphism of the complexes. The shift in degree appears naturally since multiplicative symplectic forms, while being degree two forms, give rise to degree three cocycles in the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff cohomology. More generally, a groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
equipped with a pair
(\phi,\omega)\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{G}})\oplus\Omega^{3}(M)
such that
0\oplus 0\oplus\omega\oplus\phi\in Tot^{3}(\Omega^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)}))
is a cocycle is called a
\phi
-twisted presymplectic groupoid
[
8
]
.

Another interpretation for Bott-Shulman-Stasheff cohomology is that it is the de Rham cohomology of the differentiable stack
M//{\mathcal{G}}
presented by
{\mathcal{G}}
. We note also that degree three cocycles belonging to
B^{\bullet,\bullet}
i.e. corresponding to deformation cocycles of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, and satisfying an integrality condition are related to
S^{1}
-central extensions of
{\mathcal{G}}

[
1
, Thm. 5.1]
.

6.
Examples and computations

6.1.
Deformation cohomology in small degrees

We start by interpreting cohomology classes in small degrees in a geometric way via direct computations; but see also the computations of small degrees in terms of a spectral sequence, in Section
6.5
. Using the de Rham model for the deformation cohomology, a simple computation shows that

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{0}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(M)\mid d\alpha=0,\ \delta\alpha=0\}=\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}},

i.e. the space of closed invariant
1
-forms on
M
.

In degree 1, again using the de Rham model, a deformation cocycle on
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
is given by a pair
(\zeta,\alpha)\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\oplus\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)
such that
\delta{\zeta}=0
and
d\zeta=\delta\alpha
. Using the terminology of
[
8
,
16
]
, this means that
\zeta
is a relatively
\alpha
-closed multiplicative
1
-form.

Under the isomorphism
\omega^{\flat}:T{\mathcal{G}}\to T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}
, the form
\zeta
corresponds to a vector field
X
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
-\omega^{\flat}(X)=\zeta
.
Multiplicativity of
\zeta
and of
\omega
mean that
X
is multiplicative as well, meaning that
X
is a Lie groupoid morphism
X:{\mathcal{G}}\to T{\mathcal{G}}
. Therefore,
X
is an infinitesimal automorphism of
{\mathcal{G}}
, its flow preserving the Lie groupoid structure. However, since
\zeta
is not closed but only relatively
\alpha
-closed, the flow
\phi_{\varepsilon}
of
X
does not preserve the symplectic form
\omega
. Instead, it satisfies
\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega=\omega+\delta({\varepsilon}\alpha)
, so it applies a gauge transformation by
\delta({\varepsilon}\alpha)
to
\zeta
. Because of this we call 1-cocycles
twisted infinitesimal automorphisms of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
.

A degree 1 coboundary is a pair of the form
(\delta\beta,d\beta)\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\oplus\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)
. Adding it to a relatively
\alpha
-closed multiplicative
1
-form
\zeta
we obtain a relatively
(\alpha+d\beta)
-closed multiplicative
1
-form
\zeta+\delta\beta
. We call these transformations of the form
(\zeta,\alpha)\mapsto(\zeta+\delta\beta,\alpha+d\beta)

trivial gauge transformations
. We conclude that

H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)=\frac{\text{Twisted infinitesimal automorphisms of }({\mathcal{G}},\omega)}{\text{Trivial gauge transformations}}.

In degree 2, as mentioned in Remark
5.2
, deformations of symplectic groupoids give rise, after taking a first-order approximation, to cocycles in
C^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
; equivalences between deformations provide 1-cochains transgressing the difference between the corresponding deformation cocycles. Thus

H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)=\frac{\text{Infinitesimal deformations of }({\mathcal{G}},\omega)}{\text{Infinitesimal equivalences}}.

This is the main use of the deformation cohomology, and we will study it in detail in Sections
7
and
8
.

6.2.
The cone exact sequence and proper Lie groupoids

Before we start analysing the deformation cohomology of a symplectic groupoid in particular examples, we recall a simple consequence of the definition of the deformation complex. As the mapping cone of
\hat{\omega}_{*}\circ\oplus^{2}T_{\mathrm{def}}:C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\to C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)
, it fits in a short exact sequence of complexes

0\to C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}(\omega)[-1]\to C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\to C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\to 0.

This induces the long exact sequence in cohomology

(8)

\cdots\to H_{\mathrm{def}}^{k-1}(\omega)\to H^{k}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\to H^{k}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\to}}H_{\mathrm{def}}^{k}(\omega)\to\cdots

where the connecting homomorphism
\partial=H(\hat{\omega}_{*}\circ\oplus^{2}T_{\mathrm{def}})
is the map induced in cohomology by
\hat{\omega}_{*}\circ\oplus^{2}T_{\mathrm{def}}
.

Example 6.1

(Symplectic manifolds)
.

A Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
integrating a connected non-degenerate Poisson manifold
(M,\omega^{-1})
is transitive. For a transitive Lie groupoid it holds
[
25
, Prop. 3.1]
that
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\cong H^{\bullet}(G,\mathfrak{g})
, where
G
is the isotropy Lie group at any point of the base, acting on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
via the adjoint representation. By simple dimension counting we know that the isotropy groups are discrete, so
\mathfrak{g}=0
.

Thus, for a symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},s^{*}\omega-t^{*}\omega)\rightrightarrows(M,\omega^{-1})
, the long exact sequence (
8
) implies that

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}},s^{*}\omega-t^{*}\omega)\cong H_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet-1}(s^{*}\omega-t^{*}\omega).

For the pair groupoid of
M
, any multiplicative
2
-form is cohomologically trivial, so
H_{\mathrm{def}}^{2}(M\times M,pr_{1}^{*}\omega-pr_{2}^{*}\omega)=0
. This translates to the fact that deformations of
(M\times M,pr_{1}^{*}\omega-pr_{2}^{*}\omega)
are given by gauge transformation of
\delta\omega=pr_{1}^{*}\omega-pr_{2}^{*}\omega
by
\delta\omega_{\varepsilon}
, where
\omega+\omega_{\varepsilon}
is a deformation of the symplectic structure on the base.

Another situation in which we can make further use of this long exact sequence is for proper symplectic groupoids.
A Lie groupoid is called
proper
if it is Hausdorff and

(s,t):{\mathcal{G}}\to M\times M

is a proper map. Examples of proper groupoids include compact Lie groupoids, pair groupoids, and the cotangent Lie groupoid
T^{*}G
of a compact Lie group
G
.

Proper symplectic groupoids are the subject of recent extensive work
[
23
,
24
,
76
]
, and satisfy many remarkable properties not shared by a general symplectic groupoid, and much less by non-integrable Poisson manifolds. They generalize many aspects of the geometry of compact symplectic manifolds and of Lie algebras of compact type.

Proper symplectic groupoids are also very special among proper Lie groupoids, in a way which is analogous to how the adjoint action of a compact Lie group
G
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is special among actions of
G
(compare for example
[
23
,
24
]
with Chapters 2 and 3 of
[
35
]
). For example, for an arbitrary Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows M
the isotropy Lie algebra
\mathfrak{i}_{x}
and the normal space
\nu_{x}
to the orbit of
{\mathcal{G}}
through
x\in M
are the kernel and cokernel of the anchor map of
A=\mathrm{Lie}({\mathcal{G}})
at
x
. In general there is no further relation between these two objects, which feature prominently in the study of the geometry of proper Lie groupoids; when
{\mathcal{G}}
is a symplectic groupoid, however,
\nu_{x}
inherits a linear Poisson structure from
M
by linearization at
x
, such that its dual Lie algebra
\nu_{x}^{*}
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathfrak{i}_{x}
.

We recall the following vanishing result for the deformation cohomology of proper Lie groupoids.

Theorem 6.2

(
[
25
]
, Thm 6.1)
.

If
{\mathcal{G}}
is a proper Lie groupoid, then

H^{0}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\cong\Gamma(\mathfrak{i})^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}},\ \ \ H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\cong\Gamma(\nu)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}},\ \text{ and }\ H^{k}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})=0\text{ for }k\geq 2.

Let us give a brief explanation of the objects in the statement of this result. The isotropy bundle
\mathfrak{i}
is the kernel of the anchor map of the Lie algebroid of
{\mathcal{G}}
,

\mathfrak{i}=\mathrm{Ker}(\rho:A\to TM).

Differentiating at the units the conjugation by elements
g:x\to y
induces a map
\mathrm{ad}_{g}:\mathfrak{i}_{x}\to\mathfrak{i}_{y}
.

Strictly speaking,
\mathfrak{i}
is a distribution with possibly varying rank, so it is only a bundle if
{\mathcal{G}}
is a regular groupoid. Nonetheless, it makes sense to consider
\Gamma(\mathfrak{i})
as the space of smooth sections of
A
which land in
\mathfrak{i}\subset A
. Among these, the space of invariant ones is

\Gamma(\mathfrak{i})^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}=\{\alpha\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{i})\ |\ \mathrm{ad}_{g}(\alpha_{x})=\alpha_{y}\}.

It was shown in
[
25
]
that in fact
H^{0}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\cong\Gamma(\mathfrak{i})^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}
for any Lie groupoid, not only proper ones.

The normal bundle of
{\mathcal{G}}
is

\nu=\mathrm{Coker(\rho)}=TM/\rho(A),

and once again it is only a smooth vector bundle for regular
{\mathcal{G}}
. But once more, there is a way to make sense of its space of smooth invariant sections
\Gamma(\nu)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}
. First of all, the space of smooth sections is defined as
\Gamma(\nu):=\mathfrak{X}(M)/\rho(A)
. A section
[V]\in\Gamma(\nu)
is called invariant if there is a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}({\mathcal{G}})
which is both
s
and
t
-projectable to
V
. This condition is independent of the choice of representative
V
. In the regular case, this notion recovers the usual notion of invariant sections of the normal representation of
{\mathcal{G}}
. We refer to Section
4.4
of
[
25
]
for more detail.

For an arbitrary Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
, deformation cocycles of degree one are multiplicative vector fields on
{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e., vector fields which are Lie groupoid morphisms
X:{\mathcal{G}}\to T{\mathcal{G}}
. Any such vector field
X
is in particular
s
and
t
-projectable to a vector field
V
on the base. Mapping
X
to the class of
V
modulo
\mathrm{Im}(\rho)
induces a linear map
H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\to\Gamma(\nu)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}

[
25
, Lemma 4.7]
. Part of Theorem
6.2
is that for proper Lie groupoids this map becomes an isomorphism.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
be a proper symplectic groupoid. Then there is a 7-term exact sequence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to\Gamma(\nu^{*})^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\to

\displaystyle\to\Gamma(\nu)^{{\mathcal{G}}-\mathrm{inv}}\to H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\omega)\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\to 0

and
H^{k}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\omega)
for
k>2
.

In particular, in terms of the de Rham model for deformation cohomology,

H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong\frac{H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\omega)}{d(H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}))}\cong\frac{H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\omega)}{d[\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{mult}}({\mathcal{G}})/\delta(\Omega^{1}(M))]}.

Proof.

Simply use Theorem
6.2
together with the long exact sequence (
8
), the discussion of Section
6.1
for deformation cohomology in small degrees, and recall that
\mathfrak{i}_{x}\cong\nu_{x}^{*}
for symplectic groupoids.
∎

6.3.
Zero Poisson structures of proper type

The source
1
-connected integration of the zero Poisson structure on a manifold
M
is given by the symplectic groupoid
(T^{*}M,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})
, viewed as a bundle of abelian Lie groups over
M
, and so it is never proper. However,
(M,0)
may still be integrated by a non simply connected proper symplectic groupoid, having interesting consequences for the geometry of
M
. Proper
s
-connected symplectic integrations of
(M,0)
are in 1:1 correspondence with integral affine structures on
M
(
[
23
, Prop 4.2]
, see also
[
24
, 3.1.6]
).

An integral affine structure on
M
can be described by a Lagrangian smooth full rank lattice
\Lambda
in
T^{*}M
i.e., a subbundle
\Lambda\subset T^{*}M
, whose fibres are full rank lattices in the fibres of
T^{*}M
, and which is a Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}M
. The proper symplectic integration corresponding to
\Lambda
is
(T^{*}M,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})/\Lambda
. The Lie groupoid
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}:=T^{*}M/\Lambda
is a torus bundle and the form
\omega_{\mathrm{can}}
descends to a symplectic form
\omega_{\mathcal{T}}
on it, so
(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})
is a symplectic torus bundle over
M
.

Since
(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})
is a bundle of Lie groups, its source and target maps coincide, so using the de Rham model for deformation cohomology we see that

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})=\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)^{\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}-\mathrm{inv}}=\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M).

Moreover,

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda})\cong\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda})/\delta(\Omega^{1}(M))=\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda})

and

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\omega_{\mathcal{T}})=\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl,mult}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda})/\delta(\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}(M))=\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl,mult}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}).

Therefore, in this case the 7-term sequence from Proposition
6.3
becomes

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})\to

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl,mult}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda})\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})\to 0

and
H^{k}_{\mathrm{def}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})\cong H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\omega_{\mathcal{T}})
for
k>2
.

In particular,

H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})\cong H^{2}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}),d_{dR}),

the second de Rham cohomology of the subcomplex of multiplicative forms on
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}
.

6.4.
Linear Poisson manifolds of proper type

The Lie groupoid
T^{*}G\cong G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}
integrating the linear Poisson manifold
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\pi_{\mathfrak{g}})
is proper if and only if the Lie group
G
is compact. In this case, we know from Proposition
6.3
that

H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}(T^{*}G,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\cong\frac{H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}(\omega_{\mathrm{can}})}{d[\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{mult}}(T^{*}G)/\delta(\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))]}.

But since
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is contractible,
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})=0
, so
\delta\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})=\delta d\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})=d\delta\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and therefore

H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}(T^{*}G,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\cong\frac{\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{mult,cl}}(T^{*}G)/\delta\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}{d[\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{mult}}(T^{*}G)/\delta(\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))]}=\frac{\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{mult,cl}}(T^{*}G)/d\delta\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}{d[\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{mult}}(T^{*}G)/\delta(\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))]},

which is the same as
H^{2}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{mult}}(T^{*}G),d_{dR})
.

and

Again, if we know more about
{\mathcal{G}}
, these formulas become simpler. If
{\mathcal{G}}
is proper, we can make use of the following variation of Theorem
6.2
.

Proposition 6.5
.

If
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
is a proper Lie groupoid, then

H_{\delta}^{k}(\Omega_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})_{\mathrm{cl}})=0

for all

.

Proof.

We will first rewrite the homotopy operator of

in terms of the de Rham model
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
; we then check that it restricts to a homotopy operator for the subcomplex
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})_{\mathrm{cl}}
. We recall that the homotopy operator is described in terms of a Haar system and a cut-off function, which exist for any proper Lie groupoid, and permit integration along the target fibres. We refer to
[
25
]
for details and for the proof of Theorem
6.2
using this operator.

Let
k\geq 2
and let
\beta\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k+1)})_{\delta-\mathrm{cl}}
. The homotopy operator on the de Rham model
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
assigns to
\beta
the transgression

defined by

\alpha(X^{1}_{g_{1}},...,X^{k}_{g_{k}})=\int_{s(g_{k})}\beta(X^{1}_{g_{1}},...,X^{k}_{g_{k}},\tau_{h}(ds(X^{k}_{g_{k}})))dh;

where
\tau:t^{*}TM\to T{\mathcal{G}}
is a splitting of the target map of
{\mathcal{G}}
. Notice that, since
\beta\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k+1)})
, the expression in the integral actually does not depend on the value of the tangent vectors at

(cf. Remark
4.2
). We remark that this expression can also be obtained by using the homotopy operator for VB-cohomology as in
[
10
]
. Equivalently, if

the homotopy operator has the form

\alpha(X):=\int_{t\text{-fibres}}\beta(X^{(k+1)})d\mu_{\mathcal{G}},

where

is the vector field

X^{(k+1)}(g_{1},...,g_{k+1}):=(X(g_{1},...,g_{k}),\tau_{g_{k+1}}(ds(dpr_{k}X(g_{1},...,g_{k}))))

induced by
X
.

We now prove that the transgression
\alpha
of a closed 1-form
\beta\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(\bullet)})
is a closed 1-form.
Recall that the de Rham differential of 1-forms can be written as

\begin{split}d\alpha(X,Y)&=-\alpha([X,Y])+X(\alpha(Y))-Y(\alpha(X))\\
&=L_{X}\alpha(Y)-L_{Y}\alpha(X)-\alpha(L_{X}Y).\end{split}

Let
\beta\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k+1)})_{\delta-\mathrm{cl}}
be such that
d\beta=0
, and let
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k)})
. Then,

\displaystyle(L_{X}\alpha)(Y)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\Bigr|_{{\varepsilon}=0}(\varphi^{X}_{\varepsilon})^{*}\alpha\right)(Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\Bigr|_{{\varepsilon}=0}\alpha((\varphi^{X}_{\varepsilon})_{*}Y)\circ\varphi^{X}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{t\text{-fibres}}\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\Bigr|_{{\varepsilon}=0}\beta((\varphi^{X^{(k+1)}}_{\varepsilon})_{*}Y^{(k+1)})\circ\varphi^{X^{(k+1)}}_{\varepsilon}d\mu_{\mathcal{G}}\ \ \

\displaystyle=\int_{t\text{-fibres}}L_{X^{(k+1)}}\beta(Y^{(k+1)})d\mu_{\mathcal{G}},

using that
\beta\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k+1)})
to pass from the second to the third lines. Similarly we can compute
(L_{Y}\alpha)(X)
. And finally,

\displaystyle\alpha([X,Y])

\displaystyle=\int_{t\text{-fibres}}\beta([X,Y]^{(k+1)})d\mu_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=\int_{t\text{-fibres}}\beta(L_{X^{(k+1)}}Y^{(k+1)})d\mu_{\mathcal{G}}\ \ (\text{because }\beta\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k+1)})).

Therefore, with these computations we get that

d\alpha(X,Y)=\int_{t\text{-fibres}}d\beta(X^{(k+1)},Y^{(k+1)})=0,

which proves the claim.
∎

This result, together with the formulas for
E^{k,0}_{3}
and for
E^{k-1,1}_{3}
from the beginning of this section, lead to a simpler description of

.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a proper Lie groupoid. Then for
k\geq 2

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{k}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\cong H^{k}_{\delta}\left(\frac{\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k-1)})}{d_{dR}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(k-1)}))}\right).

7.
The deformation cocycle of
s
-constant deformations, and Moser tricks

In this section we describe the deformation cocycle mentioned in remark

by using the simpler presentation of the deformation complex given by corollary

We then use such a cocycle to state a result analogous to the classical Moser Theorem in the context of symplectic groupoids. We first carry out this work for

7.1.
The deformation class

Let
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
be a deformation of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)

As we will show in proposition

We now prove that
\eta_{0}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)

associated to the deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
be an
s
-constant deformation of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)

To prove this Proposition we first enunciate two technical lemmas. In what follows, denote by

Lemma 7.2

A
k

(10)

\overline{m}^{*}\omega=p_{1}^{*}\omega-p_{2}^{*}\omega.

We just need to show that equation (

m^{*}\omega=pr_{1}^{*}\omega+pr_{2}^{*}\omega.

This follows from the diffeomorphism
\psi:{\mathcal{G}}^{[2]}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)},

(p,q)\longmapsto(p,i(q))
, where

is the inversion map of
{\mathcal{G}}
. Indeed, by applying

to equation (

(

Lemma 7.3

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\overline{m}_{\varepsilon})

Proof of proposition

Corollary 7.4
.

The 2-cocycle associated to the trivial deformation vanishes in cohomology.

Remark 7.5
.

Since a deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{{\varepsilon}},\omega_{\varepsilon})
of
({\mathcal{G}}_{0},\omega_{0})
can be seen as the deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{{\varepsilon}+{\lambda}},\omega_{{\varepsilon}+{\lambda}})
of
({\mathcal{G}}_{{\lambda}},\omega_{\lambda})
for any
{\lambda}\in I
, then the corresponding cochain
\eta_{{\lambda}}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda})
is also a cocycle. Furthermore,
in the case of a trivial deformation we can say even more: every
\eta_{\lambda}
is exact
. This follows from noting that the deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{{\varepsilon}},\omega_{\varepsilon})
of
({\mathcal{G}}_{0},\omega_{0})
being trivial implies that
({\mathcal{G}}_{{\varepsilon}+{\lambda}},\omega_{{\varepsilon}+{\lambda}})
is also a trivial deformation of
({\mathcal{G}}_{{\lambda}},\omega_{\lambda})
, for any
{\lambda}\in I.

In the next section we show that, under appropriate regularity and compactness conditions, the converse statement of the previous remark is also true.

7.2.
Moser arguments and moduli spaces

Theorem 7.6
.

(Moser Theorem for symplectic groupoids)

Let
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
be an
s
-constant deformation of a compact symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
. Then, the deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
is trivial if, and only if, the deformation class

\eta_{\varepsilon}:=\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\dot{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})

is smoothly exact.

Proof.

We will show that the smooth exactness of the deformation class
\eta_{\varepsilon}
implies the triviality of the deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
. The remark
7.5
above shows the converse statement. Thus, let us assume that
\eta_{\varepsilon}
is a smoothly exact family of cocycles. Then, we get

\delta_{{\tau}}(-\chi_{\tau})=\zeta_{\tau}\text{ and }-\dot{\omega}_{\tau}=-d(-\chi_{\tau})-\delta_{\tau}(\tilde{\alpha}_{\tau});

where
\chi_{\tau}\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{G}}_{\tau})
and
\tilde{\alpha}_{\tau}\in\Omega^{2}(M)
are smooth families of forms. The first equation amounts to
\delta^{{\tau}}_{\mathrm{def}}(X_{\tau})=\xi_{\tau}
, where
X_{\tau}
is the smooth family of vector fields such that
\chi_{\tau}=\iota_{X_{\tau}}\omega_{\tau}
for every
{\tau}
. Let
(\phi_{\varepsilon},\varphi_{\varepsilon})
be the flow of the time-dependent vector field
X_{\varepsilon}
(starting at time zero). Then, by letting
\bar{\alpha}_{\tau}:=\int_{0}^{{\tau}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\tilde{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})d{\varepsilon}
be the primitive of the curve
{\tau}\mapsto\phi_{\tau}^{*}\tilde{\alpha}_{\tau}
, we obtain

\dot{\omega}_{\tau}=-d(\iota_{X_{\tau}}\omega_{\tau})+\delta_{\tau}((\varphi_{\tau}^{-1})^{*}\frac{d}{ds}\bar{\alpha}_{\tau}),

which is,

\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\rvert_{{\varepsilon}={\tau}}\left[\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon}-\delta_{0}(\bar{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})\right]=0.

That is,

\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon}-\delta_{0}(\alpha_{\varepsilon})=\omega,\text{ for }\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\bar{\alpha}_{0}-\bar{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}.

Hence, one concludes that the deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
is trivial.
∎

As a consequence of this version of the Moser Theorem, then for a compact symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
we can describe a neighbourhood of
\omega
in a moduli space of multiplicative symplectic structures on
{\mathcal{G}}
. We say
a
moduli space and not
the
moduli space, because there are a few options of which equivalence relation to consider. We will use one that is suggested by the equivalence relation on deformations of symplectic groupoids.

The space of closed multiplicative
2
-forms on a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
is the subspace
\Omega_{\mathrm{cl,mult}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}})\subset\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
. It is a linear subspace, being the intersection of the kernel of the two linear operators
d_{dR}
and
\delta
. Non-degeneracy is an open condition, so the space of multiplicative symplectic structures on
{\mathcal{G}}
is an open neighbourhood of
\omega
in
\Omega_{\mathrm{cl,mult}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
, which we denote by
\mathcal{S({\mathcal{G}})}
.

Definition 7.7
.

Two multiplicative symplectic forms
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathcal{S(\mathcal{{\mathcal{G}}})}
are said to be
convex gauge-isotopic
if the deformation
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\varepsilon})
given by
\omega_{1}+{\varepsilon}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})
is a trivial deformation of symplectic groupoids (in particular
\omega_{\varepsilon}
is required to be non-degenerate for all
{\varepsilon}\in[0,1]
).

Denote by
\sim_{\mathrm{cgi}}
the equivalence relation on
\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})
generated by convex gauge-isotopies.

Theorem 7.8

(Moduli space)
.

Let
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
be a compact symplectic groupoid. Then there is a neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\omega
in
\mathcal{S}({{\mathcal{G}}})
and a neighbourhood
\mathcal{V}
of
0
in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, such that the map
\kappa:\mathcal{U}\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, defined by
\kappa(\omega_{1})=[0\oplus(\omega_{1}-\omega)]
induces a 1:1 correspondence

\mathcal{U}\big/\sim_{\mathrm{cgi}}\ \ \longleftrightarrow\ \ \mathcal{V}\subset H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega),

sending the equivalence class of
\omega
to
0
.

Proof.

The map
\kappa
descends to the quotient: if
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are convex gauge-isotopic, then the deformation
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{1}+{\varepsilon}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}))
of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{1})
is trivial, so its deformation class
[0\oplus(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{1})
vanishes. Therefore
[0\oplus(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
also vanishes. Thus
\kappa(\omega_{2})-\kappa(\omega_{1})=[0\oplus(\omega_{2}-\omega)]-[0\oplus(\omega_{1}-\omega)]=[0\oplus(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})]=0
.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}
be a convex neighbourhood of
\omega
in
\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})
, and let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}
be such that
\kappa(\omega_{1})-\kappa(\omega_{2})=0
. By definition,
[0\oplus(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})]=0\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, meaning that
(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})
is exact in deformation cohomology. We can consider the
s
-constant deformation
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{2}+{\varepsilon}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}))
of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{2})
(it is a deformation of symplectic groupoids because
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}
is convex). This deformation has a constant deformation class
[0\oplus(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})]=0\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{2})
for all
{\varepsilon}
, which we know to be exact. Therefore the deformation classes are smoothly exact and by Theorem
7.6
we conclude the triviality of the deformation. This implies that
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{1})
and
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{2})
are convex gauge-isotopic. Hence
\kappa
descends to an injective map.

Neighbourhoods
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
of
\omega
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of
0
can be chosen such that
\kappa
maps
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
onto
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. The reason for this is that by Proposition
6.3
any class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
has a representative of the form

with
\omega_{1}\in\Omega_{\mathrm{mult}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
. Let
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
be small enough that for every class in
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
there is such a representative

for which
\omega+\omega_{1}
is non-degenerate, and
\omega_{1}
belongs to
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}
. By definition
\kappa(\omega+\omega_{1})=[0\oplus\omega_{1}]
, so
\kappa:\mathcal{U}^{\prime}=\kappa^{-1}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{V^{\prime}}
becomes surjective. Finally, let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
be a convex neighbourhood of
\omega
, and let
\mathcal{V}=\kappa(\mathcal{U})
.
∎

Remark 7.9

(On the relation with Kodaira-Spencer maps)
.

The map
\kappa
plays a similar role to a Kodaira-Spencer map in the theory of deformations of complex structures
[
48
, Sec. 4.2]
. Allowing for some heuristics, and if we disregard issues with spaces of infinite dimension, there is a tautological (infinite-dimensional) family of symplectic groupoids

pr_{\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})}:\mathfrak{G}=({\mathcal{G}}\times\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}}),\widetilde{\omega})\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}}),

where
\tilde{\omega}
is the tautological
pr_{S({\mathcal{G}})}
-foliated
2
-form on
\mathfrak{G}={\mathcal{G}}\times S({\mathcal{G}})
with values
\tilde{\omega}(g,\omega)=\omega(g)
. The fibre
pr_{\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})}^{-1}(\omega)
of this family over
\omega\in\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})
is equal to
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
.

By analogy with the case of complex manifolds, the Kodaira-Spencer map of this family is a map
KS:T_{\omega}\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
; given a smooth curve
\gamma:I\to\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})
, define
KS(\dot{\gamma}(0))
to be the deformation class of the deformation
\textbf{p}:\gamma^{*}\mathfrak{G}\to I
.
Since
\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})
is an open subset of the vector space
\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{mult,cl}}({\mathcal{G}})
, there is a natural identification
T_{\omega}\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})\cong\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{mult,cl}}({\mathcal{G}})
. Under this identification, the Kodaira-Spencer map
KS
corresponds to
\kappa
.

For a compact symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
, taking the analogy further, a germ of a neighbourhood of
0
in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
plays the role of what is called a Kuranishi space (cf.
[
13
, Def. 4]
, see also Corollary 9 in
loc. cit.
) because infinitesimal deformations of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
are unobstructed, as the proof of Theorem
7.8
shows.

Remark 7.10

(Other versions of the Moser theorem)
.

It also makes sense to study deformations of multiplicative symplectic forms on a fixed Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
, up to a stricter equivalence relation where triviality is given by paths of symplectic groupoid isomorphisms (i.e., not allowing for cohomologically trivial gauge transformations as equivalences). In this case, the correct deformation complex to consider would be
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{mult}}({\mathcal{G}}),d_{dR})
, and a Moser theorem in that setting is detailed in
[
12
]
. It is similar to Theorem
7.6
in the sense that when
{\mathcal{G}}
is compact, deformations will be trivial if and only if their deformation classes in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mult}}({\mathcal{G}})
are smoothly exact.
This in turn will lead to a similar correspondence to that of Theorem
7.8
, of the form

\mathcal{U}/\sim\ \ \longleftrightarrow\ \ \mathcal{V}\subset H^{2}_{\mathrm{mult}}({\mathcal{G}}),

for neighbourhoods
\mathcal{U}
of
\omega
in
\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{G}})
and
\mathcal{V}
of
0
in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mult}}({\mathcal{G}})
. Here
\sim
denotes the equivalence relation generated by convex isotopies, where
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{1}
are convex-isotopic if the form
\omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega_{0}+{\varepsilon}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0})
is symplectic for all
{\varepsilon}\in[0,1]
, and there is an isotopy
F_{\varepsilon}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
such that each
F_{\varepsilon}
is a Lie groupoid isomorphism satisfying
F_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega_{0}
.

Remark 7.11
.

Any deformation
\textbf{p}:(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}\rightrightarrows\tilde{M})\to(I\rightrightarrows I)
of a compact Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
is a trivial deformation
[
25
, Thm. 7.4, Rem. 7.9]
. If moreover
p
is proper, then the entire family
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
is trivial, without the need to restrict to a smaller interval
J\subset I
.
This means that any deformation
({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
of a compact symplectic groupoid is equivalent to one of the form
({\mathcal{G}},\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon})
, and in particular
s
-constant, for
{\varepsilon}
small enough. Thus, by Proposition
7.1
, any deformation class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
is represented by a cocycle of the form
0\oplus\dot{\omega}_{0}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})\oplus C^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\omega)
, and has also a corresponding cocycle in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mult}}({\mathcal{G}})
.

Therefore, if the space of Lie groupoid structures on a compact manifold
{\mathcal{G}}
(or perhaps having an underlying submersion
s:{\mathcal{G}}\to M
as source) were locally path-connected, then the correspondences of Theorem
7.8
and of Remark
7.10
can be upgraded. They would give local descriptions not only of moduli spaces of multiplicative symplectic forms on
{\mathcal{G}}
, but of symplectic groupoids structures on the manifold
{\mathcal{G}}
(respectively, with underlying source
s:{\mathcal{G}}\to M
). Although we do not know if the space of Lie groupoid structures on
{\mathcal{G}}
is locally path-connected, in this direction we note that the space of actions of a compact Lie group on a compact manifold is, indeed, locally path-connected
[
68
]
.

8.
The deformation class of general deformations

In this section we show how general deformations of symplectic groupoids can also be handled in an infinitesimal way.

8.1.
Defining the deformation class

In order to describe the deformation class of a deformation
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
we first reinterpret the definition of the deformation cocycle in the case of
s
-constant deformations
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}={\mathcal{G}}\times I,\omega_{\varepsilon})
. The deformation cocycle
\eta_{\varepsilon}=\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\dot{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
can be alternatively expressed as

(13)

\eta_{\varepsilon}=\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}(\partial/\partial{\varepsilon}),\cdot))_{|_{{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}_{\varepsilon}}}-(\mathcal{L}_{\partial/\partial{\varepsilon}}\omega)_{|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}},

where
\partial/\partial{\varepsilon}
is the vector field on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
whose flow
F_{\varepsilon}
is
(g,{\lambda})\mapsto(g,{\lambda}+{\varepsilon})
, and

\sigma:pr_{1}^{*}T^{*}\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}\to T^{*}\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(2)}

is the map of vector bundles over
{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}
defined on Remark
4.4
by
\sigma=(dpr_{1})^{*}
.

Note also that
\omega
is a 2-form on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
which restricts to
\omega_{\varepsilon}
over the fibre
{\mathcal{G}}\times\{{\varepsilon}\}
.
This alternative description of the deformation cocycle
can be extended in such a way that the deformation
class
still makes sense for a general deformation.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
be a deformation of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathcal{G}})
, and let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}})
be a transverse vector field; that is, a vector field on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
which projects by
\textbf{p}\circ\tilde{s}
to the vector field
\partial/\partial{\varepsilon}
on
I
. Then,

(14)

\eta_{\varepsilon}:=\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,\cdot))|_{{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}_{\varepsilon}}-(\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega)|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}\ \in\ \Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{(2)})\oplus\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}).

defines a family of cocycles
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})
.
Moreover, the cohomology class
[\eta_{0}]
at time zero does not depend on the choice of the transverse vector field
X
.

Definition 8.2
.

The resulting cohomology class
[\eta_{0}]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathcal{G}})
is called
the deformation class
associated to the deformation
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
.

Remark 8.3
.

Equivalently, the family of cocycles
\eta_{\varepsilon}
can be viewed as a single cocycle
\eta
on the “
foliated version
”
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}
of
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
. That is, on the mapping cone complex of the leafwise de Rham differential
d_{\mathcal{F}}:\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(\bullet)})_{\mathcal{F}}\to\Omega^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(\bullet)})
. Here, to shorten the notation we let
\mathcal{F}
refer to the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}^{(n)}
on each
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(n)}
with leaves
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}
, induced by the foliation on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
previously denoted by
{\mathcal{F}}
, having the fibres
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
as leaves. The space
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(\bullet)})_{\mathcal{F}}
is the space of the foliated forms corresponding to forms in
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(\bullet)})
. Explicitly, we have

(15)

\eta=\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,\cdot))|_{\mathcal{F}^{(2)}}-(\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega)|_{\mathcal{F}}\ \ \in\ \Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(2)})_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}).

Proof of Proposition

8.1

Let
{\mathcal{M}}:\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(2)}\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(2)}
be the diffeomorphism defined by
{\mathcal{M}}(g,h)=(M(g,h),i(h))
, where
M
is the multiplication of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
.

On the one hand, notice that

\displaystyle M^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega

\displaystyle={\mathcal{M}}^{*}pr_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega

\displaystyle={\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{pr_{1}}}pr_{1}^{*}\omega,\ \ \ \ \text{where }\ \ X^{pr_{1}}_{(g,h)}:=(X_{g},(i_{*}X)_{h})\in T_{(g,h)}\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}pr_{1}^{*}\omega,\ \ \ \ \text{where }\ \ X^{M}_{(g,h)}\ :=({\mathcal{M}}_{*}^{-1}X^{pr_{1}})_{(g,h)}

\displaystyle\hskip 156.49014pt=(X_{gh}\cdot(i_{*}X)_{h^{-1}},X_{h})\in T_{(g,h)}\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}M^{*}\omega

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}pr_{1}^{*}\omega+{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}p_{2}^{*}\omega.

That is,

\displaystyle(M^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega)_{(g,h)}

\displaystyle=\big({\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}pr_{1}^{*}\omega+{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}p_{2}^{*}\omega\big)_{(g,h)}

\displaystyle=(d{\iota}_{X^{M}}pr_{1}^{*}\omega)_{(g,h)}+(d_{(g,h)}pr_{1})^{*}\big(d\omega(X_{gh}\cdot di(X_{h}),-,-)\big)

\displaystyle+({\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}p_{2}^{*}\omega)_{(g,h)}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(pr_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega)_{(g,h)}

\displaystyle=(pr_{1}^{*}{\iota}_{X}d\omega)_{(g,h)}+(d\;pr_{1}^{*}{\iota}_{X}\omega)_{(g,h)}

\displaystyle=(d_{(g,h)}pr_{1})^{*}d\omega(X_{g},-,-)+(d\;pr_{1}^{*}{\iota}_{X}\omega)_{(g,h)}

and

p_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega={\mathcal{L}}_{X^{M}}p_{2}^{*}\omega.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(pr_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega

\displaystyle-M^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega+p_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\omega)_{(g,h)}=

\displaystyle=(d_{(g,h)}pr_{1})^{*}d\omega(X_{g},-,-)+(d\;pr_{1}^{*}{\iota}_{X}\omega)_{(g,h)}-(d{\iota}_{X^{M}}pr_{1}^{*}\omega)_{(g,h)}

\displaystyle-(d_{(g,h)}pr_{1})^{*}\big(d\omega(X_{gh}\cdot di(X_{h}),-,-)\big)

\displaystyle=(d_{(g,h)}pr_{1})^{*}d\omega(X_{g}-X_{gh}\cdot di(X_{h}),-,-)+\big(d(pr_{1}^{*}{\iota}_{X}\omega-{\iota}_{X^{M}}pr_{1}^{*}\omega)\big)_{(g,h)}

\displaystyle=\big({\iota}_{(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,0)}pr_{1}^{*}d\omega\big)_{(g,h)}+\big(d{\iota}_{(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,0)}pr_{1}^{*}\omega\big)_{(g,h)}.

Thus, since
(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X)_{(g,h)}
is tangent to the fibres of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
and each restriction
\omega_{\varepsilon}\equiv\omega|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}
is a closed 2-form, then

\left.\big({\iota}_{(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,0)}pr_{1}^{*}d\omega\big)\right|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}=0,

for all
{\varepsilon}
, which proves the proposition.

\square

Remark 8.4
.

Alternatively, since the tangent lift map
\oplus^{2}T_{\mathrm{def}}
takes transversal vector fields to transversal vector fields, Proposition
8.1
can also be proved in terms of deformations of Lie groupoid morphisms by using the results of
[

.

8.2.
Triviality of deformations with exact class

In this section we generalize Theorem
7.6
to general deformations of compact symplectic groupoids.

Theorem 8.5
.

Let
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
be a deformation of a compact symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathcal{G}})
and let
\eta=\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,\cdot))|_{\mathcal{F}^{(2)}}-(\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega)|_{\mathcal{F}}
be the deformation cocycle in the foliated cohomology
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}
, associated to the deformation via a transverse vector field
X
on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Then,
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
is trivial if and only if
\eta
is exact.

The proof of this result will be based on the following lemma.

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
be a deformation of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathcal{G}})
as in the theorem above. Then, the associated foliated form
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
induces the isomorphism

C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}})_{{\mathcal{F}}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{(\bullet)})_{\mathcal{F}},\ \xi\mapsto-\sigma(\omega(\xi,\cdot))_{{\mathcal{F}}}=-\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}(\omega_{\mathcal{F}}(\xi,\cdot)).

Under this isomorphism, the image
\mathcal{Y}=-i_{Y}\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
of an element
Y\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}})_{{\mathcal{F}}}
satisfies

d_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{Y}=-(\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega)_{{\mathcal{F}}},\ \text{and }\ \delta_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{Y}=-\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}Y,\cdot))_{\mathcal{F}}.

Proof.

Checking that
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
induces an isomorphism is done by essentially the same arguments as those for Proposition
4.1
.

Now,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle=(-d{\iota}_{Y}\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=(-d{\iota}_{Y}\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}+\underbrace{(-{\iota}_{Y}d\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}}_{=0}

\displaystyle=-(\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega)_{\mathcal{F}};

where the second equality is due to the fact that
\omega
is
d_{\mathcal{F}}
-closed. Moreover,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{\mathcal{F}}({\iota}_{Y}\omega_{\mathcal{F}})=-\delta_{\mathcal{F}}({\iota}_{Y}\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}=-(\delta{\iota}_{Y}\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=(\delta(\omega^{b})^{*}(Y))_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=((\omega^{b})^{*}(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}Y))_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=-\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}Y,\cdot))_{\mathcal{F}}.\qed

Proof of Theorem

8.5

If
\eta
is exact, then there exists
\alpha\oplus\mathcal{Y}\in\Omega^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(\widetilde{M})\oplus\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}})_{{\mathcal{F}}}
such that

\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,\cdot))|_{\mathcal{F}^{(2)}}=\delta\mathcal{Y}

and

(16)

-(\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega)|_{\mathcal{F}}=-\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha)-d_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{Y}.

Since the foliated 2-form
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
induces the isomorphism
C^{*}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}})_{{\mathcal{F}}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}}^{(*)})_{\mathcal{F}}
(see previous Lemma), it follows that
\mathcal{Y}=-i_{Y}\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
for some vector field
Y
on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
which is tangent to the fibres
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Therefore, the first equation is equivalent to

(17)

\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}X,\cdot))|_{\mathcal{F}^{(2)}}=-\sigma(\omega(\delta_{\mathrm{def}}Y,\cdot))|_{\mathcal{F}^{(2)}},

and equation (
16
) becomes

(18)

(\mathcal{L}_{X+Y}\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}=\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha).

Thus, equation (
17
) says that
X+Y
is a multiplicative and transverse vector field on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Let us denote by
F_{\varepsilon}
the flow of
X+Y
on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Then the equation (
18
) is equivalent to

\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}F_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=F_{{\varepsilon}}^{*}\delta_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\alpha\right).

Denote by
(\Phi_{\varepsilon},\phi_{\varepsilon})
the family of restricted morphisms
F_{\varepsilon}|_{{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}}:{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\to{{\mathcal{G}}}_{\varepsilon}
. Then, by restricting the previous equation to the fibre
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}(\delta_{{{\mathcal{G}}}_{\varepsilon}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}))

\displaystyle=\delta_{{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\alpha_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\delta_{{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}}(\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\bar{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\delta_{{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}}(\bar{\alpha}_{\varepsilon});

where
\bar{\alpha}_{\tau}:=\int_{0}^{{\tau}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\alpha_{\varepsilon})d{\varepsilon}
is the primitive of the curve
{\varepsilon}\mapsto\phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\alpha_{\varepsilon}
. Hence, the sequence of equalities tells us that the expression
\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon}-\delta_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}}(\bar{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})
is constant in
{\varepsilon}
. Thus,

\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega_{0}+\delta_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}}(\bar{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}).

Therefore, if
\Phi:{\mathcal{G}}\times I\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
is the isomorphism determined by the family
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{\alpha}\in\Omega^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(M\times I)_{\mathrm{cl}}
is the closed foliated 2-form which equals
\bar{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}
when restricted to the fibre
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
, then

(\Phi^{*}\omega)_{\mathcal{F}}=(pr_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{0})_{\mathcal{F}}+\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{\alpha}),

where
pr_{\mathcal{G}}:{\mathcal{G}}\times I\to{\mathcal{G}}
is the obvious projection to
{\mathcal{G}}
. This proves the triviality of the deformation
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}},\omega)
.

\square

Remark 8.7
.

Alternatively, Theorem
8.5
could be deduced from Theorem

9.3.
Zero Poisson structures

Let us focus on the source
1
-connected symplectic groupoid
(T^{*}M,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\rightrightarrows(M,0)
integrating the zero Poisson structure on
M
. In this case, the nerve is
T^{*}M^{(n)}=\oplus^{n}T^{*}M
, so
H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^{*}M^{(n)})\cong H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(M)
, for all
k,n\geq 0
. The source fibres are contractible, being vector spaces.
We also note that for the zero Poisson bivector
\pi=0
, the differential of the Poisson complex vanishes, so
H^{\bullet}_{\pi}(M,0)=\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)
.
Thus, using Corollary
9.3
, we come to the following result.

Corollary 9.5
.

Let
M
be a homologically
2
-connected manifold. Then

H^{i}_{\mathrm{def}}(T^{*}M,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\cong H^{i}_{\pi}(M,0)\cong\mathfrak{X}^{i}(M)

for
i\geq 1
, and
H^{0}(T^{*}M,\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\cong C^{\infty}(M)/\mathbb{R}
.

9.4.
Poisson-Lie groups

A Poisson-Lie group is a Lie group
G
equipped with a Poisson structure
\pi_{G}
for which the multiplication
m:G\times G\to G
is a Poisson map, where
G\times G
is equipped with the product Poisson structure. This amounts to the bivector
\pi_{G}
being
multiplicative
. Multiplicativity of
\pi_{G}
implies that it vanishes at the identity
e\in G
, so its linearization at
e
induces a linear Poisson structure on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore it induces a Lie algebra structure on the dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The dual Lie group of
G
is then the
1
-connected Lie group integrating the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and is denoted by
G^{*}
. It is also a Poisson-Lie group, which we denote
(G^{*},\pi_{G^{*}})
.

For each
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, denote by
\xi^{l}
and
\xi^{r}
the left-invariant and the right-invariant 1-forms on
G
with value
\xi
at the identity, respectively. The maps

\lambda,\rho:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{X}(G),\ \ \ \lambda(\xi)=\pi_{G}^{\sharp}(\xi^{l}),\ \ \ \rho(\xi)=-\pi_{G}^{\sharp}(\xi^{r})

define the left and right infinitesimal dressing actions of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
on
G
, respectively.
We say that a multiplicative Poisson tensor
\pi_{G}
on
G
is complete if each left (or, equivalently, each right) dressing vector field is complete on
G
.

In
[
54
]
, Lu and Weinstein have constructed, for any Poisson-Lie group
G
, a symplectic groupoid
\Gamma
integrating
G
, with a second compatible groupoid structure on
\Gamma
making it into a symplectic groupoid integrating
G^{*}
. In general
\Gamma
is a submanifold of
G\times G^{*}\times G^{*}\times G
. In the simplest case, when
(G,\pi_{G})
is a simply connected complete Poisson-Lie group,
\Gamma
is
G\times G^{*}
, which is in this case also diffeomorphic to the double group
D
of
G
; the groupoid structures on
D
are in fact action Lie groupoid structures, for actions of
G
and
G^{*}
on each other obtained by slightly modifying the dressing actions (see
[
52
, Section 4.1]
).

Corollary 9.6
.

Let
(G,\pi_{G})
be a simply connected complete Poisson-Lie group with dual Lie group
G^{*}
and 1-connected integration denoted by
(D,\omega)\rightrightarrows(G,\pi_{G})
.

Then there are isomorphisms

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{0}(D,\omega)\cong H^{0}_{\pi_{G}}(G)/\mathbb{R},\ \ \ H_{\mathrm{def}}^{i}(D,\omega)\cong H^{i}_{\pi_{G}}(G),

for
i=1,2
, and an injective map

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{3}(D,\omega)\to H^{3}_{\pi_{G}}(G).

Proof.

Both
G
and
G^{*}
are
2
-connected, and so
D\rightrightarrows G
has
2
-connected base
G
and source fibres (diffeomorphic to
G^{*}
). The source map of
D
is a fibration, since
D
is an action groupoid. Therefore,
D\rightrightarrows G
satisfies the conditions of Corollary
9.3
with
k=2
.
∎

Exchanging the roles of
G
and
G^{*}
and bearing in mind that a Poisson-Lie group
G
is complete if and only if
G^{*}
is complete
[
52
, Prop. 2.42]
, one obtains an analogous result for the symplectic groupoid structure of
D
over
G^{*}
.
When
G
is equipped with the zero Poisson structure, then
G^{*}
is
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
seen as an abelian group, with the linear Poisson structure, and
D=T^{*}G
; so in this sense Corollary
9.6
recovers Proposition
9.1
for complete
G
. Other interesting, and non-trivial, Poisson-Lie group structures have been constructed, for example for connected compact semisimple
G

[
55
]
.

9.5.
Cotangent VB-groupoids

For any cotangent VB-groupoid
T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows A^{*}
of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
, the nerve
(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}})^{(\bullet)}
is a simplicial vector bundle over the nerve of
{\mathcal{G}}
. Moreover, the core exact sequence of
T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}
(dual to sequence
(2)
in Remark
2.15
) implies that the source fibres of
T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}
are affine bundles over the source fibres of
{\mathcal{G}}
. These facts together with Corollary
9.3
lead to the following result.

Corollary 9.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a Lie groupoid such that
{\mathcal{G}}^{(n)}
is homologically
2
-connected for all
n\geq 0
and such that its source map
s
has homologically
k
-connected fibres. Then there are isomorphisms

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{0}(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\cong H^{0}_{\pi}(A^{*})/\mathbb{R},\ \ \ H_{\mathrm{def}}^{i}(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\cong H^{i}_{\pi}(A^{*}),

for
1\leq i\leq k
, and an injective map

H_{\mathrm{def}}^{k+1}(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathrm{can}})\to H^{k+1}_{\pi}(A^{*}).

Remark 9.8
.

The results of this section indicate that at the infinitesimal level, the deformation theory of a symplectic groupoid
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
with homologically
2-
connected nerve and source fibres is very closely related with that of
(M,\pi)
itself. And moreover, that this situation occurs in many natural examples.

Both
H_{\mathrm{def}}^{2}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
and
H^{2}_{\pi}(M)
encode infinitesimal deformations modulo trivial deformations, while
H_{\mathrm{def}}^{1}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
and
H^{1}_{\pi}(M)
encode infinitesimal automorphisms modulo trivial ones.
The missing piece, from the point of view of deformation theory, is relating obstructions. On the Poisson side,
H^{3}_{\pi}(M)
encodes obstructions to extending an infinitesimal deformation to a formal one. This interpretation uses the Schouten-Nijenhuis differential graded Lie algebra (DGLA) structure on
\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)
.

There is a well known principle of deformation theory due to Deligne
[
33
]
and Drinfeld
[
34
]
, and in an equivalent form, to Schlessinger and Stasheff
[
70
]
, (now formalized as a theorem in derived deformation theory
[
56
,
69
]
) and explored further by many others; it states that every reasonable formal deformation problem in characteristic zero is controlled by a DGLA
\mathfrak{g}
. Equivalence classes of infinitesimal deformations are given by elements
[c]\in H^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
; obstructions to extending
c
to a formal deformation are detected by a squaring map
[\cdot,\cdot]:H^{2}(\mathfrak{g)}\to H^{3}(\mathfrak{g})
(degrees may vary according to the index convention for
\mathfrak{g}
).

It is not known to date how to describe such a DGLA (nor an
L_{\infty}
-algebra) structure on the deformation complex of a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
(not even when
{\mathcal{G}}
is a Lie group!). The work of
[
67
]
points to its existence, although in a non-constructive way, via an interpretation of deformations of Lie groupoids as deformations of certain kinds of diagrams of
C^{\infty}
-schemes. Similarly, we do not know how to describe such a structure for
C_{\mathrm{def}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
.
It is our hope that understanding better the close relation between the deformation complexes of
{\mathcal{G}}
and of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
with known DGLA’s (such as
\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)
, as in this section, or the deformation complex of a Lie algebroid, in the next) may help in eventually finding an explicit description of the DGLA (or
L_{\infty}
-algebra) structures on these complexes.

In any case, if
H^{3}_{\mathrm{def}}({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
does encode obstructions to extending an infinitesimal deformation of a symplectic groupoid to a formal one, it is reasonable to expect that the map of Proposition
9.1
sends the obstruction for an infinitesimal deformation
[\eta]
of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
to the obstruction of a corresponding infinitesimal deformation
[\Lambda]
of Poisson structures on the base. If that is the case, injectivity would say that if
[\Lambda]
is unobstructed, then so is
[\eta]
. On the other hand, if
[\eta]
is the deformation class associated to an actual deformation of
({\mathcal{G}},\omega)
then the corresponding infinitesimal deformation
[\Lambda]
of Poisson structures should also be unobstructed.

The map between differentiable and deformation cohomologies

In the previous section we have explored the relation between deformation cohomology and Poisson cohomology for symplectic groupoids with somewhat simple topology. We now turn our attention to what can be said about the relation between the deformation cohomology of any symplectic groupoid

and other related cohomologies, such as

and Poisson cohomology of the base.

Let

be a Poisson manifold and let

be its associated Lie algebroid. In the study of deformation of Lie algebroids of

In this section we construct the global counterpart

of the map

We collect in the appendix some material, including definitions, on double vector bundles and VB-algebroids (vector bundle objects in the category of Lie algebroids), that is of use in the proofs of this section.

10.1.
Deformation cohomology of Lie algebroids

We recall the deformation complex of a Lie algebroid
A
, denoted by
(C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{def}}(A),\delta)
and defined in

]
; we recall also its interpretation in terms of VB-algebroids.

A derivation on a vector bundle

is a linear operator
D:\Gamma(E)\longrightarrow\Gamma(E)
such that there exists a vector field
\sigma_{D}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, called the symbol of
D
, which satisfies

D(fs)=fD(s)+\sigma_{D}(f)s,\ \text{for }s\in\Gamma(E)\text{ and }f\in C^{\infty}(M).

A
multiderivation of degree

n
on
E
is a multilinear and antisymmetric map

D:\underbrace{\Gamma(E)\times\cdots\times\Gamma(E)}_{n+1\ \mathrm{times}}\longrightarrow\Gamma(E)

which is a derivation in each entry, i.e., there is a
symbol map

\sigma_{D}:\underbrace{\Gamma(E)\times\cdots\times\Gamma(E)}_{n\ \mathrm{times}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)

which is
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear in each entry and satisfies

D(s_{0},s_{1},...,fs_{n})=fD(s_{0},...,s_{n})+\sigma_{D}(s_{0},...,s_{n-1})(f)s_{n},

for
s_{i}\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M).

The space of multiderivations of degree
n
is often denoted by
Der^{n}(E)
. One sets
Der^{-1}(E)=\Gamma(E)
.

The
deformation complex of
a Lie algebroid

is the complex for which the space of
k
-cochains

consists of the multiderivations of degree

on

, i.e.,

, with differential given by the ’de Rham type’ formula

\begin{split}\delta(D)(\alpha_{0},...,\alpha_{k})=\Sigma_{i}(-1)^{i}[\alpha_{i},&D(\alpha_{0},...,\hat{\alpha}_{i},...,\alpha_{k})]+\\
&+\Sigma_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}D([\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}],\alpha_{0},...,\hat{\alpha}_{i},...,\hat{\alpha}_{j},...,\alpha_{k}).\end{split}

VB-algebroid complex

For a VB-algebroid

over

, the

of

of

(the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of

as the

-multilinear and alternating functions

; a

-cochain is called

if it is fibrewise linear with respect to the vector bundle structure of

(cf.
[

]
, see also
[

]
).

Remark 10.1
.

As pointed out in (

In fact, given any vector bundle

and

The map
i

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold. We now revisit the cochain map

An expression for the multiderivation

(

\begin{split}(R_{X_{1}}&R_{X_{2}}(Tc))_{(w_{x})}=\\

Consider now the tangent prolongation DVB of
A\longrightarrow M
. The natural pairing (
29
) existing between its (horizontal and vertical) duals induces the isomorphism

Z_{A}:T^{*}A=(TA)^{*}_{A}\longrightarrow((TA)^{*}_{TM})^{*}_{A^{*}}.

Composing such an isomorphism with the dual
(I_{A})^{*}_{A^{*}}
of
I_{A}
over
A^{*}
, one obtains the following isomorphism of DVBs

(I_{A})^{*}_{A^{*}}\circ Z_{A}:T^{*}A\longrightarrow T^{*}A^{*}

which (like
Z_{A}
) induces
-id_{A}
on the side bundles
A
and preserves the cores
T^{*}M
and sides
A^{*}
.
The
reversal isomorphism

R_{A}:T^{*}A^{*}\longrightarrow T^{*}A
is then defined by
R_{A}:=((I_{A})^{*}_{A^{*}}\circ Z_{A})^{-1}\circ(-_{A^{*}}id_{T^{*}A^{*}})
, which will be then an isomorphism of DVBs preserving the sides and inducing
-id_{T^{*}M}
on the cores.

Alternatively, it is possible to describe this map in a simple way by using local coordinates.
Let
(x^{i},u_{d})
and
(x^{i},u^{d})
be (fibred) local coordinates of the vector bundles
A\longrightarrow M
and
A^{*}\longrightarrow M
. Let then
(x^{i},u_{d},\dot{x}^{i},\dot{u}_{d})
denote the tangent coordinates on
TA
, where
(\dot{x}^{i})
are the coordinates of elements in
T_{x}M
and
(\dot{u}_{d})
the ones of tangent elements to the fibre
A_{x}
of
A\to M
, which identify with elements of
A_{x}
and therefore are also called the
core
coordinates of
TA
. Similarly, the vertical and horizontal duals of
TA
has a local description. Denote by
(x^{i},u_{d},\delta x^{i},\delta u_{d})
the cotangent coordinates on
T^{*}A
, and let
(x^{i},\zeta_{d},\dot{x}^{i},\eta_{d})
be the coordinates on the dual

\zeta_{d}

\eta_{d}

\dot{u}^{d}

(I_{A})^{*}_{A^{*}}:(T^{\bullet}A)^{*}_{A^{*}}\longrightarrow(TA^{*})^{*}_{A^{*}}

(x^{i},\alpha^{d},\beta_{i},\eta_{d})\mapsto(x^{i},\eta_{d},\beta_{i},\alpha^{d});

(x^{i},u^{d},\delta x^{i},\delta u^{d})\mapsto(x^{i},-u^{d},\delta x^{i},\delta u^{d}).

R_{A}(x^{i},u^{d},\delta x^{i},\delta u^{d})=(x^{i},\delta u^{d},-\delta x^{i},u^{d}).

Remark A.7
.

Consider two vector bundles
A\longrightarrow M_{1}
and
B\longrightarrow M_{2}
, and a vector bundle morphism
\Phi:A\longrightarrow B
covering the diffeomorphism
f:M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}
. Notice that the pointwise dual of
\Phi
induces a natural morphism
\Phi^{\star}_{f^{-1}}:B^{*}\longrightarrow A^{*}
between the duals of
B
and
A
covering the diffeomorphism
f^{-1}
.

We remark that such a dual construction can also be made for morphisms of DVBs which share one of their side bundles. For instance, let
f:A\longrightarrow B
be an isomorphism, then one has the dual of the DVB morphism
Tf:TA\longrightarrow TB
(which covers the isomorphism
f
), denoted by
(Tf)^{\star}_{f^{-1}}:T^{*}B\longrightarrow T^{*}A
.

.

A
and

.

The reversal isomorphism
R_{A}
satisfies some additional properties related to symplectic geometry: to begin with, it is an anti-symplectomorphism with respect to the canonical symplectic structures on the cotangent bundles
T^{*}A^{*}
and
T^{*}A
(
[
59
]
, Thm 9.5.2)). Another property involving the canonical symplectic structure on
T^{*}M
is given in Proposition
A.11
below.

Example A.10
.

(Cotangent Lie algebroid)
The reversal isomorphism allows to make sense of the so called
cotangent Lie algebroid
. It is well known that a Lie algebroid
A\longrightarrow M
induces a Poisson structure on its dual
A^{*}\longrightarrow M
, which is linear with respect to the vector bundle structure over
M
(
[
18
]
, Thm. 2.1.4). In this way, the cotangent bundle of
A
,

T^{*}A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R_{A}^{-1}}}{{\cong}}T^{*}A^{*}\longrightarrow A^{*}

inherits a Lie algebroid structure, which is also a VB-algebroid over
A\longrightarrow M
. Such a VB-algebroid is also often defined as the
dual VB-algebroid
of
TA
(see Section 3 in
[
40
]
).

For a Lie groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
with Lie algebroid
A_{\mathcal{G}}
, the tangent and cotangent algebroids of
A_{\mathcal{G}}
are (isomorphic to) the Lie algebroids of the tangent and cotangent groupoids (section
2.7
) of
{\mathcal{G}}
, respectively. In fact, the canonical flip
J_{\mathcal{G}}:T(T{\mathcal{G}})\longrightarrow T(T{\mathcal{G}})
restricts to the isomorphism of Lie algebroids

TA_{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j_{\mathcal{G}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A_{T{\mathcal{G}}}

over
TM
(
[
59
]
, Thm 9.7.5). And the dual of the map
J_{\mathcal{G}}
gives rise to the isomorphism between the Lie algebroid of
T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}
and the cotangent algebroid. Explicitly, the isomorphism

\Theta_{T{\mathcal{G}}}:=J_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\circ I_{T{\mathcal{G}}}:T(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}})\longrightarrow(T(T{\mathcal{G}}))^{*}_{TM}\longrightarrow T^{*}(T{\mathcal{G}})

given by the composition of the internalization map and the dual of the involution induces the isomorphism (
[
59
]
, p. 463)

A_{T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta_{\mathcal{G}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{*}A_{{\mathcal{G}}}.

The map
\Theta_{T{\mathcal{G}}}
is often called the
Tulczyjew map
, and it is closely related to the canonical symplectic structure
\omega_{\mathrm{can}}
on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proposition A.11
.

(
[
59
]
, Thm 9.6.7)
Given a manifold
M
, the Tulczyjew and reversal isomorphisms
\Theta_{TM}
and
R_{TM}
are related by the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(T^{*}M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}(TM)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}(T^{*}M),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{\mathrm{can}}^{b}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta_{TM}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{TM}}

where
\omega_{\mathrm{can}}\in\Omega^{2}(T^{*}M)
is the canonical symplectic structure on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
.

Remark A.12
.

In the case of a Poisson manifold
M
, the Tulczyjew map of
M

\Theta_{TM}:T(T^{*}M)\longrightarrow T^{*}(TM)
has an additional property: it is an isomorphism of VB-algebroids (i.e., it is an isomorphism of DVBs which preserves the Lie algebroid structures involved), between the tangent algebroid and the Lie algebroid associated to the linear Poisson structure on

A.3.
Tangent lift of algebroid cochains

Just as there is a tangent lift of Lie groupoid cochains, there is an infinitesimal version, allowing to lift algebroid cochains from a Lie algebroid
A
to the algebroid
TA
. Let us recall its definition, and detail some of its properties.

Definition A.13
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid. The
tangent lift of algebroid cochains
is the map

T:C^{\bullet}(A)\to C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lin}}(TA)

defined as follows.
An algebroid
k
-cochain
c\in C^{k}(A)=\Gamma(\Lambda^{k}A^{*})
can be regarded as a
k
-linear and skew-symmetric map
c:\bigoplus^{k}A\to\mathbb{R}
. We define its tangent lift
Tc\in C^{k}_{\mathrm{lin}}(TA)
by

Tc(v_{1},...v_{k}):=dc(v_{1},...,v_{k}),

where
(v_{1},...,v_{k})\in\bigoplus^{k}_{TM}TA
, and using the identification
T(\bigoplus^{k}A)\cong\bigoplus^{k}_{TM}TA
.

Lemma A.14
.

Let
k
be a positive integer. The tangent lift of Lie algebroid cochains
T:C^{k}(A)\longrightarrow C^{k}(TA)
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

(Linear sections)

\displaystyle\left.Tc(T\alpha_{1},...,T\alpha_{k})\right|_{w_{x}}

\displaystyle=(Tc)_{w_{x}}\left(T\alpha_{1}(w_{x}),...,T\alpha_{k}(w_{x})\right)=

\displaystyle=\left[T\left(c(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k})\right)\right](w_{x}),

(2)

(One core section)
\left.Tc(T\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k})\right|_{w_{x}}=\left.c(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k})\right|_{x}
,

(3)

(More than one core sections)
\left.Tc(T\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k-1},\hat{\alpha}_{k})\right|_{w_{x}}=0
.

Proof.

Part 1 is direct. For simplicity, we prove the parts 2 and 3 for
k=2
, the general case
(k\neq 2)
being completely analogous.

\begin{split}\left.Tc(T\alpha_{1},\hat{\alpha}_{2})\right|_{w_{x}}&=(Tc)_{(w_{x})}(T\alpha_{1}(w_{x}),\hat{\alpha}_{2}(w_{x}))\\
&=(Tc)_{(w_{x})}\left(\overbrace{T\alpha_{1}(w_{x})}^{\in\ T_{\alpha_{1}(x)}A},T0^{A}(w_{x})+_{A}(\alpha_{2}^{\uparrow})_{0^{A}_{x}}\right)\\
&=\underbrace{(Tc)_{(w_{x})}\left(T\alpha_{1}(w_{x}),T0^{A}(w_{x})\right)}_{=0}+\\
&+(Tc)_{(0^{A}_{x})}\left(\tilde{0}^{A}_{\alpha_{1}(x)},(\alpha_{2}^{\uparrow})_{0^{A}_{x}}\right)\ \ \ \ (\text{Linearity of }Tc\text{ over}\oplus^{2}_{M}A)\\
&=\left.\frac{d}{d{\lambda}}\right|_{{\lambda}=0}c(\alpha_{1}(x),{\lambda}\alpha_{2}(x))\\
&=\left.c(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\right|_{(x)}\ \ \ \ (\text{Bilinearity of }c\ (\text{over }M)),\end{split}

where in the third equality the first term vanishes by multilinearity of
Tc
with respect to the vector bundle
TA\longrightarrow TM
.

Finally, considering more than one core section:

\begin{split}\left.Tc(\hat{\alpha}_{1},\hat{\alpha}_{2})\right|_{w_{x}}&=Tc(T0^{A}(w_{x})+_{A}(\alpha_{1}^{\uparrow})_{0^{A}_{x}},T0^{A}(w_{x})+_{A}(\alpha_{2}^{\uparrow})_{0^{A}_{x}})\\
&=Tc(T0^{A}(w_{x}),T0^{A}(w_{x}))+Tc((\alpha_{1}^{\uparrow})_{0^{A}_{x}},(\alpha_{2}^{\uparrow})_{0^{A}_{x}})\\
&=0+\left.\frac{d}{d{\lambda}}\right|_{{\lambda}=0}c({\lambda}\alpha_{1}(x),{\lambda}\alpha_{2}(x))\\
&=\left.\frac{d}{d{\lambda}}\right|_{{\lambda}=0}{\lambda}^{2}\cdot c(\alpha_{1}(x),\alpha_{2}(x))\\
&=0,\end{split}

where in the third equality
Tc(T0^{A}(w_{x}),T0^{A}(w_{x}))=0
by multilinearity of
Tc
with respect to the vector bundle
TA\longrightarrow TM
.

To extend the proof to the case
k\neq 2
, one again uses the linearity of
Tc
to get a sum of a vanishing term with a simpler expression in
c
and sections of
A_{\mathcal{G}}
. ∎

Lemma A.15
.

Let
A
be a Lie algebroid over
M
. The tangent lift is a cochain complex map

T:C^{\bullet}(A)\longrightarrow C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lin}}(TA)\subset C^{\bullet}(TA).

Proof.

We divide this proof in three cases, by evaluating the cochain
\delta(Tc)
on tangent and core sections of
TA\to TM
, which together span all sections of the tangent algebroid (example
A.4
).

First we remark some useful facts about the anchor
\rho_{TA}
of
TA
and the image of core and tangent sections by the anchor. Recall that
\rho_{TA}=J_{M}\circ d\rho_{A}
. Then, on core sections

\rho_{TA}(\hat{\alpha})=(\rho(\alpha))^{\uparrow},

where for
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
X^{\uparrow}\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
denotes the vertical vector field on
TM
induced by
X
. This follows from the facts that the involution map
J_{M}
identifies
T_{M}(TM)
with the vertical bundle
V(TM)
, and it is the identity on
V_{M}(TM)
. On tangent sections,

\rho_{TA}(T\alpha)=\rho_{A}(\alpha)^{T},

where for
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
X^{T}\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
is the tangent lift of
X
. Recall also the compatibility between the tangent lifts of vector fields and functions:
X^{T}
applied to the tangent lift
Tf
of
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
is the tangent lift of the function
X(f)
.

Let now
c\in C^{k}(A)
be an algebroid
k
-cochain.

•

Tangent sections

The compatibility between the tangent lifts of vector fields and functions allows to check directly that
\delta(Tc)=T(\delta c)
when applied to
k+1
tangent sections
T\alpha_{i}
of
TA
.

•

One core section

Recall that, by the second statement of Lemma
A.14
,

Tc(T\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k})={p_{M}}^{*}(c(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k})),

thus on the one hand
T(\delta c)(T\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k+1})={p_{M}}^{*}(c(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k+1}))
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\delta(Tc)(T\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j-1}{p_{M}}^{*}(c([\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}],\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k+1}))

\displaystyle+\Sigma_{i}^{k}(-1)^{i}\rho_{TA}(T\alpha_{i})({p_{M}}^{*}c(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{i-1},\alpha_{i+1},...,\alpha_{k+1}))

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}\rho_{TA}(\hat{\alpha_{k+1}})(T(c(\alpha_{1},...\alpha_{k}))).

Now, since in the second and third row of this equation we have the tangent and vertical lifts of vector fields of
M
, we use the expression for their flows and that will allow us to prove the equality with
T(\delta c)(T\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k+1})
.

•

More than one core section

On the one hand, statement (3) of Lemma
A.14
says that

T(\delta c)(\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k},\hat{\alpha}_{k+1})=0.

On the other hand, the same statement (3) implies that

\displaystyle\delta(Tc)(\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k},\hat{\alpha}_{k+1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\rho_{TA}(\hat{\alpha}_{k})Tc(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k-1},\hat{\alpha}_{k+1})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}\rho_{TA}(\hat{\alpha}_{k+1})Tc(\alpha_{1},...,\hat{\alpha}_{k})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}(\rho_{A}(\alpha_{k}))^{\uparrow}{p_{M}}^{*}c(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k-1},\alpha_{k+1})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}(\rho_{A}(\alpha_{k+1}))^{\uparrow}{p_{M}}^{*}c(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k})

\displaystyle=0.\qed

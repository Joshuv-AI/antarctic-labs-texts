Fast algorithm for multiplication on the skein algebra of one-hole torus
Siki Wang, Helen Wong

Abstract
. The Kauffman bracket skein algebra of a surface is a generalization of the Jones polynomial invariant for links that also plays a principal role in the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev topological quantum field theory. However, the multiplicative structure of the skein algebra is not well understood, with a priori exponential complexity. We consider the case of one-hole torus, and provide a polynomial time algorithm for computing multiplication of any two skein elements. Some examples of closed form formulas for multiplication of curves with low crossing number are also given.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 57-08, 57K31, 57K16.

Keywords: Kauffman bracket, skein algebra, quantum topology.

1.
Introduction

The Kauffman bracket skein algebra of a surface
\Sigma
lies in the intersection of quantum topology and hyperbolic geometry. Originally defined as a generalization of the Jones polynomial for framed links in thickened surfaces
[
19
,
16
]
, the skein algebra
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma)
plays a principal role in the skein theoretic verison of the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev topological quantum field theory
[
2
]
. Later, it was discovered to be closely related to the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character variety of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
, which contains as real subvariety the Teichmuller space of
\Sigma
from hyperbolic geometry
[
20
,
9
,
10
,
17
]
.
This connection was further developed by studying the representation theory of the skein algebra
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, which relied on development of algebraic tools to understand its algebraic structure, e.g. its center.

In order to further develop the relationship of the skein algebra with both quantum topology and hyperbolic geometry, it is essential to understand its multiplicative structure. Unfortunately, the multiplicative structure of the skein algebra is completely solved for few surfaces beyond the commutative cases. One important exception is the skein algebra of a closed torus, for which Bullock and Przytycki computed its presentation
[
11
]
and Frohman and Gelca provided an elegant Product-to-Sum formula for the basis obtained by applying the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind to torus knots
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the one-hole torus
\Sigma_{1,1}
and the multiplicative structure of its skein algebra
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
. By computing its presentation, Bullock and Przytycki showed that
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
is isomorphic to the quantum group
U_{q}(so_{3})

[
11
]
.
However, an explicit formula describing multiplication has been combinatorially difficult, due to the existence of the boundary.
Bousseau
[
8
]
and Queffelec
[
18
]
showed strong positivity for the structural constants for multiplication in the Chebyshev basis (also known as the bracelets basis), but an analogy to the closed torus’s Product-to-Sum formula remains elusive.

Here, we provide a fast algorithm to compute multiplication in the one-hole torus. The skein algebra is spanned as a module by
multicurves
, which are disjoint unions of pairwise non-intersecting, simple closed curves. On the one-hole torus, every multicurve is a union of a
(p,q)
-torus link with some copies of the peripheral loop
\partial
. The algorithm computes the product of multicurves as a linear combination of other multicurves. This is done using a five-term recursive relation for the discrepancy between multiplication on the one-hole torus and multiplication on the closed torus.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an algorithm to compute the product of two multicurves
m,m^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
in
\mathcal{O}(i(m,m^{\prime})^{6})
steps, where
i(m,m^{\prime})
is the intersection number of
m
and
m^{\prime}
.

In contrast, computing the product by resolving all crossings is
\mathcal{O}(2^{i(m,m^{\prime})})
. Previous algorithms such as
[
3
]
to compute multiplication on the one-hole torus relied on the Euclidean Algorithm (or equivalently, traveling on the Farey graph) and are slower. See also
[
1
]
for the 4-holed sphere, whose combinatorics are closely related to that of the one-hole torus. In particular, they use an adjusted Euclidean Algorithm and conjecture that it is
\mathcal{O}(\det^{\log\det})
, where
\det
corresponds to half the intersection number of two multicurves on the 4-holed sphere.

In addition to Theorem
1.1
, we provide closed form formulas for some cases and prove them using a five-term recursive relation. We were unable to conjecture a simple closed form formula for multiplication for
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
in all cases; however, our methods show that the five-term recursive relation is sufficient to determine all multiplication tables and hence should also determine a closed form formula, should one be identified. Results of our computations also confirm the positivity proven using other methods in
[
8
,
18
]
.

Background and Notation

Let
A
be an arbitrary variable. Let
\Sigma_{g,n}
be an orientable surface with genus
g
with
n
punctures. Then the
Kauffman bracket skein algebra

\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{g,n})
is generated as a
\mathbb{Z}[A,A^{-1}]
-module by the isotopy classes of framed knots in
\Sigma\times I
, subject to the following skein relations

(1)

(Skein relation)

\begin{minipage}[28.90755pt]\includegraphics[width=28.90755pt]{rel-skein1}\end{minipage}-A\begin{minipage}[28.90755pt]\includegraphics[width=28.90755pt]{rel-skein2}\end{minipage}-A^{-1}\begin{minipage}[28.90755pt]\includegraphics[width=28.90755pt]{rel-skein3}\end{minipage}

(Framing relation)

\begin{minipage}[28.90755pt]\includegraphics[width=28.90755pt]{rel-framing} \end{minipage}+A^{2}+A^{-2}

and where multiplication is by superposition. In particular,
\alpha\cdot\beta
has
\alpha
on top of
\beta
.

For any link
K
and

, let
K^{(i)}
be the
i
-fold cable of
K
, obtained by taking
i
parallel copies
K
in the direction of the framing. One can extend this linearly. If
p(x)\in Z[A,A^{-1}][x]
is a polynomial in

such that

, then let the
threading of
K
by

be the skein

K^{p}=\sum_{i}c_{i}K^{(i)}

Note that when
K
has no crossings, then
K^{p}=p(K)
.

In this paper, we will primarily be concerned with threading by Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind
T_{n}
, which are defined recursively using:

T_{0}(x)=2
,
T_{1}(x)=x
, and
T_{k}(x)=xT_{k-1}(x)-T_{k-2}(x)
for
k\geq 2
.
Note that these polynomials are closely related to the recursion of cosines and satisfy the identity
T_{k}(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(k\theta)
.

We begin by a pair of illustrative examples that explains some of the combinatorial difficulty of the one-hole torus
\Sigma_{1,1}
in contrast to the closed torus
\Sigma_{1,0}

. Determining when a resolution contains loops bounding a disk and when a resolutions contain copies of

, the discrepancy between the closed torus case and the one-holed torus case can be quite complicated.

In this paper, we focus on the case where
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1,1}
. Every multicurve is a union of a
(p,q)
-torus link with parallel copies of the peripheral loop
\partial
. Hence the skein algebra
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
is spanned as a
\mathbb{Z}[A,A^{-1}]
-module by the set of multicurves
\mathcal{M}=\{\partial^{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N},(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
.

Note that we follow the convention
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{-p}\\
{-q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, and unless otherwise specified, we will choose
p\geq 0
. Also, note that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\emptyset
, whereas
\partial
is the peripheral loop. Both
\emptyset
and
\partial
are central elements of the skein algebra. Also remark that the crosing number
i(m,m^{\prime})
between two multicurves
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
is easily computed. When the multicurves are torus links
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, then
i\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|=ps-rq
. We also have
i(\emptyset,m)=0
and
i(\partial^{k},m)=0
for all
m\in\mathcal{M}
.

Following the lead of Frohman and Gelca, define

\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}_{T}=T_{\gcd(p,q)}\begin{pmatrix}{\frac{p}{\gcd(p,q)}}\\
{\frac{q}{\gcd(p,q)}}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}=2\emptyset
. Later in Section
4
we will also use variations
T^{\prime}
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T^{\prime}}
.

Example 2.3
.

Since
\gcd(2,2)=2
and
T_{2}(x)=x^{2}-2
, we have
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{2}\\
{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}=T_{2}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right))=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{2}\\
{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)-2
.

Let
\eta=\partial+A^{2}+A^{-2}
, and let
\mathcal{M}_{T}=\{\emptyset\}\cup\{\eta^{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\}
where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
\mathcal{M}_{T}
spans
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
as a
Z[A,A^{-1}]
-module, and
\eta
is a central element. For reasons explained below,
\mathcal{M}_{T}
will be our basis of choice.

As we saw from the calculations in Examples
2.1
and
2.2
,
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})

\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,0})
, but there are significant differences. In particular, the one-hole torus does not satisfy Frohman-Gelca’s Product-to-Sum; that is,
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\

\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
. This discrepancy is the subject of our paper.

Proposition 2.4
.

In
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
,

\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}-A^{|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}\begin{pmatrix}{p+r}\\
{q+s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}-A^{-|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}\begin{pmatrix}{p-r}\\
{q-s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}

is divisible by
\eta=\partial+A^{2}+A^{-2}
.

Proof.

In
[
11
]
, it was shown that the embedding
\Sigma_{1,1}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{1,0}
induces a surjective algebra homomorphism
\iota:\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})\to\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,0})
and
\ker(\iota)
is the principal ideal generated by
\eta
.
In
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,0})
, Frohman and Gelca’s Product-to-Sum formula

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}-A^{|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p+r}\\
{q+s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}-A^{-|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-r}\\
{q-s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}=0
holds, and
the proposition follows.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

For any
p,q,r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
, the

{q}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}{r}\\

is defined to be the skein

D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{r}\\
{q}&{s}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}-A^{|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}\begin{pmatrix}{p+r}\\
{q+s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}-A^{-|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}\begin{pmatrix}{p-r}\\
{q-s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\right)

where
\eta=\partial+A^{2}+A^{-2}
.

Alternatively, we have

(1)

\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}=A^{|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}\begin{pmatrix}{p+r}\\
{q+s}\end{pmatrix}_{T}+A^{-|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\

Computing the Frohman-Gelca discrepancy

immediately yields the structural constants for multiplication in the Chebyshev basis

for
\mathcal{S}^{A}(\Sigma_{1,1})
. A priori, computation of

Note that the discrepancy need not be a scalar, but is in general a skein.

Lemma 2.7
.

If

, then

.

Lemma 2.8
.

D\begin{pmatrix}{2}&{0}\\

Proposition 2.9
.

D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=0

Proof.

By definition,

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\

is a linear combination of products of torus links
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{k}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
0\leq k\leq p
.
In each
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{k}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, there are
k

k

\partial
. Since no term of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}
contains
\partial
, the discrepancy is zero.
∎

3.
The Recursion Rule and transformations on the one-hole torus

In this section, we gather the tools needed to compute the discrepancy. The main innovation in this paper is the Recursion Rule. A special case appears in unpublished work
[
3
]
, but to the knowledge of the authors, this general version of the Recursion Rule is new. All other tools in this section are previously known.

3.1.
The Recursion Rule

Theorem 3.1

(Recursion Rule)
.

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}_{T}\cdot D\begin{pmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+A^{|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}D\begin{pmatrix}u&p+r\\
v&q+s\end{pmatrix}+A^{-|\begin{smallmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{smallmatrix}|}D\begin{pmatrix}u&p-r\\
v&q-s\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=D\begin{pmatrix}u&p\\
v&q\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}r\\
s\end{pmatrix}_{T}+A^{|\begin{smallmatrix}u&p\\
v&q\end{smallmatrix}|}D\begin{pmatrix}u+p&r\\
v+q&s\end{pmatrix}+A^{-|\begin{smallmatrix}u&p\\
v&q\end{smallmatrix}|}D\begin{pmatrix}u-p&r\\
v-q&s\end{pmatrix}

Proof.

Expand the associativity rule
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{u}\\
{v}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\cdot\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\right)=\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{u}\\
{v}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{r}\\
{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}
. Applying Equation (
1
), the result follows immediately.
∎

3.2.
Reductions under Dehn twists

The mapping class group of the one-hole torus is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, and generated by Dehn twists. A mapping class
\phi
induces an action
\phi_{*}
on
\Sigma_{1,1}
. Specifically, if
\phi
is represented by the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, then
\phi_{*}
acts by matrix multiplication on the torus links, with
\phi_{*}\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{ap+cq}\\
{bp+dq}\end{pmatrix}
. Since
\phi
fixes the puncture,
\phi_{*}(\eta)=\eta
. It immediately follows that
\phi_{*}
also acts on the discrepancy.

Lemma 3.2

(Reduction by Mapping Class)
.

Let a mapping class
\phi
be represented by
\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. Then for any
p,q,r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\phi_{*}\left(D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{r}\\
{q}&{s}\end{pmatrix}\right)=D\left(\phi_{*}\begin{pmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{pmatrix}\right)=D\left(\begin{matrix}ap+cq&cr+ds\\
ar+cs&br+ds\end{matrix}\right).

By the Euclidean Algorithm, we may always find
\phi
so that
\phi_{*}\left(D\begin{pmatrix}{p_{0}}&{r_{0}}\\
{q_{0}}&{s_{0}}\end{pmatrix}\right)=D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{s}\end{pmatrix}
where
0\leq q<p
. Since
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{-s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we may assume
s\geq 0
. Note that if
s=0
or
q=0
, then the discrepancy is zero by Lemmas
2.7
and
2.9
. So from now on, we will take
0<q<p
and
r=0
, and we will split our analysis into the cases where
s=1
and where
s\geq 2
. For future reference, we give the forms of the Recursion Rule that we will apply to these two cases.

Corollary 3.3

(Recursion Rule to increase
p
when
r=0
and
s=1
)
.

For
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle D\begin{pmatrix}{p+1}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{-q}\begin{pmatrix}{1}\\
{0}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\cdot D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{pmatrix}-A^{-2q}D\begin{pmatrix}{p-1}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+A^{-p-q}D\begin{pmatrix}{1}&{p}\\
{0}&{q-1}\end{pmatrix}-A^{-q}D\begin{pmatrix}{1}&{p}\\

3.3.
Reducing discrepancy using symmetries

Symmetries of
\Sigma_{1,1}
, especially rotations and reflections, induce simple linear algebraic transformations in the torus knots basis for the skein algebra, and these carry over to the discrepancy.
We introduce some language to describe some of the actions that we use most frequently.

Definition 3.5
.

For a skein
K=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{M}_{T}}c_{k,i,j}\cdot\eta^{k}\begin{pmatrix}{i}\\
{j}\end{pmatrix}_{T}
with
c_{k,i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}[A,A^{-1}]
,
define:

its
twist
to be

\mathrm{tw}(K)=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{M}_{T}}c_{k,i,j}\cdot\eta^{k}\begin{pmatrix}{i}\\
{j+i}\end{pmatrix}_{T},

its
opposition
to be

\mathrm{opp}(K)=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{M}_{T}}\overline{c_{k,i,j}}\cdot\eta^{k}\begin{pmatrix}{i}\\
{i-j}\end{pmatrix}_{T},

and its
reverse
to be

\mathrm{rev}(K)=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{M}_{T}}c_{k,i,j}\cdot\eta^{k}\begin{pmatrix}{j}\\
{i}\end{pmatrix}_{T},

where
\overline{c_{k,i,j}}
denotes the conjugate, where
A
and
A^{-1}
are exchanged.

Remark that
\mathrm{tw}^{-1}\left(\sum_{\mathcal{M}_{T}}c_{k,i,j}\cdot\eta^{k}\begin{pmatrix}{i}\\
{j}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\right)=\sum_{\mathcal{M}_{T}}c_{k,i,j}\cdot\eta^{k}\begin{pmatrix}{i}\\
{j-i}\end{pmatrix}_{T}
whereas
\mathrm{opp}=\mathrm{opp}^{-1}
and
\mathrm{rev}=\mathrm{rev}^{-1}
.

Lemma 3.6

(Twist)
.

\mathrm{tw}(D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{pmatrix})=D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{p+q}&{1}\end{pmatrix}

Proof.

This is a direct corollary of Lemma
3.2
with
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(Opposition)
.

\mathrm{opp}(D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{pmatrix})=D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{p-q}&{1}\end{pmatrix}

Proof.

Represent the one-holed torus as a square with opposite edges identified, with the hole at the corners. Reflect vertically, across the
yz
-plane, and then apply a Dehn twist.
∎

Lemma 3.8

(Reverse)
.

\mathrm{rev}(D\begin{pmatrix}{1}&{q}\\
{0}&{p}\end{pmatrix})=D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{pmatrix}

Proof.

Starting from
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, reflect across vertical
yz
-plane of slope, then rotate counterclockwise around the central
z
-axis of the square by
90^{\circ}
, and finally reflect in the central horizontal plane to obtain
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}\\
{p}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Reflection vertically takes a curve of slope
q/p
to one of slope
-q/p
, and exchanges positive and negative crossings so that the coefficients
A
and
A^{-1}
are also exchanged. Rotating by
90^{\circ}
around the center takes a curve of slope
q/p
to one of slope
-p/q
, and the coefficients remain unchanged. Reflection in the plane of the square changes all over-and under-crossings. The order of multiplication is switched, and
A
and
A^{-1}
are exchanged. ∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that
q>0
. If
p=aq+c
with
a\geq 0
and
0\leq c<q
, then

D\begin{pmatrix}{1}&{p}\\
{0}&{q}\end{pmatrix}=\mathrm{rev}\circ\mathrm{tw}^{a}\left(D\begin{pmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{c}&{1}\end{pmatrix}\right)

4.
Some example calculations

In certain cases, we can obtain closed form formulas for discrepancy.
Many of these formulas are known to experts in this area, but we present them here both as an application of the Recursion Rule as well as to establish base cases for our algorithm later.

In the following, we will use the following notation for the quantum integer

\left[{k}\right]_{A^{4}}=\dfrac{A^{2k}-A^{-2k}}{A^{2}-A^{-2}}\mbox{ for integers }k\geq 1.

Also, the closed form formulas are simpler if we use a commonly used variation of the Chebyshev polynomial. Define
T^{\prime}_{k}
so that
T^{\prime}_{0}(x)=1
but otherwise
T^{\prime}_{k}(x)=T_{k}(x)
for all
k\geq 1
.
Then we have that

\begin{pmatrix}{0}\\
{0}\end{pmatrix}_{T^{\prime}}=\emptyset,\quad\mbox{ and}\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}_{T^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}_{T}\mbox{ whenever }\begin{pmatrix}{p}\\
{q}\end{pmatrix}\neq\begin{pmatrix}{0}\\
{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
p\geq 1
,

D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{1}&{1}\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}A^{-(p-2k)}\left[{k}\right]_{A^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}{p-2k}\\
{0}\end{pmatrix}_{T^{\prime}}

Proof.

By induction. Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
give the base cases
p=1,2
.
From the Recursion Rule and Lemma
2.7
, we get the identity for the induction step

\displaystyle D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-1}&{0}\\
{1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)-A^{-2}D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-2}&{0}\\
{1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)+A^{p-2}D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{p-1}\\
{0}&{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Note that we have
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{p-1}\\
{0}&{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\begin{cases}1,&p\mbox{ even}\\
0,&p\mbox{ odd}\end{cases}
from Lemmas
2.8
and
3.8
.

∎

\displaystyle D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{0}\\

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{u}\\

Proposition 4.3
.

For

,

D\begin{pmatrix}{2q}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{q+1}{2}\rfloor}\left[{2k-1}\right]_{A^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}{2(q-2k+1)}\\
{q-2k+1}\end{pmatrix}_{T^{\prime}}

Proof.

We have that
D\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{0}\\
{0}&{1}\end{pmatrix}=0
and
D\begin{pmatrix}{2}&{0}\\
{1}&{1}\end{pmatrix}=1
. All the rest can be computed using the Recursion Relation,

\displaystyle D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{2(q+1)}&{0}\\
{q+1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{2}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{2q}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)-D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{2(q-1)}&{0}\\
{q-1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)+A^{2q}D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{2}&{2q}\\

Note that applying a suitable mapping class yields

.

∎

We can continue applying the Recursion Rule indefinitely to obtain various formulas for families of

T
-Chebyshevs in the Product-to-Sum Formula in the closed torus case. The authors unfortunately were unable to find such a suitable basis in the one-hole torus case.

Remark 4.4
.

Let

denote the
k
th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind. It is defined recursively by

,
S_{1}(x)=x
, and

for

. Analogously, one may define

.
We experimented with writing the discrepancy using the

-Chebyshevs, for example

However, at least to the authors, we did not find any significant advantage of doing so.

{q+1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, the induction hypothesis applies directly.

For the third term, observe that
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{p}\\
{0}&{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is the reverse of
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Thus, one is computable if and only if the other is computable, and the inequality
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{p}\\
{0}&{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-1}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is equivalent to
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}\\
{p-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. If
p\leq q
, then the induction hypothesis applies, and in particular,
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q-2}\\
{p-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}\\
{p-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. If
p\geq q
, then we first reduce
p
mod
q
. We write
p=aq+p^{\prime}
where
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leq p^{\prime}<q
, so that the induction hypothesis applies and
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{p^{\prime}}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q-1}\\
{p^{\prime}-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. We can then compute
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
by applying a Dehn twist
a
times as in Lemma
3.2
, and
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q-1}\\
{a(q-1)+p^{\prime}-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}\\
{p-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

The fifth term is similar.
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{p}\\
{0}&{q+2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is the reverse of
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q+2}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, and it is equivalent to show
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q+2}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}\\
{p}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. If
p\leq q
, then this is covered by the induction hypothesis. If
p=q+1
, apply a transformation and then use the opposition transformation from Proposition
3.7
(alternatively, we can appeal to Equation (
3
)). For
p\geq q+2
, first reduce
p
mod
q+2
and then apply the induction hypothesis.

For the first term, the induction hypothesis implies that
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q+1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq p-2\\
0\leq n\leq q\end{subarray}}c_{mn}\begin{pmatrix}{m}\\
{n}\end{pmatrix}_{T}
, with the known coefficients
c_{mn}\in\mathbb{Z}[A,A{-1},\eta]
. By Equation (
1
),

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}\\
{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q+1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq p-2\\
0\leq n\leq q\end{subarray}}c_{mn}(A^{n}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m+1}\\
{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}+A^{-n}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m-1}\\
{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}+\eta D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{m}\\
{0}&{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right))

Since
m\leq p-2
and
n\leq q
, we have that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m+1}\\
{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T},\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m-1}\\
{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m-1}\\
{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Also, because
0\leq b\leq q
, the induction hypothesis implies that, possibly after reduction of
m
mod
n
, the discrepancy
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{n}&{0}\\
{m}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is computed and
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{n}&{0}\\
{m}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}\\
{p-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. By taking the reverse, it follows that
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{m}\\
{0}&{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is computable and
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{m}\\
{0}&{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-1}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

For the fourth term, the argument is similar.
The induction hypothesis gives
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q+1}&{0}\\
{p}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q-1}\\
{p-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, possibly after reduction of
p
mod
q+1
. Thus
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{p}\\
{0}&{q+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq p-1\\
0\leq n\leq q-1\end{subarray}}c_{mn}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}\\
{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}
, and

D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{p}\\
{0}&{q+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq p-1\\
0\leq n\leq q\end{subarray}}c_{mn}(A^{m}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}\\
{n+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}+A^{-m}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}\\
{n-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}+\eta D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}&{0}\\
{n}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)).

Because
0\leq b\leq q
, each term on the right is known, and no term is larger than
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-1}\\
{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

In the course of the proof above, we showed that when
p\geq 2,q\geq 1
, we have the discrepancy
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\sum_{a,b}c_{ab}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}\\
{b}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}
where the sum is taken over
0\leq a\leq p-2
and
0\leq b\leq q-2
. See Theorems 4.6 and 4.7 of
[
1
]
for comparable statements for the 4-holed sphere, which were proved by a careful analysis of the intersection numbers and without the organizing principle provided by the Recursion Relation.

Remark 5.3
.

Proposition
5.1
proves the validity of Algorithm
2
for computing
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for any
p,q\geq 0
. However, as can be seen by a careful analysis of the proof, we can get substantial computational savings at several key steps. First, one can apply Dehn twists, as in Corollary
3.9
, to reduce
q
mod
p
. One can also apply Proposition
3.7
to ensure that
q\leq\lfloor p/2\rfloor
. This is incorporated into Steps 3 and 5 of Algorithm
1
.

In addition, for fixed
q
, prior to an iteration of the Recursion Rule to compute
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q+1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, Algorithm
2
first computes and stores all values of
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{m}\\
{0}&{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
from values of its reverse
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q}&{0}\\
{m}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
from earlier iterations by using Dehn twists to reduce
m
mod
q
. The algorithm also pre-computes
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{m}\\
{0}&{q+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{m}\\
{0}&{q+2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
in the same way. Note that we may have to apply Proposition
3.7
to reduce
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q+1}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q+2}&{0}\\
{q+1}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{q+2}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
to reach stored values. We will see that this will give computational savings in Proposition
5.5
.

Proposition 5.4
.

The Recursion Rule computes
D\begin{pmatrix}{p}&{r}\\
{q}&{s}\end{pmatrix}
for all
p,q,r,s
.

Proof.

By applying a mapping class, we reduce to the the case of
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
0\leq q\leq\lfloor p/2\rfloor
. Proposition
5.1
proves the validity of the subalgorithm when
s=1
. For larger
s
, let us assume that we have already computed and stored
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{0}\\
{i}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for
a=1,\ldots p
and
i=0,\ldots,\lfloor p/2\rfloor
. Moreover, from the proof of Proposition
5.1
we also have that
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{0}\\
{i}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a-2}\\
{i-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Fix
s
. The base case is given by Proposition
5.1
. Assume that we have
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for all
k=1,\ldots s
and that no term of
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is larger than
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-2}\\
{q+k-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
By the Recursion Relation from Corollary
3.4
, we have

D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{s+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}-D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{s-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)+A^{ps}D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q+s}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)+A^{-ps}D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q-s}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

The last two terms can be obtained from the given values of
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, by applying Dehn twists as in Lemma
3.2
to reduce
q+s
(resp.
q-s
) mod
p
.

The second term follows directly from the induction hypothesis.

For the first term, the induction hypothesis implies
D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq p-2\\
0\leq n\leq q+s-1\end{subarray}}c_{mn}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}\\
{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}
. Thus

D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p}&{0}\\
{q}&{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}\\
{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq p-2\\
0\leq n\leq q+s-1\end{subarray}}c_{mn}(A^{m}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}\\
{n+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}+A^{-m}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}\\
{n-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{T}+\eta D\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{m}&{0}\\
{n}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)).

Each of the terms on the right may be computed by simple transformations from known values, and none is larger than
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{p-2}\\
{q+s}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

The time and space complexity of the algorithm computing
D\begin{pmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{pmatrix}
is
O\bigg(\begin{vmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{vmatrix}^{6}\bigg).

Proof.

Using coefficients from the mapping class
\phi
in Lemma 3.2, it is sufficient to consider
D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q&s\end{pmatrix}
. With a complexity of
\widetilde{O}(\log(\min(r,s)))
, the Euclidean algorithm allows us to assume that
s,r
have similar orders of magnitude.

We will estimate the space and time complexity for computing
D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q&1\end{pmatrix}
, then apply Corollary 3.4 to obtain
D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q&s\end{pmatrix}
.

First, suppose that the value of all
D\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
v&1\end{pmatrix}
for which
u\leq p
and
v\leq q
are already computed. In the five-term recursion of Corollary 3.3, the second, third, and fifth term involve accessing those precomputed values, all of which take space
O(pq)
to store. Looping over the values once therefore takes
O(pq)
time for summation and simplifications.

The first term of the five-term recursion formula involves computing the expression

\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}_{T}\cdot D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q-1&1\end{pmatrix}

which involves multiplication with stored values

D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q-1&1\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{0\leq m\leq p-2,0\leq n\leq q-1}c_{mn}\begin{pmatrix}m\\
n\end{pmatrix}_{T}

Overall, this gives us the expression

\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\cdot D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q-1&1\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{0\leq m\leq p-2,0\leq n\leq q-1}c_{mn}(A^{n}\begin{pmatrix}m+1\\
n\end{pmatrix}_{T}+A^{n-1}\begin{pmatrix}m-1\\
n\end{pmatrix}_{T}+\eta D\begin{pmatrix}1&m\\
0&n\end{pmatrix})

This involves at most
pq
of
D\begin{pmatrix}1&m\\
0&n\end{pmatrix}
’s, each with at most
pq
terms. Therefore it takes
O(p^{2}q^{2})
operations to substitute and simplify the terms. The argument is similar for the fourth term in the recursion formula.

To obtain
D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q&s\end{pmatrix}
from
D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q&1\end{pmatrix}
, one needs to apply the formula from Corollary 3.4 for each
D\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
q&i\end{pmatrix}
from
i=2,...,s
. Notice that the first multiplicative term takes
O(p^{2}q^{2})
to compute, which dominates the running time of each iteration. Counting the additional space used to store these terms, this gives an overall complexity of
O(p^{2}q^{2}s)
.

Finally, to satisfy the assumption that all
D\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
v&1\end{pmatrix}
for
u\leq p
and
v\leq q
are computed, we compute all such
D\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
v&1\end{pmatrix}
’s, with the explicit order given by Algorithm 2. This gives another loop of
O(pq)
terms, which brings both the time and space complexity to store all items to
O(p^{3}q^{3}s)
. Since we have
q=\lfloor p/2\rfloor
in the worst case, we obtain an upper bound of
O\bigg(\begin{vmatrix}p&r\\
q&s\end{vmatrix}^{6}\bigg)
for the overall complexity.
∎

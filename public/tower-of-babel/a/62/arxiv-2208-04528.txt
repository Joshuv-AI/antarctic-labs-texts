Universal quantum computation based on Nano-Electro-Mechanical Systems
Motohiko Ezawa, Shun Yasunaga, Akio Higo, Tetuya Iizuka, Yoshio Mita

Abstract
We propose to use a buckled plate as a qubit, where a double-well potential is mechanically produced by pushing the plate from both the sides. The right and left positions of the plate are assigned to be quantum states | 0 ⟩ |0\rangle and | 1 ⟩ |1\rangle . Quantum effects emerge when the displacement is of the order of picometers, although the size of a buckled plate is of the order of 1 ​ μ ​ m 1\mu m . The NOT gate is executed by changing the buckling force acting on the plate, while the Pauli-Z gate and the phase-shift gate are executed by applying electric field. A two-qubit phase shift gate is materialized with the use of an electrostatic potential. They constitute a set of universal quantum gates. An examination of material parameters leads to a feasibility of a NEMS(Nano-Electro-Mechanical System)-based quantum computer.



Introduction.
According to Moor’s law, elements of integrated
circuits become exponentially small as a function of year. The size will
become the order of nanometers within 10 years, where quantum mechanical
effects are inevitable. For example, the superposition of states and the
entanglement occur, which are absent in classical mechanics. It is
impossible to decrease the size of elements smaller than 1nm, which is a

The buckled plate has two stable positions. It can be used as a classical
bit. It has been proposed that an Ising annealing machine is executable by a
series of the buckled plates

electrostatic potential inducing the Ising interaction between two adjacent

A buckled plate would also reveal quantum effects when the displacement is

They are constructed by tuning the tension, applying electric field and

\displaystyle\int_{-y_{0}}^{y_{0}}dy\sqrt{1+\frac{\pi^{2}x_{0}^{2}}{4y_{0}^{2}}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi y}{y_{0}}}=\frac{y_{0}}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\sqrt{1+\frac{\pi^{2}x_{0}^{2}}{4y_{0}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta}

(S4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4y_{0}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}d\theta\sqrt{1+\frac{\pi^{2}x_{0}^{2}}{4y_{0}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta}=\frac{4y_{0}}{\pi}E\left(-i\frac{\pi x_{0}}{2y_{0}}\right),

where
E
is the complete elliptic integral of the second kind defined by

E\left(k\right)\equiv\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}d\theta.

(S5)

The potential energy is given by the Hooke law

V(x_{0})=\frac{\kappa}{2}\left(2L(x_{0})-2L_{0}\right)^{2},

(S6)

which we show for various
y_{0}
in Fig.
S1
(b). It is a
double-well potential. The potential minimum is shown as a function of
y_{0}
in Fig.
S1
(c).

We expand Eq.(
S6
) in terms of
x_{0}
by fixing
L_{0}
and
y_{0}
as

V(x_{0})=\frac{\kappa}{2}\left(2L_{0}-2y_{0}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4y_{0}}\left(L_{0}-y_{0}\right)\kappa\pi^{2}x_{0}^{2}+\frac{\left(L_{0}-3y_{0}\right)}{256y_{0}^{3}}\kappa\pi^{4}x_{0}^{4}+o\left(x_{0}^{6}\right).

(S7)

It is summarized as
V(x_{0})=\lambda(x_{0}^{2}-a^{2})^{2}+V_{0}+o\left(x_{0}^{6}\right)
with

\lambda\equiv\frac{\left(L_{0}-3y_{0}\right)}{256y_{0}^{3}}\kappa\pi^{4},\quad a\equiv y_{0}\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{L_{0}-y_{0}}{3L_{0}-y_{0}}}.

(S8)

The parameter
x_{0}
is determined as
x_{0}=a
by minimizing the potential

V(x_{0})
. This buckled plate is an example of a MEMS (NEMS), when its size
is of the order of micrometers (nanometers).

Second, we impose the fixed boundary condition, which reads

\left.\frac{d^{2}w}{dy^{2}}\right|_{x=-y_{0}}=\left.\frac{d^{2}w}{dy^{2}}\right|_{x=y_{0}}=0,

(S9)

whose solution is
Binh

w=x_{0}\cos\frac{\pi y}{2y_{0}}.

(S10)

The length
L(x_{0})
is given by Eq.(
S4
) precisely as in the case of
the fixed boundary condition. Hence, the potential energy is given by the
same Hooke law as Eq.(
S6
).

Units of time and space

It is straightforward to rewrite the Schrödinger equation (2) and the
Hamiltonian (3) in the main text as

i\frac{d}{d\tau}\psi\left(X,\tau\right)=H\psi\left(X,\tau\right)

(S11)

and

H=-\frac{1}{2}(d/dX)^{2}+(X^{2}-A^{2})^{2}

(S12)

in terms of the dimensionless time
\tau=t/t_{\text{u}}
and the
dimensionless parameters
X\equiv x/x_{\text{u}}
and
A\equiv a/x_{\text{u}}
, where
t_{\text{u}}=\left(m^{2}/\hbar\lambda\right)^{1/3}
and
x_{\text{u}}=\hbar^{1/3}/\left(m\lambda\right)^{1/6}
give the units of
time and space, respectively. We have carried out numerical analysis based
on the dimensionless formulas.

IV
Electrostatic energy

In the parallel-plate NEMS, the capacitance between the plates
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
is well described by

C_{\text{para}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}S}{X_{\text{cap}}+x_{1}-x_{2}},

(S13)

where
X_{\text{cap}}
is the distance between the adjacent plates without
buckling,
S
is the area of the plate, and
\varepsilon_{0}
is the
permittivity. The electrostatic potential is given by

U_{\text{cap}}=\frac{C_{\text{para}}(x_{1},x_{2})}{2}V_{1}^{2},

(S14)

when we control the voltage
V_{1}
between the plates.

We consider a set of two adjacent plates, where the potential energy is
given by

V\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\equiv V\left(x_{1}\right)+V\left(x_{2}\right)+\frac{C_{\text{para}}(x_{1},x_{2})}{2}V_{1}^{2}.

(S15)

We show the potential in the
x_{1}
-
x_{2}
plane in Fig.
S3
. In the absence of the applied voltage
V_{1}=0
, there are four-fold
degenerated bottoms at
u_{1}=\pm a
and
u_{2}=\pm a
as shown in Fig.
S3
(a), where the ground state energy is
\hbar\omega
. Under
applied voltage, they are split as

\displaystyle V\left(a,a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\left(-a,-a\right)=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}S}{X_{\text{cap}}}\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}\equiv E_{0},

(S16)

\displaystyle V\left(a,-a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{0}S}{X_{\text{cap}}+2a}\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}\equiv E_{+},

(S17)

\displaystyle V\left(-a,a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{0}S}{X_{\text{cap}}-2a}\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}\equiv E_{-}.

(S18)

We also plot the potential profile with nonzero voltage in Fig.
S3

is

a

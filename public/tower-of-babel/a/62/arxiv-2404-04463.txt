A beam that can only bend on the Cantor set
Roberto Paroni ​​​​​, Brian Seguin*

Abstract
In this work we address the following question: is it possible for a one-dimensional, linearly elastic beam to only bend on the Cantor set and, if so, what would the bending energy of such a beam look like? We answer this question by considering a sequence of beams, indexed by n n , each one only able to bend on the set associated with the n n -th step in the construction of the Cantor set and compute the Γ \Gamma -limit of the bending energies. The resulting energy in the limit has a structure similar to the traditional bending energy, a key difference being that the measure used for the integration is the Hausdorff measure of dimension ln ⁡ 2 / ln ⁡ 3 \ln 2/\ln 3 , which is the dimension of the Cantor set.

Our main goal is to understand the deflection of a one-dimensional, linearly elastic beam that can only bend on a Cantor set subject to a particular choice of boundary conditions and loads. To achieve this, we consider a sequence of beams, parametrized by
n\in\mathbb{N}
, that can only bend on the set corresponding to the
n
-th step in the construction of the Cantor set. See Figure
1
.

On the right it is depicted a possible deformation for a beam corresponding at the second step in the construction of the Cantor set.

Let
{\cal C}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\cal C}_{n}
be the Cantor set constructed from the interval
[0,\ell]
, where
{\cal C}_{n}
is the set corresponding to the
n
-th step in the construction of the Cantor set. To the
n
-beam, i.e., the beam corresponding to the
n
-th step, we associate a bending elastic energy
E^{\tiny el}_{n}

defined by

E^{\tiny el}_{n}(u)\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}b_{n}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}d{\cal L},

(1)

where
b_{n}
is the bending stiffness,
u
is the transversal displacement, and
\ell
is the length of the beam.
The part of the beam off of
{\cal C}_{n}
is modeled as rigid by setting the bending stiffness equal to
+\infty
off of
{\cal C}_{n}
. Since the measure of
{\cal C}_{n}
goes to zero, if we keep the bending stiffness on
{\cal C}_{n}
independent of
n
, the beam would become more and more rigid as
n
increases—that is, if we apply the same load to each
n
-beam the sequence of the maximum displacements will tend to zero. Said differently, the limiting beam would be rigid. Therefore, to avoid what would be a trivial result, we appropriately let
b_{n}
on
{\cal C}_{n}
decrease to zero. We show that by setting
b_{n}=(2/3)^{n}b
, with
b
a constant, the sequence of energies is bounded above and the limiting beam is not rigid. In passing, we note that the measure of
{\cal C}_{n}
is exactly
(2/3)^{n}\ell
, which partly justifies the scaling of the bending stiffness.

With these choices made, we use the theory of
\Gamma
-convergence to determine the energy of the limiting beam. We refer to Section
3
for the specification of the topology under which the
\Gamma
-limit is taken, we here only describe the results informally. The limiting energy turns out to be finite only for displacements
u
that are continuous, differentiable, and whose second derivative is a Radon measure. Also,
u^{\prime\prime}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure restricted to the Cantor set
{\cal C}
, where
s=\ln 2/\ln 3
is the Hausdorff dimension of the Cantor set. In mechanical terms we can say that the limiting beam can “bend” only on the Cantor set, that has measure zero with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Setting
k_{\cal C}
equal to the Radon-Nikodým derivative of
u^{\prime\prime}
with respect to the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure restricted to the Cantor set
{\cal C}
, the limiting elastic energy that we find is

E^{el}_{\infty}(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\cal C}\frac{b{\cal H}^{s}({\cal C})}{\ell}\,k_{\cal C}^{2}\,d{\cal H}^{s},

(2)

where
{\cal H}^{s}
is the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. From the limiting energy, but also from the definition, we deduce that
k_{\cal C}
is the (linearized) curvature of the beam. This curvature is “concentrated” on the Cantor set.

3
Main result

The goal of this section is to understand the deflection of a one-dimensional, linearly elastic beam that can only bend on a Cantor set subject to a particular choice of boundary conditions and loads. To achieve this, we consider a sequence of energies
E_{n}
that model a beam that can only bend on the
n
-th step in the construction of the Cantor set and then compute the
\Gamma
-limit.

Towards that end, consider a one-dimensional beam that has one end embedded in a rigid wall. Assume the length of the beam outside of the wall is
\ell
and the length inside is
\delta
. Choose a coordinate system so that the material points of the beam can be described by
x\in(-\delta,\ell)
. The displacement of the beam is described by a function
u:(-\delta,\ell)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, where positive
u
corresponds to a downward deflection. A single point load in the downward direction of magnitude
P>0
is applied to the beam at
x=\ell
.

The bending stiffness of the beam depends on
n
. To describe this stiffness, we first introduce the
n
-th step in the construction of the middle third Cantor set on
[0,\ell]
. To do so we introduce the functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\psi_{1}(x)\coloneqq\frac{x}{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{2}(x)\coloneqq\frac{x+2\ell}{3},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3)

Set
{\cal C}_{0}\coloneqq[0,\ell]
, and iteratively define

{\cal C}_{n}\coloneqq\psi_{1}({\cal C}_{n-1})\cup\psi_{2}({\cal C}_{n-1}),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4)

Notice that
{\cal C}_{n}
is the union of
2^{n}
intervals each of length
3^{-n}\ell
. Denote these intervals by
I_{n,i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,2^{n}\}
, so that

{\cal C}_{n}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{2^{n}}I_{n,i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|I_{n,i}|=3^{-n}\ell.

(5)

The Cantor set
{\cal C}
is then given by

{\cal C}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\cal C}_{n}.

(6)

With this notation in place, we can specify the material properties of the beam at step
n
. For a fixed
b>0
, consider the bending stiffness
b_{n}:(-\delta,\ell)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
defined by

b_{n}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\big(\tfrac{2}{3}\big)^{n}b&\text{if}\ x\in{\cal C}_{n},\\
\infty&\text{if}\ x\in(-\delta,\ell)\setminus{\cal C}_{n},\end{cases}

(7)

and the energy functional
E_{n}:W^{2,2}(-\delta,\ell)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by

E_{n}(u)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}b_{n}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}d{\cal L}-Pu(\ell)&\mbox{if }u\in{\cal W},\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(8)

where

{\cal W}:=\{u\in W^{2,2}(-\delta,\ell)\ |\ u=0\ \text{on}\ (-\delta,0]\}.

(9)

Remark 3.1
.

We have here chosen the simplest boundary and loading conditions. These can be easily changed. For instance, our main result still holds for any loading that can be taken into account with a functional that is continuous with respect to the convergence under which we take the
\Gamma
-limit.
Also, we chose to implement the boundary condition by imposing the displacement
u=0
on
(-\delta,0]
in place of
u(0)=u^{\prime}(0)=0
because, as it will be soon clear, in the point
x=0
the function
u^{\prime}
may be discontinuous.

Having infinite bending stiffness off of
{\cal C}_{n}
suggests that the beam should not be able to bend on that set. Our first result formulates and proves this fact.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
n
be fixed. If
u\in{\cal W}
and
E_{n}(u)<\infty
, then
u^{\prime\prime}=0
a.e. on
(-\delta,\ell)\setminus{\cal C}_{n}
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of
{\cal W}
that
u(0)=u^{\prime}(0)=0
. Thus, we have

|u(\ell)|\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}|u^{\prime}(y)|dy\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{0}^{y}|u^{\prime\prime}(x)|dxdy\leq\ell\int_{0}^{\ell}|u^{\prime\prime}(x)|dx,

(10)

and from Jensen’s inequality,

|u(\ell)|^{2}\leq\ell^{3}\int_{0}^{\ell}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}d{\cal L}.

(11)

So, for any
\alpha>0
, we find that

\displaystyle\infty>E_{n}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}b_{n}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}d{\cal L}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|u(\ell)|^{2}-\frac{P^{2}}{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}\big(b_{n}-\alpha\ell^{3}\big)|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}d{\cal L}-\frac{P^{2}}{2\alpha}.

(12)

Using
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\frac{2}{3}\big)^{n}\ell^{-3}b
in the previous inequality allows us to conclude that

\infty>\tfrac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\ell}b_{n}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}d{\cal L}.

(13)

Since
b_{n}
is infinite off of
{\cal C}_{n}
, this is only possible if
u^{\prime\prime}=0
a.e. on
(0,\ell)\setminus{\cal C}_{n}
. We know
u^{\prime\prime}=0
on
(-\delta,0]
from the fact that
u\in{\cal W}
.
∎

Next we establish two preliminary lemmas that will be useful in obtaining a compactness result and the

-convergence. The first one is a consequence of Theorem 2.34 of Ambrosio, Fusco, and Pallara
[
1
]

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\mu_{n}),(\eta_{n})\in{\cal M}(-\delta,\ell)
be sequences of Radon measures and
\mu,\eta\in{\cal M}(-\delta,\ell)
, with each
\eta_{n}
and
\eta
being positive, such that
\mu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu
,
\eta_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\eta
, and
\mu_{n}\ll\eta_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\Big(\frac{\mu_{n}}{\eta_{n}}\Big)^{2}d\eta_{n}<\infty,

(14)

than
\mu\ll\eta
and

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\Big(\frac{\mu_{n}}{\eta_{n}}\Big)^{2}d\eta_{n}\geq\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\Big(\frac{\mu}{\eta}\Big)^{2}d\eta.

(15)

Proof.

To establish
\mu\ll\eta
, begin by fixing
\varphi\in C_{c}(-\delta,\ell)
and set
\gamma_{n}\coloneqq\frac{\mu_{n}}{\eta_{n}}
. Notice that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\varphi d|\mu_{n}|\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}|\varphi|d|\mu_{n}|=\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}|\varphi||\gamma_{n}|d\eta_{n}\leq\Big(\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\varphi^{2}d\eta_{n}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}d\eta_{n}\Big)^{1/2}.

(16)

Utilizing (
14
), we can take the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
of the previous inequality to find that

\Big|\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\varphi d|\mu|\Big|\leq K\Big(\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\varphi^{2}d\eta\Big)^{1/2}

(17)

for some
K>0
. Suppose that
\eta({\cal B})=0
for some Borel set
{\cal B}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and find
{\cal U}
open containing
{\cal B}
such that
\eta({\cal U})<\varepsilon
. Now if
|\varphi|\leq 1
and
\text{supp}\,\varphi\subseteq{\cal U}
, we can use (
17
) to obtain

\Big|\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\varphi d|\mu|\Big|\leq K\varepsilon^{1/2}.

(18)

Taking the supremum over all such
\varphi
yields
|\mu|({\cal B})\leq|\mu|({\cal U})\leq K\varepsilon^{1/2}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
and
{\cal B}
were arbitrary, this shows that
\mu\ll\eta
. We can now set
\gamma\coloneqq\frac{\mu}{\eta}
.

To establish (
15
), start with
\varphi\in C_{c}(-\delta,\ell)
and notice

0\leq(\gamma_{n}-\varphi)^{2}=\gamma_{n}^{2}-2\gamma_{n}\varphi+\varphi^{2}.

(19)

Utilizing this, we find that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\gamma_{n}^{2}d\eta_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}(2\gamma_{n}\varphi-\varphi^{2})d\eta_{n}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big[\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}2\varphi d\mu_{n}-\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\varphi^{2}d\eta_{n}\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}2\varphi d\mu-\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}\varphi^{2}d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{-\delta}^{\ell}[\gamma^{2}-(\gamma-\varphi)^{2}]d\eta.

Since
C_{c}
functions are dense in
L^{2}
and
\varphi
was arbitrary, this implies (
15
).
∎

The next lemma characterizes the weak* limit of a sequence of measures associated with the construction of the Cantor set.

Lemma 3.4
.

The sequence of measures
\eta_{n}\coloneqq\big(\tfrac{3}{2}\big)^{n}{\cal L}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\cal C}_{n}
,

\eta=\frac{\ell}{{\cal H}^{s}({\cal C})}{\cal H}^{s}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\cal C}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta(I_{n,i})=2^{-n}\ell,

(20)

where
s=\ln 2/\ln 3
.

Proof.

Begin by noticing that the total variation of these measures is bounded, namely
|\eta_{n}|=\ell
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, there is a subsequence, also denoted by
(\eta_{n})
, and
\eta\in{\cal M}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\eta_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\eta
. Since, for any measurable set

we have that

\displaystyle{\cal L}({\cal B}\cap{\cal C}_{n+1})

\displaystyle={\cal L}({\cal B}\cap(\psi_{1}({\cal C}_{n})\cup\psi_{2}({\cal C}_{n})))={\cal L}({\cal B}\cap\psi_{1}({\cal C}_{n}))+{\cal L}({\cal B}\cap\psi_{2}({\cal C}_{n}))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}{\cal L}(\psi_{1}^{-1}({\cal B}\cap\psi_{1}({\cal C}_{n})))+\tfrac{1}{3}{\cal L}(\psi_{2}^{-1}({\cal B}\cap\psi_{2}({\cal C}_{n})))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}{\cal L}(\psi_{1}^{-1}({\cal B})\cap{\cal C}_{n})+\tfrac{1}{3}{\cal L}(\psi_{2}^{-1}({\cal B})\cap{\cal C}_{n})

and, hence,

\tfrac{1}{2}(\psi_{1\#}\eta_{n}+\psi_{2\#}\eta_{n})=\eta_{n+1}.

(21)

Thus, we can take the limit in

to obtain

\tfrac{1}{2}(\psi_{1\#}\eta+\psi_{2\#}\eta)=\eta.

(22)

We now show that there is only one measure
\eta
of total variation

that satisfies this condition.

The value
s=\ln 2/\ln 3
is the Hausdorff dimension of the Cantor set and

[

]
. For any Borel set

we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}[(\psi_{1\#}{\cal H}^{s})({\cal B})+(\psi_{2\#}{\cal H}^{s})({\cal B})]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}[{\cal H}^{s}({\cal C}\cap\psi_{1}^{-1}({\cal B}))+{\cal H}^{s}({\cal C}\cap\psi_{2}^{-1}({\cal B}))]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{3^{s}}{2}[{\cal H}^{s}(\psi_{1}({\cal C})\cap{\cal B})+{\cal H}^{s}(\psi_{2}({\cal C})\cap{\cal B})]

\displaystyle={\cal H}^{s}((\psi_{1}({\cal C})\cup\psi_{2}({\cal C})\cap{\cal B})

\displaystyle=({\cal H}^{s}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\cal C})({\cal B}).

Thus, the measure
\eta=\frac{\ell}{{\cal H}^{s}({\cal C})}{\cal H}^{s}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\cal C}

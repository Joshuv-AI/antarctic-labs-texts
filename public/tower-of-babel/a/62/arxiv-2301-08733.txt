Kudla–Millson forms and one–variable degenerations of Hodge structure
Luis E. García

Abstract
. We consider arbitrary polarized variations of Hodge structure of weight two and h 2 , 0 = 1 \displaystyle h^{2,0}=1 over a non–singular complex algebraic curve S \displaystyle S and analyze the boundary behaviour of the associated Kudla–Millson theta series using Schmid’s theorems on degenerations of Hodge structure. This allows us to prove that this theta series is always integrable over S \displaystyle S and to describe explicitly the non-holomorphic part of the Kudla–Millson generating series in terms of the mixed Hodge structures at infinity.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to study the behaviour under degeneration of Hodge structure of certain theta series introduced by Kudla and Millson to study special cycles on Shimura varieties. Unlike previous work that analyzes the case where
\displaystyle S
is a special subvariety in a toroidal compactification of a Shimura variety, here we consider polarized variations of Hodge structure with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
over an arbitrary non–singular complex curve
\displaystyle S
.

1.1.
Main results

Let
\displaystyle\overline{S}
be a connected compact Riemann surface and denote by
\displaystyle S
the Riemann surface obtained by removing a finite number of points from
\displaystyle\overline{S}
. Consider an integral polarized variation of Hodge structure (
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
–PVHS)

(1.1)

\displaystyle\textstyle{\mathbb{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},Q,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\displaystyle\textstyle{S}

of weight two with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
. Here
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the local system underlying
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
and we write
\displaystyle Q
for the polarization and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
for the Hodge filtration. Let us write
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
for the line bundle
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}
over
\displaystyle S
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}\supseteq\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
for the dual lattice of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, that is,

\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}s}=\left\{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}s}\ |\ Q(v,v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s}\right\}.

In order to state our main results succintly we will assume the following mild condition on
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
(cf. Remark
1.1

Hypothesis 1.1
.

For any
\displaystyle s\in S
, the lattice
\displaystyle(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s},Q)
is even and
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}s}
is a simple
\displaystyle\pi_{1}(S,s)
–module. Moreover, the fundamental group of
\displaystyle S
acts trivially on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and the monodromy of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
around each
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S
is unipotent and non–trivial.

We will be interested in the Noether–Lefschetz loci of
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
: for a positive rational number
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, define

(1.2)

\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}=\{s\in S\ |\ \exists v\in(\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s})\cap\mathcal{F}^{1}_{s}\text{ with }Q(v,v)=2m\}.

The locus
\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}\subset S
has a natural complex analytic space structure
[
24
, §5.3.1]
; in fact, by the celebrated theorem of Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan it is a proper algebraic subset of
\displaystyle S
with a natural scheme structure. Let us write
\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\ \mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}
for the degree of the divisor naturally associated with
\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}
and
\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{L}}
for Deligne’s canonical extension of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
to a line bundle over
\displaystyle\overline{S}
and form the generating series

(1.3)

Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)_{\mu}=-\deg(\overline{\mathcal{L}})\delta_{\mu,0}+\sum_{m>0}\deg\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}\cdot q^{m},\quad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.

When
\displaystyle S
is compact, the series
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)
are known to be modular forms of possibly half–integral weight. More precisely, let
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the metaplectic double cover of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and let
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the Weil representation of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the group algebra
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}]
, which has a standard basis
\displaystyle e^{\mu}
indexed by
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The work of Kudla and Millson
[
18
]
implies that the generating series

(1.4)

Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu}Z^{+}_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu}

is a
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
–valued modular form of weight
\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})/2
. Their proof proceeds by constructing first certain theta series

(1.5)

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}\in\Omega^{1,1}(S),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},

depending on
\displaystyle\tau\in\mathbb{H}
, that transform like non–holomorphic modular forms. When
\displaystyle S
is compact one can consider the integral
\displaystyle\smallint_{S}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
, which inherits the transformation properties of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
, and so the modularity of
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)
follows from the identity (cf.
[
18
, Theorem 2]
)

(1.6)

Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)_{\mu}=\int_{S}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}.

The goal of this paper is to generalize these results to the setting of (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle S
non-compact. In this case the differential forms
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
often have singularities around the points in
\displaystyle\overline{S}\backslash S

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
is integrable on
\displaystyle S
; note that we do not impose Hypothesis
1.1

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle S
be a smooth complex algebraic curve and
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\to S
be a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
–PVHS over
\displaystyle S
of weight two with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
such that the action of
\displaystyle\pi_{1}(S,s)
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is trivial. Then the integral

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\int_{S}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}

converges for every
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and the expression

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu}

\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})/2

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Our second main result gives the precise relation between the non-holomorphic modular form
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
and the generating series
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)
: one can write

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)-Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)=\sum_{P\in\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S}Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau),

where the term

at
\displaystyle P
. More precisely, let us write
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{Z}}
for the space of global multivalued sections of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
to a universal cover of
\displaystyle S
. The pair
\displaystyle(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
is an even lattice of signature
\displaystyle(h^{1,1},2)
. A point
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\backslash S
determines then an endomorphism
\displaystyle N(P)
of
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
(the local monodromy logarithm) and an ascending filtration
\displaystyle W(P)_{\bullet}
of
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
(the shifted weight filtration) such that the quotients

\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}:=(W(P)_{k}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}})/(W(P)_{k-1}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}

Associated with these data are positive integers

\displaystyle r_{k}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},N(P))
(
\displaystyle k=1,2
),
\displaystyle\deg(Q_{3})
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}})

\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau),\quad\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau),

valued in finite–dimensional representations
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W(P)}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}

1.2.
Relation with other works

In the setting of the PVHS parametrized by Shimura varieties of orthogonal or unitary type, several recent works address the explicit computation of correction terms to the generating series of special divisors coming from an appropriate toroidal compactification: the case of modular curves was treated by Funke in
[
10
]
and related computations for toroidal compactifications of unitary Shimura varieties where only type II degenerations appear are in
[
3
]
. Recently Bruinier and Zemel
[
4
]
have proved a result for special divisors on orthogonal Shimura varieties that is similar to the modularity statement in Theorem
1.2
. Their proof involves studying the asymptotic behaviour of Borcherds lifts along components of a toroidal compactification. A different proof (and refinement) using more geometric methods has been very recently obtained by Engel, Greer and Tayou in
[
8
]
. Our paper contributes the explicit description of boundary terms in terms of limiting mixed Hodge structures and, like
[
8
]
, it also clarifies the rationality properties of coefficients along type III contributions.

1.3.
Strategy of proof

In contrast to the above works, this paper does not rely on the theory of toroidal compactifications of Shimura varieties. Instead, our proofs are analytic in nature and use Schmid’s results on degenerations of Hodge structure, particularly his characterization of the weight filtration by growth of the Hodge norm and his nilpotent and
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}
–orbit theorems. For a fixed point
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\backslash S
these results imply that in a neighbourhood of
\displaystyle P
the variation
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
is well-approximated by a special type of nilpotent orbit
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
. We prove Theorem
1.1
by showing that
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}-\Theta_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(\tau)_{\mu}
and
\displaystyle\Theta_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(\tau)_{\mu}
are both locally integrable around
\displaystyle P
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
reduces to the computation of the residue of certain canonical Green functions for
\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}
along
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\backslash S
. We show that the residue agrees with that of the corresponding Green function for
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
, which can be computed exactly thanks to the explicit nature of nilpotent orbits.

One advantage of our methods over the use of toroidal compactifications that originally motivated the author’s interest is that the theta series
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
and Schmid’s theorems are available and in definitive form for arbitrary PVHS of weight two over the complement of a normal crossing divisor in a higher–dimensional base. Schmid’s several variables
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-orbit theorem
[
6
]
approximates a degeneration of Hodge structure in
\displaystyle n
variables by an
\displaystyle(n-1)
–dimensional pencil of one–dimensional nilpotent orbits; in particular, to use his theorem to study the boundary behaviour of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
one must first understand
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
along one–variable degenerations. In future work, the author intends to develop the methods in this paper to address the conjectural (mock) modularity of generating series of Noether–Lefschetz loci for certain VHS with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}>1
that are not naturally parametrized by Shimura varieties; for a particularly interesting example see
[
9
]
.

2.
Weight two PVHS over a complex algebraic curve

In this section we briefly review some relevant facts on variations of Hodge structure over a one–dimensional base. We will only consider integral polarized variations of weight two with Hodge numbers
\displaystyle(1,n,1)
for some positive integer
\displaystyle n
.

Throughout the paper we fix a connected compact Riemann surface
\displaystyle\overline{S}
and a finite collection of points
\displaystyle P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\in\overline{S}
, and write

S=\overline{S}-\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}.

Sections
2.1
and
2.2
collect definitions and known facts on degenerations of Hodge structure and approximation by nilpotent orbits. We refer the reader to
[
22
,
6
]
for proofs; our exposition follows closely Hain’s account
[
12
]
. Sections
2.3
and
2.4
compute some nilpotent orbits explicitly. The formulas in these Sections will be used later to understand the behaviour of Kudla–Millson forms around the points
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\backslash S
.

2.1.
Definitions

Consider an integral polarized variation of Hodge structure (
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS)
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\to S
of weight two over
\displaystyle S
. Here
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
is a triple
\displaystyle(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},Q,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
consisting of:

•

a local system
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
of free and finite rank abelian groups over
\displaystyle S
,

•

a (locally constant) non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

Q:\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}},

•

a descending filtration

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{F}^{0}\supset\mathcal{F}^{1}\supset\mathcal{F}^{2}

of the flat complex vector bundle
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}:=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}
by holomorphic vector bundles
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}
that are locally direct summands,

such that the fiber
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{s}=((\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})_{s},Q_{s},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{s})
over any
\displaystyle s\in S
defines a polarized Hodge structure of weight two. We assume that

h^{2,0}=\mathrm{rk}\mathcal{F}^{2}=1,

so that
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}
is a holomorphic line bundle over
\displaystyle S
that we denote by
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
.

For each
\displaystyle s\in S
we have the Hodge decomposition

(2.1)

\mathcal{V}_{s}=\oplus_{p+q=2}\mathcal{V}_{s}^{p,q}.

Let
\displaystyle C_{s}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{V}_{s})
denote the Weil operator: it acts as the identity on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{1,1}_{s}
and as
\displaystyle-1
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{s}^{2,0}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{s}^{0,2}
. The polarization induces a hermitian metric
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V},s}
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{s}
. This is the Hodge metric, defined by

(2.2)

\|v\|_{\mathcal{V},s}^{2}=Q(C_{s}v,\overline{v}),\quad v\in\mathcal{V}_{s}.

When only one PVHS is being considered, we will suppress
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}
from the notation and denote the Hodge norm of
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}_{s}
simply by
\displaystyle\|v\|_{s}^{2}
.

The polarization
\displaystyle Q
induces an isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\simeq\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
sending a vector
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}_{s}
to the linear functional
\displaystyle v^{\prime}\mapsto Q(v^{\prime},v)
. Composing this isomorphism with the canonical surjection
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{L}^{\vee}
dual to the inclusion
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{V}
gives an isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}^{1}\simeq\mathcal{L}^{\vee}
. In particular, to a section
\displaystyle v\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(U,\mathcal{V})
defined over
\displaystyle U\subset S
corresponds a section
\displaystyle s_{v}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(U,\mathcal{L}^{\vee})
. It will be convenient to define

(2.3)

h(s_{v})=2\|s_{v}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}^{\vee}}.

Writing
\displaystyle v_{z}=\Sigma v^{p,q}_{z}
for the Hodge decomposition of
\displaystyle v_{z}\in\mathcal{V}_{z}
, the value of
\displaystyle h(s_{v})
at
\displaystyle z\in U
is given by

(2.4)

h(s_{v})_{z}=2\|v_{z}^{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}=-2Q(v^{2,0}_{z},\overline{v^{2,0}_{z}}).

2.2.
Local monodromy and limit mixed Hodge structure

The asymptotic behaviour of
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\to S
around each of the points
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\backslash S
can be described precisely in terms of limit mixed Hodge structures using the results of Schmid in
[
22
]
. We briefly recall the results that we will use.

Let
\displaystyle\Delta=\{t\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ |t|<1\}
denote the open unit disk in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
and let
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}=\Delta-\{0\}
be the punctured open unit disk. Consider a polarized variation of Hodge structure
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},Q,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
of weight two over
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
.

2.2.1.
Local monodromy and weight filtration

For
\displaystyle s\in\Delta^{*}
, let
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s}
be the fiber of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
over
\displaystyle s
. This fiber carries an action of the fundamental group
\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\Delta^{*},s)
. We denote by

T\in\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s},Q)\subset\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s})

the monodromy operator, that is, the image in
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s})
of the generator of
\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\Delta^{*},s)
defined by the loop
\displaystyle t\mapsto se^{2\pi it}
for
\displaystyle t\in[0,1]
. Then (
[
22
, Thm. 6.1]
)
\displaystyle T
is quasi-unipotent, i.e. there exist positive integers
\displaystyle e
and
\displaystyle M
such that

(T^{e}-1)^{M}=0.

Passing to a cover of
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
of degree
\displaystyle e
, we may assume that
\displaystyle e=1
. Moreover, we can take
\displaystyle M\leq 3
(
[
22
, Thm. 6.1]
) and, if
\displaystyle T=1
, then the polarized variation of Hodge structure
\displaystyle(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},Q,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
can be extended to the open unit disk
\displaystyle\Delta
(
[
22
, Cor. 4.11]
).

Let

N=\log T=\sum_{k=1}^{M-1}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{(T-1)^{k}}{k}.

Then
\displaystyle N^{3}=0
. If
\displaystyle T\neq 1
, this leaves two possibilities:

•

\displaystyle N^{2}=0
(Type II degeneration), and

•

\displaystyle N^{2}\neq 0
(Type III degeneration).

To the nilpotent endomorphism
\displaystyle N
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}s}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
corresponds an increasing filtration
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}(N)
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}s}
by
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces called the weight filtration. It is the unique filtration

\cdots\subseteq W_{k}(N)\subseteq W_{k+1}(N)\subseteq\cdots

of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
satisfying

\displaystyle N\cdot W_{k}(N)\subseteq W_{k-2}(N)

and such that

N^{k}:W_{k}(N)/W_{k-1}(N)\to W_{-k}(N)/W_{-k-1}(N)

is an isomorphism. We write

W_{k}=W_{k-2}(N)

for the (shifted) weight filtration of
\displaystyle N
. Since
\displaystyle N^{3}=0
, this filtration satisfies
\displaystyle W_{-1}=0
and
\displaystyle W_{4}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}s}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Abusing notation, we denote by
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
the corresponding filtration of the local system
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
:

0=W_{-1}\subseteq W_{0}\subseteq W_{1}\subseteq W_{2}\subseteq W_{3}\subseteq W_{4}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Since
\displaystyle N=\log T\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}s},Q)
, we have

(2.5)

Q(Nv,w)=-Q(v,Nw).

It follows that the weight filtration
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
is self-dual: writing
\displaystyle W_{k}^{\perp}
for the orthogonal complement of
\displaystyle W_{k}
under
\displaystyle Q
, we have

W_{k}^{\perp}=W_{3-k}.

Moreover, the quotients

\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}:=W_{k}/W_{k-1}

carry canonical bilinear forms
\displaystyle Q_{k}
defined as follows: if
\displaystyle k\geq 2
and
\displaystyle\tilde{v},\tilde{w}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are represented by
\displaystyle v,w\in W_{k}
, we define

(2.6)

Q_{k}(\tilde{v},\tilde{w})=Q(v,N^{k-2}w).

If
\displaystyle k<2
, then we define
\displaystyle Q_{k}
so that the isomorphism
\displaystyle N^{2-k}:\mathrm{Gr}_{4-k}^{W}\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an isometry (
[
22
, Lemma 6.4]
).

2.2.2.
Canonical extension and limit MHS

The vector bundle
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\Delta^{*}}
carries a canonical flat connection
\displaystyle\nabla
. Let us fix a flat multi-valued basis
\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+2}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. We assume that this basis is chosen so as to provide a splitting of the weight filtration: that is,

W_{k}=\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{\mathrm{dim}W_{k}}\rangle

for
\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq 4
. Define a new basis
\displaystyle(\tilde{v}_{i})
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}
by

\tilde{v}_{i}(q)=\exp\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\log t\cdot N\right)v_{i}(q)

Note that parallel translation of along a positively oriented circle changes
\displaystyle v_{i}
to
\displaystyle Tv_{i}
and
\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\log t\cdot N\right)
to

\exp\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}(\log t+2\pi i)\cdot N\right)=\exp\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\log t\cdot N\right)\cdot T^{-1}.

It follows that the basis
\displaystyle(\tilde{v}_{i})
is single-valued and so it defines a trivialization
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta^{*}}^{n+2}\simeq\mathcal{V}
over
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
. The canonical extension
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}
is defined to be the extension of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}
as a constant bundle over
\displaystyle\Delta
, that is, the extension corresponding to
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+2}_{\Delta}
under the above isomorphism. We denote by
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
its fiber over
\displaystyle 0\in\Delta
. By (
2.5
), we have

Q(\tilde{v}_{i}(q),\tilde{v}_{j}(q))=Q(v_{i},v_{j})

and so the polarization
\displaystyle Q
extends to a symmetric bilinear form on the fiber
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
that we still denote by
\displaystyle Q
.

Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem
[
22
, Thm. 4.9]
states that the Hodge filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
extends to a filtration
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}
of the canonical extension
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
by locally direct factors. We write

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\bullet}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{0}

for the limit Hodge filtration, i.e. the corresponding filtration of
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
. Then we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}Q(F^{1}_{\mathrm{lim}},F^{2}_{\mathrm{lim}})&=0\\
N\cdot F^{2}_{\mathrm{lim}}&\subseteq F^{1}_{\mathrm{lim}}.\end{split}

Moreover, the basis
\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{1}(0),\ldots,\tilde{v}_{n+2}(0)
defines a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
-structure on the fiber
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
that we denote by
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and the weight and limit Hodge filtrations

(W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}})

define a mixed
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure on
\displaystyle V:=V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
Together with the action of
\displaystyle N
and the extension of
\displaystyle Q
to
\displaystyle V
, these filtrations define a polarized mixed
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure. More precisely, we have (cf.
[
6
, Def. (2.26)]
)

i)

\displaystyle(V,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}})
is a mixed
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure satisfying (
2.7
).

ii)

\displaystyle W_{\bullet}=W_{\bullet}(N)[-2]
.

iii)

Define

P_{2}=\ker(N:\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V\to\mathrm{Gr}_{0}^{W}V)\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V

and, for
\displaystyle k\neq 2
, set
\displaystyle P_{k}=\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V
. Then

(2.8)

\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V=P_{2}\oplus NP_{4}

and the restriction of the bilinear form
\displaystyle Q_{k}
in (
2.6
) to
\displaystyle P_{k}
defines a polarized
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure of weight
\displaystyle k
.

We write

h^{p,q}_{\mathrm{lim}}=\Gr^{p}_{F_{\mathrm{lim}}}\Gr_{p+q}^{W}V

for the Hodge numbers of the limiting mixed Hodge structure.

2.2.3.
Nilpotent orbit

Using the isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+2}_{\Delta}\simeq\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
, we extend the filtration
\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\bullet}
of
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
to a filtration, still denoted by
\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\bullet}
, of
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
. The corresponding nilpotent orbit is then given by the filtration

(2.9)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{nilp}}^{\bullet}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log t\cdot N\right)F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}}\subset\mathcal{V}.

Using the uniformisation of
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
via the exponential map

\pi:\mathbb{H}\to\Delta^{*},\quad z\mapsto t:=e^{2\pi iz}.

we may write

(2.10)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{nilp}}^{k}=e^{zN}F_{\mathrm{lim}}^{k}.

In a small enough neighbourhood of
\displaystyle 0
, the triple

(2.11)

\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}:=(\underline{V_{\mathbb{Z}}},Q,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{nilp}})

defines a variation of Hodge structures of weight two polarized by
\displaystyle Q
with the same Hodge numbers as
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
.

The variation
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
approximates
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
in the following sense. Let us denote by
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}
the period domain parametrising Hodge structures on
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}
polarised by
\displaystyle Q
with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
: it is the hermitian symmetric domain attached to the Lie group

G_{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{R}},Q).

We write
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\vee}
for the compact dual of
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}
; it contains
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}
and is a homogeneous complex manifold for
\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{C}}
. The pullback
\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}
to
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}
of the PVHS
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
defines a holomorphic map

\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{D}

satisfying
\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}(z+1)=e^{N}\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}(z)
. Since
\displaystyle e^{zN}
belongs to
\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{C}}
, we have
\displaystyle e^{-zN}\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}(z)\in\mathbb{D}^{\vee}
for every
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{H}
. This gives a holomorphic map

\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathbb{V}}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{D}^{\vee},\qquad\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathbb{V}}(z)=e^{-zN}\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}(z)

that is invariant under
\displaystyle z\mapsto z+1
, and so
\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathbb{V}}(z)
induces a holomorphic map

\Psi_{\mathbb{V}}:\Delta^{*}\to\mathbb{D}^{\vee},\quad\Psi_{\mathbb{V}}(e^{2\pi iz}):=e^{-zN}\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}(z).

Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem states that
\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathbb{V}}
extends to a holomorphic map defined on
\displaystyle\Delta

[
7
, §2.3]
. For the nilpotent orbit
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
this map is constant with

\Psi_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(t)=\Psi_{\mathbb{V}}(0)=F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}}.

The map
\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathbb{V}}
can be written as

\Psi_{\mathbb{V}}(t)=\psi_{\mathbb{V}}(t)\cdot F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}}

with

\psi_{\mathbb{V}}:\Delta\to G_{\mathbb{C}}

a holomorphic map satisfying
\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbb{V}}(0)=1
(for a canonical choice of
\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbb{V}}
, see
[
5
, (2.5)]
). Thus we may write

\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}(z)=e^{zN}\Psi_{\mathbb{V}}(e^{2\pi iz})=e^{zN}\psi_{\mathbb{V}}(t)F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}}=e^{zN}\psi_{\mathbb{V}}(t)e^{-zN}\Phi_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(z).

Equivalently, the Hodge filtrations of
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
satisfy

(2.12)

\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{t}=e^{zN}\psi_{\mathbb{V}}(t)e^{-zN}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{nilp},t}^{\bullet},\qquad t\in\Delta^{*}.

Given a norm
\displaystyle|\cdot|
on
\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
and assuming that
\displaystyle|\mathrm{Re}(z)|\leq 1/2
, we have the trivial estimate

(2.13)

|e^{zN}\psi_{\mathbb{V}}(t)e^{-zN}-1|=O(|t|(\log|t|)^{2k})

for some positive integer
\displaystyle k
.

2.2.4.
Approximation and Hodge norm estimates

We also need Schmid’s estimates for the Hodge norm: fix an angular sector

U=U(t_{0},\epsilon)=\{t\in\Delta^{*}|0<\arg(t-t_{0})<2\pi-\epsilon\}

of
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
and let
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{C}}|_{U}
. If
\displaystyle v\in W_{k}-W_{k-1}
, then

\|v\|^{2}_{t}\sim(-\log|t|)^{k-2},

uniformly on
\displaystyle U
(
[
22
, Thm. 6.6’]
). In
4.2
we will state a more precise version that also gives bounds for the derivatives of
\displaystyle\|v\|_{t}^{2}
.

2.3.
Type II degenerations

We say that a degeneration is of type II if
\displaystyle N^{2}=0
but
\displaystyle N
is non–trivial. For this type of degeneration, the Hodge numbers of the limit mixed Hodge structure are

h^{1,0}_{\mathrm{lim}}=h^{0,1}_{\mathrm{lim}}=h^{2,1}_{\mathrm{lim}}=h^{1,2}_{\mathrm{lim}}=1,\quad h^{1,1}_{\mathrm{lim}}=\mathrm{dim}V_{\mathbb{C}}-4

(all other Hodge numbers are zero) and the weight filtration is

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\image N

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\ker N

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{3}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=W_{4}=V.

Below we compute explicitly the Hodge norms
\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}
and Chern form
\displaystyle\Omega
associated with the corresponding nilpotent orbit; these computations will allow us to derive explicit expressions for Kudla–Millson forms for such degenerations. Let us assume that the limit mixed Hodge structure is
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
–split, i.e. that
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}
is the direct sum of pure Hodge structures. This will suffice for our intended application.

2.3.1.

Let
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{a,b}I^{a,b}
(
\displaystyle 0\leq a,b\leq 2)
denote the canonical bigrading defined by Deligne
[
6
, (2.12)]
. Since we assume that
\displaystyle V
is
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
–split, this bigrading is simply given by

(2.14)

I^{a,b}=F^{a}_{\mathrm{lim}}\cap\overline{F^{b}_{\mathrm{lim}}}\cap W_{a+b,\mathbb{C}}.

Then we have

W_{k,\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{a+b\leq k}I^{a,b},\quad F^{p}_{\mathrm{lim}}=\oplus_{a\geq p}I^{a,b}

and
\displaystyle I^{b,a}=\overline{I^{a,b}}
. Let us define

V_{k}=(\oplus_{a+b=k}I^{a,b})\cap W_{k,\mathbb{R}},

so that
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}=\oplus_{1\leq k\leq 3}V_{k}
. Via the isomorphism

V_{k}\simeq W_{k,\mathbb{R}}/W_{k-1,\mathbb{R}}=\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V_{\mathbb{R}},

the Hodge filtration on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V_{\mathbb{R}}
induces on
\displaystyle V_{k}
a pure
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight
\displaystyle k
(with Hodge filtration
\displaystyle F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}}\cap V_{k,\mathbb{C}}
).

Since the form
\displaystyle Q(\cdot,N\cdot)
polarizes the Hodge structure on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{3}^{W}V_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq V_{3}
, we can find a vector
\displaystyle e^{2,1}\in I^{2,1}
such that

iQ(e^{2,1},N\overline{e^{2,1}})=1.

We fix such a vector and write
\displaystyle e^{1,2}=\overline{e^{2,1}}\in I^{1,2}
,
\displaystyle e^{1,0}=Ne^{2,1}\in I^{1,0}
and
\displaystyle e^{0,1}=\overline{e^{1,0}}\in I^{0,1}
. Then
\displaystyle\{e^{2,1},e^{1,2}\}
is a basis for
\displaystyle V_{3}
and
\displaystyle\{e^{1,0},e^{0,1}\}
is a basis for
\displaystyle V_{1}
. Using
\displaystyle Q(F^{2}_{\mathrm{lim}},F^{1}_{\mathrm{lim}})=0
and
\displaystyle Q(W_{1},W_{2})=0
, one sees that
\displaystyle V_{2}
is orthogonal to
\displaystyle V_{1}\oplus V_{3}
and that in the basis
\displaystyle\{e^{2,1},e^{1,2},e^{1,0},e^{0,1}\}
the restriction of
\displaystyle Q
to
\displaystyle V_{1}\oplus V_{3}
is given by the matrix

(2.15)

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&-i\\
0&0&i&0\\
0&i&0&0\\
-i&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

2.3.2.

We now consider the nilpotent orbit: for
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{H}
, we write

F_{z}^{k}=e^{zN}F^{k}_{\mathrm{lim}}.

Since
\displaystyle N^{2}=0
and
\displaystyle I^{1,1}\subset\ker N
, we have
\displaystyle e^{zN}=1+zN
and hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{z}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\langle e_{z}^{2,0}\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{z}^{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=e^{zN}(F^{2}_{\mathrm{lim}}\oplus I^{1,1})=F^{2}_{z}\oplus I^{1,1},

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{z}^{2,0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=e^{2,1}+ze^{1,0}.

The Hodge norm of
\displaystyle e_{z}^{2,0}
can be computed explicitly using (
2.15
):

(2.16)

\begin{split}\|e_{z}^{2,0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}&=-Q(e_{z}^{2,0},\overline{e_{z}^{2,0}})\\
&=-Q(e^{2,1}+ze^{1,0},e^{1,2}+\overline{z}e^{0,1})\\
&=i(\overline{z}-z)\\
&=2\mathrm{Im}(z).\end{split}

We may think of
\displaystyle z\mapsto e_{z}^{2,0}
as a holomorphic section of the hermitian line bundle
\displaystyle F_{z}^{2}
. Writing
\displaystyle\Omega
for its Chern form, we find

(2.17)

\begin{split}\Omega&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\partial\overline{\partial}\log\|e_{z}^{2,0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}=\frac{i}{8\pi}\frac{dz\wedge d\overline{z}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}\end{split}

Using (
2.16
) we can compute the Hodge norm
\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}
of vectors
\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
. Fix such
\displaystyle v
and let
\displaystyle v_{z}^{p,q}
be the components of
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}_{z}=\oplus_{p,q}\mathcal{V}_{z}^{p,q}
. Then

v_{z}^{2,0}=f(z)e_{z}^{2,0}

for some holomorphic function
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
. We have
\displaystyle v_{z}^{0,2}=\overline{v_{z}^{2,0}}
and hence

Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})=Q(v^{0,2}_{z},e_{z}^{2,0})=-2\overline{f(z)}\mathrm{Im}(z).

This gives

(2.18)

\begin{split}h(s_{v})&=-2Q(v_{z}^{2,0},v_{z}^{0,2})\\
&=4|f(z)|^{2}\mathrm{Im}(z)\\
&=\frac{|Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\end{split}

and, for the Hodge norm,

(2.19)

\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V},z}=Q(v,v)+2h(s_{v})=Q(v,v)+\frac{2|Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}.

Let us consider the special case
\displaystyle v\in W_{2,\mathbb{R}}
. Such a vector can be written uniquely as

(2.20)

v=v_{2}+ae^{1,0}+\overline{a}e^{0,1}

with
\displaystyle v_{2}\in V_{2}
and a complex number
\displaystyle a
. Using
\displaystyle Q(W_{1},W_{2})=Q(F^{1}_{\mathrm{lim}},F^{2}_{\mathrm{lim}})=0
we find that

Q(v_{2},e_{z}^{2,0})=Q(v_{2},e^{2,1})+zQ(v_{2},e^{1,0})=0

and hence

Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})=Q(ae^{1,0}+\overline{a}e^{0,1},e^{2,1}+ze^{1,0})=-i\overline{a}.

We conclude that for
\displaystyle v\in W_{2,\mathbb{R}}
we have

(2.21)

h(s_{v})=\frac{|a|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}

and

(2.22)

\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V},z}=Q(v_{2},v_{2})+\frac{2|a|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}.

2.4.
Type III degenerations

We say that a degeneration is of type III if
\displaystyle N^{2}\neq 0
. In this case we have

h^{0,0}_{\mathrm{lim}}=h^{2,2}_{\mathrm{lim}}=1,\quad h^{1,1}_{\mathrm{lim}}=\mathrm{rank}V_{\mathbb{Z}}-2,

and all other Hodge numbers are zero, i.e. the real mixed Hodge structure
\displaystyle(V_{\mathbb{R}},W,F)
is Hodge–Tate. The weight filtration is

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=W_{1}=\mathrm{Im}N^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=W_{3}=\mathrm{Im}N+\ker N.

We will now do some computations analogous to the ones above in the type II case. We assume again that the limit mixed Hodge structure is
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
–split.

2.4.1.

Let
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{a,b}I^{a,b}
denote Deligne’s canonical bigrading
[
6
, (2.12)]
. For
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
–split type III degenerations we have
\displaystyle I^{p,q}=0
if
\displaystyle p\neq q
and

I^{p,p}=F_{\mathrm{lim}}^{p}\cap\overline{F_{\mathrm{lim}}^{p}}\cap W_{2p,\mathbb{C}}.

The bigrading satisfies

W_{2k,\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{a\leq k}I^{a,a},\quad F^{p}_{\mathrm{lim}}=\oplus_{a\geq p}I^{a.a}.

We set

V_{2k}=I^{k,k}\cap W_{k,\mathbb{R}},\quad k=0,1,2.

Then
\displaystyle V_{2k}
is a real Hodge structure of type
\displaystyle(k,k)
and
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}=\oplus V_{2k}
.

Since the form
\displaystyle Q(\cdot,N^{2}\cdot)
polarizes the Hodge structure on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq V_{4}
, we can find a vector (unique up to multiplication by
\displaystyle\pm 1
)
\displaystyle e^{2,2}\in V_{4}
such that

(2.23)

Q(e^{2,2},N^{2}e^{2,2})=1.

Then
\displaystyle Ne^{2,2}\in V_{2}
and
\displaystyle N^{2}e^{2,2}\in V_{0}
and both vectors are non-zero. Since
\displaystyle V_{0}
and
\displaystyle V_{4}
are one–dimensional we have

V_{4}=\langle e^{2,2}\rangle,\quad V_{0}=\langle N^{2}e^{2,2}\rangle.

Let us define

U=\ker(N:V_{2}\to V_{0})

Under the isomorphism
\displaystyle V_{2}\simeq\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{R}}
induced by the quotient map
\displaystyle W_{2,\mathbb{R}}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{R}}
, the subspace
\displaystyle U\subset V_{2}
corresponds to the primitive part
\displaystyle P_{2,\mathbb{R}}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{R}}
; in particular, the restriction of
\displaystyle Q
to
\displaystyle U
is positive definite. We have

V_{2}=U\oplus\langle Ne^{2,2}\rangle.

This decomposition is orthogonal for
\displaystyle Q
since
\displaystyle N\in\mathfrak{so}(V,Q)
. It follows that
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}
can be written as

(2.24)

V_{\mathbb{R}}=U\oplus\langle e^{2,2},Ne^{2,2},N^{2}e^{2,2}\rangle

with
\displaystyle\langle e^{2,2},Ne^{2,2},N^{2}e^{2,2}\rangle=U^{\perp}
(in fact this is a decomposition as real mixed Hodge structures with the natural Hodge filtrations defined by intersecting
\displaystyle F^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{lim}}
with each summand). By (
2.23
), the matrix of the restriction of
\displaystyle Q
to
\displaystyle U^{\perp}
in the basis
\displaystyle\{e^{2,2},Ne^{2,2},N^{2}e^{2,2}\}
is

(2.25)

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&-1&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

2.4.2.

Consider now the nilpotent orbit corresponding to an
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
–split type III degeneration: for
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{H}
, let

F^{k}_{z}=e^{zN}F_{\mathrm{lim}}^{k}.

Since the restriction of
\displaystyle N
to
\displaystyle U
vanishes, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{z}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\langle e_{z}^{2,0}\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{z}^{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=e^{zN}(V_{4,\mathbb{C}}\oplus V_{2,\mathbb{C}})=U_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\langle e_{z}^{2,0},Ne_{z}^{2,0}\rangle,

with

e_{z}^{2,0}:=e^{zN}e^{2,2}=e^{2,2}+zNe^{2,2}+\frac{z^{2}}{2}N^{2}e^{2,2}.

Using (
2.25
) we can compute the Hodge norm of
\displaystyle e_{z}^{2,0}
:

(2.26)

\begin{split}\|e_{z}^{2,0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}&=-Q(e_{z}^{2,0},\overline{e_{z}^{2,0}})\\
&=-Q(e^{2,2}+zNe^{2,2}+\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}N^{2}e^{2,2},e^{2,2}+\overline{z}Ne^{2,2}+\tfrac{\overline{z}^{2}}{2}N^{2}e^{2,2})\\
&=-(\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}+\tfrac{\overline{z}^{2}}{2}-|z|^{2})\\
&=2\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}.\end{split}

Writing
\displaystyle\Omega
for the first Chern form of the hermitian line bundle
\displaystyle F^{2}_{z}
, this gives

(2.27)

\Omega=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\partial\overline{\partial}\log\|e_{z}^{2,0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}=\frac{i}{4\pi}\frac{dz\wedge d\overline{z}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}.

The argument we used in the case of type II degenerations shows that

(2.28)

h(s_{v})=-2\frac{|Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})|^{2}}{Q(e_{z}^{2,0},\overline{e_{z}^{2,0}})}=\frac{|Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}

and, for the Hodge norm,

(2.29)

\|v\|_{\mathcal{V},z}^{2}=Q(v,v)+2h(s_{v})=Q(v,v)+\frac{2|Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}.

Again we consider the special case
\displaystyle v\in W_{2,\mathbb{R}}=V_{0}\oplus V_{2}
. Such a vector can be written as

(2.30)

v=v_{U}+aNe^{2,2}+bN^{2}e^{2,2}

for unique
\displaystyle v_{U}\in U
and real numbers
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
. Using
\displaystyle Q(F^{1}_{\mathrm{lim}},F^{2}_{\mathrm{lim}})=0
and
\displaystyle Q(v,Nv^{\prime})=-Q(Nv,v^{\prime})
, we find that

Q(v_{U},e_{z}^{2,0})=Q(v_{U},e^{2,2})+zQ(v_{U},Ne^{2,2})+\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}Q(v_{U},N^{2}e^{2,2})=0

and hence

(2.31)

\begin{split}Q(v,e_{z}^{2,0})&=Q(aNe^{2.2}+bN^{2}e^{2,2},e^{2,2}+zNe^{2,2}+\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}N^{2}e^{2,2})\\
&=b-az.\end{split}

We conclude that for
\displaystyle v\in W_{2,\mathbb{R}}
we have

(2.32)

h(s_{v})=\frac{|b-az|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}=a^{2}+\left(\frac{b-a\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)^{2}

and

(2.33)

\begin{split}\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V},z}&=Q(v_{U},v_{U})-a^{2}+\frac{2|b-az|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}\\
&=Q(v_{U},v_{U})+a^{2}+2\left(\frac{b-a\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Remark 2.1
.

The formulas for Hodge norms will be used in Section
4.5
to derive explicit expressions for
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)
for type III degenerations that agree with those computed by Funke in
[
10
]
.

3.
Kudla–Millson theta series and degenerations of Hodge structure

In this section we briefly review some of the needed background regarding the Weil representation and the construction of theta series attached to a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
–PVHS
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
on
\displaystyle S
by pulling back Kudla–Millson theta series via the period map associated with
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
. We will also define certain theta series attached to limiting mixed Hodge structures.

3.1.
Weil representation

3.1.1.

Let
\displaystyle L
be an even lattice, that is, a free abelian group of finite rank endowed with a non–degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\displaystyle Q:L\times L\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
\displaystyle Q(v,v)
is even for every
\displaystyle v\in L
. We denote its signature by
\displaystyle(b^{+},b^{-})
and write

L^{\vee}=\{v\in L\otimes\mathbb{Q}\ |\ Q(v,w)\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }w\in L\}

for the dual of
\displaystyle L
. Thus
\displaystyle L\subseteq L^{\vee}
, and the finite group
\displaystyle L^{\vee}/L
is known as the discriminant group of
\displaystyle L
. We denote by

\mathbb{C}[L^{\vee}/L]

its group algebra and by
\displaystyle e^{\mu}
(
\displaystyle\mu\in L^{\vee}/L
) its standard basis.

We write
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
for the metaplectic double
cover of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Its elements are pairs of the form

\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},\phi(\tau)\right),

where
\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\displaystyle\phi(\tau)
satisfies
\displaystyle\phi(\tau)^{2}=c\tau+d
. It is generated by the elements

(3.1)

\begin{split}T&=\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},1\right),\\
S&=\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\sqrt{\tau}\right).\end{split}

There is a representation
\displaystyle\rho_{L}
of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[L^{\vee}/L]
determined by the formulas

(3.2)

\begin{split}\rho_{L}(T)(e^{\mu})&=e^{\pi iQ(\mu,\mu)}e^{\mu}\\
\rho_{L}(S)(e^{\mu})&=\frac{e^{\pi i(b^{-}-b^{+})/4}}{\sqrt{|L^{\vee}/L|}}\sum_{\lambda\in L^{\vee}/L}e^{-2\pi iQ(\mu,\lambda)}e^{\lambda}.\end{split}

The representation
\displaystyle\rho_{L}
factors through a double cover of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, where
\displaystyle N
(sometimes called the level of
\displaystyle L
) is the smallest integer such that
\displaystyle NQ(\lambda,\lambda)/2
is an integer for all
\displaystyle\lambda\in L^{\vee}
.

3.1.2.

Let
\displaystyle V=L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and suppose given a filtration

0\neq W_{1}\subseteq W_{2}\subseteq V

of
\displaystyle V
by
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces such that
\displaystyle W_{1}
is isotropic and
\displaystyle W_{2}=W_{1}^{\perp}
. Define
\displaystyle L_{k}=W_{k}\cap L
and set
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}L=L_{2}/L_{1}
. Then
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}L
is an even lattice with respect to the bilinear form induced by
\displaystyle Q
. The group
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on

\mathbb{C}[(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}L)^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}L]=\mathbb{C}[(L^{\vee}\cap W_{2})/(L^{\vee}\cap W_{1}+L_{2})]

via the corresponding Weil representation
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}L}
. The map

(3.3)

\iota:\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}L}\to\rho_{L},\quad e^{\mu}\mapsto\sum_{\lambda\in(L^{\vee}\cap W_{1}+L)/L}e^{\lambda+\mu}

intertwines the
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
–actions
[
21
, Prop. 6.1]
.

3.1.3.

We briefly recall some notions of modular forms for
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{L}
; see
[
2
, Chap. 1]
for more details. Let

f:\mathbb{H}\to\rho_{L}

be a smooth function and
\displaystyle k^{+},k^{-}\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. We say that
\displaystyle f
is a non–holomorphic modular form of weight
\displaystyle(k^{+},k^{-})
valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{L}
if

(3.4)

f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=\phi(\tau)^{2k^{+}}\overline{\phi(\tau)}^{2k^{-}}\rho_{L}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},\phi(\tau)\right)f(\tau)

for every
\displaystyle\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),\phi(\tau)\right)\in\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

If
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{H}\to\rho_{L}
is holomorphic and satisfies (
3.4
) with
\displaystyle(k^{+},k^{-})=(k,0)
, then we may write

f(\tau)=\sum_{\mu}f_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu},

where the components
\displaystyle f_{\mu}
of
\displaystyle f
are weakly modular forms of weight
\displaystyle k
. We say that
\displaystyle f
is a modular (resp. cusp) form of weight
\displaystyle k
valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{L}
if it each component is a modular (resp. cusp) form of weight
\displaystyle k
.

The most important examples of modular forms valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{L}
arise from theta series. For a positive definite even lattice
\displaystyle L
and
\displaystyle\mu\in L^{\vee}
, define

\Theta_{L}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mu+L}e^{\pi iQ(\lambda,\lambda)\tau}

and set

\Theta_{L}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu}\Theta_{L}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu}.

Then
\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\tau)
is a modular form valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{L}
of weight
\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}(L)/2
(
[
1
, Thm 4.1]
).

3.2.
Kudla–Millson theta series

3.2.1.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\to S
be a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
–PVHS satisfying
1.1
. Associated with
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
there is a period map

\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}:S\to\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}

into a quotient of the hermitian symmetric space attached to
\displaystyle\mathrm{SO}(h^{1,1},2)
(see e.g.
[
22
, p. 227-228]
). More precisely, fix a point
\displaystyle s_{0}\in S
and let
\displaystyle\pi:\tilde{S}\to S
be the universal cover of
\displaystyle S
. The pullback
\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
to
\displaystyle\tilde{S}
is then a constant local system endowed with a constant bilinear form induced by
\displaystyle Q
, i.e. of the form
\displaystyle\underline{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
for some indefinite lattice
\displaystyle(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
. It carries a canonical action

(3.5)

\pi_{1}(S,s_{0})\to\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q).

Let now
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}=V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
and denote by
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}
the space of all Hodge structures on
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}
polarized by
\displaystyle Q
with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
; thus
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}
is the hermitian symmetric domain attached to the orthogonal group
\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{R}},Q)
. The pullback
\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}
induces a holomorphic map
\displaystyle\Phi_{\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}}:\tilde{S}\to\mathbb{D}
. If
\displaystyle\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
is any subgroup containing the image of (
3.5
), then the composite of
\displaystyle\Phi_{\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}}
with the projection
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\to\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
induces a holomorphic map
\displaystyle S\to\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
. Under assumption
1.1
, and identifying
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we can take for
\displaystyle\Gamma
the group

\Gamma:=\Gamma_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\left\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)\ |\ \gamma\equiv\mathrm{id}\text{ on }V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/V_{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},

and denote the corresponding period map by

(3.6)

\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}:S\to\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}.

3.2.2.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(V_{\mathbb{R}})
be the Schwartz space of
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}
. In their seminal works
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, Kudla and Millson have introduced certain differential forms

\varphi_{\mathrm{KM}}\in(\Omega^{1,1}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{S}(V_{\mathbb{R}}))^{\mathrm{SO}(V_{\mathbb{R}},Q)}

and associated theta series

\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{v\in\mu+V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\varphi_{\mathrm{KM}}(y^{1/2}v)e^{\pi ixQ(v,v)}\in\Omega^{1,1}(\mathbb{D})^{\Gamma}\simeq\Omega^{1,1}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}).

Using the period map
\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}
we can define differential forms on
\displaystyle S
canonically associated with
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
by pulling back the Kudla–Millson theta series.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, define

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}^{*}\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}(\tau)_{\mu}.

The results of Kudla and Millson imply that the forms
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
have modularity properties that can be described most easily by saying that the differential form

(3.7)

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau):=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu}\in\Omega^{1,1}(S)\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}.

transforms under
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
like a non–holomorphic modular form of weight
\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})/2
valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Using the formulas given by Kudla and Millson we can describe
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
as

(3.8)

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}v)e^{\pi ixQ(v,v)},

with
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)
(which is only locally defined, e.g. on a small disk around a given point in
\displaystyle S
) given by

(3.9)

\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)=e^{-\pi\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}(-\Omega+ih(s_{v})\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}),\quad\theta=\frac{\partial h(s_{v})}{h(s_{v})}.

We briefly explain the terms in this formula; for more details, see also
[
11
]
. The terms
\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}
and
\displaystyle h(s_{v})
have been defined in
2.1
:
\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}
denotes the Hodge norm of
\displaystyle v
and the value of
\displaystyle h(s_{v})
at
\displaystyle z\in S
is

h(s_{v})_{z}=-2Q(v^{2,0}_{z},v^{0,2}_{z}).

The term
\displaystyle\Omega
denotes the first Chern form of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
, i.e.

(3.10)

\Omega=(2\pi i)^{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\log\|s\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}

for any meromorphic section
\displaystyle s
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
.

We will sometimes write

(3.11)

\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)=e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(v),

with

(3.12)

\varphi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(v)=e^{-2\pi h(s_{v})}(-\Omega+ih(s_{v})\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}),\quad\theta=\frac{\partial h(s_{v})}{h(s_{v})}.

3.3.
Theta series and limit MHS

We now associate a vector–valued theta series to a limiting mixed Hodge structure of type II or III.

3.3.1.
Type II

For a type II degeneration, the polarization
\displaystyle Q
induces a quadratic form on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V
that we still denote by
\displaystyle Q
; note that in this case
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V=P_{2}
and hence this quadratic form is positive definite. The image of
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap W_{2}
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V
defines a lattice that we denote by
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. We write

(3.13)

\begin{split}\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}&=\mathbb{C}[(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}]\\
&=\mathbb{C}\left[W_{2}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/(W_{1}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}+W_{2}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}})\right]\end{split}

for the corresponding Weil representation of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Associated to the positive–definite even lattice
\displaystyle(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
is the theta series

\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu}\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu}.

It is a modular form valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
of weight
\displaystyle(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})-4)/2
.

More generally, the image of
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap W_{k}
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V
is a lattice that we denote by
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then

N:\mathrm{Gr}_{3}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{1}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}

is an injective map between lattices of the same rank; following
[
23
]
we write

r_{1}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},N)

for the size of its cokernel. The form
\displaystyle Q
also induces a non-degenerate bilinear pairing

(3.14)

\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{3,1}Q:\mathrm{Gr}_{3}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{1}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\displaystyle\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}_{3,1}^{W}Q)
be its discriminant, that is

\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}_{3,1}^{W}Q)=|\det(\mathrm{Gr}_{3,1}^{W}Q(\tilde{v}_{i},\tilde{w}_{j}))|

for any bases
\displaystyle(\tilde{v}_{i})
of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{3}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\displaystyle(\tilde{w}_{j})
of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{1}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Note that the form
\displaystyle Q_{3}(v,w)=Q(v,Nw)
is symplectic and takes integral values on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{3}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and hence

r_{1}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},N)\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}_{3,1}^{W}Q)=|\det(Q_{3}(\tilde{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{j}))|=\deg(Q_{3})^{2}

for a positive integer
\displaystyle\deg(Q_{3})
.

Let us write

(3.15)

\iota:\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\to\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}

for the
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
–intertwining map defined in (
3.3
); we recall that

\iota(e^{\lambda})=\sum_{\gamma\in(W_{1}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}+V_{\mathbb{Z}})/V_{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{\gamma+\lambda}.

For
\displaystyle\mu\in(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, define

(3.16)

Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)_{\mu}=\left(\frac{r_{1}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},N)}{\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}_{3,1}^{W}Q)}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{4\pi y}\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)_{\mu}

and set

(3.17)

Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu\in(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot\iota(e^{\mu}).

3.3.2.
Type III

Let us now consider degenerations of type III. Let

(3.18)

\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V=\ker(N:W_{2}/W_{1}\to W_{0})\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V.

Then
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}V
is a vector space over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
of dimension
\displaystyle n-1
. The subgroup

\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V\cap\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{2}V_{\mathbb{Z}}

is a lattice in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V
. Since
\displaystyle W_{1}
is
\displaystyle Q
-isotropic, the polarization
\displaystyle Q
induces a quadratic form on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V
that is positive definite and that we still denote by
\displaystyle Q
. Let us write

(3.19)

\begin{split}\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}&=\mathbb{C}[(\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}]\end{split}

for be the corresponding Weil representation of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Associated to the positive definite even lattice
\displaystyle(\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
is the theta series

\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu}\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu}

that transforms under
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
like a holomorphic modular form valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
of weight
\displaystyle(n-1)/2
.

The bilinear form
\displaystyle Q
induces a pairing

\mathrm{Gr}_{4,0}^{W}Q:\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{0}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\displaystyle\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}_{4,0}^{W}Q)
be its discriminant, that is

\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}_{4,0}^{W}Q)=|\det(\mathrm{Gr}_{4,0}^{W}Q(\tilde{v}_{i},\tilde{w}_{j}))|

for any bases
\displaystyle(\tilde{v}_{i})
of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\displaystyle(\tilde{w}_{j})
of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{0}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
respectively.

Let us now consider the rank one lattice

\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}=\text{image of }V_{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ in }\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V,

endowed with the positive–definite quadratic
\displaystyle Q_{4}(v,v)=Q(v,N^{2}v)
defined in (
2.6
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle L
be the rank one lattice
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
endowed with the positive–definite quadratic form
\displaystyle v\mapsto Q_{4}(v,v)
.

(i)

The lattice
\displaystyle L
is even.

(ii)

The image of
\displaystyle L
under
\displaystyle N:\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V
lies in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(iii)

The image of
\displaystyle L^{\vee}
under
\displaystyle N:\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V
contains

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}\cap N(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V)
.

Proof.

For a degeneration of type III we have

N=(T-1)-(T-1)^{2}/2

and so
\displaystyle N^{2}=(T-1)^{2}
. Hence we can write
\displaystyle N=(T-1)-N^{2}/2
and for
\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

Q(v,N^{2}v)=-Q(Nv,Nv)=-Q((T-1)v,(T-1)v)

which is an even integer since
\displaystyle(T-1)v\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. This proves (i). Part (ii) follows from the identity
\displaystyle N=(T-1)-N^{2}/2
, which implies that
\displaystyle N\equiv(T-1)\mod W_{0}
. For part (iii), suppose that
\displaystyle w\in(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}\cap N(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V)
and write
\displaystyle w=Nv
with
\displaystyle v\in\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V
. For any
\displaystyle w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

Q(v,Nw^{\prime})=-Q(w,w^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z},

i.e.
\displaystyle Q(v,v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}
for every
\displaystyle v^{\prime}\in N(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})
. Part (ii) implies that
\displaystyle N^{2}(L)\subseteq N(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})
and hence that
\displaystyle v\in L^{\vee}
.
∎

The proof the lemma shows that
\displaystyle N^{2}=(T-1)^{2}
is integral. Thus

\mathrm{Gr}^{W}N^{2}:\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{0}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}

is an injective map of lattices of the same rank; we denote its cokernel by

r_{2}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},N).

Writing
\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{4}V_{\mathbb{Z}}):=Q_{4}(v_{0},v_{0})\in 2\mathbb{N}
where
\displaystyle v_{0}
is a generator of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{4}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{4}V_{\mathbb{Z}})=r_{2}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},N)\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{4,0}Q).

Associated to the positive–definite even lattice
\displaystyle(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q_{4})
is the Weil representation

\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\mathbb{C}[(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}]

of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

and the non–holomorphic unary theta series

(3.20)

R_{\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)_{\nu}=\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{y}}\sum_{v\in\nu+\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta_{3/2}(2\pi yQ_{4}(v,v))q^{-Q_{4}(v,v)/2},

where

\beta_{3/2}(t)=\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{-3/2}e^{-tu}du.

Let us write
\displaystyle(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{-}
for the negative–definite even lattice defined by
\displaystyle-Q_{4}
. By the above lemma, the map

\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\oplus}(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{-}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}},\quad(v,w)\mapsto v+Nw

identifies
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\oplus}(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{-}
with a sublattice of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
of finite index. This induces a natural
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
–intertwining map

(3.21)

\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes\rho_{(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{-}}\simeq\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{-}}\to\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}

e^{\lambda}\otimes e^{\nu}\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{rr}0,&\text{ if }\lambda+N\nu\notin(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee},\\
\lambda+N\nu+\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}},&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let

(3.22)

\iota:\rho_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes\rho_{(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{-}}\to\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}

be the map obtained by composing (
3.21
) with the map (
3.15
). For
\displaystyle\mu\in(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, define

(3.23)

\begin{split}Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)_{\mu}=&\left(\frac{r_{2}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},N)}{2\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{4,0}Q)}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda+N\nu\equiv\mu\\
\mod\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\end{subarray}}R_{\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)_{\nu}\cdot\Theta_{\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau)_{\lambda}\end{split}

and set

(3.24)

Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu\in(\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\vee}/\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}}Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot\iota(e^{\mu}).

4.
Integrability of Kudla–Millson theta series

4.1.
A convergence result

Let
\displaystyle\overline{S}
be a compact Riemann surface and let
\displaystyle S
be obtained by removing a finite set of points from
\displaystyle\overline{S}
. Consider a polarized variation of Hodge structure
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\to S
of weight two with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
and
\displaystyle h^{1,1}=n
satisfying
1.1
. In the previous section we have attached to
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
a collection of closed differential forms

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}\in\Omega^{1,1}(S),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},

that vary smoothly in
\displaystyle\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and transform like (non-holomorphic) modular forms of weight
\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})/2
. If
\displaystyle S=\overline{S}
, i.e. when
\displaystyle S
is compact, the integral

(4.1)

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}:=\int_{S}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}

is obviously convergent, and the results of Kudla and Millson
[
18
]
show that
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
is a holomorphic modular form of weight
\displaystyle 1+n/2
with
\displaystyle q
-expansion

-\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L})\delta_{\mu,0}+\sum_{m>0}\mathrm{deg}\ \mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}\cdot q^{m}.

A little more precisely: the
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
are the components of a modular form of weight
\displaystyle 1+n/2
valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Now suppose that
\displaystyle S
is not compact; in that case, the differential form
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
might not extend to a smooth form on
\displaystyle\overline{S}
. Fortunately, as we will show below, the singularities of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
around the points in
\displaystyle\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S
are very mild. In particular,
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
is always integrable over
\displaystyle S
and so one can define
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
as in (
4.1
) for arbitrary
\displaystyle S
. This is the content of the following proposition, whose proof will comprise most of this section. Let us write

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{m\in\tfrac{1}{2}Q(\mu,\mu)+\mathbb{Z}}\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}\cdot q^{m},

with

\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}\varphi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}v).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
be a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
–PVHS over
\displaystyle S
of weight two with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
. Then the integral

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}:=\int_{S}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}

converges for all
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The forms
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}
are also integrable over
\displaystyle S
for any
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
and
we have

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{m\in\tfrac{Q(\mu,\mu)}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{S}\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}\right)q^{m}.

The expression

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau):=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}\cdot e^{\mu}

defines a (possibly non-holomorphic) modular form of weight
\displaystyle 1+n/2
valued in
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

The following remarks reduce the proof of this theorem to the analogous local question around each cusp. Moreover, they show that when addressing the local question we may assume the local monodromy to be unipotent and non-trivial.

(1)

Fix a point
\displaystyle s_{0}\in S
and a simply connected neighbourhood
\displaystyle U
of
\displaystyle s_{0}
and choose a trivialization of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U}
. The Hodge metric on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}|_{U}
can then be identified with a smooth map from
\displaystyle U
to the space of hermitian metrics on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
. Hence, after possibly shrinking
\displaystyle U
, the Hodge metric on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}|_{U}
is uniformly bounded below by some constant metric
\displaystyle v\mapsto|v|_{0}
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
. It follows that on such a neighbourhood we can find
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
so that

|\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)|_{s}<e^{-\epsilon|v|_{0}^{2}}

for every
\displaystyle s\in U
and every flat section
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
over
\displaystyle U
. By dominated convergence, this implies that the above proposition holds locally, that is, replacing
\displaystyle S
by a small enough neighbourhood of any given point
\displaystyle s_{0}\in S
. Taking a finite covering of
\displaystyle\overline{S}
shows that it suffices to prove the proposition for a coordinate neighbourhood of each cusp, i.e. for
\displaystyle S\simeq\Delta^{*}
. In the rest of Section
4
we will assume that
\displaystyle S=\Delta^{*}
and denote by
\displaystyle T
the local monodromy as in Section
2.2
.

(2)

Recall that
\displaystyle T
is quasi–unipotent: there exist positive integers
\displaystyle e
and
\displaystyle m
such that
\displaystyle(T^{e}-\mathrm{id})^{m}=0
. Let
\displaystyle\pi:\Delta^{*}\to\Delta^{*}
be the covering map of degree
\displaystyle e
. Then
\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}
is a PVHS over
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
with unipotent monodromy
\displaystyle T^{e}
. Since

\Theta_{\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}}(\tau)=\pi^{*}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau),

the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
over
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
implies that of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)
. It follows that we may assume that
\displaystyle T
is unipotent.

(3)

Finally, recall from Section
2.2
that if
\displaystyle T=\mathrm{id}
, then the period map associated to the PVHS
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
extends to
\displaystyle\Delta
; in this case the argument in (1) proves the proposition. So in the rest of Section
4
we will assume that
\displaystyle T
is unipotent and
\displaystyle T\neq 1
.

So we will prove the proposition for
\displaystyle S=\Delta^{*}
and a PVHS
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\to\Delta^{*}
with unipotent non-trivial monodromy. In order to ensure that the only degeneration of
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
happens as
\displaystyle t\to 0
, we may and do assume that
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
extends to a punctured disk centered at
\displaystyle 0
of radius strictly larger than one.

The proof is based on Schmid’s Hodge norm estimates and his nilpotent orbit and
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}
–orbit theorems. As a first step, let us write

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}+\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu},

with

(4.2)

\begin{split}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
v\in W_{2}\end{subarray}}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}v)\ e^{\pi iQ(v,v)x}\\
\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
v\notin W_{2}\end{subarray}}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}v)\ e^{\pi iQ(v,v)x},\end{split}

and similarly we write
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}=\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}+\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime\prime}_{m,\mu}
.

It turns out that the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}
and
\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime\prime}_{m,\mu}
is easier to prove: it is a straightforward consequence of the Hodge norm estimates. The integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}
and
\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}
is more delicate: our proof proceeds by bounding the difference
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)-\varphi_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(v)
and computing
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(v)
explicitly.

4.2.
Integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\prime\prime}

Let us fix a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
over the punctured unit disk

\Delta^{*}=\{t\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ 0<|t|<1\}

with unipotent non-trivial monodromy. We will first establish the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}
and
\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{m}
.

For this it suffices to work on a fixed angular sector

(4.3)

U:=\{t\in\Delta^{*}|\epsilon<\arg(t)<2\pi-\epsilon\}\subset\Delta^{*}.

In order to estimate the size of differential forms on
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
, we work with the Poincaré metric, defined by declaring that the coframe
\displaystyle\tfrac{dt}{t\log|t|}
and
\displaystyle\tfrac{d\overline{t}}{\overline{t}\log|t|}
is unitary. In particular, a form
\displaystyle\alpha\in\Omega^{1,1}(\Delta^{*})
can be written as

\alpha=\alpha_{11}\frac{dtd\overline{t}}{|t|^{2}(\log|t|)^{2}}

for a unique smooth function
\displaystyle\alpha_{11}
on
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
, and for
\displaystyle t\in\Delta^{*}
we set

|\alpha|_{t}:=|\alpha_{11}(t)|.

We say that
\displaystyle\alpha
is rapidly decreasing if
\displaystyle|\alpha|_{t}=O(t^{\epsilon})
for some
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
and
\displaystyle t
in a given
\displaystyle U
.

Fix a basis
\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+2}
adapted to the weight filtration as in
2.2.2
giving a trivialization
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}|_{U}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}^{n+2}
and denote by
\displaystyle h(t)=(h_{ij}(t))
the matrix of the Hodge metric in this basis: for a flat section
\displaystyle v=a_{1}v_{1}+\ldots a_{n+2}v_{n+2}
and
\displaystyle t\in U
we have

(4.4)

\|v\|^{2}_{t}=a^{*}h(t)a=\sum_{i,j}\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}h_{ij}(t).

The basis
\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+2}
gives a splitting of the complexified weight filtration: writing

(4.5)

Y_{k}=\langle v_{1+\dim W_{k-1}},\ldots v_{\dim W_{k}}\rangle\subset V_{\mathbb{C}},

we have
\displaystyle W_{k,\mathbb{C}}=Y_{k}\oplus W_{{k-1},\mathbb{C}}
for all
\displaystyle k\geq 0
. Following Kóllar
[
13
, Definition 5.3.(v)]
, denote by

e:\mathcal{V}|_{U}\to\mathcal{V}|_{U}

the endomorphism of the vector bundle
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}|_{U}
that acts on the fiber
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{t}
by

v\mapsto(-\log|t|)^{(k-2)/2}v\quad\text{ if }v\in Y_{k}

and set

\tilde{h}={{}^{t}}e^{-1}he^{-1}.

It follows from the Hodge norm estimates that the entries
\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{ij}
of
\displaystyle\tilde{h}
and
\displaystyle(\det\tilde{h})^{-1}
are bounded. More precisely, write
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\omega}(\Delta)
for the set of real analytic functions on
\displaystyle\Delta
and
\displaystyle L
for the set of Laurent polynomials in
\displaystyle(-\log|t|)^{1/2}
with complex coefficients and define

B\Delta=\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}(\Delta)\otimes L\ |\ f\text{ bounded}\}.

Then

(4.6)

\tilde{h}_{ij},\ (\det\tilde{h})^{-1}\in B\Delta

(cf.
[
13
, Prop. 5.4]
). Moreover,
\displaystyle B\Delta
is closed under the operators
\displaystyle t\log|t|\tfrac{d}{dt}
and
\displaystyle\overline{t}\log|t|\tfrac{d}{d\overline{t}}
. Hence, in the coframe given by
\displaystyle\tfrac{dt}{t\log|t|}
and
\displaystyle\tfrac{d\overline{t}}{\overline{t}\log|t|}
, the forms

(4.7)

\partial\tilde{h}_{ij},\overline{\partial}\tilde{h}_{ij},\partial\overline{\partial}\tilde{h}_{ij}

have components that belong to
\displaystyle B\Delta
; we will refer to forms with this property as nearly bounded (loc. cit., Def. 5.3). Note that the product of two nearly bounded forms is nearly bounded.

We can use the bounds (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) to give an estimate for the form
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a positive constant
\displaystyle C
such that

|\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)|_{t}<Ce^{-\pi\|v\|_{t}^{2}}\left(1+\|v\|_{t}^{2}\right)

for any
\displaystyle t\in U
and any
\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Let

(4.8)

p_{\mathbb{V}}(v)=\left(-\Omega+ih(s_{v})\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}\right),\quad\theta:=\frac{\partial h(s_{v})}{h(s_{v})}.

Since
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)=e^{-\pi\|v\|_{t}^{2}}p_{\mathbb{V}}(v)
, it suffices to show that

|p_{\mathbb{V}}(v)|_{t}<C(1+\|v\|_{t}^{2})

for
\displaystyle t\in U
and
\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
. Now the form
\displaystyle\Omega
is the first Chern form of the hermitian line bundle
\displaystyle F^{2}
. It is known to be bounded when the monodromy is unipotent
[
27
, Prop. 1.11]
; that is,
\displaystyle|\Omega|_{t}
is bounded on
\displaystyle\Delta^{*}
. To estimate the term
\displaystyle h(s_{v})\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}
, recall that

-2\pi i\Omega=\partial\overline{\partial}\log h(s_{v})=\frac{\partial\overline{\partial}h(s_{v})}{h(s_{v})}-\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}.

Multiplying by
\displaystyle h(s_{v})
gives

(4.9)

h(s_{v})\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}=2\pi ih(s_{v})\Omega+\partial\overline{\partial}h(s_{v}).

Since
\displaystyle h(s_{v})=2\|v^{2,0}\|_{t}^{2}\leq 2\|v\|^{2}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle|h(s_{v})\Omega|_{t}\leq 2\|v\|_{t}^{2}|\Omega|_{t}
,
and so it remains to estimate the term
\displaystyle\partial\overline{\partial}h(s_{v})
. Writing
\displaystyle v_{t}=\Sigma v^{p,q}_{t}
for the Hodge decomposition of
\displaystyle v_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{t}
, we have
\displaystyle h(s_{v})=-2Q(v_{t}^{2,0},v_{t}^{0,2})
and hence

(4.10)

\|v\|_{t}^{2}=Q(v^{1,1}_{t},v^{1,1}_{t})-2Q(v_{t}^{2,0},v_{t}^{0,2})=Q(v,v)+2h(s_{v})

and

\partial\overline{\partial}h(s_{v})=2^{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\|v\|^{2}_{t}=2^{-1}\sum\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}\partial\overline{\partial}h_{ij}(t).

Let us next give a bound for the forms
\displaystyle\partial\overline{\partial}h_{ij}(t)
. We have
\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{ij}=e^{-1}_{ij}h_{ij}
, where
\displaystyle e_{ij}(t)=(-\log|t|)^{a_{ij}/2}
and
\displaystyle a_{ij}
is the integer defined by
\displaystyle a_{ij}=(m-2)+(l-2)
if
\displaystyle v_{i}\in W_{m}-W_{m-1}
and
\displaystyle v_{j}\in W_{l}-W_{l-1}
. A direct computation shows that the forms
\displaystyle e_{ij}^{-1}\partial e_{ij}
,
\displaystyle e_{ij}^{-1}\overline{\partial}e_{ij}
and
\displaystyle e_{ij}^{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}e_{ij}
are nearly bounded; writing

\begin{split}\partial\overline{\partial}h_{ij}=\tilde{h}_{ij}\partial\overline{\partial}e_{ij}+\partial\tilde{h}_{ij}\overline{\partial}e_{ij}-\overline{\partial}\tilde{h}_{ij}\partial e_{ij}+e_{ij}\partial\overline{\partial}\tilde{h}_{ij}\end{split}

and applying (
4.7
) shows that
\displaystyle e_{ij}^{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}h_{ij}
is nearly bounded too. Thus

|\partial\overline{\partial}h(s_{v})|_{t}=O(\sum|a_{i}a_{j}|e_{ij}(t)),

and by
[
13
, Lemma 5.6]
, we have

(4.11)

\sum|a_{i}a_{j}|e_{ij}(t)=O(\|v\|_{t}^{2}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

The lemma implies the (very coarse) bound

(4.12)

\begin{split}|\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}|_{t}&\leq C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
v\notin W_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{-\pi y\|v\|_{t}^{2}}(1+y\|v\|_{t}^{2})\\
&\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}\\
v\notin W_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{-\pi y\|v\|_{t}^{2}/2},\end{split}

for some constant
\displaystyle C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
and the basis
\displaystyle(v_{i})
. Using this bound we can prove the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
, the forms
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}
and
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}^{\prime\prime}
are rapidly decreasing as
\displaystyle t\to 0
, uniformly on any angular sector
\displaystyle U
. We have

\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}=\sum_{m}\left(\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime\prime}_{m,\mu}\right)\cdot q^{m}.

Proof.

Fix a basis
\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+2}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U}
adapted to the weight filtration and denote by
\displaystyle|\cdot|
the metric on
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}
obtained from the standard metric on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
via the corresponding trivialization
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}|_{U}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}^{n+2}
. Define
\displaystyle Y_{k}
as in (
4.5
). For
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, let us write
\displaystyle v=\sum v_{k}
with
\displaystyle v_{k}\in Y_{k}
. It follows from (
4.6
) that there is a positive constant
\displaystyle c
, depending only on
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
and the basis
\displaystyle(v_{i})
, such that for all
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
we have

(4.13)

\|v\|_{t}^{2}>2c\sum_{k}|v_{k}|^{2}(-\log|t|)^{k-2}.

Combined with (
4.12
), this immediately implies the following bound: let us write
\displaystyle Y_{k}^{\mathbb{Z}}=Y_{k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}
and let
\displaystyle l=1
if
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
is of type II and
\displaystyle l=0
if
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
is of type III; then

W_{2}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}=Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus(W_{1}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee})=Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus Y_{l}^{\mathbb{Z}}

and

\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}=Y_{4-l}^{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus(W_{2}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee})=Y_{4-l}^{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus Y_{l}^{\mathbb{Z}}

and we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}|\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}|_{t}&\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}\\
v\notin W_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{-\pi y\|v\|_{t}^{2}/2}\\
&=C^{\prime}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq u\in Y_{4-l}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
v\in Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
w\in Y_{l}^{\mathbb{Z}}\end{subarray}}e^{-\pi y\|u+v+w\|_{t}^{2}/2}\\
&<C^{\prime}\sum_{0\neq u\in Y_{4-l}^{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{-\pi cy|u|^{2}(-\log|t|)^{2-l}}\\
&\quad\times\sum_{v\in Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{-\pi cy|v|^{2}}\\
&\quad\times\sum_{w\in Y_{l}^{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{-\pi cy|w|^{2}(-\log|t|)^{l-2}}.\end{split}

In the last expression, the sum over
\displaystyle u
is clearly rapidly decreasing as
\displaystyle t\to 0
, and the sum over
\displaystyle v
is independent of
\displaystyle t
. It remains to estimate the sum over
\displaystyle w
. This can be done by Poisson summation: since
\displaystyle Y_{l}^{\mathbb{Z}}=W_{1}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}
is a lattice of rank
\displaystyle l+1
, we have

(4.15)

\sum_{w\in Y_{l}^{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{-\pi cy|w|^{2}(-\log|t|)^{l-2}}=O\left(-\log|t|\right).

This shows that
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}
and
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime\prime}_{m,\mu}
are rapidly decreasing as
\displaystyle t\to 0
. The identity in the statement follows by dominated convergence.
∎

Note that (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) give the bound

(4.16)

\begin{split}|\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}|_{t}&=O\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap W_{2}}e^{-\pi y\|v\|_{t}^{2}/2}\right)\\
&=O(-\log|t|).\end{split}

This estimate will be useful later but it is not enough to guarantee the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}
.

4.3.
Reduction to nilpotent orbits

Following the strategy outlined at the end of
4.1
, we must now consider the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime}_{m}
. Our next goal is to prove the following Proposition, which shows that it is enough to consider the case where
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
is a nilpotent orbit.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\to\Delta^{*}
be a weight two polarized variation of Hodge structure with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
. Assume that the monodromy is unipotent and non–trivial and let
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
be the corresponding nilpotent orbit. For any
\displaystyle\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and any
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
, the forms

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}-\Theta_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}\ \text{ and }\ \Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}-\Theta_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}\in\Omega^{1,1}(\Delta^{*})

are rapidly decreasing as

. We have

\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}-\Theta_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}=\sum_{m}\left(\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}-\Theta_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}\right)\cdot q^{m}.

As a first step towards the proof let us establish an estimate for the difference between the Hodge norms
\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{V},t}
and
\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},t}
of a flat section
\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
as
\displaystyle t\to 0
.

Lemma 4.5
.

There are positive constants
\displaystyle A
,
\displaystyle B
such that

\left|\|v\|_{\mathcal{V},t}^{2}-\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},t}^{2}\right|\leq A|t|^{B}\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},t}^{2}

for every
\displaystyle t\in U
and
\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\pi:\mathbb{H}\to\Delta^{*}
be the uniformizing map
\displaystyle z\mapsto t=e^{2\pi iz}
and let
\displaystyle\Phi_{1}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{D}
and
\displaystyle\Phi_{2}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{D}
be the period maps induced by
\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}
and
\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
respectively. Let us write
\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathrm{SO}(V_{\mathbb{R}},Q)
and fix
\displaystyle z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. Pick differentiable lifts

\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:\mathbb{H}\to G_{\mathbb{R}}

of
\displaystyle\Phi_{1}
and
\displaystyle\Phi_{2}
, i.e. maps
\displaystyle\phi_{i}
that satisfy

\Phi_{i}(z)=\phi_{i}(z)x_{0},\quad z\in\mathbb{H},\quad i=1,2.

(For example, use the Iwasawa decomposition of
\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{R}}
.) Writing
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{x}
for the euclidean metric on
\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{D}
, we have the equivariance property
\displaystyle\|gv\|_{gx}=\|v\|_{x}
for all
\displaystyle g\in G_{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence

\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V},t}=\|v\|^{2}_{\Phi_{1}(t)}=\|\phi_{1}(t)^{-1}v\|^{2}_{x_{0}}.

and similarly
\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},t}=\|\phi_{2}(t)^{-1}v\|_{x_{0}}^{2}
. Thus it suffices to prove that

|\|\phi_{1}(t)^{-1}\phi_{2}(t)v\|_{x_{0}}^{2}-\|v\|^{2}_{x_{0}}|\leq A|t|^{B}\|v\|^{2}_{x_{0}}

for all
\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
, or equivalently to show that the norm
\displaystyle\|\phi_{1}(t)^{-1}\phi_{2}(t)\|
of the operator
\displaystyle\phi_{1}(t)^{-1}\phi_{2}(t)\in\mathrm{End}(V_{\mathbb{R}})
satisfies

|\|\phi_{1}(t)^{-1}\phi_{2}(t)\|^{2}-1|\leq A|t|^{B}.

This follows directly from Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem
[
22
, Thm 4.12]
(see also p. 244 of loc. cit. for a comparison between the operator norm and Riemannian distance on
\displaystyle\mathbb{D}
).
∎

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(v)

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists a positive constant
\displaystyle C
such that

(4.25)

\displaystyle t\in U

\displaystyle v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}

As in the proof of Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle g_{z}\in G_{\mathbb{R}}
relating
\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(t)
and
\displaystyle\Phi_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(t)

[
6
, pp. 480-481]

g_{z}=e^{xN}\tilde{g}(y)e^{-xN}\in G_{\mathbb{R}}

\Phi_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(t)=g_{z}\Phi_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(t).

The bound (

(4.26)

\sup_{v\in V_{\mathbb{R}}-0}\left|\frac{\|g_{z}v\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},t}}{\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},t}}-1\right|\leq C(-\log|t|)^{-1}.

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(y)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(y)
together with the fact that
\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{k}
maps
\displaystyle W_{n,\mathbb{R}}
to
\displaystyle W_{n+k-1,\mathbb{R}}
. For the second expression one uses that
\displaystyle\tilde{g}(\infty)-1
maps
\displaystyle W_{n,\mathbb{R}}
to
\displaystyle W_{n-2,\mathbb{R}}
(cf.
[

).

giving a trivialization

\Phi:\underline{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\overset{\sim}{\to}W_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\Phi((a_{1},a_{2}))=a_{1}\lambda_{1}+a_{2}\lambda_{2}.

Write

(4.39)

\begin{split}\lambda_{i}&=\alpha_{i}e^{1,0}+\overline{\alpha_{i}}e^{0,1},\quad i=1,2,\end{split}

for some complex numbers
\displaystyle\alpha_{1}
,
\displaystyle\alpha_{2}
. By (
2.22
), for a vector
\displaystyle a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the Hodge metric of
\displaystyle\Phi(a)
is given by

\|\Phi(a)\|_{z}=2|a_{1}\alpha_{1}+a_{2}\alpha_{2}|^{2}/\mathrm{Im}(z),

Let us define a Schwartz function
\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\tilde{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})=\phi\left(x_{1}\alpha_{1}+x_{2}\alpha_{2}\right).

Writing
\displaystyle\pi_{1}(v)=\Phi(a)
for some
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and using (
4.32
) and (
4.33
) we have

(4.40)

\begin{split}\sum_{v_{1}\in W_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}(v_{1}+\pi_{1}(v)))&=\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\tilde{\phi}\left(\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Im}(z)/2y}}\right)\right)\Omega.\end{split}

Writing
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\tilde{\phi}
for the Fourier transform of
\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}
, an application of Poisson summation gives

(4.41)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\tilde{\phi}\left(\frac{a+n}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Im}(z)/2y}}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{Im}(z)}{2y}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{2\pi im\cdot a}\mathcal{F}\tilde{\phi}(\sqrt{\mathrm{Im}(z)/2y}\cdot m).

By (
4.34
), the term corresponding to
\displaystyle m=0
vanishes, and we obtain the upper bound, uniform in
\displaystyle a
,

(4.42)

\left|\sum_{v_{1}\in W_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(\sqrt{y}(v_{1}+\pi_{1}(v)))\right|_{t}\leq\frac{\mathrm{Im}(z)}{2y}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}-0}|\mathcal{F}\tilde{\phi}(\sqrt{\mathrm{Im}(z)/2y}\cdot m)|\cdot|\Omega|_{t}

This expression decreases rapidly as
\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(z)\to\infty
, which implies the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}
. Similarly, using
\displaystyle Q(W_{1},W_{2})=0
, we may write

(4.43)

\begin{split}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mu+W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}\varphi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}v)\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in(\mu+W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}})/W_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}\sum_{v_{1}\in W_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\varphi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}(v_{1}+\pi_{1}(v))).\end{split}

Note that the outer sum is finite since
\displaystyle Q
polarizes the pure Hodge structure
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}\mathbb{V}
, which is purely of type
\displaystyle(1,1)
. The above bound establishes that
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}
is integrable for all
\displaystyle m
and implies the identity

\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}=\sum_{m}\left(\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}\right)\cdot q^{m}

by dominated convergence.

4.5.
Integrability for type III nilpotent orbits

We now determine the form
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)
and theta series
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}
for a type III nilpotent orbit
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}=\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
. We will work in the setting of
Section 2.4
; in particular, we assume that the associated limiting mixed Hodge structure is
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
–split.

4.5.1.

Let
\displaystyle v\in W_{2,\mathbb{R}}
. As in (
2.30
), we may write

\displaystyle v=v_{U}+aNe^{2,2}+bN^{2}e^{2,2}
with
\displaystyle v_{U}\in U
and real numbers
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
. By (
2.32
) and (
2.33
), we have

(4.44)

\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v)=e^{-\pi Q(v_{U},v_{U})}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(aNe^{2,2}+bN^{2}e^{2,2}).

Differentiating
\displaystyle h(s_{v})=|b-az|^{2}/\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}
gives

\theta(s_{v})=\frac{\partial h(s_{v})}{h(s_{v})}=\left(-\frac{a}{b-az}+\frac{i}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)dz

and hence

\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}=\left|\frac{b-a\mathrm{Re}(z)}{(b-az)\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right|^{2}dz\wedge d\overline{z}.

Using (
2.27
) we obtain

(4.45)

\begin{split}ih(s_{v})\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}&=\frac{|b-a\mathrm{Re}(z)|^{2}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}\cdot\frac{idz\wedge d\overline{z}}{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2}}\\
&=\left(\frac{b-a\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)^{2}4\pi\Omega.\end{split}

For
\displaystyle v=aNe^{2,2}+bN^{2}e^{2,2}
we obtain the explicit formula

(4.46)

\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(aNe^{2,2}+bN^{2}e^{2,2})=e^{-\pi a^{2}}\phi\left(\frac{b-a\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)\Omega,

where
\displaystyle\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the Schwartz function defined by

(4.47)

\phi(b)=e^{-2\pi b^{2}}(4\pi b^{2}-1).

The most important consequence of this explicit description of
\displaystyle\phi
is that its Fourier transform
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\phi
satisfies

(4.48)

\mathcal{F}\phi(0)=0,

as follows from the identity
\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{db}(-be^{-2\pi b^{2}})=e^{-2\pi b^{2}}(4\pi b^{2}-1)
.

4.5.2.

Let us now compute
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}
explicitly using the above description of
\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}
. As in
4.4
, we define
\displaystyle W_{j}^{\mathbb{Z}}=W_{j}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}}
and obtain a filtration

0\subset W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}=W_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}=W_{3}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset W_{4}^{\mathbb{Z}}=V_{\mathbb{Z}}

whose associated quotients
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}:=W_{k}^{\mathbb{Z}}/W_{k-1}^{\mathbb{Z}}
are free abelian groups (of rank one in the case of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{0}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
). Let us define

W_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}=W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap\ker N.

Fix a generator
\displaystyle v_{0}
of
\displaystyle W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and vectors
\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
of
\displaystyle W_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

W_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\langle v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\rangle,

i.e. so that
\displaystyle Y_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}:=\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\rangle
is a complement of
\displaystyle W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}
in
\displaystyle W_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. We also fix a vector
\displaystyle v_{n}^{\prime}\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}
mapping to a generator of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
and a vector
\displaystyle v_{n}\in W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\displaystyle v_{n}\equiv Nv_{n}^{\prime}\mod W_{0}
; then the image of
\displaystyle v_{n}
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
generates the rank one lattice

N(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathrm{im}(N:\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})

(cf. Lemma
3.3.2
.(ii)). Define

Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}:=\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle=Y_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\langle v_{n}\rangle

(orthogonal sum). Then
\displaystyle W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}+Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a (finite index) sublattice of
\displaystyle W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and the quotient map
\displaystyle W_{2}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V
induces an isometry

(4.49)

Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus N(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})

onto a (finite-index) sublattice of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{2}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. With the notation of
3.3.2
we may then write
\displaystyle\mu=\mu_{2}+\mu_{0}
with
\displaystyle\mu_{2}\in Y_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and
\displaystyle\mu_{0}\in W_{0}
and

(4.50)

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\lambda+\nu\equiv\mu_{2}\\
\mod W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\end{subarray}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\lambda\otimes\nu}

with

(4.51)

\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\lambda\otimes\nu}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}\in N\lambda+\langle v_{n}\rangle\\
v\in\nu+Y_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
w\in\mu_{0}+W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}\end{subarray}}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}(v^{\prime}+v+w))e^{\pi ix(Q(v^{\prime},v^{\prime})+Q(v,v))}.

To estimate this sum, we use (
2.30
) to write

(4.52)

\begin{split}v^{\prime}&=a(v^{\prime})Ne^{2,2}+b(v^{\prime})N^{2}e^{2,2}\\
v&=\pi_{U}(v)+b(v)N^{2}e^{2,2}\\
w&=b(w)N^{2}e^{2,2},\end{split}

where
\displaystyle\pi_{U}:W_{2,\mathbb{R}}\to U
is the projection to
\displaystyle U
and
\displaystyle a,b
are linear functionals on
\displaystyle W_{2,\mathbb{R}}
. Since
\displaystyle W_{0}
is anisotropic and
\displaystyle Q(W_{0},W_{2})=0
, we have
\displaystyle Q(\pi_{U}(v),\pi_{U}(v))=Q(v,v)
and
\displaystyle Q(v^{\prime},v^{\prime})=-a(v^{\prime})^{2}
and hence, by (
4.44
) and (
4.46
),

(4.53)

\begin{split}\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(v^{\prime}+v+w)&=e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}\\
&\quad\times\varphi_{\mathbb{V}}(a(v^{\prime})Ne^{2,2}+b(v+v^{\prime})N^{2}e^{2,2}+w).\\
&=e^{-\pi(Q(v,v)-Q(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}))}\\
&\quad\times\phi\left(\frac{b(v+v^{\prime}+w)-a(v^{\prime})\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)\Omega,\end{split}

with
\displaystyle\phi
given by (
4.47
).

For the theta series
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\lambda\otimes\nu}
this gives

(4.54)

\begin{split}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\lambda\otimes\nu}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}\in N\lambda+\langle v_{n}\rangle\\
v\in\nu+Y_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\end{subarray}}q^{Q(v,v)/2}\overline{q}^{(-Q(v^{\prime},v^{\prime})/2)}\\
&\qquad\times\sum_{w\in\mu_{0}+W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi\left(\sqrt{y}\frac{b(v+v^{\prime}+w)-a(v^{\prime})\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)\Omega.\end{split}

To estimate the sum over
\displaystyle\mu_{0}+W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}v_{0}
we apply Poisson summation: writing
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\phi
for the Fourier transform of
\displaystyle\phi
and

A=\frac{b(v+v^{\prime}+\mu_{0})-a(v^{\prime})\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)/\sqrt{y}},

we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi\left(A+\frac{nb(v_{0})}{\mathrm{Im}(z)/\sqrt{y}}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{Im}(z)}{|b(v_{0})|\sqrt{y}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi imA}\mathcal{F}\phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{Im}(z)}{b(v_{0})\sqrt{y}}m\right).

The term corresponding to
\displaystyle m=0
vanishes by (
4.48
); this gives the upper bound, uniform in
\displaystyle A
,

(4.55)

\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{w\in W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi\left(\sqrt{y}\frac{b(v+v^{\prime}+w)-a(v^{\prime})\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)\right|_{t}\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{\mathrm{Im}(z)}{|b(v_{0})|\sqrt{y}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}-0}\left|\mathcal{F}\phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{Im}(z)}{b(v_{0})\sqrt{y}}m\right)\right||\Omega|_{t}\end{split}

The right hand side is rapidly decreasing as
\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(z)\to\infty
. This implies the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}
. Similarly, using
\displaystyle Q(W_{0},W_{2})=0
, we have

\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\lambda+\nu\equiv\mu_{2}\\
\mod W_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}}\end{subarray}}\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\lambda\otimes\nu}

with

(4.56)

\begin{split}\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\lambda\otimes\nu}\cdot q^{m}=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}\in N\lambda+\langle v_{n}\rangle\\
v\in\nu+Y_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
Q(v+v^{\prime},v+v^{\prime})=2m\end{subarray}}q^{Q(v,v)/2}\overline{q}^{(-Q(v^{\prime},v^{\prime})/2)}\\
&\qquad\times\sum_{w\in\mu_{0}+W_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi\left(\sqrt{y}\frac{b(v+v^{\prime}+w)-a(v^{\prime})\mathrm{Re}(z)}{\mathrm{Im}(z)}\right)\Omega.\end{split}

The bound (
4.55
) and the fact that
\displaystyle Q
is positive definite on
\displaystyle Y_{2,\mathrm{prim}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and negative definite on
\displaystyle N(\mathrm{Gr}_{4}^{W}V_{\mathbb{Z}})
show that
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\lambda\otimes\nu}
is integrable for all
\displaystyle m
. The identity

\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)^{\prime}_{\mu}=\sum_{m}\left(\int_{\Delta^{*}}\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}\right)\cdot q^{m}

follows by dominated convergence.

5.
Generating series of Noether–Lefschetz numbers

The goal of this section is to determine the Fourier expansion of the non–holomorphic modular forms
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
in Theorem
4.1
. We will see that their Fourier coefficients can be expressed in terms of the degrees of the Noether–Lefschetz loci
\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}
defined below and some discrete invariants of the limiting mixed Hodge structures arising from the degeneration of
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
around each point
\displaystyle P
in
\displaystyle\overline{S}-S
.

More precisely, to
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
one can attach the
\displaystyle q
-series

Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)_{\mu}:=-\deg(\overline{\mathcal{L}})\delta_{\mu,0}+\sum_{m>0}\mathrm{deg}\ \mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}\cdot q^{m},\quad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}

as well as theta series
\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)
for each
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S
(see (
3.17
) and (
3.24
)). We will prove the following theorem which implies Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
satisfies
1.1
. For all
\displaystyle\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}/\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
,

Z_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}=Z_{\mathbb{V}}^{+}(\tau)_{\mu}+\sum_{P\in\overline{S}\backslash S}Z^{-}_{\mathbb{V},P}(\tau)_{\mu}.

The proof proceeds by checking that both sides have the same Fourier coefficients. That is, let

Z^{-}_{\mathbb{V},P}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{m}Z^{-}_{\mathbb{V},P}(y)_{m,\mu}\cdot q^{m}

be the Fourier expansion of
\displaystyle Z^{-}_{\mathbb{V},P}(\tau)_{\mu}
and write similarly

Z^{+}_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{m,\mu}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rr}\mathrm{deg}\ \mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}&\text{ if }m>0,\\
-\mathrm{deg}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}),&\text{ if }(m,\mu)=(0,0),\\
0,&\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

for the Fourier coefficients of
\displaystyle Z^{+}_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{\mu}
. Theorem
5.1
is then equivalent to the identity

(5.1)

\int_{S}\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}=Z^{+}_{\mathbb{V}}(\tau)_{m,\mu}+\sum_{P\in\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S}Z^{-}_{\mathbb{V},P}(y)_{m,\mu}

for all
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
.

5.1.
Kudla–Millson forms and Noether–Lefschetz loci

The main input needed to prove Theorem
5.1
is the computation of the residues at the boundary of certain Green functions
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}
for the Noether–Lefschetz loci obtained by pulling back Green functions for special divisors on orthogonal Shimura varieties. The latter Green functions were introduced by Kudla in
[
15
]
. Let us briefly recall their definition. Consider the Kudla–Millson theta series

\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{v\in\mu+V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\varphi_{\mathrm{KM}}(y^{1/2}v)e^{\pi ixQ(v,v)}

and let us write

\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}(\tau)_{\mu}=\sum_{m\in\tfrac{1}{2}Q(\mu,\mu)+\mathbb{Z}}\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}\cdot q^{m}

for its Fourier expansion. One of the main properties of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}(\tau)_{\mu}
is that it defines a closed differential form and its Fourier coefficients
\displaystyle\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathrm{KM}}(y)_{m,\mu}
are Poincaré dual to a certain special divisor
\displaystyle Z(m,\mu)
(see
[
14
]
) whose intersection with
\displaystyle S
gives the Noether–Lefschetz locus
\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}
.

In
[
15
]
, Kudla introduced a Green function
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}
for the special divisor
\displaystyle Z(m,\mu)
, i.e. a smooth function on
\displaystyle\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}-|Z(m,\mu)|
satisfying Green’s equation

(5.2)

\mathrm{dd^{c}}[\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}]+\delta_{Z(m,\mu)}=[\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}-\varphi_{\mathrm{KM}}(0)\delta_{(m,\mu)=(0,0)}].

Here
\displaystyle\mathrm{d^{c}}=(4\pi i)^{-1}(\partial-\overline{\partial})
, so that
\displaystyle\mathrm{dd^{c}}=-(2\pi i)^{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
, and the term
\displaystyle\delta_{(m,\mu)=(0,0)}
equals one if
\displaystyle(m,\mu)=(0,0)
and vanishes otherwise.

Pulling back
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}
by the period map
\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbb{V}}
associated with
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
, we obtain a function
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}
whose main properties are summarized in the following Proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
\displaystyle v
a local section of
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}
, define

(5.3)

\nu_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(v)=e^{-2\pi h(s_{v})}

and
\displaystyle\nu_{\mathbb{V}}(v)=e^{-\pi Q(v,v)}\nu_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(v)
. Then

(5.4)

\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}:=\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq v\in\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}\nu^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}((yu)^{1/2}v)\right)\frac{du}{u}

defines a smooth function on
\displaystyle S-|\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}|
that satisfies the differential equation

(5.5)

\mathrm{dd^{c}}[\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}]+\delta_{\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}}=[\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}+\Omega\delta_{(m,\mu)=(0,0)}]

as currents on
\displaystyle S
. (Here
\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}}
denotes the current of integration against the divisor associated with
\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}
, understood to vanish if
\displaystyle m\leq 0
.)

Let us fix
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
and choose small disks
\displaystyle D_{P,\epsilon}
around each point
\displaystyle P
in the support of
\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}
as well as in
\displaystyle\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S
whose radii tend to zero as
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
. By the above proposition and the integrability of
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}
we have

(5.6)

\begin{split}\int_{S}\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}&+\mathrm{deg}(\overline{\mathcal{L}})\delta_{(m,\mu)=(0,0)}\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{S-\cup D_{P,\epsilon}}(\Theta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}+\Omega\delta_{(m,\mu)=(0,0)})\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\partial(S-\cup D_{P,\epsilon})}\mathrm{d^{c}}\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}\\
&=\mathrm{deg}\ \mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}-\sum_{P\in\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\partial D_{P,\epsilon}}\mathrm{d^{c}}\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}\\
&=\mathrm{deg}\ \mathrm{NL}_{\mathbb{V}}(m)_{\mu}-\sum_{P\in\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S}\mathrm{res}_{P}\ \partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu},\end{split}

where in the last line
\displaystyle\mathrm{res}_{P}
denotes the residue at
\displaystyle P
of the
\displaystyle(1,0)
–form
\displaystyle\partial\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}
. Thus to establish (
5.1
) it suffices to prove the identity

(5.7)

-\mathrm{res}_{P}\ \partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}=Z^{-}_{\mathbb{V},P}(y)_{m,\mu}

for all
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
and all
\displaystyle P\in\overline{S}\,\backslash\,S
. Note that the residue
\displaystyle\mathrm{res}_{P}
depends only on the restriction of
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
to a small disk centered at
\displaystyle P
.

5.2.
Local residue computations

It follows from (
5.7
) that to prove Theorem
5.1
it suffices to prove the following three lemmas. In their statements we assume that
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}
is an arbitrary
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS of weight two with
\displaystyle h^{2,0}=1
satisfying
1.1
on the punctured unit disk
\displaystyle S=\Delta^{*}
. With the notation of Section
2.2
we define

\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq v\in(\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}})\cap W_{2}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}\nu^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}((yu)^{1/2}v)\right)\frac{du}{u}.

The strategy is now the same as for the proof of Theorem
4.1
: one first shows that the residue of
\displaystyle\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}
agrees with that of
\displaystyle\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}
and then one computes the latter residue using the explicit formulas for the “
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
–split nilpotent orbit”
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
in Sections
2.3
and
2.4
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
, we have

\mathrm{res}_{t=0}\ (\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}-\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu})=0.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
, we have

\mathrm{res}_{t=0}\ (\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}-\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu})=0.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle\mu
, we have

-\mathrm{res}_{t=0}\ \partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}=Z_{\mathbb{V},P}^{-}(\tau)_{m,\mu}.

The proof of these lemmas is analogous to the proofs of similar lemmas in Sections
4.2
,
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.5
. It will be convenient to define

(5.8)

\displaystyle W_{2}
. Since

\displaystyle v\neq 0

) when computing

and

\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}=\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}+\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)_{m,\mu}^{\prime\prime}

with

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

This reduces to

(5.11)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{res}_{t=0}\ \partial\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{V}}^{\circ}(y)_{m,\mu}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0

(5.12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0.

To prove (
5.11
) we can use (
5.10
) and the explicit expression

(5.13)

\partial\nu^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y^{1/2}v)=\partial(e^{-2\pi yh(s_{v})})=e^{-2\pi yh(s_{v})}\cdot(-\pi y\partial\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}})

(recall that
\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}=Q(v,v)+2h(s_{v})
and hence
\displaystyle 2\partial h(s_{v})=\partial\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}
). With the notation of (
4.4
), we have

\partial\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}=\sum_{i,j}\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}\partial h_{ij}(t).

As in the proof of Lemma
4.2
one shows that the forms
\displaystyle e_{ij}^{-1}\partial h_{ij}
are nearly bounded and hence that

(5.14)

|\partial\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}|_{t}\leq C\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V},t}

for some positive constant
\displaystyle C
,
giving the bound

|\partial\tilde{\Theta}_{\mathbb{V}}(uy)^{\prime\prime}_{m,\mu}|_{t}\leq C\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
v\notin W_{2}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}e^{-2\pi uyh(s_{v})}\pi uy\|v\|^{2}_{t}

and hence

(5.15)

\begin{split}|\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime\prime}_{m,\mu}|_{t}&\leq C\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
v\notin W_{2}\\

&=C\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
v\notin W_{2}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}e^{-2\pi yh(s_{v})}\frac{\|v\|_{t}^{2}}{2h(s_{v})}\\
&=e^{2\pi ym}C\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\

By (

), the factor

in the last expression is bounded above by an expression of the form
\displaystyle(1-A(-\log|t|)^{-1})^{-1}
for some
\displaystyle A>0
. The argument in the proof of Proposition
4.2
now shows that
\displaystyle|\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(y)^{\prime\prime}_{m,\mu}|_{t}

, proving (

).

To show that (
5.12
) holds one can follow closely the arguments proving Lemma

and Proposition

. One first shows by differentiating (

) that

for some positive constant
\displaystyle B
. Multiplying (

) by

yields

) and (

), this gives the bound

(5.18)

\begin{split}|\partial\nu^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}}(v)-\partial\nu^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(v)|_{t}&<C|t|^{B}e^{-\pi(2h_{\mathcal{V}}(s_{v})-A|t|^{B}\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}})}\cdot\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}(1+\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}})\end{split}

for some positive constants
\displaystyle A
,
\displaystyle B
and

.

Writing

, we have

\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi uf(v,t)}u\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}\frac{du}{u}=\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}\frac{e^{-\pi f(v,t)}}{\pi f(v,t)}

and

), there exist

and

\displaystyle f(y^{1/2}v,t)^{-1}<Ay^{-1}((-\log|t|)^{k})

\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}

,

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

It suffices to show that

|\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}-\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}|_{t}

is bounded for
\displaystyle t
in a fixed angular sector where
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{t}
denotes the Poincaré metric, i.e. that the form
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}-\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(y)^{\prime}_{m,\mu}
is nearly bounded.

To see this, let us write
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{V}}=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
. Using (
5.13
), (
4.25
) and the elementary inequality
\displaystyle|e^{x}-1|\leq|x|e^{|x|}
we estimate

(5.20)

\begin{split}\pi^{-1}|\partial\nu^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(v)&-\partial\nu^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}(v)|_{t}\\
&=|e^{-2\pi h_{\mathcal{V}}(s_{v})}\partial\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}-e^{-2\pi h_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}(s_{v})}\partial\|v\|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}|_{t}\\
&\leq|e^{-2\pi h_{\mathcal{V}}(s_{v})}-e^{-2\pi h_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}(s_{v})}|\cdot|\partial\|v\|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}|_{t}\\
&\quad+e^{-2\pi h_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}(s_{v})}\cdot|\partial\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}-\partial\|v\|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}|_{t}\\
&\leq Ce^{-\pi h_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}(s_{v})}\cdot(-\log|t|)^{-1},\end{split}

where
\displaystyle C
depends only on
\displaystyle m
. This gives

(5.21)

\begin{split}|\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},m}(y)^{\prime}-\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}},m}(y)^{\prime}|_{t}&\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}|\nu^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}((yu)^{1/2}v)-\nu^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}((yu)^{1/2}v)|_{t}\frac{du}{u}\\
&\leq C(-\log|t|)^{-1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mu+\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
Q(v,v)=2m\end{subarray}}e^{-\pi yuh_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}(s_{v})}\frac{du}{u}.\end{split}

The proof of Lemma
5.2
will show that the integrand in the last expression is
\displaystyle O((-\log|t|)/\sqrt{u})
. It follows that
\displaystyle\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{nilp}},m}(y)^{\prime}-\partial\mathfrak{g}^{\circ}_{\tilde{\mathbb{V}}^{\mathrm{nilp}},m}(y)^{\prime}
is nearly bounded.

\displaystyle\Phi

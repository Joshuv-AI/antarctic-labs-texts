Quantization for the mixtures of uniform distributions on connected and disconnected line segments
1 Asha Barua, 2 Gustavo Fernandez, 3 Ashley Gomez, 4 Ogla Lopez, 5 Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury

Abstract
. In this paper, we have considered different mixed distributions which are generated by two uniform distributions: first supported by two connected line segments, and then supported by two disconnected line segments. For these mixed distributions, we have determined the optimal sets of n n -means and the n n th quantization errors for all positive integers n n . The technique of this paper can be utilized to investigate the optimal quantization for any mixed distributions P := p ​ P 1 + ( 1 − p ) ​ P 2 P:=pP_{1}+(1-p)P_{2} , generated by any two probability distributions P 1 P_{1} and P 2 P_{2} associated with any probability vector ( p , 1 − p ) (p,1-p) for 0 < p < 1 0<p<1 with support a connected or the union of disconnected line segments.

2.
Mixed distribution
P
with support the closed interval
[0,2]

2.1.
Basic preliminaries

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two uniform distributions, respectively, on the intervals given by

J_{1}:=[0,1],\text{ and }J_{2}:=[1,2].

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be their respective density functions. Then,

f_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ if }x\in[0,1],\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{array}\right.\text{ and }f_{2}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ if }x\in[1,2],\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Let us consider the mixed distribution
P:=pP_{1}+(1-p)P_{2}
, where
0<p<1
. Notice that
P
has support the closed interval
[0,2]
. By
E(X)
, it is meant the expectation of a random variable
X
with probability distribution
P
, and
V(X)
represents the variance of
X
. By
\alpha_{n}:=\alpha_{n}(P)
, we denote an optimal set of
n
-means with respect to the probability distribution
P
, and
V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)
represents the corresponding quantization error for
n
-means.
Since
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are the density functions for the probability distributions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, respectively, we have

dP_{1}(x)=P_{1}(dx)=f_{1}(x)dx=dx,\text{ and }dP_{2}(x)=P_{2}(dx)=f_{2}(x)dx=dx.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P
be the mixed distribution defined by
P=pP_{1}+(1-p)P_{2}
. Then,
E(X)=\frac{1}{2}(3-2p)
, and
V(X)=-p^{2}+p+\frac{1}{12}
.

Proof.

We have

E(X)=\int xdP=p\int xd(P_{1}(x))+(1-p)\int xd(P_{2}(x))=p\int_{J_{1}}x\,dx+(1-p)\int_{J_{2}}x\,dx

yielding
E(X)=\frac{1}{2}(3-2p)
, and

V(X)=\int(x-E(X))^{2}dP=p\int(x-E(X))^{2}d(P_{1}(x))+(1-p)\int(x-E(X))^{2}d(P_{2}(x)),

implying

V(X)=-p^{2}+p+\frac{1}{12}
, and thus, the proposition is yielded.
∎

Note 2.3
.

Lemma
2.2
implies that the optimal set of one-mean is the set
\{\frac{1}{2}(3-2p)\}
, and the corresponding quantization error is the variance
V:=V(X)
of a random variable with probability distribution
P
. For a subset
J
of
\mathbb{R}
with
P(J)>0
,
by
P(\cdot|_{J})
, we denote the conditional probability given that
J
is occurred, i.e.,
P(\cdot|_{J})=P(\cdot\cap J)/P(J)
, in other words, for any Borel subset
B
of
\mathbb{R}
we have
P(B|_{J})=\frac{P(B\cap J)}{P(J)}
.

Let us now give the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
such that
P
is uniformly distributed over a closed interval
[a,b]
with a constant density function
f
such that
f(x)=t
for all
x\in[a,b]
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the optimal set
\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))
of
n
-means and the corresponding quantization error
V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))
of
n
-means for the probability distribution
P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})
are, respectively, given by

\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})):=\Big\{a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a)}{2n}:1\leq j\leq n\Big\},\text{ and }V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\frac{(b-a)^{3}t}{12n^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n}:=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}\}
be an optimal set of
n
-means for the probability distribution
P
with a constant density function
f
on
[a,b]
such that
f(x)=t
for all
x\in[a,b]
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, proceedings analogously as
[
RR2
, Theorem 2.1.1]
, we can show that
a_{j}=a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a)}{2n}
implying

\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\Big\{a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a)}{2n}:1\leq j\leq n\Big\}.

Notice that the probability density function is constant, and the Voronoi regions of the points
a_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
are of equal lengths. This yields the fact that the distortion errors due to each
a_{j}
are equal. Hence, the
n
th quantization error is given by

\displaystyle V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))

\displaystyle=\int\min_{a\in\alpha_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]})}(x-a)^{2}dP=nt\int_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})}(x-a_{1})^{2}dx

\displaystyle=nt\int_{a}^{a+\frac{b-a}{n}}(x-(a+\frac{b-a}{2n}))^{2}dx

implying

V_{n}(P(\cdot|_{[a,b]}))=\frac{(b-a)^{3}t}{12n^{2}}.

Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 2.8.1
.

The optimal set of two-means is
\{\frac{11}{16},\frac{25}{16}\}

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\{a_{1},a_{2}\}

P
has support

. Since the points in an optimal set are the conditional expectations in their own Voronoi regions, without any loss of generality, we can assume that
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<2

Case 1.
0<a_{1}<a_{2}\leq 1
.

Since the boundary of the Voronoi region is
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})
, we have the distortion error as

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=\frac{1}{5}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}\left(x-a_{1}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{5}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}^{1}\left(x-a_{2}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{4}{5}\int_{1}^{2}\left(x-a_{2}\right){}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{60}\left(3a_{1}^{3}+3a_{2}a_{1}^{2}-3a_{2}^{2}a_{1}-3a_{2}^{3}+60a_{2}^{2}-156a_{2}+116\right),

the minimum value of which is
\frac{37}{135}=0.274074
and it occurs when
a_{1}=\frac{1}{3},
and
a_{2}=1
.

Case 2.
0<a_{1}\leq 1<a_{2}<2

In this case, the boundary
\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})

Subcase 1.

In this subcase, the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=\frac{1}{5}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}\left(x-a_{1}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{5}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}^{1}\left(x-a_{2}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{4}{5}\int_{1}^{2}\left(x-a_{2}\right){}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP=\frac{1}{5}\int_{0}^{1}\left(x-a_{1}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{4}{5}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}\left(x-a_{1}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{4}{5}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}^{2}\left(x-a_{2}\right){}^{2}\,dx

In this case the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\alpha}(x-a)^{2}dP

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}\int_{0}^{1}\left(x-a_{1}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{4}{5}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}\left(x-a_{1}\right){}^{2}\,dx+\frac{4}{5}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)}^{2}\left(x-a_{2}\right){}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{15}\left(3a_{1}^{3}+3\left(a_{2}-3\right)a_{1}^{2}-3\left(a_{2}^{2}-3\right)a_{1}-3a_{2}^{3}+24a_{2}^{2}-48a_{2}+29\right),

Comment on “Replica Symmetry Breaking in Trajectories of a Driven Brownian Particle” Preprint: APS/123-QED
Tapas Singha, Mustansir Barma

Abstract




Recently Ueda and Sasa (US)
Ueda_Sasa2015
studied the overlap of the trajectories of two passive particles subject to driving by the same noisy Burgers field
KPZ_1986
, but independent noise. A quantity of central importance is the probability distribution
P_{t}(d)
of the interparticle distance
d
at time
t
, whose behavior indicates an interesting coexistence of a high-overlap and low-overlap phase in space-time
Ueda_Sasa2015
. Contrary to the stretched exponential form reported in
Ueda_Sasa2015
, in this Comment we show that
P_{t}(d)
in fact follows a power law, and give strong numerical evidence for this. A crucial point is that
P_{t}(d)
is a
scaling function
of
d
and
t
. Besides its theoretical importance, the scaling form immediately explains
US’s numerical findings: (i) the linear (in time) growth of mean-squared separation (ii)
the surprising time independence of probability density. It also explains why the overlap
vanishes for Edwards-Wilkinson (EW) driving in the large-time limit.

A recent study of the approach to steady state of passive sliders on a Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) surface revealed an indefinitely growing coarsening length scale
\mathcal{L}(t)\sim t^{1/z}
, underlying a scaling description
Singha_Barma2018
. Here
z=\frac{3}{2}
is the KPZ dynamic exponent. For the problem considered in
Ueda_Sasa2015
, the initial condition is one in which all particles start at the same location. We checked that scaling remains valid, through numerical simulations of particles on a stochastically evolving lattice with KPZ characteristics
Singha_Barma2018
as shown in Fig.
1
. In the limit of large
d
and
t
with
d/t^{1/z}
held fixed, Fig.
1
shows that
P_{t}(d)
assumes the scaling form

P_{t}(d)\approx\frac{1}{[\mathcal{L}(t)]^{(1+\theta)}}\ Y\left(\frac{d}{\mathcal{L}(t)}\right)

(1)

where
Y(y)\sim y^{-\nu}
as
y\rightarrow 0
, and falls exponentially as
y\rightarrow\infty
. We find
\theta\simeq\frac{1}{2}
and
\nu\simeq\frac{3}{2}
consistent with steady state values, where
\mathcal{L}(t)
in Eq.
1
plays the role of system size
Apurba2006
. The scaling form leads to a convincing data collapse, in contrast to the unscaled semi-log plot (inset of Fig.
1
; see also Fig. 3 of Ref.
Ueda_Sasa2015
) which was analyzed in terms of a stretched exponential form
Ueda_Sasa2015
.

The scaling form Eq.
1
has important implications :
(i) For large
d
and
t
, the mean-squared separation
\langle d^{2}\rangle
is straightforwardly seen to obey
\langle d^{2}\rangle\sim t
as found numerically in
Ueda_Sasa2015
. (ii) For large values of
t
, Eq.
1
implies that
P_{t}(d)
becomes time independent for
d<d^{*}
, where
d^{*}\sim\mathcal{L}(t)
. This explains the observation in
Ueda_Sasa2015
that curves for
P_{t}(d)
coincide for
d<60
in the time range
200\leq t\leq 1000
. Importantly, the fact that
d^{*}
increases with
t
implies that trajectory pairs would show overlap (Eq. 3 of
Ueda_Sasa2015
) with even larger values of localization length. Finally, the scaling form (1) holds also for the case of EW driving, but with
\theta\simeq 0
,
\nu\simeq\frac{2}{3}
and
z=2
with logarithmic corrections
Huveneers
. Thus for all
d
, the probability
P_{t}(d)
falls to zero as
[\mathcal{L}(t)]^{-1/3}\sim t^{-\frac{1}{6}}
, in contrast to the KPZ case. This explains why the overlap approaches zero as
t\rightarrow\infty
in the EW case
Ueda_Sasa2015
.

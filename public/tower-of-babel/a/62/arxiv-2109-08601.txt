Residues of bosonic string scattering amplitudes and the Lauricella functions
Sheng-Hong Lai, Jen-Chi Lee, Yi Yang

Abstract
We calculate explicitly residues of all n n -point Koba-Nielsen (KN) amplitudes by using on-shell recursion relation of string scattering amplitudes (SSA). In addition, we show that the residues of all SSA including the KN amplitudes can be expressed in terms of the Lauricella functions. This result demonstrates the exact S ​ L ​ ( K + 3 , ℂ ) SL(K+3,\mathbb{C}) symmetry of the tree-level open bosonic string theory. Moreover, we derive an iteration relation among the residues of a given SSA. This iteration relation is related to the S ​ L ​ ( K + 3 , ℂ ) SL(K+3,\mathbb{C}) symmetry and can presumably be used to soften the well-known hard SSA.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we calculate explicitly residues of all
n
-point Koba-Nielsen
(KN) amplitudes by using string on-shell recursion relation of string
scattering amplitudes (SSA). We then use mathematical induction, together with
the on-shell recursion and the shifting principle, to show that all
n
-point
SSA including the KN amplitudes can be expressed in terms of the LSSA. In
general, to explicitly calculate the LSSA form of the
n
-point KN amplitude,
one needs to apply up to (
n-4)
-step recursion
(recursion)^{n-4}
to achieve
a
4
-point LSSA. These results demonstrates the exact
SL(K+3,\mathbb{C})
symmetry of the tree-level open bosonic string theory.

Moreover, we derive an iteration relation among the residues of a given

n
-point KN amplitude. This iteration relation is a generalization of the
case of simple Veneziano amplitude to the higher point SSA. We argue that this
iteration relation is related to the
SL(K+3,\mathbb{C})
symmetry and can presumably, in the
4
-point case, be used to soften the
well-known hard Veneziano amplitude. So we expect the
SL(K+3,\mathbb{C})
symmetry can be used to soften all higher
n
-point hard SSA. This
mechanism is reminiscent of symmetry principle in QFT.

In the calculation of multi-tensor SSA, we discover that the calculation of
SSA expressed in terms of the Lauricella functions is much simpler than the
traditional calculation
KLT
. It is also interesting to see that to
express the residues of higher point SSA in terms of the Lauricella
4
-point
function, one encounters application of the multi-step recursion processes
which are associated with the multi-tensor SSA calculation.

It will be important to extend the results of this paper to the superstring
theory. However, the known construction of the string fermion vertex involved
only the
leading Regge trajectory
fermion string state of the R
sector
PFSSA
;
Osch
;
RRR
. It is a nontrivial task to construct the general
massive fermion string vertex operators
m1
;
m2
;
m3
;
m4
. Many questions
related to the construction of SSA involving the general massive fermion
string states need to be answered before one can further understand the
symmetry of superstring theory.

In addition to the dynamics of
4
-point SSA with higher spin particles, there
are many interesting issues related to higher point SSA. To name some, is
there any evidence of the
SL(K+3,\mathbb{C})
symmetry other than deriving it from SSA? In a recent publication

LLYT
, it was shown that the LSSA of three tachyons and one arbitrary
string states can be rederived from the deformed cubic string field theory
(SFT)
Taejin
. But can one use Witten SFT to obtain the
SL(K+3,\mathbb{C})
symmetry ? How to deal with the
z_{4}\rightarrow\infty
limit as was shown
in Eq.(
5.15
) when calculating SSA with higher spin particles putting at

z_{4}
? Are there linear relations among higher point (
n\geq 5
) hard SSA
similar to those of the
4
-point case? We hope to address these issues in the
next publications.

\displaystyle=\underset{m=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}E_{m}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m}\right)z^{m}.

(A.2)

Two simple applications of the functions
E_{m}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m}\right)
follow. If we set
x_{i}=x
for all
i
, we obtain

\displaystyle A\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots;z\right)=\exp\left[x\left(z+\frac{z^{2}}{2}+\frac{z^{3}}{3}+\frac{z^{4}}{4}+\frac{z^{5}}{5}\cdots\right)\right]

\displaystyle=E_{0}+E_{1}\left(x\right)z+E_{2}\left(x\right)z^{2}+E_{3}\left(x\right)z^{3}+E_{4}\left(x\right)z^{4}+\cdots

(A.3)

with

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=1=\frac{\left(x\right)_{0}}{0!}

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1}x=x=\frac{x}{1!}=\frac{\left(x\right)_{1}}{1!}

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{2!}\left(\frac{1}{1}\right)^{2}x^{2}=\frac{x\left(x+1\right)}{2!}=\frac{\left(x\right)_{2}}{2!}

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}x+\frac{1}{1!1!}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1}\right)x^{2}+\frac{1}{3!}\left(\frac{1}{1}\right)^{3}x^{3}=\frac{x\left(x+1\right)\left(x+2\right)}{3!}=\frac{\left(x\right)_{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}x+\left[\frac{1}{1!1!}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1}\right)+\frac{1}{2!}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right]x^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2!1!}\left(\frac{1}{1}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)x^{3}+\frac{1}{4!}\left(\frac{1}{1}\right)^{4}x^{4}=\frac{x\left(x+1\right)\left(x+2\right)\left(x+3\right)}{4!}=\frac{\left(x\right)_{4}}{4!}

\displaystyle\cdots

(A.4)

The coefficient functions above find application in Eq.(
3.5
) which
corresponds to the residues of the Veneziano amplitude. On the other hand, if
we set
\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots;z\right)=\left(\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2},\cdots;z\right)
, we obtain

\displaystyle A\left(\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2},\cdots;z\right)=\exp\left[\alpha_{-1}z+\frac{\alpha_{-2}z^{2}}{2}+\frac{\alpha_{-3}z^{3}}{3}+\frac{\alpha_{-4}z^{4}}{4}+\frac{\alpha_{-5}z^{5}}{5}\cdots\right]

\displaystyle=E_{0}+E_{1}\left(\alpha_{-1}\right)z+E_{2}\left(\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2}\right)z^{2}+E_{3}\left(\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2},\alpha_{-3}\right)z^{3}

\displaystyle+E_{4}\left(\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2},\alpha_{-3},\alpha_{-4}\right)z^{4}+...

(A.5)

with

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{-1}}{1}

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{-2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}}{1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{-3}}{3}+\frac{1}{1!1!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-2}}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}}{1}\right)+\frac{1}{3!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}}{1}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{-4}}{4}+\frac{1}{1!1!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-3}}{3}\right)\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}}{1}\right)+\frac{1}{2!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-2}}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2!1!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}}{1}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-2}}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}}{1}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle E_{M}

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\{N_{t}\right\},M=\underset{t=1}{\overset{\infty}{{\displaystyle\sum}}}tN_{t}}{\sum}\prod_{r=1}\frac{\left(\alpha_{-r}\right)^{N_{r}}}{N_{r}!r^{Nr}}

(A.6)

where in Eq.(
A.6

N
into
\left\{N_{r}\right\}
with
M=\underset{r=1}{\sum}rN_{r}
and

N=\underset{r=1}{\sum}N_{r}
. The coefficient
E_{M}
above reminds us of the
sum over normalized creation operators of the string spectrum at mass level

M
.

There are two relations which we used in the text to do the residue
calculation. They are

\displaystyle F_{M}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{b+c=M,b,c=0}{\sum}\frac{\left(T_{1}\right)_{b}}{b!}\frac{\left(T_{2}\right)_{c}}{c!}x_{1}^{b}x_{2}^{c}

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\{N_{r}\right\},M=\underset{r=1}{\sum}rN_{r}}{\sum}\prod_{r=1}\left(\frac{1}{N_{r}!r^{N_{r}}}\left[T_{1}x_{1}^{r}+T_{2}x_{2}^{r}\right]^{N_{r}}\right),

(A.7)

and the following recurrence relation

F_{M}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\underset{N=0}{\overset{M-1}{\sum}}F_{N}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\left[T_{1}x_{1}^{M-N}+T_{2}x_{2}^{M-N}\right].

(A.8)

To prove Eq.(
A.7
), we introduce the generating function

\displaystyle G\left(x_{1},x_{2};z\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-zx_{1}\right)^{-T_{1}}\left(1-zx_{2}\right)^{-T_{2}}

\displaystyle=\underset{b,c=0}{\sum}\frac{\left(T_{1}\right)_{b}}{b!}\frac{\left(T_{2}\right)_{c}}{c!}x_{1}^{b}x_{2}^{c}z^{b+c},

(A.9)

and

\displaystyle F_{M}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\frac{\partial^{M}}{\partial z^{M}}G\left(x_{1},x_{2};z\right)|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=\underset{b+c=M,b,c=0}{\sum}\frac{\left(T_{1}\right)_{b}}{b!}\frac{\left(T_{2}\right)_{c}}{c!}x_{1}^{b}x_{2}^{c}.

(A.10)

On the other hand, we note that

\displaystyle G\left(x_{1},x_{2},z\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-zx_{1}\right)^{-T_{1}}\left(1-zx_{2}\right)^{-T_{2}}=\exp\left[-T_{1}\ln\left(1-zx_{1}\right)-T_{2}\ln\left(1-zx_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[T_{1}\left(zx_{1}+\frac{\left(zx_{1}\right)^{2}}{2}+\frac{\left(zx_{1}\right)^{3}}{3}+\cdots\right)+T_{2}\left(zx_{2}+\frac{\left(zx_{2}\right)^{2}}{2}+\frac{\left(zx_{1}\right)^{3}}{3}+\cdots\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\left(T_{1}x_{1}+T_{2}x_{2}\right)z+\left(T_{1}x_{1}^{2}+T_{2}x_{2}^{2}\right)\frac{z^{2}}{2}+\left(T_{1}x_{1}^{3}+T_{2}x_{2}^{3}\right)\frac{z^{3}}{3}+\cdots\right]

\displaystyle=A\left(T_{1}x_{1}+T_{2}x_{2},T_{1}x_{1}^{2}+T_{2}x_{2}^{2},T_{1}x_{1}^{3}+T_{2}x_{2}^{3},\cdots;z\right),

(A.11)

so

\displaystyle F_{M}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=E_{M}\left(T_{1}x_{1}+T_{2}x_{2},T_{1}x_{1}^{2}+T_{2}x_{2}^{2},\cdots,T_{1}x_{1}^{M}+T_{2}x_{2}^{M}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{N_{r}\right\}\text{,with }M=\underset{r}{\sum}rN_{r}}\prod_{r=1}\left(\frac{\left(T_{1}x_{1}^{r}+T_{2}x_{2}^{r}\right)^{N_{r}}}{N_{r}!r^{N_{r}}}\right)

(A.12)

where we have used Eq.(
A.6
) to obtain Eq.(
A.12

A.7

\displaystyle F_{M}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}=0\end{array}}{\sum}\frac{\left(T_{1}\right)_{a_{1}}}{a_{1}!}\cdots\frac{\left(T_{n}\right)_{a_{n}}}{a_{n}!}x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\{N_{r}\right\}\text{,with fixed }M=\underset{r}{\sum}rN_{r}}{\sum}\prod_{r=1}\left(\frac{1}{N_{r}!r^{N_{r}}}\left[T_{1}x_{1}^{r}+\cdots+T_{n}x_{n}^{r}\right]^{N_{r}}\right).

(A.15)

It can be shown that

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{m}}A\left(x_{1},\cdots;z\right)|_{z=0}=\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m-1}}{\partial z^{m-1}}\left(\frac{\partial B}{\partial z}e^{B}\right)|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\underset{N=0}{\overset{M-1}{\sum}}\frac{1}{N!}\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial z^{N}}\left(\left(1-zx_{1}\right)^{-T_{1}}\left(1-zx_{2}\right)^{-T_{2}}\right)|_{z=0}\left(T_{1}x_{1}^{M-N}+T_{2}x_{2}^{M-N}\right)

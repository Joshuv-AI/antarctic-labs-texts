Asymptotic stability of solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system driven by inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
Eduard Feireisl, Young–Sam Kwon

Abstract
We consider global in time solutions of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system describing the motion of a general compressible, viscous and heat conducting fluid far from equilibirum. Using a new concept of weak solution suitable to accommodate the inhomogeneous Dirichlet time dependent data we find sufficient conditions for the global in time weak solutions to be ultimately bounded.

Introduction

The Navier–Stokes–Fourier system describing the time evolution of the mass density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
, the velocity
{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
, and the temperature
\vartheta=\vartheta(t,x)
of a general compressible, viscous, and heat–conducting fluid, endowed with inhomogeneous boundary conditions, is a prominent example of a
dissipative system
in the framework of continuum fluid mechanics. In general, a dissipative system is a thermodynamically open system
confined to a physical space
\Omega\subset R^{d}
and
considered far from equilibrium, exchanging energy and matter with the outer world. The field equations of the
Navier–Stokes–Fourier system
describing the motion in the interior of the cavity
\Omega
read:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})+\varrho{\bf g},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u},

(1.3)

where the
viscous stress

\mathbb{S}
is given by Newton’s law

\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=\mu(\vartheta)\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{d}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\eta(\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},\ \mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}),

(1.4)

and the
heat flux

{\bf q}
by Fourier’s law

{\bf q}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta.

(1.5)

The
pressure

p
and the
internal energy

e
are interrelated through Gibbs’ equation

\vartheta Ds=De+pD\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right),

(1.6)

where
s
is the
entropy
. In view of (
1.6
), the internal energy balance (
1.3
) may be replaced by the entropy balance

\partial_{t}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\right)=\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right).

(1.7)

As we shall see below, it is the entropy balance (
1.7
) that is more convenient for the
weak
formulation of the problem.

The fluid occupies a bounded domain
\Omega
, whereas the velocity
{\bf u}
and the temperature
\vartheta
satisfy the inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle={\bf u}_{B}\ \mbox{on}\ (T,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\vartheta

\displaystyle=\vartheta_{B}\ \mbox{on}\ (T,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,.

(1.9)

Accordingly, the boundary of the space–time cylinder
(T,\infty)\times\partial\Omega
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm in}

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,x)\in(T,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\ \Big|\ {\bf u}_{B}(t,x)\cdot{\bf n}(x)<0\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm wall}

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,x)\in(T,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\ \Big|\ {\bf u}_{B}(t,x)\cdot{\bf n}(x)=0\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm out}

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,x)\in(T,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\ \Big|\ {\bf u}_{B}(t,x)\cdot{\bf n}(x)>0\right\},

where
{\bf n}
denotes the outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega
. Finally, the density must be prescribed on the inflow component of the boundary,

\varrho=\varrho_{B}\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{\rm in}.

(1.10)

As shown in a series of papers by Matsumura and Nishida
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
, Valli
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
, Valli and Zajaczkowski
[
31
]
, the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system is globally well posed
in the class of classical solutions
in a small neighbourhood of a stable equilibrium. Unfortunately, this perturbation technique cannot be used in the
far from equilibrium regime

usually associated to turbulence.
In view of the well known and so far unsurmountable problems concerning the existence
of suitable
a priori
bounds for large data global in time solutions to
non–linear problems in fluid dynamics, the only available framework
are the weak solutions in the spirit of the pioneering work by Leray
[
25
]
, and later, in the context of compressible fluids, by Lions
[
26
]
.

A mathematical theory based on weak solutions for the complete fluid systems was presented in

[
11
]
. Unfortunately, the concept of weak solutions developed in
[
11
]
applies to

energetically closed
systems with
{\bf u}_{B}=0
and with (
1.9
) replaced by the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition

\nabla_{x}\vartheta\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Under these circumstances, the long time behaviour of solutions is well understood, and, in the case of a time independent driving force
{\bf g}={\bf g}(x)
, obeys the following dichotomy:

•

Either
{\bf g}=\nabla_{x}F
and then all solutions tend an equilibrium;

•

or
{\bf g}\neq\nabla_{x}F
and then

\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)\right](t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to\infty\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty

for any weak solution
(\varrho,{\bf u},\vartheta)
;

see
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
. It turns out that driving the system by means of a non–trivial volume force while keeping the conservative boundary conditions is not realistic and definitely not suitable for describing
phenomena related to turbulence.

The mathematical theory of weak solutions has been extended to the
energetically open
Navier–Stokes–Fourier system only recently in
[
12
]
, and, finally, in
[
3
]
. In particular, the inhomogeneous boundary condition for
the temperature requires a new approach, developed in
[
3
]
, based on the balance of ballistic energy in the sense of Ericksen
[
10
]
. Recently, the existence of of weak solutions has also been proved for a bi-fluid model for a mixture of two compressible
non interacting fluids with general boundary data in
[
23
]
.

Note that this kind of boundary conditions is physically relevant, in particular
for the Rayleigh–Bénard problem and Taylor experiment arising in models of turbulence, cf. Birnir and Svanstedt
[
1
]
, Constantin et al.
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
, Davidson
[
9
]
among others.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to describe the asymptotic behaviour of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with the
inhomogeneous Dirichlet
boundary conditions. We focus on the problem of
global boundedness
of trajectories and the existence of bounded absorbing sets.
This is the concept of
dissipativity in the sense of Levinson
extending the classical approach to closed systems via a Lyapunov function,
see e.g. Haraux
[
21
]
, Kuznetsov and Reitmann
[
24
, Chapter 1, Section 1.2]
. The crucial quantity
if the
ballistic energy

\int_{\Omega}E_{B}(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x},\ E_{B}(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\equiv\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)

where
{\bf u}_{B}
,
\vartheta_{B}
are suitable extensions of the boundary data inside
\Omega
.

We say that the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) is Levinson dissipative if there exists a universal constant
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
such that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}E_{B}(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

for any (weak) solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
defined on a time interval
(T_{0},\infty)
.

Our goal is to identify a class of constitutive relations (equations of state (EOS), viscosity and heat conductivity coefficients), for which the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
1.1
)–(
1.10
)
is Levinson dissipative. We adopt the following strategy:

1.

In Section
2
, we recall the concept of weak solution introduced in
[
3
]
, together with the associated ballistic energy inequality.

2.

Inspired by
[
5
]
, we introduce a class of equations of state penalizing the pressure if the density approaches a critical value
\overline{\varrho}
, see Section
3
.

3.

The main results are stated in Section
4
.

4.

In Section
5
, we show the uniform bounds of the ballistic energy proving Levinson dissipativity of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system. We also study convergence to
equilibrium solutions for a particular class of boundary data.

5.

Possible applications including the existence of global attractors, statistical solutions, and the existence of time periodic solutions are briefly sketched in Section
6
.

2
Weak solutions, ballistic energy

We recall the concept of weak solution and ballistic energy introduced in
[
3
]
.

2.1
Weak solutions

We consider the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
1.1
)–(
1.10
)
defined on the set
(T,\infty)\times\Omega
, where
T<\infty
. Although the existence theory
is formulated in terms of the initial data

\varrho(T,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ (\varrho{\bf u})(T,\cdot)={\bf m}_{0},\ \varrho s(T,\cdot)=S_{0},\ S_{0}=\varrho_{0}s(\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0}),

their specific form is irrelevant for the analysis of the present paper.

Definition 2.1

(Weak solution)
.

We say that a trio of functions
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
is a
weak solution
of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) in
(T,\infty)\times\Omega

if the following holds:

•

Equation of continuity.

\varrho\geq 0
and the integral identity

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\varrho_{B}\ [{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\varrho\ [{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.1)

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}((T,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

•

Momentum equation.

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle\in L^{r}_{\rm loc}(T,\infty;W^{1,r}(\Omega;R^{d}))\ \mbox{for some}\ r>1,

\displaystyle({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})

\displaystyle\in L^{r}_{\rm loc}(T,\infty;W^{1,r}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})),

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\varrho{\bf g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.3)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}((T,\infty)\times\Omega;R^{d})
.

•

Entropy inequality.

\displaystyle-\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.4)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}((T,\infty)\times\Omega)
,
\varphi\geq 0
.

•

Ballistic energy inequality.

For any

\tilde{\vartheta}\in C^{1}([T,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega}),\ \tilde{\vartheta}>0,\ \tilde{\vartheta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}

there holds

\displaystyle-\int_{T}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\psi\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{T}^{\infty}\psi\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[\varrho_{B}e(\varrho_{B},\vartheta_{B})-\vartheta_{B}\varrho_{B}s(\varrho_{B},\vartheta_{B})\Big]\ [{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{T}^{\infty}\psi\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta_{B})-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta_{B})\Big]\ [{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{T}^{\infty}\psi\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{T}^{\infty}\psi\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\otimes({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})+p\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{S}\Big]:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{T}^{\infty}\psi\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot({\bf g}-\partial_{t}{\bf u}_{B}-{\bf u}_{B}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{T}^{\infty}\psi\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right)+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.5)

for any
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(T,\infty)
,
\psi\geq 0
.

The symbol
{\bf u}_{B}
in (
2.5
) denotes any
C^{1}
extension of the boundary velocity
{\bf u}_{B}
. It can be shown (cf.
[
2
, Remark 2.2]
) that the specific form of the ballistic energy
inequality is independent of the extension
{\bf u}_{B}
. Specifically, if (
2.5
) holds for some
{\bf u}_{B}
, then it holds for any
{\bf u}_{B}
attaining the same boundary value. We point out that
such a statement may not hold for the temperature extension
\tilde{\vartheta}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The regularity of a weak solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
is determined by the available
a priori
bounds based mostly on the ballistic energy inequality. In particular, the density
\varrho
is only Lebesgue integrable while both
(
2.1
) and (
2.5
) refer to its trace on
\Gamma_{\rm out}
. The latter is understood in the following way. The velocity
{\bf u}
is a Sobolev function that admits a trace. Moreover, the vector field

[\varrho,\varrho{\bf u}]
having zero space–time divergence admits a normal trace on the space time cylinder
(T,\infty)\times\Omega
. Consequently,
\varrho|_{\Gamma_{\rm out}}
is determined through

\varrho{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=\varrho|_{\Gamma_{\rm out}}{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}.

The reader may consult
[
12
]
for details.

The existence of global–in–time weak solutions under certain restrictions imposed on the constitutive relations was proved in
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
. The weak solutions also comply with the weak–strong uniqueness principle, see
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
. Specifically, any weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.1
coincides with the strong solution of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system driven by the same initial/boundary data as long as the strong solution exists.

The ballistic energy inequality (
2.5
) can be written in a more concise form

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[\varrho_{B}e(\varrho_{B},\vartheta_{B})-\vartheta_{B}\varrho_{B}s(\varrho_{B},\vartheta_{B})\Big]\ [{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta_{B})-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta_{B})\Big]\ [{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\otimes({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})+p\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{S}\Big]:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot({\bf g}-\partial_{t}{\bf u}_{B}-{\bf u}_{B}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right)+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.6)

understood in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(T,\infty)
.

Our goal is to identify the class of boundary data
{\bf u}_{B}
,
\vartheta_{B}
, for which (
2.6
) gives rise to a globally bounded ballistic energy,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}.

(2.7)

for a certain
\vartheta_{B}
. In other words, the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system is Levinson dissipative. Although the ballistic energy need be non–negative, we show that its
“entropy” component is dominated by the internal energy. More specifically, the bound (
2.7
) is equivalent to

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\infty},

modulo a suitable modification of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
.

Finally, note that if
\vartheta_{B}
is a positive
constant
, then (
2.6
) reduces to an energy inequality formally similar to that for the barotropic system studied in
[
2
]
. Thus, similarly to
[
2
]
, a hard sphere pressure equation of state is necessary to keep the density bounded and to control the first integral on the right–hand side of (
2.6
). On the other hand, under the non-slip boundary conditions

{\bf u}_{B}=0
, inequality (
2.6
) gives rise to

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right)+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x};

(2.8)

whence the rightmost integral must be dominated by the dissipation

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Again this does not seem realistic unless uniform bounds on the density
\varrho
are
a priori
imposed. The above arguments justify the choice of the hard sphere pressure equation of state introduced in the section below.

3
Constitutive relations, equation of state

Before stating the main results, we introduce the structural hypotheses imposed on the equations of state motivated by
[
11
]
. We suppose

\widetilde{p}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4},\ \widetilde{e}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\varrho}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\frac{a}{\varrho}\vartheta^{4},\ a>0,

(3.1)

where
P\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
satisfies

P(0)=0,\ P^{\prime}(Z)>0\ \mbox{for}\ Z\geq 0,\ 0<\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}\leq c\ \mbox{for}\ Z>0.

(3.2)

In particular, the function
Z\mapsto P(Z)/Z^{\frac{5}{3}}
is decreasing, and we suppose

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\frac{P(Z)}{Z^{\frac{5}{3}}}=p_{\infty}\geq 0.

(3.3)

The associated entropy
s
reads

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\frac{4a}{3}\frac{\vartheta^{3}}{\varrho},

(3.4)

where

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Z)=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z^{2}}.

(3.5)

In addition, following
[
2
]
, we introduce the hard sphere perturbation of the equation of state,

\displaystyle p(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{p}(\varrho,\vartheta)+p_{\rm HS}(\varrho),

\displaystyle p_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle\in C^{1}[0,\overline{\varrho}),\ p_{\rm HS}(0)=0,\ p_{\rm HS}^{\prime}>0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\overline{\varrho}),\ \lim_{\varrho\to\overline{\varrho}-}p_{\rm HS}(\varrho)=\infty.

(3.6)

The related internal energy reads

e(\varrho,\vartheta)=\widetilde{e}(\varrho,\vartheta)+\int_{\overline{\varrho}/2}^{\varrho}\frac{p_{\rm HS}(z)}{z}\ {\rm d}z.

(3.7)

Remark 3.1
.

The main effect of the hard sphere pressure equation of state is, of course, the uniform bound imposed
a priori
on the fluid density,

0\leq\varrho(t,x)\leq\overline{\varrho},

(3.8)

for any weak solution of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system.

The transport coefficients
\mu
,
\eta
, and
\kappa
are continuously differentiable functions of the temperature
\vartheta
satisfying

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta^{\Lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta^{\Lambda}\right),\ |\mu^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\leq c\ \mbox{for all}\ \vartheta\geq 0,\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\Lambda\leq 1,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\eta(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\eta}\left(1+\vartheta^{\Lambda}\right),

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right),\ \beta\geq 0.

(3.9)

In addition, we say that
s
is compatible with the Third law of thermodynamics, if

\lim_{\vartheta\to 0}s(\varrho,\vartheta)=0\ \mbox{for any fixed}\ \varrho>0.

(3.10)

The existence theory developed in
[
3
]
can be easily modified to accommodate the hard–sphere pressure, at least in the specific form

p_{\rm HS}(\varrho)\approx(\overline{\varrho}-\varrho)^{-\beta},\ \beta>3,

cf. also
[
16
]
.

Main results

\displaystyle-\int_{I}^{I+\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\Phi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{I}^{I+\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\Phi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{I}^{I+\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf g}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\Phi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

(5.17)

All integrals on the right–hand side can be estimated exactly as in

except

that can be treated as

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\Phi\right]\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left(\|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{d\times d})}+\|(1+\vartheta)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\Phi\right]\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\left(\left\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}\right\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}+\|\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+c\Big(D,\underline{\varrho},\overline{\varrho},\underline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}\Big)\right).

With the estimate (
5.18
) at hand, we can repeat step by step the arguments of
[
2
, Section 5]
to deduce from (
5.15
), (
5.16
) the final bound

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s-\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\mathcal{B}[\Phi]\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=I}^{t=I+\tau}

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{I}^{I+\tau}\left\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}\right\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}\,{\rm d}t+\delta\int_{I}^{I+\tau}\left(\|\log(\vartheta)\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\|\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\right)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{I}^{I+\tau}\int_{\Omega}p\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c\Big(\tau,D,\underline{\varrho},\overline{\varrho},\underline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta},\|{\bf g}\|_{L^{\infty}},\Phi\Big),

for a suitable
\Phi
and
\delta>0

Similarly to
[

, relation (

) yields the conclusion of Theorem

. First observe that

\varrho e\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}p(\varrho,\vartheta)

(5.20)

see
[

Denote

\mathcal{E}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s-\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\mathcal{B}[\Phi]\right)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(5.21)

As the entropy
s
satisfies (
5.10
),

\mathcal{E}\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\frac{1}{\lambda}

(5.22)

for some
\lambda>0
.
Consequently, inequality (
5.19
) yields

[\mathcal{E}(t)]_{t=I}^{t=I+\tau}+\delta\int_{I}^{I+\tau}\mathcal{E}(s)\ {\rm d}s\leq C(\tau,\delta)\ \mbox{for some}\ \delta>0.

(5.23)

For
\tau=1
we get the following dichotomy:

1.

Either there exists

2.

Consequently, we prove that

5.1.2

Under the hypotheses of Theorem

Keeping in mind (

\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\psi(t)\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right].

.

of

and

as soon as the uniform bound on the density is established. The only piece of information to be retained being

]

5.2
Proof of Theorem
4.4

First we claim that for a given velocity field
{\bf u}_{B}
, the temperature
\vartheta_{B}
, and the total mass
M>0
, there exists a unique density profile
\varrho_{E}
solving the stationary problem (
4.9
).
Indeed, as
\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}=0
, the convective term in (
4.9
) reads

{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{E}{\bf u}_{B}\otimes{\bf u}_{B})={\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{E}{\bf u}_{B}){\bf u}_{B}+\varrho_{E}{\bf u}_{B}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{B}=-\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{E}\nabla_{x}|{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}.

Accordingly, the problem (
4.9
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\varrho_{E}\cdot{\bf u}_{B}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}p(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{B})

\displaystyle=\varrho_{E}\left(\nabla_{x}G+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{E}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=M.

(5.26)

As
P^{\prime}(0)>0
in (
3.2
) and
\vartheta_{B}
is given, the desired uniqueness result follows from

[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.4
, the ballistic energy inequality (
2.6
) simplifies considerably:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot(\nabla_{x}G-{\bf u}_{B}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

(5.27)

Moreover, by virtue of hypothesis (
4.11
),

\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot(\nabla_{x}G-{\bf u}_{B}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\left(\nabla_{x}G+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}|{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x};

whence (
5.27
) reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\varrho\left(G+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}\right)-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \leq 0.

(5.28)

It turn out that the modified ballistic energy

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+\varrho e-\varrho\left(G+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}\right)-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

is a Lyapunov function decreasing along trajectories for which

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}>0.

In particular, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}):(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B})+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta-\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}|^{2}}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t<\infty

(5.29)

for any weak solution of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system defined on the time interval
(T,\infty)
.

Let
T_{n}\to\infty
be a sequence of time. Let

\varrho_{n}(t,x)=\varrho(T_{n}+t,x),\ \vartheta_{n}(t,x)=\vartheta(T_{n}+t,x),\ {\bf u}_{n}(t,x)={\bf u}(T_{n}+t,x)

be the associated time shifts of a global in time weak solution to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier
system. It follows from (
5.28
), (
5.29
) that

\displaystyle\vartheta_{n}

\displaystyle\to\vartheta_{B}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{4+\alpha}((0,T)\times\Omega),

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{n}

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}_{B}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{\alpha}(0,T;W^{1,\alpha}(\Omega;R^{2}))

as
n\to\infty
for some
\alpha>1
. In particular,

{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\to 0\ \mbox{in}\ L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega),

which yields (cf.
[
15
, Chapter 4, Theorem 4.2]
)

\varrho_{n}\to\varrho\ \mbox{in}\ L^{\frac{5}{3}+\alpha}((0,T)\times\Omega),

(5.30)

passing to a suitable subsequence as the case may be.

Our final claim is that
\varrho=\varrho_{E}
, in particular, the convergence in (
5.30
) is unconditional, which completes the proof of Theorem
4.4
. Seeing that the limit is again a weak solution of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}_{B})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u}_{B})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}_{B}\otimes{\bf u}_{B})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta_{B})

\displaystyle=\varrho\nabla_{x}G

in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\Omega)

Consequently,

\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta_{B})=\varrho\nabla_{x}\left(G+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}\right)\ \mbox{for any}\ t\in(0,T).

Since
\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=M
, the uniqueness result
[
13
]
yields

\varrho(t,\cdot)=\varrho_{E}
for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
.

The hypotheses of Theorems

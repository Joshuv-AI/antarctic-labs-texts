Szegő kernel asymptotics and concentration of Husimi Distributions of eigenfunctions
Robert Chang, Abraham Rabinowitz

Abstract
. We work on the boundary ∂ M τ \partial M_{\tau} of a Grauert tube of a closed, real analytic Riemannian manifold M M . The Toeplitz operator Π τ ​ D ρ ​ Π τ \Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau} associated to the Reeb vector field is a positive, self-adjoint, elliptic operator on H 2 ​ ( ∂ M τ ) H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau}) . We compute λ → ∞ \lambda\to\infty asymptotics under parabolic rescaling in a neighborhood of the geodesic (Reeb) flow G τ t = exp ⁡ t ​ Ξ ρ G^{t}_{\tau}=\exp t\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}} for the spectral projection kernel Π χ , λ \Pi_{\chi,\lambda} associated to Π τ ​ D ρ ​ Π τ \Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau} . We also compute scaling asymptotics for tempered sums of Husimi distributions (analytic continuations) on ∂ M τ \partial M_{\tau} of Laplace eigenfunctions on M M . 1.
Statement of main results

The main purpose of this article is to study the
L^{p}\to L^{q}
mapping norms of the spectral projections
\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}
(
3
) and
\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}
(
15
) associated to the Szegő projector
\Pi_{\tau}
(
1
) on the boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
a of Grauert tube
M_{\tau}
. These norm estimates, which are sharp, are stated in
Theorem 1.3
and
Theorem 1.5
. A key ingredient of the proof is the on-shell off-diagonal scaling asymptotics of
\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}
on
\partial M_{\tau}
(
Theorem 1.1
). As applications, we deduce sharp
L^{p}
estimates for analytic continuations (
19
) of Laplace eigenfunctions (
Theorem 1.3
), as well as for eigenfunctions of the Toeplitz operator (
12
), whose principal symbol coincides with that of
\sqrt{-\Delta}
transported to the Grauert tube boundary (
section 1
). Unlike the Sogge estimates in the real domain, there is no ‘critical exponent’
p
separating low and high
L^{p}
norms.

We now give a terse introduction to and the precise statements of our results, postponing to
subsection 1.1
a more detailed discussion of related works. The setup involves a closed, real analytic manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
m\geq 2
. Its complexification
M_{\mathbb{C}}
admits a strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function
\rho
in a neighborhood of the totally real submanifold
M\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
. For each
0<\tau<\tau_{\mathrm{max}}
, the sublevel set
\{\sqrt{\rho}<\tau\}=:M_{\tau}
is a Kähler manifold, called the
Grauert tube of radius
\tau
.

Throughout, we work on the boundary of a Grauert tube with a fixed radius
\tau
. The
Szegő projector
associated the Grauert tube boundary

\Pi_{\tau}\colon L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})\to H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})

(1)

is the orthogonal projection onto the Hardy space of boundary values of holomorphic functions in the tube. Consider the Toeplitz operator

\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}\colon H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})\to H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau}),

(2)

where
D_{\sqrt{\rho}}=\frac{1}{i}\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
is a constant multiple of the Hamilton vector field of the Grauert tube function
\sqrt{\rho}
acting as a differential operator. As in
[
7
]
, we fix a positive, even Schwartz function
\chi
whose Fourier transform is compactly supported with
\widehat{\chi}(0)=1
and form the spectral localization

\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\chi}(t)e^{-it\lambda}\Pi_{\tau}e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}\,dt.

(3)

The classical dynamics associated to
e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}
on
\partial M_{\tau}
is the Hamilton flow

\displaystyle G^{t}_{\tau}\colon\partial M_{\tau}\to\partial M_{\tau},\quad G^{t}_{\tau}=\exp t\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}.

(4)

This flow coincides with the pullback of the Riemannian geodesic flow on
S^{*}_{\tau}M
under the diffeomorphism (
37
).

Our scaling asymptotics for (
3
) is stated in Heisenberg coordinates in the sense of
[
8
]
centered at
p\in\partial M_{\tau}
and
G^{s}_{\tau}(p)\in\partial M_{\tau}
. In these coordinates, the derivative of the flow (
4

\displaystyle DG^{s}_{\tau}:T_{p}\partial M_{\tau}\to T_{G^{s}_{\tau}(p)}\partial M_{\tau},\qquad DG^{s}_{\tau}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&M_{s}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where
M_{s}
is a symplectic matrix on
\mathbb{R}^{2(m-1)}
. Let
\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{H},M_{s}}
denote the lift to the reduced Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{red}}^{m-1}=S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{m-1}
of the metaplectic representation of
M_{s}
acting on the model Bargmann–Fock space
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{m-1})

subsection 2.5
for details. The following theorem states that under a parabolic

\lambda^{m-1}\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{H},M_{s}}

Theorem 1.1

(On-shell scaling asymptotics for
\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}
)
.

Let

\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}

3
) and

be as in (

\displaystyle\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}

\displaystyle\left(p+\left(\frac{\theta}{\lambda},\frac{u}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right);G^{s}_{\tau}(p)+\left(\frac{\phi}{\lambda},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\right)

(6)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{m,M}}{\tau^{m}}e^{is\lambda}\lambda^{m-1}\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{H},M_{s}}\left(\frac{\theta}{2\tau},\frac{u}{\sqrt{\tau}},\frac{\phi}{2\tau},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)\left[1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda^{-\frac{j}{2}}P_{j}(p,s,u,v,\theta,\phi)\right]

\displaystyle\lVert R_{N}(p,s,\theta,u,\phi,v,\lambda)\rVert_{C^{j}\left(\{|(\theta,u)|+|\left(\phi,v\right)|\leq\rho\}\right)}\leq C_{N,j,\rho}\quad\text{for }\rho>0,\ j=1,2,3,\dotsc,

(9)

and all quantities vary smoothly with

and

When

Theorem 1.3

(

Let

We now turn to estimates for eigenfunctions. The Toeplitz operator (

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}e_{\lambda_{j}}=\lambda_{j}e_{\lambda_{j}},\qquad\lVert e_{\lambda_{j}}\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\partial M_{\tau}\right)}=1.

(12)

An immediate consequence of the eigenfunction expansion

\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}=\sum_{j:\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\chi(\lambda-\lambda_{j})e_{\lambda_{j}}\otimes\overline{e_{\lambda_{j}}},

(13)

of the spectral projection (
3

in the case

Corollary 1.4

(
L^{p}

\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}

.

Let

\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}

\displaystyle\lVert e_{\lambda_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}\left(\partial M_{\tau}\right)}\leq C_{\partial M_{\tau}}\lambda_{j}^{\left(m-1\right)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\qquad(2\leq q\leq\infty)

(14)

The conclusion of

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
M
be a closed, real analytic Riemannian manifold of dimension

. Then we have the sharp estimate

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}f\rVert_{L^{q}(\partial M_{\tau})}\leq C_{\partial M_{\tau}}\lambda^{(m-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})}\qquad(2\leq q\leq\infty)

(16)

Our next set of results concern analytic continuations to the Grauert tube boundary of Laplace eigenfunctions on
M
. Let
0=\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\dotsb
be eigenvalues of
\sqrt{-\Delta}
with associated
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions

\displaystyle-\Delta\varphi_{\mu_{j}}=\mu_{j}^{2}\varphi_{\mu_{j}},\qquad\lVert\varphi_{\mu_{j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}=1.

(17)

The analytic extensions

\varphi_{\mu_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}=e^{\tau\mu_{j}}U(i\tau)\varphi_{\mu_{j}},

where

is the Poisson operator. See

for details. The probability amplitudes

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}=\frac{\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}}{\lVert\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})}}

are

\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}
in the following sense.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
\{\lambda_{j}\}
and
\{\mu_{j}\}
be the eigenvalues of
\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}{\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}}-\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})}

\displaystyle=O(1),

(20)

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}-\lambda_{j}\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})}

\displaystyle=O(1).

(21)

Furthermore, we have the sharp estimate

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{j}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\partial M_{\tau})}\leq C_{\partial M_{\tau}}\lambda_{j}^{(m-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\qquad(2\leq p\leq\infty).

Remark 1.7
.

In

Remark 1.10
.

Our techniques for proving
Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.3
hold in the more general setting of a compact, strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold

has closed range. In particular, the Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand description of the Szegő projector remains valid and quantization of the geodesic flow (

1.1.
Comparison to prior results

In
subsubsection 1.1.1
, we briefly justify the operator (
3

[
7

subsubsection 1.1.2
recalls some results on

1.1.1.
The operators
\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}
as Fourier components of the Grauert tube Szegő kernel

Let
(L,h)\to(X,\omega)
be a positive Hermitian line bundle over a closed Kähler manifold. Let
\partial D=\{\ell\in L^{*}:\lVert\ell\rVert_{h^{*}}=1\}
be the unit co-circle bundle. The orthogonal projections

\Pi_{h^{k}}\colon L^{2}(X,L^{k})\to H^{0}(X,L^{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi_{h}\colon L^{2}(\partial D)\to H^{2}(\partial D)

(29)

are the
Bergman
and
Szegő
projections, respectively. The space
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
of holomorphic sections is unitarily equivalent to the set of equivariant CR functions
f\in H^{2}(\partial D)
satisfying
f(r_{\theta}x)=e^{ik\theta}f(x)
, where
r_{\theta}
denotes the circle action on
\partial D
. Under this identification, operators (
29
) are related by

\Pi_{h^{k}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}(r_{\theta})^{*}\Pi_{h}\,d\theta.

(30)

Note that the Fourier decomposition of
\Pi_{h}
coincides with the spectral decomposition of
D_{\theta}=\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
on
\partial D
.

Turning to the Grauert tube setting of (
1
) and (
4
), the naive approach of replacing
(r_{\theta})^{*}\Pi_{h}
by
(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}
is insufficient. Instead, the former is replaced by
\Pi_{\tau}\widehat{\sigma}(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}
, where
\widehat{\sigma}
is a polyhomogeneous pseudodifferential operator on
\partial M_{\tau}
that makes the resulting composition unitary. Thus, we are led to the CR holomorphic analogue of (
30
) that is

\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\chi}(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\Pi_{\tau}\widehat{\sigma}(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}\,dt\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\chi}(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\Pi_{\tau}e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}\,dt.

(31)

Note that (
31
) is essentially the spectral decomposition of the elliptic Toeplitz operator
\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}
introduced in (
2
). The same operator is also studied in
[
31
, Theorem 0.12]
.

1.1.2.
L^{p}
estimates and quantized Hamiltonians on line bundles

In the line bundle setting
(L,h)\to(X,\omega)
, Shiffman–Zelditch
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
proved
\lVert\Pi_{h^{k}}\rVert_{L^{p}\to L^{q}}\leq Ck^{m(1/p-1/q)}
by combining the Shur–Young inequality with a near-diagonal Gaussian estimate
[
22
, Lemma 5.2]
. Consequently,
\lVert s\rVert_{L^{p}}=O(k^{m(1/2-1/p)})
for all
L^{2}
-normalized holomorphic sections
s\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
.

The proof techniques of our Grauert tube analogue,
Theorem 1.3
, are similar. But, in place of a near-diagonal scaling asymptotics, we need the full strength of
Theorem 1.1
, which is an asymptotic expansion in a
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}
-neighborhood of the orbit
p\mapsto G^{s}_{\tau}(p)
. This finer control of the time evolution under the Reeb flow (i.e.,
G^{t}_{\tau}
on
\partial M_{\tau}
or
r_{\theta}
on the circle bundle) is unnecessary in the line bundle setting because rotations of the fiber introduce only an overall phase factor to the near-diagonal scaling asymptotics.

There has also been prior work on quantized Hamiltonian flows on line bundles. More precisely, let
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
be a Hamiltonian on the classical phase space (Kähler manifold)
X
that induces a
1
-parameter group of symplectomorphisms
\varphi_{t}\colon X\to X
which lifts to a family of contactomorphisms
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\colon\partial D\to\partial D
. As shown by Zelditch
[
32
]
, these contactomorphisms may be quantized as unitary maps

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\colon L^{2}(\partial D)\to L^{2}(\partial D),\quad\Phi_{t}=R_{t}\Pi_{h}(\tilde{\varphi}_{-t})^{*}\Pi_{h},

(32)

in which
R_{-t}
is a zeroth order Toeplitz operator chosen to ensure the unitarity of
\Phi_{t}
. In a series of papers, Paoletti
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
computed scaling asymptotics for the Fourier coefficients (with respect to the
S^{1}
action) of
\Phi_{t}
near points on the graph of
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}
. When
t=0
, this specializes to the scaling asymptotics of
[
3
,
22
]
. We emphasize that in contrast,
G^{t}_{\tau}
is simultaneously playing the role of the
S^{1}
action and the Hamiltonian flow in our set up. Nevertheless, our main argument borrows heavily from that of
[
20
]
. We also mention the works of Zelditch–Zhou
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
, which treat other types of asymptotics for partial Bergman kernels of quantized Hamiltonian flows on line bundles.

1.1.3.
L^{p}
norms of eigenfunctions in the real domain

In this section we discuss how the
L^{p}
estimates of
Theorem 1.3
and
section 1
compare with those of Sogge eigenfunctions in the real domain. Let
P_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}
denote the orthogonal projection onto the span of Laplace eigenfunctions
\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}
with frequencies
\lambda\leq\lambda_{j}<\lambda+1
.

Theorem 1.11

(Sogge
[
24
]
, see also
[
25
,
15
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, then the following estimates are sharp.

\displaystyle\lVert P_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}f\rVert_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\begin{cases}C\lambda^{(\frac{n-1}{2})(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}&\text{for $2\leq q\leq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}$},\\
C\lambda^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}&\text{for $\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\leq q<\infty$}.\end{cases}

(33)

Consequently, for
L^{2}
-normalized Laplace eigenfunctions
\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}
with frequencies
\lambda_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\begin{cases}C\lambda_{j}^{(\frac{n-1}{2})(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}&\text{for $2\leq q\leq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}$},\\
C\lambda_{j}^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{for $\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\leq q<\infty$}.\end{cases}

(34)

Note the presence of a critical exponent
q_{n}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
at which the sharp estimates change. Roughly speaking, high
L^{q}
norms measure concentration around single points, whereas low
L^{q}
norms measure concentration around larger sets such as geodesics and hypersurfaces. It is well known on the round sphere
S^{n}
that the sequence of zonal spherical harmonics at a pole saturate the estimate for
q>q_{n}
. On the other hand, the sequence of highest weight spherical harmonics, that is Gaussian beams along a stable elliptic geodesic, saturate the estimate for
q<q_{n}
. However, these bounds are rarely sharp on other manifolds. For example, on the flat torus all eigenfunctions have
L^{q}
norms bounded by
O(1)
. An interesting question in this direction is which manifolds admit sequences of eigenfunctions that saturate these bounds. We point the readers to
[
26
,
28
,
29
,
27
]
for research in this topic.

Although we project onto the orthonormal basis consisting of eigenfunctions of
\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}
rather than onto the span
\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}
, thanks to
section 1
we can interpret
Theorem 1.5
as a complexified version of the theorem above. In the complex setting there is no critical exponent
q_{n}
differentiating the behavior between the low and high
L^{q}
norms. Indeed, the exponent in our sharp estimate is analogous to that of Sogge’s in the low
L^{q}
regime, and we show in
subsection 5.1
that complexified Gaussian beams are extremals for all
p
.

Our main result has an interpretation as measuring concentration in phase space. As discussed in
[
31
]
, the squares of
\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}}
are microlocal lifts of
\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}
to
\partial M_{\tau}\cong S^{*}_{\tau}M
, so they may be viewed as probability densities of finding a quantum particle at a phase space point in
\partial M_{\tau}
. Their marginals are given by the pushforward
\pi_{*}(\tilde{\varphi}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}})^{2}
under the natural projection
\pi\colon S^{*}_{\tau}M\to M
. It is natural to ask how the marginal densities of these Husimi distributions relate to eigenfunction concentration on
M
. Other types of phase space norms of eigenfunctions have been studied by Blair–Sogge
[
1
,
2
]
. We also mention the work of Galkowski
[
10
]
, which uses defect measures to study eigenfunction concentration. It would be interesting to compare the results and techniques with those of complexification.

Background

We assume throughout that
(M,g)
is a closed, real analytic Riemannian manifold of dimension
m\geq 2

,

2.1.
Kähler geometry on Grauert tubes

A real analytic Riemannian manifold
M
admits a complexification
M_{\mathbb{C}}
into which
M
embeds as a totally real submanifold. The
Grauert tube function
is defined by

\sqrt{\rho}\colon U\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\sqrt{\rho}(z)=\frac{1}{2i}\sqrt{r^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{z})},

(35)

where
r^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{w})
is the analytic extension of the square of the Riemannian distance function
r\colon M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
to a neighborhood of the diagonal in
M_{\mathbb{C}}\times\overline{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
. In a neighborhood of
M
in
M_{\mathbb{C}}
, the square
\rho
of (
35
) is the unique strictly plurisubharmonic function such that the metric induced by the Kähler form
i\partial\overline{\partial}\rho
restricts to the Riemannian metric
g
on
M
.

For each
0<\tau\leq\tau_{\mathrm{max}}
, the sublevel set

M_{\tau}=\{z\in M_{\mathbb{C}}:\sqrt{\rho}(z)<\tau\}

(36)

is called the
Grauert tube of radius
\tau
. It is diffeomorphic
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
to the co-ball bundle
B^{*}_{\tau}M=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:\lvert\xi\rvert_{x}<\tau\}
of radius
\tau
under the imaginary-time exponential map

E\colon B^{*}_{\tau}M\to M_{\tau},\quad E(x,\xi)=\exp_{x}^{\mathbb{C}}i\xi.

(37)

Let
G^{t}
denote the homogeneous geodesic flow, that is, the Hamilton flow of
|\xi|_{x}
, on the cotangent bundle. Then, for each
0<\tau\leq\tau_{\mathrm{max}}
, the
C^{\omega}
diffeomorphism (
37
) conjugates the geodesic flow on the sphere bundle
S^{*}_{\tau}M
to the Hamilton flow
G^{t}_{\tau}=\exp t\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
of the Grauert tube function on
\partial M_{\tau}
:

G^{t}_{\tau}\colon\partial M_{\tau}\to\partial M_{\tau},\quad G^{t}_{\tau}=E\circ G^{t}\circ E^{-1}|_{\partial M_{\tau}}.

(38)

With this identification in mind, we will henceforth refer to
G^{t}_{\tau}\colon\partial M_{\tau}\to\partial M_{\tau}
as the “geodesic flow.”

2.2.
Contact and CR structure on the Grauert tube boundary

The Grauert tube
M_{\tau}
is a strongly pseudoconvex domain thanks to the existence of the strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function
\sqrt{\rho}
. The pullbacks of the canonical 1-form
\alpha_{T^{*}M}=\xi\,dx
and the symplectic form
\omega_{T^{*}M}=d\xi\wedge dx
on the cotangent bundle under the diffeomorphism (
37
) are

\alpha:=(E^{-1})^{*}\alpha_{T^{*}M}=d^{c}\sqrt{\rho}\quad\text{and}\quad(E^{-1})^{*}\omega_{T^{*}M}=dd^{c}\rho.

(39)

We endow the Grauert tube boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
with the volume form

d\mu_{\tau}=(E^{-1})^{*}(\alpha_{T^{*}M}\wedge\omega_{T^{*}M}^{m-1})\Big|_{\partial M_{\tau}},

(40)

which is the pullback of the standard Liouville volume form on
S^{*}_{\tau}M
. Since
\partial M_{\tau}
is a real hypersurface in the Kähler manifold
M_{\tau_{\mathrm{max}}}\subseteq(M_{\mathbb{C}},J)
, we see that
H=JT\partial M_{\tau}\cap T\partial M_{\tau}
is a real
J
-invariant hyperplane bundle. The restriction
\alpha|_{\partial M_{\tau}}
of the 1-form in (
39
) is a contact form on
\partial M_{\tau}
with
\ker\alpha=H
.

The characteristic vector field given by
T=\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
is the unique one on
\partial M_{\tau}
satisfying
\alpha(T)=1
and
d\alpha(T,\,\cdot\,)=0
. The complexification of the decomposition
T\partial M_{\tau}=H\oplus\mathbb{R}T
yields a CR structure:

\displaystyle T^{\mathbb{C}}\partial M_{\tau}=H^{(1,0)}\partial M_{\tau}\oplus H^{(0,1)}\partial M_{\tau}\oplus\mathbb{C}T,

(41)

where
H^{(1,0)}
and
H^{(0,1)}
are the
J
-holomorphic and
J
-antiholomorphic subspaces, respectively.

2.3.
The Szegő projector and the Boutet de Movel–Sjöstrand parametrix

The Szegő projector
\Pi_{\tau}
associated to the boundary of a Grauert tube is the orthogonal projection

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}\colon L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau},d\mu_{\tau})\to H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau},d\mu_{\tau})

(42)

onto the Hardy space consisting of boundary values of holomorphic functions in
M_{\tau}
that are square integrable with respect to the volume form (
40
). This is a Fourier integral operator with a positive complex canonical relation whose real points are the graph of the identity map on the symplectic cone

\Sigma_{\tau}=\{(\zeta,r\alpha_{\zeta}):r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}\subseteq T^{*}(\partial M_{\tau})

(43)

spanned by the contact form (
39
).
Using (
37
), we can construct a symplectic equivalence

\iota_{\tau}\colon T^{*}M-0\to\Sigma_{\tau},\quad\iota_{\tau}(x,\xi)=\bigg(E\Big(x,\tau\frac{\xi}{\lvert\xi\rvert}\Big),\lvert\xi\rvert\alpha_{E(x,\tau\frac{\xi}{\lvert\xi\rvert})}\bigg).

(44)

Details of the symbol of the Szegő projector can be found in
[
4
, Theorem 11.2]
. Briefly, let
\Sigma_{\tau}^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{C}
be the complexified normal bundle of (
43
). The symbol
\sigma(\Pi_{\tau})
of
\Pi_{\tau}
is a rank one projection onto a ground state
e_{\Lambda_{\tau}}
, which is annihilated by a Lagrangian system of Cauchy–Riemann equations corresponding to a Lagrangian subspace
\Lambda_{\tau}\subseteq\Sigma_{\tau}^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

The time evolution
\Pi_{\tau}\mapsto G^{-t}_{\tau}\Pi_{\tau}G^{t}_{\tau}
under the Hamilton flow (
38
) yields another a rank one projection onto some time-dependent ground state
e_{\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}}
, where
\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}
is the pushforward of
\Lambda_{\tau}
under the flow. The quantity

\sigma_{t,\tau,0}=\langle e_{\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}},e_{\Lambda_{\tau}}\rangle^{-1}

(45)

appears in (
54
) and (
64
). See
[
35
, Section 4.3]
for details.

The Szegő kernel
\Pi_{\tau}(z,w)
is defined by the relation

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}f(z)=\int_{\partial M_{\tau}}\Pi_{\tau}(z,w)f(w)\,d\mu_{\tau}(w)\quad\text{for all $f\in L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})$}.

(46)

To describe an oscillatory integral representation for the kernel, we introduce the defining function

\varphi_{\tau}\colon M_{\tau_{\mathrm{max}}}\to[0,\infty),\quad\varphi_{\tau}(z)=\rho(z)-\tau^{2},

(47)

so that
\varphi_{\tau}<0
in
M_{\tau}
and
\varphi_{\tau}=0
on
\partial M_{\tau}
.
Let
\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})
be the analytic extension of
\varphi_{\tau}(z)=\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{z})
to
M_{\tau}\times\overline{M}_{\tau}
obtained by polarization.

\displaystyle\psi_{\tau}(z,w)=\frac{1}{i}\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})=\frac{1}{i}\left(-\frac{1}{4}r^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{w})-\tau^{2}\right).

(48)

By construction,
\psi
is holomorphic in
z
, antiholomorphic in
w
, and satisfies
\psi(z,w)=-\overline{\psi(z,w)}
. It appears as the phase function of the following parametrix for
\Pi_{\tau}
due to Boutet de Monvel and Sjöstrand.

Theorem 2.1

(The Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand parametrix,
[
6
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

With
\psi
as in (
48
), there exists a classical symbol

s\in S^{m-1}(\partial M_{\tau}\times\partial M_{\tau}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})\quad\text{with}\quad s(z,w,\sigma)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{m-1-k}s_{k}(z,w)

(49)

so that the Szegő kernel (
46
) has the oscillatory integral representation

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}(z,w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\sigma\psi_{\tau}(z,w)}s(z,w,\sigma)\,d\sigma\quad\text{modulo a smoothing kernel}.

(50)

A key estimate for
\varphi_{\tau}
(or equivalently for the phase function
\psi
) can be stated in terms of the
Calabi diastatis function
, which is defined by

D(z,w)=\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{z})+\varphi_{\tau}(w,\overline{w})-\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})-\varphi_{\tau}(w,\overline{z}).

(51)

In the closure of the Grauert tube,
[
6
, Corollary 1.3]
gives the lower bound

\displaystyle D(z,w)\geq C\big(d(z,\partial M_{\tau})+d(w,\partial M_{\tau})+d(z,w)^{2}\big)\quad\text{for $z,w\in\overline{M_{\tau}}$}.

(52)

2.4.
The Toeplitz operator
\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}
and tempered eigenfunction sums

In this section, we introduce the two operators for which we compute the scaling asymptotics in
Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.9
.

The operator
\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}
is a generalized Toeplitz operator in the sense of Boutet de Monvel–Guillemin
[
4
]
. Here,
D_{\sqrt{\rho}}=\frac{1}{i}\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
is differentiation along the Hamilton vector field of the Grauert tube function. The symbol of
D_{\sqrt{\rho}}
is nowhere vanishing on
\Sigma_{\tau}-0
, where
\Sigma_{\tau}
is the symplectic cone (
43
). Thus,
\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}
is elliptic and its spectrum discrete.

As discussed in
subsubsection 1.1.1
, the Grauert tube analogue of Fourier coefficients of the Szegő kernel are given by the spectral localizations

\displaystyle\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}=\Pi_{\tau}\chi(\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}-\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\chi}(t)e^{-it\lambda}\Pi_{\tau}e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}\,dt.

(53)

Here,
\widehat{\chi}
is a Schwartz function whose Fourier transform is compactly supported in some small neighborhood
[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
of the origin, and
\widehat{\chi}(0)=1
.

If we denote by the pullback by the Hamilton flow (
38
) of
\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
on
\partial M_{\tau}
, then due to
[
31
, Proposition 5.3]
, there exists a classical polyhomogeneous pseudodifferential operator
\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\tau}(w,D_{\sqrt{\rho}})
on
\partial M_{\tau}

so that

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}\sim\Pi_{\tau}\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\tau}(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}\quad\text{modulo a smoothing Toeplitz operator}.

(54)

The symbol
\sigma_{t,\tau}
of
\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\tau}
admits a complete asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\sigma_{t,\tau}(w,r)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{t,\tau,j}(w)r^{-j},

(55)

in which
\sigma_{t,\tau,0}=\langle e_{\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}},e_{\Lambda_{\tau}}\rangle^{-1}
is to leading order the reciprocal of the overlap of two Gaussians, as in (
45
).

Remark 2.2
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\chi}(t)e^{-it\lambda}\Pi_{\tau}\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\tau}(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}\,dt\bigg)(x,y)

(56)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\!\int_{\partial M_{\tau}}\widehat{\chi}(t)e^{-it\lambda}\Pi_{\tau}(x,w)\sigma_{t,\tau}(w)\Pi_{\tau}(G^{t}_{\tau}(w),y)\,dtdy.

(57)

In the proof of
Theorem 1.1
, we replace the two Szegő kernels in the expression above by the parametrices (
50
) and directly compute the resulting oscillatory integral in parameter
\sqrt{\lambda}
using stationary phase.

We now introduce the tempered spectral projections kernel
P_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)
constructed using analytically continued eigenfunctions. Recall the eigenequation (
17
) for the Laplacian on
M
. The eigenfunction expansion of the Schwartz kernel of the half-wave operator
U(t)=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}
is given by

\displaystyle U(t,x,y)=\sum_{j}e^{it\lambda_{j}}\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}(x)\overline{\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}(y)}.

(58)

As shown in
[
5
,
13
,
11
,
34
]
, for
0<\tau\leq\tau_{\mathrm{max}}
, the Schwartz kernel
U(t,x,y)
admits an analytic extension
U(t+i\tau,x,y)
in the time variable
t\mapsto t+i\tau\in\mathbb{C}
, and then in the spacial variable
x\mapsto z\in M_{\tau}
. Let
\mathcal{O}^{s}(\partial M_{\tau})
denote the order
s
Sobolev space of CR holomorphic functions on the Grauert tube boundary. Then the Poisson operator

U(i\tau)=e^{-\tau\sqrt{-\Delta}}\colon L^{2}(M)\to\mathcal{O}^{\frac{m-1}{4}}(\partial M_{\tau})

(59)

with kernel
U(i\tau,z,y)
is a Fourier integral operator of order
-(m-1)/4
with complex phase associated to the canonical relation
\{(y,\eta,\iota_{\tau}(y,\eta)\}\subseteq T^{*}M\times\Sigma_{\tau}
. Here, the quantities
\iota_{\tau}
and
\Sigma_{\tau}
are defined in (
44
) and (
43
). We recall the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
34
, Lemma 8.2]
)
.

Let
\Psi^{s}
denote the class of psuedodifferential operators of order
s
. Then,

(1)

U(i\tau)^{*}U(i\tau)\in\Psi^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}\left(M\right)
with principal symbol
|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}
.

(2)

U(i\tau)U\left(i\tau\right)^{*}=\Pi_{\tau}A_{\tau}\Pi_{\tau}
where
A_{\tau}\in\Psi^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\left(\partial M_{\tau}\right)
has principal symbol
|\sigma|_{g}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}
as a function on
\Sigma_{\tau}
.

To introduce the complexified spectral projection kernels, we need to further continue
U(i\tau,z,y)
anti-holomorphically in the
y
variable. Consider the operator

U^{\mathbb{C}}(t+2i\tau)=U(i\tau)U(t)U(i\tau)^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}(\partial M_{\tau})\to\mathcal{O}(\partial M_{\tau})

(60)

with Schwartz kernel

U^{\mathbb{C}}(t+2i\tau,z,w)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{i(t+2i\tau)\mu_{j}}\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}}(z)\overline{\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}}(w)}.

(61)

Set
t=0
, then the partial sums of the expression above becomes

P_{\lambda}(z,w)=\sum_{j:\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}e^{-2\tau\lambda_{j}}\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}}(z)\overline{\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}}(w)}.

(62)

To smooth out the kernel, we fix
\varepsilon>0
and fix
\chi
a positive even Schwartz function such that
\widehat{\chi}\left(0\right)=1
and
\supp\widehat{\chi}\subseteq[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
. Define

\displaystyle P_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\chi\ast d_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(z,w)\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\chi}(t)e^{-it\lambda}U^{\mathbb{C}}(t+2i\tau,z,w)\,dt.

(63)

As before, if we denote by
(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}

\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
on
\partial M_{\tau}

\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\tau}(w,D_{\sqrt{\rho}})

\partial M_{\tau}

\displaystyle U^{\mathbb{C}}(t+2i\tau)\sim\Pi_{\tau}\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\tau}\left(G^{t}_{\tau}\right)^{*}\Pi_{\tau}\quad\text{modulo a smoothing Toeplitz operator}.

(64)

The symbol
\sigma_{t,\tau}
of
\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\tau}
admits a complete asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\sigma_{t,\tau}\left(w,r\right)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{t,\tau,j}\left(w\right)r^{-\frac{m-1}{2}-j},

(65)

2.5.
Quantization of linear symplectic maps on Bargmann–Fock space

The proofs of our theorems involve Taylor expansions in appropriate coordinates to reduce the geometry to the model linear space, so we briefly review the metaplectic representation on Bargmann–Fock space used to quantize symplectic linear mappings. Details can be found
[
30
,
9
]

The Bargmann–Fock space on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{m})=\Big\{f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{m},dz)\mathrel{}\Big|\mathrel{}f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{m})\Big\}.

(66)

The reproducing Bergman kernel has the exact formula

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{H}}(z,{w})=\left(2\pi\right)^{-m}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}+z\bar{w}}.

(67)

Let
Sp(m,\mathbb{R})
denote the space of real symplectic matrices on
\mathbb{R}^{2m}=\mathbb{R}^{m}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{y}
with respect to the standard symplectic form. Then matrix multiplication
M\in Sp(m,\mathbb{R})
in real coordinates takes the form

\displaystyle M\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

(68)

We map
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
into
\mathbb{C}^{2m}
via
(x,y)\mapsto(x+iy,x-iy)=:(z,\bar{z})
. Under this mapping, (
68
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\begin{pmatrix}z\\
\bar{z}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}P&{Q}\\
\bar{Q}&\bar{P}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}z\\
\bar{z}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z^{\prime}\\
\bar{z}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

(69)

where the holomorphic component
P
and antiholomorphic component
Q
of the symplectic mapping are given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}P&Q\\
\bar{Q}&\bar{P}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{W}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{W},\quad\mathcal{W}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}I&I\\
-iI&iI\end{pmatrix}.

(70)

(The choice of normalization is taken so that
\mathcal{W}
is unitary.) The explicit formula for the holomorphic component is

\displaystyle P=\frac{1}{2}(A+D+i(C-B)).

(71)

The metaplectic representation on
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is defined by
M\mapsto\Pi_{\mathcal{H},{M}}
, the latter being a unitary operator with kernel

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{H},{M}}(z,w)=(\det P)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}\Pi_{\mathcal{H}}(z,\mathcal{M}v)\Pi_{\mathcal{H}}(v,w)\,dv,

(72)

in which we set
\mathcal{M}v:=Pv+Q\bar{v}
. (The ambiguity of the sign of
\left(\det P\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is determined by the lift to the double cover.) Explicit computations involving standard Gaussian integrals show

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{H},{M}}(z,w)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{K_{M}}(z,w)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}},

(73)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K_{M}}(z,w)

\displaystyle:=(2\pi)^{-m}(\det P)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(z\bar{Q}P^{-1}z+2\bar{w}P^{-1}z-\bar{w}P^{-1}Q\bar{w}\right)\right\}.

(74)

The principal term of
Theorem 1.1
contains the lift of
\Pi_{\mathcal{H},M}
to the reduced Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{m}_{\text{red}}\cong S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{m}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{H},M}(\theta,z,\phi,w)=e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\Pi_{\mathcal{H},M}(z,w).

(75)

and

, with the two proofs being identical. The techniques are similar to those of
[

.

3.1.
Identities in Heisenberg coordinates

We briefly recall the notion of Heisenberg coordinates. We point the reader to
[
8
]
for detailed construction of these coordinates on any strongly pseudoconvex CR manifold, and to
[
7
]
for the Grauert tube setting. Roughly speaking, in Heisenberg coordinates, the strongly pseudoconvex boundary
\partial M_{\tau}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
is, to a first approximation, the Heisenberg group viewed as a Seigel domain in complex Euclidean space. More precisely, given a point
p\in\partial M_{\tau}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
one may use the Levi procedure as in
[
8
, Section 18]
to construct holomorphic coordinates
(z_{0},z_{1},\dotsc,z_{n-1})=(z_{0},z^{\prime})
on an open neighborhood
U\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
such that for
w\in U

\displaystyle\rho(z_{0},z^{\prime})=-\Imp z_{0}+|z^{\prime}|^{2}+O\left(|z_{0}||z^{\prime}|+|z^{\prime}|^{3}\right)

(76)

We note that
(t,z^{\prime}):=(\Rep z_{0},z^{\prime})
constrained by
\rho(z_{0},z^{\prime})=0
provides a coordinate system on the open neighborhood
V=U\cap\partial M_{\tau}
in
\partial M_{\tau}
. We will refer to both the coordinates on
M_{\mathbb{C}}
as well as the coordinates on
\partial M_{\tau}
as Heisenberg coordinates. Furthermore, let
Z_{0}=\frac{T}{|T|}
where
T
is the characteristic vector field and
Z_{1},...,Z_{m-1}
denote an orthonormal frame of
T^{1,0}\partial M_{\tau}
. Then in Heisenberg coordinates centered at
p
we have

\displaystyle Z_{0}\big|_{p}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigg|_{p},\ Z_{j}\big|_{p}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\bigg|_{p}

(77)

We now record several Taylor expansions in Heisenberg coordinates established by the authors in
[
7
]
that will be useful in subsequent sections. In the following

4.1.
Proof of

We invoke the Shur-Young inequality

With

as in

, we break up the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M_{\tau}}|\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)|^{r}\,dw

\displaystyle=\int_{d(T_{\delta}(z),w)\leq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}}|\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)|^{r}\,dw

(132)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{d(T_{\delta}(z),w)\geq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}}|\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)|^{r}\,dw.

(133)

The integration by parts argument for

can be adapted to show that (

. We use

\displaystyle C\lambda^{r(m-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2(m-1)}}e^{-r\lambda\frac{|u|^{2}}{4\tau}}\,du\leq C\lambda^{(r-1)(m-1)}.

(135)

Note that

and by (
13

\displaystyle\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(\Phi^{w}_{\chi,\lambda})(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Pi_{\chi^{2},\lambda}(z,w)}{\lVert\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(\,\cdot\,,w)\rVert_{L^{p}(\partial M_{\tau})}}.

(136)

To estimate the numerator of (

), we observe

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M_{\tau}}\lvert\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)\rvert^{q}\,dw\geq\int_{d\left(T_{\delta}(z),w\right)\leq D\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)\rvert^{q}\,dw,

(137)

so by applying

to the integrand and a using similar argument used to show (

Similarly, the denominator of (

4.2.
Proof of

: norm estimates for
\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}

A standard argument
[

converts the

to that for the projection

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})}\leq C\lambda^{(m-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\partial M_{\tau})},

(139)

which we now establish. For the upper bound, we compute

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{j}<\lambda+1}\lvert\langle f,e_{j}\rangle\rvert^{2}

(140)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\chi(\lambda-\lambda_{j})^{2}\lvert\langle f,e_{j}\rangle\rvert^{2}

(141)

\displaystyle=C\lVert\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

(142)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2(m-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}}^{2}

(143)

To show this upper bound is saturated, fix

and set

. We compute

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}f_{\chi,\lambda}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{j}<\lambda+1}\lvert\chi(\lambda-\lambda_{j})\rvert^{2}\lvert e_{\lambda_{j}}(w)\rvert^{2}\lVert e_{\lambda_{j}}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

(144)

\displaystyle\geq C\sum_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{j}<\lambda+1}\lvert e_{j}(w)\rvert^{2}

(145)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\vol(\partial M_{\tau})}\int_{\partial M_{\tau}}\sum_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{j}<\lambda+1}\lvert e_{j}(z)\rvert^{2}\,dz

(146)

\displaystyle\geq C(N(\lambda+1)-N(\lambda))

(147)

\displaystyle\sim C\lambda^{m-1},

(148)

where

It follows from the proof of the sharpness of

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in L^{p}\left(\partial M_{\tau}\right)}\lambda^{-(m-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\lVert\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}f\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}}}

\displaystyle\geq C\lambda^{-\frac{(m-1)}{2}}(N(\lambda+1)-N(\lambda))^{\frac{1}{2}}

(149)

\displaystyle\geq C+o(1).

(150)

Taking the

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup_{f\in L^{p}\left(\partial M_{\tau}\right)}\lambda^{-(m-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\lVert\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}f\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}}}>0

(151)

which shows that upper bound is sharp.

5.1.
Complexified Gaussian beams as extremals: direct computation

In this section, we show that the
L^{p}
estimate of
section 1
on complexified Laplace eigenfunctions is saturated by analytic continuations of Gaussian beams on the round
S^{2}
. We use spherical coordinates

\displaystyle x=\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\quad y=\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\quad z=\cos\varphi,

(158)

where
0\leq\varphi\leq\pi
and
0\leq\theta<2\pi
. The standard spherical harmonics are the joint eigenfunctions

-\Delta_{S^{2}}Y_{N}^{m}=N(N+1)Y_{N}^{m},\quad\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}Y_{N}^{m}=mY_{N}^{m},\quad-N\leq m\leq N.

(159)

of the spherical Laplacian and the angular momentum operator. The highest weight spherical harmonic (Gaussian beam) is of the form

\displaystyle Y_{N}^{N}(\theta,\varphi)=c_{N}\sin^{N}(\varphi)e^{iN\theta},\quad c_{N}=\frac{(-1)^{N}}{2^{N}N!}\sqrt{\frac{(2N+1)!}{4\pi}}\sim N^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(160)

It is convenient to transfer the computations of Guillemin–Stenzel
[
12
]
from Cartesian coordinates to spherical coordinates. In terms of complexified Cartesian coordinates, the Grauert tube
S^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of the sphere is the set

\displaystyle\big\{(x+i\xi_{x},y+i\xi_{y},z+i\xi_{z}):(x+i\xi_{x})^{2}+(y+i\xi_{y})^{2}+(z+i\xi_{z})^{2}=1\big\},

(161)

and the Grauert tube function is

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho}(x+i\xi_{x},y+i\xi_{y},z+i\xi_{z})=\sinh^{-1}\Big[(\xi_{x}^{2}+\xi_{y}^{2}+\xi_{z}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big].

(162)

In terms of complexified spherical coordinates, we have

\displaystyle x+i\xi_{x}

\displaystyle=\sin(\varphi+i\xi_{\varphi})\cos(\theta+i\xi_{\theta})

(163)

\displaystyle=\cos(\varphi)\sinh(\xi_{\varphi})\sin(\theta)\sinh(\xi_{\theta})+\sin(\varphi)\cosh(\xi_{\varphi})\cos(\theta)\cosh(\xi_{\theta})

(164)

\displaystyle\quad+i\Big[\cos(\varphi)\sinh(\xi_{\varphi})\cos(\theta)\cosh(\xi_{\theta})-\sin(\varphi)\cosh(\xi_{\varphi})\sin(\theta)\sinh(\xi_{\theta})\Big]

(165)

\displaystyle y+i\xi_{y}

\displaystyle=\sin(\varphi+i\xi_{\varphi})\sin(\theta+i\xi_{\theta})

(166)

\displaystyle=-\cos(\varphi)\sinh(\xi_{\varphi})\cos(\theta)\sinh(\xi_{\theta})+\sin(\varphi)\cosh(\xi_{\varphi})\sin(\theta)\cosh(\xi_{\theta})

(167)

\displaystyle\quad+i\Big[\cos(\varphi)\sinh(\xi_{\varphi})\sin(\theta)\cosh(\xi_{\theta})+\sin(\varphi)\cosh(\xi_{\varphi})\cos(\theta)\sinh(\xi_{\theta})\Big]

(168)

\displaystyle z+i\xi_{z}

\displaystyle=\cos(\varphi)\cosh(\xi_{\varphi})-i\sin(\varphi)\sinh(\xi_{\varphi}).

(169)

The formula for
\sqrt{\rho}
in spherical coordinates is complicated. Since we will be simplifying our expressions by picking special values, it suffices to note

\displaystyle\xi_{x}^{2}+\xi_{y}^{2}+\xi_{z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}(\varphi)\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})\cosh^{2}(\xi_{\theta})

(170)

\displaystyle\quad+\sin^{2}(\varphi)(\cosh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\theta})+\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})).

(171)

We also note that the analytically continued highest weight spherical harmonic is of the form

\displaystyle(Y_{N}^{N})^{\mathbb{C}}(\theta+i\xi_{\theta},\varphi+i\xi_{\varphi})=c_{N}\Big[\sin(\varphi)\cosh(\xi_{\varphi})+i\cos(\varphi)\sinh(\xi_{\varphi})\Big]^{N}e^{iN\theta}e^{-N\xi_{\theta}}.

(172)

To simplify (
170
) and (
172
), we fix
\varphi=\pi/2
so that

\displaystyle\xi_{x}^{2}+\xi_{y}^{2}+\xi_{z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\cosh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\theta})+\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi}),

(173)

\displaystyle(Y_{N}^{N})^{\mathbb{C}}(\theta+i\xi_{\theta},i\xi_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=c_{N}\cosh^{N}(\xi_{\varphi})e^{iN\theta}e^{-N\xi_{\theta}}\quad(c_{N}\sim N^{\frac{1}{4}}).

(174)

We additionally set
\tau=1
, so that (
162
) and (
173
) imply the Grauert tube boundary is given by
\sinh^{2}(1)=\cosh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\theta})+\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})
. Direct computation shows the equality is satisfied whenever

\displaystyle-1<\xi_{\varphi}<1\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{\theta}=-\sinh^{-1}\Big[\operatorname{sech}(\xi_{\varphi})\big(\sinh^{2}(1)-\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big].

(175)

Note that
\xi_{\theta}<0
, so

\displaystyle\lVert(Y_{N}^{N})^{\mathbb{C}}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=c_{N}^{p}\int_{\partial S^{2}_{1}}\lvert\cosh^{N}(\xi_{\varphi})\rvert^{p}e^{-Np\xi_{\theta}}d\xi_{\theta}d\xi_{\varphi}

(176)

\displaystyle\geq c_{N}^{p}\int_{-1}^{1}\lvert\cosh^{N}(\xi_{\varphi})\rvert^{p}e^{Np\sinh^{-1}\big[\operatorname{sech}(\xi_{\varphi})\big(\sinh^{2}(1)-\sinh^{2}(\xi_{\varphi})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]}\,d\xi_{\varphi}

(177)

\displaystyle\geq c_{N}^{p}\int_{-1}^{1}\lvert\cosh^{N}(\xi_{\varphi})\rvert^{p}\,d\xi_{\varphi}

(178)

\displaystyle\geq c_{N}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}e^{Np\xi_{\varphi}}\,d\xi_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\sim N^{\frac{p}{4}}\frac{e^{Np}}{Np}.

(180)

In the last line we used

. Combined with the universal asymptotics
\lVert(Y_{N}^{N})^{\mathbb{C}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})}=N^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{N}(1+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}))
proved in
[

, we conclude

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert(Y_{N}^{N})^{\mathbb{C}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\partial S_{1}^{2})}}{\lVert(Y_{N}^{N})^{\mathbb{C}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial S_{1}^{2})}}\sim\frac{N^{\frac{1}{4}}N^{-\frac{1}{p}}e^{N}}{N^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{N}}=N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}},

showing that

5.2.
Complexified Gaussian beams as extremals: geometric explanation

As mentioned earlier, in the real domain Gaussian beams only saturate the low
L^{p}
norms whereas zonal spherical harmonics saturate the high
L^{p}
norms. As in
[
31
]
, we give a heuristic symplectic geometry explanation for why complexifications of Gaussian beams are also extremals for high
L^{p}
norms on
\partial S^{2}_{\tau}
.

Let
P
be the north pole and let
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
denote the generator of rotation about the
z
-axis. The zonal spherical harmonics denoted
Y^{0}_{N}
are semiclassical Lagrangian distributions associated to

\displaystyle\Lambda_{P}=\{g^{t}(S^{*}_{P}S^{2}):t\in\mathbb{R}\}

(182)

Under the natural projection
S^{*}S^{2}\to S^{2}
there is a blowdown singularity at
P
, which leads to peaking of sup norms. This is a heuristic explanation for the zonal harmonics saturating high
L^{p}
norms in the real domain.

Now let
E
denote the equator. The Gaussian beams
Y^{N}_{N}
associated to
E
have wavefront set

\displaystyle\Gamma=\{g^{t}(p,d\theta):p\in E,\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}

(183)

Th symplectic cone
\Sigma_{\tau}=(\partial M_{\tau},\mathbb{R}^{+}d\alpha)\cong(S^{*}_{\tau}M,\mathbb{R}^{+}d\xi)
from (
43
) is the phase space of the Grauert tube boundary. Under the identification (
44
),
\Lambda_{P}
is a Lagrangian submanifold embedded in
\partial M_{\tau}
and no blowdown singularities occur, suggesting that zonal harmonics are longer extremals in the complex domain. Instead, the geodesic flow and the lift of rotations to
S^{*}S^{2}
coincide on
\Gamma
, and
\Gamma
is a singular leaf of the foliations of
\partial M_{\tau}
generated by the geodesic flow together with rotations. This singularity suggests that Gaussian extremizes
L^{p}
norms in the complex domain.

base space admit an action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
which commutes with the projection.

We now construct the
Dirac bundle
as a sub-bundle. The total space is

\mathfrak{B}_{m}:=\{(p,\Psi):p\in X_{m},\;\Psi\in\mathfrak{S},\;\phi(p)\Psi=m\Psi\}

the base space is
X_{m}
, and the projection is

\mathfrak{B}_{m}\to X_{m},\quad\quad(p,\Psi)\mapsto p.

The fibre at
p\in X_{m}
is the linear subspace of
\mathfrak{S}
given by

\{(p,\Psi):\phi(p)\cdot\Psi=m\Psi\}

Now we have

\displaystyle\phi(p)\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\Psi

\displaystyle\implies\tau(A)\phi(p)\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\tau(A)\Psi

\displaystyle\implies\tau(A)\phi(p)\tau(A^{-1})\tau(A)\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\tau(A)\Psi

\displaystyle\implies\phi(Ap)(\tau(A)\Psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\tau(A)\Psi

by Theorem (
2.8
). That is to say,

\phi(p)\Psi=m\Psi\implies\phi(Ap)(A\cdot\Psi)=m(A\cdot\Psi)

This shows that
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
sends, for each
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, the fibre at
p
to the fibre at
A\cdot p
. Therefore, the Dirac bundle
\mathfrak{B}_{m}\to X_{m}
is an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-bundle.

We have
\phi(p_{0})=m\phi(v_{0})=m\gamma_{0}
and so the fibre at
p_{0}
is given by

\{\Psi\in\mathfrak{S}:\gamma_{0}\Psi=\Psi\}

This is the eigenspace of
\gamma_{0}
with eigenvalue
1
. Now
\gamma_{0}^{2}=1
, so
\gamma_{0}
has two eigenspaces
\mathfrak{S}_{+},\mathfrak{S}_{-}
of dimension
2
with eigenvalues
+1,-1
.

Therefore, the Dirac bundle is an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-bundle of rank
2
.

The isotropy subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
at
p_{0}
is the compact Lie group
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
. By Lemma
2.5
and Theorem
2.8
, we have

\tau(A)\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}\tau(A)

for all
A\in\mathrm{SU}_{2}
. We have

\Psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{+}\implies\gamma_{0}\Psi=\Psi\implies\tau(A)\gamma_{0}\Psi=\tau(A)\Psi\implies\gamma_{0}\tau(A)\Psi=\tau(A)\Psi\implies\tau(A)\Psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{+}

so that
\tau|_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}
leaves
\mathfrak{S}_{+}
invariant.

The vector space
\mathfrak{S}
admits a natural basis, namely
\{e_{1},e_{2},\overline{e_{1}},\overline{e_{2}}\}
. We calculate that

\displaystyle\phi(v_{0})(e_{2}+\overline{e_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{2}+\overline{e_{1}}

\displaystyle\phi(v_{0})(e_{1}-\overline{e_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{1}-\overline{e_{2}}

\displaystyle\phi(v_{0})(e_{1}+\overline{e_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(e_{1}+\overline{e_{2}})

\displaystyle\phi(v_{0})(e_{2}-\overline{e_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(e_{2}-\overline{e_{1}})

The
+1
-eigenspace of
\phi(v_{0})
is therefore given by

\mathfrak{S}_{+}=\textrm{span of}\>\{e_{1}-\overline{e_{2}},e_{2}+\overline{e_{1}}\}

Let

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{it}&0\\
0&e^{-it}\end{array}\right)

Then we have

\tau(A)e_{1}=e^{it}e_{1},\quad\tau(A)e_{2}=e^{-it}e_{2},\quad\tau(A)\overline{e_{1}}=e^{-it}\overline{e_{1}},\quad\tau(A)\overline{e_{2}}=e^{it}\overline{e_{2}}

\displaystyle\tau(A)(e_{1}-\overline{e_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{it}(e_{1}-\overline{e_{2}})

\displaystyle\tau(A)(e_{2}+\overline{e_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-it}(e_{2}+\overline{e_{1}})

The character of
\tau(A)
is therefore
e^{it}+e^{-it}
and so
\tau|_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}
is the spin
1/2
representation of
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
on
\mathfrak{S}_{+}
.

3.2.
The Dirac bundle as an associated vector bundle

Let
p_{0}=(m,0,0,0)
and note that

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/\mathrm{SU}_{2}\simeq X_{m},\quad\quad A\mapsto Ap_{0}.

We construct the associated vector bundle

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{+}

where
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
acts on
\mathfrak{S}_{+}
via the spin
1/2
representation
\tau|_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}
. The elements of the associated vector bundle are equivalence classes
[A,\Psi]

with
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
\Psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{+}
, defined as

[A,\Psi]:=\{(AT,\tau(T)^{-1}\Psi):T\in\mathrm{SU}_{2}\}

Theorem 3.1
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-bundles as follows:

\beta:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{+}\simeq\mathfrak{B}_{m}

[A,\Psi]\mapsto(Ap_{0},\tau(A)\Psi)

Proof.

Note that
\mathfrak{S}_{+}\subset\mathfrak{S}
and
\tau(A)\Psi\in\mathfrak{S}
. The map
\beta
is well-defined: replacing
A
by
AT
and
\Psi
by
\tau(T^{-1})\Psi
, we have

(AT,\tau(T)^{-1}\Psi)\mapsto(ATp_{0},\tau(AT)\tau(T)^{-1}\Psi)=(Ap_{0},\tau(A)\Psi).

We have

\displaystyle\phi(Ap_{0})\tau(A)\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(A)\phi(p_{0})\tau(A^{-1})\tau(A)\Psi\quad\textrm{by}\quad\textrm{Theorem}\;\ref{Cliff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(A)\phi(p_{0})\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(A)m\gamma_{0}\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(A)m\Psi\quad\textrm{since}\quad\Psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\tau(A)\Psi

so that
(Ap_{0},\tau(A)\Psi)\in\mathfrak{B}_{m}
as required.

Conversely, given
(p,\Psi)\in\mathfrak{B}_{m}
.
Choose
A
such that
p=Ap_{0}
and set
\Phi=\tau(A)^{-1}\Psi
. Then we have

\displaystyle\phi(p)\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\Psi

\displaystyle\implies\tau(A)\phi(p_{0})\tau(A)^{-1}\tau(A)\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\tau(A)\Phi

\displaystyle\implies\tau(A)\phi(p_{0})\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\tau(A)\Phi

\displaystyle\implies\gamma_{0}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle\implies\Phi

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{+}

Therefore, we have

\beta:(A,\Phi)\mapsto(Ap_{0},\tau(A)\Phi)=(p,\Psi)

as required.

Now
A
is determined mod
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
. If we replace
A
by
AT
with
T\in\mathrm{SU}_{2}
then we must replace
(A,\Phi)
by

(AT,y)
with
y
defined by

y=\tau(AT)^{-1}\Psi=\tau(T)^{-1}\tau(A)^{-1}\Psi=\tau(T)^{-1}\Phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{+}

This leads to the pair

(AT,\tau(T)^{-1}\Phi)

which is in the same equivalence class as
(A,\Phi)
. Therefore, the inverse of the map
\beta
is well-defined.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The Dirac bundle is isomorphic as an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-bundle to the associated vector bundles

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}S

and

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}\overline{S}

Proof.

There is a natural action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
S
. The action on the conjugate vector space is defined as

A\cdot\overline{v}=\overline{Av}

with
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
v\in S
. Now use the fact that
\mathfrak{S}_{+},S
and
\overline{S}
are isomorphic as
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
-modules.
∎

3.3.
Dirac equation

The condition

\phi(p)\Psi=m\Psi

with
\Psi\in\mathfrak{S}
is, written in full,

\left(p_{0}\gamma_{0}+p_{1}\gamma_{1}+p_{2}\gamma_{2}+p_{3}\gamma_{3}\right)\Psi=m\Psi

In the slashed notation
[
F
, p.118]
, this equation appears as

(9)

\displaystyle\not{p}\Psi=m\Psi

This is the Dirac equation. After Fourier transform of the variables
p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
, the equation appears in the more familiar form

\sum_{r=0}^{3}i\gamma_{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{r}}\Psi=m\Psi

where
\gamma_{\mu}:=\phi(v_{\mu})
with
\mu=0,1,2,3,
are
\gamma
-matrices satisfying the conditions

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}^{2}\>=\>\gamma_{3}^{2}\>=-1

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}

for all
\mu\neq\nu
.

3.4.
A pair of conjugate
2
-spinor fields

Let
\Psi
be a solution of the Dirac equation (
9
), i.e.
\phi(p)\Psi=m\Psi
. To mark the dependence on
p
, we will write
this equation as

\phi(p)\Psi_{p}=m\Psi_{p}

Then
\{\Psi_{p}:p\in X_{m}\}
is a section of the Dirac bundle
\mathfrak{B}_{m}
. By Corollary
3.2
,
\mathfrak{B}_{m}
is isomorphic to the associated bundle

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}S

The section
\{\Psi_{p}:p\in X_{m}\}
therefore determines a section of (
10
), which is a
2
-spinor field over the manifold
X_{m}
. Now
\mathfrak{B}_{m}
is also isomorphic to the conjugate bundle

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times_{\mathrm{SU}_{2}}\overline{S}

and so the section
\{\Psi_{p}:p\in X_{m}\}
determines a section of (
11
), which is the conjugate
2
-spinor field. In this way, a solution of the Dirac equation determines a pair of conjugate

2
-spinor fields over the manifold
X_{m}
.

3.5.
Comments

It is worth noting that the Clifford module structure of
\mathfrak{S}
survives up to and including the Dirac equation, for

\phi(p)
belongs to the Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}\ell(\mathbb{P}^{4},Q)
.

In the literature, in the mathematical accounts of the Dirac equation
[
Var
, Eqn.(136)]
,
[
Sim
, p.72]
,
[
Jos
, Eqn.1.3.52]
one finds the
map

A\mapsto A\oplus(A^{*})^{-1}

instead of our map

\tau(A)=A\oplus\overline{A}

From a conceptual point of view, the adjoint
A^{*}
of
A\in\mathrm{SL}(S)
is not defined, because
S
is not equipped with a sesquilinear inner product. The space
S
is endowed only with the symplectic
form
(x,y)\mapsto\varepsilon(x,y)
.

4.
The transition to spinor fields on space-time

So far, we have dealt only with
2
-spinors. In order to apply this theory to
spinor fields
on space-time, we proceed as follows.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a smooth real
4
-manifold. The first observation is that we can view
S
as a (smooth) complex
2
-plane
bundle over the base space
\mathfrak{M}
. The second observation is that the operations of conjugation, direct sum and tensor product are as easy to apply to vector bundles as to vector spaces.
Then
\overline{S}
is the conjugate of
S
,
\varepsilon
is a non-degenerate symplectic form on
S
,
V
is the real part of
S\otimes\overline{S}
,
and
g
is the Lorentz metric with signature
+---
given by

g=\varepsilon\otimes\overline{\varepsilon}.

Then
V
is a real
4
-plane bundle on
\mathfrak{M}
, equipped with the Lorentz metric tensor. At this point, we have to identify
V
with the tangent bundle
T\mathfrak{M}
of
\mathfrak{M}
:

T\mathfrak{M}=V.

In the terminology of
[
PR
, p.211]
, we must identify the world-vectors in
V
with the tangent vectors to the manifold
\mathfrak{M}
.
In that case, we can view
\mathfrak{M}
as a model of curved space-time in general relativity. The smooth sections of the vector bundle
S
are called
2
-spinor fields; the smooth sections of

\mathfrak{S}
are called
4
-spinor fields.

The whole of §2 carries over,
at no extra cost
, to the theory of spinor fields on manifolds. In this formulation, the curved space-time
\mathfrak{M}
of general relativity is

already equipped
with spinor fields.

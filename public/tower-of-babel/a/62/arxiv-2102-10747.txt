Abstract
. In this paper, we construct an infinite family of normal Cayley graphs, which are 2 2 -distance-transitive but neither distance-transitive nor 2 2 -arc-transitive. This answers a question raised by Chen, Jin and Li in 2019 and corrects a claim in a literature given by Pan, Huang and Liu in 2015.

2
-distance-transitive graph, simple group.

Introduction

In this paper, all graphs are finite, simple, and undirected.
For a graph
{\it\Gamma}
, let
V({\it\Gamma}),E({\it\Gamma}),A({\it\Gamma})
or
{\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma})
denote its vertex set,
edge set, arc set and its full automorphism group, respectively.
The graph
{\it\Gamma}
is called
G
-
vertex-transitive
,
G
-
edge-transitive
or
G
-
arc-transitive
, with
G\leq{\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma})
, if
G
is transitive on
V({\it\Gamma}),E({\it\Gamma})
or
A({\it\Gamma})
respectively, and
G
-
semisymmetric
, if
{\it\Gamma}
is
G
-edge-transitive but not
G
-vertex-transitive. It is easy to see that a
G
-semisymmetric graph
{\it\Gamma}
must be bipartite such that
G
has two orbits, namely the two parts of
{\it\Gamma}
, and the stabilizer
G_{u}
for any
u\in V({\it\Gamma})
is transitive the neighbourhood of
u
in
{\it\Gamma}
. An
s
-
arc
of
{\it\Gamma}
is a sequence
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{s}
of
s+1
vertices of
{\it\Gamma}
such that
v_{i-1},v_{i}
are adjacent for
1\leq i\leq s
and
v_{i-1}\neq v_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq s-1
. If
{\it\Gamma}
has at least one
s
-arc and

is transitive on the set of
s
-arcs of
{\it\Gamma}
, then
{\it\Gamma}
is called
(G,s)
-arc-transitive
, and
{\it\Gamma}
is said to be
s
-arc-transitive
if it is
({\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma}),s)
-arc-transitive.

For two vertices
u
and
v
in
V({\it\Gamma})
, the
distance

between
u
and
v
in
{\it\Gamma}
is the smallest length of paths between
u
and
v
, and the
diameter

{\sf diam}({\it\Gamma})
of
{\it\Gamma}
is the maximum distance occurring over all pairs of vertices.
For
i=1,2,\cdots,{\sf diam}({\it\Gamma})
, denote by
{\it\Gamma}_{i}(u)
the set of vertices at distance
i
with vertex
u
in
{\it\Gamma}
. A graph
{\it\Gamma}
is called

if, for any vertices
u,v,x,y
with
d(u,v)=d(x,y)
,
there exists
g\in{\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma})
such that
(u,v)^{g}=(x,y)
.
The graph
{\it\Gamma}
is called
(G,t)
-distance-transitive
with
G\leq{\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma})
if, for each
1\leq i\leq t
, the group
G
is transitive on the ordered pairs of form
(u,v)
with
d(u,v)=i
, and
{\it\Gamma}
is said to be
t
-distance-transitive
if it is
({\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma}),t)
-distance-transitive.

Distance-transitive graphs were first defined by Biggs and Smoth in
[
2
]
, and
they showed that there are only 12 trivalant distance-transitive graphs.
Later, distance-transitive graphs of valencies
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
and
7

were classified in
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
,
and a complete classification of distance-transitive graphs with symmetric or alternating groups of automorphisms was given by Liebeck, Praeger and Saxl
[
20
]
.
The
2
-distance-transitive but not 2-arc-transitive graphs of valency at most 6 were classified in
[
5
,
18
]
,
and the
2
-distance-primitive graphs (a vertex stabilizer of automorphism group is primitive on
both the first step and the second step neighbourhoods of the vertex) with prime valency were classified in
[
17
]
.
By definition, a
2
-arc-transitive graph is
2
-distance-transitive, but a
2
-distance-transitive graph may not be
2
-arc-transitive; a simple example is the complete multipartite graph
{\sf K}_{3,2}
.
Furthermore, Corr, Jin and Schneider
[
4
]
investigated properties of a connected
(G,2)
-distance-transitive but not
(G,2)
-arc-transitive graph of girth
4
,
and they applied the properties to classify such graphs with prime valency. For more information about
2
-distance-transitive graphs, we refer to
[
6

7
]

For a finite group
G
and a subset
S\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}
with
S=S^{-1}:=\{s^{-1}\mid s\in S\}
, the
Cayley graph

{\sf Cay}(G,S)
of the group
G
with respect to
S
is the graph with vertex set
G
and with two vertices
g
and
h
adjacent if
hg^{-1}\in S
.
For
g\in G
, let
R(g)
be the permutation of
G
defined by
x\mapsto xg
for
all
x\in G
. Then
R(G):=\{R(g)\mid g\in G\}
is a regular group of automorphisms of
{\sf Cay}(G,S)
.
It is known that a graph
{\it\Gamma}
is a Cayley graph of
G
if and only if

{\it\Gamma}
has a regular group of automorphisms on the vertex set which is isomorphic to
G
;
see
[
1
, Lemma 16.3]
and
[
25
]
.
A Cayley graph
{\it\Gamma}={\sf Cay}(G,S)
is called

if
R(G)
is a normal subgroup of
{\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma})
. The study of normal Cayley graphs was initiated by Xu
[
27
]
and has been investigated under various additional conditions; see
[
9
,
24
]
.

There are many interesting examples of arc-transitive graphs and

2
-arc-transitive graphs constructed as normal Cayley graphs. However, the status for
2
-distance-transitive graphs is different. Recently,
2
-distance-transitive circulants
were classified in
[
3
]
, where the following question was proposed:

Question 1.1
.

(
[
3
, Question 1.2]
)

Is there a normal Cayley graph which is 2-distance-transitive,
but neither distance-transitive nor 2-arc-transitive?

In this paper, we answer the above question by constructing an infinite family of such graphs.

Theorem 1.2
.

For an odd prime
p
, let
G=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{p}=b^{p}=c^{p}=1,[a,b]=c,[c,a]=[c,b]=1\rangle
and
S=\{a^{i},b^{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq p-1\}
.
Then
{\sf Cay}(G,S)
is a
2
-distance-transitive normal Cayley graph that is neither distance-transitive nor
2
-arc-transitive.

Applying this theorem, we can obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Under the notation given in Theorem
1.2
, let
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
be the graph with vertex set
\{\langle a\rangle g\ |\ g\in G\}\cup\{\langle b\rangle h\ |\ h\in G\}
and with edges all these coset pairs
\{\langle a\rangle g,\langle b\rangle h\}
having non-empty intersection in
G
. Then
{\sf Cay}(G,S)
is the line graph of
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
, and
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
is
3
-arc-transitive.

The graph
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
was first constructed in
[
21
]
as a regular cover of
{\sf K}_{p,p}
, where it is said that
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
is
2
-arc-transitive in
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
, but not
3
-arc-transitive generally for all odd primes
p
in a remark after
[
21
, Example 4.1]
. However, this is not true and Corollary
1.3
implies that
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
is always
3
-arc-transitive for each odd prime
p
. In fact,
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
is
3
-arc-regular, that is,
{\sf Aut}({\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle))
is regular on the set of
3
-arcs of
{\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle b\rangle)
.

Let

Let

.

Proof Theorem
1.2

For a positive integer
n
and a prime
p
, we use
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
to denote the cyclic group of order
n
and the elementary abelian group of order
p^{r}
, respectively. In this section, we always assume that
p
is an odd prime, and denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
consisting of all non-zero numbers in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p-1}
. Furthermore, we also set the following assumptions in this section:

G=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{p}=b^{p}=c^{p}=1,[a,b]=c,[c,a]=[c,b]=1\rangle,\ S=\{a^{i},b^{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq p-1\},

{\it\Gamma}={\sf Cay}(G,S),\ \ {\sf A}={\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma}),\ \ N=N_{\sf A}(R(G))=R(G)\rtimes{\sf Aut}(G,S),\ \mbox{ and }\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}=\langle t\rangle.

By Proposition
2.4
,
N_{\sf A}(R(G))=R(G)\rtimes{\sf Aut}(G,S)
, and
R(g)^{\delta}=R(g^{\delta})
for any
R(g)\in R(G)
and
\delta\in{\sf Aut}(G,S)
. Since
G=\langle S\rangle
,
{\it\Gamma}
is a connected Cayley graph of valency
2(p-1)
. Let

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\alpha:a\longmapsto a^{t},&b\longmapsto b,&c\longmapsto c^{t};\\
\beta:a\longmapsto a,&b\longmapsto b^{t},&c\longmapsto c^{t};\\
\gamma:a\longmapsto b,&b\longmapsto a,~&c\longmapsto c^{-1}.\end{matrix}

Since
a^{t},b,c^{t}
satisfy the same relations as
a,b,c
in
G
and
G=\langle a^{t},b,c^{t}\rangle
,
\alpha
induces an automorphism of
G
, and we still denote by
\alpha
this automorphism. Similarly,
\beta
and
\gamma
are also automorphisms of
G
.

Lemma 3.1
.

{\sf Aut}(G,S)=\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma\rangle\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p-1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p-1})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and
{\it\Gamma}
is
N
-arc-transitive. Furthermore,
N
has no normal subgroup of order
p^{2}
.

Proof.
Since
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}=\langle t\rangle
, it is easy to check that
\alpha^{p-1}=\beta^{p-1}=\gamma^{2}=1
,
\alpha\beta=\beta\alpha
and
\alpha^{\gamma}=\beta
. Thus
\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma\rangle\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p-1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p-1})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Clearly,
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\sf Aut}(G,S)
.
To prove
{\sf Aut}(G,S)=\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma\rangle\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p-1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p-1})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, it suffices to show that
|{\sf Aut}(G,S)|\leq 2(p-1)^{2}
.

Clearly,
\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma\rangle
is transitive on
S
, and hence
{\it\Gamma}
is
N
-arc-transitive. Since
G=\langle S\rangle
,
{\sf Aut}(G,S)
is faithful on
S
. By Proposition
2.1
,
|{\sf Aut}(G,S)|=|S||{\sf Aut}(G,S)_{a}|
, where
{\sf Aut}(G,S)_{a}
is the stabilizer of
a
in
{\sf Aut}(G,S)
. Note that
{\sf Aut}(G,S)_{a}
fixes
a^{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq p-1
. Again by Proposition
2.1
,
|{\sf Aut}(G,S)_{a}|\leq(p-1)|{\sf Aut}(G,S)_{a,b}|
, where
{\sf Aut}(G,S)_{a,b}
is the subgroup of
{\sf Aut}(G,S)
fixing
a
and
b
. Since
G=\langle a,b\rangle
, we obtain
{\sf Aut}(G,S)_{a,b}=1
, and then
|{\sf Aut}(G,S)|\leq 2(p-1)^{2}
, as required.

Let
H\leq N
be a subgroup of order
p^{2}
. Since
R(G)
is the unique normal Sylow
p
-subgroup of
N=R(G)\rtimes{\sf Aut}(G,S)
, we have
H\leq R(G)
, and since
|R(G):H|=p
, we have
H\unlhd R(G)
. Note that the center
C:=Z(R(G))=\langle R(c)\rangle
and
C\cap H\not=1
. Thus,

C\cap H=C
as
|C|=p
, implying
C\leq H
. Since
H/C
is a subgroup of order
p
, and
R(G)/C=\langle R(a)C\rangle\times\langle R(b)C\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
, we have
H/C=\langle R(b)C\rangle
or
\langle R(a)R(b)^{i}C\rangle
for some
0\leq i\leq p-1
. It follows that
H=\langle R(b)\rangle\times C
or
\langle R(ab^{i})\rangle\times C
for some
0\leq i\leq p-1
.

Suppose
H\unlhd N
. Since
C
is characteristic in
R(G)
and
R(G)\unlhd N
, we have
C\unlhd N
.
Recall that
R(a)^{\gamma}=R(a^{\gamma})=R(b)
. Then
(\langle R(a)\rangle\times C)^{\gamma}=\langle R(b)\rangle\times C
. This implies that both
\langle R(a)\rangle\times C
and
\langle R(b)\rangle\times C
are not normal in
N
. Thus,
H=\langle R(ab^{i})\rangle\times C
for some
1\leq i\leq p-1
.
Since
H\unlhd N
, we have
H^{\beta}=H
, that is,
\langle R(ab^{ti})\rangle\times C=H^{\beta}=H=\langle R(ab^{i})\rangle\times C
. It follows that
\langle R(ab^{ti})\rangle=\langle R(ab^{i})\rangle
and then
R(ab^{ti})=R(ab^{i})
, which further implies

. This gives rise to

, and since
(i,p)=1
, we have

, contradicting that

. Thus,

has no normal subgroup of order

.

A

of a graph
{\it\Gamma}
is a maximal complete subgraph, and the
clique graph

{\it\Sigma}
of
{\it\Gamma}
is defined to have the set of all cliques of
{\it\Gamma}
as its vertex set with two cliques adjacent in
{\it\Sigma}
if the two cliques have at least one common vertex. For a positive integer
n
,
n_{p}
denotes the largest
p
-power diving
n
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The clique graph
{\it\Sigma}
of
{\it\Gamma}
is a connected
p
-valent bipartite graph of order
2p^{2}
,
{\sf A}
has a faithful natural action on
{\it\Sigma}
, and
{\it\Sigma}
is
R(G)
-semisymmetric and

-arc-transitive. Furthermore,
|{\sf A}|_{p}=p^{3}
.

Proof.
Recall that
G=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{p}=b^{p}=c^{p}=1,[a,b]=c,[c,a]=[c,b]=1\rangle
and
S=\{a^{i},b^{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq p-1\}
. Then
{\it\Gamma}={\sf Cay}(G,S)
has exactly two cliques passing through
1
, that is, the induced subgraphs of
\langle a\rangle
and
\langle b\rangle
in
{\it\Gamma}
. Since
R(G)\leq{\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma})
is transitive on vertex set, each clique of
{\it\Gamma}
is an induced subgraph of the coset
\langle a\rangle x
or
\langle b\rangle x
for some
x\in G
. Thus, we may view the vertex set of
{\it\Sigma}
as
\{\langle a\rangle x,\langle b\rangle x\ |\ x\in G\}
with two cosets adjacent in
{\it\Sigma}
if they have non-empty intersection. It is easy to see that
\langle a\rangle x\cap\langle b\rangle y\not=\emptyset
if and only if
|\langle a\rangle x\cap\langle b\rangle y|=1
, and any two distinct cosets, either in
\{\langle a\rangle x\ |\ x\in G\}
or in
\{\langle b\rangle x\ |\ x\in G\}
, have empty intersection. Furthermore,
\langle a\rangle
has non-empty intersection with exactly
p

\langle b\rangle a^{i}
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
. Thus,
{\it\Sigma}
is a
p
-valent bipartite graph of order
2p^{2}
. The connectedness of
{\it\Sigma}
follows from that of
{\it\Gamma}
.

Clearly,
{\sf A}
has a natural action on
{\it\Sigma}
. Let
K
be the kernel of
{\sf A}
on
{\it\Sigma}
. Then

fixes each coset of
\langle a\rangle x
and
\langle b\rangle x
for all

. Since
\langle a\rangle x\cap\langle b\rangle x=\{x\}
,

and hence

. Thus,
{\sf A}
is faithful on
{\it\Sigma}
and we may let
{\sf A}\leq{\sf Aut}({\it\Sigma})
.

Note that
R(G)
is not transitive on
\{\langle a\rangle x,\langle b\rangle x\ |\ x\in G\}
, but transitive on
\{\langle a\rangle x\ |\ x\in G\}
and
\{\langle b\rangle x\ |\ x\in G\}
. Furthermore,
R(\langle a\rangle)
fixes
\langle a\rangle
and is transitive on
\{\langle b\rangle a^{i}\ |\ 0\leq i\leq p-1\}
, the neighbourhood of
\langle a\rangle
in
{\it\Sigma}
, and similarly,
R(\langle b\rangle)
fixes
\langle b\rangle

Lemma 3.3

{\sf A}={\sf Aut}({\it\Gamma})=R(G)\rtimes{\sf Aut}(G,S)

Proof.

or

3.2

Assume that

in

Assume that

(d,\ell)=(1,3)
or
(3,1)

R(G)\leq M

is transitive on
{\it\Gamma}
.

For
(d,\ell)=(1,3)
,

is a non-abelian simple group. Since

-group, Proposition
2.3
implies that

is
2
-transitive on
{\it\Gamma}
, forcing that
{\it\Gamma}
is the complete graph of order

, a contradiction.

For
(d,\ell)=(3,1)
, we have
M=T_{1}\times T_{2}\times T_{3}
. Then
|M|_{p}=p^{3}
, and since
M\unlhd{\sf A}
, we derive
R(G)\leq M
. By Lemma
3.2

M\leq{\sf Aut}({\it\Sigma})
, and
{\it\Sigma}
is
R(G)
-semisymmetric. Since

is
M

\gamma

{\it\Sigma}

.

M

. If

{\sf A}
has a normal Sylow

Now it is easy to see that the quotient graph

Proof of Theorem

.

By Lemmas

\displaystyle{\it\Gamma}_{1}(1)=S=\{a^{i},b^{i}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq p-1\},

Embeddings of weighted Sobolev spaces and a weighted fourth order elliptic equation Thanks: *: corresponding author. The research of the first author is supported by NSFC 11571093 and the third author is supported by NSFC 11671144.
Zongming Guo, Fangshu Wan, Liping Wang*

Abstract
. New embeddings of weighted Sobolev spaces are established. Using such embeddings, we obtain the existence and regularity of positive solutions with Navier boundary value problems for a weighted fourth order elliptic equation. We also obtain Liouville type results for the related equation. Some problems are still open.



Keywords: positive solutions, fourth order with weights, Liouville theorem

Mathematics Subject Classification 2000: 35B45; 35J40

1.
Introduction

We study structure of nonnegative solutions of the weighted fourth order elliptic equation

[formula]

\Delta(|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u)=|x|^{l}u^{p}\;\;\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

where
N\geq 5
,
p>1
,
\alpha,l\in\mathbb{R}
.

For a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
0\in\Omega
, we also study the Navier boundary value problem

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u)=|x|^{l}u^{p}&\mbox{in $\Omega$},\\
u=\Delta u=0&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{array}\right.

By a positive solution of (P), we mean that
u\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

u>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
u
satisfies (P) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
. By a positive solution of (Q), we mean that
u\in C^{4}(\Omega\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}({\overline{\Omega}})
,
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}({\overline{\Omega}})
,
u>0
in
\Omega
and
u
satisfies (Q) in
\Omega\backslash\{0\}
.

Throughout this paper, we assume

(1.1)

2N>N^{\prime}:=N+\alpha>4,\;\;\;\tau:=l-\alpha>-4,

(1.2)

p_{s}:=\frac{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}{N^{\prime}-4}\;(>1).

It is known from (
1.1
) that
\alpha\in(4-N,N)
.

Equation (P) and problem (Q) have been studied by many authors recently, in particular, in the case of the pure biharmonic operator, see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,
16
,
18
,
19
,

,
21
,
23
,
25
]
and the references therein.

Under the assumptions in (
1.1
), the following Liouville theorem for (P) is
still open.

Open problem 1:
Assume that
N\geq 5
and (
1.1
) holds,
u\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a nonnegative solution of (P). Then

u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
provided
1<p<p_{s}
.

The positive answer to this open problem for
\alpha=l=0
was given in
[
23
]
via the moving plane argument. But it does not apply for our case here (especially for
l>0
), since the weights do not match with the procedure of this argument. In this paper, we will give a partial answer to this open problem. More precisely, when
\alpha\in(4-N,N)
, we obtain the positive answer if
1<p<\min\{\frac{N+4}{N-4},p_{s}\}
. Especially, when
\alpha=2
we confirm the result for
1<p<p_{s}
. Namely, we have the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
N\geq 5
and (
1.1
) holds. If
u\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a nonnegative solution to (P). Then
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
provided
1<p<\min\{\frac{N+4}{N-4},p_{s}\}
.

Note that
p_{s}\leq\frac{N+4}{N-4}
is equal to
\alpha\geq\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
and
p_{s}>\frac{N+4}{N-4}
provided
\alpha<\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
. Hence Theorem
1.1
implies that
if
\alpha\geq\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
, the answer is positive for
1<p<p_{s}
while
\alpha<\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
, it only gives out the result for
1<p<\frac{N+4}{N-4}
. So there is a gap
\frac{N+4}{N-4}\leq p<p_{s}
provided
\alpha<\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
. But for the symmetric solutions or
\alpha=2
, we can still get the result in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
N\geq 5
and (
1.1
) holds. Assume also that
u\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a nonnegative radial solution to (P). Then
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
provided
1<p<p_{s}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
N\geq 5
,
\alpha=2
and
l>-2
. Assume also that
u\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|x|^{2}\Delta u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a nonnegative solution to (P). Then
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
provided
1<p<p_{s}=\frac{N+2+2l}{N-2}
.

Remark 1.4
.

There is no first order term in (
3.24
) if
\alpha=2
and we can get the monotonicity in the radial direction.

For a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
of class
C^{2}
with
0\in\Omega
, we
define
D^{1,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)
as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega\backslash\{0\})

with the norm

\|\phi\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dx

and
D^{2,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)
as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega\backslash\{0\})

with the norm

\|\phi\|_{2,\alpha}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}|\Delta\phi|^{2}dx.

Also we define
L_{l}^{q}(\Omega)\;(q\geq 1)
the space of functions
\phi
such that

|x|^{\frac{l}{q}}|\phi|\in L^{q}(\Omega)

with the norm

\|\phi\|_{L_{l}^{q}(\Omega)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|\phi|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

We now establish the following embeddings:

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain of class
C^{2}
, but not a ball, with
0\in\Omega
. Assume also that
N\geq 5
and (
1.1
) holds with
\alpha\geq\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
. Then, the embedding:

(1.3)

D^{2,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{l}^{q}(\Omega)

is continuous for any
q\in[1,p_{s}+1]
and this embedding is compact for any
q\in[1,p_{s}+1)
.

Note that
p_{s}\leq\frac{N+4}{N-4}
provided
\alpha\geq\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
. The related embeddings from
D^{1,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)
to
L_{l}^{q}(\Omega)
was obtained in
[
17
]
. Such embeddings may have been known already, but we can not find a suitable reference.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain of class
C^{2}
, but not a ball, with
0\in\Omega
. Assume also that
N\geq 5
and (
1.1
) holds with
\alpha<\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
. Then, the embedding:

(1.4)

D^{2,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{l}^{q}(\Omega)

is continuous for any
q\in[1,\frac{2N}{N-4}]
and this embedding is compact for any
q\in[1,\frac{2N}{N-4})
.

Note that
p_{s}>\frac{N+4}{N-4}
provided
\alpha<\frac{(N-4)}{4}\tau
. So the above two propositions establish the embedding theorem for
1<p<\min\{\frac{N+4}{N-4},p_{s}\}
. Actually, for radial functions in the ball, we can achieve better embedding theorem.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
B
be a ball centered at 0. Assume also that
N\geq 5
and (
1.1
) holds. Then, the embedding:

D^{2,\alpha}_{0,\mbox{{\tiny rad}}}(B)\hookrightarrow L_{l}^{q}(B)

is continuous for any
q\in[1,p_{s}+1]
and this embedding is compact for any
q\in[1,p_{s}+1)
, where
D^{2,\alpha}_{0,\mbox{{\tiny rad}}}(B)=\{\phi\in D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(B):\;\phi(x)=\phi(|x|)\}
.

Using the embeddings given in Propositions
1.5
-
1.7
, we can obtain positive solutions
u
of (Q) or positive radial solutions
u
of (Q) via variational methods for
1<p<p_{s}
or
1<p<\frac{N+4}{N-4}
.
The existence of positive radial solutions of the related weighted elliptic equations for second order was obtained in
[
26
]
.
The boot-strap argument implies that the obtained solutions
u
of (Q) satisfy
u\in C^{4}(\Omega\backslash\{0\})
or
u\in C^{4}(B\backslash\{0\})
. We will see from the theorems below that the singular point 0 of
u
is removable, i.e.,
u\in C^{4}(\Omega\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}({\overline{\Omega}})
and
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}({\overline{\Omega}})
or
u\in C^{4}(B\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}({\overline{B}})
and
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}({\overline{B}})
.

We know that
p_{s}>\frac{N+4}{N-4}
provided
\alpha<\frac{N-4}{4}\tau
. The embedding in
Proposition
1.6
implies that we can only obtain the existence and regularity of positive solutions of (Q) for
1<p<\frac{N+4}{N-4}
. The following problem is still open.

Open problem 2.
Assume that
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\;(N\geq 5)
with
0\in\Omega
but not a ball. Assume also that (
1.1
) holds with
\alpha<\frac{N-4}{4}\tau
.

Does (Q) admit a positive solution
u\in C^{4}(\Omega\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}({\overline{\Omega}})
and
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}({\overline{\Omega}})
provided
\frac{N+4}{N-4}\leq p<p_{s}
?

In this paper, we use
C
to denote a universal positive constant, which may be changed from one line to another line.

2.
Embeddings of weighted Sobolev spaces and proof of Propositions
1.5
-
1.7

In this section, we present the proof of Propositions
1.5
-
1.7
.

Proof of Proposition
1.5

Since
\alpha\geq\frac{N-4}{4}\tau
, we see that
p_{s}\leq\frac{N+4}{N-4}
.
It is known from Theorem 1.1 and Remark 2.2 of
[
6
]
that the embedding:

(2.1)

D^{2,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{l}^{p_{s}+1}(\Omega)

is continuous. That is, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
u
such that

(2.2)

\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u|^{p_{s}+1}dx\Big)^{\frac{2}{p_{s}+1}}\leq C\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta u)^{2}dx.

This inequality is corresponding to Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality for the second order operator (see
[
4
,
7
]
). Note that when
4-N<\alpha<N
,

-\gamma_{\alpha}:=\Big(\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\Big)^{2}-\Big(\frac{N-2}{2}\Big)^{2}\not\in\Lambda(S^{N-1}),

where
\Lambda(S^{N-1})
is the Dirichlet spectrum of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
S^{N-1}
.

We now show that the embedding in (
1.3
) is continuous for any
q\in[1,p_{s}+1)
. To see this, we just notice that, for
u\in D^{2,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)
and
q\in[1,p_{s}+1)
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u|^{p_{s}+1}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}+1}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta u)^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s}+1}}\;\;\;\;\;(\mbox{by \eqref{2.2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta u)^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This implies that the embedding is continuous. Note that
\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}dx\leq C
provided

N+l=N^{\prime}+\tau>0
.

To prove the embedding is compact, we divide the proof into 2 steps.

Step 1.
We show that if
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)
, there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
) such that
u_{m}
converges in
L_{l}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Suppose that
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)
, then there exists
C>0
independent of
m
such that
\|u_{m}\|_{D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)}\leq C
. Setting
v_{m}=|x|^{l}u_{m}
, we see from (
2.3
) that
v_{m}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\|v_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C
, where
C>0
is independent of
m
. Let
B
be a large ball such that
\Omega\subset\subset B
. If we denote

{\tilde{u}}_{m}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{m}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
0,&x\in B\backslash\Omega\end{array}\right.

and
{\tilde{v}}_{m}(x)=|x|^{l}{\tilde{u}}_{m}(x)
, we see that
{\tilde{v}}_{m}\in L^{1}(B)
and
\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B)}\leq C
, where
C>0
is independent of
m
. Let

{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{-N}\int_{B}\rho\Big(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\Big){\tilde{v}}_{m}(y)dy,

where
0<\epsilon<\delta:=
dist
(\partial\Omega,\partial B)
and
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\rho(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho(x)dx=1
. Obviously,
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
all have their supports in
B
. We now claim that for each fixed
\epsilon>0
, the sequence
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded and equi-continuous. Indeed, we see that, for
x\in B
,

|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}(x)|\leq\sup|\rho|\epsilon^{-N}\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B)}\leq C\epsilon^{-N},

|\nabla{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}(x)|\leq\sup|\nabla\rho|\epsilon^{-N-1}\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B)}\leq C\epsilon^{-N-1},

where
C>0
is independent of
m
. It follows from Arzela-Ascoli theorem that
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is precompact in
C^{0}({\overline{B}})
. Therefore, it is precompact in
L^{1}(B)
. We next claim that

(2.4)

{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}\to{\tilde{v}}_{m}\;\;\;\mbox{in $L^{1}(B)$ as $\epsilon\to 0$ uniformly in $m$}.

We see

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2\epsilon})}+\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B\backslash B_{2\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B_{3\epsilon})}+\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B\backslash B_{2\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon^{\eta}+\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B\backslash B_{2\epsilon})}

where
B_{2\epsilon}=\{x:\;|x|<2\epsilon\}
,
\eta=\Big[1-\frac{1}{p_{s}+1}\Big](N^{\prime}+\tau)>0
. Note that, for
q\in[1,p_{s}+1)
and
v\in D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{B_{3\epsilon}}|x|^{l}|v|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{B_{3\epsilon}}|x|^{l}|v|^{p_{s}+1}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}+1}}\Big(\int_{B_{3\epsilon}}|x|^{l}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta v)^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon^{(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s}+1})[N^{\prime}+\tau]}.

We also see that

\displaystyle\int_{B\backslash B_{2\epsilon}}|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}(x)-{\tilde{v}}_{m}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq 1}\rho(z)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B\backslash B_{2\epsilon}}\Big|\nabla{\tilde{v}}_{m}(x-\epsilon tz)\cdot\epsilon z\Big|dxdtdz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|\nabla{\tilde{v}}_{m}(z)|dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l-1}|{\tilde{u}}_{m}(x)dx+\epsilon\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l}|\nabla{\tilde{u}}_{m}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l}|{\tilde{u}}_{m}|^{p_{s}+1}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}+1}}\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l-\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}}\Big)^{\frac{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}}

\displaystyle+\epsilon\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{\alpha-2}|\nabla{\tilde{u}}_{m}(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l+2+\tau}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\Big(\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta{\tilde{u}}_{m}(x))^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l-\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}}\Big)^{\frac{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}}

\displaystyle+\epsilon\Big(\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta{\tilde{u}}_{m}(x))^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l+2+\tau}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\|{\tilde{u}}_{m}\|_{D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(B)}\Big[\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l-\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}}dx\Big)^{\frac{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}}+\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l+2+\tau}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big],

where we have used that the fact (see
[
14
,
15
,
24
]
)

(2.6)

\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha-2}|\nabla{\tilde{u}}_{m}|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta{\tilde{u}}_{m})^{2}dx,

where
C>0
is independent of
m
.
We consider two cases here: (i)
N^{\prime}+2\tau\geq 0
, (ii)
N^{\prime}+2\tau<0
.

For the case (i), we have that

\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l-\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}}dx\leq C,

\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l+2+\tau}dx\leq C,

where
C>0
is independent of
\epsilon
, and hence

\int_{B\backslash B_{2\epsilon}}|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}(x)-{\tilde{v}}_{m}(x)|dx\leq C\epsilon.

For the case (ii), we have that

\epsilon\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l-\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}}dx\Big)^{\frac{N^{\prime}+4+2\tau}{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{N^{\prime}+2\tau+4}{2}},

\epsilon\Big(\int_{B\backslash B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l+2+\tau}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{N^{\prime}+2\tau+4}{2}},

where
C>0
is independent of
\epsilon
, and hence

\int_{B\backslash B_{2\epsilon}}|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}(x)-{\tilde{v}}_{m}(x)|dx\leq 2C\epsilon^{\eta_{2}}\|{\tilde{u}}_{\epsilon}\|_{D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(B)},

where

\eta_{2}:=\frac{N^{\prime}+2\tau+4}{2}>0.

Therefore, our claim (
2.4
) holds.

Now fix
\sigma>0
, we claim that there exists a subsequence
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

of
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
such that

(2.7)

\lim_{i,j\to\infty}\sup\|{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}-{\tilde{v}}_{m_{j}}\|_{L^{1}(B)}\leq\sigma.

To show this, we choose
\epsilon>0
so small that

\|{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B)}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2},\;\;\;\mbox{for $m=1,2,\ldots$.}

Since
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is precompact in
L^{1}(B)
, there exists a subsequence
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}^{\epsilon}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m}^{\epsilon}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
that converges uniformly in
L^{1}(B)
. Therefore,
\lim_{i,j\to\infty}\sup\|{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m_{j}}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(B)}=0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B}|{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}-{\tilde{v}}_{m_{j}}|dx

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\leq\int_{B}|{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}-{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}^{\epsilon}|dx+\int_{B}|{\tilde{v}}_{m_{i}}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m_{j}}^{\epsilon}|dx+\int_{B}|{\tilde{v}}_{m_{j}}^{\epsilon}-{\tilde{v}}_{m_{j}}|dx

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\;\;\;\mbox{as $i,j\to\infty$}.

The claim (
2.7
) holds. We next employ assertion (
2.7
) with
\sigma=1,\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{i},\ldots
and a standard diagonal argument to extract a subsequence
\{{\tilde{v}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
\{{\tilde{v}}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
satisfying

\lim_{i,j\to\infty}\sup\|{\tilde{v}}_{i}-{\tilde{v}}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(B)}=0

and hence
{\tilde{v}}_{i}
converges to
{\tilde{v}}\in L^{1}(B)
as
i\to\infty
, i.e.,

(2.8)

\int_{B}||x|^{l}{\tilde{u}}_{i}-{\tilde{v}}|dx\to 0\;\;\mbox{as $i\to\infty$}.

Let
{\tilde{u}}=|x|^{-l}{\tilde{v}}
. We easily see from (
2.8
) that
{\tilde{u}}\in L_{l}^{1}(B)
and

(2.9)

\int_{B}|x|^{l}|{\tilde{u}}_{i}-{\tilde{u}}|dx\to 0\;\;\mbox{as $i\to\infty$}.

This also implies that
u_{i}
converges to
u
in
L_{l}^{1}(\Omega)
and the proof of step 1 is complete.

Step 2.
We show that if
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)
, there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
) such that
u_{m}
converges in
L^{q}_{l}(\Omega)
for
q\in(1,p_{s}+1)
.

We see that for
q\in(1,p_{s}+1)
, there exists
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{1}{q}=\lambda+\frac{1-\lambda}{p_{s}+1}
. Then for any
u_{m}\in D_{0}^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u_{m}|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u_{m}|dx\Big)^{\lambda}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u_{m}|^{p_{s}+1}dx\Big)^{\frac{1-\lambda}{p_{s}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u_{m}|dx\Big)^{\lambda}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}(\Delta u_{m})^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u_{m}|dx\Big)^{\lambda}.

It follows from the proof of step 1 that there is a subsequence

of
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
such that

as

.
Then

\Big(\int_{\Omega}||x|^{\frac{l}{q}}u_{i}-|x|^{\frac{l}{q}}u_{j}|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{l}|u_{i}-u_{j}|dx\Big)^{\lambda}\to 0\;\;\mbox{as $i,j\to\infty$}.

There exists
v\in L^{q}(\Omega)
such that

(2.11)

\int_{\Omega}||x|^{\frac{l}{q}}u_{i}-v|^{q}dx\to 0\;\;\mbox{as $i\to\infty$}.

Set

. We see from (
2.11

u\in L_{l}^{q}(\Omega)
and
u_{i}
converges to
u
in
L^{q}_{l}(\Omega)
as
i\to\infty
. This completes the proof of step 2 and the proof of this proposition. ∎

In this section, we obtain some removable singularity results for solutions to the equation (P).
Such results can be seen as regularity results of solutions to the equation (P), which have important applications in the boundary value problems.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
1<p<p_{s}
and (
1.1
) holds. Let
u(x)=u(|x|)\in H_{\alpha,\mbox{{\tiny rad}}}(B)
be a weak radial solution to the problem (Q) and
u>0
,
-|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u>0

in
B\backslash\{0\}
.
Then
u\in C^{4}(B\backslash\{0\})\cap C^{0}({\overline{B}})
,
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u\in C^{0}({\overline{B}})
.

Proof.
Define
v(r)=-r^{\alpha}\Delta u(r)
. We see that
(u,v)
satisfies the system of equations

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-(r^{N-1}u^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=r^{N-\alpha-1}v(r)&\mbox{in $(0,R)$},\\
-(r^{N-1}v^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=r^{N^{\prime}+\tau-1}u^{p}(r)&\mbox{in $(0,R)$},\\
u(R)=v(R)=0.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, we easily see from the equations in (
3.1
) that

r^{N-1}u^{\prime}(r)
and
r^{N-1}v^{\prime}(r)
are decreasing functions for
r\in(0,R)
and thus both

\lim_{r\to 0}r^{N-1}u^{\prime}(r)
and
\lim_{r\to 0}r^{N-1}v^{\prime}(r)
exist (maybe
\infty
). The facts
u\in H_{\alpha,\mbox{{\tiny rad}}}(B)
and the embedding
H_{\alpha,\mbox{{\tiny rad}}}(B)\hookrightarrow L^{q}_{l}(B)

for
1\leq q\leq p_{s}+1
imply that

(3.2)

\int_{B}|x|^{l}u^{p}dx<\infty,\;\;\;\int_{B}|x|^{-\alpha}v^{2}dx<\infty,

and

(3.3)

\int_{B}|x|^{-\alpha}|v|dx\leq\Big(\int_{B}|x|^{-\alpha}v^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{B}|x|^{-\alpha}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,

since
\alpha<N
. Therefore, for any
\epsilon>0
,

-\int_{B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{l}u^{p}dx=\int_{B_{\epsilon}}\Delta vdx=C\epsilon^{N-1}v^{\prime}(\epsilon),

-\int_{B_{\epsilon}}|x|^{-\alpha}vdx=\int_{B_{\epsilon}}\Delta udx=C\epsilon^{N-1}u^{\prime}(\epsilon).

These, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply
that
r^{N-1}u^{\prime}(r)\to 0
,
r^{N-1}v^{\prime}(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
. Integrations of the equations in (
3.1
) give that
u\in C^{2}(0,R)
,
v\in C^{2}(0,R)
.

To prove this lemma, we only need to show the following claim:

(3.4)

\lim_{r\to 0}u(r)=\tau_{u}\in(0,\infty),\;\;\;\lim_{r\to 0}v(r)=\tau_{v}\in(0,\infty),

where
\tau_{u}
and
\tau_{v}
are constants.

Since
u\in H_{\alpha,\mbox{{\tiny rad}}}(B)
and (
1.1
) holds, we have

(3.5)

\int_{0}^{R}r^{N^{\prime}-1-2\alpha}v^{2}(r)dr<\infty,

note that
\Delta u=-|x|^{-\alpha}v
.
We also obtain from the embedding:

H_{\alpha,\mbox{{\tiny rad}}}(B)\hookrightarrow L^{p_{s}+1}_{l}(B)

that

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{R}r^{N^{\prime}+\tau-1}u^{\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}-4}}(r)dr<\infty.

This implies that, for
r
near 0,

(3.7)

u(r)=o\Big(r^{-\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}}\Big),\;\;\;v(r)=o\Big(r^{-\frac{N-\alpha}{2}}\Big).

We only show
\eqref{3.6}_{1}
by using (
3.6
). The proof of
\eqref{3.6}_{2}
is similar to that of
\eqref{3.6}_{1}
by using (
3.5
). We easily see from (
3.6
) that

\int_{0}^{r}s^{N^{\prime}+\tau-1}u^{\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}-4}}(s)ds=o(1)\;\;\mbox{for $r$ near 0}.

Using the fact that
u(r)
is decreasing, we have that

\int_{0}^{r}s^{N^{\prime}+\tau-1}u^{\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}-4}}(s)ds\geq\frac{r^{N^{\prime}+\tau}}{N^{\prime}+\tau}u^{\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}-4}}(r).

This implies that

r^{N^{\prime}+\tau}u^{\frac{2(N^{\prime}+\tau)}{N^{\prime}-4}}(r)=o(1)

and

u(r)=o\Big(r^{-\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}}\Big).

Let

w(t)=r^{\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}}u(r),\;\;z(t)=r^{\frac{N-\alpha}{2}}v(r),\;\;\;t=-\ln r.

We see that
(w(t),z(t))
satisfies the problem

(3.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{tt}+(\alpha-2)w_{t}-\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}w+z=0,&t\in(-\ln R,\infty),\\
z_{tt}+(2-\alpha)z_{t}-\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}z+e^{-p_{*}t}w^{p}=0,&t\in(-\ln R,\infty),\\
w(-\ln R)=z(-\ln R)=0,\end{array}\right.

where and in the following

p_{*}=\frac{[N^{\prime}+4+2\tau]-[N^{\prime}-4]p}{2}.

Note that
p_{*}>0
provided
1<p<p_{s}
.
We know from (
3.7
) and
1<p<p_{s}
that
w(t)\to 0
,
z(t)\to 0
and
e^{-p_{*}t}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
It follows from the ODE theory on perturbation of linear systems (see
[
22
]
)
that there exist
\tau_{u}>0
,
\tau_{v}>0
such that

(3.9)

w(t)=(\tau_{u}+o(1))e^{-\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}t},\;\;\;z(t)=(\tau_{v}+o(1))e^{-\frac{N-\alpha}{2}t}\;\;\mbox{as $t\to\infty$}.

Note that the system (
3.8
) can be written to the following system

(3.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{w_{1}}=w_{2},\\
\dot{w_{2}}=\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}w_{1}-(\alpha-2)w_{2}-w_{3},\\
\dot{w_{3}}=w_{4},\\
\dot{w_{4}}=\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}w_{3}+(\alpha-2)w_{4}-e^{-p_{*}t}w_{1}^{p},\end{array}\right.

where
(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4})=(w,\dot{w},z,\dot{z})
. Since
(w_{1}(t),w_{2}(t),w_{3}(t),w_{4}(t))\to(0,0,0,0)
as
t\to\infty
, we linearize the system (
3.10
) at (0,0,0,0) and obtain the system

(3.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{h_{1}}=h_{2},\\
\dot{h_{2}}=\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}h_{1}-(\alpha-2)h_{2}-h_{3},\\
\dot{h_{3}}=h_{4},\\
\dot{h_{4}}=\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}h_{3}+(\alpha-2)h_{4}.\end{array}\right.

The matrix of (
3.11
) is

(3.12)

{\mathcal{A}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{llll}0&1&0&0\\
\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}&2-\alpha&-1&0\\
0&0&0&1\\
0&0&\frac{(N-\alpha)(N^{\prime}-4)}{4}&\alpha-2\end{array}\right).

By simple calculations, we see that the four eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
are:
\lambda_{1,2}=\mp\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}
,
\lambda_{3,4}=\mp\frac{N-\alpha}{2}
. We only choose
\lambda_{1}=-\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}

and
\lambda_{3}=-\frac{N-\alpha}{2}
, since
\lambda_{2}=\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}>0
and
\lambda_{4}=\frac{N-\alpha}{2}>0
which do not meet our requirement. We also see that
there are eigenvectors for
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
respectively:
e^{\lambda_{1}t}(1,-\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2},0,0)

and
e^{\lambda_{2}t}(0,0,1,-\frac{N-\alpha}{2})
.
These imply that (
3.9
) holds and hence our claim (
3.4
) holds. The proof of this lemma
is complete. ∎

(3.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta U=|y+\frac{x_{0}}{R}|^{-\alpha}V&\mbox{in $B_{1}$},\\
-\Delta V=|y+\frac{x_{0}}{R}|^{l}U^{p}&\mbox{in $B_{1}$}.\end{array}\right.

Note that
|y+\frac{x_{0}}{R}|\in[1,3]
for all

.

We now claim that there exists
C>0
depending only on
p,N^{\prime},\tau
, independent of
x_{0}
, such that

(3.15)

U^{\frac{p-1}{4}}(y)+V^{\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}}(y)\leq C(1+\mbox{dist}^{-1}(y,\partial B_{1})),\;\;y\in B_{1}.

Arguing by contradiction, we suppose that there exist sequences
x_{k}\in B_{\sigma/2}\backslash\{0\}
, points
z_{k}\in B_{1}
and
(U_{k},V_{k})

satisfying

(3.16)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta U_{k}=|y+\frac{x_{k}}{R_{k}}|^{-\alpha}V_{k}&\mbox{in $B_{1}$},\\
-\Delta V_{k}=|y+\frac{x_{k}}{R_{k}}|^{l}U_{k}^{p}&\mbox{in $B_{1}$},\end{array}\right.

where
R_{k}=\frac{1}{2}|x_{k}|
such that

By doubling lemma in
[
27
]
, there exists

M_{k}(y_{k})\geq M_{k}(z_{k}),\;\;M_{k}(y_{k})>2k\mbox{dist}^{-1}(y_{k},\partial B_{1}),

and

(3.17)

M_{k}(y)\leq 2M_{k}(y_{k}),\;\;\mbox{for all $y$ such that $|y-y_{k}|\leq kM_{k}^{-1}(y_{k})$}.

We have

(3.18)

\lambda_{k}:=M_{k}^{-1}(y_{k})\to 0\;\;\mbox{as $k\to\infty$},

due to
M_{k}(y_{k})\geq M_{k}(z_{k})>2k
.

Next we let

{\tilde{U}}_{k}(z)=\lambda_{k}^{\frac{4}{p-1}}U_{k}(y_{k}+\lambda_{k}z),\;\;{\tilde{V}}_{k}(z)=\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}}V_{k}(y_{k}+\lambda_{k}z).

Note that
|{\tilde{U}}_{k}|^{\frac{p-1}{4}}(0)+|{\tilde{V}}_{k}|^{\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}}(0)=1
,

(3.19)

\Big[|{\tilde{U}}_{k}|^{\frac{p-1}{4}}+|{\tilde{V}}_{k}|^{\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}}\Big](z)\leq 2,\;\;|z|\leq k,

due to (
3.17
) and we see that
({\tilde{U}}_{k},{\tilde{V}}_{k})
satisfies

(3.20)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta{\tilde{U}}_{k}=|y_{k}+\lambda_{k}z+\frac{x_{k}}{R_{k}}|^{-\alpha}{\tilde{V}}_{k},&|z|\leq k,\\
-\Delta{\tilde{V}}_{k}=|y_{k}+\lambda_{k}z+\frac{x_{k}}{R_{k}}|^{l}{\tilde{U}}_{k}^{p},&|z|\leq k.\end{array}\right.

Now, for each
A>0
and
1<q<\infty
, by (
3.19
), (
3.20
) and interior elliptic

estimates, the sequence
({\tilde{U}}_{k},{\tilde{V}}_{k})
is uniformly bounded in

. Note that

Using standard embeddings and interior elliptic Schauder estimates, after extracting subsequences
(still denoted by
\{y_{k}\}
,
\{\frac{x_{k}}{R_{k}}\}
and
\{({\tilde{U}}_{k},{\tilde{V}}_{k})\}

,

,

({\tilde{U}}_{k},{\tilde{V}}_{k})\to({\tilde{U}},{\tilde{V}})
in

. It follows that
({\tilde{U}},{\tilde{V}})\geq 0
is a classical solution of

(3.21)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta{\tilde{U}}=|y_{0}+{\tilde{x}}|^{-\alpha}{\tilde{V}},&z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta{\tilde{V}}=|y_{0}+{\tilde{x}}|^{l}{\tilde{U}}^{p},&z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

3.21

satisfies

(3.22)

where

and

). It also implies that

which completes the proof of step 1.

Step 2.

. We make the transform:

and

in

where

with

Note that

\Big(\frac{4+\tau}{p-1}-\frac{N^{\prime}-4}{2}\Big)(p-1)-p_{*}=0.

for

Proof of Theorem

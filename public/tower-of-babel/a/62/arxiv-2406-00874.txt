Uniqueness of extremal almost periodic states on the injective type III 1 \mathrm{III}_{1} factor
Michael Hartglass ∘, Brent Nelson ∙

Abstract
. Let R ∞ R_{\infty} denote the Araki–Woods factor—the unique separable injective type III 1 \mathrm{III}_{1} factor. For extremal almost periodic states φ , ψ ∈ ( R ∞ ) ∗ \varphi,\psi\in(R_{\infty})_{*} , we show that if Δ φ \Delta_{\varphi} and Δ ψ \Delta_{\psi} have the same point spectrum then ψ = φ ∘ α \psi=\varphi\circ\alpha for some α ∈ Aut ​ ( R ∞ ) \alpha\in\text{Aut}(R_{\infty}) . Consequently, the extremal almost periodic states on R ∞ R_{\infty} are parameterized by countable dense subgroups of ℝ + \mathbb{R}_{+} , up to precomposition by automorphisms. As an application, we show that KMS states for generalized gauge actions on Cuntz algebras agree (up to an automorphism) with tensor products of Powers states on their von Neumann completions.



Introduction

Using approximations by completely positive maps, Haagerup provided a new proof in
[
Haa89
]
of the uniqueness of the separable injective type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factors for
0<\lambda<1
. This uniqueness was first established by Connes in his celebrated work
[
Con76
]
, but a novel consequence of Haagerup’s proof was the uniqueness of their
\frac{2\pi}{\log(1/\lambda)}
-periodic states. For
0<\lambda\leq 1
denote

(1)

\displaystyle(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}):=\bigotimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\phi_{\lambda})

where

\phi_{\lambda}(x):=\text{Tr}\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{1+\lambda}&0\\
0&\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\end{array}\right)x\right].

These are separable injective factors of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for
0<\lambda<1
(also known as
Powers’ factors
having been first studied in
[
Pow67
]
), and type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
for
\lambda=1
. The associated states are
\frac{2\pi}{\log(1/\lambda)}
-periodic for
0<\lambda<1
, while
\tau:=\varphi_{1}
is of course the unique tracial state on
R:=R_{1}
. For
(N,\varphi)
a separable injective type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factor equipped with a
\frac{2\pi}{\log(1/\lambda)}
-periodic state and
0<\lambda<1
, Haagerup established a state-preserving isomorphism
(N,\varphi)\cong(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})
, and consequently any pair of
\frac{2\pi}{\log(1/\lambda)}
-periodic states on
R_{\lambda}
differ only by precomposition with an automorphism (see
[
Haa89
, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.5]
). Haagerup also established the uniqueness of the separable injective type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor in
[
Haa87
]
using a reduction due to Connes in
[
Con85
]
, and a second proof was published posthumously in
[
Haa16
]
that more closely followed the approach in
[
Haa89
]
. However, neither of these proofs provide a
state preserving
isomorphism and consequently the analogue of
[
Haa89
, Corollary 6.5]
has been missing in the type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
setting until now. In this article we prove such an analogue, where periodic states are replaced with so-called extremal almost periodic states.

In
[
Con74
]
, Connes defined an
almost periodic
weight as a faithful normal semifinite weight
\varphi
whose modular operator
\Delta_{\varphi}
is diagonalizable:

\Delta_{\varphi}=\sum_{\lambda\in\text{Sd}(\varphi)}\lambda 1_{\{\lambda\}}(\Delta_{\varphi}),

where we denote the point spectrum of
\Delta_{\varphi}
by
\text{Sd}(\varphi)
. An
extremal
weight is one whose centralizer subalgebra
M^{\varphi}:=\{x\in M\colon\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)=x\ \forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a factor. Almost periodic weights have the property that
(M^{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M=(M^{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M^{\varphi}
(see
[
Con72
, Theorem 10]
), and so extremal almost periodic weights only occur on factors. Additionally,
\text{Sd}(\varphi)
for an extremal almost periodic weight
\varphi
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the group of positive reals
(0,\infty)
under multiplication (see, for example,
[
Dyk97
, Lemma 4.9]
). The states
\varphi_{\lambda}
in (
1
) are extremal almost periodic: for
\lambda<1
this follows from
[
Con73
, Theorem 4.2.6]
; and
\tau
is tracial so that
\Delta_{\tau}=1
and
R^{\tau}=R
. Aperiodic examples of extremal almost periodic weights necessarily occur on type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factors, and, while not all type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factors admit such weights (see
[
Con74
, Corollary 5.3]
), the unique separable injective type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor
R_{\infty}
(also known as the
Araki–Woods factor
) has many. To see this, extend the notation in (
1
) to
\lambda>1
by letting
\phi_{\lambda}:=\phi_{1/\lambda}
and
(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}):=(R_{1/\lambda},\varphi_{1/\lambda})
. For a countable subgroup
\Lambda\leq\mathbb{R}_{+}
, denote

(2)

\displaystyle(R_{\Lambda},\varphi_{\Lambda}):=\bigotimes_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}).

If
\Lambda
is of the form
\lambda^{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
0<\lambda\leq 1
, then
(R_{\Lambda},\varphi_{\Lambda})\cong(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})
thanks to the property
(R_{\lambda}\bar{\otimes}R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}\otimes\varphi_{\lambda})=(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})
. Otherwise,
\Lambda
is dense in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
R_{\Lambda}\cong R_{\infty}
. Moreover,
\varphi_{\Lambda}
is an extremal almost periodic state (see, for example,
[
HI22
, Lemma 5.2.(i)]
). Our main result is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
N
be a diffuse separable injective factor equipped with an extremal almost periodic state
\varphi
. Then

(N,\varphi)\cong(R_{\Lambda},\varphi_{\Lambda})

where
\Lambda=\text{Sd}(\varphi)
.

If
\Lambda=\lambda^{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
0<\lambda\leq 1
then the above is simply either
[
Haa89
, Theorem 6.1]
for
\lambda<1
or
[
Con76
, Theorem 1]
for
\lambda=1
. Thus the novelty of the above theorem lies only in the case that
\Lambda
is dense in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, but nevertheless our proof simultaneously covers all cases. The proof of Theorem
A
very closely follows Haagerup’s proof of
[
Haa89
, Theorem 6.1]
and can be found in Section
5
. Roughly, the strategy is to approximate a countable generating set for
N
by a sequence of finite dimensional subfactors that when equipped with the restriction of
\varphi
are isomorphic to a finite dimensional tensor factor of
(R_{\Lambda},\varphi_{\Lambda})
. These subfactors are constructed using approximate completely positive factorizations of the identity map through matrix algebras, whose existence is established in Section
3
(paralleling
[
Haa89
, Section 5]
). Notably, Lemma
3.1
marks a technical deviation from Haagerup’s ideas, though it still essentially corresponds to
[
Haa89
, Lemma 5.3]
.
These completely positive maps also respect the modular theory of
\varphi
, a property which is needed to apply the results in Section
4
(paralleling
[
Haa89
, Section 4]
). These results, namely Theorem
4.4
, approximate the completely positive maps by
x\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{*}xa_{n}
for
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset N^{\varphi}
a partition of unity, which in turn allows one to further approximate the maps by an inner automorphism using the notion of
\delta
-related tuples from
[
Haa89
, Section 2]
. Despite how similar our results are to those in
[
Haa89
]
, working with non-cyclic subgroups of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
almost always prevents us from simply citing the corresponding results. So instead the details are presented in full and the reader should find the paper mostly self-contained.

We also remark that if one could directly argue that a separable injective type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor admits at least one extremal almost periodic state, then Theorem
A
would yield a new proof of the uniqueness of such factors. However, this remains out of reach. In fact, Haagerup’s approach in
[
Haa87
]
specifically avoids this by working instead with dominant weights, which are known to always exist for properly infinite factors by
[
CT77
, Chapter II]
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
A
we have the following.

Corollary B
.

If
\varphi
and
\psi
are extremal almost periodic states on
R_{\infty}
with
\text{Sd}(\varphi)=\text{Sd}(\psi)
, then there exists
\alpha\in\text{Aut}(R_{\infty})
satisfying
\psi=\varphi\circ\alpha
.

This corollary combined with the existence of the extremal almost periodic states
\varphi_{\Lambda}
in (
2
) implies there is a one-to-one correspondence between the collection of countable dense subgroups of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and the equivalence classes of extremal almost periodic states on
R_{\infty}
under precomposition by automorphisms.

Due to its uniqueness,
R_{\infty}
arises from a variety of constructions, and so our results can be used to recognize extremal almost periodic states on these constructions as some
\varphi_{\Lambda}
. Our last main result is an example of this involving the Cuntz algebra.
If
J
is any finite or countably infinite set, then recall that the Cuntz algebra,
\mathcal{O}_{J}
, is the universal C*-algebra generated by a family of isometries
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in J}
with mutually orthogonal ranges, and if
J
is finite it is further required that
\sum_{j\in J}v_{j}v_{j}^{*}=1
. Let
\mu\colon J\to(0,1)
be a function with the property that
\sum_{j\in J}\mu(j)=1
. Define a gauge action
\mathbb{R}\overset{\sigma}{\curvearrowright}\mathcal{O}_{J}
by

\sigma_{t}(v_{j})=\mu(j)^{it}v_{j},

for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
j\in J
. It is known that a unique KMS state
\varphi
exists for such an action (see, for example,
[
Tho17
]
). Letting
\pi_{\varphi}
be its GNS representation, we will abuse notation slightly and let
\varphi
also denote its extension to
\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime}
as a faithful normal state. Denote by
\Lambda
the multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
generated by
\mu(J)
. It has been shown by Izumi for
J
finite
[
Izu93
]
and Thomsen for
J
infinite
[
Tho19
]
that
\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime}
is a separable injective factor of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
if
\Lambda=\lambda^{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
and of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
otherwise. In the former case,
[
Haa89
, Theorem 6.1]
implies
(\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime},\varphi)\cong(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})
since
\varphi
is necessarily
\frac{2\pi}{\log(1/\lambda)}
-periodic.
In the general case we argue in Section
6
that Theorem
A
can be applied to obtain the following.

Theorem C
.

With the above notation,
\varphi
is an extremal almost periodic state on
\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime}
and there is a state preserving isomorphism
(\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime},\varphi)\cong(R_{\Lambda},\varphi_{\Lambda})
.

1. Preliminaries

We say a von Neumann algebra
M
is
separable
if it is separable in the weak* topology induced by
M\cong(M_{*})^{*}
; equivalently, if
M_{*}
is separable as a Banach space. We write
N\leq M
when
N
is a unital von Neumann subalgebra of
M
. For a family of subalgebras
\{N_{i}\leq M\colon i\in I\}
, we write
\bigvee_{i\in I}N_{i}
for the von Neumann algebra generated by
\bigcup_{i\in I}N_{i}
. For a state
\varphi
on
M
we define the inner product

\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\varphi(y^{*}x)\qquad x,y\in M,

and denote its associated norm by
\|x\|_{\varphi}
.

We assume the reader has some familiarity with weights and modular theory, but the complete details can be found in
[
Tak03
]
(see also
[
GLN24
, Section 1]
). For a faithful normal semifinite weight on
M
, its
modular automorphism group
refers to the action
\mathbb{R}\overset{\sigma^{\varphi}}{\curvearrowright}M
defined by

\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x):=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}x\Delta_{\varphi}^{-it}.

We say a subalgebra
N\leq M
is
\varphi
-invariant
if
\varphi|_{N}
is semifinite and
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(N)=N
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which is equivalent to the existence of a faithful normal
\varphi
-invariant conditional expectation from
M
onto
N
(see
[
Tak03
, Theorem IX.4.2]
). For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we denote

M^{(\varphi,\lambda)}:=\{x\in M\colon\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)=\lambda^{it}x\ \forall t\in\mathbb{R}\},

so that
M^{(\varphi,1)}=M^{\varphi}
. We call such elements
eigenoperators
of
\varphi
. We also denote

M^{(\varphi,\text{eig})}:=\text{span}\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}M^{(\varphi,\lambda)}\right),

which is a unital
*
-subalgebra of
M
, and it is weak* dense when
\varphi
is almost periodic (see
[
GLN24
, Lemma 1.4]
).

Given an almost periodic weight
\varphi
on
M
, let
\Lambda\leq\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the group generated by
\text{Sd}(\varphi)
. Equipping
\Lambda
with the discrete topology, let
G
denote its compact dual group. Identifying the dual of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\mathbb{R}
via the pairing
(s,t)=s^{it}
, the
transpose
of the inclusion map
\iota\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
gives a continuous group homomorphsim
\hat{\iota}\colon\mathbb{R}\to G
determined by

(\hat{\iota}(t)|\lambda)=(\iota(\lambda)|t)=\lambda^{it}.

The action
\mathbb{R}\overset{\sigma^{\varphi}}{\curvearrowright}M
given by the modular automorphism group of
\varphi
determines an action
G\overset{\alpha}{\curvearrowright}M
satisfying
\alpha_{\hat{\iota}(t)}=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
M^{\alpha}=M^{\varphi}
and more generally for
x\in M^{(\varphi,\lambda)}
one has
\alpha_{s}(x)=(\lambda|s)x
for all
s\in G
. We call this action of
G
the
point modular extension
of
\mathbb{R}\overset{\sigma^{\varphi}}{\curvearrowright}M
. For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, there is a normal
M^{\varphi}
-
M^{\varphi}
-bimodular idempotent map
\mathcal{E}_{(\varphi,\lambda)}\colon M\to M^{(\varphi,\lambda)}
defined by

\mathcal{E}_{(\varphi,\lambda)}(x):=\int_{G}\overline{(\lambda|s)}\alpha_{s}(x)\ d\mu(s),

where
\mu
is a normalized Haar measure on
G
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}:=\mathcal{E}_{(\varphi,1)}
is the unique
\varphi
-preserving faithful normal conditional expectation onto the centralizer. If
\varphi
is a state, then one also has

\|x\|_{\varphi}^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|\mathcal{E}_{(\varphi,\lambda)}(x)\|_{\varphi}^{2},

for all
x\in M
since
\Delta_{\varphi}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}1_{\{\lambda\}}(\Delta_{\varphi})
. See
[
Dyk95
,
Dyk97
]
for additional details.

Suppose that
F
is a finite-dimensional subfactor of a factor
N
, and
F^{c}
is defined by
F^{c}=F^{\prime}\cap N
. Note that this means
N\cong F\otimes F^{c}
. For a faithful normal state
\varphi
on
N
, the following three conditions are equivalent (see, for example,
[
Haa89
, Section 4]
):

(1)

F
is
\Delta_{\varphi}
-invariant;

(2)

there exists a
\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation from
N
onto
F
;

(3)

(N,\varphi)\cong(F,\varphi)\otimes(F^{c},\varphi)
.

For the purposes of this article, condition (3) above will be the one which is easiest to exploit. We will denote this more compactly as
\varphi=\varphi|_{F}\otimes\varphi|_{F^{c}}
.

2. Extremal almost periodic weights

In this section we present a few ways to construct new extremal almost periodic weights out of old ones. We will only need to apply them to
states
in the present article, but we prove them in the generality of weights whenever possible because they may be of independent interest. The following lemma is proven in
[
HI22
, Lemma 5.2.(ii)]
in the case of states and for
M=R_{\infty}
the Araki–Woods factor. However, the proof in the generality below is exactly the same.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with an extremal almost periodic weight. If
(N,\psi)
is a factor equipped with an almost periodic weight satisfying
\text{Sd}(\psi)\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi)
, then
\varphi\otimes\psi
is extremal almost periodic on
M\bar{\otimes}N
with
\text{Sd}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\text{Sd}(\varphi)
.

The following can be viewed as a partial converse to the previous lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M=P\bar{\otimes}N
be factor with
P
a type
\mathrm{I}
factor. Suppose
\varphi
is an extremal almost periodic weight on
M
satisfying:
\varphi|_{P}
and
\varphi|_{N}
are seminifinite;
\varphi|_{P}
is almost periodic; and
\varphi=\varphi|_{P}\otimes\varphi|_{N}
. Then
\varphi|_{N}
is extremal almost periodic with
\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{N})=\text{Sd}(\varphi)
. In particular,
\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{P})\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{N})
.

Proof.

Identifying
P\cong B(\mathcal{H})
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we have that
\varphi|_{P}(x)=\text{Tr}(h^{\frac{1}{2}}xh^{\frac{1}{2}})
for some positive (unbounded) operator
h
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi|_{P}}(x)=h^{it}xh^{-it}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\varphi|_{P}
is almost periodic,
h
is necessarily diagonalizable with respect to some family of matrix units
\{e_{i,j}\in B(\mathcal{H})\colon i,j\in I\}
, In particular one has
e_{i,i}\otimes 1\in M^{\varphi}
for all
i\in I
. Because
(N,\varphi|_{N})
can be identified with
((e_{i,i}\otimes 1)M(e_{i,i}\otimes 1),\frac{1}{\text{Tr}(he_{i,i})}\varphi)
for any
i\in I
, it follows from
[
GLN24
, Remark 1.5]
that
\varphi|_{N}
is extremal almost periodic.

Next, we see that
e_{i,j}
is an eigenoperator of
\varphi|_{P}
for each
i,j\in I
since
h
is diagonalized with respect to these matrix units, say with eigenvalue
\lambda_{i,j}
. Note that
\lambda_{j,i}=1/\lambda_{i,j}
. Using that
M^{\varphi}
is a factor we have
e_{i,i}M^{\varphi}e_{j,j}=e_{i,j}\otimes N
is non-zero for each
i,j\in I
. Hence there is a non-zero
x\in N
with
e_{i,j}\otimes x\in M^{\varphi}
, and the factorization
\varphi=\varphi|_{P}\otimes\varphi|_{N}
implies
x\in N^{(\varphi|_{N},1/\lambda_{i,j})}=N^{(\varphi|_{N},\lambda_{j,i})}
. Consequently,

\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{P})=\{\lambda_{i,j}\colon i,j\in I\}\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{N}).

Since
\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{N})
is a group and
\text{Sd}(\varphi)=\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{P})\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{N})
, it follows that
\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{N})=\text{Sd}(\varphi)
as claimed.
∎

Observe that if
\varphi
in previous lemma is a state, then of course its restrictions to
P
and
N
are automatically semifinite, but also
\varphi|_{P}=\text{Tr}(h^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\cdot\,h^{\frac{1}{2}})
is automatically almost periodic since
h
is necessarily trace class and hence diagonalizable.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be a factor with separable predual equipped with an extremal almost periodic weight
\varphi
, let
\{p_{i}\in M^{\varphi}\colon i\in I\}
be a family of projections satisfying
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
, and let
\{\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\colon i\in I\}
satisfy
\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}\in\text{Sd}(\varphi)
for all
i,j\in I
. Set

h:=\sum_{i\in I}\mu_{i}p_{i}.

Then
\psi:=\varphi(h^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\cdot\,h^{\frac{1}{2}})
is an extremal almost periodic weight with
\text{Sd}(\psi)=\text{Sd}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

The weight
\psi
is faithful since
h
is non-singular, and since
\varphi
is strictly semifinite and
p_{i}M^{\varphi}p_{i}\subset M^{\psi}
it follows that
\psi
is also strictly semifinite. Observe that
M^{(\psi,\text{eig})}
contains
\text{span}\{p_{i}M^{(\varphi,\text{eig})}p_{j}\colon i,j\in I\}
and is therefore weak* dense. Consequently,
\psi
is almost periodic by
[
GLN24
, Lemma 1.4]
.

We next compute
\text{Sd}(\psi)
. Denote
\Lambda:=\text{Sd}(\varphi)
, which is a group by virtue of
\varphi
being extremal. Fix
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
i\in I
and note that there exists a partial isometry
v\in M^{(\varphi,\lambda)}
. Since
v^{*}v
and
p_{i}
are not centrally orthogonal in
M^{\varphi}
, we can find a non-zero partial isometry
w\in M^{\varphi}
satisfying
w^{*}w\leq p_{i}
and
ww^{*}\leq v^{*}v
. Hence
v^{*}vwp_{i}=w
, and in particular
vwp_{i}
is a non-zero partial isometry. Since
vwp_{i}w^{*}v^{*}
and
p_{i}
are also not centrally orthogonal in
M^{\varphi}
, we can find another partial isometry
u\in M^{\varphi}
satisfying
u^{*}u\leq vwp_{i}w^{*}v^{*}
and
uu^{*}\leq p_{i}
. Hence
p_{i}uvwp_{i}w^{*}v^{*}=u
, and in particular
p_{i}uvwp_{i}\neq 0
. Also, the Connes cocycle derivative theorem implies

\sigma_{t}^{\psi}(p_{i}uvwp_{i})=h^{it}\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(p_{i}uvwp_{i})h^{-it}=\mu_{i}^{it}p_{i}u\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(v)wp_{i}\mu_{i}^{-it}=\lambda^{it}p_{i}uvwp_{i}.

Hence
\Lambda\subset\text{Sd}(\psi)
. The reverse inclusion follows from
\text{span}\{p_{i}M^{(\varphi,\text{eig})}p_{j}\colon i,j\in I\}
being weakly dense:
\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}\in\Lambda
implies
p_{i}M^{(\varphi,\lambda)}p_{j}\in\bigcup_{\nu\in\Lambda}M^{(\psi,\nu)}
.

Finally, we establish extremality. By replacing
\psi
with
\frac{1}{\mu_{i_{0}}}\psi
for some fixed
i_{0}\in I
, we may assume
\mu_{i}\in\Lambda
for all
i\in I
. Let
G
denote the compact dual to
\Lambda
(which we give the discrete topology) and let
G\overset{\alpha}{\curvearrowright}M
and
G\overset{\beta}{\curvearrowright}M
be the point modular extensions of
\mathbb{R}\overset{\sigma^{\varphi}}{\curvearrowright}M
and
\mathbb{R}\overset{\sigma^{\psi}}{\curvearrowright}M
, respectively. Consider the strongly continuous unitary representation

G\ni s\mapsto u_{s}:=\sum_{i\in I}(s|\mu_{i})p_{i}\in M^{\varphi}.

Then for
x\in M^{(\varphi,\lambda)}
and
i,j\in I
we have
\beta_{s}(p_{i}xp_{j})=(s|\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}\lambda)p_{i}xp_{j}
and

u_{s}\alpha_{s}(p_{i}xp_{j})u_{s}^{*}=(s|\mu_{i})p_{i}\alpha_{s}(x)p_{j}(s|\mu_{j})^{-1}=(s|\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}\lambda)p_{i}xp_{j}.

It follows that
\beta_{s}(x)=u_{s}\alpha_{s}(x)u_{s}^{*}
for all
x\in M
and hence
\alpha
and
\beta
are cocycle equivalent. Consequently,
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G\cong M\rtimes_{\beta}G
by
[
Tak03
, Theorem X.1.7.(ii)]
. By
[
GLN24
, Lemma 2.27]
, the former is a factor since
M^{\alpha}=M^{\varphi}
is a factor. Thus
M\rtimes_{\beta}G
is a factor and we saw above that
\text{Sd}(\psi)=\Lambda
, so the same lemma implies
M^{\beta}=M^{\psi}
is a factor.
∎

The next lemma is a generalization of
[
Haa89
, Lemma 5.4]
) and will be needed in Section
3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
M
be a diffuse factor equipped with an extremal almost periodic state
\varphi
, and let
(R,\tau)
be the unique separable injective type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor equipped with its unique tracial state. For
h
diagonlizable and affiliated with
R
, suppose
\psi=\tau(h^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\cdot\,h^{\frac{1}{2}})
is an almost periodic state on
R
with

\displaystyle=\varphi,

\displaystyle\varphi\circ T

Lemma 4.1

a:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}T(e_{i,j})\otimes e_{i,j}

is a positive operator in
N\otimes F
, so we can define
b:=a^{1/2}
. Then
b
is of the form

b=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{i,j}\otimes e_{i,j}

for
b_{i,j}\in N
and

T(e_{i,j})=(1\otimes\text{Tr})(a(1\otimes e_{j,i}))=(1\otimes\text{Tr})(b^{*}b(1\otimes e_{j,i}))=\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k,i}^{*}b_{k,j}.

If we set
c_{i,j}:=\sum_{k=1}^{d}e_{k,j}b_{i,k}
for each
1\leq i,j\leq d
, then it follows that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}c_{i,j}^{*}e_{k,\ell}c_{i,j}=\sum_{i,j,r,s=1}^{d}b_{i,r}^{*}e_{j,r}e_{k,\ell}e_{s,j}b_{i,s}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{i,r}^{*}e_{j,j}b_{i,s}=T(e_{k,\ell}).

Therefore

T(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}c_{i,j}^{*}xc_{i,j}

for all
x\in F
. Next we will replace the
c_{i,j}
with elements of the centralizer. Define a new state on
F
by
\psi:=\text{Tr}(h^{-1}\,\cdot\,)
. Then using
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}\circ T=T\circ\sigma_{t}^{\varphi|_{F}}
we have

\sigma_{t}^{\varphi\otimes\psi}(a)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(T(e_{i,j}))\otimes\sigma_{t}^{\psi}(e_{i,j})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}})T(e_{i,j})\otimes\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{i}}e_{i,j}=a.

Hence
a\in(N\otimes F)^{\varphi\otimes\psi}
and therefore
b
lies in this centralizer as well. Running a similar computation as above on the expanded formula for
b
yields
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(b_{i,j})=\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}b_{i,j}
for all
i,j=1,\ldots,d
, and therefore

\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(c_{i,j})=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{j}}e_{k,j}\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{k}}b_{i,k}=\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}c_{i,j}

for all
i,j=1,\ldots,d
.

Next, observe that
\varphi|_{F^{c}}
is extremal almost periodic with
\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{F})\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{F^{c}})
by Lemma
2.2
. We claim that for each
\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}\in\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{F})
there exists a finite set
\mathcal{V}_{i,j}\subset(F^{c})^{(\varphi|_{F^{c}},\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}})}
satisfying

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{i,j}}vv^{*}=1.

Indeed, we first use
[
GLN24
, Lemma 2.1]
to a find a (potentially infinite) family
\mathcal{W}\subset(F^{c})^{(\varphi|_{F^{c}},\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}})}
satisfying

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{W}}vv^{*}=1.

For
\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}\geq 1
, this same lemma implies we can chose
\mathcal{V}_{i,j}=\mathcal{W}
of size one. Thus it suffices to consider the case
\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}<1
. For some
m\in\mathbb{N}
we can partition
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathcal{W}_{m}
so that

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{W}_{k}}\varphi(vv^{*})\leq\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}

for each
k=1,\ldots,m
. Consequently,

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{W}_{k}}\varphi(v^{*}v)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{W}_{k}}\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{i}}\varphi(vv^{*})\leq 1,

and so by precomposing the
v\in\mathcal{W}_{k}
with suitable partial isometries in
(F^{c})^{\varphi|_{F^{c}}}
we can assume the range and source projections are pairwise orthogonal over
\mathcal{W}_{k}
(this uses the factoriality of
(F^{c})^{\varphi|_{F^{c}}}
). Setting
v_{k}:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{W}_{k}}v
for each
k=1,\ldots,m
, the desired finite set is then
\mathcal{V}_{i,j}:=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
.

Now,
v^{*}c_{i,j}\in N^{\varphi}
for each
1\leq i,j\leq d
and
v\in\mathcal{V}_{i,j}
, and since
F^{c}
commutes with
F
inside
N
we have

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{i,j}}(v^{*}c_{i,j})^{*}x(v^{*}c_{i,j})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{i,j}}c_{i,j}^{*}xvv^{*}c_{i,j}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}c_{i,j}^{*}xc_{i,j}=T(x)

for each
x\in F
. Thus
\{v^{*}c_{i,j}\colon 1\leq i,j\leq d,\ v\in\mathcal{V}_{i,j}\}
is the desired finite set in
N^{\varphi}
.
∎

The next pair of lemmas are relative Dixmier norm approximation results. These relate to
[
Haa89
, Corollary 3.4 and Lemma 4.5]
, respectively, but are necessarily weaker in the case that
\text{Sd}(\varphi)
is dense in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(see the proof of
[
Haa89
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
N
be a diffuse factor with an extremal almost periodic state
\varphi
. For any finite set
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in N^{(\varphi,\text{eig})}
and
\epsilon>0
there exists
\alpha\in\conv\{\text{Ad}\left(u\right)\colon u\in N^{\varphi}\}
satisfying

\|\alpha(x_{k})-\varphi(x_{k})\|<\epsilon

for each
k=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

We first observe that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x\in N^{(\varphi,\lambda)}
one has

\varphi(x)\in\overline{\conv\{u^{*}xu\,|\,u\in U(N_{\varphi)}\}}^{\|\cdot\|}.

Indeed, for
\lambda=1
this is simply the Dixmier approximation theorem for type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factors (see
[
Dix81
, Section III.5.1]
), and for
\lambda\neq 1
this follows from the same argument as in the proof of
[
Haa89
, Lemma 3.2]
. (Note that
N^{\varphi}
is indeed a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor by
[
GLN24
, Lemma 3.1]
.)

Now, let
E\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi)
be the necessarily finite set such that
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\text{span}\{N^{(\varphi,\lambda)}\colon\lambda\in E\}
, and for each
k=1,\ldots,n
write
x_{k}=\sum_{\lambda\in E}x_{k,\lambda}
where
x_{k,\lambda}\in N^{(\varphi,\lambda)}\cup\{0\}
. Iteratively applying our initial observation through some enumeration of
\{1,\ldots,n\}\times E
yields
\alpha\in\conv\{\text{Ad}\left(u\right)\colon u\in N^{\varphi}\}
satisfying

\|\alpha(x_{k,\lambda})-\varphi(x_{k,\lambda})\|<\frac{\epsilon}{|E|}

for each
k=1,\ldots,n
and
\lambda\in E
, and so the claimed inequality for
x_{k}
follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
N
be a diffuse factor equipped with an extremal almost periodic state
\varphi
, and let
F\leq N
be a finite dimensional subfactor for which

\varphi=\varphi|_{F}\otimes\varphi|_{F^{c}}.

If
\mathcal{E}_{F}\colon N\to F
is the
\varphi
-invariant conditional expectation, then for all
x\in N^{(\varphi,\text{eig})}
and
\epsilon>0
there exists
\alpha\in\conv\{\text{Ad}\left(u\right)\colon u\in\mathcal{U}(F^{c}\cap N^{\varphi})\}
satisfying

\|\alpha(x)-\mathcal{E}_{F}(x)\|<\epsilon.

Proof.

For
x\in N^{(\varphi,\text{eig})}
write

x=\sum_{\lambda\in E}x_{\lambda},

where
x_{\lambda}\in N^{(\varphi,\lambda)}
for each
\lambda
in a finite subset
E\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi)
. Fix a family of matrix units
\{e_{i,j}\colon 1\leq i,j\leq d\}
for
F
consisting of eigenoperators for
\sigma^{\varphi|_{F}}
. In fact,
\varphi=\varphi|_{F}\otimes\varphi|_{F^{c}}
implies they will also be eigenoperators for
\sigma^{\varphi}
, say
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(e_{i,j})=\lambda_{i,j}^{it}e_{i,j}
for
\lambda_{i,j}\in\text{Sd}(\varphi)
. For each
\lambda\in E
write

x_{\lambda}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}e_{i,j}x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)},

where
x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)}\in F^{c}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
. Our choice of matrix units and
x_{\lambda}\in N^{(\varphi,\lambda)}
implies
x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)}\in N^{(\varphi,\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{i,j}})}
for each
1\leq i,j\leq d
. Also, the tensor decomposition of
\varphi
implies

\mathcal{E}_{F}(x_{\lambda})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}e_{i,j}\varphi(x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)}).

Lemma
2.2
tells us that
\varphi|_{F^{c}}
is extremal almost periodic, and so we can apply Lemma
4.2
to
\{x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)}\colon\lambda\in E,\ 1\leq i,j\leq d\}\subset F^{c}
to find
\alpha\in\conv\{\text{Ad}\left(u\right)\colon u\in\mathcal{U}(F^{c}\cap N^{\varphi})\}
satisfying

\|\alpha(x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)})-\varphi(x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)})\|<\frac{\epsilon}{d^{2}|E|}.

(Note that
F^{c}\cap N^{\varphi}=(F^{c})^{\varphi|_{F^{c}}}
.) Since
F^{c}
commutes with
F
we have

\|\alpha(x)-\mathcal{E}_{F}(x)\|=\left\|\sum_{\lambda\in E}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}e_{i,j}\left[\alpha(x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)})-\varphi(x_{i,j}^{(\lambda)})\right]\right\|<\epsilon,

as claimed.
∎

The following theorem is our main result in this section and corresponds to
[
Haa89
, Theorem 4.1]
. Recall that Lemma
4.1
gave an exact Kraus representation of the completely positive map
T(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{*}xa_{j}
for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in N^{\varphi}
. Since
T
is unital, we in particular have that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{*}a_{j}=1
. However,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}a_{j}^{*}=1
may fail to hold, and it will be needed in the proof of Theorem
A
to apply
[
Haa89
, Theorem 2.3]
. In the following theorem, we obtain this latter relation by sacrificing our exact finite Kraus representation for an infinite approximate Kraus representation.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
N
be a diffuse factor equipped with an extremal almost periodic state
\varphi
, and let
F\leq N
be a finite dimensional subfactor for which

\varphi=\varphi|_{F}\otimes\varphi|_{F^{c}}

Suppose
T\colon F\to N
is a normal unital completely positive map satisfying
\varphi\circ T=\varphi|_{F}
and
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}\circ T=T\circ\sigma_{t}^{\varphi|_{F}}
. Then for every
\delta>0
there exists a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset N^{\varphi}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{*}a_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}a_{n}^{*}=1

and

\left\|T(x)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{*}xa_{n}\right\|\leq\delta\|x\|

for all
x\in F
.

Proof.

We first apply Lemma
4.1
to obtain
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in N^{\varphi}
so that

T(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}xb_{i},

for all
x\in F
. In particular, this gives

\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}=T(1)=1.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{F}\colon N\to F
to be
\sigma^{\varphi}
-preserving conditional expectation. Using
\varphi\circ T=\varphi|_{F}
and
b_{i}\in N^{\varphi}
, we have for
x\in F

\left\langle\mathcal{E}_{F}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}b_{i}^{*}\right),x\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}b_{i}^{*},x\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\left\langle 1,\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}^{*}xb_{i}\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\left\langle 1,T(x)\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\left\langle 1,x\right\rangle_{\varphi}.

Hence
\mathcal{E}_{F}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}b_{i}^{*})=1
.

Now, apply Lemma
4.3
to
\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}b_{i}^{*}
to find
\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}\text{Ad}\left(u_{j}\right)\in\conv\{\text{Ad}\left(u\right)\colon u\in\mathcal{U}(F^{c}\cap N^{\varphi})\}
satisfying

\left\|\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}b_{i}^{*}\right)-1\right\|<\frac{\delta}{2}.

Set
c_{i,j}:=t_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{j}b_{i}
. Then the above inequality gives

(5)

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{i,j}c_{i,j}^{*}-1\|<\frac{\delta}{2}.

We also have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{i,j}^{*}c_{i,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}=1,

and for
x\in F
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{i,j}^{*}xc_{i,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}xb_{i}=T(x).

Reindex the operators
c_{i,j}
as
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and then set

a_{i}:=\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}c_{i}.

for each
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{*}a_{i}=1-\frac{\delta}{2}<1,

and (
5
) implies

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{i}^{*}=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{i}^{*}-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\right]+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)<\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)+1\right)<1.

Recalling that
N^{\varphi}
is a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor by
[
GLN24
, Lemma 3.1]
, we can use
[
Haa85
, Lemma 5.1]
to find
\{a_{i}\in N^{\varphi}\colon i\geq m+1\}
so that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{*}a_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}a_{i}^{*}=1.

Note that
\sum_{i\geq m+1}a_{i}^{*}a_{i}=\frac{\delta}{2}
, and consequently

S(x):=\sum_{i=m+1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{*}xa_{i}

is a completely positive map with
\|S\|=\|S(1)\|=\frac{\delta}{2}
. Thus
\|S(x)\|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\|x\|
, and recall that
\|T(x)\|\leq\|x\|
since
\|T\|=\|T(1)\|=1
. Hence

\left\|T(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{*}xa_{i}\right\|=\left\|T(x)-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)T(x)-S(x)\right\|=\left\|\frac{\delta}{2}T(x)-S(x)\right\|\leq\delta\|x\|

for all
x\in F
.
∎

, we have

\varphi|_{F}\otimes\varphi|_{F^{c}}=(\varphi|_{G}\otimes\varphi|_{G^{c}})\circ(\text{Ad}\left(w^{*}\right)\otimes\text{Ad}\left(w^{*}\right))=\varphi|_{G}\otimes\varphi|_{G^{c}}=\varphi,

so that (i) holds. Also

(F,\varphi|_{F})\cong(G,\varphi|_{G})\cong(\mathbb{M}_{E}^{\otimes m},\phi_{E}^{\otimes m}),

for some finite set
E_{0}\subset E\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
so that (ii) and hence Claim 3 holds.

We now begin the proof of our main theorem in earnest. Using separability of
N
we write a countable generating set
\{x_{n}\in N\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
for
N
. Also enumerate
\text{Sd}(\varphi)\cap(0,1)=\{\lambda_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. We will inductively construct a sequence of commuting finite dimensional subfactors
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
N
and a sequence of finite subsets
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\text{Sd}(\varphi)
satisfying:

(a)

\inf\{\|x_{k}-y\|_{\varphi}\colon y\in F_{1}\vee\cdots\vee F_{n}\}<\frac{1}{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and each
k=1,\ldots,n
;

(b)

\varphi=\varphi|_{F_{n}}\otimes\varphi|_{F_{n}^{c}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(c)

\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}\subset E_{n}\subset E_{n+1}
;

(d)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
so that

(F_{n},\varphi|_{F_{n}})\cong(\mathbb{M}_{E_{n}}^{\otimes m_{n}},\phi_{E_{n}}^{\otimes m_{n}}).

F_{1}
and
E_{1}
are obtained by applying Claim 3 to
x_{1}
,
E_{0}=\{1\}
, and
\epsilon=1
. Suppose we have constructed
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
as above. Set

F:=F_{1}\vee\cdots\vee F_{n}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}.

Then
F
is
\sigma^{\varphi}
-invariant since (b) implies this is true for each
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
, and hence
\varphi=\varphi|_{F}\otimes\varphi|_{F^{c}}
. Fix a family of matrix units
\{e_{i,j}\in F\colon 1\leq i,j\leq d\}
so that for each
k=1,\ldots,n+1

x_{k}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}e_{i,j}x^{(k)}_{i,j}

where
x^{(k)}_{i,j}\in F^{c}
. Set

K:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\|e_{i,j}\|_{\varphi}.

Recall that
\varphi|_{F^{c}}
is extremal almost periodic with
\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{F^{c}})=\text{Sd}(\varphi)
by Lemma
2.2
, so we can apply Claim 3 to
(F^{c},\varphi|_{F^{c}})
, the finite set
\{x^{(k)}_{i,j}\colon 1\leq k\leq n+1,\ 1\leq i,j\leq d\}
, the finite set
\{\lambda_{n+1}\}\cup E_{n}
, and
\epsilon=\frac{1}{(n+1)K}
to obtain: a finite subset
E_{n+1}\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi)
satisfying (c); a finite dimensional subfactor
F_{n+1}\leq F^{c}
satisfying
\varphi|_{F^{c}}=\varphi|_{F_{n+1}}\otimes\varphi|_{F_{n+1}^{\prime}\cap F^{c}}
as well as (d); and a finite set
\{y^{(k)}_{i,j}\in F_{n+1}\colon 1\leq k\leq n+1\,1\leq i,j\leq d\}
satisfying

\|x_{i,j}^{(k)}-y_{i,j}^{(k)}\|_{\varphi}<\frac{1}{(n+1)K^{1/2}}

for each
k=1,\ldots,n+1
and
1\leq i,j\leq d
. Note that
F_{n+1}
being a subfactor of
F^{c}
implies it commutes with
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
. Composing the expectations from
N
to
F^{c}
and from
F^{c}
to
F_{n+1}
gives (b). Towards establishing (a), set

y_{k}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}e_{i,j}y_{i,j}^{(k)}

for each
k=1,\ldots,n+1
so that
y_{k}\in F\vee F_{n+1}=F_{1}\vee\cdots\vee F_{n+1}
and using (b) we have

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|_{\varphi}^{2}

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}e_{i,j}\sum_{r=1^{d}}(x_{\ell,i}^{(k)}-y_{\ell,i}^{(k)})^{*}(x_{\ell,j}^{(k)}-y_{\ell,j}^{(k)})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,r=1}^{d}\varphi(e_{i,j})\varphi((x_{\ell,i}^{(k)}-y_{\ell,i}^{(k)})^{*}(x_{\ell,j}^{(k)}-y_{\ell,j}^{(k)}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}\|e_{i,j}\|_{\varphi}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}K}\leq\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}},

and so (a) follows. Induction therefore gives us the claimed sequence
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of commuting subfactors
N
and the sequence
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite subsets of
\text{Sd}(\varphi)
.

Now, if
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is the
\varphi
=preserving conditional expectation onto
F_{1}\vee\cdots\vee F_{n}
then for each
k\leq n
we have

\|x_{k}-\mathcal{E}_{n}(x_{k})\|_{\varphi}\leq\frac{1}{n}

by (a). That is, the bounded sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n}(x_{k}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
x_{k}
in
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varphi}
-norm and therefore in the strong operator topology. Consequently,

\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}=\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}\right)^{\prime\prime}

contains
x_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\{x_{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a generating set for
N
, we see that the above must in fact equal
N
. Further, (b) implies
\varphi
restricted to
F_{1}\vee\cdots\vee F_{n}
conincides with

\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\varphi|_{F_{k}}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, (c) implies

(N,\varphi)\cong\bigotimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(F_{n},\varphi|_{F_{n}})\cong\bigotimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{M}_{E_{n}}^{\otimes m_{n}},\phi_{E_{n}}^{\otimes m_{n}}).

The inclusions
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n+1}\}\subset E_{n}\subset E_{n+1}
implies that
(M_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\phi_{\lambda_{n}})
appears infinite often in the above tensor product for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus

(N,\varphi)\cong\bigotimes_{\Lambda\cap(0,1)}(R_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})=(R_{\Lambda},\varphi_{\Lambda}),

as claimed.
\hfill\square

6. Proof of Theorem
C

Using either
[
Izu93
, Theorem 4.7]
when
J
is finite or
[
Tho19
, Example 1]
when
J
is infinte, it follows that
\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime}
is a separable injective type
\mathrm{III}
factor. Thus by Theorem
A
it suffices to show
\varphi
is extremal almost periodic with
\text{Sd}(\varphi)=\Lambda
. The generators of
\mathcal{O}_{J}
being eigenoperators with respect to
\varphi
implies the almost periodicity of
\varphi
, and moreover that
\text{Sd}(\varphi)\subset\Lambda
. Since
\text{Sd}(\varphi)
contains generators of the group
\Lambda
, we will obtain
\text{Sd}(\varphi)=\Lambda
once we know the former is a group. Therefore the proof will be complete once we have argued that
\varphi
is extremal, and this is essentially already contained in the proof of
[
Tho19
, Theorem 5]
.

Indeed,
[
Tho19
, Theorem 5]
asserts that the
Connes spectrum
(see
[
Tak03
, Section XI.2]
) of the modular automorphism group of
\sigma^{\varphi}
is given by

\Gamma(\sigma^{\varphi})=\overline{\Lambda}.

In fact, for each projection
q
in the center of
(\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime})^{\varphi}
and each
j\in J
, in the proof of
[
Tho19
, Theorem 5]
Thomsen exhibits a partial isometry
w\in(\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime})^{(\varphi,\mu(j))}
with
qwq\neq 0
. It follows that

\mu(J)\subset\text{Sd}(\varphi|_{q\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime}q})

for all such
q
. Equip
\Lambda
with the discrete topology, let
G
be its compact dual, and
G\overset{\alpha}{\curvearrowright}\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime}
be the point modular extension of the modular automorphism group of
\varphi
. Then the above, in conjunction with
[
Con74
, Equation (1)]
, shows that

\mu(J)\subset\text{Sp}(\alpha^{(q)}),

where
\alpha^{(q)}
is the restriction of
\alpha
to
qMq
and
\text{Sp}(\alpha^{(q)})
is its
Arveson spectrum
(see
[
Tak03
, Section XI.1]
). Using
[
Con73
, Proposition 2.2.2]
we see that

\mu(J)\subset\bigcap_{q}\text{Sp}(\alpha^{(q)})=\Gamma(\alpha).

But since the Connes spectrum of
\alpha
is a subgroup of
\Lambda
it follows that
\Gamma(\alpha)=\Lambda
. This in turn implies
(\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime})^{\alpha}=(\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O}_{J})^{\prime\prime})^{\varphi}
is a factor by
[
Con73
, Theorem 2.4.1]
. That is,
\varphi
is extremal.
\hfill\square

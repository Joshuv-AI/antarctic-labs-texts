Generalized SAV-exponential integrator schemes for Allen–Cahn type gradient flows
Lili Ju, Xiao Li, Zhonghua Qiao

Abstract
The energy dissipation law and the maximum bound principle (MBP) are two important physical features of the well-known Allen–Cahn equation. While some commonly-used first-order time stepping schemes have turned out to preserve unconditionally both energy dissipation law and MBP for the equation, restrictions on the time step size are still needed for existing second-order or even higher-order schemes in order to have such simultaneous preservation. In this paper, we develop and analyze novel first- and second-order linear numerical schemes for a class of Allen–Cahn type gradient flows. Our schemes combine the generalized scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) approach and the exponential time integrator with a stabilization term, while the standard central difference stencil is used for discretization of the spatial differential operator. We not only prove their unconditional preservation of the energy dissipation law and the MBP in the discrete setting, but also derive their optimal temporal error estimates under fixed spatial mesh. Numerical experiments are also carried out to demonstrate the properties and performance of the proposed schemes.

Introduction

The classic Allen–Cahn equation,
originally introduced in
[
1
]
to model the motion of the anti-phase boundaries in crystalline solids,
takes the following form:

(1)

u_{t}=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+f(u),\quad t>0,\ \bm{x}\in\Omega,

where the spatial domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

u(t,\bm{x}):[0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
is the unknown function,

\varepsilon>0
is an interfacial parameter,
and
f(u)=u-u^{3}
is the nonlinear reaction.
Equipped with the periodic or homogeneous Neumann boundary condition,
the equation (
1
) can be viewed as
the
L^{2}
gradient flow with respect to the energy functional

(2)

E(u)=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u(\bm{x})|^{2}+F(u(\bm{x}))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x},

where
F(u)=\frac{1}{4}(u^{2}-1)^{2}
(i.e.,
-F^{\prime}=f
) is the double-well potential function,
and thus satisfies the so-called
energy dissipation law

in the sense that the solution to (
1
) decreases the energy (
2
) along with the time,
i.e.,
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(u(t))\leq 0
.
The solution
u
usually represents the difference between
the concentrations of two components of the alloy,
and thus should be evaluated between
-1
and
1
naturally,
which corresponds to another important feature, the
maximum bound principle
(MBP),
i.e., if the initial value falls pointwise between
-1
and
1
,
then so is the solution for all time.
Recently, some variants of the Allen–Cahn equation (
1
)
have been developed to model various processes of phase transition,
such as the nonlocal Allen–Cahn equation for phase separations within long-range interactions
[
3
]

and the fractional Allen–Cahn equation for anomalous diffusion processes
[
23
]
,
and they also satisfy the energy dissipation law with respect to their respective energy and the MBP.
Since the analytic solutions to these models are usually not available,
numerical solutions play a key role in their study and applications. In order to obtain efficient and stable
numerical simulations and avoid nonphysical results, it is highly desirable to design
accurate numerical methods in space and time which also preserve important physical features of the models,
such as the energy dissipation law and the MBP.

In recent years, numerical schemes preserving the energy dissipation law
have attracted a lot of attention for time integration of the Allen–Cahn type equations and other gradient flows,
including convex splitting schemes
[
22
,
42
,
50
]
,
stabilized implicit-explicit (IMEX) schemes
[
18
,
46
,
51
]
,
discrete gradient schemes
[
15
,
19
,
38
]
,
exponential time differencing (ETD) schemes
[
14
,
32
,
56
]
,
invariant energy quadratization (IEQ) schemes
[
52
,
54
]
,
scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) schemes
[
9
,
43
,
44
,
45
]
,
and some variants of the SAV method
[
7
,
8
,
26
,
28
,
37
]
.
By combining the SAV approach with the Runge–Kutta (RK) method,
arbitrarily high-order linear schemes preserving energy dissipation law were developed in
[
2
,
20
]
.
In addition, there are also a large amount of literature denoted to
MBP-preserving numerical schemes for the Allen–Cahn type gradient flow problems,
such as the stabilized IMEX schemes
[
41
,
48
]

and the exponential integrator methods
[
14
,
34
]
.
Borrowing the idea of strong stability-preserving methods
[
21
]
,
MBP-preserving RK-type schemes with high-order accuracy were studied theoretically
and up to fourth-order schemes were provided for practical computations
in
[
31
,
35
,
55
]
.
However, among all schemes we have just mentioned,
only a few first-order schemes can preserve simultaneously the energy dissipation law and the MBP
unconditionally

[
14
,
16
,
41
]
(i.e., without any restriction on the time step size),
while the second-order schemes always require certain restrictions on the time step size
[
27
,
36
]
.
It is an interesting and important question whether there exist second-order or even higher-order time stepping schemes
preserving both the energy dissipation law and the MBP unconditionally.
An initial improvement was made in
[
53
]
by considering the high-order SAV-RK method
[
2
]

to guarantee the energy dissipation, and the maximum bound is enforced by the cut-off post-processing but not by the scheme itself.

The
H^{-1}
gradient flow with respect to the energy (
2
)
gives the classic Cahn–Hilliard equation
u_{t}=-\Delta(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+f(u))
,
which fails to satisfy the MBP due to the existence of the fourth-order dissipation term.
It is also worth noting that, if the nonlinear reaction function
f
is changed to the logarithmic one defined by (
55
) (i.e., corresponding to the Flory–Huggins potential) tested in our numerical experiments,
the solution of the Cahn–Hilliard equation still remains in the open interval
(-1,1)
for all the time under some appropriate boundary conditions
[
10
,
17
]
,
where
\pm 1
are the points near which the singularities occur.
Implicit or implicit-explicit numerical schemes preserving such uniform boundedness have also been developed,
where the singularity of the nonlinear term plays a crucial role

[
5
,
13
]
in their construction.

In this paper, our main purpose is to systematically develop first- and second-order (in time) linear numerical schemes
preserving both the energy dissipation law and the MBP unconditionally
for a family of Allen–Cahn type gradient flows.
More precisely, we will consider the equation (
1
)
with a more general reaction term
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by a continuously differentiable function satisfying

(3)

\text{there exists a constant $\beta>0$ such that $f(\beta)\leq 0\leq f(-\beta)$}.

The periodic or homogeneous Neumann boundary condition is equipped
and the initial condition is given as

u(0,\cdot)=u_{\text{\rm init}}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
Then, the MBP holds
[
14
]
in the sense that
if the absolute value of the initial value is bounded pointwise by
\beta
,
then the absolute value of the solution is also bounded pointwise by
\beta
for all time, i.e.,

(4)

\max_{\bm{x}\in\overline{\Omega}}|u_{\text{\rm init}}(\bm{x})|\leq\beta\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\max_{\bm{x}\in\overline{\Omega}}|u(t,\bm{x})|\leq\beta,\quad\forall\,t>0.

Furthermore, the energy dissipation law is also satisfied with respect to the energy (
2
) with

F
now being a smooth potential function satisfying
F^{\prime}=-f
.
The key ingredient is the appropriate combination of the SAV approach and the exponential integrator method
[
11
,
25
]
. Note that similar ideas have been applied to some nonlinear hyperbolic-type equations
[
12
,
29
]
.
We first reformulate the model equation (
1
) in an equivalent form
by defining an auxiliary variable, similar to the idea of the generalized SAV approach
[
8
]
.
Then, we introduce a stabilization term to the system and apply exponential integrators
to develop first- and second-order linear schemes in time. We
show that both schemes simultaneously preserve the energy dissipation law and the MBP unconditionally
under appropriate stabilizing constant.
In the error analysis, one of the major difficulties is caused by the variable coefficients from the nonlinear reaction and the stabilization terms.
By using the energy dissipation law and the MBP,
these variable coefficients are shown to be bounded from above and below by certain generic positive constants, which helps us
to successfully prove the optimal temporal convergence under fixed spatial mesh.
To the best of our knowledge,
this is the first work providing a second-order linear numerical scheme for time integration of the model Allen–Cahn type gradient flows,
with provable unconditional preservation of both the energy dissipation law and the MBP.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
,
we present the spatial discretization with the central finite difference and some useful lemmas.
In Section
3
, we propose the first- and second-order generalized SAV-exponential integrator (GSAV-EI) schemes, and then
prove their unconditional preservation of both the energy dissipation law and the MBP, followed by their temporal convergence analysis.
Numerical experiments are carried out to validate the theoretical results and demonstrate the performance of the proposed schemes in Section
4
.
Finally, some concluding remarks are given in Section
5
.

2
Spatial discretization and some preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
we consider the two-dimensional square domain
\Omega=(0,L)\times(0,L)

for the model equation (
1
) with
f
satisfying the assumption (
3
). Without loss of generality, we impose the periodic boundary condition.
Extensions to the three-dimensional case and homogeneous Neumann boundary condition do not have any difficulties.
In this section, we will present some notations related to the spatial discretization
and a few preliminary lemmas for the analysis of the time integration schemes proposed later.

Given a positive integer
M
,
let
h=L/M
be the size of the uniform mesh partitioning
\overline{\Omega}
,
and denote by
\Omega_{h}=\{(x_{i},y_{j})=(ih,jh)\,|\,1\leq i,j\leq M\}
the set of mesh points.
For a grid function
v
defined on
\Omega_{h}
, we denote
v_{ij}=v(x_{i},y_{j})
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{h}
be the set of all
M
-periodic grid functions on
\Omega_{h}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}_{h}=\{v:\Omega_{h}\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,v_{i+kM,j+lM}=v_{ij},\ k,l\in\mathbb{Z},\ 1\leq i,j\leq M\}
.
Let us apply the central finite difference method to approximate the spatial differential operators.
For any
v\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
, the discrete Laplace operator
\Delta_{h}
is defined by

\Delta_{h}v_{ij}=\frac{1}{h^{2}}(v_{i+1,j}+v_{i-1,j}+v_{i,j+1}+v_{i,j-1}-4v_{ij}),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq M,

and the discrete gradient operator
\nabla_{h}
is defined by

\nabla_{h}v_{ij}=\Big(\frac{v_{i+1,j}-v_{ij}}{h},\frac{v_{i,j+1}-v_{ij}}{h}\Big)^{T},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq M.

The eigenvalues of
\Delta_{h}
are given by
[
33
]

(5)

\lambda_{kl}=-\frac{4}{h^{2}}\Big(\sin^{2}\frac{k\pi}{M}+\sin^{2}\frac{l\pi}{M}\Big)\leq 0,\quad 0\leq k,l\leq M-1.

As usual, the discrete inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, the discrete
L^{2}
norm
\|\cdot\|
,
and the discrete
L^{\infty}
norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
can be defined respectively by

\langle v,w\rangle=h^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{M}v_{ij}w_{ij},\qquad\|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle},\qquad\|v\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq M}|v_{ij}|

for any
v,w\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
, and

\langle\bm{v},\bm{w}\rangle=\langle v^{1},w^{1}\rangle+\langle v^{2},w^{2}\rangle,\qquad\|\bm{v}\|=\sqrt{\langle\bm{v},\bm{v}\rangle}

for any
\bm{v}=(v^{1},v^{2})^{T},\bm{w}=(w^{1},w^{2})^{T}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}\times\mathcal{M}_{h}
.
By the periodicity, the summation-by-parts formula obviously holds:

\langle v,\Delta_{h}w\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{h}v,\nabla_{h}w\rangle=\langle\Delta_{h}v,w\rangle,\quad\forall\,v,w\in\mathcal{M}_{h}.

The space-discrete problem corresponding to (
1
) is then
to find a function
u_{h}:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{M}_{h}
satisfies

(6)

\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{h}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}u_{h}+f(u_{h}),\quad t>0

with
u_{h}(0)=\hat{u}_{{\rm init}}
, where
\hat{u}_{{\rm init}}
is the pointwise projection of
u_{\text{\rm init}}
onto
\mathcal{M}_{h}
. Throughout the paper, we do not differ
\hat{u}_{{\rm init}}

and
u_{{\rm init}}
anymore since there is no ambiguity.
It is easy to verify the energy dissipation law for the space-discrete problem (
6
), i.e.,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{h}(u_{h}(t))\leq 0
,
where
E_{h}
is the spatially-discretized energy functional defined as

(7)

E_{h}(v):=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}v\|^{2}+\langle F(v),1\rangle,\quad\forall\,v\in\mathcal{M}_{h}.

As shown in
[
14
]
,
the MBP is also valid for the space-discrete problem (
6
), i.e.,

(8)

\|u_{{\rm init}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\|u_{h}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\beta,\quad\forall\,t>0.

We have assumed that
f
is continuously differentiable,
so
\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
is finite.
Then the following result is valid
[
14
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Under the assumption (
3
),
if
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
holds for some positive constant
\kappa
,
then we have
|f(\xi)+\kappa\xi|\leq\kappa\beta
for any
\xi\in[-\beta,\beta]
.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is a finite-dimensional linear space,
any grid function in
\mathcal{M}_{h}
and any linear operator from
\mathcal{M}_{h}
to
\mathcal{M}_{h}

can be treated as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{M^{2}}
and a matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{M^{2}\times M^{2}}
, respectively.
For functions of matrix/operator, we have the following lemma (see
[
24
]
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\phi
be defined on the spectrum of a diagonalizable matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
, i.e.,
the values
\{\phi(\lambda_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
exist, where
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are the eigenvalues of
A
.
Then

(i)

\phi(A)
commutes with
A
and
\phi(A^{T})=\phi(A)^{T}
;

(ii)

the eigenvalues of
\phi(A)
are
\{\phi(\lambda_{i})\,|\,1\leq i\leq m\}
;

(iii)

\phi(P^{-1}AP)=P^{-1}\phi(A)P
for any nonsingular matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
.

We still use the notations
\|\cdot\|
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}

to denote the matrix induced-norms consistent with
\|\cdot\|
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
defined before, respectively.
By viewing
\Delta_{h}
as a matrix,
we know that
\Delta_{h}
is symmetric, negative semi-definite,
and weakly diagonally dominant with all diagonal entries negative.
Moreover, we have the following useful estimate,
which comes from the fact that

\Delta_{h}
is the generator of a contraction semigroup
[
14
]
,
and the proof can be found in
[
14
,
31
]
.

Lemma 3
.

For any real numbers
a\geq 0
and
b\geq 0
, we have
\|\mathrm{e}^{a\Delta_{h}-bI}\|_{\infty}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-b}
,
where
I\in\mathbb{R}^{M^{2}\times M^{2}}
is the identity matrix.

Remark 2.1
.

Apart from the central difference discretization discussed above,
the lumped mass finite element method with piecewise linear basis functions can also be adopted
and Lemma
3
still holds correspondingly
[
14
]
.
In addition, there have been some initial explorations on the MBP-preserving methods
using the fourth-order accurate spatial discretization,
such as the compact difference approximation
[
49
]

and the finite difference formulation of the
Q^{2}
spectral element method
[
47
]
,
combined with the Euler-type time-stepping approaches.
However, it is not obvious that
whether these fourth-order discrete Laplace operators satisfy Lemma
3
,
and thus it is worthy of further investigations on the combination of higher-order spatial discretizations
with the exponential integrator methods studied in this paper.

3
Generalized SAV-exponential integrator schemes

From now on, we always assume the initial value
u_{\text{\rm init}}
has the enough regularity as needed.
Let us define the bulk energy term
E_{1h}(v):=\langle F(v),1\rangle
for any
v\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
.
The continuity of
F
implies that
F
is bounded from below on
[-\beta,\beta]
.
Therefore, according to the MBP (
8
),
there exist two constants
C_{*}\geq 0
and
C^{*}\geq 0
such that

(9)

-C_{*}\leq E_{1h}(u_{h})\leq C^{*}.

Motivated by the idea of the generalized SAV approach
[
8
]
,
we define the auxiliary variable
s_{h}(t)=E_{1h}(u_{h}(t))
, and rewrite the space-discrete equation (
6
) in an alternate but equivalent form as below:

(10a)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{h}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}u_{h}+\frac{\sigma(s_{h})}{\sigma(E_{1h}(u_{h}))}f(u_{h}),

(10b)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{h}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma(s_{h})}{\sigma(E_{1h}(u_{h}))}\Big\langle f(u_{h}),\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{h}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\rangle,

where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a one-variable function satisfying the following two conditions:

(\Sigma_{1})

\sigma>0
on
\mathbb{R}
;

(\Sigma_{2})

\sigma
is continuously differentiable and
\sigma^{\prime}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}
.

These conditions are crucial to the MBP preservation and error analysis of the proposed time integration schemes in this paper.
For any
v\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, define

(11)

g(v,r):=\frac{\sigma(r)}{\sigma(E_{1h}(v))}

(clearly
g(v,r)>0
due to the condition
(\Sigma_{1})
) and the modified energy

(12)

\mathcal{E}_{h}(v,r):=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}v\|^{2}+r.

Obviously,
g(u_{h},s_{h})\equiv 1
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h},s_{h})\equiv E_{h}(u_{h})
without time discretization.

Remark 3.1
.

The form (
10
) and the conditions
(\Sigma_{1})
and
(\Sigma_{2})

differs from those given in
[
8
]
,
where
\sigma
may not be defined on the whole real line
\mathbb{R}

and the constructed schemes may be nonlinear,
while our schemes developed later are all linear.
A trivial choice for the function
\sigma
satisfying
(\Sigma_{1})
and
(\Sigma_{2})

is the positive constant mapping, e.g.,
\sigma\equiv 1
. This gives a degenerate case since we still have exactly
g(u_{h},s_{h})\equiv 1
with whatever time discretization and consequently
s_{h}
does not provide any feedback to the update of
u_{h}
at each time step, which is similar to the idea adopted in
[
39
]
.
Some nontrivial choices
[
8
]
include elementary functions such as

\sigma(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x}
,
\sigma(x)=\frac{\pi}{2}+\arctan(x)
,
\sigma(x)=1+\tanh(x)
,
or even special functions constructed by the integration such as

\sigma(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

for some continuous function
\eta\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Next we develop exponential integrators for the space-discrete system (
10
), instead of the original one (
6
).
Let us partition the time interval using the nodes
\{t_{n}=n{\tau}\}_{n\geq 0}
with a uniform time step size
{\tau}>0
,
and set
u^{n}
and
s^{n}
as the approximations of
u_{h,e}(t_{n})
and
s_{h,e}(t_{n})=E_{1h}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))
respectively, where

u_{h,e}
denotes the exact solution to the problem (
6
) (equivalently (
10
)).

3.1
First-order GSAV-EI scheme

Set
u^{0}=u_{\text{\rm init}}
and
s^{0}=E_{1h}(u^{0})
.
Suppose the numerical solution
(u^{n},s^{n})
is known for some
n\geq 0
.
Introducing a positive stabilizing constant
\kappa>0
,
the equation (
10a
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{h}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}u_{h}+g(u_{h},s_{h})f(u_{h})+\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})(u_{h}-u_{h})

\displaystyle=-L_{\kappa}^{n}u_{h}+N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h},s_{h}),

where the linear operator
L_{\kappa}^{n}=\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})I-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}
and the nonlinear operator

N_{\kappa}^{n}(v,r)=g(v,r)f(v)+\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})v,\quad\forall\,v\in\mathcal{M}_{h},\ \forall\,r\in\mathbb{R}.

We know that
L_{\kappa}^{n}
is self-adjoint and positive definite since
g(u^{n},s^{n})>0
and
\kappa>0
.
Using the variation-of-constants formula on
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
, we have

u_{h}(t_{n+1})=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}u_{h}(t_{n})+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)L_{\kappa}^{n}}N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h}(t_{n}+\theta),s_{h}(t_{n}+\theta))\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

By approximating the term
N_{\kappa}^{n}
by its value at
\theta=0
, i.e.,

N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h}(t_{n}+\theta),s_{h}(t_{n}+\theta))\approx N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h}(t_{n}),s_{h}(t_{n}))
,
we obtain the first-order exponential integrator scheme for computing
u^{n+1}
as

(13a)

\displaystyle u^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}u^{n}+\bigg(\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)L_{\kappa}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg)N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}u^{n}+{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n}),

where
\phi_{1}(a)=a^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{a}-1)
for
a\not=0
.
Integrating (
10b
) from
t_{n}
to
t_{n+1}

and using the approximation

g(u_{h}(t_{n}+t),s_{h}(t_{n}+t))f(u_{h}(t_{n}+t))\approx g(u_{h}(t_{n}),s_{h}(t_{n}))f(u_{h}(t_{n}))
,
we obtain the first-order formula for computing
s^{n+1}
as

(13b)

s^{n+1}=s^{n}-g(u^{n},s^{n})\langle f(u^{n}),u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle.

The combination of (
13a
) and (
13b
) defines the first-order generalized SAV-exponential integrator (GSAV-EI1) scheme,
which is unique solvable for any
{\tau}>0
due to its explicit formulation.

3.1.1
Energy dissipation and MBP

We first show the unconditional preservation of
the energy dissipation law with respect to the modified energy
\mathcal{E}_{h}
defined by (
12
) and the MBP of the GSAV-EI1 scheme (
13
).
As a consequence, we then prove the uniform boundedness of
g(u^{n},s^{n})
,
which is crucial to the convergence analysis.

Theorem 4

(Energy dissipation of GSAV-EI1)
.

The GSAV-EI1 scheme (
13
) is unconditionally energy dissipative
in the time-discrete sense that
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n+1},s^{n+1})\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})
holds for any
{\tau}>0
and
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Using (
13b
), some simple calculations yield

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n+1},s^{n+1})-\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}+s^{n+1}-s^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad=\varepsilon^{2}\langle\nabla_{h}u^{n+1},\nabla_{h}u^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}u^{n}\rangle-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-g(u^{n},s^{n})\langle f(u^{n}),u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=-\langle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}u^{n+1}+g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n}),u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle L_{\kappa}^{n}u^{n+1}-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n}),u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}.

It can also be derived from (
13a
) that

u^{n+1}-u^{n}=(\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}-I)u^{n}+{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n}).

Multiplying
[{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})]^{-1}=(I-\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}})^{-1}L_{\kappa}^{n}
on both sides of the above equation leads to

\displaystyle[{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})]^{-1}(u^{n+1}-u^{n})

\displaystyle=-L_{\kappa}^{n}u^{n}+N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})

\displaystyle=L_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n+1}-u^{n})-L_{\kappa}^{n}u^{n+1}+N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n}),

and thus,

(15)

L_{\kappa}^{n}u^{n+1}-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})=[L_{\kappa}^{n}-(I-\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}})^{-1}L_{\kappa}^{n}](u^{n+1}-u^{n}).

Note that
L_{\kappa}^{n}
is positive definite and
a-(1-\mathrm{e}^{-a})^{-1}a<0
for any
a>0
,
which means, by Lemma
2
, that
L_{\kappa}^{n}-(I-\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}})^{-1}L_{\kappa}^{n}
is negative definite.
Combining (
14
) and (
15
),
we obtain the energy dissipation
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n+1},s^{n+1})\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Theorem
4
states that
the GSAV-EI1 scheme (
13
) is energy dissipative
with respect to the modified energy
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})
rather than the original energy
E_{h}(u^{n})
.
Note that
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})
is only an approximation of
E_{h}(u^{n})
after time discretization since usually
s^{n}\not=E_{1h}(u^{n})
for
n>0
.

Corollary 5
.

For any
{\tau}>0
and
n\geq 0
, it holds that
s^{n}\leq E_{h}(u_{\text{\rm init}})
.

Proof.

Since
s^{0}=E_{1h}(u_{\text{\rm init}})
, we have by Theorem
4
that

\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}+s^{n}=\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n-1},s^{n-1})\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{0},s^{0})=E_{h}(u_{\text{\rm init}}).

Dropping off the nonnegative term
\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}
leads to the expected result.
∎

Theorem 6

(MBP of GSAV-EI1)
.

If
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
, then
the GSAV-EI1 scheme (
13
) preserves the MBP unconditionally, i.e.,
for any
{\tau}>0
, the time-discrete version of (
8
) is valid as follows:

(16)

\|u_{\text{\rm init}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\|u^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta,\quad\forall\,n\geq 0.

Proof.

Suppose
(u^{n},s^{n})
is given and
\|u^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
for some
n\geq 0
.
By Lemma
3
, we get

\|\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}\|_{\infty}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})}
.
Since
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
and
g(u^{n},s^{n})>0
,
by Lemma
1
, we have

\|N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})\|_{\infty}=g(u^{n},s^{n})\|f(u^{n})+\kappa u^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\kappa\beta g(u^{n},s^{n}).

Therefore, we obtain from (
13a
) that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})}\beta+\bigg(\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg)\cdot\kappa\beta g(u^{n},s^{n})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})}\beta+\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})}}{\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})}\cdot\kappa\beta g(u^{n},s^{n})=\beta.

By induction, we have
\|u^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
for any
n\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

By applying
\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}\approx(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})^{-1}
to (
13a
), we can obtain

(17)

\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{{\tau}}=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}u^{n+1}+g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n})+\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})(u^{n+1}-u^{n}).

The scheme formed by (
17
) and (
13b
) can be regarded as the stabilizing version of
the generalized-SAV scheme
[
8
]
, which also satisfies
the energy dissipation law (Theorem
4
) and the MBP (Theorem
6
).
In particular, by setting
\sigma(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x}
, it recovers exactly the first-order stabilized exponential-SAV scheme
[
30
]
.

Unlike the time-continuous case in which
g(u_{h},s_{h})\equiv 1
,
the coefficient
g(u^{n},s^{n})
may vary at each time step unless
\sigma
is chosen as a constant function,
which lead to some difficulties for the error analysis.
Fortunately, by the energy dissipation law and the MBP,
we can show that
g(u^{n},s^{n})
is bounded uniformly in
n
.
The following lemma (without proof) is useful to estimate some exponential-related functions of matrices.

Lemma 7
.

For any
a>0
, the following inequalities hold:

0<1-\mathrm{e}^{-a}<a,\qquad 0<\phi_{1}(-a)<1,\qquad 1<(1+a)\phi_{1}(-a)<2.

Corollary 8
.

Given any fixed
h>0
and
T>0
.
If
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
and
\|u_{\text{\rm init}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
, then
there are two constants
G_{*}>0
and
G^{*}>0
such that

G_{*}\leq g(u^{n},s^{n})\leq G^{*},\quad 0\leq n\leq\lfloor T/{\tau}\rfloor,

where
G_{*}
and
G^{*}
depend on
C_{*}
,
C^{*}
,
|\Omega|
,
T
,
u_{\text{\rm init}}
,
\kappa
,
\varepsilon
, and
\|f\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
,
but are independent of
{\tau}
.

Proof.

Since
\|u^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
(by Theorem
6
),
according to (
9
) and the conditions
(\Sigma_{1})
and
(\Sigma_{2})
, it holds

0<\sigma(-C_{*})\leq\sigma(E_{1h}(u^{n}))\leq\sigma(C^{*})
.
According to Corollary
5
, we have

g(u^{n},s^{n})=\frac{\sigma(s^{n})}{\sigma(E_{1h}(u^{n}))}\leq\frac{\sigma(E_{h}(u_{\text{\rm init}}))}{\sigma(-C_{*})}:=G^{*}.

Using (
5
), we then obtain the uniform bound of the spectral radius of
L_{\kappa}^{n}
,
\rho(L_{\kappa}^{n})
, as

(18)

\rho(L_{\kappa}^{n})\leq\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})+\varepsilon^{2}\rho(\Delta_{h})\leq G^{*}\kappa+\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}}{h^{2}}:=M_{h}.

Next we show the existence of the lower bound of
\{s^{n}\}
.
By making use of the MBP and the first inequality in Lemma
7
,
we derive from (
13a
) that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|I-\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}\|\|u^{n}\|+{\tau}\|\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})\|\|N_{\kappa}(u^{n},s^{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq{\tau}\rho(L_{\kappa}^{n})\cdot\beta|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}+{\tau}\cdot\kappa\beta g(u^{n},s^{n})|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq{\tau}\beta|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}(M_{h}+G^{*}\kappa).

Since
\|f(u^{n})\|\leq F_{0}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}
with
F_{0}:=\|f\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
,
we then obtain from (
13b
) that

(19)

s^{n+1}\geq s^{n}-g(u^{n},s^{n})\|f(u^{n})\|\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|\geq s^{n}-G^{*}F_{0}\beta|\Omega|(M_{h}+G^{*}\kappa){\tau}.

By recursion, noting that
s^{0}=E_{1h}(u_{\text{\rm init}})\geq-C_{*}
, we obtain

(20)

s^{n}\geq s^{0}-G^{*}F_{0}\beta|\Omega|(M_{h}+G^{*}\kappa)n{\tau}\geq-C_{*}-G^{*}F_{0}\beta|\Omega|(M_{h}+G^{*}\kappa)T:=S_{*}.

Thus

g(u^{n},s^{n})\geq\sigma(S_{*})/\sigma(C^{*}):=G_{*},

which completes the proof.
∎

3.1.2
Temporal error analysis

Note that (
13a
) is equivalent to find
u^{n+1}=w({\tau})
with
w(\theta)
satisfying

(21)

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}w(\theta)}{\mathrm{d}\theta}+L_{\kappa}^{n}w(\theta)=N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n}),&\theta\in(0,{\tau}],\\
w(0)=u^{n}.\end{cases}

Let
s_{h,e}(t)=E_{1h}(u_{h,e}(t))
.
Define
w_{h,e}(\theta)=u_{h,e}(t_{n}+\theta)
for
\theta\in[0,{\tau}]
. It holds

(22)

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}w_{h,e}(\theta)}{\mathrm{d}\theta}+L_{\kappa}^{n}w_{h,e}(\theta)=N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))+R_{1u}^{n}(\theta),&\theta\in(0,{\tau}],\\
w_{h,e}(0)=u_{h,e}(t_{n}),\end{cases}

where the truncation error

\displaystyle R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)

\displaystyle=g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}+\theta),s_{h,e}(t_{n}+\theta))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}+\theta))-g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})(u_{h,e}(t_{n}+\theta)-u_{h,e}(t_{n})).

For
s_{h,e}(t)
, we have

(23)

\displaystyle s_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-s_{h,e}(t_{n})

\displaystyle\qquad=-g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))\langle f(u_{h,e}(t_{n})),u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\rangle+{\tau}R_{1s}^{n},

for some truncation residual
R_{1s}^{n}
. Furthermore, it is easy to verify that

(24)

{\sup_{\theta\in(0,{\tau})}}\|R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)\|\leq C_{e,h}{\tau},\qquad|R_{1s}^{n}|\leq C_{e,h}{\tau},

where the constant
C_{e,h}>0
depends on
u_{h,e}
,
\kappa
,
\varepsilon
, and
\|f\|_{C^{1}[-\beta,\beta]}
.
Now we define the error functions as

(25)

e_{u}^{n}=u^{n}-u_{h,e}(t_{n}),\qquad e_{s}^{n}=s^{n}-s_{h,e}(t_{n}).

Lemma 9
.

Given any fixed
h>0
and
T>0
.
If
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
and
\|u_{\text{\rm init}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
, we have

\|g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n})-g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))\|\leq C_{g}(\|e_{u}^{n}\|+|e_{s}^{n}|),\quad 0\leq n\leq\lfloor T/{\tau}\rfloor,

where
C_{g}>0
is a constant depending on
C_{*}
,
|\Omega|
,
u_{\text{\rm init}}
, and
\|f\|_{C^{1}[-\beta,\beta]}
.

A special case of this lemma with
\sigma(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x}
has been proved in
[
30
]
.
Using (
9
) and the conditions
(\Sigma_{1})
and
(\Sigma_{2})
,
there is no essential difficulty to obtain the general result,
so we omit the proof.

Theorem 10

(Temporal error estimate of GSAV-EI1)
.

Given any fixed
h>0
and
T>0
, and let
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
.
Assume that the exact solution
u_{h,e}
is smooth enough on
[0,T]
and
\|u_{\text{\rm init}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
.
If
{\tau}>0
is sufficiently small, then we have the following error estimate
for the GSAV-EI1 scheme (
13
):

(26)

\|u^{n}-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\|+|s^{n}-s_{h,e}(t_{n})|\leq C_{h,1}{\tau},\quad 0\leq n\leq\lfloor T/{\tau}\rfloor,

where the constant
C_{h,1}>0
is independent of
{\tau}
.

Proof.

Define
e(\theta)=w(\theta)-w_{h,e}(\theta)
.
The difference between (
21
) and (
22
) leads to

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}e(\theta)}{\mathrm{d}\theta}+L_{\kappa}^{n}e(\theta)=N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))-R_{1u}^{n}(\theta),\quad\theta\in(0,{\tau}],\\
e(0)=e_{u}^{n},\end{cases}

whose solution
e({\tau})=u^{n+1}-u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})=e_{u}^{n+1}
can be expressed as

(27)

\displaystyle e_{u}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}e_{u}^{n}+{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})[N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))]

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)L_{\kappa}^{n}}R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Acting
I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}
on both sides of (
27
), we obtain

(28)

\displaystyle(1+{\tau}\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n}))(e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n})-{\tau}\varepsilon^{2}(\Delta_{h}e_{u}^{n+1}-\Delta_{h}e_{u}^{n})

\displaystyle\qquad={\tau}(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})[N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})(\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}-I)e_{u}^{n}-\int_{0}^{\tau}(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)L_{\kappa}^{n}}R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Taking the discrete inner product of (
28
) with
\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}:=(e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n})/{\tau}
, we get the reformulation of
the left-hand side (LHS) of (
28
) as

\text{LHS}={\tau}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})\|e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla_{h}e_{u}^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}e_{u}^{n}\|^{2},

and the reformulation of the right-hand side (RHS) of (
28
) as

\displaystyle\text{RHS}={\tau}\langle(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})[N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))],\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})(\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}-I)e_{u}^{n},\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\langle(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n})\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)L_{\kappa}^{n}}R_{1u}^{n}(\theta),\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

By using Corollary
8
, the identity

\|e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}=\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}-2{\tau}\langle e_{u}^{n},\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle
, and the Young’s inequality, we obtain

(29)

LHS

\displaystyle\geq{\tau}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+G_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-G_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}-2G_{*}\kappa{\tau}\langle e_{u}^{n},\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{7{\tau}}{8}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+G_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-G_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}-8G_{*}^{2}\kappa^{2}{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}.

According to (
18
), when
{\tau}\leq M_{h}^{-1}
, we have

\|I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}\|\leq 1+{\tau}\rho(L_{\kappa}^{n})\leq 2
.
By Lemma
7
, we have
\|\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}-I\|\leq\|{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}\|\leq M_{h}{\tau}
.
Thus, for
{\tau}\leq M_{h}^{-1}
, we have

(30)

RHS

\displaystyle\leq 2{\tau}\|N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))\|\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\quad+2M_{h}{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|+2{\tau}{\sup_{\theta\in(0,{\tau})}}\|R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)\|\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq 8{\tau}\|N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+8M_{h}^{2}{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+8{\tau}{\sup_{\theta\in(0,{\tau})}}\|R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)\|^{2}+\frac{3{\tau}}{8}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}.

Using Lemma
9
, we have

(31)

\displaystyle\|N_{\kappa}^{n}(u^{n},s^{n})-N_{\kappa}^{n}(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n})-g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))\|+\kappa g(u^{n},s^{n})\|e_{u}^{n}\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(C_{g}+G^{*}\kappa)\|e_{u}^{n}\|+C_{g}|e_{s}^{n}|,

and thus, we obtain from (
30
) and (
31
) that

(32)

RHS

\displaystyle\leq[16(C_{g}+G^{*}\kappa)^{2}+8M_{h}^{2}]{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+16C_{g}^{2}{\tau}|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+8{\tau}{\sup_{\theta\in(0,{\tau})}}\|R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)\|^{2}+\frac{3{\tau}}{8}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}.

Combining (
32
) with (
29
), we obtain

(33)

\displaystyle G_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-G_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{{\tau}}{2}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq[16(C_{g}+G^{*}\kappa)^{2}+8M_{h}^{2}+8G_{*}^{2}\kappa^{2}]{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+16C_{g}^{2}{\tau}|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+8{\tau}{\sup_{\theta\in(0,{\tau})}}\|R_{1u}^{n}(\theta)\|^{2}.

The difference between (
13b
) and (
23
) leads to

\displaystyle e_{s}^{n+1}-e_{s}^{n}

\displaystyle=\langle g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))-g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n}),u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-g(u^{n},s^{n})\langle f(u^{n}),e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n}\rangle-{\tau}R_{1s}^{n}.

Multiplying the above equation by
2e_{s}^{n+1}
yields

(34)

\displaystyle|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}-|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}+|e_{s}^{n+1}-e_{s}^{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=2e_{s}^{n+1}\langle g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))-g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n}),

\displaystyle\qquad\quad u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\rangle-2{\tau}g(u^{n},s^{n})e_{s}^{n+1}\langle f(u^{n}),\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle-2{\tau}R_{1s}^{n}e_{s}^{n+1}.

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
34
), using Lemma
9
, we have

(35)

\displaystyle 2e_{s}^{n+1}\langle g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))-g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n}),u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|e_{s}^{n+1}|\|g(u_{h,e}(t_{n}),s_{h,e}(t_{n}))f(u_{h,e}(t_{n}))-g(u^{n},s^{n})f(u^{n})\|\|u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{g}{\tau}|e_{s}^{n+1}|(\|e_{u}^{n}\|+|e_{s}^{n}|)\|(u_{h,e})_{t}(\theta_{n})\|\qquad\text{($t_{n}<\theta_{n}<t_{n+1}$)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}{\tau}(\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}+|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}),

where
C_{1}>0
depends on
C_{*}
,
|\Omega|
,
u_{h,e}
, and
\|f\|_{C^{1}[-\beta,\beta]}
.
For the second term on the right-hand side of (
34
), we have

(36)

\displaystyle-2{\tau}g(u^{n},s^{n})e_{s}^{n+1}\langle f(u^{n}),\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2{\tau}G^{*}\|f(u^{n})\||e_{s}^{n+1}|\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}{\tau}|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}+\frac{{\tau}}{2}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2},

where
C_{2}>0
depends on
C_{*}
,
|\Omega|
,
u_{\text{\rm init}}
, and
\|f\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
.
For the third term on the right-hand side of (
34
), we have

(37)

-2{\tau}R_{1s}^{n}e_{s}^{n+1}\leq{\tau}|R_{1s}^{n}|^{2}+{\tau}|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}.

Substituting (
35
)–(
37
) into (
34
) leads to

(38)

|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}-|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}\leq C_{1}{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+C_{1}{\tau}|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}+(1+C_{1}+C_{2}){\tau}|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}+\frac{{\tau}}{2}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+{\tau}|R_{1s}^{n}|^{2}.

Adding (
33
) and (
38
)
and using (
24
),
we reach

G_{*}\kappa(\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2})+(|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}-|e_{s}^{n}|^{2})\leq C_{3}{\tau}(\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}+|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2})+9C_{e,h}^{2}{\tau}^{3},

where
C_{3}>0
depends on
C_{*}
,
|\Omega|
,
T
,
u_{h,e}
,
\kappa
,
\varepsilon
, and
\|f\|_{C^{1}[-\beta,\beta]}
.
Finally, by applying the discrete Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

G_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}\leq\tilde{C}_{h,1}{\tau}^{2},

where
\tilde{C}_{h,1}>0
is a constant independent of
{\tau}
, which finally gives us (
26
) by taking
C_{h,1}=\sqrt{\tilde{C}_{h,1}/\min\{1,\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa\}}
.
∎

3.2
Second-order GSAV-EI scheme

Now we present the second-order generalized SAV-exponential integrator (GSAV-EI2) scheme, which is developed in the prediction-correction fashion.
Let
\kappa>0
again be the stabilizing constant.
First, we adopt the GSAV-EI1 scheme (
13
)
to generate a solution
(\widetilde{u}^{n+1},\widetilde{s}^{n+1})
as the prediction and define

\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=(u^{n}+\widetilde{u}^{n+1})/{2}
and

\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=(s^{n}+\widetilde{s}^{n+1})/{2}
.
Then, we rewrite the space-discrete system (
10a
) in the equivalent form as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{h}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}u_{h}+g(u_{h},s_{h})f(u_{h})+\kappa g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})(u_{h}-u_{h})

\displaystyle=-L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}+N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(u_{h},s_{h}),

where the linear operator
L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\kappa g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})I-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}

is self-adjoint and positive definite since
g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})>0
and
\kappa>0
,
and the nonlinear operator

(39)

N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(v,r)=g(v,r)f(v)+\kappa g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})v,\quad\forall\,v\in\mathcal{M}_{h},\ \forall\,r\in\mathbb{R}.

Applying the variation-of-constants formula gives us

(40)

u_{h}(t_{n+1})=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}u_{h}(t_{n})+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(u_{h}(t_{n}+\theta),s_{h}(t_{n}+\theta))\,\mathrm{d}\theta.\vskip-5.69046pt

Approximating the term
N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}

by its value at
\theta=\frac{{\tau}}{2}
in the above equation, i.e.,

(41)

N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(u_{h}(t_{n}+\theta),s_{h}(t_{n}+\theta))\approx N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(u_{h}(t_{n+\frac{1}{2}}),s_{h}(t_{n+\frac{1}{2}})),

we obtain the GSAV-EI2 scheme for computing
u^{n+1}
as

(42a)

\displaystyle u^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}u^{n}+\bigg(\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-({\tau}-\theta)L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg)N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}u^{n}+{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}).

To update
s^{n+1}
, we discretize (
10b
) at
t=t_{n+\frac{1}{2}}
to give

(42b)

\displaystyle s^{n+1}

\displaystyle=s^{n}-g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle f(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa}{2}g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle u^{n+1}-\widetilde{u}^{n+1},u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
42b
) is based on the Crank–Nicolson discretization,
and the third term is an artificial stabilization term of high order.

3.2.1
Energy dissipation and MBP

Similar to the analysis of the GSAV-EI1 scheme, we first prove the unconditional preservation of
the energy dissipation law and the MBP of the GSAV-EI2 scheme (
42
),
then we show the uniform boundedness of
g(u^{n},s^{n})
and
g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})
,
which is important to the error analysis.

Theorem 11

(Energy dissipation of GSAV-EI2)
.

The GSAV-EI2 scheme (
42
) is unconditionally energy dissipative
in the time-discrete sense that
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n+1},s^{n+1})\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})
holds for any
{\tau}>0
and
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4
,
some simple calculations give us

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n+1},s^{n+1})-\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})=\langle L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}u^{n+1}-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\kappa g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle u^{n+1}-\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle u^{n+1}-\widetilde{u}^{n+1},u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle(L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-(I-\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}})^{-1}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})(u^{n+1}-u^{n}),u^{n+1}-u^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}u^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}u^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|^{2}.

Then, the energy dissipation comes from the negative definiteness of the operator

L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-(I-\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}})^{-1}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

Corollary 12
.

For any
{\tau}>0
and
n\geq 0
, it holds that
s^{n}\leq E_{h}(u_{\text{\rm init}})
and
\widetilde{s}^{n+1}\leq E_{h}(u_{\text{\rm init}})
for the the GSAV-EI2 scheme (
42
).

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Corollary
5
,
the uniform boundedness of
\{s^{n}\}
is a direct consequence of Theorem
11
.
Since
\widetilde{s}^{n+1}
is generated by the GSAV-EI1 scheme (
13
),
we have
\widetilde{s}^{n+1}\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(\widetilde{u}^{n+1},\widetilde{s}^{n+1})\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(u^{n},s^{n})
according to Theorem
4
,
and thus,
\widetilde{s}^{n+1}\leq E_{h}(u_{\text{\rm init}})
.
∎

Theorem 13

(MBP of GSAV-EI2)
.

If
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
, then
the GSAV-EI2 scheme (
42
) preserves the MBP unconditionally,
i.e., for any
{\tau}>0
, the time-discrete version of MBP (
16
) is valid.

Proof.

Suppose
(u^{n},s^{n})
is given and
\|u^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
for some
n
.
According to Theorem
6
,
we know
\|\widetilde{u}^{n+1}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
,
and thus
\|\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
.
We also have from Theorem
11
that
\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\leq E_{h}(u_{\text{\rm init}})
.
Noting that (
42a
) has the same form as (
13a
),
the proof can be completed in the similar way to that of Theorem
6
.
∎

Corollary 14
.

Given any fixed
h>0
and
T>0
.
If
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
and
\|u_{\text{\rm init}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
,
then there are two constants
\widehat{G}_{*}>0
and
G^{*}>0
such that

\widehat{G}_{*}\leq g(u^{n},s^{n})\leq G^{*},\;\;\widehat{G}_{*}\leq g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\leq G^{*},\quad 0\leq n\leq\lfloor T/{\tau}\rfloor-1,

where
G^{*}
is the same constant defined in Corollary
8
,
and
\widehat{G}_{*}
depends on
C_{*}
,
C^{*}
,
|\Omega|
,
T
,
u_{\text{\rm init}}
,
\kappa
,
\varepsilon
, and
\|f\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
,
but is independent of
{\tau}
.

Proof.

As done in the proof of Corollary
8
,
the upper bound
G^{*}
of
\{g(u^{n},s^{n})\}
and
\{g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\}

is the direct result of the monotonicity of
\sigma
and Theorems
11
and
13
,
and thus,
\rho(L_{\kappa}^{n})\leq M_{h}

\rho(L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\leq M_{h}

the same constant defined in (

).
For the existence of the lower bound

\{s^{n}\}
and
\{\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\}
.
Similar to the derivations in the proof of Corollary
8
, we have

\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|\leq{\tau}\beta|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}(M_{h}+G^{*}\kappa),\qquad\|\widetilde{u}^{n+1}-u^{n}\|\leq{\tau}\beta|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}(M_{h}+G^{*}\kappa),

Then, we derive from (

) that

\displaystyle s^{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq s^{n}-g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|f(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\kappa}{2}g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|u^{n+1}-\widetilde{u}^{n+1}\|\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|

∎

Lemma 15
.

Given any fixed
h>0
and
T>0
and
assume that the exact solution
u_{h,e}
is smooth enough on
[0,T]
.
Define

\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=(u_{h,e}(t_{n})+u_{h,e}(t_{n+1}))/2
and

\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=(s_{h,e}(t_{n})+s_{h,e}(t_{n+1}))/2

It holds that

(43a)

\displaystyle u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}u_{h,e}(t_{n})+{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})+{\tau}R_{2u}^{n},

\displaystyle s_{h,e}(t_{n+1})

with the truncation terms

and

is independent of
{\tau}
.

The error functions
e_{u}^{n}
and
e_{s}^{n}
are defined by (
25

In addition, we define

\widetilde{e}_{u}^{n+1}=\widetilde{u}^{n+1}-u_{h,e}(t_{n+1}),\qquad\widetilde{e}_{s}^{n+1}=\widetilde{s}^{n+1}-s_{h,e}(t_{n+1}).

We first present a lemma on the estimates with respect to
\widetilde{e}_{u}^{n+1}
and
\widetilde{e}_{s}^{n+1}
.
The proof is similar to that of Theorem
10

and will be given in Appendix

Lemma 16
.

Given any fixed
h>0
and
T>0

Theorem 17

(Temporal error estimate of GSAV-EI2)
.

Given any fixed
h>0
and
T>0
, and let
\kappa\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{C[-\beta,\beta]}
.
Assume that the exact solution
u_{h,e}
is smooth enough on
[0,T]
and
\|u_{\text{\rm init}}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
.
If
{\tau}
is sufficiently small, then we have the following error estimate
for the GSAV-EI2 scheme (
42
):

(46)

\|u^{n}-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\|+|s^{n}-s_{h,e}(t_{n})|\leq C_{h,2}{\tau}^{2},\quad 0\leq n\leq\lfloor T/{\tau}\rfloor,

where the constant
C_{h,2}>0
is independent of
{\tau}
.

Proof.

The difference between (
42a
) and (
43a
) gives

e_{u}^{n+1}=\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}e_{u}^{n}+{\tau}\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})[N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})]-{\tau}R_{2u}^{n}.

Acting
I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
on both sides of the above equation, we obtain

(47)

\displaystyle(1+{\tau}\kappa g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}))(e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n})-{\tau}\varepsilon^{2}(\Delta_{h}e_{u}^{n+1}-\Delta_{h}e_{u}^{n})

\displaystyle\quad={\tau}(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})[N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})]

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})(\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}-I)e_{u}^{n}-{\tau}(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})R_{2u}^{n}.

Taking the discrete inner product of (
47
) with
\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}
, similarly to (
29
),
we estimate the left-hand side (LHS) of the above identity as

LHS

\displaystyle={\tau}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\kappa g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla_{h}e_{u}^{n+1}-\nabla_{h}e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{\tau}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\kappa\widehat{G}_{*}\|e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle={\tau}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}-2\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa{\tau}\langle e_{u}^{n},\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{7{\tau}}{8}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}-8\widehat{G}_{*}^{2}\kappa^{2}{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2},

and, when
{\tau}\leq M_{h}^{-1}
, we estimate the right-hand side (RHS) of (
47
) as

RHS

\displaystyle={\tau}\langle(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\phi_{1}(-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})[N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})],\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})(\mathrm{e}^{-{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}-I)e_{u}^{n},\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle-{\tau}\langle(I+{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})R_{2u}^{n},\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2{\tau}\|N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\quad+2M_{h}{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|+2{\tau}\|R_{2u}^{n}\|\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq 8{\tau}\|N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+8M_{h}^{2}{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+8{\tau}\|R_{2u}^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{3{\tau}}{8}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\qquad=g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})f(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-g(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})f(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})+\kappa g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\widetilde{e}_{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},

and
\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
are all bounded uniformly
according to the energy dissipation law and the MBP.
Similar to the proof of Lemma
9
, we can obtain

(48)

\displaystyle\|N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C_{g}+G^{*}\kappa}{2}(\|e_{u}^{n}\|+\|\widetilde{e}_{u}^{n+1}\|)+\frac{C_{g}}{2}(|e_{s}^{n}|+|\widetilde{e}_{s}^{n+1}|).

Combining (
48
) with estimates of the LHS and RHS of (
47
), we get

(49)

\displaystyle\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\widehat{G}_{*}\kappa\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{{\tau}}{2}\|\delta_{t}e_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq[8(C_{g}+G^{*}\kappa)^{2}+8M_{h}^{2}+8\widehat{G}_{*}^{2}\kappa^{2}]{\tau}\|e_{u}^{n}\|^{2}+8(C_{g}+G^{*}\kappa)^{2}{\tau}\|\widetilde{e}_{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+8C_{g}^{2}{\tau}(|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}+|\widetilde{e}_{s}^{n+1}|^{2})+8{\tau}\|R_{2u}^{n}\|^{2}.

For the error equation (
43b
), we can add a zero-value term to the right-hand side to give

(50)

\displaystyle s_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-s_{h,e}(t_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-g(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle f(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{2}g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\langle u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n+1}),u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\rangle+{\tau}R_{2s}^{n}.

The difference between (
42b
) and (
50
) leads to

\displaystyle e_{s}^{n+1}-e_{s}^{n}

\displaystyle=\big\langle g(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})f(\widetilde{u}_{h,e}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})f(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa}{2}g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle u^{n+1}-\widetilde{u}^{n+1},e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle f(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),e_{u}^{n+1}-e_{u}^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa}{2}g(\widetilde{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{s}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\langle e_{u}^{n+1}-\widetilde{e}_{u}^{n+1},u_{h,e}(t_{n+1})-u_{h,e}(t_{n})\rangle-{\tau}R_{2s}^{n}.

Multiplying the above equation by

\displaystyle|e_{s}^{n+1}|^{2}-|e_{s}^{n}|^{2}+|e_{s}^{n+1}-e_{s}^{n}|^{2}

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we study the numerical schemes preserving both the energy dissipation law and the MBP unconditionally
for a class of Allen–Cahn type gradient flows
by combining the exponential integrator method and the generalized SAV approach.
With the appropriate stabilization terms,
we develop first- and second-order GSAV-EI schemes
and prove their unconditional preservation of the energy dissipation law and the MBP in the time discrete sense,
as well as their optimal temporal error estimates under fixed spatial mesh.
Different from most existing numerical schemes,
the energy dissipation law and the MBP of the proposed GSAV-EI schemes can be established in parallel,
which provides more flexibility to apply the proposed schemes to other types of gradient flow equations
to preserve some important physical properties. We also note that the fully-discrete error estimate for the case that
the spatial mesh size and the time step size change simultaneously is still
an open question for the proposed GSAV-EI schemes and surely worthy of further study.
A major difficulty comes from the issue that
the matrix exponential
\mathrm{e}^{{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}}
, defined by a power series of the sparse matrix
{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n}
,
is dense and affects the solution globally.
In particular, to estimate the temporal truncation error of the GSAV-EI2 scheme (Lemma
15
),
an
h
-dependent bound is inevitable, and thus we fix the spatial mesh size to regard such bound as a constant
in this paper.

When constructing the second-order GSAV-EI scheme (
42
),
we approximate the term
N_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(u_{h}(t_{n}+\theta),s_{h}(t_{n}+\theta))
in (
40
)
by its value at the midpoint
\theta=\frac{{\tau}}{2}

rather than its linear interpolation in
[0,{\tau}]
.
This allows the cancellation between the nonlinear terms
in the analysis of the energy dissipation (Theorem
11
).
Instead, if we adopt the linear interpolation as usually done for the RK2 method,
two terms involving the numerical solutions at
t_{n}
and
t_{n+1}
will be included
with the
\phi
-functions of
{\tau}L_{\kappa}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
as the coefficients,
which makes the cancellation unavailable due to the different coefficients
between the updating formula for
u^{n+1}
and that for
s^{n+1}
.
For the similar reason, it is an open question whether higher-order GSAV-EI schemes exist
in either RK or multistep form, although there have been third-order multistep schemes based on the standard ETD method
for the epitaxial thin film model
[
4
,
6
]
.

It also remains interesting on how to choose the function
\sigma
appropriately for the GSAV-EI schemes in practical applications.
As we explain in Remark
4.1
,
we only use the exponential function (
56
) in Section
4

since the differences can hardly be observed for the typical choices of
\sigma
given in Remark
3.1

for the specific problems we consider in the numerical experiments.
However, their performance could be significantly different for some other situations and gradient flows, and
more careful investigation is needed. In addition, the effect of the parameter
a
on the numerical errors seems completely
opposite for the smooth and non-smooth initial data based on our observation from numerical experiments, and
such phenomenon also deserves deeper study.

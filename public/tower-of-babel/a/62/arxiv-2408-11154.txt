Tight entropy bound based on p-quasinorms.
Juan Pablo Lopez

Abstract
In the present paper we prove a family of tight upper and lower bounds for the Shannon entropy and von Neumann entropy based on the p-norms. This allows us to have an entropy estimate, a criterion for the finiteness of it and a bound on the difference of entropy, additionally, we did some numerical tests that show the efficiency of our approximations.



Keywords:
Entropy, Estimate, Bound.

2
Logarithm inequality

In this section we present a family of inequalities that allow us to bound the entropy, additionally we find the tight forms of these inequalities.

Proposition 1
.

Let
x\in[0,1]
, then there exists some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

C_{1}\left(1-x^{1-\sigma}\right)\leq-\text{log}\left(x\right)\leq C_{2}\left(\frac{1}{x^{1-\sigma}}-1\right),

(4)

for every
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Moreover, the best choice of constants is

C_{1}=C_{2}=\frac{1}{\text{ln}(2)(1-\sigma)}.

Proof.

Consider the quotients

l(x)=\frac{-\text{log}\left(x\right)}{1-x^{1-\sigma}},

(5)

s(x)=\frac{-\text{log}\left(x\right)}{x^{\sigma-1}-1},

(6)

for
x\in(0,1]
. We will prove that
l(x)
and
s(x)
are decreasing and increasing, respectively, this allows us to set a minimum and maximum at x=1.

Note that these are differentiable functions on
(0,1]
and we only need to prove that
l^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
and
s^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
, respectively.

The inequality
l^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
is equivalent to

x^{1-\sigma}(1-\text{ln}(x)(1-\sigma))\leq 1.

(7)

Consider the function
\tilde{l}(x)=x^{1-\sigma}(1-\text{ln}(x)(1-\sigma))
, this function is increasing on
(0,1]
because we easily see that
\tilde{l}^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
. Now we conclude that the maximum of
\tilde{l}(x)
is at
x=1
, which is precisely 1, proving (
7
).

On the other hand, the inequality
s^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
is equivalent to

x^{\sigma-1}(1+\text{ln}(x)(1-\sigma))\leq 1.

(8)

Let
\tilde{s}(x)=x^{\sigma-1}(1+\text{ln}(x)(1-\sigma))
, this function is increasing on
(0,1]
because
\tilde{s}^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
, then the maximum of
\tilde{s}(x)
is at
x=1
, which is precisely 1, proving (
8
).

Finally, the fact that
l(x)
and
s(x)
are decreasing and increasing, respectively, allows us to find the best constants as

C_{1}=\lim_{x\to 1}\frac{-\text{log}\left(x\right)}{1-x^{1-\sigma}}=\frac{1}{\text{ln}(2)(1-\sigma)},

(9)

C_{2}=\lim_{x\to 1}\frac{-\text{log}\left(x\right)}{x^{\sigma-1}-1}=\frac{1}{\text{ln}(2)(1-\sigma)},

(10)

∎

Now we have an infinite amount of upper and lower bounds for the entropy, but which is the best? This question will be answered in the following proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\sigma,\tilde{\sigma}\in(0,1)
, if
\sigma<\tilde{\sigma}
then

\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{x^{1-\sigma}}-1\right)\geq\frac{1}{1-\tilde{\sigma}}\left(\frac{1}{x^{1-\tilde{\sigma}}}-1\right),

(11)

\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(1-x^{1-\sigma}\right)\leq\frac{1}{1-\tilde{\sigma}}\left(1-x^{1-\tilde{\sigma}}\right),

(12)

for all
x\in[0,1]
. Additionally, we have that

\lim_{\sigma\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{x^{1-\sigma}}-1\right)=-\text{ln}(x),

(13)

\lim_{\sigma\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(1-x^{1-\sigma}\right)=-\text{ln}(x),

(14)

Proof.

Let
f(\sigma)=\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{x^{1-\sigma}}-1\right)
,
f(\sigma)
is differentiable on [0,1], then we only need to verify that
f^{\prime}(\sigma)\leq 0
. The inequality
f^{\prime}(\sigma)\leq 0
becomes

\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{x^{1-\sigma}}-1\right)+\frac{\text{ln}(x)x^{\sigma-1}}{1-\sigma}\leq 0,

which is equivalent to left side of (
4
).

Let
g(\sigma)=\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(1-x^{1-\sigma}\right)
, the inequality
g^{\prime}(\sigma)\geq 0
becomes

\frac{1-x^{1-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)^{2}}+\frac{\text{ln}(x)x^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma}\geq 0,

which is equivalent to right side of (
4
). The limits are simply an application of the L’Hôpital’s rule
[
11
]
.
∎

The Proposition
2
guaranties that if
\sigma\to 1^{-}
we obtain a better inequalities. In the next section we use the inequality (
4
) to bound and estimate the Shannon entropy.

3
Entropy bound

Using (
4
) we can formulate the inequality

\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}\left(x-x^{2-\sigma}\right)\leq-x\text{log}\left(x\right)\leq\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}\left(x^{\sigma}-x\right),

(15)

for all
x\in[0,1]
and every
\sigma\in(0,1)
. We are ready to obtain our bound for the entropy in the following proposition.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\textbf{p}=(p_{1},...,p_{n})
be a discrete probability distribution and
\sigma\in(0,1)
, then

\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(1-||\textbf{p}||_{2-\sigma})\leq S(\textbf{p})\leq\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(||\textbf{p}||_{\sigma}-1).

(16)

If
p
is an infinite discrete distribution, we have that
\textbf{p}\in\ell^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
implies

\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(1-||\textbf{p}||_{2-\sigma})\leq S(\textbf{p})\leq\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(||\textbf{p}||_{\sigma}-1),

(17)

this inequality provides the same criterion for the finiteness of the entropy presented in
[
10
]
. Moreover, the inequalities (
16
) and (
17
) are tight for every
\sigma\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Using (
15
) in the sum of the terms
p_{i}\text{log}(p_{i})
and the fact that
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
to deduce our inequalities. In the case of the infinite discrete distribution we need to ensure that
\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\sigma}<\infty
, that is why we add the condition
\textbf{p}\in\ell^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
.

If we set
\textbf{p}=(1,0,0,..)
we have that
||\textbf{p}||_{2-\sigma}=||\textbf{p}||_{\sigma})=1
and
S(\textbf{p})=0
, then our inequalities become an equalities for every .
∎

We need to guarantee that our estimates are good enough, here below we will prove that our bounds get closer as
\sigma\to 1^{-}
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
p
be a discrete probability distribution such that
\textbf{p}\in\ell^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
, then

\left|S(\textbf{p})-\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(||\textbf{p}||_{\sigma}-1)\right|\leq\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(||\textbf{p}||_{\sigma}+||\textbf{p}||_{2-\sigma}-2),

(18)

\left|S(\textbf{p})-\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(1-||\textbf{p}||_{2-\sigma})\right|\leq\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(||\textbf{p}||_{\sigma}+||\textbf{p}||_{2-\sigma}-2).

(19)

Corollary 5
.

Let
\textbf{p},\textbf{q}
be a discrete probability distributions such that
\textbf{p},\textbf{q}\in\ell^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
S(\textbf{p})\geq S(\textbf{q})
, then

S(\textbf{p})-S(\textbf{q})\leq\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(||\textbf{p}||_{\sigma}+||\textbf{q}||_{2-\sigma}-2).

(20)

Moreover, there exists a
\sigma\in(0,1)
such that

S(\textbf{p})-S(\textbf{q})\geq\frac{1}{ln(2)(1-\sigma)}(2-||\textbf{p}||_{2-\sigma}-||\textbf{q}||_{\sigma}).

(21)

The inequality (
21
) can not be formulated for all
\sigma\in(0,1)
because is possible that the upper bound of
S(\textbf{q})
is greater than
S(\textbf{p})
. But as our bounds are close enough to the entropy with
\sigma\to 1^{-}
we can ensure that for some
\sigma
the upper bound of
S(\textbf{q})
is greater than
S(\textbf{p})
is smaller than
S(\textbf{p})
.

5
Conclusions

We obtained a new but simple bounds for entropy, that can be used in infinite dimensions and for the von Neumann entropy. These bounds give an explicit relation between the entropy and the p-norms, in addition these bounds could be close enough to be used as approximations. Our finiteness criterion for the entropy coincides with one given in
[
10
]
. Additionally, we conjecture that our bounds on the entropy difference could be improved to some form of uniform continuity bounds.

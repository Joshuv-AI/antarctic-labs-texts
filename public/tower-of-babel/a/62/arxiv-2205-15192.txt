1.
Introduction

A prominent open problem in arithmetic geometry, formulated by S. Lang and H. Trotter in the 1970s
(
[
LaTr76
, Part I]
),
concerns the distribution of the Frobenius traces associated to the reductions modulo primes of an elliptic curve defined over

\mathbb{Q}
and without complex multiplication.
In recent years, this problem has been formulated in broader settings, such as that of abelian varieties
(e.g.,
[
CoDaSiSt17
]
,
[
ChJoSe20
]
, and
[
Ka09
]
).
The goal of the present article is to provide upper bounds related to the distribution of the Frobenius traces defined by the product of non-isogenous
elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and having no complex multiplication, as explained below.

Let
g\geq 1
be an integer
and
let
A/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian variety that is
isogenous over
\mathbb{Q}
to
a product of
g
elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
pairwise
non-isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

and
each without complex multiplication.
Denote by
N_{A}
the conductor of
A
.
For a prime
p\nmid N_{A}
,
we write the characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius endomorphism
acting on the reduction of
A
modulo
p
as

P_{A,p}(X)=X^{2g}+a_{1,p}(A)X^{2g-1}+\ldots+a_{2g-1,p}(A)X+a_{2g,p}(A)\in\mathbb{Z}[X].

Then, for a fixed integer
t
, we study the counting function

\pi_{A}(x,t):=\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{A},a_{1,p}(A)=t\right\}.

When
g=1
,
the asymptotic behavior of
\pi_{A}(x,t)
is predicted by the Lang-Trotter Conjecture on Frobenius traces (see
[
LaTr76
, Part I, p. 33]
for the original and
[
BaJo09
, Conjectures 2-3, p. 435]
for refined versions).
Specifically, in
[
LaTr76
]
, it is conjectured that
there exists an explicit non-negative constant
C(A,t)
,
which depends on the arithmetic of
A
and
t
, such that, as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\pi_{A}(x,t)\sim C(A,t)\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\operatorname{log}x}.

When
g=2
and
t\neq 0
, a similar asymptotic formula is predicted by a conjecture of H. Chen, N. Jones and V. Serban (see
[
ChJoSe20
, Conjecture 1.0.5, p. 3]
).
An analogous asymptotic behavior is expected to hold for arbitrary
t
and
g
in our given setting.

When
g=1
, M.R. Murty, V.K. Murty and N. Saradha
[
MuMuSa88
, Theorem, p. 254]
built on the work of
J-P. Serre
[
Se81
, Section 8, pp. 188-191]

and
proved that, under the assumption of a Generalized Riemann Hypothesis,

\pi_{A}(x,0)\ll_{A}x^{\frac{3}{4}}

and

\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x,t)\ll_{A}\frac{x^{\frac{4}{5}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{1}{5}}}
if
t\neq 0
.
More recently, D. Zywina
[
Zy15
, Theorem 1.2, p. 236]
obtained
the improvements

\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x,0)\ll_{A}\frac{x^{\frac{3}{4}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

and

\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x,t)\ll_{A}\frac{x^{\frac{4}{5}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{3}{5}}}
if
t\neq 0
.
The bound

\pi_{A}(x,0)\ll_{A}x^{\frac{3}{4}}

was also proven
unconditionally
using
the interpretation of the condition

a_{1,p}(A)=0
as
p
being a supersingular prime for the elliptic curve
A

(see
[
El91
, Theorem B, p. 131]
).
When
t\neq 0
, only the bound
\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x,t)\ll_{A}\frac{x(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{2}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{2}}
is known unconditionally,
thanks to the work of J. Thorner and A. Zaman
[
ThZa17
, Theorem 1.4, p. 4997]
,
who built on prior results of
J-P. Serre
[
Se81
, Section 8, pp. 188-191]
,
D. Wan
[
Wan90
, Theorem 1.3, p. 250]
,
and
V.K. Murty
[
Mu97
, Theorem 5.1, p. 302]
.

When
g=2
and
t\neq 0
,
H. Chen, N. Jones and V. Serban
[
ChJoSe20
, Theorem 2.4.1, p. 14]
proved that,
under the assumption of a Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x,t)\ll_{A}\frac{x^{\frac{13}{14}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{5}{7}}}
;
unconditionally,
they proved that

\displaystyle\pi_{A}(x,t)\ll_{A}\frac{x(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{2}{7}}(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{1}{7}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{8}{7}}}
.

In this paper, we prove conditional upper bounds
for
\pi_{A}(x,t)

that largely improve upon the conditional bound of H. Chen, N. Jones, and V. Serban when
g=2
,
and recover the conditional bound of M.R. Murty, V.K. Murty and N. Saradha and D. Zywina when
g=1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{Z}

and
let
A/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian variety that is isogenous over
\mathbb{Q}
to
a product of
g
elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
pairwise non-isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

and
each without complex multiplication.
Under the assumption
of Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for Dedekind zeta functions,
we have that, for any sufficiently large
x
,

\pi_{A}(x,t)\ll_{A}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\frac{x^{1-\frac{1}{3g+1}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{1-\frac{2}{3g+1}}}&\text{if $t=0$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{x^{1-\frac{1}{3g+2}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{1-\frac{2}{3g+2}}}&\text{if $t\neq 0$.}\end{array}\right.

As an immediate application of Theorem
1
,
we obtain a result about the non-lacunarity of the sequence
(a_{1,p}(A))_{p}
,
that is, about the non-zero values of
a_{1,p}(A)
.
In fact, we obtain a result about any fixed value of
a_{1,p}(A)
.

Corollary 2
.

In the setting and under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, we have that,
as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

(1)

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{A},a_{1,p}(A)\neq t\right\}\sim\pi(x).

As another quick application of Theorem
1
, we obtain
the existence of a density one set of primes
p
with large Frobenius traces
a_{1,p}(A)
.
Namely, in the setting and under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, we have that,
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

(2)

\hskip-14.22636pt\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{A},|a_{1,p}(A)|>p^{\frac{1}{3g+2}-\varepsilon}\right\}\sim\pi(x).

Without applying Theorem
1
directly, but instead
using a proof strategy similar to that of the proof of Theorem
1
,
we can improve upon (
2
) to show the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian variety that is isogenous over
\mathbb{Q}
to
a product of
g
elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
pairwise non-isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

and
each without complex multiplication.
Under the assumption
of Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for Dedekind zeta functions,
we have that,
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{A},|a_{1,p}(A)|>p^{\frac{1}{3g+1}-\varepsilon}\right\}\sim\pi(x).

The above results may be re-written solely in terms of a
g
-tuple of
elliptic curves
E_{1}/\mathbb{Q},\ldots,E_{g}/\mathbb{Q}
,
assumed to be pairwise non-isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

and each without complex multiplication,
as follows.
Denote by
N_{E_{1}},\ldots,N_{E_{g}}
the conductors of
E_{1},\ldots,E_{g}
, respectively.
For each integer
1\leq i\leq g
and for each prime
p\nmid N_{E_{i}}
,
denote by
a_{p}(E_{i})
the integer defined by

|\overline{E}_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p+1-a_{p}(E_{i})
,
where

\overline{E}_{i}
is the
reduction of
E_{i}
modulo
p
.
Taking
A:=E_{1}\times\ldots\times E_{g}
,
we have that
a_{1,p}(A)=-(a_{p}(E_{1})+\ldots+a_{p}(E_{g}))

for any prime
p\nmid N_{E_{1}}\ldots N_{E_{g}}
(to be explained in Section 6).
Now let
t\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then equivalent formulations of
Theorem
1
, Corollary
2
, and Theorem
3

are that,
under the assumption
of GRH,
for any sufficiently large
x
, we have

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{E_{1}}\ldots N_{E_{g}},a_{p}(E_{1})+\ldots+a_{p}(E_{g})=t\right\}\ll_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{g}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\frac{x^{1-\frac{1}{3g+1}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{1-\frac{2}{3g+1}}}&\text{ {\emph{if}} $t=0$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{x^{1-\frac{1}{3g+2}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{1-\frac{2}{3g+2}}}&\text{ {\emph{if}} $t\neq 0$,}\end{array}\right.

and
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
as
x\rightarrow\infty
,
we have

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{E_{1}}\ldots N_{E_{g}},a_{p}(E_{1})+\ldots+a_{p}(E_{g})\neq t\right\}\sim\pi(x),

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{E_{1}}\ldots N_{E_{g}},\left|a_{p}(E_{1})+\ldots+a_{p}(E_{g})\right|>p^{\frac{1}{3g+1}-\varepsilon}\right\}\sim\pi(x).

The general strategy of proving
an upper bound for
\pi_{A}(x,t)

is to relax the equality
a_{1,p}(A)=t
to
a congruence
\operatorname{tr}\bar{\rho}_{A,m}\left(\left(\frac{\mathbb{Q}(A[m])/\mathbb{Q}}{p}\right)\right)\equiv-t(\operatorname{mod}m)
,
where

m
is an arbitrary integer coprime to
p
,

\bar{\rho}_{A,m}
is the Galois representation associated to
the
m
-division field
\mathbb{Q}(A[m])
of
A
,
and

\left(\frac{\mathbb{Q}(A[m])/\mathbb{Q}}{p}\right)

is the Artin symbol at
p
in the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(A[m])/\mathbb{Q})
.
An upper bound for
\pi_{A}(x,t)

may then be derived by interpreting this congruence as a Chebotarev condition
in the finite Galois extension
\mathbb{Q}(A[m])/\mathbb{Q}
and by invoking an effective version of the Chebotarev Density Theorem.

Inspired by
[
MuMuSa88
]
,
we pursue a refinement of this general strategy
and relax the equality
a_{1,p}(A)=t
to a Chebotarev condition that holds in a suitably chosen proper subextension of
\mathbb{Q}(A[m])/\mathbb{Q}
in which Artin’s Holomorphy Conjecture is known to hold.
When
g\geq 2
, we unravel a few such possible subextensions and carry out the refined strategy in the subextension that leads to the best result, in particular the subextension that leads to a generalization
of the best upper bounds known for
g=1
.

When studying the Frobenius traces of an elliptic curve, it is natural to place a special focus on
elliptic curves
A/\mathbb{Q}
having the property that

\operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}(A)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, since this case is regarded as generic.
When studying the Frobenius traces of an abelian variety
A
of higher dimension,
it is then natural to place an initial focus on the following two cases:
that of an abelian variety
A/\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}(A)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, as pursued in
[
CoDaSiSt17
]
and in an upcoming paper by the present authors,
and
that of an abelian variety
that is
\mathbb{Q}
-isogenous
to a product of
g
elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
pairwise non-isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

and
each without complex multiplication,
as pursued in the present paper.
Note that, when
A/\mathbb{Q}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-isogenous
to a product of
g
elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}

that are pairwise isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, the study of
\pi_{A}(x,t)
for
g\geq 2
reduces to the study of

\pi_{A}(x,t)
for
g=1
. Other complementary cases remain to be pursued separately.

Finally, let us note that other variations of questions inspired by the Lang-Trotter Conjecture have been investigated in the setting of pairs of elliptic curves in works such as

[
FoMu95
]
,
[
AkDaJu04
]
, and
[
AkPa19
]
.

2.
The Chebotarev Density Theorem

The proofs of Theorems
1
and
3
are based on multi-step
applications of different effective versions of the Chebotarev Density Theorem, which we now recall.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields, with
G:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, and let

{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq G
be a union of conjugacy classes of
G
; throughout the paper, we will assume
{\mathcal{C}}\neq\emptyset
.
We denote by
[L:K]
the degree of
L
over
K
and by
\operatorname{disc}(L/K)\unlhd{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
the discriminant ideal of
L/K
.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in{\sum}_{K}
,
we denote
by
\wp\in{\sum}_{L}
an arbitrary prime ideal with
\wp\mid\mathfrak{p}
,
by
{\mathcal{D}}_{\wp}
the decomposition group of
\wp
in
L/K
,
and
by
{\mathcal{I}}_{\wp}
the inertia group of
\wp
in
L/K
.
We denote
by
\left(\frac{L/K}{\wp}\right)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\wp}/{\mathcal{I}}_{\wp}
the Artin symbol associated to
\wp
,
and
by

\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right):=\left\{\gamma\in G:\exists\wp\mid\mathfrak{p}\text{ such that }\gamma\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\wp}\ \text{and}\ \gamma{\mathcal{I}}_{\wp}=\left(\frac{L/K}{\wp}\right)\right\}

the Artin symbol associated to
\mathfrak{p}

in the extension
L/K
.
Notice that, for any integer
m\geq 1
,
the set

\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{m}:=\left\{\gamma^{m}:\gamma\in\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\right\}\subseteq G

is invariant under conjugation by elements of
G
.
We set

{\mathcal{P}}(L/K):=\{p:\>\ \exists\ \mathfrak{p}\in{\sum}_{K}\ \text{such that}\ \mathfrak{p}\mid p\ \text{and}\ \mathfrak{p}\mid\operatorname{disc}(L/K)\},

M(L/K):=2[L:K]|d_{K}|^{\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\displaystyle\prod_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}(L/K)}p,

and recall that

(3)

\operatorname{log}\left|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{disc}(L/K))\right|\leq([L:\mathbb{Q}]-[K:\mathbb{Q}])\left(\displaystyle\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}(L/K)}\operatorname{log}p\right)+[L:\mathbb{Q}]\operatorname{log}[L:K]

(see
[
Se81
, Proposition 5, p. 129]
).

The extension
L/K
is said to satisfy the Artin Holomorphy Conjecture (AHC) if,
for any irreducible character
\chi:G\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
the Artin
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
extends to a function that is analytic on
\mathbb{C}
when
\chi\neq 1

and that is analytic on
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{1\}
when
\chi=1
. It is known that AHC holds if
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
is abelian (
[
Ar37
]
).

We denote by
\delta_{\mathcal{C}}:G\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
the characteristic function on
{\mathcal{C}}
.
For any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in{\sum}_{K}
,
we choose an arbitrary prime ideal
\wp\in{\sum}_{L}
with
\wp\mid\mathfrak{p}
,
and then,
for any integer
m\geq 1
,
we define

\delta_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{m}\right):=\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{I}}_{\wp}|}\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\wp}\atop{\gamma{\mathcal{I}}_{\wp}=\left(\frac{L/K}{\wp}\right)^{m}}}\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\gamma).

Note that this definition is independent of the choice of
\wp
.
For an arbitrary real number
x>0
, we define

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in{\sum}_{K}\atop{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\operatorname{disc}(L/K)\atop{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})\leq x}}}\delta_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in{\sum}_{K}\atop{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\leq x}}\delta_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)^{m}\right).

For simplicity of notation,
we use

\pi_{1}(x,L/K)

and

\widetilde{\pi}_{1}(x,L/K)

to denote

\pi_{\{\operatorname{id}_{L}\}}(x,L/K)

and

\widetilde{\pi}_{\{\operatorname{id}_{L}\}}(x,L/K)
, respectively.

The Chebotarev Density Theorem states that, as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

(4)

\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)\sim\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}|}{|G|}\operatorname{li}x.

Since the growth in
x
of the counting function
\widetilde{\pi}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)
is closely related to that of
\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)

thanks to the bound

(5)

\left|\widetilde{\pi}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)-\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)\right|\ll[K:\mathbb{Q}]\left(\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\operatorname{log}x}+\operatorname{log}M(L/K)\right)

(see
[
Se81
, Proposition 7, p. 138]
),
asymptotic (
4
) is equivalent to

(6)

\widetilde{\pi}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)\sim\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}|}{|G|}\operatorname{li}x.

The advantage of considering
\widetilde{\pi}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)
over
\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L/K)
is that the former satisfies the following two functorial properties.

Proposition 4
.

Let

be a Galois extension of number fields with
G:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
and let

be a union of conjugacy classes of

(i)

Let

be a subgroup of
G
such that
every element of
{\mathcal{C}}
is conjugate to some element of
H
.
Denote by

the subfield of
L
fixed by
H

Then, for any

(ii)

Let

be a normal subgroup of
G

Denote by

the subfield of

Denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
the image of
{\mathcal{C}}
in the quotient group

Then, for any

See
[

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with

Lemma 9

Let
{\mathcal{S}}

\mathbb{Q}

Assume that

such that

(8)

\displaystyle[K_{p}:\mathbb{Q}]\leq c_{1}\quad\text{for any sufficiently large}\ p,

(9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{log}|d_{K_{p}}|\leq f(z)\quad\text{for any sufficiently large }z\text{ and all }p\leq z.

For each

,

be such that

(10)

u\leq y,

and assume that, for any

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x)}{(\operatorname{log}y)^{1+\varepsilon}}=0.

Assume GRH for Dedekind zeta functions. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists a constant
c(\varepsilon)>0
such that, for any sufficiently large
x
,

(13)

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}\leq c(\varepsilon)\displaystyle\max_{y\leq\ell\leq y+u}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}},\ell\nmid d_{K_{p}},\left(\frac{K_{p}/\mathbb{Q}}{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{p}\right\}.

The proof follows the strategy of
[
MuMuSa88
, Lemma 4.4, p. 269]
, adapted to our general setting.
We start by fixing
x>2
,
y=y(x)>2
,
u=u(x)>2
, and
\varepsilon>0
such that (
10
) - (
12
) hold.
Then, we observe that

(14)

\displaystyle(\pi(y+u)-\pi(y))\displaystyle\max_{y\leq\ell\leq y+u}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}},\ell\nmid d_{K_{p}},\left(\frac{K_{p}/\mathbb{Q}}{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{y\leq\ell\leq y+u}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}},\ell\nmid d_{K_{p}},\left(\frac{K_{p}/\mathbb{Q}}{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x\atop{p\in{\mathcal{S}}}}\#\left\{y\leq\ell\leq y+u:\ell\nmid d_{K_{p}},\left(\frac{K_{p}/\mathbb{Q}}{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x\atop{p\in{\mathcal{S}}}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{C}_{p}}(y+u,K_{p}/\mathbb{Q})-\pi_{\mathcal{C}_{p}}(y,K_{p}/\mathbb{Q})\right).

under GRH and deduce that

(15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x\atop{p\in{\mathcal{S}}}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{C}_{p}}(y+u,K_{p}/\mathbb{Q})-\pi_{\mathcal{C}_{p}}(y,K_{p}/\mathbb{Q})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x\atop{p\in{\mathcal{S}}}}\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{p}|}{[K_{p}:\mathbb{Q}]}(\pi(y+u)-\pi(y))+\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x\atop{p\in{\mathcal{S}}}}\left(E_{1}(y,u,\mathcal{C}_{p},K_{p})+E_{2}(y,u,\mathcal{C}_{p},K_{p})\right)

for some real-valued functions

,

, which depend on
y
,

,
\mathcal{C}_{p}
, and
K_{p}
,
and for which there exist absolute constants
c_{3}>0
and

Recalling assumptions (
8
) - (
9

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x\atop{p\in{\mathcal{S}}}}\left(E_{1}(y,u,\mathcal{C}_{p},K_{p})+E_{2}(y,u,\mathcal{C}_{p},K_{p})\right)\right|

\displaystyle c_{5}(y+u)^{\frac{1}{2}}(f(x)+\operatorname{log}(y+u))\ \#\left\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}

for some absolute constant
c_{5}>0
.

Thanks to
(
11
)
and the assumption of GRH (specifically, RH in this case), there exists some constant

(18)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max_{y\leq\ell\leq y+u}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}},\ell\nmid d_{K_{p}},\left(\frac{K_{p}/\mathbb{Q}}{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x\atop{p\in{\mathcal{S}}}}\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{p}|}{[K_{p}:\mathbb{Q}]}+\operatorname{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\operatorname{log}u}{u}(y+u)^{\frac{1}{2}}(f(x)+\operatorname{log}(y+u))\ \#\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}}\}\right).

\displaystyle\max_{y\leq\ell\leq y+u}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}},\ell\nmid d_{K_{p}},\left(\frac{K_{p}/\mathbb{Q}}{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{p}\right\}\gg_{\varepsilon}\#\{p\leq x:p\in{\mathcal{S}}\},

4.
Subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{g}

Our main contribution to generalizing the method of M.R. Murty, V.K. Murty, and N. Saradha
[
MuMuSa88
]
to the case of a product
of
g
non-isogenous elliptic curves without complex multiplication
consists of
unravelling suitable Galois extensions
L/K
of number fields with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)<\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{g}
for some well-chosen prime
\ell
,
and
of unravelling suitable conjugacy classes
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)

for which we can invoke the version of the Chebotarev Density Theorem given in
Corollary
8
.
With this goal in mind, we devote Section 4 to investigations of particular subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{g}
.

For a fixed rational prime
\ell
, our main focus will be on the subgroup

(19)

G(\ell):=\{(M_{1},\ldots,M_{g})\in\mathbb{G}(\ell):\operatorname{det}M_{1}=\ldots=\operatorname{det}M_{g}\}

of the product group

\mathbb{G}(\ell):=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell)^{g},

where

\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell):=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}).

Denoting
by
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}(\ell)
the multiplicative group
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

and
by
{\mathcal{G}}_{a}(\ell)
the additive group
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
,
we set

\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
0&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell):a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{m}(\ell),b_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{a}(\ell)\right\},

\mathcal{U}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell):b\in\mathcal{G}_{a}(\ell)\right\},

\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0\\
0&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell):a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{m}(\ell)\right\},

B(\ell):=\{(M_{1},\ldots,M_{g})\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}:\operatorname{det}M_{1}=\ldots=\operatorname{det}M_{g}\},

U(\ell):=\mathcal{U}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g},

U^{\prime}(\ell):=(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\cdot U(\ell),

T(\ell):=\{(M_{1},\ldots,M_{g})\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}:\operatorname{det}M_{1}=\ldots=\operatorname{det}M_{g}\}.

The following lemmas will be crucial in the proof of our main theorems.

Lemma 11
.

For any prime
\ell
,
we have that

U(\ell)
and
U^{\prime}(\ell)
are normal subgroups of
B(\ell)
,
and that
B(\ell)/U(\ell)
are
B(\ell)/U^{\prime}(\ell)
are abelian groups.

Proof.

The subgroup
\mathcal{U}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)
is normal in
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)
,
and
the quotient
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)/\mathcal{U}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)
is abelian, since
it satisfies a group isomorphism

\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)/\mathcal{U}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)
.
Consequently, the subgroup
U(\ell)
is normal in
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}
, with

\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}/U(\ell)

an abelian group. By the inclusion of subgroups

U(\ell)\subseteq U^{\prime}(\ell)\subseteq B(\ell)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g},

we deduce
that
both
U(\ell)
and
U^{\prime}(\ell)
are normal subgroups in
B(\ell)

and that
both
B(\ell)/U(\ell)
and
B(\ell)/U^{\prime}(\ell)
are abelian.
∎

Lemma 12
.

For any prime
\ell
, we have

\displaystyle|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell-1)\ell(\ell^{2}-1),

\displaystyle|\mathbb{G}(\ell)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell-1)^{g}\ell^{g}(\ell^{2}-1)^{g},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell-1)^{2g}\ell^{g},

\displaystyle|G(\ell)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell-1)\ell^{g}(\ell^{2}-1)^{g},

\displaystyle|B(\ell)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell-1)^{g+1}\ell^{g},

\displaystyle|U(\ell)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{g},

\displaystyle\left|U^{\prime}(\ell)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell-1)\ell^{g},

\displaystyle|T(\ell)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell-1)^{g+1}.

Proof.

The formula for the order of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell)
is well-known and easy to derive.
The formulae for the orders of

\mathbb{G}(\ell)
,
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}
,
U(\ell)
,
U^{\prime}(\ell)
, and
T(\ell)
are clear from the definitions of the groups.
To find the orders of
G(\ell)
and
B(\ell)
, observe that there are short exact sequences of finite groups

1\to G(\ell)\to\mathbb{G}(\ell)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{det}_{1,\ldots,g-1}}\mathcal{G}_{m}(\ell)^{g-1}\to 1,

1\to B(\ell)\to\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{det}_{1,\ldots,g-1}}\mathcal{G}_{m}(\ell)^{g-1}\to 1,

where

\operatorname{det}_{1,\ldots,g-1}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{g}):=((\operatorname{det}M_{1})(\operatorname{det}M_{2})^{-1},\ldots,(\operatorname{det}M_{1})(\operatorname{det}M_{g})^{-1})
.
We deduce that

|G(\ell)|=|\mathbb{G}(\ell)|/|\mathcal{G}_{m}(\ell)|^{g-1}=(\ell-1)\ell^{g}(\ell^{2}-1)^{g},

|B(\ell)|=|\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\ell)^{g}|/|\mathcal{G}_{m}(\ell)|^{g-1}=(\ell-1)^{g+1}\ell^{g}.

∎

Lemma 13
.

For any prime
\ell
,
we have a group isomorphism
B(\ell)/U(\ell)\simeq T(\ell)
.

Proof.

Consider the group homomorphisms

T(\ell)\hookrightarrow B(\ell)\twoheadrightarrow B(\ell)/U(\ell).

The composition has kernel
T(\ell)\cap U(\ell)=1
, hence it is injective.
Since, by Lemma
12
, each of the groups
B(\ell)/U(\ell)
and
T(\ell)
has size
(\ell-1)^{g+1}
,
the composition must be an isomorphism.
∎

5.

Let

,

, and

As usual, when we regard

.
In this case, we denote by

Before introducing the unions of conjugacy classes we are interested in investigating,

of the field

for each

Using the above notation and fixing

,
we now introduce the sets to be investigated in this section:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{(M_{1},\ldots,M_{g})\in G(\ell):\lambda_{1}(M_{i}),\lambda_{2}(M_{i})\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}\ \forall 1\leq i\leq g,\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq g}\operatorname{tr}M_{i}\equiv-t(\operatorname{mod}\ell)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\text{Borel}}(\ell,t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}(\ell,t)\cap B(\ell),

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\text{Borel}}(\ell,t)

\displaystyle:=

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
1
for arbitrary
t

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
\mathbb{Q}

that is isogenous over
\mathbb{Q}
to
a product of
g
elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
pairwise
non-isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

and
each without complex multiplication.
As in Subsection 6.1,
we fix a
g
-tuple of elliptic curves,
E_{1}/\mathbb{Q}
,
\ldots
,
E_{g}/\mathbb{Q}
,
equipped with a
\mathbb{Q}
-isogeny between
A
and
E_{1}\times\ldots\times E_{g}

of minimal degree,
d_{A}
, among all such choices.
We keep all the associated notation introduced so far.

Let
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x>2
.
By Lemma
18
,
we can bound
\pi_{A}(x,t)
from above if we succeed in bounding
\pi_{A}(x,\ell,t)
from above for some suitably chosen prime
\ell=\ell(x)
.

For now, we fix a prime
\ell\nmid 2g

such that

(35)

\operatorname{Im}\overline{\rho}_{A,\ell}=G(\ell).

The existence of infinitely many such primes
\ell

[
Se72
, Théorème 6, p. 324]
and
[
Lo16
, Theorem 1.1, p. 387]
under our hypotheses that

are without complex multiplication

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Indeed, from
[
Lo16
, Theorem 1.1, p. 387]
, we infer that
there exists a least positive integer

such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{A}

equals the inverse image under the canonical projection

of
\operatorname{Im}\overline{\rho}_{A,m_{A}}
.
In particular, this means that

\operatorname{Im}\bar{\rho}_{A,\ell}=G(\ell)

for any prime
\ell\nmid m_{A}
.
Later on, we will choose
\ell
in an explicit interval that depends on

.

The choice of a prime
\ell
such that (
35
) holds

\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])

U(\ell)
and
B(\ell)
,
namely

\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\subset\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}\subset\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell]),

for which we immediately obtain the following Galois group structures:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle G(\ell),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle B(\ell),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle U(\ell),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle B(\ell)/U(\ell).

In relation to these Galois groups, we recall from part (v) of Lemma
11

that
U(\ell)\unlhd B(\ell)
and that
B(\ell)/U(\ell)
is abelian.

Using
(
25
),
(
29
),
and
(
35
),
we deduce that
every prime

p\nmid d_{A}

counted by
\pi_{A}(x,\ell,t)

is also counted by
\pi_{\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)}(x,\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})
,
where
\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)
is
the union of conjugacy classes of
G(\ell)

studied in Lemma
14
of Section 5.
Thus

(36)

\pi_{A}(x,\ell,t)\ll_{A}\pi_{\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)}(x,\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q}).

To estimate
\pi_{\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)}(x,\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})
, we invoke part (ii) of
Corollary
8
of Section 2 with

K=\mathbb{Q}
,
L=\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])
,
G=G(\ell)
,
H=B(\ell)
,
N=U(\ell)
, and
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}(\ell,t)
.
The hypotheses of this corollary hold thanks to our assumption of GRH
and
to part (v) of Lemma
11
,
part (v) of Lemma
14
,
and Lemma
16
.
We deduce that

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)}(x,\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\text{Borel}}(\ell,t)\right|\cdot|U(\ell)|}{|B(\ell)|}\cdot\frac{x}{\operatorname{log}x}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}_{\text{Borel}}(\ell,t)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}[\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}:\mathbb{Q}]\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\operatorname{log}x}\operatorname{log}M\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\operatorname{log}x}+\operatorname{log}M\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}:\mathbb{Q}]\left(\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\operatorname{log}x}+\operatorname{log}M\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\right)\right).

Using Lemma
12
and Lemma
17
, we deduce from the above that

(37)

\pi_{\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)}(x,\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})\ll\frac{x}{\ell\operatorname{log}x}+(\ell-1)^{\frac{g}{2}}(\ell+1)^{g}\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\operatorname{log}x}\operatorname{log}M\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\right).

We estimate

\operatorname{log}M\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\right)

by putting together (
3
),
Lemma
12
,
and
the Néron-Ogg-Shafarevich criterion for

, which states that the extension
\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q}
is unramified outside of

(see
[
SeTa68
, Theorem 1, p. 493]

We obtain that

(38)

\operatorname{log}M\left(\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{U(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])^{B(\ell)}\right)\leq 2\operatorname{log}|B(\ell)/U(\ell)|+2\operatorname{log}(\ell N_{A})+\operatorname{log}2\ll g\operatorname{log}(\ell N_{A}).

Using this bound in the upper-estimate (
37
) for
\pi_{\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)}(x,\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})
, we deduce that

(39)

\pi_{\mathcal{C}(\ell,t)}(x,\mathbb{Q}(A[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})\ll\frac{x}{\ell\operatorname{log}x}+g\ \ell^{\frac{3g}{2}}\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\operatorname{log}x}\operatorname{log}(\ell N_{A}).

Finally, we apply Lemma
18
with

y\asymp\frac{x^{\frac{1}{3g+2}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{\frac{2}{3g+2}}}

and

u\asymp y^{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{log}y)^{2+\varepsilon}

for an arbitrarily chosen
\varepsilon>0
,
and conclude that

\pi_{A}(x,t)\leq c(\varepsilon)\displaystyle\max_{y\leq\ell\leq y+u}\pi_{A}(x,\ell,t)\ll_{\varepsilon,A}\frac{x^{1-\frac{1}{3g+2}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{1-\frac{2}{3g+2}}}.

7.
Final remarks

It is a natural question to ask whether one can find
subgroups and conjugacy classes other than those chosen in Sections 4 - 5
and use them, following a strategy similar to the one of Theorem
1
,
to obtain a better upper bound for
\pi_{A}(x,t)
.
We relegate this question to future research.
Below are candidates of subgroups and conjugacy classes which, used mutatis mutandis in our proof,
do not improve on Theorem
1
.

We keep the setting of Subsection 6.3.
We could replace

B(\ell)
with
T(\ell)
,

U(\ell)
with
\{I_{2g}\}
,
and

\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}_{\text{Borel}}(\ell,t)

with

\mathcal{C}_{\text{Torus}}(\ell,t)
.
For every
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, this would lead to the upper bound

(43)

\pi_{A}(x,t)\ll_{A}\frac{x^{1-\frac{1}{5g+2}}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{1-\frac{2}{5g+2}}}.

Alternatively,
we could replace

B(\ell)
with
C^{ns}_{\xi}(\ell)^{g}\cap G(\ell)
,

U(\ell)
with
\{I_{2g}\}
,
and

\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}_{\text{Borel}}(\ell,t)

with

\mathcal{C}_{ns}(\ell,\xi,t)
,
where

C^{ns}_{\xi}(\ell)
is the non-split Cartan subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell)
defined by

C^{ns}_{\xi}(\ell):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&\xi b\\
b&a\end{pmatrix}:a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell},(a,b)\neq(0,0)\right\}

for some non-square element
\xi
of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
,
and where

\hskip-14.22636pt\mathcal{C}_{ns}(\ell,\xi,t):=\left\{(M_{1},\ldots,M_{g})\in C^{ns}_{\xi}(\ell)^{g}\cap G(\ell):\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq g}\operatorname{tr}M_{i}\equiv-t(\operatorname{mod}\ell)\right\}.

Note that
C^{ns}_{\xi}(\ell)^{g}\simeq(\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}^{\times})^{g}
,
where
\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}
denotes the finite field with
\ell^{2}
elements.
Hence
C^{ns}_{\xi}(\ell)^{g}
is an abelian group.
With the above choices,
instead of considering the counting function
\pi_{A}(x,\ell,t)
,
we would consider

\pi^{ns}_{A}(x,\ell,t):=\#\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{A},a_{1,p}(A)=t,\ \ell\ \text{inert in each of the fields}\ \mathbb{Q}(\pi_{p}(E_{1})),\ldots,\mathbb{Q}(\pi_{p}(E_{g}))\}

and prove
an upper bound similar to that
of
Lemma
18
.
Then, applying
Corollary
8
,
we would obtain the previous bound (
43
), which is weaker than that of Theorem
1
.

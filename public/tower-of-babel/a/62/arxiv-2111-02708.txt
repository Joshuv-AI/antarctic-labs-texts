Quasi-Newton Methods for Saddle Point Problems and Beyond
Chengchang Liu, Luo Luo

Abstract
This paper studies quasi-Newton methods for solving strongly-convex-strongly-concave saddle point problems (SPP). We propose greedy and random Broyden family updates for SPP, which have explicit local superlinear convergence rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( 1 − 1 n ​ κ 2 ) k ⁡ ( k − 1 ) / 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}\big(\big(1-\frac{1}{n\kappa^{2}}\big)^{k(k-1)/2}\big) , where n n is dimensions of the problem, κ \kappa is the condition number and k k is the number of iterations. The design and analysis of proposed algorithm are based on estimating the square of indefinite Hessian matrix, which is different from classical quasi-Newton methods in convex optimization. We also present two specific Broyden family algorithms with BFGS-type and SR1-type updates, which enjoy the faster local convergence rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( 1 − 1 n ) k ⁡ ( k − 1 ) / 2 ) \mathcal{O}\big(\big(1-\frac{1}{n}\big)^{k(k-1)/2}\big) . Additionally, we extend our algorithms to solve general nonlinear equations and prove it enjoys the similar convergence rate.

Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the following smoothed saddle point problem

\min_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{x}}}\max_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{y}}}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}}),

for all
{{\bf x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{x}}

There are a great number of first-order optimization algorithms for solving problem (

[

[

[

[

These algorithms iterate with first-order oracle and achieve linear convergence.
Lin et al. 2020

[

They approximate the Hessian matrix based on the Broyden family updating formulas

These algorithms are well studied for convex optimization.
The famous quasi-Newton methods including Davidon-Fletcher-Powell (DFP) method

Recently,

Jin and Mokhtari 2020

Ye et al. 2021b
showed the non-asymptotic superlinear convergence rate also holds for classical DFP, BFGS and SR1 methods.

In this paper, we study quasi-Newton methods for saddle point problem (
1

To overcome this issue, we propose a variant framework of greedy and random quasi-Newton methods for saddle point problems, which approximates the square of the Hessian matrix during the iteration. Our theoretical analysis characterizes the convergence rate by the gradient norm, rather than the weighted norm of gradient used in convex optimization

For BFGS method and SR1 method, we have the faster explicit rate

Additionally, our ideas also can be used for solving general nonlinear equations.

Notation and Preliminaries

We use
\left\|\cdot\right\|
to present spectral norm and Euclidean norm of matrix and vector respectively. We denote the standard basis for
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by
\{{\bf{e}}_{1},\dots,{\bf{e}}_{n}\}
and let
{\bf I}
be the identity matrix. The trace of a square matrix is denoted by
\text{tr}(\cdot)
.
Given two positive definite matrices
{\bf G}
and
{\bf H}
, we define their inner product as

\langle{\bf G},{\bf H}\rangle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\text{tr}({\bf G}{\bf H}).

We introduce the following notation to measure how well does matrix
{\bf G}
approximate matrix
{\bf H}
:

\sigma_{{\bf H}}({\bf G})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\langle{\bf H}^{-1},{\bf G}-{\bf H}\rangle=\langle{\bf H}^{-1},{\bf G}\rangle-n.

(2)

If we further suppose
{\bf G}\succeq{\bf H}
, it holds that

{\bf G}-{\bf H}\preceq{\langle{\bf H}^{-1},{\bf G}-{\bf H}\rangle}{\bf H}=\sigma_{{\bf H}}({\bf G}){\bf H}

(3)

by
Rodomanov and Nesterov 2021a
.

Using the notation of problem (
1
), we let
{\bf z}=[{\bf x};{\bf y}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
where
n=n_{x}+n_{y}
and denote the gradient and Hessian matrix of
f
at
({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})
as

{\bf g}({\bf{z}})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{{{\bf x}}}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})\\
\nabla_{{{\bf y}}}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\nabla^{2}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})=\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{{{\bf x}}{{\bf x}}}^{2}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})&\nabla_{{{\bf x}}{{\bf y}}}^{2}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})\\[2.84544pt]
\nabla_{{{\bf y}}{{\bf x}}}^{2}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})&\nabla_{{{\bf y}}{{\bf y}}}^{2}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}.

We suppose the saddle point problem (
1
) satisfies the following assumptions.

Assumption 2.1
.

The objective function
f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})
is twice differentiable and has
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradient and
L_{2}
-Lipschitz continuous Hessian , i.e., there exists constants
L>0
and
L_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf g}({\bf{z}})-{\bf g}({\bf{z}}^{\prime})\right\|\leq L\left\|{\bf{z}}-{\bf{z}}^{\prime}\right\|

(4)

and

\displaystyle\left\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})-{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}}^{\prime})\right\|\leq L_{2}\left\|{\bf{z}}-{\bf{z}}^{\prime}\right\|.

(5)

for any
{\bf{z}}=[{\bf x};{\bf y}],{\bf{z}}^{\prime}=[{\bf x}^{\prime};{\bf y}^{\prime}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Assumption 2.2
.

The objective function
f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})
is twice differentiable,
\mu
-strongly-convex in
{{\bf x}}
and
\mu
-strongly-concave in
{{\bf y}}
, i.e., there exists
\mu>0
such that
\nabla_{{{\bf x}}{{\bf x}}}^{2}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})\succeq\mu{\bf I}
and
\nabla_{{{\bf y}}{{\bf y}}}^{2}f({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}})\preceq-\mu{\bf I}
for any
({{\bf x}},{{\bf y}}),({{\bf x}}^{\prime},{{\bf y}}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{x}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{y}}
.

Note that inequality (
4
) means the spectral norm of Hessian matrix
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})
can be upper bounded, that is

\displaystyle\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})\|\leq L.

(6)

for all
{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Additionally, the condition number of the objective function is defined as

\displaystyle\kappa\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{L}{\mu}.

3
Quasi-Newton Methods for Saddle Point Problems

The update rule of standard Newton’s method for solving problem (
1
) can be written as

{\bf z}_{+}={\bf z}-\big({\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})\big)^{-1}{\bf g}({\bf z}).

(7)

This iteration scheme has quadratic local convergence, but solving linear system (
7
) takes
{\mathcal{O}}\left(n^{3}\right)
time complexity. For convex minimization, quasi-Newton methods including BFGS/SR1
[
6
,
5
,
40
,
4
,
10
]
and their variants
[
23
,
45
,
35
,
36
]
focus on approximating the Hessian and reduce the computational cost to
{\mathcal{O}}\left(n^{2}\right)
for each round. However, all of these algorithms and related convergence analysis are based on the assumption that the Hessian matrix is positive definite, which is not suitable for our saddle point problems since
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})
is indefinite.

We introduce the auxiliary matrix
{\bf H}({\bf z})
be the square of Hessian

{\bf H}({\bf z})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\left({\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})\right)^{2}.

The following lemma means
{\bf H}({\bf z})
is always positive definite.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under Assumption
2.1
and
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mu^{2}{\bf I}\preceq{\bf H}({\bf z})\preceq L^{2}{\bf I}

(8)

for all
{\bf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
{\bf H}({\bf z})=\big({\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})\big)^{2}
.

Hence, we can reformulate the update of Newton’s method (
7
) by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf z}_{+}=&{\bf z}-\left[\big({\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})\big)^{2}\right]^{-1}{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z}){\bf g}({\bf z})\\
=&{\bf z}-{\bf H}({\bf z})^{-1}{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z}){\bf g}({\bf z}).\end{split}

(9)

Then it is natural to characterize the second-order information by estimating the auxiliary matrix
{\bf H}({\bf z})
, rather than the indefinite Hessian
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})
. If we have obtained a symmetric positive definite matrix
{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
as an estimator for
{\bf H}({\bf z})
, the update rule of (
9
) can be approximated by

{\bf z}_{+}={\bf z}-{\bf G}^{-1}{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z}){\bf g}({\bf z}).

(10)

The remainder of this section introduce several strategies to construct
{\bf G}
, resulting the quasi-Newton methods for saddle point problem with local superlinear convergence.
We should point out the implementation of iteration (
10
) is unnecessary to construct Hessian matrix
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})
explicitly, since we are only interested in the Hessian-vector product
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z}){\bf g}({\bf z})
, which can be computed efficiently
[
30
,
38
]
.

3.1
The Broyden Family Updates

We first introduce the Broyden family
[
28
, Section 6.3]
of quasi-Newton updates for approximating an positive definite matrix
{\bf H}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
by using the information of current estimator
{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose two positive definite matrices
{\bf H},{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
satisfy
{\bf H}\preceq{\bf G}
. For any
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, if
{\bf G}{\bf u}={\bf H}{\bf u}
, we define

\rm{Broyd}_{\tau}({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u})\overset{\rm{def}}{=}{\bf H}
. Otherwise, we define

\displaystyle\rm{Broyd}_{\tau}({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u})\overset{\rm{def}}{=}~~

\displaystyle\tau\left[{\bf G}-\frac{{\bf H}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf G}+{\bf G}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}+\left(\frac{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf u}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}+1\right)\frac{{\bf H}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}\right]

(11)

\displaystyle+(1-\tau)\left[{\bf G}-\frac{({\bf G}-{\bf H}){\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf H})}{{\bf u}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf H}){\bf u}}\right].

The different choices of parameter
\tau
for formula (
11
) contain several popular quasi-Newton updates:

•

For
\tau=\dfrac{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf u}}\in[0,1]
, it corresponds to BFGS update

\displaystyle\text{BFGS}({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u})\overset{\text{def}}{=}{\bf G}-\frac{{\bf G}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf G}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf u}}+\frac{{\bf H}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}.

(12)

•

For
\tau=0
, it corresponds to SR1 update

\displaystyle\rm{SR1}({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u})\overset{\rm{def}}{=}{\bf G}-\frac{({\bf G}-{\bf H}){\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf H})}{{\bf u}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf H}){\bf u}}.

(13)

The general Broyden family update as Definition
3.2
has the following properties.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
35
, Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Suppose two positive definite matrices
{\bf H},{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}

{\bf H}\preceq{\bf G}\preceq\eta{\bf H}

{\rm Broyd}_{\tau_{1}}({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u})\preceq{\rm Broyd}_{\tau_{2}}({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u}).

Additionally, for any
\tau\in[0,1]
, we have

{\bf H}\preceq{\rm Broyd}_{\tau}({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u})\preceq\eta{\bf H}.

(14)

3.3.1

The key idea of designing quasi-Newton methods for saddle point problems is characterizing the second-order information by approximating the auxiliary matrix

4
Extension for Solving Nonlinear Equations

In this section, we extend our algorithms for solving general nonlinear equations. Concretely, we consider finding the solution of the system

{\bf g}({\bf z})={\bf 0}.

(58)

The remainders of this paper do not require the operator
{\bf g}(\cdot)
related to some minimax problem and it could be any differentiable function from
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We use
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})
to present the Jacobian of
{\bf g}(\cdot)
at
{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and still follow the notation
{\bf H}({\bf z})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\big({\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})\big)^{2}
.
We suppose the nonlinear equation satisfies the following conditions.

Assumption 4.1
.

The function
{\bf g}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is differentiable and its Jacobian

is

-Lipschitz continuous. That is, for all

\left\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})-{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z}^{\prime})\right\|\leq L_{2}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|.

(59)

Assumption 4.2
.

There exists a solution
{\bf z}^{*}
of equation (
58
) such that
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z}^{*})
is non-singular. Additionally, we assume the smallest and largest singular values of
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z}^{*})
are
\mu
and
L
respectively.

We still denote the condition number as
\kappa\overset{\text{def}}{=}L/\mu
. Note that the saddle point problem (
1
) under Assumption
2.1
and
2.2
is a special case of solving nonlinear equation (
58
) under Assumption
4.1
and
4.2
.

Similar to previous section, the design of the algorithms is based on approximating the auxiliary matrix
{\bf H}({\bf z})
. Hence, we start from considering its smoothness.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under Assumption
4.1
and
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}({\bf z})-{\bf H}({\bf z}^{\prime})\|\leq 4L_{2}L\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|,

(60)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}{\bf I}\preceq{\bf H}({\bf z})\preceq 4L^{2}{\bf I},

(61)

for all
{\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime}\in\left\{{\bf z}:\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{*}\|\leq\frac{L}{8\kappa^{2}L_{2}}\right\}

Then we have the property similar to strongly self-concordance like Lemma
3.11

Lemma 4.4

For all
{\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime},{{\bf w}}\in\left\{{\bf z}:\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{*}\|\leq\frac{L}{8\kappa^{2}L_{2}}\right\}

{\bf H}({\bf z})-{\bf H}({\bf z}^{\prime})\preceq M\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|{\bf H}({{\bf w}})

with
M=8\kappa^{2}L_{2}/L

Related Work

Very recently,

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have proposed quasi-Newton methods for solving strongly-convex-strongly-concave saddle point problems.
We have characterized the second-order information by approximating the square of Hessian matrix, which avoid the issue of dealing with the indefinite Hessian directly.
We have presented the explicit local superlinear convergence rates for Broyden’s family update and a faster convergence rates for two specific methods: SR1 and BFGS updates.
Moreover, we have also extend our theory to solve the general nonlinear equation systems and provided the similar convergence results for the algorithms.

Appendix A
Efficient Implementation for Algorithm
1

For the self-completeness of this paper, we present Proposition 15 of
Lin et al. 2021b
to show Algorithm
1
can be implemented with
{\mathcal{O}}\left(n^{2}\right)
flops.

Lemma A.1

(
[
23
, Proposition 15]
)
.

In this Lemma, we show how to construct upper triangle matrix
\hat{\bf L}
from
{\bf L}
,
{\bf H}
and the greedy direction
{\bf u}
with
{\mathcal{O}}(n^{2})
flops.
From the inverse BFGS update rule of
{\bf A}={\bf G}^{-1}
, we have

{\bf A}_{+}=\left({\rm BFGS}\left({\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf u}\right)\right)^{-1}=\left({\bf I}-\dfrac{{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}\right){\bf A}\left({\bf I}-\dfrac{{\bf H}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}\right)+\dfrac{{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}.

(65)

Suppose we already have
{\bf A}={\bf L}^{\top}{\bf L}
where
{\bf L}
is an upper triangular matrix, now we construct
\hat{{\bf L}}
such that
{\bf A}_{+}={\bf L}^{\top}{\bf L}
with
{\mathcal{O}}(n^{2})
flops.

1.

First we can obtain
QR
decomposition of

{\bf L}\left({\bf I}-\dfrac{{\bf H}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}\right)={\bf L}-\dfrac{{\bf L}({\bf H}{\bf u})}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}{\bf u}^{\top}

with
{\mathcal{O}}(n^{2})
flops since it is a rank-one changes of
{\bf L}
.

2.

Second, we have
{\bf L}\left({\bf I}-\dfrac{{\bf H}{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}\right)={\bf Q}{\bf R}
, with an orthogonal matrix
{\bf Q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
and an upper triangular matrix
{\bf R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
. We denote
{\bf v}=\frac{{\bf u}}{\sqrt{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}}
, then we can view

{\bf A}_{+}={\bf R}^{\top}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf Q}{\bf R}+\dfrac{{\bf u}{\bf u}^{\top}}{{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf H}{\bf u}}={\bf R}^{\top}{\bf R}+{\bf v}{\bf v}^{\top}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf v}&{\bf R}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\bf v}^{\top}\\
{\bf R}\end{bmatrix}.

we still can obtain
QR
decomposition of
\begin{bmatrix}{\bf v}^{\top}\\
{\bf R}\end{bmatrix}
with only
{\mathcal{O}}(n^{2})
flops, leading to

\begin{bmatrix}{\bf v}^{\top}\\
{\bf R}\end{bmatrix}={\bf Q}^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}
, with an column orthogonal matrix
{\bf Q}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(n+1)\times n}
and an upper triangular matrix
{\bf R}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
and

{\bf A}_{+}={\bf R}^{\prime\top}{\bf R}^{\prime}.

Thus
{\bf R}^{\prime}
satisfies our requirements.

Appendix B
A Useful Lemma for Convergence Analysis and Proof of Lemma
3.1

This section first provides a useful lemma of symmetric positive definite matrices for our further analysis.

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose
{\bf H},{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
are symmetric positive definite matrices,
{\hat{{\bf H}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
are symmetric non-degenerate matrix where
{\bf H}=({\hat{{\bf H}}})^{2}
and

{\bf H}\preceq{\bf G}\preceq\eta{\bf H},

where
\eta>1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf I}-{\hat{{\bf H}}}{\bf G}^{-1}{\hat{{\bf H}}}\|\leq 1-\frac{1}{\eta}.

(66)

Proof.

We have the following inequality for
{\bf G}^{-1}
and
{\bf H}^{-1}

\frac{1}{\eta}{\bf H}^{-1}\preceq{\bf G}^{-1}\preceq{\bf H}^{-1},

(67)

which means

{\bf 0}\preceq{\bf H}^{-1}-{\bf G}^{-1}\preceq\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}\right){\bf H}^{-1}.

(68)

Thus we have

{\bf 0}\preceq{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf H}^{-1}-{\bf G}^{-1}){\hat{{\bf H}}}\preceq\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}\right){\hat{{\bf H}}}{\bf H}^{-1}{\hat{{\bf H}}}\preceq\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}\right){\bf I}.

(69)

So we have

\|{\bf I}-{\hat{{\bf H}}}{\bf G}^{-1}{\hat{{\bf H}}}\|=\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf H}^{-1}-{\bf G}^{-1}){\hat{{\bf H}}}\|\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}\right).

(70)

∎

B.1
The Proof of Lemma
3.1

Proof.

We partition
{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
as

{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})=\begin{bmatrix}{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{{\bf x}}{{\bf x}}}({\bf{z}})&{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{{\bf x}}{{\bf y}}}({\bf{z}})\\
{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{{\bf y}}{{\bf x}}}({\bf{z}})^{\top}&{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{{\bf y}}{{\bf y}}}({\bf{z}})\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}

where the sub-matrices
{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}({\bf{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{x}\times n_{x}}
,
{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf x}{\bf y}}({\bf{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{x}\times n_{y}}
,
{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf y}{\bf x}}({\bf{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{y}\times n_{x}}
and
{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf y}{\bf y}}({\bf{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{y}\times n_{y}}
satisfy
{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}({\bf{z}})\succeq\mu{\bf I}
,
{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf y}{\bf y}}({\bf{z}})\preceq-\mu{\bf I}
and
\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})\|\leq L
for all
{\bf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by Assumption
2.1
and
2.2
.
We denote

{\bf J}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf I}_{n_{x}}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&-{\bf I}_{n_{y}}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n},

where
{\bf I}_{n_{x}}
and
{\bf I}_{n_{y}}
are
n_{x}\times n_{x}
identity matrix and
n_{y}\times n_{y}
identity matrix respectively.
We can verified that
{\bf J}^{\top}{\bf J}={\bf I}
and

{\bf J}{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{{\bf H}}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}({\bf{z}})&\hat{{\bf H}}_{{\bf x}{\bf y}}({\bf{z}})\\
-{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf x}{\bf y}}({\bf{z}})&-{\hat{{\bf H}}}_{{\bf y}{\bf y}}({\bf{z}})\end{bmatrix}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle{\bf H}({\bf{z}})={\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})^{\top}{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})={\hat{{\bf H}}}^{\top}({\bf{z}})({\bf J}^{\top}{\bf J}){\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf{z}})=({\bf J}{\hat{{\bf H}}})^{\top}({\bf J}{\hat{{\bf H}}}).

Following Lemma 4.5 of
Abernethy et al. 2019
, we have

{\bf H}({\bf{z}})\succeq\mu^{2}{\bf I}\succ{\bf 0}.

Since
\big\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}\big\|\leq L
and
{\bf H}\succ{\bf 0}
, we have

\left\|{\bf H}({\bf z})\right\|=\big\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})^{2}\big\|\leq\big\|{\hat{{\bf H}}}({\bf z})\big\|^{2}\leq L^{2}.

and

{\bf H}({\bf z})\preceq L^{2}{\bf I}.

If we set

, we could obtain with probability

Furthermore, it holds with probability

(131)

Telescoping from

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i-1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:r_tel}}}{{\leq}}\lambda_{0}\cdot\left(\frac{4n^{3}\kappa^{6}}{\delta}\right)^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{n\kappa^{2}+1}\right)^{i-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4n^{3}\kappa^{6}}{\delta}\right)^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{n\kappa^{2}+1}\right)^{k(k-1)/2}\lambda_{0}.

Now we combine this result with Theorem
3.14

Denote by

the number of the first iteration, for which

In view of
131
denote by

Thus for all

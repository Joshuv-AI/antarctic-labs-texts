A splitting theorem for manifolds with a convex boundary component and applications
Alessandro Cucinotta and Andrea Mondino

Abstract
. We prove a warped product splitting theorem for manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below in the spirit of [Croke-Kleiner, Duke Math. J . (1992)], but instead of asking that one boundary component is compact and mean-convex, we require that it is parabolic and convex. We then deduce several applications, including splitting theorems and first Betti number rigidity results for • 3 3 -manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, • 4 4 -manifolds with weakly bounded geometry, non-negative 2 2 -Ricci curvature, scalar curvature ≥ 1 \geq 1 . In particular, the latter aswers to a rigidity question posed by [Chodosh-Li-Stryker, JEMS , (2024)]. The proofs rely on a metric gluing of Riemannian manifolds with boundary, resulting in a non-smooth metric space. To address this lack of smoothness, we employ synthetic tools specifically developed for non-smooth settings, with a focus on those based on optimal transportation.



1.
Introduction

The celebrated Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem
[
19
]
states that a (complete, connected) Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature containing a line is isometrically a product, where one of the factors is a real line.
A variant of this result was proved by Kasue in
[
49
]
. There, it is shown that
a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with mean-convex compact boundary
\partial M
and non-negative Ricci curvature, containing a half line
with initial point in
\partial M
, again is isometrically a product. In the latter case, the splitting factor is a half line.
This result fails without the compactness assumption on the boundary, as one sees considering the epigraph of a strictly convex function in Euclidean space.
In the same work, Kasue shows that a manifold with multiple mean-convex boundary components, one of which is compact, and non-negative Ricci curvature is also a product, where one of the factors is a bounded interval of the real line.
A generalization of Kasue’s results to manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below by a
negative
constant was given by Croke and Kleiner in
[
26
]
, and is recalled below.

Let us first fix some notation. In a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with boundary
\partial M
and external unit normal
\nu
, we denote by
\mathrm{I\!I}^{M}_{\partial M}
the second fundamental form of the boundary:

\mathrm{I\!I}^{M}_{\partial M}(X,Y):=g(\nabla_{X}\nu,Y),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }X,Y\in T_{x}M,\;x\in\partial M.

We set

H^{M}_{\partial M}:=\frac{1}{n-1}\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{I\!I}^{M}_{\partial M})

to be the associated mean curvature (the upper script
M
will be omitted if there is no ambiguity).
We say that a manifold with boundary
(M,g)
is convex (resp. mean convex) if its boundary satisfies
\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial M}\geq 0
(resp.
H_{\partial M}\geq 0
). Similarly, a connected component
S
of
\partial M
is said to be convex (resp. mean convex) if it satisfies
\mathrm{I\!I}_{S}\geq 0
(resp.
H_{S}\geq 0
). The intrinsic Ricci curvature of
S
is denoted by
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}
.

Theorem

(
[
26
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\delta\in\{0,1\}
and let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)\delta
and whose boundary consists of two connected components
\partial M=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
, one of which is compact. Assume that the mean curvature of
S_{1}
is
\geq\delta
and the mean curvature of
S_{2}
is
\geq-\delta
.
Then, there exists
l>0
such that
M
is isometric to
S_{1}\times[0,l]
with
the warped product metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2\delta t}g_{1}+\mathrm{d}t^{2}
, where
g_{1}
is the metric on
S_{1}
. Moreover,
\mathsf{Ric}_{S_{1}}\geq 0
.

In Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
below, we prove variants of this result. On the one hand, instead of asking for the compactness of one of the boundary components, we ask for its parabolicity, which is a weaker condition. For example,
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is parabolic. On the other hand, we assume that the parabolic boundary component has a lower bound on the second fundamental form, rather than on the mean curvature, and that it has itself nonnegative Ricci curvature. Whether Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
hold with just a mean curvature lower bound on the parabolic boundary component is an open question, which we discuss in Remark
1.5
and in Section
5
.

Recall that a manifold is said to be
parabolic
if it admits no positive fundamental solution for the Laplacian. For example, while
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is parabolic,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
is not. More generally, a manifold
(M,g)
with non-negative Ricci curvature is parabolic if and only if

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\mathsf{Vol}(B_{t}(x))}\,\mathrm{d}t=+\infty,

for some
x\in M
.
Next, we state the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq 0
and disconnected mean-convex boundary. Assume that one connected component
S\subset\partial M
is parabolic, convex, and satisfies
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}\geq 0
.

Then, there exists
l>0
such that
M
is isometric to
S\times[0,l]
with
the product metric.

The next two theorems provide variants of Theorem
1.1
where the Ricci curvature is bounded from below by a
negative
constant (compare with Croke-Kleiner’s result above).

Theorem
1.2
deals with the case where a parabolic boundary component satisfies
\mathrm{I\!I}\geq 1
, while the other boundary components satisfy
H\geq-1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)
and disconnected boundary with
H_{\partial M}\geq-1
.
Assume that one connected component
S\subset\partial M
is parabolic, has
\mathrm{I\!I}_{S}\geq 1
, and
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}\geq 0
.

Then, there exists
l>0
such that
M
is isometric to
S\times[0,l]
with
the warped product metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2t}g_{S}+\mathrm{d}t^{2}
, where
g_{S}
is the metric on
S
.

Theorem
1.3
treats the case where a parabolic boundary component satisfies
\mathrm{I\!I}\geq-1
, while the other boundary components satisfy
H\geq 1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold, with
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)
and disconnected boundary. Assume that one connected component
S\subset\partial M
is parabolic, has
\mathrm{I\!I}_{S}\geq-1
, and
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}\geq 0
. Assume also that
H_{\partial M\setminus S}\geq 1
.

Then,
S_{1}:=\partial M\setminus S
is connected, and there exists
l>0
such that
M
is isometric to
S_{1}\times[0,l]
with
the warped product metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2t}g_{1}+\mathrm{d}t^{2}
, where
g_{1}
is the metric on
S_{1}
.

Remark 1.4

(On the parabolicity assumption)
.

In Theorem
1.1
, the parabolicity assumption cannot be omitted. To see this, consider the manifold
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
given by the region bounded by a catenoid (see
[
9
,
24
]
) and a disjoint hyperplane. In this setting,
M
satisfies all the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, except for the parabolicity of the convex boundary component, since
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is not parabolic. However, the conclusion of the theorem does not hold. Similar counterexamples are expected to exist for Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. This phenomenon is related to the study of the Half Space Property (see
[
25
]
and the discussion below in the introduction) in the context of warped products.

Remark 1.5

(On the curvature assumptions for the parabolic boundary component)
.

Given the result by Croke-Kleiner, it is natural to ask whether it is possible to replace the conditions

•

\mathrm{I\!I}_{S}\geq 0
,
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}\geq 0
in Theorem
1.1
;

•

\mathrm{I\!I}_{S}\geq 1
,
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}\geq 0
in Theorem
1.2
;

•

\mathrm{I\!I}_{S}\geq-1
,
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}\geq 0
in Theorem
1.3
;

with the considerably weaker conditions

•

H_{S}\geq 0
in Theorem
1.1
;

•

H_{S}\geq 1
in Theorem
1.2
;

•

H_{S}\geq-1
in Theorem
1.3
.

It is likely that the answer to this question is affirmative (see Section
5
for a partial answer).

The stronger boundary assumptions in the aforementioned theorems stem from the gluing construction underlying our proof, which produces a sufficiently regular metric space only under appropriate control of the second fundamental form of the boundary. The following example provides a useful illustration of the role played by boundary assumptions in gluing constructions. By
[
65
]
, if
M
has
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
and
\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial M}\geq 0
, then its double (i.e. the gluing of a copy of
M
to itself along the boundary) is a
C^{0}
manifold whose metric can be approximated locally by metrics with almost nonnegative Ricci curvature. On the other hand, if
M
has
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
and
H_{\partial M}\geq 0
, then the metric of its double can only be approximated locally by metrics with almost nonnegative
scalar
curvature, which is a much weaker condition. For instance, using the surgery results in
[
43
,
44
]
, one can construct manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature and two totally geodesic boundary components which are not products.

Remark 1.6

(How to construct manifolds as in Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
)
.

Using the extensive literature on the existence and regularity of minimal surfaces, one
can construct manifolds satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
starting from fairly general assumptions. Indeed, let
(N,g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold where any
stable
minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset N
is totally geodesic, parabolic, and has
\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq 0
(existence of such manifolds follows, for instance, from
[
66
]
and
[
22
]
). Then, any smooth region
M\subset N
bounded by the disjoint union of a minimal hypersurface and a
stable
minimal hypersurface satisfies the boundary assumptions of Theorem
1.1
. Such regions can be constructed either assuming that
N
has disconnected minimal boundary using a variation of
[
57
, Lemma
4
, Section
11
]
(this is the strategy used in Corollaries
1.7
and
1.9
below), or assuming that
N
supports a free closed action of
\mathbb{Z}
using an argument from
[
66
]
(this is the strategy used in Theorem
1.10
below). Similar constructions are likely to be possible for manifolds as in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, replacing the use of minimal surfaces with the use of
\mu
-bubbles (see
[
42
,
40
]
).

We next discuss the main applications. As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we deduce splitting results for low dimensional manifolds with suitable curvature assumptions and disconnected mean-convex boundary, with no further assumptions (not even parabolicity) on the boundary. We stress that these results, in particular, do not follow from Kasue’s Theorem.

The first such result is Corollary
1.7
, which shows that a
3
-manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature and disconnected mean-convex boundary is a product. To the best of our knowledge, this statement is original. The proof relies on a new criterion to determine whether a manifold is a product (see Theorem
4.1
), on the results from
[
66
]
, and on
[
57
, Lemma
4
, Section
11
]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature and disconnected mean-convex boundary. Then,
(M,g)
splits isometrically as
\Sigma\times[0,l]
, for some manifold
(\Sigma,g^{\prime})
and some
l>0
.

Remark 1.8

(On a related result by Anderson-Rodriguez)
.

In
[
5
, Theorem 3]
, Anderson and Rodriguez proved a result similar to Corollary
1.7
, assuming an additional
uniform upper bound on the sectional curvature
and that the disconnected boundary is
minimal
.

As a second application of Theorem
1.1
, combined with
[
22
, Theorem 1.10]
and the tools used to prove Corollary
1.7
(i.e., Theorem
4.1
), we deduce a splitting result for
4
-manifolds with non-negative
2
-Ricci curvature, scalar curvature greater than
1
, weakly bounded geometry and mean-convex disconnected boundary (cf.
[
35
, Theorem 5.2]
for the case of non-negative
sectional
curvature and
minimal
boundary).
We refer to Definitions
4.4
and
4.5
(and the subsequent comments) for the precise definitions of manifolds with weakly bounded geometry and non-negative
2
-Ricci curvature (denoted
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
), and for their geometric relevance. We only mention here that
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
is an intermediate condition between
\mathsf{Sec}\geq 0
and
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
, in the sense that
\mathsf{Sec}\geq 0\Rightarrow\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0\Rightarrow\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
(M^{4},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
, scalar curvature
\geq 1
, and weakly bounded geometry. Let
N^{4}\subset M^{4}
be a smooth submanifold with mean-convex disconnected boundary. Then,
N=\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically, for some manifold
(\Sigma,g^{\prime})
and
l>0
.

Through a classical argument linking the non-vanishing of the first Betti number to existence of minimal hypersurfaces, Corollaries
1.7
and
1.9
can be used to obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(M,g)
be a manifold without boundary such that either

•

n=3
, and
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
,

or

•

n=4
,
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, and
M
has weakly bounded geometry.

If
b_{1}(M)\neq 0
, then the universal covering of
M
splits a line isometrically.

In the case of
3
-manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature, the previous theorem was known from
[
54
]
(see also
[
28
]
), with a different proof. On the other hand, the result is new for
4
-manifolds with
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
, scalar curvature
\geq 1
, and weakly bounded geometry, and it answers a rigidity question from
[
22
, Comments after Theorem 1.13]
.

As an application of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, we obtain a slice theorem for warped products over parabolic manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, see Corollary
1.11
below.
Let us give some context first.
Following
[
64
]
, a manifold
(M,g)
is said to have the Half Space Property if the only (properly embedded) minimal hypersurfaces of
M\times\mathbb{R}
contained in a half-space are the horizontal slices
M\times\{t\}
. In recent years, several results have been obtained by different authors (see, among others,
[
30
,
45
,
34
,
31
,
25
,
33
]
).
Corollary
1.11
provides a result in the spirit of the Half Space Property for (possibly warped) products. The main differences with the aforementioned classical half-space results are that:

•

Corollary
1.11
holds for mean-convex boundaries in product manifolds, not only for minimal hypersurfaces;

•

we obtain also a half-space result for sets whose boundary has mean curvature bounded below by
1
(or
-1
, depending whether they lie in the lower or upper half space), in warped products with negative curvature.

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
\delta\in\{0,1\}
. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a parabolic manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq 0
and let
M\times\mathbb{R}
be equipped with the metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2t\delta}g+\mathrm{d}t^{2}
. If
E\subset M\times(0,+\infty)
is a smooth closed set with connected boundary and mean curvature
H_{\partial E}\leq\delta
, then
E=M\times[a,+\infty)
, for some
a>0
.
If
E\subset M\times(-\infty,0)
is a smooth closed set with connected boundary and mean curvature
H_{\partial E}\leq-\delta
, then
E=M\times(-\infty,a]
, for some
a<0
.

Remark 1.12

(On a related result by Montiel)
.

In
[
59
]
, Montiel proved that if
(M,g)
is compact with non-negative Ricci curvature, and
M\times\mathbb{R}
is equipped with the metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2t}g+\mathrm{d}t^{2}
, then any hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M\times\mathbb{R}
of constant mean curvature, that is locally a graph on
M
, must be a slice.
Some generalizations of this result later appeared in
[
2
,
3
,
14
]
. Although similar in spirit, Montiel’s theorem is independent of Corollary
1.11
: indeed, it requires the mean curvature to be constant while we require the inequality
H\leq\pm 1
and we do not assume the hypersurface to be locally a graph.

We briefly outline the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Consider the metric space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
obtained by gluing
M
and
S\times[0,+\infty)
along their isometric boundaries. Let us emphasize that
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
is a non-smooth space: the glued Riemannian metric is only of class
C^{0,1}
along
S
. This lack of smoothness motivates the use of synthetic tools developed for non-smooth spaces, particularly those based on optimal transport.

Consider the distance function
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}
from
S_{2}:=\partial M\setminus S
in the glued space and its restriction to
S\times[0,+\infty)
. We denote by
\mathsf{d}_{S}
the distance from
S
in
S\times[0,+\infty)
. If we can show that
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}
is constant on
S\times\{0\}
, then the statement follows by standard arguments.

Thanks to the assumption on the fundamental form of
S
(and previous results on the Laplacian of distance functions in metric measure spaces with synthetic Ricci curvature lower bounds, see
[
36
,
16
,
50
,
58
,
37
]
), it holds
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}\leq 0
on
S\times(0,+\infty)
in distributional sense.

In particular, it holds
\Delta(\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S})\leq 0
and, calling
\nu
the exterior normal of
S\times\{0\}
in
S\times[0,+\infty)
, it also (formally) holds
\nabla(\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S})\cdot\nu\geq 0
on
S\times\{0\}
.
If
S\times[0,+\infty)
has sufficiently small volume growth at infinity, we then deduce that
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S}
is constant by an integration by parts argument, cf.
[
48
]
.
Since in general
S\times[0,+\infty)
does not satisfy the required volume growth at infinity, we multiply the volume measure of
S\times[0,+\infty)
by a suitable weight, cf.
[
29
]
.
Making an appropriate choice of such a weight, we obtain that
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S}
satisfies the previous Laplacian bound also in the weighted space, which, in addition, has the desired volume growth. We then carry out a weighted integration by parts argument to obtain that
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S}
is constant. Hence,
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}
is constant on
S\times\{0\}
.

2.
Preliminary lemmas

We first introduce a few optimal transport tools that will be used in the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

If Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
– whose statements do not rely on optimal transport theory – are taken as given, this section may be skipped, and one can proceed directly to the proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold (possibly with boundary) equipped with a locally Lipschitz continuous metric
g
. The metric
g
induces a distance
\mathsf{d}
and a volume measure, which coincides with the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathsf{H}^{n}
induced by
\mathsf{d}
.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
, we denote by
\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
and
\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
respectively Lipschitz continuous and compactly supported Lipschitz continuous functions on
\Omega
.

Assume that the metric space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
is complete. On the metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathsf{H}^{n})
one can define the Wasserstein distance (w.r.t. to the distance squared cost) between two probability measures with finite second moment. The set of probability measures with finite second moment is denoted
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
.
Given
N\in(1,+\infty)
and
\mu=\rho\mathsf{H}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
, the Rényi entropy of
\mu
with respect to
\mathsf{H}^{n}
is defined as

U_{N}(\mu|\mathsf{H}^{n}):=-\int\rho^{1-1/N}\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}^{n}.

Given
K\in\mathbb{R}
, two measures
\mu_{1}=\rho_{1}\mathsf{H}^{n},\mu_{2}=\rho_{2}\mathsf{H}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
, and an optimal plan
\Pi
between them, we define

\displaystyle T^{(t)}_{K,N}(\Pi|\mathsf{H}^{n}):=\int_{\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}}[

\displaystyle\tau^{1-t}_{K,N}(\mathsf{d}(x_{1},x_{2}))\rho_{1}^{-1/N}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\tau^{t}_{K,N}(\mathsf{d}(x_{1},x_{2}))\rho_{2}^{-1/N}(x_{2})]\,d\Pi(x_{1},x_{2}),

where, for every
s\in[0,1]
,
\tau^{s}_{\cdot,\cdot}(\cdot)
is an appropriate distortion coefficient (see
[
69
]
).

We say that
\mathsf{X}
satisfies the
{\rm CD}(K,N)
condition in
U\subset\mathsf{X}
, if the following holds.
For every pair
\mu_{1}=\rho_{1}\mathsf{H}^{n},\mu_{2}=\rho_{2}\mathsf{H}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
of measures supported in
U
, there exists a Wasserstein geodesic
\{\xi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
from
\mu_{1}
to
\mu_{2}
and an optimal coupling
\Pi
of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
such that, for every
t\in[0,1]
and every
N^{\prime}\geq N
, it holds

U_{N^{\prime}}(\xi_{t}|\mathsf{H}^{n})\leq-T^{(t)}_{K,N^{\prime}}(\Pi|\mathsf{H}^{n}).

This condition differs from the
\mathrm{CD}_{loc}(K,N)
condition that appears in the literature (see for instance
[
7
,
15
]
), since we are not requiring that the convexity of the entropy holds in a neighbourhood of every point. For more background on curvature dimension conditions in metric (measure) spaces, we refer to the foundational works
[
68
,
69
,
55
]
.

We now turn our attention to Laplacians of functions on
\mathsf{X}
, referring to
[
36
,
16
,
50
,
58
,
37
]
for more details and results on Laplacians in metric measure spaces with synthetic Ricci lower bounds. Let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set.
We say that a Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
is the distributional Laplacian of
f\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
(and we write
\Delta f=\mu
) if, for every
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
, it holds

-\int_{\Omega}\nabla f\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}^{n}=\int\phi\,d\mu.

We remark that since we are working on a smooth manifold with a continuous metric, the product
\nabla f\cdot\nabla\phi
is well defined
\mathsf{H}^{n}
-almost everywhere. In particular, for our purposes, we do not need to consider more general notions of gradients on metric spaces.

We now recall some facts about the localization technique for
\textrm{CD}(K,N)
spaces. We refer to
[
17
,
16
]
for the proofs, as well as for the definitions of transport set of a Lipschitz function, transport rays and disintegration of a measure.

Let
N>1
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
\mathsf{X}
is
\textrm{CD}(K,N)
in a neighbourhood of every point and that it is non-branching (i.e. geodesics do not branch). Let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X})
be a
1
-Lipschitz function and consider the partition of its transport set into transport rays
\{r_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in Q}
,
Q
being a set of indices with a measure
\mathsf{q}
induced by the partition.
Consider the associated disintegration of the measure
\mathsf{H}^{n}
(restricted to the transport set of
\phi
) into measures
\{h_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in Q}
, each concentrated on the corresponding transport ray.

Then,
\mathsf{q}
-almost every measure
h_{\alpha}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure of the corresponding transport ray, it admits a locally Lipschitz continuous density (that we still denote by
h_{\alpha}
), and it satisfies in weak sense

(h_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{N-1}})^{\prime\prime}+\frac{K}{N-1}h_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\leq 0.

It was shown in
[
16
]
that, if the function
\phi
is the distance function from a closed set
E\subset\mathsf{X}
, we also have

\Delta\phi=(\log h_{\alpha})^{\prime}+[\Delta\phi]^{sing}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }\mathsf{X}\setminus E\text{ in distributional sense}.

In the previous equation,

The next Lemma deals with the Laplacian of distance functions in metric spaces resulting from the gluing of two manifolds along their isometric boundaries. Lemma 2.2

to
S_{2}

\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}

The next lemmas deal with
sets minimizing the perimeter
in Riemannian manifolds, and are needed to prove Theorem
4.1
. In Euclidean space, the subject is by now classical, see for instance the monographs
[
38
,
56
]
. When the ambient space is not
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, many properties have been obtained in the more general setting of metric measure spaces. An account of this theory can be found in
[
58
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
E\subset M
be a Borel set. Given an open set
A\subset M
, the perimeter of
E
relative to
A
is defined as

P(E,A):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{A}|\nabla f_{k}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{Vol}\ :\ f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(A),\ f_{k}\to\chi_{E}\ \mbox{ in }L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)\right\}.

The set
E\subset M
is said to have locally finite perimeter if
P(E,B_{r}(x))<+\infty
for all
x\in M
and
r>0
.
If
E
has locally finite perimeter, there exists a unique Radon measure
\mu
such that
\mu(A)=P(E,A)
if
A\subset M
is open. This measure is denoted
P(E,\cdot)
.

A set of locally finite perimeter
E\subset M
is
perimeter minimizing
in an open set
A\subset M
if, for every
F\subset M
such that
F\Delta E\subset\subset A
, it holds that
P(E,A)\leq P(F,A)
. The set
E\subset M
is
locally perimeter minimizing
in
A
if for every
x\in A
there exists
r_{x}>0
such that
E
is perimeter minimizing in
B_{r_{x}}(x)
.

If
E\subset M^{n}
locally minimizes the perimeter in
M
, then it admits both an open and a closed representative, and these have the same topological boundary, which is a smooth hypersurface outside of a set
\Sigma\subset\partial E
of Hausdorff dimension at most
n-8
. Whenever we refer to the boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set, we mean the topological boundary of its open (or closed) representative.

Lemma
2.3
concerns a minimizing property of the boundaries of mean-convex sets in Riemannian manifolds. It is well known that minimal hypersurfaces locally minimize area (see
[
52
]
). Lemma
2.3
can be viewed as an analogue of this fact for sets with mean-convex boundary, and its proof follows by adapting the argument in
[
52
]
. Although this result is likely known to experts, we include the proof for completeness.

Hence, we can rewrite (

The classical

[
57
, lemma
4
, section
11
]
states that a manifold with mean convex disconnected boundary and dimension
\leq 7
contains a properly embedded stable minimal hypersurface. The next lemma is a variant of this result. We consider a manifold

with mean convex disconnected boundary and arbitrary dimension. We show that either one connected component of the boundary is a stable minimal hypersurface, or
M\setminus\partial M
contains a non-trivial perimeter minimizing set.

The logic behind this duality is that if the minimal hypersurface given by Meeks-Schoen-Yau touches the boundary, then it coincides with a boundary component by the maximum principle. Otherwise, since it is constructed via a minimization procedure,
it will bound a set which will minimize the perimeter. Lemma
2.4
also gives some area bounds for the constructed perimeter minimizing set. These are only needed for the conclusive remarks in Section
5
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Step 1:

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle(E^{l+1},B_{l+1}(p))=P(\tilde{E}^{l+1}\cup E^{l},B_{l+1}(p))

\displaystyle\leq P(\tilde{E}^{l+1},B_{l+1}(p))+P(E^{l},B_{l+1}(p))-P(\tilde{E}^{l+1}\cap E^{l},B_{l+1}(p)).

in (

\mathsf{Ric}_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\geq-(n-1)\delta
.

Consider the metric space obtained by glueing
M
and
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)
along the two boundaries isometric to
S_{1}
.
On this metric space, we consider the distance function from
S_{2}
and we denote it by
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}
.
In particular, this allows us to define
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}
on
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)
(seen as a subset of the previously defined metric space).
We claim that the function
\bar{\mathsf{d}}:=\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}\times\{0\}}
is constant on
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)
.

Let us first show how this is enough to conclude the proof. If
\bar{\mathsf{d}}:=\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}\times\{0\}}
is constant on
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)
, then
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}
is constant when restricted to
{S_{1}}
, which in turn implies that there exists a geodesic
\gamma\subset M
joining
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
whose length is equal to the distance between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. By triangle inequality,
\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}+\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}:M\to\mathbb{R}
attains its minimum on any point of
\gamma
, so that, being superharmonic by Lemma
2.1
, it is constant by the Maximum Principle. The conclusion then follows by Lemma
2.2
.

Hence, we only need to prove that
\bar{\mathsf{d}}:=\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}\times\{0\}}
is constant on
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)
.

By Lemma
2.1
, it holds
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}\leq(n-1)\delta
in distributional sense on
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)
. Hence,

(16)

\Delta(\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}-\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}\times\{0\}})\leq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)\text{ in distributional sense.}

Consider the weight function
V\in\mathsf{Lip}(S_{1}\times(0,+\infty))
given by
V=n^{\delta}\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}
.
We denote by
\Delta_{w}\bar{\mathsf{d}}
the weighted Laplacian of
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
w.r.t.
V
, i.e. the measure
\Delta_{w}\bar{\mathsf{d}}:=\Delta\bar{\mathsf{d}}-\nabla V\cdot\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}
. Thanks to (
16
) and the definition of
V
, it holds

(17)

\Delta_{w}\bar{\mathsf{d}}\leq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)\text{ in distributional sense.}

Set
v:=(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{-1}
and note that, by the chain rule, it holds in distributional sense on
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)

(18)

\Delta_{w}v=-(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{-2}\Delta_{w}\bar{\mathsf{d}}+2(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{-3}|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}\geq 0.

Let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{1}\times[0,+\infty))
be a positive function.
Consider the functions
\phi_{\epsilon}\in\mathsf{Lip}(S_{1}\times[0,+\infty))
defined by
\phi_{\epsilon}(x,t):=(1-\epsilon^{-1}t)\vee 0
.
Integrating by parts, it holds

(19)

\displaystyle\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}(\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi)\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}[\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\phi\phi_{\epsilon})]\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}+\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}[\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\phi(1-\phi_{\epsilon}))]\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}[\nabla v\cdot(\phi\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}+\phi_{\epsilon}\nabla\phi)]\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}-\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\phi(1-\phi_{\epsilon})\,e^{-V}d\Delta_{w}v.

Observe now that
\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}=-\epsilon^{-1}\nabla\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}\times\{0\}}1_{[0,\epsilon]}
, so that

(20)

\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-1}1_{[0,\epsilon]}(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{-2}\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}\times\{0\}}\leq 0.

Hence,
passing to the limit in (
19
) as
\epsilon\to 0
and taking into account (
18
), it holds

(21)

\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}(\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi)\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq 0.

Let
\psi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{1}\times[0,+\infty))
be a positive function.

We repeat a computation from
[
72
]
(see also
[
53
, Lemma
7.1
]
).
By (
21
), it holds

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}[\nabla(\psi^{2}v)\cdot\nabla v]\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}

\displaystyle=2\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\psi v(\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla v)\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}+\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\psi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol},

so that

(22)

\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\psi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq 2\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}|\psi v\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla v|\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}.

By Young’s inequality, we then obtain

\displaystyle 2\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}|\psi v\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla v|\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\psi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}

\displaystyle+8\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}v^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol},

which, combining with (
22
), gives

\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\psi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq 16\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}v^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}.

Recalling the definition of
v
, this implies

(23)

\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}\psi^{2}\frac{|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}}{(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{4}}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq 16\int_{S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}.

Let
x\in S_{1}
and
r>0
. Let
\phi_{1}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{1})
be such that
\phi_{1}\equiv 1
in
B_{r}(x)\subset S_{1}
and
\phi_{2}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}([0,+\infty))
be such that
\phi_{2}\equiv 1
in
[0,r)
, both taking values in
[0,1]
. Let
\psi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{1}\times[0,+\infty))
be defined by
\psi(y,t):=\phi_{1}(y)\phi_{2}(t)
. We denote by
|\cdot|_{t}
and
\nabla^{t}\phi_{1}
the norm and the gradient of
\phi_{1}
w.r.t. the metric
e^{2\delta t}g_{1}
on
S_{1}
, and we note that
|\nabla^{t}\phi_{1}|_{t}=e^{-\delta t}|\nabla^{0}\phi_{1}|_{0}
. In particular, it holds

|\nabla\psi|^{2}(y,t)\leq|\nabla^{0}\phi_{1}|_{0}^{2}(y)+\phi_{1}^{2}(y)(\phi_{2}^{\prime}(t))^{2}.

Hence, with this choice of
\psi
, the r.h.s. in (
23
) is bounded from above by

(24)

\begin{split}&16\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-n^{\delta}t+\delta(n-1)t}\,\mathrm{d}t\int_{S_{1}}|\nabla^{0}\phi_{1}|_{0}^{2}\,d\mathsf{Vol}_{S_{1}}\\
&\quad+16\int_{S_{1}}\phi_{1}^{2}\,d\mathsf{Vol}_{S_{1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\phi_{2}^{\prime}|^{2}e^{-n^{\delta}t+\delta(n-1)t}\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Taking an infimum over all the admissible
\phi_{2}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}([0,+\infty))
such that
\phi_{2}\equiv 1
on
[0,r)
, we deduce

(25)

\int_{B_{r}(x)\times[0,r]}\frac{|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}}{(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{4}}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq 16\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t\int_{S_{1}}|\nabla^{0}\phi_{1}|_{0}^{2}\,d\mathsf{Vol}_{S_{1}}.

Since
S_{1}
is parabolic, taking the infimum of the r.h.s. over all the functions
\phi_{1}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{1})
such that
\phi_{1}\equiv 1
on
B_{r}(x)\subset S_{1}
, we deduce
|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|\equiv 0
on
B_{r}(x)\times[0,r]
. The constancy of
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
follows from the arbitrariness of
r>0
, concluding the proof.
∎

In the previous proof, the weight
V
was needed to compensate for the excessive volume growth of the space
S_{1}\times(0,+\infty)
equipped with

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{2\delta t}g_{1}+\mathrm{d}t^{2}.

In the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we consider
S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)
equipped with

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2t}g_{2}+\mathrm{d}t^{2},

g_{2}
being the metric on
S_{2}
. Hence, the volume growth will be easier to control even without a weight.

Nevertheless, given
f\in C^{\infty}(S_{2})
, its gradient w.r.t.
e^{-2t}g_{2}
increases as

increases. Hence, also in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, a weight is needed, this time to compensate for the previously described gradient growth.
The next theorem implies Theorem
1.3
from the Introduction. It is a slightly more general result that allows for multiple boundary components to satisfy
\mathrm{I\!I}\geq-1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold, with
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)
. Let
\partial M
be the disjoint union
\partial M=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}
, where each
S_{i}
is a union of connected components of
\partial M
. Assume that the following hold.

(1)

S_{1}
is non-empty with
H_{S_{1}}\geq 1
.

(2)

S_{2}
is non-empty, connected, parabolic, with
\mathrm{I\!I}_{S_{2}}\geq-1
, and
\mathsf{Ric}_{S_{2}}\geq 0
.

(3)

S_{3}
has
\mathrm{I\!I}_{S_{3}}\geq-1
, and
\mathsf{Ric}_{S_{3}}\geq 0
.

Then,
S_{3}=\emptyset
, and there exists
l>0
such that
M
is isometric to
S_{1}\times[0,l]
with
the warped product metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2t}g_{1}+\mathrm{d}t^{2}
, where
g_{1}
is the metric on
S_{1}
.

Proof.

We argue as in Theorem
3.1
.
Let

S:=S_{2}\cup S_{3}.

Consider the manifold
S\times(0,+\infty)
equipped with the warped product metric

\tilde{g}=e^{-2t}g_{S}+\mathrm{d}t^{2},

where
g_{S}
is the metric induced by
M
on
S
. By standard computations, the second fundamental form of
S\times\{0\}
in
S\times(0,+\infty)
satisfies

\mathrm{I\!I}_{S\times\{0\}}=1
. Moreover, since
\mathsf{Ric}_{S}\geq 0
, Gauss’ equations imply that

\mathsf{Ric}_{S\times(0,+\infty)}\geq-(n-1).

In a similar fashion, we denote by
g_{2}
the restriction of
g_{S}
to
S_{2}
and we consider
S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)
, which is a connected component of
S\times(0,+\infty)
. We denote by
\mathsf{Vol}
the Riemannian volume in
S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)
w.r.t. the metric
\tilde{g}
and by
\mathsf{Vol}_{S_{2}}
the Riemannian volume on
S_{2}
w.r.t. the metric
g_{2}
.
Let
\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}\times\{0\}}
be the distance from
S_{2}\times\{0\}
in
S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)
.
It holds

\Delta\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}\times\{0\}}=-(n-1)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }S_{2}\times(0,+\infty).

Consider the metric space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
obtained by gluing
M
and
S\times(0,+\infty)
along the two boundaries isometric to
S
. On this metric space, we consider the distance function from
S_{1}
and we denote it by
\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}
.
In particular, this allows us to define
\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}
on
S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)
(seen as a subset of the previously defined metric space).
We first show that the function

\bar{\mathsf{d}}:=\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}-\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}\times\{0\}}

is constant on
S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)
.

By Lemma
2.1
, it holds
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}\leq-(n-1)
in distributional sense on
S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)
. Hence,

(26)

\Delta(\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}-\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}\times\{0\}})\leq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)\text{ in distributional sense.}

Consider the weight function
V\in\mathsf{Lip}(S_{2}\times(0,+\infty))
given by

V=2\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}.

Let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{2}\times[0,+\infty))
be a positive function.
Arguing as in Theorem
3.1
(derivation of (
21
)), one obtains, setting
v:=(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{-1}
,

(27)

\int_{S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)}(\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi)\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq 0.

If
\psi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{2}\times[0,+\infty))
is a positive function, arguing again as in Theorem
3.1
(derivation of (

(28)

\int_{S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)}\psi^{2}\frac{|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}}{(1+\bar{\mathsf{d}})^{4}}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}\leq 16\int_{S_{2}\times(0,+\infty)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,e^{-V}d\mathsf{Vol}.

Let
x\in S_{2}
and
r>0
. Let
\phi_{1}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{2})
be such that
\phi_{1}\equiv 1
in
B_{r}(x)
and
\phi_{2}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}([0,+\infty))
be such that
\phi_{2}\equiv 1
in
[0,r)
, both taking values in

. Let
\psi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(S_{2}\times[0,+\infty))
be defined by
\psi(y,t):=\phi_{1}(y)\phi_{2}(t)
.
We denote by

and

\phi_{1}

|\nabla\psi|^{2}(y,t)\leq e^{2t}|\nabla^{0}\phi_{1}|_{0}^{2}(y)+\phi_{1}^{2}(y)(\phi_{2}^{\prime}(t))^{2}.

With this choice of

, the r.h.s. in (

) is bounded from above by

\begin{split}16&\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-(n-1)t}\,\mathrm{d}t\int_{S_{2}}|\nabla^{0}\phi_{1}|_{0}^{2}\,d\mathsf{Vol}_{S_{2}}\\
&+16\int_{S_{2}}\phi_{1}^{2}\,d\mathsf{Vol}_{S_{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\phi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}e^{-(n-1)t}\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

We now conclude that

is constant on

.
Hence,

, it holds
\Delta(\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}+\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}})\leq 0

is open and closed in
\mathsf{X}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2}\times[0,+\infty))
.

contained in

.
The set

is contained in the

, while
\mathsf{d}_{S_{1}}+\mathsf{d}_{S_{2}}\geq D

4.
Applications

Corollaries
1.7
and
1.9
will be consequences of Theorem
4.1
below, which relates the problem of deciding whether a manifold with disconnected mean-convex boundary is a product with the following well studied question:

•

Given a manifold
(M,g)
, under which conditions is a stable minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
totally geodesic?

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\leq 7
and let
(M^{n},g)
be a manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature and mean-convex disconnected boundary. Assume that any properly embedded, two-sided, stable minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
is parabolic and has
\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq 0
. Then,
(M,g)
splits isometrically as
\Sigma\times[0,l]
, for some manifold
(\Sigma,g^{\prime})
and
l>0
.

Proof.

If one boundary component
\Sigma
of
\partial M
is minimal and stable, then by assumption it is parabolic and it has
\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq 0
. By the second variation formula,
\Sigma
is also totally geodesic. Hence, the claim follows by Theorem
1.1
.

If no connected component of
\partial M
is minimal and stable, we can consider the set
E\subset M
given by Lemma
2.4
relative to a connected component
\Sigma
of
\partial M
. Since
n\leq 7
,
E
is a smooth set. Since
E
minimizes the perimeter w.r.t. inner competitors, its boundary
\partial E
is a properly embedded, two-sided, stable minimal hypersurface. By assumption, each connected component of
\partial E
is parabolic and it has
\mathsf{Ric}_{\partial E}\geq 0
. By Theorem
1.1
, it follows that
E=\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically for some
l>0
. Applying the same argument to
M\setminus E
, we conclude.
∎

Remark 4.2

(On previous related results)
.

The idea of using stable minimal hypersurfaces to deduce existence of a product structure in a manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature is classical, and it has been used, for instance, in
[
5
,
4
,
54
]
. In the aforementioned results, a key step to prove existence of a product structure is to build a
foliation
of stable minimal hypersurfaces in the manifold in question. In
[
5
,
4
]
, this is possible thanks to a uniform bound on the sectional curvature. In
[
54
]
, the existence of such a foliation follows from an assumption on the fundamental group. Theorem
4.1
, being a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, does not require the construction of such a foliation.

Finally, we mention that there
are many other splitting results that can be obtained with different lower curvature bounds, and that involve the existence (or the construction) of either minimal hypersurfaces or
\mu
-bubbles (see
[
42
,
40
]
). Among many others,
[
21
,
12
]
obtain splitting results for
3
-manifolds with non-negative scalar curvature,
while splitting results for manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below in spectral sense are obtained in
[
6
,
13
,
46
]
.

The next result corresponds to Corollary
1.7
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature and disconnected mean-convex boundary. Then,
M=\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically, for some manifold
(\Sigma,g^{\prime})
and
l>0
.

Proof.

By
[
66
]
, any properly embedded, two-sided, stable minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
is totally geodesic, and the Ricci curvature of
M
in the normal direction to
\Sigma
vanishes. In particular,
\Sigma
has non-negative sectional curvature. Hence, by Bishop-Gromov’s inequality,
\Sigma
is parabolic.
The statement then follows by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Before turning our attention to Corollary
1.9
, we briefly discuss the notions of
(n-2)
-Ricci curvature and weakly bounded geometry.

Definition 4.4

(Lower bounds on
(n-2)
-Ricci curvature)
.

A manifold
(M^{n},g)
is said to have non-negative
(n-2)
-Ricci curvature (denoted
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
) if for every
p\in M
and every collection of orthonormal vectors

\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}\subset T_{p}M,

it holds

\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mathsf{Sec}(e_{n-1},e_{i})\geq 0.

The condition
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
is an intermediate condition between
\mathsf{Sec}\geq 0
and
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
, in the sense that
\mathsf{Sec}\geq 0\Rightarrow\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0\Rightarrow\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
.
An important feature of manifolds
(M^{n},g)
with
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
is the following: if
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M^{n}
is a totally geodesic hypersurface and
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
on
M^{n}
, then
\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq 0
on
\Sigma
. This is an immediate consequence of the Gauss equation since, having fixed an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1}\}
of
T_{p}\Sigma
, we have

(30)

\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}(e_{n-1},e_{n-1}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mathsf{Sec}_{\Sigma}(e_{n-1},e_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mathsf{Sec}_{M}(e_{n-1},e_{i})\geq 0.

We now recall the definition of weakly bounded geometry (after
[
22
, Definition 2.3]
).

Definition 4.5

(Weakly bounded geometry)
.

A manifold
(M^{n},g)
without boundary is said to have weakly bounded geometry if there exists
Q>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
satisfying the following. For every
x\in M
, there is an open set
U_{x}\ni x
and a
C^{2,\alpha}

local
diffeomorphism
\phi:B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{Q^{-1}}(0)\to U_{x}
such that

(1)

e^{-2Q}g_{eu}\leq\phi^{*}g\leq e^{2Q}g_{eu}
as bilinear forms.

(2)

\|\partial_{k}\phi^{*}g_{i,j}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq Q
, for every
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

We recall that a manifold
(M^{n},g)
is said to have
m
-bounded geometry (see
[
20
, Section 3]
)
if there exists
Q>0
satisfying the following. For every
x\in M
, there is a geodesic ball
B_{Q^{-1}}(x)
such that the exponential map
\mathrm{exp}_{x}:B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{Q^{-1}}(0)\to B_{Q^{-1}}(x)
is a diffeomorphism and

(1)

e^{-2Q}g_{eu}\leq\mathrm{exp}_{x}^{*}g\leq e^{2Q}g_{eu}
as bilinear forms.

(2)

\|\mathrm{exp}_{x}^{*}g_{i,j}\|_{C^{m}}\leq Q
, for every
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

The main difference between weakly bounded geometry according to Definition
4.5
and
2
-bounded geometry, besides the
C^{1,\alpha}
control on the pull back of the metric being weaker than
C^{2}
control, is that Definition
4.5
does not force a uniform positive lower bound on the volume of balls or on the injectivity radius. To see this, it is enough to consider a hyperbolic cusp, as observed in
[
22
]
. For more background on the notion of weakly bounded geometry, we refer to the original reference
[
22
]
.

Remark 4.6

(Geometric motivations for
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, and weakly bounded geometry)
.

Recently, there has been growing interest in the study of manifolds
(M^{n},g)
with
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
and
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
(see, for instance,
[
71
,
60
,
23
]
). The main conjecture on these manifolds is due to Gromov
[
41
]
, and states that one should have

(31)

\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mathsf{Vol}(B_{r}(p))}{r^{n-2}}<+\infty.

If the conjecture holds, one consequence would be the following:
4
-manifolds with
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, and
b_{1}\neq 0
have universal covering that splits off a line isometrically (compare with Theorem
1.10
). This would be true because a
4
-manifold as above has at most linear volume growth at infinity by
[
4
]
, so that its universal covering splits off a line by
[
47
]
.

Manifolds with
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, and weakly bounded geometry provide a convenient subclass in which to study the geometric consequences of combined lower bounds on Ricci and Scalar curvature.

In dimension
4
, these stronger assumptions imply that stable minimal hypersurfaces are totally geodesic by
[
22
]
(compare with
[
22
, Example 1.9]
). This fact is key in proving is Corollary
1.9
and Theorem
1.10
. We remark that the assumption of weakly bounded geometry is only needed to apply the results from
[
22
]
, and is otherwise not used in the paper.

The next result corresponds to Corollary
1.9
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(M^{4},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
, scalar curvature
\geq 1
, and weakly bounded geometry. Let
N^{4}\subset M^{4}
be a smooth submanifold with mean-convex disconnected boundary. Then,
N=\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically, for some manifold
(\Sigma,g^{\prime})
and
l>0
.

Proof.

By
[
22
]
, any properly embedded, two-sided, stable minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset N
is parabolic and totally geodesic.
The statement then follows by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

The next result corresponds to Theorem
1.10
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a manifold without boundary such that
either

•

n=3
, and
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
,

or

•

n=4
,
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, and
M
has weakly bounded geometry.

If
b_{1}(M)\neq 0
, then the universal covering of
M
splits a line isometrically.

Proof.

Modulo passing to a covering of the manifold, we may assume that
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\gamma\subset M
be a smooth loop generating
\pi_{1}(M)
.
By the argument of
[
66
, p.217, proof of Lemma 3]
, there exists a properly embedded stable minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
intersecting (a perturbation of)
\gamma
transversally with algebraic intersection number
1
. By
[
66
]
and
[
22
]
,
\Sigma
is totally geodesic and satisfies
\mathsf{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\equiv 0
,
\nu
being the normal to
\Sigma
.

Step 1
:
\Sigma
is
parabolic, and satisfies
\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq 0
.

Let us consider first the case where
n=3
and
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq 0
. In this case, the Gauss equations together with the fact that
\mathsf{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\equiv 0
imply that
\Sigma
has non-negative scalar curvature. Since
\Sigma
has dimension
2
, then it has non-negative sectional curvature and it is parabolic by Bishop-Gromov’s inequality.

Let us consider now the case where
n=4
, and
M
has
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
and weakly bounded geometry. By the Gauss equations, the assumption
\mathsf{Ric}_{2}\geq 0
implies that
\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq 0
(see the discussion around (
30
)).
Moreover, combining the Gauss equations, the fact that
\mathsf{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\equiv 0
, and
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, it follows that
\Sigma
has itself scalar curvature greater than
1
. In particular, by
[
60
]
,
\Sigma
has at most linear volume growth so that it is parabolic as claimed.

Step 2:

The image of the map
i_{\#}:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\pi_{1}(M)
induced by the inclusion is trivial.

Indeed, if this were not the case, then
i_{\#}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma))\subset\pi_{1}(M)
, being a subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, would contain a copy of
\mathrm{span}(\gamma^{k})
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
\gamma^{k}
can be homotopically deformed into a loop fully contained in
\Sigma
, which can be then deformed into a loop which is disjoint from
\Sigma
(moving away from
\Sigma
in its tubular neighbourhood). Hence, the intersection number of
\gamma^{k}
with
\Sigma
is zero. At the same time, since the intersection number of
\gamma
and
\Sigma
is one, it follows that the intersection number of
\Sigma
with
\gamma^{k}
is
k
, a contradiction.

Step 3:
conclusion of the proof.

Let
p:\tilde{M}\to M
be the universal covering of
M
, and let
q\in\Sigma\cap\gamma
. Let
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
be lifts of
\Sigma
passing through distinct points
q_{1},q_{2}\in\tilde{M}\cap p^{-1}(q)
. Observe that these exist by Step
2
and the standard lifting criterion. These two hypersurfaces are disjoint, since otherwise we would lose uniqueness of the lift at the points that they share. Consider an
n
-manifold with boundary
V\subset\tilde{M}
such that
\partial V=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
topologically (such
V
exists since
\tilde{M}
is simply connected, and
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
are properly embedded codimension-1 submanifolds without boundary).
By Corollaries
1.7
and
1.9
, it holds
V\cong\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically for some
l>0
.
Iterating this procedure for every pair of points
q_{1},q_{2}\in\tilde{M}\cap p^{-1}(q)
, we conclude.
∎

Remark 4.9

(On higher dimensional versions of Corollaries
1.7
,
1.9
, and Theorem
1.10
)
.

Both Corollary
1.7
and Theorem
1.10
(when considered under the assumptions of Corollary
1.7
) fail in higher dimensions. Indeed, there is a metric with
\mathsf{Ric}>0
on
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
constructed by Nabonnand in
[
61
]
. By the procedure used in the proof of Theorem
1.10
, in the universal covering of
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there is a region
V
whose boundary is disconnected and consists of minimal hypersurfaces. Nevertheless, this region has
\mathsf{Ric}>0
. In particular, it cannot split.

On the other hand, Corollary
1.9
and Theorem
1.10
(when considered under the assumptions of Corollary
1.9
) are expected to hold in dimension
5
. This would follow (by adapting the arguments above) if one could show that, in a
5
-manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{3}\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, and weakly bounded geometry, any boundary of a perimeter-minimizing set is totally geodesic.

There are several reasons to believe that this is the case (see
[
22
, Remark 1.8]
). One indication is that, assuming Gromov’s volume growth conjecture mentioned in Remark
4.6
, such manifolds have at most cubic volume growth at infinity. It would then follow that the boundaries of perimeter-minimizing sets (endowed with their intrinsic metric) are parabolic. The desired conclusion would then follow from the second variation formula combined with the logarithmic cut-off argument.

We note that this strategy would only require
\mathsf{Ric}_{3}\geq 0
and
\mathrm{Scal}\geq 1
, without the need for the weakly bounded geometry assumption.

Finally, the next result corresponds to Corollary
1.11
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\delta\in\{0,1\}
. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a parabolic manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{M}\geq 0
and let
M\times\mathbb{R}
be equipped with
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2t\delta}g+\mathrm{d}t^{2}
. If
E\subset M\times(0,+\infty)
is a smooth closed set with connected boundary and mean curvature
H_{\partial E}\leq\delta
, then
E=M\times[a,+\infty)
, for some
a>0
.
If
E\subset M\times(-\infty,0)
is a smooth closed set with connected boundary and mean curvature
H_{\partial E}\leq-\delta
, then
E=M\times(-\infty,a]
, for some
a<0
.

Proof.

Let
E\subset M\times(0,+\infty)
be with mean curvature satisfying
H_{\partial E}\leq\delta
. Consider the manifold

N:=M\times(0,+\infty)\setminus E.

Then,
\partial N=\partial E\cup M\times\{0\}
. Since the second fundamental form of
M\times\{0\}
in
M\times(0,+\infty)
is equal to
\delta
, the result follows from Theorem
1.1
9f
\delta=0
and Theorem
1.2
if
\delta=1
.
The case when
E\subset M\times(-\infty,0)
has mean curvature satisfying
H_{\partial E}\leq-\delta
follows analogously, applying Theorem
1.3
instead of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

5.
Conclusive remarks

In this final section, we give a partial answer to the question posed in Remark
1.5
. In Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
below, we prove variants of Theorem
1.1
where the assumptions on the second fundamental form and the Ricci curvature of the parabolic boundary component are removed. Nevertheless, we compensate by strengthening the curvature assumptions on the ambient space (
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
instead of
\mathsf{Ric}\geq 0
), and by requiring different additional conditions on a boundary component.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
and disconnected mean-convex boundary. Let
\Sigma\subset\partial M
be a boundary component which is minimal, stable, and parabolic.
Then,
M=\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically for some
l>0
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is minimal, stable, and parabolic, then it is totally geodesic by the second variation formula of the area. Since
M
has
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
, we then obtain that
\mathsf{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq 0
. Hence, the statement follows by Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,p)
be a pointed manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
and disconnected mean-convex boundary. Let
\Sigma\subset\partial M
be a boundary component with

(32)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\mathsf{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{t}(p))+\mathsf{H}^{n-1}(B_{t}(p)\cap\Sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

Then,
M=\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically for some
l>0
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
2.4
, and we observe that if item
1
of Lemma
2.4
is satisfied, taking into account our volume growth assumption, we find a connected component of
\partial M
which is minimal, stable, and parabolic (due to the assumption (
32
)). In this case the conclusion follows by Theorem
5.1
.

Hence, consider the set
E\subset M
provided by item
2
of Lemma
2.4
relative to
\Sigma
. By (
32
), it holds

(33)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\mathsf{H}^{n-1}(\partial E\cap B_{t}(p))}\,dt=+\infty.

By repeating the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 3.1]
(cf.
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
), and using that
E
minimizes the perimeter in
M\setminus\partial M
, it follows that
\partial E
is smooth and totally geodesic. Since
\mathsf{Ric}_{n-2}\geq 0
, we deduce
\mathsf{Ric}_{\partial E}\geq 0
. Moreover, (
33
) implies that each connected component of
\partial E
is parabolic. Hence,
E
is a manifold with boundary, whose boundary is the union of its topological boundary
\partial E\subset M
in
M
, and
\Sigma
. By Theorem
1.1
,
E=\Sigma\times[0,l]
isometrically for some
l>0
. Applying the same argument to
M\setminus E
, we obtain the statement.
∎

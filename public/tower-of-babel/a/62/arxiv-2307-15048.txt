Correspondence coloring of random graphs
Zdeněk Dvořák, Liana Yepremyan

Abstract
We show that Erdős-Rényi random graphs G ⁡ ( n , p ) G(n,p) with constant density p < 1 p<1 have correspondence chromatic number O ⁡ ( n / log ⁡ n ) O(n/\sqrt{\log n}) ; this matches a prediction from linear Hadwiger’s conjecture for correspondence coloring. The proof follows from a simple sufficient condition for correspondence colorability in terms of the numbers of independent sets.



1
Introduction

Hadwiger’s conjecture famously states that every
K_{k}
-minor-free
graph is
(k-1)
-colorable. This is known to be true when
k\leq 6
, as shown by
Robertson, Seymour and Thomas
[
14
]
. The validity of the conjecture for general
k

is a widely open and extremely challenging problem.
Let
f(k)
denote the maximum chromatic number of
K_{k}
-minor-free graphs, so Hadwidger’s conjecture
is the claim that
f(k)=k-1
. For more than 30 years, the best partial result towards the conjecture
was that
f(k)=O(k\log^{1/2}k)
, which follows from the tight bounds on the average degree of
K_{k}
-minor-free graphs
[
11
,
16
]
.
This barrier was broken in 2019 by Norin, Postle and Song
[
12
]
who improved the bound to
O(k\log^{1/4+o(1)}k)
.
This started a series of improvements culminating in the current best bound of
O(k\log\log k)
by Delcourt and Postle
[
8
]
.
A key element of these improvements is an understanding of the chromatic number of small
K_{k}
-minor-free graphs.
Indeed, Delcourt and Postle
[
8
]
shows that
f(k)=O(f^{\prime}(k))
, where
f^{\prime}(k)
denotes the maximum chromatic
number of
K_{k}
-minor-free graphs with
O(k\log^{4}k)
vertices.

The analogous problem has been also studied in the list coloring setting. A
list assignment
for a graph
G
is a function
L

that to each vertex
v
assigns a list
L(v)
of available colors, and an
L
-coloring
of
G
is a proper coloring
\varphi

such that
\varphi(v)\in L(v)
for every
v\in V(G)
. The minimum integer
s
such that
G
can be
L
-colored for every
assigment
L
of lists of size at least
s
is called the
list chromatic number
(or
choosability
) of
G
.
Analogously to the proper coloring case, we define
f_{l}(k)
as the maximum
of the list chromatic numbers of
K_{k}
-minor-free graphs. Hadwiger’s conjecture is known to be false in the list
coloring setting; Steiner
[
15
]
proved that
f_{l}(k)\geq(2-o(1))k
. On the other hand,
Linear Hadwiger’s Conjecture for list coloring proposes that
f_{l}(k)=O(k)
. The best partial result is by Postle
[
13
]
,
who proved that
f_{l}(k)=O(k(\log\log k)^{6})
. Again, an important starting point for the argument is a bound on the list
chromatic number of small graphs.

Dvořák and Postle
[
9
]
introduced
correspondence coloring
(also known as
DP-coloring
),
a further generalization of list coloring by refining the constraints on coloring adjacent vertices;
this notion makes it possible to use certain inductive arguments not available in the list coloring setting.
The
correspondence assignment
for a graph
G
is a pair
(L,M)
, where
L
is a list assignment
and
M=\{M_{e}:e\in E(G)\}
assigns a (not necessarily perfect) matching
M_{e}
between
\{u\}\times L(u)
and
\{v\}\times L(v)

to every edge
e=uv\in E(G)
. We say that
(L,M)
is an
\ell
-correspondence assignment
if
L
assigns at least
\ell
colors to each vertex.
An
(L,M)
-coloring
of
G
is any assignment
\varphi
of colors to
vertices of
G
such that
\varphi(v)\in L(v)
for every
v\in V(G)
and for
every
e=uv\in E(G)
, the vertices
(u,\varphi(u))
and
(v,\varphi(v))
are
non-adjacent in
M_{e}
. The
correspondence chromatic number
of
G
is the smallest integer
\ell
such that
G
has an
(L,M)
-coloring for every

\ell
-correspondence assignment
(L,M)
.

The correspondence chromatic number has attracted a lot of attention. Among its interesting properties, let us mention the following
results by Bernshteyn
[
3
,
4
]
: Every graph of average degree
d
has correspondence chromatic number at least
\Omega(d/\log d)
,
while every triangle-free graph of maximum degree
\Delta
has correspondence chromatic number at most
O(\Delta/\log\Delta)
.
Consequently, every triangle-free
d
-regular graph has correspondence chromatic number
\Theta(d/\log d)
.

Let
f_{c}(k)
denote the maximum correspondence chromatic number of
K_{k}
-minor-free graphs. Clearly
f_{c}(k)\geq f_{l}(k)
, and
thus Hadwiger’s conjecture does not hold in the correspondence chromatic number setting.
However, unlike the list coloring case, the best known bound
f_{c}(k)=O(k\sqrt{\log k})
still matches the maximum average degree.
One of the reasons why the arguments from the list coloring setting do not apply is that we do not know how to bound the correspondence
chromatic number of small graphs. Indeed, as Norin (personal communication) pointed out, nontrivial bounds are not known even for dense
random graphs; this is very relevant for the considerations of Hadwiger’s conjecture, since the tight lower bounds on the average
degree of
K_{k}
-minor-free graphs come exactly from such random graphs.

As the main result of this note, we give an upper bound on the correspondence chromatic number of Erdős-Rényi random graphs
with fixed density of edges; the bound implies that Linear Hadwiger’s conjecture for correspondence coloring holds asymptotially almost surely.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every fixed positive
p\leq 1
, Erdős-Rényi random graph
G(n,1-p)
a.a.s. has correspondence chromatic number at most
O(n/\sqrt{\log n})
.

The
Hadwiger number

h(G)
of a graph
G
is the maximum
k
such that
K_{k}
is a minor of
G
.
For fixed
0<p<1
, we have
h(G(n,p))=\Theta(n/\sqrt{\log n}))
a.a.s., see
[
5
]
. Hence, Theorem
1.1

supports Linear Hadwiger’s conjecture for correspondence coloring.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every fixed
0\leq p\leq 1
, Erdős-Rényi random graph
G(n,p)
a.a.s. has correspondence chromatic number
O(h(G))
.

The best lower bound for Theorem
1.1
that we are aware of comes from the well-known fact that for positive
p\leq 1
,
the random graph
G(n,1-p)
a.a.s. has independence number
\Omega(\log n)
, and thus
\chi(G(n,1-p))=\Omega(n/\log n)
.
Let us remark that the list chromatic number of
G(n,1-p)
is known to be
O(n/\log n)
a.a.s., see e.g.
[
1
]
.

We have already mentioned the general lower bound of Bernshteyn
[
3
]
, stating that
every graph of average degree
d
has correspondence chromatic number at least
\Omega(d/\log d)
; this also gives
\Omega(n/\log n)

as a lower bound for the correspondence chromatic number of
G(n,1-p)
for any positive
p<1
. The lower bound of Bernshteyn comes from considering
a random
\ell
-correspondence assignment, an assignment
(L,M)
such that
L(v)=\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
for each vertex
v

and
M_{e}
is chosen independently uniformly at random among the perfect matchings between
\{u\}\times\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and
\{v\}\times\{1,\ldots,\ell\}

for each edge
e=uv
. It is natural to ask whether a better analysis would not give an improved lower bound in the
case the graph we color is also random. However, as our second result, we show that this cannot be the case,
even when we consider the coloring of complete graphs.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists a function
\ell(n)=\Theta(n/\log n)
such that if
(L,M)
is a random
\ell(n)
-correspondence
assignment for the complete graph
K_{n}
, then
K_{n}
is a.a.s.
(L,M)
-colorable.

As both extreme cases of the correspondence assignment (matching the list coloring vs. completely random)
for
G(n,1-p)
only requre
O(n/\log n)
colors, we suspect that the
upper bound given in Theorem
1.1
can be improved.

Conjecture 1.4
.

For every fixed positive
p\leq 1
, Erdős-Rényi random graph
G(n,1-p)
a.a.s. has correspondence chromatic number
\Theta(n/\log n)
.

It is our hope that this conjecture will actually turn out to be false, as this would give another interesting
example of a difference of behavior between the list and correspondence chromatic numbers.

2
Sufficient condition for colorability

Our proofs are based on a sufficient condition for colorability from a given correspondence assignment that
can be extracted from the proof of Bernshteyn
[
3
]
. Let us start with some definitions.
For a nonnegative integer
n
and a nonnegative real number
b
, let

\binom{n}{\leq b}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor b\rfloor}\binom{n}{i}

denote the number of subsets of an
n
-element set of size at most
k
. Let us remark that if
n\geq 3b
, then

\binom{n}{\lfloor b\rfloor}\leq\binom{n}{\leq b}\leq 2\binom{n}{\lfloor b\rfloor}.

(1)

The
cover graph
of a correspondence assignment
(L,M)
is the graph with vertex set
\bigcup_{v\in V(G)}(\{v\}\times L(v))
and edge set
\bigcup_{e\in E(G)}M_{e}
.

For a graph
G
, let
I(G)
denote the number of (not necessarily largest or
maximal) independent sets in
G
. A
neighborhood subgraph
of
G
is any
subgraph of
G
whose vertices are contained in a neighborhood of a vertex of

G
. For example, every neighborhood subgraph of a triangle-free graph is
edgeless. Let
G
be a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
. For a nonnegative real number
b
, we say that a subgraph
F
of
G
is
b
-large
if
\binom{|V(F)|}{\leq b}>\Delta^{1/3}
.
We say that
G
is
b
-IS-rich
if every
b
-large neighborhood subgraph
F
of
G
satisfies

I(F)\geq 2\binom{|V(F)|}{\leq b}.

A correspondence assignment for
G
is
b
-IS-rich
if the same condition
holds for
b
-large neighborhood subgraphs of its cover graph. Note that
neighborhood subgraphs of the cover graph are isomorphic to neighborhood subgraphs of
G
,
and thus if
G
is
b
-IS-rich, then so is every correspondence assignment for
G
.

Example 2.1
.

If
G
is a triangle-free graph, then every neighborhood subgraph
F
of
G
is edgeless, and letting
s=|V(F)|
, we have
I(F)=2^{s}
.
Let
b=\tfrac{1}{6}\log_{2}\Delta
. If
s\leq 2b
, then
\binom{s}{\leq b}\leq 2^{s}\leq 2^{2b}=\Delta^{1/3}
.
Hence, if
F
is
b
-large, then
s>2b
and
I(F)\geq 2^{s}\geq 2\binom{s}{\leq(s-1)/2}\geq 2\binom{s}{\leq b}
.
Consequently,
G
is
(\tfrac{1}{6}\log_{2}\Delta)
-IS-rich.

The argument of Bernshteyn
[
4
]
concerning correspondence chromatic number of graphs of bounded clique number
actually applies to IS-rich graphs, giving the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

For every function
b:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
such that
b(\Delta)\leq\Delta^{o(1)}
, there exists a function
\ell:\mathbb{N}+\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\ell(\Delta)=\Theta(\Delta/b(\Delta))
and the following
claim holds. If
G
is a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
and
(L,M)
is a
b(\Delta)
-IS-rich
\ell(\Delta)
-correspondence
assignment for
G
, then
G
is
(L,M)
-colorable.

Since the proof is almost identical to the one in
[
4
]
, we give it in Appendix.
Let us note the following obvious consequence.

Corollary 2.3
.

For every function
b:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
such that
b(\Delta)\leq\Delta^{o(1)}
, there exists a function
\ell:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with
\ell(\Delta)=\Theta(\Delta/b(\Delta))

such that every
b(\Delta)
-IS-rich graph of maximum degree
\Delta
has correspondence chromatic number at most
\ell(\Delta)
.

We are not the first to make a similar observation; Theorem 1.13 in
[
6
]
is quite related
and
[
7
]
provides a substantially more powerful framework for proving this kind of colorability results (e.g., unlike their
approach, IS-richness does not apply to graphs where each neighborhood induces only
o(\Delta^{2})
edges). However,
we find the formulation above quite useful, as it is often easy to establish IS-richness. In particular, if we establish a lower bound

I(F)\geq g(|V(F)|)
valid for every (sufficiently large) neighborhood subgraph
F
of
G
, the following observation can be used to determine a
function
b
such that
G
is
b(\Delta(G))
-IS-rich,
by selecting
b(\Delta)
to be maximum such that
the conditions (
2
), (
3
),
and (
4
) hold.

Observation 2.4
.

Suppose
g:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
is an increasing unbounded derivable
function with
g(0)=0
, and for any integer
\Delta\geq 0
,
let
s_{\Delta}=\lceil g^{-1}(\Delta^{1/3})\rceil
.
Let
\Delta_{0}
be a non-negative integer and suppose that
a function
b:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
for every integer
\Delta\geq\Delta_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta^{1/3},

(2)

\displaystyle b(\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq s_{\Delta}/3\text{, and}

(3)

\displaystyle(\log g(s))^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2b(\Delta)}{s}\text{ for every $s\geq s_{\Delta}$.}

(4)

If
G
is a graph of maximum degree
\Delta\geq\Delta_{0}
and every neighborhood subgraph
F
of
G
with

s\geq s_{\Delta}
vertices satisfies
I(F)\geq g(s)
, then
G
is
b(\Delta)
-IS-rich.

Proof.

Consider any
b
-large neighborhood subgraph
F
of
G
with
s
vertices.
Note that
\binom{s_{\Delta}}{\leq b(\Delta)}\leq 2\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}\leq\Delta^{1/3}
by (
3
) and (
1
) and by (
2
); since
F
is
b
-large,
this implies
s>s_{\Delta}
. Then (
4
) implies

\displaystyle\log\frac{g(s)}{g(s_{\Delta})}

\displaystyle=\int_{s_{\Delta}}^{s}(\log g(x))^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{s_{\Delta}}^{s}\frac{2b(\Delta)}{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=2b(\Delta)\log\frac{s}{s_{\Delta}},

and thus

g(s)\geq g(s_{\Delta})\cdot\Bigl(\frac{s}{s_{\Delta}}\Bigr)^{2b(\Delta)}.

For any non-negative integer
i\leq s_{\Delta}/2
,
we have
\frac{s-i}{s_{\Delta}-i}\leq\bigl(\frac{s}{s_{\Delta}}\bigr)^{2}
; indeed, this inequality is equivalent
to
(s+s_{\Delta})i\leq ss_{\Delta}
, which holds when
s\geq s_{\Delta}\geq 2i
. Since
b(\Delta)-1<s_{\Delta}/2
by (
3
), we have

\frac{\binom{s}{b(\Delta)}}{\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}}=\prod_{i=0}^{b(\Delta)-1}\frac{s-i}{s_{\Delta}-i}\leq\Bigl(\frac{s}{s_{\Delta}}\Bigr)^{2b(\Delta)}.

Finally, note that

g(s_{\Delta})\geq\Delta^{1/3}\geq 4\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}

by the definition of
s_{\Delta}
and (
2
). Combining these inequalities and using (
1
) and (
3
), we obtain

I(F)\geq g(s)\geq g(s_{\Delta})\cdot\Bigl(\frac{s}{s_{\Delta}}\Bigr)^{2b(\Delta)}\geq 4\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}\cdot\frac{\binom{s}{b(\Delta)}}{\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}}=4\binom{s}{b(\Delta)}\geq 2\binom{s}{\leq b(\Delta)}.

Therefore,
G
is
b(\Delta)
-IS-rich.
∎

Given a lower bound
g(s)
on the number of independent sets in graphs from the
considered graph class, one typically uses the condition (
2
) to determine
b(\Delta)
,
and then mechanically verifies that conditions (
3
) and (
4
) hold.

Example 2.5
.

Suppose
G
is an
(r+1)
-colorable graph for a fixed integer
r\geq 2
, and let
F
be a neighborhood subgraph with
s
vertices.
Then
F
is
r
-colorable, and thus
\alpha(F)\geq s/r
and
I(F)\geq g(s)
for
g(s)=2^{s/r}
.
We have
s_{\Delta}=\lceil g^{-1}(\Delta^{1/3})\rceil=\Theta(r\log\Delta)
. Choosing
\gamma=\Theta(r\log r)
so that
(e\gamma)^{1/\gamma}\leq\sqrt[2r]{2}
and
\gamma\geq 2r/\log 2
,
let
b(\Delta)=\lfloor s_{\Delta}/\gamma\rfloor=\Theta(\log_{r}\Delta)
. Since
\gamma>3
, (
3
) holds. Moreover,

\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}\leq\left(\frac{es_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}\right)^{b(\Delta)}\leq(e\gamma)^{s_{\Delta}/\gamma}=2^{\frac{s_{\Delta}}{2r}}=\sqrt{g(s_{\Delta})}\leq 2\sqrt{\Delta^{1/3}}\leq\frac{1}{4}{\Delta^{1/3}}

for
\Delta
sufficiently large; hence (
2
) holds. Finally,
(\log g(s))^{\prime}=\frac{\log 2}{r}\geq\frac{2}{\gamma}\geq\frac{2b(\Delta)}{s_{\Delta}}\geq\frac{2b(\Delta)}{s}
when
s\geq s_{\Delta}
,
implying that (
4
) holds.
Hence, Observation
2.4
shows that
G
is
\Theta(\log_{r}\Delta)
-IS-rich if its maximum degree
\Delta
is sufficiently large.

Example 2.6
.

Suppose
G
is a graph of clique number at most
r+1
for a fixed integer
r\geq 2
, and let
F
be a neighborhood subgraph with
s
vertices.
Since
\omega(F)\leq r
, we have
\alpha(F)\geq s^{1/r}-1
and
I(F)\geq g(s)
for
g(s)=2^{s^{1/r}-1}
. Therefore,
s_{\Delta}=\Theta(\log^{r}\Delta)
.
Choosing sufficiently small
b(\Delta)=\Theta\Bigl(\frac{\log\Delta}{r\log\log\Delta}\Bigr)
, for sufficiently large
\Delta
we have

\binom{s_{\Delta}}{b(\Delta)}\leq s_{\Delta}^{b(\Delta)}\leq\frac{1}{4}\Delta^{1/3}.

Moreover, if
\Delta
is sufficiently large, then

\displaystyle(\log g(s))^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log 2}{rs^{1-1/r}}=\Theta\Bigl(\frac{1}{r\log^{r-1}\Delta}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\Theta\Bigl(\frac{1}{r\log^{r-1}\Delta\log\log\Delta}\Bigr)=\frac{2b(\Delta)}{s_{\Delta}}\geq\frac{2b(\Delta)}{s}

when
s\geq s_{\Delta}
. Hence, Observation
2.4
shows that
G
is
\Theta\Bigl(\frac{\log\Delta}{r\log\log\Delta}\Bigr)
-IS-rich if its maximum degree
\Delta
is sufficiently large.

Hence, Corollary
2.3
together with Examples
2.1
,
2.5
and
2.6
gives the following (previously known) results: The correspondence chromatic number of a graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
is

•

O(\Delta/\log\Delta)
if
G
is triangle-free,

•

O(\Delta/\log_{r}\Delta)
if
G
is
(r+1)
-colorable, and

•

O(r\Delta\log\log\Delta/\log\Delta)
if
\omega(G)\leq r+1
.

Observe that the neighborhood subgraphs of random correspondence assignment for a dense graph are quite sparse;
in particular for the complete graph
K_{n}
, they a.a.s. have clique number at most five, and thus Theorem
2.2
implies that

K_{n}
is a.a.s. colorable from a random
\Theta(n\log\log n/\log n)
-corespondence assignment. Theorem
1.3
follows
by a slightly more involved analysis.

3
Independent sets in random graphs

To prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, we need corresponding (a.a.s. valid) lower bounds on the number of independent
sets in random graphs. Let

I_{p}(s,t)=\binom{s}{t}p^{\binom{t}{2}}

be the expected number of independent sets of size
t
in
G(s,1-p)
.
This expression is maximized at around
t=\frac{\log s}{\log 1/p}
, at which point we have

I_{p}(s,t)\geq\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{t}p^{t^{2}/2}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{s}\log 1/p}{\log s}\right)^{t}=e^{\frac{1}{2\log 1/p}\log^{2}s-o(\log^{2}s)}.

Hence, we want to show that the number of independent sets is a.a.s. close to the expectation. For that, we use Suen’s concentration
inequality for weakly correlated events. Let us give definitions necessary to state it. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{i}:i\in I\}
be a finite set of 0-1 random variables
defined on a common probability space.
A graph
\Gamma
with vertex set
I
is a
dependency graph
of
\mathcal{A}
if for every disjoint
X,Y\subset I

such that
\Gamma
has no edges between
X
and
Y
, the sets of events
\{A_{i}:i\in X\}
and
\{A_{i}:i\in Y\}
are independent of each other.
Let

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{i\in I}A_{i}\Bigr]

\displaystyle d_{i}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{j:ij\in E(\Gamma)}A_{j}\Bigr]\text{for each $i\in I$}

\displaystyle d(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in I}d_{i}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle D(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{ij\in E(\Gamma)}A_{i}A_{j}\Bigr].

We write
\mu
,
d_{i}
,
d
and
D
when
\mathcal{A}
and
\Gamma
are clear from the context.

Theorem 3.1

(Janson
[
10
, Theorem 10 with
a=1/2
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{i}:i\in I\}
be a finite set of 0-1 random variables
defined on a common probability space and let
\Gamma
be a dependency graph of
\mathcal{A}
.
Then

\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i\in I}A_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu\right]\leq\exp\left(-\min\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{32D+8\mu},\frac{\mu}{12d}\right)\right).

We now apply this inequality to the number of intependent sets.

Lemma 3.2
.

For positive integers
s
and
t
and a positive real number
p\leq 1
such that

s\geq 2t^{2}+t
and
I_{p}(s,t)\geq s^{2}/t^{4}
, the probability that
G(s,1-p)

has at most
\tfrac{1}{2}I_{p}(s,t)
independent sets of size
t
is at most

\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}p}{80t^{4}}\right).

Proof.

Consider a randomly chosen graph
G=G(s,1-p)
. Let
I
be the set of unordered
t
-tuples of vertices of
G
,
and for
S\in I
, let
A_{S}=1
if
S
forms an independent set in
G
and
A_{S}=0
otherwise.
We have
\mathbb{E}[A_{S}]=p^{\binom{t}{2}}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{S}:S\in I\}
and let
\Gamma
be the graph with vertex set
I
where two
t
-tuples in
I

are adjacent iff their intersection has size at least two. Then
\Gamma
is a dependency graph of
\mathcal{A}
,
since if
X,Y\subseteq I
are disjoint and non-adjacent in
\Gamma
, then the values of
variables in
\{A_{S}:S\in X\}
and in
\{A_{S}:S\in Y\}
are determined by the presence or absence of edges in
G

on disjoint sets of pairs of vertices.

Clearly
\sum_{S\in I}A_{S}
is the number of independent sets of size
t
in
G
, and
\mu=\mu(\mathcal{A})=I_{p}(s,t)
.
Note that for non-negative
i\leq t-2
, we have

\frac{\binom{t}{i}\binom{s}{t-i}}{\binom{t}{i+1}\binom{s}{t-i-1}}=\frac{(i+1)(s-t+i+1)}{(t-i)^{2}}\geq\frac{s-t}{t^{2}}\geq 2

by the assumptions. Hence, for any
S\in I
,

\displaystyle d_{S}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T:ST\in E(\Gamma)}\mathbb{E}[A_{T}]=p^{\binom{t}{2}}\cdot\sum_{i=2}^{t-1}\binom{t}{i}\binom{s-t}{t-i}\leq p^{\binom{t}{2}}\cdot\sum_{i=2}^{t-1}\binom{t}{i}\binom{s}{t-i}

\displaystyle\leq p^{\binom{t}{2}}\cdot\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-i}=2p^{\binom{t}{2}}\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}.

Therefore,

\frac{\mu}{d(\Gamma)}\geq\frac{\binom{s}{t}p^{\binom{t}{2}}}{2p^{\binom{t}{2}}\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}}=\frac{\binom{s}{t}}{2\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}}\geq\frac{(s-t)^{2}}{t^{4}}\geq\frac{s^{2}}{4t^{4}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle D(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{S\in I}\sum_{i=2}^{t-1}\sum_{T\in I:|S\cap T|=i}\mathbb{E}[A_{S}A_{T}]=\frac{1}{2}\binom{s}{t}p^{2\binom{t}{2}}\sum_{i=2}^{t-1}\binom{t}{i}\binom{s-t}{t-i}p^{-\binom{i}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\binom{s}{t}p^{2\binom{t}{2}-1}\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-i}=\binom{s}{t}p^{2\binom{t}{2}-1}\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}.

Let
D=D(\Gamma)
. If
D\geq\mu
, then

\frac{\mu^{2}}{32D+8\mu}\geq\frac{\mu^{2}}{40D}\geq\frac{\binom{s}{t}^{2}p^{2\binom{t}{2}}}{40\binom{s}{t}p^{2\binom{t}{2}-1}\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}}=\frac{\binom{s}{t}p}{40\binom{t}{2}\binom{s}{t-2}}\geq\frac{s^{2}p}{80t^{4}}.

On the other hand, if
D<\mu
, then

\frac{\mu^{2}}{32D+8\mu}\geq\frac{\mu^{2}}{40\mu}=\frac{I_{p}(s,t)}{40}\geq\frac{s^{2}}{40t^{4}}

by the assumptions. The result now follows by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

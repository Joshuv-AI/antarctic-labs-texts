Can supermassive black holes triggered by general QCD axion bubbles? Preprint: BNU-23-043
Hai-Jun Li, Ying-Quan Peng, Wei Chao, Yu-Feng Zhou

Abstract
The supermassive black holes (SMBHs) are ubiquitous in the center of galaxies, although the origin of their massive seeds is still unknown. In this paper, we investigate the SMBHs formation from the general QCD axion bubbles. In this case, the primordial black holes (PBHs) are considered as the seeds of SMBHs, which are generated from the QCD axion bubbles due to an additional Peccei-Quinn (PQ) symmetry breaking after inflation. The QCD axion bubbles are formed when the QCD axion starts to oscillate during the QCD phase transition (PT). We consider the general case in which the QCD axion bubbles are formed with the bubble effective angle θ eff ∈ ( 0 , π ] \theta_{\rm eff}\in(0,\,\pi] , leading to the minimum PBH mass ∼ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 4 − 10 7 ) ​ M ⊙ \sim\mathcal{O}(10^{4}-10^{7})M_{\odot} with θ eff ∼ π \theta_{\rm eff}\sim\pi to π / 3 \pi/3 for the axion scale f a ∼ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 16 ) ​ GeV f_{a}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{16})\,\rm GeV . The PBHs at this mass region may be the seeds of SMBHs.



I
Introduction

The supermassive black holes (SMBHs) with the mass
\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{6}-10^{9})M_{\odot}
are ubiquitous in the center of galaxies
Kormendy and Richstone 1995
;
Richstone et al. 1998
;
Kormendy and Ho 2013
, where
M_{\odot}
is the solar mass.
They are also considered as the central engines of active galactic nuclei (AGN) and quasars
Lynden-Bell 1969
;
Ferrarese and Merritt 2000
.
However, the origin of SMBHs is not clear.
They may be formed from the stellar black holes (BHs) with the mass
\sim\mathcal{O}(10)M_{\odot}
through accretion and mergers
Page and Thorne 1974
;
Buonanno et al. 2008
.
However, it is difficult to account for the SMBHs at the high redshift
z\sim 7

Mortlock et al. 2011
;
De Rosa et al. 2014
;
Banados et al. 2018
since the stellar BHs have no time to grow into SMBHs.
The another scenario is considering the primordial black holes (PBHs) with the mass
\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{4}-10^{5})M_{\odot}
as the seeds of SMBHs
Salpeter 1964
;
Duechting 2004
.
In this case, the PBHs could subsequently grow up to
\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{9})M_{\odot}
due to an efficient accretion of matter on the massive seeds and mergings
Choquette et al. 2019
;
Dolgov and Porey 2019
.
(See
\rm e.g.
Refs.
Volonteri 2010
;
Dolgov 2018
for reviews of the SMBHs formation.)

The PBHs have recently gathered significant interest as the candidates of dark matter (DM)
Bird et al. 2016
;
Green and Kavanagh 2021
;
Carr and Kuhnel 2020
;
Carr and Kuhnel 2022
;
Li 2022
;
Jho et al. 2022
;
Agashe et al. 2022
;
Xie 2023
;
Gelmini et al. 2023
.
They can be formed due to the collapse of large density fluctuations produced during inflation
Carr 1975
;
Ivanov et al. 1994
;
Garcia-Bellido et al. 1996
;
Alabidi and Kohri 2009
, the cosmological phase transitions
Kodama et al. 1982
;
Hawking et al. 1982
;
Baker et al. 2021
, and the collapse of topological defects
Hawking 1989
;
Polnarev and Zembowicz 1991
;
MacGibbon et al. 1998
;
Rubin et al. 2001
;
Deng et al. 2017
;
Ferrer et al. 2019
and false vacuum bubbles
Deng and Vilenkin 2017
;
Deng 2020
;
Maeso et al. 2022
, etc.
The another scenario is considering the PBHs formation from the QCD axion bubbles in the early Universe, leading to the minimum PBH mass
\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{4})M_{\odot}
, which was first proposed in Ref.
Kitajima and Takahashi 2020
and further studied by Ref.
Li 2023
.
This scenario is similar to the PBHs formation from the baryon bubbles in the Affleck-Dine (AD) baryogenesis
Dolgov et al. 2009
;
Hasegawa and Kawasaki 2019
;
Kawasaki and Murai 2019
.
The QCD axion
Peccei and Quinn 1977a
;
Peccei and Quinn 1977b
;
Weinberg 1978
;
Wilczek 1978
is a light pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson with a spontaneously broken
U(1)
Peccei-Quinn (PQ) symmetry.
It is also the potential cold DM candidate, which can be non-thermally generated through misalignment mechanism in the early Universe
Preskill et al. 1983
;
Abbott and Sikivie 1983
;
Dine and Fischler 1983
.

The QCD axion bubbles can be generated due to an additional PQ symmetry breaking after inflation with the multiple vacua, and are formed when the QCD axion starts to oscillate during the QCD phase transition (PT)
Kitajima and Takahashi 2020
;
Li 2023
.
The axion acquires a light mass after inflation, then starts to oscillate when this mass is comparable to the Hubble parameter and settles down into different potential minima depending on its initial position.
Since the additional PQ symmetry is supposed to be temporarily broken, the axion potential will disappear before the QCD PT.
Therefore, the misalignment mechanism is still valid to calculate the final QCD axion abundance.
However, the initial misalignment angle will be split into different values by the multiple vacua.
During the QCD PT, the QCD axions start to oscillate with these different initial angles, in which the high density axion bubbles can be produced with the large initial values.
An interesting phenomenon of the QCD axion bubbles is the PBHs formation, which can be formed when the axions dominate the radiation in the bubbles.

In this paper, we focus our attention on the PBHs triggered by QCD axion bubbles as the seeds of SMBHs.
Here we assume that the axion acquires a light mass due to an additional PQ symmetry breaking after inflation with a large axion scale
f_{a}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{16})\,\rm GeV
, and then settles down into two potential minima,
\phi_{\rm min}^{0}
and
\phi_{\rm min}^{1}
.
We consider the general case in which the QCD axion bubbles are formed with the bubble effective angle
\theta_{\rm eff}\in(0,\,\pi]
.
The
\phi_{\rm min}^{0}
accounts for the DM abundance with the initial misalignment angle
\sim 0
, while the
\phi_{\rm min}^{1}
forms the high energy density QCD axion bubbles with the angle
\sim\theta_{\rm eff}
.
The PBHs triggered by QCD axion bubbles are formed after the QCD PT, which leads to the minimum PBH mass
\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{4}-10^{7})M_{\odot}
for
f_{a}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{16})\,\rm GeV
.
Compared with the critical value of the
\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{9})M_{\odot}
SMBHs seeds, the PBHs at this mass region may be the seeds of SMBHs.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we introduce the general QCD axion bubbles with the bubble effective angle
\theta_{\rm eff}\in(0,\,\pi]
.
In Sec.
III
, we investigate the PBHs as the seeds of SMBHs, which are generated from the QCD axion bubbles.
Finally, the conclusion is given in Sec.
IV
.

II
General QCD axion bubbles

In this section, we introduce the general QCD axion bubbles.
We first briefly review the misalignment mechanism.
The QCD axion effective potential is given by

\displaystyle V_{\rm QCD}(\phi)=m_{a}^{2}(T)f_{a}^{2}\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{f_{a}}\right)\right]\,,

(1)

with the axion field
\phi
, the axion decay constant
f_{a}
, the axion angle
\theta=\phi/f_{a}
, and the temperature-dependent axion mass
Borsanyi et al. 2016

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} m_{a}(T)\simeq\begin{cases}m_{a,0}\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm QCD}}\right)^{-4.08}\,,&T\geq T_{\rm QCD}\\
m_{a,0}\,,&T<T_{\rm QCD}\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(2)

where
m_{a,0}\simeq 5.70(7)\,{\mu\rm eV}(10^{12}\,{\rm GeV}/f_{a})
is the zero-temperature axion mass
Grilli di Cortona et al. 2016
,
T
is the cosmic temperature, and
T_{\rm QCD}\simeq 150\,\rm MeV
is the QCD scale.

During the QCD PT, the QCD axion starts to oscillate when its mass
m_{a}(T)
is comparable to the Hubble parameter
H(T)
with the oscillation temperature
T_{a}
.
The axion energy density at present can be described by

\displaystyle\rho_{a}(T_{0})=\frac{m_{a,0}m_{a}(T_{a})s(T_{0})}{2s(T_{a})}f_{a}^{2}\left\langle\theta_{i}^{2}f(\theta_{i})\right\rangle\chi\,,

(3)

with the entropy density
s(T)
, the present CMB temperature
T_{0}
, the initial misalignment angle
\theta_{i}
, the numerical factor
\chi\simeq 1.44

Turner 1986
, and the anharmonic factor
f(\theta_{i})
is given by
Lyth 1992
;
Visinelli and Gondolo 2009

\displaystyle f(\theta_{i})\simeq\left[\ln\left(\frac{e}{1-\theta_{i}^{2}/\pi^{2}}\right)\right]^{1.16}\,.

(4)

Then we derive the present QCD axion abundance

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Omega_{a}h^{2}&\simeq 0.14\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{a})}{61.75}\right)^{-0.42}\left(\frac{g_{*s}(T_{0})}{3.94}\right)\\
&\times\left(\frac{f_{a}}{10^{12}\rm\,GeV}\right)^{1.16}\left\langle\theta_{i}^{2}f(\theta_{i})\right\rangle\,,\end{aligned}

(5)

where
g_{*}(T)
and
g_{*s}(T)
are the numbers of effective degrees of freedom of the energy density and the entropy density, respectively, and
h\simeq 0.68
is the reduced Hubble constant.
In order to account for the observed cold DM abundance (
\Omega_{\rm DM}h^{2}\simeq 0.12

Aghanim et al. 2020
), the initial misalignment angle should be
\theta_{i}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
for the scale
f_{a}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{12})\,\rm GeV
.

The QCD axion bubbles can be generated due to an additional PQ symmetry breaking after inflation.
There are many mechanisms for this additional PQ symmetry breaking in the early Universe
Nomura et al. 2016
;
Kawasaki et al. 2016
;
Kawasaki et al. 2018
;
Chiba et al. 2004
;
Takahashi and Yamaguchi 2004
;
Higaki et al. 2014
;
Choi et al. 1997
;
Banks and Dine 1997
;
Jeong and Takahashi 2013
;
Takahashi and Yamada 2015
.
Then the axion field
\phi
acquires the effective potential, which can be approximated as

\displaystyle V_{\cancel{\rm PQ}}(\phi)\simeq\frac{1}{2}m_{a}^{2}(\phi)\left(\phi-\phi_{\rm min}^{n}\right)^{2}\,,

(6)

where
m_{a}(\phi)
is the axion effective mass,
\phi_{\rm min}^{n}
is the potential minimum, and
n
is an integer.
Note that the additional PQ symmetry is assumed to be temporarily broken with the large scale
f_{a}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{16})\,\rm GeV
, which gives the axion a light effective mass and forms the multiple vacua.
Then the axion starts to oscillate and settles down into the minimum
\phi_{\rm min}^{n}
of
V_{\cancel{\rm PQ}}(\phi)
.

The QCD axion bubbles are generated when the conventional axion potential
V_{\rm QCD}(\phi)
arises during the QCD PT.
Therefore, one of the minimum
\phi_{\rm min}^{0}
of
V_{\cancel{\rm PQ}}(\phi)
should be near the minimum of
V_{\rm QCD}(\phi)
, ensuring the correct cold DM abundance with the effective initial angle
\theta_{i,0}
.
In addition, if the initial value is greater than a critical value
\phi_{\rm crit}
, the axion will be stabilized at the another minimum
\phi_{\rm min}^{1}
with
\theta_{i,1}
.
Here we suppose that the QCD axion bubbles are produced at
\phi_{\rm min}^{1}
,
\rm i.e.
,
\theta_{i,1}>\theta_{i,0}
, we define the axion bubble effective angle

\displaystyle\boxed{\theta_{\rm eff}\in(0,\,\pi]\,.}

(7)

This can be realized with the bubble effective angle,
\rm e.g.
,
\theta_{\rm eff}=\pi

Kitajima and Takahashi 2020
and
2\pi/3

Li 2023
.
In Ref.
Kitajima and Takahashi 2020
, they showed the Witten effect as an example of the additional PQ symmetry breaking to produce the QCD axion bubbles with
\theta_{\rm eff}=\pi
.
In Ref.
Li 2023
, they discussed the axion bubbles formation from the hidden
SU(N)
gauge symmetry breaking with
\theta_{\rm eff}=2\pi/3
.
It should be emphasized that the lower limit of Eq. (
7
) is just a hypothetical scope of this angle, not means all values in this region are reasonable, which is related to the specific mechanism of the additional PQ symmetry breaking.
We do know the bubble effective angle cannot be small as
\sim 0
, in this case, we have
\theta_{i,1}\simeq\theta_{i,0}
,
\rm i.e.
, no QCD axion bubbles are produced.
However, it is difficult to define a clear lower limit of
\theta_{\rm eff}
in this work.
For discussion purposes, we take a typical lower limit
\theta_{\rm eff}=\pi/3
in what follows.
Then the effective initial misalignment angle are given by

\displaystyle\theta_{i,n}=\begin{cases}0-\theta_{i}\,,&n=0\\
\theta_{\rm eff}-\theta_{i}\,,&n=1\end{cases}

(8)

corresponding to the potential minima
\phi_{\rm min}^{0}
and
\phi_{\rm min}^{1}
, respectively.
Note that
\theta_{i}
in Eq. (
8
) is a small initial misalignment angle

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \theta_{i}&\simeq 4.29\times 10^{-3}\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{a})}{61.75}\right)^{0.21}\left(\frac{g_{*s}(T_{0})}{3.94}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\times\left(\frac{f_{a}}{10^{16}\,\rm GeV}\right)^{-0.58}\,,\end{aligned}

(9)

for
f_{a}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{16})\,\rm GeV
, which is derived from Eq. (
5
).

The QCD axion bubbles abundance is related to the inflationary fluctuations.
The volume fraction of the bubbles can be described by
Kitajima and Takahashi 2020

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\beta}{{\rm d}\ln k}\simeq\frac{\phi_{\rm crit}-\phi_{i}}{2}P(\ln(k/k_{*}),\phi_{\rm crit})\,,

(12)

where
\phi_{\rm crit}
is the critical value,
k
is the wave number,
k_{*}=0.002\,\rm Mpc^{-1}
, and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P(\ln(k/k_{*}),\phi)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma(\ln(k/k_{*}))}\\

(13)

where

\phi_{\rm crit}-\phi_{i}=4.5H_{\rm inf}
as a benchmark.

III
SMBHs from the general QCD axion bubbles

The astronomical observations indicate that SMBHs are ubiquitous in the center of galaxies with the mass
\sim 10^{9}M_{\odot}
at
z\sim 7
(
\sim 0.76\,\rm Gyr
)
Mortlock et al. 2011
;
De Rosa et al. 2014
;
Banados et al. 2018
.
However, the origin of such BHs is still a mystery.
The mass of an accreting BH with the time
t
is given by
Salpeter 1964

\displaystyle M(t)=M_{0}\exp{\left(\frac{1-\epsilon_{r}}{\epsilon_{r}}\frac{t}{t_{E}}\right)}\,,

(14)

where
M_{0}
is the seed BH mass,
\epsilon_{r}\simeq 0.1
is the radiative efficiency, and
t_{E}\simeq 0.45\,\rm Gyr
.
In this case, a seed BH with the mass
M_{0}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{2}-10^{5})M_{\odot}
would take at least
\sim 0.5\,\rm Gyr
to grow up to a
\sim 10^{9}M_{\odot}
SMBH
Choquette et al. 2019
.
Therefore, the SMBHs must either have the heavy seeds or have a primordial origin.

Finally, we comment on the SMBHs formation from the baryon bubbles in the AD baryogenesis
Kawasaki and Murai 2019
.
The SMBHs formation in the QCD axion bubbles scenario has some similarities with their mechanism.
They considered the high density baryon bubbles formation from the modified AD mechanism with the Hubble induced mass and the finite temperature effect.
The PBHs will be formed when the baryon bubbles enter the horizontal plane with the sufficiently large densities.
They also showed the PBHs produced by that mechanism may have a reasonable mass as the seeds of SMBHs.

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the SMBHs formation from the general QCD axion bubbles.
The main idea is considering the PBHs triggered by QCD axion bubbles as the seeds of SMBHs.
We first introduce the general QCD axion bubbles, which can be generated due to an additional PQ symmetry breaking after inflation and are formed when the QCD axion starts to oscillate during the QCD PT.
In this work, we do not discuss the specific mechanism of the additional PQ symmetry breaking.
We consider the general case in which the QCD axion bubbles are formed with the bubble effective angle
\theta_{\rm eff}\in(0,\,\pi]
.
The PBHs triggered by axion bubbles will be formed when the axions dominate the radiation in the bubbles, which leads to a minimum PBH mass
M_{\rm PBH}^{\rm min}
.
We show the distributions of the temperature
T_{B}
with different
\theta_{\rm eff}
for the scales
f_{a}=1\times 10^{16}\,\rm GeV
and
1\times 10^{17}\,\rm GeV
.
Then we find the minimum PBH mass
M_{\rm PBH}^{\rm min}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{4}-10^{7})M_{\odot}
with the angle
\theta_{\rm eff}\sim\pi
to
\pi/3
for
f_{a}=1\times 10^{16}\,\rm GeV
.
The PBH fractional abundance distributions on the “
\log(M_{\rm PBH}/M_{\odot})-\theta_{\rm eff}
” plane are also shown, which are strongly limited by
M_{\rm PBH}^{\rm min}
.

In order to explain the SMBHs in the galactic centers with the mass
\sim 10^{9}M_{\odot}

Abstract
. A set A A of nonnegative integers is an asymptotic basis of order h h if every sufficiently large integer can be represented as the sum of h h not necessarily distinct elements of A A . An asymptotic basis A A is minimal if removing any element of A A destroys every representation of infinitely many integers. In this paper, a new class of minimal asymptotic bases is constructed.

Let
\mathbf{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}

h

Let
A,A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}

A_{1}+\cdots+A_{h}=\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}:a_{i}\in A_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,2,\ldots,h\}

The set
A

An

For

A
\mathcal{G}
-adic sequence
is a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers

\mathcal{G}=(g_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}

such that
g_{0}=1
and
g_{i-1}
divides
g_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
.
Let
(d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be the sequence of positive integers defined by

d_{i}=\frac{g_{i}}{g_{i-1}}.

For all
i\geq 1
, we have

(1)

d_{i}\geq 2

and

(2)

g_{i}=d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{i}.

For all
i
and
j
with
0\leq i<j
, we have

(3)

\frac{g_{i+j}}{g_{i}}=d_{i+1}d_{i+2}\cdots d_{i+j}.

Every positive integer
n
has a unique
\mathcal{G}
-adic representation

n=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x_{j}g_{j}

where

x_{j}\in[0,d_{j+1}-1]

for all
j\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
and
x_{j}=0
for all sufficiently large
j

(Nathanson
[
13
,
14
]
).
Equivalently, for every positive integer
n
, there is a unique nonempty finite set
F\subseteq\mathbf{N}_{0}

and a unique set
\{x_{j}:j\in F\}
such that

(4)

n=\sum_{j\in F}x_{j}g_{j}

where
x_{j}\in[1,d_{j+1}-1]
for all
j\in F
.

For every integer
g\geq 2
, the usual
g
-adic representation uses the

\mathcal{G}
-adic sequence
\mathcal{G}=\left(g^{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
with
quotients
d_{i}=g
for all
i\geq 1
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(g_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-adic system.
If
n=\sum_{j\in F}x_{j}g_{j}
is a positive integer
with
x_{j}\in[1,d_{j+1}-1]
for all
j\in F
,
then

g_{M}\leq n<g_{M+1}

if and only if

\max(F)=M.

Proof.

If
\max(F)=M
, then
F\subseteq[0,M]
and

\displaystyle g_{M}

\displaystyle\leq x_{M}g_{M}\leq n\leq\sum_{j\in F}(d_{j+1}-1)g_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{M}(d_{j+1}-1)g_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{M}(g_{j+1}-g_{j})

\displaystyle=g_{M+1}-1<g_{M+1}.

Conversely, if
g_{M}\leq n<g_{M+1}
and
\max(F)=M^{\prime}
,
then the inequality

g_{M^{\prime}}\leq x_{M^{\prime}}g_{M^{\prime}}\leq n<g_{M+1}

implies
M^{\prime}\leq M
. If
M^{\prime}\leq M-1
, then

n\leq\sum_{j=0}^{M^{\prime}}(d_{j+1}-1)g_{j}<g_{M^{\prime}+1}\leq g_{M}

which is absurd. Therefore,
g_{M}\leq n<g_{M+1}
implies

\max(F)=M
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Let

.

Theorem 1
.

Let
h\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{W}=(W_{i})_{i=0}^{h-1}
be a partition of
\mathbf{N}_{0}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}=(g_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-adic sequence.
The set

A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})=\bigcup_{i=0}^{h-1}A_{\mathcal{G}}(W_{i})

is an asymptotic basis of order
h
with
h
-fold sumset

hA_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})=\{n\in\mathbf{N}_{0}:n\geq h\}.

Proof.

The smallest integer in the set
A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})
is
1=1\cdot g_{0}
.
It follows that
h\in hA_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})
but

[0,h-1]\cap hA_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})=\emptyset
.

Every positive integer
n
has a unique
\mathcal{G}
-adic representation
n=\sum_{j\in F}x_{j}g_{j}
,
where
F
is a nonempty finite set of nonnegative integers and
x_{j}\in[0,d_{j+1}-1]
.
For
i\in[0,h-1]
, let

F_{i}=F\cap W_{i}

and

n_{i}=\sum_{j\in F_{i}}x_{j}g_{j}.

If
F_{i}=\emptyset
, then
n_{i}=0\notin A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})
. If
F_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then

n_{i}=\sum_{j\in F_{i}}x_{j}g_{j}\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(W_{i})\subseteq A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W}).

Let

L=\left\{i\in[0,h-1]:F_{i}\neq\emptyset\right\}=\left\{i\in[0,h-1]:n_{i}\geq 1\right\}

and

|L|=\ell_{0}.

We have
\ell_{0}\in[1,h]
and

n=\sum_{i\in L}n_{i}\in\ell_{0}A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W}).

Let
\ell
be the largest integer such that
\ell\leq h
and

n\in\ell A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})
. We must prove that
\ell=h
.

If
n\in\ell A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})
, then there exist integers

n_{1},\ldots,n_{\ell}\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})

such that

n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k-1}+n_{k}+n_{k+1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}.

For each
i\in[1,\ell]
there is an integer
s_{i}\in[0,h-1]
and a set
F_{s_{i}}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}(W_{s_{i}})

such that
n_{i}
has the
\mathcal{G}
-adic representation

n_{i}=\sum_{j\in F_{s_{i}}}x_{j}g_{j}\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(W_{s_{i}}).

Suppose that
\ell<h
. If
|F_{s_{k}}|\geq 2
for some
k\in[1,\ell]
,
then there are nonempty sets
F^{\prime}_{s_{i}}
and
F^{\prime\prime}_{s_{i}}
such that

F_{s_{k}}=F^{\prime}_{s_{k}}\cup F^{\prime\prime}_{s_{k}}\qqand F^{\prime}_{s_{k}}\cap F^{\prime\prime}_{s_{k}}=\emptyset.

The integers

n^{\prime}_{k}=\sum_{j\in F^{\prime}_{s_{k}}}x_{j}g_{j}\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(W_{s_{k}})\qqand n^{\prime\prime}_{k}=\sum_{j\in F^{\prime\prime}_{s_{k}}}x_{j}g_{j}\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(W_{s_{k}})

satisfy

n_{k}=n^{\prime}_{k}+n^{\prime\prime}_{k}

and so

n=n_{1}+\cdots n_{k-1}+n^{\prime}_{k}+n^{\prime\prime}_{k}+n_{k+1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}\in(\ell+1)A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W}).

This contradicts the maximality of
\ell
, and so
|F_{s_{i}}|=1
for all
i\in[1,\ell]
and

n_{i}=x_{j_{i}}g_{j_{i}}

for some
g_{j_{i}}\in W_{s_{i}}
and
x_{j_{i}}\in[0,d_{j_{i}+1}-1]
.

If
x_{j_{k}}\geq 2
for some
k\in[1,\ell]
, then

g_{j_{k}}\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(W_{s_{k}})\qqand(x_{j_{k}}-1)g_{j_{k}}\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(W_{s_{k}})

and

n_{k}=g_{j_{k}}+(x_{j_{k}}-1)g_{j_{k}}.

It follows that

n=n_{1}+\cdots n_{k-1}+g_{j_{k}}+(x_{j_{k}}-1)g_{j_{k}}+n_{k+1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}\in(\ell+1)A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W}),

which again contradicts the maximality of
\ell
. Therefore,
x_{j_{i}}=1
for all
i\in[1,\ell]
, and

n=g_{j_{1}}+\cdots+g_{j_{k}}+\cdots+g_{j_{\ell}}.

If
j_{k}\geq 1
for some
k\in[1,\ell]
, then

g_{j_{k}}=g_{j_{k}-1}+(d_{j_{k}}-1)g_{j_{k}-1}

and

n=g_{j_{1}}+\cdots+g_{j_{k}-1}+(d_{j_{k}}-1)g_{j_{k}-1}+\cdots+g_{j_{\ell}}\in(\ell+1)A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W}),

which also contradicts the maximality of
\ell
.
Therefore,
j_{k}=0
and
g_{j_{k}}=g_{0}=1
for all
k\in[1,\ell]
, and so

n=\underbrace{g_{0}+\cdots+g_{0}}_{\text{$\ell$ summands}}=\underbrace{1+\cdots+1+\cdots+1}_{\text{$\ell$ summands}}=\ell\leq h-1,

which is absurd.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
h\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{W}=(W_{i})_{i=0}^{h-1}
be a partition of
\mathbf{N}_{0}
.
The set

A=\{0\}\cup A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})

is a basis of order
h
but not a minimal asymptotic basis of order
h
.

Proof.

Because
0\in A
and
1\in A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})\subseteq A
, we have

\ell=(h-\ell)\cdot 0+\ell\cdot 1\in hA

for all
\ell\in[0,h-1]
.
By Theorem
1
,

\{n\in\mathbf{N}_{0}:n\geq h\}=hA_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})\subseteq hA

and so
A
is a basis of order
h
.

The set
A
is not a minimal asymptotic basis of order
h

because
0\in A
and the removal of 0 from
A
gives the set
A\setminus\{0\}=A_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{W})
,
which is still an asymptotic basis of order
h
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma generalizes a result of Jia

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(g_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-adic sequence.
Let
(u_{i})_{i=1}^{p}
be a strictly increasing finite sequence of nonnegative integers,
and let
(v_{j})_{j=1}^{q}

Let

for all

y_{j}\in[1,d_{v_{j}+1}-1]

If

(5)

n=\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{q}y_{j}g_{v_{j}}

then

(6)

\sum_{u_{i}\leq u_{k}}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}\leq\sum_{v_{j}\leq u_{k}}y_{j}g_{v_{j}}

for all
k\in[1,p]
.

Proof.

Because the sequence
(u_{i})_{i=1}^{p}
is strictly increasing,
Lemma
1
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{u_{i}\leq u_{k}}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}<g_{u_{k+1}}

for all
k\in[1,p]
.
Choosing
k=p
gives

n=\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}<g_{u_{p+1}}.

Relation (
5
) implies

g_{v_{j}}\leq n<g_{u_{p}+1}

an so
v_{j}\leq u_{p}
for all
j\in[1,q]
.
This implies (
6
) for
k=p
.

In the sequence
\mathcal{G}=(g_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
, the integer
g_{i}
divides
g_{j}

for all
i\leq j
.
Let
k\in[1,p-1]
.
Because the sequence
(u_{i})_{i=1}^{p}
is strictly increasing, for all
i\in[k+1,p]
we have

u_{k}<u_{k}+1\leq u_{k+1}\leq u_{i}

and
g_{u_{k}+1}

, that is,

g_{u_{i}}\equiv 0\pmod{g_{u_{k}+1}}.

If
v_{j}\geq u_{k}+1
, then

g_{v_{j}}\equiv 0\pmod{g_{u_{k}+1}}.

Rearranging (
5
), we obtain

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{u_{i}\leq u_{k}}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}-\sum_{v_{j}\leq u_{k}}y_{j}g_{v_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{j}\geq u_{k}+1}y_{j}g_{v_{j}}-\sum_{u_{i}>u_{k}}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{g_{u_{k}+1}}.

If

\sum_{u_{i}\leq u_{k}}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}>\sum_{v_{j}\leq u_{k}}y_{j}g_{v_{j}}

then Lemma
1
gives

0<\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}-\sum_{v_{j}\leq u_{k}}y_{j}g_{v_{j}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}g_{u_{i}}<g_{u_{k+1}}.

This inequality contradicts congruence (
7
).
This completes the proof.

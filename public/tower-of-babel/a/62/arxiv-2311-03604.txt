Directional derivative of the value function for parametric set-constrained optimization problems
Kuang Bai, Jane J. Ye

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the directional derivative of the value function for a very general set-constrained optimization problem under perturbation. Under reasonable assumptions, we obtain upper and lower estimates for the upper and lower Dini directional derivative of the value function respectively, from which we obtain Hadamard directional differentiability of the value function when the set of multipliers is a singleton. Our results do not require convexity of the set involved. Even in the case of a parametric nonlinear program, our results improve the classical ones in that our regularity conditions are weaker and the directional solution set is used which is in general smaller than its nondirectional counterparts. Key words. Sensitivity analysis, directional derivative, value function, nonconvex parametric set-constrained program 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 49J52,49J53,49K40,90C26,90C31.



1
Introduction

In this paper we consider the parametric set-constrained optimization problem in the form:

(P_{x})~~~~\quad\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x,y)\quad s.t.\ P(x,y)\in C,

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the parameter or perturbation,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ P:\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
are continuously differentiable, and
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a closed set.
We define the feasible solution map as

{\cal F}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}|P(x,y)\in C\},

the (optimal) value function/marginal function as

V(x):=\inf_{y}\{f(x,y)|y\in{\cal F}(x)\}

and the optimal solution set as

S(x):=\arg\min_{y}\{f(x,y)|y\in{\cal F}(x)\}.

Recently we have studied the directional subdifferential of the value function in
[
3
]
. The main goal of this paper is to study the directional derivative of the value function.
In the case of fixed feasible set, i.e.,
P(x,y)=y
, as it is commented by Bonnans and Shapiro in
[
5
, page 272]
, the directionally differentiable behavior is typical for the value function. Indeed, by Danskin’s theorem
[
7
]
, if
P(x,y)=y
and
C
is compact then
V(x)
is directionally differentiable in any direction and the directional derivative of
V(x)
at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
can be calculated by

V^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)=\min_{y\in S(\bar{x})}\nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},y)^{T}u.

Moreover by
[
5
, Theorem 4.13]
, the compactness of set
C
can be replaced by a weaker condition such as the inf-compactness condition.
As commented by Bonnans and Shapiro in
[
5
, page 278]
,
“It is considerably more difficult to investigate differentiability properties of the optimal value function in cases where the corresponding feasible set is also subject to perturbations”.
In the case where
C=\{0\}_{p_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p_{2}}
with
p_{1}+p_{2}=p
, the parametric optimization problem is a parametric nonlinear program. Under the uniform compactness of the solution map
S(x)
near
\bar{x}
and the Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ) at each
y\in S(\bar{x})
, Gauvin and Dubeau
[
8
, Corollary 4.3]
obtained bounds of the upper/lower Dini directional derivative of the value function:

\inf_{y\in S(\bar{x})}\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar{x},y)}\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},y,\lambda)^{T}u\leq V_{-}^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)\leq V_{+}^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)\leq\min_{y\in S(\bar{x})}\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar{x},y)}\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},y,\lambda)^{T}u,

(1)

where
L(\bar{x},y,\lambda):=f(\bar{x},y)+P(\bar{x},y)^{T}\lambda
is the Lagrange function and
\Lambda(\bar{x},y)
is the set of Lagrange multipliers for problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
.
Moreover if the multiplier set
\Lambda(\bar{x},y)=\{\lambda(y)\}
is a singleton, then the upper bound is equal to the lower bound and the value function is then directionally differentiable and

V^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)=\inf_{y\in S(\bar{x})}\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},y,\lambda(y))^{T}u.

These results are extended to cover the case where
C
is an arbitrary closed convex set by Bonnans and Shapiro
[
5
, Theorem 4.26]
.

In general there is a gap between the upper and lower bounds in (
1
).
Under some additional assumptions the gap can be closed and one can conclude the directional differentiability and the formula

V^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)=\min_{y\in S(\bar{x})}\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar{x},y)}\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},y,\lambda)^{T}u.

For parametric nonlinear programs,
Janin
[
17
, Corollary 3.4]
obtained such a result under both the constant rank constraint qualification
and MFCQ. For the parametric program in the form of
(P_{x})
with a closed convex set
C
, these types of results are given by Bonnans and Shapiro in
[
5
, Theorems 4.24 and 4.25]
.

In this paper, we aim at studying the directional derivative of the value function of the very general parametric program
(P_{x})
. First we extend the result of Bonnans and Shapiro
[
5
, Theorem 4.26]
to allow
C
to be nonconvex. Moreover we obtain
the following estimates

\inf_{y\in S(\bar{x};u)}\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x},y)}\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},y,\lambda)u\leq V_{-}^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)\leq V_{+}^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)\leq\inf_{y\in S(\bar{x};u)}\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x},y)}\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},y,\lambda)u,

where
\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x},y)
denotes the set of Clarke multipliers for problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
and

S(\bar{x};u)
is a subset of the solution set
S(\bar{x})
associated with the direction
u
. Furthermore if the set of Clarke multipliers
\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x},y)=\{\lambda(y)\}
is a singleton for all
y\in S(\bar{x};u)
, then the value function is directionally differentiable and

V^{\prime}(\bar{x};u)=\inf_{y\in S(\bar{x};u)}\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},y,\lambda(y))u.

Our result has even improved the classical one

[
5
, Theorem 4.26]
in the case when the set
C
is convex since the regularity conditions we require are weaker than Robinson’s constraint qualification and the set of directional solutions
S(\bar{x};u)
may be strictly included in the solution set
S(\bar{x})
. In particular our result for the parametric nonlinear program has improved the classical result of Gauvin and Dubeau
[
8
, Corollary 4.3]
in that our regularity condition is weaker than MFCQ.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some preliminaries and preliminary results used in the paper. In Section 3, we propose first-order sufficient conditions for directional Robinson stability. In Section 4, we develop upper-/lower-bound for Dini directional derivatives of the value function for parametric set-constrained problems. Finally, an example is used to illustrate the sharpness of our estimates.

Definition 2.1
(Tangent Cone and Normal Cone)

Given a set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
,
the tangent/contingent cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is defined as

T_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\Omega-\bar{x}}{t}=\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,d_{k}\rightarrow d\ \mbox{ s.t. }\bar{x}+t_{k}d_{k}\in\Omega\ \forall k\right\}.

The regular/Clarke tangent cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is defined as

\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\liminf_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x},t\downarrow 0}\frac{\Omega-x}{t}=\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\forall t\downarrow 0,\forall x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x},\exists y\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}\ s.t.\ \frac{y-x}{t}\rightarrow d\right\}.

The regular normal cone, the limiting normal cone and the Clarke normal cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
can be defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigg|\langle\zeta,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq o(\|x-\bar{x}\|)\quad\forall x\in\Omega\right\},

\displaystyle N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x)=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigg|\exists\ x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x},\ \zeta_{k}{\rightarrow}\zeta\ \text{such that}\ \zeta_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x_{k})\ \forall k\right\},

\displaystyle N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\rm clco}N_{\Omega}(\bar{x}),

respectively.

According to
[
5
, Formula (2.88)]
, the contingent cone can be equivalently written in the following form

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,\mbox{\rm dist}(\bar{x}+t_{k}d,\Omega)=o(t_{k})\right\}.

(2)

By
[
23
, Definition 6.25 and Theorem 6.26]
,
the regular tangent cone can be defined as the following equivalent form:

\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x},x\in\Omega}T_{\Omega}(x).

For a point
\bar{x}\not\in\Omega
, it is conventional to define the corresponding tangent and normal cones to be the emptyset. From definition, we have the following relationships between various tangent cones and normal cones.

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\subseteq{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\subseteq{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\subseteq{N}^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}).

It is well-known that the Clarke normal cone and the regular tangent cone are polar to each other.

Proposition 2.1
(Tangent-Normal Polarity)

[
23
, Theorem 6.28]
For a closed set
\Omega
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, one has

\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})^{\circ},\quad\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})^{\circ}=N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}).

In particular, the set
\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
is closed and convex.

The following directional version of limiting normal cone introduced in
[
14
, Definition 2.3]
is comprehensively studied by Gfrerer (see e.g.,
[

and
[
4
]

Definition 2.2
(Directional Normal Cone)

Given a set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
and a direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the limiting normal cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
is defined by

N_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d):=\limsup_{x\xrightarrow{d}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x)=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigg|\exists\ t_{k}\downarrow 0,d_{k}\rightarrow d,\zeta_{k}\rightarrow\zeta\mbox{ s.t. }\zeta_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}+t_{k}d_{k})\ \forall k\right\},

and the Clarke normal cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
is defined by

N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d):={\rm clco}N_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d).

It is obvious that
N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x};0)=N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
,
N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d)=\emptyset
if
d\not\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
and
N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d)\subseteq N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
. It is also obvious that for all
d\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\setminus T_{bd\Omega}(\bar{x})
, one has
N^{c}_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d)=\{0\}
.
By
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
, the directional and the classical normal cone have the following relationship

N_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d)\subseteq N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\{d\}^{\perp}\qquad\forall d\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x}),

if
\Omega
is the union of finitely many convex polyhedral sets,
and the equality holds if
\Omega
is convex.
Moreover it is easy to see that
N_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d)
is homogeneous with degree zero in
d
, i.e.,
N_{\Omega}(\bar{x};\alpha d)=N_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d)
for any
\alpha>0
.
The following fact proved in
[
13
, Proposition 2]
is the directional counterpart of outer semicontinuous property of the limiting normal cone mapping (
[
23
, Proposition 6.6]
).

Proposition 2.2

Given a set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
and a direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has

N_{\Omega}(\bar{x};d)=\limsup_{x\xrightarrow{d}\bar{x}}{N}_{\Omega}(x)=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigg|\exists\ t_{k}\downarrow 0,d_{k}\rightarrow d,\zeta_{k}\rightarrow\zeta\mbox{ s.t. }\zeta_{k}\in{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}+t_{k}d_{k})\ \forall k\right\}.

Similarly, many classical concepts in variational analysis can have their directional versions. To do this, the following concept of a directional neighborhood is needed.

Definition 2.3
(Directional Neighborhood)

(
[
9
, formula (7)]
). Given a direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and positive scalars
\varepsilon,\delta
, the directional neighborhood of direction
d
is a set defined by

{\cal V}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(d):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{0\}\cup\left\{z\in\varepsilon\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}\mid|\frac{z}{\|z\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}|\leq\delta\right\}&\mbox{ if }d\not=0,\\
\varepsilon\mathbb{B}&\mbox{ if }d=0.\end{array}\right.

With the directional neighborhood at hand, many classical concepts have been exteneded to their directional versions, and a directional version of the variational analysis has been stimulated. In the sequel, we list some useful results from directional variational analysis.

Definition 2.4
(Directional semicontinuity and continuity)

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]
be finite at
\bar{x}
. We say
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
if

\varphi(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{x\xrightarrow{u}\bar{x}}\varphi(x).

We say
\varphi
is upper semicontinuous (u.s.c.) at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
if

\varphi(\bar{x})\geq\limsup_{x\xrightarrow{u}\bar{x}}\varphi(x).

We say that
\varphi
is continuous at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
if

\varphi(\bar{x})=\lim_{x\xrightarrow{u}\bar{x}}\varphi(x).

In this paper we study the directional derivatives in the following sense.

Definition 2.5
(Directional derivatives)

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
The upper Dini directional derivative of
\varphi
at
x
in direction
u
in Hadamard sense is

\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(x;u):=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0,u^{\prime}\rightarrow u}\frac{\varphi(x+tu^{\prime})-\varphi(x)}{t}.

The lower Dini directional derivative of
\varphi
at
x
in direction
u
in Hadamard sense is

\varphi^{\prime}_{-}(x;u):=\liminf_{t\downarrow 0,u^{\prime}\rightarrow u}\frac{\varphi(x+tu^{\prime})-\varphi(x)}{t}.

If both limits are finite and equal, we have then the directional derivative of
\varphi
at
x
in direction
u
in Hadamard sense

\varphi^{\prime}(x;u):=\lim_{t\downarrow 0,u^{\prime}\rightarrow u}\frac{\varphi(x+tu^{\prime})-\varphi(x)}{t}.

We say that
\varphi
is Hadamard directionally differentiable at
x
if its directional derivative in Hadamard sense exists at any direction.

It is clear that if a function is Hadamard directionally differentiable, then it is directionally differentiable in the usual sense as well, i.e.,

\varphi^{\prime}(x;u)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\varphi(x+tu)-\varphi(x)}{t}.

So the concept of Hadamard directional differentiability is stronger than the usual directional differentiability.

Robinson’s constraint qualification (Robinson’s CQ)
(see e.g.,
[
5
]
)
is an important constraint qualification and was originally defined for the set-constrained problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
where
C
is a convex set and the constraint region is convex. For a general set-constrained problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
, we say that Robinson’s CQ holds at
y\in\mathcal{F}(\bar{x})
if

\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},y)^{T}\lambda=0,\ \lambda\in N^{c}_{C}(P(\bar{x},y))\implies\lambda=0.

(3)

Let
\bar{y}\in{\cal F}(\bar{x})
. We denote the set of Clarke multipliers and the limiting multipliers at
\bar{y}
for problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
respectively by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda\in N^{c}_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))|\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}\lambda=0\right\},

\displaystyle\Lambda(\bar{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda\in N_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))|\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}\lambda=0\right\}.

It is known that (
3
) holds if and only if the set of Clarke multipliers
\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x},y)
is nonempty and compact. By the tangent-normal polarity in Proposition
2.1
, it is easy to see that Robinson’s CQ holds at
y
if and only if

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},y)\mathbb{R}^{m}+{\widehat{T}_{C}(P(\bar{x},y))}=\mathbb{R}^{p}.

(4)

Consider a conic linear program in the form of
[
5
, (2.345)]
:

\displaystyle(P)~~~~~~~\min_{x}

\displaystyle\ \ \langle\alpha,x\rangle+c

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle\ \ Ax+b\in K,

(5)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a matrix,
\alpha,b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are vectors,
c
is a scalar and
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a closed convex cone. According to
[
5
, (2.347)]
, its dual problem can be written in the form

\displaystyle(D)~~~~~~~\max_{\lambda\in K^{\circ}}

\displaystyle\ \ \langle\lambda,b\rangle+c

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle\ \ A^{T}\lambda+\alpha=0.

(6)

Define the value function for the perturbed problem of (P) as

v(y):=\inf_{x}\{\langle\alpha,x\rangle+c:Ax+b+y\in K\}.

It is known that the function
v(y)
is convex.
By
[
5
, Definition 2.146]
, we say that problem (P) is calm if
v(0)
is finite and the convex function
v(y)
is subdifferentiable at
y=0
, i.e.,
\partial v(0)\not=\emptyset.

By
[
5
, Proposition 2.147]
, if (P) is calm then there is no duality gap and the set of optimal solutions of the dual problem (D) is nonempty, i.e.,

\inf_{x}\{\langle\alpha,x\rangle+c:Ax+b\in K\}=\max_{\lambda\in K^{\circ}}\{\langle\lambda,b\rangle+c:A^{T}\lambda+\alpha=0\}.

It is known that if
v(0)
is finite and
K
is a convex polyhedral set then (P) is calm (see
[
21
]
and
[
5
, Proposition 2.186]
) and consequently the above strong duality holds automatically. Another sufficient condition for the calmness of (P) is the regularity condition

0\in{\rm int}\{A(\mathbb{R}^{n})+b-K\}.

The above condition is equivalent to that Robinson’s CQ holds at each feasible solution
of problem (P), i.e., for any feasible point
x_{0}
of (P),

0\in{\rm int}\{Ax_{0}+A(\mathbb{R}^{n}-x_{0})+b-K\}

which is equivalent to

A(\mathbb{R}^{n})-T_{K}(Ax_{0}+b)=\mathbb{R}^{m}

or equivalently

A^{T}\lambda=0,\quad\lambda\in N_{K}(Ax_{0}+b)\Longrightarrow\lambda=0.

By
[
5
, Theorem 2.165]
and the comments in
[
5
, Page 113]
, one has the following duality theorem for the conic linear program under Robinson’s CQ.

Theorem 2.1

Suppose Robinson’s CQ holds at each feasible solution of (P). Then there is no duality gap between problems (P) and (D), i.e., val(P)=val(D). Moreover, if the optimal value of (P) is finite, then the optimal solution set of the dual problem (D) is a nonempty, convex and compact set.

3
Directional Robinson stability

In this section, we study a concept of stability subject to a directional perturbation.
Although the result is mainly for the study of directional derivative of the value function in the next section, it is of independent interest.

For fixed
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}{\cal F}:=\{(x,y)\mid y\in{\cal F}(x)\}=\{(x,y)\mid P(x,y)\in C\}
, we say that the set-valued map
y\rightrightarrows P(\bar{x},y)-C
or the system
P(\bar{x},y)\in C
is metrically subregular at
\bar{y}
or the local error bound holds at
\bar{y}
if there exist constants
\kappa_{\bar{x}}\geq 0,\varepsilon\geq 0
such that

{\rm dist}(y,{\cal F}(\bar{x}))\leq\kappa_{\bar{x}}{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x},y),C)\quad\forall y\in\bar{y}+\varepsilon\mathbb{B}.

Note that the modulus of the metric subregularity depends on the parameter
x
. In stability analysis of a system with a perturbation, it is important to study when this modulus is independent of the parameter locally.
As early as in 1976, Robinson
[
20
]
studied this property for the case where
C
is a convex cone under the name of “stability” and proved that Robinson’s CQ for problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
is a sufficient condition for this kind of stability. Recently Gfrerer and Mordukhovich
[
12
]
extended this concept to the general system and call it the Robinson stability.
Usually we are interested in stability with a perturbation from a point
\bar{x}
lying in a neighborhood of
\bar{x}
. However sometimes we only concern about a perturbation from
\bar{x}
which lies in a directional neighborhood of
\bar{x}
.
In particular when we study the directional derivative of the value function we are only interested in a perturbation from
\bar{x}
in the given direction. Based on this consideration,
in
[
2
, Definition 4.7]
, a directional version of the Robinson stability property (see e.g.,
[
12
, Definition 1.1]
) was introduced for the system of inequalities.
We now define it for our more general system in the same way.

Definition 3.1
(Directional Robinson stability)

We say that
{\cal F}
satisfies the Robinson stability
{\rm(RS)}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}{\cal F}
in a direction
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there exist scalars
\kappa\geq 0
and
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that

{\rm dist}(y,{\cal F}(x))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(P(x,y),C)\quad\forall(x,y)\in(\bar{x},\bar{y})+{\mathcal{V}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)\times\varepsilon\mathbb{B}.

When
u=0
in the above definition, we say that
{\cal F}
satisfies RS at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

RS in direction
u
means that the local error bound condition holds at
\bar{y}
uniformly in a neighborhood of
\bar{x}
in direction
u
. Hence RS at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}{\cal F}
in direction
u
implies that that the system
P(\bar{x},y)\in C
is metrically subregular at
\bar{y}
.

Since
P
is smooth, it is known that the mapping
y\rightrightarrows P(\bar{x},y)-C
is metrically regular at
\bar{y}
if and only if the No Nonzero Abnormal Multiplier constraint qualification (NNAMCQ) for problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
holds at
\bar{y}
:

\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}\lambda=0,\ \lambda\in{N}_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))\implies\lambda=0.

(7)

Note that when
C
is closed and convex, the NNAMCQ is equivalent to Robinson’s CQ defined in (
3
) and for the case of nonlinear program, NNAMCQ is equivalent to MFCQ for nonlinear programs.

In the following proposition, Gfrerer and Mordukhovich
[
12
, Corollary 3.7]
extended Robinson’s result that Robinson’s CQ implies RS to the more general system where
C
is a closed set.

Proposition 3.1

[
12
, Corollary 3.7]

Let
\bar{y}\in{\cal F}(\bar{x})
. If the NNAMCQ for problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
holds at
\bar{y}
, then RS holds at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

By the well-known Ronbinson’s polyhedral multifunction theory
[
21
]
, we know that a polyhedral multifunction is upper Lipschitz continuous. It turns out that a parametric polyhedral multifunction is Robinson stable if the parameter
x
is separated from the variable
y
.

Proposition 3.2

Let
P(x,y)=a(x)+By+c
with
a:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
being continuous,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m},c\in\mathbb{R}
,
C
is the union of finitely many convex polyhedral sets and
u
be a direction. If
\mathcal{F}(x)
is nonempty for all
x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)
for some
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, then
{\cal F}
satisfies RS at each
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}{\cal F}
in direction
u
.

Proof.
By
[
21
, Proposition 1]
, for each
x
if
{\cal F}(x)\not=\emptyset
, then the metric subregularity holds, i.e.,

{\rm dist}(y,\mathcal{F}(x))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(P(x,y),C)\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(8)

Furthermore, the modulus
\kappa
depends only on the matrix
B
and the set
C
(see
[
21
, Proposition 1]
and
[
24
, proof of Theorem 1]
for details). Hence if
{\cal F}(x)\not=\emptyset
for any
x
in
{\mathcal{V}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)
, (
8
) holds for all
x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)
, which means that RS holds in direction
u
.

We say that the first order sufficient condition for metric subregularity (FOSCMS) of the system
P(\bar{x},y)\in C
at
\bar{y}
holds in direction
\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v
if

\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}\lambda=0,\ \lambda\in N_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y});\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v)\implies\lambda=0.

By Gfrerer and Klatte
[
11
, Corollary 1]
if FOSCMS of the system
P(\bar{x},y)\in C
at
\bar{y}
holds in each direction
\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
with
v\not=0
, then the set-valued map
y\rightrightarrows P(\bar{x},y)-C
is metrically subregular at
\bar{y}
. In fact, the set of all
v
satisfying

\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))

is the set of linearization cone for the feasible region of problem
(P_{\bar{x}})
at
\bar{y}
. FOSCMS holding in all nonzero directions in the linearization cone is weaker than the NNAMCQ (
7
) and it does not imply RS. It is natural to ask under what extra conditions, FOSCMS implies RS. This question is addressed by Gfrerer and Mordukhovich in

[
12
]
. Motivated by their research, we now extend their results to the directional RS.

The reason that FOSCMS in all nonzero directions in the linearization cone does not imply RS is that the dependence of the parameter
x
is not reflected in those directions. Let
u
be given. In order to modify FOSCMS so that it implies RS, we consider the following direction.
Define the image directional derivative of
P
with respect to
x
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in direction
u
as the closed cone

{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};u):=\left\{\alpha v\left|\alpha\geq 0,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,u^{k}\rightarrow u\ s.t.\ v=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y})-P(\bar{x},\bar{y})}{\|P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y})-P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|}\right.\right\}.

Obviously the convex cone generated by the vector
\nabla_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})u
is a subset of the set
{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
, i.e.,

\{\alpha\nabla_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})u|\alpha\geq 0\}\subseteq{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};u).

The equality holds if
\nabla_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})u\neq 0
.
In general, the strict inclusion may hold. When
u=0
,
{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y}):={\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};0)
coincides with the image derivative introduced in Gfrerer and Mordukhovich
[
12
, formula (3.8)]
with
\zeta(x):=\|x-\bar{x}\|
.

For
(x,y)\in{\rm gph}{\cal F}
and given direction
u
, define the closed cone

\displaystyle{D(x,y;u):=}\left\{d+\nabla_{y}P(x,y)v\left|\begin{array}[]{l}0\not=(d,v)\in({\rm Im}D_{x}P(x,y;u)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}),\\
d+\nabla_{y}P(x,y)v\in T_{C}(P(x,y))\end{array}\right.\right\}.

By taking
d=0
in the above, it is obvious that the cone
D(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
contains all directions
\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
with
v\not=0
.

We now present sufficient conditions for the directional RS using the first order information. The result extends Gfrerer and Mordukhovich
[
12
, Corollary 3.6]
in that the image directional derivative
{\rm Im}DP_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
instead of the image derivative
{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is used. Hence in the case where
u=0
our result recovers
[
12
, Corollary 3.6]
applied to the parametric program
(P_{x})
. Since for
u\not=0
,
{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
is in general strictly contained in
{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, the sufficient condition for the directional RS in Proposition
3.3
is weaker than the one proposed in
[
12
, Corollary 3.6]
. Naturally the conclusion here is only the directional RS while the conclusion in
[
12
, Corollary 3.6]
is the full/nondirectional RS. Using the proof technique of
[
12
, Theorem 3.5]
, we can now prove the following result.
Since a direction
\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
with
v\not=0
lies in
D(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
with
d=0
, it is obvious FOSCMS (
13
) holding in all directions
w\in D(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
is stronger than FOSCMS holding in all directions
\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
with
v\not=0
in that the perturbation in
x
is now considered.

Proposition 3.3
(First-order sufficient conditions for the directional RS)

Let
\bar{y}\in\mathcal{F}(\bar{x})

and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a given direction. Suppose that for every
d\in{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
and every
\tau_{k}\downarrow 0
, there exists
v
such that

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\tau_{k}(d+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v),C)}{\tau_{k}}=0,

(12)

and FOSCMS of the system
P(\bar{x},y)\in C
at
\bar{y}
holds in every direction
w\in D(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
. i.e,

\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}\lambda=0,\ \lambda\in N_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y});w)\implies\lambda=0.

(13)

Then
{\cal F}
satisfies RS at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in direction
u
.

Proof.
We prove the result by contradiction. Assume RS fails at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in direction
u
. Then there exist sequences
t_{k}\downarrow 0,u^{k}\rightarrow u,y^{k}\rightarrow\bar{y}
such that

{\rm dist}(y^{k},\mathcal{F}(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k}))>k{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C).

(14)

Without loss of generality we may assume that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\epsilon_{k}>0
such that

\|\nabla_{y}P(x,y)-\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|\leq 1/k
for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon_{k}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, and

\|\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k}-\bar{x}\|<\epsilon_{k}/2,\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|<\min\{1/k,\epsilon_{k}/2\},\|P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k})-P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|\leq 1/k^{2}.

Define

\sigma_{k}:=1/(k^{2}{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)).

(15)

Let
(\bar{y}^{k},\bar{\xi}^{k})
be an optimal solution to the minimization problem

\min_{y,\xi}\varphi_{k}(y,\xi):=\|\xi\|+\sigma_{k}\|y-y^{k}\|^{2}\quad s.t.\ P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y)+\xi\in C.

(16)

We claim that
\bar{\xi}^{k}\neq 0
. To the contrary, suppose that
\bar{\xi}^{k}=0
.
Take
\xi^{k}
such that
P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k})+\xi^{k}\in C
and

\|\xi^{k}\|={\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C).

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(y^{k},\mathcal{F}(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|y^{k}-\bar{y}^{k}\|^{2}\mbox{ since }\bar{y}^{k}\in\mathcal{F}(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)\quad\mbox{since }\varphi_{k}(\bar{y}^{k},\bar{\xi}^{k})\leq\varphi_{k}(y^{k},\xi^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{2}{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)^{2}\quad\mbox{ by }(\ref{sigma})

contradicting (
14
). The contradiction proves that
\bar{\xi}^{k}\neq 0
. Furthermore, since

\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}\leq\|\bar{\xi}^{k}\|+\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}=\varphi_{k}(\bar{y}^{k},\bar{\xi}^{k})\leq\varphi_{k}(y^{k},\xi^{k})={\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C),

and

\displaystyle\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)

(17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k\|P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k})-P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|\leq\frac{1}{k},

we have
\|\bar{y}^{k}-\bar{y}\|\leq\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|+\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|\leq 2/k
and
P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}^{k})\rightarrow P(\bar{x},\bar{y})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Next we consider the direction along which the sequence
(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}^{k})
converges to
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.
Define
\tau_{k}:=\|\bar{y}^{k}-\bar{y}\|+\|P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y})-P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\tau_{k}\downarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and without loss of generality there exists a vector
(\bar{v},\bar{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that

\bar{v}:=\lim_{k}\frac{\bar{y}^{k}-\bar{y}}{\tau_{k}}\ \mbox{and}\ \bar{d}:=\lim_{k}\frac{P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y})-P(\bar{x},\bar{y})}{\tau_{k}}.

(18)

One can easily obtain
(\bar{v},\bar{d})\neq(0,0)
with
\bar{d}\in{\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)
. To show that
\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\bar{v}\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
, by (
2
) it suffices to prove that

{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\tau_{k}(\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\bar{v}),C)=o(\tau_{k}).

(19)

By condition (
12
), for
\bar{d}
and the sequence
\{\tau_{k}\}
there exists
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\tau_{k}(\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v),C)}{\tau_{k}}=0.

Since

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{\|P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v)-(P(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\tau_{k}(\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v))\|}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{\|P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v)-(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y})+\tau_{k}\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})v)\|}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{\|\int_{0}^{1}[\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+s(\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}))-\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})]\tau_{k}v{\rm d}s\|}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{2}{k}\|v\|=0,

(20)

where the first equality follows from (
18
), one has

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v),C)}{\tau_{k}}=0.

Let
\xi^{k}
be such that
P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v)+\xi^{k}\in C
and
\|\xi^{k}\|={\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v),C)
. Then
\|\xi^{k}\|=o(\tau_{k})
.

Since
\varphi(\bar{y}^{k},\bar{\xi}^{k})\leq\varphi(\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v,\xi^{k})

one has

\displaystyle\|\bar{\xi}^{k}\|\leq

\displaystyle\|\xi^{k}\|-\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-y^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\xi^{k}\|+\sigma_{k}(2\langle\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\rangle+\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\xi^{k}\|+2\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|\cdot\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|+\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|^{2}.

(21)

By (
17
) we have

\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)}k{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)=\frac{1}{k}\rightarrow 0\ \mbox{as}\ k\rightarrow\infty.

(22)

Since
\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|\leq\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}\|+\|\bar{y}^{k}-\bar{y}\|\leq\tau_{k}(\|v\|+2\|\bar{v}\|)
and combining (
3
), (
22
)
and the facts
\|\bar{\xi}^{k}\|\leq{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)
,
\|\xi^{k}\|=o(\tau_{k})
yield

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{\|\bar{\xi}^{k}\|}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{\min\{\|\xi^{k}\|+2\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|\cdot\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|+\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|^{2},{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)\}}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{\|\xi^{k}\|}{\tau_{k}}+\lim_{k}\frac{2\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k}\|\cdot\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle+\lim_{k}\frac{\min\{\sigma_{k}\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|^{2},{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)\}}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0+\lim_{k}\frac{2/k\tau_{k}(\|v\|+2\|\bar{v}\|)}{\tau_{k}}+\lim_{k}\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_{k}{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k}),C)}\|\bar{y}+\tau_{k}v-\bar{y}^{k}\|}{\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{2}{k}(\|v\|+2\|\bar{v}\|)=0,

(23)

where the last inequality follows from (
15
).

Combining (
3
) and (
3
), one can easily obtain

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\tau_{k}(\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\bar{v}),C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|P(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\tau_{k}(\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\bar{v})-P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}\bar{v})\|+{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}+\tau_{k}\bar{v}),C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\tau_{k})+{\rm dist}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}^{k}),C)=o(\tau_{k})+\|\bar{\xi}^{k}\|=o(\tau_{k}),

where the second inequality follows from (
3
) and (
18
) and the last equality is obtained in (
3
). By (
19
),
\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\bar{v}\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
.

Finally, we are ready to deduce a contradiction. The constraint system for problem (
16
) can be written as
H_{k}(y,\xi)\in C
where
H_{k}(y,\xi):=P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y)+\xi
. Since the Jacobian
\nabla H_{k}(y,\xi)
has full row rank, the necessary optimality conditions for (

(\bar{y}^{k},\bar{\xi}^{k})
, i.e., there exists a multiplier
\lambda_{k}\in N_{C}(P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}^{k})+\bar{\xi}^{k})

2\sigma_{k}(\bar{y}^{k}-y^{k})+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},\bar{y}^{k})^{T}\lambda^{k}=0\ \mbox{and}\ \frac{\bar{\xi}^{k}}{\|\bar{\xi}^{k}\|}+\lambda^{k}=0.

(24)

Since
\|\lambda^{k}\|=1
, passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists
0\neq\bar{\lambda}=\lim_{k}\lambda^{k}
. Then taking the limit of (

, one has

\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}\bar{\lambda}=0,\ \bar{\lambda}\in N_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y});\bar{d}+\nabla_{y}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\bar{v})

contradicting condition (

Since when
\nabla_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})u\neq 0

\{\alpha\nabla_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y})u|\alpha\geq 0\}={\rm Im}D_{x}P(\bar{x},\bar{y};u)

has the following form.

Let
\bar{y}\in\mathcal{F}(\bar{x})

Remark 3.1
(Assumption Verification)

The verification of Condition (

, it was shown that for a parametric nonlinear program, RS in direction

implies that the linearization system (

Proposition 3.4

Let
\bar{y}\in{\cal F}(\bar{x})
and

. Suppose
{\cal F}
satisfies RS at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in direction

. Then there exists
v
satisfying the system

\nabla P(\bar{x},\bar{y})(u,v)\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y})).

(27)

Proof.
Since RS holds at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in direction
u
, by Definition 3.1, there exist positive scalars
\kappa,\epsilon,\delta
such that, for any

{\rm dist}(\bar{y},\mathcal{F}(x))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(P(x,\bar{y}),C)\leq\kappa\|P(x,\bar{y})-P(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|\leq L_{p}\kappa\|x-\bar{x}\|,

where

is the Lipschitz modulus of

around

. Then for any sequences

which implies that

\nabla P(\bar{x},\bar{y})(u,v)\in T_{C}(P(\bar{x},\bar{y}))

4
Directional derivatives using the directional solution set

The main purpose of this section is to extend the classical results on the directional derivative to the general parametric problem
(P_{x})
where the solution set is replaced by a directional solution set.

First we recall definition of a directional solution set.

Definition 4.1
(Directional Solution Set)

[
1
, Definition 4.5]

Let
\bar{x},u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The set of optimal solutions for problem
(P_{x})
in direction
u
is defined by

S(\bar{x};u)=\{y\in S(\bar{x})|\exists x^{k}\xrightarrow{u}\bar{x},y^{k}\rightarrow y,\ \mbox{s.t.}\ y^{k}\in S(x^{k})\}.

It is easy to see that for a nonzero direction
u
, a directional solution set is a subset of the solution set. If the directional solution set is nonempty, then we may try to use it to replace the solution set and provide tighter bounds to
the upper/lower Dini directional derivative of the value function
in (
1
).
Generally, it is possible that
S(\bar{x};u)=\emptyset
even when the solution set is nonempty and so the generalization using the directional solution set is only useful
when the directional solution is nonempty. To answer this question, we first present the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1

Let
\bar{x},u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
V(x)
is continuous at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
. Let
t_{k}\downarrow 0,u^{k}\rightarrow u
and
y^{k}\in S(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})
. If
y_{k}\rightarrow y
, then
y\in S(\bar{x};u)
.

Proof.
Since
y^{k}\in S(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})
, we have

\displaystyle f(\bar{x},y)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}f(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{V}(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})={V}(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle P(\bar{x},y)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}P(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k},y^{k})\in C,

which implies that
y\in S(\bar{x})
. By Definition
4.1
, we have

y\in S(\bar{x};u).

We now give a sufficient condition for the nonemptyness of the directional solution set.
First we present a condition which guarantees the directional lower semicontinuity of the value function.

Definition 4.2
(Directional Restricted Inf-compactness)

[
2
, Definition 4.1]
We say that the restricted inf-compactness holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
if
V(\bar{x})
is finite and there exist a compact set
\Omega_{u}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and positive numbers
\varepsilon>0,\delta>0
such that for all
x\in\bar{x}+{\cal V}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)
with
V(x)<V(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
, one always has
S(x)\cap\Omega_{u}\neq\emptyset
.

Obviously, if the restricted inf-compactness holds at
\bar{x}
in direction u=0, then the classical restricted inf-compactness defined in
[
6
, Hypothesis 6.5.1]
and
[
15
, Definition 3.8]
holds. The reader is referred to some easier to verify sufficient conditions for the directional restricted inf-compactness in
[
2
]
.

We now show that under the directional restricted inf-compactness condition and directional continuity of the value function, the directional solution set is nonempty.

Proposition 4.1

Suppose the restricted inf-compactness condition holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
. If
V(x)
is continuous at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
. Then
S(\bar{x};u)
is nonempty.

Proof.
Let
t_{k}\downarrow 0,u^{k}\rightarrow u
.
Since
V(x)
is continuous at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
, for any
\varepsilon>0

V(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})<V(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
for sufficiently large
k
. Since the restricted inf-compactness holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
with the compact set
\Omega_{u}
, there exists a sequence
y^{k}\in S(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})\cap\Omega_{u}
for
k
large enough. Without loss of generality, assume
y:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}y^{k}
. Then by Lemma
4.1
,

y\in S(\bar{x};u)\cap\Omega_{u}.

Now we give a sufficient condition for the directional continuity of the value function which is a directional version of the result in Guo et al.
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 4.2

Assume that the restricted inf-compactness holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
and there exists
\bar{y}\in S(\bar{x})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\xrightarrow{u}\bar{x}}{\rm dist}(\bar{y},\mathcal{F}(x))=0.

(29)

is continuous at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
.

Proof.
Under the restricted inf-compactness at
\bar{x}
in direction
u

is l.s.c. at
\bar{x}
in direction
u
which can be proved using a similar argument as in the proof of the non-directional case in Guo et al.
[
15
, Theorem 3.9]
. We now prove the upper semicontinuity of

at
\bar{x}
in direction

be a sequence such that

For each

This shows that

is u.s.c. at
\bar{x}
in direction

The proof is complete.

It is obvious that the RS at

\bar{x}

\bar{x}

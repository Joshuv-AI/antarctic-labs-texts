Deformed graphical zonotopal algebras
Boris Shapiro, Ilya Smirnov, Arkady Vaintrob

Abstract
. We study certain filtered deformations of the external zonotopal algebra of a given graph parametrized by univariate polynomials. We establish some general properties of these algebras, compute their Hilbert series for a number of graphs using Macaulay2, and formulate several conjectures.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
13A70, 05C25, 14D99

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite undirected graph. Wagner
[
11
]
and, independently,
Postnikov and the first author
[
7
]
introduced a commutative graded
algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
whose dimension is equal to the number of spanning
forests of
G
. They also showed that the Hilbert series of
\mathscr{C}_{G}

is a specialization of the Tutte polynomial of
G
which enumerates
the spanning forests of
G
according to
their external activity.
Wagner’s initial goal was to construct new algebraic invariants of graphs.
Postnikov and the first author were motivated by the earlier work
[
9
,
8
]
,
where it was shown that for the complete graph
G
, the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
is
isomorphic to the algebra generated by the curvature forms of tautological
Hermitian line bundles on the complete flag manifold. Soon it turned out that these
algebras are connected to several other areas, such as the theory of power
ideals, box splines, enumeration of lattice points, chip firing, etc. They have
been studied under various names: circulation algebras, Postnikov-Shapiro
algebras, forest-counting algebras, and (external) zonotopal algebras. We will
use the latter term reflecting their connection with enumeration of lattice
points in zonotopes (see e.g.
[
3
]
).

Wagner
[
10
]
and Nenashev
[
4
]
proved that the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}

determines the graphical matroid of
G
. However, non-isomorphic graphs can have
isomorphic algebras. In
[
6
]
, Nenashev and the first author introduced a
filtered algebra
\mathscr{K}_{G}
which they called a K-theoretic analogue of
\mathscr{C}_{G}
.
They showed that
\mathscr{K}_{G}
and
\mathscr{C}_{G}
are isomorphic as (non-filtered) algebras,
but, unlike
\mathscr{C}_{G}
, the filtered algebra
\mathscr{K}_{G}
is a complete invariant of
G
.

The algebra
\mathscr{K}_{G}
is a deformation of
\mathscr{C}_{G}
in the class of filtered
algebras. It is a member of a larger family of filtered deformations
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}

of
\mathscr{C}_{G}
parametrized by polynomials
f\in\mathbf{k}[u]
which was introduced
in
[
6
]
. In the current work, we begin to study this family of algebras
trying to understand their relationship to each other and to the graph
G
.

We begin Section
2
with a review of the definition and
properties of the graded algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
. Then in Section
2.2
we turn
to the deformed algebras
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
and establish some general facts about them.
In particular, we prove that under some mild
nondegeneracy assumption on
f
, the algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
is isomorphic
to
\mathscr{C}_{G}
as an unfiltered algebra.
We introduce a natural stratification of the space of such algebras
for a given graph
G
.
Section
3
discusses several properties of deformed
zonotopal algebras and the latter stratification.
Unfortunately, unlike the graded case, we cannot explicitly find the
Hilbert sequences of these algebras. In Section
4
we
collect our computations of the Hilbert sequences for several
examples performed in Macaulay 2.
Finally, in Section
5
we present a number of conjectures
and questions for further study.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Graded zonotopal algebras

By a
graph
we understand a finite undirected multigraph
G=(V,E)
,
possibly with loops, with a vertex set
V
and a (multi)set of edges
E
.

Graphs form a category
\mathscr{G}r
with morphisms
G=(V,E)\to G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
defined as
maps of pairs

(2.1)

(\gamma,\lambda)\colon(V,E)\to(V^{\prime},E^{\prime}),

injective on edges
and preserving incidences between vertices and edges
(i.e. if
e\in E
is an edge in
G
connecting
u
and
v
, then
\lambda(e)

is an edge in
G^{\prime}
connecting
\gamma(u)
and
\gamma(v)
).

There are two special kinds of graph morphisms,

edge deletions

j_{e}\colon G-e\to G

and
edge contractions

\pi_{e}\colon G\to G_{e}
, where
e\in E
is an edge of
G
,

G-e
is the subgraph of
G
obtained by removing
e
from
E
, and
G_{e}
is
the graph obtained from
G
by identifying the endpoints
u
and
v
of
e
(and
thus creating a loop for each edge connecting
u
and
v
).

All algebras in this paper are commutative unital algebras over
a fixed field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic
0
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, its
edge algebra

\Phi_{G}
is the
quotient of the polynomial algebra in
edge variables

\phi_{e},e\in E
, by their squares,

(2.2)

\Phi_{G}:=\mathbf{k}[\phi_{e}:e\in E]/(\phi_{e}^{2}).

The edge algebra is a local algebra of dimension
2^{|E|}
isomorphic to the
tensor product of
|E|
copies of the algebra of dual numbers

\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2})
, namely

\displaystyle\Phi_{G}\simeq\bigotimes_{e\in E}\big(\mathbf{k}[\phi_{e}]/(\phi_{e}^{2})\big).

It inherits a standard grading from the polynomial algebra. A basis of the
k
th
graded component is given by square-free words of length
k
in edge variables

\phi_{e}
.

Observe that this construction is functorial. Every graph morphism (
2.1
) induces a
natural homomorphism
of algebras
\lambda^{*}\colon\Phi_{G^{\prime}}\to\Phi_{G}
, defined on generators

\phi_{e^{\prime}}\in\Phi_{G^{\prime}}

as

(2.3)

\lambda^{*}(\phi_{e^{\prime}}):=\sum_{e\in\lambda^{-1}(e^{\prime})}\phi_{e},

i.e.,
\lambda^{*}(\phi_{\lambda(e)})=\phi_{e}
and

\lambda^{*}(\phi_{e^{\prime}})=0
, if
e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
is not in the image of
\lambda
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with a linear order
<
on its vertex set
V
.
The
zonotopal algebra
of
G
is the subalgebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
of the edge algebra
\Phi_{G}
generated by the elements:

(2.4)

X_{v}=\sum_{e\in G}c_{v,e}\phi_{e},\quad v\in V,

called
vertex flows
where

(2.5)

c_{v,e}=\begin{cases}\;\;\;1\quad\text{if}\;e=\{v,u\},\ v<u,\\
-1\quad\text{if}\;e=\{v,u\},\ v>u,\\
\;\;\;0\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.3
.

Even though the coefficients
c_{v,e}
in (
2.5
), and thus the
elements (
2.4
), depend on the chosen ordering
<
of the vertex set
V
, the
subalgebras obtained from two different orderings will be identified under a
graded automorphism of the algebra
\Phi_{G}
given by changing signs of some
generators
\phi_{e}
.
It is clear that loop edges of
G
do not contribute to the flow
generators
X_{v}
. For this reason, in earlier papers
[
7
,
4
,
6
]
on this
topic, graphs with loops were not considered.
In this work we allow loops because they naturally appear when we consider
graph homomorphisms involving edge contractions.

One of the important properties of algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
is that it is
functorial with respect to graph
morphisms (
2.1
).

Proposition 2.4
.

For every graph morphism
(\gamma,\lambda)\colon G\to G^{\prime}
is , the homomorphism

\lambda^{*}
(
2.3
) sends the subalgebra
\mathscr{C}_{G^{\prime}}\subset\Phi_{G^{\prime}}

to
\mathscr{C}_{G}\subset\Phi_{G}
.

Proof.

All we need to show is that for every vertex
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
,
the image of the generator
X_{v^{\prime}}
of
\mathscr{C}_{G^{\prime}}
under the homomorphism

\lambda^{*}
belongs to
\mathscr{C}_{G}\subset\Phi_{G}
.
Indeed, if
v^{\prime}\not\in\gamma(V)
then
\lambda(E)
contains no
edges incident to
v^{\prime}
, and so
\lambda^{*}(X_{v^{\prime}})=0
. If

v^{\prime}\in\gamma(V)
, then, because of the injectivity of
\lambda
, we
have
\displaystyle\lambda^{*}(X_{v^{\prime}})=\sum_{v\in\gamma^{-1}(v^{\prime})}X_{v}\in\mathscr{C}_{G}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

(1) The definition of
\mathscr{C}_{G}
involves choosing a linear order
<
on
V
. In
the above proof we assume that the orders
<
and
<^{\prime}
on
V
and
V^{\prime}
resp. are compatible,
i.e. they are chosen in such a way that the map
\gamma\colon V\to V^{\prime}
is monotone.

(2) Since the loop edges of
G
do not contribute to the generators
X_{v}
of
the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
, we can remove loops from
G
without affecting
\mathscr{C}_{G}
.
In particular, let
G/e
be the graph obtained from
G
by contracting an edge

e\in E
by removing
e
(without creating a new loop) and identifying the
endpoints
u
and
v
of
e
. Then the contracting morphism

\pi_{e}\colon G\to G_{e}
gives an injective homomorphism

(2.6)

\pi_{e}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G/e}=\mathscr{C}_{G_{e}}\to\mathscr{C}_{G}

which sends the generator
X_{w}
corresponding to the new vertex
w=\{u,v\}
to

X_{v}+X_{u}\in\mathscr{C}_{G}
.

As was proved in
[
10
,
8
]
, the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
is a power algebra,
i.e. it is isomorphic to a quotient of the polynomial algebra by an ideal
generated by powers of linear forms.
Namely, for a subset
I\subset V
of vertices denote by
D_{I}
the number
of edges
e\in E
connecting a vertex from
I
with one in the complementary
subset
I^{c}=V-I
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
10
,
8
]
)
.

The zonotopal algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
is isomorphic to the quotient of the polynomial
algebra
\mathbf{k}[x_{v}\,:\,v\in V]
by the ideal
(p_{I})_{I\subseteq V}

generated by the polynomials

(2.7)

p_{I}=\Bigl(\sum_{v\in I}x_{v}\Bigr)^{D_{I}+1}.

The dimension and the Hilbert function of the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
were also found
in
[
10
,
8
]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

(i)
The dimension of the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
is equal to the number of
spanning subforests in
G
(which is the same as the number of acyclic
subsets of edges
S\subseteq E
).

(ii)
The dimension of the
k
th graded component of
\mathscr{C}_{G}
is
equal to the number of subforests
S\subseteq E
with the external
activity
|E-S|-k
.

Proof.

Functoriality of the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
with respect to graph homomorphisms
leads to simple proofs of Theorems
2.6
and
2.7
.

Indeed, let
e\in E
be a non-loop edge of a graph
G
.
Consider two algebra homomorphisms, a projection
j_{e}^{*}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G}\to\mathscr{C}_{G-e}

(which corresponds to sending
\phi_{e}\in\Phi_{G}
to
0
) and an embedding

\pi_{e}^{*}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G/e}=\mathscr{C}_{G_{e}}\to\mathscr{C}_{G}
(which maps
X_{w}
to
X_{u}+X_{v}
).

Denote by
\displaystyle\delta_{e}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{e}}\colon\Phi_{G}\to\Phi_{G-e}
, the partial derivative with respect to the
edge variable
\phi_{e}\in\Phi_{G}
. Modulo
(\phi_{e})
the map
\delta_{e}
is indeed a derivation
of the edge algebra
\Phi_{G}
which sends the subalgebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
onto

\mathscr{C}_{G-e}
and generates an exact sequence of graded spaces

0\to\mathscr{C}_{G/e}\to\mathscr{C}_{G}\to\mathscr{C}_{G-e}[1]\to 0,

where the rightmost map (induced by
\delta
) decreases the grading by
1
.
This exact sequence implies the relation

(2.8)

H_{\mathscr{C}_{G}}(t)=H_{\mathscr{C}/e}(t)+tH_{\mathscr{C}_{G-e}}(t),

for the Hilbert series which proves Theorem
2.7
by induction on
|E|
.

To settle Theorem
2.6
we can use similar maps and an exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{B}_{G/e}\to\mathscr{B}_{G}\to\mathscr{B}_{G-e}[1]\to 0,

for quotient algebras
\mathscr{B}_{G}:=\mathbf{k}[x_{v}\,:\,v\in V]/(p_{I})_{I\subseteq V}
,
where the polynomial
p_{I}
is given by (
2.7
).
∎

In
[
5
]
G. Nenashev has shown that
\mathscr{C}_{G}
contains all
information about the graphical matroid of
G
and only it.

Proposition 2.8

(Theorem 5 of
[
5
]
)
.

Given two undirected
(multi)graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2},
algebras
\mathscr{C}_{G_{1}}
and
\mathscr{C}_{G_{2}}
are
isomorphic if and only if the graphical matroids of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
coincide.
(The latter isomorphism can be thought of either as graded or as non-graded,
the statement holds in both cases.)

2.2.
Deformed algebras
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}

2.2.1.
Definition

The main object of our study is a certain family of filtered algebras, introduced
in
[
6
]
, which we call
deformed zonotopal algebras
.

Definition 2.9
.

A formal power series
f=a_{0}+a_{1}u+a_{2}u^{2}+\ldots\in\mathbf{k}[[u]]
is called

nondegenerate
if
a_{1}\neq 0
, i.e. when
f^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
.

Definition 2.10
.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
and a nondegenerate power series
f\in\mathbf{k}[[u]]
, we define
the
deformed zonotopal algebra
of
G
associated to
f
as the
subalgebra of the edge algebra
\Phi_{G}
generated by the elements

Y_{v}:=f(X_{v})=f\bigl(\sum_{e\in E}c_{v,e}\phi_{e}\bigr),\ v\in V,

where
c_{v,e}
are given by (
2.5
).

In particular, for
f=u
,
the algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
is the usual zonotopal algebra

\mathscr{C}_{G}
discussed above and for
f=e^{u}
, this algebra
coincides with the
K
-theoretic analog
\mathscr{K}_{G}
of
\mathscr{C}_{G}
studied in
[
6
]
.

Remark 2.11
.

Since
\phi_{e}^{2}=0
, the element
X_{v}
is nilpotent with
X_{v}^{n}=0

for
n>\deg v
, and so plugging it into a power series is well-defined.
Moreover, this argument also shows that the terms of
f
of degree higher than

\mathrm{md}_{G}:=\max_{v\in V}\deg v,

the maximal degree of a vertex in
G
,
do not affect any of the generators
Y_{v}
of
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}
. Therefore, we can
restrict our attention to those
f\in\mathbf{k}[[u]]
which are polynomials in
u
of
degree at most
\mathrm{md}_{G}
.

The algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
is endowed with an increasing filtration

(2.9)

\mathbf{k}=\mathscr{C}^{f,0}_{G}\subset\mathscr{C}^{f,1}_{G}\subset\mathscr{C}^{f,2}_{G}\subset\ldots,

where the subspace
\mathscr{C}^{f,k}_{G}
is spanned by the monomials of degree at most

k
in the generators
Y_{v}=f(X_{v})
.

Remark 2.12
.

Notice that neither the algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
nor this filtration depend
on the constant term
a_{0}=f(0)
of
f
, since changing
f
by a constant
modifies
Y_{v}
by this constant which is an element of
\mathbf{k}=\mathscr{C}^{f,0}_{G}
.
It is also clear that multiplying
f
by a nonzero constant does not change
the filtration (
2.9
).

For this reason, from now on, we will assume that
f
has no constant term
i.e. that
f-u^{k}\in u^{k+1}\mathbf{k}[[u]]
for
k\geq 1
.

When
f=u
, this filtration coincides with the filtration induced by the
grading on
\mathscr{C}_{G}
.
Let us now present some basic properties of
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
proven in
[
6
]
.

Proposition 2.13

(
[
6
, Proposition 2]
)
.

If
f
is a nondegenerate series, then the algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
coincides, as a
subalgebras of
\Phi_{G}
, with the usual zonotopal algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
.
In other words, in this case the only difference between the graded algebra

\mathscr{C}_{G}
and
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
is in their filtrations.

Proof.

Firstly, by Remark
2.11
we can assume that
f
is a
polynomial.
Secondly, if
f
is a polynomial we have the inclusion
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{G}

because
f(X_{i})\in\mathscr{C}_{G}
for every
i
.
Finally, there exists a polynomial
g(u)
such that
X_{i}=g(f(X_{i}))
for every
i

which finishes the proof.
Indeed, the formal power series

g(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}u^{i}
can be found by requiring that

u=f(\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}u^{i})=c_{1}a_{1}u+(c_{1}a_{2}+c_{2}a_{1}^{2})u^{2}+(c_{1}a_{3}+2c_{2}a_{1}a_{2}+c_{3}a_{1}^{3})u^{3}+\cdots.

Under the assumption that
c_{1}\neq 0
, this system of equations can solved for
each
a_{j}
consecutively by induction.
The resulting power series
g(u)

can be truncated to a polynomial in view of
Remark
2.11
.
∎

This proposition together and the above remark explain why we focus our
attention on (nondegenerate) polynomials
f=u+\ldots
.

Theorem 2.14

(
[
6
, Theorem 6]
)
.

Let
f
be a polynomial with non-vanishing linear and quadratic terms and let

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two simple graphs without isolated vertices.
Then
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G_{1}}
and
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G_{2}}
are isomorphic as filtered algebras if
and only if the graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are isomorphic.

Definition 2.15
.

Given a graph
G
, we will call the affine space

(2.10)

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}=\{f\in\mathbf{k}[u]\,|\,f(0)=0,f^{\prime}(0)=1,\deg f\leq\mathrm{md}_{G}\}

the space of parameters of deformed zonotopal algebras
of
G
.

For
f\in{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
, we will be interested in the
Hilbert sequence

\mathscr{H}^{f}_{G}:=(\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathscr{C}^{f,j}_{G}/\mathscr{C}^{f,j-1}_{G})_{j\geq 0}

of the filtered algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
(where, by convention,
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f,-1}=0
).

Proposition 2.16

(
[
6
, Proposition 3]
; see also Theorem
3.8
.)
.

There exists a non-empty Zariski open subset
U\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
such that the
Hilbert sequences
\mathscr{H}^{f}_{G}
, for all
f\in U
, are the same and maximal among all possible Hilbert sequences
\mathscr{H}^{f}_{G}
,
f\in{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
in the
lexicographic order.

We will call the above maximal Hilbert sequence the
general Hilbert sequence
and will denote it simply by
\mathscr{H}_{G}
.

3.
Algebraic properties of deformed zonotopal algebras

3.1.
Generators and relations

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional local algebra over
\mathbf{k}
with maximal ideal

m
and with a set of algebra generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in m
.
Let
f\in\mathbf{k}[[u]]
be a nondegenerate series with
f(0)=0
. Then

(1)

the map
f\colon A\to A,\ a\mapsto f(a)
is well-defined
and invertible;

(2)

the elements
y_{1}=f(x_{1}),\dots,y_{n}=f(x_{n})
generate
A
;

(3)

for
L\in\mathbf{k}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
, the relation
L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0

holds in
A
if and only if the relation
L(f^{-1}(y_{1}),\dots,f^{-1}(y_{n}))=0

holds in
A
.

Proof.

To settle (1) notice that since
A
is an Artinian algebra, any
a\in A
is nilpotent
which implies that
f(a)
is a finite sum. Therefore
f(a)
is well-defined for any

a
. The inverse of
f
has been already constructed in the proof of
Proposition
2.13
.

Now, (2) is obvious since
f
is invertible as a map of
A
and therefore

x_{i}=f^{-1}(y_{i})
for every
i
. The first part of (3), claiming that the
relation

L(f^{-1}(y_{1}),\dots,f^{-1}(y_{N}))=0

holds in
A
, is obvious.
Conversely, assume that a relation
R(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

=0
holds in
A
. Then the
equation
R(f(x_{1}),\dots,f(x_{n}))=0
gives a relation in
A
in terms of the original
generating set
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
which then satisfies the claim because of invertibility of
f
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
and a non-degenerate
f\in\mathbf{k}[[u]]
, we have the isomorphism of
\mathbf{k}
-algebras

\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}\cong\mathbf{k}[y_{v}\,:\,v\in V]/I_{G}^{f}
; the latter is the quotient of the polynomial algebra by the ideal
I_{G}^{f}
generated by two sets

\{y_{v}^{\deg v+1}\mid v\in V\}

and

\{\left(\sum_{v\in I}f^{-1}(y_{v})\right)^{D_{I}+1}\mid I\subset V,|I|\geq 2\}

where
D_{I}
has been defined before Theorem
2.6
.

Proof.

As we have already seen,
X_{v}^{\deg v+1}=0
which implies that

Y_{v}^{\deg v+1}=(f(X_{v}))^{\deg v+1}=0

by plugging directly into the formula for
f(u)
.
By Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
2.6
,

\left(\sum_{v\in I}f^{-1}(Y_{v})\right)^{D_{I}+1}
are still relations in
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
.
Thus by mapping
y_{i}\mapsto Y_{i}
we see that
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
is
a homomorphic image of
\mathbf{k}[y_{v}\,:\,v\in V]/I_{G}^{f}
.
Due to invertibility of
f(u)
, there are no other relations (i.e., the kernel of the homomorphism is zero) as this would contradict Theorem
2.6
.
∎

In view of Proposition
2.13
, we can view each
f
as a certain choice of filtration
on a fixed algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
. We can always recover the original algebra by taking
the associated graded ring with respect to the distinguished ideal.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
f
be a nondegenerate polynomial and let
J
be the ideal
of the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}
generated by the elements
Y_{v}=f(X_{v})
,
v\in V
.
Then the associated graded algebra
\mathrm{Gr}_{J}(\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f})
for the
J
-adic filtration is isomorphic
to the graded algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
. In particular, the Hilbert series of
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
and

\mathscr{C}_{G}
coincide.

Proof.

In the notation of Corollary
3.2
, the associated graded
algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
is the quotient of
\mathbf{k}[Y_{v}]
by the initial ideal of
I_{G}^{f}
,
i.e., the ideal generated by the lowest degree homogeneous forms of elements

f\in I_{G}^{f}
. Clearly,
Y_{v}^{\deg v+1},\ v\in V,

are contained in the initial ideal and the expression

\left(\sum_{v\in I}f^{-1}(Y_{v})\right)^{D_{I}+1}

will contribute the homogeneous form arising from the linear part of
f^{-1}
,
i.e.,
\left(\sum_{v\in I}Y_{v}\right)^{D_{I}+1}
.
These forms are the defining relations of the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
and there are no other
relations because
\dim\gr(\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G})=\dim\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}=\dim\mathscr{C}_{G}
by Proposition
2.13
and properties of associated graded algebras.
Thus the graded algebras
\mathrm{Gr}_{J}(\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f})
and
\mathscr{C}_{G}
are isomorphic.
∎

Several concrete examples of such relations can be found later in the text.

Remark 3.4
.

The relation
\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}f^{-1}(Y_{v})=0
corresponding to
I=V

in Corollary
3.2

can be used to remove half of the generators of the ideal
I_{G}^{f}
.
Namely, for a subset
I\subset V
with
1<|I|<|V|
, consider the
complementary subset
I^{c}=V-I
. If
|I|<|I^{c}|
, then keep the generator

\left(\sum_{v\in I}f^{-1}(Y_{v})\right)^{D_{I}+1}
corresponding to
I
and remove the one corresponding to
I^{c}
.
If
|I^{c}|>|I|
, then keep the generator corresponding to
I^{c}

and remove the one corresponding to
I
. Finally, if
|I|=|I^{c}|
(which can
happen only if
|V|
is even), then keep any of these two generators and remove
the other one. The generator corresponding to
I=V
should not be removed.

3.2.
Stratification of the space of deformed zonotopal algebras of a graph

Let
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
be the affine space (
2.10
) of parameters of algebras

\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}
for a graph
G
.
Our ambition is to study the stratification of
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
according to the Hilbert sequences
\mathscr{H}_{G}^{f}
of
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
.

Since the generators
Y_{v},\ v\in V,
of the algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
are nilpotent,
the corresponding Hilbert sequence
\mathscr{H}_{G}^{f}
has finitely many non-zero terms.
We denote by
\overline{\mathscr{H}}_{G}^{f}
the finite sequence obtained by removing all zero terms
of
\mathscr{H}_{G}^{f}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Given a graph
G
and a sequence
M=(1,m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{n})
of positive integers,
the
associated Hilbert stratum
is the subset
S_{G}^{M}
of the parameter space
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}

consisting of all
f
such that
\overline{\mathscr{H}}_{G}^{f}=M
.

We will show that each
S_{G}^{M}
is a constructible algebraic set and we will be
interested in the decomposition of its closure into irreducible closed algebraic
components. (Observe that
S_{G}^{M}
might be empty.)
Let us describe the adjacency of these strata.

3.3.
Semicontinuity

First recall the following notion.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
X
be a topological space and let

(\Lambda,\prec)
be a partially ordered set.
We say that a function
g\colon X\to\Lambda
is upper semicontinuous if for
every
\lambda\in\Lambda,
the set

g^{-1}(\prec\lambda):=\{x\in X\mid g(x)\prec\lambda\}

is open.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
M
be a matrix with entries in
A
.
For a prime
\mathfrak{p}\in A
, let
M(\mathfrak{p})
be the matrix obtained from
M
by
replacing the entries by their images in
k(\mathfrak{p})
.
Then the real-valued function
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\rank M(\mathfrak{p})
(respectively,

\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\dim\ker M(\mathfrak{p})
) is lower (resp., upper) semicontinuous.

Proof.

We use the fact that non-vanishing of a minor is an open condition.
Namely, if
B
is a square matrix then
\det B(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
if and only if

\det B\notin\mathfrak{p}
.
Hence, for any
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\rank M(\mathfrak{p})\geq r
, there is an open
neighborhood where the same condition holds.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}]/I

be a finite
A
-module. Then

(1)

the function
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}R\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p})
is upper
semicontinuous on
\Spec R
;

(2)

for any positive integer
m
,

H_{m}\colon\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\langle X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{N}^{\alpha_{N}}\mid\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\leq m\rangle
,
where the latter is a submodule of
R\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p})
,
is also a lower semicontinuous function on
\Spec A
;

(3)

for
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{q},
we have the equality
H_{m}(\mathfrak{p})=H_{m}(\mathfrak{q})

if and only if

\langle X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{N}^{\alpha_{N}}\mid\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\leq m\rangle\otimes_{A}A/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{q}}

is a free
\otimes_{A}A/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{q}}
-module.

Proof.

Since
R
is a finite
A
-module, it can be generated (as a module) by monomials
of bounded degree. We fix such system of generators of
R
to define its
presentation as an
A
-module in the form:

A^{\oplus m}\xrightarrow{M}A^{\oplus n}\to R\to 0.

It is now clear that

\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}R\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p})=n-\rank M(\mathfrak{p})
,
so the first claim follows from Lemma
3.7
.

For the second claim, we take
V_{k}
to be a free submodule of
A^{\oplus n}

corresponding to monomials of degree at most
k
.
Then

\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\langle X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{N}^{\alpha_{N}}\mid\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\leq k\rangle=\rank_{A}V_{k}-\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}\nolimits M(\mathfrak{p})\cap V_{k}(\mathfrak{p}).

By a standard linear algebra argument

\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}\nolimits M(\mathfrak{p}))\cap V_{k}(\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\ker[M\mid-V_{k}](\mathfrak{p})

which is an upper semicontinuous function by Lemma
3.7
.

Last, observe that for any finite
A
-module
M
, the equality

\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}M\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{k(\mathfrak{q})}M\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{q})

is equivalent to
M\otimes_{A}A/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{q}}

being
A/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{q}}
-free since the generic rank and the minimal number
of generators of this module have to be equal.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}]/I

be a finite
A
-module. Suppose that
R
is generated (as a module) by monomials
of degree at most
D
and set
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus D+1}
with the
lexicographic order.
In the notation of Theorem
3.8
, define the function

H\colon\Spec A\to\Lambda,\mathfrak{p}\mapsto(H_{0},H_{1},\ldots,H_{D})
.
Then
H
is lower semicontinuous.
Moreover, for any vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots)
, the set

H^{-1}(\lambda)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec A\mid H(\mathfrak{p})=\lambda\}

is the intersection of an open set
H^{-1}(\succeq_{lex}\lambda)
and a closed
set
H^{-1}(\preceq_{lex}\lambda)
.

In particular, the closure of
H^{-1}(\lambda)
coincides with

H^{-1}(\preceq_{lex}\lambda)
.

Proof.

For the next claim, we first note that we may stop at
H_{D}
.
Since each function
H_{k}
is discrete, the set

H_{k}^{-1}(\succeq a)=H_{k}^{-1}(\succ a-1)
is open.
Thus if we set
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{D})
, we may decompose

\displaystyle H^{-1}(\succ_{lex}\lambda)=

\displaystyle H_{0}^{-1}(\succ\lambda_{0})\bigcup H_{0}^{-1}(\succeq\lambda_{0})\cap H_{1}^{-1}(\succ\lambda_{1})\bigcup

\displaystyle H_{0}^{-1}(\succeq\lambda_{0})\cap H_{1}^{-1}(\succeq\lambda_{1})\cap H_{2}^{-1}(\succ\lambda_{2})\bigcup\cdots\bigcup

\displaystyle H_{0}^{-1}(\succeq\lambda_{0})\cap\cdots\cap H_{D-1}^{-1}(\succeq\lambda_{D-1})\cap H_{D}^{-1}(\succ\lambda_{D})

and see that
H^{-1}(\succ_{lex}\lambda)
is open.
Similarly, replacing in the last line
H_{D}^{-1}(\succ\lambda_{D})
with

H_{D}^{-1}(\succeq\lambda_{D})
we see that
H^{-1}(\succeq_{lex}\lambda)

is also open.
It remains to note that
H^{-1}(\preceq_{lex}\lambda)
is closed because it
can be decomposed as:

\displaystyle H^{-1}(\preceq_{lex}\lambda)=

\displaystyle H_{0}^{-1}(\prec\lambda_{0})\bigcup H_{0}^{-1}(\preceq\lambda_{0})\cap H_{1}^{-1}(\prec\lambda_{1})\bigcup\cdots

\displaystyle\bigcup H_{0}^{-1}(\preceq\lambda_{0})\cap\cdots\cap H_{D-1}^{-1}(\preceq\lambda_{D-1})\cap H_{D}^{-1}(\prec\lambda_{D}).

∎

Corollary 3.10
.

The stratum
S_{G}^{M}
is constructive and, in the lexicographic partial order,
we have
\overline{S_{G}^{M}}=S_{G}^{\preceq M}
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.11
,
polynomial in
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is a specialization of the generic polynomial

f=u+T_{2}u^{2}+\cdots+T_{d}u^{d}
, where
d=\mathrm{md}_{G}
is the
maximal degree of
G
.
For this
f
, we define the algebra
R=\mathscr{C}^{[f]}_{G}
as the
quotient of

\mathbf{k}[X_{v},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}]
,
v\in V
, by
the relations from Corollary
3.2
. Note that the obvious relation

f^{-1}(f(u))=u
gives explicit polynomial formulas for the coefficients
of
f^{-1}
in terms of
T_{i}
.
Since
R
is a finitely generated module over

A=\mathbf{k}[T_{2},\ldots,T_{d}]
,
the theorem applies and we may use
the fact that

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
can be identified with the set
of
\mathbf{k}
-rational points of
\Spec A
.
∎

Next we introduce a natural
\mathbf{k}^{\star}
-action on
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
.

Lemma 3.11
.

For any
graph

G
, the natural
\mathbf{k}^{\star}
-action on
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
given by

f(u)\mapsto\frac{1}{\epsilon}f(\epsilon u)
with

\epsilon\in\mathbf{k}\setminus\{0\}
,
preserves the Hilbert stratification.
This action is free on
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}\setminus\{u\}
and
the point
u\in{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
belongs to
the closure of every Hilbert stratum.

Proof.

We substitute

Y_{v}=X_{v}/\epsilon

to get an isomorphism of algebras

\mathbf{k}[X_{v}\,:\,v\in V]/I_{G}^{f(u)}\cong\mathbf{k}[Y_{v}\,:\,v\in V]/(I_{G}^{f(\epsilon u)}).

The second claim follows
from the fact that if
f=u+\dots\neq u
,
then
f(u)\neq\frac{1}{\epsilon}f(\epsilon u)
for
\epsilon\neq 1
.
But, by Borel’s fixed point theorem, the closure of each stratum should contain
a fixed point.
∎

Denote by
P{\mathscr{A}}_{G}=\left({\mathscr{A}}_{G}\setminus\{u\}\right)/\mathbf{k}^{\star}
the weighted
projective space obtained as the latter quotient. Taking the quotient we obtain the induced Hilbert
stratification of
P{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
which we will be interested in. Consider the (finite)
poset of Hilbert strata. This poset has the minimal element corresponding to

\{u\}
and the maximal element corresponding to the stratum with a generic Hilbert sequence.

Corollary 3.12
.

The Hilbert sequence of the graded algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}
is minimal.
The maximal length of the Hilbert sequence of a generalized zonotopal algebras in

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is attained for
f(u)=u
and equals
|G|
which is the total number of
edges in
G
.

Example 3.13
.

For
G=K_{4}
,
P{\mathscr{A}}_{K_{4}}
is a weighted projective line (topologically
S^{2}
).
Its Hilbert stratification consists of two points corresponding to
f(u)=u+u^{2}

and
f(u)=u+u^{3}
and a complex
1
-dimensional stratum (coinciding with
S^{2}

minus two points) which is the factor of
u+au^{2}+cu^{3}
with
b\neq 0
and
c\neq 0
mod the above
\mathbf{k}^{\star}
-action, see subsection
4.4.2
below.

3.4.
Specialization

If
H
is a subgraph of
G
and
v
is a vertex of
H
, then
the embedding
H\hookrightarrow G
sends the generator
X_{v}^{G}
of
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}
to

X_{v}^{H}
. Therefore, we obtain a surjective homomorphism

\pi_{H}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}\to\mathscr{C}_{H}^{f}
.

We will now study the effect of this map on stratifications.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
M
be an
A
-module
such that
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
. Suppose that
0\neq N\subset M
is a submodule
that satisfies a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

for some
A
-module
N_{2}\neq 0,N
. Then
N=M_{1}\cap N\oplus M_{2}\cap N
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that the diagram implies that
\ker f=N\cap M_{1}\neq 0
.
Then the inclusion
N_{2}\subseteq M_{2}
given in the diagram implies that

N\subseteq N\cap M_{1}+M_{2}
as submodules of
M
. Since
N
is a submodule,
this forces the containment
N\subseteq N\cap M_{1}+N\cap M_{2}
and the claim
easily follows.
∎

As in the proof of Corollary
3.10
,
we can define
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(A)
for any ground ring
A
and
f\in A[u]
.
As above, we have a surjective homomorphism of
A
-algebras,

\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(A)\to\mathscr{C}_{H}^{f}(A)
.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
f\in A[u]
such that

f\in(u)\setminus(u^{2})
.
Let
H\subset G
be graphs.
Then the natural projection map splits and allows to identify

\mathscr{C}_{H}^{f}(A)
with a direct summand of
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(A)

in the category of filtered
A
-modules.

Proof.

It suffices to assume that
H
is obtained by removing a single edge.
We can similarly define the algebras
\Phi_{G}(A),\Phi_{H}(A)
as square-free
algebras on the sets of edges with coefficients in
A
.
It is clear from the relations that
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(A)\subset\Phi_{G}(A)
.

We note that the map

\pi_{H}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G}^{[f]}(A)\to\mathscr{C}_{H}^{[f]}(A),
sending
X_{i}^{G}\mapsto X_{i}^{H}
,
is induced by the natural projection map

\pi_{H}\colon\Phi_{G}(A)\to\Phi_{H}(A)
. By the definition of
\Phi_{G}

as a square-free algebra on the set of edges,
the map
\pi_{H}
splits, i.e.,
\Phi_{G}(A)=\Phi_{H}(A)\oplus e\Phi_{H}(A)
.
The functoriality of the definitions gives a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}_{G}^{[f]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}_{H}^{[f]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{H}}

Therefore, Lemma
3.14
asserts that

\mathscr{C}_{G}^{[f]}(A)=e\Phi_{H}(A)\cap\mathscr{C}_{G}^{[f]}(A)\oplus\Phi_{H}(A)\cap\mathscr{C}_{G}^{[f]}(A)
.
Using Lemma
3.14

again, we extend the splitting to the filtrations; note that
\pi_{H}
respects the filtration, i.e.,

\pi_{H}\left(\langle\prod f(X_{i})^{a_{i}}\mid\sum a_{i}\leq n\rangle\right)\subseteq\langle\prod(\pi_{H}f(X_{i}))^{a_{i}}\mid\sum a_{i}\leq n\rangle\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{H}^{[f]}(A).

∎

Theorem 3.16
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{k}
is algebraically closed. Then
the projection map
\pi_{H}
induces the surjective map
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}\to{\mathscr{A}}_{H}

which preserves the Hilbert stratifications, i.e., the image of a connected component
of a Hilbert stratum is still contained in one stratum.

Proof.

As in the proof of Corollary
3.10
we will parametrize
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}

as the fibers of
A:=\mathbf{k}[T_{2},\ldots,T_{v}]\to\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(A)
.

By Corollary
3.10
, it suffices to show that if

\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec A
and
s\notin\mathfrak{p}
are such that
V_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathfrak{p})\cap D(s)

is contained in a single Hilbert stratum of
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(A)
, then it is contained in
a single Hilbert stratum of
\mathscr{C}_{H}^{f}(A)
. Here,
V_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathfrak{p})
denotes the set of

\mathbf{k}
-rational points containing
\mathfrak{p}
and
D(s)
denotes the distinguished
open set of points not containing
s
.
By the construction we can pass to
B:=A_{s}/\mathfrak{p}A_{s}
and consider the
stratifications induced by
B
-algebras
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(B)
and its filtered direct
summand
\mathscr{C}_{H}^{f}(B)
, see Theorem
3.15
.

Because
\mathbf{k}
is algebraically closed, the set of
\mathbf{k}
-rational points is
dense, so the Hilbert stratification of
G
is constant on the entire
\Spec B
.
Thus, by Theorem
3.8
and Corollary
3.10
the
condition on the stratification can be restated as freeness of
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}(B)\otimes_{B}B_{\mathfrak{q}}
(as a filtered

B_{\mathfrak{q}}
-module) for every
\mathfrak{q}
-rational
point of
\Spec B_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
But a direct summand of a free module is projective and projective modules over
a local ring are free.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

Like other similar results, Theorem
3.16
shows that when
\mathbf{k}
is not
algebraically closed, it is better to consider the stratification of the entire
space
\Spec A
rather than only of the set of its
\mathbf{k}
-points.

Corollary 3.18
.

For any usual graph
G
on
n
vertices, the Hilbert stratification of
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is
a coarsening of the Hilbert stratification of
{\mathscr{A}}_{K_{n}}
, where
K_{n}
denotes a complete graph on
n
vertices.

:

\begin{cases}x_{i}^{4}=0&\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,4\\
(x_{i}+x_{j}+x_{i}^{2}+x_{j}^{2})^{5}=0&\text{ for }i\neq j\\
(x_{i}+x_{j}+x_{k}+x_{i}^{2}+x_{j}^{2}+x_{k}^{2})^{4}=0&\text{ for }i\neq j\neq k\\
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2})+c(x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}&+x_{4}^{3})=0.\end{cases}

It is immediate that
c=0
gives a quadratic relation.
It is also easy to see that no other value of
c
can give one.
Namely, since the ideal
I
generated by the first three groups of relations,
which are independent of
c
, does not contain any element of order less than
4
,
then for any
f\in I,
the polynomial

f+x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2})+c(x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+x_{4}^{3})

has a cubic term.

The case
f(u)=u+cu^{3}
is similar.
∎

5.
Outlook

Here we present a small sample of open problems about deformed zonotopal algebras
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}
for
future investigation.

Our experiments with Maculay2 show that for many graphs
G
and functions
f
, the
Hilbert sequence of the algebra
\mathscr{C}_{G}^{f}
is logarithmically concave.
In an earlier preprint version of our paper we conjectured that this
was true for all graphs
G
and all non-degenerate functions
f
.
However, in this generality the conjecture does not hold. A
counterexample is given by the complete graph
G=K_{5}
and
f=u+u^{4}
,
for which the Hilbert sequence is given by

\mathscr{H}_{G}^{f}=(1,5,14,30,53,73,60,41,9,4,1)
which is not
log-concave, since
9^{2}=81<41\cdot 4=164
.

However, recently a truly remarkable proof of this fact in the graded
case (i.e.,
f=u
) was found in
[
1
]
. This circumstance gives hope that the
log-concavity might hold for a larger class of graphs and functions.

Problem 5.1
.

Find families of graphs
G
and nondegenerate functions
f\neq u
for
which the Hilbert sequence
\mathscr{H}_{G}^{f}
is log-concave. In particular,
prove log-concavity for chain and cycle graphs and
f=u+u^{2}
.

Problem 5.2
.

When
G
is a tree, is it true that the Hilbert stratification of

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
consists of coordinate subspaces?
In particular, does
f=e^{u}
give a general Hilbert sequence?

Problem 5.3
.

Find a graph-theoretical interpretation of the
general Hilbert sequence
\mathscr{H}_{G}
?
Find a graph-theoretical interpretation of
\mathscr{H}^{f}_{G}

in some special cases, for example, for
f=e^{u}
.

Problem 5.4
.

Is it true that if
\mathscr{H}^{f}_{G_{1}}=\mathscr{H}^{f}_{G_{2}}
for every
function
f
, then
G_{1}
is isomorphic to
G_{2}
?

Problem 5.5
.

Determine
G
and
f
for which the
algebra
\mathscr{C}^{f}_{G}
is Gorenstein/quadratic/Koszul.

When
f=u
, i.e. in the graded case, the Hilbert sequences of
\mathscr{C}_{G}

satisfy the deletion-contraction relation (
2.8
) which
allows to compute them recursively. However, for
f\neq u
the
relation (
2.8
) does not hold.

Problem 5.6
.

Study the behavior of
\mathscr{H}^{f}_{G}
under standard graph operations on
graphs, for example under deletions and contractions of edges.

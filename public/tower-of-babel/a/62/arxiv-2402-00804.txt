Weakly subnormal subgroups and variations of the Baer-Suzuki theorem
Robert M. Guralnick, Hung P. Tong-Viet, Gareth Tracey

Abstract
. A subgroup R R of a finite group G G is weakly subnormal in G G if R R is not subnormal in G G but it is subnormal in every proper overgroup of R R in G G . In this paper, we first classify all finite groups G G which contains a weakly subnormal p p -subgroup for some prime p p . We then determine all finite groups containing a cyclic weakly subnormal p p -subgroup. As applications, we prove a number of variations of the Baer-Suzuki theorem using the orders of certain group elements.

Introduction

Let

Remark 1.1
.

From the previous theorems, one obtains the classification of maximal weakly subnormal
p
-subgroups. If
R
is a weakly subnormal

p
-subgroup and
M
is the unique maximal subgroup containing
R
, then
M
is the only maximal subgroup containing
O_{p}(M)

and
R\leq O_{p}(M)
. Thus,
O_{p}(M)
is the unique (up to conjugacy) maximal weakly subnormal
p
-subgroup of
G
. In all cases with

p\neq 2
,
O_{p}(M)
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
(and this is true in many but not all cases with
p=2
as well).

For applications, we need the classification of cyclic weakly subnormal
p
-subgroups. As usual, we assume that
O_{p}(G)=1
. If
G
is
p
-solvable, then the classification is given
in Theorem
1
(in that case Sylow
p
-subgroups are the only such examples), and so we assume this is not the case.

Suppose that
R
is a cyclic weakly subnormal
p
-subgroup of such a group
G
. Then
R
is contained in a unique maximal subgroup
M
and moreover,
R\leq O_{p}(M)
. If
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup
containing
R
, then
M
is the only maximal subgroup containing
P
. Conversely, if
R\leq O_{p}(M)
is cyclic and
M
is the unique maximal subgroup containing
R
(and
M
is not normal
in
G
), then
R
is weakly
subnormal. Thus, one just has to check the cases in Theorem
2
, consider elements
x
in
O_{p}(M)
, and check to see that
x
is contained
in no other maximal subgroups. Another approach is to use the results of
[
18
]
where there is a classification of cyclic subgroups contained in a unique maximal
subgroup and check to see if they are contained in
O_{p}(M)
.

Note that for
p
odd, in all but two cases (one each for
p=3
or
5
), the group is quasisimple; the Sylow
p
-subgroup is cyclic; and the unique maximal subgroup
is the normalizer of the Sylow
p
-subgroup. Moreover, if
G
happens to be a quasisimple finite group of Lie type with
p
odd, the elements are either regular semisimple
or unipotent. The only cases where
R
is unipotent is if
G=\mathrm{L}_{2}(p)
or
SL_{2}(p)
with
p\geq 5
. If
R
consists of semisimple elements, then it cannot be contained
in a proper parabolic subgroup since then it would be conjugate to a subgroup of a Levi subgroup and so would be contained in at least two parabolic subgroups. In
particular,
R
is generated by a regular semisimple element.

The main motivation for the study of weakly subnormal subgroups is to prove various variations of the Baer-Suzuki theorem. The Baer-Suzuki theorem states that if
p
is a prime,
x
is a
p
-element in a finite group
G
, and
\langle x,x^{g}\rangle
is a
p
-group for all
g\in G
, then
x\in O_{p}(G)
. Many variations of this theorem have been proved over the years (see
[
11
,
12
,
16
,
30
]
). In
[
16
]
, Guralnick and Robinson showed that if
G
is a finite group and
x\in G
is an element of order
p
such that
[x,g]
is a
p
-element for every
g\in G,
then
x\in O_{p}(G)
. Since
[x,g]=x^{-1}x^{g}\in\langle x,x^{g}\rangle
, this result (whose proof depends on the classification of finite simple groups) implies the Baer-Suzuki theorem. In fact, Guralnick and Malle
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
prove a stronger result which says that if
x\in G
is a
p
-element and
CC^{-1}
consists of only
p
-elements, where
C=x^{G}
, then
C\subseteq O_{p}(G)
. They also conjecture that if
p\neq 5
is a prime and
C
is a conjugacy class of
p
-elements in a finite group
G
with
[c,d]
a
p
-element for all
c,d\in C
, then
C\subseteq O_{p}(G)
(see
[
11
, Conjecture 1.3]
).

In our first result, we prove the following variation of the Baer-Suzuki theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
G

is a

-element for every

-element

Recall that if

is an element of a finite group

is a prime, then

is called a

-element (or a

-regular element) if its order is coprime to

to be a normal subgroup of

, the largest normal

-subgroup of

Z^{*}_{p}(G)/O_{p^{\prime}}(G)=Z(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))

In the opposite direction to Theorem

-theorem (

Theorem 6

Let

In this section, we determine the structure of finite groups with a weakly subnormal
p
-subgroup for some prime
p
. In particular, we will prove Theorems

,
2
and

. Recall that a subgroup

of

is weakly subnormal in

if

is not subnormal in

but

is subnormal in all of its proper overgroups
H
in
G
. Let
\mathcal{M}(R)
be the set of maximal subgroups of

containing

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let

be a prime divisor of
|G|.
Let

be a weakly subnormal
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then the following hold.

(i)

is the normal closure of
R
;

(ii)

\mathcal{M}(R)=\{M\}
and
R\leq O_{p}(M)
;

(iii)

is subnormal in
R\Phi(G)
and

;

(iv)

O_{p^{\prime}}(G)\leq Z(G)\cap\Phi(G)
; or

where

is a
q
-group for some prime

and

acts irreducibly
on

and centralizes

(v)

Proof.

Let

be a normal subgroup of

containing

then
R\leq O_{p}(N)\leq O_{p}(G)
and so

is subnormal in

, which is a contradiction. So (i) holds.
Part (ii) follows from Wielandt’s Zipper Lemma
[

. For part (iii), let

be the unique maximal subgroup of

containing

Since
\Phi(G)\leq M

is subnormal in

. Moreover, since

. Thus part (iii) holds.

If

normalizes a

-subgroup

) and so

By the theory of coprime group actions, there is a prime

and an

-invariant Sylow

-subgroup

of

satisfying the same condition and so we may assume that

is a

-group
and

is not central in

and

.

Suppose that

is central but not contained in the Frattini subgroup of

. Then

for

of

. Since

is central in

is normal in

Since

-subgroup of

Finally, since

is subnormal in the

and so (v) holds.
∎

Remark 2.3

Let

-subgroup of a finite group

(a)

Let

Let a finite

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p
be a prime. Suppose that

is a finite group with an abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup
P

and
G=\langle P^{g}\text{ : }g\in G\rangle
. Then

is central. If

is central, then

G=O_{p}(G)\times E(G)

Z(E(G))=O_{p^{\prime}}(G)

Proof.

Since
O_{p}(G)=\bigcap_{g\in G}P^{g}
and
G=\langle P^{g}\text{ : }g\in G\rangle
, it is clear that
O_{p}(G)\leq Z(G)
. For the remainder of the proof, suppose
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)\leq Z(G)
. Then
F(G)=Z(G)
.
We claim that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)\leq\Phi(G)
. If not, then by Gaschütz theorem,

where

and

. Since

contains a Sylow
p
-subgroup of

,

as required.

First assume that
p
is odd. Then by
[
8
, Corollary 1.2]
,
P=Z(P)\leq F^{*}(G)
(in that result, it is assumed that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
but
it is clear that all that is required is that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is central). Thus
P=Z(P)\leq F^{*}(G)
and so

G=F^{*}(G)=O_{p}(G)E(G)
(since
O_{p^{\prime}}(F(G))\leq\Phi(G))
. By inspection of
the covering groups
of the simple groups with abelian Sylow p-subgroup (see
[
9
, Section 6.1]
and
[
26
]
),

is a

p^{\prime}
-group.

Now assume that
p=2
. The only simple groups

with abelian Sylow
2
-subgroups are
{\rm J}_{1}
,
{}^{2}{\rm G}_{2}(3^{a})
with
a
odd,
\mathrm{L}_{2}(q)
with

or
\mathrm{L}_{2}(q)
with

[
29
]
. One then observes that if

is any quasisimple group with

and

even, the Sylow

-subgroups of

are nonabelian. It follows that

has odd order. Thus, all that remains is to prove that

. Since

is Frattini, it suffices to prove that

. If not, then since
G/F^{*}(G)
is generated by Sylow
2
-subgroups, there exists an element

of

of
2
-power order. Since

,

contains its centralizer, and

has abelian Sylow
2
-subgroups, such an element normalizes each component

of

, and induces a non-trivial outer automorphism of

. One can check from the list of possibilities for

above that a Sylow

is nonabelian for any outer automorphism

of

∎

4.
Applications to Baer-Suzuki type problems

We apply the reduction results in the previous sections to solve several Baer-Suzuki type problems. Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime. Let
P
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and let

We first consider the following property for the pair
(G,x)

(\mathcal{P}_{1}):[x,g]\text{ is a $p$-element for every $p^{\prime}$-element $g\in G$ of prime power order}.

Clearly
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is a Baer-Suzuki property as if
H
is any overgroup of
\langle x\rangle
in

also satisfies property

The following easy lemma will show that the pair

satisfies property

if and only if
(G/O_{p}(G),xO_{p}(G))

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Let

and

Then

(i)

If
Ng\in G/N
is an
r
-element for some prime

Ng=Ny
for some
r
-element

(ii)

If

centralizes every element of prime power order of

g
centralizes

Proof.

Let
Ng\in G/N

be a nontrivial
r
-element for some odd prime
r
. Let
r^{a}=o(Ng)
for some integer

Assume that
o(g)=r^{b}m
for some integers

with
r\nmid m.
Then
b\geq a
and so

since

. As
\gcd(r^{b},m)=1
, there exist integers

such that

. Let

. Then
y\in G
is an
r
-element and

. This proves (i). For (ii), observe that every element of

can be written as a product of elements of prime power order. The proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

To justify the claim above, let
g\in G
and let

. Assume that

is
p^{\prime}
-element of prime power order. By Lemma
4.1
(i),

for some

for some

-element of prime power order. Thus

is a

-element. Consequently, the pair

\mathcal{P}_{1}.
The converse is clear.

We now prove Theorem

[

Let the pair

Theorem 6.2

p^{\prime}
-element
in

and

. First assume that

is a

-element not conjugate to an element of

containing

If

is

-solvable, then this is clear. Indeed, writing

and any element of

So assume that

is not
p
-solvable and so by the (proof of the) previous lemma, one

(2)

(3)

The first case is an easy computation in MAGMA. For alternating groups, we

is

In the case of

In the fourth case since

Suppose finally that case (ii) from Lemma

is regular semisimple and by a slight extension of the result of Gow, we can choose

g
with

any noncentral semisimple element in the derived subgroup. Again, we

p

So we have shown in all cases, that we can choose

-element and

. Moreover,

and

invariably generate

(all we require is that they invariably generate

. We claim that

Assume now that

Let

Corollary 6.3

be a finite group and let

be a prime. In

has a cyclic Sylow

or

-singular for every

The following is a structure result for groups with a weakly subnormal subgroup of prime order.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group with a weakly subnormal subgroup

of prime order

. Then either

(i)

and

with

an odd prime; or

, and

.

(c)

G/O_{p}(G)

If
p=2
then it is clear that

with
q
an odd prime, so we will assume for the remainder of the proof that
p
is odd.

If

is non-central then the result follows from Theorem
1
. So assume that

. Then

implies that

for

an odd prime. If

, then we verify directly that
\Gamma_{k}(x)=\Gamma_{k+1}(x)
consists of
27
elements of orders

. One can then check that there exists elements

such that

is not a
p3
-element. Thus, we have
e>3
. We then observe that a Sylow
3

has order

[y,x]=z
with

3

.
Hence,

Proposition 6.6

Let

7.
Nonlinear multiplicative irreducible characters

In this final section, we present an application of Theorem
7
to the character theory of finite groups.
Let
G
be a finite group and let
\chi
be an irreducible complex character of
G
. Motivated by the concept of multiplicative functions in analytic number theory, Guralnick and Moretó
[
15
]
call
\chi
a multiplicative character if
\chi(xy)=\chi(x)\chi(y)
for every nontrivial elements
x,y\in G
with
(o(x),o(y))=1
. Clearly, every linear character of
G
is multiplicative. To obtain further examples of multiplicative characters, we need the following notation and concepts from character theory.

We write
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
for the set of all complex irreducible characters of
G
and let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. We say that
\chi
vanishes at
g\in G
if
\chi(g)=0
. If
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, we say that
\chi
vanishes off
N
if
\chi(g)=0
for every element
g\in G-N.
If
g\in G
, then we can write
g=g_{p}g_{p^{\prime}}=g_{p^{\prime}}g_{p}
, where
g_{p}
is a
p
-element, and
g_{p^{\prime}}
is a
p^{\prime}
-element of
G
.
Note that if
\chi
vanishes off a normal
p
-subgroup of
G
, then
\chi
is multiplicative.

Below are some examples of groups with a nonlinear multiplicative character.

Example 7.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime.

(i)

Recall that a finite group
G
with
|G|>2
is called a Gagola group if
G
has an irreducible character
\chi

that vanishes on all but two conjugacy classes of
G
. The character
\chi
above is called a Gagola character. In
[
5
]
, Gagola shows that every Gagola group with a Gagola character
\chi
has a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
which is an elementary abelian
p
-group for some prime
p
and that
\chi
vanishes off
N
. Thus,
\chi
is multiplicative.

(ii)

If
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel a
p
-group for some prime
p
, then any nonlinear faithful irreducible character of
G
is multiplicative.

(iii)

Trivially, every nonlinear irreducible character of a finite
p
-group is multiplicative.

(iv)

Let
K
be a proper nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
. The pair
(G,K)
is called a Camina pair if for every element
g\in G-K
, then
g
is conjugate to every element in the coset
gK
. Equivalently,
G
is a Camina pair if and only if every irreducible character
\chi
of
G
that does not contain
K
in its kernel vanishes off
K
(see
[
21
, Lemma 4.1]
). A result of Camina, (see
[
21
, Theorem 4.4]
) states that if
(G,K)
is a Camina pair, then either
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
K
or one of
G/K
or
K
is a
p
-group for some prime
p.
Thus if
(G,K)
is a Camina group and
K
is a
p
-group for some prime
p
, then every nonlinear irreducible character of
G
that does not contain
K
in its kernel is multiplicative.

An irreducible character
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is said to have
p
-defect zero (or
\chi
lies in a block of
p
-defect
0
) if
\chi(1)_{p}=|G|_{p},
where
n_{p}
denotes the
p
-part of the integer
n\geq 1
. The following result due to Knörr characterizes
p
-defect zero irreducible characters.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
be a prime and
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\chi
has
p
-defect zero.

(ii)

\chi
vanishes on every element of order
p
of
G
.

(iii)

\chi
vanishes on all
p
-singular element of
G
.

Proof.

This is part of Corollary 2.1 in
[
20
]

∎

We also need to the following result which is a special case of Lemma 2.2 in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
a,b\in G
. Let
A=a^{G}
and
B=b^{G}.
If
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is constant on
AB
, then
\chi(a)\chi(b)=\chi(ab)\chi(1)
.

We first prove the following.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Suppose that
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is a nonlinear multiplicative character. Then

(i)

If
a,b\in G
are nontrivial and
(o(a),o(b))=1
, then
\chi(a)=0
or
\chi(b)=0
. In particular,
\chi(ab)=0
.

(ii)

There exists a prime
p
and an element
w\in G
of order
p
such that
\chi(w)\neq 0.

(iii)

Let
p
be a prime such that
\chi(w)\neq 0
for some
w\in g
of order
p
. Then:

(a)

\chi(g)=0
if
g\in G
is not a
p
-element.

(b)

\chi
vanishes off
O_{p}(G)
.

(c)

|G|/\chi(1)
is a power of
p
,
\chi=\lambda^{G}
for some
\lambda\in\mathrm{Irr}(P)
, and
F^{*}(G)=O_{p}(G)
, where
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
and
F^{*}(G)
is the generalized Fitting subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

Recall that
\chi(xy)=\chi(x)\chi(y)
for all
1\neq x,y\in G
with
(o(x),o(y))=1.

(i) Let
a,b\in G
be nontrivial with
(o(a),o(b))=1
. Let
A=a^{G}
and
B=b^{G}.
Then for any
c\in AB
,
c=a^{u}b^{v}
for some
u,v\in G.
As
o(a^{u})=o(a)
and
o(b^{v})=o(b)
and
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is a class function on
G
, we see that

\chi(c)=\chi(a^{u}b^{v})=\chi(a^{u})\chi(b^{v})=\chi(a)\chi(b).

Thus
\chi
is constant on
AB
and so by Lemma
7.3
,
\chi(ab)\chi(1)=\chi(a)\chi(b)
which implies that
\chi(ab)\chi(1)=\chi(ab)
. As
\chi(1)>1
(
\chi
is nonlinear),
\chi(ab)=0
, hence
\chi(a)\chi(b)=\chi(ab)=0
and (1) follows.

(ii) Assume by contradiction that
\chi
vanishes on every element of prime order in
G
. Then by Lemma
7.2
,
\chi
has
r
-defect zero, that is,
\chi(1)_{r}=|G|_{r}
, for every prime divisor

of
|G|
, the order of
G
. However, this would imply that
\chi(1)=|G|
, which is impossible as
\chi(1)^{2}<|G|
. Therefore,
\chi
does not vanish on some element, say

p
, for some prime

(iii)(a) Suppose that
1\neq g\in G
is not a
p
-element. Then
g
is either a
p^{\prime}
-element or
g
is
p
-singular but not a
p
-element. Assume first that
g
is a
p^{\prime}
-element. Then by part (i), we have
\chi(wg)=\chi(w)\chi(g)=0
. As
\chi(w)\neq 0
,
\chi(g)=0.
Next, assume that
g
is
p
-singular but not a
p
-element. Then
g=uv
, where both

are nontrivial elements of
G
with
(o(u),o(v))=1
. Part (i) now implies that
\chi(g)=0.
Thus
\chi(g)=0
if
g\in G
is not a
p
-element.

(iii)(b) Let
1\neq x\in G
with
\chi(x)\neq 0
. It follows from part (iii)(a) that
x
is a nontrivial
p
-element. Let
y\in G
be a nontrivial
p
-element of
G
. If
xy=s
is a nontrivial
p^{\prime}
-element, then

with both
s,y^{-1}
nontrivial and
(o(s),o(y^{-1}))=1
so that by part (i), we have
\chi(x)=\chi(sy^{-1})=0
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
xy
is
1
or
p
-singular for every

-element

Now by Theorem

and the results follows.

(iii)(c) For each prime divisor

|G|

\chi
vanishes on every element of order

\chi(1)_{r}=|G|_{r}
and thus
\chi(1)_{p^{\prime}}=|G|_{p^{\prime}}
or equivalently

is a power of

The remaining claims follow from Lemma 1 and Theorem B in
[

]
.

We now prove the main result of this section, answering a question raised in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Let
\chi
be a nonlinear irreducible character of
G
. Then
\chi
is multiplicative if and only if there is a prime
p
such that
\chi
vanishes off
O_{p}(G)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is nonlinear multiplicative. By Theorem
7.4
(iii)(b),
\chi
vanishes off
O_{p}(G)
.
Conversely, assume that
\chi
vanishes off
O_{p}(G)
. We claim that
\chi
is multiplicative. Let
x,y\in G
be nontrivial elements with
(o(x),o(y))=1.
Let
z=xy.
Since
x
and
y
have coprime orders, we may assume that
p\nmid o(x)
. It follows that
x\not\in O_{p}(G)
and thus
\chi(x)=0
. Now, if
\chi(z)=0
, then
\chi(xy)=\chi(z)=0=\chi(x)\chi(y)
. Thus we may assume that
\chi(z)\neq 0
, hence
z\in O_{p}(G).
In the quotient group
\overline{G}=G/O_{p}(G)
, we see that
\overline{x}\overline{y}=\overline{1}
and hence
\overline{y}=\overline{x}^{-1}
. So
o(\overline{y})=o(\overline{x})>1
, which is impossible.
∎

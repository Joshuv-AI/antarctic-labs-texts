A note on the slicing of fibrations

Abstract
We describe the construction of the slice fibration of a given one.

2.2
Pointed categories

Suitably, taking slices of categories amounts to a universal construction. A
pointed category
is a pair
(A,\mathbbm{C})
where
\mathbbm{C}
is a non-empty category and
A
is an object of
\mathbbm{C}
, that is a functor
A:\mathbbm{1}\rightarrow\mathbbm{C}
. A category
\mathbbm{C}
equipped with a (chosen) terminal object
1
identifies a pointed category
(1,\mathbbm{C})
, which we call
terminally pointed
to be more specific. A
morphism of pointed categories
, say from
(A,\mathbbm{C})
to
(B,\mathbbm{D})
is a pair
(F,\alpha)
with
F:\mathbbm{C}\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}
a functor and
\alpha:FA\rightarrow B
a morphism of
\mathbbm{D}
, that is a natural transformation
\alpha:F\circ A\Rightarrow B
.

Remark 2
.

A morphism of pointed categories whose codomain is a terminally pointed category, say
(F,\alpha):(A,\mathbbm{C})\rightarrow(1,\mathbbm{D})
amounts to a functor
F:\mathbbm{C}\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}
together with the unique morphism
FA:FA\rightarrow 1
in
\mathbbm{D}
, which essentially is the object
FA
of
\mathbbm{D}
.

A pair of composable morphisms of pointed categories compose in accordance with the following law of composition:

\textstyle{(A,\mathbbm{D})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(GF,\beta\circ(G\alpha))}
\scriptstyle{(F,\alpha)}
\textstyle{(B,\mathbbm{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(G,\beta)}
\textstyle{(C,\mathbbm{B})}

Moreover, for every pointed category
(A,\mathbbm{D})

(1_{\mathbbm{D}},1_{A}):(A,\mathbbm{D})\rightarrow(A,\mathbbm{D})

3

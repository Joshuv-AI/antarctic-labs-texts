Critical exponent of binary words with few distinct palindromes
L​’ubomíra Dvořáková, Pascal Ochem, Daniela Opočenská

Abstract
We study infinite binary words that contain few distinct palindromes. In particular, we classify such words according to their critical exponents. This extends results by Fici and Zamboni [TCS 2013]. Interestingly, the words with 18 and 20 palindromes happen to be morphic images of the fixed point of the morphism 0 ↦ 01 \texttt{0}\mapsto\texttt{01} , 1 ↦ 21 \texttt{1}\mapsto\texttt{21} , 2 ↦ 0 \texttt{2}\mapsto\texttt{0} .

Preliminaries

An
alphabet

\mathcal{A}
is a finite set and its elements are called
letters
.
A
word

u
over
\mathcal{A}
of
length

n
is a finite string
u=u_{0}u_{1}\cdots u_{n-1}
, where
u_{j}\in\mathcal{A}
for all
j\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
.
If
{\mathcal{A}}=\{\tt 0,\tt 1,\dots,\tt d-1\}
, the length of
u
is denoted
|u|
and
|u|_{\tt i}
denotes the number of occurrences of the letter
{\tt i}\in\mathcal{A}
in the word
u
. The
Parikh vector

\vec{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
is the vector defined as
{\vec{u}}=(|u|_{\tt 0},|u|_{\tt 1},\dots,|u|_{\tt d-1})^{T}
.
The set of all finite words over
\mathcal{A}
is denoted
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
. The set
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
equipped with concatenation as the operation forms a monoid with the
empty word

\varepsilon
as the neutral element.
We will also consider the set
{\mathcal{A}}^{\omega}
of infinite words (that is, right-infinite words) and the set
{}^{\omega}{\mathcal{A}}^{\omega}
of bi-infinite words.
A word
v
is an
e
-
power
of a word
u
if
v
is a prefix of the infinite periodic word
uuu\cdots=u^{\omega}
and
e=|v|/|u|
.
We write
v=u^{e}
. We also call
u^{e}
a repetition with period
u
and exponent
e
.
For instance, the Czech word
kapka
(drop) can be written in this formalism as
(kap)^{5/3}
.
A word is
\alpha^{+}
-free
(resp.
\alpha
-free
) if it
contains no repetition with exponent
\beta
such that
\beta>\alpha
(resp.
\beta\geqslant\alpha
).

The
critical exponent

E({\bf u})
of an infinite word
{\bf u}
is defined as

E({\bf u})=\sup\{e\in\mathbb{Q}:\ u^{e}\ \text{is a factor of }{\bf u}\ \text{for a non-empty word}\ u\}\,.

The
asymptotic critical exponent

E^{*}(\bf u)
of an infinite word
{\bf u}
is defined as
+\infty
if
E({\bf u})=+\infty
, and

E^{*}({\bf u})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\{e\in\mathbb{Q}:\ u^{e}\ \text{is a factor of }{\bf u}\ \text{for some }u\ \text{of length}\ n\}\,,

otherwise.
If each factor of
{\bf u}
has infinitely many occurrences in
{\bf u}
, then
{\bf u}
is
recurrent
.
Moreover, if for each factor the distances between its consecutive occurrences are bounded, then
{\bf u}
is
uniformly recurrent
. The
language

\mathcal{L}(\bf u)
is the set of factors occurring in
\bf u
.
The language
\mathcal{L}(\bf u)
is
closed under reversal
if for each factor
w=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}
, its
reverse

w^{R}=w_{n-1}\cdots w_{1}w_{0}
is also a factor of
\bf u
.
A word
w
is a
palindrome
if
w=w^{R}
. Let us denote
\overline{\tt 0}=\tt 1
and
\overline{\tt 1}=\tt 0
, then for any binary word
w
its
bit complement
is
\overline{w}=\overline{w_{0}}\ \overline{w_{1}}\cdots\overline{w_{n-1}}
.

Consider a factor
w
of a recurrent infinite word
{\bf u}=u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}\cdots
. Let
j<\ell
be two consecutive occurrences of
w
in
\bf u
. Then the word
u_{j}u_{j+1}\cdots u_{\ell-1}
is a
return word
to
w
in
\bf u
.

The
(factor) complexity
of an infinite word
{\bf u}
is the mapping
{\mathcal{C}}_{\bf u}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
defined by

{\mathcal{C}}_{\bf u}(n)=\#\{w\in\mathcal{L}({\bf u}):|w|=n\}
.

Given a word
w\in\mathcal{L}({\bf u})
, we define the sets of left extensions, right extensions and bi-extensions of
w
in
{\bf u}
over an alphabet
\mathcal{A}
respectively as

L_{{\bf u}}(w)=\{{\tt i}\in{\mathcal{A}}:{\tt i}w\in\mathcal{L}({\bf u})\},\qquad R_{{\bf u}}(w)=\{{\tt j}\in{\mathcal{A}}:w{\tt j}\in\mathcal{L}({\bf u})\}

and

B_{{\bf u}}(w)=\{({\tt i},{\tt j})\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}:{\tt i}w{\tt j}\in\mathcal{L}({\bf u})\}.

If
\#L_{{\bf u}}(w)>1
, then
w
is called
left special (LS)
. If
\#R_{{\bf u}}(w)>1
, then
w
is called
right special (RS)
. If
w
is both LS and RS, then it is called
bispecial (BS)
.
We define
b(w)=\#B_{{\bf u}}(w)-\#L_{{\bf u}}(w)-\#R_{{\bf u}}(w)+1
and we distinguish
ordinary BS factors
with
b(w)=0
,
weak BS factors
with
b(w)<0
and
strong BS factors
with
b(w)>0
.

A
morphism
is a map
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{*}
such that
\psi(uv)=\psi(u)\psi(v)
for all words
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
.
The morphism
\psi
is
non-erasing
if
\psi(\tt i)\not=\varepsilon
for each
{\tt i}\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
Morphisms can be naturally extended to infinite words by setting

\psi(u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}\cdots)=\psi(u_{0})\psi(u_{1})\psi(u_{2})\cdots\,
.
A
fixed point
of a morphism
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
is an infinite word
\bf u
such that
\psi(\bf u)=\bf u
.
We associate to a morphism
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
the
(incidence) matrix

M_{\psi}
defined for each
k,j\in\{0,1,\dots,d-1\}
as
[M_{\psi}]_{kj}=|\psi(\tt j)|_{\tt k}
.

If there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

has positive entries, then
\psi
is a
primitive
morphism.
By definition, we have for each
u\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
the following relation for the Parikh vectors
\vec{\psi(u)}=M_{\psi}\vec{u}
.

Let
{\bf u}
be an infinite word over an alphabet
\mathcal{A}
. Then the
uniform frequency

f_{\tt i}
of the letter
{\tt i}\in\mathcal{A}
is equal to
\alpha
if for any sequence
(w_{n})
of factors of
{\bf u}
with increasing lengths

\alpha=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|w_{n}|_{\tt i}}{|w_{n}|}\,.

It is known that fixed points of primitive morphisms have uniform letter frequencies
[
9
]
.

Let
{\bf u}
be an infinite word over an alphabet

and let

Consider a factor

of

.

Given a factorial language

Theorem 8

(

)

(1)

Theorem 9
.

Let
{\bf u}
be an infinite word over an alphabet
\mathcal{A}
such that the uniform letter frequencies in
{\bf u}
exist. Let

be such that every factor

\psi({\bf u})

E^{*}({{\bf u}})=E^{*}(\psi({{\bf u}}))
.

The inequality

is proven in
[
5
]
for any non-erasing morphism under the assumption of existence of uniform letter frequencies in
{\bf u}
.
Let us prove the opposite inequality. According to the definition of

, there exist sequences
\bigl(w_{n}\bigr)
and
\bigl(v_{n}\bigr)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}|v_{n}|=\infty

2.

3.

is a prefix of the periodic word

for each

;

4.

E^{*}(\psi({{\bf u}}))=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{|w_{n}|}{|v_{n}|}
.

If
E^{*}(\psi({{\bf u}}))=1
, then, clearly,
E^{*}(\psi({{\bf u}}))\leq E^{*}({{\bf u}})
.
Assume in the sequel that
E^{*}(\psi({{\bf u}}))>1
, then we have for large enough
n
that
|w_{n}|>|v_{n}|
and moreover, by the first item,
|v_{n}|\geq L
. By assumption, both
v_{n}
and
w_{n}
have synchronization points and since
v_{n}
is a prefix of
w_{n}
for large enough
n
, we may write

w_{n}=x_{n}\bullet\psi(w^{\prime}_{n})\bullet y_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{n}=x_{n}\bullet\psi(v^{\prime}_{n})\bullet z_{n}\,,

where we highlighted the first and the last synchronization point (not necessarily distinct) in

and
v_{n}
and where

and

are uniquely given factors of
{\bf u}
and the lengths of

,

are smaller than

.

By the third item, we have

where

is a proper prefix of

and

.

There are two possible cases for

.

(a)

is bounded, but as

, it follows that

for large enough

.

(b)

Or there is a subsequence
(u_{j_{n}})
of
(u_{n})
such that for all

we have

. Then by assumption,

has a synchronization point and we may write

and

by injectivity of
\psi
.

(a)

In the first case, since

starts with

(b)

In the second case,

4.1.3
The asymptotic critical exponent of
\bf p

Let us determine the asymptotic critical exponent of
\bf p
using Theorem
8
.
We use the form of BS factors and their shortest return words determined above.
We get
E^{*}({\bf p})=1+\max\{A^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime},D^{\prime}\}
, where

\displaystyle A^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|w_{A}^{(n)}|}{|{r}_{A}^{(n)}|}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|{\tt 1}\varphi({\tt 012})\varphi^{3}({\tt 012})\dots\varphi^{2n-1}({\tt 012}))|}{|\varphi^{2n-1}({\tt 012})|}\,;

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|w_{B}^{(n)}|}{|{r}_{B}^{(n)}|}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|{\tt 012}\varphi^{2}({\tt 012})\varphi^{4}({\tt 012})\dots\varphi^{2n}({\tt 012}))|}{|\varphi^{2n}({\tt 012})|}\,;

\displaystyle C^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|w_{C}^{(n)}|}{|{r}_{C}^{(n)}|}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|{\tt 01}\varphi^{2}({\tt 01})\varphi^{4}({\tt 01})\dots\varphi^{2n}({\tt 01})|}{|\varphi^{2n}({\tt 01})|}\,;

\displaystyle D^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|w_{D}^{(n)}|}{|{r}_{D}^{(n)}|}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\varphi({\tt 01})\varphi^{3}({\tt 01})\dots\varphi^{2n+1}({\tt 01})|}{|\varphi^{2n+1}({\tt 01})|}\,.

By the Hamilton-Cayley theorem, we have
M_{\varphi}^{3}-2M_{\varphi}^{2}+M_{\varphi}-I=0
. Consequently, for each
w\in\{{\tt 0,1,2}\}^{*}
, if we denote
\ell_{n}:=|\varphi^{n}(w)|=(1,1,1)M_{\varphi}^{n}\vec{w}
, then
\ell_{n}
satisfies the recurrence relation
\ell_{n+3}-2\ell_{n+2}+\ell_{n+1}-\ell_{n}=0
.
Denote
\beta
the largest root of the characteristic polynomial
t^{3}-2t^{2}+t-1
;
\beta\doteq 1.75488
.
By the Perron-Frobenius theorem,
\beta
is strictly larger than the modulus of the other roots of the characteristic polynomial. We thus obtain:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}A^{\prime}&=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\cfrac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta^{2k-1}}{\beta^{2n-1}}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\beta^{2}-1};\\
&&\\
B^{\prime}=C^{\prime}&=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\cfrac{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\beta^{2k}}{\beta^{2n}}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\beta^{2}-1};\\
&&\\
D^{\prime}&=&\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\cfrac{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\beta^{2k+1}}{\beta^{2n+1}}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\beta^{2}-1}.\\
\end{array}

Consequently,
E^{*}({\bf p})=1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{\beta^{2}-1}\doteq 2.48
.

Denote
e_{n}:=|\mu(\varphi^{n}(012))|
, then it satisfies the recurrence relation
e_{n+1}=2e_{n}-e_{n-1}+e_{n-2}

with initial conditions

e_{0}=11,e_{1}=21
and
e_{2}=36
.

The explicit solution reads

e_{n}=A_{3}\beta^{n}+B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{n}+C_{3}\lambda_{2}^{n},

where

\beta\doteq 1.75488,\quad\lambda_{1}\doteq 0.12256+0.74486i,\quad\lambda_{2}=\overline{\lambda_{1}}

are the roots of the polynomial
t^{3}-2t^{2}+t-1
, and

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{11|\lambda_{1}|^{2}-42\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_{1})+36}{|\beta-\lambda_{1}|^{2}}\doteq 11.530751580\,;

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{11\beta\lambda_{2}-21(\beta+\lambda_{2})+36}{(\beta-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})}\doteq-0.265375790-0.557144391i\,;

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=\overline{B_{3}}\,.

Let us show that
A\leq\frac{17}{11}
. We have to show for all
n\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\frac{6+A_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta^{2k-1}+B_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{1}^{2k-1}+C_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{2}^{2k-1}}{A_{3}\beta^{2n-1}+B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}+C_{3}\lambda_{2}^{2n-1}}\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad\frac{17}{11},

\displaystyle 66+11A_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta^{2k-1}+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{1}^{2k-1}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 17A_{3}\beta^{2n-1}+34\operatorname{Re}(B_{2}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}),

\displaystyle 66+11A_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\beta^{2k-1}+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{1}^{2k-1}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 6A_{3}\beta^{2n-1}+12\operatorname{Re}(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}),

\displaystyle 66+11A_{3}\left(\frac{\beta^{2n-1}}{\beta^{2}-1}-\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}\right)+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\lambda_{1}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}^{2n-2}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 6A_{3}\beta^{2n-1}+12\operatorname{Re}(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}).

Since

\displaystyle\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}

\displaystyle\leq 6,

we need to prove the inequality in the form

\displaystyle 66+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\lambda_{1}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}^{2n-2}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 11A_{3}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}+12\operatorname{Re}(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}).

For the left side, we can write for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle 66+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\lambda_{1}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}^{2n-2}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq\quad 66+22|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|\frac{|\lambda_{1}|^{2n-2}+1}{|\lambda_{1}^{2}-1|}

\displaystyle\leq\quad 66+22|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|\frac{2}{|\lambda_{1}^{2}-1|}.

For the right side, we can write for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\quad 11A_{3}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}+12\operatorname{Re}(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1})\quad

\displaystyle\geq\quad 11A_{3}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}-12|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|^{2n-1}

\displaystyle\geq\quad 11A_{3}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}-12|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|.

Since the inequality

66+22|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|\frac{2}{|\lambda_{1}^{2}-1|}\quad\leq\quad 11A_{3}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}-12|B_{1}||\lambda_{1}|

holds true for the given values, we obtain
A\leq\frac{17}{11}
.

Next, we will show that
B\leq\frac{17}{11}
.

Since for
n=0
we have
\frac{|v_{B}^{(0)}|}{|\hat{r}_{B}^{(0)}|}=\frac{6+11}{11}=\frac{17}{11}
, it remains to show that
for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\frac{6+A_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\beta^{2k}+B_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda_{1}^{2k}+C_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda_{2}^{2k}}{A_{3}\beta^{2n}+B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n}+C_{3}\lambda_{2}^{2n}}\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad\frac{17}{11},

\displaystyle 66+11A_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta^{2k}+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{1}^{2k}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 6A_{3}\beta^{2n}+12\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n}\right),

\displaystyle 66+11A_{3}\frac{\beta^{2n}-1}{\beta^{2}-1}+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2n}-1}{\lambda_{1}^{2}-1}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 6A_{3}\beta^{2n}+12\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n}\right).

Now, we need to be more careful with the approximations, we will therefore prove the inequality in the form

\displaystyle 66+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2n}-1}{\lambda_{1}^{2}-1}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 11A_{3}\frac{1}{\beta^{2}-1}+A_{3}\beta^{2n}\left(6-\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}\right)+12\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n}\right).

For the left side, we can write for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle 66+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2n}-1}{\lambda_{1}^{2}-1}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq\quad 66+22|B_{3}|\frac{|\lambda_{1}|^{2n}+1}{|\lambda_{1}^{2}-1|}

\displaystyle\leq\quad 66+22|B_{3}|\frac{1+|\lambda_{1}|^{2}}{|\lambda_{1}^{2}-1|}.

For the right side, we can write for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle A_{3}\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}+A_{3}\beta^{2n}\left(6-\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}\right)+12\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{3}\lambda_{1}^{2n}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\geq\quad A_{3}\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}+A_{3}\beta^{2}\left(6-\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}\right)-12|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|^{2n}

\displaystyle\geq\quad A_{3}\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}+A_{3}\beta^{2}\left(6-\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}\right)-12|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|^{2}.

Since the inequality

66+22|B_{3}|\frac{1+|\lambda_{1}|^{2}}{|\lambda_{1}^{2}-1|}\quad\leq\quad A_{3}\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}+A_{3}\beta^{2}\left(6-\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}\right)-12|B_{3}||\lambda_{1}|^{2}

holds true for the given values, we conclude
B=\frac{17}{11}
.

Computation of
C
and
D
.
Denote
f_{n}:=|\mu(\varphi^{n}(01))|
, then it satisfies the recurrence relation
f_{n+1}=2f_{n}-f_{n-1}+f_{n-2}

with initial conditions

f_{0}=10,f_{1}=15
and
f_{2}=26
.

The explicit solution reads

f_{n}=A_{4}\beta^{n}+B_{4}\lambda_{1}^{n}+C_{4}\lambda_{2}^{n},

where

\beta\doteq 1.75488,\quad\lambda_{1}\doteq 0.12256+0.74486i,\quad\lambda_{2}=\overline{\lambda_{1}}

are the roots of the polynomial
t^{3}-2t^{2}+t-1
, and

\displaystyle A_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{10|\lambda_{1}|^{2}-30\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_{1})+26}{|\beta-\lambda_{1}|^{2}}\doteq 8.704306843\,;

\displaystyle B_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{10\beta\lambda_{2}-15(\beta+\lambda_{2})+26}{(\beta-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})}\doteq 0.647846579+0.291191845i\,;

\displaystyle C_{4}

\displaystyle=\overline{B_{4}}\,.

The computation for
C\leq\frac{17}{11}
is the same as for
B
.
Let us show that
D\leq\frac{17}{11}
. We have to show for
n\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\frac{8+A_{4}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta^{2k-1}+B_{4}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{1}^{2k-1}+C_{4}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{2}^{2k-1}}{A_{4}\beta^{2n-1}+B_{4}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}+C_{4}\lambda_{2}^{2n-1}}\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad\frac{17}{11},

\displaystyle 88+11A_{4}\left(\frac{\beta^{2n-1}}{\beta^{2}-1}-\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}\right)+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{4}\lambda_{1}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}^{2n-2}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 6A_{4}\beta^{2n-1}+12\operatorname{Re}(B_{4}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}).

Since

\displaystyle\frac{11}{\beta^{2}-1}

\displaystyle\leq 6,

we need to prove the inequality in the form

\displaystyle 88+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{4}\lambda_{1}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}^{2n-2}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq^{?}\quad 11A_{4}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}+12\operatorname{Re}(B_{4}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1}).

For the left side, we can write for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle 88+22\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{4}\lambda_{1}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}^{2n-2}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq\quad 88+22\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{B_{4}\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)+22|B_{4}|\frac{|\lambda_{1}|^{2n-1}}{|1-\lambda_{1}^{2}|}

\displaystyle\leq\quad 88+22\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{B_{4}\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)+22|B_{4}|\frac{|\lambda_{1}|}{|1-\lambda_{1}^{2}|}.

For the right side, we can write for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\quad 11A_{4}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}+12\operatorname{Re}(B_{4}\lambda_{1}^{2n-1})\quad

\displaystyle\geq\quad 11A_{4}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}-12|B_{4}||\lambda_{1}|^{2n-1}

\displaystyle\geq\quad 11A_{4}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}-12|B_{4}||\lambda_{1}|.

Since the inequality

88+22\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{B_{4}\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}\right)+22|B_{4}|\frac{|\lambda_{1}|}{|1-\lambda_{1}^{2}|}\quad\leq\quad 11A_{4}\frac{\beta}{\beta^{2}-1}-12|B_{4}||\lambda_{1}|

holds true for the given values, we obtain
D\leq\frac{17}{11}
.

We have shown that
\max\{A,B,C,D\}=B=\frac{17}{11}
, and
F<\frac{17}{11}
.
Consequently,
E(\mu({\bf p}))=1+\max\{A,B,C,D,F\}=\frac{28}{11}

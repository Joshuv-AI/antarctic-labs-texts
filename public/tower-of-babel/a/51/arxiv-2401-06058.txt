Around Homology Planes: Old and new
Rodolfo Aguilar Aguilar

Abstract
. We survey some old and new results about acyclic (affine) complex surfaces, also called homology planes. We ask several questions and leave open directions for future research.



1.
Introduction

This note is to extend a talk given in Sunny Beach on August 2023 during the conference “Generalized and Symplectic Geometry”. The talk was intended to give a survey around the classification of acyclic affine complex algebraic surfaces and to highlight some connections with topology and symplectic geometry.

Most of the note is expository although there are some new results. These new results are in the topology section and they will be explained in more detail below.

In writing these notes, I use the opportunity to present some open questions that arose in discussions with people in the conference and outside. There are already some very good surveys about homology planes, see
[
Zai98
]
and
[
Miy01
]
. For connections with
\mathbb{A}_{1}
-homotopy theory see also
[
AOsr21
]
. I try by no means to be exhaustive, and the contents here only reflects the interest of the author. He apologizes if he forgot to mention or give some credits.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
, we present the cancellation problem as a motivation for studying homology planes and present some classification results.

In Section
3
, we present some algebraic and analytic cancellation Theorems and show how this give exotic algebraic and analytic structures on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. After this, we mention how we can obtain exotic symplectic structures on some affine real spaces. We end the section by presenting a result about exotic balls.

In Section
4
, more questions and symplectic techniques are presented.

In Section
5
, we study the relation of the fundamental group of some homology planes and the fundamental group of the
3
-manifold that can be thought as its boundary. We present a new result about the structure of this variety in Theorem
5.7
and a partial computation of its character variety in Theorem
5.9
.

Finally, in Section
6
, we survey some results and questions in higher dimension and we finish presenting some open question about the cancellation problem.

2.2.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

X

H_{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0

Example 2.2
.

The Ramanujam’s surface

Using the classification of smooth projective surfaces, the Miyaoka inequality

Miy84

together with some technical computations, Gurjar and Shastri proved the following:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
GS89a
]

Homology planes are rational.

The classification of homology planes follows the lines of the classification of surfaces via the (logarithmic) Kodaira dimension, that we introduce next.

Definition 2.2

Let

be a SNC-compactification. We define the

\alpha m^{\bar{\kappa}(X)}\leq\dim H^{0}(\bar{X},m(K_{\bar{X}}+D))\leq\beta m^{\bar{\kappa}(X)}

where

The invariant
\bar{\kappa}(X)
does not depend on the chosen compactification of
X
. See
[
Iit77
]
.

When
\bar{\kappa}(X)\leq 1

X
. Recall that a quasi-pencil
L(1,n+1)

.

(2)

(3)

Let us begin first with some conjectures.

Conjecture 2.1
.

Let

be a homology plane of log general type and let
(\bar{X},D)
be a minimal SNC completion of

. Then

(1)

(tom Dieck-Petrie conjecture) Every homology plane arises from finitely many configurations of lines and conics in

.

(2)

(Koras conjecture) The automorphism group of

(3)

(4)

(Palka negativity conjecture)
\kappa(K_{\bar{X}}+\frac{1}{2}D)=-\infty

In
[

]
, T. Pełka completely classified the
\mathbb{Q}
-homology planes assuming Palka negativity conjecture. He uses the theory of almost minimal model of Palka
[
Pal19
]
. Indeed, assuming that
\kappa(K_{\bar{X}}+\frac{1}{2}D)=-\infty

(\bar{X}_{\text{min}},\frac{1}{2}D_{\text{min}})

[

\mathbb{Z}
-homology planes).

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Peł23
]
)
.

Assume (4) in Conjecture
2.1
(Palka negativity conjecture), then it implies (1),(2) and (3). In particular, the
\mathbb{Z}
-homology planes of log-general type can be arranged in finitely many discrete series, each obtained in a uniform way from an arrangement of lines and conics on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

3.
Exotic structures

3.1.
Algebraic

Using the theory developed in
[
Iit77
]
, Iitaka-Fujita proved the following cancellation theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
IF77
]
)
.

If we have an algebraic isomorphism
X\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong Y\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\bar{\kappa}(Y)\geq 0
then the cancellation property holds:
X\cong Y
.

By taking
Y
to be the Ramanujam surface of Example
2.1
(it can be shown that
\bar{\kappa}(Y)=2
), we can obtain algebraic exotic structures on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.

Corollary 1
.

The space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
admits an exotic algebraic structure for
n\geq 3
.

Indeed, using the
h
-cobordism Theorem one can show that
Y\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2n+4}
. By taking
X=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and if we suppose that
X\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong Y\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we use the cancellation Theorem
3.1
of Iitaka-Fujita, which would give the contradiction that the Ramanujam surface is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

3.2.
Analytic

Using properties of non-singular quasi-projective varieties of log-general type (e.g. every dominant holomorphic map
X\to Y
from a quasi-projective manifold to a manifold of log-general type is regular), Zaidenberg proved the following
analytic
cancellation theorem.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Zai93
]
)
.

Suppose that
X
and
Y
are smooth surfaces of log-general type, this is
\bar{\kappa}(X)=\bar{\kappa}(Y)=2
, then
X,Y,\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfy an
analytic
cancellation property.

Moreover, he constructed two infinite families of contractible homology planes of log-general type pairwise non-isomorphic. One family generalized the Ramanujam surface.

Using these infinite families, his analytic cancellation theorem
3.2
and the same argument in the proof of Corollary
1
, he obtained the following Corollary.

Corollary 2

(
[
Zai93
]
)
.

There is a countable infinite set of analytic exotic structures on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.

Moreover, in
[
FZ94
]
, by using that homology planes of log-Kodaira dimension one can have moduli, they obtained an
uncountable
number of exotic structures on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.

3.3.
Symplectic

In
[
SS05
]
, the authors studied the symplectic topology of the Ramanujam surface. More precisely, they obtained the following.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
SS05
]
)
.

Let
X
be the Ramanujam’s surface, the product
X^{m}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4m}
induces an exotic symplectic structure on
\mathbb{R}^{4m}
which is convex at infinity.

As explained in
[
Sei08
]
, this can be shown by showing that the symplectic cohomology of
X^{m}
does not vanish.

To construct an infinite countable number of exotic symplectic structures on
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
for
m>3
McLean started from a different example. He used the so-called Kaliman modification
[
Kal94
]
to construct Lefschetz fibrations. As the cancellation theorem are no longer valid in the symplectic category, he used the number of idempotents in symplectic homology as invariant to distinguish between different exotic structures.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
McL09
]
)
.

Let
m>3
. Then for each
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
there are infinitely many finite type Stein manifolds which are pairwise distinct as symplectic manifolds.

Question 2
.

Is it possible to construct uncountably many exotic symplectic structures on
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
for
m>3
?

Note that one needs a stronger invariant than symplectic cohomology if one wants to use the deformation examples of Flenner-Zaidenberg.

3.4.
Contractible four-manifolds

An important problem in low-dimensional topology is to determine which homology
3
-spheres bound contractible
4
-manifolds. See
[
Şav24
]
.

A
Mazur
manifold, is a contractible, compact, smooth
4
-dimensional manifold with boundary, which is not diffeomorphic to the standard
4
-ball and such that in its handle decomposition it contains exactly three handles: one
0
-handle, one
1
-handle and one
2
-handle.

Let us review briefly the construction of Mazur and Poénaru manifolds as in
[
Şav20
, Sec. 2.2]
which is closer to the construction by V. Poénaru
[
Poe60
]
. Consider a disk
D
smoothly embedded in
B^{4}
and take a tubular neighborhood
U
of
D
. In the boundary
\partial U
, which is diffeomorphic to
S^{1}\times S^{2}
, make an integral surgery in a knot
J
with fusion number
0
such that the
3
-manifold obtained is a homology sphere. The resulting
3
-manifold bounds a Mazur manifold, see
[
Şav20
, Lemma 2.1]
.

In this construction, if we replace the disk
D
by a ribbon disk
D\subset B^{4}
and the knot
J
by a ribbon knot with fusion number
n\geq 1
, the resulting
3
-manifold bounds a contractible four manifold with one
0
-handle,
n+1

1
-handles and
n+1

2
-handles. This is called a
Poénaru manifold
.

In order to obtain this ribbon knot, one fixes a compactification
(\bar{X},D)
of a homology plane
X=\bar{X}\setminus D
and consider the dual graph of
D
. This can also be seen as the surgery diagram of the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of
D
as follows: to every vertex associate an unknot, link two unknots if and only if there is an edge connecting the corresponding vertices and decorate the unknots with the weights of the corresponding vertices. If by applying Kirby calculus one ends with a ribbon knot, one uses Şavk’s construction of Poénaru manifolds.

By using the vast quantity of example in the literature of homology planes, in particular some examples of Zaidenberg generalizing the Ramanujam surface and contractible homology planes of log-Kodaira dimension one and using Kirby calculus, one can show the following Theorem.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
AAŞ22
]
)
.

There are four infinite families of homology
3
-spheres that bound both homology planes and Mazur/Poénaru manifolds.

In
[
AAŞ22
]
, these homology
3
-spheres bounding both homology planes and Mazur/Poénaru manifolds are called Kirby-Ramanujam spheres. In Theorem
3.5
, one infinite family consists of Mazur manifolds and the other three of Poénaru manifolds.

4.
More symplectic properties

4.1.
Search for more exotic symplectic structures

One can ask if by using other examples of contractible homology planes one can obtain more exotic symplectic structures on
\mathbb{R}^{4m}
. This is the content of the unpublished work of D. Jackson-Hanen
[
JH14
]
.

Moreover, partially motivated by this kind of quest, Ganatra-Pomerleano showed the following Theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
GP20
]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine variety and let
(\bar{X},D)
be a SNC-compactification. Then there is a multiplicative spectral sequence converging to the symplectic cohomology ring

\{E_{r}^{p,q},d_{r}\}\to SH^{*}(X)

whose first page is isomorphic as rings to the logarithmic cohomology of
(\bar{X},D)
.

For the definition of logarithmic cohomology of a pair
(\bar{X},D)
, see
[
GP21
]
.

Question 3
.

(1)

Can one use this spectral sequence to recover the Seidel-Smith result?

(2)

Can one use the classification of Pełka to obtain more exotic structures?

4.2.
A question for the contact structure of the boundary

If
X=\bar{X}\setminus D
is a homology plane and we let
U
be a closed regular tubular neighborhood of
D
, then the boundary of
\bar{X}\setminus U
inherits a contact structure.

We can say that an oriented contact
3
-manifold
(M,\xi)
is Ramanujam fillable if it is contactomorphic to the contact boundary of a homology plane.

We can try to extend the following result of singularity theory.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(M,\xi)
be an oriented contact manifold. We say it is
Milnor fillable
if it is contactomorphic to the contact boundary of an isolated complex-analytic singularity
(\mathcal{X},x)
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
CNPP06
]
)
.

Let
(M,\xi)
be any three dimensional oriented manifold. Then it admits at most one Milnor fillable structure up to contactomorphism.

Question 4
.

Does an analog of the Caubel, Némethi, Popescu-Pampu theorem
4.2
holds in this setting (probably restricting the Kodaira dimension)?

In fact, as shown in
[
Peł23
, Cor 1.10]
, there exists examples of non (algebraically) isomorphic but diffeomorphic homology planes
X=\bar{X}\setminus D
with the same dual graph for
D
. These are sometimes called Zariski-pairs, see
[
ABCT08
]
. This indicates that additional information to the dual graph is needed, we ask if the contact structure is enough for this. Moreover, similar questions and techniques have been studied in the symplectic category for rational cuspidal curves. See
[
GS22
,
GK23
]
.

4.3.
Invariance of log-Kodaira dimension

Let us gather here more questions related to homology planes.

In the compact setting, the invariance of the Kodaira dimension under diffeomorphism is shown by Friedman-Morgan
[
FM94
]
. In the symplectic category we have the following result of McLean.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
McL14
]
)
.

The logarithmic Kodaira dimension is a symplectic invariant of homology planes.

To prove this, he uses Theorem
2.1
,
2.3
and the theory he develops about the symplectic invariance of uniruledness.

Question 5
.

(McLean): Is the logarithmic Kodaira dimension a topological invariant of homology planes?

When
X

Moreover, as they are contractible, one can wonder what kind of invariants could be non-trivial in the mirror. Recall that for example, in the Ramanujam surface, symplectic cohomology is non-trivial.

5.
Topology

5.1.
Fundamental groups

Motivated by the question, often attributed to Serre, of what groups can appear as fundamental groups of complex projective manifolds, we can ask:

(1)

What groups can appear as fundamental groups of homology planes,

(2)

What groups can appear as fundamental groups at infinity for homology planes,

(3)

What is the relation between these two?

A first constrain in given by the following theorem of S. Orevkov.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
Ore97
]
)
.

Let
X=\bar{X}\setminus D
be a homology plane and for
D\subset U
a tubular neighborhood as above then the three-manifold
\partial U
is not a Seifert fibered homology sphere.

An
orbicurve group
is a group admitting a presentation of the following type:

\displaystyle\langle a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{g},c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\mid

\displaystyle\quad\quad c_{1}^{r_{1}}=\ldots=c_{s}^{r_{s}}=1,\prod_{i=1}^{g}[a_{i},b_{i}]c_{1}\ldots c_{r}=1\rangle

Theorem 5.2

(
[
AA21
]
)
.

There exists infinitely many homology planes of log-general type with infinite fundamental group. All of them admit a quotient to an infinite orbicurve group.

For the proof, a presentation of the fundamental group of the homology plane is obtained. Then, some simpler quotient (an orbicuve group) which is known to present an infinite group is deduced.

Question 7
.

Does every infinite fundamental group of a homology plane admits a surjective morphism to an infinite orbicurve group?

By a Theorem of Arapura
[
Ara97
]
, extended to orbicurves in
[
ABCAM13
]
, the morphisms from quasi-projective varieties to orbicurves are encoded in the irreducible components of the so-called
cohomology jump loci
. For a quasi-projective variety
X
, this loci consists of elements
\rho
in
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C}^{\ast})
such that, the associated local system
V_{\rho}
has non-vanishing first cohomology
H^{1}(X,V_{\rho})\not=0
.

Now, from the construction of homology planes coming from an arrangement of lines
\mathcal{A}
, every point of multiplicity three of higher will give a map to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with some orbifold structure. However, to find the examples in Theorem
5.2
, we used the complete description of the cohomology jump loci as given by Suciu in
[
Suc01
]
. In particular, we used maps not coming from points of multiplicity three or higher, but others reflecting the global structure of
\mathcal{A}
plus some carefully chosen numerical data that depend on the homology plane.

We do not know if the question
7
has a positive answer even if we assume the negativity conjecture
2.1
, but for homology planes of Kodaira dimension one it has indeed a positive answer. See below remark
5.5
.

If one would like to study the cohomology jump loci in the homology plane directly and not in the complement of an arrangement
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
a generalization of Arapura’s result is needed for non-abelian representations, for example in
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Even simpler, we can try to study the
SU(2)
character varieties. This is not easy, even for the representations of the
3
-homology sphere bounding a homology plane
X
, as we will see in subsection
5.3
. For more of representations of quasi-projective groups on
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, see
[
CS08
,
PS20
]
.

When
X
is a homology plane of log-Kodaira dimension one. It is easy to compute its fundamental group.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
X
be a homology plane of logarithmic Kodaira dimension one coming from
L(1,n+1)
with data
(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n})
then a presentation for its fundamental group is given by

(2)

\langle\gamma_{0},\ldots\gamma_{n}\mid\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{0}^{-1},[\gamma_{0},\gamma_{i}]=1,\gamma_{i}^{a_{i}}\gamma_{0}^{b_{i}}=1\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n\rangle

Remark 5.4
.

Note that from (
2
), we obtain that
H_{1}(X)
is trivial if and only if

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{rrrr}a_{1}-b_{1}&-b_{1}&\ldots&-b_{1}\\
-b_{2}&a_{2}-b_{2}&&-b_{2}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
-b_{n}&-b_{n}&&a_{n}-b_{n}\par\end{array}\right)=\pm 1

which is precisely the condition in (
1
).

Remark 5.5
.

Note that all homology planes obtained from
L(1,3)
are contractible. Indeed, note that from (
2
), more specifically, from
[\gamma_{0},\gamma_{2}]=1
and
\gamma_{0}^{-1}=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
we obtain that
[\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}]=1
and hence
\pi_{1}(X)
is abelian. By the condition (
1
) and by Remark (
5.4
), we obtain that
\pi_{1}(X)
is trivial.

Moreover, when
n\geq 3
, the homology planes obtained from
L(1,n+1)
by the above procedure are never contractible. Indeed, it admits an orbifold morphism to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
n
marked points with weights
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
.

Remark 5.6
.

Note that the presentation in (
2
) is the presentation of the fundamental group of a Seifert fibered homology sphere.

5.2.
Boundary manifolds

When
X=\bar{X}\setminus D
is a homology plane of log-Kodaira dimension one, the dual graph of
D
is as in Figure

or it can have an extra linear segment of
-2
-curves corresponding to a series of blow-ups starting in a smooth point in the line
L_{n+1}
.

Recall Theorem
3.5
. In
[
AAŞ22
]
, for an infinite family
(\bar{X}_{n},D_{n})
of homology planes of logarithmic Kodaira dimension one, it was shown that if we let
\partial U_{n}
be the boundary of a regular tubular neighborhood of

6.1.

It is a result from Fujita

Theorem 6.1

(
[

]
)

There exists a contractible quasi-affine variety
X

X

The example is obtained by the quotient of a free action of the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
on
\mathbb{A}^{5}

Theorem 6.2

(
[

]

Let

The examples are given by removing a closed subscheme

6.2.

To finish this note, let us conclude with an unexpected connection , at least to the author, between the cancellation property and moduli spaces of representations on a punctured Riemann surface.

Expectation (Su)

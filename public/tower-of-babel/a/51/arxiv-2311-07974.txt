Strongly Invertible Legendrian Links
Carlo Collari and Paolo Lisca

Abstract
. We introduce and study strongly invertible Legendrian links in the standard contact three-dimensional space. We establish the equivariant analogs of basic results separately well-known for strongly invertible and Legendrian links, i.e. the existence of transvergent front diagrams, an equivariant Legendrian Reidemeister theorem, and an equivariant stabilization theorem à la Fuch-Tabachnikov. We also introduce a maximal equivariant Thurston-Bennequin number for strongly invertible links and we exhibit infinitely many such links for which the invariant coincides with the usual maximal Thurston-Bennequin number. We conjecture that such a coincidence does not hold general and that there exist strongly invertible knots having Legendrian representatives isotopic to their reversed Legendrian mirrors but not isotopic to any strongly invertible Legendrian knot.

We start by recalling the definitions of a strongly invertible link and of a Legendrian link.
A link
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a finite collection of smoothly embedded circles.
Let
\tau\thinspace\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the involution given by

\tau(x,y,z)=(x,-y,-z).

We say that

\tau(L_{i})=L_{i}
for each connected component

, and
\tau|_{L_{i}}
has exactly two fixed points. Two strongly invertible links are
equivalent
if there is a family of strongly invertible links that connects them.
The
standard contact structure
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the tangent plane distribution
\xi_{\rm st}:=\ker(dz-ydx)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Note that
\xi_{\rm st}
is preserved by
\tau

A link is
Legendrian
if it is everywhere tangent to
\xi_{\rm st}
. Two Legendrian links are
equivalent
if there is a family of Legendrian links that connects them. In analogy with the above, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

A strongly invertible Legendrian link is a link that is simultaneously strongly invertible with respect to
\tau
and Legendrian with respect to
\xi_{\rm st}
.
Two strongly invertible Legendrian links are equivalent if they are connected by a smooth family of strongly invertible Legendrian links.

Following

]

The image

It is a well-known and easy-to-show fact that, given a front

After a small,

-small Legendrian perturbation, a Legendrian link admits a generic front

The diagram associated with a generic front will be called a

As before, we introduce the following definition in analogy to the smooth case.

A front

Remark 1.5
.

To keep things simple in this paper we mainly consider strongly invertible

As in the non-equivariant case, one could introduce

It is natural to wonder whether the maximal Thurston-Bennequin number of a strongly invertible knot is always realized by a strongly invertible Legendrian knot. This leads to the following

Definition 1.6
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a strongly invertible knot.
We define the maximal equivariant Thurston-Bennequin number of
K
, denoted
\overline{\tb}_{e}(K)
, as the maximal Thurston-Bennequin number of a strongly invertible Legendrian knot representing

.

Note that the maximal equivariant Thurston-Bennequin number can be refined to yield an invariant of strongly invertible knots with a fixed involution, by considering the maximal Thurston-Bennequin number achieved within the appropriate equivalence class of strongly invertible knots.
However, in this paper we do not consider such a refinement.

Denote by
\overline{\tb}(K)
the maximal Thurston-Bennequin number among all Legendrian representatives of
K
.
For any knot
K
the inequality
\overline{\tb}_{e}(K)\leq\overline{\tb}(K)
clearly holds, so it is natural to ask whether it is possibile to have a strict inequality.

In Section

we prove the following

Proposition 1.7
.

If
K
is either a torus knot of type
T(2,2n+1)
,

, or a twist knot.
Then, we have
\overline{\tb}_{e}(K)=\overline{\tb}(K)
.

On the other hand, in Section

we present some experimental results which suggest that the equality
\overline{\tb}_{e}=\overline{\tb}
should not hold in general.

Remark 1.8
.

One may define strong invertibility with respect to the involution
\sigma\thinspace\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
given by
\sigma(x,y,z)=(-x,-y,z)
.
Note that both the involutions

and

\tau
reverses the orientation of the contact planes, while

Moreover, while the fixed-point set

is tangent to
\xi_{\rm std}
, the fixed-point set

– which coincides with the

transverse
to
\xi_{\rm std}
.
We do not investigate strong

-invertibility in this paper.

Abstract
We consider the Cauchy problems in 𝐑 n {\bf R}^{n} for wave equations with higher derivative terms. We derive sharp growth estimates of the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the solution itself for the case of n = 1 n=1 and n = 2 n=2 . By imposing the weighted L 1 L^{1} -initial velocity, we can get the lower and upper bound estimates of the solution itself. For the case of n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we observe that the L 2 L^{2} -growth behavior of the solution never occurs in the ( L 2 ∩ L 1 ) (L^{2}\cap L^{1}) -framework of the initial data.

A natural tendency to cope with this problem is to adopt semigroup theory. For the reader’s convenience, we outline the proof based on the ideas coming from

We denote
v=u_{t}
and
A=-\Delta

(I+A)v_{t}=-Au,

where

Setting

\displaystyle U=\begin{pmatrix}u\\

we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}U=\mathcal{A}U,\\
&U(0,x)=U_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Here
\mathcal{D}(P)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(P)=\{

\displaystyle u\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}):\text{There exists}~y_{u}\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})~\text{such that}~

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{u},A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)+(y_{u},\phi),~~\forall\phi\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\},

(2.2)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the inner product of
L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})

(f,g)=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}f(x)g(x)dx,~~~f,g\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}).

Note that
\mathcal{D}(P)
is not empty because

when we take
y=0
. If
u\in\mathcal{D}(P)
, there exists a unique
y_{u}\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
such that (
2.2
) holds. Otherwise, at least there exists
y_{u}^{1}
and

\displaystyle(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{u}^{1},A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)+(y^{1}_{u},\phi)

\displaystyle(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{u}^{2},A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)+(y^{2}_{u},\phi)

for every

. Taking

Let

The above arguments imply that the linear operator

is well defined for each

Next we prove the fact
\mathcal{D}(P)=H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
, that is, for every
u\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
, there exists

such that (
2.2
) holds.

First, for each

, we define the bounded and linear functional

as following

<F_{u},\phi>=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi),~~~~\forall\phi\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}).

(2.5)

By Riesz representation theorem, there exists a unique
y_{u}\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
such that

<F_{u},\phi>=(y_{u},\phi)_{H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})},~~~~\forall\phi\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}),

(2.6)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})}
denotes the inner product of Sobolev space

and it is equivalent to

(f,~g)_{H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})}=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}f,A^{\frac{1}{2}}g)+(f,g).

(2.7)

It follows from (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) that

(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{u},A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi)+(y_{u},\phi),~~\forall\phi\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}).

(2.8)

Therefore, we have
\mathcal{D}(P)=H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
and we can define

on

. Taking the Fourier transform of both sides of (

) leads to

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}\hat{u}\bar{\hat{\phi}}~d\xi=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(|\xi|^{2}+1)\hat{y}_{u}\bar{\hat{\phi}}~d\xi.

Due to the arbitrariness of
\phi
, we obtain
|\xi|^{2}\hat{u}=(|\xi|^{2}+1)\hat{y}_{u}
, and then

\hat{y}_{u}=\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\hat{u}.

(2.9)

Before giving the following lemma, we have to define the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}:=H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})

\big([y_{1},z_{1}],[y_{2},z_{2}]\big)_{\mathcal{H}}:=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{1},A^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{2})+(y_{1},y_{2})+(A^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{1},A^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2})+(z_{1},z_{2}).

Lemma 2.1

The operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
-P&0\end{pmatrix}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}

generates a strongly continuous contraction semigroup

on

proof.

For each

, it follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{A}U,U\big)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\big([v,-P(u)],~[u,v]\big)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\hat{v}\bar{\hat{u}}~d\xi+2i\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}\text{Im}(\hat{u}\bar{\hat{v}})~d\xi.

where

\mathcal{H}
.

that

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{L_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{P^{2}}{2}\int_{L_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}d\xi-\int_{L_{0}}|A(\xi)-iB(\xi)|^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}d\xi

\displaystyle=:\frac{P^{2}}{2}I_{l}(t)-R_{l}(t).

(3.5)

By (
3.4
),
R_{l}(t)
is estimated as follows for
\gamma\in(1/2,1]

\displaystyle R_{l}(t)

\displaystyle\leq M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}\int_{L_{0}}\frac{r^{2\gamma}}{f(r)^{2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-\delta_{0}^{2}}}}(1+r^{2})r^{2(\gamma-1)}dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CM^{2}}{2\gamma-1}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}t^{-(2\gamma-1)},

(3.6)

where
r=|\xi|
, the constant
C>0
, and
t\gg 1
. Therefore, from (
3.2
), (
3
) and (
3
), one can get the desired lower bound estimate

\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\geq J_{1}(t)\geq C_{1}P^{2}t-C_{\gamma}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}t^{-(2\gamma-1)},\quad t\gg 1

with some constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{\gamma}>0
. By density argument, one can state the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Let
n=1
, and
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. Assume
u_{1}\in L^{1,\gamma}({\bf R})
. Then, it holds that

\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\geq CP^{2}t,\quad t\gg 1.

Next, we discuss the proof of the two dimensional case. We rely on the integration by parts together with a trick initiated from
[
19
]
.

Using (
1.12
) and (
3.3
), we start with the integral combing the trick function
e^{-|\xi|^{2}}

\displaystyle\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|P+(A(\xi)-iB(\xi))|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}P^{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}d\xi-\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}\left(M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}|\xi|^{2\gamma}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}P^{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}d\xi-M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}}\frac{|\xi|^{2\gamma}}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}d\xi

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2}P^{2}T(t)-M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}U(t).

(3.7)

Incidentally, this crucial idea to use the trick function has been initiated in the paper
[
19
]
. Of course, there are other ways to choose this function.

Now,
U(t)
can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{U(t)}{\omega_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}}r^{2\gamma-1}dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}}r^{2\gamma+1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}}r^{2\gamma+1}dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}}r^{2\gamma+1}dr

\displaystyle=:K_{0}.

Note that
K_{0}>0
has a finite value for
\gamma\in(0,1]
. The role of trick function
e^{-|\xi|^{2}}
is useful. Thus, from (
3.7
) one has

\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}P^{2}T(t)-M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}\omega_{2}K_{0},\quad t>0.

(3.8)

Finally, let us estimate the main term
T(t)
.

Since

2\sin^{2}(tf(r))=1-\cos(2tf(r)),

we get for
t>1

\displaystyle T(t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}\frac{2\sin^{2}(tf(r))}{f^{2}(r)}rdr

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}\frac{r}{f^{2}(r)}dr-\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}\frac{r}{f^{2}(r)}\cos(2tf(r))dr

\displaystyle=:\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}T_{1}(t)-\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}T_{2}(t).

(3.9)

Here, we see that

T_{1}(t)=\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}\frac{1+r^{2}}{r}dr\geq\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}\frac{1}{r}dr\geq e^{-1}\int_{1/t}^{1}\frac{1}{r}dr=e^{-1}\log t,\quad t>1.

(3.10)

T_{2}(t)
can be decomposed into two parts:

\displaystyle T_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}r^{-1}\cos(2tf(r))dr+\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}r\cos(2tf(r))dr

\displaystyle=:R_{1}(t)+R_{2}(t).

(3.11)

R_{2}(t)
can be estimated easily by

|R_{2}(t)|\leq\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}rdr=\frac{1}{2}(e^{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}-e^{-1})\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad t>1.

(3.12)

In order to estimate
R_{1}(t)
, we develop the integration by parts. This idea is inspired by
[
8
, Proposition A.1]
. Indeed, since

\cos(2tf(r))=\frac{1}{2f^{\prime}(r)t}\left(\frac{d}{dr}\sin(2tf(r))\right),

f^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{(1+r^{2})\sqrt{1+r^{2}}},

R_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2t}\int_{1/t}^{1}\frac{e^{-r^{2}}}{r}(1+r^{2})\sqrt{1+r^{2}}\frac{d}{dr}\sin(2tf(r))dr=:\frac{1}{2t}K(t),

(3.13)

K(t)
can be estimated by

\displaystyle K(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{e^{-r^{2}}}{r}(1+r^{2})\sqrt{1+r^{2}}\sin(2tf(r))\right]_{1/t}^{1}

\displaystyle~~~~-\int_{1/t}^{1}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{e^{-r^{2}}}{r}(1+r^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\sin(2tf(r))dr

\displaystyle=:K_{1}(t)+K_{2}(t).

(3.14)

It is obvious that

|K_{1}(t)|\leq C_{1}+C_{2}t

(3.15)

with some constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
. Next, we should check the upper bound estimate for
|K_{2}(t)|
. In fact, it holds that

\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{e^{-r^{2}}}{r}(1+r^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)=-\frac{e^{-r^{2}}}{r^{2}}(2r^{2}+1)(1+r^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}+3e^{-r^{2}}(1+r^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle K_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{1/t}^{1}\frac{e^{-r^{2}}}{r^{2}}(2r^{2}+1)(1+r^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sin(2tf(r))dr-3\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}(1+r^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\sin(2tf(r))dr

\displaystyle=:K_{2,1}(t)-K_{2,2}(t).

(3.16)

Simple calculations yield

|K_{2,2}(t)|\leq 3\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}(1+r^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}dr\leq 3e^{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}\sqrt{2}(1-\frac{1}{t})\leq C_{3}

(3.17)

for some constant
C_{3}>0
. While,

|K_{2,1}(t)|\leq\int_{1/t}^{1}e^{-r^{2}}r^{-2}(2r^{2}+1)(1+r^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}dr\leq 6\sqrt{2}e^{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}\int_{1/t}^{1}r^{-2}dr\leq C_{4}t

(3.18)

for
t\gg 1
and some constant
C_{4}>0
.

By summerizing (
3
)-(
3.18
), the estimate for
T_{2}(t)
is given by

|T_{2}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{2t}(C_{1}+C_{2}t+C_{3}+C_{4}t),\quad t\gg 1.

(3.19)

Therefore, by (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.19
), one can state the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

Let
n=2
, and
\gamma\in(0,1]
. Assume
u_{1}\in L^{1,\gamma}({\bf R}^{2})
. Then, it holds that

\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\geq CP^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

4
L^{2}
-upper bound estimates of the solution

In this section, we derive upper bound estimate of
\|u(t,\cdot)\|
as
t\to\infty
. We use the
L^{2}
-regularity together with
L^{1}
-regularity of the initial velocity.

We first treat the one dimensional case. It also suffices to derive the desired upper bound estimate by assuming
u_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})
.

From (
1.8
) and (
1.9
), we can proceed the estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}}|\frac{\sin(tf(|\xi|))}{f(|\xi|)}|^{2}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{L_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}\setminus L_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=:L_{1}(t)+L_{2}(t),

(4.1)

where the set
L_{0}
with
n=1
is defined in (
3.1
). Then, from (
1.10
) it holds that

L_{1}(t)\leq L^{2}t^{2}\omega_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{A(t)}dr=L^{2}t^{2}\omega_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}A(t)\leq C_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}t

(4.2)

for
t\gg 1
, where
C_{1}>0
is a constant, and

A(t):=\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-\delta_{0}^{2}}}.

(4.3)

While, for
L_{2}(t)
one has

\displaystyle L_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}\setminus L_{0}}\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}\setminus L_{0}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}\setminus L_{0}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{A(t)}^{\infty}r^{-2}dr+\|u_{1}\|^{2}=\omega_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{A(t)}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}t+\|u_{1}\|^{2}

(4.4)

for
t\gg 1
, where
C_{2}>0
is a constant. Thus, from (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) one can derive the desired estimate as the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1

Let
n=1
, and
u_{1}\in L^{1}({\bf R})\cap L^{2}({\bf R})
. Then, it holds that

\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq C(\|u_{1}\|^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}t),\quad t\gg 1.

Next, let us give the upper bound estimate for
n=2
based on the decomposition below

\displaystyle\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}^{2}}|\frac{\sin(tf(|\xi|))}{f(|\xi|)}|^{2}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{L_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}^{2}\setminus L_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=:G_{1}(t)+G_{2}(t),

(4.5)

where the set
L_{0}
with
n=2
is defined in (
3.1
). It also suffices to assume
u_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{2})
in the derivation of upper bound estimate.

We first treat
G_{1}(t)
to get the estimate such that

G_{1}(t)\leq L^{2}t^{2}\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{A(t)}rdr=2^{-1}L^{2}\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\delta_{0}^{2}\frac{t^{2}}{t^{2}-\delta_{0}^{2}}\leq C_{3}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}

(4.6)

with some constant
C_{3}>0
and
t\gg 1
, where one uses the fact (
1.10
).

For the estimate of
G_{2}(t)
, it must be decomposed into three parts as follows in order to get the desired growth estimate

G_{2}(t)=g_{1}(t)+g_{2}(t)+g_{3}(t):=\left(\int_{|\xi|\geq C(t)}+\int_{C(t)\geq|\xi|\geq B(t)}+\int_{B(t)\geq|\xi|\geq A(t)}\right)\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi,

(4.7)

where
t\gg 1
,
A(t)
is already defined in (
4.3
), and

B(t):=\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t-\delta_{0}^{2}}},

C(t):=\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{\log t-\delta_{0}^{2}}}.

Now, we see that

g_{1}(t)\leq\frac{1}{f^{2}(C(t))}\int_{|\xi|\geq C(t)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\frac{\log t}{\delta_{0}^{2}}\|u_{1}\|^{2},\quad t\gg 1.

(4.8)

Recall that the function
f(r)
is monotone increasing on
[0,\infty)
.

Next, about
g_{2}(t)
we have

\displaystyle g_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{B(t)}^{C(t)}\frac{1+r^{2}}{r}dr=\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\left[\log r+\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\right]_{r=B(t)}^{r=C(t)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\left(\log C(t)-\log B(t)+\frac{1}{2}(C(t)^{2}-B(t)^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(t-\delta_{0}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\log(\log t-\delta_{0}^{2})+\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}(\frac{1}{\log t-\delta_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{t-\delta_{0}^{2}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\log t

(4.9)

with some constant
C_{4}>0
and
t\gg 1
.

Thirdly,
g_{3}(t)
can be estimated similarly to (
4.9
)

\displaystyle g_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{A(t)}^{B(t)}\frac{1+r^{2}}{r}dr

\displaystyle=\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}]\left[\log r+\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\right]_{r=A(t)}^{r=B(t)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{2}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(t^{2}-\delta_{0}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\log(t-\delta_{0}^{2})+\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}(\frac{1}{t-\delta_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{t^{2}-\delta_{0}^{2}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\log t

(4.10)

with some constant
C_{5}>0
and
t\gg 1
.

Therefore, the following lemma is a direct consequence of (
4.5
), (
4.6
), (
4.7
), (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
).

Lemma 4.2

Let
n=2
, and
u_{1}\in L^{1}({\bf R}^{2})\cap L^{2}({\bf R}^{2})
. Then, it holds that

\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq C(\|u_{1}\|^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2})\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

Finally, the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are direct consequences of Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
,
4.1
and
4.2
, and the Plancherel Theorem.

5
L^{2}
-upper bound estimates for the case
n\geq 3

In this section, we give some comments in the case of
n\geq 3
. In this case, the
L^{2}
-increasing property never holds, which is verified in below by the following calculations so far.

In fact, from (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) we can proceed the estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}}|\frac{\sin(tf(|\xi|))}{f(|\xi|)}|^{2}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}\setminus M_{0}}\frac{\sin^{2}(tf(|\xi|))}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=:M_{1}(t)+M_{2}(t),

(5.1)

where the set
M_{0}
with
n\geq 3
is defined by

M_{0}:=\{\xi\in{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}\,:\,|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\}.

(5.2)

Note that

\xi\in M_{0}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(|\xi|)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then, it holds that

M_{1}(t)\leq\int_{M_{0}}\frac{1}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\omega_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}(1+r^{2})r^{n-3}dr\leq C_{1,n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}

(5.3)

for
t\geq 0
, where
C_{1,n}>0
is a constant.

While,
M_{2}(t)
can be estimated by

M_{2}(t)\leq\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}\setminus M_{0}}\frac{1}{f^{2}(|\xi|)}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq 4\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}\setminus M_{0}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C_{2,n}\|u_{1}\|

(5.4)

for
t\geq 0
, where
C_{2,n}>0
is a constant. Thus, by (
5.1
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), one can obtain the following desired estimate.

Lemma 5.1

Let
n\geq 3
, and
u_{1}\in L^{1}({\bf R})\cap L^{2}({\bf R})
. Then, it holds that

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}=\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq C(\|u_{1}\|^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}),\quad t\gg 1.

Remark 5.1

Even if non-trivial initial value
u_{0}(x)
is added, the result remains the same because of the easy estimate:

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\cos^{2}(f(\xi)t)|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\|u_{0}\|^{2}.

So, the growth property never occurs for
n\geq 3
. In this sense,
n=1,2
are exceptional.

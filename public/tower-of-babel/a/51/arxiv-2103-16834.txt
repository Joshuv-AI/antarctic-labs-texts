Dynamical behavior of a nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation for a stochastic system with tempered stable noise
Li Lin, Jinqiao Duan, Xiao Wang, Yanjie Zhang

Abstract
We characterize a stochastic dynamical system with tempered stable noise, by examining its probability density evolution. This probability density function satisfies a nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation. First, we prove a superposition principle that the probability measure-valued solution to this nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation is equivalent to the martingale solution composed with the inverse stochastic flow. This result together with a Schauder estimate leads to the existence and uniqueness of strong solution for the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation. Second, we devise a convergent finite difference method to simulate the probability density function by solving the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation. Finally, we apply our aforementioned theoretical and numerical results to a nonlinear filtering system by simulating a nonlocal Zakai equation. Key words: Nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation, superposition principle, Schauder estimate, data assimilation, Zakai equation.



Stochastic dynamic systems are widely used to describe various complex phenomena. The random fluctuations in complex phenomena are usually non-Gaussian. How to capture the uncertainly propagates and evolves for the nonlinear stochastic dynamical systems is an important issue. A popular method is to obtain the probability density function of the solution paths, which contains the complete statistical information. In this paper, we prove a superposition principle that the probability measure-valued solution to this nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation is equivalent to the martingale solution. Meanwhile, we devise a convergent finite difference method to simulate the probability density function by solving the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation, which helps us to detect the dynamic behavior of the stochastic dynamic system. Further, we apply our aforementioned theoretical
and numerical results to a nonlinear filtering system by simulating a nonlocal Zakai equation. The results established in this paper can be used to examine dynamical behaviors for financial markets, climate dynamics and physics.

Our study is divided into two parts. In the first part, we study the superposition principle and global parabolic Schauder estimate for the nonlocal FPE under the natural condition
\alpha+\beta>1

c_{1}\leq\frac{1}{e^{\lambda|y|}}\leq c_{2},~~\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

METHODOLOGY

A: The nonlocal FPE

In this paper, we consider the following stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=f(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}L_{t},

(2.2)

where
f
is a
d
-dimensional Borel measurable function, and
L_{t}
is a
d
-dimensional tempered stable Lévy process with triplet
(0,0,\nu)
. This triplet means that the process has shift zero, diffusion zero, and Lévy jump measure
\nu
. The jump measure
\nu
is obtained through multiplying the

\alpha
-stable Lévy measure by an exponentially decaying function. This transform is called exponential tilting of the Lévy measure for
\alpha
-stable Lévy measure (see
[
19
]
), i.e.,

\nu({\rm d}y)=\frac{C_{d,\alpha}{\rm d}y}{e^{\lambda|y|}|y|^{d+\alpha}}.

(2.3)

Here
C_{d,\alpha}
is a constant, and
\lambda
is the positive tempering parameter (see
[
20
]
). The parameter
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
is called the stable index.

The corresponding FPE for the SDE (
2.2
) is the following nonlocal parabolic equation, i.e.,

\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(f(x)p(x,t)\right)+\mathcal{L}p(x,t),

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}p(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left[p(x+y,t)-p(x,t)-1_{\{|y|<1\}}(y)y\cdot\nabla p(x,t)\right]\nu(\mathrm{d}y)

(2.5)

\displaystyle={\bf{p.v.}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left[p(x+y,t)-p(x,t)\right]\nu(\mathrm{d}y).

In the following, we are interested in studying the dynamical properties and numerical analysis for the equation (
2.4
) in the principle value sense.

B: Examing the solution of nonlocal FPE

Let
\mu_{t}(dx)=p(x,t)dx
be the marginal law of
X_{t}
. By Itô’s formula,
\mu_{t}
solves the following
Fokker-Planck equation in the distributional sense, i.e.,

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(f(x)\mu_{t}\right)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left[\mu_{t}(x+y)-\mu_{t}(x)-1_{\{|y|<1\}}(y)y\cdot\frac{\nabla\mu_{t}}{\partial x}\right]\nu(\mathrm{d}y).

(2.6)

The proof of the existence and uniqueness of the equation (
2.11
) is based on two ingredients. The first ingredient will be to establish the superposition principle for probability measure-valued solution to a nonlocal FPE. The second ingredient will then be to establish the global Schauder estimate for the solution of (
2.11
).

B1: Superposition Principle

Next we will establish the superposition principle for probability measure-valued solution to the nonlocal FPE.
Throughout this paper, we make the following assumptions.

Hypothesis H.1.

The drift coefficient
f
is locally bounded, measurable and there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\sup_{x}\langle x,f(x)\rangle^{+}\leq C(1+|x|^{2}),

(2.7)

where
\langle x,f(x)\rangle^{+}
denote the non-negative part of
\langle x,f(x)\rangle.

Hypothesis H.2.

The drift coefficient
f
is locally
\beta
-Hölder continuous,
\beta\in(0,1)
, i.e., there exists a positive constant
K_{0}
such that

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq K_{0}|x-y|^{\beta},~~x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~s.t~~|x-y|\leq 1.

(2.8)

Theorem 2.1

(Superposition principle)
.

Under Hypothesis
\bf{H.1}
, for every weak solution
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of nonlocal FPE (
2.6
), there is a martingale solution
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{M}^{\mu_{0}}_{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}})
such that

\mu_{t}=\mathbb{P}\circ X^{-1}_{t},~~\forall t\geq 0,

(2.9)

Proof.

Using the similar technique as
[
21
, Theorem 1.5]
, we only need to verify

\hbar^{\nu}(x):=\int_{|z|>1+|x|}\log\left(1+\frac{|z|}{1+|x|}\right)\nu(dz)<\infty.

In fact,
\forall\lambda>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
and
\beta\in(0,\alpha\wedge 1)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\alpha}
, such that

\displaystyle\hbar^{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\int_{|z|>1+|x|}\log\left(1+\frac{|z|}{1+|x|}\right)\frac{1}{e^{\lambda|z|}|z|^{1+\alpha}}dz

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\int_{|z|>1+|x|}\log\left(1+\frac{|z|}{1+|x|}\right)\frac{1}{|z|^{1+\alpha}}dz

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\int_{|z|>1+|x|}\left(\frac{|z|}{1+|x|}\right)^{\beta}\frac{1}{|z|^{1+\alpha}}dz

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\int_{|z|>1}\frac{1}{|z|^{1+\alpha-\beta}}dz

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}.

∎

Next we will show the existence and uniqueness of strong solution for the nonlocal FPE (
2.11
).

B2: Schauder Estimate

In this subsection, we are interested in the absolutely continuous solution of the nonlocal FPE (
2.6
), which satisfies the time-dependent integro-differential equation (also called nonlocal FPE), i.e.,

\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(f(x)p(x,t)\right)+\mathcal{L}p(x,t),~~p(x,0)=g(x).

(2.11)

Superposition principle tells that the existence and uniqueness of the weak solution for equation (
2.11
).
To obtain the existence and uniqueness of the strong solution, we need to establish the global Schauder estimate for the solution of (
2.11
).

Theorem 2.2
.

(Schauder estimate.)

Under Hypothesis
\bf{H.1}
and Hypothesis
\bf{H.2}
. Suppose that
\dive{f}\in C^{\beta}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}),g\in C^{(1\vee\alpha)+\beta}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}),
and that there exists a positive constant
C
, such that for the solution
p(x,t)
of nonlocal FPE (
2.11
) with
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
, it holds that

\|p(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T],C^{\alpha+\beta}_{b}\right)}\leq C\|g\|_{C^{(1\vee\alpha)+\beta}_{b}}.

(2.12)

Proof.

See Appendix I.
∎

Then by Theorem
2.2
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.1
.

(Existence and uniqueness of the solution for the nonlocal FPE.)
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
be fixed, then there exists a unique solution
u\in C_{b}^{\alpha+\beta}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the nonlocal FPE
(
2.11
), which also satisfies the Schauder estimate (
2.12
).

Proof.

Define a family of linear operators by
T_{\theta}=(1-\theta)T_{0}+\theta T_{1}
, where

\begin{split}T_{0}p_{\tau}&=p_{\tau}+\mathcal{L}^{*}p_{\tau}-f\nabla p_{\tau},\\
T_{1}p_{\tau}&=p_{\tau}+\mathcal{L}^{*}p_{\tau}-f\nabla p_{\tau}-\dive{f}\cdot p_{\tau}.\end{split}

Note that
T_{0}
is the case considered in
[
23
, Theorem 3]
, which maps
C_{b}^{\alpha+\beta}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
onto
C_{b}^{\beta}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The solvability of the nonlocal FPE
(
2.11
) is equivalent to the invertibility of the operator
T_{\theta}
. By the method of continuity, we obtain the required result.
∎

C: Numerical analysis

In this subsection, we devised a convergent finite difference method to simulate the nonlocal FPE (
2.11
).

C1: A convergent finite difference scheme

Consider the problem in two cases: one case is on a bounded standard domain
D=(-1,1)
with the absorbing condition, and the other is on the infinite domain
\mathbb{R}
. The absorbing condition is that the “partical”
X_{t}
disappears or is killed when
X_{t}
is outside a bounded domain
D
. So the probability density function
p(x,t)

of being outside of the bounded domain
D
is zero, i.e.,

p(x,t)=0,\;\;\;x\notin(-1,1).

(2.13)

In fact, by the following transformation

\displaystyle\tilde{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{2x-(a+b)}{b-a},~~\tilde{y}=\frac{2y}{b-a},

(2.14)

\displaystyle u(\tilde{x},t)

\displaystyle=p\left(\frac{(b-a)\tilde{x}+(a+b)}{2},t\right).

We can also convert the finite interval
\widetilde{{D}}=(a,b)
into standard domain
{D}=(-1,1)
. Then the nonlocal FPE (
2.11
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u(\tilde{x},t)}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial\left(\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})u(\tilde{x},t)\right)}{\partial{{\tilde{x}}}}

(2.15)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\left[u(\tilde{x}+\tilde{y},t)-u(\tilde{x},t)-I_{\{|\tilde{y}|<\frac{2}{b-a}\}}(\tilde{y})\tilde{y}\frac{\partial u(\tilde{x},t)}{\partial\tilde{x}}\right]\frac{\widetilde{C}_{\alpha}}{e^{\tilde{\lambda}|\tilde{y}|}|\tilde{y}|^{1+\alpha}}d\tilde{y},~~\tilde{x}\in(-1,1),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{b-a}f\left(\frac{(b-a)\tilde{x}+a+b}{2}\right),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(\frac{b-a}{2})^{-\alpha}C_{\alpha},~~\tilde{\lambda}=\frac{b-a}{2}\lambda.

Next, the detailed procedure of the proposed algorithm will
be described as follows:

(i)
Step one is to introduce the following function,

\int_{s}^{\infty}x^{-\varrho}e^{-x}{\rm d}x=s^{-\frac{\varrho}{2}}e^{-\frac{s}{2}}W_{-\frac{\varrho}{2},\frac{1-\varrho}{2}}(s),\text{for}\;s>0,

(2.17)

where
W
is the Whittaker W function.

The principal value of integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}1_{\{|y|<1\}}(y)y\cdot\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial x}\nu(\mathrm{d}y)
vanishes. Thus the integral term of equation (
2.11
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\left[p(x+y,t)-p(x,t)\right]\nu(\mathrm{d}y)

(2.18)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\int_{-\infty}^{-1-x}\frac{p(x+y,t)-p(x,t)}{e^{\lambda|y|}|y|^{1+\alpha}}{\rm d}y+C_{\alpha}\int_{-1-x}^{1-x}\frac{p(x+y,t)-p(x,t)}{e^{\lambda|y|}|y|^{1+\alpha}}{\rm d}y+C_{\alpha}\int_{1-x}^{\infty}\frac{p(x+y,t)-p(x,t)}{e^{\lambda|y|}|y|^{1+\alpha}}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=-C_{\alpha}p(x,t)\left[W_{1}(x)+W_{2}(x)\right]+C_{\alpha}\int_{-1-x}^{1-x}\frac{p(x+y,t)-p(x,t)}{e^{\lambda|y|}|y|^{1+\alpha}}{\rm d}y,

where

\displaystyle W_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}(1+x)^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda(1+x)}{2}}W_{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2},-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\lambda(1+x)),

(2.19)

\displaystyle W_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}(1-x)^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda(1-x)}{2}}W_{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2},-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\lambda(1-x)).

(ii)
Step two is to divide the interval
[-2,2]
into
4J
subintervals and define
x_{j}=jh
for
-2J\leq j\leq 2J
integer, where
h=\frac{1}{J}
. Using central difference scheme for the first and two derivatives and modifying the “punched-hole” trapezoidal rule in the nonlocal term, we get the discretization scheme of (
2.11
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{d{P_{j}}}{dt}:=

\displaystyle C_{h}\frac{P_{j+1}-2P_{j}+P_{j-1}}{h^{2}}-[(fP)_{x,j}^{+}+(fP)_{x,j}^{-}]

(2.20)

\displaystyle-C_{\alpha}\left[W_{1}+W_{2}\right]P_{j}+C_{\alpha}h\sum\limits_{k=-J-j,k\neq 0}^{J-j}\!{{}^{\prime\prime}}\frac{P_{j+k}-P_{j}}{e^{\lambda|x_{k}|}|x_{k}|^{1+\alpha}},

where
C_{h}=-C_{\alpha}\zeta(\alpha-1)h^{2-\alpha}
,
(fP)_{x,j}^{+}
and
(fP)_{x,j}^{-}
are defined as the global Lax-Friedrichs flux splitting
[
24
]
,
i.e.,
(fP)^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(fP\pm MP)
with
M=\max{|f(x)|}
. Here
\sum^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
denotes the quantities corresponding to the two end summation
indices are multiplied by
1/2
.

It is noted that
for solving the initial value problem of (
2.11
) on the infinite domain
\mathbb{R}
, the semi-discrete equation becomes

\displaystyle\frac{d{P_{j}}}{dt}:=

\displaystyle C_{h}\frac{P_{j+1}-2P_{j}+P_{j-1}}{h^{2}}-[(fP)_{x,j}^{+}+(fP)_{x,j}^{-}]+C_{\alpha}h\sum\limits_{k=-J-j,k\neq 0}^{J-j}\!{{}^{\prime\prime}}\frac{P_{j+k}-P_{j}}{e^{\lambda|x_{k}|}|x_{k}|^{1+\alpha}},

(2.21)

where
J=\frac{\widetilde{L}}{h}
and
\widetilde{L}
is large enough so that the results are convergent.

(
iii
) Step three is to give the condition of numerical scheme satisfying the discrete maximum principle. For the absorbing boundary condition and forward Euler scheme for time derivative, the scheme (
2.20
)
satisfies the discrete maximum principle with
f=0
, if
\bigtriangleup t
and
h
satisfy the following condition,

\displaystyle\frac{\bigtriangleup t}{h^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{2C_{\alpha}[1+\frac{1}{\alpha}-\zeta(\alpha-1)]}.

(2.22)

(
iv
) Step four is to illustrate the convergence analysis. The numerical solution

of (
2.21
) converges to the analytic solution to (
2.11
) for
x_{j}
in
[-\tilde{L}/2,\tilde{L}/2]
when the refinement path satisfies (
2.22
) and the length of the integration interval

in (
2.21
) tends to
\infty
.

Application to a nonlinear filtering problem

The data assimilation method represented by nonlinear filtering has been widely used in many fields, which is a procedure to extract system state information by observations. Here we apply our aforementioned theoretical and numerical results to a nonlinear filtering system by simulating a nonlocal Zakai equation.

3.1
The strong form of Zakai equation

Consider the following signal-observation systems on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=f(X_{t})dt+dL_{t},\\
dY_{t}&=h(X_{t})dt+d{W_{t}},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.23)

where
f
is given deterministic measurable function and
h
is a bounded measurable continuous function.
W_{t}
is the standard Brownian motion, which is independent of
L_{t}
.

Let

\mathcal{Z}_{t}=\bf{{\sigma}}(Y_{s}:0\leq s\leq t)\vee\mathcal{N},

(3.24)

where
\mathcal{N}
is the collection of all
\mathbb{P}
-negligible sets of
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
.
Define
\mathcal{Z}=\sigma(\bigcup_{t}\mathcal{Z}_{t})
. By the version of Girsanov’s change of measure theorem, we obtain a new probability measure
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
, such that the
observation
Z_{t}
becomes
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-independent of the signal variables
(X_{t},Y_{t})
. This can be done through

\frac{d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\exp\left(-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\int_{0}^{t}{{h^{i}}({X_{s}})dW_{s}^{i}}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\int_{0}^{t}{{h^{i}}{{({X_{s}})}^{2}}}}ds\right).

(3.25)

For every bounded differentiable function
\varphi
, by the Kallianpur-Striebel formula, we have the following representation

\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_{t},Y_{t})|\mathcal{Z}_{t}]=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\varphi(X_{t},Y_{t})|\mathcal{Z}\right]}{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{t}|\mathcal{Z}\right]},

(3.26)

where

\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{t}=\left.\frac{d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}{d\mathbb{P}}\right|_{\mathcal{Z}_{t}}.

(3.27)

The unnormalized conditional distribution of
\varphi(X_{t})
, given
Y_{t}
, is defined as

{{P}_{t}}(\varphi)=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\varphi(X_{t},Y_{t})|\mathcal{Z}_{t}\right].

(3.28)

Heuristically, if the unconditional distribution of the signal
{P}_{t}(\varphi)
has a density
p(x,t)
with respect to Lebesgue measure for all
t>0
, i.e.,

{P}_{t}(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)p(x,t)dx,

(3.29)

then the unnormalized density
p(x,t)
satisfies the following Zakai equation.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Hypotheses
\bf{H1}
-
\bf{H2}
and
\bf{H3}
, the probability density function
p(x,t)
satisfies the following Zakai equation, i.e.,

dp(x,t)={{A}}^{*}p(x,t)dt+h(x)p(x,t)dY_{t},

(3.30)

where

{{A}}^{*}p(x,t)=-\nabla\cdot\left(f(x)p(x,t)\right)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\backslash\{0\}}\left[p(x+z,t)-p(x,t)\right]\nu(dz).

(3.31)

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
10
, Theorem 5]
.
∎

3.2
An example

Here we consider the gradient system as the signal system with time-independent bistable potential, which describes the evolution of physical phenomena in ideal fluctuating environments.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the superposition principle and numerical analysis for the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation associated with a
stochastic dynamical system with tempered stable Lévy noise. Firstly, we have shown the superposition principle for probability measure-valued solution to a nonlocal Fokker Planck equation, which yields the equivalence between martingale problem for the stochastic dynamical system with tempered stable noise and the corresponding nonlocal parabolic equation. Secondly, we have derived a global parabolic Schauder estimate for the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation under the condition
\alpha+\beta>1
. This leads to the existence and uniqueness have been obtained in the
L^{\infty}\left(\left[0,T\right],C^{\alpha+\beta}_{b}\right)
. Thirdly, we have constructed a finite difference scheme to simulate the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation. Under a specific condition, the scheme has been shown to satisfy the discrete maximum principle and to be convergent. Finally, we have applied the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation to obtain the strong form of Zakai equation and conduct its numerical assimilation. The results established in this paper can be used to examine dynamical behaviors for financial markets, climate dynamics and physics.

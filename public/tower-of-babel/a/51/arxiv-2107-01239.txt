Extensions of symmetric operators that are invariant under scaling and applications to indicial operators
Thomas Krainer

Abstract
. Indicial operators are model operators associated to an elliptic differential operator near a corner singularity on a stratified manifold. These model operators are defined on generalized tangent cone configurations and exhibit a natural scaling invariance property with respect to dilations of the radial variable. In this paper we discuss extensions of symmetric indicial operators from a functional analytic point of view. In the first, purely abstract part of this paper, we consider a general unbounded symmetric operator that exhibits invariance with respect to an abstract scaling action on a Hilbert space, and we describe its extensions in terms of generalized eigenspaces of the infinitesimal generator of this action. Among others, we obtain a Green formula for the adjoint pairing, an algebraic formula for the signature, and in the semibounded case explicit descriptions of the Friedrichs and Krein extensions. In the second part we consider differential operators of Fuchs type on the half axis with unbounded operator coefficients that are invariant under dilation, and show that under suitable ellipticity assumptions on the indicial family these operators fit into the abstract framework of the first part, which in this case furnishes a description of extensions in terms of polyhomogeneous asymptotic expansions. We also obtain an analytic formula for the signature of the adjoint pairing in terms of the spectral flow of the indicial family for such operators.

Preliminaries

\displaystyle=A^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{\max}\subset H\to H,

\displaystyle=\overline{A}:\mathcal{D}_{\min}\subset H\to H,

\displaystyle A_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle=A^{*}\Big|_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\subset H\to H

for these operators. When the domain is not explicitly specified we will simply write

. While this does not align with standard functional analytic conventions, it is consistent with defining extensions of (formally) symmetric differential operators

Lemma 2.1
.

The group action
\kappa_{\varrho}:H\to H

\kappa_{\varrho}:\mathcal{D}_{\max}\to\mathcal{D}_{\max}

Let

. Consequently, if

, then

, and

This proves (
2.2
). We next prove the strong continuity of
\kappa_{\varrho}
on
\mathcal{D}_{\max}
. To that end, let

be arbitrary. Then
\kappa_{\varrho}v\to v
in

on

\varrho\to 1
in

\kappa_{\varrho}
on

. Both combined now imply that

, proving strong continuity of

on
\mathcal{D}_{\max}
. For

\displaystyle=\|\kappa_{\varrho}u\|_{H}^{2}+\|A_{\max}\kappa_{\varrho}u\|_{H}^{2}=\|\kappa_{\varrho}u\|_{H}^{2}+\varrho^{2m}\|\kappa_{\varrho}A_{\max}u\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varrho^{2m}\|A_{\max}u\|_{H}^{2}\leq\max\{1,\varrho^{m}\}^{2}\|u\|_{A}^{2}

, but we choose to give a direct proof here.

We first show the claim regarding
m_{0}
. To that end, we are going to prove that if
\hat{v}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}
such that
[\hat{u},\hat{v}]_{\ell}=0
for all
\hat{u}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}
then
\hat{v}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}+\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}
, and vice versa. Indeed, if

[\hat{u},\hat{v}]_{\ell}=[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell-1}\hat{u},\hat{v}]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}=[\hat{u},(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell-1}\hat{v}]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}=0

for all
\hat{u}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}
, then
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell-1}\hat{v}\in\bigl[\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}\bigr]^{[\perp]}=\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}
. Consequently, there exists
\hat{w}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}
such that
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell-1}\hat{v}=(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell}\hat{w}
, so
\hat{v}-(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)\hat{w}=\hat{u}_{0}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}
. Thus

\hat{v}=\hat{u}_{0}+(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)\hat{w}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}+\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}.

Conversely, if

\hat{v}=\hat{u}_{0}+(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)\hat{w}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}+\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell},

then
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell-1}\hat{v}=(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell}\hat{w}
and so

[\hat{u},\hat{v}]_{\ell}=[\hat{u},(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell-1}\hat{v}]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}=[\hat{u},(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell}\hat{w}]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}=[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{\ell}\hat{u},\hat{w}]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}=0

for all
\hat{u}\in\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}
. Now

\dim\bigl[\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}\bigr]=\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell+1}-\dim\ker(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)

as
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma:\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell+1}\to\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}
is surjective. So

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=\dim\bigl[\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}+\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}+\dim\bigl[\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\dim\bigl[\ran\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell+1}-\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}+\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle m_{+}+m_{-}

\displaystyle=\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}-m_{0}

\displaystyle=\bigl[\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}-\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell-1}\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\bigl[\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell+1}-\dim\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\textup{Number of Jordan blocks of size $\ell\times\ell$ for the eigenvalue $\sigma$.}

Now apply the Canonical Form Theorem from
[
26
, Theorem 5.1.1]
as previously described, and write

\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{m_{+}+m_{-}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}^{\ell}_{\sigma,j}\subset\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\ell},

where this direct sum is orthogonal with respect to
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
, and the
\hat{\mathscr{E}}^{\ell}_{\sigma,j}
are associated to
\ell\times\ell
Jordan blocks for
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
with eigenvalue
\sigma
. Moreover, let
\hat{u}_{j,1},\ldots,\hat{u}_{j,\ell}
be a Jordan basis of
\hat{\mathscr{E}}^{\ell}_{\sigma,j}
for each
j=1,\ldots,(m_{+}+m_{-})
as described in the canonical form, such that for each
j
we either have
[\hat{u}_{j,\ell},\hat{u}_{j,\ell}]_{\ell}=1
or
[\hat{u}_{j,\ell},\hat{u}_{j,\ell}]_{\ell}=-1
. Let

\displaystyle U_{+}

\displaystyle=\Span\{\hat{u}_{j,\ell};\;[\hat{u}_{j,\ell},\hat{u}_{j,\ell}]_{\ell}=1\},

\displaystyle U_{-}

\displaystyle=\Span\{\hat{u}_{j,\ell};\;[\hat{u}_{j,\ell},\hat{u}_{j,\ell}]_{\ell}=-1\}.

Because
[\hat{u}_{j,\ell},\hat{u}_{k,\ell}]_{\ell}=0
for
j\neq k
we then have
[\hat{u},\hat{u}]_{\ell}>0
for all
0\neq\hat{u}\in U_{+}
, and
[\hat{u},\hat{u}]_{\ell}<0
for all
0\neq\hat{u}\in U_{-}
. Thus
m_{\pm}\geq\dim U_{\pm}
, but because
m_{+}+m_{-}=\dim U_{+}+\dim U_{-}
we have
m_{\pm}=\dim U_{\pm}
, proving the claim about the sign characteristic associated to Jordan blocks of size
\ell\times\ell
for the eigenvalue
\sigma
.
∎

In the sequel we will also write

and

for the invariants of the Hermitian sesquilinear form (

) when the context warrants specific reference to the eigenvalue

Theorem 3.9
.

(1)

The signature of
\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)
is given by

\sig\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)-m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)\bigr).

In particular, the operator
A
admits selfadjoint extensions
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
if and only if

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)-m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)\bigr)=0.

(2)

The operator
A
admits an invariant selfadjoint extensions if and only if

\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)-m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)\bigr)=0

for every
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
(0,M_{+},M_{-})
be the invariants of the form
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
. We have

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\;\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}}\bigr],

where this direct sum is orthogonal with respect to
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
, and the restriction of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
to each of the direct summands is nondegenerate by the Canonical Form Theorem
[
26
, Theorem 5.1.1]
. Consequently,

M_{+}-M_{-}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl(M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})\bigr)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl(M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}})\bigr),

where
M_{\pm}(\hat{U})
are the invariants of the restriction
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}:\hat{U}\times\hat{U}\to\mathbb{C}
to the indicated subspace
\hat{U}
. For each
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}
the Canonical Form Theorem implies that
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)
is Lagrangian, and thus

M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}})=0.

For
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
we prove next that

M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})=\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)-m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)\bigr).

By the Canonical Form Theorem we have

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{m_{\sigma}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}

which is orthogonal with respect to
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
, the restriction of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
to each of the direct summands is nondegenerate, and each
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}
is associated to a Jordan block that either contributes a
+1
or a
-1
to the sign characteristic. We have

M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m_{\sigma}}\bigl(M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j})\bigr).

If
\dim\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}
is even and spanned by
\{\hat{u}_{1},\ldots,\hat{u}_{2k}\}
with either (
3.5
) or (
3.6
), then

\hat{U}=\Span\{\hat{u}_{1},\ldots,\hat{u}_{k}\}\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)

is Lagrangian and consequently
M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j})=0
. If
\dim\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}
is odd and spanned by
\{\hat{u}_{1},\ldots,\hat{u}_{2k+1}\}
with either (
3.5
) or (
3.6
), then

M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j})=\begin{cases}1&\textup{in case of \eqref{PlusEins}},\\
-1&\textup{in case of \eqref{MinusEins}}.\end{cases}

This is because the matrix
\bigl(\delta_{\mu,2k+2-\nu}\bigr)_{\mu,\nu=1}^{2k+1}
has
(k+1)
-times the eigenvalue
+1
and
k
-times the eigenvalue
-1
. Consequently,

M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})=\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)-m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)\bigr)

as desired, proving the first claim.

We next prove the second claim regarding the existence of invariant selfadjoint extensions. Note that
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
is an invariant selfadjoint extension if and only if
\hat{U}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)
from (
3.1
) is Lagrangian and invariant under
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. We thus have to show that invariant Lagrangian subspaces exist if and only if

\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)-m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)\bigr)=0

holds for every
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
.

Suppose first that
\hat{U}\subset\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}
is Lagrangian and invariant under
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then

\displaystyle\hat{U}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]\;\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl(\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]\oplus\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]\bigr).

Every subspace

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]

\displaystyle\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr),\quad\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2},

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]\oplus\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]

\displaystyle\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr),\quad\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2},

is isotropic, and for dimensional reasons must be Lagrangian. In particular, for every
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
we must have

M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})=\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)-m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)\bigr)=0.

Conversely, assume that
M_{+}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})-M_{-}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma})=0
for every
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
. We proceed to prove that each space admits a Lagrangian subspace
\hat{U}_{\sigma}\subset\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}
that is invariant under
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then

\hat{U}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{U}_{\sigma}\;\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}

is a Lagrangian subspace of
\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)
that is invariant under
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
as desired. Write

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{m_{\sigma}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}

according to the Canonical Form Theorem with mutually
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
-orthogonal direct summands, where each

is associated to a single Jordan block, and pick bases for each
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}
with either (
3.5
) or (
3.6
). By assumption, the odd-dimensional blocks (if any) equally distribute among the signs
+1
and
-1
. Now divide the odd-dimensional spaces
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}
up in pairs of the form
(\hat{V},\hat{W})
, where
\hat{V}
contributes
+1
and
\hat{W}
contributes
-1
, and set

\hat{U}_{(\hat{V},\hat{W})}=\Span\Bigl\{\hat{v}_{\frac{\dim\hat{V}+1}{2}}+\hat{w}_{\frac{\dim\hat{W}+1}{2}}\Bigr\},

where the two vectors
\hat{v}_{\frac{\dim\hat{V}+1}{2}}
and
\hat{w}_{\frac{\dim\hat{W}+1}{2}}
are the middle vectors in the Jordan bases for
\hat{V}
and
\hat{W}

\hat{U}_{\sigma}=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{m_{\sigma}}\Bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}\cap\ker\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{\bigl\lfloor\frac{\dim\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}}{2}\bigr\rfloor}\Bigr]\;\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{(\hat{V},\hat{W})}\hat{U}_{(\hat{V},\hat{W})}.

Then
\hat{U}_{\sigma}

4.
Semibounded operators and the Friedrichs extension

Suppose that
A_{\min}
is semibounded from below in the sense that there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\langle Au,u\rangle\geq c\langle u,u\rangle

holds for all
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}
. By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
4.1
below we in fact must have
A_{\min}\geq 0
which we are going to assume for the remainder of this section. Since we assume
A_{\min}
to have finite deficiency indices, we note that every selfadjoint extension
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
is semibounded from below, see
[
11
, Theorem 9.3.7]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\subset H\to H
be invariant and bounded from below, i.e.,

\langle Au,u\rangle\geq c\langle u,u\rangle

for all
u\in\mathcal{D}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

\inf\limits_{0\neq u\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\langle Au,u\rangle}{\langle u,u\rangle}=0,

i.e.,
A_{\mathcal{D}}
has lower bound
0
.

Proof.

Let

L=\inf\limits_{0\neq u\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\langle Au,u\rangle}{\langle u,u\rangle}.

For
0\neq u\in\mathcal{D}
we have

\langle Au,u\rangle=\varrho^{m}\langle\kappa_{\varrho}A_{\mathcal{D}}\kappa_{\varrho}^{-1}u,u\rangle=\varrho^{m}\langle A_{\mathcal{D}}\kappa_{\varrho}^{-1}u,\kappa_{\varrho}^{-1}u\rangle,

and thus

\frac{\langle A_{\mathcal{D}}u,u\rangle}{\langle u,u\rangle}=\varrho^{m}\frac{\langle A_{\mathcal{D}}\kappa_{\varrho}^{-1}u,\kappa_{\varrho}^{-1}u\rangle}{\langle\kappa_{\varrho}^{-1}u,\kappa_{\varrho}^{-1}u\rangle},

using the fact that
\kappa_{\varrho}
is unitary. Consequently, passing to the infimum over all
0\neq u\in\mathcal{D}
on both sides, we get
L=\varrho^{m}L
for all
\varrho>0
. This leaves only
L=0
or
L=-\infty
, and since
L>-\infty
by assumption we must have
L=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\hookrightarrow H
be the completion of
\mathcal{D}_{\min}
with respect to the norm

|u|_{F}^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle+\langle Au,u\rangle,\;u\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}.

Then
\kappa_{\varrho}
restricts to a strongly continuous group action
\kappa_{\varrho}\in\mathscr{L}({\mathcal{H}}_{F})
with

\|\kappa_{\varrho}\|_{\mathscr{L}({\mathcal{H}}_{F})}\leq\max\{1,\varrho^{m/2}\},\;\varrho>0.

In particular, the Friedrichs extension

\displaystyle A_{F}

\displaystyle=A^{*}\Big|_{\mathcal{D}_{F}}:\mathcal{D}_{F}\subset H\to H,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{F}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\max}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{F},

is invariant.

Proof.

We show that

\kappa_{\varrho}:\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\min},|\cdot|_{F}\bigr)\to\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\min},|\cdot|_{F}\bigr)

is continuous, and consequently extends by continuity to a bounded operator in
\mathscr{L}({\mathcal{H}}_{F})
. Because
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\hookrightarrow H
and
\kappa_{\varrho}\in\mathscr{L}(H)
this bounded extension is necessarily the restriction of
\kappa_{\varrho}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
. Indeed, for
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}
we have

\displaystyle|\kappa_{\varrho}u|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\kappa_{\varrho}u,\kappa_{\varrho}u\rangle+\langle A\kappa_{\varrho}u,\kappa_{\varrho}u\rangle=\langle u,u\rangle+\varrho^{m}\langle\kappa_{\varrho}Au,\kappa_{\varrho}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,u\rangle+\varrho^{m}\langle Au,u\rangle\leq\max\{1,\varrho^{m}\}\cdot|u|_{F}^{2},

proving the desired continuity as well as the asserted norm estimate. This implies that
A_{F}
is invariant.

It remains to show the strong continuity of
\kappa_{\varrho}
on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
. Because
\kappa_{\varrho}
is a group and
\{\kappa_{\varrho};\;\frac{1}{2}\leq\varrho\leq 2\}\subset\mathscr{L}({\mathcal{H}}_{F})
is bounded, it suffices to show that
\kappa_{\varrho}u\to u
with respect to
|\cdot|_{F}
as
\varrho\to 1
just for
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}
(see
[
20
, I.5.3]
). Because
\kappa_{\varrho}u\to u
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{H}
we thus have to prove that

\langle A(\kappa_{\varrho}u-u),\kappa_{\varrho}u-u\rangle_{H}\to 0\textup{ as }\varrho\to 1.

But this follows because
A(\kappa_{\varrho}u-u)=\varrho^{m}\kappa_{\varrho}Au-Au\to 0
as
\varrho\to 1
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{H}
.
∎

The restriction
\kappa_{\varrho}:{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
of the group action is generated by the part of the generator
\mathfrak{g}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, i.e., the operator that acts like
\mathfrak{g}
with domain

\{u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{F};\;\mathfrak{g}u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\},

see
[
20
, II.2.3]
. The norm estimate for
\kappa_{\varrho}
in Proposition
4.2
, both for
1\leq\varrho<\infty
and for
0<\varrho\leq 1
, in conjunction with the Hille-Yosida Generation Theorem
[
20
, II.3]
now implies that for every
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Im(\sigma)\notin[-m/2,0]
and every
v\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
the unique solution
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
to the equation
(\mathfrak{g}-\sigma)u=v
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
. This is in addition to the properties for
\mathfrak{g}
previously discussed in Remark
2.4
.

Our next goal is to give an explicit description of the domain of the Friedrichs extension
A_{F}
in terms of the generalized eigenspaces of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. In this context, consider the following definition derived from
[
26
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that
A
satisfies the
sign condition
if for every
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
the following two conditions hold:

•

m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)=m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)=0
for all odd
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
does not have odd-sized Jordan blocks associated with the eigenvalue
\sigma
.

•

Either

m_{+}(\sigma,\ell)=0

or

m_{-}(\sigma,\ell)=0
for all even
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus the sign characteristic associated with the eigenvalue
\sigma
is entirely negative or positive for all Jordan blocks of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Here
m_{\pm}(\sigma,\ell)
are the invariants of the Hermitian sesquilinear form (
3.7
), see Proposition
3.8
. Note that the sign condition is trivially fulfilled if
\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cap\{\sigma\in\mathbb{C};\;\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\}=\emptyset
.

We note that the sign condition holds for semibounded indicial operators considered in the later sections, see Theorem
9.2
.

If the sign condition holds,
[
26
, Theorem 5.12.4]
implies that for each
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
there exists a unique Langrangian subspace
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)
that is invariant under
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
.
More precisely, write

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{m_{\sigma}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}

according to the Canonical Form Theorem with mutually
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}
-orthogonal direct summands, where each
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}
is associated to a single Jordan block of size
(2n_{j})\times(2n_{j})
. Then

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{m_{\sigma}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,j}\cap\ker(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma)^{n_{j}}\bigr],

so
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
is spanned by the first halves of the Jordan basis elements associated to each Jordan block in the canonical form for the triple
\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}},\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}=\{\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max};\;\hat{u}=u+\mathcal{D}_{\min},\;u\in\mathcal{D}_{F}\}
. Then

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}\bigr]\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}.

If
A
satisfies the sign condition, then
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
for all
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
the Friedrichs extension is invariant. This implies that
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
is invariant under
\hat{\kappa}_{\varrho}
, and consequently
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
is also invariant under the generator
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}=\bigoplus\limits_{\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}\bigr].

We will next prove that if
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}
, and
\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}
with
\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
, then
\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
. Indeed, by assumption there exists
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\max}\cap\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
with
\hat{u}=u+\mathcal{D}_{\min}
, and
v\in\mathcal{D}_{F}
such that
\bigl(\mathfrak{g}-\sigma\bigr)u=v
. Because
v\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
we have
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, and thus
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\max}=\mathcal{D}_{F}
, so
\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
as stated. Consequently, if
\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}-\sigma\bigr)^{k}\hat{u}=0
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
, showing that
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\subset\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
for
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}
. Because the adjoint pairing

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}:\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\times\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}}\to\mathbb{C}

is nondegenerate and
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
is Lagrangian we must necessarily have
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma^{\star}}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}=\{0\}
for
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}
.

Finally, note that
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)
is Lagrangian and invariant under
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
for
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
. Consequently, if
A
satisfies the sign condition, we must necessarily have
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
. The theorem is proved.
∎

5.
The Krein extension and the order relation for invariant selfadjoint extensions

We continue to assume that
A_{\min}\geq 0
. The Friedrichs extension
A_{F}
and the Krein extension
A_{K}
are distinguished in the sense that
A_{K}\leq A_{\mathcal{D}}\leq A_{F}
for all nonnegative selfadjoint extensions
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\geq 0
. Recall that for two selfadjoint operators
T_{j}=T_{j}^{*}\geq 0
the order relation
T_{1}\leq T_{2}
holds if and only if
\mathcal{D}(T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(T_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
\|T_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H}\leq\|T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H}
for
u\in\mathcal{D}(T_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}})
. The latter is an inequality for the quadratic forms associated with the
T_{j}
, while the domain of the nonnegative square root in each case coincides with the domain of the quadratic form.
We refer to
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
for information on the Krein-von Neumann extension (but note that
A_{\min}
is not strictly positive by Lemma
4.1
, which is compensated for by the invariance of
A
under scaling in our investigation).

Our goal in this section is to prove the following two theorems.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}=\{\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max};\;\hat{u}=u+\mathcal{D}_{\min},\;u\in\mathcal{D}_{K}=\mathcal{D}(A_{K})\}
. Then

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}\bigr]\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}.

If
A
satisfies the sign condition, then
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
for all
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
A
satisfies the sign condition, and let

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{-}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}.

(1)

For every subspace
\hat{U}\subset\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{-}
that is invariant under
\hat{\kappa}_{\varrho}
there exists a unique selfadjoint invariant extension
A_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\subset H\to H
of
A
such that
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{-}=\hat{U}
.

(2)

Let
A_{\mathcal{D}_{j}}=A_{\mathcal{D}_{j}}^{*}
,
j=1,2
, be invariant selfadjoint extensions. Then
A_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\leq A_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}
if and only if
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{-}\subseteq\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{-}
, or equivalently if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}
.

We note that the first part of Theorem
5.2
is just
[
26
, Theorem 5.12.4]
when applied to the present context.

Given
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\geq 0
we let
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}\hookrightarrow H
be the domain of the quadratic form associated with
A_{\mathcal{D}}
. We have
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}(A_{\mathcal{D}}^{\frac{1}{2}})
, which equivalently can be described as the completion of
\mathcal{D}
with respect to the norm

|u|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle_{H}+\langle A_{\mathcal{D}}u,u\rangle_{H},\;u\in\mathcal{D}.

Because
|u|_{\mathcal{D}}=|u|_{F}
for
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}
the embedding
\bigl({\mathcal{H}}_{F},|\cdot|_{F}\bigr)\hookrightarrow\bigl({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{D}}\bigr)
is an isometry. Moreover, the codimension of
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is finite, where more precisely
\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}/{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}
, see
[
11
, Theorem 10.3.7]
. We write
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
for the domain of the quadratic form associated with the Krein extension
A_{K}=A_{K}^{*}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\geq 0
. Then
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}+{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, and this sum is direct modulo
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}
.

Proof.

Because
\mathcal{D}\subset\bigl({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{D}}\bigr)
is dense we have that
\bigl(\mathcal{D}+{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\bigr)/{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}/{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
is dense, and by finite dimensionality we must have
\bigl(\mathcal{D}+{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\bigr)/{\mathcal{H}}_{F}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}/{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
. This shows that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}+{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, and because
\mathcal{D}_{\max}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{F}=\mathcal{D}_{F}
the sum is direct modulo
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

The Krein extension
A_{K}:\mathcal{D}_{K}\subset H\to H
is invariant.

Proof.

This follows from a more general result by Makarov and Tsekanovskii
[
42
]
, but we give an independent proof here. We have
A_{K}=A^{*}\Big|_{\mathcal{D}_{K}}
, where the domain of the Krein extension is given by

\mathcal{D}_{K}=\{u\in\mathcal{D}_{\max};\;\exists u_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}:(A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k})_{k}\subset H\textup{ is Cauchy},\;\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}A_{\min}u_{k}=A_{\max}u\},

see Ando and Nishio
[
9
]
. We need to show that if
u\in\mathcal{D}_{K}
, then also
\kappa_{\varrho}u\in\mathcal{D}_{K}
. Let
u_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}
such that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}A_{\min}u_{k}=A_{\max}u
, and such that
(A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k})_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H
. Now
\kappa_{\varrho}u_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}
, and we have

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}A_{\min}\kappa_{\varrho}u_{k}=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\varrho^{m}\kappa_{\varrho}A_{\min}u_{k}=\varrho^{m}\kappa_{\varrho}A_{\max}u=A_{\max}\kappa_{\varrho}u.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{\varrho}u_{k}-A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{\varrho}u_{l}\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{\varrho}(u_{k}-u_{l}),A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{\varrho}(u_{k}-u_{l})\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{\min}\kappa_{\varrho}(u_{k}-u_{l}),\kappa_{\varrho}(u_{k}-u_{l})\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle\varrho^{m}\kappa_{\varrho}A_{\min}(u_{k}-u_{l}),\kappa_{\varrho}(u_{k}-u_{l})\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\varrho^{m}\langle A_{\min}(u_{k}-u_{l}),u_{k}-u_{l}\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\varrho^{m}\|A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k}-A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{l}\|_{H}^{2},

and thus
(A_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{\varrho}u_{k})\subset H
is Cauchy. This shows that
\kappa_{\varrho}u\in\mathcal{D}_{K}
and finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cap\{\sigma\in\mathbb{C};\;\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\}=\emptyset
. Then the inclusion map
\mathcal{D}_{\max}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
is well-defined and continuous. We have
\mathcal{D}_{\max}=\mathcal{D}_{K}+\mathcal{D}_{F}
, and this sum is direct modulo
\mathcal{D}_{\min}
.

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{D}_{F}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{D}_{\min}\subset\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\max}
be such that

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}.

Then
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
is selfadjoint by the Canonical Form Theorem and our present assumption that
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
does not have eigenvalues on
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
. Moreover,
A_{\mathcal{D}}
is also invariant, and therefore
A_{\mathcal{D}}\geq 0
by Lemma
4.1
. Thus
A_{K}\leq A_{\mathcal{D}}
by the fundamental property of the Krein extension which shows that
\mathcal{D}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
. Consequently,
\mathcal{D}_{F}+\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{\max}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
. Continuity of the embedding
\mathcal{D}_{\max}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
follows from the Closed Graph Theorem.

By Lemma
5.3
, every
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\max}
now has a representation
u=u_{K}+\tilde{u}
with
u_{K}\in\mathcal{D}_{K}
and
\tilde{u}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
. But
\tilde{u}=u-u_{K}\in\mathcal{D}_{\max}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{F}=\mathcal{D}_{F}
, which shows that
\mathcal{D}_{\max}=\mathcal{D}_{K}+\mathcal{D}_{F}
. We thus have
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}+\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
, and for dimensional reasons this sum must be direct. The lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We first consider the case that

\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cap\{\sigma\in\mathbb{C};\;\Im(\sigma)=-\tfrac{m}{2}\}=\emptyset.

By Lemma
5.5
we have a direct sum
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
. Now let

\hat{u}\in\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\subset\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}

be arbitrary, and write
\hat{u}=\hat{u}_{K}+\hat{u}_{F}
with
\hat{u}_{K}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}
and
\hat{u}_{F}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{F}
. Then

\hat{u}_{K}=\hat{u}+(-\hat{u}_{F})\in\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\Biggr]\oplus\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\Biggr],

see Theorem
4.4
, but because
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}
is invariant under
\hat{\kappa}_{\varrho}
by Lemma
5.4
we have

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}=\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}\bigr]\Biggr]\oplus\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}\bigr]\Biggr].

Consequently both
\hat{u},\,\hat{u}_{F}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}
, and so necessarily
\hat{u}_{F}=0
and
\hat{u}=\hat{u}_{K}\in\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}
. Thus

\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\subset\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K},

and for dimensional reasons these two spaces must be equal which proves the theorem in the present case.

We next consider the general case. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\min}\subset\mathcal{D}_{0}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\max}
be such that

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}\bigr],

and consider the operator
B_{\min}=A_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}:\mathcal{D}_{0}\subset H\to H
. Note that
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\mathcal{D}(B_{\min})
is invariant under
\kappa_{\varrho}
,
B_{\min}
is symmetric, and satisfies all the general assumptions imposed on
A_{\min}
. We have
B_{\max}=A_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{*}
, where

\mathcal{D}(A_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{*})/\mathcal{D}_{\min}(A)=\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\Biggr]\oplus\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\Biggr]\oplus\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}

by the Canonical Form Theorem, and consequently

\mathcal{D}(B_{\max})/\mathcal{D}(B_{\min})\cong\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\Biggr]\oplus\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\Biggr]

is the spectral decomposition of the quotient
\mathcal{D}(B_{\max})/\mathcal{D}(B_{\min})
associated with the generator of the induced group action. By what we have shown above, the Krein extension
B_{K}
of
B_{\min}
has domain given by

\mathcal{D}(B_{K})/\mathcal{D}(B_{\min})\cong\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma},

and we have
A_{K}=B_{K}
as both extend
A_{\min}
and
B_{\min}
, and are each minimal among the nonnegative selfadjoint extensions of these operators. In conclusion,

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}\bigr]\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}

as desired. Lastly, if
A
satisfies the sign condition, we necessarily must have
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{K}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
for
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
because
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}\subset\bigl(\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}}\bigr)
is the unique Lagrangian subspace that is invariant under
\hat{\kappa}_{\varrho}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

For every invariant extension
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
we have

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}\bigr]\Biggr]\oplus\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}\bigr]\Biggr]\oplus\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}\bigr]\Biggr],

and because
A
satisfies the sign condition we have
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
for every
\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
. Thus
\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
is part of every invariant selfadjoint extension. Consequently, by replacing the minimal extension
A_{\min}
with
A_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
if necessary, where
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}
, we may without loss of generality assume that

\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cap\{\sigma\in\mathbb{C};\;\Im(\sigma)=-\tfrac{m}{2}\}=\emptyset.

As previously mentioned, the first part of Theorem
5.2
follows from
[
26
, Theorem 5.12.4]
. It is in fact an immediate consequence of the Canonical Form Theorem. Given an invariant subspace
\hat{U}\subset\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{-}
as stated in the assumptions, the domain
\mathcal{D}
of the unique selfadjoint extension with
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}\cap\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{-}=\hat{U}
must necessarily be

\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)>-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{U}^{[\perp]}\bigr]\Biggr]\oplus\Biggl[\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\spec(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\\
\Im(\sigma)<-\frac{m}{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}\cap\hat{U}\bigr]\Biggr].

We now proceed to prove the second part of the theorem. Let
A_{\mathcal{D}}=A_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
be invariant. According to Lemma
5.3
we have
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}+{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, and because
\mathcal{D}
is invariant we conclude from Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
5.1
that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{D}_{K}+{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, and this sum is direct modulo
\mathcal{D}_{\min}
. We also note that
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{D}_{K}\subset\bigl({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{D}}\bigr)
is dense. Consequently, for invariant extensions
A_{\mathcal{D}_{j}}=A_{\mathcal{D}_{j}}^{*}
,
j=1,2
, we have
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{2}\cap\mathcal{D}_{K}\subset\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{K}
, which by the Canonical Form Theorem is equivalent to
\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}\subset\mathcal{D}_{2}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}
. Moreover, whenever
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
the embedding
\bigl({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}\bigr)\hookrightarrow\bigl({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\bigr)
is continuous by the Closed Graph Theorem, and
\|A_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H}=\|A_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H}
for
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}
. To see the latter norm identity note that it is true for
u\in\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{2}\supset\mathcal{D}_{2}\cap\mathcal{D}_{K}
, which is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}
, and so it extends by continuity to all of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}
. Consequently,
A_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\leq A_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}\subset\mathcal{D}_{2}\cap\mathcal{D}_{F}
. The theorem is proved.
∎

6.
Indicial operators

In the remaining sections we will discuss applications of the previous results to indicial operators. As discussed in the introduction, indicial operators arise as model operators associated with singularities of corner type. Taking an operator theoretical point of view, we consider indicial operators of the form

A=x^{-m}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\mu}a_{j}(xD_{x})^{j}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\subset L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\to L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0}),

(6.1)

where
m,\mu\in\mathbb{N}
and
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
are Hilbert spaces such that
E_{1}\hookrightarrow E_{0}
is continuous and dense, and the operators
a_{j}:E_{1}\to E_{0}
are continuous for
j=0,\ldots,\mu
. The (vector-valued) space
L^{2}_{b}
is the
L^{2}
-space with respect to the Haar measure
\frac{dx}{x}
on the half-line. The
indicial family
of
A

p(\sigma)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\mu}a_{j}\sigma^{j}:E_{1}\to E_{0},\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{C}

is a holomorphic family of Fredholm operators in
\mathscr{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
and satisfies suitable ellipticity assumptions, detailed below, and when considered an unbounded operator acting in
E_{0}
with domain
E_{1}
we require

p(\sigma^{\star})^{*}=p(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}

to hold for the Hilbert space adjoints for each
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
, where
\sigma^{\star}=\overline{\sigma}-im
denotes reflection about the line
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
, see also (

for

in view of
u=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1})\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0}))u
. On the other hand, with
q(\sigma)
from Lemma

we have

where

and
p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})l(\sigma+i\gamma)\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(E_{0}))
, and therefore

\|u\|_{{\mathscr{H}}}=\|\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0}))u\|_{H}\lesssim\|\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma))u\|_{H}+\|u\|_{H}

for

. This proves the first part of the proposition.

To prove the claim about the adjoint we consider

{\mathscr{P}}_{\gamma}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}0&p(\sigma+i\gamma)\\

\oplus\\

\oplus\\
E_{0}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{c}E_{0}\\
\oplus\\

Then
{\mathscr{P}}_{\gamma}(\sigma)={\mathscr{P}}_{\gamma}(\overline{\sigma})^{\star}
by (
A-2
), and

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}({\mathscr{P}}_{\gamma}):\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\\
\oplus\\
{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\end{array}\subset L^{2}_{b}\biggl(\mathbb{R}_{+};\begin{array}[]{c}E_{0}\\

E_{0}\end{array}\biggr)\to L^{2}_{b}\biggl(\mathbb{R}_{+};\begin{array}[]{c}E_{0}\\
\oplus\\

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}({\mathscr{P}}_{\gamma})\pm i:\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\\
\oplus\\

are invertible for all

for large

We next prove that the space
{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
allows localization and microlocalization and has good approximation properties. The latter is based on the following familiar continuity criterion for the strong operator topology in
L^{2}_{b}
-spaces:

Let
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))
denote the space of global operator-valued Mellin symbols of order
0
, where
F
and
G
and Hilbert spaces, i.e.,
a(x,\sigma)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))
belongs to
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))
if and only if for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
independent of
(x,\sigma)
such that

\|(xD_{x})^{\alpha}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta}a(x,\sigma)\|_{\mathscr{L}(F,G)}\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\sigma\rangle^{-\beta}.

The best constants in these estimates give rise to the seminorms for the symbol topology on
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))
.

If
\{a_{j}(x,\sigma)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))
is a sequence that is bounded in the symbol topology and converges pointwise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
to
a(x,\sigma)\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))
, then
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})\to\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)
strongly as
j\to\infty
, i.e.,
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u\to\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)u
in
L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};G)
as
j\to\infty
for every
u\in L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};F)
.

We quickly review the steps of the proof of this criterion (without loss of generality assume that
a(x,\sigma)\equiv 0
):

•

Let
u\in\mathscr{T}(\mathbb{R}_{+};F)
(recall that this means that
u(e^{t})\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R};F)
):

–

From the boundedness of the
\{a_{j}(x,\sigma)\}
and dominated convergence we first get that
[\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u](x)\to 0
in
G
pointwise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

–

As
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))\ni a\mapsto\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)u\in\mathscr{T}(\mathbb{R}_{+};G)
is continuous we can bound any continuous seminorm of
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u
on
\mathscr{T}(\mathbb{R}_{+};G)
by a constant independent of
j
. This observation gives rise to an integrable majorant of the form
F(x)=K\langle\log x\rangle^{-2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\frac{dx}{x})
such that
\|\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u(x)\|^{2}_{G}\leq F(x)
, and in conclusion
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u\to 0
in
L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};G)
as
j\to\infty
by dominated convergence.

•

Continuity of the map

S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(F,G))\ni a\mapsto\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)\in\mathscr{L}(L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};F),L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};G))

shows that
\{\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\mathscr{L}(L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};F),L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};G))
, and
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u\to 0
as
j\to\infty
for
u
in the dense subspace
\mathscr{T}(\mathbb{R}_{+};F)
. Thus
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})\to 0
strongly as
j\to\infty
.

The relevant application of this criterion for us are mollifiers in the case
F=G
and scalar symbols
a_{j}(x,\sigma)
, where we specifically choose

a_{j}(x,\sigma)=\phi(\sqrt[j]{x})\exp\bigl(-\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{2j^{2}}\bigr)

(7.7)

with
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
\phi\equiv 1
near
x=1
. Note that
a_{j}(x,\sigma)
is holomorphic in
\sigma
,
\{a_{j}(x,\sigma+i\gamma);\;j\in\mathbb{N},\;|\gamma|\leq K\}\subset S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{\sigma})
is bounded for every
K>0
, and
a_{j}(x,\sigma)\to 1
as
j\to\infty
pointwise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{C}
.
An application of the above continuity criterion combined with the analyticity and estimates on the symbols with respect to
\sigma
yield that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and every
u\in x^{\alpha}L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R};F)
we have
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u\to u
as
j\to\infty
in
x^{\alpha}L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R};F)
. Note that the extensions by continuity of
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})
to weighted
L^{2}_{b}
-spaces for different values of

are consistent on the intersection
x^{\alpha}L^{2}_{b}\cap x^{\alpha^{\prime}}L^{2}_{b}
, and thus
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})
is unambiguously defined. Observe also that
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};F)
for every

.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
a(x,\sigma)\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
be a global scalar Mellin symbol. Then

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a):{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\to{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})

is continuous. Moreover, if
\{a_{j}(x,\sigma)\}\subset S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
is a sequence of such symbols that is bounded in the global symbol topology, and such that
a_{j}(x,\sigma)
converges pointwise to the global Mellin symbol
a(x,\sigma)\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
, then
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})\to\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)
strongly in
\mathscr{L}({\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1}))
, i.e.,
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})u\to\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)u
in
{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
for each
u\in{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
.

Proof.

To prove the first claim we need to show by definition of
{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
that

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}):L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\to L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})

is bounded. Now

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1})=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}([p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0}]\#[ap(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]),

where

\displaystyle[p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]

\displaystyle\#[ap(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]

(7.9)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\mu}\frac{1}{k!}\bigl(\partial_{\sigma}^{k}p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0}\bigr)\bigl([(xD_{x})^{k}a]p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\mu}\frac{1}{k!}\bigl([\partial_{\sigma}^{k}p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}\bigr)\bigl((xD_{x})^{k}a(x,\sigma)\bigr).

Lemma
7.1
now implies that

[p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]\#[ap(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(E_{0})),

and consequently the Mellin pseudodifferential operator

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}\bigl([p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]\#[ap(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]\bigr):L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\to L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})

is bounded.

To prove the convergence statement note that boundedness of the
a_{j}(x,\sigma)
in the symbol topology implies, in particular, that the
\{a_{j}\}
form a bounded sequence with respect to the standard Fréchet topology of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. The Montel property of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
together with the pointwise convergence of the

then implies that
a_{j}\to a
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. Then

\{[p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]\#[a_{j}p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset S^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathscr{L}(E_{0}))

is bounded and

[p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]\#[a_{j}p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]\to[p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]\#[ap(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]

pointwise by (

), and so

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}\bigl([p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]\#[a_{j}p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]\bigr)\to\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}\bigl([p(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})]\#[ap(\sigma+i\gamma_{0})^{-1}]\bigr)

strongly in
\mathscr{L}(L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0}))
, thus proving that
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})\to\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)
strongly in
\mathscr{L}({\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1}))
.
∎

Theorem 7.11
.

We have

x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\cap L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\min},

and
\mathcal{D}_{\min}=x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\cap L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
if and only if
p(\sigma-im):E_{1}\to E_{0}
is invertible for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The operator
A:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\subset L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\to L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
extends to a bounded operator
A:x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\to L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
via

Au=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p(\sigma-im))(x^{-m}u)

(7.12)

for
u\in x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
by Proposition
7.6
. Consequently, writing
H=L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
, we have

\|u\|_{H}+\|Au\|_{H}\lesssim\|u\|_{H}+\|u\|_{x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}}

for
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
which proves that
x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\cap L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\min}
by Corollary
7.10
.

If
p(\sigma-im):E_{1}\to E_{0}
is invertible for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, Proposition
7.3
and (
7.12
) imply that
\|Au\|_{H}
is an equivalent norm on
x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
. Consequently, the graph norm
\|u\|_{H}+\|Au\|_{H}
and the norm
\|u\|_{H}+\|u\|_{x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}}
are equivalent on

, and therefore
\mathcal{D}_{\min}=x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\cap L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
by the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
in both spaces.

Conversely, if there exists
\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Im(\sigma_{0})=-m
such that
p(\sigma_{0}):E_{1}\to E_{0}
is not invertible, we will show in the proof of Theorem
8.2
in Section
8
that
\mathcal{D}_{\min}
contains elements of the form
u=\omega e_{1}x^{i\sigma_{0}}
, where
0\neq e_{1}\in E_{1}
and
\omega\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
is a function with
\omega\equiv 1
near
x=0
. Such functions
u
are not contained in
x^{m}L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
and thus
u\notin x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
, and so
\mathcal{D}_{\min}\neq x^{m}{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\cap L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
.
∎

8.
The maximal domain

Fix
\omega\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
with
\omega\equiv 1
near
x=0
. For each
\sigma_{0}\in\spec_{b}(A)
let

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\sigma_{0}}=\{u=\omega\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}e_{j}

\displaystyle\log^{j}(x)x^{i\sigma_{0}};\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\textup{ and }e_{j}\in E_{1},

(8.1)

\displaystyle\textup{and }p(\sigma)(Mu)(\sigma)\textup{ is holomorphic at $\sigma=\sigma_{0}$}\}.

By analytic Fredholm theory this space is finite-dimensional. Theorem

describes the structure of the maximal domain.

Theorem 8.2
.

We have

\mathcal{D}_{\max}=\mathcal{D}_{\min}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{0}\in\spec_{b}(A)\\
-m<\Im(\sigma_{0})<0\end{subarray}}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma_{0}}.

In particular,
\dim\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}<\infty
. For each
\sigma_{0}\in\spec_{b}(A)
with
-m<\Im(\sigma_{0})<0
the space
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma_{0}}=\mathscr{E}_{\sigma_{0}}+\mathcal{D}_{\min}
is the generalized eigenspace to the eigenvalue
\sigma_{0}
of the generator
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
of the induced action
\hat{\kappa}_{\varrho}
from (
6.2
) on
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{\max}
.

If
u_{\sigma_{j}}\in\mathscr{E}_{\sigma_{j}}
for
\sigma_{j}\in\spec_{b}(A)
with
-m<\Im(\sigma_{j})<0
for
j=0,1
, then the adjoint pairing between these functions is given by

[u_{\sigma_{0}},u_{\sigma_{1}}]_{A}=\res_{\sigma=\sigma_{0}}\langle p(\sigma)[Mu_{\sigma_{0}}](\sigma),[Mu_{\sigma_{1}}](\sigma^{\star})\rangle_{E_{0}},

(8.3)

where
\sigma^{\star}=\overline{\sigma}-im
is reflection about the line
\Im(\sigma)=-\frac{m}{2}
.

This means that every
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\max}
has an asymptotic expansion of the form

u\sim\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{0}\in\spec_{b}(A)\\
-m<\Im(\sigma_{0})<0\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k_{\sigma_{0}}}e_{\sigma_{0},j}\log^{j}(x)x^{i\sigma_{0}}\quad\textup{as }x\to 0\mod\mathcal{D}_{\min},

and vanishing conditions placed upon these asymptotic terms are boundary conditions as

that determine extensions. In particular, the abstract theory of the first part of this paper is applicable to the indicial operator (
6.1
).

The proof of Theorem

requires some auxiliary results.

Lemma 8.4
.

We have

{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\max}=\{u\in{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1});\;\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u\in x^{m}L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\cap L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})\},

and
A_{\max}u=x^{-m}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u
for
u\in{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\max}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in{\mathscr{H}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
and
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
be arbitrary. Using Plancherel and our standing assumptions we get

\displaystyle\langle Av,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}\bigl(p(\cdot-im)\bigr)(x^{-m}v),u\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle p(\sigma-im)[M(x^{-m}v)](\sigma),[Mu](\sigma)\rangle_{E_{0}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[M(x^{-m}v)](\sigma),p(\sigma)[Mu](\sigma)\rangle_{E_{0}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\langle x^{-m}v,\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle v(x),[x^{-m}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u](x)\rangle_{E_{0}}\,\frac{dx}{x}.

Consequently, if
x^{-m}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u\in L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
, then
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\max}
and the last integral can be rewritten as the pairing
\langle v,A_{\max}u\rangle
with
A_{\max}u=x^{-m}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u
. Conversely, if
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\max}
, we have
\langle Av,u\rangle=\langle v,A_{\max}u\rangle
, and so

\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle v(x),[x^{-m}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u](x)\rangle_{E_{0}}\,\frac{dx}{x}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle v(x),A_{\max}u(x)\rangle_{E_{0}}\,\frac{dx}{x}

for all
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{1})
. This shows
x^{-m}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u=A_{\max}u\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
, and thus
x^{-m}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(p)u=A_{\max}u\in L^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+};E_{0})
.
∎

.

Proposition 8.6

), and analyticity of
a(x,\sigma)
in
\sigma
then follows from the Paley-Wiener Theorem. To analyze the kernel cut-off operator in more detail it is convenient to rewrite it as an oscillatory integral in the form

a(x,\sigma+i\gamma)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint e^{-it\tau}e^{t\gamma}\phi(t)a_{0}(x,\sigma-\tau)\,dtd\tau\\

(A.5)

for real
x
,
\sigma
, and
\gamma
. The standard regularization procedure applied to this integral reveals that
a(x,\sigma+i\gamma)
depends smoothly on
(x,\sigma+i\gamma)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
, that the Cauchy-Riemann equations hold with respect to
\sigma+i\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, and from the symbol estimates for
a_{0}
we obtain the resulting estimates for
a
. While kernel cut-off as an operation on symbols only acts on the variable
\sigma
, the representation (
A.5
) shows that if additional parameters are present for a symbol
a_{0}
that satisfy suitable joint estimates with
\sigma
, such as the variable
x
in our case, then these joint estimates are typically preserved for the resulting analytic symbol
a
along lines parallel to the real axis. Finally, from

\displaystyle a_{0}(x,\sigma)-a(x,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint e^{-it\tau}(1-\phi(t))a_{0}(x,\sigma-\tau)\,dtd\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{i^{k}}{2\pi}\iint e^{-it\tau}\frac{1-\phi(t)}{t^{k}}[\partial^{k}_{\sigma}a_{0}](x,\sigma-\tau)\,dtd\tau,\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

we obtain that
a-a_{0}\in S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Detailed technical proofs of these statements, albeit for a different symbol class, can be found in
[
34
, Section 3]
.

Kernel cut-off allows performing real variable symbolic manipulations along one line
\Im(\sigma)=\gamma
, such as manipulations that utilize excision functions, and then pass to a holomorphic representative of the resulting symbol modulo
S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. An example for this is a proof that asymptotic expansions exist in the analytic category: Given
\mu_{j}\to-\infty
and
a_{j}\in S^{\mu_{j};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
, there exists
a(x,\sigma)\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
,
\mu=\max\mu_{j}
, such that
a\sim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}
in the sense that for every
R>0
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
a-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\in S^{-R;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
for
k\geq k_{0}
. To see this first restrict all
a_{j}(x,\sigma)
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By the standard Borel argument for real symbols there exists
a_{0}(x,\sigma)\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
a_{0}(x,\sigma)\sim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}(x,\sigma)
. Now use the kernel cut-off construction and define
a\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
via (
A.5
). Since
a-a_{0}\in S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we still have the asymptotic expansion
a(x,\sigma)\sim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}(x,\sigma)
as real symbols. Consequently,

a(x,\sigma)-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\in S^{-R;\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})

for
k\geq k_{0}
. But
S^{-R;\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})=S^{-R;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
by (
A.4
), thus establishing the desired result.

The following is another closely related application of the kernel cut-off construction.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
a\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
. Then there exists a sequence
a_{j}\in S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
such that
a_{j}\to a
in
S^{\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
as
j\to\infty
for every
\mu^{\prime}>\mu
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a function with
\chi\equiv 0
for
(|x|^{2\ell_{2}}+|\sigma|^{2\ell_{1}})^{\frac{1}{2\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}}\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 1
for
(|x|^{2\ell_{2}}+|\sigma|^{2\ell_{1}})^{\frac{1}{2\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}}\geq 2
, and define

b_{j}(x,\sigma)=\chi\bigl(\tfrac{x}{j^{\ell_{1}}},\tfrac{\sigma}{j^{\ell_{2}}}\bigr)a(x,\sigma)\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;j\in\mathbb{N}.

Then
b_{j}\to 0
in
S^{\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
j\to\infty
for every
\mu^{\prime}>\mu
, and
a-b_{j}\in S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Now let
c_{j}\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
be defined by applying the kernel cut-off operator (
A.5
) to
b_{j}
. Then
b_{j}-c_{j}\in S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and by the continuity of the kernel cut-off operator we have
c_{j}\to 0
in
S^{\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
for
\mu^{\prime}>\mu
. The lemma then follows with

a_{j}=a-c_{j}\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})\cap S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})

and (
A.4
).
∎

Given
a\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
we consider the Mellin pseudodifferential operator

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a):\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

where
\dot{C}^{\infty}
is the space of (Hilbert space valued) smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
that vanish to infinite order at
x=0
and are Schwartz functions as
x\to\infty
. Analyticity of the symbol
a(x,\sigma)
and the estimates (
A.3
) along lines parallel to the real axis ensure that the action of
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)
preserves this space. The map

S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})\times\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\ni(a,u)\mapsto\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)u\in\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

is continuous.

We assume in the sequel that the reader has some familiarity with standard (global) Mellin pseudodifferential calculus (see
[
24
,
35
,
41
,
52
]
).

Definition A.7
.

By
\Psi^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}
we denote the operators of the form

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)+G:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

with
a\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
, and the operator
G:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and its formal adjoint
G^{*}:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with respect to the
L^{2}_{b}
and Hilbert space inner products in the target and range spaces extend to bounded operators

G,\,G^{*}:x^{\alpha}H^{s}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to x^{\alpha^{\prime}}H^{s^{\prime}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

for all
\alpha,s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, and all
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
. Thus
G
and
G^{*}
are smoothing and produce Schwartz behavior as
x\to\infty
while maintaining the same order of growth as
x\to 0
.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
a\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
,
\mu\leq 0
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\mu+\ell_{1}j\leq 0
we have

\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a),\;\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)^{*}:x^{\alpha}H^{s}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to x^{\alpha-j}H^{s-\frac{\mu+\ell_{1}j}{\ell_{2}}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

for all
s,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)^{*}:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is the (formal) adjoint to
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a):\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Proof.

By assumption on
\mu
and
j
,
x^{j}a(x,\sigma)
is a standard global Mellin symbol of class
S^{\frac{\mu+\ell_{1}j}{\ell_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
, which shows the asserted mapping property for
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)
. Now

x^{j}[\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)]^{*}=[\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)x^{j}]^{*}=[x^{j}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a(x,\sigma-ij))]^{*}:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

where
x^{j}a(x,\sigma-ij)\in S^{\frac{\mu+\ell_{1}j}{\ell_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. Consequently,

x^{j}[\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)]^{*}:x^{\alpha}H^{s}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to x^{\alpha}H^{s-\frac{\mu+\ell_{1}j}{\ell_{2}}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

and the lemma is proved.
∎

Theorem A.9
.

Let
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)+G\in\Psi^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}
. Then the formal adjoint of this operator with respect to the
L^{2}_{b}
inner product belongs to the class
\Psi^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}
, where more precisely

\bigl(\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)+G\bigr)^{*}=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a^{\sharp})+G^{\prime}:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

and
a^{\sharp}\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
has the asymptotic expansion

a^{\sharp}(x,\sigma)\sim\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(xD_{x})^{k}\partial_{\sigma}^{k}[a(x,\overline{\sigma})^{*}].

(A.10)

Proof.

Let first
a\in S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
. In particular,
a\in S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
and is analytic, and from the standard global Mellin pseudodifferential calculus we have an exact representation of the formal adjoint
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)^{*}=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(b):\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where for every
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\displaystyle b(x,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint y^{-i\eta}a(xy,\overline{\sigma}+\eta)^{*}\,\frac{dy}{y}d\eta

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{k!}(xD_{x})^{k}\partial_{\sigma}^{k}[a(x,\overline{\sigma})^{*}]+r_{N}(x,\sigma)

\displaystyle=b_{N}(x,\sigma)+r_{N}(x,\sigma)

with

r_{N}(x,\sigma)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\theta)^{N-1}}{2\pi(N-1)!}\iint y^{-i\eta}[(-xD_{x})^{N}\partial_{\sigma}^{N}a](xy,\overline{\sigma}+\theta\eta)^{*}\,\frac{dy}{y}d\eta d\theta.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we take advantage of the analyticity of the symbols to shift the integration into the complex
\eta
-plane and write

\displaystyle x^{j}r_{N}

\displaystyle(x,\sigma)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\theta)^{N-1}}{2\pi(N-1)!}\iint y^{-i\eta}x^{j}[(-xD_{x})^{N}\partial_{\sigma}^{N}a](xy,\overline{\sigma}+\theta\eta)^{*}\,\frac{dy}{y}d\eta d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\theta)^{N-1}}{2\pi(N-1)!}\iint y^{-i\eta}(xy)^{j}[(-xD_{x})^{N}\partial_{\sigma}^{N}a](xy,\overline{\sigma}+\theta(\eta+ij))^{*}\,\frac{dy}{y}d\eta d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\theta)^{N-1}}{2\pi(N-1)!}\iint y^{-i\eta}[x^{j}(-xD_{x})^{N}\partial_{\sigma}^{N}a](xy,\overline{\sigma}+\theta(\eta+ij))^{*}\,\frac{dy}{y}d\eta d\theta.

From these formulas we see that for any given
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
\mu_{0}<0
there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
N\geq N_{0}
and
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
the map
S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})\ni a(x,\sigma)\mapsto x^{j}r_{N}(x,\sigma)
extends to a continuous map
S^{\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})\to S^{\mu_{0}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
, and the same is true for the map
a\mapsto x^{j}r_{N}(x,\sigma-ij)
. For these values of the parameters we then have

\displaystyle\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N}):x^{\alpha}H^{s}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to x^{\alpha-j}H^{s-\mu_{0}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

\displaystyle\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})^{*}=x^{-j}\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(x^{j}r_{N}(x,\sigma-ij))^{*}:x^{\alpha}H^{s}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to x^{\alpha-j}H^{s-\mu_{0}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

for all
\alpha,s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now let
a\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
. By Lemma
A.6
there exists a sequence
a_{\nu}\in S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
such that
a_{\nu}\to a
in
S^{\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
as
\nu\to\infty
for
\mu^{\prime}>\mu
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)u,v\rangle_{L^{2}_{b}}\longleftarrow\langle\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{\nu})u,v\rangle_{L^{2}_{b}}=\langle u,\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{\nu})^{*}v\rangle_{L^{2}_{b}}

\displaystyle=\langle u,(\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(b_{N,\nu})+\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N,\nu}))v\rangle_{L^{2}_{b}}\longrightarrow\langle u,(\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(b_{N})+\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N}))v\rangle_{L^{2}_{b}}

for
u,v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, where the extra
\nu
-parameter indicates that we use the previous formulas for
a_{\nu}
, while its absence means that the formulas are applied to the symbol
a(x,\sigma)
. In particular,
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)^{*}=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(b_{N})+\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})
for
N
large enough with the above mapping properties for
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})
. Consequently, if
a^{\sharp}\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
has the asymptotic expansion (
A.10
), then
G_{0}=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)^{*}-\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a^{\sharp})
satisfies

G_{0},\,G_{0}^{*}:x^{\alpha}H^{s}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to x^{\alpha^{\prime}}H^{s^{\prime}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

for all
\alpha,s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, and all
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
by Lemma
A.8
and the mapping properties of
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})
(as
N\to\infty
). The theorem is proved.
∎

Theorem A.11
.

Let
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{j})+G_{j}\in\Psi^{\mu_{j};\vec{\ell}}_{O}
,
j=1,2

(\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{1})+G_{1})\circ(\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{2})+G_{2})=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{1}{\#}a_{2})+G:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

G_{1}G_{2}\in\Psi^{-\infty}_{O}
in view of the defining mapping properties of the

N\geq N_{0}
and
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
the map
(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto x^{j}r_{N,\infty}(x,\sigma)
is continuous in

S^{\mu_{1}^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})\times S^{\mu_{2}^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})\to S^{\mu_{0}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}),

and the same property holds for the map
(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto x^{j}r_{N,\infty}(x,\sigma-ij)
. Thus
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N,\infty})
and
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N,\infty})^{*}
have the same mapping properties as previously stated for
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N,0})
and
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N,0})^{*}
for
N
large enough, and therefore also
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})
and
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})^{*}
have these properties.

Now let
a_{j}\in S^{\mu_{j};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
. By Lemma
A.6
there exist sequences
a_{j,\nu}\in S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
such that
a_{j,\nu}\to a_{j}
in
S^{\mu_{j}^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
as
\nu\to\infty
for
\mu_{j}^{\prime}>\mu_{j}
. For every
u\in\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
we then have for
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough

\displaystyle\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{1})\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{2})u\longleftarrow\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{1,\nu})\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{2,\nu})u

\displaystyle=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(c_{N,\nu})u+\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N,\nu})u\longrightarrow\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(c_{N})u+\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})u,

where the extra
\nu
-parameter indicates that we use the previous formulas for
a_{j,\nu}
, while its absence means that the formulas are applied to the symbols
a_{j}(x,\sigma)
. In particular,
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{1})\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{2})=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(c_{N})+\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})
for
N
large enough, and
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})
has the mapping properties previously shown. Consequently, if
a_{1}{\#}a_{2}\in S^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
has the asymptotic expansion (
A.12
), then
G_{0}=\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{1})\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{2})-\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a_{1}{\#}a_{2})
satisfies

G_{0},\,G_{0}^{*}:x^{\alpha}H^{s}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to x^{\alpha^{\prime}}H^{s^{\prime}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

for all
\alpha,s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, and all
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
by Lemma
A.8
and the mapping properties of
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(r_{N})
as
N\to\infty
. The theorem is proved.
∎

Definition A.13
.

A symbol
a(x,\sigma)\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is
right-hypoelliptic
of order
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
if
a(x,\sigma)
is invertible for sufficiently large
|x,\sigma|\gg 0
, and the inverse satisfies

\displaystyle\|a^{-1}(x,\sigma)\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle x,\sigma\rangle_{\vec{\ell}}^{-\mu^{\prime}},

(A.14)

and for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\|[D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta}a](x,\sigma)[a(x,\sigma)]^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle x,\sigma\rangle_{\vec{\ell}}^{-\ell_{1}\alpha-\ell_{2}\beta}.

(A.15)

We call
a(x,\sigma)\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
right-hypoelliptic of order
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
if its restriction to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is right-hypoelliptic of order
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
.

Suppose
a(x,\sigma)\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
is right-hypoelliptic of order
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be an excision function such that
\chi\equiv 0
near
(0,0)
and
\chi\equiv 1
for large
|x,\sigma|
so that
\chi(x,\sigma)a(x,\sigma)^{-1}
is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The estimates (
A.14
) and (
A.15
) show that
\chi(x,\sigma)a(x,\sigma)^{-1}\in S^{-\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We then apply the kernel cut-off operator (
A.5
) to this symbol to obtain a holomorphic symbol
q(x,\sigma)\in S^{-\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
which has the following properties:

Lemma A.16
.

(a)

We have
a(x,\sigma)q(x,\sigma)-1,\,q(x,\sigma)a(x,\sigma)-1\in S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
.

(b)

For
\gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,2
, we have

[D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta}a](x,\sigma+i\gamma_{2})[D_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta^{\prime}}q](x,\sigma+i\gamma_{1})\in S^{-\ell_{1}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})-\ell_{2}(\beta+\beta^{\prime});\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})

for all
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

(c)

a(x,\sigma+i\gamma)\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is right-hypoelliptic of order
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
for every
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We have
q(x,\sigma)-\chi(x,\sigma)a(x,\sigma)^{-1}\in S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and so

a(x,\sigma)q(x,\sigma)-1\in S^{(\mu-\mu^{\prime})_{+};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})\cap S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})

by (
A.4
); here
(\mu-\mu^{\prime})_{+}=\max\{\mu-\mu^{\prime},0\}
. For the same reason we also have
q(x,\sigma)a(x,\sigma)-1\in S^{-\infty}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
, proving (a).

To prove (b) we first consider
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
. By (
A.15
) we have

[D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta}a](x,\sigma)[D_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\chi a^{-1})](x,\sigma)\in S^{-\ell_{1}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})-\ell_{2}(\beta+\beta^{\prime});\vec{\ell}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

and because
q-\chi a^{-1}\in S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we can replace
\chi a^{-1}
by
q
. Taylor expansion (
A.4
) then shows that

[D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta}a](x,\sigma)[D_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta^{\prime}}q](x,\sigma)\in S^{-\ell_{1}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})-\ell_{2}(\beta+\beta^{\prime});\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}),

and translation proves (b) for general
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
. For
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
we first use (
A.4
) and get

[D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta}a](x,\sigma+i\gamma_{2})\sim\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\gamma_{2}-i\gamma_{1})^{k}}{k!}\bigl(D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta+k}a\bigr)(x,\sigma+i\gamma_{1}).

Now multiply from the right by
[D_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{\sigma}^{\beta^{\prime}}q](x,\sigma+i\gamma_{1})
. The resulting asymptotic expansion then shows that (b) also holds in the case
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
.

Finally, (c) follows from (a) and (b). By (a),
a(x,\sigma+i\gamma)
is invertible for sufficiently large
|x,\sigma|\gg 0
,
(x,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and the inverse differs from
q(x,\sigma+i\gamma)
by a rapidly decreasing function in
(x,\sigma)
. Both estimates (
A.14
) and (
A.15
) hold for
q(x,\sigma+i\gamma)
instead of
a(x,\sigma+i\gamma)^{-1}
and are stable with respect to rapidly decreasing perturbations. The lemma is proved.
∎

Theorem A.17
.

Let
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)+G\in\Psi^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}
, and suppose that
a\in S^{\mu;\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
is right-hypoelliptic of order
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
. Then there exists
\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(b)\in\Psi^{-\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}
such that

\bigl(\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(a)+G\bigr)\circ\textup{op}_{\textit{M}}(b)=1+\tilde{G}:\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

with
\tilde{G}\in\Psi^{-\infty}_{O}
.

Proof.

With the symbol
q(x,\sigma)\in S^{-\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
we have

(a{\#}q)(x,\sigma)\sim\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}[\partial_{\sigma}^{k}a](x,\sigma)[(xD_{x})^{k}q](x,\sigma)=1+r(x,\sigma)

with
r(x,\sigma)\in S^{-\ell_{2};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
by Lemma
A.16
. Now apply the formal Neumann series argument and get
r^{\prime}(x,\sigma)\in S^{-\ell_{2};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
such that
(1+r){\#}(1+r^{\prime})\sim 1
. Let
b(x,\sigma)\in S^{-\mu^{\prime};\vec{\ell}}_{O}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C})
with
b(x,\sigma)\sim q{\#}(1+r^{\prime})
. Then
a{\#}b\sim 1
, and Theorem
A.11
implies the assertion.
∎

Analogous considerations apply to left-hypoellipticity, but left-hypoellipticity is not used in this work.

Appendix B
An indefinite space arising in analytic Fredholm theory

Definition B.1

(
[
26
, Section 4.5]
)
.

Let
\bigl(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{V}\bigr)
and
\bigr(W,[\cdot,\cdot]_{W}\bigr)
be finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces equipped with Hermitian sesquilinear forms
[\cdot,\cdot]_{V}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{W}
, respectively. Let further
g\in\End(V)
and
h\in\End(W)
. Then the triples
\bigl(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{V},g\bigr)
and
\bigl(W,[\cdot,\cdot]_{W},h\bigr)
are unitarily equivalent if there exists an isomorphism
T:V\to W
such that both

[Tu,Tv]_{W}=[u,v]_{V}\;\forall u,v\in V\textup{ and }h=TgT^{-1}

hold.

The Canonical Form Theorem
[
26
, Theorem 5.1.1]
classifies triples
\bigl(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{V},g\bigr)
for
g\in\End(V)
that are selfadjoint with respect to nondegenerate
[\cdot,\cdot]_{V}
up to unitary equivalence. The generalized eigenspaces
\mathscr{E}_{\sigma_{0}}\subset V
of
g
associated with real eigenvalues
\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
are particularly interesting. By the Canonical Form Theorem we can localize to
\bigl(\mathscr{E}_{\sigma_{0}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{V},g-\sigma_{0}\bigr)
to further study these spaces. In this appendix and Appendix
C
we discuss an indefinite space from analytic Fredholm theory that arises as unitarily equivalent to
\bigl(\mathscr{E}_{\sigma_{0}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{V},g-\sigma_{0}\bigr)
for indicial operators. We refer to
[
19
,
22
,
23
,
25
]
for related investigations and results. Information about the general theory of Fredholm operator pencils and applications to differential equations can be found in
[
32
]
.

Suppose
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
are separable complex Hilbert spaces such that
E_{1}\hookrightarrow E_{0}
is densely and continuously embedded, and let

\mathscr{P}:B_{\varepsilon}(0)\to\mathscr{L}(E_{1},E_{0})

be a holomorphic operator function defined on the open disk
B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subset\mathbb{C}
for some
\varepsilon>0
. We assume that
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)
is Fredholm for all
\sigma\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)
and invertible for all
\sigma\neq 0
. We consider each operator
\mathscr{P}(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}
an unbounded operator acting in
E_{0}
with domain
E_{1}
. Under the stated assumptions,
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)
is closed and densely defined. We assume that

\mathscr{P}(\overline{\sigma})^{*}=\mathscr{P}(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}

holds. In particular,
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)
is selfadjoint for real
\sigma
. We denote the set of germs of such operator functions
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)
by
{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(E_{1},E_{0})
.

With every
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(E_{1},E_{0})
we associate its spectral flow across
\sigma=0
as follows: Pick
\varepsilon_{0},\delta_{0}>0
small enough such that
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)-\lambda:E_{1}\to E_{0}
is Fredholm for
-\delta_{0}\leq\sigma\leq\delta_{0}
and
-\varepsilon_{0}\leq\lambda\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, and such that
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\pm\varepsilon_{0}:E_{1}\to E_{0}
is invertible for
-\delta_{0}\leq\sigma\leq\delta_{0}
. Then

\SF_{\sigma=0}[\mathscr{P}(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}]=\lim\limits_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(\tr\bigl[\Pi_{[0,\varepsilon_{0})}(\mathscr{P}(\sigma))\bigr]-\tr\bigl[\Pi_{[0,\varepsilon_{0})}(\mathscr{P}(-\sigma))\bigr]\Bigr),

where
\Pi_{[0,\varepsilon_{0})}
in each case is the spectral projection onto the spectral subspace of the operator pertaining to the part of the spectrum contained in
[0,\varepsilon_{0})
. We refer to the survey by Lesch
[
40
]
for details on the spectral flow.

For any Fréchet space
F
let
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(F)
denote the space of meromorphic germs of
F
-valued functions at
0
, and let
\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)
denote the holomorphic germs. Then
\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(F):=\mathfrak{M}_{0}(F)/\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)
can be identified with the space of principal parts of Laurent expansions at
0
of
F
-valued meromorphic functions. The operator function
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(E_{1},E_{0})
induces a map

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}:\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(E_{1})\to\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(E_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}\bigl[\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1})\bigr]=\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{0})

for
\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1})\in\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(E_{1})
. Define

\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})=\{\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1})\in\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(E_{1});\;\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma)\in\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{0})\}.

Then
\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})=\ker\bigl[\mathscr{P}:\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(E_{1})\to\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(E_{0})\bigr]
, and by analytic Fredholm theory we have
\dim\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})<\infty
. We define a pairing

\displaystyle[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}:\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})\times\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})\to\mathbb{C}

via

\displaystyle[\hat{u},\hat{v}]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\hat{v}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{E_{0}}\,d\sigma=\res\limits_{\sigma=0}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\hat{v}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{E_{0}},

where
C
is a positively oriented circle of sufficiently small radius centered at the origin. It is easy to see that
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}
is well-defined (i.e. independent of representatives
\hat{u}
and
\hat{v}
modulo holomorphic germs), and that it furnishes a Hermitian sesquilinear form on
\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})
. We then consider the triple
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
, where

M_{\sigma}:\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})\to\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),\quad\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1})\mapsto\sigma\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1}).

Note that
M_{\sigma}
is selfadjoint with respect to
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we also consider the Hermitian sesquilinear forms

\begin{gathered}{}[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),\ell}:\ker M_{\sigma}^{\ell}\times\ker M_{\sigma}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{C},\\
[\hat{u},\hat{v}]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),\ell}=[M_{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{u},\hat{v}]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}.\end{gathered}

Let
(m_{0}(\ell),m_{+}(\ell),m_{-}(\ell))
be the invariants of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),\ell}
. The numbers
m_{+}(\ell)
and
m_{-}(\ell)
yield the sign characteristic for
M_{\sigma}
associated to Jordan blocks of size
\ell\times\ell
in the Canonical Form Theorem
[
26
, Theorem 5.1.1]
for
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
, see
[
26
, Theorem 5.8.1]
and Proposition
3.8
. The main theorem regarding this triple is the following.

Theorem B.2
.

\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}\bigr)
is nondegenerate, and

\sig\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}\bigr)=\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\ell)-m_{-}(\ell)\bigr)=\SF_{\sigma=0}\bigl[\mathscr{P}(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}\bigr],

where
m_{\pm}(\ell)
are the invariants associated with the triple
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
.

Proof.

We will prove the theorem by reducing it to the finite-dimensional case, considered separately in Appendix
C
. To this end, let
N=\ker(\mathscr{P}(0))
and
R=\ran(\mathscr{P}(0))
. We have the orthogonal decomposition
E_{0}=N\oplus R
. Let
\pi_{N}=\pi_{N}^{*}=\pi_{N}^{2}\in\mathscr{L}(E_{0})
be the orthogonal projection onto
N
, and let
\pi_{R}=1-\pi_{N}
. Because
\dim N<\infty
both
\pi_{N},\pi_{R}\in\mathscr{L}(E_{1})
by the Closed Graph Theorem, and we get
E_{1}=N\oplus[R\cap E_{1}]
. We decompose

\mathscr{P}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{P}_{11}(\sigma)&\mathscr{P}_{12}(\sigma)\\
\mathscr{P}_{21}(\sigma)&\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)\end{bmatrix}:\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\oplus\\
R\cap E_{1}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\oplus\\
R\end{array},

and note that
\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)
is invertible for
|\sigma|<\varepsilon
for
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

Now, for
0\leq t\leq 1
, define
\mathscr{U}_{t}(\sigma):E_{j}\to E_{j}
via

\mathscr{U}_{t}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-t\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)^{-1}\mathscr{P}_{21}(\sigma)&1\end{bmatrix}:\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\oplus\\
R\cap E_{j}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\oplus\\
R\cap E_{j}\end{array},\quad j=1,2.

Then
\mathscr{U}_{t}(\sigma):B_{\varepsilon}(0)\to\mathscr{L}(E_{j})
is holomorphic and invertible, and the same holds for

\mathscr{U}_{t}(\overline{\sigma})^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-t\mathscr{P}_{12}(\sigma)\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)^{-1}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}:\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\oplus\\
R\cap E_{j}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\oplus\\
R\cap E_{j}\end{array},

where the adjoint refers to the base Hilbert space
E_{0}
. Then

\mathscr{P}_{t}(\sigma)=\mathscr{U}_{t}(\overline{\sigma})^{*}\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\mathscr{U}_{t}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(E_{1},E_{0})

is a homotopy within
{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(E_{1},E_{0})
between
\mathscr{P}_{0}(\sigma)=\mathscr{P}(\sigma)
and

\mathscr{P}_{1}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{P}_{11}(\sigma)-\mathscr{P}_{12}(\sigma)\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)^{-1}\mathscr{P}_{21}(\sigma)&0\\
0&\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)\end{bmatrix}.

By the homotopy invariance of the spectral flow we have

\SF_{\sigma=0}[\mathscr{P}_{1}(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}]=\SF_{\sigma=0}[\mathscr{P}(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}].

Moreover, the map

\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1})\longmapsto\mathscr{U}_{1}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1})

furnishes a unitary equivalence

\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}_{1}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}_{1})},M_{\sigma}\bigr)\cong\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})},M_{\sigma}\bigr).

It therefore suffices to prove the theorem for
\mathscr{P}_{1}(\sigma)
instead of
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)
. Define

p(\sigma):=\mathscr{P}_{11}(\sigma)-\mathscr{P}_{12}(\sigma)\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)^{-1}\mathscr{P}_{21}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(N),

so
\mathscr{P}_{1}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}p(\sigma)&0\\
0&\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)\end{bmatrix}
. In view of the invertibility of
\mathscr{P}_{22}(\sigma)
we have both

\displaystyle\SF_{\sigma=0}[\mathscr{P}_{1}(\sigma):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\to E_{0}]

\displaystyle=\SF_{\sigma=0}[p(\sigma):N\to N]

and

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}_{1}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}_{1})},M_{\sigma}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\bigl(\mathscr{K}(p),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)},M_{\sigma}\bigr),

where the latter unitary equivalence is induced by projection onto
N
. The theorem is therefore reduced to considering the finite-dimensional case for
p(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(N)
, and an application of Theorem
C.5
thus finishes the proof.
∎

We conclude with the following theorem about semibounded operators.

Theorem B.3
.

Suppose
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(E_{1},E_{0})
satisfies
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\geq 0
for
\sigma
real. Then the following hold:

(1)

m_{+}(\ell)=m_{-}(\ell)=0
for
\ell
odd. The canonical form for
M_{\sigma}
does not contain any Jordan blocks of odd sizes, and
\sig\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}\bigr)=0
.

(2)

The sign characteristic for the triple
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
does not contain any negative terms, i.e., we also have
m_{-}(\ell)=0
for
\ell
even.

(3)

There exists a unique Lagrangian subspace of
\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})
that is invariant under
M_{\sigma}
, denoted by
\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Specifically, if

\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}

according to the Canonical Form Theorem with mutually
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}
-orthogonal direct summands, and each
U_{j}
is associated to a single Jordan block of
M_{\sigma}
of size
(2n_{j})\times(2n_{j})
, then

\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})_{\frac{1}{2}}=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Bigl[U_{j}\cap\ker M_{\sigma}^{n_{j}}\Bigr].

Proof.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\hat{u}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{1})\in\ker M_{\sigma}^{\ell}
be arbitrary. Then

[\hat{u},\hat{u}]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),\ell}=[\hat{u},M_{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{u}]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P})}=\res\limits_{\sigma=0}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\overline{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{u}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{E_{0}}.

We have
\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\,\sigma^{\ell}\hat{u}(\sigma)\in\mathfrak{H}_{0}(E_{0})
, and consequently

\res\limits_{\sigma=0}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\overline{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{u}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{E_{0}}=\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\to 0\\
\sigma\,\textup{real}\end{subarray}}\sigma^{\ell}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\hat{u}(\sigma)\rangle_{E_{0}}.

Note that
\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\hat{u}(\sigma)\rangle_{E_{0}}\geq 0
for real
\sigma\neq 0
by assumption. For odd
\ell
we thus get

\displaystyle\res\limits_{\sigma=0}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\overline{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{u}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sigma^{\ell}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\hat{u}(\sigma)\rangle_{E_{0}}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\res\limits_{\sigma=0}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\overline{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{u}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\sigma\to 0^{-}}\sigma^{\ell}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\hat{u}(\sigma)\rangle_{E_{0}}\leq 0,

and so
[\hat{u},\hat{u}]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),\ell}=0
, while for even
\ell
we have

\res\limits_{\sigma=0}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\overline{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{u}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{E_{0}}=\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\to 0\\
\sigma\,\textup{real}\end{subarray}}\sigma^{\ell}\langle\mathscr{P}(\sigma)\hat{u}(\sigma),\hat{u}(\sigma)\rangle_{E_{0}}\geq 0,

and so
[\hat{u},\hat{u}]_{\mathscr{K}(\mathscr{P}),\ell}\geq 0
. This proves the first two assertions of the theorem. The third assertion now follows from
[
26
, Theorem 5.12.4]
.
∎

Appendix C
Analytic crossings and spectral flow in finite-dimensional spaces

Let
F
be a complex finite-dimensional Hilbert space. For a holomorphic operator function
p(\sigma):B_{\varepsilon}(0)\to\mathscr{L}(F)
, where
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, we define its adjoint via
p^{\star}(\sigma):=[p(\overline{\sigma})]^{*}:B_{\varepsilon}(0)\to\mathscr{L}(F)
and note that it depends holomorphically on
\sigma
. By
{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
we denote the collection of all germs of holomorphic
\mathscr{L}(F)
-valued functions
p(\sigma)
defined near
\sigma=0
such that for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
the following two properties hold:

•

p(\sigma):B_{\varepsilon}(0)\to\mathscr{L}(F)
is holomorphic and invertible for
\sigma\neq 0
.

•

p
is selfadjoint in the sense that
p^{\star}=p
as operator functions on
B_{\varepsilon}(0)
.

In other words, for real
-\varepsilon<\sigma<\varepsilon
, the operator
p(\sigma):F\to F
is selfadjoint and invertible for
\sigma\neq 0
. Thus crossings of negative to positive eigenvalues (or vice versa) may occur at
\sigma=0
only, while
p(\sigma)
is analytic near
\sigma=0
. The spectral flow of
p(\sigma)
across
\sigma=0
is

\SF_{\sigma=0}[p(\sigma):F\to F]=\lim\limits_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(\tr[\Pi_{+}(p(\sigma))]-\tr[\Pi_{+}(p(-\sigma))]\Bigr),

where
\Pi_{+}
in each case denotes the spectral projection onto the span of the eigenspaces associated with positive eigenvalues of the selfadjoint operator
p(\sigma)
or
p(-\sigma)
, respectively.

As in Appendix
B
, every
p(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
induces a map

\displaystyle p:\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(F)\to\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(F),

\displaystyle p\bigl[\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)\bigr]=p(\sigma)\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)

for
\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)\in\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(F)
. Define

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(p)

\displaystyle=\ker\bigl[p:\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(F)\to\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(F)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\{\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)\in\bigl(\mathfrak{M}/\mathfrak{H}\bigr)_{0}(F);\;p(\sigma)\hat{v}(\sigma)\in\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)\}.

Note that
\dim\mathscr{K}(p)<\infty
. We define a pairing
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}:\mathscr{K}(p)\times\mathscr{K}(p)\to\mathbb{C}
via

[\hat{v},\hat{w}]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}\langle p(\sigma)\hat{v}(\sigma),\hat{w}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{F}\,d\sigma=\res\limits_{\sigma=0}\langle p(\sigma)\hat{v}(\sigma),\hat{w}(\overline{\sigma})\rangle_{F},

where
C
is any positively oriented circle centered at
\sigma=0
of sufficiently small radius. This is a Hermitian sesquilinear form on
\mathscr{K}(p)
. We thus associate with every
p(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
the triple
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(p),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
, where

\displaystyle M_{\sigma}:\mathscr{K}(p)\to\mathscr{K}(p),

\displaystyle M_{\sigma}:\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)\mapsto\sigma\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F).

Note that
M_{\sigma}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{K}(p))
is nilpotent and selfadjoint with respect to
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}
.

Definition C.1
.

For
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
we write
p\sim_{s}q
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
and an
invertible
holomorphic operator function
u(\sigma):B_{\varepsilon}(0)\to\mathscr{L}(F)
such that
u^{\star}qu=p
on
B_{\varepsilon}(0)
, i.e.,
[u(\overline{\sigma})]^{*}q(\sigma)u(\sigma)=p(\sigma):F\to F
for
\sigma\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)
.

Note that
\sim_{s}
is an equivalence relation on the set
{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
.

Proposition C.2
.

For
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
with
p\sim_{s}q
the triples
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(p),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
and
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(q),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(q)},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
are unitarily equivalent. More precisely, if
u^{\star}qu=p
with the holomorphic and invertible function
u(\sigma):B_{\varepsilon}(0)\to\mathscr{L}(F)
, then

T:\mathscr{K}(p)\to\mathscr{K}(q),\quad\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)\mapsto u(\sigma)\hat{v}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F)

furnishes a unitary equivalence between these indefinite inner product spaces. Moreover, we have

\SF_{\sigma=0}[p(\sigma):F\to F]=\SF_{\sigma=0}[q(\sigma):F\to F].

Proof.

The proof of the first statement follows immediately from the definitions. Note that
T^{-1}
is given by multiplication by
u(\sigma)^{-1}
, which exists and is holomorphic near
\sigma=0
. Finally,
M_{\sigma}
commutes with multiplication by holomorphic operator functions, so
T
is a unitary equivalence of the triples.

Regarding the spectral flow, we note that if
a=a^{*}\in\mathscr{L}(F)
is selfadjoint, then
[v,w]_{a}:=\langle av,w\rangle_{F}
is a Hermitian sesquilinear form on
F
. If
(m_{0},m_{+},m_{-})
are its invariants, then
m_{+}=\tr\bigl(\Pi_{+}(a)\bigr)
. If
b=u^{*}au
with an invertible
u
, then
u:\bigl(F,[\cdot,\cdot]_{b}\bigr)\to\bigl(F,[\cdot,\cdot]_{a}\bigr)
is a unitary equivalence of these indefinite inner product spaces. Consequently,
[\cdot,\cdot]_{a}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{b}
have the same invariants, i.e.,
m_{+}=\tr\bigl(\Pi_{+}(a)\bigr)=\tr\bigl(\Pi_{+}(b)\bigr)
, which implies the invariance of the spectral flow as claimed.
∎

The following key lemma is based on several results from analytic perturbation theory (see
[
30
,
55
]
).

Lemma C.3
.

Suppose
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
such that
p(0)
is invertible. Then there exists an orthogonal projection
\pi=\pi^{2}=\pi^{*}\in\mathscr{L}(F)
such that
p(\sigma)\sim_{s}\pi-(1-\pi)
.

Proof.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
and
\varrho\gg 0
such that with

\spec_{\pm}(p(\sigma)):=\spec(p(\sigma))\cap B_{\varrho-\varepsilon}(\pm\varrho)

we have
\spec(p(\sigma))=\spec_{+}(p(\sigma))\cup\spec_{-}(p(\sigma))
for
|\sigma|<\varepsilon
. Define

\pi(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial B_{\varrho-\varepsilon}(\varrho)}(\lambda-p(\sigma))^{-1}\,d\lambda\in\mathscr{L}(F).

Then
\pi(\sigma)=\pi^{*}(\sigma)=\pi(\sigma)^{2}
is the Riesz projection onto the generalized eigenspaces associated with eigenvalues of
p(\sigma)
that have positive real part for
|\sigma|<\varepsilon
. In particular, for
\sigma
real,
\pi(\sigma)
is an orthogonal projection. Note that
\pi(\sigma)
is holomorphic in
\sigma
in view of the Dunford integral representation formula. We’ll see momentarily that the
\pi
in the statement of the lemma is going to be
\pi:=\pi(0)
, but first define
w(\sigma)
via

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial B_{\varrho-\varepsilon}(\varrho)}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda-p(\sigma))^{-1}\,d\lambda+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial B_{\varrho-\varepsilon}(-\varrho)}(-\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda-p(\sigma))^{-1}\,d\lambda.

Holomorphic functional calculus implies that
w(\sigma)=w^{\star}(\sigma)
is invertible with
w^{\star}pw=\pi(\sigma)-(1-\pi(\sigma))
, so
p(\sigma)\sim_{s}\pi(\sigma)-(1-\pi(\sigma))
. Now, making
\varepsilon>0
smaller if necessary, we may further assume that
\|\pi(\sigma)-\pi(0)\|_{\mathscr{L}(F)}<1
for all
|\sigma|<\varepsilon
. Let then

\displaystyle u(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\bigl[\pi(\sigma)\pi(0)+[1-\pi(\sigma)][1-\pi(0)]\bigr]\bigl[1-[\pi(\sigma)-\pi(0)]^{2}\bigr]^{-\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle u^{\star}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\bigl[1-[\pi(\sigma)-\pi(0)]^{2}\bigr]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigl[\pi(0)\pi(\sigma)+[1-\pi(0)][1-\pi(\sigma)]\bigr].

We have
u^{\star}(\sigma)=u(\sigma)^{-1}
, and
u^{\star}(\sigma)\pi(\sigma)u(\sigma)=\pi(0)
. Thus
\pi(\sigma)-(1-\pi(\sigma))\sim_{s}\pi(0)-(1-\pi(0))
. In conclusion,

p(\sigma)\sim_{s}\pi(\sigma)-(1-\pi(\sigma))\sim_{s}\pi(0)-(1-\pi(0)).

∎

Proposition C.4
.

Let
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
be arbitrary. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and an orthogonal decomposition

F=\bigoplus\limits_{\ell=0}^{N}\bigl(F_{+,\ell}\oplus F_{-,\ell}\bigr)

such that

p(\sigma)\sim_{s}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{N}\bigl(\pi_{F_{+,\ell}}-\pi_{F_{-,\ell}}\bigr)\sigma^{\ell},

where
\pi_{F_{\pm},\ell}
is the orthogonal projection onto
F_{\pm,\ell}\subset F
. We note that some of the spaces
F_{\pm,\ell}
may be
\{0\}
.

Proof.

We prove this proposition by induction with respect to
\dim F
.

If
F=\mathbb{C}
we can write
p(\sigma)=\sigma^{N}\tilde{p}(\sigma)
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
\tilde{p}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(\mathbb{C})
with
\tilde{p}(0)\neq 0
. By Lemma
C.3
we have
\tilde{p}(\sigma)\sim_{s}\pm 1
, and so
p(\sigma)\sim_{s}\pm\sigma^{N}
, thus proving the result if
\dim F=1
.

We now assume that the proposition is valid for spaces
F
up to dimension
k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let then
F
be
(k+1)
-dimensional. We can write
p(\sigma)=\sigma^{\nu}\tilde{p}(\sigma)
, where
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\tilde{p}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
with
\tilde{p}(0)\neq 0
, and proving the claim for
\tilde{p}(\sigma)
implies that it holds for
p(\sigma)
as well. Thus we assume without loss of generality that
\nu=0
and
p(\sigma)=\tilde{p}(\sigma)
in the sequel.

If
p(0)
is invertible, Lemma
C.3
implies that
p(\sigma)\sim_{s}\pi-(1-\pi)
for some orthogonal projection
\pi
, thus proving the assertion in this case. So suppose now that
p(0)
is not invertible. Let
F_{0}=\ker p(0)
. Then
\{0\}\subsetneq F_{0}\subsetneq F
, so
1\leq\dim F_{0}\leq k
. We decompose
p(\sigma)
as

p(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}p_{11}(\sigma)&p_{12}(\sigma)\\
p_{21}(\sigma)&p_{22}(\sigma)\end{bmatrix}:\begin{array}[]{c}F_{0}\\
\oplus\\
F_{0}^{\perp}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{c}F_{0}\\
\oplus\\
F_{0}^{\perp}\end{array}.

Note that
p_{22}(\sigma)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F_{0}^{\perp})
is invertible for
|\sigma|
small enough. Define

u(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-p_{22}(\sigma)^{-1}p_{21}(\sigma)&1\end{bmatrix}:\begin{array}[]{c}F_{0}\\
\oplus\\
F_{0}^{\perp}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{c}F_{0}\\
\oplus\\
F_{0}^{\perp}\end{array}.

Then
u(\sigma)
is invertible, and

u^{\star}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}1&-p_{12}(\sigma)p_{22}(\sigma)^{-1}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}:\begin{array}[]{c}F_{0}\\
\oplus\\
F_{0}^{\perp}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{c}F_{0}\\
\oplus\\
F_{0}^{\perp}\end{array}.

We have

u^{\star}(\sigma)p(\sigma)u(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}p_{11}(\sigma)-p_{12}(\sigma)p_{22}(\sigma)^{-1}p_{21}(\sigma)&0\\
0&p_{22}(\sigma)\end{bmatrix},

where
q=p_{11}-p_{12}p_{22}^{-1}p_{21}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F_{0})
. Consequently, the proposition holds for
p
if it holds for both
q\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F_{0})
and
p_{22}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F_{0}^{\perp})
. But the inductive hypothesis is applicable to
q
, and Lemma
C.3
applies to
p_{22}
, thus proving the proposition for
p
. This completes the induction and finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem C.5
.

Let
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{sa}}(F)
be arbitrary. Then
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}:\mathscr{K}(p)\times\mathscr{K}(p)\to\mathbb{C}
is nondegenerate, and

\sig\bigl(\mathscr{K}(p),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}\bigr)=\sum\limits_{\ell\;\textup{odd}}\bigl(m_{+}(\ell)-m_{-}(\ell)\bigr)=\SF_{\sigma=0}\bigl[p(\sigma):F\to F\bigr],

where
m_{\pm}(\ell)
are the invariants associated with the triple
\bigl(\mathscr{K}(p),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)},M_{\sigma}\bigr)
.

Proof.

Propositions
C.2
and
C.4
imply that it suffices to prove the theorem for the special case
p(\sigma)=\sigma^{N}\textup{I}:F\to F
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In this situation

\mathscr{K}(p)=\Bigl\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}\sigma^{-j}+\mathfrak{H}_{0}(F);\;f_{j}\in F\Bigr\},

and for
\hat{v}(\sigma)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}\sigma^{-j}
and
\hat{w}(\sigma)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}g_{k}\sigma^{-k}
we get

[\hat{v},\hat{w}]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\langle f_{N+1-k},g_{k}\rangle_{F}=\biggl\langle J\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}\\
\vdots\\
f_{N}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}\\
\vdots\\
g_{N}\end{bmatrix}\biggr\rangle_{F^{N}},

where
J:F^{N}\to F^{N}
is given by
J\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}\\
\vdots\\
f_{N}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{N}\\
\vdots\\
f_{1}\end{bmatrix}
. We have
J^{2}=\textup{I}
, so
J
has eigenvalues
\pm 1
, proving the nondegeneracy of the pairing. The dimensions of the eigenspaces of
J
depend on the parity of
N
. Direct computation shows that for
N
even we have

\dim\ker(J-\textup{I})=\dim\ker(J+\textup{I})=\frac{N}{2}\dim F
, thus proving in this case that

\sig\bigl(\mathscr{K}(p),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}\bigr)=0=\SF_{\sigma=0}\bigl[p(\sigma):F\to F\bigr].

For
N
odd we have
\dim\ker(J-\textup{I})=\frac{N+1}{2}\dim F
and
\dim\ker(J+\textup{I})=\frac{N-1}{2}\dim F
, and thus

\sig\bigl(\mathscr{K}(p),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}\bigr)=\dim F=\SF_{\sigma=0}\bigl[p(\sigma):F\to F\bigr].

Finally, we note that if
\hat{v}(\sigma)
and
\hat{w}(\sigma)
above belong to
\ker[M_{\sigma}^{\ell}:\mathscr{K}(p)\to\mathscr{K}(p)]
, then

[M_{\sigma}^{\ell-1}\hat{v},\hat{w}]_{\mathscr{K}(p)}=\begin{cases}0&\ell\neq N,\\
\langle f_{N},g_{N}\rangle_{F}&\ell=N.\end{cases}

Thus
m_{+}(\ell)=m_{-}(\ell)=0
for
\ell\neq N
, while
m_{+}(N)=\dim F
and
m_{-}(N)=0
. The theorem is proved.
∎

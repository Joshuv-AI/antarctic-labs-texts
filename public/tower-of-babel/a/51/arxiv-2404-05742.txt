A geometric study of BZ operator on representations of GL n \displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{n} over non-archimedean field
Taiwang DENG

Abstract
. In this article, we geometrically study the partial Bernstein-Zelevinsky operator introduced in the author’s thesis, which generalizes the original Bernstein-Zelevinsky operator. We relate the partial Bernstein-Zelevinsky operator to the geometric induction of Lusztig and then perform explicit computations in special cases. Finally, we develop a symmetric reduction to the previously mentioned special cases



1.
Introduction

Zelevinsky
[
17
]
classifies
the admissible irreducible representations of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{n}(F)

in terms of multi-segments. More precisely,
given a multi-segment
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
, one can attach
to it an irreducible representation
\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{a}}
,
described as the unique irreducible sub-representation in
some standard representation
\displaystyle\pi(\mathbf{a})
constructed by
parabolic induction. In other words, in the
Grothendieck group
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
of the category of admissible
representations, we have

\pi(\mathbf{a})=L_{\mathbf{a}}+\sum_{\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{a}}m(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})L_{\mathbf{b}},\quad m(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

where "
\displaystyle<
" is suitable partial order imposed on the set of multi-segments.
In
[
2
, 4.5]
, Bernstein and Zelevinsky define an operator
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}
(We call it BZ operator) to be
an algebra homomorphism

\mathscr{D}:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{R},

which plays a crucial role in Zelevinsky’s classification theorem. We introduced a partial analogue of the BZ operator
in
[
6
, Definition 2.19]
(this is part of the author’s thesis
[
14
]
).

In this article,
we study the problem of computing the partial BZ operator

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(L_{\mathbf{a}})
of the irreducible representation

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{a}}
attached to a multisegment
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
. There are two motivations for this. The first is to use these computations
to calculate the multiplicities in certain induced representation
\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{a}}\times L_{\mathbf{b}}
in a forthcoming work. The second
is also related to forthcoming work, in which we study the relations between the category of equivariant perverse sheaves on quiver
varieties (in the sense of
[
6
, §4]
) and the category of perverse sheaves on flag varieties base on ideas of
[
6
, §4]
.
We understand that this is a dual version of
[
5
]
. We further want to relate the partial BZ operator to the translation functor
in representation of real Lie groups based on work of the present paper.

Let us discuss in more details about the content of the paper.
From Zelevinsky’s classification theorem, we can write

L_{\mathbf{a}}=\sum_{\mathbf{b}}\tilde{m}_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}\pi(\mathbf{a}).

Thus, the task boils down to calculating:

\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))=\sum_{\mathbf{b}}n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}L_{\mathbf{b}},~\qquad n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}\geq 0.

We first introduce a new poset structure
\displaystyle\preceq_{k}

on the set of multisegments so that we show the equivalence
between
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}>0
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
, cf. Proposition
2.6
.

The primary result of this paper
is the interpretation of the coefficient

\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}
as the value at
\displaystyle q=1
of some
Poincaré series of the Lusztig product of
two explicit perverse sheaves on the space of graded nilpotent classes, cf. Theorem

4.8
. This relies crucially on the work of Lusztig, as discussed in section
3
.
Note that the category of equivariant perverse sheaves should be
understood as the dual of the category of representations of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{n}(F)
. Given that the BZ operator is related to the Jacquet functor, on the dual side, it should relate to geometric induction.

In section
5
, we compute these Lusztig products as the push forward by a projection

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime\prime}
, cf. Theorem
5.19
, of some concrete perverse sheaf on
some space of graded nilpotent classes. This is our second main results.

Section
6
-
7
are dedicated to a concrete study of the coefficient
\displaystyle n(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})
in the Grassmanian case, from which we deduce some combinatorial formulae for it, cf. Proposition
7.6
.

In section
8
, we continue to study the case where the
multisegments are of Grassmanian type. In this case the projection
\displaystyle\beta^{\prime\prime}
is simple
(cf. Proposition
8.6
), being the natural projection

GL_{n}/P\rightarrow GL_{n}/P^{\prime}

with
\displaystyle P\subseteq P^{\prime}
two parabolic subgroups.
The constructions and proofs in this case are very close to the case of
Grassmanian type.

Finally in the last section
9
, we obtain a complete formula for

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(L_{\mathbf{a}})
in the general case, cf. Corollary
9.16
. It’s worth noting that to achieve this, we require a reduction process to the parabolic cases (cf. Proposition
9.6
) in the style of
[
6
, §6.2]
. This elucidates the extensive discussion thereof.

2.
New Poset Structure on Multisegments

We keep the same notations as in
[
6
, §2]
.
Recall that in
[
6
, Definition 2.12]
we have introduced a partial order
\displaystyle\leq
on the set of multisegments and the finite set

S(\mathbf{a})=\{\mathbf{b}:\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}\}

following
[
17
, 7.1]
.
We also recall that in
[
6
, Notation 2.15]
the standard representation
\displaystyle\pi(\mathbf{a})
was attached to a multisegment

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
and its decomposition in the Grothendieck group into irreducible ones was considered:

\pi(\mathbf{a})=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in S(\mathbf{a})}m(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})L_{\mathbf{b}},\quad m(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})\geq 0

(1)

where
\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{a}}
denotes the irreducible representation attached to
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
.
Here we have taken the cuspidal representation
\displaystyle\rho=1
to be the trivial representation of
\displaystyle GL_{1}
, an assumption that we will
make throughout this paper.
In this section we define a new poset structure
\displaystyle\preceq_{k}
depending on an
integer
\displaystyle k
on
the set of multisegments and show that the term
\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{b}}
appears in

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))
(cf.
[
6
, Definition 2.19]
) if and only if
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
.

Let
\displaystyle b(\Delta)
(resp.
\displaystyle e(\Delta)
)denote the beginning (resp. the end) of a segment
\displaystyle\Delta
.
We also use the notation

b(\mathbf{a})(\text{ \ resp. \ }e(\mathbf{a}))

(2)

to denote the multi-set of beginnings (resp. ends) in a multisegment
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
.
If
\displaystyle\Delta=[i,j]
we set
\displaystyle\Delta^{-}=[i,j-1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\displaystyle\Delta
be a segment, we define

\Delta^{(k)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\Delta^{-},\text{ if }e(\Delta)=k;\\
&\hskip-14.22636pt\Delta,\text{ otherwise }.\end{array}\right.

For a multisegment

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}=\{\Delta_{1},\cdots,\Delta_{r}\},

we define

\mathbf{a}^{(k)}=\{\Delta_{1}^{(k)},\cdots,\Delta_{r}^{(k)}\}.

Definition 2.2
.

For a well ordered multisegment
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}=\{\Delta_{1},\cdots,\Delta_{s}\}

with
\displaystyle\Delta_{1}\preceq\cdots\preceq\Delta_{s}
,
let

\mathbf{a}(k):=\{\Delta\in\mathbf{a}:e(\Delta)=k\}=\{\Delta_{i_{0}},\Delta_{i_{0}+1},\cdots,\Delta_{i_{1}}\}.

Now let
\displaystyle\Gamma\subseteq\mathbf{a}(k)
, let

\mathbf{a}(k)_{\Gamma}:=(\mathbf{a}(k)\setminus\Gamma)\cup\{\Delta^{(k)}:\Delta\in\Gamma\},

and

\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}:=(\mathbf{a}\setminus\mathbf{a}(k))\cup\mathbf{a}(k)_{\Gamma}.

We say
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
if there exist a multisegment
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}\in S(\mathbf{a})
such that

\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}(\text{ cf. \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Deng23}{}{}, Definition 2.12]}})

for some
\displaystyle\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))=\pi(\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\Gamma\subseteq\mathbf{a}(k),\Gamma\neq\emptyset}\pi(\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}).

(3)

Proof.

Let

\mathbf{a}=\{\Delta_{1},\cdots,\Delta_{r},\Delta_{r+1},\cdots,\}.

Then

\pi(\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}L_{\Delta_{i}}\times\prod_{i>r}L_{\Delta_{i}}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(L_{\Delta_{i}}+L_{\Delta_{i}^{(k)}})\times\prod_{i>r}L_{\Delta_{i}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\pi(\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\Gamma\subseteq\mathbf{a}(k),\Gamma\neq\emptyset}\pi(\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}).

∎

Recall that in
[
6
, Notation 2.16]
, we use the notation
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
to denote the Grothendieck group
of all finite length unipotent representations of
\displaystyle GL_{n}(F)
with
\displaystyle n=0,1,2,\ldots
for a non-archimedean local field
\displaystyle F
.
Note that
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
is a polynomial algebra endowed with a natural Hopf algebra structure (
[
6
, Proposition 2.17 and Corollary 2.18]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\in S(\mathbf{a})
, then
\displaystyle\pi(\mathbf{a})-\pi(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

By choosing a maximal chain of multisegments between
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}
, we can assume that

\mathbf{a}=\{\Delta_{1},\cdots,\Delta_{r}\},

\mathbf{b}=(\mathbf{a}\backslash\{\Delta_{j},\Delta_{k}\})\cup\{\Delta_{j}\cap\Delta_{k},\Delta_{j}\cup\Delta_{k}\}.

Then by
[
15
, section 4.6]
,

\pi(\mathbf{a})=\pi(\mathbf{b})+L_{\Delta_{1}}\times\cdots\times\widehat{L}_{\Delta_{j}}\times\cdots\times\widehat{L}_{\Delta_{k}}\times\cdots\times L_{\Delta_{r}}\times L_{\{\Delta_{j},\Delta_{k}\}}.

∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))=\sum_{\mathbf{b}}n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}L_{\mathbf{b}}.

(4)

Then
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}>0
if and only if
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Theorem 2.22]

the coefficients
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}
in (
4
) are
all non-negative (but notice that the
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}
are not the
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}
in loc.cit.).
Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
,
then by definition we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}
for some
\displaystyle\Gamma
.
Therefore
\displaystyle m_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}}>0
(cf. (
1
)), now
we have
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}>0
by
(
3
). Conversely,
if
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}>0
, then by
(
3
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}
for some
\displaystyle\Gamma
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

We have
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}

if and only if
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))-\pi(\mathbf{b})\geq 0

in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

By
Proposition
2.6

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}
for some
\displaystyle\Gamma\subseteq\mathbf{a}(k)
.
By Lemma
2.5

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma}
implies that

\displaystyle\pi(\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma})-\pi(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
.
Since
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))-\pi(\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma})\geq 0
by
(
3
), we have
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))-\pi(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
.
Conversely, if
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))-\pi(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
,
we have
\displaystyle n(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})>0
, hence
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}

by Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

We define for
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}
,

\ell(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})=\max_{n}\{n:\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}_{0}\geq\mathbf{b}_{1}\cdots\geq\mathbf{b}_{n}=\mathbf{b}\},

and
\displaystyle\ell(a)=\ell(\mathbf{a}_{\min},\mathbf{a})
.

Proposition 2.10
.

For any
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
, there exists
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}\in S(\mathbf{a})
,
and some subset
\displaystyle\Gamma\subseteq\mathbf{c}(k)
,
such that

\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}_{\Gamma}.

Conversely, if
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}_{\Gamma}
for some
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}\in S(\mathbf{a})
,
then
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
.

Proof.

For the converse part, suppose
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}\neq\mathbf{a}
,
by (
3
), we have
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{c}))-\pi(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
.
By Lemme
2.5
, we
know that
\displaystyle\pi(\mathbf{a})-\pi(\mathbf{c})\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
, hence
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}))-\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{c}))\geq 0

by
[
6
, Theorem 2.22]
. Therefore
\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}}>0
.
Hence we have
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
.

For the direct part, suppose that

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
, hence
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma_{1}}
for some
\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}
. We prove by induction on
\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}_{T})
.
If
\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma_{1}})=0
, then
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma_{1}}
, we are done.
Now let
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{d}\leq\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma_{1}}
such that

\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{d})=1
, by induction,

\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{\Gamma_{0}},

for some
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in S(\mathbf{a})
.
Note that by replacing
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
by
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
, we can assume that

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma_{1}}
and
\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}_{\Gamma_{1}})=1
.

By definition
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}
is obtained by
applying the elementary operation to a pair of segments
\displaystyle\{\Delta\preceq\Delta^{\prime}\}

in
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{T}
. Now we set out to construct
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}
.

•

If
\displaystyle\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\}\subseteq\mathbf{a}\setminus\{\Delta^{(k)}:\Delta\in\Gamma_{1}\}\subseteq\mathbf{a}
,
let
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}
be the multisegment obtained by applying the elementary operations to

\displaystyle\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\}
. And we have

\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}_{\Gamma_{1}}.

•

If
\displaystyle\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\}\cap\{\Delta^{(k)}:\Delta\in\Gamma_{1}\}=\{\Delta^{\prime}\}
,
then
\displaystyle\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime+}\}\in\mathbf{a}

let
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}
be the
multisegment obtained by applying the elementary operations to

\displaystyle\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime+}\}
. Then
let

\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1}\setminus\{\Delta^{\prime+}\})\cup\{\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime+}\}

and we have

\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}_{\Gamma}.

•

If
\displaystyle\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\}\cap\{\Delta^{(k)}:\Delta\in\Gamma_{1}\}=\{\Delta\}
,
then
\displaystyle\{\Delta^{+},\Delta^{\prime}\}\in\mathbf{a}

let
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}
be the
multisegment obtained by applying the elementary operations to

\displaystyle\{\Delta^{+},\Delta^{\prime}\}
. Then let

\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1}\setminus\{\Delta^{+}\})\cup\{\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}\}

and we have

\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}_{\Gamma}.

Hence we are done.

∎

We use the notation
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}
to denote the set of multisegments with support in
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
(cf.
[
6
, Definition 2.14]
).

Proposition 2.11
.

The relation
\displaystyle\preceq_{k}
defines a
poset structure on
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

By definition we have
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}

for any
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{O}
.
Suppose
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{1}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}_{2},\mathbf{a}_{2}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}_{3}
,
we want to show that
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{1}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}_{3}
.
By proposition
2.10
, there exists
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}\in S(\mathbf{a}_{2})

and
\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}\subseteq\mathbf{c}(k)

, such that

\mathbf{a}_{1}=\mathbf{c}_{\Gamma_{1}}.

Note that
by Corollary
2.8
,
the fact
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{2}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}_{3}
implies
\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k}(\pi(\mathbf{a}_{3}))-\pi(\mathbf{a}_{2})\geq 0
.
Hence we have
\displaystyle n(\mathbf{a}_{3},\mathbf{c})>0
, therefore
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}_{3}
by Proposition
2.6
.
In turn there exists a multisegment
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in S(\mathbf{a}_{3})

and
\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}\subseteq\mathbf{c}^{\prime}(k)
, such that

\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{\Gamma_{2}}.

Since we have
\displaystyle\mathbf{c}(k)\subseteq\mathbf{c}^{\prime}(k)
, we take

\Gamma_{3}:=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}\subseteq\mathbf{c}^{\prime}(k).

Now we get

\mathbf{a}_{1}=\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{\Gamma_{3}},

which implies
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{1}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}_{3}
by Proposition
2.10
.
Finally, if
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{b}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}
, then by definition
we have
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}
.

∎

Definition 2.12
.

We let

\Gamma(\mathbf{a},k)=\{\mathbf{b}:\mathbf{b}\preceq_{k}\mathbf{a}\}.

Canonical Basis and Quantum Algebras

In this section, we recall
the results of Lusztig
[

]
on canonical basis while keeping the notations of
[
6
]

and draw the connection to the algebra
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
base on

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{N}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
be the semi-group of sequences
\displaystyle(d_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
of non negative integers
which are zero for all but finitely many
\displaystyle j
.
Let
\displaystyle\alpha_{i}
be the element whose
\displaystyle i
-th term is 1 and other terms are zero.

Definition 3.2
.

We define a symmetric bilinear form on
\displaystyle\mathbb{N}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
given by

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2,&\text{ for }i=j;\\
-1,&\text{ for }|i-j|=1;\\
0,&\text{ otherwise }.\end{array}\right.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle q
be an indeterminate and
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/2})
be the fractional field of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}]
.
Let
\displaystyle U_{q}^{\geq 0}
be the
\displaystyle Q(q^{1/2})
-algebra generated by the elements
\displaystyle E_{i}
and
\displaystyle K_{i}^{\pm 1}
for
\displaystyle i\in\mathbb{Z}

with the following relations:

\displaystyle\displaystyle K_{i}K_{j}=K_{j}K_{i},~K_{i}K_{i}^{-1}=1;

\displaystyle\displaystyle K_{i}E_{i}=q^{1/2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}E_{i}K_{i};

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{i}E_{j}=E_{j}E_{i},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{ if }|i-j|>1;

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{i}^{2}E_{j}-(q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2})E_{i}E_{j}E_{i}+E_{j}E_{i}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{ if }|i-j|=1.

and let
\displaystyle U^{+}
be the subalgebra generated by
the
\displaystyle E_{i}
’s.

Remark:
This is the
\displaystyle+
part of the quantized enveloping algebra
\displaystyle U
associated by Drinfeld
and Jimbo to the root system
\displaystyle A_{\infty}
of
\displaystyle SL_{\infty}
. And for
\displaystyle q=1
, this specializes to the
classical enveloping algebra of the nilpotent radical of a Borel subalgebra.

Definition 3.4
.

We define a new order on the set of segments
\displaystyle\Sigma

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&[j,k]\lhd[m,n],\text{ if }k<n,\\
&[j,k]\rhd[m,n],\text{ if }j<m,n=k.\end{array}\right.

We also denote
\displaystyle[j,k]\lhd[m,n]
or
\displaystyle[j,k]=[m,n]
by
\displaystyle\unlhd[m,n]
.

Remark:
This should be compared with
[
6
, Definition 2.5]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The algebra
\displaystyle U_{q}^{+}
is
\displaystyle\mathbb{N}^{(\mathbb{Z})}
-graded via the weight function
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{wt}}\nolimits(E_{i})=\alpha_{i}
.
Moreover, for a given weight
\displaystyle\alpha
, the homogeneous component of
\displaystyle U_{q}^{+}
with weight
\displaystyle\alpha

is of finite dimension, and its basis are naturally parametrized by the multisegments of the same
weight.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{i_{s},j_{s}}[i_{s},j_{s}]
be a multisegment of weight
\displaystyle\alpha
(cf.
[
6
, Definition 3.1]
), note that
here we identify the weight
\displaystyle\varphi_{[i]}
with
\displaystyle\alpha_{i}
, and that

[i_{1},j_{1}]\unlhd\cdots\unlhd[i_{r},j_{r}](\text{ cf. Definition \ref{def: 7.2.4}})

Then we associate to
\displaystyle\mathbf{a}
the element

(E_{j_{1}}\cdots E_{i_{1}})\cdots(E_{j_{r}}\cdots E_{i_{r}}).

In this way we obtain the desired parametrization.
∎

Notation 3.6
.

For
\displaystyle x\in U^{+}
be an element of degree
\displaystyle\alpha
, we will denote
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{wt}}\nolimits(x)=\alpha
.

Example 3.7
.

For
\displaystyle i\leq j
, let
\displaystyle\alpha_{ij}=\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}
. Consider the homogeneous components
of
\displaystyle U^{+}
with weight
\displaystyle\alpha=2\alpha_{12}

E_{1}E_{2}E_{1}E_{2},~E_{1}E_{1}E_{2}E_{2}.

The element
\displaystyle E_{1}E_{2}E_{1}E_{2}
is parametrized by the
multisegment
\displaystyle[1]+[1,2]+[2]

\displaystyle E_{1}E_{1}E_{2}E_{2}
is parametrized

\displaystyle 2[1]+2[2]

In
[

]

\displaystyle U_{q}^{+}

Following

Definition 3.8
.

We define

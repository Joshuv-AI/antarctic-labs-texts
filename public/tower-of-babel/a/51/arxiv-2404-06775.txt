Probabilistic channel simulation using coherence
Benchi Zhao, Kosuke Ito, Keisuke Fujii

Abstract
Channel simulation using coherence, which refers to realizing a target channel with coherent states and free operations, is a fundamental problem in the quantum resource theory of coherence. The limitations of the accuracy of deterministic channel simulation motivate us to consider the more general probabilistic framework. In this paper, we develop the framework for probabilistic channel simulation using coherence with free operations. When the chosen set of free operations is the maximally incoherent operations, we provide an efficiently computable semidefinite program (SDP) to calculate the maximal success probability and derive the analytic expression of success probability for some special cases. When the chosen set of free operations is the dephasing-covariant incoherent operations (DIO), we show that if the target channel is not a resource nonactivating channel, then one cannot simulate it exactly both deterministically and probabilistically. The SDP for maximal success probability of simulating a channel by DIO is also given correspondingly.

Initial research primarily focused on the quantification and interconversion of

;

Later on, a more general framework was proposed, called

;

;

A given resource state does not necessarily allow for the exact and deterministic MIO simulation of a given target quantum channel, although for each channel there exists a state such that such a simulation is possible

;
D\́mathbf{i}az et al. 2018
. In fact, if we can only access the resource state

Theurer et al. 2019

The limitation of deterministic channel simulation motivates us to consider a more general probabilistic framework, in which the channel simulation will succeed only with some probability. The probabilistic framework has been applied to many quantum tasks and has shown advantages over deterministic ones. For example, in Ref.
Regula 2022
, the author observes that one can transfer the quantum state from
\rho
to
\sigma

, with the same input state, the output state by probabilistic distillation is closer to the maximal coherent state than deterministic distillation.

In this paper, we focus on the probabilistic channel simulation using coherence, characterizing the relation between the maximal success probability and the distance from simulated channel
{\cal L}
and target channel
{\cal N}
. In the first part, we show three cases of channel simulation with MIO depending on the types of the resource state. (i) If the resource state is the rank-
m
maximally coherent state
\omega=\Psi_{m}
, we provide the analytical expression of maximal success probability. (ii) If the resource state
\omega
is a pure coherent state, we derive the non-zero success probability. (iii) If the resource state
\omega
is a general coherent state, we provide an efficiently computable semidefinite program (SDP) to achieve the maximal success probability. In the second part, we concentrate on the channel simulation with DIO.
It was proved in Ref.
Theurer et al. 2019
that if the target channel
{\cal N}
is not a resource nonactivating channel, then it cannot be simulated exactly by DIO with any resource state deterministically. We further show that such an exact simulation is impossible even in probabilistic scenarios.
We then provide the efficiently computable SDP for achieving the maximal success probability of channel simulation with DIO. Our paper fills an important gap in the literature by establishing the probabilistic toolbox for the key resource of quantum coherence.

III
Probabilistic channel simulation with MIO

In this section, we are going to study the channel simulation with MIO under three cases. First, if the resource state is the rank-
m
maximally coherent state, we provide the analytical expression of the maximal success probability. Second, if the resource state
\omega
is a pure coherent state, we derive analytical lower bounds on the maximal success probability. Third, for general coherent states, we provide an efficiently computable SDP to determine the maximal success probability.

Theorem 1

Given an arbitrary target channel
{\cal N}
and a fixed maximally coherent state
\Psi_{m}
of dimension
m\geq 2
, the maximal success probability of the exact channel simulation with MIO is

P_{\rm MIO}({\cal N},\Psi_{m},\varepsilon=0)=\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{C_{R}({\cal N})}\Big\}.

(5)

Proof.

If a target channel
{\cal N}
can be simulated by MIO and the given resource state
\Psi_{m}
, it means that there exists an operation
{\cal M}\in{\rm MIO}
, such that

{\cal M}(\Psi_{m}\otimes\cdot)={\cal L}(\cdot)=p|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes{\cal N}(\cdot)+(1-p)|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes{\cal K}(\cdot),

with
p>0
. The output
{\cal K}(\cdot)
is a failure branch, which should be discarded. And the channel
{\cal K}
can be arbitrary MIO, i.e.,
C_{R}({\cal K})=0
.
The main idea is to prove
C_{R}({\cal L})=pC_{R}({\cal N})
, which will be obtained directly by proving
C_{R}({\cal L})\leq pC_{R}({\cal N})
and
C_{R}({\cal L})\geq pC_{R}({\cal N})
.

Due to the convexity of the ROC of channels
D\́mathbf{i}az et al. 2018
, we have

\displaystyle C_{R}({\cal L})

\displaystyle=C_{R}(p|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes{\cal N}+(1-p)|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes{\cal K})

\displaystyle\leq pC_{R}(|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes{\cal N})+(1-p)C_{R}(|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes{\cal K})

\displaystyle=pC_{R}({\cal N})+(1-p)C_{R}({\cal K})

\displaystyle=pC_{R}({\cal N}).

(6)

The third equation holds because removing and appending systems in incoherent states are in MIO.

On the other hand, from the definition of the ROC of a channel, we have

\displaystyle 1+C_{R}({\cal L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda|p{\cal N}_{[0]}+(1-p){\cal K}_{[1]}\leq\lambda{\cal M},{\cal M}\in\text{MIO}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\left\{\lambda\Big|{\cal N}_{[0]}\leq\frac{\lambda{\cal M}-(1-p){\cal K}_{[1]}}{p},{\cal M}\in\text{MIO}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\lambda\Big|{\cal N}_{[0]}\leq\frac{\lambda+p-1}{p}\cdot\frac{\lambda{\cal M}-(1-p){\cal K}_{[1]}}{\lambda+p-1},{\cal M}\in\text{MIO}\Big\}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{*},

(8)

where we define
{\cal N}_{[0]}=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes{\cal N}
and
{\cal M}_{[1]}=|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes{\cal M}
. Here, the minimum
\lambda^{*}
always exists, which can be straightforwardly derived from Theorem 2 in Ref.
D\́mathbf{i}az et al. 2018
.
For convenience, we define
{\cal M}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\lambda{\cal M}-(1-p){\cal K}_{[1]}}{\lambda+p-1}
for
{\cal M}\in MIO
.
Because the minimum is attained by
\lambda\geq 1
, the inequality in Eq. (
7
) ensures that
{\cal M}^{\prime}
is completely positive, i.e.,
0\leq[p/(\lambda+p-1)]{\cal N}_{[0]}\leq{\cal M}^{\prime}
. Since both
{\cal M},{\cal K}_{[1]}\in\mathrm{MIO}
, we have
\operatorname{Tr}[{\cal M}(\rho)]=\operatorname{Tr}\rho=\operatorname{Tr}[{\cal K}_{[1]}(\rho)]
and
{\cal M}(\rho),{\cal K}_{[1]}(\rho)\in{\cal I}
for any free state
\rho\in{\cal I}
.
Therefore, we have

{\cal M}^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{\lambda{\cal M}(\rho)-(1-p){\cal K}_{[1]}(\rho)}{\lambda+p-1}\in{\cal I}\quad(\forall\rho\in{\cal I}),

and
\operatorname{Tr}[{\cal M}^{\prime}(\rho)]=\operatorname{Tr}\rho
.
Hence, we obtain
{\cal M}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{MIO}
.
Then,
noting that
C_{R}({\cal N})=C_{R}({\cal N}_{[0]})
, we have

\displaystyle 1+C_{R}({\cal N})

\displaystyle=1+C_{R}({\cal N}_{[0]})

\displaystyle=\min\{\tau|{\cal N}_{[0]}\leq\tau{\cal M},{\cal M}\in\text{MIO}\},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{*}+p-1}{p}.

Because
\lambda^{*}=1+C_{R}({\cal L})
from Eq. (
8
), we arrive at

pC_{R}({\cal N})\leq C_{R}({\cal L}).

(9)

Combined with Eq. (
6
), we obtain

pC_{R}({\cal N})=C_{R}({\cal L}).

(10)

From Theorem 4 in Ref.
D\́mathbf{i}az et al. 2018
, there exists an MIO to simulate channel
{\cal L}
if and only if
m-1\geq C_{R}({\cal L})
. By considering Eq. (
10
), we can rephrase that there exists an MIO to simulate channel
{\cal N}
probabilistically if and only if the success probability
p
satisfies

\frac{m-1}{C_{R}({\cal N})}\geq p.

(11)

Because the probability cannot exceed 1, the maximal success probability is

P_{\text{MIO}}({\cal N},\Psi_{m},\varepsilon=0)=\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{C_{R}({\cal N})}\Big\}.

(12)

The proof is complete.

\sqcap
\sqcup

Theorem
1
implies that the success probability of simulating a channel by using MIO and maximally coherent states is always greater than zero, i.e.,
P_{\text{MIO}}({\cal N},\Psi_{m},\varepsilon=0)>0
with
m\geq 2
. In other words, any quantum channel can be simulated by MIO probabilistically with

.

According to Theorem
1
, if given enough resource, i.e.,
C_{R}(\Psi_{m})=m-1\geq C_{R}({\cal N})
, it implies the success probability equals to 1, thus we can simulate the channel deterministically. Otherwise, if we can only access a limited amount of coherence, i.e.,
C_{R}(\Psi_{m})=m-1<C_{R}({\cal N})
, then the channel fails to be simulated deterministically.
However, we can still succeed in such a simulation probabilistically, with the probability given by the ratio between the robustness of resource state
\Psi_{m}
and the robustness of coherent channel
{\cal N}
, i.e.,

p=\frac{m-1}{C_{R}({\cal N})}.

(13)

In this sense, Theorem
1
quantitatively shows in terms of the resource measures how the advantage of probabilistic channel simulation over the deterministic one appears. Examples of coherent unitary channels are studied to demonstrate such an advantage in the end of this section.

If we are allowed to simulate target channel
{\cal N}

which satisfies

Corollary 2

Given an arbitrary target channel

\displaystyle P_{\rm MIO}({\cal N},\Psi_{m},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal L}}P_{\rm MIO}({\cal L},\Psi_{m},0)

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{\min_{\cal L}C_{R}({\cal L})}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{C_{R}^{\varepsilon}({\cal N})}\Big\},

(14)

where
{\cal L}
are

.

From Corollary

, we can approximately simulate the target channel. The more error we allow, the higher the success probability that we can simulate it.

For a further step, instead of using a maximally coherent state, we consider a general pure coherent state. We obtain the following corollary, which gives a lower bound of the maximal success probability for the general pure resource state.

Corollary 3

Given target channel
{\cal N}
and coherent pure state
\psi=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
, where
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}|i\rangle
,
\psi_{i}\neq 0,n\geq 2
, the maximal success probability of channel simulation with MIO up to error

is lower bounded by

\displaystyle P_{\rm MIO}({\cal N},\psi,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\geq P_{\rm MIO}({\cal N},\Psi_{m},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\varepsilon})\times P^{\rm distill}_{\rm MIO}(|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\rightarrow\Psi_{m}),

(15)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\geq\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{C_{R}^{\varepsilon}({\cal N})}\times\frac{n^{2}}{m(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\psi_{i}|^{-2})}\Big\}},

(16)

where
P^{\rm distill}_{\rm MIO}(|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\rightarrow\Psi_{m})

Proof.

Before starting the proof, we need to note that arbitrary coherent pure state

, which is

P^{\rm distill}_{\rm MIO}(\psi\rightarrow\Psi_{m})\geq\frac{n^{2}}{m(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\psi_{i}|^{-2})}>0,

where

In order to simulate channel

using a given coherent pure state

from

\displaystyle P_{\rm MIO}({\cal N},\psi,\varepsilon)\geq P_{\rm MIO}({\cal N},\Psi_{m},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\varepsilon})\times P^{\rm distill}_{\rm MIO}(|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\rightarrow\Psi_{m}).

By considering Eq. (

\displaystyle P_{\rm MIO}({\cal N},\psi,\varepsilon)\geq\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{C_{R}^{\varepsilon}({\cal N})}\Big\}\times\frac{n^{2}}{m(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\psi_{i}|^{-2})}.

The proof is completed.

This corollary implies that if we take an arbitrary coherent pure state as a resource state, we can simulate an arbitrary channel by MIO with non-zero probability. For a more general case, where the given resource state is a coherent mixed state, we provide an efficiently computable SDP to achieve the maximal success probability of channel simulation. We thus consider a generalization of the set of free operations

Lemma 4

For any triplet
({\cal N},\omega,\varepsilon)
and operation class
{\cal O}
, the maximal success probability of coherence channel simulation
P_{\cal O}({\cal N},\omega,\varepsilon)=\max\{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}|{\cal E}(\omega\otimes\cdot)=p{\cal L}(\cdot),\frac{1}{2}||{\cal L}-{\cal N}||_{\diamond}\leq\varepsilon,{\cal E}\in{\cal O}_{\rm sub}\}
,
where
{\cal L}
are the implemented channels

Proof.

For any quantum operation
\Lambda(\omega\otimes\rho)=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes{\cal E}_{0}(\omega\otimes\rho)+|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes{\cal E}_{1}(\omega\otimes\rho)
, where
{\cal E}_{0}
and
{\cal E}_{1}
are two subnormalized operations, we can check that
\Lambda\in{\cal O}
if and only if
{\cal E}_{0},{\cal E}_{1}\in{\cal O}_{\rm sub}
, and
{\cal E}_{0}+{\cal E}_{1}
is trace preserving. Thus, the optimization in Eq. (
4
) is equivalent to finding the optimal subnormalized operations

and
{\cal E}_{1}
such that
{\cal E}_{0}(\omega\otimes\rho)=p{\cal L}(\rho),{\cal E}_{1}(\omega\otimes\rho)=(1-p)\mathbb{I}/d,\frac{1}{2}||{\cal L}-{\cal N}||_{\diamond}\leq\varepsilon
, and
{\cal E}_{0}+{\cal E}_{1}
is trace preserving. Since we can take

, without compromising the success probability, the maximal success probability of coherence channel simulation is only dependent on

\sqcup

By generalizing the free operation into the subnormalized version, Lemma

simplifies the optimization of the maximal success probability. For further steps, we formulate the success probability optimization as an efficiently computable SDP, which is shown as follows.

Proposition 5

For a given triplet
({\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B},\omega,\varepsilon)
, the maximal success probability to simulate the target channel with MIO is given by

\displaystyle t_{\rm min}=

\displaystyle\min~t;

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{R}[J_{\tilde{{\cal E}}}(\omega^{T}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})]=J_{{\cal L}_{AB}};

(20a)

\displaystyle J_{\tilde{{\cal E}}}\geq 0,\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[J_{\tilde{{\cal E}}}]\leq t\mathbb{I}_{RA};

(20b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[J_{\cal L}]=\mathbb{I}_{A};

(20d)

\displaystyle Z\geq 0,Z\geq J_{\cal L}-J_{\cal N},\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[Z]\leq\varepsilon\mathbb{I}_{A}.

Proof.

To get rid of the non-linearity, we consider the map
\tilde{{\cal E}}=t{\cal E}
, where

, the inverse of success probability, and
{\cal E}
is a subnormalized MIO. The notations of

and

are the Choi-Jamiołkowski matrix of maps
\tilde{{\cal E}}
and
{\cal L}
, respectively.

is the Choi-Jamiołkowski matrix of the target channel
{\cal N}
. Eq. (

\tilde{{\cal E}}(\omega\otimes\cdot)={\cal L}(\cdot)

20b

From the result in Ref.

A general qubit unitary possesses four real parameters. However, we can transform unitaries into each other without any additional cost by incorporating coherent unitaries before or after them. This observation implies the existence of an equivalence relation among qubit unitaries up to coherent unitaries. A unique representative of each equivalence class
Dana et al. 2017
;
D\́mathbf{i}az et al. 2018
is given in Eq. (
21
)

U_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},

(21)

where
\theta\in[0,\pi/4]
.

Here we choose the unitary channel
{\cal U}_{\theta}^{(l)}(\cdot)=U_{\theta}^{\otimes l}\cdot U_{\theta}^{\dagger\otimes l}
, as the target channel to simulate. The success probability of unitary channel simulation
{\cal U}_{\theta}^{(l)}
can be derived from the robustness of the channel. We take the default that
{\cal U}_{\theta}
refers to
l=1
. The specific statement is shown in the following.

Proposition 6

Given the triplet
({\cal U}_{\theta}^{(l)},\Psi_{m},\varepsilon=0)
, the maximal success probability of channel simulation is

\displaystyle P_{\rm MIO}({\cal U}_{\theta}^{(l)},\Psi_{m},\varepsilon=0)

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{C_{R}({\cal U}_{\theta}^{(l)})}\Big\}

(22)

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{(1+\sin{2\theta})^{l}-1}\Big\}.

(23)

Proof.

The cohering power
Mani and Karimipour 2015
;
Bu et al. 2017
of a channel
P_{R}({\cal N})
is defined as

P_{R}({\cal N})=\max_{i}\log(1+C_{R}({\cal N}(|i\rangle\!\langle i|))).

(24)

We denote
i^{*}
to be the optimal solution, i.e.,
P_{R}({\cal N})=\log(1+C_{R}({\cal N}(|i^{*}\rangle\!\langle i^{*}|)))
. In
D\́mathbf{i}az et al. 2018
, it has been proved that the cohering power of a channel
P_{R}({\cal N})
is equivalent to the log-robustness of the channel, which is
P_{R}({\cal N})=\log(1+C_{R}({\cal N}))
.
It is straightforward to have

C_{R}({\cal N})=C_{R}({\cal N}(|i^{*}\rangle\!\langle i^{*}|)).

(25)

If we replace the unitary channel
{\cal U}_{\theta}
into the equation, we can deduce
C_{R}({\cal U}_{\theta})=C_{R}({\cal U}_{\theta}(|i^{*}\rangle\!\langle i^{*}|))
directly. For a single-qubit channel, the robustness of a state is equal to its
l_{1}
norm of coherence
Napoli et al. 2016
, which is the summation of the absolute value of all non-diagonal elements
Baumgratz et al. 2014
. Then, we have

C_{R}({\cal U}_{\theta})=C_{R}({\cal U}_{\theta}(|i^{*}\rangle\!\langle i^{*}|))=C_{l_{1}}({\cal U}_{\theta}(|i^{*}\rangle\!\langle i^{*}|))=\sin 2\theta.

(26)

Note that the robustness is multiplicative under the tensor product of states
Zhu et al. 2017
, specifically

1+C_{R}(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})=(1+C_{R}(\rho_{1}))(1+C_{R}(\rho_{2})).

(27)

We correspondingly have

C_{R}({\cal U}_{\theta}^{(l)})=(1+\sin 2\theta)^{l}-1.

(28)

Recalling Theorem
1
, the maximal success probability is the ratio between the robustness of the resource state and the robustness of the target coherent channel, and the success probability
p
cannot exceed 1. Then we have

P_{\rm MIO}({\cal U}_{\theta}^{(l)},\Psi_{m},\varepsilon=0)=\min\Big\{1,\frac{m-1}{(1+\sin 2\theta)^{(l)}-1}\Big\},

(29)

which completes the proof.

IV
Probabilistic channel simulation with DIO

It was proved in Ref.
Theurer et al. 2019
that if the target channel
{\cal N}
is not a resource nonactivating channel, then it cannot be simulated exactly by DIO with any resource state deterministically. We further show that such an exact simulation is not possible even in probabilistic scenarios. Also, SDP for calculating the success probability of the channel simulation is provided.

In the previous part, we have shown that arbitrary channels can be simulated by using an appropriate resource state and MIO. But for DIO, it is not the same story. Explicitly, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 7

Theurer et al. 2019
(Appendix Eq.(109))

If a quantum channel
{\cal N}
can be implemented by a DIO channel
{\cal M}
using resource state
\omega
, i.e.
{\cal M}(\omega\otimes\cdot)={\cal N}(\cdot)
, then
{\cal N}
satisfies
\Delta\circ{\cal N}=\Delta\circ{\cal N}\circ\Delta
.

Proof.

Since
{\cal N}(\rho)={\cal M}(\omega\otimes\cdot)
holds for any quantum state, we directly have
\Delta\circ{\cal N}(\cdot)=\Delta\circ{\cal M}(\omega\otimes\cdot)
. Considering
{\cal M}
is a DIO channel, which means
\Delta\circ{\cal M}(\omega\otimes\cdot)={\cal M}\circ\Delta(\omega\otimes\cdot)
. we arrive at

\Delta\circ{\cal N}(\cdot)={\cal M}\circ\Delta(\omega\otimes\cdot).

(30)

If the input state is applied to the completely dephasing map, we directly have

\displaystyle\Delta\circ{\cal N}(\Delta(\cdot))

\displaystyle={\cal M}\circ\Delta(\omega\otimes\Delta(\cdot))

(31)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\Delta\circ{\cal N}\circ\Delta(\cdot)

\displaystyle={\cal M}\circ\Delta(\omega\otimes\cdot).

(32)

Combine Eq. (
30
) and Eq. (
32
), we have

\Delta\circ{\cal N}(\cdot)=\Delta\circ{\cal N}\circ\Delta(\cdot)

(33)

Eq. (
33
) holds for any quantum state, which implies
\Delta\circ{\cal N}=\Delta\circ{\cal N}\circ\Delta
, i.e.,
{\cal N}
is a resource nonactivating channel. The proof is complete.

\sqcap
\sqcup

In other words, this theorem implies that if a quantum channel
{\cal N}
does not satisfy the condition
\Delta\circ{\cal N}=\Delta\circ{\cal N}\circ\Delta
(also known as a resource nonactivating channel
Liu et al. 2017
), then it cannot be simulated by DIO.
Also, it is straightforward to extend this theorem to the probabilistic scenario, which is shown in the following corollary
8
.

Corollary 8

If a quantum channel
{\cal N}
can be simulated exactly by a DIO channel
{\cal M}
using resource state
\omega
with non-zero probability
p
, i.e.,
{\cal M}(\omega\otimes\cdot)=p|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes{\cal N}(\cdot)+(1-p)|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes\operatorname{Tr}[\cdot]\frac{\mathbb{I}}{d}
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity and
d
is the dimension, then
{\cal N}
satisfies
\Delta\circ{\cal N}=\Delta\circ{\cal N}\circ\Delta
.

Proof.

Denote
{\cal N}^{\prime}(\cdot)=p|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes{\cal N}(\cdot)+(1-p)|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes\operatorname{Tr}[\cdot]\frac{\mathbb{I}}{d}
. From Theorem
7
,
{\cal N}^{\prime}
is a nonactivating channel, then we directly have
\Delta\circ{\cal N}=\Delta\circ{\cal N}\circ\Delta
, which completes the proof.

\sqcap
\sqcup

Theorem
7
and
Corollary

8
tell us that not all quantum channels can be simulated exactly by DIO, even probabilistically. Take single-qubit unitary channel
{\cal U}_{\theta}
and quantum state
|+\rangle\!\langle+|
as an example. One can easily obtain

\displaystyle\Delta\circ{\cal U}_{\theta}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|+\rangle\!\langle+|})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}-\cos\theta\sin\theta&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}+\cos\theta\sin\theta\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\Delta\circ{\cal U}_{\theta}\circ\Delta({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|+\rangle\!\langle+|})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

\Delta\circ{\cal U}_{\theta}\neq\Delta\circ{\cal U}_{\theta}\circ\Delta
represents that a single qubit unitary channel
{\cal U}_{\theta}
is not a resource nonactivating channel. Thus a qubit unitary channel cannot be exactly simulated by DIO even probabilistically.

If a quantum channel
{\cal N}
can be simulated by DIO, then the success probability can be efficiently computed by the SDP as shown in Proposition

.

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{R}[J_{\tilde{{\cal E}}}(\omega^{T}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})]=J_{{\cal L}_{AB}};

\displaystyle J_{\tilde{{\cal E}}}\geq 0,\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[J_{\tilde{{\cal E}}}]\leq t\mathbb{I}_{RA};

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\operatorname{Tr}[J_{\tilde{{\cal E}}}(|i,j,k\rangle\!\langle m,n,k^{\prime}|_{RAB})]=0,~\forall i,j,m,n,k\neq k^{\prime}};

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[J_{\cal L}]=\mathbb{I}_{A};

V
Conclusion

We have focused on the probabilistic channel simulation with MIO and DIO, respectively. For the MIO part, we talked about three cases: If the resource state is the maximally coherent state, we provide an analytical expression for the maximal success probability. If we select any pure coherent state as the resource state, the maximal success probability is guaranteed to be greater than zero. If the resource state
\omega
is a general coherent state, we offer an efficiently computable SDP for achieving the maximal success probability. For the DIO part, we show that not all quantum channels can be exactly simulated by DIO, even probabilistically. Furthermore, we present an efficiently computable SDP for attaining the maximal success probability of channel simulation with DIO.

For further research, it would be interesting to apply the framework of probabilistic channel simulation to other important quantum resources such as entanglement and magic.

Appendix A
Robustness of a state

Definition 4

Robustness of state

Given an arbitrary quantum state
\rho
, the robustness of a state is defined as
Napoli et al. 2016
;
Piani et al. 2016

C_{R}(\rho)=\min_{\tau}\Big\{s\geq 0\Big|\frac{\rho+s\tau}{1+s}=\sigma\in{\cal I}\Big\},

(35)

where
{\cal I}
refers to the set of free states.

The robustness of the state quantifies the minimum amount of another state
\tau
required such that its mixed state
(\rho+s\tau)/(1+s)
is the incoherent state. We need to note that the added state
\tau
is not necessarily a free state.
Let us denote the
s^{*}
to be the optimal value of
s
, and the corresponding states are denoted as
\tau^{*}
and
\sigma^{*}
.
Then

C_{R}(\rho)=s^{*}
, and

\rho=(1+C_{R}(\rho))\sigma^{*}-C_{R}(\rho)\tau^{*},

(36)

is said to realize an optimal pseudo mixture for
\rho
. It can be characterized by
Piani et al. 2016

1+C_{R}(\rho)=\min\{\lambda|\rho\leq\lambda\sigma,~\sigma\in{\cal I}\},

(37)

where
{\cal I}
denotes the set of incoherent states and
\lambda
is real-valued.

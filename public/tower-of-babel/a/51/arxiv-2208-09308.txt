Global Rigidity of Line Constrained Frameworks
James Cruickshank, Fatemeh Mohammadi, Harshit J. Motwani, Anthony Nixon, Shin-ichi Tanigawa

Abstract




Abstract.
We consider the global rigidity problem for bar-joint frameworks where each vertex is constrained to lie on a particular line in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In our setting we allow multiple vertices to be constrained to the same line.
We give a combinatorial characterisation of generic rigidity in this setting for arbitrary line sets. Further,
under a mild assumption on the given set of lines, we give a complete combinatorial characterisation of graphs that are generically globally rigid.
This gives a
d
-dimensional extension of the well-known combinatorial characterisation of 2-dimensional global rigidity. In particular, our results imply that global rigidity is a generic property in this setting.

2020
Mathematics Subject Classification:

52C25, 05C10

Key words and phrases: bar-joint framework, global rigidity, line constrained framework

1
Introduction

Consider a discrete geometric structure consisting of a collection of points subject to a system of constraints specifying the distances between points. Such structures are often modelled by graphs with vertices corresponding to points and edges to fixed length line segments. A natural question is to what extent the given structure is unique given the topology of the underlying graph and the specified lengths. The structures are usually referred to as bar-joint frameworks and the question is then the well studied global rigidity problem (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
).

In particular, while determining global rigidity of a given framework even on the line is NP-hard
[
20
]
, a folklore result says that, generically, global rigidity on the line is equivalent to the graph being 2-connected. We will extend this result to higher dimensions giving one of a very small number of combinatorial results for generic global rigidity in arbitrary dimension (see
[
4
,
6
,
15
]
for the others). The general
d
-dimensional case is known to depend only on the underlying graph
[
7
]
and the case when
d=2
was resolved in
[
11
]
but, in general, giving a graph theoretic characterisation is a central challenge in rigidity theory.

Initially, the global rigidity problem arose from attempts to understand configurations of molecules
[
10
]
. More recently global rigidity has found a broader range of applications, for example in localizing networks from partial observations of inter-point distances, see, e.g.
[
13
]
. In such applications, it is natural to assume that some points have a fixed location, or perhaps are fixed to move on the ground, or a given wall. Such external boundary conditions can be incorporated into the constraints for a bar-joint framework as additional linear constraints (restriction to move on a fixed hyperplane) on the points
[
5
,
9
,
14
,
17
,
23
]
.
In this linearly constrained context, global rigidity has been characterised generically in the plane
[
9
]
in purely combinatorial terms. However, little is known combinatorially in dimension greater than 2 with, or without, linear constraints.

In this paper we address the global rigidity problem of bar-joint frameworks whose points are constrained to move on lines. Our research generalises that of
[
9
]
in two directions. Firstly we will work in arbitrary dimensions and consider bar-joint frameworks constrained to a fixed system of lines. Secondly we will substantially weaken the genericity hypothesis by imposing no restriction on how many points are constrained to any given line.
This generalization gives us a
d
-dimensional extension of the aforementioned combinatorial characterisation of
1
-dimensional global rigidity.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we will formally introduce our line constrained rigidity setting and give a characterisation of generic rigidity in this context in Section
3
. (Rigidity, while fundamental, is a weaker property than global rigidity where uniqueness is only required in a neighbourhood of the given framework.) This characterisation extends, to the general case, a result obtained for line constrained frameworks in
[
5
]
since there only 2 points were allowed to lie on each line. We then prove exact necessary conditions for a generic line constrained framework to be globally rigid in Section
4
; these results are analogous to the well known Hendrickson conditions for global rigidity of a bar-joint framework
[
10
]
. The remainder of the paper is devoted to showing these necessary conditions are also sufficient, and thereby giving a full combinatorial description of global rigidity. We achieve this by an inductive proof strategy, the geometric aspect of which is to prove that particular graph operations preserve generic global rigidity; this is the content of Section
5
. In Section
6
, we then establish global rigidity for small graphs of three fundamental topological types. These results can then be combined with a combinatorial reduction step, in Section
7
, to complete the proof of our characterisation.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Line constrained frameworks

Any line in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has a unique standard equation of the form
Ax=b
where
(A,b)
is a

(d-1)\times(d+1)
matrix in reduced row echelon form.
Throughout the rest of the paper,
k
is some positive integer and
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\}
will be a set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose that
A_{i}x=b_{i}
is the standard representation of the line
L_{i}
as above.
We say that
\mathcal{L}
is
parallel
if
A_{i}=A_{j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq k
and
non-parallel
otherwise.
Let
X
be the union of the sets of entries of
(A_{i},b_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
define
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L})
to be the smallest subfield of
\mathbb{R}
containing
X
.

A
partitioned graph
is a finite graph
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
together
with a partition of the vertex set
\mathcal{V}=\cup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
. Note that we allow
V_{i}
to be empty here.
Also in contrast to a multipartite graph, the induced edge set on
V_{i}
can
be non-empty.
We say that a subset
W\subset\mathcal{V}
is
crossing
if
W\cap V_{i}
is non-empty for at least two distinct values of
i
. We say that a graph or an edge
is
crossing
if its set of vertices is crossing.

Given a line set
\mathcal{L}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, an
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework
is a pair
(G,p)
where

G
is a partitioned graph and
p:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies
p(V_{i})\subset L_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
In this situation, if
u\in V_{i}
then define
L_{u}
to be
L_{i}
.
Let
Y
be the set of real numbers that appear as a coordinate in some
p(v)
and define
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p)
to be the smallest subfield of
\mathbb{R}
that contains both
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L})
and
Y
. We say that
(G,p)
(or
p
) is
\mathcal{L}
-generic
if

\mathrm{td}(\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p):\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L}))=|\mathcal{V}|
.

2.2
Isometries on lines

In the context of framework rigidity, the group of isometries of the ambient space is important since such isometries induce trivial
flexes of a framework.
In this subsection we show basic properties of the isometry group on lines.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
An
isometry of
\mathcal{L}
is a map
\theta:\cup_{L\in\mathcal{L}}L\rightarrow\cup_{L\in\mathcal{L}}L
,
such that
|\theta(x)-\theta(y)|=|x-y|
for
all
x,y\in\cup_{L\in\mathcal{L}}L
and such that

\theta(L)=L
for all
L\in\mathcal{L}
. In other
words it is an isometry of the underlying induced metric on
\cup_{L\in\mathcal{L}}L
that induces the identity permutation on the set of lines in
\mathcal{L}
.
Let
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
be the group of isometries of
\mathcal{L}
.
The following elementary observations will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{1},L_{2}\}
is a set of two lines in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
L_{1},L_{2}
are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

(1)

There is a unique pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
of points
x_{i}\in L_{i}\ (i=1,2)
such that
x_{i}
is the closest point in
L_{i}
to
L_{3-i}
.
Moreover, the coordinates of the point
x_{i}
lie in
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L})
.

(2)

If
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is a non-identity element then for
i=1,2
,
\theta|_{L_{i}}
is a reflection in
x_{i}
.

(3)

\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is cyclic of order
2
.

Proof.

Consider the quadratic polynomial
f:L_{1}\times L_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},f(y_{1},y_{2})=\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}
.
Since
L_{1},L_{2}
are not parallel,
f
is a strictly convex quadratic function and hence has a unique minimiser
(y_{1},y_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})
.
Therefore
(x_{1},x_{2})
is the unique solution to a linear system of equations with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L})
and statement
{\rm(1)}
follows from Cramer’s rule.

The strict minimality of
f
implies that any element
\gamma
of
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
must fix
x_{1},x_{2}
. Hence,
\gamma
restricted to each
L_{i}
must be either the identity or the reflection about
x_{i}
.
The half-turn rotation of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
about an axis through
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
gives a non-identity element
\gamma
of
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
by restriction.
Also if any element
\gamma
of
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
restricted to
L_{i}
is the identity, it must also be the identity on
L_{3-i}
since
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are not perpendicular. These imply (2) and (3).
∎

We note also that if
\mathcal{L}
is parallel then

\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is isomorphic to the Euclidean group of isometries of
\mathbb{R}
. If
L_{1},L_{2}
are perpendicular then
\mathrm{Isom}(\{L_{1},L_{2}\})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{L}
is not parallel then
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is
finite.

The situation is more straightforward if the set of lines are non-degenerate in a sense that we now define.
Three (non-parallel) lines
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
weakly concurrent
if
the closest point in
L_{1}
to
L_{2}
coincides with
the closest point in
L_{1}
to
L_{3}
.
Note that when
d=2
,
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
are weakly concurrent if and only if they are concurrent.
We say that a set
\mathcal{L}
of lines is in
general position
if

•

no two lines in
\mathcal{L}
are parallel,

•

no two lines in
\mathcal{L}
are perpendicular, and

•

no three lines in
\mathcal{L}
are weakly concurrent.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is in general position.
If
|\mathcal{L}|=1
, then
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is isomorphic to the
1
-dimensional Euclidean group. If
|\mathcal{L}|=2
then
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is cyclic of order two and the non-trivial element is a rotation about a common perpendicular of the two lines. If
|\mathcal{L}|\geq 3
, then
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is trivial.

Proof.

The cases
|\mathcal{L}|=1,2
follow immediately from Lemma
2.1
. Suppose that
|\mathcal{L}|\geq 3
and, for a contradiction, suppose that

\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L})
is non-trivial. So there is some

L_{1}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\theta|_{L_{1}}
is not the identity.
Choose
L_{2},L_{3}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
are pairwise distinct. Let
x\in L_{1}
be the closest point to
L_{2}
. By Lemma
2.1
,
x
is also the closest point to
L_{3}
. However this contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{L}
is in general position.
∎

We next state, in a form suitable for our purposes, one of the fundamental theorems of real algebraic geometry.
Suppose that
\mathbb{F}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{R}
. Let
X
be a semi-algebraic subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
X
is
defined over
\mathbb{F}
if there is a set of defining polynomial equations and inequalities for
X
that have coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
.

Theorem 2.3

(Tarski
[
24
]
, Seidenberg
[
21
]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{F}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{R}
and that

X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a semi-algebraic set defined over
\mathbb{F}
.
Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{c}
is a polynomial map with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
. Then

f(X)
is a semi-algebraic set defined over
\mathbb{F}
.

See
[
2
, Theorem 2.76]
for a proof of this result.

2.3
Rigidity of line constrained frameworks

Given a partitioned graph
G
and a line set
\mathcal{L}
indexed by
J
as above, let

\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}=\{p:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ such that }p(V_{i})\subset L_{i}\text{ for }i\in J\}.

Observe that
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
is a
|\mathcal{V}|
-dimensional linear subspace
of
\mathbb{R}^{d|\mathcal{V}|}
that is defined by
equations with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L})
.
Define the

measurement map

M_{G}:\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{E}}
by
M_{G}(p)=(\|p(u)-p(v)\|^{2})_{uv\in\mathcal{E}}
.
For any subgraph
H
of
G
, denote
\mathcal{L}[H]=\{L_{i}\in\mathcal{L}:V_{i}\cap V(H)\neq\emptyset\}
.

We say that
(G,p)
is
globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid
if
M_{G}(q)=M_{G}(p)
implies that
q=\theta\circ p
for some
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
,
and it is
\mathcal{L}
-rigid
if, for any
q
in a neighbourhood of
p
in
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
,

M_{G}(q)=M_{G}(p)
implies that
q=\theta\circ p
for some
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
. See Figures
1
and
2
for examples.

Figure 1:
On the left a non-rigid
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework and on the right an
\mathcal{L}
-rigid framework which is not globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

We say that a graph
G
is
generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid
if for any
\mathcal{L}
-generic
p\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
, the framework

(G,p)
is globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.
Our main goal is to give a combinatorial characterisation of partitioned graphs
G
that are globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid in the case when
\mathcal{L}
is in general position. In particular we shall see that this characterisation is independent of
\mathcal{L}
, as long as
\mathcal{L}
is in general position. Moreover, as a consequence of our characterisation, we will also be able to deduce that the property of global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity is a generic property of partitioned graphs.

For rigidity analysis, it is a common strategy to consider a linearised version known as infinitesimal rigidity.
Suppose that
(G,p)
is an
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework. An
infinitesimal
flex
of
(G,p)
is a function
f:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

(p(u)-p(v))\cdot(f(u)-f(v))=0\text{ for all $uv\in\mathcal{E}$ and}

(1)

A_{i}f(v)=0\text{ for $1\leq i\leq k,v\in V_{i}$\ ,}

(2)

where
A_{i}x=b_{i}
is the standard equation of the line
L_{i}
.
The coefficient matrix of the linear system defined by (
1
) and (
2
), denoted
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
, is called the
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity matrix
of
(G,p)
. Thus an
infinitesimal flex is an element of
\ker(R(G,p,\mathcal{L}))
.

We say that
(G,p)
is
infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid
if

\dim(\ker(R(G,p,\mathcal{L})))=\begin{cases}1&(\text{if all lines in $\mathcal{L}[G]$ are parallel})\\
0&(\text{otherwise}).\end{cases}

(3)

As observed earlier, the right hand side of (
3
) is equal to the dimension of
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
, validating our definition of infinitesimal rigidity.

In general an
\mathcal{L}
-rigid framework need not be infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. However
we will show that for
\mathcal{L}
-generic frameworks rigidity and infinitesimal rigidity are equivalent. The arguments use a well known technique due to Asimow and Roth in the case of bar-joint frameworks
[
1
]
and so we only sketch the proofs.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
(G,p)
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic and let
k=\dim\ker R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
.
Then
M_{G}^{-1}(M_{G}(p))
is an algebraic set of dimension
k
. Moreover
p
has a open neighbourhood
U
in
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
such that
M_{G}^{-1}(M_{G}(p))\cap U
is a smoothly embedded submanifold of
U
of dimension
k
.

Sketch of proof.

First observe that we can view the submatrix of
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
induced by the rows corresponding to (
1
) as the Jacobian matrix at
p
of the polynomial function
M_{G}
. The fact that
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic ensures that the rank of
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is maximal
over all points in
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
which implies the first conclusion. Moreover this rank is constant in an open neighbourhood of
p
. Now the constant rank theorem
[
22
, Theorem 9]

implies the second conclusion.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(G,p)
be a generic
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework.
Then,
(G,p)
is rigid if and only if it is infinitesimally rigid. ∎

Sketch of proof.

The case when
G[\mathcal{L}]
is parallel can be deduced from the standard Asimow-Roth theorem for bar-joint frameworks in one dimension
[
1
]
.
If
G[\mathcal{L}]
is not parallel then
(G,p)
is
\mathcal{L}
-rigid if and only if there is some neighbourhood
U
of
p
in
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
such that
U\cap M_{G}^{-1}(M_{G}(p))=\{p\}
. Now the required conclusion follows from Lemma
2.4
.
∎

In view of this, we say that
G
is
\mathcal{L}
-rigid
if
(G,p)

is infinitesimally

\mathcal{L}
-rigid for all
\mathcal{L}
-generic
p\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
. Note that the rank of
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is determined by the set of vanishing minors of
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
and it follows that the maximum rank of
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is attained at all
\mathcal{L}
-generic
p\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
.
It follows that
(G,p)
is infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid for some
p
if and only if

G
is
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

We conclude this section by deriving another useful property of generic infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid frameworks based on Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
G[\mathcal{L}]
is not parallel and that

(G,p)
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic and infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. Then

(1)

M_{G}^{-1}(M_{G}(p))
is finite, and

(2)

if
q\in M_{G}^{-1}(M_{G}(p))
and
x
is a coordinate of
q(v)
for some

v\in\mathcal{V}
, then
x
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p)
.

Proof.

Since
G[\mathcal{L}]
is not parallel and
(G,p)
is infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid, it follows that
\dim(\ker(R(G,p,\mathcal{L})))=0
.
The first conclusion now follows from Lemma
2.4
.

To prove the second conclusion, observe that since
M_{G}^{-1}(M_{G}(p))
is a finite semi-algebraic set
defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p)
, Theorem
2.3
implies that, for any
1\leq i\leq d
,
the set of
i
-th coordinates of points in
M_{G}^{-1}(M_{G}(p))
is
again a semi-algebraic
subset of
\mathbb{R}
defined over

\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p)
.
Moreover, since this set is finite, it is
a subset of the set of solutions of a nontrivial polynomial equation with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p)
.
∎

3
Characterising Generic
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity

In
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
a combinatorial characterisation of infinitesimal rigidity for line constrained frameworks was given in the case of arbitrary line constraints, but for generic point configurations.
In
[
5
]
, the genericity assumption for point configurations is defined with respect to the ambient Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and it is not difficult to see that the result in
[
5
]
leads to a characterisation for line constrained frameworks in our sense,
but only in the case that at most two vertices lie on one line as a consequence of their genericity assumption.

Our goal in this section is to extend this to a characterisation of
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity for arbitrary line sets
\mathcal{L}
with no restrictions on the vertex partition.
This can be accomplished by directly analyzing the rank of the rigidity matrix
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})

of an
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework
(G,p)
of a given graph
G
, unlike the proof in
[
5
]
, which is based on an inductive construction.

Let
v_{i}
be an unit direction vector of the line
L_{i}
.
Then any infinitesimal flex
f
of
(G,p)
is written as

f(u)=t_{u}v_{i}
for some scalar
t_{u}
for each
u\in V_{i}
.
Let
\theta_{u,v}
be the angle between the vector
v_{i}
and
p(v)-p(u)
for
each edge
uv\in\mathcal{E}
with
u\in V_{i}
.
Then the linear system (
1
) and (
2
) may be rewritten as

t_{u}\cos\theta_{u,v}+t_{v}\cos\theta_{v,u}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{(for $uv\in\mathcal{E}$)}.

(4)

The matrix
R^{\prime}(G,p,L)
representing (
4
) with variables
t_{u}\ (u\in\mathcal{V})
has size
|\mathcal{E}|\times|\mathcal{V}|
, and each column is associated with a vertex and each row is associated with an edge.
Its zero-nonzero pattern is the same as that of the incidence matrix of
G
, and each nonzero entry has the form
\cos\theta_{u,v}
.
Since
\ker R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})=\ker R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
, it suffices to analyze the rank of
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a partitioned graph.

(1)

If
\mathcal{L}[G]
is parallel then
G
is
\mathcal{L}
-rigid if and only if it
is connected.

(2)

If
\mathcal{L}[G]
is not parallel then
G
is
\mathcal{L}
-rigid if and
only if every component of
G
contains a cycle that contains an edge
uv
associated with non-parallel lines
L_{u}
and
L_{v}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}[G]
.
Let
(G,p,\mathcal{L})
be an
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework.
We analyse the rank of
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
.

Suppose first that
\mathcal{L}
is parallel,
i.e.,
v_{i}=v_{j}
for every
L_{i}
and
L_{j}
in
\mathcal{L}
.
Then, for each
uv\in\mathcal{E}
,

\cos\theta_{u,v}=-\cos\theta_{u,v}
.
Also, if
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic,
\cos\theta_{u,v}\neq 0
.
Hence, by dividing each row of
R(G,p,\mathcal{L})
associated with
uv
by
\cos\theta_{u,v}
,
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is converted to the incidence matrix of an edge-oriented digraph of
G
.
So the row matroid of
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is the graphic matroid of
G
, and hence the statement follows in the case when
\mathcal{L}[G]
is parallel.

Suppose next that
\mathcal{L}
is not parallel.
Our goal is to show that

R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is non-singular if and only if every component of
G
contains a cycle that contains an edge
uv
such that
L_{u}
and
L_{v}
are not parallel.
If
G
is not connected, then
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is the direct product of the corresponding matrices of the connected components of
G
.
Hence, it suffices to consider the case when
G
is connected.

For
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
to be non-singular,
it is necessary that
|\mathcal{E}|=|\mathcal{V}|
.
Since
G
is connected,
|\mathcal{E}|=|\mathcal{V}|
implies that

G
contains exactly one cycle
C
.
Then, we can orient each edge
e
such that
each vertex has in-degree one in the resulting directed graph.
Let
h(e)
and
t(e)
be the head and the tail of an edge
e
in this orientation.
Then, from the fact that the zero-nonzero pattern of
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is the same as that of the incidence matrix of
G
, the determinant of
R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
can be expanded as follows:

\det R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})=\prod_{e\in\mathcal{E}\setminus E(C)}\cos\theta_{h(e),t(e)}\left(\prod_{e\in E(C)}\cos\theta_{h(e),t(e)}+(-1)^{|E(C)|-1}\prod_{e\in E(C)}\cos\theta_{t(e),h(e)}\right).

If
\mathcal{L}[C]
is parallel, then
\cos\theta_{h(e),t(e)}=-\cos\theta_{t(e),h(e)}
for any edge
e
in
C
, and hence the two terms in the parentheses cancel, i.e.,

\det R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})=0
.

If
C
contains an edge
e=uv
such that
L_{u}
and
L_{v}
are not parallel, then we consider a point configuration
p
as follows.
We first put
p(w)
for all
w\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{u\}
generically on each associated line, and put
p(u)
such that
p(v)-p(u)
is orthogonal to
L_{u}
.
Then
\cos\theta_{u,v}=0
.
Moreover, since
L_{u}
is not parallel to
L_{v}
and the points are located generically except for
p(u)
,

\cos\theta_{v,u}\neq 0
and

\cos\theta_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}\neq 0
for any other edge
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
.
Thus, among the two terms in the expansion of
\det R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
, exactly one term is nonzero.
Hence
\det R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})
is non-singular.

Therefore, if
G
is connected, then
\dim\ker R^{\prime}(G,p,\mathcal{L})=0
if and only if
G
contains a cycle that has an edge
uv
associated with non-parallel lines
L_{u}
and
L_{v}
.
∎

4
Necessary Conditions for Global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity

The rest of the paper is devoted to the line constrained global rigidity
problem. In that setting the full isometry group of any finite subset of
\mathcal{L}
can play a role, so from now on we assume that

\mathcal{L}
is in general position.

When
d=2
, this is equivalent to the assumption that
no two lines are parallel and perpendicular and
no three lines are concurrent.

The case in which
G
is not crossing corresponds to the classical and well understood generic global rigidity problem for frameworks in
\mathbb{R}^{1}
. Thus we shall also assume from now on that

G
is a
crossing graph
,

i.e. at least two
V_{i}
are non-empty. These assumptions will always be in force. However, we will restate them explicitly in the statements of the main results.

We now collect necessary conditions for global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity.
We begin with two necessary connectivity conditions.
We say that a connected component of a graph is
proper
if it is a proper subgraph.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(G,p)
be a
\mathcal{L}
-generic
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose that
(G,p)
is globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. Then,
every proper connected component
H
of
G
satisfies
|\mathcal{L}[H]|\geq 3
.

Proof.

Suppose there is a proper connected component
H
of
G
with
|\mathcal{L}[H]|\leq 2
.
By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a non-identity element
\gamma\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[H])
.
Define
q
such that
q(v)=\gamma p(v)
for each vertex
v
in
H
and
q(v)=p(v)
for the remaining vertices
v
of
G
.
Then
M_{G}(p)=M_{G}(q)
holds.
However, since
H
is a proper subgraph of
G
and
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic,
q\neq\theta\circ p
for any
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
.
This contradicts the global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity of
(G,p)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(G,p)
be a
\mathcal{L}
-generic
\mathcal{L}
-constrained framework in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose that
(G,p)
is globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. Then, for every
v\in\mathcal{V}
, every connected component
H
of
G-v
satisfies
|\mathcal{L}[H]|\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a component of
G-v
.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
V(H)\subseteq V_{i}
for some
V_{i}
in the vertex partition of
G
.
Let
\gamma
be the reflection in the hyperplane containing
p(v)
that is perpendicular to
L_{i}
. Since
\mathcal{L}
is in general position and
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic,
the restriction of
\gamma
to
\bigcup_{i}L_{i}
is not in
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
.

Let
q(y)=p(y)
for all
y\in V(G)\setminus V(H)
and
q(y)=\gamma p(y)
for
y\in V(H)
.
Then
M_{G}(q)=M_{G}(p)
holds.
Hence, by the global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity of
(G,p)
,
q=\theta p
for some
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
.
Now
p(v)=q(v)
and since
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\theta=\mathrm{Id}
. Therefore

q=p
and it follows that
\gamma p(y)=p(y)
for all
y\in V(H)
contradicting the
fact that
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic since
\gamma
is a reflection on
L_{i}
.
∎

Next we prove a line constrained version of a well known
result of Hendrickson
[
10
]
.
The standard, and essentially only, known proof of Hendrickson’s result for bar-joint frameworks uses Sard’s theorem. Our approach is similar but avoids applying Sard’s theorem.
Our new technique also works for bar-joint frameworks and we believe it will be useful for rigidity problems in other settings. The following lemma is a key ingredient in our proof.
Recall that a graph is said to be
even
if every vertex has even degree.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
X
is a bounded real algebraic curve then it is homeomorphic to the geometric realisation of an even graph whose vertices are the singular points of
X
.

Proof.

Note that
X
is compact in the usual Euclidean topology, as it is both bounded and closed. Therefore, we have a finite covering
\{N_{\epsilon}(p_{i})\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
of
X
where
p_{i}\in X
and
N_{\epsilon}(p_{i})
is the
\epsilon
-neighbourhood of
p_{i}
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. As real algebraic curves have finitely many singular points
[
3
, Proposition 3.3.14]
, we can chose a finite covering such that it contains all
\epsilon
-neighbourhoods of singular points. Now using
[
3
, Theorem 9.5.7]
, for every
N_{\epsilon}(p_{i})
we have an even number of half-branches centered at
p_{i}
. From this we can construct a graph
G
with vertices corresponding to
p_{i}
and edges corresponding to the half-branches. Without loss of generality we can assume that we are in a connected component of
X
, which implies that
G
is connected. Now let
H
be the graph obtained from
G
by smoothing out all the vertices of
G
which are regular points of
X
so that the vertices of
H
precisely correspond to the singular points of
X
. In other words,
H
is a geometric realisation of an even graph with vertices corresponding to the singular points of
X
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
p\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic and that
(G,p)
is globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. Then
(G-uv,p)
is infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid for any edge
uv\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

First observe that
(G,p)
is infinitesimally rigid by Proposition
2.5
.
Hence, Lemma
2.6
implies that

\dim(\ker(R(G,p,\mathcal{L})))=0
.
Similarly, by Lemma
2.4
,
X=M_{G-uv}^{-1}(M_{G-uv}(p))
is an algebraic subset of
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
and

\dim(X)=\dim(\ker(R(G-uv,p,\mathcal{L})))\leq\dim(\ker(R(G,p,\mathcal{L})))+1=1
.
If
\dim(X)=0
then
(G-uv,p)
is infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.
So we may assume that
\dim(X)=1
and so
X
is an algebraic curve
defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p)
.

Claim 4.5
.

X
is a bounded subset of
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proof.

First we make a general observation. Suppose that
H
is a connected crossing graph and that
r\in\mathcal{L}^{V(H)}
. For any
w\in V(H)
there is some
z\in V(H)
such that
L_{w}
and
L_{z}
are not parallel. Since there is a path
w=u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}=z
in
H
, we see that for any
q\in M_{H}^{-1}(M_{H}(r))
,
\|q(w)-q(z)\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\|q(u_{i})-q(u_{i+1})\|=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\|r(u_{i})-r(u_{i+1})\|
. Since
L_{w}
and
L_{z}
are not parallel, it follows that
q(w)
lies in bounded subset of
L_{w}
and so
M_{H}^{-1}(M_{H}(r))
is bounded.

Now suppose that
G^{\prime}
is the component of
G
that contains
uv
and
G^{\prime\prime}=G-G^{\prime}
. Then
X=Y\times Z
where

Y=M_{G^{\prime}-uv}^{-1}(M_{G^{\prime}-uv}(p|_{V(G^{\prime})}))
and
Z=M_{G^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}(M_{G^{\prime\prime}}(p|_{V(G^{\prime\prime})}))
. Since
(G,p)
is globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that every component of
G^{\prime\prime}
is crossing and so
Z
is bounded using the general observation above. Thus
X
is bounded if and only if
Y
is bounded and so we may as well assume from
now that
G=G^{\prime}
is a connected graph.
If
G-uv
is a connected graph then since it is also a crossing graph the conclusion follows from the general observation above.

Hence we can assume that
G-uv
is not connected and (since
G
is connected) has two components
H
and
K
. Suppose

H
and
K
contain the vertices
u
and
v
respectively. If
|\mathcal{L}[H]|=1
then, since
p
is generic (in particular
p(u)-p(v)
is not perpendicular
to
L_{u}
), we can slide
(H,p|_{V(H)})
along
\mathcal{L}[H]
to find
(H,p^{\prime})
such that

\|p^{\prime}(u)-p(v)\|=\|p(u)-p(v)\|
and so that
p^{\prime}\neq p|_{V(H)}
, contradicting
the global rigidity of
(G,p)
. Thus
|\mathcal{L}[H]|\geq 2
, and similarly
|\mathcal{L}[K]|\geq 2
.
Let
Y=\{q\in\mathcal{L}^{V(H)}:M_{H}(q)=M_{H}(p|_{V(H)})\}
and
Z=\{q\in\mathcal{L}^{V(K)}:M_{K}(q)=M_{K}(p|_{V(K)})\}
.
Using the general observation we see that both
Y
and
Z
are
bounded. Now,

and the claim follows.
∎

Thus
X
is a bounded real algebraic curve, and so by Lemma
4.3
, it is homeomorphic to the geometric realisation of an even graph whose vertices are singular points of
X
. Also

, being

-generic, is a non-singular point of

(viewed as a graph).

In the case where

is trivial by Lemma
2.2
.
In the case where
|\mathcal{L}[G]|=2
, let
\theta
be the unique non-trivial element of
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
.
We show that
\theta
acts without fixed points on

.
To see this, suppose

fixes

.
Then by Lemma

is the reflection around

for each

is the closest point in

to

.
So

implies that each point of

lies in

or

, and

is equal to either

or

.
However, since

and

are algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L}[G])
,

would be algebraic over

.
This contradicts the
\mathcal{L}
-genericity of

induces
a fixed point free involution of

in the case

.

Thus, in all cases,

is homeomorphic to the geometric realisation of an even graph.
Let
\overline{p}
be the image of

in

.
Since

is
\mathcal{L}
-generic, Lemma

and thus a neighbourhood of

(viewed as a graph).
Let

be the component of

that contains

. Since a connected even graph is

is connected. Now
define

by

. Since

and

is not rigid, Proposition

So there are points

∎

5
Inductive Properties

Our goal is to prove the converse of Theorem
4.6
. The basic strategy will be
induction on the number of vertices of the graph and in
this section we will analyse the geometric properties of the relevant inductive operations.
For a graph
G
, a
subdivision
of an edge
uv
replaces
uv
with new edges
uw
and
wv
by adding a new vertex
w
of degree two.
The inverse operation is called
smoothing
at
w
.
We will show that the subdivision operation preserves generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity. We begin with an elementary geometric lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
L
is a line in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with equation
Ax=b
and that
p_{1},p_{2}
are distinct points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathbb{F}
be the smallest subfield of
\mathbb{R}
that contains all the entries of
A,b,p_{1},p_{2}
. Let
f:L\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be given by
f(x)=(\|x-p_{1}\|^{2},\|x-p_{2}\|^{2})
. Then the following hold:

(1)

If the line segment
[p_{1},p_{2}]
is perpendicular to
L
then
f(L)
is a half-line defined over
\mathbb{F}
.

(2)

If
[p_{1},p_{2}]
is not perpendicular to
L
then
f(L)
is a parabola that is defined over
\mathbb{F}
. In particular
f(L)
is an irreducible algebraic set defined over
\mathbb{F}
in this case.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow L
be the orthogonal projection. Then

f(x)=(\|x-\pi(p_{1})\|^{2},\|x-\pi(p_{2})\|^{2})+(\|p_{1}-\pi(p_{1})\|^{2},\|p_{2}-\pi(p_{2})\|^{2}).

Now if
\pi(p_{1})=\pi(p_{2})
then
\{(\|x-\pi(p_{1})\|^{2},\|x-\pi(p_{2})\|^{2}),x\in L\}=\{(a,a):a\geq 0\}
, whereas it is elementary to check that
if
\pi(p_{1})\neq\pi(p_{2})
then
\{(\|x-\pi(p_{1})\|^{2},\|x-\pi(p_{2})\|^{2}),x\in L\}
is a parameterisation of a parabola.
∎

Example 1
.

5.1
, we can work with

and choose local coordinates on a given line
L
such that it is represented by the

-axis i.e.,

.
The two parts of the lemma are explained as follows:

1.

If the line segment
[p_{1},p_{2}]
is perpendicular to

, then
f(L)
is a half-line. Without loss of generality we can choose
p_{1}=(0,1)
and
p_{2}=(0,-1)
. Then
f(L)
is given by
\{(t^{2}+1,t^{2}+1):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
which describes a half line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
starting at

with a slope of

from

-axis, as depicted in Figure

(left).

2.

If the line segment

is not perpendicular to

, then

is a parabola. Similar to the above case we can choose

and

, where

is given by

We also remark the following elementary fact since it is used frequently in the subsequent discussion.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be lines in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in general position,
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow L_{2}
be the orthogonal projection to

, and let

is a strictly convex function,

and which is symmetric with respect to the closest point.

Lemma

in particular imply that,
if

and

then there is

.

The following technical lemma is a key observation in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(G,p)
be an
\mathcal{L}
-generic

-constrained framework,
and let

be distinct vertices of

such that

and

are edges of

.
Suppose that
(G-w,p|_{\mathcal{V}-w})
is infinitesimally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

,
we have
\|p(u)-p(v)\|=\|q(u)-q(v)\|
.
Moreover, at least one of the following holds:

•

,

•

, or

•

there exists
\gamma\in\mathrm{Isom}(\{L_{u},L_{v},L_{w}\})
such that

for

Proof.

Pick any

,
and define

by

and

by

.
Since

is generic
it follows from Lemma

that

is a non-linear irreducible algebraic curve defined over

.
On the other hand, by Lemma

,

is either an irreducible algebraic curve over
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},q|_{\mathcal{V}-w})
or is contained in a line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},q|_{\mathcal{V}-w})
.
Moreover, by Lemma

,
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},q|_{\mathcal{V}-w})
is contained in the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p|_{\mathcal{V}-w})
.
Hence,

is also defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p|_{\mathcal{V}-w})
.
Now observe that
f(p(w))\in f(L_{w})\cap g(L_{w})
. Since at least one coordinate of
p(w)
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{L},p|_{\mathcal{V}-w})

Since

.

Let

be the orthogonal projection.

Claim 5.4
.

It follows that

(a)

\|p(u)-\pi(p(u))\|=\|q(u)-\pi(q(u))\|
and

\|p(v)-\pi(p(v))\|=\|q(v)-\pi(q(v))\|
, and

(b)

\|\pi(p(u))-\pi(p(v))\|=\|\pi(q(u))-\pi(q(v))\|
.

Proof of claim.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|p(u)-\pi(p(u))\|

\displaystyle=\min\{\sqrt{x}:(x,y)\in f(L_{w})\}

\displaystyle=\min\{\sqrt{x}:(x,y)\in g(L_{w})\}=\|q(u)-\pi(q(u))\|,

where the second equation follows from
f(L_{w})=g(L_{w})
. This proves (a).
For (b), we have

\displaystyle\|\pi(p(u))-\pi(p(v))\|

\displaystyle=\min\{\sqrt{x-\|p(u)-\pi(p(u))\|^{2}}+\sqrt{y-\|p(v)-\pi(p(v))\|^{2}}:(x,y)\in f(L_{w})\},

\displaystyle\|\pi(q(u))-\pi(q(v))\|

\displaystyle=\min\{\sqrt{x-\|q(u)-\pi(q(u))\|^{2}}+\sqrt{y-\|q(v)-\pi(q(v))\|^{2}}:(x,y)\in g(L_{w})\}.

Using (a) and
f(L_{w})=g(L_{w})
, (b) follows.
∎

By Claim

(a) and Lemma

, at least one of the followings hold for vertex

Suppose

.
By Claim

Suppose that
q(u)=p(u)
and

Let

\mathcal{L}
-generic

\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

Proof.

Let

be the neighbors of

,

\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

\mathcal{L}
-rigid

\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

Let

\|p(u)-p(v)\|=\|q(u)-q(v)\|

Since

is globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid

for some
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G-w])

\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G-w])=\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])

Now since

\mathcal{L}

,

.

.

L_{u}\neq L_{w}\neq L_{v}

is the identity,

-rigid.

-rigid.

Suppose

\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}

and
q(w)=\gamma p(w)
for some
\gamma\in\mathrm{Isom}\{L_{u},L_{w}\}
.
If
\theta
is the identity, then
we have

\|p(u)-p(w)\|=\|q(u)-q(w)\|=\|p(u)-\gamma p(w)\|
,
which implies
\gamma=\mathrm{Id}=\theta
due to the
\mathcal{L}
-genericity of
p
.
Hence, since
|\mathrm{Isom}\{L_{u},L_{w}\}|=2
by Lemma
2.2
, we can always deduce
\gamma=\theta
.
Moreover
\{L_{u},L_{w}\}=\mathcal{L}[G]
, so
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{L}[G])
. Now, replacing
q
by
\theta^{-1}q
, we see that
q(u)=p(u)
and
q(w)=p(w)
. Since
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic, we know that
L_{v}
is not perpendicular to
[p(u),p(w)]
and so
q(v)=p(v)
. Similarly
q(x)=p(x)
and so
q=p
.

Case 3:
uw
is crossing and
|\mathcal{L}[G]|\geq 3
.

Relabelling if necessary we can assume that
uv,vw,uw
are all crossing edges. By Lemma
5.3
there is some
\theta\in\mathrm{Isom}(\{L_{u},L_{v},L_{w}\})
such that

q(z)=\theta p(z)
for
z\in\{u,v,w\}
. But
L_{u},L_{v},L_{w}
are pairwise distinct and so by Lemma
2.2
,
\theta=\mathrm{Id}
and so
q(z)=p(z)
for
z\in\{u,v,w\}
. Now since
p
is
\mathcal{L}
-generic,
[p(u),p(w)]
is not perpendicular to
L_{x}
and it follows that
q(x)=p(x)
.
This completes the proof for the case when
\|\mathcal{V}\|=4
.

Now suppose that
|\mathcal{V}|\geq 5
.
Let
u,w
be the vertices of degree
3
and let
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
be the three internally vertex disjoint paths joining
u,v
.
Since
G
is type 3,
we may assume that

P_{1}
contains a crossing edge
e_{1}
not incident to
w

and
P_{2}
contains a crossing edge
e_{2}
not incident to
u
.
If
P_{3}
contains an internal vertex
x
,
then smoothing at
x
results in a graph of type 3.
Hence, by applying the induction hypothesis and then Theorem
5.5
, the generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity of
G
follows.

Therefore, we may assume
|V(P_{3})|=2
. Since
|\mathcal{V}|\geq 5
, we may further suppose
|V(P_{1})|\geq 3
.
Let
y
be a vertex of
P_{1}
, which is closest from
u
among those which are not belong to the same vertex component
V_{i}
as that of
u
in the vertex partition.
Since
|V(P_{1})|\geq 3
,
y
has a neighbor
z\notin\{u,w\}
.
Observe that smoothing at
z
results in a graph of type 3.
Hence, by applying the induction hypothesis and then Theorem
5.5
, the generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity of
G
follows.
∎

Remark 1
.

of lines in general position.
We note that type 1, 2 or 3 graphs are all circuits in the generic line-constrained matroid. However, there are circuits that are not type 1, 2, or 3.

Characterising Global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity

In this section we give the main theorem of this paper, a combinatorial characterisation of generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity.
We begin with the case when
G
is 2-connected.

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose that
G
is P-connected,
2
-connected and generically redundantly
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. Then
G
contains a subgraph of type
3
.

Proof.

Suppose, seeking a contradiction, that
G
satisfies the hypotheses of the lemma but
does not contain a type
3
subgraph.

Claim 7.2
.

There are crossing edges
e,f\in E(G)
that are vertex disjoint.

Proof.

Suppose not. Since
G
is rigid, it has a crossing edge
e
. By redundant rigidity
G-e
is rigid, so there is some crossing edge
f\neq e
. By assumption
e
and
f
are not disjoint, so
e=uv
and
f=vw
for some vertices
u,v,w
. Now, by
P
-connectivity,
G-v
has a crossing edge
g
. If
g\neq uw
then two of
e,f,g
are vertex disjoint contradicting our assumption. So
g=uw
is the only crossing edge of
G-v
. Furthermore, if there is some crossing edge
h\neq e,f
that is incident to
v
then
h,g
are disjoint crossing edges, contradicting our assumption. It follows that
e
and
f
are the only crossing edges in
G-uw
and so
G-uw
has no crossing cycle, contradicting the assumption that
G-uw
is rigid.
∎

Claim 7.3
.

There are crossing edges
e,f
and cycles
C,D
so that
e,f
are vertex disjoint and
e\in E(C)\setminus E(D)
,
f\in E(D)\setminus E(C)
.

Proof.

By Claim
7.2
we can choose vertex disjoint crossing edges
e,f\in\mathcal{E}
.
Since
G
is 2-connected there are (not necessarily distinct) cycles
C,D
such that
e\in E(C)
and
f\in E(D)
.
Suppose that one of

C,D
, without loss of generality
D
, contains both of
e,f
. If
G-\{e,f\}
is
connected then it follows that
G
has a subgraph of type 3, contradicting our assumption.
So we can assume that
G-\{e,f\}
is not connected.

Now
G-e
is rigid and so contains a crossing cycle
D^{\prime}
. Since
f
is a bridge of
G-e
,
D^{\prime}
is contained in
G-\{e,f\}
. Now
D^{\prime}
contains at least two crossing edges,
f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}
and without loss of generality we can assume that
f^{\prime}
and
e
are vertex disjoint.
Then
C\cup D^{\prime}
contains a cycle
C^{\prime}
which contains
e
and avoids
f^{\prime}
, and
e,f^{\prime},C^{\prime},D^{\prime}
are the required edges and cycles.
∎

Now we can complete the proof of Lemma
7.1
.
Let
e,f
be edges and
C,D
be cycles as in the statement of Claim
7.3
. Suppose that
e
is vertex disjoint from
D
. Since
G
is 2-connected there are two vertex disjoint paths
P_{1},P_{2}
from the endpoints of
e
to the vertex set of
D
. Since
D
contains the crossing edge
f
, the graph
P_{1}\cup P_{2}\cup e\cup D
is a type 3 graph, contradicting our assumption.

So we may suppose that
e
is incident to
D
. If
e
is a chord of
D
then since
f
and
e
are disjoint, the graph
D\cup e
is type 3. Otherwise suppose
e=uv
with
v\in V(D)
and
u\not\in V(D)
. Then, since
G
is
2
-connected, we can find a path
P
from
u
to
D
that does not contain
v
. Now
P\cup e\cup D
is a type 3 graph. Thus in all cases we arrive at a contradiction.

∎

Now we recall a basic and well-known property of 2-connected graphs.
We include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 7.4
.

Suppose that
D
is a
2
-connected graph and that
K
is a subgraph of
D
with at least two vertices. There is a sequence of subgraphs
K_{1},\dots,K_{s}
of
D
such that
K_{1}=K
,
K_{s}=D
and for
i=1,\dots,s-1
,
K_{i+1}=K_{i}\cup P_{i}
where
P_{i}
is a simple path internally vertex disjoint from
K_{i}
whose endvertices are distinct and both lie in
K_{i}
.

Proof.

Suppose that we have constructed
K_{1},\dots,K_{j}
as required and that
K_{j}\neq D
.
If
V(K_{j})=V(D)
then choose some edge
uv\in E(D)\setminus E(K_{j})
, let
P_{j}
be the subgraph of
D
induced by
\{u,v\}
.
On the other hand if
V(K_{j})\neq V(D)
then since
D
is connected we can choose an edge
uv\in E(D)
such that
u\in V(K_{j})
and
v\not\in V(K_{j})
.
Since
|V(K_{j})|\geq|V(K)|\geq 2
and since
D
is 2-connected there is a path from
v
to
V(K_{j})
that misses
u
.
Let
R
be a path of minimal length from
v
to
V(K_{j})
such that
u\not\in V(R)
and let
P_{j}=R+u+uv
.
Now let
K_{j+1}=K_{j}\cup P_{j}
and we can continue in this way until we have constructed the required sequence.
∎

In the case where
K
is the graph consisting of a single edge, the sequence
K_{1},\dots,K_{s}
is often referred to as an open ear decomposition of
D
.
Hence, we call a path
P_{i}
given in Lemma
7.4

an open ear
of
K_{i}
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Suppose that
G
is a crossing partitioned graph
and that
\mathcal{L}
is a set of lines in general position.
If
G
is P-connected, redundantly
\mathcal{L}
-rigid and
2
-connected then
G
is generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

Proof.

Let
H
be the subgraph whose existence is asserted by Lemma
7.1
.
By Lemmas
6.2
,
6.3
and
6.4

H
is generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. Now, by Lemma
7.4
there is a sequence of graphs

H=G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s}=G
and for
i=1,\dots,s-1

such that
G_{i+1}=G_{i}\cup P_{i}
, where
P_{i}
is an open ear of
G_{i}
.
Since edge-addition and subdivision preserve generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity by Theorem
5.5
, the addition of an open ear preserves generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity. Hence
G
is generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.
∎

of a graph

is a subgraph that is either a maximal 2-connected subgraph of

and whose edge set is
\{Dv:D\in B(G),v\in C(G)\cap V(D)\}
. Clearly the block-cutvertex forest is a tree if and only if

is connected. A

is a block that is a
leaf of the block-cutvertex forest.

Lemma 7.6
.

Suppose that
G
is connected, P-connected and redundantly rigid and that
L,K
are distinct leaf blocks of
G
. Then
[L,K]
is globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

Proof.

Suppose that
[L,K]=D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{k}
where

L=D_{1},\dots,D_{k}=K
are blocks of
G
. Let
v_{i}=V(D_{i})\cap V(D_{i+1})
,
i=1,\dots,k-1
.
By redundant rigidity,
K
and
L
are maximal 2-connected subgraphs of
G
.
Since
G
is P-connected there is a crossing edge

e
in
L-v_{1}
.
Since
L
is 2-connected, there is a cycle
C_{L}
in
L
such that
e\in E(C_{L})
.
Similarly,
K
has a cycle
C_{K}
that contains a crossing edge not incident to
v_{k-1}
.

By the connectivity of
G
,
G
has a path
P
with endvertices in

V(C_{L})
and
V(C_{K})
such that
P
is internally disjoint from
C_{L}
and
C_{K}
.
Let
H=C_{L}\cup C_{K}\cup P
.
Then
H
is a type 1 or type 2 partitioned graph,
and hence it is generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid by Lemmas
6.2
and
6.3
.

We now augment
H
to
[L,K]
by adding open ears sequentially.
Specifically, for each
i=1,\dots,k-1
,

E(D_{i})\cap E(H)\neq\emptyset
holds by the definition of block-cutvertex forests.
Hence, by Lemma
7.4
,
D_{i}\cap H
can be augmented to
D_{i}
by adding open ears.
Therefore,
H
can be augmented to
[L,K]
by adding open ears.
Since the addition of an open ear preserves generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity by Theorem
5.5
, the global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity of
[L,K]
follows from that of
H
.
∎

Finally, our main result.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a set of lines in general position.
A crossing partitioned graph
G
is generically
globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid if and only if
G
is
P-connected and redundantly
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

Proof.

Following Theorem
4.6
, it remains to show that if
G
is P-connected and redundantly
\mathcal{L}
-rigid then it is generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

If
G
is 2-connected then Lemma
7.5
gives the required conclusion.
If
G
is not
2-connected but is connected, then there are leaf blocks
L_{i},K_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,s

such that
L_{i}\neq K_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
,
G=\cup_{i=1}^{s}[L_{i},K_{i}]
and such that for
i=2,\dots,s
,
[L_{i},K_{i}]
shares a block with
[L_{j},K_{j}]
for some
j<i
.
Now an easy induction argument using Lemma
7.6
and Theorem
5.6
shows that
G
is generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

If
G
is not connected, then the
P
-connectivity of
G
implies that
|\mathcal{L}[H]|\geq 3
for each connected component
H
of
G
.
Hence, the generic global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity of
G
follows by applying the induction hypothesis to each connected component and then applying Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Corollary 7.8
.

Suppose that
(G,p)
is a generic globally rigid
\mathcal{L}
-framework. Then
G
is generically globally
\mathcal{L}
-rigid.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
,
G
is P-connected and redundantly
\mathcal{L}
-rigid. The result follows from Theorem
7.7
.
∎

8
Concluding Remarks

1. Theorems
3.1
and
7.7
are good characterisations in the sense that they lead quickly to efficient deterministic algorithms to check
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity and global
\mathcal{L}
-rigidity.

2. It would be natural to consider extensions of our results where lines are replaced by (two-dimensional) planes. When
\mathcal{L}
is replaced by a set of parallel planes then characterising rigidity is a straightforward extension of the plane case (see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 5.1]
) and
global rigidity is similarly easy.
However allowing the planes to be non-parallel, even for generic planes, opens up substantial difficulties that arise in the standard bar-joint rigidity model in dimension at least 3. One fundamental such difficulty is the existence of ‘flexible circuits’ in the
d
-dimensional rigidity matroid when
d\geq 3
(see
[
8
,
12
]
inter alia). Perhaps the simplest non-trivial flexible circuit that can arise in the context under discussion is the graph obtained from two copies of
K_{4}
sharing a single vertex
v
such that
v
is the only vertex on the second plane. Here the natural sparsity counts would predict rigidity but there is an obvious motion.
Similarly, extending the 2-dimensional global rigidity characterisation of Jackson and Jordán
[
11
]
to non-parallel planes is a challenging open problem.

3. A related generalisation of our results would be to allow one additional part in the partition of a partitioned graph, with this part corresponding to vertices that are not constrained to some line of
\mathcal{L}
. To avoid well known problems in dimension at least 3, let us restrict to the case when
d=2
. When both the set of points and the set of lines are generic then rigidity
[
23
]
and global rigidity
[
9
]
are understood. When the points are generic but the lines are allowed to be non-generic then a result of
[
17
]
applies for rigidity. When more than two vertices are allowed on any given line then, even with generic lines, this seems to be an open but potentially tractable problem for both rigidity and global rigidity.

4. It is possible to adapt Theorem
3.1
to apply to ‘circle constrained frameworks’; here the set of lines is replaced by a set of circles and the analogue of parallel is concentric.
The intuition for this translation is that the constraint that a point
p(v)
moves on a circle
C
at the infinitesimal level is simply the constraint that
p(v)
moves on the tangent line to
C
at
p(v)
. However this observation does not provide any information about whether the global rigidity problem is equivalent in the circle constrained rigidity model. In
[
9
]
an equivalence for global rigidity was deduced in dimension 2 in the special case where the set of points and lines is generic. It may also be interesting to extend this question to general curves.

Introduction and preliminaries

In this paper, we study a problem of estimation of the value
f(\Sigma)
of a smooth real valued functional
f
based on i.i.d. mean zero Gaussian observations

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
with values in a separable Banach space and with unknown covariance operator
\Sigma.
The complexity of such estimation problems is
naturally characterized by the effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
of covariance operator
\Sigma.
Our goal is to develop estimators of
f(\Sigma)

with an optimal dependence of the risk on the sample size
n,
effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
and the degree of smoothness of functional
f.

Up to now, this problem has been studied only in the case of a finite dimensional Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the spectrum of the covariance

\Sigma
being bounded from above and bounded away from zero. In such a “nearly isotropic” case, the effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
is of the same order as
dimension
d.
We study the problem in a dimension free framework with its complexity being characterized by
{\bf r}(\Sigma).

In the rest of Section
1
, we review the results on bounds for sample covariance operators in terms of effective rank and provide the definitions of Hölder smoothness classes in Banach spaces as well as Orlicz norms used throughout the paper. We also provide a brief review of known results on functional estimation. The main results of the paper are stated in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we discuss a functional estimation method based on linear aggregation of plug-in estimators with different sample sizes and its jackknife version.
Concentration bounds for functionals of sample covariance needed to prove the main results are developed in Section
4
. The proofs of the main results, stated in Section
2
, are provided in Section
5
, in which the first two subsections deal with the proofs of upper bounds and Subsection
5.3
deals with the proofs of minimax lower bounds.

1.1
Estimation of covariance operators and effective rank

Let
E
be a separable Banach space with the dual space
E^{\ast}.
Given a bounded linear functional
u\in E^{\ast}
and a vector
x\in E,
we will use the inner product
notation
\langle x,u\rangle
for the value of
u
at
x
(in the case when
E={\mathbb{H}}
is a Hilbert space, this becomes the actual inner product).
Denote by
L(E^{\ast},E)
the space of all bounded linear operators

A:E^{\ast}\mapsto E
equipped with the operator norm

\displaystyle\|A\|:=\sup_{u\in E^{\ast},\|u\|\leq 1}\|Au\|.

Let
X
be a Gaussian random variable in
E
with mean zero and covariance operator
\Sigma:E^{\ast}\mapsto E,

\displaystyle\Sigma u:={\mathbb{E}}\langle X,u\rangle X,u\in E^{\ast}.

Given i.i.d. copies
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of
X,
let
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}:E^{\ast}\mapsto E
be the sample covariance operator based on
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}:

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}u:=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle X_{j},u\rangle X_{j},u\in E^{\ast}.

Recall that, for
x,y\in E,
the tensor product
x\otimes y
could be viewed as a bounded operator from
E^{\ast}
to
E,
defined
as follows:
(x\otimes y)u:=x\langle y,u\rangle,u\in E^{\ast}.
Then

\Sigma={\mathbb{E}}(X\otimes X)
and
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}\otimes X_{j}.

The estimation error
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|
of sample covariance operator
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}
can be characterized in terms of operator norm
\|\Sigma\|

and so called
effective rank

{\bf r}(\Sigma)
of covariance operator
\Sigma,
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma):=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|}.

Note that

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma)=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}\langle X,u\rangle^{2}}{\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\langle X,u\rangle^{2}}\geq 1

and that
{\bf r}(\lambda\Sigma)={\bf r}(\Sigma),\lambda>0.

Moreover, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq{\rm rank}(\Sigma)\leq{\rm dim}(E),

and,
if
E={\mathbb{H}}
is a Hilbert space, then

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma)=\frac{{\rm tr}(\Sigma)}{\|\Sigma\|}.

It is also worth mentioning that the effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
was used earlier (under a different name) in the literature on local theory of Banach spaces,
in particular, in connection with so called Gaussian reformulation of Dvoretzky’s theorem (see
[
30
]
, Theorem 4.4).

The next results were proved in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following bound holds:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\asymp\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\bigvee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr).

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
t\geq 1
with probability at least
1-e^{-t},

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr|\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\bigvee\frac{t}{n}\biggr).

In the above theorems and in what follows, we write
A\lesssim B
(for variables
A,B>0
) if there exists a numerical constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB.

The notation
A\gtrsim B
is equivalent to
B\lesssim A
and the notation
A\asymp B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B.
In some cases,
it is convenient to allow the constants in the above relationships to depend on some parameters. In such cases, the signs
\lesssim,\gtrsim,\asymp
will be provided with subscripts:
say,
A\lesssim_{s}B
means that there exists
C_{s}>0

such that
A\leq C_{s}B.

The bounds of theorems
1.1
and
1.2
show that the size of
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|
is characterized in terms of
\|\Sigma\|
and effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma).

In the case of finite dimensional Euclidean space
E={\mathbb{R}}^{d},

{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq d,
so, the bounds of the theorems imply well known bounds on

\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|
in terms of dimension
d.
Such dimension dependent bounds are sharp in the cases when covariance
\Sigma
is “nearly isotropic”
in the sense that the spectrum
\sigma(\Sigma)
of covariance
\Sigma
is bounded from above and bounded away from zero by positive numerical constants.
In fact, in this case
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\asymp d.

In the “non isotropic” case, when the eigenvalues of covariance could be close to zero (in particular, in the infinite dimensional case), the dimension free
description of covariance
\Sigma
in terms of its effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
becomes necessary.

Throughout the paper, we use the generic norm notation
\|\cdot\|
for a variety of different norms in different spaces. For instance,
it is the norm of Banach space
E,
of its dual space
E^{\ast},
the operator norm in the space
L(E^{\ast},E),
the operator norms in the spaces of multilinear forms in a Banach space, etc. In such cases, we do not provide the notation
\|\cdot\|
with subscripts and its meaning should be clear from the context. Only if there is some ambiguity due to the fact that multiple norms are used in the same space, the subscripts
will be used. For instance, for operators acting in a Hilbert space
{\mathbb{H}},
we will use the notation
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for the Schatten
p
-norms
for
p\geq 1
with
\|\cdot\|_{2}
being the Hilbert–Schmidt norm and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
being the nuclear norm. Norms in function spaces and spaces of r.v.
(such as the Hölder norms and Orlicz norms) will be also provided with subscripts.

1.2
Orlicz spaces, Hölder smoothness, etc

Before stating the main results, we will introduce some notations used throughout the paper. Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be
a convex nondecreasing function with
\psi(0)=0.

The Orlicz
\psi
-norm of a r.v.
\eta
is defined as

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\psi}:=\|\eta\|_{L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}})}:=\inf\Bigl\{c\geq 0:{\mathbb{E}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|\eta|}{c}\Bigr)\leq 1\Bigr\}.

Let
L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}})
be the space of all r.v. on a probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,{\mathbb{P}})
with finite
\psi
-norm.

For
\psi(u)=u^{p},u\geq 0,p\geq 1,
the
\psi
-norm coincides with the usual
L_{p}
-norm. The choice of
\psi=\psi_{\alpha},
where

\psi_{\alpha}(u):=e^{u^{\alpha}}-1,u\geq 0,\alpha\geq 1,

yields the so called
\psi_{\alpha}
-norms and various spaces of random variables with exponentially decaying tails, in particular, the space

L_{\psi_{2}}({\mathbb{P}})
of sub-gaussian r.v. and the space
L_{\psi_{1}}({\mathbb{P}})
of sub-exponential r.v.
It is well known that, for
\alpha\geq 1,
the
\psi_{\alpha}
-norm is equivalent to the following norm defined in terms of the
L_{p}
-norms:

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}\asymp\sup_{p\geq 1}p^{-1/\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}|\eta|^{p}.

(1.1)

Note that the right hand side also defines a norm of
\eta
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and, despite the fact that in this case
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}

is not a norm (since
\psi_{\alpha}
is not convex), the equivalence (
1.1
) still holds for
\alpha\in(0,1)
(with constants depending on
\alpha
).
With a little abuse of notation, we will use the right hand side of (
1.1
) as the definition of
\psi_{\alpha}
-norm for
\alpha\in(0,1)

(in fact, it will be convenient to use this definition for all
\alpha>0
). Alternatively, it is possible to modify the definition of function
\psi_{\alpha},\alpha<1
in a neighborhood
of
0
so that it is convex and defines an equivalent Orlicz norm.

For two nondecreasing convex loss functions
\varphi,\psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with
\varphi(0)=\psi(0)=0,

we write
\varphi\preceq\psi
and say that
\varphi
is dominated by
\psi
iff there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
\varphi(u)\leq c_{1}\psi(c_{2}u),u\geq 0.

This implies that
\|\eta\|_{\varphi}\lesssim_{c_{1},c_{2}}\|\eta\|_{\psi}.

In what follows, we will measure
the risk of estimators of
f(\Sigma)
by the
\psi
-norms with a properly chosen loss function
\psi.

For an arbitrary set (space)
S,
a Banach space
F
and
g:S\mapsto F,
denote

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}:=\sup_{x\in S}\|g(x)\|.

Also, if
F_{1},F_{2}
are Banach spaces and
g:F_{1}\mapsto F_{2},
let

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\rm Lip}:=\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in F_{1},x_{1}\neq x_{2}}\frac{\|g(x_{1})-g(x_{2})\|}{\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|}

and, for
\rho\in(0,1],
let

\displaystyle\|g\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}:=\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in F_{1},x_{1}\neq x_{2}}\frac{\|g(x_{1})-g(x_{2})\|}{\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{\rho}}.

We also denote

\displaystyle{\rm Lip}(F_{1},F_{2}):=\{g:F_{1}\mapsto F_{2}:\|g\|_{{\rm Lip}}<\infty\}\ {\rm and}\ {\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F_{1},F_{2}):=\{g:F_{1}\mapsto F_{2}:\|g\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}<\infty\}.

Similarly, one can define spaces
{\rm Lip}(U,F_{2})
and
{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(U,F_{2})
and the corresponding norms
\|\cdot\|_{\rm Lip}=\|\cdot\|_{{\rm Lip}(U)},\|\cdot\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}=\|\cdot\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(U)}

for an arbitrary subset
U\subset F_{1}.

If
F_{1}=F,F_{2}={\mathbb{R}},
we write
{\rm Lip}(F)={\rm Lip}(F,{\mathbb{R}})
and

{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)={\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F,{\mathbb{R}}).

For a
k
times Fréchet differentiable functional
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},

f^{(k)}
denotes its
k
-th Fréchet derivative
(with
f^{(0)}=f
and
f^{(1)}=f^{\prime}
).
Note that, for
x\in F,

f^{(k)}(x)
is a bounded symmetric
k
-linear form. For such a form
M[h_{1},\dots,h_{k}],

its operator norm is defined as

\displaystyle\|M\|:=\sup_{\|h_{1}\|,\dots,\|h_{k}\|\leq 1}|M[h_{1},\dots,h_{k}]|.

In particular, for
k=1,

f^{\prime}(x)
can be viewed as a bounded linear functional on
F,
so,
f^{\prime}(x)\in F^{\ast}
and we can write
f^{\prime}(x)[h]=\langle h,f^{\prime}(x)\rangle,h,x\in F.

In what follows, the spaces of bounded symmetric
k
-linear forms are always equipped with the operator norm.
For
s=k+\rho,

k\geq 0,\rho\in(0,1],
the Hölder
C^{s}
-norm of
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable functional
f
is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{s}}:=\max_{0\leq j\leq k}\sup_{x\in F}\|f^{(j)}(x)\|\vee\sup_{x,y\in F,x\neq y}\frac{\|f^{(k)}(x)-f^{(k)}(y)\|}{\|x-y\|^{\rho}}.

We will also need the following weighted version of
C^{s}
-norm: for
a>0,
let

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{s,a}}:=\max_{0\leq j\leq k}a^{j}\sup_{x\in F}\|f^{(j)}(x)\|\vee a^{s}\sup_{x,y\in F,x\neq y}\frac{\|f^{(k)}(x)-f^{(k)}(y)\|}{\|x-y\|^{\rho}}.

Clearly,

\displaystyle(a\wedge 1)^{s}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\leq\|f\|_{C^{s,a}}\leq(a\vee 1)^{s}\|f\|_{C^{s}}.

Similarly, for
s=k+\rho,k\geq 0,\rho\in(0,1],
one can define
C^{s}(U)
-norms and
C^{s,a}(U)
-norms for
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable functions
f:U\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}

in an open subset
U\subset F.

Remark 1.1
.

In this paper, we are interested in smoothness of functionals on the space
L(E^{\ast},E)
of symmetric operators from
E^{\ast}
into
E
(equipped with the operator norm).
In particular, in the case when
E={\mathbb{H}}
is a separable Hilbert space and
L(E^{\ast},E)=L({\mathbb{H}})
is the space of bounded self-adjoint operators in
{\mathbb{H}},
an interesting class of functionals
is
f(S):=\langle g(S),B\rangle,S\in L({\mathbb{H}}),
where
g:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is a smooth function of real variable and
B:{\mathbb{H}}\mapsto{\mathbb{H}}
is a linear operator.
It is known from the operator theory that the Hölder smoothness of operator function
L({\mathbb{H}})\ni S\mapsto g(S)\in L({\mathbb{H}})
is related to the Besov smoothness of function
g:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}.

In particular, for all
s>0
and for all functions
g
from the Besov space
B^{s}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}}),

\displaystyle\|g(\cdot)\|_{C^{s}(L({\mathbb{H}}))}\lesssim_{s}\|g\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}})}

(see
[
12
]
, Corollary 2 and references therein). Therefore, for functional
f(S),S\in L({\mathbb{H}}),
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{s}(L({\mathbb{H}}))}\lesssim_{s}\|g\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|B\|_{1}.

This fact can be used to reduce the problem of estimation of many important functionals of covariance to the problem of estimation of Hölder smooth functionals.
For instance, this approach could be applied to the functional
f(\Sigma)=\langle P(\Sigma)u,v\rangle,
which is a bilinear form of a spectral projection
P(\Sigma)
of covariance operator
\Sigma
corresponding to its eigenvalue
\lambda(\Sigma)

that is “well separated” from the rest of its spectrum.

1.3
A brief review of recent results on estimation of functionals of covariance operators

The problem of estimation of functionals of parameters of high-dimensional and infinite-dimensional statistical models has a long history going back
to the 1970s. A very incomplete list of important references includes
[
25
,
26
,
9
,
10
,
3
,
28
,
4
,
24
,
27
,
29
,
32
,
6
]
. One of the main difficulties in this problem is related to the fact that, in the case of a high-dimensional parameter
\theta
and its
reasonable estimator
\hat{\theta}
(for instance, the maximum likelihood estimator), a naive plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\theta})
of a non-linear functional
f(\theta)
would typically have a large bias and, due to this, would fail to achieve optimal convergence rates. Thus, the development of bias reduction methods becomes a crucial ingredient of functional estimation. Motivated by this problem, several general approaches to bias reduction (jackknife, bootstrap and Taylor expansions methods) have been studied in recent paper
[
11
]
in the case of estimation of a smooth function of the parameter of binomial model and it was shown that,
even in this simple case, the analysis of these approaches lead to non-trivial problems of approximation theory.

Estimation of functionals of covariance operators (for instance, in the case of normal models) is largely motivated by high-dimensional problems in multivariate statistical analysis.
In particular, in principal component analysis, it is of importance to develop methods of estimation of linear forms of eigenvectors of unknown covariance, or of bilinear forms of its
spectral projections, as well as some other functionals. Such problems have been studied in
[
15
,
17
]
in a dimension free framework with the effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
playing the role of complexity parameter (in the case of i.i.d. data in a Hilbert space
{\mathbb{H}}
).
The bias reduction method developed in
[
15
]

for estimation of linear forms of eigenvectors of
\Sigma
was rather specialized and based on certain representations of the bias of bilinear forms of spectral projections of sample
covariance. This method was developed to the full extent in
[
18
]
yielding asymptotically efficient estimators of linear forms of eigenvectors of
\Sigma

provided that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)=o(n).
The extension of this method to the case of estimation of bilinear forms of spectral projections of
\Sigma
(corresponding to multiple eigenvalues)
has not been developed yet.

These difficulties motivated the study of more general bias reduction methods in the problem of estimation of functionals of the form
f(\Sigma):=\langle g(\Sigma),B\rangle,

where
g:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is a smooth function of real variable
[
12
,
13
]
. It is known that, if
B
is a nuclear operator and function
g
belongs to the Besov space
B^{s}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}}),
then functional
f
defined on the space of bounded self-adjoint operators (equipped with an operator norm)
is of Hölder smoothness
s
(see Remark
1.1
). Note that the bilinear form
\langle P_{\lambda}u,v\rangle
of the spectral projection
P_{\lambda}
of operator
\Sigma
corresponding
to its eigenvalue
\lambda
could be represented as
\langle g(\Sigma),B\rangle
for a smooth function
g
supported in a small enough neighborhood of
\lambda

(that does not contain other eigenvalues of
\Sigma
). Moreover, by standard perturbation arguments, this representation also holds in an operator norm neighborhood
of
\Sigma.
Thus, estimation of bilinear forms of spectral projections of
\Sigma
could be reduced to estimation of functionals of the form
\langle g(\Sigma),B\rangle.

The bias reduction method studied in
[
12
,
13
,
20
,
21
]
(called the
bootstrap chain bias reduction
)
goes back to the idea of iterated bootstrap bias reduction
[
8
]
. In the case of binomial model,
it was also studied in
[
11
]
based on the ideas and results from classical approximation theory. To describe this approach in the case of functionals of covariance (the case of general parameter
\theta
is similar),
consider the following
Wishart operator

\displaystyle({\mathcal{T}}h)(\Sigma):={\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}h(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{+}({\mathbb{H}})}g(S)P(\Sigma,dS),h\in L_{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{+}({\mathbb{H}}))

acting in the space of bounded functions on the cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{+}({\mathbb{H}})
of covariance operators in
{\mathbb{H}},
where

\displaystyle P(\Sigma,A):={\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}\{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\in A\},A\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{+}({\mathbb{H}})

is the distribution of the sample covariance operator
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}
(clearly,
P(\Sigma,A)
is a Markov kernel in the cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{+}({\mathbb{H}})).

Let
({\mathcal{B}}h)(\Sigma):=({\mathcal{T}}h)(\Sigma)-h(\Sigma),\Sigma\in{\mathcal{C}}_{+}({\mathbb{H}}).
Since the bias of the plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

of
f(\Sigma)
is equal to
({\mathcal{B}}f)(\Sigma),
the first order bias correction yields an estimator
f_{1}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}):=f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-({\mathcal{B}}f)(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

Similarly, the bias of estimator
({\mathcal{B}}f)(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
of
({\mathcal{B}}f)(\Sigma)
is equal to
({\mathcal{B}}^{2}f)(\Sigma),
and the second order bias correction
yields an estimator
f_{2}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}):=f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-({\mathcal{B}}f)(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})+({\mathcal{B}}^{2}f)(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).
After
k
iterations, we get an estimator

\displaystyle f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}({\mathcal{B}}^{j}f)(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

Another way to look at it is to observe that to find an estimator of
f(\Sigma)
with a small bias, one has to solve approximately the operator equation
({\mathcal{T}}h)(\Sigma)=f(\Sigma),\Sigma\in{\mathcal{C}}_{+}({\mathbb{H}})
and to use
h(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
as an estimator. Since
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{I}}+{\mathcal{B}},
the solution of the equation
Th=f
can be formally written as a Neumann series

\displaystyle h(\Sigma)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}({\mathcal{B}}^{j}f)(\Sigma)

and the partial sums of this series

\displaystyle f_{k}(\Sigma):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}({\mathcal{B}}^{j}f)(\Sigma)

provide approximate solutions of the equation.

It is easy to see that the bias of estimator
f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
is given by the following formula:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)=(-1)^{k}({\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}f)(\Sigma),

so, to show that the bias of estimator
f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
is sufficiently small when the functional
f
is sufficiently smooth, it is enough to control
the size of
({\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}f)(\Sigma)
for smooth
f.
This is based on representation of functions
{\mathcal{B}}^{k}f,k\geq 0
in terms
of so called
bootstrap chain
, which is a Markov chain
\{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(k)}:k\geq 0\}
with
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(0)}=\Sigma
and with transition probability
kernel
P(\Sigma;A).
This chain can be interpreted as an application of iterative parametric bootstrap to estimator
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}:
for
k=0,
the chain starts at
\Sigma;

for
k=1,
its value
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(1)}=\hat{\Sigma}_{n};
for
k=2,
its value
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(2)}
is a bootstrap estimator based on i.i.d. data
\hat{X}_{1},\dots,\hat{X}_{n}

resampled from
N(0;\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
(conditionally on
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}
), etc. It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)(\Sigma)={\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}{k\choose j}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(j)}),

which is the expectation of the
k
-th order difference of the values of functional
f
along the trajectory of bootstrap chain.
For a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},
its
k
-th order difference at point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
with step
h
is

\displaystyle(\Delta^{k}_{h}f)(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}{k\choose j}f(x+jh)

and, if
f
is
k
times continuously differentiable,
(\Delta^{k}_{h}f)(x)=O(h^{k}).

In view of theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
under the assumption
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n,

\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|
is roughly of the order
\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}},
and this is also the size of the jumps
of bootstrap chain
\{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(k)}:k\geq 0\}.
By an analogy with the behavior of the
k
-th order differences of smooth functions in the real line, one could expect that, for a
k
times continuously differentiable functional
f,
one would have that

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)(\Sigma)\lesssim\biggl(\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k}.

This would imply that estimator
f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
does have a reduced bias when
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
is small comparing with
n
and

k
is sufficiently large. The rigorous proof of this fact is, however, rather complicated and it required the development of a number of probabilistic
and analytic tools. It was done in
[
12
,
13
]
for functionals of the form
f(\Sigma)=\langle g(\Sigma),B\rangle

and in
[
21
]
for general Hölder smooth functionals, yielding sharp bounds on the risk of estimator
f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
and the proof of its asymptotic efficiency (in fact, in the last paper the problem of estimation of functionals of unknown mean and covariance of normal model was studied).
In both cases, however, it was done
only
in the case of covariances
in the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and under the assumption that the spectrum of
\Sigma
belongs to the interval
[1/a,a]
for some
a\geq 1.

Note that, in this case,
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\asymp d,
so, there is no need to use the effective rank of
\Sigma
as a complexity parameter.
This assumption essentially means that the covariance is “almost” isotropic and the results similar to the ones stated in Section
2
below
were proved for estimator
f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
with dimension
d
instead of
{\bf r}(\Sigma).
In particular, it was shown that, for such functional estimators,
the upper bound on the estimation error is of the order
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{s},
where
s
is the degree of Hölder smoothness
of the functional.

We conjecture that similar results hold for estimator
f_{k}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
in the general dimension free case with complexity of the problem characterized by the effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
, but we do not know how to prove this and the methods developed in
[
12
,
13
,
21
]
do not seem to suffice for this. However, as it is stated in Section
2
and will be proved later in the paper, such results do hold
for jackknife type estimators and their proofs are much simpler than for bootstrap chain estimators.

Minimax bounds similar to the bound of Theorem
2.7
below were proved in
[
20
]
for functionals of parameters of Gaussian shift models. In
[
21
]
, a similar result was stated for functionals of mean
and covariance of high-dimensional normal model. However, in this case, the bound is attained for functionals depending only on the mean and the result easily followed from the minimax bounds for Gaussian shift models obtained in
[
20
]
.
For functionals of covariance, the problem remained open and it is solved in the current paper (Theorem
2.7
). The proof is based on some of the ideas already present in the case of Gaussian shift model, but the argument is much more sophisticated in the case of functionals of covariance (see Section
5.3
).

Some other results on bootstrap chain bias reduction and efficient estimation of functionals of unknown parameters for Gaussian shift models, more general random shift models, log-concave location models can be found in
[
20
,
22
,
19
]
.
In
[
14
]
, this method was studied in the case of general high-dimensional parametric models under the assumption
that there exists an estimator of unknown parameter whose distribution could be approximated by Gaussian with sufficient accuracy.
In
[
34
]
, a version of Taylor expansions method of bias reduction was developed for estimation of functionals of the unknown mean
with not necessarily i.i.d. and not necessarily Gaussian additive random noise. This method was also applied in
[
34
]
to additive
functionals with not necessarily smooth components (via an approximation by smooth functionals).

Although the focus of the current paper is on estimation of Hölder smooth functionals of covariance, the bias reduction method we develop could be useful
in estimation of non-smooth functionals as well, usually, in combination with other tools (as it is the case with other bias reduction methods briefly reviewed in this section).
However, in our view, the development of higher order bias reduction methods for functionals in various smoothness classes is the basic problem in functional estimation and its understanding
is crucial for the development of similar methods for broader classes functionals lacking smoothness.

Main results

In this section, we provide a number of results on estimation error rates for smooth functionals of covariance in the

Recall that we are interested in estimation of the value
f(\Sigma)
of a functional
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
based on i.i.d. Gaussian observations

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\sim N(0,\Sigma)
with values in
E
and unknown covariance
\Sigma.

First we state a couple of results on estimation of
f(\Sigma)
for a functional
f
of smoothness
s\in(0,2].

It turns out that, in this case, a simple plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
suffices to achieve optimal error rates.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d.
N(0,\Sigma).

(i) Suppose
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies the Hölder condition
with exponent
\rho\in(0,1].
Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{\rho}\biggl[\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{\rho}+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)^{\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\Biggr)^{\rho}+\biggl(\frac{p}{n}\biggr)^{\rho}\biggr].

(ii) Suppose
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is Lipschitz and, moreover, it is Fréchet continuously differentiable with the derivative
f^{\prime}
satisfying the Hölder condition
with exponent
\rho\in(0,1].

Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)+\frac{p}{n}\biggr)+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\bigvee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{1+\rho}.

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 2.1
.

(i) Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
(i),

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1/\rho}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{\rho}.

(ii) Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
(ii),

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\bigvee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{1+\rho}.

Note that, for
s=\rho\in(0,1),
the error rate of plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
is slower than
n^{-1/2}.
For
s=1,
the
n^{-1/2}
-rate is possible, but only when the effective rank

{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim 1.
For
s=1+\rho,\rho\in(0,1],
the
n^{-1/2}
-rate is possible for the plug-in estimator even for large
{\bf r}(\Sigma),
provided that the degree of smoothness

s
is above certain threshold.
Namely, this is the case when
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
and
s\geq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}.
The optimality of the error rates of Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.1
(or, more precisely, of the local versions of these results in the case when
E={\mathbb{H}}
is a Hilbert space) follows from Theorem
2.7
stated later in this section.

In the case when the degree of smoothness
s
of the functional
f
is larger than
1,
it is also possible to prove the following theorem that, in particular, implies normal approximation properties of plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.
N(0,\Sigma)
with
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Let
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a Fréchet continuously differentiable functional with
the first derivative
f^{\prime}
satisfying the Hölder condition
with exponent
\rho\in(0,1].
Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\rho}\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl[\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\rho)/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\rho}+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{1+\rho}\biggr].

An immediate consequence is the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1/(1+\rho)}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim_{\rho}\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{1+\rho}.

Note that the error of the first order linear approximation of plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
becomes
o(n^{-1/2})
when
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2)

and
s=1+\rho>\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
which provides a way to establish normal approximation of
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
in this case.

It is well known that, for functionals
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
of smoothness
s>2,
the plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
of
f(\Sigma)
could become suboptimal due to its large bias and a bias reduction is needed to construct estimators with optimal error rates (see, e.g.,
[
12
,
13
]
). In particular, for
s>2,
there are functionals
f
for which
plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
fails to achieve
n^{-1/2}
-rate when
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
is larger that
n^{1/2}
regardless of how large the degree of smoothness
s
of the functional is.
We will study an approach to this bias reduction problem based
on
linear aggregation of several plug-in estimators with different sample sizes
and with the coefficients of the linear combination chosen in such a way that the biases on the plug-in estimators almost cancel each other out.
This idea is well known in bias reduction literature and, in particular, it leads to a class of jackknife bias reduction methods (see
[
11
]
).

To define our estimators of
f(\Sigma),
let
k\geq 1
and let
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
denote the sample sizes of plug-in estimators.
Assume that
n/c\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{k}\leq n
for some
c>1
and denote
\vec{n}:=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k}).

Define

\displaystyle T_{f,\vec{n}}^{(1)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}),

where

\displaystyle C_{j}:=\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{j}-n_{i}},j=1,\dots,k.

(2.1)

It could be shown that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}=1
(see
[
11
]
).

In the rest of the paper, the following assumption holds.

Assumption 1
.

Suppose that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}|C_{j}|\lesssim_{k}1.

Clearly, for Assumption
1
to hold, it is necessary that

n_{j+1}-n_{j}\asymp n,j=1,\dots,k-1.

For instance, one could take
n_{j}:=q^{j-k}n,j=1,\dots,k
for some
q>1.

Let now
{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm sym}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by random variables of the form
\psi(X_{1}\otimes X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\otimes X_{n}),

where
\psi:L(E^{\ast},E)\times\dots\times L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is a symmetric Borel function of
n
variables.
Let

\displaystyle T_{f,\vec{n}}^{(2)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl(T_{f,\vec{n}}^{(1)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\Bigl|{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm sym}\Bigr),

which could be easily written as a linear combination of
U
-statistics (see Section
3
).
It is well known that the plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
of
f(\Sigma)
has a large bias (despite the fact that
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}
is an unbiased estimator of
\Sigma
).
It turns out, however, that, for a smooth functional
f
with the choice (
2.1
) of weights
C_{j},
the biases of estimators
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}})
of
f(\Sigma)
almost cancel out resulting in a small bias of estimators

T^{(1)}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and
T^{(2)}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).
Of course, these estimators depend on the parameter
k
and on the choice of sample sizes
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
for which Assumption
1
holds.
Moreover,
the choice of
k
in the main results stated below depends on the degree of smoothness of the functional
f.

However, to simplify the notations, we will write in what follows
T^{(1)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=T^{(1)}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and
T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=T^{(2)}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}),
suppressing their dependence on

k
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}.

The following results will be proved.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.
N(0,\Sigma).

Let
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be Lipschitz and, moreover, for some
k\geq 2,
let it be
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable with the
k
-th derivative
f^{(k)}
satisfying the Hölder condition
with exponent
\rho\in(0,1].

Then, for
i=1,2
and for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho}\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)+\frac{p}{n}\biggr)+\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\bigvee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

Corollary 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
, for
i=1,2,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim_{s}\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}+\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\bigvee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

Remark 2.1
.

1.

Clearly, the results of Corollary
2.3
and Corollary
2.1
(ii)
imply that the same bounds also hold for the
\psi
-norm error for an arbitrary loss function

\psi\preceq\psi_{1}.

2.

If
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n,
the bound of Corollary
2.3
simplifies as follows:

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim_{s}\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}+\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

Moreover, if
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
s:=k+\rho\geq\frac{1}{1-\alpha},

then the first term of the bound is dominant and

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}=O(n^{-1/2})

yielding the classical parametric rates of convergence of estimators
T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
for sufficiently smooth functionals
f.

The next theorem shows that if
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

then

can be approximated by a sum of i.i.d. random variables with the remainder of the order

which suffices to establish the normal approximation of

\sqrt{n}(T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))
(yielding both asymptotic normality and asymptotic efficiency of estimator

).

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.

with
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Let

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho,\gamma}\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\gamma}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\gamma}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\gamma}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\gamma}\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\gamma)/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\gamma}\biggr)

\displaystyle+\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.
N(0,\Sigma)
with
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

If
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is
k

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{\beta}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho}\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{1}}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1/\beta}}{\sqrt{n}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{1/\beta-1}+\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

Since

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle X_{j}\otimes X_{j},f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle-{\mathbb{E}}\langle X\otimes X,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle,

the bounds of

, Corollary
2.2
,
Theorem
2.4

can be used to approximate the “Orcliz risk”

for losses
\psi
dominated by the sub-exponential loss

(in particular, for the

-losses) as well as to develop normal approximation

Let

\displaystyle\sigma_{f}^{2}(\Sigma):={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl(\langle X\otimes X,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle-{\mathbb{E}}\langle X\otimes X,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr)^{2}.

It could be shown (see Lemma
5.1
below) that
\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|.

Corollary 2.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sqrt{n}\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}-\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)\Bigr|\lesssim_{\rho}\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{1+\rho}.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.4
, the following bound holds:

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sqrt{n}\Bigl\|T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}-\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho,\gamma}\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\gamma}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\gamma}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\gamma}+\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\sqrt{n}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

It follows from the bound of the last corollary that, if
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

then the normalized

-risk

\sqrt{n}\Bigl\|T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

Moreover, it is possible to show that, under the same conditions,

For

the same claims hold true for the plug-in estimator

To state the results on normal approximation, it will be convenient to use so called Wasserstein
\psi
-distances
W_{\psi}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
between

\eta_{1},\eta_{2},
or, more precisely, between their distributions. For a convex non-decreasing function

with
\psi(0)=0,
define

\displaystyle W_{\psi}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=W_{\psi,{\mathbb{P}}}:=W_{L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}})}:=\inf\Bigl\{\|\eta_{1}^{\prime}-\eta_{2}^{\prime}\|_{\psi}:\eta_{1}^{\prime}\overset{d}{=}\eta_{1},\eta_{2}^{\prime}\overset{d}{=}\eta_{2}\Bigr\},

where the infimum is taken over all r.v.
\eta_{1}^{\prime},\eta_{2}^{\prime}
defined on the same probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,{\mathbb{P}})

such that
\eta_{1}^{\prime}\overset{d}{=}\eta_{1},\eta_{2}^{\prime}\overset{d}{=}\eta_{2}.

For
\psi(u)=u^{p},u\geq 0,p\geq 1,
the Wasserstein
\psi

-distance and it will be denoted

W_{p}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=W_{p,{\mathbb{P}}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}).
We can also use this definition for

Finally, it could be applied to the case
of

subject to a modification of the definition of the

-norms discussed above (see (
1.1
)).

The following corollary will be proved.

Corollary 2.6
.

(i) Under the assumptions of Theorem

,

\displaystyle W_{2,{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma))}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)},Z\biggr)\lesssim_{\rho}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|^{2}}{\sigma_{f}^{2}(\Sigma)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\sqrt{n}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{1+\rho}.

Moreover, under the assumptions of Corollary
2.2
,

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1/(1+\rho)},{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma))}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)},Z\biggr)\lesssim_{\rho}C\biggl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\biggr)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\sqrt{n}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

(ii) Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.4
,

\displaystyle W_{2,{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(T_{f}^{(2)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)},Z\biggr)\lesssim_{k,\rho,\gamma}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|^{2}}{\sigma_{f}^{2}(\Sigma)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\gamma}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\gamma}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\gamma}+\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\sqrt{n}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

Moreover, under the assumptions of Corollary
2.4
for all
\beta\in[1/2,1),

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{\beta},{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(T_{f}^{(2)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)},Z\biggr)\lesssim_{k,\rho}C\biggl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\biggr)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{1}}\|\Sigma\|^{1/\beta}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{1/\beta-1}+\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\sqrt{n}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

In both claims
C\biggl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\biggr)>0
is a constant depending only on
\frac{\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}.

Remark 2.2
.

Let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(A,B,r,\sigma_{0}):=\Bigl\{\|\Sigma\|\leq A,\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\leq B,{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq r,\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)\geq\sigma_{0}\Bigr\}

for
A>0,B>0,r>0,\sigma_{0}>0.
It immediately follows from Corollary
2.6
that, if
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
s=k+\rho>\frac{1}{1-\alpha},

k\geq 2,\rho\in(0,1],

then, for all
\beta<1,

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(A,B,n^{\alpha},\sigma_{0})}W_{\psi_{\beta},{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(T_{f}^{(2)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)},Z\biggr)\to 0\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

This easily implies the asymptotic normality of estimator
T_{f}^{(2)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of
f(\Sigma)
with
\sqrt{n}
-rate and limit variance
\sigma_{f}(\Sigma):

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(A,B,n^{\alpha},\sigma_{0})}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\biggl|{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}\biggl\{\frac{\sqrt{n}(T_{f}^{(2)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\leq x\biggr\}-{\mathbb{P}}\{Z\leq x\}\biggr|\to 0\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty,

and, moreover, for any loss function
\psi
dominated by
\psi_{\beta}
for some
\beta<1,
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{f}(A,B,n^{\alpha},\sigma_{0})}\biggl|\biggl\|\frac{\sqrt{n}(T_{f}^{(2)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\biggr\|_{L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}-\|Z\|_{\psi}\biggr|\to 0\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

For
k=1,
similar results hold for the plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

In principle, to estimate the value
f(\Sigma)
of a functional
f
at unknown covariance
\Sigma
it should be enough for the functional to be smooth
locally
in a neighborhood of
\Sigma

rather than on the whole space
L(E^{\ast},E).
We will now state such local versions of the results discussed above.
Moreover, we will show that these local bounds are minimax optimal.
Note that if
f
is Lipschitz in a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma,
then, by McShane-Whitney extension theorem, it could be extended to the whole space
with preservation of its Lipschitz constant (if
f
is, in addition, bounded in
U
by a constant, its Lipschitz extension could be chosen to be bounded by the same constant).
Thus, without loss of generality, we will assume in what follows that
f
is Lipschitz in
L(E^{\ast},E)
(and, if needed, also bounded),
but its higher order smoothness holds only locally.

We will start with a local version of Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.
N(0,\Sigma)
with
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Let
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be Lipschitz and, for some
k\geq 2,
let it be
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable in an open ball
U=B(\Sigma,\delta)

of radius
\delta>0
with the
k
-th derivative satisfying the Hölder condition with exponent
\rho\in(0,1]
in this ball.
Suppose also that
\delta\leq\|\Sigma\|
and, for a sufficiently large constant
C\geq 1,

\delta\geq C\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}.

Then, for
i=1,2
and for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho}\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr)+\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(U)}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}+\max_{2\leq j\leq k}\|f^{(j)}(\Sigma)\|\Bigl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{j}\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{cn\delta^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}\Bigr\}

(2.2)

with some constant
c>0.

If
k=1,
a similar bound holds for the plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}):

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim_{\rho}\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr)+\|f^{(1)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(U)}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{1+\rho}.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that, under the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
, the last term in the righthand side of bound (
2.5
) could be further bounded in several useful
ways. In particular,

\displaystyle\max_{2\leq j\leq k}\|f^{(j)}(\Sigma)\|\Bigl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{j}\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{cn\delta^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}\Bigr\}\leq\max_{2\leq j\leq k}\|\Sigma\|^{j}\|f^{(j)}(\Sigma)\|n^{-1/2}\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{cn\delta^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}\Bigr\}

and

\displaystyle\max_{2\leq j\leq k}\|f^{(j)}(\Sigma)\|\Bigl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{j}\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{cn\delta^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}\Bigr\}\lesssim_{k}\max_{2\leq j\leq k}\|f^{(j)}(\Sigma)\|\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{n}.

We will also prove the following local version of Theorem
2.4
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.
N(0,\Sigma)
with
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Let
f
be a
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable functional in the ball
U=B(\Sigma,\delta)

for some
\delta>0
and
k\geq 2
with

\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{k-1+\rho}(U)}<\infty
for some
\rho\in(0,1].
Suppose also that
f
is extended to a Lipschitz
functional on
L(E^{\ast},E)
with preservation of its Lipschitz constant
\|f\|_{\rm Lip}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}(U)}.

Finally, suppose that
\delta\leq\|\Sigma\|\wedge 1
and, for a sufficiently large constant
C\geq 1,

\delta\geq C\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}.

Then, for all
p\geq 1
and for all
\gamma\in(0,1],

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho,\gamma}\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{k-1+\rho}(U)}\biggl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1+\gamma}}{\delta^{\gamma}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\gamma}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\gamma)/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\gamma}\biggr)+\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}\biggr).

In particular, this implies that, for all
\beta\in[1/2,1),

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{\beta}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho,\beta}\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{k-1+\rho}(U)}\biggl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1/\beta}}{\delta^{1/\beta-1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{1/\beta-1}+\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\biggr)^{k+\rho}\biggr).

We will now state a simple corollary of Theorem
2.5
, providing a uniform version of the bound of this theorem in a set of covariance operators with bounded effective rank.
Namely, for
a>0
and
r\geq 1,
let
{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)
be the set of covariance operators
\Sigma:E^{\ast}\mapsto E

with
\|\Sigma\|\leq a
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq r.
Recall the definition of
C^{s,a}
-norms from Section
1.2
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
a>0,r\geq 1
and
\Sigma_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r).

Suppose also that, for a sufficiently large constant
C\geq 1,

\displaystyle Ca\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}<\delta\leq a\wedge 1.

(2.3)

Let
U:=B(\Sigma_{0},2\delta)
and let
s=k+\rho
for some
k\geq 2
and
\rho\in(0,1].

Then, for
i=1,2
and for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim_{s}\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\biggr)+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\biggr)^{s}\biggr)\wedge 1.

(2.4)

For
k=1,
a similar bound holds for the plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

For
p=2,
the bound simplifies as follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim_{s}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\biggr)^{s}\biggr)

(2.5)

(since, for
p=2
and under condition (
2.3
), both terms in the sum in the right hand side of (
2.4
) are bounded from above by a constant, there is no need to take the minimum with
1
).

Bounds (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) provide the size of the
maximal risk
of estimators
T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
in the class of covariance operators

{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap\{\Sigma:\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta\}
and
in the class of
s
-smooth functionals
\{f:\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1\}.

In the case of separable Hilbert space
E={\mathbb{H}},
it is possible to show that bound (
2.5
) is minimax optimal
locally around
spiked covariance operators

\Sigma_{0},
implying the optimal dependence of the bound on the sample size
n,
the effective rank
r
and the smoothness parameter
s.

A covariance operator
\Sigma_{0}
will be called a spiked covariance
of rank
d
if
\sigma(\Sigma):=\{\lambda,\mu,0\}
with
\lambda>\mu>0
and with eigenvalue
\lambda
being of multiplicity
1
and eigenvalue
\mu

being of multiplicity
d-1.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)
be a spiked covariance operator of rank
[r]
with nonzero eigenvalues
\lambda=\gamma_{1}a
and
\mu=\gamma_{2}a,

where
0<\gamma_{2}<\gamma_{1}<1
are numerical constants.
Let
\kappa:=\gamma_{2}\wedge(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})\wedge(1-\gamma_{1})
and
suppose that
\delta
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle c_{1}\gamma_{1}a\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}<\delta\leq c_{2}\kappa a\wedge 1

with sufficiently large
c_{1}\geq 1
and sufficiently small
c_{2}>0.

Let
U:=B(\Sigma_{0},2\delta)
and let
s>0.

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1}\inf_{T}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\Bigl\|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\gtrsim\biggl(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}\vee\gamma_{1}^{s/2}\gamma_{2}^{s/2}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\biggr)^{s}\biggr),

where the infimum is taken over all the estimators
T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

Finally, we will state another local minimax lower bound that, along with the results of corollaries
2.5
and
2.6
, imply the asymptotic efficiency
of estimator
T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of functional
f(\Sigma).
A similar result has been already stated in
[
21
]
and its proof relies on van Trees inequality (see
[
18
,
12
]
for similar arguments).
As in the case of Theorem
2.7
, the result will be stated in the case when
E={\mathbb{H}}
is a separable Hilbert space.
In this case, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\sigma_{f}^{2}(\Sigma)=2\|\Sigma^{1/2}f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
a>1,r\geq 1
and let
\Sigma_{0}
be a spiked covariance operator of rank
[r]
with its non-zero eigenvalues
belonging to an interval
[a^{-1}+\bar{\delta},a-\bar{\delta}]
for some
\bar{\delta}>0.

Let
U:=B(\Sigma_{0},2\bar{\delta})
and suppose
f\in C^{1}(U).

Let

\displaystyle\omega_{f^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{0},\delta):=\sup_{\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)-f^{\prime}(\Sigma_{0})\|,\delta\leq\bar{\delta}

be a local continuity modulus of
f^{\prime}
at
\Sigma_{0}.
For all
\beta>2,
there exists a constant
D_{\beta}>0
such that, for all
\delta<\bar{\delta},

\displaystyle\inf_{T_{n}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|T_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\geq 1-D_{\beta}\biggl[\frac{a\omega_{f^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{0},\delta)}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma_{0})}+a^{\beta}\delta+\frac{a^{2}}{\delta^{2}n}\biggr],

where the infimum is taken over all the estimators
T_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

Remark 2.4
.

This bound could be viewed as a non-asymptotic version of Hàjek-LeCam local asymptotic minimax theorem. If
\sigma_{f}(\Sigma_{0})
is bounded away from zero and
\omega_{f^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{0},\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0,

it implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{c\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{T_{n}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|T_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}}{\sigma_{f}(\Sigma)}\geq 1.

It is easy to deduce from Corollary
2.5
that this asymptotic minimax lower bound is attained for estimator

T^{(2)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
(or, for
s\leq 2,

for the plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
) in the class of covariances

{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)
for a
C^{s}
-smooth functional
f
under the assumptions that
r\leq n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
s>\frac{1}{1-\alpha}.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space and let
\Sigma_{0}
be a covariance operator with eigenvalues
\|\Sigma_{0}\|=\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{l}>\lambda_{l+1}\geq\lambda_{l+2}\geq\dots.

Let
g_{l}:=\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l+1}
be the spectral gap of the top eigenvalue
\|\Sigma_{0}\|
of
\Sigma_{0}.
Let
U:=B(\Sigma_{0},\delta)
for
\delta<g_{l}/8.
For
\Sigma\in U,
the orthogonal projection
P(\Sigma)
onto the subspace
generated by the eigenvectors corresponding to the first
l
eigenvalues of
\Sigma
is well defined and, moreover,
P(\Sigma)
is a
C^{\infty}
-function in
U
with
\|P^{(k)}\|_{L_{\infty}(U)}\lesssim_{k}g_{l}^{-k},k=0,1,2,\dots
(see Lemma
5.8
and Remark
5.1
). For a given nuclear operator
B
with nuclear norm
\|B\|_{1}\leq 1,

define
f(\Sigma):=\langle P(\Sigma),B\rangle.
The goal is to estimate
f(\Sigma)
based on i.i.d. observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\sim N(0,\Sigma).
The problems of this nature
are of importance in high-dimensional principal component analysis, where it is of interest to estimate bilinear forms
\langle P(\Sigma)u,v\rangle
(in particular, the matrix entries in some basis) of spectral projections of covariance operator. When
l=1
(that is, when the top eigenvalue of
\Sigma
is simple), the problem can be phrased as estimation of linear functionals of principal components and rather specialized bias reduction method for such functionals was developed and studied in
[
15
,
18
]
. This method yields
asymptotically efficient estimators in classes of covariances with
{\bf r}(\Sigma)=o(n),
but it is not known how to extend this approach to the case of spectral projections corresponding to multiple
eigenvalues. Since
f(\Sigma)
is a
C^{\infty}
-functional locally in the neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma_{0},
one can try to use estimators
T_{f,k}^{(i)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}),i=1,2
for an arbitrary
number
k\geq 2
of plug-in estimators in the linear combination. Suppose
\Sigma\in U
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq r
for some
r\geq 1.

Denote
\gamma:=\frac{\|\Sigma_{0}\|}{g_{l}}.
Suppose also that, for a large enough constant
C>0,

C\gamma\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\leq 1.

Using the bounds of Lemma
5.8
and Remark
5.1
, it is not hard to deduce from Theorem
2.5
with
s=k+1

the following bound that holds for all
k\geq 2,
for
i=1,2
and for
p\geq 1:

\displaystyle\Bigl\|T^{(i)}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\lesssim_{k}\gamma\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\gamma\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\Bigr)^{k+1}.

Further development of this approach (that might include, for instance, an adaptive choice of
k
to achieve asymptotic efficiency) is beyond the scope of the current paper.

3
Bias reduction via linear aggregation of plug-in estimators and jackknife method

In this section, we summarize several facts (many of them known) concerning an approach to bias reduction based on linear aggregation of plug-in estimators with different sample sizes and, in particular, a jackknife method
of bias reduction in the problem of estimation of a smooth functional
f({\mathbb{E}}Y)
of unknown mean
{\mathbb{E}}Y
of a random variable
Y
in a Banach space
F
based on i.i.d. observations
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
of
Y.

As in Section
1
, let
k\geq 1
and
n/c\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{k}\leq n
for some
c>1.

Let
\bar{Y}_{n}:=\frac{Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{n}}{n}
and define

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}),

where
\{C_{j}:1\leq j\leq k\}
are defined by (
2.1
) and Assumption
1
holds.
With these definitions, it is known (see
[
11
]
) that the following proposition holds:

Proposition 3.1
.

The coefficients
C_{j},j=1,\dots,k
satisfy the following properties:

(i)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}=1;

(ii)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{C_{j}}{n_{j}^{l}}=0
for
l=1,\dots,k-1.

The next proposition provides a bound on the bias of estimator
\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be
k
times continuously differentiable for some
k\geq 2
with
f^{(k)}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)
for some
\rho\in(0,1].

Let
s:=k+\rho.

Then

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y)|\lesssim_{s}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}\max_{1\leq j\leq k}{\mathbb{E}}\|\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}-{\mathbb{E}}Y\|^{s}.

A couple of simple bounds on the remainder of Taylor expansion will be used in the proof of Proposition
3.2
and throughout the rest of the paper
(similar facts were used in
[
12
,
20
]
and they can be easily derived from standard formulas for the remainder of Taylor expansion, see, e.g.,
[
5
]
, Chapter 1, Section 5.6).
For a Fréchet differentiable functional
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},
denote by

\displaystyle S_{f}(x;h):=f(x+h)-f(x)-\langle h,f^{\prime}(x)\rangle,x,h\in F

the remainder of the first order Taylor expansion of
f
at point
x\in F.

Proposition 3.3
.

For a Fréchet continuously differentiable functional
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
and for all
x,h,h^{\prime}\in F,

\displaystyle|S_{f}(x,h)|\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}\|h\|^{1+\rho}

and

\displaystyle|S_{f}(x,h)-S_{f}(x,h^{\prime})|\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}(\|h\|^{\rho}\vee\|h^{\prime}\|^{\rho})\|h-h^{\prime}\|.

The same bounds hold if
f
is Fréchet continuously differentiable in a ball
B(x,\delta):=\{y:\|y-x\|<\delta\}

and
\|h\|,\|h^{\prime}\|<\delta.

Similarly, for a
k
times Fréchet differentiable functional
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},

denote by

\displaystyle S_{f}^{(k)}(x;h):=f(x+h)-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{f^{(j)}(x)[h,\overset{j}{\dots},h]}{j!},x,h\in F

the remainder of the
k
-th order Taylor expansion of
f.

Proposition 3.4
.

For a
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable functional
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
and for all
x,h\in F,

\displaystyle|S_{f}^{(k)}(x,h)|\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}}{k!}\|h\|^{k+\rho}.

The same bound holds if
f
is
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable in a ball
B(x,\delta)

and
\|h\|<\delta.

The proof of Proposition
3.2
is based on the following simple lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F
be a Banach space and
let
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be
k
times Fréchet continuously differentiable for some
k\geq 2
with
f^{(k)}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)
for some
\rho\in(0,1].

Let
s:=k+\rho.

Let
Y,Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
be i.i.d. r.v. in
F
with
distribution
P
and let
\bar{Y}=\bar{Y}_{n}:=\frac{Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{n}}{n}.
Suppose
{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|^{s}<\infty.
Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(\bar{Y})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y)=\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\frac{\beta_{l,k}(P)}{n^{l}}+R,

where the coefficients
\beta_{l,k}(P)
depend on
P
and
f
(but not on
n
) and

\displaystyle|R|\lesssim_{s}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}{\mathbb{E}}\|\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y\|^{s}.

proof.

Let
M
be a bounded symmetric
k
-linear form on a Banach space
F
and let
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. r.v. in
F
with
{\mathbb{E}}\xi=0,

{\mathbb{E}}\|\xi\|^{k}<\infty.

Let
\bar{\xi}:=\frac{\xi_{1}+\dots+\xi_{n}}{n}.
Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}M[\bar{\xi},\overset{k}{\dots},\bar{\xi}]=n^{-k}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq n}{\mathbb{E}}M[\xi_{i_{1}},\dots,\xi_{i_{k}}].

Suppose there are
m
distinct indices
1\leq l_{1}<\dots<l_{m}\leq n
among
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
repeated with
multiplicities
k_{1},\dots,k_{m},
where
1\leq m\leq n,

k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\geq 1,

k_{1}+\dots+k_{m}=k.

Note that, if
k_{j}=1
for some
j=1,\dots,m,
then
{\mathbb{E}}M[\xi_{i_{1}},\dots,\xi_{i_{k}}]=0.

Otherwise,
k_{1}\geq 2,\dots,k_{m}\geq 2
and we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}M[\xi_{i_{1}},\dots,\xi_{i_{k}}]={\mathbb{E}}M[\xi_{1},\overset{k_{1}}{\dots}\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m},\overset{k_{m}}{\dots}\xi_{m}]=:b_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}}.

Denote

\displaystyle B_{m}:=\sum_{k_{1}+\dots+k_{m}=k,k_{j}\geq 2,j=1,\dots,m}b_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}}.

Clearly, if
k_{j}\geq 2,j=1,\dots,m,
then
m\leq k/2.
Since there are
{n\choose m}
choices of
l_{1},\dots,l_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}M[\bar{\xi},\overset{k}{\dots},\bar{\xi}]=\sum_{1\leq m\leq k/2}\frac{{n\choose m}}{n^{k}}B_{m}.

Writing
m!{n\choose m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{m,i}n^{i},
we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}M[\bar{\xi},\overset{k}{\dots},\bar{\xi}]=\sum_{1\leq m\leq k/2}\frac{B_{m}}{m!}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{m,i}n^{-(k-i)}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k/2}d_{i,k}n^{-(k-i)},

where

d_{i,k}=d_{i,k}(M,\xi):=\sum_{i\leq m\leq k/2}c_{m,i}\frac{B_{m}}{m!}

depends only on the multilinear form
M
and the distribution of r.v.
\xi
(but not on
n
).

By the Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle f(x+h)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{f^{(j)}(x)[h,\dots,h]}{j!}+S_{f}^{(k)}(x;h)

with the following bound on the remainder (see Proposition
3.4
):

\displaystyle|S_{f}^{(k)}(x;h)|\lesssim_{s}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}\|h\|^{s}.

Since
{\mathbb{E}}f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)[\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y]=0,
this implies that, for all
k\geq 2,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(\bar{Y})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y)=\sum_{j=2}^{k}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}f^{(j)}({\mathbb{E}}Y)[\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,\dots,\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y]}{j!}+{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)

with

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)|\lesssim_{s}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}{\mathbb{E}}\|\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y\|^{s}.

Let
\xi=Y-{\mathbb{E}}Y,\ \xi_{j}:=Y_{j}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,j=1,\dots,n.
Then
\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y=\bar{\xi}
and, for all
k\geq 2,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(\bar{Y})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{k}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}f^{(j)}({\mathbb{E}}Y)[\bar{\xi},\dots,\bar{\xi}]}{j!}+{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{1\leq i\leq j/2}\tilde{d}_{i,j}n^{-(j-i)}+{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y),

where
\tilde{d}_{i,j}:=d_{i,j}(f^{(j)}({\mathbb{E}Y}),\xi).
Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(\bar{Y})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y)=\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\frac{\beta_{l,k}(P)}{n^{l}}+{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y),

where the coefficients

\displaystyle\beta_{l,k}(P):=\sum_{(i,j)\in{\mathcal{J}}_{l,k}}\tilde{d}_{i,j},\ {\mathcal{J}}_{l,k}:=\{(i,j):2\leq j\leq k,1\leq i\leq j/2,j-i=l\}

depend on the distribution
P
of
Y
(but not on
n
) and

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)|\lesssim_{s}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}{\mathbb{E}}\|\bar{Y}-{\mathbb{E}}Y\|^{s}.

We will now prove Proposition
3.2
.

proof.

Using Proposition
3.1
(i) and (ii), we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}({\mathbb{E}}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\frac{\beta_{l,k}(P)}{n_{j}^{l}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\beta_{l,k}(P)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{C_{j}}{n_{j}^{l}}+R=R,

and, by Assumption
1
,

\displaystyle|R|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}|C_{j}|\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}^{(k)}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)|\lesssim_{s}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}\max_{1\leq j\leq k}{\mathbb{E}}\|\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}-{\mathbb{E}}Y\|^{s}.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\rm sym}}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all symmetric functions
\psi(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})

of r.v.
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
and define

\displaystyle\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}):={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl(\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})\Bigl|{\mathcal{F}}_{{\rm sym}}\Bigr).

Clearly,
{\mathbb{E}}\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})={\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}),
implying that the biases of these two estimators
are the same, and the bound of Proposition
3.2
holds for estimator
\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}),
too:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be
k
times continuously differentiable for some
k\geq 2
with
f^{(k)}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)
for some
\rho\in(0,1]
and let
s=k+\rho.

Then

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-f({\mathbb{E}}Y)|\lesssim_{s}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(F)}\max_{1\leq j\leq k}{\mathbb{E}}\|\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}-{\mathbb{E}}Y\|^{s}.

It is easy to see that estimator
\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
can be represented as a linear combination of
U
-statistics.
Indeed, recall that, if
h:F\times\dots\times F\mapsto F_{1}
is a symmetric function of
m\leq n
variables in a Banach space
F
with values in a Banach space
F_{1},
then

\displaystyle U_{n}h=(U_{n}h)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}):=\frac{1}{{n\choose m}}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{m}\leq n}h(Y_{j_{1}},\dots,Y_{j_{m}})

is the
U
-statistic of order
m
with kernel
h.

Then, for all
1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{m}\leq n,

\displaystyle(U_{n}h)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})={\mathbb{E}}(h(Y_{j_{1}},\dots,Y_{j_{m}})|{\mathcal{F}}_{{\rm sym}}).

Let now
h_{j}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n_{j}}):=f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}).
Then

\displaystyle\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}{\mathbb{E}}(h_{j}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n_{j}})|{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm sym})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}(U_{n}h_{j})(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}).

(3.1)

Remark 3.1
.

Note that to compute estimator
\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}),
one needs to compute the value of
f
for
\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}{n\choose n_{j}}
sample means.
Since we choose
n_{j}\asymp n,j=1,\dots,k,
this number would grow exponentially with
n.
To overcome this difficulty, one can use a Monte Carlo approximation of
the
U
-statistics
U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})
involved in the formula for
\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}).
To preserve the convergence rates (and asymptotic efficiency properties)
of the estimator, it is enough to approximate it with the accuracy
o(n^{-1/2}).
To this end, for each
j=1,\dots,k,
one can sample independently at random
N
subsets
of cardinality
n_{j}
of the sample
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
and approximate
U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})
by the average value of
f
on the sample means for these
N
subsets.
If
n=o(N),
the approximation error would be of the order
O(N^{-1/2})=o(n^{-1/2})
and this method would require computing the value of
f
just for
kN
sample means.

We will need a couple of simple (and mostly known) facts. We give their proofs for completeness.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}):=\bar{Y}_{m}.
Then, for all
m\leq n,

\displaystyle U_{n}\bar{Y}_{m}=\bar{Y}_{n}.

proof.

Indeed,

U_{n}\bar{Y}_{m}=m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}U_{n}Y_{j}=\frac{m\bar{Y}_{n}}{m}=\bar{Y}_{n}.

Let
h:F\times\dots\times F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a symmetric function of
m
variables.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
p\geq 1
and assume that
{\mathbb{E}}|h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})|^{p}<\infty.
Then

\displaystyle\|(U_{n}h)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})\|_{L_{p}}\leq\|h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})-{\mathbb{E}}h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})\|_{L_{p}}.

proof.

By Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl|(U_{n}h)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})\Bigr|^{p}={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl|{\mathbb{E}}((h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})-{\mathbb{E}}h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}))|{\mathcal{F}}_{{\rm sym}})\Bigr|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl(\Bigl|h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})-{\mathbb{E}}h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})\Bigr|^{p}|{\mathcal{F}}_{{\rm sym}}\Bigr)={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl|h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})-{\mathbb{E}}h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})\Bigr|^{p},

implying the claim.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuously differentiable functional.
Let
p\geq 1
and suppose that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)|^{p}<\infty.

Then

\displaystyle\Bigl\|U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{m})-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{m})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\leq\Bigl\|S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}.

.

proof.

Let
h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}):=f(\bar{Y}_{m}).

Then we have

\displaystyle h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})-{\mathbb{E}}h(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle+S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)

and

\displaystyle(U_{n}h)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}(U_{n}h)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})

\displaystyle=\langle U_{n}\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle+U_{n}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle+U_{n}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y).

Therefore, by Proposition
3.7
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{m})-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{m})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|U_{n}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{m}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}.

In view of Assumption
1
, we easily get the next statements.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
p\geq 1
and suppose that
{\mathbb{E}}|f(\bar{Y}_{n})|^{p}<\infty,n\geq 1.
Then

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\|f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})\|_{L_{p}}

and

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\|f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})\|_{L_{p}}

proof.

The claims immediately follow from Assumption
1
and Proposition
3.7
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
f:F\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuously differentiable functional. Let
p\geq 1
and suppose that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|S_{f}({\mathbb{E}},\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}})|^{p}<\infty,n\geq 1.

Then

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\Bigl\|S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}({\mathbb{E}}Y;\bar{Y}_{n_{j}}-{\mathbb{E}}Y)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}.

proof.

Indeed, since
\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}=1
and in view of (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}\Bigl(U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}})\rangle\Bigr).

Thus, by Proposition
3.8
and Assumption
1
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\check{T}_{f}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}Y)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{j}\Bigl(U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}})\rangle\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}|C_{j}|\Bigl\|U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-{\mathbb{E}}U_{n}f(\bar{Y}_{n_{j}})-\langle\bar{Y}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}Y,f^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}})\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

4
Concentration of smooth functionals of sample covariance operator

In this section, we derive several concentration bounds for functionals of sample covariance
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}
playing a basic role in the proofs of the main results.
In particular, we need the bounds on the
L_{p}
-norms
\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{L_{p}}
for all
p\geq 1

that imply concentration
with exponential tails of
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
around its expectation. We also need similar concentration bounds for the remainder of Taylor expansion

S_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)
for a smooth functional
f.
Some concentration inequalities of this type have been previously obtained in
[
12
,
21
]
in the case of sample covariances in Hilbert spaces. Here we extend these results to general Banach spaces and, in fact, we also simplify their proofs. Moreover, the inequalities we obtain easily imply concentration inequalities
for
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|
initially proved in
[
16
]
(see also
[
1
]
for another proof). General results on concentration of smooth functions of r.v.
satisfying Sobolev type inequalities are presented in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:L(E^{\ast},E)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a Lipschitz functional. Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr).

proof.

It is well known that a centered Gaussian r.v.
X
in a separable Banach space can be represented by the following random series:

\displaystyle X=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j}x_{j},

(4.1)

where
\{Z_{j}:j\geq 1\}
are i.i.d. standard normal r.v. and
\{x_{j}:j\geq 1\}
are vectors in
E
such that the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j}x_{j}
converges in
E
a.s.
and
\sum_{j\geq 1}\|x_{j}\|^{2}<\infty
(see, e.g.,
[
23
]
).

Let
X^{(N)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_{j}x_{j}.

Denote by
\Sigma^{(N)}
the covariance operator of
X^{(N)}.

Since
\|X-X^{(N)}\|\to 0
as
N\to\infty
a.s., it easily follows from the Gaussian concentration that, for all
p\geq 1,

{\mathbb{E}}\|X-X^{(N)}\|^{p}\to 0
as
N\to\infty.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

The sequence
\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|,N\geq 1
is nondecreasing and
\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|\to\|\Sigma\|
as
N\to\infty.

(ii)

For all
p\geq 1,

the sequence
{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{p},N\geq 1
is nondecreasing and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{p}\to{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{p}\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

(iii)

As a consequence,

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})\to{\bf r}(\Sigma)\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|=\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle x_{j},u\rangle^{2},

which is a nondecreasing sequence w.r.t.
N.

Moreover,

\displaystyle|\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|-\|\Sigma\||\leq\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}\langle x_{j},u\rangle^{2}\leq\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}\|x_{j}\|^{2}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

By Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{p}={\mathbb{E}}\biggl\|{\mathbb{E}}_{Z_{N+1}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_{j}x_{j}+Z_{N+1}x_{N+1}\Bigr)\biggr\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}{\mathbb{E}}_{Z_{N+1}}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_{j}x_{j}+Z_{N+1}x_{N+1}\biggr\|^{p}={\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N+1)}\|^{p}

implying that the sequence
{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{2},N\geq 1
is nondecreasing.
A similar argument shows that
{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{p}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{p},N\geq 1.

Since
\|X^{(N)}\|^{p}\to\|X\|^{p}
as
N\to\infty
a.s. and, for all
p^{\prime}>p,

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{p^{\prime}}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{p^{\prime}}<\infty,

we can conclude that
{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{p}\to{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{p}
as
N\to\infty.

The last claim of the lemma easily follows from the definition of
{\bf r}(\Sigma).

For
X_{k},k\geq 1,
we have

\displaystyle X_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j,k}x_{j},

where
Z_{j,k},j\geq 1,k=1,\dots,n
are i.i.d. standard normal r.v. and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j,k}x_{j}
converges in
E
a.s. for all
k=1,\dots,n.

Let

\displaystyle X_{k}^{(N)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_{j,k}x_{j},k=1,\dots,n,N\geq 1.

Denote by
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}
the sample covariance based on
X_{1}^{(N)},\dots,X_{n}^{(N)}.

Since the mapping
E\ni x\mapsto x\otimes x\in L(E^{\ast},L)
is continuous, we easily get that

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}=n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}^{(N)}\otimes X_{k}^{(N)}\to\hat{\Sigma}_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}\otimes X_{k}\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty\ {\rm a.s.}

in the space
L(E^{\ast},E)
(equipped with the operator norm).
Note that, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{p}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\biggl(n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|X_{k}\|^{2}\biggr)^{p}\leq n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\|X_{k}\|^{2p}={\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2p}<\infty

and, in view of Lemma
4.1
(ii), we also get

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{p}\leq\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{2p}={\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2p}<\infty.

Since we have
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{p}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
a.s. and, for all
p^{\prime}>p,

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}\lesssim_{p^{\prime}}{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2p^{\prime}}<\infty,

we can conclude that, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{p}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

Since
f
is Lipschitz, this implies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})|^{p}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty

and

\displaystyle\Bigl\|(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}))-{\mathbb{E}}(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}))\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

(4.2)

Thus, to obtain a bound on
\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{L_{p}},
it is now enough to bound

\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})\|_{L_{p}}

and pass to the limit as
N\to\infty.
To this end, recall that
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}=\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})
depends on
{\mathcal{Z}}:=(Z_{j,k})_{j=1,\dots N;k=1,\dots n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}.
Our main tool is the following well known form of Gaussian concentration inequalities (it could be proved, for instance, using Maurey-Pisier argument, see, e.g.,
[
7
]
, Theorem 2.1.7).
For a locally Lipschitz function
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},
denote by

\displaystyle(Lg)(z):=\inf_{U\ni z}\sup_{z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}\in U}\frac{|f(z^{\prime})-f(z^{\prime\prime})|}{\|z^{\prime}-z^{\prime\prime}\|_{\ell_{2}}}

the local Lipschitz constant of
g
at point
z
(with the infimum taken over all neighborhoods
U
of point
z
).
Note that this definition could be extended to local Lipschitz functions from a metric space into another metric space.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}\sim N(0,I_{m})
be a standard normal r.v. in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and let
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally Lipschitz
function. Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|g({\mathcal{Z}})-{\mathbb{E}}g({\mathcal{Z}})\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\|(Lg)({\mathcal{Z}})\|_{L_{p}}.

We will apply the bound of Proposition
4.1
to

g({\mathcal{Z}}):=f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})).

For
{\mathcal{Z}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}=(\tilde{Z}_{j,k})_{j=1,\dots N;k=1,\dots n},

\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}=\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})
and
\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}:=\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}(\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}),

we then have

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|=\sup_{\|u\|,\|v\|\leq 1}\biggl|n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle X_{k}^{(N)},u\rangle\langle X_{k}^{(N)},v\rangle-n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},u\rangle\langle\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},v\rangle\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|u\|,\|v\|\leq 1}\biggl|n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle X_{k}^{(N)}-\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},u\rangle\langle X_{k}^{(N)},v\rangle\biggr|+\sup_{\|u\|,\|v\|\leq 1}\biggl|n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},u\rangle\langle X_{k}^{(N)}-\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},v\rangle\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}\biggl(n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle X_{k}^{(N)}-\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},u\rangle^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggl[\sup_{\|v\|\leq 1}\biggl(n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle X_{k}^{(N)},v\rangle^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}+\sup_{\|v\|\leq 1}\biggl(n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},v\rangle^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2}+\|\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2})\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}\biggl(n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle X_{k}^{(N)}-\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},u\rangle^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\langle X_{k}^{(N)}-\tilde{X}_{k}^{(N)},u\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}(Z_{j,k}-\tilde{Z}_{j,k})\langle x_{j},u\rangle\biggr)^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}(Z_{j,k}-\tilde{Z}_{j,k})^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle x_{j},u\rangle^{2}=\langle\Sigma^{(N)}u,u\rangle\sum_{j=1}^{N}(Z_{j,k}-\tilde{Z}_{j,k})^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|\leq(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2}+\|\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2})\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\biggl(n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(Z_{j,k}-\tilde{Z}_{j,k})^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2}+\|\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2})\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|{\mathcal{Z}}-\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}\|_{\ell_{2}}.

(4.3)

The last inequality implies the following bound on the local Lipschitz constant of the mapping
{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}\ni{\mathcal{Z}}\mapsto\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}}):

\displaystyle(L\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})({\mathcal{Z}})\leq\frac{2\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}.

(4.4)

It immediately implies the bound on the local Lipschitz constant
of function
{\mathcal{Z}}\mapsto f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})):

\displaystyle(Lf(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}))({\mathcal{Z}})\leq 2\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}.

By Proposition
4.1
, we get, that for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}.

(4.5)

Note also that

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}-\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}(\tilde{\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\Bigl\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}(\tilde{\mathcal{Z}})\Bigr\|}{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}+\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}(\tilde{\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}},

which together with (
4
) implies that
{\mathcal{Z}}\mapsto\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}

is a Lipschitz function with constant
\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.
Again, by Proposition
4.1
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

(4.6)

Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) yields the bound

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2}+p\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|}{n}.

Note also that, by Lemma
4.1
,
\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|\leq\|\Sigma\|
and
{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{2}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}.

Therefore, using the bound of Theorem
1.1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\leq{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|\leq\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}+{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|.

\displaystyle\leq\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}+\biggl(\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{2}}{n}}\bigvee\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X^{(N)}\|^{2}}{n}\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}+C\biggl(\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}{n}}\bigvee\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}{n}\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\biggl(1+C\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggr)\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr),

and we get the following bound:

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr).

Passing to the limit as
N\to\infty
and using (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr).

Theorem
4.1
immediately implies the following concentration bound.

Corollary 4.1
.

For all
t\geq 1
with probability at least
1-e^{-t}

\displaystyle|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})|\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}+\frac{t}{n}\biggr).

proof.

Indeed, by Markov inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl\{|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})|\geq e\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{L_{t}}\Bigr\}\leq\frac{\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{L_{t}}^{t}}{e^{t}\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{L_{t}}^{t}}=e^{-t},

implying the claim.

The next corollary is also obvious.

Corollary 4.2
.

The following bound holds:

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{\psi_{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr).

Note also that the functional
L(E^{\ast};E)\ni B\mapsto\|B-\Sigma\|
is Lipschits with constant
1.
Applying the above statements
to this function, we get the following results initially proved in a more complicated way in
[
16
]
(see also theorems
1.1
and
1.2
).

Proposition 4.2
.

The following bounds hold:

(i)

for all
p\geq 1

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr);

(ii)

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr\|_{\psi_{1}}\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr);

(iii)

for all
t\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr|\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}+\frac{t}{n}\biggr).

We will also need concentration bounds on the remainder of the first order Taylor expansion

\displaystyle S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)=f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle

for a functional
f
with
\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}<\infty
for some
\rho\in(0,1].

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
f^{\prime}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(L(E^{*},E))
and that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.
Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\rho)/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\rho}\biggr).

In particular, it implies that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{\psi_{1/(1+\rho)}}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}.

proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, it is enough
to obtain the concentration bound for
S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma)
and then pass to the limit as
N\to\infty.

First note that, by the second bound of Proposition
3.3
, the local Lipschitz constant of the mapping

H\mapsto S_{f}(\Sigma;H)
is bounded from above as follows:

\displaystyle(LS_{f}(\Sigma;\cdot))(H)\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|H\|^{\rho}.

Together with (
4.4
), this implies the following bound on the local Lipschitz constant of

{\mathcal{Z}}\mapsto S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})-\Sigma):

\displaystyle(LS_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}(\cdot)-\Sigma))({\mathcal{Z}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})-\Sigma\|^{\rho}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})-\Sigma\|^{\rho}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})-\Sigma\|^{\rho+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})-\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\Sigma^{(N)}-\Sigma\|^{\rho}

\displaystyle+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}({\mathcal{Z}})-\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho+1/2}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\Sigma^{(N)}-\Sigma\|^{\rho+1/2}.

(4.7)

By Proposition
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\Bigl\|S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{p}\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\biggl(\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}+\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\Sigma^{(N)}-\Sigma\|^{\rho}

\displaystyle+\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho+1/2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}+\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\Sigma^{(N)}-\Sigma\|^{\rho+1/2}\biggr)

By Proposition
4.2
(i),

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\leq\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|\Bigr)^{\rho}+\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho}+\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho}\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr)^{\rho}.

(4.8)

Since
\rho+1/2<2,
we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho+1/2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\leq\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{2p}}^{\rho+1/2},

and, similarly to (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho+1/2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho+1/2}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho+1/2}+\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{\rho+1/2}\biggl(\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\biggr)^{\rho+1/2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\biggl(p^{1/2}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2+\rho}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho}

\displaystyle+\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2+\rho}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\rho)/2}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)^{\rho}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho+1/2}\biggr)

\displaystyle+p^{1/2}\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1+\rho}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho+1/2}

\displaystyle+\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho/2+3/4}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)^{\rho+1/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\rho}\biggr)

\displaystyle+\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma\|^{\rho}+\frac{\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\Sigma^{(N)}-\Sigma\|^{\rho+1/2}\biggr).

Passing to the limit as
N\to\infty,
we get

\displaystyle\Bigl\|S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\biggl(p^{1/2}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho}+\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\rho)/2}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)^{\rho}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho+1/2}\biggr)

\displaystyle+p^{1/2}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho+1/2}+\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho/2+3/4}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)^{\rho+1/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\rho}\biggr)\biggr).

Assuming that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n,
the above bound simplifies as follows:

\displaystyle\Bigl\|S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}S_{f}(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\biggl(p^{1/2}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}

\displaystyle+\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\rho)/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho+1/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho/2+3/4}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\rho}\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\rho)/2}+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1+\rho}\biggr).

(4.9)

The following corollary is obvious.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose that
f^{\prime}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rho}
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.
Then

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{\psi_{1/(1+\rho)}}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}.

proof.

It immediately follows from the fact that the right hand side of (
4
) is bounded from above by

\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}p^{1+\rho}\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}.

5.1
Upper risk bounds and normal approximation

We start with the proof of theorems
2.1

.

proof.

To prove the first bound of Theorem

, note that, for
\rho\in(0,1]
and

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\leq\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|^{\rho}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\leq\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}^{\rho}.

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\biggl[\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\frac{p}{n}\bigg],

which implies

\displaystyle\Bigl\|f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{\rho}\biggl[\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{\rho}+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)^{\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\Biggr)^{\rho}+\biggl(\frac{p}{n}\biggr)^{\rho}\biggr].

Using the first bound of Proposition
3.3
along with Theorem
1.1

4.2
(i) (for

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f(\Sigma)|\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{1+\rho}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\biggl(\frac{\|\Sigma\|}{\sqrt{n}}\biggr)^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)^{1+\rho}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{1+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{1+\rho},

(5.1)

where we also used the fact that

It remains to combine the last bound with concentration bound of Theorem

2.1
.

To prove Theorem
2.3
, we will apply the results of Section
3
with the space
L(E^{\ast},E)
playing the role of
F
and
Y=X\otimes X,

Y_{j}=X_{j}\otimes X_{j},j=1,\dots,n.

Then
\Sigma={\mathbb{E}}Y

Also,

By Proposition
3.2
, we get the following bounds on the bias of these

i=1,2,

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}T_{f}^{(i)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)|\lesssim_{k,\rho}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\max_{1\leq j\leq k}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}-\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}.

Using the bounds of Theorem
1.1
and Proposition

(i), we get that, for all

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}-\Sigma\|\Bigr\|_{L_{k+\rho}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n_{j}}}+\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n_{j}}+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n_{j}}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{k+\rho}{n_{j}}}+\frac{k+\rho}{n_{j}}\biggr).

Since
n/c\leq n_{j}\leq n,j=1,\dots,k,

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}-\Sigma\|\Bigr\|_{L_{k+\rho}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}+\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}+\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\biggr)\sqrt{\frac{k+\rho}{n}}+\frac{k+\rho}{n}\biggr)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k,\rho}\|\Sigma\|\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr).

Therefore, for
i=1,2,

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}T_{f}^{(i)}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)|\lesssim_{k,\rho}\|f^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}}\|\Sigma\|^{k+\rho}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\rm\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\biggr)^{k+\rho}.

(5.2)

On the other hand, by Proposition
3.9
, for all
p\geq 1

We will use the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

The following bound holds:

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\langle X\otimes X,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr\|_{\psi_{1}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|.

proof.

First note that, in view of (
4.1

\displaystyle\langle X\otimes X,f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle=\biggl\langle\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j}x_{j}\biggr)\otimes\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j}x_{j}\biggr),f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\biggr\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{\infty}Z_{i}Z_{j}\langle x_{i}\otimes x_{j},f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{\infty}a_{ij}Z_{i}Z_{j},

where

\displaystyle a_{ij}:=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\langle x_{i}\otimes x_{j},f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle+\langle x_{j}\otimes x_{i},f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr),i,j\geq 1.

The symmetric matrix
(a_{ij})_{i,j\geq 1}
defines in a standard way an operator
A:\ell_{2}\mapsto\ell_{2},

which is bounded and self-adjoint. Its boundedness easily follows from the fact that it is Hilbert–Schmidt since

\displaystyle\|A\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{ij}^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\langle x_{i}\otimes x_{j},f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle^{2}\leq\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|^{2}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|x_{j}\|^{2}\biggr)^{2}<\infty.

We will prove that, in fact,
A
is even a nuclear operator with
\|A\|_{1}\leq\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|.
To this end, note that, for a self-adjoint
operator
B:\ell_{2}\mapsto\ell_{2}
with
\|B\|\leq 1,
we have

\displaystyle\langle A,B\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{ij}b_{ij}=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\langle x_{i}\otimes x_{j},f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle+\langle x_{j}\otimes x_{i},f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\rangle\Bigr)b_{ij}=\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\geq 1}b_{ij}(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}+x_{j}\otimes x_{i}),f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\biggr\rangle,

which implies

\displaystyle|\langle A,B\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\biggl\|\sum_{i,j\geq 1}b_{ij}(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}+x_{j}\otimes x_{i})\biggr\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\sup_{\|u\|,\|v\|\leq 1}\biggl|\sum_{i,j\geq 1}b_{ij}\langle x_{i},u\rangle\langle x_{j},v\rangle+b_{ij}\langle x_{j},u\rangle\langle x_{i},v\rangle\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\sup_{\|u\|,\|v\|\leq 1}\biggl|\sum_{i,j\geq 1}b_{ij}\langle x_{i},u\rangle\langle x_{j},v\rangle\biggr|\leq\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\|B\|\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}\sum_{j\geq 1}\langle x_{j},u\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\sup_{\|u\|\leq 1}\langle\Sigma u,u\rangle\leq\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|.

By the duality between the nuclear norm and the operator norm, we have

\displaystyle\|A\|_{1}=\sup\Bigl\{|\langle A,B\rangle|:B\ {\rm is\ compact},\ \|B\|\leq 1\Bigr\}\leq\|\Sigma\|\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|.

5.3
Minimax lower bounds: proof of Theorem
2.7

Let
L\subset{\mathbb{H}}
be the linear span of eigenvectors of
\Sigma_{0}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda,\mu
and let
P_{L}
denote the orthogonal projection
onto
L.

Clearly,
{\rm dim}(L)=d=[r].
Let
u\in L,\|u\|=1
be a unit eigenvector of
\Sigma_{0}
corresponding to its top eigenvalue
\lambda
(it is unique up to its sign).
Then

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma_{u,\lambda,\mu}:=\lambda(u\otimes u)+\mu(P_{L}-u\otimes u)=(\lambda-\mu)(u\otimes u)+\mu P_{L}.

Since
d=[r]\leq r
and
\lambda\leq a,
we have
\Sigma_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r).

Moreover, recall that
\lambda=\gamma_{1}a,\mu=\gamma_{2}a
for
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(0,1),\gamma_{1}>\gamma_{2}.
If
\Sigma
is a covariance operator such
{\rm Im}(\Sigma)=L
and

\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta,
where
\delta\leq\kappa a
with

\kappa=(1-\gamma_{1})\wedge(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})\wedge\gamma_{2},

then
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r).

This implies that

\displaystyle\inf_{T}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{T}\sup_{{\rm Im}(\Sigma)=L,\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))^{2}.

(5.28)

Subspace
L
will be fixed throughout the proof. It will be also convenient to fix an orthonormal basis

e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
of
L
and, since
L\ni x\mapsto(\langle x,e_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle x,e_{d}\rangle)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

is an isometry, to identify
L
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and its vectors with their coordinate representations.

We start with the following simple proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
c\gamma_{1}\frac{a}{\sqrt{n}}<\delta\leq\kappa a\wedge 1
for some sufficiently large constant
c>0.
Let
U:=B(\Sigma_{0},2\delta).
Then, for
s\geq 1,

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1}\inf_{T}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\gtrsim\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}.

For
s<1,

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1}\inf_{T}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\gtrsim\gamma_{1}^{s}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{s}.

proof.

Let

\displaystyle f(\Sigma):=\frac{\langle(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0})u,u\rangle}{a}.

Clearly, this is a linear functional of
\Sigma,
so its derivatives of order two and higher are equal to
0.

Moreover, since
\kappa\leq 1/3,
we have

\displaystyle|f(\Sigma)|\leq\frac{\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|}{a}\leq\frac{2\delta}{a}\leq 2\kappa\leq 1,\Sigma\in U.

Finally,
f
is a Lipschitz functional with constant
1/a
on set
U.
Therefore,
\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1.

Let
\Sigma_{1}=\lambda_{1}(u\otimes u)+\mu(P_{L}-u\otimes u)
with
\lambda_{1}:=\gamma_{1}a+c\gamma_{1}\frac{a}{\sqrt{n}}.

Note that, since
c\gamma_{1}\frac{a}{\sqrt{n}}<\delta\leq\kappa a\wedge 1,
we have
\Sigma_{1}\in B(\Sigma_{0},\delta)
and
\Sigma_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r).

Note also that

\displaystyle|f(\Sigma_{1})-f(\Sigma_{0})|=c\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}.

To bound from below the risk of an arbitrary estimator
T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of
f(\Sigma),
we will use “the two hypotheses” method (see
[
33
]
, Theorem 2.1, Theorem 2.2).
Using (
5.3
),
we get

\displaystyle\inf_{T}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{T}\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{0}}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{0}))^{2}\bigvee{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{1}}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{1}))^{2}\Bigr).

(5.29)

Clearly, to minimize the right hand side with respect to
T,
it is enough to consider the estimators
T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
taking values in the interval
[f(\Sigma_{0}),f(\Sigma_{1})].

For such an estimator, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{0}}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{0}))^{2}\bigvee{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{1}}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{0}}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{0}))^{2}I\Bigl(|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{0})|\geq\frac{c\gamma_{1}}{2\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \bigvee{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{1}}(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{1}))^{2}I\Bigl(|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{1})|>\frac{c\gamma_{1}}{2\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c^{2}\gamma_{1}^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{n}\Bigl({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma_{0}}\Bigl\{|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{0})|\geq\frac{c\gamma_{1}}{2\sqrt{n}}\Bigr\}\bigvee{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma_{1}}\Bigl\{|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{1})|>\frac{c\gamma_{1}}{2\sqrt{n}}\Bigr\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{2}\gamma_{1}^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{n}\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{0}}\phi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\bigvee{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{1}}(1-\phi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}))\Bigr),

(5.30)

where

\displaystyle\phi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=I\Bigl(|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma_{0})|\geq\frac{c\gamma_{1}}{2\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)

could be viewed as a test for the hypothesis
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}
against the alternative
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}.

Next, we will control the KL-divergence between Gaussian distributions
N(0,\Sigma_{0})
and

\displaystyle K(N(0,\Sigma_{0})\|N(0,\Sigma_{1}))=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl({\rm tr}(\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\Sigma_{0})-d-\log{\rm det}(\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\Sigma_{0})\Bigr),

where it is assumed that
\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{1}
are operators acting in space

(where they are nonsingular).
We have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\Sigma_{0}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}(u\otimes u)+(P_{L}-u\otimes u),

Hence

\displaystyle K(N(0,\Sigma_{0})\|N(0,\Sigma_{1}))=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}-1-\log\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}-1=\frac{\gamma_{1}a}{\gamma_{1}a+c\gamma_{1}\frac{a}{\sqrt{n}}}-1=\frac{1}{1+\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}-1=-\frac{\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}{1+\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}},

\displaystyle K(N(0,\Sigma_{0})\|N(0,\Sigma_{1}))=-\frac{\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}{1+\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}-\log\biggl(1-\frac{\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}{1+\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\biggr)\asymp\frac{c^{2}}{n},

The proof of the second claim is similar, but the functional

Proposition 5.2

Note also that, under condition (

\displaystyle r\leq\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}\frac{(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2}}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}n\leq\frac{(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2}}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}n,

Recall that

\displaystyle\|t_{\omega}\|^{2}=1+\frac{2\varepsilon\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}}{\sqrt{d}}\langle u,\omega\rangle.

Let

\displaystyle h(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}I(\omega_{j}\neq\omega_{j}^{\prime}),\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}

We will use the following simple lemma (which is a modification of Varshamov-Gilbert bound).

Lemma 5.3
.

There exists a subset

such that

(i)

{\rm card}(B)\geq\frac{e^{d/8}}{2};

(ii)

|\langle u,\omega\rangle|<2,\omega\in B;

(iii)

h(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\geq d/4,\ \omega,\omega^{\prime}\in B,\omega\neq\omega^{\prime}.

Assuming that
{\mathbb{P}}
is a uniform probability distribution on the binary cube
\{-1,1\}^{d},

\langle u,\omega\rangle
is a subgaussian r.v. with parameter
1,
so, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\{\langle u,\omega\rangle\geq t\}\leq e^{-t^{2}/2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle 2^{-d}{\rm card}\{\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{d}:|\langle u,\omega\rangle|\geq t\}\leq 2e^{-t^{2}/2}.

For

we have
2e^{-t^{2}/2}<1/2.
This implies that

\displaystyle{\rm card}\{\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{d}:|\langle u,\omega\rangle|<2\}>2^{d-1}.

Note that the cardinality of any ball of radius
m
with respect to the Hamming distance in the

\displaystyle{d\choose{\leq m}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{d\choose j}.

Let

m

maximal cardinality. Then
\{\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{d}:|\langle u,\omega\rangle|<2\}
is covered by the union of balls of radius

B,
implying that
{\rm card}(B){d\choose{\leq m}}\geq 2^{d-1},
or

\displaystyle{\rm card}(B)\frac{{d\choose{\leq m}}}{2^{d}}\geq 1/2.

If
\nu\sim B(d,1/2)
denotes the binomial r.v. with parameters

and

then the last inequality can be interpreted
as

\displaystyle{\rm card}(B){\mathbb{P}}\{\nu\leq m\}\geq 1/2.

We will choose

Then, by Hoeffding’s inequality,

{\mathbb{P}}\{\nu\leq d/4\}\leq e^{-d/8}.

Thus, for the corresponding set

we have

{\rm card}(B)\geq\frac{e^{d/8}}{2}.

Denote

\displaystyle\theta_{\omega}:=\frac{t_{\omega}}{\|t_{\omega}\|},\ \omega\in B.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{d}}\leq 1/8.
Then, for all

proof.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let

\|u_{1}\|=1,\|u_{2}\|=1

\lambda>\mu>0.

The following formula holds for the Kullback-Leibler distance between

\displaystyle K(N(0,\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu})\|N(0,\Sigma_{u_{2},\lambda,\mu}))=\frac{(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})}{2}\|u_{1}\otimes u_{1}-u_{2}\otimes u_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

proof.

Indeed, taking into account that
\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}
and
\Sigma_{u_{2},\lambda,\mu}
have the same spectrum and
using well known formulas for the KL-distance between two multivariate normal distributions, we have

\displaystyle K(N(0,\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu})\|N(0,\Sigma_{u_{2},\lambda,\mu}))={\rm tr}(\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{u_{2},\lambda,\mu}-\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu})),

where
\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}^{-1}
is the inverse of operator
\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}:L\mapsto L.

Let
P_{1}=u_{1}\otimes u_{1}

P_{2}=u_{2}\otimes u_{2},

P=P_{L}.

Note that
\Sigma_{u_{2},\lambda,\mu}-\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}=(\lambda-\mu)(P_{2}-P_{1})

and

\displaystyle\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\lambda}P_{1}+\frac{1}{\mu}(P-P_{1})=\Bigl(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\mu}\Bigr)P_{1}+\frac{1}{\mu}P.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{u_{2},\lambda,\mu}-\Sigma_{u_{1},\lambda,\mu}))=(\lambda-\mu){\rm tr}\Bigl(\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\mu}\Bigr)P_{1}+\frac{1}{\mu}P\Bigr)(P_{2}-P_{1})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\mu)(\lambda^{-1}-\mu^{-1}){\rm tr}(P_{1}P_{2}-P_{1}^{2})+(\lambda-\mu)\mu^{-1}{\rm tr}(P(P_{2}-P_{1}))

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})(1-\langle P_{1},P_{2}\rangle)=\frac{(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})}{2}\|P_{1}-P_{2}\|_{2}^{2},

implying the claim.

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume that

\displaystyle 16^{2}\leq d\leq(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})n.

Also suppose that, for a sufficiently small constant

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\leq\frac{c}{(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})}\frac{d}{n}.

Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}}\max_{\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{2}.

proof.

First note that, by Lemma
5.6
for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle K\Bigl(N(0,\Sigma_{\theta,\lambda,\mu})^{\otimes n}\|N(0,\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime},\lambda,\mu})^{\otimes n}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=nK\Bigl(N(0,\Sigma_{\theta,\lambda,\mu})\|N(0,\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime},\lambda,\mu})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})}{2}n\|\theta\otimes\theta-\theta^{\prime}\otimes\theta^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})n(1-\langle\theta,\theta^{\prime}\rangle^{2}).

On the other hand, by the last bound of Lemma

\displaystyle\langle\theta,\theta^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{2-\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|^{2}}{2}\geq 1-\frac{8^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2},

implying that

\displaystyle 1-\langle\theta,\theta^{\prime}\rangle^{2}\leq 1-\Bigl(1-\frac{8^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\Bigr)^{2}=\frac{8^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\Bigl(2-\frac{8^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\Bigr)\leq 8^{2}\varepsilon^{2}

\displaystyle K\Bigl(N(0,\Sigma_{\theta,\lambda,\mu})^{\otimes n}\|N(0,\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime},\lambda,\mu})^{\otimes n}\Bigr)\leq 8^{2}(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})n\varepsilon^{2}.

Note also that, by Lemma

Let
\Sigma_{0}:=\Sigma_{u,\lambda,\mu}
and let
\delta<(\lambda-\mu)/2.

Then, for any self-adjoint operator
\Sigma

the top eigenvalue
\lambda(\Sigma)=\|\Sigma\|

\Sigma

|\lambda(\Sigma)-\lambda|<\delta.

Moreover, if
\theta(\Sigma)
is a unit eigenvector of
\Sigma

\lambda(\Sigma),

[
15
]
, bound (2.7) of Lemma 1)

\displaystyle\|\theta(\Sigma)\otimes\theta(\Sigma)-u\otimes u\|\leq\frac{4\delta}{\lambda-\mu}.

(5.37)

Since eigenvector
\theta(\Sigma)

\langle\theta(\Sigma),u\rangle\geq 0.

Denote
P(\Sigma):=\theta(\Sigma)\otimes\theta(\Sigma).

Let
\bar{\delta}:=(\lambda-\mu)/8
and
U:=B(\Sigma_{0},2\delta)
with
\delta<\bar{\delta}/2.

We will need the following analytic lemmas (we will only sketch their proofs, skipping the details).

Lemma 5.8
.

For all
k\geq 0,
the mapping
U\ni\Sigma\mapsto P(\Sigma)
is
k
times Fréchet differentiable and

\|P^{(k)}\|_{L_{\infty}(U)}\lesssim_{k}\bar{\delta}^{-k}.

Moreover, for all
\rho\in(0,1],

\|P^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(U)}\lesssim_{k,\rho}\bar{\delta}^{-k-\rho}.

proof.

First note that, by Riesz formula, the spectral projection
P(\Sigma),\Sigma\in U
can be written as

\displaystyle P(\Sigma)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma}R_{\Sigma}(z)dz,

where
R_{\Sigma}(z):=(\Sigma-zI)^{-1}
is the resolvent of
\Sigma
and
\gamma:=\{z:|z-\lambda|=4\bar{\delta}\}
is the circle centered at
\lambda
of radius
4\bar{\delta}
with a counterclockwise orientation.
Using the formula

\displaystyle R_{\Sigma+H}(z)=(I+R_{\Sigma}(z)H)^{-1}R_{\Sigma}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}(R_{\Sigma}(z)H)^{j}R_{\Sigma}(z)

that holds for all
z\in\gamma
and for all operators
H
with small enough operator norm
\|H\|,
it is not hard to show the differentiability of
P(\Sigma)
and to derive the following
formula for its
k
-th order Fréchet derivative:

\displaystyle P^{(k)}(\Sigma)[H_{1},\dots,H_{k}]=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{2\pi i}\sum_{\tau\in S_{k}}\oint_{\gamma}R_{\Sigma}(z)H_{\tau(1)}R_{\Sigma}(z)\dots R_{\Sigma}(z)H_{\tau(k)}R_{\Sigma}(z)dz,

(5.38)

where the summation is over all permutations
\tau
of indices
1,\dots,k.
Since
\|R_{\Sigma}(z)\|\lesssim\bar{\delta}^{-1},z\in\gamma
and the radius of
\gamma
is
4\bar{\delta},

it is easy to prove that

\displaystyle\|P^{(k)}(\Sigma)\|\lesssim_{k}\bar{\delta}^{-k},\Sigma\in U,

which implies the desired bound on
\|P^{(k)}\|_{L_{\infty}(U)}.

Also, it is easy to see that, for
\|H_{1}\|<2\delta,\|H_{2}\|<2\delta,

\displaystyle\|R_{\Sigma_{0}+H_{1}}(z)-R_{\Sigma_{0}+H_{2}}(z)\|\lesssim\frac{\|H_{1}-H_{2}\|}{\bar{\delta}^{2}},z\in\gamma.

Since
\|H_{1}-H_{2}\|\lesssim\bar{\delta},
we also have

\displaystyle\|R_{\Sigma_{0}+H_{1}}(z)-R_{\Sigma_{0}+H_{2}}(z)\|\lesssim\frac{\|H_{1}-H_{2}\|^{\rho}}{\bar{\delta}^{1+\rho}},z\in\gamma,

for
\rho\in(0,1],
and, using formula (
5.38
),
it is easy to prove the desired bound on

\|P^{(k)}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{\rho}(U)}.

Lemma 5.9

For all
k\geq 0,

the mapping

Let
P_{0}:=P(\Sigma_{0})=u\otimes u.

14

We now turn to the main part of the argument.
Our first goal is to construct a set of functionals
f_{k}\in C^{s}(U),k=1,\dots,d
that are “hard” to estimate. They will be defined as

f_{k}(\Sigma)=(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}h_{k}(\theta(\Sigma)),

for properly chosen
h_{k}\in C^{s}({\mathbb{H}}).

Each functional
h_{k}
will be a sum of “bumps” with disjoint supports around well separated vectors
\theta_{\omega},\omega\in B.

Namely, let
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
function with support in
[-1,1]
and with
\varphi(0)>0.
Suppose that

\|\varphi\|_{C^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim 1.
Let
\phi(u):=\varphi(\|u\|^{2}),u\in{\mathbb{H}}.
Then
\|\phi\|_{C^{s}({\mathbb{H}})}\lesssim 1.

Define

\displaystyle h_{k}(\theta):=\sum_{\omega\in B}\omega_{k}\varepsilon^{s}\phi\Bigl(\frac{\theta-\theta_{\omega}}{c\varepsilon}\Bigr),\theta\in{\mathbb{H}},k=1,\dots,d,

where
c\in(0,1/16)
is a small enough constant. The following facts are straightforward:

(i)

each functional
\theta\mapsto\phi\Bigl(\frac{\theta-\theta_{\omega}}{c\varepsilon}\Bigr)
is supported in a ball
of radius
c\varepsilon
centered at
\theta_{\omega}.

(ii)

Functionals
\phi\Bigl(\frac{\theta-\theta_{\omega}}{c\varepsilon}\Bigr),\omega\in B
have disjoint supports. Moreover,
the supports of any two of those functionals are separated by distance
\geq\varepsilon/8.

(iii)

For all
\omega\in B,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\varepsilon^{s}\phi\Bigl(\frac{\cdot-\theta_{\omega}}{c\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{C^{s}({\mathbb{H}})}\lesssim 1.

The next proposition easily follows from the above properties.

Proposition 5.3
.

(i)

For all
k=1,\dots,d,

\displaystyle\|h_{k}\|_{C^{s}({\mathbb{H}})}\lesssim 1.

(ii)

For all

and all

\displaystyle h_{k}(\theta_{\omega})=\omega_{k}\varepsilon^{s}\varphi(0).

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}(\tilde{T}_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}))^{2}\leq 4{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}(T_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}))^{2}.

\displaystyle|\tilde{T}_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})|\leq 2|T_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})|,

Define also

By Lemma

, for all
\omega\in B,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\omega}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s-1}}\tau(\hat{\omega},\omega)=\frac{8}{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s-1}}\frac{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s}}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{h(\hat{\omega},\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s-1}}\frac{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s}}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{h(\tilde{\omega},\omega)+h(\tilde{\omega},\hat{\omega})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s-1}}\frac{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s}}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{2h(\tilde{\omega},\omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\sqrt{2}}{\varphi(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{s}\varepsilon^{s-1}}\tau(\tilde{\omega},\omega).

\displaystyle\tau^{2}(\tilde{\omega},\omega)=\frac{1}{4d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\tilde{T}_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}))^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{\omega}}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\omega}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\times 8^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2s}\varepsilon^{2(s-1)}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{\omega}}\tau^{2}(\tilde{\omega},\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{8^{2}/2}{\varphi^{2}(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2s}\varepsilon^{2(s-1)}}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}\Bigl(\tilde{T}_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\times 8^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2s}\varepsilon^{2(s-1)}}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}\Bigl(T_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})\Bigr)^{2},

where we also used bound (
5.39
). This implies that

\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{\omega}}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\omega}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\times 8^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2s}\varepsilon^{2(s-1)}}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}\Bigl(T_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})\Bigr)^{2}.

Assuming that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\inf_{T}\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}\Bigl(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})\Bigr)^{2}<\tau^{2},

one can find, for each
k=1,\dots,d,
an estimator
T_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}\Bigl(T_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})\Bigr)^{2}<\tau^{2},

which implies the existence of
\hat{\theta}
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{\omega}}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\omega}\|^{2}\leq\frac{(2\times 8^{2})\tau^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2s}\varepsilon^{2(s-1)}}.

Note that, for
\lambda=\gamma_{1}a,\mu=\gamma_{2}a,

(\lambda-\mu)(\mu^{-1}-\lambda^{-1})=(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})(\gamma_{2}^{-1}-\gamma_{1}^{-1}),
so condition (
5.36
) of Lemma
5.7
becomes

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})(\gamma_{2}^{-1}-\gamma_{1}^{-1})}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}:=\varepsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

(5.40)

with a sufficiently small constant
c>0.
Recall also that, by (
5.32
), we have

d=[r]\leq\frac{(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2}}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}n.

In view of Lemma
5.7
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\tau^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(0)(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2s}\varepsilon^{2(s-1)}}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{2}

for an arbitrary
\varepsilon
satisfying the condition (
5.40
).
We set
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and conclude that

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\gtrsim(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2s}\varepsilon^{2s}\gtrsim\gamma_{1}^{s}\gamma_{2}^{s}\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^{s}.

In other words,

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\inf_{T}\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}}\Bigl(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f_{k}(\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}})\Bigr)^{2}\gtrsim\gamma_{1}^{s}\gamma_{2}^{s}\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^{s}.

Finally, by Lemma
5.5
for all
\omega\in B,

\|\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}-\Sigma_{0}\|\leq 5\sqrt{2}(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})a\varepsilon.

For
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}),
we get
\|\Sigma_{\theta_{\omega}}-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta

provided that

\delta>5\sqrt{2}c\sqrt{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}a\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

Taking also into account that
\|f_{k}\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1,k=1,\dots,d,
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1}\inf_{T}\sup_{{\rm Im}(\Sigma)=L,\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}\Bigl(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr)^{2}\gtrsim\gamma_{1}^{s}\gamma_{2}^{s}\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^{s}.

It remains to combine the last bound with (
5.3
) and to recall that
d=[r],
to get

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{C^{s,a}(U)}\leq 1}\inf_{T}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|<\delta}\Bigl\|T(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-f(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma})}\gtrsim\gamma_{1}^{s/2}\gamma_{2}^{s/2}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\Bigr)^{s}.

The claim of Theorem
2.7
immediately follows by combining propositions
5.1
and
5.2
.

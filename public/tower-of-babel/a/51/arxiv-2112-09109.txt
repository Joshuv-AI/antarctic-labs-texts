Chromatic Quasisymmetric Class Functions for combinatorial Hopf monoids
Jacob A. White

Abstract
We study the chromatic quasisymmetric class function of a linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid. Given a linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid H H , and an H H -structure h h on a set N N , there are proper colorings of h h , generalizing graph colorings and poset partitions. We show that the automorphism group of h h acts on the set of proper colorings. The chromatic quasisymmetric class function enumerates the fixed points of this action, weighting each coloring with a monomial. For the Hopf monoid of graphs this invariant generalizes Stanley’s chromatic symmetric function and specializes to the orbital chromatic polynomial of Cameron and Kayibi. We also introduce the flag quasisymmetric class function of a balanced relative simplicial complex equipped with a group action. We show that, under certain conditions, the chromatic quasisymmetric class function of h h is the flag quasisymmetric class function of a balanced relative simplicial complex that we call the coloring complex of h h . We use this result to deduce various inequalities for the associated orbital polynomial invariants. We apply these results to several examples related to enumerating graph colorings, poset partitions, generic functions on matroids or generalized permutohedra, and others.

Introduction

Given a graph
G
, let
\mathfrak{G}
be a subgroup of the automorphism group of
G
. Two colorings
f
and

are equivalent if
g=f\circ\mathfrak{g}^{-1}
for some
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
. Let
\chi(G,\mathfrak{G},k)
denote the number of equivalence classes of
k
-colorings of

. Then
\chi(G,\mathfrak{G},k)
is a polynomial, called the orbital chromatic polynomial by Cameron and Kayibi
[
10
]

In attempting to prove various properties about
\chi(G,\mathfrak{G},k)
, and a symmetric function generalization, we discovered that the best approach was to apply tools from representation theory, which required working with class functions.
We now introduce the chromatic quasisymmetric class function of a graph
\mathbf{g}
. Given a graph
\mathbf{g}
, let
F(\mathbf{g})
denote the set of all proper colorings
f:V(\mathbf{g})\to\mathbb{N}
. If
\Aut(\mathbf{g})
is the automorphism group of
\mathbf{g}
, then
\Aut(\mathbf{g})
acts on
F(\mathbf{g})
by
\mathfrak{g}f=f\circ\mathfrak{g}^{-1}
, where
f\in F(\mathbf{g})
and
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots
be commuting indeterminates, and let
\mathfrak{G}\subset\Aut(\mathbf{g})
. Given
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
, we define

\chi(\mathbf{g},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g})=\sum_{f:\mathfrak{g}f=f}\prod_{v\in V(\mathbf{g})}x_{f(v)}

where the sum is over proper colorings
f
that are fixed by
\mathfrak{g}
.
The resulting power series is a quasisymmetric function, and as we vary
\mathfrak{g}
, we obtain a function on
\mathfrak{G}
that is constant on conjugacy classes, and whose values are quasisymmetric functions. We call such functions
quasisymmetric class functions
, and call
\chi(\mathbf{g},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})
the

of
(\mathbf{g},\mathfrak{G})
. In fact,
\chi(\mathbf{g},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})
is a symmetric class function. However, many of our other examples are not symmetric. Given two
\mathfrak{G}
-modules
V
and

and their respective characters
\rho_{V}
and
\rho_{W}
, we write
\rho_{V}\leq_{\mathfrak{G}}\rho_{W}
if
V
is isomorphic to a submodule of
W
. Likewise, given a sequence

, we let
\alpha(\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}\})=(s_{1},s_{2}-s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}-s_{k-1},d-s_{k})
.
We prove the following result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{g}
be a graph on a vertex set
N
, and let
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbf{g}).
Then we have the following:

1.

Write

\chi(\mathbf{g},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})=\sum_{S\subseteq[|N|-1]}c_{\mathfrak{G},S}M_{\alpha(S)},

where the

are characters, and

.

2.

If we write
\chi(\mathbf{g},\mathfrak{G},x)=\sum_{i=0}^{|N|}f_{i}\binom{x}{i}
, then we have the following inequalities:

(a)

For

, we have
f_{i}\leq f_{i+1}
.

(b)

For

, we have
f_{i}\leq f_{|N|-i}

(c)

For all

A quasisymmetric class function which satisfies the first result is
M
-increasing
, while a polynomial which satisfies the first two conditions in the second result is
strongly flawless
.

The goal of this work is to provide a general method for creating new quasisymmetric class functions associated to combinatorial objects. An orbital version of the order polynomial of a poset was studied by Jochemko
[
15
]
, and a quasisymmetric function generalization for double posets was studied by Grinberg
[
13
]
. We generalized further to a quasisymmetric class function associated to a double poset
[

]

. Hence, we are after a general framework for constructing such quasisymmetric functions, and proving theorems about them.

Previously, Aguiar, Bergeron, and Sottile
[
2
]
have shown that the Hopf algebra of quasisymmetric functions
\qsym
is the terminal object in the category of combinatorial Hopf algebras. Thus, given any collection of combinatorial objects that form the basis of a Hopf algebra, and a character, we obtain a quasisymmetric function invariant associated to each combinatorial object. We
[
20
]
showed that, for Hopf monoids in species, we can describe the resulting quasisymmetric functions of Aguiar, Bergeron and Sottile as a generating function over
\varphi
-proper colorings. We review this construction in Subsection
3.1
, and given the informal intuition here. A linearized Hopf monoid in species involves collections of labeled combinatorial objects
\mathbf{H}[N]
, which we call
\mathbf{H}
-structures, along with rules
\mu_{M,N}:\mathbf{H}[M]\times\mathbf{H}[N]\to\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]
and

for how to combine or decompose combinatorial objects on disjoint label sets

and

to mean that

cannot be decomposed with respect to

[
4
]

as an example.

The character
\varphi
determines which combinatorial objects are
1
-colorable. Then the
\varphi
-proper colorings are functions

such that decomposing
\mathbf{h}
with respect to

,

-colorable
\mathbf{H}
-structures.

For example, for the Hopf monoid
\mathbf{G}
of graphs, the map
\Delta_{M,N}
corresponds to taking induced subgraphs, and the

-colorable graphs are independent sets, so for a graph

Given a linearized Hopf monoid
\mathbf{H}
and a
\mathbf{H}
-structure

For

The resulting power series is a quasisymmetric function, and as we vary

-valued class function on

-chromatic quasisymmetric class function of

-proper colorings with largest color at most

-partitions of a poset,

-generic functions of a matroid, as defined in

]
,

4.

]
,

5.

-partitions of double posets, as defined in
[

]
,

]
,

-generic functions of a generalized permutohedron, as defined in
[

]
,

All of these examples are covered in some detail in Section

We generalize Theorem
1
to all of the above examples by giving a uniform proof that relies only on properties of the character

We review the definition of balanced convex character in Section
6
. Most characters studied in the literature are balanced convex characters, including the characters that give rise to all the various colorings listed above. The main condition for a balanced convex character is that

-structures.
We are able to prove the following results.

.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a linearized Hopf monoid in species with linearized character

be a finite set,

, and

There are other specializations of

After we have defined the

-chromatic quasisymmetric functions in

, and various specializations in Subsection

, we state Theorem

in Section

, we discuss quasisymmetric class functions and polynomial class functions. This material was studied previously in

. We show that the property of being

In

, we studied a generalization of Steingrímsson’s coloring complex of a graph. Given a balanced convex character

Preliminaries

Given a basis
B
for a vector space
V
over
\mathbb{C}
, and
\vec{\beta}\in B,\vec{v}\in V
, we let
[\vec{\beta}]\vec{v}
denote the coefficient of
\vec{\beta}
when we expand
\vec{v}
in the basis
B
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of commuting indeterminates. Recall that an

is a sequence
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})

\alpha\models n

[\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{i_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}]F(\mathbf{x})=[\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}]F(\mathbf{x})
. Often, we will define quasisymmetric functions that are generating functions over a collection of functions. Given a function
w:S\to\mathbb{N}
, we define

\mathbf{x}^{w}=\prod_{v\in S}x_{w(v)}.

(1)

For example, the chromatic symmetric function of a graph

is defined as
\chi(G,\mathbf{x})=\sum\limits_{f:V\to\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{x}^{f}

Given an integer composition

Our proofs rely a lot on working with set compositions, and quasisymmetric functions related to set compositions.
Given a finite set

, a

as

to denote that

is a set composition of

, and let
\ell(C)=k
be the length of the composition. Given

, the associated integer composition is

\alpha(C)=(|C_{1}|,|C_{2}|,\ldots,|C_{k}|).

(2)

We refer to
\alpha(C)
as the

of

. We partially order set compositions by refinement. We write

if

.

2.1
Group actions and class functions

Given a group action
\mathfrak{G}
on a set
X
, we let
X/\mathfrak{G}
denote the set of orbits. For
x\in X
,
\mathfrak{G}_{x}
is the stabilizer subgroup, and
\mathfrak{G}(x)
is the orbit of
x
. Finally, for
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
, we let
\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(X)=\{x\in X:\mathfrak{g}x=x\}
.

There is an action of
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
on the collection of all set compositions of
N
. Given a permutation
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
, and a set composition
C\models N
, we let

\mathfrak{g}C=\mathfrak{g}(C_{1})|\mathfrak{g}(C_{2})|\cdots|\mathfrak{g}(C_{k})

where
\mathfrak{g}(C_{i})=\{\mathfrak{g}x:x\in C_{i}\}
.

We assume familiarity with the theory of complex representations of finite groups - see
[
11
]
for basic definitions. Recall that, given any group action of
\mathfrak{G}
on a finite set
X
, there is a group action on
\mathbb{C}^{X}
as well, which gives rise to a representation. The resulting representations are called
permutation representations
.
Let
R
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. Then an
R
-valued class function
is a function
\chi:\mathfrak{G}\to R
such that, for every
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\in\mathfrak{G}
, and
\chi\in C(\mathfrak{G},R)
, we have
\chi(\mathfrak{hg}\mathfrak{h}^{-1})=\chi(\mathfrak{g}).
Let
C(\mathfrak{G},R)
be the set of
R
-valued class functions from
\mathfrak{G}
to
R
. For our paper,
R
is usually
\qsym
or
\mathbb{C}[x]
. We refer to
\chi\in C(\mathfrak{G},\mathbb{C})
as class functions when no confusion arises.

There is an orthonormal basis of
C(\mathfrak{G},\mathbb{C})
given by the characters of the irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{G}
. We refer to elements
\chi\in C(\mathfrak{G},\mathbb{C})
that are integer combinations of irreducible characters as
virtual characters
, and elements that are nonnegative integer linear combinations as
effective characters
. Let
E(\mathfrak{G},\mathbb{C})
be the set of effective characters. Finally, we say
\chi
is a
permutation character
if it is the character of a permutation representation. We partially order
E(\mathfrak{G},\mathbb{C})
by saying
\chi\leq_{\mathfrak{G}}\psi
if
\psi-\chi
is an effective character.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be a basis for
R
. For
b\in\mathbf{B}
,
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
, and
\chi\in C(\mathfrak{G},R)
, let
\chi_{b}(\mathfrak{g})=[b]\chi(\mathfrak{g})
. Then
\chi_{b}
is a
\mathfrak{C}
-valued class function. Thus we can write
\chi=\sum\limits_{b\in\mathbf{B}}\chi_{b}b
. Conversely, given a family
\chi_{b}
of
\mathfrak{C}
-valued class functions, one for each
b\in\mathbf{B}
, the function
\chi
defined by
\chi(\mathfrak{g})=\sum\limits_{b\in\mathbf{B}}\chi_{b}(\mathfrak{g})b
is an
R
-valued class function in
C(\mathfrak{G},R)
.

Let
\chi
be an
R
-valued class function. We say that
\chi
is
B
-realizable
if
\chi_{b}
is a permutation character for all
b
.
If
\mathbf{B}
has a partial order on it, then we say
\chi
is
\mathbf{B}
-increasing
if for all
b\leq c
in
\mathbf{B}
, we have
\chi_{b}\leq_{\mathfrak{G}}\chi_{c}
. Assuming
\chi
is
\mathbf{B}
-realizable, this is equivalent to saying that
\chi_{c}
is the character for the representation of

on some module

, and
\chi_{b}
is the character of a representation of a submodule of

.

A
quasisymmetric class function
is a
\qsym
-valued class function. Given a quasisymmetric class function

, we write
F(\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\alpha\models n}f_{\mathfrak{G},\alpha}M_{\alpha}
, where

.

Finally, we define a function
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:C(\mathfrak{G},R)\times C(\mathfrak{G},R)\to R
by

\langle\chi,\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\overline{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})\psi(\mathfrak{g})
where
\overline{x}
is the complex conjugate. In the case where

, this is the usual inner product on class functions.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a finite group, let

be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with basis

. Fix

.

(1)

Given an irreducible character

, we have

.

(2)

Suppose

2.2
Polynomial class functions and Principal specialization

Given a polynomial
p(x)
of degree
d
, define the
f
-vector
(f_{0},\ldots,f_{d})
via
p(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}\binom{x}{i}
. We say that
p(x)
is
strongly flawless
if the following inequalities are satisfied:

1.

for
0\leq i\leq\frac{d-1}{2}
, we have
f_{i}\leq f_{i+1}
.

2.

For
0\leq i\leq\frac{d}{2}
, we have
f_{i}\leq f_{d-i}
.

There is a lot of interest in log-concave and unimodal sequences in combinatorics. We consider strongly flawless sequences to be interesting, as strongly flawless unimodal sequences can be seen as a generalization of symmetric unimodal sequences. Examples of results with strongly flawless sequences include the work of Hibi
[
14
]
and Juhnke-Kubitzke and Van Le
[
16
]
.
We denote the entries of the
f
-vector as
f_{i}(p(x))
when discussing multiple polynomials.

There is a generalization of
f
-vector for polynomial class functions, which we call the
equivariant
f
-vector
. Given a group
\mathfrak{G}
, and a polynomial class function
p(\mathfrak{G},x)
, we write
p(\mathfrak{G},x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}f_{i}\binom{x}{i}
, where the
f_{i}
are characters.
We say that
p(\mathfrak{G},x)
is
effectively flawless
if we have the following system of inequalities:

1.

For
0\leq i\leq\frac{d-1}{2}
, we have
f_{i}\leq_{\mathfrak{G}}f_{i+1}
.

2.

For
0\leq i\leq\frac{d}{2}
, we have
f_{i}\leq_{\mathfrak{G}}f_{d-i}
.

Given a quasisymmetric function

of degree
d
, there is an associated polynomial
\ps(F)(x)
given by principal specialization. For

, we set

x_{i}=\begin{cases}1&i\leq x\\

The resulting sequence is a polynomial function in

of degree

, which we denote by
\ps(F)(x)
.
If we write
F(\mathbf{x})=\sum\limits_{\alpha\models d}c_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}
, then
f_{i}(\ps(F(\mathbf{x})))=\sum\limits_{\alpha\models d:\ell(\alpha)=i}c_{\alpha}
.

Let
F(\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})
be a quasisymmetric class function of degree
d
, and
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
. Define
\ps(F)\in C(\mathfrak{G},\mathbb{C}[x])
by
\ps(F)(x;\mathfrak{g})=\ps(F(\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g}))(x).
We refer to
\ps(F)
as the principal specialization, which results in an polynomial class function.

The following results were obtained in

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(d-i)f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\models d:\ell(\alpha)=i}(d-i)\chi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\leq\beta\models d:\ell(\alpha)=i,\ell(\beta)=i+1}\chi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq_{\mathfrak{G}}\sum_{\alpha\leq\beta\models d:\ell(\alpha)=i,\ell(\beta)=i+1}\chi_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\models d:\ell(\beta)=i+1}i\chi_{\beta}

\displaystyle=if_{i+1}.

The second equality comes from observing that, given

with

3
Linearized combinatorial Hopf monoids

In this section, we review the definition of linearized combinatorial Hopf monoids. First, we define the notion of Hopf monoid in the category of linear species.
A
linear species
is a functor
\mathbf{F}:Set\to Vec
from the category of finite sets with bijections to the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{K}
and linear transformations.

A
Hopf monoid
is a Hopf monoid object in the category of linear species
[
3
]
. We refer to
[
4
,
1
]
for more details.
For every pair of disjoint finite sets
M,N
, there are multiplication maps
\mu_{M,N}:\mathbf{H}[M]\otimes\mathbf{H}[N]\to\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]
and comultiplication maps
\Delta_{M,N}:\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]\to\mathbf{H}[M]\otimes\mathbf{H}[N]
. We focus only on
connected
species, where
\dim\mathbf{H}[\emptyset]=1
.
We let
\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}=\mu_{M,N}(\mathbf{x}\otimes\mathbf{y})
.

A
set species
is an endofunctor
\mathbf{F}:Set\to Set
.
Given a set species
\mathbf{F}
, there is an associated linear species
\mathbb{K}\mathbf{F}
called the
linearization
: we define
(\mathbb{K}\mathbf{F})_{N}
to be the vector space with basis
\mathbf{F}_{N}
. We refer to
\mathbf{f}
as an
\mathbf{F}
-structure if there exists a finite set
N
such that
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{F}_{N}
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a species.
We say that
\mathbf{H}
is a
linearized
Hopf monoid if
\mathbb{K}\mathbf{H}
is a Hopf monoid and:

1.

For every pair of disjoint finite sets
M,N
, the multiplication map
\mu_{M,N}
on
\mathbb{K}\mathbf{H}
is linearized from a function
\mu_{M,N}:\mathbf{H}[M]\times\mathbf{H}[N]\to\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]
. This means that for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{H}[M],\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
, we have
\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]
.

2.

For every pair of disjoint finite sets
M,N
, the comultiplication map
\Delta_{M,N}
on
\mathbb{K}\mathbf{H}
is linearized from a function
\Delta_{M,N}:\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]\to(\mathbf{H}[M]\times\mathbf{H}[N])\sqcup\{0\}
. Thus, for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]
, if
\Delta_{M,N}(\mathbf{x})\neq 0
then there exists
\mathbf{x}|_{M}\in\mathbf{H}[M]
and
\mathbf{x}/M\in\mathbf{H}[N]
with
\Delta_{M,N}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}|_{M}\otimes\mathbf{x}/M
.

Our notion of linearized Hopf monoid is more general than the usual one found in the literature, because we allow
\Delta_{M,N}(\mathbf{h})=0
for
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[M\sqcup N]
. The advantage is that we view the species of posets as a linearized Hopf monoid, despite the fact that the coproduct is sometimes zero.

Example 6
.

Given a finite set
N
, let
\mathbf{E}_{N}=\{1\}
. This gives rise to the
exponential species
.

Example 7
.

Given a finite set
N
, we let
\mathbf{C}_{N}
denote the collection of set compositions with ground set
N
. This forms the species of set compositions. Given a set decomposition
N=S\sqcup T
, if
C\models S
is given by
C_{1}|\cdots|C_{k}
and
C^{\prime}\models T
is given by
C^{\prime}_{1}|\cdots|C^{\prime}_{r}
, then their product
C\cdot C^{\prime}
is the set composition
C_{1}|\cdots|C_{k}|C^{\prime}_{1}|\cdots|C_{r}
.
Given a set composition

, we let
C|_{M}=C_{1}\cap M|C_{2}\cap M|\cdots|C_{k}\cap M
and
C/M=C_{1}\cap N|\cdots|C_{k}\cap N
, where it is understood that we remove any empty blocks from the composition.
For example,
\Delta_{\{1,3,5\},\{2,4,6\}}(12|35|46)=1|35\otimes 2|46
.

Then
\mathbf{C}
is a linearized Hopf monoid.

Example 8
.

Given a finite set
N

\mathbf{G}[N]
denote the collection of graphs with vertex set

\mathbf{g}

\mathbf{g}|_{S}

Given a finite set

Example 10

Given a finite set

\mathbf{m}

A natural transformation
\varphi:\mathbf{H}\to\mathbb{K}\mathbf{E}
of linear species, where
\mathbf{H}
is a Hopf monoid in species, is a

if for all disjoint finite sets
M
and
N
, and all

and all

, we have

. By an abuse of notation, we will write
\varphi(\mathbf{h})
in place of
\varphi_{N(\mathbf{h})}(\mathbf{h})
, when no confusion will arise.

Suppose that
\mathbf{H}
is a linearized Hopf monoid with a character
\varphi:\mathbb{K}\mathbf{H}\to\mathbb{K}\mathbf{E}
.
We say that
\varphi
is a

\varphi_{N}(\mathbf{h})\in\{0,1\}
for all
\mathbf{H}
-structures
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
.
A
linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid
is a linearized Hopf monoid

with a
linearized
character
\varphi
.
The motivation is that many combinatorial Hopf algebras are studied where the character
\varphi
only takes on the values

and

.

First, we mention two examples of characters that are defined for every Hopf monoid.

Example 11
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a Hopf monoid. We define
\zeta(\mathbf{h})=1
for every
\mathbf{H}
-structure
\mathbf{h}
. We refer to

.

Example 12
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a Hopf monoid. We say a

-structure

3.1
Chromatic quasisymmetric class functions

We review the definition of the
\varphi
-chromatic quasisymmetric function. Given a Hopf monoid
\mathbf{H}
, a finite set
N
, and a set composition
C=C_{1}|\cdots|C_{k}\models N
, we define
\Delta_{C}=(\id_{C_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{C/C_{1}})\circ\Delta_{C_{1},C_{2}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}}
. Given a character
\varphi
, we define
\varphi_{C}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{|C|}\varphi_{C_{i}}\right)\circ\Delta_{C}
. Given
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
, we define

\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{x})=\sum_{C\models N}\varphi_{C}(\mathbf{h})M_{\alpha(C)}.

A similar quasiymmetric function invariant was defined for combinatorial Hopf algebras by Aguiar, Bergeron, and Sottile
[
2
]
. Let
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}
, and let
\mathbf{g}
be a graph on
N
. Then
\Delta_{C}(\mathbf{g})
breaks
\mathbf{g}
up into induced subgraphs
\mathbf{g}_{i}
, one for each block
C_{i}
of
C
. We see that
\varphi_{C_{i}}(\mathbf{g}_{i})=1
if
\mathbf{g}_{i}
has no edges, and is
0
otherwise. Hence the term corresponding to
C
is non-zero only when each block induces an independent set, in which case the term is
M_{\alpha(C)}
. In this manner, we see that
\Psi_{\mathbf{G},\varphi}(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{x})
is the chromatic symmetric function introduced by Stanley
[
18
]
.

Stanley’s original definition involved a weighted sum over colorings. We showed in
[
20
]
that a similar formula exists for all linearized combinatorial Hopf monoids.
Let
f:N\to\mathbb{N}
be a function. There are only finitely many colors
i
such that
f^{-1}(i)\neq\emptyset
: let
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}
be those colors. We let
N_{i}
be the set of vertices
v
such that
f(v)\leq i
.
We call
f
a proper
\varphi
-coloring of
\mathbf{h}
if
\varphi(\mathbf{h}|_{N_{i+1}}/N_{i})=1
for all
i
. Let
F_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h})
be the set of
\varphi
-proper colorings. We proved the following in
[
20
]
:

Proposition 13
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid,
N
be a finite set, and
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
.

\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{x})=\sum_{f\in F_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h})}\prod_{n\in N}x_{f(n)}.

We do not give the proof again, but we do discuss some of the main ideas.
Let
f:N\to\mathbb{N}
. Let
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
be the natural numbers for which
f^{-1}(i_{j})\neq\emptyset
. Define
C(f)=f^{-1}(i_{1})|f^{-1}(i_{2})|\cdots|f^{-1}(i_{k})
. This is the composition associated with

\mathbf{x}^{f}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{i_{j}}^{|C_{j}|}

is the weight of the unique function defined by sending the vertices of

. Given any composition

, it follows that

M_{\alpha(C)}=\sum_{f:C(f)=C}\mathbf{x}^{f}.

We say set composition

is
\varphi
-proper if
\varphi_{C}(\mathbf{h})=1
. We let
\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h})
be the set of

-proper set compositions. To prove Proposition

\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{x})=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h})}M_{\alpha(C)}.

Then we showed that

Thus we obtain

\sum\limits_{C\in\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h}))}M_{\alpha(C)}=\sum\limits_{C\in\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h}))}\sum\limits_{f:C(f)=f}\mathbf{x}^{f}=\sum\limits_{f:C(f)\in\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h}))}\mathbf{x}^{f}.

The first equality uses the relation between functions an their compositions. We know that
\mathfrak{g}C(f)=C(f)
if and only
\mathfrak{g}f=f
, and
C(f)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h})
if and only if
f\in F_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h})
. Thus we have

\sum\limits_{C\in\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h}))}M_{\alpha(C)}=\sum_{f\in\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(F_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h}))}\mathbf{x}^{f}=\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g}).

To prove the second identity, we see that

\sum\limits_{C\in\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{C}_{\varphi}(\mathbf{h}))}M_{\alpha(C)}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\models|N|}\sum\limits_{C\in\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{h},\alpha})}M_{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\models|N|}\Psi_{\mathbf{h},\alpha}(\mathfrak{g})M_{\alpha}.

Now we prove the third identity. Let
x\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
. By definition,
\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x;\mathfrak{g})
counts the number of
\varphi
-proper colorings
f
with
f(N)\subseteq[x]
and
\mathfrak{g}f=f
. However, we see that
\ps(\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x});\mathfrak{g})(x)
also counts this same set of colorings (because setting
x_{i}=0
for
i>x
reduces the summation to only those colorings where
f(N)\subseteq[x]
, and setting
x_{i}=1
for
i\leq n
counts each function with weight
1
).

Thus
\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x)=\ps(\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x}))
.
The third formula follows immediately from principal specialization, and the fact that
\ps M_{\alpha}=\binom{x}{\ell(\alpha)}
.

∎

3.2
Orbital Chromatic Quasisymmetric Function

Now we discuss our orbital invariants. Fix a linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid
\mathbf{H}
, and a
\mathbf{H}
-structure
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
where
N
is a finite set. Finally, let
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbf{h})
. Given two
\varphi
-proper colorings
f
and
g
of
\mathbf{h}
, we say
f
and
g
are
\mathfrak{G}
-equivalent if
f=g\circ\mathfrak{g}^{-1}
for some
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
. We see that if
f
and
g
are
\mathfrak{G}
-equivalent, then
\mathbf{x}^{f}=\mathbf{x}^{g}
. Let
F_{\varphi}/\mathfrak{G}
denote the set of equivalence classes. For
C\in F_{\varphi}/\mathfrak{G}
, we define
\mathbf{x}^{C}=\mathbf{x}^{f}
for any
f\in C
.

Then we define the
orbital
\varphi
-chromatic quasisymmetric function
to be

\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}^{O}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})=\sum_{C\in F_{\varphi}/\mathfrak{G}}\mathbf{x}^{C}.

Similarly, if we let
F_{\varphi,k}(\mathbf{h})
be the set of
\varphi
-colorings
f
such that
f(N)\subseteq[k]
, then
\mathfrak{G}
acts on
F_{\varphi,k}(\mathbf{h})
, and we let
\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}^{O}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x)=|F_{\varphi,k}(\mathbf{h})/\mathfrak{G}|
. This is the
orbital
\varphi
-chromatic polynomial
.

We observe that
\Psi_{\mathbf{P},\zeta}^{O}(\mathbf{p},\mathfrak{G},x)
is the orbital order polynomial introduced by Jochemko
[
15
]
.
We observe that
\Psi_{\mathbf{G},\chi}^{O}(\mathbf{g},\mathfrak{G},x)
is the orbital chromatic polynomial introduced by Cameron and Kayibi
[
10
]
.
For matroids,
\Psi_{\mathbf{M},\chi}^{O}(\mathbf{m},\mathfrak{G},x)
is a new invariant, forming an orbital version of the Billera-Jia-Reiner polynomial.

Theorem 19
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid. Fix a finite set
N
, a
\mathbf{H}
-structure
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
, and a group
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbf{h})
.
Then we have the following identities:

1.

\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}}\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g})

(6)

2.

[M_{\alpha}]\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})=|\mathcal{C}_{\varphi,\alpha}(\mathbf{h})/\mathfrak{G}|.

(7)

3.

\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x)=\ps(\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x}))=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}}\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x;\mathfrak{g}).

(8)

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{H}
,
N
,
\mathbf{h}
and
\mathfrak{G}
as in the statement of the Theorem. We first prove the first formula by comparing coefficients on both sides.
Let
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
, and
\alpha\models|N|
. Let
F_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}),\alpha}(\mathbf{g})
denote the set of
\varphi
-proper colorings
f:N\to\mathbb{N}
such that

|f^{-1}(i)|=\begin{cases}\alpha_{j}&i=i_{j}\mbox{ for some }j\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Then
\mathfrak{G}
acts on
F_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}),\alpha}(\mathbf{g})
, and

\displaystyle[\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{i_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}]\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}^{O}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=|F_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}),\alpha}(\mathbf{g})/\mathfrak{G}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}}|\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(F_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}),\alpha})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}}[\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{i_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}]\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle=[\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{i_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}]\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}}\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g})

where the second equality uses Burnside’s Lemma.

To prove the second formula, observe that

\displaystyle[M_{\alpha}]\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}^{O}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}}[M_{\alpha}]\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{G}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}}\Psi_{\mathbf{h},\alpha}(\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{C}_{\varphi,\alpha}(\mathbf{h})/\mathfrak{G}|

where the first equality is the first formula, the second equality uses the second formula of Theorem

, and the third equality comes from Burnside’s Lemma and the observation that
\Psi_{\mathbf{h},\alpha}(\mathfrak{g})=|\Fix_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{C}_{\varphi,\alpha}(\mathbf{h}))|

The first equality for the third identity follows from the definition of principal specialization. The second equality can be proven in a similar manner to our first formula.

4
Main Theorem and Examples

We now give the definition of balanced convex character, and the full version of Theorem
2
.

Definition 20
.

We say that
\varphi
is a
balanced convex character
if, for every finite set
N
and ever
\mathbf{H}
-structure
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
, the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

If
|N|=1
, then
\varphi(\mathbf{h})=1
.

2.

If
|N|>1
, then there exists non-empty
S\subset N
such that
\Delta_{S,N\setminus S}(\mathbf{h})\neq 0
.

3.

If
\varphi(\mathbf{h})=1
, and there exists
S\subseteq N
such that
\Delta_{S,N\setminus S}(\mathbf{h})\neq 0
, then
\varphi(\mathbf{h}|_{S})=\varphi(\mathbf{h}/S)=1
.

The following we proven in
[
20
]
.

Example 21
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a linearized Hopf monoid. Suppose that for all finite sets
|N|>1
and all
\mathbf{H}
-structures
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{H}[N]
, there exists non-empty
S\subset N
such that
\Delta_{S,N\setminus S}(\mathbf{h})\neq 0
. Then
\zeta
and
\chi
are balanced convex characters.

In particular,
\zeta
and
\chi
are balanced convex characters for the linearized Hopf monoids of graphs
\mathbf{G}
, posets
\mathbf{P}
, and matroids
\mathbf{M}
.

Here is the full main result:

Theorem 22
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid with a balanced convex character
\varphi
. Let
\mathbf{h}
be a
\mathbf{H}
-structure on a finite set
N
, and fix
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbf{h})
.

1.

We have
\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})
and
\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})
are
M
-increasing.

2.

We have that
\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x)
is effectively flawless. Moreover, for all
i
, we have

(|N|-i)f_{i}(\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x))\leq_{\mathfrak{G}}if_{i+1}(\Psi_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x)).

3.

We have that
\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x)
is strongly flawless. Moreover, for all
i
, we have

(|N|-i)f_{i}(\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x))\leq if_{i+1}(\Psi^{O}_{\mathbf{H},\varphi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},x)).

We prove Theorem
22
after the proof of Theorem
33
, which is used to prove the first part. The rest of the proof relies mostly on the material from Section
2
, as well as Theorem
18
and Theorem
19
.

Since
\chi
and
\zeta
are balanced convex characters for the linearized combinatorial Hopf monoids of graphs, posets, and matroids, we see that Theorem

applies to all three cases. In particular, the quasisymmetric class functions which enumerate proper colorings of a graph
\mathbf{g}
,
\mathbf{p}
-partitions of a poset
\mathbf{p}
, or
\mathbf{m}
-generic functions of a matroid
\mathbf{m}

We remark that the result for posets is already a special case for similar results we obtained previously for double posets

4.1
Acyclic mixed graphs

Given a finite set
N
, a
mixed graph
is a triple
(N,U,D)
, where
U
is a set of undirected edges, and
D
is a set of directed edges. A mixed graph is
acyclic
if it does not contain a directed cycle. If we let
\mathbf{MG}[N]
denote the set of acyclic mixed graphs on
N

\mathbf{MG}
was studied in
[
20
]
.

There are two polynomial invariants associated to acyclic mixed graphs: the weak and strong chromatic polynomial, both introduced in
[
6

[
7

1.

For every
uv\in U

f(u)\neq f(v)
.

2.

For every
(u,v)\in D,
we have
f(u)\leq f(v)
.

\mathbf{MG}

Given a mixed graph
\mathbf{g}

\bar{\chi}(\mathbf{g})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\mathbf{d}$ has at least one undirected edge}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We proved the following in
[
20

Theorem 23
.

The pairs
(\mathbf{MG},\bar{\chi})
and
(\mathbf{MG},\chi)
are linearized combinatorial Hopf monoids. Moreover, both
\bar{\chi}
and
\chi
are balanced convex characters.

We also showed that a function
f
was
\chi
-proper for
\mathbf{g}
if and only if
f
is a strong coloring, and
f
is
\bar{\chi}
-proper if and only if
f
is a weak coloring.

We are able to apply Theorem
22
to
\mathbf{MG}
with respect to either character. We focus on describing the resulting quasisymmetric class functions. Let
\mathbf{g}
be a mixed graph on a vertex set

1.

For each undirected edge

2.

For each directed edge
(u,v)\in\mathbf{g}
, we have
(\mathfrak{g}u,\mathfrak{g}v)\in\mathbf{g}

Let
\Aut(\mathbf{g})
be the automorphism group, and let
\mathfrak{g}\in\Aut(\mathbf{g})

Given a (weak) coloring

For

where the sum is over colorings that are fixed by
\mathfrak{g}

Similarly, we see that

Double Posets

Now we will discuss double posets. Given a finite set
N

(N,\leq_{1},\leq_{2})
where
\leq_{1}
and
\leq_{2}
are both partial orders on
N

\mathbf{DP}[N]

\mathbf{DP}
is a species. The Hopf algebra of double posets was introduced by Malvenuto and Reutenauer

Given a double poset
\mathbf{d}
, a pair
(m,m^{\prime})\in M
is an
inversion
if
m<_{1}m^{\prime}
and
m^{\prime}<_{2}m

\mathbf{d}

\varphi(\mathbf{d})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\mathbf{d}$ has an inversion}\\

20
]

.

The pair
(\mathbf{DP},\varphi)
is a linearized combinatorial Hopf monoid. Moreover,
\varphi
is a balanced convex character.

\varphi

1.

\mathbf{d}

Let
\mathbf{d}
be a double poset on

, and
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbf{d})

4.3
Hopf monoid of generalized permutohedra

A
generalized permutohedron
is a polytope whose normal fan is a coarsening of the braid arrangement. If we let
\mathbf{GP}[N]
denote the set of all generalized permutohedra in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then we obtain the species of generalized permutohedra. Aguiar and Ardila
[
1
]
studied the linearized Hopf monoid
\mathbf{GP}
of generalized permutohedra. This example contains many other Hopf monoids, such as graphs, posets, and matroids, as Hopf submonoids.

The multiplication operation for
\mathbf{GP}
consists of cartesian products of polytopes. As such, a generalized permutohedron is totally decomposable if and only if it is a cartesian product of zero-dimensional generalized permutohedra. In particular, it consists of only one vertex. Thus
\chi(\mathbf{p})=1
if and only if
\mathbf{p}
consists of only one vertex. This is the basic character introduced by Aguiar and Ardila
[
1
]
.
They showed that, given a generalized permutohedron
\mathbf{p}
, a function
f:N\to\mathbb{N}
is
\chi
-proper if it is maximized by a unique vertex of
\mathbf{p}
. We call these
\mathbf{p}
-generic functions.

Given
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
, we can extend
\mathfrak{g}
by linearity to get a linear isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is an automorphism of
\mathbf{p}
if
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{p}
.
Let
\Aut(\mathbf{p})
be the automorphism group of
\mathbf{p}
. Then
\Aut(\mathbf{p})
acts on the set of
\mathbf{p}
-generic functions. Given
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbf{p})
and
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
, we see that the
\chi
-chromatic quasisymmetric class function of
\mathbf{p}
is given by:

\Psi_{\mathbf{GP},\chi}(\mathbf{p},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g})=\sum_{f:\mathfrak{g}f=f}\mathbf{x}^{f}

where the sum is over
\mathbf{p}
-generic functions that are fixed by
\mathfrak{g}
.

As an example, for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
, let
\Sigma_{i,j}
be the convex hull of the standard basis vectors
e_{i},e_{i+1},\ldots,e_{j}
. Then Postnikov’s description of the Loday realization of the associahedron is the Minkowski sum
A_{n}=\sum_{i\leq j}\Sigma_{i,j}
. Consider the permutation
\omega:[n]\to[n]
given by
\omega(i)=n-i+1
. We see that, after we extend
\omega
by linearity,
\omega(\Sigma_{i,j})=\Sigma_{n-j+1,n-i+1}
. Hence
\omega(A_{n})=A_{n}
, and we have an action of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on
A_{n}
.
Then

\Psi_{\mathbf{G},\chi}(\mathbf{a}_{3},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbf{x})=M_{2,1}+3\rho M_{1,1,1}.

Since
\chi
is a balanced convex character, Theorem
22
applies to
\Psi_{\mathbf{GP},\chi}(\mathbf{p},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})
.

4.4
Hypergraphs

As another example, Aguiar and Ardila
[
1
]
also consider various Hopf monoid structures related to hypergraphs
\mathbf{HG}
. The corresponding polynomial invariants
\Psi_{\mathbf{HG},\chi}(\mathbf{h},\{e\},x)
were studied in greater detail in
[
5
]
. A
hypergraph
consists of a pair
(V,E)
, where
E
is a multiset of nonempty subsets of
V
. Given a hypergraph
\mathbf{h}
, and edge
e
, and a function
f:N\to\mathbb{N}
, we say
v\in e
is a
local maximum
if
f(v)\geq f(u)
for all
u\in e
.
A
\chi
-proper coloring is a function
f:V\to\mathbb{N}
such that every edge
e
has a unique local maximum.

Given a hypergraph
\mathbf{h}
, we see that
\Aut(\mathbf{h})
consists of those
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
such that
\mathfrak{g}e\in E
for all
e\in E
(preserving multiplicity as well). Given
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbf{h})
, we consider the action of
\mathfrak{G}
on the set of
\chi
-proper colorings.

For
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
, we have

\Psi_{\mathbf{HG},\chi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x};\mathfrak{g})=\sum_{f:\mathfrak{g}f=f}\mathbf{x}^{f}

where the sum is over
\chi
-proper colorings of
\mathbf{h}
that are fixed by
\mathfrak{g}
.

For example, let
\mathbf{h}
be the hypergraph on
\{a,b,c,d\}
whose edges consist of every subset of
\{a,b,c,d\}
of size
3
, all with multiplicity one. Then

\Psi_{\mathbf{HG},\chi}(\mathbf{h},\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z},\mathbf{x})=3\rho M_{2,1,1}+6\rho M_{1,1,1,1}.

Since
\chi
is a balanced convex character, Theorem
22
applies to
\Psi_{\mathbf{HG},\chi}(\mathbf{h},\mathfrak{G},\mathbf{x})
.

4.5
Simplicial complexes

The Coxeter Complex of type A

In this section, we discuss the Coxeter complex of type
A
, since many balanced relative simplicial complexes we are interested in are subcomplexes of the Coxeter complex of type
A
. Our goal is to review the fact that faces of the Coxeter complex can be indexed by set compositions and by flags of subsets, and there is a
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
-invariant correspondence between both collections of indexing sets.

Given a finite set
N
, let
V(N)
be the collection of proper subsets of
N
. Then we define the Coxeter complex of the type
A
,
\Sigma_{N}
, as follows:

\Sigma_{N}=\{\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{k}\}:\emptyset\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{k}\subset N\}

The faces of
\Sigma_{N}
are
flags
of proper subsets of
N
. We denote the faces
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\}
by
F_{\bdot}
and given a flag
F_{\bdot}=F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{k}
, we write
\ell(F_{\bdot})=k+1
. We also define
\kappa(F)=\{|F_{1}|,\ldots,|F_{k}|\}
. Given
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
, we let
\mathfrak{g}F_{\bdot}=\mathfrak{g}F_{1}\subset\mathfrak{g}F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\mathfrak{g}F_{k}
. Thus we see that
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
acts on
\Sigma_{N}
. We also see that
\kappa(\mathfrak{g}F_{\bdot})=\kappa(F_{\bdot})
.

To every set composition
C\models N
, there is an associated flag
F(C)=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell(C)-1}\}
. We define
F_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}C_{j}
. Note that

. Similarly, if

, and

, then there is an associated set composition
C(F)
, defined by:

1.

C_{1}=F_{1}
,

2.

C_{i}=F_{i}\setminus F_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq k-1
, and

3.

C_{k}=N\setminus F_{k-1}
.

For example, to the set composition
13|2|45
, the associated edge in

is
\{1,3\}\subset\{1,2,3\}
. Hence, we can denote faces of the Coxeter complex by flags of subsets or by set compositions.

Now we show that the functions
F
and

are
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
-invariant.
Let
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}.
Let

. Then
F(\mathfrak{g}C)_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}\mathfrak{g}(C_{j})=\mathfrak{g}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}C_{j}\right)=\mathfrak{g}F(C)_{i}
. Thus

Now fix

(\mathfrak{g}C(F_{\bdot}))_{1}=\mathfrak{g}F_{1}=\mathfrak{g}(C(F)_{1})

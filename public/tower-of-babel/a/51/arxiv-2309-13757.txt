G G -Solid rational surfaces
Antoine Pinardin

Abstract
. We classify G G -solid rational surfaces over the field of complex numbers for finite group actions.



1.
Introduction

We are interested in the equivariant birational geometry of rational surfaces over the field of complex numbers for finite group actions. Let
S
be a rational surface, and
G
be a finite group acting faithfully and biregularly on
S
. Denote by
\rho^{G}(S)
the rank of the
G
-invariant part of the Picard group of
S
. The
G
-equivariant Minimal Model Program applied to a resolution of singularities of
S
implies that
S
is
G
-birational to a
G
-Mori fibre space
, i.e. a
G
-surface in one of the following two cases:

•

A
G
-del Pezzo surface, namely a del Pezzo surface
S
such that
-K_{S}
is ample and
\rho^{G}(S)=1
,

•

A
G
-conic bundle, i.e. there is a
G
-equivariant morphism
S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with general fibre isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and such that
\rho^{G}(S)=2
.

We say that
S
is
G
-solid
if it is not
G
-birational to any
G
-conic bundle.

Main Theorem 1
.

Let
S
be a
G
-del Pezzo surface of degree
d=K_{S}^{2}
. Then
S
is
G
-solid if and only if:

•

d\leq 3
.

•

d=4
and
G
does not fix a point on
S
in general position.

•

d=5
and
G
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
or
\mathrm{D}_{5}
.

•

d=6
and
G
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
,
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, or
\mathrm{D}_{6}
.

•

d=8
,
S\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and, up to conjugation in
\mathrm{Aut}(S)
, either

–

G
has a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
,

–

or
G_{4}\subset G
and
G\not\subset G_{16}
, for two specific groups
G_{4}
and
G_{16}
.

•

S\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the group
G
does not fix a point on
S
, and is not isomorphic to
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
or
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
.

A complete answer was already given for
K_{S}\leq 4
by Segre in
[
12
]
, Manin in
[
10
]
, Das Dores-Mauri in
[
8
]
, and Dolgachev-Iskovskikh in
[
7
]
. We will quickly recall these results in section
2
. For
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we also prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
or
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
. The only
G
-Mori fibre spaces
G
-birational to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
are
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and a del Pezzo surface of degree
5
with a
G
-conic bundle structure.

In particular, for
G\cong\mathfrak{A}_{4}
and for
G\cong\mathfrak{S}_{4}
, the projective plane is not
G
-birational to any Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
.

A recent motivation for our work is given by Tschinkel, Yang, and Zhang in
[
13
]
. Both
G
-solid surfaces and surfaces which are not
G
-birational to a Hirzebruch surface are classes of divisors on threefolds which give rise to
Incompressible Divisorial Symbols
, a modern tool used in the formalism of Burnside groups to distinguish birational types of group actions. An example of applications of these techniques by Cheltsov, Tshinkel, and Zhang can be found in
[
6
]
.

Finally, let us point out that the classification of
G
-solid Fano varieties is widely open starting from dimension
3
. Some work has been achieved in the case of toric Fano threefolds, see for example
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
, and
[
6
]
.

2.2.
Existing results

The case of

-del Pezzo surfaces

-del Pezzo surface

-birational map starting from

-isomorphic surface. The surface

-birational map starting from

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

be a smooth del Pezzo surface, and

be a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(S)
such that
\rho^{G}(S)=1
.

•

If
K_{S}^{2}=1
,

is
G
-superrigid.

•

IF
K_{S}^{2}=2
or
K_{S}^{2}=3
,
S
is
G
-rigid.

In
[
8
]
, Das Dores and Mauri also classified finite groups

for which a del Pezzo surface of degree
2
or

is
G
-superrigid.

For

, the result is essentially a corollary of the classification of

-links in
[
9
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(S)
such that
\rho^{G}(S)=1
, where
S
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
4
. The following conditions are equivalent.

•

S
is
G
-rigid,

•

S
is
G
-solid,

•

does not fix a point on

outside of the

Proof.

Using the classification of Sarkisov links in
[

]
, we see that there are three possible links starting from

. The first one is the blow-up of a point not lying on a

-curve, leading to a

-conic bundle. The second one is a Geiser involution, centered at a an orbit of length

, and the last one is a Bertini involution, centered at an orbit of length

. But as mentioned in
[
15
]
, Geiser and Bertini involutions lead to
G
-isomorphic surfaces.
∎

Consider the matrices

\mathrm{D}_{5}

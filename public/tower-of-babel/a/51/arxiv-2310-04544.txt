The plausibility of the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is supported (among others) by results of type

(1.1)

N(\sigma,T):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta(\beta+i\gamma)=0\\
\beta\geq\sigma,\ 0\leq\gamma\leq T\end{subarray}}=o(N(T))\ \text{ with }\ N(T):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta(\beta+i\gamma)=0\\
0\leq\gamma\leq T\end{subarray}}1\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T

valid for all
\sigma>1/2
as
T\to\infty
.
(
1.1
) was first shown by Bohr and Landau in 1914 (
[
BL1914
]
).
The first estimate of type

(1.2)

N(\sigma,T)\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{A(\sigma)(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}\ \text{ for any }\ \varepsilon>0\ \text{ and }\ \sigma\geq 1/2

was shown few years later by Carlson
[
Car1920
]
with

(1.3)

A(\sigma)\leq 4\sigma.

It was Hoheisel
[
Hoh1930
]
who first observed that such estimates lead to arithmetic consequences about
the difference of consecutive primes.
He proved the first approximation towards the famous conjecture that there exists always a prime between two consecutive squares.
This conjecture was characterised by Landau
[
Lan1913
]
in his plenary talk at ICM1912 in Cambridge as one of the four main problems of the distribution of primes,
besides the Riemann Hypothesis.
Hoheisel
[
Hoh1930
]
could show (
p_{n}
denotes the
n
th
prime)

(1.4)

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\ll p_{n}^{\theta}\ \text{ with }\ \theta=1-\frac{1}{33\,000}.

In the proof important role was played by Carlson’s density theorem (
1.2
)–(
1.3
).

Later it was realized that a uniform estimate of the form

(1.5)

A(\sigma)\leq A\ \text{ for all }\ \sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}

yields (combined with a slightly better zero-free region than the classical one of de la Vallée Poussin)

(1.6)

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\ll_{\varepsilon}p_{n}^{1-1/A+\varepsilon}\ \text{ for any }\ \varepsilon>0.

In particular, the best possible estimate
A=2
would almost imply Landau’s conjecture.
This is especially remarkable in light of the fact that even assuming the Riemann Hypothesis the best estimate we know is

(1.7)

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\ll p_{n}^{1/2}\log^{C}p_{n}

(with
C=2
easily by the Riemann–Von Mangoldt explicit formula, with
C=1
by a deeper argument of Cramér
[
Cra1921
]
).

This explains the significance of the Density Hypothesis (DH) which states

(1.8)

N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{2(1-\sigma)}\log^{C}T\ \text{ with some }\ C>0\ \text{ for all }\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2},

or, in a slightly weaker form, using the notation (
1.2
),

(1.9)

A(\sigma)\leq 2\ \text{ for all }\ \sigma\geq 1/2\ \ \left(\Longleftrightarrow N(\sigma,T)\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{2(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}\right).

The Riemann Hypothesis clearly implies the Density Hypothesis.
However, Ingham
[
Ing1937
]
showed that also the Lindelöf Hypothesis (LH)

(1.10)

\mu\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=0,\ \text{ where }\ \mu(\alpha)=\text{\rm inf}\left\{\mu;|\zeta(\sigma+it)|\leq T^{\mu}\text{ for }\sigma\geq\alpha,\,1<|t|\leq T\right\}

implies DH.

In 1954 Turán
[
Tur1954
]
used his celebrated power-sum method
[
Tur1953
]
,
[
Tur1984
]
to give a different proof of Ingham’s result that LH implies DH.
In the same work he came very close to breaking the DH in the vicinity of the boundary line
\sigma=1
.
In the following we will use the notation:

(1.11)

s=\sigma+it,\ \ \sigma=1-\eta,\ \ A(1-\eta)=B(\eta).

His result was with a small constant
c_{1}

[
Tur1954
]

(1.12)

N(1-\eta,T)\ll T^{2\eta+\eta^{1.14}}\log^{6}T\ \text{ for }\ \eta<c_{1},

or with our new notation

(1.13)

B(\eta)\leq 2+\eta^{0.14}\ \text{ for }\eta<c_{1}.

It was 16 years later when G. Halász and Turán succeeded to break the DH
[
HT1969
]
, that is, to prove (in the refined form appearing as Theorem 38.2 of Turán’s book
[
Tur1984
]
) with the notation (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), (
1.11
)

(1.14)

B(\eta)\leq 1.2\cdot 10^{5}\eta^{1/2},\ \text{ more precisely }\ N(\sigma,T)<T^{1.2\cdot 10^{5}\eta^{3/2}}\log^{C}T.

The proof was based on

(i)

Vinogradov’s estimate
\mu(1-\eta)\ll\eta^{3/2}
;

(ii)

Turán’s power-sum method;

(iii)

a simple but ingenious idea of Halász
[
Hal1968
]
.

Soon after this Bombieri
[
Bom1971
]
gave a different proof using ideas from the large sieve coupled with (i) and (iii).
Further, Montgomery has shown the DH for
\sigma\geq 9/10
, i.e.
\eta\leq 1/10

(
[
Mon1969
]
,
[
Mon1971
]
).
Using another simple but ingenious idea Huxley
[
Hux1972
]
(with a refinement of Montgomery) reached the DH for
\eta\leq 1/6
and proved that

(1.15)

A(\sigma)\leq 12/5\ \text{ for }\ \sigma>1/2.

The DH was shown for larger and larger ranges, until improving the result
\sigma>11/14
of Jutila
[
Jut1977
]
Bourgain proved its validity
[
Bou2000
]
for

(1.16)

\sigma\geq 25/32\Longleftrightarrow\eta\leq 7/32.

Another direction of the research was to give strong density estimates for small values of
\eta
(often especially for
\eta\to 0
).
In this direction the most important results were reached (in alphabetical order) by Bourgain, Ford, Heath-Brown, Huxley, Ivic, Jutila and Montgomery.

All these results use the large sieve and in some form the mentioned idea of Halász, which needs
\eta\leq 1/4
.
This is also the reason that they cannot give improvements of the classical zero-density theorem of Ingham

[
Ing1940
]
,
A(\sigma)\leq 3/(2-\sigma)
, which is still the best today in the whole range

(1.17)

\sigma\in\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4}\right]\Longleftrightarrow\eta\in\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}\right].

Recently I gave two alternative variants for proving the DH for small values of
\eta
.
In
[
Pin2022
]
the goal was to reach a possibly simple proof while in
[
Pin2023
]
to show the strongest possible result both in specific ranges of
\eta<1/12
and for
\eta\to 0
.
Both proofs were based on Vinogradov’s method and Halász’s idea.
However, in the second
[
Pin2023
]
it was important to use recent deep results of Heath-Brown
[
Hea2017
]
, further of Bourgain, Demeter and Guth
[
BGD2016
]
.

2

The goal of the present work is

(i)

to give a possibly simple proof for all values of
\sigma>3/4
, i.e.
\eta<1/4
, which

(ii)

breaks the DH for the non-negligible range
\sigma\geq 7/8
, i.e.
\eta\leq 1/8
, further gives

(iii)

explicit density theorems for different ranges of all
\eta<1/4
with
B(\eta)\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
and

(iv)

besides the (possibly indispensable) simple idea of Halász uses only classical knowledge of the theory of Riemann’s zeta-function (classical in the sense that known since at least hundred years).

This means that we avoid the use of Turán’s method, the large sieve, Vinogradov’s method, further mean and large value theorems.
However, to break the DH we need the estimate reached by the Weyl–Hardy–Littlewood method
[
Lit1922
]
(see also (5.15.1) of the book of Titchmarsh
[
Tit1951
]
, 2
nd
edition, or the paper of Landau
[
Lan1924
]
):

(2.1)

\mu\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{(\ell+2)2^{\ell}},\quad\ell\geq 0

which is proved by Hardy and Littlewood in
[
Lit1922
]
for
\ell\geq 1
, but it is also true for
\ell=0

(\mu(0)=1/2)
by the functional equation and
\mu(1)=0
.

Alternatively, the method of Van der Corput would yield results of similar type
[
Cor1921
]
,
[
Cor1922
]
.
However, the use of (
2.1
) seems to be simpler, although Van der Corput’s result

(2.2)

\mu\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2^{\ell}-2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2^{\ell}-2}\qquad(\ell\geq 2)

may yield even stronger estimates for
N(1-\eta,T)
in many ranges of
\eta
.

In order to formulate our results we need some more notation.
For
\ell\geq 1
let

(2.3)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=1-\frac{1}{2^{\ell}},\qquad\sigma>\alpha_{\ell}\Longleftrightarrow\eta<\frac{1}{2^{\ell}},

(2.4)

\displaystyle u_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=u_{\ell}(\eta):=\frac{\mu(\alpha_{\ell})}{\sigma-\alpha_{\ell}}=\frac{\mu(\alpha_{\ell})}{2^{-\ell}-\eta},

(2.5)

\displaystyle v_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=v_{\ell}(\eta):=u_{\ell-1}(2\eta)=\frac{\mu(\alpha_{\ell-1})}{2(2^{-\ell}-\eta)}.

By the estimate (
2.1
) of Hardy and Littlewood we have for
\eta<1/2^{\ell}

(2.6)

u_{\ell}\leq\frac{1}{(\ell+2)(1-2^{\ell}\eta)},\qquad v_{\ell}\leq\frac{1}{(\ell+1)(1-2^{\ell}\eta)}\ \text{ for }\ \ell\geq 1.

Theorem 1
.

For
\ell\geq 1
,
\eta<\max(1/3,2^{-\ell})
we have

(2.7)

B(\eta)\leq\max\bigl(4u_{\ell}(\eta),3v_{\ell}(\eta)\bigr).

This implies a series of explicit density theorems for small values of
\eta
and the conditional result of Halász and Turán
[
HT1969
]
that assuming (LH) we have
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{\varepsilon}
for
\sigma>3/4
, which is for
\sigma>3/4
a strong improvement of the theorem of Ingham
[
Ing1937
]
stating
A(\sigma)=2
for
\sigma>1/2
on LH.

Corollary 1
.

The Lindelöf Hypothesis implies

(2.8)

N(\sigma,T)\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{\varepsilon}\ \text{ for }\ \sigma>3/4.

Proof.

Choosing
\ell=2
, LH and the definition (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) yield
u_{\ell}(\eta)=v_{\ell}(\eta)=0
for
\eta<1/4
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

For
\ell\geq 2
,
\eta<\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}
we have

(2.9)

B(\eta)\leq\frac{4}{(\ell+2)(1-2^{\ell}\eta)}.

Proof.

4/(\ell+2)\geq 3/(\ell+1)
for
\ell\geq 2
with equality for
\ell=2
.

Denoting by
\log_{2}x
the logarithm of base
2
and choosing
C
large (
C=C(\eta)\to\infty
as
\eta\to 0
, but with
C(\eta)=o(\log 1/\eta)
), we obtain for
\eta\in\bigl[1/2^{\ell(1+o(1))},1/2^{\ell+C}\bigr]
from (
2.7
).
∎

Corollary 3
.

\displaystyle B(\eta)\leq\frac{4}{(\ell+2)(1-2^{-C})}\leq\frac{4(1+o(1))}{\log_{2}(1/\eta)}=\frac{4\log 2(1+o(1))}{\log(1/\eta)}
.

This is naturally weaker than a result obtainable by Vinogradov’s method (see
[
Pin2023
]
and
[
Hea2017
]
) but shows still
B(\eta)\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
beyond breaking DH in some interval for
\sigma=1-\eta
close to the point
s=1
.

However, Corollary
2
yields also interesting (although already known) consequences form small values of
\ell
(
\ell=2,3
, for example), i.e., for relatively large ranges of
\eta
.
On the other hand, they are often stronger than earlier results proved in a more complicated way (as 11.30 of Ivic
[
Ivi1985
]
, for example).

Corollary 4
.

\displaystyle B(\eta)\leq\frac{1}{1-4\eta}
for
\eta<1/4
.

Corollary 5

\displaystyle B(\eta)\leq\frac{4}{5(1-8\eta)}
for

.

breaks the DH for

, i.e.
\sigma\geq 7/8
,
while Corollary

only for

But Corollary

is sharper than Corollary

for
\eta<1/24

Even the case
\ell=1

yields

\displaystyle B(\eta)\leq\frac{3}{2(1-2\eta)}
for

This breaks the DH also for
\eta\leq 1/8
, that is, for
\sigma\geq 7/8
, but it is weaker than Corollary

for
\eta<1/8
(sharper for

Abstract
. We consider the algebra A 0 ​ ( X ) A^{0}(X) of polynomial functions on a simplicial complex X X . The algebra A 0 ​ ( X ) A^{0}(X) is the 0 0 th component of Sullivan’s dg-algebra A ∙ ​ ( X ) A^{\bullet}(X) of polynomial forms on X X . All algebras are over an arbitrary field k k of characteristic 0 0 . Our main interest lies in computing the de Rham cohomology of the algebra A 0 ​ ( X ) A^{0}(X) , that is, the cohomology of the universal dg-algebra Ω A 0 ​ ( X ) ∙ \Omega^{\bullet}_{A^{0}(X)} . There is a canonical morphism of dg-algebras P : Ω A 0 ​ ( X ) ∙ → A ∙ ​ ( X ) P:\Omega^{\bullet}_{A^{0}(X)}\to A^{\bullet}(X) . We prove that P P is a quasi-isomorphism. Therefore, the de Rham cohomology of the algebra A 0 ​ ( X ) A^{0}(X) is canonically isomorphic to the cohomology of the simplicial complex X X with coefficients in k k . Moreover, for k = ℚ k=\mathbb{Q} the dg-algebra Ω A 0 ​ ( X ) ∙ \Omega^{\bullet}_{A^{0}(X)} is a model of the simplicial complex X X in the sense of rational homotopy theory. Our result shows that for the algebra A 0 ​ ( X ) A^{0}(X) the statement of Grothendieck’s comparison theorem holds.



1.
Introduction

All algebras and dg-algebras are commutative over a field

0

In

One such candidate is the universal dg-algebra

of polynomial functions on

.
There is a canonical morphism of dg-algebras

.

The main result is Theorem

, where we prove that

is a quasi-isomorphism.

In
[
10
]
the authors prove that the morphism

is surjective and give a description of its kernel.
In
[
6
]
and
[
12
, Appendix G(i)]
another description of the kernel is given.
In
[
7
, Example
3.8
]
, Gómez establishes that the morphism

not

.
We were able to correct the erroneous computation of Gómez in Remark

Grothendieck proved that for a smooth

2.
Simplicial complexes

Definition 2.1
.

We call a set
X
of finite non-empty subsets of a finite set
E
a simplicial complex if for every
v\in E
we have
\{v\}\in X
and for every
s\in X
and every non-empty subset
s^{\prime}\subset s
we have
s^{\prime}\in X
.
We denote by
V(X)
the set
E
and call its elements vertices of
X
.
The sets
s\in X
of cardinality
m+1
are called
m
-simplices.
A simplicial complex
Y
is a subcomplex of
X
if for every
s\in Y
we have
s\in X
.

We denote by
T_{p}(X)
the set of all sequences
u=(u_{0},\dots,u_{p})
of vertices of
X
for
p\geq-1
.
We denote by
\partial_{i}u
the sequence
(u_{0},\dots,\widehat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{p})
.
For a vertex
v
we denote by
v*u
the sequence
(v,u_{0},\dots,u_{p})
.
The symmetric group
\Sigma_{p+1}
acts on
T_{p}(X)
.

Consider a sequence of vertices
u\in T_{p}(X)
.
We denote by
\mathrm{St}\,u
the
star
of
u
, that is the smallest subcomplex of
X
containing all the simplices containing the vertices
u_{i}
.
If
p=-1
, we have
\mathrm{St}\,u=X
.
If the sequence
u
spans a simplex in
X
, then
\mathrm{St}\,u
is the star of this simplex.
If
p\geq 0
and the sequence
u
does not span a simplex, we have
\mathrm{St}\,u=\varnothing
.
For a subcomplex
Y
of
X
we denote by
\mathrm{St}\,_{Y}u
the smallest subcomplex of
Y
containing all the simplices containing the vertices
u_{i}
.
If
u\notin T_{p}(Y)
then
\mathrm{St}\,_{Y}u=\varnothing
.

For a simplicial complex

For a simplex

|_{b}:A^{\bullet}(a)\to A^{\bullet}(b),\qquad t_{v}\mapsto\begin{cases}0,\,v\notin b,\\

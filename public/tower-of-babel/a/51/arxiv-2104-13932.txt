Some of the known solutions to these equations give contributions which in the large

A priori there is no reason for such a matching of individual terms in the sum (as opposed to the full partition function); in particular, it has not been shown on the gravity side that the partition function localizes to a discrete sum over solutions, analogous to the Bethe Ansatz result. However, we will show that there is in fact a precise matching between the two sides.

In one direction, we show that every known solution to the Bethe Ansatz equations, which has a good large
N

N^{2}

In the other direction, we note that a priori there are many more gravitational solutions with the correct boundary conditions than Bethe Ansatz solutions, and that if we naively sum over all solutions on the gravity side we obtain divergences, since the real part of their actions is not bounded from below. However, our supersymmetric Euclidean solutions are all complex-valued, and it is not obvious what the rules for including such solutions in the gravitational theory are. We use (as previous works do) a specific prescription for evaluating the action of the complex solutions (at leading order in
1/N
) by analytic continuation. However, it is not clear when this analytic continuation is valid, since various poles may be encountered when one tries to explicitly continue the relevant contours in the gravitational path integral (to the extent that such a path integral description makes sense to begin with). And moreover, when we analytically continue only the leading contribution to some solution, we risk having subleading terms become dominant over the leading ones (for instance, a non-perturbative correction of the form

We suggest a specific prescription for which solutions should be included in the path integral, namely that solutions for which the non-perturbative contributions from wrapped D3-branes are exponentially large (rather than exponentially suppressed) should not be included. With this (reasonable but not rigorously derived) prescription, we find a precise one-to-one match between the solutions on both sides.

1.1
Future directions

There are many open questions left by our analysis. Some of them are:

•

The rules of the AdS/CFT correspondence in the presence of complex sources (such as the background metric or gauge fields) are not clear. A commonly used method is to take results for the classical action on the gravity side computed for real sources, and to analytically continue them to complex sources, where the result may also be interpreted as related to a complex gravity solution (with complex boundary conditions related to the complex sources). Analytic continuations of this type may be dangerous, both because poles or other features may be encountered when shifting the contours where the gravitational fields are valued, and because subleading contributions for real sources may become dominant for some complex-valued sources. In this paper we discuss in detail an example of the latter problem, and suggest a prescription in which only (complex) gravitational solutions which do not have any instabilities related to non-perturbative contributions becoming large should be included. It would be useful to find a rigorous justification for this prescription, by carefully analyzing the analytic continuations that are involved.

•

Our results suggest that, as the Bethe Ansatz prescription does on the CFT side, the gravitational computation of the supersymmetric partition function on
S^{3}\times S^{1}
may perhaps be localized to a discrete sum over supersymmetric gravity solutions. It would be interesting to perform such a localization on the gravity side, at least in the supergravity approximation and perhaps along the lines of
[
63
,
64
,
65
]
, and to see if it reproduces the results we found in this paper.

•

In this paper we only compared the leading exponents in the contributions of various solutions to the path integral — both for the classical gravity solutions (contributing to the logarithm of the partition function at order
N^{2}
), and for the non-perturbative corrections coming from D3-branes. From the Bethe Ansatz we can also find the subleading contributions to the logarithm of the partition function, of order
\log(N)
(already considered in
[
21
]
) and of order
1
, and we seem to find no further perturbative corrections in
1/N
. It would be interesting to reproduce these corrections from one-loop fluctuations around our gravitational solutions, and to confirm that higher-loop corrections are absent on the gravity side. In the case of the D3-branes, we can again compute on the field theory side also the coefficients of the exponentials that we matched, and it would be interesting to match those with multiplicities of D3-brane solutions on the gravity side.

•

So far there is no classification of all solutions to the Bethe Ansatz equations for the
SU(N)

\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theories, and for finite
N
there are certainly additional solutions beyond the ones we analyzed, including continuous families of solutions
[
16
,
66
,
67
]
. It would be interesting to classify all the solutions, and to understand whether some of the other solutions might have an interpretation as classical gravity solutions as well.

There are also many possible generalizations of our analysis:

•

We analyzed only the case of equal chemical potentials for the two angular momenta, since in this case the Bethe Ansatz computation of the index is the simplest. The Bethe Ansatz computation works whenever the ratio between the two angular momenta is rational
[
68
,
20
]
, but for general rational values, extra parameters appear in the sum over Bethe Ansatz solutions, and the number of these parameters is of order
N
, so that in the large
N
limit there is an exponentially large number of terms appearing in the sum. On the other hand, on the gravity side nothing drastic seems to change when the chemical potentials are not equal (or even when their ratio is not rational), though some of the details of our analysis may be modified. This suggests that the extra parameters should be summed over before matching to the gravity side, in order to still have a matching between the two sides. It would be interesting to perform these sums and to match the Bethe Ansatz results to a sum over gravity solutions also for other ratios of chemical potentials. Note that it is not obvious if this should be possible or not; even if the gravity computation localizes as mentioned above, it is not guaranteed that the same sum over solutions will appear on the gravity side as on the field theory side (only the full partition function has to match). However, our results suggest that such a mapping may be possible also for general ratios of the angular momenta chemical potentials.

•

Many of our computations, both on the field theory side (using the Bethe Ansatz) and on the gravity side, may be generalized to general theories with 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal symmetry. Indeed, the leading contributions from both sides have been matched in some other cases
[
14

20

. It would be interesting to check if also in these other cases, the full sum over Bethe Ansatz solutions may be matched in the large
N
limit to a sum over gravitational solutions, as we found for the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theories. It may also be possible to generalize the analysis to supersymmetric

partition functions of
d

[

44

70

•

Non-perturbative corrections to the Euclidean partition function coming from wrapped branes, similar to the ones we found, presumably exist for all black hole solutions in any dimension. It would be interesting to evaluate these contributions and, where possible, to match them to the field theory side.

2.1
The sphere thermal partition function

In this paper we would like to match the partition function of
\mathcal{N}=4

Using

(2.1)

We can add chemical potentials for each of these five charges, and the thermal partition function of the theory on

Z=\Tr{}\left(e^{-\beta\{\mathcal{Q},\bar{\mathcal{Q}}\}}\,e^{\pi iR_{3}}\,e^{\sum_{i}\beta(\Omega_{i}-1)\left(J_{i}+\frac{1}{2}R_{3}\right)}\,e^{\beta(\Phi_{1}-1)\frac{R_{1}-R_{3}}{2}}\,e^{\beta(\Phi_{2}-1)\frac{R_{2}-R_{3}}{2}}\right)\;.

(2.11)

For the charge assignments of our theory, the factor
e^{\pi iR_{3}}
is equivalent to
(-1)^{F}
. Note also that in the new expression, since
(R_{1}-R_{3})
and
(R_{2}-R_{3})
are even and
J_{i}+\frac{1}{2}R_{3}
is an integer, the periodicities of the remaining four chemical potentials are reduced to
(2\pi i/\beta)
.
Defining new chemical potentials for the four independent charges that, given (
2.8
), we have access to, namely

\sigma=\frac{\beta\,(\Omega_{1}-1)}{2\pi i}\;,\qquad\tau=\frac{\beta\,(\Omega_{2}-1)}{2\pi i}\;,\qquad\Delta_{a}=\frac{\beta\,(\Phi_{a}-1)}{2\pi i}\quad\text{for }a=1,2\;,

(2.12)

one finally finds that

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\Tr{}\left((-1)^{F}\,e^{-\beta\{\mathcal{Q},\bar{\mathcal{Q}}\}}\,e^{2\pi i\sigma\left(J_{1}+\frac{1}{2}R_{3}\right)}\,e^{2\pi i\tau\left(J_{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{3}\right)}\,e^{2\pi i\Delta_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}}\,e^{2\pi i\Delta_{2}\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\right)

(2.13)

\displaystyle=e^{-\beta E_{0}}\;\mathcal{I}(\sigma,\tau,\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\;,

where
\mathcal{I}
is the superconformal index (
2.6
) defined above, and
E_{0}
(which is related to the charges of the ground state on
S^{3}
) is called here the Casimir energy.
5
5

5

If our renormalization scheme requires additional counterterms, they would appear similarly to
E_{0}
.

This relation between the thermal partition function (
2.2
) (when (
2.8
) holds) and the index will enable us to relate the index that we compute in the field theory, to computations of the
S^{1}\times S^{3}
partition function using the gravitational dual.

Note that, in general,
E_{0}
is scheme dependent. The authors of
[
75
]
argued that supersymmetry dictates (if a supersymmetric regularization is used) a specific choice of renormalization scheme which fixes the value of
E_{0}
to a specific value
E_{\text{SUSY}}
depending on the chemical potentials, which is then called the supersymmetric Casimir energy. In
[
5
]
a localization computation verified (
2.13
) when (
2.8
) applies and found the supersymmetric Casimir energy.

Both of these papers relied on coupling the theory to curved space using the R-multiplet. Recently, it was argued that for theories with an anomalous
U(1)_{R}
symmetry (in the sense of non-conservation of the current in the presence of a background field, as in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM) this coupling induces supercurrent anomalies, see
[
76
,
77
,
78
,
79
,
80
,
81
,
82
]
. They suggest that additional counterterms should be added to the action in order to restore supersymmetry. These counterterms could change the value of
E_{0}
. A similar argument was made in
[
83
]
, where it was suggested that in order to regularize the theory in a supersymmetric and diffeomorphism invariant way one should add an additional counterterm to the action. This changes (
2.13
) to
Z=e^{-\beta W}e^{-\beta E_{\text{SUSY}}}\mathcal{I}
, where
W
is the additional counterterm and
E_{\text{SUSY}}
is the same as before.

We will avoid this discussion by considering the ratio between the partition function and the contribution of the vacuum, which equals the index
\mathcal{I}
in every consistent scheme. On the gravity side we realize this through ‘‘background subtraction’’, namely regularizing the gravitational action by subtracting the contribution coming from empty thermal AdS, and we compare the result of this regularization to the computation of the index.
6
6

6

Note that background subtraction is believed to give the same results for the ratio we compute as a more precise computation using holographic renormalization in the bulk, as in
[
69
]
. With the specific regularization used there,
E_{0}
matches the usual Casimir energy in a free CFT, see also Appendix A of
[
75
]
(and our footnote

).

3
Field theory analysis

We are interested in the superconformal index (
2.6
) of the four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory with gauge group
SU(N)
.

Bethe Ansatz formulation.

The index is independent of the gauge coupling, and thus can be computed exactly
[
84
,
85
,
49
,
50
]
in terms of a certain contour integral. However, in order to extract the large
N
limit, it is convenient to consider the so-called Bethe Ansatz formula
[
86
,
68
]
. In this paper we will mainly be interested in the case of two equal angular fugacities:

p=q\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\sigma=\tau\;.

(3.1)

In this case the Bethe Ansatz formula reads
7
7

7

This expression was derived in
[
68
]
assuming that the Bethe Ansatz equations have isolated solutions. It has been noticed in
[
16
,
66
,
67
]
that for the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory, the equations can also have continuous families of solutions. In this case the expression of their contributions should likely be modified. In this paper we will ignore the contributions of those continuous families, leaving their study to future work.

\mathcal{I}(q,y_{1},y_{2})=\kappa_{N}\sum_{\hat{u}\,\in\,\text{BAEs}}\mathcal{Z}(\hat{u};\Delta,\tau)\,H(\hat{u};\Delta,\tau)^{-1}\;.

(3.2)

The sum is over the solution set
\{\hat{u}\}
to a system of transcendental equations, dubbed here Bethe Ansatz Equations (BAEs). Defining the Bethe Ansatz (BA) operators

Q_{i}(u;\Delta,\tau)\,\equiv\,e^{6\pi i\sum_{j}u_{ij}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{0}(u_{ji}+\Delta_{1};\tau)\,\theta_{0}(u_{ji}+\Delta_{2};\tau)\,\theta_{0}(u_{ji}-\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2};\tau)}{\theta_{0}(u_{ij}+\Delta_{1};\tau)\,\theta_{0}(u_{ij}+\Delta_{2};\tau)\,\theta_{0}(u_{ij}-\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2};\tau)}

(3.3)

for
i=1,\dots,N
, the BA equations are given by

1=\frac{Q_{i}}{Q_{N}}\qquad\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,N-1\;.

(3.4)

The quasi-elliptic function
\theta_{0}
is defined in Appendix
A
, while
u_{ij}=u_{i}-u_{j}
. The unknowns are the “complexified
SU(N)
holonomies”
u_{i}
living on a torus of modular parameter
\tau
, namely with identifications

u_{i}\,\sim\,u_{i}+1\,\sim\,u_{i}+\tau\qquad\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,N-1\;,

(3.5)

while
u_{N}
is fixed by the constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}=0
. The BAEs (
3.4
) are invariant under such shifts.
In fact, a stronger property holds:
Q_{i}
are invariant under shifts of the components of the antisymmetric tensor
u_{ij}
by
1
or
\tau
, even relaxing the condition that
u_{ij}=u_{i}-u_{j}
.

It was proven in
[
68
]
that only the solutions that are not invariant under any non-trivial element of the Weyl group of
SU(N)
(namely, only solutions with all
u_{i}
different on the torus) actually contribute to the sum in (
3.2
). Moreover, it was shown in
[
67
]
that only the solutions to a more restrictive set of equations,

Q_{i}=(-1)^{N-1}\qquad\qquad\text{for }i=1,\dots,N

(3.6)

and dubbed “reduced Bethe Ansatz equations”, actually contribute to the index. Notice that
\prod_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}=1
identically. We stress that from each solution in terms of
u_{ij}
on the torus, one gets up to
N^{2}
solutions in terms of
u_{i}
, related by a shift of the “center of mass” of the first
N-1
components. Some of those, however, could be equivalent up to the Weyl group action, and thus the exact multiplicity should be determined by a case-by-case analysis.

The prefactor in (
3.2
) is

\kappa_{N}=\frac{1}{N!}\left(\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}\,\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta_{1};\tau,\tau)\,\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta_{2};\tau,\tau)}{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2};\tau,\tau)}\right)^{N-1}

(3.7)

where
(z;q)_{\infty}
is the
q
-Pochhammer symbol while
\widetilde{\Gamma}(u;\sigma,\tau)
is the elliptic gamma function, defined in Appendix
A
. The function
\mathcal{Z}
is given by

\mathcal{Z}(u;\Delta,\tau)=\prod_{i\neq j}^{N}\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}(u_{ij}+\Delta_{1};\tau,\tau)\,\widetilde{\Gamma}(u_{ij}+\Delta_{2};\tau,\tau)}{\widetilde{\Gamma}(u_{ij}+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2};\tau,\tau)\,\widetilde{\Gamma}(u_{ij};\tau,\tau)}

(3.8)

and it coincides (using (
A.16
)) with the integrand of the standard integral formula for the superconformal index. Finally,
H
is a Jacobian defined as

H=\det\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\frac{\partial\log(Q_{i}/Q_{N})}{\partial u_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1,\dots,N-1}\,\equiv\,\det(\mathcal{A}_{ij})\;.

(3.9)

Here we introduced the
(N-1)\times(N-1)
Jacobian matrix
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
for later convenience.

Notice that
Q_{i}
,
\kappa_{N}
,
\mathcal{Z}
and
H
are all invariant under integer shifts of
\tau
,
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, implying that the superconformal index (
3.2
) is a single-valued function of the fugacities. This is also apparent from the Hamiltonian definition (
2.6
), as already noted.

The superconformal index of the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory with gauge group
U(N)
is related to the one for gauge group
SU(N)
by the simple relation

\mathcal{I}_{U(N)}=\mathcal{I}_{U(1)}\,\mathcal{I}_{SU(N)}\;,

(3.10)

where

\mathcal{I}_{U(1)}(p,q,y_{1},y_{2})=(p;p)_{\infty}\,(q;q)_{\infty}\,\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta_{1};\sigma,\tau)\,\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta_{2};\sigma,\tau)}{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2};\sigma,\tau)}

(3.11)

is the index of the free
\mathcal{N}=4

U(1)
theory.

Hong-Liu (HL) solutions.

The full set of solutions to (
3.6
) is not known (see also footnote
7
), however, a large set was found in
[
33
,
62
]
and we will refer to them as HL solutions. They are labelled by three integers:

\{m,n,r\}\qquad\text{such that}\qquad N=m\cdot n\;,\qquad 0\leq r<n\;.

(3.12)

The solutions are

u_{j}\equiv u_{\hat{\jmath}\hat{k}}=\bar{u}+\frac{\hat{\jmath}}{m}+\frac{\hat{k}}{n}\left(\tau+\frac{r}{m}\right)\;.

(3.13)

Here we have decomposed the index
j=0,\dots,N-1
into the indices
\hat{\jmath}=0,\dots,m-1
and
\hat{k}=0,\dots,n-1
. Morever,
\bar{u}
is a constant chosen in such a way to solve the
SU(N)
constraint (but recall that what enters in all formulas are the differences
u_{ij}=u_{i}-u_{j}
). Notice that these solutions are in one-to-one correspondence with subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
of order
N
. It turns out that each HL solution has multiplicity
N
(besides the
N!
coming from permutations of
u_{i}
’s), corresponding to the inequivalent solutions to the
SU(N)
constraint.

The BAEs (
3.6
) are invariant under
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
modular transformations of the torus, namely under the generators

T:\begin{cases}\tau\mapsto\tau+1\\
u\mapsto u\end{cases}\qquad S:\begin{cases}\tau\mapsto-1/\tau\\
u\mapsto u/\tau\end{cases}\qquad C:\begin{cases}\tau\mapsto\tau\\
u\mapsto-u\;.\end{cases}

(3.14)

It follows that the HL solutions form orbits under
PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})
, completely classified by the integer
d=\gcd(m,n,r)
. The action of
PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})
is given by

T:\{m,n,r\}\mapsto\{m,n,r+m\}\;,\qquad S:\{m,n,r\}\mapsto\left\{\gcd(n,r)\,,\,\frac{m\,n}{\gcd(n,r)}\,,\,\frac{m(n-r)}{\gcd(n,r)}\right\}

(3.15)

where the last entry of
\{m^{\prime},n^{\prime},r^{\prime}\}
is understood mod
n^{\prime}
.

3.1
Contributions of Hong-Liu solutions

We will compute the contribution of each of the HL solutions (

will remain somehow implicit, we will be able to obtain a very explicit expression for

It is convenient to rewrite

(3.17)

We also define

\xi_{j}=e^{2\pi iv_{j}}\;,\qquad\qquad\zeta_{k}=e^{2\pi iw_{k}}\;.

(3.18)

3.1.1
Elliptic gamma functions

To evaluate the contribution to the index, we need to compute

(3.21)

The case

3.1.2
The Jacobian

The Jacobian was defined in (
3.9
). It is convenient to introduce an auxiliary chemical potential
\Delta_{3}
defined by

\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Delta_{a}-\sigma-\tau\in\mathbb{Z}

(3.28)

(and then set
\sigma=\tau
), in order to make manifest the action of the Weyl group of
SU(3)
— the global symmetry contained in
SU(4)_{R}
that commutes with the preserved supercharges
\mathcal{Q},\bar{\mathcal{Q}}
. We can then rewrite the BA operators (
3.3
) as

Q_{i}=e^{-2\pi i\sum_{j}u_{ij}}\prod_{a=1}^{3}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{0}(u_{ji}+\Delta_{a};\tau)}{\theta_{0}(u_{ij}+\Delta_{a};\tau)}\;.

(3.29)

With a few manipulations that we report in Appendix
B.3
, the
(N-1)\times(N-1)
Jacobian matrix
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
can be compactly written as

\mathcal{A}_{ij}=N\,b_{N}\,\mathcal{B}_{ij}+\mathcal{C}_{ij}\;.

(3.30)

Here

\displaystyle b_{N}

\displaystyle=-4+\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left[2m\Delta_{a}+1+\mathcal{G}\left(0,\frac{N\Delta_{a}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{n}{\check{\tau}}\right)\right]

(3.31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}+1

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{3}\biggl[\mathcal{G}(u_{iN},\Delta_{a};\tau)+\mathcal{G}(u_{jN},\Delta_{a};\tau)-\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{a};\tau)-\mathcal{G}(0,\Delta_{a};\tau)\biggr]

with
i,j=1,\dots,N-1
, where the function
\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)
is defined in Appendix

. Notice that
\det(\mathcal{B})=N
and
(\mathcal{B}^{-1})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-1/N
. To compute the determinant, we factorize
\mathcal{A}
and obtain

\log(H)\,\equiv\,\log\bigl(\det(\mathcal{A})\bigr)=N\log(N)+(N-1)\log(b_{N})+\log\left[\det\left(\mathbbm{1}+\frac{b_{N}^{-1}}{N}\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\right)\right]\;.

(3.32)

The last term of this expression is still rather implicit, however this will be good enough to compute the large
N
limit.

3.1.3
Total contribution

We denote the total contribution of the HL solution
\{m,n,r\}
to the index as

\mathcal{I}_{\{m,n,r\}}=N\cdot N!\cdot\kappa_{N}\,\mathcal{Z}(u;\Delta,\tau)\,H(u;\Delta,\tau)^{-1}\Big|_{(\ref{HL solution u_ij})}\;.

(3.33)

The factor
N\cdot N!
takes into account the multiplicity of the solution
\{m,n,r\}
: indeed
N!
comes from the Weyl group action that permutes the eigenvalues
u_{i}
, while
N
comes from the inequivalent choices of sets of points
\{u_{i}\}
on the torus that give rise to the same
u_{ij}
’s, as discussed after (
3.13
). Such
N
inequivalent choices correspond to shifts of the center of mass of the first
N-1
holonomies, and can be parametrized, for
(\hat{\jmath},\hat{k})\neq(m,n)
, as

u_{\hat{\jmath}\hat{k}}^{(\ell)}=\bar{u}+\frac{\hat{\jmath}}{m}+\frac{\hat{k}}{n}\left(\tau+\frac{r}{m}\right)+\frac{\ell_{1}\tau+\ell_{2}}{N}

(3.34)

with
\ell_{1}=0,\dots,m-1
and
\ell_{2}=0,\dots,n-1
.
As discussed before, all these solutions give exactly the same contribution to
\mathcal{Z}
and
H
.

Using that
\widetilde{\Gamma}(u+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2};\sigma,\tau)^{-1}=\widetilde{\Gamma}(-u+\Delta_{3};\sigma,\tau)
and putting everything together, we eventually find

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \,\rule{0.0pt}{20.00003pt}&\log\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{\{m,n,r\}}\bigr)=\mathcal{P}(m\Delta,\check{\tau})+\log(N)+2\log\left[\frac{\bigl(\check{q}^{n};\check{q}^{n})_{\infty}^{N}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\right]-N\log(\check{\tau})-(N-1)\log(b_{N})\\
&\;\;+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left[-N\log\left[\theta_{0}\biggl(\frac{N\Delta_{a}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{n}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)\right]-\log\left(\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta_{a};\tau,\tau)\right)+m\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log\left(\frac{\psi\left(\frac{k+1+m\Delta_{a}}{\check{\tau}/n}\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{k-m\Delta_{a}}{\check{\tau}/n}\right)}\right)\right]\;\\
&\;\;+\frac{\pi im}{6}\left(\sum_{a}m\Delta_{a}-2\check{\tau}+\frac{3}{2}\right)-\log\left[\det\left(\mathbbm{1}+\frac{b_{N}^{-1}}{N}\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\right)\right]\;.\rule[-18.00005pt]{0.0pt}{10.00002pt}\end{aligned}}

(3.35)

Recall that
\check{\tau}=m\tau+r
and
\check{q}=e^{-2\pi i/\check{\tau}}
.
Here
\mathcal{P}(m\Delta,\check{\tau})
is the contribution of order
N^{2}
:

\mathcal{P}(m\Delta,\check{\tau})=\frac{\pi iN^{2}}{3m}\left[\sum_{a=1}^{3}\frac{(\check{\tau}-m\Delta_{a})\bigl(\check{\tau}-m\Delta_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)(\check{\tau}-m\Delta_{a}-1)}{\check{\tau}^{2}}-\frac{\check{\tau}\bigl(\check{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)(\check{\tau}-1)}{\check{\tau}^{2}}\right]\;.

(3.36)

3.2
The large

Presumably this criterion can be justified by a careful analysis of the analytic continuation to complex-valued solutions — in particular through the study of Lefschetz thimbles — but we will not attempt to do this here. We will show below that with that criterion, the acceptable shifted solutions (for arbitrary chemical potentials) precisely match the

5
Wrapped D3-branes

Up to now we considered the classical on-shell action of our Euclidean solutions. In general, the action receives quantum corrections coming from loops of the gravity fields in these backgrounds. 6
Stable gravity solutions and the index

Let us now find the stable gravitational backgrounds that contribute to the partition function with boundary conditions given by chemical potentials
(\sigma=\tau,\,\Delta_{1,2,3})

We shall start by considering gravitational backgrounds in the first branch. Consider the stability condition (

24

If we now consider gravitational solutions on the second branch of (

4.33

The bottom line is that for each value of
\tau_{\mathrm{g}}

\Delta_{a}

Moreover, given the values of the

It would be interesting to match also the coefficients in front of the exponentials in the various non-perturbative corrections between the two sides, but this lies beyond the scope of this paper.

When there are fixed points, the orbifolds have light twisted sector states living there, while otherwise all twisted sector states are heavy. Note that all of our orbifolds involve a shift action on the Euclidean time circle. Thus, they are not related to any AdS

Appendix A
Special functions

We will use fugacities and chemical potentials related by

z=e^{2\pi iu}\;,\qquad\qquad p=e^{2\pi i\sigma}\;,\qquad\qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\;.

(A.1)

\boldsymbol{q}
-Pochhammer symbol.

We use the standard notations

The expression

(z;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\,q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}\,z^{n}=\exp\left[-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\,\frac{z^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\right]\;,

(A.3)

m\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log\Biggl(\frac{\psi\bigl(\frac{k+1+m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}+\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{n}\bigr)}{\psi\bigl(\frac{k-m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}+\frac{k_{2}-k_{1}}{n}\bigr)}\Biggr)=m\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{\ell}\left(\frac{k-m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}\tilde{y}^{\ell}\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.-\frac{k+1+m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}\Bigl(\frac{\check{q}}{\tilde{y}}\Bigr)^{\ell}\right)+\frac{1}{\ell}\left(\frac{k_{2}-k_{1}}{n}+\frac{1}{2\pi i\,\ell}\right)\bigl(\tilde{y}^{\ell}-(\check{q}/\tilde{y})^{\ell}\bigr)\right]\biggl(\frac{\tilde{\zeta}_{k_{1}}}{\tilde{\zeta}_{k_{2}}}\biggr)^{\ell}\check{q}^{k\ell}\;.

(B.11)

Then we use that the following sum vanishes:

B_{\ell,n}=\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}(k_{1}-k_{2})\,e^{2\pi i\ell(k_{1}-k_{2})/n}=0\;.

(B.12)

Therefore

(\mathrm{\ref{piece 2}})=m\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{\ell}\left(\frac{k-m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}\tilde{y}^{\ell}-\frac{k+1+m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}(\check{q}/\tilde{y})^{\ell}\right)+\frac{1}{2\pi i\,\ell^{2}}\bigl(\tilde{y}^{\ell}-(\check{q}/\tilde{y})^{\ell}\bigr)\right]\check{q}^{k\ell}A_{\ell,n}\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[m\log\left(\frac{\psi\bigl(\frac{n(k+1+m\Delta)}{\check{\tau}}\bigr)}{\psi\bigl(\frac{n(k-m\Delta)}{\check{\tau}}\bigr)}\right)-N\log\left(\frac{\psi\bigl(\frac{k+1+m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}\bigr)}{\psi\bigl(\frac{k-m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}\bigr)}\right)\right]\;.

(B.13)

Finally we put all terms together. The expression simplifies using

\displaystyle N\log\left(\widetilde{\Gamma}(m\Delta;m\tau,m\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=-\pi iN\mathcal{Q}\bigl(m\Delta;\check{\tau},\check{\tau}\bigr)

(B.14)

\displaystyle-N\log\biggl[\theta_{0}\biggl(\frac{m\Delta}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)\biggr]+N\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log\left(\frac{\psi\bigl(\frac{k+1+m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}\bigr)}{\psi\bigl(\frac{k-m\Delta}{\check{\tau}}\bigr)}\right)\;.

We obtain:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=-\pi im\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{Q}\left(\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{n}\,\check{\tau}+m\Delta;\check{\tau},\check{\tau}\right)-N\log\left(\widetilde{\Gamma}(\Delta;\tau,\tau)\right)

(B.15)

\displaystyle-N\log\biggl[\theta_{0}\biggl(\frac{N\Delta}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{n}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)\biggr]+m\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log\left(\frac{\psi\bigl(\frac{k+1+m\Delta}{\check{\tau}/n}\bigr)}{\psi\bigl(\frac{k-m\Delta}{\check{\tau}/n}\bigr)}\right)\;.

The sum over
k_{1},k_{2}
can be performed exactly:

-\pi im\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{Q}\left(\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{n}\,\check{\tau}+m\Delta;\check{\tau},\check{\tau}\right)=-\frac{\pi im}{6}\bigl(\check{\tau}-m\Delta-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\\
{}+\pi i\,\frac{N^{2}}{m}\,\frac{\bigl(\check{\tau}-m\Delta\bigr)\bigl(\check{\tau}-m\Delta-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigl(\check{\tau}-m\Delta-1\bigr)}{3\check{\tau}^{2}}\;.

(B.16)

We thus obtain the exact expression in (
3.23
).

B.2
The case
\boldsymbol{\Delta=0}

The case
\Delta=0
requires a separate treatment. Using (
A.9
), (
A.15
) and (
A.16
), we find

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\neq j}^{N}\log\bigl(\theta_{0}(u_{ij};\tau)\bigr)\Big|_{(\ref{HL solution u_ij})}

(B.17)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}\bigl(v_{j_{1}j_{2}}+w_{k_{1}k_{2}};\tau\bigr)\Bigr)-n\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}^{m-1}\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}(v_{j_{1}j_{2}};\tau)\Bigr)\;.

The sum in the second term on the second line is computed as follows:

\displaystyle-n\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}^{m-1}\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}(v_{j_{1}j_{2}};\tau)\Bigr)=-n\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}^{m-1}\left[\log\left(1-\frac{\xi_{j_{1}}}{\xi_{j_{2}}}\right)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\log\left(1-\frac{\xi_{j_{1}}}{\xi_{j_{2}}}q^{k}\right)\right]

(B.18)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-N\log(m)+2n\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell}\,A_{\ell,m}q^{k\ell}=-N\log(m)+2N\log\biggl[\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{\infty}}\biggr]\;.

We used that
\sum_{j\neq k}^{m}\log\left(1-e^{2\pi i(j-k)/m}\right)=m\log(m)
. The sum in the first term can be computed using the plethystic expansion (
A.8
) which converges for
0<\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{n}\im(\tau)<\im(\tau)
. This is not satisfied for all
k_{1},k_{2}
, therefore we split the sum using (
A.9
) as follows:

\displaystyle-\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=0}^{m-1}

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k_{1}>k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}\bigl(v_{j_{1}j_{2}}+w_{k_{1}k_{2}};\tau\bigr)\Bigr)+\sum_{k_{1}<k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl(-e^{2\pi i(v_{j_{1}j_{2}}+w_{k_{1}k_{2}})}\,\theta_{0}\bigl(-v_{j_{1}j_{2}}-w_{k_{1}k_{2}};\tau\bigr)\Bigr)\right]

\displaystyle\quad=-2\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{k_{1}>k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}\bigl(v_{j_{1}j_{2}}+w_{k_{1}k_{2}};\tau\bigr)\Bigr)-m^{2}\sum_{k_{1}<k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl(-e^{2\pi iw_{k_{1}k_{2}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad=-2\sum_{k_{1}>k_{2}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\ell}\,\frac{\left(\frac{\zeta_{k_{1}}}{\zeta_{k_{2}}}\right)^{\ell}+\left(\frac{\zeta_{k_{2}}}{\zeta_{k_{1}}}\right)^{\ell}q^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}\bigl(A_{\ell,m}+m\bigr)-\sum_{k_{1}<k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl((-1)^{m^{2}}e^{2\pi im^{2}w_{k_{1}k_{2}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad=-2m\sum_{k_{1}>k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}\bigl(mw_{k_{1}k_{2}};m\tau\bigr)\Bigr)-\sum_{k_{1}<k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl((-1)^{m^{2}}e^{2\pi im^{2}w_{k_{1}k_{2}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad=-m\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}\bigl(mw_{k_{1}k_{2}};m\tau\bigr)\Bigr)\;.

(B.19)

In the last equality we used
(-1)^{m^{2}-m}=1
. The sum can be computed using the modular properties (
A.10
):

\displaystyle-m\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\log\Bigl(\theta_{0}\bigl(mw_{k_{1}k_{2}};m\tau+r\bigr)\Bigr)

(B.20)

\displaystyle=-\pi im\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{(k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}}{n^{2}}\check{\tau}-\frac{1}{6\check{\tau}}-\frac{\check{\tau}}{6}\right]-m\sum_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}^{n-1}\log\biggl[\theta_{0}\left(\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{n};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\right)\biggr]

\displaystyle=\pi iN(n-1)\frac{\check{\tau}\bigl(\check{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)(\check{\tau}-1)}{3\check{\tau}^{2}}+\pi im(n-1)\frac{\check{\tau}}{6}-N\log(n)+2N\log\biggl[\frac{(\check{q};\check{q})_{\infty}}{(\check{q}^{n};\check{q}^{n})_{\infty}}\biggr]\;.

The computation of the sum in the second line is as in (
B.18
).
Finally, we use the modular properties (
A.5
) of the
q
-Pochhammer symbol to obtain

2N\log\left[\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{\infty}}\,\frac{(\check{q};\check{q})_{\infty}}{(\check{q}^{n};\check{q}^{n})_{\infty}}\right]=2N\log\biggl[\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(\check{q}^{n};\check{q}^{n})_{\infty}}\biggr]+N\log(-i\check{\tau})+\frac{\pi iN}{6}\left(\check{\tau}+\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\right)\;.

(B.21)

Recall that
\im(\check{\tau})>0
, therefore
\log(-i\check{\tau})=\log(\check{\tau})-i\pi/2
with the logarithm in its principal determination.
Putting the various pieces together we find (
3.25
).

B.3
The Jacobian
\boldsymbol{H}

Given the BA operators
Q_{i}
written as in (
3.29
), we compute

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\partial\log(Q_{i})}{\partial u_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial u_{ik}}{\partial u_{j}}\left[-1+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\mathcal{G}(u_{ik},\Delta_{a})\right]\;,

(B.22)

where we defined the function (see also Appendix
A
)

\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\log\left(\frac{\theta_{0}(-u+\Delta;\tau)}{\theta_{0}(u+\Delta;\tau)}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left[\frac{\theta_{0}^{\prime}(u+\Delta;\tau)}{\theta_{0}(u+\Delta;\tau)}+\frac{\theta_{0}^{\prime}(-u+\Delta;\tau)}{\theta_{0}(-u+\Delta;\tau)}\right]

(B.23)

with implicit dependence on
\tau
(unless specified). This is an even function of
u
, namely
\mathcal{G}(-u,\Delta)=\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta)
. Since we treat
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N-1}
as the independent variables while
u_{N}
is fixed by the
SU(N)
constraint, we have
\partial_{u_{j}}u_{ik}=\delta_{ij}-\delta_{kj}-\delta_{iN}+\delta_{kN}
. Therefore

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\partial\log(Q_{i})}{\partial u_{j}}=(\delta_{ij}-\delta_{iN})\left(-N+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\mathcal{G}(u_{ik},\Delta_{a})\right)+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\biggl[\mathcal{G}(u_{iN},\Delta_{a})-\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{a})\biggr]\;.

(B.24)

This leads to the following expression for the Jacobian matrix:

\mathcal{A}_{ij}=-N(1+\delta_{ij})+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\biggl[\delta_{ij}\,\mathcal{G}(u_{ik},\Delta_{a})+\mathcal{G}(u_{kN},\Delta_{a})\biggr]\\
{}+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\biggl[\mathcal{G}(u_{iN},\Delta_{a})+\mathcal{G}(u_{jN},\Delta_{a})-\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{a})-\mathcal{G}(0,\Delta_{a})\biggr]\;,

(B.25)

where
i,j=1,\dots,N-1
.
Let us compute the following quantity:

\Upsilon\,\equiv\,\sum_{l=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}\bigl(u_{i_{1}l},\Delta_{a};\tau\bigr)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{G}\left(\frac{j_{1}-j}{m}+\frac{k_{1}-k}{n}\,\frac{\check{\tau}}{m},\Delta_{a};\tau\right)\;.

(B.26)

The sum over
j
is computed easily, using the series expansion (
A.23
). One has to be careful about the domain of convergence of the series. We sum over
j
with
k
fixed, however there is no domain of
\Delta_{a},\tau
such that the full series is convergent. One can instead break the series of
\mathcal{G}
in two, each one convergent in a different domain, and recombine the two pieces at the end. Exploiting

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{2\pi i(j_{1}-j)\ell/m}=\begin{cases}m\qquad&\text{for }\ell=0\mod m\\
0&\text{for }\ell\neq 0\mod m\;,\end{cases}

(B.27)

we obtain

\Upsilon=m\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{G}\left(\frac{k_{1}-k}{n}\,\check{\tau},m\Delta_{a};\check{\tau}\right)\;.

(B.28)

Then we use the modular property (
A.25
) and perform the second sum. We obtain

\Upsilon=N\,\frac{2m\Delta_{a}+1-\check{\tau}}{\check{\tau}}+\frac{N}{\check{\tau}}\,\mathcal{G}\left(0,\frac{N\Delta_{a}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{n}{\check{\tau}}\right)\;.

(B.29)

Notice in particular that
\Upsilon
does not depend on
i_{1}
or equivalently on
(j_{1},k_{1})
. Moreover,
\Upsilon
is invariant under
m\Delta_{a}\to m\Delta_{a}+1
. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}_{ij}=N(1+\delta_{ij})\left[-4+\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left(2m\Delta_{a}+1+\mathcal{G}\Bigl(0,\frac{N\Delta_{a}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{n}{\check{\tau}}\Bigr)\right)\right]\\
{}+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\biggl[\mathcal{G}(u_{iN},\Delta_{a})+\mathcal{G}(u_{jN},\Delta_{a})-\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{a})-\mathcal{G}(0,\Delta_{a})\biggr]\;.

(B.30)

Here we recognize the expression in (
3.30
).

B.4
Perturbative corrections

We give some evidence that the term
\log\bigl[\det\bigl(\mathbbm{1}+\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}/b_{N}N\bigr)\bigr]
in (
3.47
) and (
3.49
) only leads to a contribution of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
and to non-perturbative corrections in the large
N
limit, but no
1/N
perturbative corrections. In order to do that, we expand

-\log\biggl[\det\biggl(\mathbbm{1}+\frac{\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}}{b_{N}N}\biggr)\bigg]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\Tr\biggl[\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}}{b_{N}N}\biggr)^{\!\!\ell\;}\biggr]

(B.31)

and compute the first few terms.

First order.

We compute
\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\bigr)
. The two matrices
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are defined in (
3.31
) and have size
(N-1)\times(N-1)
. Using the fact that
\mathcal{G}
is an even function, the trace can be recast as

\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{C}_{ii}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{C}_{ij}=\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{a};\tau)-N\mathcal{G}(0,\Delta_{a};\tau)\right]\;.

(B.32)

The sum inside square brackets was already computed in (
B.26
)–(
B.29
) and it equals
\Upsilon
, therefore

\displaystyle\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\bigr)

\displaystyle=N\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Biggl[\frac{2[m\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}-\check{\tau}+1}{\check{\tau}}+\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\,\mathcal{G}\biggl(0,\frac{[m\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}/n};-\frac{n}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)-\mathcal{G}\bigl(0,\Delta_{a};\tau\bigr)\Biggr]

(B.33)

\displaystyle=N\,\frac{\check{\tau}\pm 1}{\check{\tau}}+N\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Biggl[\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\,\mathcal{G}\biggl(0,\frac{[m\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}/n};-\frac{n}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)-\mathcal{G}\bigl(0,\Delta_{a};\tau\bigr)\Biggr]\;.

In the first line we used that
\Upsilon
is invariant under integer shifts of
m\Delta_{a}
, while the
\pm
signs in the second line refer to the 1
st
and 2
nd
case of parameter space, respectively. Then, using the large
N
behavior of
\mathcal{G}
in (
3.2
) and

b_{N}\bigl([m\Delta]_{\check{\tau}}\bigr)=\pm\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N})\;,

(B.34)

we obtain the large
N
result

\frac{\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\bigr)}{b_{N}N}=1\pm\check{\tau}\Biggl(1-\sum_{a=1}^{3}\mathcal{G}\bigl(0,\Delta_{a};\tau\bigr)\Biggr)+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N})

(B.35)

where, once again, the
\pm
signs refer to the 1
st
and 2
nd
case, respectively. We see that this quantity only receives non-perturbative corrections at large
N
.

Second order.

We compute
\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\bigr)
. Using that
\mathcal{C}
is a symmetric matrix, with some lengthy algebra we obtain

\displaystyle\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N-1}(\mathcal{C}_{ij})^{2}-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{C}_{ik}\mathcal{C}_{jk}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{C}_{ij}\right)^{2}

(B.36)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{a};\tau)\,\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{b};\tau)-\Upsilon(\Delta_{a};\tau)\,\Upsilon(\Delta_{b},\tau)\right]\;.

This expression is valid for any HL solution
\{m,n,r\}
. However, evaluating the summation in the second line by brute force is quite complicated and here we will content ourselves with the T-transformed solutions,
i.e.
, with the case
m=1
.

We define the following quantity:

\mathcal{U}\,\equiv\,\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{a};\tau)\,\mathcal{G}(u_{ij},\Delta_{b};\tau)

(B.37)

where
u_{ij}
is as in (
3.16
) but with
m=1
. Using the fact that
\mathcal{G}
is invariant under integer shifts of each of its arguments (see Appendix
A
), the modular transformation formula (
A.25
) and the constraint among
[m\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}\bigl(u_{ij},[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}};\check{\tau}\bigr)\,\mathcal{G}\bigl(u_{ij},[\Delta_{b}]_{\check{\tau}};\check{\tau}\bigr)

(B.38)

\displaystyle=N^{2}\,\frac{(\check{\tau}\pm 1)^{2}}{\check{\tau}^{2}}+\frac{2(\check{\tau}\pm 1)}{\check{\tau}^{2}}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}\biggl(\frac{u_{ij}}{\check{\tau}},\frac{[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\check{\tau}^{2}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}\biggl(\frac{u_{ij}}{\check{\tau}},\frac{[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)\,\mathcal{G}\biggl(\frac{u_{ij}}{\check{\tau}},\frac{[\Delta_{b}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)

where the
\pm
signs refer to the 1
st
and 2
nd
case, respectively. Using the series expansion of
\mathcal{G}
in (
A.23
) and the same method we used to compute
\Upsilon
after (
B.26
), the summation on the second line gives

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}\biggl(\frac{u_{ij}}{\check{\tau}},\frac{[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)=N^{2}\,\mathcal{G}\biggl(0,\frac{N[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{N}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)\;.

(B.39)

The summation on the third line involves the object

\mathcal{W}_{ab}\equiv\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}\biggl(\frac{u_{ij}}{\check{\tau}},\frac{[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)\,\mathcal{G}\biggl(\frac{u_{ij}}{\check{\tau}},\frac{[\Delta_{b}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{1}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)=2\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{\infty}c_{a}^{k}c_{b}^{\ell}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\biggl[\biggl(\frac{\tilde{z}_{i}}{\tilde{z}_{j}}\biggr)^{\!k+\ell}\!+\biggl(\frac{\tilde{z}_{i}}{\tilde{z}_{j}}\biggr)^{\!k-\ell}\biggr]

(B.40)

where the coefficients
c_{a}^{\ell}
are given by

c_{a}^{\ell}=\frac{\tilde{y}_{a}^{\ell}-(\check{q}/\tilde{y}_{a})^{\ell}}{1-\check{q}^{\ell}}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\Bigl[\bigl(\check{q}^{r}\tilde{y}_{a}\bigr)^{\ell}-\bigr(\check{q}^{r+1}/\tilde{y}_{a}\bigr)^{\ell}\Bigr]\;,

(B.41)

and
\tilde{z}_{i}=e^{2\pi iu_{i}/\check{\tau}}
,
\tilde{y}_{a}=e^{2\pi i[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}/\check{\tau}}
,
\check{q}=e^{-2\pi i/\check{\tau}}
. The sum over
i,j
can be performed exploiting (
B.27
), and we obtain

\mathcal{W}_{ab}=4N^{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{\infty}c_{a}^{N(\alpha+1)}c_{b}^{N(\beta+1)}+2N^{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N-1}c_{a}^{N\alpha+\gamma}\Bigl(c_{b}^{N\beta+\gamma}+c_{b}^{N(\beta+1)-\gamma}\Bigr)\;.

(B.42)

The first sum can be performed exactly using

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}c_{a}^{N\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{G}\biggl(0,\frac{N[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}}{\check{\tau}};-\frac{N}{\check{\tau}}\biggr)\;.

(B.43)

Substituting back in
\mathcal{W}_{ab}
, in
\mathcal{U}
and finally in the trace we obtain the expansion

\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\bigr)=\frac{2N^{2}}{\check{\tau}^{2}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N-1}c_{a}^{N\alpha+\gamma}\Bigl(c_{b}^{N\beta+\gamma}+c_{b}^{N(\beta+1)-\gamma}\Bigr)\;.

(B.44)

Let us study, in the large
N
limit, the summations containing the two terms in parenthesis separately. The second term involves

\displaystyle\chi^{(2)}_{ab}

\displaystyle\,\equiv\,\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N-1}c_{a}^{N\alpha+\gamma}c_{b}^{N(\beta+1)-\gamma}

(B.45)

\displaystyle\,=\,\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N-1}\sum_{r,s=0}^{\infty}\biggl[\bigl(\check{q}^{r}\tilde{y}_{a}\bigr)^{N\alpha+\gamma}-\bigl(\check{q}^{r+1}/\tilde{y}_{a}\bigr)^{N\alpha+\gamma}\biggr]\biggl[\bigl(\check{q}^{s}\tilde{y}_{b}\bigr)^{N\beta+N-\gamma}-\bigl(\check{q}^{s+1}/\tilde{y}_{b}\bigr)^{N\beta+N-\gamma}\biggr]\;.

The fundamental property of
[\Delta_{a}]_{\check{\tau}}
in (
3.40
) guarantees that
|\check{q}|<|\tilde{y}_{a}|<1
, and similarly
|\check{q}|<|\tilde{y}_{b}|<1
. Therefore the defining sum of
\chi^{(2)}_{ab}
involves terms whose absolute value is smaller than a number smaller than 1, elevated to the powers
N(\alpha+\beta)+N
. This implies that
\chi^{(2)}_{ab}
is of order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-N})
, and thus it only produces non-perturbative corrections. Next we consider

\chi^{(1)}_{ab}\,\equiv\,\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N-1}c_{a}^{N\alpha+\gamma}c_{b}^{N\beta+\gamma}\;.

(B.46)

By expanding out the coefficients
c_{a}^{\ell}
as in (
B.41
), one obtains four sums. One of them is

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N-1}\sum_{r,s=0}^{\infty}\bigl(\check{q}^{r}\tilde{y}_{a}\bigr)^{N\alpha+\gamma}\bigl(\check{q}^{s}\tilde{y}_{b}\bigr)^{N\beta+\gamma}={}

(B.47)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,r,s=0}^{\infty}\bigl(\check{q}^{r}\tilde{y}_{a}\bigr)^{N\alpha}\bigl(\check{q}^{s}\tilde{y}_{b}\bigr)^{N\beta}\,\frac{\check{q}^{r+s}\tilde{y}_{a}\tilde{y}_{b}-\bigl(\check{q}^{r+s}\tilde{y}_{a}\tilde{y}_{b}\bigr)^{N}}{1-\check{q}^{r+s}\tilde{y}_{a}\tilde{y}_{b}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\ell+1)\,\check{q}^{\ell}\,\tilde{y}_{a}\,\tilde{y}_{b}}{1-\check{q}^{\ell}\,\tilde{y}_{a}\,\tilde{y}_{b}}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N})\;.

In the first equality we summed the finite geometric series in
\gamma
, while in the second equality we only kept the terms that are finite in the large
N
limit. The other three sums that constitute
\chi^{(1)}_{ab}
can be treated similarly. The result, up to non-perturbative corrections, can be nicely expressed in terms of the function

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite classical group generated by transvections, i.e., one of SL n ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(q) , SU n ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SU}_{n}(q) , Sp 2 ​ n ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(q) , or O 2 ​ n ± ⁡ ( q ) ​ ( q ​ even ) \operatorname{O}^{\pm}_{2n}(q)~(q~\text{even}) , and let X X be a generating set for G G containing at least one transvection. Building on work of Garonzi, Halasi, and Somlai, we prove that the diameter of the Cayley graph Cay ⁡ ( G , X ) \operatorname{Cay}(G,X) is bounded by ( n ​ log ⁡ q ) C (n\log q)^{C} for some constant C C . This confirms Babai’s conjecture on the diameter of finite simple groups in the case of generating sets containing a transvection. By combining this with a result of the author and Jezernik it follows that if G G is one of SL n ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(q) , SU n ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SU}_{n}(q) , Sp 2 ​ n ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(q) and X X contains three random generators then with high probability the diameter Cay ⁡ ( G , X ) \operatorname{Cay}(G,X) is bounded by n O ⁡ ( log ⁡ q ) n^{O(\log q)} . This confirms Babai’s conjecture for non-orthogonal classical simple groups over small fields and three random generators.



1.
Introduction

This paper represents another step in the direction of the long-standing conjecture of Babai
[
4
]
*Conjecture 1.7 on the diameter of finite simple groups with respect to arbitrary generating sets.
Given
g\in G=\left\langle{X}\right\rangle
, let
\ell_{X}(g)
denote the minimal word length of
g
with respect to
X\cup X^{-1}
, and for
S\subseteq G
let
\ell_{X}(S)=\sup_{g\in S}\ell_{X}(g)
.
Babai’s conjecture predicts that
\ell_{X}(G)\leq(\log|G|)^{O(1)}
for any (nonabelian) finite simple group
G
and generating set
X
.
We call
\ell_{X}(G)
the
diameter
of
G
with respect to
X
; it is equivalent to the diameter of the undirected Cayley graph
\operatorname{Cay}(G,X)
.

The past two decades have seen a slew of progress on Babai’s conjecture.
In particular, following the seminal work of Helfgott
[
12
]
, Breuillard–Green–Tao
[
7
]
, and Pyber–Szabó
[
24
]
, the conjecture is now completely resolved in bounded Lie rank.
In the case of alternating groups
A_{n}
, while Babai’s conjecture predicts that the diameter should be bounded by a polynomial in
n
, a result of Helfgott and Seress
[
14
,
15
]
establishes a quasipolynomial bound.
Recently Bajpai, Dona, and Helfgott
[
5
]
proved a bound for classical Chevalley groups of large characteristic of the form
(\log|G|)^{O(r^{5})}
, where
r
is the rank of
G
.

Much stronger results are known about diameter with respect to typical generators (as opposed to worst-case generators, as in Babai’s conjecture).
In this formulation we assume the generators are chosen uniformly at random and we are interested in results that fail with asymptotically negligible probability.
Polynomial (in
n
) bounds for the diameter of
A_{n}
with respect to typical generators were established in
[
3
,
25
,
13
]
.
More recently, it was proved in
[

that if

(so

) then
\ell_{X}(G)\leq q^{2}n^{O(1)}
with high probability provided that

includes at least

random generators.

2.
Bounded rank

Babai’s conjecture is known for simple groups of Lie type of bounded rank.
This is a corollary of the independent work of Breuillard–Green–Tao and Pyber–Szabó.

Theorem 2.1

(Breuillard–Green–Tao
[
7
]
, Pyber–Szabó
[
24
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite simple group of Lie type of rank
n
and let
X
be a generating set for
G
. Then

\ell_{X}(G)\leq O{\left(\frac{\log|G|}{\log|X|}\right)}^{O_{n}(1)}.

Theorem

1.2
in bounded rank follows immediately from this and standard tools, as detailed below.

Proposition 2.2
.

In the situation of
Theorem

1.2
we have

\ell_{X}(G)\leq O{\left(\frac{\log|G|}{\log|X|}\right)}^{O_{n}(1)}.

Proof.

Assume
X=X^{-1}
and
1\in X
without loss of generality.
Note that
[G:G^{\prime}]=2
in the orthogonal case and
G=G^{\prime}
in the other cases.
Therefore by Schreier’s lemma
Y=X^{3}\cap G^{\prime}
is a generating set for
G^{\prime}
.
Moreover
|Y|\geq|X|/2
. Indeed, if
G^{\prime}<G
, since
X
generates
G
there is some
x_{0}\in X\smallsetminus G^{\prime}
.
Since
X\cap x_{0}G^{\prime}\subseteq x_{0}(X^{2}\cap G^{\prime})
, we have

|X|=|X\cap G^{\prime}|+|X\cap x_{0}G^{\prime}|\leq 2|X^{2}\cap G^{\prime}|\leq 2|Y|.

Applying
Theorem

2.1
to the simple quotient
S=G^{\prime}/Z(G^{\prime})
, it follows that
Y^{m}Z(G^{\prime})=G^{\prime}
, where

m\leq O(\log|S|/\log|Y|)^{O_{n}(1)}\leq O(\log|G|/\log|X|)^{O_{n}(1)}.

In particular
|Y^{m}|\geq|G^{\prime}/Z(G^{\prime})|
.
Applying Freiman’s 3/2 theorem (see
[
26
]
*Theorem 1.5.2 and Proposition 1.5.4)
we have (for any
k\geq 1
) either
Y^{2^{k}m}=G^{\prime}
or

|Y^{2^{k}m}|\geq(3/2)^{k}|Y^{m}|\geq(3/2)^{k}|G^{\prime}/Z(G^{\prime})|.

If
G
is symplectic or orthogonal we have
|Z(G^{\prime})|\leq 2
, so with
k=2
we must have
Y^{4m}=G^{\prime}
and thus
X^{12m+1}=G
.

If
G
is linear or unitary we have
G=G^{\prime}
and
|Z(G)|\leq n
, so

|Y^{2^{k}m}|\geq\min(|G|,(3/2)^{k}|G|/n).

By Landazuri–Seitz
[
19
]
and
[
21
]
*Corollary 2.5, the minimal degree of a nontrivial complex representation of
G
is at least
|G|^{c/n}>q^{c^{\prime}n}
for some constants
c,c^{\prime}>0
.
Applying
[
21
]
*Theorem 1, we get
Y^{3\cdot 2^{k}m}=G
provided that
(3/2)^{k}/n>q^{-c^{\prime}n/3}
.
It suffices to take
k
to be a suitable constant depending on
c^{\prime}
.
Thus
Y^{m^{\prime}}=X^{3m^{\prime}}=G
for some
m^{\prime}\leq O(\log|G|/\log|X|)^{O_{n}(1)}
.
∎

This special case is used in the proof of
Theorem

1.2
in two essentially different ways.
First, it allows us to assume that
n
is larger than any fixed constant, which enables to avoid low-rank pathologies.
Second, in the course of the proof of
Theorem

1.2
we will apply
Proposition

2.2
to a wide variety of bounded-rank subgroups.

3.
The transvection graph

Following
[
11
,
10
]
we define a graph based on a collection of transvections. To motivate the definition we first recall the concept of the transposition graph (which we will also use).

If
T\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
is a set of transpositions, the
transposition graph

\Gamma(T)
is the graph with vertex set
\Omega
and edge set
\{ij:(ij)\in T\}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
be a set of transpositions and let
G=\left\langle{T}\right\rangle
.
Then
G
is the direct product of the full symmetric groups on the components of
\Gamma(T)
.
In particular the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma(T)
is connected;

(2)

G
is transitive;

(3)

G=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

First assume
\Gamma(T)
is connected. We will prove
G=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
by induction on
n
, the result being obvious if
n\leq 1
.
We may assume
\Gamma(T)
is a tree by replacing
T
with a subset.
Let
i
be a leaf of
\Gamma(T)
, let
t_{0}=(ij)\in T
, and let
T_{0}=T\smallsetminus\{t_{0}\}
.
Then
\Gamma(T_{0})
can be identified with a tree with vertex set
\Omega_{0}=\Omega\smallsetminus\{i\}
,
so by induction
G_{0}=\left\langle{T_{0}}\right\rangle=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega_{0})
, and it follows easily that

G=\left\langle{G_{0},(ij)}\right\rangle=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
.

In general, let
\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{k}
be the components of
\Gamma(T)
and let
T_{i}\subseteq T
be the set of transpositions in
T
supported on
\Omega_{i}
. Then
T=T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k}
and the elements of
T_{i}
commute with the elements of
T_{j}
for
i\neq j
, so
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{k}
where
G_{i}=\left\langle{T_{i}}\right\rangle
.
By the connected case,
G_{i}=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega_{i})
for each
i
.
∎

Now let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and let
V\cong K^{n}
be a vector space over
K
of finite dimension
n\geq 2
.
A
transvection
is an element
t\in\operatorname{SL}(V)
such that
t-1
is a rank-one map.
Let
\mathfrak{T}\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(V)
be the set of transvections.
Each transvection
t\in\mathfrak{T}
has the form

t=1+v\otimes\phi\qquad(v\in V,\phi\in V^{*},\phi(v)=0).

We make
\mathfrak{T}
into a directed graph
\Gamma(\mathfrak{T})
by declaring there to be a directed edge
t\to s
if and only if
{(t-1)(s-1)\neq 0}
.
Put another way, there is an edge
1+v\otimes\phi\to 1+u\otimes\psi
if and only if
\phi(u)\neq 0
.
For any subset
T\subseteq\mathfrak{T}
we denote by
\Gamma(T)
the corresponding induced subgraph.
We also define linear subspaces
V(T)\leq V
and
V^{*}(T)\leq V^{*}
by

\displaystyle V(T)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{v:1+v\otimes\phi\in T}\right\rangle=\sum_{t\in T}\operatorname{im}(t-1)=[V,G],

\displaystyle V^{*}(T)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\phi:1+v\otimes\phi\in T}\right\rangle=\sum_{t\in T}\operatorname{ker}(t-1)^{\perp}=(V^{G})^{\perp},

where
G=\left\langle{T}\right\rangle
.
Here
[V,G]
denotes the commutator of
V
and
G
as subgroups of
V\rtimes G
, and
V^{G}
denotes the space of
G
-invariants in
V
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathfrak{T}
be an arbitrary set of transvections and let
G=\left\langle{T}\right\rangle
.
In the rest of this section we describe how important properties of
G
can be detected through careful examination of the transvection graph
\Gamma(T)
as well as the subspaces
V(T)
and
V^{*}(T)
.
We start with irreducibility of
G
.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
10
]
*Theorem 3.1)
.

The group
G\leq\operatorname{SL}(V)
is irreducible if and only if

(1)

V(T)=V
,

(2)

V^{*}(T)=V^{*}
,

(3)

\Gamma(T)
is strongly connected.

Proof.

Necessity:

Suppose
G
is irreducible. Then
[V,G]=V
and
V^{G}=0
, so (1) and (2) hold.
Now suppose
S
is a nonempty subset of
T
with no incoming edges in
\Gamma(T)
.
Then, for
t\in T
and
s\in S
,

(3.1)

(t-1)(s-1)\neq 0\implies t\in S.

Let
U=V(S)
. Then
U
is nonzero and
\left\langle{S}\right\rangle
-invariant, and the above equation shows that
T\smallsetminus S
acts trivially on
U
, so
U
is
G
-invariant, so
U=V
.
Thus for every
t\in T
there is some
s\in S
such that
(t-1)(s-1)\neq 0
, whence
t\in S
by (
3.1
).
Hence (3) holds.

Sufficiency:
Let
U\leq V
be a nonzero
G
-invariant subspace. Let
S\subseteq T
be the set of
s
such that
\operatorname{im}(s-1)\leq U
.
Let
u\in U\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
By (2) there is at least one
t\in T
such that
(t-1)u\neq 0
.
By
G
-invariance of
U
it follows that
\operatorname{im}(t-1)\leq U
, so
t\in S
.
Hence
S
is nonempty, and the same calculation shows that
S
has no incoming edges, so by (3) we have
S=T
. Thus by (1) we have
U=V
. Hence
G
is irreducible.
∎

We say
G

defined over
a subfield
L\leq K
if for some basis of
V
the elements of
G
are represented by matrices with entries in
L
.
The
defining field
of
G
is the smallest field such that
G
is defined over
L
.

Given transvections
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\in\mathfrak{T}
, the
weight
of the tuple
(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
is defined as

(3.2)

w(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=\operatorname{tr}((t_{1}-1)\cdots(t_{k}-1)).

If
t_{i}=1+v_{i}\otimes\phi_{i}
for each
i
then

w(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=\phi_{1}(v_{2})\phi_{2}(v_{3})\cdots\phi_{k}(v_{1}).

Note that
w(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})\neq 0
if and only if
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
is a directed cycle in
\Gamma(\mathfrak{T})
.

If
T\subseteq\mathfrak{T}
we denote by
L_{k}(T)
the subfield generated by the weights of the cycles of
\Gamma(T)
of length at most
k
.
Let
L(T)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}L_{k}(T)
.

Proposition 3.3

(cf.
[
10
]
*Section 3.2)
.

Assume
G
is irreducible.
Then
L(T)
is the defining field of
G
.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.2
) (and induction on
k
) that
L_{k}(T)
is equal to the field generated by
\{\operatorname{tr}(g):\ell_{T}(g)\leq k\}
,
so
L(T)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}L_{k}(T)
is the field generated by
\{\operatorname{tr}(g):g\in G\}
(the
trace field
of
G
).
It is well-known that the latter is precisely the defining field of
G
in positive characteristic
(see
[
17
]
*Corollaries 9.5(c) and 9.23).
∎

Again suppose
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\in\mathfrak{T}
. We define

d_{s}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=w(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})-(-1)^{k}w(t_{k},\dots,t_{1})

and we call the tuple
(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})

Proposition 3.4

(cf.

*Theorem 3.12)

Assume

The method of this section is inspired by

Given a vector

and a transvection

let us write

\phi(u)\neq 0
.
Similarly given

This notation is compatible with the corresponding notation for the transvection graph: if

and

Call

and nonzero

Clearly if

The following useful proposition is a partial converse.

and

,

,
V^{*}(T)=V^{*}
, and
\Gamma(T)
is strongly connected.
It follows that there are transvections

for some

such that

Write

for

Assume

is minimal for the existence of such

If

By minimality of

(2)

(3)

In particular

must be linearly independent, so

Now consider

\displaystyle=t_{1}\circ\cdots\circ t_{m-1}(v_{m}),

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\phi_{m}\circ t_{m-1}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ t_{1}^{-1}.

6.2.

Let us review the construction of the irreducible linear groups of symmetric type.
Let
G=S_{m}
and consider its standard permutation representation

, where
K=\mathbf{F}_{2}
.
The unique proper nontrivial submodules are
\ell=(K^{m})^{G}=\left\langle{e}\right\rangle
, where

, and
H=[K^{m},G]=\{x:x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}=0\}

If

is odd,
W=\ell\oplus H
.
If

is even,
\ell\leq H
.
Let

and

.
The natural image of
G
in
\operatorname{SL}(V)
is a
group of symmetric type
.
We call

odd symmetric type
if

is odd and

if

is even.

The unique nonzero

-invariant alternating bilinear form on

is given by

,
and one checks

, so

induces a
G
-invariant symplectic form on

.
Thus

is conjugate to a subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(2)
.
One may also check that

preserves a quadratic form on

if and only if

.

For small

there are various coincidences with groups of classical type:

\displaystyle S_{3}\cong S_{4}/V\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(2),

\displaystyle S_{5}\cong\operatorname{O}_{4}^{-}(2)\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(4):2,

\displaystyle S_{6}\cong\operatorname{Sp}_{4}(2),

\displaystyle S_{8}\cong\operatorname{O}_{6}^{+}(2)\cong\operatorname{SL}_{4}(2):2.

It is therefore harmless to assume

,

.
Unless

, an element of

acts on

as a transposition if and only if it acts on

as a transvection.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let

and let

be the representation defined above, where

.
Assume

.
An element

is a transposition if and only if

is transvection.

Proof.

Let

be the standard basis of

. Define

by

.
Let

. Then

acts as

on

, which descends to a transvection on

, so

is a transvection.
Conversely, suppose

is a transvection. Then

is an involution, so

has cycle type

for some

Then

acts as

on

.
The subspace

consists of all vectors

such that

for

and such that

,
and moreover such that

.
Also

has rank

unless

, in which case it has rank

if

is odd and

Therefore in order that

6.3.
Monomial subgroups

Let

be a finite field of characteristic

and let

be a divisor of

. Let

be the subgroup of

consisting of those elements which are represented in the standard basis by monomial matrices with all nonzero entries being

and

is a subgroup of

generated by transvections, irreducible for

. The transvections in

are the elements represented as

\begin{pmatrix}0&x^{-1}&0\\

In general if

, a

-invariant monomial structure
on

is a set of lines

permuted by

such that

is a basis for

.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
a>1
there is a unique

-invariant monomial structure on
V=K^{n}
.

Proof.

First observe that if
N\trianglelefteq C_{a}^{n-1}\rtimes S_{n}
and
N
is not contained in
C_{a}^{n-1}
then
N
contains
C_{a}^{n-1}
.
Indeed,
N
contains some element with nontrivial image

in
S_{n}
, so
N
contains
[C_{a}^{n-1},\pi]
.
Now it follows by a short argument that

must contain all of
C_{a}^{n-1}
.
In particular if
N
is normal and abelian then
N\leq C_{a}^{n-1}
.

Now let
G=M_{n}(a)\cong C_{a}^{n-1}\rtimes S_{n}
.
The fact that
\{Ke_{1},\dots,Ke_{n}\}
is a
G
-invariant monomial structure is obvious from the definition of
G
.
Suppose

is another
G
-invariant monomial structure.
The kernel
C_{G}(L)
of the action of
G
on

is abelian and normal, so by the above argument it must be contained in the diagonal subgroup

of

.
Moreover

is isomorphic to a subgroup of

; it follows that

.
Hence

are joint eigenvectors of

.
However it is straightforward to check that the only joint eigenvectors of

are the scalar multiples of

, so
\{Kb_{1},\dots,Kb_{n}\}=\{Ke_{1},\dots,Ke_{n}\}
.
∎

Note that

does
not
preserve a symplectic form on

if

.
Indeed, the only bilinear forms invariant on the diagonal subgroup of

are those proportional to

By similar reasoning

has neither symmetric type nor monomial type, and
\dim W>6
, so
G_{0}|W
must have classical type.
∎

6.4.
Subfield subgroups

Now let
K=\mathbf{F}_{q}
be an arbitrary finite field,
T\subseteq\mathfrak{T}
,
G=\left\langle{T}\right\rangle\leq\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
.
Assume
T
is dense,
G
is classical, and
K=L(T)
.
We aim to find a certificate
T_{0}\subseteq T^{(\log q)^{O(1)}}
of cardinality
|T_{0}|\leq 3
such that
L(T_{0})=K
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
G\leq\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
be a classical group generated by transvections with defining field
\mathbf{F}_{q}
.
Then there is a subset
T_{0}\subseteq G\cap\mathfrak{T}
of cardinality
|T_{0}|\leq 3
such that
L(T_{0})=\mathbf{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbf{F}_{q}
be a primitive element.

If
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
then we can take
T_{0}=\{t,s\}
for
t=1+\lambda e_{1}\otimes e_{2}^{*}
and
s=1+e_{2}\otimes e_{1}^{*}
.
Here
e_{i}^{*}
is defined by
e_{i}^{*}(e_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
Observe that

w(t,s)=\operatorname{tr}((t-1)(s-1))=\operatorname{tr}(\lambda e_{1}\otimes e_{1}^{*})=\lambda.

Therefore
L(T_{0})
contains
\mathbf{F}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathbf{F}_{q}
.
This completes the proof for linear and symplectic groups since both contain copies of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
.

Now consider
G=\operatorname{SU}_{3}(\sqrt{q})
. Since all unitary forms are equivalent it suffices to consider the unitary form

f(x,y)=x_{1}y_{2}^{\theta}+x_{2}y_{1}^{\theta}+x_{3}y_{3}^{\theta},

where
x^{\theta}=x^{\sqrt{q}}
is the involutary automorphism.
Now for
v\in V=\mathbf{F}_{q}^{3}
let us denote by
v^{*}\in V^{*}
the map
u\mapsto f(u,v)
.
Note that
e_{1},e_{2}
is a hyperbolic pair.
Let
\epsilon\in\mathbf{F}_{q}
be such that
\epsilon^{\theta}=-\epsilon
and consider
t_{i}=1+\epsilon v_{i}\otimes v_{i}^{*}
for
i=1,2,3
, where
v_{1}=e_{1}
,
v_{2}=e_{2}
, and

v_{3}=\epsilon^{-3}\lambda e_{1}+e_{2}+ze_{3},

where
z
is chosen so that
v_{3}
is singular (recall that
\operatorname{Fix}(\theta)=\{zz^{\theta}:z\in K\}
for finite fields).
Since
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
are singular, we have
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\in G
, and

w(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=\epsilon^{3}f(v_{2},v_{1})f(v_{3},v_{2})f(v_{1},v_{3})=\lambda.

Since
\operatorname{SU}_{3}(\sqrt{q})\leq\operatorname{SU}_{n}(\sqrt{q})
for
n\geq 3
this completes the proof for unitary groups.

Finally consider an orthogonal group
G=\operatorname{O}_{n}^{\pm}(q)
,
n\geq 6
,
p=2
.
The transvections in
G
are precisely those of the form
1+v\otimes v^{*}
where
Q(v)=1

and
v^{*}
is defined by
v^{*}(u)=f(u,v)
.
Observe that

w(1+u\otimes u^{*},1+v\otimes v^{*})=f(u,v)^{2}.

Let
H\leq V
be a
4
-dimensional hyperbolic subspace (see
[
1
]
*(21.2)) and let
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}
be a hyperbolic basis for
H
.
With respect to this basis we have, for
x,y\in H
,

\displaystyle Q(x)

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4},

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1}+x_{3}y_{4}+x_{4}y_{3}.

Let
u=e_{1}+e_{2}
and
v=\lambda e_{1}+e_{3}+e_{4}
. Then
Q(u)=Q(v)=1
and
f(u,v)=\lambda
. Since finite fields are perfect,
\lambda^{2}
generates
\mathbf{F}_{q}
, so it suffices to take
T_{0}=\{1+u\otimes u^{*},1+v\otimes v^{*}\}
.
∎

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathfrak{T}
be dense,

classical,

, and assume

Then there is a subset

Proof.

First assume

.

First, by

6.9

5.3

Now suppose

Now for

6.5.
Orthogonal subgroups

The last thing remaining is distinguish orthogonal groups from symplectic groups. Throughout this subsection assume
K=\mathbf{F}_{q}
is a finite field of characteristic
2
.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
(V,Q)
be a nondegenerate quadratic space over a finite field
K=\mathbf{F}_{q}
.
Let
w+H\subseteq V
be an affine subspace of codimension
2
.
Assume
\dim V\geq 10
.
Then
Q(w+H)=K
.

Proof.

For
v\in H\cap w^{\perp}
we have
Q(w+v)=Q(w)+Q(v)
, so it suffices to show that
Q(H_{1})=K
for
H_{1}=H\cap w^{\perp}
. Write
H_{1}=(H_{1}\cap H_{1}^{\perp})\oplus H_{2}
. Then
H_{2}
is nondegenerate and
H_{2}
has codimension at most
6
, so
\dim H_{2}\geq 4
.
It follows from the classification of quadratic forms over finite fields

[
1
]
*(21.2) that
Q(H_{2})=K
.
∎

Now assume
T\subseteq\mathfrak{T}\cap\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(q)
is dense,
L(T)=K=\mathbf{F}_{q}
, and
G=\left\langle{T}\right\rangle
is classical.

Proposition 6.13
.

There is a constant
C
such that the following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is an orthogonal group,

(2)

every nondegenerate subset
T_{0}\subseteq T^{(n\log q)^{O(1)}}
of cardinality at most
C
generates a subgroup
G_{0}=\left\langle{T_{0}}\right\rangle
that preserves an orthogonal structure on
V_{0}=V(T_{0})=[V,G_{0}]
.

Proof.

(1)
\implies
(2):
Suppose
G
preserves an orthogonal form
Q
with corresponding symplectic form
f
. If
T_{0}\subseteq T
is nondegenerate then
V_{0}=V(T_{0})
is an
f
-nondegenerate subspace of
V
. Thus
Q_{0}=Q|V_{0}
is a
G_{0}
-invariant orthogonal form on
V_{0}
.

(2)
\implies
(1):

First, by
Propositions

6.9
,
6.11
,
5.2
, and
5.3
, there is a bounded-cardinality nondegenerate subset
T_{0}\subseteq T^{(\log q)^{O(1)}}
such that
G_{0}=\left\langle{T_{0}}\right\rangle
acts irreducibly and classically on
V_{0}=V(T_{0})
,
\dim V_{0}\geq 10
, and
L(T_{0})=K
. Note
G_{0}
must be an orthogonal group. By
Proposition

2.2
,

\ell_{T}(G_{0})\leq O(\log q)^{O(1)},\qquad\ell_{G_{0}\cap\mathfrak{T}}(G_{0})\leq O(1).

Thus by replacing
T
with
T^{(\log q)^{O(1)}}\cap\mathfrak{T}
we may assume
T_{0}\subseteq T
and
\ell_{T}(G_{0})\leq O(1)
.

Now we apply
Proposition

3.5
. For each
t\in T
there is a unique
v_{t}\in V
such that
t=1+v_{t}\otimes v_{t}^{*}
.
Consider some linear relation

\sum_{t\in T}\lambda_{t}v_{t}=0.

Suppose
\lambda
is supported on
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
, and we may assume
m\leq n+1
.
We must show that
\tilde{Q}(\lambda)=0
, where

\tilde{Q}(\lambda)=\sum_{t\in T}\lambda_{t}^{2}+\sum_{\{t,s\}\in\binom{T}{2}}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}f(v_{s},v_{t}).

Define also

\tilde{f}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=\tilde{Q}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})-\tilde{Q}(\lambda)-\tilde{Q}(\lambda^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in T\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\lambda_{s}\lambda^{\prime}_{t}f(v_{s},v_{t}).

We may think of
\lambda
as an element of
K^{\mathfrak{T}}
, and
\tilde{Q}
defines an orthogonal form on
K^{\mathfrak{T}}
with corresponding symplectic form
\tilde{f}
.

We aim to reduce the support of
\lambda
as much as possible.
Suppose
m\geq 10
, i.e., the support of
\lambda
contains at least
10
transvections
t_{1},\dots,t_{10}
.
Applying
Propositions

5.2
and
5.3
,
T_{0}\cup\{t_{1},\dots,t_{10}\}
is contained in a bounded-cardinality nondegenerate set
T_{1}\subseteq T
such that
G_{1}=\left\langle{T_{1}}\right\rangle
acts irreducibly on
V_{1}=V(T_{1})
. Since
T_{0}\subseteq T_{1}
,
G_{1}
must act classically, and therefore
G_{1}
must be an orthogonal group by hypothesis.
Since
G_{0}\leq G_{1}
, by
Proposition

2.2
we have

\ell_{T}(G_{1})\leq\ell_{T_{1}\cup G_{0}}(G_{1})\ell_{T}(G_{0})\leq O(1).

By
Lemma

6.12
, we can find a transvection
t\in G_{1}\subseteq T^{L}
(for some constant
L
) such that
t=1+v\otimes v^{*}
for

v\in\lambda_{1}v_{1}+\lambda_{2}v_{2}+Kv_{3}+\cdots+Kv_{10}.

Hence we split the given linear relation into two shorter linear relations

\displaystyle v+\lambda_{1}v_{1}+\lambda_{2}v_{2}+\mu_{3}v_{3}+\cdots+\mu_{10}v_{10}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle v+(\lambda_{3}+\mu_{3})v_{3}+\cdots+(\lambda_{10}+\mu_{10})v_{10}+\sum_{i>10}\lambda_{i}v_{i}

\displaystyle=0.

Repeating this argument for
\left\lfloor{m/10}\right\rfloor
disjoint subsets of
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{m}\}
of size
10
, we find that we can add
\leq m/10
transvections

such that our original linear relation can be written as a sum of linear relations of support
\leq 11
and one of support at most

, i.e.,
\lambda=\lambda^{(1)}+\cdots+\lambda^{(s)}
where each

has support size at most

\sum_{t\in T^{L}}\lambda_{t}^{(j)}v_{t}=0\qquad(j=1,\dots,s).

Now we repeat this argument for

and each of the
\lambda^{(j)}
, etc,
\log n
times.
The end result is that
\lambda
gets written as a sum of some
\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(N)}
where each vector
\mu
has support size

, and

\sum_{t\in T^{L^{\log n}}}\mu^{(j)}_{t}v_{t}=0\qquad(j=1,\dots,N).

Note that
L^{\log n}=n^{O(1)}
.
Finally we use

(6.2)

\tilde{Q}(\lambda)=\tilde{Q}{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu^{(j)}\right)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{Q}(\mu^{(j)})+\sum_{1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq N}\tilde{f}(\mu^{(j)},\mu^{(j^{\prime})}).

We claim that each term on the right-hand side of (
6.2
) is zero.
Indeed, first consider some
\tilde{Q}(\mu)
,
\mu=\mu^{(j)}
.
We know that
\mu
is supported on a set of
O(1)
transvections
t\in T^{L^{\log n}}
.
By
Propositions

5.2
and
5.3
, the union of
T_{0}
with the support of
\mu
is contained in a bounded-cardinality nondegenerate set
T_{1}\subseteq T^{L^{\log n}}
such that
G_{1}=\left\langle{T_{1}}\right\rangle
acts irreducibly on
V_{1}=[V,T_{1}]
.
Since
T_{0}\subseteq T_{1}
,
G_{1}
acts classically and therefore as an orthogonal group on
V_{1}
.
Applying the forward direction of
Proposition

3.5
, we find that
\tilde{Q}(\mu)=0
.
By the same argument applied to the union of the supports of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
,
\mu^{\prime}=\mu^{(j^{\prime})}
, we also have
\tilde{Q}(\mu+\mu^{\prime})=\tilde{Q}(\mu)+\tilde{Q}(\mu^{\prime})+\tilde{f}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})=0
, and since
\tilde{Q}(\mu)=\tilde{Q}(\mu^{\prime})=0
we have
\tilde{f}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})=0
.
This proves the claim.
Hence
\tilde{Q}(\lambda)=0
and the proof is complete.
∎

Conclusion

Theorem 6.14

(Certification)
.

\overline{T}_{1}
be the image of
T_{1}
in
\overline{G}_{1}
.
By
Proposition

4.1
,
\Gamma(T_{1})
and
\Gamma(\overline{T}_{1})
are naturally identified and the weights of corresponding cycles are equal.
In particular,
L(\overline{T}_{1})=L(T_{1})=K
, so
\overline{G}_{1}
has defining field
K
.
Also
\Gamma(\overline{T}_{1})
contains symplectic cycles if and only if
\Gamma(T_{1})
does, and ditto for unitary cycles.
We also have
\dim W\geq 2000
.
The symmetric-type and monomial-type certificates ensure that
\overline{G}_{1}
has neither symmetric type nor monomial type.
Thus
\overline{G}_{1}
must have the same type as
G
.
∎

7.
Bounding the diameter

Finally we turn to the proof of our main theorem,
Theorem

1.2
. Let
G
be one of the groups in scope and let
X
be a generating set for
G
containing a transvection
t
.
Let

T_{m}=\{t^{x_{1}\cdots x_{m}}:x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in X\},\qquad G_{m}=\left\langle{T_{m}}\right\rangle.

Then
G_{0}\leq G_{1}\leq\cdots
is an increasing sequence of subgroups of
G
such that
G_{i}=G_{i+1}
implies
G_{i}=\left\langle{t^{G}}\right\rangle=G
.
Let
m
be the first index such that
G_{m}=G
.
Then
2^{m}\leq|G_{m}|=|G|
, so
m\leq\log_{2}|G|
, and
\left\langle{T_{m}}\right\rangle=G
.
This demonstrates that for the purpose of proving
Theorem

1.2
it is sufficient to assume
X\subseteq\mathfrak{T}
(cf.
[
10
]
*Lemma 2.1).

Thus assume
T\subseteq\mathfrak{T}
is a generating set for
G
.
In particular,
\left\langle{T}\right\rangle
is irreducible.
Now we apply
Theorem

6.14
to obtain a certificate
T_{0}\subseteq T^{(n\log q)^{O(1)}}
of cardinality
O(1)
.
We may assume
T_{0}
is weakly nondegenerate and
\Gamma(T_{0})
is strongly connected.
Let
G_{0}=\left\langle{T_{0}}\right\rangle
.
Unless
G=\operatorname{SL}(V)
,
T_{0}
is in fact nondegenerate and
G_{0}
is a classical group with the same type as
G
, defining field
K
, and domain
V_{0}=[V,G_{0}]
of dimension
\dim V_{0}\leq|T_{0}|=O(1)
.
By
Proposition

2.2
,
\ell_{T_{0}}(G_{0})\leq O(\log q)^{O(1)}
.
By replacing
T
with
T^{(n\log q)^{O(1)}}\cap\mathfrak{T}
we may thus assume
T_{0}\subseteq G_{0}\cap\mathfrak{T}\subseteq T
.

If
G=\operatorname{SL}(V)
,
T_{0}
may only be weakly nondegenerate, but
G_{0}|W_{0}=\operatorname{SL}(W_{0})
where
W_{0}=V_{0}/V_{0}^{G_{0}}
,
V_{0}=[V,G_{0}]
, and
\dim W_{0}>10
.
By
Propositions

2.2
and
4.5
we have
\ell_{T_{0}}(G_{0})\leq O(\log q)^{O(1)}
.
Thus again by replacing
T
with
T^{(n\log q)^{O(1)}}\cap\mathfrak{T}
we may assume
T_{0}\subseteq G_{0}\cap\mathfrak{T}\subseteq T
.

Let
V=\mathbf{F}_{q}^{n}
be the natural module for
G
.
Then
V
is a linear, unitary, symplectic, or characteristic-2 orthogonal space.
Let us call a vector
v\in V

transvective
if
there is a transvection
t\in G\cap\mathfrak{T}
such that
[V,t]=\left\langle{v}\right\rangle
.
If
V
is linear or symplectic, all vectors are transvective;
if
V
is unitary, a vector is transvective if and only if it is singular;
if
V
is orthogonal, a vector is transvective if and only if it is nonsingular.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}
for transvective vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
.
Then
v-\lambda v_{i}-\mu v_{j}
is transvective for some
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and
\lambda,\mu\in K
.

Proof.

Clearly we may assume
V
is unitary or orthogonal, as otherwise all vectors are transvective.

Unitary case.

(Cf.
[
10
]
*Lemma 4.19, which assumes
q
is odd).
We will take
\mu=0
.
If
v
is singular then we can take
\lambda=0
.
If
v
is nonsingular then

f(v,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(v_{i},v)\neq 0,

so
f(v_{i},v)\neq 0
for some
i
and

f(v-\lambda v_{i},v-\lambda v_{i})=f(v,v)-\operatorname{tr}(\lambda f(v_{i},v)).

Since the trace map
\operatorname{tr}:\mathbf{F}_{q}\to\mathbf{F}_{\sqrt{q}}
is surjective and
f(v,v)\in\mathbf{F}_{\sqrt{q}}
, we can choose
\lambda\in K
so that
f(v,v)=\operatorname{tr}(\lambda f(v_{i},v))
, and it follows that
v-\lambda v_{i}
is singular, as required.

Orthogonal case.

Observe that

Q(v-\lambda v_{i})=Q(v)-\lambda f(v,v_{i})+\lambda^{2}Q(v_{i}).

Since
Q(v_{i})\neq 0
, there are at most two
\lambda\in K
for which this is zero, so we are done unless
q=2
,
Q(v)=0
, and
f(v,v_{i})=1
for all
i
.
In this case there must be some
i,j
such that
f(v_{i},v_{j})\neq 0
, and

Q(v-v_{i}-v_{j})=Q(v_{i})+Q(v_{j})+f(v,v_{i})+f(v,v_{j})+f(v_{i},v_{j})=1,

so we are done.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
be a basis for
V
with
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
transvective.
For
v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}b_{i}\in V
, let
s(v)
be the number of nonzero coefficients
x_{i}
.
Then any transvective vector
v
with
s(v)=k
is the sum of
4
transvective vectors
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
with
s(v_{i})\leq k/2+2
for each
i
.

Proof.

Clearly we may write
v=u_{1}+u_{2}
for some (not necessarily transvective)
u_{1},u_{2}\in V
such that
s(u_{1})\leq k/2+1
and
s(u_{2})\leq k/2
.
By
Lemma

7.1
, there are indices
i,j
and
\lambda,\mu\in K
such that
v_{1}=u_{1}-\lambda b_{i}-\mu b_{j}
is transvective and
s(v_{1})\leq s(u_{1})\leq k/2+1
.
Let
u_{2}^{\prime}=u_{1}+\lambda b_{i}+\mu b_{j}
.
Applying
Lemma

7.1
again, there are indices
i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\in K
such that
v_{2}=u_{2}^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}b_{i^{\prime}}-\mu^{\prime}b_{j^{\prime}}
is transvective and
s(v_{2})\leq s(u_{2}^{\prime})\leq k/2+2
.
Let
v_{3}=\lambda^{\prime}b_{i^{\prime}}
and
v_{4}=\mu^{\prime}b_{j^{\prime}}
.
Then
v=v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}+v_{4}
, by construction
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
are transvective, and
s(v_{1})\leq k/2+1
,
s(v_{2})\leq k/2+2
, and
s(v_{3}),s(v_{4})\leq 1
.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

T^{n^{O(1)}}\supseteq G\cap\mathfrak{T}
.

Proof.

Case
G\neq\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
.
Assume
V
is a symplectic, unitary, or orthogonal space.
Let
f
be the invariant form and for each
v\in V
let
v^{*}
be the dual element associated to
v
by
f
.
Recall that a vector
v\in V
is transvective if and only if there is a transvection
t\in G\cap\mathfrak{T}
such that
t=1+\lambda v\otimes v^{*}
for some
\lambda\in K
.
By
Proposition

3.2
, there is a (transvective) basis
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
for
V
such that
1+\mu_{i}b_{i}\otimes b_{i}^{*}\in T
for some
\mu_{i}\in K
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.
Let
s:V\to\{0,\dots,n\}
be the support function defined in
Lemma

7.2

and for each
k\geq 1
let
T^{(k)}
be the set of transvections
t=1+\lambda v\otimes v^{*}\in G\cap\mathfrak{T}
such that
s(v)\leq k
.
It suffices to prove that
T^{(5)}\subseteq T^{O(1)}
and
T^{(2k)}\subseteq(T^{(k+2)})^{O(1)}
for each
k\geq 1
, for then it follows that

G\cap\mathfrak{T}=T_{n}\subseteq T^{O(1)^{\left\lceil{\log_{2}n}\right\rceil}}=T^{n^{O(1)}}.

Let
t\in T^{(5)}
, say
t=1+\lambda v\otimes v^{*}
and
v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}b_{i}
,
k\leq 5
.
By
Propositions

5.2
and
5.3
,
T_{0}\cup\{1+\mu_{i}b_{i}\otimes b_{i}^{*}:1\leq i\leq k\}
is contained in a nondegenerate set
T_{1}\subseteq T
of cardinality
O(1)
such that
\Gamma(T_{1})
is strongly connected.
Then
G_{1}=\left\langle{T_{1}}\right\rangle
is a classical group with the same type as
G
and defining field
K
and domain
V_{1}=[V,G_{1}]
.
Since
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\in V_{1}
it follows that
v\in V_{1}
and
t\in G_{1}
.
By
Proposition

2.2
and the fact that
T
contains
G_{0}\cap\mathfrak{T}
and
\log|G_{0}\cap\mathfrak{T}|\geq\log q
, we have
G_{1}\subseteq T^{O(1)}
.
Thus
T^{(5)}\subseteq T^{O(1)}
.

Similarly, let
k\geq 1
and let
t\in T^{(2k)}
, say
t=1+\lambda v\otimes v^{*}
where
s(v)\leq 2k
.
By
Lemma

7.2
,
v=v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}+v_{4}
where
s(v_{i})\leq k+2
for each
i
.
Let
t_{i}\in T^{(k+2)}
be a transvection of the form
1+\lambda_{i}v_{i}\otimes v_{i}^{*}
for
i=1,2,3,4
.
By
Propositions

5.2
and
5.3
,
T_{0}\cup\{1+\lambda_{i}v_{i}\otimes v_{i}^{*}:i=1,2,3,4\}
is contained in a nondegenerate set
T_{1}\subseteq T^{(k+2)}
of cardinality
O(1)
such that
\Gamma(T_{1})
is strongly connected.
Then
G_{1}=\left\langle{T_{1}}\right\rangle
is a classical group with the same type as
G
and defining field
K
and domain
V_{1}=[V,G_{1}]
.
Since
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\in V_{1}
, it follows exactly as above using
Proposition

2.2
that
t\in G_{1}\subseteq(T^{(k+2)})^{O(1)}
.
Thus
T^{(2k)}\subseteq(T^{(k+2)})^{O(1)}
, as required.

Case
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
.
Now all vectors are transvective.
Again by
Proposition

3.2
there are elements
1+b_{i}\otimes f_{i}\in T
(
1\leq i\leq n
) such that
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
is a basis for
V
, as well as elements
1+c_{i}\otimes g_{i}\in T
(
1\leq i\leq n
) such that
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
is a basis for
V^{*}(T)
.
Define
s(v)
exactly as before with respect to the basis
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
and
s(\phi)
analogously for
\phi\in V^{*}
with respect to the basis
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
.
For each
k\geq 1
let
T^{(k)}
be the set of all transvections
t=1+v\otimes\phi\in\mathfrak{T}
such that
s(v)\leq k
and
s(\phi)\leq k
.
It suffices to prove that
T^{(1)}\subseteq T^{O(1)}
and
T^{(2k)}\subseteq(T^{(k)})^{O(1)}
for each
k\geq 1
.

Let
t\in T^{(1)}
, so without loss of generality
t=1+\lambda b_{i}\otimes g_{j}
for some indices
i,j
and
\lambda\in K
.
By
Propositions

5.2
and
5.3
,
T_{0}\cup\{1+b_{i}\otimes f_{i},1+c_{j}\otimes g_{j}\}
is contained in a bounded-cardinality weakly nondegenerate set

Applying

4.5

,

.
Since

.

Similarly, let

and let

, say

where

and
s(\phi)\leq 2k
.
Write

and

where

Let

for

and observe that

.
By

5.2
and

,

is contained in a weakly nondegenerate set

such that

is strongly connected.
Observe that

and

, so

.
Exactly as before we have

, so we have proved that

, as required.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

\ell_{T}(G)\leq n^{O(1)}
.

Proof.

By the previous proposition,
\ell_{T}(G\cap\mathfrak{T})\leq n^{O(1)}
.
By the Liebeck–Shalev theorem
[
20
]
*Theorem 1.1 we have
\ell_{G\cap\mathfrak{T}}(G)\leq O(n)
.
Thus

\ell_{T}(G)\leq\ell_{T}(G\cap\mathfrak{T})\ \ell_{G\cap\mathfrak{T}}(G)\leq n^{O(1)},

as claimed.
∎

This completes the proof of
Theorem

1.2
, since
\ell_{X}(T)\leq(n\log q)^{O(1)}
.

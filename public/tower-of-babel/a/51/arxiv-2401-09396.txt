Abstract
. Given a smooth curve C / ℚ C/\mathbb{Q} with genus ≥ 2 \geq 2 , we know by Faltings’ Theorem that C ⁡ ( ℚ ) C(\mathbb{Q}) is finite. Here we ask the reverse question: given a finite set of rational points S ⊆ ℙ n ​ ( ℚ ) S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q}) , does there exist a smooth curve C / ℚ C/\mathbb{Q} contained in ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} such that C ⁡ ( ℚ ) = S C(\mathbb{Q})=S ? 1.
Introduction

The genus
g
of a smooth projective curve
C/\mathbb{Q}
determines the possibilities for
its set of rational points. If
g=0
, then
C(\mathbb{Q})=\emptyset
or
C(\mathbb{Q})

is infinite.
If
g=1
, then
C(\mathbb{Q})=\emptyset
, or by the Mordell–Weil theorem,
the set
C(\mathbb{Q})
can be given the structure of a finitely generated abelian group.
Mazur’s torsion theorem
[
10
]

restricts the possible torsion subgroups of elliptic curves. As a consequence,
for example, there are no genus
1
curves
C/\mathbb{Q}
with exactly
11
rational
points.
For
g\geq 2
, Faltings’ theorem
[
3
]
asserts that
C(\mathbb{Q})
is finite. The Uniformity conjecture of Caporaso, Harris, and Mazur
[
2
]
, predicts that
\#C(\mathbb{Q})
is
uniformly bounded as
C
ranges through curves
C/\mathbb{Q}
of fixed genus
g\geq 2
.

It is therefore natural to ask about the possible restrictions on
\#C(\mathbb{Q})

as
C
ranges through all curves
C/\mathbb{Q}
of all possible genera
g\geq 2
. Poonen
[
11
]
has shown that every value of
\#C(\mathbb{Q})
is possible
1
1

1

In fact, that every value of
\#C(k)
is possible for any global field
k
.
. In this paper, we show that there are no restrictions on the set
C(\mathbb{Q})

itself, beyond finiteness.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geq 2
.
Given any finite set of rational points
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, there exists a smooth projective curve
C/\mathbb{Q}
contained
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, of genus
g\geq 2
, such that
C(\mathbb{Q})=S
.

Our proof gives an effective algorithm for computing the curve
C
from the given set of rational points
S
. It builds on earlier constructions due to Gajović
[
4
]

and the current author
[
12
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{R}
denote the perfect powers of the ring
R
:

\mathcal{P}_{R}=\{a^{m}:a\in R,m\geq 2\}.

They show that for a finite set of perfect powers
S\subset\mathcal{P}_{R}
, for

and

respectively, there is a polynomial
f_{S}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
such that
f_{S}(X)\cap\mathcal{P}_{R}=S
. We will show how the construction of the polynomial

in those papers can be adapted to give a construction of a suitable curve

in our context.

2.
Superelliptic Curves

The proof of Theorem
1
involves constructing suitable ’superelliptic’ curves, and then gluing them together. By a
superelliptic curve
C
over
\mathbb{Q}

[
5
]
we mean a smooth projective curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
associated to the affine model given by

C:\;y^{m}=f(x),

where
f
is separable of degree
d\geq 3
and
m\geq 2
is an integer. If
m=2
and
d=3
then
C
is an elliptic curve,
whereas if
m=2
and
d\geq 5
then
C
is hyperelliptic.
When
m=d
, the genus
g
of the curve is
g=(d-1)(d-2)/2
; thus if
d\geq 4
,
then
g\geq 2
and so
C(\mathbb{Q})
is finite by Faltings’ theorem
[
3
]
.

We begin by assuming our prescribed set of points
S
satisfies some nice conditions.

Definition 1
.

Let
S=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{r},b_{r})\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}(\mathbb{Q})
be a finite set of distinct rational points in the affine plane.
We say that

is

if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

if

then

,

(ii)

a_{i},b_{j}\neq 0
for all

,

(iii)

a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i,j
.

For any such

, we can construct a superelliptic curve
C
whose set of affine rational points is exactly
S
,
and such that
C
has no rational points at infinity. In Section
6
, we will show how to extend the argument to sets in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Theorem 2
.

Given an acceptable set
S\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, and a finite subset
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{C}
,
there exists a separable polynomial
f_{S,\mathcal{B}}(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
of some degree
d\geq 4
, not vanishing at any point in
\mathcal{B}
,
such that
C(\mathbb{Q})=S
, where

C
is the superelliptic curve associated to the affine model

C\;:\;y^{d}=f_{S,\mathcal{B}}(X).

Our general argument will not apply to the case when the set of prescribed points
S
has size
|S|=1
. In this case, we can take
any curve
C/\mathbb{Q}
of genus
g\geq 2
with one rational point. A
suitable change of coordinates will send the point in
S
to the point on
C
. For instance, take the hyperelliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with affine model

C:y^{2}=x^{5}-2.

We verify in Magma
[
1
]
that the rational points on the Jacobian,
J(\mathbb{Q})
, form a group of rank 0 with trivial torsion subgroup. Since
C(\mathbb{Q})
injects into
J(\mathbb{Q})
, we conclude that
C
has no rational points other than the one at infinity.

From now on we assume that
S=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{r},b_{r})\}
is acceptable and
|S|=r>1
. Set

d=18r+3,

and let

m:=\prod_{j<k}^{r}(a_{j}-a_{k}).

Let
L(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
be the Lagrange interpolation polynomial passing through the points
S^{\prime}
, where

S^{\prime}=\{(a_{i},b_{i}^{d})\mid(a_{i},b_{i})\in S\},

which is of the form

L(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}b_{j}^{d}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq r\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}\frac{X-a_{k}}{a_{j}-a_{k}}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
S=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{r},b_{r})\}
be an acceptable set of points of size

|S|=r
. Let
n
be any integer such that
n\geq r
.
Then there exists an irreducible polynomial

h(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[X]

satisfying the following conditions

(i)

h(a_{i})=b_{i}^{d}
for all
(a_{i},b_{i})\in S
,

(ii)

h(X)
has degree
n
,

(iii)

m\cdot h(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Let

b(X)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{r}(X-a_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}[X].

Since
S
is acceptable,
b_{j}\neq 0
for all

, so
L(X)
and
b(X)
are coprime. Choose

to be monic and coprime to

of degree

, and let

R(X,Y)=L(X)+b(X)c(X)Y\in\mathbb{Q}(Y)[X].

This is irreducible in
\mathbb{Q}(Y)[X]
.
By Hilbert’s

[
13
]
, there is a thin set
A\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
such that if

, then

is irreducible over

\mathbb{Q}
. It follows that there exist infinitely many integers

.
Choose any such

and let

. Then

will satisfy the conditions above.
∎

Although the polynomial
h(X)
interpolates the points of
S
, we cannot be sure that its associated superelliptic curve does not have other rational points. Our approach will be to build, from
h(X)
, a superelliptic curve

of degree

such that all rational points on

map to the rational points of an elliptic curve

. This elliptic curve will be chosen to have rank 0, and this will allow us to conclude there are no other rational points on

.

3.
Twists of the Mordell curve

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
rk
denote its Mordell-Weil rank, so that
E(\mathbb{Q})\cong E(\mathbb{Q})_{tors}\times\mathbb{Z}^{rk}
. Let

denote the quadratic twist of

by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{k})
. A celebrated conjecture of Goldfeld’s predicts that as

varies, the rank should be 0 half of the time, and 1 the other half. Namely,

Conjecture 4

(Goldfeld)
.

[
6
]

Let
E_{k}
be the quadratic twist of
E
by
k
. Then

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{|k|<X}rk(E_{k})}{\#\{k:|k|<X\}}=\frac{1}{2}.

In our construction, we will relate the rational points on our superelliptic curve to the rational points on a quadratic twist

of the Mordell curve
E
by a prime

, where

E:Y^{2}=X^{3}-1,

and

E_{p}:pY^{2}=X^{3}-1.

We want to choose

so that

. Juyal, Moody and Roy
[
9
]
use the technique of 2-descent to show it is sufficient to choose

.

Theorem 5
.

[
9
]

Let
E:Y^{2}=X^{3}-1
be the Mordell curve, and let

E_{p}:pY^{2}=X^{3}-1

denote its twist by a prime

. Then for all primes
p\equiv 5\mod 12
, we have

E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})\cong\{(1,0),\infty\}.

4.
Choosing a prime

We continue with our construction of

Proposition 6

Let

as above. Given a finite set of values

Every

Lemma 7
.

Let

as above. For all but finitely many primes

,

(h(X)-1)g(X,p)+1

is separable.

Proof.

First, because

is irreducible, its discriminant is non-zero and has finite support. For those primes

is separable modulo
p
.
Now rewrite the polynomial

as

(2)

(h(X)-1)g(X,t)+1\;=\;h(X)\;+\;tH(X)\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{6r+n}c_{i}X^{i}.

where

is some polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. For all but finitely many values of
p
, we can specialise

at

so that the valuations of the coefficients are:

v_{p}(c_{i})=\begin{cases}0&0\leq i\leq n,\\
1&n+1\leq i\leq 6r+n.\end{cases}

Thus the Newton polygon of the polynomial (
2
)
consists of two segments of lengths
n
and
6r

and slopes
0
and

respectively.
By the theory of Newton polygons,
[
7
]
*7.2.3
(
2
) will factor as the product of two irreducible polynomials in

(h(X)-1)g(X,p)+1\;=\;F_{1}(X)F_{2}(X)

corresponding to the two line segments of the Newton polygon.
Here

has degree

and its roots have valuation
0
,
and

has degree

and its roots have valuation

.
In particular,

are coprime.
Moreover,

is separable modulo

is separable in

, for all but finitely many

.
The second factor

is irreducible because it is pure of slope

. This means their product

is separable for all but finitely many primes

.
∎

If

Suppose

. First, let

be a prime such that

. Assume

. As the

Now, let

.
Now, assume

6.
Proof of Theorem
1

We have shown that given an acceptable set
S\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, and a finite subset
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{C}
,
there exists a separable polynomial
f_{S,\mathcal{B}}(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
of some degree

, not vanishing at any point in

,
such that its associated superelliptic curve has rational point set

.
In this section, we show how we can generalise this to sets in
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, and glue together the superelliptic curves to generalise the result to finite sets in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Remark 12
.

If, in the statement of Theorem

, we had not asked that

to find a smooth curve

Lemma 13

Let

We write

Consider the following subvarieties of

C\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be the projective closure of the affine variety

C\;=\;V\left(\,x_{2}^{d}-f_{2}(x_{1})\,,\,x_{3}^{d}-f_{3}(x_{1})\,,\,\cdots\,,\,x_{n}^{d}-f_{n}(x_{1})\,\right)\;\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}.

Note that
C
has function field

\mathbb{Q}(C)=\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt[d]{f_{2}(X)},\sqrt[d]{f_{3}(X)},\dotsc,\sqrt[d]{f_{n}(X)}\right)

which is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
. Thus
C
is an absolutely irreducible curve.
It follows from Theorem
2
that
C(\mathbb{Q})=\{P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r}\}
. Moreover, we note
that the construction ensures that
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{n}
have pairwise distinct complex roots. It follows from this,
using the Jacobian criterion,
that the curve
C
is smooth.

∎

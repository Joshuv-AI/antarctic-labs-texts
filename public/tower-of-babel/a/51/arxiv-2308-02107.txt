Well-posedness for Ohkitani model and long-time existence for surface quasi-geostrophic equations
Dongho Chae, In-Jee Jeong, Jungkyoung Na, Sung-Jin Oh

Abstract
We consider the Cauchy problem for the logarithmically singular surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) equation, introduced by Ohkitani, ∂ t θ − ∇ ⟂ ​ log ​ ( 10 + ( − Δ ) 1 2 ) ​ θ ⋅ ∇ θ = 0 , \begin{split}\partial_{t}\theta-\nabla^{\perp}\log(10+(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}})\theta\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\end{split} and establish local existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions in the scale of Sobolev spaces with exponent decreasing with time. Such a decrease of the Sobolev exponent is necessary, as we have shown in the companion paper [ 7 ] that the problem is strongly ill-posed in any fixed Sobolev spaces. The time dependence of the Sobolev exponent can be removed when there is a dissipation term strictly stronger than log. These results improve wellposedness statements by Chae, Constantin, Córdoba, Gancedo, and Wu in [ 4 ] . This well-posedness result can be applied to describe the long-time dynamics of the δ \delta -SQG equations, defined by ∂ t θ + ∇ ⟂ ( 10 + ( − Δ ) 1 2 ) − δ ​ θ ⋅ ∇ θ = 0 , \begin{split}\partial_{t}\theta+\nabla^{\perp}(10+(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-\delta}\theta\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\end{split} for all sufficiently small δ > 0 \delta>0 depending on the size of the initial data. For the same range of δ \delta , we establish global well-posedness of smooth solutions to the dissipative SQG equations.

One may extend the above well-posedness result to the cases where the multiplier

1.3
Ideas of the proof

To understand how such a losing estimate in the scale of time-dependent Sobolev spaces is possible even when the time-independent estimate fails, it is instructive to make an analogy with the well-known Cauchy--Kovalevskaya theorem, which gives local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem in the analytic class when the loss of derivative is less than or equal to one
2
2

2

We do not expect the analytic regularity to propagate in time for the gSQG equation (
1.10
) with
singular

\Gamma
; that is, Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem fails. Indeed, one may prove some analytic (and even Gevrey) illposedness results for linear and nonlinear SQG equations near quadratically degenerate shear steady states, closely following the arguments of
[
7
,
31
]
. Note that formally the loss of derivative in the gSQG equation with singular
\Gamma
is always strictly larger than one.
.

To be concrete, the key point in the proof of Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem for the model equation
\partial_{t}b=\Lambda b
on
\mathbb{R}
with initial data

is that if one considers the time-dependent multiplier
m(t,\xi):=\exp((c_{0}-Ct)|\xi|),
(with
c_{0}>0
depending on the radius of analyticity of

) then we have

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|{m(t,\xi)\widehat{b}(\xi)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}m(t,\xi)\widehat{b}(\xi)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int m^{2}(t,\xi)\overline{\widehat{b}(\xi)}\partial_{t}\widehat{b}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{split}

so that for
C>0
large, we can absorb the right hand side into the positive term on the left hand side. Now, as a reasonable linear model of the logarithmically singular SQG equation whose initial value problem is illposed in a
fixed
Sobolev space
[
7
,
31
]

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b=x\partial_{x}\ln(10+\Lambda)b+\frac{1}{2}\ln(10+\Lambda)b\end{split}

(1.15)

\mathbb{R}
. More generally, we can consider

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b=x\partial_{x}\Gamma(\Lambda)b+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(\Lambda)b,\end{split}

(1.16)

where
\Gamma
is a multiplier with the symbol
\gamma=\gamma(|\xi|)\geq 0

singular
in the sense that
\gamma(|\xi|)\rightarrow\infty
as
|\xi|\rightarrow\infty
. When
\gamma
is
not

\gamma
. The presence of last terms in (

1.16

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|{\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\|{\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0\end{split}

which models conservation of

1.10

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\widehat{b}(\xi)=-\partial_{\xi}(\xi\gamma(|\xi|)\widehat{b})+\frac{1}{2}\gamma(|\xi|)\widehat{b},\end{split}

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int m^{2}(t,\xi)|\widehat{b}(t,\xi)|^{2}=\int m\dot{m}|\widehat{b}|^{2}+\Re\int m^{2}\,\overline{\widehat{b}}\left(-\partial_{\xi}(\xi\gamma(|\xi|)\widehat{b})+\frac{1}{2}\gamma(|\xi|)\widehat{b}\right)\end{split}

and

\begin{split}-\Re\int m^{2}\,\overline{\widehat{b}}\partial_{\xi}(\xi\gamma(|\xi|)\widehat{b})&=\Re\int\partial_{\xi}(m^{2}\,\overline{\widehat{b}})\xi\gamma(|\xi|)\widehat{b}=\Re\int(2m\partial_{\xi}m\overline{\widehat{b}}+m^{2}\partial_{\xi}\overline{\widehat{b}})\xi\gamma(|\xi|)\widehat{b}\\

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int m^{2}(t,\xi)|\widehat{b}(t,\xi)|^{2}=\int m(\xi)\left(\dot{m}+\xi\gamma(|\xi|)\partial_{\xi}m-m\xi\partial_{\xi}\gamma(|\xi|)\right)|\widehat{b}|^{2}\leq 0\end{split}

\begin{split}\dot{m}+\xi\gamma(|\xi|)\partial_{\xi}m-m\xi\partial_{\xi}\gamma(|\xi|)\leq 0.\end{split}

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\epsilon>0

Proof.

We note that

\begin{split}&{\left\|\left[\partial_{j}\left(I-(10+\Lambda)^{-\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},f\right]\Lambda^{s}g\right\|}_{L^{2}}\leq{\left\|\int\left|\Phi_{j}(\xi,\eta)\right||\hat{f}(\eta)||\xi-\eta|^{s}|\hat{g}(\xi-\eta)|\,d\eta\right\|}_{L^{2}_{\xi}},\end{split}

where

\Phi_{j}(\xi,\eta):=\xi_{j}\left(1-(10+|\xi|)^{-\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-(\xi-\eta)_{j}\left(1-(10+|\xi-\eta|)^{-\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the mean value theorem, we obtain
\rho\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{split}\left|\Phi_{j}(\xi,\eta)\right|&\leq C|\eta|\left(\left(1-(10+|\rho\xi+(1-\rho)(\xi-\eta)|)^{-\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\
&\left.\qquad+\delta(10+|\rho\xi+(1-\rho)(\xi-\eta)|)^{-\delta}\left(1-(10+|\rho\xi+(1-\rho)(\xi-\eta)|)^{-\delta}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\\
&=C|\eta|\left(\left(1-(10+|(\xi-\eta)+\rho\eta|)^{-\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\
&\left.\qquad+\delta(10+|(\xi-\eta)+\rho\eta|)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left((10+|(\xi-\eta)+\rho\eta|)^{\delta}-1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).\end{split}

Using

\begin{split}\left(1-(10+|(\xi-\eta)+\rho\eta|)^{-\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(10+|(\xi-\eta)+\rho\eta|)\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(10+|\xi-\eta|)+\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(10+|\eta|)\right),\end{split}

\begin{split}&\delta(10+|(\xi-\eta)+\rho\eta|)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left((10+|(\xi-\eta)+\rho\eta|)^{\delta}-1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

we have

\begin{split}\left|\Phi_{j}(\xi,\eta)\right|&\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(10+|\xi-\eta|)|\eta|\left(1+\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(10+|\eta|)\right).\end{split}

Hence, Young’s convolution inequality gives

\begin{split}&{\left\|\left[\partial_{j}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(10+\Lambda),f\right]\Lambda^{s}g\right\|}_{L^{2}}\\

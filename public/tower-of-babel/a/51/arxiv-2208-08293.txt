On definable groups in real closed fields with a generic derivation, and related structures
Ya’acov Peterzil, Anand Pillay, Françoise Point

Abstract
We study finite-dimensional groups definable in models of the theory R ​ C ​ F ∂ RCF_{\partial} of real closed fields with a generic derivation (also known as C ​ O ​ D ​ F CODF , the theory of closed ordered differential field [ 25 ] ). We prove that any such group Γ \Gamma definably embeds in a semialgebraic group G G . We explain how our methods work in the general context of strongly model complete theories T T of large “geometric” fields with a generic derivation, which includes the cases where T T is the theory of pseudofinite fields and T = T ​ h ​ ( ℚ p ) T=Th({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}) . We also give a general theorem on recovering a definable group from generic data in the context of geometric theories. Finally we extend the methods to o o -minimal theories with a generic derivation, due to Fornasiero and Kaplan, [ 8 ] , and open theories of topological fields with a generic derivation, due to Cubides-Kovacsics and the third author, [ 15 ] .

Introduction

The aim is to describe finite-dimensional definable groups in models of the theory
RCF_{\partial}
, also known as closed ordered differential fields. In particular any such group
G
definably embeds in a group
H
which is semialgebraic, namely definable in the restriction to the field language.

The case of infinite-dimensional groups is of course also interesting and will be considered in future work.

The proof goes through various stages. First, in Section 3, from a finite-dimensional group

we obtain a “generically defined” group in

, which we may call a semialgebraic pre-group. Then, in Section 4, we construct, from the generically defined group, a definable group

in
RCF

G

Although the results and proofs are first presented for groups definable in models of
RCF_{\partial}
, they go through in various ways to more general or related contexts, as mentioned in the abstract. Details are given below and in subsequent sections.

One of the reasons that we start with the theory

is that it is fairly well-known and of interest to several researchers.

In a paper in preparation we will adapt the theory of algebraic

Optimal insider control of stochastic Volterra equations
Olfa Draouil

Abstract
We study the problem of optimal inside control of a stochastic Volterra equation driven by a Brownian motion and a Poisson random measure. We prove a sufficient and a necessary maximum principle for the optimal control when the trader has only partial information available to her decisions and on the other hand, may have some inside information about the future of the system. The results are applied to the problem of finding the optimal insider portfolio in a financial market where the risky asset price is given by a stochastic Volterra equation.

Introduction

In this paper we consider an optimal control problem for a stochastic process
X(t)=X(t,Z)=X(t,z)|_{z=Z}
defined as the solution of a stochastic Volterra (integral) equation (SVIE) given by

\displaystyle X(t)=X^{u}(t,Z)=\xi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z))dB(s)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z),\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)

(1.1)

Here
B(t)
and
\tilde{N}(dt,d\zeta)
is a Brownian motion and an independent compensated Poisson random measure, respectively, jointly defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},P)
satisfying the usual conditions.
The process
u(t)=u(t,Z)=u(t,z)_{z=Z}
is our insider control
process, where
Z
is a given
\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}
-measurable random variable for some
T_{0}>0
, representing
the inside information available to the controller.
Note that from (
1
) we get:

\displaystyle dX(t)=\xi^{\prime}(t)dt+b(t,t,X(t,Z),u(t,Z))dt+(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z))ds)dt

\displaystyle+\sigma(t,t,X(t,Z),u(t,Z))dB(t)+(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z))dB(s))dt

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,t,X(t,Z),u(t,Z),\zeta)\tilde{N}(dt,d\zeta)+(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z),\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta))dt.

(1.2)

We assume that the inside information is of
initial enlargement
type. Specifically, we assume that the inside filtration
\mathbb{H}
has the form

\mathbb{H}=\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T},\text{ where }\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{t}\vee\sigma(Z)

(1.3)

for all
t
, where
Z
is a given
\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}
-measurable random variable, for some
T_{0}>0
(constant).
Here and in the following we use the right-continuous version of
\mathbb{H}
, i.e. we put

\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{H}_{t^{+}}=\bigcap_{s>t}\mathcal{H}_{s}.

We also assume that the
Donsker delta functional
of
Z
exists (see below). This assumption implies that the Jacod condition holds, and hence that
B(\cdot)
and
N(\cdot,\cdot)
are semimartingales with respect to
\mathbb{H}
. See e.g.
[
DØ2
]
for details.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a given family of admissible controls, required to be
\mathcal{K}_{t}=\mathcal{G}_{t}\vee\sigma(Z)
-predictable, where
\mathbb{G}=\{\mathcal{G}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
is a given subfiltration of
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
in the sense that
\mathcal{G}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. That is mean that
\mathcal{K}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
We assume that the value at time
t
of our insider control process
u(t,x)
is allowed to depend on both
Z
and
\mathcal{G}_{t}
. In other words,
u(.,x)
is assumed to be
\mathbb{K}
-adapted. Therefore it has the form

u(t,\omega)=u_{1}(t,Z,\omega)

(1.4)

for some function
u_{1}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that
u_{1}(.,z)
is
\mathbb{G}
-adapted for each
z\in\mathbb{R}
. For simplicity (albeit with some abuse of notation) we will in the following write
u
instead of
u_{1}
.
In other word, we study the case when the controller has only partial information available to her decisions and on the
other hand, may have some inside information about the
future of the system.

Let
\mathbb{U}
denote the set of admissible control values. We assume that the functions

\displaystyle b(t,s,x,u,z)

\displaystyle=b(t,s,x,u,z,\omega):[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\sigma(t,s,x,u,z)

\displaystyle=\sigma(t,s,x,u,z,\omega):[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\gamma(t,s,x,u,z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\gamma(t,s,x,u,z,\zeta,\omega):[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}

are given bounded

functions with respect to
t,x
and
u
and
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes in
(s,\omega)
for each given
t,x,u,z,\zeta
.
The
performance functional

of a control process

is defined by

J(u)=\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X(t),u(t,Z),Z)dt+g(X(T),Z)],

(1.6)

where

\displaystyle f(t,x,u,z):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle g(x,z):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}

(1.7)

are given bounded functions,
C^{1}
with respect to
x
and
u
,
f
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted for each
x,u,z
and

is
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable for each

. The functions

and

are called the

and

, respectively. For completeness of the presentation we allow these functions to depend explicitly on the future value
Z
also, although this would not be the typical case in applications. But it could be that
f
and
g
are influenced by the future value
Z
directly through the action of an insider, in addition to being influenced indirectly through the control process
u
and the corresponding state process
X
.

Problem 1.1

Find
u^{\star}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{A}}J(u)=J(u^{\star}).

(1.8)

2
The Donsker delta functional

To study this problem we adapt the technique of the paper
[
DØ1
]
to the stochastic Volterra equation (SVE) and we combine this with the method for optimal control of SVE developed in
[
AØ
]
. We first recall briefly the definition and basic properties of the Donsker delta functional:

Definition 2.1

Let
Z:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a random variable which also belongs to
(\mathcal{S})^{\ast}
. Then a continuous functional

\delta_{Z}(.):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(\mathcal{S})^{\ast}

(2.1)

is called a Donsker delta functional of
Z
if it has the property that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(z)\delta_{Z}(z)dz=g(Z)\quad a.s.

(2.2)

for all (measurable)
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that the integral converges.

For example, consider the special case when
Z
is a first order chaos random variable of the form

Z=Z(T_{0});\text{ where }Z(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)dB(s)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s,\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta),\mbox{ for }t\in[0,T_{0}]

(2.3)

for some deterministic functions
\beta\neq 0,\psi
such that

\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\{\beta^{2}(t)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{2}(t,\zeta)\nu(d\zeta)\}dt<\infty\text{ a.s. }

(2.4)

and for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\rho>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}e^{\rho\zeta}\nu(d\zeta)<\infty.\\

This condition implies that the polynomials are dense in
L^{2}(\mu)
, where
d\mu(\zeta)=\zeta^{2}d\nu(\zeta)
. It also guarantees that the measure
\nu
integrates all polynomials of degree
\geq 2
.

In this case it is well known (see e.g.
[
MØP
]
,
[
DiØ1
]
, Theorem 3.5, and
[
DØP
]
,
[
DiØ2
]
) that the Donsker delta functional exists in
(\mathcal{S})^{\ast}
and is given
by

\displaystyle\delta_{Z}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp^{\diamond}\big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{ix\psi(s,\zeta)}-1)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}ix\beta(s)dB(s)

(2.5)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{ix\psi(s,\zeta)}-1-ix\psi(s,\zeta))\nu(d\zeta)-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\beta^{2}(s)\}ds-ixz\big]dx,

where
\exp^{\diamond}
denotes the Wick exponential.
Moreover, we have for
t<T_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\big[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ix\psi(s,\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)+\int_{0}^{t}ix\beta(s)dB(s)

(2.6)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{ix\psi(s,\zeta)}-1-ix\psi(s,\zeta))\nu(d\zeta)ds-\int_{t}^{T_{0}}\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\beta^{2}(s)ds-ixz\big]dx.

(2.7)

If
D_{t}
and
D_{t,\zeta}
denotes the
Hida-Malliavin derivative
at
t
and
t,\zeta
with respect to
B
and
\tilde{N}
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\big[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ix\psi(s,\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)+\int_{0}^{t}ix\beta(s)dB(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{ix\psi(s,\zeta)}-1-ix\psi(s,\zeta))\nu(d\zeta)ds-\int_{t}^{T_{0}}\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\beta^{2}(s)ds-ixz\big]ix\beta(t)dx

(2.8)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[D_{t,z}\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\big[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ix\psi(s,\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)+\int_{0}^{t}ix\beta(s)dB(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{ix\psi(s,\zeta)}-1-ix\psi(s,\zeta))\nu(d\zeta)ds-\int_{t}^{T_{0}}\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\beta^{2}(s)ds-ixz\big](e^{ix\psi(t,z)}-1)dx.

(2.9)

For more information about the Donsker delta functional, Hida-Malliavin calculus and their properties, see
[
DØ1
]
.

From now on we assume that
Z
is a given random variable which also belongs to
(\mathcal{S})^{\ast}
, with a Donsker delta functional
\delta_{Z}(z)\in(\mathcal{S})^{\ast}

satisfying

\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{T},P)

(2.10)

and

\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}(\mathbb{E}[D_{t}\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}])^{2}dt]<\infty,\text{ for all }z.

(2.11)

3
Transforming the insider control problem to a related parameterized non-insider problem

Since
X(t)
is
\mathbb{H}
-adapted, we get by using the definition of the Donsker delta functional
\delta_{Z}(z)
of
Z
that

X(t)=X(t,Z)=X(t,z)_{z=Z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}X(t,z)\delta_{Z}(z)dz

(3.1)

for some
z
-parameterized process
X(t,z)
which is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted for each
z
.
Then, again by the definition of the Donsker delta functional we can write

\displaystyle X(t)=\xi(t,Z)+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z))dB(s)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,s,X(s,Z),u(s,Z),\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)

\displaystyle=\xi(t,z)_{z=Z}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))_{z=Z}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))_{z=Z}dB(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z),\zeta)_{z=Z}\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(t,z)\delta_{Z}(z)dz+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))\delta_{Z}(z)dzds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))\delta_{Z}(z)dzdB(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z),\zeta)\delta_{Z}(z)dz\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\{\xi(t,z)+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))dB(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z),\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta)\}\delta_{Z}(z)dz.

(3.2)

Comparing (
3.1
) and (
3
) we see that (
3.1
) holds if we for each
z

X(t,z)
as the solution of the classical (but parameterized ) SVIE

\displaystyle dX(t,z)=\xi^{\prime}(t,z)dt+b(t,t,X(t,z),u(t,z))dt+(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))ds)dt

\displaystyle+\sigma(t,t,X(t,z),u(t,z))dB(t)+(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z))dB(s))dt

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,t,X(t,z),u(t,z),\zeta)\tilde{N}(dt,d\zeta)+(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}(t,s,X(s,z),u(s,z),\zeta)\tilde{N}(ds,d\zeta))dt.

(3.3)

As before let
\mathcal{A}
be the given family of admissible
\mathbb{K}-
adapted controls
u
.
Then in terms of
X(t,z)
the performance functional
J(u)
of a control process
u\in\mathcal{A}
defined in (
1.6
) gets the form

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X(t,Z),u(t,Z),Z)dt+g(X(T,Z),Z)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X(t,z),u(t,z),z)\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]dt

\displaystyle+g(X(T,z),z)\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\Big\}dz]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}j(u)(z)dz,

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle j(u)(z)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X(t,z),u(t,z),z)\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]dt

\displaystyle+g(X(T,z),z)\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}].

(3.5)

Thus we see that to maximize

for each value of the parameter

. Therefore Problem 1.1 is transformed into the problem

Problem 3.1

For each given
z\in\mathbb{R}
find
u^{\star}=u^{\star}(t,z)\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{A}}j(u)(z)=j(u^{\star})(z).

(3.6)

4
A sufficient-type maximum principle

In this section we will establish a sufficient maximum principle for Problem 3.1.

Problem 3.1 is a stochastic control problem with a standard (albeit parameterized) stochastic Volterra equation (
3
) for the state process
X(t,z)
, but with a non-standard performance functional given by (
3
). We can solve this problem by a modified maximum principle approach, as follows:

Let
L
and
L_{\zeta}
be the set of all stochastic processes with parameter space
[0,T]
and
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{0}
, respectively, where
\mathbb{R}_{0}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Define the Hamiltonian functionals:

H_{0}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(4.1)

and

H_{1}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\times L\times L_{\zeta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(4.2)

by

\displaystyle H_{0}(t,x,z,u,p,q,r):=f(t,x,z,u)E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]+b(t,t,x,z,u)p+\sigma(t,t,x,z,u)q

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(t,t,x,z,u,\zeta)r(\zeta)\nu(d\zeta)

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle H_{1}(t,x,z,u,p,D_{t}p(.),D_{t,\zeta}p(.)):=\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial b}{\partial s}(s,t,x,z,u)p(s,z)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}(s,t,x,z,u)E[D_{t}p(s,z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial s}(s,t,x,z,u,\zeta)E[D_{t,\zeta}p(s,z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\nu(d\zeta)ds.

(4.4)

Here
\mathcal{R}
denotes the set of all functions
r(\cdot):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that the last integral above converges.The quantities
p,q,r(\cdot)
are called the
adjoint variables
.
Define

H(t,x,z,u,p(.),q(.),r(.)):=H_{0}(t,x,z,u,p,q,r)+H_{1}(t,x,u,p(.),D_{t}p(.),D_{t,\zeta}p(.)).

(4.5)

The
adjoint processes

p(t,z),q(t,z),r(t,z,\zeta)
are defined as the solution of the
z
-parameterized backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dp(t,z)=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(t,z)dt+q(t,z)dB(t)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}r(t,z,\zeta)\tilde{N}(dt,d\zeta);\\
\quad(t,z)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}\\
p(T,z)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(X(T,z),z)\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}];\quad z\in\mathbb{R}\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

where

\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(t,z)=\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(t,X(t,z),u(t,z),z,p(t,z),q(t,z),r(t,z,.)).

(4.7)

We can now state the first maximum principle for our problem (
3.6
):

Theorem 4.1

[Sufficient-type maximum principle]

Let
\hat{u}\in\mathcal{A}
, and denote the associated solution of (
3
) and (
4.6
) by
\hat{X}(t,z)
and

(\hat{p}(t,z),\hat{q}(t,z),\hat{r}(t,z,\zeta))
, respectively. Assume that the following hold:

1.

x\rightarrow g(x,z)

2.

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{U}}E[H\big(t,\hat{X}(t,z),w,\widehat{p}(t,z),\widehat{q}(t,z),\hat{r}(t,z,\zeta)\big)|\mathcal{G}_{t}]

=E[H\big(t,\widehat{X}(t,z),\widehat{u}(t,z),\widehat{p}(t,z),\widehat{q}(t,z),\hat{r}(t,z,\zeta)\big)|\mathcal{G}_{t}]

Then

]

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(\widehat{X}(T,z))\mathbb{E}[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\tilde{X}(T,z)\Big]=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{p}(T,z)\widetilde{X}(T,z)]

Example: Optimal insider portfolio in a financial market modeled by a Volterra equation

In this example, we choose
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{F}
.
Consider a financial market where the unit price
S_{0}(t)
of the risk free asset is

S_{0}(t)=1,t\in[0,T]

(7.1)

and the unit price process
S(t)
of the risky asset has no jumps and is given by

\begin{cases}dS(t)=b_{0}(t,t,Z)S(t)dt+\sigma_{0}(t,t,Z)S(t)dB(t)\\
+(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial t}(t,s)S(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial t}(t,s)S(s)dB(s))dt;t\in[0,T]\\
S(0)>0\end{cases}

(7.2)

Then the wealth process
X(t)=X^{\Pi}(t)
associated to a portfolio
u(t)=\Pi(t)
, interpreted
as the fraction of the wealth invested in the risky asset at time
t
, is described by the linear stochastic Volterra equation

X(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t,s,Z)\Pi(s)X(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{0}(t,s,Z)\Pi(s)X(s)dB(s);t\geq 0

(7.3)

or, in differential form

\begin{cases}dX(t)=b_{0}(t,t,Z)\Pi(t)X(t)dt+\sigma_{0}(t,t,Z)\Pi(t)X(t)dB(t)\\
+(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial t}(t,s)\Pi(s)X(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial t}(t,s)\Pi(s)X(s)dB(s))dt;t\in[0,T]\\
X(0)=x_{0}>0\end{cases}

(7.4)

Let
U
be a given utility function. We want to find
\Pi\in\mathcal{A}
such that

J(\Pi^{*})=\sup_{\Pi\in\mathcal{A}}J(\Pi),

(7.5)

where

J(\Pi):=E[U(X^{\Pi}(T))].

(7.6)

Note that, in terms of our process
X(t,z)
we have

\begin{cases}dX(t,z)=b_{0}(t,t,z)\Pi(t)X(t,z)dt+\sigma_{0}(t,t,z)\pi(t,z)X(t,z)dB(t)\\
+(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial t}(t,s,z)\pi(s,z)X(s,z)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial t}(t,s,z)\pi(s,z)X(s,z)dB(s))dt;t\in[0,T]\\
X(0,z)=x_{0}>0\end{cases}

(7.7)

or in the integral form:

X(t,z)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t,s,z)\pi(s,z)X(s,z)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{0}(t,s,z)\pi(s,z)X(s,z)dB(s);t\in[0,T]

(7.8)

We assume that
b_{0}(t,s,z)=b_{0}(t,s,z,\omega)
and
\sigma_{0}(t,s,z)=\sigma_{0}(t,s,z,\omega)
are given bounded processes, and that

b_{0}(t,s)
and
\sigma_{0}(t,s)
are
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable for all
s,t
and
C^{1}
with respect to

t
for all
s
, a.s.
We also assume that

\sigma_{0}(t,s,z)\geq c_{0}\text{ a.s. for all t, }s\in[0,T]\text{ for some constant }c_{0}>0.

(7.9)

We assume that
x_{0}>0
. If
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
, then it follows that
X(t,z)>0
for all
t\in[0,T]
. To see this, note that
from (
7.7
) we get

\displaystyle X(t,z)=x_{0}\exp(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{0}(s,s,z)\pi(s,z)dB(s)+\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(s,s,z)\pi(s,z)-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}(s,s,z)\pi^{2}(s,z)+\alpha(s,z)ds)>0,

(7.10)

where

\displaystyle\alpha(s,z):=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial s}(s,r,z)\pi(r,z)X(r,z)dr+\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial s}(s,r,z)\pi(r,z)X(r,z)dB(r).

(7.11)

The performance functional gets the form

J(\pi)=E[U(X(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]]

(7.12)

This is a problem of the type investigated in the previous sections (in the special case with
no jumps) and we can apply the results there to solve it, as follows:
The Hamiltonian gets the form, with
u=\pi
,

\displaystyle H(t,x,z,\pi,p,q)=b_{0}(t,t,z)\pi xp+\sigma_{0}(t,t,z)\pi xq

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)\pi xp(s,z)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)\pi xE[D_{t}p(s,z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]ds

(7.13)

while the BSDE of the adjoint processes becomes

\begin{cases}dp(t,z)=[b_{0}(t,t,z)\pi(t,z)p(t,z)+\sigma_{0}(t,t)\pi(t,z)q(t,z)\\
+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)\pi(s,z)p(s,z)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)\pi(s,z)E[D_{t}p(s)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]ds]dt\\
+q(t,z)dB(t);t\in[0,T]\\
p(T,z)=U^{\prime}(X(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\end{cases}

(7.14)

Suppose there exists an optimal control
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
for (5.18) with corresponding
\hat{X},\hat{p},\hat{q}
. Then,

E[\frac{\partial H}{\partial\pi}(t,\hat{X}(t,z),\pi,\hat{p},\hat{q})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]_{\pi=\hat{\pi}(t)}=0

(7.15)

i.e.,

\displaystyle E[b_{0}(t,t,z)\hat{X}(t,z)\hat{p}(t,z)+\sigma_{0}(t,t,z)\hat{X}(t)\hat{q}(t,z)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)\hat{X}(t,z)\hat{p}(s,z)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)\hat{X}(t,z)E[D_{t}\hat{p}(s,z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]ds|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0.

(7.16)

Since
\hat{X}(t)>0
, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle b_{0}(t,t,z)\hat{p}(t)+\sigma_{0}(t,t,z)\hat{q}(t)

\displaystyle+E[\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)\hat{p}(s)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)E[D_{t}\hat{p}(s)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]ds|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0.

(7.17)

We deduce that the corresponding BSDE (
7.14
) reduces to

\begin{cases}d\hat{p}(t,z)=\hat{q}(t,z)dB(t);0\leq t\leq T\\
\hat{p}(T,z)=U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}],\end{cases}

(7.18)

which has the unique solution

\hat{p}(t,z)=E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{t}],\quad\hat{q}(t,z)=D_{t}\hat{p}(t,z).

(7.19)

Substituted (
7.19
) into (
7
), this gives the equation

\displaystyle E[b_{0}(t,t,z)U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]+\sigma_{0}(t,t,z)D_{t}\{U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{s}]ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)E[D_{t}\{U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]ds|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0,

(7.20)

where we have used that

\displaystyle D_{t}E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=E[D_{t}\{U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\}|\mathcal{F}_{t}],

(7.21)

Equation (
7
) can be simplified to

\displaystyle b_{0}(t,t,z)E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{t}]+\sigma_{0}(t,t,z)E[D_{t}\{U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle+E[\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial b_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]ds|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle+E[\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial\sigma_{0}}{\partial s}(s,t,z)D_{t}\{U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]\}ds|\mathcal{F}t]=0,

(7.22)

or

\sigma_{0}(T,t,z)D_{t}E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{t}]+b_{0}(T,t,z)E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0.

(7.23)

By (
7.9
) we see that (
7.23
) can be written

\frac{D_{t}Y(t)}{Y(t)}=-\frac{b_{0}(T,t,z)}{\sigma_{0}(T,t,z)},

(7.24)

where

Y(t,z)=E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{t}].

(7.25)

By the chain rule for Malliavin derivatives, we deduce from that

D_{t}(lnY(t,z))=-\frac{b_{0}(T,t,z)}{\sigma_{0}(T,t,z)}.

(7.26)

On the other hand, since
Y(t,z)
is a positive martingale, there exists an adapted process
\theta_{0}(t,z)
such that

dY(t,z)=\theta_{0}(t,z)Y(t,z)dB(t)

(7.27)

i.e.,

Y(t,z)=Y(0,z)\exp(\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{0}(s,z)dB(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{0}^{2}(s,z)ds).:

(7.28)

From (
7.28
) we get

D_{t}(\ln Y(t,z))=D_{t}(\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{0}(s,z)dB(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{0}^{2}(s,z)ds)=\theta_{0}(t,z),

(7.29)

Comparing (
7.26
) and (
7.29
) we conclude that

\theta_{0}(t,z)=-\frac{b_{0}(T,t,z)}{\sigma_{0}(T,t,z)}

(7.30)

and hence, by (
7.25
),

\displaystyle E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=Y(t,z)

\displaystyle=E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]]\exp(\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{0}(s,z)dB(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{0}^{2}(s,z)ds).

(7.31)

c=E[U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]].

(7.32)

From (
7

U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{T}]=c\exp(\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{0}(s,z)dB(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{0}^{2}(s,z)ds).

(7.33)

To make this more explicit, we proceed as follows:
Define

M(t,z):=E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

(7.34)

Then by the generalized Clark-Ocone theorem

\begin{cases}dM(t,z)=E[D_{t}\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]dB(t)=\Phi(t,z)M(t,z)dB(t)\\
M(0,z)=1\end{cases}

(7.35)

where

\displaystyle\Phi(t,z)=\frac{E[D_{t}\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]}{E[\delta_{Z}(z)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]}.

(7.36)

Solving this SDE for

M(t,z)=\exp(\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(s,z)dB(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{2}(s,z)ds).

(7.37)

Substituting this into (
7.33

U^{\prime}(\hat{X}(T,z))=c\exp(\int_{0}^{T}(\theta_{0}(s,z)-\Phi(s,z))dB(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}(\theta_{0}^{2}(s,z)-\Phi^{2}(s,z))ds).

(7.38)

Then we can deduce that

\hat{X}(T,z)=(U^{\prime})^{-1}(c\exp(\int_{0}^{T}(\theta_{0}(s,z)-\Phi(s,z))dB(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}(\theta_{0}^{2}(s,z)-\Phi^{2}(s,z))ds))=F(c).

(7.39)

On the other hand, if we define

\hat{K}(t,s,z):=\sigma_{0}(t,s,z)\hat{\pi}(s,z)\hat{X}(s,z),

(7.40)

then by (
7.8
), the pair

\hat{X}(t,z)=F(c)-\int_{t}^{T}\frac{b_{0}(t,s,z)}{\sigma_{0}(t,s,z)}\hat{K}(t,s,z)ds-\int_{t}^{T}\hat{K}(t,s,z)dB(s);0\leq s\leq T.

(7.41)

By Theorem 3.2 in [20] the solution of this equation is unique. Putting
t=0
and taking expectation in
(
7.41

x_{0}=E[F(c)]-\int_{0}^{T}E[\frac{b_{0}(t,s,z)}{\sigma_{0}(t,s,z)}\hat{K}(t,s,z)]ds.

(7.42)

This equation determines implicitly the value of
c
.
Hence by (
7.39
) we have found the optimal terminal wealth
\hat{X}(T,z)
. Then, finally we obtain the optimal
portfolio
\hat{\pi}
by (
7.40
).
Conversely, since the functions
x\rightarrow U(x)
and
(x,\pi)\rightarrow H(t,x,\pi,\hat{p},\hat{q})

\hat{\pi}
found
above satisfies the conditions of Theorem 4.1, and hence
\hat{\pi}
is indeed optimal.

Theorem 7.1

\sigma_{0}(t,s)>0

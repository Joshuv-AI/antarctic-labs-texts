Abstract
. We focus on checking the validity of the half-plane property on two prominent classes of transversal matroids, namely lattice path matroids and bicircular matroids. We show that lattice path matroids satisfy the half-plane property. Subsequently, we show an explicit example of a bicircular matroid that is not a positroid and discuss the negative correlation properties of bases of transversal matroids. We prove that sparse paving matroids do not satisfy the Rayleigh property, which helps us gain new perspectives about conjectures on negative correlation in basis elements of matroids in general.

transversal matroids. We realize that if all the coefficients have the same value, then the stability of the matching polynomials also applies stability of the basis-generating polynomial. The pair
(G,A)
is called
nice
if there exists a collection
\{\lambda_{e}\},e\in E
of nonnegative edge weights so that
c(S;\lambda)
has the same nonzero value for all bases
S
of
\mathsf{M}[G,A]
.

Definition 14
.

The transversal matroid
\mathsf{M}
is called
nice
if there exists a nice pair
(G,A)
such that
\mathsf{M}\simeq\mathsf{M}[G,A]
.

4.
Lattice path matroids satisfy the half-plane property

We first recall the iterative description of lattice path matroids proven in
[
7
]
,

Theorem 15

(Theorem 6.7
[
7
]
)
.

A matroid
\mathsf{M}
is a lattice path matroid if and only if the ground set can be written as
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}\}
so that each restriction
\mathsf{M}_{i}:=\mathsf{M}|\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i}\}
is formed from
\mathsf{M}_{i-1}
by either

(i)

adding
x_{i}
as a coloop,

(ii)

adding
x_{i}
as a loop, or

(iii)

adding
x_{i}
via the principal extension of
\mathsf{M}_{i-1}
generated by the closure of an independent set of the form
\{x_{h},x_{h+1},\ldots,x_{i-1}\}
for some
h
with
h<i
.

We also recall that the half-plane property for a matroid
\mathsf{M}
is conserved under taking minors, duals, 2-sums, principal extensions, principal truncations, and direct sums
[
13
, Section 4]
.

We also would like to mention some important examples of lattice path matroids, that we know satisfy the half-plane property. Firstly, uniform matroids, which are known to be lattice path matroids, do satisfy the half-plane property
[
13
, Section 9]
. Additionally, lattice path matroids that are snakes are known to be graphical matroids
[
27
, Theorem 2.2]
which means that snakes are regular matroids, which in turn implies that basis-generating polynomials for snakes are determinantal, hence snakes are SOS- Rayleigh, which means they are strongly Rayleigh and therefore snakes do satisfy the half-plane property.

With these results, we now can present the following result on lattice path matroids,

Theorem 16
.

Lattice path matroids satisfy the half-plane property.

Proof.

We consider
\mathsf{M}
to be a rank
k
lattice path matroid on
n
elements. Firstly, we consider the case when
\mathsf{M}
is connected. We proceed in our proof via induction on the number of elements
n
of
\mathsf{M}
. For the base case, we consider
n=2
, we realize that
\mathsf{M}
is either a uniform matroid or a snake, and in both these cases we realize that the basis generating polynomial does satisfy the half-plane property as proven previously in
[
13
,
27
]
.

Hence, we proceed with the case where we consider the case that all lattice path matroids on
n
elements satisfy the half-plane property. We now consider a lattice path matroid
M
on
n+1
elements and by Theorem
15
we know that
M
is obtained in a recursive way by the addition of a loop, coloop, or via principal truncation to a matroid
M^{\prime}
on
n
elements. In the case of the addition of a loop, we know that the basis generating polynomial of
M
and
M^{\prime}
is the same, and
M^{\prime}
does satisfy the half-plane property because of our induction hypothesis, therefore
M
also satisfies the half-plane property. Similarly, if we consider the addition of a coloop
e
and if
f
is the basis generating polynomial of
\mathsf{M}^{\prime}
, then
f
satisfies the half-plane property and the basis generating polynomial of
\mathsf{M}
is
g=x_{e}\cdot f
. We compute the
Rayleigh difference

\Delta_{i,j}
for the polynomial
g
for
\{i,j\}\subseteq\{[n]\cup e\}

(i\neq e,j\neq e)
,

\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}=x_{e}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}},\quad\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}=x_{e}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=x_{e}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}

\Delta_{ij}(g)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\cdot g=(x_{e})^{2}(\Delta_{ij}(f))\geq 0

and see that it is non-negative in this case. For the other possible case we consider
i=e
and
j\in[n]
, the Rayleigh difference is given as

\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{e}}=f,\quad\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}=x_{e}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}

\Delta_{ij}(g)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\cdot g=(x_{e}\cdot f\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}-x_{e}\cdot f\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}})=0

and is also non-negative in this case, which means that
M
also satisfies the half-plane property.

The remaining case is when the matroid
\mathsf{M}
is obtained via a principal extension of a matroid on
n
elements and we know that the half-plane property is conserved for a
nice
principal extension
[
13
, Proposition 4.12]
. Consider the set
F=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1}\}
and we consider principal extension of
\mathsf{M}
by the addition of an element
x_{i}
to
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{i-1}
via
F
. We know that the bases of
\text{tr}_{F}(\mathsf{M})
are of the form
B\setminus f
where
f\in F
and the bases of the principal extension
\mathsf{M}+_{F}x_{i}
are of the form

\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{M}+_{F}x_{i})=\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{M})\cup\{B\cup\{x_{i}\}:B\in\mathcal{B}(\text{tr}_{F}(\mathsf{M}))\}

To ensure that this principal extension is nice, the coefficients
\lambda_{f},f\in F
need to satisfy the equality

\sum_{f\in F:B\cup\{f\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{M})}\lambda_{f}=1

where
B\in\mathcal{B}(\text{tr}_{F}(\mathsf{M}))
. We see that for our given choice of
F
, let
S=\{f\in F:B\cup\{f\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{M}),B\in\mathcal{B}(\text{tr}_{F}(\mathsf{M}))\}\subseteq F
and let
|S|=k\leq|F|
. Then if fix the value of

\lambda_{j}=\frac{1}{k},j\in S

then we see that the equation for a nice principal extension is satisfied and therefore by
[
13
, Proposition 4.12]

\mathsf{M}
also satisfies the half-plane property in this case. With this, we prove that all connected lattice path matroids satisfy the half-plane property.

We now consider the case when the lattice path matroid is disconnected. In this case
\mathsf{M}
can be expressed as a direct sum of
\mathsf{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{M}_{k}
, where each
\mathsf{M}_{i}
is a connected lattice path matroid which we showed satisfy the half-plane property. But we know that the half-plane property is also conserved under taking direct sums
[
13
, Section 4.3]
, therefore
\mathsf{M}
satisfies the half-plane property in this case as well. Hence, the proof.

6.
Sparse paving matroids are not Rayleigh

We recall the following definitions,

Definition 24
.

A matroid
\mathsf{M}
is said to be
paving
if it has no circuits of size less than the rank
r(\mathsf{M})
.

Definition 25
.

A matroid
\mathsf{M}
is said to be
sparse paving
if
\mathsf{M}
and its dual
\mathsf{M}^{*}
both are paving.

Paving and sparse paving matroids are immensely important classes of matroids, especially in understanding the asymptotics of matroids, as it is conjectured that almost all matroids are paving. With the enumeration of all matroids on up to 8 elements done in
[
1
]
, it was speculated by Crapo and Rota that paving matroids would dominate any enumeration of matroids, which is stated as a conjecture in
[
32
, Conjecture 1.6]
and they also conjecture that asymptotically almost every matroid has a sparse paving matroid as a minor
[
32
, Conjecture 1.7]
. Then in
[
36
]
Pendavingh and Pol prove that on log scale, almost all matroids are sparse paving, i.e., if
m_{n}
denotes the number of matroids on
n
elements and
s_{n}
denotes the number of sparse paving matroids on
n
elements, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log s_{n}}{\log m_{n}}=1

and it is conjectured that if the log factor is removed, then almost all matroid are sparse paving.

In
[
24
]
, Jerrum proves the first result concerning negative correlation properties of sparse paving matroids.

Lemma 26

(Lemma 2
[
24
]
)
.

Sparse paving matroids are balanced.

At this juncture, we also would like to recall some results concerning 3-connected matroids. Firstly, in
[
29
]
Lowrance, Oxley, Semple and Welsh proved the following result about the asymptotics of 3-connected matroids,

Theorem 27

(Theorem 4.2
[
29
]
)
.

Almost all n-element matroids are 3-connected.

Also, 3-connectivity is intrinsically linked with 2-sums,

Theorem 28

(Theorem 8.3.1
[
35
]
)
.

A
2-
connected matroid
\mathsf{M}
is not
3-
connected if and only if
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{1}\oplus_{2}\mathsf{M}_{2}
for some matroids
\mathsf{M}_{1}
and
\mathsf{M}_{2}
, each of which has at least three elements and is isomorphic to a proper minor of
\mathsf{M}
.

We now prove the following result about sparse paving matroids,

Theorem 29
.

The class of sparse paving matroids is not Rayleigh.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and base our arguments on the dominating classes in the enumeration of matroids. We assume that sparse paving matroids are Rayleigh, and let
m_{n}
denote the number of matroids on
n
elements,
r_{n}
denote the number of Rayleigh matroids on
n
elements, and
s_{n}
denote the number of sparse paving matroids on n elements. Then by our assumption
s_{n}<r_{n}
. Also, since the class of Rayleigh matroids is closed under
2
-sums, the number of Rayleigh matroids that are obtained as a
2-
sum, which we denote by
r^{\text{2-sum}}_{n}
, provides a dominating class of matroids on log scale because by the work in
[
36
]
we know that sparse paving matroids dominate any enumeration of matroids on log-scale. But by Theorem
27
we know that even on log scale, almost all matroids are 3-connected, which would imply that they would not be obtained via a 2-sum. This gives us a contradiction. Hence, the proof.

7.
Conclusions and Future Work

Our results in this article help us understand the half-plane property, especially when considered over the class of transversal matroids
[
13
, Question 13.17]
. Some previously known classes of matroids that do satisfy the half-plane property are regular matroids, uniform matroids, sixth roots of unity matroids, and all matroids of rank two. Recently, in
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
, the authors conducted a census on all matroids on at most eight elements to list matroids that do not satisfy the half-plane property and they provide a list of 22 sparse paving matroids of rank four on eight elements that do not satisfy the half-plane property. The authors use a criterion of checking the half-plane property stated in
[
39
, Theorem 3]
which states that positive Rayleigh difference for a single pair of elements
(i,j)
is sufficient enough to check for half-plane property and the authors in
[
28
]
develop an algorithm based on this to verify half-plane property of matroids. This is a complementary approach to our results and can also be viable for checking the half-plane property for other classes of matroids.

Another aspect of our work is to understand the
c
-Rayleigh property for the basis generating polynomials of the class of matroids, especially for the class of transversal matroids. A polynomial
f\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
is called
c
-Rayleigh if

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}(x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}(x)\geq c\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}(x)f(x)

for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
1\leq i,j\leq n
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,\infty)
.

The following conditions are equivalent for any non-empty

The problem to verify this conjecture on transversal matroids was suggested in

Verify whether the class of bicircular matroids is Rayleigh. Do they also satisfy the half-plane property?

Strong coupling of antiferromagnetic resonance with sub-THz cavity fields
M. Białek † \dagger, J. Zhang, H. Yu, J.-Ph. Ansermet

Abstract
Strong coupling of electromagnetic cavity fields with antiferromagnetic spin waves in hematite ( α \alpha -Fe 2 O 3 ) was achieved above room temperature. A cube of hematite was placed in a metallic tube and transmission was measured, using a continuous-wave THz spectrometer. Spectra, collected as a function of temperature, reveal the formation of magnetic polaritons.

g_{s}=2

V_{c}=\pi r_{c}^{2}l_{c}\approx 0.528
mm

In Eq.

III.3
Higher cavity modes

Raw data for the spectra are dominated by multiple interferences that are not strongly interacting with the cavity and are weakly temperature-dependent.
We normalized both the measured spectra and fitted functions to a base frequency that is temperature-dependent and passes though the middle of the interaction. This reduced artifacts at the cavity mode frequency that would show if instead, we normalized for example to the first recorded spectrum. We could eliminate this interference background even better by calculating temperature-derivative spectra, i.e. by subtracting from one another successive spectra, thus having amplitude derivatives:

\frac{d|S_{21}|}{dT}[dB]=\frac{20}{\Delta T}\log_{10}\frac{|S_{21}(f,T+\Delta T)|}{|S_{21}(f,T)|},

(5)

and phase derivatives:

\frac{d(\arg{S_{21}})}{dT}=\frac{\arg S_{21}(f,T+\Delta T)-\arg S_{21}(f,T)}{\Delta T}.

(6)

Figure 4:

Temperature-derivative spectra: (a) magnitude and (c) phase. (b and d) Corresponding fits using Eq.
7

The advantage of looking at temperature-differential spectra is that it is possible to see small features, like a weak middle line passing through the interaction region (Fig.
4
a and c) that is almost invisible in normalized spectra. This middle line can be qualitatively explained by taking into account the 2nd cavity mode at
f_{2}=258.4

S_{21}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{a_{j}}{i(f-f_{j}(T))-\frac{\kappa_{j}}{2}+\frac{G_{j}^{2}}{i(f-f_{r}(T))-g/2}},

(7)

In Fig.
4

7

a_{2}=(4.5-i1.1)
GHz,
G_{2}=0.56

\kappa_{2}=14.7
GHz.
These values are consistent with our idea of a weak interaction with the 2nd cavity mode that has a lower quality factor that the 1st mode. This simple model is not fully sufficient above 250 GHz. We think that a more precise estimate might require taking into account other weakly interacting cavity modes or interaction between cavity modes that are mediated by the magnetic resonance. We found that the observed phase temperature-differential spectra (Fig.

IV
Summary

In conclusion, we observed strong coupling between the quasi-antiferromagnetic resonance (qAFMR) of a hematite cube (
\alpha
-Fe
2
O
3
) located in a cavity near room temperature. We showed a method of achieving a strong light-matter coupling regime in antiferromagnets which is applicable to a broad range of these materials
The avoided crossing, which is characteristic of polariton dynamics, occurred near 243 GHz. The cooperativity
C
, which is a measure of the coupling strength with respect to both the magnetic resonance and the cavity line widths (Eq.
2
), was estimated at 40.

Appendix C
C: 2-port analysis

Let us represent a cavity as a device with 2 ports. At each port, we distinguish incoming and outgoing waves. The total voltage and current at each port are given by

V_{i}=V_{i}^{+}+V_{i}^{-}\qquad I_{i}=I_{i}^{+}+I_{i}^{-}\qquad(i=1,2)

(8)

One way of characterizing the cavity is to specify the parameters
A,B,C,D
defined by

\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}\\
I_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}V_{2}\\
I_{2}\end{pmatrix}

(9)

For a two-port device in which each port sees the common impedance
Z_{3}
, the
A,B,C,D

parameters are given by
Pozar11

A=1\quad B=0\quad C=\frac{1}{Z_{3}}\quad D=1

(10)

The scattering matrix is defined by

\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}^{-}\\
V_{2}^{-}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{11}&S_{12}\\
S_{21}&S_{22}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}^{+}\\
V_{2}^{+}\end{pmatrix}

(11)

In particular, the
S_{21}
parameter is given by

S_{21}=\left.\frac{V_{2}^{-}}{V_{1}^{+}}\right|_{V_{2}^{+}=0}

(12)

It is straightforward to calculate that,

S_{21}=\frac{1}{1+Z_{0}/(2Z_{3})}

(13)

where
Z_{0}
is the transmission line (or wave guide) characteristic impedance at the ports.

Let us apply this formalism to analyze a magnetic material located inside the cavity. We represent the cavity by discrete
R,L,C
components in series. We assume that the measurement setup is such that away from a resonance of the cavity or from a resonance of the material in it, the signal propagates through the cavity without loss, i.e.
S_{21}=1
. We have,

Z_{3}=i\omega L+\frac{1}{i\omega C}+R

(14)

We assume that the
RLC
circuit is designed so that the transmission is nearly one at all frequencies, i.e.
Z_{3}\gg Z_{0}
. Then (
13
) yields,

S_{21}\approx 1-\frac{Z_{0}}{2Z_{3}}

(15)

We take into account the presence of the material in the cavity by assuming that the inductance
L
is filled entirely with a medium of an effective permittivity
\mu
written in the form of a Lorentzian line shape, so as to account for the magnetic resonance in the material

L=\mu L_{0}=(1+\frac{\Delta\mu\,\omega_{m}^{2}}{\omega_{m}^{2}-\omega^{2}-j\omega\gamma_{m}})L_{0},

(16)

where
L_{0}
is an inductance of an empty cavity,
\Delta\mu
is a unitless parameter that characterizes the coupling of the material to the cavity,
\omega_{m}
is its frequency and
\gamma_{m}
defines its width. Using
Z_{3}
given by (
14
) and (
16
), (
15
) gives,

S_{21}=1-\frac{i\omega Z_{0}C/2}{1+i\omega RC-\omega^{2}CL_{0}\left(1+\frac{\Delta\mu\,\omega_{m}^{2}}{\omega_{m}^{2}-\omega^{2}-i\omega\gamma_{m}}\right)}

(17)

Let us call
\omega_{c}^{2}=1/(L_{0}C)
in reference to the resonance of the empty cavity. We consider a situation when
\omega
,
\omega_{c}
and
\omega_{m}

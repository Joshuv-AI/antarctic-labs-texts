Local Solvability of quasilinear Pseudo- differential Operators of Real Principal Type
Nils Dencker Lund University

Abstract
. In this paper we prove local solvability of quasilinear pseudodifferential operators which has homogeneous principal symbol of real principal type. This is a generalization of Theorem A.1 in arXiv:2403.19054, which treated the case of quasilinear partial differential operators of order 2. The proof is by microlocalization to first order model operators.

35A01 (primary) 35S05, 58J40, 47G30 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

The solvability of differential operators has been an important issue since Hans Lewy
[
10
]
presented a complex vector field that was not solvable anywhere. Hörmander
[
4
]
then showed that almost all linear differential operators are
not
solvable. After some development, Nirenberg and Treves
[
11
]
formulated their famous conjecture 1969 that the condition for solvability of a linear (pseudo)differential operator of principal type is condition (
\Psi
). This is an invariant condition on the sign changes of the imaginary part of the principal symbol on the bicharacteristics of the real part. Principal type means that the principal symbol vanishes of first order on its zeros, so the bicharacteristics are not degenerate. The necessity of condition (
\Psi
) was proved by Hörmander
[
7
]
in 1980, the sufficiency was proved by the author
[
1
]
in 2006, and improved by Lerner
[
9
]
.

One important class of differential operators are those of
real principal type
which are the operators of principal type with real principal symbols. For the linear PDO of real principal type, Hörmander
[
3
]
proved solvability already in 1955 which he extended to pseudodifferential operators
[
5
]
in 1972. We shall extend this solvability result to quasilinear pseudodifferential operators, showing local solvability with solutions having prescribed derivatives at a given point. The solution would in general not be unique but the result can be used, e.g., for finding local solutions vanishing of some order at a point. For a special case, see the Appendix of
[
2
]
.

To state the problem, let
f(x)\in C^{\infty}
and consider the equation

(1.1)

P(u,\partial^{\alpha}u,x,D)u=f,\quad\partial^{\alpha}u(x_{0})=u_{\alpha}\quad|\alpha|<m

where
P
is a nonlinear pseudodifferential operator in the
x
variables of order
m\in\mathbf{N}
depending
C^{\infty}
on
u
and
\partial^{\alpha}u
for
|\alpha|<m
.
Then we have

(1.2)

P(v,x,D)=p_{m}(v,x,D)+p_{m-1}(v,x,D)

modulo
\Psi^{m-2}
where

with
v=(u,\dots,\partial^{\alpha}u,\dots)\in C^{\infty}
for
{|\alpha|<m}
.

We shall simplify the notation and write

instead of
p(u,\partial^{\alpha}u,x,\xi)
and
\overline{u}
instead of
(u_{0},\dots,u_{\alpha},\dots)
,
|\alpha|<m
.
We shall assume that the principal symbol

is homogeneous of real principal type so that, if
\xi_{0}\neq 0
then, after a linear symplectic change of variables, we have
\partial_{\xi}p_{m}\neq 0
when

at

.
Here the symbols

We will use the classical Kohn-Nirenberg quantization having classical symbol expansions and assume that

is properly supported so that

maps
C^{\infty}
to
C^{\infty}
and
C^{\infty}_{0}
to
C^{\infty}_{0}

Since the principal symbol is real valued, we shall also treat the case of real solvability.

Definition 1.1
.

If the symbols satisfy
p_{j}(v,x,\xi)=\overline{p}_{j}(v,x,-\xi)
,
\forall\,j
, then

maps real valued functions to real valued functions, since

, so
\operatorname{Re}p(x,D)u=p(x,D)\operatorname{Re}u

Operators with this property will be called real operators.

The following is the main result of the paper.

.

Let

, and the principal symbol

)

Qu=iQF_{0}(t)v=(iF_{0}(t)D_{t}+S_{0})v=F_{0}(t)F_{1}(t)f+S_{0}v\\
=f+S_{1}f+S_{0}v=f+S_{1}f+S_{0}\int_{0}^{t}F_{1}(s)f\,ds=f+Sf

where
S_{0}
,
S_{1}
and
S\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R},I^{-1})
.
∎

Now by integrating in
\xi
we find that
S\in I^{-\infty}
has smooth kernel
S(x,y)
. Then the term
Sf(x)=\int S(x,y)f(y)\,dy
can be made small if
f
has support in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
x_{0}
. In fact, let
\phi_{\delta}(x)=\phi((x-x_{0})/\delta)
where
0<\delta\leq 1
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,
\phi
has support where
|x|<2
and is equal to 1 when
|x|\leq 1
, so that
\phi(x/\delta)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{x_{0},2\delta})
if
B_{x_{0},\delta}=\left\{\,x:|x-x_{0}|\leq\delta\,\right\}
and
\phi(x/\delta)=1
in
B_{x_{0},\delta}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S(x,y)\in C^{\infty}
and

S_{\delta}(x,y)=\phi_{\delta}(x)S(x,y)\phi_{\delta}(y)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{x_{0},2\delta}\times B_{x_{0},2\delta})
.
The mapping
S_{\delta}:C^{\infty}\mapsto C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{x_{0},2\delta})
is given by
S_{\delta}f(x)=\int S_{\delta}(x,y)f(y)\,dy
, and for
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{x_{0},\delta})
we have
S_{\delta}f(x)=Sf(x)
when
|x|\leq\delta
.
For
\delta
small enough,
\operatorname{Id}+S_{\delta}
has the inverse
(\operatorname{Id}+S_{\delta})^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-S_{\delta})^{j}\cong\operatorname{Id}

modulo operators with kernels in
C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{x_{0},2\delta}\times B_{x_{0},2\delta})
.

Proof.

We may assume that
x_{0}=0
, clearly
S_{\delta}f(x)=Sf(x)
if
\phi_{\delta}f=f
and
\phi_{\delta}(x)=1
.
If
f\in C^{\infty}
then
L^{\infty}
norm is
\|S_{\delta}f\|_{\infty}\leq c_{n}2^{n}\delta^{n}\|S\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}
.
By induction we get

(3.4)

\|S_{\delta}^{j}f\|_{\infty}\leq c_{n}2^{n}\delta^{n}\|S_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\|S^{j-1}_{\delta}f\|_{\infty}\\
\leq c_{n}^{j}2^{jn}\delta^{jn}\|S\|^{j}_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}\qquad j>1

where the kernels of
S_{\delta}^{j}
are in
C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{0,2\delta}\times B_{0,2\delta})
. Thus the series
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-S_{\delta})^{j}
converges in
L^{\infty}
if
c_{n}2^{n}\delta^{n}\|S\|_{\infty}<1
.
Derivation of the terms in the series will only give factors
O(\delta^{-1})
so the convergence is in
C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{0,2\delta}\times B_{0,2\delta})
.
Then the inverse
(\operatorname{Id}+S_{\delta})^{-1}=\operatorname{Id}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-S_{\delta})^{j}\cong\operatorname{Id}
modulo operators with kernels in
C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{x_{0},2\delta}\times B_{x_{0},2\delta})
.
∎

4.
A Calculus Lemma

The approximate solution
u
in (
3.1
) depends
C^{\infty}
on the data
f
and
v
, but we shall need stronger estimates. For that we shall use the
L^{2}
Sobolev norms:

(4.1)

\|\varphi\|^{2}_{(k)}=\|\langle D\rangle^{k}\varphi\|^{2}\qquad\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbf{R}

Then the continuity of
a(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m}
depend uniformly on
v\in C^{\infty}
in theses spaces.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
a(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m_{1}}
and
b(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m_{2}}
depend
C^{\infty}
on
v(x)
then we find that
a(v,x,D)b(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m_{1}+m_{2}}
also depends
C^{\infty}
on
v(x)
.
There exists
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
so that for any
a(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{0}
depending
C^{\infty}
on
v(x)\in C^{\infty}
, and for any
k\in\mathbf{R}
there exists
C_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+})
so that

(4.2)

\|a(v,x,D)\varphi\|_{(k)}\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|\varphi\|_{(k)}\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}

There exists
\ell\in\mathbf{R}
so that for any
a(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m_{1}}
and
b(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m_{2}}
depending
C^{\infty}
on
v(x)
, and for any
k\in\mathbf{R}
there exists
C_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+})
so that

(4.3)

\|\,[a(v,x,D),b(v,x,D)]\varphi\|_{(k)}\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|\varphi\|_{(k+m_{1}+m_{2}-1)}\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}

There exists
\ell\in\mathbf{R}
so that for any
a(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m}
depending
C^{\infty}
on
v(x)
having real valued symbol modulo
S^{m-1}
, and for any
k\in\mathbf{R}
there exists
C_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+})
so that

(4.4)

\|\operatorname{Im}a(v,x,D)\varphi\|_{(k)}\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|\varphi\|_{(k+m-1)}\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}

where
2i\operatorname{Im}a(v,x,D)=a(v,x,D)-a^{*}(v,x,D)
depends
C^{\infty}
on
v
. Here the functions
C_{k}(t)
only depend on the seminorms of the symbols.

Proof.

First we note that by definition any seminorm of order
k\in\mathbf{N}
of
a(v,x,\xi)\in S^{m}
is bounded by
\|v\|_{C^{k}}
for some
k\in\mathbf{R}
. By the Sobolev embedding theorem, the
C^{k}
norm of
v
can be bounded by the norm
\|v\|_{(k+s)}
with
s>n/2
.

If
a(v,x,\xi)\in S^{m_{1}}
and
b(v,x,\xi)\in S^{m_{2}}
then
a(v,x,D)b(v,x,D)=c(v,x,D)
is given by

(4.5)

c(v,x,\xi)=e^{i\langle D_{\xi},D_{y}\rangle}a(v,x,\xi)b(v,y,\eta)\big|_{\begin{subarray}{c}y=x\\
\eta=\xi\end{subarray}}

The mapping
a,b\mapsto c
is weakly continuous on the symbol classes
S^{m}
so that any seminorm of
c
only depends on some seminorms of
a
and
b
, see
[
8
, Th. 18.4.10
′
]
.
(Weak continuity means that the restriction to a bounded set is continuous in the
C^{\infty}
topology, see
[
8
, Def. 18.4.9]
.)
Thus if
a(v,x,D)
and
b(v,x,D)
depend
C^{\infty}
on
v\in C^{\infty}
then
c(v,x,D)
also does.
Observe that the number of seminorms that is needed does not depend on the symbol classes
S^{m_{j}}
.

We may reduce the estimate (
4.2
) to the case
k=0
by replacing
a(v,x,D)
with
A(x,D)=\langle D\rangle^{k}a(v(x),x,D)\langle D\rangle^{-k}\in\Psi^{0}
and
\varphi
with
\langle D\rangle^{k}\varphi
. Here the symbol expansion of

A(x,\xi)\cong a(v(x),x,\xi)+kD_{x}a(v(x),x,\xi)\xi\langle\xi\rangle^{-2}+\dots+A_{j}(x,\xi)+\dots

where the term
A_{j}(x,\xi)\in S^{-j}
has the factor
D^{j}_{x}a(v(x),x,\xi)
,
\forall\,j
. The error term for the expansion
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}A_{j}(x,D)
is
r_{n}(x,D)\in\Psi^{-n-1}
. This operator has a
C^{0}
kernel bounded by
\|v\|_{C^{n+1}}
which gives a bound of
r_{n}(x,D)
on
L^{2}
. The
L^{2}
norm of
A_{j}(x,D)
is bounded by a fixed seminorm of
A_{j}(x,\xi)
,
\forall\ j
, see
[
8
, Th. 18.6.3]
. This seminorm in turn depends on some seminorm of
a(v,x,\xi)
which gives (
4.2
) for some
C_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+})
.

Any seminorm of the symbol of the commutator
[a(v,x,D),b(v,x,D)]\in S^{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}
depends on the same seminorm of the symbols of the compositions
a(v,x,D)b(v,x,D)
and
b(v,x,D)a(v,x,D)
. These seminorms in turn depend on some seminorms of
a(v,x,\xi)
and
b(v,x,\xi)
. Thus we obtain (
4.3
) from (
4.2
) for some
C_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+})
.

If
a\in S^{m}
then the adjoint
a^{*}(v,x,D)
is given by

(4.6)

a^{*}(v,x,\xi)=e^{i\langle D_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}\overline{a}(v,x,\xi)

which is weakly continuous in the symbol class
S^{m}
by
[
8
, Th. 18.1.7]
. If
a
is real modulo
S^{m-1}
then
\operatorname{Im}a(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m-1}
. Thus any seminorm of the symbol of
\operatorname{Im}a(v,x,D)
is bounded by some seminorms of
a(v,x,\xi)
, giving (
4.4
) for some
C_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+})
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The results of Lemma
4.1
also holds for
a(v,x,D)\in\Psi^{m}
depending
C^{\infty}
on
v
composed by

depending
C^{\infty}
on

. Operators in

functions of

.

In fact, Theorem 9.1 in
[

]
shows that the conjugation of

) after change of variables, see for example (9.2)
′′

.

5.
The Microlocal Estimate

Next, we are going to prove estimates for the microlocalized operators.
Then we will use ON coordinates
(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
and for
k\in\mathbf{R}
and
T>0
define the local norms

(5.1)

\|\varphi\|^{2}_{k,T}=\int_{|t|\leq T}\|\varphi\|_{k}^{2}(t)\,dt\qquad\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}

and
\|\varphi\|_{k,j,T}=\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{j}\varphi\|_{k,T}
,
\forall\,j\in\mathbf{R}
,
with
\|\varphi\|_{k}^{2}(t)=\int|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{k}\varphi(t,x)|^{2}\ dx
.
The following result gives uniform estimates in the Sobolev norms, for simplicity we only take
k\in\mathbf{N}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
v,\,f\in C^{\infty}_{0}
and
u\in C^{\infty}
be a solution to

(5.2)

Q(v,t,x,D)u=\partial_{t}u+\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}(v,t,x,D_{x})\partial_{x_{j}}u\\
+A_{0}(v,t,x,D_{x})u=f\qquad u(0,x,w)=0

where
A_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R},\Psi^{0})
depends
C^{\infty}
on
v
,
\forall\,j
, and
A_{j}
is real valued modulo
S^{-1}
for
j>0
. Then there exists
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
so that for any
k\in\mathbf{N}
there exists
C_{k}(r)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+})
so that

(5.3)

\|\phi u\|_{(k)}^{2}\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|f\|_{(k)}^{2}

if
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}
has support where
|t|\leq 1
.
The estimate only depends on the seminorms of the symbol of
Q
and
\phi
.

Thus, for any
k\in\mathbf{N}
we get uniform local bounds on
\|\phi u\|_{(k)}
when
\|v\|_{(\ell)}
is uniformly bounded. Now
Q
is a differential operator in
t
but a
\Psi
DO in
x
, so in the proof we shall use Lemma
4.1
in the
x
variables.

Proof.

Let
A\partial_{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}
and
\langle u,u\rangle_{k}(t)=\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)
be the sesquilinear form,
then

(5.4)

\partial_{t}\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)=2\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial_{t}u,u\rangle_{k}(t)=2\operatorname{Re}\langle f,u\rangle_{k}(t)\\
-2\operatorname{Re}\langle A\partial_{t}u,u\rangle_{k}(t)-2\operatorname{Re}\langle A_{0}u,u\rangle_{k}(t)

Conjugating with
e^{-Ct}
gives

(5.5)

\partial_{t}(e^{-Ct}\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t))=e^{-Ct}\big(2\operatorname{Re}\langle f,u\rangle_{k}(t)\\
-2\operatorname{Re}\langle A\partial_{x}u,u\rangle_{k}(t)-2\operatorname{Re}\langle A_{0}u,u\rangle_{k}(t)-C\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)\big)

where

(5.6)

2\operatorname{Re}\langle A\partial_{x}u,u\rangle_{k}(t)=2\operatorname{Re}\langle[\langle D_{x}\rangle^{k},A]\partial_{x}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{-k}w,w\rangle_{0}(t)\\
+\langle[\operatorname{Re}A,\partial_{x},]w,w\rangle_{0}(t)+2\operatorname{Re}\langle i\operatorname{Im}A\partial_{x}w,w\rangle_{0}(t)=\langle R_{k}w,w\rangle_{0}(t)

where
[\operatorname{Re}A,\partial_{x}]
and
\operatorname{Im}A\partial_{x}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R},\Psi^{0})
and
w=\langle D_{x}\rangle^{k}u
. The calculus gives that the operator
[\langle D_{x}\rangle^{k},A]\partial_{x}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{-k}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R},\Psi^{0})
, so that
R_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R},\Psi^{0})
depends
C^{\infty}
on
v
.

Since
\|w\|_{0}=\|u\|_{k}
we find by
using Lemma
4.1
that

(5.7)

|\langle R_{k}w,w\rangle_{0}(t)|\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{\ell}(t))\|w\|_{0}^{2}(t)=C_{k}(\|v\|_{\ell}(t))\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)

for some
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
and
C_{k}(r)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R})
, and clearly

(5.8)

|\langle f,u\rangle_{k}(t)|\leq\|f\|_{k}^{2}(t)+\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)

We also obtain from Lemma
4.1
that

(5.9)

|\langle A_{0}u,u\rangle_{k}(t)|\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{\ell}(t))\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)

where in the following we will take the maximum of different
\ell
and
C_{k}(r)
.
Summing up, there exists
C_{k}(r)
so that

(5.10)

\partial_{t}(e^{-Ct}\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t))\leq e^{-Ct}\left((C_{k}(\|v\|_{\ell}(t))-C)\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)+C_{k}(\|v\|_{\ell}(t))\|f\|_{k}^{2}(t)\right)

Now we may replace
C_{k}(r)
by a nondecreasing function
and put

C_{k}=\max_{|t|\leq 1}C_{k}(\|v\|_{\ell}(t))\leq C_{k}\left(\max_{|t|\leq 1}\|v\|_{\ell}(t)\right)

where
\|v\|_{\ell}(t)\leq C_{0}\|v\|_{(\ell+1)}
,
\forall\,t
, by Sobolev’s inequality.
Since
\|u\|_{k}(0)=0
we find by integrating that

(5.11)

e^{-C_{k}t}\|u\|_{k}^{2}(t)\leq e^{C_{k}}C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell+1)})\|f\|_{k,1}^{2}\qquad t\in[-1,1]

Integrating over
[-1,1]
we obtain that

(5.12)

\|u\|_{k,1}^{2}\leq 2e^{2C_{k}}C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell+1)})\|f\|_{k,1}^{2}

where
\|f\|_{k,1}\leq\|f\|_{(k)}
.
Renaming
2e^{2C_{k}}C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell+1)})
as
C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell+1)})
and changing
\ell

we obtain

(5.13)

\|u\|_{k,1}^{2}\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|f\|^{2}_{(k)}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbf{N}

Next, we shall estimate

(5.14)

\|u\|_{k,j,1}^{2}\leq C_{k,j}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|f\|^{2}_{(k+j)}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbf{N}

when
j\in\mathbf{N}
. For that it suffices to estimate
\|D_{t}^{j}u\|_{k,1}

which we shall do by induction. The case
j=0
is given by (
5.13
), and we assume that (
5.14
) holds for
i\leq j
for some
j\in\mathbf{N}
. Since
Qu=\partial_{t}u+Au=f
with
A\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R},\Psi^{1})
, we have

(5.15)

\|D_{t}^{j+1}u\|_{k,1}\leq\|D_{t}^{j}f\|_{k,1}+\|D_{t}^{j}Au\|_{k,1}

where
D_{t}^{j}A=AD_{t}^{j}+\sum_{0\leq i<j}B_{i}D_{t}^{i}

with
B_{i}(t,x,D_{x})\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R},\Psi^{1})
being a
\Psi
DO in
x
depending
C^{\infty}
on
t
and
v
. By using Lemma
4.1
and integrating over
[-1,1]
we find that

(5.16)

\|D_{t}^{j}Au\|_{k,1}\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq j}C_{k,i}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|u\|_{k,i,1}

so the induction hypothesis gives (
5.14
) for any
j\in\mathbf{N}
.

Finally, we shall show that

(5.17)

\|\phi u\|^{2}_{(k)}\leq C_{k}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|f\|^{2}_{(k)}\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbf{N}

if
\phi\in C^{\infty}
is supported where
|t|\leq 1
.
To estimate
\|\phi u\|_{(k)}^{2}
it suffices to estimate
\|D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{t}^{j}\phi u\|
for
|\alpha|+j\leq k
.
We have that

[D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{t}^{j},\phi]=\sum_{|\beta|+i\leq k}B_{\beta,i}D_{x}^{\beta}D_{t}^{i}

where
B_{\beta,i}\in C^{\infty}
has support where
|t|\leq 1
.
Thus, (
5.14
) gives that

(5.18)

\|D_{x}^{\beta}D_{t}^{j}\phi u\|\leq C\sum_{0\leq i\leq j}\|u\|_{k-i,i,1}\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq j}C_{k-i,i}(\|v\|_{(\ell)})\|f\|^{2}_{(k)}

which completes the proof.
∎

6.

Next, we shall solve the IVP for the linearized equation

P(v(x),x,D)u(x)=f(x)

where
f
,
v\in C^{\infty}

and
P
is on the form (

) with principal symbol

homogeneous of degree

In the following, we shall suppress the parameter

To solve equation (

at

svch that

, which will be handled as a perturbation, we obtain from Proposition

that if

then

(6.2)

Q_{j}u_{j}=(\operatorname{Id}+s_{j})a_{j}^{-1}\varphi_{j}f=(a_{j}^{-1}+r_{j})\varphi_{j}f\qquad u_{j}\big|_{t=0}=0

where
s_{j}
and

.

But
u_{j}
may not be localized near
\Gamma_{j}
.
To handle the localization and the error term

we
shall microlocalize

depending on parameters. We shall use the cut-off

.
We shall also cut off with

\Phi

.
We find that

(6.3)

u_{j}=\psi_{j,\varrho}\Phi u_{j}+(1-\psi_{j,\varrho})\Phi u_{j}+(1-\Phi)u_{j}=u_{j,\varrho}+S_{j,\varrho}f

when
|x|<1/2
, where

and

since
\mathbb{F}_{j}a_{j}^{-1}\varphi_{j}\in I^{0}
. Thus we find that
S_{j,\varrho}f(x)
depends on the values of

By the Arzela-Ascoli theorem there exists a subsequence

to a limit

on

By taking the limit of the equation (

(7.12)

when

.
We also obtain that

\varrho^{m-1-|\alpha|}c_{\alpha,x,\varrho}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
,
|\alpha|<m
, are

.

Thus, if

) when

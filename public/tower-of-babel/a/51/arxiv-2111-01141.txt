Kramers-Wannier-like duality defects in ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) d gauge theories
Justin Kaidi, Kantaro Ohmori, Yunqin Zheng

Abstract
We introduce a class of non-invertible topological defects in ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) d gauge theories whose fusion rules are the higher-dimensional analogs of those of the Kramers-Wannier defect in the ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) d critical Ising model. As in the lower-dimensional case, the presence of such non-invertible defects implies self-duality under a particular gauging of their discrete (higher-form) symmetries. Examples of theories with such a defect include S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) Yang-Mills (YM) at θ = π \theta=\pi , 𝒩 = 1 {\mathcal{N}}=1 S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) super YM, and 𝒩 = 4 {\mathcal{N}}=4 S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) super YM at τ = i \tau=i . We also introduce an analogous construction in (2+1)d, and give a number of examples in Chern-Simons-matter theories.



Symmetries have been a driving force behind modern advances in theoretical physics. Recent developments have led to several extensions of the notion of global symmetry. One such example is higher-form symmetry
Gaiotto et al. 2015
, which has had numerous applications such as constraining the IR phases of pure Yang-Mills (YM) theory
Gaiotto et al. 2017
.

Another type of generalized symmetry is non-invertible symmetry.
The prototypical example of such a symmetry is the one arising from the Kramers-Wannier self-duality of the (1+1)d Ising model at the critical point. This duality can be implemented by a topological defect line
{\mathcal{N}}

Verlinde 1988
;
Aasen et al. 2016
;
Freed and Teleman 2018
. If one performs the duality twice, one projects out the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-odd operators, meaning that the composition rule of the topological defect satisfies

{\mathcal{N}}\times{\mathcal{N}}=1+\eta_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}~,

(1)

with
\eta_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
the symmetry defect implementing the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
twist of the spin system.
The only topological defects in the Ising CFT are
{\mathcal{N}}
and
\eta_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
, which means that there is no inverse
{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}
such that
{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}\times{\mathcal{N}}=1
, and therefore the defect
{\mathcal{N}}
cannot be thought of as implementing a group action.

The basic idea of non-invertible symmetry is to consider
any
topological defect as a form of generalized symmetry. This means that one must extend the notion of symmetry beyond groups, leading in (1+1)d to a mathematical construction known as a fusion category
Moore and Seiberg 1989
;
Fuchs et al. 2002
;
Etingof et al. 2005
.
The Ising category is one of the simplest such fusion categories.

Though non-invertible symmetries are relatively well-studied in (1+1)d (see e.g.
Carqueville and Runkel 2016
;
Bhardwaj and Tachikawa 2018
;
Chang et al. 2019
;
Lin and Shao 2021
;
Thorngren and Wang 2019
;
Komargodski et al. 2021
;
Huang and Lin 2021
;
Thorngren and Wang 2021
;
Huang et al. 2021
;
Inamura 2021
for recent developments in continuum QFTs), examples in dimensions greater than two remain limited, except in topological QFTs.
We mention just one example here
Nguyen et al. 2021
, in which non-invertible lines were used to study the string tension in (2+1)d
U(1)\rtimes S_{N}
semi-abelian gauge theory.

In this letter, we provide a general procedure for obtaining non-invertible defects in 3+1-dimensions, starting with any theory with ’t Hooft anomaly for discrete higher-form symmetries of a particular form. By gauging a subset of the symmetries appearing in the anomaly, the defect associated with the remaining symmetry becomes non-invertible (such a construction was first suggested in
Tachikawa 2020
). For the particular cases we study, the resulting non-invertible defect will be shown to be a generalization of the Kramers-Wannier defect in (1+1)d. Our defects will have similar implications for self-duality of the gauge theories. Our result can be seen as a continuum analog of the Kramers-Wannier duality of lattice
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge theories in (3+1)-dimensions
Freed and Teleman 2018
, whose corresponding defect has been studied in
Koide et al. 2021
.

We illustrate the existence of these defects and their potential dynamical applications through the example of
SO(3)
gauge theory with zero and one supercharges, as well as
{\mathcal{N}}=4

SU(2)
super YM. In the appendices we give details as well as various generalizations of our construction, including to
(2+1)
-dimensional theories (Appendix
D
) and
(3+1)
-dimensional theories with symmetries besides
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
(Appendix
C
).

I
General construction

I.1
Kramers-Wannier-like duality defect

Our starting point is a (3+1)d theory
{\mathcal{T}}
with zero-form symmetry
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
(which can be either linear or anti-linear) and one-form symmetry
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}
. Associated to these symmetries are codimension-1 and -2 topological defects. We will denote the background fields of
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
and
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
as
A^{(1)}
and
B^{(2)}
, respectively. The
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
symmetry defect inserted on
M_{3}
in the presence of
B^{(2)}
will be denoted by
D(M_{3},B^{(2)})
. Throughout we will assume that
M_{3}
is oriented.

For simplicity, assume that the spacetime manifold
X_{4}
is spin. The two symmetries can have a mixed ’t Hooft anomaly, captured by a 5d integral built from the background gauge fields
A^{(1)}
and
B^{(2)}
.
We will be interested in the particular case of a ’t Hooft anomaly of the form

\displaystyle\pi\int_{X_{5}}A^{(1)}\cup{{\mathcal{P}}(B^{(2)})\over 2}~,

(2)

with
{\mathcal{P}}(B^{(2)})
the Pontrjagin square of
B^{(2)}
and
\partial X_{5}=X_{4}
. The mixed anomaly (
2
) implies that the
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
defect
D(M_{3},B^{(2)})
is anomalous under
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
transformations, and hence only the combination

\displaystyle D(M_{3},B^{(2)})e^{i\pi\int_{M_{4}}{\mathcal{P}}(B^{(2)})/2}

(3)

with
\partial M_{4}=M_{3}
is invariant under gauge transformations of the background field
B^{(2)}
. Note that in (
3
) the dependence on
M_{4}
is only through a term involving the classical background
B^{(2)}
, so (
3
) should still be regarded as a genuine 3d invertible defect.

We will be interested in understanding the gauging of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}
.
Upon gauging, the background field
B^{(2)}
is promoted to a dynamical field
b^{(2)}
. From (
3
), we see that
D(M_{3},b^{(2)})
is no longer well-defined since it is not invariant under the dynamical gauge transformations of
b^{(2)}
. To make it well-defined, we must either couple to a dynamical bulk, or couple to a 3d TQFT
\mathfrak{T}(M_{3},b^{(2)})
which cancels the anomaly, thereby absorbing the bulk dependence. Since we will be interested in intrinsically 3d defects, we will pursue the latter strategy. The TQFT cancelling the anomaly is not unique. However, it was shown in
Hsin et al. 2019
that any such TQFT can be factorized into the decoupled tensor product of two theories
\mathfrak{T}(M_{3},b^{(2)})=\widehat{\mathfrak{T}}(M_{3})\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}(M_{3},b^{(2)})
, where
\widehat{\mathfrak{T}}(M_{3})
does not couple to the dynamical field
b^{(2)}
. The theory
{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}(M_{3},b^{(2)})
is simply
U(1)_{2}
Chern Simons theory, i.e. the minimal TQFT that lives on the boundary of
e^{i\pi\int_{M_{4}}{\mathcal{P}}(b^{(2)})/2}
. Since tensoring a decoupled TQFT only changes the overall normalization of the defect, we choose the TQFT to be the minimal
{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}
for simplicity. Hence we find a well-defined genuinely 3d defect

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3}):=D(M_{3},b^{(2)}){\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}(M_{3},b^{(2)})~.

(4)

Note that
{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})
explicitly depends on the dynamical field
b^{(2)}
.
The defect
{\mathcal{N}}
, when regarded as an operator, is linear (resp. anti-linear) if and only if
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
before gauging is linear (resp. anti-linear).

We now show that
{\mathcal{N}}
satisfies Kramers-Wannier-like fusion rules; in particular, it is non-invertible. To begin, consider the case in which
{\mathcal{N}}
is linear. In this case the dual
\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}
is just equal to
{\mathcal{N}}
itself. This follows since
D=\overline{D}
from
D^{2}=1
and
\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}=\mathcal{A}^{2,-1}\cong\mathcal{A}^{2,1}
, with the last equality following from the fact that
U(1)_{2}
is time-reversal symmetric as a spin-TQFT
Fidkowski et al. 2013
;
Seiberg and Witten 2016
.
We also note that the tensor product theory
\mathcal{A}^{2,1}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{2,-1}
, often called the “double-semion” theory
Freedman et al. 2004
;
Levin and Wen 2005
, is equivalent to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Dijkgraaf-Witten (DW) theory
Dijkgraaf and Witten 1990
with non-trivial DW twist. The DW twist can be written as
(-1)^{\int_{M_{3}}a^{3}}
where
a\in H^{1}(M_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field. Indeed, the
K
-matrix for the double-semion theory,
K=\mathrm{diag}(2,-2)
, can be rotated to that of the
BF
representation of the DW theory,
K=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
2&1\end{pmatrix}
, by an
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
transformation.

These considerations motivate the following result: upon fusion of two
{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})
defects, one obtains a non-trivial
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
DW theory living on
M_{3}
. Poincaré duality allows one to exchange the sum over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
1-cocycles
a
with a sum over 2-cycles
\Sigma
, upon which the DW twist
e^{i\pi\oint_{M_{3}}a^{3}}
becomes the triple intersection number
Q(\Sigma)
of
\Sigma
in
M_{3}
.
1
1

1

By Banchoff’s theorem
Banchoff 1974a
;
Banchoff 1974b
,
Q(\Sigma)
is equivalent mod 2 to the Euler character of
\Sigma
, i.e.
(-1)^{Q(\Sigma)}=(-1)^{\chi(\Sigma)}
. Hence it is independent of
M_{3}
and nontrivial only when
\Sigma
is unorientable.
This gives the following fusions rules,

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})\times{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{|H^{0}(M_{3},\mathbbm{Z}_{2})|}\sum_{\Sigma\in H_{2}(M_{3},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}(-1)^{Q(\Sigma)}L(\Sigma),\end{split}

(5)

where
L(\Sigma):=e^{i\pi\oint_{\Sigma}b^{(2)}}
. The normalization factor is related to the volume of the gauge group of the DW theory. A more explicit derivation of both the normalization and the fusion rules will be given in Appendices
A
and
B
. Since this is a sum of more than one operator, we see that
{\mathcal{N}}
is a non-invertible defect.

On the other hand, in the case when
{\mathcal{N}}
is anti-linear, we have
{\mathcal{N}}\times{\mathcal{N}}=D{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}\times D{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}=D^{2}{\mathcal{A}}^{2,-1}\times{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}
, since
D
flips the orientation of
{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}
as the former passes though the latter.
Combining this with
D^{2}=1
, we see that
{\mathcal{N}}\times{\mathcal{N}}
again hosts the double-semion theory, giving the same fusion rules as in (
5
). In summary,
{\mathcal{N}}
satisfies the fusion rule (
5
) regardless of whether it is linear or anti-linear.

We may now consider the fusion of
{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})
and
L(\Sigma)
for some
\Sigma
embedded in
M_{3}
. Note that in (
4
) the one-form symmetry of
{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}
is coupled to the bulk dynamical field
b^{(2)}
. This means that the Wilson line of
{\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}
, which has a non-trivial one-form charge, has to be bounded by the bulk global one-form symmetry generator
L
. In other words,
L
can end on
{\mathcal{N}}
without costing energy, and furthermore it can be absorbed.
In the process of absorption, the boundary of
L
, identified with the Wilson line, sweeps out a surface
\Sigma
in
M_{3}
, producing a sign
(-1)^{Q(\Sigma)}
from the framing anomaly of the Wilson line. This effect is derived in Appendix
A
. Hence we obtain the fusion rules

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})\times L(\Sigma)=(-1)^{Q(\Sigma)}{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})~,

(6)

where
\Sigma
is embedded in
M_{3}
.

Finally, the
L\times L
fusion rule is obvious,

\displaystyle L(\Sigma)\times L(\Sigma)=1~.

(7)

The fusion rules (
5
), (
6
) and (
7
) are reminiscent of the fusion rules of the Ising fusion category in
2
d. For this reason, we refer to the non-invertible defect
{\mathcal{N}}
in the 4d theory
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
as a “Kramers-Wannier-like defect.” Though we do not work it out here, we expect these fusion rules to yield a fusion 3-category
Douglas and Reutter 2018
;
Johnson-Freyd 2020
. As we now explain,
{\mathcal{N}}
implements a self-duality transformation on
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
.

I.2
Self-duality

We now explain why the gauged theory
{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}
has a notion of self-duality.
We begin by considering the partition function of
{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}
,
2
2

2

We take the integration measure to be defined with implicit division by the volume of the gauge group.

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}}[C^{(2)}]=\int{\mathcal{D}}b^{(2)}\,Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[b^{(2)}]e^{i\pi\int_{X_{4}}C^{(2)}b^{(2)}}~,

(8)

where
C^{(2)}
is the background field for the quantum
\widehat{\mathbbm{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}
symmetry, whose corresponding defect is
L
.
If we further gauge
C^{(2)}
, the last factor becomes a delta functional for
b^{(2)}
and we reobtain the original theory
\mathcal{T}
.

To find self-duality in
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
, we first include a Dijkgraaf-Witten term
\frac{\pi}{2}{\mathcal{P}}(C^{(2)})
and then gauge. This gives

\displaystyle\int{\mathcal{D}}c^{(2)}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}}[c^{(2)}]\,\,e^{{i}\int_{X_{4}}{\pi\over 2}{\mathcal{P}}(c^{(2)})+\pi c^{(2)}A^{(2)}}

\displaystyle\quad=\int{\mathcal{D}}c^{(2)}\,{\mathcal{D}}b^{(2)}\,Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[b^{(2)}]\,e^{i\int_{X_{4}}\pi c^{(2)}b^{(2)}+{\pi\over 2}{\mathcal{P}}(c^{(2)})+\pi c^{(2)}A^{(2)}}

\displaystyle\quad=\int{\mathcal{D}}b^{(2)}\,Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[b^{(2)}]\,\,e^{-i{\pi\over 2}\int_{X_{4}}{\mathcal{P}}(b^{(2)}+A^{(2)})}

(9)

where we have made a change of variables
c^{(2)}\rightarrow c^{(2)}-b^{(2)}-A^{(2)}
and dropped a contribution from the TQFT
\int{\mathcal{D}}c^{(2)}\,e^{i{\pi\over 2}\int{\mathcal{P}}(c^{(2)})}
, which can be continuously deformed to the trivial theory.
We next use the following anomalous transformation law of
{\mathcal{T}}
under
global

\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
transformations:

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[b^{(2)}]e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{X_{4}}{\mathcal{P}}(b^{(2)})}=\begin{cases}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[b^{(2)}],&\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}\text{is linear}\\
Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}[b^{(2)}],&\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}\text{is anti-linear}.\end{cases}

(10)

Eq.(
I.2
) then reduces to

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}}[A^{(2)}]\,e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{X_{4}}{\mathcal{P}}(A^{(2)})}

(11)

if
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
is linear, and

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(1)}}^{*}[A^{(2)}]\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{X_{4}}{\mathcal{P}}(A^{(2)})}

(12)

if
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
is anti-linear. To complete the self-duality, we need only add a compensating counterterm (and in the anti-linear case, do a complex conjugation
K
).

To begin, recall that the defect

We begin by determining the fusion rule

.
As mentioned in the main text, the theory on this product is the double-semion theory, which is dual to

gauge theory with a DW twist. To see this explicitly, we start from

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})\times{\mathcal{N}}(M_{3})={\mathcal{A}}^{2,1}(M_{3})\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{2,-1}(M_{3})\\

Integrating out

Next we determine the fusion rule

In general, gauging a one-form symmetry

We begin by decomposing

\displaystyle n\,\mathrm{d}_{M}a^{(k-1)}

\displaystyle n\,\int_{0}^{1}dt\,\mathrm{d}_{M}\beta^{(k-1)}(t)=n\,\int_{0}^{1}dt\,{{\partial}\omega^{(k)}\over{\partial}t}

\displaystyle n\,(\omega^{(k)}(1)-\omega^{(k)}(0))=0~.

We now restrict to

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{1}dt\,{{\partial}\over{\partial}t}\omega^{(2)}(t)\wedge\tilde{\beta}^{(1)}(t)

\displaystyle\Phi({\mathcal{P}}(b^{(2)}))=2\,a^{(1)}\cup a^{(1)}\cup a^{(1)}\,\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,4~.

(35)

D.2
Non-invertible surface defect
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
implementing self-duality

We now return to the codimension-1 defect
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(M_{2})
and show that it implements a self-duality similar to that in the (3+1)d theories discussed in the main text.

The partition functions of the theory
{\mathcal{T}}
and its gauging
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{T}}/(\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(1)}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{2,2}^{(0)})
are related via

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}}[\widehat{B}^{(1)},\widehat{A}_{2}^{(2)}]=\int&{\mathcal{D}}b^{(2)}{\mathcal{D}}a_{2}^{(1)}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[a_{2}^{(1)},b^{(2)}]\times\\
&\hskip 7.22743pte^{i\pi\int_{X_{3}}b^{(2)}\widehat{B}^{(1)}+a_{2}^{(1)}\widehat{A}_{2}^{(2)}}~.\end{split}

(55)

We now show that
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
is invariant under the transformation
TST
. The operation
T
is defined to be addition of a counterterm
e^{i\pi\int\widehat{B}^{(1)}\widehat{A}_{2}^{(2)}}
, while
S
is defined as gauging
\widehat{\mathbbm{Z}}_{2,2}^{(1)}\times\widehat{\mathbbm{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
. Under
ST
, the partition function of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
becomes

\begin{split}&\int{\mathcal{D}}\widehat{b}^{(1)}{\mathcal{D}}\widehat{a}_{2}^{(2)}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}}[\widehat{b}^{(1)},\widehat{a}_{2}^{(2)}]e^{i\pi\int_{X_{3}}\widehat{b}^{(1)}\widehat{a}_{2}^{(2)}+\widehat{b}^{(1)}\widetilde{B}^{(2)}+\widehat{a}_{2}^{(2)}\widetilde{A}_{2}^{(1)}}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\int{\mathcal{D}}b^{(2)}{\mathcal{D}}a_{2}^{(1)}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[a_{2}^{(1)},b^{(2)}]e^{i\pi\int_{X_{3}}(b^{(2)}+\widetilde{B}^{(2)})(a_{2}^{(1)}+\widetilde{A}_{2}^{(1)})}~.\end{split}

(56)

We further use the fact that
{\mathcal{T}}
is invariant under
\mathbbm{Z}_{2,1}^{(0)}
and has the mixed anomaly (
46
), which implies that

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[a_{2}^{(1)},b^{(2)}]=Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[a_{2}^{(1)},b^{(2)}]\,e^{i\pi\int_{X_{3}}a_{2}^{(1)}b^{(2)}}~,

(57)

i.e.
Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[a_{2}^{(1)},b^{(2)}]
is zero unless
e^{i\pi\int_{X_{3}}a_{2}^{(1)}b^{(2)}}
is trivial.
Then (
56
) reduces to

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}}[\widetilde{A}_{2}^{(1)},\widetilde{B}^{(2)}]\,e^{i\pi\int_{X_{3}}\widetilde{A}_{2}^{(1)}\widetilde{B}^{(2)}}~,

(58)

where the counterterm can be removed by a further
T
transformation. Hence
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
is indeed self-dual under
TST
, analogous to the (3+1)d case in the main text.

One can further argue that the self-duality
TST
is implemented via the non-invertible line
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(M_{2})
. The discussion is almost identical to that around Figure
1
in the main text. In the current case the
S
transformation implements a gauging of
\widehat{\mathbbm{Z}}_{2,2}^{(1)}\times\widehat{\mathbbm{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
, so between the two
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
defects is a fine mesh of
W
and
L_{i}
defects.

However, unlike in the (3+1)d case, we notice that now the first
T
operation does not contribute a nontrivial phase factor (i.e. an analog of
(-1)^{Q(\Sigma)}
) to the fusion rules. This can be confirmed by using the map
\Phi
defined in (
24
) of Appendix
B
. Indeed, let us denote the images of
\widehat{b}^{(1)}
and
\widehat{a}_{2}^{(2)}
under the map
\Phi
by

\displaystyle\beta^{(0)}:=\Phi(\widehat{b}^{(1)})~,\hskip 14.45377pt\alpha^{(1)}:=\Phi(\widehat{a}_{2}^{(2)})~.

(59)

Then one finds that

\displaystyle\Phi(\widehat{b}^{(1)}\cup\widehat{a}_{2}^{(2)})\,=\,2\,\beta^{(0)}\,\alpha^{(1)}\cup\alpha^{(1)}

(60)

which vanishes mod 2.

D.3
Examples

We now provide some examples of non-invertible surfaces and lines in (2+1)-dimensions.

D.3.1
Non-invertible lines in Chern-Simons-matter theories

We start by taking
{\mathcal{T}}
to be the Chern-Simons-matter theory

\displaystyle SO(N)_{K}~\text{with}~N_{f}~\text{adjoint scalars}~.

(61)

We will restrict to the case of
N,K,N_{f}=0\mod 4
. Such theories have 1-form center symmetry
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(1)}
, 0-form charge conjugation symmetry
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{C}}
and 0-form magnetic symmetry
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{M}}
. Since adding a mass term for all the flavors does not break any of the above symmetries, the ‘t Hooft anomalies can be found from the pure
SO(N)_{K}
Chern-Simons theory obtained by large positive (degenerate) mass deformation. The anomaly of the 1-form symmetry is
\frac{\pi NK}{4}\int{\mathcal{P}}(B^{(2)})/2
, which vanishes on arbitrary manifolds, but there is a nontrivial mixed anomaly

\displaystyle\pi\int_{X_{4}}B^{(2)}\cup A^{\mathsf{C}}\cup A^{\mathsf{M}}

(62)

which is of the same form as (
46
)
Córdova et al. 2018
. One could also use the large negative mass deformation to obtain
SO(N-N_{f})_{K}
, with the anomalies remaining the same.

We can now apply the general discussion in the previous subsections here. After gauging
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{C}}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{M}}
, the theory becomes

\displaystyle Pin^{+}(N)_{K}~\text{with}~N_{f}~\text{adjoint scalars}

(63)

where adjoint means that the scalar transforms in the adjoint representation of the
\mathfrak{so}(N)
Lie algebra. The general discussion of the previous subsections shows that (
63
) has a non-invertible topological line satisfying the fusion rules (
53
).

Note that the non-invertible topological lines appear in both the pure Chern-Simons theory
Pin^{+}(N)_{K}
, as well as the Chern-Simons-matter theory with or without mass. In the former case, the non-invertible topological lines are simply anyons which are ubiquitous for generic 3d TQFTs. However, the presence of non-invertible topological lines in the latter is nontrivial. Indeed, it is typically expected that upon coupling to matter and turning on finite Yang-Mills couplings, the topological lines of Chern Simons theories become non-topological, unless they are the generators of conventional 1-form symmetries (which are invertible). We now see that this is not entirely true: there exist non-invertible lines which remain topological even after deforming away from the TQFT limit!

The same conclusion holds if we replace the adjoint scalars by fermions in an appropriate representation, with Chern-Simons levels suitably adjusted. We provide two examples:

1.

Take
{\mathcal{T}}
to be the Chern-Simons-matter theory
SO(N)_{K+\frac{(N-2)N_{f}}{2}}
with
N_{f}
Majorana fermions in the adjoint representation, with
N,K,N_{f}=0\mod 4
. Gauging
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{C}}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{M}}
gives

Pin^{+}(N)_{K+\frac{(N-2)N_{f}}{2}}
with
N_{f}
adjoint Majorana fermions,
which also has a non-invertible topological line satisfying the fusion rules (
53
).

2.

Take
{\mathcal{T}}
to be Chern Simons matter theory
SO(N)_{K+\frac{(N+2)N_{f}}{2}}
with
N_{f}
Majorana fermions in the two-index symmetric representation, again with
N,K,N_{f}=0\mod 4
. Gauging
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{C}}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{\mathsf{M}}
gives

Pin^{+}(N)_{K+\frac{(N+2)N_{f}}{2}}
with
N_{f}

S
Majorana fermions
where
S
represents the two index symmetric representation of
\mathfrak{so}(N)
. This theory again has non-invertible topological lines.

It would be nice to see if the non-invertible lines can be matched across Chern-Simons-matter dualities. However, as far as we know, Chern-Simons-matter dualities for
N_{f}\in 4\mathbbm{Z}
flavors of adjoint or symmetric tensor matter have not been studied in the literature (but see
Gomis et al. 2018
;
Córdova et al. 2018
;
Choi et al. 2020
for dualities involving

Choi 2020
for dualities involving

Appendix E
(1+1)d Kramers-Wannier duality

In this appendix we show that the usual (1+1)d Kramers-Wannier line and self-duality can be understood in a way analogous to that in the main text. For related discussions, see
Karch et al. 2019
;
Lin and Shao 2021
.

E.1
Non-invertible defect

The starting point is the theory
{\mathcal{T}}
of a single Majorana fermion. This theory has a pair of 0-form symmetries, denoted
(-1)^{\mathsf{F}}
and
(-1)^{\mathsf{F_{L}}}
, which are respectively total and left-moving fermion parities.
These symmetries have a mixed ‘t Hooft anomaly, which implies that the partition function on
X_{2}
with the spin structure
{\mathcal{S}}
can absorb the Arf-invariant
Witten 2016
,

\displaystyle Z[X_{2},{\mathcal{S}}]\,\,=\,\,Z[X_{2},{\mathcal{S}}]\,(-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(X_{2},{\mathcal{S}})}~.

(66)

In other words, one finds
Z[X_{2},{\mathcal{S}}]=0
whenever
(-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(X_{2},{\mathcal{S}})}=-1
. When
X_{2}=T^{2}
, this is manifested by the zero mode that is present for
RR
spin structure. The mixed anomaly also implies that the topological defect line
D(M_{1},{\mathcal{S}})
corresponding to
(-1)^{\mathsf{F_{L}}}
can be made invariant under background transformations by coupling it to a bulk term

\displaystyle D(M_{1},{\mathcal{S}})\,e^{i\pi\mathrm{Arf}(M_{2},{\mathcal{S}})}

(67)

with
{\partial}M_{2}=M_{1}
.

To gauge
(-1)^{\mathsf{F}}
we replace
{\mathcal{S}}
by
{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda
, with
\lambda
a dynamical
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
gauge field which should be integrated over. Since everything now depends only on the combination
{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda
(rather than
{\mathcal{S}}
and
\lambda
independently), to simplify notation we will pick a spin structure
{\mathcal{S}}
such that
(-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}({\mathcal{S}})}=1
and suppress
{\mathcal{S}}
throughout. The
(-1)^{\mathsf{F_{L}}}
defect after gauging then becomes
D(M_{1},\lambda)
. From (
67
) this is no longer gauge-invariant, and hence it must be dressed with an appropriate 1d theory. Indeed, we may define the gauge invariant line

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(M_{1}):=D(M_{1},\lambda)\,{\mathcal{M}}(M_{1},\lambda)~

(68)

with
{\mathcal{M}}(M_{1},\lambda)
a Majorana zero mode
Kitaev 2001
;
Kapustin et al. 2015
. When we consider the product of two
{\mathcal{N}}(M_{1})
, there are a pair of Majorana zero modes on the resulting line, and this pair admits a consistent quantization with Hilbert space of dimension 2. Recalling that the spinor representation
\rho_{\psi}
fuses via
\rho_{\psi}\times\rho_{\psi}=\rho_{0}\oplus\rho_{\mathrm{vec}}
, we conclude that we must have the fusion rules quoted in (
1
).

E.2
Self-duality

We now show that the self-duality in this theory is of the same form
TST
as in the main text. Here
S
represents gauging of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
, and
T
represents addition of an Arf twist. To show this, first note that

\displaystyle Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}}[\Omega]={1\over\sqrt{b_{1}}}\sum_{\lambda\in H^{1}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[\lambda](-1)^{\int_{X_{2}}\lambda\cup\Omega}~,

(69)

where
b_{1}:=|H^{1}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})|
.
Then upon doing
T
and
S
transformations, we have

\displaystyle{1\over\sqrt{b_{1}}}\sum_{\omega\in H^{1}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}}[\omega](-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(\omega)+\int_{X_{2}}\omega\cup\Lambda}

(70)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,={1\over b_{1}}\sum_{\lambda,\omega\in H^{1}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[\lambda](-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(\omega)+\int_{X_{2}}\omega\cup\Lambda+\int_{X_{2}}\lambda\cup\omega}~.

Using (
66
), the above partition function is equivalent to

\displaystyle{1\over b_{1}}\sum_{\lambda,\omega\in H^{1}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[\lambda]\,(-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(\lambda+\omega)+\int_{X_{2}}\omega\cup\Lambda}~,

(71)

where we have used

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arf}(A+B)+\mathrm{Arf}(A)+\mathrm{Arf}(B)=\int A\cup B~.

(72)

Finally, changing variables from
\omega
to
\omega+\lambda
gives

\displaystyle{1\over\sqrt{b_{1}}}\sum_{\lambda\in H^{1}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[\lambda]\,(-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(\Lambda)+\int_{X_{2}}\lambda\cup\Lambda}

(73)

upon noting that
\sum_{\omega}(-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(\omega)}=\sqrt{b_{1}}
. This is indeed equal to the original theory
Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}}[\Lambda]
up to a factor of
(-1)^{\mathrm{Arf}(\Lambda)}
, which can be removed by a
T
transformation.
To summarize, we have shown that the theory
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
is self-dual under
TST
.

E.3
Making contact with Kramers-Wannier duality

Note that the theory
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
discussed above is not the usual bosonization map. Hence if
{\mathcal{T}}
is a free massless Majorana fermion, the theory
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
whose self-duality we just discussed is not the critical Ising model. In particular,
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
depends on the choice of spin structure:
Z_{{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}}[\Omega]
is not invariant under the change of spin structure
\Omega\to\Omega+\Lambda
.

Instead, the bosonic theory
{\mathcal{B}}
is given by
Ji et al. 2020
;
Karch et al. 2019

Z_{{\mathcal{B}}}[\Omega]={1\over\sqrt{b_{1}}}\sum_{\lambda\in H^{1}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{{\mathcal{T}}}[\lambda](-1)^{\int_{X_{2}}\lambda\cup\Omega+\mathrm{Arf}(\Omega)}

(74)

which is related to
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
via

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}=T\left({\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}\right)~.

(75)

Using the self duality of
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
obtained above, i.e.
{\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)}=TST({\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)})
, we find that

\displaystyle S({\mathcal{B}})=ST({\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)})=T({\mathcal{T}}/\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{(0)})={\mathcal{B}}~.

(76)

This is exactly the standard Kramers-Wannier duality when
{\mathcal{B}}
is the critical Ising model.

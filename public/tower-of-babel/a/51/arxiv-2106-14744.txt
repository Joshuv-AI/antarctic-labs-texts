Continuous data assimilation and long-time accuracy in a C 0 C^{0} interior penalty method for the Cahn-Hilliard equation
Amanda E. Diegel, Leo G. Rebholz

Abstract
We propose a numerical approximation method for the Cahn-Hilliard equations that incorporates continuous data assimilation in order to achieve long time accuracy. The method uses a C 0 interior penalty spatial discretization of the fourth order Cahn-Hilliard equations, together with a backward Euler temporal discretization. We prove the method is long time stable and long time accurate, for arbitrarily inaccurate initial conditions, provided enough data measurements are incorporated into the simulation. Numerical experiments illustrate the effectiveness of the method on a benchmark test problem.

In certain problem settings, partial observable or measurement values of the solution may be available. This has been studied for many different evolutionary physical partial differential equation (PDE) systems in recent years

We propose and analyze herein a particular discretization for the Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation together with CDA, in an effort to obtain long time accuracy and stability of computed solutions to CH equations. To our knowledge, there is no literature for CDA applied to the CH equation. Perhaps the main reason for this is that the typical CDA application and analysis does not seem possible (at least not to the authors of this paper) for the second order mixed CH equation, which is a more commonly used formulation than the fourth order formulation above. At the PDE level, the CDA system we consider takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi-\Delta\left(\phi^{3}-\phi\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi+\omega I_{H}(\phi-\varphi)

\displaystyle=0,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\phi(0)

\displaystyle=\phi_{0},

(1.2b)

The discretization method we choose is a C
0

1.1

Fully Discrete C
0
Interior Penalty FEM with Data Assimilation

Let
M
be a positive integer and
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{M}=T
be a uniform partition of
[0,T]
. A fully discrete C
0
interior penalty method for (

\phi_{h}^{m-1}\in Z_{h}
and true solution

such that

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\phi_{h}^{m},\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\left(\phi_{h}^{m}\right)^{3}-\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right),\nabla\psi\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\phi_{h}^{m},\psi\right)+\omega\left(I_{H}\left(\phi_{h}^{m}-\varphi^{m}\right),\psi\right)=0,

(3.1)

for all
\psi\in Z_{h}
with initial data taken to be
\phi_{h}^{0}:=P_{h}\phi_{0}
where
P_{h}:H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow Z_{h}
is a Ritz projection operator such that

\displaystyle a_{h}^{IP}\left(P_{h}\phi-\phi,\xi\right)=0\quad\forall\,\xi\in Z_{h},\quad\left(P_{h}\phi-\phi,1\right)=0,

(3.2)

and where
\displaystyle\delta_{\Delta t}\phi_{h}^{m}:=\frac{\phi_{h}^{m}-\phi_{h}^{m-1}}{\Delta t}

We will begin by showing that solutions to (

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\phi_{h}^{m-1}\in Z_{h}
and
\varphi^{m}\in H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)
be given. Then, if
\omega<\dfrac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{2C_{P}^{2}C_{I}^{2}H^{2}}

Proof.

Let

and

be given and

\displaystyle\left(G_{h}(\phi_{h}^{m}),\chi\right):=

\displaystyle\,\left(\phi_{h}^{m}-\phi_{h}^{m-1},\chi\right)+\Delta t\left(\nabla\left(\left(\phi_{h}^{m}\right)^{3}-\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right),\nabla\chi\right)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t\,a_{h}^{IP}\left(\phi_{h}^{m},\chi\right)+\omega\Delta t\left(I_{H}\left(\phi_{h}^{m}-\varphi^{m}\right),\chi\right).

(3.3)

It is a well-known consequence of Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem

that

has a solution

Using Young’s inequality, (
2.3

and

\displaystyle\left(G_{h}(\chi),\chi\right)\geq

\displaystyle-\left(\phi_{h}^{m-1},\chi\right)-\Delta t\left(\nabla\phi_{h}^{m-1},\nabla\chi\right)-\omega\Delta t\left(\chi-I_{H}\chi,\chi\right)-\omega\Delta t\left(I_{H}\varphi^{m},\chi\right)

\displaystyle\,(1+\omega\Delta t)\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+3\Delta t\left\|\chi\nabla\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}^{2}-\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,(1+\omega\Delta t)\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+3\Delta t\left\|\chi\nabla\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}^{2}-\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\,(1+\omega\Delta t)\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+3\Delta t\left\|\chi\nabla\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}^{2}-\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2}\Delta t\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}-\omega C_{P}C_{I}H\Delta t\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}-\omega C_{I}\Delta t\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,(1+\omega\Delta t)\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+3\Delta t\left\|\chi\nabla\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t}{4}\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}^{2}-\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t}{4}\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\omega^{2}C_{P}^{2}C_{I}^{2}H^{2}\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\omega C_{I}^{2}\Delta t}{2}\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\omega\Delta t}{2}\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining like terms and multiplying by 2 now provides the bound

\displaystyle 2\left(G_{h}(\chi),\chi\right)\geq

\displaystyle\,\left[1+\omega\Delta t\left(1-\frac{2\omega C_{P}^{2}C_{I}^{2}H^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right]\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+6\Delta t\left\|\chi\nabla\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t\left\|\chi\right\|_{2,h}^{2}

\displaystyle-\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{4\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\omega C_{I}^{2}\Delta t\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,0,

if
0<\omega<\dfrac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{2C_{P}^{2}C_{I}^{2}H^{2}}
and
\left\|\chi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=q^{2}=\left(1+\dfrac{4\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\omega C_{I}^{2}\Delta t\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H_{N}^{2}(\Omega))
represent the true solution and black
H
and
\omega
be chosen so that

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}:=\frac{\omega C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}-2\omega^{2}C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}-4}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}>0,

(3.4)

i.e.
H
sufficiently small and
\omega
sufficiently large.
Then, for any
m,h,\Delta t>0
, solutions to the CDA-FEM (
3.1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|\phi_{h}^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda\Delta t}\right)^{m}+\frac{\omega\,\varepsilon^{2}C_{I}^{2}}{\omega\varepsilon^{2}-2\omega^{2}C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}-4}\,\Phi\leq C_{data},

where
\Phi:=\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}
.

Proof.

Setting
\psi=\phi_{h}^{m}
in (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\phi_{h}^{m},\phi_{h}^{m}\right)+3\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\nabla\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left(\nabla\phi_{h}^{m-1},\nabla\phi_{h}^{m}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\phi_{h}^{m},\phi_{h}^{m}\right)+\omega\left(I_{H}(\phi_{h}^{m}-\varphi^{m}),\phi_{h}^{m}\right)=0.

Now, adding and subtracting appropriate terms and using Lemma
2.3
along with properties (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and (
2.12
), Young’s inequality, and the polarization identity,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}-\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\omega\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+3\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\nabla\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\omega\left(\phi_{h}^{m}-I_{H}\phi_{h}^{m},\phi_{h}^{m}\right)+\omega\left(I_{H}\varphi^{m},\phi_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\nabla\phi_{h}^{m-1},\nabla\phi_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\omega\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}-I_{H}\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\omega\left\|I_{H}\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\omega\,C_{I}H\left\|\nabla\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\omega\,C_{I}\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\omega\,C_{I}C_{P}H\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\omega\,C_{I}\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\omega^{2}\,C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{\omega C_{I}^{2}}{2}\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining like terms, multiplying by
2\Delta t
, and dropping positive terms on the left hand side of the equation above, we get that

\displaystyle\left[1+\omega\,\Delta t-\frac{2\omega^{2}\,C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\,\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\right]\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\omega\,C_{I}^{2}\Delta t\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Multiplying by
\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left[\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}(1+\omega\Delta t)-2\omega^{2}C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}\right]\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\omega\,C_{I}^{2}C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}\left\|\varphi^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which leads to

\displaystyle\left[1+\Delta t\left(\frac{\omega C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}-2\omega^{2}C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}-4}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}\right)\right]\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\omega\,C_{I}^{2}C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}\Phi.

Requiring
\displaystyle\lambda_{0}:=\frac{\omega C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}-2\omega^{2}C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}-4}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+4\Delta t}>0
and applying Lemma
2.4
yields the desired result: for any
m,h,\Delta t>0

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|\phi_{h}^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda_{0}\Delta t}\right)^{m}+\frac{\omega C_{I}^{2}C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{\omega C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}-2\omega^{2}C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}-4}\,\Phi\leq C_{data}.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Lemma
3.2
is satisfied if the nudging parameter
\omega
is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
which would then require
H
to be
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
. However, the numerical experiments provided in Section
5
suggests that
H
can be taken much larger than that.

Define
C_{inf}:=\sup_{0\leq m<\infty}\|\phi_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Then under the conditions from Lemma
3.2
, we have
C_{inf}<\infty
on any regular mesh since
by the inverse inequality and the 2D Agmon inequality,

C_{inf}=\sup_{0\leq m<\infty}\|\phi_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sup_{0\leq m<\infty}Ch^{-1}\|\phi_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{2}}\leq CC_{data}h^{-1}.

While this is the best long-time
L^{\infty}
bound we were able to prove, we expect
C_{inf}=O(1)
and not
O(h^{-1})
, since computing CH in practice yields

|\phi_{h}^{m}(x)|\leq 1.1
or
1.2
, and never as high as 2. Furthermore, if we assume a finite end time
T
, then it is likely that we can prove this with the usual techniques
[
18
]

that
\max_{0\leq m\leq T/\Delta t}\|\phi_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ce^{T}
, with
C
depending on data and independent of
h
and
\Delta t
. Since CH solutions generally converge quickly to a steady state solution, we expect

\max_{0\leq m\leq T/\Delta t}\|\phi_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup_{0\leq m<\infty}\|\phi_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{\infty}},
from which we again infer that
C_{inf}
will be independent of
h
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,{\infty};H_{N}^{2}(\Omega))
represent the true solution and suppose that

\frac{1}{\Delta t}+\omega>\left(\frac{C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\omega^{2}+18(C_{inf})^{4}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

(3.5)

Then solutions to the CDA-FEM (
3.1
) are unique.

Remark 3.5
.

The condition (
3.5
) is satisfied with a sufficiently small
\Delta t
, or by
\omega
being sufficiently large while
H
is sufficiently small. We note that
this is a sufficient condition.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{h}^{m-1}\in Z_{h}
and
\varphi^{m}\in H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)
be given. Let
U
and
V
represent two solutions to (
3.1
). Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(U-V,\psi\right)+\left(\nabla(U^{3}-V^{3}),\nabla\psi\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(U-V,\psi\right)+\omega\left(I_{H}\left(U-V\right),\psi\right)=0.

Setting
\psi=U-V
and invoking Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t}+\omega\right)\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\left\|U-V\right\|_{2,h}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=-\left(\nabla(U^{3}-V^{3}),\nabla(U-V)\right)+\omega\left((U-V)-I_{H}(U-V),U-V\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sqrt{2}\left\|U^{3}-V^{3}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{2,h}+\omega\left\|(U-V)-I_{H}(U-V)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sqrt{2}\left\|U^{2}+UV+V^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{2,h}+C_{I}H\omega\left\|\nabla(U-V)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 3\sqrt{2}(C_{inf})^{2}\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{2,h}+C_{I}C_{P}H\omega\left\|U-V\right\|_{2,h}\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{18(C_{inf})^{4}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left\|U-V\right\|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\omega^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Combining like terms leads to

\displaystyle\left(\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t}+\omega\right)-\left(\frac{C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\omega^{2}+18(C_{inf})^{4}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right)\left\|U-V\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left\|U-V\right\|_{2,h}^{2}\leq 0.

Therefore, the solution to (
3.1
) is unique under the condition that (
3.5
) holds.
∎

4
Error Estimates

We are now in a position to prove that the global in time error estimates may be established in the
L^{2}
norm. We provide a rigorous convergence analysis for the semi-discrete method in the appropriate energy norms. Note that the CDA-FEM (
3.1
) is not well-defined for solutions to (
1.2
) since
Z_{h}\not\subset H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)
. Therefore, we define
W_{h}\subset H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)
to be the Hsieh-Clough-Tocher micro finite element space associated with
\mathcal{T}_{h}
as in
[
9
]
. We furthermore define the linear map
E_{h}:Z_{h}\rightarrow W_{h}\cap H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)
as in
[
9
]
which allows us to consider the following problem: Find
\varphi\in H_{N}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\varphi,\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\varphi^{3}-\varphi\right),\nabla\psi\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)

\displaystyle\,=\left(\partial_{t}\varphi,\psi-E_{h}\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\varphi^{3}-\varphi\right),\nabla(\psi-E_{h}\psi)\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi,\psi-E_{h}\psi\right),\quad\forall\,\psi\in Z_{h}.

(4.1)

Solutions of (
4
) are consistent with solutions of (
2.2
) since
a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi,E_{h}\psi\right)=a\!\left(\varphi,E_{h}\psi\right)
for all
\psi\in Z_{h}
.

We introduce the following notation:

\displaystyle\mathbbold{e}^{m}=\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m}+\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\quad\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m}:=\varphi^{m}-P_{h}\varphi^{m},\quad\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}:=P_{h}\varphi^{m}-\phi_{h}^{m},

where
\varphi^{m}:=\varphi(t_{m})
. Using this notation and subtracting (
3.1
) from (
4
), we have for all
\psi\in Z_{h}

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\mathbbold{e}^{m},\psi\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\mathbbold{e}^{m},\psi\right)+\omega\left(I_{H}\mathbbold{e}^{m},\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\left((\varphi^{m})^{3}-(\phi_{h}^{m})^{3}\right),\nabla\psi\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\varphi^{m}-\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\mathbbold{e}^{m-1},\nabla\psi\right)+\left(\nabla(\varphi^{m}-\varphi^{m-1}),\nabla\psi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\psi-E_{h}\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\left(\varphi^{m}\right)^{3}-\varphi^{m}\right),\nabla(\psi-E_{h}\psi)\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m},\psi-E_{h}\psi\right).

Invoking the properties of the Ritz projection operator, we have for all
\psi\in Z_{h}

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\psi\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\psi\right)+\omega\left(I_{H}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\left((\varphi^{m})^{3}-(\phi_{h}^{m})^{3}\right),\nabla\psi\right)=-\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m},\psi\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\omega\left(I_{H}\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m},\psi\right)+\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\varphi^{m}-\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\psi\right)+\left(\nabla(\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m-1}+\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}),\nabla\psi\right)+\left(\nabla(\varphi^{m}-\varphi^{m-1}),\nabla\psi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\psi-E_{h}\psi\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\left(\varphi^{m}\right)^{3}-\varphi^{m}\right),\nabla(\psi-E_{h}\psi)\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m},\psi-E_{h}\psi\right).

(4.2)

Adding and subtracting appropriate terms and setting
\psi=\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}
, we arrive at the key error equation

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\omega\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=-\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)-\omega\left(I_{H}\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\omega\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-I_{H}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\delta_{\Delta t}\varphi^{m}-\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\nabla\left((\varphi^{m})^{3}-(\phi_{h}^{m})^{3}\right),\nabla\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\nabla(\mathbbold{e}_{P}^{m-1}+\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}),\nabla\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\nabla(\varphi^{m}-\varphi^{m-1}),\nabla\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\left(\varphi^{m}\right)^{3}-\varphi^{m}\right),\nabla(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m})\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right).

(4.3)

The following lemma will bound many of the terms on the right hand side of (
4
) by oscillations in the time derivative of the concentration
\partial_{t}\varphi
. The procedure, known as a medius analysis, has been utilized in much of the literature found on the C
0
-IP method and details can be found in
[
9
]
. It relies on an equivalent formulation of the bilinear form
a_{h}^{IP}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
for functions satisfying
w\in H^{4}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
and
v\in H^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{IP}\left(w,v\right):=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\left(\Delta^{2}w\right)v\,dx+\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial n_{e}^{2}}\}\kern-3.125pt\}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial w}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket dS

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\left(\left\llbracket\frac{\partial\Delta w}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket v-\left\llbracket\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n_{e}^{2}}\right\rrbracket\{\kern-3.125pt\{\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{e}}\}\kern-3.125pt\}-\left\llbracket\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n_{e}\partial t_{e}}\right\rrbracket\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{e}}\right)dS

\displaystyle+\sigma\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\frac{1}{|e|}\int_{e}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial w}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket\left\llbracket\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket dS,

(4.4)

where
H^{s}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)|v_{K}\in H^{s}(K)\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\varphi^{m}
is a weak solution to (
2.2
). Then for any
m,h,\tau>0
,

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\left(\varphi^{m}\right)^{3}-\varphi^{m}\right),\nabla(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m})\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left[\text{Osc}_{j}(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m})\right]^{2}+\left\|\varphi^{m}-P_{h}\varphi^{m}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}\right)+\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}

for
t^{*}\in(t_{m-1},t_{m})
where
\text{Osc}_{j}(\nu)
is referred to as the oscillation of
\nu
(of order
j
) defined by

\displaystyle\text{Osc}_{j}(\nu):=\left(\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h^{4}\left\|\nu-\tilde{\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.5)

and where
\tilde{\nu}
is the
L^{2}
orthogonal projection of
\nu
on
P_{j}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
, the space of piecewise polynomial functions of degree less than or equal to
j
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\nu-\tilde{\nu})\psi\,dx=0\quad\forall\,\psi\in P_{j}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}).

Proof.

Properties of the Ritz projection operator (
3.2
) lead to,

\displaystyle a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m}-P_{h}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+a_{h}^{IP}\left(P_{h}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=-a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m}-P_{h}\varphi^{m},E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+a_{h}^{IP}\left(P_{h}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla^{2}(P_{h}\varphi^{m}-\varphi^{m}):\nabla^{2}(E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m})dx+\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{\frac{\partial^{2}\left(E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)}{\partial n_{e}^{2}}\}\kern-3.125pt\}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial P_{h}\varphi^{m}}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket dS

\displaystyle\qquad+a_{h}^{IP}\left(P_{h}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

(4.6)

Furthermore, the alternative definition (
4
) yields the following

\displaystyle a_{h}^{IP}\left(P_{h}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)=\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\left(\Delta^{2}P_{h}\varphi^{m}\right)\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{\frac{\partial^{2}\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)}{\partial n_{e}^{2}}\}\kern-3.125pt\}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial P_{h}\varphi^{m}}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket dS+\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial\Delta P_{h}\varphi^{m}}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)dS

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial^{2}P_{h}\varphi^{m}}{\partial n_{e}^{2}}\right\rrbracket\{\kern-3.125pt\{\frac{\partial\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)}{\partial n_{e}}\}\kern-3.125pt\}dS-\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial^{2}P_{h}\varphi^{m}}{\partial n_{e}\partial t_{e}}\right\rrbracket\frac{\partial\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)}{\partial t_{e}}dS

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\frac{1}{|e|}\int_{e}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial P_{h}\varphi^{m}}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket\left\llbracket\frac{\partial\left(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)}{\partial n_{e}}\right\rrbracket dS.

(4.7)

Combining equations (
4
)–(
4
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\nabla\left(\left(\varphi^{m}\right)^{3}-\varphi^{m}\right),\nabla(\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m})\right)+\varepsilon^{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\varphi^{m},\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-E_{h}\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}-\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{8}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\omega^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{36\left((C_{inf})^{2}+(C_{data}^{\prime})^{2}\right)^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2C_{P}^{4}(C^{\prime\prime}_{data})^{2}}{\omega}h^{2}+2C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{4}(C^{\prime}_{data})^{2}\omega h^{2}+\frac{2C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{data}(\Delta t)^{2}}{\omega}+\frac{36C_{P}^{4}(C^{**}_{data})^{2}\left((C_{inf})^{2}+(C_{data}^{\prime})^{2}\right)^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}h^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{8C_{P}^{4}(C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{data})^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}h^{2}+\frac{C}{C_{coer}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left[\text{Osc}_{j}(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m})\right]^{2}+(C^{**}_{data})^{2}h^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{8}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\omega^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{36\left((C_{inf})^{2}+(C_{data}^{\prime})^{2}\right)^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{data}^{*}\left(h^{2}+(\Delta t)^{2}\right)+\frac{C}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left[\text{Osc}_{j}(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m})\right]^{2}\right),

(4.22)

where
C_{data}^{*}
depends on
\varepsilon,\omega
, etc. but does not depend on the time step size
\Delta t
or the mesh size
h
. Multiplying by
2\Delta t
, combining like terms and dropping a few of the positive terms on the left hand side, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left[1+\omega\Delta t-2\Delta t\left(\frac{2C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\omega^{2}+36\left((C_{inf})^{2}+(C_{data}^{\prime})^{2}\right)^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right]\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+16\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{data}^{*}\Delta t\left(h^{2}+(\Delta t)^{2}\right)+\frac{C\Delta t}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left[\text{Osc}_{j}(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m})\right]^{2}\right).

(4.23)

Multiplying by
\dfrac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+16\Delta t}
leads to

\displaystyle\left[1+\Delta t\left(\frac{\omega C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}-4C_{I}^{2}C_{P}^{2}H^{2}\omega^{2}-72\left((C_{inf})^{2}+(C_{data}^{\prime})^{2}\right)^{2}-16}{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+16\Delta t}\right)\right]\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left\|\mathbbold{e}_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\dfrac{C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta t}{(C_{coer}\varepsilon^{2}+16\Delta t)}\left(h^{2}+h^{5}+(\Delta t)^{2}\right)C_{data}^{*},

(4.24)

where we have used the bound
\text{Osc}_{j}(\partial_{t}\varphi^{m})\leq Ch^{2+\min\{\ell,k+1\}}
such that
k=2
is the polynomial degree of the finite element space
Z_{h}
and
\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{3}(\Omega))
(
\ell=3
) by the higher regularity (
4.11
) assumption to obtain a bound on the oscillations of
\partial_{t}\varphi
. (See
[
9
]
for details.)

Lemma
2.4
now guarantees the required results provided the choice of

and

to hold.

Numerical Experiments

In this section, we present results of several numerical experiments which demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed data assimilation finite element method. The Firedrake Project
[
38
]
was used to perform all numerical experiments. We use a square domain
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
and take
\mathcal{T}_{h}
to be a regular triangulation of
\Omega
consisting of right isosceles triangles which is a quasi-uniform family. (We use a family of meshes
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
such that no triangle in the mesh has more than one edge on the boundary.) Additionally, in each experiment, we set the interfacial width parameter
\varepsilon=0.05
.

The data assimilation term
\omega\left(I_{H}(\phi_{h}^{m}-\varphi^{m}),\psi\right)
was computed as follows. A true solution
\varphi
was obtained at all times by selecting a cross shaped region as initial conditions as shown in the top right image of Figure
6
, setting the nudging parameter
\omega=0
, and solving the CH equation using the C
0
interior penalty FEM (
3.1
). A data assimilation grid size
H
was chosen and grid points were identified and located on the finite element mesh. A vector was then created such that the value of 1 was assigned for all nodes corresponding to these grid points and a value of 0 was assigned for all other nodes. Let us name this vector
v
. Then the data assimilation term
\omega\left(I_{H}(\phi_{h}^{m}-\varphi^{m}),\psi\right)
was computed by

\displaystyle\omega\left(I_{H}(\phi_{h}^{m}-\varphi^{m}),\psi\right)=\omega\left(v\phi_{h}^{m}-v\varphi^{m},v\psi\right),

where we note that
v\in Z_{h}
and that this is equivalent to the interpolation method onto a coarse mesh of piecewise constants
Z_{H}
, as described in
[
39
]
. Finally, in each of the experiments, the initial conditions for the numerical solution
\phi_{0}
was set to random initial conditions as shown in Figure
6
.

The first numerical experiment demonstrates the effectiveness of the CDA-FEM for various grid sizes
H
. For this experiment, we set the nudging parameter
\omega=\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}}=400
as indicated by the theory above. We then chose five different grid sizes
H=0.011049,0.015625,0.03125,

0.0625,
and
0.125
, which correspond respectively to 8,100, 4,096, 1,024, 256 and 64 grid points, while the fine mesh uses piecewise quadratics and has 33,025 grid points. Theorem
4.3
provides a sufficient condition that the grid size should be chosen as
H=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
but our experiments suggest that a grid size much coarser than that will produce good results.

6
Conclusions and Future Directions

We proposed, analyzed and tested a CDA-FEM method for the Cahn-Hilliard equations. A fourth order formulation of Cahn-Hilliard was used, and so that common FE software packages could be used, the spatial discretization used was C
0
interior penalty. We proved long time stability and accuracy of the method, provided enough measurement points and a large enough nudging parameter. Numerical tests revealed the method is very effective.

For future work, there are several important questions that remain unresolved. First, making a CDA method work for the more
commonly used second order mixed formulation is an important next step. Second, the analytical results herein give sufficient conditions on
H
and
\omega
for the results to hold, but our numerical tests suggest these conditions are not sharp. Hence an improved analysis that sharpens these bounds may be possible. Finally, extending these results to two-phase flow is an important future direction.

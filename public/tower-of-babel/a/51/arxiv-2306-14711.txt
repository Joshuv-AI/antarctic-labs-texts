The moduli space of cyclic covers in positive characteristic This file documents compositio version 1.15 and was last revised 2011/01/24.
Huy Dang, Matthias Hippold

Abstract
We study the p p -rank stratification of the moduli space 𝒜 ​ 𝒮 ​ 𝒲 ( d 1 , d 2 , … , d n ) \ASW_{(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})} , which represents ℤ / p n \mathbb{Z}/p^{n} -covers in characteristic p > 0 p>0 whose ℤ / p i \mathbb{Z}/p^{i} -subcovers have conductor d i d_{i} . In particular, we identify the irreducible components of the moduli space and determine their dimensions. To achieve this, we analyze the ramification data of the represented curves and use it to classify all the irreducible components of the space. In addition, we provide a comprehensive list of pairs ( p , ( d 1 , d 2 , … , d n ) ) (p,(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})) for which 𝒜 ​ 𝒮 ​ 𝒲 ( d 1 , d 2 , … , d n ) \ASW_{(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})} in characteristic p p is irreducible. Finally, we investigate the geometry of 𝒜 ​ 𝒮 ​ 𝒲 ( d 1 , d 2 , … , d n ) \ASW_{(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})} by studying the deformations of cyclic covers which vary the p p -rank and the number of branch points.



Classification:
14H30 (primary), 14H10, (secondary).

keywords
Artin-Schreier-Witt theory, moduli space, connectedness.

1
Introduction

Consider an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. An
n
-Artin-Schreier-Witt
k
-curve, or simply an Artin-Schreier-Witt curve when
n
is fixed, refers to a smooth, projective, connected
k
-curve
Y
that serves as a
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-cover of the projective line. It is worth noting that any Galois cover can be split into its tame and wild parts. The tame part can be understood through the well-known Kummer theory. Hence, studying these Artin-Schreier-Witt curves is sufficient for comprehending all cyclic covers. Moreover, these curves can be effectively constructed and analyzed using explicit methods as they are determined by a (class of) length-
n
Witt vector over
k
. Additionally, the genus of the covering and the
p
-rank of its Jacobian can be easily derived from the represented Witt vector, as discussed in Section
2.2
.

When
n=1
,
Y
is called an Artin-Schreier curve and has been extensively studied. Pries and Zhu defined the moduli space
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
that parametrizes Artin-Schreier curves of fixed genus
g
and established many nice properties
[
PZ12
]
. In this paper, we introduce the moduli space
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
(
\underline{\iota}=(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
), which represents
n
-Artin-Schreier-Witt curves whose
\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}
sub-covering has a genus
g_{i}
associated with
\iota_{i}
. We study its stratification by
p
-rank into strata denoted by
\ASW_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
, where
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
determines the
p
-ranks. Points in these strata correspond to curves whose
\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}
sub-covering has a
p
-rank of
s_{i}
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

1.

The irreducible components of
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
are in bijection with the set of partitions
M=(\iota_{j,i})\in\mathbb{M}_{r\times n}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
of
\underline{\iota}
with no “essential parts” (see Section
4.3
).

2.

The irreducible component of
\ASW_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
associated with the partition
M=(\iota_{j,i})\in\mathbb{M}_{r\times n}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
has dimension given by

s_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(\iota_{j,i}-1-\left\lfloor\frac{\iota_{j,i}-1}{p}\right\rfloor\right).

As an application, we determine all cases when
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
is irreducible.

Corollary 1.2
.

The moduli space
\ASW_{(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n})}
is irreducible if and only if the following conditions hold:

1.

\iota_{1}=2
or
\iota_{1}=3
.

2.

\iota_{i}=p\iota_{i-1}-p+1
or
\iota_{i}=p\iota_{i-1}-p+2
for
2\leq i\leq n
.

Another fascinating aspect of
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
is the existence of coverings corresponding to certain closed points in the moduli space, where the number of branch points varies while remaining in the same flat family. In other words, it is possible to construct a flat deformation of such a cover, in which the number of branch points changes while the genus remains constant. This phenomenon was first studied by Mézard in her thesis
[
Méz98
]
and was later utilized by Pop in the solution to the Oort conjecture
[
Pop14
]
. Recently, the first author has developed a technique employing the concept of Hurwitz tree (introduced by Henrio
[
Hen00
]
and enhanced by Bouw, Brewis, Wewers
[
BW06
]

[
BW09
]
) to determine the existence of a deformation with specified ramification data
[
Dan20c
,
Dan20b
]
. In this manuscript, we will utilize the main result from that work to establish relations between the moduli spaces.

Proposition 1.3
.

The canonical morphism
\ASW_{(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n-1},\iota_{n},\ldots,\iota_{m})}\xrightarrow{}\ASW_{(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n-1},\iota_{n})}
is closed.

As a consequence, we can prove some results regarding the (dis)connectedness of
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
p\geq 5
and
3\leq\iota_{1}\leq 2p-2
, the moduli space
\ASW_{(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n})}
is disconnected.

Outline

This manuscript presents a generalization of the work in
[
PZ12
]
and closely follows its structure. In Section §
2
, we provide a comprehensive overview of Artin-Schreier-Witt theory and introduce a combinatorial concept called the branching datum. This datum encodes the ramification data of cyclic coverings with a fixed genus at each level, generalizing the notion of “partitions of conductors” used by Pries and Zhu while preserving its desirable properties. In Section
3
, we construct the moduli space
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
and introduce its
p
-rank strata
\ASW_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
. Additionally, we identify the irreducible components of
\ASW_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
. The deformation of elements in
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
is discussed in Section
4
, where we employ this information to determine the irreducibility of
\ASW_{\underline{\iota}}
. Finally, in Section
5
, we explore the connections between the moduli spaces and derive various results concerning their connectedness.

2.1
Partitions of integer matrix

This section contains technical content. For better understanding, readers are advised to refer to Section
2.2
before proceeding.

Consider a prime number
p
, and let
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
be an
n
-tuple satisfying the following conditions:

a)

d_{1}\geq 1

b)

d_{i}\geq pd_{i-1}-p

We define
\Omega_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})}
as the set of

of the tuple
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. These partitions are

of the form:

M=\begin{bmatrix}d_{1,1}&d_{1,2}&\ldots&d_{1,n}\\
d_{2,1}&d_{2,2}&\ldots&d_{2,n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
d_{r,1}&d_{r,2}&\ldots&d_{r,n}\\
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{r\times n}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})

subject to the following conditions:

1.

d_{i,1}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}

2.

d_{i,j}\geq pd_{i,j-1}-p+1

3.

if
d_{i,j}>pd_{i,j-1}-p+1
, then
d_{i,j}=pd_{i,j-1}-p+1+a_{j}
, where
a_{j}
is coprime to
p
, and

4.

\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i,j}=d_{j}
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,r
, let
r_{j}(M)
denote the
j
-th row of the matrix
M
. Let
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. We define
\Omega_{\underline{d},\underline{s}}
as the subset of
\Omega_{\underline{\iota}}
consisting of matrices
M=[d_{i,j}]
where the number of non-zero entries in the
i
-th column is precisely
s_{i}
. Furthermore, let
M^{p^{n-i}}
be the sub-
r\times i
matrix of
M
that consists of the first
i
columns.

We introduce a partial ordering denoted by
\prec
on
\Omega_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})}
as follows: Given an
r_{1}\times n
matrix
M_{1}
and an
r_{2}\times n
matrix
M_{2}
, we say
M_{1}\prec M_{2}
if each column of
M_{2}
is a partition of the corresponding column in
M_{1}
. To illustrate, consider the case when
p=3
. We can observe the following relation:

\begin{bmatrix}7&21\\
3&8\\
\end{bmatrix}\prec\begin{bmatrix}4&10\\
3&11\\
3&8\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Similar to the approach in
[
PZ12
]
, this ordering gives rise to a directed graph denoted as
G_{\underline{\iota}}
. The vertices of the graph correspond to the elements of
\Omega_{\underline{\iota}}
. For any
M,N\in\Omega_{\underline{\iota}}
, there exists an edge from
M
to
N
in
G_{\underline{\iota}}
if
M\prec N
and there is no other partition between them.

2.2
Artin-Schreier-Witt curves

Artin-Schreier-Witt theory (see, e.g.,
[
Lan02
]
) states that for a field
K
of characteristic
p
, there is an isomorphism

\cohom^{1}(G_{K},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})\cong W_{n}(K)/\wp(W_{n}(K)),

where
G_{K}
is the absolute Galois group of
K
,
W_{n}(K)
is the ring of length-
n
Witt vectors over
K
, and
\wp(y):=F(y)-y
is the Artin-Schreier-Witt isogeny. We say that two elements
\underline{f},\underline{g}\in W_{n}(K)
are in the same
Artin-Schreier-Witt class
if there exists
\underline{h}\in W_{n}(K)
such that
\underline{f}=\underline{g}+\wp(\underline{h})
. Such a class is denoted by
[\underline{f}]
.

Now, let’s consider an arbitrary commutative subgroup
V
of
W_{n}(K)
. We define
\wp^{-1}V
as the set of
\underline{a}\in W_{n}(\overline{K})
such that
\wp(\underline{a})\in V
. Furthermore, we denote the field obtained by adjoining all elements
\underline{a}\in\wp^{-1}V
to
K
as
K(\wp^{-1}V)
. The following proposition offers a classification of extensions
E/K
that are abelian with exponent at most
p^{n}
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Lor08
, §26, Theorem 6]
)
.

For a field
K
of characteristic
p
, using the above notations, the map

V\mapsto K(\wp^{-1}V)

(1)

establishes a bijection between the set of subgroups
V
of
W_{n}(K)
containing
\wp(W_{n}(K))
and the set of subfields
E
of
\overline{K}
such that
E/K
is abelian with exponent at most
p^{n}
. The groups
G=\gal(K(\wp^{-1}V))
and
V/\wp W_{n}(K)
are in a dual relationship, meaning that each is canonically isomorphic to the group of characters of the other.

From there, we can deduce a classification of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-extensions of
K
in terms of Witt vectors.

Corollary 2.2
.

The map (
1
) induces a bijection between the set of subgroups
V
containing
\wp(W_{n}(K))
of
W_{n}(K)
generated by
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in W_{n}(K)
, where
a_{1}\not\in\wp K
, and
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-extensions of
K
.

Henceforth, we will use the term “field extension” interchangeably with “character” (in the context of
\cohom^{1}(G_{K},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
). Consequently, we can refer to the product of field extensions.

Due to Artin-Schreier-Witt theory and the correspondence between covers of curves and extensions of function fields, we can represent any
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-cover
\phi_{n}:Y_{n}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
be an affine equation of the form

\wp(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=\underline{f},

(2)

where
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
is an element of the ring
W_{n}(k(x))
. Moreover, for
1\leq i<n
, the character
\phi_{i}:=(\phi_{n})^{p^{n-i}}
corresponds to the unique
\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}
-subcover
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
of
\phi_{n}
given by

\wp(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i})=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}).

(3)

We also denote
\phi_{n}
by
\mathfrak{K}_{n}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
. The following proposition is well-known.

Proposition 2.3
.

Every vector
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in W_{n}(k(x))
belongs to the same Artin-Schreier-Witt class as a reduced vector
\underline{g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
, where none of the non-zero terms in the partial fraction decomposition of
g_{i}
has degree divisible by
p
.

Proof.

When
n=1
, any term
a
of
g_{1}
of degree divisible to
p
is a
p
-power, i.e.,
a=b^{p}
for some
b\in k(x)
. Hence,
a
could be killed off by adding
b^{p}-b
to

. When
n>1
, one can employ the classical induction technique for Witt vectors (e.g.,
[
Lor08
, §26, Addendum]
). See
[
Ach17
, Lemma A.2.3]
for a more detailed proof.
∎

We call such a vector
\underline{g}

reduced
. For the rest of this discussion, we assume that
\underline{f}
is reduced.

Proposition 2.4
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of reduced Witt vectors
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in W_{n}(k(x))
modulo the natural
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})^{\times}
-action, where
a_{1}\neq 0
, and the set of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
.

Proof.

Recall that, as stated in Corollary
2.2
, every group
\langle\underline{a},\wp(W_{n}(k(x)))\rangle/\wp(W_{n}(k(x)))\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
corresponds to a cyclic-order-
p^{n}
cover. Let
V_{p^{n}}\subset W_{n}(k(x))/\wp(W_{n}(k(x)))
denote the set of order-
p^{n}
elements, which can be represented by reduced length-
n
Witt vectors with non-zero first entries. It is straightforward to verify that the action of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})^{\times}
on

by multiplication induces a faithful action on

. Furthermore, it is worth noting that the orbit of each element generates the same extension of

. Therefore, the proposition follows.
∎

Now, let us examine the ramification of
\phi_{n}
. Suppose
\mathscr{P}:=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}
is the set of poles of the
f_{i}
’s. Then
\mathscr{P}
is also the branch locus of
\phi_{n}
. Moreover, for each ramified point

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{n},Q_{j}}/\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},P_{j}}
. Hence, we can define the ramification filtration of
\phi_{n}
at a branch point

.

Suppose the inertia group of

is
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m_{j}}
(where
n\leq m_{j}
). We say that the
i
-th ramification break
of
\phi_{n}
at
P_{j}
is
-1
for

. For
i>n-m_{j}
, the
i
-th ramification break of
\phi_{n}
at
P_{j}
is the
(i-n+m_{j})
-th one of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{n},Q_{j}}/\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},P_{j}}
. We denote the
i
-th upper ramification break of
\phi_{n}
at

by

, and we define the

-th conductor of

to be

. One then can explicitly compute the ramification filtration at each branch point of

in terms of

as follows.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
Gar02
, Theorem 1]
)
.

With the assumptions and the notations as above, we have

u_{j,i}=\max\{p^{i-l}\deg_{(x-P_{j})^{-1}}(f_{l})\mid l=1,\ldots,i\},

(4)

for
i>n-m_{j}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

With the above notations, the genus of
Y_{i}
is

g_{Y_{i}}=1+p^{i}(g_{X}-1)+\frac{\sum_{l=1}^{i}(\sum_{j=1}^{r}e_{j,l})(p^{l}-p^{l-1})}{2}

(5)

Proof.

Applying the Grothendieck-Ogg-Shafarevich (GOS) formula
[
MR077
, Exp. X formula 7.2]
to
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\xrightarrow{}X
, we obtain

2g_{Y_{i}}-2=\deg(\phi_{i})(2g_{X}-2)+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sw_{P_{j}}(\phi_{i}),

(6)

where
\sw_{P_{j}}(\phi_{i})
is the Swan conductor of
\phi_{i}
at
P_{j}
. See also
[
KS08
, Theorem 4.2.9]
for a generalized version of the formula to higher dimension
According to
[
Pop14

, the value of

(which Pop calls the degree of the different) is determined by the ramification at

\sw_{P_{j}}(\phi_{i})=\sum_{l=1}^{i}e_{j,l}(p^{l}-p^{l-1}).

(7)

This immediately implies the claim about the genus of
Y_{i}
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

An alternative derivation of equation (
5
) can be obtained by considering the ramified points. Let
P_{j}\in\mathscr{P}
be a ramified point, and let

be the inertia degree of
\phi_{i}
at

. The number of ramified points in

is then

\begin{split}2g_{Y_{i}}-2&=\deg(\phi_{i})(2g_{X}-2)+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{l=1}^{p^{i-m^{i}_{j}}}\deg\big(\mathscr{D}_{Q_{j,l}}\big).\\
&=p^{i}(2g_{X}-2)+\sum_{j=1}^{r}p^{i-m^{i}_{j}}\deg\big(\mathscr{D}_{Q_{j,1}}\big),\end{split}

(8)

where
\mathscr{D}_{Q_{j,1}}
is the different at

in the classical sense
[
Ser79

. Specifically, we have

\deg\big(\mathscr{D}_{Q_{j,1}}\big)=\sum_{s=i-m^{i}_{j}+1}^{i}e_{j,s}(p^{s-i+m^{i}_{j}}-p^{s-i+m^{i}_{j}-1}).

Therefore, we obtain
\sw_{P_{j}}(\phi_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{p^{i-m^{i}_{j}}}\deg\big(\mathscr{D}_{Q_{j,l}}\big)=\sum_{l=1}^{i}e_{j,l}(p^{l}-p^{l-1})
and thus (

[

A matrix of the form (

Proof.

“

2.3
The
p
-rank of Artin-Schreier-Witt curves and partitions

Recall that the
p
-rank of a curve
C/k
, denoted by
p_{C}
, is defined as the integer such that the cardinality of
\text{Jac}(C)[p](\overline{k})
is
p^{p_{C}}
. This birational invariant holds significant importance as it has been shown to influence the automorphism group
\text{Aut}(C)

[
Nak87
]
and the Newton polygon of
C
’s associated
L
-function
[
AP14
]
.

When a cover
\rho:X\xrightarrow{}Y
is
G
-Galois, where
G
is a
p
-group, the Deuring-Shafarevich formula
[
Cre84
, Corollary 1.8]
indicates that

1-p_{X}=\lvert G\rvert(1-p_{Y})-\sum_{x\in X^{\ram}}(e_{x}-1),

(10)

where
X^{\ram}
represents the ramified locus of
\rho
, and
e_{x}
is the ramification index at the point
x
. We now apply this formula to the case where
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
,
Y\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}=\Proj k[x,u]
, and
\rho
is defined by a reduced
\underline{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in W_{n}(k(x))
. This leads to the following expression:

p_{X}=1-p^{n}+\sum_{x_{i}\in X^{\ram}}(e_{x_{i}}-1),

where
e_{x_{i}}=p^{m_{i}}
with
m_{i}:=\min\{j\mid\nu_{P_{i}}(f_{j})<0\}
. Suppose in addition that
\rho
has branching datum
M
as in (
9
). Then one can apply the analysis on Remark
2.7
to get

p_{X}=1-p^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}p^{n-i}(p^{i}-1)s_{i}.

(11)

where
s_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) is the number of branch points of ramification index
p^{i}
. Thus, the
p
-rank of a cover is determined by
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
.
In addition, a straight-forward computation shows that, if one fixed the
p
-ranks of the sub-covers of a family of Artin-Schreier-Witt covers, then
\underline{s}
is also fixed. The
n
-Artin-Schreier-Witt curves of conductors
\underline{\iota}
and
p
-ranks corresponding to
\underline{s}
are intimately related to the partition set
\Omega_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
as defined in §
2.1
.

Lemma 2.14
.

There exists an Artin-Schreier-Witt
k
-curve with conductors
\underline{\iota}
and
p
-ranks
\underline{s}
if and only if
\Omega_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
is nonempty.

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
” Suppose
\phi_{n}
is an Artin-Schreier-Witt
k
-curve with conductors
\underline{\iota}
and
p
-ranks
\underline{s}
. By Theorem
2.5
, the associated matrix of
\phi_{n}
belongs to
\Omega_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
, implying that
\Omega_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
is nonempty.

“
\Leftarrow
” Let
M
be a matrix in
\Omega_{\underline{\iota},\underline{s}}
, we can apply the process in the proof of Proposition
2.8
to construct a curve with datum
M
.
∎

Moduli space of Artin-Schreier-Witt curves

Consider fixed parameters
p,\underline{\iota}=(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n})
and a fixed algebraically closed ground field

of characterisic

. In this section, we study the strata
\mathcal{ASW}_{\underline{\iota},M}
of the moduli space
\mathcal{ASW}_{\underline{\iota}}
where

is a matrix of the form (

over

3.1
Artin-Schreier-Witt covers

Let

be a

-scheme. An

-curve is a smooth, proper, surjective, finitely presented morphism

whose geometric fibers are smooth, projected connected curves. Similar as in
[
PZ12
]
, we define an

over

to be an

-curve for which there exists an (unspecified) inclusion

. By tracking different data associated to Artin-Schreier-Witt curves, we can define three different Moduli functors:

Definition 3.1
.

Fix the data of conductors
\underline{\iota}
. We define the following Moduli functors from the category of
k
-schemes to the category of sets:

1.

The functor
\mathcal{ASW}_{\underline{\iota}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
maps

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\mapsto\{C/S\}/_{\sim}

(12)

where
C
is a
S
-curve, such that there exists an inclusion
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\to Aut(C/S)
,
C/S
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}
and each geometric fiber of the associated quotient map
C\to C/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
is a
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-cover with conductors
\underline{\iota}
.

2.

The functor
\mathcal{ASW}act_{\underline{\iota}}
maps

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\mapsto\{C/S,\kappa:\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\to Aut(C/S)\}/_{\sim}

(13)

with
C
as above, but here the inclusion

is part of the data.

3.

The functor
\mathcal{ASW}cov_{\underline{\iota}}
maps

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\mapsto\{C/S,\kappa:\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\to Aut(C/S),\psi:C/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}\}/_{\sim}

(14)

with
C,\kappa
as above and a fixed isomorphism

The moduli functors
\mathcal{ASW}_{\underline{\iota}}
and
\mathcal{ASW}cov_{\underline{\iota}}
are those of intrinsic interest, while one can think about
\mathcal{ASW}act_{\underline{\iota}}
as an auxiliary object sitting in between them.

Remark 3.2
.

As for each
0\geq i\geq n
there is a unique
\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}
subgroup in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
, so we can factor the map

uniquely into a sequence of
\mathbb{Z}/p
-Galois covers. With this observation, we can rephrase

as:

\begin{split}\mathcal{ASW}\cov_{\overline{\iota}}:\{k\text{-Schemes}\}&\xrightarrow{}\text{Sets}\\
S&\mapsto\{\phi:C_{n}\xrightarrow{}C_{n-1}\xrightarrow{}\ldots\xrightarrow{}C_{0}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}\}/_{\sim}\end{split}

(15)

where

is
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-Galois, hence

is
\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}
-Galois. The curve

can be identified as the
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n-i}
-invariant part of
C_{n}
.

A direct computation with the Riemann-Hurwitz formula shows that with this notation, it holds for the genera of the subquotients

\displaystyle g_{C_{i}}=1+p^{i}(g_{C_{0}}-1)+\frac{\sum_{l=1}^{i}\iota_{l}(p^{j}-p^{j-1})}{2}.

(16)

Remark 3.3
.

We restrict ourselves to the case where the genus of
C_{1}
is at least
2
because of technical reasons. This is necessary to avoid the existence of infinitesimal automorphisms on subquotients and could be avoided by adding markings to the curve. However, these fringe cases are not really relevant for our theory, so they are ignored at this point.

3.2
The branch divisor

Suppose

such that the degree of

is
\iota_{i}

]

Suppose

, one can associate with

a set of
n

, and
{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}}

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S^{\prime}}
. Let

be the

matrix

, where

. The partition

of
\underline{\iota}
induces a natural stratification
\C\divisor_{\underline{\iota},M}
of
\C\divisor_{\underline{\iota}}
by varying the sections

associated with
\underline{D}
.

We set
\mathcal{C}\divisor_{\underline{\iota}}^{1}:=\bigcup_{M\in\Omega_{\underline{\iota}}}\mathcal{C}\divisor_{\underline{\iota},M}

.

If

4.2
The Hurwitz tree technique

Let

be a one-point cover. The
Hurwitz tree
is a valuable tool for investigating the possibility of deforming
\chi
with a given type. It is a combinatorial-differential object that takes the shape of the dual graph of a cover’s semistable model. Each edge is assigned a
thickness
corresponding to the associated annulus. Additionally, each vertex of the tree encodes the degeneration data of the restriction of
\chi
to the corresponding closed subdisc, measured by the

. These conductors are described by
2
-tuples of the form
(\delta,\omega)
, where:

•

\delta\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
is the
depth Swan conductor
,

•

For
\delta>0
,
\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{\kappa}
is the
differential Swan conductor
,

•

For
\delta=0
,
\omega\in W_{n}(\kappa)
is the
reduction type
.

This information not only determines whether the associated cover has good reduction but also identifies the precise locations of the singularities
[
Kat87
]
. The concept was initially formulated by Henrio
[
Hen00
]
to address the lifting problem for
\mathbb{Z}/p
in mixed characteristic. Subsequently, Bouw, Brewis, and Wewers made improvements to the technique
[
BW06
]
,
[
BW09
]
. In
[
Dan20b
]
, the first author extended the concept to the equal-characteristic setting, enabling the use of combinatorics and differential equation tools to study Artin-Schreier-Witt deformations. For the purposes of this manuscript, we will adopt this extended definition.

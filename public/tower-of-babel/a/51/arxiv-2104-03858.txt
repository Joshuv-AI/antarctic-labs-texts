Introduction and Main results

In this article, we establish the existence of unique weak solution for the problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{split}-\Delta_{H,p}u&=\frac{f(x)}{u^{q(x)}}\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{split}\right.

and existence of at least two weak solutions for

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{split}-\Delta_{H,p}u&=\frac{\lambda}{u^{q(x)}}+u^{r}\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{split}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
and
0<q\in C(\bar{\Omega})
. Our other important assumptions for (
1.1
) are
2\leq p<\infty
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
is a nonnegative function (see Theorem
1.4
-
1.5
for precise assumptions). For the problem (
1.2
), we assume the parameter
\lambda
to be positive and
r\in(p-1,p^{*}-1)
, where
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
is the critical Sobolev exponent for
1<p<N
(refer Theorem
1.6
-
1.7
). The key operator in our problems, that is the Finsler p-Laplacian is defined as

\Delta_{H,p}u:=\text{div}\big(H(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u)\big),

where
\nabla_{\eta}
denotes the gradient operator with respect to the
\eta
variable and
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
is known as the Finsler-Minkowski norm satisfying the following hypothesis:

(H0)

H(x)\geq 0
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(H1)

H(x)=0
, if and only if
x=0
.

(H2)

H(tx)=|t|H(x)
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(H3)

H\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\right)
.

(H4)

the Hessian matrix
\nabla_{\eta}^{2}\big(\frac{H^{2}}{2}\big)(x)
is positive definite for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

The dual
H_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
of
H
is defined by

(1.3)

H_{0}(\xi):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle x,\xi\rangle}{H(x)}.

More details on this can be found in Bao-Chern-Shen

Remark 1.1
.

Since all norms in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
are equivalent, there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

C_{1}|x|\leq H(x)\leq C_{2}|x|,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We are able to define an equivalent norm on the Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as

(1.4)

\|u\|_{X}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Examples:
Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(i)

then, for

(1.5)

H_{t}(x):=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}|^{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{t}};

(ii)

for
\lambda,\mu>0
, we define

(1.6)

H_{\lambda,\mu}(x):=\sqrt{\lambda\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{4}}+\mu\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}}.

Then, one may check that the functions
H_{t},H_{\lambda,\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
given by (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) satisfies all the hypothesis from
(H0)-(H4)
or refer Mezei-Vas
[
36
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

For
i=1,2
if
\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}
are positive real numbers such that
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\mu_{1}}\neq\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\mu_{2}}
, then
H_{\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}}
and

given by (
1.6
) defines two non-isometric norms in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Moreover, for
H=H_{t}
given by (
1.5
) we have

(1.7)

\Delta_{H_{t},p}u=\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u:=\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u),\,(p\text{-Laplacian})\,\text{if }t=2,\,1<p<\infty,\\
\mathcal{S}_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i}\Big),\,\text{if }t=p\in(1,\infty),\end{cases}

where

This last remark puts an important touch to our problem stating that Finsler p-Laplacian generalizes the
p
-Laplacian. So our result are true for a general set of quasilinear operators and we strongly point out here that (

The equations (
1.1
) and (

where

which is a continuous function up to the boundary and it is positive. Due to the blow up phenomenon, critical point theory fails here and the standard technique of approximating the problem with a non singular set up comes to our rescue. Another striking feature is the singular exponent being a function here which covers both
0<q(x)<1
and
q(x)\geq 1
cases together. In the
0<q(x)<1
for all

is not bounded by

near the boundary of

1.2

we show that the variational techniques using the Gateaux differentiability of corresponding energy functional can be advantageous to show existence of at least two weak solutions.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and

we have

(A)

x\cdot\nabla_{\eta}H(x)=H(x)
.

(B)

\nabla_{\eta}H(tx)=\text{sign}(t)\nabla_{\eta}H(x)

(C)

|\nabla_{\eta}H(x)|\leq C

(D)

is stricly convex.

Corollary 2.2

On account of Lemma

(2.1)

H(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(x)\cdot x=H(x)^{p}\geq C_{1}|x|^{p},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(2.2)

\big|H(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(x)\big|\leq C_{2}|x|^{p-1},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and}

(2.3)

H(tx)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(tx)=|t|^{p-2}tH(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Lemma 2.4

&=H(x)^{p}+H(y)^{p}-H(x)^{p-1}\langle\nabla_{\eta}H(x),y\rangle-H(y)^{p-1}\langle\nabla_{\eta}H(y),x\rangle\\
&\geq H(x)^{p-1}+H(y)^{p-1}-H(x)^{p-1}H(y)-H(y)^{p-1}H(x)\\
&=\big(H(x)^{p-1}-H(y)^{p-1}\big)\big(H(x)-H(y)\big).\end{split}

∎

3.
Preliminaries for the proof of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5

We present proof of our first main result in this section.
Here again, we assume
1<p<\infty
,
unless otherwise mentioned. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and nonnegative
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, let us define
f_{n}(x):=\min\{f(x),n\}
and consider the following approximated problem

(3.1)

\left.\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{H,p}u&=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{(u^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{q(x)}}\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right\}

First, we prove the following useful result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be a nonnegative function in
\Omega
. Then there exists a unique solution
u\in X\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
of the problem

(3.2)

\begin{split}-\Delta_{H,p}u=g\text{ in }\Omega,\,u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{split}

such that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
c_{\omega}
satisfying
u\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\omega
.

Proof.

Existence:
We define the energy functional
J:X\to\mathbb{R}
by

J(u):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}gu\,dx.

Noting Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.11
, it can be easily seen that
J
is coercive, weakly lower semicontinuous and a strictly convex functional. Therefore,
J
has a unique minimizer
u
in
X
. Since
J\in C^{1}(X)
, so
u\in X
is the unique solution of the equation

(3.3)

-\Delta_{H,p}u=g\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Noting
g\geq 0
and choosing
u_{-}:=\min\{u,0\}
as a test function in (
3.3
), by (
2.1
) we obtain

\begin{split}C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{-}|^{p}\,dx&\leq\int_{\Omega}H\big(\nabla u_{-}\big)^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u)\nabla u_{-}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}gu_{-}\,dx\leq 0,\end{split}

which gives,
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

Boundedness:
For any
k\geq 1
, we define a subset of
\Omega
given by

A(k):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\geq k\text{ in }\Omega\}.

Choosing
\phi_{k}:=(u-k)^{+}=\max\{u-k,0\}
as a test function in (
3.3
) and using the continuity of the mapping
X\hookrightarrow L^{l}(\Omega)
for some
l>p
from Lemma
2.11
along with (
2.1
) we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla\phi_{k})^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g\phi_{k}\penalty\ dx\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{A(k)}(u-k)\,dx\leq C_{0}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|A(k)|^{\frac{l-1}{l}}\|\phi_{k}\|,\end{split}

where
C_{0}
is the Sobolev constant. Hence, we have

(3.4)

\|\phi_{k}\|^{p-1}\leq C|A(k)|^{\frac{l-1}{l}},

for some positive constant
C
depending on
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. We choose
h
such that
1<k<h
then using (
3.4
),
(u(x)-k)^{p}\geq(h-k)^{p}
on
A(h)
and
A(h)\subset A(k)
we get

\displaystyle(h-k)^{p}|A(h)|^{\frac{p}{l}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A(h)}(u(x)-k)^{l}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{l}}\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}(u(x)-k)^{l}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{l}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{k}\|^{p}\leq C|A(k)|^{\frac{p(l-1)}{l(p-1)}}.

Hence, we obtain

|A(h)|\leq\frac{C}{(h-k)^{l}}|A(k)|^{\frac{l-1}{p-1}}.

Therefore, observing that
\frac{l-1}{p-1}>1
, using Kinderlehrer-Stampacchia
[
34
, Lemma B.1]
we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,

for some positive constant
C
depending on
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Positivity:
Moreover since
g\neq 0
, we have
u\neq 0
in
\Omega
. Noting Corollary
2.2
and Corollary
2.7
, we can apply Heinonen-Kilpeläine-Martio
[
32
, Theorem 3.59]
so that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
c_{\omega}>0
such that
u\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\Omega
. Thus
u>0
in
\Omega
.
∎

Then, we have the following result concerning the problem (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
. Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique positive solution
u_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
to the problem (

) such that
u_{n+1}\geq u_{n}
in
\Omega
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover for every
\omega\Subset\Omega

(independent of

u_{n}\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\omega

Proof.

Step
1
. (Existence)
We observe that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

g_{n}(x):=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\big(v^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{q(x)}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}

is nonnegative. Therefore, by Lemma
3.1
we can define the map
T:L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega)

where

(3.5)

\left.\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{H,p}w_{n}&=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{(v^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{q(x)}}\text{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{n}&>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,w_{n}=\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right\}

such that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega

c_{\omega}
satisfying
w_{n}\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\omega
. Now, using
w_{n}
as a test function in the problem (
3.5
) and the fact that

(3.6)

\|w_{n}\|\leq Cn^{\frac{\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1}{p-1}},

for some constant

. We define

\begin{split}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&=\lambda\|T(v_{1})-T(v_{2})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq 2\lambda CC_{1}n^{\frac{\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1}{p-1}},\end{split}

where

is given by (

) and

is the Sobolev constant. Therefore, the set

is bounded in

.
To prove the continuity of

, let

strongly in

(3.7)

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla w_{n,k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla w_{n,k})\nabla(w_{n,k}-u_{n})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)}{(v_{k}^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{q(x)}}(w_{n,k}-w_{n})\,dx,

(3.8)

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla w_{n,k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla w_{n,k})\nabla(w_{n,k}-w_{n})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)}{(v^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{q(x)}}(w_{n,k}-w_{n})\,dx.

3.8

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(x)\left\{\left(v_{k}^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-q(x)}-\left(v^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-q(x)}\right\}(w_{n,k}-w_{n})\,dx\\

for some positive constant

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(x)\left\{\big(v_{k}^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{-q(x)}-\left(v^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-q(x)}\right\}(w_{n,k}-w_{n})\,dx\\

(3.11)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\left(v_{k}^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-q(x)}-\left(v^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-q(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dx=0.

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{q(x)}}-\frac{f_{n+1}(x)}{\left(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\right)^{q(x)}}\right\}(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\\

Hence from (

Let us denote the energy functional

Lemma 4.1

There exists

\inf\limits_{\|v\|\leq R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)<0\;\text{and}\;\inf\limits_{\|v\|=R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)\geq\rho,\text{ for }\lambda\in(0,\Lambda).

is given by Lemma

We fix

\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{r+1}\penalty\ dx\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{r+1}{p*}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{(\frac{p^{*}}{r+1})^{\prime}}}\leq Cl\|v\|^{r+1},

for some positive constant

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(te_{1})}{t}=-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\epsilon^{-q(x)}e_{1}\,dx<0,

\inf\limits_{\|v\|\leq R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)<0.

Moreover, since

, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{0,\epsilon}(v)\geq\frac{R^{p}}{p}-\frac{ClR^{r+1}}{r+1}:=2\rho\,(\text{say})>0.

We define

\Lambda:=\frac{\rho}{\sup\limits_{\|v\|=R}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-q(x)}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx\right)},

which is a positive constant and since

. We know that

(4.1)

(v^{+}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}\leq(v^{+})^{1-q(x)},

which gives

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla v)^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{r+1}\,dx-\frac{\lambda}{1-q(x)}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-q(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=I_{0,\epsilon}(v)-\frac{\lambda}{1-q(x)}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-q(x)}\,dx.

Therefore

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\|v\|=R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\limits_{\|v\|=R}I_{0,\epsilon}(v)-\lambda\sup\limits_{\|v\|=R}\left(\frac{1}{1-q(x)}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\rho-\lambda\sup\limits_{\|v\|=R}\left(\frac{1}{1-q(x)}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx\right)\geq\rho,

if
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda).

Lastly, it is easy to see that
I_{0,\epsilon}(te_{1})\to-\infty
, as

which implies that we can choose

such that

. Hence

I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(Te_{1})\leq I_{0,\epsilon}(Te_{1})<-1

which completes the proof.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
4.1
, we have

\inf\limits_{\|v\|=R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)\geq\rho\,\max\{I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(Te_{1}),I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(0)\}=0.

Our next Lemma ensures that

satisfies the Palais Smale
(PS)_{c}
condition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
2\leq p<N
, then
I_{\lambda,\epsilon}
satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
condition, for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
that is if
\{u_{k}\}\subset X
is a sequence satisfying

(4.2)

I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(u_{k})\to c\;\text{and}\;I_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{k})\to 0

as
k\to\infty
, then
\{u_{k}\}
contains a strongly convergent subsequence in
X
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{k}\}\subset X
satisfies (
4.2
) then we claim that
\{u_{k}\}
must be bounded in
X
. To see this using (
4.1
), we obtain

(4.3)

\begin{split}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(u_{k})-\frac{1}{r+1}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}&=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r+1}\right)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{k})^{p}\,dx-{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx\\
&\quad+\frac{\lambda}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{k}\penalty\ dx\\
&\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r+1}\right)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{k})^{p}\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{k}^{+})^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{\lambda}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{k}\penalty\ dx\\
&\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r+1}\right)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{k})^{p}\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{k}^{+})^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx-\frac{\lambda C}{\epsilon(r+1)}\|u_{k}\|,\end{split}

for some positive constant
C
(independent of
k
) where we have used Lemma
2.11
and the fact
0<q(x)<1
in
\overline{\Omega}
. Due to the same reasoning, we obtain

(4.4)

\begin{split}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{k}^{+})^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx&\geq-\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{k}|^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx\\
&\geq\frac{-1}{1-\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\{|u_{k}|\geq 1\}}|u_{k}|^{1-q(x)}dx+\int_{\Omega\cap\{|u_{k}|<1\}}|u_{k}|^{1-q(x)}dx\right)\\
&\geq\frac{-1}{1-\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\{|u_{k}|\geq 1\}}|u_{k}|dx+\int_{\Omega\cap\{|u_{k}|<1\}}|u_{k}|^{1-\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}dx\right)\\
&\geq-C\left(\|u_{k}\|+\|u_{k}\|^{1-\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\right),\end{split}

for some positive constant
C
independent of
k
. Thus inserting (
4.4
) into (
4.3
) and using the fact
r+1>p
along with Remark
1.1
, we get

(4.5)

I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(u_{k})-\frac{1}{r+1}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}\geq C_{1}\|u_{k}\|^{p}-C\left(\|u_{k}\|+\|u_{k}\|^{1-\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\right),

for some positive constant
C_{1}
(independent of
k
). Also from (
4.2
) it follows that for
k
large enough

(4.6)

\left|I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(u_{k})-\frac{1}{r+1}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}\right|\leq C+o(\|u_{k}\|),

for some positive constant
C
(independent of
k
).
Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), our claim follows since
p>1
. By reflexivity of
X
, there exists
u_{0}\in X
such that up to a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
X
as
k\to\infty
.

Claim:

u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
X
as
k\to\infty
.

By (
4.2
), we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{\int}_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{k})\nabla u_{0}\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{0}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{0}\penalty\ dx\right)=0

and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{\int}_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{k})\nabla u_{k}\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{k}\penalty\ dx\right)=0.

Now

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(H(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{k})-H(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{0})\big).\nabla(u_{k}-u_{0})\,dx\\
&=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q}u_{k}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q}u_{0}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{0}\penalty\ dx\right)\\
&\quad-\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{0}).\nabla u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{0})^{p}\penalty\ dx\right).\end{split}

From weak convergence of
\{u_{k}\}
we get

(4.8)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{0})\nabla u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{0})^{p}\penalty\ dx\right)=0.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\left|(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{0}\right|\leq\epsilon^{-q(x)}u_{0}\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}\left|\epsilon^{-q(x)}u_{0}\right|dx\leq\|\epsilon^{-q(x)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|\penalty\ dx<+\infty.

Thus Lebesgue Dominated convergence theorem gives that

(4.9)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{0}\penalty\ dx=\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{0}\penalty\ dx.

Since
u_{k}\to u_{0}
pointwise in
\Omega
, for any measurable subset
E
of
\Omega
we have

\begin{split}\int_{E}|(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{k}|\penalty\ dx&\leq\int_{E}\epsilon^{-q(x)}u_{k}\penalty\ dx\leq\|\epsilon^{-q(x)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}|E|^{\frac{p^{*}-1}{p^{*}}}\leq C(\epsilon)|E|^{\frac{p^{*}-1}{p^{*}}},\end{split}

so from Vitali convergence theorem, it follows that

(4.10)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{k}\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-q(x)}u_{0}\penalty\ dx.

Similarly, due to
r+1<p^{*}
, we have

\int_{E}|(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{0}|\penalty\ dx\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{E}(u_{k}^{+})^{rp^{*}{{}^{\prime}}}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}{{}^{\prime}}}}\leq C_{3}|E|^{\alpha}

and

\int_{E}|(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{k}|\penalty\ dx\leq\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{E}(u_{k}^{+})^{rp^{*}{{}^{\prime}}}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p*{{}^{\prime}}}}\leq C_{4}|E|^{\beta}

for some positive constants
C_{3},C_{4},\alpha
and
\beta
. Therefore Vitali convergence theorem gives

(4.11)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{0}\penalty\ dx=\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+})^{r}u_{0}\penalty\ dx,

and

(4.12)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{r}u_{k}\penalty\ dx=\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+})^{r}u_{0}\penalty\ dx.

Using (
4.8
), (
4.9
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) in (
4.7
), we obtain

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(H(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{k})-H(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{0})\big).\nabla(u_{k}-u_{0})\,dx=0.

Then using (
2.16
) from Corollary
2.9
, we obtain
u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
X
as
k\to\infty
which proves our claim.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

From Lemma
4.1
, Proposition
4.2
and Mountain Pass Lemma, for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
, there exists a
\zeta_{\epsilon}\in X
such that
I_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{\prime}(\zeta_{\epsilon})=0
and

I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\zeta_{\epsilon})=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\gamma(t))\geq\rho>0,

where

\Gamma=\big\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X):\gamma(0)=0,\gamma(1)=Te_{1}\big\}.

Using (
4.4
) together with Vitali convergence theorem, if
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
X
, then we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{k}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{0}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\,dx.

Therefore,
I_{\lambda,\epsilon}
is weakly lower semicontinuous. Furthermore, as a consequence of Lemma
4.1
, since for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
we have
\inf\limits_{\|v\|\leq R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)<0
, there exists a nonzero
\nu_{\epsilon}\in X
such that
\|\nu_{\epsilon}\|\leq R
and

(4.13)

\inf\limits_{\|v\|\leq R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)=I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\nu_{\epsilon})<0<\rho\leq I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\zeta_{\epsilon}).

Thus,
\zeta_{\epsilon}
and
\nu_{\epsilon}
are two different non trivial critical points of
I_{\lambda,\epsilon}
, provided
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

There exists a constant
\Theta>0
(independent of
\epsilon
) such that
\|v_{\epsilon}\|\leq\Theta
, where
v_{\epsilon}=\zeta_{\epsilon}
or
\nu_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

The result trivially holds if
v_{\epsilon}=\nu_{\epsilon}
so we deal with the case
v_{\epsilon}=\zeta_{\epsilon}
. Recalling the terms from Lemma
4.1
, we define
A=\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}I_{0,\epsilon}(tTe_{1})
then

A\geq\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(tTe_{1})\geq\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\gamma(t))=I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\zeta_{\epsilon})\geq\rho>0>I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\nu_{\epsilon}).

Therefore

(4.14)

\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla\zeta_{\epsilon})^{p}\,dx-{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx-\frac{1}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}\zeta_{\epsilon}^{r+1}\penalty\ dx\leq A.

Choosing
\phi=-\frac{\zeta_{\epsilon}}{p+1}
as a test function in
(P_{\lambda,\epsilon})
we obtain

(4.15)

-\frac{1}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla\zeta_{\epsilon})^{p}\,dx+\frac{\lambda}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\zeta_{\epsilon}}{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}\,dx+\frac{1}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}\zeta_{\epsilon}^{r+1}\,dx=0.

Adding (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r+1}\right)\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla\zeta_{\epsilon})^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx-\frac{\lambda}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\zeta_{\epsilon}}{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}\,dx+A

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}}{1-q(x)}\penalty\ dx+A

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\zeta_{\epsilon}\|+\|\zeta_{\epsilon}\|^{1-\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}})+A,

for some positive constant
C
being independent of
\epsilon
, where the last inequality is deduced using the estimate (
4.4
), Hölder inequality along Lemma
2.11
. Therefore, using
r+1>p
, the sequence
\{\zeta_{\epsilon}\}
is uniformly bounded in
X
with respect to
\epsilon
. This completes the proof.

Proof of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:

(a)

Uniqueness:

Let
u,v\in X
be two distinct weak solutions of
(
1.1
)
. Then by Lemma
2.13
we can choose
\phi=(u-v)^{+}
as a test function in (
1.10
) to get

\langle-\Delta_{H,p}u+\Delta_{H,p}v,(u-v)^{+}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f\left(\frac{1}{u^{q(x)}}-\frac{1}{v^{q(x)}}\right)(u-v)^{+}\,dx\leq 0.

Using Lemma
2.5
we obtain
u\leq v
in
\Omega
. Similarly, choosing
\phi=(v-u)^{+}
as a test function in (
1.10
) we get
v\leq u
in
\Omega
. Hence the uniqueness follows.

(b)

Existence:
Let
u_{n}\in X
denotes the weak solution of (
3.1
) then choosing
u_{n}
as a test function in (
3.1
) and using Remark
1.1
, we get

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{n})^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)u_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{q(x)}}\,dx.

Now denote by
\omega_{\delta}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{\delta}}
then by Lemma
3.2
, we get
u_{n}\geq c_{\omega_{\delta}}>0
in
\omega_{\delta}
. We observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}(x)u_{n}}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{q(x)}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{{\Omega}_{\delta}}}\frac{f_{n}(x)u_{n}}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{q(x)}}\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\delta}}\frac{f_{n}(x)u_{n}}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{q(x)}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\overline{{\Omega}_{\delta}}}f(x)u_{n}^{1-q(x)}\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\delta}}\frac{f(x)}{u_{n}^{q(x)}}u_{n}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\overline{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\cap\{u_{n}\leq 1\}}f(x)\,dx+\int_{{\overline{{\Omega}_{\delta}}}\cap\{{u_{n}\geq 1\}}}f(x)u_{n}\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\delta}}\frac{f(x)}{c_{\omega_{\delta}}^{q(x)}}u_{n}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\big(1+\|c_{\omega_{\delta}}^{-q(x)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)\int_{\Omega}f(x)u_{n}\,dx.

Let
2\leq p<N
. Then, using H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder’s inequality and Lemma
2.11
we obtain for
f\in L^{m}(\Omega)
with
m=(p^{*})^{\prime}

\|u_{n}\|^{p}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+C\|f\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\|u_{n}\|,

which implies that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
X
. Thus up to a subsequence, by Lemma
2.11
for
1\leq t<p^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup u\text{ weakly in }X,

\displaystyle\,u_{n}

\displaystyle\to u\text{ strongly in }L^{t}(\Omega),\text{ and }

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle\to u\text{ pointwise in }\Omega\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi=\omega
where
\omega\Subset\Omega
. Then by Lemma
3.2
, there exists a constant
c_{\omega}>0
independent of
n
such that
u_{n}\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\omega
. Hence, we get

\frac{f_{n}}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{q(x)}}\phi\leq c_{\omega}^{-q(x)}f(x)\phi(x)\in L^{1}(\Omega).

Thus we can apply Boccardo-Murat
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
to obtain that upto a subsequence still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nabla u_{n}(x)=\nabla u(x)\text{ for }x\text{ in }\Omega.

Hence, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}H(\nabla u_{n}(x))^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H\big(\nabla u_{n}(x)\big)=H(\nabla u(x))^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H\big(\nabla u(x)\big)\text{ for }x\text{ in }\Omega.

Moreover, by Corollary
2.2
we observe that

\|H(\nabla u_{n})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{n})\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\leq\|u_{n}\|^{p}\leq C,

for some positive constant
C
which is independent of
n
.
As a consequence, the sequence

H(\nabla u_{n})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{n})\rightharpoonup H(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u)\text{ weakly in }X\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Indeed, since the weak limit is independent of the subsequence, the above fact holds for every
n
.
Therefore, we have

(5.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u_{n}(x))^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u_{n}(x))\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla u)\nabla\phi\,dx,

for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover by the Lebsegue dominated convergence theorem, we have

(5.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{q(x)}}\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f}{u^{q(x)}}\,\phi\,dx\text{ for all }\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Now, we define by

u:=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}.

Then, due to the monotonicity of
\{u_{n}\}
we get
u\geq c_{\omega}>0
for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
. Hence from (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we get
u
is our required solution and the result follows.

Noting Lemma
2.11
, for
p\geq N
the result follows similarly.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
:

As a resultant of Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.6
, upto a subsequence we get that
\zeta_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup\zeta_{0}
and
\nu_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup\nu_{0}
weakly in
X
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, for some non negative
\zeta_{0},\nu_{0}\in X
. In the sequel, we establish that
\zeta_{0}\neq\nu_{0}
and forms a weak solution to our problem (
1.2
). For convenience, we denote by
v_{0}
either
\zeta_{0}
or
\nu_{0}
. We prove the result in the following steps.

Step
1
.
Here, we prove that
v_{0}\in X
is a weak solution to the problem (
1.2
).

We observe that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
t\geq 0
,

\frac{\lambda}{(t+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}+t^{r}\geq\frac{\lambda}{(t+1)^{q(x)}}+t^{r}\geq\text{min}\left\{1,\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}:=C>0,\text{ say}.

As a consequence we get

-\Delta_{H,p}\,v_{\epsilon}=\frac{\lambda}{(v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}+v_{\epsilon}^{r}\geq C>0.

By Lemma
3.1
, let
\xi\in X
satisfies

-\Delta_{H,p}\xi=C\text{ in }\Omega,\,\xi>0\text{ in }\Omega,

such that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
c_{\omega}>0
satisfying
\xi\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\Omega
.
Then, for every nonnegative
\phi\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H(v_{\epsilon})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(v_{\epsilon})\nabla\phi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{(v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}+v_{\epsilon}^{r}\Big)\phi\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}C\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla\xi)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla\xi)\nabla\phi\,dx.

Now choosing
\phi=(\xi-v_{\epsilon})^{+}
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\Big\{H(\nabla\xi)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla\xi)-H(\nabla v_{\epsilon})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla v_{\epsilon})\Big\}\nabla(\xi-v_{\epsilon})^{+}\,dx\leq 0.

Now applying Lemma
2.5
, we obtain
v_{\epsilon}\geq\xi
in
\Omega
. Hence, we get the existence of a constant
c_{\omega}>0
(independent of
\epsilon
) such that

(5.3)

v_{\epsilon}\geq c_{\omega}>0,\text{ for every }\omega\Subset\Omega.

Therefore using
r+1<p^{*}
along with Lemma
4.6
and the fact (
5.3
) as in the proof of Theorem
1.4
we can apply Boccardo-Murat
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
to obtain

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla v_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla v_{0})\nabla\phi\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi}{v_{0}^{q}(x)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{r}\phi\,dx.

Hence the claim follows.

Step
2
.
Now we are going to prove that
\zeta_{0}\neq\nu_{0}
.

Choosing
\phi=v_{\epsilon}\in X
as a test function in
(P_{\lambda,\epsilon})
we get

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla v_{\epsilon})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla v_{\epsilon})\nabla\phi\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{\epsilon}}{(v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}v_{\epsilon}^{r+1}\,dx.

Since
r+1<p^{*}
, using Lemma
2.11
we obtain

\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}(v_{\epsilon})^{r+1}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{r+1}\,dx.

Moreover, since

0\leq\frac{v_{\epsilon}}{(v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}\leq v_{\epsilon}^{1-q(x)},

using (
4.4
) together with Vitali convergence theorem, we get

\lambda\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{\epsilon}}{(v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{q(x)}}\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{1-q(x)}\,dx.

Therefore for every
\phi\in X
, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla v_{\epsilon})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}H(\nabla v_{\epsilon})\nabla\phi\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{1-q(x)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{r+1}\,dx.

Using Lemma
2.13
we can choose
\phi=v_{0}
as a test function in (
1.11
) to deduce that

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla v_{0})^{p}\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{1-q(x)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{r+1}\,dx.

Hence we obtain

\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla v_{\epsilon})^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla v_{0})^{p}\,dx.

By the Vitali convergence theorem, we get

\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}[(v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{1-q(x)}-\epsilon^{1-q(x)}]\,dx=\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{1-q(x)}\,dx,

which together with the strong convergence of
v_{\epsilon}
in
X
implies

\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v_{\epsilon})=I_{\lambda}(v_{0}).

Hence from (
4.13
) we get
\zeta_{0}\neq\nu_{0}.

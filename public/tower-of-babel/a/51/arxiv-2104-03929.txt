Abstract
. Celebrated theorems of Roth and of Matoušek and Spencer together show that the discrepancy of arithmetic progressions in the first n n positive integers is Θ ⁡ ( n 1 / 4 ) \Theta(n^{1/4}) . We study the analogous problem in the ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} setting. We asymptotically determine the logarithm of the discrepancy of arithmetic progressions in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} for all positive integer n n . We further determine up to a constant factor the discrepancy of arithmetic progressions in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} for many n n . For example, if n = p k n=p^{k} is a prime power, then the discrepancy of arithmetic progressions in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} is Θ ⁡ ( n 1 / 3 + r k / ( 6 ​ k ) ) \Theta(n^{1/3+r_{k}/(6k)}) , where r k ∈ { 0 , 1 , 2 } r_{k}\in\{0,1,2\} is the remainder when k k is divided by 3 3 . This solves a problem of Hebbinghaus and Srivastav.



1.
Introduction

Given a finite set
\Omega
, a (two-)coloring of
\Omega
is a map
\chi:\Omega\to\{1,-1\}
, and a partial coloring is a map
\chi:\Omega\to\{-1,0,1\}
. For
A\subseteq\Omega
, let
\chi(A)=\sum_{x\in A}\chi(x).
For a family
\mathcal{A}
of subsets of
\Omega
, the discrepancy of
\mathcal{A}
is defined to be

\disc(\mathcal{A}):=\min_{\chi}\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}}|\chi(A)|,

where the minimum is over all colorings of
\Omega
. The discrepancy measures the guaranteed irregularity of colorings with respect to a set system. Discrepancy theory is a rich area of study, see the books
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
9
]
.

In particular, the study of discrepancy of arithmetic progressions has a long history, including notable results of Weyl from 1916
[
13
]
and Roth from 1964
[
11
]
. Let
[n]:=\{1,2,3,\dots,n\}
and
\mathcal{A}
be the set of arithmetic progressions in
[n]
. Using Fourier analysis,
Roth
[
11
]
proved that there is an absolute constant
c>0
such that

\disc(\mathcal{A})\geq cn^{\frac{1}{4}}.

The exponent
1/4
was unexpected, as random colorings suggest that the best exponent might be
1/2
. Later, improving on a result of Montgomery and Sárközy (see Problem 10 in
[
5
]
), Beck
[
2
]
proved that Roth’s lower bound is sharp up to a polylogarithmic factor. It was a big challenge to remove the polylogarithmic factor and show that Roth’s bound is sharp up to a constant factor, and it was finally done by Matoušek and Spencer
[
10
]
via entropy and partial coloring methods.

The modular variant is also a very natural problem to study. For a positive integer
n
, an arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a set of the form
\{a+kd:0\leq k<l\}
for any
a,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
l\geq 0
. To avoid repeated elements in the set, we may assume that
l\leq\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be the set of all arithmetic progressions in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. The quantity we are interested in is

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n}):=\min_{\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}}\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A)|.

In the case where
n=p
is a prime, the following lower and upper bounds are proved by Hebbinghaus and Srivastav
[
7
]
and Alon and Spencer (Theorem 13.1.1 in
[
1
]
) respectively. There exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

c_{1}\sqrt{p}\leq\disc(\mathcal{A}_{p})\leq c_{2}\sqrt{p\log p}.

Hebbinghaus and Srivastav
[
7
]
wrote that it seemed to be a difficult open problem to close the
O(\sqrt{\log p})
multiplicative gap between the upper and lower bounds. As part of our results, we remove the
\sqrt{\log p}
factor in the upper bound and resolve the problem of determining
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{p})
up to a constant factor.

The problem for general
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is more challenging and interesting. Note that any arithmetic progression in
[n]
is also an arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, so Roth’s lower bound from the integer case also applies. As the number of sets in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is polynomial in
n
, considering a random coloring gives the following upper bound (see Theorem 13.1.1 in
[
1
]
). So there are
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}n^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq c_{2}n^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Our main theorem asymptotically determines the logarithm of
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
. It shows that
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
depends heavily on the
arithmetic structure of
n
, and neither of the above bounds are sharp.

Let
\omega(n)
be the number of distinct prime factors of
n
and
d(n)
be the number of divisors of
n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for any positive integer
n
,

\frac{1}{8\sqrt{d(n)}}\cdot\min_{r|n}\left(\frac{n}{r}+\sqrt{r}\right)\leq\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq\min_{r|n}\left(\frac{n}{r}+c\sqrt{r}\cdot 2^{\omega(r)}\right).

Note that we have
2^{\omega(r)}\leq 2^{\omega(n)}\leq d(n)=n^{o(1)}
(see
[
6
]
).
This implies that

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})=n^{\frac{1}{3}+x+o(1)},

where
x\geq 0
is the largest real number such that there is no factor of
n
in the range
(n^{\frac{2}{3}-x},n^{\frac{2}{3}+2x})
.
Thus, our results determine the correct exponent of
n
for
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
up to
o(1)
.

Notice that when
n
has a bounded number of factors, Theorem
1.1
determines
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
up to a constant factor. In particular, when
n
is prime, we have
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})=\Theta(n^{1/2})
, and this solves the problem of Hebbinghaus and Srivastav
[
7
]
discussed earlier.

We actually prove slightly stronger bounds, but we choose the formulation in Theorem
1.1
for simplicity. These stronger bounds give the following sharp bound for
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
when
n
is a prime power.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
n=p^{k}
for prime
p
and positive integer
k
, then

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})=\Theta\left(p^{\frac{k-\lfloor k/3\rfloor}{2}}\right)=\Theta\left(n^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{r_{k}}{6k}}\right),

where
r_{k}\in\{0,1,2\}
is the remainder when
k
is divided by
3
.

Remark 1.3
.

It is also natural to study the discrepancy problem in the case where the coloring function
\chi
is allowed to take any value in the unit circle in the complex plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
.
For example, this choice of coloring functions is studied in Tao’s
[
12
]
remarkable solution to the Erdős discrepancy problem. By comparing the definitions of the coloring functions, we know that the discrepancy under the more general choice of
\chi
is at most as large as the discrepancy under the choice
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
. We remark that our proof is robust enough to give the same lower bound on discrepancy when
\chi
is under the above choice. See Remark
4.8
for details on how to extend the proof of the lower bound to the more general setting.

Notations
.

Throughout the paper, all logarithms are base
e
unless otherwise specified.

We use symbols
c,c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
, etc. to denote absolute constants. To avoid using too many symbols in different parts of the paper, we reuse these symbols to denote different constants. We make no attempt to optimize constant factors in our results.

We treat elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
as if they are in
\mathbb{Z}
in the following ways. For an element
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and integers
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, we say
a\leq r\leq b
if there exists an element
r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
a\leq r^{\prime}\leq b
and
r^{\prime}\equiv r\;(\mathrm{mod}\;n)
. The notation is similar if either
\leq
is replaced with
<
. We typically use it when
0\leq a\leq b<n
, so this notation should not cause any confusion. For any
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we may also define
\operatorname{gcd}(a,n)=\operatorname{gcd}(a^{\prime},n)
for any
a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a^{\prime}\equiv a\;(\mathrm{mod}\;n)
. For two nonnegative integers
a
and
b
, we write
a|b
if
a
divides
b
. For any factor
r
of
n
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we say that
r|a
if
r|\operatorname{gcd}(a,n)
.

2.
The First Step towards the Upper Bounds

We use the following version of a lemma of Matoušek and Spencer
[
10
]
to show there is a partial coloring that colors a constant fraction of the
elements of a set system with low discrepancy. The proof of this lemma uses the entropy method.

Lemma 2.1

(Section 4.6 in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
(V,C)
be a set system on
n
elements, and let a number
\Delta_{S}\geq 2\sqrt{|S|}
be given for each set
S\in C
. If

(2.1)

\sum_{S\in C:S\neq\emptyset}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta_{S}^{2}}{4|S|}\right)\leq\frac{n}{50},

then there is a partial coloring
\chi
that assigns
\pm 1
to at least
n/10
variables (and
0
to the rest) satisfying
|\chi(S)|\leq\Delta_{S}
for each
S\in C
.

The following lemma shows that there is a partial coloring of a constant fraction of any subset
X
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
such that modular arithmetic progressions have low discrepancy. The proof utilizes the previous lemma applied to a special family of intersections of
X
with modular arithmetic progressions. Each set in this special family has size a power of
2
. We show that any set which is an intersection of
X
with a modular arithmetic progression can be written as the union of two sets, and each of them is a set difference of two sets that each has a canonical decomposition into sets of different sizes from this special family. We obtain the lemma by putting these together and using the triangle inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a set of size
m>0
. There exists a partial coloring
\chi:X\to\{-1,0,1\}
that assigns
\pm 1
to at least
m/10
elements in
X
such that

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq 200m^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\AP_{n}(a,d,i,j):=\{a+kd:i\leq k\leq j\}
where
a,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. By definition,

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\left\{\AP_{n}(a,d,0,l-1):a,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{n},0\leq l\leq\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}\right\}.

Consider the following decomposition of sets in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
. Let

C_{1}=\left\{\AP_{n}(a,d,i,j):1\leq d<n,0\leq a<\operatorname{gcd}(n,d),0\leq i\leq j<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}\right\}.

Now we show that any set
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
can be written as the disjoint union of at most two sets in
C_{1}
. For any set
A=\AP_{n}(a,d,0,l-1)
with
a,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
0\leq l\leq\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
, we may assume that
l>0
, or otherwise it is an empty set. If
d=0
, then the set is the singleton
\{a\}
, which is also
\AP_{n}(0,1,a,a)\in C_{1}
by choosing
i,j
to be the integer representative of
a
in the range
[0,n)
. Now we may assume
1\leq d<n
. Note that
\{kd:0\leq k<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}\}
splits
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
into
\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
equal-sized intervals, so there is some
0\leq k<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
such that
0\leq a-kd<\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)
. Define
a^{\prime}=a-kd
, and we have

\AP_{n}(a,d,0,l-1)=\AP_{n}(a^{\prime},d,k,k+l-1).

Moreover we have
k<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
and
l-1<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
, so
0\leq k+l-1<2\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
. If
k+l-1<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
, then
\AP_{n}(a^{\prime},d,k,k+l-1)\in C_{1}
. If
k+l-1\geq\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}
, then we may write

\AP_{n}(a^{\prime},d,k,k+l-1)=\AP_{n}\left(a^{\prime},d,k,\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}-1\right)\cup\AP_{n}\left(a^{\prime},d,0,k+l-1-\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}\right).

Hence we know that for any partial coloring
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{-1,0,1\}
, we always have

(2.2)

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq 2\max_{A\in C_{1}}|\chi(A\cap X)|.

Now we study the sets in
C_{X}:=\{A\cap X:A\in C_{1}\}
. By definition we have

(2.3)

\max_{A\in C_{1}}|\chi(A\cap X)|=\max_{B\in C_{X}}|\chi(B)|.

For each
1\leq d<n
and
0\leq a<\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)
, we define
A_{d,a}=\left\{a+kd:0\leq k<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)}\right\}
, and define
X_{d,a}=A_{d,a}\cap X
. Since each element
x
in
X_{d,a}
is associated to an integer
0\leq k<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)}
via the relation
x=a+kd
, we may order elements in
X_{d,a}
in ascending order of
k
. We write
X_{d,a}=\left\{x_{d,a}^{(1)},\dots,x_{d,a}^{(l_{d,a})}\right\}
, where
l_{d,a}=|X_{d,a}|
. For integers
i
and
j
, we define
X_{d,a}(i,j)=\left\{x_{d,a}^{(t)}:i\leq t\leq j\right\}
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, because each element of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is in exactly one
A_{d,a}
for
0\leq a<\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)
, we have

(2.4)

\sum_{a=0}^{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)-1}l_{d,a}=\sum_{a=0}^{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)-1}|X\cap A_{d,a}|=|X|=m.

For
A=\AP_{n}(a,d,i,j)
, if we take
i^{\prime}
to be the smallest index such that
x_{d,a}^{(i^{\prime})}=a+k_{1}d
with
k_{1}\geq i
(or
l_{d,a}+1
if no such index with
0\leq k_{1}<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)}
exists), and
j^{\prime}
to be the largest index such that
x_{d,a}^{(j^{\prime})}=a+k_{2}d
with
k_{2}\leq j
(or
0
if no such index with
0\leq k_{2}<\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)}
exists), then we have
A\cap X=X_{d,a}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
. Hence each nonempty set in
C_{X}
is of the form
X_{d,a}(i,j)
with
1\leq i\leq j\leq l_{d,a}
. Therefore we may write

C_{X}=\{X_{d,a}(i,j):1\leq d<n,0\leq a<\operatorname{gcd}(n,d),1\leq i\leq j\leq l_{d,a}\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

Now we further decompose sets in
C_{X}
into canonical sets. Formally,

(2.5)

C_{2}:=\left\{X_{d,a}(1+(t-1)2^{i},t2^{i}):1\leq d<n,0\leq a<\operatorname{gcd}(n,d),0\leq i,1\leq t\leq\left\lfloor\frac{l_{d,a}}{2^{i}}\right\rfloor\right\}.

Notice that each set
S\in C_{X}
can be written as
U\setminus V
with
V\subseteq U
where both
U
and
V
can be written as the disjoint union of sets in
C_{2}
of different sizes. This is trivially true for the empty set. For
S=X_{d,a}(i,j)
, we may take
U=X_{d,a}(1,j)
and
V=X_{d,a}(1,i-1)
. If we write
j
in binary form
2^{b_{1}}+2^{b_{2}}+\dots+2^{b_{s}}
for
b_{1}>b_{2}>\dots>b_{s}
, then
U
is the disjoint union of sets of the form
X_{d,a}(1+\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}2^{b_{k}},\sum_{k=1}^{t}2^{b_{k}})
for
t=1,\dots,s
. We can decompose
V
similarly.

Note that sets in
C_{2}
are all of size
2^{i}
for
2^{i}\leq m
. If
\chi:X\to\{-1,0,1\}
is such that for all
0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m
we have
\chi(S)\leq\Delta_{i}
for any set
S\in C_{2}
of size
2^{i}
, then by using the decomposition property above we have

(2.6)

\max_{B\in C_{X}}|\chi(B)|\leq 2\sum_{0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m}\Delta_{i}.

For each
i
, we upper bound the number of sets in
C_{2}
of size
2^{i}
as following. For each
1\leq d<n
and
0\leq a<\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)
, the number of choices of
t
in (
2.5
) is
\lfloor{l_{d,a}}/{2^{i}}\rfloor
. By (
2.4
) the number of such sets is at most

(2.7)

\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}\sum_{a=0}^{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)-1}\left\lfloor\frac{l_{d,a}}{2^{i}}\right\rfloor\leq\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}\sum_{a=0}^{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)-1}\frac{l_{d,a}}{2^{i}}=\frac{(n-1)m}{2^{i}}.

Now we define
b(0)=0
and for
0<s\leq n
,

(2.8)

b(s)=5\sqrt{s}\left(\log\frac{en}{s}\right)^{1/2}.

From the definition, we know that
b(s)\geq 2\sqrt{s}
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
. We want to show that there exists a partial coloring
\chi
that colors at least
m/10
elements in
X
, and for any
S\in C_{2}
,

(2.9)

|\chi(S)|\leq b(|S|).

In order to apply Lemma
2.1
to
X
and
C=C_{2}
, it remains to check that

(2.10)

\sum_{S\in C_{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{b(|S|)^{2}}{4|S|}\right)\leq m/50.

From the definition of
b(\cdot)
in (
2.8
), we know that

(2.11)

\sum_{S\in C_{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{b(|S|)^{2}}{4|S|}\right)=\sum_{S\in C_{2}}e^{-\frac{25}{4}}\cdot\left(\frac{|S|}{n}\right)^{\frac{25}{4}}\leq e^{-6}\cdot\sum_{S\in C_{2}}\left(\frac{|S|}{n}\right)^{2}.

Using the bound in (
2.7
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{S\in C_{2}}\left(\frac{|S|}{n}\right)^{2}&\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m}\frac{(n-1)m}{2^{i}}\left(\frac{2^{i}}{n}\right)^{2}\leq 2m.\end{split}

Put this into (
2.11
). We get

\sum_{S\in C_{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{b(|S|)^{2}}{4|S|}\right)\leq e^{-6}\cdot 2m\leq m/50.

This shows that (
2.10
) holds. By Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that there exists a partial coloring
\chi:X\to\{-1,0,1\}
that assigns
\pm 1
to at least
m/10
elements, and (
2.9
) holds for any
S\in C_{2}
. Thus in (
2.6
) we may take
\Delta_{i}=b(2^{i})
for
0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m
.

It remains to show that
\chi
satisfies the desired property. By (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.6
),

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq 4\sum_{0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m}b(2^{i})=20\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor{\log_{2}m}\rfloor}2^{i/2}\left(\log\frac{en}{2^{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 200\cdot\sqrt{m}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To obtain the last inequality, observe that the sum is roughly a geometric series with ratio
2^{1/2}
(although there is an extra logarithmic factor that slightly complicates this) and can be bounded by a constant factor times the largest term. A careful analysis gives the claimed bound.

Hence the partial coloring
\chi
satisfies the desired inequality.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a set of size
m>0
. There exists an absolute constant
c
such that there is a coloring
\chi:X\to\{-1,1\}
satisfying

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq cm^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular, for
X=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we have

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})=O(\sqrt{n}).

Proof.

The main idea is to iteratively apply Lemma
2.2
to the set of uncolored elements of
X
until all elements of
X
are colored.

Start with
X_{0}=X
. For each
i\geq 0
, we apply Lemma
2.2
to
X_{i}
to get a partial coloring
\chi_{i}:X_{i}\to\{-1,0,1\}
, and we let
X_{i+1}=\chi_{i}^{-1}(0)\subseteq X_{i}
to be the set of uncolored elements in
i
-th iteration. We continue this process until the
k
-th iteration where all elements are colored (i.e.
X_{k+1}=\emptyset
). Let
\chi
be the final coloring given by
\chi(x_{i})=\chi_{i}(x_{i})
if
x_{i}\in X_{i}\setminus X_{i+1}
for
0\leq i\leq k
. We know that
|X_{i}|\leq 0.9|X_{i-1}|
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, so
|X_{i}|\leq(0.9)^{i}|X_{0}|=(0.9)^{i}m
. Moreover, we know that for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, noting that
x^{1/2}\cdot\left(\log\frac{en}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is monotonically increasing for real number
x\in(0,n]
,

\begin{split}|\chi(A\cap X)|&=\left|\sum_{i=0}^{k}\chi_{i}(X_{i}\cap A)\right|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left|\chi_{i}(X_{i}\cap A)\right|<\sum_{i=0}^{k}200\cdot|X_{i}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{n}{e|X_{i}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq 200m^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k}(0.9)^{\frac{i}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{(0.9)^{i}m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<cm^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

for some appropriate choice of the absolute constant
c
.
∎

3.
Upper Bounds

By Corollary
2.3
, we have
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})=O(\sqrt{n})
. In this section, we prove the following upper bound on the same quantity, which shows that the previous bound is not always tight, and that
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
depends on the factorization of
n
. Recall that
\omega(r)
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of
r
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for any positive integer
n
, we have

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq\min_{r|n}\left(\frac{n}{r}+c\sqrt{r}\cdot 2^{\omega(r)}\right).

The proof of Theorem
3.1
consists of two steps. The first key step is to establish an upper bound on
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
using a coloring of
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
where
r
divides
n
that simultaneously has low discrepancy with respect to two particular families of arithmetic progressions in
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
. This is achieved by Lemma
3.2
.
To accomplish this, we need to introduce a special type of arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is the set of all arithmetic progressions in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We define
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
to be the set of all congruence classes of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Formally, for any
r,i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, let

C(r,i):=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:x\equiv i\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)\}

and

\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}:=\{C(r,i):r,i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\}.

In particular we know that
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{n}
. In Lemma
3.2
we obtain an upper bound on
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
if there is an
r
that divides
n
and a coloring
\chi
of
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
that has low discrepancy over both
\mathcal{A}_{r}
and
\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}
.

The second step is to find a coloring of
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
which has nearly optimal discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{r}
and
\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}
simultaneously. Finally we will complete the proof by applying Lemma
3.2
with this coloring of
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
r
be a positive factor of
n
. For any
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{r}\to\{1,-1\}
,

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{r}}|\chi(A)|+\frac{n}{r}\cdot\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|.

Proof.

Let
m=n/r
. For the subgroup
(m)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we have a quotient map
\tau:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{n}/(m)=\mathbb{Z}_{r}
given by
\tau(x)=\overline{x}=x+(m)
. Suppose we are given an arbitrary
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{r}\to\{1,-1\}
. Now we define
\chi^{\prime}=\chi\circ\tau:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
. It suffices to show

(3.1)

\max_{A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi^{\prime}(A^{\prime})|\leq\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{r}}|\chi(A)|+m\cdot\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|.

Let
A^{\prime}=\{a+kd:0\leq k<L\}
for some
a,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be an arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of length
L
. We may assume that
L\leq n/{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)}
so that there is no repeated element in
A^{\prime}
.

Let
L_{0}=r/\operatorname{gcd}(r,\overline{d})
where
\overline{d}=\tau(d)
. For any integer
t>0
, we know that
\{\overline{a}+k\overline{d}:t\leq k<t+L_{0}\}
is a set of size
L_{0}
in
\mathcal{A}^{0}_{n}
, as it covers each element in the congruence class exactly once. For any
0\leq l<L_{0}
, as the set
\{\overline{a}+k\overline{d}:t\leq k<t+l\}
has no repeated element, it is a set of size
l
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Now we may write
L=qL_{0}+r
for some integers
q
and
0\leq r<L_{0}
. We know that

q\leq\frac{L}{L_{0}}\leq\frac{n/\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)}{r/\operatorname{gcd}(r,\overline{d})}=m\cdot\frac{\operatorname{gcd}(r,d)}{\operatorname{gcd}(n,d)}\leq m.

Applying the triangle inequality, we get

\begin{split}|\chi^{\prime}(A^{\prime})|&=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\chi^{\prime}(a+kd)\right|=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\chi(\overline{a}+k\overline{d})\right|=\left|\sum_{t=0}^{q-1}\sum_{k=tL_{0}}^{(t+1)L_{0}-1}\chi(\overline{a}+k\overline{d})+\sum_{k=qL_{0}}^{qL_{0}+r-1}\chi(\overline{a}+k\overline{d})\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{t=0}^{q-1}\left|\sum_{k=tL_{0}}^{(t+1)L_{0}-1}\chi(\overline{a}+k\overline{d})\right|+\left|\sum_{k=qL_{0}}^{qL_{0}+r-1}\chi(\overline{a}+k\overline{d})\right|\\
&\leq q\cdot\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|+\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{r}}|\chi(A)|\leq m\cdot\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|+\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{r}}|\chi(A)|.\end{split}

Since the bound is uniform for all
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, we conclude that (
3.1
) holds.
∎

After the above deduction, the second step is to find a coloring
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
that has nearly optimal discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
simultaneously. Formally we have the following statement.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that the following holds. Suppose that
n
is a positive integer with
k
distinct prime factors. Then there exists
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
such that

\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|\leq 1\quad\and\quad\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A)|\leq c\sqrt{n}\cdot 2^{k}.

We can now deduce Theorem
3.1
from the above two steps.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
assuming Lemma
3.3
.

For any positive factor
r
of
n
, by Lemma
3.3
, there is a coloring
\chi
of
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
such that

\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|\leq 1\quad\and\quad\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{r}}|\chi(A)|\leq c\sqrt{r}\cdot 2^{\omega(r)},

where
c
is an absolute constant. Then applying Lemma
3.2
, we conclude that

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq\frac{n}{r}+c\sqrt{r}\cdot 2^{\omega(r)}.

As this holds for all positive factors
r
of
n
, the desired result follows.
∎

It remains to prove Lemma
3.3
.
We first present a proof of the case that
n
is a prime power. The proof of this case is simpler and illustrates the main ideas in the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists absolute constant
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
n=p^{\alpha}
for some prime
p
and positive integer
\alpha
. Then there exists
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
such that

\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|\leq 1\quad\and\quad\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A)|\leq c\sqrt{n}.

To find a coloring with low discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
,
the main idea is to color simultaneously two disjoint subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
that are translations of each other, and we hope that
\chi(S_{1}\cap A_{0})
and
\chi(S_{2}\cap A_{0})
cancel out for many
A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
. The
c\sqrt{n}
upper bound on the discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
in Lemma
3.4
is achieved via Corollary
2.3
. We first derive the following lemma that gives a desired coloring for a subset
X
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, provided that
X
is an initial interval of some special length.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists absolute constant
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
n=p^{\alpha}
for some prime
p
and positive integer
\alpha
. Suppose that
X=\{0,\dots,m-1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
for some
m\leq n
satisfies that
m=sp^{\beta}
with
s
even and
\beta
being a nonnegative integer. Then there exists
\chi:X\to\{1,-1\}
such that

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq c\sqrt{m}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
r=p^{\gamma}
with
0\leq\gamma\leq\beta
,

\chi(C(r,w)\cap X)=0.

Proof.

Let
S=\{0,\dots,m/2-1\}
. By Corollary
2.3
, there is
\chi_{0}:X\to\{1,-1\}
such that

|\chi_{0}(A\cap S)|\leq c_{0}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\log\frac{2en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

holds for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, where
c_{0}
is an absolute constant. We know that
X
is the disjoint union of
S
and
S+\frac{m}{2}
, i.e. each element
x\in X
is of the form
x=s+v\frac{m}{2}
for some
s\in S
and
v\in\{0,1\}
. We define
\chi:X\to\{1,-1\}
given by
\chi(s+v\frac{m}{2})=(-1)^{v}\cdot\chi_{0}(s)
for any
s\in S
and
v\in\{0,1\}
.

For any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, noting that
A-\frac{m}{2}
is also an arithmetic progression,

\begin{split}|\chi(A\cap X)|&=|\chi(A\cap S)+\chi(A\cap(S+m/2))|=|\chi_{0}(A\cap S)+(-1)\cdot\chi_{0}((A-m/2)\cap S)|\\
&\leq|\chi_{0}(A\cap S)|+|\chi_{0}((A-m/2)\cap S)|\leq 2\cdot c_{0}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\log\frac{2en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\sqrt{m}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where
c
is an absolute constant. Next we verify that for any
r=p^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\leq\beta
and any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,
\chi(C(r,w)\cap X)=0
. Note that
m/2
is a multiple of
r
, so
C(r,w)-m/2=C(r,w)
. Hence we have

\chi(C(r,w)\cap X)=\chi_{0}(C(r,w)\cap S)+(-1)\cdot\chi_{0}((C(r,w)-m/2)\cap S)=\chi_{0}(C(r,w)\cap S)-\chi_{0}(C(r,w)\cap S)=0.

We have thus shown the existence of such a coloring
\chi
with the desired properties.
∎

In the case
n=p^{\alpha}
, we partition
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
into a few sets, each being an interval of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of length an even number times a power of
p
as in Lemma
3.5
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Note that for any
C(r,w)\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
, we have
C(r,w)=C(\operatorname{gcd}(r,n),w)
. Hence it suffices to check
|\chi(C(r,w))|\leq 1
where
r
divides
n=p^{\alpha}
, i.e.,
r=p^{\gamma}
for some nonnegative
\gamma\leq\alpha
.

Let
c_{0}
be the constant in Lemma
3.5
.

If
p=2
, we can take
m=n=2\cdot 2^{\alpha-1}
in Lemma
3.5
. Thus, there exists
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
such that

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A)|\leq c_{0}\sqrt{n},

and that for any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and any
r=2^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\leq\alpha-1
,
\chi(C(r,w))=0
. This means that, for any
r=2^{\gamma}
, if
\gamma\leq\alpha-1
, then
\chi(C(r,w))=0
; if
\gamma=\alpha
, then
C(r,w)
contains a single element
w
, so
|\chi(C(r,w))|=1
in this case. We conclude that

\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|\leq 1.

If
p>2
is an odd prime, then we partition
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
into a few intervals:
X_{0}=\{0\},X_{i}=\{x:p^{i-1}\leq x<p^{i}\}
for
1\leq i\leq\alpha
. Note that for each
i\geq 1
,
X_{i}
is an interval of length
(p-1)\cdot p^{i-1}
. We would like to apply Lemma
3.5
to each
X_{i}
for
i\geq 1
. It is applicable because the property is maintained by translation. Therefore for each
1\leq i\leq\alpha
there is a coloring
\chi_{i}:X_{i}\to\{1,-1\}
such that

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi_{i}(A\cap X_{i})|\leq c_{0}\sqrt{(p-1)\cdot p^{i-1}}\left(1+\log\frac{p^{\alpha}}{(p-1)\cdot p^{i-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for each
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
r=p^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\leq i-1
, we have
\chi_{i}(C(r,w)\cap X_{i})=0.

For
X_{0}
we may take any arbitrary
\chi_{0}:X_{0}\to\{1,-1\}
. Note that
X_{0}=\{0\}
contains only one element, so
|\chi_{0}(A\cap X_{0})|\leq 1
for all
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

Since
X=\coprod_{i=0}^{\alpha}X_{i}
, we define
\chi:X\to\{1,-1\}
such that
\chi(x_{i})=\chi_{i}(x_{i})
for all
0\leq i\leq\alpha
and
x_{i}\in X_{i}
.
Consequently, we know that for each
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
,

\begin{split}|\chi(A)|=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha}|\chi_{i}(A\cap X_{i})|\leq 1+\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}c_{0}\sqrt{(p-1)\cdot p^{i-1}}\left(1+\log\frac{p^{\alpha}}{(p-1)\cdot p^{i-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\sqrt{n}\end{split}

for some appropriate constant
c
. Moreover, for each
C(r,w)\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
, we may assume that
r=p^{\gamma}
for some
0\leq\gamma\leq\alpha
and it gives that

\chi(C(r,w))=\chi\left(C(r,w)\cap\left(\bigcup_{i=0}^{\gamma}X_{i}\right)\right)+\sum_{i=\gamma+1}^{\alpha}\chi_{i}(C(r,w)\cap X_{i}).

Now, for each
i\geq\gamma+1
, i.e.
\gamma\leq i-1
, we have
\chi_{i}(C(r,w)\cap X_{i})=0
. Also note that
\bigcup_{i=0}^{\gamma}X_{i}
is
\{x:0\leq x<p^{\gamma}\}
, so there is exactly one element of
C(r,w)
in it. Hence

|\chi(C(r,w))|=\left|\chi\left(C(r,w)\cap\left(\bigcup_{i=0}^{\gamma}X_{i}\right)\right)\right|=1.

Thus we may conclude that

\max_{A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}|\chi(A_{0})|\leq 1.

Hence for both
p=2
and
p
is odd, we can always find such a coloring
\chi
.
∎

Finally we extend the argument above to arbitrary
n
using Chinese remainder theorem.

Notations
.

Suppose that number
n
has prime factorization
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
. By Chinese remainder theorem, we have an isomorphism

\psi_{n}:\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

Again, we first show the following analogue of Lemma
3.5
. This shows that if
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
has some special structure, then we have a coloring of
X
with low discrepancy with respect to both
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
n
be a positive integer with prime factorization
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
. For each
1\leq i\leq k
, let
T_{i}\leq p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
be a positive integer. Let
X=\{\psi_{n}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k}):0\leq t_{i}<T_{i}\mathrm{\;for\;all\;}1\leq i\leq k\}
. Then
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a set of
m=\prod_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
elements. Suppose that
I\subseteq[k]
is a subset of indices and
(\beta_{i})_{i\in I}
is a sequence of nonnegative integers, such that for each
i\in I
,
T_{i}=s_{i}p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}
for some even number
s_{i}
. Then there exists a coloring
\chi:X\to\{-1,1\}
such that the following holds. Its discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
satisfies that

(3.2)

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq c2^{\frac{|I|}{2}}\cdot\sqrt{m}\left(|I|+1+\log\frac{n}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and any
i\in I
,

(3.3)

\chi(C(n/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}},w)\cap X)=0.

Proof.

Let
L=|I|
. Let
S_{i}=T_{i}/2
if
i\in I
, and
S_{i}=T_{i}
otherwise. For each
i\in I
, from the condition that
T_{i}/p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}
is even, we know that
S_{i}
is a multiple of
p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}
. We define

X_{0}=\{\psi_{n}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k}):0\leq t_{i}<S_{i}\mathrm{\;for\;all\;}1\leq i\leq k\}.

We know that
X_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a set of size
m/2^{L}
. Next we apply Corollary
2.3
to get the constant
c_{0}>0
and a partial coloring of
\chi_{0}:X_{0}\to\{-1,1\}
such that

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi_{0}(X_{0}\cap A)|\leq c_{0}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2^{L}}}\left(1+\log\frac{2^{L}n}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\sqrt{\frac{m}{2^{L}}}\left(L+1+\log\frac{n}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
c
is another absolute constant.

For each
v=(v_{i})_{i\in I}\in\{0,1\}^{I}
(binary tuples of length
L
indexed by
I
), we define
\sgn(v)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}}
, and let
u_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be the unique element, by the Chinese remainder theorem, such that

u_{v}\equiv v_{i}S_{i}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}})\mathrm{\;for\;all\;}i\in I\quad\and\qquad u_{v}\equiv 0\;(\mathrm{mod}\;p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})\mathrm{\;for\;all\;}j\notin I.

Thus we have the decomposition of
X
into disjoint copies of translations of
X_{0}
:

X=\coprod_{v\in\{0,1\}^{I}}X_{v},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{where}\quad X_{v}:=u_{v}+X_{0}.

This implies that each
x\in X
can be written in the form
x=u_{v}+x_{0}
for some
v\in\{0,1\}^{I}
and
x_{0}\in X_{0}
. Now we define
\chi:X\to\{1,-1\}
by
\chi(u_{v}+x_{0})=\sgn(v)\cdot\chi(x_{0})
for all
v\in\{0,1\}^{I},x_{0}\in X_{0}
.
For any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, noting that
A-u_{v}
is also in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, so

\begin{split}|\chi(X\cap A)|&=\left|\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}^{I}}\chi(X_{v}\cap A)\right|=\left|\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}^{I}}\sgn(v)\cdot\chi_{0}(X_{v}\cap A-u_{v})\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}^{I}}\left|\chi_{0}(X_{0}\cap(A-u_{v}))\right|\leq 2^{L}\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi_{0}(X_{0}\cap A)|\\
&\leq 2^{L}\cdot c\sqrt{\frac{m}{2^{L}}}\left(L+1+\log\frac{n}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=c2^{\frac{L}{2}}\cdot\sqrt{m}\left(L+1+\log\frac{n}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

This shows that the coloring
\chi
satisfies the condition (
3.2
) on the discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

It remains to show (
3.3
). For each
i\in I
, let
r_{i}=n/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}}=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
.
For any
i\in I
and any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we know that
C(r_{i},w)
contains
p^{\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}}
elements. If
C(r_{i},w)\cap X=\emptyset
, then (
3.3
) trivially holds. It suffices to check
\chi(C(r_{i},w)\cap X)=0
for
w=\psi_{n}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})\in X_{v}
for some
v\in\{0,1\}^{I}
. Since
p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
divides
r_{i}
, each element in
C(r_{i},w)
is congruent to
t_{j}
mod
p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
for
j\neq i
. This implies that
C(r_{i},w)
only has nonempty intersection with
X_{v}
and
X_{v^{\prime}}
where
v,v^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{I}
differ only at the entry indexed by
i
. Moreover by the definition of
u
,
u_{v}-u_{v^{\prime}}
is congruent to
0
mod
p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
for all
j\neq i
, and is congruent to
\pm\frac{s_{i}}{2}p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}
mod
p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
. Thus
u_{v}-u_{v^{\prime}}
is a multiple of
r_{i}
, which implies
C(r_{i},w)=C(r_{i},w)+(u_{v}-u_{v^{\prime}})
, or equivalently
C(r_{i},w)-u_{v}=C(r_{i},w)-u_{v^{\prime}}
. Therefore we have

\begin{split}\chi(C(r_{i},w)\cap X)&=\chi(C(r_{i},w)\cap X_{v})+\chi(C(r_{i},w)\cap X_{v^{\prime}})\\
&=\sgn(v)\cdot\chi_{0}((C(r_{i},w)-u_{v})\cap X_{0})+\sgn(v^{\prime})\cdot\chi_{0}((C(r_{i},w)-u_{v^{\prime}})\cap X_{0})=0.\end{split}

This is true for all
i\in I
and
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and thus we conclude that
\chi
satisfies (
3.3
) as well.
∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
3.3
. The idea is that, we partition
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
into a few subsets, one for each factor
r
of
n
, and for each of them we use Lemma
3.6
to get a coloring of this subset with low discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and nearly optimal discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
. Finally we show that the full coloring we get from combining these subset colorings satisfies the properties.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Suppose that
n
has prime factorization
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
. For each
1\leq i\leq k
, we partition
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}
into
\alpha_{i}+1
intervals
\left(S^{(t)}_{i}\right)_{t=0}^{\alpha_{i}}
in the following way. If
p_{i}=2
, then we set
S_{i}^{(\alpha_{i})}=\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}
and
S_{i}^{(t)}=\emptyset
for all
0\leq t<\alpha_{i}
. If
p_{i}>2
, then we set
S_{i}^{(0)}=\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}
and
S_{i}^{(t)}=\{x:p_{i}^{t-1}\leq x<p_{i}^{t}\}
for
1\leq t\leq\alpha_{i}
. In particular we have
|S^{(t)}_{i}|\leq p_{i}^{t}
for all
i
and
t
. Moreover, for each
i
and
t>0
,
S_{i}^{(t)}
is always an interval of length
s_{i}p_{i}^{t-1}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}
for some even
s_{i}
.

For each factor
r=p_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\delta_{k}}
of
n
, we define
Y_{r}=S_{1}^{(\delta_{1})}\times\cdots\times S_{k}^{(\delta_{k})}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}}
and
X_{r}=\psi_{n}(Y_{r})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
Since each
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}
is partitioned into
S_{i}^{(t)}
for
0\leq t\leq\alpha_{i}
, their product is partitioned into
Y_{r}
for
r
divides
n
, so
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is partitioned into
X_{r}
for
r
divides
n
. Because
|S^{(t)}_{i}|\leq p_{i}^{t}
for all
i
and
t
,

(3.4)

|X_{r}|=|Y_{r}|=\prod_{i=1}^{k}|S_{i}^{(\delta_{i})}|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\delta_{i}}=r.

For each nonempty
X_{r}
with
r=p_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\delta_{k}}
, we would like to apply Lemma
3.6
to get a coloring of
X_{r}
with bounded discrepancy. Note that we can translate
Y_{r}
so that all intervals
S_{j}^{(\delta_{j})}
for
1\leq j\leq k
start at
0
, and notice those properties from Lemma
3.6
are invariant under translation, so it is applicable to
X_{r}
. Let
I_{r}=\{i\in[k]:\delta_{i}>0\}\subseteq[k]
. We have that for each
i\in I_{r}
,
S_{i}^{(\delta_{i})}
is an interval of length
s_{i}p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}
for some even
s_{i}
and
\beta_{i}=\delta_{i}-1
. This
implies that we can find a partial coloring
\chi_{r}:X_{r}\to\{-1,1\}
such that

(3.5)

\begin{split}\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi_{r}(A\cap X_{r})|&\leq c_{0}2^{\frac{|I_{r}|}{2}}\sqrt{|X_{r}|}\left(|I_{r}|+1+\log\frac{n}{|X_{r}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

and for any
i\in I_{r}
and any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,

(3.6)

\chi_{r}(C(n/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}},w)\cap X_{r})=\chi_{r}(C(n/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i}+1},w)\cap X_{r})=0.

Having defined
\chi_{r}
as above for each nonempty
X_{r}
, we now construct
\chi
by setting
\chi(x_{r})=\chi_{r}(x_{r})
for each
x_{r}\in X_{r}
for all
r
that divides
n
. We first show that
\chi
has low discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
. By (
3.4
) we have
|X_{r}|\leq r
. By definition we have
|I_{r}|\leq k
. Also note that
x^{1/2}\cdot\left(k+1+\log\frac{n}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is increasing for
x\in(0,n]
. Combining these together, we know that for any nonempty
X_{r}
,

(3.7)

2^{\frac{|I_{r}|}{2}}\sqrt{|X_{r}|}\left(|I_{r}|+1+\log\frac{n}{|X_{r}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{k}{2}}\sqrt{r}\left(k+1+\log\frac{n}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{k}{2}}(k+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{r}\left(\log\frac{en}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.7
), there exists an absolute constant
c_{1}
such that for each nonempty
X_{r}
,

(3.8)

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi_{r}(A\cap X_{r})|\leq c_{0}2^{\frac{k}{2}}(k+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\sqrt{r}\left(\log\frac{en}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c_{1}2^{\frac{3}{4}k}\cdot\sqrt{r}\left(1+\log\frac{n}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This is also true for
X_{r}=\emptyset
. Moreover we know that for any
r=p_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\delta_{k}}
,

\sqrt{r}\left(1+\log\frac{n}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\delta_{i}/2}\left(1+\log\frac{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{p_{i}^{\delta_{i}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{-(\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i})/2}\left(1+(\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i})\log p_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Summing this over all factors
r=p_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\delta_{k}}
for
0\leq\delta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}
, we have that if we set
t_{i}=\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i}
,

\begin{split}\sum_{r|n}\sqrt{r}\left(1+\log\frac{n}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot\sum_{\delta_{1}=0}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\delta_{k}=0}^{\alpha_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{-(\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i})/2}\left(1+(\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i})\log p_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\sqrt{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{t_{i}=0}^{\alpha_{i}}p_{i}^{-t_{i}/2}(1+t_{i}\log p_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq\sqrt{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{t_{i}=0}^{\infty}p_{i}^{-t_{i}/2}(1+t_{i}\log p_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).\end{split}

Note that for
p\geq 2
,

f(p):=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}p^{-t/2}(1+t\log p)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is well-defined and tends to
1
as
p\to\infty
. Thus there exists an absolute constant
P>0
such that if
p\geq P
, then
f(p)<2^{\frac{1}{4}}
. Since there are only finitely many prime numbers less than
P
, we conclude that there exists an absolute constant
c_{2}
such that

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{t_{i}=0}^{\infty}p_{i}^{-t_{i}/2}(1+t_{i}\log p_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}f(p_{i})\leq c_{2}\cdot 2^{\frac{k}{4}}.

Thus we have

(3.9)

\sum_{r|n}\sqrt{r}\left(1+\log\frac{n}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{t_{i}=0}^{\infty}p_{i}^{-t_{i}/2}(1+t_{i}\log p_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq c_{2}2^{\frac{k}{4}}\sqrt{n}.

Thus for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, by (
3.8
) and (
3.9
),

|\chi(A)|=\left|\sum_{r|n}\chi_{r}(A\cap X_{r})\right|\leq\sum_{r|n}c_{1}2^{\frac{3}{4}k}\cdot\sqrt{r}\left(1+\log\frac{n}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c_{1}c_{2}2^{k}\sqrt{n}.

Here we set
c=c_{1}c_{2}
to get the desired bound on the discrepancy over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Now we study the discrepancy of
\chi
over
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
. Note that
C(r^{\prime},w)=C(\operatorname{gcd}(r^{\prime},n),w)
. We may only consider the set of
C(r^{\prime},w)\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}
for which
r^{\prime}=p_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\gamma_{k}}
is a factor of
n
. By definition of
\chi
, we have the relation that

(3.10)

\chi(C(r^{\prime},w))=\sum_{r|n}\chi_{r}(C(r^{\prime},w)\cap X_{r}).

If
r^{\prime}
is not a multiple of
r=p_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\delta_{k}}
, then we know that there exists some
1\leq i\leq k
for which
\gamma_{i}\leq\delta_{i}-1
. It follows that
\delta_{i}>0
, and
i\in I_{r}
. Since
r^{\prime}
is a factor of
n
and
\gamma_{i}\leq\delta_{i}-1
, it is a factor of
n/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i}+1}
. Consequently, the congruence class
C(r^{\prime},w)
can be partitioned into congruence classes of the form
C(n/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-\delta_{i}+1},w^{\prime})
. Noting that (
3.6
) holds for any
w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we know that if
r
is not a factor of
r^{\prime}
, then
\chi_{r}(C(r^{\prime},w)\cap X_{r})=0
.
Thus we can remove all summands on the right hand side of (
3.10
) except those
r
that divides
r^{\prime}
. We get

\chi(C(r^{\prime},w))=\sum_{r|r^{\prime}}\chi_{r}(C(r^{\prime},w)\cap X_{r})=\chi\left(C(r^{\prime},w)\cap\left(\bigcup_{r|r^{\prime}}X_{r}\right)\right).

Note that we are taking union over all
r=p_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\delta_{k}}
for which
0\leq\delta_{i}\leq\gamma_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. We have

\begin{split}\bigcup_{r|r^{\prime}}X_{r}&=\psi_{n}\left(\bigcup_{r|r^{\prime}}Y_{r}\right)\\
&=\psi_{n}\left(\bigcup_{\delta_{1}=0}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\bigcup_{\delta_{k}=0}^{\gamma_{k}}S_{1}^{(\delta_{1})}\times\cdots\times S_{k}^{(\delta_{k})}\right)\\
&=\psi_{n}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\bigcup_{\delta_{i}=0}^{\gamma_{i}}S_{i}^{(\delta_{i})}\right)\right).\end{split}

From our construction, we know that
\bigcup_{\delta_{i}=0}^{\gamma_{i}}S_{i}^{(\delta_{i})}=\emptyset
if
p_{i}=2
and
\gamma_{i}<\alpha_{i}
, and otherwise

\bigcup_{\delta_{i}=0}^{\gamma_{i}}S_{i}^{(\delta_{i})}=\{x:0\leq x<p_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}.

Thus we may conclude that

(3.11)

\bigcup_{r|r^{\prime}}X_{r}\subseteq\{\psi_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{k}):0\leq t_{i}<p_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\mathrm{\;for\;all\;}1\leq i\leq k\}.

Let
Z_{r^{\prime}}
be the set on the right hand side of (
3.11
). Any two distinct elements of
Z_{r^{\prime}}
differ on some
t_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, i.e., they have different remainders when divided by
p_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}
for some
i
. Therefore their difference is not divisible by
r^{\prime}
. Hence there is at most one element of
C(r^{\prime},w)
in
\bigcup_{r|r^{\prime}}X_{r}\subseteq Z_{r^{\prime}}
for any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We conclude that for any
r^{\prime}
that divides
n
and any
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,

|\chi(C(r^{\prime},w))|=\left|\chi\left(C(r^{\prime},w)\cap\left(\bigcup_{r|r^{\prime}}X_{r}\right)\right)\right|\leq 1.

This finishes our proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

By being a little more careful with bounds in the above proof, we may improve the latter inequality in Lemma
3.3
to

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A)|\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot 2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)k},

where the
o(1)
term tends to
0
as
k\to\infty
. This in turn improves the bound on

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
to
\min_{r|n}\left(\frac{n}{r}+\sqrt{r}\cdot 2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\omega(r)}\right)

in Theorem
3.1
, where the
o(1)
term goes to
0
as
\omega(r)\to\infty
.

4.
Lower Bounds

In this section, we prove the lower bounds in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. These proofs use Fourier analysis.
We first set up helpful notations and prove a consequence of the Plancherel theorem in Lemma
4.1
.

Notations
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
. Any coloring
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
is a special case. In this section, all summations are over
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
unless otherwise specified.

We define the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
by
\widehat{f}(r)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f(x)e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{n}xr}
.

For two functions
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, their convolution
f_{1}*f_{2}:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by
f_{1}*f_{2}(a)=\sum_{x}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(a-x)
. We have the convolution identity
\widehat{f_{1}*f_{2}}=\widehat{f_{1}}\cdot\widehat{f}_{2}
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
that divides
n
, we say that
x\equiv y\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)
for
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
if
x=y+kr
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We define, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
that divides
n
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,

g_{f}(a,r):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:x\equiv a\!\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)}f(x)\quad\and\quad G_{f}(r):=\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}|g(a,r)|^{2}.

We see that
g_{f}(a,r)=g_{f}(a+kr,r)
, so
g_{f}(\cdot,r)
can be treated as a function on
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
.

Let
M
be a set or a multiset of elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. For
a,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we define
rM
to be the multiset
\{rx:x\in M\}
, and
a+M
to be the multiset
\{a+x:x\in M\}
. We define
m_{M}
to be the multiplicity function, i.e.
m_{M}(x)
is the multiplicity of
x
in
M
. In particular, when
M
is a set,
m_{M}=1_{M}
is the indicator function. We also define

f(M)=\sum_{x\in M}f(x).

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, let
\widehat{f}
be its Fourier transform. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be a factor of
n
. Then

\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\left|\widehat{f}\left(k\cdot\frac{n}{r}\right)\right|^{2}=rG_{f}(r).

Proof.

Let
t=n/r
. For each
0\leq k\leq r-1
, we know that by definition

\widehat{f}(kt)=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}f(x)e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{n}x\cdot kt}=\sum_{x=0}^{tr-1}f(x)e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{r}xk}=\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}g_{f}(a,r)e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{r}ak}.

Hence for
g:\mathbb{Z}_{r}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
g(a):=g_{f}(a,r)
, its Fourier transform is
\widehat{g}(k)=\widehat{f}(kt)
. By the Plancherel theorem we have

\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\left|\widehat{f}\left(k\cdot\frac{n}{r}\right)\right|^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}|\widehat{g}(k)|^{2}=r\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}|g(a)|^{2}=rG_{f}(r).

∎

In Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, we prove lower and upper bounds on the same quantity respectively. Combining them we get Corollary
4.4
. This corollary lower bounds the discrepancy of a function
f
over arithmetic progressions in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
by the Fourier coefficients of
f
.

The next lemma lower bounds the quantity by a weighted
L^{2}
sum of Fourier coefficients
\widehat{f}
. The weights depend on the arithmetic structure of
n
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
,
1\leq m\leq n
be an integer, and
M:=\{0,1\dots,m-1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Then

(4.1)

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|f(a+bM)|^{2}\geq\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\max\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right).

Proof.

We show some properties about the multiplicity function. For the set
M
, we have

(4.2)

f(a+bM)=\sum_{x\in M}f(a+bx)=\sum_{x}f(x)m_{a+bM}(x)=\sum_{x}f(x)m_{-bM}(a-x)=f*m_{-bM}(a).

For any
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we have

(4.3)

\widehat{m_{-uM}}(v)=\sum_{x}m_{-uM}(x)e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{n}vx}=\sum_{x\in-uM}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{n}vx}=\sum_{y\in-M}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{n}vuy}=\widehat{m_{-M}}(uv).

Applying (
4.3
) twice we have
\widehat{m_{-uM}}(v)=\widehat{m_{-vM}}(u).
We have

(4.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{a}\sum_{b}|f(a+bM)|^{2}&=\sum_{b}\sum_{a}|f*m_{-bM}(a)|^{2}=\sum_{b}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r}\left|\widehat{f*m_{-bM}}(r)\right|^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{b}\sum_{r}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\left|\widehat{m_{-bM}}(r)\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{b}\sum_{r}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\left|\widehat{m_{-rM}}(b)\right|^{2}\\
&=\sum_{r}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{b}\left|\widehat{m_{-rM}}(b)\right|^{2}\right)=\sum_{r}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\sum_{s}|m_{-rM}(s)|^{2},\end{split}

where the first equality uses (
4.2
), the second uses the Planchrel theorem, the third uses the Fourier identity of convolution, the fourth uses (
4.3
) twice, and the last uses the Plancherel theorem.
Now we evaluate
\sum_{s}|m_{-rM}(s)|^{2}
. Let
k=\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)
. When
km\leq n
, we know that
rM
contains
m
elements with multiplicity 1. Thus in this case we have

\sum_{s}|m_{-rM}(s)|^{2}=m.

Otherwise when
km>n
, we know that
rM
contains elements that are multiples of
k
. There are
\frac{n}{k}
such elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Therefore, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\sum_{s}|m_{-rM}(s)|^{2}=\sum_{t=0}^{\frac{n}{k}-1}|m_{-rM}(tk)|^{2}\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\frac{n}{k}-1}|m_{-rM}(tk)|\right)^{2}}{n/k}=\frac{m^{2}k}{n}.

Hence we may conclude that for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,

(4.5)

\sum_{s}|m_{-rM}(s)|^{2}\geq\max\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right).

Combining this with (
4.4
), we obtain (
4.1
).
∎

The next lemma gives an upper bound on the quantity above. The upper bound involves the discrepancy of
f
over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and some arithmetic sums of
f
. Let
\phi(\cdot)
be Euler’s totient function.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that

T_{f}:=\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|f(A)|.

Let
m\leq n
be a positive integer, and
M:=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Then we have

(4.6)

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|f(a+bM)|^{2}\leq n^{2}T_{f}^{2}+\sum_{0\leq k<m:k|N}m^{2}\frac{\phi(k)}{k}G_{f}(n/k).

Proof.

For each fixed
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we analyze the summation
\sum_{a}|f(a+bM)|^{2}
.
Let
r=\operatorname{gcd}(b,n)
and
k=n/r
. There are two possibilities:
rm\leq n
or
rm>n
.

If
rm\leq n
, then each element in
bM
has multiplicity
1
. Therefore
a+bM
is a set for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Moreover from our definition of
M=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
, we know that
a+bM\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
is an arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. In this case, for any
a
,
|f(a+bM)|\leq T_{f}
, so

(4.7)

\sum_{a}|f(a+bM)|^{2}\leq\sum_{a}T_{f}^{2}=nT_{f}^{2}.

If
rm>n
, i.e.,
m>k
, then some elements in
bM
have multiplicity greater than
1
. The bound in (
4.7
) no longer applies. Let
K=\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
and it follows that each element in
bK
has multiplicity
1
. Moreover, it covers each multiple of
r
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
exactly once. Hence

f(a+bK)=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}f(a+bt)=g_{f}(a,r).

Suppose that
m=qk+s
for integers
q
and
0\leq s<k
. Then we may write
m_{M}=m_{S}+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}m_{s+jk+K}
where
S=\{0,1,\dots,s-1\}
.
Thus, for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we have

(4.8)

\begin{split}f(a+bM)&=f(a+bS)+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}f(a+b\cdot(s+jk)+bK)\\
&=f(a+bS)+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}g_{f}(a+b\cdot(s+jk),r)=f(a+bS)+q\cdot g_{f}(a,r).\end{split}

In the last step, we use the fact that
b\cdot(s+jk)
is a multiple of
r
.

Since
s<k
, each element in
bS
has multiplicity at most
1
, and further
a+bS\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
for all
a
. Thus

(4.9)

|f(a+bS)|\leq T_{f}.

We now partition
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
into congruence classes mod
r
. In particular, we have

(4.10)

\sum_{a}|f(a+bM)|^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\sum_{a\equiv i\!\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)}|f(a+bM)|^{2}.

For each
a^{\prime}\equiv i\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)
, there are exactly
s
choices of
a\equiv i\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)
such that
a^{\prime}\in a+bS
. Thus,

(4.11)

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(i+jr+bS)=\sum_{a\equiv i\!\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)}f(a+bS)=s\cdot g_{f}(i,r).

Consequently, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{a\equiv i\!\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)}|f(a+bM)|^{2}&=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|f(i+jr+bM)|^{2}\\
[\textrm{by }\eqref{eqn:lem-4.4-1}]\quad&=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|f(i+jr+bS)+q\cdot g_{f}(i,r)|^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(|f(i+jr+bS)|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\left(q\cdot f(i+jr+bS)\overline{g_{f}(i,r)}\right)+q^{2}|g_{f}(i,r)|^{2}\right)\\
[\textrm{by }\eqref{eqn:lem-4.4-2}]\quad&\leq kT_{f}^{2}+kq^{2}|g_{f}(i,r)|^{2}+2q\operatorname{Re}\left(\overline{g_{f}(i,r)}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(i+jr+bS)\right)\\
[\textrm{by }\eqref{eqn:lem-4.4-4}]\quad&=kT_{f}^{2}+kq^{2}|g_{f}(i,r)|^{2}+2qs|g_{f}(i,r)|^{2}\\
&\leq kT_{f}^{2}+|g_{f}(i,r)|^{2}\cdot\frac{k^{2}q^{2}+2kqs+s^{2}}{k}\\
[\textrm{by }m=kq+s]\quad&=kT_{f}^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{k}|g_{f}(i,r)|^{2}.\end{split}

Put this into (
4.10
). We know that in the second case where
rm>n
, i.e.,
k<m
,

(4.12)

\sum_{a}|f(a+bM)|^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\sum_{a\equiv i\!\;(\mathrm{mod}\;r)}|f(a+bM)|^{2}\leq nT_{f}^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{k}G_{f}(r)=nT_{f}^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{k}G_{f}(n/k).

The second case happens exactly when
\operatorname{gcd}(b,n)=n/k
for some
k<m
which divides
n
. The number of such choices of
b
is exactly
\phi(k)
for any fixed
k
. Hence combining (
4.7
) and (
4.12
), we get (
4.6
).
∎

Combining Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we deduce the following general lower bounds.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\widehat{f}
be its Fourier transform. Suppose that

T_{f}:=\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|f(A)|.

For any positive integer
m\leq n
, we have

(4.13)

n^{2}T_{f}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq k<m:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{f}(n/k)\geq\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\max\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right).

In Corollary
4.4
, the inequality involves
G_{f}
and
\widehat{f}
besides
T_{f}
. We aim to get a lower bound on
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
that only depends on arithmetic structure of
n
(Proposition
4.7
). To achieve that, we need two lemmas (Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.6
) to remove the dependency on
\widehat{f}
and
G_{f}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\widehat{f}
be its Fourier transform and
m\leq n
be a positive integer. Then

\sum_{1\leq k\leq n:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{f}(n/k)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, we know that

\frac{n}{k}G_{f}(n/k)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:k|r}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}.

Thus we have (noticing that
\sum_{k|l}\phi(k)=l
for any positive integer
l
)

\begin{split}\sum_{1\leq k\leq n:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{f}(n/k)&=\sum_{1\leq k\leq n:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{n}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:k|r}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\frac{m^{2}}{n}\sum_{1\leq k\leq n:k|n,k|r}\phi(k)\\
&=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\frac{m^{2}}{n}\sum_{1\leq k\leq n:k|\operatorname{gcd}(n,r)}\phi(k)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\frac{m^{2}}{n}\operatorname{gcd}(r,n).\end{split}

Hence we have the desired equality.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\widehat{f}
be its Fourier transform. Let
m,l\leq n
be positive integers. Then

(4.14)

\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}\cdot\min\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right)\leq\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{f}(n/k)+\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|n}\frac{mn}{k}G_{f}(n/k).

Proof.

It suffices to compare the coefficient of each
|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}
on both sides after expanding all terms of
G_{f}
on the right hand side (
4.14
) using Lemma
4.1
. By Lemma
4.1
, for any
k
that divides
n
,

\frac{n}{k}G_{f}(n/k)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:k|r}|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}.

For each
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, the coefficient
t_{r}
of
|\widehat{f}(r)|^{2}
on the right hand side of (
4.14
) is given by

\begin{split}t_{r}&=\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{n}+\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}m=\frac{m^{2}}{n}\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}\phi(k)+m\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}1.\end{split}

If
\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)\leq l
, then we know that the first summation sums over all factors of
\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)
, and the second summation is zero. Hence we know that in this case,

t_{r}=\frac{m^{2}}{n}\operatorname{gcd}(r,n).

If
\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)>l
, then in particular the second summation contains at least one term
k=\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)
, and the first summation is nonnegative. Hence in this case,

t_{r}>m.

We may conclude that
t_{r}\geq\min\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right)
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. This yields (
4.14
).
∎

Using the lemmas above, we prove a lower bound on
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
which depends only on the arithmetic structure of
n
.

Proposition 4.7
.

For any positive integers
n
and
l\leq n
,

\frac{1}{(\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n}))^{2}}\leq\frac{8}{n}\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|n}\phi(k)+2\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|n}\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

Proof.

For simplicity let us denote

S_{1}:=\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|n}\phi(k)\quad\and\quad S_{2}:=\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|n}\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

We aim to show

(4.15)

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\frac{8}{n}S_{1}+2S_{2}}.

Because we always have
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\geq 1
, we may assume that
S_{1}<\frac{n}{8}
.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\{1,-1\}
be any coloring of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and let
T_{\chi}:=\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A)|.
Let
m\leq n
be a positive integer to be determined later. By Corollary
4.4
, we know that

(4.16)

n^{2}T_{\chi}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq k<m:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k)\geq\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}\cdot\max\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right).

By Lemma
4.5
, we know that

\sum_{1\leq k\leq n:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}\cdot\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n}.

Subtract both sides from (
4.16
). We get

(4.17)

n^{2}T_{\chi}^{2}+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}\cdot\min\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right)\geq\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}\cdot m+\sum_{m\leq k\leq n:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k).

Since
\chi
takes value in
\{-1,1\}
, we know that

(4.18)

\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}=nG_{\chi}(n)=n^{2}.

Note that each
G_{\chi}(\cdot)
is nonnegative. By (
4.18
), the right hand side of (
4.17
) is lower bounded by

(4.19)

\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}\cdot m+\sum_{m\leq k\leq n:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k)\geq n^{2}m.

For the left hand side of (
4.17
), we apply Lemma
4.6
for
m
and
l
and get

(4.20)

\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}\cdot\min\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right)\leq\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k)+\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|n}\frac{mn}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k).

Note that by definition, each single
g_{\chi}(a,n/k)
is the sum of
k
values of
\chi
. Because
\chi
takes value in
\{-1,1\}
, we have

(4.21)

G_{\chi}(n/k)\leq\frac{n}{k}\cdot k^{2}=nk.

Moreover, each
g_{\chi}(a,n/k)
is
\chi(A)
for some
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, so
|g_{\chi}(a,n/k)|\leq T_{\chi}
. This means that for all
k
,

(4.22)

G_{\chi}(n/k)\leq\frac{n}{k}\cdot T_{\chi}^{2}.

We bound the first term on the right hand side of (
4.20
) using (
4.21
) and get

(4.23)

\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|n}\frac{m^{2}\phi(k)}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k)\leq m^{2}n\sum_{1\leq k\leq l:k|n}\phi(k)=nm^{2}\cdot S_{1}.

On the other hand, we bound the second term on the right hand side of (
4.20
) using (
4.22
) and get

(4.24)

\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|n}\frac{mn}{k}G_{\chi}(n/k)\leq\sum_{l<k\leq n:k|n}\frac{mn^{2}}{k^{2}}T_{\chi}^{2}=n^{2}mT_{\chi}^{2}\cdot S_{2}.

Put (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) into (
4.20
). We get

(4.25)

\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}|\widehat{\chi}(r)|^{2}\cdot\min\left(\frac{m^{2}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(r,n)}{n},m\right)\leq nm^{2}\cdot S_{1}+T_{\chi}^{2}n^{2}mS_{2}.

Finally we put (
4.19
) and (
4.25
) into (
4.17
). We get

\left(n^{2}+n^{2}mS_{2}\right)T_{\chi}^{2}\geq n^{2}m-nm^{2}S_{1}.

Dividing both sides by
n^{2}m
, we get

(4.26)

\left(\frac{1}{m}+S_{2}\right)T_{\chi}^{2}\geq 1-\frac{m}{n}S_{1}.

Now we pick
m=\lfloor n/(2S_{1})\rfloor
. Note that
S_{1}<n/8
from our assumption, so
m\geq\frac{n}{4S_{1}}
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{m}\leq\frac{4}{n}S_{1}\quad\and\quad 1-\frac{m}{n}S_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Put them into (
4.26
). We conclude that

T_{\chi}^{2}\geq\frac{1-\frac{m}{n}S_{1}}{\frac{1}{m}+S_{2}}\geq\frac{1/2}{\frac{4}{n}S_{1}+S_{2}}=\frac{1}{\frac{8}{n}S_{1}+2S_{2}}.

Since this bound applies to all
T_{\chi}^{2}
, it also applies to
(\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n}))^{2}=\min_{\chi}T_{\chi}^{2}
. Hence we have (
4.15
).
∎

Remark 4.8
.

As mentioned earlier in Remark
1.3
,
the proof above also applies to the case where
\chi
takes value in the unit circle on the complex plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
(instead of
\{1,-1\}
). Just note that (
4.18
) and (
4.21
) hold in this more general case as well, and all other steps are identical.

Now we prove two corollaries. The first shows that the upper bound in Theorem
3.1
is tight up to an
n^{o(1)}
factor.

Corollary 4.9

(Lower bound in Theorem
1.1
)
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that, for any positive integer
n
,

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\geq\frac{1}{8\sqrt{d(n)}}\cdot\min_{r|n}\left(\frac{n}{r}+\sqrt{r}\right),

where
d(n)
is the number of factors of
n
.

Proof.

Let
t_{1}
be the minimum factor of
n
that is at least
n^{\frac{2}{3}}
, and let
t_{2}
be the maximum factor of
n
less than
n^{\frac{2}{3}}
. As

2\cdot\min\left(\sqrt{t_{1}},\frac{n}{t_{2}}\right)\geq\min\left(\sqrt{t_{1}}+\frac{n}{t_{1}},\sqrt{t_{2}}+\frac{n}{t_{2}}\right)=\min_{r|n}\left(\frac{n}{r}+\sqrt{r}\right),

it suffices to show that

(4.27)

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\geq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{d(n)}}\cdot\min\left(\sqrt{t_{1}},\frac{n}{t_{2}}\right).

Now we apply Proposition
4.7
to
n
and
l=\frac{n}{t_{1}}
. We bound the two summations as follows. We have

(4.28)

\sum_{1\leq k\leq\frac{n}{t_{1}}:k|n}\phi(k)\leq\sum_{1\leq k\leq\frac{n}{t_{1}}:k|n}k\leq\frac{n}{t_{1}}\cdot d(n).

Note that
t_{2}
is the largest factor of
n
less than
t_{1}
, so the minimum
k
in
\{\frac{n}{t_{1}}<k\leq n:k|n\}
is
n/t_{2}
. Thus we have

(4.29)

\sum_{\frac{n}{t_{1}}<k\leq n:k|n}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq\sum_{\frac{n}{t_{2}}\leq k\leq n:k|n}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq\frac{t_{2}^{2}}{n^{2}}\cdot d(n).

Using above two bounds, we get

(\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n}))^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\frac{8}{t_{1}}d(n)+2\frac{t_{2}^{2}}{n^{2}}d(n)}\geq\frac{1}{16d(n)}\min\left(t_{1},\frac{n^{2}}{t_{2}^{2}}\right).

This is equivalent to (
4.27
), so we have the expected inequality.
∎

The second corollary proves the lower bound in Theorem
1.2
. The main observation is that the factor
d(n)
can be removed in (
4.28
) and (
4.29
) when
n
is a prime power.

Corollary 4.10

(Lower bound in Theorem
1.2
)
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
k
be a positive integer. Then for
n=p^{k}
,

(4.30)

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\geq\frac{1}{4}p^{\frac{k-\lfloor k/3\rfloor}{2}}.

Proof.

In Proposition
4.7
, we pick
l=p^{t}
for some
1\leq t<n
to be determined later.
Note that the factors of
n
between
1
and
l
are given by
s=p^{i}
for
0\leq i\leq t
, which are all factors of
l
. Hence we have

\sum_{1\leq s\leq l:s|n}\phi(s)=l=p^{t}.

All factors of
n
larger than
l
are given by
s=p^{i}
for
t+1\leq i\leq k
. Thus

\sum_{l<s\leq l:s|n}\frac{1}{s^{2}}=\sum_{i=t+1}^{k}p^{-2i}\leq p^{-2t-2}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{2}}+\frac{1}{p^{4}}+\cdots\right)\leq 2p^{-2t-2}.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.7
we get

\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\geq\sqrt{\frac{1}{8p^{t-k}+4p^{-2t-2}}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\cdot\min\left(p^{\frac{k-t}{2}},p^{t+1}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\cdot p^{\min(\frac{k-t}{2},t+1)}.

We pick
t=\lfloor{k/3}\rfloor
to get (
4.30
).

∎

5.
Concluding Remarks

In Theorem
1.1
, the upper and lower bounds are off by a factor of
O(d(n)^{3/2})
. We were not able to close this gap, and it seems that major improvement of either bound would require new observations.

Problem 5.1
.

Determine
\disc(\mathcal{A}_{n})
up to a constant factor for all
n
.

There are other notions of discrepancy besides the one studied in this paper. Among them there is the
hereditary discrepancy
, defined for a system
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
as

\herdisc(\mathcal{A}):=\max_{X\subseteq\Omega}\disc(\mathcal{A}|_{X})

where
\mathcal{A}|_{X}=\{A\cap X:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
. Clearly
\disc(\mathcal{A})\leq\herdisc(\mathcal{A})
. For the set
\mathcal{A}
of arithmetic progressions in
[n]
, Matoušek and Spencer
[
10
]
proved a stronger statement that
\herdisc(\mathcal{A})=O(n^{\frac{1}{4}})
. This is because their partial coloring method works not just for
\mathcal{A}
, but also for
\mathcal{A}|_{X}
for any
X\subseteq[n]
.

In contrast, our construction of the coloring in Section
3
is only valid for coloring the whole set
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. While it can be adapted so that the same upper bound (possibly with a larger constant factor) applies to some special subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, it does not work for all subsets
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

Problem 5.2
.

Estimate the hereditary discrepancy of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

By Corollary
2.3
we have
\herdisc(\mathcal{A}_{n})=O(n^{\frac{1}{2}})
. The method used in Section
2
can be adapted to give the following slightly stronger statement. Let
\phi(\cdot)
be Euler’s totient function.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists a constant
c
such that
for all positive integers
n
, we have

\herdisc(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq c\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

We leave the proof of this theorem to the Appendix. It shows that the upper bound
O(n^{\frac{1}{2}})
is not always tight. It would be interesting to determine if there is a matching lower bound of the form
n^{\frac{1}{2}-o(1)}
.

Appendix A
Improved bound for hereditary discrepancy

In this appendix, we prove Theorem
5.1
, an improved upper bound on the hereditary discrepancy of modular arithmetic progressions. We need the following lemma
which gives a partial coloring bound.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a set of size
m>0
. There exists an absolute constant
c
such that there is a partial coloring
\chi:X\to\{-1,0,1\}
that assigns
\pm 1
to at least
m/10
elements in
X
such that

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq c\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Assuming this lemma, we may now prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
assuming
A.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a set of size
m
. We show that there exists a coloring
\chi:X\to\{1,-1\}
such that, for some absolute constant
c
,

(A.1)

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq c\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

The idea is that we iteratively apply Lemma
A.1
to the set of uncolored elements until there are at most
\phi(n)
elements left, and then apply Corollary
2.3
to color the remaining elements. Let
c_{0},c_{1}
be the constants in Lemma
A.1
and Corollary
2.3
.

Start with
X_{0}=X
. For each
i\geq 0
, we apply Lemma
A.1
to
X_{i}
to get a partial coloring
\chi_{i}:X_{i}\to\{-1,0,1\}
, and we let
X_{i+1}=\chi_{i}^{-1}(0)\subseteq X_{i}
to be the set of uncolored elements in
i
-th iteration. We continue this process until the
(k-1)
-th iteration where there are at most
\phi(n)
elements left (i.e.
|X_{k}|\leq\phi(n)
). Then we apply Corollary
2.3
to get a coloring
\chi_{k}:X_{k}\to\{-1,1\}
. Let
\chi
be the final coloring given by
\chi(x_{i})=\chi_{i}(x_{i})
if
x_{i}\in X_{i}\setminus X_{i+1}
for
0\leq i\leq k-1
, and
\chi(x_{k})=\chi_{k}(x_{k})
. We know that
|X_{i}|\leq 0.9|X_{i-1}|
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, so
|X_{k}|\leq(0.9)^{k}|X_{0}|=(0.9)^{k}m\leq(0.9)^{k}n
.
Because we stop when there are at most
\phi(n)
elements left, we shall see that
k\leq 1+\log_{0.9}\frac{m}{n}\leq 10\log\frac{en}{m}
.

Applying the bounds on the discrepancy of
\chi_{i}
from Lemma
A.1
and Corollary
2.3
, we conclude that for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
,

\begin{split}|\chi(A\cap X)|&=\left|\sum_{i=0}^{k}\chi_{i}(X_{i}\cap A)\right|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left|\chi_{i}(X_{i}\cap A)\right|+\left|\chi_{k}(X_{k}\cap A)\right|\\
&\leq k\cdot c_{0}\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+c_{1}|X_{k}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{|X_{k}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&<10c_{0}\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}+c_{1}\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{m}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\end{split}

for
c=10c_{0}+c_{1}
being an absolute constant. Since it holds for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, we have (
A.1
).
∎

We are left to prove Lemma
A.1
. We first need a slight generalization of the partial coloring lemma in Lemma
2.1
. In Lemma
2.1
we require
\Delta_{S}\geq 2\sqrt{|S|}
. Here we allow
\Delta_{S}
to take any positive value.

Lemma A.2

(Section 4.6 in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
(V,C)
be a set system on
n
elements, and let a number
\Delta_{S}>0
be given for each set
S\in C
. Suppose that

(A.2)

\sum_{S\in C:S\neq\emptyset}g\left(\frac{\Delta_{S}}{\sqrt{|S|}}\right)\leq\frac{n}{5}

where

(A.3)

g(\lambda)=\begin{cases}10e^{-\lambda^{2}/4}&\textrm{if }\lambda\geq 2,\\
10\log(1+2\lambda^{-1})&\textrm{if }0<\lambda<2.\end{cases}

Then there exists a partial coloring
\chi
that assigns
\pm 1
to at least
n/10
variables (and
0
to the rest), satisfying
|\chi(S)|\leq\Delta_{S}
for each
S\in C
.

We now prove Lemma
A.1
.

Proof of Lemma
A.1
.

We use the same decomposition as in Lemma
2.2
to get the family
C_{2}
of nonempty subsets of
X
. From the argument in Lemma
2.2
, we know that sets in
C_{2}
are of size
2^{i}
for
2^{i}\leq m
. If
\chi:X\to\{-1,0,1\}
is such that for all
0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m
we have
\chi(S)\leq\Delta_{i}
for any set
S\in C_{2}
of size
2^{i}
, then

(A.4)

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq 4\sum_{0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m}\Delta_{i}.

Then we apply a better bound on the number of sets in
C_{2}
of size
2^{i}
, which we shall denote as
f_{i}
. The notations here are the same as in Lemma
2.2
. For each
1\leq d<n
and
0\leq a<\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)
, the number of choices of
t
in (
2.5
) is
\lfloor l_{d,a}/2^{i}\rfloor
. Yet note that
l_{d,a}\leq\frac{n}{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)}
. Hence if
\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)>\frac{n}{2^{i}}
, then there are no such sets included in the set
X_{d,a}
. Therefore we have the following better bound on
f_{i}
:

f_{i}\leq\sum_{1\leq d\leq n-1:\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)\leq\frac{n}{2^{i}}}\sum_{a=0}^{\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)-1}\left\lfloor\frac{l_{d,a}}{2^{i}}\right\rfloor=\sum_{1\leq d\leq n-1:\operatorname{gcd}(d,n)\leq\frac{n}{2^{i}}}\frac{m}{2^{i}}\leq\sum_{1\leq l\leq\frac{n}{2^{i}}:l|n}\phi(n/l)\cdot\frac{m}{2^{i}}.

Because
\phi(ab)\geq\phi(a)\phi(b)
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
, we know that
\phi(n/l)\leq\phi(n)/\phi(l)
for all
l
that divides
n
. Also note that
0<\phi(n)/\phi(l)
for all
l
that does not divide
n
. By Landau
[
8
, p. 184]
, for any
x\geq 1
,
\sum_{l\leq x}1/\phi(l)\leq c_{0}\log ex
for some absolute constant
c_{0}
. Hence we have

(A.5)

f_{i}\leq\frac{m}{2^{i}}\sum_{1\leq l\leq\frac{n}{2^{i}}:l|n}\phi(n/l)\leq\frac{m}{2^{i}}\phi(n)\sum_{1\leq l\leq\frac{n}{2^{i}}}\frac{1}{\phi(l)}\leq c_{0}\frac{m}{2^{i}}\phi(n)\log\frac{en}{2^{i}}.

For simplicity we denote
M=\phi(n)\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}
. We define
b:(0,n]\to\mathbb{R}
, given by

(A.6)

b(s)=\begin{cases}c_{1}\sqrt{s}\cdot\left(\frac{s}{M}\right)^{-1}&\textrm{if }s\geq M,\\
c_{1}\sqrt{s}\cdot\left(\frac{s}{M}\right)^{-0.1}&\textrm{if }s<M,\end{cases}

where
c_{1}>2
is an absolute constant to be determined later. We would like to show that there exists a partial coloring that colors at least
m/10
elements in
X
, such that for any
S\in C_{2}
,

(A.7)

|\chi(S)|\leq b(|S|).

In order to apply Lemma
A.2
, it suffices to verify that

(A.8)

\sum_{S\in C_{2}}g\left(\frac{b(s)}{\sqrt{|S|}}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}m\rfloor}f_{i}\cdot g\left(b(2^{i})2^{-i/2}\right)\leq m/5.

For fixed
i
, we denote
\tau=2^{i}/M
, so
2^{i}=\tau M\geq\tau\phi(n)
. Observe that

f_{i}\leq c_{0}m\cdot\tau^{-1}\cdot\frac{\log\frac{en}{2^{i}}}{\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}}\leq c_{0}m\tau^{-1}\frac{\log\frac{en}{\tau\phi(n)}}{\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}}=c_{0}m\tau^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\log\tau^{-1}}{\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}}\right).

When
\tau<1
, we have
b(2^{i})2^{-i/2}=c_{1}\tau^{-0.1}
and

f_{i}\leq c_{0}m\tau^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\log\tau^{-1}}{\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}}\right)\leq c_{0}m\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1}).

When
\tau\geq 1
, we have
b(2^{i})2^{-i/2}=c_{1}\tau^{-1}
and

f_{i}\leq c_{0}m\tau^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\log\tau^{-1}}{\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}}\right)\leq c_{0}m\tau^{-1}.

Therefore if we write the summation in (
A.8
) in terms of
\tau
, we have

(A.9)

\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}m\rfloor}f_{i}\cdot g\left(b(2^{i})2^{-i/2}\right)=c_{0}m\left(\sum_{\tau\geq 1}\tau^{-1}g(c_{1}\tau^{-1})+\sum_{\tau<1}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1})g(c_{1}\tau^{-0.1})\right),

where the summation of
\tau
is over a geometric sequence with ratio
2
. By definition of
g
in (
A.3
),
g
is monotonically decreasing. Let
T
be a large absolute constant to be determined later. We have

\sum_{\tau\geq T}\tau^{-1}g(c_{1}\tau^{-1})\leq\sum_{\tau\geq T}\tau^{-1}g(2\tau^{-1})=\sum_{\tau\geq T}\tau^{-1}\cdot 10\log(1+\tau).

Since
\sum_{\tau\geq 1}\tau^{-1}\cdot 10\log(1+\tau)
converges, there exists sufficiently large constant
T
satisfying

(A.10)

\sum_{\tau\geq T}\tau^{-1}g(c_{1}\tau^{-1})\leq\frac{1}{20c_{0}}.

We bound the second term on the right hand side of (
A.9
) similarly. We have

\sum_{\tau<T^{-1}}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1})g(c_{1}\tau^{-0.1})\leq\sum_{\tau<T^{-1}}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1})g(2\tau^{-0.1})=\sum_{\tau<T^{-1}}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1})\cdot 10e^{-\tau^{-0.2}}.

Since
\sum_{\tau<1}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1})\cdot 10e^{-\tau^{-0.2}}
converges, there exists sufficiently large constant
T
satisfying

(A.11)

\sum_{\tau<T^{-1}}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1})g(c_{1}\tau^{-0.1})\leq\frac{1}{20c_{0}}.

Hence, there exists constant
T
such that whenever
c_{1}>2
, (
A.10
) and (
A.11
) hold. Note that there are at most
(1+\log_{2}T)
terms in each of the ranges
1\leq\tau<T
and
T^{-1}\leq\tau<1
, and that
g(x)
is monotonically decreasing and tends to zero as
x
goes to infinity. We can choose
c_{1}>2
sufficiently large so that

(A.12)

\sum_{1\leq\tau<T}\tau^{-1}g(c_{1}\tau^{-1})\leq(1+\log_{2}T)\cdot g(c_{1}T^{-1})\leq\frac{1}{20c_{0}}

and

(A.13)

\sum_{T^{-1}\leq\tau<1}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau^{-1})g(c_{1}\tau^{-0.1})\leq(1+\log_{2}T)\cdot T(1+\log T)g(c_{1})\leq\frac{1}{20c_{0}}.

Combining inequalities (
A.10
), (
A.11
), (
A.12
) and (
A.13
), there exists a constant
c_{1}
such that

(A.14)

\sum_{\tau\geq 1}\tau^{-1}(1+\log\tau)g(c_{1}\tau^{-1})+\sum_{\tau<1}\tau^{-1}g(c_{1}\tau^{-0.1})\leq\frac{1}{5c_{0}}.

We use this constant
c_{1}
to define the function
b(\cdot)
in (
A.6
). Combining (
A.9
) and (
A.14
), we have

\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}m\rfloor}f_{i}\cdot g\left(b(2^{i})2^{-i/2}\right)\leq c_{0}m\cdot\frac{1}{5c_{0}}=\frac{m}{5}.

Therefore (
A.8
) holds. By Lemma
A.2
, there exists a partial coloring
\chi:X\to\{-1,0,1\}
that assigns
\pm 1
to at least
m/10
elements in
X
such that (
A.7
) holds. For this coloring
\chi
, we may choose
\Delta_{i}=b(2^{i})
in (
A.4
). Again we denote
\tau=2^{i}/M
, or equivalently
2^{i}=\tau M
. We have

\Delta_{i}=b(2^{i})=\begin{cases}c_{1}\sqrt{M}\cdot\tau^{-0.5}&\textrm{if }\tau\geq 1,\\
c_{1}\sqrt{M}\cdot\tau^{0.4}&\textrm{if }\tau<1.\end{cases}

Put this in (
A.4
). We know that
\chi
satisfies that

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq 4\sum_{0\leq i\leq\log_{2}m}\Delta_{i}=4c_{1}\sqrt{M}\left(\sum_{\tau\geq 1}\tau^{-0.5}+\sum_{\tau<1}\tau^{0.4}\right).

Note that summation of
\tau
is over a geometric sequence with ratio
2
. Hence we have

\sum_{\tau\geq 1}\tau^{-0.5}\leq\frac{1}{1-2^{-0.5}}\quad\and\quad\sum_{\tau<1}\tau^{0.4}\leq\frac{1}{1-2^{-0.4}}.

Therefore we conclude that we can find
\chi
that assigns
\pm 1
to at least
m/10
elements in
X
and satisfies

\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\chi(A\cap X)|\leq 4c_{1}\sqrt{M}\left(\frac{1}{1-2^{-0.5}}+\frac{1}{1-2^{-0.4}}\right)\leq c\sqrt{M}=c\phi(n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{en}{\phi(n)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some appropriate absolute constant
c
.∎

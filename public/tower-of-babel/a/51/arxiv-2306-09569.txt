Probing general U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} models with non-universal lepton charges at FASER/FASER2, COHERENT and long-baseline oscillation experiments Preprint: CPPC-2023-03 arXiv: arXiv:2306.09569
Tobias Felkl, Tong Li, Jiajun Liao, Michael A. Schmidt

Abstract
The general anomaly-free U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} models allow non-universal lepton charges. We explore the sensitivities of FASER/FASER2, COHERENT and DUNE/T2HK precision experiments to the new gauge boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} and the new CP-even scalar ϕ \phi . With non-universal lepton charges, distinctive reaches at FASER/FASER2 emerge in the regime of low m Z ′ m_{Z^{\prime}} and small gauge coupling g B ​ L g_{BL} for different U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} charge setups. The COHERENT experiment and the future long-baseline experiments DUNE/T2HK also provide complementary probes to the available parameter space. For m ϕ < 2 ​ m Z ′ m_{\phi}<2m_{Z^{\prime}} , the search for the scalar ϕ \phi at FASER/FASER2 is sensitive to the mixing angle between the scalar singlet and the SM Higgs. In the case of m ϕ > 2 ​ m Z ′ m_{\phi}>2m_{Z^{\prime}} , the kinematically allowed decay ϕ → Z ′ ​ Z ′ \phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} changes the lifetime and decay rates of the scalar ϕ \phi . The sensitivity reach highly depends on the Z ′ Z^{\prime} mass and the gauge coupling g B ​ L g_{BL} .

Introduction

Neutrino oscillations have been observed by various neutrino experiments
in the last two decades. Their explanation requires non-vanishing neutrino masses which cannot be accounted for by the Standard Model (SM).
It thus provides a strong motivation for new physics beyond the SM associated with neutrinos.
The most economical mechanism for generating neutrino masses is the seesaw mechanism
Minkowski:1977sc
;
Yanagida:1979as
;
GellMann:1980vs
;
Glashow:1979nm
;
Mohapatra:1979ia
;
Shrock:1980ct
;
Schechter:1980gr
, in which one
introduces at least two right-handed neutrinos. Right-handed neutrinos also allow the introduction of an extra Abelian gauge symmetry
U(1)^{\prime}
by choosing their charges to cancel gauge anomalies
Carlson:1986cu
.
The simplest and best-studied anomaly-free formulation for a gauged
U(1)^{\prime}
would be the
U(1)_{B-L}
extension
Davidson:1978pm
;
Mohapatra:1980qe
;
Marshak:1979fm
;
Wetterich:1981bx
;
Masiero:1982fi
;
Mohapatra:1982xz
;
Buchmuller:1991ce
, where
B
and
L
stand
for baryon number and lepton number, respectively. In this model, the
U(1)^{\prime}
has no
U(1)_{Y}
component and the charges are the same for three generations of SM fermions, i.e.,
B=1/3
for SM quarks and
L=-1
for charged leptons and neutrinos.

This is however only one special case. The anomaly conditions allow non-universal
U(1)^{\prime}
charges for different generations of the SM leptons.
The non-universal gauge couplings lead to a non-trivial flavor structure for the fermion masses and may in particular lead to texture zeros in the neutrino Yukawa couplings and the Majorana mass matrix
Araki:2012ip
;
Liao:2013rca
;
Bhatia:2017tgo
;
Bonilla:2017lsq
;
Babu:2017olk
;
Ellis:2017nrp
;
Alonso:2017uky
;
Bhatia:2021eco
. Phenomenologically, the general
U(1)^{\prime}
models with non-universal lepton charges can result in distinguishable searches in high-intensity experiments and may evade the severe constraints by electron beam-dump experiments
Abdallah:2020biq
;
Bauer:2020itv
;
Kling:2020iar
;
delaVega:2021wpx
;
Coy:2021wfs
;
Amaral:2021rzw
. Moreover, the new scalar field spontaneously breaking the
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry is not necessarily very heavy and can be naturally below 1 TeV or even much lighter
Iso:2009ss
;
Okada:2012sg
;
Dev:2021qjj
. The new light scalar in the same model can also be probed through the mixing with the SM Higgs boson.

The newly predicted particles in
U(1)^{\prime}
models with very weak couplings would become long-lived and can be searched for at the Forward Physics Facility (FPF) such as the far-forward experiments at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). FASER (ForwArd Search ExpeRiment) is an on-going experiment in the far-forward region of ATLAS and located about 480 m from the ATLAS interaction point
Feng:2017uoz
;
FASER:2018eoc
. The detector will be upgraded to FASER2 in the high luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) era. The long-lived particles (LLPs) can be produced by proton-beam bremsstrahlung and the rare decays of mesons, and then decay in the detector. The searches for LLPs such as a dark photon, a dark Higgs as well as the vector boson in
U(1)_{B-L}
model have been simulated at FASER/FASER2
Feng:2017vli
;
FASER:2018eoc
;
Boiarska:2019vid
;
Araki:2020wkq
;
Li:2021rzt
;
Asai:2022zxw
. We expect that the new gauge boson in
U(1)^{\prime}
models with non-universal gauge couplings would lead to distinctive search reaches.

The
U(1)^{\prime}
models also introduce non-standard interactions (NSIs) between neutrinos and other SM fermions via neutral current interactions after integrating out the new gauge boson.
Coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering (CE
\nu
NS)
PhysRevD.9.1389
and precision neutrino oscillation experiments can probe the new physics in such NSIs. The CE
\nu
NS process was first observed by the COHERENT experiment in the Spallation Neutron Source (SNS) at the Oak Ridge National Laboratory
COHERENT:2017ipa
. The neutrinos measured at COHERENT are produced by the decays of stopped pions
and muons through
\pi^{+}\to\mu^{+}\nu_{\mu}
and
\mu^{+}\to e^{+}\nu_{e}\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
. Thus, the COHERENT results would be sensitive to the
U(1)^{\prime}
models with gauge couplings to electron or muon neutrinos
Heeck:2018nzc
;
Flores:2020lji
;
Cadeddu:2020nbr
;
delaVega:2021wpx
;
Cheung:2021tmx
;
AtzoriCorona:2022moj
. Moreover, due to the change of neutrino propagation in matter in terms of neutral current NSIs, the long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiments such as DUNE are also sensitive to probe the NSIs in the
U(1)^{\prime}
models
Flores:2020lji
;
Chatterjee:2021wac
;
Asai:2022zxw
.

In this work, we study general
U(1)^{\prime}
models with non-universal lepton charges. A new scalar singlet is introduced with non-zero
U(1)^{\prime}
charge. After the scalar acquires a vacuum expectation value, the
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry is broken and the masses of the neutral gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
and of Majorana neutrinos are generated. The scalar is mixed with the SM Higgs doublet and there are two CP-even physical Higgses, i.e., the SM-like
h
and
\phi
.
We then consider the sensitivities of above precision experiments to the new particles in three scenarios
Dev:2021qjj
:

•

Z^{\prime}
production and decay with non-universal lepton charges ;

•

\phi
production and decay for
m_{\phi}<2m_{Z^{\prime}}
or
m_{\phi}>2m_{Z^{\prime}}
with
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
open ;

•

Z^{\prime}
production when
m_{\phi}>2m_{Z^{\prime}}
with
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
open .

In the first scenario, the non-universal lepton charges affect the lifetime and decay rates of the gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
. We expect distinctive reaches in the regime of low
m_{Z^{\prime}}
and small gauge couplings for different
U(1)^{\prime}
charge setups. The dimensionless coupling
parameters in the NSIs are scaled as
\epsilon\sim g^{2}_{BL}v^{2}/m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
where

GeV is the electroweak vacuum expectation value and
g_{BL}
is the
U(1)^{\prime}

g_{BL}^{2}/m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
.
In the second scenario with
m_{\phi}<2m_{Z^{\prime}}
, analogous to the dark Higgs case
Feng:2017vli
, the search reach of
\phi
is sensitive to the mixing angle with the SM Higgs boson. However, if
m_{\phi}>2m_{Z^{\prime}}
, the kinematically allowed decay
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
changes the lifetime and decay rates of the scalar
\phi

\phi
depends on additional parameters which are the
Z^{\prime}
mass and the gauge coupling
g_{BL}
. Moreover, with
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
open in the third scenario, additional production modes of the
Z^{\prime}
appear.

3.1
The sensitivity of FASER/FASER2 to the gauge boson
Z^{\prime}

For the gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
, as seen in the last section, both of its mass and its couplings to the SM matter particles are determined by the gauge coupling
g_{BL}
. A very weak coupling
g_{BL}
can induce a light and long-lived
Z^{\prime}
which can be searched for in far-forward experiments such as FASER/FASER2.
Throughout this section, we assume that the scalar can be neglected, either due to its large mass or tiny scalar mixing. We will estimate and comment on the possibly additional
Z^{\prime}
contribution from
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
in Sec.
4.2
.

The major production mechanisms of
Z^{\prime}
bosons at FASER include proton-beam bremsstrahlung as well as the decays of light mesons
Feng:2017uoz
.
The
Z^{\prime}
production cross sections and meson decay widths can be easily obtained
from those of the dark photon
A^{\prime}
with
\varepsilon
being the
\gamma-A^{\prime}
kinetic mixing, as presented in the darkcast package
Ilten:2018crw
.
The approximate ratio of the bremsstrahlung cross sections is

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(pZ\to pZZ^{\prime})}{\sigma(pZ\to pZA^{\prime})}\approx\frac{g_{BL}^{2}(2Q^{\prime}_{u}+Q^{\prime}_{d})^{2}}{(\varepsilon e)^{2}}\;,

(20)

where
Q^{\prime}_{u}=Q^{\prime}_{d}=1/3
in our setup.
The general decay width ratios of
\pi^{0}
and
\eta
are then given by

\displaystyle{\Gamma(\pi^{0}\to Z^{\prime}\gamma)\over\Gamma(\pi^{0}\to A^{\prime}\gamma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big({g_{BL}\over\varepsilon e}\Big)^{2}{|(Q^{\prime}_{u}-Q^{\prime}_{d}){\rm BW}_{\rho}+3(Q^{\prime}_{u}+Q^{\prime}_{d}){\rm BW}_{\omega}|^{2}\over|{\rm BW}_{\rho}+{\rm BW}_{\omega}|^{2}}\;,

(21)

\displaystyle{\Gamma(\eta\to Z^{\prime}\gamma)\over\Gamma(\eta\to A^{\prime}\gamma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big({g_{BL}\over\varepsilon e}\Big)^{2}{|9(Q^{\prime}_{u}-Q^{\prime}_{d}){\rm BW}_{\rho}+3(Q^{\prime}_{u}+Q^{\prime}_{d}){\rm BW}_{\omega}+6Q^{\prime}_{s}{\rm BW}_{\phi}|^{2}\over|9{\rm BW}_{\rho}+{\rm BW}_{\omega}-2{\rm BW}_{\phi}|^{2}}\;,

(22)

where
{\rm BW}_{V}
denotes the Breit-Wigner form factors
Tulin:2014tya
;
Ilten:2018crw
for vector mesons
V=\rho,\omega,\phi
in the framework of vector meson dominance (VMD)
Fujiwara:1984mp
. In FASER detectors, the gauge boson can decay into all kinematically accessible states. The
Z^{\prime}
with a mass smaller than
2m_{\tau}
mainly decays into neutrinos,
e^{+}e^{-}
,
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and light hadrons.
The partial width of the
Z^{\prime}
decay into SM final states is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(Z^{\prime}\to f\bar{f})={C_{f}(g_{BL}Q^{\prime}_{f})^{2}\over 12\pi}m_{Z^{\prime}}\left(1+{2m_{f}^{2}\over m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\sqrt{1-{4m_{f}^{2}\over m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}}\;,

(23)

where the color factor is
C_{f}=1
for charged leptons, 3 for quarks, and
1/2
for active neutrinos. Here we assume the right-handed neutrinos are heavy enough to decouple. The VMD approach was also used to estimate the decay width for specific hadronic final states as stated in Ref.
Ilten:2018crw
.

3.2
The search reach of COHERENT and neutrino oscillation for
Z^{\prime}

While FASER and FASER2 are far-forward detectors which are sensitive to long-lived particles and thus small gauge couplings
g_{BL}
, COHERENT and neutrino oscillation experiments probe larger gauge couplings
g_{BL}
. Thus COHERENT and neutrino oscillation experiments provide complementary sensitivity to FASER and other far-forward detectors.

3.2.1
COHERENT

In order to account for the latest data release on the measurement of coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering (CE
\nu
NS) in CsI by COHERENT
COHERENT:2021xmm
, we employ the Poissonian least-squares function

\chi^{2}=2\sum_{i=2}^{9}\sum_{j=1}^{11}\left[N^{\text{th}}_{ij}-N^{\text{exp}}_{ij}+N^{\text{exp}}_{ij}\ln\frac{N^{\text{exp}}_{ij}}{N^{\text{th}}_{ij}}\right]+\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\sigma_{0}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{3}}{\sigma_{3}}\right)^{2}

(24)

with

N^{\text{th}}_{ij}=(1+\alpha_{0})N^{\text{CE$\nu$NS}}_{ij}(\alpha_{4},\alpha_{6},\alpha_{7})+(1+\alpha_{3})N^{\text{SSB}}_{ij}

(25)

as well as
\sigma_{0}=0.1145
and
\sigma_{3}=0.021
. For more information on how to obtain the bin-wise signal prediction
N^{\text{CE$\nu$NS}}_{ij}
from integrating over the relevant differential cross sections and flux distributions, we refer the reader to Ref.
DeRomeri:2022twg
, including the references therein, that our analysis is closely aligned with. Several systematic uncertainties are captured via the nuisance parameters
\alpha_{i}
, the numerical values of which are unknown a priori and thus must be fitted from the data. The above indicates that we only include the beam-uncorrelated steady-state background (SSB) which constitutes over 98% of the background as is reported in
COHERENT:2021xmm
.
We also follow their distribution into energy bins, expressed in photoelectrons, with the endpoints

E\sim(8,12,16,20,24,32,40,48,60)~\text{PE}

(26)

and recoil time bins with the endpoints

t_{\text{rec}}\sim(0,0.125,0.25,0.375,0.5,0.625,0.75,0.875,1,2,4,6)~\mu\text{s}.

(27)

We do not include the lowest-energy bin
E\sim(0,8)~\text{PE}
in the analysis, because the used detector efficiency function is negative in that region. The contribution from the
Z^{\prime}
boson to CE
\nu
NS is induced via a modification of the effective weak charge of the nucleus (see for instance Ref.
AtzoriCorona:2022moj
),

\displaystyle Q_{\nu,k}

\displaystyle=Q^{\text{SM}}_{i,k}+Q^{Z^{\prime}}_{i,k}=g_{p,i}Z_{0,k}+g_{n,i}N_{0,k}+\frac{g_{BL}^{2}Q^{\prime}_{i}\left(Z_{0,k}+N_{0,k}\right)}{\sqrt{2}G_{F}\left(|\mathbf{q}|^{2}+m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\right)}

(28)

with the three neutrino species
\nu_{i}\in\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\overline{\nu}_{\mu}
and the two nuclei
k=\text{Cs},\text{I}
, and
\mathbf{q}
denotes the 3-momentum of the virtual
Z^{\prime}
boson.
We have explicitly checked that the contributions from neutrino-electron scattering are entirely negligible in the considered mass range
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 0.01
GeV.
The contour lines visible in Figs.
2
,
3
and
4
arise from bounding the profile
\Delta\chi^{2}\equiv\chi^{2}-\chi^{2}_{\text{min}}
from above, where for each (fixed) set of model parameters (
Q^{\prime}_{e},Q^{\prime}_{\mu},g_{BL},m_{Z^{\prime}}
) we choose the nuisance parameters
\alpha_{i}
such that
\chi^{2}
is minimised, and
\chi^{2}_{\text{min}}
is the (global) minimum value taken when both the model parameters and the nuisance parameters are optimised simultaneously. The confidence level is set to 90%. As can be seen from Eq. (
28
), there is a degeneracy in the model parameters since we are not sensitive to the gauge coupling and charges individually, but only to the product
g_{BL}^{2}Q_{i}^{\prime}
.

For the
Z^{\prime}
mass and gauge coupling fixed, we find that the following two non-SM-like cases are generally in good agreement with the COHERENT data: (i)
Q_{\mu}^{\prime}>0
for
|Q_{\mu}^{\prime}|
large, and (ii)
Q_{e}^{\prime}<0
for
|Q_{e}^{\prime}|
large and, if
Q_{\mu}^{\prime}<0
,
|Q_{\mu}^{\prime}|
not too large, as can be seen in the bottom plot of Fig.
3
. We interpret this as an indication that besides SM-like cases, there is also a preference for negative corrections to
Q_{i,k}^{\text{SM}}
.
In the
U(1)_{B-L}
model, for a range of
Z^{\prime}
masses this can be realised if the
g_{BL}
gauge coupling becomes large enough. Indeed, the lower tip of a non-excluded region for
g_{BL}\gtrsim 10^{-3}
is visible the top-left plot of Fig.
2
. This is in agreement with Fig.
4
which demonstrates that for the benchmark case
Q_{\mu}^{\prime}=-1
and a moderately large
|Q_{e}^{\prime}|
, a specific region of larger
g_{BL}

can exist that is less constrained than
g_{BL}

smaller. The
U(1)_{B-3L_{e}-L_{\mu}+L_{\tau}}
model represents a more extreme case to which COHERENT does not provide any sensitivity and thus there is no constraint at all. Lastly, we comment that the slope of the lower bound of the excluded region for the
U(1)_{B-L}
,
U(1)_{B-3L_{\mu}}
and
U(1)_{B-L_{e}-3L_{\mu}+L_{\tau}}
models approaches a constant value for larger
Z^{\prime}
masses, consistent with the expectation that the contribution to CE
\nu
NS will approximately scale like
g_{BL}^{2}/m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
in that regime.

3.2.2
Neutrino oscillation experiments

The Hamiltonian for neutrino propagation in matter
in the presence of a
U(1)^{\prime}
model becomes

\displaystyle H={1\over 2E}U\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&\delta m_{21}^{2}&0\\
0&0&\delta m_{31}^{2}\\
\end{array}\right)U^{\dagger}+V_{CC}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1+\epsilon_{ee}&\epsilon_{e\mu}&\epsilon_{e\tau}\\
\epsilon_{e\mu}^{\ast}&\epsilon_{\mu\mu}&\epsilon_{\mu\tau}\\
\epsilon_{e\tau}^{\ast}&\epsilon_{\mu\tau}^{\ast}&\epsilon_{\tau\tau}\\
\end{array}\right)\;,

where
U
is the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata (PMNS) matrix,
V_{CC}=\sqrt{2}G_{F}N_{e}
is the standard matter potential, and
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{f}\epsilon^{f}_{\alpha\beta}N_{f}/N_{e}
with
N_{f}
being the number density of fermions
f=u,d
and
e
. The quark number densities satisfy

\displaystyle N_{u}=2N_{p}+N_{n}\;,~~N_{d}=N_{p}+2N_{n}\;,

(36)

where
N_{p}
and
N_{n}
are proton and neutron number densities, respectively, and the matter neutrality implies
N_{p}=N_{e}
.
The diagonal parameters of non-standard neutrino interactions (NSI) are given by
Han:2019zkz

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{f}={g_{BL}^{2}Q^{\prime}_{\alpha}Q^{\prime}_{f}\over\sqrt{2}G_{F}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\;,

(37)

where
\alpha
denotes charged lepton flavor.
Note that there are no off-diagonal terms, since the theory is defined in the basis where the charged lepton masses are diagonal.
As a result, in our general
U(1)^{\prime}
models, the diagonal components of the matter part in the Hamiltonian are

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\alpha\alpha}={g_{BL}^{2}\over\sqrt{2}G_{F}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\Big[Q^{\prime}_{\alpha}Q^{\prime}_{e}+Q^{\prime}_{\alpha}Q^{\prime}_{q}\Big(3+3{N_{n}\over N_{e}}\Big)\Big]\;,

(38)

where
N_{n}/N_{e}\approx 1.051
in Earth matter.

In Table
2
, we show the
2\sigma
allowed ranges for the diagonal NSI parameters from the simulation of future long-baseline experiments DUNE and T2HK
Liao:2016orc

Han:2019zkz

\epsilon
parameters. Thus, there is no bound on the

\epsilon^{e}_{\alpha\alpha}
, only quark couplings

, or components
\epsilon_{\alpha\alpha}
from a global analysis including CE
\nu
NS data within an effective theory and thus only applicable for
m_{Z^{\prime}}>50

5
Conclusions

We study the general
U(1)^{\prime}
models with non-universal lepton charges. A scalar singlet is introduced with non-zero
U(1)^{\prime}
charge to spontaneously break the
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge symmetry. This model with small masses and couplings can be tested in precision experiments such as FASER/FASER2, COHERENT and the long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiments DUNE and T2HK.
We explore the sensitivities of above precision experiments to the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
and the new CP-even scalar
\phi
. Our main findings are the following:

•

With non-universal lepton charges, distinctive reaches emerge in the regime of low
m_{Z^{\prime}}
and small gauge coupling for different
U(1)^{\prime}
charge setups. The COHERENT experiment and the future long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiments DUNE and T2HK also provide complementary sensitivities.

•

For
m_{\phi}<2m_{Z^{\prime}}
, the search for the scalar
\phi
at FASER/FASER2 is sensitive to the mixing angle between the scalar singlet and the SM Higgs, which is analogous to the dark Higgs search.

•

In the case of
m_{\phi}>2m_{Z^{\prime}}
, the kinematically allowed decay
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
changes the lifetime and decay rates of the scalar
\phi
. The probe reach highly depends the
Z^{\prime}
mass and the gauge coupling
g_{BL}
as additional parameters.
With
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
open, an additional production mode of
Z^{\prime}
appears which is however subdominant to other production modes.

\displaystyle y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=-2Q^{\prime}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&*\\
0&*&0\\
*&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\;,

\displaystyle y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=2Q^{\prime}_{\mu}))

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&*\\
\end{pmatrix}\;,

(65)

For the most general case with
Q^{\prime}_{e}\neq Q^{\prime}_{\mu}\neq Q^{\prime}_{\tau}
, we have the Yukawa matrices

\displaystyle y^{D}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}*&0&0\\
0&*&0\\
0&0&*\\
\end{pmatrix}\;,

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\;,

(66)

\displaystyle y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=-(Q^{\prime}_{e}+Q^{\prime}_{\tau}))

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&*\\
0&0&0\\
*&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\;,

\displaystyle y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=-2Q^{\prime}_{e})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}*&0&0\\

\end{pmatrix}\;,

(67)

with the ranks as

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}[y^{D}]=3\;,~\mathrm{rank}[M]=0\;,~\mathrm{rank}[y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=-(Q^{\prime}_{e}+Q^{\prime}_{\tau}))]=2\;,~\mathrm{rank}[y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=-2Q^{\prime}_{e})]=1\;,

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}[M+y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=-(Q^{\prime}_{e}+Q^{\prime}_{\tau}))]=2\;,~\mathrm{rank}[M+y^{M}(Q^{\prime}_{\Phi}=-2Q^{\prime}_{e})]=1\;.

(68)

Again, the additional scalar fields are needed to give a full rank mass matrix.

It turns out that adding one scalar singlet can lead to three massive light neutrinos and no zeros in the neutrino mass matrix in the case with one non-zero lepton charge. In other non-universal models, additional scalar fields are needed to generate a full rank mass matrix and remove the zero entries in the neutrino mass matrix. Alternatively, we could generate the missing entries in the Dirac neutrino Yukawa matrix. The cases with one or two independent zeros were also discussed in the literature, where either one or two scalar singlets are needed. To summarize, it is quite arbitrary to include the scalar singlets breaking the non-universal gauge symmetry to give the allowed PMNS matrix.

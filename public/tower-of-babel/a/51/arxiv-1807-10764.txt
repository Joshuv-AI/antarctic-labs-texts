Power Corrections for N-Jettiness Subtractions at 𝒪 ⁡ ( 𝜶 𝒔 ) {\cal O}(\alpha_{s})\!\! Preprint: MIT-CTP 5007 DESY 18-120
Markus A. Ebert, Ian Moult, Iain W. Stewart, Frank J. Tackmann, Gherardo Vita, and Hua Xing Zhu

Abstract
We continue the study of power corrections for N N -jettiness subtractions by analytically computing the complete next-to-leading power corrections at 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s ) {\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s}) for color-singlet production. This includes all nonlogarithmic terms and all partonic channels for Drell-Yan and gluon-fusion Higgs production. These terms are important to further improve the numerical performance of the subtractions, and to better understand the structure of power corrections beyond their leading logarithms, in particular their universality. We emphasize the importance of computing the power corrections differential in both the invariant mass, Q Q , and rapidity, Y Y , of the color-singlet system, which is necessary to account for the rapidity dependence in the subtractions. This also clarifies apparent disagreements in the literature. Performing a detailed numerical study, we find excellent agreement of our analytic results with a previous numerical extraction.

2
N-Jettiness Subtractions, Definitions and Notation

In this section we briefly review
N
-jettiness subtractions
Boughezal:2015dva
;
Gaunt:2015pea
in the context of color-singlet production, and discuss the structure of the power corrections to the subtraction scheme. This also allows us to define the notation that will be used in the rest of this paper. For a detailed discussion, we refer the reader to ref.
Gaunt:2015pea
.

To compute a cross section for color-singlet production
\sigma(X)
, where
X
denotes some set of cuts on the Born phase space, we write the cross section as an integral over the differential cross section in the resolution variable
\mathcal{T}_{0}

\displaystyle\sigma(X)

\displaystyle=\sigma(X,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}})+\int_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma(X)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}}\,,

(1)

where

\displaystyle\sigma(X,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle\equiv\int^{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma(X)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}}\,.

(2)

For a general measure, the 0-jettiness
\mathcal{T}_{0}
can be defined as
Stewart:2010pd
;
Jouttenus:2011wh

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0}=\sum_{i}\min\biggl\{\frac{2q_{a}\cdot k_{i}}{Q_{a}}\,,\frac{2q_{b}\cdot k_{i}}{Q_{b}}\biggr\}\,,

(3)

where the sum runs over all hadronic momenta
k_{i}
in the final state.
Here,
q_{a,b}
are projected Born momenta (referred to as label momenta in SCET), which are given in terms of the
total leptonic invariant mass
Q
and rapidity
Y
as

\displaystyle q_{a}^{\mu}=x_{a}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}=Qe^{Y}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}\,,\qquad q_{b}^{\mu}=x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}=Qe^{-Y}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}\,,

(4)

where

n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)\,,\quad\bar{n}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,-1)

(5)

are lightlike vectors along the beam directions.
The choice in eq. (
4
) corresponds to parameterizing the Born phase space in terms of
Q
and
Y
,
and this choice already enters in the leading-power factorization theorem, where the beam functions
are evaluated at
x_{a,b}=Qe^{\pm Y}
.

The
Q_{a,b}
measures in eq. (
3
) determine the different definitions of 0-jettiness.
Two different definitions, originally introduced in refs.
Stewart:2009yx
;
Berger:2010xi

as beam thrust, are the leptonic and hadronic definitions given by

leptonic:

\displaystyle Q_{a}

\displaystyle=Q_{b}=Q\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0}^{\mathrm{lep}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\min\biggl\{\frac{x_{a}E_{\mathrm{cm}}}{Q}n\cdot k_{i}\,,\,\frac{x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}}{Q}\bar{n}\cdot k_{i}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\min\biggl\{e^{Y}n\cdot k_{i}\,,\,e^{-Y}\bar{n}\cdot k_{i}\biggr\}

hadronic:

\displaystyle Q_{a,b}

\displaystyle=x_{a,b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0}^{\mathrm{cm}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\min\Bigl\{n\cdot k_{i}\,,\,\bar{n}\cdot k_{i}\Bigr\}\,.

(6)

It has been shown
Moult:2016fqy
that the power corrections for the hadronic definition are poorly behaved, and grow exponentially with rapidity, while the
e^{\pm Y}
factor in the measure for the leptonic definition exactly avoids this effect.

For later convenience, we write the dimensionful and dimensionless
0
-jettiness resolution variables
in terms of a
Y
-dependent parameter
\rho(Y)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0}^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\min\Bigl\{\rho_{x}\,k_{i}^{+},\rho_{x}^{-1}\,k_{i}^{-}\Bigr\}\,,\qquad\tau^{x}\equiv\frac{\mathcal{T}_{0}^{x}}{Q}\,,

(7)

with

leptonic:

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{lep}}=e^{Y}\,,\qquad\tau^{\mathrm{lep}}=\frac{\mathcal{T}_{0}^{\mathrm{lep}}}{Q}\,,

hadronic:

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}=1\,,\qquad\tau^{\mathrm{cm}}=\frac{\mathcal{T}_{0}^{\mathrm{cm}}}{Q}\,.

(8)

In the following, we will mostly drop the subscript
0
on
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, since there should be no cause for confusion that our results are for 0-jettiness.
For generic results that apply to both the leptonic and hadronic definitions we also drop the superscript and simply use
\mathcal{T}
and
\tau
, keeping a generic parameter
\rho
when necessary.

To implement the
N
-jettiness subtractions, we now add and subtract a subtraction term to the cross section (suppressing the dependence on the Born measurements
X
for simplicity)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathcal{T}_{{\mathrm{cut}}})+\int_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}}+\bigl[\sigma(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}})-\sigma^{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}})\bigr]

\displaystyle\equiv\sigma^{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}})+\int_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}_{0}}+\Delta\sigma(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}})\,.

(9)

Since
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is a zero jet resolution variable, for
\tau=\mathcal{T}_{0}/Q\to 0
we can expand the differential cross section
\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}\tau
and its cumulative
\sigma(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})
about the soft and collinear limits from
\tau\to 0
and
\tau_{\mathrm{cut}}\to 0
as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(4)}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\dotsb\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\sigma(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{(0)}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})+\sigma^{(2)}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})+\sigma^{(4)}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})+\dotsb\,.

Here
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}/\mathrm{d}\tau
and
\sigma^{(0)}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})
contain all leading-power terms,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\sim\delta(\tau)+\biggl[\frac{\ln^{j}\tau}{\tau}\biggr]_{+}\,,\qquad\sigma^{(0)}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})\sim\ln^{j}\tau_{\mathrm{cut}}\,.

(11)

These terms must be included in the subtraction term to obtain a finite result, namely

\sigma^{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}})=\sigma^{(0)}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cut}}/Q)\,[1+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})]\,.

(12)

The further terms in the series expansion in eq. (
10
) are suppressed by powers of
\tau

\displaystyle\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2k)}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{O}(\tau^{k}\ln^{j}\tau)\,,\qquad\sigma^{(2k)}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})\sim\mathcal{O}(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}}^{k}\ln^{j}\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})\,.

(13)

While these terms with
k\geq 1
do not need to be included in the subtraction term, the size of the neglected term,
\Delta\sigma(\tau_{\mathrm{cut}})
is determined by the leading-power corrections that are left out of
\sigma^{\rm sub}
. Therefore, including additional power corrections in
\sigma^{\rm sub}
can significantly improve the performance of the subtraction. Indeed, general scaling arguments imply that up to an order of magnitude in performance can be gained for each subleading power logarithm that is included in the subtractions
Gaunt:2015pea
. For the leading logarithms, this was explicitly confirmed for most partonic channels in the numerical studies in refs.
Moult:2016fqy
;
Moult:2017jsg
. Here, we extend the calculation to the NLL terms at
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s})
, which yields the nonlogarithmic terms, hence giving the complete NLP result. The remaining NLO power corrections then scale at worst as
\alpha_{s}\tau_{{\mathrm{cut}}}^{2}\log\tau_{{\mathrm{cut}}}
, and will be very small, as we will see in our numerical studies.

3
Born Measurements at Subleading Power

We begin by discussing in some detail the treatment of the Born measurements,
Q^{2}
and
Y
, which plays an important role at subleading powers. We will use the soft and collinear expansions from SCET, which provide a convenient language when discussing the power expansion of QCD amplitudes at fixed order. We will not need to employ any of the field theory technology from SCET for our analysis here.

3.1
General Setup and Notation

Consider the production of a color-singlet final state
L
of fixed invariant mass
Q
and rapidity
Y
,
together with an arbitrary measurement
\mathcal{T}

that only acts on hadronic radiation and gives
\mathcal{T}=0
at Born level.
Since the observable
\mathcal{T}
resolves soft and collinear emissions
it will induce large logarithms
\ln(\mathcal{T}/Q)
.
Our goal is to expand the cross section in
\mathcal{T}
(or
\tau=\mathcal{T}/Q
)
in order to systematically understand its logarithmic structure.

Consider proton-proton scattering with the underlying partonic process

\displaystyle a(p_{a})+b(p_{b})\to L(p_{1},\cdots)+X(k_{1},\cdots)\,,

(14)

where
L
is the leptonic (color-singlet) final state and
X
denotes additional QCD radiation.
Its cross section reads

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\mathrm{d}\zeta_{a}\mathrm{d}\zeta_{b}\,\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{2}}\int\!\biggl(\prod_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(2\pi)\delta_{+}(k_{i}^{2})\biggr)\int\!\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b};\{k_{i}\},q)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{(d)}(p_{a}+p_{b}-k-q)\,\delta(Q^{2}-q^{2})\,\delta\biggl(Y-\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{q^{-}}{q^{+}}\biggr)\,\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k_{i}\})\bigr]\,.

(15)

Here, the incoming momenta are given by

\displaystyle p_{a}^{\mu}=\zeta_{a}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}\,,\qquad p_{b}^{\mu}=\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}\,,

(16)

k=\sum_{i}k_{i}
is the total outgoing hadronic momentum, and
q
is the total leptonic momentum.
Since our measurements are not sensitive to the details of the leptonic final state,
we have absorbed the leptonic phase space integral into the matrix element,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b};\{k_{i}\},q)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{L}(q)\,|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b};\{k_{i}\},\{p_{j}\})|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Phi_{L}(q)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}p_{j}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(2\pi)\delta_{+}(p_{j}^{2}-m_{j}^{2})\,(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{(d)}\Bigl(q-\sum_{j}p_{j}\Bigr)\,.

(17)

The matrix element
{\mathcal{M}}
contains the renormalization scale
\mu^{2\epsilon}
,
which as always is associated with the renormalized coupling
\alpha_{s}(\mu)
, and may also contain virtual corrections.
For now the measurement function
\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k_{i}\})
is kept arbitrary.

We can now solve the
Q^{2}
and
Y
measurements to fix the incoming momenta as

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\biggl(k^{-}+e^{+Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+k_{T}^{2}}\biggr)\,,

\displaystyle\zeta_{b}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\biggl(k^{+}+e^{-Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+k_{T}^{2}}\biggr)\,.

(18)

Taking the Jacobian factors from solving the
\delta
functions into account, eq. (
3.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int\!\biggl(\prod_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(2\pi)\delta_{+}(k_{i}^{2})\biggr)\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}A(Q,Y;\{k_{i}\})\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k_{i}\})\bigr]\,,

(19)

where we defined

\displaystyle A(Q,Y;\{k_{i}\})\equiv|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b},\{k_{i}\},q=p_{a}+p_{b}-k)|^{2}

(20)

to stress that the squared matrix element only depends on the Born measurements
Q
and
Y
,
which fix the incoming momenta through eqs. (
16
) and (
3.1
),
and the emission momenta
k_{i}
.
Note that we have left implicit in our notation in eq. (
19
) the dependence of
\zeta_{a,b}
on
k
through eq. (
3.1
).
They are restricted to
\zeta_{a,b}\in[0,1]
, which is implicit in the support of the proton PDFs.

3.2
Power Expansion in Soft and Collinear Limits

Instead of solving the
\mathcal{T}
measurement function to express (some of the)
k_{i}
in terms of
\mathcal{T}
, we find that a convenient strategy to organize the expansion in
\mathcal{T}
is to multipole expand the final state momenta.
At this stage we need only assume that
\mathcal{T}
is a
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
observable,
which is true for many definitions of
N
-jettiness. For such observables, it is known from
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
that
we can organize the cross section in terms of a power counting parameter
\lambda\sim\sqrt{\tau}
.
All momenta
k_{i}
can then be categorized as either collinear or soft modes
(since we work in
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
these are often called ultrasoft, although we will not make this distinction),
whose momenta scale as

\displaystyle n{-}\text{collinear}:\quad k_{n}\sim Q\,(\lambda^{2},1,\lambda)\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\bar{n}{-}\text{collinear}:\quad k_{\bar{n}}\sim Q\,(1,\lambda^{2},\lambda)\,,

\displaystyle\text{soft}:\hskip 51.21504ptk_{s}\sim Q\,(\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2})\,,

where we decomposed each momentum into lightcone coordinates

k^{\mu}=k^{-}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}+k^{+}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}+k_{\perp}^{\mu}\equiv(k^{+},k^{-},k_{\perp})\,.

(22)

Here
n
and
\bar{n}
are lightlike vectors satisfying
n\cdot\bar{n}=2
. The components of the momenta that scale like
\lambda^{2}
are referred to as residual momenta.
The soft momenta are homogeneous, and have purely residual scaling. Overlap between the soft and collinear modes occurring in integrals over final state momenta is removed by the zero-bin subtraction procedure
Manohar:2006nz
.

The benefit of this decomposition is that it allows one to expand eq. (
19
) in
\lambda
,
agnostic of the actual measurement
\mathcal{T}
.
The LP result is then simply obtained by expanding the cross section through
\lambda^{0}
,
the NLP result by expanding through
\lambda^{2}
, etc.
Note that when performing this expansion, all other factors, such as
Q,E_{\rm cm}\sim\lambda^{0}
.

While the expansion of the matrix element is of course process dependent,
we can give general expressions for the incoming momentum fractions eq. (
3.1
),
independent of the process and observable
\mathcal{T}
.
If
k
is a soft momentum, then the expansion required at NLP is given by

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{a}\biggl(1+\frac{k^{-}e^{-Y}}{Q}\biggr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,,

\displaystyle\zeta_{b}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{b}\biggl(1+\frac{k^{+}e^{+Y}}{Q}\biggr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,,

(23)

where we factored out the Born momentum fractions

\displaystyle x_{a}=\frac{Qe^{+Y}}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\,,\qquad x_{b}=\frac{Qe^{-Y}}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\,.

(24)

In the
n
-collinear limit, we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{a}\biggl[\biggl(1+\frac{k^{-}e^{-Y}}{Q}\biggr)+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\biggr]+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,,

\displaystyle\zeta_{b}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{b}\biggl[1+\biggl(\frac{k^{+}e^{+Y}}{Q}+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\biggr)\biggr]+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,.

(25)

For clarity, we have grouped terms of the same power counting in round brackets.
Similarly, one can obtain the
\bar{n}
-collinear limit,
or any combination as might appear when combining multiple emissions.

4
Master Formula for Power Corrections to Next-to-Leading Power

In this section we derive a master formula for the NLP corrections.
This formula applies to any
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
observable in color-singlet production.
In sec.
5
, we will apply it to derive explicit results for Drell-Yan and gluon-fusion Higgs production.

4.1
General Setup for Color-Singlet
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
Observables

For reference, we start with the LO cross section for the production
of a color-singlet final state
L
of invariant mass
Q^{2}
and rapidity
Y
,
together with an (up to now arbitrary) measurement
\mathcal{T}
acting only on hadronic radiation,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mathrm{LO}}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,\delta(\mathcal{T})\,,

(26)

where
x_{a,b}=\frac{Q}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}e^{\pm Y}
and
A^{\mathrm{LO}}
is the squared matrix element
in the Born kinematics, see eq. (
20
).
For future reference, we also define the LO partonic cross section,
\hat{\sigma}^{\rm LO}(Q,Y)
, by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mathrm{LO}}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,f_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\,\delta(\mathcal{T})\,,\qquad\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)=\frac{A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,.

(27)

Next, consider an additional real emission to the Born process.
Eq. (
19
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,(2\pi)\delta_{+}(k^{2})\,\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,A(Q,Y;\{k\})\,\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]\,,

(28)

where we remind the reader that the incoming momenta
p_{a,b}
are given by eq. (
3.1
),

\displaystyle p_{a}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{a}(k)E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}=\biggl(k^{-}+e^{+Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+k_{T}^{2}}\biggr)\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle p_{b}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{b}(k)E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}=\biggl(k^{+}+e^{-Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+k_{T}^{2}}\biggr)\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}\,.

(29)

From these solutions, we see the interesting feature that at subleading power, regardless of the type of final-state emission, the momenta entering both PDFs are modified.

Since we do not measure the azimuthal angle of
k
, it can be integrated over,

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,(2\pi)\delta_{+}(k^{2})=\frac{\Omega_{2-2\epsilon}}{4(2\pi)^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon}}=\frac{(4\pi)^{-2+\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon}}\,.

(30)

Eq. (
28

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon}}\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{(4\pi)^{2}2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\,A(Q,Y;\{k\})\,\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]\,.

(31)

So far, this expression is still exact.
In the next step, we wish to expand the NLO cross section in
\lambda\sim\mathcal{T}/Q
.
When
\mathcal{T}
is a
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
observable, we can use the EFT knowledge from
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
to expand the momentum
k
in both collinear and soft limits, as discussed in sec.
3
.

At NLP, we need to expand eq. (
31
) consistently through
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
.
The
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
power corrections arise from the following sources:

•

The incoming momenta
\zeta_{a,b}
: While collinear and soft limits yield
quite different power expansions, both give a well-defined expansion in
\lambda

We thus simply define the expansion

\displaystyle\zeta_{a,b}=x_{a,b}\biggl[\frac{1}{z_{a,b}}+\Delta_{a,b}^{(2)}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\biggr]\,,

(32)

where
z_{a,b}\sim\lambda^{0}
and
\Delta_{a,b}^{(2)}\sim\lambda^{2}
.
We have pulled out the Born momentum fractions
x_{a,b}
and written
1/z_{a,b}
as a fraction
for later convenience.
Explicit expressions can be obtained from eqs. (
3.2
) and (
3.2
),
and will be given below.

•

PDFs: Since the momenta
\zeta_{a,b}
enter the PDFs, these also have to be power expanded,

\displaystyle f_{a,b}(\zeta_{a,b})

\displaystyle=f_{a,b}\biggl(\frac{x_{a,b}}{z_{a,b}}\biggr)+x_{a,b}\Delta_{a,b}^{(2)}\,f^{\prime}_{a,b}\biggl(\frac{x_{a,b}}{z_{a,b}}\biggr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})

\displaystyle\equiv f_{a,b}+x_{a,b}\Delta_{a,b}^{(2)}\,f^{\prime}_{a,b}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,.

(33)

•

Flux factor: Similar to the PDFs, we have to expand the flux factor

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}}=\frac{z_{a}z_{b}}{x_{a}x_{b}}\biggl[1-z_{a}\Delta^{(2)}-z_{b}\Delta^{(2)}_{b}\biggr]\,.

(34)

•

Matrix element: The expansion of the matrix element depends both on the process and the considered limit.
Here, we define the LP and NLP expansions by

\displaystyle A(Q,Y;\{k\})=A^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\}))+A^{(2)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+\cdots\,.

(35)

In the soft limit
A^{(0)}\sim\lambda^{-4}
and
A^{(2)}\sim\lambda^{-2}

while in the collinear limit
A^{(0)}\sim\lambda^{-2}
and
A^{(2)}\sim\lambda^{0}

, which is why the soft and collinear corrections enter at the same order.

For example, for a
2\to 2
process, the matrix element can be written in terms
of the Mandelstam variables

\displaystyle s_{ab}

\displaystyle=2p_{a}\cdot p_{b}=\frac{Q^{2}}{z_{a}z_{b}}\left[1+z_{a}\Delta^{(2)}_{a}+z_{b}\Delta^{(2)}_{b}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\right]\,,

\displaystyle s_{ak}

\displaystyle=-2p_{a}\cdot k=-k^{+}Qe^{+Y}\left(\frac{1}{z_{a}}+\Delta^{(2)}_{a}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\right)\,,

\displaystyle s_{bk}

\displaystyle=-2p_{b}\cdot k=-k^{-}Qe^{-Y}\left(\frac{1}{z_{b}}+\Delta^{(2)}_{b}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\right)\,.

(36)

Since these terms now have a definite power counting, one can simply insert eq. (
•
‣
4.1
)
into
A(Q,Y;\{k\})
and expand to the required order in
\lambda
.

•

Measurement: Depending on the observable, the measurement function
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
may also
receive power corrections. Since
0
-jettiness is defined in terms of
n
,
\bar{n}
,
Q
and

\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]
.

Inserting all these expansions into eq. (
31

{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon}}\frac{z_{a}z_{b}\,f_{a}f_{b}}{(4\pi)^{2}2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}A^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})\,\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]

(37)

and the NLP master formula

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon}}\frac{z_{a}z_{b}}{(4\pi)^{2}2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigl\{A^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})\Bigl[f_{a}f_{b}\Bigl(-z_{a}\Delta_{a}^{(2)}-z_{b}\Delta_{b}^{(2)}\Bigr)+x_{a}\Delta_{a}^{(2)}f^{\prime}_{a}f_{b}+x_{b}\Delta_{b}^{(2)}f_{a}f^{\prime}_{b}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad+f_{a}f_{b}\,A^{(2)}(Q,Y;\{k\})\Bigr\}\,,

(38)

where
z_{a,b}\equiv z_{a,b}(k)
and
\Delta_{a,b}^{(2)}\equiv\Delta_{a,b}^{(2)}(k)
are defined by eq. (
32

\mathcal{T}
.
Although the focus of this paper is on the power corrections,
in appendix

term together with the square bracketed factor on the second line of eq. (

term.
We will discuss this in more detail in sec.

.

4.3
Soft Master Formula for 0-Jettiness

The expansion of the incoming momenta
\zeta_{a,b}
for a soft emission

k\sim(\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2})
has been given in eq. (

),

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{a}\biggl(1+\frac{k^{-}e^{-Y}}{Q}\biggr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,,

\displaystyle\zeta_{b}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{b}\biggl(1+\frac{k^{+}e^{+Y}}{Q}\biggr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,,

(48)

so the explicit expressions for the expansion eq. (
32
) are

\displaystyle z_{a}

\displaystyle=1\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}_{a}=\frac{k^{-}e^{-Y}}{Q}\,,

\displaystyle z_{b}

\displaystyle=1\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}_{b}=\frac{k^{+}e^{+Y}}{Q}\,.

(49)

Plugging back into eq. (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{s}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]\biggl\{\frac{1}{Q}A^{(0)}(Q,Y,\{k\})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\biggl[f_{a}\,f_{b}\,(-k^{-}e^{-Y}-k^{+}e^{+Y})+k^{-}e^{-Y}\,x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}\,f_{b}+k^{+}e^{+Y}\,f_{a}\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+f_{a}\,f_{b}\,A^{(2)}(Q,Y,\{k\})\biggr\}\,.

(50)

Here, the measurement is given by eq. (
39
),

\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(k)\bigr]=\Theta(\rho k^{+}-\rho^{-1}k^{-})\delta(\mathcal{T}-\rho^{-1}k^{-})+\Theta(\rho^{-1}k^{-}-\rho k^{+})\delta(\mathcal{T}-\rho k^{+})\,.

(51)

We can further simplify eq. (

) by utilizing the fact that

and

or

\displaystyle A^{(0)}(Q,Y,\{k\})=\frac{\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)}{k^{+}k^{-}}\,,\quad A^{(2)}(Q,Y,\{k\})=\frac{\overline{A}_{+}^{(2)}(Q,Y)}{k^{+}}+\frac{\overline{A}_{-}^{(2)}(Q,Y)}{k^{-}}\,.

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{s}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{\mathcal{T}^{-2\epsilon}}{Q}\frac{1-2\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\,\biggl\{\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\biggl[f_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\biggl(-\frac{\rho}{e^{Y}}-\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggr)+\frac{\rho}{e^{Y}}\,x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})+\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\,f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+f_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\,\biggl[\rho Q\,\overline{A}_{+}^{(2)}(Q,Y)+\frac{Q}{\rho}\,\overline{A}_{-}^{(2)}(Q,Y)\biggr]\biggr\}\,.

(54)

4.4
Universality of Power Corrections for 0-Jettiness

Having derived our master formulas, in this section we comment on the universality of the power corrections.
In both the collinear and soft limits the power corrections arising from the derivatives of the PDFs
and from the expansion of the flux factor are proportional to the LP matrix element
A^{(0)}(Q,Y)
,
see eqs. (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).
Since the factorization properties of
A^{(0)}(Q,Y)

are universal, most of the NLP corrections are universal as well,
in the sense that they essentially reduce to the LO cross section times a universal factor,
as we will make explicit below.
The only process-dependent piece arises from the NLP expansion
A^{(2)}(Q,Y)
of the matrix element.
We stress that this limit is defined in our particular choice of Born measurements
Q^{2}
and
Y
.
Using different observables, e.g.
q^{\pm}
, the NLP corrections to
\zeta_{a,b}
in eq. (
3.1
) would change,
inducing also a change of the NLP matrix element.

4.4.1
Universality of Collinear Limit

We begin by considering the
n
-collinear limit of a real emission amplitude in detail.
We consider the Born process

\kappa_{a}(q_{a})+\kappa_{b}(q_{b})\to L(q_{a}+q_{b})\,,

(55)

where
\kappa_{i}
denotes all quantum numbers, including flavor,
of the incoming partons, and
L
is the leptonic final state of momentum
q=q_{a}+q_{b}
.
The incoming momenta for the hard collision are given by

\displaystyle q_{a}^{\mu}=x_{a}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}=Qe^{+Y}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}\,,\qquad q_{b}^{\mu}=x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}=Qe^{-Y}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}\,.

(56)

Now consider that parton
a
arises from an
n
-collinear splitting of a parton with flavor
a^{\prime}
,

\kappa^{\prime}_{a}(q^{\prime}_{a})+\kappa_{b}(q_{b})\to L(q^{\prime}_{a}+q_{b}-k)+\kappa_{1}(k)\,.

(57)

To describe this at leading power, we only need the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{0})
relations
for the momenta of the incoming partons, which can be read off from eqs. (
4.2
) and (
4.2
),

\displaystyle{q^{\prime}}_{a}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{a}^{\mu}}{z_{a}}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})=\frac{Qe^{Y}}{z_{a}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})\,.

(58)

The
n
-collinear emission is given by eq. (
45
),

\displaystyle k^{\mu}=Qe^{Y}\frac{1-z_{a}}{z_{a}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\rho}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}+\sqrt{Q\mathcal{T}\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\frac{1-z_{a}}{z_{a}}}n_{\perp}^{\mu}\,.

(59)

It follows that the leptonic momentum
q^{\prime}=q^{\prime}_{a}+q^{\prime}_{b}-k=q+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})

is equal to the Born momentum
q=q_{a}+q_{b}
, and hence the collinear splitting does not
affect the leptonic phase space at LP.

The LP limit only exists if the splitting
\kappa^{\prime}_{a}\to\kappa_{a}+\kappa^{\prime}_{1}
is allowed,
in which case it is given by the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{-2})
piece of the squared amplitude,

\displaystyle A_{a^{\prime}b\to Lk}(Q,Y,\{k\})=\frac{8\pi\alpha_{s}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}}{Qe^{Y}k^{+}}P_{aa^{\prime}}(z_{a},\epsilon)A_{ab\to L}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{0})\,,

(60)

where the
1/k^{+}
gives rise to the
\lambda^{-2}
behavior of the amplitude.
Here the
P_{aa^{\prime}}
are the
\epsilon
-dimensional splitting functions which are summarized in appendix
A
.

Recall that in our case, the measurement fixes
k^{+}=\mathcal{T}/\rho
.
In the notation of eq. (
35
), we hence have for the LP matrix element

\displaystyle A_{a^{\prime}b\to Lk}^{(0)}(Q,Y,\{k\})=8\pi\alpha_{s}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}\frac{\rho}{Q\mathcal{T}e^{Y}}P_{aa^{\prime}}(z_{a},\epsilon)A_{ab\to L}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,.

(61)

These results enable us to explicitly give the universal part of the NLP result in the collinear limit.
Inserting into the collinear master formula eq. (
4.2
)
and converting to the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme, we find

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

(62)

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{Y}}{Q\rho}\times\biggl(\frac{Q\mathcal{T}}{\mu^{2}}\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggr)^{-\epsilon}\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int_{x_{a}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{a}}{z_{a}}\,\frac{z_{a}^{\epsilon}}{(1-z_{a})^{\epsilon}}P_{aa^{\prime}}(z_{a},\epsilon)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[f_{a^{\prime}}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)f_{b}(x_{b})\frac{(1-z_{a})^{2}-2}{z_{a}}+\frac{1-z_{a}}{z_{a}}x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)f_{b}(x_{b})+\frac{1+z_{a}}{z_{a}}f_{a^{\prime}}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{x_{a}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{a}}{z_{a}}\,\frac{f_{a}(x_{a}/z_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{Qe^{Y}}{\rho}\biggl(\frac{Q\mathcal{T}e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggr)^{-\epsilon}\frac{z_{a}^{\epsilon}}{(1-z_{a})^{\epsilon}}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{2}\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\,A^{(2)}(Q,Y,\{k\})\,.

Here, we factored out the LO partonic cross section eq. (
27
),
which is only possible because the collinear splitting leaves the leptonic
momentum invariant at LP.
We have made explicit the universal piece and nonuniversal components.
As already discussed, the full nonuniversal structure arises from the NLP matrix element

A^{(2)}(Q,Y,\{k\})
in the last line.

It would also be interesting to understand if there is a universal structure to
A^{(2)}(Q,Y)
.
This has recently been studied in ref.
Nandan:2016ohb
for pure
n
gluon scattering amplitudes at the level of the Cachazo-He-Yuan scattering equations
Cachazo:2013hca
;
Cachazo:2013iea
, where it was proven that in the subleading power collinear limits the tree-level amplitude factorizes into a convolution of the
n-1
gluon integrand and a universal collinear kernel. It would be interesting to understand this at the level of the amplitude itself, as well as for fermions. Unlike at leading power, we do not expect that there are universal subleading power splitting functions that are simply functions of
z
, but there may exist splitting functions that involve differential or integral operators, as occurs in the soft limit at subleading power
Low:1958sn
;
Burnett:1967km
. Understanding this will be particularly important for generalizing the calculation of the power corrections to more complicated processes.

4.4.2
Universality of Soft Limit

As for the collinear case, the LP soft limit of the matrix element is universal.
Following similar steps as in sec.
4.4.1
, one can express the LP soft limit by

\displaystyle A_{ab\to Lk}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})=\frac{16\pi\alpha_{s}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}\mathbf{C}}{k^{+}k^{-}}\times A_{ab\to L}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,,

(63)

which only exists for
ab=gg,q\bar{q}
and where
\mathbf{C}=C_{A},C_{F}
is the appropriate Casimir constant.
We thus obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{s}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{Q}\frac{\alpha_{s}\mathbf{C}}{\pi}\biggl[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\ln\frac{\mathcal{T}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-1\biggr]\biggl[f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\biggl(-\frac{\rho}{e^{Y}}-\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggr)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\frac{\rho}{e^{Y}}\,x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})+\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\,f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{f_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})}{(4\pi)^{2}2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathcal{T}^{-2\epsilon}\frac{1-2\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\biggl[\rho|\overline{A}_{+}^{(2)}(Q,Y)|^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}|\overline{A}_{-}^{(2)}(Q,Y)|^{2}\biggr]\,.

(64)

As for the collinear case, this emphasizes that the terms arising
from the expansion of the PDFs and flux factor are universal,
in the sense that they only depend on the universal LP soft limit of the amplitude.
The only nonuniversal contributions are
|\overline{A}_{\pm}^{(2)}|^{2}
.
However, these terms can in fact be derived from universal formulae
Low:1958sn
;
Burnett:1967km
;
DelDuca:1990gz
involving differential operators.
This has been recently studied in the threshold limit,
where one only requires soft contributions
DelDuca:2017twk
.
However, when one is away from the threshold limit as considered here, one in general requires collinear contributions,
which as discussed above, are not (yet) known to be universal.

for Higgs and Drell-Yan production in all partonic channels.
Since we only consider cases that are

(65)

where as always

\displaystyle x_{a}=\frac{Qe^{Y}}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\,,\quad x_{b}=\frac{Qe^{-Y}}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\,.

\displaystyle s_{ab}=2p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\,,\quad s_{ak}=-2p_{a}\cdot k\,,\quad s_{bk}=-2p_{b}\cdot k\,.

(67)

This allows us to straightforwardly obtain the LP and NLP expansion using eq. (

).

Throughout this section we consider on-shell Higgs production,

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)=\frac{A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}=2\pi\delta(Q^{2}-m_{H}^{2})\frac{|{\mathcal{M}}_{gg\to H}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q)|^{2}}{2Q^{2}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{2}}\,.

Dawson:1990zj
;

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}Q^{4}}{576\pi^{2}v^{2}}\biggl(\frac{4\pi\mu_{\rm MS}^{2}}{m_{t}^{2}}\biggr)^{2\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1+\epsilon)}{1-\epsilon}\,.

(69)

5.1.1
gg\to Hg

The spin- and color-averaged squared amplitude for
g(p_{a})+g(p_{b})\to H(q)+g(k)
is given by
Dawson:1990zj

\displaystyle A_{gg\to Hg}(Q,Y,\{k\})

\displaystyle=A^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)\times\frac{8\pi\alpha_{s}C_{A}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}}{Q^{4}(1-\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[(1-2\epsilon)\frac{Q^{8}+s_{ab}^{4}+s_{ak}^{4}+s_{bk}^{4}}{s_{ab}s_{ak}s_{bk}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\frac{(Q^{4}+s_{ab}^{2}+s_{ak}^{2}+s_{bk}^{2})^{2}}{s_{ab}s_{ak}s_{bk}}\biggr]\,.

(70)

n
-Collinear Limit

Expanding eq. (
5.1.1
) using eqs. (
•
‣
4.1
) and (
4.2
),

\displaystyle A^{(0)}_{gg\to Hg}(Q,Y;\{k\})

\displaystyle=16\pi\alpha_{s}C_{A}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}A^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)\frac{(1-z_{a}+z_{a}^{2})^{2}}{(1-z_{a})z_{a}}\frac{\rho e^{-Y}}{Q\mathcal{T}}\,,

(71)

\displaystyle A^{(2)}_{gg\to Hg}(Q,Y;\{k\})

\displaystyle=16\pi\alpha_{s}C_{A}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}A^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)\frac{1}{Q^{2}z_{a}^{2}}\biggl[1+5z_{a}^{2}-z_{a}^{3}+2z_{a}^{4}-z_{a}^{5}-2z_{a}^{2}\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\biggr]\,.

Since our scaling variable is
\lambda\sim\sqrt{\mathcal{T}/Q}
,
we clearly see that
A^{(0)}\sim\lambda^{-2}
and
A^{(2)}\sim\lambda^{0}
,
as required at LP and NLP.

Inserting these expansions into eq. (
4.2
) and converting to the
\overline{\text{MS}}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)\times\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{\pi}\int_{x_{a}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{a}}{z_{a}}\frac{1}{Q}\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{T}Q}{\mu^{2}}\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggr)^{-\epsilon}\frac{z_{a}^{\epsilon}}{(1-z_{a})^{\epsilon}}\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{\frac{(1-z_{a}+z_{a}^{2})^{2}}{(1-z_{a})z_{a}}\biggl[f_{a}\,f_{b}\frac{(1-z_{a})^{2}-2}{2z_{a}}+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}\,f_{b}\,\frac{1-z_{a}}{2z_{a}}+f_{a}\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}\,\frac{1+z_{a}}{2z_{a}}\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+f_{a}\,f_{b}\frac{1}{z_{a}^{2}}\biggl[1+5z_{a}^{2}-z_{a}^{3}+2z_{a}^{4}-z_{a}^{5}-2z_{a}^{2}\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\biggr]\biggr\}\,.

\displaystyle(1-z_{a})^{-1-\epsilon}=-\frac{\delta(1-z_{a})}{\epsilon}+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{a})+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon)\,,

(73)

where

We also combine the two separate

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{f_{g}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)\,f_{g}(x_{b})\biggl[\biggl(\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\ln\frac{Q\mathcal{T}e^{Y}}{\mu^{2}\rho}\biggr)\,\delta(1-z_{a})

\displaystyle\hskip 108.12054pt+\frac{1-2z_{a}+8z_{a}^{2}-14z_{a}^{3}+12z_{a}^{4}-10z_{a}^{5}+3z_{a}^{6}}{2z_{a}^{2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{a})\biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}f^{\prime}_{g}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)\,f_{g}(x_{b})\,\frac{(1-z_{a}+z_{a}^{2})^{2}}{2z_{a}}

\displaystyle\qquad+f_{g}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{b})\,\biggl[\biggl(-\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\ln\frac{Q\mathcal{T}e^{Y}}{\mu^{2}\rho}\biggr)\,\delta(1-z_{a})

\displaystyle\hskip 122.34692pt+\frac{(1+z_{a})(1-z_{a}+z_{a}^{2})^{2}}{2z_{a}^{2}}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{a})\biggr]\biggr\}\,.

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1-2z_{a}+8z_{a}^{2}-14z_{a}^{3}+12z_{a}^{4}-10z_{a}^{5}+3z_{a}^{6}}{2z_{a}^{2}}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{a})\,\biggr]\delta(1-z_{b})\,,

\displaystyle C_{f^{\prime}_{g}f_{g},n}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=4C_{A}\,\frac{e^{Y}}{Q\rho}\,\frac{(1-z_{a}+z_{a}^{2})^{2}}{2z_{a}}\,\delta(1-z_{b})\,,

\displaystyle C_{f_{g}f^{\prime}_{g},n}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=4C_{A}\,\frac{e^{Y}}{Q\rho}\,\biggl[\biggl(-\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\ln\frac{Q\mathcal{T}e^{Y}}{\mu^{2}\rho}\biggr)\,\delta(1-z_{a})

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[f_{g}(x_{a})f_{g}(x_{b})\biggl(-\frac{\rho}{e^{Y}}-\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggr)+\frac{\rho}{e^{Y}}\,x_{a}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{a})\,f_{g}(x_{b})+\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\,f_{g}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{b})\biggr]\,.

we find almost complete
agreement with the hadronic results of ref.
Boughezal:2018mvf
, up to a relatively simple
term.

8
Conclusions

In this paper, we have computed the next-to-leading power corrections in the
N
-jettiness resolution variable for Drell-Yan and gluon-fusion Higgs production at NLO. This builds on our previous work by computing the non-logarithmically enhanced terms at this order. These results enable the performance of the
N
-jettiness subtraction method to be improved, and provide important information on the structure of subleading power corrections beyond the leading logarithms.
Our calculation is based on a master formula applicable to
\text{SCET}_{\text{I}}
observables, and highlights a large degree of universality of these power corrections.

We explained in detail the issue of the treatment of Born measurements at subleading power. We have shown that an apparent disagreement in the literature arises due to the fact that the representation used to obtain the power corrections in refs.
Boughezal:2016zws
;
Boughezal:2018mvf
is only valid when integrated over all rapidities, and therefore cannot be directly compared with the results of refs.
Moult:2016fqy
;
Moult:2017jsg
and those in the present paper, which are differential in rapidity.
We show that after integration over rapidity the LL results agree.
Further details can be found in sec.
6
.

We find that the rapidity dependence of the NLL terms is quite sizeable and is therefore important
to know to be able to improve the subtractions.
One reason for this effect is the different
x
-dependence of parton luminosities
or derivatives of PDFs appearing in the power corrections as compared to the Born-level
parton luminosity. Hence, one can expect this to be a generic feature of subleading
power corrections.

We also compared our analytic NLL results for gluon fusion Higgs production and Drell-Yan to numerical predictions for these NLO power corrections obtained from a fit to data from MCFM. In all cases, excellent agreement was found. In addition we studied the extent to which the inclusion of the NLL power corrections improves the subtraction. At NLO, the inclusion of the NLL power corrections completely captures the
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau)
terms. Numerically, summing over production channels, the inclusion of these results reduces the size of the power corrections by two orders of magnitude in Higgs production and three orders of magnitude in Drell-Yan production.

There are a number of directions for future work. It will be interesting to extend the calculation of the NLL power corrections to NNLO. Generically we expect up to an order of magnitude improvement could also be obtained by extending the known LL power corrections at this order to NLL. Beyond fixed order, the derivation of subleading power renormalization group evolution equations at NLL would allow for the all-orders prediction of the NLL terms. Finally, while we have focused here on color-singlet production, our results provide an important step toward the calculation of the NLP corrections at higher orders and for more complicated processes.

Appendix A
Derivation of NLO Leading Power Results

At leading power the singular terms for
N
-jettiness are most easily obtained from known factorization formulas
Stewart:2009yx
;
Stewart:2010tn
, which describe the singular behavior of the observable to all orders. The fixed order approach of this paper is therefore most useful when such factorization formula are not available, or well understood, such as at subleading power. However, it can also be applied to reproduce the LP results. In this appendix we illustrate this at NLO, by reproducing the one-loop beam and soft functions for beam thrust.

To relate the beam and soft functions as defined in SCET to our calculation in this work,
recall the LP factorization formula for beam thrust
Stewart:2009yx
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,H_{ab}(Q,\mu)\int\mathrm{d}t_{a}\mathrm{d}t_{b}\,B_{a}(t_{a},x_{a},\mu)B_{b}(t_{b},x_{b},\mu)S\biggr(\mathcal{T}-\frac{t_{a}}{Q}-\frac{t_{b}}{Q},\mu\biggr)\,,

(130)

where the superscript
(0)
refers to LP, and as before
x_{a,b}=\frac{Q}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}e^{\pm Y}
.
The hard function
H_{ab}
describes virtual corrections to the hard process

ab\to L
,
B_{a,b}
are the two beam functions and
S
is the soft function.
The beam functions can be further matched onto normal PDFs,

\displaystyle B_{i}(t,x,\mu)=\sum_{j}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(t,z,\mu)f_{j}\biggl(\frac{x}{z},\mu\biggr)\,.

(131)

All of these functions have definitions as field theory matrix elements in the EFT.
Their fixed order definitions give rise to UV divergences, which are as usual removed
by a renormalization procedure, which in turn gives rise to RGEs that can be used
to resum large logarithms of
\mathcal{T}
.
In the approach presented in this paper, the same divergences appear as
1/\epsilon
IR divergences in the soft and collinear limits of QCD amplitudes.

At LO, we have

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{LO}}_{ij}(Q^{2},\mu)=1\,,\quad{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}(t,x,\mu)=\delta_{ij}\delta(t)\,,\quad S^{\mathrm{LO}}(\mathcal{T},\mu)=\delta(\mathcal{T})\,.

(132)

At one loop, the convolution structure thus becomes trivial. Working with hard, beam, soft, and PDFs in the bare factorization theorem we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0,1)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}\,H^{\mathrm{NLO}}_{ab}(Q^{2},\epsilon)\delta(\mathcal{T})f_{a}(x_{a},\epsilon)f_{b}(x_{b},\epsilon)

\displaystyle+\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}\sum_{a^{\prime}}\int_{x_{a}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{a}}{z_{a}}\,Q\,{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathrm{NLO}}_{aa^{\prime}}(Q\mathcal{T},z_{a},\epsilon)f_{a^{\prime}}(x_{a}/z_{a},\epsilon)f_{b}(x_{b},\epsilon)

\displaystyle+\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}\sum_{b^{\prime}}\int_{x_{b}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{b}}{z_{b}}\,Q\,{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathrm{NLO}}_{bb^{\prime}}(Q\mathcal{T},z_{b},\epsilon)f_{a}(x_{a},\epsilon)f_{b^{\prime}}(x_{b}/z_{b},\epsilon)

\displaystyle+\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}f_{a}(x_{a},\epsilon)f_{b}(x_{b},\epsilon)S^{\mathrm{NLO}}(\mathcal{T},\epsilon)\,.

(133)

Note the extra factor of
Q
in the beam contributions, arising from
t_{a,b}
and
\mathcal{T}

having different mass dimensions.
We have written eq. (
A
) in a form similar to our master formulas,
such that we can easily read off the one-loop beam function kernels and soft function. The
\epsilon

A

A.1
Leading-Power Expansion of Matrix Elements

The leading-power behavior of real emission matrix elements in the soft and collinear limits
is universal, see e.g.
Catani:1996vz
, and has already been used in sec.
4.4
.
Here, we briefly review the relevant formulas, and give the relevant one-loop expressions.

Given the Born process

\displaystyle\kappa_{a}(q_{a})+\kappa_{b}(q_{b})\to L(q_{a}+q_{b})\,,

(134)

where the incoming momenta are given by

\displaystyle q_{a}^{\mu}=Qe^{Y}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}\,,\quad q_{b}^{\mu}=Qe^{-Y}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}\,,

(135)

and we write the one-emission process as

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}_{a}(q^{\prime}_{a})+\kappa^{\prime}_{b}(q^{\prime}_{b})\to L(q^{\prime}_{a}+q^{\prime}_{b}-k)+\kappa_{1}(k)\,.

(136)

In the soft limit
k^{\mu}\ll q_{a}^{\mu},q_{b}^{\mu}
, the squared matrix element obeys the LP relation

\displaystyle A^{(0)}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\to Lk}(Q,Y;\{k\})

\displaystyle=\frac{16\pi\alpha_{s}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}\mathbf{C}}{k^{+}k^{-}}\times\delta_{aa^{\prime}}\,\delta_{bb^{\prime}}\,A^{\mathrm{LO}}_{ab\to L}(Q,Y)\,,

(137)

where we made explicit that a soft emission can not change the incoming flavors.

\mathbf{C}=C_{F},C_{A}
is the Casimir constant for
ab=q\bar{q},gg
.

In the LP
n
-collinear limit, the particle
k
arises from the splitting
\kappa^{\prime}_{a}\to\kappa_{a}+\kappa_{1}
.
If this splitting is allowed, at LP we have (in the notation of sec.
4
)

q^{\prime}_{a}=q_{a}/z_{a}
and
q^{\prime}_{b}=q_{b}
, and the LP limit of the matrix element is given by

\displaystyle A^{(0)}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\to Lk}(Q,Y,\{k\})=\frac{8\pi\alpha_{s}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}}{Qe^{Y}k^{+}}P_{aa^{\prime}}(z_{a},\epsilon)\times\delta_{bb^{\prime}}\,A_{ab\to L}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,.

(138)

Similarly, in the
\bar{n}
-collinear limit arising from
\kappa^{\prime}_{b}\to\kappa_{b}+\kappa_{1}
,
at LP we get
q^{\prime}_{a}=q_{a},q^{\prime}_{b}=q_{b}/z_{b}
,

\displaystyle A^{(0)}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\to Lk}(Q,Y,\{k\})=\frac{8\pi\alpha_{s}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}}{Qe^{-Y}k^{-}}P_{bb^{\prime}}(z_{b},\epsilon)\times\delta_{aa^{\prime}}\,A_{ab\to L}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,.

(139)

The one-loop splitting functions in
d=4-2\epsilon
dimensions are given by
Catani:1996vz

\displaystyle P_{qq}(z,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\biggl[\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}-\epsilon(1-z)\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle P_{gq}(z,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\biggl[\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}-\epsilon z\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle P_{qg}(z,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=T_{F}\biggl[1-\frac{2z(1-z)}{1-\epsilon}\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle P_{gg}(z,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=2C_{A}\biggl[\frac{z}{1-z}+\frac{1-z}{z}+z(1-z)\biggr]\,.

(140)

Note that we flipped the notation of
qg
and
gq
relative to
Catani:1996vz
,
following the standard convention.

A.2
NLO Soft Function

The NLO LP soft function follows from combining eq. (
37
) with eq. (
137
)
using the same steps as in sec.
4.3
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{s}^{(0,1)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon}}\frac{f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})}{(4\pi)^{2}2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}A^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})\,\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{\alpha_{s}\mathbf{C}}{\pi}f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\times\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{+}k^{-})^{\epsilon+1}}\delta\bigl[\mathcal{T}-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\{k\})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\frac{\alpha_{s}\mathbf{C}}{\pi}f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac{2}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\mu}(\mathcal{T}/\mu)^{-1-2\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\frac{\alpha_{s}\mathbf{C}}{4\pi}f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\biggl[-\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}\delta(\mathcal{T})+\frac{8}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\mu}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}/\mu)

\displaystyle\hskip 147.95424pt-\frac{16}{\mu}{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}/\mu)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\delta(\mathcal{T})+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon)\biggr]\,.

(141)

The
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(x)
are the standard one-dimensional plus distributions, see e.g.
Ligeti:2008ac
for details.
Note that there, the precise definition of the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme is important.
We use

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\equiv\mu^{2}_{\overline{\rm MS}}=\frac{4\pi}{e^{\gamma_{E}}}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2}\,.

(142)

If one were to use
\mu^{2\epsilon}=\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}
, one would miss the
\pi^{2}/3
term.
For the NLP results presented in the main text, both definitions yield identical results.

Taking eq. (
A.2
) and adding the virtual soft diagram, and then comparing to eq. (
A
), the one-loop bare soft function can be read off as

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{NLO}}(\mathcal{T},\epsilon)=\frac{\alpha_{s}\mathbf{C}}{4\pi}\biggl[-\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}\delta(\mathcal{T})+\frac{8}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\mu}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}/\mu)-\frac{16}{\mu}{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}/\mu)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\delta(\mathcal{T})\biggr]\,.

(143)

The finite terms precisely yield the renormalized one-loop soft function
Stewart:2009yx
.

Applying the LP master formula eq. (
37

n
-collinear case

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}^{(0,1)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\times A(Q,Y;\{k\})\,.

n

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}^{(1){\rm real}}_{ij}(t,z,\epsilon)=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\biggl[\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\biggl(\frac{t}{\mu^{2}}\frac{e^{Y}}{\rho}\biggr)^{-1-\epsilon}\frac{z^{\epsilon}P_{ij}(z,\epsilon)}{(1-z)^{\epsilon}}\biggr]\,.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{\rho}^{2}}\left(\frac{t}{\mu_{\rho}^{2}}\right)^{-\epsilon-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta(t)}{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\mu_{\rho}^{2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(t/\mu_{\rho}^{2})-\epsilon\frac{1}{\mu_{\rho}^{2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(t/\mu_{\rho}^{2})+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon)^{2}\,,

(147)

\displaystyle(1-z)^{-1-\epsilon}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\delta(1-z)+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)-\epsilon{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(1-z)+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon)^{2}\,,

(148)

Example:

From eq. (

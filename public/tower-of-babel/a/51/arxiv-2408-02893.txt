A priori estimates and Liouville-type theorems for the semilinear parabolic equations involving the nonlinear gradient source Thanks: Corresponding author: Zhengce Zhang Thanks: Keywords: Liouville-type theorem; Local gradient estimates; Bernstein method; Doubling lemma Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35K58; 35B45; 35B53; 35B33
Wenguo Liang and Zhengce Zhang

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the local and global properties of nonnegative solutions for semilinear heat equation u t − Δ ​ u = u p + M ​ | ∇ u | q u_{t}-\Delta u=u^{p}+M|\nabla u|^{q} in Ω × I ⊂ ℝ N × ℝ \Omega\times I\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R} , where M > 0 M>0 , and p , q > 1 p,q>1 . We first establish the local pointwise gradient estimates when q q is subcritical, critical and supercritical with respect to p p . With these estimates, we can prove the parabolic Liouville-type theorems for time-decreasing ancient solutions. Next, we use Gidas-Spruck type integral methods to prove the Liouville-type theorem for the entire solutions when q q is critical. Finally, as an application of the Liouville-type theorem, we use the doubling lemma to derive universal priori estimates for local solutions of parabolic equations with general nonlinearities. Our approach relies on a parabolic differential inequality containing a suitable auxiliary function rather than Keller-Osserman type inequality, which allows us to generalize and extend the partial results of the elliptic equation (Bidaut-Véron, Garcia-Huidobro and Véron (2020) [ 6 ] ) to the parabolic case.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the qualitative properties of parabolic equations with the nonlinear gradient source

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta u=u^{p}+M|\nabla u|^{q}\ \ {\rm in}\ \Omega\times I,

where
\Omega
is a domain,
I
is an interval of
\mathbb{R}
, and
M>0
,
p,q>1
are parameters. Throughout this paper, the operators
\nabla
and
\Delta
only apply to the spatial variables. The solution will always refer to the classical solution contained in
C^{2,1}(\Omega\times I)
.

When
M=0
, equation (
1.1
) reduces to

(1.2)

u_{t}-\Delta u=u^{p}.

Gidas and Spruk used integral estimates in
[
15
]
to prove the nonexistence of positive global classical stationary solutions when
1<p<p_{S}:=(N+2)/(N-2)
for
N>2
. Chen and Li used the moving planes method to provide a new proof of the nonexistence result in
[
11
]
. For the steady state equations, the exponent
p_{S}
is sharp in the sense that there exist positive bounded radial solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 3)
when
p\geq p_{S}
(see
[
9
]
and
[
27
, Section 9]
). Then
Bidaut-Véron
[
5
]
adapted the integral method to study the blow-up profiles of solutions to parabolic problem (
1.2
) for
1<p<p_{B}
, where

p_{B}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{N(N+2)}{(N-1)^{2}},\quad&\text{if}\ N>1,\\
&\infty,\quad&\text{if}\ N=1.\end{aligned}\right.

At the same time, Merle and Zaag
[
22
]
proved the nonexistence of positive solutions which satisfy decay assumption of (
1.2
) for
1<p<p_{S}
. However, almost all known results of the optimal Liouville-type theorems for (
1.2
) require either a more restrictive assumption on
p
or deal with a special class of solutions. Recently, Quittner
[
26
]
improved the above theorems based on refined energy estimates for the rescaled solution.

Concerning the problems where the nonlinearity contains the spatial derivatives of
u
, the well-known model in this direction is Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(1.3)

u_{t}-\Delta u=|\nabla u|^{q},

which arises in both stochastic control problems and KPZ type models of surface growth through ballistic deposition, see
[
3
,
4
,
17
,
18
]
.
For the steady state problem, Lions
[
19
]
demonstrated that any classical solution in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for
q>1
is a constant. The Bernstein estimates are the key to proving this Liouville-type theorem. Moreover, for the half-space steady state problem, Porretta and Véron
[
25
]
proved the one-dimensional symmetry for
1<q\leq 2
. Filippucci, Pucci, and Souplet obtained a similar symmetry result for
q>2
in
[
14
]
using the moving planes technique and Bernstein estimates. For the parabolic problem, the Liouville-type theorem for (
1.3
) attracted much attention. Souplet and Zhang
[
31
, Section 3]
proved gradient estimates for locally upper bounded solution of (
1.3
). Based on these estimates, they demonstrated that ancient solutions remain constants under certain growth conditions at infinity. For further information on the gradient estimates of equation (
1.3
), we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
33
,
35
]
.

For the case of
M<0
in (
1.1
), the pioneering paper due to Chipot and Weissler
[
12
]
investigated the possible effects of a gradient term on the global existence or nonexistence, then it was proposed by Souplet
[
28
]
as a population dynamics model to represent the evolution of biological population density. For the case of
M>0
, Tayachi and Zaag
[
32
]
obtained the blow-up behavior of positive solutions. More results concerning the existence and nonexistence, blow-up behavior of solutions to (
1.1
) for
M\neq 0
can be found in
[
8
,
13
,
20
,
34
,
36
]
.
It should be noted that few results about gradient estimates of solutions for (
1.1
) are presented. However, for the elliptic equation corresponding to (
1.1
) when
M>0
, Bidaut-Véron, Garcia-Huidobro, and Véron
[
6
]
used the Bernstein method and Keller-Osserman’s estimates to establish a priori estimates and determine the nonexistence of solutions. By using the integral identity and Young’s inequality, Ma, Wu, and Zhang
[
21
]
have recently completed the results of nonexistence of solutions in the critical case, that is,
q=2p/(p+1)
.

This paper aims to establish gradient estimates and Liouville-type theorems for equation (
1.1
) with
M>0
. Before proving the main results, we intend to introduce some preliminary notes in this paper.

Let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
). Define

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{-1}u(\lambda^{\frac{1-p}{2}}x,\lambda^{1-p}t),\ \ \lambda>0.

Clearly,
u_{\lambda}
is solution of the equation

u_{t}-\Delta u=u^{p}+\lambda^{\frac{(p+1)q-2p}{2}}M|\nabla u|^{q}.

In the case that
\lambda^{(p+1)q/2}\sim\lambda^{p}
as
\lambda
approaches infinity, it holds that
q=2p/(p+1)
. In this context, we refer to
q
as critical with respect to
p
.
Likewise, if
\lambda^{(p+1)q/2}=o(\lambda^{p})
or
\lambda^{(p+1)q/2}\gg\lambda^{p}
as
\lambda
approaches infinity, we say that
q
is subcritical or supercritical with respect to
p
, that is,
q<2p/(p+1)
or
q>2p/(p+1)
. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
29
]
.

As far as we know, there is no Liouville-type theorem for parabolic problem (
1.1
) when
M>0
. The presence of the time derivative term and the combined nonlinearities make the problem much more challenging. On the one hand, there has no Keller-Osserman type estimates for parabolic problems, similar to the one for the elliptic problem provided in
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
, to study the pointwise gradient estimates. On the other hand, because of the presence of nonlinear gradient term, we cannot find a weighted energy functional for the rescaled problem as in
[
26
]
. Thus, the energy estimates technique does not work any more.

Our main goal is to obtain the pointwise and integral gradient estimates of the solutions for (
1.1
). We use the maximum principle for parabolic differential inequality which contains various auxiliary functions to overcome the lack of Keller-Osserman type estimates. In addition, we consider the integral estimates of solutions rather than the energy estimates of the rescaling solutions. It is noteworthy that in local estimates, the gradient term
M|\nabla u|^{q}
is regarded as a major or perturbation term. Specifically, we treat the gradient term as the main term when working with the pointwise gradient estimates, and add assumptions on solutions in order to eliminate the nonnegative terms associated with
u^{p}
. When considering the integral estimates, we regard the terms that contain
M|\nabla u|^{q}
as perturbation terms. By applying Young’s inequality and attaching suitable assumption on
M
, we can obtain that the perturbation terms will be controlled by other terms.

Now we present the main results. The first result concerns the local pointwise gradient estimates for solutions of (
1.1
) in
Q_{T,R}
when
q
is subcritical, where

Q_{T,R}=B(x_{0},R)\times(0,T),

x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
R,T>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>1
,
1<q<2p/(p+1)
. For any
M>0
, assume
u
is the nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{T,R}
that satisfies

(1.4)

u\leq c_{N,p,q}M^{\frac{2}{2p-(p+1)q}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad u_{t}\leq 0\quad\text{in}\ Q_{T,R}

for some constant
c_{N,p,q}>0
.
Then there exists a constant
C:=C(N,M,p,q)>0
, such that

(1.5)

|\nabla u(x,t)|\leq C\left(R^{-1}+R^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}+t^{-\frac{1}{q}}\right)\quad\text{in}\ Q_{T,R/2}.

The gradient estimates of Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.3
) in
[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
is the same as estimates (
1.5
). It should be noted that if we ignore the coefficient
C(N,M,p,q)
, the power terms in (
1.5
) are independent of
p
. Actually, we first introduce an auxiliary function
f\leq 0
with
f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}>0
, and
(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})^{\prime}<0
, and consider the transformation
v=f^{-1}(-u)
. Setting
w=|\nabla v|^{2}
, we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(w):=

\displaystyle w_{t}-\Delta w-\mathcal{H}\cdot\nabla w

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2p(-f)^{p-1}w+2\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}}(-f)^{p}w+2\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}w^{2}-2(q-1)M(f^{\prime})^{q-2}f^{\prime\prime}w^{\frac{q+2}{2}}-|D^{2}v|^{2},

where
\mathcal{H}
is given in Lemma
2.1
. Note that the last negative term in (
1.6
) can eliminate the first two nonnegative perturbation terms on the right side of the inequality. Namely, we intend to obtain that

2p(-f)^{p-1}w+2\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}}(-f)^{p}w-|D^{2}v|^{2}\leq 0,

which requires the assumptions (
1.4
) on
u
. It should be pointed out that the boundedness assumption in (
1.4
) is the same as the one stated in
[
6
, Theorem
\rm A^{\prime}
]
for elliptic equations. Therefore, based on the given assumptions, we shall obtain

\mathcal{L}(w)\leq-2(q-1)M(f^{\prime})^{q-2}f^{\prime\prime}w^{\frac{q+2}{2}}.

Furthermore, replacing
\mathcal{L}(w)
by
\mathcal{L}(w\eta)
ensures that the arguments above are always valid for a given cut-off function
\eta
. Thus, it comes that

(1.7)

\mathcal{L}(w\eta)\leq-\frac{q-1}{2}M(f^{\prime})^{q-2}f^{\prime\prime}w^{\frac{q+2}{2}}\eta+C\left(R^{-(q+2)}+R^{-\frac{q+2}{q-1}}\right),

where
C>0
depends on
N,M,p
and
q
. By using the maximum principle to equation (
1.7
), we can conclude that the power terms in the gradient estimates (
1.5
) are independent of
p
.

As a consequence of the local gradient estimates in Theorem
1.1
, we state the following Liouville-type theorem for bounded solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>1
and
1<q<2p/(p+1)
. For any
M>0
, assume
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
that satisfies

u\leq c_{N,p,q}M^{\frac{2}{2p-(p+1)q}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad u_{t}\leq 0\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)

for some constant
c_{N,p,q}>0
.
Then
u\equiv 0
.

It is remarkable that the time monotonicity condition
u_{t}\leq 0
is also required in the proof of the Liouville-type theorem. In fact, we have demonstrated that the ancient solutions are independent of the space variables
x
based on the estimates (
1.5
). Consequently, it derives that
0\leq u^{p}=u_{t}\leq 0
and thus,
u\equiv 0
.

In the case that
q
is critical, the situation is much more delicate, for the reason that the coefficient of gradient term plays a key role. In what follows, we provide the local gradient estimates for the locally upper bounded solution.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>1
and
q=2p/(p+1)
. Assume
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{T,R}
that satisfies
u\leq b
for some constant
b\geq 1
and

u_{t}\leq 0\quad\text{in}\ Q_{T,R}.

Then there exists a constant
C:=C(N,M,p)>0
such that for any

M\geq M_{0}:=\left(6N(p+1)\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\left(\frac{p+1}{p-1}\right)^{1/2}.

the solution satisfies

(1.8)

|\nabla u(x,t)|\leq Cb\left(R^{-1}+R^{-\frac{p+1}{p-1}}+t^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}\right)\quad\text{in}\ Q_{T,R/2}.

As a consequence of (
1.8
), the following Liouville-type theorem comes into existence.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p>1
and
q=2p/(p+1)
. Assume
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
for
M\geq M_{0}
, which satisfies
u_{t}\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
and

(1.9)

u(x,t)=o\left(|x|+|t|^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}\right)\quad\text{as}\ |x|+|t|^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}\to\infty.

Then
u\equiv 0
. In particular, any bounded nonnegative time-decreasing solution of (
1.1
) is
u\equiv 0
.

Since the gradient estimates in (
1.8
) depends on the upper bound of the solution, it is necessary to make a priori assumption on the behavior of
u
at infinity. However, it is uncertain whether the assumption (
1.9
) is sharp.

Our next results deal with the case that
q
is supercritical.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
p>1
,
q>2p/(p+1)
. Assume
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{T,R}
, and
u_{t}\leq\tau
in
Q_{T,R}
for any
\tau\geq 0
.

(i) For any
M>0
, the solution
u
satisfies

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x,t)|\leq C(N,p,q)\left(M^{-\frac{p+1}{(p+1)q-2p}}+M^{-\frac{1}{q}}\tau^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)+C(N,M,p,q)\left(R^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2(q-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ Q_{T,R/2}.

(ii) For any
M>0
, if

u\geq c_{N,p,q}\left(M^{-\frac{2}{(p+1)q-2p}}+\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\quad\text{in}\ Q_{T,R}

for some
c_{N,p,q}>0
. Then the solution
u
satisfies

|\nabla u(x,t)|\leq C\left(R^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2(q-1)}}\right)\quad\text{in}\ Q_{T,R/2},

where
C:=C(N,M,p,q)>0
.

Remark 1.6
.

The upper boundedness assumption for
u_{t}
in Theorem
1.5
can be removed by adding assumptions on
u_{0}
and
u
for the associated Dirichlet and Cauchy problems

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-\Delta u&=u^{p}+M|\nabla u|^{q},\ &&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\

u(x,t)&=u_{0}(x),\ &&x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

For given

, we select
R\leq{\rm dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
and then

. In fact, assuming that

is uniformly bounded,
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\Delta u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and using the maximum principle for

, we have

u_{t}\leq\big|\Delta u_{0}+u_{0}^{p}+M|\nabla u_{0}|^{q}\big|\quad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T).

We also give a nonexistence result for the ancient solution in what follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
p>1
and

. Assume
u_{t}\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
. Then there exists a constant

, there is no positive solution of (

) in

Next, we use the integral Bernstein method to obtain the following result when

is critical and

is small.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
1<p<p_{B}
and
q=2p/(p+1)
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}(N,p)>0
such that for any

, the nonnegative solution for (

) in

The proof of Theorem

follows the arguments as in

5
,
30

,

In

23
,
24
]
, the authors demonstrated that the Liouville-type theorems ensure the validity of universal estimates. Furthermore, it was found that the Liouville-type theorems were equivalent to the universal estimates in

. Using the recalling arguments and the doubling property, we will give universal estimates for the solution of (

) as well as the estimates for a more general problem given in (

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
1<p<p_{B}
,
q=2p/(p+1)
,

and

be an arbitrary domain of

Finally, we will derive universal estimates for general superlinear parabolic equation without scale invariance, given by

where

.

Let

,

be an arbitrary domain of

. Assume

2.
The Bernstein method

In this section, we establish the local pointwise estimates for nonnegative solutions of (
1.1
). These estimates are crucial in proving the Liouville-type theorems for the ancient solution.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
to be chosen later. Set
R^{\prime}=3R/4
. We select a cut-off function
\eta\in C^{2}\left(\overline{B}\left(x_{0},R^{\prime}\right)\right),0\leq\eta\leq 1
, satisfies
\eta=1
for
|x-x_{0}|\leq R/2
,
\eta=0
for
\left|x-x_{0}\right|=R^{\prime}
and such that

(2.1)

\left.\begin{array}[]{rl}|\nabla\eta|&\leq CR^{-1}\eta^{\alpha}\\
\left|D^{2}\eta\right|+\eta^{-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}&\leq CR^{-2}\eta^{\alpha}\end{array}\right\}\text{ for }\left|x-x_{0}\right|<R^{\prime},

with
C=C(\alpha)>0
. Indeed, such a function is given in
[
31
]
as
\eta=\rho^{k}
, where
\rho(x)=1-R^{\prime-2}|x-x_{0}|^{2}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
k\geq 2/(1-\alpha)
.

We first present a differential inequality of the gradient, which follows the method in
[
31
]
and will be used several times in this article.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
R,T>0
. Assume that
u\in C^{2,1}(Q_{T,R})
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
). Define
v=f^{-1}(-u)
, where
f
is a
C^{3}
monotonous function. Set
w=|\nabla v|^{2}
and
z=w\eta
. Then at any point such
|\nabla u|>0
,
z
satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(z)\leq

\displaystyle 2p(-f)^{p-1}w\eta+2\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}}(-f)^{p}w\eta+2\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}w^{2}\eta

\displaystyle-2(q-1)M(f^{\prime})^{q-2}f^{\prime\prime}w^{\frac{q+2}{2}}\eta+\sqrt{N}|D^{2}\eta|w+qM(f^{\prime})^{q-1}|\nabla\eta|w^{\frac{q+1}{2}}

\displaystyle+2\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right||\nabla\eta|w^{\frac{3}{2}}+C_{1}(N)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\eta^{-1}w-|D^{2}v|^{2}\eta\quad\text{in}\ Q_{T,R^{\prime}},

where the differential operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(z)=\partial_{t}z-\Delta z+\mathcal{H}\cdot\nabla z,

and

\mathcal{H}=\left[qM(f^{\prime})^{q-1}w^{\frac{q-2}{2}}-2\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right]\nabla v.

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{2,1}(Q_{T,R})
be a nonnegative classical solution of

3.
The integral Bernstein
method

In order to get the integral estimates related with the solution and its gradient, we recall the following lemma from
[
27
, Lemma 8.9]
(see also
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
). This lemma establishes a set of integral estimates that related to a positive function of its gradient and Laplacian.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an arbitrary domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
0\leq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and
0<v\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. Fix
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and we denote dy
\int
the integral on
\Omega
. Set

I_{a}=\int\varphi v^{a-2}|\nabla v|^{4},\quad J_{a}=\int\varphi v^{a-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\Delta v,\quad K_{a}=\int\varphi v^{a}(\Delta v)^{2}.

Then for any
k\in\mathbb{R}
with
k\neq-1
, there holds

(3.1)

\alpha I_{a}+\beta J_{a}+\gamma K_{a}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int v^{a}|\nabla v|^{2}\Delta\varphi+\int v^{a}\Delta v\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi+(a-k)\int v^{a-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi,

where

\alpha=-\frac{N-1}{N}k^{2}+(a-1)k-\frac{a(a-1)}{2},\quad\beta=\frac{N+2}{N}k-\frac{3}{2}a,\quad\gamma=-\frac{N-1}{N}.

In the following lemma, we shall provide local integral estimates for both the solution and its gradient.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an arbitrary domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
T>0
, and
0\leq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\times(-T,T))
. Let
u
be a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\times(-T,T)
. Fix
\theta>0
and set

v=u+\theta,\quad f_{\theta}=u^{p}-v^{p}.

For given
k\in\mathbb{R}
, denote

I=\iint\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4},\quad L=\iint\varphi v^{2p},\quad G=M\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2+q},

where
\iint
denotes the integral on
\Omega\times(-T,T)
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\alpha I+\delta L-\beta G\leq

\displaystyle C\iint\varphi\left(M^{2}|\nabla v|^{2q}+M|v_{t}||\nabla v|^{q}+v^{-1}|v_{t}||\nabla v|^{2}+v^{p}|v_{t}|+Mv^{p}|\nabla v|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle+C\iint\varphi v_{t}^{2}+C\iint|\varphi_{t}|v^{p+1}+C\iint|\Delta\varphi||\nabla v|^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle+C\iint|\nabla\varphi|\left(v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{3}+\left|v_{t}-v^{p}-M|\nabla v|^{q}\right||\nabla v|+v^{p}|\nabla v|\right)

\displaystyle+C\iint\left(\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}+\varphi v^{p}+|\nabla\varphi||\nabla v|\right)f_{\theta}+CF_{\theta},

where
C=C(N,k)>0
,

F_{\theta}=\iint\varphi\left[f_{\theta}^{2}-2(v_{t}-v^{p}-M|\nabla v|^{q})f_{\theta}\right],

and

\displaystyle\alpha=

\displaystyle-\frac{N-1}{N}k^{2}-k,

\displaystyle\delta=

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{p}\frac{N+2}{N}-\frac{N-1}{N},

\displaystyle\beta=

\displaystyle\frac{N+2}{N}k.

Proof.

We integrate inequality (
3.1
) with respect to time. Choosing
a=0
in Lemma
3.1
, we observe that
I=\int_{-T}^{T}I_{0}
, and set
J=\int_{-T}^{T}J_{0}
,
K=\int^{T}_{-T}K_{0}
. It is obtained

\alpha I+\beta J+\gamma K\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint|\nabla v|^{2}\Delta\varphi+\iint\Delta v\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi-k\iint v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi,

where

\displaystyle\alpha=-\frac{N-1}{N}k^{2}-k,\quad\beta=\frac{N+2}{N}k,\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=-\frac{N-1}{N}.

We next consider the terms
K
and
J
by using the perturbation equation

(3.3)

v_{t}-\Delta v=v^{p}+M|\nabla v|^{q}+f_{\theta}.

From integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle K=

\displaystyle\iint\varphi(\Delta v)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\varphi\left(v_{t}^{2}+v^{2p}+M^{2}|\nabla v|^{2q}-2v_{t}v^{p}-2Mv_{t}|\nabla v|^{q}+2Mv^{p}|\nabla v|^{q}\right)+F_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\varphi v_{t}^{2}+L+M^{2}\iint\varphi|\nabla v|^{2q}+\frac{2}{p+1}\iint\varphi_{t}v^{p+1}

\displaystyle-2M\iint\varphi v_{t}|\nabla v|^{q}+2M\iint\varphi v^{p}|\nabla v|^{q}+F_{\theta},

and

\displaystyle J=

\displaystyle\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\left(v_{t}-v^{p}-M|\nabla v|^{q}-f_{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}v_{t}-\iint\varphi v^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{2}-M\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2+q}-\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}f_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\iint v^{p}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla v+\frac{1}{p}\iint v^{p}\varphi{\rm{div}}(\nabla v)+\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\left(v_{t}-M|\nabla v|^{q}-f_{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}v_{t}+\frac{1}{p}\iint v^{p}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi+\frac{1}{p}\iint\varphi v^{p}v_{t}-\frac{1}{p}L

\displaystyle-\frac{M}{p}\iint\varphi v^{p}|\nabla v|^{q}-M\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2+q}-\frac{1}{p}\iint\varphi v^{p}f_{\theta}-\iint\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}f_{\theta}.

Then substituting the expansions of
K
and
J
into (
3.1
), we infer that

\displaystyle\alpha I+\delta L-\beta G

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\iint\varphi\left(v_{t}^{2}+M^{2}|\nabla v|^{2q}+M|v_{t}||\nabla v|^{q}+v^{-1}|v_{t}||\nabla v|^{2}+v^{p}|v_{t}|+Mv^{p}|\nabla v|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle+C\iint|\varphi_{t}|v^{p+1}+C\iint|D^{2}\varphi||\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\iint|\nabla\varphi|\left(v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{3}+\bigr|v_{t}-v^{p}-M|\nabla v|^{q}\bigr||\nabla v|+v^{p}|\nabla v|\right)

\displaystyle+C\iint\left(\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}+\varphi v^{p}+|\nabla\varphi||\nabla v|\right)f_{\theta}+CF_{\theta},

where

\delta=\gamma-\frac{\beta}{p},

which proves the estimates (
3.2
).
\Box

Remark 3.3
.

We consider the positive function
u+\theta
instead of
u
in order to apply Lemma
3.1
. However, we require the additional complication to handle the perturbation terms
f_{\theta}
and

We now derive the integral estimates of solution.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume

and

Proof.
The proof consists of two steps.

Step 1: Study of the coefficients and test function.
Since

p<p_{B}=\frac{N(N+2)}{(N-1)^{2}},

we can choose
k<0
and
k\neq-1
such that
\alpha,\delta>0
, which is equivalent to

\frac{p(N-1)}{N+2}<-k<\frac{N}{N-1}.

To estimate the right side of (
3.2
), we shall select an appropriate test function
\varphi
. Let
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{1}\times(-1,1)\right)
, such that
\xi=1
in
B_{1/2}\times(-1/2,1/2)
and
0\leq\xi\leq 1
. We can fix
\bar{\alpha}>0
such that

\frac{3p+1}{4p}<\bar{\alpha}<1.

Fix

, by taking

with

sufficiently large, then we have

(3.4)

|\nabla\varphi|\leq CR^{-1}\varphi^{\bar{\alpha}},\quad|\Delta\varphi|+\varphi^{-1}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\varphi|\leq CR^{-2}\varphi^{\bar{\alpha}}.

In the rest of the proof,
\iint
denotes the integral over
Q_{R}
.

Step 2: Integral inequalities.

Now we estimate the right side of (
3.2
) as follows. Fix
\varepsilon>0
, we first consider the terms containing
\varphi
. By Young’s inequality and
q=2p/(p+1)
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle M^{2}\varphi|\nabla v|^{2q}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)M^{\frac{4}{q}}\varphi v^{\frac{2(q-2)p}{q}}|\nabla v|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)M^{\frac{4}{q}}\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}.

We also get

\displaystyle M\varphi|v_{t}||\nabla v|^{q}\leq

\displaystyle\varphi v_{t}^{2}+CM^{2}\varphi|\nabla v|^{2q},

\displaystyle\varphi v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}v_{t}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+C(\varepsilon)\varphi v_{t}^{2},

\displaystyle\varphi v^{p}|v_{t}|\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)\varphi v_{t}^{2},

and

M\varphi v^{p}|\nabla v|^{q}\leq\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)\varphi M^{2}|\nabla v|^{2q}.

Next for the terms containing
|\nabla\varphi|
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{3}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{\frac{3}{4}}v^{-\frac{3}{2}}|\nabla v|^{3}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}+C(\varepsilon)\left(v^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi^{-\frac{3}{4}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+C(\varepsilon)v^{2}\varphi^{-3}|\nabla\varphi|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{\frac{1}{p}}v^{2}\right)^{p}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{p}}|\nabla\varphi|^{4}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{4p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{\frac{4p}{p-1}},

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi||v_{t}||\nabla v|\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}|v_{t}|\right)^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla\varphi||\nabla v|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v_{t}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\varphi^{-1}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v_{t}^{2}+\varepsilon\left(v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\left(v|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\varphi^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v_{t}^{2}+\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+C(\varepsilon)v^{2}\varphi^{-3}|\nabla\varphi|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v_{t}^{2}+\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{4p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{\frac{4p}{p-1}},

and

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|v^{p}|\nabla v|\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{\frac{1}{4}}v^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla v|\right)^{4}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{4}}v^{p+\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+C(\varepsilon)\varphi^{-\frac{1}{3}}|\nabla\varphi|^{\frac{4}{3}}v^{\frac{4p+2}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{\frac{2p+1}{3p}}v^{\frac{4p+2}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3p}{2p+1}}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{3p}}|\nabla\varphi|^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{4p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{\frac{4p}{p-1}}.

Since
q=2p/(p+1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle M|\nabla\varphi||\nabla v|^{q+1}\leq

\displaystyle CM|\nabla\varphi||\nabla v|\left[\left(v^{-\frac{q}{2}}|\nabla v|^{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}+v^{\frac{q}{2-q}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle CM|\nabla\varphi|v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{3}+CM|\nabla\varphi|v^{p}|\nabla v|.

We now consider the terms containing
|\varphi_{t}|
and
|\Delta\varphi|

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\varphi_{t}|v^{p+1}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}v^{p+1}\right)^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\varphi_{t}|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\varphi_{t}|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle|\Delta\varphi||\nabla v|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{1}{2}}v|\Delta\varphi|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+C(\varepsilon)v^{2}\varphi^{-1}|\Delta\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\varepsilon\left(v^{2}\varphi^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{p}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{p}}|\Delta\varphi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

and

Therefore, substituting these estimates into (

\displaystyle\alpha I+\delta L-\beta G

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\varepsilon I+\varepsilon L+C(\varepsilon)M^{\frac{4}{q}}I+\iint\varphi v_{t}^{2}\right)+C(\varepsilon)\iint\varphi f^{2}_{\theta}

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon)\left[\iint\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{4p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{\frac{4p}{p-1}}+\iint\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\Delta\varphi|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}+\iint\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\varphi_{t}|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\right].

Finally, we estimate the time derivative term of
v
. From (
3.3

(3.9)

\displaystyle\iint\varphi v_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\varphi v_{t}\left(v^{p}+M|\nabla v|^{q}+\Delta v+f_{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\iint\varphi\left(v^{p+1}\right)_{t}+M\iint\varphi v_{t}|\nabla v|^{q}+\iint\varphi v_{t}\Delta v+\iint\varphi v_{t}f_{\theta}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\iint\varphi v^{-2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\varepsilon\iint\varphi v^{2p}+C(\varepsilon)\iint\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\varphi_{t}|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}.

Substituting (

\displaystyle\iint\varphi v_{t}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\varepsilon I+\varepsilon L+C(\varepsilon)M^{\frac{4}{q}}I\right)

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon)\iint\left[\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{4p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{\frac{4p}{p-1}}+\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\varphi_{t}|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\right]+C(\varepsilon)\iint\varphi f^{2}_{\theta}.

Hence, from (
3.11
) and (
3.8
), we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\alpha I+\delta L-\beta G

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\varepsilon I+\varepsilon L+C(\varepsilon)M^{\frac{4}{q}}I\right)+C(\varepsilon)\iint\varphi f^{2}_{\theta}

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon)\iint\left[\left(\varphi^{-\frac{3p+1}{4p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)^{\frac{4p}{p-1}}+\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\Delta\varphi|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}+\left(\varphi^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\varphi_{t}|\right)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\right].

We select
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(N,p)>0
suitable small, and there exists
\varepsilon_{0}(N,p)>0
such that, for
0<M\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

C(\varepsilon)M^{\frac{4}{q}}=C(N,p)M^{\frac{4}{q}}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}.

Combining with (
3.4
), and using the fact
\bar{\alpha}>(3p+1)/(4p)
, we obtain

\frac{\alpha}{2}I+\frac{\delta}{2}L-\beta G\leq C(N,p)R^{-\frac{4p}{p-1}+N+2}+C(\varepsilon)\iint\varphi f^{2}_{\theta}.

Using the fact that
\beta<0
and

\int_{-R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}/2}\int_{B_{R/2}}\varphi v^{2p}(x,t){\rm d}x{\rm d}t\to\int_{-R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}/2}\int_{B_{R/2}}u^{2p}(x,t){\rm d}x{\rm d}t\quad\text{as}\ \theta\to 0,

then Lemma
3.4
follows as
\theta\to 0
.
\Box

The proof of Theorem
1.8
.

From Lemma
3.4
, we know that

\int_{-R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}/2}\int_{B_{R/2}}u^{2p}(x,t){\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leq C(N,p)R^{-\frac{4p}{p-1}+N+2}.

Since
p<p_{B}\leq p_{S}
, letting
R\to\infty
, we deduce
u\equiv 0
.
\Box

Remark 3.5
.

Since equation (
1.1
) is scaling invariant when
q=2p/(p+1)
. Consequently, Theorem
1.8
can be proved using the universal bound of the solution as in
[
27
, Theorem 21.2]
. In fact, by repeating the same argument as in Lemma
3.4
(replacing the integration region
Q_{R}
by
Q_{1}
), we obtain that

(3.13)

\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\int_{B_{1/2}}u^{2p}(x,t){\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leq C(N,p).

For any
R>0
, then the rescaling function

u_{R}(x,t)=R^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(Rx,R^{2}t)

is also a solution of equation (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
for
q=2p/(p+1)
. Then from (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{-R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}/2}\int_{B_{R/2}}u^{2p}(y,s){\rm d}y{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{4p}{p-1}+N+2}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\int_{B_{1/2}}u_{R}^{2p}(x,t){\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p)R^{-\frac{4p}{p-1}+N+2}.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we deduce
u\equiv 0
.

4.
Universal estimates via Liouville-type theorems

We first recall the useful doubling lemma due to Poláčik, Quitter and Souplet
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
, which allows us to structure the rescaling procedure and obtain the local estimates. The doubling property is an expansion derived from Hu
[
16
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathscr{X},d)
be a complete metric space with metric
d
, and let
\emptyset\neq D\subset\Sigma\subset\mathscr{X}
, with
\Sigma
closed. Set
\Gamma=\Sigma\backslash D
. Let
M:D\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be bounded on compact subsets of
D
and fix a real number
k>0
. If
y\in D
is such that

M(y){\rm{dist}}(y,\Gamma)>2k,

then there exists
x\in D
such that

M(x){\rm{dist}}(x,\Gamma)>2k,\ \ M(x)\geq M(y),

and

M(z)\leq 2M(x)\ \ \text{\rm for all}\ z\in D\cap\overline{B}_{\mathscr{X}}\left(x,kM^{-1}(x)\right).

The proofs of Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
.
We set

M(u)=u^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+|\nabla u|^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}},

then the estimates (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) can be written as

(4.1)

M(u(x,t))\leq C\left(\sigma+d_{P}^{-1}((x,t),\partial D)\right),\quad(x,t)\in D,

where
C>0
and

\sigma:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&\text{under the assumptions of Theorem \ref{unifor-esti}},\\
&1,&&\text{under the assumptions of Theorem \ref{them:f-g}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Assume that the estimates (
4.1
) fails. Then there exist sequences of domains
\Omega_{k}
, times
T_{k}>0
, solutions
u_{k}
of (
1.1
) or (
1.12
) in
D_{k}:=\Omega_{k}\times(0,T_{k})
, respectively, and points
(y_{k},\tau_{k})\in D_{k}
such that

(4.2)

M_{k}(y_{k},\tau_{k}):=M(u_{k}(y_{k},\tau_{k}))>2k\left(\sigma+d_{P}^{-1}\left((y_{k},\tau_{k}),\partial D_{k}\right)\right)\geq 2kd_{P}^{-1}((y_{k},\tau_{k}),\partial D_{k}).

We use Lemma
4.1
with
\mathscr{X}=\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
, equipped with the parabolic distance
d_{P}
,
\Sigma=\Sigma_{k}=\overline{D}_{k}
and
\Gamma=\partial D_{k}
. Then there exist points
(x_{k},t_{k})\in D_{k}
, such that

(4.3)

M_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})>2kd_{P}^{-1}((x_{k},t_{k}),\partial D_{k}),\quad M_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})\geq M_{k}(y_{k},\tau_{k})>2k\sigma,

and

(4.4)

M_{k}(x,t)\leq 2M_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})\quad\text{in}\ \hat{D}_{k}:=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1};\ d_{P}\left((x,t),(x_{k},t_{k})\right)\leq kM_{k}^{-1}(x_{k},t_{k})\right\}.

Using the first inequality of (
4.3
), we have

d_{P}((x,t),(x_{k},t_{k}))<\frac{1}{2}d_{P}((x_{k},t_{k}),\partial D_{k}),\quad(x,t)\in\hat{D}_{k},

which implies
\hat{D}_{k}\subset D_{k}
. We set

(4.5)

\lambda_{k}=M_{k}^{-1}(x_{k},t_{k}),

and then rescale
u_{k}
by setting

(4.6)

v_{k}(y,s)=\lambda_{k}^{2/(p-1)}u_{k}\left(x_{k}+\lambda_{k}y,t_{k}+\lambda_{k}^{2}s\right),\quad(y,s)\in\tilde{D}_{k},

where

\tilde{D}_{k}:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N};\ |y|<k/2\right\}\times\left(-k^{2}/4,k^{2}/4\right).

Then the function
v_{k}
satisfies

\partial_{s}v_{k}-\Delta v_{k}=v^{p}_{k}+M|\nabla v_{k}|^{2p/(p+1)}\quad\text{in}\ \tilde{D}_{k},

or

\partial_{s}v_{k}-\Delta v_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2p/(p-1)}f\left(\lambda_{k}^{-2/(p-1)}v_{k}\right)+M|\nabla v_{k}|^{2p/(p+1)}\quad\text{in}\ \tilde{D}_{k}

under the assumptions of Theorem
1.9
or Theorem
1.10
, respectively. From (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we have

\left[v_{k}^{(p-1)/2}+|\nabla v_{k}|^{(p-1)/(p+1)}\right](0,0)=\lambda_{k}M_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})=1.

By (
4.4
), we also have

\left[v_{k}^{(p-1)/2}+|\nabla v_{k}|^{(p-1)/(p+1)}\right](y,s)\leq 2,\quad(y,s)\in\tilde{D}_{k}.

Using the assumption (
1.13
) on
f
and the boundedness of
\nabla v_{k}
, we derive

0\leq\lambda_{k}^{2p/(p-1)}f\left(\lambda_{k}^{-2/(p-1)}v_{k}(y,s)\right)+M|\nabla v_{k}(y,s)|^{2p/(p+1)}\leq C(1+M),\quad(y,s)\in\tilde{D}_{k}.

where
C=C(N,p,l)>0
.

Moreover, by the parabolic
L^{p}
estimates and embedding theorem, up to a subsequence, there exists a
v\in W^{2,1;r}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R})
such that

v_{k}\to v\quad\text{in}\ C^{2,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R})

for any
1<r<\infty
and
v
is nontrivial. Thus, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.9
,
v
is a nonnegative nontrivial solution of equation (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
with
0<M\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, this contradicts to Theorem
1.8
and thus proves Theorem
1.9
.

Now we consider the case of Theorem
1.10
. Applying the maximum principle, there exists a
s^{*}\in[-\infty,0)
such that

v=0\quad\text{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,s^{*}],

and

v>0\quad\text{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(s^{*},\infty).

Using (
4.3
) and (
4.5
), we obtain

\lambda_{k}\to 0\quad\text{\rm as}\ k\to\infty.

Again using assumption (
1.13
), we find that for fixed
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(s^{*},\infty)
,

\lambda_{k}^{2p/(p-1)}f\left(\lambda_{k}^{-2/(p-1)}v_{k}(y,s)\right)=v_{k}^{p}(y,s)s_{k}^{-p}f(s_{k})\to lv^{p}(y,s)\quad\text{\rm as}\ k\to\infty,

where
s_{k}=\lambda^{-2/(p-1)}v_{k}(y,s)
.
Consequently, we derive that
v
satisfies

v_{t}-\Delta v=lv^{p}+M|\nabla v|^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\quad\text{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(s^{*},\infty).

Since the uniqueness of solutions for the corresponding Cauchy problem guarantees
s^{*}=-\infty
, we deduce that
v
is a nonnegative nontrivial solution of

v_{t}-\Delta v=lv^{p}+M|\nabla v|^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\quad\text{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then under the transformation

w=l^{\frac{1}{p-1}}v,

we know that
w
is the nonnegative nontrivial solution of the equation

w_{t}-\Delta w=w^{p}+Ml^{-\frac{1}{p+1}}|\nabla w|^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\quad\text{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}.

When
M\leq\varepsilon_{1}:=l^{1/(p+1)}\varepsilon_{0}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(N,p)>0
defined in Theorem
1.8
, the nontrivial property of
w
contradicts to Theorem
1.8
. This proves Theorem
1.10
.

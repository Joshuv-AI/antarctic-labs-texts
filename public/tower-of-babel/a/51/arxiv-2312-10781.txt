The mixed Hilden braid group and the plat equivalence in handlebodies
Paolo Cavicchioli, Sofia Lambropoulou

Abstract
. Given a knot or link in the handlebody, H g H_{g} , of genus g g we prove that it can always be represented as the plat closure of a braid in H g H_{g} . We further establish the Hilden braid group for the handlebody, as a subgroup of the mixed braid group, whose elements have the first g g strands fixed forming the identity braid. We then formulate and prove the algebraic equivalence connecting mixed plats belonging to the same link isotopy class in H g H_{g} . The plat closure representation can be particularly suitable for computing knot invariants.

Introduction

The representation of links as plat closures of braids with an even number of strands dates back to the works of Hilden
[
18
]
and of Birman
[
7
]
, who in 1976 proved that any link in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
S^{3}
, the 3-sphere, could be represented as the plat closure of a braid in some braid group
\mathcal{B}_{2n}
.
As an example, recall that all rational knots/links (also known as 2-bridge knots/links) can be represented as the plat closure by a braid with 4 strands, leaving the last strand as the identity strand
[
3
]
. Further, in
[
18
]
Hilden described the plat isotopy of braids in the same braid group
\mathcal{B}_{2n}
, by means of a subgroup
K_{2n}
of
\mathcal{B}_{2n}
, the Hilden braid group. In
[
7
]
Birman extended the work of Hilden by the stabilization moves, to provide the equivalence relation for braids having isotopic plat closures. This braid equivalence is analogous to the classical Markov equivalence for braids with isotopic standard closures
[
24
]
. The Markov theorem together with the Alexander braiding theorem
[
1
]
furnish the algebraic theory for the isotopy equivalence of knots and links represented as standard closures of braids.

Since then, a lot of work has been done using the plat representation. For instance, in
[
6
]
Bigelow studied the possibility of computing the Jones polynomial of a link, represented as plat closure of a braid, in terms of the action of that braid over a homological pairing defined on a covering of the configuration space of
n
points into the
2n
-punctured disc. This result was analyzed in recent years also from a computational point of view
[
25
,
15
]
and in order to compute other link invariants, such as the Conway potential function
[
28
]
or other polynomial invariants
[
14
]
.

The above led many researchers to generalize the results obtained in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the more general setting of c.c.o. 3-manifolds and handlebodies. For instance, using Heegaard surfaces, in
[
12
]
Doll introduced the notion of
(g,b)
-decomposition or generalized bridge decomposition for links in a closed, connected and orientable (from now on c.c.o.)
3
-manifold, opening the way to the study of links in 3-manifolds via surface braid groups and their plat closures, see works of Bellingeri and Cattabriga
[
5
]
and Cattabriga and Gabrovšek
[
10
]
.

In a parallel development, in
[
22
]
, Lambropoulou and Rourke established the analogues of the Alexander and Markov theorems for knots and links in c.c.o. 3-manifolds, representing them first as mixed links in
S^{3}
and then as standard closures of mixed braids. For the equivalence they used the concept of the
L
-move. In
[
21
]
the mixed braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
is introduced and studied, which is the subgroup of the Artin braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g+n}
that contains the identity braid
I_{g}
on
g
strands as a fixed subbraid, enabling the algebraic formulation of the Markov theorem in c.c.o. 3-manifolds
[
23
]
.
Adapting the work in
[
22
]
to the setting of a genus
g
handlebody,
H_{g}
, in
[
21
]
Häring and Lambropoulou
[
17
]
, established the analogues of the Alexander and Markov theorems for knots and links in
H_{g}
, by representing them as standard closures of mixed braids, and utilizing the mixed braid groups and results from
[
23
]
. see also
[
26
]
.

The goal of this article is to establish the counterpart plat closure theory for handlebodies. We first show that it is always possible to represent a link in the handlebody
H_{g}
of genus
g
via the plat closure of a braid in
H_{g}
(Theorem
3.4
). Then we establish the Hilden braid group for
H_{g}
as the Hilden braid subgroup,
K_{g,2n}
, of the mixed braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
, and we provide a set of generators for
K_{g,2n}
(Theorem
4.3
). We finally formulate and prove the algebraic equivalence connecting two plats in
H_{g}
belonging to the same isotopy class (Theorem
5.5
). We are especially interested in this last part because it would be the starting point of the construction and computation of polynomial link invariants for links in the setting of handlebodies, and then extend further to the case of other 3-manifolds. With the present paper we fill in a gap in the literature, namely to study the plat closure for braids in a handlebody and formulate and prove their isotopy equivalence using the mixed Hilden braid group.

We also studied the problem of links in handlebodies in
[
11
]
from a different aspect: we proved that given the representation of a knot or link via the plat closure of a braid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, in a handlebody or in a thickened surface, it is possible to create algorithmically another braid representing the same link type but with the standard closure, and vice versa. In this paper we establish that every knot or link in
H_{g}
has a plat representative.

In Section
1
we recall the notions of interest in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Namely, the Artin braid group
\mathcal{B}_{m}
, the existence of standard and plat closure of braids, the braidings of knots and links to plats and to standardly closed braids, as well as the corresponding equivalence relations reflecting isotopy. In particular, in Section
1.4
we recall the definition of the Hilden braid group used in the plat equivalence.
In Section
2
we recall the representation of the handlebody
H_{g}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by the identity braid
I_{g}
and of links and braids in
H_{g}
by mixed links and mixed braids, and the definition and a presentation of the mixed braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g,m}
.
In Section
3
we first recall the standard closure of mixed braids, the mixed braiding and the
mixed braid equivalence (analogues of the Alexander and Markov theorems for
H_{g}
). We then define the plat closure of braids in
H_{g}
and of mixed braids, and we state and prove the first part of the main result, the existence of a representation of links in handlebodies via plat closure of braids in Section
3.2
.
In Section
4
we define the Hilden braid group for
H_{g}
and the mixed Hilden braid group, as the Hilden subgroup of the mixed braid group. Then, starting from a set of generators of the classical Hilden braid group, we obtain a set of generators for the mixed Hilden braid group, which is a crucial ingredient in proving the equivalence theorem.
Finally, in Section
5

1.
Preliminaries: the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}

In this section we first recall classical braids and their group structure. Then their standard closure, the Alexander theorem for braiding any oriented link, as well as the braid equivalence corresponding to classical link isotopy (the Markov theorem and its
L
-move analogue). We then proceed with recalling the plat closure for braids and the plat braiding. Finally, we recall the Hilden braid subgroup of the braid group and the plat closure braid equivalence corresponding to classical link isotopy, all by following the work of Birman on plats
[
7
]
. All of this will be our basic tools to prove the equivalence theorem in the setting of handlebodies.

1.1.
The Artin braid group

The Artin braid group
[
2
]
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, is defined as the fundamental group of the unordered configuration space of
n
points on the real plane, i.e. a group which elements are equivalence classes of
n
paths
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}),\psi_{i}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
going from a set of
n
points of the plane to themselves, and never intersecting (
\psi_{i}(t)\neq\psi_{j}(t),\forall t\in[0,1],i,j\in{1,\dots,n},i<j
).

The classical presentation of
B_{n}
is generated by elements
\sigma_{i},i\in{1,\dots,n-1}
, which exchanges strands
i
and
i+1
as in Fig.
1
, with relations:

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i},\qquad\text{if }|i-j|\geq 2

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}.

For further information concerning the braid group the reader can refer, for example, to
[
8
,
19
]
.

\sigma_{j}
and
\sigma_{j}^{-1}
generators.

1.2.
The standard closure, the braiding and the braid equivalence

In order for a braid to represent a link we have to ‘close’ it, for example identifying the starting set of points on the plane with itself. This operation can be viewed as connecting the top endpoints to their corresponding bottom endpoints with simple unlinked arcs, like in the right part of Fig.
2
. This is called
standard closure
of a braid. It is important to note that this closure naturally gives rise to an oriented link: indeed, if we consider the strands of the braid to be oriented downward, then all closing arcs will be oriented upward and the whole orientation is coherent.

Figure 2
.
On the right, the standard closure of a braid and its isotopy to sliding the closing arcs to the back of the braid. On the left, the plat closure of the same braid.

Conversely, by a result of H. Brunn and of J.W. Alexander
[
1
,
9
]
:

Theorem 1.1

(The Alexander theorem)
.

Every oriented link in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be represented as the standard closure of a braid in some
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Different braids (also with a different number of strands) can be closed to give the same (oriented) link up to isotopy. A theorem by A.A. Markov
[
24
]
(with an improvement by N. Weinberg
[
27
]
, reducing the original 3 moves to 2) gives us a way of determining whether closing two braids results in isotopic links or not:

Theorem 1.2

(The Markov theorem)
.

Let
\beta_{n}\in\mathcal{B}_{n},\beta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
be two braids, and let
L_{n},L_{m}
be the two links obtained by the standard closure of
\beta_{n},\beta_{m}
respectively. Then
L_{n}
is equivalent to
L_{m}
if and only if
\beta_{n}
can be obtained from
\beta_{m}
after a finite sequence of these two moves:

•

Conjugation:
\beta\sim\gamma\beta\gamma^{-1},\quad\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
;

•

Stabilization move:
\beta\sim\beta\sigma_{n}^{\pm 1}\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1},\quad\beta\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Fig.
3
illustrates abstractly the 2 moves of the theorem. In the figure a red closing arc is indicated, showing the isotopy between the (standard) closures of the middle and right-hand braid.

Figure 3
.
The conjugation (on the left) and the stabilization move (on the right).

Some years ago Lambropoulou and Rourke proved in
[
22
]
another version of the Markov theorem, with only one equivalence move: the
L
-move
. This is defined as follows (see abstract illustration in Fig.
4
): take a braid
\beta\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
and a point
P
in one of its strands and not aligned to any of the crossings or endpoints of
\beta
; cut the arc at
P
, bend the two resulting arcs apart by a small isotopy and introduce two new vertical arcs (slightly tilted) to two new
top and bottom endpoints in the vertical line of
P
. These two new arcs are both oriented downward and they run either both
under
all other arcs of the diagram (first instance in Fig.
4
) or both
over
(third instance in Fig.
4
) all other arcs of the diagram, thus performing an
under
L
-move
(or
L_{u}
-move
) and a
over
L
-move
(or

L_{o}
-move
) respectively. The right-hand illustration of the figure indicates an equivalent version of an
L_{o}
-move, where an in-box crossing is introduced, using braid isotopy. This last form allows to have algebraic expressions for the
L
-moves (see
[
22
]
). In the figure we have also indicated red closing arcs for the
L
-moves, showing that the (standard) closures of all braids are isotopic.

Figure 4
.
Performing an
L
-move. The two results are the
under
and
over

L
-moves. The right-hand illustration shows the version of an
L
-move with an in-box crossing.

The new statement is the following:

Theorem 1.3

(
L
-move braid equivalence)
.

Let
\beta_{n}\in\mathcal{B}_{n},\beta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
be two braids, and let
L_{n},L_{m}
be the two links obtained by the standard closure of
\beta_{n},\beta_{m}
respectively. Then
L_{n}
is equivalent to
L_{m}
if and only if
\beta_{n}
can be obtained from
\beta_{m}
after a finite sequence of
L
-moves and braid isotopies.

Note that the stabilization move of Theorem
1.2
is a special case of an
L
-move. Further, from Theorem
1.3
it follows that the braid conjugation of Theorem
1.2
can be achieved by the
L
-moves.

1.3.
The plat closure and representation of links as plat closures of braids

Let
B
be a braid with an even number of strands, i.e.
B\in\mathcal{B}_{2n}
some
n
. By
[
7
]
one can close the open ends by connecting adjacent top or bottom endpoints using simple arcs (see left part of Fig.
2
). This procedure is called the
plat closure
of the braid and the resulting link
plat
, denoted as
\overline{B}
.

We can extend the notion of plat closure also to braids with an odd number of strands as follows: let
\beta\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, we consider
{B}^{\prime}
to be the image of
\beta
under the natural embedding of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
into
\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
, and we define the plat closure of
\beta
as the plat closure of
{B}^{\prime}
with the procedure described above. In practice, we add an extra strand in the far right of the braid
\beta
.

It is important to note that, closing in the plat way does not keep the natural orientation of the braid. In fact, when we close the braid using those short arcs, we connect two downward strands.

In
[
7
]
(Proposition 1) Birman proved that it is always possible to describe any link as the plat closure of a braid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This is the plat analogue of the classical Alexander theorem that uses the standard closure of braids. The idea of Birman’s algorithm is simple: in a projection plane equipped with top to bottom direction, consider a knot or link diagram with no horizontal arcs. Then pull by an isotopy every local maximum to the top and every local minimum to the bottom, as abstracted in Fig.
5
. The result will be a plat.

Figure 5
.
The braiding process.

In
[
11
]
we remarked that, if one closes a braid via the classical standard closure and push by isotopy the closing arcs right next to the endpoints, then the resulting closed braid is a plat. See rightmost part of Fig.
2
. This observation provides an alternative proof of the Birman result (see
[
11
, Theorem 3.2]
).

1.4.
The Hilden braid group

Now we want to recall a subgroup of the braid group
\mathcal{B}_{2n}

[
18

\mathbb{R}^{3}

\pi:\{z=0\}

\mathbb{R}^{3}
into two halves; let the upper half be
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
and let the set of endpoints of the closure arcs in
\pi
be

Figure 6

\mathbb{R}^{3}

Then the group of isotopy classes
\Psi=[\psi]
of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms

is the classical braid group

.

The

[

1.5.
Plat equivalence

In
[
7
]
Birman proves that every link can be represented as the plat closure of a braid in some
\mathcal{B}_{2n}
. She also proves a result concerning equivalence between plat closures of braids:

Theorem 1.6
.

[
7
]

Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be two links, and let
B_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{2n_{1}},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{2n_{2}}
be two braids such that their plat closures define the same link types as
L_{1},L_{2}
respectively. Then
L_{1}
is equivalent to
L_{2}
if and only if there exists an integer
t\geq\max(n_{1},n_{2})
such that, for each
m\geq t
the elements:

B_{i}^{\prime}=B_{i}\sigma_{2n_{i}}\sigma_{2n_{i}+2}\dots\sigma_{2m-2}\in\mathcal{B}_{2m},\qquad i=1,2

are in the same double coset of
\mathcal{B}_{2m}
modulo the Hilden subgroup
K_{2m}
.

Fig.
9
illustrates abstractly the move in Theorem
1.6
. It can be seen using Reidemeister I moves that these two braids, closed in the plat way, give rise to the same link isotopy class.

Figure 9
.

B\in\mathcal{B}_{2n}
and the braid
B\sigma_{2n_{i}}\sigma_{2n_{i}+2}\dots\sigma_{2m-2}\in\mathcal{B}_{2m}
.

Figure 10
.

The equivalence move in Theorem
1.6
, as illustrated in Fig.
9
, is a multiple application of the
stabilization move
for plats (for a proper definition see Section
5
, Definition

). The stabilization move is depicted in Fig.

A crucial observation is now due.

Remark 1.7
.

Both instances of the stabilization move utilized in the equivalence relation between plats are identical to the standardly closed versions of the

-moves (recall Fig.

), forgetting orientation.

2.
Braids and plats in handlebodies

In this section we first discuss links and braids in a handlebody and recall their representations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the mixed links and mixed braids
[
22
,
17
]
. We also recall from
[
17
]
the standard closure of mixed braids, as adapted to handlebodies, and the analogues of braiding and braid equivalence for the standard closure. We proceed with defining the notion of plat closure for mixed braids and provide an algorithm for turning a link in a handlebody into a plat, directly analogous to that of
[
7
]
for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and also to
[
10
]
for thickened surfaces and for closed, connected and orientable 3-manifolds. We proceed with defining the appropriate Hilden braid subgroup for the mixed braid group and formulate and prove the plat closure mixed braid equivalence in the setting of handlebodies.

2.1.
Handlebody representation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

Let
H_{g}
be the abstract handlebody of genus
g
.
H_{g}
can be constructed from a 3-ball with
g
1-handles attached (see left part of Fig.

). Note that, for
g=0
then
H_{0}
is just the 3-ball, while for
g=1
then

is the solid torus. Equivalently,
H_{g}
can be constructed as the product space of the

-punctured disc,

, cross the interval (middle illustration of Fig.

H_{g}=D_{g}\times[0,1].

.

.

Hence
H_{g}
, as embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, may be viewed as the complement of the identity braid

on
g
indefinitely extended strands (see right illustration of Fig.

). For viewing

in

we consider the "compactification", namely all strands of

in

).

2.2.
Links in handlebodies and mixed links

Let now
L
be a link in
H_{g}
. Then, using the above representation. we can consider
L
to lie in the interior of
H_{g}=D_{g}\times[0,1]
, except perhaps for finitely many points lying in the boundary components
D_{g}\times\{0,1\}
. Further, fixing

pointwise,
L
may be unambiguously represented by the mixed tangle (or
mixed link
)

, see left illustration of Fig.

I_{g}
forms the so-called
fixed part
of the mixed link, while
L
forms the so-called
moving part
of the mixed link. If we remove
I_{g}
from
I_{g}\cup L
we are left with a link
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which we shall call a
template
for the initial link
L
in
H_{g}
. Note that, for a given template
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
there are infinitely many links in
H_{g}
having
L
as the moving part and
I_{g}
as the fixed part, that is, there are infinitely many links in
H_{g}
with the given template. Further, two (polygonal) links in
H_{g}
are called
isotopic
if there is a finite sequence of the well-known
\Delta
-moves taking one to the other. An analogous definition carries through to the corresponding mixed links, see
[
22
,
17
]
.

.

2.3.
Braids in handlebodies, mixed braids and the mixed braid group

Definition 2.1
.

A
braid
\beta
in
H_{g}
on
n
strands
is defined as an embedding
\phi
of
n
distinct copies of
I=[0,1]
,
I_{j}=[0,1],j=1,\dots,n
in
H_{g}
, such that each
\phi_{j}:=\phi|_{I_{j}}
is monotonous, and the top
n
endpoints of the braid
\beta
lie in
D_{g}\times\{0\}
and the corresponding bottom
n
endpoints of
\beta
lie in
D_{g}\times\{1\}
, in the realization of
H_{g}
as
D_{g}\times[0,1]
.

Following
[
22
,
17
]
, the braid
\beta
in
H_{g}
is represented uniquely in
S^{3}
by a
geometric mixed braid
on
n
moving strands, denoted
I_{g}\cup\beta
, which is an element of the Artin braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g+n}
and which contains the identity braid
I_{g}
on
g
strands as a fixed subbraid (see abstraction in middle illustration of Fig.
12
).

By removing
I_{g}
we are left with the
moving subbraid

\beta
on
n
strands. We shall call this braid
\beta
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
a
template
for the initial braid
\beta
in
H_{g}
. Note that, for a given template
\beta
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
there are infinitely many geometric mixed braids having
\beta
as the moving subbraid and
I_{g}
as the fixed subbraid and, consequently, infinitely many braids in
H_{g}
with the given template.

A special case of geometric mixed braid on
g+n
strands is one for which, by removing the last
n
strands we are left with the identity braid
I_{g}
. Such a mixed braid shall be called
algebraic mixed braid
(notion introduced in
[
21
]
). See abstraction in rightmost illustration of Fig.
12
). We shall use the same notation for algebraic mixed braids as for the generality of geometric mixed braids.

It is established in
[
21
]
that the set of all algebraic mixed braids on
g
fixed strands and
n
moving strands form a group, the
mixed braid group
,
\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
. In other words,
\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
is the subgroup of all elements in
\mathcal{B}_{g+n}
for which the first
g
strands form the identity braid
I_{g}
. Of course, for
g=0
, we obtain the standard definition of the Artin braid group in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, while for
g=1
, we obtain the Artin braid group of type
B
,
\mathcal{B}_{1,n}
, which is the algebraic counterpart of knot theory in the solid torus and in the lens spaces (see
[
20
]
). It is further established in
[
17
]
that the groups
\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, comprise the algebraic counterpart for knots and links in
H_{g}
.

In
[
21
]
, it is proved that
\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
has a presentation with generators: the elementary crossings among moving strands
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
(see Fig.
13
) and the
loop generators

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{g}
, where
\alpha_{i}
represents a right-handed looping of the first moving strand around the
i
-th fixed strand, as depicted in Fig.
13
, where also the inverse of a loop generator is depicted. Further, the generators satisfy the relations:

(1)

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{j}&=&\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}&|k-j|>1,\\
\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_{k}&=&\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k+1}&1\leq k\leq n-1,\\
\alpha_{r}\sigma_{k}&=&\sigma_{k}\alpha_{r}&k\geq 2,\ 1\leq r\leq g,\\
\alpha_{r}\sigma_{1}\alpha_{r}\sigma_{1}&=&\sigma_{1}\alpha_{r}\sigma_{1}\alpha_{r}&1\leq r\leq g,\\
\alpha_{r}(\sigma_{1}\alpha_{s}\sigma_{1}^{-1})&=&(\sigma_{1}\alpha_{s}\sigma_{1}^{-1})\alpha_{r}&s<r.\end{array}

Figure 13
.
The generators of
\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
.

From the above, an algebraic mixed braid
I_{g}\cup\beta
on
n
moving strands represents uniquely in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
a braid
\beta
on
n
strands in
H_{g}=D_{g}\times[0,1]
, such that its endpoints lie all within a disc on the right-hand side of
D_{g}
. This leads to the following:

Definition 2.2
.

A braid
\beta
on
n
strands in
H_{g}=D_{g}\times[0,1]
, such that its endpoints lie all within a disc on the right-hand side of
D_{g}
shall be called
algebraic braid in
H_{g}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Fixing a set of
n
points
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\}
in
D_{g}
we observe that the set of all braid on
n
strands in
H_{g}
, with endpoints in
\mathcal{C}
forms a group by concatenation along the surface
D_{g}
. Clearly, groups related to different sets of endpoints are isomorphic. So, without loss of generality, one may assume that the set
\mathcal{C}
lies in a disc
A
on the right-hand side of
D_{g}
, as in Fig.
14
. By definition, these groups for different sets of endpoints coincide with the
surface braid group of
D_{g}
on
n
strands
, which we shall denote
\mathcal{B}_{D_{g},n}
(the surface braid groups were introduced in
[
13
]
, see also
[
16
,
4
]
). Furthermore, by the well-defined correspondence between mixed braids and braids in
H_{g}
, and of their operations, the surface braid group
\mathcal{B}_{D_{g},n}
is easily shown to be isomorphic to the mixed braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
.

C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{n}
on the two levels

and

.

3.
Representing links via braids in handlebodies

3.1.
The standard closure for mixed braids, the mixed braiding and the mixed braid equivalence

Let
I_{g}\cup\beta
be a geometric mixed braid, representing uniquely the braid
\beta
in
H_{g}
. We recall from
[
17
]
that, in order to define well the (standard) closure of
I_{g}\cup\beta
, we first label each pair of corresponding endpoints of
\beta
with ‘o’ (for over) or ‘u’ (for under). Then, the
standard closure
of
I_{g}\cup\beta
is an oriented mixed link obtained by joining with simple unlinked arcs each pair of corresponding endpoints of the moving subbraid
\beta
, according to its label: the simple arc will be lying entirely over or entirely under the rest of the mixed braid, if its label is ‘o’ or ‘u’ respectively. See Fig.
15
(a) for an abstract illustration.

Note that, the definition of standard closure for mixed braids induces the definition of
standard closure for braids in
H_{g}=D_{g}\times[0,1]
.

Figure 15
.
(a) the standard closure of a geometric mixed braid;
(b) a parted mixed braid and its homologous closure.

As pointed out in
[
17
]
, different choices of labels for a geometric mixed braid will, in general, result in non-isotopic mixed links. Yet, this is not the case if one restricts to algebraic mixed braids, since a closing arc can freely move through isotopy from the front to the back of the mixed braid (and vice versa), without intersecting any fixed strand.

The inverse of the closing operation is also true
[
17
]
:
any oriented mixed link (representing uniquely an oriented link in
H_{g}
), can be braided to a geometric mixed braid with isotopic standard closure. This is an analogue of the classical Alexander theorem for knots and links in
H_{g}
. Furthermore, the parting operation enables a geometric mixed braid to be represented by an algebraic mixed braid with isotopic standard closure. Fig.
15
(b) depicts a parted mixed braid and its homologous closure. Hence,
any oriented mixed link can be braided to an algebraic mixed braid with isotopic standard closure.
This is the algebraic analogue of the Alexander theorem for knots and links in
H_{g}
.

Moreover, mixed link isotopy for oriented links in
H_{g}
is translated in
[
17
]
into mixed braid equivalence: first as
L
-equivalence among geometric mixed braids (an analogue of the one-move Markov theorem), and then as an algebraic mixed braid equivalence in
\bigcup_{n}{\mathcal{B}_{g,n}}
(an analogue of the classical Markov theorem). For details the reader may consult
[
17
]
.

3.2.
The plat closure for braids in
H_{g}
and for mixed braids

Definition 3.1
.

Let
I_{g}\cup B
be a geometric mixed braid with an even number of moving strands, say
2n
. For defining the plat closure of
I_{g}\cup B
we first number the set of top endpoints of
B
, from left to right, with numbers
1,1^{\prime},2,2^{\prime},\ldots,n,n^{\prime}
. To each pair
(i,i^{\prime})
a label ‘o’ (for over) or ‘u’ (for under) is assigned. Then, we use a simple arc, say
\gamma_{i}
, as joining arc for the pair of endpoints
(i,i^{\prime})
, so that
\gamma_{i}
runs entirely over or entirely under any fixed strand of the mixed diagram, according to the label of
(i,i^{\prime})
. We repeat the above procedure, independently, for the set of corresponding bottom endpoints. ‘Independently’ in the sense that the labels of the closing arcs, say
\delta_{i}
, for joining the pairs of bottom endpoints are not related to the labels of the closing arcs
\gamma_{i}
for joining the pairs of top endpoints. View Fig.
16
for an example. The resulting mixed link is a
plat closure
for
I_{g}\cup B
, called
mixed plat
, and shall be denoted
I_{g}\cup\overline{B}
.

In the special case where
I_{g}\cup B
is an algebraic mixed braid the resulting plat shall be called
algebraic mixed plat
.

Remark 3.2
.

As for the case of standard closure, the definition of plat closure for mixed braids induces the definition of
plat closure for braids in
H_{g}=D_{g}\times[0,1]
. Only, here we also add small local isotopies of the closing arcs of a braid
B
, so that the resulting plat, denoted
\overline{B}
, lies in the interior of
H_{g}
, except perhaps finitely many points.

Remark 3.3
.

Different labels for closing arcs may result in non-isotopic plats. An example is illustrated in Fig.
16
. Yet, note that the label of a joining arc is irrelevant if no fixed strands of
I_{g}
lie between the two endpoints. It follows, in particular, that if
I_{g}\cup B
is an algebraic mixed braid, then no labels are required for forming the plat closure, as no choices are involved in this case and the plat closure is determined uniquely.

Figure 16
.
Two different examples of plat closure of a mixed braid. The two resulting links are not isotopic.

3.3.
Representing mixed links as plat closures of mixed braids

Let
L
be a link in
H_{g}
represented by the mixed link
I_{g}\cup L
. We want to show that the mixed link
I_{g}\cup L
may be isotoped to the (labelled) plat closure of a geometric mixed braid, providing thus an analogue of the Birman braiding theorem for knots and links in
H_{g}
. Namely, we have the following, which is also an analogue of the braiding theorem in
H_{g}
, but for plats.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
L
be a link in the genus
g
handlebody
H_{g}
, represented by the mixed link
I_{g}\cup L
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
L
may be braided to a braid in
H_{g}
with even number of strands, whose plat closure is isotopic to
L
in
H_{g}
. Equivalently,
I_{g}\cup L
may be braided to a geometric mixed braid with even number of moving strands, whose plat closure is isotopic to
I_{g}\cup L
.

Proof.

We shall adapt the braiding algorithms of
[
7
,
10
]
to our setting. Indeed, let
\overrightarrow{D_{g}}
be the unit vector orthogonal to
D_{g}
.
It is always possible (using local isotopies, if needed) to consider
L\subset D_{g}\times[0.25,0.75]
.

Consider a polygonal representation of
L
, with set of vertices
\{b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}
and with no edges parallel to
D_{g}\times\{0\}
(which is always possible by a general position argument). Following
[
7
, Lemma 2]
, we have that for any 3 consecutive vertices
b_{j-1},b_{j},b_{j+1}
of
L
the line
l_{j}
orthogonal to
D_{g}
through
b_{j}
admits a neighbourhood
N_{j}
which meets the link only in the edges
[b_{j-1},b_{j}]
and
[b_{j},b_{j+i}]
(see Fig.
17
). We will say that
L
is in standard position.

Figure 17
.
The braiding process.

Then, starting from
b_{0}
, for every three points
b_{j-1},b_{j},b_{j+1}
with

\left(\overrightarrow{[b_{j-1},b_{j}]}\cdot\overrightarrow{D_{g}}\right)\cdot\left(\overrightarrow{[b_{j},b_{j+1}]}\cdot\overrightarrow{D_{g}}\right)<0

(meaning that
b_{j}
is either a local maximum or a local minimum) consider the intersections
[b_{j-1},b_{j}]\cap N_{j}=b^{\prime}_{j-1}
,
[b_{j},b_{j+1}]\cap N_{j}=b^{\prime}_{j+1}
. If
\overrightarrow{[b_{j-1},b_{j}]}\cdot\overrightarrow{D_{g}}>0
(resp.
<0
) let
b^{\prime}_{j}=l_{j}\cap D_{g}\times\{1\}
(resp
b^{\prime}_{j}=l_{j}\cap D_{g}\times\{0\}
). If we replace the edge sequence
b_{j-1},b_{j},b_{j+1}
with
b_{j-1},b^{\prime}_{j-1},b^{\prime}_{j},b^{\prime}_{j+1},b_{j+1}
the isotopy class of the link does not change, while now the link intersects once
D_{g}\times\{0,1\}
.

Repeating this operation for every three points satisfying the above condition, we will obtain an isotopic link meeting the discs
D_{g}\times\{0\}
and
D_{g}\times\{1\}
in exactly, say,
n
points, and every other disc
D_{g}\times\{t\},t\in(0,1)
, in exactly
2n
points. It follows from Artin’s definition of a braid
[
2
]
that such a link determines a well-defined geometric mixed braid
I_{g}\cup B
on
2n
moving strands. In turn, this mixed braid represents the link
L
via plat closure in
H_{g}=D_{g}\times[0,1]
.
∎

Furthermore, we prove that any mixed link (resp. link in
H_{g}
) may be braided to (resp. represented by) an
algebraic mixed braid
(recall Definition
2.2
) via plat closure. For this we first recall from
[
7
]
and adapt the definition of the spike move.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\overline{B}
be a link in
H_{g}
in plat form, represented in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by the mixed plat
I_{g}\cup\overline{B}
. Let also
b_{i},b_{i+1},b_{i+2}
be three consecutive points of
\overline{B}
, such that
b_{i+1}
is a boundary point (i.e.
\in D_{g}\times\{0,1\}
) and such that the triangle
[b_{i},b_{i+1},b_{i+2}]
is entirely contained in
D_{g}\times[0,1]
and intersects
\overline{B}
only on its sides
[b_{i},b_{i+1}]
and
[b_{i+1},b_{i+2}]
(see Fig.
18
). Edges like
[b_{i},b_{i+1}],[b_{i+1},b_{i+2}]
are called
boundary edges
. Let now
b^{\prime}_{i+1}
be a new boundary point on the same boundary component as
b_{i+1}
and let
b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+2}
be interior points satisfying the conditions:

•

[b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+1},b^{\prime}_{i+2}]\cap L=\emptyset
and
[b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+1},b^{\prime}_{i+2}]
lies entirely in
D_{g}\times[0,1]
;

•

for the region
[b_{i},b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+2},b_{i+2}]
bounded by the points
b_{i},b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+2},b_{i+2}
we have:

[b_{i},b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+2},b_{i+2}]\subset D_{g}\times[0,1]\ \ \&\ \ [b_{i},b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+2},b_{i+2}]\cap L=\{b_{i},b_{i+2}\}
.

A
spike move
for the plat
\overline{B}
consists in replacing the edges defined by the sequence
b_{i},b_{i+1},b_{i+2}
by the edges defined by the sequence
b_{i},b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+1},b^{\prime}_{i+2},b_{i+2}
. Then the isotopy class of the plat
\overline{B}
does not change and the spike move preserves the plat form.

A
spike move for the mixed plat

I_{g}\cup\overline{B}
is defined analogously, except that two of the conditions above are replaced by the conditions:

[b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+1},b^{\prime}_{i+2}]\cap(I_{g}\cup\overline{B})=\emptyset\ \mbox{and}\ [b_{i},b^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i+2},b_{i+2}]\cap I_{g}=\emptyset.

The conditions imply, in particular, that if a fixed strand overlies (resp. underlies) the triangle
[b_{i},b_{i+1},b_{i+2}]
then the fixed strand also overlies (resp. underlies) the replacement of the spike move. Then the isotopy class of the mixed plat
I_{g}\cup\overline{B}
does not change and the spike move preserves the mixed plat form.

Figure 18
.
A spike move.

We can now sharpen Theorem
3.4
to representing links in
H_{g}
as plat closures of algebraic mixed braids.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
L
be a link in the genus
g
handlebody
H_{g}
, represented by the mixed link
I_{g}\cup L
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
L
may be braided to an algebraic braid in
H_{g}
with even number of strands, whose plat closure is isotopic to
L
in
H_{g}
.
Equivalently,
I_{g}\cup L
may be braided to an algebraic mixed braid
I_{g}\cup B\in\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
, for some
n
, representing
I_{g}\cup L
via plat closure.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
, we first obtain from
I_{g}\cup L
an isotopic geometric mixed plat
I_{g}\cup\overline{B}
, where
I_{g}\cup B\in\mathcal{B}_{g+2n}
for some
n
, such that
B
comes with a set of
2n
closing labels. Note that
I_{g}\cup B
does not necessarily belong to the mixed braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
. For doing this we adapt the parting technique, as in
[
17
]
for the standard closure.

Indeed, we perform spike moves to all boundary edges which are on the left of some strands of
I_{g}
, so that we part the mixed plat, bringing all the boundary edges to the right of all strands of
I_{g}
(see an example in Fig.
19
). We shall refer to this procedure as a
plat parting
of the geometric braid. It is important to note that a spike move, by definition, respects locally the label of a closing arc. Otherwise, the closing labels do not restrict the spike moves. In the end of the parting operation we obtain an algebraic mixed plat,
I_{g}\cup\overline{B^{\prime}}
, such that
I_{g}\cup B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
. At this moment, the
2n
closing labels do not matter anymore, as no fixed strands are in between moving strands, recall Remark
3.3
.

Equivalently, on the level of
H_{g}
, using Theorem
3.4
we first obtain from
L
a plat in
H_{g}
and then, using appropriate spike moves, we bring the resulting plat to a new one whose closing arcs lie within a disc on the right-hand side of
D_{g}
.
∎

4.
The mixed Hilden braid group

As in the classical case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we want to define the Hilden braid group also in the setting of a handlebody. We will do that adapting Definition
1.4
to the context of the surface braid group of
D_{g}
(recall Remark
2.3
) resp. the mixed braid group.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{2m}\}
be a set of
2m
points in
D_{g}
, which w.l.o.g. lie in a disc
A
on the right-hand side of
D_{g}
. The
Hilden group of the handlebody
H_{g}
, denoted
K_{D_{g},2m}
, is defined as the subgroup of
\mathcal{B}_{D_{g},2m}
generated by the equivalence classes of homeomorphisms of
D_{g}\times I
leaving the set of closure arcs invariant on its top/bottom boundary.

Furthermore, the
mixed Hilden group

K_{g,2m}
is defined as the subgroup of the mixed braid group
\mathcal{B}_{g,2m}
generated by the equivalence classes of homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
leaving the set of bottom (resp. top) plat closure arcs invariant on its boundary.

Remark 4.2
.

Clearly, an isomorphism between
\mathcal{B}_{D_{g},2m}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g,2m}
induces an isomorphism between
K_{D_{g},2m}
and
K_{g,2m}
. Hence, and following the idea of Remark
2.3
, the mixed Hilden braid group can be viewed either as the Hilden subgroup of the mixed braid group of the handlebody or the Hilden subgroup of the surface braid group of
D_{g}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

A set of generators for
K_{g,2m}
is

\{\sigma_{1},\ \lambda_{1}=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2},\ \mu_{i}=\sigma_{2i}\sigma_{2i-1}\sigma_{2i+1}\sigma_{2i},\ 1\leq i\leq m-1;\ \tau_{i,2j-1},\ 1\leq i\leq g,\,1\leq j\leq m\},

where:

\displaystyle\tau_{i,1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}\,\sigma_{1}\alpha_{i}\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\tau_{i,2j-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2j-2}\sigma_{2j-3}\sigma_{2j-1}\sigma_{2j-2}\dots\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{2}\alpha_{i}\sigma_{1}\,\alpha_{i}\,\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma_{3}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{-1}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\dots\sigma_{2j-2}^{-1}\sigma_{2j-1}^{-1}\sigma_{2j-3}^{-1}\sigma_{2j-2}^{-1}

The generators
\sigma_{1},\lambda_{1},\mu_{i},\tau_{i,2j-1}
shall be referred to as ‘mixed Hilden generators’.

View Fig.
20
for the generators
\sigma_{1},\lambda_{1}
and
\mu_{i}
and Fig.
21
for the generators
\tau_{i,2j-1}
.

Figure 20
.
The mixed Hilden generators
\sigma_{1},\lambda_{1}
and
\mu_{i}
.

.
The mixed Hilden generator

.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}_{g,2m}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{B}_{g+2m}
, the idea is to make use of Proposition
1.5
. For this, we need to also argue that
K_{g,2m}
is a subgroup of
K_{g+2m}
. If
g
is even
then
K_{g+2m}
is well-defined and, hence,
K_{g,2m}
coincides with the subgroup of
K_{g+2m}
keeping fixed the first
g
strands. In the case of
g
odd
this does not make sense, because the mixed Hilden subgroup is defined only for an even number of moving strands. For this reason we will introduce in this case a
dummy strand
with number zero on the left of the first strand of any element of
K_{g+2m}
as well as on the left of the first fixed strand of any element of
K_{g,2m}
. The dummy strand does not interact with any other strand of the braid. In this way, every braid will have
(1+g+2m)
strands, and we can define then the dummied
K^{\prime}_{g,2m}
to be the Hilden subgroup of the dummied
K^{\prime}_{g+2m}
, for
g
odd. With the above trick, we can always assume that
K_{g+2m}
is well-defined and
K_{g,2m}
is a subgroup of
K_{g+2m}
.

In using Proposition
1.5
, we will start by examining the full set of generators for the group
K_{g+2m}
.
Then, the generators of
K_{g,2m}
will be the ones inherited by
K_{g+2m}
, but with different enumeration and some small adjustments for both cases for
g
, even or odd. First of all, since the first
g
strands of an element of
K_{g,2m}
are fixed, the
\sigma_{i}
generators involving the first
g
strands need to be eliminated. Likewise
for the generators
\mu_{i}
with indices smaller than
g
, since the first
g
strands do not participate in the plat closure. Second, we can divide
\rho_{ij}
and
\omega_{ij}
generators into three families depending on their indices:

•

2i,2j\leq g
: these generators need to be eliminated, since they move fixed strands;

•

2i\leq g,\ 2j>g
: these generators still exist in
K_{g,2m}
, but we need to write them using generators of the mixed braid group

and renumber the indices;

5.
The equivalence between plat closed mixed braids

Before going on, let us recall from
[
7
]
the definition of stabilization move for plat closed braids, adapted to the case of a link in the handlebody
H_{g}
, or equivalently, a mixed link.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
a,b,c
be consecutive vertices of our link in
H_{g}
(resp. a mixed link
I_{g}\cup L
) in polygonal form. Let
b^{\prime}
(resp.
b^{\prime\prime}
) be the projections of
b
onto
D_{g}\times\{0\}
,
D_{g}\times\{1\}
(resp. on the top and bottom lines containing the endpoints), and
b_{1},b_{2}
the intersection points of a sufficiently small neighbourhood of
b
with the segments
[a,b]
and
[b,c]
. The
stabilization move
is the substitution of
[a,b]
and
[b,c]
with the segments
[a,b_{1}],[b_{1},b^{\prime}],[b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}],[b^{\prime\prime},b_{2}]
and
[b_{2},c]
. View Fig.
23
.

By definition, a stabilization move is an isotopy.

.

H_{g}
.

Let us now consider two plat closed mixed braids
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
such that one can be obtained by the other by only applying elements of the mixed Hilden group
K_{g,2n}
. Then, since all the generators of
K_{g,2n}
represent local isotopies for a plat closed mixed braid, the two braids represent the same link isotopy class via plat closure. To formulate and prove the converse statement, a previous result of Birman helps us:

Lemma 5.2
.

(
[
7
, Lemma 8]
)
Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be two isotopic polygonal links in general position, i.e. with no horizontal edges and such that each vertex has nothing above or underneath it in a small neighbourhood of it. Then it is possible to connect the two links by a finite sequence of spike moves and stabilization moves.

The lemma is proved in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, by examining the possible
\Delta
-moves. The only difference when adapting to
H_{g}
is that the
\Delta
-moves miss the vertical lines of the punctures. But this fact does not present any obstacle, since the spike moves and the stabilization moves are the same moves as in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and they also miss the vertical lines (the same arguments carry through also to the setting of mixed links). The spike move for the case of
H_{g}
is defined in Definition
3.5
, while the stabilization move is defined just above, in Definition
5.1
. Namely we have:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be two isotopic polygonal links in the handlebody
H_{g}
, resp. mixed links, in general position, i.e. with no horizontal edges and such that each vertex has nothing above or underneath it in a small neighbourhood of it. Then it is possible to connect the two links by a finite sequence of spike moves and stabilization moves.

Before formulating and proving the plat equivalence for the handlebody we will introduce a special case of a stabilization move, the
st_{k}
move.

Definition 5.4
.

An
st_{k}
move
is a stabilization move between two algebraic mixed braids
B,B^{\prime}\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
, defined as:

\begin{array}[]{cccl}st_{k}:&\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}&\rightarrow&\mathcal{B}_{g,2n+2}\\
&B&\mapsto&B\sigma_{2k}^{-1}\sigma_{2k+1}^{-1}\dots\sigma_{2n-1}^{-1}\sigma_{2n}\sigma_{2n-1}\dots\sigma_{2k+1}\sigma_{2k}\end{array}

This move, depicted abstractly in Fig.
24
, represents a special case of a stabilization move between algebraic mixed braids, taking place at the bottom of an even numbered strand
2k
, with a positive crossing.

.

st_{k}
move.

Theorem 5.5
.

Two braids
B,B^{\prime}\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
determine, via plat closure, equivalent links in the handlebody
H_{g}
of genus
g
if and only if they are connected by braid relations and a finite sequence of the following moves:

\begin{array}[]{rrcccl}h_{\sigma}:&\sigma_{1}B&\longleftrightarrow&B&\longleftrightarrow&B\sigma_{1},\\[3.0pt]
h_{\lambda}:&\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}B&\longleftrightarrow&B&\longleftrightarrow&B\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2},\\[3.0pt]
h_{\mu}:&\sigma_{2i}\sigma_{2i-1}\sigma_{2i+1}\sigma_{2i}B&\longleftrightarrow&B&\longleftrightarrow&B\sigma_{2i}\sigma_{2i-1}\sigma_{2i+1}\sigma_{2i},\ \ i=1,\dots,n\\[3.0pt]
h_{\tau}:&\alpha_{j}\sigma_{1}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{1}B&\longleftrightarrow&B&\longleftrightarrow&B\alpha_{j}\sigma_{1}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{1},\ \ j=1,\dots,g\\[3.0pt]
st_{n}:&&&B&\longleftrightarrow&B\sigma_{2n}\end{array}

The moves
h_{\sigma},h_{\lambda},h_{\mu},h_{\tau}
reflect the generators of the mixed Hilden braid group (recall Figs.
20
and
21
), so do not alter the number of strands, while the move
st_{n}
, defined above, realizes the stabilization move that may take place at the bottom right of the last strand numbered
2n
.

Proof.

The one direction of the proof is obvious. Given now a link
L
in
H_{g}
in standard position, we obtain by Theorem
3.4
an isotopic link (a plat) with
n
points of intersection with
D_{g}\times\{0\}
, say
Y^{u}=\{Y_{1}^{u},\dots,Y^{u}_{n}\}
, and
n
points of intersection with
D_{g}\times\{1\}
, say
Y^{l}=\{Y_{1}^{l},\dots,Y^{l}_{n}\}
.
In order to find an element
B\in\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
such that its plat closure is isotopic
L
, we have to perform plat parting to the geometric mixed plat. The plat parting comprises a sequence of specific spike moves and is described in the proof of Theorem
3.6
.

For each parting move there is a choice of performing it by pulling over or under strands to the right.
For proving Theorem
5.5
we will argue that these choices are included in the equivalence relation.
Indeed, for a parting choose two paths
p
and
p^{\prime}
in the configuration space of
n
points on
D_{g}
connecting, respectively, the two sets of boundary points
Y^{u}
and
Y^{l}
to a set of points
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\}
, where
C_{i}
is an internal point of the disk
A
(recall Fig.
14
). This choice of paths is not unique: if
p
and
q
are two choices for
Y^{l}
, the composition of
p
and the inverse of
q
is an element in the fundamental group of the configuration space of
\mathcal{C}
, that could be realized as a composition of elements of the mixed Hilden braid group. The same construction holds for the set of upper boundary points. This means that any two element of
\mathcal{B}_{g,2n}
obtained as above for the same link
L
in standard position are connected by moves
h_{\sigma},h_{\lambda},h_{\mu},h_{\tau}
.
It would be illuminating to consider the special case of the choice of the plat parting (recall Fig.
21
) by pulling a pair of consecutive strands
over
a fixed strand versus the choice of pulling them
under
the same fixed strand. Such a choice difference shows in the final algebraic braid words as a mixed Hilden generator
\tau_{i,2j-1}
(recall Fig.
21
).

By Lemma
5.2
, it is now sufficient to show how stabilization and spike moves change the algebraic mixed braid representative
B

\overline{B}

Recall that a stabilization move only adds a trivial loop to

at any point. Using the sequence of moves depicted in Fig.

Let us, finally, consider a spike move taking place in
\overline{B}
, obtaining

C_{i}
into another point in the disk

C_{i}

The plane

Suppose, finally, that starting from an algebraic mixed plat we perform a spike move toward the left part of the braid, obtaining a geometric mixed plat. In this case, we just use for the parting the inverse move.

The proof of the Theorem is now complete.

1.
Introduction

The Grothendieck’s vanishing theorem says that

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for all
j>d:=\dim R
where
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)

is noetherian and local. As soon as the ring is not regular
we see
H^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k,k)\neq 0
. Also, it is well-known
that
H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N)=0
for all
j>d
provided
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty

or
\operatorname{id}(N)<\infty
or even some modifications of these, see e.g.

[
16
]
.
Despite a lot of works in this area, and up to my knowledge, there is not so data on the following basic problem:

Problem 1.1
.

What is
\operatorname{cd}(M,N):=\sup\{j:H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N)\neq 0\}
?

And one may ask even more; what is the significance of
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)
?
Towards the symmetry in the vanishing of
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(-,+)
, for finitely generated modules over local Gorenstein rings, Huneke and Jorgensen
[
17
]
introduced
“
\operatorname{AB}
-rings”. This class of rings lives between complete-intersection and Gorenstein rings. We refer the reader to see
[
17
]
for the systematic study of
\operatorname{AB}
-rings.
Now, let
R
be an
\operatorname{AB}
ring. In Section 2 we prove the following:

(a)

\operatorname{cd}(M,N)<\infty\Rightarrow\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\leq 2\dim R
.

(b)

For
M
be locally free over the punctured spectrum, we have

\operatorname{cd}(M,N)<\infty\Rightarrow\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\leq\dim R.

(c)

For
M
be maximal Cohen-Macaulay and locally free over the punctured spectrum, we have

\operatorname{cd}(M,M)<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\emph{Misfree},

when
\dim R>1
. In particular,
\operatorname{cd}(M,M)=\dim(R)
.

(d)

For
M,N
be maximal Cohen-Macaulay and locally free over the punctured spectrum, we have
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)=\operatorname{cd}(N,M)
.

The above item (c) may be considered as the higher-dimensional version of recent work

[
18
, Corollary 4.12]
with a connection to the famous conjecture of Huneke and Wiegand. From now on
\varphi:R\to R
denotes the
Frobenius endomorphism
. In the sequel, we will see how that the above computations over
\operatorname{AB}
-rings motive us to prove the following result over the much larger class of rings:

(e)

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay, F-finite and
n\gg 0
. Then

\operatorname{cd}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,M)<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{id}_{R}(M)<\infty.

Recall that
\operatorname{id}_{R}(-)
denotes the injective dimension of
(-)
. Now, let
m>0
be any integer and
R
be Cohen-Macaulay. Part (e) implies that
\operatorname{cd}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,{{}^{\varphi_{m}}\!}R)<\infty
iff
R
is regular.
We also present the dual parallel to
(e)
, where we do not assume F-finiteness, see Proposition
2.11
.
Here, we do not care about
n\gg 0
, provided the ring is complete-intersection, and even in the particular case
R
is normal and locally
\operatorname{AB}
.

To prove these results, we reduce the local cohomology problems to the vanishing of certain ext-modules. In Section 3 we continue with the later:

Problem 1.2
.

Let
-\in\{{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,R\}
and
i>0
.
When is
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,-)=0
?

The history of this comes back to
[
15
]
, where Herzog assumed and studied vanishing of all Ext-modules, see
[
15
]
. It seems this is more subtle than the classical dual results
[
24
,
20
]
. So, to the end of introduction, we assume the rings are F-finite.
In particular, we deal with the higher (lower) analogue of the recent work
[
8
, 1.4]
. This naturally leads us to study the reflexivity problem of
{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R
. We determine conditions under which the vanishing of few many

, or even a single value of
i
,

implies the vanishing of all. Here, we summarized some of them:

(i)

Suppose

. Suppose

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R)=0
for

(ii)

Suppose

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R)=0
for some

(iii)

Suppose

is normal Cohen-Macaulay. Then

is self-dual and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,R)=0

(iv)

We study the questions asked in
[

and
[
22

Let us close the introduction, by recalling the relation between

and the usual local cohomology by means of Grothendieck’s local duality.

2.
When is
H_{m}^{+}(M,N)=0
?

In this note
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)

is a commutative noetherian and local ring, and all modules are finitely generated, otherwise socialized.
For two
R
-modules
M
and

N
, the
i^{th}
generalized local cohomology of
M
and
N
is defined by

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N):=\underset{n}{\varinjlim}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M,N)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
R
be an
\operatorname{AB}
ring, let
M
and
N
be finitely generated
R
-modules. If
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)<\infty
then
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\leq 2\dim R
.

Proof.

We may assume the ring is complete. We look at the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow N_{1}:=\operatorname{Syz}_{1}(N)\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0

where
F
is free. This gives the exact sequence

H^{i-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N)\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N_{1})\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,F)\quad(+)

Recall that
\operatorname{AB}
-rings are Gorenstein, so the injective dimension of
F
is finite. From
(+)
we see
\operatorname{cd}(M,N_{1})<\infty
.
Let
d:=\dim(R)
and set
N_{d}:=\operatorname{Syz}_{1}(N)
. By repetition of this, we see
\operatorname{cd}(M,N_{d})<\infty
. Suppose
the desired claim holds with the additional Cohen-Macaulay assumption of
N
. Since

N_{d}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay we obtain that

\operatorname{cd}(M,N_{d})\leq 2d
. Let
j>2d
, and look at

0=H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,F)\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N_{d-1})\longrightarrow H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N_{d}),

we obtain that

H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N_{d-1})=0
. By repetition of this, we see
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\leq 2d
. So, without loss of generality, we may and do assume that
N
is Cohen-Macaulay. This allows us to apply
[
16
, Lemma 3.4]

and to deduce for any
i\geq 0
that

H^{d+i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N)^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R,N)^{\vee})\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N^{\ast}).

Here
(-)^{\vee}
is the Matlis functor. So,
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N^{\ast})=0
for all
i\gg 0
. But, tor-index of
\operatorname{AB}
-rings is finite, and in fact is equal to dimension of
R
(see discussion after than
[
17
, Proposition 3.2]
). Let

\Sigma:=\{(L_{1},L_{2}):\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(L_{1},L_{2})=0\emph{forall}i\gg 0\}.

By definition, tor-index is

\sup\{n:\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{n}(L_{1},L_{2})\neq 0\emph{and}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{>n}(L_{1},L_{2})=0\}_{(L_{1},L_{2})\in\Sigma}.

Let
j:=\max\{d,\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\}
. There is nothing to prove
if
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)<d
, so we may assume that
i:=\operatorname{cd}(M,N)-d\geq 0
. In sum,
(M,N^{\ast})\in\Sigma
and that

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N^{\ast})\neq 0\emph{and}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{>i}(M,N^{\ast})=0.

By definition,

\operatorname{cd}(M,N)-\dim R=i\leq\operatorname{Tor-index}(R)=\dim R,

and so
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\leq 2\dim R
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
R
be an
\operatorname{AB}
ring, let
M
and
N
be finitely generated
R
-modules so that
M
is locally free over the punctured spectrum. If
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)<\infty
then
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\leq\dim R
.

Proof.

We may assume the ring is complete. Without loss of generality, we may and do assume that
N
is Cohen-Macaulay. Suppose
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\geq d:=\dim(R)
, and let
i:=\operatorname{cd}(M,N)-d\geq 0
. We show that
i=0
. Indeed,
recall that

H^{d+i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,N)^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N^{\ast})\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d+i}(M,N)^{\vee}\quad(+),

where the last isomorphism is taken from
[
19
, Lemma 3.5(2)]
. But, ext-index of
\operatorname{AB}
-rings is equal to
\dim R
(see
[
17
, Proposition 3.2]
). Let

\Sigma:=\{(L_{1},L_{2}):\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(L_{1},L_{2})=0\emph{forall}i\gg 0\}.

By definition, ext-index is

\sup\{n:\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{n}(L_{1},L_{2})\neq 0\emph{and}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{>n}(L_{1},L_{2})=0\}_{(L_{1},L_{2})\in\Sigma}.

In sum,
(M,N^{\ast})\in\Sigma
and that

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d+i}(M,N^{\ast})\neq 0\emph{and}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{>d+i}(M,N^{\ast})=0.

By definition,

d\leq d+i\leq\operatorname{Ext-index}(R)=\dim R,

and so
i=0
and consequently
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)\leq\dim R
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
R
be an
\operatorname{AB}
ring, let
M
and
N
be maximal Cohen-Macaulay and locally free over the punctured spectrum. Then
\operatorname{cd}(M,N)<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{cd}(N,M)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
d:=\dim(R)
. In view of
(+)
from Proposition
2.2
it is enough to note that

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{>d}(M,N)=0\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{>d}(N,M)=0.

This is subject of
[
17
, 4.3]
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a normal and locally
\operatorname{AB}
-ring of prime characteristic. If
R
is
F
-finite, then
\operatorname{cd}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R)<\infty
iff
R
is regular.

Recall that
R
is locally
\operatorname{AB}
means
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
\operatorname{AB}
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. This is an open problem
[
2
]
that
\operatorname{AB}
rings are locally
\operatorname{AB}
.

Proof.

First, suppose that

\operatorname{cd}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R)<\infty
. The proof is proceeds via induction on
d:=\dim R
. Recall that normality means
(S_{2})+(R_{1})
, so the desired claim is clear for

. We now want to verify the inductive hypothesis, and drive an additional property. Since

is maximal Cohen-Macaulay,
H^{d+i}_{\mathfrak{m}}({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R,{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R)^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R),({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R)^{\ast})
, and the later module behaves nicely with respect to localization, namely,

and so
\operatorname{Tor}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}_{i}(({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}}),({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\ast})=0
. Again, thanks to
H^{\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})+i}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}}),({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}}))^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}_{i}(({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}}),({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\ast})
, we deduced that
\operatorname{cd}(({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}}),({{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R_{\mathfrak{p}}))<\infty
. It is easy to see that

is
F
-finite, and from the assumption it is locally
\operatorname{AB}
. In view of the inductive hypothesis,

is regular over punctured spectrum.
By a result of Kunz
[
20
]

{{}^{\varphi_{n}}\!}R
is locally free over the punctured spectrum, also it is maximal Cohen-Macaulay. The claim now follows from Corollary
2.5
.

The reverse part is an immediate application of Kunz theorem along with Grothendieck’s vanishing theorem.

Weak colourings of Kirkman triple systems
Andrea C. Burgess, Nicholas J. Cavenagh, Peter Danziger, David A. Pike

Abstract
A δ \delta -colouring of the point set of a block design is said to be weak if no block is monochromatic. The chromatic number χ ⁡ ( S ) \chi(S) of a block design S S is the smallest integer δ \delta such that S S has a weak δ \delta -colouring. It has previously been shown that any Steiner triple system has chromatic number at least 3 3 and that for each v ≡ 1 v\equiv 1 or 3 ( mod 6 ) 3\pmod{6} there exists a Steiner triple system on v v points that has chromatic number 3 3 . Moreover, for each integer δ ⩾ 3 \delta\geqslant 3 there exist infinitely many Steiner triple systems with chromatic number δ \delta . We consider colourings of the subclass of Steiner triple systems which are resolvable. A Kirkman triple system consists of a resolvable Steiner triple system together with a partition of its blocks into parallel classes. We show that for each v ≡ 3 ( mod 6 ) v\equiv 3\pmod{6} there exists a Kirkman triple system on v v points with chromatic number 3 3 . We also show that for each integer δ ⩾ 3 \delta\geqslant 3 , there exist infinitely many Kirkman triple systems with chromatic number δ \delta . We close with several open problems.



Keywords:
Triple systems, Kirkman triple systems, Colourings of designs, Weak colouring

The main aim of this section is to prove Theorem

.
We begin with some definitions.
Recall that a

\mathcal{G}
of the point set

of blocks of size

A

of

A frame thus can be thought of as a partially resolvable group divisible design, where each partial parallel class misses exactly one group.
A

Remark 3.1

By a standard straightforward counting argument, in a Kirkman frame, for each

there are

which exclude

Theorem 3.2

(
[

]
)

There is a Kirkman frame of type

if and only if

,

is even and

Kirkman frames have also been classified when all but one group has uniform size, and the group sizes are either small or divisible by

Theorem 3.3

(
[

,

]
)

Let

if and only if the following all hold:

A

Kirkman frames are a key tool for constructing Kirkman triple systems, particularly Kirkman triple systems with subsystems. The standard methods are summarized in

Suppose there exists a Kirkman frame on

6
Kirkman triple systems from quadruple systems

We first review a standard construction for Kirkman triple systems that makes use of quadruple systems.
Given a quadruple system
Q
of order
v

on vertex set
\{q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{v-1}\}
,
we build a Kirkman triple system
K(Q)
of order
2v+1
as follows.

The vertex set
V
of
K(Q)
is given by:

\{\infty\}\cup\{q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}\mid i\in\{0,1,\ldots,v-1\}\}.

For each block
\{w,x,y,z\}
from

Q
, place
a KTS
(9)
on
the vertex set

\{\infty\}\cup\{w,x,y,z,w^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\}

in such a way that
the triples including
\infty
are

\{\infty,w,w^{\prime}\}
,

\{\infty,x,x^{\prime}\}
,

\{\infty,y,y^{\prime}\}
and

\{\infty,z,z^{\prime}\}
.
Observe that such a KTS
(9)
placement is not unique, so in turn
K(Q)
may not be unique.

It is well known that

K(Q)
is a Kirkman triple system; see
Theorem 5.1.3 from
[
23
]
.
Building on this construction, we obtain some results which may be useful in future work to construct Kirkman triple systems with fixed chromatic number.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
Q
is a quadruple system of order
v
and if there is a
K(Q)
which is

\delta
-colourable,
then
Q
has a

\delta
-colouring.

Proof.

Let
A=\{q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{v-1}\}
and
B=\{q_{0}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},\dots,q_{v-1}^{\prime}\}
.
From the definition of
K(Q)
, we may assume that the vertices of
K(Q)
are labelled with

\{\infty\}\cup A\cup B

in such a way that for each

\{w,x,y,z\}\subset A

that is a block of
Q
,
there is a KTS
(9)
on the vertex set:

\{\infty,w,x,y,z,w^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\}.

Moreover,
\{\infty,q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}\}
is a triple for each
0\leqslant i\leqslant v-1
.

Fix a

\delta
-colouring of the points of
K(Q)
.
Observe that in this colouring, when restricted to the point set
A
of
Q
, the block
\{w,x,y,z\}
is not monochromatic.
We therefore conclude that the

\delta
-colouring yields
a
\delta
-colouring of
Q
such that no block is monochromatic.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

If
Q
is a quadruple system, then
for any
K(Q)
,

\chi(K(Q))\geqslant\chi(Q)
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\delta\geqslant 2
.
If

Q
is
a

\delta
-colourable quadruple system
of order
v
, then there
is a

K(Q)
which is a

2\delta
-colourable Kirkman triple system of order
2v+1
.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a

\delta
-colourable quadruple system of order
v
.
Let
A=\{q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{v-1}\}
and

B=\{q_{0}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},\dots,q_{v-1}^{\prime}\}
.
Fix a

\delta
-colouring of
Q
on the vertex set

\{1,2,\dots,\delta\}

Consider a Kirkman triple system

based on

as described above.
By the definition of
K(Q)
,

\{\infty,q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}\}
is a triple for each

, and
for each
\{w,x,y,z\}\subset A

that is a block of
Q
,

contains the blocks of a KTS
(9)
on the vertex set

\{\infty,w,x,y,z,w^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\}.

Moreover, for each block
\{w,x,y,z\}

,
y
and

to be points that do not all have the same colour,
and include
the following triples in

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\{x,y,z\},&\{w,y,z^{\prime}\},&\{x^{\prime},w,z\},&\{x,w,y^{\prime}\},\\
\{x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\},&\{w^{\prime},y^{\prime},z\},&\{x,w^{\prime},z^{\prime}\},&\{x^{\prime},w^{\prime},y\}.\\
\end{array}

We now colour the points of

\{1,2,\dots,2\delta\}

Whenever vertex

is coloured with

be coloured with

Finally, colour
\infty
with colour

Hence, for each block
\{w,x,y,z\}
in

are mapped to at least two different colours,

\{x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\}
are also mapped to at least two different colours.

Observe that for each of the triples

\{\infty,w,w^{\prime}\},\{\infty,x,x^{\prime}\},\{\infty,y,y^{\prime}\},\{\infty,z,z^{\prime}\},\{x,y,z\},\{x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\}

Each of the remaining six triples of the KTS
(9)
arising from
\{w,x,y,z\}

-colourable.
∎

2
-colourable quadruple system of order
3n+1
if and only if

n\equiv 0
or
1\pmod{4}
.

Corollary 6.7
.

For each
n\equiv 0
or
1\pmod{4}
,
there exists a Kirkman triple system

K(6n+3)
, say
S
, such that
\chi(S)\leqslant 4
.

7
Conclusion

Theorem
1.3
posits, for each integer
\delta\geqslant 3
, the existence of infinitely many
\delta
-chromatic Kirkman triple systems. In the particular case that
\delta=3
, Theorem
1.2
shows that there is a
3
-chromatic Kirkman triple system for every order
v\equiv 3\pmod{6}
,
thereby completely determining the spectrum for
3
-chromatic Kirkman triple systems.

For
\delta=4
, Theorem
4.5
gives a
4
-chromatic Kirkman triple system whenever
v\equiv 33
or
129\pmod{384}
. It is known that a
4
-chromatic Steiner triple system exists for every admissible order
v\geqslant 21

[
8
,
17
]
; whether the same is true for Kirkman triple systems remains open. We tested the 66,937 KTS(21) with nontrivial automorphism group for colouring properties, and found that they are all
3
-chromatic; thus, a
4
-chromatic KTS(21) would necessarily be free of nontrivial automorphisms.

Question 7.1
.

Does there exist a
4
-chromatic
{\rm KTS}(21)
?

Given the complete existence result for
4
-chromatic KTS(
v
) for orders in several congruence classes, it seems likely that they will exist more generally. We thus make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 7.2
.

There is a
4
-chromatic Kirkman triple system of every order
v\equiv 3\pmod{6}
with
v\geqslant v_{0}
for some integer
v_{0}\geqslant 21
.

The value of
v_{0}
in Conjecture
7.2
is open for debate, and clearly the actual value (if it exists) is dependent on the answer to Question
7.1
. The smallest order for which we have actually found a
4
-chromatic Kirkman triple system is
v=33
, and so we conjecture that
v_{0}\leqslant 33
.

Considering higher chromatic numbers, de Brandes, Phelps and Rödl
[
3
]
showed that for every integer
\delta\geqslant 3
, there exists a value
v_{0}
for which there is a
\delta
-chromatic Steiner triple system of every admissible order
v\geqslant v_{0}
. We conjecture that an analogous result holds for Kirkman triple systems.

Conjecture 7.3
.

For every integer
\delta\geqslant 3
, there exists an integer
v_{0}
such that whenever
v\geqslant v_{0}
and
v\equiv 3\pmod{6}
, there is a
\delta
-chromatic Kirkman triple system of order
v
.

In Section
6
, we explored colouring properties of Kirkman triple systems formed from quadruple systems. Specifically, Theorems
6.1
and
6.3
together imply that if
Q
is a
\delta
-chromatic quadruple system, then
there exists a
K(Q)
such that

\delta\leqslant\chi(K(Q))\leqslant 2\delta
. Applying this result with
2
-chromatic quadruple systems, we obtain Kirkman triple systems of orders congruent to
3
and
9\pmod{24}
whose chromatic number is either 3 or 4 (Corollary
6.7
). While Theorem
6.5
gives conditions on the quadruple system
Q
which would imply that
\chi(K(Q))\leqslant\delta+1

for some
K(Q)

, we note that these conditions cannot be satisfied for a
2
-colouring of
Q
. Nevertheless, Example
6.4
shows that a
2
-chromatic quadruple system may give rise to a
3
-chromatic Kirkman triple system.
We therefore ask the following question.

Question 7.4
.

Does there exist a quadruple system
Q
and a
K(Q)
where

Q
is
2
-chromatic and

K(Q)
is
4
-chromatic?

More generally, are there conditions on a
2
-chromatic quadruple system
Q
that characterize whether
K(Q)
is
3
- or
4
-chromatic?

For a
\delta
-chromatic quadruple system with
\delta\geqslant 3
, we ask if the lower bound from Corollary
6.2
on the chromatic number of
K(Q)
is tight; a positive answer would yield an additional method to construct Kirkman triple systems with specified chromatic number.

Question 7.5
.

Let
\delta\geqslant 3
be an integer. Does there exist a
\delta
-chromatic quadruple system
Q

and a
K(Q)

such that
\chi(K(Q))=\delta
?

We also ask whether the upper bound from Theorem
6.3
is tight, particularly if
\delta\geqslant 3
.

Question 7.6
.

Does there exist a
\delta
-chromatic quadruple system
Q

and a
K(Q)

such that
\chi(K(Q))=2\delta
?

Finally, we note that the question of colouring Kirkman triple systems can be generalized in several ways. It would be interesting to study colourings of resolvable BIBD
(v,k,\lambda)
for arbitrary values of
k
and/or
\lambda
. Another natural extension would be to consider colourings of resolvable
\ell
-cycle decompositions of the complete graph (or the complete graph with a 1-factor
I
removed, for even orders). More generally, the
Oberwolfach Problem
OP
(F)
asks, given a 2-regular graph
F
of order
v
, whether there exists a 2-factorization of
K_{v}
or
K_{v}-I
in which each 2-factor is isomorphic to
F
(see
[
7
]
for further details); requiring a solution to the Oberwolfach Problem or its variants (such as the Hamilton-Waterloo Problem) which satisfies vertex-colouring properties provides an open area of study.

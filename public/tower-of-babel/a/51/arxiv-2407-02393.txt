Approximate symmetries, insulators, and superconductivity in continuum-model description of twisted WSe 2
Maine Christos, Pietro M. Bonetti, Mathias S. Scheurer

Abstract
Motivated by the recent discovery of superconductivity in twisted bilayer WSe 2 , we analyze the correlated physics in this system in the framework of a continuum model for the moiré superlattice. Using the symmetries in a fine-tuned limit of the system, we identify the strong-coupling ground states and their fate when the perturbations caused by finite bandwidth, displacement field, and the phase of the intralayer potential are taken into account. We classify the superconducting instabilities and, employing a spin-fermion-like model, study the superconducting instabilities in proximity to these insulating particle-hole orders. This reveals that only a neighboring intervalley coherent phase (with zero or finite wave vector) is naturally consistent with the observed superconducting state, which we show to be crucially affected by the non-trivial band topology. Depending on details, the superconductor will be nodal or a chiral gapped state while further including electron-phonon coupling leads to a fully gapped, time-reversal symmetric pairing state.

Dispersion as perturbation.—
We next analyze what happens when the SU(2) symmetry is broken due to finite

in the dispersion, i.e., when

As in both experiments

Conclusion.—
In summary, we have studied the possible insulating phases as a result of Coulomb interaction in a continuum-model description of twisted WSe
2
, showing that IVC or VP is favored depending on whether the
D
-induced SU(2)-breaking perturbations in the bandwidth or in the form factors dominate. Using the valley-fermion model description of the pairing glue in Eq. (
7
), we find that only IVC fluctuations are consistent with superconductivity, necessarily leading to a superconducting order parameter with nodes or broken time-reversal symmetry. As samples with superconductivity show insulating (superconducting) behavior at larger
D
(smaller
D
), this IVC-based pairing glue is also consistent with the strong-coupling analysis.
The superconductor becomes fully gapped when additional interactions from phonons are significant.
There are many natural directions for future works, such as including more bands and generalizing this continuum-model study to heterobilayers
Scherer et al. 2022
;
Slagle and Fu 2020
;
Crépel et al. 2023
or more exotic novel forms of moiré superlattices
Wang et al. 2024
;
Putzer et al. 2024
.

Appendix A
Numerical solution of gap equation

The most general form of interactions in the space of the active bands we study for pairing is:

H_{\text{att.}}=-\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}d^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\eta}\lambda_{j}^{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})d_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},\eta^{\prime}}d^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime}}\lambda_{j}^{\eta^{\prime\prime}\eta^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}},-\boldsymbol{q})d_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q},\eta^{\prime\prime\prime}}

(8)

where
A
is the sample area,
\boldsymbol{k}
and
\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}
are summed over the first Brillouin zone and
\boldsymbol{q}
is summed over all momenta. For intervalley fluctuations, i.e., fluctuations of
\phi_{x,y}
, we only keep
j=x,y
in Eq. (
8
); after using time-reversal symmetry to fix the gauge of the wavefunctions, we find the following LGE:

\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right)^{+-}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\chi_{-\boldsymbol{q}}\right)\frac{1-2n_{F}(E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})}{2E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\right)^{-+}|\lambda_{x,y}^{+-}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},-\boldsymbol{q})|^{2}

(9)

Here
E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}
is the dispersion in the
+
spin-valley flavor with the chemical potential fixed for every value of displacement field
D
such that hole-doping
\nu=1
.
\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is defined as:

\left(\Delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right)^{\eta-\eta}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\chi_{-\boldsymbol{q}})\lambda_{j}^{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})\langle d_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{q},-\eta^{\prime}}d_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},\eta^{\prime}}\rangle\lambda_{j}^{-\eta-\eta^{\prime}}(-\boldsymbol{k},-\boldsymbol{q})

(10)

In the case where there is more than one type of fluctuation contributing to pairing, the
\lambda_{j}
may be summed over
j=x,y,z,0
in the above. We also multiply the IVC interactions by an additional factor of
\frac{1}{2}
relative to when we consider fluctuations of an order with only a single component, since at the level of the gap equation,
\lambda_{x}
and
\lambda_{y}
provide an identical contribution. After using Fermi-Dirac statistics, Eq. (
9
) can be rewritten as:

\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k}}\right)^{-+}=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\chi_{-\boldsymbol{q}}\right)\frac{1-2n_{F}(E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})}{2E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\right)^{-+}|\lambda_{x,y}^{+-}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},-\boldsymbol{q})|^{2}

(11)

For VP fluctuations (
\phi_{z}
) and, thus,
j=z
in Eq. (
8
), the LGE we solve is:

\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right)^{-+}=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\chi_{-\boldsymbol{q}}\right)\frac{1-2n_{F}(E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})}{2E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\right)^{-+}|\lambda_{z}^{++}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},-\boldsymbol{q})|^{2}

(12)

The linearized gap equation for
\phi_{0}
, or equivalently the electron-phonon interactions we study, is the same as for VP fluctuations but with the opposite sign:

\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right)^{-+}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\chi_{-\boldsymbol{q}}\right)\frac{1-2n_{F}(E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})}{2E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\right)^{-+}|\lambda_{0}^{++}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},-\boldsymbol{q})|^{2}

(13)

Unless otherwise specified, all solutions are calculated at
T=1
K. We sum
\boldsymbol{q}
out to 2 shells outside the first Brillouin zone, an approximation justified by the falling off of both the form factors and
\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}
.

For the case where we study the relative strength of different types of fluctuations or phonons (
\phi_{0}
), for example, IVC fluctuations (
\phi_{x,y}
) and phonons [in Fig.
3
(a-d) of the main text], we solve a gap equation of the following form:

\begin{split}\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right)^{-+}=-(1-x)\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\chi_{-\boldsymbol{q}}\right)&\frac{1-2n_{F}(E^{+}_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{q}})}{2E^{+}_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{q}}}\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{q}}\right)^{-+}|\lambda_{x,y}^{+-}(-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{q})|^{2}\\
&+x\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\chi_{-\boldsymbol{q}}\right)\frac{1-2n_{F}(E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})}{2E^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\left(\Delta^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\right)^{-+}|\lambda_{0}^{++}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},-\boldsymbol{q})|^{2}\end{split}

(14)

where
x
is a parameter which is used to tune the relative strengths of each interactions.

Appendix C
Topological constraints on form factors and impact on pairing

In this appendix, we discuss how the non-zero Chern number of the active bands necessarily leads to a non-trivial momentum dependence of the form factors. As IVC fluctuations provide the most promising pairing glue in our theory, we illustrate this using the intervalley form factors
\lambda^{+-}_{x,y}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})
entering the associated LGE in Eq. (
11
). Using time-reversal symmetry to relate the wave functions in the two valleys, the form factors can be written as
\lambda^{+-}_{x}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})=\braket{u^{*}_{+}(-\boldsymbol{k})|u_{+}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q})}
, and similarly for the
y
component, where
\ket{u_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{k})}
are the Bloch states in valley
\eta
. Viewing
\lambda^{+-}_{x}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})
as a function
s_{\boldsymbol{k}_{0}}(\boldsymbol{q})=\lambda^{+-}_{x}(\boldsymbol{k}_{0},\boldsymbol{q})
of the momentum transfer
\boldsymbol{q}
at fixed
\boldsymbol{k}=\boldsymbol{k}_{0}
, we conclude that
s_{\boldsymbol{k}_{0}}(\boldsymbol{q})
has to vanish at least for one
\boldsymbol{q}
in the Brillouin zone. Since, otherwise, we could use
\ket{u^{*}_{+}(-\boldsymbol{k}_{0})}
as a (momentum
\boldsymbol{q}
independent) “trial Bloch state” that, upon projection to the band
\{u_{+}(\boldsymbol{k}_{0}+\boldsymbol{q})\}_{\boldsymbol{q}}
, would define a smooth and periodic gauge for that band. This would be at odds with the non-zero Chern number
PhysRevB.74.235111
.

Three-fold rotational symmetry further implies that
s_{\boldsymbol{k}_{0}}(\boldsymbol{q})=s_{C_{3z}\boldsymbol{k}_{0}}(C_{3z}\boldsymbol{q})
. Therefore, for high-symmetry choices of
\boldsymbol{k}_{0}
with
C_{3z}\boldsymbol{k}_{0}=\boldsymbol{k}_{0}
, these zeros are either pinned at high-symmetry
\boldsymbol{q}
, which are themselves
C_{3z}
invariant, or appear in multiples of three. These expectations are confirmed by Fig.
10
, where the intervalley form factors for the active band at the Fermi surface are shown as a function of
\boldsymbol{q}
at fixed
\boldsymbol{k}
. For the high-symmetry choices
\boldsymbol{k}=\Gamma
and
K
, the zeros are indeed pinned at high-symmetry points, too. While generic
\boldsymbol{k}
leads to zeros at generic positions.

Based on the LGE in Eq. (
11
), it is natural that these topology-related momentum dependencies in the form factors influence the superconducting energetics. From Fig.
2
(b), we can see that making the peak of
\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}
more concentrated around
\boldsymbol{q}=0
by decreasing
\alpha
favors the
E
state. As such, it seems natural that the zero at
\boldsymbol{q}=0
in Fig.
10
(a) is an important driving force for the
A_{1}
state’s dominance in Fig.
2
. To test this hypothesis, we have removed the momentum-dependence of the form factors,
\lambda_{x,y}\rightarrow 1
, in the LGE and, indeed, this favors the
E
pairing instability, see Fig.
11
.

Multiple Magnetorotons and Spectral Sum Rules in Fractional Quantum Hall Systems
Dung Xuan Nguyen, F. D. M. Haldane, E. H. Rezayi, Dam Thanh Son, Kun Yang

Abstract
We study numerically the the charge neutral excitations (magnetorotons) in fractional quantum Hall systems, concentrating on the two Jain states near quarter filling, ν = 2 / 7 \nu=2/7 and ν = 2 / 9 \nu=2/9 , and the ν = 1 / 4 \nu=1/4 Fermi-liquid state itself. In contrast to the ν = 1 / 3 \nu=1/3 states and the Jain states near half filling, on each of the two Jain states ν = 2 / 7 \nu=2/7 and ν = 2 / 9 \nu=2/9 the graviton spectral densities show two, instead of one, magnetoroton peaks. The magnetorotons have spin 2 and have opposite chiralities in the ν = 2 / 7 \nu=2/7 state and the same chirality in the ν = 2 / 9 \nu=2/9 state. We also provide a numerical verification of a sum rule relating the guiding center spin s ¯ \bar{s} with the spectral densities of the stress tensor.



Introduction.
—The exploration of topological phases of
matter started with the discovery of the fractional quantum Hall (FQH)
effect
Tsui et al. 1982
;
Laughlin 1983
;
Wen 1990
. Under a strong magnetic
field, electrons in two dimensions form strongly correlated quantum
Hall systems. In the lowest Landau level (LLL) limit, the kinetic
energy of the electrons is constant, and the two-dimensional electron
system is driven to numerous exotic topological phases depending on
the filling fraction and the effective interactions. The topological
characteristics of the FQH ground state and its charged excitations
can be understood using the wavefunction approach, pioneered by
Laughlin
Laughlin 1983
and further developed by many other
authors
Arovas et al. 1984
;
Read and Green 2000
;
Nayak and Wilczek 1996
;
Haldane and Rezayi 1988
;
Greiter et al. 1991
. The idea of the composite fermion
(CF) by Jain
Jain 1989
;
Jain 2007
provides an explanation of numerous
gapped FQH states observed in experiment
Pan et al. 2003
;
Pan et al. 2002
and an
intuitive construction of model wavefunctions, and suggests the
abelian and non-abelian braiding statistic of quasi-particles and
quasi-holes
Hansson et al. 2017
;
Nayak et al. 2008
. Inspired by Jain’s intuitive picture, a
field theory description of FQH states was developed (the
Halperin-Lee-Read, or HLR, theory), based on the idea of flux
attachment
Lopez and Fradkin 1991
, which predicts a gapless Fermi-liquid
state
Halperin et al. 1993
at half filling, which has been confirmed
experimentally
Kang et al. 1993
.

Recently, modifications to the HLR theory has been suggested to make
it consistent with the symmetries of a single Landau level.
Particle-hole symmetry, which has been long an issue
Kivelson et al. 1997
;
Lee 1999
,
is restored in the Dirac CF theory for FQH states near half
filling
Son 2015
. The dipole coupling of the CF to the
electric field takes care of the consistency of the theory with
diffeomorphism
Nguyen et al. 2018a
;
Du et al. 2021
. In combination,
these two modifications lead to response functions consistent with all
known symmetries (see, e.g., Ref.
Hofmann 2021
).

An important feature of gapped quantum Hall states is the existence of
the neutral magnetoroton mode, first suggested by Girvin, MacDonald
and Platzman (GMP)
Girvin et al. 1986
and later observed in
experiment
Pinczuk et al. 1998
;
Kang et al. 2000
;
Kukushkin et al. 2009
. Though GMP originally
introduced the magnetoroton as a charge density wave, recent
works
Haldane 2011a
;
Haldane 2009
;
Golkar et al. 2016a
, employing the
lowest Landau level symmetries, suggest that magnetoroton has spin 2
and thus can be considered as a massive “emergent graviton” in FQH
systems. The magnetoroton has been studied from many different
perspectives: by constructing the
wavefunction
Yang et al. 2012
;
Yang 2013
(which conforms
with the spin-2 structure), as an excitation of the CF crossing

\Lambda
levels
Majumder et al. 2009
, by exact
diagonalization
Scarola et al. 2000
;
Jain and Kamilla 1997
;
Balram et al. 2013
;
Dev and Jain 1992
;
Yang and Haldane 2014
;
Möller and Simon 2005
,
or within the Dirac CF
theory
Golkar et al. 2016a
;
Nguyen et al. 2018a
;
Nguyen et al. 2018b
, where
it is interpreted as the shear deformation of the composite Fermi
surface. In the latter studies, the chirality of the magnetoroton is
determined by the direction of the residual magnetic field seen by the
Dirac CFs, and the positions of the minima of the magnetoroton
dispersion relation match the experimental
results
Pinczuk et al. 1998
;
Kang et al. 2000
;
Kukushkin et al. 2009
rather well.

Very recently, the Dirac CF theory has been generalized to Jain states
near
\nu=\frac{1}{4}

Conclusion.
—In this Letter, we compute numerically the
graviton spectral densities for the
\nu=2/7
,
2/9
, and
1/4

states. For the first two states, we observe in the spectral
densities two magnetoroton peaks, one at low energy with chirality
that depends on the residual magnetic field acting on the CFs (and
thus are opposite for the
\nu=2/7
and
2/9
states), and one at high
energy with the same chirality for the two states. The higher-energy
magnetoroton is also observed in a spectral density of the
\nu=1/4

state, and this magnetoroton has approximately the same energy in all
three filling fractions. The result is consistent with the
two-magnetoroton model of FQH states near
1/4
proposed in
Ref.
Nguyen and Son 2021a
. In addition, we have verified the FQH
graviton spectral sum rules for the two Jain states and the Fermi
liquid state
\nu=1/4
.

We hope that our results will motivate the experimental exploration of
the magnetoroton spectrum of the FQH states near
\nu=1/4
, as well as
more detail study of the Haldane mode which may reveal its nature.

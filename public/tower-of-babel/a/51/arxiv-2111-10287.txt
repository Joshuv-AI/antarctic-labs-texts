A Minkowski-type inequality in the AdS-Melvin space
Daniel Xia, Pei-Ken Hung

Abstract
. The AdS-Melvin spacetime was introduced by Astorino and models the AdS soliton with electromagnetic charge. It is a static spacetime with a time-symmetric Cauchy hypersurface, which we refer to as the AdS-Melvin space. In this paper, we study a sharp Minkowski-type inequality for surfaces embedded in the AdS-Melvin space. We first prove the inequality for special cases in which the surface enjoys axisymmetry or is a small perturbation of a coordinate torus. We then use a weighted normal flow to show that the inequality holds for general surfaces.



1.
Introduction

The classical Minkowski inequality for a closed convex hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
reads

(1.1)

\int_{\Sigma}H\,dA\geq|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}|\Sigma|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

Here
H
stands for the mean curvature of
\Sigma
. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
a round sphere. Applying the inverse mean curvature flow, Guan and Li
[
5
]
showed that the convexity condition for (
1.1
) can be relaxed to mean convexity and star-shapedness. The star-shapedness can be further weakened to an outward minimizing condition using the weak inverse mean curvature flow of Huisken and Ilmanen
[
7
]
. It is still an open problem whether (
1.1
) holds for general mean convex hypersurfaces.

In
[
11
]
, Penrose gave an interpretation of (
1.1
) from the view of general relativity and cosmic censorship. The total mean curvature is viewed as the amount of mass needed to turn
\Sigma
into a black hole and (
1.1
) can be (heuristically) derived from the Penrose inequality. We refer readers to
[
9
]
for a detailed account of the Penrose ineqaulity and the connection to (
1.1
).

Based on the black hole collapse interpretation, (
1.1
) has been generalized to other ambient spaces. In
[
3
]
, Brendle, Wang and the second author extended this inequality to convex, star-shaped hypersurfaces
\Sigma
in the Anti-de-Sitter Schwarzschild space. Let
\Sigma_{H}
be the horizon and
\phi
be the static potential in the Anti-de-Sitter Schwarzschild space. The inequality reads

(1.2)

\int_{\Sigma}\phi H\,dA-n(n-1)\int_{\Omega}\phi\,dV\geq(n-1)|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\left(|\Sigma|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}-|\Sigma_{H}|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\right),

where
\Omega
is the bounded region with boundary
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\cup\Sigma_{H}
. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a coordinate sphere. For a bounded region with outward minimizing boundary

\Sigma
in the Schwarzschild space with mass
m
, Wei
[
12
]
proved that

(1.3)

\frac{1}{(n-1)|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\int_{\Sigma}\phi H\,dA\geq\left(\frac{|\Sigma|}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}-2m,

with rigidity for a coordinate sphere. Recently, McCormick
[
10
]
showed that the inequality (
1.3
) holds for asymptotically flat static spacetimes of dimension
3\leq n\leq 7
. Note that all of these inequalities are for static manifolds with spherical infinity.

In 1999, Horowitz and Myers
[
6
]
discovered an exact solution to the Einstein equations with a negative cosmological constant, which they called an AdS soliton. It is static and asymptotically locally Anti-de Sitter. The static slice, referred to as
the Horowitz-Myers geon

[
13
]
, is asymptotically locally hyperbolic with a toroidal conformal infinity.

Horowitz and Myers postulated that the Horowitz-Myers geon is the ground state for a conjectural non-supersymmetric AdS/CFT correspondence
[
6
]
. More precisely, let
(M,g)
be an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with toroidal conformal infinity and scalar curvature greater than or equal to
-n(n+1)
. Then the total mass is at least equal to the mass of the Horowitz-Myers geon. Moreover, the rigidity holds if and only if
(M,g)
is isometric to the Horowitz-Myers geon. Progress on the Horowitz-Myers conjecture has been very limited. For small perturbations of Horowitz-Myers the conjecture was proven by Constable and Myers
[
4
]
and Woolgar proved the rigidity of this conjecture in 3 dimensions
[
13
]
. Recently, a Minkowski-type inequality was proved on the Horowitz-Myers geon by Alaee and the second author
[
1
]
. In this paper, we consider its generalization to the AdS soliton coupled with a Maxwell field. Such a spacetime is called called
the AdS-Melvin spacetime
and we give a short description in the next paragraph.

In
[
2
]
, a generalization of AdS soliton is introduced and is referred to as the AdS-Melvin spacetime. The AdS-Melvin spacetime is a static solution to the Einstein-Maxwell equations with a negative cosmological constant. The spacetime metric and the potential of the Maxwell field have the explicit form
[
8
]

{}^{(4)}{g}_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-r^{2}dt^{2}+r^{-2}F(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}dx^{2}+r^{2}F(r)dy^{2},

\displaystyle F(r)=1-r^{-3}-br^{-4},\ A_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=-\frac{b^{1/2}}{r}dy.

Here
b>0
is a positive constant. The ranges of the coordinates are

(1.4)

\displaystyle t\in\mathbb{R},\ r\in(r_{s},\infty),\ x\in[0,P_{x}],\ y\in[0,P_{y}].

Here
r_{s}
is the largest positive root of
F(r)
. We identify
x=x+P_{x}
and
y=y+P_{y}
. In particular, the
(x,y)
coordinates parametrize a torus. The period
P_{x}
is an arbitrary positive number and the period
P_{y}
is chosen such that the metric can be extended smoothly to
r=r_{s}
. It is straightforward to check that

P_{y}=\frac{4\pi}{r_{s}^{2}F^{\prime}(r_{s})}.

When we send
b
to zero, the AdS-Melvin spacetime reduces to the AdS soliton introduced by Horowitz and Myers.

In this paper, we mainly consider the time-symmetric Cauchy hypersurface
M=\{t=0\}
in the AdS-Melvin spacetime and we refer to it as the
AdS-Melvin space
. Topologically,
M
is homeomorphic to a solid torus. The induced metric on
M
is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}=r^{-2}F(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}dx^{2}+r^{2}F(r)dy^{2}.

The metric in (
1.5
) is complete and is asymptotically locally hyperbolic. Actually, if we replace
F(r)
in (
1.5
) by
1
,
g
becomes the hyperbolic metric under the horosphere coordinates.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface embedded in
M
which is a graph over the torus. In other words, there is a smooth function
s(x,y)
such that
\Sigma=\{r=s(x,y)\}
. Let
g
be the induced metric on
\Sigma
and
H
be the mean curvature with respect to the outward normal vector. Denote by
\Omega
the region enclosed by
\Sigma
. Define the quantity
Q(\Sigma)
by

(1.6)

Q(\Sigma):=\int_{\Sigma}Hr\,dA-6\int_{\Omega}r\,dV.

We are mainly concerned with the following Minkowski-type inequality:

(1.7)

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma)\geq P_{x}P_{y}(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1}).

In this paper, we prove that (
1.7
) holds under various assumptions on
\Sigma
. Moreover, under those assumptions, we show that equality holds only if
\Sigma
is a coordinate torus given by
\{r\equiv\textup{const}\}
.

To start, we consider a surface
\Sigma
which is symmetric along the
x
or
y
direction. For these types of surfaces, (
1.7
) holds true.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface embedded in
M
which is a graph over the torus. Assume that
\Sigma
is symmetric along the
x
or
y
direction. Then (
1.7
) holds. Moreover, equality is achieved if and only if
\Sigma
is a coordinate torus.

Our next result considers the validity of (
1.7
) when
\Sigma
is a perturbation of a coordinate torus.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
r_{0}>r_{s}
and a smooth function
\phi(x,y)
on the torus. For
\varepsilon
small, consider

(1.8)

\tilde{s}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=r_{0}+\varepsilon\phi(x,y)

and the graph
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}=\{(\tilde{s}_{\varepsilon}(x,y),x,y)\,|\,(x,y)\in T^{2}\}.

Then we have

(1.9)

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}Q(\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon})\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}=0,

and

(1.10)

\frac{d^{2}}{d\varepsilon^{2}}Q(\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon})\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\geq 0.

Moreover, equality holds in
(
1.10
)
if and only if
\phi
is a constant function.

After establishing (
1.7
) for the special cases in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we use a normal flow to investigate more general surfaces. Concretely, given a surface
\Sigma
in
M
, we consider the normal flow
\varphi:[0,T_{0})\times T^{2}\to M
with initial data
\Sigma
that satisfies

(1.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=r^{-1}\nu.

The speed
r^{-1}
is chosen such that the quantity
Q
is monotonically nonincreasing. See Proposition
4.1
for more details. We show that (
1.7
) holds provided we can extend
T_{0}
to infinity.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be an embedded surface in
M
which is a graph over the torus. Assume that the flow (
1.11
) exists for all time. Then (
1.7
) holds. Moreover, equality is achieved if and only if
\Sigma
is a coordinate torus.

Notation

We explain here the notation we use in this paper. We use
x^{a},x^{b}
,
a,b=1,2,3
to denote a coordinate of the AdS-Melvin space
M
. We write
\bar{g}_{ab}
for the AdS-Melvin metric on
M
and write
\bar{\nabla}
for the corresponding Levi-Civita connection. The volume form is denoted as
dV
. We write
F(r)
for the function

(1.12)

F(r)=1-r^{-3}-br^{-4}.

We often omit the arguments in
F(r)
and
\frac{dF}{dr}(r)
and denote them by
F
and
F^{\prime}
respectively. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
F^{a}
for
(F(r))^{\lambda}
.

For an embedded suface
\Sigma
in
M
, we use
x^{i},x^{j}
,
i,j=1,2
to denote a coordinate on
\Sigma.
The induced metric of
\Sigma
is denoted by
g_{ij}
and we write
\nabla
for the Levi-Civita connection. The unit outward normal vector of
\Sigma
is denoted by
\nu
and
H
is the mean curvature with respect to
\nu
. The area form is denoted by
dA
. In the case that
\Sigma
is a graph over the torus as
\Sigma=\{r=s(x,y)\}
, we write
F
,
F^{\lambda}
and
F^{\prime}
as abbreviations of
F(s(x,y))
,
(F(s(x,y)))^{\lambda}
and
\frac{dF}{dr}(s(x,y))
respectively.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we compute the geometric quantities appearing in (
1.7
) in terms of the height function
s(x,y)
. In Section 3, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
using formulas derived in Section 2. In Section 4, we establish the monotonicity of
Q(\Sigma_{t})
along the flow (
1.11
). The limit of
Q
is computed in Section 5, leading to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Geometry of graphs

In this section we record basic geometric quantities of an embedded surface
\Sigma\subset M
that is a graph over the torus. We start with the Christoffel symbols of
(M,\bar{g})
. Recall that
M
is covered by a coordinate system
(r,x,y)\in(r_{s},\infty)\times T^{2}
and the metric
\bar{g}
is given by (
1.5
).

Lemma 2.1
.

The nonzero Christoffel symbols are as follows:

(2.1)

\begin{split}&\bar{\Gamma}_{rr}^{r}=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}F\big(r^{-2}F^{-1}\big)^{\prime},\ \bar{\Gamma}_{xx}^{r}=-r^{3}F,\\
&\bar{\Gamma}_{yy}^{r}=-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}F\big(r^{2}F\big)^{\prime},\ \bar{\Gamma}_{rx}^{x}=\bar{\Gamma}_{xr}^{x}=r^{-1},\\
&\bar{\Gamma}_{ry}^{y}=\bar{\Gamma}_{yr}^{y}=\frac{1}{2}r^{-2}F^{-1}\big(r^{2}F\big)^{\prime}.\end{split}

Next, we consider a surface which is a graph over the torus. Let
s(x,y)
be a smooth function defined on
T^{2}
. Let

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Sigma=\{(s(x,y),x,y)\,|\,(x,y)\in T^{2}\}

with the parameterization
\varphi(x,y)=(s(x,y),x,y).
We compute below the induced metric
g
on
\Sigma
. From

(2.3)

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\ \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y},

we have

(2.4)

\begin{split}g_{xx}&=r^{2}+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2},\ g_{xy}=r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y},\\
g_{yy}&=r^{2}F+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Under these coordinates, we have

(2.5)

\det g=r^{6}F^{2}N^{2},

where
N
is defined by

(2.6)

N^{2}=r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(1+r^{-4}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\right).

The inverse metric is then given by

(2.7)

\begin{split}r^{6}F^{2}N^{2}g^{xx}&=r^{2}F+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2},\ r^{6}F^{2}N^{2}g^{xy}=-r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y},\\
r^{6}F^{2}N^{2}g^{yy}&=r^{2}+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

The area form of
\Sigma
and volume form of
M
are computed to be

(2.8)

dA=r^{3}FN\,dxdy,\ {dV}=r\,drdxdy.

We denote by
\nu
the unit outward normal vector of
\Sigma
. From (
2.3
) we have

(2.9)

\nu=N^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-r^{-4}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-r^{-4}F^{-2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right).

Consider the function
z(x,y)
defined through

(2.10)

\displaystyle z^{2}=1+r^{-4}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}.

In view of (
2.6
), the relation between
z
and
N
is given by

(2.11)

\displaystyle N^{2}=r^{-2}F^{-1}z^{2}.

Recall that
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection on
\Sigma
. In the lemma below, we compute
|\nabla r|^{2}
and
|\nabla y|^{2}
. These formulas will be used in expressing
\Delta r
in terms of the mean curvature
H
.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

(2.12)

|\nabla r|^{2}=r^{2}F(1-z^{-2}),

and

(2.13)

|\nabla y|^{2}=r^{-2}F^{-1}-r^{-6}F^{-3}z^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

In terms of
z
, the inverse metric can be expressed as

(2.14)

\begin{split}r^{4}Fz^{2}g^{xx}&=r^{2}F+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2},\ r^{4}Fz^{2}g^{xy}=-r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y},\\
r^{4}Fz^{2}g^{yy}&=r^{2}+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Through a direct computation,

\displaystyle r^{4}Fz^{2}|\nabla r|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(r^{2}F+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\left(r^{2}+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+2\left(-r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=r^{2}F\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}=r^{6}F^{2}(z^{2}-1).

Here we use (
2.10
) for the last equality. Then (
2.12
) follows by rearranging terms.

We turn to (
2.13
). Through a direct computation,

\displaystyle|\nabla y|^{2}=g^{yy}

\displaystyle=r^{-4}F^{-1}z^{-2}\left(r^{2}+r^{-2}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{-4}F^{-1}z^{-2}\cdot r^{2}\left(z^{2}-r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}-r^{-6}F^{-3}z^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}.

∎

Next, we calculate the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The second fundamental form of
\Sigma
is given by

(2.15)

\begin{split}N\cdot h_{xx}&=-r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}+(3r^{-3}F^{-1}+2^{-1}r^{-2}F^{-2}F^{\prime})\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+r,\\
N\cdot h_{yy}&=-r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{3}{2}r^{-4}F^{-2}(r^{2}F)^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(r^{2}F)^{\prime},\\
N\cdot h_{xy}&=-r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x\partial y}+(r^{-3}F^{-1}+r^{-4}F^{-2}(r^{2}F)^{\prime})\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}.\end{split}

Proof.

Recall that
\bar{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of the Ads-Melvin metric
\bar{g}
. From (
2.3
), we compute

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}

From (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}+2^{-1}r^{2}F(r^{-2}F^{-1})^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}-r^{3}F\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+2r^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}.

Similar calculation yields

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}+2^{-1}r^{2}F(r^{-2}F^{-1})^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}-2^{-1}r^{2}F(r^{2}F)^{\prime}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+r^{-2}F^{-1}(r^{2}F)^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

and

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x\partial y}+2^{-1}r^{2}F(r^{-2}F^{-1})^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+r^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+2^{-1}r^{-2}F^{-1}(r^{2}F)^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

The second fundamental form can now be computed through taking the inner product with the unit normal vector
-\nu
given in (
2.9
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

The mean curvature
H
of
\Sigma
is given by

(2.16)

\begin{split}{r^{6}F^{2}N^{3}}H=&\bigg(-1-r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\bigg)\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}+\bigg(-F^{-1}-r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\bigg)\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}\\
&+2r^{-4}F^{-2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x\partial y}+(4r^{-1}+F^{-1}F^{\prime})\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\\
&+\left(4r^{-1}F^{-1}+\frac{3}{2}F^{-2}F^{\prime}\right)\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}.\end{split}

Proof.

The assertion follows by plugging (
2.7
) and (
2.15
) into

H=g^{xx}h_{xx}+g^{xy}h_{xy}+g^{yx}h_{yx}+g^{yy}h_{yy}.

∎

Lastly, we calculate the relation between the mean curvature
H
and
\Delta r
. To do so, we record the Hessian of
r
below.

Lemma 2.5
.

We have the equalities

(2.17)

\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}r=rF\bar{g}_{ab}+2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}\bar{\nabla}_{a}r\bar{\nabla}_{b}r+2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}\bar{\nabla}_{a}y\bar{\nabla}_{b}y

and

(2.18)

\bar{\Delta}r=3rF+r^{2}F^{\prime}.

Proof.

From (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}_{rr}^{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}F\left(r^{-2}F^{-1}\right)^{\prime}=-r^{-1}-2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime},

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}_{xx}^{r}

\displaystyle=-r^{3}F,

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}_{yy}^{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}F\left(r^{2}F\right)^{\prime}=-r^{3}F^{2}-2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}rdx^{a}dx^{b}=

\displaystyle\left(r^{-1}+2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}\right)dr^{2}+\left(r^{3}F\right)dx^{2}+\left(r^{3}F^{2}+2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}\right)dy^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(r^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{3}Fdx^{2}+r^{3}F^{2}dy^{2}\big)+2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}dr^{2}+2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}dy^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rF\bar{g}_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}+2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}dr^{2}+2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}dy^{2}.

Then (
2.17
) follows. (
2.18
) can then be derived directly from (
2.17
).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

We have the equality

(2.19)

\begin{split}\Delta r=-&rF^{1/2}z^{-1}H+2rF+2^{-1}r^{2}F^{\prime}\\
&+2^{-1}r^{2}F^{\prime}(1-z^{-2})-2^{-1}r^{-2}F^{-2}F^{\prime}z^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

From
\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}r,\nu)=rF^{1/2}z^{-1}
and (
2.17
), we derive

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}r=

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}r-\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}r,\nu)h_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rF{g}_{ij}+2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}{\nabla}_{i}r{\nabla}_{j}r+2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}{\nabla}_{i}y{\nabla}_{j}y-rF^{1/2}z^{-1}h_{ij}.

Taking the trace, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta r=2rF+2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}|\nabla r|^{2}+2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}|\nabla y|^{2}-rF^{1/2}z^{-1}H.

Using (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), the above becomes

\displaystyle\Delta r=

\displaystyle 2rF+2^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}\left(r^{2}F(1-z^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle+2^{-1}r^{4}FF^{\prime}\left(r^{-2}F^{-1}-r^{-6}F^{-3}z^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\right)-rF^{1/2}z^{-1}H.

Then (
2.19
) follows by rearranging terms.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

In this section, we provide the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. Theorem
1.1
is separated into Proposition
3.2
(the
y
-symmetry case) and Proposition
3.5
(
x
-symmetry case). These two propositions are proved in Section
3.1
and Section
3.2
respectively. In Section
3.3
, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that
\Sigma
is defined as the surface
r=s(x,y)
for a smooth function
s
defined on
T^{2}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have the equality

(3.1)

\displaystyle Q\left(\Sigma\right)-P_{x}P_{y}\left(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1}\right)=\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+2^{-1}\right)\,dydx.

Proof.

By (
2.8
), we have

\int_{\Sigma}Hr\,dA=\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}Hs^{4}FN\,dxdy

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}r\,dV

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\int_{r_{s}}^{s(x,y)}r^{2}\,drdydx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\frac{1}{3}\left(s(x,y)^{3}-r_{s}^{3}\right)\,dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(-P_{x}P_{y}r_{s}^{3}+x\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}s^{3}\,dydx\right).

Therefore,
Q\left(\Sigma\right)
is equal to

\int_{\Sigma}Hr\,dV-6\int_{\Omega}r\,dV=2P_{x}P_{y}r_{s}^{3}+\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3})\,dydx.

Subtracting
2(P_{x}P_{y}r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1})
from both sides, we obtain (
3.1
).
∎

3.1.
Surfaces with
y
-symmetry

We first prove Theorem
1.1
assuming that
\Sigma
has
y
-symmetry. That is, the function
s(x,y)
is independent of
y
. We aim to show the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be the graph defined by
r=s(x)
for a smooth periodic function
s
with period
P_{x}
. Then

Q\left(\Sigma\right)\geq P_{x}P_{y}\left(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1}\right).

Equality holds if and only if
s
is a constant function.

Let us define a function that will assist in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\lambda=\lambda(r)
be a solution to the ordinary differential equation

(3.2)

\frac{d\lambda}{dr}=r^{-2}\left(F(r)\right)^{-1/2}.

We abuse notation and write
\lambda(x)=\lambda(s(x))
, so that

(3.3)

\frac{d\lambda}{dx}=r^{-2}F^{-1/2}\frac{ds}{dx}.

Then

-r^{2}F^{1/2}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dx^{2}}=-\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+2^{-1}r^{-1}F^{-1}(4-r^{-3})\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dx^{2}}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1/2}\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{-2}F^{-1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1/2}\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}-2^{-1}r^{-3}F^{-3/2}\left(4F+rF^{\prime}\right)\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1/2}\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}-2^{-1}r^{-3}F^{-3/2}\left(4-r^{-3}\right)\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}.

Multiplying both sides by
r^{2}F^{1/2}
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Define a function
\lambda=\lambda(x)
by (
3.3
). Then

(3.4)

Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}=-r^{2}F^{1/2}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dx^{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

Since
\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}=0
, Lemma
2.4
implies that

H=r^{-6}F^{-2}N^{-3}\left(2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}-\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+r^{-1}F^{-1}\left(4F+rF^{\prime}\right)\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\right).

Note that

4F+rF^{\prime}=4\left(1-r^{-3}-br^{-4}\right)+r\left(3r^{-4}+4br^{-5}\right)=4-r^{-3}

and

s^{2}FN=1+r^{-4}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}=1+r^{-4}F^{-1}\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle Hs^{4}N-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}N^{-2}\left(2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}-\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+r^{-1}F^{-1}\left(4F+rF^{\prime}\right)\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)-2r^{3}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}N^{-2}\left(2r^{3}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+r^{-1}F^{-1}(4-r^{-3})\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)-2r^{3}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}N^{-2}\left(-r^{-4}F^{-1}\left(2r^{3}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}-\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+r^{-1}F^{-1}(4-r^{-3})\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}N^{-2}\left(-\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+2^{-1}r^{-4}F^{-1}(4r^{3}-1)\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{d^{2}s}{dx^{2}}+2^{-1}r^{-4}F^{-1}(4r^{3}-1)\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)\left(1+r^{-4}F^{-1}\left(\frac{ds}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}.

By Lemma
3.3
, the last expression is equal to

-r^{2}F^{1/2}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dx^{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1},

concluding the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

In view of Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to show that

\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+2^{-1}\right)\,dydx\geq 0.

Since
s
is independent of
y
, this is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\left(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,dx\geq 0.

By Lemma
3.4
,

\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\left(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,dx=\int_{0}^{P_{x}}-\left(s^{2}\sqrt{F}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dx^{2}}\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}\,dx,

which may be rewritten as

\int_{0}^{P_{x}}-\left(s^{2}\sqrt{F}\frac{d}{dx}\left(\arctan\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\right)\right)\,dx.

Through integration by parts, this is equal to

\displaystyle\left(-s^{2}\sqrt{F}\arctan\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\right)\bigg|_{x=0}^{P_{x}}+\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\arctan\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\right)\frac{d}{dx}(s^{2}\sqrt{F})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\frac{ds}{dx}\frac{d}{ds}(s^{2}\sqrt{F})\arctan\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\,s^{2}\sqrt{F}\frac{d}{ds}(s^{2}\sqrt{F})\arctan\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the final inequality holds because the integrand is nonnegative:

\arctan\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dx}\right)\frac{d\lambda}{dx},\ s^{2}\sqrt{F},\ \frac{d}{ds}(s^{2}\sqrt{F})\geq 0.

In order for equality to hold, we require
\frac{d\lambda}{dx}=0
identically, so that
\lambda(s(x))
is a constant function. Since
\lambda
is strictly increasing,
s(x)
must be a constant function as well. This completes the proof of our first special case.
∎

3.2.
Surfaces with

-symmetry

Here, we prove Theorem

assuming that

has

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be the graph defined by

for a periodic smooth function

. Then

Q\left(\Sigma\right)\geq P_{x}P_{y}\left(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1}\right).

Equality holds if and only if

We follow a similar strategy as the proof for
y

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\lambda=\lambda(r)
be a solution to the ordinary differential equation

(3.5)

\frac{d\lambda}{dr}=r^{-2}\left(F(r)\right)^{-1}.

We abuse notation and write
\lambda(y)=\lambda(s(y))
, so that

(3.6)

\frac{d\lambda}{dy}=r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{ds}{dy}.

Then

-r^{2}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dy^{2}}=-F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+r^{-1}F^{-2}(2F+rF^{\prime})\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dy^{2}}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}F}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}F}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}-r^{-3}F^{-2}(2F+rF^{\prime})\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}.

Multiplying both sides by
-r^{2}
implies the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Define a function
\lambda=\lambda(y)
by (
3.6
). Then

(3.7)

Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}=-r^{2}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dy^{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

Since
\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}=0
, Lemma
2.4
implies that

H=r^{-6}F^{-2}N^{-3}\left(2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}-F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+\frac{8F+3rF^{\prime}}{2rF^{2}}\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}N^{-2}\left(2r^{3}-\frac{1}{2}-F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+\frac{8F+3rF^{\prime}}{2rF^{2}}\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)-2r^{3}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}N^{-2}\left(-r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(2r^{3}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}-F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+\frac{8F+3rF^{\prime}}{2rF^{2}}\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}F^{-1}N^{-2}\left(-F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+\frac{2F+rF^{\prime}}{rF^{2}}\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-F^{-1}\frac{d^{2}s}{dy^{2}}+\frac{2F+rF^{\prime}}{rF^{2}}\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)\left(1+r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{ds}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}.

By Lemma
3.6
, the final expression is equal to

-r^{2}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dy^{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1},

as desired.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.5
.

By Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to show that

\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+2^{-1}\right)\,dydx\geq 0.

Since
s
is independent of

, this is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,dy\geq 0.

By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(Hs^{4}FN-2s^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P_{y}}-s^{2}\,\frac{d^{2}\lambda}{dy^{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P_{y}}-s^{2}\frac{d}{dy}\arctan\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)dy.

Using integration by parts, this is equal to

\displaystyle\left(-s^{2}\arctan\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)\right)\bigg|_{y=0}^{P_{y}}+\int_{0}^{P_{y}}2s\frac{ds}{dy}\arctan\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P_{y}}2s^{3}F\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\arctan\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)dy

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the final inequality holds since the integrand is nonnegative:

2s^{3}F,\ \frac{d\lambda}{dy}\arctan\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dy}\right)\geq 0.

In order for equality to hold, we require
\frac{d\lambda}{dy}=0
identically, so that
\lambda(s(y))
is a constant function. Since
\lambda
is strictly increasing,
s(y)
must be a constant function as well.
∎

3.3.
Perturbation

We now prove Theorem
1.2
, where the surface
\Sigma
is a small perturbation of a constant-height surface. For reference, we reproduce the statement below.

Proposition 3.8
.

Fix
r_{0}>r_{s}
and a smooth function
\phi(x,y)
on the torus. For
\varepsilon
small, consider

(3.8)

\tilde{s}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=r_{0}+\varepsilon\phi(x,y),

and the graph
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}

(3.9)

\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}=\{(\tilde{s}_{\varepsilon}(x,y),x,y)\,|\,(x,y)\in T^{2}\}.

Then we have

(3.10)

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}Q(\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon})\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}=0,

and

(3.11)

\frac{d^{2}}{d\varepsilon^{2}}Q(\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon})\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\geq 0.

Moreover, equality holds in (
3.11
) if and only if
\phi
is a constant function.

For later convenience, we write

(3.12)

\displaystyle F_{0}=F(r_{0}),\ F^{\prime}_{0}=F^{\prime}(r_{0})

for the values of
F
and
F^{\prime}
on
\tilde{\Sigma}_{0}
.
Let
d\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}
denote the area form of
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
denote the region enclosed by
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}
. We use the tilde in
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}
and analogous expressions to distinguish them from
\Sigma_{t}
in the next section, where we consider the normal flow. For brevity, other quantities of
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}
will not carry the tildes or subscripts. For instance, we still write
H
for the mean curvature of
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

On the surface
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
, the following expansion holds.

(3.13)

\frac{1}{r^{2}FN^{2}}=1-\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{4}F_{0}}-\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{4}F^{2}_{0}}+O(\varepsilon^{3}),

(3.14)

\frac{1}{F}=\frac{1}{F_{0}}-\varepsilon\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Proof.

Since all partial derivatives of
r
with respect to
\varepsilon
are first-order in
\varepsilon
, equation (
3.13
) follows from the definition of
N
as

r^{2}FN^{2}=1+\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{4}F}+\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{4}F^{2}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=F(r_{0}+\varepsilon\phi)=F_{0}+\varepsilon\phi F^{\prime}_{0}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Taking the reciprocal, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F_{0}}-\varepsilon\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

On the surface
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}
, we have the following expansion in
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle Hr^{4}FN

\displaystyle-2r^{3}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{1}{F_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{4F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{2r_{0}F_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{r_{0}F_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Proof.

Note that

\frac{4F+rF^{\prime}}{2rF}=\frac{4F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{2r_{0}F_{0}}+O(\varepsilon),\ \frac{2F+rF^{\prime}}{rF^{2}}=\frac{2F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{r_{0}F_{0}^{2}}+O(\varepsilon).

Using these equations and Lemma
3.9
, we compute

\displaystyle Hr^{4}FN=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{r^{2}FN^{2}}\left(2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}-\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{1}{F}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{4F+rF^{\prime}}{rF}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{8F+3rF^{\prime}}{2rF^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}-\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{1}{F}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{4F+rF^{\prime}}{rF}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{8F+3rF^{\prime}}{2rF^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}\right)\left(\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{4}F}+\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{4}F^{2}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2r^{3}F+\frac{1}{2}r^{4}F^{\prime}-\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{1}{F}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{4F+rF^{\prime}}{2rF}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F+rF^{\prime}}{rF^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2r^{3}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{1}{F}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{4F+rF^{\prime}}{2rF}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F+rF^{\prime}}{rF^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Combining this with (
3.8
), it follows that

\displaystyle Hr^{4}FN-2r^{3}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{1}{F}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{4F+rF^{\prime}}{2rF}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F+rF^{\prime}}{rF^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{F_{0}}-\varepsilon\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{2})\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{4F_{0}+rF^{\prime}_{0}}{2rF_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{2F_{0}+rF^{\prime}_{0}}{rF_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{1}{F_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{4F_{0}+rF^{\prime}_{0}}{2rF_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F_{0}+rF^{\prime}_{0}}{rF_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{1}{F_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{4F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{2r_{0}F_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{r_{0}F_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{3}),

as desired.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.8
.

By Lemma
3.10
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}}Hr\,dV_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}}-6\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}r\,dV_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{0}^{P_{x}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{1}{F_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)\,\ dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{x}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}\,dxdy-\frac{1}{F_{0}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{x}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\,dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=0}^{P_{x}}\right)dy-\frac{1}{F_{0}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{x}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\bigg|_{x=0}^{P_{y}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 0.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}}Hr\,dV_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}}-6\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}r\,dV_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2\int\limits_{0}^{P_{x}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(\frac{4F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{2r_{0}F_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{r_{0}F_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\right)dydx.

We compute that

\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\frac{\phi F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{2}}\,dy=\frac{F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}\left(\phi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\bigg|_{y=0}^{P_{y}}-\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)\,dy\right)=-\frac{F^{\prime}_{0}}{F_{0}^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\,dy.

Therefore, the second derivative is

2\int\limits_{0}^{P_{x}}\int\limits_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(\frac{4F_{0}+r_{0}F^{\prime}_{0}}{2r_{0}F_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{2F_{0}}{r_{0}F_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\right)dydx,

which is nonnegative since the integrand is nonnegative. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}=0
, i.e.
\phi
is a constant function. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

In this section, we deform a surface

\rho
be a function defined on
M
. Let
\varphi:[0,T_{0})\times T^{2}\to M
be a family of embedded graphs satisfying

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\rho\nu.

Here
\nu
is the outward unit normal. Later we will specialize to the speed
\rho=r^{-1}
and consider

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=r^{-1}\nu.

Nevertheless, we will keep
\rho
unspecified in computing evolution equations in order to make its role apparent. We write
\Sigma_{t}
for the image of
\varphi(t,\cdot)
and write
dA_{t}
for the area form of
\Sigma_{t}
. Recall that the quantity
Q(\Sigma_{t})
is defined in (
1.6
). The goal of this section is to prove the following monotonicity formula.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), it holds that

(4.3)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})\leq 0.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma_{t}
is a coordinate torus.

In Section
4.1
, we record several ingredients that are needed to prove Proposition
4.1
. These ingredients include the evolution equations, the Gauss equation and a static inequality for the AdS-Melvin space. In Section
4.2
we combine the formulas in Section
4.1
to finish the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

4.1.
Basic formulas

We start with the evolution equations along the flow (
4.1
). The evolution equations for the area form and the mean curvature are given by

(4.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,dA_{t}=\rho H\,dA_{t}

and

(4.5)

\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}=-\Delta\rho-\rho\,\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)-\rho|A|^{2}.

Here
\overline{\textup{Ric}}
stands for the Ricci curvature of the ambient space. The derivations of the above equations are provided in Appendix
B
for the reader’s convenience.

Next, we record the Gauss equation. Let
R
and
\bar{R}
be the scalar curvature of
\Sigma
and
M
respectively. Then we have

(4.6)

R-H^{2}+|A|^{2}=\bar{R}-2\overline{\text{Ric}}(\nu,\nu).

Recall that
\nabla
and
\bar{\nabla}
are the Levi-Civita connection on
\Sigma_{t}
and
M
respectively. Given a smooth function
\psi
on
M
, we have

(4.7)

\Delta\psi=\bar{\Delta}\psi-\bar{\nabla}^{2}\phi(\nu,\nu)-H\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}\psi,\nu).

Lastly, we need a static inequality for
r
.

Lemma 4.2
.

In the AdS-Melvin space with metric
\bar{g}
given by (
1.5
), the coordinate function
r
satisfies

(4.8)

\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}r-\bar{\Delta}r\cdot\bar{g}_{ab}-r\cdot\bar{R}_{ab}\geq-\frac{2b}{r^{3}}\bar{g}_{ab}.

Proof.

Combining (
2.17
), (
2.18
) and (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}r-\bar{\Delta}r\cdot\bar{g}_{ab}-r\cdot\bar{R}_{ab}=\left(2F^{\prime}+2^{-1}rF^{\prime\prime}\right)(F^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{a}r\bar{\nabla}_{b}r+r^{4}F\,\bar{\nabla}_{a}y\bar{\nabla}_{b}y).

From (
1.12
),
2F^{\prime}+2^{-1}rF^{\prime\prime}=-2br^{-5}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}r-\bar{\Delta}r\cdot\bar{g}_{ab}-r\cdot\bar{R}_{ab}=-\frac{2b}{r^{3}}(r^{-2}F^{-1}\,\bar{\nabla}_{a}r\bar{\nabla}_{b}r+r^{2}F\,\bar{\nabla}_{a}y\bar{\nabla}_{b}y).

From (
1.5
), this implies

\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}r-\bar{\Delta}r\cdot\bar{g}_{ab}-r\cdot\bar{R}_{ab}\geq-\frac{2b}{r^{3}}\bar{g}_{ab}.

∎

4.2.
Monotonicity

We prove in this section the monotonicity inequality (
4.3
). Combining the formulas in Section
4.1
, we calculate
\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})
with a general speed as the following.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the flow (
4.1
), it holds that

(4.9)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\rho(-2\Delta r-rR)\,dA_{t}.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}Hr\,dA_{t}-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}r\,dV\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}r+H\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}\,dA_{t}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}Hr\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}dA_{t}-6\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\rho r\,dV.

From (
4.5
), (
4.1
) and (
4.4
), the above equals

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left((-\Delta\rho-\rho|A|^{2}-\rho\overline{\text{Ric}}(\nu,\nu))r+H\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}r,\nu)\rho+Hr\cdot\rho H-6\rho r\,\right)dA_{t}.

Applying the Gauss equation (
4.6
) and integration by parts, this is equal to

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\rho\left(-\Delta r+r\overline{\text{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)+H\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}r,\nu)-6r+rR-r\bar{R}\right)\,dA_{t}.

Combining (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have

H\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}r,\nu)+\Delta r+r\overline{\text{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\leq\frac{2b}{r^{3}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\rho\left(-2\Delta r-rR-r\bar{R}-6r+\frac{2b}{r^{3}}\right)dA_{t}.

From (
A.2
),
-r\bar{R}-6r+\frac{2b}{r^{3}}=0
. Then (
4.9
) follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

In view of (
4.9
), it suffices to show that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{2\Delta r}{r}+R\,dA_{t}\geq 0.

Observe that

\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla r}{r}\right)=\frac{\Delta r}{r}-\frac{|\nabla r|^{2}}{r^{2}}.

By the divergence theorem, we have

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{\Delta r}{r}-\frac{|\nabla r|^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)dA_{t}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla r}{r}\right)dA_{t}=0.

Notice that
\Sigma_{t}
is topologically a torus. The Gauss-Bonnet theorem implies that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}R\,dA_{t}=0.

We then conclude that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{2\Delta r}{r}+R\,dA_{t}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{2|\nabla r|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dA_{t}\geq 0.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
r
is a constant on
\Sigma_{t}
.
∎

Proposition 5.1

\lim_{t\to\infty}Q(\Sigma_{t})=P_{x}P_{y}(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1}).

Combining Proposition

.

) with initial data

to infinity. Proposition

implies that

for all

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma)\geq\lim_{t\to\infty}Q(\Sigma_{t})=P_{x}P_{y}(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1}).

∎

The rest of the section is devoted to proving Proposition
5.1

\varphi:[0,\infty)\times T^{2}\to M
is a family of embeddings which solves (
4.2
). We write
\Sigma_{t}
for the image of
\varphi(t,\cdot)
and denote by

the height function of

. That is, under the coordinates

In Sections

.

5.1.
C^{0}
estimates

We start by calculating
\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), we have the equality

(5.1)

\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}=sFN.

Here
F=F(s)
and
N
is defined in (
2.6
).

Proof.

Notice that the normal projection of
\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
equals the velocity
r^{-1}\nu
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\nu\right)=\bar{g}\left(r^{-1}\nu,\nu\right)=r^{-1}.

From (
2.9
) and (
1.5
), it follows that

\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}=r^{-1}\bar{g}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\nu\right)^{-1}=rFN.

∎

Next, we show that
s
is comparable to
t
along the flow.

Lemma 5.3
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,

(5.2)

\max_{(x,y)\in T^{2}}|s(t,x,y)-t|\leq C_{0}.

Proof.

Let
s_{\text{max}}(t)
and
s_{\text{min}}(t)
denote the maximum and minimum of
s(t,x,y)
on the torus respectively. It suffices to show that
s_{\text{max}}(t)-t
and
t-s_{\text{min}}(t)
are bounded from above. We give a proof for the latter; the proof for the former is analogous.

At a minimum point of
s
, we have
N=s^{-1}F^{-1/2}
. Lemma
5.2
and (
1.12
) imply that

\frac{ds_{\min}}{dt}\geq F^{-1/2}(s_{\min}).

Let
C^{-2}>0
be the supremum of
F(r)
for
r\in[r_{s},\infty)
. The above implies that

s_{\min}(t)\geq Ct+s_{\min}(0).

Notice that, from (
1.12
),
F^{-1/2}(r)\geq 1-r^{3}
for
r
large enough. Therefore, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for
t\geq t_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle 1-\frac{ds_{\min}(t)}{dt}\leq s_{\min}^{-3}(t)\leq\left(Ct+s_{\min}(0)\right)^{-3}.

Integrating the above inequality, for all
t\geq t_{0}
we obtain,

\displaystyle(t-s_{\min}(t))-(t_{0}-s_{\min}(t_{0}))\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t}\left(C\tau+s_{\min}(0)\right)^{-3}d\tau\leq(2Cs_{\min}(0)^{2})^{-1}.

As a result, by setting

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{0}=\max_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}(t-s_{\min}(t))+(2Cs_{\min}(0)^{2})^{-1},

we have
t-s_{\min}(t)\leq C_{0}^{\prime}
for all
t\geq 0
.

∎

5.2.

estimates

Recall that

is defined in (
2.10
) which measures the gradient of the height function. In this section, we prove that

decays at a rate

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for all

(5.3)

\max_{(x,y)\in T^{2}}|z^{2}(t,x,y)-1|\leq C_{1}(t+1)^{-4}.

Remark 5.5
.

From (
2.10
) and Lemma

, Lemma

implies that there exists a constant

such that for all

and

\left|\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right|,\left|\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}.

We begin by deriving the evolution equation of

.

Lemma 5.6
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), we have

(5.5)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial z^{2}}{\partial t}=&-4r^{-1}zF^{1/2}(z^{2}-1)-r^{-4}zF^{-5/2}F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\\
&+2r^{-4}F^{-1/2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}+2r^{-4}F^{-3/2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}.\end{split}

Proof.

From (
2.10
), we compute

\displaystyle\frac{\partial z^{2}}{\partial t}=\

\displaystyle\frac{\partial r^{-4}}{\partial t}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+r^{-4}\frac{\partial F^{-1}}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+2r^{-4}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x\partial t}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial r^{-4}}{\partial t}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+r^{-4}\frac{\partial F^{-2}}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+2r^{-4}F^{-2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y\partial t}.

From (
2.10
), we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial r^{-4}}{\partial t}F^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{\partial r^{-4}}{\partial t}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}=-\frac{4}{r}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}(z^{2}-1)

and

(5.7)

\displaystyle r^{-4}\frac{\partial F^{-1}}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+r^{-4}\frac{\partial F^{-2}}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}=-F^{-1}F^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}\left(z^{2}-1+r^{-4}F^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}\right).

Using (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we derive

\displaystyle\frac{\partial z^{2}}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{4}{r}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}-F^{-1}F^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}\right)(z^{2}-1)-r^{-4}F^{-3}F^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+2r^{-4}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x\partial t}+2r^{-4}F^{-2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y\partial t}.

Next, we compute the second order derivatives of
s
. Note that we can rewrite (
5.1
) in terms of
z
as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}=F^{1/2}z.

Taking a derivative along the
x
-direction, we have

\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}F^{-1/2}F^{\prime}z\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}+F^{1/2}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}.

Similarly,

\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}F^{-1/2}F^{\prime}z\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}+F^{1/2}\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}.

Therefore,

(5.8)

\begin{split}&2r^{-4}F^{-1}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial x\partial t}+2r^{-4}F^{-2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial y\partial t}\\
=\ &zF^{-1/2}F^{\prime}(z^{2}-1)+2r^{-4}F^{-1/2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}+2r^{-4}F^{-3/2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}.\end{split}

In conclusion,

(5.9)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial z^{2}}{\partial t}=&\left(-\frac{4}{r}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}-F^{-1}F^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}+zF^{-1/2}F^{\prime}\right)(z^{2}-1)\\
&-r^{-4}F^{-3}F^{\prime}\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+2r^{-4}F^{-1/2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}+2r^{-4}F^{-3/2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}.\end{split}

Then (
5.5
) follows by replacing
\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}
by
zF^{1/2}
.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
z_{\max}(t)
be the maximum of

at time

over the torus. Then

satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(z_{\max}^{2}(t)-1)\leq-4r^{-1}z_{\max}(t)F^{1/2}(z_{\max}^{2}(t)-1).

Proof.

Note that at the maximum point of

,

. Together with

, (

) follows from (

).
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

By Lemma
5.3
there exists a constant

satisfying

|s(t,x,y)-t|<C_{0}.

We compute

\lim_{t\to\infty}t\left(F^{1/2}(s(t,x,y))-1\right)=0>-C_{0}=\lim_{t\to\infty}t\left(\frac{t+C_{0}}{t+2C_{0}}-1\right).

∎

5.3.

The goal of this section is to show that the positive part of

Under the flow (

Lemma 5.9

Under the flow (

Using (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\left(-r^{-1}F^{1/2}z^{-1}-2r^{-1}\right)T-r^{-1}|T_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle+2r^{-1}F+2^{-1}F^{\prime}-2r^{-1}-2r^{-1}F^{1/2}z^{-1}-r^{-1}\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle+2^{-1}F^{\prime}(1-z^{-2})-2^{-1}r^{-4}F^{-2}F^{\prime}z^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}-2r^{-1}F(1-z^{-2}).

Then (

) follows by rearranging terms.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Under the flow (
4.2

\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}-\left(-r^{-1}F^{1/2}z^{-1}-2r^{-1}\right)T+r^{-1}|T_{ij}|^{2}\leq C_{3}(1+t)^{-4}.

Proof.

By Lemma

Lemma

We are now ready to prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma
5.8
.

By Lemma
5.10
, we have

\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}\leq\left(-r^{-1}F^{1/2}z^{-1}-2r^{-1}\right)T-r^{-1}|T_{ij}|^{2}+C_{3}(1+t)^{-4}.

By (

\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}\leq-\left(3(1+t)^{-1}+O((1+t)^{-2})\right)T+C_{3}(1+t)^{-4}.

Define

. The above implies there exists a constant

such that

\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}\leq C(1+t)^{-2}U+C_{4}(1+t)^{-1}.

We may rewrite this as

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Ue^{C/(1+t)}\right)\leq C_{3}(1+t)^{-1}e^{C/(1+t)}\leq C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-1}.

Here
C^{\prime}=C_{3}e^{C}
. Let
U_{\max}(t)
be the maximum of
U
at time
t
. By integrating the above inequality, we have

U_{\max}(t)e^{C/(1+t)}\leq U_{\max}(0)e^{C}+C^{\prime}\log(1+t).

This implies

\displaystyle H-2\leq e^{-C/(1+t)}\left(C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-3}\log(1+t)+e^{C}(1+t)^{-3}U_{\max}(0)\right).

Then (
5.11
) follows by setting
C_{2}=C^{\prime}+e^{C}U_{\max}(0)
.
∎

5.4.
Proof of Proposition
5.1

In this section we prove Proposition
5.1
. Recall that
\varphi:[0,\infty)\times T^{2}\to M
is a family of embeddings satisfying (
4.2
) and that
\Sigma_{t}
is the image of
\varphi(t,\cdot)
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), there exists a constant
C_{4}>0
such that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\Delta r|\,{dA_{t}}\leq C_{4}\big(\log(1+t)+1\big).

Proof.

Let
(\Delta r)_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(|\Delta r|+\Delta r)
and
(\Delta r)_{-}=\frac{1}{2}(|\Delta r|-\Delta r)
be the positive and negative parts of
\Delta r
respectively. By the divergence theorem,

0=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Delta r\,{dA_{t}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left((\Delta r)_{+}-(\Delta r)_{-}\right){dA_{t}}.

It thus suffices to show that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\Delta r)_{-}\,{dA_{t}}\leq C\big(\log(1+t)+1\big).

From (
2.19
), (
5.15
), (
5.16
) and Lemma
5.8
, we have

(\Delta r)_{-}\leq C(1+t)^{-2}\big(\log(1+t)+1\big).

Similarly, from (
2.8
) and (
5.15
), we have

\displaystyle{dA_{t}}\leq C(1+t)^{2}dydx.

Combining the two yields the result.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}F^{-1/2}z\Delta r\,{dA_{t}}\right)=0.

Proof.

By the divergence theorem,

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}F^{-1/2}z\Delta r\,{dA_{t}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(F^{-1/2}z-1\right)\Delta r\,{dA_{t}}.

Using (
5.15
), we have

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left|\left(F^{-1/2}z-1\right)\Delta r\right|{dA_{t}}\leq C(1+t)^{-3}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left|\Delta r\right|{dA_{t}}.

The result then follows from Lemma
5.11
.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

Under the flow (
4.2
), we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(Hr-2r-2^{-1}r^{-2}\right){dA_{t}}\right)=0.

Proof.

Let us rewrite (
2.19
) in the form

\begin{split}r(H-2)=-&F^{-1/2}z\Delta r+2r(F^{-1/2}z-1)+2^{-1}r^{2}F^{-1/2}F^{\prime}z\\
&+2^{-1}r^{2}F^{-1/2}F^{\prime}z(1-z^{-2})-2^{-1}r^{-2}F^{-5/2}F^{\prime}z^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

By Lemma
5.4
and Remark
5.5
, we have

\begin{split}2^{-1}r^{2}F^{-1/2}F^{\prime}z(1-z^{-2})&=O((1+t)^{-6}),\\
2^{-1}r^{-2}F^{-5/2}F^{\prime}z^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)^{2}&=O((1+t)^{-6}).\end{split}

Therefore

r(H-2)=-F^{1/2}z\Delta r+2r(F^{-1/2}z-1)+2^{-1}r^{2}F^{-1/2}F^{\prime}z+O((1+t)^{-6}).

Note that

\displaystyle 2r(F^{1/2}z-1)

\displaystyle=-r^{-2}+o((1+t)^{-2}),

\displaystyle 2^{-1}r^{2}F^{-1/2}F^{\prime}z

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}r^{-2}+o((1+t)^{-2}).

Thus

Hr-2r-2^{-1}r^{-2}=-F^{-1/2}z\Delta r+o((1+t)^{-2}).

Upon integration, the desired limit follows by using Lemmas
5.3
and
5.12
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
5.1
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Recall that

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma):=\int_{\Sigma}Hr\,dA-6\int_{\Omega}r\,dV.

By Lemma

, it suffices to show that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(2r+2^{-1}r^{-2}\right)\,{dA_{t}}-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}r\,{dV}\right)=P_{x}P_{y}(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1}).

Using (

), we may rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(2r+2^{-1}r^{-2}\right)\,{dA_{t}}-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}r\,{dV}-P_{x}P_{y}(2r_{s}^{3}-2^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P_{x}}\int_{0}^{P_{y}}\left(2r^{3}F^{1/2}z-2r^{3}+2^{-1}F^{1/2}z+2^{-1}\right)dydx.

From (

Upon adding the two and integrating, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Appendix A
Curvature of the AdS-Melvin space

In this section, we compute the Riemannian curvature tensor of AdS-Melvin space whose metric is given by (
1.5
).

Lemma A.1
.

The Ricci curvature of
\bar{g}
is given by

(A.1)

\begin{split}\overline{R}_{ab}=&\left(-2r^{-2}-\frac{5}{2}r^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}F^{-1}F^{\prime\prime}\right)\bar{\nabla}_{a}r\bar{\nabla}_{b}r+(-2r^{2}F-r^{3}F^{\prime})\bar{\nabla}_{a}x\bar{\nabla}_{b}x\\
&+\left(-2r^{2}F-\frac{5}{2}r^{3}FF^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}r^{4}FF^{\prime\prime}\right)\bar{\nabla}_{a}y\bar{\nabla}_{b}y.\end{split}

Also, the scalar curvature is given by

(A.2)

\displaystyle\bar{R}

\displaystyle=-6+\frac{2b}{r^{4}}.

Proof.

We begin by computing the Riemannian curvature tensor of
\bar{g}
through the Gauss-Codazzi equations. Consider the surface
S_{1}:=\{y=\textup{constant}\}
. Because
S_{1}
is perpendicular to the Killing vector
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
, it is totally geodesic and has vanishing second fundamental form. By the Gauss equation, the sectional curvature of the plane generated by
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
equals the Gauss curvature of
S_{1}
, which is
-F-2^{-1}rF^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\overline{R}_{rxrx}=-1-2^{-1}rF^{-1}F^{\prime}.
Moreover, by the Codazzi equation, we have
\overline{R}_{yxrx}=\overline{R}_{yrxr}=0.

Applying the same argument to the surface
S_{2}=\{x=\textup{constant}\}
, we have
\overline{R}_{ryry}=-F-2rF^{\prime}-2^{-1}r^{2}F^{\prime\prime}
and
\overline{R}_{xyry}=0
.

Consider the surface
S_{3}=\{r=\textup{constant}\}
. From Lemma
2.1
, we have that the second fundamental forms of
S_{3}
is given by
h_{xx}=r^{2}F^{1/2}
,
h_{yy}=r^{2}F^{3/2}+2^{-1}r^{3}F^{1/2}F^{\prime}
and
h_{xy}=0
. Notice that the induced metric of
S_{3}
is flat. From the Gauss equation, we have
\overline{R}_{yxyx}=-r^{4}F^{2}-2^{-1}r^{5}FF^{\prime}
. In short, we have

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{rxrx}=-1-2^{-1}rF^{-1}F^{\prime},\ \overline{R}_{ryry}=-F-2rF^{\prime}-2^{-1}r^{2}F^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{yxyx}=-r^{4}F^{2}-2^{-1}r^{5}FF^{\prime},\ \overline{R}_{yxrx}=\overline{R}_{yrxr}=\overline{R}_{xyry}=0.

Together with (
1.5
), we derive

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{rr}=

\displaystyle-2r^{-2}-\frac{5}{2}r^{-1}F^{-1}F^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}F^{-1}F^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{xx}=

\displaystyle-2r^{2}F-r^{3}F^{\prime},

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{yy}=

\displaystyle-2r^{2}F^{2}-\frac{5}{2}r^{3}F^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}r^{4}FF^{\prime\prime},

and
\overline{R}_{rx}=\overline{R}_{ry}=\overline{R}_{xy}=0
. This proves (
A.1
). Moreover, we have
R=-6F-6rF^{\prime}-r^{2}F^{\prime\prime}.
Then (
A.2
) follows by plugging
F=1-r^{-3}-br^{-4}
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any unit vector
\nu
we have

(A.3)

|\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)+2|\leq Cr^{-3}.

Proof.

From Lemma
A.1
and (
1.12
), the eigenvalues of the Ricci curvature are given by

-2+\frac{1}{2r^{3}}+\frac{2b}{r^{4}},-2+\frac{1}{2r^{3}}+\frac{2b}{r^{4}},\ \textup{and}\ -2-\frac{1}{r^{3}}-\frac{2b}{r^{4}}.

Then the assertion follows directly.
∎

Appendix B
Derivation of (
4.4
) and (
4.5
)

In this section, we derive (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) under the flow (
4.1
), which we conveniently recall here as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\rho\nu.

Here
\varphi:[0,T_{0})\times T^{2}\to M
is a family of embeddings and
\rho
is a smooth function on
M
. We write
\Sigma_{t}
for the image of
\varphi(t,\cdot)
. We use
g_{ij}
and
h_{ij}
to denote the induced metric and the second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{t}
respectively. First, we have the evolution equation of the metric

(B.1)

\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}=2\rho h_{ij},\ \frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial t}=-2\rho h^{ij}.

They can be derived as the following.

Derivation of (
B.1
).

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)+\bar{g}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right).

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}(\rho\nu),\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)=\rho h_{ij}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)=\rho h_{ij}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}=2\rho h_{ij}.

Also, since

0=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(g_{ik}g^{kj})=g_{ik}\frac{\partial g^{kj}}{\partial t}+g^{kj}\frac{\partial g_{ik}}{\partial t},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}

\displaystyle=-g^{ik}g^{j\ell}\frac{\partial g_{k\ell}}{\partial t}=-2\rho g^{ik}g^{j\ell}h_{ij}=-2\rho h^{ij}.

∎

Now we are ready to prove (
4.4
).

Derivation of (
4.4
).

We recall the derivative of a determinant. Let
B(t)=(b_{ij}(t)),1\leq i,j\leq n
and
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
be a smooth family of invertible matrices. Denote by
B^{-1}(t)=(b^{ij}(t))
the inverse matrix of
B(t)
. Then it holds that

(B.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\det B=\det B\cdot b^{ji}\frac{db_{ij}}{dt}.

In local coordinates
\{x^{1},x^{2}\}
, we have

\displaystyle dA_{t}=\sqrt{\det g}\,dx^{1}dx^{2}.

From (
B.2
) and (
B.1
), we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sqrt{\det g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\det g}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\det g)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\det g}}\cdot\det g\cdot g^{ji}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\det g}\cdot g^{ji}\cdot 2\rho h_{ij}=\rho H\sqrt{\det g}.

This implies (
4.4
).
∎

Next, we calculate the evolution equation for the unit normal vector
\nu
. Recall that
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}
. The time derivative of
\nu
is given by

(B.3)

\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t}=-\nabla\rho.

The calculation for (
B.3
) can be found below.

Derivation of (
B.3
).

We compute

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\bar{g}\left(\nu,\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial t\partial x^{i}}\right)=-\bar{g}\left(\nu,\frac{\partial(\rho\nu)}{\partial x^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\bar{g}\left(\nu,\rho\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial x^{i}}+\nu\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial x^{i}}\right)=-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial x^{i}}.

Also,

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial t}{\partial t}\bar{g}(\nu,\nu)=0.

Therefore
\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t}=-\nabla\rho
.
∎

Using (
B.3
), we derive the evolution equation of the second fundamental form:

(B.4)

\frac{\partial h_{ij}}{\partial t}=-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho+\rho\bar{R}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\nu,\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)+\rho h_{i}^{k}h_{kj}.

Derivation of (
B.4
).

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{ij}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)+\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\nu,\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial x^{j}\partial t}\right).

The first term is

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)+\bar{g}\left(\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}-\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\right)\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}(-{\nabla}\rho),\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)+\bar{R}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=-{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{j}\rho+\bar{R}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=-{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{j}\rho+\rho\bar{R}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\nu,\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)

and the second term is

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}}\nu,\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial x^{j}\partial t}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}\left(h_{i}^{k}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{k}},\frac{\partial(\rho\nu)}{\partial x^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}\left(h_{i}^{k}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{k}},\rho h_{j}^{\ell}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho h_{i}^{k}h_{j}^{\ell}g_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=\rho h_{i}^{k}h_{kj}.

The result follows.
∎

Finally, we can prove (
4.5
) by combining (
B.1
) and (
B.4
).

Derivation of (
4.5
).

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(g^{ij}h_{ij})=\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial t}h_{ij}+\frac{\partial h_{ij}}{\partial t}g^{ij}

\displaystyle=-2\rho h^{ij}h_{ij}+\left(-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho+\rho\bar{R}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}},\nu,\nu,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{j}}\right)+\rho h_{i}^{k}h_{kj}\right)g^{ij}

\displaystyle=-2\rho|A|^{2}-\Delta\rho-\rho\overline{\text{Ric}}\left(\nu,\nu\right)+\rho|A|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\Delta\rho-\rho\overline{\text{Ric}}\left(\nu,\nu\right)-\rho|A|^{2}.

∎

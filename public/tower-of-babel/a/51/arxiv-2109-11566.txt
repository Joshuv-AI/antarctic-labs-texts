Progress towards analytically optimal angles in quantum approximate optimisation
D. Rabinovich, R. Sengupta, E. Campos, V. Akshay, J. Biamonte

Abstract
The Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm is a p p layer, time variable split operator method executed on a quantum processor and driven to convergence by classical outer loop optimisation. The classical co-processor varies individual application times of a problem/driver propagator sequence to prepare a state which approximately minimizes the problem’s generator. Analytical solutions to choose optimal application times (called angles) have proven difficult to find, whereas outer loop optimisation is resource intensive. Here we prove that optimal Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm parameters for p = 1 p=1 layer reduce to one free variable and in the thermodynamic limit, we recover optimal angles. We moreover demonstrate that conditions for vanishing gradients of the overlap function share a similar form which leads to a linear relation between circuit parameters, independent on the number of qubits. Finally, we present a list of numerical effects, observed for particular system size and circuit depth, which are yet to be explained analytically.

2
Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm

We consider an
n
-qubit complex vector space
V_{n}=[\mathbb{C}^{2}]^{\otimes n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
with fixed standard computational basis
B_{n}=\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}^{\otimes n}
. For an arbitrary target state
\ket{t}\in B_{n}
(equivalently
\ket{t},t\in\{0,1\}^{\times n}
) we define propagators

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\beta)\equiv e^{-i\beta H_{x}},\penalty\ \mathcal{V}(\gamma)\equiv e^{-i\gamma P},

(2)

where
P=\outerproduct{t}{t}
and
{H}_{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}
is the one-body mixer Hamiltonian with
X_{j}
the Pauli matrix acting non-trivially on the
j
-th qubit.

A
p
-depth (
p
layer) QAOA circuit prepares a quantum state
\ket{\psi}
as:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_p(\bm\gamma,\bm\beta)}=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{p}[{\mathcal{U}}(\beta_{k}){\mathcal{V}}(\gamma_{k})]\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}},

(3)

where
\gamma_{k}\in[0,2\pi)
,
\beta_{k}\in[0,\pi)
.
The optimisation task is to determine QAOA optimal parameters for which the state prepared in (
3
) achieves maximum absolute value of the overlap
g_{p}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})=\innerproduct{t}{\psi_p(\bm\gamma, \bm\beta)}
with the target
\ket{t}
. In other words, we search for

\displaystyle(\bm{\gamma}_{opt},\bm{\beta}_{opt})\in\arg\max_{\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta}}\absolutevalue{g_p(\bm\gamma,\bm\beta)}

(4)

Note that the problem is equivalent to the minimization of the ground state energy of Hamiltonian
P^{\perp}=\mathbb{1}-\outerproduct{t}{t}
,

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta}}\bra{\psi_p ( \bm\gamma, \bm\beta)}P^{\perp}\ket{\psi_p (\bm\gamma, \bm\beta)}=1-\max_{\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta}}\absolutevalue{g_p(\bm\gamma, \bm\beta)}^{2}.

(5)

Remark 1

(Inversion symmetry)
.

Under the affine transformation

\displaystyle(\gamma,\beta)\to(2\pi-\gamma,\pi-\beta)

(6)

the absolute value of the overlap remains invariant as
g_{p}\to(-1)^{n}g_{p}^{*}
. Therefore, this narrows the search space to
\gamma_{k}\in[0,\pi)
,
\beta_{k}\in[0,\pi)
, whereas maximums inside the restricted region determine maximums in the composite space using Eq. (
6
).

Proposition 2

(Overlap invariance)
.

The overlap function
g_{p}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})
is invariant with respect to
\ket{t}\in B_{n}
.

Proof.

Each
\ket{t}=\ket{t_1t_2\dots t_n}\in B_{n}
determines a unitary operator
U=U^{\dagger}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{t_{j}}.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle g_{p}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})=

\displaystyle\bra{\bm 0}U^{\dagger}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{p}e^{-i\beta_{k}H_{x}}e^{-i\gamma_{k}U(\outerproduct{\bm 0}{\bm 0})U^{\dagger}}\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{\bm 0}U^{\dagger}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{p}e^{-i\beta_{k}H_{x}}[Ue^{-i\gamma_{k}(\outerproduct{\bm 0}{\bm 0})}U^{\dagger}]\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{\bm 0}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{p}e^{-i\beta_{k}H_{x}}e^{-i\gamma_{k}\outerproduct{\bm 0}{\bm 0}}\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}}.

(7)

The first equality follows from
U\ket{\bm 0}=\ket{t}
where
\ket{\bm 0}=\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
.
The second equality follows from the definition of the matrix exponential. The third equality follows as
U
commutes with
H_{x}
as does any analytic function of
H_{x},

and
U\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=\ket{+}^{\otimes n}.
Thus, the overlap is seen to be target independent.

Theorem 6
.

Optimal
p=1
QAOA parameters relate as
\gamma=\pi-2\beta
.

Proof.

To maximize the absolute value of the overlap

\displaystyle g\equiv g_{1}(\gamma,\beta)=\bra{\bm 0}e^{-i\beta H_{x}}e^{-i\gamma P}\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}},

(9)

with
P=\outerproduct{\bm 0}{\bm 0}
we use the standard conditions
\dfrac{\partial(gg^{*})}{\partial\gamma}=\dfrac{\partial(gg^{*})}{\partial\beta}=0
. Setting the first derivative to zero we arrive at

\displaystyle\bra{\bm 0}e^{-i\beta H_{x}}e^{-i\gamma P}P\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}}g^{*}=\bra{+}^{\otimes{n}}Pe^{i\gamma P}e^{i\beta H_{x}}\ket{\bm 0}g.

(10)

Using the explicit form of the projector and the fact that
\bra{\bm 0}e^{-i\beta H_{x}}\ket{\bm 0}=\cos^{n}\beta
Eq. (
10
) simplifies into

\displaystyle g=g^{*}e^{-2i\gamma}\Leftrightarrow ge^{i\gamma}=g^{*}e^{-i\gamma},

(11)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\arg g=-\gamma.

(12)

Then the derivative of expression (
9
) with respect to
\beta
is set to zero and we arrive at

\displaystyle\bra{\bm 0}e^{-i\beta H_{x}}H_{x}e^{-i\gamma P}\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}}g^{*}=\bra{+}^{\otimes{n}}e^{i\gamma P}H_{x}e^{i\beta H_{x}}\ket{\bm 0}g.

(13)

Moving

is compensated as follows:

\displaystyle\bra{\bm 0}e^{-i\beta H_{x}}\{e^{-i\gamma P}H_{x}+(e^{-i\gamma}-1)[H_{x},P]\}\ket{+}^{\otimes{n}}g^{*}

\displaystyle=\bra{+}^{\otimes{n}}\{H_{x}e^{i\gamma P}+[P,H_{x}](e^{i\gamma}-1)\}e^{i\beta H_{x}}\ket{\bm 0}g.

(14)

After simplification (see remark
7
) we arrive at

\displaystyle-gA=g^{*}A^{*}e^{-i\gamma},

(15)

where

.
Now

is substituted from Eq. (
11
) to establish

\displaystyle-e^{-i\gamma}A=A^{*}.

(16)

Thus, similar to Eq. (
12
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\arg A=\dfrac{\gamma+\pi}{2}.

(17)

A
is calculated as

\displaystyle A\sqrt{2^{n}}=\bra{\bm 0}(H_{x}-n)e^{i\beta H_{x}}\ket{\bm 0}=-n\cos^{n-1}\beta e^{-i\beta},

(18)

which shows that

Thus, from Eq. (

\displaystyle\pi-\beta=\dfrac{\gamma+\pi}{2},

(19)

which finally establishes
\gamma=\pi-2\beta
.
∎

The optimal

4.3
Saturation in layerwise training at

It was demonstrated

Due to this effect, naive layerwise training performance falls below global training.
Training saturation in layerwise optimisation was reported in

Most surprisingly, the saturation depth

4.4
Removing saturation in layerwise training

Any modification in the layerwise training process that violates the necessary saturation conditions can remove the system from its original saturation points. This idea was exploited in

5
Conclusion

We have proven a relationship between optimal Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm parameters for
p=1
and in the thermodynamic limit, we recover optimal angles. We demonstrated the effect of parameter concentrations for
p=1
QAOA circuits using an operator formalism. Finally, we present a list of numerical effects, observed for particular system size and circuit depth, which are yet to be explained analytically. These unexplained effects include both limitations and advantages to QAOA. While difficult, adding missing theory to these subtle effects would improve our understanding of variational algorithms.

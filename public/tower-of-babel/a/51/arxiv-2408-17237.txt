A nonlinear elasticity model in computer vision
John M. Ball, Christopher L. Horner

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to analyze a nonlinear elasticity model introduced by the authors in [ 11 ] for comparing two images, regarded as bounded open subsets of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} together with associated vector-valued intensity maps. Optimal transformations between the images are sought as minimisers of an integral functional among orientation-preserving homeomorphisms. The existence of minimisers is proved under natural coercivity and polyconvexity conditions, assuming only that the intensity functions are bounded measurable. Variants of the existence theorem are also proved, first under the constraint that finite sets of landmark points in the two images are mapped one to the other, and second when one image is to be compared to an unknown part of another. The question is studied as to whether for images related by an affine mapping the unique minimiser is given by that affine mapping. For a natural class of functional integrands an example is given guaranteeing that this property holds for pairs of images in which the second is a scaling of the first by a constant factor. However for the property to hold for arbitrary pairs of affinely related images it is shown that the integrand has to depend on the gradient of the transformation as a convex function of its determinant alone. This suggests a new model in which the integrand depends also on second derivatives of the transformation, and an example is given for which both existence of minimisers is assured and the above property holds for all pairs of affinely related images.

Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to analyze a nonlinear elasticity model introduced in
[
11
]
for comparing two images
P_{1}=(\Omega_{1},c_{1}),\;P_{2}=(\Omega_{2},c_{2})

2
Nonlinear elasticity model

We represent an image by a pair
P=(\Omega,c)
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, and
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,K):=\{d\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}):d(x)\in K\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\}
,
K
a nonempty bounded closed convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(for example
K=[0,1]^{m}
), is an
intensity map
describing the greyscale intensity (
m=1
), the intensity of colour channels (
m>1
), and possibly other image characteristics. We seek to compare two images

P_{1}=(\Omega_{1},c_{1}),\;P_{2}=(\Omega_{2},c_{2})

by minimising (
1
) among a suitable class of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms
y:\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{2}
. Apart from the requirement that
y(\Omega_{1})=\Omega_{2}
we do not impose any other boundary condition, with the aim of better comparing images with important boundary features.

We identify images related by a proper rigid transformation through an equivalence relation; for two images
P=(\Omega,c)
and
P^{\prime}=(\Omega^{\prime},c^{\prime})
we write
P\sim P^{\prime}
if for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
R\in SO(n)

\Omega^{\prime}=E(\Omega),\;c^{\prime}(E(x))=c(x),\text{ where }E(x)=a+Rx.

Natural conditions on the integrand
\psi
are:

(C1) (
Invariance under proper rigid transformations
)

If
P_{1}\sim P_{1}^{\prime}
,
P_{2}\sim P_{2}^{\prime}
, with corresponding rigid transformations
E_{1}(x)=a_{1}+R_{1}x
,
E_{2}(x)=a_{2}+R_{2}x
,
then

\displaystyle I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)=I_{P_{1}^{\prime},P_{2}^{\prime}}(E_{2}\circ y\circ E_{1}^{-1}).

(6)

It is shown in
[
11
]
that (C1) is equivalent to the condition that
\psi(c_{1},c_{2},\cdot)
is
isotropic
, i.e.

\displaystyle\psi(c_{1},c_{2},QAR)=\psi(c_{1},c_{2},A)

(7)

for all
c_{1},c_{2}\in K,A\in M^{n\times n}_{+}
, and
Q,R\in SO(n)
. (The range
K
of the intensity maps was not made explicit in
[
11
]
, but this does not affect the arguments.)

(C2) (
Identification of equivalent images.
)

I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)=0
iff
P_{1}\sim P_{2}
with corresponding rigid transformation
y
.

It is shown in
[
11
]
that, for sufficiently regular
y
,
(C2) is equivalent to the condition that for
c_{1},c_{2}\in K

\displaystyle\psi(c_{1},c_{2},A)=0\text{ iff }c_{1}=c_{2}\text{ and }A\in SO(n).

(8)

(C3) (
Symmetry with respect to interchanging images.
) (See
[
19
,
34
,
29
,
32
]
.)

I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)=I_{P_{2},P_{1}}(y^{-1}).

This holds for all
P_{1},P_{2}
(see
[
11
]
) if and only if for
c_{1},c_{2}\in K

\displaystyle\psi(c_{1},c_{2},A)=\psi(c_{2},c_{1},A^{-1})\det A.

(9)

A class of integrands
\psi
satisfying (C1)-(C3) is given by

\displaystyle\psi(c_{1},c_{2},A)=\Psi(A)+f(c_{1},c_{2},\det A),

(10)

where

(a)
\Psi:M^{n\times n}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
is continuous and isotropic, with
\Psi(A)=\det A\cdot\Psi(A^{-1})
,
\Psi^{-1}(0)=SO(n)
,

(b)
f:K\times K\times(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is continuous, with
f(c_{1},c_{2},\delta)=\delta f(c_{2},c_{1},\delta^{-1})
,
f(c_{1},c_{2},1)=0
iff
c_{1}=c_{2}
.

Examples of
f
satisfying (b) are given by

\displaystyle f(c_{1},c_{2},\delta)=(1+\delta)g(c_{1},c_{2}),

(11)

where
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,\infty)
is continuous with
g(c_{1},c_{2})=g(c_{2},c_{1})
and
g(c_{1},c_{2})=0
if and only if
c_{1}=c_{2}
, for example
g(c_{1},c_{2})=|c_{1}-c_{2}|^{2}
,
and the mass-preserving form (correcting the corresponding expression in
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle f(c_{1},c_{2},\delta)=(1+\delta^{-1})|c_{1}-c_{2}\delta|^{2},

(12)

which vanishes for
c_{1}=c_{2}\delta
(see
[
18
]
).

Remark 1
.

Both forms of
f
are convex in
\delta>0
, while
f
given by (
12
) is convex in the triple
(\delta,c_{1},c_{2}\delta)
on account of the well-known result that
(\delta,c)\mapsto\frac{|c|^{2}}{\delta}
is convex on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
(we use this observation in Theorem
11
below).

Although the conditions (C1)-(C3) are natural and are used in Section
4
, they do not form part of the hypotheses for the existence of minimisers in Theorem
3
below.

3
Existence of minimisers

3.1
Problem formulation and hypotheses

Throughout we assume that
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are bounded homeomorphic Lipschitz domains.
For
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
define the set of admissible maps
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}={\mathcal{A}}_{p}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{p}=\{y\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,y:\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{2}\text{ an orientation-preserving}

(13)

\displaystyle\hskip-115.63243pt\text{homeomorphism},y^{-1}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{2},\mathbb{R}^{n})\}.

Here, for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Sobolev space of maps
y:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
that are measurable with respect to
n-
dimensional Lebesgue measure
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
and possess a weak derivative
Dy
such that

\|y\|_{1,p}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(|y(x)|^{p}+|Dy(x)|^{p}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

and
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the set of such maps with

\|y\|_{1,\infty}:={\rm ess\,sup}_{x\in\Omega}\left(|y(x)|+|Dy(x)|\right)<\infty.

We say that a map
y\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
orientation-preserving
if
\det Dy(x)>0
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. By a result of Vodop’yanov & Gol’dstein
[
57
]
any
y\in W^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a representative (which we always choose) that is continuous on
\Omega
. Furthermore (see e.g. Fonseca & Gangbo
[
26
]
)

y
maps sets of
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
measure zero to sets of measure zero (the
N
-property) and hence measurable sets to measurable sets, for any
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
measurable subset
E\subset\Omega

the
multiplicity function

N(y,\Omega,z):=\#\{x\in\Omega:y(x)=z\}
is a measurable function of
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for any

L^{\infty}
map
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{s}
the change of variables formula

\displaystyle\int_{E}\varphi(y(x))\det Dy(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{R^{n}}\varphi(z)N(y,E,z)\,{\mathrm{d}}z

(14)

holds. We will make use of the following result
[
14
]
, which is motivated by earlier work of Iwaniec & Onninen
[
33
]
:

Theorem 1
.

Let
y^{(j)}:\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{2}
be a family of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms such that

(i)

y^{(j)}\rightharpoonup y
in
W^{1,n}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(ii)

\sup_{j}\int_{\Omega_{1}}h(\det Dy^{(j)}(x))\,dx<\infty
for some continuous function
h:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\lim_{\delta\to\infty}\frac{h(\delta)}{\delta}=\infty
.

Then
y^{(j)},y
have continuous extensions
\tilde{y}^{(j)},\tilde{y}
to
\bar{\Omega}_{1}
, and
\tilde{y}^{(j)}\to\tilde{y}
uniformly in
\bar{\Omega}_{1}
.

Corollary 2
.

For
p\geqslant n
every
y\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
has an extension
\tilde{y}
to
\bar{\Omega}_{1}
with
\tilde{y}:\bar{\Omega}_{1}\to\bar{\Omega}_{2}
surjective and a homeomorphism, and
\tilde{y}(\partial\Omega_{1})=\partial\Omega_{2}
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case
p=n
, so let
y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
. Then, since
\det A
is an
n^{\rm th}
order polynomial in
A
,
\det Dy\in L^{1}(\Omega_{1})
. Hence there exists a continuous function
h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{\delta\to\infty}\frac{h(\delta)}{\delta}=\infty
and
\int_{\Omega_{1}}h(\det Dy(x))\,dx<\infty
(directly, or as a consequence of the de la Vallée Poussin Theorem
[
22
, p24]
). Hence, applying Theorem
1
to the constant sequence
y^{(j)}:=y
we deduce that
y
has a extension
\tilde{y}:\bar{\Omega}_{1}\to\Omega_{2}
. Similarly
\xi:=y^{-1}
has a continuous extension
\tilde{\xi}:\bar{\Omega}_{2}\to\bar{\Omega}_{1}
. Since

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}(\tilde{y}(x))=x,

\displaystyle\;x\in\Omega_{1},

\displaystyle\tilde{y}(\xi(z))=z,

\displaystyle\;z\in\Omega_{2},

we obtain the same relations for
x\in\bar{\Omega}_{1}
and
z\in\bar{\Omega}_{2}
, from which the injectivity and surjectivity of
\tilde{y}
and
\tilde{\xi}
follows. Since
\tilde{y}(\Omega_{1})=y(\Omega_{1})=\Omega_{2}
it follows that
\tilde{y}(\partial\Omega_{1})=\partial\Omega_{2}
.

In the sequel we drop the tildes and write
\tilde{y}=y,\,\tilde{\xi}=\xi
etc.

We make the following technical hypotheses on
\psi
and
c_{1},c_{2}
.

(H1) (
Continuity
)
\psi:K\times K\times M^{n\times n}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
is continuous,

(H2) (
Coercivity
)
\psi(c,d,A)\geqslant C(|A|^{n}+|A^{-1}|^{n}\det A+h(\det A))

for all
c,d\in K,A\in M^{n\times n}_{+}
, where
C>0
is a constant and
h:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\lim_{\delta\to\infty}\frac{h(\delta)}{\delta}=\infty,\lim_{\delta\to 0+}h(\delta)=\infty
,

(H3) (
Polyconvexity
)
\psi(c,d,\cdot)
is polyconvex for each
c,d\in K
, i.e. there is a function
g:K\times K\times\mathbb{R}^{\sigma(n)}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
with
g(c,d,\cdot)
convex, such that

\psi(c,d,A)=g(c,d,{\mathbf{J}}_{n-1}(A),\det A)\text{ for all }c,d\in K,A\in M^{n\times n}_{+},

where
{\mathbf{J}}_{n-1}(A)
is the list of all minors (i.e. subdeterminants) of
A
of order
\leqslant n-1
and
\sigma(n)
is the number of such minors,

(H4) (
Bounded measurable intensities
)
c_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1},K)
,
c_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{2},K)
.

We note that a sufficient condition for (H2) to hold is that for some
p>n

\displaystyle\psi(c,d,A)\geqslant C(|A|^{p}+|A^{-1}|^{p}\det A)-C_{1},

(15)

where
C>0,C_{1}
are constants, since then

\displaystyle\psi(c,d,A)\geqslant\frac{C}{2}\left(|A|^{n}+|A^{-1}|^{n}\det A+h(\det A)\right),

(16)

where

\displaystyle h(\delta):=n^{\frac{p}{2}}(\delta^{\frac{p}{n}}+\delta^{1-\frac{p}{n}})-\delta-1-\frac{2C_{1}}{C}.

(17)

This follows from the elementary inequality
t^{p}\geqslant t^{n}-1
for
t\in(0,\infty)
and the Hadamard inequality
|B|^{n}\geqslant n^{\frac{n}{2}}\det B
for
B\in M^{n\times n}_{+}
applied to
B=A
and
B=A^{-1}
.

Examples of
\psi
satisfying (H1)-(H3) as well as conditions (C1)-(C3) are given by (
10
) with
\Psi
given by (
45
) below and
f
given by (
11
) or (
12
). Note that the polyconvexity condition (H3) is invariant under the interchange of images transformation (
9
) (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.6]
for the case
n=3
).

3.2
Existence theorem

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
(H1)-(H4)
hold and that
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is nonempty. Then there exists an absolute minimiser of

I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\psi(c_{1}(x),c_{2}(y(x)),Dy(x))\,\mathrm{d}x

in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Before proving Theorem
3
we indicate some differences between our hypotheses and those in other papers where existence for nonlinear elasticity based image comparison functionals is proved without the addition of higher-order gradient terms in the integrand. Rumpf
[
49
]
takes
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}
with boundary condition
y(x)=x
for
x\in\partial\Omega_{1}
(invertibility of
y
being assured via the global inverse function theorem
[
4
]
), and assumes both a more special additive polyconvex form for
\psi
and the stronger condition that the intensity maps are continuous outside a singular set. Similar hypotheses are made in Droske & Rumpf
[
23
]
though the form of the registration term is different, and in Iglesias
[
29
]
. In these papers stronger coercivity conditions of the type (
15
) with
p>n
are assumed. The coercivity condition in
[
18
]
involves minors in the spirit of
[
3
]
, but global invertibility is not addressed. Unlike in these papers we carry out a direct minimization in the class of Sobolev homeomorphisms without fixing
y
on
\partial\Omega_{1}
, and avoiding the use of inverse function theorems.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We suitably adapt the proofs of
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
.
We first note that
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)
is well defined for
y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
since
y^{-1}
has the
N
-property and hence the function
c_{2}(y(\cdot))
is measurable and independent of the representative of
c_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{s})
. Also, by (
14
) we have that

\displaystyle I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)=\int_{\Omega_{2}}\psi(c_{1}(\xi(z)),c_{2}(z),D\xi(z)^{-1})\det D\xi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}}z,

(18)

where
\xi:=y^{-1}
.

We may assume that
\inf_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)<\infty
, since otherwise any
y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
is a minimiser.
Let
y^{(j)}
be a minimizing sequence for
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
with corresponding inverses
\xi^{(j)}
. Then by (H2) and the boundedness of
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
we have that
y^{(j)}
is bounded in
W^{1,n}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\xi^{(j)}
is bounded in
W^{1,n}(\Omega_{2},\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Also, if
n=1
, from the term in
h
in (H2) and the de la Vallée Poussin theorem, we have that
\det Dy^{(j)}=y^{(j)}_{x}
and
\det D\xi^{(j)}=\xi^{(j)}_{z}
are sequentially weakly compact in
L^{1}(\Omega_{1})
,
L^{1}(\Omega_{2})
respectively. Thus we can assume that

\displaystyle y^{(j)}\rightharpoonup y\text{ in }W^{1,n}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;\xi^{(j)}\rightharpoonup\xi\text{ in }W^{1,n}(\Omega_{2},\mathbb{R}^{n}).

By Corollary
2
we have that

Lemma 5

Lemma 6

Define

3.3
Landmark based registration

We now show that for
n\geqslant 2
a minimiser exists under the constraint that
y
maps a finite number
N\geqslant 1
of distinct landmark points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}
belonging to
\Omega_{1}
to corresponding distinct landmark points
q_{1},\ldots,q_{N}
in
\Omega_{2}
. We define the corresponding set of admissible maps

\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}:=\{y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}:y(p_{i})=q_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,N\}.

Theorem 7
.

Suppose that
n\geqslant 2
, that
(H1)-(H4)
hold and that
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
is nonempty. Then there exists an absolute minimiser
y
of
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}
in
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, and
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)<\infty
.

Proof.

By assumption there exists some
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
. Let
\tilde{q}_{i}=w^{-1}(q_{i}),i-1,\ldots,N
. Since
n\geqslant 2
, by
[
15
, Lemma 2.1.10]
,
[
37
]
,
[
35
, p472]
the group of smooth diffeomorphisms of
\Omega_{1}
which are equal to the identity outside some compact set is
N
-transitive. Thus there exists a smooth diffeomorphism
\varphi:\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{1}
which is equal to the identity outside some compact subset of
\Omega_{1}
and such that
\varphi(p_{i})=\tilde{q}_{i}
for each
i
. In particular
D\varphi
and
D(\varphi^{-1})
are uniformly bounded in
\Omega_{1}
. Define
\tilde{y}=w^{-1}\circ\varphi
. Then
\tilde{y}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}),\tilde{y}^{-1}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{2},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\tilde{y}(p_{i})=q_{i}
for each
i
. Hence
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
is nonempty and
\inf_{\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}}I_{P_{1},P_{2}}<\infty
. The result now follows because for a minimising sequence
y^{(j)}
of
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}
in
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
as in Theorem
3
with
y^{(j)}\rightharpoonup y
in
W^{1,n}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have
y^{(j)}(p_{i})=q_{i}\to y(p_{i})
for each
i
, and hence
y\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
.

Remark 3
.

If we assume only that
p_{i}\in\bar{\Omega}_{1}
,
q_{i}\in\bar{\Omega}_{2}
then
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
may be empty. Suppose that
p_{i}\in\partial\Omega_{1}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
,
p_{i}\in\Omega_{1}
otherwise. Then by Theorem
2
a necessary condition for
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
to be nonempty (so that a minimiser exists) is that

\displaystyle q_{i}\in\partial\Omega_{2}\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant k,\;q_{i}\in\Omega_{2}\text{ otherwise}.

(29)

Consider the case when
\Omega_{1}=B_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<1\}
(equivalently for any image
\Omega_{1}
of
B_{n}
under an orientation-preserving bi-Lipschitz map, such as an
n-
cube). Then if
n=2
the necessary condition (
29
) is not sufficient. For example let
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}=B_{2}
and
N=k=4
with
p_{1}=q_{1}=(0,1)
,
p_{2}=q_{3}=(0,1)
,
p_{3}=q_{2}=(-1,0)
,
p_{4}=q_{4}=(0,-1)
. Then any
y\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
restricted to
S^{1}=\partial B_{2}
would be a homeomorphism of
S^{1}
with
y(p_{i})=q_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant 4
, which is impossible since then the connected image under
y
of the arc
C:=\{(\cos\theta,\sin\theta):0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}\}
would contain either
q_{3}
or
q_{4}
, so that either
p_{3}\in C
or
p_{4}\in C
, which is not the case.

However if
n\geqslant 3
then (
29
)
is
sufficient. Following the proof of Theorem
7
it is enough to prove this in the case
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}=B_{n}
. Applying the result on transitivity cited in the proof to the manifold
S^{n-1}=\partial B_{n}
, whose dimension is
\geqslant 2
, we obtain a smooth diffeomorphism
f
of
S^{n-1}
with
f(p_{i})=q_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
. The radial extension

\Phi(x)=|x|f\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)

is a bi-Lipschitz self-map of the closed ball
\bar{B}_{n}
. If
k=N
then
\Phi\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
. If
k<N
then, applying
(N-k)-
transitivity for diffeomorphisms of
B_{n}
, there exists a diffeomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}
of
B_{n}
equal to the identity near
S^{n-1}
such that
\tilde{\varphi}(\Phi(p_{i}))=q_{i}
for

, so that
\tilde{\varphi}\circ\Phi\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
. (We remark that

cannot in general be extended to a diffeomorphism of
\bar{B}_{n}
, though, for example, a theorem of Smale
[
52
]
(see Thurston
[
54
, Theorem 3.10.11]
) states that this is possible for

.)

3.4
Comparing one image with part of another

In this subsection we regard
P_{1}=(\Omega_{1},c_{1})
as a template image to be detected as a homeomorphic part of the image
P_{2}=(\Omega_{2},c_{2})
, allowing for changes of scale, perspective, or other affine transformations. (Because in this situation
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
haves different roles, integrands
\psi
that do not satisfy (C3) may be appropriate.)

Let
S
be a relatively closed subset of
M^{n\times n}_{+}
. We consider the minimisation of
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)
subject to the constraint that
y(\Omega_{1})=a+A\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
A\in S
, and establish existence of minimisers.

Interesting examples with relevance to particular object detection and recognition problems include

(i)

S=\{\mu{\mathbf{1}}:0<\lambda\leqslant\mu\leqslant\Lambda<\infty\}
for fixed
\lambda,\Lambda
, detecting an undistorted template image within a target image at a range of scalings (or a fixed scaling if
\lambda=\Lambda
);

(ii)

S=\{\mu R:R\in SO(n),0<\lambda\leqslant\mu\leqslant\Lambda<\infty\}
for fixed
\lambda,\Lambda
, allowing for rotation, scaling and magnification/reduction within a certain closed range.

(iii)

S=M^{n\times n}_{+}
, allowing for any affine transformation of
\Omega_{1}
.

We define the set of admissible maps by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n,S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\{y\in W^{1,n}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}):y:\Omega_{1}\to a+A\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}\text{ a homeomorphism}

\displaystyle\hskip-166.2212pt\text{for some }a\in\mathbb{R}^{n},A\in S\text{ with }y^{-1}\in W^{1,n}(a+A\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n})\}.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose
(H1)-(H4)
hold and that
\mathcal{A}_{n,S}
is nonempty. Then there exists an absolute minimiser of
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{n,S}
.

Proof.

As in Theorem
3
we consider a minimising sequence
y^{(j)}
for which

\lim_{j\to\infty}I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y^{(j)})=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{A}_{n,S}}I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y):=l<\infty.

(30)

(If desired we can take
y^{(j)}
to be for each
j
a minimiser of
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y)
in
\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{1},a^{(j)}+A^{(j)}\Omega_{1})
, whose existence is guaranteed by Theorem
3
, but this does not simplify the proof.) We have that
y^{(j)}(\Omega_{1})=a^{(j)}+A^{(j)}\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
for some
a^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},A^{(j)}\in M^{n\times n}_{+}
. We first claim that
a^{(j)}
and
A^{(j)}
are bounded. Let
B(x_{0},\varepsilon)\subset\Omega_{1}
for some
x_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\Omega_{2}
is bounded we have that
|a^{(j)}+A^{(j)}(x_{0}+\varepsilon z)|\leqslant M<\infty
whenever
|z|<1
. Hence
|A^{(j)}\varepsilon z|\leqslant|a^{(j)}+A^{(j)}(x_{0}+\varepsilon z)-(a^{(j)}+A^{(j)}x_{0})|\leqslant 2M
, from which it follows that
|A^{(j)}|\leqslant 2M\varepsilon^{-1}
, so that the
A^{(j)}
, and hence also the
a^{(j)}
, are bounded. Therefore we may assume that
A^{(j)}\to A
and
a^{(j)}\to a
as

.

Since

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\det Dy^{(j)}\,\mathrm{d}x={\mathcal{L}}^{n}(a^{(j)}+A^{(j)}\Omega_{1})=(\det A^{(j)}){\mathcal{L}}^{n}(\Omega_{1}),

(31)

if
\det{A^{(j)}}\to 0
, then
\det Dy^{(j)}\to 0
in
L^{1}(\Omega_{1})
and so for a subsequence, not relabelled,
\det Dy^{(j)}\to 0
a.e. in
\Omega_{1}
. Hence, by Fatou’s Lemma,

\infty=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\,h(\det Dy^{(j)})\,dx\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y^{(j)})<\infty.

This contradiction proves that
\det A>0
, so that
A\in S
.

Now define
B^{(j)}:=A^{(j)}A^{-1}
and
\tilde{y}^{(j)}:\Omega_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{y}^{(j)}(x):=a+(B^{(j)})^{-1}(y^{(j)}(x)-a^{(j)}),

(32)

so that
Dy^{(j)}(x)=B^{(j)}D\tilde{y}^{(j)}(x)
and
\tilde{y}^{(j)}(\Omega_{1})=a+A\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
. It is easily checked that
\tilde{y}^{(j)}:\Omega_{1}\to a+A\Omega_{1}
is a homeomorphism for each
j
. Note that
\lim_{j\to\infty}B^{(j)}={\mathbf{1}}
, where
{\mathbf{1}}
denotes the identity matrix in
M^{n\times n}
. Then

\displaystyle I_{P_{1},P_{2}}(y^{(j)})=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\psi(c_{1}(x),c_{2}(a^{(j)}+B^{(j)}(\tilde{y}^{(j)}(x)-a)),B^{(j)}D\tilde{y}^{(j)}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x.

(33)

As in Theorem
3
we may suppose that

\tilde{y}^{(j)}\rightharpoonup y\text{ in }W^{1,n}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;\tilde{\xi}^{(j)}\rightharpoonup\xi\text{ in }W^{1,n}(a+A\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\tilde{\xi}^{(j)}:=(\tilde{y}^{(j)})^{-1}
,
and it follows as before that
y:\Omega_{1}\to a+A\Omega_{1}
is a homeomorphism with inverse
\xi
. A modification of Lemma
4
shows that for a further subsequence

c_{2}(a^{(j)}+B^{(j)}(\tilde{y}^{(j)}(x)-a))\to c_{2}(y(x))
a.e. in
\Omega_{1}
. The key point is to show that for
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{a+A\Omega_{1}}c_{2}(a^{(j)}+B^{(j)}(z-a))\varphi(\xi(z))\det D\tilde{\xi}^{(j)}(z)\,{\mathrm{d}}z=\int_{a+A\Omega_{1}}c_{2}(z)\varphi(\xi(z))\det D\xi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}}z.

But this follows since
\det D\tilde{\xi}^{(j)}\rightharpoonup\det D\xi
in
L^{1}(\alpha+A\Omega_{1})
and (extracting a further subsequence)

c_{2}(a^{(j)}+B^{(j)}(\cdot-a))\to c_{2}
boundedly a.e. in
\alpha+A\Omega_{1}
.
The remainder of the proof of Theorem
3
is then easily adapted to show that

is a minimiser.

Motivated by the work of Berkels, Effland and Rumpf

We assume that

and

Let

Given an integer

Lemma 9
.

For
N\geqslant 1,M\geqslant 1
and

we have that

\displaystyle F_{N+M}(c,e)\leqslant F_{N}(c,d)+F_{M}(d,e).

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon>0
there exist

with

\sum_{i=1}^{N}F(c_{i},c_{i+1})<F_{N}(c,d)+\varepsilon,\;\sum_{i=N+1}^{M+N}F(c_{i},c_{i+1})<F_{M}(d,e)+\varepsilon.

Hence

and the result follows by letting

.

Now define for

\displaystyle\rho(c,d):=\inf_{N\geqslant 1}F_{N}(c,d)=\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(c,d).

(35)

Lemma 10

Suppose

\displaystyle F(c,d)\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}F(c^{(j)},d^{(j)})

Proof.

By Theorem

4
Quasiconvexity and affine scaling

4.1
Affinely related images and a corresponding quasiconvexity condition

Suppose that the images
P_{1}=(\Omega_{1},c_{1})
and
P_{2}=(\Omega_{2},c_{2})
are affinely related, i.e. for some
M\in M^{n\times n}_{+}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}=M\Omega_{1}+a,\;\;c_{2}(Mx+a)=c_{1}(x).

(40)

We ask whether we can choose
\psi
such that for each such pair of affinely related images the unique minimiser
y
of
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
is
y(x)=Mx+a
. Note that without loss of generality we can assume that
a=0
, since
y
is a minimiser of
I_{P_{1},P_{2}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(\Omega_{1},M\Omega_{1}+a)
iff
y-a
is a minimiser of
I_{P_{1},\tilde{P}_{2}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(\Omega_{1},M\Omega_{1})
, where
\tilde{P}_{2}:=(M\Omega_{1},c_{2}(\cdot+a))
. Therefore we consider below only pairs
P_{1}=(\Omega_{1},c_{1})
and
P_{2}=(\Omega_{2},c_{2})
of
linearly related
images, i.e.

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}=M\Omega_{1},\;\;c_{2}(Mx)=c_{1}(x).

(41)

We consider
\psi
of the form (
10
), (
11
), that is

\displaystyle\psi(c_{1},c_{2},A)=\Psi(A)+(1+\det A)g(c_{1},c_{2}),

(42)

where
\Psi:M^{n\times n}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
is isotropic with
\Psi(A)=\det A\cdot\Psi(A^{-1})
,
\Psi^{-1}(0)=SO(n)
, and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
continuous with
g(c_{1},c_{2})=g(c_{2},c_{1})
,
g(c_{1},c_{2})=0
iff
c_{1}=c_{2}
.
Thus, since
g(c_{1}(x),c_{2}(Mx))=0
, we require that for all
y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
with
y(\Omega_{1})=M\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left(\Psi(Dy(x))+(1+\det Dy(x))g(c_{1}(x),c_{2}(y(x)))\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\geqslant\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Psi(M)\,\mathrm{d}x,\;\;

(43)

with equality iff
y(x)=Mx
.

Since
g\geqslant 0
, a sufficient condition for (
43
) to hold is that

\displaystyle-\hskip-11.56346pt\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Psi(Dy)\,\mathrm{d}x\geqslant\Psi(M)

(44)

for all
\Omega_{1}
and
y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, where
-\hskip-9.39545pt\int_{\Omega_{1}}f\,dx:=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(\Omega_{1})}\int_{\Omega_{1}}f\,dx
. If we require (
43
) to hold for a sufficiently large family of linearly related image pairs then (
44
) is also necessary. Indeed, given
y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}=\mathcal{A}_{n}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
choose
c:\Omega_{1}\to K
to be a bounded measurable function invariant under the map
M^{-1}y:\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{1}
; for example we can take
c
to be constant, but there can be nonconstant such
c
. Suppose that (
43
) holds for some sequence of image pairs
P_{1}^{(j)}=(\Omega_{1},c_{1}^{(j)}),\,P_{2}^{(j)}=(M\Omega_{1},c_{2}^{(j)})
, where
c_{1}^{(j)}:\Omega_{1}\to K
,
c_{1}^{(j)}(x)\to c(x)
boundedly a.e. in
\Omega_{1}
and
c^{(j)}_{2}(z):=c_{1}^{(j)}(M^{-1}z)
. Then
c^{(j)}_{2}(y(x))\to c(M^{-1}y(x))=c(x)
boundedly a.e. in
\Omega_{1}
, so that

boundedly a.e. in
\Omega_{1}
. Hence passing to the limit

in (
43
) for

we get (
44
) for

.

The inequality (

, in which the usual requirement that

for

is weakened to

. The requirement that (

for

is

, the central convexity condition of the multi-dimensional calculus of variations, which is implied by polyconvexity (see, e.g.
[

]
). (Note, however, that in the calculus of variations literature the quasiconvexity inequality is not usually subject to the extra condition that

is a homeomorphism. For a recent discussion of this issue see
[
1
]
.)

4.5

Theorem

5
Remarks on regularity and numerical implementation

In order to understand some of the issues relating to regularity and rigorous numerical implementation, it is worth recalling the state of the art for the case of pure nonlinear elasticity with no image comparison terms, namely for energy functionals of the form

\displaystyle I(y)=\int_{\Omega}\Psi(Dy(x))\mathrm{d}x,

(85)

with
\Psi:M^{n\times n}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
a sufficiently smooth free-energy function satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{\det A\to 0+}\Psi(A)=\infty.

(86)

Although one would like to be able to handle quasiconvex
\Psi
, the existence theory for minimisers of
I
subject to appropriate boundary conditions is currently restricted to the case of polyconvex
\Psi
satisfying growth hypotheses such as (H2), with models of natural polymers such as rubber having a form similar to (
45
). Although a common failure mechanism for such polymers is cavitation (see
[
6
]
), growth conditions such as (H2) do not allow cavitation, and from an experimental perspective it seems reasonable to expect that minimisers are smooth. However this has not been proved for any interesting case if
n\geqslant 2
(for a discussion see
[
8
, Section 2.3]
). It is not even known if minimisers are locally Lipschitz. Furthermore, despite (
86
), it is a longstanding open problem to decide whether every minimiser
y
satisfies

\displaystyle\det Dy(x)\geqslant\mu>0\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega

(87)

for some
\mu
.

When we add image comparison terms the most we can hope for in general as regards the regularity of minimisers is that they are bi-Lipschitz. In fact consider the case
n=m=1
and
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}=(0,1)
with
\psi
given by (
10
), (
11
) and
g(c_{1},c_{2})=\varepsilon^{-1}|c_{1}-c_{2}|^{2}
, where we will later choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. Thus we have to minimise

\displaystyle I(y)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\varepsilon\Psi(y_{x})+(1+y_{x})|c_{1}(x)-c_{2}(y(x))|^{2}\right)\,dx

(88)

over
y\in\mathcal{A}_{1}:=\{y\in W^{1,1}(0,1):y(0)=0,y(1)=1,y_{x}>0\text{ a.e.},\,y^{-1}\in W^{1,1}(0,1)\}
. We assume that
\Psi\geqslant 0
is
C^{1}
and strictly convex with
\Psi(1)=0
and
\lim_{p\to 0+}\Psi(p)=\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\Psi(p)}{p}=\infty
. Let us take

c_{1}(x)=\chi_{(0,\frac{1}{2})}(x),\;c_{2}(x)=\chi_{(0,\frac{3}{4})}(x).

Then by Theorem
3
there exists a minimiser
y
of
I
in
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
a:=y^{-1}(\frac{3}{4})\in(0,1)
. Suppose first that
a\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
. Then
|c_{1}(x)-c_{2}(y(x))|^{2}=\chi_{(a,\frac{1}{2})}(x)
, so that
y
is a minimiser of

I(v)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\varepsilon\Psi(v_{x})+(1+v_{x})\chi_{(a,\frac{1}{2})}(x)\right)\,dx

for
v\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
such that
v(a)=y(a),\,v(\frac{1}{2})=y(\frac{1}{2})
. Since
\Psi
is strictly convex we have that
y_{x}
is constant in each of the intervals
(0,a)
,
(a,\frac{1}{2})
and
(\frac{1}{2},1)
, and if these constants are all equal then we must have
y(x)=x
. The same argument holds in the case
a\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
. Hence the minimiser
y
is Lipschitz but not
C^{1}
unless
y(x)=x
, when
I(y)=\frac{1}{2}
. But choosing

\bar{y}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{3}{2}x,&0\leqslant x\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{1}{2}(1+x),&\frac{1}{2}\leqslant x\leqslant 1,\end{array}\right.

we find that
I(\bar{y})=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\left(\Psi(\frac{3}{2})+\Psi(\frac{1}{2})\right)<\frac{1}{2}
for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, so that the minimiser is bi-Lipschitz but not
C^{1}
.

Even in the special case (
88
) it does not seem obvious whether every minimiser is bi-Lipschitz for arbitrary
c_{1},c_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. However if we assume more regularity, that
c_{1},c_{2}\in C^{1}([0,1])
, then any minimiser
y
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism, so that in particular
y_{x}(x)\geqslant\mu>0
for a.e.
x\in(0,1)
. This follows by writing (taking
\varepsilon=1
)

I(y)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\Psi(x,y_{x})+\theta(x,y)\right)\,dx+\tau,

where
\Psi(x,p)=\Psi(p)+(1+p)|c_{1}(x)|^{2}
,
\theta(x,y)=|c_{2}(y)|^{2}-2c_{1}(x)c_{2}(y)+2c_{1x}(x)C_{2}(y)
,
C_{2}(y):=\int_{0}^{y}c_{2}(s)\,ds
and
\tau
is a constant, and applying the regularity result
[
5
, Théorème 2]
for integrands having this form.

As for nonlinear elasticity, from a rigorous perspective the formulation of a provably convergent minimization scheme is challenging due to the possible occurence of the Lavrentiev phenomenon, whereby the infimum of the energy may depend on the function space, this being related to possible unboundedness of
Dy
or vanishing of
\det Dy
. Such difficulties are compounded for our models due to possible discontinuities in the intensity maps. Furthermore the minimization is to be carried out in a space of Sobolev homeomorphisms. In particular, the approximation of Sobolev homeomorphisms by piecewise affine homeomorphisms, such as given by piecewise affine finite elements, is a tricky issue, that has only been resolved for
n=2
by Iwaniec, Kovalev and Onninen
[
31
]
. Some of the methods for overcoming the Lavrentiev phenomenon that have been proposed and analyzed are described in
[
7
]
.

These issues are to some extent simplified, but not eliminated, for energy functionals with regularizing higher derivative terms, for which higher order elements are required. For such models, for example, it is possible to prove the existence of minimizers satisfying (
87
) under suitable growth conditions
[
43
]
. In second and higher order gradient theories of elasticity higher derivative terms are typically added to represent interfacial energy, often in cases for which the underlying nonlinear elastic energy is not quasiconvex, such as in the modelling of martensitic microstructures (see, for example,
[
12
,
9
]
). It is possible that the use of such non quasiconvex energies could be of value for the analysis of images with fine structure, such as those arising in materials science.

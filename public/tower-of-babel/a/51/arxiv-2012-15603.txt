Higher-order vector Peregrine solitons and asymptotic estimates for
the multi-component nonlinear Schrödinger equations

Abstract
We first report the first- and higher-order vector Peregrine solitons (alias rational rogue waves) for the any multi-component NLS equations based on the loop group theory, an explicit ( n + 1 ) \left(n+1\right) -multiple eigenvalue of a characteristic polynomial of degree ( n + 1 ) (n+1) related to the condition of Benjamin-Feir instability, and inverse functions. Particularly, these vector rational rogue waves are parity-time symmetric for some parameter constraints. A systematic and effective approach is proposed to study the asymptotic behaviors of these vector rogue waves such that the decompositions of rogue waves are related to the so-called governing polynomials ℱ ℓ ​ ( z ) \mathcal{F}_{\ell}(z) , which pave a powerful way in the study of vector rogue wave structures of the multi-component integrable systems. The vector rogue waves with maximal amplitudes can be determined via the parameter vectors, which is interesting and useful in the multi-component physical systems. Keywords: Multi-component NLS equations, Lax pair, Darboux transform, Benjamin-Feir instability, higher-order vector Peregrine solitons, parity-time symmetry, asymptotic estimates

In this work, we try to propose an approach to analyze the high-order RWs quantitatively, which may prove the

We first find a new explicit

2.1
Darboux transform with simple poles

A rigorous inverse scattering analysis to the spectral problem (
4
) under the nonzero plane-wave background:

q_{j}\sim a_{j}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}b_{j}x\pm\theta_{\pm}},\qquad x\to\pm\infty,

(8)

is still an open question until now, where
a_{j}
,

’s are arbitrary non-zero real parameters. Partial results can be recalled in
[

.
To avoid the rigorous inverse scattering analysis on the non-vanishing background, we assume that:

Assumption 1
.

If
\mathbf{q}(x,t)\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cup\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then there exists a sectional analytic matrix:

\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t):\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(\mathbb{C}/(\Sigma\cup\mathbb{R}))\to{\rm SU}(n+1,\mathbb{C}),

(9)

where the contour
\Sigma
is cut of some Riemann surface, such that

•

\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)
is meromorphic for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}/(\Sigma\cup\mathbb{R})
,

•

\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I}+\mathbf{M}_{1}(x,t)\lambda^{-1}+\mathbf{M}_{2}(x,t)\lambda^{-2}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-3})
in the neighborhood of
\infty
,

•

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Phi(\lambda;x,t)\right)\Phi^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathrm{i}\left(\lambda\sigma_{3}+\mathbf{Q}\right)
, where
\Phi(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(x+\lambda t)\sigma_{3}}\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\lambda;0,0)
is holomorphic in
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

Actually, we just consider the construction of exact analytic solutions, which implies that the above assumptions will be satisfied automatically.
Further, we find the symmetric property of the holomorphic function.

Lemma 1
.

\Phi(\lambda;x,t)\Phi^{{\dagger}}(\lambda^{*};x,t)=\mathbb{I}_{n+1}
if the holomorphic function
\Phi(\lambda;x,t)
in Eqs. (
4

5

\Phi(\lambda;0,0)=\mathbb{I}_{n+1}

Proof.

See Appendix A.
∎

On the other hand, by the asymptotic behavior of
\Phi(\lambda;x,t)
, we can set

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(x+\lambda t)\sigma_{3}}\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\lambda;0,0),

(10)

where
\mathbf{N}(\lambda;x,t)\equiv\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(x+\lambda t)\sigma_{3}}
satisfies the following Riemann-Hilbert problems:

\mathbf{N}_{+}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{N}_{-}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{J}(\lambda),\qquad\lambda\in\Sigma,

(11)

where
\mathbf{J}(\lambda)
is a certain jump matrix
and
\Sigma
is a certain contour. And the analytic matrix
\mathbf{N}(\lambda;0,0)=\mathbf{M}(\lambda;0,0)
also satisfies the same jump condition. Thus the matrix function
\Phi(\lambda;x,t)
is holomorphic in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}.
So are the matrices of
\Phi_{x}(\lambda;x,t)
and
\Phi_{t}(\lambda;x,t)
. By the sectional analytic matrix
\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)
, the integrable hierarchy can be constructed:

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Theta(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)\sigma_{3}\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)
, then we have the expansions in the neighorhood of
\infty

\Theta(\lambda;x,t)=\sigma_{3}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{j}(x,t)\lambda^{-j},

(12)

where
\Theta_{j}
’s are the differential polynomials of
\mathbf{Q}
and
\sigma_{3}
.

Proof.

See Appendix B.
∎

Define the following evolutionary hierarchy:

\mathbf{V}_{j}(\lambda;x,t):=[\mathrm{i}\,\lambda^{j}\Theta(\lambda;x,t)]_{+},

(13)

where the subscript

means taking the principal part of the corresponding expansions. Lemma
2
can generate the first two terms

\Theta_{1}=\mathbf{Q}\equiv[\mathbf{M}_{1},\sigma_{3}],\quad\Theta_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{3}(\mathrm{i}\mathbf{Q}_{x}+\mathbf{Q}^{2}).

(14)

Therefore the first non-trivial flow
\mathbf{V}_{2}(\lambda;x,t)
is just
\mathbf{V}(\lambda;x,t)
given by Eq. (
5
).

Then we consider [cf. Eq. (

\Phi_{x}(\lambda;x,t)\Phi^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{M}_{x}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)+\mathrm{i}\,\lambda\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)\sigma_{3}\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t),

and

\Phi_{t}(\lambda;x,t)\Phi^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{M}_{t}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)+\mathrm{i}\,\lambda^{2}\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)\sigma_{3}\mathbf{M}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t),

which implies the Lax pair (
4
) and (
5
) by the Liouville theorem and Lemmas
1
and
2
. Now we would like to proceed to construct the Darboux matrix by calculating the residue.

Let
\Phi(\lambda;x,t)
be the analytic matrix function solution of Lax pair (
4
) and (
5
) in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Choosing any point
\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
(i.e., the upper half complex plane), we consider the following gauge transformation

\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\Phi(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{1}^{-1}(\lambda;0,0),\quad\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\equiv\mathbb{I}+\frac{\mathbf{P}_{1}(x,t)}{\lambda-\lambda_{1}^{*}},

(17)

which requires the following conditions:

•

the determinant
\oint_{C}{\rm d}\ln\det(\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t))=2\pi\mathrm{i}\left(N-P\right)=0
, where
N
denotes the number of zeros and
P
denotes the number of poles,

•

{\rm rank}(\mathbf{P}_{1}(x,t))=1
,
\mathbf{P}_{1}^{2}(x,t)=\alpha\mathbf{P}_{1}(x,t)
, where
\alpha
is a undetermined constant,

•

The same formal Lax pair
\Phi^{[1]}_{x}=\mathbf{U}^{[1]}\Phi^{[1]}
and
\Phi^{[1]}_{t}=\mathbf{V}^{[1]}\Phi^{[1]}
, where
\mathbf{U}^{[1]}=\mathbf{U}\big|_{\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{Q}^{[1]}},\,\mathbf{V}^{[1]}=\mathbf{V}\big|_{\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{Q}^{[1]}}

\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda(x+\lambda t)\sigma_{3}}\to\mathbb{I}

By the Abel formula and the above third requirement, we know that the determinant
\det(\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t))
satisfies

\partial_{x}\det(\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t))=\partial_{t}\det(\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t))=0,

which deduces that

\det(\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t))=1+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda-\lambda_{1}^{*}}.

(18)

Thus the Darboux matrix and its inverse can be rewritten as

\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I}+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda-\lambda_{1}^{*}}\frac{\varphi_{1}(x,t)\psi_{1}(x,t)}{\psi_{1}(x,t)\varphi_{1}(x,t)},\qquad\mathbf{T}_{1}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I}-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda-\lambda_{1}^{*}+\alpha}\frac{\varphi_{1}(x,t)\psi_{1}(x,t)}{\psi_{1}(x,t)\varphi_{1}(x,t)},

where
\varphi_{1}(x,t)
is a column vector and
\psi_{1}(x,t)
is a row vector. Therefore the residues for
\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)
at
\lambda=\lambda_{1}^{*}
and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\underset{\lambda=\lambda_{1}^{*}}{\rm Res}\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)&=\displaystyle\alpha\frac{\varphi_{1}(x,t)\psi_{1}(x,t)}{\psi_{1}(x,t)\varphi_{1}(x,t)}\Phi(\lambda_{1}^{*};x,t)\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{\varphi_{1}(0,0)\psi_{1}(0,0)}{\psi_{1}(0,0)\varphi_{1}(0,0)}\right)=0,\\
\underset{\lambda=\lambda_{1}^{*}-\alpha}{\rm Res}\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)&=\displaystyle-\alpha\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{\varphi_{1}(x,t)\psi_{1}(x,t)}{\psi_{1}(x,t)\varphi_{1}(x,t)}\right)\Phi(\lambda_{1}^{*}-\alpha;x,t)\frac{\varphi_{1}(0,0)\psi_{1}(0,0)}{\psi_{1}(0,0)\varphi_{1}(0,0)}=0.\end{array}

Furthermore, by the linear algebra we know that

\begin{split}\psi_{1}\Phi(\lambda_{1}^{*};x,t)\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{\varphi_{1}(0,0)\psi_{1}(0,0)}{\psi_{1}(0,0)\varphi_{1}(0,0)}\right)=0,\quad\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{\varphi_{1}(x,t)\psi_{1}(x,t)}{\psi_{1}(x,t)\varphi_{1}(x,t)}\right)\Phi(\lambda_{1}^{*}-\alpha;x,t)\varphi_{1}(0,0)=0.\end{split}

(23)

1

\left(\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda^{*};x,t)\right)^{{\dagger}}\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I},

\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{1}^{{\dagger}}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I}.

(24)

Further, the condition
\alpha=\lambda_{1}^{*}-\lambda_{1}
and
\varphi_{1}(x,t)=\psi_{1}^{{\dagger}}(x,t)=\Phi(\lambda_{1};x,t)\varphi_{1}(0,0)
can deduce the above sufficient condition (

[

Proof.

Consider the matrix function

\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{M}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(x+\lambda t)\sigma_{3}}\left(\mathbf{M}^{[1]}(\lambda;0,0)\right)^{-1},\quad\mathbf{M}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t).

(27)

Since
\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)
can be expanded in the neighborhood of
\infty
:

\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda)=\mathbb{I}-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}^{*})\mathbf{P}_{1}(x,t)\lambda^{-1}+O(\lambda^{-2})

(28)

then we have

\mathbf{M}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda)\mathbf{M}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I}+\left[\mathbf{M}_{1}(x,t)-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}^{*})\mathbf{P}_{1}(x,t)\right]\lambda^{-1}+O(\lambda^{-2}),

(29)

which implies the Bäcklund (or Darboux) transformation (
26
). Through the Liouville theorem, we have

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)\right)\left(\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)\right)^{-1}&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\right)\mathbf{T}_{1}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)+\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{U}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{1}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)\\
&=\mathbf{U}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t),\\
\end{split}

(30)

and

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)\right)\left(\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)\right)^{-1}&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\right)\mathbf{T}_{1}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)+\mathbf{T}_{1}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{V}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{1}^{-1}(\lambda;x,t)\\
&=\mathbf{V}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t),\end{split}

(31)

which prove that the holomorphic function
\Phi^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)
solves the Lax pair (
4
) and (
5
) by replacing the potential function
\mathbf{Q}(x,t)
with Eq. (
26
).
∎

2.2
Darboux transform with
k
high-order poles

Here we extend the Darboux matrix with a general case containing
k
high-order poles for the
n
-NLS equations to present the Darboux transformation:

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{q}(x,t)\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cup\mathbf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a smooth solution and
\Phi(\lambda;x,t)
is an analytic matrix solution in the whole complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Then the Darboux transformation

\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I}-\mathbf{Y}_{N}\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{{\dagger}},

(32)

converts the Lax pair (
4
) and (
5
) into the new one by replacing the potential functions

\mathbf{q}^{[N]}(x,t)=\mathbf{q}(x,t)-2\mathbf{Y}_{N,2}\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_{N,1}^{{\dagger}},

(33)

where
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathbf{D}_{1},\mathbf{D}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{D}_{k}\right)
,
\mathbf{D}_{s}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{He}(i-j+1)}{\left(\lambda-\lambda_{s}^{*}\right)^{i-j+1}}\right)_{0\leq i,j\leq r_{s}-1},s=0,1,\cdots,k
,
\mathrm{He}(x)
is the heaviside function [
\mathrm{He}(x)=1
for
x>0
and zero otherwise],
\mathbf{M}=\left(\mathbf{M}_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
,
\mathbf{M}_{i,j}=\left(M_{i,j}^{[s,\ell]}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq r_{i}-1\\
0\leq\ell\leq r_{j}-1\end{subarray}}
with

\displaystyle M_{i,j}^{[s,\ell]}=\sum_{p=0}^{s}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\binom{p+m}{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m}}{\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}^{*}\right)^{p+m+1}}\left(\varphi_{i}^{[s-p]}\right)^{\dagger}\varphi_{j}^{[\ell-m]},

(34)

the subscript
\mathbf{Y}_{N,1}
denotes the first row vector of
\mathbf{Y}_{N}
, the subscript
\mathbf{Y}_{N,2}
the second up to
n+1
-th row vector,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{N}=\left(\varphi_{1}^{[0]},\varphi_{1}^{[1]},\cdots,\varphi_{1}^{[r_{1}-1]},\varphi_{2}^{[0]},\varphi_{2}^{[1]},\cdots,\varphi_{2}^{[r_{2}-1]},\cdots,\varphi_{k}^{[0]},\varphi_{k}^{[1]},\cdots,\varphi_{k}^{[r_{k}-1]}\right),\quad N=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i},

(35)

and
\varphi_{i}^{[j]}=\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\lambda}\right)^{j}\Phi(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{v}(\lambda)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq k,0\leq j\leq r_{i}-1
.

Proof.

The general Darboux matrices can be constructed by the successive iterations of elementary Darboux transformation. Suppose that we iterate the Darboux transformation at
\lambda=\lambda_{i}
for
r_{i}
times. The Darboux matrices can be obtained by the above-mentioned Theorem
3
. We merely need to rewrite the general Darboux matrices by the above compact form (
32
).

By the form of poles, assume that the general Darboux matrix can be written as the form

\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{l=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{l}}\frac{1}{(\lambda-\lambda_{l}^{*})^{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{l}-i+1}|z_{l}^{[j+i-1]}(x,t)\rangle\langle y_{l}^{[j]}(x,t)|,

(36)

where the notations
|\cdot\rangle,\langle\cdot|
represent the row and column vector respectively, and
|\cdot\rangle=(\langle\cdot|)^{{\dagger}}
.
By the formula

\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{N}^{{\dagger}}(\lambda^{*};x,t)=\mathbb{I}_{n+1},

(37)

we have the residues:

\underset{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}{\rm Res}\left((\lambda-\lambda_{i})^{l}\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{N}^{{\dagger}}(\lambda^{*};x,t)\right)=0,\quad l=0,1,\cdots,r_{i}-1;\quad i=1,2,\cdots,k,

(38)

which imply that

\sum_{j=1}^{l}\left[\frac{1}{(j-1)!}\left(\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\lambda}\right)^{j-1}\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}\right]\left|y_{i}^{[l-j+1]}(x,t)\right\rangle=0.

(39)

On the other hand, through the holomorphic function

\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)\Phi(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{N}^{-1}(\lambda;0,0)=\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)\Phi(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{N}^{{\dagger}}(\lambda^{*};0,0)

(40)

we have

\underset{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}{\rm Res}\left((\lambda-\lambda_{i})^{l}\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{N}^{{\dagger}}(\lambda^{*};x,t)\right)=0,\qquad l=0,1,\cdots,r_{i}-1;\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,k,

(41)

which can yield

\sum_{j=1}^{l}\left(\frac{1}{(j-1)!}\left(\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\lambda}\right)^{j-1}\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}\right)\\
\times\left(\sum_{s=1}^{l-j+1}\frac{1}{(s-1)!}\left(\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\lambda}\right)^{s-1}\Phi(\lambda;x,t)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}\left|y_{i}^{[l-j+2-s]}(0,0)\right\rangle\right)=0.

(42)

Combining Eq. (
39
) with Eq. (
42
), we arrive at

\left|y_{i}^{[m]}\right\rangle=\sum_{s=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(s-1)!}\left(\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\lambda}\right)^{s-1}\Phi(\lambda;x,t)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}}\left|y_{i}^{[m+1-s]}(0,0)\right\rangle.

(43)

Meanwhile, by the linear algebra and Eq. (
39
), the row vectors
|z_{i}^{[m]}(x,t)\rangle
can be solved exactly, which deduces the formula (
32
).
The Bäcklund transformation (
26
) between old and new potential functions can be deduced by the following equations:

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)+\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{U}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{U}^{[N]}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{T}_{N}(\lambda;x,t),

(44)

through the expansions at the neighborhood of
\infty
.
∎

Remark 1
.

The general Darboux matrix and the corresponding Bäcklund transformation can be rewritten with the other formulation due to the Taylor expansion on the spectral parameters. Here we just write it in a compact and uniform form.

A direct application for the above theorem is to construct new exact solutions by the Bäcklund transformation. For the vector NLS equations, we can obtain the multi-solitons on the zero background, multi-breathers on the non-vanishing background and so on. In the current work, we focus on the rational RW solutions at the branch point with a genus zero curve. Actually, the above formula (
33
) is flexible, we do not need to confirm the fixed formula to construct the exact solutions. Thus when we confront the explicit problems, the suitable modifications on the formulas is better to represent them in a more compact form.

3.1

To study the vector rational RWs of the

To apply the above-mentioned Darboux transformation with the initial non-zero PW background (

4
) and (
5
) with

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \Phi_{x}&=\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}}\left(\lambda;x,t\right)\Phi,\quad\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda;x,t):=\mathrm{i}\left(\lambda\sigma_{3}+\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{bg}}\right),\\

which can be reduced to the system of linear constant-coefficient PDEs

\Psi_{x}=\mathrm{i}\mathbf{H}\Psi,\quad\Psi_{t}=\mathrm{i}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}^{2}+\lambda\mathbf{H}-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)\mathbb{I}_{n+1}\right]\Psi

(47)

via the gauge transformation

\Phi=\mathbf{G}\Psi
with

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\\
\mathbf{a}&-\lambda\mathbb{I}_{n}+\mathbf{b}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{b}=\mathrm{diag}\left(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n}\right).

To study vector rational RW solutions of system (
3
) we require that the coefficient matrix
\mathbf{H}
in system (
47
) must admit the multiple eigenvalues.
Here suppose that
\mathbf{H}
has an
(n+1)
-multiple eigenvalue at
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
. In the following, we will firstly derive the existence condition of the
(n+1)
-multiple eigenvalue.
For convenience, we define
\mathbf{H}_{0}:=\mathbf{H}\left|\right._{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}
and a polynomial of degree
(n+1)
as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}D(z):=&\mathrm{det}\left[\left(z-\lambda_{0}\right)\mathbb{I}_{n+1}-\mathbf{H}_{0}\right]\\
=&\displaystyle\left(z-2\lambda_{0}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(z+b_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[a_{j}^{2}\prod_{k\neq j}\left(z+b_{k}\right)\right],\end{array}

where
z-\lambda_{0}
is regarded as the eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}_{0}
.

Then one has the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Given the real-valued amplitudes
a_{j}
’s (
a_{j}\not=0
) and wave-numbers
b_{j}
’s in the PW background (
45
), the polynomial
D(z)
has an (n+1)-multiple root

if and only if
b_{k}\not=b_{j}\,(k\not=j)
and

\displaystyle a_{j}^{2}=-\frac{\left(b_{j}+z_{0}\right)^{n+1}}{\prod_{k\neq j}\left(b_{j}-b_{k}\right)},\quad z_{0}=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(2\lambda_{0}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}\right).

(51)

Proof.

(Necessity
\Rightarrow
) Since
z=z_{0}
is the
(n+1)
-multiple root of
D(z)
, that is,

\displaystyle D(z)=(z-z_{0})^{n+1}.

(52)

Matching the
\mathcal{O}\left(z^{n}\right)
term in Eqs. (
3.1
) and (
52
) makes sure that the second expression of Eq. (
51
) holds.
One can perform the reduction to absurdity to verify that
b_{j}^{\prime}s
are mutually different. Without loss of generality, one can suppose
b_{1}=b_{2}=\cdots=b_{m}
,
m\geq 2
and
b_{1},b_{m+1},b_{m+2},\cdots,b_{n}
are mutually different.
In this case, one obtains

D(z)=p(z)\left(z+b_{1}\right)^{m-1}
,
where

\displaystyle p(z)=\left(z-2\lambda_{0}\right)\prod_{j=m}^{n}\left(z+b_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}^{2}\prod_{k=m+1}^{n}\left(z+b_{k}\right)-\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=m\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(z+b_{k}\right).

Since

has an
(n+1)
-multiple root, then it follows that

p(z)=\left(z+b_{1}\right)^{n-m+2}
.
Then one deduces

, which is a contradiction. At last, one deduces

.

(Sufficiency
\Leftarrow
) Define the polynomial

\displaystyle\widehat{p}(z):=\left(z-2\lambda_{0}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(z+b_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}\prod_{k\neq j}\left(z+b_{k}\right)-\left(z-z_{0}\right)^{n+1}.

One can obtain that the degree of
\widehat{p}(z)
is less than
n
and
\widehat{p}(-b_{j})=0
. Since
b_{j}^{\prime}s
are mutually different, it follows that

. Thus the Lemma follows.
∎

In Lemma

, one establishes the sufficient and necessary condition of the
(n+1)
-multiple root for

. Next, one will seek the suitable and mutually different real

and non-zero real

.

For the case

and non-zero real

-multiple eigenvalue for the case.

Without loss of generality, one can assume

.

1)

As

\displaystyle a_{1}^{2}=-\frac{\left(b_{1}+z_{0}\right)^{n+1}}{\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(b_{1}-b_{k}\right)}<0,

which is a contradiction.

2)

As

•

As

\displaystyle a_{1}^{2}=-\frac{\left(b_{1}+z_{0}\right)^{n+1}}{\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(b_{1}-b_{k}\right)}<0,

•

As

\displaystyle a_{2}^{2}=-\frac{\left(b_{2}+\chi_{0}\right)^{n+1}}{\prod_{k\neq 2}\left(b_{2}-b_{k}\right)}<0,

which is a contradiction.

∎

Lemma 6
.

As
\Im\left(\lambda_{0}\right)\neq 0
, the parameters

and

To find the suitable

(n+1)

By matching the term

\mathbf{T}_{p,t}+\mathbf{T}_{p}\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{bg}}=\mathbf{V}^{[1]}\mathbf{T}_{p}
, one yields

. Then one derives that

\|\mathbf{q}^{[1]}\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\mathrm{i}\left(\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{x}\right)_{1,1}
and
|q_{j}^{[1]}|^{2}=\left|a_{j}\right|^{2}+2\mathrm{i}\left(\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{x}\right)_{j+1,j+1}
. Together with
\left(\mathbf{\varPhi}_{p}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\varPhi}_{p}\right)=-\left(n+1\right)\zeta\mathbf{\varPhi}_{p}^{\dagger}\sigma_{3}\mathbf{\varPhi}_{p}
, then the formula (
57
) follows. This completes the proof.

3.2
{\cal PT}
-symmetric vector rational RW solutions

Proposition 1
.

The vector rational RW
\mathbf{q}^{[1]}(x,t)
given by Eq. (
56
) is of the parity-time-reversal (
{\cal PT}
) symmetric structure:

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}^{[1]}(x,t)={\cal PT}\mathbf{q}^{[1]}(x,t)={\cal P}\mathbf{q}^{[1]}(x,-t)^{*},

(63)

if
\Re(\lambda_{0})=0
and
\bm{\beta}=\mathbf{J}\bm{\beta}^{*}
, where the matrix
\mathbf{J}
and the parity operator
{\cal P}
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times n}\vskip 7.22743pt\\
\mathbf{0}_{n\times 1}&-\mathcal{P}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\cal P}=\begin{pmatrix}&&1\\
&\iddots&\\
1&&\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n},\quad{\cal P}^{2}=\mathbb{I}_{n},

and the time-reversal operator
{\cal T}
is:
t\to-t,\,\ {\rm i}\to-{\rm i}
.

Proof.

As
\Re(\lambda_{0})=0
, it is easy to know that the plane-wave solution (
53
) is parity-time-reversal symmetric, i.e.
\mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{bg}}(x,t)={\cal P}\mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{bg}}(x,-t)^{*}
,
from which one derives the symmetries of
\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{U}(\lambda;x,t)\big|_{{\bf Q}={\bf Q}^{\mathrm{bg}}}
and
\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{V}(\lambda;x,t)\big|_{{\bf Q}={\bf Q}^{\mathrm{bg}}}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{J}\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}}(-\lambda^{*};x,-t)^{*}\mathbf{J},\quad\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda;x,t)=-\mathbf{J}\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{bg}}(-\lambda^{*};x,-t)^{*}\mathbf{J}.

Since
\Phi_{p}(\lambda_{0};x,t)
in Eq. (
60
) is the fundamental vector solution of

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}=\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda_{0};x,t)\Phi,\quad\Phi_{t}=\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda_{0};x,t)\Phi

(64)

thus so is
\mathbf{J}\Phi_{p}(-\lambda_{0}^{*};x,-t)^{*}
.
From
\bm{\beta}=\mathbf{J}\bm{\beta}^{*}
, one obtains
\Phi_{p}(\lambda_{0};0,0)=\mathbf{J}\Phi_{p}(-\lambda_{0}^{*};0,0)^{*}
. It follows from the uniqueness of the solution of Eq. (
64
) that

\Phi_{p}(\lambda_{0};x,t)=\mathbf{J}\Phi_{p}(-\lambda_{0}^{*};x,-t)^{*}
.
In particular, one has
\Phi_{p}(\lambda_{0};x,t)=\mathbf{J}\Phi_{p}(\lambda_{0};x,-t)^{*}
for
\Re(\lambda_{0})=0
. Then the Darboux matrix
\mathbf{T}_{p}
given by Eq. (
61
) has the symmetry

\mathbf{T}_{p}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{J}\mathbf{T}_{p}(-\lambda^{*};x,-t)^{*}\mathbf{J}
.
By the relation between the initial
\left(\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}},\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{bg}}\right)
and transformed
\left(\mathbf{U}^{[1]},\mathbf{V}^{[1]}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\left(\mathbf{T}_{p,x}(\lambda;x,t)+\mathbf{T}_{p}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda;x,t)\right)\mathbf{T}_{p}(\lambda;x,t)^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\left(\mathbf{T}_{p,t}(\lambda;x,t)+\mathbf{T}_{p}(\lambda;x,t)\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{bg}}(\lambda;x,t)\right)\mathbf{T}_{p}(\lambda;x,t)^{-1},

then one deduces

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=\mathbf{J}\mathbf{U}^{[1]}(-\lambda^{*};x,-t)^{*}\mathbf{J},\quad\mathbf{V}^{[1]}(\lambda;x,t)=-\mathbf{J}\mathbf{V}^{[1]}(-\lambda^{*};x,-t)^{*}\mathbf{J},

which imply that Eq. (
63
) holds. Thus we complete the proof.
∎

3.3

The explicit formula (

Proposition 2
.

Let the matrix
\mathbf{S}=\left(\bm{\xi}_{0},\bm{\xi}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\xi}_{n}\right)
and
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})=\mathbf{S}^{-1}\bm{\beta}

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}_{j}=\left(0,\frac{a_{1}}{\left(\mathrm{i}\zeta+b_{1}\right)^{j+1}},\frac{a_{2}}{\left(\mathrm{i}\zeta+b_{2}\right)^{j+1}},\cdots,\frac{a_{n}}{\left(\mathrm{i}\zeta+b_{n}\right)^{j+1}}\right)^{\mathrm{T}},\quad j=1,2,\cdots,n.

(65)

Then the complete classification of the vector rational RW solution (
56
) can be determined by the degree of the polynomial
\mathbf{A}\bm{\beta}
, where
\mathbf{A}\bm{\beta}
is the polynomial of degree
j
with respect to the variables
x
and

as

,
\alpha_{j+1}=\cdots=\alpha_{n}=0
(

Proof.

The vector parameter
\bm{\alpha}
is well-defined due to

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}\,\mathbf{S}=\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta}\right)^{\frac{n\left(n+1\right)}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sin\omega_{j}\right)^{j}\neq 0.

Since
\mathrm{i}\zeta
is the
(n+1)
-multiple root of
D(z)=0
, then one can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\zeta-2\lambda_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{a_{j}^{2}}{\mathrm{i}\zeta+b_{j}}=0,\quad 1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{a_{j}^{2}}{\left(\mathrm{i}\zeta+b_{j}\right)^{2}}=0,\quad\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{a_{j}^{2}}{\left(\mathrm{i}\zeta+b_{j}\right)^{k+1}}=0,\quad 2\leq k\leq n,

which derives that

\mathbf{B}^{j}\bm{\xi}_{j}\neq\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{B}^{j+1}\bm{\xi}_{j}=\mathbf{0}\,(1\leq j\leq n)
.
Then we complete the proof.

3.5
Vector rational RWs with ultrahigh amplitudes

In this subsection, we consider the vector rational RWs with the maximal amplitude. From the formula of the general first-order vector rogue waves (
56
), the parameter
\bm{\beta}
is related to the amplitude. Next, we will seek the suitable
\bm{\beta}
corresponding to the rogue wave with the maximal amplitude. We will consider two kinds of cases: the maximal average amplitude
and the maximal amplitude of a certain component
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{GA}[1]:=\max_{\bm{\beta},x,t}\frac{\|\mathbf{q}^{[1]}\|_{1}}{\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{1}},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{j}[1]:=\max_{\bm{\beta},x,t}\left|q_{j}^{[1]}/a_{j}\right|.

(93)

Proposition 3
.

Given the formula of the vector RW solutions (
56
),
\mathcal{GA}[1]
can be attained at
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}
and
(x,t)=(0,0)
, and
\mathcal{A}_{j}[1]
can be attained at
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}_{j}
and
(x,t)=(0,0)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{GA}[1]

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\left(n+1\right)\sqrt{n}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\csc{\omega_{j}}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\ln n}\right),\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(94)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{j}[1]

\displaystyle=1+(n+1)\sin\omega_{j}

(95)

where
\bm{\eta}=\left(\sqrt{n},\mathrm{i},\mathrm{i},\cdots,\mathrm{i}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}
,
\bm{\eta}_{j}=\left(1,0,\cdots,0,\mathrm{i},0,\cdots,0\right)^{\mathrm{T}}
, and the non-zero
\mathrm{i}
is the
j+1
-th entry of
\bm{\eta}_{j}
.

Proof.

Given the
(n+1)
-dimensional vectors
\bm{\gamma}=\left(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\right)
and
\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}=\left(\left|\gamma_{0}\right|,\left|\gamma_{1}\right|\mathrm{i},\cdots,\left|\gamma_{n}\right|\mathrm{i}\right)
, one has the following inequality

\displaystyle\left|a_{j}-\frac{2\mathrm{i}\left(n+1\right)\zeta\gamma_{j}\gamma_{0}^{*}}{\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\bm{\gamma}}\right|\leq\left|a_{j}-\frac{2\mathrm{i}\left(n+1\right)\zeta\left(\left|\gamma_{j}\right|\mathrm{i}\right)\left|\gamma_{0}\right|^{*}}{\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}^{\dagger}\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}}\right|,

from which one can claim that the maximal value of
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|a_{j}-2\mathrm{i}\left(n+1\right)\zeta\gamma_{j}\gamma_{0}^{*}/(\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\bm{\gamma})\right|
can be reached at the subset
\mathbb{C}_{1}:=\left\{\bm{\gamma}=\left(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\big|\,\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\gamma_{j}\in\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R},\gamma_{0}\geq 0,\left(\gamma_{j}\right)_{\mathrm{I}}\geq 0,1\leq j\leq n\right\}
.

Given a vector
\mathbf{c}=\left(c_{0},c_{1}\mathrm{i},c_{2}\mathrm{i},\cdots,c_{n}\mathrm{i}\right)\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
, where
c_{j}\geq 0\,(0\leq j\leq n)
, one can derive

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|a_{j}+\frac{2\left(n+1\right)\zeta c_{j}c_{0}}{\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}\mathbf{c}}\right|=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\csc\varphi_{j}+\frac{2\left(n+1\right)c_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}}{\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}^{2}}\right)\left|\zeta\right|.

(96)

The right-hand side of Eq. (
96
) is a multivariate function with respect to variables
c_{0},c_{1},\cdots,c_{n}
, and its maximal value is achieved at
c_{0}=c_{1}\sqrt{n}
,
c_{j}=c_{1}\,(1\leq j\leq n)
. Then the greatest value can be obtained at
\bm{\eta}
. Note that
\mathbf{A}
in Eq. (
56
) is identity matrix at
(x,t)=(0,0)
, thus one confirms
\mathcal{GA}[1]
is attained at
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}
,
(x,t)=(0,0)
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{GA}[1]=\frac{\|\mathbf{q}^{[1]}(0,0)\|_{1}}{\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{1}}\bigg|_{\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}}=1+\frac{\left(n+1\right)\sqrt{n}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\csc{\omega_{j}}}.

By the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\ln\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\leq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\csc\omega_{j}}{n+1}\leq 1+\ln\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right),

one derives

\mathcal{GA}[1]=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{n}/\ln n\right),\,n\to\infty
.

The case of
\mathcal{A}_{j}[1]
can be verified by the estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{j}[1]=\left|q_{j}^{[1]}(0,0)/a_{j}\right|_{\bm{\beta}=\mathbf{c}}=1+\frac{2(n+1)c_{0}c_{j}\sin\omega_{j}}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{2(n+1)c_{0}c_{j}\sin\omega_{j}}{c_{0}^{2}+c_{j}^{2}}\leq 1+(n+1)\sin\omega_{j},

where
\mathbf{c}=\left(c_{0},c_{1}\mathrm{i},\cdots,c_{n}\mathrm{i}\right)\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
. Then we complete the proof.
∎

4.1
The inverse function method and an explicit formula via two determinants

In this subsection, we present a novel technique of the inverse function to find an explicit formula of the
N
-th-order vector rational RW solutions of the
n
-NLS equation (
3
) via the multi-fold Darboux transformation, where
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. By the idea of the generalized Darboux transformation, we need to make the perturbation of the spectral parameter
\lambda_{0}
.

Let
\lambda=\lambda_{0}\left(1+\epsilon^{n+1}\right)
with
\epsilon
being a small perturbation parameter. Denote the eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathbf{H}+\lambda\mathbb{I}_{n+1}
by
\mu_{0}:=\mu_{0}(\epsilon),\ldots,\mu_{n}:=\mu_{n}(\epsilon)
, which satisfy the characteristic equation
|(\mu_{j}(\epsilon)-\lambda)\mathbb{I}_{n+1}-\mathbf{H}|=0
.
The main difficulty of the application of the generalized Darboux transformation to multi-component model is that
\mu_{j}(\epsilon)
’s can not be explicitly represented. By recalling the idea of the generalized Darboux transformation, we find that these explicit representations are in fact not necessary. We just need to solve the values
\mu_{j}
,
\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}/\mathrm{d}\epsilon
,
\mathrm{d}^{2}\mu_{j}/\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}
,
\cdots
at
\epsilon=0
explicitly. In next theorem, the explicit formula of the
N
-th order rogue wave solutions is presented.

Theorem 3
.

The explicit formula of the
N
-th order vector rational RW solutions for the
n
-NLS system (
3
) is derived as

\displaystyle q_{j}^{[N]}(x,t)=q_{j}^{\mathrm{bg}}+2\frac{\mathrm{det}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{M}&\mathbf{Y}_{1}^{\dagger}\vskip 3.61371pt\\
\mathbf{Y}_{j+1}&0\end{pmatrix}}{\mathrm{det}\,\mathbf{M}},\quad j=1,2,\cdots n

(99)

with the intensity of the vector potential
\mathbf{q}^{[N]}
being

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}^{[N]}\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\ln(\mathrm{det}\,\mathbf{M}),

(100)

where
\mathbf{Y}_{j}
is the
j
-th rows of the matrix
\mathbf{Y}=\left(\widetilde{\Phi}_{0},\widetilde{\Phi}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{\Phi}_{N-1}\right)
with

\widetilde{\Phi}_{k}=\sum_{s=0}^{k}\widehat{\Phi}_{s}\bm{\gamma}_{k-s}\bm{\beta}\,(k=0,1,\ldots,N-1)
,
\bm{\gamma}_{0},\bm{\gamma}_{1}\cdots,\bm{\gamma}_{N-1},\bm{\beta}
being
(n+1)
-dimensional free constant column vectors, and
\widehat{\Phi}_{s}
are
(n+1)
-dimensional column vector as

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{s}=\left(\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\left(sn+s\right)}\left(0\right)}{\left(sn+s\right)!}\mathbf{R}_{0},\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\left(sn+s+1\right)}\left(0\right)}{\left(sn+s+1\right)!}\mathbf{R}_{1},\cdots,\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\left(sn+s+n\right)}\left(0\right)}{\left(sn+s+n\right)!}\mathbf{R}_{n}\right),

(101)

with
\mathbf{R}_{s}=\left(g_{0}^{s}\left(z_{0}\right),g_{1}^{s}\left(z_{0}\right),\cdots,g_{n}^{s}\left(z_{0}\right)\right)^{\dagger},\,(s=0,1,\ldots,n)

and

\displaystyle g_{k}:z\mapsto\left|f(z)\right|^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left[\mathrm{arg}\left(f(z)\right)+2k\pi\right]/(n+1)},\,\,f(z)=2\lambda_{0}\prod_{s=1}^{n}\left(z+b_{s}\right),\,\,\mathrm{arg}\left(f(z)\right)\in\left(-\pi,\pi\right],\,\,k=0,\ldots,n,

Conclusions and discussions

In conclusion, we have explicitly found the existence condition of
\left(n+1\right)
-multiple eigenvalues for
n
-component nonlinear Schödinger equation, which play a fundamental role in the study of using the Darboux transformation to construct vector rational rogue waves.
Base on the proper plan-wave solution
\mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{bg}}
for the
\left(n+1\right)
-multiple eigenvalues,
we first presented the explicit formulae of both first-order and high-order vector rational RW solutions. For the first-order vector rational
RW solutions, a complete classification is proposed based on the degree
\ell
of
\mathbf{A}\bm{\beta}
. When
\ell=1
, we classify the first-order vector RWs into three types of fundamental RWs (bright, dark, and four-pated RWs). However, when
\ell\geq 2
, the so-called
new governing polynomials

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(z)
’s are introduced to study the asymptotic behaviors of first-order vector RWs as some parameters tend to infinity. The leading term of asymptotic formula is the linear superposition of some fundamental RWs, whose central location is related to the governing polynomial
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(z)
, and can be deduced. Two kinds of amplitude constants
\mathcal{GA}[1],\mathcal{A}_{j}[1]
are also studied and the first-order vector rogue wave solutions with
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}
and
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}_{j}
have the properties:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{GA}[1]=1+\dfrac{\left(n+1\right)\sqrt{n}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\csc{\omega_{j}}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{\ln n}\right),\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\to\infty,\\
\mathcal{A}_{j}[1]=1+(n+1)\sin\omega_{j}.\end{array}

Be recalling the idea of the generalized Darboux transformation, we find the explicit zeros of the polynomial of degree
(n+1)
with the parameter
\epsilon
is not necessary and one just need to yield the coefficients of the Taylor expansion at
\epsilon=0
. By performing the technique of inverse function, we present the explicit formula of high-order vector rogue wave solutions. As the direct application, two kinds of constants
\mathcal{GA}[N],\mathcal{A}_{j}[N]
related to the amplitude, are also studied. The
N
-th-order rogue wave solution with
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}
and
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\eta}_{j}
has the properties:

\displaystyle\mathcal{GA}[N]=1+\frac{N\left(n+1\right)\sqrt{n}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\csc{\omega_{j}}},\quad\mathcal{A}_{j}[N]=1+N\left(n+1\right)\sin\omega_{j}.

(137)

The condition of the multiple root plays the fundamental and essential role of the existence of the rogue wave solutions. In this paper, we present a religious rogue wave theory for the
n
-component NLS system with an
(n+1)
-multiple root. Recalling the Lemma
4
, the condition of
(n+1)
-multiple root is determined based on
n
mutually different
b_{j}^{\prime}s
. In the following we give the general existence condition of the multiple root according to different cases of the mutuality of
b_{j}^{\prime}s
. Without loss of generality, suppose
b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m}
are mutually different and
b_{m+1},b_{m+2},\cdots,b_{n}\in\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m}\}
, where
1\leq m\leq n
. We define
m
mutually exclusive index sets
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\cdots,\Lambda_{m}
such that
b_{i}=b_{j}
for
i\in\Lambda_{j}
. As the plane wave solution
\mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{bg}}
in Eq. (
45
) is chosen as

\displaystyle b_{j}=\frac{2\Im\left(\lambda_{0}\right)}{m+1}\cot\frac{j\pi}{m+1}-2\Re\left(\lambda_{0}\right),\quad\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}a_{i}^{2}=\left(\frac{2\Im\left(\lambda_{0}\right)}{m+1}\csc\frac{j\pi}{m+1}\right)^{2},\quad j=1,2,\cdots,m,

(138)

the characteristic polynomial
D(z)
in Eq. (
3.1
) has the following form of multiple root

\displaystyle D\left(x\right)=\left(x-z_{0}\right)^{m+1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(x+b_{j}\right)^{\left|\Lambda_{j}\right|-1},\quad z_{0}=\frac{2\mathrm{i}\Im\left(\lambda_{0}\right)}{m+1}+2\Re\left(\lambda_{0}\right),

(139)

where
\left|\Lambda_{j}\right|
denotes the amount of the entries in the index set
\Lambda_{j}
. With the general existence condition and the process of the paper, one can study the semi-rational solutions.

The idea used in this paper can also be extended to other
n
-component integrable nonlinear wave systems such as the higher-order n-component NLS system (e.g., n-component Hirota system, n-component fifth-oder NLS system), n-component mKdV system, n-component complex mKdV system, n-component KP system, and etc. These issues will be given in another literature.

The recent studies on the RW theory in the integrable system shows that the RW solutions almost possess the uniform structure. For the fixed order RW solutions, one of them will attain the maximum peak, the others can be decomposed into the asymptotic linear superposition of lower order RWs. Simultaneously, there exist infinite many hierarchy of RW solutions similar as the soliton hierarchy. And the infinite order RW are constructed recently from the Riemann-Hilbert representation of Darboux transformation
[
75
]
. The RW solutions can not be involved in the traditional inverse scattering transform since the RW solutions will have the same scattering data as the background solution or the so-called spectral singularity
[
78
]
. The recent proposed robust inverse scattering transform can be used to deal with the spectral singularity of RWs on the non-vanishing background
[
78
]
.

On the other hand, as for the non-integrable equation, up to now there is no systematic construction on the RW solution for the non-integrable model with MI under the non-vanishing background except for the non-integrable defocusing NLS equation with time-dependent potential possessing the RW solution
[
82
]
. The existence or non-existence of RWs on the non-integrable systems with MI is still a puzzle up to now
[
83
]
which is similar as the existence or nonexistence of two-soliton solution in the non-integrable KdV equation
[
84
,
85
]
. It is widely believed that the mechanism of RWs is MI. The RWs were grown in the background of MI. Thus the orbital stability theory will not adapt for the studies of RWs
[
86
]
. Thus how to develop a theory to study the stabilities of RWs is also open for us. All in all, further studies on the RWs will not only impulse the development of integrable systems but also bring the new topic to the theoretical analysis and numerical studies of non-integrable dispersive equations in the fields of applied mathematics and mathematical physics.

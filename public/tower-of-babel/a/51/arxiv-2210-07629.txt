Abstract
. We present some properties of positive closed currents of type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) on compact non-kählerian surfaces related to our previous study of these objects started in [ CT20 ] .



1.
Introduction

The first systematic study of compact non-kählerian complex surfaces was done by Kodaira as part of his comprehensive work on the classification of compact complex surfaces in the sixties. This was followed by work of M. Inoue and Ma. Kato in the seventies who constructed examples of new classes of non-kählerian surfaces. Together with the elliptic surfaces and the Hopf surfaces these are the only classes of compact non-kählerian surfaces known to this day. In fact the Global Spherical Shell Conjecture
claims that any non-kählerian compact complex surface should belong to one of these classes,
[
Nak84
]
, see also
[
Tel10
]
. A positive answer to this conjecture would mean finishing the last step lacking for a complete classification of all compact complex surfaces up to bimeromorphic equivalence.

One approach to the study of compact non-kählerian surfaces has been through the algebraic objects they may admit, such as algebraic curves. It is a problem though to show that such objects exist at all on compact non-kählerian surfaces. Instead, we know by a result of Harvey and Lawson that the lack of Kähler metrics is equivalent to the existence of positive currents which are
(1,1)
-components of a boundary,
[
HL83
]
.
This result was further refined in
[
Lam99
]
where it is shown that any compact non-kählerian surface admits non-trivial positive
d
-exact currents of type
(1,1)
. In
[
HL83
]
the structure of these currents was described for elliptic, Hopf and Inoue surfaces.
This description was extended to the other class of known surfaces, that of Kato surfaces, in
[
Tom08
]
. Inspired by these structural results, by phenomena appearing in the study of the Kähler rank on surfaces,
[
CT13
]
, as well as by Brunella’s postumuous paper
[
Bru14
]
, we introduce in
[
CT20
]
an invariant on a class of
d
-exact positive currents which helps us to distinguish two classes among the known non-kählerian surfaces, namely parabolic and hyperbolic surfaces, see Section
2
.
Hoping that this could lead to a fruitful approach towards the Global Spherical Shell Conjecture, we formulate in
[
CT20
]
three conjectures related to the presence of certain
d
-exact positive currents on the surface under study. Under the perspective of these conjectures we analyse in the present paper further properties of exact positive
(1,1)
-currents on compact non-kählerian surfaces.
More precisely, we discuss the following:

(1)

If
T
is an exact positive
(1,1)
-current on a compact non-kählerian surface
X
, we will examine the set
L^{2}_{-1}(T|X)
of points
x\in X
around which
T
is locally in
L^{2}_{-1}
. We show that on parabolic surfaces
L^{2}_{-1}(T|X)
contains the complement of the union of all divisors of
X
. We then refine our previous conjecture
2.5
.

(2)

We establish the existence of a distingushed current related to the Green function on hyperbolic surfaces.
We relate this to our conjecture
2.3
.

(3)

Related to the conjecture
2.4
we show that any exact positive
(1,1)
-current
T\in L^{2}_{-1}(X)
with
I(T)\neq 0
induces a singular Gauduchon current on
X
of a special form, see Section
2
for notations and terminology.

2.
Background

We refer the reader to the monograph
[
BHPVdV04
]
for general facts on compact complex surfaces, to the survey paper
[
Nak84
]
on more specific facts on non-kählerian compact complex surfaces and to our paper
[
CT20
]
for more recent results on positive currents on such surfaces.

Let
X
be non-kählerian compact complex surface. The existence of non-trivial exact positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
implies that the natural map
j:H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1,1}_{A}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
from the Bott-Chern cohomology of
X
in degree
(1,1)
to the corresponding Aeppli cohomology has a non-trivial kernel. It can be moreover shown that this kernel is one-dimensional, cf.
[
CT20
, Appendix]
. We denote by
\tau
a smooth representative of a class of a non-trivial exact positive
(1,1)
-current in the Bott-Chern cohomology group
H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Its class may be thought of as a positive generator of
\mbox{Ker}(H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1,1}_{A}(X,{\mathbb{R}}))
.

We denote by
L^{2}_{-1}(X)
the space of
(1,1)
-currents on
X
with coefficients in
L^{2}_{-1}
.
The degeneration of the Frölicher spectral sequence at the
E_{1}
-level for compact complex surfaces implies that any exact
(1,1)
current
T
on
X
admits a ”primitive current”, that is a bidegree
(0,1)
-current
S

such that
T=\partial S
and
\bar{\partial}S=0
. It is not difficult to see that in this case we also have
\bar{\partial}S=0
, hence
T=\mbox{d}S
.

In
[
CT20
]
we show that if
T
is positive of type
(1,1)
, in
L^{2}_{-1}(X)
,
d
-exact and if
S
is a primitive current of
T
as above, then

S
is in
L^{2}(X)
and
i\bar{S}\wedge S
is
i\partial\bar{\partial}
-closed
hence defines
a linear form

Ker(H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1,1}_{A}(X,{\mathbb{R}}))\to\mathbb{R},\ \ \alpha\mapsto\int_{X}\alpha\wedge i\bar{S}\wedge S.

This linear form only depends on
T
and not on the chosen primitive current
S
. We will denote it by
I(T)
.
When
T
is moreover smooth the vanishing or non-vanishing of the form
I(T)
served in
[
CT13
]
to distinguish between two fundamentally different dynamical behaviours of the induced holomorphic foliation on
X
. This distinction is investigated also for
T
singular in the case when
X
is a known non-kählerian compact complex surface and fits to the following clssification proposed in
[
CT20
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
CT20
, Definition 3.5]
)
We say that a non-kählerian compact complex surface
X
is parabolic
if its minimal model belongs to one of the classes: Hopf surfaces, Enoki surfaces, non-kählerian elliptic surfaces.
We say that
X
is hyperbolic if its minimal model is either an Inoue surface, an Inoue-Hirzebruch surface or an intermediate Kato surface.

We may express the results in
[
CT20
]
concerning the vanishing of the forms

as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a non-kählerian compact complex surface.

(1)

If
X
is hyperbolic then all its exact positive
(1,1)
-currents

are in
L^{2}_{-1}(X)
and have
I(T)=0
.

(2)

If

is parabolic then

admits exact positive
(1,1)
-currents

not in
L^{2}_{-1}(X)
. Morever all its exact positive
(1,1)
-currents

in

We recall the following conjectures from
[

]
which together give a partial converse to Proposition

Conjecture 2.3
.

If

is a non-kählerian surface all of whose exact positive
(1,1)
-currents

Conjecture 2.4

If

(1,1)
-currents

Conjecture 2.5

If

is a non-kählerian surface admitting an exact positive
(1,1)
-current not in

3.
The
L^{2}_{-1}
-locus

In this section we state a refinement of Conjecture

and show that it is verified for all parabolic surfaces.

Definition 3.1
.

For a
(1,1)
-current

-locus,

Conjecture 3.2
.

If
X
is a non-kählerian surface admitting an exact positive
(1,1)
-current

and if

X
some exact positive
(1,1)
-current with non-vanishing Lelong number at

Note that by
[
CT20
, Proposition 3.6]
all exact positive
(1,1)
-currents on a hyperbolic surface

As remarked in
[

using a result of

Remark 3.3
.

Conjecture

may be reformulated as follows.
“If

(1,1)
current on a non-kählerian surface

This will be a consequence of the following Propositions.

Proposition 3.4
.

All non-trivial

(1,1)
-currents on an Enoki surface

are positive multiples of the current of integration along the cycle of rational curves on
X
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

be a non-kählerian elliptic surface and

be a fiber of its elliptic fibration. Then there exists some exact positive
(1,1)
-current

on

and with vanishing Lelong numbers at all points of

. On the other hand the current of integration along

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
X
be a non-elliptic Hopf surface.

(1)

If
X
is of class
0
, then all its non-trivial
exact positive
(1,1)
-currents are positive multiples of the current of integration along the unique elliptic curve of

(2)

If
X
is of class
1
and

(1,1)
-current

Remark 3.7
.

The above Propositions immediately imply that Conjecture
3.2
holds for minimal parabolic surfaces. Its extension to the non-minimal case needs a bit of care since the condition
T\in L^{2}_{-1}(X)
does not behave well under blow-up. Namely, if
p:Y\to{\mathbb{B}}
is the blow-up of the origin in the unit ball

in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and if
T=i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
is a closed
positive current on the unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
such that
\partial\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B})
, then it is not true in general that
\partial p^{*}\varphi\in L^{2}(Y)

See
[

]

However on parabolic surfaces the only interesting case where blowing-up may occur in a point away from null-homologuous divisors is the case of Hopf surfaces of class

(1,1)
-currents

These will be therefore locally in

A

(1)

g(z_{1},z_{2})=(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\lambda z_{2}^{m},\alpha_{2}z_{2}),

where

(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}^{m})\lambda=0,\ 0<|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha_{2}|<1.

A primary Hopf surface

and
\alpha_{1}^{k_{1}}=\alpha_{2}^{k_{2}}

,

.

\Omega_{1}
differs from
\tilde{\Omega}
by a factor which is a function of
\rho
, it will be enough to show that

is of the form
\pi^{*}(f)
for some non-negative generalized function on
]0,1[
.
Let
\psi_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon>0
be regularizing kernels on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and let
h_{\epsilon}
be the regularization of

by means of
\psi_{\epsilon}
, i.e.

h_{\epsilon}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\psi_{\epsilon}(x)h(y-x)\mbox{d}x.

It is clear that

is invariant with respect to the translation group generated by
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\phi
mentioned above and hence descends to

. Moreover the smooth forms

are closed since the differential operator

has constant coefficients with respect to the chosen coordinate functions and thus commutes with regularization. It follows that

defined by

. Since these leaves are dense in the fibers of

\displaystyle{\frac{\partial h_{\epsilon}}{\partial\theta_{j}}=0}

. Making

\displaystyle{\frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta_{j}}=0}

\pi^{*}(f)
for some non-negative generalized function

on
]0,1[
, cf.
[
Sch66
, IV.5.Exemple 1]
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.6
(2).

Le
T
be an exact positive
(1,1)
-current on a non-elliptic Hopf surface

of class
1

to

has coefficients in

.
For this we use the characterization of such residual currents on

given by Lemma

. To simplify computations we make use again of our previous notations
\displaystyle{\zeta=\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}^{r}}}
,

\Omega_{1}:=\frac{i\eta\wedge\bar{\eta}}{|z_{2}|^{2(r+1)}}=\mbox{d}u\wedge\mbox{d}\alpha=i\mbox{d}\zeta\wedge\mbox{d}\bar{\zeta}.

Then for a non-negative generalized function

T^{\prime}=f(u)\Omega_{1}.

We claim that an
L^{\infty}_{1}

in the variable

exists such that

over

. Indeed, direct computations give

i\partial\bar{\partial}h_{1}=(h_{1}^{\prime}+uh_{1}^{\prime\prime})\Omega_{1}

so setting

Its general solution is of the form

where

\displaystyle{k_{0}(u):=\int_{u_{0}}^{u}f(t)\mbox{d}t}

3.6
(2).

We now construct an example of a current

on

as above which extends over the curve

as a closed positive current

no longer has

extends if and only if it is locally of finite mass at points of
E_{1}
if and only if the trace measure

with respect to any positive
(1,1)
-form on

\omega:=\frac{i\mbox{d}z_{1}\wedge\mbox{d}\bar{z_{1}}}{|z_{1}|^{2}}+\frac{i\mbox{d}z_{2}\wedge\mbox{d}\bar{z_{2}}}{|z_{1}|^{2r^{\prime}}}.

We get

\displaystyle{\omega=\left(\frac{\mbox{d}u}{u}+2r\mbox{d}\theta_{3}\right)\wedge\mbox{d}\theta_{1}+\frac{2\mbox{d}\theta_{3}\wedge\mbox{d}\theta_{2}}{u^{r^{\prime}}}}

\frac{\omega\wedge\Omega_{1}}{\omega^{2}}=\frac{\left(r^{2}+\frac{1}{u^{r^{\prime}}}\right)\mbox{d}\theta_{3}\wedge\mbox{d}\theta_{2}\wedge\mbox{d}u\wedge\mbox{d}\theta_{1}}{2\frac{\mbox{d}u}{u^{r^{\prime}+1}}\wedge\mbox{d}\theta_{1}\wedge\mbox{d}\theta_{3}\wedge\mbox{d}\theta_{2}}

.

If we write

4.
Special currents in the case
I=0

Throughout this section we assume that
X
is a non-Kähler compact complex surface on which all
d
-exact currents are in
L^{2}_{-1}(X)
and satisfy
I(T)=0
. Under these
assumptions, we show that
there exists a
distinguished
positive
d
-exact current on
X
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}
the cone of positive
d
-exact
(1,1)
currents on

X
. By the above assumptions, we have
{\mathcal{P}}\subset L^{2}_{-1}(X)
and
if
T=\partial S\in{\mathcal{P}}
, then
S
is a
\bar{\partial}
-closed
(0,1)
form
with
L^{2}
coefficients and from the condition
I(T)=0
it follows that
i\bar{S}\wedge S
is
d
-exact.

Set

{\mathcal{C}}=\{T\in{\mathcal{P}}|\int_{X}T\wedge g=\int_{X}i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g\}

where
g
is a fixed Gauduchon metric on
X
. Note that the set
{\mathcal{C}}
does not depend on
g
. In fact,
T
and
i\bar{S}\wedge S
, being
d
-exact, they are cohomologous to a multiple of
\tau
in the Bott-Chern group
H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
, hence there are
c,c^{\prime}>0
and
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
quasi-plurisubharmonic functions sunch that
T=c\tau+i\partial\bar{\partial}\lambda
and
i\bar{S}\wedge S=c^{\prime}\tau+i\partial\bar{\partial}\lambda^{\prime}
. Then
T
is in
{\mathcal{C}}
iff
c=c^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
C
a positive constant which depends on
g
such that

\int_{X}T\wedge g\leq C,\forall\,T\in{\mathcal{C}}

Proof.

Let
T=\partial S\in{\mathcal{P}}
. Then, from Hölder’s inequality, it follows that

\int_{X}T\wedge g=\int_{X}\partial S\wedge g=\int_{X}S\wedge\partial g\leq||S||_{L^{2}}||\partial g||_{L^{2}}=C_{1}\left(\int_{X}i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and if
T\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then
\int_{X}T\wedge g=\int i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g
, hence
\int_{X}T\wedge g\leq C_{1}^{2}

∎

Let
c=\sup\{\int_{X}T\wedge g|T\in{\mathcal{C}}\}<\infty
.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a unique
T\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
\int_{X}T\wedge g=c
.

Proof.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that

\int_{X}T_{n}\wedge g\to c
. Let

T_{n}=\partial S_{n}
, where

S_{n}\in L^{2}_{0,1}(X)
. We can assume that

T_{n}\to T
weakly (as distributions), and that

S_{n}\to S\in L^{2}_{0,1}(X)
weakly (in the Hilbert space

L^{2}_{0,1}(X)
). Then
\int_{X}T\wedge g=c
, and we want to show that

\int_{X}i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g=c
. Since
S_{n}\to S
weakly in
L^{2}_{0,1}(X)
, it follows that

\int_{X}i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g=||S||^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf||S_{n}||^{2}_{L^{2}}=c

by the weakly lower semicontinuity of the norm.
Set
\displaystyle{c^{\prime}=\int_{X}i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g}
, so that the above inequality implies
c^{\prime}\leq c
. If
c^{\prime}=0
, then
S=0
, hence
c=0
, contradiction. Therefore
c^{\prime}\neq 0
, and if we consider the current
\displaystyle{T^{\prime}=\frac{c}{c^{\prime}}T}
, then
T^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
so
\displaystyle{\int_{X}T^{\prime}\wedge g=\frac{c^{2}}{c^{\prime}}\leq c}
. This implies that
c^{\prime}\geq c
, hence
T\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

In order to prove the uniqueness, consider
T_{1}=\partial S_{1},T_{2}=\partial S_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that

\int_{X}T_{1}\wedge g=\int_{X}i\bar{S}_{1}\wedge S_{1}\wedge g=\int_{X}T_{2}\wedge g=\int_{X}i\bar{S}_{2}\wedge S_{2}\wedge g=c

and we want to prove that
S_{1}=S_{2}
.

Let
\displaystyle{S=\frac{1}{2}(S_{1}+S_{2})}
,
T=\partial S
and

\lambda=\frac{\int_{X}T\wedge g}{\int_{X}i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g}

such that
\lambda T\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Then
\int_{X}\lambda T\wedge g\leq c
and this implies

\int_{X}i\bar{S}\wedge S\wedge g\geq c=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}i\bar{S}_{1}\wedge S_{1}\wedge g+\frac{1}{2}\bar{S}_{2}\wedge S_{2}\wedge g

therefore

\int_{X}i(\bar{S}_{1}-\bar{S}_{2})\wedge(S_{1}-S_{2})\wedge g\leq 0

Hence the
L^{2}
norm of
S_{1}-S_{2}
is
0
, so
S_{1}=S_{2}
.
∎

We expect the current
T
in
{\mathcal{C}}
for which which
\int_{X}T\wedge g=c
to have some additional properties, for instance we think that it should satisfy the equation
T=i\bar{S}\wedge S
.

5.
Gauduchon currents on non-Kähler surfaces

In this section, we prove that, given a
d
-exact positive current
T
in
L_{2}^{-1}(X)
such that
I(T)\neq 0
, then there exists a certain pluriharmonic positive current that dominates some Gauduchon metric. One may call such a current a
Gauduchon current
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a non-Kähler surface,
g
a
fixed Gauduchon metric on
X
,
T^{\prime}
a nef positive pluriharmonic
current such that
\int_{X}T^{\prime}\wedge\tau>0
, and
T
a nef non-zero
d
-exact
positive current. Then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and

\chi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
a
real distribution such that
T^{\prime}+T+i\partial\bar{\partial}\chi\geq\varepsilon g

Proof.

First note that Lemme 1.4 in
[
Lam99
]
, which is stated for a smooth
(1,1)
-form
\theta
, is also valid in the case of a
(1,1)
-current. Hence, according to this Lemma, it is enough to prove that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\int_{X}(T^{\prime}+T)\wedge h\geq\varepsilon\int_{X}g\wedge h

for all Gauduchon metrics
h
on
X
. Suppose by contradiction, that there exists a sequence
(h_{n})_{n}
of Gauduchon metrics on
X
such that

\int_{X}(T^{\prime}+T)\wedge h_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}g\wedge h_{n},\forall n\geq 1

We can normalize the metrics
h_{n}
such that
\int_{X}h_{n}\wedge g=1
and we can assume, without loss of generality, that
(h_{n})_{n}
converges weakly to a
i\partial\bar{\partial}
-closed positive current
R
. It follows that
\int_{X}T^{\prime}\wedge h_{n}\to 0
and
\int_{X}T\wedge h_{n}\to 0
. Since
T
is nef and
d
-closed, it follows that it is
d
-exact (Proposition 2.4 in
[
CT20
]
), and hence there exists
c>0
and
\lambda
a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on
X
such that
T=c\tau+i\partial\bar{\partial}\lambda
. Therefore

c\int_{X}R\wedge\tau=\lim_{n}c\int_{X}h_{n}\wedge\tau=\lim_{n}\int_{X}h_{n}\wedge T=0

and this implies that
R
is
d
-exact (Proposition 2.4 in
[
CT20
]
) and there exists
c^{\prime}>0
and
\lambda^{\prime}
a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on
X
such that
R=c^{\prime}\tau+i\partial\bar{\partial}\lambda^{\prime}
. But then

\langle\{T^{\prime}\}_{A},\{R\}_{BC}\rangle=c^{\prime}\int_{X}T^{\prime}\wedge\tau=\lim_{n}\int_{X}T^{\prime}\wedge h_{n}=0

Here we used Lemma 5.3 in
[
CT20
]
. This is a contradiction with the condition
\int_{X}T^{\prime}\wedge\tau>0
. From Lamari’s result, it follows that there exists
\chi
a real distribution and
\varepsilon>0
such that
T^{\prime}+T+i\partial\bar{\partial}\chi\geq\varepsilon g
.
∎

Proposition
5.1
implies the following corollary which can be thought of as the singular version of formula
(2.1)
in
[
CT13
]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
X
be a non Kähler surface and
g
a Gauduchon metric on
X
. Let
T=\partial S
be a
d
-exact positive current such that
T\in L^{2}_{-1}(X)
,
I(T)\neq 0
and
T
is nef. Then there exists
\chi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
a real distribution and
\varepsilon>0
such that
T+i\bar{S}\wedge S+i\partial\bar{\partial}\chi\geq\varepsilon g

Proof.

Indeed, the current
i\bar{S}\wedge S
is
i\partial\bar{\partial}
-closed and nef (Proposition 2.2 in
[
CT20
]
), so we can use Proposition
5.1
to prove the existence of
\chi
and
\varepsilon
.

∎

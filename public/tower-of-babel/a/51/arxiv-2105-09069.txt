Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the Dirichlet problem for a class of Hessian quotient equations on Riemannian manifolds. Under the assumption of an admissible subsolution, we solve the existence and the uniquness for the Dirichlet problem in a domain without any geometric restrictions on the boundary, based on the a priori estimates for the solutions to the Hessian quotient type equations.

Primary 35J15; Secondary 35B45.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with smooth boundary
\partial M
and
\overline{M}:=M\cup\partial M
.
In this paper, we study the Dirichlet problem for a class of Hessian quotient equations

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}(\lambda[U])=\psi(x,u,\nabla u)&&in~M,\\
&u=\varphi&&on~\partial M,\end{aligned}\right.

where
U=\tau(\Delta u)g-\nabla^{2}u
with
\tau\geq 1
,
\nabla^{2}u
denotes the Hessian of
u
,
\lambda[U]=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
are the
eigenvalues of
U
with respect to the metric
g
and

\psi
is a positive
C^{\infty}
function with respect to
(x,z,p)\in\overline{M}\times\mathbb{R}\times T_{x}M
, where
T_{x}M
denotes the tangent space of
M
at
x
.

Our interest on the solvability of equation (
1.1
) is motivated from
the complex Monge-Ampère type equations. Recently, Harvey-Lawson
[
18
,
19
]
introduced a class of functions
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, named
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic, such that the complex Hessian matrix

(1.2)

\displaystyle\bigg[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{m}\partial\overline{z}_{m}}\Big)\delta_{ij}-\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{i}\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

is nonnegative definite. For
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic functions, one can consider the following complex Monge-Ampère equations

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}\bigg(\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{m}\partial\overline{z}_{m}}\Big)\delta_{ij}-\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{i}\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\bigg)=\psi.

If
\psi
does not depend on
\nabla u
,
the Dirichlet problem for (
1.3
) on strict pseudo-convex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
was solved by Li
[
24
]
, who also considered a general class of operators. Tosatti-Weinkove
[
32
,
33
]
showed that the associated complex
Monge-Ampère equation can be solved on any compact Kähler manifold.
Harvey-Lawson
[
17
,
18
]
investigated the corresponding complex Monge-Ampère equation and sloved the Dirichlet problem with
\psi=0
on suitable domains. Then Han-Ma-Wu
[
20
]

considered
k
-convex solutions of complex Laplace equation.
Moreover, the complex Hessian equation involving a gradient term on the left hand sides has attracted the interest of many authors due to its geometric applications such as the Gauduchon conjecture, which was solved by Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove in their work
[
30
]
, and also see Guan-Nie
[
11
]
.
For more references, we refer the readers to
[
15
,
4
,
5
,
31
]
and
references therein.

If the complex Hessian matrix is replaced by the real Hessian matrix in (
1.2
),
a natural question is whether we can study the regularity and solvability to
the Dirichlet boundary problem for this kind of fully nonlinear equation (such as (
1.1
)). This work is a further study on the Dirichlet problem for (
1.1
) with gradient terms on the right sides of the equation following a recent work by Chu-Jiao
[
3
]
.
To ensure the ellipticity of (
1.1
), we need
\lambda[U]\in\Gamma_{k}
. Hence we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in C^{2}(M)
is called admissible (i.e.
(\eta,k)
-convex) if
\lambda[U]\in\Gamma_{k}
for any
x\in M
,
where
\Gamma_{k}
is the Garding cone

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0,\forall~1\leq j\leq k\}.

The main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
l+2\leq k\leq n
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
,
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M}\times\mathbb{R}\times T_{x}M)
with
\psi,\psi_{z}>0
.
Assume that there exists an admissible subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(\overline{M})
satisfying

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}\left(\lambda[\underline{U}]\right)\geq\psi(x,\underline{u},\nabla\underline{u})&&in~M,\\
&\underline{u}=\varphi&&on~\partial M,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\underline{U}=\tau(\Delta\underline{u})g-\nabla^{2}\underline{u}
. Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) is uniquely solvable for
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
with
\lambda[U]\in\Gamma_{k}
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, a major challenge comes from second order estimates for a domain with arbitrary boundary shape except being smooth. We establish the following global second order estimates.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
l+2\leq k\leq n
,
\varphi\in C^{2}(\partial M)
,
\psi\in C^{2}(\overline{M}\times\mathbb{R}\times T_{x}M)
with
\psi,\psi_{z}>0
,
u\in C^{4}(M)\cap C^{2}(\overline{M})
be an admissible solution of Dirichlet problem (
1.1
).
Assume that there exists an admissible subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(\overline{M})
satisfying (
1.4
). There exists
C
depending on
n,k,l,\|u\|_{C^{1}},\|\underline{u}\|_{C^{2}},\inf\psi,\|\psi\|_{C^{2}}
and the curvature
tensor
R
such that

\sup_{\overline{M}}|\nabla^{2}u|\leq C.

If
U=\tau(\Delta u)g-\nabla^{2}u
is replaced by the Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}u
, equation (
1.1
) becomes the classical Hessian quotient equation

(1.5)

\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}\left(\lambda[\nabla^{2}u]\right)=\psi(x,u,\nabla u)\quad\mbox{in}~M,

which has been widely studied in the past decades for the Euclidean case.
When
\psi=\psi(x)
,

C^{2}
estimates were treated by Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
1
]
for
l=0
, where they treated a general class of fully nonlinear equations under conditions on the geometry of
\partial M
, followed by
[
6
,
26
]
. Then such estimates for (
1.5
) have been established by Trudinger
[
35
]
, Ivochkina-Trudinger-Wang
[
22
]
who considered the degenerate case, Guan
[
9
]
who considered to treat a general class of fully nonlinear equations on Riemannian
manifolds, without geometric restrictions to the boundary.
When
\psi=\psi(x,u,\nabla u)
, equation (
1.5
) falls into the setup of Guan-Jiao
[
10
]
(see also
[
7
]
), and the
C^{2}
estimate was obtained under the concavity assumption of
\psi
on
\nabla u
. In Theorem
1.3
, we remove this concavity assumption for equation (
1.1
).

It would be worthwhile to note that this type of equation (
1.1
) arise naturally from many other important geometric problems. Another example is a class of prescribed curvature problems. A
(0,2)
-tensor on a hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{ij}=Hg_{ij}-h_{ij},

where
g_{ij}
is the induced metric of
M
from
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,

h_{ij}
and
H
are the second fundamental form and the mean
curvature of
M
respectively. The
(n-1)
-convex
hypersurface (i.e.
\eta_{ij}
is nonnegative definite) has
been studied intensively by Sha
[
27
,
28
]
, Wu
[
37
]
, and
Harvey-Lawson
[
16
]
. Recently, Chu-Jiao
[
3
]

considered the following prescribed curvature problem

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\eta_{ij}(X))=\psi(X,\nu(X)),\quad X\in M,

where
\nu
was the unit outer normal vector of
M
. Later on,
The authors
[
2
]
studied the corresponding Hessian
quotient type prescribed curvature problem. Moreover,
an analogue of equation (
1.1
) on compact manifolds also appeared naturally in conformal geometry, see Gursky-Viaclovsky
[
14
]
, Li-Sheng
[
23
]
and Sheng-Zhang
[
29
]
.

1.2
and Theorem

.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we recall
the definition of elementary symmetric function for
1\leq k\leq n

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}.

We also set
\sigma_{0}=1
and
\sigma_{k}=0
for
k>n
or
k<0
. The Garding cone is defined by

\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{i}(\lambda)>0,\forall 1\leq i\leq k\}.

We denote
\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}
and

\sigma_{k-2}(\lambda|ij)=\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{i}\partial\lambda_{j}}
. Next, we list some properties of

\sigma_{k}
which will be used later.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq k\leq n
, then we have

(1)
\Gamma_{1}\supset\Gamma_{2}\supset\cdot\cdot\cdot\supset\Gamma_{n}
;

(2)
\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)>0
for
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
and

1\leq i\leq n
;

(3)
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sigma_{k}(\lambda|i)+\lambda_{i}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)
for
1\leq i\leq n
;

(4)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial[\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}]^{\frac{1}{k-l}}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\geq[\frac{C^{k}_{n}}{C^{l}_{n}}]^{\frac{1}{k-l}}
for

\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
and
0\leq l<k
;

(5)
\Big[\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{k-l}}
are
concave in
\Gamma_{k}
for
0\leq l<k
;

(6) If
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdot\cdot\cdot\geq\lambda_{n}
,
then
\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|1)\leq\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|2)\leq\cdot\cdot\cdot\leq\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|n)
for
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
;

(7)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)=(n-k+1)\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

All the properties are well known. For example, see Chapter XV in

[
25
]
or
[
21
]
for proofs of (1), (2), (3), (6) and
(7); see Lemma 2.2.19 in
[
13
]
for the proof of (4); see

[
1
]
and
[
25
]
for the proof of (5).
∎

The generalized Newton-MacLaurin inequality is as follows, which
will be used later.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
\lambda\in\Gamma_{m}
and
m>l\geq 0
,
r>s\geq 0
,
m\geq r
,
l\geq s
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{{\sigma_{m}(\lambda)}/{C_{n}^{m}}}{{\sigma_{l}(\lambda)}/{C_{n}^{l}}}\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{m-l}}\leq\Bigg[\frac{{\sigma_{r}(\lambda)}/{C_{n}^{r}}}{{\sigma_{s}(\lambda)}/{C_{n}^{s}}}\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{r-s}}.

Proof.

See
[
34
]
.
∎

In this paper,
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection on
(M,g)
and the curvature tensor
is defined by

R(X,Y)Z=-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z+\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.

Let
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
be local frames on
M
and denote
g_{ij}=g(e_{i},e_{j})
,
\{g^{ij}\}=\{g_{ij}\}^{-1}
,
while the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
and curvature coefficients are given respectively by
\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}=\Gamma^{k}_{ij}e_{k}
and

R_{ijkl}=g(R(e_{k},e_{l})e_{j},e_{i}),\quad R^{i}_{jkl}=g^{im}R_{mjkl}.

We shall write
\nabla_{i}=\nabla_{e_{i}}
,
\nabla_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}-\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\nabla_{k}
, etc.
For a differentiable function
u
defined on
M
, we usually identify
\nabla u
with its gradient,
and use
\nabla^{2}u
to denote its Hessian which is locally given by
\nabla_{ij}u=\nabla_{i}(\nabla_{j}u)-\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\nabla_{k}u
. We note that
\nabla_{ij}u=\nabla_{ji}u
and

(2.1)

\nabla_{ijk}u-\nabla_{jik}u=R^{l}_{kij}\nabla_{l}u,

(2.2)

\nabla_{ij}(\nabla_{k}u)=\nabla_{ijk}u+\Gamma^{l}_{ik}\nabla_{jl}u+\Gamma^{l}_{jk}\nabla_{il}u+\nabla_{\nabla_{ij}e_{k}}u,

(2.3)

\nabla_{ijkl}u-\nabla_{ikjl}u=R^{m}_{ljk}\nabla_{im}u+\nabla_{i}R^{m}_{ljk}\nabla_{m}u,

(2.4)

\nabla_{ijkl}u-\nabla_{jikl}u=R^{m}_{kij}\nabla_{ml}u+R^{m}_{lij}\nabla_{km}u.

From (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we obtain

(2.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{ijkl}u-\nabla_{klij}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{m}_{ljk}\nabla_{im}u+\nabla_{i}R^{m}_{ljk}\nabla_{m}u+R^{m}_{lik}\nabla_{jm}u

\displaystyle+R^{m}_{jik}\nabla_{lm}u+R^{m}_{jil}\nabla_{km}u+\nabla_{k}R^{m}_{jil}\nabla_{m}u.

For convenience, we introduce
the following notations

F(U)=\bigg[\frac{\sigma_{k}(\lambda[U])}{\sigma_{l}(\lambda[U])}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{k-l}},\quad F^{ij}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial U_{ij}},\quad F^{ij,rs}=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial U_{ij}\partial U_{rs}},\quad Q^{ij}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{ij}},\quad Q^{ij,rs}=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial u_{ij}\partial u_{rs}}.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
be an admissible solution of equation (
1.1
). Under orthonormal local frames
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
, equation (
1.1
) is expressed in the form

(2.6)

F(U):=f(\lambda[U])=\psi.

For simplicity, we shall still write equation (
1.1
) in the form (
2.6
) even if
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
are not necessarily orthonormal, although more precisely it should be

F([\gamma^{ik}U_{kl}\gamma^{lj}])=\psi,

where
\gamma^{ij}
is the square root of
g^{ij}:\gamma^{ik}\gamma^{kj}=g^{ij}
. Whenever we differentiate the equation, it will make no difference as long as we use covariant derivatives.
Assume that
\overline{A}
is an
n\times n
matrix and
T:\overline{A}\rightarrow T(\overline{A})
is defined as
T(\overline{A})=\tau(tr(\overline{A}))I-\overline{A}
. Let
Q=F\small{\circ}T
, then equation (
1.1
)
can also be written as

Q(\nabla^{2}u):=\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{\lambda}[\nabla^{2}u])=\psi,

Hence
Q^{ij}=\frac{\partial Q}{\partial u_{ij}}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{ij}}=\tau\sum_{l}F^{ll}\delta_{ij}-F^{ij}
and then

(2.7)

Q^{ij}u_{ij}=F^{ij}U_{ij}=f_{i}\lambda_{i}=\widetilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}=\psi.

Differentiating (
2.6
), we get

(2.8)

Q^{ij}\nabla_{k}u_{ij}=F^{ij}\nabla_{k}U_{ij}=\psi_{k}+\psi_{z}u_{k}+\psi_{p_{i}}u_{ik}.

The following propositions are essential which will be used later. More details can be seen in
[
2
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a smooth
(\eta,k)
-convex closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

and
0\leq l<k-1
. Then the operator

F(U)=\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}(\lambda[U])}{\sigma_{l}(\lambda[U])}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}

is elliptic and concave with respect to
U
. Moreover we have

\sum F^{ii}\geq\left(\frac{C_{n}^{k}}{C_{n}^{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
U
be a diagonal matrix with
\lambda[U]\in\Gamma_{k}
,
0\leq l\leq k-2
and
k\geq 3
. Then

-F^{1i,i1}(U)=\frac{F^{11}-F^{ii}}{U_{ii}-U_{11}},\quad\forall~i\geq 2.

3.
C^{1}
Estimates

In this section, we consider the lower and upper bounds, gradient estimates for the admissible solution to equation (
1.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
be an admissible solution for equation (
1.1
).
Under the assumptions mentioned in Theorem
1.2
, then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\sup_{\partial M}\varphi
and the subsolution
\underline{u}
such that

\sup_{x\in\overline{M}}|u(x)|\leq C.

Proof.

On the one hand, according to Definition
1.1
, it is easy to see that

\lambda[U]\in\Gamma_{k}\subset\Gamma_{1}
, which implies that
tr(\lambda[U])=(\tau n-1)\Delta u>0
.
Combined with the maximum principle, we have

\sup_{\overline{M}}u\leq\sup_{\partial M}\varphi.

On the other hand, we know that there exists an admissible subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(\overline{M})
satisfying (
1.4
).
By the fact
\psi_{z}>0
and the comparison principle,

u\geq\underline{u},\quad\forall~x\in\overline{M}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
l+2\leq k\leq n
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
,
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M}\times\mathbb{R}\times T_{x}M)
with
\psi,\psi_{z}>0
. If
u\in C^{2}(\overline{M})
is the solution of equation (
1.1
),
then

\sup_{M}|\nabla u|\leq C(1+\sup_{\partial M}|\nabla u|),

where
C
is a constant depending on
n,k,l,\|u\|_{C^{0}},\|\psi\|_{C^{1}}
and the curvature tensor
R
.

Proof.

Consider the auxiliary function

P(x)=ve^{\phi(u)},

where
v=1+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}
,
\phi(u):\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function satisfying

\phi^{\prime}(u)>0,\quad\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-(\phi^{\prime}(u))^{2}\geq\varepsilon

for some positive constant
\varepsilon
depending on
\|u\|_{C^{0}}
.

Suppose that
P
attains its maximum at
x_{0}\in M
. By rotating the coordinates, we diagonal the matrix
\nabla^{2}u
. In the following, we write simply
u_{i}=\nabla_{i}u
,
u_{ij}=\nabla_{ij}u
and
u_{ijk}=\nabla_{k}u_{ij}
, then at
x_{0}
,

(3.1)

0=P_{i}=(u_{ii}u_{i}+v\phi^{\prime}u_{i})e^{\phi(u)},

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0\geq P_{ii}=\left(u_{ii}^{2}+u_{k}u_{kii}+2u_{i}^{2}u_{ii}\phi^{\prime}+u_{i}^{2}v\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\right)+v\phi^{\prime}u_{ii}\right)e^{\phi(u)}.

We assume that
v\leq|\nabla u|^{2}
, i.e.,
|\nabla u|^{2}\geq 2
. Otherwise our result holds.
Let

\mathcal{S}=\{i\in(1,\cdots,n)\mid u_{i}\neq 0\}.

Obviously
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
and we derive

u_{ii}=-v\phi^{\prime}<0,\quad i\in\mathcal{S}

by (
3.1
). From the mean curvature
H>0
, we have

Q^{ii}\geq\sum_{l\neq i}F^{ll}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l}F^{ll},

which implies

(3.3)

\displaystyle Q^{ii}u_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S}Q^{ii}u_{i}^{2}\geq\sum_{i\in S}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l}F^{ll}\right)u_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l}F^{ll}\right)|\nabla u|^{2}=\frac{1}{2(\tau n-1)}\left(\sum_{l}Q^{ll}\right)|\nabla u|^{2}.

Since
Q^{ii}\geq 0
and by Ricci identity, we have
u_{kij}=u_{ijk}+R^{l}_{jki}u_{l}
, then

(3.4)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle Q^{ii}\left(u_{k}u_{kii}+2u_{i}^{2}u_{ii}\phi^{\prime}+u_{i}^{2}v\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\right)+v\phi^{\prime}u_{ii}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{k}u_{k}+\psi_{z}u_{k}^{2}+\psi_{p_{k}}u_{k}u_{kk}+R^{l}_{iki}Q^{ii}u_{k}u_{l}+2\phi^{\prime}Q^{ii}u_{i}^{2}u_{ii}

\displaystyle+v\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\right)Q^{ii}u_{i}^{2}+v\phi^{\prime}Q^{ii}u_{ii}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\psi_{k}u_{k}-v\phi^{\prime}\psi_{p_{k}}u_{k}+R^{l}_{iki}Q^{ii}u_{k}u_{l}+v\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}-(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\right)Q^{ii}u_{i}^{2}+v\phi^{\prime}\psi

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{l}Q^{ll}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4(\tau n-1)}|\nabla u|^{4}-\overline{C}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)-\overline{C}\phi^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{3}-\overline{C}\phi^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}-\overline{C}|\nabla u|,

where
\overline{C}
is a constant depending on
\|\psi\|_{C^{1}}
and the curvature tensor
R
.

On the one hand, if
\frac{\varepsilon}{4(\tau n-1)}|\nabla u|^{4}-\overline{C}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 0
, then
|\nabla u|\leq C
. Otherwise by (
3.4
) and the fact

\sum_{l}Q^{ll}\geq(\tau n-1)\left(\frac{C_{n}^{k}}{C_{n}^{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}
, we derive

(\tau n-1)\left(\frac{C_{n}^{k}}{C_{n}^{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4(\tau n-1)}|\nabla u|^{4}-\overline{C}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)-\overline{C}\phi^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{3}-\overline{C}\phi^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}-\overline{C}|\nabla u|\leq 0,

then
|\nabla u|\leq C
, the lemma is proved.
∎

Next, we derive the global
C^{1}
estimates for the solution of equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
be an admissible solution for equation (
1.1
).
Under the assumptions mentioned in Theorem
1.2
, then

\sup_{\overline{M}}|\nabla u|\leq C,

where
C
is a constant depending on
n,k,l
,
\|u\|_{C^{0}}
,
\|\underline{u}\|_{C^{1}}
,
\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}}
,
\|\psi\|_{C^{1}}
and the curvature tensor
R
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.2
, we are left with the task of estimating the exterior normal derivative of
u
on
\partial M
. Let
h
be the harmonic function in
M
which equals
\varphi
on
\partial M
, then we derive

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta(u-h)>0&&in~M,\\
&u-h=0&&on~\partial M.\end{aligned}\right.

The maximum principle implies
u\leq h
in
M
. Therefore,

\underline{u}\leq u\leq h\quad\mbox{in}~M.

.

Case 1:
|\nu_{\mu}-\nu_{\lambda}|<\beta
.

Since
\nabla_{n}d(x_{0})=1
,
\nabla_{\alpha}d(x_{0})=0
for all
\alpha<n
, we can choose a
constant
\delta_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq|\nabla d|\leq 1,\quad-\widetilde{C}_{1}I\leq\nabla^{2}d\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}I,\quad\forall x\in M_{\delta}

for any
\delta<\delta_{0}
, where
\widetilde{C}_{1}
depends on the geometry of
\partial M
.
Note that
\nu_{\lambda}-\beta\mathbf{1}\in\Gamma_{n}
,
\mathbf{1}=(1,\cdots,1)
, then

(4.21)

\displaystyle F^{ii}\geq\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k}F^{kk},\quad\forall~1\leq i\leq n.

By the definition of
v
, we have

(4.22)

\displaystyle Lv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}v-\psi_{p_{i}}\nabla_{i}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{ij}\left(\nabla_{ij}(u-\underline{u})+t\nabla_{ij}d-N\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d-Nd\nabla_{ij}d\right)

\displaystyle-\psi_{p_{i}}\left(\nabla_{i}(u-\underline{u})+t\nabla_{i}d-Nd\nabla_{i}d\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(u-\underline{u})+(t-Nd)Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}d-NQ^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d+\widetilde{C}_{2}+\widetilde{C}_{2}t+\widetilde{C}_{2}N\delta,

where
\widetilde{C}_{2}
depends on
\|\psi\|_{C^{1}}
,
\|u\|_{C^{1}}
and
\|\underline{u}\|_{C^{1}}
.
By the concavity of
F
, we have

Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(u-\underline{u})=F^{ij}(\nabla_{ij}U-\nabla_{ij}\underline{U})\leq 0.

Thus, we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle Lv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(t-Nd)Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}d-NQ^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d+\widetilde{C}_{2}+\widetilde{C}_{2}t+\widetilde{C}_{2}N\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}t\sum_{i}Q^{ii}+\widetilde{C}_{2}+\widetilde{C}_{2}t+N\widetilde{C}_{1}\delta\sum_{i}Q^{ii}-NQ^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d+\widetilde{C}_{2}N\delta.

By (
4.21
), we have

(4.24)

\displaystyle Q^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d=\tau\sum_{l}F^{ll}|\nabla d|^{2}-F^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d\geq\frac{(n-1)\beta}{4\sqrt{n}}\sum_{l}F^{ll}.

Note that

(4.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{l}F^{ll}=\frac{1}{\tau n-1}\sum_{i}Q^{ii}\geq\left(\frac{C_{n}^{k}}{C_{n}^{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}:=\widetilde{C}_{3}.

Combining with (
4.23
)-(
4.25
), we get

\displaystyle Lv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}t(\tau n-1)\sum_{l}F^{ll}+\frac{\widetilde{C}_{2}+\widetilde{C}_{2}t}{\widetilde{C}_{3}}\sum_{l}F^{ll}

\displaystyle+N\widetilde{C}_{1}(\tau n-1)\delta\sum_{l}F^{ll}-N\frac{(n-1)\beta}{4\sqrt{n}}\sum_{l}F^{ll}+\frac{\widetilde{C}_{2}N\delta}{\widetilde{C}_{3}}\sum_{l}F^{ll}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\widetilde{C}_{2}}{\widetilde{C}_{3}}\sum_{l}F^{ll},

if we choose the constants
t,N,\delta
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &t\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{2}}{\widetilde{C}_{1}\widetilde{C}_{3}(\tau n-1)+\widetilde{C}_{2}},\\
&N\geq\frac{20\widetilde{C}_{2}\sqrt{n}}{\widetilde{C}_{3}(n-1)\beta},\\
&\delta\leq\min\{\delta_{0},\frac{2t}{N}\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Case 2:
|\nu_{\mu}-\nu_{\lambda}|\geq\beta
.

From Lemma 2.1 in
[
9
]
, we know that for some uniform constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,

Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(\underline{u}-u)=F^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(\underline{U}-U)\geq f_{i}(\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i})\geq\varepsilon_{0}(1+\sum_{i}F^{ii}).

In according to (
4.22
), we have

(4.26)

\displaystyle Lv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}(1+\sum_{i}F^{ii})-\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(\underline{u}-u)+2NQ^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d\right)

\displaystyle+(t-Nd)\widetilde{C}_{1}(\tau n-1)\sum_{l}F^{ll}+\widetilde{C}_{2}+\widetilde{C}_{2}t+\widetilde{C}_{2}N\delta.

By the concavity of
F
,

\displaystyle Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(\underline{u}-u)+2NQ^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(\underline{U}-U)+2NF^{ij}(\tau|Dd|^{2}\delta_{ij}-\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F\left(\nabla_{ij}\underline{U}+2N(\tau|Dd|^{2}\delta_{ij}-\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d)\right)-F(\nabla_{ij}U)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}(\mu+2N\lambda[A])-\psi,

where
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{n})
and
A=\left[\tau|Dd|^{2}\delta_{ij}-d_{i}d_{j}\right]_{n\times n}
. Since
\lambda[A]\geq\frac{1}{4}\mbox{diag}(0,1,\cdots,1)
, then we have

\displaystyle Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(\underline{u}-u)+2NQ^{ij}\nabla_{i}d\nabla_{j}d\geq\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}(\mu+\overline{\lambda})-\widetilde{C}_{2},

where
\overline{\lambda}=\mbox{diag}(0,\frac{N}{2},\cdots,\frac{N}{2})
.

Next, based on the range of
k
, we consider the following two conditions.

When
k=n
, since
F(\underline{U})\geq\psi(x,\underline{u},D\underline{u})>0
, we know that
\sigma_{n}(\mu)\geq\widetilde{C}_{4}
, then

(4.27)

\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\sigma_{l}}(\mu+\overline{\lambda})\geq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{4}N^{n-1}}{C_{n}^{l}(\mu_{\mbox{max}}+N)^{l}}\geq\widetilde{C}_{5}N^{n-1-l},

where
\widetilde{C}_{5}
depends on
\inf\psi,n,k,l
and
\|\underline{u}\|_{C^{2}}
.

When
3\leq k\leq n-1
, we assume that
N>|4\mu|^{2}+1
, then

(4.28)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}(\mu+\overline{\lambda})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\left(\mbox{diag}(\mu_{1},\frac{N}{4},\cdots,\frac{N}{4})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{n-1}^{k}(\frac{N}{4})^{k}+\mu_{1}C_{n-1}^{k-1}(\frac{N}{4})^{k-1}}{C_{n-1}^{l}(\frac{N}{4})^{l}+\mu_{1}C_{n-1}^{l-1}(\frac{N}{4})^{l-1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{n-1}^{k}(\frac{N}{4})^{k}-\frac{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4}C_{n-1}^{k-1}(\frac{N}{4})^{k-1}}{C_{n-1}^{l}(\frac{N}{4})^{l}+C_{n-1}^{l-1}(\frac{N}{4})^{l}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{5}N^{k-l-1}.

By (
4.26
)-(
4.28
), we can choose
t,N,\delta
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &t\leq\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{12\widetilde{C}_{1}(\tau n-1)},1\right\},\\
&N\geq\max\left\{\left(10\widetilde{C}_{2}\widetilde{C}_{5}^{-\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)^{\frac{k-l}{k-l-1}},16n\mu_{\mbox{max}}^{2}+1\right\},\\
&\delta\leq\min\left\{\delta_{0},\frac{2t}{N}\right\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Thus

\displaystyle Lv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}(1+\sum_{i}F^{ii})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{C}_{5}N^{k-l-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}+3t\widetilde{C}_{1}(\tau n-1)\sum_{l}F^{ll}+5\widetilde{C}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4}(1+\sum_{i}F^{ii}).

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
be an admissible solution for equation (
1.1
). Under the assumptions mentioned in Theorem
1.2
,
there exists a constant
C
depending only on
n,k,l,\|u\|_{C^{1}},\|\underline{u}\|_{C^{2}},\inf\psi
,
\|\psi\|_{C^{2}}
and the curvature tensor
R
such that

\sup_{\overline{M}}|\nabla^{2}u|\leq C.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, we only need to derive boundary estimates.
For any
x_{0}\in\partial M
, we can choose the local frames
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
around
x_{0}
such
that
e_{n}
is interior normal to
\partial M
.

\mathbf{Case~1:}
Estimates of
\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u,\alpha,\beta=1,\cdots,n-1
on
\partial M
.

Consider a point
x_{0}\in\partial M
. Since
u-\underline{u}=0
on
\partial M
, therefore,

\nabla_{\alpha\beta}(u-\underline{u})=-\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})B_{\alpha\beta}\quad\mbox{on}~\partial M,

where
B_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\nabla_{\alpha}e_{\beta},e_{n}\rangle
denotes the second fundamental form of
\partial M
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u|\leq C\quad\mbox{on}~\partial M,

where
C
depends on
\|u\|_{C^{1}}
and
\|\underline{u}\|_{C^{2}}
.

\mathbf{Case~2:}
Estimates of
\nabla_{\alpha n}u
,
\alpha=1,\cdots,n-1
on
\partial M
.

Let

(4.29)

\Phi=A_{1}v+A_{2}\rho^{2}-A_{3}\sum_{\beta<n}|\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})|^{2},

then combining with Lemma
4.2
, we claim that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &L(\Phi\pm\nabla_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u}))\leq 0&&in~M_{\delta},\\
&\Phi\pm\nabla_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})\geq 0&&on~\partial M_{\delta},\end{aligned}\right.

for suitably chosen positive constants
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
and
L
,
v
are defined in Lemma
4.2
. First we have for some uniform constant
\widehat{C}_{0}
,

L(\rho^{2})=Q^{ij}\nabla_{ij}(\rho^{2})-\psi_{p_{i}}\nabla_{i}(\rho^{2})\leq\widehat{C}_{0}(1+\sum_{i}Q^{ii}),

and by (
2.2
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
),

(4.30)

\displaystyle|L\nabla_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2Q^{ij}\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{l}\nabla_{jl}u+C(1+\sum_{i}Q^{ii})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widehat{C}_{1}(1+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}|\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}|+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}),

where
\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}(i=1,\cdots,n)
are the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}u
.

Furthermore, we get

(4.31)

\displaystyle L|\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Q^{ij}\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})\nabla_{ij}\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})+2Q^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})

\displaystyle-2\psi_{p_{i}}\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2Q^{ij}u_{i\beta}u_{j\beta}-\widehat{C}_{2}\left(1+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}|\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}|+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}\right).

By Proposition 2.19 in
[
8
]
, we know that there exists an index
r
such that

(4.32)

\sum_{\beta<n}Q^{ij}u_{i\beta}u_{j\beta}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq r}\widetilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2}.

Since
\widetilde{f}
satisfies
\widetilde{f}_{i}=\frac{\partial\widetilde{f}}{\partial\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}}=\sum_{i}Q^{ii}>0
,
\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}=\sum_{i}Q^{ii}u_{ii}=\psi>0
and
\widetilde{f}
is a concave function, then by Corollary 2.21
in
[
8
]
, for index
r
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.33)

\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}|\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}|\leq\varepsilon\sum_{i\neq r}\widetilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}+Q(r),

where
Q(r)=\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{\lambda})-\widetilde{f}(\mathbf{1})
if
\lambda_{r}\geq 0
,
\mathbf{1}=(1,\cdots,1)
and for some constant
K_{0}\geq 0
,

Q(r)=\varepsilon nK_{0}^{2}\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{1}{\widetilde{f}_{i}},\quad\mbox{if}~\lambda_{r}<0.

Hence (
4.30
)-(
4.33
) yield that

\displaystyle A_{3}\sum_{\beta<n}L|\nabla_{\beta}(u-\underline{u})|^{2}\pm L(\nabla_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2A_{3}\sum_{\beta<n}Q^{ij}u_{i\beta}u_{j\beta}-\widehat{C}_{1}\left(1+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}|\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}|+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle-A_{3}\widehat{C}_{2}(n-1)\left(1+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}|\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}|+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle A_{3}\sum_{i\neq r}\widetilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2}-(A_{3}\widehat{C}_{2}(n-1)+\widehat{C}_{1})\left(1+\varepsilon\sum_{i\neq r}\widetilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}+Q(r)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(A_{3}-A_{3}\widehat{C}_{2}(n-1)\varepsilon-\widehat{C}_{1}\varepsilon\right)\sum_{i\neq r}\widetilde{f}_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2}-A_{3}\widehat{C}_{3}(1+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-A_{3}\widehat{C}_{3}(1+\sum_{i}\widetilde{f}_{i}),

by choosing
0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\frac{1}{\widehat{C}_{2}(n-1)},1\right\}
and
A_{3}>\max\left\{\frac{\widehat{C}_{1}\varepsilon}{1-\widehat{C}_{2}(n-1)\varepsilon},1\right\}
.
Combine with Lemma
4.2
and choose
A_{1}\gg A_{2}\gg A_{3}\gg 1
, then

\left\{\begin{aligned} &L\left(\Phi\pm\nabla_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})\right)\leq 0\quad&&in~M_{\delta},\\
&\Phi\pm\nabla_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})\geq 0\quad&&on~\partial M_{\delta}.\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore by the maximum principle, we have

\Phi\pm\nabla_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})\geq 0\quad in~M_{\delta}.

Thus we obtain

|\nabla_{n\alpha}u(x_{0})|\leq\nabla_{n}\Phi(x_{0})+|\nabla_{n\alpha}\underline{u}(x_{0})|\leq C,\quad\alpha=1,\cdots,n-1.

\mathbf{Case~3:}
Estimates of
\nabla_{nn}u
on
\partial M
.

We only need to show the uniform upper bound

\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})\leq C,\quad\forall~x_{0}\in\partial M,

since
\Gamma_{k}\subset\Gamma_{1}
implies
\Delta u\geq 0
and the lower bound for
\nabla_{nn}u
follows from the estimate of
\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u
and
\nabla_{\alpha n}u
.
We will divide the proof into two conditions. The case
\tau=1
is more complicated and need classified discussion.

When
\tau>1
, we have

\displaystyle[U(x_{0})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\Delta u(x_{0})I-\nabla^{2}u(x_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mbox{diag}(\tau\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0}),\cdots,\tau\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0}),(\tau-1)\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0}))-\overline{C}_{0}I

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle((\tau-1)\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})-\overline{C}_{0})I,

where
\overline{C}_{0}
depends on
\|\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u\|_{C^{0}}
and
\|\nabla_{\alpha n}u\|_{C^{0}}
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\psi(x_{0},u(x_{0}),\nabla u(x_{0}))=F(U)(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{ij}(x_{0})U_{ij}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle((\tau-1)\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})-\overline{C}_{0})\sum_{l}F^{ll}.

Thus we obtain the upper bound as desired.

When
\tau=1
. By lemma 1.2 of
[
1
]
and the estimates of
\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u
,
\nabla_{\alpha n}u
, we can choose
R_{1}>0
sufficiently large such that if
\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})>R_{1}
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \widetilde{\lambda}_{i}[\nabla_{ij}u(x_{0})]&=\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}[\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u(x_{0})]+o(1),\quad i=1,\cdots,n-1,\\
\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}[\nabla_{ij}u(x_{0})]&=\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})\left(1+O(\frac{1}{\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})})\right).\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\widetilde{\lambda}[\nabla_{ij}u]=(\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}[\nabla_{ij}u],\cdots,\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}[\nabla_{ij}u])
denotes the eigenvalues of the
n\times n
matrix
\nabla^{2}u
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\prime}[\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u]=(\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime}[\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u],\cdots,\widetilde{\lambda}_{n-1}^{\prime}[\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u])
denotes the eigenvalues of the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix
\left[\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u\right]_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1}
. For convenience, we denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}=\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}[\nabla_{ij}u],\quad\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{\prime}[\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u],\quad\widehat{\lambda}_{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\widetilde{\lambda}_{l}-\widetilde{\lambda}_{i},\quad\widehat{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{\prime}.

If
k<n
,

\displaystyle F^{k-l}(U)(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\left(\sum_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}(x_{0})-\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}(x_{0}),\cdots,\sum_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}(x_{0})-\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}(x_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\left(\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}(x_{0})+\widehat{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime}(x_{0})+o(1),\cdots,\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}(x_{0})+\widehat{\lambda}_{n-1}^{\prime}(x_{0})+o(1),\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}(x_{0})+o(1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}^{k}_{n}(x_{0})+o\left(\widetilde{\lambda}^{k-1}_{n}(x_{0})\right)}{C_{n}^{l}\widetilde{\lambda}^{l}_{n}(x_{0})+O\left(\widetilde{\lambda}^{l-1}_{n}(x_{0})\right)},

which implies the uniform upper bound of
\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})
.

If
k=n
, we show the uniform upper bound of
\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})
by proving that there are uniform constants
\overline{C}_{1}
such that

(4.34)

\min_{x\in\partial M}tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u])\geq\overline{C}_{1}>0.

Suppose we have found such
\overline{C}_{1}
, then

\displaystyle F^{n-l}(U)(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\sigma_{l}}\left(\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}(x_{0})+\widehat{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime}(x_{0})+o(1),\cdots,\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}(x_{0})+\widehat{\lambda}_{n-1}^{\prime}(x_{0})+o(1),\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}(x_{0})+o(1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{C}_{1}\widetilde{\lambda}^{n-1}_{n}(x_{0})+o\left(\widetilde{\lambda}^{n-1}_{n}(x_{0})\right)}{C_{n}^{l}\widetilde{\lambda}^{l}_{n}(x_{0})+O\left(\widetilde{\lambda}^{l-1}_{n}(x_{0})\right)},

which implies the uniform upper bound of
\nabla_{nn}u(x_{0})
. Hence we only need to prove (
4.34
).

Suppose that
tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u])
attains its minimum at
x_{1}\in\partial M
. To show (
4.34
), we may assume
tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u(x_{1})])<\frac{1}{2}tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x_{1})])
, since otherwise we are done as
tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x_{1})])=\sum_{i}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}[\nabla_{ij}\underline{u}]-\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}[\nabla_{ij}\underline{u}]>\overline{C}_{2}
. Let us compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha\alpha}u=\nabla_{\alpha\alpha}\underline{u}-\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})B_{\alpha\alpha}\quad\mbox{on}~\partial M.

It follows that

(4.35)

\displaystyle\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})(x_{1})\sum_{\alpha}B_{\alpha\alpha}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x_{1})])-tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u(x_{1})])

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x_{1})])>\frac{\overline{C}_{2}}{2}.

For any
x\in\partial M
near
x_{0}
, applying that
tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}u])\mid_{\partial M}
is minimized at
x_{1}
yields,

\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})(x)\sum_{\alpha}B_{\alpha\alpha}(x)\leq tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x)])-tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x_{1})])+\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})(x_{1})\sum_{\alpha}B_{\alpha\alpha}(x_{1}).

Note that
B_{\alpha\alpha}
is smooth near
\partial M
and
0<u-\underline{u}\leq C
, adding (
4.35
), we can choose a constant
\delta
sufficiently small such that

\sum_{\alpha}B_{\alpha\alpha}\geq\overline{C}_{3}>0\quad\mbox{in}~M\cap B_{\delta}(x_{1})

for some uniform constant
\overline{C}_{3}>0
. Therefore

\displaystyle\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})(x_{1})=\Psi(x_{1}),\quad\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})(x)\leq\Psi(x)\quad\mbox{on}~B_{\delta}(x_{1})\cap\partial M,

where
\Psi=\left(\sum_{\alpha}B_{\alpha\alpha}(x)\right)^{-1}\left(tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x)])-tr([\nabla_{\alpha\beta}\underline{u}(x_{1})])+\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})(x_{1})\sum_{\alpha}B_{\alpha\alpha}(x_{1})\right)
is smooth in
M\cap B_{\delta}(x_{1})
.

We now apply the argument of
\nabla_{\alpha n}
again. For
A_{1}\gg A_{2}\gg A_{3}\gg 1
, it remains to prove that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &L\left(\Phi+\Psi-\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})\right)\leq 0\quad&&in~M\cap B_{\delta}(x_{1}),\\
&\Phi+\Psi-\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})\geq 0\quad&&on~\partial\left(M\cap B_{\delta}(x_{1})\right).\end{aligned}\right.

According to the maximum principle, we have

\Phi+\Psi-\nabla_{n}(u-\underline{u})\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}~M\cap B_{\delta}(x_{1}).

Thus
\nabla_{n}\Psi(x_{1})-\nabla_{nn}(u-\underline{u})(x_{1})\geq-\nabla_{n}\Phi(x_{1})\geq-C
, which implies that
\nabla_{nn}u(x_{1})\leq C
.
Therefore,

\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}(x_{1})\leq C,\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

It is clear that
\widehat{\lambda}=(\widehat{\lambda}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\lambda}_{n})\in\Gamma_{n}
. Hence

\displaystyle\psi^{n-l}(x_{1},u(x_{1}),\nabla u(x_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{n}(\widehat{\lambda})}{\sigma_{l}(\widehat{\lambda})}\leq\frac{\sigma_{n}(\widehat{\lambda})}{C_{n}^{l}\sigma^{\frac{l}{n}}_{n}(\widehat{\lambda})}=\frac{\sigma^{1-\frac{l}{n}}_{n}(\widehat{\lambda})}{C_{n}^{l}}.

It follows that

\widehat{\lambda}_{i}(x_{1})\geq\overline{C}_{4}.

We assume without loss of generality that the eigenvalue
\widehat{\lambda}_{i}(x_{1})

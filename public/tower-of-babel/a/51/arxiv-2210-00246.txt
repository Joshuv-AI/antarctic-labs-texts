Polyubles, Poisson homogeneous spaces and multi-flag varieties
Shaoqiang Deng, Chuangchuang Kang, Shizhuo Yu

Abstract
. A polyuble of a Manin triple can be regarded as the “ n n -th power” of it, which plays an important rule in the study of Poisson geometry, mathematical physics and Lie theory. In this paper, we first construct an isomorphism between the m ​ n mn -ble and the n n -ubles of m m -uble by colored graph and point out it is unique. Then, we construct a class of Poisson homogeneous spaces and obtain a class of Poisson homeomorphisms between them based on the first main result. Last, we apply first two main results to multi-flag varieties as well as multi-double flag varieties and construct a class of global Poisson isomorphisms between them as well as their T T -leaves.

Let

1.3.
Poisson homogeneous spaces induced by polyubles

Manin triple plays an important role in the study of Poisson geometry. Recall from
[
12
]
that a Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
is a manifold
M
with a bi-vector field
\pi
such that
\{f,g\}=\pi(df,dg)
is a Poisson bracket. A Poisson Lie group is a Lie group
G
with a
\pi_{G}
such that the group multiplication is a Poisson map. Recall from
[
12
]
that a simply-connected Poisson Lie group
(G,\pi_{G})
is one-one corresponding to a Manin triple
((\mathfrak{d},\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{d}}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})

Let
(G,\pi_{G})
be a simply connected Poisson Lie group with Manin triple
\mathcal{M_{\mathfrak{g}}}=((\mathfrak{d},\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{d}}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})

A
n
-uble of a Poisson Lie group
(G,\pi_{G})
is the Poisson Lie group, denoted by
G^{(n)}
, whose corresponding Manin triple is
\mathcal{M_{\mathfrak{g}}}^{n}
. Notice that
G^{(n)}
is locally but not globally isomorphic
G^{n}
in general. The version of Theorem A in Poisson Lie groups says that
(G^{(mn)},\pi_{G^{(mn)}})
is isomorphic to
((G^{(m)})^{(n)},\pi_{(G^{(m)})^{(n)}})
as Poisson Lie groups.

A homogeneous space
P
of
G
with a Poisson structure
\pi_{P}
is called
a Poisson homogeneous space
of Poisson Lie group
(G,\pi_{G})
if the action of
(G,\pi_{G})
on
(P,\pi_{P})
is a Poisson map, see
[
4
]
for reference. Not only the general theory of Poisson homogeneous spaces but also concrete examples such as flag varieties play important roles in the study of aspects such as symmetric spaces
[
11
]
, symplectic groupoids
[
19
]
and total positivity
[
20
]
.
Let
\mathcal{M}=((\mathfrak{d},\langle,\rangle),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
be a Manin triple and
M
a manifold. Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{d}\rightarrow V^{1}(M)
be the left Lie algebra action of
\mathfrak{d}
on
M
. Let
\{\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\{x_{1},...,x_{m}\}
the dual basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with respect to
\langle,\rangle
. Recall from
[
12
,
22
]
that

(1.1)

r_{\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathfrak{d}\otimes\mathfrak{d}.

is a quasi-triangular
r
-matrix of
\mathfrak{d}
. Denote
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}}\in\mathfrak{d}\wedge\mathfrak{d}
the skew-symmetric part of
r_{\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}}
.
In
[
18
,
21
]
, an equivalent condition that
\sigma(r_{\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}})
is a Poisson structure and a sufficient condition that

Let
D
be a simply-connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}
and

Theorem B
(Special Case of Theorem
3.19
and Theorem

)

(1)
The bivector field

2.1.
Preliminaries on hom-Lie algebras

As a generalization of Lie algebras, hom-Lie algebras were introduced in
[
8
]
, arose from the study of q-deformations of Witt and Virasoro algebras in
[
9
]
, which plays an important role in physics, mainly in discrete and deformed vector fields
[
14
,
15
]
.
We first recall some basic definitions and propositions of hom-Lie algebras from
[
1
,
27
,
28
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
hom-Lie algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
that consists of a vector space
\mathfrak{h}
, a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
, and an algebra homomorphism
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
such that the following hom-Jacobi identity holds:

(2.2)

[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),[y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y),[z,x]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(z),[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,\quad\forall\,x,y,z\in\mathfrak{h}.

A hom-Lie algebra is said to be
involutive
, if
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
. A subspace
\mathfrak{k}\subset\mathfrak{h}
is a
hom-Lie subalgebra
of
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
, if
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathfrak{k})\subset\mathfrak{k}
and for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{k}
,
[x,x^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathfrak{k}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h}_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}})
and
(\mathfrak{h}_{2},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}_{2}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}_{2}})
be two hom-Lie algebras. A
homomorphism
from
(\mathfrak{h}_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}})
to
(\mathfrak{h}_{2},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}_{2}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}_{2}})
is a linear map
f:\mathfrak{h}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{2}
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle f\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathfrak{h}_{2}}\circ f,

(2.4)

\displaystyle f[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}

\displaystyle=[f(x),f(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}_{2}},\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}.

An
isomorphism
of hom-Lie algebras is an invertible homomorphism of hom-Lie
algebras. Two hom-Lie algebras are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists an
isomorphism between them.

Definition 2.3
.

A
representation

(V,\rho,\alpha)
of hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
on a vector space
V
with respect to
\alpha\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))\circ\alpha

\displaystyle=\alpha\circ\rho(x),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}})\circ\alpha

\displaystyle=\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))\circ\rho(y)-\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y))\circ\rho(x),\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{h}.

Let
V^{*}
be the dual vector space of
V
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the bilinear pairing between
V
and
V^{*}
,
\alpha^{*}\in\mathfrak{gl}(V^{*})
satisfying

(2.7)

\langle\alpha(v),\xi\rangle=\langle v,\alpha^{*}(\xi)\rangle,\quad\forall~v\in V,\xi\in V^{*}.

Define a linear map
\rho^{*}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V^{*})
by

(2.8)

\langle\rho^{*}(x)(\xi),v\rangle+\langle\xi,\rho(x)v\rangle=0,\quad\forall~x\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 2.4
.

(
[
27
]
)

With the above notations,
(V^{*},\rho^{*},\alpha^{*})
is a representation of hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
if and only if the following two equations hold:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\alpha\circ\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))

\displaystyle=\rho(x)\circ\alpha,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\alpha\circ\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=\rho(x)\circ\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y))-\rho(y)\circ\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)),\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{h}.

The triple
(V^{*},\rho^{*},\alpha^{*})
is called the
dual representation
of hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
.

A representation
(V,\rho,\alpha)
is called
admissible
if
(V^{*},\rho^{*},\alpha^{*})
is also a representation, i.e. condition (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) in the above proposition are satisfied. It is straight forward to see that
(\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{ad},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a representation of hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Where
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
is a linear map and for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}
,
\mathrm{ad}_{x}(y)=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}
. We call
(\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{ad},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
the
adjoint representation
of hom-Lie algebra. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the dual space of
\mathfrak{h}
,
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
. Define a linear map
\mathrm{ad}^{*}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
by

(2.11)

\langle\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}(\xi),y\rangle+\langle\xi,\mathrm{ad}_{x}(y)\rangle=0,\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{h},\,\xi\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}.

Then
(\mathfrak{h}^{*},\mathrm{ad}^{*},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
is a representation if and only if the following two equations hold:

(2.12)

\displaystyle[(\mathrm{Id}-\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2})(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.13)

\displaystyle[(\mathrm{Id}-\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2})(x),[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y),z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=[(\mathrm{Id}-\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2})(y),[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall\,x,y,z\in\mathfrak{h}.

We call
(\mathfrak{h}^{*},\mathrm{ad}^{*},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
the
coadjoint representation
of hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
. And a hom-Lie algebra is called
admissible hom-Lie algebra
if (
2.12

2.13

If
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
, then
(\mathfrak{h}^{*},\mathrm{ad}^{*},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{u},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{u}},\phi_{\mathfrak{u}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}})
be an even dimensional quadratic hom-Lie algebra. With the above notations,

Proof.

For all
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{u}
,

\displaystyle[(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:direct-product-n}}{=}

\displaystyle(-[a_{1}^{\prime},a_{1}]_{\mathfrak{u}},\cdots,-[a_{n}^{\prime},a_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime}),(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}},

and

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}[(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:polyuble-hom-n}}{=}

\displaystyle(\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}[a_{1},a_{1}^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{u}},\cdots,\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}[a_{n},a_{n}^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}.

), for all

, we have

\displaystyle[\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),[(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime}),(a_{1}^{\prime\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime\prime})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}

\displaystyle+[\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime}),[(a_{1}^{\prime\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime\prime}),(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}(a_{1}),\cdots,\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}(a_{n})),([a_{1}^{\prime},a_{1}^{\prime\prime}]_{\mathfrak{u}},\cdots,[a_{n}^{\prime},a_{n}^{\prime\prime}]_{\mathfrak{u}})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}

\displaystyle+[(\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}(a_{1}^{\prime}),\cdots,\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}(a_{n}^{\prime})),([a_{1}^{\prime\prime},a_{1}]_{\mathfrak{u}},\cdots,[a_{n}^{\prime\prime},a_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}

\displaystyle(0,\cdots,0).

are hom-Lie algebras.

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:polyuble-hom-n}}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\langle\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}(a_{j}),a_{j}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\langle a_{j},\phi_{\mathfrak{u}}(a_{j}^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\langle(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}}(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{n}^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}},

Therefore,
(\mathfrak{u}^{n},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}},\phi_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}^{n}})

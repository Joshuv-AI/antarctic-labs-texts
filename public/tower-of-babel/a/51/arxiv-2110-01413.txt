The passage from the integral to the rational group ring in algebraic K \displaystyle K -theory
Georg Lehner

Abstract
. An open question is whether the map K 0 ~ ​ ℤ ​ G → K 0 ~ ​ ℚ ​ G \displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G in reduced K \displaystyle K -theory from the integral to the rational group ring is trivial for any group G \displaystyle G , . 1.
Introduction

Let
\displaystyle G
be a group,
\displaystyle R
a ring and define
\displaystyle RG
to be the group algebra of
\displaystyle G
over
\displaystyle R
. The algebraic
\displaystyle K
-theory group is defined as

K_{0}RG\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbb{Z}\left\{\text{isomorphism classes of f.g.\ projective}~RG\text{-modules}\right\}/\equiv

where
\displaystyle\equiv
is the equivalence relation generated by
\displaystyle[A\oplus B]\equiv[A]+[B]
.

For
\displaystyle R
being the ring of integers, the subgroup of
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Z}G
generated by the free modules is always a split summand isomorphic to
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
. The
reduced

\displaystyle K
-theory group
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G
is defined as the quotient of
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Z}G
by this summand.

The group
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G
is an important invariant of
\displaystyle G
appearing in a variety of geometric problems, most notably as the group containing Wall’s finiteness obstruction. Wall
[
Wal65
]
showed that for every finitely dominated CW-complex
\displaystyle X
with fundamental group
\displaystyle G=\pi_{1}(X)
, there exists an element
\displaystyle w(X)
in
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G
which is trivial iff
\displaystyle X
is actually finite. Moreover, every element of
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G
can be realized in this way from some finitely dominated CW-complex.

However,
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G
tends to be very hard to compute in general. One of the few structural things that can be said about
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G
is a theorem due to Swan that for
\displaystyle G
being a finite group
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G
is finite.

Changing the base ring to the rational numbers, we can define
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
in a similar manner. As before, we obtain a splitting
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Q}G\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
. For
\displaystyle G
a finite group,
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Q}G
is inherently easier to compute than its integral counterpart. Since in this case
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}G
is a finite dimensional semisimple algebra over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
, it splits as a product of matrix algebras over division algebras over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
, one for each irreducible
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-representation of
\displaystyle G
. This means that
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Q}G\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r_{\mathbb{Q}}},
where
\displaystyle r_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the number of isomorphism classes of irreducible
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-representations of
\displaystyle G
. In particular,
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
is a free abelian group of rank
\displaystyle r_{\mathbb{Q}}-1
.

We can thus see that for
\displaystyle G
being a finite group, the map
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow K_{0}\mathbb{Q}G
defined via
\displaystyle[P]\mapsto[P\otimes\mathbb{Q}]
, for
\displaystyle P
being a f.g. projective
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}G
-module, is an isomorphism on the summands corresponding to free modules over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}G
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}G
respectively, and trivial on the quotients

\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G,

since there it is a homomorphism from a finite to a free abelian group. Swan also showed the following slightly stronger result.

Theorem 1.1

(Swan,
[
Swa60
]
)
.

Suppose
\displaystyle G
is a finite group and
\displaystyle P
a finitely generated projective
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}G
-module. Then
\displaystyle P\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is free.

The statement that
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
is free does not generalize to arbitrary groups. In fact, Kropholler, Moselle
[
KM91
]
, and Leary
[
Lea00
]
constructed specific examples of groups which have
\displaystyle 2
-torsion elements in
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Q}G
. This means we cannot expect to find a straightforward generalization of Swan’s theorem to infinite groups.

Bass
[
Bas76
]
investigated this question and formulated what is now known as the strong Bass conjecture for
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Z}G
. For this, let
\displaystyle r\colon K_{0}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow HH_{0}(\mathbb{Z}G)
denote the Hattori-Stallings trace map and define
\displaystyle r_{P}(g)
as the coefficient in the sum

r(P)=\sum_{[g]\in\text{Conj}(G)}r_{P}(g)

under the isomorphism
\displaystyle HH_{0}(RG)=\bigoplus_{\text{Conj}(G)}R
. This gives a function
\displaystyle r_{P}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.

Conjecture 1.2

(Strong Bass Conjecture for
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Z}G
,
[
Bas76
]
)
.

The value
\displaystyle r_{P}(g)
is
\displaystyle 0
for
\displaystyle g\neq 1
.

Lück, Reich
[
LR05
]
, Section 3.1.3, show that the strong Bass conjecture for
\displaystyle K_{0}\mathbb{Z}G
follows from the stronger claim that the map

\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G

vanishes rationally, and they also show that this holds true if
\displaystyle G
satisfies the Farrell-Jones conjecture.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
LR05
]
, Proposition 3.11)
.

Assume
\displaystyle G
satisfies the Farrell-Jones conjecture. Then the map
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\otimes\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is trivial.

We will give a definition of the Farrell-Jones conjecture below. In Remark 3.13 they ask whether this is true integrally. This conjecture was also published in
[
Rei08
]
.

(Integral

-to-

-conjecture)
.

The map
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
is trivial.

\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G

\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
-Conjecture is false. A counterexample is given by the group
\displaystyle G\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=QD_{32}\ast_{Q_{16}}QD_{32}
, where
\displaystyle QD_{32}

\displaystyle 32
, and
\displaystyle Q_{16}

.

The other half of this paper is an investigation into how much the map
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\textbf{K}\mathbb{Z}G

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}G

\displaystyle\text{Or}G_{\text{VCyc}}

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle G/H

\displaystyle H

Now let
\displaystyle E\text{Fin}
be a fixed model for the classifying space of finite subgroups together with a chosen CW-structure
\displaystyle(E\text{Fin}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Write

(f,g)\colon\coprod_{i\in I}G/H_{i}\times S^{0}\rightarrow\coprod_{j\in J}G/K_{j}

for the degree
\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle E\text{Fin}
with the
\displaystyle H_{i}
and
\displaystyle K_{j}
being finite subgroups of
\displaystyle G
.
For a functor
\displaystyle F\colon\text{Or}G\rightarrow\text{Ab}
define

\text{ker}^{F}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\text{ker}(F(f)-F(g))\colon\bigoplus_{i\in I}F(G/H_{i})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in J}F(G/K_{j}).

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
\displaystyle G
satisfies the Farrell-Jones conjecture. There is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\text{ker}}^{\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\text{ker}}^{\text{SC}}\rightarrow{\text{ker}}^{K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\text{im}}(\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G)\rightarrow 0.

This gives a certain limitation on the map
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
. The terms
\displaystyle\text{ker}^{\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}},\text{ker}^{\text{SC}}
and
\displaystyle\text{ker}^{K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}}
are computable by representation theoretic techniques and this is what allowed the computation of the above counterexample. The groups
\displaystyle\text{ker}^{\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}}
and
\displaystyle\text{ker}^{\text{SC}}
are always free, and
\displaystyle\text{ker}^{K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}}
is free
\displaystyle p
-locally away from the prime
\displaystyle 2
. We will define the functor
SC
in Section
5
and give a characterization in terms of
\displaystyle p
-adic characters for finite groups in Section
6.3
.

One could still ask if the image of the map
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G
is in fact only
\displaystyle 2
-torsion. The author at present believes this to be false. Examples of groups with odd torsion are however more challenging to construct and will be saved for a later publication.

Definition 4.1

We are concerned with one particular type of functor on the orbit category - the functor that associates to
\displaystyle G/H

8.
Virtually cyclic groups

A group
\displaystyle G
is called virtually cyclic if it contains a cyclic subgroup of finite index. Virtually cyclic groups can be classified into three families of groups.

Lemma 8.1

(See
[
Hem04
]
, Lemma 11.4.)
.

A group
\displaystyle G
is virtually cyclic if it is of one of the three forms

•

\displaystyle G
is finite.

•

\displaystyle G
is finite-by-infinite cyclic. This means that there is an exact sequence of groups

1\rightarrow H\rightarrow G\rightarrow C_{\infty}\rightarrow 1

with
\displaystyle H
being finite, and
\displaystyle C_{\infty}
an infinite cyclic group. We will call
\displaystyle G
of type
\displaystyle\text{VC}1
.

•

\displaystyle G
is finite-by-infinite dihedral. This means that there is an exact sequence of groups

1\rightarrow H\rightarrow G\rightarrow D_{\infty}\rightarrow 1

with
\displaystyle H
being finite, and
\displaystyle D_{\infty}
an infinite dihedral group. We will call
\displaystyle G
of type
\displaystyle\text{VC}2
.

Before we begin with the analysis of type
\displaystyle 1
or type
\displaystyle 2
virtually cyclic groups, we state some properties of their negative
\displaystyle K
-theory.

8.1.
Negative
\displaystyle K
-theory of virtually cyclic groups

The following theorem due to Farrell, Jones extends Carter’s results to virtually cyclic groups.

Theorem 8.2

(
[
FJ95
]
, Theorem 2.1.)
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be a virtually infinite cyclic group. Then

(a)

\displaystyle K_{n}\mathbb{Z}G=0
for all integers
\displaystyle n\leq-2
.

(b)

\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}G
is generated by the images of
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}F
under the maps induced by
the inclusions
\displaystyle F\subset G
where
\displaystyle F
varies over representatives of the conjugacy
classes of finite subgroups of
\displaystyle G
.

(c)

\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}G
is a finitely generated abelian group.

This has a few implications for groups that satisfy the Farrell Jones conjecture.

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be a group satisfying the Farrell Jones conjecture. Then

•

\displaystyle K_{n}\mathbb{Z}G=0
for all integers
\displaystyle n\leq-2
.

•

The functor
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}-
satisfies finite assembly in the sense that

K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}G\cong\text{colim}_{G/H\in\text{Or}G_{\text{Fin}}}K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}H.

•

Let
\displaystyle k
be a field of characteristic
\displaystyle 0
. Then
\displaystyle K_{n}kG=0
for all integers
\displaystyle n\leq-1
.

Proof.

Theorem
8.2
(a) implies that the functor
\displaystyle\textbf{K}\mathbb{Z}-
is
\displaystyle(-1)
-connective, when restricted to the category
\displaystyle\text{Or}G_{\text{VCyc}}
and thus

\textbf{K}\mathbb{Z}G\simeq\text{colim}_{G/H\in\text{Or}G_{\text{VCyc}}}\textbf{K}\mathbb{Z}H

is
\displaystyle(-1)
-connective as well, since the subcategory of
\displaystyle(-1)
-connective spectra is closed under colimits. This implies the first statement. Furthermore, since the functor
\displaystyle\pi_{-1}
is a left adjoint when restricted to
\displaystyle(-1)
-connective spectra, we get the isomorphism

K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}G\cong\text{colim}_{G/H\in\text{Or}G_{\text{VCyc}}}K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}H.

Hence to show the second statement we need to show that

\text{colim}_{G/H\in\text{Or}G_{\text{Fin}}}K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}H\rightarrow\text{colim}_{G/H\in\text{Or}G_{\text{VCyc}}}K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}H

4.18
already states that it is injective. Surjectivity is implied by Theorem

The statement that
\displaystyle K_{n}kG=0
for
\displaystyle k
a field of characteristic
\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle n\leq-1

satisfies finite assembly, see

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle H

9.3.
The group
\displaystyle QD_{32}*_{Q_{16}}QD_{32}

The group we want to consider is the group

A concrete presentation is given by

\displaystyle Q_{16}

. This means that

\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}Q_{16}\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}_{2}Q_{16}

\displaystyle Q_{16}

9.4.
Other examples

The group
\displaystyle QD_{32}
is not special beyond the property that the map
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}Q_{16}\rightarrow K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}QD_{32}
is not injective. In fact, the group
\displaystyle Q_{16}
sits inside
\displaystyle 5
different groups of order
\displaystyle 32
. This can be checked with the
\displaystyle GAP

Here the value
\displaystyle(16,9)
refers to the ID of
\displaystyle Q_{16}
in the SmallGroups library in
\displaystyle GAP
.

We will analyse them case by case:

(1)

ID = [ 32, 19 ], also known as
\displaystyle QD_{32}
: As discussed
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}QD_{32}=0
.

(2)

ID = [ 32, 20 ], also known as
\displaystyle Q_{32}
: Here we can show that
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}Q_{16}\rightarrow K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}Q_{32}
induces an isomorphism.

(3)

ID = [ 32, 41 ], also known as
\displaystyle Q_{16}\times C_{2}
: Here we can show that
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}(Q_{16}\times C_{2})=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
and the map
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}Q_{16}\rightarrow K_{-1}(Q_{16}\times C_{2})
corresponds to the diagonal
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
. In particular it is injective.

(4)

ID = [ 32, 42 ], also known as
\displaystyle C_{4}\circ D_{8}
: This group has
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}C_{4}\circ D_{8}=0
.

(5)

ID = [ 32, 44 ], also known as
\displaystyle C_{8}.C_{2}^{2}
: Here we can show that the map
\displaystyle K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}Q_{16}\rightarrow K_{-1}\mathbb{Z}(C_{8}.C_{2}^{2})
induces an isomorphism.

In summary, the only virtually cyclic groups of type 2 that contain
\displaystyle Q_{16}
as kernel for which
\displaystyle\text{im}(\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G)
is non-trivial are the groups

QD_{32}\ast_{Q_{16}}QD_{32},QD_{32}\ast_{Q_{16}}(C_{4}\circ D_{8}),~\text{and}~(C_{4}\circ D_{8})\ast_{Q_{16}}(C_{4}\circ D_{8}).

In each of those cases we have

\text{im}(\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Z}G\rightarrow\widetilde{K_{0}}\mathbb{Q}G)=\mathbb{Z}/2.

10.
Comparison to related functors

We can ask if there is a more general statement to the one considered in this paper on
\displaystyle K_{0}
for higher
\displaystyle K
-groups. Here the most natural way to generalize to
\displaystyle\pi_{1}
would be to understand the map

\text{Wh}_{1}(\mathbb{Z};G)\rightarrow\text{Wh}_{1}(\mathbb{Q};G).

For finite
\displaystyle G
, we have that

\text{Wh}_{1}(\mathbb{Z};G)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r_{\mathbb{R}}-r_{\mathbb{Q}}}\oplus SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}G),

where
\displaystyle r_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\displaystyle r_{\mathbb{Q}}
are the number of real and rational representations, respectively, and
\displaystyle SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}G)
is a finite group, given as the kernel of the map
\displaystyle K_{1}(\mathbb{Z}G)\rightarrow K_{1}(\mathbb{Q}G)
. (See
[
Oli88
]
, page 6)

Using the groups of type
\displaystyle G=H\times(C_{\infty})^{2}
, the Bass-Heller-Swan decomposition already tells us that any defects of the maps in
\displaystyle K_{-1}
,
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}
and
\displaystyle\text{Wh}_{1}
for virtually cyclic groups
\displaystyle H
will enter the picture. Thus we can easily find counterexamples to the possibility that for example the map
\displaystyle\text{Wh}_{1}(\mathbb{Z};G)\rightarrow\text{Wh}_{1}(\mathbb{Q};G)
is an isomorphism rationally (injectivity fails for the corresponding statement in
\displaystyle K_{-1}
for
\displaystyle H
being any group
\displaystyle H
with an element of non-prime power order and surjectivity fails for
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}
for any non-trivial group
\displaystyle H
) or that it kills all torsion (not true in
\displaystyle\widetilde{K_{0}}
by the results of this paper).

Definition A.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category. A
\displaystyle t
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
is a pair of full subcategories
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\leq 0}
, both closed under isomorphisms, such that the following three conditions hold. Here
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\geq n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\leq n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\leq 0}
are defined as the essential images under the functors
\displaystyle\Sigma^{n}
for all
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

•

If
\displaystyle X\in\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
,
\displaystyle Y\in\mathcal{D}_{\leq-1}
then
\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)=0
.

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
,
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\leq-1}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\leq 0}

•

For all objects
\displaystyle X
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
we have a distinguished triangle

X^{\prime}\rightarrow X\rightarrow X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow\Sigma X^{\prime}

with
\displaystyle X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle X^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\leq-1}
.

A
\displaystyle t
-structure on a stable
\displaystyle\infty
-category is defined to be a
\displaystyle t
-structure on its homotopy category
\displaystyle h\mathcal{C}
. We define two types of subcategories of
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
, namely
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
, as the full subcategories of
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
corresponding to the subcategories
\displaystyle h\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
and
\displaystyle h\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
of
\displaystyle h\mathcal{C}
, respectively. The inclusions of the subcategories
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
admit right adjoints denoted by
\displaystyle\tau_{\geq n}
, which act as the identity when restricted to
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
. Consequently,
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
is closed under colimits in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
. Dually, the subcategories
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
admit left adjoints
\displaystyle\tau_{\geq n}
, which act as the identity on
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
, and
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
is closed under limits in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
(
[
Lur17
]
Proposition 1.2.1.5.). The compositions
\displaystyle\tau_{\geq n}\circ\tau_{\leq m}
and
\displaystyle\tau_{\leq m}\circ\tau_{\geq n}

[
Lur17
]

\displaystyle A\mapsto A[m,n]

\displaystyle A\mapsto A[n]
in the case
\displaystyle n=m
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

Theorem A.2

(See

Let

A.2.
Functor categories and
\displaystyle t
-structures

Given a stable
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
and a small
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
, we know that
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
is again a stable
\displaystyle\infty
-category. If we have a
\displaystyle t
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
, we can put a natural
\displaystyle t
-structure on
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
:

Definition A.8
.

Suppose
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is a stable
\displaystyle\infty
-category with
\displaystyle t
-structure and
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
a small
\displaystyle\infty
-category. The object-wise
\displaystyle t
-structure on
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
is defined via

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\leq 0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\geq 0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}).

We view the category of functors
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
as the full subcategory of functors
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
with values in the subcategory
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
and similarly for
\displaystyle\geq 0
.

Proof.

We have to check three things:

•

\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\leq 0}
is closed under
\displaystyle\Omega
. This is true since limits are computed object-wise and
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
is closed under limits. Similarly,
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\geq 0}
is closed under
\displaystyle\Sigma
.

•

Given
\displaystyle X
in
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\geq 1}
and
\displaystyle Y
in
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\leq 0}
the abelian group
\displaystyle\pi_{0}\text{Nat}(X,Y)
is zero. To see this, note that the valuewise adjunction between the inclusion of
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\geq 1}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
and
\displaystyle\tau_{\geq 1}
induces an adjunction on the functor categories. This gives the equivalence of mapping spaces (but not mapping spectra!)

\text{Nat}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\simeq\text{Nat}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,\tau_{\geq 1}Y)\simeq 0

since
\displaystyle\tau_{\geq 1}Y
is value-wise the zero object, and therefore zero in
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
.

•

For any
\displaystyle X
in
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
there is a fiber sequence

X_{1}\rightarrow X\rightarrow X_{0}

with
\displaystyle X_{1}
in
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\geq 1}
and
\displaystyle X_{0}
in
\displaystyle\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})_{\leq 0}
. To see this, note that we have a fiber sequence
\displaystyle\tau_{\geq 1}\rightarrow\text{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\tau_{\leq 0}
of functors
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. Precomposing with
\displaystyle X
gives the fiber sequence

\tau_{\geq 1}X\rightarrow X\rightarrow\tau_{\leq 0},X

which is our desired fiber sequence.

∎

Remark A.9
.

The heart of this
\displaystyle t
-structure is given as

\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}\simeq\text{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})\simeq\text{Fun}(h\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}),

where the right equivalence follows from
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
being a
\displaystyle 1
-category.

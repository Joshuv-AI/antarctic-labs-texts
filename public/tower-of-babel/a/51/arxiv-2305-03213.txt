Toric supervarieties with one odd dimension
Eric Jankowski

Abstract
. In this paper we describe the notion of a toric supervariety, generalizing that of a toric variety from the classical setting. We give a combinatorial interpretation of the category of quasinormal toric supervarieties with one odd dimension using decorated polyhedral fans. We then use this interpretation to calculate some invariants of these supervarieties and extract geometric information from them.

14A22, 14L30, 14M25, 14M30

1.
Introduction

Toric varieties form a rich and interesting class of well-behaved examples in algebraic geometry. Many important varieties are toric: affine spaces, projective spaces, Hirzebruch surfaces, and countless more. Toric varieties (especially normal ones) can be described by purely combinatorial data, a feature that allows one to quickly understand important information about a given variety without significant computation. As a result, they provide a concise dictionary between algebraic geometry and polyhedral combinatorics.

It is natural to ask to what extent this story generalizes to the super setting. Of course, one first requires the notion of an algebraic supertorus. A suitable idea arises in
[
5
]
, where it is said that a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
is
quasitoral
if
[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}_{0}]=0
. Maximal quasitoral subalgebras play the same role in the theory of Lie superalgebras that maximal toral subalgebras play in the ordinary setting. Following this analogy, an algebraic supertorus will be a supergroup whose underlying variety is a torus and whose Lie superalgebra is quasitoral.

This definition allows us to consider supervarieties with open dense orbits of supertori, which we find exhibit many similarities to classical toric varieties. One of the most important results in the classical theory gives an equivalence of categories between normal toric varieties and polyhedral fans. It is seen in this paper that a similar equivalence, using decorated fans, can be formulated in the super case with one odd dimension.

2.
Toric varieties

Our ground field is
\mathbb{C}
, although we could just as well work over any algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

2.1.
Algebraic tori

Definition 2.1
.

An
algebraic torus
is an algebraic group
T
isomorphic to some
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong\Spec\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
is the multiplicative group of the complex numbers.

A torus
T
has both a
character lattice

M=\Hom(T,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
and a
cocharacter lattice

N=\Hom(\mathbb{C}^{\times},T)
. These lattices are dual to each other, admitting a nondegenerate perfect pairing

\displaystyle\langle\,,\rangle:M\times N\to\mathbb{Z}

given by composing the morphisms of algebraic groups
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to T\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Given an arbitrary lattice
N
, one may define the torus
T_{N}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and observe that
T_{N}
has cocharacter lattice
N
and character lattice
M=N^{*}
. The character lattice
M
is naturally a basis for the regular functions on
T_{N}
, in the sense that the coordinate ring satisfies
\mathbb{C}[T_{N}]=\mathbb{C}[M]
.

If we fix an identification
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then we likewise obtain
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
T_{N}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
, so that
\mathbb{C}[T_{N}]\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},...,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.

2.2.
Affine toric varieties

Definition 2.2
.

A
toric variety
is an (irreducible) algebraic variety
X
containing a torus
T
as a Zariski open subset such that the action of
T
on itself extends to an algebraic action of
T
on the whole variety.

An affine toric variety may be constructed in a number of equivalent ways. Firstly, given a finite set
\mathscr{A}=\{m_{1},...,m_{r}\}
in a lattice
M^{\prime}
, one can define
N^{\prime}=(M^{\prime})^{*}
and subsequently a morphism of varieties

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}:T_{N^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{r}

given by
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}(t)=(t^{m_{1}},...,t^{m_{r}})
. The Zariski closure of the image
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}(T_{N^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
becomes an affine toric variety
Y_{\mathscr{A}}
with torus
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}(T_{N^{\prime}})\cong T_{(\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{A})^{*}}
.

Such subvarieties of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
may instead be characterized by their
toric ideals
, which are prime ideals of the form

(x^{\ell_{+}}-x^{\ell_{-}}\mid\ell_{+},\ell_{-}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}\text{ and }\ell_{+}-\ell_{-}\in L)

for some sublattice
L\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. Equivalence with the prior construction may be established by viewing
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
as a map
T_{N^{\prime}}\to(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
and defining
L
as the kernel of the induced map
\bar{\Phi}_{\mathscr{A}}^{*}:\mathbb{Z}^{r}\to M
on character lattices.

Equivalently, one may begin with an
affine semigroup
, i.e. a finitely generated semigroup
S
that can be embedded in a lattice. The semigroup algebra
\mathbb{C}[S]
then becomes the coordinate ring of the toric variety
\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]
whose torus is
T_{(\mathbb{Z}S)^{*}}
. If
S=\mathbb{N}\mathscr{A}
, then
\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]\cong Y_{\mathscr{A}}
.

We observe that
T_{N}
acts on monomials
x^{m}
in its coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[T_{N}]=\mathbb{C}[M]
by the corresponding characters
m\in M
. Moreover, if
\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]
is an affine toric variety with torus
T_{N}
, then
\mathbb{C}[S]
is a
T_{N}
-invariant subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[M]
.

A
toric morphism
between toric varieties is one which restricts to a group homomorphism on their underlying tori. A toric morphism between affine toric varieties
\phi:\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]\to\Spec\mathbb{C}[S^{\prime}]
is characterized by the fact that its dual map
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{C}[S^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{C}[S]
arises from a map of semigroups
S^{\prime}\to S
.

2.3.
Normal toric varieties

Definition 2.3
.

An algebraic variety is
normal
if the local ring at every point is an integrally closed domain.

Normal toric varieties can be described especially easily with combinatorial data. In order to obtain a normal affine toric variety
\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]
, one requires that the semigroup
S
be
saturated
, meaning that if
km\in S
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}S
and
k\geq 0
, then
s\in S
.

Such semigroups can also be characterized in terms of cones. Let
N
be a lattice in a real vector space
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. A
cone

\sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a nonempty subset closed under addition and multiplication by nonnegative scalars. We say
\sigma
is
polyhedral
if it is generated (under these operations) by finitely many points, and
rational
if these points can be taken to lie in
N
. Finally,
\sigma
is
strongly convex
if it contains no positive-dimensional subspace of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
M=N^{*}
be the dual lattice of
N
, so
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is the dual vector space of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. The
dual cone
of
\sigma
is the cone
\check{\sigma}=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle m,u\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }u\in\sigma\}
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. If
\sigma
is a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone, then the lattice points
\check{\sigma}\cap M
of
\check{\sigma}
form a saturated affine semigroup called
S_{\sigma}
. This construction leads to the classic characterization of normal affine toric varieties.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be an affine toric variety with torus
T_{N}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

X
is normal.

(b)

X\cong\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]
for a saturated affine semigroup
S\subseteq M=N^{*}
such that
\mathbb{Z}S=M
.

(c)

X\cong X_{\sigma}:=\Spec\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
for a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

The language of cones is a powerful one, as it allows us to translate results about toric varieties into results about convex geometry, and vice-versa. Moreover, it allows for a nice characterization of arbitrary normal toric varieties via polyhedral fans:

A
face
of the cone
\sigma
is a sub-cone
H_{m}\cap\sigma
of
\sigma
, where
H_{m}:=\ker m\cap\sigma=\{u\in\sigma\mid\langle m,u\rangle=0\}
for some
m\in\check{\sigma}
. A
fan

\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite collection of strongly convex rational polyhedral cones
\sigma
such that
\Sigma
is closed under taking faces, and the intersection of any two cones in
\Sigma
is a face of each.

One may construct a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
from a fan
\Sigma
by gluing together the open affines
X_{\sigma}
for
\sigma\in\Sigma
along their intersections (which correspond to intersections of cones). A theorem of Sumihiro
[
13
]
asserts that all normal toric varieties arise in this way.

Toric morphisms between normal toric varieties can likewise be described entirely in terms of their corresponding fans. In particular, a morphism
\phi:X_{\Sigma}\to X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
is toric if and only if it arises from a lattice map
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
that is compatible with the fans in the sense that for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there is
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
. In this sense, the category of normal toric varieties is equivalent to the category of fans.

3.
Algebraic supergeometry

We briefly introduce the basic notions of complex algebraic supergeometry that will be relevant to this paper.

3.1.
Superalgebras

Definition 3.1
.

A
superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded, unital, associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
.

If
a\in A
is homogeneous, we denote by
|a|\in\{0,1\}
the
parity
of
a
. For the purposes of this paper, all superalgebras
A
will be
commutative
in the sense that the multiplication satisfies
a\cdot b=(-1)^{|a|\cdot|b|}b\cdot a
for homogeneous elements
a\in A_{|a|}
and
b\in A_{|b|}
. We will generally use Roman letters to denote even elements and Greek letters to denote odd elements; e.g.
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}]
is a superalgebra with two even generators and three odd generators.

Morphisms of superalgebras
will always respect parity. Note that if
A
and
B
are superalgebras, then so is
A\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B
:

\displaystyle(A\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B)_{0}

\displaystyle=(A_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B_{0})\oplus(A_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B_{1})

\displaystyle(A\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B)_{1}

\displaystyle=(A_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B_{1})\oplus(A_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B_{0})

Let
A
be a commutative superalgebra. We say
A
is an
integral superdomain
if the ideal
(A_{1})
generated by
A_{1}
is a prime ideal containing all the zero divisors of
A
. It is
local
if it has a unique maximal ideal. As in the usual setting, the localization of a superalgebra at a prime ideal is local.

A
Hopf superalgebra
is a superalgebra
A
which is simultaneously a super coalgebra and is equipped with a map called the antipode, satisfying all the usual diagrams.

3.2.
Superspaces

Definition 3.2
.

A
superspace

X=(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X})
is a pair consisting of a topological space
|X|
and a sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{X}
of superalgebras such that each stalk
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
is a local superalgebra. A
morphism of superspaces
is a pair
\phi=(|\phi|,\phi^{\#}):X\to Y
for a continuous map
|\phi|:|X|\to|Y|
and a morphism of
\phi^{\#}:\mathscr{O}_{Y}\to|\phi|_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}
of sheaves on
|Y|
which becomes a local morphism of local superalgebras on each stalk.

We say that
X
is a
superscheme
if the odd part
\mathscr{O}_{X,1}
of the structure sheaf is a quasi-coherent sheaf of
\mathscr{O}_{X,0}
-modules. An
open subscheme
of
X
is given by the restriction of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
to an open set
U\subseteq|X|
, and a
closed subscheme
is an equivalence class of closed immersions in the usual sense. If
|X|
is Noetherian and irreducible, we write
\mathbb{C}(X)
for the stalk of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
at the generic point.

Let
(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})
be the ideal sheaf in
\mathscr{O}_{X}
generated by the odd elements at every open
U\subseteq|X|
. Let
\mathscr{O}_{\overline{X}}
be the sheaf of ordinary algebras defined by
\mathscr{O}_{\overline{X}}(U)=(\mathscr{O}_{X}/(\mathscr{O}_{X,1}))(U)
. We call
\overline{X}:=(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X}^{r})
the
bosonic reduction
of
X
.

Given a superalgebra
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
, we observe that
A
is an
A_{0}
-module via left multiplication, allowing us to construct the
affine superscheme

\Spec A=(|\Spec A_{0}|,\widetilde{A})
where
\widetilde{A}
is the sheaf associated to the module
A
. Letting
\overline{A}=A/(A_{1})
, we note that
|\Spec A_{0}|=|\Spec\overline{A}|
since the two algebras differ only by nilpotents. Hence
\overline{\Spec A}\cong\Spec\overline{A}
. It can be shown that affine superschemes are the local models of arbitrary superschemes (
[
1
]
, Proposition 10.1.3).

The following definition is due to Sherman
[
12
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

An
algebraic supervariety
is a superscheme
X
over
\mathbb{C}
such that all of the following conditions hold:

(i)

X
is irreducible (i.e.
|X|
is an irreducible topological space).

(ii)

X
is separated (i.e. the diagonal morphism
X\to X\times_{\mathbb{C}}X
is a closed embedding).

(iii)

X
is of finite type (i.e.
X
admits a cover by finitely many open affine subschemes
\Spec A
where the
A
are finitely generated superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
).

(iv)

For any open subscheme
U\subseteq X
, the restriction map
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)\to\mathbb{C}(X)
is injective.

(v)

\mathbb{C}(X)
is an integral superdomain.

The latter two conditions provide a weakened version of the usual integrality condition for ordinary algebraic varieties. An algebraic supervariety is moreover
integral
if (v) is strengthened to

(v
′
)

For any open subscheme
U\subseteq X
,
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)
is an integral superdomain.

Unless a supervariety
X
is integral, it is not true that its bosonic reduction
\overline{X}
is an algebraic variety (since integrality will fail). However, it is still possible to extract an ordinary algebraic variety from an arbitrary supervariety: Define an ideal sheaf
\mathscr{I}
in
\mathscr{O}_{X}
by setting
\mathscr{I}(U)
equal to the preimage of
(\mathbb{C}(X)_{1})
under the restriction map
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)\to\mathbb{C}(X)
. Then
\mathscr{I}
contains
(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})
by part (iv) of the above definition, so we may define the
underlying variety

X_{0}:=(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X}/\mathscr{I})
. To see that it is a variety, we observe that the quotient kills all zero divisors in
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)
by part (v) of the above definition. One may alternatively construct
X_{0}
as the reduced induced closed subscheme of
\overline{X}
with the same topological space
|X|
.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the subalgebra
A=\mathbb{C}[x,\xi_{1},x\xi_{2},\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]
. One can check that
X=\Spec A
is a non-integral supervariety with
\overline{X}\cong\Spec\mathbb{C}[x,\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]
and
X_{0}\cong\Spec\mathbb{C}[x]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a supervariety. The following are equivalent:

(a)

X
is affine.

(b)

\overline{X}
is affine.

(c)

X_{0}
is affine.

Proof.

See
[
14
]
for (a)
\iff
(b) and
[
7
]
, Exercise III.3.1 for (b)
\iff
(c).
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
X
be a supervariety. The following are equivalent:

(a)

X
is integral.

(b)

\overline{X}
is integral.

(c)

\overline{X}=X_{0}
.

Proof.

For (a)
\implies
(c), suppose
X
is integral. It suffices to show that
\mathscr{I}=(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})
. The inclusion
(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})\subseteq\mathscr{I}
is already known. Let
\xi\in\mathscr{I}(U)
, so
\xi^{2}=0
. Since
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U)
is integral, we have
\xi\in(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})(U)
, and so
\overline{X}=X_{0}
. Conversely, if
\overline{X}=X_{0}
(both of which are closed subschemes of
X
), then by the correspondence of closed subschemes with ideal sheaves, we obtain
\mathscr{I}=(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})
and hence
X
is integral. Therefore (a)
\iff
(c).

We also have (b)
\iff
(c) because
X_{0}
is the reduced induced closed subscheme structure on
\overline{X}
.
∎

The following definition occurs in
[
12
]
, Section 3.3.2.

Definition 3.7
.

Given an algebraic supervariety
X
, we may find a dense open subscheme
U\subseteq X
such that its bosonic reduction
\overline{U}
is smooth. The conormal sheaf
(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})/(\mathscr{O}_{X,1})^{2}
is coherent on
U
, so it trivializes over a dense open subscheme
\overline{U}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{U}
. We then define the

of

to be the dimension of
\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2}

.

The following result shows that for the purposes of this paper, the distinction between

and

Proposition 3.8
.

An
(n|1)
-dimensional supervariety is integral.

Proof.

It suffices to show for an affine supervariety
X
. We have an injection
\mathbb{C}[X]\to\mathbb{C}(X)
where
\mathbb{C}(X)_{1}^{2}=0
. Hence
(\mathbb{C}(X)_{1})=\mathbb{C}(X)_{1}
, so all zero divisors in

3.3.
Algebraic supergroups

Definition 3.9
.

An
affine algebraic supergroup
is any of the following equivalent notions:

(a)

The spectrum of a finitely-generated Hopf superalgebra
R
which is an integral superdomain

(b)

A group object
G
in the category of affine supervarieties

(c)

An affine supervariety
G=\Spec R
whose functor of points
G(A):=\Hom(R,A)
is group-valued

(d)

A pair
G=(G_{0},\mathfrak{g})
where
G_{0}
is an ordinary (irreducible) affine algebraic group,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra such that
\Lie G_{0}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and
\mathfrak{g}
is equipped with a
G_{0}
-module structure such that the corresponding
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module is adjoint (called a
Harish-Chandra pair
)

We use these notions (which can be found in
[
1
]
, Sections 7 and 11) interchangeably throughout this paper. The requirement that the Hopf superalgebra
R
of part (a) be integral is needed only to guarantee irreducibility of the supergroup. If we relax the assumption that a supervariety must be integral (and accordingly adjust for the possibility of multiple generic points), then this requirement may be dropped, and definitions (a)-(d) remain equivalent.

The notation in the definition of a Harish-Chandra pair is intentional, as
G_{0}=\overline{G}
is the underlying algebraic group of
G
and
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie superalgebra (i.e. tangent space at the identity) of
G
. To see the equivalence of definition (a) with definitions (b) and (c), we note the following:

Proposition 3.10
.

An affine algebraic supergroup is an integral supervariety.

Proof.

Using definition (b), consider the bosonic reduction
\overline{G}
, which is an affine group scheme satisfying all properties of an algebraic variety, except possibly reducedness. But any group scheme over a field of characteristic 0 is reduced (
[
10
]
, I, Theorem 1.1 and I, Corollary 3.9 and II, Theorem 2.4), so indeed
\overline{G}
is integral. We are therefore finished by Proposition
3.6
.
∎

An
action
of an algebraic supergroup on a supervariety is defined as a morphism

\rho:G\times X\to X

satisfying the usual properties.

4.
Algebraic supertori

Definition 4.1
.

An
algebraic supertorus
is an algebraic supergroup
T=(T_{0},\mathfrak{t})
such that
T_{0}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
is an ordinary algebraic torus lying in the center of
T
.

This definition is motivated by the theory of Cartan subalgebras of quasireductive Lie superalgebras as in
[
11
]
. A
quasireductive
Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is one whose even part
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is reductive. In this setting, one chooses a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and defines
\mathfrak{h}
as the centralizer of
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus, an algebraic supertorus is an algebraic supergroup
T
such that
T_{0}
is a torus and
\Lie T
is a Cartan subalgebra of a quasireductive Lie superalgebra. The representation theory of these superalgebras (and thus of algebraic supertori) has been studied in
[
5
]
.

Example 4.2
.

Define
Q(1)
as the supergroup whose
A
-points consist of matrices

\begin{pmatrix}a&\xi\\
\xi&a\end{pmatrix}

for
a\in A_{0}^{\times}
and
\xi\in A_{1}
, equipped with the usual matrix multiplication. Then
Q(1)
is a
(1|1)
-dimensional supertorus.

4.1.
Supergroup structure

If
\dim T
=
(n|1)
, then
\mathfrak{t}
is an
(n|1)
-dimensional Lie superalgebra with
\mathfrak{t}_{0}\subset Z(\mathfrak{t})
and an odd generator
\theta
such that
\theta^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta]\in\mathfrak{t}_{0}
. An
(n|1)
-dimensional supertorus is therefore parameterized by a point
c\in\mathfrak{t}_{0}\cong N_{\mathbb{C}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
, where
N
is the cocharacter lattice of
T_{0}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Denote by
T_{N}
the ordinary algebraic torus with character lattice
N
, and
T_{N,c}=(T_{N},\mathfrak{t})
the supertorus with one odd dimension corresponding to the parameter
c=\theta^{2}\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
M=N^{*}
be the character lattice of
T_{N}
. The supertorus
T_{N,c}
will have coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[T_{N,c}]=\mathbb{C}[M,\xi]
, with a Hopf superalgebra structure

\displaystyle\Delta x^{m}

\displaystyle=x^{m}\otimes x^{m}(1+\langle m,c\rangle\xi\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle Sx^{m}

\displaystyle=x^{-m}

\displaystyle\varepsilon x^{m}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\Delta\xi

\displaystyle=\xi\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi

\displaystyle S\xi

\displaystyle=-\xi

\displaystyle\varepsilon\xi

\displaystyle=0

where
\langle\,,\rangle
is the perfect pairing
M\otimes N\to\mathbb{Z}
, extended
\mathbb{C}
-linearly to
M\otimes N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Fixing an identification
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
so that
c=(c_{1},...,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, write
T_{N,c}(A)=\Hom(\Spec A,T_{N,c})
for the functor of points of
T
evaluated on a superalgebra
A
. Then the group law of
T_{N,c}(A)
is given by

\displaystyle(t_{1},...,t_{n}\mid\xi)\cdot(u_{1},...,u_{n}\mid\eta)

\displaystyle=(t_{1}u_{1}(1+c_{1}\xi\eta),...,t_{n}u_{n}(1+c_{n}\xi\eta)\mid\xi+\eta)

for
t_{i},u_{i}\in(A_{0})^{\times}
and
\xi,\eta\in A_{1}
.

4.2.
Representation theory

The coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[T_{N,c}]
admits an action by
\mathfrak{t}
, wherein the even part acts as usual (fixing
\xi
) and the odd generator acts by
x^{m}\xi\mapsto x^{m}
and
x^{m}\mapsto\langle m,c\rangle x^{m}\xi
. Using the above identification
T_{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we may write
\mathfrak{t}
as the subalgebra

\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\left(x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},...,x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)\oplus\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\xi\left(c_{1}x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+...+c_{n}x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)

of the Lie superalgebra of derivations on
\mathbb{C}[T_{N,c}]\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},...,x_{n}^{\pm 1},\xi]
.
Thus, as a representation of
T_{N,c}
,
\mathbb{C}[T_{N,c}]
splits into a direct sum of
(1|1)
-dimensional representations
L(m)=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}(x^{m},x^{m}\xi)
for
m\in M
.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
T=T_{N,c}
, then
L(m)
is irreducible if and only if
\langle m,c\rangle\neq 0
. If
\langle m,c\rangle=0
, then
L(m)
is indecomposable but has a
(1|0)
-dimensional subrepresentation
\langle x^{m}\rangle
.

Proof.

The even part preserves the span of each monomial, and the odd part acts as above.
∎

4.3.
Decomposability

Under an identification
T_{N}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
as above, we obtain
c=(c_{1},...,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Without loss of generality, arrange the indices so that

c=(c_{1,1},...,c_{1,k_{1}},...,c_{r,1},...,c_{r,k_{r}},0,...,0)

where for each
i
,
\{c_{i,1},...,c_{i,k_{i}}\}
is a maximal collection of nonzero rational multiples of an element of
\mathbb{C}
. That is, we may assume
c_{i,j}=\ell_{i,j}c_{i}
for some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\ell_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Since the
\GL(n,\mathbb{Z})
-orbit of a point in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
consists of all
n
-tuples with the same least common multiple, there exists
g_{i}\in GL(k_{i},\mathbb{Z})
such that
g(\ell_{i,1},...,\ell_{i,k_{i}})=(\ell_{i},0,...,0)
for some
\ell_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
g=(g_{1},...,g_{r})\in\GL(k_{1},\mathbb{Z})\times...\times\GL(k_{r},\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\GL(n,\mathbb{Z})
, then (up to re-ordering the entries) we obtain

gc=(c_{1},...,c_{r},0,...,0)

for some
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent elements
c_{1},...,c_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
. It follows that

T_{N,c}\cong T_{\mathbb{Z}^{r},(c_{1},...,c_{r})}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-r}.

Definition 4.5
.

We say a supertorus
T
(of any dimension) is
indecomposable
if it cannot be written as a product
T^{\prime}\times T^{\prime\prime}
of two smaller supertori.

We have seen that if the entries of
c=(c_{1},...,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
admit a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear dependency, then
T_{\mathbb{Z}^{n},c}
is decomposable. The following proposition shows that this condition exactly determines indecomposability of a supertorus with one odd dimension.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
T=T_{\mathbb{Z}^{n},c}
be a supertorus such that
c_{1},...,c_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent. Then
T
is indecomposable.

Proof.

Suppose
T=T^{\prime}\times T^{\prime\prime}
. Since
T
has only one odd dimension, we may assume without loss of generality that
T^{\prime\prime}
is a purely even torus of nonzero dimension. Hence
T^{\prime}=T_{\mathbb{Z}^{r},c^{\prime}}
for some
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
, and
T^{\prime\prime}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-r}
. Then
c=(c_{1}^{\prime},...,c_{r}^{\prime},0,...,0)
is a contradiction which proves the result.
∎

4.4.
Morphisms

We will need the following lemmas on morphisms between supertori.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\phi:T\to T^{\prime}
be a morphism of supergroups, where
T
and
T^{\prime}
are both supertori. Then the image of
\phi
is a supertorus which is closed in
T^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We may view the morphism as living in the category of Harish-Chandra pairs, so that we have a morphism
\phi_{0}:T_{0}\to T_{0}^{\prime}
of algebraic groups and a compatible morphism
d\phi:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
of Lie superalgebras. By the classical fact (
[
2
]
, Proposition 1.1.1),
\phi_{0}(T_{0})
is a closed subtorus of
T_{0}^{\prime}
. Moreover,
d\phi(\mathfrak{t})
is a Lie superalgebra whose even part is the Lie algebra of
\phi_{0}(T_{0})
. Since
t_{0}^{\prime}
lies in the center of
t^{\prime}
, the same holds for
\Lie\phi_{0}(T_{0})\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{\prime}
in
d\phi(\mathfrak{t})\subseteq\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
, so the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\phi:T_{N,c}\to T_{N^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
be a morphism of supervarieties. Then
\phi
is a morphism of supergroups if and only if all of the following conditions hold.

(i)

There is a lattice map
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
such that
\phi|_{T_{N}}=\bar{\phi}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
.

(ii)

The dual map
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{C}[M^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{C}[M,\xi]
satisfies
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})=a\xi
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
.

(iii)

\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}=\bar{\phi}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}}:N_{\mathbb{C}}\to N^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
\bar{\phi}(c)=a^{2}c^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Condition (i) is required as in the usual setting. In order for
\phi
to be a morphism of supergroups, we require
\phi^{*}
to be a morphism of Hopf superalgebras. In particular, we require
(\phi^{*}\otimes\phi^{*})\circ\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta\circ\phi^{*}
. Since each of these maps is a morphism of superalgebras, it suffices to check the identity on the generators
x^{m^{\prime}}
and
\xi^{\prime}
. First, observe that

\displaystyle(\phi^{*}\otimes\phi^{*})\circ\Delta^{\prime}(\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Delta\circ\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(\phi^{*}\otimes\phi^{*})(\xi^{\prime}\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Delta(\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})\otimes 1+1\otimes\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}\Delta(x^{k_{i}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}x^{k_{i}}\xi\right)\otimes 1+1\otimes\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}x^{k_{i}}\xi\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}x^{k_{i}}\otimes x^{k_{i}}(\xi\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi)

where we write
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}x^{k_{i}}\xi
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
and
k_{i}\in M
.
Since the two sides are equal, it follows that all
k_{i}=0
and hence
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})=a\xi
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, for
m^{\prime}\in M
, we have

\displaystyle(\phi^{*}\otimes\phi^{*})\circ\Delta^{\prime}(x^{m^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\Delta\circ\phi^{*}(x^{m^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=(\phi^{*}\otimes\phi^{*})(x^{m^{\prime}}\otimes x^{m^{\prime}}(1+\langle m^{\prime},c^{\prime}\rangle)\xi^{\prime}\otimes\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Delta(x^{\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime})})

\displaystyle=x^{\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime})}\otimes x^{\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime})}(1+\langle m^{\prime},c^{\prime}\rangle\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})\otimes\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=x^{\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime})}\otimes x^{\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime})}(1+\langle\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime}),c\rangle\xi\otimes\xi)

so that
a^{2}\langle m^{\prime},c^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime}),c\rangle
for all
m^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
. Since
\langle\,,\rangle
is a nondegenerate perfect pairing, we know
\langle\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime}),c\rangle=\langle m^{\prime},\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}(c)\rangle
and hence
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}(c)=a^{2}c^{\prime}
. The other axioms of a Hopf algebra morphism are routine to check, and yield no additional requirements.
∎

4.5.
The moduli space

One can verify that isomorphism classes of
(n|1)
-dimensional supertori for
c\neq 0
depend only on the line through
c\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
, up to automorphisms
\GL(T_{N})
of the torus
T_{N}
. That is, the moduli space of
(n|1)
-dimensional supertori is the disjoint union of
\mathbb{P}(N_{\mathbb{C}})/\GL(T_{N})\cong\mathbb{P}^{n-1}/\GL(n,\mathbb{Z})
and a point (representing the
c=0
case).

4.6.
Supertori with higher odd dimension

If
T=(T_{N},\mathfrak{t})
is a supertorus with
\mathfrak{t}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}(x_{1},...,x_{n})\oplus\Span_{\mathbb{C}}(\theta_{1},...,\theta_{s})
, then its supergroup structure is determined entirely by the parameters
c_{ij}\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
representing the coefficients of
\frac{1}{2}[\theta_{i},\theta_{j}]=(c_{ij})_{1}x_{1}+...+(c_{ij})_{n}x_{n}
. Since
[\cdot,\cdot]
is symmetric on
\mathfrak{t}_{1}
, the
c_{ij}
arrange themselves into a symmetric
s\times s
matrix (with entries in
N_{\mathbb{C}}
).

Definition 4.9
.

Let
N
be a lattice, and
(c_{ij})
a symmetric square matrix with entries in
N_{\mathbb{C}}
. Denote by
T_{N,(c_{ij})}
the supertorus with the given parameters.

The supertorus
T_{N,(c_{ij})}
has coordinate Hopf superalgeba
\mathbb{C}[M,\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]
where
M=N^{*}
and
s
is the order of the matrix
(c_{ij})
. The Hopf algebra structure is given by

\displaystyle\Delta x^{m}

\displaystyle=x^{m}\otimes x^{m}\left(1+\sum_{i,j=1}^{s}\langle m,c_{ij}\rangle\xi_{i}\otimes\xi_{j}\right)

\displaystyle Sx^{m}

\displaystyle=x^{-m}

\displaystyle\varepsilon x^{m}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\Delta\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=\xi_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi_{i}

\displaystyle S\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=-\xi_{i}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=0

and likewise the group law on
A
-points is given by

\displaystyle(t_{1},...,t_{n}\mid\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s})\cdot(u_{1},...,u_{n}\mid\eta_{1},...,\eta_{s})

\displaystyle=\left(t_{1}u_{1}\left(1+\sum_{i,j=1}^{s}(c_{ij})_{1}\xi_{i}\eta_{j}\right),...,t_{n}u_{n}\left(1+\sum_{i,j=1}^{s}(c_{ij})_{n}\xi_{i}\eta_{j}\right)\;\Bigg|\;\xi_{1}+\eta_{1},...,\xi_{s}+\eta_{s}\right)

for
t_{i},u_{i}\in(A_{0})^{\times}
and
\xi_{,}\eta_{i}\in A_{1}
.

The following lemma generalizes Lemma
4.8
, and can be proven in a virtually identical way.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\phi:T_{N,(c_{ij})}\to T_{N^{\prime},(c_{ij}^{\prime})}
be a morphism of supervarieties. Then
\phi
is a morphism of supergroups if and only if all of the following conditions hold.

(i)

There is a lattice map
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
such that
\phi|_{T_{N}}=\bar{\phi}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
.

(ii)

The dual map
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{C}[M^{\prime},\xi_{1}^{\prime},...,\xi_{s^{\prime}}^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{C}[M,\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]
satisfies
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime}_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{ij}\xi_{j}
for some
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
.

(iii)

\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}=\bar{\phi}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}}:N_{\mathbb{C}}\to N_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}
satisfies
\bar{\phi}(c_{k\ell})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{s^{\prime}}a_{ik}a_{j\ell}c_{ij}^{\prime}
for all
k,\ell=1,...,s
.

If we define a
supertorus datum
as a tuple
(N,(c_{ij})_{i,j=1,...,s})
, then we can formulate a category of supertorus data with the above objects, and morphisms
(\phi,(a_{ij})_{i=1,...,s^{\prime};\,j=1,...,s}):(N,(c_{ij}))\to(N^{\prime},(c_{ij}^{\prime}))
as in the above lemma. Then the lemma yields the following result:

Proposition 4.11
.

The category of algebraic supertori is equivalent to the category of supertorus data.

5.
Affine toric supervarieties

Definition 5.1
.

A
toric supervariety
is a supervariety
X
containing a supertorus
T
as an open subscheme such that the action of
T
on itself extends to an action of
T
on the whole supervariety.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a toric supervariety with torus
T
. Then the underlying variety
X_{0}
is a toric variety with torus
T_{0}
.

Proof.

The action of
T_{0}
on
X
preserves the defining ideal
\mathscr{I}
of
X_{0}
.
∎

In this paper, we will assume
X
is
(n|1)
-dimensional, so by Proposition
3.8
we may use
X_{0}
and
\overline{X}
interchangeably. We will begin by assuming
X
is affine, which by Proposition
3.5
is equivalent to the assumption that
X_{0}
is affine.

5.1.
From a finite set in a monoid

Given a supertorus
T^{\prime}=T_{N^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
, let
M^{\prime}=(N^{\prime})^{*}
be the character lattice of
T_{0}^{\prime}=T_{N^{\prime}}
and
\mathscr{A}=\{m_{1},...,m_{r}\}
a finite set in
M^{\prime}
. We wish to construct a super-analog of the variety
Y_{\mathscr{A}}
described in section
2.2
. Define

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\prime}=\mathscr{B}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{m_{i}\in\mathscr{A}\mid\langle m_{i},c^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0\}=\{m_{1},...,m_{q}\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{m_{i}\in\mathscr{A}\mid\langle m_{i},c^{\prime}\rangle=0\}=\{m_{q+1},...,m_{r}\}

so that
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
. Moreover, let
\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}=\{n_{q+1},...,n_{s}\}
be a finite set in the monoid
\mathbb{N}\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
, and set
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}
. (We assume henceforth that
\mathscr{B}
is nonempty so that we do not degenerate to the classical, purely-even case.) We will write
n_{j}=m_{j}
for
j=1,...,q
so that
\mathscr{B}=\{n_{1},...,n_{s}\}
, and
n_{j}=\sum_{i=q+1}^{r}p_{i}^{j}m_{i}
for
j=q+1,...,s
for some
p_{i}^{j}\in\mathbb{N}
. We can do the same for
j=1,...,q
, in which case we have
p_{i}^{j}=1
if
i=j
and
p_{i}^{j}=0
otherwise.

Now define a morphism of supervarieties

\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}:T^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}^{r|s}

at the level of
A
-points (for
A
a superalgebra) by

\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(t\mid\xi)=(t^{m_{1}},...,t^{m_{r}}\;|\;t^{n_{1}}\xi,...,t^{n_{s}}\xi)\in\mathbb{C}^{r|s}(A)

for
t\in((A_{0})^{\times})^{\dim N^{\prime}}
and
\xi\in A_{1}
. Denote by
J\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]
the ideal of functions vanishing on the image of
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
. Then the Zariski closure of the image is defined as the closed subvariety
V(J):=\Spec\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]/J
of
\mathbb{C}^{r|s}
, henceforth referred to as
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Given
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}\subseteq M
as above,
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
is an affine toric supervariety whose supertorus is
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})=T_{(\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{A})^{*},\bar{\Phi}_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(c^{\prime})}
, where
\bar{\Phi}_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(c^{\prime})
is defined in as in Lemma
4.8
.

Proof.

Let
T^{\prime\prime}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}\times\mathbb{C}^{0|s}\subset\mathbb{C}^{r|s}
as a supervariety without any assumption of a supergroup structure. We can view
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
as a map of supergroups
T^{\prime}\to T^{\prime\prime}
in the following way: We may freely change the underlying classical variety of the codomain to
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
. Now it remains to impose a supertorus structure on
T^{\prime\prime}
, so let
A
be a superalgebra and let
t,u\in((A_{0})^{\times})^{\dim N^{\prime}}
and
\xi,\eta\in A_{1}
. If
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
is to be a group homomorphism, we must have

\displaystyle(t^{m_{1}},...,t^{m_{r}}\mid t^{n_{1}}\xi,...,t^{n_{s}}\xi)\cdot(u^{m_{1}},...,u^{m_{r}}\mid u^{n_{1}}\eta,...,u^{n_{s}}\eta)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(t\mid\xi)\cdot\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(u\mid\eta)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}((t\mid\xi)\cdot(u\mid\eta))

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(t_{1}u_{1}(1+c_{1}^{\prime}\xi\eta),...,t_{n}u_{n}(1+c_{n}^{\prime}\xi\eta)\mid\xi+\eta)

\displaystyle=\left(t^{m_{1}}u^{m_{1}}(1+\langle m_{1},c^{\prime}\rangle\xi\eta),...,t^{m_{r}}u^{m_{r}}(1+\langle m_{r},c^{\prime}\rangle\xi\eta)\mid t^{n_{1}}u^{n_{1}}(\xi+\eta),...,t^{n_{s}}u^{n_{s}}(\xi+\eta)\right)

where we identify
T_{0}^{\prime}
with
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
and
M^{\prime}
with
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Thus, a potential group law on
T^{\prime\prime}(A)
is

(a_{1},...,a_{r}\mid\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{s})\cdot(b_{1},...,b_{r}\mid\beta_{1},...,\beta_{s})=(d_{1},...,d_{r}\mid\delta_{1},...,\delta_{s})

where

\displaystyle d_{i}

\displaystyle=a_{i}b_{i}+\langle m_{i},c^{\prime}\rangle\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\text{for }i=1,...,q

\displaystyle d_{i}

\displaystyle=a_{i}b_{i}

\displaystyle\text{for }i=q+1,...,r

\displaystyle\delta_{j}

\displaystyle=b^{p^{j}}\alpha_{j}+a^{p^{j}}\beta_{j}

\displaystyle\text{for }j=1,...,s.

For
j=1,...,q
, the latter simplifies to

\displaystyle\delta_{j}

\displaystyle=b_{j}\alpha_{j}+a_{j}\beta_{j}.

It is routine to check that this operation makes
T^{\prime\prime}(A)
into a group with identity element
(1,...,1\mid 0,...,0)
, and moreover
T^{\prime\prime}
into an
(r|s)
-dimensional supertorus which decomposes as
Q(1)^{q}\times T^{\prime\prime\prime}
for some
(r-q|s-q)
-dimensional supertorus
T^{\prime\prime\prime}
.

It follows by Lemma
4.7
that
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
is a supertorus closed in
T^{\prime\prime}
, so
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}\cap T^{\prime\prime}=\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
because
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
is the closure of
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
. As in the classical setting,
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
is open in
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
and irreducible, so
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
is also irreducible.

We now verify that
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
acts on
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
via the functor of points. Since
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})\subset T^{\prime\prime}
, the action is by homeomorphisms and so takes supervarieties to supervarieties. For a superalgebra
A
, it follows that
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})(A)=(t,\xi)\cdot\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})(A)\subseteq(t,\xi)\cdot Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(A)
for
t\in(A_{0}^{*})^{\dim N^{\prime}},\xi\in A_{1}
. Hence
(t,\xi)\cdot Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(A)
is the functor of points of a supervariety containing
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
, meaning
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(A)\subseteq(t,\xi)\cdot Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(A)
by definition of the Zariski closure. Replacing
(t,\xi)
with its inverse
(t^{-1},-\xi)
yields the reverse inclusion, so in fact
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(A)=(t,\xi)Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(A)
. Therefore
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
is an affine toric supervariety with torus
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
.

As in the usual setting, the even part
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})_{0}
of the torus will have character lattice
\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{A}
. Since we assume
s>0
, we also have one dimension of odd functions (generated by
\xi
). Viewing
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
as a morphism
T^{\prime}\to\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
, we see that
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}^{*}(\xi)=\xi
and hence the additional parameter
c
is
\bar{\Phi}_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(c^{\prime})
by Lemma
4.8
.
∎

5.2.
The ideal of an affine toric supervariety

As in the usual case, we are interested in describing the ideal
J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})
of functions that vanish on
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
. The map
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
can be viewed as a map to an
(r|s)
-dimensional torus, inducing a morphism of lattices

\hat{\Phi}_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}:\mathbb{Z}^{r|s}\to M^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{0|1}

mapping the coordinate vectors of the domain as

\displaystyle e_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto(m_{i}\mid 0)

\displaystyle i=1,...,r

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mapsto(n_{j}\mid 1)

\displaystyle j=1,...,s.

The kernel
L
of this morphism records the relations among the
m_{i}
and
n_{j}
, so if
(\ell\mid\ell^{\prime})\in L
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ell_{i}(m_{i}\mid 0)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\ell_{j}^{\prime}(n_{j}\mid 1)=0
. Notice that
\sum_{j=1}^{s}\ell_{j}^{\prime}=0
due to the “odd” coordinate.

For
\ell=(\ell_{1},...,\ell_{r}\mid\ell_{1}^{\prime},...,\ell_{s}^{\prime})\in L
, set

\displaystyle\ell_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{i}>0}\ell_{i}e_{i}

\displaystyle\ell_{-}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell_{i}<0}\ell_{i}e_{i}

\displaystyle\ell_{+}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{j}^{\prime}>0}\ell_{j}^{\prime}e_{j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ell_{-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell_{j}^{\prime}<0}\ell_{j}^{\prime}e_{j}^{\prime}

so that
\ell=(\ell_{+}-\ell_{-})+(\ell_{+}^{\prime}-\ell_{-}^{\prime})
and
\ell_{\pm}\in\mathbb{N}^{r},\ell_{\pm}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
. It follows immediately that the binomial

\displaystyle x^{\ell_{+}}\xi^{\ell_{+}^{\prime}}-x^{\ell_{-}}\xi^{\ell_{-}^{\prime}}

vanishes on
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(T^{\prime})
and hence also on its closure
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
. Since each
\xi_{i}
is sent to a multiple of
\xi
, the monomials
\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
also vanish on
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
. Thus, it is sufficient to consider only those binomials which contain either no
\xi_{j}
or exactly one in each term.

Proposition 5.4
.

The ideal of the affine toric supervariety
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r|s}
is

\displaystyle J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})

\displaystyle=(x^{\ell_{+}}\xi^{\ell_{+}^{\prime}}-x^{\ell_{-}}\xi^{\ell_{-}^{\prime}}\mid\ell\in L)+(\xi_{i}\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=(x^{k_{+}}\xi^{k_{+}^{\prime}}-x^{k_{-}}\xi^{k_{-}^{\prime}}\mid k_{\pm}\in\mathbb{N}^{r},k_{\pm}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}\text{ and }k_{+}-k_{-}+k_{+}^{\prime}-k_{-}^{\prime}\in L)+(\xi_{i}\xi_{j})

Proof.

The proof is largely the same as in the classical case: Firstly, the two ideals on the right are equal by an identical proof, and we let
J_{L}\subseteq J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})
denote this ideal. Fixing identifications
T_{0}^{\prime}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
and
M^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the map
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}:T^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}^{r|s}
is given by monomials
t^{m_{i}}
for
i=1,...,r
and
t^{n_{j}}\xi
for
j=1,...,s
in the variables
t_{1},...,t_{n},\xi
.

Now pick a monomial order
\geq
on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]
, say lexicographic order with
x_{1}>...>x_{r}>\xi_{1}>...>\xi_{s}
. While this is not precisely a monomial order in the traditional sense due to the presence of odd variables, we will not invoke any properties that do not hold in this situation. If
J_{L}\neq J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})
, we can pick
f\in J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})\backslash J_{L}
with minimal leading monomial
x^{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
or
x^{a}\xi_{j}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{a_{i}}\right)\xi_{j}
. We may assume without loss of generality that
f
has leading coefficient 1, and that
f
contains no summands in
(\xi_{i}\xi_{j})
.

Since
f(t^{m_{1}},...,t^{m_{r}},t^{n_{1}}\xi,...,t^{n_{s}}\xi)\equiv 0
as a polynomial in
t_{1},...,t_{n},\xi
and
f
contains no summands in
(\xi_{i}\xi_{j})
, there must be cancellation involving the leading term. Hence
f(x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s})
contains a monomial
x^{b}<x^{a}
or
x^{b}\xi_{k}<x^{a}\xi_{j}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t^{m_{i}})^{a_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t^{m_{i}})^{b_{i}}\quad\quad\text{or}\quad\quad\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t^{m_{i}})^{a_{i}}\right)t^{n_{j}}\xi=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t^{m_{i}})^{b_{i}}\right)t^{n_{k}}\xi,

implying

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}m_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}m_{i}\quad\quad\text{or}\quad\quad\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}m_{i}+n_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}m_{i}+n_{k}.

Therefore
a-b\in L
or
(a+e_{j}^{\prime})-(b+e_{k}^{\prime})\in L
, so by the second description of
J_{L}
it follows that
f-x^{a}+x^{b}
or
f-x^{a}\xi_{j}+x^{b}\xi_{k}
also lies in
J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})\backslash J_{L}
but has strictly smaller leading term. With this contradiction, we are finished.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

In the classical setting, there is a nice classification of affine toric varieties via toric ideals (i.e. prime ideals generated by binomials arising from a lattice as above). A super-analog of this result is possible but rather less interesting, since it fails to describe the torus action on the corresponding supervariety, and in particular the parameter
c\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
. Additionally, our constraint of one odd dimension makes the description quite cumbersome.

5.3.
From an affine semigroup

Recall from section
2.2
that an affine toric variety is isomorphic to
\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]
for some affine semigroup
S
, and every
\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]
has the structure of an affine toric variety. The generalization to the super case with one odd dimension requires the same data with one minor addition.

Let
T_{N}
be the ordinary torus with character lattice
N^{*}=M=\mathbb{Z}S
. For
c\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
, let
J_{c}
be the
T_{N}
-invariant ideal
(S-\ker c)=(x^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[S]\mid\langle m,c\rangle\neq 0)
of
\mathbb{C}[S]
, where
S-\ker c
denotes the set difference. Note that
J_{c}\neq 0
unless
c=0
.

Lemma 5.6
.

(a)

Let
S
be an affine semigroup, and let
J\subseteq\mathbb{C}[S]
be a nonzero
T_{N}
-invariant ideal that contains
J_{c}
. Then
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[S]\oplus\xi J)
is an affine toric supervariety whose torus is
T_{N,c}
.

(b)

The ideal
J
has a finite generating set consisting of monic monomials. Moreover, there is a minimal generating set of this type, unique up to multiplication by invertible monomials in
\mathbb{C}[S]
. In particular, if
S
is a pointed semigroup (i.e. the only unit is 1), then there is a unique minimal generating set.

(c)

Suppose
S=\mathbb{N}\mathscr{A}
for a finite set
\mathscr{A}
in a lattice
M^{\prime}
(
\supset M=\mathbb{Z}S
) such that
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
as above where
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}=\{m\in\mathscr{A}\mid\langle m,c\rangle_{M\times N_{\mathbb{C}}}=0\}
. Let
\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}
be a finite set in
\mathbb{N}\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
such that
J=(x^{n_{j}}\mid n_{j}\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime})
. Then
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[S]\oplus\xi J)\cong Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
.

Proof.

(a)

Follows from (c) and Proposition
5.3
.

(b)

Since
J
is
T_{N}
-invariant, it has a generating set by monic monomials. For finiteness, notice that
\mathbb{C}[S]
is a quotient of some
\mathbb{C}[y_{1},...,y_{k}]
, which is Noetherian. It therefore remains to prove uniqueness.

Let
\mathscr{S}_{1}
and
\mathscr{S}_{2}
be two generating sets by monic monomials, and let
x^{m_{1}}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}\backslash\mathscr{S}_{2}
. Since
x^{m_{1}}\in J
, we must have
x^{m_{1}}=ax^{m_{2}}
for some monomial
a\in\mathbb{C}[S]
and
m_{2}\in\mathscr{S}_{2}
. Since
x^{m_{2}}\in J
, we must have
x^{m_{2}}=bx^{m_{3}}
for some monomial
b\in\mathbb{C}[S]
and
m_{3}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}
. Hence
x^{m_{1}}=abx^{m_{3}}
and so either
m_{1}=m_{3}
or
x^{m_{1}}
is redundant in
S_{1}
. In the event that
m_{1}=m_{3}
, it holds that
a=b^{-1}
, so
x^{m_{1}}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}
and
x^{m_{2}}\in\mathscr{S}_{2}
differ only by an invertible monomial.

(c)

We first observe that the defining condition
\langle m,c\rangle_{M\times N_{\mathbb{C}}}=0
of
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
is equivalent to
\langle m,c^{\prime}\rangle_{M^{\prime}\times N_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}}=0
because
M=\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{A}
is a sublattice of
M^{\prime}
via
\bar{\phi}^{*}:M\to M^{\prime}
and likewise
N
is a quotient of
N^{\prime}
via
\bar{\phi}:N^{\prime}\to N
.

The morphism
\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}:T_{(M^{\prime})^{*},c^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{C}^{r|s}
corresponds to a map

\pi:\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]\to\mathbb{C}[M^{\prime},\xi]

given by
x_{i}\mapsto x^{m_{i}}
and
\xi_{j}\mapsto x^{n_{j}}\xi
. The image of
\pi
is exactly
\mathbb{C}[S]\oplus\xi J
because
J
is the ideal generated by the
x^{n_{j}}
. Hence, if we can show that
\ker\pi
is the ideal
J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[S]\oplus\xi J

\displaystyle=\Im\pi

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]/\ker\pi

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]/J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}]

and so (c) will hold. But this follows by Proposition
5.4
. ∎

The condition that
J
is nonzero is required only in the case
c=0
, in order to ensure that the resulting supervariety is not purely even.

Example 5.7
.

If
S=\mathbb{N}
and
c=1
, then we obtain the supertorus
T_{\mathbb{Z},1}\cong Q(1)
and the ordinary toric variety
\Spec\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}]\cong\Spec\mathbb{C}[x]\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}
. We observe that
J_{c}=(x)
, so
\Spec\mathbb{C}[x,x\xi]
and
\Spec\mathbb{C}[x,\xi]
are two toric supervarieties with torus
Q(1)
. While these two spaces are isomorphic as supervarieties, they admit different torus actions and are in fact not isomorphic as toric supervarieties. For more on morphisms of toric supervarieties, see Section
5.5
.

The data of the ideal
J
is equivalent to the data of its minimal generating set by monomials, which can be characterized as follows.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
S
be an affine semigroup and
T_{(\mathbb{Z}S)^{*},c}
a supertorus. For
m,m^{\prime}\in S
, we write
m^{\prime}\leq_{S}m
if there exists
m^{\prime\prime}\in S
such that
m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}=m
. A nonempty finite set
\mathscr{B}\subset S
is
c
-admissible
if:

(i)

For all
m\in S-\ker c
, there is
b\in\mathscr{B}
such that
b\leq_{S}m
.

It is moreover
minimal
if:

(ii)

If
a,b\in\mathscr{B}
satisfy
a\leq_{S}b
, then
a=b
.

Lemma 5.9
.

The nonempty finite set
\mathscr{B}\subset S
is (minimal)
c
-admissible if and only if it is a (minimal) generating set for a nonzero ideal
J\subseteq\mathbb{C}[S]
containing
J_{c}
.

Proof.

Condition (1) is equivalent to the ideal generated containing
J_{c}
, and condition (2) is equivalent to minimality.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
X
be an affine supervariety. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

X
is an affine toric supervariety whose torus is
T=T_{N,c}
.

(b)

X=Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
for a finite set
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
in a lattice and
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}
nonempty where
\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}
is a finite set in
\mathbb{N}\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}=\{m\in\mathscr{A}\mid\langle m,c\rangle=0\}
.

(c)

X=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[S]\oplus\xi J)
for an affine semigroup
S
and a nonzero
T_{N}
-invariant ideal
J\subseteq\mathbb{C}[S]
that contains
J_{c}
.

(d)

X=\Spec\mathbb{C}[S,\xi\mathscr{B}]
for an affine semigroup
S
and an
c
-admissible set
\mathscr{B}\subset S
.

Proof.

We have already seen that (d)
\iff
(c)
\iff
(b)
\implies
(a). Now suppose (a) holds, so
X
is an affine toric supervariety containing the supertorus
T
. The inclusion
T\hookrightarrow X
induces a map of coordinate rings

\mathbb{C}[X]\to\mathbb{C}[T]

which is injective by part (iv) of Definition
3.3
since
T
is an open subscheme of the supervariety
X
. Thus we may view
\mathbb{C}[X]
as a subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[T]
and in particular
\mathbb{C}[X_{0}]
as a subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[T_{0}]
. From our knowledge of the usual case, this implies
\mathbb{C}[X_{0}]=\mathbb{C}[X]_{0}=\mathbb{C}[S]
for an affine semigroup
S
.

Since
T
acts on
X
, it also acts on
\mathbb{C}[X]
, so that
\mathbb{C}[X]
is a subrepresentation of
\mathbb{C}[T]
. For each monomial
x^{m}\xi\in\mathbb{C}[X]
, it holds that
x^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[X]
as well, so
\mathbb{C}[X]_{1}=\xi J
for some
T_{N}
-invariant ideal
J\subset\mathbb{C}[S]
. If
L(m)
is irreducible (i.e. if
\langle m,c\rangle\neq 0
), then
x^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[X]
implies
x^{m}\xi\in\mathbb{C}[X]
, meaning
J
must contain
J_{c}
. Therefore (c) follows from (a).
∎

Remark 5.11
.

The
c
-admissible set
\mathscr{B}
can be taken as the finite set
\mathscr{B}
occurring in
Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
, but it is not in general minimal: Consider the supertorus
Q(1)\cong T_{\mathbb{Z},1}\cong\Spec\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1},\xi]
as a toric supervariety containing itself. Here, we may take
S=\mathbb{Z}
, so that
X\cong Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
for
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}=\{\pm 1\}
because
\mathscr{B}
must contain
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}-\ker c
. However, such
\mathscr{B}
cannot be minimal, since
1
and
-1
generate the same ideal of
\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
.

Example 5.12
.

For every affine toric variety
X_{0}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]\supset T_{N}
and supertorus
T_{N,c}\supset T_{N}
, there exists a
trivial
toric supervariety structure given by
J=\mathbb{C}[S]
.

Example 5.13
.

Suppose
T=T_{N,c}
is an indecomposable supertorus. Then for a given affine toric variety
X_{0}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]
with torus
T_{N}
,
J_{c}
is generated by all non-constant monomials in
\mathbb{C}[S]
. If
S
is not pointed (i.e. it contains two distinct elements which are inverses), then
J_{c}=(1)
and the only toric supervariety whose underlying variety is
X_{0}
and whose torus is
T
, is the trivial one.

If
S
is pointed, then
J_{c}
is a maximal ideal, so there is a unique nontrivial toric supervariety structure, in which
J=J_{c}
.

5.4.
Some further computations

We have seen from Proposition
5.10
that an affine toric supervariety
X=\Spec A\supset T_{N,c}
with one odd dimension consists of the data of an ordinary affine toric variety
X_{0}=\Spec A_{0}\supset T_{N}
together with the additional data of a nonzero
T_{N}
-invariant ideal
J\subset A_{0}
that contains
J_{c}
. In this case, it holds that
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
where
A_{1}=\xi J
. In this section, we provide some further information about the
fermionic ideal

J
.

Corollary 5.14
.

Let
X_{0}=\Spec A_{0}
be an affine toric variety with torus
T_{0}=T_{N}
. Affine toric supervarieties with underlying variety
X_{0}
correspond to nonzero
A_{0}\times T_{0}
-bimodules
J
such that
J_{c}\subseteq J\subseteq A_{0}
. Equivalently if
c\neq 0
, they correspond to
T_{N}
-invariant submodules (or quotients) of
A_{0}/J_{c}
.

Example 5.15
.

Let
T=T_{\mathbb{Z}^{2},(1,0)}\cong Q(1)\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
X_{0}=\Spec A_{0}
for
A_{0}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2},x_{2}]
. Here, we have
J_{(1,0)}=(x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2})
, so
A_{0}/J_{(1,0)}=\mathbb{C}[x_{2}]/(x_{2}^{2})
. This module has three torus-invariant ideals, namely
(0),(x_{2})
, and itself. These ideals yield toric supervarieties whose coordinate rings are, respectively,
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2},x_{2},x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2}\xi]
,
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2},x_{2},x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2}\xi,x_{2}\xi]
, and
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2},x_{2},\xi]
.

Let us consider the toric supervariety
X=\Spec A
for
A=\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2},x_{2},x_{1}^{\pm 1}x_{2}\xi]
in more detail. To witness it as a subvariety of some
\mathbb{C}^{r|s}
, we take the three generators of
A_{0}
and the two generators of
A_{1}
, arranging them into finite sets as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\prime}=\mathscr{B}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{(1,1),(-1,1)\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{(0,1)\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\varnothing

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}:T_{\mathbb{Z}^{2},(1,0)}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{3|2}

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}(t_{1},t_{2},\xi)

\displaystyle=(t_{1}t_{2},t_{1}^{-1}t_{2},t_{2}\mid t_{1}t_{2}\xi,t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}\xi)

and indeed
X\cong Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
.

To illustrate the naturality of the data of the fermionic ideal, we may verify that the Duflo-Serganova functor

DS_{Q}:\mathbb{C}[X]\mapsto\frac{\ker Q|_{\mathbb{C}[X]^{Q^{2}}}}{Q(\mathbb{C}[X]^{Q^{2}})}

of
[
3
]
outputs the data of
J
when
Q=\xi\left(c_{1}x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+...+c_{n}x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}
is the odd vector field arising from the action of
\mathfrak{t}_{1}
on
\mathbb{C}[X]
.

Proposition 5.16
.

With notation as above,
DS_{Q}\mathbb{C}[X]\cong A_{0}/J
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathbb{C}[X]\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},...,x_{n}^{\pm 1},\xi]
. Since
Q^{2}=c_{1}x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+...+c_{n}x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
, it holds that
Q^{2}(f(x)+g(x)\xi)=Q^{2}(f(x))+Q^{2}(g(x))\xi
. For a monomial
x^{m}
, we obtain
Q^{2}(x^{m})=\langle m,c\rangle x^{m}
and hence

\mathbb{C}[X]^{Q^{2}}=(H\cap\mathbb{C}[X]_{0})\oplus(\xi H\cap\mathbb{C}[X]_{1})

where

H=\bigoplus_{m\in\ker c}\mathbb{C}x^{m}

is the “hyperplane” orthogonal to
c
.

We observe that
Q(H)=0
and
Q(g(x)\xi)=g(x)
, so

\displaystyle Q(\mathbb{C}[X]^{Q^{2}})

\displaystyle=Q(H\cap\mathbb{C}[X]_{0})+Q(\xi H\cap\xi J)

\displaystyle=H\cap J

and
\ker Q|_{\mathbb{C}[X]^{Q^{2}}}=H\cap\mathbb{C}[X]_{0}
. Hence

\displaystyle DS_{Q}\mathbb{C}[X]

\displaystyle=\frac{H\cap\mathbb{C}[X]_{0}}{H\cap J}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[X]_{0}/J,

where the final isomorphism is due to the fact that
J\supseteq J_{c}
and
H+J_{c}=\mathbb{C}[X]_{0}
.
∎

5.5.
Morphisms

Let
X=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[S]\oplus\xi J)
and
X^{\prime}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[S^{\prime}]\oplus\xi^{\prime}J^{\prime})
be affine toric supervarieties with supertori
T=T_{N,c}
and
T^{\prime}=T_{N^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
, respectively. Recall that a morphism of supergroups
T\to T^{\prime}
consists of a morphism
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
of lattices and a number
a\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}(c)=a^{2}c^{\prime}
.

Definition 5.17
.

A morphism
\phi:X\to X^{\prime}
of supervarieties is called
toric
if
\phi(T)\subseteq T^{\prime}
and
\phi|_{T}:T\to T^{\prime}
is a morphism of supergroups.

Before we characterize toric morphisms, we will establish some notation. The corresponding maps on coordinate superalgebras are

\displaystyle\phi^{*}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{C}[S^{\prime}]\oplus\xi^{\prime}J^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}[S]\oplus\xi J

\displaystyle\phi|_{T}^{*}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{C}[M^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{C}[M,\xi]

where
M=\mathbb{Z}S
and
M^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}S^{\prime}
. Since the conditions in the above definition imply that
\phi_{0}:X_{0}\to X_{0}^{\prime}
is a toric morphism, it follows that
\phi_{0}^{*}:\mathbb{C}[S^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{C}[S]
arises from a morphism
\hat{\phi}^{*}:S^{\prime}\to S
of semigroups, which extends to a morphism
\bar{\phi}^{*}:M^{\prime}\to M
of lattices. We write
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
for the dual map of
\bar{\phi}^{*}
.

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be the supertori of the affine toric supervarieties
X
and
X^{\prime}
as above.

(a)

A morphism
X\to X^{\prime}
of supervarieties is toric if and only if
\phi^{*}_{0}
arises from a semigroup morphism
\hat{\phi}^{*}:S^{\prime}\to S
, and
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}(c)=a^{2}c^{\prime}
where
a\in\mathbb{C}
is the
\xi
-coefficient of
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})
.

(b)

A morphism
\phi=(\bar{\phi},a):T\to T^{\prime}
of supergroups extends to a morphism
\Spec\mathbb{C}[S]\to\Spec\mathbb{C}[S^{\prime}]
of toric supervarieties if and only if
\bar{\phi}^{*}(S^{\prime})\subseteq S
, and either
a=0
or
\phi^{*}(J^{\prime})\subseteq J
.

(c)

A toric morphism is equivariant, meaning for any superalgebra
A
, it holds that
\phi(t\cdot x)=\phi(t)\cdot\phi(x)
for all
t\in T(A)
and
x\in X(A)
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in the usual case (
[
2
]
, Proposition 1.3.14), except that we use Lemma
4.8
to determine when
\phi|_{T}
is a morphism of supergroups. Notice that the condition
a=0
or
\phi^{*}(J^{\prime})\subseteq J
is equivalent to the condition
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime}J^{\prime})\subseteq\xi J
, since
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})=a\xi
.
∎

6.
Quasinormal toric supervarieties

We call a supervariety
quasinormal
if its underlying variety is normal, or equivalently if the local ring at every point is an integral superdomain
A
such that
A/(A_{1})
is integrally closed.

6.1.
The affine case

Suppose henceforth that
X_{\sigma}\supset T_{N}
is the normal affine toric variety corresponding to the cone
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
, so
X_{\sigma}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
where
S_{\sigma}
is the semigroup of the dual cone
\check{\sigma}
. If
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then
X_{\sigma}
contains
X_{\tau}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau}]
as an open subvariety, and
\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau}]=\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}
where
m_{\sigma,\tau}\in S_{\sigma}
is such that
\tau=\{u\in\sigma\mid\langle m_{\sigma,\tau},u\rangle=0\}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Using the above notation, suppose
X_{\sigma,J^{\sigma}}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]\oplus\xi J^{\sigma})
is the affine toric supervariety with underlying variety
X_{\sigma}
and fermionic ideal
J^{\sigma}
.

(a)

The localization

(\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]\oplus\xi J^{\sigma})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau}]\oplus\xi J^{\tau}

is the coordinate ring of the open subvariety
X_{\tau,J^{\tau}}
obtained by restricting to the open affine
X_{\tau}
. In particular, it holds that
J^{\sigma}_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}
.

(b)

Let
J^{\sigma}_{c}=(x^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]\mid\langle m,c\rangle\neq 0)
, and likewise for
\tau
. It holds that
(J^{\sigma}_{c})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}_{c}
.

(c)

As defined in part (a),
X_{\tau,J^{\tau}}
is an affine toric supervariety whose torus is
T_{N,c}
.

Proof.

(a)

The fact that
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau}]
is standard, so it remains to prove
J^{\sigma}_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}
. This follows immediately by computing sections over the basic open set defined by
x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}
.

(b)

Let
x^{m}
be a generator of
J^{\sigma}_{c}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, so
\langle m,c\rangle\neq 0
and
x^{m-km_{\sigma,\tau}}\in(J^{\sigma}_{c})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}
. Now consider the quantity
\langle m-km_{\sigma,\tau},c\rangle.
If
\langle m_{\sigma,\tau},c\rangle=0
, then indeed
x^{m-km_{\sigma,\tau}}\in J_{c}^{\tau}
. Otherwise
\langle m_{\sigma,\tau},c\rangle\neq 0
, so
x^{\pm m_{\sigma,\tau}}\in J_{c}^{\tau}
and hence
J_{c}^{\tau}=(1)
.

To see that
(J^{\sigma}_{c})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}\supseteq J^{\tau}_{c}
, let
x^{m}
be a generator of
J_{c}^{\tau}
, so
x^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau}]
satisfies
\langle m,c\rangle\neq 0
. By part (a),
m=m^{\prime}-km_{\sigma,\tau}
for some
x^{m^{\prime}}\in S_{\sigma}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\langle m_{\sigma,\tau},c\rangle=0
, then
\langle m^{\prime},c\rangle\neq 0
and hence
x^{m^{\prime}}\in J_{c}^{\sigma}
, so
x^{m}\in(J_{c}^{\sigma})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}
. Otherwise
\langle m_{\sigma,\tau},c\rangle\neq 0
, so
x^{\pm m_{\sigma,\tau}}\in(J_{c}^{\sigma})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}
and therefore
(J_{c}^{\sigma})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=(1)
.

(c)

This follows from part (b), as
(J^{\sigma}_{c})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}_{c}
. ∎

That is, in order to determine a quasinormal affine toric supervariety over the torus
T_{N,c}
, one requires the data of a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
decorated by a nonzero
T_{N}
-invariant ideal
J^{\sigma}\supset J_{c}
of
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
. When restricting to faces of
\sigma
, one finds that we may decorate each face with a certain localization
J^{\tau}
of
J^{\sigma}
. These decorations will play an important role later, when we describe the non-affine case.

First, we translate Proposition
5.18
to the language of cones.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose we have strongly convex rational polyhedral cones
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\subset N^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
, a morphism
\phi=(\bar{\phi},a):T_{N,c}\to T_{N^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
of supergroups, and nonzero
T_{N}
-,
T_{N^{\prime}}
-invariant ideals
J\supseteq J_{c}^{\sigma}
and
J^{\prime}\supseteq J_{c^{\prime}}^{\sigma^{\prime}}
of
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
and
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]
, respectively. Then
\phi
extends to a map of affine toric supervarieties
\phi:X_{\sigma,J}\to X_{\sigma^{\prime},J^{\prime}}
if and only if
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
, and either
a=0
or
\phi^{*}(J^{\prime})\subseteq J
.

The following lemma will be useful in section
7.5
, but is also interesting in its own right.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(\bar{\phi},a):X_{\sigma,J}\to X_{\sigma^{\prime},J^{\prime}}
be a map of toric supervarieties, and let

\phi^{*}:\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]\oplus\xi^{\prime}J^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]\oplus\xi J

be the dual map. Then
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime}J_{c^{\prime}})\subseteq\xi J_{c}
.

Proof.

Let
m^{\prime}\in S_{\sigma^{\prime}}-\ker c^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\phi}^{*}(m^{\prime}),c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m^{\prime},\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}(c)\rangle

\displaystyle=a^{2}\langle m^{\prime},c^{\prime}\rangle,

which is nonzero if
a\neq 0
. In this case, we have
\phi^{*}(J_{c}^{\prime})\subseteq J_{c}
. If
a=0
, then
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime})=0
and the lemma still holds.
∎

6.2.
The general case

Let
X
be a quasinormal toric supervariety whose torus is
T=T_{N,c}
. Then
X_{0}=X_{\Sigma}
for a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. By the results of the previous section, each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is decorated by a suitable ideal
J^{\sigma}\supset J^{\sigma}_{c}
of
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
such that if
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then
J^{\tau}
is a particular localization of
J^{\sigma}
. We show that these data give a complete classification of quasinormal toric supervarieties for the given torus
T
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
,
c\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\{J^{\sigma}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]\mid\sigma\in\Sigma\}
a collection of nonzero ideals such that each
J^{\sigma}\supseteq J^{\sigma}_{c}
. If
(J^{\sigma})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}
whenever
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
in
\Sigma
, then the affine toric supervarieties
U_{\sigma}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]\oplus\xi J^{\sigma})
can be glued together along their intersections to produce a toric supervariety whose underlying variety is
X_{\Sigma}
and whose torus is
T_{N,c}
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
6.1
(a) and the classical version of this statement.
∎

Hence, quasinormal toric supervarieties can be described by a fan
\Sigma
decorated by suitable ideals. These ideals arrange nicely into a coherent ideal sheaf
\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}
, called the
fermionic sheaf
, obtained by gluing together the coherent sheaves
\widetilde{J}^{\sigma}
on each open affine described by a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
. The fermionic sheaf of a quasinormal toric supervariety admits the following expected properties:

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mathscr{J}
be the fermionic sheaf of a quasinormal toric supervariety with underlying variety
X_{\Sigma}
and torus
T=T_{N,c}
.

(a)

\mathscr{J}
contains the minimal fermionic sheaf
\mathscr{J}_{c}
, obtained by gluing the sheaves
\widetilde{J}_{c}^{\sigma}
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

(b)

\mathscr{J}
is
T_{N}
-equivariant.

Proof.

(a)

Note that
\mathscr{J}_{c}
is well-defined by Proposition
6.1
(b). Since each
J^{\sigma}\supseteq J^{\sigma}_{c}
, the claim follows.

(b)

We know the claim holds for
U=U_{\sigma}
. The rest is standard; see e.g.
[
9
]
. ∎

We have thus proven:

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
X
be a quasinormal supervariety and
T=T_{N,c}
. Then
X
is a toric supervariety with supertorus
T
if and only if
X\cong X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{J}}:=(X_{\Sigma},\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}\oplus\xi\mathscr{J})
for a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and a nonzero
T_{N}
-equivariant ideal sheaf
\mathscr{J}\supseteq\mathscr{J}_{c}
in
\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}
.

6.3.
A combinatorial approach

The classification data
(\Sigma,\mathscr{J})
above is only semi-combinatorial in the sense that
\mathscr{J}
is an algebraic object. In this section, we represent
\mathscr{J}
with more combinatorial data; namely, we replace
\mathscr{J}
with a collection
\mathscr{B}
of local generators of
\mathscr{J}
.

Definition 6.7
.

As in Definition
5.8
, for
m,m^{\prime}\in S_{\sigma}
we write
m^{\prime}\leq_{\sigma}m
if there is
m^{\prime\prime}\in S_{\sigma}
such that
m=m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}
.

Definition 6.8
.

Let
T_{N,c}
be a supertorus and
\Sigma
a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, let
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
be a nonempty finite set in
S_{\sigma}
. We say that
\mathscr{B}=\{\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
is a set of
c
-admissible decorations
for the fan
\Sigma
if for all cones
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma
:

(i)

For all
m\in S_{\sigma}-\ker c
, there is
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
such that
b\leq_{\sigma}m
.

(ii)

For all
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
, there is
b^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
such that
b^{\prime}\leq_{\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}}b
.

(iii)

If
a,b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
satisfy
a\leq_{\sigma}b
, then
a=b
.

Notice that unlike in Definition
5.8
, here we require minimality (iii) as part of admissibility. This requirement is not strictly necessary for the classification, but it simplifies some matters in Section
7.3
.

Lemma 6.9
.

Condition (ii) of Definition
6.8
holds for all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma
if and only if it holds for all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
such that one is a face of the other.

Proof.

Suppose it holds in all cases of faces, and let
\tau=\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
. Now let
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
. Then there is
a\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau}
such that
a\leq_{\tau}b
, and likewise
b^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
such that
b^{\prime}\leq_{\tau}a
since
\tau
is a face of both
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. But then
b^{\prime}\leq_{\tau}a\leq_{\tau}b
, so we are finished.
∎

That is, we may replace condition (ii) with

(ii
′
)

Let
\tau
be a face of
\sigma
. Then

•

For all
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
, there is
a\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau}
such that
b\leq_{\tau}a
.

•

For all
a\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau}
, there is
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
such that
a\leq_{\tau}b
.

The following lemma shows that
\mathscr{B}
consists of local generating sets of the sheaf
\mathscr{J}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, let
J^{\sigma}
be a monomial ideal in
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
and
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}\subset S_{\sigma}
a finite generating set of
J^{\sigma}
. Then

(a)

J^{\sigma}\supseteq J^{\sigma}_{c}
if and only if condition (i) of Definition
6.8
holds.

(b)

J^{\sigma}_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}
if and only if condition (ii)
\approx
(ii
′
) of Definition
6.8
holds.

(c)

\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is minimal if and only if condition (iii) of Definition
6.8
holds.

Proof.

First observe that for
m\in S_{\sigma}
, we have
x^{m}\in J^{\sigma}
if and only if there is a generator
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
and an element
f\in\mathbb{C}[S]_{\sigma}
such that
fx^{b}=x^{m}
. In particular,
f
will always be a monomial, so this is equivalent to
b\leq_{\sigma}m
.

(a)

The ideal
J_{c}^{\sigma}
is generated by
S_{\sigma}-\ker c
, so condition (i) exactly says that all generators of
J^{\sigma}_{c}
belong to
J^{\sigma}
.

(b)

Notice that
J^{\tau}
and
J^{\sigma}_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}
are both ideals of
\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau}]=\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}
, generated by
\mathscr{B}_{\tau}
and
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
, respectively. Let
a\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau}
. By (ii’), there is
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
such that
b\leq_{\tau}a
, i.e. that
x^{a}\in J^{\sigma}_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}
. Likewise, let
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
, so there is
a\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau}
such that
a\leq_{\tau}b
, i.e. that
b\in J^{\tau}
. Therefore
J^{\sigma}_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}
. The converse follows by tracing backwards through the same reasoning.

(c)

If
a\leq_{\sigma}b
and
a\neq b
, then
b
remains in the ideal generated by
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}-\{b\}
, so
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is not minimal. Conversely, if
B_{\sigma}
is not minimal, then we can find
a,b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
satisfying
a\leq_{\sigma}b
. ∎

We write
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
for the toric supervariety defined by gluing the affine toric supervarieties
X_{\sigma,\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}}
for
\sigma\in\Sigma
along their intersections as before. Notice by Lemma
5.6
(b) that each
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is unique up to invertible elements of
S_{\sigma}
.

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
X
be a quasinormal supervariety and
T=T_{N,c}
. Then
X
is a toric supervariety with supertorus
T
if and only if
X\cong X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
for a fan
\Sigma
with
c
-admissible decorations
\mathscr{B}
.

Example 6.12
.

Consider
(n|1)
-dimensional projective superspace as a supervariety; i.e.
\mathbb{P}^{n|1}=(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathscr{O}(-1))=\Proj\mathbb{C}[x_{0},...,x_{n},\xi]
. Its underlying variety
\mathbb{P}^{n}
can be viewed as a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
where
\Sigma
is the fan whose cones are the positive spans of the proper subsets of
\{e_{1},...,e_{n},-(e_{1}+...+e_{n})\}
.

Using the Proj construction, the affine charts of
\mathbb{P}^{n|1}
have coordinate rings
\mathbb{C}[\frac{x_{0}}{x_{i}},...,\frac{x_{n}}{x_{i}},\frac{\xi}{x_{i}}]
for
i=0,...,n
. Letting
z_{i}=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}
for
i=1,...,n
and
\eta=\frac{\xi}{x_{0}}
, we can write these rings as
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},...,z_{n},\eta]
and
\mathbb{C}[z_{i}^{-1},z_{i}^{-1}z_{1},...,z_{i}^{-1}z_{n},z_{i}^{-1}\eta]
for
i=1,...,n
. Notably,
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},...,z_{n}]
is the semigroup algebra of the dual cone to the
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-span of
\{e_{1},...,e_{n}\}
, and
\mathbb{C}[z_{i}^{-1},z_{i}^{-1}z_{1},...,z_{i}^{-1}z_{n}]
is that of
\{e_{1},...,\hat{e}_{i},...,e_{n},-(e_{1}+...+e_{n})\}
where
\hat{e}_{i}
denotes the exclusion of
e_{i}
. Denote these cones by
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,...,n
respectively.

The torus
T_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
of this toric variety acts on the coordinate rings by

(t_{1},...,t_{n})\cdot x_{0}^{m_{0}}x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{m_{n}}=x_{0}^{m_{0}}(t_{1}x_{1})^{m_{1}}\cdots(t_{n}x_{n})^{m_{n}}.

It follows that
\mathbb{P}^{n|1}
can be expressed as a decorated fan with
\sigma_{0}
decorated by
\{1\}
and
\sigma_{i}
decorated by
\{x_{i}^{-1}\}
(the characters by which
T_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
acts on the respective odd generators in the coordinate rings). Then if
\mathbb{P}^{n|1}
is a toric supervariety, the torus must be
T_{\mathbb{Z}^{n},c}
where
\langle m,c\rangle=0
for all characters
x^{m}
of the form
z_{i}^{-1}z_{j}
for
i,j>0
. But
z_{i}^{-1}z_{j}=x_{i}^{-1}x_{j}
, so in particular we must have
c=0
or
c=(1,...,1)
, up to scale.

6.4.
Morphisms

The category of quasinormal toric supervarieties with one odd dimension can then be described as having the above objects
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
with
T
allowed to vary, and morphisms as in Definition
5.17
, which we characterize below. First, recall from Section
2.3
that a lattice map
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
is called
compatible with the fans

\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}},N_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
if for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there is
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
.

Definition 6.13
.

Let
\phi_{0}:X_{\Sigma}\to X_{\Sigma}^{\prime}
be a morphism of toric varieties induced by a lattice map
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
compatible with the fans
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
in
N^{\prime}
. Let
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}
be
c
- and
c^{\prime}
-admissible decorations for their respective fans. We say that
\bar{\phi}
is
compatible with the decorations
if whenever
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\prime}
, there is
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
such that
b\leq_{\sigma}\bar{\phi}^{*}(b^{\prime})
.

Proposition 6.14
.

Using the prior notation, let
\phi^{\#}:\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}}\to\phi_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}
be the corresponding morphism of sheaves on
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
, and let
Z\subset X_{\Sigma}
and
Z^{\prime}\subset X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
be the closed subschemes corresponding to the ideal sheaves
\mathscr{J}
and
\mathscr{J}^{\prime}
obtained from the decorations
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}
, respectively. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\bar{\phi}
is compatible with the decorations.

(b)

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
, it holds that
\phi_{0}|_{U_{\sigma}}^{*}(J^{\sigma^{\prime}})\subseteq J^{\sigma}
.

(c)

\phi^{\#}|_{\mathscr{J}^{\prime}}
takes
\mathscr{J}^{\prime}
to
\phi_{*}\mathscr{J}
.

(d)

\phi|_{Z}
is a morphism of schemes
Z\to Z^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For (a)
\iff
(b), observe that
\bar{\phi}^{*}(\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}})
generates
\phi_{0}|_{U_{\sigma}}^{*}(J^{\sigma^{\prime}})
. Hence
\phi_{0}|_{U_{\sigma}}^{*}(J^{\sigma^{\prime}})\subseteq J^{\sigma}
is equivalent to the condition that for all
b^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, there is
b\in\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
such that
b\leq_{\sigma}\bar{\phi}^{*}(b^{\prime})
.

The map
\phi_{0}|_{U^{\sigma}}^{*}:\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]\to\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
is the same as

\Res^{\phi^{-1}(U_{\sigma^{\prime}})}_{U_{\sigma}}\circ\phi^{\#}(U_{\sigma^{\prime}}):\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}}(U_{\sigma^{\prime}})\to\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}(U_{\sigma}),

so if we restrict further to
J^{\sigma^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]
and
\mathscr{J}^{\prime}(U_{\sigma^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}}(U_{\sigma^{\prime}})
, we see immediately that (b)
\iff
(c). The fact that (c)
\iff
(d) is standard.
∎

Hence if
X_{\sigma,J}
and
X_{\sigma^{\prime},J^{\prime}}
are affine, compatibility with decorations is exactly the condition that
\phi^{*}(J^{\prime})\subseteq J
as in Proposition
6.2
. The following condition is equivalent to the condition that
\phi^{*}(\xi^{\prime}J^{\prime})\subseteq\xi J
.

Definition 6.15
.

Let
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
and
X_{\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}}
be toric supervarieties with supertori
T_{N,c}
and
T_{N^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
, respectively. If
(\bar{\phi},a):T_{N,c}\to T_{N^{\prime}c,}
is a morphism of supergroups, we say that
(\bar{\phi},a)
is
compatible with the decorated fans
if
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
is compatible with the fans, and either
a=0
, or
\bar{\phi}
is compatible with the decorations.

Proposition 6.16
.

Let
N,N^{\prime}
be lattices and
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
fans in
N_{\mathbb{R}},N_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
with
c
-,
c^{\prime}
-admissible decorations
\mathscr{B}
,
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}
, respectively. Suppose
\phi=(\bar{\phi},a):T_{N,c}\to T_{N^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
is a morphism of supergroups. Then
\phi
extends to a morphism of toric supervarieties
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}\to X_{\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}}
if and only if
(\bar{\phi},a)
is compatible with the decorated fans.

Proof.

First suppose that
\phi
extends. By the analogous result in the purely even setting,
\bar{\phi}
is compatible with the fans. Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
, so the extension
\phi
restricts to a map

\phi|_{U_{\sigma,J^{\sigma}}}:U_{\sigma,J^{\sigma}}\to U_{\sigma^{\prime},J^{\sigma^{\prime}}}

for any
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
. By Proposition
6.2
, we see that
a=0
or
\phi_{0}|_{U_{\sigma}}^{*}(J^{\sigma^{\prime}})\subseteq J^{\sigma}
, which is sufficient by Proposition
6.14
.

Conversely, suppose the compatibility conditions hold. By Proposition
6.2
, these conditions guarantee that for each open affine toric subvariety
U_{\sigma,\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}}\subseteq X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
, there is an open affine toric subvariety
U_{\sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}}\subseteq X_{\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}}
such that
\phi
extends to a toric morphism
U_{\sigma,\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}}\to U_{\sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}}
. Moreover, these partial extensions are compatible with each other on intersections because intersections are also open affine toric subvarieties. Therefore these partial extensions may be glued together into a global extension, finishing our proof.
∎

Definition 6.17
.

We define the category of
decorated fans
as follows: The objects are quadruples
(N,c,\Sigma,\mathscr{B})
where
N
is a lattice,
c\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
,
\Sigma
is a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\mathscr{B}
is a
c
-admissible family of decorations for
\Sigma
. The morphisms are tuples
(\bar{\phi},a):(N,c,\Sigma,\mathscr{B})\to(N^{\prime},c^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime})
where
\bar{\phi}:N\to N^{\prime}
is a lattice map,

is such that
\bar{\phi}_{\mathbb{C}}(c)=a^{2}c^{\prime}
, and
(\bar{\phi},a)
is compatible with the decorated fans. Composition of morphisms is given by

Theorem 1
.

The category of quasinormal toric supervarieties with one odd dimension is equivalent to the category of decorated fans.

7.
The geometry of quasinormal toric supervarieties

Now that we have described the category of quasinormal toric supervarieties of one odd dimension, we seek to understand their geometry.

7.1.
Split toric supervarieties

Roughly speaking, a supervariety is split if it is smooth in the odd directions.

Definition 7.1
.

A supervariety
X
is
graded
if there is a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
of
\mathscr{O}_{X_{0}}
-modules such that
\mathscr{O}_{X}\cong\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathscr{F}
. It is called
split
if
\mathscr{F}
can be chosen to be locally free (i.e. a vector bundle).

As we have seen, every quasinormal
(n|1)
-dimensional toric supervariety is graded, with
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{J}
the fermionic sheaf and
\Lambda^{2}\mathscr{J}=0
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
X=X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}=X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{J}}
be a quasinormal toric variety with torus
T_{N,c}
. The following are equivalent:

(a)

X
is split.

(b)

Each
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is a singleton.

(c)

Each
J^{\sigma}
is a principal ideal.

(d)

\mathscr{J}
is a line bundle on
X_{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

We have (a)
\iff
(d) from the definition of split and (b)
\iff
(c) from the definition of principal. Now if (d) holds, then
\mathscr{J}|_{U_{\sigma}}
is a line bundle on the affine toric variety
U_{\sigma}
. A result of Gubeladze
[
6
]
shows that an arbitrary vector bundle on an affine toric variety is trivial, so
\mathscr{J}|_{U_{\sigma}}=\widetilde{J}^{\sigma}
where
J^{\sigma}\cong\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
as a
\mathbb{C}[S^{\sigma}]
-module. That is,
J^{\sigma}
is principal for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

Conversely, if each ideal is principal, then each
\widetilde{J}^{\sigma}
is a trivial line bundle, and so
\mathscr{J}
trivializes over the cover of
X_{\Sigma}
by the
U_{\sigma}
.
∎

Definition 7.3
.

We shall henceforth refer to a family
\mathscr{B}
of decorations as
split
if each
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is a singleton.

7.2.
Smooth toric supervarieties

We seek to establish a characterization of smooth toric supervarieties. First, we recall the definition of a smooth supervariety and a smooth cone.

Definition 7.4
.

Let
X=(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X})
be a supervariety and
\mathfrak{p}\in X(\mathbb{C})=|X|
a closed point. Denote by
B
the stalk
\mathscr{O}_{X,\mathfrak{p}}
. We say that
X
is
smooth at

\mathfrak{p}
if
\overline{B}:=B/(B_{1})
is a regular local ring, and
B\cong\overline{B}[\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n}]
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that
X
is
smooth
if it is smooth at all points
\mathfrak{p}
.

It is shown in
[
12
]
, Proposition 3.7.4 that if
X
is smooth at
\mathfrak{p}
, then
B=\mathscr{O}_{X,\mathfrak{p}}
is an integral superdomain. Consequently, only integral supervarieties can be smooth, and the condition that
\overline{B}
is a regular local ring is equivalent to the condition that
X_{0}=\overline{X}
is smooth at
\mathfrak{p}
.

Definition 7.5
.

Let
N
be a lattice, and
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone. We say that
\sigma
is
smooth
if its minimal generators form part of a basis for
N
. A fan
\Sigma
is
smooth
if all its cones are smooth.

Let us begin in the affine case.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
X
be an affine toric supervariety with torus
T_{N,c}
. Then
X
is smooth if and only if
X\cong X_{\sigma,\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}}
for a smooth cone
\sigma
and a singleton
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

If
X
is smooth, then so is
X_{0}
. But a smooth variety is normal, and so
X=X_{\sigma,\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}}
for a smooth cone
\sigma
and a
c
-admissible finite set
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}=\{m_{1},...,m_{s}\}\subset S_{\sigma}
. Then the underlying variety is
X_{0}\cong\Spec\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},x_{r+1}^{\pm 1},...,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]=\mathbb{C}^{r}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-r}
with torus
T_{0}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
. Localizing at
\mathfrak{p}=(0^{r},1^{n-r})
, we set
B=\mathbb{C}[S]_{\mathfrak{p}}
and find
B\cong\overline{B}[x^{m_{1}}\xi,...,x^{m_{s}}\xi]
wherein
x^{m_{i}}\xi\notin\overline{B}[x^{m_{j}}\xi]
for
i\neq j
. By definition of smoothness, it follows that
s=1
.

On the other hand, if
\sigma
is a smooth cone and
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is a singleton, then by the above reasoning we have
B\cong\overline{B}[x^{m}\xi]\cong\overline{B}[\xi]
with
\overline{B}
a regular local ring. Therefore
X_{\sigma,\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}}
is smooth.
∎

The general case follows immediately from Propositions
7.2
and
7.6
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
be a toric supervariety with torus
T_{N,c}
. Then
X
is smooth if and only if
X\cong X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
for a smooth fan
\Sigma
and split decorations
\mathscr{B}
.

Example 7.7
.

In this example, we show that every
(1|1)
-dimensional toric supervariety is smooth. Note that the three 1-dimensional ordinary toric varieties (up to isomorphism) are the torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and the affine and projective lines

We now consider the possibilities over

7.3.
Fibers of
\mathscr{J}

One way to glean information from the fermionic sheaf
\mathscr{J}
is by computing its fibers, which will turn out to extract the fan decorations

.

In the classical setting of a normal toric variety

with torus

(

2
]
, Theorem 3.2.6).
We denote by

-orbit of

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a closed point in
\Orb(\sigma)
. Then
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is unique when viewed as a subset of the character lattice of
\Stab_{T_{N}}(\mathfrak{p})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.6
(b),
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
is unique up to multiplication by monomials which are invertible in
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
. The invertible monomials in
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
are exactly

for
m\in\sigma^{\perp}\cap M
, since
S_{\sigma}=\check{\sigma}\cap M
. Observe that
\sigma^{\perp}\cap M
is the character lattice of the orbit
T_{N}\mathfrak{p}
, viewed as an algebraic torus.

Let

, and let
m\in\sigma^{\perp}\cap M
. Both
x^{b}
and
x^{b+m}
can be viewed as characters
T_{N}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. It suffices to show that they become the same character of
\Stab_{T_{N}}(\mathfrak{p})
when we compose them with the map
\Stab_{T_{N}}(\mathfrak{p})\to T_{N}
. We have
T_{N}\mathfrak{p}\cong T_{N}/\Stab_{T_{N}}(\mathfrak{p})
by the orbit-stabilizer theorem, so the statement that
x^{m}
belongs to the character lattice of
T_{N}\mathfrak{p}
is exactly the statement that
x^{m}
is trivial on
\Stab_{T_{N}}(\mathfrak{p})
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
be a quasinormal toric supervariety with supertorus
T_{N,c}
, and let
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a closed point in
\Orb(\sigma)
. Then the fiber

, as a representation of

, is a direct sum of the characters in
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Localizing at
\mathfrak{p}
means we invert monomials not in
\mathfrak{p}
. In the event that
\mathfrak{p}
belongs to the
T_{N}
-orbit corresponding to
\sigma
, we invert the monomials in
\sigma^{\perp}
. Hence, when we quotient
J^{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{p}}
by
\mathfrak{p}J^{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we are left with a direct sum of the desired characters.
∎

7.4.
Orbits and stabilizers

In the ordinary setting, both the orbit and the stabilizer of a closed point in a toric variety can be viewed as algebraic tori. In particular, if the torus is
T_{N}
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\Orb(\sigma)
, let
N_{\sigma}
be the sublattice of
N
spanned by the points in
\sigma\cap N
, and
N(\sigma)=N/N_{\sigma}
. Then
\Stab_{T_{N}}(\mathfrak{p})\cong T_{N_{\sigma}}
and
T_{N}\cdot\mathfrak{p}=\Orb(\sigma)\cong T_{N(\sigma)}
. Additionally, the Zariski closure of the orbit
\Orb(\sigma)
is a toric variety with torus
T_{N(\sigma)}
. We will prove analogous results in the super case.

We recall from
[
4
]
the notion of the stabilizer supergroup.

Definition 7.12
.

Let
G
be an affine algebraic supergroup acting on a supervariety
X
. Given a closed point
\mathfrak{p}\in|X|
, the
stabilizer supergroup functor
is the group-valued functor
\Stab_{G}(\mathfrak{p})
on the category of superalgebras, given by

\Stab_{G}(\mathfrak{p})(A)=\{g\in G(A)\mid g\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{A}=\mathfrak{p}_{A}\}

where
\mathfrak{p}_{A}
is
\mathfrak{p}
, viewed as an
A
-point of
X
. By [2],
\Stab_{G}(\mathfrak{p})
is naturally an affine algebraic supergroup.

We may similarly define the
orbit functor

G\cdot\mathfrak{p}
as

(G\cdot\mathfrak{p})(A)=G(A)\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{A}

so that the orbit-stabilizer theorem holds, in the sense that

(G\cdot\mathfrak{p})(A)\cong G(A)/\Stab_{G}(\mathfrak{p})(A)

at the level of sets.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in|X|
be a closed point in a toric supervariety
X
with one odd dimension. To compute its orbit and stabilizer, we may assume without loss of generality that
X=Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}
is affine, so that
X=\Spec\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]/J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})
by the proof of Lemma
5.6
(c). Then
\mathfrak{p}=(a_{1},...,a_{r}\mid 0,...,0)
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
on which
all the ordinary polynomials in
J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})
vanish. Suppose that
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{r},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{s}]/J(Y_{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}})\cong\mathbb{C}[x^{m_{1}},...,x^{m_{r}},x^{n_{1}}\xi,...,x^{n_{s}}\xi]
, and recall that
n_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{j}m_{i}
for some
p_{i}^{j}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
n_{j}=m_{j}
for
j=1,...,q
.

Proposition 7.13
.

Using the above notation, it holds that

\Stab_{T_{N,c}}(\mathfrak{p})\cong\begin{cases}T_{N_{\sigma},c}&\text{ if }a^{p^{j}}=0\text{ for all }i=1,...,s\\
T_{N_{\sigma}}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

where

lies in the subspace

and

T_{N,c}\cdot\mathfrak{p}\cong\begin{cases}T_{N(\sigma)}&\text{ if }a^{p^{j}}=0\text{ for all }i=1,...,s\\

where
\bar{c}
is the image of

.

Proof.

For
(t\mid\xi)\in T_{N,c}(A)
and
(a_{1},...,a_{r}\mid\eta_{1},...,\eta_{s})\in X(A)
, we use the group law from Proposition
5.3
to calculate

\displaystyle(t\mid\xi)

\displaystyle\cdot(a_{1},...,a_{r}\mid\eta_{1},...,\eta_{s})

\displaystyle=(

\displaystyle t^{m_{1}}a_{1}+t^{m_{1}}\langle m_{1},c\rangle\xi\eta_{1},...,t^{m_{q}}a_{1}+t^{m_{q}}\langle m_{q},c\rangle\xi\eta_{q},

\displaystyle t^{m_{q+1}}a_{q+1},...,t^{m_{r}}a_{r}\mid

\displaystyle t^{n_{1}}a_{1}\xi+t^{n_{1}}\eta_{1},...,t^{n_{q}}a_{q}\xi+t^{n_{q}}\eta_{q},

\displaystyle t^{n_{q+1}}a^{p^{q+1}}\xi+t^{n_{q+1}}\eta_{q+1},...,t^{n_{q+1}}a^{p^{q+1}}\xi+t^{n_{q+1}}\eta_{q+1})

and so in particular,

\displaystyle(t\mid\xi)\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{A}

\displaystyle=(t\mid\xi)\cdot(a_{1},...,a_{r}\mid 0,...,0)

\displaystyle=(t^{m_{1}}a_{1},...,t^{m_{r}}a_{r}\mid t^{n_{1}}a^{p^{1}}\xi,...,t^{n_{s}}a^{p^{s}}\xi)

\displaystyle=(t^{m_{1}}a_{1},...,t^{m_{r}}a_{r}\mid t^{m_{1}}a_{1}\xi,...,t^{m_{q}}a_{q}\xi,t^{n_{q+1}}a^{p^{q+1}},...,t^{n_{s}}a^{p^{s}}\xi).

It follows that
(t|\xi)
stabilizes

if and only if

and either

for all

.

In the former case, this implies that

for

(a)

If
\Orb_{c}(\sigma)\cong T_{N(\sigma)}
as in the first case of the previous proposition, then
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}(\sigma)
is the ordinary toric variety with fan
\bar{\Sigma}=\{\bar{\tau}\mid\sigma\leq\tau\}
where
\bar{\tau}
denotes the image of
\tau
in
N(\sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}=N_{\mathbb{R}}/(N_{\sigma})_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(b)

If
\Orb_{c}(\sigma)\cong T_{N(\sigma),\bar{c}}
as in the second case of the previous proposition, then
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}(\sigma)
is the toric supervariety with fan
\bar{\Sigma}
as above, and decorations
\mathscr{B}_{\bar{\tau}}=\mathscr{B}_{\tau}\cap\sigma^{\perp}
for
\tau\geq\sigma
.

Proof.

Part (a) follows immediately from the classical situation (
[
2
]
, Proposition 3.2.7). We obtain the fan in part (b) likewise. The fact that
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}(\sigma)
is a toric supervariety follows immediately from the definitions. It therefore remains to find the decorations of part (b).

Since
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}(\sigma)
is a closed subvariety of
X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}
, it is affine-locally a quotient at the level of rings. That is, if
U_{\bar{\tau}}\subseteq X_{\Sigma,\mathscr{B}}(\sigma)
is the affine open corresponding to
\bar{\tau}
for
\tau\geq\sigma
, then
\mathbb{C}[U_{\bar{\tau},\mathscr{B}_{\bar{\tau}}}]
is a quotient of
\mathbb{C}[U_{\tau,B_{\tau}}]
. In particular,
\mathbb{C}[U_{\tau,\mathscr{B}_{\tau}}]\to\mathbb{C}[U_{\bar{\tau},\mathscr{B}_{\bar{\tau}}}]
is the surjection
\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau},\xi\mathscr{B}_{\tau}]\to\mathbb{C}[S_{\tau}\cap\sigma^{\perp},\xi(\mathscr{B}_{\tau}\cap\sigma^{\perp})]
given by mapping any
x^{m}
with
m\notin\sigma^{\perp}
to 0. Therefore
\mathscr{B}_{\bar{\tau}}
can be viewed as
\mathscr{B}_{\tau}\cap\sigma^{\perp}
, so we are finished.
∎

7.5.
Fiber products

In this section, we determine when fiber products (i.e. pullbacks) exist in the category of quasinormal toric supervarieties of one odd dimension. Consider the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N^{\prime},c^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N^{\prime\prime},c^{\prime\prime},\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Q,d,\Lambda,\mathscr{C})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\phi}^{\prime},a^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime\prime})}

in the category of decorated fans, and suppose
a^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime\prime}
are not both 0. We construct the fiber product as follows:

Define a lattice

N:=N^{\prime}\times_{Q}N^{\prime\prime}=\{(n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime})\in N^{\prime}\times N^{\prime\prime}\mid\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(n^{\prime})=\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(n^{\prime\prime})\}

and a fan

\Sigma:=\Sigma^{\prime}\times_{\Lambda}\Sigma^{\prime\prime}=\{(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in\Sigma^{\prime}\times\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\mid\text{there is }\lambda\in\Lambda\text{ such that }\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma^{\prime}),\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\lambda\}

so that
(N,\Sigma)
is the fiber product of
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N^{\prime\prime},\Sigma^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Q,\Lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\phi}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime}}

in the usual category of fans (or equivalently, toric varieties). Define

c=c^{\prime}\times_{d}c^{\prime\prime}:=((a^{\prime\prime})^{2}c^{\prime},(a^{\prime})^{2}c^{\prime\prime})

and observe that

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}^{\prime}((a^{\prime\prime})^{2}c^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime})^{2}d

\displaystyle=\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime}((a^{\prime})^{2}c^{\prime\prime}),

so
c\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

It remains to describe the decorations. Towards this end, we first describe the character lattice
M=N^{*}
. We also write
M^{\prime}=(N^{\prime})^{*}
,
M^{\prime\prime}=(N^{\prime\prime})^{*}
, and
P=Q^{*}
.

Definition 7.16
.

Let
S
be a semigroup contained in a lattice
L
. The
saturation
of
S
in
L
is given by
\Sat(S)=\{\ell\in L\mid k\ell\in S\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

We collect some elementary results about saturations of semigroups:

Lemma 7.17
.

(a)

If
S\subseteq L
is a semigroup, then
\Sat(S)
is the smallest saturated semigroup in
L
that contains
S
.

(b)

Let
L^{\prime}
be a sublattice of a lattice
L
. Then
L/L^{\prime}
is also a lattice if and only if
L^{\prime}
is saturated in
L
.

(c)

The saturation of an affine semigroup is affine.

We may now apply this lemma to describe
M
.

Lemma 7.18
.

(a)

The natural surjection
\pi:M^{\prime}\times M^{\prime\prime}\to M
has kernel
\Sat\big((\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{*}\times(-\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime})^{*}(P)\big)
, so that
M\cong(M^{\prime}\times M^{\prime\prime})/\Sat\big((\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{*}\times(-\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime})^{*}(P)\big)
.

(b)

If
\sigma=(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in\Sigma
, then
S_{\sigma}
is the saturation of
\pi(S_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}})
.

Proof.

(a)

We have a surjection
\pi:M^{\prime}\times M^{\prime\prime}\to M
given by
\langle\pi(m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}),(n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime})\rangle=\langle m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\rangle+\langle m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}\rangle
for
(m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime})\in M^{\prime}\times M^{\prime\prime}
and
(n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime})\in N
. Notice that

\displaystyle\langle\pi(m^{\prime}+(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{*}(p),m^{\prime\prime}-(\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime})^{*}(p)),(n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\rangle+\langle m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{*}(p),n^{\prime}\rangle-\langle(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{*}(p),n^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\rangle+\langle m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle p,\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(n^{\prime})\rangle-\langle p,\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(n^{\prime\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\rangle+\langle m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}\rangle

since
\phi^{\prime}(n^{\prime})=\phi^{\prime\prime}(n^{\prime\prime})
, so
\ker\pi
contains
(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{*}\times(-\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime})^{*}(P)
. But
M
is a lattice, so by Lemma
7.17
(b) we know
\ker\pi
is saturated and hence by (a),
\ker\pi\supseteq\Sat\big((\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{*}\times(-\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime})^{*}(P)\big)
. Equality follows by counting dimensions.

(b)

We first note that
S_{\sigma}\supseteq\pi(S_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}})
because if
(m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime})\in S_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}
, then both summands of
\langle\pi(m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}),(n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime})\rangle=\langle m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\rangle+\langle m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}\rangle
are nonnegative for all
(n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime})\in\sigma
. Then
\Sat(\pi(S_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}))\subseteq S_{\sigma}
is a saturated semigroup which is affine by Lemma
7.17
(c). Hence by the universal property of the fiber product, the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[\Sat(\pi(S_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}))]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[S_{\lambda}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\phi^{\prime\prime})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\phi^{\prime})^{*}}

In this diagram, the dashed arrow arises from the universal property of the fiber product in the (opposite) category of toric varieties, and the unlabelled solid arrows arise from the natural morphisms of semigroups. The composition of the dashed arrow with the arrow in the reverse direction is the unique endomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
satisfying the universal property. Since
\Sat(\pi(S_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}))\subseteq S_{\sigma}
, these arrows are isomorphisms. Hence
S_{\sigma}=\Sat(\pi(S_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times S_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}))
. ∎

We now describe the decorations. For each
\sigma=(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in\Sigma
, let

J^{\sigma}=\begin{cases}J_{c}^{\sigma}+((\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\mathscr{B}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}^{\prime\prime}))&\text{ if }a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\neq 0\\
J_{c}^{\sigma}+(\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times\{0\})&\text{ if }a^{\prime}=0\\
J_{c}^{\sigma}+(\{0\}\times\mathscr{B}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}^{\prime\prime})&\text{ if }a^{\prime\prime}=0\end{cases}

and define
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}=\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times_{\mathscr{C}}\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}
as the minimal generating set of
J^{\sigma}
. We write
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\times_{\mathscr{C}}\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}
for the decorations
\{\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times_{\mathscr{C}}\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\}_{(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in\Sigma}
.

Lemma 7.19
.

The collection
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\times_{\mathscr{C}}\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}
defined above is
c
-admissible.

Proof.

It suffices to show that condition (ii
′
) holds. Let
\tau=(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})
be a face of
\sigma=(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})
. Let
m\in J^{\sigma}
, so either
m\in J^{\sigma}_{c}
or
m
belongs to the ideal generated by
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times\{0\}
or
\{0\}\times\mathscr{B}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}^{\prime\prime}
. In the former case, condition (ii
′
) holds because
(J^{\sigma}_{c})_{x^{m_{\sigma,\tau}}}=J^{\tau}_{c}
. In the latter case, we apply condition (ii
′
) of
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}
or
\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime}
to arrive at the result. The other half of the argument follows by the same reasoning.
∎

Lemma 7.20
.

Let
\bar{\psi}^{\prime}:N\to N^{\prime}
and
\bar{\psi}^{\prime\prime}:N\to N^{\prime\prime}
be the natural maps. Then
(\bar{\psi}^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
and
(\bar{\psi}^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime})
are compatible with the decorated fans.

Proof.

Let
\sigma=(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in\Sigma
. It suffices to show that
(\psi^{\prime})^{*}(\xi^{\prime}J^{\sigma^{\prime}})\subseteq\xi J^{\sigma}
and
(\psi^{\prime\prime})^{*}(\xi^{\prime\prime}J^{\sigma^{\prime\prime}})\subseteq\xi J^{\sigma}
. But the images of
J^{\sigma^{\prime}}
and
J^{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}
are exactly the ideals generated by
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times\{0\}
and

, respectively. These generators are present in

exactly when
a^{\prime}\neq 0
and
a^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
, respectively, so the lemma follows.
∎

The above lemmas provide the data sufficient to describe the fiber product.

Proposition 7.21
.

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N,c,\Sigma,\mathscr{B})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N^{\prime},c^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N^{\prime\prime},c^{\prime\prime},\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{B}^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=N^{\prime}\times_{Q}N^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}\times_{d}c^{\prime\prime}

Suppose

Remark 7.22
.

If

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Q,d,\Lambda,\mathscr{C})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\widetilde{\psi}^{\prime},\widetilde{b}^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\widetilde{\psi}^{\prime\prime},\widetilde{b}^{\prime\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\psi}^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\psi}^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\phi}^{\prime},0)}

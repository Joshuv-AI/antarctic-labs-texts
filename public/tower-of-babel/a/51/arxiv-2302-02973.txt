A gradient estimate for the Hermitian
Monge-Ampère equation

Abstract




Abstract.
We improve our previous gradient estimate for the Monge-Ampère equation on a compact Hermitian manifold and give a estimates for the non-mixed second order derivatives. These estimates are required to apply either the Evans-Krylov
C^{2,\alpha}
estimates or the third derivatives estimates for equations with a gradient term.

Keyword.
Monge-Ampère equation, Hermitian manifold, gradient estimate.

Mathematical Subject Classification : 32U05, 32U40, 53C55

1.
Introduction

The complex Monge-Ampère equation over the last half century has been the subject of intensive investigations. In the framework of Kähler geometry, a source of interest in this equation goes back to the well known conjecture of Calabi : To every closed (1,1)-form
R
representing the first Chern class of a Kähler manifold
(M,g)
of complex dimension
n\geq 2
, there is a unique Kähler metric in the same Kähler class whose Ricci form is
R
.

In case the complex manifold has vanishing first Chern class, write

\omega=\mathrm{i}g_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\lambda}\wedge\mathrm{d}z^{\overline{\mu}},

Calabi realized that his conjecture can be reduced to solving a Monge-Ampère equation of the following form

(\omega+\mathrm{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=e^{f}\omega^{n}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\omega+\mathrm{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi>0,

for a real valued function
\varphi
, where
f
is a smooth real function on
M
. The equation was solved by Aubin [1-2] and Yau [13]. The main purpose of this paper is to go back on those estimates which were not required in the above authors works but became of considerable interest in the last two decades.

The two authors were able to derive independently estimates for the Laplacian of the solution assuming only a uniform estimate of the solution. The gradient estimate could be bypassed. On the other hand, the third derivatives estimate, given once more independently by the two authors, did not make use of the estimate of second order derivatives of pure indexes. To deal with equations involving a gradient term, we need to derive these bypassed estimates.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold, without boundary. Denote by
\nabla
the Chern connection and by
TM
the tangent bundle of
M
. For a nonnegative smooth real valued function
F
on
TM\times\mathbb{R}
, we consider the following Monge-Ampère equation

(1)

\left(\omega+\mathrm{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi\right)^{n}=F\omega^{n},

with the condition that
\omega+\mathrm{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi>0
. The function
\varphi
is said to be admissible. For
(x,p)\in TM
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we write

F_{x}(\bullet)=F(\bullet,p,t),\quad F_{p}(\bullet)=F(x,\bullet,t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad F_{t}(\bullet)=F(x,p,\bullet).

Our gradient estimate may be stated as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{3}(M)
be an admissible solution of
(1)
such that
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}
for a positive constant
C_{0}
. Suppose that there exists a real number
r
such that
r<2
and that, for all
(x,p,t)\in TM\times[-C_{0},C_{0}]
,

(2)

F\leq C\left(1+|p|^{r}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left|\nabla\left[(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{x}\right]\right|,\;\left|\left[(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{t}\right]^{\prime}\right|,\;\left|p^{\alpha}\frac{\partial(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{p}}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\right|\leq CF^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
(p^{a})
are the natural coordinates in the fibers of
TM
. Then

|\nabla\varphi|\leq C_{1},

where the constant
C_{1}
depends only on
C_{0}
,
M
and the constant in
(2)
. In case
F
does not depend on the gradient, hypothesis (2) is not required and
C_{1}
depends only on
C_{0}
,
M
and an upper bound for
\|\nabla F^{\frac{1}{n}}\|_{\infty}
.

This gradient estimate appears for the first time in [8] and [9] in the nineties. For the next few years there was no activity on the Monge-Ampère equation on Hermitian manifolds until very recently, when the results were rediscovered P. Guan [7], B. Guan-Q. Li [6], D.H Phong-J. Sturm [11] and . Moreover, S.Dinew-S.Kolodziej [4] also studied the equation in the weak sense and obtained the uniform estimates of the solution via suitably constructed pluripotential theory.

Following the same strategy as that of Aubin and Yau, see also P. Cherrier [3], one can now estimate the Laplacian. It follows from this estimate that the metrics
g
and
g^{\prime}
defined by
(g_{\lambda\overline{u}}+\partial_{\lambda\overline{u}}\varphi)
are equivalent : there exists a strictly positive constant
a
such that

a^{-1}g\leq g^{\prime}\leq ag.

The dependency of the constant
a
on the geometry of
M
is quite explicit. It also depends on a
C^{2}
-norm of
F
on the compact

{\mathscr{K}}=\{(x,p,t)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}\;:\;x\in M,\ |p|\leq C_{1},\ |t|\leq C_{0}\}.

The next step in Aubin and Yau works was to estimate the third derivatives. For equations with gradient term, one can not handle this estimate directly without completing the
C^{2}
-estimate. These estimates are also required to apply the Evans-Krylov theorem for
C^{2,\alpha}
-estimates. We have the following estimates for the second derivatives of non-mixed type.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}^{4}(M)
be an admissible
solution of equation
(1)
such that
\|\varphi\|\leq C_{0}
and
\|\nabla\varphi\|\leq C_{1}
. Then

|\nabla_{\lambda\mu}\varphi\nabla^{\lambda\mu}\varphi|\leq C_{2},

where the constant
C_{2}
depends only on
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
, on
M
in terms of its geometry, a bound of the
C^{2}
-norm of
F
on the compact
{\mathscr{K}}
and
\displaystyle\min_{\mathscr{K}}F
.

2.
Proof of theorem 1

In local coordinates
(z^{\lambda})
, write
\displaystyle z^{\overline{\lambda}}=\overline{z^{\lambda}}
,
\displaystyle e_{\lambda}=\partial_{\lambda}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\lambda}}
,

g=g_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{d}z^{\overline{\mu}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g^{-1}=g^{\lambda\overline{\mu}}e_{\lambda}\otimes e_{\overline{\mu}}.

The Laplacians
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
with respect to the metrics
g
and
g^{\prime}
acting on functions are defined as follows

\Delta\varphi=g^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta^{\prime}\varphi=g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\varphi.

The norms of a tensor
T=T_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}
with respect to the metrics
g
and
g^{\prime}
are denoted as follow
g
and
g^{\prime}
acting on functions are defined as follows

|T|^{2}=g^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}T_{\alpha}T_{\overline{\beta}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|T|^{\prime 2}=g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}T_{\alpha}T_{\overline{\beta}}.

Since, we are restricting ourselves to the smooth case, we can write equation (1) as follows

(3)

\det\left(\delta^{\lambda}_{\mu}+\nabla^{\lambda}_{\mu}\varphi\right)=F(x,\nabla\varphi,\varphi).

A key ingredient in the proof of theorem 1 is the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{3}(M)
be an admissible
solution of
(3)
. Then

(4)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Delta^{\prime}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}&=&2\mathrm{Re}\left<\nabla\log F,\nabla\varphi\right>_{g}+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}^{\ \ \lambda}\varphi+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-n+\Delta\varphi\\[8.53581pt]
&&+2\mathrm{Re}\left(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}T^{\nu}_{\lambda\alpha}\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\nu\overline{\beta}}\varphi\right)+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}R_{\alpha\overline{\beta}\rho\overline{\mu}}\nabla^{\rho}\varphi\nabla^{\overline{\mu}}\varphi,\end{array}

The notation
\mathrm{Re}(z)
stands for the real part of the complex
z
.

Proof.
In the following, we denote
A=g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}^{\lambda}\varphi
. Let us compute
\Delta^{\prime}\varphi
,

\Delta^{\prime}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}=2\mathrm{Re}(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\overline{\beta}\lambda}\varphi\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi)+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}^{\lambda}\varphi+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\lambda\overline{\beta}}\varphi.

To interchange the order of derivation in the third derivative terms, we use the following relations

(5)

\nabla_{\alpha\overline{\beta}\lambda}\varphi=\nabla_{\lambda\alpha\overline{\beta}}\varphi+T^{\nu}_{\lambda\alpha}\nabla_{\nu\overline{\beta}}\varphi

and

(6)

\nabla_{\alpha\overline{\beta\mu}}\varphi=\nabla_{\overline{\mu}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\varphi+T^{\overline{\nu}}_{\overline{\mu\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\overline{\nu}}\varphi+g^{\rho\overline{\nu}}R_{\alpha\overline{\beta}\rho\overline{\mu}}\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\varphi.

So that

(7)

\Delta^{\prime}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}=2\mathrm{Re}(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\lambda\alpha\overline{\beta}}\varphi\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi)+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}^{\lambda}\varphi+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\lambda\overline{\beta}}\varphi+E.

where

\displaystyle E=2\mathrm{Re}(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}T^{\nu}_{\lambda\alpha}\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\nu\overline{\beta}}\varphi)+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g^{\rho\overline{\nu}}R_{\alpha\overline{\beta}\rho\overline{\mu}}\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\varphi\nabla^{\overline{\mu}}\varphi.

Using the definition of
g^{\prime}
, we see that

(8)

\nabla_{\nu\overline{\beta}}\varphi=g^{\prime}_{\nu\overline{\beta}}-g_{\nu\overline{\beta}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g^{\prime}_{\nu\overline{\beta}}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}.

So

(9)

g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\lambda\overline{\beta}}\varphi=+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-n+\Delta\varphi.

Now, differentiating once equation
(3)
, we obtain

(10)

g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{a\alpha\overline{\beta}}\varphi=\nabla_{a}\log f\quad\mbox{for all }a\in\{1,\dots,n,\overline{1},\dots,\overline{n}\}.

Reporting (9) and (10) into (7) gives the relation (4).

Proof of theorem 1.
Let
k
be strictly positive real to be determined later. Denote

\Gamma(\varphi)=|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\exp[\exp[k(C_{0}-\varphi)]].

Since
M
is compact,
\Gamma
attains its maximum at some point
P\in M
. We are done if we prove that
|\nabla\varphi|(P)\leq C
. So, assume that

(11)

|\nabla\varphi|(P)\geq 1.

The function
\log\Gamma
attains also a maximum at
P
. Therefore

(12)

\frac{\nabla_{\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}-ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}\nabla_{\alpha}\varphi=0,

and writing that the Laplacian
\Delta^{\prime}\log\Gamma=g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\log\Gamma
of
\log\Gamma
is non positive at
P
, we obtain

(13)

\frac{\Delta^{\prime}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}-\frac{{|\nabla|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|^{\prime}}^{2}}{|\nabla\varphi|^{4}}+ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}(k|\nabla\varphi|^{\prime 2}-\Delta^{\prime}\varphi)\leq 0.

We treat first the second term in the left hand side of
(13)
. Since
\displaystyle{|\nabla\log\Gamma(\varphi)|}^{\prime 2}(P)=0
, we can write

(14)

\frac{{|\nabla|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|^{\prime}}^{2}}{|\nabla\varphi|^{4}}=-k^{2}e^{2k(C_{0}-\varphi)}{|\nabla\varphi|^{\prime}}^{2}+2\frac{ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}\mathrm{Re}(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}\varphi).

Expanding
\displaystyle\nabla|\nabla\varphi|^{2}
, in view of
(8)
, we see that

2\mathrm{Re}\left(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}\nabla_{\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}\varphi\right)=2\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-|\nabla\varphi|^{\prime 2}\right)+2\mathrm{Re}\left(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g^{\lambda\overline{\mu}}\nabla_{\alpha\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\mu}}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}\varphi\right).

Therefore, using the inequality of Cauchy, we get

2\mathrm{Re}\left(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g^{\lambda\overline{\mu}}\nabla_{\alpha\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\mu}}\varphi\nabla_{\overline{\beta}}\varphi\right)\leq\frac{A}{ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}}+ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{\prime 2}

and reporting into (14) gives us the desired control. Namely,

(15)

\frac{|\nabla^{\prime}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|^{2}}{|\nabla\varphi|^{4}}\leq\frac{A}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}+2ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}.

To deal with the first term in right hand side of
(13)
, notice that

\frac{1}{n}\nabla_{\lambda}\log F=\frac{\nabla_{\lambda}F^{\frac{1}{n}}}{F^{\frac{1}{n}}}=\frac{\nabla_{\lambda}\left[(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{x}\right]}{F^{\frac{1}{n}}}+\frac{\left[(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{t}\right]^{\prime}}{F^{\frac{1}{n}}}\nabla_{\lambda}\varphi+\frac{\frac{\partial(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{p}}{\partial p^{a}}}{F^{\frac{1}{n}}}\nabla_{\lambda a}\varphi.

\nabla_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}\varphi=\nabla_{\overline{\mu}\lambda}\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nabla_{\lambda\mu}\varphi=\nabla_{\mu\lambda}\varphi+T^{\nu}_{\mu\lambda}\nabla_{\nu}\varphi,

we obtain the following equality

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}\mathrm{Re}\left(\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\lambda}\log F\right)&=&\displaystyle\frac{2}{F^{\frac{1}{n}}}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{\partial(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{p}}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\nabla_{\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)+\frac{2}{F^{\frac{1}{n}}}\mathrm{Re}\left(\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\lambda}(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{x}\right)\\[11.38109pt]
&&\displaystyle+\frac{2(F^{\frac{1}{n}})^{\prime}_{t}|\nabla\varphi|}{F^{\frac{1}{n}}}+f^{\prime}_{t}|\nabla\varphi|\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\lambda=1}^{n}\frac{\partial(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{p}}{\partial p^{\lambda}}T^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\varphi\nabla_{\nu}\varphi\right),\end{array}

Now, in view of (12), we can write

\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{\partial(F^{\frac{1}{n}})_{p}}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\nabla_{\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)=ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{p}}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\nabla_{\alpha}\varphi\right)

and the assumption (11) and the structure conditions
(2)
imply that

(16)

2\mathrm{Re}(\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\lambda}\log F)\geq-C_{3}ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2},

where the constant
C_{3}
depends on
C_{0}
, the constant in (2) and
M
.

Inserting inequality
(16)
into
(4)
and taking account of the fact that

2\mathrm{Re}\left(g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}T^{\nu}_{\lambda\alpha}\nabla^{\lambda}\varphi\nabla_{\nu\overline{\beta}}\varphi\right)+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}R_{\alpha\overline{\beta}\rho\overline{\mu}}\nabla^{\rho}\varphi\nabla^{\overline{\mu}}\varphi\geq-C_{4}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}(1+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}),

where the constant
C_{4}
depends only on an upper bound on
M

(17)

\frac{\Delta^{\prime}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}\geq\frac{A}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}-2C_{4}\left(1+g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right)-C_{3}ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}.

Reporting (15) and (17) into (13) and taking account of
\Delta^{\prime}\varphi=n-g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}
, we see that

\left[ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}-2C_{4}\right]g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}+k^{2}e^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}|\nabla\varphi|^{\prime 2}\leq C_{5}ke^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}.

Taking
k=2C_{4}+1
, since
\displaystyle e^{k(C_{0}-\varphi)}\geq 1
, we get

(18)

g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\leq C_{5}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\nabla\varphi|^{\prime 2}(P)\leq C_{5}.

In a
g
-orthonormal frame such that the matrix
(\nabla_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}\varphi)
is diagonal. We have
\displaystyle g^{\prime\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+\nabla_{\lambda\overline{\lambda}}\varphi}
and, for each

Summing over all

n+\bigtriangleup\varphi\leq C_{6}|\nabla\varphi|^{r}

Finally, the inequality
\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\leq(n+\bigtriangleup\varphi){|\nabla\varphi|^{\prime}}^{2}
combined with the second inequality in (18) implies that

|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\leq C_{5}C_{6}|\nabla\varphi|^{r}.

The gradient of

is estimated at

. So, the proof of theorem 1 is complete.

Proof of theorem 2

Using our gradient estimate, we can adapt the calculations of P. Cherrier [3] to estimate the Laplacian which guarantees the equivalence between the metrics
g
and
g^{\prime}

(19)

a^{-1}g\leq g^{\prime}\leq ag

The constant
a
depends only on the geometry of
M
, a uniform estimates of the solution and its gradient, a bound of the
C^{2}
-norm of
F

Lemma 2
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{3}(M)

(3)

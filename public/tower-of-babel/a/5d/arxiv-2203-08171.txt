Asymptotic geometric Tevelev degrees of hypersurfaces
Carl Lian

Abstract
. Let ( C , p 1 , … , p n ) (C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}) be a fixed general pointed curve and let ( X , x 1 , … , x n ) (X,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}) be a smooth hypersurface of degree e e and dimension r r with n n general points. We consider the problem of enumerating maps f : C → X f:C\to X of degree d d (as measured in the ambient projective space) such that f ⁡ ( p i ) = x i f(p_{i})=x_{i} . When e e is small compared to r r and d d is large compared to g g , e e , and r r , these numbers have been computed first by passing to a virtual count in Gromov-Witten theory obtained by Buch-Pandharipande, and then by showing (in work of the author with Pandharipande) that the virtual counts are enumerative via an analysis of boundary contributions in the moduli space of stable maps. In this note, we give a simpler computation via projective geometry.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Tevelev degrees

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
of dimension
r
, and let
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
be a non-zero effective curve class. Fix
g\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
in the stable range
2g-2+n>0
so that the moduli space of stable curves
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is non-empty.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
the moduli space of stable maps, and let
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
be the open subspace of maps with smooth domain. We consider the canonical morphisms

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\times X^{n}

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}

\displaystyle:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}

remembering in the first factor the (stabilized) domain curve of a stable map, and in the second factor the images of the marked points.

We will be concerned with the (virtual) degrees of the maps
\tau,\overline{\tau}
, counting maps
f:C\to X
of degree
\beta
with
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
, where
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
is a fixed general curve and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
are general points. The moduli space of stable maps

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
has virtual dimension equal to the dimension of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}

if and only if

(1)

\int_{\beta}c_{1}(T_{X})=r(n+g-1)\,.

If the dimension constraint (
1
) holds, then we expect to find a
finite number of maps in question. We will assume (
1
), often without mention, throughout this paper.

If it is further the case that all
dominating
components of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
are generically smooth of the expected dimension, then we may define the geometric Tevelev degrees of
X
to be the corresponding degrees of
\tau
.

Definition 1
.

Assume that (
1
) holds and that all components of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
dominating
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\times X^{n}
under the map

\tau:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\times X^{n}

are generically smooth of the expected dimension. Then, we define the
geometric Tevelev degree

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}\in\mathbb{Z}
to be the (set-theoretic) degree of
\tau
.

By definition, the geometric Tevelev degree
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}
counts maps with
smooth
domain. This count, when defined, is a priori always a non-negative integer because automorphism groups never appear in a general fiber of
\tau
, see
[
13
, Remark 4]
.

As is well-known, the components of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
can behave wildly and in particular have the wrong dimension, in which case the geometric Tevelev degrees may not be well-defined. On the other hand, upon passing to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, one gains access to a virtual fundamental class living in the expected homological dimension, and its pushforward by
\tau
allows one to define virtual Tevelev degrees in greater generality.

Definition 2
.

Assume that (
1
) holds. The
virtual Tevelev degree

{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}\in\mathbb{Q}
is defined by

\overline{\tau}_{*}([\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\vir})={\mathsf{vTev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}\cdot[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}]\in A^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}),

where

\overline{\tau}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}

is as above.

While the enumeration of maps with fixed domain, viewed from various perspectives, is an old question, the systematic study of Tevelev degrees has received renewed interest after a result of Tevelev
[
15
, Theorem 6.2]
showing that
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,(g+1)[\mathbb{P}^{1}],g+3}=2^{g}
(see Definition
1
) for all
g\geq 0
. We review some of the subsequent results.

1.2.
Calculations of virtual Tevelev degrees

The virtual Tevelev degrees are, at least in principle, easier to compute, owing to the following formula of Buch-Pandharipande.

Theorem 3
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.3]

Assume that (
1
) holds. Then, we have:

{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}=\Coeff({\mathsf{P}}^{\star n}\star{\mathsf{E}}^{\star g},q^{\beta}{\mathsf{P}}).

Here,
{\mathsf{P}}\in H^{*}(X)\subset QH^{*}(X)
denotes the point class in the small quantum cohomology ring of
X
,
{\mathsf{E}}
denotes the
quantum Euler class

{\mathsf{E}}=\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}^{\vee}\star\gamma_{j}\in QH^{*}(X)

where
\{\gamma_{j}\},\{\gamma^{\vee}_{j}\}
are dual bases for
H^{*}(X)
with respect to the (classical) cup product,
\star
denotes the quantum product, and
\Coeff(\alpha,q^{\beta}{\mathsf{P}})
denotes the
q^{\beta}{\mathsf{P}}
-coefficient when
\alpha
is expanded as a power series in the quantum variable
q
.

From Theorem
3
, one can obtain explicit formulas for virtual Tevelev degrees in a number of examples. In the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
, we have:

Theorem 4
.

[
5
, (3)]

Assume that (
1
) holds, that is, that
n=\frac{r+1}{r}\cdot d-g+1
. Then,

{\mathsf{vTev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{r}}_{g,d,n}=(r+1)^{g}.

Here, we use
\beta=d
to denote the class of a curve of degree
d
. Note that in order for
n=\frac{r+1}{r}\cdot d-g+1
to be an integer, we require
r|d
. A closely related virtual count was obtained by Bertram-Daskalopoulos-Wentworth before the systematic development of virtual fundamental classes in Gromov-Witten theory, see
[
3
, Theorem 2.9]
.

For low degree hypersurfaces, there is a similar result. For a hypersurface
X_{e}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
of degree
e
, condition (
1
) becomes

(2)

n=\frac{r+2-e}{r}\cdot d-g+1.

Theorem 5
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.5]
Let

X_{e}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree

e
and dimension
r
. Suppose that
3\leq e\leq\frac{r+3}{2}
. Then, for all
g,d,n\geq 0
satisfying
2g-2+n>0
and (
2
), we have

{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X_{e}}_{g,d,n}=((e-1)!)^{n}\cdot(r+2-e)^{g}\cdot e^{(d-n)e-g+1}.

The case of quadric hypersurfaces
(e=2)
is exceptional, see
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
.

A non-trivial aspect of the proof of Theorem
5
is controlling the contributions from primitive cohomology of
X
. The explicit computation of
{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X_{e}}_{g,d,n}
in the remainder of the Fano range
\frac{r+4}{2}\leq e\leq r+1
remains open, but Cela
[
6
]
has given partial results.

See also
[
5
, §3-4]
for results on
{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}
when
X=G/P
(which also includes the case of quadric hypersurfaces).

1.3.
Calculations of geometric Tevelev degrees

The computation of geometric Tevelev degrees is more subtle. Complete results were first given in the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
by Cela-Pandharipande-Schmitt
[
8
]
via intersection theory on Hurwitz spaces of branched covers. Brill-Noether theory guarantees that the geometric Tevelev degrees of
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
(in fact, of any
\mathbb{P}^{r}
) are well-defined for all
d\geq 1
.

Theorem 6
.

[
8
, Theorem 6]

For all
g,d,n\geq 0
satisfying
2g-2+n>0
and
n=2d-g+1
, we have:

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,d,n}=2^{g}-\sum_{i=0}^{g-d-1}\binom{g}{i}+(g-d-1)\binom{g}{g-d}+(d-g-1)\binom{g}{g-d+1}.

Binomial coefficients
\binom{g}{i}
with
i<0
are interpreted to vanish. In particular,
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,d,n}=2^{g}
whenever
d\geq g+1
(equivalently,
d\leq n-2
), agreeing with the virtual count of Theorem
4
.

Later work of the author with Farkas gave the following alternate formula via limit linear series and Schubert calculus.

Theorem 7
.

[
9
, Theorem 1.3]

For all
g,d,n\geq 0
satisfying
2g-2+n>0
and
n=2d-g+1
, we have:

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,d,n}=\int_{\Gr(2,d+1)}\sigma_{1}^{g}\sum_{i+j=2d-2-g}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}.

That the expression on the right evaluates to
2^{g}
when
d\geq g+1
is non-trivial; a brute force calculation using the Pieri rule is possible, but a combinatorial proof via the RSK algorithm was given by Gillespie-Reimer-Berg
[
10
]
. The formulas of Theorems
6
and
7
have been extended to counts of covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with arbitrary ramification profiles, see
[
7
]
.

The calculation of
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{r}}_{g,d,n}
for
r>1
is considerably more difficult and will be addressed in forthcoming work
[
12
]
. However, when
d
is large compared to
r
and
g
, it is again true that the geometric and virtual Tevelev degrees agree:

Theorem 8
.

[
9
, Theorem 1.2]

Suppose that
d\geq rg+r
, or equivalently that
d\leq n-2
. Then, for all
g\geq 0
, we have:

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{r}}_{g,d,n}=(r+1)^{g}.

This leads to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 9
.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective Fano variety and let
g
be a fixed genus. Then, there exists a constant
C_{X,g}
depending only on
X
and
g
such that, if
\int_{\beta}c_{1}(T_{X})>C_{X,g}
, then

{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}={\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,\beta,n}

That is, the virtual Tevelev degree is enumerative when
\beta
is sufficiently positive. (In particular, the geometric Tevelev degree is well-defined.)

Partial results in this direction have been given by the author with Pandharipande:

Theorem 10
.

Conjecture
9
holds for (among other examples):

•

[
13
, Theorem 10]
homogeneous varieties
X=G/P
,

•

[
13
, Theorem 11]
hypersurfaces
X_{e}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
of degree
e
, where
r>(e+1)(e-2)
.

Combining the enumerativity for hypersurfaces in Theorem
10
with the virtual calculations of Theorem
5
yields an explicit
asymptotic
calculation of geometric Tevelev degrees of hypersurfaces of low degree, that is, when the degree
d
of
f:C\to X
is sufficiently large. We state the conclusion for completeness.

Theorem 11
.

Let
X_{e}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree

e
and dimension
r
. Suppose that
e\geq 3
and
r>(e+1)(e-2)
. Then

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X_{e}}_{g,d,n}=((e-1)!)^{n}\cdot(r+2-e)^{g}\cdot e^{(d-n)e-g+1}

for all
d
sufficiently large (compared to
g,e,r
).

1.4.
Geometric Tevelev degrees of hypersurfaces via projective geometry

Given the simplicity of the formulas of Theorem
11
, the authors of
[
5
]
speculate that a proof via projective geometry along the lines of the asymptotic calculation of
[
13
]
for
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
should be possible (see also
[
13
, Question 28]
). In this paper, we give such a proof.

We first outline the strategy; assume first for simplicity that
g=0
, where the key idea already appears. Let
V=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(d))
and let
\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})\cong\mathbb{P}^{(r+2)(d+1)-1}
denote the space of
(r+2)
-tuples
[f_{0}:\cdots:f_{r+1}]
, where
f_{i}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(d))
, the
f_{j}
taken up to simultaneous scaling. A generic choice of
f_{j}
defines a map
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
of degree
d
, but the degree of this map drops when the
f_{j}
share common factors.

Now, fix general points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X_{e}=Z(F)\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
. The condition that either
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
or all of
f_{0},\ldots,f_{r+1}
vanish at
p
defines a linear subspace
{\mathsf{Inc}}(p_{i},x_{i})\subset\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})
of codimension
r+1
. We wish to intersect the
{\mathsf{Inc}}(p_{i},x_{i})
with the locus
J^{\prime}_{F}
consisting of
f
with image in
X_{e}
, that is, with
F(f_{0},\ldots,f_{r+1})=0
. The subscheme
J^{\prime}_{F}
is an intersection of
de+1
hypersurfaces of degree
e
in
\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})
.

One quickly sees that the loci
{\mathsf{Inc}}(p_{i},x_{i})
and
J^{\prime}_{F}
do not intersect in the expected dimension, owing to the fact that the
x_{i}
are general points in
X_{e}
, not in
\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
. This is easily circumvented by imposing instead the condition that
f(p_{i})\in L_{i}
, where
L_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
is a fixed general line through
x_{i}
; the corresponding subscheme
{\mathsf{Inc}}(p_{i},L_{i})\subset\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})
now has the correct expected codimension of
r
upon restriction to
J^{\prime}_{F}
, but introduces an extra factor of
e
in the intersection number, coming from the
e-1
other points of
L_{i}\cap X
.

There is, however, a more serious problem: typically, the intersection of the
{\mathsf{Inc}}(p_{i},L_{i})
and
J^{\prime}_{F}
contains positive-dimensional excess loci corresponding to degenerate
f
. For example, if
d\geq n
, we may take
f_{0},\ldots,f_{r+1}
to be constant multiples of each other vanishing on all of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
, such that the image of the constant map
f=[f_{0}:\cdots:f_{r+1}]
lies in
X_{e}
. As (
1
) implies that

n\sim\frac{r+2-e}{r}\cdot d,

we have that if
e\geq 3
, even when
d
becomes sufficiently large, such degenerate maps
f
always exist.

We resolve the issue by blowing up the linear subspaces on
\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})
where all of
f_{0},\ldots,f_{r+1}
vanish at one of the
p_{i}
. More explicitly, let

Y\subset\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}

be the incidence correspondence (cut out by bilinear equations) consisting of
(f,x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})
for which either
f_{0},\ldots,f_{r+1}
simultaneously vanish on
p_{i}
or
f(p_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}
. Then, we cut out (in a precise way) a subscheme
J_{F}\subset Y
approximating the
proper transform

J^{\prime}_{F}\subset\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})
.

The key observation is that, on the locus where
f
has a simple base-point
p_{i}
, if
\widetilde{f}
denotes the map of degree
d-1
obtained by dividing by the common factor, then
(f,x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})\in J_{F}
if and only if the points
\widetilde{x}_{i}:=\widetilde{f}(p_{i})
and
x^{\prime}_{i}
lie on a line contained in
X_{e}
(Proposition
17
).

This new condition may alternatively be regarded as the requirement that there exist a stable map of degree
d
to
X_{e}
defined by the degree
d-1
map
\widetilde{f}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X_{e}
and a rational tail mapping isomorphically to the line on
X_{e}
through
\widetilde{x}_{i}
and
x^{\prime}_{i}
. In light of this comparison, we employ a similar analysis as in
[
13
]
to argue that, under the conditions of Theorem
11
, the intersection of
J_{F}
and the pullback to
Y
of

\prod_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}\subset(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}

is a reduced, zero-dimensional subscheme supported on the locus of
f
which are well-defined at all of the
p_{i}
. In particular, the degree of this zero-dimensional subscheme is equal to
e^{n}\cdot{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X_{e}}_{0,d,n}
.

When
g
is arbitrary, we relativize this construction over the Jacobian
\Jac^{d}(C)
(following the strategy of
[
3
]
) to prove Theorem
11
.

We will record, along the way of the proof, a new effective bound on
d
, above which the virtual Tevelev degrees are enumerative. We now state the more refined result.

Theorem 12
.

Let
X_{e}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree

e
and dimension
r
. Suppose that
e\geq 3
and
r>(e+1)(e-2)
.

Then,

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X_{e}}_{g,d,n}=((e-1)!)^{n}\cdot(r+2-e)^{g}\cdot e^{(d-n)e-g+1}

for
all

d
if
g=0
, and whenever

d>\frac{r((3g-2)(1+e)+1+g(r+2))}{r-(e+1)(e-2)}

if
g>0
.

That no condition on
d
is necessary when
g=0
is also
[
13
, Corollary 34]
.

In fact, for a
general
hypersurface
X_{e}
of degree
e\leq r-1
, then the irreducibility of the full Kontsevich space
\overline{M}_{0,n}(X_{e},d)
(due to Riedl-Yang
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
, and also Harris-Roth-Starr
[
11
]
, Beheshti-Kumar
[
2
]
in smaller ranges for
e
) implies that the virtual Tevelev degree
{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X_{e}}_{0,d,n}
is enumerative. In particular, if
3\leq e\leq\frac{e+3}{2}
and
X_{e}
is general, then the formula
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X_{e}}_{0,d,n}=((e-1)!)^{n}\cdot e^{(d-n)e+1}
holds for a fixed pair of integers
(d,n)
satisfying (
2
), see Remark
23
. (To guarantee the formula to hold on
X_{e}
for
any

(d,n)
, one should take
X_{e}
to be
very
general.) We thank Eric Riedl for pointing this out.

We also note here that, while we will not need the arguments of
[
13
]
in full generality, the transversality analysis of our new construction confronts the same issues of controlling excess dimensions of curves on hypersurfaces, and in particular, produces the same bound
r>(e+1)(e-2)
for (asymptotic) enumerativity. Thus, our proof of Theorem
12
, which was previously established through the virtual calculation of
[
5
]
and then the enumerativity analysis of
[
13
]
, may be viewed in comparison in the following light. First, we give a more elementary path to the counts obtained as virtual degrees in
[
5
]
. Then, we apply special cases of the arguments of
[
13
]
to show that these counts are equal to the desired geometric Tevelev degrees.

1.5.
Further directions

We make several remarks concerning extensions of this work.

(i)

We expect the method to go through for complete intersections of low degree, see also
[
5
, §5]
and
[
13
, Proposition 26]
.

(ii)

We will see that our calculation uses the fact that the degree
e
of the hypersurface
X_{e}
is at least 3 in an essential way, see step (3) of §
2.3
. However, we believe that minor modifications should allow one to handle the exceptional case
e=2
similarly.

(iii)

Our transversality analysis in §
2.4
, which makes no improvements on the method of
[
13
]
, seems to go through highly suboptimal bounds. We expect more refined estimates of excess dimensions of families of stable maps on hypersurfaces to allow for stronger results.

(iv)

While our calculation (provably) produces a geometrically enumerative count only under the conditions given in Theorem
12
, our construction produces a cycle class of homological dimension 0 and degree equal to the corresponding virtual Tevelev degree under the much milder bounds
r\geq 2e-3
and
d\geq 2g
. Thus, it seems plausible that excess contributions in our geometric setup can be identified with boundary contributions to the virtual Tevelev degrees in greater generality.

(v)

When
e>\frac{r+3}{2}
, our method fails because because the construction imposes no non-trivial condition on
f
whenever
f
has a
double
base-point at
x_{i}
, which allows
f
to vanish to order 2 at
all

x_{i}
whenever
d\geq 2n
. However, working on further (iterated) blowups of
\mathbb{P}(V^{r+2})
should allow one to extend the calculation to larger degrees
e
and predict formulas for the corresponding virtual Tevelev degrees, see
[
6
]
.

(vi)

One can consider more general insertions at the marked points of
X
, which will be carried out in a later version of
[
5
]
, see also
[
4
]
. More precisely, for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, fix a cohomology class
\Omega_{i}\in H^{m_{i}}(X)
. Then, define
{\langle\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{n}\rangle}^{X}_{g,n}
by the integral

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}}\overline{\tau}_{*}([\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\vir})\cdot([(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})]\otimes\Omega_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Omega_{n})\in\mathbb{Q},

where
[(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})]\in A_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
is the class of any (general) curve and

\overline{\tau}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}

is the forgetful morphism as before. The case
\Omega_{i}=[x_{i}]\in H^{2r}(X)
corresponds to the virtual Tevelev degree
{\mathsf{vTev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
defined earlier. Note that
{\langle\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{n}\rangle}^{X}_{g,n}
vanishes unless

\int_{\beta}c_{1}(T_{X})=r(g-1)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}.

When
X=X_{e}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
is a smooth hypersurface and the
\Omega_{i}
are pullbacks of powers of the hyperplane class on
\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
, our geometric calculation produces more generally a zero-dimensional cycle of degree equal (up to a factor of
e^{n}
, as in the case
\Omega_{i}=[x_{i}]
) to
{\langle\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{n}\rangle}^{X}_{g,n}
, see §
2.6
. We expect again these counts to be enumerative for fixed
X,g
and
d
large, but we do not consider this aspect of the question here.

2.
Proof of Theorem
12

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let
X=Z(F)\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
, where
F\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r+1},\mathcal{O}(e))
, be a smooth hypersurface of degree
e\geq 3
, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
be general points. (We will drop the subscript
e
from the introduction denoting the degree of
X
in the rest of the paper.) Fix a general pointed curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})

\widetilde{f}(p_{i})
and
x^{\prime\prime}_{i}
, such that
\overline{f}(p^{\prime}_{i})=x^{\prime\prime}_{i}
. Denote the degree of
\overline{f}
by
d^{\prime}=d-b_{0}-2b_{2}
.

The map
\overline{f}
lives in a stratum

\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}(X,d^{\prime})

parametrizing stable maps whose domain curve is homeomorphic to
D
. (When
b_{1}=0
, we have that
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
is the open stratum of stable maps with smooth domain.)

Proposition 19
.

Let
\tau:\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}}
be the forgetful map. Assume that at least one of
b_{0},b_{1},b_{2}
is strictly positive. Then, under the assumptions of Theorem
12
, the map
\tau
is not dominant.

Proof.

Let
\delta=\vdim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,d))=\dim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n})
. Then, we have

\dim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}})=\Delta-(r+1)b_{2}

and

\displaystyle\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})

\displaystyle=\vdim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}(X,d^{\prime}))-b_{1}

\displaystyle=\delta-(d-d^{\prime})(r+2-e)-b_{2}-b_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\delta-(2r+5-2e)b_{2}-(r+2-e)b_{0}-b_{1}.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\dom}
be the union of the components of
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
dominating the target of
\tau
. We now argue as in
[
13
, Proposition 22]
to show that

\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\dom})<\dim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}}),

which will immediately yield a contradiction.

If
n-b_{1}-b_{2}\geq\max(2g,1)
, then
[
13
, Proposition 13, Lemma 16]
implies that
\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\dom})=\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})
, so we have

\displaystyle\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})

\displaystyle=\delta-(2r+5-2e)b_{2}-(r+2-e)b_{0}-b_{1}

\displaystyle<\delta-(r+1)b_{2}

\displaystyle=\dim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}}),

as long as
r\geq 2e-3
and at least one of
b_{0},b_{1},b_{2}
is strictly positive.

Otherwise, suppose that
n-b_{1}-b_{2}\leq\max(2g-1,0)
. We claim first that

\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\dom})\leq\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})+h^{1}(D_{\spine},\overline{f}^{*}T_{X}).

Indeed, for a general stable map
\overline{f}
in
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\dom}
, all components
R_{i}
of
D
other than
D_{\spine}
are lines passing through a general point of
X
, onto which the restriction of
T_{X}
is therefore globally generated. Indeed, we have
T_{X}|_{D}\cong\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}(a_{i})
for some integers
a_{i}
, and the
a_{i}
must be non-negative in order for the points
x_{i}=\overline{f}(p_{i})
to deform to first order in all tangent directions.

In particular, these lines move in a family of expected dimension (
[
13
, Proposition 13]
), and the condition of passing through any fixed node locally has the expected codimension of
r
. As a result, the excess dimension
\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\dom})-\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})
is bounded above by that of the stable map when restricted to
D_{\spine}
, which is
h^{1}(D_{\spine},\overline{f}^{*}T_{X})
.

We now have

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\dom})

\displaystyle\leq\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})+h^{1}(D_{\spine},\overline{f}^{*}T_{X})

\displaystyle\leq\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})+(d^{\prime}-b_{1})e+1+g(r+2)

\displaystyle\leq\vdim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})+(d-n-b_{2}+\max(2g-1,0))e+1+g(r+2)

\displaystyle=\delta-(2r+5-2e)b_{2}-(r+2-e)b_{0}-b_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(d-n-b_{2}+\max(2g-1,0))e+1+g(r+2)

\displaystyle\leq\delta-(2r+5-2e)b_{2}-(n-b_{2}-\max(2g-1,0)))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(d-n-b_{2}+\max(2g-1,0))e+1+g(r+2)

\displaystyle=\delta-(2r+4-e)b_{2}-n+(d-n)e

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\max(2g-1,0)(1+e)+1+g(r+2)

\displaystyle\leq\dim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}})-n+(d-n)e

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\max(2g-1,0)(1+e)+1+g(r+2)

\displaystyle=\dim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}})+d\cdot\frac{(e+1)(e-2)-r}{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[\max(2g-1,0)+(g-1)](1+e)+1+g(r+2)

\displaystyle<\dim(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}}),

as long as
(e+1)(e-2)<r
and

d>\frac{r((\max(2g-1,0)+(g-1))(1+e)+1+g(r+2))}{r-(e+1)(e-2)}.

When
(e+1)(e-2)<r
and
g=0
, the lower bound on
d
is negative, so we obtain the conclusion for all
d
; if
g>0
, the bound becomes

d>\frac{r((3g-2)(1+e)+1+g(r+2))}{r-(e+1)(e-2)}.

∎

Corollary 20
.

Any
(f,x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})\in T
has
f
base-point free.

Proof.

Proposition
19
shows that
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
does not dominate the target of the forgetful map

\tau:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}(X,d^{\prime})\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n-b_{2}}\times X^{n-b_{2}}.

Thus, varying over all possible
\Gamma
, we conclude that for a fixed general pointed curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
, there exists a non-empty open set
U\subset X^{n}
with the property that, for any
(x^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime\prime}_{n})\in U
, there does not exist a stable map
\overline{f}
as above with
(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2})\neq(0,0,0)
and
\overline{f}(p^{\prime}_{i})=x^{\prime\prime}_{i}
.

It now suffices to show that, for fixed
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in U
, a general choice of lines
L_{i}\ni x_{i}
(assumed transverse to
X
) has the property that, for any choices of
x^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in L_{i}\cap X
, we have
(x^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime\prime}_{n})\in U
. Suppose that this is not the case. Then, consider the (proper) map

\lambda:X^{n}\backslash U\to\prod_{i}\mathbb{P}_{i},

where
\mathbb{P}_{i}\cong\mathbb{P}^{r}
is the space of lines through
x_{i}
, sending
(x^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime\prime}_{n})\in X^{n}
to the lines
L_{i}
through
x_{i},x^{\prime\prime}_{i}
. For dimensional reasons,
\lambda
cannot be dominant, and a general collection of
(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})
in the complement of the image suffices.
∎

Proposition 21
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
12
, the subscheme

is reduced of the expected dimension 0. In particular, the intermediate subschemes

J_{F}\subset J^{(e)}_{F}\subset Y\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}

.

Proof.

The second claim will follow from the first. Corollary
20
shows that every point of

corresponds to an honest map

of degree

with

of

. We first observe that, because the
L_{i},X
intersect transversely at the

, and because

has been constrained to lie on both

, we have that

is trivial upon projection to the factor

.

From here, one easily verifies that, in fact,

defines a non-trivial relative tangent vector at
[f]
to the forgetful morphism

\tau:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\times X^{n}.

On the other hand,
[

(for fixed

Let

be a general hypersurface of degree

. Then, the Kontsevich space
\overline{M}_{0,n}(X,d^{\prime})
is irreducible and generically smooth of the expected dimension for all positive

[

,
2
,

]

If we assume further that

2.5.
Calculation

We now compute the degree of
T
. We do so by following the construction; in each step, we obtain a new (excess) cycle class by capping with a class pulled back from the ambient space
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}
. Let
H=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1))
be the relative hyperplane class on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})
, and let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
be the hyperplane classes on the factors
\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
.

(1)

Y\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}
is cut out by pulling back the class of the diagonal
\mathbb{P}^{r+1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}\times\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
under the rational map
\ev:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}\dashrightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{r+1}\times\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}
. Because
\ev
is given by relative linear projections and the codimension of the base locus is strictly larger than that of the class being pulled back, the class of the subscheme
Y
is

[Y]=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(H^{r+1}+H^{r}H_{i}+\cdots+H_{i}^{r+1})\right)\cap[\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}].

(2)

Arguing as in Lemma
16
, we have

[J^{(e)}_{F}]^{\exc}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{e}((k-1)H+(e+1-k)H_{i})\right)\cap[Y]

in
A_{\dim(Y)-ne}(J^{(e)}_{F})
. Indeed,
J^{(e)}_{F}\subset Y
is cut out by sections of the (pulled back) line bundles

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})}(e)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-(e+1-k)E_{i})

for
k=1,2,\ldots,e
and exceptional divisors
E_{i}\subset Y_{p_{i}}
.

(Compare
[
5
, §5.3]
.)

(3)

Consider the top Chern class

c_{t}(\nu^{*}(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes e}(-ep_{1}-\cdots-ep_{n}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})}(e)))

where
t=(d-n)e-g+1
is the rank of the bundle
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes e}(-ep_{1}-\cdots-ep_{n}))
on
\Jac^{d}(C)
. This is equal to

\sum_{m=0}^{g}c_{m}(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes e}(-ep_{1}-\cdots-ep_{n})))\cdot(eH)^{t-m}

where we have dropped the
\nu^{*}
on the bundle pulled back from
\Jac^{d}(C)
. Note that the Chern classes in the first factor vanish above degree
\dim(\Jac^{d}(C))=g
.

Now, a standard computation (see e.g.
[
1
, Chapter VIII, §2]
) shows that

c_{m}(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes e}(-ep_{1}-\cdots-ep_{n})))=\frac{(-e^{2})^{m}}{m!}\cdot\theta^{m}\in H^{2m}(\Jac^{d}(C)),

where
\theta\in H^{2}(\Jac^{d}(C))
is the class of the theta divisor.

Therefore,

[J_{F}]^{\exc}=\left(e^{t}\cdot\sum_{m=0}^{g}\frac{(-e)^{m}}{m!}\cdot\theta^{m}H^{t-m}\right)\cap[J^{(e)}_{F}]^{\exc}.

(4)

T\subset J_{F}
is cut out by
r
linear conditions on each factor of
\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
, and has the expected dimension of 0. Therefore,

[T]=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}^{r}\right)\cap[J_{F}]^{\exc}

Note that this holds even if the intermediate schemes
J_{F},J^{(e)}_{F}
have the wrong dimension; restricting the construction to a neighborhood of
T
avoiding all excess components yields simply the fundamental class of
T
in the end.

Proposition 24
.

The degree of
T
as a 0-cycle on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{r+1})^{n}
is

(e!)^{n}\cdot(r+2-e)^{g}\cdot e^{(d-n)e+1}.

Proof.

Applying the projection formula, we need to multiply the formulas for the four classes computed above, and extract all coefficients with degree exactly
r+1
in each
H_{i}
. Because we automatically get
r
factors of
H_{i}
from step (4) and one factor of
H_{i}
from the term
(eH_{i})
in step (2), we must take the term
H^{r+1}
in all
n
factors in step (1), and the terms
(e-1)H,(e-2)H,\ldots,H
each
n
times (in addition to the contributions from
eH_{i}
) in step (2).

We therefore find that

[T]=(H_{1}\cdots H_{n})^{r+1}\cdot(e)!^{n}\cdot e^{t}\cdot H^{n(r+1)+(e-1)n}\cdot\sum_{m=0}^{g}\frac{(-e)^{m}}{m!}\cdot\theta^{m}H^{t-m},

Pushing forward to
\Jac^{d}(C)
and applying the projection formula, we obtain

\nu_{*}[T]=(e)!^{n}\cdot e^{t}\cdot\sum_{m=0}^{g}\frac{(-e)^{m}}{m!}\cdot\theta^{m}\cdot s_{g-m}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2}).

Finally,
[
1
, Chapter VIII, §2]
(see also
[
3
, Proof of Theorem 2.9]
) shows that

s_{g-m}(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus r+2})=(r+2)^{g-m}\cdot\frac{\theta^{g-m}}{(g-m)!},

so we conclude that

\displaystyle\deg(T)

\displaystyle=(e)!^{n}\cdot e^{t}\cdot\frac{\deg(\theta^{g})}{g!}\cdot\sum_{m=0}^{g}\binom{g}{m}(-e)^{m}(r+2)^{g-m}

\displaystyle=(e!)^{n}\cdot(r+2-e)^{g}\cdot e^{(d-n)e-g+1}.

where we apply Poincaré’s formula
[
1
, p. 25]
and have substituted back
t=(d-n)e-g+1
.
∎

We now conclude the main Theorem.

Proof of Theorem
12
.

Combine Proposition
24
and Corollary
22
.

2.6.
More general insertions

We observe here that one can just as easily replace the incidence condition in step (4) that
x^{\prime}_{i}\in L_{i}
with the condition that
x^{\prime}_{i}
lie in a linear space
X_{i}\cong\mathbb{P}^{\ell_{i}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
. In order for the expected number of maps
f:C\to X
of degree
d
satisfying the property that
f(p_{i})\in X_{i}
to be finite, we require

d(r+2-e)=r(n+g-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}.

The corresponding virtual count is
{\langle\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{n}\rangle}^{X}_{g,n}
, where
\Omega_{i}=[X_{i}\cap X]
, see (
(vi)
) of §
1.5
.

One may construct a scheme
T_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}}
of
expected
dimension 0 as above, parametrizing maps
f:C\to X
of degree
d
with
f(p_{i})\in X_{i}
. Write now

(1+z+\cdots+z^{r+1})\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{e-1}(j+(e-j)z)=\alpha_{1}z+\cdots+\alpha_{e+r+1}z^{e+r+1},

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{e+r+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If it happens that
T_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}}
has actual dimension 0, then proceeding as in Proposition
24
, we compute

\deg(T_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}})=(r+2-e)^{g}\cdot e^{(d-n)e-g+1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{\ell_{i}},

which matches the virtual degree
{\langle\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{n}\rangle}^{X}_{g,n}
upon dividing by
e^{n}
as in the proof of Theorem
11
, see
[
4
, pp. 21-23]
. We do not consider the question of transversality here.

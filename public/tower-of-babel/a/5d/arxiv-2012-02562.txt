Rational approximation of holomorphic maps
Jacek Bochnak, Wojciech Kucharz

Abstract
Let X X be a complex nonsingular affine algebraic variety, K K a compact holomorphically convex subset of X X , and Y Y a homogeneous variety for some complex linear algebraic group. We prove that a holomorphic map f : K → Y f\colon K\to Y can be uniformly approximated on K K by regular maps K → Y K\to Y if and only if f f is homotopic to a regular map K → Y K\to Y . However, it can happen that a null homotopic holomorphic map K → Y K\to Y does not admit uniform approximation on K K by regular maps X → Y X\to Y . Here, a map φ : K → Y \varphi\colon K\to Y is called holomorphic (resp. regular) if there exist an open (resp. a Zariski open) neighborhood U ⊆ X U\subseteq X of K K and a holomorphic (resp. regular) map φ ~ : U → Y \tilde{\varphi}\colon U\to Y such that φ ~ | K = φ \tilde{\varphi}|_{K}=\varphi .



Key words and phrases.
Algebraic manifold, holomorphic map, regular map, approximation.

Introduction

Throughout this paper,
algebraic varieties
are complex algebraic varieties understood in the sense of Serre
[
27
, p. 226]
. Each algebraic variety has an underlying structure of a complex space. Nonsingular algebraic varieties are complex (holomorphic) manifolds and will be called
algebraic manifolds
. Morphisms of algebraic varieties will be called
regular maps
(clearly, they are also holomorphic maps). Unless explicitly stated otherwise, all topological notions relating to algebraic varieties will refer to the Euclidean topology determined by the standard metric on
\mathbb{C}
.

An algebraic variety
Y
is said to be
homogeneous
for an algebraic group
G
if
G
acts transitively on
Y
, the action
G\times Y\to Y
,
(a,y)\mapsto a\cdot y
being a regular map. Note that each homogeneous algebraic variety is an equidimensional algebraic manifold. An algebraic group is said to be
linear
if it is biregularly isomorphic to a Zariski closed subgroup of the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, for some
n
.

Let
X
,
Y
be algebraic varieties and let
K
be a compact subset of
X
. A map
f\colon K\to Y
is said to be
holomorphic
(resp.
regular
) if it is the restriction of a holomorphic (resp. regular) map
\tilde{f}\colon U\to Y
defined on an open (resp. a Zariski open) neighborhood
U\subseteq X
of
K
. We say that a holomorphic map
f\colon K\to Y
can be
approximated by regular maps from
K
into
Y
if for every neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(K,Y)
of
f
, where
\mathcal{C}(K,Y)
is the space of all continuous maps endowed with the compact-open topology, there exists a regular map
K\to Y
that belongs to
\mathcal{U}
. The compact-open topology on
\mathcal{C}(K,Y)
is the same as the uniform convergence topology with respect to a metric
d
on
Y
which induces the Euclidean topology on
Y
. Thus, a holomorphic map
f\colon K\to Y
can be approximated by regular maps from
K
into
Y
if and only if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
rational
map
\varphi
from
X
into
Y
, with domain of definition containing
K
, such that
d(f(x),\varphi(x))<\varepsilon
for all
x\in K
.

Recall that a compact subset
K
of a reduced complex space
Z
is
holomorphically convex
in
Z
if for every point
p\in Z\setminus K
there exists a holomorphic function
h\colon Z\to\mathbb{C}
with
|h(p)|>|h(x)|
for all
x\in K
. In particular, every compact geometrically convex set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is holomorphically convex.

The main result of the present paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be an affine algebraic manifold,
K
a compact holomorphically convex set in
X
, and
Y
a homogeneous algebraic manifold for some linear algebraic group. Then, for a holomorphic map
f\colon K\to Y
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

f
can be approximated by regular maps from
K
into
Y
.

(b)

f
is homotopic to a regular map from
K
into
Y
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
we get the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

For
X
,
K
,
Y
as in Theorem
1.1
, every null homotopic holomorphic map from
K
into
Y
can be approximated by regular maps from
K
into
Y
.

Since every continuous map defined on a geometrically convex subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is null homotopic, we also get the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
K
be a compact geometrically convex set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
Y
be a homogeneous algebraic manifold for some linear algebraic group. Then every holomorphic map from
K
into
Y
can be approximated by regular maps from
K
into
Y
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
2
, where we first develop technical tools among which is the concept of
cascade
inspired by Gromov’s key notation of
spray

[
18
]
. Theorems
2.7
and
2.9
are of independent interest. In Example
1.5
we discuss relationships between our results and previous relevant results due to Forstnerič
[

,

]
. Subsequently we give two other illustrative examples. In the remainder of this section we assume that Theorem
1.1
holds.

Let
f\colon K\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function defined on a compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{C}
. By the Runge approximation theorem, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a rational function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{C}
without poles in
K
such that
|f(x)-\varphi(x)|<\varepsilon
for all
x\in K
. Now suppose that the compact set
K

is holomorphically convex in
\mathbb{C}
(equivalently, the set
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
is connected). Then, according to another variant of the Runge approximation theorem, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a regular (= polynomial) function
\psi\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
|f(x)-\psi(x)|<\varepsilon
for all
x\in K
.

These two versions of the Runge approximation theorem suggest two different general problems. Given two algebraic manifolds
X,Y
and a holomorphic map
f\colon K\to Y
defined on a compact subset
K
of
X
, consider the following.

Problem I
.

Under what assumptions can
f
be uniformly approximated on
K
by regular maps from
K
into
Y
?

Problem II
.

Under what assumptions can
f
be uniformly approximated on
K
by regular maps from
X
into
Y
?

The case where
Y=\mathbb{C}
(or, equivalently,

) is classical, see the excellent recent survey by Fornaes, Forstnerič and Wald
[
11
]
and the references therein. Some linearization methods are used if

is not a vector space. Assuming algebraic subellipticity of

(see
[
12
, Definition 2.1]
or
[
13
, Definition 5.6.13(e)]
), Forstnerič
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
, Lárusson and Truong
[
25
]
, and Kusakabe
[
24
]
obtained interesting results concerning Problem

. On the other hand,
[
6
,
23
]
and the present paper are contributions addressing Problem

. Suppose
now that
\dim X=1
and

is an arbitrary compact subset of

. The recent result of Benoist and

implies that approximation as in Problem

(resp. Problem

) is always

is a homogeneous space for some linear algebraic group (resp. a nonsingular

[

, approximation

(note that the analogous statement is false for some nonsingular quartic surfaces in

is of interest only for

.

The following serves to fix some notation.

Example 1.4
.

Here are some homogeneous algebraic manifolds for linear algebraic groups.

(i)

Every linear algebraic group
G
is a homogeneous manifold for
G
under action by left translations.

(ii)

If
G
is a linear algebraic group and
H
is a Zariski closed subgroup of

, then the quotient
G/H
is a homogeneous algebraic manifold for
G
. Each homogeneous algebraic manifold for
G
is, up to biregular isomorphism, of the form
G/H
.

(iii)

The Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
of

is a homogeneous algebraic manifold for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, complex projective
n
-space
\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{G}(1,n+1)
is homoegeneous for
\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
.

(iv)

For every nonnegative integer

the complex unit
n

\Sigma^{n}=\{(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}:z_{0}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n}^{2}=1\}

is a homogeneous algebraic manifold for the complex special orthogonal group
\mathrm{SO}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
. The set
\Sigma^{n}(\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\Sigma^{n}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of real points of
\Sigma^{n}
is the real unit
n
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Note that
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a deformation retract of
\Sigma^{n}
.

Next we discuss relationships between Problem
I
and Problem
II
in the context of Corollary
1.3
and
[

, Corollary 6.15.2]
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
K
be a compact geometrically convex set in
X\coloneqq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
Y
be an algebraic manifold. By Corollary
1.3
, if
Y
is homogeneous for some linear algebraic group, then every holomorphic map form
K
into
Y
can be uniformly approximated on
K
by regular maps from
K
into
Y
. On the other hand, by
[
13
, Corollary 6.15.2]
, if
Y
is algebraically subelliptic, then every holomorphic map from
K
into
Y
can be uniformly approximated on
K
by regular maps from
X
into
Y
.

Suppose that
Y
is homogeneous for a linear algebraic group
G
. Recall that a character of
G
is a homomorphism of algebraic groups
G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. If
G
is connected and without nontrivial characters, then
Y
is algebraically flexible
[
1
, Proposition 5.4]
, hence algebraically elliptic (therefore also algebraically subelliptic)
[
13
, Proposition 5.6.22(c)]
. Now, let
H
be the isotropy group of a point
y\in Y
and let us identify
Y
with the quotient
G/H
. Assume that
G
admits a nontrivial character
\chi\colon G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with
\chi(H)=\{1\}
. Note that the regular map
\varphi\colon G/H\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, defined by
\varphi(aH)=\chi(a)
for all
a\in G
, is surjective. Therefore we can choose a holomorphic map
f\colon K\to G/H
such that the composite map
\varphi\circ f\colon K\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is nonconstant. We claim that
f
cannot be uniformly approximated on
K
by regular maps from
X
into
G/H
. Indeed, supposing the claim false, we conclude that the nonconstant holomorphic map
\varphi\circ f\colon K\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
can be uniformly approximated on
K
by regular maps from
X
into
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. This yields a contradiction because every regular map
X\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is constant. It follows that the homogeneous algebraic manifold
G/H
is not algebraically subelliptic. Thus, Problems
I
and
II
lead to quite different results, and the methods of
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
18
,
22
,
24
,
25
]
based on algebraic subellipticity are not always applicable for maps into homogeneous algebraic manifolds.

As noted in Example
1.4
(iv)
, the complex
n
-sphere
\Sigma^{n}
is homogeneous for the group
\mathrm{SO}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
, which is connected and has no nontrivial character. Consequently,
\Sigma^{n}
is algebraically elliptic.

By
[
13
, Proposition 6.4.1(a)]
, if
A
is an algebraic subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of dimension at most
{n-2}
, then the complement
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus A
is algebraically elliptic. Such complements are homogeneous only in exceptional cases.

In conclusion, the notions of algebraic subellipticity and algebraic homogeneity are complementary: neither implies the other.

The next example requires some preparation. Let
X
be a quasiprojective algebraic manifold. For any nonnegative integer
k
, a cohomology class in
H^{2k}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is said to be
algebraic
if it corresponds via the cycle map to an algebraic cycle of codimension
k
on
X
, see
[
15
, Chapter 19]
. The set
H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(X;\mathbb{Z})
of all algebraic cohomology classes in
H^{2k}(X;\mathbb{Z})
forms a subgroup. The groups
H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(-;\mathbb{Z})
have the expected functorial property: If
f\colon X\to Y
is a regular map between quasiprojective algebraic manifolds, then

f^{*}(H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(Y;\mathbb{Z}))\subseteq H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(X;\mathbb{Z}),

where
f^{*}\colon H^{2k}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2k}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is the homomorphism induced by
f
.

Now, fix a positive integer
k
. If
U
is a Zariski open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, then
H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(U;\mathbb{Z})=0
(it is sufficient to note that each algebraic cycle on
U
is the restriction of an algebraic cycle on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, and
H^{2k}(\mathbb{C}^{m};\mathbb{Z})=0
). Therefore, given a regular map
\varphi\colon K\to Y
from a compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into a quasiprojective algebraic manifold
Y
, we get

\varphi^{*}(H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(Y;\mathbb{Z}))=0\quad\text{in }H^{2k}(K;\mathbb{Z}).

This is the case since
\varphi
is the restriction of a regular map
\psi\colon U\to Y
defined on a Zariski open neighborhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{m}
of
K
, and

\psi^{*}(H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(Y;\mathbb{Z}))\subseteq H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(U;\mathbb{Z})=0,

where the inclusion holds by the functorial property of
H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(-;\mathbb{Z})
.

Example 1.6
.

Let
m,n,p
be integers satisfying
1\leq m\leq 2n-1<p
and let
r_{j},R_{j}
be real numbers with
0<r_{j}<R_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. The annulus

K_{j}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:r_{j}\leq|z|\leq R_{j}\}

is a compact holomorphically convex subset of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, and hence the Cartesian product
K\coloneqq K_{1}\times\cdots\times K_{m}
is a compact holomorphically convex subset of the
m
-fold product
X\coloneqq(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
. Clearly,
X
is an affine algebraic manifold. We claim that each regular map
\varphi\colon K\to\mathbb{G}(n,p)
into the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
is null homotopic. The proof depends on some topological constructions. Let
U(n,p)
denote the tautological vector bundle over
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
. To any continuous map
h\colon K\to\mathbb{G}(n,p)
one can assign the pullback vector bundle
h^{*}U(n,p)
over
K
. This gives rise to a map

\sigma\colon[K,\mathbb{G}(n,p)]\to\tilde{K}_{\mathbb{C}}(K)

from the set
[K,\mathbb{G}(n,p)]
of homotopy classes of continuous maps
K\to\mathbb{G}(n,p)
into the group
\tilde{K}_{\mathbb{C}}(K)
of stable equivalence classes of topological
\mathbb{C}
-vector bundles over
K
. Since
K
has the homotopy type of the
m
-dimensional torus
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{m}
, it follows from
[
21
, Chap. 8, Theorems 2.6 and 4.2]
that the map
\sigma
is bijective (the inequalities
1\leq m\leq m\leq 2n-1<p
are needed here). Now, the map
\varphi\colon K\to\mathbb{G}(n,p)
being regular, in view of the discussion preceding Example
1.6
, we get

\varphi^{*}(H^{2k}(\mathbb{G}(n,p));\mathbb{Z})=0\quad\text{in }H^{2k}(K;\mathbb{Z})

for all positive integers
k
(note that
H_{\mathrm{alg}}^{2k}(\mathbb{G}(n,p);\mathbb{Z})=H^{2k}(\mathbb{G}(n,p);\mathbb{Z})
). Therefore, for the pullback vector bundle
\varphi^{*}U(n,p)
over
K
, we get
c_{k}(\varphi^{*}U(n,p))=\varphi^{*}(c_{k}(U(n,p)))=0
, where
c_{k}(-)
stands for the
k
th Chern class. By
[
2
, §2.5]
, the vector bundle
\varphi^{*}U(n,p)
is topologically stably trivial. We conclude that the map
\varphi
is null homotopic, as claimed.

We know precisely the size of the set
[K,\mathbb{G}(n,p)]
of homotopy classes. Indeed,
\sigma
is a bijection and, by
[
2
, §2.5]
,
\tilde{K}_{\mathbb{C}}(K)
is a free Abelian group of rank equal to the rank of the direct sum
\bigoplus_{k>0}H^{2k}(K;\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, the set
[K,\mathbb{G}(n,p)]
is infinite if
m\geq 2
. Finally, let us note that each continuous map
K\to\mathbb{G}(n,p)
is homotopic to the restriction of a holomorphic map
X\to\mathbb{G}(n,p)
. This is the case since
K
is a retract of
X
, and each continuous map
X\to\mathbb{G}(n,p)
is homotopic to a holomorphic map by Grauert’s theorem
[
16
]
(see
[
18
]
and
[
13
, Thoerem 5.4.4]
for more general results).

Given an affine (complex) algebraic variety
X
defined over
\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
X(\mathbb{R})
the set of real points of
X
. Note that each compact subset of
X(\mathbb{R})
is holomorphically convex in
X
. Indeed, we may assume that
X
is an algebraic subset of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, for some
m
, defined by polynomial equations with real coefficients. Then
X(\mathbb{R})=X\cap\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an algebraic subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. It is well-known that every compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is holomorphically convex in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Consequently, each compact subset of
X(\mathbb{R})
is holomorphically convex in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, hence it is also holomorphically convex in
X
.

Let
X
be an affine (complex) algebraic manifold defined over
\mathbb{R}
,
K
a compact subset of
X(\mathbb{R})
, and
Y
an algebraic manifold. We may regard both
X(\mathbb{R})
and
Y
as real analytic manifolds. Clearly, a map
f\colon K\to Y
is holomorphic if and only if there exists a real analytic map
\varphi\colon W\to Y
defined on an open neighborhood
W\subseteq X(\mathbb{R})
of
K
such that
\varphi|_{K}=f
. By
[
17
]
,
Y
admits a real analytic embedding in some real Euclidean space (this is straightforward and does not require
[
17
]
if
Y
is quasiprojective). Hence, according to
[
28
, Theorem 2]
, each continuous map from
W
into
Y
can be uniformly approximated on
K
by real analytic maps from
W
into
Y
. Consequently, each continuous map from
K
into
Y
can be uniformly approximated on
K
by holomorphic maps from
K
into
Y
.

In our last example below we refer to real algebraic sets and real regular maps (see
[
4
]
for a detailed treatment of these notions).

Example 1.7
.

Let
M
be a compact connected
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold of dimension
n
. We assert that there exists an affine (complex) algebraic manifold
X
defined over
\mathbb{R}
such that its real part
K=X(\mathbb{R})
is diffeomorphic to
M
and every continuous map from
K
into the complex unit
n
-sphere
\Sigma^{n}
can be approximated by (complex) regular maps from
K
into
\Sigma^{n}
. This can be proved as follows. By
[
5
, Proposition 4.5]
, there exists a nonsingular real algebraic set
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, for some
m
, such that
K
is diffeomorphic to
M
and each continuous map from
K
into
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is homotopic to a (real) regular map from
K
into
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
Z
be the Zariski closure of
K
in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. The singular locus
S
of
Z
is an algebraic subset of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
defined by polynomial equations
P_{1}=0,\ldots,P_{k}=0
, where the polynomials
P_{i}
have real coefficients. Setting
P\coloneqq P_{1}^{2}+\cdots+P_{k}^{2}
, we get

S(\mathbb{R})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:P(x)=0\}.

By construction,
K=Z(\mathbb{R})
is disjoint from
S(\mathbb{R})
, and hence

X\coloneqq Z\setminus\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:P(x)=0\}

is an affine (complex) algebraic manifold defined over
\mathbb{R}
, with
X(\mathbb{R})=K
. Let
f\colon K\to\Sigma^{n}
be a continuous map. Our goal is to prove that
f
can be uniformly approximated on
K
by regular maps. Let
j\colon\mathbb{S}^{n}\hookrightarrow\Sigma^{n}
be the inclusion map and let
\rho\colon\Sigma^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a deformation retraction. The composite
\rho\circ f\colon K\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
is homotopic to a (real) regular map
h\colon K\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Setting
g=j\circ h
, we have
\rho\circ g=h
, and hence the maps
\rho\circ f,\rho\circ g\colon K\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
are homotopic. Consequently, the maps
f,g\colon K\to\Sigma^{n}
are also homotopic. Since
f
is uniformly approximable by holomorphic maps from
K
into
\Sigma^{n}
, we may assume that
f
itself is a holomorphic map. In view of Theorem
1.1
the proof is complete.

Regular maps are in general too rigid for approximation of holomorphic maps. Nash maps, which form an intermediate class between regular and holomorphic maps, are more flexible. Demailly, Lempert and Shiffman
[
10
]
and Lempert
[
26
]
proved that a holomorphic map from a Runge domain in an affine variety into a quasiprojective variety can be uniformly approximated on compact sets by Nash maps.

2
Sections of amenable submersions

In this section we work with vector bundles which are always either holomorphic or algebraic vector bundles. Let
Y
be a complex (holomorphic) manifold or an algebraic manifold. Given a vector bundle
p\colon E\to Y
over
Y
, with total space
E
and bundle projection
p
, we may refer to
E
as a vector bundle over
Y
. If
y\in Y
, we let
E_{y}\coloneqq p^{-1}(y)
denote the fiber of
E
over
y
and write
0_{y}
for the zero vector in
E_{y}
. The set
Z(E)=\{0_{y}:y\in Y\}
is called the zero section of
E
. In particular, if
E=Y\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the product vector bundle over
Y
, then
E_{y}=\{y\}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
0_{y}=(y,0)
, and
Z(E)=Y\times\{0\}
(here
0
is the zero vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
). We write
TY
for the tangent bundle to
Y
and
T_{y}Y
for the tangent space to
Y
at
y\in Y
.

Next we introduce some notations and definitions, and prove three technical lemmas.

Notation 2.1
.

Let
X,Z
be two algebraic manifolds and let
h\colon Z\to X
be a regular map which is a surjective submersion. Furthermore, let
V(h)
denote the algebraic vector subbundle of the tangent bundle
TZ
to
Z
defined by

V(h)_{z}=\operatorname{Ker}(d_{z}h\colon T_{z}Z\to T_{h(z)}X)\quad\text{for all }z\in Z,

where
d_{z}h
is the derivative of
h
at
z
. Clearly,
V(h)_{z}
is the tangent space to the fiber
h^{-1}(h(z))
.

For any subset
A
of
X
, a map
\alpha\colon A\to Z
is called a
section
of
h\colon Z\to X
if
h(\alpha(x))=x
for all
x\in A
. Given an open subset
U
of
X
, we call a continuous map
{F\colon U\times[0,1]\to Z}
a
homotopy of holomorphic sections
if for every
t\in[0,1]
the map
F_{t}\colon U\to Z
,
{x\mapsto F(x,t)}

We have the following result on approximation of holomorphic sections by regular sections.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let

be an amenable regular submersion from an algebraic manifold

onto an affine algebraic manifold

. Let

be a compact holomorphically convex subset of

be a holomorphic section of

. Then

can be approximated by regular sections defined over
K
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exist an open neighborhood
U\subseteq X
of
K
and a homotopy

of holomorphic sections of
h\colon Z\to X
such that

is a regular section and
F_{1}|_{K}=f
. Shrinking
U
if necessary, we may assume that
U
is Stein. Choose an open subset
U_{0}
of
X
such that its closure
\overline{U}_{0}
is compact and
K\subseteq U_{0}\subseteq\overline{U}_{0}\subseteq U
. Let
(E=Z\times\mathbb{C}^{m},E^{0},s)
and
\eta\colon U_{0}\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be as in Lemma
2.6
. In particular, by
(2.6.3)
, we get

s(F_{0}(x),\eta_{1}(x))=F_{1}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in U_{0},

where
\eta_{1}\colon U_{0}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
\eta_{1}(x)=\eta(x,1)
.

Since
X
is an affine algebraic manifold, we may assume that
X
is an algebraic subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Hence, by
[
19
, p. 245, Theorem 18]
, every holomorphic function on

is the restriction of a holomorphic function on

. It follows that the compact set

is polynomially convex in

, being holomorphically convex in

. Now, represent

as

, where

is an open neighborhood of

in

of

is a closed complex submanifold of

once again, the holomorphic map
{\eta_{1}|_{U_{1}}\colon U_{1}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}}
has a holomorphic extension

, the map

can be uniformly approximated on

by polynomial maps

. If

is a polynomial map with

sufficiently close to

, then
(F_{0}(x),\beta(x))\in E^{0}
for all

is a section of

\tilde{f}_{0}|_{L}
and
\tilde{f}_{1}|_{L}
, as required.

Since the compact set
K
is holomorphically convex in
X
, there exists an open Stein subset
U
of
X
with
K\subseteq U\subseteq L
. By Grauert’s theorem
[
16
]
, there is a continuous map
F\colon U\times[0,1]\to Y
such that for every
t\in[0,1]
the map
F_{t}\colon U\to Y
is holomorphic and
F_{0}=\tilde{f}_{0}|_{U}
,
F_{1}=\tilde{f}_{1}|_{U}
. Hence
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are homotopic through holomorphic maps.
∎

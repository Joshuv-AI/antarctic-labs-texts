1.
Introduction

1.1.
Existing Results I: Distances and Simplices in Subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Recall that the
upper Banach density
of a measurable set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

(1.1)

\delta^{*}(S)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|S\cap(t+Q(N))|}{|Q(N)|},

where
|\cdot|
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Q(N)
denotes the cube
[-N/2,N/2]^{n}
.

A result of Furstenberg, Katznelson, and Weiss
[
6
]
states that
if
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
has positive upper Banach density, then its distance set

\{|x-x^{\prime}|\,:\,x,x^{\prime}\in S\}

contains all sufficiently large numbers.
Note that the distance set of any set of positive Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
automatically contains all sufficiently small numbers (by the Lebesgue density theorem) and that it is easy to construct a set of positive upper density which does not contain a fixed distance by placing small balls centered on an appropriate square grid.

Theorem A

(Furstenberg, Katznelson, and Weiss
[
6
]
)
.

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\delta^{*}(S)>0
,
then there exists a
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S)
such that
S
is guaranteed to contain pairs of points
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
with
|x_{2}-x_{1}|=\lambda
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

This result was later reproved using Fourier analytic techniques by Bourgain in
[
1
]
where he established the following more general result for all configurations of
n
points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose affine span is
n-1
dimensional, namely for all non-degenerate simplices.

Theorem B

(Bourgain
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a non-degenerate simplex of
n
points.
If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\delta^{*}(S)>0
, then
there exists a threshold
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S,\Delta)
such that
S
contains an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

Recall that a finite point configuration
\Delta^{\prime}
is said to be an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta

if there exists a bijection
\phi:\Delta\to\Delta^{\prime}
such that
|\phi(v)-\phi(w)|=\lambda\,|v-w|
for all
v,w\in\Delta
, i.e. if
\Delta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\lambda\Delta
(the dilation of
\Delta
by a factor
\lambda
) via a rotation and translation.

Bourgain deduced Theorem B as an immediate consequence of the following stronger quantitative result for measurable subsets of the unit cube of positive measure. In the proposition below, and throughout this article, we shall refer to a decreasing sequence
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
as
lacunary
if
\lambda_{j+1}\leq\lambda_{j}/2
for all
1\leq j<J
.

Proposition B

(Bourgain
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a non-degenerate simplex of
n
points.
For any
0<\delta\leq 1
there exists a constant
J=O_{\Delta}(\delta^{-3n})
such that if
1\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J}
is any lacunary sequence and
S\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
with
|S|\geq\delta
, then there exists
1\leq j<J
such that
S
contains an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta
for all
\lambda\in[\lambda_{j+1},\lambda_{j}]
.

In
[
12
]
the authors provided a short direct proof of Theorem B without using Proposition B. It is based on the observation that uniformly distributed sets
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
contain the expected “number” of isometric copies of dilates
\lambda\Delta
and that all sets of positive upper density become uniformly distributed at sufficiently large scales.
However, for the purposes of this paper it will be important to recall Bourgain’s indirect approach.

To see that Proposition B implies Theorem B notice that if Theorem B were not to hold for some set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of upper Banach density
\delta^{*}(S)>\delta>0
, then there must exist a lacunary sequence
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J}\geq 1
, with
J
the constant in Proposition B, such that
S
does not contain an isometric copy of
\lambda_{j}\Delta
for any
1\leq j\leq J
. Taking a sufficiently large cube
Q
with side length
N\geq\lambda_{1}
and
\,|S\cap Q|\geq\delta|Q|\,
and scaling back
Q\to[0,1]^{n}
contradicts Proposition B.

We further note that by taking
\lambda_{j}=2^{-j}
in Proposition B we obtain the following “Falconer-type” result for subsets of
[0,1]^{n}
of positive Lebesgue measure.

Corollary B
.

If
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a non-degenerate simplex of
n
points,
then any
S\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
with
|S|>0
will necessarily contain an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta
for all
\lambda
in some interval of length at least
\exp(-C_{\Delta}|S|^{-3n})
.

Bourgain further demonstrated in
[
1
]
that no result along the lines of Theorem B can hold for configurations that contain any three points in arithmetic progression along a line, specifically showing that for any
n\geq 1
there are sets of positive upper Banach density in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which do not contain an isometric copy of configurations of the form
\{0,y,2y\}
with
|y|=\lambda
for all sufficiently large
\lambda
.
This should be contrasted with the following remarkable result of Tamar Ziegler.

Theorem C

(Ziegler
[
25
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be any configuration of
k
points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
.

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
has positive upper density, then there exists a threshold
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S,\mathcal{F})
such that
S_{\varepsilon}
contains an isometric copy of
\lambda\mathcal{F}
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, where
S_{\varepsilon}
denotes the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
S
.

Bourgain’s example was later generalized by Graham
[
9
]
to establish that the condition that
\varepsilon>0
in Theorem C is necessary and cannot be strengthened to
\varepsilon=0
for any given
non-spherical
configuration
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for any
n\geq 1
, that is for any finite configuration of points that cannot be inscribed in some sphere. We note that the sets constructed by Bourgain and Graham have the property that for any
\varepsilon>0
their
\varepsilon
-neighborhoods will contain arbitrarily large cubes and hence trivially satisfy Theorem C with
\lambda_{0}=0
.

It is natural to ask if
any
spherical configuration
\mathcal{F}
, beyond the known example of simplices, has the property that every positive upper Banach density subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for some sufficiently large
n
, contains an isometric copy of
\lambda\mathcal{F}
for all sufficiently large
\lambda
, and even to conjecture that this ought to hold for
all
spherical configurations.
The first breakthrough in this direction came in
[
12
]
when the authors established this for configurations of four points forming a
2
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and more generally for any configuration that is the direct product of two non-degenerate simplices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for suitably large
n
.

The purpose of this article is to present a strengthening of the results in
[
12
]
and to extend them to cover configurations with a higher dimensional product structure in both the Euclidean and discrete settings.

1.2.
New Results I: Rectangles and Products of Simplices in Subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

The first main result of this article is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be
2^{d}
points forming the vertices of a fixed
d
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

(i)

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
has positive upper Banach density, then
there exists a threshold
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S,\mathcal{R})
such that
S
contains an isometric copy of
\lambda\mathcal{R}
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

(ii)

For any
0<\delta\leq 1
there exists a constant
c=c(\delta,\mathcal{R})>0
such that any
S\subseteq[0,1]^{2d}
with
|S|\geq\delta
is guaranteed to contain an isometric copy of
\lambda\mathcal{R}
for all
\lambda
in some interval of length at least
c
.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{R}
has sidelengths given by
t_{1},\dots,t_{d}
, then the isometric copies of
\lambda\mathcal{R}
in both
(i)
and
(ii)
above can all be realized in the special form
\{x_{11},x_{12}\}\times\cdots\times\{x_{d1},x_{d2}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with each
|x_{j2}-x_{j1}|=\lambda t_{j}
.

The multi-dimensional extension of Szemerédi’s theorem on arithmetic progressions in sets of positive density due to Furstenberg and Katznelson
[
5
]
implies, and is equivalent to the fact, that there are isometric copies of
\lambda\mathcal{R}
in
S
for
arbitrarily
large
\lambda
, with sides parallel to the coordinate axis. Theorem
1.1
states that there is an isometric copy of
\lambda\mathcal{R}
in
S
for
every
sufficiently large
\lambda
, but only with sides parallel to given 2-dimensional coordinate subspaces which provides an extra degree of freedom for each side vector of the rectangle
\mathcal{R}
.

A weaker version of Theorem
1.1
, with
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
replaced with
\mathbb{R}^{5d}
, was later established by Durcik and Kovač in
[
4
]
using an adaptation of arguments of the second author with Cook and Pramanik in
[
3
]
. This approach also makes direct use of the full strength of the multi-dimensional Szemerédi theorem and as such leads to quantitatively weaker results.

Our arguments work for more general patterns where
d
-dimensional rectangles are replaced with direct products of non-degenerate simplices.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{d}}
and each
\Delta_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}
is a non-degenerate simplex of
n_{j}
points.

(i)

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
has positive upper Banach density, then
there exists a threshold
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S,\Delta)
such that
S
contains an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

(ii)

For any
0<\delta\leq 1
there exists a constant
c=c(\delta,\Delta)>0
such that any
S\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
with
|S|\geq\delta
is guaranteed to contain an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta
for all
\lambda
in some interval of length at least
c
.

Moreover the isometric copies of
\lambda\Delta
in both
(i)
and
(ii)
above can all be realized in the special form
\Delta_{1}^{\prime}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{d}^{\prime}
with each
\Delta_{j}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}
an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta_{j}
.

Quantitative Remark.
A careful analysis of our proof reveals that the constant
c(\delta,\Delta)
can be taken greater than
W_{d}(C^{\prime}_{\Delta}\delta^{-3n_{1}\cdots n_{d}})^{-1}
where

W_{k}(m)
is a tower of exponentials defined by
W_{1}(m)=\exp(m)
and
W_{k+1}(m)=\exp(W_{k}(m))
for
k\geq 1
.

1.3.
Existing Results II: Distances and Simplices in Subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

The problem of counting isometric copies of a given non-degenerate simplex in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(with one vertex fixed) has been extensively studied via its equivalent formulation as the number of ways a quadratic form can be represented as a sum of squares of linear forms, see
[
11
]
and
[
19
]
. This was exploited by the second author in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
to establish
analogous results to those described in Section
1.1
above for subsets of the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of positive upper density.

Recall that the
upper Banach density
of a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is analogously defined by

(1.2)

\delta^{*}(S)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|S\cap(t+Q(N))|}{|Q(N)|},

where
|\cdot|
now denotes counting measure on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
Q(N)
the discrete cube
[-N/2,N/2]^{n}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

In light of the fact that any pairs of distinct points

\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has the property that the square of the distance between them
|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{2}
is always a positive integer we introduce the convenient notation

\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}:=\{\lambda\,:\,\lambda>0\text{ and }\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Theorem A
′

(Magyar
[
16
]
)
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
.

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{5}
has upper Banach density at least
\delta
, then
there exists an integer
q_{0}=q_{0}(\delta)
and
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S)
such that

S
contains pairs of points

\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
with
|x_{2}-x_{1}|=q_{0}\lambda
for all
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

Theorem B
′

(Magyar
[
17
]
)
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
and
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n+3}
be a non-degenerate simplex of
n
points.

(i)

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n+3}
has upper Banach density at least
\delta
,
then
there exists an integer
q_{0}=O(\exp(C_{\Delta}\delta^{-13n}))
and
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S,\Delta)
such that
S
contains an isometric copy of
q_{0}\lambda\Delta
for all
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

(ii)

If
N\geq\exp(2C_{\Delta}\delta^{-13n})
, then any
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}^{2n+3}
with cardinality
|S|\geq\delta N^{2n+3}
will necessarily contain an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta
for some
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
1\leq\lambda\leq N
.

Note that the fact that
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
could fall entirely into a fixed congruence class of some integer
1\leq q\leq\delta^{-1/n}
ensures that the
q_{0}
that appears in Theorems A
′
and B
′
above must be divisible by the least common multiple of all integers
1\leq q\leq\delta^{-1/n}
. Indeed if
S=(q\mathbb{Z})^{n}
with
1\leq q\leq\delta^{-1/n}
then
S
has upper Banach density at least
\delta
, however the distance between any two points
x,y\in S
is of the form
|x-y|=q\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
.

However, in both Theorems A
′
and Part (i) of Theorem B
′
, one can take
q_{0}=1
if the sets
S
are assumed to be suitably uniformly distributed on congruence classes of small modulus. This leads via an easy density increment strategy to short new proofs, see
[
14
]
for Theorem A
′
and Section
8
for Part (i) of Theorem B
′
.

The original argument in
[
17
]
deduced Theorem B
′
from the following discrete analogue of Proposition B.

Proposition B
′

(Magyar
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n+3}
be a non-degenerate simplex of
n
points.

For any
0<\delta\leq 1
there exist constants
J=O_{\Delta}(\delta^{-3n})
and
q_{0}=O(\exp(C_{\Delta}\delta^{-13n}))
such that if
N\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J}\geq 1
is any lacunary sequence in
q_{0}\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
and
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}^{2n+3}
with cardinality
|S|\geq\delta N^{2n+3}
, then
S
will necessarily contain an isometric copy of
\lambda_{j}\Delta
for some
1\leq j\leq J
.

To see that Proposition B
′
implies Theorem B
′
notice that if Part (i) of Theorem B
′
were not to hold for some set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n+3}
of upper Banach density
\delta^{*}(S)>\delta>0
with
q_{0}
from Proposition B
′
, then there must exist a lacunary sequence
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J}\geq 1
in
q_{0}\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
, with
J
the constant from Proposition B
′
, such that
S
does not contain an isometric copy of
\lambda_{j}\Delta
for any
1\leq j\leq J
. Since we can find a sufficiently large cube
Q
with integer side length
N
that is divisible by
q_{0}
and greater than
\lambda_{1}
such that
\,|S\cap Q|\geq\delta|Q|\,
, this contradicts Proposition B
′
.
Part (ii) of Theorem B
′
follows from Proposition B
′
by taking
\lambda_{j}=2^{J-j}q_{0}
.

1.5.
Notations and Outline.

We will consider the parameters
d,n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}
fixed and will not indicate the dependence on them. Thus we will write
f=O(g)
if
|f|\leq C(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})g
. If the implicit constants in our estimates depend on additional parameters
\varepsilon,\delta,K,\ldots
the we will write
f=O_{\varepsilon,\delta,K,\dots}(g)
. We will use the notation
f\ll g
to indicate that
|f|\leq c\,g
for some constant
c>0
sufficiently small for our purposes.

Given an
\varepsilon>0
and a (finite or infinite) sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots>0
, we will say that the sequence is
\varepsilon
-
admissible
if
L_{j}/L_{j+1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
L_{j+1}\ll\varepsilon^{2}L_{j}
for all
j\geq 1
. Moreover, if
q\in\mathbb{N}
is given and
L_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
for all
1\leq j\leq J
, then we will call the sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}

(\varepsilon,q)
-
admissible
if in addition
L_{J}/q\in\mathbb{N}
. Such sequences of scales will often appear in our statements both in the continuous and the discrete case.

Our proofs are based on a weak hypergraph regularity lemma and an associated counting lemma developed in the context of Euclidean spaces and the integer lattice.
In Section
2
we introduce our approach in the model case of finite fields and prove an analogue of Theorem

in this setting. In Section

for a single simplex and ultimately establish the base case of our general inductive approach to Theorem
1.2
. In Section
4
we address Theorem
1.2
for the direct product of two simplices, this provides a new proof (and strengthening) of the main result of
[
12
]
and serves as a gentle preparation for the more complicated general case which we present in the Section
5
. The proof of Theorem

is outlined in

, while a short direct proof of Part (i) of Theorem B

2.
Model case: vector spaces over finite fields.

In this section we will illustrate our general method by giving a complete proof of Theorem
1.1
in the model setting of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes the finite field of
q
elements. We do this as the
notation and arguments are more transparent in this setting yet many of the main ideas are still present.

We say that two vectors
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
are orthogonal, if
x\cdot y=0
, where “
\cdot
” stands for the
usual dot product. A rectangle in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is then a set
\mathcal{R}=\{x_{1},y_{1}\}\times\cdots\times\{x_{n},y_{n}\}
with side vectors
y_{i}-x_{i}

being pairwise orthogonal.

The finite field analogue of Theorem
1.1
is the following

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
0<\delta\leq 1
there exists an integer
q_{0}=q_{0}(\delta)
with the following property:

If
q\geq q_{0}
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, then any
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}
with
|S|\geq\delta\,q^{2d}
will contain points

\{x_{11},x_{12}\}\times\cdots\times\{x_{d1},x_{d2}\}\subseteq V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{d}\quad\text{with \ $|x_{j2}-x_{j1}|^{2}=t_{j}$ for $1\leq j\leq d$}

where
we have written
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}=V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{d}
with
V_{j}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
pairwise orthogonal coordinate subspaces.

2.1.
Overview of the proof of Proposition
2.1

Write
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}=V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}
with
V_{j}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
pairwise orthogonal coordinate subspaces.
For any
\underline{t}:=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}
we define

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{S}):=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}_{1}\in V_{1}^{2},\dots,\underline{x}_{d}\in V_{d}^{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\prod\limits_{(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{d})\in\{1,2\}^{d}}1_{S}(x_{1\ell_{1}},\dots,x_{d\ell_{d}})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j1})

where we used the shorthand notation
\underline{x}_{j}:=(x_{j1},x_{j2})
for each
1\leq j\leq d
and the averaging notation:

\mathbb{E}_{x\in A}f(x):=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{x\in A}f(x)

for a finite set
A\neq\emptyset
. We have also used the notation

\sigma_{t}(x)=\begin{cases}q\quad\text{if $|x|^{2}=t$}\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for each
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Note that the function
\sigma_{t}
may be viewed as the discrete analogue of the normalized surface area measure on the sphere of radius
\sqrt{t}
. It is
well-known, see
[
10
]
, that

\mathbb{E}_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\ \sigma_{t}(x)=1+O(q^{-1/2})

and for all
\xi\neq 0
one has

\hat{\sigma}_{t}(\xi):=\mathbb{E}_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\ \sigma_{t}(x)\,e^{2\pi i\frac{x\cdot\xi}{q}}=O(q^{-1/2}).

Note that if
\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{S})>0
, then this implies that
S
contains a rectangle of the form
\{x_{11},x_{12}\}\times\cdots\times\{x_{d1},x_{d2}\}
with
x_{j1},x_{j2}\in V_{j}
and
|x_{j2}-x_{j1}|^{2}=t_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq d
.

Our approach to Proposition
2.1
in fact establishes the following quantitatively stronger result.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
there exists an integer
q_{0}=q_{0}(\varepsilon)
with the following property:

If
q\geq q_{0}
, then for any
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
one has

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{S})>\left(\frac{|S|}{q^{2d}}\right)^{2^{d}}-\varepsilon

where we have written
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}=V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}
with
V_{j}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
pairwise orthogonal coordinate subspaces.

A crucial observation in the proof of Proposition
2.2
is that the averages
\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{S})
can be compared to ones which can be easily estimated from below.
We define, for any
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}
, the (unrestricted) count

\mathcal{M}(1_{S}):=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}_{1}\in V_{1}^{2},\dots,\underline{x}_{d}\in V_{d}^{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\prod\limits_{(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{d})\in\{1,2\}^{d}}1_{S}(x_{1\ell_{1}},\dots,x_{d\ell_{d}}).

It is easy to see, by carefully applying Cauchy-Schwarz
d
times to
\mathbb{E}_{x_{11}\in V_{1},\dots,x_{d1}\in V_{d}}1_{S}(x_{11},\dots,x_{d1})
, that

(2.1)

\mathcal{M}(1_{S})\geq\left(\frac{|S|}{q^{2d}}\right)^{2^{d}}.

Our approach to Proposition
2.2
therefore reduces to establishing that for any
\varepsilon>0
one has

(2.2)

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{S})\,=\,\mathcal{M}(1_{S})+O(\varepsilon)+O_{\varepsilon}(q^{-1/2}).

The validity of (
2.2
) will follow immediately from the
d=k
case of Proposition
2.3
below.
However, before we can state this
counting lemma
we need to introduce some further notation from the theory of hypergraphs, notation that we shall ultimately make use of throughout the paper.

2.2.
Hypergraph Notation and a Counting Lemma

In order to streamline our notation we will make use the language of hypergraphs.
For
J:=\{1,\ldots,d\}
and
1\leq k\leq d
, we let

\mathcal{H}_{d,k}=\{e\subseteq J;\ |e|=k\}
denote the full
k
-regular hypergraph on the vertex set
J
. For
K:=\{jl;\ j\in J,\,l\in\{1,2\}\}

we define the projection
\pi:K\to J
as
\pi(jl):=j
and use this in turn to define the hypergraph bundle

\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}:=\{e\subseteq K;\ |e|=|\pi(e)|=k\}

using the shorthand notation
\underline{2}=(2,2,\ldots,2)
to indicate that

|\pi^{-1}(j)|=2
for all
j\in J
.

Notice when
k=d
then
\mathcal{H}_{d,d}
consists of one element, the set
e=\{1,\ldots,d\}
, and

\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{2}}=\{\,\{1l_{1},\ldots,dl_{d}\};\ (l_{1},\ldots,l_{d})\in\{1,2\}^{d}\}.

Let
V:=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2d}
and
V=V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{d}
with
V_{j}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
pairwise orthogonal coordinate subspaces. For a given
\underline{x}=(x_{11},x_{12},\ldots,x_{d1},x_{d2})\in V^{2}
with
x_{j1},\,x_{j2}\in V_{j}
and a given edge

e=\{1l_{1},\ldots,dl_{d}\}
, we write

\underline{x}_{e}:=(x_{1l_{1}},\ldots,x_{dl_{d}}).

Note that the map
\underline{x}\to\underline{x}_{e}
defines a
projection
\pi_{e}:V^{2}\to V
.
With this notation, we can clearly now write

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{S})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V^{2}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{2}}}1_{S}(\underline{x}_{e})\ \prod_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j1})

\mathcal{M}(1_{S})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V^{2}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{2}}}1_{S}(\underline{x}_{e}).

Now for any
1\leq k\leq d
and any edge
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}
, i.e.
e^{\prime}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
|e^{\prime}|=k
, we let
V_{e^{\prime}}:=\prod_{j\in e^{\prime}}V_{j}
. For every
\underline{x}\in V^{2}
and
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
, we define
\underline{x}_{e}:=\pi_{e}(\underline{x})
where
\pi_{e}:V^{2}\to V_{\pi(e)}
is the natural projection map.

Our key
counting lemma
, Proposition
2.3
below, which we will establish by induction on
1\leq k\leq d
below, is then the statement that given a family of functions
f_{e}:V_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1],\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
, the averages (generalizing those discussed above) which are defined by

(2.3)

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}):=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V^{2}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e})\ \prod_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j1})

(2.4)

\mathcal{M}(f_{e};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}):=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V^{2}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e}).

are approximately equal. Specifically, one has

Proposition 2.3

(Counting Lemma)
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
. For any collection of functions

f_{e}:V_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}

one has

(2.5)

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})\,=\,\mathcal{M}(f_{e};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})+O(\varepsilon)+O_{\varepsilon}(q^{-1/2}).

If we apply this Proposition with
d=k
and
f_{e}=1_{S}
for all
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{2}}
, then Theorem
2.1
clearly follows given the lower bound (
2.1
).

2.3.
Proof of Proposition
2.3

We will establish Proposition
2.3
by inducting on
1\leq k\leq d
.

For
k=1
the result follows from the
basic observation that
if
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to[-1,1]
and let
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\ f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})\,\sigma_{t}(x_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\hat{f}_{1}(\xi)\hat{f}_{2}(\xi)\hat{\sigma}_{t}(\xi)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\hat{f}_{1}(0)\hat{f}_{2}(0)+O(q^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\ f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})+O(q^{-1/2})

by the properties of the function
\hat{\sigma}
given above.

To see how this implies Proposition
2.3
for
k=1
we note that since
\mathcal{H}^{\underline{2}}_{d,1}=\{jl:\ 1\leq j\leq d,\,1\leq l\leq 2\}
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,1}^{\underline{2}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{x_{j1},x_{j2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\ f_{j1}(x_{j1})f_{j2}(x_{j2})\,\sigma_{t}(x_{j2}-x_{j1})

\displaystyle=\,\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{x_{j1},x_{j2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}\ f_{j1}(x_{j1})f_{j2}(x_{j2})+O(q^{-1/2})=\mathcal{M}(f_{e};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,1}^{\underline{2}})+O(q^{-1/2}).

The induction step has two main ingredients,
the first is an estimate of the type which is often referred to as a generalized von-Neumann
inequality, namely

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
. For any collection of functions
f_{e}:V_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}

one has

(2.7)

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})\,\leq\,\min_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}\|f_{e}\|_{\Box(V_{\pi(e)})}+O(q^{-1/2})

where for any
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
and
f:V_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
we define

(2.8)

\|f\|^{2^{k}}_{\Box(V_{\pi(e)})}:=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V_{\pi(e)}^{2}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}f(\underline{x}_{e}).

The corresponding inequality for the multilinear expression
\mathcal{M}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})
, namely the fact that

\mathcal{M}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})\leq\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}\|f_{e}\|_{\Box(V_{\pi(e)})}\leq\min_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}\|f_{e}\|_{\Box(V_{\pi(e)})}

is well-known and is referred to as the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
[
8
]
.

The second and main ingredient is an approximate decomposition of a graph to simpler ones, and is essentially the so-called weak (hypergraph) regularity lemma of Frieze and Kannan
[
7
]
. We choose to state this from a somewhat more abstract/probabilistic point of view, a perspective that will be particularly helpful when we consider our general results in the continuous and discrete settings.

We will first introduce this in the case
d=2
. A bipartite graph with (finite) vertex sets
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
is a set
S\subseteq V_{1}\times V_{2}
and a function
f:V_{1}\times V_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
may be viewed as weighted bipartite graph with weights
f(x_{1},x_{2})
on the edges
(x_{1},x_{2})
. If
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are partitions of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
respectively then
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}
is a partition
V_{1}\times V_{2}
and we let
\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{P})
denote the function that is constant and equal to
\mathbb{E}_{x\in A}f(x)
on each atom
A=A_{1}\times A_{2}
of
\mathcal{P}
.
The weak regularity lemma states that for any
\varepsilon>0
and for any weighted graph
f:V_{1}\times V_{2}\to[-1,1]
there exist partitions
\mathcal{P}_{i}
of
V_{i}
with
|\mathcal{P}_{i}|\leq 2^{O(\varepsilon^{-2})}
for
i=1,2
, so that

(2.9)

\quad|\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}\in V_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{x_{2}\in V_{2}}(f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{P}))(x_{1},x_{2})\,\,\mathbf{1}_{U_{1}}(x_{1})\mathbf{1}_{U_{2}}(x_{2})|\leq\varepsilon

for all
U_{1}\subseteq V_{1}
and
U_{2}\subseteq V_{2}
. Informally this means that the graph
f
can be approximated with precision
\varepsilon
with the “low complexity” graph
\mathbb{E}(f,\mathcal{P})
.
If we consider the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{i}
generated by the partitions
\mathcal{P}_{i}
and the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{1}\vee\mathcal{B}_{2}
generated by
\mathcal{P}_{1}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}
then we have
\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})
, the so-called conditional expectation function of
f
. Moreover it is easy to see, using Cauchy-Schwarz, that estimate (
2.9
) follows from

(2.10)

\|f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B}_{1}\vee\mathcal{B}_{2})\|_{\Box(V_{1}\times V_{2})}\leq\varepsilon.

With this more probabilistic point of view the weak regularity lemma says that the function
f
can be approximated with precision
\varepsilon
by a low complexity function
\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B}_{1}\bigvee\mathcal{B}_{2})
, corresponding to
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{i}
on
V_{i}
generated by
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
sets. This formulation is also referred to as a Koopman- von Neumann type decomposition, see Corollary 6.3 in
[
23
]
.

We will need a natural extension to
k
-regular hypergraphs. See
[
22
,
8
]
, and also
[
2
]
for extension to sparse hypergraphs. Given an edge
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}
of
k
elements we define its boundary
\partial e^{\prime}:=\{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k-1};\ \mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\subseteq e^{\prime}\}
. For each
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=e^{\prime}\backslash\{j\}\in\partial e^{\prime}
let

\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\prime}
be a
\sigma
-algebra on
V_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}:=\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}V_{j}
and
\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}:=\{U\times V_{j};\ U\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}\}
denote its pull-back over the space
V_{e^{\prime}}
.
The
\sigma
-algebra
\,\mathcal{B}=\bigvee_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial e^{\prime}}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}\,
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra on
\partial e^{\prime}
containing
\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
for all

\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial e^{\prime}
. Note that the atoms of
\mathcal{B}
are of the form
A=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial e^{\prime}}A_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
where
A_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
is an atom
of
\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
. We say that the complexity of a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
is at most
m
, and write
\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}})\leq m
, if it is generated by
m
sets.

Lemma 2.2

(Weak hypergraph regularity lemma)
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
and
f_{e}:V_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
be a given function for each
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0

there exists
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
on
V_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
for each
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k-1}
such that

(2.11)

\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}})=O(\varepsilon^{-2^{k+1}})

and

(2.12)

\|f_{e}-\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\bigvee_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial\pi(e)}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}})\|_{\Box(V_{\pi(e)})}\,\leq\,\varepsilon\quad\text{for all \,$e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}$}.

The proof of Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
are presented in Section

We close this subsection by demonstrating how these lemmas can be combined to establish Proposition

2.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,

\sigma

By Lemma

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})=\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(\bar{f}_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})+O(\varepsilon)+O(q^{-1/2})

The conditional expectation functions
\bar{f}_{e}

the
\sigma

1_{A_{e}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial\pi(e)}1_{A_{e\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{A_{e}};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V^{2}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}\prod_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial\pi(e)}1_{A^{\prime}_{e\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}}(\underline{x}_{p_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}(e)})\ \prod_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j1})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V^{2}}\prod_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{H}^{\underline{2}}_{d,k-1}}\underbrace{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}},\,\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial\pi(e)\\

\mathcal{N}_{\underline{t}}(1_{A_{e}};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})=\mathcal{M}(1_{A_{e}};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})+O(\varepsilon_{1})+O_{\varepsilon_{1}}(q^{-1/2})

for any

Proof of Lemma

f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to[-1,1]
and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

Now, fix an edge, say
e_{0}=(11,21,\ldots,k1)
. Partition the edges
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
into three groups; the first group
consisting of edges
e
for which
1\notin\pi(e)
, the second where
11\in e
and write
e=(11,e^{\prime})
with
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d-1,k-1}^{\underline{2}}
and the third when
12\in e
, using the notation
\mathcal{H}_{d-1,k-1}^{\underline{2}}:=\{(j_{2}l_{2},\ldots,j_{k}l_{k})\}
.
Accordingly we can write

(2.16)

\mathcal{N}_{t}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in V^{2}}\prod_{1\notin\pi(e)}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e})\!\!\!\!\!\!\prod_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d-1,k-1}^{\underline{2}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!f_{(11,e^{\prime})}(x_{11},\underline{x}_{e^{\prime}})\!\!\!\!\!\!\prod_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d-1,k-1}^{\underline{2}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!f_{(12,e^{\prime})}(x_{12},\underline{x}_{e^{\prime}})\,\prod_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j1}).

If for given
x\in V_{1}
and
\underline{x}^{\prime}=(x_{21},x_{22},\ldots,x_{d1},x_{d2})\in V_{2}^{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}^{2}
we define

g_{1}(x,\underline{x}^{\prime}):=\prod_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d-1,k-1}^{\underline{2}}}f_{(11,e^{\prime})}(x,\underline{x}_{e^{\prime}})\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad g_{2}(x,\underline{x}^{\prime}):=\prod_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d-1,k-1}^{\underline{2}}}f_{(12,e^{\prime})}(x,\underline{x}_{e^{\prime}})

then we can write

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{t}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{21},x_{22},\ldots,x_{d1},x_{d2}}\prod_{1\notin\pi(e)}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e})\prod_{j=2}^{d}\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},x_{12}}\ g_{1}(x_{11},\underline{x}^{\prime})g_{2}(x_{12},\underline{x}^{\prime})\,\sigma_{t_{1}}(x_{12}-x_{11}).

By (
2.15
) we can estimate the inner sum in (
2.17
) by the square root of

\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},x_{12}}\ g_{1}(x_{11},\underline{x}^{\prime})g_{1}(x_{12},\underline{x}^{\prime})+O(q^{-1/2}).

Thus by Cauchy-Schwarz, and the fact that
f_{e}:V_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
for all
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
, we can conclude that

(2.18)

\mathcal{N}_{t}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},x_{12},\dots,x_{d1},x_{d2}}\prod_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d-1,k-1}^{\underline{2}}}\!\!\!\!f_{(11,e^{\prime})}(x_{11},\underline{x}_{e^{\prime}})f_{(11,e^{\prime})}(x_{12},\underline{x}_{e^{\prime}})\,\prod_{j=2}^{d}\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j2}).

The expression on the right hand side of the inequality above is similar to that in (
2.16
) except for the following changes. The functions
f_{e}
for

1\notin e
are eliminated i.e. replaced by 1, as well as the factor
\sigma_{t_{1}}
. The functions
f_{(12,e^{\prime})}
, are replaced by

f_{(11,e^{\prime})}
for all
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\underline{2}}_{d-1,k-1}
. Repeating the same procedure for
j=2,\ldots,k
one eliminates all the factors

\sigma_{t_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq k
, moreover all the functions
f_{e}
for edges
e
such that
j\notin\pi(e)
for some
1\leq j\leq k
, which leaves only the edges
e
so that
\pi(e)=(1,2,\ldots,k)
, moreover for such edges the functions
f_{e}
are eventually replaced by

f_{e_{0}}=f_{11,21,\ldots,k1}
. The factors
\sigma_{t_{j}}(x_{j2}-x_{j1})
are not changed for
j>k
however as the function
f_{e_{0}}
does not depend on the variables
x_{jl}
for
j>k
, averaging over these variables gives rise to a factor of
1+O(q^{-1/2})
. Thus one obtains the following final estimate

(2.19)

\mathcal{N}_{t}(f_{e};\ e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}})^{2^{k}}\leq\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},x_{12},\ldots,x_{k1},x_{k2}}\prod_{\pi(e)=(1,\ldots,k)}f_{e_{0}}(\underline{x}_{e})+O(q^{-1/2})=\|f_{e_{0}}\|_{\Box(V_{\pi(e_{0})})}^{2^{k}}+O(q^{-1/2}).

This proves the lemma, as it is clear that the above procedure can be applied to any edge in place of
e_{0}=(11,21,\ldots,k1)
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

For a function
f_{e}:V_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
and a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}
on

V_{\pi(e)}
define the
energy
of
f_{e}
with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}
as

\mathcal{E}(f_{e},\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}):=\|\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)})\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{x\in V_{\pi(e)}}\,|\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)})(x)|^{2},

and for a family of functions
f_{e}
and
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}
,
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
its total energy as

\mathcal{E}(f_{e},\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}):=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}}\mathcal{E}(f_{e},\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}).

We will show that if (
2.12
) does not hold for a family of
\sigma
-algebras
\,\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}=\bigvee_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial{\pi(e)}}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}\,
, then the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
can be refined so that the total energy of the system increases by a
quantity depending only on
\varepsilon
. Since the functions
f_{e}
are bounded the total energy of the system is
O(1)
, the
energy increment process must stop in
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
steps, and (
2.12
) must hold. The idea of this procedure appears
already in the proof of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma
[
20
]
, and have been used since in various places

[
7
,
22
,
8
]
.

Initially set
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}:=\{\emptyset,V_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}\}
and hence
\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}=\{\emptyset,V_{\pi(e)}\}
to be the trivial

\sigma
-algebras. Assume that in general (
2.12
) does not hold for a family of
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
, with
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k-1}
.
Then there exists an edge
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{2}}
so that
\|g_{e}\|_{\Box(V_{\pi(e)})}\geq\varepsilon
, with
g_{e}:=f_{e}-\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)})
. Let

e=(11,\ldots,k1)
for simplicity of notation, hence
\pi(e)=(1,\ldots,k)
. Then, with notation
\underline{x}^{\prime}=(x_{12},\ldots,x_{k2})
, one
has

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2^{k}}\leq\|g_{e}\|_{\Box(V_{\pi(e)})}^{2^{k}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},x_{12},\ldots,x_{k1},x_{k2}}\prod_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}=1,2}g_{e}(x_{1l_{1}},\ldots,x_{kl_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x_{12},\ldots,x_{k2}}\Bigl|\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},\ldots,x_{k1}}g_{e}(x_{11},\ldots,x_{k1})\,\prod_{j=1}^{k}h_{j,\underline{x}^{\prime}}(x_{11},\ldots,x_{j-1\,1},x_{j+1\,1},\ldots,x_{k1})\Bigr|

for some functions
h_{j,\underline{x}^{\prime}}
that are bounded by 1 in magnitude.
Indeed if and edge
e\neq(11,\ldots,k1)
then
x_{e}
does not depend at least one of the variables
x_{j1}
. Thus there must be
an
\underline{x}^{\prime}
for which the inner sum in the above expression is at least
\varepsilon^{2^{k}}
. Fix such an
\underline{x}^{\prime}
. Decomposing the functions
h_{j,\underline{x}^{\prime}}
into their positive and negative parts and then writing them as an
average of indicator functions, one obtains that there sets
B_{j}\subseteq V_{\pi(e)\backslash\{j\}}
such that

\Bigl|\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},\ldots,x_{k1}}g_{e}(x_{11},\ldots,x_{k1})\,\prod_{j=1}^{k}1_{B_{j}}(x_{11},\ldots,x_{j-1\,1},x_{j+1\,1},\ldots,x_{k1})\Bigr|\,\geq\,2^{-k}\,\varepsilon^{2^{k}}

which can be written more succinctly, using the inner product notation, as

(2.20)

\Bigl|\langle f_{e}-\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}),\prod_{j=1}^{k}1_{B_{j}}\rangle\Bigr|\,\geq\,2^{-k}\,\varepsilon^{2^{k}}.

For
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\partial\pi(e)\backslash\{j\}
let
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
and the set
B_{j}
and let

\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}^{\prime}:=\bigvee_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial{\pi(e)}}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}^{\prime}\,
. Since the functions
1_{B_{j}}
are measurable with respect to the

\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}^{\prime}
for all
1\leq j\leq k
, we have that

(2.21)

\langle f_{e}-\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}^{\prime}),\prod_{j=1}^{k}1_{B_{j}}\rangle=0

and hence, by Cauchy-Schwarz, that

(2.22)

\|\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)})\|_{2}^{2}=\|\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}-\|\mathbb{E}(f_{e}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\,2^{-2k}\,\varepsilon^{2^{k+1}}.

Note that the first equality above follows from the fact that conditional expectation function
\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})
is the orthogonal projection
of
f
to the subspace of
\mathcal{B}
-measurable functions in
L^{2}
. This also implies that energy of a function is always increasing
when the underlying
\sigma
-algebra is refined, and (
2.22
) tells us that the energy of
f_{e}
is increased by at least

c_{k}\,\varepsilon^{2^{k+1}}
.

For
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\notin\partial\pi(e)
we set
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
. Then the total energy of the family
f_{e}
with
respect to the system
\,\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e)}^{\prime}=\bigvee_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial\pi(e)}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
,
e\in\mathcal{H}^{\underline{2}}_{d,k}
is also increased by at least

c_{k}\,\varepsilon^{2^{k+1}}
.

It is clear that the complexity of the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}
are increased by at most 1, hence, as
explained above, the lemma follows by applying this energy increment process at most
O(\varepsilon^{-2^{k+1}})
times.
∎

3.
The base case of an inductive strategy to establish Theorem
1.2

In this section we will ultimately establish the base case of our more general inductive argument.
We however start by giving a quick review of the proof of Theorem
1.2
when
d=1
(which contains both Theorem B and Corollary B as stated in Section
1.1
), namely the case of a single simplex. This was originally addressed in
[
1
]
and revisited in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
.

3.1.
A Single Simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a fixed cube and let
l(Q)
denotes its side length.

Let
\Delta^{0}=\{v_{1}=0,v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a fixed non-degenerate simplex and define
t_{kl}:=v_{k}\cdot v_{l}
for
2\leq k,l\leq n
where
``\cdot"
is the dot product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Given
\lambda>0
, a simplex
\Delta=\{x_{1}=0,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
isometric to
\lambda\Delta^{0}
if and only if
x_{k}\cdot x_{l}=\lambda^{2}t_{kl}
for all
2\leq k,l\leq n
. Thus the configuration space

S_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}
of isometric copies of
\lambda\Delta^{0}
is a non-singular real variety given by the above equations. Let
\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}

be natural normalized surface area measure on
S_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}
, described in
[
1
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
13
]
.
It is clear that the variable

x_{1}
can be replaced by any of the variables
x_{i}
by redefining the constants
t_{kl}
.

For any family of functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}:Q\to[-1,1]
and
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
we define the multi-linear expression

(3.1)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):=\fint_{x_{1}\in Q}\int_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}}f_{1}(x_{1})\ldots f_{n}(x_{n})\,d\sigma_{\lambda\Delta_{0}}(x_{2}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}-x_{1})\,dx_{1}.

We note that all of our functions are 1-bounded and both integrals, in fact all integrals in this paper, are normalized.
Recall that we are using the normalized integral notation

\fint_{A}f:=\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}f.

Since the normalized measure

\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}
is supported on
S_{\lambda\Delta_{0}}
we will not indicate the support of the variables
(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
explicitly.

Note also that if
S\subseteq Q
is a measurable set and
\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})>0
then
S
must contain an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta^{0}
.
The following proposition (with
Q=[0,1]^{n}
) is a quantitatively stronger version of Proposition B that appeared in Section
1.1
and hence immediately establishes Theorem
1.2
for
d=1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
there exists an integer
J=O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log\varepsilon^{-1})
with the following property:

Given any lacunary sequence
l(Q)\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J}
and
S\subseteq Q
, there is some
1\leq j<J
such that

(3.2)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})>\left(\frac{|S|}{|Q|}\right)^{n}-\varepsilon

for all
\lambda\in[\lambda_{j+1},\lambda_{j}]
.

Our approach to establishing Proposition
3.1
is to compare the above expressions to simpler ones for which it is easy to
obtain lower bounds.
Given a scale
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
we define the multi-linear expression

(3.3)

\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):=\fint_{t\in Q}\fint_{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\in t+Q(\lambda)}f_{1}(x_{1})\ldots f_{n}(x_{n})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}\,dt

where
Q(\lambda)=[-\frac{\lambda}{2},\frac{\lambda}{2}]^{n}
and
t+Q(\lambda)
is the shift of the cube
Q(\lambda)
by the vector
t
.
Note that if
S\subseteq Q
is
a set of
measure
|S|\geq\delta|Q|
for some
\delta>0
, then for a given
\varepsilon>0
, Hölder implies

(3.4)

\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})=\fint_{t\in Q}\left(\fint_{x\in t+Q(\lambda)}1_{S}(x)\,dx\right)^{n}dt\geq\left(\fint_{t\in Q}\fint_{x\in t+Q(\lambda)}1_{S}(x)\,dx\,dt\right)^{n}\geq\delta^{n}-O(\varepsilon),

for all scales
0<\lambda\ll\varepsilon\,l(Q)
.

Recall that for any
\varepsilon>0
we call a sequence
L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}

\varepsilon
-
admissible
if
L_{j}/L_{j+1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
L_{j+1}\ll\varepsilon^{2}L_{j}
for all
1\leq j<J
. Note that given any lacunary sequence
l(Q)\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J^{\prime}}
with
J^{\prime}\gg(\log\varepsilon^{-1})\,J
,
one can always finds an
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence of scales
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}
such that for each
1\leq j<J
the interval
[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
contains at least two consecutive elements from the original lacunary sequence.

In light of this observation, and the one above regarding a lower bound for
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})
, our proof of Proposition
3.1
reduces to establishing the following “counting lemma”.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
. There exists an integer
J_{1}=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
such that for any
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence of scales
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{1}}
and
S\subseteq Q
there is some
1\leq j<J_{1}
such that

(3.5)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\lambda\in[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
.

There are two main ingredients in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, this will be typical to all of our arguments. The first ingredient is a result which establishes that the our multi-linear forms
\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
are controlled by an appropriate norm which measures the
uniformity of distribution of functions
f:Q\to[-1,1]
with respect to particular scales
L
. This is analogous to estimates in additive combinatorics
[
8
]
which are often referred to as generalized von-Neumann inequalities.

The result below was proved in
[
12
]
for
Q=[0,1]^{n}
, however a simple scaling of the variables
x_{i}
transfers the result to an
arbitrary cube
Q
.

Lemma 3.1

(A Generalized von-Neumann inequality
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
, and
0<L\ll\varepsilon^{6}\lambda
.

For any collections of functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}:\,Q\to[-1,1]
we have

(3.6)

|\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})|\leq\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\|f_{i}\|_{U^{1}_{L}(Q)}+O(\varepsilon)

where for any
f:Q\to[-1,1]
we define

(3.7)

\|f\|_{U^{1}_{L}(Q)}^{2}:=\fint_{t\in Q}\Bigl|\fint_{x\in t+Q(L)}f(x)\,dx\Bigr|^{2}dt

with
t+Q(L)
denoting the shift of the cube
Q(L)=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n}
by the vector
t
.

The corresponding inequality for the multilinear expression
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
, namely the fact that

\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\leq\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\|f_{i}\|_{U^{1}_{L}(Q)}+O(\varepsilon)

whenever
0<L\ll\varepsilon^{6}\lambda
follows easily from Cauchy-Schwarz
together with the simple observation that

\|f\|_{U^{1}_{L}(Q)}\leq\|f\|_{U^{1}_{L^{\prime}}(Q)}+O(\varepsilon)

whenever
L^{\prime}\ll\varepsilon L
.

The second key ingredient, proved in
[
13
]
and generalized in Lemma
3.3
below, is a Koopman-von Neumann type decomposition of functions
where the underlying
\sigma
-algebras are generated by cubes of a fixed length. To recall it, let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a cube,

L>0
be scale that divides
l(Q)
,
Q(L)=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{L,Q}
denote the collection of cubes
t+Q(L)
partitioning the cube
Q
and
\Gamma_{L,Q}
denote the grids corresponding to the centers of the cubes.
By a slightly abuse of notation we also write
\mathcal{G}_{L,Q}
for the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the grid.
Recall that the conditional expectation function
\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{L,Q})
is constant and equal to
\fint_{A}f
on each cube
A\in\mathcal{G}_{L,Q}
.

Lemma 3.2

(A Koopman-von Neumann type decomposition
[
13
]
)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a cube.

There exists an integer
\bar{J}_{1}=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
such that for any
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{\bar{J}_{1}}
and function
f:Q\to[-1,1]
there is some
1\leq j<\bar{J}_{1}
such that

(3.8)

\|f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q})\|_{U^{1}_{L_{j+1}}(Q)}\leq\varepsilon

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q}
be the grid obtained from Lemma
3.2
for the functions
f=1_{S}
for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\bar{f}:=\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q})
, then by (
3.6
) and multi-linearity, we have

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f,\ldots,f)=\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(\bar{f},\ldots,\bar{f})+O(\varepsilon),

and also

\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(f,\ldots,f)=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(\bar{f},\ldots,\bar{f})+O(\varepsilon)

provided for
\varepsilon{{}^{-6}}L_{j+1}\ll\lambda
.
Thus in showing (
6.4
) one can replace the functions
f
with
\bar{f}
.
If we make the additional assumption that
\lambda\ll\varepsilon L_{j}
then it is easy to see,
using the fact that the function
\bar{f}
is constant on the cubes
Q_{t}(L_{j})\in\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q}
, that

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(\bar{f},\ldots,\bar{f})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q}(\bar{f},\ldots,\bar{f})+O(\varepsilon).

Since the condition
\varepsilon^{-6}L_{j+1}\ll\lambda\ll\varepsilon L_{j}
can be replaced with
L_{j+1}\ll\lambda\ll L_{j}
if one passes to a subsequence of scales, for example
L_{j}^{\prime}=L_{5j}
, this completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

3.2.
The base case of a general inductive strategy

In this section, as preparation to handle the case of products of simplices, we prove a parametric version of Proposition
3.2
, namely Proposition
3.3
below, which will serve as the base case for later inductive arguments.

Let
Q=Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{d}
with
Q_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
be cubes of equal side length
l(Q)
. Let
L
be a scale dividing
l(Q)
and for each
\underline{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\Gamma_{L,Q}
let
Q_{\underline{t}}(L)=\underline{t}+Q(L)
and
Q_{t_{i}}(L)=t_{i}+Q_{i}(L)
. Note that
Q_{\underline{t}}(L)=Q_{t_{1}}(L)\times\dots\times Q_{t_{d}}(L)
. Here
Q(L)=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n}
and
Q_{i}(L)=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n_{i}}
for each
1\leq i\leq d
.

Let
\Delta_{i}^{0}=\{v^{i}_{1},\ldots,v^{i}_{n_{i}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
be a non-degenerate simplex for each
1\leq i\leq d
.

Proposition 3.3

(Parametric Counting Lemma on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for Simplices)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
R\geq 1
.
There exists an integer
J_{1}=J_{1}(\varepsilon,R)=O(R\,\varepsilon^{-4})
such that for any
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence of scales
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{1}}
with the property that
L_{0}
divides
l(Q)
and collection of functions

f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}:\,Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})\to[-1,1]
with
1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq k\leq n_{i}
,
1\leq r\leq R
and
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}

there exists
1\leq j<J_{1}
and a set

of size

such that

(3.9)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0},Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,f_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,f_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\lambda\in[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
and
\underline{t}\notin T_{\varepsilon}

uniformly in

and

.

The proof of Proposition
3.3
will follow from Lemma

and the
following generalization of Lemma

in which we simultaneously consider a family of functions supported on the subcubes in a partition of an original cube

.

Lemma 3.3

(A simultaneous Koopman-von Neumann type decomposition)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,
m\geq 1
, and
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a cube.
There exists an integer
\bar{J}_{1}=O(m\varepsilon^{-3})
such that for any
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{\bar{J}_{1}}
with the property that
L_{0}
divides
l(Q)
, and collection of functions

f_{1,t},\dots,f_{m,t}:\,Q_{t}(L_{0})\to[-1,1]

defined for each
t\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
, there is some
1\leq j<\bar{J}_{1}
and a set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}|
such that

(3.10)

\|f_{i,t}-\mathbb{E}(f_{i,t}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q_{t}(L_{0})})\|_{U^{1}_{L_{j+1}}(Q_{t}(L_{0}))}\leq\varepsilon

for all
1\leq i\leq m
and

.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq d
. For
1\leq k\leq n_{i}
and
\underline{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}\,
, we will abuse notation and write

f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}):=f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}(x_{i})

for
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})
.

If we apply Lemma
3.3
to the family of functions
f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}
on
Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})
for

\,1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq k\leq n_{i}
, and
1\leq r\leq R
, with
m=(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d})R
, then this produces a grid
\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q}
for some
1\leq j\leq\bar{J}_{1}=O(\varepsilon^{-3}R)
, and a set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\,\varepsilon|\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}|
, such that

\|f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}-\mathbb{E}(f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q})\|_{U^{1}_{L_{j+1}}(Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0}))}\leq\varepsilon

uniformly for
1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq k\leq n_{i}
and
1\leq r\leq R
for
t\notin T_{\varepsilon}.

Since
f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}(x_{i})
for
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})
it is easy to see that

\|f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}-\mathbb{E}(f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q})\|_{U^{1}_{L_{j+1}}(Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0}))}=\|f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}-\mathbb{E}(f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q_{i}})\|_{U^{1}_{L_{j+1}}(Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0}))}.

Let
\bar{f}_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}:=\mathbb{E}(f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q_{i}})\,
, then by Lemma
3.1
, one has

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0},Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,f_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})=\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0},Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(\bar{f}_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,\bar{f}_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})+O(\varepsilon),

and

\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,f_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(\bar{f}_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,\bar{f}_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\underline{t}\notin T_{\varepsilon}
provided
\varepsilon^{-6}L_{j+1}\ll\lambda
.
Finally, if we also have
\lambda\ll\varepsilon L_{j}
then it is easy to see that

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0},Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(\bar{f}_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,\bar{f}_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(\bar{f}_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,\bar{f}_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})+O(\varepsilon)

as the functions
\bar{f}_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}
are constant on cubes

of
\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q_{i}}
, which are of size
L_{j}\ll\varepsilon L_{0}
.

Passing first to a subsequence of scales, for example
L_{j}^{\prime}=L_{5j}
, the condition
\varepsilon^{-6}L_{j+1}\ll\lambda\ll\varepsilon L_{j}
can be replaced with

so this completes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

We conclude this section with a sketch of the proof of Lemma
3.3
. These arguments are standard, see for example the proof of Lemma
3.2
given in
[
12
]
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

First we make an observation about the
U^{1}_{L}(Q)
-norm.
Suppose
0<L^{\prime}\ll\varepsilon^{2}L
with
L^{\prime}
dividing
L
. If
s\in\Gamma_{L^{\prime},Q}
and
t\in Q_{s}(L^{\prime})
then
|t-s|=O(L^{\prime})
and hence

\fint_{x\in Q_{t}(L)}g(x)\,dx=\fint_{x\in Q_{s}(L)}g(x)\,dx+O(L^{\prime}/L)

for any function
g:Q\to[-1,1]
. Moreover, since the cube
Q_{s}(L)
is partitioned into the smaller cubes
Q_{t}(L^{\prime})
, we have by Cauchy-Schwarz

\Bigl|\fint_{x\in Q_{s}(L)}\,g(x)\,dx\Bigr|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{t\in\Gamma_{L^{\prime},Q_{s}(L)}}\Bigl|\fint_{x\in Q_{t}(L^{\prime})}g(x)\,dx\Bigr|^{2}.

From these observations it is easy to see that

\|g\|_{U^{1}_{L}(Q)}^{2}=\fint_{t\in Q}\Bigl|\fint_{x\in Q_{t}(L)}g(x)\,dx\Bigr|^{2}\,dt\leq\mathbb{E}_{t\in\Gamma_{L^{\prime},Q}}\Bigl|\fint_{x\in Q_{t}(L^{\prime})}g(x)\,dx\Bigr|^{2}+O(L^{\prime}/L)

and we note that the right side of the above expression is
\|\mathbb{E}(g|\mathcal{G}_{L^{\prime},Q})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}
since the conditional expectation function
\mathbb{E}(g|\mathcal{G}_{L^{\prime},Q})
is constant and equal to
\fint_{x\in Q_{t}(L^{\prime})}g(x)\,dx
on the cubes
Q_{t}(L^{\prime})
.

Suppose that (
3.10
) does not hold for some
1\leq i\leq m
for every
t
in some set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|>\varepsilon\,|\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}|
. If we apply the above observation to
g:=f_{i,t}-\mathbb{E}(f_{i,t}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q_{t}(L_{0})})
, for every
t\in T_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain by orthogonality that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\mathbb{E}(f_{i,t}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j+2},Q_{t}(L_{0})})\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t}(L_{0}))}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\mathbb{E}(f_{i,t}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q_{t}(L_{0})})\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t}(L_{0}))}^{2}+c\varepsilon^{2}

for some constant
c>0
.

If we now define
f_{i}:Q\to[-1,1]
such that
f_{i}|_{(Q_{t}(L_{0}))}=f_{i,t}
, for
1\leq i\leq m
, average over
t\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
, and use the fact
\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{t\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}}\|f_{i,t}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t}(L_{0}))}^{2}
, we obtain

(3.11)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\mathbb{E}(f_{i}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j+2},Q})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\mathbb{E}(f_{i}|\mathcal{G}_{L_{j},Q})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+c\varepsilon^{3}.

It is clear that the sums in the above expressions are bounded by
m
for all
j\geq 1
, thus (
3.11
) cannot hold for some
1\leq j\leq\bar{J}_{1}
for
\bar{J}_{1}:=C\,m\,\varepsilon^{-3}
. This implies that (
3.10
) must hold for some
1\leq j\leq\bar{J}_{1}
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
and all
t\notin T_{\varepsilon}
for a set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon\,|\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}|
.
∎

4.1.
Proof of Theorem

Let

with
Q_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
Q_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
be cubes of equal side length

and
\Delta^{0}=\Delta^{0}_{1}\times\Delta^{0}_{2}

\Delta^{0}_{1}=\{v_{11},\ldots,v_{1n_{1}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
\Delta^{0}_{2}=\{v_{11},\ldots,v_{2n_{2}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}

In order to “count” configurations of the form
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
with
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
isometric copies of
\lambda\Delta^{0}_{1}
and

respectively for some

in a set

the multi-linear expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{2}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(\{f_{kl}\})

\displaystyle:=\fint_{x_{11}\in Q_{1}}\fint_{x_{21}\in Q_{2}}\int_{x_{12},\ldots,x_{1n_{1}}}\int_{x_{22},\ldots,x_{2n_{2}}}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n_{1}}\prod_{l=1}^{n_{2}}\ f_{kl}(x_{1k},x_{2l})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad d\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}_{1}}(x_{12}-x_{11},\ldots,x_{1n_{1}}-x_{11})\,d\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}_{2}}(x_{22}-x_{21},\ldots,x_{2n_{2}}-x_{21})\,dx_{21}\,dx_{11}

for any family of functions

with

and
1\leq l\leq n_{2}
.

Indeed, if
f_{kl}=1_{S}
for all

and
1\leq l\leq n_{2}
then the above expression is 0 unless there exists a configuration
\Delta\subseteq S
of the form
\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}
with
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
isometric copies of
\lambda\Delta^{0}_{1}
and
\lambda\Delta^{0}_{2}
respectively.

The short argument presented in Section
1.1
demonstrating how both Theorem B and Corollary B follow from Proposition B, and hence from Proposition
3.1
, applies equally well to each of our main theorems. This reduces our main theorems to analogous quantitative results involving an arbitrary lacunary sequence of scales. In the case
d=2
of Theorem
1.2
this stronger quantitative result takes the following form:

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
there exists an integer
J=O(\exp(C\varepsilon^{-13}))
with the following property:

Given any lacunary sequence
l(Q)\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J}
and
S\subseteq Q
, there is some
1\leq j<J
such that

(4.1)

\mathcal{N}^{2}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(\{1_{S}\})>\left(\frac{|S|}{|Q|}\right)^{n_{1}n_{2}}-\varepsilon

for all
\lambda\in[\lambda_{j+1},\lambda_{j}]
.

Our approach to establishing Proposition
4.1
is again to compare the above expressions to simpler ones for which it is easy to
obtain lower bounds.
For any
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
and family of functions
f_{kl}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\to[-1,1]
with
1\leq k\leq n_{1}
and
1\leq l\leq n_{2}
we consider

\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\lambda,Q}(\{f_{kl}\}):=\fint_{\underline{t}\in Q}\fint_{\underline{x}_{1}\in(t_{1}+Q_{1}(\lambda))^{n_{1}}}\fint_{\underline{x}_{2}\in(t_{2}+Q_{2}(\lambda))^{n_{2}}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{1}}\prod_{l=2}^{n_{2}}f_{kl}(x_{1k},x_{2l})\,d\underline{x}_{2}\,d\underline{x}_{1}\,d\underline{t}

where
\underline{t}=(t_{1},t_{2})\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
,
\underline{x}_{i}=(x_{i1},\dots,x_{in_{i}})
and

for

.

Note that if
S\subseteq Q
is
a set of
measure

for some

, then careful applications of Hölder’s inequality give

\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\lambda,Q}(\{1_{S}\})\geq\fint_{\underline{t}\in Q}\left(\fint_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\underline{t}+Q(\lambda)}1_{S}(x_{1},x_{2})\,dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{n_{1}n_{2}}d\underline{t}\,\geq\delta^{n_{1}n_{2}}-O(\varepsilon)

for all scales
0<\lambda\ll\varepsilon\,l(Q)
.

In light of the observation above, and the discussion preceding Proposition
3.2
, we see that Proposition
4.1
, and hence Theorem
1.2
when
d=2
, will follows as a consequence of the following

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. There exists an integer
J_{2}=O(\exp(C\varepsilon^{-12}))

such that for any
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence of scales
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{2}}
and
S\subseteq Q
there is some
1\leq j<J_{2}
such that

(4.2)

\mathcal{N}^{2}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(\{1_{S}\})=\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\lambda,Q}(\{1_{S}\})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\lambda\in[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
.

There are again two main ingredients in the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
The first establishes that the our multi-linear forms
\mathcal{N}^{2}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(\{f_{kl}\})
are controlled by an appropriate box-type norm attached to a scale
L
.

Let
Q=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
be a cube. For any scale
0<L\ll l(Q)
and function

we define its local box norm at scale
L
to be

(4.3)

\|f\|_{\Box_{L}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})}^{4}:=\fint_{\underline{t}\in Q}\|f\|_{\Box(\underline{t}+Q(L))}^{4}\,d\underline{t}

where
Q(L)=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
and

(4.4)

\|f\|_{\Box(\widetilde{Q})}^{4}:=\fint_{x_{11},x_{12}\in\widetilde{Q}_{1}}\fint_{x_{21},x_{22}\in\widetilde{Q}_{2}}f(x_{11},x_{21})f(x_{12},x_{21})f(x_{11},x_{22})f(x_{12},x_{22})\,dx_{11}\ldots dx_{22}

for any cube
\widetilde{Q}\subseteq Q
of the form
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{Q}_{1}\times\widetilde{Q}_{2}
with
\widetilde{Q}_{j}\subseteq Q_{j}
for
j=1,2
.

Lemma 4.1

(A Generalized von-Neumann inequality
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
, and
0<L\ll\varepsilon^{24}\lambda
.

For any collections of functions
f_{kl}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\to[-1,1]
with
1\leq k\leq n_{1}
and
1\leq l\leq n_{2}
we have both

(4.5)

|\mathcal{N}^{2}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(\{f_{kl}\})|\leq\min_{1\leq k\leq n_{1},\,1\leq l\leq n_{2}}\|f_{kl}\|_{\Box_{L}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})}+O(\varepsilon)

(4.6)

|\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\lambda,Q}(\{f_{kl}\})|\leq\min_{1\leq k\leq n_{1},\,1\leq l\leq n_{2}}\|f_{kl}\|_{\Box_{L}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})}.

The result above was essentially proved in
[

]
for the multi-linear forms

when

.
For completeness we include its short proof in Section

The second and main ingredient is an analogue of a weak form of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma due to Frieze and Kannan
[

]
. The more probabilistic formulation, we will use below, can be found for example in

]

For any cube
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and scale

that divides
l(Q)
we will let
Q(L)=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n}
and
\mathcal{G}_{L,Q}
denote the collection of cubes

partitioning the cube

and let
\Gamma_{L,Q}
denote grid corresponding to the centers of these cubes.
We will say that a finite
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
on

\mathcal{G}_{L,Q}

and for simplicity of notation will write

.

Recall that if we have two
\sigma

\sigma
-algebra on

generated by the sets

with

.

Lemma 4.2

(Weak regularity lemma in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
Q=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
with
Q_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
Q_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
be cubes of equal side length

.

There exists an integer
\bar{J}_{2}=O(\varepsilon^{-12})
such that for any
\varepsilon^{4}
-admissible sequence
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{\bar{J}_{2}}
and function
f:Q\to[-1,1]
there is some
1\leq j\leq\bar{J}_{2}
and a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
of scale

on

such that

(4.7)

\|f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\|_{\Box_{L_{j+1}}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})}\leq\varepsilon

which has the additional local structure that for each

\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{1,\underline{t}}
on

and
\mathcal{B}_{2,\underline{t}}

\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{i,\underline{t}})=O(j)

\mathcal{B}_{\underline{t}}=\mathcal{B}_{1,\underline{t}}\vee\mathcal{B}_{2,\underline{t}}.

Comparing the above statement to Lemma

of scale

has a direct product structure only locally, inside each cube

Assuming for now the validity of Lemmas

. We will make crucial use of Proposition

, namely our parametric counting lemma on

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Let

, and

.
We now choose a subsequence

Applying Lemma

, guarantees the existence of a
\sigma

\mathcal{B}

\|f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\|_{\Box_{L^{\prime}_{j+1}}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})}\leq\varepsilon

.
Moreover, we know that
\mathcal{B}
has the additional local structure that for each

\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{1,\underline{t}}
on

\mathcal{B}_{2,\underline{t}}
on

\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{i,\underline{t}})=O(\varepsilon^{-12})

\mathcal{B}_{\underline{t}}=\mathcal{B}_{1,\underline{t}}\vee\mathcal{B}_{2,\underline{t}}.

Thus, if we let

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\widetilde{Q}}(1_{S}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}}):=\fint_{\underline{x}_{1}\in\widetilde{Q}_{1}^{n_{1}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\cdots\ \fint_{\underline{x}_{d}\in\widetilde{Q}_{d}^{n_{d}}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}}}1_{S}(\underline{x}_{e})\,d\underline{x}_{1}\ldots d\underline{x}_{d}

for any cube
\widetilde{Q}\subseteq Q
of the form
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{Q}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widetilde{Q}_{d}
with
\widetilde{Q}_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
.
Note that if
S\subseteq Q
is
a set of
measure
|S|\geq\delta|Q|
for some
\delta>0
, then careful applications of Hölder’s inequality give

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q}(1_{S}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}})\geq\fint_{\underline{t}\in Q}\left(\fint_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\underline{t}+Q(\lambda)}1_{S}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{d}\right)^{n_{1}\cdots\,n_{d}}d\underline{t}\,\geq\delta^{n_{1}\cdots\,n_{d}}-O(\varepsilon)

for all scales
0<\lambda\ll\varepsilon\,l(Q)
.

In light of the discussion above, and that preceding Proposition
3.2
, we see that Proposition
5.1
, and hence Theorem
1.2
in general, will follows as a consequence of the following

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. There exists an integer
J_{d}=J_{d}(\varepsilon)
such that for any
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence of scales
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{d}}
and
S\subseteq Q
there is some
1\leq j<J_{d}
such that

(5.5)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(1_{S}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}})=\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q}(1_{S}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\lambda\in[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
.

The validity of Proposition
5.2
will follow immediately from the
d=k
case of Proposition
5.3
below.

5.1.
Reduction of Proposition
5.2
to a more general “local” counting lemma

For any given
1\leq k\leq d
and collection of functions
f_{e}:Q_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
we define the following multi-linear expressions

(5.6)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}):=\int_{\underline{x}_{1}}\cdots\int_{\underline{x}_{d}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e})\ d\sigma^{\lambda}_{1}(\underline{x}_{1})\ldots.d\sigma^{\lambda}_{d}(\underline{x}_{d})

and

(5.7)

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q}(f_{e}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}):=\fint_{\underline{t}\in Q}\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\underline{t}+Q(\lambda)}(f_{e}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})\,d\underline{t}

where
Q(\lambda)=[-\frac{\lambda}{2},\frac{\lambda}{2}]^{n}
and

(5.8)

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\widetilde{Q}}(f_{e}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}):=\fint_{\underline{x}_{1}\in\widetilde{Q}_{1}^{n_{1}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\cdots\ \fint_{\underline{x}_{d}\in\widetilde{Q}_{d}^{n_{d}}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e})\,d\underline{x}_{1}\ldots d\underline{x}_{d}

for any cube
\widetilde{Q}\subseteq Q
of the form
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{Q}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widetilde{Q}_{d}
with
\widetilde{Q}_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
.

Our strategy to proving Proposition
5.2
is the same as illustrated in the finite field settings, that is we would like to compare averages
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})
to those of
\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q}(f_{e}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})
, at certain scales
\lambda\in[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
, inductively for
1\leq k\leq d
.
However in the Euclidean case, an extra complication emerges due to the fact the (hypergraph) regularity lemma, the analogue of Lemma
2.2
, does not produce
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}}
, for
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k-1}^{\underline{n}}
, on the cubes

. In a similar manner to the case for

discussed in the previous section, we will only obtain
\sigma
-algebras “local” on cubes

at some scale

. This will have the effect that the functions
f_{e}
will be replaced by a family of functions
f_{e,\underline{t}}
, where
\underline{t}
runs through a grid
\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
.

To be more precise, let
L>0
be a scale dividing the side-length
l(Q)
. For
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L,Q}
and
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}
we will use
\underline{t}_{e^{\prime}}
to denote the projection of
\underline{t}
onto
Q_{e^{\prime}}
and

to denote the projection of the cube
Q_{\underline{t}}(L)
centered at
\underline{t}
onto
Q_{e^{\prime}}
.
It is then easy to see that for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

(5.9)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L,Q}}\,\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q_{\underline{t}}(L)}(f_{e,\underline{t}}\,;e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

and

(5.10)

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L,Q}}\,\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q_{\underline{t}}(L)}(f_{e,\underline{t}}\,;e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

provided
0<\lambda\ll\varepsilon L
where
f_{e,\underline{t}}
denotes the restriction of a function
f_{e}
to the cube
Q_{\underline{t}}(L)
.

At this point the proof of Proposition
5.2
reduces to showing that the expressions in (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) only differ by
O(\varepsilon)
at some scales
\lambda\in[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
, given an
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}
, for any collection of bounded functions
f_{e,\underline{t}}
,
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
,
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
. Indeed, our crucial result will be the following

Proposition 5.3

(Local Counting Lemma)
.

Let

0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
M\geq 1
. There exists an integer
J_{k}=J_{k}(\varepsilon,M)
such that for any
\varepsilon
-admissible sequence of scales
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{k}}
with the property that
L_{0}
divides
l(Q)
, and collection of functions

f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m}:Q_{t_{\pi(e)}}(L_{0}):\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
,
1\leq m\leq M
and
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}

there exists
1\leq j<J_{k}
and a set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}|
such that

(5.11)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

for all

and
\underline{t}\notin T_{\varepsilon}

uniformly in

and

.

5.2.
Proof of Proposition
5.3

We will prove Proposition
5.3
by induction on

. For

this is basically Proposition
3.3
.

Indeed, in this case for a given

with

and hence both

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,1}^{\underline{n}})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\ \mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0},Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{i1,\underline{t}}^{m},\ldots,f_{in_{i},\underline{t}}^{m})

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,1}^{\underline{n}})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\ \mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{i1,\underline{t}}^{m},\ldots,f_{in_{i},\underline{t}}^{m}).

By Proposition
3.3
there exists an
1\leq j<J_{1}=O(M\varepsilon^{-4})
and an exceptional set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}|
, such that uniformly for
\underline{t}\notin T_{\varepsilon}
and for

, one has

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0},Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{i1,\underline{t}}^{m},\ldots,f_{in_{i},\underline{t}}^{m})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,Q_{t_{i}}(L_{0})}(f_{i1,\underline{t}}^{m},\ldots,f_{in_{i},\underline{t}}^{m})+O(\varepsilon)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,1}^{\underline{n}})=\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q_{\underline{t}}(L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,1}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

as the all factors are trivially bounded by 1 in magnitude. This implies (
5.11
) for

.

For the induction step we again need two main ingredients.
The first establishes that the our multi-linear forms
\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{e};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})
are controlled by an appropriate box-type norm attached to a scale
L
.

Let
Q=Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{d}
and
1\leq k\leq d
. For any scale
0<L\ll l(Q)
and function

with

we define its local box norm at scale
L
by

(5.12)

\|f\|_{\Box_{L}(Q_{e^{\prime}})}^{2^{k}}:=\fint_{\underline{s}\in Q_{e^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{\Box(\underline{s}+Q(L))}^{2^{k}}\,d\underline{s}

where

(5.13)

\|f\|_{\Box(\widetilde{Q})}^{2^{k}}:=\fint_{x_{11},x_{12}\in\widetilde{Q}_{1}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\cdots\ \fint_{x_{k1},x_{k2}\in\widetilde{Q}_{k}}\prod_{(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k})\in\{1,2\}^{k}}f(x_{1\ell_{1}},\dots,x_{k\ell_{k}})\,dx_{11}\,dx_{12}\ldots\,dx_{k1}\,dx_{k2}

for any cube
\widetilde{Q}
of the form
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{Q}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widetilde{Q}_{k}
.

Lemma 5.1

(Generalized von-Neumann inequality)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
and
0<L\ll(\varepsilon^{2^{k}})^{6}\lambda
.

For any
1\leq k\leq d
and collection of functions
f_{e}:Q_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
we have both

(5.14)

|\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{e};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})|\leq\min_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}\|f_{e}\|_{\Box_{L}(Q_{\pi(e)})}+O(\varepsilon)

(5.15)

|\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,Q}(f_{e};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})|\leq\min_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}\|f_{e}\|_{\Box_{L}(Q_{\pi(e)})}.

The crucial ingredient is the following analogue of the weak hypergraph regularity lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Parametric weak hypergraph regularity lemma for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
M\geq 1
, and
1\leq k\leq d
.

There exists
\bar{J}_{k}=O(M\varepsilon^{-2^{k+3}})
such that for any
\varepsilon^{2^{k}}
-admissible sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{\bar{J}_{k}}
with the property that
L_{0}
divides
l(Q)
and collection of functions

with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
,
1\leq m\leq M
, and
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}

there is some
1\leq j<\bar{J}_{k}
and
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}
of scale
L_{j}
on
Q_{\underline{t}_{e^{\prime}}}(L_{0})
for each
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
and

such that

(5.16)

\|f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m}-\mathbb{E}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e),\underline{t}})\|_{\Box_{L_{j+1}}(Q_{\underline{t}_{\pi(e)}}(L_{0}))}\leq\varepsilon

uniformly for all
t\notin T_{\varepsilon}
,

, and

Moreover, the

-algebras

\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},\underline{s}}

with

for each

(5.17)

\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}\bigr\rvert_{Q_{\underline{s}_{e^{\prime}}}(L_{j})}=\bigvee_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial e^{\prime}}\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},\underline{s}}.

5.2
is the parametric and simultaneous version of the extension of Lemma 3.7 to the product of

\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}(L_{j+2}))
of scale
L_{j+2}
on
Q_{\underline{t}_{e^{\prime}}}(L_{0})
, with
e^{\prime}=\pi(e)
, such that (
5.43
) holds.

For all
e^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}
with
e^{\prime\prime}\neq e^{\prime}
let
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime},\underline{s}}(L_{j+2})
be the restriction of the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime},\underline{s}^{\prime}}(L_{j})
to the cube
Q_{\underline{s}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}}(L_{j+2})
, where
\underline{s}^{\prime}
is such that
\underline{s}\in Q_{\underline{s}^{\prime}}(L_{j})
. By (
5.39
) this implies that
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime\prime},\underline{s}}(L_{j+2})
is also the restriction of
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime\prime},\underline{s}^{\prime}}(L_{j})
to the cube
Q_{\underline{s}_{e^{\prime\prime}}}(L_{j+2})
, and hence the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime\prime},\underline{t}}(L_{j+2})
is generated by the grid
\mathcal{G}_{L_{j+2},Q_{\underline{t}_{e^{\prime\prime}}}(L_{0})}
and the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime\prime},\underline{t}}(L_{j})
.

We have therefore defined a family of the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}(L_{j+2})
for
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}
, satisfying

\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}\|\mathbb{E}(f^{m}_{e,\underline{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e),\underline{t}}(L_{j+2}))\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\underline{t}_{\pi(e)}}(L_{0}))}^{2}\geq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}\|\mathbb{E}(f^{m}_{e,\underline{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e),\underline{t}}(L_{j}))\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\underline{t}_{\pi(e)}}(L_{0}))}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2^{k+2}}}{2^{2k+6}}.

Using the fact that
|T_{\varepsilon}|\geq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}|
and averaging over
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L_{0},Q}
it follows using the notations of (
5.36
) that

\mathcal{E}(f^{m}_{e,\underline{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}(L_{j+2}))\,\geq\,\mathcal{E}(f^{m}_{e,\underline{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}(L_{j}))+\frac{\varepsilon^{2^{k+3}}}{2^{2k+6}}.

As the total energy
\mathcal{E}(f^{m}_{e,\underline{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}(L_{j}))
is bounded by
O(M)
, the process must stop at a step
j=O(M\,\varepsilon^{-2^{k+3}})
where (
5.16
) holds for a
\sigma
-algebra of “local complexity” at most
j
, completing the proof of Lemma
5.2
. ∎

6.
The base case of an inductive strategy to establish Theorem
1.4

In this section we will ultimately establish the base case of our more general inductive argument.
We will however start by giving a (new) proof of Theorem B
′
, namely the case
d=1
of Theorem
1.4
.

6.1.
A Single Simplex in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

Let
\Delta^{0}=\{v_{1}=0,v_{2},\dots,v_{n_{1}}\}
be a fixed non-degenerate simplex of
n_{1}
points in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
n=2n_{1}+3
and define
t_{kl}:=v_{k}\cdot v_{l}
for
2\leq k,l\leq n_{1}
.
Recall, see
[
17
]
, that a simplex
\Delta=\{m_{1}=0,\dots,m_{n_{1}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is isometric to
\lambda\Delta^{0}
if and only if

m_{k}\cdot m_{l}=\lambda^{2}t_{kl}
for all
2\leq k,l\leq n_{1}
.

For any positive integer
q
and
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
we define
S_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}(m_{2},\dots,m_{n_{1}}):\mathbb{Z}^{n(n_{1}-1)}\to\{0,1\}
be the function whose value is 1 if
m_{k}\cdot m_{l}=\lambda^{2}t_{kl}
with both
m_{k}
and
m_{l}
in
(q\mathbb{Z})^{n}

for all
2\leq k,l\leq n_{1}
and is equal to 0 otherwise. It is a well-known fact in number theory, see
[
11
]
or
[
17
]
, that for
n\geq 2n_{1}+1
we have that

\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}}S_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}})=\rho(\Delta^{0})\,(\lambda/q)^{(n-n_{1})(n_{1}-1)}(1+O(\lambda^{-\tau}))

for some absolute constant
\tau>0
and some constant
\rho(\Delta^{0})>0
, the so-called singular series, which can be interpreted as the product of the densities of the solutions of the above system of equations among the
p
-adics and among the reals.
Thus if we define

\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}:=\rho(\Delta^{0})^{-1}(\lambda/q)^{-(n-n_{1})(n_{1}-1)}S_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}

then
\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}
is normalized in so much that

\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}}\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}})=1+O(\lambda^{-\tau})

for some absolute constant
\tau>0
.

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a fixed cube and let
l(Q)
denotes its side length.
For any family of functions

f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{1}}:Q\to[-1,1]

and
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
we define the following two multi-linear expressions

(6.1)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{1}}):=\mathbb{E}_{m_{1}\in Q}\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}}f_{1}(m_{1})\ldots f_{n_{1}}(m_{n_{1}})\,\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}(m_{2}-m_{1},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}-m_{1})

and

(6.2)

\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,q,Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{1}}):=\mathbb{E}_{t\in Q}\,\mathbb{E}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}\in t+Q(q,\lambda)}\ f_{1}(m_{1})\ldots f_{n_{1}}(m_{n_{1}})

where
Q(q,\lambda):=[-\frac{\lambda}{2},\frac{\lambda}{2}]^{n}\cap(q\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.
Note that if
S\subseteq Q
and
\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})>0
then
S
must contain an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta^{0}
, while if
|S|\geq\delta|Q|
for some
\delta>0
then as before Hölder implies that

(6.3)

\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,q,Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})\geq\delta^{n}-O(\varepsilon)

for all scales
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
0<\lambda\ll\varepsilon\,l(Q)
.

Recall that for any
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and positive integer
q
we call a sequence
L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}

(\varepsilon,q)
-
admissible
if
L_{j}/L_{j+1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
L_{j+1}\ll\varepsilon^{2}L_{j}
for all
1\leq j<J
and
L_{J}/q\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that if
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J^{\prime}}\geq 1
is any lacunary sequence in
q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
J^{\prime}\gg(\log\varepsilon^{-1})\,J+\log q
,
one can always finds an
(\varepsilon,q)
-admissible sequence of scales
L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}
with the property that for each
1\leq j<J
the interval
[L_{j+1},L_{j}]
contains at least two consecutive elements from the original lacunary sequence.

In light of these observations we see that the following “counting lemma” ultimately establishes a quantitatively stronger version of Proposition B
′
that appeared in Section
1.3
and hence immediately establishes Theorem
1.4
for
d=1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
q_{j}:=q_{1}(\varepsilon)^{j}
for
j\geq 1
with
q_{1}(\varepsilon):=\lcm\{1\leq q\leq C\varepsilon^{-10}\}
.

There exists
J_{1}=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
such that for any
(\varepsilon,q_{J_{1}})
-admissible sequence of scales
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{1}}
and
S\subseteq Q
there is some
1\leq j<J_{1}
such that

(6.4)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q_{j},Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,q_{j},Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\lambda\in q_{j}\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
L_{j+1}\leq\lambda\leq L_{j}
.

As in the continuous setting the proof of Proposition
6.1
has two main ingredients, namely Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
below.
In these lemmas, and for the remainder Sections
6
and
7
, we will continue to use the notation

q_{1}(\varepsilon):=\lcm\{1\leq q\leq C\varepsilon^{-10}\}

for any given
\varepsilon>0
.

Lemma 6.1

(A Generalized von Neumann inequality)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
with
qq_{1}(\varepsilon)|q^{\prime}
, and
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
\lambda\ll l(Q)
and
1\ll L\ll\varepsilon^{10}\lambda
.
For any collection of functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{1}}:Q\to[-1,1]
we have

(6.5)

|\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{1}})|\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq n_{1}}\|f_{i}\|_{U^{1}_{q^{\prime},L}(Q)}+O(\varepsilon)

where for any function
f:Q\to[-1,1]
we define

(6.6)

\|f\|_{U^{1}_{q,L}(Q)}:=\Bigl(\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{t\in Q}|f*\chi_{q,L}(t)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

with
\chi_{q,L}
denoting the normalized characteristic function of the cubes
Q(q,L):=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n}\cap(q\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.

For any cube
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of side length
l(Q)
and
q,L\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
q\ll L
with
L
dividing
l(Q)
, we shall now partition
Q
into cubic grids
Q_{t}(q,L)=t+((q\mathbb{Z})^{n}\cap Q(L))
, with
Q(L)=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n}
as usual. These grids form the atoms of a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}_{q,L,Q}
.
Note that if
q|q^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime}|L
then
\mathcal{G}_{q,L,Q}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime},Q}
.

Lemma 6.2

(A Koopman-von Neumann type decomposition)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
q_{j}:=q_{1}(\varepsilon)^{j}
for all
j\geq 1
.
There exists an integer

\bar{J}_{1}=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
such that any
(\varepsilon,q_{\bar{J}_{1}})
-admissible sequence of scales
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{\bar{J}_{1}}
and function
f:Q\to[-1,1]
there is some
1\leq j<\bar{J}_{1}
such that

(6.7)

\|f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{q_{j},L_{j},Q})\|_{U^{1}_{q_{j+1},L_{j+1}}(Q)}\,\leq\,\varepsilon.

The reduction of Proposition
6.1
to these two lemmas is essentially identical to the analogous argument in the continuous setting as presented at the end of Section
3.1
, we choose to omit the details.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

We will rely on some prior exponential sum estimates, specifically Propositions 4.2 and 4.4 in
[
17
]
.
First we deal with the case
n_{1}\geq 3
. By the change of variables
m_{1}:=m_{1},\ m_{i}:=m_{i}-m_{1}
for
2\leq i\leq n_{1}
, one may write

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{1}}):=\mathbb{E}_{m_{1}\in Q_{N}}\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}}f_{1}(m_{1})f_{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})\cdots f_{n_{1}}(m_{1}+m_{n_{1}})\,\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}).

We now write

\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}})=\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0\prime},q}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}-1})\,\sigma_{\lambda,q}^{m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}-1}}(m_{n_{1}})

where
\Delta^{0\prime}=\{v_{1}=0,v_{2},\dots,v_{n_{1}-1}\}
and for each
m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}-1}\in(q\mathbb{Z})^{n}
we are using
\sigma_{\lambda,q}^{m_{2},\dots,m_{n_{1}-1}}(m)
denote the (essentially) normalized indicator function of the subset of
(q\mathbb{Z})^{n}
that contains
m
if and only if
m\cdot m_{k}=\lambda^{2}t_{kn_{1}}
for all
2\leq k\leq n_{1}
.

Using the fact that
|f_{i}|\leq 1
, together with Cauchy-Schwarz and Plancherel, one can then easily see that

(6.8)

|\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{1}})|^{2}\leq|Q|^{-1}\int_{\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\widehat{f}_{n_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}H_{\lambda,q}(\xi)\,d\xi

with

H_{\lambda,q}(\xi)=\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}}\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0\prime},q}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}-1})\,|\widehat{\sigma_{\lambda,q}^{m_{2},\ldots,m_{n_{1}-1}}}(\xi)|^{2}.

It then follows by Propositions 4.2 and 4.4 in
[
17
]
, with
\delta=\varepsilon^{4}
and after rescaling by
q
, that
in addition to being non-negative and
uniformly bounded in
\xi
we in fact have

(6.9)

H_{\lambda,q}(\xi)=O(\varepsilon)\quad\text{
whenever}\quad\left|q\xi-\frac{l}{q_{1}(\varepsilon)}\right|\geq\frac{q}{\varepsilon^{4}\lambda},

for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

We note that the expression
H_{\lambda,q}(\xi)
may be interpreted as the Fourier transform of the indicator function of the set of integer points on a certain variety, and estimate (
6.9
) indicates that this concentrates near rational points of small denominator. It is this crucial fact from number theory which makes results like Theorem B
′
possible.

Since

\widehat{\chi}_{q,L}(\xi)=\frac{q^{n}}{L^{n}}\sum_{m\in[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2})^{n},\ q|m}e^{-2\pi im\cdot\xi}

it is easy to see that
\widehat{\chi}_{q,L}(l/q)=1
for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and that there exists some absolute constant
C>0
such that

(6.10)

0\leq 1-\widehat{\chi}_{q,L}(\xi)^{2}\leq C\,L\,|\xi-l/q|

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. It is then easy to see using our assumption that
qq_{1}(\varepsilon)|q^{\prime}
that

(6.11)

0\leq H_{\lambda,q}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\chi}_{q^{\prime},L}(\xi)^{2})\leq C\varepsilon

for some constant
C>0
uniformly in
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
provided
L\ll\varepsilon^{5}\lambda
.
Substituting inequality (
6.7
) into (
6.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{1}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|Q|^{-1}\left(\int|\hat{f}_{n_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}H_{\lambda}(\xi)\widehat{\chi}_{q^{\prime},L}(\xi)^{2}\,d\xi\,+\,\int|\hat{f}_{n_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}H_{\lambda}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\chi}_{q^{\prime},L}(\xi)^{2})\,d\xi\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{n_{1}}\|_{U^{1}_{q^{\prime},L}(Q)}^{2}+O(\varepsilon)

provided
L\ll\varepsilon^{5}\lambda
.
This proves Lemma
6.1
for
k\geq 3
, as it is clear that by re-indexing the above estimate holds for any of the functions
f_{i}
in place of
f_{n_{1}}
.
For
n_{1}=2
an easy modification of arguments in
[
14
]
, specifically the proof of Lemma 3 therein, establishes that

|\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{1},f_{2})|^{2}\leq\|f_{i}\|_{U^{1}_{q^{\prime},L}(Q)}^{2}+O(\varepsilon)

for
i=1,2
provided
L\ll\varepsilon^{5}\lambda
. ∎

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

Let
q,L\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L|N
,
q|L
. The “modulo
q
” grids
Q_{t}(q,L)=t+Q(q,L)
partition the cube
Q
with
t
running through the set
\Gamma_{q,L,Q}=\{1,\ldots,q\}^{n}+\Gamma_{L,Q}
, where
\Gamma_{L,Q}
denote the centers of the “integer” grids
t+Q(L)
in an initial partition of
Q
. Let
q^{\prime},L^{\prime}
be positive integers so that
q|q^{\prime}
,
L^{\prime}|L
and
L^{\prime}\ll\varepsilon^{2}L
. If
s\in\Gamma_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime},Q}
and
t\in Q_{s}(q^{\prime},L^{\prime})
then
|t-s|=O(L^{\prime})
and hence

\mathbb{E}_{x\in Q_{t}(q,L)}g(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x\in Q_{s}(q,L)}g(x)+O(L^{\prime}/L)

for any function
g:Q\to[-1,1]
. Moreover, since the cube
Q_{s}(q,L)
is partitioned into the smaller cubes
Q_{t}(q^{\prime},L^{\prime})
, we have by Cauchy-Schwarz

|\mathbb{E}_{x\in Q_{s}(q,L)}\,g(x)|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{t\in\Gamma_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime},Q_{s}(q,L)}}|\mathbb{E}_{x\in Q_{t}(q^{\prime},L^{\prime})}g(x)|^{2}.

From this it is easy to see that

\|g\|_{U^{1}_{q,L}(Q)}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{t\in Q}|\mathbb{E}_{x\in Q_{t}(q,L)}g(x)|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{t\in\Gamma_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime},Q}}\ |\mathbb{E}_{x\in Q_{t}(q^{\prime},L^{\prime})}g(x)|^{2}+O(L^{\prime}/L)

and we note that the right side of the above expression is
\|\mathbb{E}(g|\mathcal{G}_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime},Q})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}
since the conditional expectation function
\mathbb{E}(g|\mathcal{G}_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime},Q})
is constant and equal to
\mathbb{E}_{x\in Q_{t}(q^{\prime},L^{\prime})}g(x)
on the cubes
Q_{t}(q^{\prime},L^{\prime})
.

Now suppose (
6.7
) does not hold for some
j\geq 1
, that is

\|f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{q_{j},L_{j},Q})\|_{U^{1}_{q_{j+1},L_{j+1}}(Q)}^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2}.

Since
L_{j+2}\ll\varepsilon^{2}L_{j+1}
,
L_{j+2}|L_{j}
, and
q_{j+1}|q_{j+2}
we
can apply the above observations to
g:=f-\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{q_{j},L_{j},Q})
and obtain, by orthogonality, that

(6.12)

\|\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{q_{j+2},L_{j+2},Q})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\geq\|\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{G}_{q_{j},L_{j},Q})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+c\varepsilon^{2}

for some constant
c>0
.
Since the above expressions are clearly bounded by
1
, the above procedure must stop in
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
steps at which (
6.7
) must hold for some
1\leq j\leq\bar{J}_{1}(\varepsilon)
with
\bar{J}_{1}(\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
.
∎

6.2.
The base case of our general inductive strategy

Let
Q=Q_{1}\times\ldots\times Q_{d}
with
Q_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n_{i}+3}
be cubes of equal side length
l(Q)
and
\Delta_{i}^{0}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n_{i}+3}
be a non-degenerate simplex of
n_{i}
points for
1\leq i\leq d
.

We note that for any
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and scale
L_{0}
dividing
l(Q)
if
\underline{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
, then the corresponding grids
Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})
in the partition of
Q
take the form
Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})=Q_{t_{1}}(q_{0},L_{0})\times\dots\times Q_{t_{d}}(q_{0},L_{0})
.

As in the continuous setting we will ultimately need a parametric version of Proposition
6.1
, namely Proposition
6.2
below.

Proposition 6.2

(Parametric Counting Lemma on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for Simplices)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
R\geq 1
.

There exists an integer
J_{1}=J_{1}(\varepsilon,R)=O(R\,\varepsilon^{-4})
such that for any
(\varepsilon,q_{J_{1}})
-admissible sequence of scales
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{1}}

with
L_{0}
dividing
l(Q)
and
q_{j}:=q_{0}q_{1}(\varepsilon)^{j}
for
0\leq j\leq J_{1}
with
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and collection of functions

f_{k,\underline{t}}^{i,r}:\,Q_{t_{i}}(q_{0},L_{0})\to[-1,1]
with
1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq k\leq n_{i}
,
1\leq r\leq R
and
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}

there exists
1\leq j<J_{1}
and a set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}|
such that

(6.13)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0},q_{j},Q_{t_{i}}(q_{0},L_{0})}(f_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,f_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\lambda,q_{j},Q_{t_{i}}(q_{0},L_{0})}(f_{1,\underline{t}}^{i,r},\ldots,f_{n_{i},\underline{t}}^{i,r})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\lambda\in q_{j}\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
L_{j+1}\leq\lambda\leq L_{j}
and
\underline{t}\notin T_{\varepsilon}

uniformly in
1\leq i\leq d
and
1\leq r\leq R
.

This proposition follows, as the analogous result did in the continuous setting, from Lemma
6.1
and the follow
parametric version of Lemma
6.2
.

Lemma 6.3

(A simultaneous Koopman-von Neumann type decomposition)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
m\geq 1
, and
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a cube. There exists an integer
\bar{J}_{1}=O(m\varepsilon^{-3})
such that for any
(\varepsilon,q_{\bar{J}_{1}})
-admissible sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{\bar{J}_{1}}

with
L_{0}
dividing
l(Q)
and
q_{j}:=q_{0}q_{1}(\varepsilon)^{j}
for
0\leq j\leq\bar{J}_{1}
with
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and collection of functions

f_{1,t},\ldots f_{m,t}:Q_{t}(q_{0},L_{0})\to[-1,1]

defined for each
t\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
,
there is some
1\leq j<\bar{J}_{1}
and a set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}|
such that

(6.14)

\|f_{i,t}-\mathbb{E}(f_{i,t}|\mathcal{G}_{q_{j},L_{j},Q_{t}(q_{0},L_{0})}\|_{U^{1}_{q_{j+1},L_{j+1}}(Q_{t}(q_{0},L_{0}))}\,\leq\varepsilon

for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
t\notin T_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma
6.3
above is of course the discrete analogue of Lemma
3.2
.
Since the proofs of Proposition
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
are almost identical to the arguments presented in Section

we choose to omit these details.

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
: The general case

After the preparations in Section
6
we can proceed very similarly as in Section
5
to prove our main result in the discrete case, namely Theorem
1.4
. The main difference will be that given
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
1\leq k\leq d
, we construct a positive integer
q_{k}(\varepsilon)
and assume that all our sequences of scales will be
(\varepsilon,q_{k}(\varepsilon))
-admissible. The cubes
Q_{\underline{t}}(L)
will be naturally now be replaced by the grids
Q_{\underline{t}}(q,L)
of the form that already appear in Section
6
where we always assume
q|L
.

Let
\Delta^{0}=\Delta_{1}^{0}\times\ldots\times\Delta_{d}^{0}
with each
\Delta_{i}^{0}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n_{i}+3}
a non-degenerate simplex of
n_{i}
points for
1\leq i\leq d
and
Q=Q_{1}\times\ldots\times Q_{d}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
Q_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n_{i}+3}
cubes of equal side length

(taken much larger than the diameter of
\Delta^{0}
). We will use the same parameterizations in terms of hypergraph bundles
\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
and corresponding notations as in Section
5
to count the configurations
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\times\ldots\times\Delta_{d}\subseteq Q

with each
\Delta_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0}
for some
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
.

Given any positive integer
q
and
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
we will make use of the notation

(7.1)

\sum_{\underline{x}_{i}}f(\underline{x}_{i})\,\sigma^{i}_{\lambda,q}(\underline{x}_{i}):=\mathbb{E}_{x_{i1}\in Q_{i}}\sum_{x_{i2},\ldots,x_{in_{i}}}f(\underline{x}_{i})\,\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}_{i},q}(x_{i2}-x_{i1},\ldots,x_{in_{i}}-x_{i1})\,dx_{i1}

with
\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}_{i},q}
as defined in the previous section and
\underline{x}_{i}=(x_{i1},\ldots,x_{in_{i}})\in Q_{i}^{n_{i}}
.

Note that if
S\subseteq Q
then the density of configurations
\Delta
in
S
, of the form
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\times\ldots\times\Delta_{d}
with each
\Delta_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta_{i}^{0}
for some
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
is given by the expression

(7.2)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(1_{S}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}}):=\sum_{\underline{x}_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\underline{x}_{d}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}}}1_{S}(\underline{x}_{e})\ \sigma^{1}_{\lambda,q}(\underline{x}_{1})\ldots\sigma^{d}_{\lambda,q}(\underline{x}_{d}).

More generally, for any given
1\leq k\leq d
and a family of functions
f_{e}:Q_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
we define the multi-linear expression

(7.3)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}):=\sum_{\underline{x}_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\underline{x}_{d}}\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e})\ \sigma^{1}_{\lambda,q}(\underline{x}_{1})\ldots.\sigma^{d}_{\lambda,q}(\underline{x}_{d}).

as well as

(7.4)

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}):=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{t}\in Q}\ \mathcal{M}^{d}_{\underline{t}+Q(q,L)}\,(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})

where
Q(q,L)=Q_{1}(q,L)\times\cdots\times Q_{d}(q,L)
with each
Q_{i}(q,L)=(q\mathbb{Z}\cap[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}])^{2n_{i}+3}
and

(7.5)

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\widetilde{Q}}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}):=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}_{1}\in\widetilde{Q}_{1}^{n_{1}}}\cdots\ \mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}_{d}\in\widetilde{Q}_{d}^{n_{d}}}\,\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}f_{e}(\underline{x}_{e})

for any cube
\widetilde{Q}\subseteq Q
of the form
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{Q}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widetilde{Q}_{d}
with
\widetilde{Q}_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
.

We note that it is easy to show, as in the continuous, that
if
S\subseteq Q
with

|S|\geq\delta|Q|
for some
\delta>0
then

(7.6)

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q,Q}(1_{S};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}})\geq\delta^{n_{1}\cdots\,n_{d}}-O(\varepsilon)

for all scales
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
0<\lambda\ll\varepsilon\,l(Q)
. In light of this observation and the discussion preceding Proposition
6.1
the proof of Theorem
1.4
reduces, as it did in the continuous setting, to the following

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.
There exist positive integers
J_{d}=J_{d}(\varepsilon)
and
q_{d}(\varepsilon)
such that for any
(\varepsilon,q_{d}(\varepsilon)^{J_{d}})
-admissible sequence of scales
l(Q)\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{1}}
and

there is some
1\leq j<J_{d}
such that

(7.7)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q_{j},Q}(1_{S}\,;\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}})=\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q_{j},Q}(1_{S};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon),

for all

with

.

Quantitative Remark.
A careful analysis of our proof reveals that there exist choices of

and

which are less than
W_{d}(\log(C_{\Delta}\varepsilon^{-3}))
and
W_{d}(C_{\Delta}\varepsilon^{-13})
respectively where

is again the tower-exponential function defined by

and
W_{k+1}(m)=\exp(W_{k}(m))
for

.

The proof of Proposition
7.1
follows along the same lines as the analogous result in the continuous setting. As before we will compare the averages
\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})
to those of
\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})
, at certain scales
q
and
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with with
L_{j+1}\leq\lambda\leq L_{j}
, inductively for
1\leq k\leq d
. As the arguments closely follow those given in Section
5
we will be brief and emphasize mainly just the additional features.

7.1.
Reduction of Proposition
7.1
to a more general “local” counting lemma

For any given
1\leq k\leq d
and a family of functions
f_{e}:Q_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}

it is easy to see that for any
\varepsilon>0
, scale
L_{0}>0
dividing the side-length
l(Q)
, and
q_{0}|q
we have

(7.8)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}}\,\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

and

(7.9)

\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{L,Q}}\,\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q,Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

provided
0<\lambda\ll\varepsilon L_{0}
where
f_{e,\underline{t}}
denotes the restriction of a function
f_{e}
to the cube
Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})
.

Thus the proof of Proposition
7.1
reduces to showing that the expressions in (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) only differ by
O(\varepsilon)
for all scales
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
L_{j+1}\leq\lambda\leq L_{j}
, given an
(\varepsilon,q)
-admissible sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}
, for any collection of bounded functions
f_{e,\underline{t}}
,
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
,
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
. Indeed, our crucial result will be the following

Proposition 7.2

(Local Counting Lemma in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
q_{0},M\in\mathbb{N}
.

There exist positive integers
J_{k}=J_{k}(\varepsilon,M)
and
q_{k}(\varepsilon)
such that for any
(\varepsilon,q_{J_{d}})
-admissible sequence of scales
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J_{1}}

with
L_{0}
dividing
l(Q)
and
q_{j}:=q_{0}\,q_{k}(\varepsilon)^{j}
for
j\geq 1
, and collection of functions

f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m}:Q_{t_{\pi(e)}}(q_{0},L_{0}):\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
,
1\leq m\leq M
and
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}

there exists
1\leq j<J_{k}
and a set
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
of size
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}|
such that

(7.10)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q_{j},Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})=\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q_{j},Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})}(f_{e,\underline{t}};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

for all
\lambda\in q_{j}\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
L_{j+1}\leq\lambda\leq L_{j}
and
\underline{t}\notin T_{\varepsilon}

uniformly in
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
and
1\leq m\leq M
.

Note that if
k=d
,
L_{0}=l(Q)
,
q_{0}=M=1
, then
|\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}|=1
, and moreover if
f_{e,\underline{t}}=1_{S}

for all
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
for a set
S\subseteq Q
, then Proposition
7.2
reduces to precisely Proposition
7.1
. In fact, Proposition
7.2
is a parametric, multi-linear and simultaneous extension of Proposition
7.1
which we need in the induction step, i.e. when going from level
k-1
to level
k
.

7.2.
Proof of Proposition
7.2

We will prove Proposition
7.2
by induction on
1\leq k\leq d
.

For
k=1
this is basically Proposition
6.2
, exactly as it was in the base case of the proof of Proposition
5.3
.

For the induction step we will again need two main ingredients.
The first establishes that the our multi-linear forms
\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})
are controlled by a box-type norm attached to scales
q^{\prime}
and
L
.

Let
Q=Q_{1}\times\ldots\times Q_{d}
with
Q_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2n_{i}+3}
be cubes of equal side length
l(Q)
and
1\leq k\leq d
. For any scale
0<L\ll l(Q)
and function
f:Q_{e^{\prime}}\to[-1,1]
with
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}
we define its local box norm at scales
q^{\prime}
and
L
by

(7.11)

\|f\|_{\Box_{q^{\prime},L}(Q_{e^{\prime}})}^{2^{k}}:=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{s}\in Q_{e^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{\Box(Q_{\underline{s}}(q^{\prime},L))}^{2^{k}}

where

(7.12)

\|f\|_{\Box(\widetilde{Q})}^{2^{k}}:=\mathbb{E}_{x_{11},x_{12}\in\widetilde{Q}_{1}}\cdots\ \mathbb{E}_{x_{k1},x_{k2}\in\widetilde{Q}_{k}}\prod_{(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k})\in\{1,2\}^{k}}f(x_{1\ell_{1}},\dots,x_{k\ell_{k}})

for any cube
\widetilde{Q}
of the form
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{Q}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widetilde{Q}_{k}
.
We note that (
7.4
) and (
7.5
) are special cases of (
7.11
) and (
7.12
) with
k=d
,
\underline{n}=(2,\ldots,2)
, and
f_{e}=f
for all
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,d}^{\underline{n}}
.

Lemma 7.1

(A Generalized von-Neumann inequality on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
)
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
with
qq_{1}(\varepsilon)|q^{\prime}
, and
\lambda\in q\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}

with
\lambda\ll l(Q)
and
1\ll L\ll(\varepsilon^{2^{k}})^{10}\lambda
.
For any collection of functions
f_{e}:Q_{\pi(e)}\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
we have both

(7.13)

|\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})|\leq\min_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}\,\|f_{e}\|_{\Box_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime}}(Q_{\pi(e)})}+O(\varepsilon)

and

(7.14)

|\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q,Q}(f_{e};e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})|\leq\min_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}}\,\|f_{e}\|_{\Box_{q^{\prime},L^{\prime}}(Q_{\pi(e)})}.

The proof of inequalities (
7.13
) and (
7.14
) follow exactly as in the continuous case, see Lemma
5.1
, using Lemma
6.1
in place of Lemma
3.1
. We omit the details.

The crucial ingredient is again a parametric weak hypergraph regularity lemma, i.e. Lemma
5.2
adapted to the discrete settings. The proof is essentially the same as in the continuous case, with exception that the
\Box_{L_{j}}
-norms are replaced by
\Box_{q_{j},L_{j}}
-norms where
q_{j}=q_{0}q^{j}
is a given sequence of positive integers and
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{J}
is an
(\varepsilon,q_{J})
-admissible sequence of scales. To state it we say that a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
on a cube
Q
is of
scale

(q,L)
if it is refinement of the grid
\mathcal{G}_{q,L,Q}
, i.e. if its atoms partition each cube
Q_{\underline{t}}(q,L)
of the grid. We will always assume that
q|L
and
L|l(Q)
.
Recall also that we say the complexity of a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
is at most
m
, and write
\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B})\leq m
, if it is generated by
m
sets.

Lemma 7.2

(Parametric weak hypergraph regularity lemma for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
1\leq k\leq d
,
q_{0},q,L_{0},M\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
q_{j}:=q_{0}q^{j}
for
j\geq 1
.
There exists
\bar{J}_{k}=O(M\varepsilon^{-2^{k+3}})
such that for any
(\varepsilon^{2^{k}},q_{\bar{J}_{k}})
-admissible sequence
L_{0}\geq L_{1}\geq\cdots\geq L_{\bar{J}_{k}}
with the property that
L_{0}
divides
l(Q)
and collection of functions

f^{m}_{e,\underline{t}}:Q_{\underline{t}_{\pi(e)}}(q_{0},L_{0})\to[-1,1]
with
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
,
1\leq m\leq M
, and
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}

there is some
1\leq j<\bar{J}_{k}
and
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}
of scale
(q_{j},L_{j})
on
Q_{\underline{t}_{e^{\prime}}}(q_{0},L_{0})
for each
\underline{t}\in\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
and
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}
such that

(7.15)

\|f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m}-\mathbb{E}(f_{e,\underline{t}}^{m}|\mathcal{B}_{\pi(e),\underline{t}})\|_{\Box_{q_{j+1},L_{j+1}}(Q_{\underline{t}_{\pi(e)}}(L_{0}))}\leq\varepsilon

uniformly for all
t\notin T_{\varepsilon}
,
e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}}
, and
1\leq m\leq M
, where
T_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}
with
|T_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon|\Gamma_{q_{0},L_{0},Q}|
.

Moreover, the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}
have the additional local structure that the exist
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},\underline{s}}
on
Q_{\underline{s}_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}}}(q_{j},L_{j})
with
\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},\underline{s}})=O(j)
for each

,

, and
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial e^{\prime}
such that
if
\underline{s}\in Q_{\underline{t}}(q_{0},L_{0})
, then

(7.16)

\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\underline{t}}\bigr\rvert_{Q_{\underline{s}_{e^{\prime}}}(q_{j},L_{j})}=\bigvee_{\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\partial e^{\prime}}\mathcal{B}_{e^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},\underline{s}}.

The proof of Lemma
7.2
follows exactly as the corresponding proof of Lemma
5.2
in the continuous setting, so we will omit the details.
We will however provide some details of how one deduces Proposition
7.2
, from Lemmas

and

.

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition

(7.17)

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q_{l},Q_{\underline{s}}(q_{j}^{\prime},L^{\prime}_{j})}(f_{e,\underline{s}}^{m};\,e\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k}^{\underline{n}})=\sum_{\underline{r}}\alpha_{\underline{s},\underline{r},m}\ \mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q_{l},Q_{\underline{s}}(q_{j}^{\prime},L^{\prime}_{j})}\,(g_{\mathfrak{f},\underline{s}}^{\underline{r}};\,\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k-1}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon)

, where each

\mathcal{N}^{d}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},q_{l},Q_{\underline{s}}(q_{j}^{\prime},L^{\prime}_{j})}\,(g_{\mathfrak{f},\underline{s}}^{\underline{r}};\,\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k-1}^{\underline{n}})=\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\lambda,q_{l},Q_{\underline{s}}(q_{j}^{\prime},L^{\prime}_{j})}\,(g_{\mathfrak{f},\underline{s}}^{\underline{r}};\,\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{d,k-1}^{\underline{n}})+O(\varepsilon_{1})

S_{\varepsilon_{1}}\subseteq\Gamma_{q_{j}^{\prime},L^{\prime}_{j},Q}
is a set of size
|S_{\varepsilon_{1}}|\leq\varepsilon_{1}|\Gamma_{q_{j}^{\prime},L^{\prime}_{j},Q}|
.

The remainder of the proof follows as just as it did for Proposition
5.3
.
∎

8.
Appendix: A short direct proof of Part (i) of Theorem B
′

We conclude by providing a short direct proof of Part (i) of Theorem B
′
, namely the following

Theorem 8.1

(Magyar
[
17
]
)
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
and
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2k+3}
be a non-degenerate simplex of
k
points.

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2k+3}
has upper Banach density at least
\delta
,
then
there exists an integer
q_{0}=q_{0}(\delta)
and
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(S,\Delta)
such that
S
contains an isometric copy of
q_{0}\lambda\Delta
for all
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
we define

q_{\varepsilon}:=\lcm\{1\leq q\leq C\varepsilon^{-10}\}

with
C>0
a (sufficiently) large absolute constant.
Following
[
14
]
we further define
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
to be
\varepsilon
-uniformly distributed (modulo
q_{\varepsilon}
)

if its
relative
upper Banach density on any residue class modulo
q_{\varepsilon}
never exceeds
(1+\varepsilon^{2})
times its density on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, namely if

\delta^{*}(S\,|\,s+(q_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z})^{d})\leq(1+\varepsilon^{2})\,\delta^{*}(S)

for all
s\in\{1,\dots,q_{\varepsilon}\}^{d}
.
It turns out that this notion is closely related to the
U^{1}_{q,L}(Q)
-norm introduced in Section
6
. Recall that for any cube
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and function
f:Q\to[-1,1]
we define

(8.1)

\|f\|_{U^{1}_{q,L}(Q)}:=\Bigl(\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{t\in Q}|f*\chi_{q,L}(t)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

with
\chi_{q,L}
denoting the normalized characteristic function of the cubes
Q(q,L):=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{n}\cap(q\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.
Note that the
U^{1}_{q,L}(Q)
-norm measures the mean square oscillation of a function with respect to cubic grids of size
L
and gap
q
.

The following observation from
[
14
]
(specifically Lemmas 1 and 2) is key to our short proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. If
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be
\varepsilon
-uniformly distributed with
\delta:=\delta^{*}(S)>0
, then there exists an integer
L=L(S,\varepsilon)>0
and cubes
Q
of arbitrarily large side length
l(Q)
with
l(Q)\gg\varepsilon^{-4}L
such that

\|1_{S}-\delta 1_{Q}\|_{U^{1}_{q_{\varepsilon},L}(Q)}=O(\varepsilon).

Let
\Delta^{0}=\{v_{1}=0,v_{2},\dots,v_{k}\}
be a fixed non-degenerate simplex of
k
points in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
n=2k+3
and define
t_{ij}:=v_{i}\cdot v_{j}
for
2\leq i,j\leq k
. We now define a function which counts isometric copies of
\lambda\Delta^{0}
.

Recall, see
[
17
]
, that a simplex
\Delta=\{m_{1}=0,\dots,m_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is isometric to
\lambda\Delta^{0}
if and only if

m_{i}\cdot m_{j}=\lambda^{2}t_{ij}
for all
2\leq i,j\leq k
.
For any
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
we define
S_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}(m_{2},\dots,m_{k}):\mathbb{Z}^{n(k-1)}\to\{0,1\}
be the function whose value is 1 if
m_{i}\cdot m_{j}=\lambda^{2}t_{ij}

for all
2\leq i,j\leq k
and is equal to 0 otherwise. It is a well-known fact in number theory, see
[
11
]
or
[
17
]
, that for
n\geq 2k+1
we have that

\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}}S_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{k})=\rho(\Delta^{0})\,\lambda^{(n-k)(k-1)}(1+O(\lambda^{-\tau}))

for some absolute constant
\tau>0
and constant
\rho(\Delta^{0})>0
, the so-called singular series, which can be interpreted as the product of the densities of the solutions of the above system of equations among the
p
-adics and among the reals.
Thus if we define

\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}:=\rho(\Delta^{0})^{-1}\lambda^{-(n-k)(k-1)}S_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}

then
\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}
is normalized in so much that

\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}}\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}(m_{2},\ldots,m_{k})=1+O(\lambda^{-\tau})

for some absolute constant
\tau>0
.

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a fixed cube and let
l(Q)
denotes its side length.
For any family of functions

f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}:Q\to[-1,1]

and
0<\lambda\ll l(Q)
we define

(8.2)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k}):=\mathbb{E}_{m_{1}\in Q}\sum_{m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}}f_{1}(m_{1})\ldots f_{k}(m_{k})\,\sigma_{\lambda\Delta^{0}}(m_{2}-m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}-m_{1}).

It is clear that if
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{k}=1_{S}
restricted to
Q
, then the above expression is a normalized count of the isometric copies of
\lambda\Delta^{0}
in
S\cap Q
. Thus, Theorem
8.1
will follow from Lemma
8.1
and the following special case (with
q=1
) of Lemma
6.1
.

Lemma 8.2

(A Generalized von Neumann inequality)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.

If
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
\lambda\ll l(Q)
and
1\ll L\ll\varepsilon^{10}\lambda
then for any collection of functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}:Q\to[-1,1]
we have

(8.3)

|\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})|\leq\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\|f_{j}\|_{U^{1}_{q_{\varepsilon},L}(Q)}+O(\varepsilon).

This compares with the purely number theoretic fact that the number of simplices
\Delta=\{v_{1}=0,v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
isometric to
\lambda\Delta^{0}
is asymptotic to
\rho(\Delta^{0})\,\lambda^{(n-k)(k-1)}
. Thus, under the same conditions as in Lemma
8.2
, we have

(8.4)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(1_{Q},\dots,1_{Q})=1+O(\lambda^{-\tau})+O(\varepsilon)

provided one also has
\lambda\ll\varepsilon l(Q)
.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll\delta^{k}

and
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a set of upper Banach density
\delta
.

We assume first that
S
is
\varepsilon
-uniformly distributed.
Select a scale
L=L(\varepsilon,S)
and a sufficiently large cube
Q
so that the conclusion of Lemma
8.1
holds. For a given
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
with
\lambda\ll\varepsilon l(Q)
and
L\ll\varepsilon^{10}\lambda
write
1_{S}=\delta 1_{Q}+g
and substitute this decomposition into the multi-linear expression
\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})
. Then by Lemma
8.2
and (
8.3
)-(
8.4
), we have that

(8.5)

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda\Delta^{0},Q}(1_{S},\dots,1_{S})\geq\delta^{k}-O(\varepsilon)

and we can conclude that
S
must contain an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta^{0}
.

If
S
is not
\varepsilon
-uniformly distributed, then its upper Banach density is increased to at least
\delta_{1}:=(1+\varepsilon^{2})\delta
when restricted to a residue class
s+(q_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. Identify
s+(q_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
with
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and simultaneously the set
S|_{s+(q_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z})^{n}}
with a set
S_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, via the map
y\to q_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(y-s)
. Note that if
S_{1}
is
\varepsilon
-uniformly distributed then it contains an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta^{0}
for all sufficiently large
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
and hence
S
contains an isometric copy of
q_{\varepsilon}\lambda\Delta^{0}
.

Repeating the above procedure one arrives to a set
S_{j}=q_{\varepsilon}^{-j}(S-s_{j})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some
s_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in

\lambda\Delta^{0}
for all sufficiently large
\lambda\in\sqrt{\mathbb{N}}
. ∎

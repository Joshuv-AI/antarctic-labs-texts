Fermiophobic light Higgs boson in the type-I two-Higgs-doublet model Preprint: APCTP Pre2022–016
Jinheung Kim, Soojin Lee, Prasenjit Sanyal, Jeonghyeon Song

Abstract
The null results in the new physics searches at the LHC do not exclude an intermediate-mass new particle if it is fermiophobic. Type-I in the two-Higgs-doublet model accommodates a fermiophobic light Higgs boson h f h_{\rm f} if α = π / 2 \alpha=\pi/2 . The heavier CP -even Higgs boson explains the observed Higgs boson at a mass of 125 GeV. We first obtain the still-valid parameter space satisfying the theoretical requirements, flavor-changing neutral currents in B B physics, the cutoff scale above 1 TeV, Higgs precision data, and the direct collider search bounds at high energy colliders. We also study the high energy scale behavior via the analysis of the renormalization group equations. An important result is that the fermiophobic type-I can maintain the stability of the scalar potential all the way up to the Planck scale if m h f m_{h_{\rm f}} is larger than half the observed Higgs boson mass. Since the parameter space is severely curtailed, especially for the high cutoff scale, the signal rates in the multi-photon states of new Higgs bosons at the LHC are well predicted. We suggest the processes of 4 ​ γ + V ​ V ′ 4\gamma+VV^{\prime} ( V ( ′ ) = Z , W ± V^{(\prime)}=Z,W^{\pm} ) as the golden discovery channels for the model since they enjoy an almost background-free environment and substantial selection efficiencies for four photons.

Introduction

The standard model (SM) of particle physics has achieved unprecedented success
in explaining almost all the experimental data at high energy colliders.
Nevertheless, we firmly believe that there is new physics beyond the SM (BSM)
because the SM does not have the answers for the naturalness problem, the fermion mass hierarchy,
the origin of
CP
violation in the quark sector, the baryogenesis, the non-zero neutrino masses,
and the identity of dark matter.
In the hope that a new physics signal is just around the corner,
the ATLAS and CMS collaborations have tried hard to find a new particle,
but not succeeded so far.

The absence of a new signal in the current data set at the LHC
is usually attributed to very heavy or very light masses of new particles.
But there exists an intriguing alternative, a fermiophobic new particle.
If a new particle does not couple to the SM fermions,
its production via quark-antiquark annihilation is highly suppressed.
If the new particle is a color singlet as suggested in many BSM models,
the gluon fusion production through quark loops is also forbidden.
The infeasibility of the direct production of a fermiophobic particle naturally explains the absence of new signals
at the LHC.

If the model remains theoretically valid up to very high energy scale like the Planck scale,
it will become a better candidate for the UV theory.
The RGE analysis requires one specific BSM model.

IV
LHC phenomenology

Now that the finally allowed parameter space is significantly limited,
the observation of the fermiophobic type-I at the LHC
is more predictive than ever.
We first discuss the decays of
h_{\rm f}
,
which are only into electroweak bosons:
the loop-induced decay of
h_{\rm f}\to gg
also vanishes because it occurs through quarks.
The off-shell decays of
h_{\rm f}
into
VV^{*}

is feasible due to the deviation from the Higgs alignment, especially in the case of
m_{h_{\rm f}}=96{\;{\rm GeV}}
.
The radiative decay of
h_{\rm f}
into
{\gamma\gamma}
gets the contribution
from
W^{\pm}
and
H^{\pm}
in the loop.
The coupling for the
h_{\rm f}
-
H^{+}
-
H^{-}
vertex is

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{hH^{+}H^{-}}=\frac{1}{v^{2}}\left[(2M_{H^{\pm}}^{2}-m_{h_{\rm f}}^{2})c_{\beta}+\frac{m_{h_{\rm f}}^{2}-M^{2}}{c_{\beta}}\right]\simeq\frac{t_{\beta}}{v^{2}}(m_{h_{\rm f}}^{2}-M^{2}),

(14)

where
M^{2}=m_{12}^{2}/(s_{\beta}c_{\beta})
and

\hat{\lambda}_{hH^{+}H^{-}}
is defined by
\mathscr{L}_{\rm tri}\supset v\hat{\lambda}_{hH^{+}H^{-}}h_{\rm f}H^{+}H^{-}
.
Since the finally allowed parameter points prefer large
t_{\beta}
and intermediate
M_{H^{\pm}}
,
the decay mode of
h_{\rm f}\to{\gamma\gamma}
will be important.

The branching ratios of the BSM Higgs bosons crucially depend on the mass of
h_{\rm f}
.
Let us first consider the case of
m_{h_{\rm f}}=30{\;{\rm GeV}}
.
Very light
h_{\rm f}
exclusively decays into
{\gamma\gamma}
with
\mathcal{B}(h_{\rm f}\to{\gamma\gamma})\simeq 100\%
.
The other BSM Higgs bosons,
A
and
H^{\pm}
,
also have a single dominant decay mode,

A\to Zh_{\rm f}
and
H^{\pm}\to W^{\pm}h_{\rm f}
, which have almost 100% branching ratios.
The main reason is the kinematic gain from the light
m_{h_{\rm f}}
and the heavy enough
M_{A/H^{\pm}}

as shown in Fig.
1
.

m_{h_{\rm f}}=96{\;{\rm GeV}}

The leading decay mode of
h_{\rm f}
is into
WW^{*}
with the branching ratio of about 70%.
We ascribe it to the sizable deviation from the Higgs alignment and the tree-level decay.
The diphoton mode is next-to-leading with
\mathcal{B}(h_{\rm f}\to{\gamma\gamma})\lesssim 28\%
.
The third dominant decay mode is
h_{\rm f}\to ZZ^{*}
with the branching ratio of about 10%.
The pseudoscalar
A
has the most diverse decay modes among the BSM Higgs bosons.
For
M_{A}\lesssim 120{\;{\rm GeV}}
,

A\to{b\bar{b}}
is the leading decay mode, followed by
A\to gg
and
A\to{\tau^{+}\tau^{-}}
.
For
M_{A}\gtrsim 150{\;{\rm GeV}}
,
the bosonic mode of
A\to Zh_{\rm f}
becomes dominant.
Above the kinematic threshold,
the decays of
A\to ZH
and
A\to{t\bar{t}}
can be substantial
with a few percent branching ratios.
The charged Higgs boson mainly decays into
W^{\pm}h_{\rm f}

Cheung:2022ndq

except for small mass window of
M_{H^{\pm}}\in[80,\,110]{\;{\rm GeV}}
where
H^{\pm}\to\tau\nu
is dominant.
Above the kinematic threshold,
the branching ratios of
H^{\pm}\to tb
and
H^{\pm}\to W^{\pm}H
can be of the order of 1%.

Brief comments on the possibility of
h_{\rm f}
as a long-lived particle are in order here.
If the total decay width of
h_{\rm f}

is small enough to make
h_{\rm f}
long-lived,
we see a signal of a photon pair from the secondary vertex displaced from the primary vertex.
If a particle has
\Gamma_{\rm tot}=10^{-11}{\;{\rm GeV}}
, for example,
the decay length
d\simeq\beta\gamma\times 10^{-6}{\rm m}

where
\gamma=E/m
and
\beta=v/c
.
Since photons do not leave any track in the inner tracker,
we have to deduce the secondary vertex from the photon tracks in the ECAL.
In Ref.
Banerjee:2019ktv
,
it was shown that we need the decay length larger than 0.1 cm to probe the signal,
which require
\Gamma_{\rm tot}<10^{-13}{\;{\rm GeV}}
even with
\gamma=10
.
We find that the minimum of
\Gamma_{\rm tot}^{h_{\rm f}}
is
1.3\times 10^{-12}{\;{\rm GeV}}
for
m_{h_{\rm f}}=20{\;{\rm GeV}}
,

3.9\times 10^{-12}{\;{\rm GeV}}
for
m_{h_{\rm f}}=30{\;{\rm GeV}}
,
and

1.9\times 10^{-9}{\;{\rm GeV}}
for
m_{h_{\rm f}}=96{\;{\rm GeV}}
.
It is difficult to see the displaced diphoton signals of a long-lived
h_{\rm f}
at the LHC in the fermiophobic type-I.

Considering the branching ratios of the BSM Higgs bosons,
we study the following multi-photon states at the LHC:

\displaystyle 4\gamma+Z:

\displaystyle pp\to A(\to Zh_{\rm f})h_{\rm f}\to 4\gamma+Z,

(15)

\displaystyle pp\to H(\to h_{\rm f}h_{\rm f})Z\to 4\gamma+Z,

\displaystyle 4\gamma+W^{\pm}:

\displaystyle pp\to H^{\pm}(\to W^{\pm}h_{\rm f})h_{\rm f}\to 4\gamma+W^{\pm},

\displaystyle pp\to H(\to h_{\rm f}h_{\rm f})W^{\pm}\to 4\gamma+W^{\pm},

\displaystyle 4\gamma+W^{\pm}Z:

\displaystyle pp\to H^{\pm}(\to W^{\pm}h_{\rm f})A(\to Zh_{\rm f})\to 4\gamma+W^{\pm}Z,

(16)

\displaystyle 4\gamma+W^{+}W^{-}:

\displaystyle pp\to H^{+}(\to W^{+}h_{\rm f})H^{-}(\to W^{-}h_{\rm f})\to 4\gamma+W^{+}W^{-}.

Note that the Higgs alignment favors the productions of
{q\bar{q}}\to Z^{*}\to HA
and

q\bar{q}^{\prime}\to W^{*}\to H^{\pm}A
.

The first noteworthy result in Fig.

that the signal rate of
m_{h_{\rm f}}=30{\;{\rm GeV}}

m_{h_{\rm f}}=96{\;{\rm GeV}}

This feature is attributed primarily to the kinematic advantage of very light
m_{h_{\rm f}}
.
Unexpected is the result that the signal rate of
4\gamma+VV^{\prime}
is almost compatible with
that of
4\gamma+V

4\gamma+VV^{\prime}
has one more particle in the final state.
It is because both
4\gamma+V

4\gamma+VV^{\prime}
are basically
2\to 2
processes with large branching ratios:
see Eqs. (
15

and (

).
Since
4\gamma+VV^{\prime}
has one more tagging particle, which is hard for the SM backgrounds to mimic,
we suggest
4\gamma+VV^{\prime}
as the golden discovery channel for the light fermiophobic Higgs boson.

Now, let us discuss the correlation of the signal rate to cutoff scale.
For the case of
m_{h_{\rm f}}=30{\;{\rm GeV}}
, which has a low cutoff scale below
7{\;{\rm TeV}}
,
there is no point in discussing the correlation.
In the case of
m_{h_{\rm f}}=96{\;{\rm GeV}}
, however,
the cutoff scale is widely distributed, from
1{\;{\rm TeV}}
to the Planck scale.
So, the correlation has a profound implication on the LHC phenomenology.
The color code of
\Lambda_{\rm c}
in Fig.
4
clearly shows that
the maximum signal rate for all the multi-photon final states
happens when the model has
\Lambda_{\rm c}\sim 10^{18}{\;{\rm GeV}}
.

A critical question is whether we can discover the fermiophobic
h_{\rm f}

via the multi-photon signals at the HL-LHC.
We find that the SM backgrounds are almost negligible.
Let us consider the process of
4\gamma+W^{\pm}
as an example.
The irreducible background,
demanding four photons and one lepton with
p_{T}^{\gamma}>10{\;{\rm GeV}}
,

p_{T}^{\ell}>20{\;{\rm GeV}}
,
|\eta^{\gamma,\ell}|<2.5
, and
the isolation cuts of
\Delta R>0.4
where
\Delta R=\sqrt{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}

has the cross section about
1{\;{\rm ab}}

Arhrib:2017wmo
.
If we require two gauge bosons associated with four photons,
the irreducible backgrounds are totally negligible.
The reducible backgrounds are from QCD jets accompanying one or two gauge bosons,
with the QCD jet misidentified as a photon.
But the mistagging probability is very small as
P_{j\to\gamma}\simeq 10^{-3}

Wang:2021pxc
.
The final states with four photons require the multiplication of
P_{j\to\gamma}
four times,
which makes the reducible backgrounds also ignorable.
Therefore, we conclude that the
4\gamma+VV^{\prime}
states have an almost background-free environment.

The final question is about the selection efficiencies of
4\gamma+VV^{\prime}

No matter how negligible the backgrounds are,
small cross sections raise concerns about whether we can observe enough signal events
especially when the selection efficiencies are very small.
Full state-of-the-art simulations, customized for

4\gamma+W^{\pm}Z

V
Conclusions

The null results in the BSM searches at the LHC can coexist with an intermediate-mass new particle
if the new particle is fermiophobic.
Type-I in the 2HDM with the condition of
\alpha=\pi/2
provides a fermiophobic Higgs boson,
a lighter
CP
-even Higgs boson.
The heavier
CP
-even Higgs boson describes the observed SM-like Higgs boson at a mass of
125{\;{\rm GeV}}
.
In this letter, we have studied the validity of the fermiophobic type-I
not only at the electroweak scale but also at higher energy scale.

For the comprehensive study,
we have first obtained the still-valid parameter space satisfying the theoretical requirements
(bounded-from-below scalar potential, unitarity, perturbativity, vacuum stability),
flavor-changing neutral currents in
B
physics, the cutoff scale above
1{\;{\rm TeV}}
, Higgs precision data,
and direct collider search bounds from the LEP, Tevatron, and LHC.
For every viable parameter point, the cutoff scale
\Lambda_{\rm c}
has been calculated
through the RGE analysis.
The most important result is that the fermiophobic type-I
can maintain the theoretical validity all the way up to the Planck scale when the mass
of the fermiophobic Higgs boson is larger than about
62.5{\;{\rm GeV}}
.
Even the mild condition of
\Lambda_{\rm c}>1{\;{\rm TeV}}
severely restricts the parameter space,
which predicts definite signal rates of the BSM Higgs bosons in the multi-photon states at the LHC.
Based on the studies of the decay rates and the production cross sections of the BSM Higgs bosons,
we have suggested the
4\gamma+VV^{\prime}
processes as the golden discovery channels for the model
since they enjoy almost background-free environment and substantial selection efficiencies.

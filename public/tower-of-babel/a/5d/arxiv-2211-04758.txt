1.1.
Universality in sparse graphs

Another natural graph class is
\mathcal{H}(n,\Delta)
, the class of all
n
-vertex graphs with maximum degree at most
\Delta
.
For dense random graphs
G(n,p)

[
31

shows that

is universal for
\mathcal{H}(n,\Delta)

\Delta
is a constant.
Unlike the dense case, the study of universality in sparse random graphs is proven to be a challenging task.
Following some initial progress by Krivelevich
[
32
]
and Kim
[
29
]
, Johansson, Kahn and Vu
[
26

showed (among other things) that the threshold for the existence of a

n^{-\frac{2}{\Delta+1}}(\log n)^{1/\binom{\Delta+1}{2}}

For universality, Dellamonica, Kohayakawa, Rödl and Ruciński
[
18
]
(for
\Delta\geq 3

[
30
]
(for
\Delta=2
) showed that

are
\mathcal{H}(n,\Delta)
-universal for
p=\tilde{\Omega}(n^{-1/\Delta})
.
The case
\Delta\geq 3
has been subsequently improved by Ferber and Nenadov
[
20
]
, and the case
\Delta=2
, namely, for the 2-universality, the optimal threshold
p=C\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right)^{1/3}
was established in an excellent work of Ferber, Kronenberg and Luh
[
19
]
.

Now let us switch to another fruitful area, the study of sparse pseudorandom graphs.
One prominent class of such graphs are expander graphs.
Given a graph
G

n

An

-

d
-regular graph on
n

The well-known Expander Mixing Lemma gives a good estimate on the edge distribution of an

-graph, which suggests that if

is much smaller than the degree

has strong expansion properties. Therefore, many typical embedding problems asks for the best possible condition on
spectral gap

\frac{\lambda}{d}
to ensure certain structures.
For instance, a celebrated conjecture of Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
34
]
states that there exists
c>0
such that for large enough

, every
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
\frac{\lambda}{d}<c
is Hamiltonian.
Similar to sparse random graphs, embedding (and universality) problems appear to be very hard in
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs.
The sparse blow-up lemma developed in
[
1
]
shows that
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs with
\lambda=o((d/n)^{\max\{4,(3\Delta+1)/2\}}n)
are
\mathcal{H}(n,\Delta)
-universal.

In this paper, we shall focus on the following problem on tree-universality proposed by Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
7

in 2007.

Question 1.2

[

Is it true that for every
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}

Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov

obtained the following result on embedding almost spanning trees (subsequent improvement on the spectral gap was obtained by Balogh,

Theorem 1.3
.

[

For all

Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

For sufficiently large integer
n
and all
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
, every

(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with

Note that when

We also remark that our proof also works under a slightly more general context, namely, the
(p,\beta)
-bijumbled graphs with a minimum degree condition.
Indeed, a graph

(p,\beta)
-bijumbled if for every vertex sets

It is well-known that

(d/n,\lambda)
-bijumbled.

Theorem 1.5

For sufficiently large

\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
, every

-vertex

(p,\beta)
-bijumbled with

{\beta}\leq\frac{pn}{4\Delta^{5\sqrt{\log n}}}

Note that the minimum degree condition we enforce in Theorem

-regular (namely,

1.2.
Universality for
(n,d)
-expanders

Our next result is on the universality problem in a broader class of graphs, namely, the
(n,d)
-expander graphs, defined as follows.
The notion of

-expander is first formulated by Johannsen, Krivelevich and Samotij, which is an adaptation of the expansion properties investigated by Hefetz, Krivelevich and Szabó

for the Hamiltonicity of highly connected graphs.

Definition 1.6
.

[
25
]

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

, an
n
-vertex graph

is an
(n,d)
-
expander

if

satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

|N_{G}(X)|\geq d|X|
for all
X\subseteq V(G)
with
1\leq|X|<\frac{n}{2d}

(ii)

e_{G}(X,Y)>0
for all disjoint
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|=|Y|\geq\frac{n}{2d}
.

Montgomery
[
37
]
proposed a slightly stronger version of expansion property as follows.

Definition 1.7
.

For a graph
G
and a set
W\subseteq V(G)
, we say
G

d
-expands
into
W
if

(i)

|N_{G}(X,W)|\geq d|X|
for all
X\subseteq V(G)
with
1\leq|X|<\lceil\frac{|W|}{2d}\rceil
, and,

(ii)

e_{G}(X,Y)>0
for all disjoint
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|=|Y|=\lceil\frac{|W|}{2d}\rceil
.

Clearly, if
G

d
-expands into
W

G[W]
itself is a
(|W|,d)
-expander.
More often, we call a graph
G

m
-
joined
if there is an edge in

between every two disjoint sets of at least
m

Johanssen, Krivelevich and Samotij
[
25
]
were the first to show that expander graphs are tree-universal, and thus provide explicit constructions of sparse tree-universal graphs, e.g. by the celebrated construction of Ramanujan graphs by Lubotzky-Phillips-Sarnak
[

.
Since expanders are locally sparse (e.g. with small clique number), this complements previous constructions of Bhatt, Chung, Leighton and Rosenberg
[

that are locally dense, namely, their constructions contain a large number of cliques of size

Now we present the results of Johanssen, Krivelevich and Samotij
[

]
on spanning tree universality.

Theorem 1.8
.

[

There exists an absolute constant

such that the

with
\Delta\leq cn^{1/3}

(n,7\Delta n^{2/3})
-expander is

-universal.

They also proved the following result on the containment of almost spanning trees.

Theorem 1.9
.

[

Let

and

(n,d)
-expander is

There exist absolute constants

1.3.2.
Maker-Breaker Game

An
(a:b)
Maker-Breaker game is played on a finite hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{F})
between two players,
Maker and Breaker. The game is played in turns, starting with Maker’s turn. In each of
their turns, Maker claims
a
and Breaker claims
b
previously unclaimed vertices, respectively. Maker’s objective is to claim all vertices of a hyperedge by the end of the game. In this case, Maker wins the game. Breaker’s
objective is to claim at least one vertex in each hyperedge by the end of the game. In this
case, Breaker wins the game. The game ends when all vertices have been claimed, by which
time either Maker or Breaker have won. The numbers
a
and
b
are called the
biases
of Maker and Breaker, respectively. We say that an
(a:b)
Maker-Breaker game is Maker’s win if Maker has a strategy that allows him to win the game regardless of Breaker’s strategy, otherwise the game is Breaker’s win. For a more detailed discussion, we refer the reader to
[
10
]
.

Johanssen, Krivelevich and Samotij
[
25
]
formulated a Maker-Breaker Expander Game.

Definition 1.15
.

[
25
, Maker-Breaker Expander Game]

For
n,\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
, the
Maker-Breaker
(n,d)
-expander game
on a graph
G
is the Maker-Breaker game on the hypergraph
H=(E(G),\mathcal{F})
, where
\mathcal{F}
consists of all edge sets
F\subseteq E(G)
such that the subgraph
(V(G),F)
is an
(n,d)
-expander.

They showed that if the
(1:b)
Maker-Breaker expander game is played on an
(n,15bd\log n)
-expander, then Maker can always secure all edges of an
(n,d)
-expander.

Theorem 1.16
.

[
25
]

There exists an absolute constant
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following statement
holds. Let
n,b\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
n\geq n_{0}
and
d\geq 3
. Then the
(1:b)
Maker-Breaker

(n,d)
-expander game is Maker’s win on every
(n,15bd\log n)
-expander.

Similarly they formulated a
Maker-Breaker Tree-Universality Game
in which Maker tries to
claim a subset of the edges

such that the subgraph
(V(G),F)
is
\mathcal{T}(n,\Delta)
-universal. Using the tree-universality result in Theorem

.

[

There exists an absolute constant

such that the following statement

(1:b)

Combining Theorems

Corollary 1.18

There exists an absolute constant

1.4.
Technical Contribution

Our approach builds on previously known ones but also introduces new twists.
A crucial new idea is the
reconstruction of tree array
(see Definition
3.7
and Lemma
3.8
), which allows us to reconstruct local parts of the almost spanning tree so that we can embed a specific set of vertices of the tree to a (small) vertex set with good expansion property.
In particular, this is also the bottle-neck of the current proof, that is, the expansion property required in our proof comes from the need of reconstruction of paths of length
\sqrt{\log n}
, which can be understood as our requirement on the radius of the host graph.
Although the idea is natural, we are not aware of similar treatments in the literature.
Finally, we expect that the techniques developed here will find other applications in related problems, in particular, in embedding (connected) spanning substructures.

1.5.
Basic notation

Given a graph
G
and two vertex sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, we define
\Gamma_{G}(A):=\bigcup_{v\in A}N_{G}(v)
and
N_{G}(A):=\Gamma(A)\setminus A
. We write
N_{G}(A,B)=N_{G}(A)\cap B
and for a vertex
v
, let
d_{G}(v,B)=|N_{G}(v)\cap B|
. Throughout the paper, we will often omit the subscript term
G
to ease the notation, unless otherwise stated.

For
d,h\in\mathbb{N}
, a
d
-ary
tree
of height

h
is a rooted tree in which every non-leaf vertex has
d
children and every leaf is of distance exactly
h
from the root.
Given a tree
T
, let
T^{\prime}
be a subtree obtained by removing all leaves in
T
.
A
pendant star
in
T
is a maximal star centered at a leaf vertex of
T^{\prime}
, and the unique neighbour of the center in
T^{\prime}
is the
root
of the pendant star (see Figure
1
).
A path
P
in
T
is a
bare
path if all internal vertices of
P
have degree exactly two in
T
.
A
caterpillar
in
T
consists of a bare path in
T^{\prime}
as the
central
path
and a (possibly empty) set of leaves in
T
attached to internal vertices of the central path, where
branching
vertices are the internal vertices attached with at least one leaf and we call each such leaf a

leg
. Moreover, the
length
and
ends
of a caterpillar refer to the length and ends of the corresponding central path, respectively. Given a path
P
and two distinct vertices
a,b\in V(P)
, denote by
P(a,b)
the subpath that connects
a
and
b
in
P
.

Given two graphs
H,G
, an embedding of
H
in
G
is an injective mapping
\phi:V(H)\rightarrow V(G)
such that
\{\phi(u),\phi(v)\}\in E(G)
for every edge

. We often use
\phi|_{H}
to denote an embedding of
H
(in

).

2.
Outline of the proofs

In this section we give an outline of our proofs, and compare with previous approaches.
Firstly, what is common for previous proofs
[
25
,
33
,
38
]
is to distinguish the trees according to the number of leaves.
The following key observation is due to Krivelevich
[
33
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
33
]

For any integers
n,k>2
, an
n
-vertex tree either has at least
\frac{n}{4k}

leaves or a collection of at least
\frac{n}{4k}
vertex disjoint bare paths each of length
k
.

Based on this fact, previous approaches distinguish the trees in
\mathcal{T}(n,\Delta)
into two classes.
If a tree
T
has many leaves, then we remove these leaves and obtain a subtree
T^{\prime}
.
The subtree
T^{\prime}
can be embedded in the host graph by almost-spanning tree embedding results (e.g. Theorem
1.9
), and then the leaves of
T
can be further embedded to the remaining vertices by matching-based arguments.

We first outline the proof of Theorem
1.8
in
[
25
]
.
The proof of Theorem
1.8
uses another version of Lemma
2.1
, that is,
T
either has many leaves, or has
one
long bare path.
The authors of

Montgomery’s proof of Theorem

In fact, for random graphs, in

This is easily done by the

However, the case when

Montgomery developed a novel method,
absorption using bipartite template

This new method has found many applications in embedding problems in sparse graphs and hypergraphs.

Now we discuss briefly on our proof ideas.
In fact, for the many-bare-path case we could follow the embedding strategy (and some of the results) of Montgomery.
However, since we do not work with random graphs, the many-leave case is no longer free (modulo the almost spanning tree embedding results), and in fact becomes challenging.
Indeed, using a result of
[
25
]
, one can partition the graph

into multiple blocks while reserving the expansion property in each block (i.e.,

Thus, letting

be the leaves of

be the set of the parents of

by an embedding

in a (huge) block of

is a block

with good expansion property (namely,

).
However, this “one-sided” expansion is too weak for us to establish a star-matching result on the bipartite graph

.
The hope is to strengthen the embedding

so that

also enjoys a good expansion property and thus the matching-type result can be applied to

.
To achieve this, it is natural to check

similarly as in

and split into further cases.

Corollary 2.2

The novel part of our approach lies in the construction of the almost spanning tree, so that roughly speaking (a large set of) the leaves of

3.2.
Building star-matchings

We will use two results on matchings and here we first give a notion of generalized matching as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a bipartite graph
G=(A,B,E)
and an function

with
\sum_{u\in A}f(u)=|B|
, an

into

has

More often we call such an

Lemma 3.3
.

[
25

Let

and

be a graph. Suppose that two disjoint

(1)

(2)

for all

with
|X|=|Y|=m
.

(3)

|N_{G}(w)\cap A|\geq m
for all

Then, for every

with
\sum_{u\in A}f(u)=|B|
, there exists an
f
-matching from
A
into
B
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
G
be a graph. Suppose that two disjoint
sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
satisfy that

(1)

|N_{G}(X)\cap B|\geq d|X|,N_{G}(Y)\cap A|\geq d|Y|
for all
X\subseteq A,Y\subseteq B
with
1\leq|X|,|Y|\leq m
;

(2)

e_{G}(X,Y)>0
for all
X\subseteq A,Y\subseteq B
with
|X|=|Y|=m

Then, for every

with
\sum_{u\in A}f(u)=|B|

f
-matching from

into

.

Proof.

To build a desired star-matching, we shall show a generalized Hall’s condition (see, e.g., Bollobás
[
12
]
)), that is,

(3.1)

\displaystyle|N(X)\cap B|\geq\sum_{v\in X}f(v)\penalty\ \text{for every}\penalty\ X\subseteq A.

From the assumption that every set
X
of at most
m
vertices in
A
has at least
d|X|
neighbors in
B
, we know that (
3.1
) holds when
|X|\leq m
. It remains to consider the following two cases depending on the size of
|X|
:

•

m<|X|\leq|A|-m
: By the assumption
(2)
and
|B|=\sum_{v\in A}f(v)
, we have

|N(X)\cap B|>|B|-m\geq\sum_{v\in A}f(v)-\sum_{v\in A\setminus X}f(v)=\sum_{v\in X}f(v),

where the second inequality follows as
m\leq|A|-|X|\leq\sum_{v\in A\setminus X}f(v)
.

•

|A|-m<|X|\leq|A|

. We may assume that

for otherwise we are done. As there is no edge between

and

, we obtain that
|T|<m
and

. Then it follows by assumption that
d|T|\leq|N(T)\cap A|\leq|A\setminus X|\leq\sum_{v\in A\setminus X}f(v)

|N(X)\cap B|=|B|-|T|=\sum_{v\in X}f(v)+\sum_{v\in A\setminus X}f(v)-|T|>\sum_{v\in X}f(v).

In all cases, condition (

) holds and this yields a desired star-matching covering

. ∎

|Y|\geq\frac{3}{4}n-\frac{2\beta}{p}\sqrt{\frac{n}{|X|}}\geq\frac{3}{4}n-\frac{2\beta}{p}\sqrt{\frac{p^{2}n^{2}}{100\beta^{2}}}>\frac{n}{2}\geq d_{1}|X|.

If
|X|<100\frac{\beta^{2}}{p^{2}n}
and assume for contradiction that
|Y|<d_{1}|X|
, then
\delta|X|\leq e(X,X\cup Y)\leq p|X||X\cup Y|+\beta\sqrt{|X||X\cup Y|}\leq p|X|^{2}(d_{1}+1)+\beta|X|\sqrt{d_{1}+1}
. Recall that
d_{1}=\frac{pn}{4\beta}\geq 100
and
|X|<100\frac{\beta^{2}}{p^{2}n}
. It follows that

\delta\leq p(d_{1}+1)|X|+\beta\sqrt{d_{1}+1}<50\beta+\sqrt{\frac{p\beta n}{2}}\leq 50\sqrt{\frac{p\beta n}{400}}+\sqrt{\frac{p\beta n}{2}}<4\sqrt{p\beta n},

a contradiction.

The case when
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph is much easier. First reset
d_{1}=\frac{d}{2\lambda}>4
and by Expander Mixing Lemma,
e(X,Y)\geq\frac{d}{n}|X||Y|-\lambda\sqrt{|X||Y|(1-\frac{|X|}{n})(1-\frac{|Y|}{n})}>\frac{d}{n}(\frac{\lambda n}{d})^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}n}{d}=0
for every two disjoint vertex sets
X,Y
size at least
\frac{n}{2d_{1}}=\frac{\lambda n}{d}
. Then we will show that for every
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|<\frac{\lambda n}{d}
,
N(X)\geq d_{1}|X|
. Suppose this is invalid for some
X\subseteq V(G)
, i.e,
N(X)<d_{1}|X|
. Similarly set
Y=N(X)
and thus

d|X|\leq e(X,X\cup Y)<\frac{d}{n}|X||X\cup Y|+\lambda\sqrt{|X||X\cup Y|}\leq\frac{d}{n}(d_{1}+1)|X|^{2}+\lambda|X|\sqrt{d_{1}+1}.

Then it implies that
d<\frac{d}{n}(d_{1}+1)|X|+\lambda\sqrt{d_{1}+1}\leq\lambda(\frac{d}{2\lambda}+1)+\lambda\sqrt{\frac{d}{2\lambda}+1}
, i.e,
\frac{d}{2\lambda}<\sqrt{\frac{d}{2\lambda}+1}+1
, a contradiction to the fact that
\lambda<\frac{d}{8}
.
∎

On product decomposition
Ming-Deh A. Huang (USC, mdhuang@usc.edu)

Abstract
. Given a finite set W W in k ¯ n \bar{k}^{n} where k ¯ \bar{k} is the algebraic closure of a field k k one would like to determine if W W can be decomposed as ∏ i = 1 n V i \prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i} where V i ⊂ k ¯ V_{i}\subset\bar{k} under a linear transformation, that is, W → λ ∏ i = 1 n V i W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i} where λ ∈ G ​ l n ​ ( k ¯ ) \lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k}) . We assume that W W is presented as W = Z ⁡ ( ℱ ) W=Z(\mathcal{F}) , the zero set of a polynomial system ℱ \mathcal{F} in n n variables over k k . We study algebraic characterization of such product decomposition. For decomposition into component sets of the same cardinality we obtain a stronger characterization and show that the decomposition in this case is essentially unique (up to permutation and scalar multiplication of coordinates). We investigate computational problems that arise from the decomposition problem.

1.
Introduction

Suppose
W\subset\bar{k}^{n}
is a finite set where
k
is a field and
\bar{k}
is the algebraic closure of
k
.
We say that
W
has a product decomposition if

W=\lambda(\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i})
where
V_{i}\subset\bar{k}
is finite and
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
.

We study algebraic characterizations of product decomposition. In §
2
we begin with a simple characterization in Theorem
2.1
. We then provide a stronger characterization in Theorem
2.2
for the case where
W\subset\bar{k}^{n}
has product decomposition

W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
with
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
,
V_{i}\subset\bar{k}
and
|V_{i}|=d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Moreover in Theorem
2.3
we show that in this case product decomposition is unique up to permutation and scalar multiplication of coordinates, provided
d
is not divisible by the characteristic of
k
.

Suppose

W=Z({\mathcal{G}})
, the zero set of a set
{\mathcal{G}}
consisting of
n
polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
of degree bounded by
d
in
\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then by Theorem
2.2

Z({\mathcal{G}})
has product decomposition into component sets of the same cardinality if and only if
there exists
\mu\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
such that letting
{\mathcal{L}}
be the linear vector space spanned by
{\mathcal{G}}
, then the isomorphic linear vector space
\mu^{*}({\mathcal{L}})=\{h\circ\mu:h\in{\mathcal{L}}\}
has a basis

f_{i}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, where each
f_{i}(x_{i})
is of degree
d
without multiple roots.
Therefore such a decomposition exists for
W
if and only if

(1.1)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}\\
.\\
.\\
g_{n}\end{array}\right)=\rho\circ\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}\\
.\\
.\\
f_{n}\end{array}\right)\circ\lambda

for some
f_{i}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, of degree
d
without multiple roots, and
\lambda,\rho\in Gl_{n}(k)
.

Treating
f_{i}
,
\lambda,\rho
as unknown, we arrive at a problem
of solving a system of polynomials of degree
d
in
O(n^{2})
unknown and
O(n^{d})
conditions. In general algebraic algorithms for solving polynomial systems take time exponential in the number of variables, which is
O(n^{2})
in this case (see for example
[
4
]
).
Whether the system characterized by Eq.
1.1
admits more efficient solution is an interesting open question.

In §
3
we study last fall degrees of polynomial systems associated with algebraic sets that admit product decomposition. More generally we consider polynomial systems specified by a set

{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}
with
g_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\deg(g_{i})\leq d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, such that
there exists
\mu\in Gl_{n}(k)
where the linear space spanned by
g_{i}\circ\mu
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, contains some

f_{i}\in k[x_{i}]
with
\deg f_{i}\leq d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
We study the last fall degrees
[
2
,
3
]
of such polynomial
systems.
In Theorem
3.1
we prove
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\leq d
, where
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}
is the last fall degree of
{\mathcal{G}}
. If
d=O(1)
then
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}=O(1)
.
Finding
k
-rational solutions amounts to solving
the polynomial system
{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}
, where
{\mathcal{E}}
consists of the field equations
x_{i}^{q}-x_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
and
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In Theorem
3.5
we show that the last fall degree
d_{{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}\leq d+q
. In situations where the number of
k
-rational solutions is
O(1)
, it follows from Proposition 2.11 of
[
3
]
that the problem can be solved in time
n^{O(d+q)}
, hence
n^{O(q)}
if

.
An improvement can be made in finding
k
-rational point by reducing the problem to solving a system
{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
in
O(nm)
variables and degree
O(dp)
, where
q=p^{m}
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
consists of
x_{ij}^{p}-x_{i\ j+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=0,\ldots,m-1
, and

is taken modulo
m
. In Theorem
3.5
we show that the last fall degree
d_{{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\leq dp

k
-rational solutions is
O(1)
, then it follows that the problem can be solved in time
(nm)^{O(dp)}
, which is
(nm)^{O(1)}
providing

and
p=O(1)
.

2.
Algebraic characterization

We begin with a simple algebraic characterization of product decomposition.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
W\subset\bar{k}^{n}
.
The following two statements are equivalent:

(1)

W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
with
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
and
V_{i}
is a finite subset of
\bar{k}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

(2)

There exist polynomials
f_{i}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, each having distinct roots in
\bar{k}
, and
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
such that
W=Z(\{f_{1}\circ\lambda,\ldots,f_{n}\circ\lambda\})
.

Proof
Suppose
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
with
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
and
V_{i}
is a finite subset of
\bar{k}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
f_{i}(x_{i})=\prod_{\alpha\in V_{i}}(x_{i}-\alpha)
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Then for
\alpha\in\bar{k}^{n}
,
\alpha\in W
if and only if
\lambda(\alpha)\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
, if and only if
f_{i}\circ\lambda(\alpha)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. So (1) implies (2).

Now assume (2). Put
V=\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
where
V_{i}
is the set of roots of
f_{i}(x_{i})
. Then
\alpha\in W
if and only if
f_{i}\circ\lambda(\alpha)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
if and only if
\lambda(\alpha)\in V
. So (2) implies (1).
\Box

Suppose
I
is an ideal of
\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. For
i\geq 0
, let
I_{i}
denote the submodule of
I
consisting of all
f\in I
with
\deg f=i
.

For
S\subset\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
let
Z(S)
denote the zero set of
S
, that is
Z(S)=\{v\in\bar{k}^{n}:f(v)=0\ \ \forall f\in S\}
.
For
V\subset\bar{k}^{n}
let
I(V)=\{f\in\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]:f(v)=0\ \ \forall v\in V\}
.

Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
below provide a stronger characterization for the case of product decomposition into components of the same cardinality. More precisely we restrict our attention to product decomposition of the form
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
with
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
and
V_{i}
is a finite subset of
\bar{k}
of the same cardinality
d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
W\subset\bar{k}^{n}
and
W=Z({\mathcal{G}})
where
{\mathcal{G}}
consists of
n
polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
of degree bounded by
d
in
\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
with
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
,
V_{i}\subset\bar{k}
and
|V_{i}|=d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

(2)

g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
are linearly independent and they form a basis of
I(W)_{d}
as a
\bar{k}
-vector space. Moreover there are
f_{i}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, of degree
d
without multiple roots such that
f_{i}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, form a basis for the vector space spanned by
g_{i}\circ\mu
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, for some
\mu\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
W\subset\bar{k}^{n}
and
|W|=d^{n}
. Suppose
d
is not divisible by the characteristic of
k
. If
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
with
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
,
V_{i}\subset\bar{k}
and
|V_{i}|=d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, then such product decomposition is unique up to permutation and coordinate-wise scalar multiplication.
More explicitly
if
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda^{\prime}}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{\prime}_{i}
with
V^{\prime}_{i}\subset\bar{k}
and
|V^{\prime}_{i}|=d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
then

\lambda^{\prime}=\rho\circ\lambda
where
\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\alpha_{1}x_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{n}x_{\sigma_{n}})
for all
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\bar{k}^{n}
, where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\bar{k}
and
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})
is a permutation of
(1,\ldots,n)
.

To prove the two theorems, we will need some technical preparation.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
V_{i}
is a finite set of
\bar{k}
with
|V_{i}|=d_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
For
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
\deg_{x_{i}}f<d_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, if
f(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})=0
for all
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
, then
f=0
.

Proof
The lemma can be proved by induction on
n
. The statement is true for
n=1
since a polynomial in
x_{1}
of degree less than
d_{1}
cannot have
d_{1}
roots unless it is 0.

Suppose the statement is true for
n-1
.

Write
f=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{1}-1}f_{i}x_{1}^{i}
where
f_{i}\in\bar{k}[x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
For
j>1
since
\deg_{x_{j}}f<d_{j}
and
\deg_{x_{j}}f=\max_{i=0,\ldots,d_{1}-1}\deg_{x_{j}}f_{i}
, it follows that

\deg_{x_{j}}f_{i}<d_{j}
for
i=0,\ldots,d_{1}-1
.

Let
(\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\prod_{i=2}^{n}V_{i}
. Then by assumption
f(x_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n})
vanishes at
every
\beta_{1}\in V_{1}
, hence it has at least
d_{1}
roots. Since its degree in
x_{1}
is no greater than
\deg_{x_{1}}f
, which is less than
d_{1}
, we conclude that it is 0. So
f_{i}(\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n})=0
for
i=0,\ldots,d_{1}-1
.

Since
\deg_{x_{j}}f_{i}<d_{j}
for
j=2,\ldots,n
and
f_{i}(\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n})=0
for all
(\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\prod_{i=2}^{n}V_{i}
, it follows by induction that
f_{i}=0
. This is true for each
i=0,\ldots,d_{1}-1
. So
f=0
.
\Box

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f_{i}(x_{i})
be a monic polynomial in
x_{i}
of degree
d
without multiple roots over
\bar{k}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
I\subset\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the ideal generated by
f_{i}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
I_{d}
is a
\bar{k}
-vector space with
f_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, as a basis.

Proof
Let
V_{i}
be the set of roots of
f_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. If
f\in I
then
f
vanishes at all points of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
. Suppose
f\in I_{d}
. Then
f
as a polynomial in
\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree
d
can be written in the form
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})+g
where
\alpha_{i}\in\bar{k}
and
g\in\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
where

\deg_{x_{i}}g<d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\in I
and
f\in I
, it follows
g\in I
hence
g
vanishes at all points of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
. Since
\deg_{x_{i}}g<d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that
g=0
. Therefore
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})
with
\alpha_{i}\in\bar{k}
.
\Box

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
V,W\subset\bar{k}^{n}
and
V=\lambda(W)
where
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
. Then we have a
\bar{k}
-linear bijection

I(V)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda^{*}}}{{\to}}I(W)
where
\lambda^{*}(f)=f\circ\lambda
for
f\in I(V)
.
Moreover
\lambda^{*}(I(V)_{i})=I(W)_{i}
for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof
If
f\in I(V)
then
f(V)=f(\lambda(W))=0
hence
\lambda^{*}(f)\in I(W)
. It is easily checked that
\lambda^{*}
is
\bar{k}
-linear. It is injective since
f\circ\lambda=0
implies
(f\circ\lambda)\circ\lambda^{-1}=f=0
. It is surjective since for
g\in I(W)
,
g=f\circ\lambda
where
f=g\circ\lambda^{-1}\in I(V)
. Finally the map is degree preserving since
\lambda
is linear. Hence
\lambda^{*}(I(V)_{i})=I(W)_{i}
for all
i\geq 0
.
\Box

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
g(x)
is univariate and monic of degree
d
where
d
is not divisible by the characteristic of the field
k
. Suppose
\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}
with
a_{i}\in\bar{k}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
If
g\circ\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})
where
\beta_{i}\in\bar{k}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, then
\ell=a_{i}x_{i}
for some
i
.

Proof
We will prove the lemma by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
is trivial. Suppose inductively the lemma is true for
n-1
. Let
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
, be the roots of
g
. Then

g\circ\ell=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\ell-\alpha_{i})
. So we have

\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\ell-\alpha_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}f_{i}(x_{i}).

We see that for the left-hand-side of the equality, the coefficient for
x_{1}^{d-1}x_{2}
is
da_{1}^{d-1}a_{2}
, which must be 0 by virtue of the right hand side. Since
d
is not divisible by the characteristic of
k
, we must have
a_{1}a_{2}=0
.
Say
a_{1}=0
, then
\ell=a_{2}x_{2}+\ldots+a_{n}x_{n}
and
\beta_{1}=0
. We are reduced to the case of
n-1
. By induction we conclude that
\ell=a_{i}x_{i}
for some
i\neq 1
. Similarly if
a_{2}=0
we conclude that
\ell=a_{i}x_{i}
for some
i\neq 2
.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
2.2

Assume (2). Then the space spanned by the
n
polynomials
g_{i}\circ\mu
,

has dimension exactly

, since it contains
f_{i}(x_{i})
,

, which are clearly independent. Moreover the space is also spanned by
f_{i}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let

be the set of roots of

for

. Let

. Then by Lemma

,

,

and they also form a basis

.
Let

. Then

if and only if

,

. So

Assume (1). We claim that

. By Lemma

. Let

. By Lemma

,

. Let

be the set of roots of

. Then

,

, form a basis of

, which as argued above is also spanned by

,

\Box

3.
Last fall degree and Computation

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite set of polynomials in
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
For
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we let
V_{\mathcal{F},i}
be the smallest
k
-vector space such that

(1)

\{f\in\mathcal{F}:\deg(f)\leq i\}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F},i}
;

(2)

if
g\in V_{\mathcal{F},i}
and if
h\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
\deg(hg)\leq i
, then
hg\in V_{\mathcal{F},i}
.

We write
f\equiv_{i}g\pmod{\mathcal{F}}
, for
f,g\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, if
f-g\in V_{\mathcal{F},i}
.

Put
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and let
R_{\leq i}
denote the
k
-vector space consisting of polynomials in
R
of degree no greater than
i
for
i\geq 0
. The
last fall degree
as defined in
[
2
]
(see also
[
3
]
) is the largest
d
such that
V_{\mathcal{F},d}\cap R_{\leq d-1}\neq V_{\mathcal{F},d-1}
. We denote the last fall degree of
\mathcal{F}
by
d_{\mathcal{F}}
.

As shown in
[
2
,
3
]
the last fall degree is intrinsic to a polynomial
system, independent of the choice of a monomial order, always bounded by the degree of regularity, and invariant under linear change of variables and linear change of equations.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
W\subset\bar{k}^{n}
and
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\to}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
with
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(\bar{k})
,
V_{i}\subset\bar{k}
and
|V_{i}|\leq d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}\subset\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
Z({\mathcal{F}})=W
and the vector space spanned by
\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})
contains
f_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
where
f_{i}(x_{i})=\prod_{\alpha\in V_{i}}(x_{i}-\alpha)
. Then
d_{{\mathcal{F}}}\leq d
.

Proof
The last fall degree is invariant under linear change of variables and linear change of equations (Proposition 2.6 (part v) of
[
3
]
). Therefore we are reduced to the case that
W=\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
contains
f_{i}(x_{i})=\prod_{\alpha\in V_{i}}(x_{i}-\alpha)
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
We have

f_{i}(x_{i})=x_{i}^{d}-h_{i}(x_{i})
with
\deg h_{i}<d
. So

x_{i}^{d_{i}}\equiv_{d_{i}}h_{i}(x_{i})\pmod{f_{i}}
. Inductively we get

x_{i}^{j}\equiv_{j}h_{ij}(x_{i})\pmod{f_{i}}
for some
h_{ij}(x_{i})
of degree less than
d_{i}
, for all
j\geq 0
.
From this it is easy to see that for all
f\in\bar{k}[x_{1}\ldots x_{n}]
, we have

f\equiv_{\max(d,\deg f)}\bar{f}\pmod{I}
where
\bar{f}\in\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
\deg_{x_{i}}\bar{f}<d_{i}
for all
i
.
Finally
f\in I
if and only if
\bar{f}\in I
, and since
\deg_{x_{i}}\bar{f}<d_{i}
for all
i
, we conclude from
Lemma
2.4

\bar{f}\in I
if and only if
\bar{f}=0
. It follows that
I_{i}=V_{{\mathcal{F}},i}
for
i\geq d
. Hence
d_{{\mathcal{F}}}\leq d
.
\Box

3.1.
Last fall degree with field equations

Let
k
be a finite field with
q=p^{m}
elements where
p
is prime.
Suppose
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(k)
and
{\mathcal{F}}\subset k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a finite set.
Let
\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})=\{f\circ\lambda:f\in{\mathcal{F}}\}
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}=\{x_{i}^{q}-x_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
, the set of field equations for
k
.
Let
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. Suppose
\lambda=(a_{ij})
with
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
a_{ij}\in k
. Let

\ell_{i}(\vec{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}x_{j}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, so that
\lambda(\vec{x})=(\ell_{i}(\vec{x}))_{i=1}^{n}
.
Let
\lambda({\mathcal{E}})=\{\ell_{i}(\vec{x}^{q}-\vec{x}):i=1,\ldots,n\}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup{\mathcal{E}}}=d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}
.

Proof
As noted before, the last fall degree is invariant under linear change of variables and linear change of equations (Proposition 2.6 (part v) of
[
3
]
). So
d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}})}=d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}
.
Since

(\vec{x}^{q}-\vec{x})\circ\lambda=((\ell_{i}(\vec{x}))^{q}-\ell_{i}(\vec{x}))_{i=1}^{n}=(\ell_{i}(\vec{x}^{q}-\vec{x}))_{i=1}^{n},

it follows that
\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{E}})=\lambda({\mathcal{E}})
.
Since
{\mathcal{E}}
and
\lambda({\mathcal{E}})
are related by a linear change of equations, so are

\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup\lambda({\mathcal{E}})
and
\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup{\mathcal{E}}
, so

d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup{\mathcal{E}}}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup\lambda({\mathcal{E}})}
. Therefore

d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}})}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{E}})}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup\lambda({\mathcal{E}})}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup{\mathcal{E}}}.

\Box

We observe that
Z({\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}})=Z_{k}({\mathcal{F}})
where
Z_{k}({\mathcal{F}})=Z({\mathcal{F}})\cap k^{n}
. The degree of field equations for
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be reduced by introducing more variables. More precisely
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}=Z(x^{q}-x)\simeq Z(\{x^{p}-x_{1},x_{1}^{p}-x_{2},\ldots,x_{m-1}^{p}-x\})
where
x\in k
corresponds to
(x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1})
, with
x_{1}=x^{p}
,…,
x_{m-1}=x_{m-2}^{p}
. Now consider
nm
variables
x_{ij}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1
, and identify
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathcal{F}}
with
f(x_{10},\ldots,x_{n0})
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
include the polynomials
x_{i0}^{p}-x_{i1}
,…,
x_{i\ m-1}^{p}-x_{i0}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
Z({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\simeq k^{n}
where
(x_{ij})
corresponds to
(x_{i0})
, and

Z({\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\simeq Z_{k}({\mathcal{F}})
.

Let
\sigma
denotes the Frobenius
p
-th power map:
\sigma(x)=x^{p}
for
x\in\bar{k}
.
Suppose
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(k)
. Let
\tilde{\lambda}\in Gl_{nm}(k)
such that
\tilde{\lambda}
is block-diagonally decomposed as
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1})
with
\lambda_{j}=\lambda^{\sigma^{j}}
acting on the block

(x_{1j},\ldots,x_{nj})
. Let
\vec{x}_{j}=(x_{1j},\ldots,x_{nj})
, for
j=0,\ldots,m-1
. Then

x_{ij}\circ\tilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{j}(\vec{x}_{j}))_{i}
, so
\vec{x}_{j}\circ\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda_{j}(\vec{x}_{j})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}=d_{\lambda^{*}{{\mathcal{F}}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}

Proof
Again, the last fall degree is invariant under linear change of variables and linear change of equations (Proposition 2.6 (part v) of
[
3
]
). So
d_{\tilde{\lambda}^{*}({\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})}=d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}
.
Let
\vec{x}_{i}=(x_{1i},\ldots,x_{ni})
, for
i=0,\ldots,m-1
.
Let
\vec{y}_{i}=\lambda_{i}(\vec{x}_{i})
for
i=0,\ldots,m-1
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}
consists of
y_{ij}^{p}-y_{i\ j+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=0,\ldots,m-1
(and where
j+1
is taken

\mod m
).
Then

(\vec{x}_{j}^{p}-\vec{x}_{j+1})\circ\tilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{j}(\vec{x}_{j}))^{p}-\lambda_{j+1}(\vec{x}_{j+1})=\vec{y}_{j}^{p}-\vec{y}_{j+1},

where the index
j+1
is taken
\mod m
.
Hence
\tilde{\lambda}^{*}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})={\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}
.

Let
\mu=\lambda^{-1}
, and
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{-1}
for
i=0,\ldots,m-1
.
Then for
j=0,\ldots,m-1
,
\vec{x}_{j}=\mu_{j}(\vec{y}_{j})
and

\vec{x}_{j}^{p}-\vec{x}_{j+1}=(\mu_{j}(\vec{y}_{j}))^{p}-\mu_{j+1}(\vec{y}_{j+1})=\mu_{j+1}(\vec{y}_{j}^{p}-\vec{y}_{j+1}),

where the index
j+1
is taken
\mod m
.

We conclude that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}=\tilde{\mu}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime})
where
\tilde{\mu}\in Gl_{mn}(k)

is block-diagonally decomposed as
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m-1})
.

We have

d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}=d_{\tilde{\lambda}^{*}({\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup\tilde{\mu}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime})}=d_{\lambda^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}.

\Box

3.2.
Solving for
k
-rational points

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k
be a finite field with
|k|=q=p^{m}
. Let
f\in k[x_{0}]
with
d=\deg f
and
{\mathcal{F}}=\{f,x_{0}^{p}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1}^{p}-x_{0}\}
.
Then
x_{i}\equiv_{pd}g_{i}
with
g_{i}\in k[x_{0}]
and
\deg g_{i}<d
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
. Suppose
g=gcd(f,x_{0}^{q}-x_{0})\in k[x_{0}]
. Then
g\equiv_{dp}0\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Proof
We have
x_{0}^{p}\equiv_{\max\{p,d\}}g_{1}\pmod{f}
for some
g_{1}\in k[x_{0}]
with
\deg g_{1}<d
.
We have
g_{1}^{p}\equiv_{pd}g_{2}\pmod{f}
for some
g_{2}\in k[x_{0}]
with
\deg g_{2}<d
. Inductively we have

g_{i}^{p}\equiv_{pd}g_{i+1}\pmod{f}
with
g_{i+1}\in k[x_{0}]
and
\deg g_{i+1}<d
, for
i=1,\ldots,m-2
.
In particular it follows that
x_{0}^{q}-x_{0}\equiv h\pmod{f}
where
h=g_{m}-x_{0}
, so
gcd(x_{0}^{q}-x_{0},f)=gcd(f,h)
.

We have

x_{1}\equiv_{p}x_{0}^{p}\equiv_{\max\{p,d\}}g_{1}\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}}

, and
inductively,

x_{i+1}\equiv_{p}x_{i}^{p}\equiv_{pd}(g_{i})^{p}\equiv_{pd}g_{i+1}\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}},

for
i=1,\ldots,m-2
.
Finally,
x_{0}\equiv_{p}x_{m-1}^{p}\equiv_{pd}g_{m-1}^{p}\equiv_{pd}g_{m}\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}}
.
It follows that
h\equiv_{pd}0\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}}
, consequently
gcd(h,f)\equiv_{pd}0\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}}
. Therefore

gcd(x_{0}^{q}-x_{0},f)\equiv_{pd}0\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}}
.
\Box

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
k
be a finite field with
|k|=q=p^{m}
.
Let

{\mathcal{G}}=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}
where
g_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
\deg g_{i}\leq d
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
f_{i}\in k[x_{i}]
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
\vec{g}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}\\
.\\
.\\
g_{n}\end{array}\right)
and
\vec{f}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}\\
.\\
.\\
f_{n}\end{array}\right)
. Suppose
\vec{g}=\rho\circ\vec{f}\circ\lambda
with

\rho,\lambda\in Gl_{n}(k)
.
Then (1)
d_{{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}\leq d+q
where
{\mathcal{E}}=\{x_{i}^{q}-x_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
. (2)
d_{{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\leq dp
, where
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
consists of
x_{ij}^{p}-x_{i\ j+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1
and
j+1
is taken

\mod m
, and
g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is identified with
g(x_{10},\ldots,x_{n0})\in k[x_{ij}:1=1,\ldots,n,j=0,\ldots,m-1]
.

Proof
Let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
and
\mu=\lambda^{-1}
. Then
\vec{f}=\rho^{-1}\circ\vec{g}\circ\mu
. We see that
{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mu^{*}{\mathcal{G}}
are related by
\rho^{-1}
. So by Lemma
3.2
,
d_{{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}=d_{\mu^{*}{{\mathcal{G}}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}=d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}
. By Lemma
3.3
,
d_{{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}=d_{\mu^{*}{{\mathcal{G}}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}=d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}
. So to prove (1) and (2) we are reduced to proving
d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}\leq d+q
and
d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\leq dp
.

To prove
d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}\leq d+q
,
let

I^{\prime}
be the ideal generated by
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}
.
Let
h_{i}(x_{i})=gcd(x_{i}^{q}-x_{i},f_{i}(x_{i}))
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
I^{\prime}
is generated by
{\mathcal{H}}=\{h_{i}(x_{i}):i=1,\ldots,n\}
.
Since
h_{i}=A(x_{i}^{q}-x_{i})+Bf_{i}
with
A,B\in k[x_{i}]
with
\deg A<\deg f_{i}
and
\deg B<q
,
h_{i}(x_{i})\equiv_{d_{i}+q}0\pmod{\{x_{i}^{q}-x_{i},f_{i}(x_{i})\}}
. So

h_{i}(x_{i})\equiv_{d_{i}+q}0\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}
.
Let
c_{i}=\deg h_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
As with the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
it is easy to see that for all
f\in\bar{k}[x_{1}\ldots x_{n}]
, we have

f\equiv_{\max(c_{i},\deg f)}\bar{f}\pmod{{\mathcal{H}}}
where
\bar{f}\in\bar{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
\deg_{x_{i}}\bar{f}<c_{i}
for all
i
.
Finally
f\in I^{\prime}
if and only if
\bar{f}\in I^{\prime}
, and since
\deg_{x_{i}}\bar{f}<d_{i}
for all
i
, we conclude from
Lemma
2.4

\bar{f}\in I^{\prime}
if and only if
\bar{f}=0
. It follows that
I_{i}=V_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},i}
for
i\geq d+q
. Hence
d_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}\leq d+q
.

Next we prove
d_{{\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\leq dp
in a similar fashion by using Lemma
3.4
. Put
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathcal{F}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
and let
J
be the ideal generated by
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\}
where
h_{i}=gcd(f_{i},x_{i}^{q}-x_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
By Lemma
3.4

{\mathcal{H}}\subset V_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime},dp}
.
Then

J=\langle{\mathcal{H}}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\rangle
and
J\cap k[x_{i0}:i=1,\ldots,n]=\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle
.
Moreover
Z({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime})
maps bijectively to
Z({\mathcal{H}})\subset k^{n}
through the projection map sending
(x_{ij})_{i=1,\ldots,n;j=0,\ldots,m-1}
to
(x_{i0})_{i=1,\ldots,n}
.
By Lemma
3.4
,
x_{ij}\equiv_{pd}g_{ij}\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}}
with
g_{ij}\in k[x_{i0}]
and
\deg g_{ij}<d
. We have

x_{ij}^{2}\equiv_{pd}g_{ij}^{2}\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}}
, and
g_{ij}^{2}\equiv_{2d}g_{ij,2}\pmod{h_{i}}
with
g_{ij,2}\in k[x_{i0}]
and
\deg g_{ij,2}<d
. Inductively we see that
x_{ij}^{e}\equiv_{pd}g_{ij,e}\pmod{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}}
with
g_{ij,e}\in k[x_{i0}]
and
\deg g_{ij,e}<d
, for
e<p
.
From this it is easy to see that for all
f\in k[x_{ij}:i=1,\ldots,n;j=0,\ldots,m-1]
,

f\equiv_{\max(\deg f,pd)}\bar{f}
where
\bar{f}\in k[x_{i0}:i=1,\ldots,n]
and
\deg_{x_{i0}}(\bar{f})<\deg h_{i}
for all
i
. So
f\in J
if and only if
\bar{f}\in J\cap k[x_{i0}:i=1,\ldots,n]=\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle
. We conclude from
Lemma
2.4

\bar{f}\in\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle
if and only if
\bar{f}=0
. It follows that
J_{i}=V_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime},i}
for
i\geq pd
. Hence
d_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}\leq pd
.

\Box

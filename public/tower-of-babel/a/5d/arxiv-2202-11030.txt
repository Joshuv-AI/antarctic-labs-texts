Birational classification of pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree 8 8 Thanks: This work is supported by the Russian Science Foundation under grant №18-11-00121.
Andrey Trepalin

Abstract
. Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be a perfect field. Recently J.-L. Colliot-Thélène showed that two pointless quadric surfaces over 𝕜 \Bbbk are birationally equivalent if and only if they are isomorphic. We show that this result holds for arbitrary del Pezzo surfaces of degree 8 8 with the Picard number 1 1 , and describe minimal surfaces birationally equivalent to a given pointless del Pezzo surface of degree 8 8 .



1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field, and
Q
be a smooth quadric surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
. If
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed then all smooth quadrics are isomorphic, but if
\Bbbk
is not algebraically closed then two quadrics can be non-isomorphic. The biregular classification of quadric surfaces over nonclosed fields is well-known (see Section 2 for the details). Moreover, recently in
[
CT21
]
J.-L. Colliot-Thélène showed that the birational classification is almost the same.

Note that if there is a
\Bbbk
-point on a quadric surface
Q
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
, then one can consider a projection from this point to
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
. This projection is a birational map
Q\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
. Therefore any quadric with a
\Bbbk
-point is
\Bbbk
-rational. In particular, any two such quadrics are birationally equivalent over
\Bbbk
. But if there are no
\Bbbk
-points on a quadric then it is obviously not
\Bbbk
-rational. The birational classification of pointless quadric surfaces is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
CT21
]
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically nonclosed field such that
\operatorname{char}\Bbbk\neq 2
, and let
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
be two pointless quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
. Then
Q_{1}
is birationally equivalent to
Q_{2}
if and only if
Q_{1}\cong Q_{2}
.

The aim of this paper is to obtain an analogue of Theorem
1.1
for the case of arbitrary pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
. Let us recall the definition of a del Pezzo surface.

Definition 1.2
.

A
del Pezzo surface
is a smooth projective surface
X
such that the anticanonical class
-K_{X}
is ample. The number
d=K_{X}^{2}
is called the
degree
of a del Pezzo surface
X
.

Any quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
. Moreover, over algebraically closed field any del Pezzo surface of degree
8
is either isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, or isomorphic to the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
at a point. Therefore for a perfect field
\Bbbk
and a del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
there are two possibilities: either
\overline{X}=X\otimes_{\Bbbk}\overline{\Bbbk}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
, or
\overline{X}
is isomorphic to the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
at a point. In the former case if
X(\Bbbk)\neq\varnothing
then
X
is isomorphic to a quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
. In the latter case there is a unique
(-1)
-curve on
\overline{X}
. This curve is defined over
\Bbbk
, therefore we can contract this curve and get a morphism
{X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}}
. In particular, one has
X(\Bbbk)\neq\varnothing
. Thus for any pointless del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
the surface
\overline{X}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
.

If
X
is a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
then
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{X}\right)=\operatorname{Pic}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\right)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Therefore the Picard number
\rho(X)=\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is equal to
1
or
2
, since
\operatorname{Pic}\left(X\right)\subset\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{X}\right)
.

To establish more results about birational classification of pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
we need the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

A smooth projective surface
S
is called
minimal
if any birational morphism of smooth surfaces
S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.

The classification of minimal geometrically rational surfaces is well-known.

Theorem 1.4

(cf.
[
Isk79
, Theorems 1, 4, 5]
)
.

Let
S
be a minimal geometrically rational surface. Then either
S
admits a conic bundle structure over a conic with
\rho(S)=2
, or
S
is a del Pezzo surface with
\rho(S)=1
. In the former
case
K_{S}^{2}\notin\{3,5,6,7\}
,
and for
K_{S}^{2}=8
the surface
S
is not isomorphic to the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
at a point.

Note that any pointless del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
is minimal. If
\rho(X)=1
then it is obvious, and if
\rho(X)=2
then
X
is isomorphic to
C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are smooth conics, and the projections
X\rightarrow C_{1}
and
X\rightarrow C_{2}
define two structures of conic bundles on
X
.

We want to study which surfaces are birationally equivalent to pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
. Obviously, for any variety one can equivariantly blow up a collection of points defined over
\Bbbk
and get a birationally equivalent variety. Therefore for a given pointless del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
the natural problem is to find minimal surfaces birationally equivalent to
X
.

For the case
\rho(X)=2
the answer seems to be well-known to experts. For example, many results for this case can be found in
[
Kol05
]
. For completeness we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field, and let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
such that
\rho(X)=2
. Then
X
is a product of two smooth conics and there are two following possibilities.

(1)

The surface
X
is isomorphic to
C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two non-trivial smooth conics not isomorphic to each other. If the Brauer product
C_{3}=C_{1}*C_{2}
(for the definition see Definition
3.1
or
[
Kol05
, 7]
) is defined, then any minimal surface birationally equivalent to
X
is isomorphic to
C_{1}\times C_{2}
,
C_{1}\times C_{3}
or
C_{2}\times C_{3}
. Otherwise, any minimal surface birationally equivalent to
X
is isomorphic to
X
.

(2)

The surface
X
is isomorphic to
C\times C
or
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, where
C
is a non-trivial smooth conic. Then any minimal surface birationally equivalent to
X
is isomorphic
to
C\times C
,

C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
or to a
\Bbbk
-form of Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{2k}
admitting a conic bundle structure over
C
. Such
\Bbbk
-form is unique up to isomorphism for a given
k
.

For the case
\rho(X)=1
we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field, and let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
such that
\rho(X)=1
. Then any minimal surface birationally equivalent to
X
is isomorphic to
X
.

A geometrically rational surface
X
admitting a structure of a conic bundle is called a
relatively minimal conic bundle
if the Picard number of this surface is
2
.

Let
X
be a minimal del Pezzo surface with
\rho(X)=1
or a relatively minimal conic bundle. The surface
X
is called
birationally rigid
if for any birational
map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
,
where
X^{\prime}
is a minimal del Pezzo surface
X^{\prime}
with
\rho(X^{\prime})=1
or a relatively minimal conic bundle, one has
X^{\prime}\cong X
(for the precise definition of birational rigidity in general case and some properties see e.g.
[
Ch05
]
).

In Section
4
we show that in general case a pointless del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
is birationally equivalent to a (non-minimal) del Pezzo surface of degree
6
admitting a structure of relatively minimal conic bundle. For a given surface
X
these surfaces are parametrised by quadratic extensions
F/\Bbbk
such that
X_{F}(F)\neq\varnothing
. Therefore
X
is not birationally rigid.

Let us recall that the
index

I(V)
of a variety
V
is the greatest common divisor of the degrees of closed points on
V
. We give a description of birationally rigid del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field, and let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

One has
I(X)=4
.

(b)

There are no points of degree
2
on
X
.

(c)

The Amitsur subgroup
\operatorname{Am}(X)\subset\operatorname{Br}(\Bbbk)
(see Definition
2.4
or
[
Lied17
, Definition 2.8]
) contains an element that does not correspond to a conic.

(d)

The surface
X
is birationally rigid.

Actually for any pointless del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
we describe all possible minimal del Pezzo surfaces and relatively minimal conic bundles birationally equivalent to
X
. Note that if
X
is a minimal del Pezzo surface
X
with
\rho(X)=1
of degree
1
,
2
or
3
, or a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
4
, then
X
is birationally rigid (see
[
Isk96
, Theorems 4.4 and 4.5]
). On the other hand by
[
Isk96
, Chapter 4]
if
X
is a del Pezzo surface of degree at least
5
with
\rho(X)=1
and
X(\Bbbk)\neq\varnothing
or
X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
is a conic bundle with
\rho(X)=2
,
X(\Bbbk)\neq\varnothing
and
K_{X}^{2}\geqslant 5
then
X
is
\Bbbk
-rational. In particular, all these surfaces are birationally equivalent to each other.

A non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface
X
(i.e. a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
9
) is not birationally equivalent to any conic bundle, and if a minimal del Pezzo surface
X^{\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
X
then either
X^{\prime}\cong X
, or
X^{\prime}\cong X^{\mathrm{op}}
, where
X^{\mathrm{op}}

is the Severi–Brauer
surface such that the central simple algebras corresponding to
X
and
X^{\mathrm{op}}
are opposite (see
[
Sh20
, Corollary 2.4 and Theorem 2.10]
and
[
Wei22
]
).

Note that a geometrically rational surface
X
with
K_{X}^{2}=7
is never minimal by
[
Isk79
, Theorem 4]
, and a del Pezzo surface of degree
5
always has a
\Bbbk
-point (see
[
SD72
]
).

An interesting problem is to describe all minimal surfaces birationally equivalent to a given del Pezzo surface
X
with
\rho(X)=1
, or a conic bundle
X\rightarrow C
with
\rho(X)=2
. The cases of pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
and pointless conic bundles with
0
or
2
degenerate fibres are considered in this paper. Therefore the remaining cases are conic bundles with at least
4
degenerate fibres, pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
6
and del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
with
\Bbbk
-points. One of the particular questions is whether there exist two birationally equivalent del Pezzo surfaces
X
and
X^{\prime}
of degree
4
, such that
X
and
X^{\prime}
are not isomorphic. Note that some useful results about birational classification of minimal conic bundles and minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
with
\Bbbk
-points are obtained in
[
Sk86
, Theorem 2.5 and Corollary 3.3]
.

The plan of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we recall some notions and properties of pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
and give a definition of Amitsur
subgroup
\operatorname{Am}(X)\subset\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)
(see Definition
2.4
), that is a birational invariant. Also we give a description of all geometrically rational surfaces with non-trivial
\operatorname{Am}(X)
, and show how one can restore a del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
by
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
, where
\mathbb{L}
is a
splitting field
of
X
: unique quadratic extension of
\Bbbk
such that
\rho\left(X_{\mathbb{L}}\right)=2
.

In Section
3
we consider pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
with the Picard number
2
. Any such surface
X
is isomorphic to a product of two conics. We consider Sarkisov links for these surfaces, describe possibilities for minimal surfaces birationally equivalent to
X
, and show that any other surface is not birationally equivalent to
X
. As a result of this section we prove Theorem
1.5
.

In Section
4
we consider pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
with the Picard number
1
. We show that for any such surface
X
any Sarkisov link or sequence of Sarkisov links leads to an isomorphic surface or a certain non-minimal del Pezzo surface of degree
6
obtaining a relatively minimal conic bundle structure. Finally, we prove Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
, and give an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
for the case of a perfect field.

G_{\Bbbk}

X

X\otimes\overline{\Bbbk}

\overline{X}

X

\operatorname{Pic}(X)
.

A

A\sim B

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}}\left(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(n)\right)

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we review some results about biregular classification of del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
, and give an alternative description of this classification in terms of the Brauer group.

Throughout this section we assume that
X
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
over a perfect field
\Bbbk
, such that
\overline{X}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. The following lemma gives biregular classification of such surfaces.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
ShV18
, Lemma 3.4(i and ii)]
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field, and let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
, such that
\overline{X}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. The following assertion hold.

(i)

The surface
X
is isomorphic to the product
C_{1}\times C_{2}
of two conics over
\Bbbk
, or to
R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
, where
\mathbb{L}
is a quadratic extension of
\Bbbk
and
C
is a conic over
\mathbb{L}
. In the former case one has
\rho(X)=2
, while in the latter
\rho(X)=1
. Furthermore, in the former case the (non-ordered) pair of conics
\{C_{1},C_{2}\}
is uniquely determined by
X
; in the latter case the extension
\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk
and the conic
C
are uniquely determined by
X
up to conjugation by the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk\right)
.

(ii)

The surface
X
is isomorphic to a quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
if and only if
X\cong C\times C

or
X\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}N_{\mathbb{L}}
,
for some conic
N
over
\Bbbk
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field, and let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
{\rho(X)=1}
. In this case
X\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
. Let
C^{\prime}
be a
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk\right)
-conjugate conic of
C
. Then
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C\times C^{\prime}
. In particular,
X
is pointless if and only if
X_{\mathbb{L}}
is pointless.

For the both cases
\rho(X)=2
and
\rho(X)=1
we want to give an interpretation of Lemma
2.1
in terms of the Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)
(for the definition of the Brauer group and properties see, for example
[
GS18
, Chapter 3]
). Let us recall some facts about this group.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
CTKM08
, Proposition 5.1]
)
.

Let
V
be a smooth projective geometrically irreducible variety over
\Bbbk
. Then there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(V)\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{V})^{G_{\Bbbk}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk(V)\right).

Definition 2.4

(see
[
Lied17
, Definition 2.8]
)
.

Let
V
be a smooth projective geometrically irreducible variety over
\Bbbk
. The group

\operatorname{Am}(V)=\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{V})^{G_{\Bbbk}}/\operatorname{Pic}(V)\subset\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)

is called
Amitsur subgroup
of
V
in
\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)
.

Note that
\operatorname{Am}(V)
is a birational invariant since it is the kernel
of the map
\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk(V)\right)
(see
[
Lied17
, Proposition 2.10]
).

We want to describe minimal geometrically rational surfaces
S
with non-trivial
\operatorname{Am}(S)
. These surfaces are described in
[
CTKM08
, Proposition 5.3]
, but we obtain a more detailed description. By Theorem
1.4
the surface
S
is either a del Pezzo surface with
\rho(S)=1
, or admits a conic bundle structure over a smooth conic with
\rho(S)=2
.

Let us start from the case of conic bundle. We say that a conic is
non-trivial
if it is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
. Note that for each conic
C
we can define a
class

b(C)
, that is an element in
2
-torsion subgroup of the Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)
(see, for example,
[
GS18
, Section 3.3]
). If two conics are not isomorphic then they have different classes, and
b(C)
is trivial if and only if
C
is trivial. Moreover, for a conic
C
the class
b(C)
is a generator of
\operatorname{Am}(C)
.

If for a smooth projective geometrically irreducible variety
V
there exists
a map
V\dashrightarrow C
,
where
C
is a smooth conic, then this map induces embeddings
\operatorname{Pic}(C)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(V)
and
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{C})^{G_{\Bbbk}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{V})^{G_{\Bbbk}}
, since we can consider the preimage of a general geometric point on
C
. Therefore
\operatorname{Am}(C)\subset\operatorname{Am}(V)
. In particular, if
C
is non-trivial then
\operatorname{Am}(V)
is non-trivial, since it contains
b(C)
.

Now we can describe conic bundles
S\rightarrow C
with non-trivial
\operatorname{Am}(S)
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
S
be a surface admitting a conic bundle structure
S\rightarrow C
over a smooth conic
C
over a perfect field
\Bbbk
, such that
\rho(S)=2
and
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is non-trivial. Then there are five possibilities.

(1)

The surface
S
is isomorphic to
C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two non-trivial smooth conics not isomorphic to each other. The group
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
is generated by
b(C_{1})
and
b(C_{2})
.

(2)

The surface
S
is isomorphic to
C\times C
, where
C
is a non-trivial smooth conic. The group
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
b(C)
.

(3)

The surface
S
is isomorphic to
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, where
C
is a non-trivial smooth conic. The group
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
b(C)
.

(4)

The surface
S
is a
\Bbbk
-form of Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{2k}
admitting a conic bundle structure over a smooth non-trivial conic
C
, where
k
is a positive integer.
The group
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
b(C)
.

(5)

The surface
S
admits a conic bundle structure over a smooth non-trivial conic
C
with
2k
degenerate geometric fibres, where
k
is a positive integer.
The group
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
b(C)
.

Proof.

Note that
\rho\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}=\rho\left(S\right)=2
. Therefore
\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)
is a sublattice of
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}
of finite index.

We start from the case, when the conic bundle
S\rightarrow C
has a degenerate geometric fibre. Let us show that
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}
is generated by
-K_{\overline{S}}
and the class
F
of a geometric fibre. Otherwise there exists a class
D
in
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}
, such that
mD\sim-K_{\overline{S}}+nF
. Let
E
be an irreducible component of a geometric degenerate fibre. Then
E
is a
(-1)
-curve, and one has
-K_{\overline{S}}\cdot E=1
and
F\cdot E=0
. Therefore

m(D\cdot E)=mD\cdot E=\left(-K_{\overline{S}}+nF\right)\cdot E=1.

It is possible if and only if
m=\pm 1
, but in this case
D
lies in the lattice generated by
-K_{\overline{S}}
and
F
.

Note that
\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)
contains
-K_{S}
and
2F
, since there is a point of degree
2
on
C
, and a fibre over this point has class
2F
. Therefore
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is non-trivial if and only if
\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)
does not contain
F
, and for this case
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
b(C)
. This is possible if and only if
C
is a non-trivial conic. Also note that if
S\rightarrow C
has degenerate fibres over odd number of geometric points, then
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, since there is a point of odd degree on
C
.

Now assume that the conic bundle
S\rightarrow C
does not have degenerate geometric fibres. Then either
\overline{S}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
, or
S
is a
\Bbbk
-form of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
where
m\geqslant 1
.
In the latter case there is a unique section
H
of
S\rightarrow C
such that
H^{2}=-m
.
The group
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}=\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)
is generated by the class of
H
and a class
F
of a geometric fibre. The group
\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)
contains
2F
and the class of
H
, since
H
is unique and therefore defined over
\Bbbk
. Therefore
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is non-trivial if and only if
\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)
does not contain
F
, and for this case one has
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. This is possible if and only if
C
is a non-trivial conic. Also note that
-K_{S}\sim 2H+(m+2)F
, therefore
mF\in\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)
, and if
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is non-trivial then
m
is even.

Now assume that
S\cong C_{1}\times C_{2}
and
\overline{S}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. Note that
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\right)
is generated by the classes
A
and
B
of fibres of the projections on the first and the second factors of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. If
\rho(S)=2
then
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
contains
2A
and
2B
. Therefore if
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is non-trivial then
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
is
\langle 2A,2B\rangle
,
\langle 2A,A+B\rangle
,
\langle 2A,B\rangle
or
\langle A,2B\rangle
.

If
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
contains
A
or
B
then
C_{1}
or
C_{2}
respectively is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, and
S\cong C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, where
C
is a non-trivial smooth conic. For this case
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
b(C)
.

Let us show that
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
contains
A+B
if and only if
S
is isomorphic to a quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
. If
A+B
lies in
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
, then the linear system
|A+B|
defines
an embedding
S\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
and the image is a quadric surface. Conversely, if
S
is isomorphic to a quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
then any hyperplane section of this quadric has class
A+B
in
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
. Therefore for this case we can apply Lemma
2.1
and get that
S
is isomorphic to
C\times C
, where
C
is a smooth conic over
\Bbbk
. If
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is non-trivial then
C
is non-trivial, and for this case
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
b(C)
.

If
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
does not contain
A
,
B
and
A+B
, then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are non-trivial and not isomorphic to each other. For this case
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
is generated by
b(C_{1})
and
b(C_{2})
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

Let

, …

\Bbbk

branched into this collection of points. Assume that

\mathbb{F}

and

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}

\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk
and not defined over
\mathbb{F}
.

Let
\iota_{1}
be the involution of the double cover
H\rightarrow C
, and
\iota_{2}
be an involution acting on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
such that
\iota_{2}\left(q_{i}\right)=q_{i}
. Consider an involution
\iota

that acts on

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
as
\iota_{1}
and

respectively. The quotient

of a bundle
Y\rightarrow C\cong H/\langle\iota_{1}\rangle
such that its general fibre is a smooth conic. The images of fixed points of

A_{1}
-singularities. Therefore there are
4k

2k
fibres of
Y\rightarrow C
over the points

Let
\widetilde{Y}\rightarrow Y

over the points

with

degenerate fibres over the points

Now we describe minimal del Pezzo surfaces
S
with

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface over a perfect field
\Bbbk
, such that
\rho(S)=1
and
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is non-trivial. Then there are two possibilities.

(1)

The surface
S
is a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface,
{K_{S}^{2}=9}
,
and
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

The surface
S
is a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
isomorphic to
R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
, where
\mathbb{L}
is a quadratic extension of
\Bbbk
and
C
is a conic over
\mathbb{L}
, such that for any conic
N
over
\Bbbk
the conics
C
and
N_{\mathbb{L}}
are not isomorphic. One has
{\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Note that
\rho\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}=\rho\left(S\right)=1
. Therefore
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}
is generated by a class
D
such that for a certain number
k
one has
kD=-K_{S}
, since
-K_{S}
obviously lies
in
\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)\subset\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}
.

Assume that there is a
(-1)
-curve
E
on
\overline{S}
, then one has

1=-K_{\overline{S}}\cdot E=kD\cdot E=k(D\cdot E).

Therefore
k=1
, since
D\cdot E
is integer. Thus in this case
\operatorname{Pic}\left(S\right)=\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}
, and
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is trivial.

If there are no
(-1)
-curves on
\overline{S}
then
\overline{S}
is either
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
, or
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. In the former case
S
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
9
, i.e. Severi–Brauer surface. A trivial Severi–Brauer surface is just
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
, and
\operatorname{Am}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}\right)
is trivial. For a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface
S
it is well-known that
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
is generated by
-K_{S}
, and
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}=\operatorname{Pic}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}=\operatorname{Pic}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\right)
is generated by the class of a line on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. Therefore
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\overline{S}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
then
K_{S}^{2}=8
. As in the case
\rho(S)=2
, the group
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\right)
is generated by the classes
A
and
B
of fibres of the projections on the first and the second factors of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
, and
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
contains
-K_{S}\sim 2A+2B
. If
\rho(S)=1
, then the Galois group
G_{\Bbbk}
permutes
A
and
B
, and
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{S}\right)^{G_{\Bbbk}}
is generated by the class
A+B
. Therefore if
\operatorname{Am}(S)
is not trivial, then
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
does not contain
A+B
.

As in the proof of Proposition
2.5
one can easily show that
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
contains
A+B
if and only if
S
is isomorphic to a quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
. Therefore for this case we can apply Lemma
2.1
and get that
S
is isomorphic to
R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}N_{\mathbb{L}}
, where
N
is a conic over
\Bbbk
. Thus for
the case
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
the surface
S
is not isomorphic to a quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
. By Lemma
2.1
the surface
S
is isomorphic to
R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
, where
\mathbb{L}
is a quadratic extension of
\Bbbk
and
C
is a conic over
\mathbb{L}
, such that for any conic
N
over
\Bbbk
the conics
C
and
N_{\mathbb{L}}
are not isomorphic.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

It is well known, that two non-trivial Severi–Brauer surfaces
S
and
S^{\prime}
are birationally equivalent if and only if
S\cong S^{\prime}
or
S^{\prime}\cong S^{\mathrm{op}}
, where
S^{\mathrm{op}}
is the Severi–Brauer surface such that the central simple algebras corresponding to
S
and
S^{\mathrm{op}}
are opposite (see
[
Ami55
, Corollary 9.5]
). Any other minimal surface is not birationally equivalent to
S
and
S^{\prime}
by
[
Sh20
, Corollary 2.4 and Theorem 2.10]
(see also
[
Wei22
]
). Applying Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
we can obtain an alternative proof of this fact.

By Proposition
2.7
a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface
S
has
\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
generated by the class
b(S)
of
S
. If a surface
S^{\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
S

then
\operatorname{Am}(S^{\prime})=\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore
S
is not birationally equivalent to any relatively minimal conic bundle by Proposition
2.5
. If
S^{\prime}
is a minimal del Pezzo surface then
S^{\prime}
is a Severi–Brauer surface by Proposition
2.7
, and
b(S^{\prime})

generates
\operatorname{Am}(S^{\prime})=\operatorname{Am}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
.
There are two possibilities:
either
b(S^{\prime})=b(S)
and
S^{\prime}\cong S
, or
b(S^{\prime})=-b(S)
and
S^{\prime}\cong S^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Note that non-isomorphic del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
can have the same group
\operatorname{Am}(X)
. For example, if
X
is a pointless quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
then
\operatorname{Am}(X)
is trivial. Therefore we want to consider more invariants for such surfaces.

For a given del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
there exists a quadratic extension
\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk
, such that
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are smooth conics over
\mathbb{L}
. The field
\mathbb{L}
is uniquely determinated by
X
, since
\mathbb{L}=\overline{\Bbbk}^{K}
, where
K
is the kernel of the action of
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\Bbbk\right)
on
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})
. For simplicity of notation we will say that
\mathbb{L}
is the
splitting field
of
X
.

We can consider the group
\operatorname{Am}\left(X_{\mathbb{L}}\right)\subset\operatorname{Br}\left(\mathbb{L}\right)
. This group is obviously a birational invariant. By Lemma
2.1
one has
X\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
, where
C
is a smooth conic over
\mathbb{L}
. Let
C^{\prime}
be the conjugate conic. Then by Corollary
2.2
one has
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C\times C^{\prime}
.

Applying Proposition
2.5
we can see that there are three possibilities for
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
:

•

\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
is trivial, if and only if
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
;

•

\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, if and only if
C\cong C^{\prime}
and
C
is non-trivial;

•

\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
, if and only if
C
and
C^{\prime}
are not isomorphic.

Therefore for a given del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
we can consider a pair of invariants
\left(\mathbb{L},\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})\right)
. We show that this pair is a biregular invariant.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be two del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=\rho(X^{\prime})=1
. Then
X
and
X^{\prime}
have the same splitting field
\mathbb{L}
and
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})=\operatorname{Am}(X^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}})
if and only
if
X\cong X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
X\cong X^{\prime}
then they obviously have the same splitting field
\mathbb{L}

and
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})=\operatorname{Am}(X^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}})
.

Now assume that
X
and
X^{\prime}
have the same splitting field
\mathbb{L}
and
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})=\operatorname{Am}(X^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}})
and show that
X\cong X^{\prime}
.

Note that
X\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
, where
b(C)\in\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
. For
a
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk\right)
-conjugate
conic
C^{\prime}
one has
R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C^{\prime}
by Lemma
2.1
. Moreover,
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C\times C^{\prime}
by Corollary
2.2
.

If
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
is trivial then
C\cong C^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
. If
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
then there is a unique non-trivial element in
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
corresponding to
C\cong C^{\prime}
.

If
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
then there are three non-trivial elements in
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
. Two of these elements are
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk\right)
-conjugate since in this case
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C\times C^{\prime}
, where
C
and
C^{\prime}
are not isomorphic. Therefore even if the third non-trivial element in
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
corresponds to a conic
\widetilde{C}
then the
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk\right)
-conjugate conic is isomorphic to
\widetilde{C}
. Then
for
\widetilde{X}=R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}\widetilde{C}
one has
\widetilde{X}_{\mathbb{L}}\cong\widetilde{C}\times\widetilde{C}
, and
\operatorname{Am}(\widetilde{X}_{\mathbb{L}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Thus if
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
then we can definitely restore the pair of conjugate conics
C
and
C^{\prime}
such that
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C\times C^{\prime}

and
X\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C^{\prime}
.

Therefore in any case
X^{\prime}\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C\cong X
.
∎

In this section we consider Sarkisov links for pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree

.

Let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with the Picard number
2
. By Lemma
2.1
in this case
X
is isomorphic to a product of two smooth conics, such that at least one of these conics is non-trivial. Therefore by Proposition
2.5

either
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
,
or
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

We start from the case
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
. Assume that there exists a minimal surface
X^{\prime}
, and a birational map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
. Then
\operatorname{Am}(X^{\prime})=\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
. By Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
it is possible only if
X^{\prime}\cong C^{\prime}_{1}\times C^{\prime}_{2}
, where
C^{\prime}_{1}
and
C^{\prime}_{2}
are two smooth non-trivial non-isomorphic conics. Moreover,
[
Kol05
, Theorem 2]
implies that if for a minimal surface
X^{\prime}\cong C^{\prime}_{1}\times C^{\prime}_{2}
one has
\operatorname{Am}(X^{\prime})=\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
, then
X^{\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
X
.

Actually we want to remind some results and constructions from
[
Kol05
]
to describe birational maps from
X
more explicitly.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two smooth conics, and
b(C_{1})
and
b(C_{2})
be the classes of these conics in
\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)
. If there exists a conic
C_{3}
, such that
b(C_{3})=b(C_{1})+b(C_{2})
, then
C_{3}
is called the
Brauer product
of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. We denote the Brauer product of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
by
C_{1}*C_{2}
.

We want to show that
C_{1}*C_{2}
is defined if and only if there exists a quadratic extension
F/\Bbbk
, such that
C_{1}\otimes F\cong C_{2}\otimes F\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
. In the other words there are points of degree
2
on
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, such that the corresponding geometric points are defined over
F
. Note that this condition holds if
C_{1}\cong C_{2}
, or if
C_{1}
or
C_{2}
is trivial.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two smooth conics. Suppose that there exist points of degree
2
on
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, such that the corresponding geometric points are defined over a quadratic extension
F/\Bbbk
. Then there exists a conic
C_{3}=C_{1}*C_{2}
, and
X\cong C_{1}\times C_{2}
is birationally equivalent to
C_{1}\times C_{3}
and
C_{2}\times C_{3}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{1}:X\rightarrow C_{1}
and
\pi_{2}:X\rightarrow C_{2}
be the projections. Let
A
and
B
be the classes in
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})
of geometric fibres of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
respectively. Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be conjugate fibres of
\pi_{1}
defined over
F
, and
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be conjugate fibres of
\pi_{2}
defined over
F
. Then the pair of points
A_{1}\cap B_{1}
and
A_{2}\cap B_{2}
are defined over
F
. We can equivariantly blow up this pair of points, contract the proper transforms of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, and get a surface
X^{\prime}
.

Note that the birational map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
respects the projection
\pi_{1}:X\rightarrow C_{1}
. Therefore
X^{\prime}
admits a structure of a conic bundle over
C_{1}
. Moreover,
K_{X^{\prime}}^{2}=K_{X}^{2}=8
, therefore
X^{\prime}
is a
\Bbbk
-form of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
. Also note that the proper transforms of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are sections of the conic bundle
X^{\prime}\rightarrow C_{1}
with selfintersection number
0
. For
n>0
there are no sections of
\mathbb{F}_{n}\rightarrow C_{1}
with selfintersection number
0
. Thus
X^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{F}_{0}
that is a product of two conics.

One of these conics is
C_{1}
, since
X^{\prime}
admits a structure of a conic bundle over
C_{1}
. Denote the other conic by
C_{3}
. Note that preimage of a geometric point on
C_{3}
under the sequence of maps
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}\rightarrow C_{3}
has class
A+B
in
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{X}\right)
.
Therefore
b(C_{3})\in\operatorname{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)

is
b(C_{1})+b(C_{2})
,
since the preimages of geometric points on
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
under the projections
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
have classes
A
and
B
in
\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{X}\right)
respectively. Thus
C_{3}=C_{1}*C_{2}
,
and
X\cong C_{1}\times C_{2}
is birationally equivalent to
X^{\prime}\cong C_{1}\times C_{3}
. In the same way we can show that
X
is birationally equivalent to
C_{2}\times C_{3}
.
∎

The following lemma directly follows from the proof of
[
Kol05
, Lemma 8]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two smooth conics, such that for any pair of points of degree
2
on
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
the corresponding geometric points are not defined over any quadratic extension
\mathbb{F}/\Bbbk
. Then a conic with class
b(C_{1})+b(C_{2})
does not exist.

One can find an example of such pair of conics in
[
Cao18
, Example 5.4]
.

Now consider the case
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. By Proposition
2.5
the surface
X
is isomorphic either to
C\times C
, or to
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
where
C
is a smooth non-trivial conic. Moreover, by Lemma
3.2
for a given smooth non-trivial conic
C
the surfaces
C\times C
and
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
are birationally equivalent. In the following lemma we show that these surfaces are birationally equivalent to certain
\Bbbk
-forms of Hirzebruch surfaces.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
C
be a smooth non-trivial conic. Then the surfaces
C\times C
,
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, and
\Bbbk
-forms of Hirzebruch surfaces
\mathbb{F}_{2k}
admitting a structure of conic bundle over
C
are birationally equivalent.

Proof.

The surfaces
C\times C
and
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
are birationally equivalent by Lemma
3.2
,
since
C*C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
.

Now consider
X\cong C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
. Let
\pi_{1}:X\rightarrow C
and
\pi_{2}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
be the projections. Then for a
\Bbbk
-point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, the preimage
B=\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)
is a section of
\pi_{2}
. Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be conjugate fibres of
\pi_{1}
. Then the points
A_{1}\cap B
and
A_{2}\cap B
are conjugate. We can equivariantly blow up this pair of points, contract the proper transforms of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, and get a surface
X^{\prime}
.

The birational map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
respects the projection
\pi_{1}:X\rightarrow C
. Therefore
X^{\prime}
admits a structure of a conic bundle over
C
. Moreover,
K_{X^{\prime}}^{2}=K_{X}^{2}=8
, therefore
X^{\prime}
is a
\Bbbk
-form of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
. Note that the proper transform of
B
is a section of the conic bundle
X^{\prime}\rightarrow C
with selfintersection number
-2
. Therefore
n=2
.
Thus
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
is birationally equivalent to a
\Bbbk
-form of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{2}
admitting a structure of a conic bundle over
C
.

Now consider a
\Bbbk
-form
S
of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{2k}
admitting a structure of a conic bundle
\pi:S\rightarrow C
. There is a unique section
H
of
\pi
such that
H^{2}=-2k
. Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be conjugate fibres of
\pi
. Then the points
A_{1}\cap H
and
A_{2}\cap H
are conjugate. We can equivariantly blow up this pair of points, contract the proper transforms of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, and get a surface
S^{\prime}
.

The birational map
S\dashrightarrow S^{\prime}
respects the projection
\pi:S\rightarrow C
. Therefore
S^{\prime}
admits a structure of a conic bundle over
C
. Moreover,
K_{S^{\prime}}^{2}=K_{S}^{2}=8
, therefore
S^{\prime}
is a
\Bbbk
-form of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
. Note that the proper transform of
H
is a section of the conic bundle
S^{\prime}\rightarrow C
with selfintersection number
-2k-2
. Therefore
n=2k+2
. Thus
S
is birationally equivalent to a
\Bbbk
-form of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{2k+2}
admitting a structure of conic bundle over
C
.

Thus we see that all surfaces listed in Lemma
3.4
are birationally equivalent.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8

with
\rho(X)=2
.
Then
\overline{X}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
, and
X
is a product of two conics. If
\operatorname{Am}(X)
is trivial
then
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
and
X(\Bbbk)\neq\varnothing
. Therefore
\operatorname{Am}(X)
is non-trivial, and by Proposition
2.5

either
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
,
or
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
then
X
is a product of two non-trivial smooth conics
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, not isomorphic to each other. If the Brauer product
C_{3}=C_{1}*C_{2}
is defined
then
X\approx C_{1}\times C_{3}\approx C_{2}\times C_{3}
by Lemma
3.2
.

If a minimal surface
X^{\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
X
then

\operatorname{Am}(X^{\prime})=\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}.

By Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
this is possible only if
X^{\prime}
is a product of two smooth conics
C^{\prime}_{1}
and
C^{\prime}_{2}
. Moreover,
b(C^{\prime}_{1})
and
b(C^{\prime}_{2})
are distinct non-trivial elements of
\operatorname{Am}(X)
. Therefore if
C_{3}
is defined then
X^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
C_{1}\times C_{2}
,
C_{1}\times C_{2}
or
C_{2}\times C_{3}
,
and otherwise
X^{\prime}\cong C_{1}\times C_{2}\cong X
.
We have proved Theorem
1.5
(1).

If
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
then either
X\cong C\times C
, or
X\cong C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, where
C
is a non-trivial smooth conic. By Lemma
3.4
the surface
X
is birationally equivalent to
C\times C
,
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, and
\Bbbk
-forms of Hirzebruch surfaces
\mathbb{F}_{2k}
admitting a structure of conic bundle over
C
.

Assume that a minimal surface
X^{\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
X
and not listed before. Then the map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
can be decomposed into a sequence of Sarkisov links:

X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow\ldots\dashrightarrow X_{n-1}\dashrightarrow X_{n}=X^{\prime}.

In particular there exists a Sarkisov link
f:S\dashrightarrow S^{\prime}
, such that
S
is listed in Lemma
3.4
, and
S^{\prime}
is not listed. For geometrically rational surfaces Sarkisov links are described in
[
Isk96
, Theorem 2.6]
. All surfaces listed in Lemma
3.4
admit a structure of a conic bundle over
C
. Therefore
f
has type
\mathrm{II}
,
\mathrm{III}
or
\mathrm{IV}
. Links of types
\mathrm{II}
and
\mathrm{IV}
preserve
K_{S}^{2}
and
\operatorname{Am}(S)
. Therefore in this case
S^{\prime}
is one of the surfaces listed
in Proposition
2.5
(2)–(4),
and also in Lemma
3.4
. For
S
with
\rho(S)=2
and
K_{S}^{2}=8
the only possible link of type
\mathrm{III}
is a contraction of
(-1)
-section on a
\Bbbk
-form of a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{1}
. But such surface is not listed in Lemma
3.4
, therefore this link is impossible, and
X^{\prime}
must be isomorphic to a surface listed in Lemma
3.4
. We have proved Theorem
1.5
(2).
∎

4.
Sarkisov links for the case
\rho(X)=1

In this section we consider Sarkisov links for pointless del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
with the Picard number
1
, and prove Theorem
1.6
.

Let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with the Picard number
1
. By Lemma
2.1
in this case
X
is isomorphic to
R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
, where
\mathbb{L}
is a quadratic extension of
\Bbbk
and
C
is a conic over
\mathbb{L}
. The field
\mathbb{L}
is uniquely determined by
X
.
The pair
\left(\mathbb{L},\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})\right)
is a biregular invariant of
X
by Theorem
2.9
. We want to find possibilities for minimal surfaces
X^{\prime}
birationally equivalent to
X
.

Note that by Corollary
2.2
one has
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C\times C^{\prime}
, where
C^{\prime}
is a conic conjugate to
C
under the action of
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk\right)
. Note that
X
is pointless if and only if
X_{\mathbb{L}}
is pointless too. In particular, if
X
is pointless then
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
is non-trivial.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
. Then any point on
X
has even degree.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a point of odd degree on
X
. Then there exists a point of odd degree on
X_{\mathbb{L}}
, and images of this point under the projection on
C
and
C^{\prime}
are points of odd degree. There are no points of odd degree on any non-trivial conic.
Therefore
C\cong C^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
,
and thus
X\left(\Bbbk\right)\neq\varnothing
.

We obtain a contradiction. Therefore any point on
X
has even degree.
∎

Now we want to consider Sarkisov links for the surface
X
. These links are described in
[
Isk96
, Theorem 2.6 (i and ii)]
. In our case such a link must have tipe
\mathrm{I}
or
\mathrm{II}
, since
X
is a del Pezzo surface with
\rho(X)=1
. Any link of type
\mathrm{I}
is a blowup
X_{1}\rightarrow X
of a point of degree
d
, where
X_{1}
has a structure of a conic bundle over a smooth conic and
\rho(X_{1})=2
. Any link of type
\mathrm{II}
is a composition
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma^{-1}
, where
\sigma:Z\rightarrow X
is a blowup of a point of degree
d
, and
\sigma_{1}:Z\rightarrow X_{1}
is a contraction of a set of disjoint conjugate
(-1)
-curves, that differs from the exceptional divisor of
\sigma
. In our case
d=2
,
d=4
or
d=6
by Lemma
4.1
. For
d=2
the corresponding link has type
\mathrm{I}
, and for
d=4
and
d=6
the corresponding link has type
\mathrm{II}
. For each of these three links we want to describe
X_{1}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
. Let
X\dashrightarrow X_{1}
be a Sarkisov link corresponding to the blowup of a point of degree
6
. Then
X_{1}\cong X
.

Proof.

This case is considered in
[
Isk96
, Theorem 2.6.(ii),
K_{X}^{2}=8
,
d=6
]
, and there is explicitely written that
X_{1}\cong X
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
. Let
X\dashrightarrow X_{1}
be a Sarkisov link corresponding to the blowup of a point of degree
4
. Then
X_{1}\cong X
.

Proof.

This case is considered in
[
Isk96
, Theorem 2.6.(ii),
K_{X}^{2}=8
,
d=4
]
, and for this case
K_{X_{1}}^{2}=8
and
\rho(X_{1})=1
. Therefore by Lemma
2.1
one has
R_{\mathbb{L}_{1}/\Bbbk}C_{1}
, where
\mathbb{L}_{1}
is the splitting field of
X_{1}
and
C_{1}
is a smooth conic over
\mathbb{L}_{1}
. Let us show that
\mathbb{L}_{1}=\mathbb{L}
. It is sufficient to prove that
\rho(\left(X_{1}\right)_{\mathbb{L}})=2
.

Let
\pi_{1}:X_{\mathbb{L}}\rightarrow C
and
\pi_{2}:X_{\mathbb{L}}\rightarrow C^{\prime}
be the projections. Let
A
and
B
be the classes in
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})
of geometric fibres of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
respectively. The map
X\dashrightarrow X_{1}
is a composition
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma^{-1}
, where
\sigma:Z\rightarrow X
is a blowup of a point of degree
4
and
\sigma_{1}:Z\rightarrow X_{1}
is a contraction of a set of disjoint conjugate
(-1)
-curves. Let
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
be the classes in
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{Z})
of geometrically irreducible components of the exceptional divisor of
\sigma
.

The surface
Z
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
4
, and thus there are
16

(-1)
-curves on
Z
, having classes
E_{i}
,
A-E_{i}
,
B-E_{i}
and
A+B+E_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4}E_{j}
in
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{Z})
.
The four
(-1)
-curves
with classes
A+B+E_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4}E_{j}
are conjugate and disjoint, therefore
\sigma_{1}:Z\rightarrow X_{1}
is the contraction of these curves.

Let
k
be the number of
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\mathbb{L}\right)
-orbits on the set
E_{i}
(actually
k=1
or
k=2
). Then the number of
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\mathbb{L}\right)
-orbits on the set
A+B+E_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4}E_{j}
is also
k
, since
A+B-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4}E_{j}
is
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\mathbb{L}\right)
-invariant. Therefore

\rho(\left(X_{1}\right)_{\mathbb{L}})=\rho(Z_{\mathbb{L}})-k=\rho(X_{\mathbb{L}})=2.

The del Pezzo surfaces
X
and
X_{1}
of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=\rho(X_{1})=1
have the same splitting field and
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})=\operatorname{Am}((X_{1})_{\mathbb{L}})
, since
X\approx X_{1}
. Therefore
X\cong X_{1}
by Theorem
2.9
.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

Note that in Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
the surface
X
is not necessary pointless.

The link of type
\mathrm{I}
corresponding to the blowup
Z\rightarrow X
of two conjugate geometric points is considered in
[
Isk96
, Theorem 2.6.(i),
K_{X}^{2}=8
]
. In this case
Z
admits a structure of a conic bundle
\pi:Z\rightarrow C
with two degenerate geometric fibres, where
C
is a conic such that
b(C)
is the generator of
\operatorname{Am}(X)
(in particular,
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
if

is trivial). In the following proposition we collect some facts about such conic bundles following
[
Isk79
]
and
[
Isk96
]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\pi:S\rightarrow C
be a conic bundle over a smooth conic
C
with two degenerate geometric fibres, such that
\rho(S)=2
. Then
K_{S}^{2}=6
, and
S
has the following properties.

(i)

The surface
S
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
6
(see
[
Isk79
, Theorem 5]
).

(ii)

There are six
(-1)
-curves on
S
: four components of the singular fibres, and two disjoint conjugate sections
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
of
\pi
. These curves form a hexagon.

(iii)

The surface
S
is not minimal (see
[
Isk79
, Theorem 4]
).

(iv)

There exists a unique link
S\rightarrow Y
of type
\mathrm{III}
that is the contraction of the pair
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The surface
Y
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
\rho(Y)=1
(see
[
Isk96
, Theorem 2.6(iii)]
).

(v)

Any other link
S\dashrightarrow S_{1}
has type
\mathrm{II}
, and preserves a structure of a conic bundle over
C
(see
[
Isk96
, Theorem 2.6]
). Such link is a composition
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma^{-1}
, where
\sigma
is a blowup of a point of degree
d
such that the corresponding geometric points do not lie on the degenerate fibres, and
\sigma_{1}
is the contraction of the proper transforms of
d
fibres containing these points. In particular, the conic bundles
S\rightarrow C
and
S_{1}\rightarrow C
have degenerate fibres over the same points on
C
,
\rho(S_{1})=2
and
K_{S_{1}}^{2}=6
. Therefore all described above properties hold for
S_{1}
.

Now we can consider the link of type
\mathrm{I}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
. Consider a sequence of Sarkisov links

X\leftarrow Z=Z_{0}\dashrightarrow Z_{1}\dashrightarrow\ldots\dashrightarrow Z_{n-1}\dashrightarrow Z_{n}=Z^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime},

where the first link has type
\mathrm{I}
, the last link has type
\mathrm{III}
, and all other links have type
\mathrm{II}
. Then
X^{\prime}\cong X
.

Proof.

The surface
Z
admits a structure of a conic bundle over a smooth conic
\pi:Z\rightarrow C
with two degenerate fibres. Denote the components of these fibres by
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. These components are
(-1)
-curves on
Z
. By Proposition
4.5
(ii) there are two disjoint conjugate sections
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
of
\pi
that are
(-1)
-curves. Without loss of generality we can assume that

A_{1}\cdot E_{1}=E_{1}\cdot B_{1}=B_{1}\cdot A_{2}=A_{2}\cdot E_{2}=E_{2}\cdot B_{2}=B_{2}\cdot A_{1}=1.

Let
\Gamma
be the kernel of the action of
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\Bbbk\right)
on the set of
(-1)
-curves on
Z
, and
\mathbb{F}=\overline{\Bbbk}^{\Gamma}
. Then
\rho\left(Z_{\mathbb{F}}\right)=\rho\left(\overline{Z}\right)=4
, and
\rho(X_{\mathbb{F}})=2
. Therefore
\mathbb{L}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}
. Note that the pairs
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, and
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are defined over
\mathbb{L}
, but no one of these curves is defined over
\mathbb{L}
, since
X_{\mathbb{L}}
is pointless. Therefore the group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{L}\right)
has order
2
and pairwisely permutes
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
,
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, and
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

By Proposition
4.5
(v) all surfaces
Z_{i}
are del Pezzo surfaces of degree
6
admitting a structure of a conic bundle
Z_{i}\rightarrow C
with two degenerate fibres. Let
f:Z\dashrightarrow Z^{\prime}
be the composition of the Sarkisov links
Z_{i}\dashrightarrow Z_{i+1}
. Then
A^{\prime}_{1}=f(A_{1})
,
A^{\prime}_{2}=f(A_{2})
,
B^{\prime}_{1}=f(B_{1})
and
B^{\prime}_{2}=f(B_{2})
are components of the degenerate fibres of
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow C
. By Proposition
4.5
(ii) there are two disjoint conjugate sections
E^{\prime}_{1}
and
E^{\prime}_{2}
of
\pi^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\rightarrow C
that are
(-1)
-curves. We can assume that
A^{\prime}_{1}\cdot E^{\prime}_{1}=1
, and want to show that
E^{\prime}_{1}\cdot B^{\prime}_{1}=1
.

Let
R=f^{-1}_{*}\left(E^{\prime}_{1}\right)
be the proper transform of
E^{\prime}_{1}
on
Z
. If we blow up a point
p
on
\overline{Z}
and contract a proper transform of the fibre containing
p
, then for the proper transform
\widetilde{R}
of
R
one has
\widetilde{R}^{2}-R^{2}=\pm 1
(
-1
if
p\in R
, and
1
otherwise). By Proposition
4.5
(v) any link
Z_{i}\dashrightarrow Z_{i+1}
is a composition
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma^{-1}
, where
\sigma
is a blowup of a point of degree
d
such that the corresponding geometric points do not lie on degenerate fibres, and
\sigma_{1}
is the contraction of the proper transforms of
d
fibres containing these points. Moreover,
d
is even, since otherwise there is a point of odd degree on
X
that is impossible by Lemma
4.1
. Thus
R^{2}-\left(E^{\prime}_{1}\right)^{2}
is even, and
R^{2}
is odd.

Note that the group
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{4}
is generated by
A_{1}
,
E_{1}
,
B_{1}
and the class
of a fibre
F\sim A_{1}+B_{2}\sim A_{2}+B_{1}
.
Therefore
R\sim aA_{1}+eE_{1}+bB_{1}+fF
. The curve
R
is a section of
\pi:Z\rightarrow C
, thus
e=1
, since
F^{2}=F\cdot A_{1}=F\cdot B_{1}=0
and
F\cdot E_{1}=1
. One
has
A_{1}\cdot R=A^{\prime}_{1}\cdot E^{\prime}_{1}=1
,
thus
a=0
, since
A_{1}\cdot B_{1}=A_{1}\cdot F=0
,
A_{1}\cdot E_{1}=1
and
A_{1}^{2}=-1
. Therefore
R\sim E_{1}+bB_{1}+fF
, and

R^{2}=E_{1}^{2}+b^{2}B_{1}^{2}+f^{2}F^{2}+2bE_{1}\cdot B_{1}+2fE_{1}\cdot F+2bfB_{1}\cdot F=-1-b^{2}+2b+2f.

The number
b
is even, since the intersection number
R^{2}
is odd. One has

B_{1}\cdot R=B_{1}\cdot\left(E_{1}+bB_{1}+fF\right)=1-b.

Therefore
b=0
, since
b
is even and
B_{1}\cdot R\geqslant 0
. Thus
E^{\prime}_{1}\cdot B^{\prime}_{1}=R\cdot B_{1}=1
.

Now we see that for the set of
(-1)
-curves on
Z^{\prime}
we have

A^{\prime}_{1}\cdot E^{\prime}_{1}=E^{\prime}_{1}\cdot B^{\prime}_{1}=B^{\prime}_{1}\cdot A^{\prime}_{2}=A^{\prime}_{2}\cdot E^{\prime}_{2}=E^{\prime}_{2}\cdot B^{\prime}_{2}=B^{\prime}_{2}\cdot A^{\prime}_{1}=1.

The group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{L}\right)
has order
2
and pairwisely permutes
A^{\prime}_{1}
and
A^{\prime}_{2}
,
B^{\prime}_{1}
and
B^{\prime}_{2}
, and also
E^{\prime}_{1}
and
E^{\prime}_{2}
. Therefore
\rho\left(Z^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}}\right)=3
. By Proposition
4.5
(iv) the map
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is the contraction of the disjoint conjugate curves
E^{\prime}_{1}
and
E^{\prime}_{2}
. Each of those curves is not defined over
\mathbb{L}
, therefore
\rho\left(X^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}}\right)=2
. It means that
\mathbb{L}
is the splitting field of
X^{\prime}
. One has
\operatorname{Am}(X_{\mathbb{L}})=\operatorname{Am}(X^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}})
, since
X\approx X^{\prime}
. Therefore
X\cong X^{\prime}
by Theorem
2.9
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Note that Lemma
4.6
does not work if
X(\Bbbk)\neq\varnothing
. Consider a sequence of Sarkisov links

X\leftarrow Z\dashrightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime},

where the first link has type
\mathrm{I}
and the last link has type
\mathrm{III}
. The second link corresponds to the blowup of a
\Bbbk
-point
p
on a smooth fibre of
Z\rightarrow C
and the contraction of the proper transform of the fibre passing through
p
, and has type
\mathrm{II}
. Let us show that
X
and
X^{\prime}
are not isomorphic to each other. We use the notation established in the proof of Lemma
4.6
for the
(-1)
-curves on
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
. We want to show that
E^{\prime}_{1}\cdot B^{\prime}_{1}=0
.

Let
R
be the proper transform of
E^{\prime}_{1}
on
Z
. Then
R^{2}
is even, and
R\sim E_{1}+bB_{1}+fF
. Thus

R^{2}=E_{1}^{2}+b^{2}B_{1}^{2}+f^{2}F^{2}+2bE_{1}\cdot B_{1}+2fE_{1}\cdot F+2bfB_{1}\cdot F=-1-b^{2}+2b+2f.

The number
b
is odd, since the intersection number
R^{2}
is even. One has

B_{1}\cdot R=B_{1}\cdot\left(E_{1}+bB_{1}+fF\right)=1-b.

Therefore
b=1
, since
b
is odd and
B_{1}\cdot R\geqslant 0
. Thus
E^{\prime}_{1}\cdot B^{\prime}_{1}=R\cdot B_{1}=0
.

Now we see that for the set of
(-1)
-curves on
Z^{\prime}
we have

A^{\prime}_{1}\cdot E^{\prime}_{1}=E^{\prime}_{1}\cdot A^{\prime}_{2}=A^{\prime}_{2}\cdot B^{\prime}_{1}=B^{\prime}_{1}\cdot E^{\prime}_{2}=E^{\prime}_{2}\cdot B^{\prime}_{2}=B^{\prime}_{2}\cdot A^{\prime}_{1}=1.

The group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{L}\right)
has order
2
and pairwisely permutes
A^{\prime}_{1}
and
A^{\prime}_{2}
,
B^{\prime}_{1}
and
B^{\prime}_{2}
, and the
(-1)
-curves
E^{\prime}_{1}
and
E^{\prime}_{2}
are defined over
\mathbb{L}
. Therefore
\rho\left(Z^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}}\right)=3
. By Proposition
4.5
(iv) the map
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is the contraction of the disjoint conjugate curves
E^{\prime}_{1}
and
E^{\prime}_{2}
. Each of those curves is defined over
\mathbb{L}
, therefore
\rho\left(X^{\prime}_{\mathbb{L}}\right)=1
, that means that
\mathbb{L}
is not the splitting field of
X^{\prime}
. Hence
X
and
X^{\prime}
are not isomorphic to each other by Theorem
2.9
.

As a by-product of Lemma
4.6
we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
Z
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
6
admitting a structure of a relatively minimal conic bundle
Z\rightarrow C
, and
Z\dashrightarrow Z_{1}
be a Sarkisov link of type
\mathrm{II}
. Then
Z\cong Z_{1}
.

We decompose the proof into several lemmas that are interesting themselves.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
C
be a conic over a perfect field
\mathbb{L}
. Then the group
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
transitively acts on the set of points of degree
2
, such that the corresponding geometric points are defined over a quadratic extension
\mathbb{L}\left(\zeta\right)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\zeta
satisfies the equation
\zeta^{2}+a\zeta+b=0
, and
\bar{\zeta}
be the other root of this equation.

Let
\left(p_{1},p_{2}\right)
and
\left(q_{1},q_{2}\right)
be two pairs of conjugate points on
C
such that the points
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are defined over
\mathbb{L}\left(\zeta\right)
. Consider an embedding
C\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{L}}
. The two lines passing through
\left(p_{1},p_{2}\right)
and
\left(q_{1},q_{2}\right)
are defined over
\mathbb{L}
. Therefore we can choose coordinates in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{L}}
such that these lines are given by
x=0
and
y=0
, and the homogeneous coordinates of
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are

(0:\zeta:1),\qquad(0:\bar{\zeta}:1),\qquad(\zeta:0:1),\qquad(\bar{\zeta}:0:1)

respectively. Then
C
is given by the equation

x^{2}+y^{2}+axz+ayz+bz^{2}=\theta xy

for some
\theta\in\mathbb{L}
, and the automorphism
x\leftrightarrow y
acts on
C
and permutes the pairs
\left(p_{1},p_{2}\right)
and
\left(q_{1},q_{2}\right)
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
S
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
\rho(S)=1
over a perfect field
\Bbbk
. Then the group
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
transitively acts on the set of points of degree
2
, such that the corresponding geometric points are defined over a quadratic extension
\Bbbk\left(\zeta\right)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
one has
S\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
, where
\mathbb{L}
is the splitting field of
S
and
C
is a conic over
\mathbb{L}
. Let
\left(p_{1},p_{2}\right)
and
\left(q_{1},q_{2}\right)
be two pairs of conjugate points on
C
such that the points
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are defined over
\Bbbk\left(\zeta\right)
. Note that
\zeta\notin\mathbb{L}
since
S_{\mathbb{L}}
is pointless by Corollary
2.2
.

By Corollary
2.2
one has
S_{\mathbb{L}}\cong C\times C^{\prime}
, where
C^{\prime}
is the
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk\right)
-conjugate conic of
C
. Let
\pi_{1}:S_{\mathbb{L}}\rightarrow C
be the projection. One has
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}\left(S\right)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(C)
by Remark
2.11
,
and the group
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}\left(S\right)
faithfully acts on
C
, since
S\cong R_{\mathbb{L}/\Bbbk}C
. Consider
an element
g\in\operatorname{Aut}^{0}\left(S\right)
such that the induced action of
g
on
C
maps the pair
\left(\pi_{1}(p_{1}),\pi_{1}(p_{2})\right)
to the pair
\left(\pi_{1}(q_{1}),\pi_{1}(q_{2})\right)
(such
g
exists by Lemma
4.9
).

Note that the
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}\left(\zeta\right)/\Bbbk\right)
-orbit of the fibre
F=\pi_{1}^{-1}(\pi_{1}(q_{1}))
consists of the two fibres of
S_{\mathbb{L}}\rightarrow C
passing through
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, and the two fibres of
S_{\mathbb{L}}\rightarrow C^{\prime}
passing through
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
. These fibres have exactly four common geometric points: the conjugate points
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, and the two other conjugate points
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
. Note that
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are not defined over
\Bbbk\left(\zeta\right)
, since otherwise
F
is defined over
\Bbbk\left(\zeta\right)
and
S
splits over
\Bbbk\left(\zeta\right)\neq\mathbb{L}
. Therefore the pair
\left(g(p_{1}),g(p_{2})\right)
coincides with the pair
\left(q_{1},q_{2}\right)
, and we are done.
∎

Now we can prove Proposition

Now we prove Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
, and give an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
for the case of a perfect field.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
X
be a pointless del Pezzo surface of degree
8
with
\rho(X)=1
. Assume that a minimal surface
X^{\prime\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
X
. Then
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime\prime}
can be decomposed into a sequence of Sarkisov links:

X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow\ldots\dashrightarrow X_{n-1}\dashrightarrow X_{n}=X^{\prime\prime}.

By Lemma
4.1
any Sarkisov link corresponds to the blowup of
X
at a point of degree
6
,
4
or
2
. For the cases of degree
6
and
4
this link immediately gives a surface
X_{1}\cong X
by Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
respectively. For the case of degree
2
the sequence of links passes through some non-minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree
6
(see Proposition
4.5
(iii)) and terminates at a del Pezzo surface
X^{\prime}\cong X
by Lemma
4.6
. Therefore any minimal del Pezzo surface, obtained in a sequence of Sarkisov links starting from

, is isomorphic to

. In particular,
X^{\prime\prime}\cong X
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Note that by
[
Lied17
, Theorem 5.1]
there is an embedding
X\hookrightarrow P
, where
P
is a Severi–Brauer threefold such that the class
b(P)
lies in
\operatorname{Am}(X)\subset\operatorname{Br}(\Bbbk)
.

One can easily find a point of degree
4
on
X
, therefore
I(X)
divides
4
. Moreover,
I(P)
divides
I(X)
since any point on
X
is also a point on
P
. There is a point of degree
I(P)
on
P
by
[
Kol16
, Theorem 53]
.

Assume that
I(P)\leqslant 2
, then there is a point of degree
2
on
P
. The line
\overline{L}
passing through the pair of the corresponding points in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}=\overline{P}
is invariant under the action of the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\Bbbk\right)
. Therefore there is a smooth curve
L\subset P
of degree
1
. One has
L\cdot X=2
. Thus there is a point of degree
2
on
X
, and
I(X)=2
or
I(X)=1
.

Note that the classes
b(L)
and
b(P)
coincide in
\operatorname{Br}(\Bbbk)
by
[
Kol16
, Definition-Lemma 31]
. Moreover these classes coincide with the image of the class
-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}
in
\operatorname{Am}(X)
. Therefore if
\rho(X)=1
then any element of
\operatorname{Am}(X)
corresponds to a conic since
\operatorname{Am}(X)
is generated by the image of the class
-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}
. Moreover, in this case
X
is not birationally rigid since one can blow up a point of degree
2
and get a del Pezzo surface of degree
6
with a structure of a relatively minimal conic bundle. If
\rho(X)=2
then any element of
\operatorname{Am}(X)
corresponds to a conic, and
X
is not birationally rigid by Lemma
3.2
.

Now assume that
I(P)=4
, then
P
cannot contain a conic by Theorem
[
Kol16
, Theorem 53]
, and the element
b(P)\in\operatorname{Am}(X)
does not correspond to a conic. Moreover, one has
I(X)=4
, and there are no points of degree
2
on
X
since
I(P)
divides
I(X)
.

If
\rho(X)=2
then
X\cong C_{1}\times C_{2}
and
\operatorname{Am}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
. Any minimal surface
X^{\prime}
birationally equivalent to
X
must be a product
C^{\prime}_{1}\times C^{\prime}_{2}
of two non-isomorphic smooth non-trivial conics by Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
. Therefore
b(C^{\prime}_{1})
and
b(C^{\prime}_{2})
are not trivial and are not equal to
b(P)
. Thus the sets
\{b(C^{\prime}_{1}),b(C^{\prime}_{2})\}
and
\{b(C_{1}),b(C_{2})\}
coincide, and
X^{\prime}\cong X
. Hence
X
is birationally rigid.

If
\rho(X)=1
then the only possible Sarkisov link is described in Lemma
4.3
, and therefore
X
is birationally rigid.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
for the case of a perfect field.

Consider two pointless birationally equivalent quadric surfaces
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
. These surfaces are minimal. If
\rho(Q_{1})=1
then by Theorem
1.5
one has
Q_{1}\cong Q_{2}
.

If
\rho(Q_{1})=2
then by Lemma
2.1
one has
Q_{1}\cong C_{1}\times C_{1}
. By Theorem

any minimal surface birationally equivalent to

has Picard number

, and therefore
Q_{2}\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
by Lemma

. Thus by Theorem
1.5
(2) one has

.
∎

Weak limits of the J J -flow and the deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow on Kähler surfaces: boundary cases
Rei Murakami

Abstract
. We prove that if a pair of Kähler classes is J J -nef, the J J -flow on a compact Kähler surface converges to a weak solution of the Monge-Ampère equation in the sense of currents. We also establish the same convergence behavior for the deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow. The method is based on a property of a limit of viscosity subsolutions.

53C55, 53E30

1.
Introduction

The study of geometric flows has emerged as a powerful tool in understanding the complex structure and dynamics of Kähler manifolds. They provide a geometric process by which one can deform a Kähler manifold in a controlled manner, aiming to achieve a certain optimality. A critical question in the study is its long-time behavior, particularly its convergence properties, which are naturally related to a canonical structure on Kähler manifolds.
This paper delves into the analysis of the convergence behavior of two geometric flows, the
J
-flow and the deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow.

Let us consider an
n
-dimensional Kähler manifold
X
endowed with two Kähler forms,
\chi
and
\omega
. The
J
-flow, introduced by Donaldson
[
Don99
]
to study a certain moment map, is a PDE of
\chi
-psh functions given by

\begin{cases}\dot{\varphi_{t}}=c-\Lambda_{\chi_{t}}\omega,\\
\varphi|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0},\end{cases}

where
\dot{\varphi_{t}}
denotes the time derivative of
\varphi_{t}
, the constant
c
is determined by

c=\frac{\int_{X}n\,\omega\wedge\chi^{n-1}}{\int_{X}\chi^{n}},

and
\Lambda_{\chi_{t}}
denotes the trace operator with respect to
\chi_{t}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{t}
.
A stationary point of the
J
-flow satisfies the
J
-equation,

\Lambda_{\chi}\omega=c,

discovered by Xiuxiong Chen independently in
[
Chen00
]
from the perspective of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics.
As in
[
Song20
]
,
we use the following terminologies:

Definition 1.1
.

A pair of Kähler classes
([\chi],[\omega])
is called

(1)

J
-positive
if for any
p\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and any
p
-dimensional subvariety
V\subset X
,
we have

\frac{\int_{V}p\,\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1}}{\int_{V}\chi^{p}}<c.

(2)

J
-nef
if for any
p\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and any
p
-dimensional subvariety
V\subset X
,
we have

\frac{\int_{V}p\,\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1}}{\int_{V}\chi^{p}}\leq c.

The solvability of the
J
-equation is now known to be equivalent to the
J
-positivity of
([\chi],[\omega])

[
Chen21
,
DP21
,
Song20
]
. On the other hand,
[
Chen04
]
showed that the
J
-flow exists for all time, and
[
SW08
]
showed that the
J
-flow converges smoothly to the solution of the
J
-equation if it exists.

In this paper, we investigate the long-time behavior of the
J
-flow of a
J
-nef pair.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler surface. Let
\chi
and
\omega
be Kähler forms and assume
([\chi],[\omega])
is
J
-nef. Then the
J
-flow
\{\chi_{t}\}
converges, in the sense of currents, to the unique current
\chi_{\infty}
such that
c\chi_{\infty}-\omega
is a positive current and

(1.3)

\langle(c\chi_{\infty}-\omega)^{2}\rangle=\omega^{2},

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the non-pluripolar product.

Remark 1.4
.

•

In dimension 2, we can see that a pair
([\chi],[\omega])
is
J
-nef if and only if the class
[c\chi-\omega]
is nef and big (the bigness follows from
[c\chi-\omega]^{2}>0
and
[
DP04
, Theorem 0.5]
). Therefore, the existence and the uniqueness of the solution of (
1.3
) are proved in
[
BEGZ10
]
. What we prove here is that the
J
-flow converges to the solution.

•

Thoerem
1.2
solves one of the conjectures posed in
[
DMS23
]
(see below Conjecture 1.5 there) on Kähler surfaces in
J
-nef cases.

The theorem was proved in
[
FLSW14
]
when
[c\chi-\omega]
is additionally semipositive. In this case, they, moreover, proved that the
J
-flow converges smoothly outside a finite number of curves.

The strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is decomposed into 3 steps.

Step 1

L^{2}
-convergence of the time derivative.
We prove that the time derivative or equivalently
c-\Lambda_{\chi}\omega
converges to zero in
L^{2}(\omega)
. The key is the convexity of the
\mathcal{J}
-functional along the
J
-flow.

Step 2

Viscosity Subsolution.
We prove that the
L^{2}
-convergence implies that the limit is a viscosity subsolution in the sense of Definition
2.6
. The strategy has already appeared in
[
CS17
, Proposition 21]
. The key is a property of a limit in the viscosity theory (Proposition
2.7
).

Step 3

From subsolution to solution.
We observe that a viscosity subsolution is a subsolution as a measure. In dimension 2, the
J
-equation is equivalent to the Monge-Ampère equation, and the statement was claimed in
[
EGZ11
, Corollary 2.6]
. Since we could not find proof, we provide it in this paper (Lemma
2.13
). We use here the facts that the solution of (
1.3
) exists and that it has minimal singularities. With the converse inequality from
[
BEGZ10
, Proposition 1.20]
, we complete the proof.

We also apply the method to the deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills (dHYM for short) flow. Consider an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold
X
endowed with two smooth closed real
(1,1)
-forms
\alpha
and
\omega
, where
\omega
is Kähler. The dHYM flow, introduced in
[

]
, is defined by

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\dot{\varphi}_{t}=\cot{\theta_{\omega}(\alpha_{t})}-\cot{\theta_{0}},\\
\varphi|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0},\end{cases}

where
\theta_{\omega}(\chi)=\sum_{i}\mathrm{arccot}\,\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}
’s are eigenvalues of
\omega^{-1}\alpha
. The cotangent function

The constant

The dHYM flow is designed to study the dHYM equation

originally investigated by
[

]
, inspired by concepts in mirror symmetry as discussed in
[
LYZ01
]
.
In this paper, we assume

]
proved statements analogous to
[

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

be a compact Kähler surface. Let
\alpha

be closed real
(1,1)

\omega
is Kähler. Assume

Again, the bigness of

follows from

The theorem was proved in

The paper is organized as follows. In Subsection

.

2.
The
J
-flow

2.1.
The asymptotic slope of the
\mathcal{J}
-functional along the
J
-flow

In this subsection, we prove that the time derivative of the
J
-flow converges to zero in the
L^{2}
-norm if the pair is
J
-nef.
We start with the observation that the
\mathcal{J}
-functional, given as

\mathcal{J}(0)=0,\quad d\mathcal{J}(\varphi)(\psi)=\int_{X}\psi\,\left(\Lambda_{\chi_{\varphi}}\omega-c\right)\chi_{\varphi}^{n},

is convex along the
J
-flow.
The following lemma is key to proving it.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
Has19
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
F_{\chi,\omega}
be the operator defined by

F_{\chi,\omega}(\varphi)=(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,\omega)_{\chi}+(\partial\Lambda_{\chi}\omega,\bar{\partial}\varphi)_{\chi}.

Then, we have

\int_{X}\varphi\,F_{\chi,\omega}(\varphi)\,\chi^{n}\leq 0

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The
\mathcal{J}
-functional is convex along the
J
-flow.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

We can compute as

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{t})

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{2}\,\chi_{t}^{n}=-\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,\ddot{\varphi_{t}}\,\chi^{n}_{t}-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{2}\,n\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\wedge\chi^{n-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,\ddot{\varphi_{t}}\,\chi^{n}_{t}+\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,n\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\dot{\varphi}_{t}\wedge\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\wedge\chi^{n-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}_{t},\omega)_{\chi_{t}}\,\chi^{n}_{t}-\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,n\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\Lambda_{\chi_{t}}\omega\wedge\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\wedge\chi^{n-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,F_{\chi_{t},\omega}(\dot{\varphi}_{t})\,\chi^{n}_{t}.

Therefore, the claim follows by Lemma
2.1
.
∎

We are now in a position to establish the convergence in
L^{2}(\omega)
. The statement readily follows if
J
-nefness corresponds to the
\mathcal{J}
-functional being bounded from below, given the nature of the
J
-flow as a gradient flow of the
\mathcal{J}
-functional. While such a correspondence appears plausible, proof remains elusive. The best achievement to date is
[
SD20
, Theorem 23]
, which suffices for validating the statement.

Proposition 2.3
.

If a pair of Kähler classes
([\chi],[\omega])
is
J
-nef, then the time derivative of the
J
-flow converges to zero in
L^{2}(\omega)
.

Proof.

Note that

(2.4)

\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{2}\,\chi_{t}^{n}=-\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{t})\geq 0.

Since the
\mathcal{J}
-functional is convex along the
J
-flow by Lemma
2.2
, we know that there exists a constant
\mu\leq 0
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{t})=\mu.

We claim that
\mu=0.
Since the pair
([\chi],[\omega])
is
J
-nef, by
[
SD20
, Theorem 23]
, we see that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}\cap PSH(X,\chi)
,

\mathcal{J}(\varphi)\geq-\varepsilon\,\mathrm{E}^{\chi}_{\chi}(\varphi)-C_{\varepsilon},

where
\mathrm{E}^{\chi}_{\chi}
is defined by

\mathrm{E}^{\chi}_{\chi}(0)=0,\quad d\mathrm{E}^{\chi}_{\chi}(\varphi)(\psi)=\int_{X}\psi\,(n-\Lambda_{\chi_{\varphi}}\chi)\chi_{\varphi}^{n}.

Along the
J
-flow, we have

\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,\chi_{\varphi_{t}}^{n}=0.

The maximum principle for the
J
-flow implies that

|\dot{\varphi}_{t}|<C

for some constant C.
Thus, we get

\mathrm{E}^{\chi}_{\chi}(\varphi_{t})\leq A+C\,t

for some constant
C
, where
A=E^{\chi}_{\chi}(\varphi_{0})
.
Combining these estimates, we have

\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{t})\geq-C\varepsilon t-C_{\varepsilon}-A\varepsilon.

By dividing both hands by
t
and letting
t
tend to
\infty
, we have

\mu\geq-C\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
can be arbitrary and
\mu\leq 0
, we get
\mu=0.
Since the maximum principle implies that
|\dot{\varphi_{t}}|<C
for some constant
C
, we also know that

\chi_{t}>C\omega

for some constant
C>0
.
Therefore, by (
2.4
), the equality
\mu=0
confirms the statement.
∎

2.2.
Viscosity subsolutions

For positive definite Hermitian
n\times n
matrices
A
and
B
, we define

P_{B}(A)=\max_{k=1,\dots,n}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}\right),\quad Q_{B}(A)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}},

where
\lambda_{j}
’s are eigenvalues of the matrix
B^{-1}A
.
For Kähler forms
\chi
and
\omega
, we define

P_{\omega}(\chi)=P_{I_{n}}(\omega^{-1}\chi),\quad Q_{\omega}(\chi)=Q_{I_{n}}(\omega^{-1}\chi),

where
\omega^{-1}\chi
is seen as a matrix. By Proposition
2.3
, along the
J
-flow
\varphi_{t}
, we have

\|Q_{\omega}(\chi_{t})-c\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\rightarrow 0.

We prove that this convergence implies that the weak limit is a viscosity subsolution. We first recall the definition of the viscosity subsolution in a domain (see
[
CIL92
,
DDT19
]
).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain and
A
an
n\times n
-matrix-valued function which is positive definite and Hermitian for any
x\in\Omega
. An upper semicontinuous function
\varphi
is said to be a viscosity subsolution of
Q_{A}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)=c
at
x\in\Omega
, denoted by

Q_{A}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)(x)\leq_{v}c,

if for any upper test
q
of
\varphi
at
x\in\Omega
(i.e., a function
q
is
C^{2}
-function defined on a neighborhood
U
of
x
and satisfies
q\geq\varphi
with
q(x)=\varphi(x)
), we have

Q_{A(x)}\left((\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}q)(x)\right)\leq c.

An upper semicontinuous function
\varphi
is said to be a viscosity subsolution in
\Omega
, denoted by

Q_{A}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)\leq_{v}c,

if
Q_{A}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)(x)\leq_{v}c
at any point
x\in\Omega
.

We give the following definition of viscosity subsolutions on Kähler manifolds. For the Monge-Ampère equation, the definition has appeared in
[
EGZ11
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold. We say that a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
\chi
is a viscosity subsolution, denoted by

Q_{\omega}(\chi)\leq_{v}c,

if for any point
x\in X
and any coordinate neighborhood
U
of
x
, we have

Q_{\omega}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)(x)\leq_{v}c,

where
\varphi
is an upper semicontinuous local potential of
\chi
.

Consider
\psi_{t}:=\varphi_{t}-\sup{\varphi_{t}}
. By weak compactness, we have an
L^{1}
-limit
\psi_{\infty}
of
\psi_{t}
with
\sup{\psi_{\infty}}=0
if we take a subsequence. To prove
Q_{\omega}(\chi_{\infty})\leq_{v}c
, where
\chi_{\infty}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{\infty}
, the following proposition on the limit behavior in the viscosity theory is essential:

Proposition 2.7

(
[
CIL92
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain,
v
an upper semicontinuous function on
\Omega
,
z\in\Omega
, and
q
an upper test of
v
at
z
. Suppose also that
u_{n}
is a sequence of upper semicontinuous functions on
\Omega
such that

(i)

there exists
x_{n}\in\Omega
such that
(x_{n},u_{n}(x_{n}))\rightarrow(z,v(z))
,

(ii)

if
z_{n}\in\Omega
and
z_{n}\rightarrow x\in\Omega
, then
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}u_{n}(z_{n})\leq v(x)
.

Then there exist
\hat{x}_{n}\in\Omega
and upper tests of
u_{n}
at
\hat{x}_{n}
denoted by
q_{n}
such that

(\hat{x}_{n},u_{n}(\hat{x}_{n}),Dq_{n}(\hat{x}_{n}),D^{2}q_{n}(\hat{x}_{n}))\rightarrow(z,v(z),Dq(z),D^{2}q(z)).

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose that a sequence of Kähler forms
\chi_{k}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\psi_{k}\rightarrow\psi_{\infty}\,\text{in $L^{1}(\omega)$ and}\ \|Q_{\omega}(\chi_{k})-c\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\rightarrow 0.

Then, we have

Q_{\omega}(\chi_{\infty})\leq_{v}c,

where
\chi_{\infty}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{\infty}
.

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

We follow the proof of
[
CS17
, Proposition 21]
.
Fix a point
p\in X
and take a coordinate neighborhood
U
of
p
. Let
\omega_{0}
be a Kähler form on some neighborhood
U_{0}\subset U
with constant coefficients such that
\omega_{0}\leq\omega
. Denote by
f
an upper semicontinuous local potential of
\chi
on
U_{0}
. Take a standard mollifier
\rho
, i.e., a function
\rho
satisfies

\rho\geq 0,\ \rho(w)=0\text{ for $|w|>1$},\ \text{and }\int\rho(w)dw=1.

Define
u_{k}=f+\psi_{k}
and denote by
u^{\delta}_{k}
the regularization of
u_{k}
, i.e.,

u^{\delta}_{k}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}u_{k}(z-\delta w)\rho(w)dw.

The function
u^{\delta}_{k}
is defined on
U_{0,\delta}=\{z\in U_{0}\mid d(z,\partial U_{0})>\delta\}.

We see that for
z\in U_{0,\delta}
,

(2.9)

\begin{split}Q_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u^{\delta}_{k})(z)&=Q_{I_{n}}\left(\omega^{-1}_{0}(z)\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{k}(z-\delta w)\rho(w)dw\right)\\
&=Q_{I_{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\omega^{-1}_{0}\chi_{k})(z-\delta w)\rho(w)dw\right)\\
&\leq Q_{I_{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\omega^{-1}\chi_{k})(z-\delta w)\rho(w)dw\right)\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left(Q_{I_{n}}(\omega^{-1}\chi_{k})\right)(z-\delta w)\rho(w)dw\\
&\leq\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\left(Q_{I_{n}}(\omega^{-1}\chi_{k})\right)(z-\delta w)-c\right|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}\|\rho\|_{2}+c\\
&\leq C_{\delta}\|Q_{\omega}(\chi_{k})-c\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+c,\end{split}

where
C_{\delta}
is a constant depending on
\delta
and
B_{1}
is a unit ball with a center zero. In the second equality, we used the fact that
\omega_{0}
has constant coefficients. In the first inequality, we used that
\omega_{0}\leq\omega
. In the second inequality, we used that
Q_{I_{n}}
is convex. When
k
tends to
\infty
, the last line tends to
c
. We claim that
Q_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u^{\delta}_{\infty})\leq_{v}c
on
U_{0,\delta}
. Indeed, note that
u^{\delta}_{k}
converges to
u^{\delta}_{\infty}
uniformly in
U_{0,\delta}
. Therefore, a sequence
\{u^{\delta}_{k}\}
and the limit
u^{\delta}_{\infty}
satisfy the conditions in Proposition
2.7
. Choose a point
z\in U_{0,\delta}
and an upper test
q
of
u^{\delta}_{\infty}
at
z
. By Proposition
2.7
, there exist points
\hat{x}_{k}
and upper tests
q_{k}
of
u^{\delta}_{k}
at them such that the assertion satisfies. It implies

Q_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}q_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\rightarrow Q_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}q)(z)\leq c,

which yields
Q_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u^{\delta}_{\infty})\leq_{v}c
on
U_{0,\delta}
.
Next, we let
\delta
tend to zero. Since
u_{\infty}=f+\psi_{\infty}
is psh, a sequence
\{u^{\delta}_{\infty}\}
is decreasing. This property implies that the sequence satisfies the conditions in Proposition
2.7
.
Indeed, condition
(i)
is obvious by taking the same point as a sequence. For condition

, note that for any fixed
\delta_{0}>0
, the function

is upper semicontinuous, so for any

and

satisfying

\limsup{u^{\delta}_{\infty}}(z_{\delta})\leq\limsup{u^{\delta_{0}}_{\infty}}(z_{\delta})\leq u^{\delta_{0}}_{\infty}(x).

Letting

tend to zero, we get condition

. Therefore we can apply Proposition

again to get

Q_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{\infty})\leq_{v}c\ \text{on $U_{0}$}.

Especially at point
p
, for any upper test

of
u_{\infty}
at
p
, we have

Q_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}q)(p)\leq c.

By taking a sequence of Kähler forms

with constant coefficients such that
\omega_{i}\leq\omega
and
\omega_{i}(p)\rightarrow\omega(p)
(by taking

Q_{\omega}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}q)(p)\leq c.\qed

Remark 2.10
.

The arguments in the proof clarify that the condition on the positivity of currents introduced in
[
Chen21
, Definition 3.3]
implies the condition of viscosity subsolutions. Namely, if a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
\chi
is in
\bar{\Gamma}_{\omega,c}
, then

P_{\omega}(\chi)\leq_{v}c.
Here, a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
\chi
is in
\bar{\Gamma}_{\omega,c}
if for any open subset
O
of any coordinate neighborhood and any Kähler form
\omega_{0}
on
O
with constant coefficients such that
\omega_{0}\leq\chi
, we have

P_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{\delta})\leq c

for any
\delta>0
,
where
\varphi
is a local potential of
\chi
and
\varphi_{\delta}
is its regularization via convolution.
By standard techniques in the viscosity theory, we can prove that they are equivalent.

Proposition 2.11
.

A closed positive
(1,1)
-current
\chi
satisfies
P_{\omega}(\chi)\leq_{v}c
if and only if
\chi\in\bar{\Gamma}_{\omega,c}
.

Proof.

We follow the arguments in
[
EGZ11
, Proposition 1.11 and Theorem 1.9]
. Assume
P_{\omega}(\chi)\leq_{v}c
.
Take an open subset
O
of a coordinate neighborhood and a Kähler form
\omega_{0}
with constant coefficients on
O
such that
\omega_{0}\leq\omega
. Note that an upper test of a psh function is psh as in the proof of
[
EGZ11
, Proposition 1.3]
. Thus, we have
P_{\omega_{0}}(\chi)\leq_{v}c
on
O
. Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be upper semicontinuous local potentials of
\chi
and
\omega
respectively. Define
\varphi_{k}:=\sup\{\varphi,a\psi-k\}
, where
k
is a positive integer and
a
is a real number large enough to satisfy
P_{\omega}(a\omega)\leq c
. Since the supremum of a finite number of viscosity subsolutions is a viscosity subsolution, we have
P_{\omega_{0}}(\varphi_{k})\leq_{v}c
on
O
. Denote by
\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}
the sup convolution of
\varphi_{k}

\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\sup\left\{\varphi_{k}(y)-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}y\in O\right\}

is bounded. By this definition of

has constant coefficients, from the proof of

on

Note that

is semiconvex since the supremum of semiconvex functions is semiconvex. By Aleksandrov’s theorem,
\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}
is twice differentiable almost everywhere.
Thus, by the convexity of

, for

\displaystyle P_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon})_{\delta})(z)

\displaystyle=P_{\omega_{0}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon})(z-\delta w)\rho(w)dw\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}}P_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon})(z-\delta w)\rho(w)dw

\displaystyle\leq c.

Since a sequence

decreases to

∎

We expect that the viscosity subsolution obtained in Subsection

Lemma 2.13

Define

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{J}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{J}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=2\sin{\theta_{0}}\left(\int_{X}n\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\dot{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\biggl.\wedge\left(\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_{\varphi}+\sqrt{-1}\omega)^{n-1}-\cot{\theta_{\omega}(\alpha_{\varphi})}\mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{\varphi}+\sqrt{-1}\omega)^{n-1}\right)\biggr).

is an upper semicontinuous local potential of
\alpha_{\varphi_{k}}+\varepsilon\omega
and other notations are analogous to (
2.9
). By (
3.9
), the same observation as in the proof of Proposition

∎

The dHYM version of Proposition
2.11
is the following:

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler form. Then a current
\alpha
satisfies
P_{\omega}(\alpha)\leq_{v}-\cot{\theta_{0}}
if and only if for any
\varepsilon>0
, any open subset of a coordinate neighborhood
O
, and any Kähler form
\omega_{0}
with constant coefficients on

which is sufficiently close to
\omega

P_{\omega_{0}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})\leq_{v}-\cot{\theta_{0}},

where
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is a local potential of
\alpha+\varepsilon\omega
and
\varphi^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}

The proof is identical to that of Proposition
2.11
, and is therefore omitted.

The arguments in Subsection

Explicit Relations of Some Variants of Convoluted Multiple Zeta Values
Ce Xu and Jianqiang Zhao[1mm] a. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241000, PRCb. Department of Mathematics, The Bishop’s School, La Jolla, CA 92037, USA

Abstract




Abstract.
Kaneko and Yamamoto introduced a convoluted variant of multiple zeta values (MVZs) around 2016. In this paper, we will first establish some explicit formulas involving these values and their alternating version by using iterated integrals, which enable us to derive some explicit relations of the multiple polylogarithm (MPL) functions. Next, we define convoluted multiple
t
-values and multiple mixed values (MMVs) as level two analogs of convoluted MZVs, and, similar to convoluted MZVs, use iterated integrals to find some relations of these level two analogs. We will then consider the parametric MPLs and the parametric multiple harmonic (star) sums, and extend the Kaneko-Yamamoto’s “integral-series” identity of MZVs to MPLs and MMVs. Finally, we will study multiple integrals of MPLs and MMVs by generalizing Yamamoto’s graphical representations to multiple-labeled posets.

Keywords
: Multiple zeta values, multiple polylogarithm, multiple mixed values, iterated integrals, integral-series relation, posets.

11M32, 11M99, 11G55, 06A11.

A finite sequence

For a composition

In general, let

({{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}})

More generally, let
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}

Formulas of Some Iterated Integrals

The theory iterated integrals was developed first by K.T. Chen in the 1960’s
[
3
,
4
]
. It has played important roles in the study of algebraic topology and algebraic geometry in past half century. Its simplest form over
\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f_{p}(t)dtf_{p-1}(t)dt\cdots f_{1}(t)dt:=\displaystyle\int\limits_{a<t_{p}<\cdots<t_{1}<b}f_{p}(t_{p})f_{p-1}(t_{p-1})\cdots f_{1}(t_{1})dt_{1}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{p}

which can be easily extended to iterated path integrals over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. From
[
13

\displaystyle\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}\left(x_{1},\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}},\dotsc,\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}}{x_{r-1}}\right)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}\,dt}{1-x_{r}t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}\,dt}{1-x_{1}t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}.

(2.5)

If changing

to
xx_{j}
, then

\displaystyle\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}\left(xx_{1},\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}},\dotsc,\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}}{x_{r-1}}\right)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}\,dt}{1-x_{r}t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}dt}{1-x_{1}t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}.

(2.6)

Setting

in (

) we find that the single-variable MPL

\displaystyle\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}(x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}.

(2.7)

To save space, for any composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and

Some Variants of Convoluted MZVs

3.1
Kaneko-Yamamoto Type Convoluted MZVs

Kaneko and Yamamoto initiated the study of the convoluted MZVs defined by

\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}\circledast{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}^{\star})=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})\zeta^{\star}_{n}(l_{2},\ldots,l_{s})}{n^{k_{1}+l_{1}}}.

4
Parametric Multiple Polylogarithms

In this section, we define the parametric MPL (star) function and parametric multiple harmonic (star) sum, and establish some new identities. In particular, we extend the results of Sakugawa-Seki
[
10
, Thms. 2.10 and 2.13]
.

Definition 4.1
.

For any complex parameter
a
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, and complex variables
{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r})\in{\Delta}^{r}
, we define the
parametric multiple harmonic sum
and
parametric multiple harmonic star sum
with
r
-variable by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}\geq 1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{x_{j}^{n_{j}+a}}{(n_{j}+a)^{k_{j}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq 1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{x_{j}^{n_{j}+a}}{(n_{j}+a)^{k_{j}}},

respectively, where if
n<k
then
{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a):=0
and
\zeta_{n}(\emptyset;\emptyset;a)=\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\emptyset;\emptyset;a):=1
.
For
(k_{1},x_{1})\neq(1,1)
, we define the
parametric multiple polylogarithm function
and
parametric multiple polylogarithm star function
with
r
-variable by

\displaystyle\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a),\quad\text{and}\quad\Li^{\star}_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a).

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
10
, Thm. 2.11]
)
For any composition
{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r})\in{\Delta}^{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\zeta_{n}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj})\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1})=0,

(4.32)

where
\zeta_{n}(\emptyset;\emptyset)=\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\emptyset;\emptyset):=1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any composition
{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r})\in{\Delta}^{r}
and any
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{x_{j}^{n_{j}+l}}{(n_{j}+l)^{k_{j}}}=(-1)^{r}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\zeta_{n+l}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj})\zeta^{\star}_{l}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1}).

(4.33)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the depth
r
. When the depth is
1
, the left hand side is
\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{m+l}}{(m+l)^{k_{1}}}
and the right-hand side is
\zeta_{n+l}(k_{1};x_{1})-\zeta^{\star}_{l}(k_{1};x_{1})=\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{n+l}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{m}}{m^{k_{1}}}=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{m+l}}{(m+l)^{k_{1}}}
. Hence the case
r=1
is proved.

Assume
r\geq 2
and the theorem holds for lower depths. By inductive assumption we have

LHS of (
4.33
)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n_{1}+l}}{(n_{1}+l)^{k_{1}}}(-1)^{r-1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{j}\zeta_{n_{1}+l-1}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0pt2,j+1};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0pt2,j+1})\zeta^{\star}_{l}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+2};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+2})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n_{1}+l}}{(n_{1}+l)^{k_{1}}}\zeta_{n_{1}+l-1}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0pt2,j};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0pt2,j})\right)\zeta^{\star}_{l}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\left(\zeta_{n+l}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj})-\zeta_{l}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj})\right)\zeta^{\star}_{l}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\zeta_{n+l}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj})\zeta^{\star}_{l}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1}),

where we used the Lemma
4.1
in the last step. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Proof.

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}};a)+(-1)^{r}\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}};a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}(-1)^{j}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{r}>n_{1}>0}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}+a}}{(n_{1}+a)^{k_{1}}}\zeta_{n_{1}-1}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0pt2,j};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0pt2,j};a)\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}^{n_{r}+a}}{(n_{r}+a)^{k_{r}}}\zeta^{\star}_{n_{r}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1,r-1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1,r-1};a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{j}\displaystyle\sum_{n_{r}=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}^{n_{r}+a}}{(n_{r}+a)^{k_{r}}}\zeta_{n_{r}-1}(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj};a)\zeta^{\star}_{n_{r}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1,r-1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{\hskip-2.0ptj+1,r-1};a)

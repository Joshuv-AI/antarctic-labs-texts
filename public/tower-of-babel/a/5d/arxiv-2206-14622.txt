The structuring effect of a Gottlieb element on the Sullivan model of a space
Gregory Lupton, Samuel Bruce Smith

Abstract
. We show a Gottlieb element in the rational homotopy of a simply connected space X X implies a structural result for the Sullivan minimal model, with different results depending on parity. In the even-degree case, we prove a rational Gottlieb element is a terminal homotopy element. This fact allows us to complete an argument of Dupont to prove an even-degree Gottlieb element gives a free factor in the rational cohomology of a formal space of finite type. We apply the odd-degree result to affirm a special case of the 2 ​ N 2N -conjecture on Gottlieb elements of a finite complex. We combine our results to make a contribution to the realization problem for the classifying space B ​ aut 1 ​ ( X ) B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X) . We prove a simply connected space X X satisfying B ​ aut 1 ​ ( X ℚ ) ≃ S ℚ 2 ​ n B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{2n} must have infinite-dimensional rational homotopy and vanishing rational Gottlieb elements above degree 2 ​ n − 1 2n-1 for n = 1 , 2 , 3 . n=1,2,3.

2.
Gottlieb Elements and Basis Change for Sullivan Models

In this section, we observe that a Gottlieb element
v\in V^{n}
induces a
change of basis isomorphism
for the Sullivan minimal model
(\land V,d)
. The idea is as follows. Start with an automorphism
\phi\colon\land V\to\land V
of graded algebras. Then
\phi
induces a new differential
d^{\prime}
on
\land V
given by
d^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}\circ d\circ\phi
. The map

\phi\colon(\land V,d^{\prime})\to(\land V,d).

is, tautologically, a DG algebra isomorphism. Also note that, since
d
is decomposable,
d^{\prime}
is decomposable as well and
\phi
is an isomorphism of minimal DG algebras.

A derivation
\theta
of
(\land V,d)
induces a derivation
\theta^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}\circ\theta\circ\phi
of
\land V
. If
\theta
is a derivation cycle, i.e.,
[d,\theta]=0,
then we see

[d^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}]=\big[\phi^{-1}\circ d\circ\phi,\phi^{-1}\circ\theta\circ\phi\big]=\phi^{-1}\circ[d,\theta]\circ\phi=0.

We focus first on the structuring effect of an odd-degree Gottlieb element
x\in V^{2n+1}.
Let
\theta
be of degree
2n+1
and satisfy
[d,\theta]=0
and

\theta(x)=1
. Write
V=\langle x\rangle\oplus W
for
W\subseteq V
a complementary subspace to
\langle x\rangle
in
V
. Define a linear map
\phi\colon V\to\land V
by setting
\phi(x)=x
and, for each
v\in W
,

(2.1)

\phi(v)=v-x\theta(v).

Extend multiplicatively to a map of algebras
\phi\colon\land V\to\land V
. It is easy to see that
\phi
is an automorphism. Notice further that

\displaystyle\phi(v)\phi(v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\big(v-x\theta(v)\big)\big(v^{\prime}-x\theta(v^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=vv^{\prime}-x\theta(v)v^{\prime}-(-1)^{|v|}\,xv\theta(v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=vv^{\prime}-x\theta(vv^{\prime})

It follows that
\phi(\chi)=\chi-x\theta(\chi)
for a general
\chi\in\land W
. Also note that, again for a general
\chi\in\land W
, we have

\phi(x\chi)=\phi(x)\phi(\chi)=x\big(\chi-x\theta(\chi)\big)=x\chi
.
As above, define
d^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}\circ d\circ\phi
and
\theta^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}\circ\theta\circ\phi
so that
[d^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}]=0
. We prove

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\land V,d)
be a Sullivan minimal model with a Gottlieb element
x\in V^{2n+1}
. Let
(\land V,d^{\prime})
be as constructed above and
\theta^{\prime}
the induced derivation cycle of degree
2n+1
of the model
(\land V,d^{\prime})
. Write
V=\langle x\rangle\oplus W
. Then:

(a)

\theta^{\prime}(x)=1
and
d^{\prime}(x)=dx

(b)

For
\chi\in\land W
, we have
\theta^{\prime}(\chi)=x\,\lambda(\chi)\hbox{\, for some \, }\lambda\in\mathrm{Der}_{4n+2}(\land W)

(c)

For
\chi\in\land W
,

d^{\prime}(\chi)=-\theta^{\prime}(\chi)\,dx+d^{\prime}_{0}(\chi)=-x\lambda(\chi)dx+d^{\prime}_{0}(\chi)

for some
d^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathrm{Der}_{-1}(\land W)
and
\lambda\in\mathrm{Der}_{4n+2}(\land W)
as in (b).

Proof.

Part (a) is immediate since
\phi(x)=x
and further
\phi
is the identity on elements of degree
\leq 2n
. For (b) and (c), we begin with a general observation. Suppose that
\psi\colon\land V\to\land V
is a derivation. Let
\chi\in\land W
.
If we define linear maps on
\land W
by

\psi(\chi)=x\psi_{1}(\chi)+\psi_{0}(\chi),

then both
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{0}
are derivations of
\land W
(of different degrees). This is easy to check, as follows. For a product of terms
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\land W
, write

\displaystyle\psi(\chi\chi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\psi(\chi)\chi^{\prime}+(-1)^{|\psi||\chi|}\chi\psi(\chi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\big(x\,\psi_{1}(\chi)+\psi_{0}(\chi)\big)\chi^{\prime}+(-1)^{|\psi||\chi|}\chi\big(x\,\psi_{1}(\chi^{\prime})+\psi_{0}(\chi^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=x\big(\psi_{1}(\chi)\chi^{\prime}+(-1)^{|\psi||\chi|+|\chi|}\chi\psi_{1}(\chi^{\prime})\big)+\big(\psi_{0}(\chi)\chi^{\prime}+(-1)^{|\psi||\chi|}\chi\psi_{0}(\chi^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=x\psi_{1}(\chi\chi^{\prime})+\psi_{0}(\chi\chi^{\prime}).

In the last line, we used that
\psi_{0}
has the same degree as
\psi
, whereas the degree of
\psi_{1}
is of opposite parity to the degree of
\psi
, so we have

|\psi|+1\equiv|\psi_{0}|\mod 2
.

Applying this decomposition to
\theta
gives

(2.2)

\theta(\chi)=x\,\theta_{1}(\chi)+\theta_{0}(\chi).

As just observed, this defines derivations
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
of
\land W
with
\theta_{0}
of degree
2n+1
and
\theta_{1}
of degree
4n+2
. The isomorphism
\phi
is given by

(2.3)

Furthermore, as already noted, we have
\phi(x\chi)=x\chi
, and hence also
\phi^{-1}(x\chi)=x\chi
. Applying
\phi^{-1}
to (

We use the notation from (

), (

(b) We calculate:

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(\chi)=\phi^{-1}\circ\theta\circ\phi(\chi)

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}\circ\theta\big(\chi-x\,\theta_{0}(\chi)\big)

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}\big(x\,\theta_{1}(\chi)+\theta_{0}(\chi)-1\,\theta_{0}(\chi)+x\,\theta_{0}\circ\theta_{0}(\chi)\big)

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}\big(x\,\big(\theta_{1}(\chi)+\theta_{0}\circ\theta_{0}(\chi)\big)\big)

\displaystyle=x\,\big(\theta_{1}(\chi)+\theta_{0}\circ\theta_{0}(\chi)\big),

with the last line following from the observation made leading into (

. So

is a derivation of degree

. We have

(c) As in (

), we write

(2.5)

which defines

and

as derivations of

We now compute:

\displaystyle\theta\circ d(\chi)

\displaystyle=\theta\big(x\,d_{1}(\chi)+d_{0}(\chi)\big)

\displaystyle=1\cdot d_{1}(\chi)-x\,\big(x\,\theta_{1}(d_{1}(\chi))+\theta_{0}(d_{1}(\chi))\big)+x\,\theta_{1}(d_{0}(\chi))+\theta_{0}(d_{0}(\chi))

Adding these two identities, whose left-hand sides sum to zero, gives us the following two identities amongst derivations of

Now we calculate:

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(\chi)

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}\circ d\circ\phi(\chi)=\phi^{-1}\circ d\big(\chi-x\,\theta_{0}(\chi)\big)

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}\big(x\,d_{1}(\chi)+d_{0}(\chi)-dx\,\theta_{0}(\chi)+x\,d_{0}\circ\theta_{0}(\chi)\big)

\displaystyle=x\,d_{1}(\chi)+\big(d_{0}(\chi)+x\,\theta_{0}\circ d_{0}(\chi)\big)

4.
The realization problem for

The problem of realizing

as a classifying space, of producing a rational space

with

is a Sullivan minimal model

giving a Lie algebra isomorphism:

Here

(\land V,d)
admitting a non-bounding derivation cycle

such that

is non-bounding, as well, and that these two classes span the homology of derivations:

Write

.
We make one further hypothesis. We assume

\theta_{b}(y_{0})=1\hbox{\, for some \, }y_{0}\in V^{4n-2}.

Note that the hypothesis (

) corresponds to the case that

5.
The Universal Fibration and the Realization Problem

Question
1.3
naturally leads to consideration of the universal fibration. For observe that
Y_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
for some space
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
implies the existence of a fibration

X_{\mathbb{Q}}\to E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\to B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq Y_{\mathbb{Q}}

that is universal for fibrations with fibre
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
. We consider this expanded view here. Write
\partial_{\infty}\colon\Omega B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\to X_{\mathbb{Q}}
for the connecting homomorphism. We recall
\alpha\in\pi_{n}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
is a Gottlieb element if and only if
\alpha=(\partial_{\infty})_{\sharp}(\beta)
for some
\beta\in\pi_{n+1}(B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}))

[
7
, Th.2.6]
.

Suppose now we have such universal fibration for
Y=S^{2}
. That is, suppose we have a simply connected space
X
with
B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq S^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. By Theorem
4
,
X
has vanishing rational Gottlieb elements. By Gottlieb’s result, mentioned above,
p_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\colon E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\to B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
induces a surjection on rational homotopy groups. We can also see this directly from the long-exact homotopy sequence of the universal fibration.
As a consequence, we deduce:

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
X
is a simply connected and satisfies
B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
. Then the universal fibration
X_{\mathbb{Q}}\to E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\to B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
with fibre
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
has a section.

Proof.

We obtain a section of
p_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\colon E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\to B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
as the lift of the identity homotopy class
S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}\simeq B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\to B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
to
E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
∎

Proposition
5.1
gives a strong restriction on a rational space
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying
B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq S^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Namely,
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
must have the property that every rational fibration
X_{\mathbb{Q}}\to E_{\mathbb{Q}}\to B_{\mathbb{Q}}
has a section. We give an example to show that it is possible to have a non-trivial, sectioned fibration with base
S^{2}
and fibre
X
such that
X
has no rational Gottlieb elements.

Example 5.2
.

Let
p\colon S^{2}\vee S^{2}\to S^{2}
denote the projection onto the first summand. Convert
p
into a fibration, to obtain a fibre sequence

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\textstyle{S^{2}\vee S^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{S^{2}.}

Then
p
admits the obvious section
i_{1}\colon S^{2}\to S^{2}\vee S^{2}
which is just inclusion into the first summand. Also, the fibration is not trivial, even after rationalization, since
H^{*}(S^{2}\vee S^{2};{\mathbb{Q}})
does not split as a tensor product
H^{*}(S^{2};{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})
. Next, note that
X
has infinitely many non-zero rational homotopy groups since, from the long exact sequence in rational homotopy, we have
\pi_{i}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\pi_{i}((S^{2}\vee S^{2})_{\mathbb{Q}})
for
i\geq 4
, and
S^{2}\vee S^{2}
has infintely many non-zero rational homotopy groups.
To see that
X
has no rational Gottlieb elements, we apply the mapping theorem for rational L.S. category. Note that that the fibre inclusion
j\colon X\to S^{2}\vee S^{2}
induces an injection of rational homotopy groups. Thus, implies
\mathrm{cat}_{0}(X)\leq\mathrm{cat}_{0}(S^{2}\vee S^{2})=1

[
5
, Th.28.6]
. Now
X
is
\pi
-infinite and so it follows that
X
has the rational homotopy type of a wedge of at least two spheres. Thus, the homotopy Lie algebra
\pi_{*}(\Omega X_{\mathbb{Q}})
is a free Lie algebra on at least two generators. It follows directly that
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
has no nontrivial Gottlieb elements; for recall a Gottlieb element
\alpha\in\pi_{n}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
has vanishing Whitehead products
[\alpha,\beta]=0
for all
\beta\in\pi_{q}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}).

The rationalization of the fibration of Example
5.2
cannot be a universal fibration. We argue as follows. First by a Serre spectral sequence argument,
H^{i}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\not=0
for
i\geq 2
. Since
\mathrm{cat}_{0}(X)=1
,
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an infinite wedge of spheres. We prove:

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
X
has the rational homotopy type of a wedge of infinitely many spheres. Then
B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
is
\pi
-infinite.

Proof.

We note that Gatsinzi has proved essentially this fact for
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
a finite wedge (cf.
[
6
]
).
We use the model for
B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
described in
[
15
, VII.2]
that derives from a Quillen model of

. Since
X
is a wedge of spheres, it has Quillen model a free Lie algebra
{\mathbb{L}}(V)
with zero differential and

a graded vector space of generators isomorphic to the (de-suspension of the) reduced homology of

. We assume this homology is infinite-dimensional, and so write
V=\{v_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with the generators

written in order of increasing degree: thus
i<j\implies|v_{i}|\leq|v_{j}|
. Now the model for

is a DG Lie algebra written as

(s{\mathbb{L}}(V)\oplus\mathrm{Der}\ \!{\mathbb{L}}(V),\delta),

where

denotes the abelian Lie algebra on the vector space

with degrees shifted up by one,

denotes the usual Lie algebra of derivations that increase degree, except that in degree

restricts to the usual differential on

which, since we assume

, where we have
\delta(sx)=\mathrm{ad}(x)\in\mathrm{Der}\ \!{\mathbb{L}}(V)

. See
[
15

for details. Suppose that

is the first generator of degree strictly greater than that of

. Then we define an infinite sequence of non-zero derivations

for

, which therefore is non-zero in infinitely many degrees.
∎

Theorem 5.4

Suppose

\theta
and
\beta
are both
D
-cycles, each term in this (locally finite) sum is a derivation that is a
D
-cycle. Hence,
\widehat{\beta}
is a

D
-cycle. But observe that we have

[\theta,\widehat{\beta}]=([\theta,\beta]-1\cdot[\theta,\beta])+(-y\,\big[\theta,[\theta,\beta]\big]+y\,\big[\theta,[\theta,\beta]\big])+\cdots=0.

Then our assumption that
\theta
is not a zero divisor implies that
\widehat{\beta}=D(\eta)
for some
\eta\in\mathrm{Der}_{q+1}(\land V)
. However, we have
\widehat{\beta}=\beta_{0}
. This follows by writing out terms in
\widehat{\beta}(\chi)
, as

\displaystyle\widehat{\beta}(\chi)

\displaystyle=\beta(\chi)-y\,[\theta,\beta](\chi)+\cdots

\displaystyle=\beta(\chi)-y\,\theta\circ\beta(\chi)+\cdots+\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\,\theta^{n}\circ\beta(\chi)+\cdots

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{r+1}(\chi)+y\alpha_{r+2}(\chi)+y^{2}\alpha_{r+3}(\chi)+\cdots)

\displaystyle\hbox{\hskip 54.2025pt}-y\,\theta(\alpha_{r+1}(\chi)+y\alpha_{r+2}(\chi)+y^{2}\alpha_{r+3}(\chi)+\cdots)

\displaystyle\hbox{\hskip 54.2025pt}+\cdots+\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\,\theta^{n}(\alpha_{r+1}(\chi)+y\alpha_{r+2}(\chi)+y^{2}\alpha_{r+3}(\chi)+\cdots)+\cdots

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{r+1}(\chi)+y\alpha_{r+2}(\chi)+y^{2}\alpha_{r+3}(\chi)+\cdots)

\displaystyle\hbox{\hskip 54.2025pt}-(y\alpha_{r+2}(\chi)+2y^{2}\alpha_{r+3}(\chi)+3y^{3}\alpha_{r+4}(\chi)+\cdots)

\displaystyle\hbox{\hskip 54.2025pt}+\cdots+(y^{n}\alpha_{r+1+n}(\chi)+{{n+1}\choose{n}}y^{n+1}\,\alpha_{r+1+n+1}(\chi)+\cdots)+\cdots.

Notice above, in passing from the first to the second lines, we replaced
\mathrm{ad}^{n}(\theta)(\beta)(\chi)
with
\theta^{n}\circ\beta(\chi)
since
\theta(\chi)=0
.
A careful tallying of the terms in this last expression reveals that it consists of a sum of terms
y^{n}\alpha_{r+1+n}(\chi)
for
n\geq 0
and, for
n\geq 1
, the coefficient of

y^{n}\alpha_{r+1+n}(\chi)
is the alternating sum

1-n+{{n}\choose{2}}+\cdots+(-1)^{t}\,{{n}\choose{t}}+\cdots+(-1)^{n}.

But this is zero, since it is the value of
(1-x)^{n}
when
x=1
. That is, we have

D(\eta)(\chi)=\widehat{\beta}(\chi)=\alpha_{r+1}(\chi).

But this means that we have
D(\eta)=\alpha_{r+1}
, as derivations of
\land W
, and setting
\eta=\eta_{r}
completes the inductive step. Notice that
\alpha_{r}
is of degree

|\alpha_{r}|=|\alpha|-2mr,

and
|\eta_{r}|=|\alpha_{r}|+1
, so for a fixed
\chi
only finitely many
\eta_{r}(\chi)
can be non-zero, for degree reasons. It follows from the induction that,
with

\eta=\sum_{i\geq 0}\ y^{i}\eta_{i},

we have
D(\eta)=\alpha
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

Suppose
X
with Sullivan minimal model
(\land V,d)
satisfies
B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq(S^{3}\vee\cdots\vee S^{3})_{\mathbb{Q}}.
Let
p_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\colon E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}\to B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
denote the universal fibre map for
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Suppose
(p_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}})_{\sharp}\colon\pi_{3}(E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}})\to\pi_{3}(B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}))
is not surjective. Then there is a Gottlieb element
y\in V^{2}
. Since
(\land V,d)
is simply connected,
dy=0
. Thus we have a factorization
(\land V,d)\cong(\land(y),0)\otimes(\land W,d)
for
W
complementary to
\langle y\rangle
in
V
. We may apply Lemma
5.5
. However, here we have

H_{*}(\mathrm{Der}(\land V),D)\cong\pi_{*}\big(\Omega B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\big)\cong\pi_{*}\big(\Omega(S^{3}\vee\cdots\vee S^{3})_{\mathbb{Q}}\big)

and the latter is the free graded Lie algebra generated in degree
2
by at least two elements. No element of degree
2
(or any other degree) can be a zero divisor and yet neither can we have
H_{\geq 2}(\mathrm{Der}(\land V),D)
one-dimensional (indeed,
H_{\geq 2}(\mathrm{Der}(\land V),D)
is infinite-dimensional). We have a contradiction.

We may assume
(p_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}})_{\sharp}\colon\pi_{3}(E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}})\to\pi_{3}(B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}))
is surjective. Then, for each summand
S^{3}_{\mathbb{Q}}
involved in the wedge, we may lift the inclusion
S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{3}\to(S^{3}\vee\cdots\vee S^{3})_{\mathbb{Q}}
through
p_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}
to a map
S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{3}\to E_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Assembling these liftings gives a lifting of the identity map of
B\mathrm{aut}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq(S^{3}\vee\cdots\vee S^{3})_{\mathbb{Q}}
through
p_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}
and so a section of the universal fibration.
∎

We conclude with a homotopy-theoretic proof of Theorem
1
using the universal fibration for rational spaces.

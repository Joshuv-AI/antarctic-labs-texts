Quantum Algorithm based on Quantum Fourier Transform for Register-by-Constant Addition
Filipe Chagas Ferraz

Abstract
Since Shor’s [ 8 ] proposition of the method for factoring products of prime numbers using quantum computing, there has been a quest to implement efficient quantum arithmetic algorithms. These algorithms are capable of applying arithmetic operations simultaneously on large sets of values using quantum parallelism. Draper [ 9 ] proposed an addition algorithm based on the quantum Fourier transform whose operands are two quantum registers, which I refer to as register-by-register addition. However, for cases where there is the need to be added a constant value to a target register, Draper’s algorithm is more complex than necessary in terms of number of operations ( depth ) and number of qubits used. In this paper, I present a more efficient addition algorithm than Draper’s for cases where there needs to be added just a constant to a target register.

2
Draper’s register-by-register addition algorithm

Draper
[
9
]
proposed a register-by-register addition algorithm based on the quantum Fourier transform. Draper’s addition algorithm performs as in the following mapping:

\lvert a,b\rangle\mapsto\lvert a,b+a\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle

(1)

In the equation
1
,
a
and
b
are natural numbers.
\lvert a\rangle
and
\lvert b\rangle
are states each composed by
N
qubits, i.e.,
\lvert a\rangle=\lvert a_{N},....,a_{2},a_{1}\rangle
and
\lvert b\rangle=\lvert b_{N},...,b_{2},b_{1}\rangle
such that
a_{j}\in\{0,1\}
and
b_{j}\in\{0,1\}
for any
j\in\{1,...,N\}
.

The capacity for parallel processing of this algorithm is seen when the operand registers have multiple superposed values. For a pair of registers initialized as
\lvert\psi_{1}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{N}-1}\alpha_{j}\lvert j\rangle
and
\lvert\psi_{2}\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}\beta_{k}\lvert k\rangle
, the Draper’s adder (represented by
D
) operates as shown in the following equation:

\begin{split}D(\lvert\psi_{1}\rangle\otimes\lvert\psi_{2}\rangle)=\\
D\left[\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2^{N}-1}\alpha_{j}\lvert j\rangle\right)\otimes\left(\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}\beta_{k}\lvert k\rangle\right)\right]=\\
D\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2^{N}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}\lvert j,k\rangle\right)=\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2^{N}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}D\lvert j,k\rangle=\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2^{N}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}\lvert j,j+k\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle\end{split}

(2)

Equation
2
shows that when Draper’s adder is applied over a pair of registers initialized with multiple superposed values, the result in the second register is a superposition of all possible modular sums
j+k\pmod{2^{N}}
, each with probability amplitude
\alpha_{j}\beta_{k}
.

Figure 1:
Quantum circuit of the Draper’s addition algorithm

The structure of the Draper’s adder consists of a sequence of
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
controlled
R_{l}
operations between a QFT and an inverse QFT, where
N
is the number of qubits per operand and
R_{l}
is a unitary single-qubit operator defined as:

R_{l}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2^{l}}}\end{bmatrix}

(3)

When there needs to add a constant to a target register of
N
qubits, i.e., to transform the state
\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}w_{k}\lvert k\rangle
of the second register into
\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}w_{k}\lvert k+c\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle
for
c\in\mathbb{Z}
, Draper’s adder requires at least
\lceil\log_{2}{c}\rceil
additional qubits for storing the value
c
. In section 3, I present a QFT-based adder with fewer operations and that does not require additional qubits to add a constant to a target register.

Proposed register-by-constant addition algorithm

The proposed register-by-constant addition algorithm operates on a single target quantum register, eliminating the need for a second register for storing the constant value. The operation performed by this algorithm is defined for
N
qubits as the mapping
\lvert a\rangle\mapsto\lvert a+c\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle
, where
a
is the natural value stored in the target register before the operation, and
c
is the constant added to the target register after the operation. A circuit-like representation of this algorithm is presented in figure
2
, and a pseudocode implementation is presented in algorithm
1
.

q_{1},q_{2},...,q_{N}

\lvert a\rangle
. In a situation where the register is not initialized with multiple superposed values,
\lvert a\rangle
can be defined as
\lvert a\rangle=\lvert a_{N},...,a_{2},a_{1}\rangle
such that
a=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2^{k-1}a_{k}
and
a_{k}\in\{0,1\}
. Each qubit
q_{k}

a_{k}

Implementation in pseudocode of the register-by-constant addition algorithm

The register-by-constant addition algorithm can be represented as the following unitary linear operator:

U_{+}(c)=U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\times U_{\phi(+)}(c)\times U_{\text{QFT}}

(4)

The operators
U_{\text{QFT}}
and
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}
are, respectively: the quantum Fourier transform and its inverse; and the operator
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
is the register-by-constant adder for a constant
c
in Fourier basis. The operator
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
is defined for
N
qubits as:

\begin{split}U_{\phi(+)}(c)=\bigotimes_{\tau=1}^{N}R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-t}}\right)\\
=R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-N}}\right)\otimes...\otimes R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-1}}\right)\end{split}

(5)

Where
t=N-(\tau-1)
and
R_{z}(\theta)
is a single-qubit
z
-rotation operator, defined as:

R_{z}(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{i\theta}\end{bmatrix}

(6)

The application of
U_{+}(c)
over a register initialized with a state
\lvert\psi\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}w_{k}\lvert k\rangle
is defined as follows:

\begin{split}U_{+}(c)\lvert\psi\rangle=U_{+}(c)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}w_{k}\lvert k\rangle\right)\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}w_{k}U_{+}(c)\lvert k\rangle\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{N}-1}w_{k}\lvert k+c\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle\end{split}

(7)

The number of operations required by the register-by-constant addition algorithm for
N
qubits is
T(N)=T_{\text{QFT}}(N)+N+T_{\text{IQFT}}(N)
, where
T_{\text{QFT}}(N)
is the number of operations required by QFT and
T_{\text{IQFT}}(N)
is the number of operations required by IQFT. Assuming
T_{\text{QFT}}(N)=T_{\text{IQFT}}(N)=\frac{N(N+1)}{2}

[
6
]
, we have
T(N)=N(N+1)+N=N^{2}+2N
, and therefore the register-by-constant addition algorithm is
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
.

The Draper’s register-by-register addition algorithm with two operand registers of
N
qubits needs
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
controlled operations between the QFT and IQFT, whereas the presented register-by-constant addition algorithm with a target register of
N
qubits needs only
N
single-qubit operations between the QFT and IQFT.

4
Correctness proof of the register-by-constant addition algorithm

The correctness of the register-by-constant addition algorithm can be demonstrated by analyzing it in matrix form. First, the state of
N
qubits
\lvert a\rangle
for
a\in\{x\in\mathbb{N}:0\leq x\leq 2^{N}-1\}
is expanded in matrix form:

\begin{split}\lvert a\rangle=[\lambda_{j}]\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N}\times 1},\quad\lambda_{j}=\begin{cases}1,\quad j=a\\
0,\quad j\neq a\end{cases}\end{split}

(8)

In equation
8
, the expression
[\lambda_{j}]\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N}\times 1}
corresponds to a matrix of
2^{N}
rows and
1
column. The variable
j
corresponds to the row index of the respective
\lambda_{j}
entry of the matrix, such that
0\leq j\leq 2^{N}-1
.

The operator
U_{\text{QFT}}
, which is the quantum discrete Fourier transform, is expanded in matrix form as:

U_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[\omega^{jk}]\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N}\times 2^{N}}

(9)

In the equation
9
, the expression
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[\omega^{jk}]\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N}\times 2^{N}}
corresponds to a matrix of
2^{N}
rows and
2^{N}
columns of which entries are
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}\omega^{jk}
, where
j
is the row index of the entry and
k
is the column index of the entry. The symbol
\omega
is a constant defined as
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2^{N}}}

[
6
,
10
]
.

The operation
U_{\text{QFT}}\lvert a\rangle
results in a column matrix equivalent to the column of index
a
of the matrix
U_{\text{QFT}}
. Describing this operation algebraically, we have:

U_{\text{QFT}}\lvert a\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[\omega^{ja}]\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N}\times 1}

(10)

In equation
10
, the expression
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[\omega^{ja}]\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N}\times 1}
corresponds to a column matrix with
2^{N}
rows whose entries are
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}\omega^{ja}
, where
j
is the row index of the entry. This expression is equivalent to
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[...,\omega^{ja},...]^{\intercal}
.

The application of
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}
over
U_{\text{QFT}}\lvert a\rangle
results in
\lvert a\rangle
itself, which leads us to the following observation:

\begin{split}U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[...,\omega^{ja},...]^{\intercal}\right)=\lvert a\rangle,\\
0\leq a\leq 2^{N}-1\end{split}

(11)

The equation
11
is constrained to
0\leq a\leq 2^{N}-1
. However, for any
a\in\mathbb{N}
, the expression
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[...,\omega^{ja},...]^{\intercal}\right)
results in
\lvert a\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle
and this is due to the periodicity of
a\mapsto\omega^{ja}
. This statement is demonstrated in section 4.1.

Based on equation
11
, it can be deduced that:

\begin{split}U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[...,\omega^{j(a+c)},...]^{\intercal}\right)=\\
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[...,\omega^{ja}\omega^{jc},...]^{\intercal}\right)=\\
\lvert a+c\rangle,\\
0\leq a+c\leq 2^{N}-1\end{split}

(12)

In general, for any
\lvert x\rangle
such that
0\leq x\leq 2^{N}-1
, it is considered that
U_{\text{QFT}}\lvert x\rangle=\lvert\phi(x)\rangle
and
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\lvert\phi(x)\rangle=\lvert x\rangle
, where
\lvert\phi(x)\rangle
is a notation for the equivalent of
\lvert x\rangle
in Fourier basis.

Based on the statement presented in equation
12
, it comes to the conclusion that, in order to create the mapping
\lvert\phi(a)\rangle\mapsto\lvert\phi(a+c)\rangle
, it is necessary to multiply each entry
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}\omega^{ja}
of
\lvert\phi(a)\rangle
by a factor
\omega^{jc}
. This operation can be described as the matrix product
U_{\phi(+)}(c)\lvert\phi(a)\rangle
, since the operator
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
is expanded in matrix form as shown in the following equation:

\begin{split}U_{\phi(+)}(c)=[\lambda_{(j,k)}]\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N}\times 2^{N}},\\
\lambda_{(j,k)}=\begin{cases}\omega^{jc},\quad j=k\\
0,\quad j\neq k\end{cases}\end{split}

(13)

The equation
13
defines
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
as a diagonal matrix whose main diagonal entries are
\omega^{jc}
, where
j
and
k
are, respectively, the row index and the column index of the entry. This definition can also be written as
U_{\phi(+)}(c)=\text{diag}[1,\omega^{c},\omega^{2c}...,\omega^{(2^{N}-1)c}]
or simply
U_{\phi(+)}(c)=\text{diag}[...,\omega^{jc},...]
. The equivalence between the definitions of
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
in equations
5
and
13
is demonstrated by induction in section 4.2.

Based on the facts presented here, it is possible to conclude that:

\begin{split}\lvert a+c\rangle=\\
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\lvert\phi(a+c)\rangle=\\
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\times U_{\phi(+)}(c)\lvert\phi(a)\rangle=\\
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\times U_{\phi(+)}(c)\times U_{\text{QFT}}\lvert a\rangle=\\
U_{+}(c)\lvert a\rangle,\\
0\leq a+c\leq 2^{N}-1\end{split}

(14)

4.1
Modularity

A function
f(\alpha)=e^{i\alpha}=\cos{\alpha}+i\sin{\alpha}
has a periodicity of
2\pi
, which means that
e^{i\alpha}=e^{i(\alpha\pmod{2\pi})}=e^{i(\alpha+2\pi n)}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Replacing
\alpha
with
\theta\frac{2\pi}{2^{N}}
, we have
e^{\theta\frac{2\pi i}{2^{N}}}=e^{i(\theta\frac{2\pi}{2^{N}}+2\pi n)}=e^{\theta\frac{2\pi i}{2^{N}}}e^{2\pi ni}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We have
e^{\theta\frac{2\pi i}{2^{N}}}=\omega^{\theta}
and
e^{2\pi ni}=e^{\left(\frac{2\pi i}{2^{N}}\right)n2^{N}}=\omega^{n2^{N}}
, and by assuming that
e^{\theta\frac{2\pi i}{2^{N}}}=e^{\theta\frac{2\pi i}{2^{N}}}e^{2\pi ni}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\omega^{\theta}=\omega^{\theta}\omega^{n2^{N}}=\omega^{\theta+n2^{N}}
, therefore
\omega^{\theta}=\omega^{\theta\pmod{2^{N}}}
. Based on this, we obtain the following statement from the equation
11
:

\begin{split}U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\lvert\phi(x)\rangle=\\
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[...,\omega^{jx},...]^{\intercal}\right)=\\
U_{\text{QFT}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N}}}[...,\omega^{j(x\pmod{2^{N}})},...]^{\intercal}\right)=\\
\lvert x\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle\end{split}

(15)

Equation 15 implies that for any
0\leq a\leq 2^{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{N}
, we have:

U_{+}(c)\lvert a\rangle=\lvert a+c\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle

(16)

4.2
Demonstration of the equivalence between the definitions
5
and
13

The proposition to be demonstrated is:

\begin{split}U_{\phi(+)}(c)=\bigotimes_{\tau=1}^{N}R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-t}}\right)\\
=\text{diag}[...,\omega^{jc},...]\end{split}

(17)

Where
t=N-(\tau-1)
,
\text{diag}[...,\omega^{jc},...]
is a diagonal matrix whose entries are
\omega^{jc}
and
j
is the row index of the entry.

The expression
R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-t}}\right)
can be expanded as:

\begin{split}R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-t}}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{i\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-t}}}\end{bmatrix}=\\
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{\left(\frac{2i\pi}{2^{N}}\right)\frac{c}{2^{1-t}}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{\frac{c}{2^{1-t}}}\end{bmatrix}\end{split}

(18)

To show that the proposition
17
is true, a mathematical induction is employed. We begin by expanding
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
for
N=1
:

\begin{split}\bigotimes_{\tau=1}^{N}R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-t}}\right)\bigg\rvert_{N=1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{\frac{c}{2^{1-1}}}\end{bmatrix}\\
=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{c}\end{bmatrix}\end{split}

(19)

The equation
19
satisfies the proposition
17
.

Expanding
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
for
N=2
, we have:

\begin{split}\bigotimes_{\tau=1}^{N}R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-t}}\right)\bigg\rvert_{N=2}=\\
R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-2}}\right)\otimes R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{N-1}}\right)=\\
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{\frac{c}{2^{1-2}}}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{\frac{c}{2^{1-1}}}\end{bmatrix}=\\
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{2c}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{c}\end{bmatrix}=\\
\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&\omega^{c}&0&0\\
0&0&\omega^{2c}&0\\
0&0&0&\omega^{c}\omega^{2c}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\\
\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&\omega^{c}&0&0\\
0&0&\omega^{2c}&0\\
0&0&0&\omega^{3c}\\
\end{bmatrix}\end{split}

(20)

The equation
20
also satisfies the proposition
17
.

To proof that the proposition
17
is true for any number of qubits, an inductive step is conducted on the equation
21
.

\begin{split}\bigotimes_{\tau=1}^{M}R_{z}\left(\frac{c\pi}{2^{M-t}}\right)\bigg\rvert_{M=N+1}=\\
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{\frac{c}{2^{1-(N+1)}}}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\text{diag}[...,\omega^{j_{n}c},...]=\\
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\omega^{c2^{N}}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\text{diag}[...,\omega^{j_{n}c},...]=\\
\text{diag}[...,\omega^{j_{n}c},...,\omega^{c2^{N}}\omega^{j_{n}c},...]=\\
\text{diag}[...,\omega^{j_{n}c},...,\omega^{(j_{n}+2^{N})c},...]=\\
\text{diag}[...,\omega^{j_{m}c},...]\end{split}

(21)

In equation
21
, it is assumed that the proposition
17
is true for
N
qubits and it is verified that, under this assumption, it is also true for
M=N+1
qubits. The variable
j_{n}
corresponds to the row index of a matrix of
N
rows and columns, and the variable
j_{m}
corresponds to the row index of a matrix of
M
rows and columns.

The equation
17
is therefore true for any number of qubits.

5
Rewriting the register-by-register adder using the
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
operator

It is possible to rewrite the Draper’s register-by-register adder using the
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
operator defined in sections 3 and 4. This alternative representation is easier to understand than the original one.

As already mentioned in section 2, the register-by-register adder executes the mapping
\lvert a,b\rangle\mapsto\lvert a,b+a\pmod{2^{N}}\rangle
, where
\lvert a\rangle=\lvert a_{N},...,a_{1}\rangle
and
a_{j}\in\{0,1\}
.

As in Draper’s adder, this alternative register-by-register adder consists of a series of controlled gates applied on the qubits of the second register between QFT and IQFT, i.e, in the Fourier basis. Since it is possible to define
b+a
as
b+\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}2^{j-1}
, the state of the second register in the Fourier basis can be changed from
\lvert\phi(b)\rangle
to
\lvert\phi(b+a)\rangle
by applying a
U_{\phi(+)}(2^{j-1})
controlled by
a_{j}
on this register for each
j\in\{1,...,N\}
.

3
shows a quantum circuit of the register-by-register adder using controlled
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
gates.

6
Conclusion

This paper presented an arithmetic addition algorithm based on the quantum Fourier transform specific for cases where there needs to be added just a constant to a target register, which is called here register-by-constant adder. This adder uses fewer qubits than those proposed in
[
7
,
9
]
, as it requires neither an additional register to store the constant nor ancillary carry qubits. Furthermore, the register-by-constant adder needs fewer operations between the QFT and IQFT than Draper’s adder.

This paper also showed an alternative representation of the Draper’s adder using the operator
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
, which is easier to understand than the original one.

For futher works, it might be interesting to benchmark between the register-by-constant adder and other addition algorithms on real quantum hardware to compare the noise of the outputs of these algorithms. Moreover, alternative representations using
U_{\phi(+)}(c)
of the QFT-based multiplier covered in
[
13
]
can be made, and more QFT-based algorithms can be designed using this operator.

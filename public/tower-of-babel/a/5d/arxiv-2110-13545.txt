Core-Collapse Supernova Search Strategy: Gravitational Waves and Low-Energy Neutrinos
Giulia Pagliaroli

Abstract
Core-collapse supernovae are expected to produce multimessenger signals. Low-energy neutrinos and gravitational waves are important to study the explosion mechanism of these events. The simulations and detections of gravitational waves from these events are still challenging due to broad range of expected progenitors as well as their stochasticity. In this manuscript, we give a timely introduction to a proposed method to combine gravitational waves and neutrinos from core-collapse supernovae; more detailed discussion will be provided in a forthcoming paper [ 1 ] ( arXiv:2107.02050 ). We discuss how to exploit the time profile of the neutrino signals in order to improve the efficiency search. Moreover, we describe the combination of neutrino data from several detectors. The goal is to produce a strategy for combining the neutrinos and gravitational waves as a multimessenger search.

GW and LEN signals highly depend on the initial conditions of the CCSN, as progenitor mass, rotation, etc. The ideal case is to perform a coherent GW-LEN analysis by considering simulated GW and LEN signals resulting from the same numerical simulation. Unfortunately, no coherent GW-LEN numerical simulations providing successful CCSN explosion exist up to now. Several simulations provide both signals for the first half of a second, till the explosion, while the LEN emission duration is expected to last
O(10)

Data and Analysis Strategy

Here, we will discuss in brief the data and analysis for GWs, for LENs, as well as a possible strategy to do a combined multimessenger search in the context of our work. More details can be seen in
[
1
]
. We consider a conservative threshold on global false alarm rate (FAR) of 1/1000 years.

The GW analysis uses the cWB algorithm, a pipeline that has been used widely inside the LIGO and Virgo collaborations. For this work, we simulate Gaussian noise with a spectral sensitivity based on the expected Advanced LIGO
[
22
,
21
]
and Advanced Virgo detectors
[
23

2
have been generated for distances: 5, 15, 20, 50, 60, 700 kpc. We inject 1 GW signal in about 100 seconds, wide enough to allow two consecutive waveforms. To ensure sufficient statistics, for each distance and model we inject
\sim 2500

864
per day for GW candidates
[
1

to pass to the multimessenger analysis, as per requirement of the combined FAR of 1/1000 years. The efficiency curves for each distance can be seen in c.f. Fig. 2 of

In the standard LEN analysis for CCSNe, a time series data set is binned in a sliding time window of
w=20
seconds. The group of events in each bin is defined as a
cluster
and the number in the cluster is called multiplicity
m
. The multiplicity distribution of background-only events is expected to follow a Poisson distribution. For each
i
-th cluster, the significance can be estimated. The significance is correlated with its
imitation frequency
(
f^{\mathrm{im}}
, more detail in
[
1
]
) defined as:

f^{\mathrm{im}}_{i}(m_{i})=N\times\sum_{k=m_{i}}^{\infty}P(k),

(1)

where
P(k)
is the Poisson term, representing the probability that a cluster of multiplicity
k
is produced by the background with a given frequency and time window.

In our case, we exploit the temporal behaviour of CCSNe by defining a new parameter
\xi\equiv m/\Delta t
, where
\Delta t
is the duration of a cluster (see
[
1
]
). Thus, it is formulated a new modified
2-parameter
(
m
and
\xi
) imitation frequency for each cluster, called
F^{\mathrm{im}}
:

F^{\mathrm{im}}_{i}(m_{i},\xi_{i})=N\times\sum_{k=m_{i}}^{\infty}P(k,\xi_{i}),

(2)

where the term
P(k,\xi_{i})
provides the joint probability of a cluster, given multiplicity
k

and

\xi_{i}
, produced by the background. The final form can be seen in Eq. 5 of
[
1
]
, which is the main equation in this work,

F^{\mathrm{im}}_{i}(m_{i},\xi_{i})=N\times\sum_{k=m_{i}}^{\infty}P(k)\int_{\xi=\xi_{i}}^{\infty}\mathrm{PDF}(\xi\geq\xi_{i}|k)d\xi.

(3)

We simulate
\sim 10
years of background data for each neutrino detector. Moreover, the CCSN simulated signals are injected into the background data for each detector model and for each source distance coherently in time. These clusters of neutrino events are extracted via the Monte Carlo method. We inject signals at a rate of 1 per day.

5
Conclusion

In this proceeding, we have given a timely introduction of our proposed method for multimessenger search of CCSNe via GWs and LENs; more details will be provided in the forthcoming paper
[
1
]
. The multimessenger campaign between GWs and LENs with the proposed method can gain the overall efficiency. Due to the higher sensitivity in LEN detectors, we can also do a targeted search in the (hopefully near) future to search for GWs from CCSNe

Efficient verification of entangled continuous-variable quantum states with local measurements
Ye-Chao Liu, Jiangwei Shang, Xiangdong Zhang

Abstract
Continuous-variable quantum states are of particular importance in various quantum information processing tasks including quantum communication and quantum sensing. However, a bottleneck has emerged with the fast increasing in size of the quantum systems which severely hinders their efficient characterization. In this work, we establish a systematic framework for verifying entangled continuous-variable quantum states by employing local measurements only. Our protocol is able to achieve the unconditionally high verification efficiency which is quadratically better than quantum tomography as well as other nontomographic methods. Specifically, we demonstrate the power of our protocol by showing the efficient verification of entangled two-mode and multimode coherent states with local measurements.

Introduction

Continuous-variable (CV) quantum systems have demonstrated their unique role in various quantum information processing applications

II
General framework

The task of quantum state verification is to determine whether the states
{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots}
output
from a device, all of which are supposed to be the target state
|\psi\rangle
(DV or CV), are cases either

{\sigma_{i}=\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}}
for all
i
, or
{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\leq 1-\epsilon}
for all
i
.
Then, a verification protocol
\Omega
can be generally constructed by several dichotomic-outcome projective measurement settings
\{\Omega_{l},\openone-\Omega_{l}\}
, such that

\displaystyle\Omega=\sum_{l}\mu_{l}\Omega_{l}\,,

(1)

where
\{\mu_{l}\}
is a probability distribution.
With the requirement that the target state should always pass all the measurements, i.e.,
\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=1
, errors of the verification protocol occur only when the noisy states
\sigma
pass the protocol with the maximal probability
Pallister et al. 2018
;
Zhu and Hayashi 2019b

\displaystyle\max_{\langle\psi|\sigma|\psi\rangle\leq 1-\epsilon}\mathrm{tr}(\Omega\sigma)=1-[1-\lambda_{2}(\Omega)]\epsilon=1-\nu\epsilon\,,

(2)

where
\lambda_{2}(\Omega)
is the second-largest eigenvalue of
\Omega
, and

\nu:=1-\lambda_{2}(\Omega)
denotes the spectral gap from the maximal eigenvalue.
Then, after
N
measurements, the maximal worst-case probability that the verifier fails to detect the “bad” case is given by
(1-\nu\epsilon)^{N}
.
Hence, to achieve a confidence level
1-\delta
, the number of copies of the states required is

\displaystyle N\geq\frac{1}{\ln[(1-\nu\epsilon)^{-1}]}\ln\delta^{-1}\approx\frac{1}{\nu}\epsilon^{-1}\ln\delta^{-1}\,.

(3)

In practice, searching for the optimal verification protocol is a demanding task, if not impossible at all.
For CV quantum states, in particular, the intrinsic nature of their infinite dimensional Hilbert space makes the spectral decomposition for
\Omega
even more challenging.
To render the task, we may restrict the type of noisy states to be in some specific form.
Here, we focus on the noisy states such that
{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=1-\epsilon}
for all
i
,
which is allowable since other states with
{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}<1-\epsilon}
would not make
the verification worse
Pallister et al. 2018
, thus the original task is retained.
By choosing a set of bases
\mathcal{S}
constructed from the target state
|\psi\rangle
and all of the mutually orthonormal states
\{\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}\}
that form the subspace orthogonal to
|\psi\rangle
,
we can write the noisy states as

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}=(1-\epsilon)\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}+\sum_{j}\epsilon_{i,j}\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}|$}+{\rm N.D.}\,,

(4)

where
\sum_{j}\epsilon_{i,j}=\epsilon
and
{\rm N.D.}
represents the non-diagonal terms in
\mathcal{S}
,
i.e.,
|\phi^{\prime}\rangle
\langle\phi|
for all
{\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}\neq\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}}\in\mathcal{S}
.
Thus, the optimization for the spectral gap can be reduced to

\displaystyle\nu_{\text{\rm opt}}:=\max_{\Omega}\min_{i}\sum_{l}\mu_{l}k_{l,i}\,,

(5)

where
{k_{l,i}:=1-\sum_{j}\frac{\epsilon_{i,j}}{\epsilon}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}|$}\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}\in[0,1]}
;
see Appendix A
sup
for the detailed derivation.
Note that the above framework including the optimization method works for both the CV and DV scenarios.

III
One-mode coherent state superpositions

The one-mode coherent states are defined as

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\,,~~\mbox{with}~\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\,,

(6)

where
|n\rangle
denotes the Fock states (or number states), which form a complete set of bases in the Hilbert space.
The verification of
|\alpha\rangle
can be easily done by invoking the projective measurement
|\alpha\rangle
\langle\alpha|
, which is achievable via a Kennedy receiver
Wittmann et al. 2008
;
DiMario and Becerra 2018
,
i.e., a SPD combined with the displacement
D(-\alpha)
in front,

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\alpha|$}\equiv D^{\dagger}(-\alpha)\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}D(-\alpha)\,.

(7)

Then, we consider the superposition of one-mode coherent states, usually referred to as the coherent state superpositions (CSSs)
Bužek et al. 1992
;
Takahashi et al. 2008
.
Typical examples of CSSs are the even and odd coherent cat states
Dodonov et al. 1974
,

\displaystyle\mbox{$|+_{\alpha}\rangle$}=\frac{\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\alpha\rangle$}}{\sqrt{C_{+}^{(\alpha)}}}\,,\quad\mbox{$|-_{\alpha}\rangle$}=\frac{\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}-\mbox{$|-\alpha\rangle$}}{\sqrt{C_{-}^{(\alpha)}}}\,,

(8)

with the normalization
C_{\pm}^{(\alpha)}=2\bigl(1\pm\mathrm{e}^{{-2|\alpha|^{2}}}\bigr)
.
They are useful in various quantum information processing tasks including quantum teleportation
van Enk and Hirota 2001
, quantum computation
Ralph et al. 2003
;
Lund et al. 2008
, and quantum metrology
Gilchrist et al. 2004
.
These two cat states can be discriminated using the parity measurement
Kuang and Zhu 1996
;
Besse et al. 2020

\displaystyle\pi=\sum_{n}\mbox{$|2n\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2n|$}-\sum_{n}\mbox{$|2n+1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2n+1|$}\,,

(9)

such that

\pi^{+}\mbox{$|+_{\alpha}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|+_{\alpha}\rangle$}\,,\quad\pi^{-}\mbox{$|-_{\alpha}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|-_{\alpha}\rangle$}\,,

(10)

which can be realized by PNRDs.
The superscripts
\pm
indicate the projectors onto the
eigenspace with the corresponding eigenvalues
\pm 1
.
Note, however, that the parity measurement solely is not sufficient to verify the even nor odd coherent cat states, as for instance,
|+_{\alpha}\rangle
and
|+_{\beta}\rangle
with
{\alpha\neq\pm\beta}
have the same behavior under the parity measurement.

Upon this point, we have revealed a significant difference between CV and DV quantum states regarding their verification, namely the CSSs including the symmetric cat states, cannot be verified straightforwardly.
The reason is due to the intrinsic nature of the infinite dimension of CV systems which results in the fact that realization of a deterministic arbitrary local operation in CV is still an open problem.
Nevertheless, some of the entanglement operations in CV are easier to implement instead.
For instance, in optical systems, consider the beam splitter (BS) with the form

\displaystyle B(\theta)=\exp\bigl[\mathrm{i}\theta\bigl(\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{2}+\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\bigr)\bigr],

(11)

where
\hat{a}_{1(2)}^{\dagger},\hat{a}_{1(2)}
are the creation and annihilation operators for the first (second) mode, respectively.
The parameter
{\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})}
determines the transmissivity of the BS, i.e.,
{\cos^{2}\theta\in(0,1)}
.
Then, by coupling to an ancilla vacuum mode
|0\rangle
, we can convert, for example, the even coherent cat state to

\displaystyle B(\theta)\mbox{$|+_{\alpha}\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{+}^{(\alpha)}}}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha\cos\theta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\mathrm{i}\alpha\sin\theta\rangle$}

(12)

\displaystyle+\mbox{$|-\alpha\cos\theta\rangle$}\mbox{$|-\mathrm{i}\alpha\sin\theta\rangle$}\Bigr).

This is the entangled coherent state, which we show how to verify in the next section.

IV
Two-mode entangled coherent states

The entangled coherent states (ECSs) usually refer to the superposition of two-mode coherent states with the form
\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}
(unnormalized) in specific
Sanders 1992
;
Sanders 2012
.
Here, we consider a more general form such that

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\Bigr),

(13)

where the normalization is
C_{\alpha,\beta}=2\bigl[1+\mathrm{e}^{-(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2})/2}\bigr]
.
Note that the transformed even coherent cat state in Eq. (
12
) is a special case of
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
under proper local displacement operations.
Furthermore, a class of more general entangled states with the form (unnormalized)

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\rangle$}:=\mbox{$|\alpha_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha_{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|\beta_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta_{2}\rangle$}

(14)

is locally equivalent to
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
when
{\alpha_{1,2},\beta_{1,2}\in\mathbb{R}}

Munro et al. 2000
;
Jeong et al. 2001
.
See Appendix B
sup
for all the derivations.

Although similar in form as the Bell states in the DV scenario, verification of the two-mode
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
cannot use the analytical technique as in Ref.
Li et al. 2019
or the numerical approach as in Ref.
Yu et al. 2019
.
The infinite dimensional Hilbert space of the CV systems severely restricts the type of measurements that can be realizable.
Hence, we start by fully exploring their physical properties.

Measurements of the SPDs can be described by
\{\tau^{+}=\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$},\tau^{-}=\openone-\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}\}
, using which one finds the relation
(\tau^{-}\otimes\tau^{-})\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}=0
.
Thus, to verify
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
, the first measurement setting we can employ is

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\!=\!\openone\!-\tau^{-}\!\otimes\tau^{-}\!=\!\openone\!-\!\sum_{n,m=1}^{\infty}\!\bigl(\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle n|$}\otimes\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle m|$}\bigr)\,,\quad

(15)

which satisfies
\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}
.
Physically speaking, this setting implies that the target state must have at least one mode that has no photon.
However, the possibility that no photons emerge from both modes together cannot be ruled out since
\mbox{$\langle\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}|$}(\tau^{+}\otimes\tau^{+})\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}\neq 0
, which is rather different from the property of the DV entangled states, like the Bell states.

Next, we define the displacement operation
{\mathbb{D}(-\alpha,-\beta):=D(-\alpha)\otimes D(-\beta)}
acting on the two modes, which transforms the target state
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
to another ECS state
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{-\alpha,-\beta}\rangle
.
With this, we have the following measurement setting

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}^{\text{\rm ECS}}=\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}\bigl(-\alpha,-\beta\bigr)\bigl(\openone\!-\tau^{-}\!\otimes\tau^{-}\bigr)\mathbb{D}\bigl(-\alpha,-\beta\bigr)\,,

(16)

which is in fact a generalized Kennedy receiver for two-mode states.

The third measurement setting

\displaystyle\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}}=\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\!\bigl(\pi\otimes\pi\bigr)^{+}\mathbb{D}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)

(17)

comes from the fact that ECSs have the parity symmetry under proper displacement, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},\!-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\!\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}\!=\!\frac{C_{\!+\!}\mbox{$|+_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle$}\mbox{$|+_{\frac{\beta}{2}}\rangle$}\!-\!C_{\!-\!}\mbox{$|-_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle$}\mbox{$|-_{\frac{\beta}{2}}\rangle$}}{2\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}}}\,,\qquad

(18)

where
C_{\pm}=\sqrt{C_{\pm}^{(\alpha/2)}C_{\pm}^{(\beta/2)}}
.

\mathbb{D}_{l}

The outcome 1 represents the “pass” instance and 0 for “fail”.

Briefly speaking, the first two measurement settings
\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}}

Together they ensure the superposition of the two states

\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}}

.

The two-mode entangled coherent states
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
can be verified efficiently by the protocol

\displaystyle\Omega^{\text{\rm ECS}}=\sum_{l=1}^{3}\mu_{l}\Omega_{l}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\,,

(19)

where the probability distribution
\{\mu_{l}\}
is arbitrary.
An optimal efficiency can be obtained by optimizing
\{\mu_{l}\}
under specific scenarios as constrained by Eq. (

In Fig.

The target states to be verified are input into two separate channels, followed by the displacement operation
\mathbb{D}_{l}
, and finally measured by photon detectors.
The displacement
\mathbb{D}_{l}
has three different cases
\bigl\{\mathbb{D}_{1}\!=\!\openone\otimes\openone,\mathbb{D}_{2}\!=\!D(\!-\alpha)\otimes D(\!-\beta),\mathbb{D}_{3}\!=\!D(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2})\otimes D(\!-\frac{\beta}{2})\bigr\}

As mentioned by Theorem

is determined by the optimal verification efficiency

\{\mu_{l}\}
under specific scenarios as constrained by Eq. (

Before proceeding to give a concrete example, we have a quick remark regarding the PNRDs.
In practice, finite resolution of the PNRDs for the parity measurements always leads to a systematic error.
We can circumvent this problem by directly dismissing the results when PNRDs are saturated.
For instance, consider the PNRD
(3)
with four outcomes
\{0,1,2,3+\}
, such that one can keep the outcomes
\{0,1,2\}
only by discarding the rest.
Some efficiency will be lost during this process, and the probability to get the useful results with PNRD
(r)
for a one-mode CV state

is given by

\displaystyle p(r)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}|\mbox{$\langle i|$}\psi\rangle|^{2}\,.

(20)

However, we emphasize that when
\alpha
is small, the loss is negligible.
For example, considering the one-mode even coherent cat state

with
\alpha\!=\!1

(3)

As for PNRD
(20)

V
multimode entangled coherent states

Here we consider a class of multimode entangled coherent states with the form

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\Biggl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}\Biggr),

(22)

where the normalization is
C=2\bigl[1+\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{i}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}/2}\bigr]
.
Depending on the number of modes, we refer to them as the
m
-mode GHZ-like coherent states,
which are generalizations of the states in Ref.
Jeong and An 2006
.
Note that, for
m=2
, states
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
and
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}2}\rangle
are locally equivalent.
In fact, a class of generalized GHZ-like states

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle$}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\beta_{i}\rangle$}

(23)

are locally equivalent to
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle
when
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
i
; see Appendix B
sup
for the derivations.

To verify
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle
, consider the following
{2(m\!-\!1)}
measurement settings

\displaystyle\Omega_{2l-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{l}\biggl\{\!\Bigl[B_{2l\!-\!1}^{\dagger}\bigl(\openone\!-\tau^{-}\!\otimes\tau^{-}\bigr)B_{2l\!-\!1}\Bigr]\!\otimes\openone^{\otimes(m-2)}\!\biggr\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{l}\biggl\{\!\Bigl[B_{2l}^{\dagger}\bigl(\openone\!-\tau^{-}\!\otimes\tau^{-}\bigr)B_{2l}\Bigr]\!\otimes\openone^{\otimes(m-2)}\!\biggr\},

(24)

with
l\!=\!1,2,\cdots,m\!-\!1
, where
{B_{2l-1}=D(-\alpha_{l})\otimes\openone}
and
B_{2l}=\openone\otimes D(-\alpha_{l+1})
are local operations.
The symbol
\mathcal{P}_{l}
indicates the permutation that only the
l
and
l+1
modes are operated on for each setting.
Moreover, we need one more measurement setting

\displaystyle\Omega_{2m-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}=\Biggl[\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}D^{\dagger}\!\left(-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\right)\Biggr]\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{+}\Biggl[\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}D\!\left(-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\right)\Biggr],\qquad

(25)

where
\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{+}
is the projector onto the eigenspace with eigenvalue
+1
of the parity measurement
\pi^{\otimes m}
.
With these, we have the following theorem for verifying
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle
; see Appendix F
sup
for the proof.

Theorem 2
.

The
m
-mode GHZ-like coherent states
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\rangle
can be verified efficiently by the protocol

\displaystyle\Omega^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}=\sum_{l=1}^{2m-1}\mu_{l}\Omega_{l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\,,

(26)

where the probability distribution
\{\mu_{l}\}
is arbitrary.
An optimal efficiency can be obtained by optimizing
\{\mu_{l}\}
under specific scenarios as constrained by Eq. (

).

Two remarks are in order.
First, except for the last setting which requires PNRDs on each mode, the other
2(m\!-\!1)
measurement settings have exactly the same framework as
\Omega^{\text{\rm ECS}}
shown in Fig

Second, if changing the superposition of the two terms in
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle
from
+
to
-
, they can still be verified efficiently with similar experimental settings; see Appendix G
sup

VI
Conclusion

We have developed a systematic framework for verifying continuous-variable quantum states with local measurements only.
Same as in the discrete-variable scenario, the high verification efficiency with the resource overhead of
{N\propto O(\epsilon^{-1}\ln\delta^{-1})}
within infidelity
\epsilon
and confidence level
{1-\delta}
is retained.
This efficiency is quadratically better than quantum tomography as well as other nontomographic methods which usually require the resource in the order of
{N\propto O(\epsilon^{-2}\ln\delta^{-1})}
.
The high verification efficiency of our protocol is confirmed with the demonstration for verifying entangled two-mode and multimode coherent states.

As an outlook, it is interesting to extend the current study to verifying other important CV quantum states, and even CV quantum processes.
Also, the adversarial scenario
Zhu and Hayashi 2019b
;
Zhu and Hayashi 2019c
;
Hayashi and Morimae 2015
;
Takeuchi et al. 2019
is worth considering, of which correlations among the input states can be included.
In such a case the scaling of the verification efficiency is expected to be kept, but deteriorate by a constant factor
Zhu and Hayashi 2019b
;
Zhu and Hayashi 2019c
.
Moreover, with the techniques including adaptive measurements
Yu et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2019b
and nondemolition photon counting
Liu et al. 2021
;
Munro et al. 2005
;
Guerlin et al. 2007
, our protocol has the potential for further improvement.

Appendix A
Appendix A: Detailed derivation of the general framework

We focus on the noisy states such that
{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=1-\epsilon}
, which is allowable since other states with
{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}<1-\epsilon}
would not make the verification worse
Pallister et al. 2018
.
By choosing a set of bases
\mathcal{S}
constructed from the target state
|\psi\rangle
and all of the mutually orthonormal states
\{\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}\}
that form the subspace orthogonal to
|\psi\rangle
, we can write the noisy states as

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}=\sum_{{\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$},\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mbox{$\langle\phi^{\prime}|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\phi|$}=(1-\epsilon)\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}+\sum_{j}\epsilon_{i,j}\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}|$}+\text{\rm N.D.}\,,

(27)

where
{\mathrm{tr}(\sigma_{i})=1}
indicates
{\sum_{j}\epsilon_{i,j}=\epsilon}
, and
N.D.
corresponds to the non-diagonal terms in
\mathcal{S}
.
With the constraint
\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}
, which implies
\mbox{$\langle\psi^{\perp}_{i,j}|$}\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\Omega_{l}\sigma_{i})=1-\epsilon+\sum_{j}\epsilon_{i,j}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}|$}\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}\,.

(28)

Notice that
\mbox{$\langle\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}|$}\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}
is the probability for the state
|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle
to pass the measurement setting
\Omega_{l}
which is in the range
[0,1]
, and the normalized parameters
\frac{\epsilon_{i,j}}{\epsilon}
are determined by the structure of the noisy states
\sigma_{i}
.
Hence, the summation
\sum_{j}\frac{\epsilon_{i,j}}{\epsilon}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}|$}\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}
is fixed in accordance with the measurement setting
\Omega_{l}
and the specific type of noisy states
\sigma_{i}
that we consider.
Next, define

\displaystyle k_{l,i}=1-\sum_{j}\frac{\epsilon_{i,j}}{\epsilon}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}|$}\Omega_{l}\mbox{$|\psi_{i,j}^{\perp}\rangle$}\,,

(29)

which is thus independent of the infidelity
\epsilon
.
Following this definition, we have the linear relationship between the passing probability of the noisy state
\sigma_{i}
and the infidelity
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\Omega_{l}\sigma_{i})=1-k_{l,i}\epsilon\,,\quad k_{l,i}\in[0,1]\,.

(30)

Then we have

\displaystyle\max_{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=1-\epsilon}\mathrm{tr}(\Omega\sigma)=1-\min_{i}\sum_{l}\mu_{l}k_{l,i}\epsilon\quad\overset{i\to\infty}{=}\quad\max_{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\leq 1-\epsilon}\mathrm{tr}(\Omega\sigma)=1-\nu\epsilon\,.

(31)

Therefore, optimization of the spectral gap
\nu
can be written as

\displaystyle\nu_{\text{\rm opt}}=\max_{\Omega}\min_{i}\sum_{l}\mu_{l}k_{l,i}\,,

(32)

with the number of measurements required is

\displaystyle N\approx\frac{1}{\nu_{\text{\rm opt}}}\epsilon^{-1}\ln\delta^{-1}\,.

(33)

Note that if all the possible noisy states with
{\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=1-\epsilon}
are considered, an optimal upper bound for
N
can be obtained.
However, if only considering some typical noisy states, the optimization can be simplified, which is often enough for practical implementations and the number of measurements is reduced accordingly.

Appendix B
Appendix B: Local equivalence between several classes of CV states

We first discuss the nature of the displacement operators in more details.
The displacement operator is also known as the shift operator

\displaystyle D(\alpha)=\mathrm{e}^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\alpha^{*}\hat{a}}\,,

(34)

where
{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}}
represents the amount of displacement, and
\hat{a}^{\dagger},\hat{a}
are the creation and annihilation operators.
The coherent states can be obtained by displacing the vacuum, i.e.,

\displaystyle D(\alpha)\mbox{$|0\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\,.

(35)

The product of two displacement operators is

\displaystyle D(\alpha)D(\beta)=\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha\beta^{*}-\alpha^{*}\beta)/2}D(\alpha+\beta)\,.

(36)

Thus, all of the one-mode coherent states are locally equivalent under displacement operations, as

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha\beta^{*}-\alpha^{*}\beta)/2}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Im(\alpha\beta^{*})}

(37)

is the global phase which is physically irrelevant,
and
\Im(\cdot)
denotes the imaginary part of the number.

Case 1:

Consider the balanced two-mode coherent state as defined in Eq. (
14
),

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}^{(2)}}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha_{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|\beta_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta_{2}\rangle$}\Bigr),

(38)

where the normalization is given by
\tilde{C}^{(2)}=2+\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(|\alpha_{1}|^{2}+|\beta_{1}|^{2}+|\alpha_{2}|^{2}+|\beta_{2}|^{2}\bigr)\right]\!\left(\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}^{*}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}^{*}\beta_{2}}+\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}^{*}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}^{*}}\right)
.
With displacements operated on both modes, we have

\displaystyle D(-\beta_{1})\otimes D(-\alpha_{2})\mbox{$|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}^{(2)}}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}^{*}-\alpha_{1}^{*}\beta_{1})/2}\mbox{$|\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha_{2}^{*}\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}^{*})/2}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}\rangle$}\Bigr].

(39)

It is easy to find that
D(-\beta_{1})\otimes D(-\alpha_{2})\mbox{$|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\rangle$}
and

|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1},\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}}\rangle
are equivalent whenever the constraint

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}^{*})+\Im(\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}^{*})=2n\pi\,,\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z})

(40)

is satisfied,
of which real values of
\alpha_{1,2}
and
\beta_{1,2}
can always meet.

Case 2:

We consider a more general form of the equivalence between the transformed even coherent cat state as in Eq. (
12
) and the ECS.
The one-mode balanced CSSs can be written as
Bužek et al. 1992

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm BCSS}}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C^{\text{\rm BCSS}}}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}+\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\Bigr),

(41)

where the normalization is
C^{\text{\rm BCSS}}=2+\mathrm{e}^{-(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2})/2}\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{\alpha^{*}\beta}+\mathrm{e}^{\alpha\beta^{*}}\bigr)
.
By coupling to a vacuum mode, we utilize a beam splitter
B(\theta)
and get

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\Psi_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C^{\text{\rm BCSS}}}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha\cos\theta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\mathrm{i}\alpha\sin\theta\rangle$}+\mbox{$|\beta\cos\theta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\mathrm{i}\beta\sin\theta\rangle$}\Bigr).

(42)

Next, with the displacement
D(-\beta\cos\theta)\otimes D(-\mathrm{i}\alpha\sin\theta)
, it is converted to

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\Psi_{\alpha,\beta}^{D}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C^{\text{\rm BCSS}}}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha\beta^{*}-\alpha^{*}\beta)\cos^{2}\theta}\mbox{$|(\alpha-\beta)\cos\theta\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{*}\beta-\alpha\beta^{*})\sin^{2}\theta}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\mathrm{i}(\beta-\alpha)\sin\theta\rangle$}\Bigr].

(43)

Then, in order to make
|\Psi_{\alpha,\beta}^{D}\rangle
equivalent to the ECS, one needs the constraint

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha\beta^{*})=2n\pi\,,\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z})\,,

(44)

and the even coherent cat state is such a case with
\alpha=\beta
.

Case 3:

The generalized multimode GHZ-like states are

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}^{(m)}}}\biggl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\beta_{i}\rangle$}\biggr),

(45)

with the normalization given by
\tilde{C}^{(m)}=2+\exp\!\left[-\sum\bigl(|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+|\beta_{i}|^{2}\bigr)/2\right]\!\left[\exp\bigl(\sum\alpha_{i}^{*}\beta_{i}\bigr)+\exp\bigl(\sum\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}^{*}\bigr)\right]
.
With displacements, they are transformed to

\displaystyle\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}D(-\beta_{i})\mbox{$|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}^{(m)}}}\biggl(\exp\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}^{*}-\alpha_{i}^{*}\beta_{i})/2\Bigr]\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}\biggr).

(46)

Then, if the constraint

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\Im(\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}^{*})=2n\pi\,,\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z})

(47)

is satisfied,
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle
and
|\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle
are locally equivalent.
Similarly, real values of
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
for all
i
can meet this requirement.

Additionally, we note that
|\psi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\rangle
and
|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}2}\rangle
are equivalent under the local displacement
\openone\otimes D(-\beta)
for arbitrary
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, as the constraint in Eq. (
47
) is always satisfied.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\!\right)\!\Bigl[B^{\prime}\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)+B^{\prime}\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}-\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(-\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C^{\prime}\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)+C^{\prime}\bigl(-\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}-\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\Bigr]\times\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{B^{\prime}+C^{\prime}}{2}\big(\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\big),

and the constraint
\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}}\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}
gives

\displaystyle B^{\prime}=C^{\prime}\,.

(60)

Therefore, the eigenstate of all the measurement settings
\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}},\Omega_{2}^{\text{\rm ECS}},\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}}
is the entangled coherent state

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1+e^{-|\alpha|^{2}})}}\Bigl[\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\Bigr].

(61)

∎

Appendix D
Appendix D: Verification of a different kind of ECS and its generalization

Following the proof of Theorem
1
in Appendix C, here we present the verification protocol for a different kind of entangled coherent state with the form
Sanders 1992

\displaystyle\mbox{$|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}^{(-)}}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}-\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\Bigr),

(62)

where the normalization is
C_{\alpha,\beta}^{(-)}=2\bigl[1-\mathrm{e}^{-(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2})/2}\bigr]
.
This state is different from
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
in Eq. (
13
) by a sign in the phase.

Using two-mode generalized Kennedy receivers, the state
|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle
behaves exactly the same as that of
|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle
.
The difference between these two states lies in their difference of the parity symmetry under displacements.
For
|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\!\mbox{$|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}^{(-)}}}\Bigl[C_{1}\mbox{$|-_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle$}\mbox{$|+_{\frac{\beta}{2}}\rangle$}-C_{2}\mbox{$|+_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle$}\mbox{$|-_{\frac{\beta}{2}}\rangle$}\Bigr],

(63)

where
C_{1}=\sqrt{C_{-}^{(\alpha/2)}C_{+}^{(\beta/2)}}
and
C_{2}=\sqrt{C_{+}^{(\alpha/2)}C_{-}^{(\beta/2)}}
.
Thus, to verify
|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
, the measurement settings are

\displaystyle{\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}}=\openone-\tau^{-}\otimes\tau^{-}\,,

(64)

\displaystyle{\Omega_{2}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}^{\text{\rm ECS}}=\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}(-\alpha,-\beta)(\openone-\tau^{-}\otimes\tau^{-})\mathbb{D}(-\alpha,-\beta)\,,

(65)

\displaystyle{\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\bigl(\pi\otimes\pi\bigr)^{-}\mathbb{D}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\!.

(66)

See the following theorem with its proof.

Theorem 3
.

The two-mode entangled coherent states
|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle
can be verified efficiently by the protocol

\displaystyle{\Omega^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}=\sum_{l=1}^{3}\mu_{l}{\Omega_{l}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}\,,

(67)

where the probability distribution
\{\mu_{l}\}
is arbitrary.
An optimal verification efficiency can be obtained by optimizing
\{\mu_{l}\}
under specific scenarios as constrained by Eq. (
5
).

Proof.

The first part of the proof is the same as that in Appendix C, such that the eigenstate of both
{\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}
and
{\Omega_{2}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}=B\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+C\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\,,\qquad(\forall B,C\in\mathbb{C})\,.

(68)

Then, considering the measurement
\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}
, we have

\displaystyle{\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\!\right)\!\Bigl[B\sum\mbox{$|2n\rangle$}\mbox{$|2m+1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2n|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2m+1|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+B\sum\mbox{$|2n+1\rangle$}\mbox{$|2m\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2n+1|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2m|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}

(69)

\displaystyle\qquad~~~+C\sum\mbox{$|2n\rangle$}\mbox{$|2m+1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2n|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2m+1|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+C\sum\mbox{$|2n+1\rangle$}\mbox{$|2m\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2n+1|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 2m|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}\!\left(\!-\frac{\alpha}{2},-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\!\Bigl[B\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(-\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)+B\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}-\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}-\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)+C\bigl(-\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\bigl(\mbox{$|\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|-\frac{\beta}{2}\rangle$}\bigr)\Bigr]\times\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{B-C}{2}\bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}-\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\bigr).

The constraint
{\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}
leads to

\displaystyle C=-B\,.

(70)

Therefore, the eigenstate of all the three measurement settings
\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)},\Omega_{2}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)},\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}
is the entangled coherent state

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi^{\prime}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}^{(-)}}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}-\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\Bigr).

(71)

∎

Furthermore, we consider a class of two-mode entangled coherent state with the general form
Sanders 1992

\displaystyle\mbox{$|{\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}^{(-)}}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|\alpha_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha_{2}\rangle$}-\mbox{$|\beta_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta_{2}\rangle$}\Bigr),

(72)

which is locally equivalent to
|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle
whenever the constraint

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}^{*})+\Im(\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}^{*})=2n\pi\,,\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z})

(73)

is satisfied.
The normalization is given by
\tilde{C}^{(-)}=2-\exp\bigl[-\frac{1}{2}(|\alpha_{1}|^{2}+|\beta_{1}|^{2}+|\alpha_{2}|^{2}+|\beta_{2}|^{2})\bigr]\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}^{*}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}^{*}\beta_{2}}+\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}^{*}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}^{*}}\bigr)
.
Moreover,
|{\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}\rangle
is also locally equivalent to
|{\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\rangle
whenever the constraint

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}^{*})+\Im(\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}^{*})=(2n+1)\pi\,,\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z})

(74)

is satisfied.
Hence, we conclude that the state
|{\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}}\rangle
can be verified by
\Omega^{\text{\rm ECS}}
or
\Omega^{\text{\rm ECS}(-)}
under proper local displacement operations whenever we have

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}^{*})+\Im(\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}^{*})=n\pi\,,\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z})\,.

(75)

Similarly, the constraint for verifying
|{\tilde{\psi}^{\text{\rm ECS}}}\rangle
as in Eq. (
14
) can be relaxed as the same.

Appendix E
Appendix E: Example of the optimization for
1/\nu_{\text{\rm opt}}

In this section we show how to perform numerical optimizations to find the optimal efficiency of
1/\nu_{\text{\rm opt}}
.
Due to the infinite dimensional Hilbert space of the CV quantum states, we cannot consider all the possible noises as that in the DV scenario
Pallister et al. 2018
.
Instead, here we tackle the problem from a more practical perspective.

The most common noise in reality is the decoherence of a mode due to photon absorption losses, which can be described by a single parameter
\eta
to represent the fraction of photons that survives the noisy channel, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\otimes\mbox{$|0\rangle$}_{E}\rightarrow\mbox{$|\sqrt{\eta}\alpha\rangle$}\otimes\mbox{$|\sqrt{1-\eta}\alpha\rangle$}_{E}\,,

(76)

where
\mbox{$|\cdot\rangle$}_{E}
refers to the environment mode.
Then we make the reasonable assumption that (i): The output state is measured immediately after the displacement, so the photon loss occurs mainly before the displacement.
With this assumption, we have

Decoherent States Type A:

The decoherent states caused by photon losses can be described as
\sigma_{a}=\mbox{$|\phi_{a}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\phi_{a}|$}
where

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi_{a}\rangle$}:=\mbox{$|\alpha-(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2\rangle$}\mbox{$|(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2\rangle$}+\mbox{$|(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha-(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2\rangle$}\,,

(77)

in which the normalization is omitted.
The passing probability
\mathrm{tr}(\Omega_{l}\sigma_{i})
of the decoherent states depends on the transmission probability
\eta
, so does the infidelity
\epsilon=1-\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\sigma_{i}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}
.
Then we can use the parameter
\eta
as a bridge to link the passing probability
\mathrm{tr}(\Omega_{l}\sigma_{i})
and the infidelity
\epsilon
.
Considering the case of
\alpha=1
, for the decoherent state
\sigma_{a}
with three measurement settings, the parameters
k_{l,i}
as defined in Eq. (
30
) of Appendix A are given by

\displaystyle k_{a}=\{0.42(7),0.42(7),0.00(0)\}\,.

(78)

The numerical results show that
{\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}}}
is not necessary for verifying such a decoherent state as we always have
\mathrm{tr}({\Omega_{3}^{\text{\rm ECS}}}\sigma_{a})=1
.
Moreover, we can confirm the symmetry of
\sigma_{a}
with respect to the two settings
{\Omega_{1}^{\text{\rm ECS}}}
and
\Omega_{2}^{\text{\rm ECS}}
.
Note that the numerical results presented here and below are all based on using the resolution PNRD
(5)
.

Another possible decoherence is from the imperfect construction of the states.
For instance, the construction decoherence from imperfect displacements can be described as

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\rightarrow\mbox{$|\alpha+\Delta\rangle$}\,,

(79)

where the small value
\Delta
represents the displacement error.
Then we make another reasonable assumption that (ii): The construction decoherence mainly comes from displacement of the two modes.
With this assumption, we have

Decoherent States Type B:

Considering the extreme case of one-mode error only, the decoherent states caused by displacement error can be described as
\sigma_{e}=\mbox{$|\phi_{e}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\phi_{e}|$}
where

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi_{e}\rangle$}:=\mbox{$|\alpha+\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mbox{$|\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\,.

(80)

The numerical results are

\displaystyle k_{e}=\{0.31(5),0.31(5),0.99(3)\}\,.

(81)

Decoherent States Type C:

Considering symmetric two-mode error, the decoherent states caused by displacement error can be described as
\sigma_{s}=\mbox{$|\phi_{s}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\phi_{s}|$}
where

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi_{s}\rangle$}:=\mbox{$|\alpha+\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\Delta\rangle$}+\mbox{$|\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha+\Delta\rangle$}\,.

(82)

The numerical results are

\displaystyle k_{s}=\{0.39(9),0.33(1),0.99(2)\}\,.

(83)

Next, we move on to consider these two kinds of decoherence effects together.
With the above two reasonable assumptions, the decoherent states can be defined as

|\phi_{e}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\alpha-(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2+\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2\rangle$}+\mbox{$|(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2+\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha-(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2\rangle$}\,,

|\phi_{s}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\alpha-(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2+\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2+\Delta\rangle$}+\mbox{$|(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2+\Delta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha-(1-\sqrt{\eta})\alpha/2+\Delta\rangle$}\,,

(84)

which are the noisy states in Eq. (
21
) of the main text used for demonstration.
Note that
\sigma_{e}=\sigma_{e}(100\%)
and
\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{s}(100\%)
are the cases if considering the displacement error only, and we relabel
\sigma_{a}=\sigma_{\Delta=0}
for clarity which is the case if considering the photon loss only.
In the following we discuss various scenarios.

Case 1:

If considering the photon loss and the displacement error independently, the decoherent states are given by

\displaystyle\{\sigma_{i}\}=\{\sigma_{e}(100\%),\sigma_{s}(100\%),\sigma_{\Delta=0}\}\,.

(85)

The parameters
k_{l,i}
form the following
3\text{(number of settings)}\times 3\text{(types of noise)}
matrix

\displaystyle K_{A}=\left[\begin{matrix}0.31(5)&0.39(9)&0.42(7)\\

With this, we can optimize the QSV protocol as in Eq. (

).
The objective of the optimization is to find the probability distribution
\{\mu_{l}\}
of the three measurement settings which makes the passing probability of the worst case to be minimal.
To be specific, we calculate the passing probability of all the possible decoherent states that we consider, and find the maximal one (i.e. the worst case) with certain
\{\mu_{l}\}
.
Then the probability distribution
\{\mu_{l}\}
is varied in order to get the optimal protocol.
In this case the optimal efficiency is given by

with

The parameters
k_{l,i}
form the following
3\text{(number of settings)}\times 4\text{(types of noise)}
matrix

Case 3:

If considering the photon loss and the symmetric two-mode displacement error together, the decoherent states are given by

\displaystyle\{\sigma_{i}\}=\{\sigma_{s}(100\%),\sigma_{s}(99\%),\sigma_{s}(98\%),\sigma_{\Delta=0}\}\,.

(89)

The parameters
k_{l,i}
form the following
3\text{(number of settings)}\times 4\text{(types of noise)}
matrix

\displaystyle K_{C}=\left[\begin{matrix}0.39(9)&0.42(2)&0.44(5)&0.42(7)\\

Hence, the optimal efficiency is given by

{1/\nu_{\text{\rm opt}}\approx 2.40(7)}
with

{\{\mu_{l}\}=\{0.97(2),0.00(0),0.02(8)\}}
.

Case 4:

If considering the photon loss and two kinds of displacement errors altogether, the decoherent states are given by

\displaystyle\{\sigma_{i}\}=\{\sigma_{e}(100\%),\sigma_{e}(99\%),\sigma_{e}(98\%),\sigma_{s}(100\%),\sigma_{s}(99\%),\sigma_{s}(98\%),\sigma_{\Delta=0}\}\,.

Hence, the optimal efficiency is given by

. See the results shown in Fig.

Appendix F: Proof of Theorem

Similar to the proof of Theorem

, we prove Theorem
2
by showing that

(1)

The eigenstate
|\phi\rangle
of all the
2(m\!-\!1)
measurement settings as defined in Eq. (
V
) is the superposition of the states
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}
and
\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}

(2)

|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\rangle

\Omega_{2m-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}
, where the measurement setting is defined in Eq. (
25

As shown in Appendix B, the two-mode GHZ-like state

|\psi^{\text{\rm ECS}}_{\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2}}\rangle

\openone\otimes D(-\alpha_{2})
.
Then, we have the following two lemmas directly.

Lemma 1
.

The eigenstates of
\Omega_{2l-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}
and
\Omega_{2l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}

(\forall l=1,2,\cdots,m\!-\!1)
are the superposition

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi_{l}\rangle$}=x\mbox{$|\alpha_{l}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha_{l+1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}+y\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\,,

(93)

where

are the normalized superposition amplitudes, and
|\psi_{1}\rangle
and
|\psi_{2}\rangle
are the states in the subspace formed without the two modes
l
and
l+1
.

Lemma 2
.

The eigenstates of
\Omega_{2l-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}
,
\Omega_{2l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}
,
\Omega_{2l+1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}
, and
\Omega_{2l+2}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}(\forall l=1,2,\cdots,m\!-\!2)
are the superposition

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi_{l}\rangle$}=x\mbox{$|\alpha_{l}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha_{l+1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha_{l+2}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}+y\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\,,

(94)

where
x,y
are the normalized superposition amplitudes, and
|\psi_{1}\rangle
and
|\psi_{2}\rangle
are the states in the subspace formed without the three modes

, and
l+2
.

Proof.

For clarity, we assign the labels
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
to the modes
\alpha_{l},\alpha_{l+1},\alpha_{l+2}

Following Lemma
1
, we have the eigenstates for
\Bigl\{\Omega_{2l-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}},\Omega_{2l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\Bigr\}
and
\Bigl\{\Omega_{2(l+1)-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}},\Omega_{2(l+1)}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\Bigr\}
given by

|\phi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}A_{k}\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\mbox{$|k\rangle$}+\sum_{k}B_{k}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|k\rangle$}\,,

(95)

|\phi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}C_{k}\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|k\rangle$}\mbox{$|\gamma\rangle$}+\sum_{k}D_{k}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|k\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\,,

(96)

where

are the normalized superposition amplitudes.
In order to get the eigenstate of all the four measurement settings, we let
\mbox{$|\phi_{1}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\phi_{2}\rangle$}

and expand them under the Fock bases

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m,p}A_{p}\mbox{$\langle n|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle m|\beta\rangle$}\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$|p\rangle$}+\sum_{p}B_{p}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|p\rangle$}

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m,p}C_{m}\mbox{$\langle n|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle p|\gamma\rangle$}\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$|p\rangle$}+\sum_{m}D_{m}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\,.

Note that the same terms should have the same superposition amplitudes.
To be specific, one has

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|p\rangle$}\,,&(p\neq 0)\\
\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|p\rangle$}\,,&(n\neq 0,p\neq 0)\end{array}\right\}&\Rightarrow&B_{p}=0\,,\quad(p\neq 0)\,,\\
\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\,,&(m\neq 0)\\
\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\,,&(n\neq 0,m\neq 0)\end{array}\right\}&\Rightarrow&D_{m}=0\,,\quad(m\neq 0)\,,\\
\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$|p\rangle$}\,,&(m\neq 0,p\neq 0)\\
\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\,,&(n\neq 0)\end{array}\right\}&\Rightarrow&A_{p}\mbox{$\langle m|\beta\rangle$}=C_{m}\mbox{$\langle p|\gamma\rangle$}\,,\quad(\forall m,p)\,,\\
\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\,,&\\
\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\,,&(n\neq 0)\end{array}\right\}&\Rightarrow&B_{0}=D_{0}\,.\end{array}

By defining
A_{p}/\mbox{$\langle p|\gamma\rangle$}=C_{m}/\mbox{$\langle m|\beta\rangle$}=T
and
B_{0}=D_{0}=K
, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\phi_{1}\rangle$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}T\mbox{$\langle p|\gamma\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\mbox{$|p\rangle$}+K\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}=T\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\gamma\rangle$}+K\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}

(111)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}T\mbox{$\langle m|\beta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\mbox{$|\gamma\rangle$}+K\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\phi_{2}\rangle$}\,,

for the subspace
l,l+1,l+2
.
Finally, we conclude that the eigenstate can be written as

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=T\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}\mbox{$|\gamma\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}+K\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\,.

(112)

∎

Therefore, we deduce that the eigenstates of all the
2(m\!-\!1)
measurement settings of the verification protocol
\Omega^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}
are the superposition of the states
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}
and
\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}
, of which we write as

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=x\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+y\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}\,.

(113)

Then, we consider the last measurement setting
\Omega^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}
, in which
\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{+}
is the projector onto the eigenspace with eigenvalue 1 of the operator
\pi^{\otimes m}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\pi^{\pm}\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{C_{\pm}}}{2}\mbox{$|\pm_{\alpha}\rangle$}=\frac{\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$}\pm\mbox{$|-\alpha\rangle$}}{2}\,.

(114)

Considering the symmetry of
\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{+}
that

\displaystyle\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{+}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
\sum t_{i}=\text{even}\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\pi^{t_{i}}\,,\quad(\text{here }\pi^{0}=\pi^{+},\pi^{1}=\pi^{-}\text{ for clarity})\,,

(115)

direct calculation shows

\displaystyle\Omega_{2m-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl[\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}D^{\dagger}(-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2})\biggr]\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{+}\biggl[x\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\rangle$}+y\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\frac{-\alpha_{i}}{2}\rangle$}\biggr]

(116)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{2^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
\sum t_{i}=\text{even}\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl[\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+(-1)^{t_{i}}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\Bigr]+\frac{y}{2^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
\sum t_{i}=\text{even}\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl[\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+(-1)^{t_{i}}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{2^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
\sum t_{i}=\text{even}\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl[\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+(-1)^{t_{i}}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\Bigr]+\frac{y}{2^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
\sum t_{i}=\text{even}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\sum t_{i}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl[\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+(-1)^{t_{i}}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x+y}{2^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\

With the constraint
\Omega_{2m-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}
, we have

.
Then the eigenstate of all the measurement settings of the protocol

must be the coherent GHZ-like state

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\psi^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\Biggl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}\Biggr).

(117)

Appendix G
Appendix G: Verification of a different kind of GHZ-like state and its generalization

Consider a different class of multimode entangled coherent state

\displaystyle\mbox{$|{\psi^{\prime}}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C^{\prime}}}\Biggl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}-\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}\Biggr),

where the normalization is

Similar to the verification of
|{\psi}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle

|{\psi^{\prime}}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\rangle

(2m\!-\!1)

\displaystyle{\Omega^{\prime}}_{2l-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{2l-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}=\mathcal{P}_{l}\biggl\{\!\Bigl[B_{2l\!-\!1}^{\dagger}(\openone\!-\tau^{-}\!\otimes\tau^{-})B_{2l\!-\!1}\Bigr]\!\otimes\openone^{\otimes(m-2)}\!\biggr\},

\displaystyle{\Omega^{\prime}}_{2l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}

\displaystyle\Omega_{2l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}=\mathcal{P}_{l}\biggl\{\!\Bigl[B_{2l}^{\dagger}(\openone\!-\tau^{-}\!\otimes\tau^{-})B_{2l}\Bigr]\!\otimes\openone^{\otimes(m-2)}\!\biggr\},

(119)

\displaystyle{\Omega^{\prime}}_{2m-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Biggl[\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}D^{\dagger}(-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2})\Biggr]\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{-}\Biggl[\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}D(-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2})\Biggr],

with

, where

and

are local operations.
The symbol

indicates the permutation that only the
l
and
l+1
modes are operated on for each setting.
See the following theorem with its proof.

Theorem 4
.

The
m
-mode GHZ-like coherent states
|{\psi^{\prime}}^{\text{\rm GHZ-m}}\rangle
can be verified efficiently by the protocol

\displaystyle{\Omega^{\prime}}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}=\sum_{l=1}^{2m-1}\mu_{l}{\Omega^{\prime}}_{l}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}\,,

(120)

where the probability distribution
\{\mu_{l}\}

An optimal verification efficiency can be obtained by optimizing

Proof.

Same as the proof in Appendix F, the eigenstates of all the
2(m\!-\!1)
measurement settings
{\Omega^{\prime}}_{2l-1}^{\text{\rm GHZ-}m}

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=x\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\alpha_{i}\rangle$}+y\mbox{$|0\rangle$}^{\otimes m}\,.

Considering the symmetry of the parity measurement

\displaystyle\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{-}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
\sum t_{i}=\text{odd}\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\pi^{t_{i}}\,,\quad(\text{here }\pi^{0}=\pi^{+},\pi^{1}=\pi^{-}\text{ for clarity})\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl[\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}D^{\dagger}(-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2})\biggr]\bigl(\pi^{\otimes m}\bigr)^{-}\biggl[x\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\rangle$}+y\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{$|\frac{-\alpha_{i}}{2}\rangle$}\biggr]

(123)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{2^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\

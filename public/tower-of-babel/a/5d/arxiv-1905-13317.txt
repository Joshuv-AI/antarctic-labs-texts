Talagrand’s inequality in planar Gaussian field percolation
Alejandro Rivera

Abstract
Let f f be a stationary isotropic non-degenerate Gaussian field on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . Assume that f = q ∗ W f=q*W where q ∈ L 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) ∩ C 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) and W W is the L 2 L^{2} white noise on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . We extend a result by Stephen Muirhead and Hugo Vanneuville by showing that, assuming that q ∗ q q*q is pointwise non-negative and has fast enough decay, the set { f ≥ − ℓ } \{f\geq-\ell\} percolates with probability one when ℓ > 0 \ell>0 and with probability zero if ℓ ≤ 0 \ell\leq 0 . We also prove exponential decay of crossing probabilities and uniqueness of the unbounded cluster. To this end, we study a Gaussian field g g defined on the torus and establish a superconcentration formula for the threshold T ​ ( g ) \textup{T}(g) which is the minimal value such that { g ≥ − T ( g ) } \{g\geq-\textup{T}(g)\} contains a non-contractible loop. This formula follows from a Gaussian Talagrand type inequality.

Standard results on Gaussian fields

The results presented in this appendix are well known, though we were unable to find a reference presenting them in the setting of the present paper.

A.1
Orthogonal expansions and the Cameron-Martin space

Let
\gamma
be a Gaussian measure on
\mathcal{T}
and let
f
be a Gaussian field on
\mathcal{T}
with law
\gamma
, defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and let
G\subset\Omega
be the
L^{2}
-closure of the set of variables
f(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{T}
. Then, the
Cameron-Martin space
of
f
is the space
H_{\gamma}
of functions
h_{\xi}:x\mapsto\mathbb{E}[\xi f(x)]
for
\xi\in G
. If
H_{\gamma}
is finite dimensional, then
f
has the law of a standard Gaussian vector in
H_{\gamma}
in the following sense. Let
(e_{j})_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
be an orthonormal basis in
H_{\gamma}
. First,
f\in H
a.s. Moreover, the family
(\langle f,e_{j}\rangle)_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
is a family of independent standard normals.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be indepenent standard normals and let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in C^{0}(\mathcal{T})
be such that
(e_{k})_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
is linearly independent. Let
f=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}
. Then, the family
(e_{k})_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
is an orthonormal basis for the Cameron-Martin space of
f
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the Cameron-Martin space of
f
. Let
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Since
(e_{j})_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
is linearly independent, there exists
x_{k}\in\mathcal{T}
such that for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
e_{j}(x)=\delta_{jk}
. In particular,
\mathbb{E}[f(x_{k})f]=e_{k}
belongs to
H
. Conversly, since for each
x\in\mathcal{T}
,
\mathbb{E}[f(x)f]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}(x)e_{k}
,
(e_{j})_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
generates
H
. Now, let
j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then,
\langle e_{j},e_{k}\rangle=\mathbb{E}[f(x_{j})f(x_{k})]=e_{j}(x_{k})=\delta_{jk}
so that
(e_{l})_{l\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
is an orthonormal family in
H
.
∎

A.2
Supremum bounds

We
\mathcal{T}
equip with the distance associated to the metric inherited from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which we denote by
dist
. For each
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu\in]0,1[
let
C^{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
be the space of
l
times differentiable functions
q:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
whose derivatives of order
l
are of Hölder class
\nu
. We equip this space with the usual norm:

\|q\|_{C^{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})}=\max_{|\alpha|\leq l}\left[\max_{x\in\mathcal{T}}|\partial^{\alpha}q(x)|+\sup_{x\neq y}\textup{dist}(x,y)^{-\nu}|\partial^{\alpha}q(x)-\partial^{\alpha}q(y)|\right]\,.

Moreover, we let
C^{l,\nu;l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
be the space of functions
K:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq l
,
\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K
exists and is of Hölder class
C^{\nu}
in the joint variables. We equip this space with the norm
\|K\|_{C^{l,\nu;l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})}
defined as follows:

\max_{|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq l}\left[\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{T}}|\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K(x,y)|+\sup_{(x,y)\neq(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}|\partial^{\alpha,\alpha}K(x,y)-\partial^{\alpha,\alpha}K(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\left(\textup{dist}(x,y)+\textup{dist}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right)^{-\nu}\right]\,.

Here and below,
\partial^{\alpha,\beta}
means the partial derivative
\partial^{\alpha}
is applied to the first variable while
\partial^{\beta}
is applied to the second variable. The following lemma is a well known fact on the sample path regularity of Gaussian fields.

Lemma A.2

(see Corollary 1.7 of
[
3
]
together with Appendix A of
[
21
]
)
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu\in]0,1[
. Let
f
be a Gaussian field on
\mathcal{T}
with covariance function
K\in C^{l,\nu;l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
. Then,
f
is almost surely of class
C^{l}
. Moreover, for each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq l
and each
x,y\in\mathcal{T}

\mathbb{E}\left[\partial^{\alpha}f(x)\partial^{\beta}f(y)\right]=\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K(x,y)\,.

Next we need a lemma to control the covariance of a stationary field in terms of its covariance square root.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu\in]0,1[
, let
q\in C^{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
. Then, the convolution
\kappa(x)=q*q(x)=\int_{\mathcal{T}}q(y)q(x-y)dy
exists there exists
C=C(l,d,\nu)<+\infty
such that

\|\kappa\|_{C^{l}(\mathcal{T})}\leq C\|q\|_{W^{l,2}}^{2}\textup{ and }\|\kappa\|_{C^{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})}\leq C\|q\|_{C^{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})}\|q\|_{W^{l,1}(\mathcal{T})}\,.

The first upper bound is useful because it requires a slower decay of
q
to work while the second one is useful because it captures the Hölder regularity of the covariance.

Proof.

First of all, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq l
and each
x\in\mathcal{T}
, by integration by parts,

|\partial^{\alpha+\beta}\kappa(x)|\leq\int_{\mathcal{T}}|\partial^{\alpha}q(y)\partial^{\beta}q(x-y)|dy\leq\|q\|_{W^{l,2}}^{2}\leq\|q\|_{W^{l,1}}\|q\|_{C^{l}}\,.

Observe that the first inequality shows the first statement of the lemma. Next, for each
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
distinct,

|\partial^{\alpha+\beta}\kappa(x)-\partial^{\alpha+\beta}\kappa(y)|\leq\int_{\mathcal{T}}|\partial^{\alpha+\beta}q(z)||q(x-z)-q(y-z)|dz\leq\|q\|_{W^{l,1}}\|q\|_{C^{l,\nu}}\textup{dist}(x,y)^{\nu}

so that
\|\partial^{\alpha+\beta}\kappa\|_{C^{\nu}}\leq\|q\|_{W^{l,1}}\|q\|_{C^{l,\nu}}
. Summing the two inequalities over the possible values of
\alpha+\beta
with
|\alpha+\beta|\leq l
we get the result.
∎

Remark A.4

Lemma A.5

(A.2)

Remark A.6

. We can define a Gaussian field on
\mathcal{T}\times J_{l}
defined as
(x,\alpha)\mapsto\partial^{\alpha}f(x)
. Then,
\|f\|_{C^{l}(\mathcal{T})}
is the sup-norm of this process. The maximal pointwise variance of this process is bounded by
M_{K,l,\nu}^{2}
so the aforementioned theorem applies and gives (
A.2
).
∎

A.3
An approximation result

For each
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu\in]0,1[
, let
\Gamma_{l}(\mathcal{T})
(resp.
\Gamma_{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
be the space of Gaussian measures on
C^{l}(\mathcal{T})
(resp.
C^{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
) equipped with the topology of weak-* convergence in
C^{l}
(resp.
C^{l,\nu}
) topology. For each
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{T})
, let
H_{\gamma}
be the Cameron-Martin space of
\gamma
.

Lemma A.7
.

Fix
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu\in]0,1[
. Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of Gaussian measures in
\Gamma_{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
(resp.
\Gamma_{l}(\mathcal{T})
) converging to
\gamma
in
\Gamma_{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
(resp.
\Gamma_{l}(\mathcal{T})
). Let
K
be the covariance function of the field defined by
\Gamma
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
K_{n}
be the covariance of the field defined by
\gamma_{n}
. Then, the sequence
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
K
in
C^{l,\nu;l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
(resp.
C^{l;l}(\mathcal{T})
).

Proof.

Let us first assume convergence in
\Gamma_{l}(\mathcal{T})
. By Skorokhod’s representation theorem, we can find a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Gaussian fields converging a.s. to a Gaussian field
f
in
C^{l,\nu}
-norm such that
f
has law
\gamma
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{n}
has law
\gamma_{n}
. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq l
. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be continuous function with compact support. Then,

\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{T}}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi\left(\partial^{\alpha}f_{n}(x),\partial^{\beta}f_{n}(y)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi\left(\partial^{\alpha}f(x),\partial^{\beta}f(y)\right)\right]\right|\\
\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{x,y}\left|\varphi\left(\partial^{\alpha}f_{n}(x),\partial^{\beta}f_{n}(y)\right)-\varphi\left(\partial^{\alpha}f(x),\partial^{\beta}f(y)\right)\right|\right]

which tends to
0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
since
\varphi
is uniformly continuous and
(\partial^{\alpha}f_{n},\partial^{\beta}f_{n})
converges uniformly on
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}
. Thus, the vectors
(\partial^{\alpha}f_{n}(x),\partial^{\beta}f_{n}(y))
converge to
(\partial^{\alpha}f(x),\partial^{\beta}f(y))
uniformly in
(x,y)
. But this implies that
\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K_{n}
converges uniformly to
\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus, convergence in
\Gamma_{l}
implies convergence of covariances in
C^{l;l}
. Assume now that
(\gamma_{n})_{n_{\in}\mathbb{N}}
converges in
\Gamma_{l,\nu}(\mathcal{T})
to
\gamma
. As before, the supremum over
x,x^{\prime},y\in\mathcal{T}
,
x\neq x^{\prime}
of the following quantity tends to
0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
.

\Big|\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi\left(\textup{dist}(x,x^{\prime})^{-\nu}\left(\partial^{\alpha}f_{n}(x)-\partial^{\alpha}f_{n}(x^{\prime})\right),\partial^{\beta}f_{n}(y)\right)\right]-\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi\left(\textup{dist}(x,x^{\prime})^{-\nu}\left(\partial^{\alpha}f(x)-\partial^{\alpha}f(x^{\prime})\right),\partial^{\beta}f(y)\right)\right]\Big|\,.

This shows that the map
(x,x^{\prime},y)\mapsto\textup{dist}(x,x^{\prime})^{-\nu}\left(\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K_{n}(x,y)-\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K_{n}(x^{\prime},y)\right)
converge uniformly as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. By symmetry we conclude that
\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K_{n}
converges to
\partial^{\alpha,\beta}K
in the
C^{0,\nu;0,\nu}
topology as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

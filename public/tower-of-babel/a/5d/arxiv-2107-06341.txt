Hybrid A Posteriori Error Estimators for Conforming Finite Element Approximations to Stationary Convection-Diffusion-Reaction equations
Difeng Cai, Zhiqiang Cai

Abstract
We consider the a posteriori error estimation for convection-diffusion-reaction equations in both diffusion-dominated and convection/reaction-dominated regimes. We present an explicit hybrid estimator, which, in each regime, is proved to be reliable and efficient with constants independent of the parameters in the underlying problem. For convection-dominated problems, the norm introduced by Verfürth [ 33 ] is used to measure the approximation error. Various numerical experiments are performed to ( 1 ) (1) demonstrate the robustness of the hybrid estimator; ( 2 ) (2) show that the hybrid estimator is more accurate than the explicit residual estimator and is less sensitive to the size of reaction, even though both of them are robust.



1
Introduction

The a posteriori error estimation has been an indispensable tool
in handling computationally challenging problems.
For elliptic partial differential equations (PDEs) consisting of terms characterizing diffusion, reaction and convection,
the solution may display a strong interface singularity,
interior or boundary layers, etc.,
due to discontinuous coefficients or terms in significantly different scales, etc.
Consequently,
it is a challenging task to design a
general
a posteriori error estimator
that is accurate and robust enough
to resolve local behaviors of the exact solution without spending much computational resource.

Explicit residual estimators are directly related to the error and have been well-studied since 1970s
(cf.
[
7
,
6
,
30
,
9
,
23
,
32
,
31
,
33
,
29
]
).
They are easy to compute, applicable to a large class of problems, valid for higher order elements, etc.
More importantly,

robust
residual estimators have been proposed for various problems.
For diffusion problems with discontinuous coefficient,
[
9
,
23
]
established robustness with respect to coefficient jump
under the monotonicity or quasi-monotonicity assumption of the diffusion coefficient.
For singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion problems,
Verfürth
[
32
]
pioneered a residual estimator that is robust with respect to the size of reaction.
For convection-dominated problems,
it is still under debate on how to choose a suitable norm to measure the approximation error
(cf.
[
28
,
31
,
24
,
25
,
33
,
26
]
).
Sangalli
[
25
,
26
]
proposed a norm
incorporating the standard energy norm and
a seminorm of order
1/2
and developed the a posteriori error analysis in the one-dimensional setting.
Verfürth
[
33
]
introduced
a norm incorporating the standard energy norm and
a dual norm of the convective derivative.
With respect to this norm, the explicit residual estimator in
[
33
]
was proved to be robust.
Moreover, it was shown in
[
29
]
that the framework of residual estimators is applicable for various stabilization schemes.
Those developments make residual estimators competitive when dealing with challenging problems.
One drawback, however,
is that residual estimators tend to overestimate the true error by a large margin (cf.
[
14
,
10
]
).
This calls for the need of an estimator as general as the residual estimator but with improved accuracy.

Recovery-based estimators,
e.g., the Zienkiewicz–Zhu (ZZ) estimator and its variations
(cf.
[
34
,
35
,
27
,
13
,
21
,
8
,
18
]
, etc.),
are quite popular in the engineering community.
However, unlike residual estimators,
the robustness of those estimators
with respect to issues like coefficient jump, dominated convection or reaction, etc.,
has not been emphasized or studied in detail yet (cf.
[
22
]
).
On coarse meshes, it is known that ZZ-type estimators are in general unreliable,
and counterexamples can be easily constructed where the estimator vanishes but the true error is large (cf.
[
3
,
10
]
).
For linear elements,
[
18
,
17
]

adds two additional terms (one of them is the element residual) to the ZZ estimator to ensure reliability on coarse meshes.
For higher order elements, however, a straightforward extension of the original ZZ estimator
[
34
,
35
]

usually fails and developing a viable estimator is nontrivial.
For example, Bank, Xu, and Zheng in
[
8
]
recently introduced a recovery-based estimator for Lagrange triangular elements of degree
p
,
and their estimator requires recovery of all partial derivatives of
p^{\rm th}
order
instead of the gradient.

Recently, the so-called
hybrid estimator
was introduced in
[
10
,
12
]
for diffusion problems with discontinuous coefficients.
The explicit hybrid estimator shares all advantages of the robust residual estimator
[
9
,
23
]

and numerical results indicate that the hybrid estimator is more accurate than the residual estimator (cf.
[
10
]
).
This opens a door of finding an alternative of the residual estimator with improved accuracy.
Thus one may ask if it is possible to construct hybrid estimators for more general problems
and if the hybrid estimator is still more accurate than the residual estimator.

In this manuscript,
we introduce the hybrid estimator as well as flux recoveries for convection-diffusion-reaction equations.
In diffusion-dominated regime,
the flux recovery as well as the hybrid estimator is a natural extension of the one in
[
10
]
.
In convection/reaction-dominated regime,
the flux recovery in each element depends on the size of diffusion.
Roughly speaking,
in elements with resolved diffusion (see Section
4.2.1
),
the recovered flux is same to the diffusion-dominated case;
in elements where diffusion is not resolved,
inspired in part by the method of Ainsworth and Vejchodský
[
4
, Section 3.4]
,
the recovered flux is defined piecewisely in each element (see Section
4.2.2
).
The hybrid estimator in the convection/reaction-dominated regime is analogously defined as in
[
10
]
with proper weights from
[
33
]
.
In each regime, we prove that the hybrid estimator is equivalent to the robust residual estimator (for example,
[
33
]
for the convection/reaction-dominated regime) and
then the robustness follows immediately from that of the residual estimator.
The hybrid estimator is explicit and valid for higher order elements.
Various numerical results show that, compared to the explicit residual estimator,
the hybrid estimator is more accurate and the corresponding effectivity index is less sensitive to the size of reaction.

The rest of the manuscript is organized as follows.
The model problems and finite element discretizations are introduced in Section
2
.
Section
3
collects results on robust residual estimators.
After the flux recovery presented in Section
4
,
the hybrid estimator is defined in Section
5
along with robust a posteriori error estimates.
Section
6
gives the proof of the local equivalence between
the residual estimator and the hybrid estimator.
Numerical results are shown in Section
7
.

2
Problems and Discretizations

Let
\Omega
be a polygonal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\,
(
d=2,\,3
) with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
consisting of two disjoint components
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
.
By convention, assume that
\text{diam}(\Omega)=O(1)
.
Consider the stationary convection-diffusion-reaction equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\text{div}(\alpha\nabla u)+\bm{a}\cdot\nabla u+bu&=f,\quad&&\text{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
u&=0,\quad&&\text{on}\;\;\Gamma_{D},\\
-\alpha\nabla u\cdot\bm{n}&=g_{{}_{N}},\quad&&\text{on}\;\;\Gamma_{N},\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

with
\alpha(x)\geq\delta
, for almost all
x\in\Omega
and for some constant
\delta>0
.
Assume that:

(A1)

\bm{a}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}
and
b\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
;

(A2)

there are two constants
\beta\geq 0
and
c_{b}\geq 0
,
independent of
\alpha
, such that

b-\frac{1}{2}\text{div}\,\,\bm{a}\geq\beta\;\text{in}\;\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert b\rVert_{\infty}\leq c_{b}\beta;

(A3)

\text{meas}(\Gamma_{D})>0
and
\Gamma_{D}
contains the inflow boundary

\{x\in\partial\Omega:\bm{a}(x)\cdot\bm{n}(x)<0\}.

Depending on the magnitude of the
\alpha
(with respect to
\bm{a}
and
b
),
two regimes are studied in this paper:

1.

diffusion-dominated regime
:
there exists a constant
C_{b}\geq 0
such that

|\bm{a}(x)/\alpha(x)|\leq C_{b}\quad\text{and}\quad|b(x)/\alpha(x)|\leq C_{b}\quad\text{for almost all }x\in\Omega;

2.

convection/reaction-dominated regime
:

\alpha(x)\equiv\epsilon\ll 1
for a constant
\epsilon>0
.
This is the so-called singularly perturbed problem.

Let

H^{1}_{D}(\Omega):=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0\}.

Define the bilinear form on
H^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
by

B(u,v):=(\alpha\nabla u,\nabla v)+(\bm{a}\cdot\nabla u,v)+(bu,v),\quad\forall\,u,v\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega),

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{S}
denotes the
L^{2}
inner product on set
S
and the subscript
S
is omitted when
S=\Omega
. The
L^{2}
norm on
S
is denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{S}
.

The weak formulation of (
1
) is to find
u\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
such that

B(u,v)=(f,v)-(g_{{}_{N}},v)_{\Gamma_{N}},\quad\forall\,v\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega).

(2)

It follows from integration by parts and the assumptions in (A2) and (A3) that, for any
v\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
,

(\bm{a}\cdot\nabla v,v)+(bv,v)=\frac{1}{2}(v^{2},\bm{a}\cdot\bm{n})_{\Gamma_{N}}+(v^{2},b-\frac{1}{2}\text{div}\,\,\bm{a})\geq\beta\lVert v\rVert^{2},

(3)

where
\bm{n}
denotes the unit outward vector normal to
\Gamma_{N}
.
The energy norm induced by
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left(\lVert\alpha^{1/2}\nabla v\rVert^{2}+\beta\lVert v\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2},\quad\forall\,v\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega),

where
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{S}
denotes the energy norm over
S
and
the subscript
S
is omitted when
S=\Omega
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a regular triangulation of
\Omega
(see, e.g.,
[
15
]
).
Define the following sets associated with the triangulation
{\mathcal{T}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle:\text{ the set of all vertices},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle:\text{ the set of all edges}(d=2)/\text{faces}(d=3),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{I}

\displaystyle:\text{ the set of all interior edges}(d=2)/\text{faces}(d=3),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{D}

\displaystyle:\text{ the set of all edges}(d=2)/\text{faces}(d=3)\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}

\displaystyle:\text{ the set of all edges}(d=2)/\text{faces}(d=3)\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{K}

\displaystyle:\text{ the set of edges}(d=2)/\text{faces}(d=3)\text{ in an element }K\in{\mathcal{T}}.

For a simplex
S\in{\mathcal{T}}\cup\mathcal{E}
,
denote by
|S|
and
h_{S}
its measure and diameter, respectively.
Denote by
R_{K}
the inradius of
K\in{\mathcal{T}}
.
The shape regularity of the triangulation requires the existence of a generic constant
C_{0}>1

such that

h_{K}\leq C_{0}R_{K},\quad\forall\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}

(4)

holds true for each mesh
{\mathcal{T}}
in the adaptive mesh refinement procedure.

We associate each
e\in\mathcal{E}
with a unit normal
\bm{n}_{e}
, which is chosen as the unit outward normal if
e\subset\partial\Omega
.
Denote by
K_{e}^{+}
and
K_{e}^{-}
the two elements
sharing
e
such that the unit outward normal of
K_{e}^{+}
on
e
coincides with
\bm{n}_{e}
.
For
e\in\mathcal{E}_{I}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}
, let
K_{e}^{\prime}
denote the element next to
K

sharing
e
in common.
Let
\omega_{e}
be the union of elements adjacent to
e\in\mathcal{E}

and
\omega_{K}
be the union of elements that share at least one face with
K\in{\mathcal{T}}
.
Unless otherwise stated,

\bm{n}
always denotes the unit outward normal vector on
\partial K
.

For
k=0,1,2,\dots
,
let
P_{k}(S)
denote the set of polynomials of degree at most
k
on
S\in{\mathcal{T}}\cup\mathcal{E}

and
\Pi_{S}^{k}
denote the
L^{2}(S)
-projection onto
P_{k}(S)
.
Define the conforming finite element space of order
k
(
k\geq 1
) by

\displaystyle V_{{\mathcal{T}}}

\displaystyle:=\{v\in C(\Omega):v|_{K}\in P_{k}(K),\;\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}},\text{ and }v|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0\}.

For each
K\in{\mathcal{T}}
, the Raviart-Thomas space of index
k-1
(
k\geq 1
) is

\mathrm{RT}_{k-1}(K):=\left\{\bm{\tau}\in L^{2}(K)^{d}:\bm{\tau}=\bm{p}+\bm{x}q,\;\bm{p}\in P_{k-1}(K)^{d},\;q\in P_{k-1}(K)\right\}.

The projected data
\bar{f}
and
\bar{g}_{{}_{N}}
are defined by

\bar{f}|_{K}:=\Pi_{K}^{k-1}f,\quad\forall\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{g}_{{}_{N}}|_{e}:=\Pi_{K}^{k-1}g_{{}_{N}},\quad\forall\,e\in\mathcal{E}_{N},

respectively.

The standard finite element approximation for problem (
1
) is to find
u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}}
such that

B(u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}},v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})=(f,v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})-(g_{{}_{N}},v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})_{\Gamma_{N}},\quad\forall\,v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}}.

(5)

In the case that the convection is dominant, one often adds a stabilization term along the convective direction.
For example, the so-called SUPG method in
[
19
]

is to find
u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}}
such that

B_{\delta}(u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}},v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})=l_{\delta}(v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}),\quad\forall\,v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}},

(6)

where the stabilized bilinear and linear forms are given by

B_{\delta}(u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}},v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})=B(u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}},v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\delta_{K}(-\epsilon\Delta u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}+\bm{a}\cdot\nabla u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}+bu_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}},\bm{a}\cdot\nabla v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})_{K}

for all
u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}},v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}}
and

l_{\delta}(v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})=(f,v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})-(g_{{}_{N}},v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})_{\Gamma_{N}}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\delta_{K}(f,\bm{a}\cdot\nabla v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})_{K}

for all
v_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}}
,
respectively.
Here, the stabilization parameters
\delta_{K}
are nonnegative and satisfy

\delta_{K}\lVert\bm{a}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq Ch_{K},\quad\forall\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}.

To measure the convective derivative,
the following dual norm was used in
[
33
]
:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}=\sup_{v\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\dfrac{\langle\phi,v\rangle}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}},

where
\phi
is in the dual space of
H^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing.
Following
[
33
]
,
the dual norm will be combined with the energy norm to measure the approximation error
in the convection-dominated regime.

To keep the exposition simple, we ignore data oscillation in coefficients
by further assuming that
for each
K\in{\mathcal{T}}
,

\alpha_{\!{}_{K}}:=\alpha|_{K}
,
\bm{a}|_{K}
, and
b|_{K}
are constants.
The algorithm and analysis remain valid without this assumption
if we replace those quantities with their proper projections
and add the corresponding oscillation error in the estimates.

Define

\alpha_{e}:=\max_{K\subseteq\omega_{e}}\alpha_{\!{}_{K}},\quad\alpha_{\max}:=\max_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\alpha_{\!{}_{K}},\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{\min}:=\min_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\alpha_{\!{}_{K}}.

For diffusion-dominated case,
we are interested in the case where
\alpha
may be discontinuous
and the hybrid estimator is robust with respect to the discontinuity.
For convection/reaction-dominated case (
\alpha=\epsilon\ll 1
),
we design a hybrid estimator that is robust with respect to
\epsilon
and
\beta

in appropriate norms.

3
Residual Estimator

Let
\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}=-\alpha\nabla u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
be the numerical flux,
then the element residual
r_{K}\in L^{2}(K)
and the flux jump across
e\in\mathcal{E}
are given by

r_{K}:=\bar{f}-\bm{a}\cdot\nabla u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}-bu_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}-\text{div}\,\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}

(7)

and

j_{e}:=\begin{cases}(\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|_{K_{e}^{+}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|_{K_{e}^{-}})\cdot\bm{n}_{e},&\text{if }e\in\mathcal{E}_{I},\\[5.69054pt]
\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cdot\bm{n}-\bar{g}_{{}_{N}},&\text{if }e\in\mathcal{E}_{N},\\[5.69054pt]
0,&\text{if }e\in\mathcal{E}_{D},\end{cases}

(8)

respectively.
For
S\in{\mathcal{T}}\cup\mathcal{E}
, define the weight
\gamma_{{}_{S}}
as below

\gamma_{{}_{S}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{in diffusion-dominated regime},\\
\min\{1,h_{S}^{-1}\alpha_{{}_{S}}^{1/2}\beta^{-1/2}\},&\text{in convection/reaction-dominated regime}.\end{cases}

(9)

The residual estimator is defined by

\eta=\left(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\eta_{K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{with}\quad\eta_{K}^{2}=\gamma_{{}_{K}}^{2}h_{K}^{2}\alpha_{\!{}_{K}}^{-1}\lVert r_{K}\rVert_{K}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\gamma_{e}h_{e}\alpha_{e}^{-1}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}^{2}.

(10)

Note that for diffusion-dominated problems,
the
\eta
is a simple extension of the one in
[
9
]
or
[
23
]
for pure diffusion problems;
for convection/reaction-dominated problems,
the
\eta
is introduced by Verfürth in
[
33
]
.
In fact,

\gamma_{{}_{S}}h_{S}\epsilon_{{}_{S}}^{-1/2}
is same to the weight defined in
[
33
, Eq.(3.4)]
.
Here,
\gamma_{{}_{S}}
is additional weight needed for convection/reaction-dominated problems.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that in the case of vanishing reaction
(
i.e.,
\beta=0
)
,
\gamma_{{}_{S}}=1

and consequently the weights in
\eta_{K}
(
10
) are identical to the ones for diffusion problems
[
9
]
.

In the remainder of this section, we will discuss reliability and efficiency bounds of the estimator
\eta
.

3.1
Convection/reaction-dominated regime

The global reliability and efficiency bounds of the
\eta
were established by Verfürth in
[
33
, Theorem 4.1]
.
The reliability and efficiency constants are uniform with respect to
\epsilon
and
\beta
.
For reader’s convenience, they are cited below.

Define the data oscillation on
K
by

\Theta_{K}^{2}=\gamma_{{}_{K}}^{2}h_{K}^{2}\alpha_{\!{}_{K}}^{-1}\lVert f-\bar{f}\rVert_{K}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{N}}\gamma_{e}h_{e}\alpha_{e}^{-1}\lVert g_{{}_{N}}-\bar{g}_{{}_{N}}\rVert_{e}^{2},\quad\forall\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
2
) and
u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
be the solution of (
5
) or (
6
).
Let
\eta_{K}
be defined in (
10
).
Then

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{a}\cdot\nabla(u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}^{2}\leq C_{1}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\left(\eta_{K}^{2}+\Theta_{K}^{2}\right)

and

\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\eta_{K}^{2}\leq C_{2}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{a}\cdot\nabla(u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\Theta_{K}^{2}\right),

where the constants
C_{1}
and

are independent of
\epsilon,\beta
, and any mesh-size.

with or without subscripts to denote a generic nonnegative constant,

\epsilon
and
\beta
for dominant convection/reaction, or

for the dominant diffusion,
but may depend on the shape parameter of mesh

and on the polynomial degree

The reaction-dominated diffusion problem,
i.e., with
\bm{a}=0
,

corresponds to the singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\epsilon\Delta u+u&=f\quad&&\text{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad&&\text{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(11)

The assumptions in Section

For the finite element approximation in (

) to the problem in (

in (

.

Let

3.2
Diffusion-dominated regime

For the diffusion-dominated case,
following the pure diffusion case in
[
9
]
or
[

]
,

These estimates are proved to be robust in terms of the discontinuity of the diffusion coefficient

To this end, let

u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
be the finite element solution in (

An alternative and often used expression of the element residual

r_{K}=-\text{div}\,(\alpha\nabla(u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}))+\bm{a}\cdot\nabla(u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})+b(u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})+\bar{f}-f.

(12)

Theorem 3.3
.

Under the monotonicity assumption
[
9
, Hypothesis 2.7]
of
\alpha
, the residual estimator
\eta
satisfies the following reliability estimate: there exists a positive constant
C
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leq C\left(\eta^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\Theta_{K}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
w:=u-u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
.
To prove the reliability bound,
according to
[
9
]
,
it suffices to derive an estimate of the error in the form below:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}(r_{K},w-w_{{\mathcal{T}}})_{K}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}(j_{e},w-w_{{\mathcal{T}}})_{e}

(13)

\displaystyle+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}(\bar{f}-f,w-w_{{\mathcal{T}}})_{K}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}(\bar{g}_{{}_{N}}-g_{{}_{N}},w-w_{{\mathcal{T}}})_{e},\quad\forall\,w_{{\mathcal{T}}}\in V_{{\mathcal{T}}}.

To do so, it follows from integration by parts, (
12

4
Flux Recovery

We show in this section how to recover a suitable flux in
H(\text{div};\Omega)
, denoted by
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}
,
such that the resulting hybrid estimator is robust.
Same to
[
10
]
, the recovered normal component
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|_{K}\cdot\bm{n}=\hat{g}_{{}_{K}}\in L^{2}(\partial K)
on
e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
is defined as a weighted average of normal components:

\hat{g}_{{}_{K}}|_{e}:=\begin{cases}\lambda_{K,e}\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|_{K}\cdot\bm{n}+(1-\lambda_{K,e})\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|_{K_{e}^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{n},&\text{if }e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{I},\\[5.69054pt]
\bar{g}_{{}_{N}},&\text{if }e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cdot\bm{n},&\text{if }e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{D},\end{cases}

(14)

where
K_{e}^{\prime}
is the element sharing
e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
and the average weight is given by

\lambda_{K,e}=\frac{\alpha_{\!{}_{K}}^{-1}h_{K}}{\alpha_{\!{}_{K}}^{-1}h_{K}+\alpha_{\!{}_{K_{e}^{\prime}}}^{-1}h_{K_{e}^{\prime}}}.

For the diffusion-dominated case,
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}
is defined in (
15
).
For convection/reaction-dominated case, the form of
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}
in an element
K
depends on the size of the element
K
.
Its construction remains the same as in (
15
) if
R_{K}
is relatively small.
Otherwise, it is essentially the numerical flux with transitional regions to be in
H(\text{div};K)
.

4.1
Diffusion-dominated regime

In the diffusion-dominated regime, the flux recovery is a natural extension of the one in
[
10
]
for pure diffusion problems.
We define
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|_{K}\in\mathrm{RT}_{k-1}(K)
by assigning the degrees of freedom:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cdot\bm{n}&=\hat{g}_{{}_{K}}\quad\text{on }\partial K,\\[5.69054pt]
\text{div}\,\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}&=\Pi_{K}^{k-1}(\bar{f}-\bm{a}\cdot\nabla u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}-bu_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}})+J_{K}\quad\text{in }K,\\[5.69054pt]
\int_{K}\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cdot\bm{q}dx&=\int_{K}\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cdot\bm{q}dx\quad\forall\,\bm{q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{k-2}(K),\quad k\geq 2,\end{aligned}\right.

(15)

where

J_{K}:=|K|^{-1}\left(\int_{\partial K}\hat{g}_{{}_{K}}ds+\int_{K}\bm{a}\cdot\nabla u_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}+bu_{{}_{{\mathcal{T}}}}-fdx\right)

(16)

and

\mathcal{Q}_{k-2}(K):=\{\bm{q}\in P_{k-2}(K)^{d}:(\bm{q},\nabla p)_{K}=0,\,\forall\,p\in P_{k-1}(K)\}.

(17)

It is easily seen that
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\in H(\text{div};\Omega)

and
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}
coincides with the one in
[
10
, Eq.(3.14)]
when
\bm{a}
and
b
vanish.

4.2
Flux recovery in convection/reaction-dominated regime

In the case that
\alpha=\epsilon
is a constant,
the weight is
\lambda_{K,e}=\frac{h_{K}}{h_{K}+h_{K_{e}^{\prime}}}
.
This may be simplified as

for all

if the size of each element is close to sizes of its neighboring elements.
With given normal components of the recovered flux on each face,

4.2.1

On element

),
by (
29
) we have

\hat{r}_{K}=r_{K}-\text{div}\,(\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}})=\left(I-\Pi_{K}^{k-1}\right)r_{K}-J_{K},

which yields

\lVert\hat{r}_{K}\rVert_{K}\leq\lVert r_{K}\rVert_{K}+\lVert J_{K}\rVert_{K}.

(31)

Now, (
28
) is a direct consequence of (
30
), (
31
), and the following bound

\lVert J_{K}\rVert_{K}\leq C\left(\lVert r_{K}\rVert_{K}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}h_{e}^{-1/2}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}\right),

which follows from the divergence theorem and the triangle and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities that

\displaystyle|J_{K}|

\displaystyle=|K|^{-1}\left\lvert\int_{\partial K}(\hat{g}_{{}_{K}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|_{K}\cdot\bm{n})ds-\int_{K}r_{K}dx\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq|K|^{-1}\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}h_{e}^{1/2}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}+|K|^{-1/2}\lVert r_{K}\rVert_{K}\right).

This completes the proof of (
28
) and, hence, (
27
).
∎

Proof in
Case (ii)
.

When
R_{K}>\epsilon^{1/2}\beta^{-1/2}
,
the fact that
h_{S}>R_{S}
implies

\gamma_{{}_{S}}=\epsilon^{1/2}h_{S}^{-1}\beta^{-1/2},\quad S=K\text{ or }S\in\mathcal{E}_{K}.

(32)

To prove (
27
), it suffices to show that

\epsilon^{-1}\lVert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\rVert_{K}^{2}+\beta^{-1}\lVert\hat{r}_{K}\rVert_{K}^{2}\leq C\left(\beta^{-1}\lVert r_{K}\rVert_{K}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\epsilon^{-1/2}\beta^{-1/2}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}^{2}\right).

(33)

To this end, we first estimate
\lVert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\rVert_{K}
.
Note that

|\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}|=\begin{cases}0,&\text{in }K_{\Delta},\\
(1-\lambda_{K,e})(1-\epsilon^{-1/2}\beta^{1/2}y)|j_{e}(\bm{x}_{Q_{e}}+x\bm{t})|,&\text{in }Q_{e}\\
\end{cases}

for all
e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
, where
x,y
are local coordinates in
Q_{e}
,
and

\text{div}\,(\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}})=J_{\omega}\quad\text{in}\;\omega=T_{e},S_{e}.

(34)

Define at this moment
Q_{K}=\cup_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}Q_{e}
. A straightforward calculation gives

\epsilon^{-1}\lVert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\rVert_{K_{\Delta}\cup Q_{K}}^{2}=\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\rVert_{Q_{e}}^{2}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{-1/2}\beta^{-1/2}}{3}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}^{2}.

(35)

The estimate of
\lVert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\rVert_{\omega}
for
\omega=T_{e}
and
S_{e}
is analogous to Case (i).
It follows from (
34
), the fact that
\text{diam}(\omega)=O(\epsilon^{1/2}\beta^{-1/2})
, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\lVert\text{div}\,(\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}})\rVert_{\omega}=\lVert J_{\omega}\rVert_{\omega}\leq C\epsilon^{-1/4}\beta^{1/4}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e},

(36)

which, together with
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
, implies

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-1/2}\lVert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\rVert_{\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\beta^{-1/2}\lVert J_{\omega}\rVert_{\omega}+\epsilon^{-1/4}\beta^{-1/4}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}\right)\leq C\epsilon^{-1/4}\beta^{-1/4}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}

for all
e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
.
Combining with (
35
) gives

\epsilon^{-1}\lVert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}\rVert_{K}^{2}\leq C\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\epsilon^{-1/2}\beta^{-1/2}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}^{2}.

(37)

It remains to estimate
\beta^{-1}\lVert\hat{r}_{K}\rVert_{K}^{2}
. By (
23
) and the triangle inequality, we have

\lVert\hat{r}_{K}\rVert_{K}\leq\lVert r_{K}\rVert_{K}+\lVert\text{div}\,(\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}})\rVert_{K}.

(38)

It follows from the definition of
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}
that

\left\lvert\text{div}\,(\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}})\right\rvert=\begin{cases}0,&\text{in }K_{\Delta},\\
(1-\lambda_{e})\epsilon^{-1/2}\beta^{1/2}|j_{e}(\bm{x}_{Q_{e}}+x\bm{t})|,&\text{in }Q_{e},\\
|J_{\omega}|,&\text{in }\omega=T_{e},S_{e}\end{cases}

for all
e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
.
A straightforward calculation yields

\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert\text{div}\,(\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}})\rVert_{Q_{e}}\leq\epsilon^{-1/4}\beta^{1/4}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e},

which, together with (
36
),
implies

\lVert\text{div}\,(\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}-\bm{\sigma}_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}})\rVert_{K}\leq C\epsilon^{-1/4}\beta^{1/4}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert j_{e}\rVert_{e}.

(39)

(
37
) – (
39
)
imply (
33
)
and the proof is complete.

∎

Non-exactness of toric Poisson structures
David Martínez Torres, Marcelo Silva

Abstract
. We prove that a Poisson structure on a projective toric variety which is invariant by the torus action and whose symplectic leaves are the torus orbits is not exact. This is deduced from a geometric criterion for non-exactness of Poisson structures with a finite number of symplectic leaves.



1.
Introduction

The Poisson cohomology of a Poisson manifold generalizes the de Rham cohomology of a symplectic manifold.
In general Poisson cohomology is very difficult to compute and it barely extends well-known features of the de Rham cohomology. For instance, whereas
it is true that the Poisson
tensor
\pi
defines a degree two Poisson cohomology class, this being a generalization of the closedness of a symplectic form,
it is no longer true that a (non-zero) Poisson structure on a compact manifold be non-exact. That is, there may exist
a vector field
X
such that

(1)

\mathcal{L}_{X}\pi=-\pi.

The are some criteria for non-exactness of a Poisson structure:

•

A Poisson manifold whose induced Lie algebroid structure on its cotangent bundle is integrated by a compact source 1-connected
(symplectic) groupoid is non-exact
[
2
]
;

•

A Poisson manifold on a compact even dimensional manifold such that the top exterior power of the Poisson tensor vanishes transversely is non-exact

[
3
,
8
]
.

•

A Poisson manifold with non-trivial modular class and a representative acting on multivector fields in a semisimple fashion is non-exact. This is a criterion
of Lu cited in
[
10
]
and applied to show non-exactness of the Lie-Poisson structure on a compact connected semisimple Lie group introduced in
[
7
]

and of the induced Bruhat Poisson structure on its manifold of full flags (and of a family of Poisson homogeneous structures
in the manifold of full flags introduced in
[
5
]
).

Bruhat Poisson structures belong to the class of Poisson structures with a finite number of symplectic leaves. Members of this latter class of Poisson
structures on compact manifolds may be exact.
In this note we introduce the following elementary geometric criterion for non-exactness of Poisson structures with a finite
number of symplectic leaves:

If there exists a Poisson submanifold whose induced Poisson structure is non-exact then the ambient Poisson
structure is non-exact.

The criterion is an immediate consequence of the following:

Proposition 1
.

If a Poisson manifold with a finite number of leaves is exact, then any of its Poisson submanifolds
must be exact.

Our application is to
toric Poisson structures
on (smooth projective) toric varieties: Poisson structures invariant by the torus action whose symplectic leaves equal the finitely many torus orbits (see for instance
[
1
]
).

Theorem 1
.

Toric Poisson structures are non-exact.

In fact our criterion also allows to recover Lu’s result on the non-exactness of Bruhat Poisson structures on manifolds of full flags,
and it also applies to Bruhat Poisson structures on arbitrary flag manifolds.

Proofs of the results

On a Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)

\mathcal{R}_{\pi}

of

Lemma 1
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold and let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that

\mathcal{L}_{X}\pi=-\pi
. Then the subsets of
\mathcal{R}_{\pi}
are saturated by integral curves of
X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in M
. On a small neighborhood
U
of
x
we have a flow map
\Psi:U\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to M
which integrates
X
.
The integration of (
1
) is

(2)

\Psi_{t*}\pi=\mathrm{e}^{-t}\pi.

Because
\Psi_{t}
is a diffeomorphism by (
2
) over the integral curve of
X
through
x
in
U
the
rank of
\pi
must be constant. Hence we deduce
that the rank of
\pi
is constant on integral curves. Thus integral curves of
X
are contained in
the subsets of
\mathcal{R}_{\pi}
.
∎

A subset
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi}
is saturated
by equidimensional symplectic leaves, but, in general, it may be far from being a submanifold of
M
. Union of subsets of
\mathcal{R}_{\pi}

do not fit in general into submanifolds of
M
, either. For instance, in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with coordinates

\pi=f\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}
is Poisson for any

The subsets of
\mathcal{R}_{\pi}
are the connected components of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash f^{-1}(0)

and the smooth path connected components of
f^{-1}(0)
. Since any closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the zero set of some smooth function,
the subsets in
\mathcal{R}_{\pi}
can be very complicated.

Remark 2
.

Proposition
1
is generalizes the fact that any symplectic submanifold of an exact
symplectic manifold is exact. The partition
\mathcal{R}
must appear because vector fields (more generally, multivector fields) may not
be tangent to some Poisson submanifolds. For instance, (
1

\mathbb{R}^{3}

\pi=x\partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{z}+y\partial_{z}\wedge\partial_{x}+z\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y},

and the opposite of the Euler vector field.
However, this vector field it is not tangent to the individual symplectic leaves —the spheres— whose induced Poisson structure is non-exact
for strictly positive radius.

Toric Poisson structures on projective toric varieties have a finite number of symplectic leaves and — as we will recall — they posses
Poisson submanifolds of dimension two. A Poisson surface with a
finite number of leaves can be exact. For instance, the Poisson structure
\pi=(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is exact; equation
(
1

Moreover, it extends to an exact Poisson structure on the sphere
with two symplectic leaves (one uses as change of coordinates the radial inversion).

The next result — which we include for the sake of completeness — is well-known to experts.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\pi=f\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}
be a rotationally invariant Poisson structure in a disk
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

of some radius
r_{0}\in(0,\infty]
,
where
f\in C^{\infty}(D)
vanishes at the origin and with order 2.
Then
\pi
is non-exact.

Proof.

Let
r\partial_{r},\partial_{\theta}
denote the Euler and rotational vector fields.
The invariance of
\pi
implies that
f(x,y)=g(r^{2})
,
x^{2}+y^{2}=r^{2}
,

g\in C^{\infty}([0,r_{0}))
.
The vanishing at the origin at order 2 implies that we can factor

g(r^{2})=r^{2}h(r^{2}),\quad h\in C^{\infty}([0,r_{0})),\quad h(0)\neq 0,

and, therefore,
\pi=hr\partial_{r}\wedge\partial_{\theta}
.
Let us assume that there exists
X\in\mathfrak{X}(D)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}\pi=-\pi
. Because
\pi
is rotationally invariant we may assume without loss
of generality that
X
is rotationally invariant. Equivalently,

X=a(r^{2})r\partial_{r}+b(r^{2})\partial_{\theta},\quad a,b\in C^{\infty}([0,r_{0})).

From the Leibniz rule for the Lie derivative and the invariance of
X
we deduce

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{X}(hr\partial_{r}\wedge\partial_{\theta})&=(\mathcal{L}_{X}hr\partial_{r})\wedge\partial_{\theta}+hr\partial_{r}\wedge\mathcal{L}_{X}\partial_{\theta}=\mathcal{L}_{ar\partial_{r}}(hr\partial_{r})\wedge\partial_{\theta}=\\
&=(a\mathcal{L}_{r\partial_{r}}h-h\mathcal{L}_{r\partial_{r}}a)r\partial_{r}\wedge\partial_{\theta}=2r^{2}(h^{\prime}a-ha^{\prime})r\partial_{r}\wedge\partial_{\theta}.\end{split}

Hence exactness of
\pi
forces
h(0)=0
, which contradicts the assumption on
f
.
∎

One can show that each individual symplectic leaf of a toric Poisson structure is exact.

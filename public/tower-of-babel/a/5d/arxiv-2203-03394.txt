Semidefinite programming lower bounds on the squashed entanglement
Hamza Fawzi, Omar Fawzi

Abstract
The squashed entanglement is a widely used entanglement measure that has many desirable properties. However, as it is based on an optimization over extensions of arbitrary dimension, one drawback of this measure is the lack of good algorithms to compute it. Here, we introduce a hierarchy of semidefinite programming lower bounds on the squashed entanglement.



1
Introduction

The squashed entanglement is an entanglement measure introduced by Christandl and Winter
[
CW04
]
. It satisfies many desirable properties of an entanglement measure such as monotonicity under local operations and classical communication (LOCC), additivity under tensor products, a monogamy relation
[
KW04
]
and faithfulness
[
BCY11
]
. In fact, it is the only known entanglement measure satisfying these properties. These mathematical properties also have operational applications: the squashed entanglement provides an upper bound on the distallable entanglement and the distillable key
[
Chr06
,
CEH+07
,
CSW12
,
Wil16
]
. For a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
on
A\otimes B
, the squashed entanglement is defined as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}(A:B)_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\rho_{ABE}}I(A:B|E)_{\rho}\ ,

(1)

where the infimum is taken over all finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
E
and all extensions
\rho_{ABE}
of the state
\rho_{AB}
, i.e.,
\mathrm{tr}_{E}\rho_{ABE}=\rho_{AB}
. The conditional mutual information is defined in equation (
4
) below.
Note that as shown in
[
Shi16
]
, considering infinite dimensional separable Hilbert spaces
E
leads to the same quantity. We also note that a similar quantity was defined in
[
Tuc99
,
Tuc02
]
.

To define the conditional mutual information it is useful to start with the quantum relative entropy. For density operators
\rho,\sigma
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the quantum relative entropy is defined by

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{tr}(\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma))&\text{if }\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

For a density operator
\rho_{AE}
on
A\otimes E
, we define the conditional von Neumann entropy
H(A|E)_{\rho}=-D(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})
where
\rho_{E}=\mathrm{tr}_{A}\rho_{AE}
and
I_{A}
denotes the identity operator on
A
. The conditional mutual information is then defined by

I(A:B|E)_{\rho}=H(A|E)_{\rho}-H(A|BE)_{\rho}\ .

(4)

Note that by considering a purification
\rho_{ABDE}
of the state
\rho_{ABE}
and using the fact that
H(A|BE)_{\rho}=-H(A|D)_{\rho}
, we can write the squashed entanglement as follows:

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}(A:B)_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\rho_{ABDE}}H(A|D)_{\rho}+H(A|E)_{\rho}\ ,

(5)

where the infimum is over arbitrary finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
D
and
E
and extensions
\rho_{ABDE}
of the state
\rho_{AB}
.

2
Squashed entanglement and
f
-divergences

To obtain our hierarchy of semidefinite programs, it is useful to generalize the squashed entanglement (
1
) to standard
f
-divergences (also known as quasi-relative entropies). Let
f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function
such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x}=0
and let
f(0^{+}):=\lim_{x\to 0}f(x)
(which could be infinite).

Definition 2.1

(Standard
f
-divergences
[
Pet86
,
HM17
]
)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma
be positive semidefinite operators on
\mathcal{H}
with spectral decompositions
\rho=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}P_{j}
and
\sigma=\sum_{k}\mu_{k}Q_{k}
. We define

\displaystyle D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma):=\sum_{j:\lambda_{j}>0}\sum_{k:\mu_{k}>0}\lambda_{j}f(\mu_{k}\lambda_{j}^{-1})\mathrm{tr}(P_{j}Q_{k})+f(0^{+})\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho(I-\sigma^{0})\right)\ ,

(6)

where
\sigma^{0}
denotes the projector onto the support of
\sigma
.
1
1

1

Note that in this definition, the roles of
\rho
and
\sigma
are sometimes inverted.

If
f
is operator convex, then
D_{f}
is jointly convex and satisfies the data processing inequality, i.e., for any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
, we have
D_{f}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)
.

Note that
D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)
is linear and monotone in
f
for fixed positive semidefinite operators
\rho,\sigma
, i.e.,
D_{f}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{g}(\rho\|\sigma)
if
f(x)\leq g(x)
for all
x\in(0,\infty)
.
When
f(x)=-\log x
, we obtain the quantum relative entropy. As done in
[
FSP18
,
BFF21
]
, we use an approximation of the function
-\log
using rational functions based on a Gauss-Radau quadrature (see e.g.,
[
DR84
, page 103]
). In order to do that, we introduce the functions
f_{t}:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
for
t\in[0,1]
as

\displaystyle f_{t}(x):=\frac{x-1}{t(x-1)+1}\ .

(7)

It is simple to see that
-f_{t}
is operator convex for all
t\in[0,1]
. In addition, the condition
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{-f_{t}(x)}{x}=0
is satisfied for all
t\in(0,1]
, but not when
t=0
. For
t=0
, we take the convention that
D_{-f_{0}}
is still defined via (
6
), i.e.,
D_{-f_{0}}(\rho\|\sigma)=1-\mathrm{tr}(\rho^{0}\sigma)
(which is closely related to the so-called min-relative entropy
D_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)=-\log(\rho^{0}\sigma)
), and not as in
[
HM17
]
which would simply be
1-\mathrm{tr}(\sigma)
.
With our convention
D_{-f_{0}}(\rho\|\sigma)=\lim_{t\to 0}D_{-f_{t}}(\rho\|\sigma)
. In summary, for all
t\in[0,1]
,
D_{-f_{t}}
is jointly convex and is thus a valid divergence satisfying the data-processing inequality.

The following proposition shows that one can choose
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
and positive weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}
such that taking the linear combination of these divergences gives a good approximation of the quantum relative entropy.

Proposition 2.2

(Gauss-Radau quadrature)
.

For any positive integer
m\geq 1
, there exists a choice of nodes
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}\in(0,1]
and weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}>0
such that for all
x\in(0,+\infty)

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(-\frac{1}{\ln 2}r_{m}(x)\right)-\left(-\log x\right)\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}\ln 2}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}-2\right)\quad\text{ where }\quad r_{m}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}f_{t_{i}}(x)\ .

(8)

Proof.

As in
[
BFF21
]
, we fix the node
t_{m}=1
and the Gauss-Radau quadrature gives the existence of nodes
t_{1},\dots,t_{m-1}\in(0,1)
and weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}>0
such that
\ln x-r_{m}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in(0,+\infty)
.
This establishes the first inequality
0\leq\left(-\frac{1}{\ln 2}r_{m}(x)\right)-\left(-\log x\right)
.

To prove the second inequality, we will establish the statement

\displaystyle r_{m}(x)+r_{m}(1/x)\geq\frac{-1}{m^{2}}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}-2\right)\ .

(9)

Note using the fact that
\ln x-r_{m}(x)\geq 0
, the inequality (
9
) implies that

\displaystyle r_{m}(x)-\ln(x)\geq r_{m}(x)-\ln x-(\ln(1/x)-r_{m}(1/x))\geq\frac{-1}{m^{2}}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}-2\right)\ ,

which establishes the desired upper bound in (
8
).

We now focus on (
9
). Using the Gauss-Radau quadrature property, one can show that we have
\ln(x)-r_{m}(x)=O((x-1)^{2m})
in the neighborhood of
x=1
and
w_{m}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}
(see
[
BFF21
]
for more details). Recalling that

\displaystyle r_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}w_{i}\frac{x-1}{t_{i}(x-1)+1}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{x-1}{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{w_{i}}{1-t_{i}}\frac{x-1}{\frac{t_{i}}{1-t_{i}}x+1}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{x-1}{x}

and putting all the fractions to the same denominator, we can write
r_{m}(x)=\frac{-1}{m^{2}}\frac{F_{m}(x)}{xG_{m-1}(x)}
where

\displaystyle G_{m-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(\frac{t_{i}}{1-t_{i}}x+1\right)

\displaystyle-F_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{w_{i}}{1-t_{i}}(x-1)\cdot x\cdot\prod_{j\in\{1,\dots,m-1\},j\neq i}\left(\frac{t_{j}}{1-t_{j}}x+1\right)+(x-1)\cdot\prod_{j\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}}\left(\frac{t_{j}}{1-t_{j}}x+1\right)\ .

Note that
\deg F_{m}=m
,
\deg G_{m-1}=m-1
and they are normalized so that
F_{m}(0)=G_{m-1}(0)=1
.
Then we have:

r_{m}(1/x)=-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{xF_{m}(1/x)}{G_{m-1}(1/x)}=-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{\hat{F}_{m}(x)}{\hat{G}_{m-1}(x)}

where we let
\hat{F}_{m}(x)=x^{m}F_{m}(1/x)
and
\hat{G}_{m-1}=x^{m-1}G_{m-1}(1/x)
. Note that
\hat{F}_{m}
and
\hat{G}_{m-1}
are both monic polynomials (i.e., the leading coefficient is
1
) since
F_{m}(0)=G_{m-1}(0)=1
.

Summing we get

r_{m}(x)+r_{m}(1/x)=-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{F_{m}(x)\hat{G}_{m-1}(x)+x\hat{F}_{m}(x)G_{m-1}(x)}{xG_{m-1}(x)\hat{G}_{m-1}(x)}.

(10)

We know that around
x=1
,
r_{m}(x)-\ln(x)=O((x-1)^{2m})
, which implies
r_{m}(1/x)+\ln(x)=O((x-1)^{2m})
and so by summing we get
r_{m}(x)+r_{m}(1/x)=O((x-1)^{2m})
. Since the numerator of (
10
) has degree
2m
this means that it has to be a multiple of
(x-1)^{2m}
, i.e., we must have

r_{m}(x)+r_{m}(1/x)=-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{c_{0}(x-1)^{2m}}{xG_{m-1}(x)\hat{G}_{m-1}(x)}

where
c_{0}
is the leading term of the numerator, i.e., it is the leading term of
G_{m-1}
so
c_{0}=\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{t_{i}}{1-t_{i}}
.
The main observation now is to note that
G_{m-1}(x)\hat{G}_{m-1}(x)\geq(x+1)^{2m-2}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle G_{m-1}(x)\hat{G}_{m-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(\frac{t_{i}}{1-t_{i}}x+1\right)\left(\frac{t_{i}}{1-t_{i}}+x\right)

\displaystyle=c_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(x+\frac{1-t_{i}}{t_{i}}\right)\left(\frac{t_{i}}{1-t_{i}}+x\right)

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(x+1)^{2}=c_{0}(x+1)^{2(m-1)}

where we used the fact that for
x,a>0
,
(x+a)(x+1/a)\geq(x+1)^{2}
.
This implies that

\displaystyle r_{m}(x)+r_{m}(1/x)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{(x-1)^{2}}{x}\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)^{2m-2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{(x-1)^{2}}{x}

as desired.
∎

In analogy with the expression (
5
), we can define a variant of the squashed entanglement for
f
-divergences.

Definition 2.3

(
f
-squashed entanglement)
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state acting on
A\otimes B
. We define

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(f)}(A:B)_{\rho}:=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\rho_{ABDE}}-D_{f}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})-D_{f}(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})\ ,

where the infimum is over all extensions
\rho_{ABDE}
of
\rho_{AB}
. In particular, we define

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}:=E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2})}(A:B)_{\rho}\quad\text{ where $r_{m}$ is defined in\penalty\ \eqref{eq:log_rational}}.

Note that we have

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}(A:B)_{\rho}=E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(-\log)}(A:B)_{\rho}\geq E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}\qquad\text{ for any }m\geq 1\ .

We emphasize that in general the
f
-squashed entanglement could be negative and is not an entanglement measure. It will however be a useful tool in the rest of the paper. The main property of the
f
-divergences for
f=-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}
that we will use to construct semidefinite programs is a variational expression of
D_{f}
that we introduce in the next section. Before that we establish basic properties of
E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any state
\rho_{AB}
and positive integer
m
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}\leq E_{\mathrm{sq}}(A:B)_{\rho}\leq E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}+\frac{2d_{A}-2}{m^{2}\ln 2}\ .

In addition, if
\rho_{AB}
is pure, we have

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln 2}\mathrm{tr}\big(\rho_{A}r_{m}(\rho_{A}^{-1})\big)\ ,

where
\rho_{A}^{-1}
denotes the generalized inverse of
\rho_{A}=\mathrm{tr}_{B}\rho_{AB}
.

Proof.

We simply use Definition
2.1
together with inequality (
8
). This gives

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}(A:B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\rho_{ABDE}}-D(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})-D(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\rho_{ABDE}}-D_{g}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})-D_{g}(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})\ ,

where
g(x)=-\frac{1}{\ln 2}r_{m}(x)-\frac{1}{m^{2}\ln 2}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}-2\right)
. Note that for the function
f_{0}(x)=x-1
and
f_{1}(x)=(x-1)/x
, we have

\displaystyle D_{f_{0}}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})=\mathrm{tr}(\rho_{AD}^{0}I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})-1\leq d_{A}-1

\displaystyle D_{f_{1}}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})=1-\mathrm{tr}(\rho_{AD}^{2}I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D}^{-1})\geq 1-d_{A}\ .

As a result,

\displaystyle-D_{g}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})\leq-D_{-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})+\frac{1}{m^{2}\ln 2}2(d_{A}-1)\ ,

which proves the desired bound.

If
\rho_{AB}
is pure, then any purification has the form
\rho_{ABDE}=\rho_{AB}\otimes\rho_{DE}
and as a result,

\displaystyle D_{f}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})

\displaystyle=D_{f}(\rho_{A}\|I_{A})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho f(\rho^{-1})\right)\ ,

using the defining formula (
6
) and using the notation
\rho^{-1}
for the generalized inverse of
\rho
.
∎

3
Approximating the squashed entanglement with noncommutative polynomial optimization

We now use the variational expression for
D_{-f_{t}}
that was established in
[
BFF21
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
BFF21
]
)
.

Let
t\in[0,1]
and recall that
f_{t}(x)=\frac{x-1}{t(x-1)+1}
. Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be positive semidefinite operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then

D_{-f_{t}}(\rho\|\sigma)=-\inf_{Z}\frac{1}{t}\left\{\mathrm{tr}(\rho)+\mathrm{tr}(\rho(Z+Z^{*}))+(1-t)\mathrm{tr}(\rho Z^{*}Z)+t\mathrm{tr}(\sigma ZZ^{*})\right\}\ ,

(11)

where the infimum is over bounded operators
Z
on
\mathcal{H}
.

We fix an orthonormal basis for
A
that we denote
\{|a\rangle\}_{a\in[d_{A}]}
and one for
B
that we denote
\{|b\rangle\}_{b\in[d_{B}]}
.
For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we say that the density operator
\rho_{AB\mathcal{H}}
acting on
A\otimes B\otimes\mathcal{H}
is an extension of
\rho_{AB}
if
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\rho_{AB\mathcal{H}}=\rho_{AB}
. We write
\rho_{AB\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{a_{1}a_{2}b_{1}b_{2}}|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}|\otimes|b_{1}\rangle\!\langle b_{2}|\otimes\rho[a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2}]
where
\rho[a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2}]
is a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
. With this notation, an extension of
\rho_{AB}
is defined by a family of operator
\rho[a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2}]
satisfying
\mathrm{tr}\rho[a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2}]=\mathrm{tr}(|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}|\otimes|b_{1}\rangle\!\langle b_{2}|\rho_{AB})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
m\geq 1
and let
t_{1},\dots,t_{m},w_{1},\dots,w_{m}
be the nodes and weights from Proposition
8
. We define the matrix-valued polynomial
{\bf P}^{(m)}
in noncommuting variables
{\bf Z}=\{Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}],Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}]^{*}\}_{i,a_{1},a_{2}}
by

\displaystyle{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Z}):=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}P^{i}_{a_{1},a_{2}}({\bf Z})\right)_{a_{1},a_{2}\in[d_{A}]}

(12)

where

\displaystyle P^{i}_{a_{1},a_{2}}({\bf Z})

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{i}}{t_{i}\ln 2}\Bigg(Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}]+Z_{i}[a_{2},a_{1}]^{*}+(1-t_{i})\sum_{a_{3}}Z_{i}[a_{3},a_{1}]^{*}Z_{i}[a_{3},a_{2}]

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta_{a_{1}=a_{2}}\left(1+t_{i}\sum_{a_{3},a_{4}}Z_{i}[a_{3},a_{4}]Z_{i}[a_{3},a_{4}]^{*}\right)\Bigg)\ ,

(13)

where
\delta_{a_{1}=a_{2}}=0
when
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
and
\delta_{a_{1}=a_{2}}=1
otherwise. Note that when the variables
Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}]
are replaced with operators on
\mathcal{H}
, then
{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Z})
is a Hermitian operator of
A\otimes\mathcal{H}
.
Then for any density operator
\rho_{AB}
on
A\otimes B
, we have

E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}=\inf\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{A\mathcal{H}}\frac{{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Z})+{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Y})}{2}\right)

(14)

where the infimum is over finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
, extensions
\rho_{AB\mathcal{H}}
of the state
\rho_{AB}
and two mutually commuting families of operators
{\bf Z}
and
{\bf Y}
on
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

We start from the expression in Definition (
2.3
) with
f=-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}
and consider a fixed extension
\rho_{ABDE}
.

We have

\displaystyle D_{-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln 2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}D_{-f_{t_{i}}}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{w_{i}}{t_{i}\ln 2}\inf_{Z_{i}}\mathrm{tr}(\rho_{AD}(I+Z_{i}+Z_{i}^{*}))+\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{AD}\left((1-t_{i})Z_{i}^{*}Z_{i}+t_{i}I_{A}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{A}Z_{i}Z_{i}^{*}\right)\right)\ ,

where
Z_{i}
is a bounded operator on
A\otimes D
and we used Theorem
3.1
. We can write such operators in the form
Z_{i}=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}|\otimes Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}]
. With this notation, we have

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}|\otimes Z_{i}[a_{2},a_{1}]^{*}

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{*}Z_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}|\otimes Z_{i}[a_{2},a_{1}]^{*}\right)\left(\sum_{a_{3},a_{4}}|a_{3}\rangle\!\langle a_{4}|\otimes Z_{i}[a_{3},a_{4}]\right)=\sum_{a_{1},a_{4}}|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{4}|\otimes\sum_{a_{2}}Z_{i}[a_{2},a_{1}]^{*}Z_{i}[a_{2},a_{4}]

\displaystyle Z_{i}Z_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1},a_{4}}|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{4}|\otimes\sum_{a_{2}}Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}]Z_{i}[a_{4},a_{2}]^{*}\ .

Writing
\rho_{AD}=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}|a_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}|\otimes\rho[a_{1},a_{2}]
we get

\displaystyle-D_{-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\bf Z}\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho[a_{1},a_{2}]\sum_{i=1}^{m}P^{i}_{a_{1},a_{2}}({\bf Z})\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\bf Z}\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{AD}{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Z})\right)\ ,

where the infimum is over all operators
{\bf Z}=\{Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}],Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}]^{*}\}_{i,a_{1},a_{2}}
acting on
D
and the noncommutative polynomials
P^{i}_{a_{1},a_{2}}({\bf Z})
are defined by (
13
).

Proceeding in the same way for the term
-D_{-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}}(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})
, we can write for a given extension
\rho_{ABDE}
:

\displaystyle-D_{-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}}(\rho_{AD}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{D})-D_{-\frac{r_{m}}{\ln 2}}(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bf Z},{\bf Y}}\mathrm{tr}(\rho_{ADE}({\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Z})\otimes I_{E}+{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Y})\otimes I_{D}))\ ,

where
{\bf Z}
and
{\bf Y}
are families of operators acting on
D
and
E
respectively. Taking the infimum over all extensions
\rho_{ABDE}
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}=\inf\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{ADE}\frac{{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Z})\otimes I_{E}+{\bf P}^{(m)}({\bf Y})\otimes I_{D}}{2}\right)\ ,

where the infimum is over all choices of finite-dimensional spaces
D
and
E
and operators
{\bf Z}
and
{\bf Y}
on
D
and
E
respectively. Note that the operators
Z_{i}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes I_{E}
and
I_{D}\otimes Y_{j}[a_{3},a_{4}]
act on the tensor product space
\mathcal{H}=D\otimes E
and commute. Thus, we clearly obtain a lower bound by taking the infimum over finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and now both collections
{\bf Z}
and
{\bf Y}
act on
\mathcal{H}
and they mutually commute. This shows the inequality
\geq
of equation (
14
). Equality then follows from the now well-known fact that two commuting families of operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space can be mapped isomorphically to operators acting on different tensor factors of a tensor product of two finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, see
[
SW08
]
for a proof.
∎

3.1
Semidefinite relaxation

The expression in (
14
) is a noncommutative polynomial optimization problem and thus one can use semidefinite programming hierarchies for it
[
PNA10
]
. We now explain how this can be done. Let
\mathcal{R}
be the ring of noncommutative polynomials in the
4md_{A}^{2}
variables

For any integer

be the ring of

\mathcal{R}

\mathcal{R}^{\nu\times\nu}

\mathbf{P}^{(m)}({\bf Z})

We can express the optimization problem (
14
) in the following form:

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\inf_{L:\mathcal{R}^{d_{A}d_{B}\times d_{A}d_{B}}\to\mathbb{C}}&\frac{1}{2}L\left(\mathbf{P}^{(m)}({\bf Z})\otimes I_{B}+\mathbf{P}^{(m)}({\bf Y})\otimes I_{B}\right)\\
\text{s.t.}&L(|a_{1}b_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}b_{2}|\otimes 1)=\langle a_{1}b_{1}|\rho_{AB}|a_{2}b_{2}\rangle\;\;\forall a_{1},a_{2}\in[d_{A}],b_{1},b_{2}\in[d_{B}]\end{array}

(16)

where the optimization is over all linear forms
L:\mathcal{R}^{d_{A}d_{B}\times d_{A}d_{B}}\to\mathbb{C}
that can be written as

\begin{aligned} L(\mathbf{P})&=\mathrm{tr}{\;\sigma_{AB\mathcal{H}}\;\mathbf{P}(\underline{{\bf Z}},\underline{{\bf Y}})}\end{aligned}\qquad\forall\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{R}^{d_{A}d_{B}\times d_{A}d_{B}}

(17)

where
\mathcal{H}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space,
\sigma_{AB\mathcal{H}}
is any state on
AB\mathcal{H}
, and
\underline{{\bf Z}},\underline{{\bf Y}}
are operators on
\mathcal{H}
.
The linear constraints on
L
in (
16
) ensure that the state
\sigma_{AB\mathcal{H}}
is an extension of
\rho_{AB}
(indeed, the notation
|a_{1}b_{1}\rangle\!\langle a_{2}b_{2}|\otimes 1

\mathcal{R}^{d_{A}d_{B}\times d_{A}d_{B}}
that has an identity in position

It is hard in general to require that
L

17
). The
k

16

L(\boldsymbol{p}\boldsymbol{p}^{*})\geq 0\;\;\forall\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{R}_{k}^{d_{A}d_{B}\times 1}

(18)

where
\mathcal{R}_{k}
is the space of degree
k
polynomials in
\mathcal{R}
. The constraint (
18
) can be encoded as a positive semidefinite constraint on a matrix of size
\dim\mathcal{R}_{k}^{d_{A}d_{B}}
.

4
Discussion

The semidefinite programs (SDPs) that we constructed for the squashed entanglement raise a number of questions. First, does this family of SDPs converge to the squashed entanglement? We know from Proposition
2.4
that as
m\to\infty
, we have
E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}
approaches
E_{\mathrm{sq}}(A:B)_{\rho}
, but what remains unclear is whether the SDP relaxations for
E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}
converge for a fixed
m
. The difficulty comes from the fact that the general convergence results in
[
PNA10
]
give an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space but we could only prove (
14
) for finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. In other words, similar to Tsirelson’s problem, the challenge here comes from comparing the commuting model to the tensor product model for the specific problem defined in (
14
). Another question is to use the specific structure of the polynomials in Theorem
3.2
in order to obtain finite convergence bounds for this family of SDPs, where one can hope to use techniques that were used to establish noncommutative Positivestellensatz
[
Hel02
,
HKM12
]
. Such a convergence could also lead to bounds on the dimension of the extension system sufficient to approximate the squashed entanglement. Note that the NP-hardness of the squashed entanglement
[
Hua14
]
rules out a convergence that is too fast. For example, if
E_{\mathrm{sq}}^{(m)}(A:B)_{\rho}
was always equal to the SDP relaxation at level
k=1
, we could take
m
polynomial in the dimension and get a polyonmial-time algorithm to approximate the squashed entanglement with additive error that is inverse polynomial in the dimension, which is ruled out by
[
Hua14
]
. Finally, a limitation of the method we propose is that the size of the SDPs grow quickly in practice and this makes it difficult to run except in very small dimension. It would be interesting to find more efficient methods.

The variational approach to s s -fractional heat flows and the limit cases s → 0 + s\to 0^{+} and s → 1 − s\to 1^{-}
V. Crismale, L. De Luca, A. Kubin, A. Ninno, M. Ponsiglione

Abstract
. This paper deals with the limit cases for s s -fractional heat flows in a cylindrical domain, with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, as s → 0 + s\to 0^{+} and s → 1 − s\to 1^{-} . To this purpose, we describe the fractional heat flows as minimizing movements of the corresponding Gagliardo seminorms, with respect to the L 2 L^{2} metric. First, we provide an abstract stability result for minimizing movements in Hilbert spaces, with respect to a sequence of Γ \Gamma -converging uniformly λ \lambda -convex energy functionals. Then, we provide the Γ \Gamma -convergence analysis of the s s -Gagliardo seminorms as s → 0 + s\to 0^{+} and s → 1 − s\to 1^{-} , and apply the general stability result to such specific cases. As a consequence, we prove that s s -fractional heat flows (suitably scaled in time) converge to the standard heat flow as s → 1 − s\to 1^{-} , and to a degenerate ODE type flow as s → 0 + s\to 0^{+} . Moreover, looking at the next order term in the asymptotic expansion of the s s -fractional Gagliardo seminorm, we show that suitably forced s s -fractional heat flows converge, as s → 0 + s\to 0^{+} , to the parabolic flow of an energy functional that can be seen as a sort of renormalized 0 0 -Gagliardo seminorm: the resulting parabolic equation involves the first variation of such an energy, that can be understood as a zero (or logarithmic) Laplacian. Keywords: Gagliardo seminorms; Γ \Gamma -convergence; Fractional heat flow AMS subject classifications: 49J45 35R11 35K20



Introduction

In this paper we consider the
s
-fractional heat equation (
s\in(0,1)
)

(0.1)

u_{t}(t)+C(s)(-\Delta)^{s}u(t)=0\,,\qquad t\geq 0

posed in a bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions, and we study its asymptotic behavior as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
.
We adopt a purely variational approach, combining the theory of
minimizing movements
for
gradient flows
and
\Gamma
-convergence properties for the underlying energies.

The fractional heat equation may be seen as the
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the squared
s
-Gagliardo seminorm

[u]^{2}_{s}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

with the support of
u
contained in
\Omega
when the equation is posed in a bounded domain with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The asymptotic behavior of
s
-Gagliardo seminorms has been studied by several authors.
The case
s\to 1^{-}
has been first considered in
[
6
]
, where it is proven that the pointwise limit of the squared
s
-Gagliardo seminorms multiplied by
(1-s)
is given by (a multiple of) the Dirichlet integral. Such a result is indeed proven for every exponent
1<p<+\infty
(
[\cdot]_{s}
corresponds to
p=2
). For
p=1
only a control of the limit in terms of the total variation is provided, allowing to characterize the BV space; this has been extended in several directions: first, in
[
13
]
, showing that the pointwise limit is exactly (a multiple of) the total variation, and then in
[
26
,
21
]
for more general kernels. The asymptotic behavior of (relative)
fractional perimeters (roughly said, considering characteristic functions in the framework of
[
13
]
) is provided in
[
3
]
in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence.

For what concerns the limit as
s\to 0^{+}
, the literature is much poorer. In
[
23
]
the authors show that, as
s\to 0^{+}
, the squared
s
-fractional Gagliardo seminorms multiplied by
s
pointwise converge to (a multiple of) the squared
L^{2}
-norm
(see also
[
16
]
for a similar result in the context of
s
-fractional perimeters).
The corresponding asymptotic analysis in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence has been developed in
[
15
]
in the context of fractional perimeters (that is, restricting to characteristic functions). A functional with more interesting properties is obtained in the limit as
s\to 0^{+}
by studying the next order term in the asymptotic expansion of the squared
s
-fractional Gagliardo seminorms: in
[
15
]
it is shown, still restricting to fractional perimeters, that the corresponding
\Gamma
-limit provides a new nonlocal energy, referred to as
0
-fractional perimeter
.

The first step in our analysis is to extend the results in
[
15
]
to the seminorms. In fact, we remove the constraint on the admissible functions to be characteristic functions
and we obtain a
\Gamma
-convergence expansion (as
s\to 0^{+}
) with respect to the
L^{2}
topology for functions whose support is in a bounded set
\Omega
.
The zero-order
\Gamma
-convergence result is Theorem
1.2
, while
the next order analysis is done in Theorem
1.4
.
The
\Gamma
-convergence analysis
of the (scaled)
s
-Gagliardo seminorms as
s\to 1^{-}
is done in Theorem
2.1
, giving back the
\Gamma
-convergence version of the result in
[
6
]
.

The above asymptotic results, which are of independent interest,
serve as a tool for the stability of the corresponding parabolic flows.
Stability of gradient flows with respect to the
\Gamma
-convergence of the corresponding energies is nowadays a classical problem, which has been widely investigated in recent years in increasing generality (we refer, for instance, to
[
28
,
27
,
4
]
).
In the present paper we exploit the fact that the underlying energies are uniformly (with respect to
s
)
\lambda
-convex.

The gradient flows of
\lambda
-convex energies, namely energies which are convex up to a quadratic perturbation multiplied by
\lambda
, admits a unique solution.
Moreover, such a solution can be approximated by the discrete-in-time implicit Euler scheme; namely, the solution coincides with the so called minimizing movements solution.
This well-known fact is the content of Theorem
3.6
, which provides also quantitative convergence estimates for the discrete-in-time solutions to the unique solution of the parabolic flow, the convergence rates depending essentially on
\lambda
and on the initial data (see also
[
2
,
12
,
25
]
).
The uniformity of the convergence rates is the key tool for proving Theorem
3.8
, which guarantees stability for gradient flows in Hilbert spaces with respect to sequences of
\Gamma
-converging uniformly
\lambda
-convex functionals.

With Theorems
3.6
and
3.8
on hand, we are in a position to provide our stability results for the
s
-fractional heat flows as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
.

First, we apply Theorem
3.6
in order to show that for every
s\in(0,1)
there exists a unique solution to the Cauchy problem governed by the
s
-fractional parabolic flow (
0.1
) and that such a solution is the limit, as the time step vanishes, of the discrete-in-time evolution.
Actually, the parabolic flow in the abstract result Theorem
3.6
is written - as usual - in terms of a differential inclusion of
-u_{t}
in the subdifferential of the underlying energy evaluated in
u
, which in general could be multivalued. This is not our case, since the
s
-Gagliardo seminorms are differentiable in the fractional Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
, which are dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
; this implies (see Proposition
3.7
) that along the parabolic flow, the subdifferential of the nonlocal energy reduces to a point for a.e.
t>0
. Actually existence, uniqueness and regularity for the fractional heat flows have been extensively studied; we refer the interested reader to
[
9
,
18
]
and the references therein.
Moreover, in the framework of nonnegative solutions for the fractional heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the problem has been studied in the context of a general Widder theory
[
30
]
, in e.g.
[
5
,
8
,
29
]
, with even not regular (but nonnegative) initial datum.

Finally, since the energies we consider are uniformly
\lambda
-convex, we can apply the abstract stability result Theorem
3.8
to obtain the asymptotic behavior of the
s
-fractional heat flows as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
.
This is contained in our main results, Theorems
4.4
,
4.5
,
4.6
: the limit evolutions are an ODE corresponding to an exponential growth for the
0
-th order as
s\to 0^{+}
, a
0
-fractional heat equation for the first order as
s\to 0^{+}
, and the classical local heat equation as
s\to 1^{-}
. The stability consists in a weak
H^{1}
-in-time-convergence, which is proven to be strong if the initial data are well prepared, namely if the approximating initial data are a recovery sequence for the limit datum with respect to the
\Gamma
-converging energies. Furthermore, in this case for every time
t
the approximating evolutions are recovery sequences (with respect to the
\Gamma
-converging energies) for the limit evolution,
namely there is convergence of the corresponding energies for every
t
.

A result similar in spirit to our asymptotic analysis for
s\to 1^{-}
is provided in
[
1
]
, where the authors consider limit of convolution type energies and of the corresponding parabolic flows as the support of the convolution kernel concentrates to a point. In that paper the authors focus on homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, and are able to deal also with anisotropic kernels; we believe that, up to minor differences in the setting, their approach and the one developed in this paper are consistent and that our analysis as
s\to 1^{-}
could be derived by their analysis with minor modifications. As well, we believe that our results (also for
s\to 0^{+}
) could be generalized to natural variants of the fractional heat flows considered here, dealing for instance with different boundary conditions as well as anisotropic variants of the underlying Gagliardo seminorms.

The paper is structured as follows: in Sections
1
and
2
we study the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
, respectively. Section
3
collects the abstract stability result for gradient flows of uniformly
\lambda
-convex
\Gamma
-converging energies. In Section
4
we prove the main results of convergence for
s
-fractional parabolic flows as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
.

u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
we have that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\hat{F}^{s}(u)=\hat{F}^{0}(u)\,.

Proof.

In view of the definition of
\hat{F}^{s}
in (
1.8
) it is enough to show

(1.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}G^{s}_{1}(u)=G^{0}_{1}(u)\,,

(1.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}J^{s}_{1}(u)=J^{0}_{1}(u)\,.

We start by proving (
1.13
).
To this end, we note that, since
\tilde{u}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

|\tilde{u}(y)-\tilde{u}(x)|^{2}\leq\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|x-y|^{2}\,.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set such that
\mathrm{dist}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U)>1
and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
; we have

\displaystyle|G^{s}_{1}(u)-G^{0}_{1}(u)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{U}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|^{2}\bigg|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{B}_{1}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|^{2}\bigg|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

By Dominated Convergence Theorem the second addend in the righthand side tends to zero (for fixed
\varepsilon
) as
s\to 0^{+}
, while the first addend is bounded from above by

|U|\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|z|^{d+2s-2}}\,\mathrm{d}z
, which tends to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. This clearly yields (
1.13
).

Finally, (
1.14
) is a trivial consequence of the Dominated Convergence Theorem, once noticed that

J^{s}_{1}(u)=-\int_{\Omega}u(x)\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

∎

Lemma 1.8

(Density of smooth functions)
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{0}(\Omega)
there exists
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
such that
u_{k}\to u
(strongly) in
L^{2}

and

\lim_{k\to+\infty}J^{0}_{1}(u_{k})=J^{0}_{1}(u)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to+\infty}G^{0}_{1}(u_{k})=G_{1}^{0}(u).

Proof.

This result is proven in
[
24
, Theorem 3.29]
, for domains with a continuous boundary. For the reader’s convenience we recall the sketch of the proof: Up to a partition of the unity argument, one may assume
\Omega
to be the subgraph of a continuous function:
thus it is enough to approximate first with
u_{\delta}(x):=u(x^{\prime},x_{n}+\delta)
, for small
\delta
, whose support is well contained in
\Omega
, and then to take
u_{\delta}\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}
, for a family of mollifiers
\{\phi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
and small
\varepsilon
.
∎

The limsup inequality in Theorem
1.4
follows directly from the density proved above.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
(iii).

Let
u\in\mathcal{H}^{0}(\Omega)
. By Lemma
1.8
there exists a sequence of functions
\{u^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
u^{k}\to u
in
L^{2}
and

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\hat{F}^{0}(u^{k})=\hat{F}^{0}(u)\,.

In view of Lemma
1.7
we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\hat{F}^{s_{n}}(u^{k})=\hat{F}^{0}(u^{k})\qquad\textrm{for every }k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Therefore, by a standard diagonal argument, there exists a sequence
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
u^{n}=u^{k(n)}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying the desired properties.
∎

2.
\Gamma
-convergence of the squared
s
-Gagliardo seminorms as
s\rightarrow 1^{-}

Here we study the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals
(1-s)F^{s}
as
s\rightarrow 1^{-}
, where
F^{s}
is defined in (
1.1
).
The candidate
\Gamma
-limit is the functional
F^{1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

F^{1}(u):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x&\text{ if }u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,,\\
&+\infty&\textrm{elsewhere in }L^{2}(\Omega)\,.\end{aligned}\right.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be such that
s_{n}\rightarrow 1^{-}
as
n\to+\infty
. The following
\Gamma
-convergence result holds true.

(i)

(Compactness) Let
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that

(2.1)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{n})+\|u^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq M,

for some constant
M
independent of
n
. Then, up to a subsequence,
u^{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for some
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

(
\Gamma
-liminf inequality) For every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for every
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, it holds

F^{1}(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{n})\,.

(iii)

(
\Gamma
-limsup inequality) For every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
there exists
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

(2.2)

F^{1}(u)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{n})\,.

2.1.
Proof of Compactness

This subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.1
(i).
To accomplish this task, we adopt the strategy in
[
3
]
adapting it to our case.
To this purpose, for every function
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
\tau_{h}v
the shift of
v
by
h
, defined by
\tau_{h}v(\cdot):=v(\cdot+h)
.
We recall the following two classical results.

Theorem 2.2

(Fréchet-Kolmogorov)
.

Let
\{v^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such tha
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|v^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq M
, for some constant
M
independent of
n
.
If

\lim_{|h|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\tau_{h}v^{n}-v^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0\,,

then
\{v^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is pre-compact in
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if there exists
C>0
such that

\|\tau_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C|h|\quad\textrm{ for every }h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

For every
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every
t>0
we define the set

(2.3)

A_{t}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\mathrm{dist}(x,A)<t\}.

The following result which allows to estimate the
L^{2}
distance of a function from its shift has been proven in
[
3
, Proposition 5]
in
L^{1}
; for the sake of completeness, we state
and prove it also in our case.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a constant
C(d)>0
such that the following holds true:
for every
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every open bounded set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

(2.4)

\|\tau_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\leq C(d)\frac{|h|^{2}}{\rho^{d+2}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\|\tau_{y}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}_{|h|})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\quad\textrm{for every }\rho\in(0,|h|],

with
\Omega^{\prime}_{|h|}
defined as in (
2.3
).

Proof.

The proof closely resembles the one of
[
3
, Proposition 5]
.
Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(B_{1})
be a fixed function with
\varphi\geq 0
and
\int_{B_{1}}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. For every
\rho>0
we define the functions
U_{\rho},V_{\rho}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
as

U_{\rho}(x):=\frac{1}{\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}}v(x+y)\varphi\bigg(\frac{y}{\rho}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}y\,,\qquad V_{\rho}(x):=\frac{1}{\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}}(v(x)-v(x+y))\varphi\bigg(\frac{y}{\rho}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}y\,;

clearly, for every
\rho>0
and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

v(x)=U_{\rho}(x)+V_{\rho}(x)\,,

and hence

(2.5)

|\tau_{h}v(x)-v(x)|^{2}\leq 3|U_{\rho}(x+h)-U_{\rho}(x)|^{2}+3|V_{\rho}(x)|^{2}+3|V_{\rho}(x+h)|^{2}\,.

By Jensen inequality, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

(2.6)

|V_{\rho}(\xi)|^{2}\leq\frac{\omega_{d}}{\rho^{d}}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\int_{B_{\rho}}|v(\xi)-\tau_{y}v(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Moreover, by the change of variable
z=x+y
, we have that

U_{\rho}(x)=\frac{1}{\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}v(z)\varphi\bigg(\frac{z-x}{\rho}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}z\,,

whence we deduce that

\begin{split}\mathrm{D}\,U_{\rho}(x)=&-\frac{1}{\rho^{d+1}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}v(z)\mathrm{D}\varphi\bigg(\frac{z-x}{\rho}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}z\\
=&-\frac{1}{\rho^{d+1}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}(v(z)-v(x))\mathrm{D}\varphi\bigg(\frac{z-x}{\rho}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}z\\
=&-\frac{1}{\rho^{d+1}}\int_{B_{\rho}}(v(x+y)-v(x))\mathrm{D}\varphi\bigg(\frac{y}{\rho}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}y\,;\end{split}

therefore, by the fundamental Theorem of Calculus and by Jensen inequality, we obtain

(2.7)

\begin{split}&|U_{\rho}(x+h)-U_{\rho}(x)|^{2}\leq|h|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{D}\,U_{\rho}(x+th)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\omega_{d}\frac{|h|^{2}}{\rho^{d+2}}\|\mathrm{D}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{y}v(x+th)-v(x+th)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Now, by (
2.5
), (
2.6
), and (
2.7
), taking
\rho<|h|
, we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}|\tau_{h}v(x)-v(x)|^{2}\leq&3\omega_{d}\frac{|h|^{2}}{\rho^{d+2}}\|\mathrm{D}\varphi\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{y}v(x+th)-v(x+th)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&+3\omega_{d}\frac{|h|^{2}}{\rho^{d+2}}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{y}v(x)-v(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&+3\omega_{d}\frac{|h|^{2}}{\rho^{d+2}}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{y}v(x+h)-v(x+h)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,.\end{split}

Finally, by integrating (
2.8
) on
\Omega^{\prime}
, by Fubini theorem, we obtain (
2.4
) with
C(d):=3\omega_{d}(2\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{2})
.
∎

We recall the following version of Hardy’s inequality, that is proven in
[
3
, Proposition 6]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a Borel measurable function. Then for all
l\geq 0
we have

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{\rho^{d+l+1}}\int_{0}^{\rho}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\rho\leq\frac{1}{d+l}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{g(t)}{t^{d+l}}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\textrm{for every }r\geq 0.

The following result will be used in the proof of Theorem
2.1
(i). It is the
L^{2}
analog of
[
3
, Proposition 4]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

There exists a constant
\bar{C}(d)>0
such that for every
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every bounded open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for every
s\in(0,1)
, and for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

\|\tau_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\leq|h|^{2s}\bar{C}(d)(1-s)\int_{B_{|h|}}\frac{\|\tau_{y}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}_{|h|})}^{2}}{|y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

Proof.

For every fixed
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we define the function
g_{v}:[0,|h|]\to\mathbb{R}
as

g_{v}(t):=\int_{\partial B_{t}}\|\tau_{y}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}_{|h|})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\,.

By integrating in polar coordinates formula (
2.4
) we have

(2.9)

\|\tau_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\leq C(d)\frac{|h|^{2}}{\rho^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{\rho}g_{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

By multiplying both sides of (
2.9
) by
\rho^{1-2s}
and
integrating in the interval
[0,|h|]
, using Lemma
2.5
and the very definition of
g_{v}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\tau_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 2C(d)(1-s)|h|^{2s}\int_{0}^{|h|}\frac{1}{\rho^{d+2s+1}}\int_{0}^{\rho}g_{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(d)(1-s)|h|^{2s}\int_{0}^{|h|}\frac{g_{v}(t)}{t^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(d)(1-s)|h|^{2s}\int_{B_{|h|}}\frac{\|\tau_{y}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}_{|h|})}^{2}}{|y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
2.1
(i).

Proof of Theorem
2.1
(i).

By Proposition
2.6
and by the upper bound (
2.1
) we obtain that for every open bounded set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(2.10)

\|\tau_{h}\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(d,M)|h|^{s_{n}}\,,

where we recall that
\tilde{u}^{n}
is the extension of
u^{n}
to
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
. Therefore, the sequence
\{\tilde{u}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
2.2
, and hence there exists a function
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
v=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
, such that, up to a subsequence,
\tilde{u}^{n}\rightarrow v
in
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Now, sending
n\to+\infty
in (
2.10
), we obtain that for every open bounded set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

\|\tau_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(d,M)|h|\quad\textrm{for every }h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

and hence by Theorem
2.3
(choosing
\Omega^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the above inequality) we obtain that
\mathrm{D}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
v=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
, by the regularity of
\partial\Omega
, we have that
v
is the extension to
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
of a function
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, thus concluding the proof.
∎

2.2.
Proof of the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality

Here we prove the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality in Theorem
2.1
. The proof of this result closely resembles the one of
[
6
, Theorem 2]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
(ii).

We can assume without loss of generality that (
2.1
) holds true so that
the function
u
is actually in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Claim 1.

Let
\eta\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(B_{1})
be a standard mollifier, i.e.,
\eta\geq 0
and
\int_{B_{1}}\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, we set
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\eta(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})
.
Then, for every
s\in(0,1)

\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}]^{2}_{s}\leq F^{s}(v)\qquad\textrm{for every }v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\textrm{ and for every }\varepsilon>0\,,

where
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}:=\tilde{v}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}
.

Indeed, setting
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ :\ \text{dist}(x,\Omega)\leq\varepsilon\}
, we have that
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
;
therefore, by applying Jensen inequality to the probability measure
\eta_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}z
, we get

\displaystyle{}^{2}_{s}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\tilde{v}(x-z)-\tilde{v}(y-z)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\tilde{v}(x-z)-\tilde{v}(y-z)|^{2}}{|x-z-(y-z)|^{d+2s}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2F^{s}(v)\,.

Claim 2.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and for every
R>0
, it holds

(2.11)

\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\,\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla\tilde{u}^{n}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}(\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial B_{R}))^{2(1-s_{n})}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}(1-s_{n})\frac{[\tilde{u}^{n}_{\varepsilon}]^{2}_{s_{n}}}{2}\,,

where
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}:=\tilde{u}^{n}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}
, with
\eta_{\varepsilon}
as in Claim 1.

Indeed, by Taylor expansion, using that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq M
we have that

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(y)|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big|^{2}|x-y|^{2}

\displaystyle-\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|x-y|^{3}-\|u^{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|x-y|^{4}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big|^{2}|x-y|^{2}-C(\varepsilon,M)(|x-y|^{3}+|x-y|^{4})\,,

Therefore, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, setting
\delta:=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial B_{R})
, we get

(2.12)

\displaystyle(1-s_{n})\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{n}}}\,\mathrm{d}y\geq(1-s_{n})\int_{B_{\delta}(x)}\frac{|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{n}}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-s_{n})\int_{B_{\delta}(x)}\Big|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big|^{2}|x-y|^{2(1-s_{n})-d}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-(1-s_{n})C(\varepsilon,M)\int_{B_{\delta}(x)}\frac{|x-y|^{3}+|x-y|^{4}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{n}}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{d}}{2}\delta^{2(1-s_{n})}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)|^{2}-(1-s_{n})C(\varepsilon,M,d)\,,

where in the last equality we integrated over spherical boundaries from 0 to
\delta
, using that
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)\cdot\theta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\omega_{d}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)|^{2}
.
By integrating (
2.12
) over
B_{R}
, we get (
2.11
).

By Claim 1 and Claim 2, for every
\varepsilon>0
and for every
R>0
we have that

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{n})\geq\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)|^{2}(\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial B_{R}))^{2(1-s_{n})}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

whence, using that for every
\varepsilon>0
the sequence
\{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equi-Lipschitz,
we get that, up to a (not relabeled) subsequence,

(2.13)

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{n})\geq\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{n}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Notice that
\tilde{u}^{n}_{\varepsilon}\to\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}:=\tilde{u}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\to+\infty
.
By (
2.13
) and by (
2.1
), we have that in fact
\tilde{u}^{n}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
in
H^{1}(B_{R})
.

In conclusion, by (
2.13
), we deduce that for every
\varepsilon>0

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{n})\geq\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

whence the claim follows sending first
\varepsilon\to 0
(using that
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\to\tilde{u}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
)
and then
R\to+\infty
.
∎

2.3.
Proof of the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality

The proof of the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality relies on the pointwise convergence of
(1-s)F^{s}
to
F^{1}
(as
s\to 1
) for smooth functions with compact support and on the density of smooth functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
As for the pointwise convergence we recall the following result, proved in
[
22
]
in a more general setting.

Theorem 2.7
.

For every
v\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it holds

\lim_{s\rightarrow 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{\omega_{d}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla v(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

With Theorem
2.7
on hand we can prove Theorem
2.1
(iii) using standard density arguments in
\Gamma
-convergence.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
(iii).

It is enough to prove the claim only for
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
For every
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
there exists
\{u^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
u^{k}\rightarrow u
(as
k\rightarrow+\infty
) in
H^{1}(\Omega)
. In view of Theorem
2.7
we have that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{k})=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1-s_{n}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\tilde{u}^{k}(x)-\tilde{u}^{k}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{n}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\tilde{u}^{k}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{k}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Therefore by a standard diagonal argument there exists
\{k_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}u^{k_{n}}=u,\quad\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}(1-s_{n})F^{s_{n}}(u^{k_{n}})\leq\frac{\omega_{d}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=F^{1}(u)\,,

i.e., (
2.2
).
∎

3.
Minimizing movements for
\lambda
-convex functionals defined on a Hilbert space

In this section we develop the general theory that will allow us to study the stability of the
s
-fractional heat flow as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
.
Throughout this section
\mathscr{H}
is a generic Hilbert space,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}
is the inner product of
\mathscr{H}
and
|\cdot|_{\mathscr{H}}
is the norm induced by such a scalar product. In the abstract setting of this section, we denote by
\dot{v}
the time derivative of any function
v
from a time interval with values in
\mathscr{H}
.

Definition 3.1

(
\lambda
-convexity,
\lambda
-positivity,
\lambda
-coercivity)
.

Let
\lambda>0
. We say that a function
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is
\lambda
-convex if the function
f(\cdot)+\frac{\lambda}{2}|\cdot|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}
is convex.
Moreover, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-positive if

\mathcal{F}(x)+\frac{\lambda}{2}|x|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\geq 0
for every
x\in\mathscr{H}
,
and we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-coercive if the sublevels of the function
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)+\frac{\lambda}{2}|\cdot|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}
are bounded.

Remark 3.2
.

We notice that if
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-positive, then
\mathcal{F}
is
\tilde{\lambda}
-coercive for every
\tilde{\lambda}>\lambda
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper, strongly lower semicontinuous function which is
\lambda
-convex and
\lambda
-positive for some
\lambda>0
. Then for every
0<\tau<\frac{1}{2\lambda}
and for every
y\in\mathscr{H}
the problem

(3.1)

\min\biggl\{\mathcal{F}(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}:\;x\in\mathscr{H}\biggr\}

admits a unique solution.

Proof.

We preliminarily notice that, since
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-convex and strongly lower semicontinuous, then the function
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|\cdot|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}
is strictly convex and strongly lower semicontinuous and, in turn, weakly lower semicontinuous. Clearly, this implies that also
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|\cdot-y|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}
is weakly lower semicontinuous.
Moreover, by Remark
3.2
, we have that
\mathcal{F}
is
\frac{1}{2\tau}
-coercive.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is proper,

0\leq\inf\biggl\{\mathcal{F}(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}:\;x\in\mathscr{H}\biggr\}\leq M\,,

for some
M>0
.
Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{H}
be a sequence such that

(3.2)

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{F}(x_{k})+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x_{k}-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}=\inf\biggl\{\mathcal{F}(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}:\;x\in\mathscr{H}\biggr\}\,.

By triangle inequality, for
k
sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle 2M\geq

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x_{k})+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x_{k}-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\geq\mathcal{F}(x_{k})+\frac{1}{4\tau}|x_{k}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\tau}|y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}

whence, in view of the
\frac{1}{2\tau}
-coercivity of the function
\mathcal{F}
, we deduce that, up to a subsequence,
x_{k}\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\rightharpoonup}x_{\infty}
for some
x_{\infty}\in\mathscr{H}
.
Therefore, by (
3.2
) and by the weak lower semicontinuity of the function
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|\cdot-y|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}
, we obtain

\inf\biggl\{\mathcal{F}(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}:\;x\in\mathscr{H}\biggr\}=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{F}(x_{k})+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x_{k}-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\\
\geq\mathcal{F}(x_{\infty})+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x_{\infty}-y|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,,

i.e., that
x_{\infty}
is a minimizer of the problem in (
3.1
).

Finally, the uniqueness of the solution is a consequence of the strict convexity of the functional

\mathcal{F}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|\cdot-y|^{2}
.
∎

For every function
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
we denote by
D(\mathcal{F})
the set of all
x\in\mathscr{H}
such that
\mathcal{F}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.4

(Fréchet subdifferential)
.

For
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
and
x\in D(\mathcal{F})
, the Fréchet subdifferential of
\mathcal{F}
at
x
is defined as

\partial\mathcal{F}(x):=\biggl\{v\in\mathscr{H}:\liminf_{y\to x}\frac{\mathcal{F}(y)-\mathcal{F}(x)-\langle v,y-x\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}}{|y-x|_{\mathscr{H}}}\geq 0\biggl\}\,.

Remark 3.5
.

Whenever
\mathcal{F}
is a
\lambda
-convex function it holds that

(3.3)

\partial\mathcal{F}(x)=\biggl\{v\in\mathscr{H}:\,\mathcal{F}(y)-\mathcal{F}(x)-\langle v,y-x\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\geq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\,|y-x|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\quad\text{for every }y\in\mathscr{H}\biggr\}\,.

Indeed, for a convex function
\phi
,
v\in\partial\phi(x)
if and only if
\phi(y)-\phi(x)-\langle v,y-x\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\geq 0
for every
y\in\mathscr{H}
, namely the Fréchet subdifferential coincides with the usual subdifferential of convex analysis.
Then, being
\mathcal{F}

\lambda
-convex and since
\partial\Big(\phi+\frac{\lambda}{2}|\cdot|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\Big)=\partial\phi+\lambda\,\cdot
, it holds that
v\in\partial\mathcal{F}(x)
if and only if

\mathcal{F}(y)+\frac{\lambda}{2}|y|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}-\mathcal{F}(x)-\frac{\lambda}{2}|x|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}-\langle v+\lambda x,y-x\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\geq 0\quad\text{for every }y\in\mathscr{H}\,,

which coincides with the condition in (
3.3
) since
|y-x|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}=|y|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}-|x|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}-2\langle x,y-x\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper, strongly lower semicontinuous function which is
\lambda
-positive and
\lambda
-convex, for some
\lambda>0
, and let
x_{0}\in D(\mathcal{F})
. For every
0<\tau<\frac{1}{2\lambda}
, we denote by
\{x_{k}^{\tau}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
the discrete-in-time evolution for
\mathcal{F}
with initial datum
x_{0}
, defined by

(3.4)

x_{0}^{\tau}:=x_{0}\,,\qquad x_{k+1}^{\tau}\in\arg\!\min\biggl\{\mathcal{F}(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x-x_{k}^{\tau}|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\biggr\}\quad\textrm{for every }k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\,.

Since
x_{0}\in D(\mathcal{F})
, then
x_{k}^{\tau}\in D(\mathcal{F})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Furthermore, we define the piecewise-affine interpolation
x^{\tau}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
of
\{x^{\tau}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
as

(3.5)

x^{\tau}(t):=x_{k}^{\tau}+\frac{x_{k+1}^{\tau}-x_{k}^{\tau}}{\tau}(t-k\tau),\quad t\in[k\tau,(k+1)\tau).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper, strongly lower semicontinuous function which is
\lambda
-convex and
\lambda
-positive, for some
\lambda>0
. Let moreover
x_{0}\in D(\mathcal{F})
. Then, there exists a unique solution
x\in H^{1}([0,+\infty);\mathscr{H})
to the following Cauchy problem

(3.6)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}(x(t))\quad\text{for a.e.\ $t\in[0,+\infty)$},\\
x(0)=x_{0}\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, for every
T>0
,
x^{\tau}\rightharpoonup x
in
H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
,
where
x^{\tau}
is defined in (
3.5
) for
0<\tau<\frac{1}{2\lambda}
.
Furthermore,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\dot{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}\leq 4^{8\lambda T+4}(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\textrm{ for every }T>0\,,

(3.8)

\displaystyle|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\leq 8\tau\,12^{8\lambda t+4}\big(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\big)\textrm{ for every }t\geq 0\,,\,\tau<\frac{1}{16\lambda}\,.

Proof.

Uniqueness.

Let
T>0
and let
x_{1},x_{2}\in H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
satisfy the Cauchy problem (
3.6
) up to time
T
.
We first observe that

(3.9)

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},v_{1}-v_{2}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\leq\lambda\,|y_{1}-y_{2}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\quad\textrm{for every }y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathscr{H}\,,\,-v_{i}\in\partial\mathcal{F}(y_{i})\textrm{ for }i=1,2\,.

Indeed, by (
3.3
), we have

\mathcal{F}(y)-\mathcal{F}(y_{1})+\langle v_{1},y-y_{1}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\geq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\,|y-y_{1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,,\quad y\in\mathscr{H}\,,

which, for
y=y_{2}
implies

(3.10)

\mathcal{F}(y_{2})-\mathcal{F}(y_{1})+\langle v_{1},y_{2}-y_{1}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\geq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\,|y_{2}-y_{1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2};

analogously

(3.11)

\mathcal{F}(y_{1})-\mathcal{F}(y_{2})+\langle v_{2},y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\geq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\,|y_{2}-y_{1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,.

Therefore, (
3.9
) follows by summing (
3.10
) and (
3.11
).

Finally, by formula (
3.9
) we have

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}|x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}=2\langle\dot{x}_{1}(t)-\dot{x}_{2}(t),x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\leq 2\lambda\,|x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\quad\text{ for a.e. $t\in[0,T]$,}\,

which, by Gronwall’s Lemma, implies

|x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\leq|x_{0}-x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}e^{2\lambda t}=0\quad\text{ for a.e. $t\in[0,T]$\,,}

i.e.,
x_{1}(t)=x_{2}(t)
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
. We notice that the solution is in
C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
by the Sobolev embedding of
H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
into
C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
, so that
x_{1}(t)=x_{2}(t)
when passing to the continuous representatives.

Existence.

We first prove that for every
T>0
the functions
x^{\tau}
defined in (
3.5
) converge (as
\tau\to 0
) weakly in
H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
to some function
x\in H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})

and then we show that the limit
x
satisfies (
3.6
) up to time
T
.

By (
3.4
) we have that

(3.12)

\mathcal{F}(x_{k+1}^{\tau})+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x_{k+1}^{\tau}-x_{k}^{\tau}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\leq\mathcal{F}(x_{k}^{\tau}),\quad\text{for every $k\in\mathbb{N}$\,,}

which together with the
\lambda
-positivity of
\mathcal{F}
implies that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{1}{\tau}\left|x^{\tau}_{k+1}-x^{\tau}_{k}\right|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left(\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}_{k})-\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}_{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}_{0})-\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}_{K+1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}|x^{\tau}_{K+1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}|x^{\tau}_{K+1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}-\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}_{K+1})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\mathcal{F}(x_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}|x^{\tau}_{K+1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right)\qquad\textrm{for every }K\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Set
\hat{T}=\frac{1}{8\lambda}
and let
0<\tau\leq\frac{1}{16\lambda}
.
We set
\hat{K}:=\left\lceil\frac{\hat{T}}{\tau}\right\rceil
; by (
3.13
),
we have

(3.14)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\hat{T}}|\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq&\sum_{k=0}^{\hat{K}}\frac{1}{\tau}\left|x^{\tau}_{k+1}-x^{\tau}_{k}\right|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\leq 2\left(\mathcal{F}(x_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}|x^{\tau}_{\hat{K}+1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right)\,.\end{split}

Moreover, by triangle and Jensen inequalities and using again (
3.13
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|x_{\hat{K}+1}^{\tau}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}-|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\leq|x_{\hat{K}+1}^{\tau}-x_{0}|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau(\hat{K}+1)\sum_{k=1}^{\hat{K}+1}\frac{1}{\tau}\left|x^{\tau}_{k}-x^{\tau}_{k-1}\right|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}=\tau(\hat{K}+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\hat{K}}\frac{1}{\tau}\left|x^{\tau}_{k+1}-x^{\tau}_{k}\right|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\hat{T}+2\tau)\Big(\mathcal{F}(x_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}|x_{\hat{K}+1}^{\tau}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\Big)\,,

which, recalling that
0<2\tau\leq\frac{1}{8\lambda}=\hat{T}
, implies that

(3.15)

|x^{\tau}_{\hat{K}+1}|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}\mathcal{F}(x_{0})+4|x_{0}|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\,.

By (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), we have that, for every
\tau
small enough,

(3.16)

\|\dot{x}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,\hat{T});\mathscr{H})}\leq 4(\mathcal{F}(x_{0})+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,.

Iterating the estimates in (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), using also that
\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}_{k})
is not increasing with respect to
k
, we deduce that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}

\begin{split}|x^{\tau}(j\hat{T})|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}&\leq 4^{j}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\Big),\\
\|\dot{x}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,j\hat{T});\mathscr{H})}&\leq 4^{j+3}(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,.\end{split}

In particular, for every
T>0
, we have that

(3.17)

\|\dot{x}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}\leq 4^{8\lambda T+4}(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,.

Therefore, for every
T>0
,
\|x^{\tau}\|_{H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})}
is uniformly bounded and hence, up to a subsequence,
x^{\tau}\rightharpoonup x
in
H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
for some
x\in H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
; this, in particular, implies the convergence in
C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
and hence that
x(0)=x_{0}
.
Passing to the limit in (
3.17
) we readily get (
3.7
).

Now we aim at proving that
x
solves (
3.6
) up to time
T
,
for every
T>0
, that is

(3.18)

\dot{x}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}(x(t))\qquad\textrm{ for almost every }t\in(0,T)\,.

To this end, we define the piecewise-constant interpolation
\tilde{x}^{\tau}:[0,+\infty)\to\mathscr{H}
of
\{x_{k}^{\tau}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
as

\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t):=x_{k+1}^{\tau},\quad t\in[k\tau,(k+1)\tau)\,,

and we notice that, by minimality, for
\tau
small enough,

(3.19)

\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}(\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t))\qquad\textrm{for almost every }t\in[0,+\infty)\,.

We claim that

(3.20)

\tilde{x}^{\tau}\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\rightarrow}x\quad\textrm{ in }L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})\,,\quad\textrm{ for every }T>0\,.

Indeed, by triangle inequality, we have that

(3.21)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{x}^{\tau}-x\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}&\leq 2\|x^{\tau}-x\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}+2\|\tilde{x}^{\tau}-x^{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}\\
&\leq 2\|x^{\tau}-x\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}+2\tau^{2}\|\dot{x}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}\,,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used that

x^{\tau}(t)-\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t)=\frac{x_{k+1}^{\tau}-x_{k}^{\tau}}{\tau}(t-(k+1)\tau)=\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)(t-(k+1)\tau),\quad\textrm{for every }t\in(k\tau,(k+1)\tau)\,.

Therefore, by (
3.21
) and (
3.7
) we get

\|\tilde{x}^{\tau}-x\|_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}^{2}\leq 2\|x^{\tau}-x\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathscr{H})}+2\tau^{2}4^{8\lambda T+4}(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,,

which, sending
\tau\to 0
and recalling that
x^{\tau}\rightharpoonup x
in
H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
, implies (
3.20
).

With (
3.20
) on hand, we are in a position to prove (
3.18
).
Let
t_{0}\in(0,T)
be a Lebesgue point of the function
\dot{x}:[0,T)\to\mathscr{H}
. By
(
3.19
), we have that

(3.22)

\mathcal{F}(y)\geq\mathcal{F}(\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t))-\langle\dot{x}^{\tau}(t),y-\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}|y-\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,\qquad\textrm{for every }y\in\mathscr{H}\,.

Let
y\in\mathscr{H}
and
h>0
; by integrating (
3.22
) in the interval
(t_{0},t_{0}+h)
and dividing by
h
, we obtain

\mathcal{F}(y)\geq\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+h}\mathcal{F}(\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+h}\langle\dot{x}^{\tau}(t),y-\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+h}\frac{\lambda}{2}|y-\tilde{x}^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

which, sending
\tau\to 0
, and using the strong lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{F}
, the weak
L^{2}
-convergence of
\dot{x}^{\tau}
to
\dot{x}
, and (
3.20
), yields

\mathcal{F}(y)\geq\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+h}\mathcal{F}({x}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+h}\langle\dot{x}(t),y-{x}(t)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+h}\frac{\lambda}{2}|y-{x}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Now, since
x\in C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
and since
t_{0}
is a Lebesgue point for
\dot{x}
, sending
h\to 0
in the formula above, and using again that
\mathcal{F}
is strongly lower semicontinuous, by the arbitrariness of
y
, we get (
3.18
).

Finally, we prove that (
3.8
) holds true. Let
\eta^{\tau}:[0,+\infty)\to(0,\tau]
be the function defined by
\eta^{\tau}(t)=(k+1)\tau-t
for every
t\in[k\tau,(k+1)\tau)
.
By (
3.19
),

\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}(x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t)))\qquad\textrm{ for every }t>0\,,

which, using (
3.6
) and (
3.9
), yields

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle x(t)-x^{\tau}(t),\dot{x}(t)-\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle x(t)-x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t)),\dot{x}(t)-\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle+2\langle x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t))-x^{\tau}(t),\dot{x}(t)-\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\lambda|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t))|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}+2|x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t))-x^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}|\dot{x}(t)-\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\lambda|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}+8\lambda|x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t))-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle+2|x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t))-x^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}(|\dot{x}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}+|\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\lambda|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}+\tau(8\lambda\tau+3)(|\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}+|\dot{x}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\lambda|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}+4\tau(|\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}+|\dot{x}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,,

where in the last inequality we have used that
\tau\leq\frac{1}{16\lambda}
(recall also

|x^{\tau}(t+\eta^{\tau}(t))-x^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}=\eta^{\tau}(t)|\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}\leq\tau|\dot{x}^{\tau}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}
) .
By integrating the equation above and using (
3.7
) and (
3.17
), we get

\displaystyle|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}8\lambda|x(s)-x^{\tau}(s)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\,\mathrm{d}s+4\tau\int_{0}^{t}(|\dot{x}^{\tau}(s)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}+|\dot{x}(s)|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}8\lambda|x(s)-x^{\tau}(s)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\,\mathrm{d}s+8\tau 4^{8\lambda t+4}(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})\,.

Setting
\alpha(s):=8\tau 4^{8\lambda s+4}(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})
and
\beta(s):=8\lambda
for every
s>0
, we have

\displaystyle|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)|x(s)-x^{\tau}(s)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\alpha(t)\,.

Noticing that
\alpha
is non-decreasing, by Gronwall Lemma we get

\displaystyle|x(t)-x^{\tau}(t)|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\leq

\displaystyle\alpha(t)e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}=8\tau 4^{8\lambda t+4}(|\mathcal{F}(x_{0})|+\lambda|x_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})e^{8\lambda t}\,,

thus providing (
3.8
).
∎

For every vectorial space
\mathscr{V}
we denote by
\mathscr{V}^{*}
the algebraic dual space of
\mathscr{V}
and by
\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
the topological dual space of
\mathscr{V}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lower semicontinuous function which is
\lambda
-convex, for some
\lambda>0
and let
x\in D(\mathcal{F})
.
Let
\hat{\mathscr{H}}
be a dense subspace of
\mathscr{H}
.
If there exists
T\in(\hat{\mathscr{H}})^{*}
such that

(3.23)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}(x+t\varphi)-\mathcal{F}(x)}{t}=T(\varphi)\qquad\textrm{for every }\varphi\in\hat{\mathscr{H}}\,,

then, either
\partial\mathcal{F}(x)=\emptyset
or
\partial\mathcal{F}(x)=\{v\}
, where
v
is the (unique) element in
\mathscr{H}
satisfying
T(\varphi)=\langle v,\varphi\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}
for every
\varphi\in\hat{\mathscr{H}}
. In particular,

T\in(\hat{\mathscr{H}})^{\prime}
and
v
is its unique continuous extension to
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\hat{\mathscr{H}}
is dense in
\mathscr{H}
, in order to get the claim it is enough to prove that for every
v\in\partial\mathcal{F}(x)

(3.24)

\langle v,\varphi\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=T(\varphi)\qquad\textrm{for every }\varphi\in\hat{\mathscr{H}}\,.

To this purpose, we notice that every
v\in\partial\mathcal{F}(x)
satisfies

\mathcal{F}(x+t\varphi)-\mathcal{F}(x)-t\langle v,\varphi\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\geq-t^{2}\frac{\lambda}{2}|\varphi|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\qquad\textrm{for every }\varphi\in\hat{\mathscr{H}},\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

which, dividing by
t
, yields

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}(x+t\varphi)-\mathcal{F}(x)}{t}=\langle v,\varphi\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\qquad\textrm{for every }\varphi\in\hat{\mathscr{H}}\,;

therefore, in view of (
3.23
), we get (
3.24
).
∎

The following Theorem provides a convergence result for gradient-flows associated to a
\Gamma
-converging sequence of functions satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
3.6
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\{\mathcal{F}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\mathcal{F}^{n}:\mathscr{H}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a sequence of proper, strongly lower semicontinuous functions which are
\lambda
-convex and
\lambda
-positive, for some
\lambda>0
independent of
n
. Let
\{x_{0}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{H}
be such that
x_{0}^{n}\in D(\mathcal{F}^{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
S:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x_{0}^{n})<+\infty
and
x_{0}^{n}\to x^{\infty}_{0}

for some
x^{\infty}_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
. Assume that one of the following statements is satisfied:

(a)

The functions
\mathcal{F}^{n}
are equicoercive and
\Gamma
-converge to some proper function
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
with respect to the weak
\mathscr{H}
-convergence (as
n\to+\infty
) . Moreover, the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality is satisfied with respect to the strong
\mathscr{H}
-convergence, i.e., for every
y\in\mathscr{H}
there exists a sequence
\{y^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
y^{n}\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\to}y
such that
\mathcal{F}^{n}(y^{n})\to\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(y)
as
n\to+\infty
.

(b)

The functions
\mathcal{F}^{n}

\Gamma
-converge to some proper function
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
with respect to the strong
\mathscr{H}
-convergence (as
n\to+\infty
) and every sequence
\{y^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{H}
with
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}^{n}(y^{n})+\frac{\lambda}{2}|y^{n}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}<+\infty
, admits a strongly convergent subsequence.

Then,
x_{0}^{\infty}\in D(\mathcal{F}^{\infty})
and, for every
T>0
, the solutions
x^{n}
to the Cauchy problem

(3.25)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}^{n}(x(t))\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
x(0)=x_{0}^{n}\end{cases}

weakly converge, as
n\to+\infty
, in
H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
to the unique solution
x^{\infty}
to the problem

(3.26)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x(t))\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
x(0)=x^{\infty}_{0}\,.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if

(3.27)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n}_{0})=\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x^{\infty}_{0})\,,

then, we have that

(3.28)

x^{n}\to x^{\infty}\qquad\textrm{(strongly) in }H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})\quad\textrm{ for every }T>0\,,

(3.29)

x^{n}(t)\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\to}x^{\infty}(t)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n}(t))\to\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x^{\infty}(t))\qquad\textrm{for every }t\geq 0\,.

Proof.

We preliminarily notice that, if either (a) or (b) is satisfied, then the function
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
is strongly lower semicontinuous,
\lambda
-convex and
\lambda
-positive and
x^{\infty}_{0}\in D(\mathcal{F}^{\infty})
.
Moreover, by Theorem
3.6
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unique solution
x^{n}
to (
3.25
).

Let
0<\tau<\frac{1}{2\lambda}
and let
\{x^{\infty,\tau}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the discrete-in-time evolution in (
3.4
) for
x_{0}:=x_{0}^{\infty}
and
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
.
Analogously, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{x^{n,\tau}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the discrete-in-time evolution in (
3.4
) for
x_{0}:=x_{0}^{n}
and
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}^{n}
.
By Proposition
3.3
,
\{x^{\infty,\tau}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{x^{n,\tau}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are uniquely determined.
Furthermore, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{k}(\cdot)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{n}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|\cdot-x^{n,\tau}_{k-1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\qquad\textrm{for every }n\in\mathbb{N}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\infty,\tau}_{k}(\cdot)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\tau}|\cdot-x^{\infty,\tau}_{k-1}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,.

We first show that, if either (a) or (b) is satisfied, then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}

(3.30)

\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n,\tau}_{k})\to\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x^{\infty,\tau}_{k})\quad\textrm{and}\quad|x^{n,\tau}_{k}-x^{\infty,\tau}_{k}|_{\mathscr{H}}\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }n\to+\infty\,.

By finite induction, it is enough to show (
3.30
) for
k=1
. We distinguish the two cases in which either (a) or (b) holds true.

Assume first that (a) holds true.
By the assumptions on
x^{n}_{0}
, we have that

\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{1}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})\leq\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n}_{0})\leq S\,,

whence, using that for
\frac{1}{2\tau}>\lambda
the functions
\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{1}(\cdot)
are equicoercive, we deduce that, up to a subsequence,
x^{n,\tau}_{1}\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\rightharpoonup}y_{1}
for some
y_{1}\in\mathscr{H}
.
Moreover, since
|x_{0}^{n}-x_{0}^{\infty}|_{\mathscr{H}}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and since the functions
\mathcal{F}^{n}

\Gamma
-converge to the function
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
with respect to the weak
\mathscr{H}
-convergence, we have that

(3.31)

\mathcal{I}^{\infty,\tau}_{1}(y_{1})\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}|x^{n,\tau}_{1}-x^{n}_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{1}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})\,.

Furthermore, since the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality is satisfied with respect to the strong
\mathscr{H}
-convergence, there exists
\{\bar{x}^{n,\tau}_{1}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{H}
such that

(3.32)

\bar{x}^{n,\tau}_{1}\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\to}x^{\infty,\tau}_{1}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\mathcal{F}^{n}(\bar{x}^{n,\tau}_{1})\to\mathcal{F}(x^{\infty,\tau}_{1})\,,

where
x^{\infty,\tau}_{1}
is the unique solution to the problem (
3.4
) with
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
and
k=1
. Therefore, by (
3.31
) and (
3.32
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\infty,\tau}_{1}(y_{1})\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}|x^{n,\tau}_{1}-x_{0}^{n}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{1}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{1}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{1}(\bar{x}^{n,\tau}_{1})=\mathcal{I}^{\infty,\tau}_{1}(x^{\infty,\tau}_{1})\,,

whence, by the minimality of
x^{\infty,\tau}_{1}
, we deduce that all the inequalities above are in fact equalities and, in particular, that
y_{1}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{I}^{\infty,\tau}_{1}
; in view of the uniqueness of the minimizer of
\mathcal{I}^{\infty,\tau}_{1}
, we deduce that
y_{1}=x^{\infty,\tau}_{1}
.
By Urysohn Lemma, this implies that the whole sequence
\{x^{n,\tau}_{1}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

x^{\infty,\tau}_{1}
.
Moreover, using that

\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x^{\infty,\tau}_{1})+\frac{1}{2\tau}|x^{\infty,\tau}_{1}-x^{\infty}_{0}|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}|x^{n,\tau}_{1}-x_{0}^{n}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\,,

we deduce that

\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x^{\infty,\tau}_{1})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})\quad\textrm{ and }\quad|x^{\infty,\tau}_{1}-x^{\infty}_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}|x^{n,\tau}_{1}-x^{n}_{0}|_{\mathscr{H}}\,,

which

3.30
) (for

and then for all

).

Assume now that (b) holds true.
As above (recall

) we have that

\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})+\lambda|x^{n,\tau}_{1}-x^{n}_{0}|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\leq\mathcal{I}^{n,\tau}_{1}(x^{n,\tau}_{1})\leq S\,,

whence, by the strong compactness property of the functions

for some
y_{1}\in\mathscr{H}
.
Moreover, since

\Gamma

\mathscr{H}

By the fundamental theorem of
\Gamma
-convergence and by the uniqueness of the minimizer of the problem (
3.4
) with

and

3.30

) for both the cases (a) and (b).

Now we show that for every

,

, where

is the unique solution to (

) .

.

\displaystyle|x^{n}(t)-x^{\infty}(t)|_{\mathscr{H}}\leq

) and (

4.
Convergence of the
s
-fractional heat flows

This section is devoted to the proof of the stability of the
s
-fractional heat flows as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
.
In the first part, we define the
s
-fractional laplacian for
s\in(0,1)
and for
s=0
. The second part contains the convergence theorems, which are the main results of the paper.
In this section we denote by
v_{t}
the partial time derivative of a function
v
.

4.1.
The
s
-fractional laplacian for
s\in(0,1)
and for
s=0

For every
s\in(0,1)
and for every
\psi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the
s
-fractional laplacian of
\psi
is defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}\psi(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{2\psi(x)-\psi(x+z)-\psi(x-z)}{|z|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}z,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

In
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
it is proven that the above integral is finite, that
(-\Delta)^{s}\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and that

(4.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}\psi(x)=2\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(0)}\frac{\psi(x)-\psi(x+z)}{|z|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,.

For every
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
we define the
s
-fractional laplacian of
u
by duality as

(4.2)

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,\varphi\rangle:=\langle u,(-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{\varphi}\rangle,\qquad\textrm{for all }\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Here and below
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard scalar product in
L^{2}
.
Clearly, the
s
-fractional laplacian is nothing but the first variation of the squared Gagliardo
s
-norm, as shown below.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. For every
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
and for every
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
we have

(4.3)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{F^{s}(u+t\varphi)-F^{s}(u)}{t}=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,\varphi\rangle.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{F^{s}(u+t\varphi)-F^{s}(u)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y))(\tilde{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{\varphi}(y))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(x)\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(0)}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{\varphi}(x+z)}{|z|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}u(y)\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(0)}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(y)-\tilde{\varphi}(y-z)}{|z|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u,(-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{\varphi}\rangle=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,\varphi\rangle,

where we have used the change of variable
z=y-x
, (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
∎

For every
\psi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define the
0
-fractional laplacian of
\psi
as

(-\Delta)^{0}\psi(x):=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{2\psi(x)-\psi(x+z)-\psi(x-z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}}\frac{\psi(x+z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

We notice that
(-\Delta)^{0}\psi
is well-defined for every
\psi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
since

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|2\psi(x)-\psi(x+z)-\psi(x-z)|}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq 2\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|\psi(x+z)-\psi(x)|}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\\
\leq C[\psi]_{0,1}\,.

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}}\frac{|\psi(x+z)|}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}\,.

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
11
]
the notion of
logarithmic laplacian

L_{\Delta}
has been introduced as follows

\mathrm{L}_{\Delta}\psi(x):=c_{d,1}(-\Delta)^{0}\psi(x)+c_{d,2}\psi(x)\,,

where
c_{d,1}
and
c_{d,2}
are specific constant depending only on the dimension
d
.
Such a logarithmic laplacian would correspond to renormalizing the Gagliardo
s
-seminorm of
\psi
by removing all but a finite amount of the blowing up quantity
\frac{\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{s}
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{H}^{0}_{0}(\Omega)

we define
0
-fractional laplacian of
u
by duality as

(4.4)

\langle(-\Delta)^{0}u,\varphi\rangle:=\langle u,(-\Delta)^{0}\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\,,\qquad\textrm{for all }\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)\,.

Clearly, the
0
-fractional laplacian is the first variation of the functional
\hat{F}^{0}
, as shown in the following result (we recall that the functions
G^{0}_{1}
and
J^{0}_{1}
have been introduced in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), respectively).

Proposition 4.3
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{H}^{0}_{0}(\Omega)
and for every
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{G^{0}_{1}(u+t\varphi)-G^{0}_{1}(u)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle u,\int_{B_{1}}\frac{2\tilde{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{\varphi}(x+z)-\tilde{\varphi}(x-z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\Big\rangle

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{J^{0}_{1}(u+t\varphi)-J^{0}_{1}(u)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle u,-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(x+z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\Big\rangle\,,

so that

(4.7)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\hat{F}^{0}(u+t\varphi)-\hat{F}^{0}(u)}{t}=\langle(-\Delta)^{0}u,\varphi\rangle\,.

Proof.

Fix
u\in\mathcal{H}^{0}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then, using the change of variable
z=y-x
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{G^{0}_{1}(u+t\varphi)-G^{0}_{1}(u)}{t}=\iint_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\frac{(\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y))(\tilde{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{\varphi}(y))}{|x-y|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(x)\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{\varphi}(x+z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}u(y)\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(y)-\tilde{\varphi}(y-z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle u,\int_{B_{1}}\frac{2\tilde{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{\varphi}(x+z)-\tilde{\varphi}(x-z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\Big\rangle\,,

i.e., (
4.5
).
Moreover, using again the change of variable
z=y-x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{J^{0}_{1}(u+t\varphi)-J^{0}_{1}(u)}{t}=-2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}\frac{\tilde{u}(x)\tilde{\varphi}(y)}{|x-y|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(x)\Big(-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(y)}{|x-y|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle u,-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(x+z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\Big\rangle\,,

namely, (
4.6
).
Finally, (
4.7
) follows from (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), using (
4.4
).
∎

4.2.
The main results

Here we first state and then prove the convergence results for the parabolic flows corresponding to the (either scaled or renormalized)
s
-Gagliardo seminorms as
s\to 0
and
s\to 1
. These follow by collecting the preparatory results of the previous sections, and Lemma
4.7
for the first order convergence as
s\to 0^{+}
.

We start with the convergences as
s\to 0^{+}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be such that
s_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
. Let
u^{0}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and let
\{u_{0}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
u^{n}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s_{n}}_{0}(\Omega)
,
S:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}F^{s_{n}}(u^{n}_{0})<+\infty
and

u^{n}_{0}\to u^{0}_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unique solution
u^{n}\in H^{1}([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
to

(4.8)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-s_{n}(-\Delta)^{s_{n}}u(t)\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in[0,+\infty)\\
u(0)=u^{n}_{0}\,,\end{cases}

and such a solution satisfies
(-\Delta)^{s_{n}}u^{n}(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for
a.e.\,t\geq 0
.
Moreover, for every
T>0
,
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u^{0}
in
H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
as
n\to+\infty
, where
u^{0}\in H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
is the unique
solution to

(4.9)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-d\omega_{d}u(t)\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
u(0)=u^{0}_{0}\,.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if

\lim_{n\to+\infty}s_{n}F^{s_{n}}(u^{n}_{0})=F^{0}(u^{0}_{0})\,,

then,
u^{n}\to u^{0}
(strongly) in
H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
for every
T>0
, and

\|u^{n}(t)-u^{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\textrm{and}\quad s_{n}F^{s_{n}}(u^{n}(t))\to F^{0}(u^{0}(t))\qquad\textrm{for every }t\geq 0\,.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be such that
s_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
. Let
u^{0}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and let
\{u^{n}_{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)

and

u^{n}_{0}\to u^{0}_{0}
in

. Then,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unique solution
u^{n}\in H^{1}([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}^{s_{n}}_{0}(\Omega))
to

(4.10)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-\Big[(-\Delta)^{s_{n}}u(t)-\frac{d\omega_{d}}{s_{n}}u(t)\Big]\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
u(0)=u^{n}_{0}\,,\end{cases}

and such a solution satisfies
(-\Delta)^{s_{n}}u^{n}(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for
a.e.\,t\geq 0
.
Moreover,
u^{0}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{0}_{0}(\Omega)
and, for every
T>0
,
u^{n}\to u^{0}
in
H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
as
n\to+\infty
, where
u^{0}\in H^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{0}_{0}(\Omega))
is the unique solution to

(4.11)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-(-\Delta)^{0}u(t)\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\\

and such a solution satisfies
(-\Delta)^{0}u^{0}(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for
a.e.\,t\geq 0
.
Furthermore, if

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\hat{F}^{s_{n}}(u^{n}_{0})=\hat{F}^{0}(u^{0}_{0})\,,

u^{n}\to u^{0}
(strongly) in

for every

\|u^{n}(t)-u^{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\hat{F}^{s_{n}}(u^{n}(t))\to\hat{F}^{0}(u^{0}(t))\qquad\textrm{for every }t\geq 0\,.

The result below shows the convergence toward the classical heat equation as

of the rescaled in time

,

Proof.

Now we show that the functionals
\hat{F}^{s}
are
\lambda
-convex for every
s\in[0,1)
.
We preliminarily notice that the functionals

are convex for every

. Therefore, it is enough to show that the functionals

are
\lambda
-convex. To this end, for every

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t^{2}}f(t)\geq 0

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t^{2}}f(t)=2J_{1}^{s}(v)+\lambda\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq(-2|\Omega|+\lambda)\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq 0\,,

which implies the

Mean-field games of speedy information access with observation costs
Dirk Becherer, Christoph Reisinger, Jonathan Tam

Abstract
We investigate mean-field games (MFG) in which agents can actively control their speed of access to information. Specifically, the agents can dynamically decide to obtain observations with reduced delay by accepting higher observation costs. Agents seek to exploit their active information acquisition by making further decisions to influence their state dynamics so as to maximise rewards. In a mean-field equilibrium, each generic agent solves individually a partially observed Markov decision problem in which the way partial observations are obtained is itself subject to dynamic control actions, while no agent can improve unilaterally given the actions of all others. We formulate the mean-field game with controlled costly information access as an equivalent standard mean-field game on an augmented space, by utilizing a parameterisation of the belief state by a finite number of variables. With sufficient entropy regularisation, a fixed point iteration converges to the unique MFG equilibrium. Moreover, we derive an approximate ε \varepsilon -Nash equilibrium for a large but finite population size and small regularisation parameter. We illustrate our (extended) MFG of information access and of controls by an example from epidemiology, where medical testing results can be procured at different speeds and costs.

Introduction

In dynamic decision making, one often has the opportunity to improve the quality of one’s observations by expending extra resources. Balancing a trade-off between information acquisition and associated costs is then a key question that encompasses both exploration to gain information and, based upon that, the exploitation of optimised actions to maximise one’s rewards. Medical laboratories can invest in personnel resources or infrastructure to reduce waiting times for testing results, enabling faster diagnosis and treatment for patients. For investment decisions in finance, the gathering and processing of information can delay actual trading decisions: in high frequency trading,
latency is aggregated over multiple stages of communication with the exchange; for individual investors,
optimal inattention can be a rational response to the cost and efforts required to respond to stock market fluctuations. Also, the costs and waiting times associated with different health service options (e.g., public vs. private health screening and treatment) can affect individual choices on personal and societal health. In epidemic situations, for instance, such individual decisions can moreover be affected by the perceived collective behaviour of the population. While some of these decisions are more static in nature (e.g., investing in infrastructure), some allow for dynamic choices (e.g., an individual paying for a faster test turn-around). We will primarily focus on the latter, mathematically and computationally more challenging situation.

With likewise examples in mind, we introduce a novel mean-field game (MFG) model in discrete time, in which agents actively control their speed of access to information. During the game, agents can adjust their information access speed by respective costly efforts, and exploit their dynamic information flow to control their state dynamics to maximise expected rewards. This gives rise to a problem of partial observations, in which the flow of information is not exogenously given but instead dynamically controlled by the agents. Consequently, the information flow is endogenously obtained as part of a mean-field equilibrium. We model the speed of information access in the MFG through observations which are delayed to varying degrees, and utilise the information structure to construct a suitable augmentation of the state space. The augmented space includes past actions taken within the dynamic delay period, and serves as the finite state space of an equivalent MFG in standard form. Thereby, numerical schemes for discrete MFGs can be employed to compute approximate mean-field Nash equilibria (MFNE).

This paper covers three themes: (1) dynamic control of information access by observation delay, (2) observation costs, and (3) analysis of associated MFGs incorporating said two features. Standard Markov decision processes (MDP) assume that state observations are received instantaneously, followed by instantaneously applied actions. This limits the applicability of MDPs in many real-life situations, where it is often the case that observation delay arises due to inherent features or practical limitations of a system.

Mean-field games.
In stochastic games, the computation of Nash equilibria is often intractable for a large number of players. Mean-field games (MFGs), first introduced in

Our work.
We model agents’ strategic choices about their speed of information access in the game by a novel MFG in which the dynamic information access
in itself is subject to costly controls. Throughout the paper, we assume that both the state and action spaces are finite. The agents participating in the game exercise control over two aspects: actions that directly influence their state transition dynamics and rewards (as usual in MFG), and the temporal duration of their access speed to information, modelled as an observation delay. Agents dynamically choose from a given finite set of delay periods, each value of which corresponds to some observation cost. To acquire more timely observations, higher information costs are incurred, and vice versa.

Our framework here differs from existing works in that the observation delay is not exogenously given, as in the constant case
[
4
,
6
]
, nor is it a given random variable as in the stochastic case
[
40
,
15
,
16
]
. Instead, the length of the delay is dynamically and actively decided by the agent, based on a trade-off between the extra cost versus the accuracy of more speedy observations, the latter of which can be exploited though better informed control of the dynamics and hence higher rewards. The choice of the delay period becomes an extra part of the control in the optimisation problem in tandem with the agent’s actions. When considering this as a single agent problem, as it occurs during the optimisation step where the measure flow is given, we refer to it as a Markov controllable delay model (MCDM). The MCDM can be reformulated in terms of a POMDP, by augmenting the state with the most recent observation and the actions taken thereafter, to constitute a Markovian system in a fully observable form. This permits for dynamic programming in the respective augmented state space to obtain the Bellman equation.

When viewed as part of the overall MFG, the partial information structure of the problem implies that the measure flow should be specified on the augmented space for the fixed point characterisation of the MFNE. However, the underlying transition dynamics and reward structure depend on the distribution of the (non-observed) states at the present time. In the models of
[
48
,
49
]
with partial information being of quite different structure, given by noisy observations, the barycenter map is used to map measures on a suitably augmented space to the respective measures on the underlying states. However, our model here differs in two aspects. Firstly, our belief state (posterior distribution of non-observed states) admits a description by finitely many parameters, so that the equivalent fully-observed MFG is established on a finite-dimensional augmented space, which is motivated by and helps for numerical computation. Secondly, due to the structure from actively controlled information delay, the observation kernel depends on the distribution of the states throughout each moment in time across the entire delay period. Thus, taking an average of a distribution over the augmented space of parameters, as a barycenter map would do, is not applicable here. Instead, by using the delay structure, we explicitly
calculate the required map from a measure flow on the augmented space to a sequence of measures on the underlying (non-augmented) states. Intuitively, this corresponds to an agent estimating the distribution of the current states of the population, given the observations they possess. We detail the construction of the MCDM in
Section

2
and the corresponding MFG formulation, which we will also refer to as the MFG-MCDM, with its MFNE definition in
Section

3

The second part of this paper, from Section

We employ the seminal (relative) entropy regularisation technique, which aids convergence of the classical iterative scheme: computing an optimal policy (of a single player) for a fixed measure flow, followed by computing the distribution (in augmented state) resulting from said policy. In the standard MFG model, it
has been shown that the fixed point operator for the regularised problem is contractive under mild conditions

Apart from actively controlled information access, which is the main contribution, our analysis with compact Polish state and action spaces also extends some results due to

[

The contributions of this article can be summarised as follows.

1.

We show dynamical programming for a Markov controllable delay model (MCDM), an MDP model where an individual agent can exercise dynamic control over the latency of their observations, with less information delay being more costly. The problem is cast in terms of a partially observed MDP (POMDP) with controlled but costly partial observations, for which the belief state can be paramterised by a finite number of variables. Solving this POMDP is shown to be equivalent to solving an MDP on an augmented state space, whose extension also involves past actions taken during the (non-constant but dynamically controlled) delay period.

2.

We introduce a corresponding mean-field game (MFG) where speedy information access is subject to the agents’ strategic control decisions. For a fixed measure flow, which describes the statistical population evolution, the ensuing single agent control problem becomes an MCDM. Although a mean-field Nash equilibrium (MFNE) is defined in terms of the augmented space, the underlying dynamics and rewards still depend on the underlying state distribution. We show how a measure flow on the underlying space is determined and computed from that of the augmented space. This construction exploits our parameterisation of the belief state; whereas the barycenter approach in
[
48
]
for a measure-valued belief state does not apply here.

3.

By using a sufficiently strong (relative) entropy regularisation in the reward functional, we prove that the regularised MFG-MCDM has a unique MFNE, which is described by a fixed point and can serve as an approximate Nash equilibrium for a large but finite population size. Our setting with the regularised MFG in Polish spaces contributes to a line of work by
[
5
,
19
]
, whose articles are using finite state and action spaces. Results extend to a MFG formulated on infinite horizon with non-stationary but time-dependent measure flows. We also outline a straightforward extension of the results above to the case of MFG-MCDMs with extended interaction being through both the controls and the states.

4.

We illustrate our model by an example from epidemiology, in which we compute both qualitative effects from actively controlled information access and respective costs to the equilibrium, and also the quantitative properties of convergence relating to the entropy regularisation. For the computation, we employ the Prior Descent algorithm
[
19
]
, using the
mfglib
Python package
[

]
as a basis for the MFG-MCDM. This example also demonstrates the extension of MFG of information access to (extended) MFG of controls
[

]
, as it is derived in

6
.

Brief outline of the framework

We present in this section an outline of the framework and main results of the paper. We emphasise that the focus here is on the intuition and general ideas, and for this purpose we frequently refer here to specific places in later sections for the precise definitions.

We refer to our model in consideration as a Markov controllable delay model (MCDM), which consists of an MDP

representing the distribution of the population, we consider instead the objective

\displaystyle J_{\bm{\mu}}(\pi)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}\left(r(x_{t},a_{t},\mu_{t})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i_{t}=k\}}\right)\right].

The notion for a solution for a mean-field game is a mean-field Nash equilibrium (MFNE). This is characterised as a fixed point of the composition of a best-response map and a measure flow map. The best-response map outputs an optimal policy
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}
that maximises
J_{\bm{\mu}}(\pi)
for a fixed measure flow
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}
. Conversely, the measure flow map outputs a measure flow
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}
corresponding to a given policy
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}
. The fixed point condition acts as a consistency condition, requiring that the distribution of players under an optimal policy computed from a measure flow at Nash equilibrium, to be exactly the measure flow itself.

As the MCDM is a model of partial information, it is more convenient to formulate the MFNE in an augmented problem, in the space
\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq\bigcup_{d=d_{K}}^{d_{0}}(\{d\}\times\mathcal{X}\times A^{d})
. The equivalence of the two formulations for the single agent problem is established in
2.7
. However, in the augmented formulation of the mean-field game, the mean-field flow will be defined in the augmented space
\mathcal{Y}
, whilst the transition kernel and reward still depend on measures on the underlying space
\mathcal{X}
. We therefore construct a map that takes
a measure flow
\bm{\nu}
on the augmented space, to a corresponding
\bm{\mu}^{\nu}
on the underlying space, through exploiting the finite parameterisation of the belief state and integrating
\bm{\nu}
repeatedly against the transition kernel
p
(cf.
3.9
). This allows us to define the best response map
\Phi^{\rm aug}:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})^{\infty}\to\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}
and the measure flow map
\Psi^{\rm aug}:\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})^{\infty}
as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\bm{\nu})

\displaystyle=\left\{\hat{\pi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}:J_{\bm{\mu}^{\nu}}(\hat{\pi})=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}J_{\bm{\mu}^{\nu}}(\pi)\right\},

\displaystyle\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t+1}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Y}\times U}p_{y}\Big(\cdot\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t}(\mathrm{d}y),

where
U\coloneqq A\times\mathcal{I}
and
p_{y}
denote the transition kernel in the augmented space (cf. (
3.2
)), so that for the MCDM, a MFNE is defined to be a fixed point of the map
\Psi^{\rm aug}\circ\Phi^{\rm aug}
(cf.
3.13
).

Our result concerns a regularised MFG of the MCDM, where we show that the fixed point iteration for the regularised game is contractive, therefore establishing the uniqueness of a regularised MFNE. This requires the addition of a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence term in the value function. For a reference measure
q\in\mathcal{P}(U)
, measure flow
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})^{\infty}
and regularisation parameter
\eta>0
we consider the regularised objective

\displaystyle J_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}\left(r_{y}\big(y_{t},u_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)-\eta D_{KL}(\pi_{t}\|q)\right)\right],

where
r_{y}
represents the reward formulated in the augmented space (cf. (
3.3
)), and in particular includes the observation costs. In the regularised game, for a fixed measure flow
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})^{\infty}
, the optimal policy is given by the softmax policy

\displaystyle\pi^{\rm soft}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\coloneqq\frac{\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)/\eta\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,{u}^{\prime})/\eta\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})}q(\mathrm{d}u),

where
Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
is the associated
Q
-function for
J_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
(see (
4.2
)). Thus, by defining the regularised best-response map to output the softmax policy associated to the measure flow
\bm{\nu}
(cf.
4.3
), we have the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

The fixed point operator
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi^{\mathrm{reg}}
is a contraction mapping on the Banach space
(\Delta^{\infty}_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}},\delta_{\infty})
, provided that
\zeta>C_{p,r}
in the metric (
1.2
), and
\eta>K_{\zeta,p,r}
. Here, the constant
C_{p,r}
depends only on the bounds and Lipschitz constants of the transition kernel
p
and reward function
r
, and
K_{\zeta,p,r}
depends in addition on the chosen value of
\zeta
. Hence, by Banach’s fixed point theorem, there exists a unique fixed point for
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi^{\mathrm{reg}}
, which is a regularised MFNE.

In particular, the threshold for
\eta
is lower when the discount factor
\gamma
is high or the transition kernel’s dependence on the population distribution is low, i.e., for small values of
L_{p}
. The precise statement of the theorem above with more explicit thresholds for
\zeta
and
\eta
is given in
4.11
. Finally, we show that a regularised MFNE does indeed give an approximate Nash equilibrium for the finite player game. In the finite player game, each player’s policy depends also on the empirical distribution of the other players. In a game with
N
players, the objective for player
n
is given by

\displaystyle J^{N}_{n}(\pi)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}\big(r(x^{n}_{t},a^{n}_{t},e^{N}_{t})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i^{n}_{t}=k\}}\big)\right],\ \pi=(\pi^{1},\ldots,\pi^{N})\in\mathcal{A},

where
\mathcal{A}=\prod^{N}_{n=1}\mathcal{A}^{n}
denotes the space of admissible policies for the
N

3.3
). A regularised MFNE with sufficiently small regularisation is then an approximate Nash equilibrium for a finite player game with sufficiently larger number of players. We state this more precisely in the theorem below.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(\eta_{j})_{j}
be a sequence with
\eta_{j}\downarrow 0
. For each
j
, let
(\pi^{(j)}_{*},\bm{\nu}^{(j)}_{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})^{\infty}
be the associated regularised MFNE, defined via
4.3

\varepsilon>0
, there exists
{j}^{\prime},{N}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq{j}^{\prime}
and
N\geq{N}^{\prime}
, the policy
(\pi^{(j)}_{*},\ldots,\pi^{(j)}_{*})
is
\varepsilon
-Nash for the
N
-player game. That is,

\displaystyle J^{N}_{n}(\pi^{(j)}_{*},\ldots,\pi^{(j)}_{*})\geq\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}J^{N}_{n}(\pi,\pi^{(j),-n}_{*})-\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all $n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$},

where
\pi^{(j),-n}_{*}
represents the policy

Control of information speed: single agent case

We first state the definition of a Markov controllable delay model (MCDM) below, which characterises the scenarios where agents can control their information delay.

Definition 2.1
.

A Markov controllable delay model (MCDM) is a tuple
\langle\mathcal{X},A,\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},p,r\rangle
, where

•

the
state space

\mathcal{X}
and the
action space

A
are non-emoty Polish spaces, equipped with their respective Borel sigma fields;

•

the set of
delay values
is
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{0},\ldots,d_{K}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
0\leq d_{K}<\ldots<d_{0}
;

•

the set of
cost values
is
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{K}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
0=c_{0}<c_{1}\ldots<c_{K}
;

•

the
transition kernel
is a map
p:\mathcal{X}\times A\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
;

•

the
reward function

r:\mathcal{X}\times A\to[0,\infty)
is bounded and measurable.

We will make the following additional assumption for the rest of the paper.

Assumption 2.2
.

The state space
\mathcal{X}
and the action space
A
are compact, the reward function
r
is continuous, and the transition kernel
p
is weakly continuous, i.e. if
(x_{n},a_{n})\to(x,a)\in\mathcal{X}\times A
, then
{p(\cdot\mid x_{n},a_{n})\to p(\cdot\mid x,a)}
weakly.

2.1
mimics that of a standard MDP, but with the addition of delay and cost values. The effect of the variable delay in the control problem will become apparent with the construction of admissible policies, see
2.4
. In the following, we assume that the problem initiates at time
t=0
, and denote prior observations with negative indices.

Definition 2.3
.

Denote by
\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\{0,1,\ldots,K\}
the
intervention set
. Let
H_{0}\coloneqq(\mathcal{X}\times A)^{d_{0}}\times\mathcal{X}
, and define recursively
H_{t}\coloneqq H_{t-1}\times A\times\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{X}
,
t\geq 1
. A

\pi=(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a sequence of (Markov) kernels
\pi_{t}:H_{t}\to\mathcal{P}(A\times\mathcal{I})
.

The intervention set
\mathcal{I}
represetns an agent’s control over their delay in an MCDM. Since the delay restricts the amount of information that the agent possesses, the following definition of admissible policies is used.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
h_{t}=(x_{-d_{0}},a_{-d_{0}},\ldots,x_{0},a_{0},i_{0},\ldots,a_{t-1},i_{t-1},x_{t})\in H_{t}

(\tilde{\iota}_{t})_{t}

\tilde{\iota}_{t}=\max\{\tilde{\iota}_{t-1}+1,d_{i_{t-1}}\}

\tilde{\iota}_{0}=d_{0}

\pi

\phi^{d}_{t}:\mathcal{X}^{d_{0}+t-d+1}\times A^{t+d_{0}}\to\mathcal{P}(A\times\mathcal{I})
,

,

\displaystyle\pi_{t}(\cdot\mid h_{t})=\phi^{\tilde{\iota}_{t}}_{t}(\cdot\mid x_{-d_{0}},\ldots,x_{t-{\tilde{\iota}_{t}}},a_{-d_{0}},\ldots,a_{t-1}).

The set of admissible policies for the MCDM is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}

With an admissible policy
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}
and an initial distribution
q_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(H_{0})

on

31

\omega=(x_{-d_{0}},a_{-d_{0}},\ldots,x_{0},a_{0},i_{0},\ldots)\in H_{\infty}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle=q_{0}(\mathrm{d}h_{0})\ p(\mathrm{d}x_{-d_{0}+1}\mid x_{-d_{0}},a_{-d_{0}})\ldots p(\mathrm{d}x_{0}\mid x_{-1},a_{-1})

\displaystyle\qquad\pi_{0}(\mathrm{d}a_{0},\mathrm{d}i_{0}\mid h_{0})\ p(\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mid x_{0},a_{0})\ \pi(\mathrm{d}a_{1},\mathrm{d}i_{1}\mid h_{1})\ldots

The objective function for the infinite horizon problem with discounted cost is then

\displaystyle J(\pi)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}\left(r(x_{t},a_{t})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i_{t}=k\}}\right)\right],

(2.1)

where
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}
is the expectation over the measure
\mathbb{P}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}

\gamma\in(0,1)
is the discount factor. If the problem starts at some time

\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{t,q_{0}}
for the expectation. If

, then we write
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{h_{0}}
(resp.
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{t,h_{0}}

\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\delta_{h_{0}}}
(resp.
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{t,\delta_{h_{0}}}
).

The above setup provides the following interpretation of the sequential evolution of an MCDM. At time
t
, the controller observes the underlying state
x_{t-d}\in\mathcal{X}
for some
d\in[d_{K},d_{0}]
, with knowledge of their applied actions
a_{t-d},\ldots,a_{t-1}\in A
. Based on this information, the controller applies an action
a_{t}
and receives a reward
r(x_{t},a_{t})
, which we assume not to be observable until
x_{t}
becomes observable. The controller then chooses a value for
i_{t}
, which determines their cost
c_{i_{t}}
, as well as their next delay period of
d_{i_{t}}
units. This process then repeats at the next time. To ensure that the setup is sensible in its interpretation, if
i_{t}=k
and the current delay

is shorter than
d_{k}
, then the delay at time
t+1
will simply be extended to
d+1
units (in reality, paying a higher cost for a longer delay is clearly sub-optimal, so such a choice of
i_{t}
would not practically occur).

Since the control problem arising from an MCDM is non-Markovian in its current form, we shall establish an equivalence with a fully observable MDP, so that we can apply dynamic programming. As noted in
[
4
]
, this is akin to solving a POMDP by constructing an equivalent MDP on the belief state. Yet, here the information basis for our decision problem is not described by observations being noisy functions of the underlying state being obtained concurrently. Instead, it is given by the last delayed state we had actively chosen to observe in the past, at some cost,
complemented by our actions taken since then.

Definition 2.5
.

For
d_{K}\leq d\leq d_{0}

\mathcal{Y}_{d}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}\times A^{d}

\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq\bigcup_{d=d_{K}}^{d_{0}}\left(\{d\}\times\mathcal{X}\times A^{d}\right)=\bigsqcup^{d_{0}}_{d=d_{K}}\mathcal{Y}_{d},

where

\displaystyle\delta_{Y}(y,{y}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\delta_{d}(y,{y}^{\prime})\wedge 1,\quad&y,{y}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}_{d},\ d_{K}\leq d\leq d_{0},\\
1,&y\in\mathcal{Y}_{d},\ {y}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}_{{d}^{\prime}},\ d\neq{d}^{\prime},\end{cases}

where
\delta_{d}
is any metric that induces the product topology on

Thus the augmented variable includes the delay of the system at the current time, the underlying state that is observed with that delay, and the actions applied from that moment up to the present. We shall write an element
y\in\mathcal{Y}
in the form

(d,x,a_{-d},\ldots,a_{-1}),\ \mbox{or }(d,x,(a)^{-1}_{-d})

\langle\mathcal{Y},A\times\mathcal{I},p_{y},r_{y}\rangle

{p_{y}:\mathcal{Y}\times(A\times\mathcal{I})\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y}})

\displaystyle p_{y}(\mathrm{d}\hat{y}\mid y,\tilde{a},i)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}p^{(d-d_{i}+1)}\big(\mathrm{d}\hat{x}\mid x,(a)_{-d}^{-d_{i}}\big)\otimes\delta_{\{(a_{-d_{i}+1},\ldots,a_{-1},\tilde{a})\}}(\mathrm{d}\hat{\bm{a}})\otimes\delta_{d_{i}}(\hat{d}),&\ d_{i}\leq d\leq d_{0};\\
\delta_{x}(\mathrm{d}\hat{x})\otimes\delta_{(a_{-d},\ldots,a_{-1},\tilde{a})}(\mathrm{d}\hat{\bm{a}})\otimes\delta_{d+1}(\hat{d}),&\ d_{K}\leq d<d_{i},\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
\mathrm{d}\hat{y}=\sqcup_{\hat{d}}\ \mathrm{d}\hat{x}\mathrm{d}\hat{\bm{a}}

\displaystyle r_{y}(y,\tilde{a},i)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{X}}r({x}^{\prime},\tilde{a})p^{(d)}(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime}\mid x,\bm{a})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i=k\}}.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
{H}^{\prime}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}

Given a policy

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{aug}}({\pi}^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{{\pi}^{\prime}}_{\tilde{q}_{0}}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}r_{y}(y_{t},a_{t},i_{t})\right],

where

Proposition 2.7
.

Given an initial distribution

\displaystyle q_{0}(\cdot)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{Y}}p^{(d)}(\cdot\mid x,\bm{a})\tilde{q}_{0}(\mathrm{d}y),\quad y=(d,x,\bm{a}).

Then solving the augmented MDP is equivalent to solving the MCDM problem, and it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}J(\pi)=\sup_{{\pi}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{aug}}}J_{\mathrm{aug}}({\pi}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Following the argument in
[
31

\displaystyle H_{t}\ni(x_{-d_{0}},a_{-d_{0}},\ldots,x_{0},a_{0},i_{0},\ldots,x_{t})\leftrightarrow(y_{0},a_{0},i_{0},\ldots,y_{t-1},a_{t-1},i_{t-1},y_{t})\in\mathcal{H}_{t},

where

\displaystyle{\pi}^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot\mid{h}^{\prime}_{t})\coloneqq\pi_{t}(\cdot\mid h_{t}),\quad t\geq 0.

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}J(\pi)=\sup_{{\pi}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{aug}}}J_{\mathrm{aug}}({\pi}^{\prime})

Proposition 2.8

Let

\displaystyle J^{*}(y)\coloneqq\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{y}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}r_{y}(y_{t},a_{t},i_{t})\right],

\displaystyle J^{*}(y)=\max_{(\tilde{a},i)\in A\times\mathcal{I}}\left\{\int_{\mathcal{X}}r({x}^{\prime},\tilde{a})p^{(d)}(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime}\mid x,\bm{a})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i=k\}}+\gamma\int_{\mathcal{Y}}J^{*}({y}^{\prime})p_{y}(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime}\mid y,\tilde{a},i)\right\},

where

Proof.

As the equivalent augmented formulation is now a fully observable problem, the dynamic programming equation follows from a standard argument for the case of a fully observable MDP, see e.g.
[

∎

Remark 2.9
.

When considering the MFG in the next section, a deterministic measure flow representing the population distribution introduces an implicit time dependence within the transition kernel and reward. The generic single agent problem in the definition of the MFNE then becomes time-inhomogeneous. The time-homogeneous setup in this section readily generalises directly to a setup with time-inhomogeneous transition kernels, rewards and dynamic programming equations. However, for ease of exposition we choose to present the MCDM under the time-homogeneous setting here.

, defined recursively by the relation: for

\displaystyle p^{(t)}(\mathrm{d}x_{t}\mid x_{0},(a)_{0}^{t-1},(\mu)_{0}^{t-1})\coloneqq p(\mathrm{d}x_{t}\mid x_{t-1},a_{t-1},\mu_{n-1})\ p^{(t-1)}(\{x_{t-1}\}\mid x_{0},(a)_{0}^{t-2},(\mu)_{0}^{t-2}).

(3.1)

We shall also frequently make use of the following lemma in subsequent proofs.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any fixed
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, there exists a measure
q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
such that

is absolutely continuous with respect to

for all

and

.

Proof.

Since

and

is continuous with respect to

, the set

is compact in

be a corresponding dense set of measures, and define

\displaystyle q(C)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\rho_{n}(C),\quad C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}).

Clearly

implies
\rho_{n}(C)=0

3.1
Finite agent game with observation delay

We consider an
N
-player game with mean-field interaction, where each agent can control their observation delay. Take a tuple
\langle\mathcal{X},A,\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},p,r\rangle
as in
3.1
. Let
x^{n}_{t}
and
a^{n}_{t}
be the state and action taken by the
n
th
player at time
t
respectively. Then the
n
th
player receives a reward of
r(x^{n}_{t},a^{n}_{t},e^{N}_{t})
at time
t
, where
e^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{k=1}\delta_{x^{k}_{t}}
is the empirical distribution of the states of the agents. The player’s next transition from state
x^{n}_{t}
is then determined by the transition kernel

p(\mathrm{d}x^{n}_{t+1}\mid x^{n}_{t},a^{n}_{t},e^{N}_{t})
.

Definition 3.3
.

A policy
\pi=(\pi^{1},\ldots,\pi^{N})
for the
N
-player MCDM is a sequence of maps
\pi^{n}_{t}:(H_{t})^{N}\to\mathcal{P}(U)
for
1\leq n\leq N
,
t\geq 0
(cf.
2.3
).
\pi
is admissible if at each time
t
, there exists a sequence of kernels
\phi^{j,d}_{t}:\mathcal{X}^{d_{0}+t-d+1}\times A^{t+d_{0}}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})^{t+1}\to\mathcal{P}(U)
,
d_{K}\leq d\leq d_{0}
, such that for each
h_{t}\in(H_{t})^{N}
,

\displaystyle\pi^{n}_{t}(\cdot\mid h_{t})=\phi^{n,\tilde{\iota}^{n}_{t}}_{t}(\cdot\mid x^{n}_{-d_{0}},\ldots,x^{n}_{t-{\tilde{\iota}^{n}_{t}}},a^{n}_{-d_{0}},\ldots,a^{n}_{t-1},\tilde{e}^{N}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{e}^{N}_{t}).

where
\tilde{\iota}^{n}_{t}
is the delay for the
n
th
player at time
t
(cf.
2.4
), and
\tilde{e}^{N}_{t}
is the empirical distribution of the augmented state, i.e.

\displaystyle\tilde{e}^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{k=1}\delta_{y^{k}_{t}}(\cdot),\quad y^{k}_{t}=(\tilde{\iota}^{k}_{t},x^{k}_{t-\tilde{\iota}^{k}_{t}},a^{k}_{t-\tilde{\iota}^{k}_{t}},\ldots,a^{k}_{t-1}).

The set of admissible policies for
N
-players is denoted by
\mathcal{A}
, and the set of admissible policies for player
n
is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{n}
, so that
\mathcal{A}=\prod^{N}_{n=1}\mathcal{A}^{n}
.

Given
q_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})^{N}
, player
n
’s objective is given by

\displaystyle J^{N}_{n}(\pi)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}\left(r(x^{n}_{t},a^{n}_{t},e^{N}_{t})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i^{n}_{t}=k\}}\right)\right],\ \pi=(\pi^{1},\ldots,\pi^{N})\in\mathcal{A}.

If the other players’ strategies are considered fixed, then we can consider
J^{N}_{n}
as a function of the policy of player
n
only, and we write
J^{N}_{n}(\pi^{n},\pi^{-n})
, where
\pi^{-n}=(\pi^{1},\ldots,\pi^{n-1},\pi^{n+1},\ldots,\pi^{N})
. The notion of optimality in the
N
-player game can be captured by the concept of a Nash equilibrium, which intuitively says that, at equilibrium, no player can make gains by deviating from their current strategy, provided that all other players remain at their strategy.

Definition 3.4
.

\pi_{*}\in\mathcal{A}
is a Nash equilibrium for the
N
-player MCDM if for each
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

\displaystyle J^{N}_{n}(\pi_{*})=\sup_{\pi^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}J^{N}_{n}(\pi^{n},\pi_{*}^{-n}),

For
\varepsilon>0
, a policy
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
is an
\varepsilon
-Nash equilibrium for the
N
-player MCDM if for each
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

\displaystyle J^{N}_{n}(\pi)\geq\sup_{\pi^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}J^{N}_{n}(\pi^{n},\pi^{-n})-\varepsilon.

In general, the Nash equilibrium is hard to characterise and computationally intractable. It is also impractical to search over policies that depend on the distribution of all players. Therefore, it is more useful to consider a search over Markovian policies for each player, and formulate the equilibrium condition with respect to such policies. As such, the common approach for modelling partially observable games is to consider Markovian policies as above
[
48
]
. This is a reasonable assumption as in practice it will be hard for each agent to keep track of the movement of all other players when the number of players is large. A policy is
Markovian
if
\pi=(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is such that
\pi_{t}:\mathcal{Y}^{N}\to(\mathcal{P}(U))^{N}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}
denote the set of Markovian policies, and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}^{n}
for the set of Markovian policies for player
n
, so that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}=\prod^{N}_{n=1}\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}^{n}
.

Definition 3.5
.

\pi_{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}
is a Markov–Nash equilibrium for the
N
-player MCDM if for each
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

\displaystyle J^{N}_{n}(\pi_{*})=\sup_{\pi^{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}^{n}}J^{N}_{n}(\pi^{n},\pi_{*}^{-n}),

For
\varepsilon>0
, a policy
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}
is an
\varepsilon
-Markov–Nash equilibrium for the MCDM if for each
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

\displaystyle J^{N}_{n}(\pi)\geq\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{mrkv}}^{n}}J^{N}_{n}(\pi^{n},\pi^{-n})-\varepsilon.

3.2
Mean-field game with observation delay

The computation and characterisation of (Markov–)Nash equilibria is typically intractable due to the curse of dimensionality and the coupled dynamics across the different agents. Therefore, as an approximation, we consider the infinite population limit by sending the number of players
{N\to\infty}
, and replace the empirical distribution of the agents with a measure flow
{\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{t})_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}}
. We consider the viewpoint of one representative agent, and assume that its interactions with members of the population, modelled by the measure flow
\bm{\mu}
, are symmetric.

Let
\langle\mathcal{X},A,\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},p,r\rangle
be an MCDM with mean-field interaction. Let
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}
be a fixed measure flow. At time

under the transition kernel
p(\mathrm{d}x_{t+1}\mid x_{t},a_{t},\mu_{t})
, and collects a reward of
r(x_{t},a_{t},\mu_{t})
. We impose the following additional assumptions on the MCDM-MFG for the rest of the paper.

Assumption 3.6
.

(a)

There exists a constant

such that for all

,

,
{\mu,\hat{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}})

\displaystyle\delta_{TV}\left(p(\cdot\mid x,a,\mu),\ p(\cdot\mid\hat{x},\hat{a},\hat{\mu})\right)\leq L_{p}(\delta_{\mathcal{X}}(x,\hat{x})+\delta_{A}(a,\hat{a})+\delta_{TV}(\mu,\hat{\mu})).

(b)

There exists a constant

such that for all

,

,
{\mu,\hat{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}})

\displaystyle\lvert r(x,a,\mu)-r(\hat{x},\hat{a},\hat{\mu})\rvert\leq L_{r}(\delta_{\mathcal{X}}(x,\hat{x})+\delta_{A}(a,\hat{a})+\delta_{TV}(\mu,\hat{\mu})).

Moreover,

is bounded by a constant

.

Given a fixed measure flow
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}
and initial condition

\displaystyle J_{\bm{\mu}}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}\left(r(x_{t},a_{t},\mu_{t})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i_{t}=k\}}\right)\right].

By

\displaystyle p_{y}(\mathrm{d}\hat{y}\mid y,\tilde{a},i)

\displaystyle r_{y}(y,u,\bm{\mu})\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{X}}r({x}^{\prime},a_{t},\mu_{t})\ p^{(d)}(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime}\mid x,\bm{a},(\mu)^{t-1}_{t-d})-\sum^{K}_{k=1}c_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i=k\}}.

Given

Proposition 3.7

3.3
Mean-field Nash equilibrium

We proceed to define the mean-field Nash equilibrium (MFNE) for the mean-field MCDM problem. As the presence of observation delays leads to a non-Markovian problem, it is more convenient to formulate the MFNE fixed point in terms of the augmented space. We shall give the MFNE definition in terms of both the underlying and augmented problem, and show that the two notions are equal. We note that even in the augmented formulation, the objective function
J_{\bm{\mu}}
depends on the measure flow
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}
on the underlying state space. Therefore, in order to define the MFNE in the augmented problem, we construct a map that takes for each time
t
, a measure
\nu_{t}
on the augmented space, to a sequence of measures
(\mu_{t,d})^{d_{0}}_{d=0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{d_{0}+1}
. We will use the following operation which integrates a transition kernel against a measure.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
E,F,G
be Borel spaces. Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(E\times F)
and
p:E\times\mathcal{B}(G)\to[0,1]
be a transition kernel. We define the measure

as follows. For any open set
O=O_{1}\times O_{2}\in G\times F
,

\displaystyle(v\star p)(O_{1}\times O_{2})=\int_{E}p(O_{1}\mid e)\nu(\mathrm{d}e\times O_{2}).

We will typically take
E=\mathcal{X}\times A
,
F=A^{d-1}
and
G=\mathcal{X}
for some

. In this setting,
v\star p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}\times A^{d-1})
, and we can define
v\star_{k}p\coloneqq(v\star_{k-1}p)\star p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}\times A^{d-k})
for

. If
\bar{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
is a sequence of transition kernels, write
v\star_{k}\bar{p}\coloneqq((v\star p_{1})\star\ldots)\star p_{k}
. We will also write

when we want to indicate the dependence of the transition kernel

on the measure
\mu
.

To construct the aforementioned map, we enlarge
\mathcal{Y}
to

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{d=d_{K}}^{d_{0}}\left(\{d\}\times\mathcal{X}^{d_{0}-d+1}\times A^{d}\right)=\bigsqcup^{d_{0}}_{d=d_{K}}\left(\mathcal{X}^{d_{0}-d+1}\times A^{d}\right)\eqqcolon\bigsqcup^{d_{0}}_{d=d_{K}}\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{d}.

In this instance, an element
\tilde{y}_{n}\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}
can be viewed as

\tilde{y}_{n}=(d,x_{n-d_{0}},\ldots,x_{n-d},a_{n-d},\ldots,a_{n-1})
. This extra enlargement of
\mathcal{Y}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}

enables
the measure flow construction in
3.9

formulation
of the MFNE fixed-point condition as
in
3.13
.

Definition 3.9

Let
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{t})_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
. For each

, we write
\nu_{t}=\sum_{d}w^{d}_{t}\nu^{d}_{t}
, where

and
\nu^{d}_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{d})
. For

, let
\nu^{x_{t-{d}^{\prime}}}_{t}
and

be the marginals of
\nu_{t}
and
\nu^{d}_{t}
in the

, we define
\mu_{t,d_{0}}=\nu^{x_{t-d_{0}}}_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
. Then, for each

\displaystyle\mu_{t,{d}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{d_{0}}_{d=d_{K}}w^{d}_{t}\xi^{d}_{t,{d}^{\prime}},\quad\xi^{d}_{t,{d}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}),

\displaystyle\xi^{d}_{t,{d}^{\prime}}(C)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\nu^{x_{t-{d}^{\prime}}\mid d}_{t}(C),&d\leq{d}^{\prime};\\
(\nu^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p^{d,{d}^{\prime}}_{\mu})(C\times A^{{d}^{\prime}}),&d>{d}^{\prime},\end{cases}\quad p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\mu}=(p^{\mu_{t,d}},\ldots,p^{\mu_{t,{d}^{\prime}-1}}),\ C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}),

where
\nu^{d,x,a}_{t}
is the marginal of
\nu^{d}_{t}
on the

We interpret

The following lemma shows that the mapping

Proposition 3.10

Proof.

\nu_{t}=\sum_{d}w^{d}_{t}\nu^{d}_{t}
and
\hat{\nu}_{t}=\sum_{d}\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\hat{\nu}^{d}_{t}
. We use the shorthand

\displaystyle\delta(d)=\delta_{TV}(\mu_{t,d},\hat{\mu}_{t,d}),\quad\delta(\bar{d},{d}^{\prime})=\delta_{\max}((\mu_{t,d})^{\bar{d}}_{{d}^{\prime}},(\hat{\mu}_{t,d})^{\bar{d}}_{{d}^{\prime}}).

By a similar argument to
Lemma

3.2
, we can find a measure
q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, against which
\xi^{\bar{d}}_{t,{d}^{\prime}}
is absolutely continuous for all
0\leq\bar{d},{d}^{\prime}<d_{0}
. Therefore, with slight abuse of notation, we shall use
\xi^{\bar{d}}_{t,{d}^{\prime}}
to denote both the measure and its corresponding density. By definition we have

\displaystyle\delta(d_{0})=\delta_{TV}(\nu^{x_{t-d_{0}}}_{t},\hat{\nu}^{x_{t-d_{0}}}_{t})\leq\delta_{TV}(\nu_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t}).

Suppose now that
{d}^{\prime}<d_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\delta({d}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\Bigg\lvert\underbrace{\sum^{d_{0}}_{d=d_{K}}w^{d}_{t}\xi^{d}_{t,{d}^{\prime}}({x}^{\prime})}_{I_{1}({x}^{\prime})}-\underbrace{\sum^{d_{0}}_{d=d_{K}}\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\hat{\xi}^{d}_{t,{d}^{\prime}}({x}^{\prime})}_{I_{2}({x}^{\prime})}\Bigg\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime})

(3.4)

We can write
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
as

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum^{{d}^{\prime}}_{d=d_{K}}w^{d}_{t}\nu^{x_{t-{d}^{\prime}}|d}_{t}+\underbrace{\sum^{d_{0}}_{d={d}^{\prime}+1}w^{d}_{t}\cdot[\nu^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\mu}]_{\mathcal{X}}}_{J_{1}}\ ,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum^{{d}^{\prime}}_{d=d_{K}}\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\hat{\nu}^{x_{t-{d}^{\prime}}|d}_{t}+\underbrace{\sum^{d_{0}}_{d={d}^{\prime}+1}\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\cdot[\hat{\nu}^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\hat{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{X}}}_{J_{2}}\ ,

where
[\cdot]_{\mathcal{X}}
denotes the marginal of the measure on
\mathcal{X}
(cf.
3.9
). Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\lvert J_{1}({x}^{\prime})-J_{2}({x}^{\prime})\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum^{d_{0}}_{d={d}^{\prime}+1}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\Bigg\lvert w^{d}_{t}\cdot[v^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\mu}]_{\mathcal{X}}\ ({x}^{\prime})-\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\cdot[\hat{v}^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\hat{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{X}}\ ({x}^{\prime})\Bigg\rvert q_{x}(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum^{d_{0}}_{d={d}^{\prime}+1}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\Bigg\lvert w^{d}_{t}\cdot\Big([v^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\mu}]_{\mathcal{X}}-[\hat{v}^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\hat{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{X}}\Big)\ ({x}^{\prime})\Bigg\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\ \qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathcal{X}}\Bigg\lvert w^{d}_{t}\cdot[v^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\hat{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{X}}\ ({x}^{\prime})-\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\cdot[\hat{v}^{d,x,a}_{t}\star_{d-{d}^{\prime}}p_{d,{d}^{\prime}}^{\hat{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{X}}\ ({x}^{\prime})\Bigg\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2\sum^{d_{0}}_{d={d}^{\prime}+1}\left((d-{d}^{\prime})(L_{p})^{d-{d}^{\prime}}\cdot\delta(d,{d}^{\prime}+1)+\lvert w^{d}_{t}\nu^{d,x,a}_{t}-\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\hat{\nu}^{d,x,a}_{t}\rvert\right).

Returning to (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\delta({d}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\bigg(\lvert J_{1}({x}^{\prime})-J_{2}({x}^{\prime})\rvert+\sum^{{d}^{\prime}}_{d=d_{K}}\left\lvert w^{d}_{t}\nu^{x_{t-{d}^{\prime}}|d}_{t}({x}^{\prime})-\hat{w}^{d}_{t}\hat{\nu}^{x_{t-{d}^{\prime}}|d}_{t}({x}^{\prime})\right\rvert\bigg)q(\mathrm{d}{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum^{d_{0}}_{d={d}^{\prime}+1}(d-{d}^{\prime})(L_{p})^{d-{d}^{\prime}}\cdot\delta(d,{d}^{\prime}+1)+\delta_{TV}\left(\nu^{(x)^{t-{d}^{\prime}}_{t-d_{0}},(a)^{t-{d}^{\prime}-1}_{t-d_{0}}}_{t},\ \hat{\nu}^{(x)^{t-{d}^{\prime}}_{t-d_{0}},(a)^{t-{d}^{\prime}-1}_{t-d_{0}}}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum^{d_{0}}_{d={d}^{\prime}+1}(d-{d}^{\prime})(L_{p})^{d-{d}^{\prime}}\cdot\delta(d_{0})+\delta_{TV}\left(\nu^{(x)^{t-{d}^{\prime}}_{t-d_{0}},(a)^{t-{d}^{\prime}-1}_{t-d_{0}}}_{t},\ \hat{\nu}^{(x)^{t-{d}^{\prime}}_{t-d_{0}},(a)^{t-{d}^{\prime}-1}_{t-d_{0}}}_{t}\right),

where the superscripts above
\nu_{t}
and
\hat{\nu}_{t}
denote their marginals on the corresponding coordinates. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{t})=\max_{0\leq d\leq d_{0}}\delta_{TV}(\mu_{t,d},\hat{\mu}_{t,d})\leq\sum^{d_{0}}_{d=0}(dL_{p})^{d}\ \delta_{TV}(\nu_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t}),

as required.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

A superficially similar task is solved using an elegant approach in
[
48
,
49
]
for a partial observation problem of a different kind, where the respective
\bm{\mu}^{\nu}=(\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t})_{t}
can be obtained as barycenters of measures
{\nu}_{t}
describing the fully observable belief state (posterior state distribution). The barycenter approach makes use of the different structure of their partial observation setting, where observations of states are perturbed by unbiased noise. In our case, the belief state is parameterised by a finite set given by past observations, so the notion of taking the barycenter does not apply here. Instead, exploiting the fact that our state and action spaces are assumed to be finite, we will construct the required
\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}
explicitly by repeatedly applying the transition kernel (
3.2
).

We are now ready to define the mean-field Nash equilibrium (MFNE) through a fixed-point characterisation. Note that since policies can only rely on past information, we require an additional measure
\nu_{0}
to act as an initial condition.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{t})_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}
and
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})
such that
\mu_{0}=\mathcal{M}(\nu_{0})
. Define:

(i)

The best-response map
\Phi_{\nu_{0}}:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}\to\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}
, given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\nu_{0}}(\bm{\mu})\coloneqq\left\{\hat{\pi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}:J_{\bm{\mu}}(\hat{\pi})=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}J_{\bm{\mu}}(\pi)\right\}.

(ii)

The measure flow map
\Psi:\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}
, defined recursively by
\Psi(\pi)_{0}\coloneqq\mu_{0}
and

\displaystyle\Psi(\pi)_{t+1}(\cdot)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{X}}p(\cdot\mid x,\pi^{a}_{t},\mu_{t})\Psi(\pi)_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)\right]

where
\pi^{a}_{t}
is the marginal of
\pi_{t}
on
A
.

(iii)

{(\pi^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}}
is a mean-field Nash equilibrium (MFNE), if it is a fixed point of the map, i.e.
\pi^{*}\in\Phi_{\nu_{0}}(\bm{\mu}^{*})
and
\bm{\mu}^{*}=\Psi(\pi^{*})
.

To define an MFNE in the augmented space, for any measure flow
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
, consider the augmented objective

\displaystyle J_{\bm{\nu}}(\pi)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\nu_{0}}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}r_{y}(y_{t},u_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t})\right],

where
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\nu_{0}}
is the expectation induced by initial measure
\nu_{0}
, policy
\pi
, and transition kernel
p_{y}
, noting that the induced measures
\bm{\mu}^{\nu}
are used when evaluating
p_{y}
in (
3.2
).

Definition 3.13
.

Let
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{t})_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
. Define:

(i)

The best-response map
\Phi^{\mathrm{aug}}:\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}\to\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}
, given by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\bm{\nu})\coloneqq\left\{\hat{\pi}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}:J_{\bm{\nu}}(\hat{\pi})=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}J_{\bm{\nu}}(\pi)\right\}.

(ii)

The measure flow map
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}:\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\to\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
, defined recursively by
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{0}\coloneqq\nu_{0}
and

\displaystyle\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t+1}(\cdot)\coloneqq\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\times U}p_{y}\Big(\cdot\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t}(\mathrm{d}y).

(iii)

{(\pi^{*},\bm{\nu}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}}
is a mean-field Nash equilibrium (MFNE) for the augmented MCDM problem, if it is a fixed point of the map
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi^{\mathrm{aug}}
, i.e.,
\pi^{*}\in\Phi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\bm{\nu}^{*})
and
\bm{\nu}^{*}=\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi^{*})
.

Note that thanks to the definition of
\Psi^{\rm aug}
in (ii), the support of any measure
\bm{\nu}^{*}
that satisfies the MFNE condition is consistent (cf.
2.4
). The following proposition demonstrates the equivalence of the two formulations of the MFNE.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
{(\pi^{*},\bm{\nu}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}}
be an MFNE for the augmented problem. Then the induced pair
{(\pi^{*},\bm{\mu}^{\nu,*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}}
is an MFNE for the underlying problem. Conversely, given
{(\pi^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})^{\infty}}
, the induced pair
{(\pi^{*},\bm{\nu}^{\mu,*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}}
is an MFNE for the augmented problem.

Proof.

Assume that
(\pi^{*},\bm{\nu}^{*})
is an MFNE in the augmented space. By definition we have
\pi^{*}\in\Phi(\bm{\mu}^{\nu})
and also
\mu^{\nu,*}_{0}=\mathcal{M}(\nu_{0})=\Psi(\pi^{*})_{0}
. Since
\bm{\nu}^{*}
is the image of
\Psi^{\rm aug}
, it holds that
\mu^{\nu,*}_{t,0}=\mu^{\nu,*}_{t+1,1}
for any
t\geq 0
. Next, assume
\mu^{\nu,*}_{t}=\Psi(\pi^{*})_{t}
holds true, then

\displaystyle\Psi(\pi)_{t+1}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{X}}p(\cdot\mid x,\pi^{a,*}_{t},\mu^{\nu,*}_{t})\mu^{\nu,*}_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\int_{\mathcal{X}}p(\cdot\mid x,a,\mu^{\nu}_{t+1,1})\mu^{\nu,*}_{t+1,1}(\mathrm{d}x)\pi^{a,*}_{t}(\mathrm{d}a)

\displaystyle=\mu^{\nu,*}_{t+1,0}(\cdot)=\mu^{\nu,*}_{t+1}(\cdot),

noting that the distribution of
\pi^{a,*}_{t}
is the same as the marginal of
\nu^{*}_{t+1}
on the variable
a_{t}
. Therefore the claim that
(\pi^{*},\bm{\mu}^{\nu,*})
is an MFNE for the underlying problem holds by induction.

Conversely, let
(\pi^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
be an MFNE for the underlying problem. With the joint distribution of all states and actions given, we can directly construct a measure flow
\bm{\nu}^{\mu,*}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
such that
\bm{\mu}^{\bm{\nu}^{\mu,*}}=\bm{\mu}^{*}
, so that
\pi^{*}\in\Phi^{\rm aug}(\bm{\nu}^{\mu,*})
. The condition that
\bm{\nu}^{\mu,*}=\Psi^{\rm aug}(\pi^{*})
is trivially satisfied.
∎

The existence of MFNE for discrete MFGs in the fully observable case is demonstrated in
[
47
]
, and further extended to the partial information case in
[
48
]
. For completeness sake, we include the result here, citing the relevant result.

Theorem 3.15
.

Under the assumptions of the MCDM in
2.1
,
2.2
, and
3.6
(a), there exists a mean-field Nash equilibrium for the mean-field game under the MCDM, in the sense of
3.12
(or
3.13
).

Proof.

By
3.7
, the augmented transition kernel
p_{y}
is weakly continuous. Together with the aforementioned assumptions, we can apply
[
48
, Theorem 3.1]
, which derives the existence of MFNE through an application of the Kakutani fixed point theorem to suitable correspondences.
∎

4
Regularised MFG-MCDM

It is well-known that in MFGs, the MFNE need not be unique, and that in general the fixed point operator given by
{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi^{\mathrm{aug}}}
is not contractive
[
19
]
. In order to compute an approximate MFNE, we mirror the approaches of
[
19
,
5
]
and consider an entropy regularised game. For
P,Q
in
\mathcal{P}(U)
, the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence of
P
from
Q
is defined to be

\displaystyle D_{KL}(P\|Q)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\int_{U}\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q}(u)\right)Q(\mathrm{d}u),&P\ll Q,\\
\infty,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q}
is the Radon–Nikodym derivative of
P
.

Assumption 4.1
.

Throughout the rest of the paper, we assume that any reference measure
q\in\mathcal{P}(U)
has full support on
U
.

With
\eta>0
and
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{\tilde{Y}})^{\infty}
, and
q\in\mathcal{P}(U)
acting as a reference measure, we consider the regularised objective function
J_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}:\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle J_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(\pi)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{q_{0}}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}\left(r_{y}\big(y_{t},u_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)-\eta D_{KL}(\pi_{t}\|q)\right)\right],

and the corresponding regularised optimisation problem with the value function
J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}:\mathbb{N}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y)\coloneqq\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{t,y}\left[\sum^{\infty}_{s=t}\gamma^{s-t}\left(r_{y}\big(y_{s},u_{s},\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{s}\big)-\eta D_{KL}(\pi_{s}\|q)\right)\right].

In this regularised game, optimal policies are of softmax form as described next.

Theorem 4.2
.

The value function
J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
is the unique solution to the Bellman equation

\displaystyle J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y)=\sup_{\hat{q}\in\mathcal{P}(U)}\int_{U}\left(r_{y}\big(y_{t},u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)-\eta\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{q}}{\mathrm{d}q}(u)+\gamma\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t+1,y^{\prime})p_{y}\big(\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)\right)\hat{q}(\mathrm{d}u),

(4.1)

and satisfies

\displaystyle J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y)=\eta\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}u)\right),

where the function
Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}:\mathbb{N}\times\mathcal{Y}\times U
, defined by

\displaystyle Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)\coloneqq r_{y}\big(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)+\gamma\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t+1,y^{\prime})p_{y}\big(\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big),

(4.2)

is bounded and measurable. Moreover, the softmax policy
\pi^{\rm soft}
, defined by

\displaystyle\pi^{\rm soft}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\coloneqq\frac{\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)/\eta\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,{u}^{\prime})/\eta\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})}q(\mathrm{d}u)

is the unique optimal policy that attains the supremum in (
4.1
).

Proof.

This is a specific case of the dynamic programming principle for a full information problem. We refer to
[
35
, Theorem B.1]
for a self-contained exposition of the idea of the proof for the stationary problem, the extension of which to the non-stationary case is straightforward.
∎

We next define the regularised MCDM-MFNE by an analogous fixed point criterion.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{t})_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
and
\eta>0
.

(i)

We define the map
\Phi_{\eta}:\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}\to\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}
to be the best-response map from
4.2
, given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\eta}(\bm{\nu})_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\coloneqq\frac{\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)/\eta\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)/\eta\right)q(\mathrm{d}u)}q(\mathrm{d}u),\quad t\geq 0.

(ii)

We define the map
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}:\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\to\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
to be the measure flow map, where
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{0}=\nu_{0}
and

\displaystyle\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t+1}(\cdot)=\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\int_{U}p_{y}\Big(\cdot\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t}(\mathrm{d}y),\quad t\geq 0.

(iii)

A regularised MFNE for the MCDM problem
(\pi^{*},\bm{\nu}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}\times\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
is defined by a fixed point
\bm{\nu}^{*}
of
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi_{\eta}
, for which
\pi^{*}=\Phi_{\eta}(\bm{\nu}^{*})
(best policy response to a given measure flow) and
\bm{\nu}^{*}=\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi^{*})
(measure flow induced by policy) holds.

Our next step is to show that the fixed point operator
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi_{\eta}
forms a contraction mapping under a suitable choice of metric and regularisation parameter
\eta
, such that the iteration of these maps converges towards the unique fixed point, which is the regularised MFNE. We combine the approaches of
[
19
,
5
]
, extending their proof to the case of a non-stationary infinite horizon problem on general Polish spaces with time-varying measure flows, as well as the inclusion of the map
\nu_{t}\mapsto\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}
in the definitions of
\Phi_{\eta}
and
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}
.

In order to demonstrate contraction of the regularised iterations, we show next a series of propositions regarding the Lipschitz continuity of the individual mappings. We consider a truncation at some finite time horizon
T
, defining the functions
J^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
by

\displaystyle J^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm DM}}\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{t,y}\left[\sum^{T}_{s=t}\gamma^{s-t}\left(r_{y}\big(y_{s},u_{s},\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{s}\big)-\eta D_{KL}(\pi_{s}\|q)\right)\right],&t\leq T,\\
0,&t>T.\end{cases}

Similarly, let us define the truncated
Q
-functions by

\displaystyle Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)\coloneqq\begin{cases}r_{y}\big(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)+\gamma\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}J^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t+1,y^{\prime})p_{y}\big(\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big),&t\leq T,\\
0,&t>T.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.4
.

For
0\leq t<T
, the truncated
Q
-functions satisfy

\displaystyle Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)

\displaystyle=r_{y}\big(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\eta\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t+1,{y}^{\prime},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)p_{y}(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}),

\displaystyle Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)

\displaystyle=r_{y}\big(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\eta\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T-1,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,{y}^{\prime},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)p_{y}(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}).

Proof.

This follows directly from
4.2
and bythe definition of
Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
.
∎

It is a standard result that the value function and
Q
-functions in finite horizon converges pointwise to their infinite horizon counterparts
[
32
, Section 4.2]
. In particular, we will use the fact that
J^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
and

Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
converges pointwise towards
J^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
and
Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
respectively in the analysis of the remainder of the section.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
\bm{\nu},\hat{\bm{\nu}}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
, and for all
(t,y,u)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathcal{Y}\times U
,
\lvert Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\nu}(t,y,u)\rvert\leq q^{*}
and
\lvert Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)\rvert\leq q^{*}
, where
q^{*}\coloneqq M_{R}/(1-\gamma)
.

Proof.

First, we have
Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,\cdot,\cdot)=0
for
t>T
, and

\displaystyle\lvert Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(T,y,u)\rvert=\lvert r_{y}\big(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{T}\big)\rvert\leq M_{R}\eqqcolon q_{T,T}.

Then, for each
t<T
,

\displaystyle\lvert Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq M_{R}+\gamma\eta\sup_{{y}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\left\lvert\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t+1,{y}^{\prime},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq M_{R}+\gamma\eta\left(\frac{q_{T,t+1}}{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=M_{R}+\gamma q_{T,t+1}\eqqcolon q_{T,t}

where
q_{T,t+1}
is the bound for
Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t+1,y,u)
. As
T\to\infty
,
q_{T,0}
converges to the fixed point
q^{*}
of the map
x\mapsto\gamma x+M_{R}
. Moreover, for all
T>0
and
0\leq t\leq T
,
q_{T,t}\leq q^{*}
. By pointwise convergence, the other bound for
Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}
follows by sending
T\to\infty
.
∎

Next, by induction we conclude the following statement:

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\bm{\nu},\hat{\bm{\nu}}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}
. Then
for each
T\geq 0
, the truncated
Q
-functions satisfies

\displaystyle\left\lvert Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)-Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,y,u)\right\rvert\leq l_{T,t}\ \delta_{TV}(\nu_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t}),\quad 0\leq t\leq T,\ y\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}},\ u\in U,\ \eta\geq 0,

(4.3)

where the doubly-indexed sequence
(l_{T,t})_{T,t}
, with
0\leq t\leq T
, is given by

\displaystyle l_{T,T}\coloneqq L_{R}L_{M}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad l_{T,t}\coloneqq\left(L_{R}L_{M}+\gamma\exp\left(\frac{2q^{*}}{\eta}\right)l_{T-1,t}+2\gamma q^{*}L_{P}L_{M}\right).

Proof.

For the base case
T=0
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert Q^{0,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(0,y,u)-Q^{0,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(0,y,u)\rvert=\lvert r_{y}\big(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{0}\big)-r_{y}\big(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{0}\big)\rvert\leq L_{R}L_{M}\delta_{TV}(\nu_{0},\hat{\nu}_{0})

Now assume the hypothesis holds for some

\displaystyle\left\lvert Q^{T+1,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)-Q^{T+1,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,y,u)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\left\lvert r_{y}(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t})-r_{y}(y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{t})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\gamma\eta\Bigg\lvert\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,{y}^{\prime},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)p_{y}(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,{y}^{\prime},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)p_{y}(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{t})\Bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle L_{R}\cdot\delta_{\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{t})

\displaystyle+\gamma\eta\sup_{{y}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\left\lvert\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,{y}^{\prime},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)-\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,{y}^{\prime},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\gamma\eta\ \delta_{TV}\left(p_{y}(\cdot\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t}),\ p_{y}(\cdot\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{t})\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left\lvert\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\hat{\nu}}}(t,y_{\mathrm{max}},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)-\log\left(\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\hat{\nu}}}(t,y_{\mathrm{min}},{u}^{\prime})}{\eta}\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\

\displaystyle L_{R}\cdot\delta_{\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{t})+\gamma\eta(I_{1}+I_{2})

where we used the fact that
\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}
is compact and
1.2
to obtain the the last term in the last inequality for some

. Then, by
Lemma

4.5
that
\lVert Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}\rVert_{\infty}\leq q^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\frac{2}{\eta}q^{*}L_{P}\ \delta_{\mathrm{max}}(\bm{\mu}^{\nu}_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\hat{\nu}}_{t})\leq\frac{2}{\eta}q^{*}L_{P}L_{M}\delta_{TV}(\nu_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t}).

To bound
I_{1}
, we use
1.4
as follows. Let
C(U)
be the Banach space of continuous functions from the compact set
U
to
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the sup norm. Let

be the functional defined by

L(f)=\log\left(\int_{U}\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)\right)
. It is easy to verify that

is Fréchet differentiable, and for

the Fréchet derivative

is given by

\displaystyle dL_{f}(h)=\frac{\int_{U}h(u)\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)}{\int_{U}\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)},\quad h\in C(U).

By
1.4
, there exists
t^{*}\in(0,1)
such that for
f^{*}(\cdot)=(t^{*}Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,{y}^{\prime},\cdot)-(1-t^{*})Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,{y}^{\prime},\cdot))/\eta
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{{y}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\lvert dL_{f^{*}}\{(Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,{y}^{\prime},\cdot)-Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,{y}^{\prime},\cdot))/\eta\}\rvert

\displaystyle=\sup_{{y}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\left\lvert\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{\int_{U}(Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,{y}^{\prime},u)-Q^{T,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,{y}^{\prime},u))\exp(f^{*}(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)}{\int_{U}\exp(f^{*}(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)}\right\rvert

where the last line follows from the induction assumption and the fact that
\lVert f^{*}\rVert_{\infty}\leq q^{*}/\eta
. Combining all the above, we complete the induction step by observing that

\displaystyle\left\lvert Q^{T+1,*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)-Q^{T+1,*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,y,u)\right\rvert\leq\left(L_{R}L_{M}+\gamma\exp\left(\frac{2q^{*}}{\eta}\right)l_{T,t}+2\gamma q^{*}L_{P}L_{M}\right)\delta_{TV}(\nu_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t}).

∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Consider the metric
\delta_{Q}
given by

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}},Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\\
u\in U\end{subarray}}\left\lvert Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)-Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,y,u)\right\rvert.

Let
l_{\eta}\coloneqq(L_{M}(L_{R}+2\gamma q^{*}L_{P}))/(1-\gamma\exp(\frac{2q^{*}}{\eta}))
. Then for any
\eta>\frac{2M_{R}}{-(1-\gamma)\log\gamma}
, we have that

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}},Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}})\leq l_{\eta}\delta_{\infty}(\bm{\nu},\hat{\bm{\nu}}).

Proof.

Since
\frac{2M_{R}}{-(1-\gamma)\log\gamma}=-\frac{2q^{*}}{\log\gamma}
,
\eta>\frac{2M_{R}}{-(1-\gamma)\log\gamma}
implies that
\gamma\exp\left(\frac{2q^{*}}{\eta}\right)<1
. Now fix
t>0
in (
4.3
) and send

. The sequence
l_{T,t}
converges towards to the unique fixed point of the map

\displaystyle x\mapsto L_{R}L_{M}+\gamma\exp\left(\frac{2q^{*}}{\eta}\right)x+2\gamma q^{*}L_{P}L_{M},

which is given by
l_{\eta}
, so that for each

,

\displaystyle\left\lvert Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)-Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,y,u)\right\rvert\leq l_{\eta}\ \delta_{TV}(\nu_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t}).

Since

does not depend on

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}},Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}})

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\\
u\in U\end{subarray}}\left\lvert Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)-Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,y,u)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}l_{\eta}\ \delta_{TV}(\nu_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t})

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

On the Banach space
C(U)
(equipped with the sup norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
), the map
I:C(U)\to C(U)
, given by

\displaystyle I(f)(\cdot)\coloneqq\frac{\exp(f(\cdot))}{\int_{U}\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)},

is Fréchet differentiable. At each
f\in C(U)
, its Frèchet derivative
dI_{f}
is given by

\displaystyle dI_{f}(h)(\cdot)=I(f)(\cdot)\left[h(\cdot)-\int_{U}h(u)I(f)(u)q(\mathrm{d}u)\right],\quad h\in C(U).

The Frèchet derivative
dI_{f}\in L(C(U),C(U))
is a bounded linear operator with operator norm
\lVert dI_{f}\rVert\leq 2\lVert I(f)\rVert_{\infty}
.

Proof.

We use the capital
O
for the Landau notation in the proof as follows. For functions
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
G:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that
g(x)\coloneqq O(G(x))
if there exists
M>0
such that
\lvert g(x)\rvert\leq M\lvert G(x)\rvert
as
x\to 0
. Now let
f\in C(U)
and let
Z\coloneqq\int_{U}\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)
. Let
h\in C(U)
be such that
\frac{1}{Z}\int_{U}h(u)\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)<1
. By Taylor’s approximation for
x\mapsto\exp(x)
at the point
h(u)
, we get

\displaystyle\left[\int_{U}\exp(f(u)+h(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{U}\exp(f(u))(1+h(u)+O(h(u)^{2}))q(\mathrm{d}u)\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\left[1+\frac{\int_{U}h(u)\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)}{Z}+O(\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}^{2})\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\left(1-\frac{\int_{U}h(u)\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)}{Z}+O(\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}^{2})\right),

using
\frac{1}{1+x}=1-x+O(x^{2})
for the last equality. Then we have

\displaystyle I(f+h)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(f(x))(1+h(x)+O(h(x)^{2}))}{Z}\left(1-\frac{\int_{U}h(u)\exp(f(u))q(\mathrm{d}u)}{Z}+O(\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=I(f)(x)+I(f)(x)h(x)-I(f)(x)\int_{U}h(u)I(f)(u)q(\mathrm{d}u)+O(\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}^{2}).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}\to 0}\frac{\lVert I(f+h)-I(f)-I(f)[h-\int_{U}h(u)I(f)(u)q(\mathrm{d}u)]\rVert}{\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}}=0,

to conclude
dI_{f}(h)(\cdot)=I(f)(\cdot)\left[h(\cdot)-\int_{U}h(u)I(f)(u)q(\mathrm{d}u)\right]
, as claimed. Now for any
g\in C(U)
and
u\in U
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert dI_{f}(g)(u)\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert I(f)(u)\left[g(u)-\int_{U}g({u}^{\prime})I(f)({u}^{\prime})q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})\right]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert I(f)(u)g(u)\rvert+\lvert I(f)(u)\rvert\int_{U}\lvert g({u}^{\prime})I(f)({u}^{\prime})\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert I(f)\rVert_{\infty}\lVert g\rVert_{\infty},

where the last inequality uses the fact that
\int_{U}I(f)(u)q(\mathrm{d}u)=1
, as the integrand is continuous on the compact set
U
, hence integrable. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert dI_{f}\rVert=\inf\big\{c\geq 0:\lVert dI_{f}(g)\rVert_{\infty}\leq c\lVert g\rVert_{\infty},\ g\in C(U)\big\}\leq 2\lVert I(f)\rVert_{\infty}.

∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\eta^{*}
be a constant with
\eta^{*}>\frac{2M_{R}}{-(1-\gamma)\log\gamma}
. Then for any
\eta\geq\eta^{*}
, the map

-Lipschitz continuous with

Proof.

Given any
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}

to the softmax policy

, given by

\displaystyle\pi^{\rm soft}_{\bm{\nu},t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\coloneqq\frac{\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,u)/\eta\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,y,{u}^{\prime})/\eta\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})}q(\mathrm{d}u),\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\Phi_{\eta}(\bm{\nu}),\Phi_{\eta}(\hat{\bm{\nu}}))=\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\sup_{y\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\delta_{TV}(\pi^{\rm soft}_{\bm{\nu},t}(\cdot\mid y),\pi^{\rm soft}_{\bm{\nu},t}(\cdot\mid y)),\quad\zeta>1.

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\Phi_{\eta}(\bm{\nu}),\Phi_{\eta}(\hat{\bm{\nu}}))

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\ \delta_{TV}\left(\pi^{\mathrm{soft}}_{\bm{\nu},t}(\cdot\mid\hat{y}),\ \pi^{\mathrm{soft}}_{\hat{\bm{\nu}},t}(\cdot\mid\hat{y})\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{U}\left\lvert\frac{\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,\hat{y},u)/\eta\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,\hat{y},{u}^{\prime})/\eta\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})}-\frac{\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,\hat{y},u)/\eta\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,\hat{y},{u}^{\prime})/\eta\right)q(\mathrm{d}{u}^{\prime})}\right\rvert q(\mathrm{d}u).

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\frac{\lVert I(f)-I(\hat{f})\rVert_{\infty}}{2}

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\frac{1}{\eta}\sup_{u\in U}\lvert tQ^{*}_{\eta,\bm{\nu}}(t,\hat{y},u)+(1-t)Q^{*}_{\eta,\hat{\bm{\nu}}}(t,\hat{y},u)\rvert\lVert f-\hat{f}\rVert_{\infty}

∎

Next, we show that the measure flow map
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}
from

in the metrics

and

) and (

Proposition 4.10
.

For
\zeta\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\zeta>2L_{P}L_{M}+2
in the metric from
\delta_{\infty}
(

\displaystyle\delta_{\infty}(\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi),\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi}))\leq L_{\Psi}\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi,\hat{\pi}),

where

\displaystyle L_{\Psi}\coloneqq\frac{2L_{P}}{2L_{P}L_{M}+1}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\zeta-2L_{P}L_{M}-2}+\frac{1}{\zeta-1}\right).

Proof.

We show inductively that

\displaystyle\delta_{TV}(\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t},\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})_{t})\leq S_{t}\ \delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi,\hat{\pi}),\quad t\geq 0,

(4.4)

for constants
S_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
S_{t+1}=2L_{P}+2S_{t}(L_{P}L_{M}+1)
,
S_{0}=0
. For
t=0
, we have from
4.3
,

\displaystyle\delta_{TV}(\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{0},\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})_{0})=\delta_{TV}(\nu_{0},\nu_{0})=0.

Assume the hypothesis (
4.4
) holds true for some
t>0
. By
Lemma

3.2
one can write the total variation distance between
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t}
and
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})_{t}
with respect to some dominating measure
q
. We write
\psi(\pi)_{t}
and
\psi(\hat{\pi})_{t}
for their respective densities. This leads to

\displaystyle\delta_{TV}\left(\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t+1},\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})_{t+1}\right)=\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\Bigg\lvert\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\int_{U}\bigg(p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\psi(\pi)_{t}(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\Big)\hat{\pi}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\psi(\hat{\pi})_{t}(y)\bigg)\Bigg\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(J_{1}+J_{2}),

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\left\lvert\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\int_{U}\left(p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)-p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\Big)\hat{\pi}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\right)\psi(\pi)_{t}(y)\ \right\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\times\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\int_{U}\left\lvert p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)-p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\Big)\hat{\pi}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\right\rvert\psi(\pi)_{t}(y)q(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime}),

and

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\times\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\int_{U}p_{y}\left({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\right)\hat{\pi}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\left\lvert\psi_{t}(\pi)(y)-\psi(\hat{\pi})_{t}(y)\right\rvert q(\mathrm{d}{y}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq 2\ \delta_{TV}(\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t},\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})_{t}).

The integral over
u\in U
in

can be simplified to

\displaystyle\int_{U}\left\lvert p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)-p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\Big)\hat{\pi}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\int_{U}p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\lvert\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)-\hat{\pi}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)\rvert

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{U}\left\lvert p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)-p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\Big)\right\rvert\hat{\pi}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u\mid y)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\bigg(p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u_{\mathrm{max}},\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)-p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u_{\mathrm{min}},\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)\bigg)\delta_{TV}(\pi_{t}(\cdot\mid y),\hat{\pi}_{t}(\cdot\mid y))

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{u\in U}\left\lvert p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big)-p_{y}\Big({y}^{\prime}\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\Big)\right\rvert,

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2L_{P}\ \delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi,\hat{\pi})+2\sup_{y\in\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}\sup_{u\in U}\delta_{TV}\left(p_{y}\Big(\cdot\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}\Big),p_{y}\Big(\cdot\mid y,u,\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle S_{t}=\frac{2L_{P}}{2L_{P}L_{M}+1}(2L_{P}L_{M}+2)^{t}-\frac{2L_{P}}{2L_{P}L_{M}+1}.

\displaystyle\delta_{\infty}(\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi),\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi}))

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\zeta^{-t}\delta_{TV}(\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)_{t},\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\hat{\pi})_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{t=1}\frac{S_{t}}{\zeta^{t}}\ \delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi,\hat{\pi})

\displaystyle=\frac{2L_{P}}{2L_{P}L_{M}+1}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\zeta-2L_{P}L_{M}-2}+\frac{1}{\zeta-1}\right)\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi,\hat{\pi}).

∎

The next statement, showing the regularised fixed point operator of the MFG-MCDM to be a contraction map, now is essentially a corollary of the previous propositions.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\zeta
and
\eta^{*}
be constants such that
\zeta>2L_{P}L_{M}+2

\eta^{*}>\frac{2M_{R}}{-(1-\gamma)\log\gamma}

\displaystyle q^{*}=\frac{M_{R}}{1-\gamma},\quad l_{\eta^{*}}=\frac{L_{M}(L_{R}+2\gamma q^{*}L_{P})}{1-\gamma\exp\left(\frac{2q^{*}}{\eta^{*}}\right)},\quad L_{\Psi}=\frac{2L_{P}}{2L_{P}L_{M}+1}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\zeta-2L_{P}L_{M}-2}+\frac{1}{\zeta-1}\right).

Then, for any
\eta

\displaystyle\eta>\sqrt{2q^{*}l_{\eta^{*}}L_{\Psi}},

the fixed point operator
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi_{\eta}
is a contraction mapping on the space
(\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty},\delta_{\infty})
, where the constant
\zeta
in the metric
\delta_{\infty}
in (
1.2
) is as chosen above. In particular, there exists a unique fixed point for
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi_{\eta}

Proof.

Let
\bm{\nu},\hat{\bm{\nu}}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}})^{\infty}

where we recall from

∎

Remark 4.12
.

Mean field games with (relative entropy) regularisation are relevant both as approximations to non-regularised MFGs (see
[
19
]
) and also as MFGs of research interest in their own right (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
).
Introducing regularisation terms into an MFG, as a means towards approximation, clearly changes the MFNE. Following
[
19
]
,
5.3
in Section
5
is going to state conditions under which MFNE in the regularised game, with regularisation
\eta
being sufficiently small, also approximate MFNE in the non-regularised game with finitely many players, for the number of players being large enough. Following

[
19
, Sect.4]
,
we note that
4.11
(respectively,
5.3
)
requires
\eta
to be sufficiently large (respectively, small). Despite a gap left between such assumptions in theory, it was possible in our computational experiments to find
\eta>0
small such as to observe both convergence of iterates as well as a good approximation to the MFNE of the non-regularised MFG.

In regards to reinforcement learning approaches, entropy regularisation offers beneficial exploration and exploitation properties, see
[
19
,
5
]
.
In our regularised MFG, for regularisation parameter
\eta
being large enough, there exists a unique MFNE, which is determined by the fixed point contraction in
4.11
.
Although MFNE exist for any
\eta>0
, as shown in
3.15
, they do not have to be unique for weakly (or even un-)regularized MFGs. In such circumstances, a Banach fixed point contraction is clearly impossible. Thus, to calculate non-unique and (all) multiple MFNE becomes a question of interest, which appears to be less studied in the literature; but note recent progress by
[
28
]
.

Remark 5.4
.

The above
\varepsilon
-optimal policies for the finite player game, constructed from mean-field equilibria, only depend on the agents’ observations of their own augmented state variables and on time, but not on their respective empirical distributions amongst all agents. This is due to the fact that the empirical distribution of the population in the augmented state variables is approximated by the time-varying but deterministic measure flow from the fixed point characterisation of the mean-field game equilibrium. Intuitively, the deterministic nature of this flow in the large population limit corresponds to a law of large numbers being applied to empirical measures (by propagation of chaos).

We want to emphasize, moreover, that the deterministic measure flow in the augmented state space also reveals the proportions in the large population, which are attained endogenously in mean-field equilibrium with respect to the chosen information access (i.e. actively controlled observation delay), and moreover the respective distributions of actions taken during the delay period by the respective subpopulations. This is illustrated in the computational example in
Section

7
. Analogous comments apply also to the approximate
\varepsilon
-Nash equilibria as described above.

The simple nature of the model allows a straightforward interpretation of the MFNE, illustrating the effects of costly information acquisition on population behaviour. Other epidemiological models include the SIR (susceptible-infected-recovered) variant, as well as models involving demographic and geographical heterogeneity, such as

In our numerical experiments, we allow the dynamics to depend on the joint distribution of states and actions, which is crucial in capturing the basic effects from infection and distancing.

\displaystyle p(S\mid I,U)

\displaystyle=p(S\mid I,D)=p_{\mathrm{rec}},

\displaystyle r(S,D)=-0.5,

\displaystyle r(I,U)=-1.5,\quad

\displaystyle r(I,D)=-1.0.

For the computation of MFNEs for our model, we utilise the
mfglib
Python package
[
26
]
. We incorporate our own script for the mapping
\bm{\nu}\mapsto\bm{\mu}^{\nu}
so that the existing library can be adapted for the MFG-MCDM, and in particular for the computation on the augmented space. We first initialise with a uniform policy as the reference measure
q
, and repeatedly apply the mapping
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}\circ\Phi_{\eta}
for a range of values of the regularisation parameter
\eta
. As a benchmark to test for the convergence towards a regularised MFNE, we utilise the exploitability score, which, for a policy
\pi
, is defined by

\displaystyle\mbox{Expl}(\pi)\coloneqq\max_{\tilde{\pi}}J_{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\tilde{\pi})}(\tilde{\pi})-J_{\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}(\pi)}(\pi).

The exploitability score measures the suboptimality gap for a policy
\pi
when computed with the measure flow induced by the map
\Psi^{\mathrm{aug}}
. An exploitability score is 0 if and only if
\pi
is an MFNE for the MFG, and a score of
\varepsilon
indicates that
\pi
is an
\varepsilon
-MFNE. We refer to the literature such as
[
29
,
43
,
38
]
for a more detailed discussion. As the exploitability score depends on the rewards and the initial policy, we consider instead the relative exploitability scaled by the initial value.

require a larger number of iterations for convergence. For
\eta

\eta
guarantees convergence of the iteration,
this may simultaneously lead to a MFNE of the regularized MFG which may approximate the non-regularized MFG problem poorly. Moreover, searching for a suitable value of
\eta
is computationally expensive.

To mitigate the above issues, we utilise the prior descent algorithm
[
19
]
. Here, the reference measure is dynamically updated, by using the policy obtained from the previous iteration to determine the reference measure for the next iteration. The reference measure can also be updated after a number of iterations instead, creating a double loop for the algorithm. We summarise the prior descent algorithm for the MFG with control of information speed in
Algorithm

1
. The relative exploitability score is plotted in the bottom row of

7.1
. We see that prior descent vastly outperforms the case of using a fixed prior. In
[
19
]
, the prior descent algorithm is further improved by using the heuristic
\eta_{j+1}=\eta_{j}\cdot c
for some constant
c>1
, gradually increasing the regularisation to aid convergence. We also applied this heuristic for our problem, but for our case we do not see significant differences compared to initialising with large fixed values of
\eta

Remark 7.1
.

Given the above example on medical testing, it appears that choices about more speedy but costly test results, for example as required for air travel during the COVID-19 pandemic, are often to be decided upon not at the time when the test result is to be
received
, but at the (earlier) time when the test sample is being
taken
. Although the MFG in the present paper is formulated for the former variant, let us explain briefly how our analysis indeed provides the correct solution also for the latter.

The former variant, though possibly artificial for applications, is more easily formulated as a dynamic programming problem. Conversely,
a decision problem with pre-commitment on the acquisition of costly information with adjustable delay periods, appears to be a more complicated formulation. To see that the optimal MFNE policy from our general framework formulated for the former variant provides simultaneously the solution for the latter variant, it suffices to note that the optimal policy at an MFNE, according to our augmented state space setting whose variables are showing in
2.5
, is based solely on the latest state
x
that has been observed, and the string of actions
(a)_{-d}^{-1}
taken by the optimal policy since then, which all have been made subsequently solely based on
x
. That means that the representative agent already knows at the time of the medical test, the future date at which they will receive the test results. In other words, the strategy can be interpreted more naturally as a decision about information acquisition being made actually at the timing of the medical test. Likewise reasoning applies beyond the concrete example of the present section.

In regards to the acquisition costs
c_{0},\ldots,c_{K}
for timely information access, it would clearly be possible to modify the setup for payment dates of information access to be at the earlier (e.g. testing) date instead of at the later date (test results received). This would simply require a suitable
discount such that the time-adjusted values of respective payments coincide, that means taking
c_{i}\gamma^{i}
if the payment is to be done at the earlier time of medical testing.

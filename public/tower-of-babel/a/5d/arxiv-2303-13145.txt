Normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of hypergraphs
Leyou Xu, Bo Zhou

Abstract
In this paper, we give tight bounds for the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of hypergraphs that are not necessarily uniform, and provide an edge version interlacing theorem, a Cheeger inequality, and a discrepancy inequality that are related to the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues for uniform hypergraphs. Keywords: hypergraph, normalized Laplacian eigenvalue, interlacing inequality, Cheeger inequality, discrepancy inequality Mathematics Subject Classifcation: 05C65, 05C69, 05C50

Introduction

Let
H
be a hypergraph with vertex set
V(H)
and edge set
E(H)
, where every edge is a subset of
V(H)
containing at least two vertices.
If each edge consists of
k
vertices, we say
H
is
k
-uniform.
For
v\in V(H)
, denote by
E_{H}(v)
(
E_{v}
for convenience) the set of edges of
H
containing
v
, i.e.,
E_{H}(v)=\{e\in E(H):v\in e\}
.
Let
d_{H}(v)=|E_{H}(v)|
(
d_{v}
for convenience) be the degree of
v
in
H
.
Let
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)
. If there exists an edge containing both
u
and
v
, then we say
u
and
v
are adjacent and otherwise we say
u
and
v
are non-adjacent.
Denote by
E_{H}(u,v)
(
E_{uv}
for convenience) the set of edges containing both
u
and
v
in
H
, i.e.,
E_{H}(u,v)=\{e\in E(H):\{u,v\}\subseteq e\}
.
Let
e_{H}(u,v)=|E_{H}(u,v)|
(
e_{uv}
for convenience) be the co-degree of
u
and
v
in
H
.

For any nonempty proper vertex subset
S
of a hypergraph
H
, the volume of
S
is defined as

\mathrm{vol}(S)=\sum_{v\in S}d_{H}(v),

and the edge boundary
\partial S
of
S
is defined as the multiset of edges of
H
with at least one vertex in
S
and at least one vertex outside
S
, where the multiplicity of an edge
e\in\partial S
is

|e\cap S||e\cap(V(H)\setminus S)|
. It is easy to see that
|\partial S|=\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus S}e_{H}(u,v)
.
The Cheeger constant (isoperimetric number)
h(H)
of a hypergraph
H
is defined as
[
17
]

h(H)=\min\left\{\frac{|\partial S|}{\mathrm{vol}(S)}:\emptyset\neq S\subset V(H),\mathrm{vol}(S)\leq\mathrm{vol}(V(H)\setminus S)\right\}.

For a hypergraph
H
, Banerjee
[
2
]
proposed a new version adjacency matrix
A(H)=(a_{uv})_{u,v\in V(H)}
of
H
(with a fixed ordering of the vertices), where

a_{uv}=\begin{cases}\sum_{e\in E_{uv}}\frac{1}{|e|-1},&\mbox{if $u$ and $v$ are adjacent},\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We mention that another closely related version of adjacency matrix of
H
was proposed in
[
12
]
as the
n\times n
matrix
B(H)=(b_{uv})_{u,v\in V(H)}
where

\displaystyle b_{uv}=\begin{cases}e_{uv},&\mbox{if $u\neq v$, and $u,v\in V(H)$},\\[2.84526pt]
0,&\mbox{if $u=v\in V(H)$}.\end{cases}

If
H
is
k
-uniform, then
B(H)=(k-1)A(H)
. So there is no essential difference between the spectral properties based on the two versions of adjacency matrix of a
k
-uniform hypergraph.
Note that for any
u\in V(H)
,

\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}a_{uv}=\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}\sum_{e\in E_{uv}}\frac{1}{|e|-1}=\sum_{e\in E_{u}}\sum_{v\in e\setminus\{u\}}\frac{1}{|e|-1}=\sum_{e\in E_{u}}1=d_{u}.

Let
D(H)=\mbox{diag}\{d_{H}(u):u\in V(H)\}
be the degree diagonal matrix of
H
(with the same fixed ordering of the vertices as above).
The Laplacian matrix of
H
is the matrix
L(H)=D(H)-A(H)
, see
[
2
,
19
]
.
The normalized Laplacian matrix of
H
is defined as

\mathcal{L}(H)=D(H)^{-1/2}L(H)D(H)^{-1/2}

with the convention that the
(u,u)
-entry of
D(H)^{-1}
is equal to
0
if
d_{H}(u)=0
.
Clearly, this is a generalization of the normalized Laplacian matrix of a ordinary graph.
Note that the matrix
\mathcal{L}(H)
is symmetric. So it has
|V(H)|
real eigenvalues.
Denote by
\lambda_{1}(H)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{|V(H)|}(H)
the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}(H)
, which we call the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of
H
.

The normalized normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of ordinary graphs have been extensively, see
[
10
]
.

An edge version of Cauchy’s interlacing theorem on the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues for an ordinary graph has been given in
[
6
]
. More work in this direction may be found in
[
5
,
14
]
.

Cheeger inequality is the fundamental result in spectral graph theory, which relates
the eigenvalues of an associated matrix with combinatorial property of a graph via
the Cheeger constant.
As the discrete analogues of the continuous counterpart, there are
such inequalities for different versions of the Laplacian matrix (operator)
and various isoperimetric variations of the Cheeger constant
[
1
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
16
,
19
]
.
For a hypergraph
H
with
\emptyset\neq S\subset V(H)
,
E(S,V(H)\setminus S)
denotes the set of edges of
H
with at least one vertex in
S
and at least one vertex outside
S
.
By defining another version of the Cheeger constant
as

\min\left\{\frac{|E(S,V(H)\setminus S)|}{|S|}:\emptyset\neq S\subset V(H),|S|\leq|V(H)\setminus S|\right\}
,
Banerjee
[
2
]
established the (Cheeger) inequality relating the second smallest Laplacian eigenvalue with this Cheeger constant. Mulas
[
17
]
gave a Cheeger inequality via a different Laplacian matrix for hypergraphs. Work in this line was studied for oriented hypergraphs by Mulas and Zhang
[
18
]
.

In the literatures, there are different ways to measure the discrepancy of a graph or hypergraph
[
3
,
9
]
. Chung
[
9
]
considered the volume of each vertex subset, and take the ‘expectation’ to be proportional to the product of the volumes. In this way, the discrepancy can be bounded using the spectral gap of the normalized Laplacian matrix of a graph. In
[
8
,
11
]
,
the discrepancy inequality bounds the edge distribution between two sets based on the spectral gap.
This was also extended to singular values of the adjacency matrix in
[
4
]
for variations of discrepancy.

In this paper, we address the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of hypergraphs.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. The preliminaries are provided in Section
2
.
In Section
3
, we establish tight bounds for the largest and the second smallest normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of hypergraphs that are not necessarily uniform.
In Section
4
, we prove an edge version of Cauchy’s interlacing theorem on the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of
k
-uniform hypergraphs.
In Section
5
, we present the Cheeger inequality that relates the second smallest normalized Laplacian eigenvalue and the Cheeger constant for
k
-uniform hypergraphs.
In Section
6
, we gave a discrepancy inequality for
k
-uniform hypergraphs that bounds the edge distribution between two sets based on the normalized Laplacian spectral gap.
The new results exhibit some significant differences between the normalized Laplacian eihenvalues of graphs and hypergraphs.

2
Preliminaries

If a hypergraph
H
contains isolated vertices, then the row and column of
\mathcal{L}(H)
corresponding to an isolated vertex in
H
are both zero.
Let
H^{\prime}
be the hypergraph obtained from
H
by deleting all isolated vertices.
Then each eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}(H^{\prime})
is also an eigenvalue of
L(H)
, and
0
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}(H)
with multiplicity
s
more than that of
\mathcal{L}(H^{\prime})
, where
s
is the number of isolated vertices in
H
.
So we only consider hypergraphs without isolated vertices in this paper unless otherwise stated.
Let
\mathbf{x}
be a nonzero
n
-dimensional column vector indexed by the vertices of a hypergraph
H
on
n
vertices. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\top}L(H)\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V(H)}x_{u}\left(d_{u}x_{u}-\sum_{v\neq u}\sum_{e\in E_{uv}}\frac{1}{|e|-1}x_{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V(H)}x_{u}\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}\sum_{e\in E_{uv}}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\left(x_{u}-x_{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{u\in e}x_{u}\sum_{v\in e\setminus\{u\}}(x_{u}-x_{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}\left(x_{u}-x_{v}\right)^{2},

so

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}\left(\frac{x_{u}}{\sqrt{d_{u}}}-\frac{x_{v}}{\sqrt{d_{v}}}\right)^{2}.

For vertices
u
and
v
of a hypergraph
H
, a path from
u
to
v
in
H
is an
alternating sequence of distinct vertices and edges of the form
v_{1},e_{1},v_{2},e_{2},\dots,v_{k}

such that
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subseteq e_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
, where
v_{1}=u
and
v_{k}=v
.
If there is a path from
u
to
v
for any
u,v\in V(H)
, then we say that
H
is connected.

Denote by
\mathbf{1}_{n}
and
\mathbf{0}_{n}
the
n
-dimensional all ones column vector and the zero vector of order
n
, respectively.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
H
is a hypergraph on
n
vertices, then
\mathcal{L}(H)
is semi-definite,
\lambda_{n}(H)=0
with a corresponding eigenvector
D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{n}
, and the multiplicity
of
\lambda_{n}(H)=0
is exactly the number of components of
H
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be any nonzero
n
-dimensional column vector indexed by the vertices of
H
.
Then

\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}\left(\frac{x_{u}}{\sqrt{d_{u}}}-\frac{x_{v}}{\sqrt{d_{v}}}\right)^{2}\geq 0.

So
\mathcal{L}(H)
is semi-definite.

Note that
\mathcal{L}(H)D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{n}=D(H)^{-1/2}L(H)\mathbf{1}_{n}=D(H)^{-1/2}\mathbf{0}_{n}=\mathbf{0}_{n}
.
So
\lambda_{n}(H)=0
with a corresponding eigenvector
D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{n}
.

Furthermore, suppose that
\mathbf{x}
is an eigenvector associated with
\lambda_{n}(H)=0
. That is,

\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}_{n}
, equivalently,
\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}=0
as
\mathcal{L}(H)
is semi-definite positive. Let
\mathbf{y}=D(H)^{-1/2}\mathbf{x}
. Then

y_{u}=y_{v}
for two adjacent vertices
u
and
v
of
H
.
Any two different vertices
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
in a component of
H
are connected by a path, so
y_{u^{\prime}}=y_{v^{\prime}}
.
Denote by
c
the number of components of
H
. Then the system
\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}
has exactly
c
basic solutions, one for each component of
H
.
This shows that the multiplicity of
\lambda_{n}(H)=0
is exactly
c
.
∎

Given an
n
-vertex hypergraph
H
, as the trace of
\mathcal{L}(H)
is the number of non-isolated vertices in
H
and
H
contains no isolated vertices, i.e.,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(H)=n
. As
\lambda_{n}(H)=0
, one has
\lambda_{n-1}(H)\leq\frac{n}{n-1}\leq\lambda_{1}(H)
.

Let
H
be a hypergraph of order
n
.
Let
\mathbf{y}=D(H)^{-1/2}\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}
. Then

\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}=\frac{\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}}.

(1)

We denote by
R_{H}(\mathbf{y})
the expression in the right hand side of (
1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex
k
-uniform hypergraph.
Then

\lambda_{1}(H)=\frac{1}{k-1}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}}\frac{\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}}

and

\lambda_{n-1}(H)=\frac{1}{k-1}\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{y}\bot D(H)\mathbf{1}_{n}}\frac{\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{y}=D(H)^{-1/2}\mathbf{x}
for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}
. By the Rayleigh-Ritz theorem (see Theorem 4.2.2 in
[
14
]
),

\lambda_{1}(H)=\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}}R_{H}(\mathbf{y}).

By Theorem
2.1
,
0
is the smallest normalized Laplacian eigenvalue of
H

with a corresponding eigenvector
D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{n}
. Then
\mathbf{x}\bot D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{n}
if and only if
\mathbf{y}\bot D(H)\mathbf{1}_{n}
.
By the Courant-Fischer theorem (see Theorem 4.2.11 in
[
14
]
),

\lambda_{n-1}(H)=\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{x}\bot D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{n}}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{y}\bot D(H)\mathbf{1}_{n}}R_{H}(\mathbf{y}).

As
H
is
k
-uniform, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(k-1)}\sum_{e\in E(H)}\sum_{u\in e}\sum_{v\in e\setminus\{u\}}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(k-1)}\sum_{u\in V(H)}\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}\sum_{e\in E_{uv}}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2},

so the result follows.
∎

3
Bounds for the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues

To warm up, we give bounds from the largest and the second smallest normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of hypergraphs, extending known results from graphs to hypergraphs (see, e.g.
[
10
]
).

For a hypergraph
H
and any subset
X\subset V(H)
, let
\overline{X}=V(H)\setminus X
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
H
be
k
-uniform hypergraph with
m
edges. For any subset
\emptyset\neq X\subset V(H)
,

\lambda_{1}(H)\geq\frac{|\partial X|km}{(k-1)\mathrm{vol}(X)(km-\mathrm{vol}(X))}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be an
n
-dimensional vector defined on
V(H)
with

x_{u}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{vol}(X),&\mbox{if }u\notin X,\\
-\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X}),&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(H)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{u\in X}\sum_{v\in\overline{X}}e_{uv}(\mathrm{vol}(X)+\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X}))^{2}}{(k-1)\left(\sum_{u\in X}d_{u}\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})^{2}+\sum_{u\in\overline{X}}d_{u}\mathrm{vol}(X)^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\partial X|(\mathrm{vol}(X)+\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X}))}{(k-1)\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})}.

As
H
is
k
-uniform, we have
\mathrm{vol}(X)+\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})=km
, so the result follows.
∎

In the case
k=2
, the result above may be found in
[
15
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
H
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph on with
m
edges and maximum degree
\Delta
.
Then

\lambda_{1}(H)\geq\frac{km}{km-\Delta}
.

Proof.

Let
u
be a vertex of
H
with maximum degree and
X=\{u\}
. Then the result follows from Theorem
3.1
by noting that
|\partial X|=(k-1)\Delta
and
\mathrm{vol}(X)=\Delta
.
∎

For a hypergraph
H
, an independent set of
H
is a subset of
V(H)
, in which any pair of vertices is non-adjacent.
The independence number of
H
, written as
\alpha(H)
, is the maximum cardinality of all independent sets in
H
.
So, if
X
is an independent set of a
k
-uniform hypergrph

, then
|\partial X|=(k-1)\mathrm{vol}(X)
and hence the following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
H
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph with
m
edges.
For any independent set
X
of
H
,
\lambda_{1}(H)\geq\frac{km}{km-\mathrm{vol}(X)}
. Moreover, if the minimum degree of
H
is
\delta
, then
\lambda_{1}(H)\geq\frac{km}{km-\alpha\delta}
, where
\alpha
is the independence number of
H
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For a hypergraph
H
on
n
vertices,
\lambda_{1}(H)\leq 2
with equality if and only if
H
has
a
2
-uniform bipartite component.
Moreover, if
H
is
k
-uniform, then
\lambda_{1}(H)\leq\frac{k}{k-1}
and it is attained if
|E(H)|<n
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be an eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(H)
and
\mathbf{y}=D(H)^{-1/2}\mathbf{x}
.
Then

\lambda_{1}(H)=\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}=\frac{\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}}.

Note that for each edge
e\in E(H)
,

\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}^{2}+y_{v}^{2}-2y_{u}y_{v}).

For
e\in E(H)
, let
y_{e}=\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}^{2}+y_{v}^{2}-2y_{u}y_{v})

\displaystyle=(|e|-1)\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}-2\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}y_{u}y_{v}

\displaystyle=(|e|-1)\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}-\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}\sum_{v\in e,v\neq u}y_{v}

\displaystyle=(|e|-1)\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}-\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}(y_{e}-y_{u})

\displaystyle=|e|\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}-y_{e}\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}

\displaystyle=|e|\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}-y_{e}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|e|\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2},

so

\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}\leq\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{|e|}{|e|-1}\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}.

(2)

As
|e|\geq 2
for each edge
e\in E(H)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{|e|}{|e|-1}\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{e\in E(H)}\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{u\in V(H)}\sum_{e\in E_{u}}y_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2},

so
\lambda_{1}(H)\leq 2
.

Suppose that
\lambda_{1}(H)=2
. Then by the above argument, we have

\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}=0

(3)

\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}\neq 0\Rightarrow|e|=2

for any

From (

for any vertex

has an edge containing at least three vertices, then

for any vertex of this component. Note that
\mathbf{y}
is nonzero. So there is at least a component
F
that is
a

Conversely, suppose that one component

\lambda_{1}(F)=2
. By the above argument,
\lambda_{1}(H)\leq 2
. Evidently,

\lambda_{1}(H)=2

\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}\leq\frac{k}{k-1}\sum_{e\in E(H)}\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}^{2}=\frac{k}{k-1}\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}.

Therefore,
\lambda_{1}(H)\leq\frac{k}{k-1}
with equality if and only if there is a nonzero vector

\mathbf{y}

such that
\sum_{u\in e}y_{u}=0

the homogeneous system of

in

.

It was shown in
[
10

that for any non-complete graph

\lambda_{n-1}(G)\leq 1
.
The case for hypergraphs is somewhat different.
For example, let
R_{4,3}
be a
4

3
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
V(R_{4,3})=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
and edge set
E(R_{4,3})=\{\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\},\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{4}\},\{v_{1},v_{3},v_{4}\}\}
. This is not the complete

4

3

.
It is easy to see that

\mathcal{L}(R_{4,3})=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}&1&-\frac{1}{4}&-\frac{1}{4}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}&-\frac{1}{4}&1&-\frac{1}{4}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}&-\frac{1}{4}&-\frac{1}{4}&1\end{pmatrix}.

By a direct calculation,

\lambda_{3}(R_{4,3})=\frac{5}{4}>1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For an
n
-vertex hypergraph
H
with at least two non-adjacent vertices,
\lambda_{n-1}(H)\leq 1
.

Proof.

Denote by
z
and
w
the two non-adjacent vertices in
H
.
Let
\mathbf{x}
be the vector with

x_{u}=\begin{cases}d_{z},&\mbox{if }u=w,\\
-d_{w},&\mbox{if }u=z,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

As

\mathbf{x}^{\top}D(H)\mathbf{1}_{n}=\sum_{u\in V(H)}x_{u}d_{u}=d_{w}d_{z}-d_{z}d_{w}=0,

we have by Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}(H)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{x}\bot D(H)\mathbf{1}_{n}}R_{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,w\}\subseteq e}d_{z}^{2}+\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,z\}\subseteq e}d_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle=d_{z}^{2}\sum_{e\in E_{w}}\sum_{u\in e\setminus\{w\}}\frac{1}{|e|-1}+d_{w}^{2}\sum_{e\in E_{z}}\sum_{u\in e\setminus\{z\}}\frac{1}{|e|-1}

\displaystyle=d_{z}^{2}d_{w}+d_{w}^{2}d_{z}

and

\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}=d_{w}d_{z}^{2}+d_{z}d_{w}^{2}.

So

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\leq\frac{d_{z}^{2}d_{w}+d_{w}^{2}d_{z}}{d_{w}d_{z}^{2}+d_{z}d_{w}^{2}}=1.\qed

Let
H
be a connected hypergraph.
The distance between vertices
u
and
v
in
H
is the length (number of edges) of a shortest path connecting them, and the diameter of
H
is the maximum distance between any pair of vertices in
H
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex connected
k
-uniform hypergraph with diameter
d
. Then
\lambda_{n-1}(H)>\frac{2}{dk(k-1)^{2}\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, there is a vector

\mathbf{x}
with

\lambda_{n-1}(H)=\frac{\sum_{e\in E(H)}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}}.

Let
w\in V(H)
with
|x_{w}|=\max\{|x_{u}|:u\in V(H)\}
.
Assume that
x_{w}>0
.
As
\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}=0
, there exists a vertex
z\in V(H)
with
x_{z}<0
.
Let
P
be the path from
w
to
z
. Then
|E(P)|\leq d
.
For any edge
e\in E(H)
, let
a_{e}=\max\{x_{u}:u\in e\}
and
b_{e}=\min\{x_{u}:u\in e\}
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}\geq\frac{2}{k(k-1)}\left(\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}|x_{u}-x_{v}|\right)^{2}\geq\frac{2}{k(k-1)}(a_{e}-b_{e})^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(P)}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{k(k-1)}\sum_{e\in E(P)}(a_{e}-b_{e})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{|E(P)|k(k-1)}\left(\sum_{e\in E(P)}|a_{e}-b_{e}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{dk(k-1)}\left(\sum_{e\in E(P)}|a_{e}-b_{e}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{dk(k-1)}\left(x_{w}-x_{z}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{2}{dk(k-1)}x_{w}^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{e\in E(H)}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sum_{e\in E(P)}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle>\frac{2x_{w}^{2}}{dk(k-1)^{2}\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2x_{w}^{2}}{dk(k-1)^{2}\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{w}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{dk(k-1)^{2}\mathrm{vol}(V(H))},

as desired.
∎

4
Interlacing theorem

Chen et al.
[
6
]

provided an edge version of Cauchy’s interlacing theorem on the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues for an ordinary graph
G
with
e\in E(G)
,

\lambda_{i+1}(G)\leq\lambda_{i}(G-e)\leq\lambda_{i-1}(G)\mbox{ for each $i=1,\dots,n$}

with
\lambda_{0}(G)=2
and
\lambda_{n+1}(G)=0
.
Let
K_{4}^{3}
be the complete
4
-vertex
3
-uniform hypergraph.
For the hypergraph
R_{4,3}
defined as above, it is obvious that
R_{4,3}=K_{4}^{3}-e
with edge
e=\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
.
By a direct calculation,
\lambda_{1}(K_{4}^{3})=\lambda_{2}(K_{4}^{3})=\lambda_{3}(K_{4}^{3})=\frac{4}{3}

and
\lambda_{2}(R_{4,3})=\lambda_{3}(R_{4,3})=\frac{5}{4}
. So the above interlacing inequalities do not apply to hypergraphs directly.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex hypergraph which may contain isolated vertices. Let
W
be the set of isolated vertices in
H
.
Let
M=\mbox{diag}\{m(u):u\in V(H)\}
, where

m(u)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }u\in W,\\
d_{H}(u),&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
i=1,\dots,n-1
.
For vectors
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
j=1,\dots,n-i
, let

\mathbf{y}=M^{-1/2}\mathbf{x}\mbox{ and }\mathbf{y}^{(j)}=M^{1/2}\mathbf{x}^{(j)}.

Then

\mathbf{y}^{\top}\mathbf{y}^{(j)}=(M^{-1/2}\mathbf{x})^{\top}(M^{1/2}\mathbf{x}^{(j)})=\mathbf{x}^{\top}M^{-1/2}M^{1/2}\mathbf{x}^{(j)}=\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{(j)},

so
\mathbf{x}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)}
if and only if
\mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{y}^{(j)}
.
Note that
M^{1/2}D(H)^{-1/2}
is a diagnol matrix, where the entry corresponding to
u
is
0
if
u\in W
and
1
otherwise.
As the entry in row and column corresponding to each isolated vertex in
L(H)
is zero, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{y}^{\top}M^{1/2}D(H)^{-1/2}L(H)D^{-1/2}M^{1/2}\mathbf{y}}{(M^{1/2}\mathbf{y})^{\top}(M^{1/2}\mathbf{y})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1}{|e|-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in\overline{W}}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}+\sum_{u\in W}y_{u}^{2}}.

(5)

Denote by
M_{H}(\mathbf{y})
the expression in the right side of (
4
).
Particularly, if
W=\emptyset
, then
M_{H}(\mathbf{y})=R_{H}(\mathbf{y})
.
The well known Courant-Fischer theorem (see Theorem 4.2.11 in
[
14
]
) may be stated as

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(H)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{x}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{y}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{y}^{(n-i)}}M_{H}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{y}^{{(1)}},\dots,\mathbf{y}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{y}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{y}^{(n-i)}}M_{H}(\mathbf{y}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(H)=\min_{\mathbf{y}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{y}^{(i-1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{y}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{y}^{(i-1)}}M_{H}(\mathbf{y}).

For
i=1,\dots,n
,

\mathbf{e}_{i}
denotes the
n
-dimensional column vector with its
i
-th entry
e_{i}
to be
1
and any other entry
0
. For a hypergraph
H
with

, as usual,
H-e
denotes the subhypergraph obtained from
H
by removing the edge
e
. Here we note that
H-e
(and even

) may have isolated vertices in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex
k
-uniform hypergraph and
e\in E(H)
. Then each
i=1,\dots,n
,

\lambda_{i+k-1}(H)\leq\lambda_{i}(H-e)\leq\lambda_{i-k+1}(H)

with
\lambda_{j}(H)=\frac{k}{k-1}
if
j<1
and
\lambda_{j}(H)=0
if
j>n
.

Proof.

By Theorems
2.1
and
3.2
,
\lambda_{n}(H)=0
and
\lambda_{1}(H)\leq\frac{k}{k-1}
.

If
H
contains exactly one edge
e
, the result is trivial.
Suppose that
H
contains at least two edges. Let
e=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
with
d_{H}(v_{1})\geq 2
.
Let

W=\{v:v\in e\mbox{ and }v\not\in e^{\prime}\mbox{ for any }e^{\prime}\in E(H)\setminus\{e\}\}.

That is,
W
is the set of isolated vertices in
H^{\prime}:=H-e
and
t=|W|
.

Claim 1.

\lambda_{i-k+1}(H)\geq\lambda_{i}(H^{\prime})
for
i=k,\dots,n
.

It is trivial for
i=n
. Suppose that
i=k,\dots,n-1
. By the Courant-Fischer theorem,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(H^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{z}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}}M_{H^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\mathbf{x}^{{(1)}},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop{\mathbf{z}\bot\mathbf{x}^{{(j)}},j=1,\dots,n-i\atop\mathbf{z}\bot(\mathbf{e}_{1}+(k-1)\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),\ell=2,\dots,k}}M_{H^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z})

For fixed
\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as
(n-i)+(k-1)<n
, there is a vector
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}
satisfying

\mathbf{z}\bot\mathbf{x}^{{(j)}}
for
j=1,\dots,n-i
and
\mathbf{z}\bot(\mathbf{e}_{1}+(k-1)\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
for
\ell=2,\dots,k
.
Let
a=z_{v_{k}}
. Then

z_{1}=-(k-1)a
and
z_{v_{\ell}}=a
for
\ell=2,\dots,k
.
Thus

\displaystyle M_{H^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{f\in E(H)}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq f}(z_{u}-z_{v})^{2}-\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e}(z_{u}-z_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{H}(u)z_{u}^{2}-\sum_{u\in e\setminus W}z_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{f\in E(H)}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq f}(z_{u}-z_{v})^{2}-k^{2}a^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{H}(u)z_{u}^{2}-(k(k-1)-t)a^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{f\in E(H)}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq f}(z_{u}-z_{v})^{2}-k^{2}a^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{H}(u)z_{u}^{2}-k(k-1)a^{2}}.

As in (
2
), we have
\sum_{f\in E(H)}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq f}(z_{u}-z_{v})^{2}\leq\frac{k}{k-1}\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{H}(u)z_{u}^{2}
, so

M_{H^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z})\leq\frac{\sum_{f\in E(H)}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq f}(z_{u}-z_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{H}(u)z_{u}^{2}}\\
=M_{H}(\mathbf{z}).

Thus

\lambda_{i}(H^{\prime})\leq\max_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop{\mathbf{z}\bot\mathbf{x}^{{(j)}},j=1,\dots,n-i\atop\mathbf{z}\bot(\mathbf{e}_{1}+(k-1)\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),\ell=2,\dots,k}}M_{H}(\mathbf{z}).

Now by the Courant-Fischer theorem again, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i-k+1}(H)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i+k-1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{z}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,n-i+k-1}M_{H}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\geq\max_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i+k-1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\atop{\mathbf{x}^{(n-i+\ell-1)}=\mathbf{e}_{1}+(k-1)\mathbf{e}_{\ell},\ell=2,\dots,k}}\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{z}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,n-i+k-1}M_{H}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(n-i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop{\mathbf{z}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,n-i\atop\mathbf{z}\bot(\mathbf{e}_{1}+(k-1)\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),\ell=2,\dots,k}}M_{H}(\mathbf{z}).

So Claim 1 follows.

Claim 2.

\lambda_{i}(H^{\prime})\geq\lambda_{i+k-1}(H)
for
i=1,\dots,n-k+1
.

By the Courant-Fischer theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(H^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(i-1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{x}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,i-1}M_{H^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\mathbf{x}^{{(1)}},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(i-1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop{\mathbf{x}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,i-1,\atop x_{1}=\dots=x_{k}}}\frac{\sum_{f\in E(H)}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq f}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{H}(u)x_{u}^{2}-(k-t)x_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(i-1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop{\mathbf{x}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,i-1\atop\mathbf{x}\bot(\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),\ell=2,\dots,k}}\frac{\sum_{f\in E(H)}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq f}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{H}(u)x_{u}^{2}-(k-t)x_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(i-1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop{\mathbf{x}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,i-1\atop\mathbf{x}\bot(\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),\ell=2,\dots,k}}M_{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(i+k-2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\}\atop\mathbf{x}\bot\mathbf{x}^{(j)},j=1,\dots,i+k-2}M_{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i+k-1}(H),

so Claim 2 follows.

5
Cheeger inequality

In this section, we give a Cheeger inequality to relate the Cheeger constant to the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of hypergraphs.
We extend such the Cheeger inequality
established by Chung
[
10
]
(see also
[
7
,
8
]
) from graphs to uniform hypergraphs as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
H
be a connected
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices.
Then

\frac{h^{2}(H)}{2(k-1)^{2}}\leq 1-\sqrt{1-\frac{h^{2}(H)}{(k-1)^{2}}}\leq\lambda_{n-1}(H)\leq\frac{2}{k-1}h(H).

Let
H
be a connected
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices. We divide the proof of Theorem
5.1
into the following three lemmas by merging the techniques from
[
8
,
16
]
.
Obviously,
\frac{h^{2}(H)}{2(k-1)^{2}}\leq 1-\sqrt{1-\frac{h^{2}(H)}{(k-1)^{2}}}
.
Losing a factor
k-1
in estimating
\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}+y_{v})^{2}
in the proof of Lemma
5.2
led to the incorrect form:

\max\left\{\frac{h^{2}(H)}{2(k-1)},1-\sqrt{1-\frac{h^{2}(H)}{(k-1)^{2}}}\right\}\leq\lambda_{n-1}(H).

Lemma 5.1
.

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\leq\frac{2}{k-1}h(H)
.

Proof.

Let
S\subset V(H)
be the set of vertices such that
h(H)=\frac{|\partial S|}{\mathrm{vol}(S)}
.
Let
\mathbf{x}
be an
n
-dimensional column vector defined on
V(H)
with

x_{u}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(S)},&\mbox{if }u\in S,\\
-\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S})},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As

\mathbf{x}^{\top}D(H)\mathbf{1}_{n}=\sum_{u\in V(H)}x_{u}d_{u}=\sum_{u\in S}\frac{d_{u}}{\mathrm{vol}(S)}-\sum_{u\in\overline{S}}\frac{d_{u}}{\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S})}=0,

we have by Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}(H)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{v\in\overline{S}}e_{uv}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(S)}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S})}\right)^{2}}{\left(k-1\right)\left(\sum_{u\in S}d_{u}\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(S)^{2}}+\sum_{u\in\overline{S}}d_{u}\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S})^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(S)}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S})}\right)\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{v\in\overline{S}}e_{uv}.

As
\mathrm{vol}(S)\leq\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S})
, we have

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\leq\frac{2}{k-1}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(S)}\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{v\in\overline{S}}e_{uv}=\frac{2}{k-1}\cdot\frac{|\partial S|}{\mathrm{vol}(S)}=\frac{2}{k-1}h(H).\qed

Lemma 5.2
.

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\geq\frac{h^{2}(H)}{2(k-1)^{2}}
.

In the published version, it appeared to be
\lambda_{n-1}(H)\geq\frac{h^{2}(H)}{2(k-1)}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{z}
be an eigenvector of
\mathcal{L}(H)
corresponding to
\lambda_{n-1}(H)
. Let
\mathbf{x}=D(H)^{-1/2}\mathbf{z}
.
From
\lambda_{n-1}(H)\mathbf{z}=\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{z}
, we have

\lambda_{n-1}(H)z_{u}=z_{u}-\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{e_{uv}}{(k-1)\sqrt{d_{u}d_{v}}}z_{v}\mbox{ for }u\in V(H),

i.e.,

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\sqrt{d_{u}}x_{u}=\sqrt{d_{u}}x_{u}-\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{e_{uv}}{(k-1)\sqrt{d_{u}d_{v}}}\sqrt{d_{v}}x_{v}\mbox{ for }u\in V(H).

Let
W=\{u\in V(H):x_{u}>0\}
. it is evident that
\emptyset\neq W\subset V(H)
.
Assume that
\mathrm{vol}(W)\leq\mathrm{vol}(\overline{W})
.
Let
\mathbf{y}
be the
n
-dimensional vector defined on
V(H)
with

y_{u}=\begin{cases}x_{u},&\mbox{if }u\in W,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}(H)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in W}\sqrt{d_{u}}x_{u}\left(\lambda_{n-1}(H)\sqrt{d_{u}}x_{u}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in W}\sqrt{d_{u}}x_{u}\left(\sqrt{d_{u}}x_{u}-\sum_{v\neq u}\frac{e_{uv}}{(k-1)\sqrt{d_{u}d_{v}}}\sqrt{d_{v}}x_{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in W}x_{u}\left(d_{u}x_{u}-\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{e_{uv}}{k-1}x_{v}\right).

As
d_{u}=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}
for any
u\in V(H)
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{u\in W}x_{u}\left(d_{u}x_{u}-\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{e_{uv}}{k-1}x_{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{u\in W}x_{u}\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}(x_{u}-x_{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{u\in W}x_{u}\sum_{v\in W\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}(x_{u}-x_{v})+\sum_{u\in W}x_{u}\sum_{v\in\overline{W}}e_{uv}(x_{u}-x_{v})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{u\in W}x_{u}\sum_{v\in W\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}(x_{u}-x_{v})+\sum_{u\in W}x_{u}\sum_{v\in\overline{W}}e_{uv}x_{u}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{u\in W}y_{u}\sum_{v\in W\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})+\sum_{u\in W}y_{u}\sum_{v\in\overline{W}}e_{uv}y_{u}\right).

Note that in the sum
\sum_{u\in W}y_{u}\sum_{v\in W\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})
, any pair
\{u,v\}\subseteq W
contributes
e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})y_{u}+e_{uv}(y_{v}-y_{u})y_{v}=e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}
. That is,

\sum_{u\in W}y_{u}\sum_{v\in W\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq W}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}.

Note also that

\sum_{u\in W}y_{u}\sum_{v\in\overline{W}}e_{uv}y_{u}=\sum_{u\in W}\sum_{v\in\overline{W}}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}

and

\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq W}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}+\sum_{u\in W}\sum_{v\in\overline{W}}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}.

Thus

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2},

(6)

which implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}(H)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}}

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}+y_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}+y_{v})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}|y_{u}^{2}-y_{v}^{2}|\right)^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}+y_{v})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}|y_{u}^{2}-y_{v}^{2}|\right)^{2}}{2(k-1)^{2}\left(\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}\right)^{2}},

where the second inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the third inequality follows because

\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}+y_{v})^{2}\leq\sum_{u\in V(H)}\sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{u\}}e_{uv}(y_{u}^{2}+y_{v}^{2})=2(k-1)\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}.

The factor
k-1
is lost in
2(k-1)\sum_{u\in V(H)}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}
in early versions.

Assume that
\{y_{u}:u\in V(H)\}=\{t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{m}\}
, where
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}
.
Let
V_{i}=\{u\in V(H):y_{u}\geq t_{i}\}
for
i=0,1,\dots,m
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}|y_{u}^{2}-y_{v}^{2}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq a<c\leq m}\sum_{u\in V(H)\atop y_{u}=t_{a}}\sum_{v\in V(H)\atop y_{v}=t_{c}}e_{uv}(t_{c}^{2}-t_{a}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq a<c\leq m}\sum_{u\in V(H)\atop y_{u}=t_{a}}\sum_{v\in V(H)\atop y_{v}=t_{c}}e_{uv}\sum_{i=a+1}^{c}(t_{i}^{2}-t_{i-1}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{a=0}^{i-1}\sum_{c=i}^{m}\sum_{u\in V(H)\atop y_{u}=t_{a}}\sum_{v\in V(H)\atop y_{v}=t_{c}}e_{uv}(t_{i}^{2}-t_{i-1}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\overline{V_{i}}}\sum_{v\in V_{i}}e_{uv}(t_{i}^{2}-t_{i-1}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\partial V_{i}|\left(t_{i}^{2}-t_{i-1}^{2}\right).

Note that
V_{m}\subset V_{m-1}\subset\dots V_{1}=W\subseteq V_{0}=V(H)
. So
\mathrm{vol}(V_{i})\leq\mathrm{vol}(W)\leq\mathrm{vol}(\overline{W})\leq\mathrm{vol}(\overline{V_{i}})
, and thus
|\partial V_{i}|\geq h(H)\mathrm{vol}(V_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,m
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}|y_{u}^{2}-y_{v}^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq h(H)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{vol}(V_{i})\left(t_{i}^{2}-t_{i-1}^{2}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle=h(H)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{vol}(V_{i})t_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{vol}(V_{i+1})t_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=h(H)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(\mathrm{vol}(V_{i}\setminus V_{i+1})t_{i}^{2}+\mathrm{vol}(V_{m})t_{m}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=h(H)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}.

By combining (
7
) and (
8
), we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\geq 1-\sqrt{1-\frac{h^{2}(H)}{(k-1)^{2}}}
.

Proof.

We use notations from the proof of the previous lemma.
From (
6
) and
\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}x_{u}^{2}=\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}
, we have

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\geq\frac{\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}}{(k-1)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}}:=M.

As

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}+y_{v})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}\left(2(y_{u}^{2}+y_{v}^{2})-(y_{u}-y_{v})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}e_{uv}(y_{u}^{2}+y_{v}^{2})-M(k-1)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=(2-M)(k-1)\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2},

we have from (
7
) and (
8
) that

\displaystyle M\geq\frac{h^{2}(H)\left(\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}\right)^{2}}{(2-M)(k-1)^{2}\left(\sum_{u\in W}d_{u}y_{u}^{2}\right)^{2}}=\frac{h^{2}(H)}{(2-M)(k-1)^{2}},

i.e.,

(k-1)^{2}M^{2}-2(k-1)^{2}M+h^{2}(H)\leq 0,

so

\lambda_{n-1}(H)\geq M\geq 1-\sqrt{1-\frac{h^{2}(H)}{(k-1)^{2}}}\,.\qed

6
Discrepancy inequality

For any two nonempty subsets
X,Y
of hypergraph
H
, let

M(X,Y)
be the multiset of edges in
H
with at least one vertex in
X
and at least one different vertex in
Y
. The multiplicity of an edge
e\in M(X,Y)
is
\sum_{u\in e\cap X}|e\cap Y\setminus\{u\}|
.
Particulaly,
\partial X=M(X,V(H)\setminus X)
if
\emptyset\neq X\subset V(H)
.
Let
m(X,Y)=|M(X,Y)|
.
Then
m(X,Y)=\sum_{u\in X}\sum_{v\in Y}e_{uv}
.
If
H
is an ordinary graph, it is of interest to find a fixed
\alpha
(depending up normalized Laplacian spectrum) so that

\left|m(X,Y)-\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(Y)}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}\right|\leq\alpha\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})\mathrm{vol}(Y)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{Y})}}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}

for all
\emptyset\neq X,Y\subset V(H)
. Such an inequality that bounds the edge distribution between two sets based on the spectral gap is known as a discrepancy inequality
[
8
]
.
It states that a small spectral gap of a graph implies that the edge distribution is close to random.

We extend Chung’s discrepancy inequality
[
11
, Theorem 3]

(or
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
) from graphs to
k
-uniform hypergraphs.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
H
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices.
For any two subsets
X,Y\subseteq V(H)
, we have

\left|\frac{m(X,Y)}{k-1}-\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(Y)}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}\right|\leq\lambda\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})\mathrm{vol}(Y)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{Y})}}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))},

where
\lambda=\max\{|1-\lambda_{i}(H)|:i=1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{z}_{X}
and
\mathbf{z}_{Y}
be the
n
-dimensonal column vectors defined on
V(H)
with

\mathbf{z}_{X}(u)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }u\in X\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}\mbox{ and }\mathbf{z}_{Y}(u)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }u\in Y,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that

\mathbf{z}_{X}^{\top}A(H)\mathbf{z}_{Y}=\sum_{u\in X}(A\mathbf{z}_{Y})_{u}=\sum_{u\in X}\sum_{v\in Y}\frac{e_{uv}}{k-1}=\frac{m(X,Y)}{k-1}

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{X}^{\top}A(H)\mathbf{z}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{z}_{X}^{\top}D(H)^{1/2}(I_{n}-\mathcal{L}(H))D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left(D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{X}\right)^{\top}\left(I_{n}-\mathcal{L}(H)\right)\left(D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{Y}\right).

Let
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}
be the orthonormal eigenvectors of
\mathcal{L}(H)
with
\mathcal{L}(H)\mathbf{x}_{i}=\lambda_{i}(H)\mathbf{x}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, where we may choose
\mathbf{x}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}}D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{n}
by Theorem
2.1
.
Assume that
D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}
and
D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{Y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{X}^{\top}A(H)\mathbf{z}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right)^{\top}(I_{n}-\mathcal{L}(H))\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}(1-\lambda_{i}(H))

\displaystyle=a_{n}b_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{i}(1-\lambda_{i}(H)),

so

\left|\frac{m(X,Y)}{k-1}-a_{n}b_{n}\right|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{i}(1-\lambda_{i}(H))\right|.

Note that

a_{n}=\mathbf{x}_{n}^{\top}D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{X}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}},\ b_{n}=\mathbf{x}_{n}^{\top}D(H)^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{Y}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(Y)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}},

and

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))},\ \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(Y)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{Y})}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}.

So, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{m(X,Y)}{k-1}-\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(Y)}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{i}(1-\lambda_{i}(H))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})\mathrm{vol}(Y)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{Y})}}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))},

as desired.
∎

Recall that
\alpha(H)
denotes the independence number of a hypergraph
H
.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
H
be a
k
-uniform hypergrpah on
n
vertices and
m
edges with minimum degree
\delta
. Then

\alpha(H)\leq\frac{km\lambda}{(1+\lambda)\delta},

where
\lambda=\max\{|1-\lambda_{i}(H)|:i=1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Proof.

Let
X=Y
be a maximum independent set of
H
.
Then
|X|=\alpha(H)
and
m(X,Y)=0
.
By Theorem
6.1
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)^{2}}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))}\leq\lambda\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})}{\mathrm{vol}(V(H))},

so

\mathrm{vol}(X)\leq\lambda\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})
.
Note that
\mathrm{vol}(\overline{X})=km-\mathrm{vol}(X)
.
So

\mathrm{vol}(X)\leq\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}km.

As
\mathrm{vol}(X)\geq\delta|X|\geq\delta\alpha(H)
, we have

\alpha(H)\leq\frac{km\lambda}{(1+\lambda)\delta}.\qed

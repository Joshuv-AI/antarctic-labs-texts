Acoustic Source Localization in Shallow Water: A Probabilistic Focalization Approach
Florian Meyer 1 and Kay L. Gemba 2

Abstract
This paper presents a Bayesian estimation method for the passive localization of an acoustic source in shallow water. Our probabilistic focalization approach estimates the time-varying source location by associating direction of arrival (DOA) observations to DOAs predicted based on a statistical model. Embedded ray tracing makes it possible to incorporate environmental parameters and characterize the nonlinear acoustic waveguide. We demonstrate performance advantages of our approach compared to matched field processing using data collected during the SWellEx-96 experiment.

Introduction

In the shallow water environment, knowledge of the acoustic waveguide can be used to exploit multipath propagation for source localization. In particular, it has been demonstrated that a typical shallow water waveguide offers sufficient coherence times and ray-path diversity (or aperture) to determine the range and depth of an acoustic source from the measurements provided by a single vertical line array (VLA)

II-A

Source State and Association Vectors

The state of the source at time
n
is
\bm{x}_{n}\!\triangleq[\bm{p}_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}\;v_{n}]^{\mathrm{T}}\hskip-0.85358pt
, where

is the source speed in range. The source state

n\hskip-0.85358pt=0

\bm{x}_{0}

f(\bm{x}_{0})

The DOA observations

,
m\hskip-0.85358pt\in\{1,\dots,M_{n}\}

did not originate from any propagation path (this is known as a

), or if a propagation path did not give rise to any observation (this is known as a
missed detection
). The probability that a propagation path is “detected” in the sense that it generates an observation
z_{m,n}
in the DOA estimation stage is denoted by
d_{k}\big(\bm{p}_{n}\big)\!\in\hskip-0.85358pt[0,1]
. (For positions
\bm{p}_{n}
for which a propagation path
k
is geometrically impossible, we set
d_{k}\big(\bm{p}_{n}\big)=0
.) False alarms are independent and identically distributed as
f_{\text{FA}}\big(z_{m,n}\big)
. The number of false alarms is assumed Poisson distributed with mean
\mu_{\text{FA}}\hskip-0.85358pt

[
22
,
13
,
14
]
.
f_{\text{FA}}\big(z_{m,n}\big)
, and
\mu_{\text{FA}}\hskip-0.85358pt
are known. The DOA observations resulting from propagation paths that are not modeled among the
K
selected paths are treated as false alarms. Finally, we introduce the total observation vector
\bm{z}_{n}\hskip-0.85358pt\triangleq\hskip-0.85358pt\big[z_{1,n}\hskip 0.85358pt\cdots\hskip 0.85358ptz_{M_{n},n}\big]^{\mathrm{T}}\hskip-0.85358pt
that is assumed sorted in descending order, i.e.,
z_{m,n}\hskip-0.85358pt\geqslant\hskip-0.85358ptz_{m+1,n}

m\hskip-0.85358pt\in\{1,\dots,M_{n}\hskip-0.85358pt-\hskip-0.85358pt1\}

The associations between DOA observation

and propagation path

K

data association vector

\bm{a}_{n}=\big[a_{1,n}\cdots\hskip 0.85358pta_{K,n}\big]^{\mathrm{T}}\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt

if it does not generate any observation. Due to observation-origin uncertainty,

is a random variable. However, since the DOAs of the propagation paths have a fixed order
[
23
]
and each propagation path can generate at most one DOA observation
[
22
,
13
,
14
]
at any time

, only certain data association vectors
\bm{a}_{n}
are valid. To facilitate identifying invalid vectors

, we order its elements

such that for the case where there are no false alarms and no

\bm{z}_{n}\hskip-0.85358pt=\big[z_{a_{1,n},n}\hskip 0.85358pt\cdots\hskip 0.85358ptz_{a_{K,n},n}\big]^{\mathrm{T}}\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt

k\hskip-0.85358pt>\hskip-0.85358ptk^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358pt

.

For examples, let

us assume that we use the

for the SB,

for the DP,
k=3
for the BB, and

a_{2,n}\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt2
,
a_{3,n}\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt3
, and
a_{4,n}\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt4
as well as

(cf. with the DOAs of the four propagation paths at the VLA shown in Fig.

At time

which is a marginal of the joint posterior PDF

|\bm{z}_{1:n})

Acoustic data sampled at

We process all 13 tones for the 20 minute interval indicated in Fig.

\pm

L=3

586

35\%

VI
Conclusion

We introduced a probabilistic focalization approach for the localization and tracking of an acoustic source in shallow water. Our method probabilistically associates observed DOAs to modeled DOAs and jointly estimates the time-varying source location. We demonstrated performance advantages compared to MFP using data collected during the
SWellEx-96
experiment. Notably, despite relying on less environmental information, the proposed method can outperform MFP in terms of range and depth estimation

accuracy.

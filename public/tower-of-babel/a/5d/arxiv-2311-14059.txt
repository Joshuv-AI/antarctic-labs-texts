Fluctuations, correlations, and Casimir-like forces in the homogeneous cooling state of a granular gas Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Jesús David Jiménez Oliva, Pablo Rodriguez-Lopez, Nagi Khalil

Abstract
The fluctuating hydrodynamics by Brey et. al. is analytically solved to get the long-time limit of the fluctuations of the number density, velocity field, and energy density around the homogeneous cooling state of a granular gas, under physical conditions where it keeps stable. Explicit expressions are given for the non-white contributions in the elastic limit. For small dissipation, the latter is shown to be much smaller than the inelastic contributions, in general. The fluctuation-induced Casimir-like forces on the walls of the system are calculated assuming a fluctuating pressure tensor resulting from perturbing its Navier-Stokes expression. This way, the Casimir-like forces emerges as the correlation between the longitudinal velocity and the energy density. Interestingly, the fluctuation-induced forces push/pull the system towards the square or rectangular geometry when they vanish, in good agreement with the event-driven numerical simulations.



I
Introduction

The Casimir effect is the appearance of quantum-thermal fluctuation-induced interactions of the electromagnetic field between objects. Casimir showed in his seminar works
Casimir 1948
;
Casimir and Polder 1948
that quantum fluctuations of the electromagnetic field, even at zero temperature, make two parallel plates in vacuum to attract each other. In this case, boundary conditions at the plates restrict the fluctuation modes, giving rise to an energy density in between the plates different form that outside and, importantly, dependent on the distance between the plates, which ultimately induces the attraction. The Casimir effect has been studied in different configurations, taking into account the properties of the involded materials
Lifshitz 1956
and the particular geometry of the experiment
Emig et al. 2007
;
Rahi et al. 2009
;
Lambrecht, Neto, and Reynaud 2006
. See
Woods, Rodriguez-Lopez, and Podgornik 2016
;
Dantchev 2023
for recent reviews.

However, the Casimir effect is not a purely electromagnetic interaction, but an emergent interaction of the electromagnetic field due to its fluctuations
Rodriguez-Lopez, Brito, and Soto 2011a
. Moreover, Casimir-like forces or fluctuation-induced forces can appear between objects immersed in any kind of field under fluctuations. For this reason, the Casimir effect can be understood as an universal interaction.

Fluctuation-induced interactions have been studied in many different physical systems
Kardar and Golestanian 1999
;
Gambassi 2009
;
Woods, Rodriguez-Lopez, and Podgornik 2016
showing that two main ingredients can be identified for Casimir-like forces to arise: large enough fluctuations and a confining geometry
Rodriguez-Lopez, Brito, and Soto 2011b
. When we come to thermal, classical systems, the former condition typically requires being close to a critical point
Fisher and de Gennes 1978
. This is the case of Casimir-like forces measured within a critical binary mixture
Krech 1999
;
Hertlein et al. 2008
;
Fisher and de Gennes 2003
;
Gambassi et al. 2009
, in wetting films
Squarcini, Romero-Enrique, and Parry 2023
;
Squarcini, Romero-Enrique, and Parry 2022
, Ising systems
Dantchev and Dietrich 2023
, and Brownian motion of colloids
Maciołek and Dietrich 2018
, just to mention a few examples. Moreover, there is a growing interest in the study of Casimir-like forces in out-of-equilibrium systems, given that many of them show non-negligible fluctuations even from critical points
Golestanian 2005
. Examples include Soret-Casimir effect for system driven by a thermal gradient
Najafi and Golestanian 2004
;
Kirkpatrick, De Zárate, and Sengers 2013
;
Kirkpatrick, de Zárate, and Sengers 2014
, liquid mixtures
Kirkpatrick, De Zárate, and Sengers 2015
;
Kirkpatrick, de Zárate, and Sengers 2016a
;
Kirkpatrick, de Zárate, and Sengers 2016b
, reaction-diffusion systems
Brito, Marconi, and Soto 2007
, and active matter
Fava, Gambassi, and Ginelli 2022
, among many others.

Of particular relevance for our study is the granular Casimir effect postulated by Brito et. al. in
Cattuto et al. 2006
;
Brito, Soto, and Marconi 2007
and further generalized to non-equilibrium systems
Rodriguez-Lopez, Brito, and Soto 2011a
;
Rodriguez-Lopez, Brito, and Soto 2011c
;
Rodriguez-Lopez 2011
. Brito and co-workers measured and calculated the Casimir-like force between two large intruders in a thermostated granular gas. Interestingly, according to their theory, the force between intruders originates form an unbalance renormalized pressure, due to density fluctuations and density-temperature correlations. Moreover, the existence of fluctuation-induced forces is not the approach to a critical point but an intrinsic property of the granular dynamics.

In this article, we study Casimir-like forces in the homogeneous cooling state (HCS) of a granular gas
Brey, Ruiz-Montero, and Cubero 1996
;
Garzó and Dufty 1999
;
Goldhirsch, Noskowicz, and Bar-Lev 2003
. This state can be reached by a granular gas in a system with periodic boundary conditions, and is macroscopically characterized by being spatially homogeneous, with zero velocity field, and decreasing in time energy. The HCS is known to be fundamental to understand the macroscopic dynamics of a granular gases, by playing the role of a reference state around which the hydrodynamic description can be constructed
Brey et al. 1998
;
Khalil, Garzó, and Santos 2014
;
Khalil and Garzó 2020
, for instance. Hence, advancing in the study of the fluctuation properties of the HCS seems natural. In particular, we consider a hypercube system and compute the fluctuation-induced forces acting on the "virtual" walls under physical conditions where the HCS keeps stable. This provides us with information about the role of fluctuations in the stability of the HCS.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
II
is devoted to the definitions and to introduce the fluctuating hydrodynamic description of the system in terms of the fluctuations of the number density, the velocity field, and the energy density. The values of most coefficients can be found in Appendix
A
. We use the theory by Brey et. al.
Brey, Maynar, and De Soria 2009
;
Brey, Maynar, and de Soria 2011
, systematically derived from the fluctuating Boltzmann equation for smooth inelastic hard spheres or disks. It has two main differences if compared to previous theories
Van Noije et al. 1997
;
Van Noije, Ernst, and Brito 1998
;
Van Noije et al. 1999
: it includes the contribution of the density gradient to the heat flux, which has been shown to be relevant under some physical conditions
Brey and Ruiz-Montero 2004
;
Brey and Ruiz-Montero 2005
;
Khalil 2016
, and it also accounts for the non-white properties of the fluctuation terms as well as the breakdown of fluctuation-dissipation relations
Brey, de Soria, and Maynar 2008
.

The fluctuating hydrodynamics is analytically solved in Sec.
III
to obtain the long-time limit of the fluctuations and correlations of the fluctuations of the hydrodynamic fields in the Fourier space. Explicit expressions are given for the elastic case with small wave vector, providing the first order contribution of the non-white properties of the sources, as well as the leading inelastic contributions for small dissipation and small wave vector. The long-time limit is considered because only in this case the amplitudes of the the fluctuations of the sources have an explicit known dependence on time, which in turn is sufficient for the subsequent computation of the Casimir-like forces. Further calculation details are given in Appendix
B
.

The results of Sec.
III
are used to derive an expression for the fluctuation-induced force on the "virtual" walls of the system in Sec.
IV
. We first propose an expression for the pressure tensor up to second order in the fluctuations and then derive the Casimir-like force using a microscopic cutoff, given by the particles diameter. Explicit expression of the force is given for the rectangular (2D) system, under some approximations as done in Appendix
C
. It is shown that the fluctuating contribution to the force on both the horizontal and vertical walls vanishes for a square system, being different from zero in other cases. The results are compared against event-driven molecular dynamics simulations. Details of the numerical simulations can be found in Appendix
D
. We finish with a discussion and some conclusions in Sec.
V
.

II
Fluctuating hydrodynamics

The granular gas is modeled as
N
smooth hard spheres (
d=3
) or disks (
d=2
) in a rectangular container with volume
V=L_{1}\cdots L_{d}
and periodic boundary conditions. All particles have the same mass
m
and diameter
\sigma
, moving freely between inelastic collisions characterized by a constant coefficient of normal restitution
\alpha\in[0,1]
. The case
\alpha=1
corresponds to the elastic limit.

II.1
Homogeneous cooling state

It is well known that under periodic boundary conditions, when the system is small enough, a granular gas can reach the so-called homogeneous cooling state (HCS). From a macroscopic point of view, the HCS is characterized by a spatially homogeneous number density
n_{H}=\frac{N}{V}
, zero mean velocity, and a decreasing-in-time granular temperature
T_{H}(t)
. The latter is related with the total energy density
\frac{d}{2}n_{H}T_{H}
and obeys the Haff’s law
Haff 1983
;
Khalil 2018
:

\partial_{s}T_{H}=-\zeta_{0}T_{H}.

(1)

The time scale
s
is proportional to the number of accumulated collisions, defined as

ds=\frac{v_{0}}{\lambda}dt,

(2)

where

v_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{2T_{H}}{m}}

(3)

and

\lambda=\frac{1}{n_{H}\sigma^{d-1}}

(4)

are proportional to the thermal velocity and the mean free path, respectively. The dimensionless cooling rate
\zeta_{0}
is a function of
\alpha
, whose approximate expression is given in Appendix
A
, and takes into account the dissipation of energy due the inelastic collisions. Hence, it vanishes in the elastic limit
\alpha\to 1
.

II.2
Fluctuations around the HCS

As in the elastic case
Landau and Lifshitz 2013
, the hydrodynamics quantities fluctuate around their values in the HCS. In
Brey, Maynar, and De Soria 2009
;
Brey, Maynar, and de Soria 2011
closed fluctuating Navier-Stokes equations were derived for the fluctuations of the number density
\delta n
, momentum density (proportional to the velocity field)
\delta\bm{G}
, and the energy density
\delta E
. These quantities depend on position
\bm{r}
and time
s
.

Before providing the fluctuating equations, it is convenient to introduce dimensionless hydrodynamics quantities as

\displaystyle\delta\rho(\bm{\ell},s)=\frac{\delta n(\bm{\ell},s)}{n_{H}},

(5)

\displaystyle\delta\bm{\omega}(\bm{\ell},s)=\frac{\delta G(\bm{\ell},s)}{mn_{H}v_{0}(s)},

(6)

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon(\bm{\ell},s)=\frac{\delta E(\bm{\ell},s)}{\frac{d}{2}n_{H}T_{H}(s)},

(7)

where
\bm{\ell}
is a dimensionless vector measuring positions in unit of the local mean free path

\bm{\ell}=\frac{\bm{r}}{\lambda}.

(8)

The corresponding Fourier quantities, denoted by
\delta\rho(\bm{k},s)
,
\delta\bm{\omega}(\bm{k},s)
, and
\delta\epsilon(\bm{k},s)
are given by

\delta\rho(\bm{\ell},s)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\delta\rho({\bm{k}},s)e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\ell}},

(9)

and similarly for the other two fluctuation fields. We have introduced the dimensionless volume

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\dots\mathcal{L}_{d},

(10)

with

\mathcal{L}_{i}=\frac{L_{i}}{\lambda}=n_{H}\sigma^{d-1}L_{i},\qquad i=1,\dots,d,

(11)

the dimensionless lengths. Since we are assuming a finite system with periodic boundary conditions, the wave vectors of the Fourier sum are restricted such as

k_{i}=\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{i}}n_{i},\quad n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad i=1,\dots,d,

(12)

Finally, the fluctuations are real magnitudes, hence

[\delta\rho(\bm{k},s)]^{*}=\delta\rho(-\bm{k},s),

(13)

and similarly for the velocity and energy fluctuations.

II.3
Fluctuating Navier-Stokes equations

From the results in
Brey, Maynar, and de Soria 2011
, the Langevin Navier-Stokes equations for the fluctuations of the hydrodynamic quantities in the Fourier space read

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\delta\rho

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i\bm{k}\cdot\delta\bm{\omega}=0,

(14)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\delta\bm{\omega}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\right)\delta\bm{\omega}+\eta\frac{d-2}{d}{\bm{k}}\bm{k}\cdot\delta\bm{\omega}

(15)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i\frac{\bm{k}}{2}\delta\epsilon={\bm{W}},

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\delta\epsilon

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}-\frac{2}{d}k^{2}(\kappa-\mu)\right]\delta\rho+i\frac{d+2}{d}\bm{k}\cdot\delta\bm{\omega}

(16)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}+\frac{2}{d}k^{2}\kappa\right)\delta\epsilon={\mathcal{E}},

where we have removed the dependence of the quantities on
\bm{k}
and
s
to easy the notation. The new quantities are the dimensionless transport coefficients, namely the viscosity
\eta
, the thermal conductivity
\kappa
, and the diffusive heat conductivity
\mu
. They are time dependent quantities whose asymptotic values for large times are given in the Appendix
A
. Moreover, the two magnitudes on the right-hand side of the second and third equations are uncorrelated noise sources whose statistical properties read

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{W}}\right\rangle=\bm{0},

(17)

\displaystyle\left\langle W_{i}(\bm{k},s)W_{j}(\bm{k}^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right\rangle\simeq\frac{d}{2(d-1)}c_{w}\delta_{\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{d-2}{d}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\right)e^{\lambda_{4}|s-s^{\prime}|},

(18)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathcal{E}}\right\rangle=0,

(19)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{k},s){\mathcal{E}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right\rangle\simeq\left[c_{\epsilon}^{(1)}\delta(s-s^{\prime})\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 85.35826pt\left.+c_{\epsilon}^{(2)}k^{2}e^{\lambda_{5}|s-s^{\prime}|}\right]\delta_{\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}},

(20)

where the approximate relations hold for
s\gg 1,\,s^{\prime}\gg 1
and the new coefficients
c_{w},\,c_{\epsilon}^{(1)}
, and
c_{\epsilon}^{(2)}
are explicitly given in Appendix
A
. The exponents
\lambda_{4}
and
\lambda_{5}
can be written as a function of the transport coefficients:

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}\simeq-\frac{1}{2\eta}+\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2},

(21)

\displaystyle\lambda_{5}\simeq-\frac{3(d+2)-2\kappa\zeta_{0}}{4(3\kappa-2\mu)}.

(22)

Note that, on the one hand, the fluctuating Navier-Stokes equations (
14
)–(
16
) are restricted to small gradients, i.e. small values of
k
, as well as to small hydrodynamic fluctuations. Moreover, noise terms are no longer white but include a finite (exponential) correlation time. On the other hand, they include the elastic case for
\alpha=1
. However, the Landau theory
Landau and Lifshitz 2013
for the molecular fluids is recovered not only by taking
\alpha=1
, but also by assuming that the noise terms are delta-correlated, which is true provided time correlations among the noise terms decay faster than the typical hydrodynamic time scales, namely

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{4}s}\to\frac{2}{|\lambda_{4}|}\delta(s),

(23)

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{5}s}\to\frac{2}{|\lambda_{5}|}\delta(s).

(24)

This is equivalent to assuming that
|\lambda_{4}|s\gg 1,\,|\lambda_{4}|s\gg 1
for
s
in the hydrodynamic time scale.

III
Spatial correlations

In this section we solve the fluctuating Navier-Stokes equations to get the steady-state spatial correlations of the fluctuating hydrodynamic quantities. In doing so, we first consider the general solution to the set (
14
)–(
16
). Then we use it to get the structure factors.

III.1
General solution

In order to solve the fluctuating Navier-Stokes equations, it is useful to decompose the fluctuation of the velocity into its component along
\bm{k}
and normal to it:

\displaystyle\delta\phi=-i\frac{\bm{k}}{k}\cdot\delta\bm{\omega},

(25)

\displaystyle\delta\bm{\varphi}=-i\delta\bm{\omega}-\delta\phi\frac{\bm{k}}{k},

(26)

where the imaginary unit
i
has been introduced to make the coefficients of the resulting equations real.

By means of the new quantities, the equation for
\delta\bm{\varphi}
decouples form the rest:

\partial_{s}\delta\bm{\varphi}+\left(\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\right)\delta\bm{\varphi}={\bm{W}}_{\perp},

(27)

where

{\bm{W}}_{\perp}=-i{\bm{W}}-W_{\parallel}\frac{\bm{k}}{k},

(28)

and

W_{\parallel}=-i{\bm{W}}\cdot\frac{\bm{k}}{k}.

(29)

As for the reminder fluctuating quantities, we have a closed set of equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\delta\rho-k\delta\phi=0,

(30)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\delta\phi+\left[\frac{2(d-1)}{d}\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\right]\delta\phi+\frac{k}{2}\delta\epsilon={W}_{\parallel},

(31)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\delta\epsilon+\left[\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}-\frac{2}{d}(\kappa-\mu)k^{2}\right]\delta\rho-\frac{d+2}{d}k\delta\phi

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 79.6678pt+\left(\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}+\frac{2}{d}\kappa k^{2}\right)\delta\epsilon={\mathcal{E}}.

(32)

The Langevin equation (
27
) for the perpendicular component of the fluctuations of the velocity is decoupled from the rest and can be directly solved:

\delta\bm{\varphi}(s,k)=\int_{0}^{s}ds_{1}\,e^{-\left(\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\right)(s-s_{1})}\bm{W}_{\perp}(s_{1},k),

(33)

where we have assumed that
\delta\bm{\varphi}(0)=\bm{0}
. It is readily seen that
\delta\bm{\varphi}
diverges as time rises for wave vectors
\bm{k}
such as
\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}<0
. Hence, in order for the linear regime described by system (
27
), (
30
)–(
III.1
) to hold for large times
s\gg 1
, we have to restrict ourselves to wave vectors larger than
k_{m}
:

k_{m}=\sqrt{\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2\eta}}.

(34)

This restriction is related to the linear stability of the HCS, extensively discussed in the literature
Brey et al. 1998
;
Brey, Ruiz-Montero, and Dominguez 2008
. Note that the wave-vector limitation is irrelevant in the elastic limit, since
\zeta_{0}\to 0
for
\alpha\to 1
, while
\eta
keeps positive.

Proceeding analogously, the general solution to (
30
)–(
III.1
) can be written as

\delta\bm{u}(s,k)=\int_{0}^{s}ds_{1}\,e^{(s-s_{1})A}\bm{b}(s_{1},k),

(35)

where we have introduced the following matrix notation

\displaystyle\bm{u}=(\delta\rho,\delta\phi,\delta\epsilon)^{t},

(36)

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}0&k&0\\
0&-\nu&-\frac{k}{2}\\
\beta&\frac{d+2}{d}k&-\gamma\end{pmatrix},

(37)

\displaystyle\bm{b}=(0,W_{\parallel},{\mathcal{E}})^{t}.

(38)

and

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{2(d-1)}{d}\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2},

(39)

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{2}{d}(\kappa-\mu)k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2},

(40)

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{2}{d}\kappa k^{2}+\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}.

(41)

The previous coefficients depend on
\alpha
and
k
.

It can be seen that the eigenvalues of
A
have all negative real parts for a wide range of values of
\alpha
(including
\alpha=1
) provided
k>k_{m}
. Hence, under the latter conditions, Eq. (
35
) provides the time dependence of the fluctuations for all times.

III.2
Structure factor

From the previous results we directly obtain that
\delta\bm{\varphi}
is uncorrelated from the other fluctuating magnitudes. Using
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle
to denote average over the noise around the HCS, we directly obtain that

\left\langle\delta\bm{\varphi}(s,k)\delta\bm{u}(s^{\prime},k^{\prime})\right\rangle=0,

(42)

since the noise terms
\bm{W}_{\perp},\,W_{\parallel}
, and
\mathcal{E}
are uncorrelated. On the other hand, after some calculations we obtain the two-time correlation function for
\delta\bm{\varphi}

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\bm{\varphi}_{i}(s,k)\delta\bm{\varphi}_{j}(s^{\prime},k^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}^{2}\eta}{2N\left(\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\right)}k^{2}\delta_{\bm{k},-\bm{k}}

(43)

\displaystyle\quad\times\delta_{ij}e^{-\left(\eta k^{2}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\right)|s-s^{\prime}|},

which holds for
s,s^{\prime}\gg 1
. We refer to
Brey, de Soria, and Maynar 2008
;
Brey, Maynar, and De Soria 2009
for a deeper analysis of the correlations of the transverse velocity.

Focusing on the correlations among the other fields, the components of
\delta\bm{u}
, it is also readily seen that only components with opposite wave vectors can be correlated. Again, this is a direct consequence of the statistical properties of the nose terms given in Eqs. (
II.3
) and (
II.3
). Hence, in order to account for the relevant correlations taking place at the same time, we consider the following structure-factor vector:

\displaystyle\bm{S}=(\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\rho^{*}\right\rangle,\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\phi^{*}\right\rangle,\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\qquad\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\phi^{*}\right\rangle,\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle,\left\langle\delta\epsilon\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle)^{t},

(44)

where all quantities are functions of the time
s
and the wave vector
\bm{k}
. In Appendix
B
we show that the steady-state value of
\bm{S}
is given by

\bm{S}=M^{-1}\bm{Q},

(45)

where

M=\begin{pmatrix}0&2k&0&0&0&0\\
0&-\nu&-\frac{k}{2}&k&0&0\\
\beta&\frac{d+2}{d}k&-\gamma&0&k&0\\
0&0&0&-2\nu&-k&0\\
0&\beta&0&\frac{d+2}{d}k&-\nu-\gamma&-\frac{k}{2}\\
0&0&2\beta&0&\frac{2(d+2)}{d}k&-2\gamma\end{pmatrix}

(46)

and

\displaystyle\bm{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\frac{c_{w}(\gamma-\lambda_{4})}{|\lambda_{4}I+A|}k\\
-\frac{c_{\epsilon}^{(2)}}{2|\lambda_{5}I+A|}k^{2}\\
-\frac{2c_{w}\lambda_{4}(\gamma-\lambda_{4})}{|\lambda_{4}I+A|}\\
\frac{c_{w}(\beta-\frac{d+2}{d}\lambda_{4})}{|\lambda_{4}I+A|}k+\frac{c_{\epsilon}^{(2)}\lambda_{5}}{2|\lambda_{5}I+A|}k\\
-c_{\epsilon}^{(1)}-\frac{2c_{\epsilon}^{(2)}\lambda_{5}(\nu-\lambda_{5})}{|\lambda_{5}I+A|}\end{array}\right)\rightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
0\\
-\frac{2c_{w}}{|\lambda_{4}|}\\
0\\
-c_{\epsilon}^{(1)}-\frac{2c_{\epsilon}^{(2)}}{|\lambda_{5}|}\end{array}\right).

The limiting expression for
\bm{Q}
holds for
|\lambda_{4}|
and
|\lambda_{5}|
much larger than the entries of
A
.

III.3
Correlations and fluctuations for
k=0

For
k=0
the matrix
M
is singular, and we have to solve Eq. (
110
) directly, without inverting
M
. It is not difficult to see that all correlations and fluctuations vanishes for
k=0
but the energy fluctuations

\left\langle\delta\epsilon\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle_{k=0}=\frac{4\mathcal{V}^{2}}{N}a_{33}(\alpha).

(60)

This expression provide the global energy fluctuation around the HCS, as already reported and fully studied in
Brey et al. 2004
;
Brey et al. 2005
. For
\alpha=1
, it is
a_{33}=0
and no global energy fluctuations exist, as expected.

III.4
Correlations for the elastic case

For
\alpha=1
the solution given by Eq. (
45
) provides the correlations and fluctuations in the elastic case. It is readily seen that

\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\phi^{*}\right\rangle=0.

(61)

Keeping up to order
k^{3}
, the other quantities read:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\rho^{*}\right\rangle\simeq 1-\frac{2\left[(d-1)(d+2)^{3}\eta^{3}+8\kappa^{3}\right]}{(d+2)^{2}\left[(d-1)(d+2)\eta+2\kappa\right]}k^{2},

(62)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle\simeq 1-\frac{2\left[(d-1)(d+2)^{3}\eta^{3}+8\kappa^{3}\right]}{d(d+2)\left[(d-1)(d+2)\eta+2\kappa\right]}k^{2},

(63)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\phi^{*}\right\rangle\simeq\frac{1}{2}-

\displaystyle\quad\frac{(d-1)(d+2)\left[3d(d+2)\eta^{3}+4\eta^{2}\kappa\right]+8\kappa^{3}}{d(d+2)\left[(d-1)(d+2)\eta+2\kappa\right]}k^{2},

(64)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle\simeq\frac{8(d-1)\eta\kappa\left[-(d+2)^{2}\eta^{2}+4\kappa^{2}\right]}{d^{2}(d+2)\left[(d-1)(d+2)\eta+2\kappa\right]}k^{3},

(65)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\epsilon\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle\simeq\frac{d+2}{d}

(66)

\displaystyle-\frac{2\left\{(d-1)(d+2)\left[(d+2)^{3}\eta^{3}+8\eta\kappa^{2}\right]+8(d+4)\kappa^{3}\right\}}{d^{2}(d+2)\left[(d-1)(d+2)\eta+2\kappa\right]}k^{2},

where the transport coefficients
\eta
and
\kappa
take their elastic values.

It is important to note that, in the elastic limit, the dependence of the fluctuations and correlations on
k
disappear when noise is suppose to be white. This way, Eqs. (
61
)–(
66
) provide the first
k
-corrections to the classical results
Landau and Lifshitz 2013
when exponential decay of the noise correlations are taking into account. Moreover, the corrections also disappear in the limit
\eta,\,\kappa\ll 1
which is related to the conditions
|\lambda_{4}|,\,|\lambda_{5}|\gg 1
, as it is evident from Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
).

III.5
Correlations and fluctuations for
k>0
and
\alpha<1

In the general case, i.e.
k>0,
and
\alpha<1
, the exact expressions for the correlations are given by Eq. (
45
). Particularly, the density and the longitudinal velocity are also found to be uncorrelated:

\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\phi^{*}\right\rangle=0.

(67)

For the other magnitudes, we can not take the limit of small
k
directly. Since we are assuming that the HCS is stable, i.e. that
k^{2}>\zeta_{0}/(2\eta)
, we take
k^{2}\sim\zeta_{0}\sim\mu
small and of the same order, while the other transport coefficients are taken of zeroth order in

. This way, we can show the explicit contribution of the dissipation, at least in its weak limit, as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\rho^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\frac{d}{4}\zeta_{0}}{\left(\kappa k^{2}-\frac{d}{4}\zeta_{0}\right)}\times

(68)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\frac{2(d-1)\eta-(d-2)\kappa}{(d-1)(d+2)\eta+2\kappa}k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\rho\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 1-\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}},

(69)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\phi^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{k^{2}_{0}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}}\right),

(70)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{4[(d^{2}-1)\eta+\kappa]}{d^{2}}k_{0}^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}}k,

(71)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\epsilon\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{d+2}{d}\left(1-\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}}\right),

(72)

where

k_{0}^{2}=\frac{d^{2}\zeta_{0}}{4\left[(d-1)(d+2)\eta+2\kappa\right]},

(73)

and
\eta
and
\kappa
evaluated at
\alpha=1
.

It is worth noting that the previous results coincide with that obtained using the approximation of white noise. Moreover, if we set
\zeta_{0}=0
then
k_{0}=0
and we recover the leading orders of the elastic case, Eqs. (
61
)–(
66

k=0
. Nevertheless, the inelastic contribution is relevant, in general, even in the case of the energy-velocity correlations in Eq. (

IV
Casimir-like forces

The results of the previous sections are used to compute the Casimir-like forces on the “virtual” walls of the system. In doing so, we first postulate an expression for the pressure tensor up to second order in the fluctuations. Our final goal is to provide an explicit expression for the fluctuation-induced forces for the two-dimension geometry which we compare against event-driven molecular dynamic simulations.

IV.1
Second order pressure tensor

We assume that the contributions of the fluctuations to the pressure tensor
P_{ij}
around the HCS can be obtained by perturbing its Navier-Stokes expression
Brey and Cubero 2001
as
1
1

1

The prefactor
2
of
2\eta
appears because of the way the viscosity is written in its dimensionless form.

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{ij}\equiv\frac{P_{ij}}{n_{H}T_{H}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle(1+\delta\epsilon)\delta_{ij}-2\eta\sqrt{\frac{1+\delta\epsilon}{1+\delta\rho}}\left(\partial_{\ell_{i}}\frac{\delta\omega_{j}}{1+\delta\rho}\right.

(74)

\displaystyle\left.+\partial_{\ell_{j}}\frac{\delta\omega_{i}}{1+\delta\rho}-\frac{2}{d}\partial_{\bm{\ell}}\cdot\frac{\delta\bm{\omega}}{1+\delta\rho}\delta_{ij}\right).

The zeroth order in the fluctuations reads
n_{H}T_{H}\delta_{ij}
which is the nonfluctuating hydrodynamic pressure. The first-order has zero mean and does not contribute to the Casimir force. Hence, we retain the second order:

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{ij}^{(c)}\simeq 2\eta\left[\partial_{\ell_{i}}(\delta\omega_{j}\delta\rho)+\partial_{\ell_{j}}(\delta\omega_{i}\delta\rho)-\frac{2}{d}\partial_{\bm{\ell}}\cdot(\delta\bm{\omega}\delta\rho)\delta_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\eta(\delta\varepsilon-\delta\rho)\left(\partial_{\ell_{i}}\delta\omega_{j}+\partial_{\ell_{j}}\delta\omega_{i}-\frac{2}{d}\partial_{\bm{\ell}}\cdot\delta\bm{\omega}\delta_{ij}\right).

(75)

The actual dimensionless second-order pressure tensor
P_{ij}^{(c)}
should have an additional noisy term, not directly related with the fluctuating quantities. However, we expect it to have zero mean, as happens with the first-order contribution
Brey, Maynar, and de Soria 2011
.

Using the fact that
\delta\rho
and
\delta\bm{\omega}
are uncorrelated, see previous section, we have

\left\langle\tilde{P}_{ij}^{(c)}\right\rangle\simeq-\eta\left\langle\delta\epsilon\left(\partial_{\ell_{i}}\delta\omega_{j}+\partial_{\ell_{j}}\delta\omega_{i}-\frac{2}{d}\partial_{\bm{\ell}}\cdot\delta\bm{\omega}\delta_{ij}\right)\right\rangle.

(76)

After some algebra, this expression can be written using the Fourier quantities as

\left\langle\tilde{P}_{ij}^{(c)}\right\rangle\simeq\frac{2\eta}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\left(\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{d}\right)k\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle_{k},

(77)

where we have used that
\left\langle\delta\bm{\varphi}\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle=0
. The prime of the sum stands for its truncation. Here we assume that this is due to the approach to the microscopic scale, namely when (approximately)
k>\frac{2\pi\lambda}{\sigma}
, with
\sigma
the grain diameter.

Since the sum in (
77
) includes, for any
\bm{k}
, the vector
-\bm{k}
as well, then

\left\langle\tilde{P}_{ij}^{(c)}\right\rangle=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad i\neq j.

(78)

Moreover, it is readily seen that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle\tilde{P}_{ii}^{(c)}\right\rangle=0.

(79)

The Casimir-like force
F_{i}^{(c)}
on a given wall normal to the
i
-direction comes from the contribution of the fluctuation to the pressure tensor:

F_{i}^{(c)}=n_{H}T_{H}\left\langle\tilde{P}_{ii}^{(c)}\right\rangle S_{i},

(80)

where
S_{i}=V/L_{i}
is the area (
d=3
) or length (
d=2
) of the wall. We shall used the dimensionless Casimir-like force per unit area, which can be written as

f_{i}^{(c)}=\frac{F_{i}^{(c)}}{n_{H}T_{H}S_{i}}\simeq\frac{2\eta}{d\mathcal{V}^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\frac{dk_{i}^{2}-k^{2}}{k}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle_{k}.

(81)

By symmetry consideration, when all sides of the system have the same length
L_{1}=\dots=L_{d}
we have

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\frac{k_{x}^{2}}{k}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\frac{k_{y}^{2}}{k}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle_{k}

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dots=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\frac{k}{d}\left\langle\delta\phi\delta\epsilon^{*}\right\rangle_{k}.

Hence, our Casimir-like force is zero for a
d
-dimension cube.

IV.2
Casimir forces for a rectangular system (
d=2
)

For a
d=2
system, using Eq. (
77
), the approximate result of Eq. (
71
), and some other approximations, the dimensionless Casimir force per unit length on the vertical walls can be written as

\displaystyle f_{1}^{(c)}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{(3\eta+\kappa)\eta\zeta_{0}}{16(2\eta+\kappa)}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}}{\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{2}-\mathcal{L}_{2}^{2}}{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{2}^{2}}

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n_{H}\sigma}\frac{(3\eta+\kappa)\eta\zeta_{0}}{16(2\eta+\kappa)}\frac{L_{1}+L_{2}}{L_{1}L_{2}}\frac{L_{1}^{2}-L_{2}^{2}}{L_{1}^{2}+L_{2}^{2}},

and for the horizontal walls
f_{2}^{(c)}=-f_{1}^{(c)}
. The details of the computation are in Appendix
C
.

The force

is clearly negative for
L_{1}>L_{2}

L_{1}<L_{2}

V
Discussion and conclusions

In this work, we have studied the long-time limit of the fluctuations and correlations of the density, velocity, and energy around the homogeneous cooling state (HCS), under condition where the HCS is stable. The calculation have been carried out using the fluctuating theory by Brey et. al.
Brey, Maynar, and De Soria 2009
;
Brey, Maynar, and de Soria 2011
, which considers any dimensionality and non-white properties of the noise terms, and is expected to be valid for a dilute gas beyond the limit of small dissipation (
\alpha\sim 1
). Our results provide a non-white correction to the classical results in the elastic case [Eqs. (
61
)–(
66
)] as well as explicit expressions for small dissipation [Eqs. (
67
)–(
72
)]. Moreover, we have found that non-white contributions can be neglected for small dissipation and small wave vector, provided the HCS keeps stable.

The dissipation introduces new dependence of the fluctuations and correlations on the wave vector, which vanishes smoothly as we approach the elastic limit. For small dissipation, the main dependence on
k
of most correlations comes from the function
k_{0}^{2}/(k^{2}+k_{0}^{2})
where
k_{0}^{2}
is proportional to the cooling rate (dissipation) as given by Eq. (
73
). Similar dependence has also been found in driven systems
Van Noije et al. 1999
;
Brito, Soto, and Marconi 2007
. However we stress that in our case the previous function is no longer divergent for small
k
because, on the one hand, we can no longer neglect
k_{0}
as it is given by the degree of dissipation and, on the other hand, the stability condition
k>k_{m}\simeq k_{0}
, with
k_{m}
given by Eq. (
34
), ensures a minimal value of
k
which, again, depends on
\alpha
. This is one more manifestation of the difference between driven and non-driven granular gases, which now relays on the stability of the state around which fluctuations are studied.

The long-time limit of the fluctuations and correlations has been used to compute the fluctuation-induced or Casimir-like force on the walls of the system, by first assuming a form of the pressure tensor up to second order in the fluctuations of the hydrodynamic fields, resulting from perturbing its Naiver-Stokes form. This way, the only contribution to the force comes from the correlations between the longitudinal velocity and the energy, as opposite to what has been proposed in the driven case
Cattuto et al. 2006
;
Brito, Soto, and Marconi 2007
;
Shaebani, Sarabadani, and Wolf 2012
where density-density and density-temperature correlations are the relevant ones. The latter emerges as the perturbation of the hydrostatic pressure (equation of state) as a function of the density and temperature. In our case, a contribution of this kind would produce a systematic deviation of the Casimir-like force, which has not been observed in simulations. The selection of the temperature instead of the energy in the fluctuating hydrodynamic description seems to play an important role at this point. Further investigation of this aspect has to be carried out in order to clarify the relevance of the two complementary mechanisms, that could be done even using recent experimental setups
Pitikaris et al. 2022
;
Yu, Schröter, and Sperl 2020
.

Our theory predicts a zero Casimir-like force for a cube or square system. Moreover, for the two-dimension case, the force on a vertical wall, for instance, is a decreasing function of the horizontal length of the system, being zero only when the length equals the height, which is confirmed with event-driven simulations, even beyond the limit of small dissipation. Hence, the fluctuations have the important effect of stabilizing the square configuration. A similar effect is also expected for the three-dimension case.

Appendix B
Computation of the structure factor

The starting point to compute the correlations among fluctuating fields at the same time and opposite wave vectors, is to define the matrix structure factor as

\displaystyle S(s,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\bm{u}(s,k)[\delta\bm{u}(s,-k)]^{t}\right\rangle

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\bm{u}(s,k)[\delta\bm{u}(s,k)]^{\dagger}\right\rangle,

where last equality holds from Eq. (
13

37

38

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{b}\delta\bm{u}^{\dagger}+\delta\bm{u}\bm{b}^{\dagger}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}ds_{1}\,B(s-s_{1})e^{(s-s_{1})A^{t}}

(99)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}ds_{1}e^{(s-s_{1})A}\,B(s-s_{1}),

) and (

Appendix C
Approximate computation of the Casimir forces

Using Eq. (
77
) with Eq. (
83
) and the result of Eq. (
71
), valid for small dissipation and small values of
k
, the dimensionless Casimir force per unit length for a
d=2
system reads

f_{1}^{(c)}\simeq\frac{(3\eta+\kappa)\eta\zeta_{0}}{2(2\eta+\kappa)N}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\frac{k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}},

(111)

where

k_{0}^{2}=\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2(2\eta+\kappa)},

(112)

and the prime indicates a truncation of the sum, as specified bellow. The sum can be written as

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\frac{k_{1}^{2}-k_{y}^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}}=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n_{1},n_{2}}S_{n_{1},n_{2}}

(113)

with

S_{n_{1},n_{2}}=\frac{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}n_{1}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{y}}n_{2}\right)^{2}}{\left[\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}n_{1}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{y}}n_{2}\right)^{2}\right]+k_{0}^{2}}.

(114)

Taking into account that
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
take positive and negative values, we have

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\bm{k}}\frac{k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n_{1},n_{2}}S_{n_{1},n_{2}}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\left(S_{n_{1},n_{2}}+S_{n_{2},n_{1}}\right)

(115)

\displaystyle=2\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n_{1}>0,n_{2}>0}\left(S_{n_{1},n_{2}}+S_{n_{2},n_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq 2\int_{0}^{n_{M}}dn_{1}dn_{2}\,\left(S_{n_{1},n_{2}}+S_{n_{2},n_{1}}\right).

In the first approximation of the previous equation we assume that the truncation of the sum is as
n_{1},\,n_{2}<n_{M}
and not over the values of
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, with

n_{M}\simeq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}}{4\pi n_{H}\sigma^{2}}.

(116)

The second approximation is an integral approximation of the sum. In order to compute the integral, we introduce further approximations:

\displaystyle S_{n_{1},n_{2}}+S_{n_{2},n_{1}}=\left[\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{2\left[\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\right)^{2}\right]n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}+k_{0}^{2}(n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2})}{\left[\left(\frac{2\pi n_{1}}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi n_{2}}{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\right)^{2}+k_{0}^{2}\right]\left[\left(\frac{2\pi n_{2}}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi n_{1}}{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\right)^{2}+k_{0}^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle\quad\simeq 2\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{2}-\mathcal{L}_{1}^{2}}{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{2}^{2}}\frac{4n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}+\frac{2k_{0}^{2}}{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\right)^{2}}(n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2})}{\left[n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+\frac{2k_{0}^{2}}{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\right)^{2}}\right]^{2}},

(117)

which is valid provided
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{2}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{2}^{2}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2}
. Finally, assuming that
\frac{2k_{0}^{2}}{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\right)^{2}}\sim 1
and
n_{m}\gg 1
and taking into account that

N=n_{H}L_{1}L_{2}=\frac{1}{n_{H}\sigma^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2},

(118)

we arrive at Eq. (
83
).

Privacy-Utility Tradeoff Based on α \alpha -lift
Mohammad A. Zarrabian, and Parastoo Sadeghi

Abstract
Information density and its exponential form, known as lift, play a central role in information privacy leakage measures. α \alpha -lift is the power-mean of lift, which is tunable between the worst-case measure max-lift ( α = ∞ \alpha=\infty ) and more relaxed versions ( α < ∞ \alpha<\infty ). This paper investigates the optimization problem of the privacy-utility tradeoff (PUT) where α \alpha -lift and mutual information are privacy and utility measures, respectively. Due to the nonlinear nature of α \alpha -lift for α < ∞ \alpha<\infty , finding the optimal solution is challenging. Therefore, we propose a heuristic algorithm to estimate the optimal utility for each value of α \alpha , inspired by the optimal solution for α = ∞ \alpha=\infty and the convexity of α \alpha -lift with respect to the lift, which we prove. The numerical results show the efficacy of the algorithm and indicate the effective range of α \alpha and privacy budget ε \varepsilon with good PUT performance.

We propose Algorithm
1
, which is a heuristic approach to find solutions for the problem in (
19
)-(
20
).
Algorithm
1
takes
\mathbf{P}_{SX}
and two ordered sets
\mathcal{A}=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{|\mathcal{A}|}\}
, and
\mathcal{B}=\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{|\mathcal{B}|}\}
as its main input, where
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}>\cdots>\alpha_{|\mathcal{A}|}
and
\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}<\cdots<\varepsilon_{|\mathcal{B}|}
.
Other auxiliary parameters will be explained shortly. The output is a feasible solution for
\mathbf{P}_{X|Y},\mathbf{P}_{Y}
and the corresponding output alphabet
\mathcal{Y}_{i,j}
and utility
I_{i,j}(X;Y)
for each pair of
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j})
.

The utility value is generally expected to increase when
\varepsilon
increases and when
\alpha
decreases. However, due to the heuristic nature of Algorithm
1
, one may obtain a slightly lower utility for
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j})
compared to the utility of
(\alpha_{i^{\prime}},\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}})
where
\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{i^{\prime}}
or
\varepsilon_{j}>\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}
. To address this, we propose considering multiple
\varepsilon
and
\alpha
values as follows.

1) The algorithm initializes
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}
by
P_{X|Y=y}
s in (
18
), for
\alpha=\infty
and each
\varepsilon_{j}\in\mathcal{B}
(line 3). These vectors are feasible but not extremal for
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j})
. Nevertheless, they guarantee that if a solution is optimally achievable for
\alpha=\infty
and
\varepsilon_{j}
it is also achievable for any
(\alpha_{i}<\infty,\varepsilon_{j})
.

2) Recalling Remark
1
, we note that the solution
\mathbf{P}_{X|Y}
for
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j})
is also feasible for
\alpha_{i^{\prime}}<\alpha_{i}
or
\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}>\varepsilon_{j}
. Therefore in line 7, for each pair of
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j})
, we make a union of the feasible set
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}
and the previous solutions for
(\alpha_{i-1},\varepsilon_{j})
and
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j-1})
saved in
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha_{i-1}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{j-1}}
, respectively. This ensures that if a solution is achievable for
\alpha_{i-1}>\alpha_{i}
or
\varepsilon_{j-1}<\varepsilon_{j}
, then it is also achievable for
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j})
. Note the input
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha_{0}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}=\mathcal{U}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{0}}=\emptyset
for all
i,j
makes line 7 valid even for
i,j=1
.

Once
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}
is set as described above, in lines 8-11, we add extra points to it as follows. The values in set
\mathcal{B^{\prime}}_{j}
are set to
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon_{j}+\frac{k(\varepsilon_{j+1}-\varepsilon_{j})}{n_{j}},0\leq k\leq n_{j}-1
, during initialization. For each
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B^{\prime}}_{j}
, we compute
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{\infty}
, the vertices of
\Pi_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
according to (
14
) and add those elements
\mathbf{V}\in\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{\infty}
such that their corresponding
\alpha
-lift is smaller than or equal to
e^{\varepsilon_{j}}
, but bigger than or equal to
(1-\delta)e^{\varepsilon_{j}}
for some small
\delta
.
For the largest value
\varepsilon_{|\mathcal{B}|}
, we use the auxiliary input
\varepsilon_{|\mathcal{B}|+1}>\varepsilon_{|\mathcal{B}|}
and interpolate
n_{|\mathcal{B}|}
points between them to estimate
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{|\mathcal{B}|}}
.

Finally, with
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}
in hand, in line 12 we solve (
15
) for
(\alpha_{i},\varepsilon_{j})
and calculate the utility by the derived
\mathbf{P}_{Y|X}
and
\mathbf{P}_{Y}
from (
16
)-(
18
). The answer is saved as
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}
for the following steps in the loops. In the next section, we examine the efficacy of this method via numerical evaluation.

IV
Numerical Results

We evaluate Algorithm
1
via numerical simulations. We have generated
100
distributions
\mathbf{P}_{SX}
with
|\mathcal{S}|=6
and
|\mathcal{X}|=10
, and the presented results are averaged over these distributions where

\mathcal{A}=\{\infty,100,10,3,1.5\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{0.005,0.01,0.015,\cdots,0.95\}
.
We set
\delta=10^{-2}
, leading to a
1\%
estimation error for extreme vectors.
We also set
\varepsilon_{|\mathcal{B}|+1}=1
and
n_{j}=3
for all
j
. The averaged value of
\varepsilon_{\alpha_{i}}^{\text{max}}
is shown in Figure
1
by vertical lines with the corresponding color of
\alpha_{i}
.

Figure
1
shows the results versus the
\alpha
-lift privacy budget
\varepsilon
, where the normalized utility,
\frac{I(X:Y)}{H(X)}
, is shown in Fig.
1(a)
,
\alpha
-lift leakage,
\max_{y}\log\ell_{\alpha}\left(\mathbf{P}_{S|Y=y}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{S}\right)
, in Fig.
1(b)
, and max-lift leakage,
\max_{y,s}i(s,y)
, in Fig.
1(c)
, respectively.
These numerical results show a general trend that can be used to interpret the operational significance of choosing suitable values of
\alpha
for PUT optimization.
Fig.
1(a)
shows that the utility is decreasing in
\alpha
, where for a fixed
\varepsilon
, a smaller value of
\alpha
results in higher utility.
One can observe that the utility and privacy leakage values are almost the same for
\alpha=\infty
and
\alpha=100
.
This can be explained by referring to the properties of
\alpha
-lift: for large
\alpha
, larger lift values become dominant and, after some point (e.g., in our simulations
\alpha=100
), only the max-lift is effective, which is equal to
\alpha
-lift for
\alpha=\infty
.

In Fig.
1(b)
, we observe that the increment rate of
\alpha
-leakage decreases after a value of
\varepsilon
depending on the value of
\alpha
.
For instance, consider
\alpha=3
where
\alpha
-leakage increment rate decreases after
\varepsilon\approx 0.37
and remains almost constant for
\varepsilon>0.55
.
The reason is that the values of
0.37
is the mean value of
\varepsilon^{max}_{3}
, after which there is no extremal vector to satisfy
\ell_{\alpha}\left(\mathbf{P}_{S|X}\mathbf{P}_{X|Y=y}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{S}\right)=\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon}
.
If we compare Fig.
1(a)
with Fig.
1(b)
we see that the utility values are very close to
1
after the corresponding
\varepsilon^{max}_{\alpha_{i}}
, which means
\mathbf{P}_{X|Y}
is the unity matrix and there is no privacy.
Figure.
1(c)
depicts max-lift leakage for each
\alpha
and confirms that while
\alpha
-lift is restricted by
\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon}
, max-lift leakage could be much larger than this value. This is a natural property of
\alpha
-lift which is a more relaxed measure compared to the max-lift.
In Fig.
1(d)
, we have compared the utility values in Fig.
1(a)
with the corresponding values given by the subset merging (SM) method in
[
12
]
. Algorithm
1
enhances utility significantly, confirming its near optimality.
Moreover, subset merging does not maintain the properties of
\alpha
-lift privacy for
\alpha=100
as its utility value is less than that of
\alpha=\infty.

Note that utility values for subset merging are very small for
\varepsilon<0.1
and large
\alpha
. These are not shown in order not to clutter the figure.

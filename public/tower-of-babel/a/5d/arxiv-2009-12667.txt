Regularized Topology Learning of unknown Networked Linear Dynamical System excited by Cyclostationary inputs
Initial response to reviewer’s comments

Summary

We express our gratitude to the anonymous reviewers and the associate editor for taking the time and effort to go through the manuscript. Thank you for the constructive feedback and encouragement. We believe we have addressed all the comments of the reviewers.

This article makes significant contributions existing prior-work for passive learning of a network topology learning of dynamical systems involving multiple agents interacting over a directed graph with complex-valued interdependencies, driven by exogenous cyclostationary inputs. We would like to emphasize that determining topology only from passively collected data in the presence of latent nodes adds significant challenges for recovery of network topology with provable guarantees.

We first provide detailed response to the reviewers and provide our response to AE comments in the last section.

Response:

On the lifting approach
: We agree with the reviewer that cyclostationary generalization can be dealt by lifted WSS process. However, the lifting process does raise challenges where matricial entities have to be analyzed. Furthermore, the inferences about topology still have to be inferred about agents, though after lifting each agent now corresponds to a vector time-series. As the article demonstrates, a boolean decision of a link being present or not does introduce subtleties. Moreover, computational tractability also becomes important to resolve. Regardless of the ease or difficulty, the impact of the generalization is substantial as the applicability of the algorithms is vastly increased and deserves attention. We also note that the new revision is in the brief paper format.

On the Conditional Independence based approaches:
The approaches for estimating network topology based on latent-variable graphical models using conditional independence (that we are aware of) are restricted to recovering the moral graph. For a given directed graph

the topology is the undirected graph

, where
\mathcal{E}_{M}:=\{(i,j)|j\in\mathcal{V},i\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(j)\cup\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}(j)\cup\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G}}(j)\}.
The moral graph (also known as conditional independence graph), which may contain substantial additional edges (

Comments:
For the topology identification using partial observations, Assumption 19 (now Assumption
7
in the revised article) is quite strong. If this is not satisfied, is there any remedy for Algorithm 3 to yield the consistent identification.

Response:

For a bidirected generative graph with tree topology, the distance between the hidden nodes is crucial. Indeed, it is a fundamental identifiability issue; assumptions on the structure or the distance between hidden nodes are essential. Note that the problem is very challenging as the number of latent nodes is also not assumed to be known and has to be determined from only observed data. Another route is to introduce more structural constraints; for example, when the generative graph is not bidirected, then two hidden nodes can be placed at three hops away such that Markov blankets of the two hidden nodes do not overlap to recover reconstructability. Please refer to the discussion on the placement of the hidden nodes from
[
12
]
. Please also note that the assumptions are required to prove correct reconstructability; the algorithm is data-driven and thus can be applied to time-series data even if the underlying data-generative model is not known or does not satisfy the assumptions. We have applied our methods to Ocean currents reconstruction from temperature data; the underlying physics is quite complex and nonlinear. A good reconstruction indicates the reasonableness of the model assumptions.

Comments:
In the numerical simulation part, an empirical threshold
\tau
is adopted to yield the topology identification. Is there any theoretical guidance for selecting
\tau
in order to yield the exact topology recovery as claimed in this paper.

Response:

The sample complexity analysis (determining
\tau
) of the regularized Wiener filter estimator (
8
) is not a focus of the article. When the process are wide-sense stationary, then the sample-complexity of the optimization problem (
8
) is addressed in authors’ work
[
4
]
; the tools and mathematics are quite elaborate and different from the one employed in this article. Theoretical guarantees on the choice of the regularization parameter, threshold, sample size
n,N
are a part of our future work and outside the scope of the present work.

We present next the ad-hoc but rational procedure we have employed on determining
\tau.

For a given threshold
\tau
we increase sample size till the graph does not change much for any further increment of the sample size. A large
\tau
implies the links detected have a higher chance of being true; so false detection is lessened while missed true links is likely larger with higher
tau.
we start the simulation with a large
\tau
and mark the links detected as being "true" links. Decreasing
\tau
reveals more true links and possibly more spurious links. Also typically there is a range of
\tau
where the number of links detected does not increase; this observation is speculative. For the data employed this
\tau
was found to be
0.3
.

On the issue of regularizer:

On the issue of amount of data:

On references: One of the reviewers (for Review 340799) has alluded to references on approaches that are based on conditional independence (CI) tests without providing specifics; we have provided a response and would appreciate it if more specifics are provided. A large emphasis of the article is on removing spurious edges in moral graphs that are problematic for interaction graphs that have bidirected links. CI tests largely recover the moral graphs.

In summary, we believe the theory we have developed for reconstruction of interaction network of cyclostationary processes with and without hidden nodes in the asymptotic limit of infinite data size for a large class of systems meets the standards of a brief paper in Automatica. The simulations with and without regularizer, for simulated data and with real data provide evidence of the applicability. Again we believe with the applicability evidence on real field data (where certainly much more work can be done by researchers in this area), we believe the article makes a strong brief paper.

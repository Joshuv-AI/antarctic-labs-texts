Non-Hermitian Topology in Hermitian Topological Matter
Shu Hamanaka, Tsuneya Yoshida, Kohei Kawabata

Abstract
Non-Hermiticity gives rise to distinctive topological phenomena absent in Hermitian systems. However, connection between such intrinsic non-Hermitian topology and Hermitian topology has remained largely elusive. Here, considering the bulk and boundary as an environment and system, respectively, we demonstrate that anomalous boundary states in Hermitian topological insulators exhibit non-Hermitian topology. We study the self-energy capturing the particle exchange between the bulk and boundary, and show that it detects Hermitian topology in the bulk and induces non-Hermitian topology at the boundary. As an illustrative example, we reveal non-Hermitian topology and concomitant skin effect inherently embedded within chiral edge states of Chern insulators. We also identify the emergence of hinge states within effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonians at surfaces of three-dimensional topological insulators. Furthermore, we comprehensively classify our correspondence across all the tenfold symmetry classes of topological insulators and superconductors. Our work uncovers hidden connection between Hermitian and non-Hermitian topology, and provides an approach to identifying non-Hermitian topology in quantum matter.

Non-Hermitian Hamiltonians in closed systems
.—Our central idea involves conceptually dividing the bulk and boundary of a closed system into an
environment
and
system
, respectively (Fig.
1
).
The single-particle Hamiltonian of the entire system reads

H=\begin{pmatrix}H_{\rm bulk}&T\\
T^{{\dagger}}&H_{\rm edge}\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
H_{\rm bulk}
(
H_{\rm edge}
) is the Hamiltonian in the bulk (at the boundary), and
T
denotes the coupling between the bulk and boundary.
From the original Schrödinger equation
H\,(\ket{\psi}_{\rm bulk}\,\ket{\psi}_{\rm edge})^{T}=\left(E+\text{i}\eta\right)(\ket{\psi}_{\rm bulk}\,\ket{\psi}_{\rm edge})^{T}
with an infinitesimal number
\eta>0
reflecting causality, we project the boundary degree of freedom and derive the effective Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{eff}}\left(E\right)=H_{\mathrm{edge}}+\Sigma\left(E\right)
with the self-energy
Datta 1995
;
Moiseyev 2011
;
sup

\Sigma\left(E\right)\coloneqq T^{{\dagger}}\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}T.

(2)

The self-energy
\Sigma\left(E\right)
captures the continuous exchange of particles between the
bulk and boundary for given energy
E\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consequently,
\Sigma\left(E\right)
acquires non-Hermiticity, describing finite lifetimes of quasiparticles that escape from the boundary to the bulk.
Non-Hermiticity of
\Sigma\left(E\right)
emerges through the sufficiently strong bulk-boundary coupling
T
, which cannot be captured by the Lindblad master equation due to its inherent weak coupling assumption
Gorini et al. 1976
;
Lindblad 1976
;
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
.

Crucially, topology of the original Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
should leave an imprint on that of the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\rm eff}\left(E\right)
.
For example, when
H
is a quantum Hall (Chern) insulator,
H_{\rm eff}\left(E\right)
effectively describes chiral edge states for
E
within an energy gap.
Intuitively, chirality or nonreciprocity of the anomalous boundary states should yield non-Hermitian topology, as can be seen in the celebrated Hatano-Nelson model
Hatano and Nelson 1996
;
*Hatano-Nelson-97
.
In this Letter, we substantiate this intuition and demonstrate that the self-energy bridges Hermitian bulk topology and non-Hermitian boundary topology.
Below, for fixed
E
(i.e., Markov approximation

;
Hatano 2021
),
we study non-Hermitian topology of
H_{\rm eff}\left(E\right)
in prototypical topological insulators.

The Bloch Hamiltonian reads

(3)

In fact,

Discussions
.—In this Letter, we reveal inherent non-Hermitian topology of anomalous boundary states in Hermitian topological matter.
We demonstrate that the self-energy quantifying the particle exchange between the bulk and boundary detects Hermitian topology in the bulk and non-Hermitian topology at the boundary.
While point-gap topology of anomalous boundary states was investigated in the presence of non-Hermitian external perturbations
Ma et al. 2024
;
Schindler et al. 2023
;
Nakamura et al. 2023
, we here show non-Hermitian topology inherently embedded within Hermitian topological boundary states.

While this Letter focuses on clean noninteracting systems, our formalism should be extended to topological matter with disorder or many-body interactions, which we investigate in future work.
For example, we can revisit the interaction-induced reduction of topological classification from our non-Hermitian perspective
Fidkowski and Kitaev 2010
;
*Fidkowski-11
.
Additionally, it merits further study to develop a field-theoretical understanding of our correspondence.
Our finding implies that anomaly of topological boundary states has close connection with a different type of anomaly accompanying non-Hermitian topology
Kawabata et al. 2021
.
It also provides a microscopic approach to realizing anomalous states and circumventing the fermion doubling theorem in non-Hermitian lattice models
Nielsen and Ninomiya 1981a
;
*NN-81b
;
Karsten 1981
;
Fujikawa and Suzuki 2004
;
Armitage et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2023
.

I
Effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

We consider an edge Hamiltonian
H_{\rm edge}
that is attached to a bulk Hamiltonian
H_{\rm bulk}
.
The whole system is described as

H=\begin{pmatrix}H_{\rm bulk}&T\\
T^{{\dagger}}&H_{\rm edge}\end{pmatrix},

(I.1)

where
T
denotes the coupling between the bulk and the edge.
The Green’s function
G
of
H
is defined by
Datta 1995

\left[\left(E+\text{i}\eta\right)-H\right]G=1,

(I.2)

or

\begin{pmatrix}E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}&-T\\
-T^{{\dagger}}&E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm edge}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}G_{\rm bulk}&G_{\rm bulk-edge}\\
G_{\rm edge-bulk}&G_{\rm edge}\end{pmatrix}=1,

(I.3)

with an infinitesimal number
\eta>0
.
This equation reads

\displaystyle\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}\right)G_{\rm bulk-edge}-TG_{\rm edge}

\displaystyle=0,

(I.4)

\displaystyle-T^{{\dagger}}G_{\rm bulk-edge}+\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm edge}\right)G_{\rm edge}

\displaystyle=1,

(I.5)

leading to

G_{\rm bulk-edge}=\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}TG_{\rm edge}\eqqcolon G_{\rm bulk}^{(0)}TG_{\rm edge},

(I.6)

with the Green’s function
G_{\rm bulk}^{(0)}\coloneqq\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}
of the isolated bulk.
Moreover, we have

\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm edge}-\Sigma\right)G_{\rm edge}=1,

(I.7)

where we introduce the self-energy
\Sigma
of the bulk as

\Sigma\coloneqq T^{{\dagger}}\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}T=T^{{\dagger}}G_{\rm bulk}^{(0)}T.

(I.8)

The effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian at the edge reads

H_{\rm eff}\coloneqq H_{\rm edge}+\Sigma=H_{\rm edge}+T^{{\dagger}}\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}T.

(I.9)

Let
E_{n}
be an eigenenergy of
H_{\rm bulk}
and
\ket{\psi_{n}}
be the corresponding eigenstate.
Then, we have the spectral decomposition

\Sigma=T^{{\dagger}}\left(\sum_{n}\frac{\ket{\psi_{n}}\bra{\psi_{n}}}{E+\text{i}\eta-E_{n}}\right)T.

(I.10)

Example

As the simplest example, we consider a single-band model
Ryndyk et al. 2009

H_{ij}=t\left(\delta_{i,j+1}+\delta_{i,j-1}\right)\quad\left(t>0\right).

(I.11)

Let
\ket{\psi_n}=(\psi_{1}^{(n)},\psi_{2}^{(n)},\cdots,\psi_{L}^{(n)})
be an eigenstate of
H_{\rm bulk}
.
Then, the Schrödinger equation
H\ket{\psi_n}=E_{n}\ket{\psi_n}
reads

t\psi_{j-1}^{(n)}+t\psi_{j+1}^{(n)}=E_{n}\psi_{j}^{(n)}\quad\left(j=2,3,\cdots,L-1\right),\quad\psi_{0}^{(n)}=\psi_{L+1}^{(n)}=0.

(I.12)

The eigenenergies and eigenstates are given as

E_{n}=2t\cos k_{n},\quad\psi_{j}^{(n)}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L+1}}\sin\left(k_{n}j\right);\quad k_{n}\coloneqq\frac{n\pi}{L+1}\quad\left(n=1,2,\cdots,L\right).

(I.13)

Thus, the self-energy of the bulk is obtained as

\Sigma=t^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{L}\frac{|\psi_{j}^{(n)}|^{2}}{E+\text{i}\eta-E_{n}}=\frac{2t^{2}}{L+1}\sum_{n=1}^{L}\frac{\sin^{2}k_{n}}{E+\text{i}\eta-2t\cos k_{n}}.

(I.14)

In the semi-infinite limit
L\to\infty
, we have

\Sigma=2t^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{L}\frac{\Delta k_{n}}{\pi}\frac{\sin^{2}k_{n}}{E+\text{i}\eta-2t\cos k_{n}}\to 2t^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dk}{\pi}\frac{\sin^{2}k}{E+\text{i}\eta-2t\cos k}=\frac{E}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4t^{2}}{E^{2}}}\right).

(I.15)

The imaginary part of
\Sigma
appears for
E
within the bulk bandwidth
\left|E\right|<2t
, which reflects the particle exchange between the bulk and the edge.

II
Symmetry classification of non-Hermitian self-energy

We study symmetry of effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonians introduced in Sec.
I
.
Specifically, we show that when original Hermitian Hamiltonians belong to the Altland-Zirnbauer symmetry class
Altland and Zirnbauer 1997
, the corresponding effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonians belong to the Altland-Zirnbauer
†
symmetry class
Kawabata et al. 2019b
.
The similar correspondence was derived, for example, for quadratic Lindbladians
Lieu et al. 2020
and reflection matrices in scattering processes
Kawabata et al. 2023
.
Because of this correspondence, the topological classification of
d
-dimensional Hermitian Hamiltonians coincides with that of
\left(d-1\right)
-dimensional effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonians
Kawabata et al. 2019b
;
Lee et al. 2019b
;
Bessho and Sato 2021
;
Schindler et al. 2023
;
Nakamura et al. 2023
.

II.1
Time-reversal symmetry

Suppose that the original Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
respects time-reversal symmetry:

\mathcal{T}H^{*}\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H

(II.1)

with a unitary matrix
\mathcal{T}
.
Since time reversal acts only on the internal degrees of freedom, we have

\mathcal{T}H^{*}_{\rm bulk}\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H_{\rm bulk},\quad\mathcal{T}H^{*}_{\rm edge}\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H_{\rm edge},\quad\mathcal{T}T^{*}\mathcal{T}^{-1}=T.

(II.2)

Consequently, we have

\mathcal{T}\Sigma^{T}\left(E\right)\mathcal{T}^{-1}=T^{{\dagger}}\left(E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}T=\Sigma\left(E\right),

(II.3)

and hence

\mathcal{T}H_{\rm eff}^{T}\left(E\right)\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H_{\rm eff}\left(E\right).

(II.4)

Thus, for arbitrary
E
, the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\rm eff}
respects time-reversal symmetry
†

Kawabata et al. 2019b
.
Notably,
H_{\rm eff}
is not invariant under time reversal (i.e.,
\mathcal{T}H_{\rm eff}^{*}\mathcal{T}^{-1}\neq H_{\rm eff}
).

II.2
Particle-hole symmetry

Suppose that the original Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
respects particle-hole symmetry:

\mathcal{C}H^{*}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-H

(II.5)

with a unitary matrix
\mathcal{C}
.
Since particle-hole transformation acts only on the internal degrees of freedom, we have

\mathcal{C}H^{*}_{\rm bulk}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-H_{\rm bulk},\quad\mathcal{C}H^{*}_{\rm edge}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-H_{\rm edge},\quad\mathcal{C}T^{*}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-T.

(II.6)

Consequently, we have

\mathcal{C}\Sigma^{*}\left(E\right)\mathcal{C}^{-1}=T^{{\dagger}}\left(E-\text{i}\eta+H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}T=-\Sigma\left(-E\right),

(II.7)

and hence

\mathcal{C}H_{\rm eff}^{*}\left(E\right)\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-H_{\rm eff}\left(-E\right).

(II.8)

Thus, at the particle-hole-symmetric point
E=0
, the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\rm eff}\left(E=0\right)
respects particle-hole symmetry
†

Kawabata et al. 2019b
.
Notably,
H_{\rm eff}
does not respect particle-hole symmetry even at
E=0
[i.e.,
\mathcal{C}H_{\rm eff}^{T}\left(E=0\right)\mathcal{C}^{-1}\neq-H_{\rm eff}\left(E=0\right)
].

II.3
Chiral symmetry

Suppose that the original Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
respects chiral symmetry:

\Gamma H\Gamma^{-1}=-H

(II.9)

with a unitary matrix
\Gamma
.
Since chiral transformation acts only on the internal degrees of freedom, we have

\Gamma H_{\rm bulk}\Gamma^{-1}=-H_{\rm bulk},\quad\Gamma H_{\rm edge}\Gamma^{-1}=-H_{\rm edge},\quad\Gamma T\Gamma^{-1}=-T.

(II.10)

Consequently, we have

\Gamma\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\left(E\right)\Gamma^{-1}=T^{{\dagger}}\left(E-\text{i}\eta+H_{\rm bulk}\right)^{-1}T=-\Sigma\left(-E\right),

(II.11)

and hence

\Gamma H_{\rm eff}^{{\dagger}}\left(E\right)\Gamma^{-1}=-H_{\rm eff}\left(-E\right).

(II.12)

Thus, at the chiral-symmetric point
E=0
, the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\rm eff}\left(E=0\right)
respects chiral symmetry
Kawabata et al. 2019b
.
Notably,
H_{\rm eff}
does not respect sublattice symmetry even at
E=0
[i.e.,
\Gamma H_{\rm eff}\left(E=0\right)\Gamma^{-1}\neq-H_{\rm eff}\left(E=0\right)
].

\beta\left(k_{y}\right)
are given as

\lambda_{\mathrm{edge}}(k_{y})=t\sin{k_y},\quad\beta(k_{y})=-\left(\frac{m}{t}+\cos k_{y}\right).

(III.26)

Thus, the self-energy is calculated as

\Sigma_{\mathrm{edge}}(k_{y})=\frac{t^{2}(1-\beta^{2})}{2(E+\text{i}\eta-t\sin k_{y})}(\sigma_{0}-\sigma_{y}).

(III.27)

For
k_{y}=0
or
k_{y}=\pi
, this self-energy essentially reduces to that of the SSH model [see Eq. (
III.5
)].
Notably, these expressions are valid only for

1>\absolutevalue{\beta}=\absolutevalue{\frac{m}{t}+\cos{k_y}}

(III.28)

so that the above chiral edge state can be normalized.

IV
Current formula

We derive the current formula in Eq. (
8
) of the main text (see also
“Chapter 8: Non-equilibrium Green’s function formalism” in Ref.
Datta 1995
, as well as
“Chapter 9: Coherent transport” and “Appendix: advanced formalism” in Ref.
Datta 2005
).
We consider a noninteracting fermionic Hamiltonian described by

\hat{H}=\sum_{i,j}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}H_{ij}\hat{a}_{j},

(IV.1)

where
\hat{a}_{i}
(
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}
) is a fermionic annihilation (creation) operator at site
i
,
and
H_{ij}
is a single-particle
Hermitian
Hamiltonian.
The Heisenberg equation for the annihilation operator
reads

\text{i}\hbar\frac{d\hat{a}_{i}(t)}{dt}=[\hat{a}_{i}(t),\hat{H}]=\sum_{j}H_{ij}\hat{a}_{j}(t).

(IV.2)

We rewrite this equation as

\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t)\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}H_{\rm bulk}&T\\
T^{{\dagger}}&H_{\rm edge}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t)\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t)\end{pmatrix},

(IV.3)

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t)
[
\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t)
] is a row vector
of annihilation operators
acting on the bulk (edge).
We expand the annihilation operator for the bulk as

\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t)=\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t)+\hat{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(t),

(IV.4)

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t)
denotes the unperturbed annihilation operator satisfying
\text{i}\hbar\left(d/dt\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t)=H_{\rm bulk}\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t)
.
From the original Heisenberg equation in Eq. (
IV.3
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}-H_{\rm bulk}\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(t)

\displaystyle=T\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t),

(IV.5)

\displaystyle\left(\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}-H_{\rm edge}\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t)

\displaystyle=T^{\dagger}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t)+\hat{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(t)\right).

(IV.6)

We introduce the retarded Green’s function
g^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t-t^{\prime})
for the bulk

\left(\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}-H_{\rm bulk}\right)g^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t-t^{\prime})=\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(IV.7)

Here, the argument of the Green’s function depends only on the time difference
t-t^{\prime}
since the Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{bulk}}
is independent of time.
Using the Green’s function, we obtain the formal solution of Eq. (
IV.5
) by the convolution integral,

\hat{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(t)=\int dt^{\prime}g^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t-t^{\prime})T\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime}),

(IV.8)

and hence

\left(\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}-H_{\rm edge}\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t)=T^{\dagger}\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t)+\int dt^{\prime}\Sigma(t-t^{\prime})\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime})

(IV.9)

with the self-energy defined as

\Sigma(t-t^{\prime})\coloneqq T^{\dagger}g^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t-t^{\prime})T.

(IV.10)

Similarly, introducing the retarded Green’s function
g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t-t^{\prime})
for the edge
by

\left(\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}-H_{\rm edge}\right)g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t-t^{\prime})=\int dt^{\prime\prime}\Sigma(t-t^{\prime\prime})g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime})+\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(IV.11)

we obtain the formal solution of Eq. (
IV.9
) as

\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}(t)=\int dt^{\prime}g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t-t^{\prime})T^{\dagger}\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t^{\prime}).

(IV.12)

Similar calculations for the creation operator lead to

\left(\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}+H_{\rm edge}^{T}\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(t)=-\int dt^{\prime}\Sigma^{*}(t-t^{\prime})\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})-T^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)^{{\dagger}}}(t)

(IV.13)

and hence

\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(t)=\int dt^{\prime}g_{\mathrm{edge}}^{*}(t-t^{\prime})T^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)^{{\dagger}}}(t^{\prime}).

(IV.14)

We define the Fourier transform of
f(t-t^{\prime})
as

\mathcal{F}\left[f(t-t^{\prime})\right]\coloneqq\int d(t-t^{\prime})e^{\text{i}\frac{E}{\hbar}(t-t^{\prime})}f(t-t^{\prime}).

(IV.15)

The Fourier transforms of the Green’s functions
g^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(t-t^{\prime})
in Eq. (
IV.7
) and
g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t-t^{\prime})
in Eq. (
IV.11
) are

G_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\left[g_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime})\right]=\frac{1}{E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\mathrm{bulk}}},\quad G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\left[g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t-t^{\prime})\right]=\frac{1}{E+\text{i}\eta-H_{\mathrm{edge}}-\Sigma(E)},

(IV.16)

with
\Sigma(E)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\left[\Sigma(t-t^{\prime})\right]
.
Here, an infinitesimal number
\eta>0
is introduced so that the initial condition can be satisfied appropriately.

Now, we introduce the two-time current operator
Datta 1995
;
Datta 2005

J(t,t^{\prime})\coloneqq\left(\frac{d}{dt}+\frac{d}{dt^{\prime}}\right)C(t,t^{\prime}),

(IV.17)

with the two-time correlation function

C_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})\coloneqq\langle\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\hat{a}_{i}(t)\rangle,

(IV.18)

where the bracket denotes the average over a thermal equilibrium state.
In the limit
t^{\prime}\rightarrow t
, the diagonal element of
J(t,t^{\prime})
gives the local current.
From Eqs. (
IV.12
) and (
IV.13
), the correlation function of the edge,

C_{\mathrm{edge};ij}(t,t^{\prime})\coloneqq\langle\hat{a}_{{\mathrm{edge}};j}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\hat{a}_{{\mathrm{edge}};i}(t)\rangle
,
satisfies

\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt}C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime})=-C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime})H_{\mathrm{edge}}-\int dt^{\prime\prime}g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t,t^{\prime\prime})T-\int dt^{\prime\prime}C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})\Sigma^{\dagger}(t,t^{\prime\prime}),

(IV.19)

with the isolated bulk correlation function

C_{\mathrm{bulk};ij}^{(0)}(t,t^{\prime})\coloneqq\langle\hat{a}^{(0)^{{\dagger}}}_{\mathrm{bulk};j}(t^{\prime})\hat{a}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk};i}(t)\rangle
.
Here, we assume that the bulk maintains the local equilibrium even after attaching the edge
Datta 1995
;
Datta 2005
.
Similarly, from Eqs. (
IV.9
) and (
IV.14
), we have

\text{i}\hbar\frac{d}{dt^{\prime}}C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime})=H_{\mathrm{edge}}C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime})+\int dt^{\prime\prime}T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})Tg_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(t,t^{\prime\prime})+\int dt^{\prime\prime}\Sigma(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime\prime}).

(IV.20)

The first term of the right-hand side in Eqs. (
IV.19
) and (
IV.20
) represents the particle current inside the edge; we below omit it since it is irrelevant to the current between the bulk and edge, and hence non-Hermitian topology.
Additionally, the second (third) term in Eqs. (
IV.19
) and (
IV.20
) describes the particle current from the bulk to the edge (from the edge to the bulk).
Then, the net current is

J(t,t^{\prime})=J_{\mathrm{inflow}}(t,t^{\prime})-J_{\mathrm{outflow}}(t,t^{\prime})

(IV.21)

with

\displaystyle\text{i}\hbar J_{\mathrm{inflow}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\int dt^{\prime\prime}g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t,t^{\prime\prime})T+\int dt^{\prime\prime}T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})Tg_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(t,t^{\prime\prime}),

(IV.22)

\displaystyle\text{i}\hbar J_{\mathrm{outflow}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int dt^{\prime\prime}C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})\Sigma^{\dagger}(t,t^{\prime\prime})-\int dt^{\prime\prime}\Sigma(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime\prime}).

(IV.23)

At thermal equilibrium, from the cyclicity of the trace, the correlation functions
C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t,t^{\prime})
and
C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime})
depend only on the argument
t-t^{\prime}
.
After the Fourier transformation, the
E
-resolved currents read,

\displaystyle\text{i}\hbar J_{\mathrm{inflow}}(E)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\left[J_{\mathrm{inflow}}(t-t^{\prime})\right]=-G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)T+T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)TG_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(E),

(IV.24)

\displaystyle\text{i}\hbar J_{\mathrm{outflow}}(E)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\left[J_{\mathrm{outflow}}(t-t^{\prime})\right]=C_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\Sigma^{\dagger}(E)-\Sigma(E)C_{\mathrm{edge}}(E).

(IV.25)

To calculate the correlation function
C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)
of the isolated bulk,
we diagonalize the Hamiltonian,

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{bulk}}=\sum_{n}E_{n}\hat{\gamma}^{{(0)}^{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{bulk};n}\hat{\gamma}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk};n},\quad\hat{\gamma}^{(0)^{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{bulk};n}\coloneqq\sum_{m}\hat{a}^{(0)^{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{bulk};m}U_{mn},

(IV.26)

where
E_{n}
is eigenenergy of the single-particle Hamiltonian
H_{\rm bulk}
, and
U_{mn}
is a unitary matrix of the single-particle eigenstates.
Then, we have

C_{\mathrm{bulk};ij}^{(0)}(E)=\sum\limits_{k}U_{ik}U^{\dagger}_{kj}\delta(E-E_{k})\langle\hat{\gamma}^{(0)^{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{bulk};k}\hat{\gamma}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk};k}\rangle=f_{0}\left(E\right)\sum_{k}U_{ik}U_{kj}^{{\dagger}}\delta\left(E-E_{k}\right),

(IV.27)

with the Fermi distribution function
f_{0}\left(E_{k}\right)=\langle\hat{\gamma}^{(0)^{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{bulk};k}\hat{\gamma}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{bulk};k}\rangle
.
Using the Green’s function in Eq. (
IV.16
), i.e.,

G_{\mathrm{bulk};ij}^{(0)}(E)=\sum_{k}\frac{U_{ik}U^{{\dagger}}_{kj}}{E-E_{k}+\text{i}\eta}=\sum_{k}U_{ik}U^{{\dagger}}_{kj}\left[\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{1}{E-E_{k}}\right)-\text{i}\pi\delta\left(E-E_{k}\right)\right],

(IV.28)

we have

C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)=\frac{\text{i}f_{0}(E)}{2\pi}\left[G_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)-G_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)^{\dagger}}(E)\right],

(IV.29)

and hence

T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)T=\frac{\text{i}f_{0}(E)}{2\pi}\left[\Sigma(E)-\Sigma^{\dagger}(E)\right]=\frac{f_{0}(E)}{2\pi}\Gamma(E),

(IV.30)

with the broadening matrix

\Gamma(E)\coloneqq\text{i}\left[\Sigma(E)-\Sigma^{\dagger}(E)\right].

(IV.31)

Next, from Eqs. (
IV.12
) and (
IV.14
), we have

C_{\mathrm{edge}}(t,t^{\prime})=\int dt_{1}\int dt_{2}g_{\mathrm{edge}}(t^{\prime},t_{2})T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(t_{2},t_{1})Tg_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(t,t_{1})

(IV.32)

and hence

C_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)=G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)T^{\dagger}C_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(E)TG_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(E),

(IV.33)

further leading to

C_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)=\frac{f_{0}(E)}{2\pi}G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\Gamma(E)G_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(E)=\frac{f_{0}(E)}{2\pi}A_{\mathrm{edge}}(E),

(IV.34)

where
A_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)
is the spectral function

A_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\coloneqq\text{i}\left[G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)-G_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(E)\right]=G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\Gamma(E)G_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(E).

(IV.35)

Then, from Eqs. (
IV.30
) and (
IV.34
), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{inflow}}(E)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}(E)}{2\pi\text{i}\hbar}\left[\Gamma(E)G_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\dagger}(E)-G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\Gamma(E)\right],

(IV.36)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{outflow}}(E)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}(E)}{2\pi\text{i}\hbar}\left[A_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\Sigma^{\dagger}(E)-\Sigma(E)A_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\right],

(IV.37)

and hence

J(E)=J_{\mathrm{inflow}}(E)-J_{\mathrm{outflow}}(E)=\frac{f_{0}(E)}{2\pi\hbar}\left(\left[\Sigma(E),G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\right]+\left[\Sigma(E),G_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)\right]^{\dagger}\right)

(IV.38)

with the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{eff}}(E)\coloneqq H_{\mathrm{edge}}+\Sigma(E)
.
From the relation

\big[\Sigma,G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]+\big[\Sigma,G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]^{\dagger}=-\big[H_{\mathrm{edge}},G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]-\big[H_{\mathrm{edge}},G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]^{\dagger},

(IV.39)

Eq. (
IV.38
) reduces to Eq. (
8
) in the main text.
In passing, “
f_{0}/2\pi\hbar
” is dropped in Eq. (
8
).

We also introduce the
E
-resolved current density
j(y,E)
at site
y
,
summing up the internal degree of freedom
\alpha
as

j(y,E)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{\alpha}\bra{y,\alpha}\left(\big[\Sigma,G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]+\big[\Sigma,G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]^{\dagger}\right)\ket{y,\alpha}.

(IV.40)

Notably, while
j\left(y,E\right)
can be nonzero, the net current
\sum_{y=1}^{L}j\left(y,E\right)
flowing between the bulk and edge always vanishes owing to the cyclicity of the trace:

\sum_{y=1}^{L}j\left(y,E\right)=\Tr\left(-\big[H_{\mathrm{edge}},G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]-\big[H_{\mathrm{edge}},G_{\mathrm{edge}}\big]^{\dagger}\right)=0,

(IV.41)

where the trace is taken over both sites
y
and the internal degrees
\alpha
of freedom.
This is consistent with the absence of net current at equilibrium
Datta 2005
.

Additionally, the current density
j(y,E)
vanishes under the periodic boundary conditions along the
y
direction.
Owing to translation invariance, the Hamiltonians can be written as

H_{\mathrm{edge}}=\sum_{k;\alpha,\beta}h_{\mathrm{edge}}^{\alpha\beta}(k)\ket{k,\alpha}\bra{k, \beta},\quad H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{k;\alpha,\beta}h_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\alpha\beta}(k)\ket{k,\alpha}\bra{k, \beta},

(IV.42)

where
k
is momentum, and
\alpha
and
\beta
specify the internal degree of freedom.
Then, from the cyclicity of the trace, we have

\Tr_{\alpha}[h_{\mathrm{edge}}(k),h_{\mathrm{eff}}(k)]=0,

(IV.43)

where the trace is taken only for the internal degree of freedom.
Hence, we have
\Tr_{\alpha}[H_{\mathrm{edge}},H_{\mathrm{eff}}]=0
and

j(y,E)=-\bra{y}\Tr_{\alpha}\left([H_{\mathrm{edge}},G_{\mathrm{edge}}]+[H_{\mathrm{edge}},G_{\mathrm{edge}}]^{{\dagger}}\right)\ket{y}=0.

(IV.44)

This is consistent with the absence of the local current under the periodic boundary conditions, as shown in Fig.
4
(c) of the main text.

V
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
skin effect in time-reversal-invariant topological insulators

We demonstrate the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
skin effect in a time-reversal-invariant topological insulator.
As a prototypical model, we study the Bernevig-Hughes-Zhang (BHZ) model
Bernevig et al. 2006
with the spin-orbit coupling,

H_{\mathrm{BHZ}}\left(k_{x},k_{y}\right)=\left(t\sin{k_x}\right)\sigma_{z}\tau_{x}+\left(t\sin{k_y}\right)\tau_{y}+\left(m+t\cos{k_x}+t\cos{k_y}\right)\tau_{z}+\Delta\sigma_{x}\tau_{y},

(V.1)

where
\sigma_{i}
’s and
\tau_{i}
’s (
i=x,y,z
) are Pauli matrices, and
t,m,\Delta\in\mathbb{R}
are real parameters.
The BHZ model preserves time-reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}H\left(\bm{k}\right)\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H\left(-\bm{k}\right)
with
\mathcal{T}=\text{i}\sigma_{y}\mathcal{K}
(
\mathcal{T}^{2}=-1
) and belongs to class AII, to which the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant is assigned.
We apply the open boundary conditions along the
x
direction and regard the edge at
x=1
as a system and the remaining bulk as an environment.
In Fig.
S1
, we show the right eigenstates of the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian under the open boundary conditions along the
y
direction.
We find that the Kramers pairs of the right eigenstates are localized at the opposite edges, confirming the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
skin effect.

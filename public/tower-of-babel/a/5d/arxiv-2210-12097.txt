Robust Singular Values based on L1-norm PCA
Duc H. Le ‡ and Panos P. Markopoulos †∗

Abstract
Singular-Value Decomposition (SVD) is a ubiquitous data analysis method in engineering, science, and statistics. Singular-value estimation, in particular, is of critical importance in an array of engineering applications, such as channel estimation in communication systems, electromyography signal analysis, and image compression, to name just a few. Conventional SVD of a data matrix coincides with standard Principal-Component Analysis (PCA). The L2-norm (sum of squared values) formulation of PCA promotes peripheral data points and, thus, makes PCA sensitive against outliers. Naturally, SVD inherits this outlier sensitivity. In this work, we present a novel robust non-parametric method for SVD and singular-value estimation based on a L1-norm (sum of absolute values) formulation, which we name L1-cSVD. Accordingly, the proposed method demonstrates sturdy resistance against outliers and can facilitate more reliable data analysis and processing in a wide range of engineering applications.



I
Introduction

Singular-Value Decomposition (SVD) has established itself as a powerful tool, ubiquitous in various engineering applications. For example, applying SVD to the channel matrix of a multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) channel decomposes the MIMO channel into multiple single-input single-output (SISO) channels with gains corresponding to singular values, which enables efficient power allocation and channel capacity estimation
[
1
,
2
]
. Furthermore, SVD has been extensively employed in various watermarking schemes
[
3
,
4
]
, direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation
[
5
,
6
]
, restructuring of deep neural network acoustic models
[
7
]
, electromyography (EMG) signal analysis
[
8
]
, etc.

Another data analysis method that is closely related to SVD is Principal-Component Analysis (PCA), which is also useful in a number of fields, such as machine learning, signal processing, and pattern recognition
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. The traditional PCA method seeks to maximize the L2-norm of the variance of the projected coordinates on the principal components (PCs). However, because of its emphasis on the square of the coordinates, PCA is sensitive against corruption from gross and sparse outliers.

Thus, there has been considerable research effort in reformulating PCA employing the L1-norm instead (L1-PCA), which is able to suppress the effect of extreme data points
[
12
,
13
]
. The exact solution to L1-PCA can be obtained in polynomial time with respect to the number of data points
[
14
]
. However, optimality can be traded for lower computational complexity, which has been implemented in a greedy approach
[
15
]
, a semidefinite programming approach
[
16
]
, an alternating algorithm
[
17
]
, and a bit-flipping algorithm
[
18
]
, just to name a few. Apart from L1-PCA, another line of research that aims to ameliorate the effect of outliers corruption is Robust Principal-Component Analysis (RPCA)
[
19
]
, which strives to decompose a matrix into a sparse and a low-rank component.

On the other hand, besides the PCs, or equivalently the left singular vectors of SVD
[
20
]
, a robust, outlier-resistant acquisition of singular values (SVs) is also of great interest. Regrettably, L1-PCs, while robust against outliers, do not possess the attractive property of their L2-norm counterpart to diagonalize the data matrix
\mathbf{X}

[
21
]
, thus making the extension from L1-PCA to SVs estimation non-trivial.

In this paper, we leverage the robustness of the subspace found by previous L1-PCA algorithms to apply to SVs estimation. To this end, we propose an algorithm named L1-cSVD that finds SVs and right singular vectors from a given L1-subspace by solving a re-orthogonalization problem. We then test the performance of this L1-cSVD algorithm with synthetic and real dataset against the state-of-the-art RPCA, which corroborates the robustness of the proposed algorithm in preserving SVs when facing outliers.

II
Technical Background

II-A

Standard SVD and PCA

SVD decomposes a
D\times N
matrix
\mathbf{X}
as
[
20
]

\displaystyle{\bf X=U\Sigma V}^{T},

(1)

where
{\mathbf{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times d}
and
{\mathbf{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
are orthonormal matrices, defined as the left and right singular vectors respectively,
{\mathbf{\Sigma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a positive-valued diagonal matrix whose diagonal elements are the singular values (SVs), and
d={\rm rank}(\mathbf{X})
. This is the “compact” SVD (cSVD) where the left and right singular vectors corresponding to zero SVs are disregarded
[
20
]
. For simplicity, we will refer to “compact” SVD as SVD throughout this paper.

Standard SVD is very closely related to PCA, since the first
K\,(K\leq d)
left singular vectors of
\bf{U}
are also the first
K
PCs of
\mathbf{X}
, maximizing the L2-norm projection
[
9
]

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{L2}=\underset{\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{S}^{D\times K}}{\rm argmax}{||\mathbf{Q}^{T}\mathbf{X}||}_{2,2},

(2)

where
\mathbb{S}^{D\times K}
denotes the set of orthonormal matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{D\times K}
(Stiefel manifold) and
||\cdot||_{2,2}
denotes the Frobenius or L2-norm of its matrix argument
[
20
]
.

II-B

L1-PCA

The proposed method builds on top of L1-PCA, which is mathematically formulated by replacing the L2-norm in the optimization problem of (
2
) with the L1-norm (sum of absolute values), as

\displaystyle{\bf Q}_{L1}=\underset{\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{S}^{D\times K}}{\rm argmax}||\mathbf{Q}^{T}\mathbf{X}||_{1,1}.

(3)

L1-PCA can be extended to robust SVs estimation by taking the standard SVD of the projected matrix
\mathbf{Q}_{L1}\mathbf{Q}_{L1}^{T}\mathbf{X}
. The optimal solution to (
3
) was presented for the first time in
[
14
]
and has polynomial cost in
N
. In this work, we focus on suboptimal approaches with lower time complexity.

II-B
1
Greedy Algorithm with Successive Nullspace Projection

Kwak
[
15
]
proposed an iterative algorithm to solve (
3
) when
K=1
. The algorithm can be summarized as

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}^{(t)}={\rm sgn}\left(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{b}^{(t-1)}\right),

(4)

t=2,3,4,...
, where
\mathbf{b}^{(1)}\subset\{\pm 1\}^{N}
is an antipodal binary vector that can be randomly initialized. Then, the PC can be approximated to be
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{Xb}/||\mathbf{Xb}||_{2}
. For
K>1
, the PCs of
\mathbf{Q}
are found in a greedy way, by replacing
\mathbf{X}
with its projection onto the nullspace of the previously found PCs. It is important to note that because L1-PCA is not scalable, meaning the PCs themselves are dependent on the number of PCs being found, the greedy approach is suboptimal.

II-B
2
Iterative Alternating Algorithm

Nie et al.
[
17
]
presented a method that finds the column vectors of
\mathbf{Q}
jointly. The iterative algorithm can be summarized as

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{(t)}={\rm sgn}(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{Q}^{(t-1)}),\mathbf{Q}^{(t)}=\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{XB}^{(t)}),

(5)

t=2,3,4,...
, where
\mathcal{U}(\cdot)
returns the closest orthonormal matrix using the Procrustes theorem
[
20
]
and
\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\subset\{\pm 1\}^{N\times K}
is a binary matrix that can be arbitrarily initialized.

II-B
3
Bit-flipping Algorithm

Markopoulos et al.
[
18
]
proposed an algorithm that calculates the effect of flipping any single bit of the binary matrix
\mathbf{B}
on the optimization metric of (
3
) and flips the bit that yields the highest increase to the metric. The algorithm converges to the optimal L1-PCs with high frequency and frequently achieves higher value in the optimization metric of (
3
) than previous alternatives.

II-C

RPCA

Another line of research to the problem of robustly recovering a low-rank structure from a corrupted matrix is RPCA
[
19
]
. This approach seeks to decompose a matrix
\mathbf{X}
into a low-rank component
\mathbf{L}
and a sparse component
\mathbf{S}
that models sparse outliers by solving the problem

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{L,S,L+S=X}}{\rm minimize}||\mathbf{L}||_{*}+\lambda||\mathbf{S}||_{1,1},

(6)

where
(\cdot)_{*}
indicates the nuclear norm (sum of singular values). The problem essentially promotes the sparsity of
\mathbf{S}
by minimizing its L1-norm and the sparsity of the SVs of
\mathbf{L}
by minimizing the nuclear norm of
\mathbf{L}
or equivalently the L1-norm of its SVs, thus making
\mathbf{L}
low-rank. The performance of RPCA depends largely on
\lambda

[
22
]
. In this work, we set
\lambda=1/\sqrt{M}
where
M
is the larger dimension of
\mathbf{X}

[
19
]
. RPCA can be extended to SVs estimation by taking the conventional SVD of the extracted low-rank component, i.e.,
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{\Sigma},\mathbf{V})={\rm SVD}(\mathbf{L})
.

III
Proposed Method

III-A

Proposed Algorithm: L1-cSVD

We now formulate our L1-norm based SVD approach to be

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\approx\mathbf{U}_{L1}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}\mathbf{V}^{T}_{L1},

(7)

where the left and right singular vectors
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
and
\mathbf{V}_{L1}
, respectively, are orthonormal and
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}
is diagonal. As a result, this decomposition has to be an approximated one because the only exact decomposition with such constraints on
\mathbf{U}_{L1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}
and
\mathbf{V}_{L1}
would be the conventional SVD due to its uniqueness property.

We call our algorithm L1-“compact” SVD (L1-cSVD) to emphasize that we only collect
K\leq D\leq N
SVs and singular vectors from
\mathbf{X}
. We carry over the property of SVD that the left singular vectors are also the PCs and set
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
to the L1-PCs
\mathbf{Q}_{L1}
obtained by solving (
3
). This choice of
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
ensures that the subspace found is robust against outliers
[
14
]

[
18
]
. As mentioned in section
II-B
, the problem in Eq. (
3
) has been studied extensively and there are multiple algorithms available to choose from, the importance of which will be discussed in detail in section
III-B
. For now, we assume that a good
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
can be found. In this section, we propose an algorithm to find the SVs
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}
from the left singular vectors
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
.

Conventional SVD has an attractive property which states that the left singular vectors
\mathbf{U}_{L2}=\mathbf{Q}_{L2}
from (
2
) also diagonalize
\mathbf{X}
; i.e.,
\mathbf{U}_{L2}^{T}\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L2}\mathbf{V}_{L2}^{T}
is an orthogonal matrix or equivalently
\mathbf{U}_{L2}^{T}\mathbf{XX}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L2}
is diagonal, and it is the only orthonormal matrix with this property
1
1

1

In this paper, “orthogonal matrix” means a matrix whose column vectors are orthogonal but not necessarily normalized
. Thus, an orthonormal
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
generally cannot diagonalize
\mathbf{X}
, while the formulation of L1-cSVD in (
7
) requires that
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}\mathbf{V}_{L1}^{T}
is orthogonal. As a result, the problem of L1-cSVD becomes finding the closest orthogonal matrix
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}\mathbf{V}_{L1}^{T}
to
\mathbf{U}_{L1}^{T}\mathbf{X}
using the L1-norm,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1},\mathbf{V}_{L1})=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{V}}\in\mathbb{S}^{N\times K,}\\
\mathbf{\Sigma}\in{\rm diag}(\mathbb{R}^{K})\end{subarray}}{\rm argmin}{||\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1}-\mathbf{V}\mathbf{\Sigma}||}_{1,1}.

(8)

This is a non-convex problem due to the orthonormality constraint on
\mathbf{V}

[
23
]
. We will solve for the matrices
\mathbf{\Sigma}
and
\mathbf{V}
suboptimally by an alternating method. For fixed
\mathbf{V}
, finding
\mathbf{\Sigma}
can be equivalently decomposed into
K
individual problems

\displaystyle\underset{\sigma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}}{\rm minimize}{||(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1})_{:,i}-\sigma_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}||}_{1},

(9)

i=1,2,...,K
, where
\sigma_{i}
is the
i^{\rm th}
SV in
\mathbf{\Sigma}
. This problem is simply seeking a scaling factor
\sigma_{i}
that minimizes the L1-distance between vectors
(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1})_{:,i}
and
\sigma_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
. The answer is found in
[
24
]
to be
\sigma_{i}=(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1})_{j_{\rm opt},i}/v_{j_{\rm opt},i}
where

\displaystyle j_{\rm opt}=\underset{j\in\{1,2,...,N\}}{\rm argmin}{\Bigg|\Bigg|(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1})_{:,i}-\frac{(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1})_{j,i}}{v_{j,i}}\mathbf{v}_{i}\Bigg|\Bigg|}_{1}

,

(10)

which performs exhaustive search on
N
candidates for
\sigma_{i}
chosen such that
(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1})_{:,i}
is equal to
\sigma_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
on the
j^{\rm th}
entry. From the
N
candidates, the one that returns the least L1 error will be chosen to be
\sigma_{i}
. On the other hand, for fixed
\mathbf{\Sigma}
,
\mathbf{V}
is found by solving

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{S}^{N\times K}}{\rm minimize}{||\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1}-\mathbf{V}\mathbf{\Sigma}||}_{1,1},

(11)

which is essentially an L1-norm Orthogonal Procrustes problem. A solution to this problem using a smoothed version of the L1-norm has been studied in
[
25
]
. However, in this paper, for lower computational complexity, we will use the solution to the L2-Orthogonal Procrustes problem instead, since it is empirically observed that given the L1-informed
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
and
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}
, the L1 solution for
\mathbf{V}
gives similar result to its L2 counterpart while taking much longer to solve. Thus, we set
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{U^{\prime}V^{\prime}}^{T}
where
{\mathbf{(U^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime},V^{\prime})}}={\rm SVD}(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{L1}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1})
. This result for
\mathbf{V}
is then used to update the SVs
\mathbf{\Sigma}
, which is used to refine
\mathbf{V}
in an alternating fashion until convergence. Upon termination of the alternating updates,
\mathbf{V}_{L1}=\mathbf{V}
and
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}=\mathbf{\Sigma}
. The algorithm can be summarized in the pseudocode in Algorithm 1.

III-B

Importance of Choosing Left Singular Vectors
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
: Joint vs Greedy

As previously mentioned, the L1-PCA problem of Eq. (
3
) finding the left singular vectors
\mathbf{Q}_{L1}=\mathbf{U}_{L1}
, on which
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}
and
\mathbf{V}_{L1}
are dependent, can be solved by Greedy solutions
[
15
]
or Joint solutions
[
17
,
18
]
. Because L1-PCA is not scalable, the Joint solutions have a more optimal
||\mathbf{Q}_{L1}^{T}\mathbf{X}||_{1,1}
metric.

However, since maximizing the L1-norm promotes balance among the columns of
\mathbf{Q}_{L1}^{T}\mathbf{X}
, Joint L1-PCA also inadvertently rotates the L1-PCs along the L1-subspace to make the projections more balanced. Therefore, their L1-PCs are less aligned to the L2-PCs than those found by Greedy L1-PCA, which finds individual L1-PCs without having to balance with other L1-PCs.

Coincidentally, since the paper is concerned with robust SVs estimation, finding good PCs should be given a priority to finding a good subspace because the SVs are directly tied to their corresponding PCs. As a result, we elect to choose the Greedy solution in
[
15
]
to find
\mathbf{Q}_{L1}
in Eq. (
3
).

IV-B

Performance Analysis with Synthetic Dataset

In this section, we will compare the SVs estimation criterion of the L1-cSVD algorithm against the conventional SVD and RPCA.

IV-B
1
Signal Model

We consider a clean data matrix
\mathbf{X}^{\rm clean}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
of rank-K
(K\leq D\leq N)
, containing data from the subspace spanned by
\mathbf{U}_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{D\times K}
, which is kept constant for the experiment.
\mathbf{V}_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{N\times K}
is a random orthonormal matrix and the SVs
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{0}
are drawn from a log-uniform distribution. First,
\mathbf{X}^{\rm clean}
is corrupted by Gaussian noise
\mathbf{N}
with a signal-to-noise ratio
{\rm SNR}=||\mathbf{N}||_{2,2}^{2}/||\mathbf{\Sigma}_{0}||_{2,2}^{2}
. Then, the noisy data matrix is further corrupted by matrix
\mathbf{O}
containing outliers from a subspace spanned by
\mathbf{R}_{o}\in\mathbb{S}^{D\times K_{o}}
, which is also kept constant for the experiment. The probability of corruption is
P_{o}
, so
\mathbf{\Gamma}\in\{0,1\}^{D\times N}
has
P_{o}
chance of a column vector being
\mathbf{1}
while the rest are
\mathbf{0}
. The entries of
\mathbf{S}_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{K_{o}\times N}
are drawn from a zero-mean normal distribution with variance chosen to attain a certain outlier-to-signal ratio, defined as
{\rm OSR}=||\mathbf{O}||_{2,2}^{2}/||\mathbf{\Sigma}_{0}||_{2,2}^{2}
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{X^{\rm corrupted}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{\rm clean}+\mathbf{N}+\mathbf{O}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{0}\mathbf{V}_{0}^{T}+\mathbf{N}+\mathbf{\Gamma}\odot\mathbf{R}_{o}\mathbf{S}_{o}.

(12)

2
Singular-Value Preservation

\displaystyle R_{\rm sv}=\frac{||\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\rm estimated}-\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\rm clean}||_{2,2}}{||\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\rm clean}||_{2,2}},

(13)

where
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\rm clean}
is calculated by applying the conventional SVD on the clean data matrix
\mathbf{X}^{\rm clean}
and
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\rm estimated}
is the estimated SVs from the corrupted dataset
\mathbf{X}^{\rm corrupted}
by applying different SVD algorithms.

L1-PCA has the same SVs estimation error as standard SVD, indicating that the extension from robustness of PCs to robustness of SVs is not trivial. RPCA has the same performance at every OSR due to its ability to separate the sparse component effectively. However, the reconstructed low-rank component is not necessarily robust, since its SVs estimation error is about 40%. This can be attributed to
\mathbf{X}^{\rm corrupted}

We are also interested in comparing the preservation of the individual SVs, defined by

\displaystyle R_{\rm sv,i}=\frac{(\sigma_{i}^{\rm estimated}-\sigma_{i}^{\rm clean})^{2}}{(\sigma_{i}^{\rm clean})^{2}},

.

The most significant finding is that the robustness of L1-cSVD is clearly demonstrated in the estimation of the first and also most important SV, which only deviates less than 10% from the clean dominant SV for OSR up to almost 10dB. On the other hand, RPCA incurs a constant 30-70% error on all SVs across different OSR values.

In addition, the performance of RPCA is strongly dependent on the parameter

IV-C

Bayesian Classifier

In this section, we apply the robust L1-cSVD method to the Bayesian classifier problem. We choose the “Vowel” dataset from Penn Machine Learning Benchmarks (PMLB)
[
26
]
. The chosen dataset has

We apply a Bayesian Classifier on each training dataset
\mathbf{X}^{(i)}

, where we find the median vector
\mathbf{m}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}

\displaystyle d_{i}=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{D}\Bigg(\frac{\mathbf{u}^{(i)T}_{j}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{m}^{(i)})}{\sigma^{(i)}_{j}/\sqrt{N}}\Bigg)^{2}}.

According to the Bayesian Classifier
[

]
,

To assess the robustness of L1-cSVD, we then additively corrupt 3 out of the 75 entries of the training dataset

with outliers of 25 times the average power of an entry of

Direction-of-Arrival Estimation

In this experiment, we choose a linear array with
M=8
sensors uniformly spaced by
\lambda/2
taking
T=200
snapshots of 3 incoming signals with directions of arrival (DOAs)
-45^{\circ},0^{\circ}
and
60^{\circ}
. The received signal can be written to be
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{AS}+\mathbf{N}

[
5
]
, where
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times T}
describes the signal of interest (SoI) received at a sensor at a time snapshot,
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{\theta}}
is the array manifold matrix with
a_{m,k}=\exp\left[-jm\pi\sin(\theta_{k})\right]
, and
\mathbf{N}
is noise with SNR = 10 dB. The DOAs grid is chosen to be from
-90^{\circ}
to
90^{\circ}
with
1^{\circ}
spacing, so
N_{\theta}=180
. Because L1-cSVD is developed for real data,
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{Y}
are realified to be
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2M\times N_{\theta}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2M\times T}
by concatenating their real and imaginary components
[
6
,
14
]
.

The task of DOA estimation is to reconstruct
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\theta}\times T}
, which describes the amplitude of the incoming signals from
N_{\theta}
DOAs at
T
time snapshots. To better distinguish between spatially close sources, Malioutov et. al
[
5
]
proposed a method that enforces sparsity within every column of
\mathbf{S}
, since signal sources can be considered sparse in space but not in time, using the L12-norm. In addition, to reduce time complexity, a dimensionality reduction preprocessing step is used, where
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
is replaced with
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{\rm SV}=\mathbf{U}_{K}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{K}
, where
\mathbf{U}_{K}
are the first
K
left singular vectors and
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{K}
are the first
K
SVs of
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
, with
K=3
being the expected number of sources. Thus, we solve

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{S}^{\rm SV}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\theta}\times K}}{\rm minimize}||\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}^{\rm SV}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{S}^{\rm SV}||_{2,2}+\lambda||\mathbf{S}^{\rm SV}||_{1,2},

(16)

where
\lambda
is a regularization parameter, to obtain the robust spatial spectrum. This method is called
\ell 1
-SVD
[
5
]
, since it uses an L1-norm to enforce spatial sparsity and SVD for dimensionality reduction. It is not to be confused with L1-cSVD in this work, which formulates and solves a compact SVD scheme using the L1-norm.
Unfortunately, using conventional SVD for dimensionality reduction means that
\mathbf{Y}^{\rm SV}
can be sensitive to outliers. To demonstrate this, we corrupt
\mathbf{Y}
with jammer signals coming from DOAs
-30^{\circ},30^{\circ}
and
50^{\circ}
. Each jammer corrupts 10 time snapshots at random with power of 20 times the SoI power (OSR = 0 dB). We reconstruct
\mathbf{S}^{\rm SV}
from the corrupted received signal using the
\ell 1
-SVD method in
[
5
]
with either SVD and L1-cSVD used for dimensionality reduction for comparison.

In Fig.
6
, it can be observed that by using L1-cSVD for dimensionality reduction before applying
\ell 1
-SVD for DOAs estimation, the jammers’ peaks in the spectrum are effectively suppressed compared to using conventional SVD.

V
Conclusions

We presented a novel algorithm for signal value decomposition, based on L1-PCA. This algorithm is the first one to extend the robustness against outliers of L1-PCA problem to finding robust SVs. We showed that such an extension is not obvious and proposed a problem formulation to find more robust SVs. We solved this problem by means of the proposed non-parametric L1-cSVD algorithm, which utilizes the L1-PCA basis
\mathbf{U}_{L1}
and reorthogonalizes
\mathbf{U}_{L1}^{T}\mathbf{X}
to find
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{L1}
and
\mathbf{V}_{L1}
with additional complexity
\mathcal{O}(N^{3}K^{2})
. Our algorithm was tested on SVs estimation, Bayesian Classification, and DoA estimation, on both real and synthetic data. All experiments demonstrate that the proposed L1-cSVD algorithm is more robust in SVs estimation against corruption from sparse and gross outliers compared to the conventional SVD and RPCA, while maintaining similar performance to SVD for clean data.

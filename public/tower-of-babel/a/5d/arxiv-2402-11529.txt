Bailey pairs, radial limits of q q -hypergeometric false theta functions, and a conjecture of Hikami
Jeremy Lovejoy, Rishabh Sarma

Abstract
. In the first part of this paper we prove a conjecture of Hikami on the values of the radial limits of a family of q q -hypergeometric false theta functions. Hikami conjectured that the radial limits are obtained by evaluating a truncated version of the series. He proved a special case of his conjecture by computing the Kashaev invariant of certain torus links in two different ways. We prove the full conjecture using Bailey pairs. In the second part of the paper we show how the framework of Bailey pairs leads to further results of this type.

1.
Introduction

The first main goal of this paper is to prove a conjecture of Hikami
[
5
]
on the values of the radial limits of certain
q
-hypergeometric series. To state Hikami’s conjecture, first recall the
q
-Pochhammer symbol, defined for
n\geq 0
by

(x)_{n}=(x;q)_{n}=(1-x)(1-xq)\cdots(1-xq^{n-1}),

and the
q
-binomial coefficient (or Gaussian polynomial)

\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}=\begin{cases}\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n-k}(q;q)_{k}},&\text{if $0\leq k\leq n$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Next, for
m\geq 2
and
0\leq a\leq m-2
, define the
q
-hypergeometric series
\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)
for
|q|<1
by
1
1

1

Hikami included the prefactor
m
for convenience. For consistency, we preserve it here.

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)=mq^{\frac{(m-a-1)^{2}}{4m}}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}\geq 0}

\displaystyle(-1)^{n_{m-1}}q^{\binom{n_{m-1}+1}{2}+n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-2}^{2}+n_{a+1}+\cdots+n_{m-2}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{m-2}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{i,a}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}.

Hikami
[
5
, Corollary 7]
showed that the series
\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)
is the weight
1/2
false theta function

\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)=m\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(n)q^{n^{2}/4m},

where
\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(n)
is the odd periodic function defined by

\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(n)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if $n\equiv m-a-1\pmod{2m}$},\\
-1,&\mbox{if $n\equiv m+a+1\pmod{2m}$},\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is known that such false theta functions have well-defined limiting values as
q
approaches a root of unity radially from inside the unit disk and that the resulting function is a quantum modular form
[
4
]
. Hikami’s conjecture states that in the case of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)
these radial limits are given by evaluating a truncated version of the series. Namely, define the polynomial
Y_{m,N}^{(a)}(q)
by

Y_{m,N}^{(a)}(q)=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}=0}^{N-1}(-1)^{n_{m-1}}q^{\binom{n_{m-1}+1}{2}+n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-2}^{2}+n_{a+1}+\cdots+n_{m-2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-2}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{i,a}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}.

Then we have the following. Here and throughout the paper we let
\zeta_{N}=e^{2\pi i/N}
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Hikami
[
5
]
)
.

For any
m\geq 2
and
0\leq a\leq m-2
we have

\lim_{q\rightarrow\zeta_{N}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)=\zeta_{N}^{\frac{(m-a-1)^{2}}{4m}}Y_{m,N}^{(a)}(\zeta_{N}).

Hikami proved his conjecture in the case
a=0
by showing that both sides are essentially the Kashaev invariant of the torus link
T(2,2m)
. He writes that it is unclear whether these expressions for
a\neq 0
correspond to any quantum invariant, perhaps hinting that another method is needed to prove the full conjecture. Here we show that the full conjecture follows from the theory of Bailey pairs. In fact, it does so quite easily once the Bailey pair framework is set up. Moreover, it holds for any root of unity.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\zeta_{N}^{M}
be a primitive
N
th root of unity with
N>0
. Then for any
m\geq 2
and
0\leq a\leq m-2
we have

\lim_{q\rightarrow\zeta_{N}^{M}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)={\zeta_{N}}^{\frac{M(m-a-1)^{2}}{4m}}Y_{m,N}^{(a)}(\zeta_{N}^{M}).

In the second part of the paper we show that a similar phenomenon occurs for other families of
q
-hypergeometric false theta functions constructed using Bailey pairs. That is, the radial limits at roots of unity are obtained by evaluating the truncated series. See Theorems
3.3
,
3.7
, and
3.11
.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we prove Hikami’s conjecture. In Section 3 we review the computation of radial limits of false theta functions and present three more results along the lines of Theorem
1.2
. We conclude with some remarks on quantum
q
-series identities in the spirit of
[
6
]
that arise from our work.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
. Before proceeding to the proof we note two facts. First, at any primitive
N
th root of unity, Hikami
[
5
, Proposition 10]
computed the limiting values of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)
.

Lemma 2.1

(Hikami)
.

For coprime integers
M
and
N
with
N>0
we have

\lim_{q\rightarrow\zeta_{N}^{M}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q)=m\sum_{n=0}^{mN}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(n)\left(1-\frac{n}{mN}\right)\zeta_{N}^{Mn^{2}/4m}.

(2.1)

Second, we need a result from the classical theory of Bailey pairs. Recall that a pair of sequences
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is called a Bailey pair relative to
(a,q)
if

\beta_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{(q)_{n-k}(aq)_{n+k}}.

(2.2)

The Bailey lemma (see
[
1
, p. 270]
or
[
2
, Lemma 5.1]
) says that if
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
(a,q)
then so is
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})
, where

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{(b)_{n}(c)_{n}}{(aq/b)_{n}(aq/c)_{n}}(aq/bc)^{n}\alpha_{n}

(2.3)

and

\beta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{(aq/bc)_{n}}{(q)_{n}(aq/b)_{n}(aq/c)_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(b)_{k}(c)_{k}(q^{-n})_{k}q^{k}}{(bcq^{-n}/a)_{k}}\beta_{k}.

(2.4)

Putting this into (
2.2
) with
b,c\to\infty
and using

(q)_{n-k}=\frac{(q)_{n}}{(q^{-n})_{k}}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}-nk},

we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
(q,q)
, then

(q^{2})_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(q^{-n})_{k}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}+(n+1)k}\beta_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(q^{-n})_{k}}{(q^{2+n})_{k}}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}+(n+1)k}\alpha_{k}.

With these two lemmas in hand, we are ready to prove Hikami’s conjecture.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We begin by recalling the Bailey pair relative to
(q,q)
in
[
7
, Prop. 4.1]
,

\alpha_{n}=\frac{(1-q^{(a+1)(2n+1)})(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}+(a+1)n^{2}+(m-a-1)(n^{2}+n)}}{1-q}

and

\beta_{n}=\beta_{n_{m}}=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-1}^{2}+n_{a+1}+\cdots+n_{m-1}}}{(q)_{n_{m}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}.

Here
m\geq 1
and
0\leq a\leq m-1
. When
m=1
(and
a=0
) the above sum is understood to be empty, and we have

\beta_{n}=\beta_{n_{1}}=\frac{1}{(q)_{n_{1}}}.

Using this Bailey pair with
m\mapsto m-1
and substituting into Lemma
2.2
with
n=N-1
we obtain

\displaystyle(q)_{N}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}=0}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{1-N})_{n_{m-1}}(-1)^{n_{m-1}}q^{\binom{n_{m-1}+1}{2}+Nn_{m-1}+n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-2}^{2}+n_{a+1}+\cdots+n_{m-2}}}{(q)_{n_{m-1}}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{m-2}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(q^{1-N})_{k}}{(q^{1+N})_{k}}(1-q^{(a+1)(2k+1)})q^{mk^{2}+(m-a-1)k+Nk}.

Note that for
q
a primitive
N
th root of unity the left hand side vanishes, giving

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(1-q^{(a+1)(2k+1)})q^{mk^{2}+(m-a-1)k}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{-(m-a-1)^{2}}{4m}}\sum_{k=0}^{2mN}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)q^{k^{2}/4m},

where the second equality follows from a short computation involving completing the square. Dividing both sides of (
2.5
) by
(q)_{N}
and taking
\lim_{q\to\zeta_{N}^{M}}
we then have

\displaystyle Y_{m,N}^{(a)}(\zeta_{N}^{M})

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\to\zeta_{N}^{M}}\frac{1}{(q)_{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(q^{1-N})_{k}}{(q^{1+N})_{k}}(1-q^{(a+1)(2k+1)})q^{mk^{2}+(m-a-1)k+Nk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\lim_{q\to\zeta_{N}^{M}}\frac{1}{1-q^{N}}q^{\frac{-(m-a-1)^{2}}{4m}}\sum_{k=0}^{2mN}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)q^{k^{2}/4m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4mN^{2}}\zeta_{N}^{\frac{-M(m-a-1)^{2}}{4m}}\sum_{k=0}^{2mN}k^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\zeta_{N}^{Mk^{2}/4m}.

(2.6)

In the above we have used the fact that for
q
a primitive
N
th root of unity,

\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1-q^{i}x)=\frac{1-x^{N}}{1-x},

which gives

(q;q)_{N-1}=N.

Now, to finish the proof we compute

\displaystyle\zeta_{N}^{\frac{M(m-a-1)^{2}}{4m}}Y_{m,N}^{(a)}(\zeta_{N}^{M})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4mN^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{2mN}k^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\zeta_{N}^{Mk^{2}/4m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4mN^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{mN}k^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\zeta_{N}^{Mk^{2}/4m}+\sum_{k=mN}^{2mN}k^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\zeta_{n}^{Mk^{2}/4m}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4mN^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{mN}k^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\zeta_{N}^{Mk^{2}/4m}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt+\sum_{k=0}^{mN}(2mN-k)^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(2mN-k)\zeta_{N}^{M(2mN-k)^{2}/4m}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4mN^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{mN}k^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\zeta_{N}^{Mk^{2}/4m}-\sum_{k=0}^{mN}(2mN-k)^{2}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\zeta_{N}^{Mk^{2}/4m}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{mN}\chi_{2m}^{(a)}(k)\left(m-\frac{k}{N}\right)\zeta_{N}^{Mk^{2}/4m}

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\rightarrow\zeta_{N}^{M}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(a)}(q),

the last equality following from (
2.1
). This establishes Theorem
1.2
.

∎

3.
Further examples

3.1.
Preliminaries

In this section we find further examples of families of
q
-hypergeometric false theta functions of weight
1/2
whose radial limits are given by evaluating a truncated version of the
q
-series. There are certainly more, but we limit ourselves to applications of some Bailey pairs which have already played a role in the study of
q
-series and weight
3/2
false theta functions at roots of unity
[
7
]
. Here we will need a specialization of the Bailey lemma different from Lemma
2.2
. Namely, if we take
a=b=q
and
c\to\infty
in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) and then use the definition (
2.2
) with
n\to\infty
we have the following, which is well-known.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
(q,q)
, then

\sum_{n\geq 0}(q)_{n}(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}\beta_{n}=(1-q)\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}\alpha_{n}.

We also require a formula which allows us to compute the radial limits of false theta functions as we approach a root of unity. We cite this more or less verbatim from Hikami
[
5
, Proposition 9]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
C_{f}(n)
be a periodic function with mean value
0
and modulus
f
. Then as
t\searrow 0
we have the asymptotic expansion

\sum_{n\geq 1}C_{f}(n)e^{-n^{2}t}\sim\sum_{k\geq 0}L(-2k,C_{f})\frac{(-t)^{k}}{k!},

where

L(-k,C_{f})=-\frac{f^{k}}{k+1}\sum_{n=1}^{f}C_{f}(n)B_{k+1}\left(\frac{n}{f}\right),

with
B_{k}(x)
being the
k
th Bernouilli polynomial.

In each of the following subsections we first use a known Bailey pair together with Lemma
3.1
to produce a family of
q
-hypergeometric false theta functions. These are not all necessarily new, but we include the derivations for completeness. Next we use Lemma
3.2
to compute the radial limits of the false theta functions. Finally, we use the same Bailey pair in Lemma
2.2
to produce a truncated version of the
q
-series whose values at roots of unity coincide with the radial limits of the infinite series.

3.2.
Example 1

For
m\geq 2
define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)=\frac{2m-1}{2}q^{\frac{(2m-3)^{2}}{8(2m-1)}}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}\geq 0}

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n_{m-1}}q^{\binom{n_{m-1}+1}{2}+n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-2}^{2}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m-2}}}{(-q)_{n_{1}}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{m-2}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

and its truncated version

Z_{m,N}(q)=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^{n_{m-1}}q^{\binom{n_{m-1}+1}{2}+n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-2}^{2}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m-2}}}{(-q)_{n_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-2}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}.

Our goal is to prove that the radial limits of
\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)
are well-defined at odd roots of unity and that the values are essentially given by
Z_{m,N}(q)
. We restrict to odd roots of unity to avoid poles arising from the term
(-q)_{n_{1}}
in the denominator of
Z_{m,N}(q)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\zeta_{N}^{M}
be a primitive odd
N
th root of unity. Then

\lim_{q\rightarrow\zeta_{N}^{M}}\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)=\zeta_{N}^{\frac{M(2m-3)^{2}}{8(2m-1)}}Z_{m,N}(\zeta_{N}^{M}).

This result will follow from the next three propositions. We first prove that
\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)
is a false theta function.

Proposition 3.4
.

We have the identity

\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)=\frac{2m-1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\chi_{4m-2}(k)\,q^{\frac{k^{2}}{8(2m-1)}},

where
\chi_{4m-2}(k)
is the odd periodic function defined by

\chi_{4m-2}(k)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if $k\equiv 2m-3\pmod{4m-2}$},\\
-1,&\mbox{if $k\equiv 2m+1\pmod{4m-2}$},\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We begin with the Bailey pair relative to
(q,q)
(see
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
or
[
8
, p. 373]
),

\alpha_{n}=\frac{1-q^{2n+1}}{1-q}(-1)^{n}q^{mn^{2}+(m-1)n}

(3.1)

and

\beta_{n}=\beta_{n_{m}}=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-1}^{2}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m-1}}}{(q)_{n_{m}}(-q)_{n_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}.

(3.2)

Here
m\geq 1
and when
m=1
we have

\beta_{n}=\beta_{n_{1}}=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n_{1}}}.

Using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) with
m\mapsto m-1
in Lemma
3.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{2m-1}{2}q^{\frac{(2m-3)^{2}}{8(2m-1)}}\sum_{n\geq 0}(1-q^{2n+1})q^{\binom{n+1}{2}+(m-1)n^{2}+(m-2)n}

and the result then follows after completing the square on the right-hand side.
∎

Next we employ Lemma
3.2
to calculate the radial limits of
\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)
as
q
approaches an odd root of unity.

Proposition 3.5
.

For coprime integers
M
and
N
with
N
odd and positive we have

\lim_{q\rightarrow\zeta_{N}^{M}}\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(q)=\dfrac{-1}{8(2m-1)N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{(4m-2)N}k^{2}\chi_{4m-2}(k)\zeta_{N}^{\frac{k^{2}}{8(2m-1)}M}.

Proof.

Regarding
\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}
as a function of
\tau
with
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}\left(\frac{M}{N}+i\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2m-1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\chi_{4m-2}(k)e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{M}{N}+i\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)\frac{k^{2}}{8(2m-1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2m-1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)e^{\frac{-k^{2}}{8(2m-1)}t},

where

C_{(4m-2)N}(k)=\chi_{4m-2}(k)e^{\frac{Mk^{2}}{2(4m-2)N}\pi i}.

We have

\displaystyle C_{(4m-2)N}(k+(4m-2)N)

\displaystyle=\chi_{4m-2}(k+(4m-2)N)e^{\frac{M\pi i(k^{2}+2kN(4m-2)+N^{2}(4m-2)^{2})}{2(4m-2)N}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{4m-2}(k)e^{\frac{M\pi ik^{2}}{2(4m-2)N}}e^{M\pi i(k+N(2m-1))},

and using the fact that
N
is odd together with the definition of
\chi_{4m-2}(k)
this gives

C_{(4m-2)N}(k+(4m-2)N)=C_{(4m-2)N}(k).

Similarly, we have

C_{(4m-2)N}((4m-2)N-k)=-C_{(4m-2)N}(k).

(3.3)

Note that by (
3.3
) we have

\sum_{k=1}^{(4m-2)N}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)=\sum_{k=0}^{(4m-2)N}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)=0.

(3.4)

Now using Lemma
3.2
we have the asymptotic expansion as
t\searrow 0
,

\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}\left(\frac{M}{N}+i\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)\sim\frac{2m-1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{L(-2k,C_{(4m-2)N})}{k!}\left(-\dfrac{t}{8(2m-1)}\right)^{k}.

Using this together with
B_{1}(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}
we compute the limiting value as follows,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\downarrow\frac{M}{N}}\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{2m-1}{2}L(0,C_{(4m-2)N})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2m-1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{(4m-2)N}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)B_{1}\left(\dfrac{k}{(4m-2)N}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2m-1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{(4m-2)N}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)\left(\frac{k}{(4m-2)N}\right)\ \ \ \text{(by \eqref{Cprop3ex1})}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{(4m-2)N}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)\left(\dfrac{k}{4N}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)\left(\dfrac{k}{4N}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}C_{(4m-2)N}((4m-2)N-k)\left(\dfrac{(4m-2)N-k}{4N}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)\left(\frac{2m-1}{2}-\dfrac{k}{2N}\right)\ \ \ \text{(by \eqref{Cprop2ex1})}.

Using the fact that

\frac{2m-1}{2}-\dfrac{k}{2N}=\frac{-1}{8(2m-1)N^{2}}\left(k^{2}-((4m-2)N-k)^{2}\right)

we then obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\downarrow\frac{M}{N}}\widetilde{\Psi}_{m}(\tau)=\dfrac{-1}{8(2m-1)N^{2}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}k^{2}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}((4m-2)N-k)^{2}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-1}{8(2m-1)N^{2}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}k^{2}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}((4m-2)N-k)^{2}C_{(4m-2)N}((4m-2)N-k)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-1}{8(2m-1)N^{2}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(2m-1)N}k^{2}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)+\sum_{k=(2m-1)N}^{(4m-2)N}k^{2}C_{(4m-2)N}(k)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-1}{8(2m-1)N^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(4m-2)N}k^{2}\chi_{4m-2}(k)e^{\frac{k^{2}}{2(4m-2)N}M\pi i},

which gives the result.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

For

, a primitive odd

Z_{m,N}(q)=\frac{-1}{8(2m-1)N^{2}}\zeta_{N}^{\frac{-M(2m-3)^{2}}{8(2m-1)}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{(4m-2)N}k^{2}\chi_{4m-2}(k)\,\zeta_{N}^{\frac{Mk^{2}}{8(2m-1)}}.

(3.5)

Proof.

Inserting the Bailey pair from (

) and (

, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}=0}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{1-N})_{n_{m-1}}(-1)^{n_{m-1}}q^{Nn_{m-1}+\binom{n_{m-1}+1}{2}+n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m-2}^{2}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m-2}}}{(q)_{n_{m-1}}(-q)_{n_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-2}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(q^{1-N})_{k}(1-q^{2k+1})q^{-\binom{k}{2}+(N-1)k+mk^{2}+(m-1)k}}{(q)_{N+k}}.

Then taking

to be a primitive odd
N
-th root of unity
\zeta_{N}^{M}

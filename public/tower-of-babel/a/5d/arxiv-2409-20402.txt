Abstract
. This paper aims to determine the predual of the Bergman space A λ 1 A_{\lambda}^{1} on the Siegel upper half-space. To achieve this, a Bloch-type space ℬ ~ \widetilde{\mathcal{B}} is introduced and studied, and some of its essential properties are established. We identify the little Bloch-type space ℬ ~ 0 \widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0} with the predual of A λ 1 A_{\lambda}^{1} via a duality pairing.

Primary 32A10; Secondary 32A18, 32A36.

1.
introduction

Let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional complex Euclidean space. The Siegel upper half-space of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the set

\mathcal{U}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)>0\right\},

where
\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)=\mathrm{Im}\,z_{n}-|z^{\prime}|^{2}
.
Here and throughout the paper, we use the notation

z=(z^{\prime},z_{n}),\quad\text{where}\,z^{\prime}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\,\,\text{and}\,\,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{1}.

For
0<p<\infty
and
\lambda>-1
, the weighted Bergman space
A_{\lambda}^{p}(\mathcal{U})
consists of holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathcal{U}
for which

\|f\|_{p,\lambda}=\bigg\{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}|f(z)|^{p}dV_{\lambda}(z)\bigg\}^{1/p}<\infty,

where
dV_{\lambda}(z)=c_{\lambda}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\lambda}dV(z)
,

c_{\lambda}=\frac{\Gamma(n+1+\lambda)}{4\pi^{n}\Gamma(1+\lambda)},

and
dV
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
It is well known that there is an orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathcal{U},dV_{\lambda})
onto
A_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathcal{U})
, which can be expressed as an integral operator:

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}f(z)=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}K_{\lambda}(z,w)f(w)dV_{\lambda}(w),

where

K_{\lambda}(z,w)=\left[\frac{i}{2}(\overline{w}_{n}-z_{n})-z^{\prime}\cdot\overline{w^{\prime}}\right]^{-(n+1+\lambda)}.

See, for instance,
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
.
For brevity, set

\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)=\frac{i}{2}(\overline{w}_{n}-z_{n})-z^{\prime}\cdot\overline{w^{\prime}}.

Note that
\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)=\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,z)
. Thus, the kernel
K_{\lambda}(z,w)
can be rewritten as

K_{\lambda}(z,w)=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)^{n+1+\lambda}}.

Set
\mathbf{i}=(0^{\prime},i)
. Let

\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)=K_{\lambda}(z,w)-K_{\lambda}(\mathbf{i},w),

and its corresponding projection be

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}f(z)=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)f(w)dV_{\lambda}(w).

In the one-dimensional case, the upper half-plane, Coifman and Rochberg
[
3
]
briefly showed that the dual of Bergman space
A^{1}
can be realized as the projection of
L^{\infty}
under
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
. Békollé
[
1
,
2
]
successively generalized this result to the setting of the Siegel upper half-space and the symmetric Siegel domain of type II,
by introducing a notion of Bloch-type space as a bridge. Naturally, one might ask what is the predual of
A^{1}
on these domains.
This paper is devoted to treating this problem in the setting of the Siegel upper half-space.
To this end, a type of Bloch space is introduced and sdudied.

Let
b\mathcal{U}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)=0\}
be the boundary of
\mathcal{U}
. Clearly,

\mathcal{L}_{j}:=\partial_{j}+2i\bar{z}_{j}\partial_{n}\quad(j=1,\ldots,n-1)

forms a global basis for the space of tangential
(1,0)
vector fields on the boundary
b\mathcal{U}
.
Write
\mathcal{L}_{n}:=\partial_{n}
.
In
[
11
]
a notion of Bloch space on
\mathcal{U}
is introduced in a natural way, denoted by
\mathcal{B}
, consisting of holomorphic functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{U}}|\widetilde{\nabla}f(z)|<\infty,

where

|\widetilde{\nabla}f|=\Bigg(2\boldsymbol{\rho}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|\mathcal{L}_{j}f|^{2}+8\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}|\mathcal{L}_{n}f|^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}.

|\widetilde{\nabla}|
is usually referred to the invariant gradient since it is invariant under the automorphism group
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{U})
; see for instance
[
13
, Chapter 3]
.
It was shown in
[
11
]
that the semi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}}
is equivalent to the Bloch semi-norm on the unit ball. Consequently,
\mathcal{B}
is isomorphic to the Bloch space on the unit ball and shares many of its functional properties.
Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}f(\mathbf{i})=0
for appropriate functions
f
.
It leads to a notion of Bloch-type space, denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, the subspace of
\mathcal{B}
such that functions vanish at
\mathbf{i}
.

The key tool of the paper is the following integral representation.
To our knowledge, similar results have not been found in the literature of holomorphic functions in the unbounded domains.
Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}
denote the set of nonnegative integers,
and for a multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, set
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}=(\mathcal{L}_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\mathcal{L}_{n})^{\alpha_{n}}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, then

f=\frac{(-2i)^{N}\Gamma(1+\lambda)}{\Gamma(1+\lambda+N)}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}f)

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\lambda>-1
.

By Theorem
1.1
, to our surprise, the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is identical to a type of Bloch space introduced by Békollé in
[
1
]
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{*}
denote the space of holomorphic functions satisfying

\sup_{z\in\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)|\mathcal{L}_{n}f(z)|<\infty,

and let
\mathcal{N}
be the subspace annihilated by
\mathcal{L}_{n}
.
He showed that
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{0}}(L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U}))=\mathcal{B}_{*}/\mathcal{N}
.
Consequently,
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{*}/\mathcal{N}
coincide (see Proposition
6.2
).
However, Békollé only conducted a glance at this space.
One of the contributions of the paper is to improve the research.
Moreover, the advantage of the notion of
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is that it provides a convenient way to analyze its structure, as we shall see.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any positive integer
N
,

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\approx\|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}f\|_{\infty}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}

as
f
ranges over
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. Here
\langle\alpha\rangle=|\alpha^{\prime}|/2+\alpha_{n}
.

Here and throughout the paper, we abbreviate inessential constants involved in inequalities by writing
A\lesssim B
for positive quantities
A
and
B
if the ratio
A/B
has a positive upper bound. Also,
A\approx B
means both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

Theorem
1.2
indicates that the space
\mathcal{B}
can be tough of as the limit case of the Bergman spaces
A_{\lambda}^{p}(\mathcal{U})
as
p\to\infty
;
see an analogue for Bergman space in
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
.
For harmonic Bloch functions on the upper half-space of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a similar result was obtained by Ramey and Yi
[
10
, Theorem 5.13]
.

Finally, we give the characterization of the predual of
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
.
Let
\partial\widehat{\mathcal{U}}:=b\mathcal{U}\cup\{\infty\}
and
C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
be the space of complex-valued continuous functions
f
in
\mathcal{U}
such that
f(z)\to 0
as
z\to\partial\widehat{\mathcal{U}}
.
The little Bloch-type space
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
is the subspace of
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
such that
|\widetilde{\nabla}f|\in C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
\lambda>-1
. The predual of
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
is isomorphic to
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
(with equivalent norms) under the duality pairing

\langle f,g\rangle_{\lambda}=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(z)\overline{g(z)}dV_{\lambda}(z)

for
f
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
and
g
in
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents auxiliary results.
Section 3 includes the cancellation property of
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
.
Section 4 studies the projection
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
.
Section 5 is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
.
Theorem
1.2
is proved in Section 6 .
Finally, our main purpose, Theorem
1.3
is established in Section 7.

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that
1\leq p<\infty
,
t>-1
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\begin{cases}\lambda>\frac{t+1}{p}-1,&1<p<\infty,\\
\lambda\geq t,&p=1.\end{cases}

If
f\in A_{t}^{p}(\mathcal{U})
then
f=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}f
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
7
, Lemma 13]
)
.

Let
\theta>0
and
\gamma>-1
. The identities

(2.1)

\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,u)|^{n+\theta}}=\frac{4\pi^{n}\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{n+\theta}{2}\right)}\ \boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{-\theta}

and

(2.2)

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\gamma}dV(w)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\theta+\gamma}}=\frac{4\pi^{n}\Gamma(1+\gamma)\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{n+1+\theta+\gamma}{2}\right)}\ \boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{-\theta}

hold for all
z\in\mathcal{U}
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Given
r>0
, the inequalities

(2.3)

\frac{1-\tanh(r)}{1+\tanh(r)}\leq\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,u)|}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,v)|}\leq\frac{1+\tanh(r)}{1-\tanh(r)}

hold for all
z,u,v\in\mathcal{U}
with
\beta(u,v)\leq r
, where
\beta(u,v)
is the Bergman metric on
\mathcal{U}
given by

\beta(u,v)=\tanh^{-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u)\boldsymbol{\rho}(v)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,v)|^{2}}}.

Note that
z\to\partial\widehat{\mathcal{U}}
if and only if
\beta(z,\mathbf{i})\to\infty
.
Indeed, first note that

(2.4)

\sqrt{|z|^{2}+1}\leq 2|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|\leq|z|+1\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\leq|z|.

Then we have

4\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)/(|z|+1)^{2}\leq\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)/|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}\leq 4\min\{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z),|z|/(|z|^{2}+1)\}.

It follows that
z\to\partial\widehat{\mathcal{U}}
if and only if
\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)/|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}\to 0
, as desired.

Let
\mathbb{B}
denote the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Recall that
\mathbb{B}
and

are biholomorphic equivalent through the Cayley transform
\Phi:\mathbb{B}\to\mathcal{U}
given by

(z^{\prime},z_{n})\;\longmapsto\;\left(\frac{z^{\prime}}{1+z_{n}},i\left(\frac{1-z_{n}}{1+z_{n}}\right)\right).

We refer to

2.2.
Heisenberg Group

We briefly recall several basic facts about the Heisenberg group that can be found in
[
12
, Chapter XII]
.
The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
is the set

\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}=\{[\zeta,t]:\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1},t\in\mathbb{R}\}

endowed with the group operation

[\zeta,t]\cdot[\eta,s]=[\zeta+\eta,t+s+2\mathrm{Im}(\zeta\cdot\bar{\eta})].

The identity element is
[0,0]
and the inverse of

[\zeta,t]
is
[\zeta,t]^{-1}=[-\zeta,-t]
.
The measure
dh
on
\mathbb{H}
is the usual Lebesgue measure
d\zeta dt
on
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, here we write
h=[\zeta,t]
; it is Haar measure for
\mathbb{H}
.

To each element
h=[\zeta,t]
of
\mathbb{H}
, we associate the following (holomorphic) affine self-mapping of

\mathcal{U}
:

h:\;(z^{\prime},z_{n})\longmapsto(z^{\prime}+\zeta,z_{n}+t+2iz^{\prime}\cdot\bar{\zeta}+i|\zeta|^{2}).

These mappings are simply transitive on the boundary
b\mathcal{U}
,
so we can identify the Heisenberg group with
b\mathcal{U}
via its action on the origin

\mathbb{H}\ni[\zeta,t]\,\longmapsto\,(\zeta,t+i|\zeta|^{2})\in b\mathcal{U}.

This identification allows us to transport the Haar measure
dh
on
\mathbb{H}
to the measure
d\boldsymbol{\beta}
on
b\mathcal{U}
;
that is, we have the integration formula

\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}fd\boldsymbol{\beta}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{H}}f(h(0))dh

for suitable functions
f
.
Moreover,
d\boldsymbol{\beta}
is
\mathbb{H}
-invariant; that is,
d\boldsymbol{\beta}(h(z))=d\boldsymbol{\beta}(z)
for each
h\in\mathbb{H}
.
Finally, for every
h\in\mathbb{H}
and
z\in\mathcal{U}
,
u\in b\mathcal{U}
, one has

\boldsymbol{\rho}(h(z),h(u))=\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,u).

3.
Cancellation property of
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s>0
. Then, for every
z\in\mathcal{U}
,

\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,z)^{n+s}}=0.

Proof.

Fix
z\in\mathcal{U}
and set
h_{z}=[-z^{\prime},-\mathrm{Re}\,z_{n}]\in\mathbb{H}
. One easily checks that
h_{z}(z)=\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\mathbf{i}
.
Using the change of variables
u\mapsto h_{z}^{-1}(u)
and the invariance of
d\boldsymbol{\beta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,z)^{n+s}}=\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(h^{-1}_{z}(u),z)^{n+s}}=\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,h_{z}(z))^{n+s}}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\mathbf{i})^{n+s}}=\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{(-\frac{i}{2}[u_{n}+i\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)])^{n+s}}.

Note that an element of
b\mathcal{U}
has the form
(u^{\prime},t+i|u^{\prime}|^{2})
. A straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{(u_{n}+i\boldsymbol{\rho}(z))^{n+s}}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{n-1}}{(n-2)!}\int\limits_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\frac{(\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda)^{n-2}}{(\lambda+i\boldsymbol{\rho}(z))^{n+s}}dm_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{n-1}}{(n-2)!}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(x+i(y+\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)))^{n+s}}\bigg\}y^{n-2}dy.

The inner integral vanishes by a contour integration argument, which proves the lemma.
∎

We refer to the following result as the cancellation property of
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\lambda>-1
and
f\in A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
. Then, for every
t>0
,

\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}f(u+t\mathbf{i})d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)=0.

Consequently,

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}f(z)dV_{\lambda}(z)=0.

Proof.

Let
f\in A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
. By Lemma
2.1
,

f(u+t\mathbf{i})=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{f(w)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u+t\mathbf{i},w)^{n+1+\lambda}}dV_{\lambda}(w)

for any
u\in b\mathcal{U}
and any
t>0
. Since
\boldsymbol{\rho}(u+t\mathbf{i},w)=\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,w+t\mathbf{i})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}f(u+t\mathbf{i})d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\bigg\{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{f(w)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,w+t\mathbf{i})^{n+1+\lambda}}dV_{\lambda}(w)\bigg\}d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\bigg\{\int\limits_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,w+t\mathbf{i})^{n+1+\lambda}}\bigg\}f(w)dV_{\lambda}(w)=0,

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.1
.
The interchange of the order of integration is justified as follows.
Note that
\boldsymbol{\rho}(w+t\mathbf{i})=\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)+t
.
By (
2.1
),

\int_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,w+t\mathbf{i})|^{n+1+\lambda}}=\frac{4\pi^{n}\Gamma(1+\lambda)}{\Gamma^{2}\!\left(\frac{n+1+\lambda}{2}\right)}\frac{1}{(\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)+t)^{1+\lambda}}.

Therefore,

\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Bigg(\int_{b\mathcal{U}}\frac{d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(u+t\mathbf{i},w)|^{n+1+\lambda}}\Bigg)|f(w)|\,dV_{\lambda}(w)\lesssim t^{-(1+\lambda)}\,\|f\|_{1,\lambda}<\infty.

Finally, the identity

\int_{\mathcal{U}}f(z)\,dV_{\lambda}(z)=c_{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{b\mathcal{U}}f(u+t\mathbf{i})\,d\boldsymbol{\beta}(u)\,t^{\lambda}\,dt

yields the second assertion.
∎

4.
The projection
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
from
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
into
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}

In this section we investigate the projection
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
. We begin with an integral estimate of its kernel.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\lambda>-1
. Then, for every
z\in\mathcal{U}
, one has

(4.1)

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\big|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)\big|dV_{\lambda}(w)\lesssim 1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}.

Moreover,

(4.2)

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\big|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(u,w)\big|dV_{\lambda}(w)dV_{\lambda}(u)\lesssim\left\{1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}\right\}^{2}.

Proof.

Using the elementary inequality

(4.3)

\left|\frac{1}{A^{n+1+\lambda}}-\frac{1}{B^{n+1+\lambda}}\right|\leq(n+1)\left(\frac{|A-B|}{|A|^{n+1+\lambda}|B|}+\frac{|A-B|}{|A||B|^{n+1+\lambda}}\right),

with
A=\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)
and
B=\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)\big|\leq(n+1)\left\{\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)-\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda}|\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)|}+\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)-\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)||\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)|^{n+1+\lambda}}\right\}.

Since
|z_{n}+i|>|z_{n}-i|
and
|z_{n}+i|>|z^{\prime}|^{2}
for all
z\in\mathcal{U}
, we have

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{\rho}(

\displaystyle\mathbf{i},w)-\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}-i|+|z^{\prime}||w^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}+i|+|z_{n}+i|^{1/2}|w_{n}+i|^{1/2}=|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|+2|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{1/2}|\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)|^{1/2}.

Consequently,

\big|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)\big|\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{4}K_{j}(z,w),

where

\displaystyle K_{1}(z,w)=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda}|\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)|},

\displaystyle\quad K_{2}(z,w)=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{1/2}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda}|\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)|^{1/2}},

\displaystyle K_{3}(z,w)=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)||\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)|^{n+1+\lambda}},

\displaystyle\quad K_{4}(z,w)=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{1/2}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|\,|\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{i},w)|^{n+1/2+\lambda}}.

Let
z=\Phi(\eta)
and
w=\Phi(\xi)
. For suitable exponents
a,b,s,k
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{s}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{a}|\boldsymbol{\rho}(w,\mathbf{i})|^{b}}\left\{1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(w,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)}\right\}^{k}dV(w)

\displaystyle=4|1+\eta_{n}|^{a}\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{(1-|\xi|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\eta\cdot\overline{\xi}|^{a}|1+\xi_{n}|^{2(n+1+s)-a-b}}\left(\log\frac{e}{1-|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{k}dV(\xi).

By
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
, it follows that

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}K_{j}(z,w)dV_{\lambda}(w)\lesssim 1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)},\qquad j=1,\ldots,4,

which proves (
4.1
).
To obtain (
4.2
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}K_{j}(z,u)\left\{1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u)}\right\}dV_{\lambda}(u)\lesssim\left\{1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}\right\}^{2},

which again follows from
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda>-1
. Then, for all
z,w\in\mathcal{U}
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(\cdot,w))(z)=\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w).

Proof.

Fix
z,w\in\mathcal{U}
. By definition,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(\cdot,w))(z)=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(u,w)dV_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)K_{\lambda}(u,w)dV_{\lambda}(u)-\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)K_{\lambda}(\mathbf{i},w)dV_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=\overline{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)}K_{\lambda}(w,u)dV_{\lambda}(u)}-K_{\lambda}(\mathbf{i},w)\overline{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)}dV_{\lambda}(u)}.

Using (
4.1
), we see that
\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,\cdot)}\in A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
.
By Lemma
2.1
,

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)}K_{\lambda}(w,u)dV_{\lambda}(u)=\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)},

and by the cancellation property of
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
,

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)}dV_{\lambda}(u)=0.

The conclusion follows by taking complex conjugates.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\lambda>-1
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
. Set
f=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(g)
.
Then
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
if
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
,

\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\in C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
if
g\in C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
.

Proof.

Assume first that
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
. By (
4.1
), the function
f
is holomorphic in
\mathcal{U}
.
A direct computation gives

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\left\{\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)^{n+1+\lambda}}\right\}=C(n,\lambda,\alpha)\frac{\left(\overline{{z}^{\prime}}-\overline{{w}^{\prime}}\right)^{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)^{n+1+\lambda+|\alpha|}},

where
C(n,\lambda,\alpha)
is a constant depending on
n
,
\lambda
and
\alpha
.
Since
\big|(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}\big|\lesssim|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{\frac{|\alpha^{\prime}|}{2}}
by
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
and
|\alpha|=\langle\alpha\rangle+|\alpha^{\prime}|/2
, we obtain

\frac{\big|(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}\big|}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+|\alpha|}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f(z)\big|\lesssim

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\big|(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}\big|\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+|\alpha|}}|g(w)|dV(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle}}|g(w)|dV(w).

An application of (
2.2
) shows that
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
.

Now assume
g\in C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
.
Given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that

\sup\left\{|g(w)|:w\in\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{D(\mathbf{i},R)}\right\}<\epsilon,

where
D(\mathbf{i},R):=\{w\in\mathcal{U}:\beta(w,\mathbf{i})<R\}
.
We decompose

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{D(\mathbf{i},R)}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle}}\,dV(w)}_{=:I_{1}(z)}\,\varepsilon

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\int_{\overline{D(\mathbf{i},R)}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle}}\,dV(w)}_{=:I_{2}(z)}\|g\|_{\infty}.

By (
2.2
),
I_{1}(z)
is bounded.
For
w\in D(\mathbf{i},R)
, Lemma
2.3
yields

\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle}}.

Hence,

I_{2}(z)\lesssim\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle}}\lesssim\min\left\{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{\langle\alpha\rangle},\frac{{|z|}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}}{(|z|^{2}+1)^{(n+1+\lambda+\langle\alpha\rangle)/2}}\right\},

where we use (
2.4
) in the last step.
It implies that
I_{2}(z)\to 0
as
z\to\partial\widehat{\mathcal{U}}
.
Therefore,
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\in C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\lambda>-1
. Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
maps
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
boundedly into
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
,
and maps
C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
boundedly into
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
.

Proof.

Recall that

|\widetilde{\nabla}f|^{2}=2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{j}f|^{2}+8|\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}f|^{2}.

An application of Lemma
4.3
to all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=1
gives the result.
∎

5.
Integral representation

For any
t>0
, let
\mathcal{A}^{-t}
denote the space of holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathcal{U}
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{t}|f(z)|<\infty.

These spaces are usually called Korenblum spaces, following the terminology used for the unit disk (c.f.
[
6
]
).
Clearly,
\mathcal{A}^{-t}\not\subset A_{\lambda}^{p}(\mathcal{U})
.
Nevertheless, functions in
\mathcal{A}^{-t}
admit the same integral representation as Bergman functions.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
t>0
and
f\in\mathcal{A}^{-t}
. Then
f=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}f
for any
\lambda>t-1
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>t-1
. By (
2.2
), the integral

\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\lambda}}|f(w)|dV(w)

converges for every
z\in\mathcal{U}
.
Let
z=\Phi(\xi)
and
w=\Phi(\eta)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}f(z)

\displaystyle=c_{\lambda}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)^{n+1+\lambda}}f(w)dV(w)

\displaystyle=4c_{\lambda}\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{\left(\frac{1-|\eta|^{2}}{|1+\eta_{n}|^{2}}\right)^{\lambda}}{\left(\frac{1-\xi\cdot\overline{\eta}}{(1+\xi_{n})(1+\overline{\eta}_{n})}\right)^{n+1+\lambda}}f(\Phi(\eta))\frac{1}{|1+\eta_{n}|^{2(n+1)}}dV(\eta)

\displaystyle=(1+\xi_{n})^{n+1+\lambda}\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{1}{(1-\xi\cdot\overline{\eta})^{n+1+\lambda}}\frac{f(\Phi(\eta))}{(1+\eta_{n})^{n+1+\lambda}}dv_{\lambda}(\eta)

\displaystyle:=(1+\xi_{n})^{n+1+\lambda}\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{F(\eta)}{(1-\xi\cdot\overline{\eta})^{n+1+\lambda}}dv_{\lambda}(\eta),

where
dv_{\lambda}(\xi)=4c_{\lambda}(1-|\xi|^{2})^{\lambda}dV(\xi)
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{B}
.
A direct estimate gives

\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}}|F(\eta)|dv_{\lambda}(\eta)\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(\Phi(\eta))^{-t}}{|1+\eta_{n}|^{n+1+\lambda}}dv_{\lambda}(\eta)\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{(1-|\eta|^{2})^{\lambda-t}}{|1+\eta_{n}|^{n+1+\lambda-2t}}dv(\eta)<\infty.

Then by the reproducing formula of weighted Bergman spaces on
\mathbb{B}
, we obtain

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}f(z)=(1+\xi_{n})^{n+1+\lambda}F(\xi)=f(z),

as desired.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. If
\mathcal{L}_{n}f\equiv 0
then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Suppose
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{n}f\equiv 0
. Then
f
is independent of the
n
-th coordinate, and hence the same is true for each
\partial_{j}f
.
Consequently,

\big|\widetilde{\nabla}f(z)\big|^{2}=2\left(\mathrm{Im}\,z_{n}-|z^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left|\partial_{j}f(z^{\prime})\right|^{2}.

Fix
z^{\prime}
and let
\mathrm{Im}\,z_{n}\to\infty
.
Then the right-hand side of the above equality tends to infinity, whereas the left-hand side remains bounded.
This is possible only if
\partial_{j}f\equiv 0
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.
Hence
f
is constant. Since
f(\mathbf{i})=0
, we conclude that
f\equiv 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
and fix
\lambda>-1
. Set

b_{N}:=\frac{(-2i)^{N}\Gamma(1+\lambda)}{\Gamma(1+\lambda+N)}.

We divide the argument into three cases.

Case I:
N=1
.

In this case the desired identity takes the form

(5.1)

f(z)=b_{1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)dV_{\lambda}(w).

To prove this, put

g(z)=f(z)-b_{1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)dV_{\lambda}(w).

Since
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, we have
\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}f\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
,
and therefore Corollary
4.4
implies that
g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
.
A direct computation yields

\mathcal{L}_{n}g(z)=\mathcal{L}_{n}f(z)-\mathcal{P}_{\lambda+1}(\mathcal{L}_{n}f)(z)=0,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{n}f\in\mathcal{A}^{-1}
together with Lemma
5.1
.
Lemma
5.2
now shows that
g\equiv 0
, which proves (
5.1
).

Case II:
N=0
.

Using (
5.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}f(z)=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)f(u)dV_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=b_{1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\bigg\{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(u,w)\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)dV_{\lambda}(w)\bigg\}dV_{\lambda}(u).

By (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(u,w)\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\big|dV_{\lambda}(w)dV_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\left\{1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}\right\}^{2}<\infty,

so Fubini’s theorem applies. Hence

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}f(z)=

\displaystyle b_{1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\bigg\{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(u,w)dV_{\lambda}(u)\bigg\}dV_{\lambda}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)dV_{\lambda}(w)=f(z),

where we used Lemma
4.2
and again (
5.1
).

Case III:
N\geq 2
.

Applying
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}
to both sides of (
5.1
) and multiplying by
\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{N}
,
we have

\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{N}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}f(z)=\frac{b_{1}}{b_{N}}c_{\lambda+N}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{N}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)^{n+1+\lambda+N}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{1+\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)dV(w).

By Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}f)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{1}}{b_{N}}c_{\lambda+N}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\bigg\{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u)^{N}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,w)^{n+1+\lambda+N}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{1+\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)dV(w)\bigg\}dV_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{1}}{b_{N}}c_{\lambda}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{1+\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\bigg\{\overline{c_{\lambda+N}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u)^{\lambda+N}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w,u)^{n+1+\lambda+N}}\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)}dV(u)\bigg\}}dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{1}}{b_{N}}c_{\lambda}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{1+\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,w)dV(w)=\frac{f(z)}{b_{N}},

where we used that
\overline{\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,\cdot)}\in A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
and Lemma
2.1
, and again (
5.1
).
The interchange of integral order is justified as follows. By (
2.2
) and (
4.1
),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\left|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u)^{N}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,w)^{n+1+\lambda+N}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{1+\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\right|dV(w)dV_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\big|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\big|\bigg\{\boldsymbol{\rho}(u)^{N}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}dV(w)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(u,w)|^{n+1+\lambda+N}}\bigg\}dV_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\big|\widetilde{K_{\lambda}}(z,u)\big|dV_{\lambda}(u)\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\left\{1+\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,\mathbf{i})|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}\right\}<\infty

for every
z\in\mathcal{U}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

6.
Norm equivalence

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
. The map
f\mapsto\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f
is bounded from
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
into
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
, and bounded from
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
into
C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
).
Then
\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}f\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
(resp.
C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
) and
\|\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}f\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}
.
Theorem
1.1
tells us that

f=b_{1}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}f)

for any
\lambda>-1
. This together with Lemma
4.3
gives the desired boundedness.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\lambda>-1
. The operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
is bounded from
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
onto
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, and from
C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
onto
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
.

Proof.

The boundedness and the “into” part were already established in Corollary
4.4
.
The “onto” part follows directly from Theorem
1.1
with
N=1
.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It suffices to show that

\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\langle\alpha\rangle}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}f\|_{\infty}.

The first inequality is an immediate consequence of Proposition
6.1
.
To prove the second one, note from Theorem
1.1
that

f=b_{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}f)
,
together with Proposition
6.2
yields the desired result.
∎

An application of Theorem
1.2
could generalize Lemma
5.2
to all cases.
We refer to it as the uniqueness property for functions in
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. It shows how differently Bloch functions on
\mathcal{U}
behave when compared with those on the unit ball.

Corollary 6.3
.

Suppose
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. If
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\equiv 0
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

The argument is divided into two steps.

Step 1: The case
\alpha=(0^{\prime},N)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N}f\equiv 0
. Then
f
is necessarily a polynomial of degree at most
N-1
in the variable
z_{n}
, namely

f(z)=f_{N-1}(z^{\prime})z_{n}^{N-1}+f_{N-2}(z^{\prime})z_{n}^{N-2}+\cdots+f_{0}(z^{\prime}),

where each
f_{j}
is holomorphic in
z^{\prime}
.
Differentiating, we obtain

\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{N-1}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{N-1}f(z)=(N-1)!\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)^{N-1}f_{N-1}(z^{\prime}).

By Theorem
1.2
, the left-hand side is bounded on
\mathcal{U}
, whereas the right-hand side tends to
+\infty
whenever
z^{\prime}
is fixed and
\mathrm{Im}\,z_{n}\to+\infty
, unless
f_{N-1}\equiv 0
.
Iterating this argument yields
f_{N-2}=\cdots=f_{1}\equiv 0
, so that
f(z)=f_{0}(z^{\prime})
.
Lemma
5.2
then forces
f\equiv 0
.

Step 2: The general case.

Assume now that
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f\equiv 0
for some multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Using the expansion

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}f~=~\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\leq\alpha^{\prime}}\binom{\alpha^{\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}(2i\bar{z}^{\prime})^{\gamma^{\prime}}\partial^{\alpha-(\gamma^{\prime},-|\gamma^{\prime}|)}f,

we may regard the left-hand side as a polynomial in
\bar{z}^{\prime}
.
Since this polynomial vanishes identically, all its coefficients must be zero; hence

\partial^{\alpha-(\gamma^{\prime},-|\gamma^{\prime}|)}f\equiv 0\qquad\text{for all }\gamma^{\prime}\leq\alpha^{\prime}.

In particular, taking
\gamma^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}
yields

\mathcal{L}_{n}^{|\alpha|}f\equiv 0.

By Step 1, this implies
f\equiv 0
.
The proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

To address potential concerns, readers might note that the parallels between Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and Corollary
6.3
, and those in our previous work
[
9
]
on weighted Bergman space, possibly questioning whether this constitutes merely a straightforward generalization or follows the same technical path.
This is not the case.
In the case of weighted Bergman space, the uniqueness property served as the starting point, from which the integral representation and norm equivalences were subsequently derived.
While for Bloch-type space, the logical direction is reversed. The integral representation with respect to
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
constitutes the central ingredient, the norm equivalence follow from the boundedness and integral representation of
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}
, and only thereafter the uniqueness property is obtained.

7.
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
as the predual of
A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})

We denote by
M(\mathcal{U})
the space of complex Radon measures on
\mathcal{U}
, and for
\mu\in M(\mathcal{U})
we write

\|\mu\|=|\mu|(\mathcal{U}),

where
|\mu|
denotes the total variation of
\mu
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\lambda>-1
,
\mu\in M(\mathcal{U})
, and define

g(w):=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w,z)^{n+2+\lambda}}d\mu(z),\quad w\in\mathcal{U}.

Then
g\in A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
and
\|g\|_{1,\lambda}\lesssim\|\mu\|
.

Proof.

By (
2.2
), there is a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\bigg|\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w,z)^{n+2+\lambda}}d\mu(z)\bigg|dV_{\lambda}(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\bigg\{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\lambda}dV(w)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(w,z)|^{n+2+\lambda}}\bigg\}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)d|\mu|(z)\leq C\|\mu\|,

which proves the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For each
g\in A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
, it is obvious that the map

f\longmapsto\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}f)\overline{g}dV_{\lambda},\quad f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0},

defines a bounded linear functional on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
, with norm
\lesssim\|g\|_{1,\lambda}
.

Now let
\Lambda
be a bounded linear functional on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
.
By Theorem
1.2
, the map

\mathcal{F}:\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}\to C_{0}(\mathcal{U}),\ \ \ f\mapsto\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}f,

is an embedding whose range is a closed subspace of
C_{0}(\mathcal{U})
.
Therefore,
\Lambda\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}

is a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0})
.
By Hahn-Banach extension theorem and Riesz representation theorem, there exists
\mu\in M(\mathcal{U})
such that

\|\mu\|=\|\Lambda\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}\|
and

\Lambda(f)=\Lambda\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}f)=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{F}fd\mu=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(z)d\mu(z)

for all
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
. Given
\beta>0
, it follows from Lemma
5.1
that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\mathcal{P}_{\beta}(\mathcal{L}_{n}f)(z)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\bigg\{c_{\beta}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\beta}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)^{n+1+\beta}}\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)dV(w)\bigg\}d\mu(z).

Using (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\beta}}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\beta}}|\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)dV(w)d|\mu|(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)\bigg\{\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\beta-1}dV(w)}{|\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)|^{n+1+\beta}}\bigg\}d|\mu|(w)\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\|\mu\|.

Thus Fubini’s theorem applies, and we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda(f)=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)^{\beta}\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\bigg\{c_{\beta}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z,w)^{n+1+\beta}}d\mu(z)\bigg\}dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{\rho}(w)\mathcal{L}_{n}f(w)\overline{g(w)}dV_{\lambda}(w),

where
\lambda=\beta-1>-1
and

g(w)=c_{\beta}\int\limits_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}(z)}{\boldsymbol{\rho}(w,z)^{n+2+\lambda}}d\overline{\mu}(z).

By Lemma
7.1
,
g\in A_{\lambda}^{1}(\mathcal{U})
, and moreover

\|g\|_{1,\lambda}\lesssim\|\mu\|=\|\Lambda\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}\|\lesssim\|\Lambda\|.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Lower Bounds and properties for the average number of colors in the non-equivalent colorings of a graph
Alain Hertz, Hadrien Mélot, Sébastien Bonte, Gauvain Devillez, [3mm] Department of Mathematics, Industrial Engineering Polytechnique Montréal - Gerad, Montréal, CanadaCorresponding author. Email: alain.hertz@gerad.ca, [3mm] Computer Science Department - Algorithms LabUniversity of Mons, Mons, Belgium

Abstract




Abstract.

We study the average number
\mathcal{A}(G)
of colors in the non-equivalent colorings of a graph
G
. We show some general properties of this graph invariant and determine its value for some classes of graphs. We then conjecture several lower bounds on
\mathcal{A}(G)
and prove that these conjectures are true for specific classes of graphs such as triangulated graphs and graphs with maximum degree at most 2.

Keywords:
graph coloring, average number of colors, graphical Bell numbers.

1
Introduction

A coloring of a graph
G
is an assignment of colors to its vertices such that adjacent vertices have different colors. The chromatic number
\chi(G)
is the minimum number of colors in a coloring of
G
. The total number
\mathcal{B}(G)
of non-equivalent colorings (i.e., with different partitions into color classes) of a graph
G
is the number of partitions of the vertex set of
G
whose blocks are stable
sets (i.e., sets of pairwise non-adjacent vertices). This invariant has been studied by several authors in the last few years
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
]
under the name of (graphical) Bell number.

Recently, Hertz et
a
l. have defined a new graph invariant
\mathcal{A}(G)

which is equal to the average number of colors in the non-equivalent colorings of a graph
G
.
It can be seen as a generalization of a concept linked to Bell numbers. More precisely, the Bell numbers
({\sf B}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
count the number of different ways to partition a set that has exactly
n
elements. The 2-Bell numbers
({\sf T}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
count the total number of blocks in all partitions of a set of
n
elements. Odlyzko and Richmond
[
14
]
have studied the average number
{\sf A}_{n}
of blocks in a partition of a set of
n
elements, which can be defined as
{\sf A}_{n}=\frac{{\sf T}_{n}}{{\sf B}_{n}}.

The graph invariant
\mathcal{A}(G)
that we study in this paper generalizes
{\sf A}_{n}
. Indeed, when constraints (represented by edges in
G
) impose that certain pairs of elements (represented by vertices) cannot belong to the same block of a partition,
\mathcal{A}(G)
is the average number of blocks in the partitions that respect all constraints. Hence, for a graph of order
n
,
\mathcal{A}(G)={\sf A}_{n}
if
G
is the empty graph of order
n
.

As shown in
[
10
]
,
\mathcal{A}(G)
can help discover nontrivial inequalities for the Bell numbers. For example, we will see that
\mathcal{A}({\sf P}_{n})=\frac{{\sf B}_{n}}{{\sf B}_{n-1}}
and
\mathcal{A}({\sf P}_{n})<\mathcal{A}({\sf P}_{n+1})
for
n\geq 1
, where
{\sf P}_{n}
is the path on
n
vertices. This immediately implies
{\sf B}_{n}^{2}<{\sf B}_{n-1}{\sf B}_{n+1}
, which means that the sequence
({\sf B}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is strictly log-convex. This result has also been proved recently by Alzer
[
2
]
using numerical arguments.

The best possible upper bound for
\mathcal{A}(G)
is clearly the order
n
of
G
since all colorings of
G
use at most
n
colors and
\mathcal{A}({\sf K}_{n})=n
for the clique
{\sf K}_{n}
of order
n
. It seems however much more complex to define a lower bound for
\mathcal{A}(G)
, as a function of
n
, which is reached by at least one graph of order
n
. We think that the best possible lower bound is
\frac{{\sf B}_{n+1}-{\sf B}_{n}}{{\sf B}_{n}}
and is reached by the empty graph of order
n
. But it is just a conjecture we are trying to prove.

In the next section we fix some notations, while Section
3
is devoted to basic properties of
\mathcal{A}(G)
.
In Section
4
, we give values of
\mathcal{A}(G)
for some particular graphs
G
that we will deal with later. We then state in Section
5
three conjectures on a lower bound for
\mathcal{A}(G)
and prove that they are true
for graphs
G
with maximum degree
\Delta(G)\leq 2
and for triangulated graphs.

2
Notation

For basic notions of graph theory that are not defined here, we refer to Diestel
[
4
]
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple undirected graph. The
order

n=|V|
of
G
is its number of vertices and the
size

m=|E|
of
G
is its number of edges. We write
G\simeq H
if
G
and
H
are two isomorphic graphs, and
\overline{G}
is the complement of
G
. We denote by
{\sf K}_{n}
(resp.
{\sf C}_{n}
,
{\sf P}_{n}
and
\overline{{\sf K}}_{n}
) the
complete graph
(resp. the
cycle
, the
path
and the empty graph) of order
n
. We write
{\sf K}_{a,b}
for the complete bipartite graph where
a
and
b
are the cardinalities of the two sets of vertices of the bipartition. For a subset
S
of vertices in a graph
G
, we write
G[S]
for the subgraph of
G
induced by
S
.

Let
N(v)
be the set of neighbors of a vertex
v
in
G
. A vertex
v
is
isolated
if
|N(v)|=0
and is
dominant
if
|N(v)|=n-1
(where
n
is the order of
G
). We write
\Delta(G)
for the
maximum degree
of
G
. A vertex
v
of a graph
G
is
simplicial
if the induced subgraph
G[N(v)]
of
G
is a clique. A graph is
triangulated
if each of its induced subgraphs contains a simplicial vertex.

Let
u
and
v
be two vertices in a graph
G
of order
n
, We use the following notations:

•

G_{\mid uv}
is the graph (of order
n-1
) obtained from
G
by identifying (merging) the vertices
u
and
v
and, if
uv\in E(G)
, by removing the edge
uv
;

•

if
uv\in E(G)
,
G-uv
is the graph obtained by removing the edge
uv
from
G
;

•

if
uv\notin E(G)
,
G+uv
is the graph obtained by adding the edge
uv
in
G
;

•

G-v
is the graph obtained from
G
by removing
v
and all its incident edges.

Given two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
(with disjoint sets of vertices), we write
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
for the
disjoint union
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, while the
join

G_{1}+G_{2}
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is the graph obtained from
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
by adding all possible edges between the vertices of
G_{1}
and those of
G_{2}
. Also,
G\cup p{\sf K}_{1}
is the graph obtained from
G
by adding
p
isolated vertices.

A
coloring
of a graph
G
is an assignment of colors to the vertices of
G
such that adjacent vertices have different colors. The
chromatic number

\chi(G)
of
G
is the minimum number of colors in a coloring of
G
. Two colorings are
equivalent
if they induce the same partition of the vertex set into color classes. Let
S(G,k)
be the number of non-equivalent colorings of a graph
G
that use
exactly

k
colors. Then, the total number
\mathcal{B}(G)
of non-equivalent colorings of a graph
G
is defined by

\mathcal{B}(G)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S(G,k)=\sum_{k=\chi(G)}^{n}S(G,k),

and the total number
\mathcal{T}(G)
of color classes in the non-equivalent colorings of a graph
G
is defined by

\mathcal{T}(G)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}kS(G,k)=\sum_{k=\chi(G)}^{n}kS(G,k).

The average number
\mathcal{A}(G)
of colors in the non-equivalent colorings of a graph
G
can therefore be defined as

\mathcal{A}(G)=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G)}{\mathcal{B}(G)}.

Note that

\mathcal{B}(\overline{{\sf K}}_{n})={\sf B}_{n},\mathcal{T}(\overline{{\sf K}}_{n})={\sf T}_{n}
, and
\mathcal{A}(\overline{{\sf K}}_{n})={\sf A}_{n}
. As another example, consider the complement
\overline{{\sf P}}_{5}
of a path on 5 vertices. As shown in Figure
1
, there are three non-equivalent colorings of
\overline{{\sf P}}_{5}
with 3 colors, four with 4 colors, and one with 5 colors, which gives
\mathcal{B}(\overline{{\sf P}}_{5})=8
,
\mathcal{T}(\overline{{\sf P}}_{5})=30
and
\mathcal{A}(\overline{{\sf P}}_{5})=\frac{30}{8}=3.75.

The non-equivalent colorings of
\overline{{\sf P}}_{5}
.

3
Basic properties of
\mathcal{A}(G)

As for several other invariants in graph coloring, the
deletion-contraction
rule (also often called the
Fundamental Reduction Theorem

[
6
]
) can be used to compute
\mathcal{B}(G)
and
\mathcal{T}(G)
. More precisely, let
u
and
v
be any pair of distinct vertices of
G
. As shown in
[
8
,
12
]
, we have

\displaystyle S(G,k)=S(G-uv,k)-S(G_{\mid uv},k)

\displaystyle\quad\forall uv\in E(G),

(1)

\displaystyle S(G,k)=S(G+uv,k)+S(G_{\mid uv},k)

\displaystyle\quad\forall uv\notin E(G).

(2)

It follows that

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{B}(G)=\mathcal{B}(G-uv)-\mathcal{B}(G_{\mid uv})\\
\mathcal{T}(G)=\mathcal{T}(G-uv)-\mathcal{T}(G_{\mid uv})\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\forall uv\in E(G),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{B}(G)=\mathcal{B}(G+uv)+\mathcal{B}(G_{\mid uv})\\
\mathcal{T}(G)=\mathcal{T}(G+uv)+\mathcal{T}(G_{\mid uv})\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\forall uv\notin E(G).

Theorem 1
.

Given any two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we have

\mathcal{A}(G_{1}+G_{2})=\mathcal{A}(G_{1})+\mathcal{A}(G_{2}).

Proof.

As observed in
[
1
]
, given any coloring of
G_{1}+G_{2}
, none of the vertices of
G_{1}
can share a color with a vertex of
G_{2}
, which immediately gives
\mathcal{B}(G_{1}+G_{2})=\mathcal{B}(G_{1})\mathcal{B}(G_{2})
. For
\mathcal{T}(G_{1}+G_{2})
, assuming that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are of order
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, respectively, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(G_{1}+G_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}(k+k^{\prime})S(G_{1},k)S(G_{2},k^{\prime})=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}S(G_{1},k)\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}(k+k^{\prime})S(G_{2},k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}S(G_{1},k)\left(k\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}S(G_{2},k^{\prime})+\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}k^{\prime}S(G_{2},k^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}kS(G_{1},k)\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}S(G_{2},k^{\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}S(G_{1},k)\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n_{2}}k^{\prime}S(G_{2},k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(G_{1})\mathcal{B}(G_{2})+\mathcal{B}(G_{1})\mathcal{T}(G_{2}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(G_{1}+G_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G_{1}+G_{2})}{\mathcal{B}(G_{1}+G_{2})}=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G_{1})\mathcal{B}(G_{2})+\mathcal{B}(G_{1})\mathcal{T}(G_{2})}{\mathcal{B}(G_{1})\mathcal{B}(G_{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G_{1})}{\mathcal{B}(G_{1})}+\frac{\mathcal{T}(G_{2})}{\mathcal{B}(G_{2})}=\mathcal{A}(G_{1})+\mathcal{A}(G_{2}).

∎

10
]
.

Corollary 2
.

If
v
is a dominant vertex of a graph
G
, then,

\mathcal{A}(G)=\mathcal{A}(G-v)+1.

Proof.

If
v
is a dominant vertex of a graph
G
, then
G\simeq(G-v)+{\sf K}_{1}
, and since
\mathcal{A}({\sf K}_{1})=1
, Theorem
1
gives
\mathcal{A}(G)=\mathcal{A}(G-v)+1
.
∎

In the following, given a subset
W
of vertices in a graph
G
, we denote by
S_{W,i}(G,k)
the number of non-equivalent colorings of
G
that use exactly
k
colors, and where exactly
i
of them appear on
W
. Hence,
S(G,k)=\sum_{i=0}^{|W|}S_{W,i}(G,k)
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
v
be a vertex in a graph
G
of order
n
and let
N(v)
be its set of neighbors in
G
. Then

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(G)=\mathcal{B}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)
, and

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(G)=\mathcal{T}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k(k-i)+1)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)
.

Proof.

Since
S_{N(v),i}(G,k)=S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k-1)+(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S(G,k)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}S_{N(v),i}(G,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k-1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}kS(G,k)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}kS_{N(v),i}(G,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}kS_{N(v),i}(G-v,k-1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}k(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k+1)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}k(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}kS_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k(k-i)+1)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k(k-i)+1)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k).\qed

Theorem 4
.

Let
v
be a vertex in a graph
G
. If
\chi(G[N(v)])\geq|N(v)|-3
then
\mathcal{A}(G)>\mathcal{A}(G-v)
.

Proof.

Let
n
be the order of
G
. We know from Lemma
3
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(G)-\mathcal{A}(G-v)=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G-v)+a}{\mathcal{B}(G-v)+b}-\frac{\mathcal{T}(G-v)}{\mathcal{B}(G-v)}=\frac{a\mathcal{B}(G-v)-b\mathcal{T}(G-v)}{\mathcal{B}(G)\mathcal{B}(G-v)}

where
\displaystyle a=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k(k-i)+1)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)
and

\displaystyle b=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}(k-i)S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)
.

It suffices to show that
a\mathcal{B}(G{-}v){-}b\mathcal{T}(G{-}v)>0
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of pairs
(k,i)
such that
S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k){>}0
. Since
\chi(G[N(v)])\geq|N(v)|-3
, we have
k\geq i\geq|N(v)|-3
for all
(k,i)\in\mathcal{P}
.
For two pairs
(k,i)
and
(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{P}
, we write
(k,i){>}(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
if
k{>}k^{\prime}
or
k{=}k^{\prime}
and
i{>}i^{\prime}
. Also, let
f(k,k^{\prime},i,i^{\prime})=S_{N(v),i}(G{-}v,k)S_{N(v),i^{\prime}}(G{-}v,k^{\prime})
. We have

\displaystyle\quad a\mathcal{B}(G{-}v)-b\mathcal{T}(G{-}v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad a\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)-b\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{|N(v)|}kS_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,i)\in\mathcal{P}}S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)^{2}\Bigl({(k(k-i)+1)-(k-i)k}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{(k,i)>(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})}f(k,k^{\prime},i,i^{\prime})\Bigl({(k(k{-}i){+}1){+}(k^{\prime}(k^{\prime}{-}i^{\prime}){+}1){-}(k{-}i)k^{\prime}{-}(k^{\prime}{-}i^{\prime})k}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{(k,i)\in\mathcal{P}}S_{N(v),i}(G{-}v,k)^{2}{+}\sum_{(k,i){>}(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})}f(k,k^{\prime},i,i^{\prime})\Bigl({(k{-}k^{\prime})^{2}{-}(k{-}k^{\prime})(i{-}i^{\prime}){+}2}\Bigr).

Note that
\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
since
S_{N(v),|N(v)|}(G-v,n-1)=1
. Hence,
\sum_{(k,i)\in\mathcal{P}}S_{N(v),i}(G-v,k)^{2}{>}0
, and it is sufficient to prove that
(k-k^{\prime})^{2}-(k-k^{\prime})(i-i^{\prime})+2\geq 0

for every two pairs
(k,i)
and
(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{P}
with
(k,i){>}(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
. For two such pairs
(k,i)
and
(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
, we have
i-i^{\prime}\leq|N(v)|-(|N(v)|-3)=3
. Hence,

•

if
k=k^{\prime}
, then
(k-k^{\prime})^{2}-(k-k^{\prime})(i-i^{\prime})+2=2>0
;

•

if
k=k^{\prime}+1
, then
(k-k^{\prime})^{2}-(k-k^{\prime})(i-i^{\prime})+2=3-(i-i^{\prime})\geq 0
;

•

if
k=k^{\prime}+2
, then
(k-k^{\prime})^{2}-(k-k^{\prime})(i-i^{\prime})+2=6-2(i-i^{\prime})\geq 0
;

•

if
k\geq k^{\prime}+3
, then
(k-k^{\prime})^{2}-(k-k^{\prime})(i-i^{\prime})+2\geq 2
.∎

Corollary 5
.

If
v
is a vertex of degree at most 4 in a graph
G
, then
\mathcal{A}(G)>\mathcal{A}(G-v)
.

Proof.

Since
|N(v)|\leq 4
, we have:

•

if
N(v)=\emptyset
, then
\chi(G[N(v)])=0>-3=|N(v)|-3
;

•

if
N(v)\neq\emptyset
, then
\chi(G[N(v)])\geq 1\geq|N(v)|-3
.

In both cases, we conclude from Theorem
4
that
\mathcal{A}(G)>\mathcal{A}(G-v)
.

∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
v
be a simplicial vertex in a graph
G
. Then
\mathcal{A}(G)>\mathcal{A}(G-v)
.

Proof.

Since
v
is simplicial in
G
, we have
\chi(G[N(v)])=|N(v)|>|N(v)|-3
. Hence, we conclude from Theorem
4
that
\mathcal{A}(G)>\mathcal{A}(G-v)
.

∎

Theorem 7
.

Let
v
be a simplicial vertex of degree at least one in a graph
G
of order
n
, and let
w
be one of its neighbors in
G
.
Then
\mathcal{A}(G)>\mathcal{A}(G-vw)
.

Proof.

Let
H=(G-v)\cup{\sf K}_{1}
. In other words,
H
is obtained from
G-v
by adding an isolated vertex. It follows from Lemma
3
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(H)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{0}(k-i)S_{\emptyset,i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}kS(G-v,k)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(H)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{0}(k(k-i)+1)S_{\emptyset,i}(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(k^{2}+1)S(G-v,k).

Also, since
S_{N(v),i}(G{-}v,k){=}0
for
i\neq|N(v)|
, we have
S(G{-}v,k){=}S_{N(v),|N(v)|}(G{-}v,k)
and it follows from Lemma
3
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(G)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(k-|N(v)|)S(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathcal{B}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}kS(G-v,k)\Big)-|N(v)|\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}S(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(H)-|N(v)|\mathcal{B}(G-v)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(G)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Big(k(k-|N(v)|)+1\Big)S(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathcal{T}(G-v)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(k^{2}+1)S(G-v,k)\Big)-|N(v)|\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}kS(G-v,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(H)-|N(v)|\mathcal{T}(G-v).

Similarly, since
v
is simplicial (of degree
|N(v)|-1
) in
G-vw
, we have

\mathcal{B}(G-vw)=\mathcal{B}(H)-(|N(v)|-1)\mathcal{B}(G-v)

and

\mathcal{T}(G-vw)=\mathcal{T}(H)-(|N(v)|-1)\mathcal{T}(G-v).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(G)-\mathcal{A}(G-vw)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G)}{\mathcal{B}(G)}-\frac{\mathcal{T}(G-vw)}{\mathcal{B}(G-vw)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(H){-}|N(v)|\mathcal{T}(G{-}v)}{\mathcal{B}(H){-}|N(v)|\mathcal{B}(G{-}v)}{-}\frac{\mathcal{T}(H){-}(|N(v)|{-}1)\mathcal{T}(G{-}v)}{\mathcal{B}(H){-}(|N(v)|{-}1)\mathcal{B}(G{-}v)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(H)\mathcal{B}(G{-}v){-}\mathcal{B}(H)\mathcal{T}(G{-}v)}{\mathcal{B}(G)\mathcal{B}(G{-}vw)}.

Since
v
is isolated in
H
, it is simplicial and we know from Corollary
6
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(H)>\mathcal{A}(G-v)

\displaystyle\iff\frac{\mathcal{T}(H)}{\mathcal{B}(H)}>\frac{\mathcal{T}(G-v)}{\mathcal{B}(G-v)}

, with equality if and only
G\simeq{\sf K}_{2}{\cup}(n{-}2){\sf K}_{1}
.
∎

The proofs that Conjectures
15
and
16

\Delta(G)=2

Lemma 20
.

\mathcal{A}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})>\mathcal{A}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})
for all
n\geq 3
and all graphs
G
.

Proof.

Let
H\simeq{\sf P}_{2}
if
n=3
and
H\simeq{\sf C}_{n-1}
if
n>3
. We know from Equations (
3
) that
\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})=\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})-\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)
and
\mathcal{T}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})=\mathcal{T}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})-\mathcal{T}(G\cup H)
. Since
{\sf P}_{n}
is a partial subgraph of
{\sf C}_{n}
, we have
\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})>\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})
. Altogether, this gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})-\mathcal{A}(G\cup H)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})}-\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup H)}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})-\mathcal{T}(G\cup H)}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})-\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)}-\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup H)}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)-\mathcal{T}(G\cup H)\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)}

\displaystyle>\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)-\mathcal{T}(G\cup H)\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})}-\frac{\mathcal{T}(G\cup H)}{\mathcal{B}(G\cup H)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n})-\mathcal{A}(G\cup H)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(G\cup{\sf C}_{n})>\mathcal{A}(G\cup{\sf P}_{n}).

For
n\geq 3
, let
{\sf Q}_{n}
be the graph obtained from
{\sf P}_{n}
by adding an edge between an extremity
v
of
{\sf P}_{n}
and the vertex at distance 2 from
v
on
{\sf P}_{n}
.

Lemma 21
.

If
n\geq 3
,
0\leq x\leq p
and
1\leq k\leq n
, then

S({\sf Q}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)=\sum_{i=0}^{x}{x\choose i}S({\sf Q}_{n+i}\cup(p-x){\sf K}_{1},k).

Proof.

The result is clearly true for
p=0
. For larger values of
p
, we proceed by induction. Since the result is clearly true for
x=0
, we assume
x\geq 1
. Equations (
2
) imply

\displaystyle S({\sf Q}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=S({\sf Q}_{n+1}\cup(p{-}1){\sf K}_{1},k)+S({\sf Q}_{n}\cup(p{-}1){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{x-1}{x{-}1\choose i}S({\sf Q}_{n+i+1}\cup(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)+\sum_{i=0}^{x-1}{x{-}1\choose i}S({\sf Q}_{n+i}\cup(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{x}{x{-}1\choose i{-}1}S({\sf Q}_{n+i}\cup(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)+\sum_{i=0}^{x-1}{x{-}1\choose i}S({\sf Q}_{n+i}\cup(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S({\sf Q}_{n{+}x}{\cup}(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k){+}S({\sf Q}_{n}{\cup}(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{x-1}\left({x{-}1\choose i{-}1}+{x{-}1\choose i}\right)S({\sf Q}_{n{+}i}{\cup}(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{x}{x\choose i}S({\sf Q}_{n+i}\cup(p{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k).\vskip-28.45274pt\qed

Lemma 22
.

If
n\geq 3
is an odd number and
1\leq k\leq n
, then

S({\sf C}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)=\sum_{i=0}^{(n-3)/2}S({\sf Q}_{2i+3}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k).

Proof.

The result is clearly true for
n=3
since
{\sf C}_{3}\simeq{\sf Q}_{3}
. For larger values of
n
, we proceed by induction. It follows from Equations (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle S({\sf C}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=S({\sf P}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1})-S({\sf C}_{n-1}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(S({\sf Q}_{n}{\cup}p{\sf K}_{1},k){+}S({\sf P}_{n{-}1}{\cup}p{\sf K}_{1},k)\Bigr){-}\Bigl(S({\sf P}_{n{-}1}{\cup}p{\sf K}_{1},k){-}S({\sf C}_{n{-}2}{\cup}p{\sf K}_{1},k)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=S({\sf Q}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)+S({\sf C}_{n-2}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=S({\sf Q}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)+\sum_{i=0}^{(n-5)/2}S({\sf Q}_{2i+3}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{(n-3)/2}S({\sf Q}_{2i+3}\cup p{\sf K}_{1},k).

∎

Lemma 23
.

If
n
and
x
are two numbers such that

and

\smash{S}({\sf C}_{3}\cup(n-3){\sf K}_{1},k)=S({\sf C}_{x}\cup(n-x){\sf K}_{1},k)+\sum_{i=0}^{x-5}\alpha_{i}S({\sf Q}_{i+4}\cup(n-x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\alpha_{i}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{x-3\choose i}-1&\mbox{if }i\mbox{ is even}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle{x-3\choose i}&\mbox{if }i\mbox{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
{\sf Q}_{3}\simeq{\sf C}_{3}
we know from Lemma

that

\displaystyle S({\sf Q}_{3}\cup(n-3){\sf K}_{1},k)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{x-3}{x-3\choose i}S({\sf Q}_{i+3}{\cup}(n-x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{(x{-}3)/2}{x{-}3\choose 2i}S({\sf Q}_{2i+3}{\cup}(n{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle{+}\sum_{i=0}^{(x{-}5)/2}{x{-}3\choose 2i+1}S({\sf Q}_{2i+4}\cup(n{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k).

It then follows from Lemma

that

\displaystyle S({\sf Q}_{3}{\cup}(n{-}3){\sf K}_{1},k){=}

\displaystyle S({\sf C}_{x}{\cup}(n{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k){+}\sum_{i=1}^{(x{-}5)/2}\left({x{-}3\choose 2i}{-}1\right)S({\sf Q}_{2i+3}{\cup}(n{-}x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{(x-5)/2}{x-3\choose 2i+1}S({\sf Q}_{2i+4}\cup(n-x){\sf K}_{1},k)

\displaystyle S({\sf C}_{x}\cup(n-x){\sf K}_{1},k)+\sum_{i=0}^{x-5}\alpha_{i}S({\sf Q}_{i+4}\cup(n-x){\sf K}_{1},k).

If

Since

by iteratively adding vertices of degree

, we know from Corollary

\mathcal{A}({\sf Q}_{i}\cup p{\sf K}_{1})\leq\mathcal{A}({\sf Q}_{n-1}\cup p{\sf K}_{1})
. Moreover, it is proved in

\mathcal{A}({\sf Q}_{n-1}\cup p{\sf K}_{1})<\mathcal{A}({\sf P}_{n}\cup p{\sf K}_{1})

Corollary 25
.

If
n\geq 5
,

, then

\mathcal{A}({\sf C}_{3}\cup(n-3){\sf K}_{1})<\mathcal{A}({\sf C}_{x}\cup(n-x){\sf K}_{1}).

Lemma

Theorem 26
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
with
\Delta(G)=2
.
Then,

\mathcal{A}(G)\geq L_{2}(n,\chi(G))

with equality if and only if
G\simeq{\sf K}_{\chi(G)}\cup(n{-}\chi(G)){\sf K}_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
\Delta(G)=2
,
G
is the disjoint union of paths and cycles. If
G
does not contain any odd cycle, then
\chi(G)=2
. It then follows from Theorem
7
and Lemma
20
that the edges of
G
can be removed sequentially, with a strict decrease of
\mathcal{A}(G)
at each step, until we get
{\sf K}_{2}\cup(n{-}2){\sf K}_{1}
.

If
\chi(G){=}3
, then at least one connected component of
G
is an odd cycle
{\sf C}_{x}
with
x{\leq}n
. Again, we know from Theorem
7
and Lemma
20
that the edges of
G
can be removed sequentially, with a strict decrease of
\mathcal{A}(G)
at each step, until we get
{\sf C}_{x}\cup(n{-}x){\sf K}_{1}
. It then follows from Corollary
25
that
\mathcal{A}(G)\geq\mathcal{A}({\sf C}_{x}\cup(n{-}x){\sf K}_{1})\geq\mathcal{A}({\sf C}_{3}\cup(n{-}3){\sf K}_{1})
, with equalities if and only if
G\simeq{\sf C}_{3}\cup(n{-}3){\sf K}_{1}\simeq{\sf K}_{3}\cup(n{-}3){\sf K}_{1}
.

∎

Theorem 27
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
with
\Delta(G)=2
.
Then,

\mathcal{A}(G)\geq L_{3}(n,\Delta(G)+1)

with equality if and only if
G\simeq{\sf K}_{1,2}\cup(n{-}3){\sf K}_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
\Delta(G)=2
,
G
is the disjoint union of paths and cycles. Also,
G
contains at least one vertex
u
of degree 2. Let
v
and
w
be two neighbors of
u
in
G
. It follows from Theorem
7
and Lemma
20
that the edges of
G
can be removed sequentially, with a strict decrease of
\mathcal{A}(G)
at each step, until the edge set of the remaining graph
H
is
\{uv,uw\}
. But
H
is then isomorphic to
{\sf K}_{1,2}\cup(n{-}3){\sf K}_{1}
.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

We have established several properties for a recently defined graph invariant, namely the average number
\mathcal{A}(G)
of colors in the non-equivalent colorings of a graph
G
. We then looked at bounds for
\mathcal{A}(G)
. It is easy to prove that
\mathcal{A}(G)\leq\mathcal{A}({\sf K}_{n})=n
for all graphs of order
n
, with equality if and only if
G\simeq{\sf K}_{n}
. Hence,
n
is the best possible upper bound on
\mathcal{A}(G)
for a graph
G
of order
n
. We think that the best possible lower bound on
\mathcal{A}(G)
for a graph
G
of order
n
is
\mathcal{A}(\overline{{\sf K}}_{n})
. We have shown that despite its apparent simplicity, this conjecture cannot be proven using simple techniques like sequential edge removal. We have then refined this conjecture by proposing lower bounds related to the chromatic number
\chi(G)
and to the maximum degree
\Delta(G)
of
G
. We have thus stated three open problems. We have shown that these three conjectures are true for triangulated graphs and for graphs with maximum degree at most 2.

Optimal collection of radiation emitted by a trapped atomic ensemble
Á. Kurkó, P. Domokos, A. Vukics, T. Bækkegaard, N. T. Zinner, J. Fortágh, D. Petrosyan

Abstract
Trapped atomic ensembles are convenient systems for quantum information storage in the long-lived sublevels of the electronic ground state and its conversion to propagating optical photons via stimulated Raman processes. Here we investigate a phase-matched emission of photons from a coherently prepared atomic ensemble. We consider an ensemble of cold atoms in an elongated harmonic trap with normal density distribution, and determine the parameters of paraxial optics to match the mode geometry of the emitted radiation and optimally collect it into an optical waveguide.



I
Introduction

An important yet difficult task in quantum information and communication is
deterministic generation of optical photons in well-defined spatial and temporal modes.
Photons can serve as flying qubits to encode quantum information and reliably transmit
it over long-distances via quantum channels
Kimble2008
;
OBrien2009
.
For optical photons, such quantum channels are free space or fiber waveguides.
On the other hand, atomic ensembles have good coherence properties and strong dipole transitions
for efficient coupling to optical photons
EITrev2005
;
Hammerer2010
;
Sangouard2011
.
Moreover, atoms can couple to microwave fields and thereby be interfaced with superconducting circuits,
which are among the most advanced quantum processors
Kurizki2015
.
The atomic ensembles can then play the role of the quantum memories and microwave to optical transducers

Kurizki2015
;
Petrosyan2019
;
Covey2019
to interconnect distant superconducting quantum circuits via optical fibers.

Here we consider photon emission from an atomic ensemble coherently prepared in a collective storage
state corresponding to a single symmetric spin-wave excitation. This preparation step may be the result of
collective coupling of alkali atoms on the ground state hyperfine transition
Verdu2009
;
Hattermann2017

or on the highly excited Rydberg transition
Petrosyan2009
;
Hogan2012
to a microwave resonator that in turn
contains superconducting qubits. A spontaneous Raman process triggered by a coupling laser pulse converts
the collective spin excitation of the atoms to an optical photon emitted predominantly in the phase-matched direction.
We examine the spatio-temporal profile of the emitted photon and determine the parameters of the paraxial optical elements
to optimally collect this photon into a single-transverse-mode optical waveguide.
The waveguide can then transmit the photon encoding the quantum information to a distant atomic ensemble, where a reverse process,
equivalent to dynamical light storage in an electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) medium
EITrev2005
,
will coherently convert the optical excitation to a collective microwave spin excitation.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
and in Appendices
A
and
B
, we present the mathematical
formalism to describe the photon emission by coherently prepared atoms and photon collection by Gaussian optics.
In Sec.
III
we present the results of analytic and numerical calculations for optimal photon collection,
followed by conclusions in Sec.
IV
.

Interaction of atoms with a radiation field

Consider an ensemble of
N
three-level atoms with the long-lived ground-state sublevels
\,|g\rangle
and
\,|s\rangle

and an electronically excited level
\,|e\rangle
, as shown in Fig.
1
(a).
We assume that the atoms initially in the ground state
\,|G\rangle\equiv\,|g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{N}\rangle

are transferred to the collective single-excitation storage state

\,|S\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,|g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,s_{j},\ldots,g_{N}\rangle

by a weak microwave or Raman process that acts symmetrically on all the atoms.
A spatially uniform laser pulse couples near-resonantly the storage state
\,|s\rangle

to the excited state
\,|e\rangle
with Rabi frequency
\Omega
. An atom at position

\bm{r}
in the excited state
\,|e\rangle
then emits a photon into the free-space radiation field

\hat{E}(\bm{r})=\sum_{k}\hat{a}_{k}u_{k}(\bm{r})
, with modes
\{u_{k}(\bm{r})\}
forming a complete basis,
and decays to the ground state
\,|g\rangle
. The Hamiltonian of the system is

H=\sum_{k}\hbar\omega_{k}\hat{a}_{k}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\mu=g,s,e}\hbar\omega_{\mu}\,|\mu\rangle_{j}\langle\mu|\,\\
-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Bigl[\hbar\Omega e^{i(\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}-\omega_{c}t)}\,|e\rangle_{j}\langle s|\,\\
+\bm{\wp}_{eg}\cdot\hat{E}(\bm{r}_{j})\,|e\rangle_{j}\langle g|\,+\mathrm{H.c}\Bigr],

(1)

where the first term on the r.h.s. is the Hamiltonian for the field modes with energies
\hbar\omega_{k}
,
the second term corresponds to the Bohr energies
\hbar\omega_{\mu}
of the atomic levels
\,|\mu\rangle
(
\mu=g,s,e
),
the third term describes the interaction of the atoms with the coupling laser with frequency
\omega_{c}
and wavevector

\bm{k}_{c}\parallel\hat{\bm{z}}
, and the last term describes the coupling of the atoms to the free-space radiation field
with the dipole moment
\bm{\wp}_{eg}
on the transition
\,|e\rangle\to\,|g\rangle
. We set the energy of the ground state to zero,

\hbar\omega_{g}=0
, and assume that
\omega_{s}\ll\omega_{e}
(
\omega_{c}\simeq\omega_{e}
).

The state vector of the system with the single atomic or photonic excitation can be expanded as

\displaystyle\,|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}c_{j}e^{-i\omega_{s}t}\,|s_{j}\rangle\otimes\,|0\rangle+\sum_{j}b_{j}e^{-i\omega_{e}t}\,|e_{j}\rangle\otimes\,|0\rangle

\displaystyle+\,|G\rangle\otimes\sum_{k}a_{k}e^{-i\omega_{k}t}\,|1_{k}\rangle,

where
\,|s_{j}\rangle\equiv\,|g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,s_{j},\ldots,g_{N}\rangle
,

\,|e_{j}\rangle\equiv\,|g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,e_{j},\ldots,g_{N}\rangle
,

\,|G\rangle\equiv\,|g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{N}\rangle
, and

\,|1_{k}\rangle\equiv\hat{a}_{k}^{{\dagger}}\,|0\rangle
is the state of the radiation field with a single photon in mode
k
.
The state vector is normalized as
\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{j}(|c_{j}|^{2}+|b_{j}|^{2})+\sum_{k}|a_{k}|^{2}=1
,
and it evolves according to the Schrödinger equation
\partial_{t}\,|\Psi\rangle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}H\,|\Psi\rangle
,
leading to a set of equations for the atomic amplitudes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Omega^{*}e^{-i\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}b_{j}e^{i\Delta_{c}t},

(2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Omega e^{i\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}c_{j}e^{-i\Delta_{c}t}+i\sum_{k}g_{k}(\bm{r}_{j})a_{k}e^{i(\omega_{e}-\omega_{k})t},\qquad

(2b)

with
\Delta_{c}=\omega_{c}-\omega_{e}
, and an equation for the field amplitudes cast in the integral form

a_{k}(t)=i\sum_{j}g_{k}^{*}(\bm{r}_{j})\int_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}b_{j}(t^{\prime})e^{i(\omega_{k}-\omega_{e})t^{\prime}},

(3)

where
g_{k}(\bm{r}_{j})=\frac{\bm{\wp}_{eg}\cdot u_{k}(\bm{r}_{j})}{\hbar}
.

II.1
Atoms

We substitute Eq. (
3
) into Eq. (
2b
) and use the basis of the plane waves

u_{k}(\bm{r})=\hat{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{\bm{k},\sigma}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{k}}{2\epsilon_{0}V}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}

within the quantization volume
V
. We then replace the summation over the modes by an integration, and
use the Born-Markov approximation to eliminate the radiation field
ScullyZubary1997
;
PLDP2007
,
while neglecting the field-mediated interatomic interactions assuming sufficiently large mean interatomic
distance
k\langle|\bm{r}_{j}-\bm{r}_{i}|\rangle\gg 1

Lehmberg1970
;
Thirunamachandran
;
Miroshnychenko2013
.
We thus obtain the usual spontaneous decay rate

\Gamma=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\frac{4k_{e}^{3}|\wp_{eg}|^{2}}{3\hbar}
and the Lamb shift
of level
\,|e\rangle
that can be incorporated into
\omega_{e}
. Equations (
2
) reduce to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Omega^{*}e^{-i\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}b_{j}e^{i\Delta_{c}t},

(4a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Gamma/2\,b_{j}+i\Omega e^{i\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}c_{j}e^{-i\Delta_{c}t}.

(4b)

We next assume a resonant laser
\Delta_{c}=0
with sufficiently weak Rabi frequency
|\Omega|\ll\Gamma
and
set
\partial_{t}b_{j}=0
, obtaining
b_{j}\simeq i\frac{\Omega}{\Gamma/2}e^{i\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}}c_{j}
. Substituting
this into Eq. (
4a
) and performing the integration, we finally obtain

\displaystyle b_{j}(t)\simeq ic_{j}(0)\beta(t)e^{i\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}_{j}},

(5)

\displaystyle\beta(t)\equiv\frac{\Omega(t)}{\Gamma/2}\exp\left[-\int_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}\frac{|\Omega(t^{\prime})|^{2}}{\Gamma/2}\right],

with the initial condition
b_{j}(0)=0
and
c_{j}(0)=\frac{e^{i\phi_{j}}}{\sqrt{N}}\;\forall\;j\in\{1,N\}
,
where we included a spatial phase
\phi_{j}
of the single-excitation spin wave.
In Sec.
III
we will analyze the influence of this phase on the photon collection into Gaussian optical modes.

II.2
Field

We may define the wavefunction of the emitted single-photon field via

E(\bm{r},t)\equiv\langle 0|\,\langle G|\,\hat{E}(\bm{r})\,|\Psi(t)\rangle

ScullyZubary1997
;
PLDP2007
.
Assuming an isotropic dipole moment, in Appendix
A
we show that

E(\bm{r},t)\propto\sum_{j}\frac{e^{-i\omega_{e}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|/c)}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|}b_{j}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|/c)
,
and for an atomic ensemble with density
\rho(\bm{r})
, the emitted field is

E(\bm{r},t)=\frac{\wp_{eg}k_{e}^{2}}{\sqrt{3}\,2\pi\epsilon_{0}}\int\!d^{3}r^{\prime}\rho(\bm{r}^{\prime})\,\frac{e^{-i\omega_{e}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|/c)}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|}\\
\times b_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|/c)\,.

(6)

Paraxial optics

We assume that
N\gg 1
atoms in a harmonic trap have a cylindrically symmetric, normal density distribution

\rho(\bm{r})=N\frac{e^{-(x^{2}+y^{2})/2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}-z^{2}/2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}\sigma_{z}}

(7)

with the width
\sigma_{\perp}
and length
\sigma_{z}
(standard deviations).
Consistent with the assumption of closely spaced energy levels
\,|g\rangle
and
\,|s\rangle
(
\omega_{c}\simeq\omega_{e}
),
we have
k_{c}\simeq k_{e}
such that
|k_{c}-k_{e}|\ll 1/\sigma_{z}
.
The radiation emitted in the phase-matched direction
\bm{k}_{e}\simeq\bm{k}_{c}\parallel\hat{\bm{z}}

is collected by paraxial optics which feeds it into a waveguide with a single transverse mode and a continuum of
1D modes
k
, see Fig.
1
(b). The atomic ensemble is placed at the focus of the collecting optics
with the beam waist
w_{0}
. The corresponding field modes that couple to the waveguide are then the Gaussian modes

\displaystyle v_{k}(\bm{r})=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{k}}{2\epsilon_{0}AL}}\,\upsilon_{k}(\bm{r}),

(8a)

\displaystyle\upsilon_{k}(\bm{r})\equiv\frac{\zeta_{k}}{q_{k}^{*}(z)}\exp\left[ik\left(z+\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2q_{k}^{*}(z)}\right)\right]

(8b)

\displaystyle=i\frac{w_{0}}{w(z)}\exp\left[ikz-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{w(z)^{2}}+i\frac{k(x^{2}+y^{2})}{2R(z)}-i\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}\right],

where
A=\pi w_{0}^{2}/2
is the cross-section at the focus
z=0
and
L
is the quantization length,

\zeta_{k}=kw_{0}^{2}/2
is the Rayleigh length and
q_{k}(z)=z+i\zeta_{k}
is the complex beam parameter,

w(z)=w_{0}\sqrt{1+z^{2}/\zeta_{k}^{2}}
is the transverse beam size at position
z
,

R(z)=z+\zeta_{k}^{2}/z
is the radius of curvature of the phase front, and

\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}=\arctan(z/\zeta_{k_{e}})
is the Gouy phase.

Our aim is to maximize the collection of radiation emitted by the atoms by the paraxial optical elements.
If we, however, attempt to calculate the overlap between the single-photon field of Eq. (
6
)
and the resonant
k=k_{e}
Gaussian mode of Eq. (
8
) as a volume integral

\left|\int\!d^{3}r\,v_{k_{e}}^{*}(\bm{r})E(\bm{r})\right|^{2}
, it will diverge inside
the atomic ensemble, where
\rho(\bm{r}^{\prime})\neq 0
, due to the
|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{-1}
term.

Photon number in the forward Gaussian modes

An alternative, more tractable approach that we use here is to calculate directly
the total number of photons
n(t)
emitted into the 1D continuum
\{k\}
of
the forward Gaussian modes
v_{k}(\bm{r})
all having the same waist
w_{0}
at
z=0
.
We have

\displaystyle n(t)=\sum_{k}|a_{k}(t)|^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle a_{k}(t)=i\sum_{j}g^{*}_{k}(\bm{r}_{j})\int_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}\,b_{j}(t^{\prime})e^{i(\omega_{k}-\omega_{e})t^{\prime}},

where
g_{k}(\bm{r}_{j})=\frac{\wp_{eg}}{\hbar}v_{k}(\bm{r}_{j})
is now the coupling strength of atom
j

at position
\bm{r}_{j}
to the
k
’s Gaussian mode of Eq. (
8
).

In Appendix
B
we show that for a single atom placed at the origin,
\bm{r}_{j}=0
,
the amount of radiation collected by the Gaussian paraxial optics,

n(t)=\frac{\varsigma}{2A}\Gamma\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,|b(t^{\prime})|^{2},

(10)

is proportional to the ratio of the atomic absorption cross-section
\varsigma=3\lambda_{e}^{2}/2\pi

to the cross-section
A=\pi w_{0}^{2}/2
of the focused Gaussian beam at the atomic position.
For many atoms
N
with the density distribution of Eq. (
7
), we have

n(t)=\frac{|\wp_{eg}|^{2}}{2\pi\hbar\epsilon_{0}cA}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\omega_{k}\,\omega_{k}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}^{N}\upsilon_{k}^{*}(\bm{r}_{j})\upsilon_{k}(\bm{r}_{j^{\prime}})\\
\times\iint_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}dt^{\prime\prime}\,b_{j}(t^{\prime})b_{j^{\prime}}^{*}(t^{\prime\prime})e^{i(\omega_{k}-\omega_{e})(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})}\\
=\frac{|\wp_{eg}|^{2}}{2\pi\hbar\epsilon_{0}cA}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\omega_{k}\,\omega_{k}\iint\!d^{3}r\,d^{3}r^{\prime}\rho(\bm{r})\upsilon_{k}^{*}(\bm{r})\rho(\bm{r}^{\prime})\upsilon_{k}(\bm{r}^{\prime})\\
\times\iint_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}dt^{\prime\prime}\,b_{\bm{r}}(t^{\prime})b_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}^{*}(t^{\prime\prime})e^{i(\omega_{k}-\omega_{e})(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})}.

(11)

Substituting here Eq. (
5
) with
c_{\bm{r}}(0)=e^{i\phi(\bm{r})}/\sqrt{N}
, where
\phi(\bm{r})

is a spatial phase profile of the stored spin-wave, we have

\displaystyle n(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\wp_{eg}|^{2}}{2\pi\hbar\epsilon_{0}cA}\frac{1}{N}\iint_{0}^{t}\!dt^{\prime}dt^{\prime\prime}\,\beta(t^{\prime})\beta(t^{\prime\prime})

(12)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\omega_{k}\,\omega_{k}\,\Xi_{k}e^{i(\omega_{k}-\omega_{e})(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})},

\displaystyle\quad\Xi_{k}\equiv\left|\int\!d^{3}r\rho(\bm{r})\upsilon_{k}^{*}(\bm{r})e^{i\bm{k}_{c}\cdot\bm{r}+i\phi(\bm{r})}\right|^{2}.

We can proceed along the Weisskopf-Wigner approximation
ScullyZubary1997
;
Miroshnychenko2013

by replacing
\omega_{k}
and
\Xi_{k}
by their resonant values
\omega_{e}
and
\Xi_{k_{e}}
and pulling them out
of the frequency integral, which, upon extending the lower limit of integration, reduces to
2\pi\delta(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})
.
We then obtain

n(t)=\frac{\varsigma}{2A}|\xi|^{2}\,N\Gamma B(t),

(13)

where
B(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\beta^{2}(t^{\prime})
contains the time-dependence of the photon envelope,
while the overlap integral is

\xi\equiv\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}\sigma_{z}}\int\!d^{3}r\exp\left(i\phi(\bm{r})-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}\right)\\
\times\frac{\zeta_{k_{e}}}{q_{k_{e}}(z)}\exp\left(-ik_{e}\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2q_{k_{e}}(z)}\right),

(14)

where we used that
k_{e}\simeq k_{c}
(
\bm{k}_{c}\parallel\hat{\bm{z}}
).
Note that in Eq. (
13
) the geometric factor is

\frac{\varsigma}{2A}|\xi|^{2}=\frac{6|\xi|^{2}}{(k_{e}w_{0})^{2}}\equiv\mathcal{G}\,.

(15)

Equations (
13
)-(
14
) are the central equations of this paper.
Our aim is to find the optimal value for the Gaussian beam waist
w_{0}
that maximizes
the probability of collecting the photon
n(\infty)\propto\mathcal{G}N
emitted by
an ensemble of
N
trapped atoms with the given width and length
\sigma_{\perp},\sigma_{z}

(standard deviations) of the density distribution.

III
Optimal photon collection

We now present the results of our calculations for three different cases
of the initial spatial phase distribution in the atomic cloud centered at the beam focus.

III.0.1
Uniform spatial phase

Assume that the initial atomic spin-wave has a uniform spatial phase

\phi(\bm{r})=0\,\forall\,\bm{r}.

(16)

Consider first the case of the atom cloud with the spatial dimensions
much smaller than the Rayleigh length,
\sigma_{\perp},\sigma_{z}\ll\zeta_{k_{e}}
.
The mode function appearing in Eg. (
14
) can then be approximated as

\frac{\zeta_{k_{e}}}{q_{k_{e}}(z)}\exp\left(-ik_{e}\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2q_{k_{e}}(z)}\right)\simeq\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{w_{0}^{2}}+i\frac{z}{\zeta_{k_{e}}}\right),

where we have used that near the focus the beam waist is
w_{0}
, the beam radius of curvature
R
tends to infinity,
while the Gouy phase can be linearized as
\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}\simeq z/\zeta_{k_{e}}
.
Equation (
14
) reduces to a Gaussian integral and we obtain

\displaystyle|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{w_{0}^{4}}{(2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}+w_{0}^{2})^{2}}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{2\sigma_{z}}{k_{e}w_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right]

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{w}_{0}^{4}}{(2\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{2}+\bar{w}_{0}^{2})^{2}}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{2\bar{\sigma}_{z}}{\bar{w}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right],

where the dimensionless quantities are defined as
\bar{\sigma}_{z,\perp}\equiv k_{e}\sigma_{z,\perp}
and
\bar{w}_{0}\equiv k_{e}w_{0}
.
The optimal beam waist that maximizes the geometric factor
\mathcal{G}=6|\xi|^{2}/\bar{w}_{0}^{2}
is

\bar{w}^{\text{max}}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\left[\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{2}+P(\bar{\sigma}_{\perp},\bar{\sigma}_{z})+\frac{\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{4}+6\bar{\sigma}_{z}^{2}}{P(\bar{\sigma}_{\perp},\bar{\sigma}_{z})}\right]},

(18)

P(\bar{\sigma}_{\perp},\bar{\sigma}_{z})=\Bigl[\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{6}+36\bar{\sigma}^{2}_{\perp}\bar{\sigma}_{z}^{2}\\
+3\sqrt{6\bar{\sigma}_{z}^{2}\left(\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{8}+22\bar{\sigma}^{4}_{\perp}\bar{\sigma}_{z}^{2}-4\bar{\sigma}_{z}^{4}\right)}\Bigr]^{1/3}.

In the limit of
\sigma_{z}\to 0
, the photon collection efficiency is maximized for
w^{\text{max}}_{0}=\sqrt{2}\sigma_{\perp}
,
with
|\xi|^{2}\to 1/4
and thus
\mathcal{G}\simeq 3/4\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{2}
.
This is of course an intuitive result meaning that the waist of the Gaussian beam should be equal
to the width of the atomic ensemble. As will be seen below, this result also applies to the atomic ensembles
with sufficiently large width, which is, however, not optimal for photon collection by the paraxial Gaussian optics.

When the atomic cloud is larger than the Rayleigh length, the integral of Eq. (
14
) can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\xi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{k_{e}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\sigma_{\perp}^{2}\sigma_{z}}\int\!dz\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}}{q^{*}_{k_{e}}(z)}

(19)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\!dxdy\exp\left[-(x^{2}+y^{2})\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}-i\frac{k_{e}}{2q^{*}_{k_{e}}(z)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{k_{e}}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}\,\sigma_{z}}\int dz\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}}{z-i\zeta_{k_{e}}-ik_{e}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}\frac{k_{e}w_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{z}}\,e^{\frac{\left(k_{e}w_{0}^{2}/2+k_{e}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}\right)^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\frac{k_{e}w_{0}^{2}/2+k_{e}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}\frac{\bar{w}_{0}^{2}}{\bar{\sigma}_{z}}\,e^{\frac{\left(\bar{w}_{0}^{2}/2+\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{2}\right)^{2}}{2\bar{\sigma}_{z}^{2}}}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\frac{\bar{w}_{0}^{2}/2+\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}\bar{\sigma}_{z}}\right).

In Fig.
2

\bar{w}^{\text{max}}_{0}

\mathcal{G}
for various values of
\bar{\sigma}_{z},\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}
.
The corresponding maxima for

We notice that for a wide range of values of

III.0.2
Spatial phase compensating the Gouy phase

Recall that the Gouy phase
\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}(z)=\arctan(z/\zeta_{k_{e}})
of the Gaussian beam varies with
z
.
We may therefore further improve the photon collection efficiency if we imprint onto the stored atomic spin-wave
a spatial phase that would compensate the Gouy phase,

\phi(\bm{r})=-\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}(z).

(20)

This can be achieved, e.g., by using spatially varying electric or magnetic fields or ac Stark shifts
induced by off-resonant lasers
Korzeczek2020
. Now the integral of Eq. (
14
) can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\xi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{k_{e}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\sigma_{\perp}^{2}\sigma_{z}}\int\!dz\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}}{|q^{*}_{k_{e}}(z)|}

(21)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\!dxdy\exp\left[-(x^{2}+y^{2})\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}-i\frac{k_{e}}{2q^{*}_{k_{e}}(z)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{k_{e}}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}\,\sigma_{z}}\int dz\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}}{|q^{*}_{k_{e}}(z)|}\cdot\frac{q^{*}_{k_{e}}(z)}{q^{*}_{k_{e}}(z)-ik_{e}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{k_{e}}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}\,\sigma_{z}}\int dz\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}-i\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}(z)}}{z-i\zeta_{k_{e}}-ik_{e}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}.

Alternatively, we can write

\displaystyle\!\!\xi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\sigma_{\perp}^{2}\sigma_{z}}\int\!dz\frac{w_{0}}{w(z)}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}

(22)

\displaystyle\;\times\int\!dxdy\exp\left[-(x^{2}+y^{2})\left(\frac{1}{w(z)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}-i\frac{k_{e}}{2R(z)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{iw_{0}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{z}}\int\!dz\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}w(z)R(z)}{2R(z)\sigma_{\perp}^{2}+w(z)^{2}R(z)-ik_{e}w(z)^{2}\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}.

We numerically maximize
|\xi|^{2}/(\bar{w}_{0})^{2}
for different values of
\bar{\sigma}_{z},\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}
, and
show the resulting
\bar{w}^{\text{max}}_{0}
in Fig.
2
(a2) and the corresponding maxima for
\mathcal{G}
in Fig.
2
(b2).
Note that while the overall behavior of the geometric factor is similar to the previous case of uniform spatial phase,
we nevertheless obtain good photon collection efficiency
n(\infty)\propto\mathcal{G}N\gtrsim 5
(
N=10^{3}
)
even for longer atomic clouds
\bar{\sigma}_{z}\lesssim 300
(
\sigma_{z}\lesssim 50\lambda_{e}
) since we compensate
for the Gouy phase that changes the sign across the focal point
z=0
. In other words, when a photon emitted
from a highly elongated atomic ensemble,
\sigma_{z}\gg\zeta_{k}
(
\bar{\sigma}_{z}\gg\bar{w}_{0}^{2}/2
), with
a uniform spatial phase
\phi(\bm{r})=0
is collected into a focused Gaussian mode,
the photon amplitudes originating from the regions of
z<0
and
z>0
interfere partially destructively,
while for the spatial phase
\phi(\bm{r})=-\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}(z)
this interference is constructive.

III.0.3
Full spatial phase compensation of a Gaussian beam

We finally examine the case of the spatial phase of the atomic spin wave having the same spatial dependence as that of a
focused Gaussian beam,

\phi(\bm{r})=k\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2R(z)}-\phi_{\mathrm{Gouy}}(z),

(23)

which would correspond to a photon from a Gaussian mode stored in an atomic ensemble,
e.g., via stopping a dark-state polariton in the EIT regime
EITrev2005
.
For the integral of Eq. (
14
) we then obtain

\displaystyle\xi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\sigma_{\perp}^{2}\sigma_{z}}\int\!d^{3}r\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}\right)

(24)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{w_{0}}{w(z)}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{w^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\frac{w_{0}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{z}}\int\!dze^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\sigma_{z}^{2}}}\frac{w(z)}{w(z)^{2}+2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}.

For a small atomic cloud,
w(z)\simeq w_{0}
, we have the analytic result
|\xi|^{2}=w_{0}^{4}/(w_{0}^{2}+2\sigma_{\perp}^{2})^{2}
,
which is the same as in Eq. (
17
) in the limit of
\sigma_{z}\rightarrow 0
. For larger cloud sizes,
we again maximize numerically
|\xi|^{2}/\bar{w}_{0}^{2}
for different values of
\bar{\sigma}_{z},\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}
,
obtaining
\bar{w}^{\text{max}}_{0}
and the corresponding maxima for
\mathcal{G}
which are shown, respectively,
in Fig.
2
(a3) and (b3). Even though
\bar{w}^{\text{max}}_{0}\simeq\sqrt{2}\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}

for even smaller values of
\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}
as compared to case 2 above, the maximum for the photon collective
efficiency
n(\infty)\propto\mathcal{G}N
has nearly the same dependence on
\bar{\sigma}_{z},\bar{\sigma}_{\perp}

as that with only the Gouy phase compensation. The physical reason for this result is that the curvature of the phase front
of a Gaussian mode plays only a minor role in narrow atomic ensembles yielding the largest photon collection efficiency.

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

Cold, trapped atoms can strongly interact with radiation of different frequencies and therefore
be used for coherently interfacing microwave and optical systems and components.
When converting atomic excitations to photons, an important consideration for efficient
collection of radiation is the directionality of the emitted photons.
A well-known approach for efficient photon collection is to place the atoms or other emitters into a resonant cavity
with sufficiently strong coupling so that the Purcell enhancement of the photoemission rate into the cavity mode
largely exceeds the free-space spontaneous decay rate with the photon emitted in a random direction
PLDP2007
.
In contrast, using wave-mixing or stimulated Raman processes, a coherent spin wave of a large atomic ensemble
can be converted into a photon emitted predominantly into the phase-matched direction with well-defined
temporal and spatial profile
Petrosyan2018
;
Petrosyan2019
.

In this paper, we have addressed the question of how to optimally collect this photon
created from the atomic spin wave by a stimulated Raman process.
Assuming an atomic ensemble with Gaussian density distribution – typical for cold atoms in harmonic traps –
we have found that the emitted photon is best mode-matched by a Gaussian optical mode with the waist
equal to the width of the atomic ensemble, provided it is not too small (larger than the wavelength)
[see Fig.
2
(a)].
For narrower ensembles, the optimal waist of the Gaussian beam should be larger, but the photon
collection efficiently decreases for highly elongated ensembles [see Fig.
2
(b)].
The main reason for this is the reduction of the spatial overlap between the atomic cloud and
the tightly focused Gaussian beam whose transverse width rapidly increases away from the focus.
Hence, compact, i.e. narrow and short, atomic ensembles offer the best mode matching with appropriately
focused Gaussian beams. But since increased probability of directional photoemission also requires
a large number of atoms
N
and large optical depth of the ensemble [see Eq. (
13
)],
compact atomic ensembles will have high density, which necessitates considerations of atom-atom
interactions and multiple scattering
Petrosyan2021
.

Appendix A
Single-photon wavefunction

The wavefunction of the emitted single-photon field can be defined via
ScullyZubary1997
;
PLDP2007
;
Miroshnychenko2013

\displaystyle E(\bm{r},t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle 0|\,\langle G|\,\hat{E}(\bm{r})\,|\Psi(t)\rangle=\sum_{k}u_{k}(\bm{r})a_{k}(t)e^{-i\omega_{k}t}

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sum_{k}u_{k}(\bm{r})\sum_{j}g_{k}^{*}(\bm{r}_{j})\int_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}b_{j}(t^{\prime})e^{-i\omega_{k}(t-t^{\prime})-i\omega_{e}t^{\prime}}.\qquad

For simplicity, we assume an isotropic dipole moment

\bm{\wp}_{eg}\cdot\hat{\bm{\varepsilon}}=\wp_{eg}/\sqrt{3}\,\forall\,|\hat{\bm{\varepsilon}}|=1

and use the basis of the plane waves, leading to

\displaystyle E(\bm{r},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\frac{\wp_{eg}}{\sqrt{3}(2\pi)^{3}\epsilon_{0}}\sum_{j}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}b_{j}(t^{\prime})e^{-i\omega_{e}t^{\prime}}

(26)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dkk^{2}\omega_{k}e^{-i\omega_{k}(t-t^{\prime})}\int_{4\pi}\!\!d\Omega_{k}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j})},\qquad

where we replaced the summation over the modes by an integration,

\sum_{k}\to 2\frac{V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\!d^{3}k=2\frac{V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dk\,k^{2}\int_{4\pi}\!d\Omega_{k}

with the factor of 2 accounting for the two orthogonal photon polarizations
\sigma=1,2
for each
\bm{k}

(
\hat{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{\bm{k},\sigma}\perp\bm{k}
). The integration over
the
4\pi
solid angle with
d\Omega_{k}=\sin\theta d\theta d\varphi
leads to

4\pi\frac{\sin(k|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|)}{k|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|}=-i\frac{2\pi c}{\omega_{k}|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|}(e^{ik|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|}-\mathrm{c.c})
.
We substitute this into the above equation, assume that during the photon emission
k

is peaked around the atomic resonance
k_{e}=\omega_{e}/c
and pull
k_{e}^{2}
out of the integral,
and extend the lower limit of integration over
k
to
-\infty
, as in the Weisskopf-Wigner
approximation
ScullyZubary1997
;
Miroshnychenko2013
. We then have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!dk(e^{ik|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|-ick(t-t^{\prime})}-e^{-ik|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|-ick(t-t^{\prime})})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{c}\delta(t^{\prime}-t+|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|/c)+\frac{2\pi}{c}\delta(t^{\prime}-t-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|/c).

Upon substitution into Eq. (
26

E(\bm{r},t)=\frac{\wp_{eg}k_{e}^{2}}{\sqrt{3}\,2\pi\epsilon_{0}}\sum_{j}\frac{e^{-i\omega_{e}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|/c)}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|}b_{j}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{j}|/c).

(27)

For a single atom at the origin,
\bm{r}_{j}=0

\bm{r}
and time

For an ensemble of
N\gg 1
atoms with density
\rho(\bm{r})

\int\!d^{3}r\rho(\bm{r})=N

the emitted field is then given by Eq. (

Single-atom emission into the Gaussian modes

Consider the photon emission by a single atom placed at the origin,
\bm{r}_{j}=0
,
and therefore
g_{k}(0)=i\frac{\wp_{eg}}{\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{k}}{2\epsilon_{0}LA}}

In Eq. (
9

\sum_{k}\to 2\frac{L}{2\pi c}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{k}

with a single atom contributing,

\displaystyle n(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\wp_{eg}|^{2}\omega_{e}}{\hbar\epsilon_{0}cA}\iint_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}dt^{\prime\prime}\,b(t^{\prime})b^{*}(t^{\prime\prime})\,\delta(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\frac{|\wp_{eg}|^{2}\omega_{e}}{\hbar\epsilon_{0}cA}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!dt^{\prime}\,|b(t^{\prime})|^{2}

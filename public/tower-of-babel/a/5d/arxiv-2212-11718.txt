Abstract
. The free energy of TAP-solutions for the SK-model of mean field spin glasses can be expressed as a nonlinear functional of local terms: we exploit this feature in order to contrive abstract REM-like models which we then solve by a classical large deviations treatment. This allows to identify the origin of the physically unsettling quadratic (in the inverse of temperature) correction to the Parisi free energy for the SK-model, and formalizes the true cavity dynamics which acts on TAP-space, i.e. on the space of TAP-solutions. From a non-spin glass point of view, this work is the first in a series of refinements which addresses the stability of hierarchical structures in models of evolving populations.

2.
SK, TAP, and Plefka.

The SK-model is the archetypical mean field spin glass: for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider centered Gaussians
(g_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
issued on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. These Gaussians,
the disorder
, are assumed to be all independent and with variance
1/N
. The SK-Hamiltonian, defined on the Ising configuration space is then

\sigma\in\Sigma_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\pm 1\}^{N}\mapsto H_{N}(\sigma)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\,.

(1)

The quenched SK-free energy to inverse temperature
\beta>0
and external field
h\in\mathbb{R}
is

Nf_{N}(\beta,h)\equiv\log\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{N}}\exp\left(\beta H_{N}(\sigma)+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}\right)\,.

(2)

In order to ’solve’ the model, Thouless, Anderson and Palmer
[
25
]
play the delicate (and debatable) card of the spin magnetisation
m_{i}\equiv\left<\sigma_{i}\right>_{\beta,h,N},i=1\dots N
as order parameter of the theory, with
\left<\right>_{\beta,h,N}
denoting average with respect to the quenched Gibbs measure. By means of a nonrigorous (and troublesome) diagrammatic expansion, Thouless
et. al.
suggest that the following approximation holds true with overwhelming probability:

Nf_{N}(\beta,h)=\max_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\Delta}f_{\text{TAP}}(\boldsymbol{m})+o(N)\quad(N\uparrow\infty),

(3)

where

i)

the
TAP-free energy
is given by

\displaystyle Nf_{TAP}(\boldsymbol{m})

\displaystyle\equiv\beta\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}g_{ij}m_{i}m_{j}+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}

(4)

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}N\left[1-\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}^{2}\right]^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}I(m_{i})\,;

ii)

for
m\in[-1,1]

I(m)\equiv\frac{1+m}{2}\log(1+m)+\frac{1-m}{2}\log(1-m)\,;

is the classical coin tossing rate function; and

iii)

\Delta\subset[-1,1]^{N}
an unspecified set of restrictions on the quenched magnetisations
\boldsymbol{m}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Plefka has shown
[
19
]
that the TAP-approximation (
3

\boldsymbol{m}\mapsto f_{\text{TAP}}(\boldsymbol{m})
must necessarily be
concave

[
19

it is claimed that this should indeed be the case provided that

\text{\emph{Plefka's criterium:}}\qquad\frac{\beta^{2}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(1-m_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}<1\,.

This condition is widely accepted within the theoretical physics literature (in other words: this restriction should definitely appear in the definition of the
\Delta
-set) but there seems to be divergent opinions if this suffices for the validity of the high temperature expansions and thus of the TAP-approximation (
4

[

Assuming the validity of the TAP-approximation (
4

\nabla f_{\text{TAP}}(\boldsymbol{m})=0\Longleftrightarrow m_{i}=\tanh\left(h+\beta\sum_{j\neq i}g_{ij}m_{j}-\beta^{2}(1-q_{N}(\boldsymbol{m}))m_{i}\right)\,,\;i=1\dots N,

(5)

where
q_{N}(\boldsymbol{m})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}(1/N)\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}^{2}

In the theoretical physics literature it is claimed that, for large enough

\boldsymbol{m}^{\alpha},\alpha=1\dots 2^{\Theta N}
, where
\Theta=\Theta(\beta,h)

Let us now assume to be given a TAP-solution

\beta\sum_{j\neq i}g_{ij}m_{j}^{\alpha}=\tanh^{-1}\left(m_{i}^{\alpha}\right)-h+\beta^{2}\left\{1-q_{N}\left(\boldsymbol{m}^{\alpha}\right)\right\}m^{\alpha}_{i}.

(6)

Plugging this into (
4

\displaystyle f_{\text{TAP}}\left(\alpha\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\left[\frac{1}{2}m_{i}^{\alpha}\tanh^{-1}\left(m_{i}^{\alpha}\right)+\frac{h}{2}m_{i}^{\alpha}-I(m^{\alpha}_{i})\right]+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\left\{1-q_{N}({\bf m}^{\alpha})^{2}\right\}.

3.
The REM in TAP: definition, and main results.

We start with some notation:
(S,\mathcal{S})
denotes a Polish space and
C(S),C_{b}(S)
the spaces of all real valued continuous, resp. continous bounded functions on
S
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{d}\right)
the space of Borel probability measures on
S^{d}
, endowed with the topology of weak convergence of measures. Notice that
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{d}\right)
is Polish itself and we can consider one of the standard metrics (e.g. Prokhorov) that makes it a complete, separable metric space. Given a measure
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{d}\right)

B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}

\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}

\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{d}\right)
with center
\boldsymbol{\nu}
and ray

. Our abstract Hamiltonian, which parallels (
15

\boldsymbol{\Phi}:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]=\Phi_{1}\left[\rho_{1}\right]+{\sf{E}}_{\rho_{2}}(f_{2})

(17)

where
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(S)
are the marginals of
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
on the first, resp. second coordinate,
\Phi_{1}:\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(S)\to\mathbb{R}

To lighten notation, we shorten
{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(u)\equiv\int u(\boldsymbol{x}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}(d\boldsymbol{x})
for the expectation of a function
u:S^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
w.r.t. a given
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{d}\right)
and
\text{var}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(u)\equiv{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(u^{2})-{{\sf{E}}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(u)^{2}

\boldsymbol{\rho}\circ u^{-1}
for the push-forward of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
along a
\boldsymbol{\rho}
-measurable

. Finally we indicate with
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:S^{2}\to S
the natural projections on the first, resp. second coordinate.

More specifically:

Definition 3.1
.

\boldsymbol{\Phi}:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R}
denotes a functional of the form (
17
) with

\Phi_{1}[\rho]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\Phi\left({\sf{E}}_{\rho}(g^{2})\right)+{\sf{E}}_{\rho}(f_{1})\qquad\forall\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(S)

where

H1)

\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice differentiable concave function with
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)<0

x\in\mathbb{R}
;

H2)

g,f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{b}(S)
with
\sup g=1,\inf g=-1
.

The random Hamiltonian of our abstract model in a configuration
\alpha
of the configuration space
\{1,\dots,2^{N}\}
for a finite volume
N\in\mathbb{N}
is then defined as

H_{N}(\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}N\boldsymbol{\Phi}\left[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\right]

(18)

where for every
\alpha\in\{1,\cdots,2^{N}\}

\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(X_{\alpha,i},Y_{\alpha,i})}

(19)

are the empirical measures associated to independent sequences of
S^{2}
-valued, i.i.d. random vectors
\{(X_{\alpha,i},Y_{\alpha,i})\}_{i=1}^{N}

with common distribution
\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma
, for some product measure
\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}(S^{2})
.

Some words on the assumptions given in the above definition: concavity is imposed on
\Phi
to warrant thermodynamical stability, somewhat in line with Plekfa’s convergence criterium recalled in Remark
2.1
; the boundedness of

is technically convenient, but also tailor-suited for our applications such as the SK-model. The variables
X_{\alpha,\cdot}
correspond to the random energies associated to the configuration

encode the disorder necessary to perturbate the Hamiltonian with an extensive cavity dynamics.

To shorten notation, we define

f(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(y)

(20)

so that the nonlinear functional
\boldsymbol{\Phi}:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R}
in the definition

is

\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\Phi\left({\sf{E}}_{\rho_{1}}(g^{2})\right)+{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}({f}).

(21)

Notice that the continuity of the real valued function

\boldsymbol{\Phi}
is continous on
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)

We consider the partition function, free energy and Gibbs measure associated to the Hamiltonian (
18
): for finite volume
N\in\mathbb{N}
, these are defined as usual as

Z_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{2^{N}}\exp H_{N}(\alpha),\qquad F_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N},

(22)

and for
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,2^{N}\}

{\mathcal{G}}_{N}(\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}Z_{N}^{-1}\exp{H_{N}(\alpha)}.

(23)

Finally, given two configurations
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
, we define their abstract overlap as

q_{N}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}g\left(X_{\alpha,i}\right)g\left(\,X_{\alpha^{\prime},i}\right).

(24)

Our first result concerns the limiting free energy, and requires some notation: set

\mathcal{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right):\quad H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\leq\log 2\}\,,

(25)

with

Theorem 3.2
.

(Boltzmann-Gibbs principle).
The infinite volume limit of the free energy (
22
) exists
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, is non-random, and given by

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}F_{N}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}}\,\,\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{\nu}]-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\log 2\,.

(26)

A complete solution of our abstract models thus requires a discussion of the Boltzmann-Gibbs variational principle (
26
). This will be achieved by relating it to a simpler, Parisi-like variational principle in finite dimensions. To see how this comes about, we recall from
[
6
]
that for all
f\in\mathcal{L}\left(S^{2},\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma\right)
with

\mathcal{L}\left(S^{2},\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\left\{u\in C\left(S^{2}\right):\quad{\sf{E}}_{\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma}\left(e^{\lambda u}\right)<\infty\,\,\,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

(27)

almost surely it holds

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{2^{N}}\exp{N{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}}(f)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in K}\left\{{\sf{E}}_{\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma}(f)-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\,\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\right\}

(28)

\displaystyle=\inf_{0\leq m\leq 1}\left\{\frac{1}{m}\log{\sf{E}}_{\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma}(\exp{\,mf})+\frac{\log 2}{m}\right\}.

The first equality is the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle given in Theorem
3.2
for a Hamiltonian of the form (
18
) without nonlinear term. The second equality, in full agreement with the Parisi theory
[
18
]
, establishes a duality between the Gibbs principle and a finite-dimensional minimization problem. We shall call the target function in the minimization problem a
Parisi function
; its (unique, cfr.

]

on

\exp{\overline{m}f}\equiv\exp{\overline{m}(f_{1}\circ\pi_{1}+f_{2}\circ\pi_{2})}

Our second main result provides the analogous duality principle for the nonlinear Hamiltonian (

(q,m)\in[0,1]^{2}

\displaystyle Z^{q,m}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}{\sf{E}}_{\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma}\left(\exp{m\left[\Phi^{\prime}(q)g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}+f\right]}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\exp{m\left[f_{1}(x)+\Phi^{\prime}(q)g^{2}(x)\right]}\,\mu(dx)\cdot\int\exp{mf_{2}(y)}\,\gamma(dy)

\emph{P}(q,m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\Phi(q)-q\Phi^{\prime}(q)+\frac{1}{m}\left(\log Z^{q,m}+\log 2\right),\,

the following holds.

Theorem 3.3
.

(Parisi principle). It holds:

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}F_{N}=\inf_{(q,m)\in[0,1]^{2}}\emph{P}(q,m)+\log 2\,,

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.

When comparing the

We will see in the course of the proof that such corrections, which are constituent parts of the Parisi free energy for the SK-model, play the role of

In order to formulate the statement, we point out that for our abstract models,

corresponds to the situation where the parameter
\bar{m}
which achieves the minimum in Parisi principle is such that
\bar{m}<1
. As will become clear below, if the minimum point
(\bar{q},\bar{m})\in[0,1]^{2}
of a Parisi function (
30
) is such that
\bar{m}<1
, then the measure

\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\equiv\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(S)

is the optimal measure for the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle and satisfies

\begin{cases}H(\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)=\bar{m}\left[\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\bar{q}+{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f)\right]-\log Z^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}=\log 2,\\
{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\nu}_{1}}(g^{2})=\bar{q},\end{cases}

(34)

where
\bar{\nu}_{1}\equiv\nu_{1}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}
is the first marginal of
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
; in particular, in low temperature the side constraint on the relative entropy is saturated.

Theorem 3.4
.

(Onset of Derrida-Ruelle cascades).
Assume that at least one of the measures
{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}\circ(g\circ\pi_{1}-\bar{q})^{-1}
or
{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}\circ(f-{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f))^{-1}
has a density w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Then, for a system in low temperature, the point process
\left\{\mathcal{G}_{N}(\alpha)\right\}_{\alpha\leq 2^{N}}
associated to the Gibbs measure (
23
) converges weakly as
N\to\infty
to a Poisson-Dirichlet point process with parameter
\overline{m}
.

Let us furthermore denote by

\langle O_{N}({\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})\rangle_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}Z_{N}^{-2}\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}O_{N}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}){\mathcal{G}}_{N}(\alpha){\mathcal{G}}_{N}(\alpha^{\prime})

the average with respect to the
replicated Gibbs measure
of a quantity
O_{N}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
depending on two configurations
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
; the following then holds for the limiting law of the abstract overlap (
24
).

Proposition 3.5
.

(Overlap concentration).
Let

\bar{q}=\int g(x)^{2}{\bar{\nu}_{1}}(dx),\qquad\bar{q}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\left[\int g(x){\bar{\nu}_{1}}(dx)\right]^{2}\,.

(35)

Then, under the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
, it holds

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\,\delta_{\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}}(q_{N}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})-\bar{q})^{2}\,\right\rangle_{N}=0,

(36)

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\,\delta_{\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}}\left(q_{N}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})-\bar{q}_{0}\right)^{2}\,\right\rangle_{N}=0.

(37)

We thus see that our abstract models correctly recover
all
of the main features of the Parisi theory under a 1RSB approximation, in a generic setting. For the readers convenience, we shall conclude by briefly dwelling on the upshot of our analysis for the concrete case of the SK-model.

3.1.
SK vs. REM-TAP

We recall that the 1RSB Ansatz
[
18
]
for the limiting free energy of the SK model (
1
) reads

\displaystyle f_{\text{1RSB-SK}}(\beta,h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\inf_{0\leq q_{0},\leq q_{1}\leq 1,m_{1}\in[0,1]}\Bigg\{\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\left[(1-m_{1})q_{1}^{2}+m_{1}q_{0}^{2}-2q_{1}+1\right]+\log 2+

(38)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{m_{1}}\int\log\left\{\int\exp m_{1}\log\cosh\left[\beta(\sqrt{q_{0}}u+\sqrt{q_{1}-q_{0}}v+h)\right]\varphi(dv)\right\}\,\varphi(du)\Bigg\},

where
\varphi
is the standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}
. As a matter of fact, this is a
degenerate
2RSB-formula: indeed, the law of the pure states which hides behind (
38
) is that of a superposition of two Derrida-Ruelle processes
[
21
]
with parameters
0\leq m_{0}\leq m_{1}\leq 1
, with the first one eventually absorbed through the limiting procedure
m_{0}\downarrow 0
: this operation gives rise to the aforementioned degeneracy, to the ”common trunk” captured by the second Gaussian
du
-integral, and stands behind the
third
order parameter
q_{0}
. For better comparison, one may in fact simply set
q_{0}=0
in the above formula. Due to this complication, comparison with our 1RSB-setting shall be taken
cum grano
. Notwithstanding, we do get a number of important insights: for the Hamiltonian
H_{N}(\alpha)\equiv Nf_{\text{REM-TAP}}\left(\alpha\right)
, and recalling SK1-3) above, the REM-TAP approximation leads in fact to a free energy

\displaystyle

\displaystyle f_{\text{REM-TAP}}(\beta,h)=\inf_{0\leq q\leq 1,m\in[0,1]}\Bigg\{\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}(q^{2}+1)+\log 2+

(39)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{m}\log\int\exp m\log\cosh(h+\beta x)\cdot e^{m\left[-\frac{\beta}{2}x\tanh(h+\beta x)-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}q\tanh^{2}(h+\beta x)\right]}\,\varphi(dx)\Bigg\}

(The term
\exp{m\left[-\frac{\beta}{2}x\tanh(h+\beta x)-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}q\tanh^{2}(h+\beta x)\right]}
in (
39
) plays a role in the cavity dynamics only, and is completely irrelevant when it comes to the free energy: for the sake of the current discussion, such factor is an artefact which can also be immediately removed : simply set
f_{1}\equiv 0
). The appearance of the
quadratic
term in the first line on the r.h.s. above is central to this work: although a constituent part of (
38
), such terms cannot be explained by the linear models studied in
[
6
,
7
]
. As already mentioned, these corrections turn out to be Lagrange multipliers accounting for the non-linearities induced by the true REM-models hiding ”within” the TAP-free energies.

Let us also spend a few words on the cavity dynamics for the SK-model, i.e. for the Hamiltonian given by the perturbed REM-TAP
H_{N;\varepsilon}(\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}Nf_{\text{REM-TAP}}^{(\varepsilon)}\left(\alpha\right)
, and
with
f_{2}(y)\equiv f_{2}(\varepsilon;y)\equiv\varepsilon\log\cosh(y)
. Let us denote by
(\bar{q}_{\varepsilon},\bar{m}_{\varepsilon})\in[0,1]^{2}
the minimum of the corresponding Parisi function, and by
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q}_{\varepsilon},\bar{m}_{\varepsilon}}
the associated generalized Gibbs measure. We furthermore denote by
F_{N,\varepsilon}
the law of the overlap
q_{N}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})=(1/N)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tanh(h+\beta g_{\alpha,i})\tanh(h+\beta g_{\alpha^{\prime},i})
under the perturbed, finite volume Gibbs measure, and by
F_{N}
that of the
un
-perturbed:

x\in[0,1]\mapsto F_{N,\varepsilon}(x)\equiv{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\,{\bf 1}_{q_{N}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\leq x}\,\right\rangle_{N;\varepsilon},\qquad F_{N}(x)\equiv F_{N,0}(x).

(40)

Proposition
3.5
would imply that in low temperature (
\bar{m}_{\varepsilon}<1
) the law of the overlap of two relevant TAP-solutions for the SK-model is given by

F_{\varepsilon}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{if}\quad x<\bar{q}_{0;\varepsilon}\\
\bar{m}_{\varepsilon}\quad&\text{if}\quad\bar{q}_{0;\varepsilon}\leq x<\bar{q}_{\varepsilon}\\
1\quad&\text{otherwise}\end{cases};

(41)

where

\bar{q}_{\varepsilon}=\int\tanh^{2}(h+\beta x)\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(dx,dy),\qquad\bar{q}_{0;\varepsilon}=\left[\int\tanh(h+\beta x)\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(dx,dy)\right]^{2}.

A Parisi fixed point equation
[
18
, III.63]
encoding the stability of the hierarchical structure under the extensive cavity dynamics would then appear through the
imposition

F(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(!)}}{{=}}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}F_{\varepsilon}(x),\qquad\forall\,x\in[0,1].

(42)

In case of a REM-approximation (1RSB), it is easily seen that the self-consistency (
42
) is, in fact,
automatically
satisfied and as such a void requirement: in order to avoid trivializations one needs a much more sophisticated GREM-TAP approximation, but this will be addressed in forthcoming works.

4.
Proofs.

4.1.
The Boltzmann-Gibbs principle: proof of Theorem
3.2
.

In this subsection we prove Theorem
3.2
, i.e. the validity of the following Boltzmann-Gibbs principle

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}F_{N}=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}}\,\Omega[\boldsymbol{\nu}]+\log 2\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.},

(43)

where to lighten notation we shortened

\displaystyle\Omega[\boldsymbol{\rho}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]-H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)

(44)

\displaystyle=\Phi\left({\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1}}(g^{2})\right)+{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}({f})-H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)

being
\boldsymbol{\Phi}:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R}
the functional defined in
3.1
.

Remark 4.1
.

It is a well known fact that
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)
is lower semicontinous, strictly convex and with compact sub-levels. Therefore, for compact
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
and an upper semicontinous
\Omega
, the generalized Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem on metric spaces ensures the existence of a solution to

\sup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{C}}\Omega[\boldsymbol{\nu}].

(45)

Moreover, the assumption H1) on
\Phi
, together with the fact that
g\in[-1,1]
, suffices to get strict concavity of the functional
\Omega:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R}
. This implies that, for a convex
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
, the existence of a solution to a problem of the form (
45
) implies its uniqueness. In other words: if
\mathcal{C}
is compact, there exists a solution to (
45
); if
\mathcal{C}
is also convex, such solution is furthemore unique: since the set
\mathcal{K}
, i.e. the sub-level of the relative entropy appearing in the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle (
43
), is convex and compact, the variational principle (
43
) admits a unique solution. (Recall also that, in a complete metric space like
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
a set is sequentially compact if and only if it is compact, every compact set is closed and every closed subset of a compact set is compact).

We will proceed via a second moment method on the counting variable

\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(E\right)=\#\{\alpha:\quad\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in E\},

defined for every
E\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
. The proof is analogous to the one given in
[
6
]
and relies both on the independence of the
\{\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{2^{N}}
as well as on the continuity of
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]
. We start with a technical Lemma, which will be useful also later, that provides the upper bound to the free energy - see equation (
46
). Here and henceforth we denote by
B_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}B_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}},r}=\{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right):\quad d(\boldsymbol{\nu},\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}})<r\}
the open ball centered in
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
, and with radius
r>0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\text{argmax}_{\,\mathcal{K}\,}\Omega
. Then,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely :

\bullet

For all
r>0
,

\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\not\in B_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[L_{N,\alpha}]}<F,

\bullet

With
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{K}}\Omega[\boldsymbol{\nu}]=\Omega[\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}]
, it holds:

\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{2^{N}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}\leq F\,.

(46)

Proof.

Let
r\geq 0
. Shorten
B_{0}^{c}\equiv\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
, denote by
B_{r}^{c}
the complement of
B_{r}
in
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
:

B^{c}_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right):\quad d(\boldsymbol{\rho},\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}})\geq r\})=\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\setminus B_{r},

and set

F_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\sup\limits_{\mathcal{K}\cap B_{r}^{c}}\Omega\quad&\text{if}\quad\mathcal{K}\cap B_{r}^{c}\neq\emptyset,\\
&-\infty\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(47)

Notice that if
r:\,\,\mathcal{K}\cap B^{c}_{r}=\emptyset
it trivially holds
F_{r}<F_{0}\equiv F
. If instead
r:\,\,\mathcal{K}\cap B_{r}^{c}\neq\emptyset
then, by compactness (see Remark
4.1
), we can find
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{r}\in\mathcal{K}\cap B^{c}_{r},\,\,\boldsymbol{\nu}_{r}\neq\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
s.t.

F_{r}=\max_{\mathcal{K}\cap B^{c}_{r}}\Omega=\Omega[\boldsymbol{\nu}_{r}]

and, as the maximal measure
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
of
\Omega
on
\mathcal{K}
is unique, the strict inequality

F_{r}=\Omega[\boldsymbol{\nu}_{r}]<\Omega[\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}]=F

holds also in this case. Hence, in order to settle the Lemma, we just need to prove the following

\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}\leq F_{r}\quad\forall r\geq 0.

(48)

In order to see this, consider a decreasing sequence of positive real numbers
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{n}\in(0,1)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the open set
\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}^{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}
, together with its closure
\overline{\mathcal{K}^{c}_{a_{n}}}
, where for every
a\geq 0

\mathcal{K}_{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right):\quad H\left(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma\right)\leq\log 2+a\}.

Sanov’s Theorem implies that

\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}\left(L_{N,1}\in\overline{\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}^{c}}\right)&=\exp{[-\inf_{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\overline{\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}^{c}}}H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)N+o(N)]}\quad\\
&\leq 2^{-N}e^{-a_{n}N+o(N)}\end{aligned}(N\to\infty),\\

(49)

hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\alpha\in\{1,\cdots,2^{N}\}:\quad\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N}\mathbb{P}\left(L_{N,1}\in\overline{\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}^{c}}\right)\leq e^{-a_{n}N+o(N)}.

(50)

Through the Borel-Cantelli lemma, (
50
) implies that

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum\limits_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha})}\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}

(51)

If
r,n
are such that
\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}=\emptyset
, the latter implies

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B^{c}_{\epsilon}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}

\displaystyle=-\infty\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}

(52)

Notice that by construction it holds
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}
, which implies that if
\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}
is empty then
F_{r}=-\infty
, i.e. the claim (
48
) is exactly (
52
).

We can therefore assume
r,n:\,\,\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}\neq\emptyset
and, for an aribitrary
\delta>0
cover the compact set
\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}
through open balls

\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}\subset\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{\epsilon}}B_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\nu}})

such that for each
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}
the associated ray
r_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}>0
is small enough s.t.

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\rho})-\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})<\delta\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\rho}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}),

(53)

and

H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)-\inf_{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\nu}})}}H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)<\frac{\delta}{2}.

(54)

This is clearly possible by upper semicontinuity of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
and the fact that

\inf_{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(r)}}H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\to H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\quad(r\to 0).

By compactness, we can extract a finite sub-cover:
\exists M<\infty,\{\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}
s.t.
\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}B_{\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}}(r_{i})
, where we shortened

r_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}r_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}},\,\,B_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}B_{\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}}(r_{i}).

Therefore, almost surely, it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\sum\limits_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B_{i}}\exp{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha})}

(55)

\displaystyle\leq\delta+\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\exp{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i})}\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(\,\overline{B}_{i}\,\right)

(recall that
\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(\,\overline{B}_{i}\,\right)
is the variable that counts the
\alpha-
s for which
\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B_{i}
).

Markov’s inequality implies that for each
i=1,\cdots,M

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(\overline{B}_{i}\right)\geq e^{N[\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\delta]}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-N}{\mathbb{E}}\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(\overline{B}_{i}\right)\exp{N[H(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)-\delta]}

(56)

with

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(\overline{B}_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2^{N}\mathbb{P}\left(L_{N,1}\in\overline{B}_{i}\right)=2^{N}\exp{[-\inf_{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\overline{B}_{i}}H\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma\right)N+o(N)]}\quad(N\to\infty),

(57)

the last step by Sanov’s theorem. Plugging (
57
) into (
56
), and using (
54
), we find that for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,M\}

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(\overline{B}_{i}\right)<e^{N[\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\delta]}\right)\geq 1-\exp\left[{-\frac{\delta}{2}N+o(N)}\right]\quad(N\to\infty).

This, together with Borel-Cantelli, implies

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap\in B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\exp{N(\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{i}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma))}+\log 2+2\delta

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in B^{c}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\log 2+2\delta.

Being
\delta
arbitrary, the latter, together with (
51
) give

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}}\Omega[\boldsymbol{\nu}]+\log 2.

Notice that, again by compactness, we can select a measure
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n}\in\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}
s.t.

\max\limits_{\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\cap B^{c}_{r}}\Omega=\Omega[\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n}],

which implies that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}

\displaystyle\leq\Omega[\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n}]+\log 2.

By construction it holds
B^{c}_{r}\cap\mathcal{K}_{a_{n}}\subseteq B^{c}_{r}\cap\mathcal{K}_{1}\,\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, specifically the whole sequence
\{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
lies in the compact set
B^{c}_{r}\cap\mathcal{K}_{1}
and we can extract a convergent subsequence:
\{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
s.t.
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n_{k}}\to{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
weakly as
k\to\infty
for some
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\mathcal{K}_{1}\cap B^{c}_{r}
. We get

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B^{c}_{r}}e^{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}]}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,\Omega[\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n_{k}}]+\log 2

(58)

\displaystyle\leq\Omega[{\boldsymbol{\xi}}]+\log 2,

the last step by upper semicontinuity of
\Omega
. It is also immediately checked that

{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\mathcal{K}\cap B^{c}_{r}\equiv\mathcal{K}_{0}\cap B^{c}_{r},

(59)

which implies the claim (
48
) straightforwardly through (
58
).

The validity of (
59
) is a consequence of the fact that for every
\delta>0
there exists
k_{\delta}>0
s.t.
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n_{k}}\in\mathcal{K}_{\delta}\cap B^{c}_{r}
for all
k\geq k_{\delta}
and that by compactness of the latter it must hold
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\mathcal{K}_{\delta}\cap B_{r}^{c}
. Specifically:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\bigcap\limits_{\delta>0}K_{\delta}\cap B^{c}_{r}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\delta>0}\left\{\boldsymbol{\nu}:H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\leq\log 2+\delta\right\}\cap B^{c}_{r}

\displaystyle=\left\{\boldsymbol{\nu}:H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\leq\log 2\right\}\cap B^{c}_{r}=\mathcal{K}\cap B^{c}_{r},

and the Lemma follows.
∎

The proof of the lower bound requires a refinement of our moments’ analysis. A key ingredient is the strong concentration of the
\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(\,V\right)
-random variable around its mean:

Lemma 4.3
.

(Variance estimate)
For every
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}
and any open neigborhood
U
of
\boldsymbol{\nu}
, there exists
B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\subset U
and
\delta>0
such that for large enough
N

\mathbb{V}ar\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\leq e^{-N\delta}({\mathbb{E}}\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right))^{2}.

(60)

Proof.

For each
B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}
, and by independence, the second moment of
\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)
satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\,\mathcal{A}^{\,2}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)+\sum\limits_{\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha^{\prime}}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)

(61)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,1}\in\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}\,\right)+\left[{\mathbb{E}}\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\right]^{2},

and thus

\mathbb{V}ar\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\leq 2^{N}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,1}\in\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}\,\right).

(62)

Notice that the statement of the Lemma is trivial if
\boldsymbol{\nu}=\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma
, therefore we assume
\boldsymbol{\nu}\neq\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma
; this implies that there exists
B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\subset U
and
\eta>0
such that
\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma\notin\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}
and

H(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r})=H(\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}})=H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)-\eta.

Together with Sanov’s theorem, the latter implies that for
N
large

\displaystyle 2^{N}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,1}\in\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp N\left[\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\frac{7}{6}\eta\right]

(63)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{\eta}{2}N}\exp 2N\left[\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\frac{5}{6}\eta\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{\eta}{2}N}\left[2^{N}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,1}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\right]^{2}=e^{-\frac{\eta}{2}N}\left[{\mathbb{E}}\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\right]^{2}

where in the second line we used that, as
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in K
,
\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\geq 0
. The Lemma now follows from (
62
) with
\delta\equiv\frac{\eta}{2}
.

∎

For
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}
,
\delta>0
let
U
be an open neigborhood of
\boldsymbol{\nu}
such that

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-\Phi(\boldsymbol{\rho})\leq\delta\qquad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\rho}\in U,

and let
\overline{B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}\subset U
be the ball prescribed by Lemma
4.3
. Then

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{N}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum\limits_{\alpha:\,\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}\exp{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{L}_{N,\alpha})}

\displaystyle\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})+\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)-\delta.

Moreover, Lemma (
4.3
) together with Chebyshev’s inequality and Borel-Cantelli, immediately implies that

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\quad\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

By Sanov’s theorem, we thus get

\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathcal{A}_{N}\left(B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}\right)\geq-\inf_{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\log 2\quad\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

All in all,

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{N}

\displaystyle\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-\inf_{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in B_{\boldsymbol{\nu},r}}H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\log 2-\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\log 2-\delta.

Since
\delta
is arbitrary, by taking the supremum over
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}
, we get the lower bound

\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{N}\geq\sup\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{K}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\log 2,

and Theorem
3.2
follows.

4.2.
The Parisi principle: proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Our proof of the Parisi principle crucially relies on some classic properties of the relative entropy functional
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)\to H(\boldsymbol{\rho}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\in[0,\infty]
which are recalled in the Appendix
A
. We start with a couple of technical and straightforward results.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\overline{q},\overline{m})
be a minimum point of the Parisi function
\emph{P}
, defined in (
30
), on
[0,1]^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{m}\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)=\log 2,

(64)

\displaystyle\overline{m}=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\leq\log 2,

{\sf{E}}_{\nu^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}_{1}}\left(g^{2}\right)=\overline{q}

(65)

being
\nu^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(S)
the marginal of
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
on the first coordinate.

Moreover, it holds

\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}]-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)=\emph{P}(\overline{q},\overline{m})+\log 2.

(66)

Proof.

As a lower semicontinuous function on a compact set,
\emph{P}
must attain a minimum on
[0,1]^{2}
, which we denote by
(\overline{q},\overline{m})\in[0,1]^{2}
.
Since
\emph{P}(q,m)\to+\infty
for
m\to 0^{+}
,
\overline{m}
must lie in
(0,1]
: this implies that
\partial_{m}\emph{P}(\overline{q},\overline{m})\leq 0
with equality if
\overline{m}\in(0,1)
. Through this and the expression for
\partial_{m}\emph{P}
in (
33
) we get that the implications (
64
) hold true.

Now, assume
ad absurdum

{\sf{E}}_{\nu^{q,m}_{1}}\left(g^{2}\right)\neq\overline{q}.

(67)

Since
\Phi^{\prime\prime}<0
the assumption (
67
) implies
\overline{q}\in\{0,1\}
, as otherwise one would get from the equation for
\partial_{q}P
in (
33
) that
\partial_{q}\emph{P}(\overline{q},\overline{m})\neq 0
which is impossible if
\overline{q}\in(0,1)
. As
0
is a left border value in order for it to be a component of a minumum point it should hold
\partial_{q}\emph{P}(\overline{q},\overline{m})\geq 0
. But this together with (
67
), (
33
) and
\Phi^{\prime\prime}<0
would imply
{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{q,m}_{1}}\left(g^{2}\right)<\overline{q}=0
, which contradicts the assumption
-1\leq g\leq 1
. Using that
\sup g^{2}\leq 1
, the case
\overline{q}=1
brings to an analogous contradiction. It thus follows that also (
65
) holds true.

Moreover, as (
32
) is valid for all couples
(q,m)
, we find that
\emph{P}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\emph{P}(q,m)=\Phi(q)-q\Phi^{\prime}(q)

\displaystyle+{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{q,m}}\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(q)g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)\,+

(68)

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{m}\left(\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{q,m}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\right)-\log 2.

Using (
65
) we get

\displaystyle\emph{P}(\overline{q},\overline{m})=

\displaystyle\Phi(\overline{q})-\overline{q}\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})+{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}}\left(f\right)+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})\overline{q}+\frac{1}{\overline{m}}\left(\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\right)-\log 2

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left({\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}}\left(g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)\right)+{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}}\left(f\right)+\frac{1}{\overline{m}}\left(\log 2-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\right)-\log 2.

From the latter and (
64
) the equation (
66
) follows straighforwardly. This concludes the Proof of the Lemma.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

There exists a minimum point
(\overline{q},\overline{m})
of
\emph{P}
on
[0,1]^{2}
such that the unique optimal measure for the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle (
26
) is
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}
; i.e.

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{K}}\,\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)

where
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}
is the generalized Gibbs measure with Radon-Nikodym derivative w.r.t.
\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma
given by (
31
) for
(q,m)=(\overline{q},\overline{m})
.

Proof.

Consider
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\in\mathcal{K}
solution of the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle (
26
).
Then, let
(\overline{q},\overline{m})
be a minimum point of
\emph{P}
on
[0,1]^{2}
. Lemma (
4.4
), specifically (
64
), ensures that
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\in K
; this amounts to say that
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}
is a viable candidate to solve the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle.

We now use Proposition
A.3
from the Appendix
A
, recalling the variational expression for relative entropy functionals, to show that

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\leq\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\nu}\in K,

(70)

which proves the Proposition. Specifically, as
g,f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{b}(S)
, we can apply
A.3
to
\overline{m}(\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}+f)\in C_{b}(S^{2})
to see that

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\geq H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+\overline{m}\int\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})

(71)

for all
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
. Specifically

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(f)-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(f)-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)-\overline{m}\int\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})

(72)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \,={\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}}&(f)-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)-\int\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})\,+\\
&+(1-\overline{m})\int\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} =-\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})&\left(\,{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\left(g\circ\pi_{1}\right)-\bar{q}\,\right)+{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}}(f)-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)\,+\\
&+(1-\overline{m})\int\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})\end{aligned}

where in the last line we used that, as shown in Lemma
4.4
, for a minimum point
(\bar{q},\bar{m})
of
P
it holds

\overline{q}={\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}}\left(g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right).

(73)

Since
\Phi
is concave and differentiable, it satisfies

\Phi(x)-\Phi^{\prime}(y)(x-y)\leq\Phi(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Through (
72
), and again (
73
), this implies

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)

\displaystyle=\Phi\left({\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\left(g\circ\pi_{1}\right)\right)+{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\left(f\right)-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma).

(74)

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})-H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)+(1-\overline{m})\int\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}).

From the latter we see that if
\overline{m}=1
the claim (
70
) follows immediately.
If instead
\overline{m}\in(0,1)
then
H(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)=\log 2
and Proposition
A.3
implies that

\overline{m}\int\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})\leq H(\boldsymbol{\nu}\mid\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)-\log 2\leq 0

for all
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in K
.
Since both
\overline{m}
and
1-\overline{m}
are non-negative this implies

(1-\overline{m})\int\left(f+\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}\right)d(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}})\leq 0

which used on (
74
) gives (
70
), which is therefore now proved also for
\overline{m}\in(0,1)
.

All in all, we have shown that if
(\overline{q},\overline{m})
is a minimum point of
\emph{P}
on
[0,1]^{2}
then the unique extremal measure
\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
of the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle (
26
) must be one of the
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}
, namely the thesis of the Proposition is settled.
∎

4.3.
The limiting Gibbs measure: proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Here and henceforth, we will denote by
{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}\left(S^{2}\right)
the measure solving the Boltzmann-Gibbs variational principle for a system in low temperature; i.e.

d{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\exp\bar{m}\left[\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\,g^{2}(x)+f(x,y)\right]}{Z^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}}\,d\mu(x)d\gamma(y)\,,

where
(\bar{q},\bar{m})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\text{argmin}_{[0,1]^{2}}\emph{P}
and
\bar{m}<1
.
Some notation: let
\{(Z_{i},W_{i})\}_{i\leq N}
be i.i.d.
S^{2}
-valued random vectors with common distribution
{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}
and defined on a probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},{\sf{E}})
. Setting
\boldsymbol{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}(Z_{1},W_{1},\cdots,Z_{N},W_{N})\in S^{2N}
define

X_{N}({\bf Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left(g^{2}(Z_{i})-\bar{q}\right),\quad\quad Y_{N}({\bf Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left(f(Z_{i},W_{i})-{\sf{E}}_{{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}}(f)\right)

and consider the real valued random vectors

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{N}\\
Y_{N}\end{array}\right)\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{N}(\boldsymbol{Z})\\
Y_{N}(\boldsymbol{Z})\end{array}\right),

(75)

together with their covariance matrix, say
\Sigma
, which we assume to be invertible.

Set

C_{\bar{q},\bar{m}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-\bar{m}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\bar{q})+\left(1,\,-\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\right)\cdot\Sigma^{-1}\cdot\left(1,\,-\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\right)^{\top},

(76)

where
\left(1,\,-\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\right)^{\top}
is the transpose of the vector
\left(1,\,-\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and ”
\cdot
” the matrix-vector product.

Notice that
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\bar{q})<0
implies
C\equiv C_{\bar{q},\bar{m}}>0
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
, the point process

\Xi_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{2^{N}}\delta_{H_{N}(\alpha)-N\left[\Phi(\bar{q})-{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f)\right]-\omega_{N}},

(77)

where

\omega_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-\frac{1}{\bar{m}}\log\sqrt{2\pi N|\Sigma|C};

(78)

converges weakly to a Poisson point process with intensity measure
e^{-\bar{m}z}dz
.

Theorem
3.4
follows from

i)

Proposition
4.6
together with

ii)

the exponential transform

H_{N}(\alpha)-N\left[\Phi(\bar{q})-{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f)\right]-\omega_{N}\mapsto\exp\left(H_{N}(\alpha)-N\left[\Phi(\bar{q})-{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f)\right]-\omega_{N}\right)\,,

which maps the Poisson point process with intensity measure
e^{-\bar{m}z}dz
to a Poisson point process on the positive line with intensity measure
t^{-\bar{m}-1}dt
;

iii)

the fact that infinite volume limit and the Gibbs-normalization commute.

Items
ii-iii)
are fairly standard in the literature: their proof is omitted, but we refer the reader to, say,
[
6
]
for details.

For the sake of simplicity, we shall prove Proposition
4.6
assuming that Theorem
B.1
(see Appendix
B
and
[
3
, Theorem
19.5
]
) holds for the normalized vectors (
75
); this is equivalent to the assumption that at least one measure among
{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}\circ(g\circ\pi_{1}-\bar{q})^{-1}
and
{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}\circ(f-{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f))^{-1}
has a density w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

In low temperature, for any
(\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)^{{\small\otimes}N}-
measurable function
F:S^{2N}\to\mathbb{R}

2^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(F\left(X_{1,1},Y_{1,1},\dots,X_{1,N},Y_{1,N}\right)\right)={\sf{E}}\left(e^{-\bar{m}\sqrt{N}(\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})X_{N}+Y_{N})}\,F(\boldsymbol{Z})\right).

(79)

Proof.

By definition, for any
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in S^{2N},y_{i}\in S^{2}
, it holds:

\displaystyle\frac{d(\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)^{{\small\otimes}N}}{d{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}^{{\small\otimes}N}}({\bf y})

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d(\mu{\small\otimes}\gamma)}{d{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}}(y_{i})=\exp\left(N\log Z^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}-\bar{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\,g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}(y_{i})+f(y_{i})\right)\,,

(80)

hence

2^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(F\left(X_{1,1},Y_{1,1},\dots,X_{1,N},Y_{1,N}\right)\right)=e^{N\left(\log Z^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}+\log 2\right)}\int e^{-\bar{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})g^{2}\circ\pi_{1}(y_{i})+f(y_{i})}F({\bf y})d{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}^{{\small\otimes}N}({\bf y}).

(81)

By the entropy condition (

),

\log Z^{\bar{q},\bar{m}}+\log 2=\bar{m}\left[\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\bar{q}+{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f)\right].

(82)

Plugging this in (
81
), and remembering the definition of the vectors (

), the Lemma follows straightforwardly.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.6
.

We will show that for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Xi_{N}(K)\right)=\int_{K}e^{-\bar{m}z}dz\,.

(83)

Due to the complete independence over the
\alpha
-s, this suffices to prove the Lemma by Kallenberg’s theorem
[
16
, Theorem 4.15]
. To this aim, recall that, by definition,

H_{N}(1)=N\Phi\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}g^{2}(X_{1,i})\right)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f(X_{1,i},Y_{1,i}),

(84)

so that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\Xi_{N}(K)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{2^{N}}\delta_{H_{N}(\alpha)-N(\Phi(\bar{q})+{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f))-\omega_{N}}(K)\right]

(85)

\displaystyle=2^{N}\mathbb{P}\left[H_{N}(1)-N(\Phi(\bar{q})+{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f))-\omega_{N}\in K\right]

\displaystyle=2^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{N}(1)-N(\Phi(\bar{q})+{\sf{E}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(f))-\omega_{N}\in K}\right].

By Lemma
4.7
,

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\Xi_{N}(K)\right]={\sf{E}}\left[e^{-\bar{m}\sqrt{N}(\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})X_{N}+Y_{N})}\boldsymbol{1}_{N\left[\Phi\left(\frac{X_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}+\bar{q}\right)-\Phi(\bar{q})\right]+\sqrt{N}Y_{N}-\omega_{N}\in K}\right].

(86)

As
\Phi
is twice differentiable, for any fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
we can consider a map

x\in I_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\left[-\sqrt{N},\,\,\sqrt{N}\right]\,\to\,\xi_{N}(x)\in I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}[\bar{q}-1,\,\bar{q}+1],

(87)

that to any

associates a point
\xi_{N}(x)\in I
such that

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{N}}+\bar{q}\right)-\Phi(\bar{q})-\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\frac{x}{\sqrt{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{N}(x))\frac{x^{2}}{N}

(88)

\displaystyle=R_{N}(x)\frac{x^{2}}{N},

(89)

As
\bar{q},g^{2}(x)\in[0,1]\,\,\forall x\in S
, it holds

X_{N}\in I_{N}\,\,\text{for any realization of}\,\,X_{N};

(90)

specifically we can write

N\left[\Phi\left(\frac{X_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}+\bar{q}\right)-\Phi(\bar{q})\right]\equiv\sqrt{N}\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})X_{N}+R_{N}(X_{N})X_{N}^{2}.

(91)

Notice also (recalling that, by assumption,
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(a)<0\,\,\forall a\in\mathbb{R}
):

R_{N}(x)<0\quad\forall x\in I_{N},

(92)

\lim_{N\to\infty}R_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\bar{q})<0\quad\text{uniformly for}\,\,x\in o(\sqrt{N}),

(93)

\text{if}\qquad R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\,\inf\limits_{I}|\Phi^{\prime\prime}|>0\qquad\text{then}\qquad|R_{N}(x)|\geq R\quad\forall x\in I_{N}.

(94)

Going back to (
86
) we rewrite it as

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\Xi_{N}(K)\right]=\int_{I_{N}\times\mathbb{R}}e^{-\bar{m}\sqrt{N}(\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})x+y)}\Psi_{N}(x,y)dQ_{N}(x,y)

(95)

where
Q_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the distribution of

The relative entropy.

Definition A.1

Given a couple

\infty\quad&\text{else}.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(126)

It is a well-known fact that the Definition
A.1
is equivalent to the following (see
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
for details)

H(\nu\mid\mu)=\sup_{u\,\in\,C_{b}(S)}\left\{\int ud\nu-\log\int e^{u}d\mu\right\}

(127)

and it is easily seen that

Definition A.2

For all

Proposition A.3

For all

,

it holds

H(\nu\mid\mu)\geq H(G_{u}\mid\mu)+\int u\,d(\nu-G_{u}).

Proof.

From the variational definition of
H
(

) we have

H(\nu\mid\mu)\geq\int ud\nu-\log Z_{u}\quad\forall\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(S),\forall u\in C_{b}(S).

Moreover

H(G_{u}\mid\mu)=\int\log\left(\frac{dG_{u}}{d\mu}\right)dG_{u}=\int udG_{u}-\log Z_{u},

and the thesis follows straightforwardly.

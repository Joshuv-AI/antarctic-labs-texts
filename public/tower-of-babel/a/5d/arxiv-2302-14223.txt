Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi Bound for Multiparameter Quantum-State Estimation Problem
Jun Suzuki

Abstract
In this work we propose a Bayesian version of the Nagaoka-Hayashi bound when estimating a parametric family of quantum states. This lower bound is a generalization of a recently proposed bound for point estimation to Bayesian estimation. We then show that the proposed lower bound can be efficiently computed as a semidefinite programming problem. As a lower bound, we also derive a Bayesian version of the Holevo-type bound from the Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound. Lastly, we prove that the new lower bound is tighter than the Bayesian quantum Cramér-Rao bounds.



I
Introduction

Bayesian parameter estimation with prior knowledge on unknown parameters naturally enters when estimating signals upon communication processes
vTbook1
.
Quantum communication is a promising near term communication technology,
which can transmit information more securely and efficiently than the classical protocols.
There have been many investigation on how classical and/or quantum information can be transmitted faithfully over a given noisy quantum channel, see for example
gt07
;
wilde13
;
holevo19
.
Quantum Bayesian estimation is a key ingredient for decoding classical information encoded in quantum states efficiently. Quantum Bayesian estimation also has gotten a great attention in the field of quantum sensing and quantum metrology
jd15
;
dbgmb19
;
gsp21
;
nsp21
.

Quantum Bayesian estimation was initiated about half a century ago by Personick
personick_thesis
;
personick71
. Due to recent advances in the quantum estimation theory, the quantum Bayesian estimation problem has gotten a renew interest by the community.
Several quantum Bayesian bounds were proposed for the Bayes risk, see for example,
personick_thesis
;
personick71
;
hlg70
;
hayashi_review
;
tsang_zk
;
lt16
;
rd2020
;
demkowicz2020
;
tsang_gl
.
However, most of them do not capture the genuine quantum nature,
since known lower bounds are based on almost a direct translation of classical Bayesian bounds.
In particular, previously proposed lower bounds are derived by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz type inequality with respect to a certain choice for inner products on an operator space.
Holevo initiated the investigation of nontrivial lower bounds for quantum estimation in the context of the general statistical decision problems
holevo_1973
.
He also analyzed lower bounds for the Bayes risk based on the quantum Fisher information matrices
holevo_bayes
;
holevo_monograph
;
holevo_qest
.
In particular, he gave a thorough analysis on the Gaussian shift model in the Bayesian setting.

When estimating non-random parameters, the Holevo bound established the unique nature of the quantum estimation theory
holevo
;
nagaoka89
.
This is because it is expressed as a certain optimization problem without use of any quantum Fisher information matrix.
Later, Nagaoka proposed a tighter bound for two-parameter estimation
nagaoka89
.
This lower bound is based on a different statistical problem in which one aims at finding an approximated diagonalization of two noncommuting matrices
nagaoka91
.
This Nagaoka’s result was generalized by Hayashi for any finite number of noncommuting matrices
hayashi99
.
In a recent paper
lja2021
, the Nagaoka bound for parameter estimation was generalized to estimating any number of non-random parameters, which was named as the Nagaoka-Hayashi bound.
In this paper, we attempt to make a further step toward developing genuine quantum bounds based on the Nagaoka-Hayashi bound in Bayesian parameter estimation.
In particular, we propose two Bayesian versions of these lower bounds, the Nagaoka-Hayashi bound and the Holevo-type bound.
The unique nature of the proposed lower bounds is that they are expressed as a certain optimization problem.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 gives a brief summary of existing quantum Bayesian bounds.
In Sec. 3, we propose two new quantum Bayesian bounds, and we also show that the proposed lower bound is tighter than the
previously known Bayesian quantum Cramér-Rao bounds.
In Sec. 4, we conclude and list some open problems.
Technical lemmas are given in Appendix.

II
Preliminaries

Let
{\cal H}
be a finite dimensional Hilbert space and denote by
{\cal S}({\cal H})
the totality of density matrices on
{\cal H}
.
A quantum parametric model is a smooth family of density matrices on
{\cal H}
,
\{S_{\theta}\,|\,\theta\in\Theta\}
, which is parametrized by
n
-parameter
\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{n})
.
In the following, we consider a regular model, in particular,
S_{\theta}
is full rank for all
\theta\in\Theta
and
the map
\theta\mapsto S_{\theta}
is smooth and one-to-one.
A measurement is described by a set of positive semidefinite matrices
\Pi_{x}
where the index
x
corresponds to a measurement outcome.
The set of operators corresponding to a quantum measurement is normally called a positive operator-valued measure (POVM), and it is defined by

\Pi=\{\Pi_{x}\};\ \forall x\in{\cal X},\Pi_{x}\geq 0,\ \sum_{x\in{\cal X}}\Pi_{x}=I,

where the measurement outcomes are labeled by an arbitrary set
{\cal X}
and
I
is the identity operator on
{\cal H}
.
When the measurement outcomes are labelled with a continuous set,
the condition on the POVM elements is
\forall x,\Pi_{x}\geq 0,\ \int_{\cal X}dx\,\Pi_{x}=I
.

Measurement outcome is described by a random variable
X
that obeys the conditional probability distribution:

p_{\theta}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\theta}\Pi_{x}\right\}
where
\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\cdot\right\}
denotes the trace on
{\cal H}
.
The expectation value for a random variable
X
is denoted by
E_{\theta}[X|\Pi]=\sum_{x}xp_{\theta}(x)
.
To infer the parameter value, we use an estimator that returns values on the set
\Theta
:

\hat{\theta}=(\hat{\theta}_{i}):\ {\cal X}\to\Theta
.
The performance of the estimator is quantified by a loss function:

L:\ \Theta\times\Theta\to\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}|x\geq 0\}\cup\{\infty\}.

In this study, we adopt
L(\theta,\hat{\theta})=\sum_{i,j}(\hat{\theta}_{i}-\theta_{i})\mathsf{W}_{ij}(\theta)(\hat{\theta}_{j}-\theta_{j})
.
Here
\mathsf{W}(\theta)=[\mathsf{W}_{ij}(\theta)]
is an
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrix, called a weight (cost) matrix.
As a special case,
W
can be parameter independent.
In the language of statistical decision theory, the set
(\Pi,\hat{\theta})
is called a quantum decision.

The main objective of parameter estimation about quantum states
\{S_{\theta}\}
is to
find the best quantum decision
(\Pi,\hat{\theta})
that minimizes the loss function.
As the measurement outcomes are random, we need to further identify a risk for this optimization.

Definition 1
.

The Bayes risk for a given prior probability distribution
\pi(\theta)
on
\Theta
is defined by

\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]:=\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)E_{\theta}\big[L\big(\theta,\hat{\theta}(X)\big)\big|\Pi\big].

(1)

With this quantum Bayes risk, the objective is to find the best quantum decision that minimizes the risk, i.e. the minimization problem over all possible quantum decisions
(\Pi,\hat{\theta})
:

\inf_{\Pi,\hat{\theta}}\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}].

(2)

Denoting the joint distribution by
p(\theta,x):=\pi(\theta)p_{\theta}(x)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\pi(\theta)S_{\theta}\Pi_{x}\right)
,
the Bayes risk (
1
) is also written as

\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]=E_{p}\big[L\big(\theta,\hat{\theta}(X)\big)\big],

where
E_{p}[\cdot]
denotes the expectation value with respect to the joint distribution
p(\theta,x)
.

In the following discussion, we will also express the Bayes risk in terms of the mean square error (MSE) matrix whose
(j,k)
component is defined by

\mathsf{V}_{\theta,jk}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]:=E_{\theta}\big[(\hat{\theta}_{j}(X)-\theta_{j})(\hat{\theta}_{k}(X)-\theta_{k})\big|\Pi\big].

This then gives an alternative expression for the Bayes risk:

\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]=\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathsf{W}(\theta)\mathsf{V}_{\theta}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\right\},

(3)

where
\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\cdot\right\}
denotes the trace for matrices on the
n
-dimensional parameter space.

II.1
Quantum van Tree inequality

The classical van Tree inequality is based on the covariance inequality
vTbook1
.
This inequality is applicable to bound the Bayesian MSE matrix

\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{B}}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]:=\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\mathsf{V}_{\theta}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}].

Assume mild regularity conditions on the prior and the parametric model
gill_levit
;

\pi
and
p_{\theta}
are absolutely continuous, and
\pi
vanishes at the boundaries of the parameter set
\Theta
.
The resulting matrix inequality is

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{B}}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]

\displaystyle\geq\left(J_{\mathrm{B}}[\Pi]\right)^{-1},

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{B}}[\Pi]

\displaystyle:=J(\pi)+\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)J_{\theta}[\Pi],

\displaystyle J_{ij}(\pi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\frac{\partial\ell_{\pi}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\frac{\partial\ell_{\pi}}{\partial\theta_{j}},

where
\ell_{\pi}(\theta):=\log\pi(\theta)
and
J_{\theta}[\Pi]
is the Fisher information matrix about the distribution
p_{\theta}(x)
.
The van Tree inequality can be generalized in order to include the parameter dependent weight matrix.
This can be accomplished by the use of the Gill-Levit bound
gill_levit
.
A quantum version of the Gill-Levit bound was recently proposed
tsang_gl
.

It was Personick who proposed a quantum version of the van Tree inequality for the Bayes risk
personick_thesis
;
personick71
.
Without going into the details, we give his result.
It is known that the Fisher information matrix is bounded by the quantum Fisher information matrix
J^{\mathrm{Q}}_{\theta}
.
Using this fact, one can derive the quantum van Tree inequality:

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{B}}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]

\displaystyle\geq(J^{\mathrm{Q}}_{\mathrm{B}})^{-1},

(4)

\displaystyle J^{\mathrm{Q}}_{\mathrm{B}}

\displaystyle:=J(\pi)+\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)J^{\mathrm{Q}}_{\theta}.

With this inequality, one gets a lower bound for the Bayes risk (
3
) when
the weight matrix
\mathsf{W}
is parameter independent.

\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\geq{\cal C}_{\mathrm{vT}}:=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathsf{W}(J^{\mathrm{Q}}_{\mathrm{B}})^{-1}\right\}.

Well-known examples for the quantum Fisher information matrices are the symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD) Fisher information matrix
helstrom67
and the right logarithmic derivative (RLD) Fisher information matrix
yuen-lax
.
Originally, this lower bound (
4
) was proven in Personick’s thesis
(
personick_thesis
, Sec.2.2.2)
immediately after the pioneering work by Helstrom that formulated point estimation about quantum states
helstrom67
.
In his thesis, he also derived quantum versions of Bhattacharyya and Barankin bounds, however his results seem too early to be appreciated by the community at the time.

II.2
Bayesian quantum Cramér-Rao bounds

II.2.1
Bayesian SLD Cramér-Rao bound

Personick also proposed a different method to derive a lower bound for the Bayes risk in the same paper where he proposed the quantum van Tree inequality
personick71
.
In the published paper, he considered one-parameter estimation, and then he proved this lower bound is tight. However, it is less known that he also derived a lower bound for the general
n
-parameter estimation problem
personick_thesis
.

Define the averaged states and the first moment by

\displaystyle S_{\rm B}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)S_{\theta},

(5)

\displaystyle{D}_{\mathrm{B},j}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\theta_{j}S_{\theta}.

(6)

Next, consider a set of Hermitian matrices
L_{j}
satisfying the so-called Bayesian version of the SLD equation:

{D}_{\mathrm{B},j}=\frac{1}{2}(S_{\rm B}L_{j}+L_{j}S_{\rm B}).

(7)

For a positive definite averaged state
S_{\rm B}
, the Bayesian SLD
L_{j}
is uniquely defined by the solution.
Then, the real symmetric matrix
\mathsf{K}=[\mathsf{K}_{jk}]
, the Bayesian SLD Fisher information matrix, is defined by

{\mathsf{K}}_{jk}=\langle L_{j},L_{k}\rangle_{S_{\rm B}}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\rm B}(L_{j}L_{k}+L_{k}L_{j})\right\}.

(8)

Here,
\langle X,Y\rangle_{S_{\rm B}}:=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\rm B}(X^{\dagger}Y+YX^{\dagger})\right\}/2

is the symmetrized inner product for linear operators
X,Y
on
{\cal H}
with respect to the state
S_{\rm B}
,
and
X^{\dagger}
denotes the Hermitian conjugation of
X
.

For one parameter estimation, Personick proved the following inequality
personick71
.

\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{B}}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\geq\mathsf{m}-\mathsf{K},

where
\mathsf{m}=\int d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\theta^{2}
is the second moment.
Since the random parameter
\theta
is scalar,
the second term is
\mathsf{K}=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\rm B}L_{1}^{2}\right\}
.

Almost half century after the seminal work by Personick,
Rubio and Dunningham generalized the Personick bound
based on a different approach
rd2020
.
They proved

\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{B}}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\geq\mathsf{M}-\mathsf{K},

(9)

where the first term
\mathsf{M}
is an
n\times n
real symmetric matrix whose
(j,k)
component is defined by

\mathsf{M}_{jk}=\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\theta_{j}\theta_{k}.

This is the second-moment matrix of the random variable
\theta
.
The second term is a contribution regarded as the quantum nature, and can be interpreted as the Bayesian version of the SLD Fisher information matrix.
Their bound, Eq. (
9
) with a parameter-independent weight matrix, then takes the form

{\cal C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathsf{W}(\mathsf{M}-{\mathsf{K}})\right\}.

In the following, we call this lower bound as the Bayesian SLD Cramér-Rao bound.

II.2.2
Bayesian RLD Cramér-Rao bound

The above construction of the Bayesian SLD Fisher information invites us to utilize the Bayesian version of other quantum Fisher information matrices.
Holevo proposed a bound based on the Bayesian RLD Fisher information matrix for the parameter-independent weight matrix case
holevo_1973
.
He applied it to the general quantum Gaussian system and derived the optimal estimator when the prior distribution is also a Gaussian distribution
holevo_monograph
.
Below we state the result only and its derivation can be found in Ref.
holevo_1973
.

Let
S_{\rm B}
and
{D}_{\mathrm{B},j}
the Bayesian averaged state and the first moment defined before.
We define the Bayesian RLD
\widetilde{L}_{j}
by the RLD-type equation:

{D}_{\mathrm{B},j}=S_{\rm B}\widetilde{L}_{j}.

(10)

Under the assumption of positivity of states, we have the unique solution
\widetilde{L}_{j}=S_{\rm B}^{-1}{D}_{\mathrm{B},j}
.
Define an Hermitian
n\times n
matrix
\widetilde{K}
whose
(j,k)
component is

\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{jk}:=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\rm B}\widetilde{L}_{k}\widetilde{L}_{j}^{\dagger}\right\}.

(11)

The Bayesian version of the RLD Cramér-Rao bound is given as

{\cal C}_{\mathrm{RLD}}:=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathsf{W}(\mathsf{M}-\mathrm{Re}\,\widetilde{\mathsf{K}})\right\}+\mathrm{TrAbs}\left\{\mathsf{W}\,\mathrm{Im}\,\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}\right\},

(12)

where
\mathrm{Re}\,A
(
\mathrm{Im}\,A
) denotes the component-wise real (imaginary) part of
A
for a matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
,
and
\mathrm{TrAbs}\left\{A\right\}
denotes the sum of the absolute values for the eigenvalues of
A
.

III
Results

In this section, we propose a new lower bound for the Bayes risk, which is applicable for arbitrary weight matrix. To state the main theorem, we need to introduce several notations first.

III.1
Alternative expression for the Bayes risk

Noting the MSE matrix
\mathsf{V}_{\theta}
consists of four terms

\mathsf{V}_{\theta,jk}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]=\sum_{x}\hat{\theta}_{j}(x)\hat{\theta}_{k}(x)\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\theta}\Pi_{x}\right\}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt-\sum_{x}\theta_{j}\hat{\theta}_{k}(x)\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\theta}\Pi_{x}\right\}\\
-\sum_{x}\hat{\theta}_{j}(x)\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\theta}\Pi_{x}\right\}\theta_{k}+\theta_{j}\theta_{k},

we introduce the following Hermitian matrices on
{\cal H}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{jk}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\hat{\theta}_{j}(x)\Pi_{x}\hat{\theta}_{k}(x)\quad(j,k=1,2,\dots,n),

\displaystyle X_{j}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\hat{\theta}_{j}(x)\Pi_{x}\quad(j=1,2,\ldots,n).

Importantly, these matrices are solely defined by a quantum decision
\Pi,\hat{\theta}
, and hence
they are independent of model parameter
\theta
.
In the following, we often omit the argument
\Pi
and
\hat{\theta}
, when it is clear from the context.
With these definitions, the MSE matrix is expressed as

\mathsf{V}_{\theta,jk}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\theta}(\mathbb{L}_{jk}-\theta_{j}X_{k}-X_{j}\theta_{k}+\theta_{j}\theta_{k})\right\}.

We next define a matrix and a vector which are defined on the extended Hilbert space
lja2021
.
Consider the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}:={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\otimes{\cal H}
, and define
\mathbb{L}
on
\mathbb{H}
whose
(j,k)
block -matrix component is given by
\mathbb{L}_{jk}
.
We also define a column vector
X
whose
j
th component is
X_{j}
by
X=(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})^{\intercal}
.
Here, we denote transpose of matrices and vectors with respect to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by
(\cdot)^{\intercal}
.
The fundamental inequality is stated in the following lemma, which is a variant of Holevo’s lemma
holevo
.

Lemma 2

(
hayashi99
)
.

For all POVMs and estimators,
\mathbb{L}
and
X
obey the matrix inequality:

\mathbb{L}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\geq X[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\left(X[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\right)^{\intercal}.

The weighted trace of the MSE matrix then takes of the form:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathsf{W}(\theta)\mathsf{V}_{\theta}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j,k}\mathsf{W}_{jk}(\theta)\mathrm{Tr}\left\{S_{\theta}(\mathbb{L}_{jk}-\theta_{j}X_{k}-X_{j}\theta_{k}+\theta_{j}\theta_{k})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathbb{S}\mathbb{L}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{D(\theta)X^{\intercal}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{XD^{\intercal}(\theta)\right\}+\mathsf{w}(\theta).

In this expression, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle:=[\mathbb{S}_{jk}(\theta)]\ \mbox{with}\ \mathbb{S}_{jk}(\theta):=\mathsf{W}_{jk}(\theta)S_{\theta},

\displaystyle D(\theta)

\displaystyle:=[D_{j}(\theta)]\ \mbox{with}\ D_{j}(\theta):=\sum_{k}\mathsf{W}_{jk}(\theta)\theta_{k}S_{\theta},

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j,k}\theta_{j}\mathsf{W}_{jk}(\theta)\theta_{k}.

By definition,
\mathbb{S}(\theta)=\mathsf{W}(\theta)\otimes S_{\theta}
is an operator on the extended Hilbert space.

D(\theta)
is a column vector with Hermitian matrix elements.
These quantities are determined by the quantum statistical model and the weight matrix.

After combining the above expressions and the integration with respect to the prior,
we obtain the alternative form of the Bayes risk:

Lemma 3
.

The Bayes risk is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{L}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{D}X^{\intercal}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{X\overline{D}^{\intercal}\right\}+\overline{\mathsf{w}},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{S}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Theta}\,d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\mathbb{S},

(13)

\displaystyle\overline{D}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Theta}\,d\theta\,\pi(\theta)D(\theta),

(14)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathsf{w}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Theta}\,d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\mathsf{w}(\theta),

(15)

where
\overline{\cdot}
denotes the averaged operators with respect to the prior distribution.

We emphasize that everything is exact so far.
We also remind ourselves that
\mathbb{L}
and
X
are functions of a POVM
\Pi
and an estimator
\hat{\theta}
.

III.2
New Bayesian bounds

To derive a lower bound for the Bayes risk
\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]
, we follow the same line of logic used in Ref.
lja2021
.
Combining Lemma
2
and Lemma
3
gives the main result of the paper.

Theorem 4

(Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound)
.

For any POVM
\Pi
and estimator
\hat{\theta}
, the following lower bound holds for the Bayes risk.

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]\geq{\cal C}_{\mathrm{NH}}

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{\mathrm{NH}}:=\min_{\mathbb{L},X}\left\{\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{L}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{D}X^{\intercal}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{X\overline{D}^{\intercal}\right\}\right\}+\overline{\mathsf{w}}.

(16)

Here optimization is subject to the constraints:

\forall j,k,\mathbb{L}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}_{kj}
,
\mathbb{L}_{jk}
is Hermitian,
X_{j}
is Hermitian, and
\mathbb{L}\geq{X}X^{\intercal}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{L}_{*}
and
X_{*}
be the optimal quantities calculated from an optimal POVM
\Pi_{*}
and an optimal estimator
\hat{\theta}_{*}
.
Then, the following chain of inequalities holds.

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}[\Pi,\hat{\theta}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathsf{R}[\Pi_{*},\hat{\theta}_{*}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{L}_{*}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{D}X_{*}^{\intercal}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{X_{*}\overline{D}^{\intercal}\right\}+\overline{\mathsf{w}}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\mathbb{L},X}\left\{\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{L}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{D}X^{\intercal}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{X\overline{D}^{\intercal}\right\}\right\}+\overline{\mathsf{w}}.

The first inequality follows by definition of the optimizer.
The second line is due to Lemma
3
.
To get the third line, we apply Lemma
2
.
In the last line, optimization is subject to the constraints stated in the theorem in particular
\mathbb{L}\geq{X}X^{\intercal}
.

∎

The main difference from the Nagaoka-Hayashi bound for the point estimation setting
lja2021
is that
there is no constraint about the locally unbiasedness.
The next result shows that the proposed Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound can be computed efficiently by a semidefinite programming (SDP) problem.

Proposition 5
.

The Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound is semidefinite programming.

Proof.

To put the Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound in the SDP form, we write the first three terms of Eq. (
16
) as

\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{L}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{D}X^{\intercal}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{X\overline{D}^{\intercal}\right\}\\
=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\overline{\mathbb{S}}&-\overline{D}\\
-\overline{D}^{\intercal}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{L}&X\\
X^{\intercal}&1\end{array}\right)\right\}.

Clearly, this is an SDP problem, since the constraint on the variable
\mathbb{L}\geq{X}X^{\intercal}
is equivalent to a positive semidefinite condition:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{L}&X\\
X^{\intercal}&1\end{array}\right)\geq 0.

Other constraints on
\mathbb{L}
and
X
can also be put in the trace condition for the variable (see Supplementary Note 4 of Ref.
lja2021
how to implement these conditions as semidefinite programming).

∎

As the Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound involves two optimization,
we shall propose lower bounds, which are expressed as optimization over only
X
.
When estimating two parameters, the Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound (
16
)
will be called as the Bayesian Nagaoka bound as in point-wise estimation.
This will be denoted as
{\cal C}_{\mathrm{N}}
.
For the Bayesian Nagaoka bound, we show the following lower bound
that is expressed as an optimization problem with respect to the variable
X
only.

Theorem 6

(Lower bound for the Bayesian Nagaoka bound)
.

When the number of parameters is equal to two, the Bayesian Nagaoka bound
{\cal C}_{\mathrm{N}}
is bounded as follows.

{\cal C}_{\mathrm{N}}\geq\min_{X=(X_{1},X_{2})^{\intercal}}\big\{\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathrm{sym}_{+}\left(\sqrt{\overline{\mathbb{S}}}XX^{\intercal}\sqrt{\overline{\mathbb{S}}}\right)\right\}\\
\hskip 8.53581pt+\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\sqrt{\mathrm{Det}\left\{\mathsf{W}(\theta)\right\}}\mathrm{TrAbs}\left\{S_{\theta}[X_{1}\,,\,X_{2}]\right\}\\
-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{D}X^{\intercal}\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{X\overline{D}^{\intercal}\right\}\big\}+\overline{\mathsf{w}}.

(17)

Here optimization is subject to:
X_{1},X_{2}
are Hermitian.

In this theorem,
\mathrm{sym}_{+}(\mathbb{A}):=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{A}^{\intercal})
denotes the symmetrized matrix with respect to the first Hilbert space of the extended Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\otimes{\cal H}
, i.e.,
for
\mathbb{A}=[\mathbb{A}_{jk}]\in\mathbb{H}^{dn\times dn}
,
[\mathrm{sym}_{+}(\mathbb{A})]_{jk}=(\mathbb{A}_{jk}+\mathbb{A}_{kj})/2
.

Proof.

This theorem is proven by using Lemma
16
in Appendix.

We next turn our attention to the Bayesian Holevo-type bound which is in general lower than
the Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound.

Theorem 7

(Bayesian Holevo-type bound)
.

{\cal C}_{\mathrm{NH}}\geq{\cal C}_{\mathrm{H}}\\
{\cal C}_{\mathrm{H}}:=\min_{X_{j}:\mathrm{Hermitian}}\big\{\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathsf{Z}[\overline{\mathbb{S}},X]\right\}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\Theta}d\theta\,\pi(\theta)\mathrm{TrAbs}\left\{\mathrm{Im}\,\mathsf{Z}[\mathbb{S}(\theta),X]\right\}\\
-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\overline{D}^{\intercal}X\right\}-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{X^{\intercal}\overline{D}\right\}\big\}+\overline{\mathsf{w}},

(18)

where
\mathsf{Z}[\mathbb{S},X]

Parameter-independent weight matrix

To make a comparison to existing lower bounds in the literature,

III.4
Relation to the Bayesian quantum Cramér-Rao bounds

We claim the proposed Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound is tighter than the Bayesian quantum Cramér-Rao bounds.

IV
Conclusion and outlook

In summary we proposed a new lower bound for the Bayes risk, called the Bayesian Nagaoka-Hayashi bound.
This bound then can be bounded by a Bayesian version of the Holevo bound.
It was shown that the new lower bound is tighter than the existing lower bound based on the Bayesian quantum Cramér-Rao bounds
personick71
;
holevo_1973
;
rd2020
.
The proposed Bayesian lower bounds are based on the idea developed in the previous publication
lja2021
.

In this paper, we only derived bounds, and hence there are many future directions.
Firstly, we need to analyze achievability of new proposed bounds both in the asymptotic and non asymptotic theory.
For example, Ref.
gill08
investigated the first order asymptotics of the Bayes risk.
Secondly, relations to other Bayesian bounds need to be examined.
In particular, a further analysis is needed when the weight matrix is parameter-dependent.
Thirdly, an extension including random parameters in the presence of nuisance parameters will be
important. Non-random parameter estimation with the nuisance parameter has been investigated recently
syh2021
;
tad2020
.
Extensions of these formulations in the Bayesian setting will be presented in the future work.

Basics of factorization in a scalar Yukawa field theory Preprint: JLAB-THY-22-3760
F. Aslan, L. Gamberg, J. O. Gonzalez-Hernandez, T. Rainaldi, T. C. Rogers

Abstract
The factorization theorems of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) apply equally well to most simple quantum field theories that require renormalization but where direct calculations are much more straightforward. Working with these simpler theories is convenient for stress-testing the limits of the factorization program and for examining general properties of the parton density functions (pdfs) or other correlation functions that might be necessary for a factorized description of a process. With this view in mind, we review the steps of factorization in a real scalar Yukawa field theory for both deep inelastic scattering (DIS) and semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering (SIDIS) cross sections. In the case of SIDIS, we illustrate how to separate the small transverse momentum region, where transverse momentum dependent (TMD) pdfs are needed, from a purely collinear large transverse momentum region, and we examine the influence of subleading power corrections. We also review the steps for formulating TMD factorization in transverse coordinate space, and we study the effect of transforming to the well-known b ∗ b_{*} -scheme. Within the Yukawa theory, we investigate the consequences of switching to a generalized parton model (GPM) approach, and compare with a fully factorized approach. Our results highlight the need to address similar or analogous issues in QCD.



I
Introduction

It is often the goal of hadronic scattering experiments to gain an increased understanding of the intrinsic properties of the scattered hadrons. To this end, the parton model
Feynman 1972
, wherein hadrons are viewed as collections of nearly free
point-like constituents, is indispensable as a framework for constructing models of intrinsic structure and relating them to high-energy scattering observables.
However, in theories that require renormalization, the operators that count the number of elementary particles in a target are beset by divergences, and the steps for dealing with them sometimes require modifications of the intuitive expectations that arise from a purely parton model framework. A recently discussed example concerns the question of whether parton density functions must be strictly positive definite
Candido et al. 2020
; a literal probability density interpretation would imply strict positivity, but it turns out that the pdfs in a typical renormalizable quantum field theory can violate positivity, depending on the choice of renormalization scheme and the scales
Collins et al. 2022
. While issues such as these are naturally relevant when interpreting measurements in terms of partonic constituents, they also have important practical and phenomenological consequences. For example, with regard to the positivity question, it is important to know if strict positivity should be imposed directly on fit parametrizations. This is relevant to, among other things, recent debates about the evidence for an intrinsic charm component in the proton pdf
Ball et al. 2022
;
Courtoy et al. 2022
;
Guzzi et al. 2022
. Indeed, charm quark pdf extractions do appear to require that negative pdfs be allowed (see, for example, Figure 1 of
Ball et al. 2022
).
Other examples of the role of positivity-related constraints can be found in Refs.
D’Alesio et al. 2020
;
Aamodt et al. 2010
;
Gamberg et al. 2022
.

In quantum field theory (QFT), the parton model gets placed on firmer footing through the factorization theorems
Collins and Soper 1987
;
Collins et al. 1989
;
Collins 2011
.
Nowadays, however, generalizations of a basic partonic picture play a role in scenarios far beyond the original leading power descriptions of inclusive processes like inclusive deep inelastic scattering
Liu 2019
;
Abdul Khalek et al. 2022
. They appear, for example, in the factorization theorems for semi-inclusive processes where TMD pdfs and fragmentation functions (ffs) are important. The parton model also accounts for spin dependent effects, and there are partonic descriptions of higher-twist behavior
Efremov and Teryaev 1982
;
Qiu and Sterman 1991
;
Jaffe and Ji 1992
;
Ji 1993
;
Tangerman and Mulders 1994
;
Mulders and Tangerman 1996
;
Bacchetta et al. 2007
;
Qiu et al. 2020
. In all such cases, it is important to stress-test the limits of any assumptions that are rooted in a parton model picture. Confinement, non-Abelian gauge invariance, and the general complexity of strongly coupled nonperturbative quantum field theory makes this difficult in real QCD. However, many of the steps in standard factorization derivations are not specific to QCD, but instead apply rather generally to most of the simpler relativistic renormalizable quantum field theories found in the introductory chapters of textbooks. By retracing the steps of factorization in those theories, where contributions from all spacetime scales can be handled perturbatively, it becomes straightforward to confirm the most basic consequences of factorization while also probing their limits. Indeed, it is possible to find departures from parton model expectations arising from the need for renormalization alone.
A recently discussed example is the use of a scalar Yukawa theory
Collins et al. 2022
, already mentioned above, to illustrate the possibility of positivity violations in pdfs defined with the
\overline{\rm MS}
renormalization scheme. Simple field theories are useful more generally for stress-testing other aspects of factorization and related assertions regarding pdfs and notions of intrinsicness in the presence of renormalization

This motivates us in the present paper, to further explore the factorization of simple QFTs in DIS. We will present calculations of the cross sections for both DIS and SIDIS in a scalar Yukawa model theory with non-zero masses for all fields first
without
factorization. By keeping the coupling small, we ensure that all parts of these calculation can be handled simply with low order Feynman diagrams. Next, we will retrace the basic steps involved in factorizing the graphs in the large-
Q
, fixed Bjorken-
x
deep inelastic limit. It then becomes possible to compare the unfactorized, unapproximated results with standard collinear and TMD factorization treatments. Sensitivity to the mass scales in the Lagrangian serves as a measure of sensitivity to intrinsic large distance dynamics, analogous to the sensitivity to confinement scale physics in QCD. We will compare factorized and unfactorized versions of the same calculations and note how the sizes of the differences between them can provide guidance on questions relevant to implementations of both TMD and collinear factorization at moderate hard scales.

Some of the questions to be addressed are:

•

What are typical sizes of subleading powers (or higher twist) at moderate
Q
and how important are they for maintaining reasonable agreement with the unfactorized cross section?

•

What are typical consequences of switching between different precise definitions for objects like pdfs? For example, what is the effect of using a collinear pdf defined as the cutoff integral of a TMD pdf as opposed to the usual renormalized definition for the pdf?

•

What are the relative sizes of contributions from large and small transverse momentum in TMD parton densities, and how important are the large-
q_{\text{T}}
corrections to TMD factorization in transverse momentum dependent cross sections?

•

In TMD factorization, it is standard to transform from transverse momentum space to coordinate
\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}
space. Then, one identifies small-
\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}
contributions with collinear factorization contributions and the large-
\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}
contributions with the intrinsic or nonperturbative properties of hadrons. In standard approaches to TMD factorization, the large-
\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}
is sequestered in the exponential of functions labeled
g
. What is the typical impact of implementing this separation, and how sensitive are results to the choice of large-
\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}
modeling?

We will begin in Sec.

V

VI

We will also discuss the steps for recovering collinear factorization by integrating TMD factorized expressions over all transvere momentum, and we will compare with an approach that only uses TMD pdfs (the GPM). In Sec.
VII
, we will comment on the lower boundary in
Q

VIII

The well-known steps for deriving factorization in a gauge theory carry over straightforwardly to the scalar Yukawa theory, and indeed are much simpler due to the absence of large coupling, confinement, gauge degrees of freedom and the need for Wilson lines. Notice that the graphs in Fig.

The divergences that do appear in our calculations of those graphs at intermediate stages are, therefore, artifacts of factorization approximations. Thus, our calculations will help clarify the nature of those divergences.

f_{0,i/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}})=\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muw^{-}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{w}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\xi p^{+}w^{-}+i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{\text{T}}}\;\langle p|\,\bar{\psi}_{0,i}(0,w^{-},\boldsymbol{w}_{\text{T}}){\frac{\gamma^{+}}{2}}\psi_{0,i}(0,0,\boldsymbol{0}_{\text{T}})\,|p\rangle\,.

(28)

To get a renormalized TMD pdf in the Yukawa theory, we only need to switch to the renormalized field

f_{0,i/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}})=Z_{2}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muw^{-}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{w}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\xi p^{+}w^{-}+i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{\text{T}}}\;\langle p|\,\bar{\psi}_{i}(0,w^{-},\boldsymbol{w}_{\text{T}}){\frac{\gamma^{+}}{2}}\psi_{i}(0,0,\boldsymbol{0}_{\text{T}})\,|p\rangle\,=Z_{2}f_{i/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu).

(29)

Since the wavefunction renormalization has the form
Z_{2}=1+\order{a_\lambda}
, there is no
\order{a_\lambda}
self-energy contribution in the
\order{a_\lambda}
graphs in Fig.
2
, so we will have no explicit
Z_{2}
contribution to our
\order{a_\lambda}
quark-in-hadron TMD pdf. Therefore, the expression for the TMD pdf
f_{q/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)
is obtained by simply dropping the counterterm in Eq. (
27
), keeping the integrand of the first term, and taking the limit to 4 dimensions:

\displaystyle f^{(1)}_{q/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)

\displaystyle{}=\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{\pi}(1-\xi)\frac{k^{2}_{\text{T}}+\chi(\xi)^{2}}{\big[k^{2}_{\text{T}}+\Delta(\xi)^{2}\big]^{2}}\,.

(30)

We will need these expressions in later sections.

Dealing with divergences and evolution in the Yukawa theory is far simpler than in a gauge theory due to the absence of Wilson lines or light-cone divergences.
In the Yukawa theory above, TMD evolution equation for the quark-in-hadron TMD pdf is also very simple because it only involves the wavefunction normalization
Z_{2}
in Eq. (
29
). The TMD evolution equation
(
Collins 2011
, Sec. 8.71)
is just

\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\ln\mu}\nolimits{}}\ln f_{q/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)=-2\gamma_{2}(a_{\lambda}(\mu))\,,

(31)

where

\gamma_{2}(a_{\lambda}(\mu))\equiv\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\ln Z_{2}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\ln\mu}\nolimits{}}\,.

(32)

At lowest order,

\gamma_{2}^{(1)}(a_{\lambda}(\mu))=-\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{2}\,.

(33)

The general solution to the TMD evolution equation is

f_{q/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)=f_{q/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu_{0})\exp\left\{-2\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\mu}\nolimits{}}{\mu}\gamma_{2}(a_{\lambda}(\mu))\right\}\,,

(34)

where evolution is from a reference scale
\mu_{0}
up to a generic large scale
\mu
.

There are alternative ways to provide an exact definition to a collinear pdf free of UV divergences. One way that very closely coincides with parton model intuition is to define it as the TMD pdf integrated up to a cutoff
k_{c}
,

f^{c}_{q/p}(\xi;\mu;k_{c})\equiv\pi\int_{0}^{k_{c}^{2}}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}f_{q/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)\,.

(35)

Normally,
k_{c}
is set equal to
\mu
, but this need not be the case.
This approach is preferred in some areas of small-
x
physics, e.g.
Kimber et al. 2001
;
Watt et al. 2003
;
Guiot 2020
, where the relation is taken as a definition for the TMD pdf, and is usually called an “unintegrated” pdf. With the TMD pdf calculated in Eq. (
30
), the cutoff definition for the collinear pdf is

f^{c}_{q/p}(\xi;\mu;k_{c})=a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-\xi)\left[\ln\left(\frac{\Delta(\xi)^{2}+k_{c}^{2}}{\Delta(\xi)^{2}}\right)-\frac{k_{c}^{2}}{k_{c}^{2}+\Delta(\xi)^{2}}+\frac{k_{c}^{2}\chi(\xi)^{2}}{\Delta(\xi)^{2}\left[\Delta(\xi)^{2}+k_{c}^{2}\right]}\right]\,.

(36)

This definition of the collinear pdf only equals the standard
\overline{\rm MS}
definition in Eq. (
24
) if
k_{c}=\mu
and
\order{m^2/\mu^2}
corrections are neglected. Beyond lowest order, the connection between the cutoff and the renormalized definitions can also involve non-power-suppressed terms, and in gauge theories there are complications with the Wilson line in relations like Eq. (
35
) that we will not address here (see, however, the detailed discussion in
Collins 2003
).

V
collinear factorization

Now that we have identified the pdf contributions, we may build up the factorized expressions for structure functions starting from the exact results for Fig.
2
and applying the approximations appropriate to the deeply inelastic regime. We seek the form of the standard collinear factorization theorem for inclusive DIS,

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}(p,q)

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\int_{x_{\rm bj}}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits}{\xi}\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}_{i/i^{\prime}}(x_{\rm bj}/\xi,q;\mu)f_{i^{\prime}/p}(\xi;\mu)+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}_{i/i^{\prime}}\otimes f_{i^{\prime}/p}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,,

(37)

where
\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}_{i/i^{\prime}}(x_{\rm bj}/\xi,q)
is a partonic structure tensor (with suitable subtractions, to be discussed below) for a
massless, on-shell partonic target of flavor
i^{\prime}
,
f_{i^{\prime}/p}(\xi;\mu)
is a pdf for a parton
flavor
i^{\prime}
in target
p
,
\mu
is the renormalization group scale, and
\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}
is a
sum over all flavors.
The analogous expressions for structure functions are

\displaystyle F_{1}(x_{\rm bj},Q^{2})

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{ii^{\prime}}\int_{x_{\rm bj}}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits}{\xi}\widehat{F}_{1,i/i^{\prime}}(x_{\rm bj}/\xi,\mu^{2}/Q^{2};\mu)f_{i^{\prime}/p}(\xi;\mu)+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,

(38)

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\widehat{F}_{1,i/i^{\prime}}\otimes f_{i^{\prime}/p}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,,

\displaystyle F_{2}(x_{\rm bj},Q^{2})

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\int_{x_{\rm bj}}^{1}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits\widehat{F}_{2,i/i^{\prime}}(x_{\rm bj}/\xi,\mu^{2}/Q^{2};\mu)f_{i^{\prime}/p}(\xi;\mu)+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,

(39)

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{ii^{\prime}}\xi\widehat{F}_{2,i/i^{\prime}}\otimes f_{i^{\prime}/p}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,.

In the limit that the
\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}
terms are negligible, the structure functions have
process-specific hard parts,
\widehat{F}_{1,2}
, that are insensitive to large spacetime distances. But the collinear pdfs
f_{i^{\prime}/p}
account for the intrinsic properties of the target, so we should expect them to retain sensitivity to
m
.

In this section, we will systematically step through the approximations necessary to factorize the graphs in Fig.
2
as in Eqs. (
38
)–(
39
) for the Yukawa theory.
The specific task is to expand in small

m^{2}/Q^{2}
and confirm that factorization is satisfied order-by-order in
a_{\lambda}
. For example, a structure function (say
F_{1}
) becomes

\displaystyle F_{1}(x_{\rm bj},Q)

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{ii^{\prime}}(\widehat{F}_{1}^{(0)}+\widehat{F}_{1}^{(1)}+\cdots)_{i/i^{\prime}}\otimes(f^{(0)}+f^{(1)}+\cdots)_{i^{\prime}/p}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{ii^{\prime}}\widehat{F}_{1,i/i^{\prime}}\otimes f_{i^{\prime}/p}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,,

(40)

where the superscript “
(n)
” refers to the order in
a_{\lambda}
, and the “
\cdots
” refers to higher orders in
a_{\lambda}
.

\widehat{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. Including the trivial
convolution with the zeroth order pdf in Eq. (
IV
) produces the second term of Eq. (

),

\displaystyle\widehat{F}^{(1)}_{1,q/p}\otimes f^{(0)}_{p/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{2}\int_{0}^{\hat{k}_{\rm m}^{2}(x_{\rm bj})}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}\left(\frac{(1-x_{\rm bj})(1-\kappa(x_{\rm bj}))}{\kappa(x_{\rm bj})k^{2}_{\text{T}}}-\frac{x_{\rm bj}(2x_{\rm bj}^{2}-6x_{\rm bj}+3)}{Q^{2}(1-x_{\rm bj})^{2}\kappa(x_{\rm bj})}\right)-\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{2}(1-x_{\rm bj})\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{\hat{k}_{\rm m}^{2}(x_{\rm bj})}\,.

(61)

Now the integral over
k_{\text{T}}
is well-behaved in both the UV and IR limits, confirming that
no lower cutoff was needed, so we have removed the
k_{\rm cut}
from Eq. (

Equation (

) accounts for the zeroth order contribution to the hard partonic structure function while
Eq. (

) accounts for the first
\order{a_\lambda}
contribution. Both must
be present in order to have complete factorization with only power suppressed and
\order{a_\lambda^2}
errors.

Fully factorized result

Combining Eq. (
V.2
) and Eq. (

\displaystyle F_{1}(x_{\rm bj},Q)

\displaystyle{}=\underbrace{\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{2}(1-x_{\rm bj})\left(\frac{(m_{q}+x_{\rm bj}m_{p})^{2}}{\Delta(x_{\rm bj})^{2}}+\ln\left[\frac{\mu^{2}}{\Delta(x_{\rm bj})^{2}}\right]-1\right)}_{\hat{F}_{1,q/q}^{(0)}\otimes f^{(1)}_{q/p}}+

\displaystyle{}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{2}\left[2(1-x_{\rm bj})\ln\left(2\right)-\frac{2x_{\rm bj}^{2}-6x_{\rm bj}+3}{2(1-x_{\rm bj})}\right]-\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{2}(1-x_{\rm bj})\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{\hat{k}_{\rm m}^{2}(x_{\rm bj})}\right)}_{\hat{F}_{1,q/p}^{(1)}\otimes f^{(0)}_{p/p}}\,

\displaystyle{}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}, a_\lambda^2}\,.

(62)

Equation (
62

\displaystyle{}F_{1}(x_{\rm bj},Q)=\sum_{i}\int_{x_{\rm bj}}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi}\times

\displaystyle\times\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\left\{\delta\left(1-\frac{x_{\rm bj}}{\xi}\right)\delta_{qi}+a_{\lambda}(\mu)\left(1-\frac{x_{\rm bj}}{\xi}\right)\left[\ln\left(4\right)-\frac{\left(\frac{x_{\rm bj}}{\xi}\right)^{2}-3\frac{x_{\rm bj}}{\xi}+\frac{3}{2}}{\left(1-\frac{x_{\rm bj}}{\xi}\right)^{2}}-\ln\frac{4x_{\rm bj}\mu^{2}}{Q^{2}(\xi-x_{\rm bj})}\right]\delta_{pi}\right\}}_{\hat{F}_{1,q/i}(x_{\rm bj}/\xi,\mu/Q;a_{\lambda}(\mu))}\times

\displaystyle{}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}, a_\lambda^2}\,.

(63)

The second line is now the (subtracted) hard partonic structure function through
\order{a_\lambda}

\order{a_\lambda}

in the form of Eq. (

m/Q\to 0

a_{\lambda}

Several well-known features of factorization are recognizable in Eq. (
63
). First, the hard factor
\hat{F}_{1,q/i}
on the second line is a partonic DIS
structure function, it is process-specific, and it depends on the process-specific kinematical variable
Q

m_{q}
,
m_{s}
or
m_{p}
that govern intrinsic structure over large spacetime scales.
Conversely, the pdf

on the third line does depend on intrinsic scales, but it is universal in that it follows directly (Eq. (
IV

24

Second, the logarithmic dependence on
\mu

a_{\lambda}(\mu)

residual
\mu
dependence is in the
\order{a_\lambda(\mu)^2}

running of parameters like
a_{\lambda}(\mu)

orders.

In an asymptotically free theory like QCD, the goal would be to ensure that higher order terms in the perturbative expansion of the hard part remain small or finite as

\hat{F}_{1,q/i}(x_{\rm bj}/\xi,\mu/Q;a_{\lambda}(\mu))
need to be kept under control by choosing to set

With such a choice, all
Q
-dependence
in the hard part
resides in the running of the coupling, which vanishes in the DIS limit. In the non-asymptotically free Yukawa toy theory that we are using here, there are fewer advantages to doing this, but the steps nevertheless very clearly illustrate the procedure. Thus, we are generally interested in the pdf defined with its scale of order
Q
,
f_{q/p}(\xi;Q)
.

The steps above apply in the same way to the

structure function, giving

\displaystyle{}\times\underbrace{\left\{\delta\left(1-\xi\right)\delta_{ip}+a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-\xi)\left[\frac{\chi(\xi)^{2}}{\Delta(\xi)^{2}}+\ln\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\Delta(\xi)^{2}}\right)-1\right]\delta_{iq}\right\}}_{f_{i/p}(\xi;\mu)}

VI
TMD Factorization & SIDIS

So far, we have focused on collinear factorization and DIS, but we may regard the same set of graphs in Fig.
2
as contributions to SIDIS and use TMD factorization. For this, we will take the observed final state particle
B
to be the “quark” of the Yukawa theory in Sec.
III
. The TMD factorization formula for the hadronic tensor is then

\displaystyle{}W^{\mu\nu}(x_{\rm bj},Q,z_{h},\boldsymbol{P}_{B\text{T}})

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{ij}H_{ij}(\mu/Q;\mu)^{\mu\nu}\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{1\text{T}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{2\text{T}}}\nolimits{}f_{i/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\text{T}};\mu)D_{B/j}(z_{h},z_{h}\boldsymbol{k}_{2\text{T}};\mu)\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\text{T}}+\boldsymbol{k}_{1\text{T}}-\boldsymbol{k}_{2\text{T}})+Y^{\mu\nu}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{ij}H_{ij}(\mu/Q;\mu)^{\mu\nu}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mskip-0.59999mu\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits}{(2\pi)^{2}}~e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}~\tilde{f}_{i/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)~\tilde{D}_{B/j}(z_{h},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)+Y^{\mu\nu}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{ij}H_{ij}(\mu/Q;\mu)^{\mu\nu}\left[f_{i/p},D_{B/j}\right]+Y^{\mu\nu}+\order{\frac{m^2}{Q^2}}\,.

(66)

The form of the first term on the first line is well-known from TMD parton model treatments. The transverse momentum
\boldsymbol{P}_{B\text{T}}
is with respect to the Breit frame, while the momenta in the integrand are in the hadron frame. On the second line, the transverse momentum convolution has been re-expressed in transverse coordinate space, as is very common in treatments that implement TMD evolution in real QCD. We have used the transverse momentum variable

\boldsymbol{q}_{\text{T}}\equiv-\frac{\boldsymbol{P}_{B\text{T}}}{z_{\rm N}}\,,

(67)

which is the hadron frame transverse momentum of the virtual photon.
On the last line we have used a very standard bracket notation (e.g.
Tangerman and Mulders 1995
;
Mulders and Tangerman 1996
) for transverse convolution integrals, especially in the context of hadron structure. The
Y^{\mu\nu}
is the large-
q_{\text{T}}
correction term to account for
q_{\text{T}}\approx Q
. The coordinate space correlation functions are

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)

\displaystyle{}=\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}f_{i/p}(\xi,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)

(68)

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{q/j}(\zeta,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)

\displaystyle{}=\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{2\text{T}}}\nolimits{}e^{i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}D_{q/i}(\zeta,\zeta\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)\,.

(69)

Note the factor of
\zeta
multiplying
\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}
in the argument of the TMD FF. The hard factor is

H_{ij}(\mu/Q;\mu)^{\mu\nu}=\frac{z_{h}}{2}{\rm Tr}[\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{+}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{-}]|H(\mu/Q;\mu)|^{2}_{ij}\,,

(70)

where
|H(\mu/Q;\mu)|^{2}
is a hard vertex factor that in perturbation theory takes the form

|H(\mu/Q;\mu)|^{2}_{ij}=1+\order{a_\lambda(\mu)}\,.

(71)

The unpolarized quark structure functions follow from Eq. (
12
),

\displaystyle{}{\rm P}_{1}^{\mu\nu}H_{ij}(\mu/Q;\mu)_{\mu\nu}=H_{1}(\mu/Q;\mu)\delta_{iq}\delta_{jq}=2\,z_{h}|H(\mu/Q;\mu)|^{2}_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle{}{\rm P}_{2}^{\mu\nu}H_{ij}(\mu/Q;\mu)_{\mu\nu}=H_{2}(\mu/Q;\mu)\delta_{iq}\delta_{jq}=4\,z_{h}x_{\rm bj}|H(\mu/Q;\mu)|^{2}_{ij}\,.

(72)

The above is general, and applies equally to QCD and to the Yukawa theory. However, the expressions simplify considerably when we specialize to the low order Yukawa theory graphs of Fig.
2
. Then, there is only one flavor of struck parton, so we may drop the sums over flavor indices. Also, there is only one particle flavor that can appear in the final state, namely the quark. So
B=q
and we may drop the sum over
B
. The TMD FF has the trivial form in the current region of the
W
-term,

D(z_{h},z_{h}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)=\delta(1-z_{h})\delta^{(2)}(z_{h}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}})\,.

(73)

Therefore, we may integrate the cross section over
z_{\rm N}
to evaluate the
\delta
-function at
z_{\rm N}=1
. The cross section that we will consider, therefore, is actually

\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{\rm N}}\nolimits{}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\sigma_{\rm SIDIS}}\nolimits{}}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mux_{\rm bj}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muy}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\psi}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{\rm N}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{P}_{B\text{T}}}\nolimits}\,,

(74)

with

approximated by

in Eq. (

VII
The input scale
Q_{0}

The steps required to construct both the collinear and TMD factorization expressions in the previous two sections relied on expansions in
m^{2}/Q^{2}
, so the factorized expressions are useful approximations only when
Q
is sufficiently large compared with intrinsic mass scales. Below some value, the justification for any truncation in powers of
m/Q
fails. Therefore, applications of factorization generally require one to specify a (possibly
x_{\rm bj}
-dependent) minimum
Q=Q_{0}
below which the expansion is no longer trusted. Typically, one tries to choose
Q_{0}
to be as small as can be reasonably justified so as to maximally exploit factorization techniques over the widest possible kinematical range. Sometimes, this is achieved by including parametrizations of subleading power behavior
Accardi et al. 2010
;
Accardi et al. 2016
. In standard treatments of DIS, a typical
Q_{0}
is usually between approximately
1
and
4
GeVs.

The sample curves from the Yukawa theory shown in Fig.
4
demonstrate the limited validity of the factorization method as
Q
decreases. With the values of
m_{q}
,
m_{s}
, and
m_{p}
chosen in those figures, the choice of
Q_{0}
should be no smaller than around
1
GeV, although for small
x_{\rm bj}
is appears to be possible to push
Q_{0}
lower.
3
3

3

The reason is that the errors in the power expansion go like
x_{\rm bj}m^{2}/Q^{2}
at small
x_{\rm bj}
, at least for these low order graphs.

Notice that it is the only size of the external kinematical variables
Q
and
x_{\rm bj}
relative to the intrinsic that determines the level of agreement between the unfactorized and factorized expressions. If we neglect the running of the parameters, then there is no dependence at all upon the auxiliary parameter
\mu
(see Eq. (
62
) and Fig.
5
). In QCD, that arbitrariness in the choice of renormalization scale is exploited to minimize the size of higher order errors.

One way to introduce an extra adjustable parameter in a way that might allow the factorized expression to be improved along the lines of
Accardi et al. 2010
;
Accardi et al. 2016
, for at least some regions of kinematics, is to
switch out the
\overline{\rm MS}
renormalized definition for the pdf with the cutoff definition in Eq. (
35
).
The new parameter this introduces is the cutoff scale
k_{c}
, and one may attempt to adjust this to extend agreement between the factorized and unfactorized expressions to smaller
Q
.

For a generic unfactorized structure function
F
, the percent errors introduced by factorization are

\begin{split}\delta F^{\overline{\rm MS}}\equiv\left|\frac{F-F^{\overline{\rm MS}}}{F}\right|\cdot 100,\hskip 28.45274pt\delta F^{c}\equiv\left|\frac{F-F^{c}}{F}\right|\cdot 100\,,\end{split}

(98)

where the
c
and
\overline{\rm MS}
superscripts indicate if it is the
\overline{\rm MS}
or the cutoff definitions of the collinear pdfs that are used in the calculation.
While
k_{c}\approx\mu
is the natural choice in the latter case, the size of
\delta F^{c}
depends on the exact value of the cutoff. Interesting scenarios are those where one can find a
k_{c}
such that
\delta F^{c}<\delta F^{\overline{\rm MS}}
for at least some range of kinematics. Whether this is possible depends on the details of the long-range dynamics of the specific theory like the size of the intrinsic mass scales. However, it is interesting to investigate how difficult it is to construct examples. One example in the Yukawa theory is shown in Fig.
9
. The figure compares the result of using two definitions of the collinear pdf by considering the disagreement with the unfactorized
F_{1}
at a hard scale
Q=2.5
GeV. The dotted yellow curve is with the standard
\overline{\rm MS}
collinear pdf while the dotted green curve is with the cutoff definition from Eq. (
36
).
Note that the largest mass is the “spectator mass”
m_{s}=2
GeV and it has been modified from the previous examples to introduce larger
\sim m/Q
errors at large
x_{\rm bj}
. Along with the unfactorized and factorized versions of
F_{1}
, we plot the percent error defined as

\%\,\text{err}\equiv\left|\frac{F-F^{r}}{F}\right|\cdot 100,

(99)

where in the case of those examples it is
F=F_{1}
and the subscript
r
is a placeholder for the choices of the UV scheme. In this particular example, it appears that the use of a cutoff scheme reduces the error relative to the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme in the large
x_{\rm bj}
region. To relate the cutoff
k_{c}
to external kinematics, we try the following form,

k_{c}(x_{\rm bj},Q)=\frac{Q}{Q_{0}}\left(a+bx_{\rm bj}+ce^{(x-x_{\text{max}})^{2}/d^{2}}\right)\,,

(100)

and tune the parameters
a
,
b

c

F_{1}
at
Q\approx Q_{0}
. The plot in Fig.
9

Q_{0}=2.5

\tilde{f}^{\text{OPE}}_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{*};\mu_{b_{*}})
is no longer an accurate approximation to
\tilde{f}_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{*};\mu_{b_{*}})
. The quality of such a fit is just an artifact of the choice of

and

, and not a successful application of the OPE (see also, for instance, discussions after Fig. 5 in Ref.

It is preferable, therefore, to keep
b_{\text{max}}
small and instead refine the parametrization of
g_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}})
in a way that ensures that it interpolates smoothly into the small-
b_{\text{T}}
region.

In a literal parton model where the pdfs are exactly number densities, the TMD and collinear pdfs are related through the integral,

\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits f_{q/p}(\xi,k_{\text{T}})=f_{q/p}(\xi)\,.

(122)

As exact relations, integrals like these fail in theories that require renormalization like QCD and the Yukawa toy theory because the integral over
k^{2}_{\text{T}}
is UV divergent. After the integral is regulated, Eq. (
122
) only holds in general in an approximate sense. We saw an example of this with the cutoff definition of the collinear pdf in Eq. (
35
); the integral of the TMD pdf over
k^{2}_{\text{T}}
up to a cutoff
k_{c}^{2}
only equaled the standard
\overline{\rm MS}
pdf up to subleading power corrections.

It is straightforward to recover the approximate collinear-TMD pdf correspondence of Eq. (
122
) in the coordinate space treatment of the TMD pdf and the
\tilde{f}^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};Q)
approximation, but by regulating the small-
b_{\text{T}}
rather than the large
k_{\text{T}}
behavior.
First, one writes the Fourier transform of
\tilde{f}^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};Q)
back to transverse momentum space,

f^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};Q)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}e^{i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}\tilde{f}^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};Q)\,.

(123)

The divergence that comes from integrating over all transverse momentum now appears as the divergence at
b_{\text{T}}=0
in the integrand of Eq. (
123
). To regulate it, one may replace
b_{\text{T}}
inside
\tilde{f}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};Q)
with a function that levels off at a constant lower bound
b_{\text{min}}
as
b_{\text{T}}\to 0
. An example is
Collins et al. 2016
,

b_{c}(b_{\text{T}})=\sqrt{b^{2}_{\text{T}}+b_{\text{min}}^{2}}\,,

(124)

but any well-behaved interpolating function is acceptable.
Replacing
\tilde{f}^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};Q)
in Eq. (
123
) by
\tilde{f}^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x,b_{c}(b_{\text{T}});Q)
and integrating over all transverse momentum gives

\displaystyle\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}f^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};Q)

\displaystyle{}\to\tilde{f}^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},b_{\text{min}};Q)

\displaystyle=f_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj};b_{0}/b_{*}(b_{\text{min}}))

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-2\int_{b_{0}/b_{*}(b_{\text{min}})}^{Q}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\mu}\nolimits{}}{\mu}\gamma_{2}(a_{\lambda}(\mu))-g_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},b_{\text{min}})\right\}\,

\displaystyle=f_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj};b_{0}/b_{\text{min}})

\displaystyle+\order{\frac{\bmin^2}{\bmax^2},m^2 \bmax^2,m^2 \bmin^2,a_\lambda^2}\,.

(125)

When
m\ll 1/b_{\text{max}}\ll Q
, and
b_{\text{min}}\approx b_{0}/Q
, the error terms on the last line are negligible, and the result is the expected

\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}f^{\text{Evol}}_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};Q)\approx f_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj};Q)\,.

(126)

One possibly misleading aspect of with this way of reconstructing the integral relation in Eq. (
122
) is that it might seem to suggest that there is no role for
g_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{min}})
after the transverse momentum integration.
However, recall that
Q\approx Q_{0}
is, by definition, an acceptably large
Q
to use with factorization. But when
Q\approx Q_{0}
, we have both
b_{\text{max}}\approx 1/Q_{0}
and
b_{\text{min}}\approx b_{0}/Q_{0}
. Therefore, near
Q\approx Q_{0}
, the ratio
b_{\text{min}}/b_{\text{max}}
does not give a power suppression, and dropping the errors in the last line of Eq. (
125
) is unjustified. We can see this problem directly in the Yukawa theory calculation. For example, factorization at
x_{\rm bj}\approx 0.1
describes the unfactorized cross section very well even for
Q\approx 2.0
GeV. However, with
b_{\text{max}}=0.25
GeV and
b_{\text{min}}=b_{0}/(2~\text{GeV})
,

\frac{f_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj}=.1;b_{0}/b_{*}(b_{\text{min}}))}{f_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj}=.1;b_{0}/b_{\text{min}})}\approx 1.4\,.

(127)

While it is true that the approximate integral relation in Eq. (
122
) does hold very well even for these scales, it requires that one keep an accurate
g_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{min}})
in the exponent on the second line of Eq. (
125
),

\frac{f_{q/p}^{\text{Evol}}(x_{\rm bj}=.1;b_{\text{min}};2~\text{GeV})}{f_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj}=.1;b_{0}/b_{\text{min}})}\approx 1.0\,.

(128)

In these calculations, we have used
m_{q}=0.3
GeV, and
m_{p}=m_{s}=1.0
GeV.
Dropping the
g_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{min}})
is only acceptable in the limit where
Q\gg Q_{0}
. In other words, near the input scale where hadronic structure effects are most likely to be relevant, and where it is desirable to preserve the parton structure interpretation embodied by Eq. (
122
) as closely as possible, the
g
-functions cannot be neglected.

IX
Discussion

We have shown that full calculations of DIS cross sections in QFTs that are simpler than QCD, but which nonetheless require renormalization, are useful for highlighting general but subtle properties of parton densities and for examining the limits of factorization while side stepping issues like confinement, large coupling, gauge invariance, and other complicating features of QCD. We have illustrated several examples in this paper, including the demonstration in Sec.
VI
of how the large-
q_{\text{T}}
“
Y
-term” contribution to SIDIS is necessary to maintain a reasonably accurate description after the inclusive integral over all
q_{\text{T}}
, and the importance of the
g_{q/p}(x_{\rm bj},\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}})
function in Eq. (
110
) for maintaining the usual parton model relationship, Eq. (
122
), between the collinear and TMD pdfs near the input scale
Q\approx Q_{0}
. (Compare Eq. (
127
) and Eq. (
128
).) Our discussion of the GPM following Eq. (
88
) emphasizes the importance of taking into account the UV divergences in TMD functions when they are integrated over transverse momentum, especially in applications that require higher precision than a leading power parton model can provide. See
Rogers 2020
for more on this.

The steps for factorizing the cross section, reviewed in Secs.
IV
–
V
, exemplify the difference between what in
Collins et al. 2022
are called “track-A” and “track-B” approaches. The steps in this paper are in the track-A approach; they begin with the bare pdf in Eq. (
19
), and no actual collinear divergences ever arise.

Of course, a detailed description of the transition between “perturbative” (
m
-independent) and “nonperturbative” (
m
-sensitive) behavior depends entirely on the nature of intrinsic mass scales like
m_{q}
,
m_{s}
and
m_{p}
.

Although many basic features of factorization are present in both QCD and in the Yukawa toy theory, there are, of course, major differences between the two theories, and attempts to draw any conclusions about one from the other should be made only with extreme caution. In the Yukawa theory, the target “hadron” state we have considered is not a bound state, all interactions are point-like, and there is no confinement or asymptotic freedom. In some sense, the Yukawa theory is a version of the scalar diquark model that is sometimes used in phenomenology (e.g.
Bacchetta et al. 2008
;
Kang et al. 2010
;
Guerrero and Accardi 2020
) in the limit that the quark-hadron coupling is point-like. One particularly noticeable difference comes from absence of soft gluons and lightcone divergences in the Yukawa theory as compared to the gauge theories. These soft gluons are responsible for, among other things, the well-known nonperturbative Collins-Soper evolution factor
e^{-g_{K}\ln(Q/Q_0)}
that is present in QCD but not in Eq. (
115
). (See, e.g., Eq. (114) of
Gonzalez-Hernandez
et al. 2022
.) They also lead to the TMD pdf turning negative at large transverse momentum. See, for example, Fig. 5 of
Gonzalez-Hernandez
et al. 2022
. Contrast this with the plots in Fig.
13
for the Yukawa theory, which are strictly positive.
Indeed, in the Yukawa theory, the transition to the small transverse momentum asymptote happen rather quickly as
k_{\text{T}}
decreases below
Q
– see Fig.
6
, especially for smaller
x_{\rm bj}
. In QCD, one generally must consider very small
k_{\text{T}}
before the asymptotic limit is reached. See, for example,
Boglione et al. 2015
.

Despite these cautionary remarks, we nevertheless hope that calculations like these will be useful for stress testing other general assertions about factorization and the properties of pdfs in the future. To this end, we have made a convenient Wolfram Mathematica package for generating the cross sections and pdfs of this paper available at
yuk
.

A.2
Organization of the calculation

We organize each graphical contribution to the hadronic tensor by expressing it as

W^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{4}k}\nolimits{}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{N^{\mu\nu}}{D}(2\pi)\delta_{+}((P-k)^{2}-m_{s}^{2})(2\pi)\delta_{+}((k+q)^{2}-m_{q}^{2})\,.

(155)

where

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{\text{INT}}

\displaystyle{}\equiv\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{+}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{-}}\nolimits{}\frac{N^{\mu\nu}}{D}\delta_{+}((P-k)^{2}-m_{s}^{2})\delta_{+}((k+q)^{2}-m_{q}^{2})\,.

\displaystyle\langle N\rangle W^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{B}\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{\rm N}}\nolimits{}}{4z_{\rm N}}W_{\text{SIDIS}}^{\mu\nu}\,.

In all the graphs of Fig.
2

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle{}=\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{\rm N}}\nolimits{}}{4z_{\rm N}}W_{\text{SIDIS}}^{\mu\nu}\,.

(158)

so

\displaystyle\frac{W^{\mu\nu}_{\text{INT}}}{32\pi^{3}}=\int\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz_{\rm N}}\nolimits{}}{4z_{\rm N}}W_{\text{SIDIS}}^{\mu\nu}\,.

Since

A.3
Structure functions

For dealing with specific structure functions, we first write another abbreviation,

(168)

And

(169)

So a natural next step is to determine

\displaystyle{}\Gamma^{+}_{-1}=\left.\int_{0}^{k_{\rm m}^{2}}\;\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muk^{2}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}_{\text{T}}}{k_{\rm m}^{2}}}}\frac{1}{(k^{2}-m_{q}^{2})}\right|_{\theta<\pi/2}=\frac{2k_{\rm m}^{2}}{BC}\ln\left(\frac{B-A}{B(C+1)-A}\right)

Appendix B
The small-
b_{\text{T}}
operator product expansion

In this appendix, we extract the small-
b_{\text{T}}
limit of the TMD pdf (or ff) in collinear factorization. We start with the quark pdf in Eq. (
68
) and consider the limit where
b_{\text{T}}\approx 1/\mu
with
\mu/m\to\infty
,
In the region of the integrand where
k_{\text{T}}\approx m
, we can expand it as,

e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}f_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)=f_{i/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)+\order{\Tscsq{k}{} \Tscsq{b}{} }=f_{i/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)+\order{\frac{\Tscsq{k}{}}{\mu^2} }\,.

(204)

Thus, we define the small-
k_{\text{T}}
part of the approximation to Eq. (
68
) as

\tilde{f}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}\approx 1/\mu;\mu)\approx\int_{\text{UVR}}\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}f_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)\equiv f_{q/p}(x;\mu)\,.

(205)

Dropping the
e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}
factor causes the integral over
k_{\text{T}}
to become UV divergent, so we must introduce a UV regulator and/or UV renormalization scheme to have a well-defined
f(x;\mu)
. We notate this with the “
UVR
,” for “UV regulator,” on the integral sign. The specific choice of UV regulator is arbitrary. Equation (
205
) is a poor approximation because of the UV divergence and the
\order{\Tscsq{k}{}/\mu^2}
correction in Eq. (
204
), which is unsuppressed when
k_{\text{T}}\approx\mu
. However, it can be corrected by using collinear perturbation theory for large enough
\mu
. To implement this, we rewrite Eq. (
68
) in the small
b_{\text{T}}
region as

\tilde{f}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}\approx 1/\mu;\mu)=f_{q/p}(x;\mu)+\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}\left\{e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}f_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)-\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}})f_{q/p}(x;\mu)\right\}\,.

(206)

The integrand of the second term in Eq. (
206
) is the contribution from the
\order{\Tscsq{k}{}/\mu^2}\sim\order{\Tscsq{k}{}\Tscsq{b}{}}
error in Eq. (
204
) if we transform into
b_{\text{T}}
-space and use the definition in Eq. (
205
). The full integral in Eq. (
206
) is both IR and collinear finite, so there is no need for a
UVR
in the integral. By construction, its only unsuppressed contribution comes form the region where
k_{\text{T}}
is comparable to
\mu
. However, the
k_{\text{T}}\approx\mu
behavior of
f_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)
is expressible in collinear factorization,

k_{\text{T}}f_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\approx\mu;\mu)=\sum_{i}k_{\text{T}}\frac{1}{k^{2}_{\text{T}}}\left[\mathcal{C}_{q/i}\otimes f_{i/p}\right](x,k_{\text{T}}/\mu)+\order{\frac{m^2}{\Tscsq{k}{}}}\,.

(207)

See Eq. (
83
) for the Yukawa theory version of this.
The hard coefficient
\mathcal{C}_{q/i}
can be expanded in collinear perturbation theory. It is dimensionless, but it depends on logarithms of
k_{\text{T}}/\mu
.
The
\order{m^2/\Tscsq{k}{}}
in Eq. (
207
) combines with the
\order{\Tscsq{k}{}\Tscsq{b}{}}
error from Eq. (
204
) to give an overall error that is suppressed by
\order{m^2 \Tscsq{b}{}}
.
Up to
\order{m^2 \Tscsq{b}{}}
terms, therefore, the right side of Eq. (
206
) only involves collinear pdfs. This is the small-
b_{\text{T}}
OPE for the unpolarized case. Notice that the unsuppressed term in Eq. (
206
) is not completely unique because it depends upon the scheme used in Eq. (
205
) for treating the UV region.

The full statement of the transverse coordinate space OPE for the quark TMD pdf is

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{q/p}(x,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)=\sum_{j}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu\xi}\nolimits{}}{\xi}\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{q/j}(x/\xi,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)\tilde{f}_{j/p}(\xi;\mu)+\order{m^2 \Tscsq{b}{}}{}\,,

(208)

Reading off the first term from Eq. (
206
) gives the zeroth order hard coefficient

\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{q/j}^{(0)}=\delta(1-x/\xi)\delta_{qj}\,.

(209)

The higher order
\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{q/j}^{(n)}
with
n\geq 1
are calculable from the second term in Eq. (
206
). The steps are to: i.) calculate the integral, ii.) extract the small-
b_{\text{T}}
limit, iii.) drop any
b_{\text{T}}
-suppressed corrections, and iv.) identify the
\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{q/j}^{(n)}
coefficients. The coefficients are independent of the target, so one generally uses a massless parton target to calculate. Thus, one calculates

\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}\left\{e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}f^{\text{partonic}}_{q/i}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)-\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}})f^{\text{partonic}}_{q/i}(x;\mu)\right\}\,,

(210)

where
i
is a massless parton. With a massless parton target,
f^{\text{partonic}}_{q/i}(x,\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}};\mu)
can, in general, involve terms proportional to
\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}})
.

In the Yukawa theory example, it is easiest to do the calculation of
\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{q/j}^{(1)}
by dealing with each term in the integral in Eq. (
210
) separately. We will also make the replacement
k^{2}_{\text{T}}\to k^{2}_{\text{T}}+m^{2}
in propagator denominators to regulate any collinear divergences at intermediate steps. Then we may combine the two terms in Eq. (
210
) and set
m\to 0
. The second term in Eq. (
210
) is just the negative of the collinear pdf (see Eq. (
27
)), and with
\overline{\rm MS}
renormalization it is

-\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{\pi}(2\pi\mu)^{2\epsilon}(1-\xi)\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2-2\epsilon}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}\frac{k^{2}_{\text{T}}}{\big[k^{2}_{\text{T}}+m^{2}\big]^{2}}-\overline{{\rm MS}}\;\;\text{C.T.}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon\to 0}}{{=}}-a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-\xi)\left[-1+\ln\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(211)

We may calculate the first term in Eq. (
210
) in four dimensions since it is finite. It is

\displaystyle\frac{a_{\lambda}(\mu)}{\pi}(1-\xi)\int\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999mu^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}}\nolimits{}e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}}}\frac{k^{2}_{\text{T}}}{\big[k^{2}_{\text{T}}+m^{2}\big]^{2}}

\displaystyle{}=2a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-\xi)\left[K_{0}\left(b_{\text{T}}m\right)-\frac{b_{\text{T}}m}{2}K_{1}\left(b_{\text{T}}m\right)\right]\,

\displaystyle{}=a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-\xi)\left[-\ln\left(\frac{b^{2}_{\text{T}}m^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\right)-1\right]+\order{m^2 \Tscsq{b}{}}\,.

(212)

Adding Eq. (
212
) and Eq. (
211
) and setting
m=0
gives for Eq. (
210
)

\displaystyle-a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-\xi)\ln\left(\frac{b^{2}_{\text{T}}\mu^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{j}\int_{\xi}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits{}}{z}\left[-a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-z)\ln\left(\frac{b^{2}_{\text{T}}\mu^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\right)\delta_{jp}\right]\delta(1-\xi/z)\delta_{pp}

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{j\in p}\int_{\xi}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}\mskip-0.59999muz}\nolimits{}}{z}\mathcal{\tilde{C}}^{(1)}_{q/j}(z,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)\tilde{f}^{(0)}_{j/p}(\xi/z;\mu)\,.

(213)

So we read off

\mathcal{\tilde{C}}^{(1)}_{q/j}(z,\boldsymbol{b}_{\text{T}};\mu)=-a_{\lambda}(\mu)(1-z)\ln\left(\frac{b^{2}_{\text{T}}\mu^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}{4}\right)\delta_{jp}\,,

(214)

which corresponds to Eq. (
104
).

The manipulations above work equally well if, instead of the
k^{2}_{\text{T}}\to k^{2}_{\text{T}}+m^{2}
replacement in Eq. (
211
), we used dimensional regularization to handle the collinear divergences, as is more standard in QCD. But the use of
m^{2}
makes the separate rolls of UV and collinear behavior very transparent. The full result for the OPE contains, in addition to the term in Eq. (
214
), another term for
j=s
, but we do not write it here since it does enter explicitly in the calculations in the main body of the text.

Full description of Benjamin-Feir instability for generalized Korteweg-de Vries equations
Alberto Maspero, Antonio Milosh Radakovic

Abstract
In this paper we consider a family of generalized Korteweg-de Vries equations and study the linear modulational instability of small amplitude traveling waves solutions. Under explicit non-degeneracy conditions on the dispersion relation, we completely describe the spectrum near the origin of the linearized operator at such solutions and prove that the unstable spectrum (when present) is composed by branches depicting always a closed figure “8”. We apply our abstract theorem to several equations such as the Whitham, the gravity-capillary Whitham and the Kawahara equations, confirming that the unstable spectrum of the corresponding linearized operators exhibits a figure “8” instability, as it was observed before only numerically. Our method of proof uses a symplectic version of Kato’s theory of similarity transformation to reduce the problem to determine the eigenvalues of a 3 × 3 3\times 3 complex Hamiltonian and reversible matrix. Then, via a block-diagonalization procedure, we conjugate such matrix into a block-diagonal one composed by a 2 × 2 2\times 2 Hamiltonian and reversible matrix, describing the unstable spectrum, and a single purely imaginary element describing the stable eigenvalue.

Introduction

The generalized Korteweg-de Vries (gKdV) equations are a family of dispersive partial differential equations of the form

\partial_{t}u+{\mathcal{M}}(D)u_{x}+(u^{2})_{x}=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}\ ,

The family of equations (

the Korteweg-de-Vries, the Benjamin-Ono, the Whitham, the Kawahara equations.

[

,

In view of this, the block-diagonalization procedure performed in Section 5 conjugates the original

A final difference is that we require only a minimum amount of

where the dependence on

A consequence is that we need extra care in dealing with the size of the remainders in the expansions of the matrix elements in Proposition

6.1
Whitham’s type equations

We first focus on Whitham’s type equations. The Whitham equation is a famous nonlinear dispersive partial differential equation, modelling unidirectional, two-dimensional water waves at the surface of an irrotational, inviscid fluid. It is characterized by the same dispersion relation as the original water waves equation, and can be adapted to include effects such as surface tension or a constant vorticity, by modifying the dispersion relation. In particular, it has the form (
1.1
), where the operator
{\mathcal{M}}(D)

has symbol

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathtt{h}}(\xi)=\sqrt{\frac{\tanh({\mathtt{h}}\xi)}{\xi}}\hskip 70.0001pt\mbox{(classical Whitham)}

(6.1)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathtt{h}},\kappa}(\xi)=\sqrt{(1+\kappa\xi^{2})\frac{\tanh({\mathtt{h}}\xi)}{\xi}}\qquad\mbox{(gravity-capillary Whitham)}

(6.2)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathtt{h}},\gamma}(\xi)=\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{\tanh({\mathtt{h}}\xi)}{\xi}+\sqrt{\frac{\tanh({\mathtt{h}}\xi)}{\xi}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\frac{\tanh^{2}({\mathtt{h}}\xi)}{\xi^{2}}}\qquad\mbox{(vorticity-modified Whitham)}

(6.3)

where
{\mathtt{h}}>0
is a parameter representing the depth of the fluid,
\kappa>0
the surface tension coefficients and
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
a constant vorticity.

Modulational instability for the Whitham equations in (
6.1
)–(
6.3
) has been studied in
[
15
,
16
]
, where it has been proved that, for certain values of the parameters

, the linearized Whitham equations at a small amplitude traveling wave solutions have unstable eigenvalues near the origin.

1.2
we now show that the spectrum of the linearized operator actually depicts a closed ”figure 8”.

\displaystyle=\sigma\frac{1}{k({\mathfrak{m}}(1)-{\mathfrak{m}}(k))}f_{k}^{-\sigma}-\frac{\lambda}{k^{2}({\mathfrak{m}}(1)-{\mathfrak{m}}(k))^{2}}f_{k}^{\sigma}+\lambda^{2}\varphi_{k}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)\,\ ,

Proof.

(ii)

{\mathcal{L}}_{0,0}

{\mathtt{L}}_{0,0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&k({\mathfrak{m}}(1)-{\mathfrak{m}}(k))\\
-k({\mathfrak{m}}(1)-{\mathfrak{m}}(k))&0\end{pmatrix}\ .

(A.13)

\det{\mathtt{L}}_{0,0}=k^{2}({\mathfrak{m}}(1)-{\mathfrak{m}}(k))^{2}\neq 0

{\mathtt{L}}_{0,0}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{-1}{k({\mathfrak{m}}(1)-{\mathfrak{m}}(k))}\\
\frac{1}{k({\mathfrak{m}}(1)-{\mathfrak{m}}(k))}&0\end{pmatrix}\ .

(A.14)

(A.15)

∎

Appendix B
Technical lemmas

Lemma B.1
.

Let
f:\overline{B(\mu_{0})}\times\overline{B({\epsilon}_{0})}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a
{\mathcal{C}}^{n}
function,
n\geq 2
, where by
\overline{B(r)}
we denote the closed ball in
{\mathbb{R}}
of center
0
and radius
r
.

(i)

Assume
f(\mu,0)=0
for any
(\mu,{\epsilon})\in B(\mu_{0})\times B({\epsilon}_{0})
. Then
f(\mu,{\epsilon})={\epsilon}\,g(\mu,{\epsilon})
for some
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{n-1}
.

(ii)

Assume
|f(\mu,{\epsilon})|\leq C|\mu|^{a_{0}}|{\epsilon}|^{b_{0}}\sum_{i}\mu^{a_{i}}\,{\epsilon}^{b_{i}}
on
B(\mu_{0})\times B({\epsilon}_{0})
for some constant
C>0
and natural numbers
1\leq a_{0}+b_{0}<n
. Then
f(\mu,{\epsilon})=\mu^{a_{0}}{\epsilon}^{b_{0}}\,g(\mu,{\epsilon})
for some
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{n-a_{0}-b_{0}}
fulfilling

|g(\mu,{\epsilon})|\leq C\sum_{i}\mu^{a_{i}}\,{\epsilon}^{b_{i}}
.

(iii)

Assume
f(0,0)=0
, and
n\geq 1
. Then
\exists C>0
such that
f(\mu,{\epsilon})\leq C(|\mu|+|{\epsilon}|)
for every
(\mu,{\epsilon})\in\overline{B(\mu_{0})}\times\overline{B({\epsilon}_{0})}
.

Proof.

(i)
Put
h(\tau):=f(\mu,\tau{\epsilon})
. Then

f(\mu,{\epsilon})=\underbrace{f(\mu,0)}_{=0}+{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\tau}h(\tau){\rm d}\tau=g(\mu,{\epsilon})\ ,\qquad g(\mu,{\epsilon})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{n-1}

proving the first item.

(ii)
At least one among
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
is not zero. Assume it is
b_{0}
. First notice that, if
|f(\mu,{\epsilon})|\leq C|\mu|^{a_{0}}|{\epsilon}|^{b_{0}}\sum_{i}\mu^{a_{i}}\,{\epsilon}^{b_{i}}
, then
f(\mu,0)=0
. Using
i)
, we have that
f(\mu,{\epsilon})={\epsilon}h_{1}(\mu,{\epsilon})
, with
h_{1}(\mu,{\epsilon})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{n-1}
satisfying
|h_{1}(\mu,{\epsilon})|\leq C|\mu|^{a_{0}}|{\epsilon}|^{b_{0}-1}\sum_{i}\mu^{a_{i}}\,{\epsilon}^{b_{i}}
.

We can then iterate the procedure
b_{0}-1
times, obtaining a sequence of functions
h_{k}(\mu,{\epsilon})
,
k=2,\ldots,b_{0}
, fulfilling

h_{k-1}(\mu,{\epsilon})={\epsilon}h_{k}(\mu,{\epsilon})\ ,\qquad h_{k}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{n-k}\ ,\qquad|h_{k}(\mu,{\epsilon})|\leq C|\mu|^{a_{0}}|{\epsilon}|^{b_{0}-k}\sum_{i}\mu^{a_{i}}\,{\epsilon}^{b_{i}}\ .

In particular
f(\mu,{\epsilon})={\epsilon}^{b_{0}}h_{b_{0}}(\mu,{\epsilon})
. Repeating the same procedure in the variable
\mu
allows us to gather a factor
\mu^{a_{0}}
from
h_{b_{0}}(\mu,{\epsilon})
, proving the result.

(iii)
It is a standard application of the mean value theorem.
∎

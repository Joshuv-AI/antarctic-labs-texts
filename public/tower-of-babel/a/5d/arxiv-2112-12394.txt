So-Yeon Lee, Young-Tak Oh

Abstract
. Let k k and m m be positive integers and λ / μ \lambda/\mu a skew partition. We compute the principal specialization of the skew Schur polynomials s λ / μ ​ ( x 1 , … , x k ) s_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}) modulo q m − 1 q^{m}-1 under suitable conditions. We interpret the results thus obtained from the viewpoint of the cyclic sieving phenomenon on semistandard Young skew tableaux of shape λ / μ \lambda/\mu . As an application, we deal with evaluations of the principal specialization of the skew Schur polynomials at roots of unity.

1.
Introduction

The cyclic sieving phenomenon was introduced by Reiner, Stanton, and White in
[
16
]
as a generalization of Stembridge’s
q=-1
phenomenon
[
19
,
20
]
.
Let
X
be a finite set on which a finite cyclic group
C
acts and let
X(q)
be a polynomial in
q
with nonnegative integer coefficients.
For
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
\omega_{d}
be a
d
th primitive root of unity.
We say that the triple
(X,C,X(q))
exhibits the
cyclic sieving phenomenon
if

\#X^{c}=X(\omega_{{\rm o}(c)})
for all
c\in C
,
where
X^{c}
is the set of fixed points under the action of
c

and
{\rm o}(c)
is the order of
c
.

We identify a partition with the corresponding Young diagram.
Given a Young diagram
\lambda
, let
\mathsf{SSYT}_{m}(\lambda)
be the set of semistandard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
with entries in
\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
In
[
17
]
, Rhoades proved representation-theoretically that if
\lambda
is of rectangular shape, the triple

\left(\mathsf{SSYT}_{m}(\lambda),~\langle{\mathsf{pr}}\rangle,~q^{-\kappa(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}(1,q,\ldots,q^{m-1})\right)

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon, where
\mathsf{pr}
is
jeu-de-taquin
promotion,

\kappa(\lambda)=\sum_{i\geq 1}(i-1)\lambda_{i}
,
and
s_{\lambda}(1,q,\ldots,q^{m-1})
is the principal specialization of the
Schur polynomial

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
.
Since then, there have been several studies on the following issues:

(1)

When
\lambda
is not of rectangular shape, what conditions guarantee the existence
of an action of a finite cyclic group
C
on
\mathsf{SSYT}_{m}(\lambda)

such that the triple

\left(\mathsf{SSYT}_{m}(\lambda),~C,~q^{-\kappa(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}(1,q,\ldots,q^{m-1})\right)

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon?

(2)

In (1), can we describe the cyclic group action explicitly?

For instance, see
[
4
,
6
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
In particular, it was shown in
[
13
]
that for any Young diagram that satisfies the condition
\gcd(|\lambda|,m)=1
,
the triple

\displaystyle\left(\mathsf{SSYT}_{m}(\lambda),~\langle{\mathbf{\mathtt{c}}}\rangle,~q^{-\kappa(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}(1,q,\ldots,q^{m-1})\right)

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.
Here the action
\mathbf{\mathtt{c}}
arises naturally from the
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
-
crystal
structure of
\mathsf{SSYT}_{m}(\lambda)
.

In this paper, we consider cyclic sieving phenomena associated with semistandard Young skew tableaux.
Unlike tableaux of normal shape, little is known about cyclic sieving phenomena that occur on semistandard Young skew tableaux.
It has been conjectured by Alexandersson and Amini
[
2
, Conjecture 3.4]
that for any
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
there exists an action of a cyclic group
C_{m}
of order
m
on
\mathsf{SSYT}_{k}(\lambda)
such that
the triple

(\mathsf{SSYT}_{k}(m\lambda),~C_{m},~s_{m\lambda}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1}))

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.
Here
m\lambda
denotes the stretched Young diagram of
\lambda
by
m
.
This conjecture turns out to be true in
[
14
]
,
where a crystal-theoretical generalization of this phenomenon is also provided.
Recently, a skew version of this conjecture has been proposed by Alexandersson-Pfannerer-Rubey-Uhlin
[
4
, Conjecture 50]
, which states that
there is an action of a cyclic group
C_{m}
of order
m
on the set
\mathsf{SSYT}_{k}(m\lambda/m\mu)
such that the triple

(\mathsf{SSYT}_{k}(m\lambda/m\mu),~C_{m},~s_{m\lambda/m\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1}))

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.

The present paper concerns the conjecture given in
[
4
, Conjecture 50]
.
We begin by computing the principal specialization of

s_{m\lambda/m\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})

modulo
q^{m}-1
.
For instance,
in case where
\lambda=(3,3,2,1),\mu=(2,1),m=9
, and
k=4
,
we observe that

\displaystyle s_{9\lambda/9\mu}(1,q,q^{2},q^{3})\equiv 54665112\frac{q^{9}-1}{q-1}-3\frac{q^{9}-1}{q^{3}-1}+1\pmod{q^{9}-1}.

This congruence tells us that the conjecture is not necessarily true.
However, under the condition that
k
is divisible by
m
, we prove that the conjecture is true.
To be precise, our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers
and
\lambda/\mu
a skew partition.
If
\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}
is divisible by
m
for all
i\geq 1
, then
there exists an action of a cyclic group
C_{m}
of order
m
such that the triple

(\mathsf{SSYT}_{km}(\lambda/\mu),~C_{m},~s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{km-1}))

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.

We also prove that the conjecture is true whenever
\lambda/\mu
is a border strip, which is our second main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers
and
\lambda/\mu
a border strip.
If
\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}
is divisible by
m
for all
i\geq 1
, then
there exists an action of a cyclic group
C_{m}
of order
m
such that the triple

(\mathsf{SSYT}_{k}(\lambda/\mu),~C_{m},~s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1}))

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.

As an application, we deal with evaluations of the principal specialization of the skew Schur polynomials at roots of unity.
For each
d\,|\,k
, Reiner, Stanton, and White
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
provide a formula for
s_{\lambda}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1})
when
q
is specialized to a
d
th primitive root of unity, which involves
the notion of
d
-cores
and
d
-quotients
of
\lambda
.
Using a result obtained in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
we successfully generalize this formula to skew shapes
(see Theorem
5.4
).
In particular, the case where
d=k
is investigated in detail.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
,
we collect the materials required to develop our arguments.
In Section
3
and Section
4
, the proofs of Theorem
1.1

and Theorem
1.2
are provided, respectively.
In Section
5
,
a skew version of
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
is provided.
The final section is devoted to a few remarks for readers’ understanding.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Partitions, skew partitions, and border strip tableaux

A
partition
is any sequence

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)

of nonnegative integers in decreasing order
and containing only finitely many nonzero terms.
The
length

\ell(\lambda)
of
\lambda
is defined to be the
number of positive parts of
\lambda
.
Unless otherwise specified, we usually omit the zero parts of
\lambda
and simply write as

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)})
.
Given any positive integer
m
, we set
m\lambda
to be the stretched partition
(m\lambda_{1},m\lambda_{2},\ldots,m\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)})
.
A
skew partition
is a pair of partitions
(\lambda,\mu)
such that the Young diagram of
\lambda
contains the Young diagram of
\mu
; it is denoted by
\lambda/\mu
. If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots)
, then the containment of diagrams means that
\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}
for all
i
.

A
border strip
is a connected skew shape with no
2\times 2
square. Define the
height

{\rm ht(B)}
of a border strip
{\rm B}
to be one less than its number of rows.
Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots)
be a weak composition of
n
.
Define a
border-strip tableau
of shape
\lambda/\mu
(where
|\lambda/\mu|=n
) and type
\alpha
to be an assignment of positive integers to the squares of
\lambda/\mu
such that

•

every row and column is weakly increasing,

•

the integer
i
appears
\alpha_{i}
times, and

•

the set of squares occupied by
i
forms a border strip.

Define the
height

{\rm ht(T)}
of a border-strip tableau
T

to be

{\rm ht}(T)={\rm ht(B_{1})}+{\rm ht(B_{2})}+\cdots+{\rm ht(B_{k})},

where
{\rm B_{1}},\ldots,{\rm B_{k}}
are the nonempty border strips appearing in
T
.
For more details, see
[
18
, Section 7.17]
.

2.2.
The abacus model for partitions, cores and quotients

Let us review the abacus model introduced by James and Kerber
[
10
, Ch. 2.7]

to encode partitions and their quotients, some of which will be slightly modified for the simplicity of the arguments.

With
d\geq 1
fixed, we take an abacus with
d
vertical runners,
numbered
0,1,\ldots,d-1
from left to right, and we mark positions
0,1,2,\ldots
on these runners, reading from left to right along successive rows.
Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
be a partition.
For
r\geq\ell
, let
\delta_{r}
be the staircase partition
(r-1,r-2,\ldots,1)

and let
\lambda+\delta_{r}
be the sequence

\left(\lambda_{1}+r-1,\ldots,\lambda_{i}+(r-i),\ldots,\lambda_{r-1}+1,\lambda_{r}\right),

where
\lambda_{i}=0
if
i>\ell
.
Once
r
is chosen, the
d
-abacus display for
\lambda

is obtained by circling the elements of
\lambda+\delta_{r}

from the row containing the position labeled by
0
to the row containing
\lambda_{1}+r-1
.
It should be mentioned that
r>\ell
if and only if the position labeled by
0
is circled.
We call a circled position a
bead

and a uncircled position a
non-bead
for simplicity.
See Figure
2.1
for an illustration.

Let
\lambda_{\emptyset}
be the
d
-core
of
\lambda
, which can be obtained by removing
border strips of size
d
from the diagram of
\lambda
as many as possible.
It is well known that
the abacus display for
\lambda_{\emptyset}
can be obtained from the abacus display for
\lambda

by moving all the beads as far up their runners as they will go
and then removing the rows containing beads only.
For each
0\leq i\leq d-1
, let
\lambda^{(i)}
be the partition whose
j
-part
is the number of non-beads above the
j
th bead from the bottom on runner
i
of the abacus display for
\lambda
.
The sequence
(\lambda^{(0)},\ldots,\lambda^{(d-1)})

is called
the
d
-quotient of
\lambda
.

Next, let us introduce the abacus display for skew partitions.
Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew partition with
\ell(\lambda)=\ell
.
Once
d
and
r\geq\ell
are chosen, the
d
-abacus display for
\lambda/\mu
can be obtained by drawing the
d
-abacus for
\mu
above the
d
-abacus for
\lambda
.
It should be mentioned that we are using the same
r
in obtaining the
d
-abaci for
\lambda
and
\mu
.
In the present paper,
r
will always be set to be
\ell
.
If the Young diagram of
\lambda^{(i)}
contains the Young diagram of
\mu^{(i)}
for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
, the
d
-quotient
of
\lambda/\mu
is defined to be the sequence

(\lambda^{(0)}/\mu^{(0)},\ldots,\lambda^{(d-1)}/\mu^{(d-1)})
.
Otherwise, we say that the
d
-quotient of
\lambda/\mu
does not exist.
For more information on the abacus display for skew partitions, we refer the readers to
[
4
, Section 5]
.

2.3.
Basic results and definitions necessary for proofs

Here we collect the basic results and definitions necessary to develop our arguments.
The first result to introduce is due to Reiner, Stanton, and White.
Let
k
,
m
, and
n
be positive integers.
Suppose that a cyclic group
C_{m}
of order
m
acts
nearly freely
on
\{1,\ldots,k\}
, equivalently it is generated by an element
c\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
whose cycle type is either

(1)

a
cycles of size
m
, so that
k=am
(and
C_{m}
acts freely), or

(2)

a
cycles of size
m
and one singleton cycle, so that
k=am+1

for some positive integer
a
.
Let
X
be an
n
-multiset of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and

X(q)\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}n+k-1\\
n\end{bmatrix}_{q}.

Under this assumption, they prove in
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
that
the triple
(X,C_{m},X(q))
exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.
Furthermore, letting
A_{l}(k,n)
denote the coefficient of
q^{l}
in

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}n+k-1\\
n\end{bmatrix}_{q}\pmod{q^{k}-1}

with the convention that
A_{1}(1,n)=1
,
they show in
[
16
, Corollary 1.3]
that

(2.1)

\displaystyle A_{l}(k,n)=\sum_{d\mid n,k,l}A_{1}\left(\frac{k}{d},\frac{n}{d}\right).

Let
h_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
be the
n
th homogeneous symmetric polynomial in variables

x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
.
It is easy to see that the principal specialization of
h_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
equals
X(q)
,
that is,

h_{n}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1})=\begin{bmatrix}n+k-1\\
n\end{bmatrix}_{q}.

Thus the theorem
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
tells us that
if a cyclic group of order
m
act nearly freely on
\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
then

h_{n}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1})\equiv\sum_{d\mid m}a_{d}\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{\frac{m}{d}}-1}\pmod{q^{m}-1}

for some nonnegative integers
a_{d}
’s.

The second result to introduce is due to Alexandersson and Amini.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Alexandersson and Amini
[
2
, Theorem 2.7]
)

Let
f(q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q]
and suppose
f(\omega_{m}^{j})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for each
j=1,\ldots,m
, where
\omega_{m}
denotes a primitive
m
th root of unity.
Let
X
be any set of size
f(1)
.
Then there exists an action of a cyclic group
C
of order
m
on
X
such that
(X,C,f(q))

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon if and only if for each
d\,|\,m
,

\sum_{j\mid d}\mu(d/j)f(\omega_{m}^{j})\geq 0.

Here,
\mu
is the Möbius function.

In Lemma
2.2
,
suppose that
f(q)
satisfies the condition that

f(q)\equiv\sum_{d\mid m}a_{d}\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{\frac{m}{d}}-1}\pmod{q^{m}-1}

for some integers
a_{d}
’s.
Then it holds that

(2.2)

\sum_{j\mid d}\mu(d/j)f(\omega_{m}^{j})=da_{d}

(for instance, see the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
).
Hence, if
a_{d}\geq 0
for all
d\,|\,n
, then

(X,C,f(q))
exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon
for a unknown cyclic group action and

a_{d}
is the number of
C
-orbits with exactly
d
elements.

Motivated by this observation, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

(a)

We say that
f(q)
is a
pre-CSP polynomial modulo
m
if

\displaystyle f(q)\equiv\sum_{d\mid m}a_{d}\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{\frac{m}{d}}-1}\pmod{q^{m}-1}

for some integers
a_{d}
’s.

(b)

We say that
f(q)
is a
CSP polynomial modulo
m

if it is a pre-CSP polynomial modulo
m
with
a_{d}\geq 0
for all divisors
d
of
m
.

In Definition
2.3
(a), the coefficient
a_{d}
is denoted by

\left[\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{\frac{m}{d}}-1}\right]f(q)

for each divisor
d
of
m
because it is uniquely determined.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We begin by introducing two important lemmas.
Given positive integers
m
and
n
, we denote by
(m,n)
the greatest common divisor of
m
and
n

and
[m,n]
the least common multiple of
m
and
n
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any positive integers
k
,
m
and
n
,

h_{n}(1,q,\ldots,q^{km-1})
is a CSP polynomial modulo
m
.
Furthermore, for each divisor
d
of
m
,

\displaystyle\left[\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{\frac{m}{d}}-1}\right]h_{n}(1,q,\ldots,q^{km-1})=\begin{cases}\left[\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1}\right]h_{nd/m}(1,q,\ldots,q^{kd-1})&\text{if $\frac{m}{d}$ divides $n$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By the theorem
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
, we can see that

h_{n}(1,q,\ldots,q^{km-1})
is a CSP polynomial modulo
m
as well as a CSP polynomial modulo
km
.
Let
e
be any divisor of
km
and set

b_{e}\coloneqq\left[\frac{q^{km}-1}{q^{\frac{km}{e}}-1}\right]h_{n}(1,q,\ldots,q^{km-1}).

In view of (
2.1
), we have that

(3.1)

\displaystyle b_{e}=\begin{cases}A_{1}\left(e,\frac{ne}{km}\right)&\text{if $\frac{km}{e}$ divides $n$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Fix a divisor
d
of
m

and let
\mathcal{A}_{d}
be the set of divisors
e
of
km
satisfying that
(\frac{km}{e},m)=\frac{m}{d}
.
Setting

a_{d}\coloneqq\left[\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{\frac{m}{d}}-1}\right]h_{n}(1,q,\ldots,q^{km-1})

gives rise to the following relation between
a_{d}
and
b_{e}
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle a_{d}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}}\frac{e}{d}b_{e}.

In case where
\frac{m}{d}
divides
n
,
applying (
3.1
) to this equality yields that

\displaystyle a_{d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}\\
\frac{km}{e}\mid n\end{subarray}}\frac{e}{d}b_{e}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}\\
\frac{km}{e}\nmid n\end{subarray}}\frac{e}{d}b_{e}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}\\
\frac{km}{e}\mid n\end{subarray}}\frac{e}{d}b_{e}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}\\
\frac{km}{e}\mid n\end{subarray}}\frac{e}{d}A_{1}\left(e,\frac{ne}{km}\right).

Otherwise, there is no element
e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
with
\frac{km}{e}|n
,
and thus
a_{d}=0
by (
3.1
) together with (
3.2
).

For the second assertion, observe that

\mathcal{A}_{d}
is identical to the set of divisors
e
of
kd
satisfying that
(\frac{kd}{e},d)=1
.
Set

a_{d}^{\prime}\coloneqq\left[\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1}\right]h_{nd/m}(1,q,\ldots,q^{kd-1}).

Then we have

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}\\
\frac{kd}{e}\mid\frac{nd}{m}\end{subarray}}\frac{e}{d}A_{1}\left(e,\frac{ne}{km}\right).

Now our assertion follows from the fact that,
for each
e\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
,
\frac{km}{e}
divides
n
if and only if
\frac{kd}{e}
divides

\frac{nd}{m}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and let
a,b
be divisors of
m
.
Then,

(3.3)

\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{a}-1}\cdot\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{b}-1}\equiv\frac{m}{[a,b]}\cdot\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{(a,b)}-1}\pmod{q^{m}-1}.

Proof.

For simplicity, let
d\coloneqq(a,b)
and
l\coloneqq[a,b]
.
Then we can write
a=da^{\prime}
and
b=db^{\prime}
with
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
.
The left hand side of (
3.3
) equals

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<m/a\\
0\leq j<m/b\end{subarray}}q^{ai+bj}

and the right hand side of (
3.3
) equals

\frac{m}{l}\sum_{0\leq k<m/d}q^{dk}.

Hence the assertion can be proven by showing that, for each
0\leq k<\frac{m}{d}
, the number of solutions to the linear congruence

(3.4)

ai+bj\equiv dk\pmod{m}\quad\text{ with $0\leq i<\frac{m}{a}$ and $0\leq j<\frac{m}{b}$}

is given by
\frac{m}{l}
.
To do this, we first note that
the linear congruence

ai+bj\equiv dk\pmod{m}
is equivalent to
a^{\prime}i+b^{\prime}j\equiv k\pmod{\frac{m}{d}}
.
Then we consider the set

(3.5)

\left\{a^{\prime}i+b^{\prime}j\;:\;0\leq i<\frac{m}{a},\;\;0\leq j<\frac{m}{b}\right\}.

For each
0\leq c<\frac{m}{ab^{\prime}}
, we claim that the subset

\left\{a^{\prime}(i+cb^{\prime})+b^{\prime}j\;:\;0\leq i<b^{\prime},\;\;0\leq j<\frac{m}{b}\right\}

is a complete system of residues modulo
\frac{m}{d}
.
Note that the number of elements in this set is
\frac{m}{d}
.
Suppose that

Lemma 4.1

\lambda_{i}-d+k&\text{if $j=i+k$,}\end{cases}

\tilde{\lambda}+\delta_{l}=(\ldots,\hbox{\hskip 5.74341pt\hskip-3.75pt\hbox{$\underbrace{\lambda_{i+1}+l-(i+1)}$}\hskip-3.75pt\hskip-5.74341pt\raisebox{-13.49002pt}{\hbox{$i^{\rm th}$}}\hskip-5.74341pt\hskip 5.74341pt},\lambda_{i+2}+l-(i+2),\ldots,\lambda_{i+k}+l-(i+k),\hbox{\hskip 18.5038pt\hskip-3.75pt\hbox{$\underbrace{\lambda_{i}+(l-i)-d}$}\hskip-3.75pt\hskip-18.5038pt\raisebox{-13.49002pt}{\hbox{$(i+k)^{\rm th}$}}\hskip-18.5038pt\hskip 18.5038pt},\ldots).

Using this action, one can see that

\mathfrak{S}_{l}

One can see that

Continue this process until we get

The expressions of

we derive that the set of the expressions of
{\bf perm}(\lambda/\mu)
obtained in this way is
one-to-one correspondence with
{\rm BST}(\lambda/\mu,d)

Proposition 5.3
.

The following theorem is a skew version of
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
d\,|\,N
and let
\omega_{d}
be a primitive
d
th root of unity.
Then
s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,\omega_{d},\ldots,\omega_{d}^{N-1})
is zero unless the
d
-quotient of
\lambda/\mu
exists, in which case

s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,\omega_{d},\ldots,\omega_{d}^{N-1})={\rm sgn}(\chi^{\lambda/\mu}((d^{m})))\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}s_{\lambda^{(i)}/\mu^{(i)}}(\hbox{\hskip 5.6238pt\hskip-3.75pt\hbox{$\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}$}\hskip-3.75pt\hskip-5.6238pt\raisebox{-9.8611pt}{\hbox{$\frac{N}{d}$}}\hskip-5.6238pt\hskip 5.6238pt}),

where
|\lambda/\mu|=dm
and the
d
-quotient of
\lambda/\mu
is
(\lambda^{(0)}/\mu^{(0)},\ldots,\lambda^{(d-1)}/\mu^{(d-1)})
.

Proof.

In the case where the
d
-quotient of
\lambda/\mu
does not exist, the proof can be done in the same way as in
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
.
Choosing
X=(1,\omega_{d},\ldots,\omega_{d}^{N-1},0,0,\ldots)

gives
p_{j}(X)=0
unless
d\,|\,j
.
Hence, in view of (
5.1
), one has that

s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,\omega_{d},\ldots,\omega_{d}^{N-1})=\sum_{\nu:d\text{-stretched}}\chi^{\lambda/\mu}(\nu)\frac{p_{\nu}(1,\omega_{d},\ldots,\omega_{d}^{N-1})}{z_{\nu}}.

By Lemma
5.1
, from the condition that the
d
-quotient of
\lambda/\mu
does not exist it follows that
{\rm BST}(\lambda/\mu,d)
is empty.
Thus
{\rm BST}(\lambda/\mu,\nu)
is also empty.
Combining this with the formula

\chi^{\lambda/\mu}(\nu)=\sum_{{\rm B}\in{\rm BST}(\lambda/\mu,\nu)}(-1)^{\rm ht(B)},

we have
\chi^{\lambda/\mu}(\nu)=0
and our assertion follows.

Now suppose that the
d
-quotient of
\lambda/\mu
exists.
Let
l\coloneqq\ell(\lambda)
,
\sigma\coloneqq{\bf perm}(\lambda/\mu)
, and
\mathsf{M}\coloneqq\sigma\cdot\mathsf{M}(\lambda/\mu)
.
Here
\sigma
acts on
\mathsf{M}(\lambda/\mu)
by permuting the rows of
\mathsf{M}(\lambda/\mu)
.
Then

s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{N-1})=\det(\mathsf{M}(\lambda/\mu))={\rm sgn}(\sigma)\det(\mathsf{M})={\rm sgn}(\chi^{\lambda/\mu}((d^{m})))\det(\mathsf{M}).

The last equality follows from Proposition
5.3
.
Note that
\det(\mathsf{M})
is a pre-CSP polynomial modulo
N
.
Write

\det(\mathsf{M})\equiv\sum_{e\mid N}c_{e}\frac{q^{N}-1}{q^{\frac{N}{e}}-1}\pmod{q^{N}-1},

where
c_{e}
’s are integers.

Consider the
d
-abacus display for
\lambda/\mu
and let
(\lambda^{(0)}/\mu^{(0)},\ldots,\lambda^{(d-1)}/\mu^{(d-1)})
be the
d
-quotient of
\lambda/\mu
.
For
0\leq t\leq d-1
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{t}\coloneqq\{1\leq i\leq l:\text{the bead for $\mu_{i}+l-i$ is on runner $t$ of the abacus display}\}.

By the definition of
\sigma
, one can easily see that the bead for
\lambda_{i}+l-i
is on runner
t
if and only if
\sigma(i)\in\mathcal{C}_{t}
.
Therefore, it follows that
\mathsf{M}_{i,j}
is divisible by
d
if and only if
i,j\in\mathcal{C}_{t}
for some
0\leq t\leq d-1
.
Using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, for each divisor
e\,|\,\frac{N}{d}
, one can derive that

(5.2)

c_{e}=\left[\frac{q^{N}-1}{q^{\frac{N}{e}}-1}\right]\prod_{0\leq t\leq d-1}s_{\lambda^{(t)}/\mu^{(t)}}(1,q^{d},\ldots,q^{d(\frac{N}{d}-1)}).

Using (
2.2
), one can derive from (
5.2
) that

\displaystyle\det(\mathsf{M})|_{q=\omega_{d}}=\sum_{e\mid\frac{N}{d}}ec_{e}

\displaystyle=\prod_{0\leq t\leq d-1}s_{\lambda^{(t)}/\mu^{(t)}}(1,q^{d},\ldots,q^{d(\frac{N}{d}-1)})\Bigr|_{q=\omega_{d}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{0\leq t\leq d-1}s_{\lambda^{(t)}/\mu^{(t)}}(\hbox{\hskip 5.6238pt\hskip-3.75pt\hbox{$\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}$}\hskip-3.75pt\hskip-5.6238pt\raisebox{-9.8611pt}{\hbox{$\frac{N}{d}$}}\hskip-5.6238pt\hskip 5.6238pt}).

∎

Remark 5.5
.

(a) Reiner, Stanton, and White proved
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
by using the hook-content formula for
s_{\lambda}(1,q,\ldots,q^{N-1})
, so one is tempting to
prove Theorem
5.4
in a similar manner.
However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, this formula has not yet extended to general skew shapes (see
[
12
, Section 8]
).

(b) A similar result has been provided in
[
11
, Examples I.5.24]

in a slightly different form.

The rest of this section is devoted to the case where
d=N
.
Let
\ell(\lambda)\leq N
.
From
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
it follows that

s_{\lambda}(1,\omega_{N},\ldots,\omega_{N}^{N-1})
is zero unless the
N
-core of
\lambda
is empty, in which case

(5.3)

s_{\lambda}(1,\omega_{N},\ldots,\omega_{N}^{N-1})={\rm sgn}(\chi^{\lambda}((N^{m})))\quad\text{with $|\lambda|=Nm$.}

This evaluation can also be found in
[
11
, Examples I.3.17]
.
Recall that the
Kostka-Foulkes polynomial

K_{\lambda,\nu}(q)
is defined via the expansion

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})=\sum_{\nu}K_{\lambda,\nu}(q)P_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N};q),

where
P_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N};q)
is the Hall-Littlewood polynomial indexed by
\nu
.
And the
skew Kostka-Foulkes polynomial

K_{\lambda/\mu,\nu}(q)
is defined by

(5.4)

K_{\lambda/\mu,\nu}(q)\coloneqq\langle s_{\lambda/\mu}(X),~Q^{\prime}_{\nu}(X;q)\rangle,

where
Q^{\prime}_{\nu}(X;q)=\sum_{\gamma}K_{\gamma,\nu}(q)s_{\gamma}(X)
(see
[
7
]
).

Combining (
5.3
) with
[
7
, Theorem 9.14]
, for any partition
\lambda
with
|\lambda|=Nm
and
\ell(\lambda)\leq N
,
we have

(5.5)

K_{\lambda,m^{N}}(\omega_{N})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{(N-1)m}\,\,{\rm sgn}(\chi^{\lambda}((N^{m})))&\text{ if the $N$-core of $\lambda$ is empty,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We will derive a skew version of this formula.
To begin with, we observe that

Q^{\prime}_{(m^{N})}(X;\omega_{N})=(-1)^{(N-1)m}p_{N}\circ h_{m}(X)=(-1)^{(N-1)m}\sum_{\lambda\vdash Nm}s_{\lambda}(1,\omega_{N},\ldots,\omega_{N}^{N-1})s_{\lambda}(X),

where
p_{N}\circ h_{m}
denotes the plethysm of
h_{m}
by
p_{N}
.
The first equality follows from
[
7
, Theorem 9.2]
and the second equality follows from
[
11
, Examples I.8.7]
.
Plugging this identity to (
5.4
) yields that, for any
\lambda/\mu
with
|\lambda/\mu|=Nm
,

(5.6)

K_{\lambda/\mu,m^{N}}(\omega_{N})=(-1)^{(N-1)m}s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,\omega_{N},\ldots,\omega_{N}^{N-1}).

Define a
horizontal strip
to be a skew shape with no two squares in the same column.
The following corollary is the desired skew version of (
5.5
).

Corollary 5.6
.

(cf.
[
11
, Examples I.5.24]
)

Let
\omega_{N}
be a primitive
N
th root of unity.

(a)

s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,\omega_{N},\ldots,\omega_{N}^{N-1})
is zero unless
\lambda^{(i)}/\mu^{(i)}
is a horizontal strip for all
0\leq i\leq N-1
, in which case

s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,\omega_{N},\ldots,\omega_{N}^{N-1})={\rm sgn}(\chi^{\lambda/\mu}((N^{m})))\quad\text{ with }|\lambda/\mu|=Nm.

(b)

For any
\lambda/\mu
with
|\lambda/\mu|=Nm
,

K_{\lambda/\mu,m^{N}}(\omega_{N})
is zero unless

K_{\lambda/\mu,m^{N}}(\omega_{N})=(-1)^{(N-1)m}\,\,{\rm sgn}(\chi^{\lambda/\mu}((N^{m}))).

Proof.

(a) follows from Theorem
5.4
when
d=N
.

(b) can be obtained by combining (a) with (
5.6
).
∎

6.
Remarks

We provide a few remarks for readers’ understanding.

(1)

As stated in Introduction, the present paper is motivated by the conjecture in
[
4
, Conjecture 50]
,
which is equivalent to saying that
s_{m\lambda/m\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1})
is a CSP polynomial modulo
m
.
It was shown in
[
1
]
that this is true in case where
(k,m(|\lambda|-|\mu|))=1
.
However, one can find a counterexample in case where
k
is not divisible by
m
and
(k,m(|\lambda|-|\mu|))\neq 1
.
For instance, if
\lambda=(3,3,2,1),\mu=(2,1),m=9
, and
k=4
, using a computer,
one can easily see that

\displaystyle s_{9\lambda/9\mu}(1,q,q^{2},q^{3})\equiv 54665112\frac{q^{9}-1}{q-1}-3\frac{q^{9}-1}{q^{3}-1}+1\pmod{q^{9}-1}.

On the other hand, in case where
\lambda=(3,3,1)
,
\mu=(2,1)
,
m=4
, and
k=6
,

k
is not divisible by
m
but
(k,|m\lambda/m\mu|)=(6,16)=2\neq 1
.
In this case, one can see that

s_{4\lambda/4\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{5})\equiv 1576440\frac{q^{4}-1}{q-1}+264\frac{q^{4}-1}{q^{2}-1}+12\pmod{q^{4}-1}.

It would be very nice to find a sharp condition for the triples
(m\lambda/m\mu,m,k)

such that
s_{m\lambda/m\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1})
is a CSP polynomial modulo
m
.

(2)

Based on
[
3
, Theorem 37]
, one may naively expect that any stretched skew Schur polynomial
s_{m\lambda/m\mu}(1,q,\ldots,q^{k-1})
can be a CSP polynomial modulo
m
when multiplied by an appropriate power of
q
.
However, this immediately turns out to be false.
For instance, as shown in (1),

s_{9\lambda/9\mu}(1,q,q^{2},q^{3})
is not a CSP polynomial modulo
9

when
\lambda=(3,3,2,1)
,
\mu=(2,1)
,
m=9
, and
k=4
.
In this case,
q^{i}s_{9\lambda/9\mu}(1,q,q^{2},q^{3})
are not CSP polynomials modulo
9
for all
1\leq i\leq 8
, and which can be easily checked with the help of a computer.
Indeed, they are not even pre-CSP polynomials modulo
9
.

(3)

It is well known that there is a one-to-one correspondence

\mathsf{SSYT}_{k}(\lambda/\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\bigcup_{|\lambda|=|\mu|+|\nu|}\mathsf{SSYT}_{k}(\nu)^{c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}},

where
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
is the Littlewood-Richardson number indexed by
\lambda
,
\mu
and
\nu

(see
[
8
, Section 5]
or
[
5
, Example 3.3]
).
However, some
\nu
’s appearing on the right hand side may not be
m
-stretched,
thus
[
14
, Corollary 3.4]
cannot be applicable in attacking the conjecture in
[
4
, Conjecture 50]
.
For example, in case where
\lambda=(3,2),\mu=(1)
,
m=4
, and
k=4
,
there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathsf{SSYT}_{4}(4\lambda/4\mu)
and

\mathsf{SSYT}_{4}(11,5)\cup\mathsf{SSYT}_{4}(12,4)\cup\mathsf{SSYT}_{4}(8,8)\cup\mathsf{SSYT}_{4}(10,6)\cup\mathsf{SSYT}_{4}(9,7).

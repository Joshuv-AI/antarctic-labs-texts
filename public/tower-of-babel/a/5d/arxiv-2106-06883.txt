Axial meson contribution to proton form factors
N.Korchagin, A.Radzhabov

Abstract
The effect of axial meson exchange on extraction of proton electromagnetic form factors in e ​ p ep scattering is calculated. Taking as an example f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) f_{1}(1285) meson exchange, it is quantitatively shown how it affects Rosenbluth and polarization transfer methods. The contribution of an axial meson is negligible in comparison with the traditional box-type two photon exchange and could not be responsible for difference of form factors extracted by different techniques. However, results support the idea that meson exchange could explain deviation of individual polarizations.



I
Introduction

Understanding of the internal structure of proton is a cornerstone of the theory of strong interaction.
Effectively the proton composition is encoded in form factors.
In electron-proton scattering one can measure electric
G_{E}
and magnetic
G_{M}
form factors (also called as Sachs form factors).
The first is related with electric charge distribution and the second is with magnetization.

There are two techniques to extract
G_{E,M}
. Both utilize the one photon exchange(OPE) approximation.
First method uses unpolarized
ep
cross section measurements.
It exploits the linearity of the cross-section in OPE approximation with respect to some kinematic variable
\varepsilon
(meaning will be discussed later) to extract
G_{E,M}
.
This method is known as Rosenbluth technique(proposed by Rosenbluth
Rosenbluth:1950yq
).

Second method is used in experiments with polarized beams.
It was proposed by Akhiezer
AkhiezerJETP:1958
and later developed in works
Akhiezer:1968ek
;
Akhiezer:1974em
.
Longitudinally polarized electron is scattered off the unpolarized nucleon and the recoil nucleon gets a polarization
\vec{e}+N\to e+\vec{N}
.
The ratio of transverse to longitudinal polarizations of the recoil nucleon is proportional to
G_{E}/G_{M}
.
This technique is also called polarization transfer method.

Surprisingly behavior of form factors obtained by different methods disagrees.
In Rosenbluth technique
G_{E}
and
G_{M}
fall with the same rate with
Q^{2}
.
The ratio
\mu_{p}G_{E}/G_{M}
is close to 1, while from polarization transfer experiments this ratio decreases with
Q^{2}
.
For more details we refer the reader to reviews
PerdrisatReview:2006
;
PacettiReview:2015
;
PunjabiReview:2015
.

It is common belief that discrepancy originates from higher
\alpha
order corrections.
They can be divided on real(bremsstrahlung) and virtual: vacuum polarization, self-energy corrections, vertex corrections and two photon exchange(TPE).
For polarization transfer bremsstrahlung is small in comparison with TPE effects
BorisyukKobushkin:2014
.
Due to the nature of measuring the ratio of cross sections, corrections are almost canceled.
For the available
Q^{2}
range bremsstrahlung gives less than
1\%
correction to
G_{E}/G_{M}
.
Meanwhile TPE contribution grows significantly at high
Q^{2}
, especially at high beam energy.
On the other hand, for Rosenbluth method, bremsstrahlung is significant and it is considered during data analysis.
However there are concerns about its completeness: additional higher order corrections and large logarithms which are not included to analysis could be important
Arbuzov:2015vba
;
Arbuzov:2019blj
.

Commonly TPE is represented as box and crossed-box diagrams,
Fig.
1
(a,b).
Contributions with elastic and inelastic intermediate states could resolve inconsistency between form factors extracted by Rosenbluth and polarization transfer methods
BlundenMelnitchoukTjon:2005
.
However, other polarization observables tend to disagree with most theoretical models. For example measurement of the longitudinal recoil polarization
P_{L}
has noticeable deviation at forward scattering angles from OPE and TPE corrections worsen consistency
GEp2gammaCollMeziane:2010
;
Puckett:2017flj
.
Moreover, only part of such calculation for TPE can be done in a model independent way.
A structure of intermediate hadron states influence results(for more details see reviews
ArringtonReview:2011
;
AfanasevTPEreview:2017
).

Figure 1:
Possible TPE diagrams. Box, cross-box and the meson exchange diagrams. Blobs represent non-point-like vertices.

There is an additional TPE contribution from exchange of a single
C
-even meson, which is connected to the lepton by two photons, Fig.
1
(c).
Such diagrams were examined in
ChenZhou:2013
;
KoshchiiAfanasev:2016
.
Pseudoscalar meson contribution is exactly zero for the cross section of unpolarized particles.
Authors of
KoshchiiAfanasev:2016
are focused at MUSE experiment kinematics, i.e. small beam energies and
Q^{2}
. In such case the primary candidate for the exchanged particle is
\sigma
-meson.
Here we concentrate on the high
Q^{2}
kinematics region where the deviation appears between cross section and polarized data.

The contribution of axial-vector mesons was proposed long time ago by Drell and Sullivan
Drell:1965is
as a possible explanation for the deviation of theory from experiment for the hyperfine splitting.
In the recent paper
Dorokhov:2017nzk
contribution of axial mesons in muonic hydrogen spectroscopy was calculated. The effect is significant for the Zemach radii although small for the charge radius.
Most important is that the meson correction is the same order as TPE for
1S
level.
This fact inspired us to calculate the effect of axial mesons on extraction of form factors from elastic
ep
scattering.
We repeat analytical results of
RekaloTomasi:2004
and also estimate quantitatively axial-vector contribution.

This work is organized in the following way.
In the next section the formalism of OPE approximation is briefly discussed.
In the section
III
the contribution of axial vector exchange to polarization transfer and cross section is calculated.
Then, the section
IV
discusses the calculation of effective electron-meson and proton meson couplings.
The last section
V
presents the numerical results for the new contribution.

II
Elastic lepton-proton scattering formalism

We consider elastic lepton-proton scattering
l(k)+p(p)\to l(k^{\prime})+p(p^{\prime})
.
The exchanged photon or meson has momenta

q=p^{\prime}-p=k-k^{\prime},\qquad q^{2}=-Q^{2}.

(1)

The full amplitude
\mathcal{M}
is the sum of the photon exchange
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
and axial meson exchange
\mathcal{M}_{A}
amplitudes

\mathcal{M}^{2}=|\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}+\mathcal{M}_{A}|^{2}\approx|\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}|^{2}+2\text{Re}\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{A}^{*}.

(2)

|\mathcal{M}_{A}|^{2}
is suppressed by a higher orders of
\alpha
.

\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
and
\mathcal{M}_{A}
are represented by the left and right Feynman diagrams in Fig.
2
respectively.
The meson-lepton coupling is treated as an effective axial vertex with the coupling
g_{eA}
.
This coupling is calculated in the section
IV
.

Figure 2:
One photon and meson exchange contributions. Blobs represent non point-like vertices.

Amplitudes are expressed in terms of lepton and hadron currents. Vector currents are

\displaystyle j^{(\gamma)}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=Q_{l}e\bar{u}(k^{\prime})\gamma_{\mu}u(k),

(3)

\displaystyle J^{(\gamma)}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=Q_{h}e\bar{U}(p^{\prime})\Big[\gamma_{\mu}F_{1}(Q^{2})-\frac{\sigma_{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}}{2M_{p}}F_{2}(Q^{2})\Big]U(p),

(4)

where
Q_{l},Q_{h}
are lepton and nucleon charges in units of electron charge(i.e.
-1
for electron,
+1
for proton),
e
is the electron charge(
e^{2}/4\pi=\alpha_{QED}
),

\sigma_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}[\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}]
,
M_{p}
is the proton mass.

F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are Dirac and Pauli form factors.

For the axial meson exchange the lepton and hadron axial currents are

\displaystyle j^{(A)}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=g_{eA}(Q^{2})\bar{u}(k^{\prime})\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}u(k),

(5)

\displaystyle J^{(A)}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=g_{pA}(Q^{2})\bar{U}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}U(p),

(6)

where
g_{eA}(Q^{2})
is the effective coupling of a lepton with an axial meson and
g_{pA}(Q^{2})
is the proton-meson coupling.
Further the
Q^{2}
dependency of couplings is omitted for shortness.

Figure 3:
Breit frame kinematics.

It is convenient to do calculation in Breit frame (reader can find pedagogical introduction in this technique in lecture notes
RekaloLecture:2002gv
).
This frame is chosen in such a way that the photon energy is
0
.
The photon 3-momentum is chosen along
+z
direction
\vec{q}=(0,0,q_{B})
,
scattering is in the
xz
-plane and the electron mass is neglected. Momenta in this frame are

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=(E_{e},\frac{q_{B}}{2}\cot\frac{\theta_{B}}{2},0,\frac{q_{B}}{2}),

\displaystyle k^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(E_{e},\frac{q_{B}}{2}\cot\frac{\theta_{B}}{2},0,-\frac{q_{B}}{2}),

\displaystyle E_{e}^{2}=\frac{Q^{2}}{4\sin^{2}\theta_{B}/2},

(7)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=(E_{p},0,0,-q_{B}/2),

\displaystyle p^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(E_{p},0,0,q_{B}/2),

\displaystyle E_{p}^{2}=M_{p}^{2}(1+\tau),

(8)

where
\tau=Q^{2}/4M_{p}^{2}
and
\theta_{B}
is the electron scattering angle in Breit frame.
The
\theta_{B}
and the Lab frame scattering angle
\theta_{e}
are related as

\cot^{2}(\theta_{B}/2)=\frac{\cot^{2}(\theta_{e}/2)}{1+\tau}.

(9)

It is convenient to introduce a following dimensionless variable
\nu
:

\nu=\frac{p\cdot k}{M_{p}^{2}}-\tau=\frac{s-u}{4M_{p}^{2}}=\frac{E_{e}E_{p}}{M_{p}^{2}}=\frac{E_{1}+E_{2}}{2M_{p}}=\sqrt{\tau(1+\tau)}\sqrt{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}},

(10)

where
E_{1,2}
are initial and final electron energies in Lab frame

E_{1}=M_{p}(\nu+\tau),\qquad E_{2}=E_{1}-2M_{p}\tau=M_{p}(\nu-\tau),

(11)

and
\varepsilon
is

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{1+2(1+\tau)\tan^{2}(\theta_{e}/2)}.

(12)

The
\varepsilon
is often erroneously called in literature as a degree of longitudinal polarization. In fact, it is a degree of linear polarization
Akhiezer:1974em
.

The contribution of meson exchange in unpolarized and polarized scattering is calculated in the next section in the described framework.

III
Axial vector contribution

The OPE amplitude is well known and it has a simple form in Breit frame
RekaloLecture:2002gv
:

\displaystyle\overline{|\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}|}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\text{spin}}j^{(\gamma)}_{\mu}(j^{(\gamma)}_{\mu^{\prime}})^{*}J^{(\gamma)}_{\nu}(J^{(\gamma)}_{\nu^{\prime}})^{*}\frac{g^{\mu\nu}g^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}}{Q^{4}}

(13)

\displaystyle=(Q_{l}^{2}Q_{h}^{2})e^{4}\frac{4M_{p}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\Big((G_{E}^{2}+\tau G_{M}^{2})\cot^{2}(\theta_{B}/2)+2\tau G_{M}^{2}\Big)

(14)

\displaystyle=(Q_{l}^{2}Q_{h}^{2})e^{4}\frac{1}{\tau(1+\tau)}\Big(G_{E}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{\varepsilon}G_{M}^{2}\Big)\cot^{2}(\theta_{e}/2).

(15)

The last line corresponds to transition to the Lab frame.
Electric and magnetic form factors
G_{E,M}
are related with Dirac and Pauli form factors as

G_{E}=F_{1}-\tau F_{2},\qquad G_{M}=F_{1}+F_{2}.

(16)

Calculation for interference between OPE and meson exchange amplitudes is straightforward and gives

\displaystyle 2\text{Re}\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=2\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\textrm{spin}}j_{\mu}^{(A)}(j_{\mu^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)})^{*}J_{\nu}^{(A)}(J_{\nu^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)})^{*}\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{q^{2}}\frac{g_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}-q_{\mu^{\prime}}q_{\nu^{\prime}}/q^{2}}{q^{2}-m_{A}^{2}}

(17)

\displaystyle=2(Q_{l}Q_{h}e^{2})\frac{g_{eA}g_{pA}}{(Q^{2}+m_{A}^{2})Q^{2}}L^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}

(18)

\displaystyle=(Q_{l}Q_{h}e^{2})\frac{g_{eA}g_{pA}}{(Q^{2}+m_{A}^{2})Q^{2}}16E_{e}E_{p}Q^{2}G_{M}

(19)

\displaystyle=(Q_{l}Q_{h}e^{2})\frac{g_{eA}g_{pA}}{(Q^{2}+m_{A}^{2})}\Big(\frac{8M_{p}Q}{\sin(\theta_{B}/2)}\sqrt{(1+\tau)}G_{M}\Big),

(20)

where
m_{A}
is the mass of an axial meson.
Notice that
L^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}
is spin averaged and does not contain couplings.

The cross section is related with the amplitude in Lab frame as

\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{|\mathcal{M}|^{2}}{64\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{E_{2}}{E_{1}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{M_{p}^{2}}.

(21)

Historically the
ep
cross section is represented as a correction to the Mott cross section, which is a cross section for scattering of spin
1/2
point-like particles

\frac{d\sigma_{M}}{d\Omega}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{Q^{2}}\left(\frac{E_{2}}{E_{1}}\right)^{2}\cot^{2}\left(\frac{\theta_{e}}{2}\right).

(22)

The final formula for cross-section is

\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{d\sigma_{M}/d\Omega}{\varepsilon(1+\tau)}\Bigg(\varepsilon G_{E}^{2}+\tau G_{M}^{2}+\frac{(Q_{l}Q_{h})g_{eA}g_{pA}}{2\alpha\pi}\frac{\tau\nu(1-\varepsilon)}{\tau+m_{A}^{2}/4M_{p}^{2}}G_{M}\Bigg).

(23)

The part in parenthesis is so-called reduced cross section. The last term corresponds to the meson exchange contribution.
It behaves as
\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}
in comparison with the OPE part
(\varepsilon G_{E}^{2}+\tau G_{M}^{2})
.
At
\varepsilon\to 1
, which corresponds to forward scattering, the interference correction goes to
0
.
At
\varepsilon\to 0
correction is maximum.
Our result is similar to the old calculation of Drell and Sullivan
Drell:1965is
but differs by factor 2 from Eq. (8) in
RekaloTomasi:2004
.

As the next step the meson exchange contribution in polarized scattering is calculated. The setup is following: there is scattering of a massless, longitudinally polarized electron
k
with helicity
\lambda
on the unpolarized proton
p
.
The recoil proton
p^{\prime}
receives polarization, described by the vector
a^{\mu}
.
In the OPE approximation, the product of lepton and hadron tensors in Breit frame is

\displaystyle L^{(\gamma\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(\gamma\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\text{Tr}[\cancel{k}^{\prime}\gamma_{\mu}\cancel{k}(\lambda\gamma_{5})\gamma_{\nu}]

\displaystyle\times\text{Tr}[(\cancel{p}^{\prime}+M_{p})(1-\gamma_{5}\cancel{a})\Big(\gamma_{\mu}F_{1}-\frac{\sigma_{\mu\rho}q_{\rho}}{2M_{p}}F_{2}\Big)(\cancel{p}+M_{p})\Big(\gamma_{\nu}F_{1}-\frac{\sigma_{\rho\nu}q_{\rho}}{2M_{p}}F_{2}\Big)]

\displaystyle=\frac{16\lambda M_{p}\tau}{1+\tau}G_{M}\Big[E_{e}E_{p}(a\cdot q)\big(G_{E}-G_{M}\big)+M_{p}^{2}(1+\tau)G_{E}(a\cdot(k+k^{\prime}))\Big],

(24)

where summation over the polarization of the final lepton and average over the polarization of the initial proton is preformed.
The 4-vector
a^{\mu}
is related with the rest frame polarization vector
\xi^{\mu}=(0,\vec{\xi})
by boost:

a_{\mu}=\Big(\frac{\vec{\xi}\cdot\vec{p}}{M_{p}},\vec{\xi}+\frac{(\vec{\xi}\cdot\vec{p})\vec{p}}{M_{p}(E_{p}+M_{p})}\Big).

(25)

The products
(a\cdot q)
and
(a\cdot(k+k^{\prime}))
for different polarizations in Breit frame are

\displaystyle P_{x}:

\displaystyle(a_{x}\cdot q)=0,

\displaystyle(a_{x}\cdot(k+k^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=-q_{B}\cot(\theta_{B}/2),

\displaystyle P_{z}:

\displaystyle(a_{z}\cdot q)=-\frac{q_{B}}{M_{p}}E_{p},

\displaystyle(a_{z}\cdot(k+k^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{B}}{M_{p}}E_{e},

(26)

\displaystyle P_{y}:

\displaystyle(a_{y}\cdot q)=0,

\displaystyle(a_{y}\cdot(k+k^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=0.

For
P_{x}
and
P_{z}
polarizations the well known formulas
Akhiezer:1968ek
;
Akhiezer:1974em
are reproduced

\displaystyle L^{(\gamma\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(\gamma\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}(P_{x})

\displaystyle=-4\lambda M_{p}Q^{3}G_{M}G_{E}\cot(\theta_{B}/2)=-32\lambda M_{p}^{4}\tau\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon\tau}{1-\varepsilon}}G_{E}G_{M},

(27)

\displaystyle L^{(\gamma\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(\gamma\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}(P_{z})

\displaystyle=2\lambda\frac{Q^{4}G_{M}^{2}}{\sin(\theta_{B}/2)}=32\lambda M_{p}^{4}\tau^{2}\sqrt{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}}G_{M}^{2}.

(28)

For
P_{y}
polarization the amplitude is zero in OPE approximation.

The interference between photon and meson exchange amplitudes is

L^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}(a)=16\lambda M_{p}\Big[(a\cdot q)\big((E_{e}^{2}+M_{p}^{2}\tau)G_{M}-E_{e}^{2}G_{E}\big)-E_{e}E_{p}G_{E}(a\cdot(k+k^{\prime}))\Big].

(29)

It is worthwhile to note the absence of
g_{pA}
because it is the common factor “outside”
L^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}
in Eq. (
17
).
Using Eq. (
III
) for longitudinal and transverse polarization one can get

\displaystyle L^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}(P_{x})

\displaystyle=-8\lambda Q^{2}M_{p}^{2}\frac{\sqrt{1+\tau}}{\sin(\theta_{B}/2)}\cot(\theta_{B}/2)G_{E}

(30)

\displaystyle=-8\lambda Q^{2}M_{p}^{2}\frac{\sqrt{2\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)(1+\tau)}}{1-\varepsilon}G_{E},

(31)

\displaystyle L^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}(P_{z})

\displaystyle=4\lambda M_{p}Q^{3}\sqrt{1+\tau}(1+\cot^{2}(\theta_{B}/2))G_{M}

(32)

\displaystyle=8\lambda M_{p}Q^{3}\frac{\sqrt{1+\tau}}{1-\varepsilon}G_{M}.

(33)

The interference does not give contribution to
P_{y}
polarization because
L^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}W^{(A\gamma)}_{\mu\nu}(P)
depends on the same scalar products as in OPE. Also only imaginary part of TPE gives rise of
P_{y}
.

The final result for polarization transfer is

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(P_{x})

\displaystyle=-\lambda\frac{\sigma_{M}/d\Omega}{\varepsilon(1+\tau)}\sqrt{2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\tau}\Big[G_{E}G_{M}+(Q_{l}Q_{h})\frac{g_{eA}g_{pA}G_{E}}{4\alpha\pi}\frac{\nu}{\tau+m_{A}^{2}/4M_{p}^{2}}\Big],

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(P_{z})

\displaystyle=\lambda\frac{\sigma_{M}/d\Omega}{\varepsilon(1+\tau)}\tau\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\Big[G_{M}^{2}+(Q_{l}Q_{h})\frac{g_{eA}g_{pA}G_{M}}{2\alpha\pi(1+\varepsilon)}\frac{\nu}{(\tau+m_{A}^{2}/4M_{p}^{2})}\Big].

(35)

One can rewrite it in the form as in
RekaloTomasi:2004
and result for
P_{x}
polarization has an additional factor
\sqrt{\frac{1+\tau}{\tau}}
.

The all contributions to the cross section and polarization transfer is obtained.
To get numerical result and compare with experiments it is necessary
to know the effective couplings
g_{eA}
and
g_{pA}
.
In the next section
that couplings are estimated.

IV
Effective couplings

This section is dedicated to calculation of the effective couplings of an axial meson to the electron
g_{eA}
and to the proton
g_{pA}
.

f_{1}(1285)
is taken as an example of axial meson.

Figure 4:
The effective lepton-meson coupling is given by the triangle loop.

The meson-photons vertex can be parameterized in the form
Pascalutsa:2012pr
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\,ie^{2}\varepsilon_{\rho\sigma\tau\alpha}\,\Bigg\{R^{\mu\rho}(l_{1},l_{2})R^{\nu\sigma}(l_{1},l_{2})\,(l_{1}-l_{2})^{\tau}\,\nu\,F^{(0)}(l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+\,R^{\nu\rho}(l_{1},l_{2})\left(l_{1}^{\mu}-\frac{l_{1}^{2}}{\nu}l_{2}^{\mu}\right)l_{1}^{\sigma}\,l_{2}^{\tau}\,F^{(1)}(l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+\,R^{\mu\rho}(l_{1},l_{2})\left(l_{2}^{\nu}-\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{\nu}l_{1}^{\nu}\right)l_{2}^{\sigma}\,l_{1}^{\tau}\,{F^{(1)}}(l_{2}^{2},l_{1}^{2})\Bigg\},

(36)

where superscripts in form factors
F^{(0)}
and
F^{(1)}
indicate a helicity state of an axial meson.

F^{(0)}
is symmetric under
l_{1}\leftrightarrow l_{2}
.
Normalization factor
1/m_{A}^{2}
which appears in
Pascalutsa:2012pr
is included to form factors.
Note that this equation is for the case when a meson is on the mass-shell.
The off-shellness is considered as additional
q^{2}
dependency of
F^{(i)}
form factors and discussed further.
Other ingredients of the Eq. (
IV
) are

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=(l_{1}\cdot l_{2}),

(37)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=(l_{1}\cdot l_{2})^{2}-l_{1}^{2}l_{2}^{2},

(38)

\displaystyle R^{\mu\nu}(l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{X}\,\bigl\{\nu\left(l_{1}^{\mu}\,l_{2}^{\nu}+l_{2}^{\mu}\,l_{1}^{\nu}\right)-l_{1}^{2}\,l_{2}^{\mu}\,l_{2}^{\nu}-l_{2}^{2}\,l_{1}^{\mu}\,l_{1}^{\nu}\bigr\},

(39)

\displaystyle l_{1\mu}R^{\mu\nu}(l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=l_{2\mu}R^{\mu\nu}(l_{1},l_{2})=l_{1\nu}R^{\mu\nu}(l_{1},l_{2})=l_{2\nu}R^{\mu\nu}(l_{1},l_{2})=0.

(40)

The effective electron-meson coupling is determined by the loop integral, Fig.
4

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{2})\Big[e^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\mu\nu}\left(g_{ab}-\frac{q_{a}q_{b}}{q^{2}}\right)\gamma_{\mu}(\cancel{l}+m)\gamma_{\nu}}{l_{1}^{2}l_{2}^{2}(l^{2}-m^{2})}\Big]u(k_{1}).

(41)

Any arbitrary expression can be decomposed onto a Dirac matrix basis,

\displaystyle A=a_{0}I+a_{5}\gamma_{5}+v_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}+a_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}+T_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu},

(42)

where coefficients determined by

\begin{split}a_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{D}A,\quad a_{5}=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{D}(\gamma^{5}A),\quad v^{\mu}=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{D}(\gamma^{\mu}A),\\
a_{\mu}=-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{D}(\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}A),\quad T^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{8}\mathrm{Tr}_{D}(\sigma^{\mu\nu}A).\end{split}

(43)

The only nonzero projection for axial-vector vertex is
a_{\mu}
.
As result one could write the meson-lepton effective interaction as
g_{eA}(Q^{2})\bar{u}(k^{\prime})\gamma^{5}\gamma_{\mu}u(k)
, where

\displaystyle g_{eA}(Q^{2})

\displaystyle=ie^{4}\int\frac{d^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{N}{l_{1}^{2}l_{2}^{2}(l^{2}-m^{2})},

(44)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=2\Big(\frac{(l\cdot q)}{q^{2}}\nu(l_{1}^{2}-l_{2}^{2})F^{(0)}(Q^{2},l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})+(l\cdot l_{1})\frac{X}{\nu}{F^{(1)}}(Q^{2},l_{2}^{2},l_{1}^{2})+(l\cdot l_{2})\frac{X}{\nu}F^{(1)}(Q^{2},l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})\Big).

(45)

To proceed one needs to know form factors
F^{(i)}
.
At present we have only few experimental data from L3 Collaboration on the transition form factor
L3_f1
;
L3_2
for
f_{1}(1285)
.
In this work the same parameterization as in
Dorokhov:2017nzk
is used.
Namely, with the L3 data
one can parameterize
F^{(0)}
as

\displaystyle F^{(0)}(Q^{2},l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})=

\displaystyle F^{(0)}(Q^{2},0,0)F(l_{1}^{2})F(l_{2}^{2}),

(46)

\displaystyle F(l_{i}^{2})=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}-l_{i}^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(47)

For the numerical estimate we fix the slope of form factors according to the L3 data to
\Lambda_{f_{1}(1285)}=1.04
GeV.
Effect of off-shellness is considered by

F^{(0)}(Q^{2},0,0)=F^{(0)}(M_{A},0,0)e^{-(Q^{2}+M_{A}^{2})/M_{A}^{2}}.

(48)

The values of the form factors can be fixed from the L3 data using the relations given by the nonrelativistic quark model calculating the triangle anomaly diagram:

F^{(0)}(M_{A}^{2},0,0)=-F^{(1)}(M_{A}^{2},0,0),\qquad\tilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}}=\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}M_{A}^{5}}{12}[F^{(1)}(M_{A}^{2},0,0)]^{2},

(49)

where
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}}
is a meson decay width.
Moreover, the nonrelativistic quark model requires the sign of
F^{(0)}
is positive and

F^{(0)}_{f_{1}(1285)}(M_{f_{1}}^{2},0,0)=(0.266\pm 0.043)~\text{GeV}^{-2}

(50)

VI
Conclusion

Contribution of axial-vector meson exchange to unpolarized
ep
cross section and polarization transfer is calculated.
For this purpose, effective lepton-meson coupling was estimated.

The coupling of electron to
f_{1}(1285)
is same order of magnitude as to scalar
\sigma
meson:
g_{ef_{1}}\approx 10^{-6}
.
It changes sign at very low
Q^{2}\approx 0.2~\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
and has maximum at
1~\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
.
Meanwhile, coupling
f_{1}(1285)
-muon is much smaller.

Correction to OPE for unpolarized cross section is of order
0.1\%
, which is significantly smaller than the current experimental precision.
Correction has the maximum at
\varepsilon=0
and
Q^{2}\approx 3~\mathrm{GeV}
.
For polarization ratio correction has the similar scale:
0.05\%
.
More interesting result is for individual polarizations.
It has correct qualitative behavior and decrease difference between theory and experiment for longitudinal polarization. Consideration of other mesons could explain data.

Finally, one can conclude that contributions of axial mesons exchanges could not be responsible for difference of form factors extracted by Rosenbluth and polarization transfer methods. However results support the idea that meson exchange could be behind deviation from OPE of individual polarizations.

Appendix A
Calculation of
g_{eA}

This appendix is dedicated to details of lepton-meson coupling calculation.
g_{eA}
is the product of the following integral

\displaystyle g_{eA}=

\displaystyle ie^{4}\int\frac{d^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{N}{l_{1}^{2}l_{2}^{2}(l^{2}-m^{2})},

(53)

\displaystyle N=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\frac{((k+l_{1})\cdot q)}{q^{2}}\nu(l_{1}^{2}-l_{2}^{2})F^{(0)}(l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+((k+l_{1})\cdot l_{1})\frac{X}{\nu}{F^{(1)}}(l_{2}^{2},l_{1}^{2})+((k+l_{1})\cdot l_{2})\frac{X}{\nu}F^{(1)}(l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})\Big).

(54)

In the NRQM
F^{(0,1)}
form factors are related with Adler’s form factors as

\displaystyle F^{(0)}(l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})=-A_{4},\quad F^{(1)}(l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})=\frac{\nu}{X}(l_{2}^{2}-\nu)A_{4},\quad{F^{(1)}}(l_{2}^{2},l_{1}^{2})=\frac{\nu}{X}(l_{1}^{2}-\nu)A_{4}.

(55)

In such case terms with
F^{(1)}
cancels out.
Therefore one can assume that the main contribution comes from
F^{(0)}
term.
Thus considering it and rewriting Eq. (
54
) in terms of momenta shown in Fig.
4
, the numerator
N
becomes

\displaystyle N=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q^{2}}(l\cdot p-l^{2}-k_{1}\cdot k_{2})(l\cdot q)^{2}F^{(0)},

(56)

where
p=k_{1}+k_{2}
and
p\cdot q=0
.
Form factor is parameterized as

\displaystyle F^{(0)}(l_{1}^{2},l_{2}^{2})=\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}-l_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}-l_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(57)

The full expression for lepton-meson coupling is

\displaystyle g_{eA}(Q^{2})=

\displaystyle ie^{4}F^{(0)}(M_{A}^{2},0,0)e^{-(Q^{2}+M_{A}^{2})/M_{A}^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{2(l\cdot p-l^{2}-k_{1}\cdot k_{2})(l\cdot q)^{2}}{q^{2}(l^{2}-m^{2})(l-k_{1})^{2}(l-k_{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}-l_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}-l_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(58)

Note that Eq. (
54
) is for a meson on the mass-shell. The effect of off-shellness is taken into account by the factor
\exp(-(Q^{2}+M_{A}^{2})/M_{A}^{2})
.
F^{(0)}(M_{A}^{2},0,0)
is form factor normalization.

The integral could be calculated using following general scheme: use Laplace transformation, do shift in integration momenta
l
to make integral Gaussian and take it.
Then, integral over
\alpha
-s is transformed to relative coordinates
L
and
x_{i}
.

L

x_{i}
is done numerically.

First step is to apply following integral representation to propagators

\frac{1}{(p_{i}^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon)^{k}}=\frac{(-i)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha_{i}e^{i\alpha_{i}(p_{i}^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon)}\alpha_{i}^{k-1}.

(59)

As result one gets the exponent in power of

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}l_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}l_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}l^{2}+\alpha_{4}l_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{5}l_{2}^{2}+B

\displaystyle=\bar{a}_{1}l_{1}^{2}+\bar{a}_{2}l_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}l^{2}+B

(60)

\displaystyle=\Delta l^{2}+l\cdot(\bar{a}_{1}k_{1}+\bar{a}_{2}k_{2})+B,

(61)

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{4};\qquad\bar{a}_{2}=\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{5},\qquad B=-\alpha_{3}m^{2}-(\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5})\Lambda^{2},\qquad\Delta=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i},

where

denotes the part independent of

Transition to integral representation adds
(-i)^{7}=i
as a common factor.

Further, one needs to do momentum shift and take Gaussian integral over
l
. Shifting

\displaystyle\left(\frac{l^{2}q^{2}}{4}+(V\cdot q)^{2}\right)\left(V\cdot p-l^{2}-V^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)-l^{2}(V\cdot q)^{2}.

(63)

After shifting the expression in exponent becomes

\displaystyle\Delta l^{2}+\frac{1}{\Delta}(\bar{a}_{1}\bar{a}_{2}q^{2}+\bar{a}_{1}\alpha_{3}k_{1}^{2}+\bar{a}_{2}\alpha_{3}k_{2}^{2})+B.

(64)

\displaystyle\Delta l^{2}+\frac{1}{\Delta}(\bar{a}_{1}\bar{a}_{2}q^{2})+B.

(65)

Full result is proportional to

\displaystyle\propto\int\!\!\!d^{4}l\,\frac{2}{q^{2}}\Bigg[\left(\frac{l^{2}q^{2}}{4}+(V\cdot q)^{2}\right)\left(V\cdot p-l^{2}-V^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)-l^{2}(V\cdot q)^{2}\Bigg]e^{i\Delta l^{2}}\exp\Big[\frac{i}{\Delta}(\bar{a}_{1}\bar{a}_{2}q^{2}+B)\Big].

Conjectures P1-P15 for hyperbolic Coxeter groups of rank 3
Jianwei Gao, Xun Xie

Abstract
. We prove Lusztig’s conjectures P1-P15 for hyperbolic Coxeter groups of rank 3. Our proof enables us to give a description of the 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -function and Kazhdan-Lusztig cells for these Coxeter groups.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 20C08; Secondary 20F55

1.
Introduction

Lusztig proposed a series of conjectures, called P1-P15, on Hecke algebras with unequal parameters in
[
Lus03
, §14]
. They are stated in a very general form, i.e. for any finitely generated Coxeter group and any positive weight function. These conjectures predict that there are nice relations between
\mathbf{a}
-functions and cells, and one can define a kind of asymptotic rings.
Lusztig proved these conjectures for constant weight functions (the equal parameter case) by assuming the boundedness conjecture about the
\mathbf{a}
-function
[
Lus03
, §13.4]
and the positivity of the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis. The positivity has been proved by
[
EW14
]
, using some deep ideas from the Hodge theory. However, there is no positivity for the unequal parameter case, and the problem becomes mysterious. Up to now we have no efficient way to prove P1-P15 for arbitrary parameters.
The main difficulty probably lies in computing
\mathbf{a}
-functions.

In
[
Lus03
]
, Lusztig proved P1-P15 for infinite dihedral groups and the quasi-split case.
For P1-P15 of finite Weyl groups, only the case of type
B_{n}
with “non-asymptotic parameters” remains open, see
[
Gec11
]
and references therein. The universal Coxeter group case is proved in
[
SY15
]
. Recently, the case of affine Weyl groups of rank 2 is proved in
[
GP18
,
GP19
]
, by studying cell representations
and a connection with the Plancherel formula.

This paper is a subsequent work of
[
Xie19
]
, where the second named author proved P1-P15 for Coxeter groups with complete graph and right-angled Coxeter groups. The aim of this paper is to complete the proof of conjectures P1-P15 for all Coxeter groups of rank 3. The cases of finite and affine types are known, see
[
Bon17
, 25A.1]
and
[
GP18
,
GP19
]
.
Thus we focus on hyperbolic Coxeter groups of rank 3.
Since the cases of Coxeter groups with complete graph and right-angled Coxeter groups have been proved in
[
Xie19
]
, we only need to consider Coxeter groups
(W,S)

such that
S=\{r,s,t\}
,
m_{rt}=2
,
\frac{1}{m_{rs}}+\frac{1}{m_{rs}}<\frac{1}{2}
, and at least one of
m_{rs}
,
m_{st}
is not
\infty
.
These groups are divided into three classes in our proofs:

(1)

\infty=m_{rs}>m_{st}\geq 3
;

(2)

\infty>m_{rs},m_{st}\geq 4
but
(m_{rs},m_{st})\neq(4,4)
;

(3)

\infty>m_{rs}\geq 7,m_{st}=3
.

Their Coxeter graphs look like

r
s
t
m_{rs}
m_{st}
.

The main methods of proving P1-P15 in
[
Xie19
]
are applying decreasing induction on
\mathbf{a}
-values and proving a kind of decomposition formula for some Kazhdan-Lusztig basis elements in a quotient algebra.
In this paper, we prove P1-P15 for Coxeter groups in (1) (2) and (3) by similar methods.
However, the proofs of some key properties become complicated. In fact all the sections
4
–
8
of this paper are devoted to prove them. Once we obtain these properties, we can repeat arguments in
[
Xie19
]
to prove P1-P15, see
[
Xie19
, §9]
.
The proofs in sections
6
-
8
depend on some explicit expansions of products in the Hecke algebra (section
5
). Since results from section
5
are discrete, we have to verify case by case in sections
6
-
8
.
The ideas of the proofs are simple, and for most cases the verification is easy.

The
\mathbf{a}
-functions and cells are often not easy to determine, even for equal parameter cases. A benefit of our proof of P1-P15 is that we have a description of the
\mathbf{a}
-functions and left (right) cells, see Theorem
9.2
. In the complete graph case, each
W_{N}
(the set of elements of
W
with
\mathbf{a}
-value
N
) is either empty or a two-sided cell. However this is not correct in our present situation. We give a complete list of cases where
W_{N}
contains more than one two-sided cells, see Theorem
9.6
. As a corollary, we conclude that, in the equal parameter case, a non-empty
W_{N}
is always a two-sided cell. This confirms
[
BGS14
, Conj. 3.1]
in the case of rank 3. At last, we give some examples to show how the cell partitions depend on the parameters, which should be helpful for understanding the semi-continuity conjecture formulated in

[
Bon09
]
.

This article is organized as follows.
In section
2
, we prepare some notations and basic facts.
In section
3
, we state Conjecture
3.1
for Coxeter groups of dimension 2, and then claim that it implies P1-P15 (Theorem
3.2
).
A more general form of Conjecture
3.1
is given in
[
Xie19
, §9]
.
In section
4
, we prepare some facts about dihedral groups that we will use. In section
5
, we give explicit expansions of some products, which are fundamental in our proof of key properties. The main goal of sections
6
-
8
is to prove Conjecture
3.1
for those Coxeter groups listed above, see Proposition
6.1
, Lemma
7.1
, and Proposition
8.1
. In the section
9
, we arrive at our main result and determine two-sided cells.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us fix some notations. Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter group with

. For

in

. The neutral element of

. For

, the parabolic subgroup generated by

is denoted by

. If

. For

with

, we use notation

instead of

and
w_{st}
instead of
w_{\{s,t\}}

of some elements

with
l(x)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}l(x_{i})
, then we say
x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{k}
is a

. We use the notation
x_{1}\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{k}
to indicate that
x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{k}
is a reduced product. For
x,y\in W
, we say

appears in

, if there exist
w,z\in W
such that

. For
x\in W
,

\mathcal{L}(x)=\{s\in S\mid sx<x\},\mathcal{R}(x)=\{s\in S\mid xs<x\}.

Let
L:W\to\mathbb{N}
be a weight function on
W
. In other words,
L
satisfies
L(xy)=L(x)+L(y)
for any
x,y\in W
with
l(xy)=l(x)+l(y)
. Unless otherwise stated,
L
is assumed to be positive, i.e.
L(s)>0
for any
s\in S
. We call
(W,S,L)
a (positively) weighted Coxeter group. Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials in indeterminate
q
. The Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
of
(W,S,L)
is a unital associative algebra over
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}
-basis
\{T_{w}\mid w\in W\}
subject to relations:

T_{ww^{\prime}}=T_{w}T_{w^{\prime}}\text{ if }l(ww^{\prime})=l(w)+l(w^{\prime}),

\text{ and }T_{s}^{2}=1+\xi_{s}T_{s}\text{ with }\xi_{s}=q^{L(s)}-q^{-L(s)}\in\mathcal{A}.

For
0\neq a=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}q^{i}\in\mathcal{A}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\deg a=\max\{i\mid\alpha_{i}\neq 0\}
. For
0\in\mathcal{A}
, we define
\deg 0=-\infty
. For
h=\sum_{w\in W}a_{w}T_{w}\in\mathcal{H}
with
a_{w}\in\mathcal{A}
, we define
\deg h=\max\{\deg a_{w}\mid w\in W\}
. This gives a function
\deg:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}
.

Denote by
C_{w}
,
w\in W
the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of
\mathcal{H}
. We have
C_{w}=\sum_{y\leq w}p_{y,w}T_{y}
with
p_{y,w}\in\mathcal{A}_{<0}=q^{-1}\mathbb{Z}[q^{-1}]
for
y<w
and
p_{w,w}=1
. Moreover,
C_{w}
is invariant under the bar involution
\overline{\phantom{q}}
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\overline{q}=q^{-1}
and
\overline{T_{w}}=T_{w^{-1}}^{-1}
. Using Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, one can define preorders
\prec_{{L}}
,
\prec_{{R}}
,
\prec_{{LR}}
and equivalence relations
\sim_{{L}}
,
\sim_{{R}}
,
\sim_{{LR}}
on
W
. The associated equivalence classes are called respectively left cells, right cells and two-sided cells. Let
f_{x,y,z}
and
h_{x,y,z}
be elements of
\mathcal{A}
given by

T_{x}T_{y}=\sum_{z\in W}f_{x,y,z}T_{z},\quad C_{x}C_{y}=\sum_{z\in W}h_{x,y,z}C_{z}.

For
w\in W
, define

\mathbf{a}(w):=\max\{\deg h_{x,y,w}\mid x,y\in W\}.

Then
\mathbf{a}:W\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is called Lusztig’s
\mathbf{a}
-function. Define
\gamma_{x,y,z^{-1}}\in\mathbb{Z}
to be the coefficient of
q^{\mathbf{a}(z)}
in
h_{x,y,z}
.
For
w\in W
, integers
\Delta(w)
and
n_{w}
are
defined by

p_{e,w}=n_{w}v^{-\Delta(w)}+\text{ terms of lower degree, with }n_{w}\neq 0.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{z\in W\mid\mathbf{a}(z)=\Delta(z)\}
.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

W_{\geq N}:=\{w\in W\mid\mathbf{a}(w)\geq N\},

W_{>N}:=W_{\geq(N+1)},\quad W_{N}:=W_{\geq N}\setminus W_{>N},

and similarly define
W_{\leq N}
,
W_{<N}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\geq N}=\mathcal{D}\cap W_{\geq N}
, and similarly define
\mathcal{D}_{N}
etc. The boundedness conjecture says that

W_{>N_{0}}=\emptyset\text{ for }N_{0}=\max\{L(w_{I})\mid I\subseteq S\text{ with }W_{I}\text{ finite}\}.

(2.1)

This conjecture holds for Coxeter groups of rank 3 by
[
Zho13
,
Gao19
]
.

3.
Coxeter groups of dimension 2

In this section,
(W,S)
is a Coxeter group such that all its finite parabolic subgroups have rank 1 or 2, and at least one of them has rank 2.
We say that this kind of Coxeter group has dimension 2, because its Davis complex has dimension 2. For example, infinite Coxeter groups of rank 3 have dimension 2.

Let

D=\{w_{J}\mid J\subseteq S,|W_{J}|<\infty\}\ \sqcup\ \{sw_{st}\mid s,t\in S,\infty>m_{st}\geq 4,L(t)>L(s)\}.

Let

\mathbf{a}^{\prime}(w_{J})=L(w_{J})\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathbf{a}^{\prime}(sw_{st})=L(t)+(\frac{m_{st}}{2}-1)(L(t)-L(s)).

\displaystyle D_{\geq N}

\displaystyle=\{d\in D\mid\mathbf{a}^{\prime}(d)\geq N\},\quad D_{N}=D_{\geq N}\setminus D_{\geq N+1},

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\geq N}\setminus\Omega_{\geq N+1},\quad\Omega_{<N}=W\setminus\Omega_{\geq N}.

(W_{I},I,L)

If

, we set

4.1.
Possible monomials in
f_{u,v,w}

For
u,v,w\in W_{I}
,

is a polynomial of
\xi_{s}
and
\xi_{t}
with nonnegative coefficients. We view
\xi_{s}
and
\xi_{t}
as variables, and say
\xi_{s}^{m}\xi_{t}^{n}
appears in

if the coefficient of
\xi_{s}^{m}\xi_{t}^{n}
in

is nonzero. (It is possible that
\xi_{s}=\xi_{t}
, but this does not affect the following statements.)

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\infty\neq m_{st}\geq 3
.
For
u,v\in W_{I}\setminus\{w_{I}\}
, consider possible monomials that appear in
f_{u,v,sts}
. At least one of the following (maybe overlapped) situations happens:

(1)

f_{u,v,sts}=0
or has a nonzero constant term;

(2)

\xi_{s}
appears in
f_{u,v,sts}
, and
(u,v)
is one of the following pairs:

–

(s\cdot u^{\prime},u^{\prime-1}\cdot sts)
for some
u^{\prime}\in W_{I}
;

–

(tw_{I},w_{I}s)
;

–

(sts\cdot u^{\prime},u^{\prime-1}\cdot s)
for some
u^{\prime}\in W_{I}
;

–

(sw_{I},w_{I}t)
;

(3)

\xi_{t}
appears in
f_{u,v,sts}
, and
(u,v)
is one of the following pairs:

–

(st\cdot u^{\prime},u^{\prime-1}\cdot ts)
for some
u^{\prime}\in W_{I}
;

–

(sw_{I},w_{I}s)
.

Proof.

Assume first
\xi_{s}\neq\xi_{t}
.
We consider the product

since

\xi_{s}^{2}\xi_{t}
or
\xi_{s}\xi_{t}
or

, it is easy to see

or

, which contradicts with the assumption

.

Suppose
\xi_{s}
appears in
f_{u,v,sts}=f_{sts,u,v^{-1}}
and consider the product
T_{sts}T_{u}
. If the third factor of

gives
\xi_{s}
, then
\mathcal{L}(u)=\{s\}
,
stu=v^{-1}
. Thus,

•

if

, then
(u,v)=(s\cdot u^{\prime},u^{\prime-1}\cdot sts)
for some
u^{\prime}\in W_{I}
;

•

if
t\cdot u=w_{I}
, then
(u,v)=(tw_{I},w_{I}s)
.

Similarly if the first factor of
sts
gives
\xi_{s}
, then
s\in\mathcal{L}(tsu)
and
tsu=v^{-1}
. Since
u,v\neq w_{I}
, we have two cases:

•

if
\mathcal{L}(u)=\{s\}
, then
(u,v)=(sts\cdot u^{\prime},u^{\prime-1}\cdot s)

9.1.
P1-P15

Now we arrive at the following main result.

Theorem 9.1
.

Conjectures P1-P15 hold for any weighted Coxeter group of rank 3.

Proof.

First note that the boundedness conjecture for Coxeter groups of rank 3 has been proved in
[

,
Gao19
]
.

is an irreducible Coxeter group with

,
m_{st}
,
m_{rt}
are all odd, then

only admits a constant weight function, and P1-P15 hold by
[

, §16]
. The only irreducible finite Coxeter group which admits unequal parameters is of type

, and P1-P15 can be proved directly as noted by
[

, 25.1A]
. For affine Weyl groups of type

and

[

,

]
. The remaining cases are those Coxeter groups listed in section

8.1

Abstract
. In this paper we develop the p p -thinness and the p p -fine topology for the asymptotic behavior of p p -superharmonic functions at singular points. We consider these as extensions of earlier works on superharmonic functions in dimension 2 in [ AH73 ] , on the Riesz and Log potentials in higher dimensions in [ Mi96 , Section 2.5 and 2.6] , and on p p -harmonic functions in [ KV86 ] . It is remarkable that, contrary to the above cases, the p p -thinness for the singular behavior (cf. Definition 2.3.1 ) differs from the p p -thinness for continuity by the Wiener criterion for p p -superharmonic functions. As applications of asymptotic estimates of p p -superharmonic functions, we also obtain asymptotic estimates of solutions to a class of fully nonlinear elliptic equations, which go beyond [ Lab02 , Theorem 3.6] . This paper grows out of our recent papers on the potential theory in conformal geometry [ MQ21a , MQ21b , MQ22 , LMQZ ] .

1.1.
On the Wolff potentials and the
p
-fine topology

In nonlinear potential theory, one important tool for the study of
p
-Laplace equations is the Wolff potential that in some way substitutes the Riesz potential
and the Log potential in linear potential theory

W^{\mu}_{1,p}(x,\,r)=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{\mu(B(x,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t}

for a nonnegative Radon measure
\mu
and

. We first focus on the asymptotic behavior of the Wolff potentials at singular points.
After the introduction of
the
p
-thinness for the singular behavior in Definition
2.3.1
, we establish

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative finite Radon measure in
\Omega
and
B(x_{0},3r_{0})\subset\Omega

Then there is a subset
E
that is
p
-thin for the singular behavior at
x_{0}
such that

\lim_{x\to x_{0}\text{ and }x\notin E}|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}W_{1,p}^{\mu}(x,\,r_{0})=\frac{p-1}{n-p}\mu(\{x_{0}\})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

that is

-thin for the singular behavior at

\lim_{x\to x_{0}\text{ and }x\notin E}\frac{W_{1,n}^{\mu}(x,\,r_{0})}{\log\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}}=\mu(\{x_{0}\})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

To confirm the

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is

n

\mu

\lim_{x\to 0\text{ and }x\in E}\frac{W^{\mu}_{1,p}(x,r_{0})}{\log\frac{1}{|x|}}>\liminf_{x\to 0}\frac{W^{\mu}_{1,p}(x,r_{0})}{\log\frac{1}{|x|}}.

To deal with the singular behavior of

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{-i-1}\leq|x-x_{0}|\leq 2^{-i}\};

\displaystyle\Omega_{i}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{-i-2}\leq|x-x_{0}|\leq 2^{-i+1}\}.

.

) or the equivalent Wiener sum criterion

by Definition

2.4.
The
p
-fine topology

In potential theory, the introduction of a fine topology based on a notion of thin sets often provides insightful analysis of
the subtle continuity deficiency under usual Euclidean topology.
For the Riesz potentials, in
[
Mi96
, Theorem 5.1 and 5.2 in Section 2.5]
, it has been verified that the topology based on the notion of

\alpha
-thinness defined at the beginning of
[
Mi96
, Section 2.5]
is the fine one for
\alpha
-continuity. Interestingly, the same fine topology
also works for the
\alpha
-limit of the Riesz potentials divided by the kernels at singular points
shown in
[
Mi96
, Theorem 5.3 and 5.4 in Section 2.5]
, which gives the
effective description of the asymptotic behavior and has had geometric applications in our
recent work
[
MQ21a
,
MQ21b
,
MQ22
,
LMQZ
]
. Similarly, in dimension 2, as pointed out in the
discussion near the end of
[
AH73
]
(cf.
[
MQ21a
, Section 2.2]
),
the two different notions of thinness for continuity and the singular behavior for the Log potentials
turn out to be equivalent due to
[
Br44
, Theorem 2]
and therefore induce the same fine topology.

For
p
-superharmonic functions, the
p
-thinness for continuity by the Wiener integral criterion (
2.2.3
) in
Definition
2.2.1
has been shown to be good for a fine topology in
[
KM94
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.3]
for
p\in(1,n]
,
analogous to
[
Mi96
, Theorem 5.1 and 5.2 in Section 2.5]
for the Riesz potentials.
However, contrary to what are described in the above about the Riesz potentials and the Log potentials, it is remarkable that
the notion of
p
-thinness in Definition
2.3.1
for the singular behavior differs from that by Definition
2.2.1
for
continuity when
p\in(1,n)
or
p=n\geq 3
.
Theorem
2.3.1
as the analogue of
[
Mi96
, Theorem 5.3 and Theorem 6.3 in Chapter 2]

on the Riesz and Log potentials in the previous subsection tells that the topology induced by
p
-thinness for the singular behavior indeed is sufficient
to give the asymptotics. Next we want to establish the analogue of
[
Mi96
, Theorem 5.4 in Section 2.5]
to confirm the topology induced by the

p
-thinness for the singular behavior in Definition
2.3.1
is indeed the fine topology for getting asymptotics for
p\in(1,n]
.
Namely, we have Theorem
2
, which is restated for readers’ convenience as follows:

Theorem 2.4.1
.

Suppose that

is a subset that is

3.1.
Arsove-Huber type theorem for

For the convenience of readers, let us restate Theorem

Theorem 3.1.1

\alpha_{0}(n,p)

[

]

(cf.
[

MQ21b
]
for

-superharmonic functions in higher dimensions) and

-harmonic functions
in
[

]
at singularities. Our proof is focsued on

on
S
.
Then, according to Definition
2.1.2
, it
suffices to check the comparison principle. For any domain
D\subset\subset\Omega
, let
v
be a
p_{\Gamma}
-harmonic function in
D
and
v\leq u
on

\partial D
. We want to verify that
v\leq u
in
D
. Assume otherwise, then there is a domain
D_{1}\subset D
, where
v>u
in
D_{1}
and
v=u
on

\partial D_{1}
. It is easily seen that
\bar{D}_{1}\subset\Omega\setminus S
, which violates the comparison principle for
p_{\Gamma}
-superharmonic
functions on
D_{1}
.
∎

We remark that, from the proof of
[
MQ21b
, Lemma 3.2 and 3.3]
(see also
[
BV89
, Proposition 1.1]
when
S
is an isolated point),
it is easily seen that
-\Delta_{p_{\Gamma}}u
is a Radon measure under even somewhat weaker assumptions. Consequently, we have

Corollary 3.2.1
.

Suppose that
u
is nonnegative and that
u\in C^{2}(\Omega\setminus S)
for a compact subset
S
of a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
And suppose
\lim_{x\to S}u(x)=+\infty.

Assume -
\lambda(D^{2}u(x))\in\Gamma
for
p_{\Gamma}\in(1,n]
. Then
S
is of Hausdorff dimension not greater than
n-p_{\Gamma}
and,
for
x_{0}\in S
, there are a subset
E
that is
p_{\Gamma}
-thin
for the singular behavior at
x_{0}
and a nonnegative number
m
such that

\lim_{x\to x_{0}\text{ and }x\notin E}\frac{u(x)}{G_{p_{\Gamma}}(x,x_{0})}=m.

Moreover
u(x)\geq mG_{p_{\Gamma}}(x,x_{0})-c_{0}
in some neighborhood of

.

It is well-known that the polar set of a
p
-superharmonic function is of zero
p
-capacity (see, for example,
[
HKM93
, Theorem 10.1]
). Therefore, in the light of

[
MQ21b

(see also
[

,
SY88
]
), the polar set of a
p
-superharmonic function is of Hausdorff dimension not greater than
n-p
.
It turns out we have a rather effective way to calculate
p_{\Gamma}
for a cone associated with a homogeneous, symmetric, convex function of
n
-variables.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a cone given by a homogeneous, symmetric, convex function
F(\lambda)
on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
(-\frac{n-1}{p-1},1,1,\cdots,1)\in\partial\Gamma=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:F(\lambda)=0\}
. Then

\Gamma\subset\mathcal{A}^{(p)}
and
p_{\Gamma}=p
.

Proof.

Due to the symmetric property with respect to the variables
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
, we only need to
verify that the cone
\Gamma
is supported by the hyperplane

\mathcal{P}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(p-1)\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}=0\},

which clearly supports the cone
\mathcal{A}^{(p)}
and is with the normal vector
(p-1,1,1,\cdots,1)
. By the assumption, we know that

F(-\frac{n-1}{p-1}t,t,\cdots,t)=0

for all
t\geq 0
. To verify that
\mathcal{P}
supports
\Gamma
along the ray
\mathcal{R}=t(-\frac{n-1}{p-1},1,1,\cdots,1)
is to check if the
normal direction of
\Gamma
along the ray
\mathcal{R}
is the same as that of
\mathcal{P}
. After differentiation, we derive

(-\frac{n-1}{p-1}\partial_{\lambda_{1}}F+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\partial_{\lambda_{i}}F)|_{(-\frac{n-1}{p-1}t,t,\cdots,t)}=(-\frac{n-1}{p-1}\partial_{\lambda_{1}}F+(n-1)\partial_{\lambda_{k}}F)|_{(-\frac{n-1}{p-1}t,t,\cdots,t)}=0

for
k=2,3,\cdots,n
, which yields that
\nabla F
to
\Gamma
along the ray
\mathcal{R}
is indeed in the same direction of the normal vector
(p-1,1,1,\cdots,1)

to the hyperplane
\mathcal{P}
. With the ray
\mathcal{R}
in the boundary of both cones it is obvious that
p_{\Gamma}=p
. The proof is therefore
completed.
∎

Consequently, we can calculate
p_{\Gamma^{k}}
easily.

Corollary 3.2.2
.

For the positive cone
\Gamma^{k}
, we have

(3.2.2)

p_{\Gamma^{k}}=\frac{n(k-1)}{n-k}+2\in[2,n]

for
1\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof.

This simply is because

\sigma_{k}(-\frac{n-k}{k},1,1,\cdots,1)=-\frac{n-k}{k}\left(\begin{aligned} k-1\\
n-1\end{aligned}\right)+\left(\begin{aligned} &k\\
n&-1\end{aligned}\right)=0.

∎

Remarkably, we are able to derive an asymptotic estimates that extends
[
Lab02
, Theorem 3.6]
significantly.

Corollary 3.2.3
.

Suppose that
u
is nonnegative and that

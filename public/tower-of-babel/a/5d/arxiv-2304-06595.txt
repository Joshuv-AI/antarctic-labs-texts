Formal degrees of genuine Iwahori-spherical representations
Ping Dong, Fan Gao, and Runze Wang

Abstract
. For irreducible genuine Iwahori-spherical discrete series representations of central covers, we verify the Hiraga–Ichino–Ikeda formula for their formal degrees. We also compute the Whittaker dimensions of these representations, when their associated modules over the genuine Iwahori–Hecke algebra are one-dimensional.

Introduction

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbbm{Q}_{p}
with discrete valuation
|\cdot|_{F}
. Let
O_{F}\subset F
be the ring of algebraic integers and let
\mathfrak{p}_{F}\subset O_{F}
be its prime ideal. Fix a uniformizer
\varpi\in\mathfrak{p}_{F}
with
\left|\varpi\right|_{F}=q^{-1}
. Let
{\bf G}
be a connected semisimple algebraic group scheme over
O_{F}
such that the geometric fiber is
split
over
F
. Write
G:={\bf G}(F)
. Assume that
F^{\times}
contains the full group
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}
of
n
-th roots of unity. We are interested in the
\epsilon
-genuine representations of the Brylinski–Deligne central cover
\widetilde{G}
as in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{q}}

where
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}
always acts via a fixed embedding
\epsilon:\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
. Denote by
{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})
the set of isomorphism classes of
\epsilon
-genuine irreducible representations of
\widetilde{G}
.
For every subset
H\subset G
, we write
\widetilde{H}:=\mathfrak{q}^{-1}(H)
.

For an irreducible discrete series
\pi
of the linear group
G
, it was conjectured by Hiraga–Ichino–Ikeda
[
HII
,
HIIc
]
that (with respect to a canonically defined Haar measure
\mu_{G,\psi}^{\natural}
depending on an additive character
\psi
of
F
)

(1.1)

\deg(\pi,\mu_{G,\psi}^{\natural})=\frac{\dim\tau_{\pi}}{\left|\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{\pi}}\right|}\cdot\left|\gamma(0,\phi_{\pi},{\rm Ad},\psi)\right|,

where
(\phi_{\pi},\tau_{\pi})
is an enhanced Langlands parameter of
\pi
, and
\gamma(s,\phi_{\pi},{\rm Ad},\psi)
the adjoint gamma-factor. We refer to
[
HII
,
CKK12
,
Qiu12
,
GaIc14
,
CiOp15
,
ILM17
,
FeOp20
,
FOS20
,
Rap21
,
FOS22
]
and references therein for some recent work on (

) in the past decade. We should remark also that prior to (
1.1
), there were already explicit computations of some formal degrees such as in the works of Borel
[
Bor76
]
, Reeder
[
Ree94
,
Ree-00
]
and DeBacker–Reeder
[
DR
]
.

Assume that
p\nmid n
and thus fix a splitting of
\widetilde{G}
over
K:={\bf G}(O_{F})
. This gives a splitting of
\widetilde{G}
over the standard Iwahori subgroup
I\subset K
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
be the genuine Iwahori–Hecke algebra of
\widetilde{G}
with respect to
I
. We consider the formal degree problem of genuine Iwahori-spherical representations of
\widetilde{G}
and verify the following, which is probably well-known to the experts.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.3
)
.

Let
\widetilde{G}
be an
n
-fold cover of a split semisimple
G
with
p\nmid n
. Let
\pi_{\sigma}\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})
be the genuine Iwahori-spherical representation associated with
\sigma\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I))
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}
be the Deligne–Langlands correspondence for
\widetilde{G}
given in Definition
3.3
, depending on the choice of the Deligne–Langlands correspondence for split linear groups as in
[
FOS22
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Let
(\phi_{\sigma},\tau_{\sigma}):=\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}(\pi_{\sigma})
be the Deligne–Langlands parameter of
\pi_{\sigma}
. Assume that
\psi
is of conductor
O_{F}
and
\pi_{\sigma}
is a discrete series representation. Then one has

\deg(\pi_{\sigma},\mu_{\widetilde{G},\psi}^{\natural})=\frac{\dim\tau_{\sigma}}{\left|\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{\sigma}}\right|}\cdot\left|\gamma(0,\phi_{\sigma},{\rm Ad},\psi)\right|,

where
\mu_{\widetilde{G},\psi}^{\natural}
is the measure of
\widetilde{G}
naturally associated with the canonical measure
\mu_{G,\psi}^{\natural}
, as given in (
4.9
).

We briefly summarize the main steps in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which occupy §
3
and §
4
:

\bullet

In §
3
, we define a Deligne–Langlands correspondence for irreducible genuine Iwahori-spherical representations
\pi
of
\widetilde{G}
, i.e., we associate an enhanced parameter
(\phi_{\pi},\tau_{\pi})
to such
\pi
, see Definition
3.3
. This is achieved in several steps.
First, the map
\pi\mapsto\pi^{I}
gives a bijection between irreducible genuine Iwahori-spherical representations of
\widetilde{G}
and irreducible
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
-modules. Denote this correspondence by

\pi_{\sigma}\leftrightarrow\sigma,

where
\sigma\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I))
. Second, by using a distinguished genuine character
\theta
, one has an algebra isomorphism
\varphi_{\theta}:\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)\simeq\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})
, where
G_{Q,n}
is the linear algebraic group satisfying
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}\simeq\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
. By transport of action, one has an irreducible module
\sigma_{\theta}:=\varphi_{\theta}^{*}(\sigma)
over
\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})
.
Then, using a Kazhdan–Lusztig–Reeder (KLR) parametrization for
\sigma_{\theta}
, one obtains an enhanced
L
-parameter
(\phi_{\sigma_{\theta}},\tau_{\sigma_{\theta}})
with
\phi_{\sigma_{\theta}}
valued in
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}\simeq\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
. Lastly, the distinguished genuine character
\theta
also gives an injection
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
, and thus one has an
L
-parameter
\phi_{\pi}
of
\pi
valued in
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
. Similar consideration applies in order to define
\tau_{\pi}
using
\tau_{\sigma_{\theta}}
, and this gives the sought Deligne–Langlands parameter
(\phi_{\pi},\tau_{\pi})
of
\pi
. The parameter
(\phi_{\pi},\tau_{\pi})
depends on a choice of the KLR parametrization for
G_{Q,n}
, but does not depend on the choice of
\theta
. This latter independence on
\theta
is shown in Proposition
3.2
.

\bullet

In §
4
, we start with reducing the computation of
\deg(\pi_{\sigma},\mu_{\widetilde{G}})^{-1}
to a sum over the modified extended Weyl group
\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
, see Lemma
4.1
. Here the measure
\mu_{\widetilde{G}}
is such that
\mu_{\widetilde{G}}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}\times I)=1
. In fact, the right hand side of the equality in Lemma
4.1
, in the linear algebraic case, is often taken as the definition of the inverse of the formal degree for discrete series representations of Iwahori–Hecke algebras. Let
\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}\in{\rm Irr}(G_{Q,n})
be the Iwahori-spherical representation associated with
\sigma_{\theta}
given as above. In Proposition
4.2
we make a term by term comparison between
\deg(\pi_{\sigma},\mu_{\widetilde{G}})^{-1}
and
\deg(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}},\mu_{G_{Q,n}})^{-1}
.
By a change of Haar measures and invoking the fact that Hiraga–Ichino–Ikega formula for
\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}
is verified in
[
FOS22
, Theorem 2]
for the linear group
G_{Q,n}
(with respect to the KLR parametrization for
\sigma_{\theta}
given in
[
FOS22
, Theorem 3.1]
there), we immediately obtain Theorem
1.1
.

In the last section §
5
, we fix
G
to be almost simple and simply-connected. We compute for the
n
-fold “oasitic covers”
\widetilde{G}
of such
G
the Whittaker dimension of the discrete series
\pi_{\sigma}
, when
\sigma
is one-dimensional. The Whittaker dimension is a polynomial of
n
, as
n
varies. This resonates with the fact that the wavefront sets of genuine representations of a cover tend to be larger. Moreover, the wavefront sets of
\pi_{\sigma}
are not dictated by their
L
-parameters, and this is in contrast with their formal degrees as shown in Theorem
1.1
above.

2.
Covers and generalities on discrete series

2.1.
Central covers

We retain the notation from the beginning of §
1
. Let
G
be the
F
-rational points of a semisimple group scheme
{\bf G}
defined over
O_{F}
. We assume that the geometric fiber (still denoted by)
{\bf G}
is split over
F
. Denote by

(X,\ \Phi,\ \Delta;\ Y,\Phi^{\vee},\Delta^{\vee})

the root datum of
G
, where
X
is the character lattice and
Y
the cocharacter lattice of a split torus
T\subset G
. Here
\Delta
is a choice of simple roots, and
Y^{sc}\subset Y
is the coroot sublattice and
X^{sc}\subset X
the root lattice. Denote by
W
the Weyl group of the coroot system.

Let

Q:Y\longrightarrow\mathbbm{Z}

be a
W
-invariant quadratic form, and let
B_{Q}(y,z):=Q(y+z)-Q(y)-Q(z)
be the associated bilinear form. Assume that
F^{\times}
contains the full group
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}
of
n
-th roots of unity. For any pair
(D,\eta={\bf 1})
with
D
being a “bisector” of
Q
(see
[
GG
, §2.6]
), one has an associated Brylinski–Deligne covering group
\widetilde{G}_{D}^{(n)}
, which is a central extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{G}_{D}^{(n)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{q}}

of
G
by
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}
, for details see
[
BD
,
GG
,
We6
]
. For simplicity, we will write
\widetilde{G}^{(n)}
or even just
\widetilde{G}
for
\widetilde{G}_{D}^{(n)}
. For every subset
H\subset G
we denote
\widetilde{H}:=\mathfrak{q}^{-1}(H)
. For a fixed embedding

\epsilon:\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times},

an
\epsilon
-genuine representation is one such that
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}
acts via
\epsilon
.

The complex dual group
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
of
\widetilde{G}
is determined by its root datum

(Y_{Q,n},\ \Delta_{Q,n}^{\vee};\ X_{Q,n},\ \Delta_{Q,n}).

Here
Y_{Q,n}\subset Y
is the character lattice of
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
given by

Y_{Q,n}=\left\{y\in Y:\ B_{Q}(y,z)\in n\mathbbm{Z}\text{ for all }z\in Y\right\},

and
X_{Q,n}:={\rm Hom}_{\mathbbm{Z}}(Y_{Q,n},\mathbbm{Z})
. The set
\Delta_{Q,n}^{\vee}
consists of the re-scaled simple coroots

\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee}:=n_{\alpha}\alpha^{\vee}:=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,Q(\alpha^{\vee}))}\alpha^{\vee}.

Let
Y_{Q,n}^{sc}\subset Y_{Q,n}
be the sublattice spanned by
\Delta_{Q,n}^{\vee}
.
We have

Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})={\rm Hom}(Y_{Q,n}/Y_{Q,n}^{sc},\mathbbm{C}^{\times}).

We also set
\alpha_{Q,n}:=n_{\alpha}^{-1}\alpha
for every root
\alpha
, and thus
\Delta_{Q,n}:=\left\{\alpha_{Q,n}\right\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}
.

Let
\text{WD}_{F}=W_{F}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})
be the Weil–Deligne group of
F
. In
[
We6
]
, Weissman constructed the L-group
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
for
\widetilde{G}
, which is an extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{G}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}\widetilde{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{WD}_{F}.}

Here
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
arises from an abelian central extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{\widetilde{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\times}}

via the push-out by the inclusion
Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
and the pull-back via the reciprocity map
\text{WD}_{F}\twoheadrightarrow W_{F}\to F^{\times}
. One always has
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}\simeq\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\rtimes\text{WD}_{F}
; equivalently, the extension of
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
splits, where one of such splittings is provided by the local Langlands correspondence for covering torus. However, in general it is not true that
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}\simeq\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\times\text{WD}_{F}
. If the extension
E_{\widetilde{G}}
splits over
F^{\times}
, then
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
is isomorphic to
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\times\text{WD}_{F}
; this is trivially true if
Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})=\left\{1\right\}
, i.e., if
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
is of adjoint type.

2.2.
Distinguished genuine characters

Let
\widetilde{T}\subset\widetilde{G}
be the covering torus of
\widetilde{G}
. We assume that there exists a “distinguished” genuine central character
\theta:Z(\widetilde{T})\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
, first introduced by Savin
[
Sav04
]
. See
[
GG
, §6]
for a discussion on the necessary conditions for its existence. In particular, it is also shown in
[
GG
, Theorem 6.6]
that the set of all distinguished central characters of
Z(\widetilde{T})
, whenever exist, is a torsor over

{\rm Hom}(F^{\times}/F^{\times n},Z^{\heartsuit}(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})),

where

Z^{\heartsuit}(\widetilde{G}^{\vee}):={\rm Hom}(Y_{Q,n}/(nY+Y_{Q,n}^{sc}),\mathbbm{C}^{\times})\subset Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee}).

Note that if
G
is simply-connected and semisimple, then
nY\subset Y_{Q,n}^{sc}
and thus
Z^{\heartsuit}(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})=Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})
.

Among several good properties of a distinguished genuine character
\theta
are the following:

(i)

It is always Weyl-invariant and satisfies

\theta(\widetilde{h}_{\alpha}(a^{n_{\alpha}}))=1

for every
a\in F^{\times}
and
\alpha\in\Delta
. Here
\widetilde{h}_{\alpha}(a^{n_{\alpha}})\in Z(\widetilde{T})
is a natural lifting of the torus element
h_{\alpha}(a^{n_{\alpha}})\in T
, defined using the Steinberg relations.

(ii)

Every such
\theta
gives rise to a splitting of
E_{\widetilde{G}}
over
F^{\times}
and thus an isomorphism

(2.1)

i_{\theta}:{}^{L}\widetilde{G}\simeq_{\theta}\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\times\text{WD}_{F}.

Relying on a nontrivial additive character
\psi:F\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
and thus the associated Weil index, a detailed construction of distinguished genuine characters is given in
[
GG
, §7]
.

2.3.
Discrete series and formal degrees

For an embedding
\epsilon:\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
, we denote by
{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})

the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible
\epsilon
-genuine representations of
\widetilde{G}
.
For every
(\pi,V)\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})
, we have the contragredient representation
(\pi^{\vee},V^{\vee})
which is realized by the smooth vectors in the algebraic dual of
V
. The action is specified by requiring

{\left\langle v,\pi^{\vee}(g)(\lambda)\right\rangle}={\left\langle\pi(g^{-1})v,\lambda\right\rangle}

for every
v\in V
and
\lambda\in V^{\vee}
, where
{\left\langle-,-\right\rangle}:V\times V^{\vee}\to\mathbbm{C}
is the canonical pairing. In particular, we have

\pi^{\vee}\in{\rm Irr}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G}),

where
\bar{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-1}
is the inverse of
\epsilon
.

For every pair
(v,\lambda)\in V\times V^{\vee}
, we have the function of matrix coefficient

f_{v,\lambda}:\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{C}

given by
f_{v,\lambda}(g):={\left\langle\pi(g^{-1})v,\lambda\right\rangle}
.
Let
\mu_{\widetilde{G}}
be a Haar measure of
\widetilde{G}
. A representation
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})
is called a discrete series if every matrix coefficient
f_{v,\lambda}
of
\pi
satisfies

\int_{\widetilde{G}}\left|f_{v,\lambda}(g)\right|^{2}\mu_{\widetilde{G}}(g)<\infty.

Let
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})
be a discrete series representation. Then there exists
\deg(\pi,\mu_{\widetilde{G}})\in\mathbbm{R}_{>0}
such that

(2.2)

\int_{\widetilde{G}}f_{v_{1},\lambda_{1}}(g)\cdot f_{v_{2},\lambda_{2}}(g^{-1})\ \mu_{\widetilde{G}}(g)=\frac{{\left\langle v_{2},\lambda_{1}\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle v_{1},\lambda_{2}\right\rangle}}{\deg(\pi,\mu_{\widetilde{G}})}

for all
v_{i},\lambda_{i}
, see
[
Ren10
, Chapitre IV3.3]
or
[
HC70
, Page 5]
. In this case, the number
\deg(\pi,\mu_{\widetilde{G}})
is called the formal degree of
\pi
with respect to the measure
\mu_{\widetilde{G}}
. Note that for any
c\in\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
,

\deg(\pi,c\cdot\mu_{\widetilde{G}})=c^{-1}\cdot\deg(\pi,\mu_{\widetilde{G}}).

3.
Parameters for genuine Iwahori-spherical representations

The goal of this section is to define a Deligne–Langlands correspondence for irreducible genuine Iwahori-spherical representations of
\widetilde{G}
, by using a correspondence for linear algebraic groups already established by Kazhdan–Lusztig
[
KL2
]
and Reeder
[
Ree4
]
.

3.1.
Structure of
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)

Henceforth, we assume that
p\nmid n
and fix a splitting
s_{K}
of
\widetilde{G}
over
K:={\bf G}(O_{F})
. Let
I\subset K
be the Iwahori subgroup associated with
{\bf B}(\kappa_{F})
, where
\kappa_{F}
is the residue field of
F
. We view
K,I\subset\widetilde{G}
via
s_{K}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I):=C_{\bar{\epsilon},c}^{\infty}(I\backslash\widetilde{G}/I)
the genuine Iwahori–Hecke algebra consisting of
\bar{\epsilon}
-genuine locally constant compactly supported functions
f
on
\widetilde{G}
which are
I
-biinvariant, i.e.,
f
satisfies

f(\zeta agb)=\bar{\epsilon}(\zeta)\cdot f(g)

for all
\zeta\in\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}
and
a,b\in I
. Throughout the paper, we fix the Haar measure
\mu_{\widetilde{G}}
on
\widetilde{G}
such that

\mu_{\widetilde{G}}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}\times I)=1.

The algebra multiplication on
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
is given by the convolution
f_{1}*f_{2}
.

To recall the structure of
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
, we consider

N_{Q,n}:=N_{\widetilde{G}}({\bf T}(O_{F})),

the normalizer of
{\bf T}(O_{F})
inside
\widetilde{G}
.
The group
N_{Q,n}
sits in a short exact sequence

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}\times{\bf T}(O_{F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{Q,n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wp}

where

\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}:=Y_{Q,n}\rtimes W

is the modified extended affine Weyl group. It is known that the support of
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
is
IN_{Q,n}I
, see
[
Sav04
]
.

Let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(Z(\widetilde{T}))
be a Weyl-invariant distinguished genuine character as discussed in §
2.2
. In the tame setting when
p\nmid n
, we may further assume that
\theta
is unramified, i.e., trivial on
Z(\widetilde{T})\cap{\bf T}(O_{F})
. Following Savin
[
Sav04
, Page 125]
, to every

one can naturally associate an element

e_{w}^{\theta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I).

We recall the definition of
e_{w}^{\theta}
. First, since
\theta
is
W
-invariant and trivial on the intersection of
{\bf T}(O_{F})
and the subgroup of
K
generated by
\left\{\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}(-1):\alpha\in\Delta\right\}
, we can extend
\theta
to get

\theta:N_{Q,n}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}

such that
\theta(\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}(1))=1
for every
\alpha\in\Delta
. Here
\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}(t)=\widetilde{e}_{\alpha}(t)\widetilde{e}_{-\alpha}(t^{-1})\widetilde{e}_{\alpha}(t)\in\widetilde{G}
is the element defined by using the canonical unipotent lifting
\widetilde{e}_{\alpha}(\cdot)
of
e_{\alpha}(\cdot)
. For every
w\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
, let
\widetilde{w}:=\widetilde{w}_{\alpha_{1}}(1)...\widetilde{w}_{\alpha_{k}}(1)\in N_{Q,n}
be the natural representative of
w=w_{\alpha_{1}}...w_{\alpha_{k}}
written in a minimal decomposition.
Then one has a unique element
e_{w}^{\theta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
supported on
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}I\widetilde{w}I
satisfying

(3.2)

e_{w}^{\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}\theta^{-1}(x)&\text{ if }x\in\wp^{-1}(w),\\
0&\text{ if }x\in N_{Q,n}-\wp^{-1}(w).\end{cases}

Let
W_{\rm ex}:=Y\rtimes W
be the extended affine Weyl group associated with
G
. Recall the length function

l_{G}:W_{\rm ex}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{N}

that is explicitly given by

(3.3)

l_{G}(w)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}(s)}\left|{\left\langle\alpha,y\right\rangle}\right|+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}(s)}\left|{\left\langle\alpha,y\right\rangle}+1\right|,

where
w=(y,s)\in W_{\rm ex}
with
y\in Y,s\in W
and
\Phi^{+}(s)
(resp.
\Phi^{-}(s)
) consists of all positive roots such that
s^{-1}(\alpha)>0
(resp.
s^{-1}(\alpha)<0
). For every
w_{1},w_{2}\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
, we have

(3.4)

e_{w_{1}}^{\theta}*e_{w_{2}}^{\theta}=e_{w_{1}w_{2}}^{\theta}

whenever
l_{G}(w_{1}w_{2})=l_{G}(w_{1})+l_{G}(w_{2})
.

An element
y\in Y
is called positive or dominant if
{\left\langle y,\alpha\right\rangle}\geqslant 0
for every simple root
\alpha
. The element
e_{y}^{\theta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
is invertible for positive
y\in Y_{Q,n}
. Consider

\rho_{G}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha,

the sum taken over all positive roots of
G
.
For every
y\in Y_{Q,n}
, we write

t_{y}^{\theta}:=q^{-{\left\langle y,\rho_{G}\right\rangle}}e_{y_{1}}^{\theta}\cdot(e_{y_{2}}^{\theta})^{-1},

where
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y_{Q,n}
are any two positive elements such that
y=y_{1}-y_{2}
. The elements in
\left\{e_{w}^{\theta}:w\in W\right\}
and
\left\{t_{y}^{\theta}:y\in Y_{Q,n}\right\}
then give the Bernstein presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
as follows, due to G. Savin
[
Sav88
,
Sav04
]
(see also
[

,
GG
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

The Iwahori–Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
has the following description:

\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)=\big\langle t_{y}^{\theta},e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}:\ y\in Y_{Q,n},\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta^{\vee}\big\rangle

with relations given by

(i)

(e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}-q)(e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}+1)=0
.

(ii)

(e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}e_{w_{\beta}}^{\theta})^{r}=(e_{w_{\beta}}^{\theta}e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta})^{r}
if
w_{\alpha}w_{\beta}
is of order
2r
.

(iii)

(e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}e_{w_{\beta}}^{\theta})^{r}e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}=(e_{w_{\beta}}^{\theta}e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta})^{r}e_{w_{\beta}}^{\theta}
if
w_{\alpha}w_{\beta}
is of order
2r+1
.

(iv)

t_{y}^{\theta}\cdot t_{y^{\prime}}^{\theta}=t_{y+y^{\prime}}^{\theta}
.

(v)

Write
\langle y,\alpha\rangle=mn_{\alpha}
. Then

e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}\cdot t_{y}^{\theta}=\begin{cases}t_{y^{w_{\alpha}}}^{\theta}\cdot e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}+(q-1)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}t_{y-kn_{\alpha}\alpha^{\vee}}^{\theta}&\text{if }m>0,\\
t_{y}^{\theta}\cdot e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}&\text{if }m=0,\\
t_{y^{w_{\alpha}}}^{\theta}\cdot e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}-(q-1)\sum_{k=0}^{1-m}t_{y+kn_{\alpha}\alpha^{\vee}}^{\theta}&\text{if }m<0.\end{cases}

In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)\simeq_{\theta}\mathbbm{C}[Y_{Q,n}]\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathcal{H}_{W}
as
\mathbbm{C}
-vector spaces, where the subalgebra
\mathcal{H}_{W}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
is generated by
\left\{e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta}:\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta\right\}
and the commutative subalgebra
\mathbbm{C}[Y_{Q,n}]
generated by
\left\{t_{y}^{\theta}:y\in Y_{Q,n}\right\}
.

Let
G_{Q,n}
be the linear algebraic group whose root datum is given by

(X_{Q,n},\Phi_{Q,n};Y_{Q,n},\Phi_{Q,n}^{\vee}).

In particular, we have

G_{Q,n}^{\vee}\simeq\widetilde{G}^{\vee},

where
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}
is the Langlands dual of
G_{Q,n}
. Note that
\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}=Y_{Q,n}\rtimes W
is then the extended affine Weyl group for
G_{Q,n}
. Also,
G_{Q,n}
has a natural choice of simple roots and simple coroots inherited from the choice of
\Delta
.
Let
I_{Q,n}\subset G_{Q,n}
be the Iwahori subgroup of
G_{Q,n}
, similar as
I\subset G
.
In the Iwahori–Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})
, one has the natural element

E_{w}\in\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})

for every

w\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
, as an analogue of
e_{w}^{\theta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
. Consider

\rho_{G_{Q,n}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha_{Q,n}>0}\alpha_{Q,n},

the half sum of positive roots of
G_{Q,n}
. For every
y\in Y_{Q,n}
, we define

T_{y}:=q^{-{\left\langle y,\rho_{G_{Q,n}}\right\rangle}}E_{y_{1}}\cdot E_{y_{2}}^{-1},

where
y=y_{1}-y_{2}
with
y_{1},y_{2}\geqslant 0
. Then

\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})\simeq\mathbbm{C}[Y_{Q,n}]\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathcal{H}_{W}

and is generated by
\left\{E_{w_{\alpha}}:\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta\right\}
and
\left\{T_{y}:y\in Y_{Q,n}\right\}
with relations exactly in parallel with Theorem
3.1
(i)–(v). Thus, depending on the choice of the distinguished genuine character
\theta
of
Z(\overline{T})
, we have an algebra isomorphism

(3.5)

\varphi_{\theta}:\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})

given by

\varphi_{\theta}(e_{w_{\alpha}}^{\theta})=E_{w_{\alpha}}\text{ and }\varphi_{\theta}(t_{y}^{\theta})=T_{y}.

This induces a natural map

(3.6)

\varphi_{\theta}^{*}:{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I))\longrightarrow{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))

of irreducible left modules over the two algebras.

3.2.
LLC for
{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})^{I}

Let

{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})^{I}\subset{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})

be the subset of irreducible genuine Iwahori-spherical representations
\pi
of
\widetilde{G}
, i.e.,
\pi^{I}\neq\left\{0\right\}
.
By Borel and Casselman, one has a natural bijection

(3.7)

\mathscr{E}:{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})^{I}\longrightarrow{\rm Irr}_{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I))

given by

\pi\mapsto\pi^{I},

see
[
Sav04
,
FlKa86
]
for a discussion for covers.

We will use
\varphi_{\theta}^{*}
in (
3.6
) to transport a local Langlands correspondence for Iwahori-spherical representations of
G_{Q,n}
to the cover
\widetilde{G}
. Thus, we recall a Deligne–Langlands correspondence

\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}:{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))\longrightarrow{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},G_{Q,n}^{\vee})

established by Kazhdan–Lusztig
[
KL2
]
for
G_{Q,n}
with connected center, and extended to the general case by Reeder
[
Ree4
]
. See also
[
ABPS17
]
for a discussion of the local Langlands correspondence for constituents of (not necessarily unramified) principal series of a linear algebraic group. Here
{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},G_{Q,n}^{\vee})
consists of pairs
(\phi,\tau)
, where

(3.8)

\phi:\text{WD}_{F}\longrightarrow G_{Q,n}^{\vee}

is a (
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}
-conjugacy class of) group homomorphism that is trivial on the inertial subgroup
I_{F}\subset W_{F}
, and
\tau\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{\phi})
with

\mathcal{S}_{\phi}:=\pi_{0}({\rm Cent}_{G_{Q,n}^{\vee}}(\phi)/Z(G_{Q,n}^{\vee})).

Such an
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}
is injective and satisfies several natural properties. A pair
(\phi,\tau)
lies in the image of
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}
if and only if
\tau
occurs in certain homology space given as follows.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
denote the variety of Borel subgroups of
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}
, and let
\mathfrak{B}^{\phi}\subset\mathfrak{B}
denote the subvariety consisting of Borel subgroups of
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}
containing

\phi(W_{F}\times B_{2}),

where
B_{2}\subset{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})
is the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices. The group
{\rm Cent}_{G_{Q,n}^{\vee}}(\phi)
acts naturally on
\mathfrak{B}^{\phi}
and thus on the homology
H_{*}(\mathfrak{B}^{\phi},\mathbbm{C})
. The action factors through
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}
and gives a representation of
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}
on
H_{*}(\mathfrak{B}^{\phi},\mathbbm{C})
. The requirement on
\tau\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{\phi})
such that
(\phi,\tau)
lies in the image of
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}
is that it appears in
H_{*}(\mathfrak{B}^{\phi},\mathbbm{C})
: such
\tau
is called geometric.

Consider

{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}_{\rm geo}(\text{WD}_{F},G_{Q,n}^{\vee}):=\left\{(\phi,\tau):\ \tau\text{ is geometric}\right\}\subset{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},G_{Q,n}^{\vee}).

Then one has a bijection

(3.9)

\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm DL}:{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))\longrightarrow{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}_{\rm geo}(\text{WD}_{F},G_{Q,n}^{\vee})

arising from
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}
.

We also recall the set
{\rm Par}^{\rm KLR}(G_{Q,n}^{\vee})
of Kazhdan–Lusztig–Reeder (KLR) parameters, which are often convenient to compute. Consider the pair
(s,u)
, where
s\in T_{Q,n}^{\vee}
and
u\in G_{Q,n}^{\vee}
is unipotent such that

sus^{-1}=u^{q}.

We have the component group

\mathcal{S}_{s,u}:=\pi_{0}({\rm Cent}_{G_{Q,n}^{\vee}}(s,u)/Z(G_{Q,n}^{\vee}))

of
s,u
. A KLR parameter is just
(s,u,\tau)
with
\tau\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{s,u})
. Let
\mathfrak{B}^{s,u}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be the subvariety consisting of all Borel subgroups of
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}
that contain both
s
and
u
. Again,
{\rm Cent}_{G_{Q,n}^{\vee}}(s,u)
acts naturally on
H_{*}(\mathfrak{B}^{s,u},\mathbbm{C})
and the action factors through
\mathcal{S}_{s,u}
. A representation
\tau\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{s,u})
is called geometric if it appears in
H_{*}(\mathfrak{B}^{s,u},\mathbbm{C})
.
This gives the subset

{\rm Par}^{\rm KLR}_{\rm geo}(G_{Q,n}^{\vee})\subset{\rm Par}^{\rm KLR}(G_{Q,n}^{\vee})

consisting of
(s,u,\tau)
where
\tau
is geometric.

The map

\phi\mapsto(s_{\phi},u_{\phi}),

where

s_{\phi}:=\phi\left(\varpi,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q^{1/2}&0\\
0&q^{-1/2}\end{array}\right)\right)\text{ and }u_{\phi}:=\phi\left(1,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\right),

gives a bijection between the
\phi
as in (
3.8
) and such pair
(s,u)
. If
\phi
corresponds to
(s,u)
as above, then

\mathcal{S}_{s,u}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{\phi}.

Thus, one has a bijection

{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}_{\rm geo}(\text{WD}_{F},G_{Q,n}^{\vee})\longrightarrow{\rm Par}^{\rm KLR}_{\rm geo}(G_{Q,n}^{\vee})

given by
(\phi,\tau)\mapsto(s_{\phi},u_{\phi},\tau)
. Accordingly, one has a bijection

\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm KLR}:{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))\longrightarrow{\rm Par}^{\rm KLR}_{\rm geo}(G_{Q,n}^{\vee}).

In fact,
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm KLR}
was the form established by Kazhdan–Lusztig
[
KL2
]
and Reeder
[
Ree4
]
.

Since
G_{Q,n}^{\vee}\simeq\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
, we can view

(\phi,\tau)\in{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},\widetilde{G}^{\vee})

and want to define an analogue of such parameters valued in
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
instead of
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
.
For every
\xi:\text{WD}_{F}\longrightarrow{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
, we define

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}:=\pi_{0}({\rm Cent}_{{}^{L}\widetilde{G}}(\xi)/Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})).

Also, denote by
{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},{}^{L}\widetilde{G})
the set of pairs
(\xi,\gamma)
where
\xi
is a group homomorphism as above and
\gamma\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
.

Recall the isomorphism

i_{\theta}:{}^{L}\widetilde{G}\simeq\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\times\text{WD}_{F}

from (
2.1
), which then gives an embedding of
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
given by
g\mapsto i_{\theta}^{-1}(g,1)
, and thus a parameter

i_{\theta}(\phi):\text{WD}_{F}\longrightarrow{}^{L}\widetilde{G}

given by

i_{\theta}(\phi)(a)=i_{\theta}^{-1}(\phi(a),a).

It is easy to see that one has

\mathcal{S}_{i_{\theta}(\phi)}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{\phi}.

By transport of structure, this gives a representation
i_{\theta}(\tau)\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{i_{\theta}(\phi)})
for every
\tau\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{\phi})
. We thus have a map

(3.10)

i_{\theta}^{*}:{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},\widetilde{G}^{\vee})\longrightarrow{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},{}^{L}\widetilde{G})

given by

i_{\theta}^{*}(\phi,\tau):=(i_{\theta}(\phi),i_{\theta}(\tau)).

Consider the following composite

(3.11)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}=i_{\theta}^{*}\circ\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm DL}\circ\varphi_{\theta}^{*}\circ\mathscr{E}:{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},{}^{L}\widetilde{G}),}

where
\mathscr{E},\varphi_{\theta}^{*},\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm DL}
and
i_{\theta}^{*}
are given in (
3.7
), (
3.6
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) respectively. Note that
\mathcal{L}^{\rm DL}_{G_{Q,n}}
is not completely canonical, and the simplest such example arises from the unitary unramified principal series
I(\chi)
of
{\rm SL}_{2}
that is reducible. For more discussions on this issue, see
[
ABPS17
, §14]
. Thus,
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}
is not completely canonical. However, the dependence on
\theta
in the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}
can be eliminated, as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

For a fixed Deligne–Langlands correspondence
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm DL}
of
G_{Q,n}
, the map
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}
is independent of the choice of any unramified distinguished genuine character
\theta
.

Proof.

We first show that the
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
-valued L-parameter associated with
\pi
via
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}
is independent of
\theta
.

Suppose we have unramified distinguished genuine characters
\theta_{j}
of
Z(\widetilde{T})
with
1\leqslant j\leqslant 2
, and thus also KLR parameter
(s_{\theta_{i}},u_{\theta_{i}},\tau_{\theta_{i}})
relative to
\theta_{i}
. Consider the isomorphism

\varphi=\varphi_{\theta_{2}}\circ\varphi_{\theta_{1}}^{-1}:\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}).

To understand the relation between
(s_{\theta_{1}},u_{\theta_{1}},\tau_{\theta_{1}})
and
(s_{\theta_{2}},u_{\theta_{2}},\tau_{\theta_{2}})
, it suffices to look at the effect of
\varphi
on
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm DL}
. By the definition of
e_{w}^{\theta_{i}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
we see that

\varphi(E_{w})=E_{w}

if
w\in W
. On the other hand, for
y\in Y_{Q,n}
dominant, we have

e_{y}^{\theta_{1}}=\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}}(\varpi^{y})\cdot e_{y}^{\theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I).

If
y=x-z
with positive
x,z\in Y_{Q,n}
, then

q^{{\left\langle y,\rho_{G}\right\rangle}}\cdot t_{y}^{\theta_{1}}=e_{x}^{\theta_{1}}\cdot(e_{z}^{\theta_{2}})^{-1}=\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}}(\varpi^{x})\cdot e_{x}^{\theta_{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}}(\varpi^{z})\cdot e_{z}^{\theta_{2}}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}}(\varpi^{y})\cdot t_{y}^{\theta_{2}}\cdot q^{{\left\langle y,\rho_{G}\right\rangle}}.

Thus,
t_{y}^{\theta_{1}}=(\theta_{2}/\theta_{1})(\varpi^{y})\cdot t_{y}^{\theta_{2}}
. Then we get

\varphi(T_{y})=(\theta_{2}/\theta_{1})(\varpi^{y})\cdot T_{y}

for every
y\in Y_{Q,n}
. Associated with
\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}
is an element

z_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}}\in Z(G_{Q,n}^{\vee})\simeq Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})

given by
z_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}}(y):=(\theta_{2}/\theta_{1})(\varpi^{y})
for every
y\in Y_{Q,n}
.
Consider the map

\varphi^{*}:{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))\longrightarrow{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))

induced from
\varphi
. Let
\sigma(s,u,\tau):=(\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm KLR})^{-1}(s,u,\tau)\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))
be associated with any KLR parameter
(s,u,\tau)\in{\rm Par}^{\rm KLR}_{\rm geo}(G_{Q,n}^{\vee})
.
Then we have

(3.12)

\varphi^{*}(\sigma(s,u,\tau))=\sigma(s\cdot z_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}},u,\tau),

see
[
ABPS17
, Page 843, (114)]
. This shows that

(3.13)

s_{\theta_{2}}=s_{\theta_{1}}\cdot z_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}},\quad u_{\theta_{2}}=u_{\theta_{1}},\quad\tau_{\theta_{2}}=\tau_{\theta_{1}}.

Denote by

\phi_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}}:\text{WD}_{F}\to Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}^{\vee}

the unique homomorphism that is trivial on
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})
and
I_{F}\subset W_{F}
, and such that
\phi_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}}({\rm Frob})=z_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}}
. It is easy to see that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\times\text{WD}_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}\widetilde{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{G}^{\vee}\times\text{WD}_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}\widetilde{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\theta_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\theta_{2}}}

commutes, where
f(g,x):=(g\cdot\phi_{\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}}(x),x)
. Combining the relations in (
3.13
) and the above diagram, it follows that

i_{\theta_{1}}(\phi_{s_{\theta_{1}},u_{\theta_{1}}})=i_{\theta_{2}}(\phi_{s_{\theta_{2}},u_{\theta_{2}}}).

This shows that the
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
-valued L-parameter associated with
\pi
via
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}^{\rm DL}
is independent of
\theta
, and we write it as
\phi_{\pi}
.

From the above discussion we have canonical isomorphisms

(3.14)

\mathcal{S}_{s_{\theta_{1}},u}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{\pi}}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{s_{\theta_{2}},u}.

Also,
\tau_{\theta_{1}}
and
\tau_{\theta_{2}}
correspond to each other via the map induced from (
3.14
). Hence, they gives rise to the same element in
{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{\pi}})
.

In view of the equivalence between
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm DL}
and
\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm KLR}
, the above completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
L-functions and gamma-factors

Let

R:{}^{L}\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}(V)

be a finite dimensional algebraic representation afforded on a finite-dimensional complex vector space
V
, i.e., its restriction to
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
is an algebraic group homomorphism. For every
\pi\in{\rm Irr}(\widetilde{G})^{I}
one can define the L-function

L(s,\pi,R):=L(s,R\circ\phi_{\pi}),

where the right hand side denotes the local Artin L-function, see
[
Tat1
,
Roh
]
.
Moreover, Langlands and Deligne defined the
\varepsilon
-factor
\varepsilon(s,R\circ\phi_{\pi},\psi)
, which is uniquely characterized by certain properties (see loc. cit.), and thus one has the gamma-factor

\gamma(s,\pi,R,\psi):=\gamma(s,R\circ\phi_{\pi},\psi)=\varepsilon(s,R\circ\phi_{\pi},\psi)\cdot\frac{L(1-s,R^{\vee}\circ\phi_{\pi})}{L(s,R\circ\phi_{\pi})},

where
R^{\vee}
denotes the contragredient representation of
R
.

By the construction of
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
, one has a canonical isomorphism

{}^{L}\widetilde{G}/Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})\simeq(\widetilde{G}^{\vee}/Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee}))\times\text{WD}_{F}.

Thus if
R={\rm Ad}
is the adjoint representation of
{}^{L}\widetilde{G}
on
{\rm Lie}(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})
, then it factors through

{\rm Ad}^{\dagger}:\widetilde{G}^{\vee}/Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})\longrightarrow{\rm GL}({\rm Lie}(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})).

In particular, this means that for any distinguished genuine character
\theta
one has

(3.15)

L(s,\pi,{\rm Ad})=L(s,{\rm Ad}^{\dagger}\circ\phi_{\pi,\theta}),\quad\gamma(s,\pi,{\rm Ad},\psi)=\gamma(s,{\rm Ad}^{\dagger}\circ\phi_{\pi,\theta})

where
\phi_{\pi,\theta}:=\mathcal{L}_{G_{Q,n}}^{\rm DL}\circ\varphi_{\theta}^{*}\circ\mathscr{E}(\pi)\in{\rm Par}^{\rm DL}(\text{WD}_{F},\widetilde{G}^{\vee})
is the L-parameter of
\pi
relative to
\theta
. Here
\phi_{\pi,\theta}
is valued in
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}
, but we also naturally view it as valued in
\widetilde{G}^{\vee}/Z(\widetilde{G}^{\vee})
by abuse of notation.

4.
Formal degrees and the Hiraga–Ichino–Ikeda formula

We retain the tame setting as in §
3.1
and let
(\sigma,V_{\sigma})
be an irreducible (and thus necessarily finite-dimensional) representation of
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
.
Consider the irreducible genuine Iwahori-spherical representation

\pi_{\sigma}:=\mathscr{E}^{-1}(\sigma)\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G})

afforded by a certain vector space
V_{\pi_{\sigma}}
, where
\mathscr{E}
is as in (
3.7
). Let
(\sigma^{\vee},V_{\sigma}^{\vee})
be the contragredient representation of

specified

{\left\langle v,\sigma^{\vee}(u)(\lambda)\right\rangle}={\left\langle\sigma(\check{u})v,\lambda\right\rangle}

where
\check{u}(g):=u(g^{-1})
for all

. Note that
\check{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G},I)
and thus

is an irreducible representation of
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{G},I)
. Let
(\pi_{\sigma}^{\vee},V_{\pi_{\sigma}}^{\vee})
be the contragredient representation of
(\pi_{\sigma},V_{\pi_{\sigma}})
given in
§
2.3
.

The isomorphism
\varphi_{\theta}
gives rise to an irreducible representation
\varphi_{\theta}^{*}(\sigma)
of
\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})
. For simplicity of notation, we write

\sigma_{\theta}:=\varphi_{\theta}^{*}(\sigma).

This gives
(\sigma_{\theta},V_{\sigma})\in{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n}))
, where the representation
\sigma_{\theta}
is realized on the space
V_{\sigma}
. Let
(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}},V_{\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}})\in{\rm Irr}(G_{Q,n})
be the irreducible
I_{Q,n}
-spherical representation associated with
\sigma_{\theta}
. It is clear that there is a vector space isomorphism

\eta:(\pi_{\sigma})^{I}\longrightarrow(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}})^{I_{Q,n}}

that is equivariant with respect to the
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{G},I)
and
\mathcal{H}(G_{Q,n},I_{Q,n})
actions on the domain and codomain respectively.

We choose
v\in(\pi_{\sigma})^{I}
and set
v^{\prime}:=\eta(v)\in(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}})^{I_{Q,n}}
. The canonical pairing between
\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}
and
\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}^{\vee}
gives an isomorphism (see
[

, Proposition 4.2.5]
)

((\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}})^{I_{Q,n}})^{\vee}\simeq(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}^{\vee})^{I_{Q,n}}.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}\in(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}^{\vee})^{I_{Q,n}}
be such that
{\left\langle v^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}\neq 0
. It gives an element
{\left\langle\eta(\cdot),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}\in((\pi_{\sigma})^{I})^{\vee}
. Again, the canonical isomorphism

((\pi_{\sigma})^{I})^{\vee}\simeq(\pi_{\sigma}^{\vee})^{I}

gives a
\lambda\in(\pi_{\sigma}^{\vee})^{I}
such that
{\left\langle\cdot,\lambda\right\rangle}={\left\langle\eta(\cdot),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}
. That is, we have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\pi_{\sigma})^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}})^{I_{Q,n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{C},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda^{\prime}}

which satisfies

(4.1)

{\left\langle\sigma(u)(\cdot),\lambda\right\rangle}={\left\langle\sigma_{\theta}(\varphi_{\theta}(u))\eta(\cdot),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}

for all

. In particular,
{\left\langle v,\lambda\right\rangle}={\left\langle v^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
v\in\pi_{\sigma}^{I},\lambda\in(\pi_{\sigma}^{\vee})^{I}
as above, one has

\deg(\pi_{\sigma},\mu_{\widetilde{G}})^{-1}=\frac{1}{{\left\langle v,\lambda\right\rangle}^{2}}\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}}q^{-l_{G}(w)}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w}^{\theta})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w^{-1}}^{\theta})(v),\lambda\right\rangle},

where
\mu_{\widetilde{G}}
is the measure of
\widetilde{G}
such that
\mu_{\widetilde{G}}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}\cdot I)=1
.

Proof.

Since
v
and
\lambda
are both
I
-fixed, the matrix coefficients
f_{v,\lambda}
is
I
-biinvariant. Thus, in view of the definition of
\deg(\pi_{\sigma},\mu_{\widetilde{G}})
in (
2.2
), it suffices to show that

(4.2)

\int_{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n}I\widetilde{w}I}{\left\langle\pi_{\sigma}(g^{-1})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle\pi_{\sigma}(g)(v),\lambda\right\rangle}=q^{-l_{G}(w)}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w}^{\theta})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w^{-1}}^{\theta})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}.

The left hand side of (
4.2
) is equal to

q^{l_{G}(w)}\cdot{\left\langle\pi_{\sigma}(\widetilde{w}^{-1})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle\pi_{\sigma}(\widetilde{w})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w}^{\theta}),\lambda\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{G}}e_{w}^{\theta}(g)\cdot{\left\langle\pi_{\sigma}(g)(v),\lambda\right\rangle}\ \mu_{\widetilde{G}}(g)

\displaystyle=q^{l_{G}(w)}\cdot\theta^{-1}(\widetilde{w})\cdot{\left\langle\pi_{\sigma}(\widetilde{w}),\lambda\right\rangle}.

Similarly, we have

{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w^{-1}}^{\theta}),\lambda\right\rangle}=q^{l_{G}(w)}\cdot\theta(\widetilde{w})\cdot{\left\langle\pi_{\sigma}(\widetilde{w}^{-1}),\lambda\right\rangle}.

Combining the above, one obtains (
4.2
) and completes the proof.
∎

Recall that
\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}=Y_{Q,n}\times W
is the extended affine Weyl group of
G_{Q,n}
. Thus, one has the length function

l_{G_{Q,n}}:\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{N}

which is explicitly given by

(4.3)

l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}(s)}\left|{\left\langle n_{\alpha}^{-1}\alpha,y\right\rangle}\right|+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}(s)}\left|{\left\langle n_{\alpha}^{-1}\alpha,y\right\rangle}+1\right|.

Here
w=(y,s)\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
with
y\in Y_{Q,n},s\in W
and
\Phi^{\pm}(s)
are the same as in (
3.3
). It follows from Lemma
4.1
that for
\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}}\in{\rm Irr}(G_{Q,n})
one has analogously the equality

\deg(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}},\mu_{G_{Q,n}})^{-1}=\frac{1}{{\left\langle v^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}^{2}}\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}}q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma_{\theta}(E_{w})(v^{\prime}),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma_{\theta}(E_{w^{-1}})(v^{\prime}),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle},

where
\mu_{G_{Q,n}}
is the measure such that
\mu_{G_{Q,n}}(I_{Q,n})=1
. In fact, the right hand side of the above equality is often taken as the definition of formal degree of discrete series representation of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of a linear group, see
[
Mat77-B
, Page 37]
or the discussion in
[
FeOp20
, §4.1]
.

The following proposition plays a crucial role in the comparison between
\deg(\pi_{\sigma},\mu_{\widetilde{G}})
and
\deg(\pi_{\sigma_{\theta}},\mu_{G_{Q,n}})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every
w\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
, one has

(4.4)

q^{-l_{G}(w)/2}\cdot\varphi_{\theta}(e_{w}^{\theta})=q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)/2}\cdot E_{w}

and thus

(4.5)

q^{-l_{G}(w)}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w}^{\theta})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w^{-1}}^{\theta})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}=q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma_{\theta}(E_{w})(v^{\prime}),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma_{\theta}(E_{w^{-1}})(v^{\prime}),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}.

Proof.

We check (
4.4
) in three steps by considering:

(i)

when
w\in W
,

(ii)

when
w=y\in Y_{Q,n}^{+}
is dominant,

(iii)

when
w=(y,s)\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
is a general element.

First, the equality (
4.4
) in case (i) follows from (
4.1
) and the fact that
l_{G}(w)=l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)
and
\varphi_{\theta}(e_{w}^{\theta})=E_{w}
for
w\in W
. For case (ii) we note that for
y\in Y_{Q,n}^{+}
dominant, one has
\varphi_{\theta}(t_{y}^{\theta})=T_{y}

and thus

q^{-{\left\langle y,\rho_{G}\right\rangle}}\cdot\varphi_{\theta}(e_{y}^{\theta})=q^{-{\left\langle y,\rho_{G_{Q,n}}\right\rangle}}\cdot E_{y}.

Since
l_{G}(y)=2{\left\langle y,\rho_{G}\right\rangle}
and similarly
l_{G_{Q,n}}(y)=2{\left\langle y,\rho_{G_{Q,n}}\right\rangle}
, the equality (
4.4
) is clear in this case.

Now we consider case (iii) and thus a general
w=(y,s)\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}=Y_{Q,n}\rtimes W
with
y\in Y_{Q,n}
and
s\in W
. We write for brevity
w=ys
and recall an argument from the proof of
[
Sav04
, Proposition 6.3]
. If
y
is not positive, then there exists a simple root
\alpha
such that
{\left\langle\alpha,y\right\rangle}<0
. Using the formula of
l_{G}
in (
3.3
), we have

l_{G}(w_{\alpha}\cdot w)=l_{G}(w)+1.

Inductively, one can find
s^{\prime}\in W
such that
s^{\prime}w=zs^{\prime\prime}
with
z\in Y_{Q,n}^{+}
and
s^{\prime\prime}\in W
satisfying

(4.6)

l_{G}(s^{\prime}w)=l_{G}(z)+l_{G}(s^{\prime\prime})=l_{G}(s^{\prime})+l_{G}(w).

Note that
{\left\langle\alpha,y\right\rangle}<0
if and only if
{\left\langle n_{\alpha}^{-1}\alpha,y\right\rangle}<0
, and thus one also has
l_{G_{Q,n}}(w_{\alpha}\cdot w)=l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)+1
by checking (
4.3
) instead. Hence, for the above
s^{\prime}\in W
it also gives

(4.7)

l_{G_{Q,n}}(s^{\prime}w)=l_{G_{Q,n}}(z)+l_{G_{Q,n}}(s^{\prime\prime})=l_{G_{Q,n}}(s^{\prime})+l_{G_{Q,n}}(w).

It follows from (
3.4
) and (
4.6
) that

q^{-l_{G}(s^{\prime})/2}q^{-l_{G}(w)/2}\cdot\varphi_{\theta}(e_{s^{\prime}}^{\theta})\cdot\varphi_{\theta}(e_{w}^{\theta})=q^{-l_{G}(z)/2}q^{-l_{G}(s^{\prime\prime})/2}\cdot\varphi_{\theta}(e_{z}^{\theta})\cdot\varphi_{\theta}(e_{s^{\prime\prime}}^{\theta}).

Similarly, by using (
3.4
) and (
4.7
) one has

q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(s^{\prime})/2}q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)/2}\cdot E_{s^{\prime}}\cdot E_{w}=q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(z)/2}q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(s^{\prime\prime})/2}\cdot E_{z}\cdot E_{s^{\prime\prime}}.

Applying (i) above to
s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}
and (ii) to
z
, and noting the fact that
E_{s^{\prime}}
is invertible, we obtain (
4.4
) for
w
.

Lastly, equality (
4.4
) coupled with (
4.1
) give

(4.8)

q^{-l_{G}(w)/2}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma(e_{w}^{\theta})(v),\lambda\right\rangle}=q^{-l_{G_{Q,n}}(w)/2}\cdot{\left\langle\sigma_{\theta}(E_{w})(v^{\prime}),\lambda^{\prime}\right\rangle}

for every
w\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
. A similar equality holds for
w^{-1}
in place of
w
. We have
l_{G}(w)=l_{G}(w^{-1})
for every
w\in\widetilde{W}_{\rm ex}
, which then gives (
4.5
). This completes the proof.
∎

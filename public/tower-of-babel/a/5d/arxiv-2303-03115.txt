Casimir-Lifshitz force for moving graphene
Mauro Antezza, Natalia Emelianova, Nail Khusnutdinov

Abstract
We consider the system of two parallel sheets of graphene which are moving with relative parallel velocity 𝒗 \bm{v} and calculate the Casimir energy by using the scattering approach. The energy has real and imaginary parts. The real part contains a velocity contribution to the force perpendicular to the sheets. The imaginary part is related to a friction force parallel to the graphene sheets. We prove that the friction appears if the relative velocity becomes greater than the velocity of Fermi and if the modulo of reflection coefficient is greater than the one which is significant for virtual photon production. We analyze in detail the real contribution to the Casimir energy for two systems – graphene/graphene and ideal metal/graphene. In the non-relativistic case v ≪ v F v\ll v_{F} , the relative correction to the Casimir energy ( ℰ v − ℰ 0 ) / ℰ 0 (\mathcal{E}_{v}-\mathcal{E}_{0})/\mathcal{E}_{0} is proportional to the ( v / c ) 2 (v/c)^{2} (the maximum value is 0.0033 0.0033 for the gapeless case and v = v F v=v_{F} ) for the first system, and it is zero up to the Fermi velocity v = v F v=v_{F} for system ideal metal/graphene.

Introduction

Nowadays, the Casimir effect
Casimir 1948
;
*Lifshitz:1956:tmafbs
;
*Dzyaloshinskii:1961:gtvwf
is a well-known and experimentally observed phenomenon – the macroscopic evidence of the microscopic quantum origin of fields and matter (see, for example, recent review
Woods et al. 2016
and book
Bordag et al. 2009a
). The Casimir effect for real materials depends on the physical properties of solids and shapes of their boundaries, temperatures of bodies and fields, chemical potential, etc.
Bordag et al. 2009a
. The different temperatures of the solids lead to heat transfer, and the non-equilibrium Casimir effect
Antezza et al. 2004
;
*Antezza:2005:nabotsafoote
;
*Pitaevskii:2006:LbsCfote
;
*Obrecht:2007:mottdotcpf
;
*Antezza:2008:clfoote
takes place. The new materials such as graphene
Klimchitskaya et al. 2022
;
*Abbas:2017:staemotcfigm
, Weyl semi-metals
Wilson et al. 2015
;
*Rodriguez-Lopez:2020:scorCitIaIIWs
;
*Farias:2020:cfbwscm
, and topological matter
Lu 2021
demonstrate the great diversity of properties of the Casimir effect.

Graphene is a mono-atomic two-dimensional layer of Carbon atoms, and the main role of the Casimir effect for graphene plays its
2D
conductivity tensor. In the frameworks of Kubo approach it was calculated in Ref.
Gusynin and Sharapov 2006
and by using
(2+1)
D quantum electrodynamics in Ref.
Bordag et al. 2009b
;
*Fialkovsky:2011:FCefg
.

The Casimir force depends on the relative velocity of slabs, too. The uniform motion perpendicular to the slabs was considered in Ref.
Bordag et al. 1984
;
*Bordag:1986:ceumm
for scalar field and electromagnetic fields (see also the recent review
Dodonov 2020
on the dynamical Casimir effect). The relative movement with velocity parallel to the slabs gives two different contributions to the Casimir force – perpendicular and parallel to the slabs. The first one, for dielectric layers, was considered in Ref.
Maslovski 2011
, where repulsive Casimir force was observed. The second contribution is called van der Waals/Casimir (quantum or non-contact) friction. It was investigated for a long time (see, for example,
Pendry 1997
;
*Philbin:2009:nqfbump
;
*Brevik:2022:feaoe
and reference therein and the recent review
Volokitin and Persson 2007
).

The Casimir friction for graphene was considered in Ref.
Farias et al. 2017
;
*Farias:2015:fatqfeaadf
in the framework of effective action. It was obtained that the friction appears as an imaginal part of effective action and the threshold for the friction force appears – it is zero if the relative velocity is smaller than the velocity Fermi. This statement is in agreement with Ref.
Maghrebi et al. 2013a
where it was shown that a frictional force arises when the relative velocity exceeds that of light in the medium. The Dirac electron is described by the Dirac equation with velocity Fermi instead of the speed of light.

In this paper, we use the following approach to obtain the Casimir energy of graphene moving with relative parallel velocity. By using the Lorentz transformation of graphene’s conductivity tensor
Bordag et al. 2009b
we obtain the tensor of the moving graphene and then use the formulas obtained in Ref.
Fialkovsky et al. 2018
to obtain the real and imaginary parts of the Casimir energy and the force. To do this we rotate the contour of integration from real frequencies to imaginary ones. The integration over the imaginary axis gives the real contribution to the Casimir force perpendicular to the sheets of graphene. The specific complex frequencies produce an imaginary contribution which is the Casimir friction – the force parallel to the sheets. In this paper, we consider the real contribution, only.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we consider the Lorentz transformation for the action. In Sec.
III
the tensor of conductivity of moving graphene in the laboratory frame is derived. We use the definition of conductivity as a boundary condition in the form of Ohm low and the Lorentz transformation to the laboratory frame.

The Sec.
IV

\xi=-\mathrm{i}\omega
(Sec.
IV.1
) gives the
real
contribution, only to the energy. This statement is the consequence of the general relations for the conductivity tensor. These relations are proved in the Appendix
A

contribution to the energy and force may come from the last part, only (Sec.
IV.2
) which has the form of contour integrals along close contours in the upper and down half-planes of complex frequencies
\omega
which corresponds to the opposite direction of scattering. The non-zero contributions from these integrals exist if some specific condition is satisfied. This condition in the scalar form has been noted by Lifshitz in Ref.
Lifshitz 1956
and also in Ref.
Kampen et al. 1968
;
*Gerlach:1971:eovdwfbsatspi

In this section, we proved the threshold for velocity – the imaginary contribution to the energy is zero up to the velocity of Fermi. We proved also that this contribution takes pales if and only if the modulo of the reflections coefficient is greater than 1, that is the photon production has to appear.

In Sec.
V
we consider in detail the velocity correction to the perpendicular force, due to the real part of the energy, and for two systems – graphene/graphene and graphene/ideal metal cases.

The direct calculations of the boosted polarization tensor are shown in Appendix

\displaystyle=\bm{\eta}^{*}_{2}(\omega,-\bm{v}).

(40)

Taking into account these relations, only, it is easy to show that the
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is real (see Appendix
A
) and

\mathcal{E}_{2}=\Re\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\ln\det\left[\mathbf{I}-e^{-2ak_{E}}\bm{\mathcal{R}}(\mathrm{i}k_{E})\right].

(41)

This expression has represented in Ref.
Fialkovsky et al. 2018
in the following manifest form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\ln\left(1+e^{-4ak_{E}}\frac{\xi^{2}k_{E}^{2}}{b_{1}b_{2}}\det\bm{\eta}_{1}\det\bm{\eta}_{2}\right.

\displaystyle-\left.e^{-2ak_{E}}\left[\frac{\xi^{2}k_{E}^{2}}{b_{1}b_{2}}\left[(1-\det\bm{\eta}_{1})(1-\det\bm{\eta}_{2})+\det(\bm{\eta}_{1}-\bm{\eta}_{2})\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{\xi k_{E}}{b_{1}}-\frac{\xi k_{E}}{b_{2}}+1\right]\right),

(42)

where
b_{i}=\xi^{2}\tr\bm{\eta}_{i}+(\bm{k}\bm{k}\bm{\eta}_{i})+\xi k_{E}\bigl(1+\det\bm{\eta}_{i}\bigr)
.

IV.2
\mathcal{E}_{1}
: The Casimir friction

Let us proceed to the last contribution which comes from the integrals over closed contours
\gamma_{\pm}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}}\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\{\oint_{\gamma_{-}}\mathrm{d}\omega\ln\det\left[\mathbf{I}-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\bm{\mathcal{R}}(-k_{3})\right]\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\oint_{\gamma_{+}}\mathrm{d}\omega\ln\det\left[\mathbf{I}-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\bm{\mathcal{R}}(k_{3})\right]\right\}.

(43)

This part is the source of the Casimir friction because it is imaginary. The contribution to pressure reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}=\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{\oint_{\gamma_{-}}\mathrm{d}\omega k_{3}\frac{e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}(\tr\bm{\mathcal{R}}(-k_{3})-2e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\det\bm{\mathcal{R}}(-k_{3}))}{\det\left[\mathbf{I}-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\bm{\mathcal{R}}(-k_{3})\right]}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\oint_{\gamma_{+}}\mathrm{d}\omega k_{3}\frac{e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}(\tr\bm{\mathcal{R}}(k_{3})-2e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\det\bm{\mathcal{R}}(k_{3}))}{\det\left[\mathbf{I}-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\bm{\mathcal{R}}(k_{3})\right]}\right\}.

(44)

Therefore, the imaginary part of force may appear due to zeros of the denominator

\det\left[\mathbf{I}-e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\bm{r}^{\prime}_{1}(\pm k_{3})\bm{r}_{2}(\pm k_{3})\right]=0,

(45)

as residues in these points.

For the scalar Fresnel coefficients these poles have been noted by Lifshitz in Ref.
Lifshitz 1956
when the integration over real axes is changed to the imaginary one (see also
Kampen et al. 1968
;
*Gerlach:1971:eovdwfbsatspi
). In the Ref.
Henkel et al. 2004
(see also review
Joulain et al. 2005
) noted that the roots of this relation in the scalar case may appear in the complex plane, only. Lifshitz
Lifshitz 1956
proved an absence of poles for normal dielectrics.

Let us suppose that the matrix
\bm{\mathcal{R}}(\pm k_{3})=\bm{r}^{\prime}_{1}(\pm k_{3})\bm{r}_{2}(\pm k_{3})
has eigenvalues
r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})
and
r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})
, which means that the following relation is valid

\bm{\mathcal{R}}(\pm k_{3})=\mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{\pm}\mathbf{T},

(46)

where

\mathbf{D}_{\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})&0\\
0&r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})\end{pmatrix}.

(47)

For
v=0
, the
r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}
and
r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}
are the squares of reflection coefficients of TM and TE modes respectively for a system of two planes with the corresponding sign of
k_{3}
.

Taking into account Eq. (
46
) and relation

\oint_{\gamma_{-}}\mathrm{d}\omega k_{3}+\oint_{\gamma_{+}}\mathrm{d}\omega k_{3}=0,

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}=\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}

\displaystyle\left\{\oint_{\gamma_{-}}\mathrm{d}\omega k_{3}\left[\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(-k_{3})}+\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(-k_{3})}\right]\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\oint_{\gamma_{+}}\mathrm{d}\omega k_{3}\left[\frac{1}{1-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(k_{3})}+\frac{1}{1-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(k_{3})}\right]\right\}.

(48)

Therefore, the contributions to these integrals come from the roots

1-e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})=0,\ 1-e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})=0,

(49)

as residues in these poles if the poles are inside the corresponding contour. For real frequencies, these relations describe the waveguide modes
Bordag 2012
, which are the evanescent modes with
\omega<k
. A similar relation was considered in Ref.
Silveirinha 2014
.

Let us denote the solutions of equations

1-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(-k_{3})=0,\ 1-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(-k_{3})=0,

(50)

by
\omega^{n-}_{\mathrm{tm}},\omega^{n-}_{\mathrm{te}}
with negative imaginary parts
\Im\omega^{n-}_{\mathrm{tm}}<0
,
\Im\omega^{n-}_{\mathrm{te}}<0
, and the solutions of equations

1-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(k_{3})=0,\ 1-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(k_{3})=0,

(51)

by
\omega^{n+}_{\mathrm{tm}},\omega^{n+}_{\mathrm{te}}
with positive imaginary parts
\Im\omega^{n+}_{\mathrm{tm}}>0
,
\Im\omega^{n+}_{\mathrm{te}}>0
. Then, the pressure reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\sum_{n}\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[\underset{\omega=\omega^{n+}_{\mathrm{tm}}}{\Res}\frac{k_{3}}{1-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(k_{3})}-\underset{\omega=\omega^{n-}_{\mathrm{tm}}}{\Res}\frac{k_{3}}{1-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(-k_{3})}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\underset{\omega=\omega^{n+}_{\mathrm{te}}}{\Res}\frac{k_{3}}{1-e^{2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(k_{3})}-\underset{\omega=\omega^{n-}_{\mathrm{te}}}{\Res}\frac{k_{3}}{1-e^{-2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(-k_{3})}\right].

(52)

Let us analyze the general properties of existing poles in the contours following by Lifshitz
Lifshitz 1956
. We represent the relations (
49
) in the following form

e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}=r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{-2}(\pm k_{3}),\ e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}=r_{\mathrm{te}}^{-2}(\pm k_{3}),

(53)

and consider the modulo of both sides.

It is easy to see that
\sign(\Im k_{3})=\sign(\Im\omega)
. Therefore, the modulo of the left-hand side of Eqs. (
53
) reads

\left|e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}\right|=\left(e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}}e^{\mp 2\mathrm{i}ak_{3}^{*}}\right)^{1/2}=e^{\mp 2a\Im k_{3}}=e^{-2a|\Im k_{3}|}<1.

(54)

Therefore, the solutions exist if and only if the relations

\left|r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{-2}(\pm k_{3})\right|<1,\left|r_{\mathrm{te}}^{-2}(\pm k_{3})\right|<1,

(55)

are valid, or

\left|r_{\mathrm{tm}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})\right|>1,\left|r_{\mathrm{te}}^{2}(\pm k_{3})\right|>1.

(56)

It means that it has to be photon production.

Let us consider now the threshold of velocity for the Casimir friction. It was found in the Ref.
Farias et al. 2017
;
*Farias:2015:fatqfeaadf
in the framework of effective action. Here we prove this threshold by using the simple observation that the origin for the Casimir friction is the mixing between positive and negative frequencies
Maghrebi et al. 2013a
.

For graphene at rest,
v=0
, the spectrum is defined by relation
\tilde{k}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}-v_{F}^{2}k^{2}}=0
. We obtain two branches

\omega^{\pm}_{0}/k=\pm v_{F}.

(57)

For moving graphene the spectrum is defined by relation
\tilde{k}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\omega^{\prime 2}-v_{F}^{2}k^{\prime 2}}=0
. We have a quadratic equation

\omega^{2}-v_{F}^{2}k^{2}+\frac{1-v_{F}^{2}}{1-v^{2}}(\omega^{2}v^{2}+(\bm{k}\bm{v})^{2}-2\omega(\bm{k}\bm{v}))=0,

(58)

with solutions

\displaystyle\omega^{\pm}_{v}/k

\displaystyle=v\frac{1-v_{F}^{2}}{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\cos\varphi

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle v_{F}\frac{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\sqrt{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}-v^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})\cos^{2}\varphi}.

(59)

The expression under the square root is always positive. Here, the
\varphi
is the angle between vectors
\bm{k}
and
\bm{v}
. It is easy to verify that
\lim_{v\to 0}\omega_{v}^{\pm}=\omega^{\pm}_{0}
.

To find a condition for mixing the frequencies let us first for simplicity expand (
59
) over velocity
v<1
. We set
v=v_{F}\beta
and expand over
v_{F}\ll 1
:

\omega_{v}^{\pm}/k=v_{F}(\pm 1+\beta\cos\varphi)+O(v_{F}^{3}).

(60)

For
\beta<1\ (v<v_{F})
we observe that
\omega_{v}^{+}>0
and
\omega_{v}^{-}<0
for all
\varphi
, that is there is no mixing between modes. In the opposite case
\beta>1\ (v>v_{F})
, there are domains of angle
\varphi
where
\omega_{v}^{+}<0
or
\omega_{v}^{-}>0
– the mixing appears and therefore the Casimir friction appears.

This statement is valid without expansion over
v
. Indeed, the condition
\omega_{v}^{+}<0
reads

v_{F}\frac{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\sqrt{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}-v^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})\cos^{2}\varphi}<-v\frac{1-v_{F}^{2}}{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\cos\varphi.

The first observation is that
\varphi\in\left[\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2}\right]
, because expression on right hand side should be positive. With this limitation, one can put in a square on both sides and one obtains a condition

\cos^{2}\varphi>1-\frac{v^{2}-v_{F}^{2}}{v^{2}\left(1-v_{F}^{2}\right)}.

(61)

We observe that this relation can not be valid if
v<v_{F}
. For
v>v_{F}
there are domains of
\varphi
where solutions exist and therefore the mixing of modes exists, and the Casimir friction comes up.

The condition
\omega_{v}^{-}>0
reads

v_{F}\frac{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\sqrt{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}-v^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})\cos^{2}\varphi}<v\frac{1-v_{F}^{2}}{1-v^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\cos\varphi.

Therefore,
\varphi\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]
. With this limitation one can put in a square both sides and one obtains the same condition

\cos^{2}\varphi>1-\frac{v^{2}-v_{F}^{2}}{v^{2}\left(1-v_{F}^{2}\right)},

(62)

which has solutions for
v>v_{F}
, only.

Let us consider threshold for photon production. It happens if
Maghrebi et al. 2013a

(63)

From Fig.

VI
Discussion and conclusion

We considered here the Casimir energy for two parallel graphenes with relative velocity
v
in the framework of scattering theory. To obtain the conductivity tensor of moving graphene in the laboratory frame the Lorentz transformation of the boundary conditions is used, which defines the conductivity tensor in the terms of the polarization tensor. The Casimir energy consists of two parts – real (Sec.
IV.1
) and imaginary (Sec.
IV.2
). The real part is a velocity correction to the usual Casimir energy and the imaginary one is the Casimir friction. For both of these energies, the Fermi velocity plays a crucial role. The Casimir friction is zero up to the Fermi velocity, for
v<v_{F}
. This fact is the simple consequence of the observation that the origin for the Casimir friction is the mixing between positive and negative frequencies
Maghrebi et al. 2013a
(see the end of Sec.
IV.2
) and was considered in Ref.
Farias et al. 2017
;
*Farias:2015:fatqfeaadf
in the framework of effective action approach. The Casimir friction appears if the Eqs. (
49
) have corresponding solutions with a non-zero imaginary part of the frequency. It takes place if and only if the modulo of reflection coefficients are greater than one (
56
). The detailed evaluations of the Casimir friction will be considered in a separate paper.

We considered here in detail the real part of the energy for two systems – graphene/graphene and ideal metal/graphene with a relative velocity
v
. In the first system, the velocity correction to the Casimir energy grows up as
v^{2}
(
70
) and then falls after some maximum value (
4
). The position of this maximum is defined by the velocity Fermi and has linear dependence with
(ma)
(
72
). For the system ideal metal/graphene, the energy correction is zero up to some value which has the same form (
75
) and is defined by the velocity Fermi (see Fig.
5
).

Appendix A
Analytical properties of conductivity

To calculate the Casimir energy we have to consider the conductivity as a function of complex frequency
\omega=\omega_{r}+\mathrm{i}\omega_{i}
. The general property of permittivity
(
Landau and Lifshitz 1984
, §82)
is

\bm{\varepsilon}(-\omega^{*})=\bm{\varepsilon}^{*}(\omega).

(77)

For conductors
\bm{\varepsilon}(\omega)=\mathbf{I}+2\mathrm{i}\bm{\eta}(\omega)/\omega
, and

\bm{\eta}(-\omega^{*})=\bm{\eta}^{*}(\omega).

(78)

A graphene at rest has the following conductivity

\bm{\eta}_{1}=\eta_{\mathrm{gr}}\frac{\tilde{k}}{\omega}\left(\mathbf{I}+v_{F}^{2}\frac{\bm{k}\otimes\bm{k}}{\tilde{k}^{2}}\right)\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\frac{\tilde{k}}{2m}\right),

(79)

where
\eta_{\mathrm{gr}}=2\pi\sigma_{\mathrm{gr}}=\pi\alpha/2=0.0114
, and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mathrm{i}}{\pi y}\left\{1-\frac{y^{2}+1}{y}\arctanh y\right\},

\displaystyle\tilde{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\omega^{2}-v_{F}^{2}\bm{k}^{2}}.

Obviously, that
y(-\omega^{*})=y(\omega^{*})=y^{*}(\omega)
, and therefore

\arctanh y(-\omega^{*})=\arctanh y^{*}(\omega)=\arctanh^{*}y(\omega),

(80)

and

\widetilde{\Phi}(y(-\omega^{*}))=-\widetilde{\Phi}^{*}(y(\omega)).

Therefore, the relation (
78
) is valid:
\bm{\eta}_{1}(-\omega^{*})=\bm{\eta}_{1}^{*}(\omega)
. Then, it is easy to verify that

\bm{\eta}_{1}(\omega^{*})=-\bm{\eta}_{1}^{*}(\omega).

(81)

At the imaginary axis
\omega=\mathrm{i}\xi
we obtain

\bm{\eta}_{1}(\mathrm{i}\xi)=\bm{\eta}_{1}^{*}(\mathrm{i}\xi),\ \bm{\eta}_{1}(-\mathrm{i}\xi)=-\bm{\eta}_{1}^{*}(\mathrm{i}\xi)=\bm{\eta}_{1}(\mathrm{i}\xi),

(82)

and conductivity is real function

\bm{\eta}_{1}(\mathrm{i}\xi)=\eta_{\mathrm{gr}}\frac{k_{F}}{\xi}\left(\mathbf{I}-v_{F}^{2}\frac{\bm{k}\otimes\bm{k}}{k_{F}^{2}}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{k_{F}}{2m}\right),

(83)

where

\displaystyle\Phi(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi y}\left\{1+\frac{y^{2}-1}{y}\arctan y\right\},

\displaystyle k_{F}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\xi^{2}+v_{F}^{2}\bm{k}^{2}}.

The conditions (
82
) for function
\tilde{\bm{\eta}}_{1}(\xi)=\bm{\eta}_{1}(\mathrm{i}\xi)
read

\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{1}(\xi)=\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{1}(\xi)^{*},\ \widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{1}(-\xi)=-\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{1}(\xi)^{*}=-\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{1}(\xi).

(84)

The conductivity of the moving graphene has form (
23
)

\bm{\eta}_{2}=\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}\mathbf{G}\bm{\eta}^{\prime}_{2}\mathbf{G}^{T},

(85)

where

\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}-\frac{\omega}{(ku)}\frac{\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u}}{u_{0}+1}+\frac{\bm{u}\otimes\bm{k}}{(ku)},

(86)

and

\bm{\eta}^{\prime}_{2}=\eta_{\mathrm{gr}}\frac{\tilde{k}^{\prime}}{\omega^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{I}+v_{F}^{2}\frac{\bm{k}^{\prime}\otimes\bm{k}^{\prime}}{\tilde{k}^{\prime 2}}\right)\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\frac{\tilde{k}^{\prime}}{2m}\right).

(87)

Here,

\displaystyle\tilde{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\omega^{\prime 2}-v_{F}^{2}\bm{k}^{\prime 2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tilde{k}^{2}+\gamma^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})(\omega^{2}\bm{v}^{2}+(\bm{k}\bm{v})^{2}-2\omega(\bm{k}\bm{v}))},

(88)

and
\omega^{\prime}=(ku)=\gamma(\omega-(\bm{k}\bm{v}))
.

We observe now that

\tilde{k}^{\prime}(-\omega^{*},\bm{v})=\tilde{k}^{\prime*}(\omega,-\bm{v}),\ \omega^{\prime}(-\omega^{*},\bm{v})=-\omega^{\prime*}(\omega,-\bm{v}),

and (
y^{\prime}=\tilde{k}^{\prime}/2m
)

\widetilde{\Phi}(y^{\prime}(-\omega^{*},\bm{v}))=-\widetilde{\Phi}^{*}(y^{\prime}(\omega,-\bm{v})).

Therefore,

\bm{\eta}^{\prime}_{2}(-\omega^{*},\bm{v})=\bm{\eta}^{\prime*}_{2}(\omega,-\bm{v}).

(89)

It is easy to see from (
86
) that,

\mathbf{G}(-\omega^{*},\bm{v})=\mathbf{G}^{\prime*}(\omega,-\bm{v})

and therefore,

\bm{\eta}_{2}(-\omega^{*},\bm{v})=\bm{\eta}^{*}_{2}(\omega,-\bm{v}).

(90)

The conductivity of the moving graphene obeys the relation (
78
) but with a corresponding changing direction of the velocity. It is similar to the case with an external magnetic field.

Then, because

\displaystyle\tilde{k}^{\prime}(\omega^{*},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\tilde{k}^{\prime*}(-\omega,-\bm{v})=\tilde{k}^{\prime*}(\omega,\bm{v}),

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(\omega^{*},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=-\omega^{\prime*}(-\omega,-\bm{v})=\omega^{\prime*}(\omega,\bm{v}),

we have

\widetilde{\Phi}(y^{\prime}(\omega^{*},\bm{v}))=-\widetilde{\Phi}^{*}(y^{\prime}(\omega,\bm{v})),

and therefore,

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}^{\prime}_{2}(\omega^{*},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\bm{\eta}^{\prime*}_{2}(-\omega,-\bm{v})=-\bm{\eta}^{\prime*}_{2}(\omega,\bm{v}),

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}_{2}(\omega^{*},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\bm{\eta}^{*}_{2}(-\omega,-\bm{v})=-\bm{\eta}^{*}_{2}(\omega,\bm{v}).

At the imaginary axis
\omega=\mathrm{i}\xi
, the conductivity
\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{2}(\xi,\bm{v})=\bm{\eta}(\mathrm{i}\xi,\bm{v})
is not real, but it obeys to relations

\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{2}(\xi,\bm{v})=\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}^{*}_{2}(\xi,-\bm{v}),\ \widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{2}(-\xi,\bm{v})=-\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}^{*}_{2}(\xi,\bm{v}).

(91)

The scattering matrix (
33
) at the imaginary axes reads

\bm{r}_{i}(\mathrm{i}k_{E})=-\frac{\xi^{2}\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i}+\bm{k}\otimes(\bm{k}\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i})+\mathbf{I}\xi k_{E}\det\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i}}{\xi^{2}\tr\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i}+(\bm{k}\bm{k}\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i})+\xi k_{E}(1+\det\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i})}.

(92)

Due to relations (
84
) and (
91
) we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{1}(\mathrm{i}k_{E},-\xi)

\displaystyle=\bm{r}_{1}(\mathrm{i}k_{E},\xi)=\bm{r}^{*}_{1}(\mathrm{i}k_{E},\xi)

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{2}(\mathrm{i}k_{E},-\xi,\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\bm{r}^{*}_{1}(\mathrm{i}k_{E},\xi,\bm{v}).

(93)

Let us consider the energy (
41
)

\mathcal{E}_{2}=\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi L(\xi,\bm{v}),

(94)

where

L(\xi,\bm{v})=\ln\det\left[1-e^{-2ak_{E}}\bm{r}^{\prime}_{1}(\mathrm{i}k_{E})\bm{r}_{2}(\mathrm{i}k_{E})\right].

Taking into account the relations (
93
) we obtain,
L(-\xi,\bm{v})=L^{*}(\xi,\bm{v})
, and therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}=\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi L(\xi,\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\iintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\Re L(\xi,\bm{v}).

(95)

Therefore, the
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is a real quantity and does not give a contribution to the Casimir friction.

Appendix B
Direct calculation of the boosted PT

The PT in the frame
K^{\prime}
has the following form

\tilde{\Pi}^{\prime ab}(k^{\prime})=\mathrm{i}e^{2}\iiintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tr[\tilde{\gamma}^{a}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(p^{\prime}+k^{\prime})\tilde{\gamma}^{b}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(p^{\prime})],

(96)

where

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(p^{\prime})=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{a}p^{\prime}_{a}+m}{(\tilde{\gamma}^{b}p^{\prime}_{b})^{2}-m^{2}},

(97)

and
\tilde{\gamma}^{a}=\tilde{\mathrm{g}}^{a}_{b}\gamma^{b}
,
\tilde{\mathrm{g}}^{a}_{b}=\diag(1,v_{F},v_{F})
.

In the frame
K
one has the same form of PT

\tilde{\Pi}^{ab}(k)=\mathrm{i}e^{2}\iiintop\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tr[\tilde{\Gamma}^{a}\mathcal{S}(p+k)\tilde{\Gamma}^{b}\mathcal{S}(p)],

(98)

but with matrices
\tilde{\Gamma}^{a}
and the spinor Green function
\mathcal{S}
.

To obtain these matrices and Green function we make boost
x^{a}\to x^{\prime a}=\Lambda^{a^{\prime}}_{\ b}x^{b}\ (x^{\prime}=\Lambda x)
. Then the bispinors
\psi(x),\overline{\psi}(x)
in new coordinates read
\psi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=S\psi(x),\ \overline{\psi}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\overline{\psi}(x)S^{-1}
(see, for example,
Itzykson and Zuber 2006
). Therefore,

\tilde{\Gamma}^{a^{\prime}}=S\tilde{\gamma}^{b}S^{-1}\Lambda_{\ b}^{a^{\prime}},\ \mathcal{S}=\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}^{a}p_{a}+m}{(\tilde{\Gamma}^{b}p_{b})^{2}-m^{2}}.

(99)

Obviously, that
\Gamma^{a^{\prime}}=\gamma^{a^{\prime}}
.

By straightforward calculations we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{F}\gamma^{a}+u^{a}(1-v_{F})\not{u},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v_{F}\not{p}+(1-v_{F})(up)\not{u}+m}{p^{2}v_{F}^{2}+(up)^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})-m^{2}},

(100)

where
\not{u}=\gamma^{a}u_{a}
and
(up)=u^{a}p_{a}
. Taking into account the above expressions we arrive at the following expression of polarization tensor (
98
)

\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{\mathrm{i}e^{2}}{2\pi^{3}}\iiintop\nolimits\frac{t^{\mu\nu}\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{Q(q)Q(p)},

(101)

where

\displaystyle t^{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{F}^{4}p^{(a}q^{b)}+v_{F}^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})(uq)p^{(a}u^{b)}+v_{F}^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})(up)q^{(a}u^{b)}

(102)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(1-v_{F}^{2})\left[m^{2}-(qp)v_{F}^{2}+(up)(uq)(1-v_{F}^{2})\right]u^{a}u^{b}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle v_{F}^{2}\left[m^{2}-(qp)v_{F}^{2}-(up)(uq)(1-v_{F}^{2})\right]\mathrm{g}^{ab}.

Here,
q=p+k
,
Q(p)=p^{2}v_{F}^{2}+(up)^{2}(1-v_{F}^{2})-m^{2}
, and
a^{(ab)}=a^{ab}+a^{ba}
. Then, taking into account the Pauli-Villars renormalization we arrive at (
14
).

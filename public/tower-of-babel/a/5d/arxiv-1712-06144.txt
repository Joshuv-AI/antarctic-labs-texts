Search for the \cmsSymbolFace ​ X ​ ( 5568 ) \cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568) state decaying into \PBzs ​ \Pgppm \PBzs\Pgppm in proton-proton collisions at s = 8 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=8\TeV


Abstract
A search for resonance-like structures in the \PBzs ​ \Pgppm \PBzs\Pgppm invariant mass spectrum is performed using proton-proton collision data collected by the CMS experiment at the LHC at s = 8 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=8\TeV , corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 19.7 \fbinv . The \PBzs \PBzs mesons are reconstructed in the decay chain \PBzs → \PJGy ​ ϕ \PBzs\to\PJGy\,\phi , with \PJGy → \PGm + ​ \PGm − \PJGy\to\PGm^{+}\PGm^{-} and ϕ → \PKp ​ \PKm \phi\to\PKp\PKm . The \PBzs ​ \Pgppm \PBzs\Pgppm invariant mass distribution shows no statistically significant peaks for different selection requirements on the reconstructed \PBzs \PBzs and \Pgppm \Pgppm candidates. Upper limits are set on the relative production rates of the \cmsSymbolFace ​ X ​ ( 5568 ) \cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568) and \PBzs \PBzs states times the branching fraction of the decay \cmsSymbolFace ​ X ​ ( 5568 ) ± → \PBzs ​ \Pgppm \cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)^{\pm}\to\PBzs\Pgppm . In addition, upper limits are obtained as a function of the mass and the natural width of possible exotic states decaying into \PBzs ​ \Pgppm \PBzs\Pgppm .

\cmsNoteHeader

BPH-16-002

The evidence presented by the D0 Collaboration of a new state decaying
to
\PBzs\Pgppm

[
1
]
initiated considerable interest within
the exotic hadron community
(discussed, e.g., in Refs.
[
2
,
3
]

and references therein) and
triggered a similar search by the LHCb Collaboration
[
4
]
.
The D0 experiment reported an unexpected, narrow structure, named
X(5568)
,
in the
\PBzs\Pgppm
invariant mass distribution
and interpreted it as a hadron composed of four quarks of different
flavors (
\PQb\PAQs\PQu\PAQd
; inclusion of charge-conjugate modes
is implied throughout this Letter).
The measured mass and natural width of this state are

5567.8\pm 2.9\stat{}^{+0.9}_{-1.9}\syst\MeV

and
21.9\pm 6.4\stat{}^{+5.0}_{-2.5}\syst\MeV
, respectively
[
1
]
.
Possible quantum numbers for the state are
J^{P}=0^{+}
,
if the
\PBzs\Pgppm
is produced in an
S
-wave,
or
J^{P}=1^{+}
, if the decay proceeds via the chain

\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)^{\pm}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}\Pgppm
,
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}\to\PBzs\gamma
and the photon is not reconstructed.
In the latter case, the mass of the new state would be shifted
by
m_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}}-m_{\PBzs}
with respect to the measured
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
mass, where
m_{\PBzs}

and
m_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}}
are the nominal
\PBzs
and
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}
masses
[
5
]
.

The LHCb Collaboration searched for the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
state and reported
a negative result
[
4
]
.
Further independent searches are needed either to confirm
the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
state or to set stronger limits on its production.
In particular, the CMS detector can probe a central kinematic region of

\PBzs
candidates similar to that of D0, complementing the LHCb
search in the forward region.
Recently, the CDF and ATLAS Collaborations reported independently
negative search results for the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)

[
6
,
7
]
,
while the D0 Collaboration presented additional evidence for the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)

by adding
\PBzs
mesons reconstructed in semileptonic decays
[
8
]
.

This Letter presents a search for the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
state performed by
the CMS Collaboration at the LHC. The data sample corresponds to
19.7
\fbinv
of proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp
) collisions
at
\sqrt{s}=8\TeV
collected in 2012.
The
\PBzs\Pgppm
candidates are reconstructed through the decay

\PBzs\to\PJGy\,\phi
, with
\PJGy\to\PGmp\PGmm
and
\phi\to\PKp\PKm
.
The relative production rate of
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
, with
respect to
\PBzs
, times the branching fraction
of the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)^{\pm}\to\PBzs\Pgppm
decay is calculated using the relation

\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}\equiv\frac{\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\cmsSymbolFace{X}+\text{anything})\,{\mathcal{B}}(\cmsSymbolFace{X}\to\PBzs\Pgppm)}{\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PBzs+\text{anything})}=\frac{N_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}}{\epsilon_{\text{rel}}N_{\PBzs}},

(1)

where
\cmsSymbolFace{X}=\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)^{\pm}
,
N_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}
(
N_{\PBzs}
) is the number of
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)~(\PBzs)
signal
candidates reconstructed in data and

\epsilon_{\text{rel}}=\epsilon_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}/\epsilon_{\PBzs}

is the relative efficiency.
The D0 Collaboration measured
\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}=(8.6\pm 2.4)\%

and
(8.2\pm 3.1)\%
for
\pt(\PBzs)>10
and 15
\GeV

[
1
]
.

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid
of 6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T.
Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead
tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and
scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of a barrel and two
endcap sections. Muons are detected
in the pseudorapidity range
\abs{\eta}<2.4

in gas-ionization chambers embedded in
the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
The main subdetectors used for the present analysis are
the silicon tracker and the muon detection system.
The silicon tracker measures charged particles within the
range
\abs{\eta}<2.5
.
For nonisolated particles with transverse momentum
1<\pt<10\GeV

and
\abs{\eta}<1.4
,
the track resolutions are typically 1.5% in
\pt
and 25–90 (45–150)
\mum
in the transverse (longitudinal) impact parameter
[
9
]
.
Matching muons to tracks measured in the silicon tracker
results in a relative
\pt
resolution for muons
with
\pt<10\GeV
of 0.8–3.0% depending on

\abs{\eta}

[
10
]
.
A more detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition
of the coordinate system used and the relevant kinematic variables,
can be found in Ref.
[
11
]
.

Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger
system
[
12
]
. The first level (L1), composed of custom
hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors.
The second level, known as the high-level trigger
(HLT), consists of a farm of processors running a version of the full event
reconstruction software optimized for fast processing.
This analysis uses events collected with HLT algorithms requiring
two muons that are consistent with originating from a
\PJGy
meson
decaying at a significant distance from the luminous region.

The reconstruction of the
\PBzs
candidates closely follows the
procedure described in Ref.
[
13
]
, where the CP-violating
phase
\phi_{s}
was measured using
the same decay chain,
\PBzs\to\PJGy\,\phi
, with
\PJGy\to\PGmp\PGmm
and
\phi\to\PKp\PKm
,
reconstructed from the same data set and triggered by the same
L1 and HLT algorithms.

The reconstruction requires two muons of opposite charge
that must match those that triggered the event readout.
The offline muon selection is more restrictive
than the trigger requirements and includes:

\pt(\PGmpm)>4\GeV
,
|\eta(\PGmpm)|<2.2
,
\pt(\PGmp\PGmm)>7\GeV
,
soft muon identification
[
10
]
,
the dimuon vertex
\chi^{2}
fit probability
P_{\mathrm{vtx}}(\PGmp\PGmm)>10\%
,
and the dimuon mass within the range 3.04 to
3.15\GeV
.
The angle
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}(\PGmp\PGmm)
in the transverse plane
between
\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}(\PGmp\PGmm)
and the vector
\vec{D}_{xy}(\PGmp\PGmm)
from
the beam axis to the dimuon vertex is required to
satisfy
\cos{\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}(\PGmp\PGmm)}>0.9
.
The dimuon vertex transverse displacement divided by its uncertainty,

D_{xy}(\PGmp\PGmm)/\sigma_{D_{xy}(\PGmp\PGmm)}
, must be greater than 3.

The two muons are combined with two other oppositely charged
tracks in the event, neither identified as a muon and assumed to
be kaons, which must each have

\pt(\PKpm)>0.7\GeV
and satisfy high-purity
track
[
9
]
requirements.
The invariant mass of the kaon pair candidate,
M(\PKp\PKm)
, is
required to be within
{\pm}10\MeV
of the
known
\phi(1020)
meson mass
[
5
]
.

The
\PBzs
candidates are obtained using a kinematic vertex fit
to the two muon and two kaon tracks,
with the dimuon candidate mass constrained to
the nominal
\PJGy
meson mass
[
5
]

(the mass of the
\PKp\PKm
candidate is not constrained because the
width of the
\phi(1020)
resonance exceeds the mass resolution).
Additional requirements imposed on the
\PBzs
candidates include

\pt(\PBzs)>10\GeV
,
P_{\mathrm{vtx}}(\PBzs)>1\%
,

D_{xy}(\PBzs)/\sigma_{D_{xy}(\PBzs)}>3
,
and
\cos{\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}(\PBzs)}>0.99
,
where
D_{xy}(\PBzs)
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}(\PBzs)
are analogous
to the corresponding dimuon variables and are measured
with respect to the primary interaction vertex (PV).
The events contain multiple
\Pp\Pp
collisions from the same or nearby
bunch crossings (pileup), with an average of 16 collisions per event.
The PV is chosen as the one with the smallest angle between the
vector from the collision point to the
\PBzs
candidate decay vertex
and the
\PBzs
candidate momentum.

An extended unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to the
\PJGy\PKp\PKm

invariant mass,
M(\PJGy\PKp\PKm)
, distribution yields
49\,277\pm 278

\PBzs
signal candidates, where the signal and background
components are modeled by a double-Gaussian and an exponential
function, respectively, as shown in Fig.
1
.
In the fit, the common mean (
\mu_{\PBzs}
), the fraction of the
second Gaussian function (
f
), and the widths (
\sigma_{1,2}
) of
the two signal Gaussian functions (given in Fig.
1
), as well as
the parameter of the exponential function, are left free.
Signal and lower and upper sideband mass regions are defined, respectively,
by the intervals
[-2\sigma_{\PBzs},\,+2\sigma_{\PBzs}]
,
[-10\sigma_{\PBzs},\,-4\sigma_{\PBzs}]
, and

[\,4\sigma_{\PBzs},\,10\sigma_{\PBzs}]
around
\mu_{\PBzs}
,
as indicated in Fig.
1
.
Here,
\sigma_{\PBzs}\simeq 14\MeV
represents
the standard deviation of the double-Gaussian function.
In the signal region, the signal purity is about 85%
and the number of multiple
\PBzs
candidates in a single event is
negligible.

The pion candidate from the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)^{\pm}\to\PBzs\Pgppm
decay is required to
be a track used in the PV fit, with
\pt(\Pgppm)>0.5\GeV
, and satisfy
track quality requirements
[
9
]
.
The average number of
\PBzs\Pgppm
candidates per event in the

\PBzs
signal region is 1.8.
Constraints on the angle between the momenta of the
\PBzs
and
\Pgppm

candidates are not imposed in this analysis, because such requirements
sculpt the
\PBzs\Pgppm
invariant mass distribution in a nontrivial way,
as discussed in Appendix
.1
.
Motivated by the low momentum of the
\Pgppm
from the signal decay,
together with the strong correlation between
M(\PJGy\PKp\PKm\Pgppm)

and
M(\PJGy\PKp\PKm)
, the invariant mass of a
\PBzs\Pgppm
candidate is defined as

M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)=M(\PJGy\PKp\PKm\Pgppm)-M(\PJGy\PKp\PKm)+m_{\PBzs}
.
This improves the
\PBzs\Pgppm
mass resolution by a factor of 5,
as found in Monte Carlo (MC) simulations.

Simulated MC events are produced with
Pythia

v6.424
[
14
]
.
The
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
is simulated as a spin-0 particle of mass and width equal to

m_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}\equiv 5567.8\MeV
and
\Gamma_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}\equiv 21.9\MeV
, and is
forced to decay to a
\PBzs
meson and a
\Pgppm
using the phase-space model
in
EvtGen
1.3.0
[
15
]
.
For the
\PBzs
signal generation,
EvtGen
simulates
the
\PBzs\to\PJGy\,\phi
,
\PJGy\to\PGmp\PGmm
, and
\phi\to\PKp\PKm
decays, including the
effects from mixing and CP violation.
Final-state photon radiation is included in
EvtGen
using

PHOTOS

[
16
,
17
]
.
The events are then passed through a detailed
Geant4
-based
simulation
[
18
]
of the CMS detector with the
same trigger and reconstruction algorithms used on data.
The simulation includes pileup effects at the same rate as observed in data.

Additional requirements are imposed to reduce
the level of background:

\pt(\PBzs)>25\GeV
,
\pt(\Pgppm)>1\GeV
, and

.
Figure

for events in the lower and higher sideband and signal regions.
Only the latter two distributions have a clear excess around

This excess is consistent with the decays
\PB_{1}(5721)^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\Pgpp
,

\PB^{*}_{2}(5747)^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\Pgpp
, and
\PB^{*}_{2}(5747)^{+}\to\PBz\Pgpp
,
where the decay
\PBz\to\PJGy\PKp\Pgpm
is misreconstructed as
\PBzs\to\PJGy\PKp\PKm

(the photon from the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\to\PBz\gamma
decay is not reconstructed).
The peaks in the
M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)
distribution corresponding to the
decays
\PB^{(*)}_{1,2}{}^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\Pgpp
are shifted by
m_{\PBzs}-m_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)0}}

with respect to the nominal masses of the
\PB^{(*)}_{1,2}{}^{+}
states
[
5
]
.

A possible
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
signal contribution in the
M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)
spectrum
is modeled by a relativistic S-wave Breit–Wigner (BW) function, with mass
and width parameters fixed to
m_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}
and
\Gamma_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}
, respectively. The
BW is convolved with a triple-Gaussian resolution function whose parameters
are obtained from the simulated data (standard deviation of the
triple-Gaussian function is about
2.2\MeV
in the region of interest).
The background shape is approximated by a function of the form

(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{Pol}_{n}(x)
,
where
x=M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)
,
x_{0}=m_{\PBzs}+m_{\Pgppm}
,
with
m_{\Pgppm}
the
\Pgppm
mass
[
5
]
, and
\mathrm{Pol}_{n}(x)
represents a
polynomial function of order
n
. For the default shape
n=3
is used.
The polynomial coefficients, as well as the
exponent
\alpha
and the signal and background yields,
are obtained from the unbinned extended maximum-likelihood fit
shown in Fig.
3
(a).
The fit returns a signal yield of
N_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}=-85\pm 160
events.
The procedure is repeated requiring
\pt(\PBzs)>15\GeV
, and
the fit results displayed in Fig.
3
(b)
give
N_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}=-103\pm 122
events.

Several cross-checks are performed and in all cases
the signal yield is consistent with zero. They include
repeating the fit with the following variations:
the background model parameters are fixed to the values
obtained from the fit with the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
signal region excluded;
the background model is fixed to the shape obtained from
simulated
\PBzs
mesons combined with pion candidates
from the same simulated event;
different kinematic requirements and reconstruction
quality criteria are imposed on the

\PBzs\Pgppm
,
\PBzs
, and
\Pgppm
candidates;
collision events with multiple reconstructed
candidates are removed from the data sample,
and alternative background functions and fit regions
are used.

An upper limit on
\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}
, defined in Eq. (
1
),
is computed using the asymptotic
CL
S

[
19
,
20
]
method
developed in Ref.
[
21
]
.
The limit takes into account the following sources of
systematic uncertainty:
the uncertainty in the mass and the width of the
BW measured by the D0 Collaboration
[
1
]
;
the uncertainty in
N(\PBzs)
;
the pion tracking efficiency uncertainty of
3.9\%

[
9
]
;
the uncertainty in
\epsilon_{\text{rel}}
due to the finite number
of simulated events;
the description of the background by alternative
approximation functions, including the shape obtained
from simulation;
and modifications of the signal function due to
variations of the resolution function and the efficiency
with respect to
M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)
(both negligible).
The measured upper limit is

\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}<1.1\%
at 95% confidence level (CL) for the
baseline selection criteria (
\pt(\PBzs)>10\GeV
) and

\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}<1.0\%
at 95% CL for the
analysis requiring
\pt(\PBzs)>15\GeV
.
Using simulations of a spin-1 state decaying to
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}\Pgppm
,
where
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}\to\PBzs\gamma
and where the mass is shifted by
m_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}}-m_{\PBzs}
,
the upper limits were verified to differ negligibly between either
the spin-1 or spin-0 assumption.

Upper limits are also obtained for different values of
mass and natural width (
\Gamma
) of a possible
\PBzs\Pgppm
resonance,
as shown in Fig.
4
.
For these limits, no systematic uncertainties related to the mass and width
of the exotic state are assigned.
On the other hand, an additional systematic uncertainty in the relative
efficiency of up to 6% is estimated for the extrapolation to high-mass
resonances from the low-mass simulation.
The limits are obtained for values of
\Gamma
from
10
to
50\MeV

in
10\MeV
steps,
while the mass takes values from
m_{\PBzs}{+}m_{\Pgppm}{+}\Gamma

up to
5.9\GeV{-}1.5\Gamma
in order to consider
a possible exotic state with higher
mass decaying to the
\PBzs\Pgppm
final
state
[
22
,
23
]
.
No significant excess is found throughout the region considered.

In summary, a search for the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
state is performed by the CMS Collaboration
using
\Pp\Pp
collision data collected at
\sqrt{s}=8\TeV
and corresponding
to an integrated luminosity of
19.7\fbinv
.
With about 50 000
\PBzs
signal candidates,
no significant structure in the
\PBzs\Pgppm

invariant mass spectrum is found around the mass reported
by the D0 Collaboration (nor for masses up to
5.9\GeV
).
The absence of a peak is supported by direct comparison with
the events in the
\PBzs
sidebands, and by fits to the
\PBzs\Pgppm

invariant mass distribution with
a resonant component included,
using different kinematic selection requirements,
as well as variants of the background modeling, fit regions,
and quality criteria.

Upper limits on the relative production rates
of the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
and
\PBzs
states, multiplied by the unknown branching
fraction of the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)^{\pm}\to\PBzs\Pgppm
decay, are computed to be:

\displaystyle\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}<1.1\%~\text{at}~95\%\text{ CL for }\pt(\PBzs)>10\GeV\text{ and}

\displaystyle\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}<1.0\%~\text{at}~95\%\text{ CL for }\pt(\PBzs)>15\GeV.

The upper limits on
\rho_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}
presented in this Letter are a factor of
two more stringent than the previous best limits, and do not
confirm the existence of the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
state.
These limits are also valid for a spin-1 state decaying into
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}_{\cmsSymbolFace}{s}\Pgppm
.
Additionally, upper limits
are set for different values of mass and natural width
of a hypothetical exotic resonance decaying into
\PBzs\Pgppm
.

.1
Effect of
\Delta R(\PBzs,\Pgppm)

requirement on the
M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)
distribution

The D0 Collaboration obtains a significance above five
standard deviations for the
\cmsSymbolFace{X}(5568)
state only when an upper limit on

\Delta R=\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}}

is imposed, namely,
\Delta R<0.3
.
Here,
\Delta\eta
and
\Delta\phi
are the pseudorapidity and azimuthal
angle (in radians) differences between the directions of the
\PBzs
and
\Pgppm
.
As shown in Fig.
5
, a requirement on this variable
significantly changes the
M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)
distribution
and can even produce a peaking shape;
therefore, it is not used in this analysis.

The
M^{\Delta}(\PBzs\Pgppm)
distributions for events in the
\PBzs
(a) signal
and (b) sideband regions for different

\Delta R
requirements.
The uncertainties are not shown for the sake of clarity.
The vertical band indicates the region
m_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}\pm\Gamma_{\cmsSymbolFace{X}}
.

Visualizing Kraus operators for dephasing noise during application of the SWAP \sqrt{\mathrm{\mathrm{SWAP}}} quantum gate
Nicolas André da Costa Morazotti, Reginaldo de Jesus Napolitano

Abstract
We consider the case of a SWAP \sqrt{\mathrm{SWAP}} quantum gate and its optimized entangling action, via continuous dynamical decoupling, in the presence of dephasing noise. We illustrate the procedure in the specific case where only the two-qubit operation is controlled and no single-qubit operations are included in the description. To compare the optimized dynamics in the presence of noise with the ideal case, we use the standard fidelity measure. Then we discuss the importance of using optimized gates in the quantum operational-probabilistic theory. Because of their importance for the explicit construction of the completely positive maps representing the operations, we derive optimized Kraus operators in this specific case, focusing on the entanglement operation. We then show how to visualize the time evolution of each Kraus operator as a curve in a three-dimensional Euclidean space. Finally, we connect this formalism with the operational framework of quantum mechanics by describing a possible set of measurements that could be performed to obtain the Kraus operators.

Introduction

Optimal control has been a regularly studied subject for a long time,
mainly for studies on spin dynamics
Khaneja2005
,
and it has become increasingly more relevant in the context of the
quantum information science
PhysRevA.85.052327
;
Green_2013
;
PhysRevA.102.052203
.
A very exciting prospect has been already envisaged starting in the
first decade of the present century: the development of a quantum
internet
Kimble2008
;
Ritter2012
;
quantum-net
. Recent experimental
efforts have managed to establish a quantum-communication network
over 4600 km

. In this context of quantum networking
and communications, important topics have been referred to as transduction
Mirhosseini2020

and transfer of entanglement
Budich_2010
;
PhysRevA.98.062329
.
In particular, flying qubits which get entangled on the fly with target
qubits with which they interact have been considered in the context
of scattering

. Inspired by these
basic investigations, realistic implementation of distribution of
entanglement over quantum networks is already occurring
PhysRevLett.125.260506
;
PhysRevLett.126.020503
.
However, all these possibilities are plagued by the unavoidable noise
causing decoherence, which limits immensely the progress of implementation
of quantum technologies, mainly when the carriers of quantum information
are not photons
Bogdanov532
. It is, therefore, indispensable
to understand the effects of quantum noise in quantum-information
processing, including ways to correct for errors or to prevent them
from happening
RevModPhys.87.307
.

Any quantum gate able to entangle two qubits together with general
single-qubit operations will suffice for universal quantum processing
michaelnielsen2011
.
Realization of entanglement between pairs of qubits by approaching
and subsequently splitting them has been recently demonstrated using
lattice surgery
Erhard2021
. Also, addressability of individual
qubits has even been extended to chromium molecules

.
Indistinguishability symmetry has been explored in the context of
entanglement and quantum control
Nosrati2020
;
PhysRevLett.125.180402
;
PhysRevLett.125.230502

Along the lines of these particular recent developments, the
\sqrt{\mathrm{SWAP}}

gate has long been realized in the laboratory using rubidium atoms
in optical lattices, promising control over the effective exchange
interaction
Anderlini2007
. Based on this possibility of articulating
the time dependence of the effective interaction between two qubits,
here we approach the case of quantum control of a
\sqrt{\mathrm{SWAP}}

entangling quantum gate in the presence of dephasing noise, which
we simulate by introducing a boson bath. Relaxation times due to amplitude
damping, because it involves transfer of energy by dissipation, are
usually much longer than pure-decoherence times
quiroga
. We,
therefore, focus on the situation where we have the complete action
of the
\sqrt{\mathrm{SWAP}}
gate fast compared to relaxation, but
slow compared to dephasing.

We optimize the time dependence during the application of the entangling
gate, while noise is present, by the well-studied continuous
dynamical-decoupling procedure
PhysRevA.75.022329
. We compare the
optimized noisy dynamics with the ideal, noiseless one by calculating
the fidelity measure of our output operation. With the optimization of
the gate under the perturbation due to coupling with the environment, we
derive a set of corresponding Kraus
operators

;
KEYL2002431
. These operators provide the
open-system evolution of any initial reduced matrix and are required to

mechanics

;
PhysRevA.84.012311
;
Oreshkov2015
.
Given the importance of the Kraus operators in the open-system dynamics
of quantum-information processing, we present a prescription to
visualize them via a one-to-one correspondence between each Kraus
operator and a three-dimensional real vector. The whole dynamics as
embedded in the time dependence of each Kraus operator is, thus,
transcribed into the trajectory described by each of the corresponding
vectors. Although our focus is on the
\sqrt{\mathrm{SWAP}}
gate under
dephasing, we believe the visualization prescription may serve to
inspire its generalization to more complex situations, analogously to
the recent extension of the Bloch-sphere and Bloch-vector concepts to
the case of a qutrit

and, more generally, of a
qudit

.

II
Optimization of the
\sqrt{\mathrm{SWAP}}

quantum gate

The SWAP quantum gate has been considered both theoretically and experimentally
Anderlini2007

and, together with universal one-qubit operations, can be used to
implement universal quantum computing
michaelnielsen2011
.
Giving its importance, we investigate its optimal application by means
of controlling its strength as a function of time to be optimized,
as the experimental implementation of Ref.
Anderlini2007
has
given evidence to its feasibility. Thus, we consider the gate Hamiltonian
given by

\displaystyle H_{G}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\Omega\left(t\right)G,

(1)

where
G
is an operator that acts on the two qubits, as we
specify below, and
\Omega\left(t\right)
is assumed to be a controlled
frequency whose time dependence we can choose arbitrarily.
Let us consider dephasing noise by introducing a boson field
quiroga
.
In this case, the total spin-boson Hamiltonian, much studied in the
dissipative case
PhysRevLett.46.211
;
RevModPhys.59.1
, can be
written as

\displaystyle H\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{G}\left(t\right)+\hbar\sum_{s}\omega_{s}b_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}

(2)

\displaystyle+\hbar\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sigma_{k,z}\sum_{s}\left(g_{k,s}b_{s}+g_{k,s}^{\ast}b_{s}^{\dagger}\right),

where
\omega_{s}
is the frequency of the boson in its
s
th mode,

b_{s}
and
b_{s}^{\dagger}
are the annihilation and creation
operators of a boson quantum in the
s
th mode,
\sigma_{k,z}
is
the
z\text{-axis}

k
th qubit, and
g_{k,s}

is the coupling constant of the
k
th qubit with the
s
th boson
degree of freedom. For concreteness, as we have emphasized above,
let
G

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|+\left|1\right\rangle\left\langle 1\right|\otimes\left|1\right\rangle\left\langle 1\right|

(3)

\displaystyle+\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 1\right|\otimes\left|1\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|+\left|1\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 1\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}_{S}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1,x}\sigma_{2,x}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1,y}\sigma_{2,y}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1,z}\sigma_{2,z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{I}_{S}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2}}{2}.

Hence, it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle G^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{S},

(4)

where
\mathbb{I}_{S}
is the two-qubit identity operator. Here,
the states of each qubit are given by the eigenstates of the Pauli operator
for the
z
axis, forming the logical basis set:
\left\{\left|0\right\rangle,\left|1\right\rangle\right\}.

Conventionally, in the third line of Eq. (
3
) we adopt the indices

k=1,2
as referring to the qubits whose operator factors in the
tensor products are on the left and on the right, respectively.

In the absence of noise, that is, choosing

in Eq. (
2
),
the simplest choice of
\Omega\left(t\right)
that takes the factorized
state
\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|1\right\rangle,
after

\tau,
to a target state that is maximally entangled,
namely,

\displaystyle\left|\psi\left(\tau\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|1\right\rangle-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left|1\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle,

(5)

is simply a constant:
\Omega\left(t\right)=\pi/4\tau.

Instead of the notation of Eq. (
3
) for the two-qubit kets and
bras, henceforth we use a more compact notation for two-qubit logical
kets in terms of their counterpart physical kets, namely,
\left|2m+n\right\rangle\equiv\left|m\right\rangle\otimes\left|n\right\rangle

for
m,n\in\left\{0,1\right\}.
The interaction between the qubits
and the bosons in Eq. (
2
) does not couple the subspace spanned
by
\left\{\left|0\right\rangle,\left|3\right\rangle\right\}

to the one spanned by
\left\{\left|1\right\rangle,\left|2\right\rangle\right\}.

Furthermore,
G
only entangles the input states
\left|1\right\rangle

and
\left|2\right\rangle
in this new logical notation, that is,
if we start with initial logical state
\left|0\right\rangle

or
\left|3\right\rangle,
the dynamics driven by
G,
besides
not leaving the subspace spanned by
\left\{\left|0\right\rangle,\left|3\right\rangle\right\},

will not generate a superposition between
\left|0\right\rangle

and
\left|3\right\rangle.
Therefore, since our purpose is to study
optimized entangling evolutions, we just need to consider the logical
subspace spanned by
\left\{\left|1\right\rangle,\left|2\right\rangle\right\}

and, for concreteness, let us define the starting two-qubit state,
at
t=0,
as the one whose logical representation is given by the

\left|1\right\rangle.
We want to have an ideal superposition

G
acts for a total time interval

5
), but that in the compact notation,

\displaystyle\left|\psi\left(\tau\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|1\right\rangle-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left|2\right\rangle.

(6)

Restricted to the relevant subspace spanned by
\left\{\left|1\right\rangle,\left|2\right\rangle\right\},

we have reduced the two-qubit physical system to a logical single-qubit

the Pauli matrix
\sigma_{x}
acting on the logical basis as the
exchange operator:

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\left|1\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|2\right\rangle

(7)

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\left|2\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|1\right\rangle.

(8)

In this simpler formulation, Eq. (
2
) is now mapped to the following
total spin-boson Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H_{S}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\Omega\left(t\right)\sigma_{x}+\hbar\sum_{s}\omega_{s}b_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}

(9)

\displaystyle+\hbar\sigma_{z}\sum_{s}\left(g_{s}b_{s}+g_{s}^{\ast}b_{s}^{\dagger}\right),

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}\left|1\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|1\right\rangle,

(10)

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}\left|2\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left|2\right\rangle,

(11)

and we have defined

\displaystyle g_{s}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle g_{1,s}-g_{2,s}.

(12)

To treat the effects of dephasing on the gate action, we now change

\rho\left(t\right)
to its interaction-picture
counterpart:

\displaystyle\rho_{I}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle U_{0}^{\dagger}\left(t\right)\rho\left(t\right)U_{0}\left(t\right),

(13)

where

\displaystyle U_{0}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\exp\left[-i\Phi\left(t\right)\sigma_{x}\right]\exp\left(-i\sum_{s}\omega_{s}tb_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}\right),

(14)

with

\displaystyle\Phi\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\Omega\left(t^{\prime}\right).

(15)

It is now important to notice that
\rho\left(t\right)
and
\rho_{I}\left(t\right)

are meant to represent density-matrix operators of the whole system,
that is, the system that is composed by the logical qubit and the

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\psi\left(0\right)\right\rangle\left\langle\psi\left(0\right)\right|,

usually we take the initial state of the thermal bath, since it is thermal, as a mixed

\displaystyle\rho_{B}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\left(-\beta\hbar\sum_{s}\omega_{s}b_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}\right)}{Z},

(17)

where

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k_{B}T},

(18)

T

k_{B}
is Boltzmann constant, and

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\left[\exp\left(-\beta\hbar\sum_{s}\omega_{s}b_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}\right)\right]

(19)

In the interaction picture, the corresponding evolution of the global
density-matrix operator is given by

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{I}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[H_{I}\left(t\right),\rho_{I}\left(t\right)\right],

(20)

with

\displaystyle H_{I}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle U_{0}^{\dagger}\left(t\right)H_{int}U_{0}\left(t\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\sigma_{z}\left(t\right)\sum_{s}\left[g_{s}b_{s}\exp\left(-i\omega_{s}t\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.g_{s}^{\ast}b_{s}^{\dagger}\exp\left(i\omega_{s}t\right)\right],

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[i\Phi\left(t\right)\sigma_{x}\right]\sigma_{z}\exp\left[-i\Phi\left(t\right)\sigma_{x}\right]

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}\cos\left[2\Phi\left(t\right)\right]+\sigma_{y}\sin\left[2\Phi\left(t\right)\right].

The interaction-picture global dynamics are thus dictated by Eq. (
20
)
and are, therefore, unitary. That is to say that there is a unitary
operator,
U_{I}\left(t\right),
acting on the qubit and the boson
bath, such that

\displaystyle\rho_{I}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{I}\left(t\right)\rho_{I}\left(0\right)U_{I}^{\dagger}\left(t\right),

(23)

with

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}U_{I}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{I}\left(t\right)U_{I}\left(t\right)

(24)

and

\displaystyle U_{I}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{S}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B},

(25)

where
\mathbb{I}_{B}
is the unitary operator acting on the boson
Hilbert space. In Eq. (
23
), the initial global state here is
prepared at
t=0
as a factored state:

\displaystyle\rho_{I}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\left(0\right)\rho_{B}\left(0\right),

(26)

where
\rho_{S}\left(0\right)
and
\rho_{B}\left(0\right)
are
given by Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
), respectively.
From Eqs. (
23
) and (
26
), it follows that

\displaystyle\rho_{IS}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\left[U_{I}\left(t\right)\rho_{S}\left(0\right)\rho_{B}\left(0\right)U_{I}^{\dagger}\left(t\right)\right],

(27)

which is the reduced density operator that describes the state of
the logical qubit, also in the interaction picture.

From now on let us adopt the convenient index notation in which
\left(\mathbb{I}_{S},\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}\right)=\left(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\right).

We thus can write

\displaystyle U_{I}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}B_{\mu}\left(t\right)\sigma_{\mu},

(28)

noticing that the operators
B_{\mu}\left(t\right),
for
\mu=0,1,2,3,

act only on the boson states. Using Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{IS}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}\sum_{\nu=0}^{3}\sigma_{\mu}\rho_{S}\left(0\right)\sigma_{\nu}M_{\nu,\mu}\left(t\right),

(29)

where we have defined the process-matrix elements

\displaystyle M_{\nu,\mu}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\left[B_{\nu}^{\dagger}\left(t\right)B_{\mu}\left(t\right)\rho_{B}\left(0\right)\right].

(30)

All we have to do next is to calculate the functions

for
\nu,\mu\in\left\{0,1,2,3\right\}.

III
Numerical simulations

Our simulations can be carried out by solving the master equation

;
Chaturvedi1979

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{IS}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\left\{\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\left[H_{I}\left(t\right),\left[H_{I}\left(t^{\prime}\right),\rho_{B}\left(0\right)\rho_{IS}\left(t\right)\right]\right]\right\},

VI
Conclusion

In summary, we have considered the entangling universal quantum gate

\sqrt{\mathrm{SWAP}}
under the perturbation of a dephasing bath
using a spin-boson Hamiltonian. Under the guidance of quantum operational-probabilistic theories, we argue for the importance of the calculation
of Kraus operators, required to implement the completely-positive
maps that are essential in the context of these theories. Usually
one expects us to provide some means to overcome most of the deleterious
effects of the decoherence promoted by the environmental noise. We
accordingly have chosen continuous dynamical decoupling as such a protective
control procedure and have calculated a set of Kraus operators for the
residual dephasing. Inspired by the Bloch-vector trajectories which
describe the reduced-state evolution of the system, in this case starting from
an initial condition, we have prescribed a three-dimensional vector
representation for the whole residual-noise dynamics, proving that
such curves are sufficient to represent their corresponding Kraus-operator
histories. We once again emphasize the relevance of the fact that
the four Kraus-vector trajectories represent the whole dynamics of
the qubit system, independently of its initial state. Finally, we have discussed the
procedure to obtain the process matrix and, thus, the Kraus operators and their visual representation, in the
OPT framework, where only a set of POVM measurement frequencies are assumed known.

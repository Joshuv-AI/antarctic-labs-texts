Convergence Analysis of a Preconditioned Steepest Descent Solver for the Cahn-Hilliard Equation with Logarithmic Potential
Amanda E. Diegel, Cheng Wang, Steven M. Wise

Abstract
In this paper, we provide a theoretical analysis for a preconditioned steepest descent (PSD) iterative solver that improves the computational time of a finite difference numerical scheme for the Cahn-Hilliard equation with Flory-Huggins energy potential. In the numerical design, a convex splitting approach is applied to the chemical potential such that the logarithmic and the surface diffusion terms are treated implicitly while the expansive concave term is treated with an explicit update. The nonlinear and singular nature of the logarithmic energy potential makes the numerical implementation very challenging. However, the positivity-preserving property for the logarithmic arguments, unconditional energy stability, and optimal rate error estimates have been established in a recent work and it has been shown that successful solvers ensure a similar positivity-preserving property at each iteration stage. Therefore, in this work, we will show that the PSD solver ensures a positivity-preserving property at each iteration stage. The PSD solver consists of first computing a search direction (which requires solving a constant-coefficient Poisson-like equation) and then takes a one-parameter optimization step over the search direction in which the Newton iteration becomes very powerful. A theoretical analysis is applied to the PSD iteration solver and a geometric convergence rate is proved for the iteration. In particular, the strict separation property of the numerical solution, which indicates a uniform distance between the numerical solution and the singular limit values of ± 1 \pm 1 for the phase variable, plays an essential role in the iteration convergence analysis. A few numerical results are presented to demonstrate the robustness and efficiency of the PSD solver. Key words. Cahn-Hilliard equation, logarithmic Flory Huggins energy potential, positivity preserving, energy stability, preconditioned steepest descent iteration solver, iteration convergence analysis AMS Subject Classification 35K30, 65L06, 65M12

E(\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\left((1+\phi)\ln(1+\phi)+(1-\phi)\ln(1-\phi)-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\right)d{\bf x},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot({\cal M}(\phi)\nabla\mu),

\displaystyle:=\delta_{\phi}E=\ln(1+\phi)-\ln(1-\phi)-\theta_{0}\phi-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\phi,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(\phi(t))=

\displaystyle\ -\int_{\Omega}{\cal M}(\phi)|\nabla\mu|^{2}d{\bf x}\leq 0.

(1.4)

In addition, the system defined in (

Although the theoretical analysis has been well-established for the first order convex splitting numerical scheme to the Cahn-Hilliard equation (

1.3

The focus of this paper will therefore be centered on the development and analysis of an iterative method for the numerical implementation of a first-order-in-time convex splitting numerical scheme to the Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1.2
) – (

A naïve iterative approach may lead to a numerical solution not satisfying the positivity-preserving property in the iteration process. As an example, the full approximation storage (FAS) multi-grid method was applied in

In this article, we propose and analyze an alternative iterative method, called the preconditioned steepest descent (PSD) solver, for the numerical implementation of the convex splitting numerical scheme to the Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1.2
) – (
1.3
) with Flory-Huggins energy potential. The PSD solver for the p-Laplacian equation was considered in a pioneering work
[
38
]
, while an application of the PSD algorithm to a more general, regularized elliptic equation is analyzed in
[
33
]
, in which a much sharper iteration convergence rate has been established due to the higher order diffusion term involved. More applications of the PSD solver have been reported to various gradient flow models
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
32
,
34
,
63
]
, etc. The robustness of this approach is demonstrated again in the numerical implementation of the algorithm to the Cahn-Hilliard equation with logarithmic energy potential, as described in
[
13
]
. The key point is to use a linearized version of the nonlinear operator as a pre-conditioner to obtain a search direction. In other words, at each iteration stage, the surface diffusion operator and the
(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}
operator (for the temporal derivative) are kept the same as the original form, and a constant-coefficient linear operator is used to approximate the nonlinear part in the chemical potential expansion. In turn, the resulting equation for the search direction is efficiently solved with the help of FFT, since all the linear operators have eigenfunctions that are exactly the same as the Fourier basis functions. Afterward, with the search direction available, a one-parameter optimization of the corresponding numerical energy functional over the search direction is taken at the iteration stage. In fact, it is a strictly convex optimization in terms of the parameter, with singular and monotone logarithmic terms involved. Again, a careful positivity-preserving analysis ensures a unique solution of this one-parameter optimization, and the positivity of the logarithmic arguments are theoretically justified. Since it is a convex optimization, the Newton’s iteration can be efficiently implemented and the positivity property will be preserved in the iteration process if the initial guess is sufficiently accurate.

To verify the advantage of such a numerical solver, we present an iteration convergence analysis of the PSD iteration algorithm. Based on the fact that the equations can be reformulated as minimization problems involving strictly convex functionals in Hilbert spaces, the convexity analysis enables us to theoretically derive the convergence analysis for the nonlinear iterative solver. However, such an analysis is much more challenging than the gradient equations with a polynomial approximation of the energy potential since a positivity-preserving property must be justified at each iteration stage. Moreover, a uniform distance between the numerical solution and the singular limit values (of
\pm 1
), i.e. the strict separation property, must be established to pass through the nonlinear estimates associated with the logarithmic terms. More specifically, the convergence estimate at the previous time step gives a discrete
H_{h}^{1}
bound of the initial iteration error. Meanwhile, the non-increasing numerical energy (at each iteration stage) indicates a uniform discrete
\ell^{2}
bound of the numerical solution in the iteration process. Furthermore, a careful application of a discrete Sobolev embedding establishes a connection between the discrete
\ell^{2}
norm and the corresponding energy norm associated with the preconditioning stage. All these techniques lead to a theoretical justification of the geometric convergence rate for the PSD iteration solver. As a result, an
H_{h}^{1}
convergence estimate for the iteration error leads to the strict separation property of the numerical solution at the next iteration stage, with the help of an inverse inequality. To our knowledge, this is the first result of a nonlinear iteration convergence for an iteration solver applied to a singular energy potential gradient flow.

2.3
Strict separation property of the numerical solution and other preliminaries

In this subsection, we derive a strict separation property for the numerical solution of (

\overline{\Phi_{N}^{m}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\,\Phi_{N}(\cdot,t_{m})\,d{\bf x}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\,\Phi_{N}(\cdot,t_{m-1})\,d{\bf x}=\overline{\Phi_{N}^{m-1}},\quad\forall\ m\in\mathbb{N},

Lemma 2.5
.

Let the initial data

Lemma 2.6

3
The preconditioned steepest descent iteration solver

In this section, we present the preconditioned steepest descent iteration solver. For the numerical solution of (
2.6
), we consider the discrete operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{h}(\phi):=(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{k})+{\Delta t}(\ln(1+\phi)-\ln(1-\phi))-\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta_{h}\phi,

(3.1)

and we set

\displaystyle f=\theta_{0}{\Delta t}\phi^{k}.

(3.2)

Hence, given
\phi^{k}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
, solving the numerical scheme (
2.6
) for
\phi^{k+1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
is equivalent to solving the following nonlinear system

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{h}(\phi)=f,\quad\mbox{for}\,\,\,\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}.

(3.3)

It should be understood that the analysis focuses on a single iteration of the numerical method (
2.6
) and we thus utilize the notation
\phi:=\phi^{k+1}
throughout the remainder of the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\phi^{k}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
be given. Define the discrete energy

\displaystyle J_{h}(\phi):=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi-\phi^{k}\|_{-1,h}^{2}+{\Delta t}(\langle 1+\phi,\ln(1+\phi)\rangle+\langle 1-\phi,\ln(1-\phi)\rangle)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\phi\|_{2}^{2}-\langle f,\phi\rangle,

(3.4)

over the admissible set

\displaystyle W_{h}:=\left\{\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}\Big|\phi-\phi^{k}\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}\right\}.

(3.5)

Then, solving (
3.3
) is equivalent to minimizing
J_{h}(\phi)
.

Proof.

A direct calculation reveals that
J_{h}
is twice Gataeux differentiable and convex. Specifically, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi)(v)=\langle(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{k})+{\Delta t}(\ln(1+\phi)-\ln(1-\phi)),v\rangle+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\langle\nabla_{h}\phi,\nabla_{h}v\rangle-\langle f,v\rangle,

(3.6)

for any
v\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
, and

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\phi)(v,w)=\langle(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}v,w\rangle+{\Delta t}\left\langle\frac{1}{1+\phi}+\frac{1}{1-\phi},vw\right\rangle+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\langle\nabla_{h}v,\nabla_{h}w\rangle,

(3.7)

for any
v,w\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
. This implies the convexity of
J_{h}
, due to the fact that

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\phi)(v,v)=\langle(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}v,v\rangle+{\Delta t}\left\langle\frac{1}{1+\phi}+\frac{1}{1-\phi},v^{2}\right\rangle+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\langle\nabla_{h}v,\nabla_{h}v\rangle\geq 0.

(3.8)

Setting
\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi)(v)=0
concludes the proof.
∎

In a steepest descent approach for finding the minimizer of
J_{h}(\phi)
, the general strategy is to find the normalized steepest descent direction
d_{n}\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
such that

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(v)(d_{n})=-\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(v)\right\|_{*},

(3.9)

under the restriction that

\displaystyle\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}}=1,

(3.10)

for all
v\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
, where
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}}
is a norm on
\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
and
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{*}
is the standard dual norm defined by

\displaystyle\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(v)\right\|_{*}:=\sup_{u\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}}\frac{|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(v)(u)|}{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}}}.

(3.11)

However, the steepest descent method is not optimal. We therefore introduce preconditioning. Specifically, we introduce the operator
A_{h}
such that

\displaystyle A_{h}\psi:=(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\psi+{\Delta t}\psi-\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta_{h}\psi,

(3.12)

for
\psi\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
. This operator is clearly symmetric and positive definite. In fact, the standard steepest descent solver would lead to a very slow iteration convergence rate, especially for a high-dimensional optimization problem with a high condition number. A preconditioning approach, such as the one given by (
3.12
), provides a search direction much closer to the exact error than the standard gradient direction. In fact, among the operators involved in the nonlinear scheme (
3.1
), a linearized approach has to be taken. In particular, the temporal derivative and the surface diffusion parts correspond to constant-coefficient linear terms, so that the form of these two linear operators is exactly kept in the preconditioning process (
3.12
). Meanwhile, the nonlinear term
{\Delta t}(\ln(1+\phi)-\ln(1-\phi))
is monotone, and its linearized Lipschitz constant, which relies on its derivative,
\frac{{\Delta t}}{1-\phi^{2}}
, is expected to be of
O({\Delta t})
, provided that the separation property is satisfied. In turn, we take a linear term,
{\Delta t}\psi
, to approximate the change associated with the nonlinear term. Such a combined choice will greatly improve the iteration convergence rate for a convex optimization with elliptic structure, as will be demonstrated by the theoretical analysis in the later sections. Also see the related analysis in
[
33
]
.

With this operator at our disposal, the preconditioned steepest descent method is defined via the following algorithm.

Algorithm 1

Preconditioned Steepest Descent Solver

Define
\phi^{(0)}:=\phi^{k}
.

For
n\geq 0
, solve

\displaystyle A_{h}d_{n}=f-\mathcal{N}_{h}(\phi^{(n)}),

(3.13)

for
d_{n}\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
.

Determine step length
\bar{\alpha}
via

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}={\rm argmin}_{\alpha}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)}+\alpha d_{n})={\rm argzero}_{\alpha}\Big\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)}+\alpha d_{n}),d_{n}\Big\rangle.

(3.14)

Set

\displaystyle\phi^{(n+1)}=\phi^{(n)}+\bar{\alpha}d_{n}.

(3.15)

Remark 3.2
.

Equation (
3.13
) can be exactly and efficiently implemented by an FFT-based finite difference solver.

Remark 3.3
.

It is observed that
J_{h}
is a strictly convex function, and the convex energy terms,
\langle 1+\phi,\ln(1+\phi)\rangle
,
\langle 1-\phi,\ln(1-\phi)\rangle
, have singular and monotone derivatives as
\phi\searrow-1
and
\phi\nearrow 1
. In turn, the one-parameter function in (
3.14
), namely
\Big\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)}+\alpha d_{n}),d_{n}\Big\rangle
, is strictly convex in terms of
\alpha
, and this one-parameter function has singular and monotone derivatives as
(\phi^{(n)}+\alpha d_{n})\to\pm 1
. As a result, with an application of the positivity-preserving analysis technique reported in
[
13
]
, there is a unique solution to this one-dimensional optimization problem, with
-1<\phi^{(n+1)}<1
, at a point-wise level.

4
The geometric convergence of the preconditioned steepest descent solver

We will now show that the preconditioned steepest descent solver has a geometric convergence rate. The proof of this fact requires the following lemmas and corollary.

Lemma 4.1
.

The search direction
d_{n}
defined in (
3.13
) is the steepest descent direction, at
\phi^{(n)}\in W_{h}
with respect to the norm
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{A_{h}}
, where

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}=\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{-1,h}^{2}+{\Delta t}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}u_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(4.1)

Proof.

The proof follows similarly to that found in
[
14
]
. By definition, the normalized steepest decent direction at the point
v\in W_{h}
is a vector
d\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
satisfying (
3.9
). From (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\langle A_{h}d_{n},v\rangle=-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})(v),

(4.2)

for all
v\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
. Note that
d_{n}
is the Riesz representation of the functional
\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})
in the space
\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
with respect to the norm
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{A_{h}}
. Hence

\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}}=\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|_{*},

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})(d_{n})

\displaystyle=-\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}=-\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|_{*}\cdot\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}},

(4.4)

for all
d_{n}\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\{\phi^{(n)}\}
be the sequence generated by (
3.15
). Then we have

\displaystyle J_{h}(\phi^{(n+1)})\leq J_{h}(\phi^{(n)}).

(4.5)

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
u,v\in\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm per}
, the following inequality is valid

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(u)-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(v),u-v\rangle\geq C_{\rm LB}\left\|u-v\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2},

(4.6)

with
C_{\rm LB}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\varepsilon{\Delta t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\}
.

Proof.

A careful calculation reveals that

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(u)-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle={\Delta t}\langle\ln(1+u)-\ln(1+v)-\ln(1-u)+\ln(1-v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\|u-v\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\nabla_{h}(u-v)\|_{2}^{2}.

(4.7)

Meanwhile, the following estimates are available by the convexity of the logarithmic terms:

\displaystyle\langle\ln(1+u)-\ln(1+v),u-v\rangle\geq 0,

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle\langle-\ln(1-u)+\ln(1-v),u-v\rangle\geq 0.

(4.9)

As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(u)-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\|u-v\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}(u-v)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}(u-v)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\sqrt{{\Delta t}}\|u-v\|_{2}^{2},

(4.10)

in which we have utilized Proposition
2.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}(u-v)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{{\Delta t}}\|u-v\|_{-1,h}\cdot\left\|\nabla_{h}(u-v)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{{\Delta t}}\|u-v\|_{2}^{2}.

(4.11)

In comparison with the form of
\left\|u-v\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\left\|u-v\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}=\|u-v\|_{-1,h}^{2}+{\Delta t}\left\|u-v\right\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}(u-v)\right\|_{2}^{2},

(4.12)

we conclude that estimate (
4.6
) is valid by choosing
C_{\rm LB}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\varepsilon{\Delta t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\{\phi^{(n)}\}
be the sequence generated by (
3.15
). Furthermore, suppose that

1+\phi^{(n)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},\quad 1-\phi^{(n)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},

(4.13)

at a point-wise level and define the iteration error
e_{n}:=J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})-J_{h}(\phi)
. Then we have

\displaystyle e_{n}\leq\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi),\phi^{(n)}-\phi\rangle\leq C_{\rm UB}\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|_{*}^{2},

(4.14)

and

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\theta^{n})(d_{n},d_{n})\right|\leq C_{\rm D2}\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2},

(4.15)

for any
\theta^{n}
in the line segment from
\phi^{(n)}
to
\phi^{(n+1)}
, where the constants
C_{\rm UB}
,
C_{\rm D2}
have the following forms:

\displaystyle C_{\rm UB}=C_{\rm LB}^{-1}=\max\left\{2,\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\},\quad\text{and}\quad C_{\rm D2}=1+4\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.16)

Proof.

By the properties of the convexity, we have

\displaystyle e_{n}=J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})-J_{h}(\phi)\leq\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi),\phi^{(n)}-\phi\rangle.

(4.17)

Using the fact that
\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi)\equiv 0
, combined with an application of inequality (
4.6
) (from Lemma
4.3
) and Young’s inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi),\phi^{(n)}-\phi\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)}),\phi^{(n)}-\phi\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|_{*}\left\|\phi-\phi^{(n)}\right\|_{A_{h}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{\rm LB}^{-1}\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|_{*}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}C_{\rm LB}\left\|\phi-\phi^{(n)}\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{\rm LB}^{-1}\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|_{*}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi),\phi^{(n)}-\phi).

(4.18)

Therefore, we can take constant
C_{\rm UB}=C_{\rm LB}^{-1}=\max\left\{2,\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}
, such that

\displaystyle e^{n}\leq\langle\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})-\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi),\phi^{(n)}-\phi\rangle\leq C_{\rm UB}\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|_{*}^{2}.

(4.19)

Next we derive an estimate for
\left|\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\theta^{n})(d_{n},d_{n})\right|
. We begin by applying the discrete Hölder inequality to (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) to obtain the following bounds:

\displaystyle|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi)(v)|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{-1,h}\cdot\left\|v\right\|_{-1,h}+S_{0}{\Delta t}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|v\right\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}\phi\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|\nabla_{h}v\right\|_{2}+\left\|f\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|v\right\|_{2},

(4.20)

and

\displaystyle|\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\phi)(v,w)|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v\right\|_{-1,h}\cdot\left\|w\right\|_{-1,h}+S_{1}{\Delta t}\left\|v\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|w\right\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}v\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|\nabla_{h}w\right\|_{2},

(4.21)

where

\displaystyle S_{0}=\left\|\ln\left(\frac{1+\phi}{1-\phi}\right)\right\|_{\infty},\quad\text{and}\quad S_{1}=\left\|\frac{2}{1-\phi^{2}}\right\|_{\infty}.

(4.22)

With the strict separation assumption (
4.13
) at hand, the following bounds for
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
become available:

\displaystyle S_{0}=\left\|\ln\frac{1+\phi^{k+1,(n)}}{1-\phi^{k+1,(n)}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\ln(4\epsilon_{0}^{-1}),\quad\text{and}\quad S_{1}=\left\|\frac{2}{1-(\phi^{k+1,(n)})^{2}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 8\epsilon_{0}^{-1}.

(4.23)

Thus, with an application of (
4.21
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\theta^{n})(d_{n},d_{n})\right|\leq\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{-1,h}^{2}+S_{1}{\Delta t}\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(4.24)

On the other hand, an application of the discrete Sobolev inequality (
2.4
) (in Proposition
2.2
) indicates that

\displaystyle\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq 2\varepsilon{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{-1,h}\cdot\left\|\nabla_{h}d_{n}\right\|_{2}\geq 2\varepsilon{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(4.25)

Consequently, a substitution of (
4.25
) into (
4.24
) yields

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\theta^{n})(d_{n},d_{n})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}+S_{1}{\Delta t}\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+4\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)(\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}).

(4.26)

In comparison with the form of
\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}=\|d_{n}\|_{-1,h}^{2}+{\Delta t}\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\left\|\nabla_{h}d_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2},

(4.27)

we conclude that estimate (
4.15
) is valid by choosing
C_{\rm D2}=1+4\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. ∎

Remark 4.5
.

We see that
C_{\rm UB}=O(1)
if
{\Delta t}=O(\varepsilon^{2})
, while
C_{\rm UB}=O(\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}})
with a small
\varepsilon
value. A similar scaling law is available for
C_{\rm D2}
. Specifically,
C_{\rm D2}=O(\epsilon_{0}^{-1})
if
{\Delta t}=O(\varepsilon^{2})
, while
C_{\rm D2}=O(\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}})
with a small
\varepsilon
value.

Theorem 4.6
.

Under the separation assumption (
2.15
) for the exact PDE solution, let
\{\phi^{(n)}\}
be the sequence generated by (
3.15
). Furthermore, it is assumed that

1+\phi^{(n)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},\quad 1-\phi^{(n)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},

(4.28)

at a point-wise level. Then we have

\displaystyle e_{n}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n}e_{0},\quad\text{with}\quad\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}<1.

(4.29)

Proof.

From the definition of the steepest descent direction, we apply (
4.15
) (from Lemma
4.4
) and get the following inequality, for an arbitrary
\alpha
:

\displaystyle J_{h}(\phi^{(n)}+\alpha d_{n})-J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\alpha\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})(d_{n})+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\theta)(d_{n},d_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})(d_{n})+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}C_{\rm D2}\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{A_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big(-\alpha+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}C_{\rm D2}\big)\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|^{2}_{*}.

(4.30)

Hence, the minimum is achieved at
\bar{\alpha}=\frac{1}{C_{\rm D2}}
and we have

\displaystyle e_{n+1}-e_{n}

\displaystyle=J_{h}(\phi^{(n+1)})-J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})

\displaystyle\leq J_{h}(\phi^{(n)}+\bar{\alpha}d_{n})-J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm D2}}\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|^{2}_{*}.

(4.31)

Therefore,

\displaystyle e_{n}-e_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2C_{\rm D2}}\left\|\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi^{(n)})\right\|^{2}_{*}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}e_{n},

(4.32)

in which the estimate (
4.14
) of Lemma
4.4
was applied in the last step. Hence,

\displaystyle e_{n+1}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)e_{n},

(4.33)

and the desired result follows.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

A geometric convergence rate is assured by Theorem
4.6
. Regarding the convergence constant, we observe that
C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}=O(\epsilon_{0}^{-1})
for a time step choice of
{\Delta t}=O(\varepsilon^{2})
, while
C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}=O(\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-2}{\Delta t})
with a small
\varepsilon
value. In turn, this estimate leads to a convergence rate of
\alpha_{0}^{n}
, with
\alpha_{0}=1-O({\Delta t}^{-1}\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{2}})
such that
0<\alpha_{0}<1
for
{\Delta t}=O(\varepsilon^{2})
.

This analysis also verifies the following well-known fact observed in the extensive numerical experiments: the steepest descent nonlinear iteration provides a fast convergence for a small time step size. Specifically, with a smaller value of
\varepsilon
, the numerical implementation becomes more and more challenging. Fortunately, the choice of a small time step size also accelerates the convergence speed for a small value of
\varepsilon
.

In fact, because of a general estimate
C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}=O(\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-2}{\Delta t})
, we see that an
O(1)
geometric convergence rate is ensured if the time step size satisfies a constraint
{\Delta t}\varepsilon^{-2}=O(\epsilon_{0})\ll 1
. Meanwhile, for the first order scheme (
2.6
), the
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;H_{h}^{-1})\cap\ell^{2}(0,T;H_{h}^{1})
convergence analysis, as presented in
[
13
]
, only requires a constraint
{\Delta t}\leq\varepsilon^{2}
. This constraint is milder than the
O(1)
geometric iteration convergence rate requirement, since the convexity analysis has greatly helped the error estimate. On the other hand, if an
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\ell^{2})\cap\ell^{2}(0,T;H_{h}^{2})
error analysis is derived for the numerical scheme, a more severe time step constraint would be needed in the estimates, due to the complicated nonlinear expansion structure. Also see the related work
[
47
]
, in which the
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\ell^{2})
error estimate has been presented for the Poisson-Nernst-Planck system.

Remark 4.8
.

If the first order numerical scheme (
2.6
) could be exactly implemented, the positivity-preserving, energy stability and optimal rate convergence analysis would be unconditional, i.e., the convergence estimate (
2.13
) would be always valid, and there is no constraint for the time step size
{\Delta t}
. Meanwhile, in terms of the PSD iteration solver to implement the numerical algorithm (
2.6
), the above estimates imply that, the iteration convergence rate will be greatly accelerated with an additional constraint
{\Delta t}\leq O(\epsilon_{0}\varepsilon^{2})
. However, even if such an additional constraint is not satisfied, the iteration estimate (
4.29
) still indicates a geometric convergence rate for the PSD iteration, although the convergence speed will not be as good as the one with the additional constraint
{\Delta t}\leq O(\epsilon_{0}\varepsilon^{2})
. Extensive numerical experiments have revealed that, five to ten iteration stages would be sufficient for the implementation in most practical computational examples, with reasonable physical parameters and time step sizes.

The contraction estimate (
4.29
) is valid for the error of the discrete energy (
3.4
). Meanwhile, such a contraction estimate is not directly available for the numerical error associated with the phase variable at the
k+1
time step:
q_{n}:=\phi^{(n)}-\phi
. However, we are still able to derive a geometric convergence estimate for such a numerical error. As in the previous section, we define
\phi:=\phi^{k+1}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let the initial data
\Phi(\,\cdot\,,t=0)\in C^{6}_{\rm per}(\Omega)
and suppose the exact solution
\Phi
for the Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1.2
) – (
1.3
) is of regularity class
\mathcal{R}
. Additionally, suppose that the exact solution for the Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1.2
) – (
1.3
) satisfies the separation property (
2.15
). Let
\phi^{(n)}
be the sequence generated by (
3.15
) and define the numerical error associated with the phase variable at the
(k+1)
-th time step to be
q_{n}:=\phi^{(n)}-\phi
. Then, for any
n\geq 0
, provided
{\Delta t}
and
h
are sufficiently small and we take a linear refinement of
{\Delta t}
such that
C_{L}h\leq{\Delta t}\leq C_{U}h
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}q_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2C_{\rm R}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n}({\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.34)

and

\displaystyle\|q_{n}\|_{-1,h}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n}({\Delta t}^{\frac{3}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.35)

which implies that

\displaystyle\|q_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{h}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}+2C_{\rm R}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n}({\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.36)

with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}<1.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by induction. The base case with
n=0
follows from inequalities (
2.26
) and (
2.27
) and the fact that
\Delta t,h>0
are chosen small enough.

Now, let

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}q_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2C_{\rm R}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n}({\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.37)

and

\displaystyle\|q_{n}\|_{-1,h}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n}({\Delta t}^{\frac{3}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.38)

so that

\displaystyle\|q_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{h}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}+2C_{\rm R}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n}({\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.39)

for some
n>0
. As a result, the following iteration error estimate becomes available:

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}q_{n}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{-1}C_{\rm R}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{4}}+h^{\frac{3}{4}}),

(4.40)

in which the fact that
\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)<1
has been applied. Its substitution into the discrete inverse inequality (
2.20
) leads to

\|q_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{\rm Inv}\|\nabla_{h}q_{n}\|_{2}}{h^{\delta_{0}}}\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{-1}C_{\rm Inv}C_{\rm R}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{8}}+h^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},

(4.41)

for
\delta_{0}<\frac{1}{8}
, and provided that
{\Delta t}
and
h
are sufficiently small. A combination of (
4.41
) and the strict separation property (
2.18
) (for the exact numerical solution of (
2.6
), at
m=k+1
) results in

1+\phi^{(n)},\,\,1-\phi^{(n)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4},\quad\mbox{at a point-wise level}.

(4.42)

Additionally, the following functional inequality is available:

\displaystyle J_{h}(\phi^{(n+1)})-J_{h}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi)(q_{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\beta)(q_{n+1},q_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\phi\phi}J_{h}(\beta)(q_{n+1},q_{n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|q_{n+1}\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}q_{n+1}\|_{2}^{2},

(4.43)

with
\beta
in the line segment from
\phi^{(n)}
to
\phi
. Note that the second step comes from the facts that
\delta_{\phi}J_{h}(\phi)\equiv 0
and

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{1}{1+\theta}+\frac{1}{1-\theta},q_{n+1}^{2}\right\rangle\geq 0.

(4.44)

As a direct consequence, a combination of Lemma
4.4
and Theorem
4.6
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|q_{n+1}\|_{-1,h}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}}{2}\|\nabla_{h}q_{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e_{n+1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n+1}e_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n+1}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi-\phi^{k}\|_{-1,h}^{2}+{\Delta t}R_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{\Delta t}\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n+1}R_{k},

(4.45)

where
R_{k}
has been defined in Lemma
2.6
and we have used the fact that

\displaystyle-\langle f,\phi^{(o)}\rangle+\langle f,\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\phi^{k+1}\rangle-\langle f,\phi^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\phi^{k+1}-\phi^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}{\Delta t}\langle\phi^{k},\phi^{k+1}-\phi^{k}\rangle.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}q_{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2C_{\rm R}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n+1}({\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.46)

and

\displaystyle\|q_{n+1}\|_{-1,h}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}\right)^{n+1}({\Delta t}^{\frac{3}{2}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(4.47)

with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2C_{\rm UB}C_{\rm D2}}<1.

An application of Proposition
2.2
and the definition of the discrete
H^{1}
norm concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

For simplicity of presentation, we only provide the analysis for the 2-D Cahn-Hilliard equation (

.

For the sake of brevity, we only consider a constant mobility,

.

6
Concluding remarks

In this article, the preconditioned steepest descent (PSD) iteration solver is considered to implement a finite difference numerical scheme for the Cahn-Hilliard equation with Flory-Huggins energy potential. A convex-concave decomposition is applied to the energy functional, and the convex splitting numerical approximation to the chemical potential: implicit treatment for the singular logarithmic term and the surface diffusion term, combined with an explicit update for the expansive concave term. The positivity-preserving analysis, unconditional energy stability, and the optimal rate error estimate have been theoretically derived in a recent work. In terms of the numerical implementation of this nonlinear and singular numerical scheme, we propose a preconditioned steepest descent iteration solver in the computation, based on the fact that the implicit parts of the numerical scheme are associated with a strictly convex energy. This iteration solver consists of a computation of the search direction (involved with a Poisson-like equation), and a one-parameter optimization over the search direction. At a theoretical level, a geometric convergence rate is proved for the PSD iteration, and the positivity-preserving property is theoretically established at each iteration stage in the process. Moreover, a uniform distance estimate between the numerical solution and the singular limit values of
\pm 1
for the phase variable has played an essential role in the theoretical analysis. Such an iteration convergence analysis and positivity-preserving analysis is a first for a phase field model with a singular energy potential. A few numerical examples are presented to demonstrate the robustness and efficiency of the PSD solver.

Linear damping and depletion in flowing plasma with strong sheared magnetic fields
Han Liu, Nader Masmoudi, Cuili Zhai, Weiren Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the long-time behavior of the solution for the linearized ideal MHD around sheared velocity and magnetic field under Stern stability condition. We prove that the velocity and magnetic field will converge to sheared velocity and magnetic field as time approaches infinity. Moreover a new depletion phenomenon is proved: the horizontal velocity and magnetic field at the critical points will decay to 0 0 as time approaches infinity.



1.
Introduction

The appearance of large coherent structures is an important phenomena in the magnetic fluid. The study of the long-time behavior of MHD waves is a very active field in physics and mathematics
[
5
,
11
,
24
]
.

1.1.
The linearized MHD system

In this paper, we consider the magnetic fluid described by the two-dimensional incompressible ideal MHD equations on the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\{(x,y)|x\in\mathbb{T},y\in\mathbb{T}\}

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\mathcal{U}+\mathcal{U}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{U}-\mathcal{H}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{H}+\nabla\mathcal{P}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{U}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{H}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{U}=0,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathcal{U}=0,\ \ \nabla\cdot\mathcal{H}=0,\end{array}\right.

with initial data
\mathcal{U}(0,x,y)
and
\mathcal{H}(0,x,y)
.
Here
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
,
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
and
\mathcal{P}
denote the velocity field, magnetic field, and the total pressure of the magnetic fluid, respectively.

System (
1.1
) has an equilibrium
\mathcal{U}_{s}=(u(y),0)
,
\mathcal{H}_{s}=(b(y),0)
,
\mathcal{P}_{s}=\text{const.}
. We shall focus on the asymptotic behavior of the linearized 2D MHD equations around this equilibrium, which take the form

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}U_{1}+u\partial_{x}U_{1}+\partial_{x}p+u^{\prime}U_{2}-b\partial_{x}H_{1}-b^{\prime}H_{2}=0,\\
\partial_{t}U_{2}+u\partial_{x}U_{2}+\partial_{y}p-b\partial_{x}H_{2}=0,\\
\partial_{t}H_{1}+u\partial_{x}H_{1}+b^{\prime}U_{2}-b\partial_{x}U_{1}-u^{\prime}H_{2}=0,\\
\partial_{t}H_{2}+u\partial_{x}H_{2}-b\partial_{x}U_{2}=0,\\
\nabla\cdot U=0,\ \ \nabla\cdot H=0.\end{array}\right.

We introduce the vorticity
\omega=\partial_{x}U_{2}-\partial_{y}U_{1}
and the current density
j=\partial_{x}H_{2}-\partial_{y}H_{1}
which satisfy the following equations:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\omega+u\partial_{x}\omega-b\partial_{x}j=u^{\prime\prime}U_{2}-b^{\prime\prime}H_{2},\\
\partial_{t}j+u\partial_{x}j-b\partial_{x}\omega=b^{\prime\prime}U_{2}-u^{\prime\prime}H_{2}+u^{\prime}\partial_{x}H_{1}-u^{\prime}\partial_{y}H_{2}+b^{\prime}\partial_{y}U_{2}-b^{\prime}\partial_{x}U_{1}.\end{array}\right.

We further introduce the stream function
\psi
and the magnetic potential function
\phi,
satisfying
U=(\partial_{y}\psi,-\partial_{x}\psi)
,
\omega=-\Delta\psi
and
H=(\partial_{y}\phi,-\partial_{x}\phi)
,
j=-\Delta\phi,
which allow us to derive the following system, satisfied by
(\psi,\phi)
:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}(\Delta\psi)+u\partial_{x}(\Delta\psi)-b\partial_{x}(\Delta\phi)=u^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}\psi-b^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}\phi,\\
\partial_{t}(\Delta\phi)+u\partial_{x}(\Delta\phi)-b\partial_{x}(\Delta\psi)=b^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}\psi-u^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}\phi-2u^{\prime}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\phi+2b^{\prime}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\psi.\end{array}\right.

Taking the Fourier transform in
x
and inverting the operator
(\partial_{y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}),
we rewrite the system as

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\Big(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widehat{\psi}\\
\widehat{\phi}\\
\end{array}\Big)(t,\alpha,y)=-i\alpha M_{\alpha}\Big(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widehat{\psi}\\
\widehat{\phi}\\
\end{array}\Big)(t,\alpha,y),\\
&(\widehat{U}_{1},\widehat{U}_{2})=(\partial_{y}\widehat{\psi},-i\alpha\widehat{\psi}),\ (\widehat{H}_{1},\widehat{H}_{2})=(\partial_{y}\widehat{\phi},-i\alpha\widehat{\phi}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha\neq 0
and

(1.6)

M_{\alpha}=-\Delta_{\alpha}^{-1}\left[\begin{matrix}&u^{\prime\prime}-u\Delta_{\alpha}&-b^{\prime\prime}+b\Delta_{\alpha}\\
&b\Delta_{\alpha}+b^{\prime\prime}+2b^{\prime}\partial_{y}&-u\Delta_{\alpha}-u^{\prime\prime}-2u^{\prime}\partial_{y}\end{matrix}\right].

For the homogenous equilibrium
u=0,\,b(y)=\text{const.}
,
\widehat{U}_{2}
and
\widehat{H}_{2}
satisfy a 1-D wave equation, which is stable but exhibits no decay. There are few rigorous mathematical results on the non-flowing plasma with inhomogeneous sheared magnetic field. In
[
18
]
, Tataronis and Grossmann predicted that the vertical components of velocity and magnetic field may decay by phase mixing, to which a mathematically rigorous proof was given by Ren and Zhao in
[
15
]
, under the condition that the magnetic field is positive and strictly monotone. If the positivity assumption on the magnetic field is removed, which allows the direction of the sheared magnetic field to change, then it turns out that magnetic reconnection occurs in infinite time, generating the magnetic island. This phenomenon was predicted by Hirota, Tatsuno and Yoshida
[
6
]
and later justified by Zhai, Zhang and Zhao
[
22
]
. For the flowing plasma
u\neq 0
, fewer mathematical rigorous results are available. We refer to
[
6
,
14
,
22
]
for the long time behaviors of the solutions to the MHD equations linearized around a flowing plasma.

Notations:
Let us specify the notations to be used throughout the paper. We denote by
A\lesssim B
an estimate of the form
A\leq CB
and by
A\sim B
an estimate of the form
C^{-1}B\leq A\leq CB,
where
C
is a constant. Given a function
f(x,y),
we denote its Fourier transform in
x
-variable as
\widehat{f}(\alpha,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-ix\alpha}f(x,y)dx,
where
\alpha
is the wave number. We shall use the Japanese bracket notation
\langle x\rangle:=\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}.

1.2.
Vertical damping and horizontal depletion

In this paper, we focus on the long time behavior of the solution to the linearized MHD equation (
1.5
).

Our first main result states as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Vertical damping)
.

Let
u,b\in C^{3}(\mathbb{T})
be such that
b>|u|\geq 0
and the critical points of
(u\pm b)
are non-degenerate. Let
\alpha\neq 0
be a fixed wave number and let
\left(\widehat{\psi},\widehat{\phi}\right)
solve (
1.5
) with initial data
\left(\widehat{\psi}_{0},\widehat{\phi}_{0}\right)\in(H^{3}\times H^{3})
. Then the following space-time estimate holds:

(1.7)

\left\|\left(\widehat{\psi},\widehat{\phi}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{y}}\leq C_{\alpha}\left\|\left(\widehat{\psi}_{0},\widehat{\phi}_{0}\right)\right\|_{H^{3}_{y}}.

In particular,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left\|\left(\widehat{U}_{2},\,\widehat{H}_{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}}=0
.

Remark 1.2
.

The condition
|u|<|b|
is called Stern stability condition (see
[
16
]
).

Remark 1.3
.

Formally, the space-time estimate may indicate that
\left\|\left(\widehat{U}_{2},\,\widehat{H}_{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle t\rangle^{\beta}}
with
\beta>{\frac{1}{2}}
. The study of the precise decay rate shall be our forthcoming work.

Remark 1.4
.

For the case of flowing plasma with constant velocity or non-flowing plasma
(u=\mathrm{const}.)
, it holds that

(1.8)

\|U(t),H(t)\|_{H^{k}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}\sim\|U_{0},H_{0}\|_{H^{k}_{x}L^{2}_{y}},\ k\geq 0,

which implies linear growth of vorticity and current density, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\omega(t),j(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle\|\omega_{0},j_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.

The proof can be found in Appendix C.
By (
1.8
), the total energy is almost conserved. Hence, the vertical damping in Theorem
1.1
shows that energy is transferred from the vertical direction to the horizontal direction. Whether similar energy conservation results are true for the flowing plasma with
u\not=\mathrm{const.}
remains an open question.

The vertical damping is induced by a certain mixing mechanism similar to the vorticity mixing that leads to inviscid damping for linearized Euler equations (see
[
9
,
10
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
23
]
). Readers may consult
[
2
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
]
for recent progress in nonlinear inviscid damping.

To better illustrate the mixing mechanism, let us recall the system in terms of
({U}_{1},{H}_{1})
:

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}U_{1}+u\partial_{x}U_{1}-b\partial_{x}H_{1}=L_{1},\\
\partial_{t}H_{1}+u\partial_{x}H_{1}-b\partial_{x}U_{1}=L_{2},\end{array}\right.

where
(L_{1},L_{2}):=\left(b^{\prime}H_{2}-u^{\prime}U_{2}-2\partial_{x}\Delta^{-1}(b^{\prime}\partial_{x}H_{2}-u^{\prime}\partial_{x}U_{2}),u^{\prime}H_{2}-b^{\prime}U_{2}\right)
can be seen as nonlocal forcing terms depending on
{U}_{2}
and
{H}_{2}.

By the incompressibility condition, we can check that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{x}{U}_{1},\partial_{x}{H}_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-1}_{y}}\sim\|({U}_{2},{H}_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.

Then the mixing of
({U}_{1}\pm{H}_{1})
would lead to the linear damping of
({U}_{2},{H}_{2}).

Let us consider a toy model, obtained by neglecting the nonlocal forcing terms
(L_{1},L_{2})
in the linearized system (
1.9
), i.e.,

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}U_{1}+u\partial_{x}U_{1}-b\partial_{x}H_{1}=0,\\
\partial_{t}H_{1}+u\partial_{x}H_{1}-b\partial_{x}U_{1}=0.\end{array}\right.

It is then easy to see that the Elsässer variables
\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{\pm}:=U_{1}\pm H_{1}
satisfy certain transport equations and then

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\widehat{U}_{1}+\widehat{H}_{1}\right)(t,\alpha,y)=\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{+}_{1,in}(\alpha,y)e^{-i\alpha(u-b)t},\\
\left(\widehat{U}_{1}-\widehat{H}_{1}\right)(t,\alpha,y)=\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{-}_{1,in}(\alpha,y)e^{-i\alpha(u+b)t},\end{split}

with
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{\pm}_{1,in}
denoting the initial data.

Regarding the toy model (
1.10
), we have the following conclusions.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
u,b\in C^{3}(\mathbb{T})
be such that
u\pm b
have only non-degenerate critical points. Then the solution of (
1.10
) with initial data
({U}_{1,in},{H}_{1,in})
satisfies

(1.12)

\|(\partial_{x}{U}_{1},\partial_{x}{H}_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-1}_{y}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|({U}_{1,in},{H}_{1,in})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}H^{1}_{y}}.

Moreover, if the initial data
({U}_{1,in},{H}_{1,in})
vanish at all the critical points of
(u\pm b)
, then it holds that

(1.13)

\|(\partial_{x}{U}_{1},\partial_{x}{H}_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-1}_{y}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle t\rangle}\|({U}_{1,in},{H}_{1,in})\|_{H^{-1}_{x}H^{2}_{y}}.

The proof of (
1.12
) can be found in
[
20
]
; via the same dual method one can prove (
1.13
). In fact, the decay rate of
t^{-1/2}
for the toy model (
1.10
) is optimal, as we know, via the classical stationary phase approximation (see Chapter VIII of
[
17
]
), that there exists a class of initial data such that the corresponding solutions satisfy

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{x}{U}_{1},\partial_{x}{H}_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-1}_{y}}\sim\frac{1}{\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

The space-time estimate in Theorem (
1.1
), however, fails to hold for the toy model (
1.10
). Exploring the mechanisms behind the enhanced damping for the complete system (
1.9
), we found a new dynamical phenomenon apart from velocity mixing: the depletion of horizontal velocity and magnetic field
({U}_{1},{H}_{1})
at the critical points of
u\pm b
. This leads to our second main result, which states as follows:

Theorem 1.6

(Horizontal depletion)
.

Let
u,b
satisfy the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
.
Let
y_{0}
be a critical point of
(u+b)
or
(u-b)
, i.e.,
u^{\prime}(y_{0})=b^{\prime}(y_{0})
or
u^{\prime}(y_{0})=-b^{\prime}(y_{0})
. Let
\left(\widehat{U}_{1},\widehat{H}_{1}\right)
correspond to the solution to (
1.5
) with initial data
\left(\widehat{\psi}_{0},\widehat{\phi}_{0}\right)\in(H^{3}\times H^{3})
. Then it holds that

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left|\left(\widehat{U}_{1},\,\widehat{H}_{1}\right)(t,\alpha,y_{0})\right|=0.

Remark 1.7
.

From (
1.11
), we can see that the horizontal depletion in Theorem
1.6
is not true for the toy model (
1.10
).

For a more precise description of the long time behavior of the horizontal components, we conjecture that there exist some final states
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,\infty}^{\pm}
and some
\kappa>0
such that

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\widehat{U}_{1}+\widehat{H}_{1}\right)(t,\alpha,y)\sim\left(\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{+}_{1,\infty}\right)(\alpha,y)e^{-i\alpha(u-b)t}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-\kappa}),\\
\left(\widehat{U}_{1}-\widehat{H}_{1}\right)(t,\alpha,y)\sim\left(\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{-}_{1,\infty}\right)(\alpha,y)e^{-i\alpha(u+b)t}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-\kappa}).\end{split}

Theorem
1.6
reveals that
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,\infty}^{\pm}
vanish at all the critical points of
(u\pm b).
Our conjecture (
1.14
) shall imply the space-time estimate (
1.7
) and even a precise decay rate, provided that suitable regularity of the final states could be proven.

The parallel phenomenon exists in the realm of hydrodynamics. Vorticity depletion for the linearized 2D Euler equations around shear flows, first predicted by Bouchet and Morita in
[
3
]
, was later mathematically proven in
[
20
]
by Wei, Zhang and Zhao. A similar vorticity depletion result for the 2D Euler equation linearized around a radially symmetric and strictly decreasing vorticity distribution is due to Bedrossian, Coti-Zelati and Vicol
[
1
]
.
As far as we know, this is the first paper studying the depletion of the horizontal velocity and magnetic field for the linearized MHD equations.

Comparing the toy model (
1.10
) with the complete system (
1.9
), we observe at least three significant effects of the nonlocal terms
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
:

(1)

Altering the final state, as
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{\pm}_{1,in}
on the right hand side of (
1.11
) are changed into some other final state
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{\pm}_{1,\infty}
on the right hand side of (
1.14
);

(2)

Causing
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{\pm}_{1,\infty}
to vanish at the critical points of
(u\pm b)
;

(3)

Enhancing the damping.

This demonstrates that the nonlocal terms
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
in (
1.9
) cannot simply be neglected or be regarded as mere perturbations on the toy model.

Remark 1.8
.

The significance of the nonlocal terms can also be seen from the resolvent estimate. Indeed, if we consider the toy model (
1.10
) instead, the Sturmian equation (
2.2
) in Section 3 would become
\left((u-c)^{2}-b^{2}\right)\partial_{y}\Phi=F,
which is much simpler and yields an obvious estimate

|\partial_{y}\Phi|\lesssim{|(u-c)^{2}-b^{2}|}^{-1}.

Yet, as we shall see in Section 3, at any critical point
y_{0}
of
(u+b)
or
(u-b),
the solution
\Phi
to the actual Sturmian equation (
2.2
) enjoys a non-trivial estimate

|\partial_{y}\Phi(y_{0})|\lesssim|(u(y_{0})-c)^{2}-b(y_{0})^{2}|^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

This
\frac{1}{4}
-improvement, resulting from the effects of
L_{1}
and
L_{2},
is the key to the depletion result. For more details, we refer to Section 3 and 4.

1.3.
Outline of the paper

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the representation formula, which is a contour integral of the resolvent. The study of the resolvent is then reduced to that of the Sturmian equation. In Section 3, we study the Sturmian equation and establish a uniform estimate as well as the limiting absorption principle. This is the most technical part of the paper as our assumptions include rather general sheared velocity and magnetic field, which in turn results in a range of situations that need to be discussed separately from each other. A novelty here is the use of ODE techniques in dealing with the situation involving the critical points. In Section 4, we prove the main theorems by the resolvent estimate.

2.
The Dunford integral and the Sturmian equation

The basic idea for the study of the long-time behavior of the solution to (
1.5
) is to acquire a precise formula of
(\psi,\phi),
which requires understanding the spectral properties of the linearized operator
M_{\alpha}
. Indeed, it is easy to check that the spectrum
\sigma(M_{\alpha})=\mathrm{Ran}\,(u+b)\cup\mathrm{Ran}\,(u-b)
. Then we have the Dunford integral

(2.1)

\begin{split}\Big(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widehat{\psi}\\
\widehat{\phi}\\
\end{array}\Big)(t,\alpha,y)&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}e^{-i\alpha tc}(cI-M_{\alpha})^{-1}\Big(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widehat{\psi}\\
\widehat{\phi}\\
\end{array}\Big)(0,\alpha,y)dc\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}}e^{-i\alpha tc}(cI-M_{\alpha})^{-1}\Big(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widehat{\psi}\\
\widehat{\phi}\\
\end{array}\Big)(0,\alpha,y)dc,\end{split}

where
\Omega
contains the spectrum
\sigma(M_{\alpha})
and
\Omega_{\epsilon}
is the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
\sigma(M_{\alpha})
. Here the last equality holds due to the fact that for
c\notin\sigma(M_{\alpha})
, the resolvent
(cI-M_{\alpha})^{-1}\Big(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widehat{\psi}\\
\widehat{\phi}\\
\end{array}\Big)(0,\alpha,y)
is analytic in
c
.
With the representation formula (
2.1
), we have reduced our problem to the study of the resolvent
(cI-M_{\alpha})^{-1}
and the limit of the contour integral.

Assume that

\big(cI-M_{\alpha}\big)^{-1}\Big(\begin{array}[]{l}\widehat{\psi}_{0}\\
\widehat{\phi}_{0}\end{array}\Big)(\alpha,y)=\Big(\begin{array}[]{l}\Psi_{1}\\
\Phi_{1}\end{array}\Big)(\alpha,y,c).

Then a direct calculation shows that
(\Psi_{1},\Phi_{1})
solves the following system, for
c\notin\sigma(M_{\alpha})
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(u-c)\Delta_{\alpha}\Psi_{1}-u^{\prime\prime}\Psi_{1}-b\Delta_{\alpha}\Phi_{1}+b^{\prime\prime}\Phi_{1}=\Delta_{\alpha}\widehat{\psi}_{0},\\
(u-c)\Phi_{1}-b\Psi_{1}=-\widehat{\phi}_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Here
\Delta_{\alpha}=\partial_{y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}
. Let
\Phi_{1}(\alpha,y,c)=b(y)\Phi(\alpha,y,c)
, and then

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}(\alpha,y,c)=(u(y)-c)\Phi(\alpha,y,c)+\widehat{\phi}_{0}(\alpha,y)/b(y).

Thus, we obtain

(2.2)

\begin{split}&\partial_{y}\left(\left(\left(u(y)-c\right)^{2}-b(y)^{2}\right)\partial_{y}\Phi(\alpha,y,c)\right)-\alpha^{2}\left(\left(u(y)-c\right)^{2}-b(y)^{2}\right)\Phi(\alpha,y,c)\\
&=\Delta_{\alpha}\widehat{\psi}_{0}(\alpha,y)-\big(u(y)-c\big)\Delta_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\widehat{\phi}_{0}(\alpha,y)}{b(y)}\right)+u^{\prime\prime}(y)\frac{\widehat{\phi}_{0}(\alpha,y)}{b(y)}:=F(\alpha,y,c).\end{split}

This is the so-called Sturmian type equation.

3.
Uniform estimates and limiting absorption principle

Let
\Omega_{\epsilon_{0}}
denote the
\epsilon_{0}
-neighborhood of
\text{Ran}\,(u+b)\,\cup\,\text{Ran}\,(u-b)
in
\mathbb{C}.
We shall study Equation (
2.2
) with
c\in\Omega_{\epsilon_{0}}.
We introduce the Elsässer variables
Z_{\pm}:=u\pm b.
Hence, we can rewrite the equation of
\Phi(\alpha,y,c)
as

(3.1)

\partial_{y}((Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi)-\alpha^{2}(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\Phi=F.

This section is devoted to the proofs of the following propositions, which are crucial to our main results.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for
c\in\left(\Omega_{\epsilon_{0}}\setminus\left(\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{-}\right)\right),
the solution to (
3.1
) satisfies the following bound, uniform with respect to
c

\|\Phi(\alpha,\cdot,c)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi(\alpha,\cdot,c)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|F(\alpha,\cdot,c)\|_{H^{1}}.

The estimate on
\Phi
can be continued (in
c
) up to the boundary
\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{-}.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
c\in\left(\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{-}\right),
there exist
\Phi^{\pm}(\alpha,\cdot,c)\in L^{2}
such that as
\epsilon\to 0^{+},

\Phi(\alpha,\cdot,c\pm i\epsilon)\to\Phi^{\pm}(\alpha,\cdot,c)
in
L^{r}
with
r\in(1,2)
and

\left\|\Phi^{\pm}(\alpha,\cdot,c)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|F(\alpha,\cdot,c)\|_{H^{1}}.

We shall prove Proposition
3.1
by contradiction. Suppose that the proposition is false, then there exists a sequence
\{c_{n},\Phi_{n},F_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying the equation

(3.2)

\partial_{y}((Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n})-\alpha^{2}(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\Phi_{n}=F_{n},

such that

•

c_{n}\in\left(\Omega_{\epsilon_{0}}\setminus\big(\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{-}\big)\right),

•

\|\Phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}}=1
,

•

F_{n}\to 0
in
H^{1}
as
n\to\infty.

For convenience, we shall use the notation
q_{n}:=(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n}
from time to time in this section.

As
|c_{n}|\leq C
and
\|\Phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|q_{n}\|_{H^{1}}=1,
we know that

•

c_{n}\to c

•

\displaystyle-\langle\Phi_{n},Z^{\prime}_{-}(Z_{+}-c_{n})f+(Z_{-}-c_{n})Z^{\prime}_{+}f+(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})f^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}},\ \forall\,f\in C^{\infty}_{0}.

the weak limit

•

The limit

or
\left(\text{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\text{Ran}\,Z_{-}\right).

Let us first consider the case when

In this case, it’s straightforward to show that

in

and

in

where
\Phi
is the classical solution to (

\overline{\Phi},
integrating by parts and separating the real and imaginary parts, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\left(u(y)-\mathrm{Re}\,c\right)^{2}-\left(b(y)\right)^{2}-(\mathrm{Im}\,c)^{2}\right)\left(|\partial_{y}\Phi(y,c)|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\Phi(y,c)|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}y=0,

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}(u(y)-\mathrm{Re}\,c)\left(|\partial_{y}\Phi(y,c)|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\Phi(y,c)|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}y=0.

Multiplying (

and adding it to (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(u(y)^{2}-b(y)^{2}-(\mathrm{Re}\,c)^{2}-(\mathrm{Im}\,c)^{2}\right)\left(|\partial_{y}\Phi(y,c)|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\Phi(y,c)|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}y=0.

The condition
|u|<b
then guarantees that

The difficult situation is when
c_{n}\to c\in\left(\text{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\text{Ran}\,Z_{-}\right)
as

which renders Equation (
3.2
) degenerate at the the sets
(Z_{+})^{-1}(c):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{T}:Z_{+}(y)=c\right\}
and
(Z_{-})^{-1}(c):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{T}:Z_{-}(y)=c\right\}.
The condition

ensures that

Hence, if

then
c\notin\text{Ran}\,Z_{-},
and vice versa. In this section, we will always provide detailed proofs only for the case that

as those for the case
c_{n}\to c\in\text{Ran}\,Z_{-}
are essentially similar.

By our assumptions on
(u\pm b),
the sets

s=+
or
-,
consist of two possible types of points:

(2)

and

In our analysis, the two situations require separate treatments. To better distinguish between the two, we denote a critical point as

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\left(\Omega_{\epsilon_{0}}\setminus\left(\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{-}\right)\right)
be such that

for a certain

Let the triple
\{c_{n},\Phi_{n},F_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfy the equation (
3.2
) along with the following conditions

•

\Phi_{n}\rightharpoonup\Phi
in
L^{2}
and
(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n}\rightharpoonup(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi
in
H^{1}
in
\mathcal{I},

•

F_{n}\to F
in
H^{1}
in
\mathcal{I},

for some interval
\mathcal{I}:=[y_{1},y_{2}]\subset\mathbb{T}
such that there exists

in
\mathcal{I}
with
|Z_{s}^{\prime}(y_{c})|>0;

Z_{s}^{\prime}(y_{c})Z_{s}^{\prime}(y)>0,

\forall\,y\in\mathcal{I},
where
s=+
or
-.

Then
\Phi_{n}\rightarrow\Phi
in

and
(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n}\rightarrow(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi
in

.

In particular, if
F\equiv 0,
then
\Phi\in H^{1}(\mathcal{I})
and

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{I}}(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi f^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y+\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathcal{I}}(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\Phi f\,\mathrm{d}y=0,\ \forall f\in H^{1}_{w,0}(\mathcal{I}),

where
H^{1}_{w,0}=\left\{f\in L^{2}:\left((y-y_{c})\partial_{y}f\right)\in L^{2}\text{ and }f|_{y=y_{1}}=f|_{y=y_{2}}=0\right\}.

Proof.

To facilitate the proof, let us assume that
c\in\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{+}
. Then for
\epsilon<\frac{\min_{y\in\mathbb{T}}b-\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}}{3}
, there exists
N>0
such that for any
n>N
,
|c_{n}-c|<\epsilon.
Thus there exists
C>0
such that
|Z_{-}-c_{n}|>C^{-1}>0
for
n>N
. Without loss of generality, let us also assume that
n>N
and
\mathrm{Im}\,c_{n}>0
. Thus, there exists
y_{c_{n}}\in\mathcal{I}
such that
Z_{+}(y_{c_{n}})=\mathrm{Re}\,c_{n}
and
y_{c_{n}}\to y_{c}
as
c_{n}\to c.

We recall
q_{n}=(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n}
with
c_{n}\in\Omega_{\epsilon_{0}}\setminus\left(\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{+}\cup\mathrm{Ran}\,Z_{-}\right).
Dividing (
3.2
) by
(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})
and differentiating, we see that
q_{n}
solves the following equation

(3.7)

\partial_{y}\left(\frac{\partial_{y}q_{n}}{(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})}\right)-\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{q_{n}}{(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})}\right)=\partial_{y}\left(\frac{F_{n}}{(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})}\right).

By Equation (
3.2
),
q_{n}
also satisfies

(3.8)

\displaystyle q^{\prime}_{n}=F_{n}+\alpha^{2}(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\Phi_{n},

(3.9)

\displaystyle q^{\prime\prime}_{n}-\alpha^{2}q_{n}=F^{\prime}_{n}+\alpha^{2}Z^{\prime}_{-}(Z_{+}-c_{n})\Phi_{n}+\alpha^{2}(Z_{-}-c_{n})Z^{\prime}_{+}\Phi_{n}.

From Equation (
3.9
) and the assumptions
\Phi_{n}\rightharpoonup\Phi
in
L^{2},

q_{n}\rightharpoonup q:=(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi
in
H^{1}
and
F_{n}\to F
in

we can infer that

(3.10)

\|q^{\prime\prime}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|F^{\prime}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\left(\|\Phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|q_{n}\|_{H^{1}}\right),

which, along with the assumption that
q_{n}\rightharpoonup(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi
in

implies strong convergence, i.e.,

(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n}\rightarrow(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi\text{ in }H^{1}.

By our assumption and Sobolev embedding,
\|q_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})}\leq\|q_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{I})}<C,
it is true that

\|(y-y_{c_{n}})\partial_{y}\Phi_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{I})}<C,\ \forall\,p<\infty.

Applying the compactness result from Appendix
B
, we have

\Phi_{n}\to\Phi\text{ in }L^{2}.

Now we consider the particular case
F_{n}\to 0
in
H^{1}
.
As
q_{n}
satisfies, for
f\in C_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{I}),

(3.11)

-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\left(q^{\prime}_{n}f^{\prime}+\alpha^{2}q_{n}f\right)(y)}{(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})}\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(F_{n}f^{\prime})(y)}{(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})}\,\mathrm{d}y.

By the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(y)}{(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})}\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{2b(y)}\frac{\Gamma(y)}{Z_{-}-c_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{2b(y)}\frac{\Gamma(y)}{Z_{+}-c_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

and

-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\text{Log}\left(Z_{+}(y)-c_{n}\right)\partial_{y}\left(\frac{\Gamma(y)}{2b(y)Z_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}(y)}\right)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{2b(y)}\frac{\Gamma(y)}{Z_{+}-c_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

we obtain that the right hand side of Equation (
3.11
) can estimated as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(F_{n}f^{\prime})(y)}{(Z_{-}-c_{n})(Z_{+}-c_{n})}\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{2b(y)}\frac{(F_{n}f^{\prime})(y)}{Z_{-}-c_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\text{Log}\left(Z_{+}(y)-c_{n}\right)\partial_{y}\left(\frac{(F_{n}f^{\prime})(y)}{2b(y)Z_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}(y)}\right)\mathrm{d}y\right|\lesssim\|F_{n}\|_{H^{1}}.

Passing to the limit as
n\to\infty,
we can see that the right hand side of Equation (
3.11
) vanishes as
F_{n}\to 0
in
H^{1},
and we obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle 0=-\text{p.v.}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\left(q^{\prime}f^{\prime}+\alpha^{2}qf\right)(y)}{(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)}\,\mathrm{d}y+i\pi\frac{\left(q^{\prime}f^{\prime}+\alpha^{2}qf\right)(y_{c})}{2b(y_{c})Z_{+}^{\prime}(y_{c})},\ \forall f\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{I}).

Let
\chi
be a smooth cut-off function such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1,

\chi\equiv 1
near
y_{\lambda}
and
\text{supp}\,\chi\Subset\mathcal{I}.
Let us specify a test function

The imaginary part of Equation (

) then reads

which in turn reveals that

By the facts that
\partial_{y}{\Phi}(y,c)=\frac{q(y,c)}{(Z_{-}-c)(Z_{+}-c)}\sim\frac{q}{y-y_{c}},

q(y_{c})=0
and Hardy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I})}\leq C\|q^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I})}.

Thus, we conclude that
\Phi(\cdot,c)\in H^{1}(\mathcal{I})
.

Multiplying both sides of Equation (
3.1
) by
f\in H^{1}_{w,0}(\mathcal{I}),
passing to the limit as

and integrating by parts, we obtain the identity in (

Lemma 3.4
.

i.e.,

we have, for

Appendix B
A compactness lemma

We prove a useful compactness result.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{I}:=[a,b]
be such that
y_{n}\to\frac{a+b}{2}.
Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a family of functions defined on
\mathcal{I}
satisfying the uniform bound

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{r}}+\|(y-y_{n})\partial_{y}f_{n}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C,\ 1<r<p<\infty,

then there exist
f_{\infty}\in L^{p}
and a subsequence
\{f_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that as
j\to\infty,

\displaystyle f_{n_{j}}\rightarrow f_{\infty}\text{ in }L^{r},\ 1<r<p.

Proof.

By virtue of Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem, the proof of the above compactness result amounts to showing that the family
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equicontinuous in
L^{r}.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{h}:=[y_{n}-h,y_{n}+h]
and
\mathcal{I}_{2h}:=[y_{n}-2h,y_{n}+2h]
(Note that
\mathcal{I}_{2h}\subset\mathcal{I}
for sufficiently small
h
.) We have

\begin{split}\|f_{n}(x+h)-f_{n}(x)\|_{L^{r}}^{r}=&\int_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{h}}\left|\int_{x}^{x+h}f^{\prime}_{n}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\right|^{r}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}\left|f_{n}(x+h)-f_{n}(x)\right|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
:=&I_{*}+I_{**}.\end{split}

Noticing that
|x-y_{n}|\geq h
for
x\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{h},
we use Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequality to estimate
I_{*}
as follows –

\begin{split}I_{*}=&\int_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{h}}h^{r}\left|\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}(x^{\prime}-y_{n})f^{\prime}_{n}(x^{\prime})\cdot\left(\frac{1}{x^{\prime}-y_{n}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\right|^{r}\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq&C_{p}\int_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{h}}\left(h\|(x-y_{n})f_{n}\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{r}h^{-\frac{r}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Ch^{r\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}.\end{split}

As for
I_{**},
we let
\chi
be a smooth cut-off function such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
\mathcal{I}_{2h}.
Integration by parts yields the following identity –

\int_{\mathcal{I}}|f_{n}|^{p}\chi\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\mathcal{I}}(y-y_{n})|f_{n}|^{p}\chi^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y-\frac{p}{2}\int_{\mathcal{I}}\chi(y-y_{n})\left(f^{\prime}_{n}|f_{n}|^{p}f_{n}^{-1}+\partial_{y}\overline{f_{n}}|f_{n}|^{p}\overline{f_{n}}^{-1}\right)\mathrm{d}y,

which, along with Sobolev inequality
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{h})}\leq C\|f_{n}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{h})},\ q>1,
leads to

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{I}_{h})}^{p}\leq C_{p}\|(y-y_{n})\partial_{y}f_{n}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+C_{h}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{h})}^{p}\leq C.

Hence, we can simply estimate
I_{**}
by Hölder’s inequality –

I_{**}\leq\int_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}\left(|f_{n}(x+h)|^{r}+|f_{n}(x)|^{r}\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\|f_{n}\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{I}_{2h})}h^{\frac{p-r}{p}}\leq Ch^{1-\frac{r}{p}}.

Therefore, the desired compactness result is true.
∎

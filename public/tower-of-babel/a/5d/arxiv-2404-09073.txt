Abstract
. In a recent paper, we introduced and studied the class of admissible noncommutative domains 𝒟 g − 1 ​ ( ℋ ) {\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}}) in B ​ ( ℋ ) n B({\mathcal{H}})^{n} associated with admissible free holomorphic functions g g in noncommutative indeterminates Z 1 , … , Z n Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n} . Each such a domain admits a universal model 𝐖 := ( W 1 , … , W n ) {\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}) of weighted left creation operators acting on the full Fock space with n n generators. We also obtain results concerning the commutant lifting and Toeplitz-corrona in our setting as as well as some results on the boundary property for universal models.

1.
Universal operator models associated with admissible free holomorphic functions

In this section, we introduce the class of free holomorphic functions admissible for multi-variable
operator model theory. Each such a function uniquely defines an
n
-tuple
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})

of weighted left creation operators acting on the full Fock space with
n
generators, which turns out to be the universal model for a certain maximal noncommutative domain in
B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
.

Let
H_{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complex Hilbert space with orthonormal
basis

e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
, where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The full Fock space of
H_{n}
is defined by

F^{2}(H_{n}):=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H_{n}^{\otimes k},

where
H_{n}^{\otimes 0}:={\mathbb{C}}1
and
H_{n}^{\otimes k}
is the (Hilbert)
tensor product of
k
copies of
H_{n}
.
Define the
left creation
operators

S_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n}),\ i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, by

S_{i}\varphi:=e_{i}\otimes\varphi,\quad\varphi\in F^{2}(H_{n}),

and the
right creation operators

R_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n})
by

R_{i}\varphi:=\varphi\otimes e_{i}
,
\varphi\in F^{2}(H_{n})
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
be the unital free semigroup on
n
generators

g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
and the identity
g_{0}
. The length of
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
is defined by
|\alpha|:=0
if
\alpha=g_{0}
and

|\alpha|:=k
if

\alpha=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}
, where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We set
e_{\alpha}:=e_{g_{i_{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{g_{i_{k}}}
and
e_{g_{0}}=1
, and note that
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
is an othonormal basis for
F^{2}(H_{n})
.
Given a formal power series
f:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
,
a_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, in indeterminates
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
, we say that
f
is
a
free holomorphic function
in a neighborhood of the origin if there is
\delta>0
such that the series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
is convergent in norm for any

(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\rho}
and any Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, where

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\rho}:=\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}:\ \|X_{1}X_{1}^{*}+\cdots+X_{n}X_{n}^{*}\|^{1/2}\leq\rho\}.

We refer the reader to
[
40
]
for basic properties of free holomorphic functions.
Let
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
be a collection of strictly positive real numbers such that

b_{g_{0}}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be the associated formal power series.
We define the
weighted left creation operators

W_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n})
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, associated with
g
by setting
W_{i}:=S_{i}D_{i}
, where

S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
are the left creation operators on the full
Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
and the diagonal operators
D_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n})

are defined by setting

D_{i}e_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}}e_{\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},

where
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
is the othonormal basis for
F^{2}(H_{n})
. We associate with
g
the
n
-tuple
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
.

Definition 1.1
.

A free holomorphic function
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
is said to be admissible for operator model theory if the following conditions are satisfied.

(a)

The collection
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
consists of strictly positive real numbers such that

b_{g_{0}}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(b)

The formal power series
g:=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
is a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin, which is equivalent to

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}b_{\alpha}^{2}\right)^{1/2k}<\infty.

(c)

The condition

\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left\|\sum_{p=0}^{k}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|<\infty

holds, where
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
is the inverse of
g
and
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is the
n
-tuple of the weighted left creation operators associated with
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
.

The
n
-tuple
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
of weighted left creation operators is called universal model associated with
g
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin, with
b_{\alpha}>0
, if
|\alpha|\geq 1
, and with inverse
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}a_{\gamma}Z_{\gamma}
.
The noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is defined as the set of all
n
-tuples of operators

X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
satisfying the following conditions.

(i)

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})\geq 0
, where

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*}):=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}

is convergent in the weak operator topology.

(ii)

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})X_{\alpha}^{*}\leq I
, where the convergence is in the weak operator topology.

(iii)

X
is said to be a Cuntz tuple in

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
if
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})=0
.

(iv)

X
is said to be a pure element in

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
,
if

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})X_{\alpha}^{*}=I.

(v)

The pure part of
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is defined by setting

{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}}):=\{X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}}):X\text{ is pure}\}.

We call
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})

admissible noncommutative domain
if
g
is an admissible free holomorphic function for operator model theory.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
is a radial
n
-tuple with respect to the set
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
if
there is
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
rX:=(rX_{1},\ldots,rX_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
for every
r\in(\delta,1)
. If, in addition,
rX
is pure for every
r\in(\delta,1)
, we say that
X
is a radially pure
n
-tuple with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
. The noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is called radially pure domain if any
X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is a radially pure
n
-tuple.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
.
If there is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{E}}
and an isometric operator
V:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}

{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is a universal model for

.

We define the

noncommutative Berezin kernel
associated with an arbitrary
n
-tuple

in

K_{g,T}:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})({\mathcal{H}})}
given by

K_{g,T}h:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h,\quad h\in{\mathcal{H}}.

We proved in
[
47
]
the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
g
be an admissible free holomorphic function and let

. Then there is a Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{D}}
such that

where

.
In this case, we have

and the noncommutative Berezin kernel

If

is the universal model associated with

admits a Beurling type characterization of the joint invariant subspaces of

g_{s}

(see Example
1.7
).

First, we recall from
[
47
]
the following result on the universal model
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
associated with an admissible free holomorphic function.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be two admissible free holomorphic functions and let

{\bf W}^{(f)}:=(W_{1}^{(f)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(f)})\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bf W}^{(g)}:=(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})

be the universal models associated with
f
and
g
, respectively. If
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain such that

{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{f^{-1}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}

and
Y\in B(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}})
is a self-adjoint operator, then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{D}}
and an operator
\Psi:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
such that

Y=\Psi\Psi^{*}
and

\Psi(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\Psi,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(ii)

Y-\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\alpha}(W_{\alpha}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})Y(W_{\alpha}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\geq 0
, where
g^{-1}=1-\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Suppose that item (i) holds. Since
(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain, we have
c_{\alpha}\geq 0
and

\begin{split}\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\alpha}(W_{\alpha}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})Y(W_{\alpha}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})&=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\alpha}(W_{\alpha}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\Psi\Psi^{*}(W_{\alpha}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\\
&=\Psi\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\alpha}(W_{\alpha}^{(g)}W_{\alpha}^{(g)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\right)\Psi^{*}\leq\Psi\Psi^{*}=Y,\end{split}

which proves the implication (i)
\implies
(ii). Conversely, assume that inequality (ii) holds.
Define the completely positive linear map
\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}:B(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}})\to B(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}})
by setting

\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}(T):=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\alpha}(W_{\alpha}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})T(W_{\alpha}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})
. According to Proposition
2.1
,

{\bf W}^{(f)}:=(W_{1}^{(f)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(f)})
is a pure
n
-tuple in
{\mathcal{D}}_{f^{-1}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
. Consequently, since
{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{f^{-1}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}
, we have
SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}^{k}(I)=0
. Taking into account condition (ii), we have
\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}(Y)\leq Y
, which implies

-\|Y\|\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}^{k}(I)\leq\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}^{k}(Y)\leq\|Y\|\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}^{k}(I).

Hence, we deduce that SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}^{k}(Y)=0
which together with inequality
\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}^{k}(Y)\leq Y
implies
Y\geq 0
.
For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, define the operator
E_{i}:Y^{1/2}(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}})\to Y^{1/2}(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}})
by setting

(2.1)

Since
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain, we have
c_{g_{i}}>0
and, consequently,

\begin{split}c_{g_{i}}\|E_{i}Y^{1/2}y\|^{2}&=\|\sqrt{c_{g_{i}}}Y^{1/2}(W_{i}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})y\|^{2}=c_{g_{i}}\left<(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})Y(W_{i}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})y,y\right>\\
&\leq\left<\phi_{{\bf W}^{(f)}}(Y)y,y\right>\leq\left<Yy,y\right>=\|Y^{1/2}y\|^{2}\end{split}

for any
y\in F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
. One can see now that the operator
E_{i}
is well-defined and has a unique extension,
also denoted by
E_{i}
, to a bounded operator on
{\mathcal{E}}:=\overline{Y^{1/2}(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}})}
.

F_{i}:=E_{i}^{*}\in B({\mathcal{E}})
and note that

\begin{split}\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}\|F_{\alpha}^{*}Y^{1/2}y\|^{2}&=\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq m}\left\|\sqrt{c_{\alpha}}Y^{1/2}{W}_{\alpha}^{(f)*}y\right\|^{2}\\

for any

and
m\geq 1

\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{*}\leq I

F=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{E}})

\|\phi_{F}^{k}(I)\|\leq 1

SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{F}^{k}(I)=0
, which shows that
F\in{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{E}})
.
According to Theorem
1.5
, we have

(2.2)

K_{g,F}F_{i}^{*}=(W^{(g)*}_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})K_{g,F},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

{\mathcal{D}}:=\overline{\text{\rm range}\,\Delta_{g^{-1}}(F,F^{*})}

2.1

2.2

\begin{split}\Psi(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})&=Y^{1/2}K_{g,F}^{*}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})=Y^{1/2}F_{i}K_{g,F}^{*}\\
&=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})Y^{1/2}K_{g,F}^{*}=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\Psi\end{split}

for any

. Since

is an isometry, we deduce that

\Psi\Psi^{*}=Y^{1/2}K_{g,F}^{*}K_{g,F}Y^{1/2}=Y

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f
and
g
be two admissible free holomorphic functions and let

{\bf W}^{(f)}:=(W_{1}^{(f)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(f)})\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bf W}^{(g)}:=(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})

P_{\mathcal{M}}(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})P_{\mathcal{M}}=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})P_{\mathcal{M}}

2.1

n

Corollary 2.5
.

f

\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}\leq 1

Proposition 2.6
.

be an admissible free holomorphic function with inverse

g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}.

g_{2}:=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n}}{d}\right)^{k},\quad\text{ where }\ d=\sup_{{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\atop{i=1,\ldots,n}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}.

{\mathcal{D}}_{g_{1}^{-1}}

The fact that
{\mathcal{D}}_{g_{1}^{-1}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{g_{2}^{-1}}

If
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g_{1}^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})

[

{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g_{1}^{-1}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}

{\bf W}^{(g)}=(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})
be the universal model of the domain
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}

\|W_{i}^{(g)}\|\leq\sqrt{d}

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{d}W_{i}^{(g)}W_{i}^{(g)*}\leq I
. This shows that
{\bf W}^{(g)}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g_{2}^{-1}}
, where

g_{2}^{-1}:=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{d}Z_{i}
. According to Theorem
2.2
,
to show that
{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g_{2}^{-1}}
, it is enough to prove that
{\bf W}^{(g)}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g_{2}^{-1}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
. To this end, let us prove that SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{{\bf W}^{(g)}}^{k}(I)=0
, where

\Gamma_{{\bf W}^{(g)}}(Y):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{d}W_{i}^{(g)}YW_{i}^{(g)*}
. Indeed, since

W_{i}^{(g)}=S_{i}D_{i}^{(g)}
, where
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
are the left creation operators, we deduce that
\|D_{i}^{(g)}\|\leq\sqrt{d}
. Consequently,
\Gamma_{{\bf W}^{(g)}}(I)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}S_{i}^{*}\leq I

\|\Gamma^{k}_{{\bf W}^{(g)}}(I)\|\leq 1
for any

. On the other hand, it is easy to see that, for every

\Gamma^{k}_{{\bf W}^{(g)}}(I))\to 0
strongly as

{\bf W}^{(g)}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g_{2}^{-1}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be an arbitrary admissible free holomorphic function and set

there is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{D}}

\Psi:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
such that

{\mathcal{M}}=\Psi(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}})

and

\sqrt{d}\Psi(S_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\Psi,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
are the left creation operators on the full Fock space

.

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.6
and Theorem
2.4
, the proof is similar to the proof of Corollary
2.5
.
In this case,
g^{-1}=1-\frac{1}{d}(Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n})
and the universal model associated with the regular domain

is
{\bf W}^{(g)}=(\sqrt{d}S_{1},\ldots,\sqrt{d}S_{n})
.
∎

In what follows, we address the uniqueness of the Beurling-Lax-Halmos type representation of the joint invariant subspaces. We need some preliminary results.
We recall that a subspace
{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}
is
called co-invariant under
{\mathcal{S}}\subset B({\mathcal{K}})
if
X^{*}{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
for any
X\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be an admissible free holomorphic function with inverse
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}a_{\gamma}Z_{\gamma}
and let
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the corresponding weighted left creation operators associated with
g
.
If

{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
is a co-invariant subspace under each operator

W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}
, then

\overline{\text{\rm span}}\,\left\{\left(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right){\mathcal{M}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}){\mathcal{M}}}.

Proof.

Define the subspace

{\mathcal{E}}:=\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}){\mathcal{M}}}\subset{\mathcal{K}}
and let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
\varphi\neq 0
, with Fourier representation

\varphi=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}e_{\alpha}\otimes h_{\alpha}
,

h_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{K}}.

Let
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
be such that
h_{\beta}\neq 0
and note that

(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(W_{\beta}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\varphi=1\otimes\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{\beta}}}h_{\beta}.

Since
{\mathcal{M}}
is a
co-invariant subspace under each operator
W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}
,
we deduce that
h_{\beta}\in{\mathcal{E}}
and

(W_{\beta}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(1\otimes h_{\beta})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{\beta}}}e_{\beta}\otimes h_{\beta}\in F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}},\quad\text{ for }\ \beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

Consequentyly,
\varphi=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}e_{\beta}\otimes h_{\beta}\in F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}},

which shows that
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}
and

{\mathcal{Y}}:=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\,\left\{\left(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right){\mathcal{M}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}.

To prove the reverse inclusion, it is enough to show that
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{Y}}
. To this end, let

h\in(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}){\mathcal{M}}
with
h\neq 0
and note that there exists
g\in{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}
such that
g=1\otimes h+\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\geq 1}e_{\alpha}\otimes h_{\alpha}.
According to Proposition
2.1
, we have

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*}):=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}

converges in the strong operator topology and
\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}
.
Consequently,

\begin{split}1\otimes h&=(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})g=(\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})g\end{split}

and, using the fact that
{\mathcal{M}}
is co-invariant under each operator
W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}
,
we deduce that
h\in{\mathcal{Y}}
for any
h\in(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}){\mathcal{M}}
.
Hence, we deduce that
(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(1\otimes{\mathcal{E}})\subset{\mathcal{Y}}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, which
implies

\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}}e_{\alpha}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{Y}}
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

Consequently,
F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{Y}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Now, we can easily deduce the following result.

Corollary 2.9
.

A subspace
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
is reducing under each operator

W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}

if and only if there exists a subspace
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}
such
that

{\mathcal{M}}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}.

Let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the corresponding weighted left creation operators associated with an admissible free holomorphic function
f
.
Fix an
n
-tuple
Y:=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})\in B({\mathcal{K}})^{n}
.
A bounded linear operator
A:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{K}}
is called multi-analytic with respect to

{\bf W}
and

if

A(W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=Y_{i}A,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

The support of

{\mathcal{N}}

such that

.
According to Proposition
2.8
, we have

\text{\rm supp}\,A=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\,\left\{\left(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right){\mathcal{M}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}},

where
{\mathcal{G}}:=\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\left(A^{*}({\mathcal{K}})\right)}
. Note that
A|_{\text{\rm supp}\,A}:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{K}}
is a multi-analytic operator with respect to
{\bf W}
and
Y
, and

AA^{*}=(A|_{\text{\rm supp}\,A})(A|_{\text{\rm supp}\,A})^{*}
.

Let
A_{1}:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{K}}
and
A_{2}:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\to{\mathcal{K}}
be multi-analytic operators with respect to

and

and

.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the corresponding weighted left creation operators associated with an admissible free holomorphic function
f
.
Let
A_{1}:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{K}}
and
A_{2}:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\to{\mathcal{K}}
be multi-analytic operators with respect to
{\bf W}
and
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})\in B({\mathcal{K}})^{n}
, such that

A_{1}A_{1}^{*}=A_{2}A_{2}^{*}.

Then there is a partial isometry
V:{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{2}
such that

A_{1}=A_{2}(I\otimes V),

where
I\otimes V:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{2}
is a partially isometric multi-analytic operator with respect to
{\bf W}
, having the initial space
\text{\rm supp}\,A_{1}
and the final space
\text{\rm supp}\,A_{2}
. In particular,
A_{1}|_{\text{\rm supp}\,A_{1}}
coincides with
A_{2}|_{\text{\rm supp}\,A_{2}}
.

Proof.

Suppose
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
and recall (see Poposition
2.1
) that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*}):=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1},

where the convergence is in the strong operator topology.
Since
A_{1}A_{1}^{*}=A_{2}A_{2}^{*}
, we obtain

\begin{split}A_{1}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{1}})A_{1}^{*}&=A_{1}\left(\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\right)A_{1}^{*}\\
&=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}A_{1}A_{1}^{*}Y_{\alpha}^{*}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}A_{2}A_{2}^{*}Y_{\alpha}^{*}\\
&=A_{2}\left(\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}}\right)A_{2}^{*}=A_{2}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})A_{2}^{*}.\end{split}

Hence, we deduce that

\left\|(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{1}})A_{1}^{*}x\right\|=\left\|(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})A_{2}^{*}x\right\|,\qquad x\in{\mathcal{K}}.

Set
{\mathcal{G}}_{s}:=\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{s}})\left(A_{s}^{*}({\mathcal{K}})\right)}
for
s=1,2
and define the unitary operator
U:{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
by

U((P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{1}})A_{1}^{*}x):=(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})A_{2}^{*}x,\qquad x\in{\mathcal{K}}.

This implies that there is a unique partial isometry
V:{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{2}
with initial space
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and final space
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
, extending
U
. Moreover, we have
A_{1}V^{*}=A_{2}|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}}
, where we use the natural identification
{\mathbb{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{j}={\mathcal{K}}_{j}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{j}
,
j=1,2
. Since
A_{1},A_{2}
are multi-analytic operators with respect to
{\bf W}
and
Y
, we have

\begin{split}A_{1}\left(I\otimes V^{*}\right)(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})(1\otimes h)&=Y_{\alpha}A_{1}(I\otimes V^{*})(1\otimes h)\\
&=Y_{\alpha}A_{2}(1\otimes h)=A_{2}(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})(1\otimes h)\end{split}

for any
h\in{\mathcal{K}}_{2}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. Hence, taking into account that

\Span\left\{(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})({\mathbb{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{2}):\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}_{2},

we deduce that
A_{1}\left(I\otimes V^{*}\right)=A_{2}
, which implies
A_{1}=A_{2}(I\otimes V)
and completes the proof.
∎

Theorem
2.4
shows that
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
is a joint invariant subspace under

, where

Note that setting

, where

{\mathcal{M}}=\Psi_{0}\left(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Psi_{0}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})\Psi_{0}

and

Theorem
2.10

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
f
and

be two admissible free holomorphic functions and let

\Psi_{1}|_{\text{\rm supp}\,\Psi_{1}}

.

3.
Minimal dilations for pure
n
-tuples of operators and dilation index

In this section, we study the minimal dilations of the pure
n
-tuples of operators in the noncommutative domain
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
. We show that, under certain conditions, the minimal dilation is unique. In addition, we introduce the dilation index and discuss its significance in dilation theory.

Let
g
be an admissible free holomorphic function and let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the universal model associated with
g
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
and a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{G}}
, we say that the
n
-tuple
(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})
is a dilation (coextension) of
T
if

T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})|_{{\mathcal{H}}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
{\mathcal{H}}
is identified with a coinvariant subspace under each operators
W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}
. The dilation is called minimal if

\overline{\text{\rm span}}\,\left\{\left(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}\right){\mathcal{H}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}}.

The dilation index of
T
, denoted by
d_{T}
, is the minimum dimension of
{\mathcal{G}}
such that

(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})
is a dilation of
T
.

The first result gives some equivalent statements for the minimality condition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}}
be a co-invariant subspace under
each operator
W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

\overline{\text{\rm span}}\,\left\{\left(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}\right){\mathcal{H}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}}
;

(ii)

there is no proper subspace
{\mathcal{N}}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\mathcal{H}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{N}}
;

(iii)

\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}){\mathcal{H}}}={\mathcal{G}}
;

(iv)

{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\bigcap{\mathcal{G}}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Since the implication (i)
\implies
(ii) is obvious, we prove that (ii)
\implies
(i).
Assume that (ii) holds. According to Proposition
2.8
, we have

\overline{\text{\rm span}}\,\left\{\left(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}\right){\mathcal{H}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{P_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{H}}},

under the identification of
{\mathbb{C}}1\otimes{\mathcal{G}}
with
{\mathcal{G}}
,
which shows that
{\mathcal{H}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{P_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{H}}}
. Consequently, we must have
{\mathcal{G}}=\overline{P_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{H}}}
. Therefore, items (i) and (iii) hold. Note also that the implication (iii)
\implies
(i) is due to Proposition
2.8
.

Now, we assume that item (iii) holds and suppose that there is
x\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\bigcap{\mathcal{G}}
such that
x\neq 0
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
is invariant under each operator
W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we deduce that

F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}x\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
. This shows that
{\mathcal{H}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes({\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathbb{C}}x)
and, consequently,
P_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{H}}\subset{\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathbb{C}}x
, which is a
contradiction.

Assume that (iv) holds and suppose that
\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}){\mathcal{H}}}\neq{\mathcal{G}}
. Then there is
y\in{\mathcal{G}}
,
y\neq 0
such that
y\perp(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}){\mathcal{H}}
. Hence, we have

\left<y,h\right>=\left<P_{\mathcal{G}}y,h\right>=0
for any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
, which implies
y\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
. Therefore,

y\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\bigcap{\mathcal{G}}=\{0\}
, which is a contradiction. This shows that (iii) is equivalent to (iv).
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a pure
n
-tuple and assume that

T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
{\mathcal{G}}
is a Hilbert space and
{\mathcal{H}}
is a co-invariant subspace under each operator
W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}
. Then

\dim\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}\leq\dim{\mathcal{G}}.

If
(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})
is a minimal dilation of
T
, then

\dim\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}=d_{T},

where
d_{T}
is the dilation index.

Proof.

Since

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta_{g^{-1}}(W,W^{*})\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})|_{\mathcal{H}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})|_{\mathcal{H}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}P_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{H}},

we deduce that
\dim\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}\leq\dim{\mathcal{G}}
.

Now, assume that
(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})
is a minimal dilation of
T
.
According to Proposition
3.2
,
\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}){\mathcal{H}}}={\mathcal{G}}
.
Hence,

\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}=\overline{P_{\mathcal{H}}P_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{H}}}=\overline{P_{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{G}}}
. Assume, by contradiction, that

(3.1)

\dim\overline{P_{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{G}}}<\dim{\mathcal{G}}.

Then there is
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{G}}
,
x_{0}\neq 0
such that
P_{\mathcal{H}}x_{0}=0
. Indeed, if
P_{\mathcal{H}}x\neq 0
for any
x\in{\mathcal{G}}
, then the operator
P_{\mathcal{H}}|_{\mathcal{G}}:{\mathcal{G}}\to\overline{P_{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{G}}}
is one-to-one and, therefore,

\dim\overline{P_{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{G}}}\geq\dim{\mathcal{G}}
, which contradicts relation (
3.1
). Note that
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{G}}\cap{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
and, due to Proposition
3.2
, contradicts the fact that
(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})
is a minimal dilation of
T
. Therefore, relation (
3.1
) is false. Hence,

\dim{\mathcal{G}}\leq\dim\overline{P_{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{G}}}=\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}.

On the other hand, the first part of the proof shows that
\dim\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}\leq\dim{\mathcal{G}}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

According to Proposition
3.3
, if the dilation is minimal then the dilation index is equal to
\dim{\mathcal{G}}
. Is the converse true
?
The next result shows that the answer is positive if the dilation index is finite. In addition, we show how to get a minimal dilation from an arbitrary one.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a pure
n
-tuple with dilation index
d_{T}
and assume that

T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
{\mathcal{G}}
is a Hilbert space and
{\mathcal{H}}
is a co-invariant subspace under
W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}
.
If

, then the following statements hold.

(i)

If

, then there is a subspace

such that

\dim({\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathcal{E}})=d_{T},\quad F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp},\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathcal{E}}})|_{\mathcal{H}},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(ii)

d_{T}=\dim{\mathcal{G}}
if and only if the dilation
(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})
is minimal.

If

, then, taking

, we have

\dim{\mathcal{G}}_{0}=d_{T},\quad F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes({\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathcal{G}}_{0})\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp},\quad T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}})|_{\mathcal{H}},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and
(W_{1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}})
is a minimal dilation of
T
.

Proof.

Assume that
d_{T}<\infty
. If
d_{T}<\dim{\mathcal{G}}
, then Proposition
3.3
shows that
(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})
is not a minimal dilation of
T
and, due to Proposition
3.2
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{G}}\neq\{0\}
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a maximal subspace of
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
is invariant under
W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}
, we have
F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
.
Consequently,

T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathcal{E}}})|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

If

and, consequently, we can find a subspace

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\neq{\mathcal{E}}
, such that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
, which contradicts the maximality of
{\mathcal{E}}
. Therefore, we must have
\dim({\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathcal{E}})=d_{T}
. This proves item (i).

To prove item (ii), assume that

{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{G}}=\{0\}

Indeed, if we assume, by contradiction, that there is a non-zero vector

and

for

, we have

3.2
, we have
\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}){\mathcal{H}}}={\mathcal{G}}
.
Conversely, assume that

3.2
,
(W_{1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}})
is a minimal dilation of
T
. Using Proposition
3.3
, we deduce that

. This proves item (ii).

To prove the last part of this proposition, assume that
d_{T}=\infty
and take
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}:=\overline{(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}){\mathcal{H}}}
. Due to Theorem
2.8
, we have

(3.2)

\overline{\text{\rm span}}\,\left\{\left(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}\right){\mathcal{H}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}}_{0}.

Since

and

is an invariant subspace under the operators

F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes({\mathcal{G}}\ominus{\mathcal{G}}_{0})\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
and

T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}})|_{\mathcal{H}}
for all

.
Note that relation (
3.2

(W_{1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{G}}_{0}})
is a minimal dilation of
T

3.3

.
The proof is complete.

We say that two

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
T^{\prime}=(T^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,T^{\prime}_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
be pure
n
-tuples and let
(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})
and
(W_{1}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}})
be the canonical dilations of
T
and
T^{\prime}
, respectively. Then
T
is unitarily equivalent to
T^{\prime}
if and only if there is a unitary operator
U:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
such that

(I\otimes U){\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}

(I\otimes U)(W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})=(W_{i}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}})(I\otimes U),\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Proof.

Assume that

is a unitary operator such that
VT_{i}=T_{i}^{\prime}V
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Then, we have

V\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})=\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T^{\prime},T^{\prime*})V

which induces a unitary operator

U:\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}\to\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T^{\prime},T^{\prime*}){\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}

Now, we show that the unitary operator
I_{F^{2}(H_{n})}\otimes U:=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T^{\prime},T^{\prime*}){\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}

, and

\begin{split}(I\otimes U)K_{g,T}h&=(I\otimes U)\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes V\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h\\
&=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}VT_{\alpha}^{*}h=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{\prime*}Vh\\
&=K_{g,T^{\prime}}Vh\end{split}

for any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

C^{*}({\bf W})
such that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(\pi({\bf W}),\pi({\bf W}^{*}))=0,

is called
Cuntz type representation
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
g
be an admissible free holomorphic function with

g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
and
let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left creation operators associated with
g
. Assume that
the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}
converges in the operator norm topology.

If
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})}
, then there is a
*
-representation
\pi:C^{*}({\bf W})\to{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}
on a separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}
satisfying the following properties.

(i)

\pi
annihilates the compact operators in
C^{*}({\bf W})
and

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(\pi({\bf W}),\pi({\bf W})^{*})=0,

where
\pi({\bf W}):=(\pi(W_{1}),\ldots,\pi(W_{n}))
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{H}}
can be identified with a
*
-cyclic co-invariant subspace of
{\mathcal{K}}:=(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}})\bigoplus{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}

under the operators

(W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\bigoplus\pi(W_{i})
such that

T_{i}^{*}=[(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\bigoplus\pi(W_{i})^{*}]|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
{\mathcal{D}}:=\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}
. Moreover,
T\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
4.1
, there is a unique unital
completely contractive linear map

\Phi_{T}:{\mathcal{S}}:=\overline{\Span}\{W_{\alpha}W_{\beta}^{*}:\ \alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\}\to B({\mathcal{H}}),

such that
\Phi_{T}(W_{\alpha}W_{\beta}^{*})=T_{\alpha}T_{\beta}^{*}
for any
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.
Applying Arveson extension theorem

[
5
]
to the map
\Phi_{T}
, we find a unital
completely positive linear map
\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}:C^{*}({\bf W})\to B({\mathcal{H}})
such the
\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}|_{\mathcal{S}}=\Phi_{T}
. Let
\tilde{\pi}:C^{*}({\bf W})\to B(\tilde{\mathcal{K}})
be a
minimal Stinespring dilation
[
54
]
of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}
. Then

\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}(X)=P_{{\mathcal{H}}}\tilde{\pi}(X)|{\mathcal{H}},\quad X\in C^{*}({\bf W}),

and
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{\tilde{\pi}(X)h:\ h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}.
Since, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}(W_{i}W_{i}^{*})&=T_{i}T_{i}^{*}=P_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\pi}(W_{i})\tilde{\pi}(W_{i}^{*})|{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=P_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\pi}(W_{i})(P_{\mathcal{H}}+P_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}})\tilde{\pi}(W_{i}^{*})|{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}(W_{i}W_{i}^{*})+(P_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\pi}(W_{i})|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}})(P_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}}\tilde{\pi}(W_{i}^{*})|{\mathcal{H}}),\end{split}

it is easy to see that
P_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\pi}(W_{i})|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}}=0
and

(4.1)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}(W_{\alpha}X)&=P_{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{\pi}(W_{\alpha})\tilde{\pi}(X))|{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=(P_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\pi}(W_{\alpha})|{\mathcal{H}})(P_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\pi}(X)|{\mathcal{H}})=\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}(W_{\alpha})\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}(X)\end{split}

for any
X\in C^{*}({\bf W})
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. Since

P_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\pi}(W_{i})|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}}=0
,

{\mathcal{H}}
is an
invariant subspace under each operator
\tilde{\pi}(W_{i})^{*}
.
Consequently, we have

(4.2)

\tilde{\pi}(W_{i})^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}(W_{i}^{*})=T_{i}^{*},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

By the Wold decomposition of Theorem
4.2
,
the representation
\tilde{\pi}
decomposes into a direct sum

\tilde{\pi}=\pi_{0}\oplus\pi
on
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}
,
where
\pi_{0}
,
\pi
are disjoint representations of

C^{*}({\bf W})
on the Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}
,
respectively, such that

(4.3)

{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\simeq F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}},\quad\pi_{0}(X)=X\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}},\quad X\in C^{*}({\bf W}),

for some Hilbert space
{\mathcal{G}}
, and
\pi
is a representation such that

\pi(\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n})))=0
, where
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
stands for all compact operators in
B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
. Now, taking into account that
\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}

is a
rank-one projection in
C^{*}({\bf W})
, we have that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(\pi({\bf W}),\pi({\bf W})^{*})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\dim{\mathcal{G}}=\dim(\text{\rm range}\,\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})).

Taking into account that the Stinespring representation
\tilde{\pi}
is minimal and
using the proof of Theorem
3.7
, we have

\begin{split}\text{\rm range}\,\tilde{\pi}(P_{\mathbb{C}})&=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\tilde{\pi}(X)h:\ X\in C^{*}({\bf W}),h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}\\
&=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\tilde{\pi}(Y)h:\ Y\in\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n})),h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}\\
&=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\tilde{\pi}(W_{\alpha}P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}W_{\beta}^{*})h:\ \alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}\\
&=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\tilde{\pi}(W_{\beta}^{*})h:\ \beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}.\end{split}

Using relation (
4.1
), we deduce that

\begin{split}\left<\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\tilde{\pi}(W_{\alpha}^{*})h,\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\tilde{\pi}(W_{\beta}^{*})k\right>&=\left<h,\pi(W_{\alpha})\pi(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\pi(W_{\beta}^{*})k\right>\\
&=\left<h,T_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\beta}^{*}k\right>\\
&=\left<\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h,\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\beta}^{*}k\right>\end{split}

for any
h,k\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Consequently, there is a unitary operator

\Lambda:\text{\rm range}\,\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\to\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}
defined by

\Lambda(\tilde{\pi}(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})\tilde{\pi}(W_{\alpha}^{*})h):=\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h,\quad h\in{\mathcal{H}},\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

This shows that

\dim[\text{\rm range}\,\pi(P_{{\mathbb{C}}1})]=\dim\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}=\dim{\mathcal{G}}.

The required dilation is obtained using relations (
4.2
) and
(
4.3
), and identifying
{\mathcal{G}}
with

\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}
. Since
T_{i}^{*}=[(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\bigoplus\pi(W_{i})^{*}]_{\mathcal{H}}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, it is easy to see that
T\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Note that any radial-pure
n
-tuple
T
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is in
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})}
, and, consequently, Theorem
4.1
applies.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
be a radially pure domain such that
g^{-1}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
has the property that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}
converges in the operator norm, and let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
. Then

T\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
if and only if there is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{D}}
and a Cuntz
*
-representation
\pi:C^{*}({\bf W})\to{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}
on a separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}
such that

T_{i}^{*}=[(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\bigoplus\pi(W_{i})^{*}]|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a radially pure domain, we have
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})\subset\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})}
. The direct implication follows from Theorem
4.3
.
To prove the converse, assume that
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
satisfies the relation above.
Since
{\bf W}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
and
\pi({\bf W})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}}_{\pi})
, we conclude that
T\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
. The proof is complete.
∎

We remark that, in the particular case when

g^{-1}=(1-\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq N}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha})^{m}
,
N,m\geq 1,

and

c_{\alpha}\geq 0
with
c_{g_{i}}>0
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a radially pure domain. This domain was studied in
[
41
]
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
4.3
and that

\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\left(\frac{b_{g_{p}\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}g_{p}\gamma}}-\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{p}\gamma}}\right)=0

for every
i,p\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Then the dilation of Theorem
4.3
is minimal, i.e.,

{\mathcal{K}}=\bigvee\limits_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}
, and
it is unique up to a unitary equivalence.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.7
, part (iii), we have

C^{*}({\bf W})=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{W_{\alpha}W_{\beta}^{*}:\ \alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\}.

Consequently, the map
\widetilde{\Phi}_{T}

in the proof of Theorem
4.3
is unique. The uniqueness of the
minimal Stinespring representation
[
54
]
implies the
uniqueness of the minimal dilation of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

representation theory of

-algebras
[

]
, one can use Theorem
4.3
to obtain
the following result.

Remark 4.6
.

Under the hypotheses of Corollary

4.5
, let
T^{\prime}:=(T_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,T_{n}^{\prime})\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})}
and
let
V^{\prime}:=(V_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,V_{n}^{\prime})
be the corresponding
dilation. Then
T
and
T^{\prime}
are unitarily equivalent
if and only if

\dim\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}=\dim\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T^{\prime},{T^{\prime}}^{*}){\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}

and there are unitary operators

U:\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}\to\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T^{\prime},{T^{\prime}}^{*}){\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}

and
\Gamma:{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi^{\prime}}

such that
\Gamma\pi(W_{i})=\pi^{\prime}(W_{i})\Gamma
for all

, and

\left[\begin{matrix}I_{F^{2}(H_{n})}\otimes U&0\\
0&\Gamma\end{matrix}\right]{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}.

Corollary 4.7
.

Assume the hypotheses of Corollary

4.5
. Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in T\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})}
and
let
V:=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be its minimal dilation on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}:=(F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}})\bigoplus{\mathcal{K}}_{\pi}
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

V
is a pure
n
-tuple in
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}})
if and only if

T

is a pure
n
-tuple in
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
;

(ii)

V
is a Cuntz
n
-tuple in
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}})
, i.e.
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})=0
, if and only if
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})=0
.

Proof.

Note that

\begin{split}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\alpha}^{*}&=P_{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})V_{\alpha}^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}\left[\begin{matrix}I_{F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}}&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right]|{\mathcal{H}}.\end{split}

Consequently,
T
is a pure
n
-tuple
if and only if
P_{\mathcal{H}}P_{F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}}|{\mathcal{H}}=I_{\mathcal{K}}
. The later
condition is equivalent to
{\mathcal{H}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
. On the
other hand, since
F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
is
reducing for
V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}
, and
{\mathcal{K}}
is the smallest
reducing subspace for
V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}
, which contains
{\mathcal{H}}
, we
must have
{\mathcal{K}}=F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
.
To prove item (ii), note that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})=\left[\begin{matrix}\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}}&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right]

and, consequently,
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})=0
if and only if

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}}=0
.
Since
\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}
, the later relation is equivalent to
{\mathcal{D}}=\{0\}
.
Therefore,
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})=0
if and only if
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Commutant lifting and Toeplitz-corona theorems

The goal of this section is to prove a commutant lifting theorem and use it to provide factorization results for multi-analytic operators and a Toeplitz-corona theorem in our setting.

Let
f
and
g
be two admissible free holomorphic functions and let

{\bf W}^{(f)}:=(W_{1}^{(f)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(f)})\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bf W}^{(g)}:=(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})

be the universal models associated with the noncommuative domains
{\mathcal{D}}_{f^{-1}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
, respectively.
We assume that
{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{f^{-1}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}
.
Similarly to the proof of Theorem
2.2
, one can show that given a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{E}}_{*}
,
there is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{F}}
and an isometry
V:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{*}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
such that

(5.1)

V(W_{i}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})=(W_{i}^{(g)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}})V,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

We need this result in what follows.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}
be a co-invariant subspace under each operator

W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}}
and let
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{*}
be a co-invariant subspace under each operator

W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}}
. Let
X\in B({\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{G}}_{2})
and define
\tilde{X}:=(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})X:{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\to V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})
.

If the
n
-tuples
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
and
T^{\prime}=(T_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,T_{n}^{\prime})
are defined by

T_{i}:=P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\ \text{ and }\ T_{i}^{\prime}:=P_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{F}}})|_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})},

then the following statements hold.

(i)

X[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=T_{i}X
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
if and only if

\tilde{X}[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=T_{i}^{\prime}\tilde{X}\quad\text{ for every }\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(ii)

\|X\|\leq 1
if and only if
\|\tilde{X}\|\leq 1
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{*}
is a co-invariant subspace under each operator

W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}}
and
V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
is an isometry, relation (
5.1
) implies

(W_{i}^{(f)*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}=(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})^{*}(W_{i}^{(g)*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}})(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}),\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Taking the adjoint of the later relation and multiplying to the left by
V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
, we obtain

(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}=P_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}})(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}).

Consequently,

(5.2)

P_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}})(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})\tilde{X}=(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}X.

On the other hand, we have

\tilde{X}[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})X[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}].

Note that if
X[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=T_{i}X
, then relation (
5.2
) implies

\tilde{X}[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=T_{i}^{\prime}\tilde{X}.

Conversely, if the later relation holds, we can use again relation (
5.2
) to deduce that

(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})X[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=P_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}})(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})\tilde{X}=(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}]X.

Since
X\in B({\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{G}}_{2})
and
V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
is an isometry, we obtain

X[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}]X,

which completes the proof of part (i). Since
\tilde{X}:=(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})
, part (ii) is obvious. The proof is complete.
∎

In
[
47
]
, we introduced the noncommutative Hardy algebras
F^{\infty}(g)
and
R^{\infty}(g)
associated with formal power series
g
with strictly positive coefficients and provided a
w^{*}
-continuous
F^{\infty}(g)
-functional calculus for pure (resp. completely non-coisometric)
n
-tuples of operators in the noncommutative domain
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
. We recall that the noncommutative Hardy space
F^{\infty}(g)
satisfies the relation

F^{\infty}(g)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})}^{SOT}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})}^{WOT}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})}^{w*},

where
{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})
stands for the algebra of all polynomials in
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
and the identity.
Moreover,
F^{\infty}(g)
is the sequential SOT-(resp. WOT-, w*-) closure of
{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})
.
Similar results hold for the noncommutative Hardy algebra
R^{\infty}(g)
. In addition, we have
R^{\infty}(g)=\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\}^{\prime}=F^{\infty}(g)^{\prime}
.

An operator
A:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
is called multi-analytic operators with respect to the universal models

{\bf W}^{(f)}:=(W_{1}^{(f)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(f)})\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bf W}^{(g)}:=(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})

if

A(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{F}}})A,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

In what follows, we provide a commutant lifting theorem in our setting.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
f
and
g
be two admissible free holomorphic functions and let

{\bf W}^{(f)}:=(W_{1}^{(f)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(f)})\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bf W}^{(g)}:=(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})

be the associated universal models.
We assume that
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain and
{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{f^{-1}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}
be a co-invariant subspace under each operator

W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}}
and let
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{*}
be a co-invariant subspace under each operator

W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}}
.

If
X\in B({\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{G}}_{2})
is a contraction such that

X[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}]X,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

then there is a contractive operator
\Gamma:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{*}
such that

\Gamma(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})\Gamma,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

X^{*}=\Gamma^{*}|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
, and
\|\Gamma\|=\|X\|
. Moreover, there is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{F}}
and a contractive multi-analytic operator
\Phi\in R^{\infty}(g)\bar{\otimes}_{min}B({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
and a co-isometric multi-analytic operator
F:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{*}
such that

F(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{F}}})=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{*}})F,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and
\Gamma=F\Phi
.

Proof.

Define
\tilde{X}:=(V|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}})X:{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\to V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})
. Due to Proposition
5.1
, we have

\tilde{X}[P_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}})|_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}]=P_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{F}}})|_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})}\tilde{X},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Since
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain, we can apply Theorem 4.2 from
[
44
]
to find a contractive operator

\Phi\in R^{\infty}(g)\bar{\otimes}_{min}B({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
such that
\Phi^{*}|_{V({\mathcal{G}}_{2})}=\tilde{X}^{*}
and

\|\Phi\|=\|\tilde{X}\|=\|X\|
.
Setting
\Gamma:=F\Phi
, where
F:=V^{*}
, we obtain

Theorem 5.4

and

6.
Boundary property for universal models

In this section, we provide a large class of universal models
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
with the boundary property. In this case, we show that the full Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
is rigid with respect to
{\bf W}
.

We recall some basics concerning the Arveson’s theory of boundary representations
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a linear subspace of
{\mathcal{B}}
, which contains the identity of
{\mathcal{B}}
, and such that
{\mathcal{B}}=C^{*}({\mathcal{S}})
. An irreducible representation
\pi:C^{*}({\mathcal{S}})\to B({\mathcal{H}})
is said to be a
boundary representation
for
{\mathcal{S}}
if
\pi|_{\mathcal{S}}
has a unique completely positive extension to
{\mathcal{B}}
, namely,
\pi
itself. Arveson’s boundary theorem
[
6
]
states that if
{\mathcal{S}}\subset B({\mathcal{H}})
is an irreducible linear subspace such that
I\in{\mathcal{S}}
and the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\mathcal{S}})
contains the compact operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
, then the identity representation of

C^{*}({\mathcal{S}})
is a boundary representation for
{\mathcal{S}}
if and only if the quotient map

Q:B({\mathcal{H}})\to B({\mathcal{H}})/{{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})}
is not completely isometric on the linear span of
{\mathcal{S}}\cup{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that
b_{\alpha}>0
and

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty,\quad\text{ for every }\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and
let
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left creation operators associated with
g
.
We recall that, due to Theorem
3.6
, the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
is irreducible.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that
{\bf W}
has the boundary property if the identity representation of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
is a boundary representation for the operator space
\Span\{I,W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\}
.

The next result provides a large class of universal models
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
with the boundary property.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that
b_{\alpha}>0
and

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty,\quad\text{ for every }\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and
let
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left creation operators associated with
g
.
Assume that the following conditions hold.

(i)

There is
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\left(\frac{b_{g_{p}\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}g_{p}\gamma}}-\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{p}\gamma}}\right)=0,\qquad p\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(ii)

The following limit exists and
\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}\gamma}}<\sup_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}\gamma}}
.

Then the
n
-tuple
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
has the boundary property.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.6
, if item (i) holds, then
the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
contains all the compact operators in
B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
.
Using the definition of the operators
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
, we deduce that

(6.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{*}W_{i}=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}\gamma}}\right)P_{{\mathbb{C}}e_{\gamma}},

where
P_{{\mathbb{C}}e_{\gamma}}
is the orthogonal projection onto the one-dimensional space
{\mathbb{C}}e_{\gamma}
.
Setting
a:=\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}\gamma}}
, we deduce that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{*}W_{i}-aI=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}\gamma}}-a\right)P_{{\mathbb{C}}e_{\gamma}}.

Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{*}W_{i}-aI
is a compact operator and, consequently, the essential norm satisfies the inequality

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{*}W_{i}\right\|_{e}\leq a.

On the other hand, using relation (
6.1
), we deduce that

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{*}W_{i}\right\|=\sup_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{i}\gamma}}.

Now, due to item(ii), we obtain

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{*}W_{i}\right\|_{e}<\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{*}W_{i}\right\|.

Therefore, the Calkin map

Q:B({\mathcal{H}})\to B({\mathcal{H}})/{{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})}
is not completely isometric on the linear span
\Span\{I,W_{1},\ldots,W_{n},W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*}\}
. Indeed, taking

M=\left(\begin{matrix}W_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
W_{n}&0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\right),

we have
\|M\|^{2}_{e}=\|M^{*}M\|_{e}<\|M^{*}M\|=\|M\|^{2}
.
Applying now Arveson’s boundary theorem, we conclude that the identity representation of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
is a boundary representation for the operator space
\Span\{I,W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\}
.
Hence, the
n
-tuple
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
has the boundary property.
The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that
b_{\alpha}>0
,

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty,\quad\text{ for every }\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

If

are the weighted left creation operators associated with

then

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin
with
b_{\alpha}=b_{\beta}>0
if

such that the following limit exists and

for any

with

.
Then the
n
-tuple

associated with

Example 6.5
.

Let

and let

Example 6.6
.

For each

, let

In this case, we have

, we deduce that,

associated with

,

. In this case,
{\bf W}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
, the
n
-tuple of left creation operators on the full Fock space.

Using the idea of the proof of Proposition 8.1 from
[
8
]
, we prove the following.

Proposition 6.7
.

Assume that the universal model
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
has the boundary property. If
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
is a sequence (
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
or
N=\infty
) of operators in the commutant of the set
\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\}
such that

A_{1}^{*}A_{1}+A_{2}^{*}A_{2}+\cdots=I,

then
A_{k}=c_{k}I
for some
c_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{2}=1
.

Proof.

Define the map
\Omega:C^{*}({\bf W})\to B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
by setting

\Omega(X):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}A_{i}^{*}XA_{i}
. When
N=\infty
, the convergence of the series is in the strong operator topology. Due to the fact that
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\}^{\prime}
, we have
\Omega(I)=I
,
\Omega(W_{i})=W_{i}
and
\Omega(W_{i}^{*})=W_{i}^{*}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Since
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
has the boundary property, we must have
\Omega(X)=X
for every
X\in C^{*}({\bf W})
.
Define
U:=\left[\begin{matrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}\right]:F^{2}(H_{n})\to\bigoplus_{1}^{N}F^{2}(H_{n})
and note that
U
is an isometry.
Consider the representation
\pi:C^{*}({\bf W})\to\bigoplus_{1}^{N}F^{2}(H_{n})
defined by
\pi(X):=\oplus_{1}^{N}X
.
Note that
\Omega(X)=U^{*}\pi(X)U
for all
X\in C^{*}({\bf W})
and

\begin{split}(UX-\pi(X)U)^{*}(UX-\pi(X)U)&=X^{*}U^{*}UX-U^{*}\pi(X^{*})UX-X^{*}U^{*}\pi(X)U+U^{*}\pi(X^{*}X)U\\
&=XX^{*}-\Omega(X^{*})X-X^{*}\Omega(X)+\Omega(X^{*}X)=0\end{split}

for all
X\in C^{*}({\bf W})
. Consequently,
UX=\pi(X)U
, which shows that
A_{k}X=XA_{k}
for every
X\in C^{*}({\bf W})
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
.
Since the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
is irreducible (see Theorem
3.6
), we deduce that
A_{k}=c_{k}I
for some
c_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{2}=1
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

The
n
-tuple of left creation operators
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
on the full Fock space

Proof.

Note that the right creation operators

are in the commutant of

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})
and
{\mathcal{N}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})
be closed invariant invariant subspaces under
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
. We say that
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}
are equivalent if there is a unitary operator
U:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{N}}
such that

UW_{i}=W_{i}U,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

We say that an invariant subspace
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})
under

is rigid (or that
({\mathcal{M}},{\bf W})
is rigid) if any invariant subspace
{\mathcal{N}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})
which is unitarily equivalent to

must be

itself.

Proposition 6.9
.

Assume that the universal model
{\bf W}:=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
associated with
g
has the boundary property. Then
(F^{2}(H_{n}),{\bf W})
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})
be an invariant invariant subspaces under
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
and let
U:F^{2}(H_{n})\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a unitary operator such that
UW_{i}=W_{i}U
for every

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.
Since
U^{*}U=I
, we can apply Proposition
6.7
to deduce that
U=cI
for some
c\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\}
. Consequently,
{\mathcal{M}}=F^{2}(H_{n})
, which completes the proof.
∎

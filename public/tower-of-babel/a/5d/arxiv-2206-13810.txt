Abstract
In the paper, we firstly study the algebraic structures of ℤ p ​ ℤ p k \mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}} -additive cyclic codes and give the generator polynomials and the minimal spanning set of these codes. Secondly, a necessary and sufficient condition for the ℤ p ​ ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}} -additive cyclic code whose Gray image is linear (not necessarily cyclic) over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} is given. Moreover, as for some special families of cyclic codes over ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}} and ℤ p ​ ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}} , the linearity of the Gray images is determined.

2.1
Gray Map and Gray Image

In
[
19
]
, the classical Gray map
\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{p}
is defined as

\phi(\theta)=\theta_{0}(1,1,\cdots,1)+\theta_{1}(0,1,\cdots,p-1),

where
\theta=\theta_{0}p+\theta_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
,
0\leqslant\theta_{0},\theta_{1}<p
and
\phi(\theta)
is a vector of length
p
.
The homogeneous weight
[
19
]

wt_{hom}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
is

wt_{hom}(x)=\begin{cases}p,&\text{if}\quad x\in p\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\setminus\{0\},\\
p-1,&\text{if}\quad x\notin p\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}},\\
0,&\text{if}\quad x=0.\end{cases}

The homogeneous distance is
d_{hom}(\bm{u},\bm{v})=wt_{hom}(\bm{u}-\bm{v})
, where
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{n}
.
Then
\phi
is an isometry from
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{n},d_{hom})
to
(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{pn},d_{H})
, where
d_{H}
is the Hamming distance on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{p}
.

For positive integers
\alpha
and
\beta
, let
n=\alpha+p\beta
and
\Phi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be an extension of the Gray map
\phi
,
which is defined as:

\Phi(\bm{x},\bm{y})=(\bm{x},\phi(y_{1}),\phi(y_{2}),\cdots,\phi(y_{\beta}))

for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}
, and
\bm{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{\beta})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{\beta}
.
The
p
-ary code
C=\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-
linear code

or the
p
-ary image
of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive code.
We denote
\langle C\rangle
the
linear span
of the codewords of
C
.

2.2
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-Additive Cyclic Codes

Recall some definitions about
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic codes in
[
24
,
29
]
.

The code
\mathcal{C}
is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive code
with
parameter
(\alpha,\beta)
if it is a subgroup of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\beta}
, where
k\geqslant 2
is an integer and
\gcd(\beta,p)=1
.
Obviously,
\mathcal{C}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\beta}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{C}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\beta}
, and from the theorem of finite abelian groups, it is isomorphic to an abelian
structure
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{k}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\gamma_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k-1}}^{\gamma_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{\gamma_{k-1}}

where
\gamma_{i}\geqslant 0
are integers for
i=0,1,\cdots,k
.
Thus, the size of
\mathcal{C}
is
p^{\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{k}+k\gamma_{1}+(k-1)\gamma_{2}+\cdots+2\gamma_{k-1}}
.

Let
X
be the set of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
coordinate positions, and
Y
be the set of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
coordinate positions, so
|X|=\alpha
and
|Y|=\beta
.
In general, say the first
\alpha
positions corresponds to the set
X
and the last
\beta
positions corresponds to the set
Y
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{X}
and
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
be the punctured codes of
\mathcal{C}
by deleting the coordinates outside
X
and
Y
, respectively.

Throughout this paper, the
order
of a codeword
\bm{v}
(or a vector) is the additive order, i.e.,
the smallest positive integer
s
such that
s\bm{v}=\bm{0}
.
Then the subcode
\mathcal{C}_{p}
is defined as

\mathcal{C}_{p}=\left\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}:p\bm{c}=\bm{0}\right\}.

Let
\kappa
be the dimension of the
p
-ary linear code
(\mathcal{C}_{p})_{X}
.
If
\alpha=0
, then
\kappa=0
.
Consisting of all these parameters, we will say that
\mathcal{C}
is of
type

(\alpha,\beta;\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k};\kappa)
.

When
k=2
, the type is always written as
(\alpha,\beta;\gamma,\delta;\kappa)
, i.e.,
the code is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\gamma}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{\delta}
.
Let
\kappa_{1}
be the dimension of the linear subcode
\{(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{C}\}
and
\kappa_{2}=\kappa-\kappa_{1}
.
Similarly, it’s known that the linear subcode
\{(\bm{0},\bm{v}_{2})\in\mathcal{C}\}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{\delta_{2}}
for some
0\leqslant\delta_{2}\leqslant\delta
, then let
\delta_{1}=\delta-\delta_{2}
.
A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive code
\mathcal{C}
is called
separable
if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{X}\times\mathcal{C}_{Y}
.
By definition, a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive code is separable if and only if
\kappa_{2}=\delta_{1}=0
; that is
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
and
\delta=\delta_{2}
.

Definition 1
.

[
1
,
29
]

A subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\beta}
is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive code;

(2)

For any codeword
\bm{u}=(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{u}^{\prime\prime})=(u^{\prime}_{0},u^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,u^{\prime}_{\alpha-1},u^{\prime\prime}_{0},u^{\prime\prime}_{1},\cdots,u^{\prime\prime}_{\beta-1})\in\mathcal{C}
, its cyclic shift

\pi(\bm{u})=(\pi(\bm{u}^{\prime}),\pi(\bm{u}^{\prime\prime}))=(u^{\prime}_{\alpha-1},u^{\prime}_{0},\cdots,u^{\prime}_{\alpha-2},u^{\prime\prime}_{\beta-1},u^{\prime\prime}_{0},\cdots,u^{\prime\prime}_{\beta-2})

is also in
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 1
.

[
8
,
3
]

Since
\kappa=\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}
and
\delta=\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}
,
where
\kappa_{1}
is the dimension of the subcode
\{(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{C}\}

and
\delta_{2}
is the dimension of the subcode
\{(\bm{0},\bm{v}_{2})\in\mathcal{C}\}
whose codewords are all of order
p^{2}
,
then the generator matrix can be written as:

\mathcal{G}_{S}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cccc|ccccc}I_{\kappa_{1}}&T&T^{\prime}_{p_{1}}&T_{p_{1}}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&I_{\kappa_{2}}&T^{\prime}_{p_{2}}&T_{p_{2}}&pT_{2}&pT_{\kappa_{2}}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&pT_{1}&pT^{\prime}_{1}&pI_{\gamma-\kappa}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\hline\cr\bm{0}&\bm{0}&S_{\delta_{1}}&S_{p}&S_{11}&S_{12}&R_{1}&I_{\delta_{1}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&S_{21}&S_{22}&R_{2}&R_{\delta_{1}}&I_{\delta_{2}}\\
\end{array}\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
I_{r}
is the identity matrix of size
r\times r
;
T_{p_{i}},T^{\prime}_{p_{i}},S_{\delta_{1}}
and
S_{p}
are matrices over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
;

T_{1},T_{2},T_{\kappa_{2}},T^{\prime}_{1}
and
R_{i}
are matrices over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
with entries in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
;
and
S_{ij}
are matrices over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
.
Besides,
S_{\delta_{1}}
and
T_{\kappa_{2}}
are square matrices of full rank
\delta_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
, respectively.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the generator matrix of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive cyclic code, then
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}_{S}
should be permutation-equivalent,
i.e., the codes generated by
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}_{S}
are permutation-equivalent.
Moreover, if the rows of
\mathcal{G}
are arranged as
\mathcal{G}_{S}
, we call
\mathcal{G}
is
in the form
of
\mathcal{G}_{S}
.

3.1
Generator Polynomials of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-Additive Cyclic Codes

In
[
1
]
, the authors studied the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mathbb{Z}_{4}
-additive cyclic codes.
Recall that there exists a bijection between
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\beta}
and

R_{\alpha,\beta}=R_{\alpha}\times R_{\beta}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{\alpha}-1)\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]/(x^{\beta}-1)

given by

(u^{\prime}_{0},\cdots,u^{\prime}_{\alpha-1},u^{\prime\prime}_{0},\cdots,u^{\prime\prime}_{\beta-1})\longmapsto(u^{\prime}_{0}+\cdots+u^{\prime}_{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha-1},u^{\prime\prime}_{0}+\cdots+u^{\prime\prime}_{\beta-1}x^{\beta-1}).

Hence, any codeword can be regarded as a vector or as a polynomial.
This subsection is devoted to giving the structures of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic codes in terms of polynomials.
For simplicity,
a
,
b
,
f
always refer to the polynomials
a(x),b(x),f(x)
.
Denote the
p
-ary reduction of a polynomial
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
by
\overline{h}\equiv h\pmod{p}
.
Then define the following multiplication

h\star(c_{1},c_{2})=(\overline{h}c_{1},hc_{2}),

where
(c_{1},c_{2})\in R_{\alpha,\beta}
and the products of the right side are the standard polynomial products in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{\alpha}-1)

and
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]/(x^{\beta}-1)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code, and we will determine the set of generators for
\mathcal{C}

as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
-submodule of
R_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}^{\beta}
.
Define the map
\Psi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]/(x^{\beta}-1)
, where
\Psi((g_{1},g_{2}))=g_{2}
.
Obviously,
\Psi
is a module homomorphism.
Then, we claim that its image is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]/(x^{\beta}-1)
and its kernel is a submodule of
\mathcal{C}
.
Moreover, the image of
\Psi
can be regarded as an ideal in the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]/(x^{\beta}-1)
.
Note that
\ker(\Psi)=\{(a,0)\in\mathcal{C}\mid a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{\alpha}-1)\}
, then we define the set
I
to be

I=\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{\alpha}-1)\mid(a,0)\in\ker(\Psi)\}.

It’s clear that
I
is an ideal and hence a cyclic code in the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{\alpha}-1)
.
More details about these ideals can be seen in
[
11
]
.

By the first isomorphism theorem and
[
11
, Theorem 6]
, we have

\mathcal{C}/\ker(\Psi)\cong\langle f_{0},pf_{1},\cdots,p^{k-1}f_{k-1}\rangle=\langle f\rangle,

where
f_{k-1}\mid f_{k-2}\mid\cdots\mid f_{1}\mid f_{0}\mid(x^{\beta}-1)
and
f=f_{0}+pf_{1}+\cdots+p^{k-1}f_{k-1}
.

Thus, any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code can be generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
-submodule of
R_{\alpha,\beta}
by two elements
of the form
(a,0)
and
(b,f)
for some
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{\alpha}-1)
,
i.e., any element in the code
\mathcal{C}
can be described as

d_{1}\star(a,0)+d_{2}\star(b,f),

where
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
.
In fact,
d_{1}
can be restricted in the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
.
Hence, we get

\mathcal{C}=\langle(a,0),(b,f)\rangle,

with
p
-ary polynomials
a
,
b
.

Summarize the above discussions, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

The multiplication
\star
above is well-defined and
R_{\alpha,\beta}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
-module with respect to this multiplication.
Moreover, a code
\mathcal{C}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
-submodule of
R_{\alpha,\beta}
.

The following theorem will give the generator polynomial of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code of type
(\alpha,\beta;\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k};\kappa)
with
\gcd(\beta,p)=1
.
Then
\mathcal{C}
can be identified as

(1)

\mathcal{C}=\langle(a,0)\rangle
, where
a\mid(x^{\alpha}-1)\pmod{p}
;

(2)

\mathcal{C}=\langle(b,f)\rangle
, where
f=f_{0}+pf_{1}+p^{2}f_{2}+\cdots+p^{k-1}f_{k-1}
with
f_{k-1}\mid f_{k-2}\mid\cdots\mid f_{1}\mid f_{0}\mid(x^{\beta}-1)
;

(3)

\mathcal{C}=\langle(a,0),(b,f)\rangle
, where
a\mid(x^{\alpha}-1)\pmod{p}
,

f=f_{0}+pf_{1}+p^{2}f_{2}+\cdots+p^{k-1}f_{k-1}
with
f_{k-1}\mid f_{k-2}\mid\cdots\mid f_{1}\mid f_{0}\mid(x^{\beta}-1)
and
b
is a polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
satisfying
\deg(b)<\deg(a)
.
Moreover,
a\mid h_{k-1}b
, where
f_{k-1}h_{k-1}=x^{\beta}-1
.

Proof.

It’s sufficient to show that
\deg(b)<\deg(a)
and
a\mid h_{k-1}b
.

•

Suppose
\deg(b)\geqslant\deg(a)
, then using the division algorithm (over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
), we have
b=qa+r
for some polynomials
q
and
r
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
\deg(r)<\deg(a)
.
Thus,

D=\langle(a,0),(r,f)\rangle=\langle(a,0),(b,f)\rangle=C,

since
(b,f)=q\star(a,0)+(r,f)
.

•

Since
f_{k-1}\mid f_{k-2}\mid\cdots\mid f_{1}\mid f_{0}
, then
h_{k-1}f\equiv 0\pmod{(x^{\beta}-1)}
.
Consider that

\Psi\left(\frac{x^{\beta}-1}{f_{k-1}}\star(b,f)\right)=\Psi\left(\left(h_{k-1}b,0\right)\right)=0.

Hence,

\left(h_{k-1}b,0\right)\in\ker(\Psi)\subseteq\mathcal{C}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad a\mid h_{k-1}b.

Thus, we complete the proof.
∎

In this subsection, we will give the minimal spanning set of a

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle(a,0),(b,f)\rangle
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code of type

(\alpha,\beta;\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k};\kappa)
with
\gcd(\beta,p)=1
,
where
f=f_{0}+pf_{1}+p^{2}f_{2}+\cdots+p^{k-1}f_{k-1}
with
f_{k-1}\mid f_{k-2}\mid\cdots\mid f_{1}\mid f_{0}\mid(x^{\beta}-1)
.
Let
\deg(f_{i})=t_{i}
,
\deg(a)=t_{a}
, then we define

T_{1}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\alpha-t_{a}-1}\left\{x^{i}\star(a,0)\right\},T_{2}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\beta-t_{0}-1}\left\{x^{i}\star(b,f)\right\},

and

S_{j}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{t_{j}-t_{j+1}-1}\left\{x^{i}\star(\overline{h_{j}}b,h_{j}f)\right\},

where
h_{j}f_{j}\equiv x^{\beta}-1\pmod{p^{k}}
,
j=0,1,\cdots,k-2
.
Then
S=T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup S_{0}\cup S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{k-2}
forms a minimal spanning set for
\mathcal{C}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-module.
Moreover, we have
\gamma_{0}=\alpha-t_{a}
,
\gamma_{1}=\beta-t_{0}
,
\gamma_{i}=t_{i-2}-t_{i-1}
for
2\leqslant i\leqslant k
.

Proof.

Let
c
be a codeword of
\mathcal{C}
, then there exist polynomials
\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
such that

c=\overline{\lambda_{1}}\star\left(a,0\right)+\mu_{1}\star\left(b,f\right).

(1)

If
\deg(\overline{\lambda_{1}})\leqslant\alpha-\deg(a)-1
, then
\overline{\lambda_{1}}\star(a,0)
can be spanned by
T_{1}
.
Otherwise, by the division algorithm, we get two polynomials
\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
such that

\overline{\lambda_{1}}=\left(\frac{x^{\alpha}-1}{a}\right)\overline{\lambda_{2}}+\overline{\mu_{2}},

where
\overline{\mu_{2}}=0
or
\deg(\overline{\mu_{2}})\leqslant\alpha-\deg(a)-1
.
Hence,

\overline{\lambda_{1}}\star(a,0)=\overline{\mu_{2}}\star(a,0).

Thus, we can assume that
\overline{\lambda_{1}}\star(a,0)
is spanned by
T_{1}
.

(2)

If
\deg(\mu_{1})\leqslant\beta-\deg(f)-1=\beta-t_{0}-1
, then
\mu_{1}\star(b,f)
is spanned by
T_{2}
.
Otherwise, by the division algorithm, we get

\mu_{1}=\left(\frac{x^{\beta}-1}{f_{0}}\right)q_{0}+r_{0}=h_{0}q_{0}+r_{0},

where
r_{0}=0
or
\deg(r_{0})\leqslant\beta-t_{0}-1
, which implies that

\mu_{1}\star(b,f)=q_{0}\star(\overline{h_{0}}b,h_{0}f)+r_{0}\star(b,f).

Obviously,
r_{0}\star(b,f)
is spanned by
T_{2}
.
Thus, we only need to consider
q_{0}\star(\overline{h_{0}}b,h_{0}f)
.

(3)

The degree of
q_{0}
is important. If
\deg(q_{0})\leqslant t_{0}-t_{1}-1
, then we are done.
Otherwise, by division algorithm again, we have

q_{0}=\left(\frac{x^{\beta}-1}{h_{0}f_{1}}\right)q_{1}+r_{1}=\frac{h_{1}}{h_{0}}q_{1}+r_{1},

for some
q_{1},r_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
with
r_{1}=0
or
\deg(r_{1})\leqslant t_{0}-t_{1}-1
.
Thus,

q_{0}\star(\overline{h_{0}}b,h_{0}f)=q_{1}\star(\overline{h_{1}}b,h_{1}f)+r_{1}\star(\overline{h_{0}}b,h_{0}f),

which implies that
r_{1}\star(\overline{h_{0}}b,h_{0}f)
is spanned by
S_{0}
.

(4)

As for
q_{1}
, we have similar discussion.
For general, if
\deg(q_{j})\leqslant t_{j}-t_{j+1}-1
for some
0\leqslant j\leqslant k-3
, then we are done.
Otherwise, by division algorithm again, we have

q_{j}=\left(\frac{x^{\beta}-1}{h_{j}f_{j+1}}\right)q_{j+1}+r_{j+1}=\frac{h_{j+1}}{h_{j}}q_{j+1}+r_{j+1},

for some
q_{j+1},r_{j+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}[x]
with
r_{j+1}=0
or
\deg(r_{j+1})\leqslant t_{j}-t_{j+1}-1
.
Thus,

q_{j}\star(\overline{h_{j}}b,h_{j}f)=q_{j+1}\star(\overline{h_{j+1}}b,h_{j+1}f)+r_{j+1}\star(\overline{h_{j}}b,h_{j}f),

which implies that
r_{j+1}\star(\overline{h_{j}}b,h_{j}f)
is spanned by
S_{j}
.
Note that
h_{k-2}f=p^{k-1}h_{k-2}f_{k-1}
but
h_{k-1}f=h_{k-1}f_{k-1}=x^{\beta}-1=0
.
Therefore, if
j=k-2
, then

q_{k-2}\star(\overline{h_{k-2}}b,h_{k-2}f)=q_{k-1}\star(\overline{h_{k-1}}b,0)+r_{k-1}\star(\overline{h_{k-2}}b,h_{k-2}f).

Since
a\mid\frac{x^{\beta}-1}{f_{k-1}}b=h_{k-1}b
, then
q_{k-2}\star(\overline{h_{k-2}}b,h_{k-2}f)
is spanned by
T_{1}
and
S_{k-2}
.

Therefore,
S=T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup S_{0}\cup S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{k-2}
is a spanning set.
Moreover, no element in
S
is a linear combination of the other elements, so
S
is minimal. On the other hand, the order of the codewords spanned by
S_{j}
is just
p^{k-1-j}
.
Then
\gamma_{0}=\alpha-t_{a}
,
\gamma_{1}=\beta-t_{0}
, and
\gamma_{i}=t_{i-2}-t_{i-1}
for
2\leqslant i\leqslant k
.
∎

4
p
-ary Images of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-Additive Cyclic Codes

In
[
9
]
and
[
27
]
, the authors studied the Gray images of the cyclic codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, respectively.
This section is devoted to generalizing the results about Gray images to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, respectively.
In
[
26
]
, the Gray map
\Phi
defined for quaternary codes satisfies
\Phi(\bm{u}+\bm{v})=\Phi(\bm{u})+\Phi(\bm{v})+\Phi(2\bm{u}\ast\bm{v})
,
where
\ast
denotes the
componentwise product
of two vectors.
As for the codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, we have a similar result.
Now, we reprove
[
17
, Lemma 2]
by
[
23
, Lemma 2]
and a slightly different proof.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{\alpha+\beta}),\bm{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{\alpha+\beta})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{\beta}
, then

\Phi(\bm{u}+\bm{v})=\Phi(\bm{u})+\Phi(\bm{v})+\Phi(pP(\bm{u},\bm{v})),

(2)

where
P(\bm{u},\bm{v})=\left(P(u_{1},v_{1}),\cdots,P(u_{\alpha+\beta},v_{\alpha+\beta})\right)
and

P(u_{i},v_{i})\equiv\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\sum_{b=p-a}^{p-1}\frac{\prod_{m=0}^{p-1}(u_{i}-m)(v_{i}-m)}{(u_{i}-a)(v_{i}-b)}\pmod{p}=\begin{cases}1,&\overline{u}_{i}+\overline{v}_{i}\geqslant p,\\
0,&\overline{u}_{i}+\overline{v}_{i}<p,\\
\end{cases}

(3)

with
\overline{u}_{i}\equiv u_{i}\pmod{p}
,
\overline{v}_{i}\equiv v_{i}\pmod{p}
.

Proof.

It’s sufficient to show the values of
P(u_{i},v_{i})
in (
3
).
Note that

\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\sum_{b=p-a}^{p-1}\frac{\prod_{m=0}^{p-1}(x-m)(y-m)}{(x-a)(y-b)}\equiv\sum_{a+b\geqslant p}\frac{(x^{p}-x)(y^{p}-y)}{(x-a)(y-b)}\pmod{p},

since
x^{p}-x\equiv\prod_{m=0}^{p-1}(x-m)\pmod{p}
when
p
is prime.
For
0\leqslant x,y\leqslant p-1
, if
x\neq a
or
y\neq b
, then

\frac{(x^{p}-x)(y^{p}-y)}{(x-a)(y-b)}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Then, according to the famous Wilson’s Theorem:
(p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod{p}
, we have

\sum_{a+b\geqslant p}\frac{(x^{p}-x)(y^{p}-y)}{(x-a)(y-b)}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b\geqslant p,\\
x=a,y=b\end{subarray}}\frac{(x^{p}-x)(y^{p}-y)}{(x-a)(y-b)}\pmod{p}=\begin{cases}1,&x+y\geqslant p,\\
0,&x+y<p.\\
\end{cases}

The equality (
3
) holds since
P(x,y)=P(\bar{x},\bar{y})
when
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive code.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-linear code
C=\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is linear if and only if
pP(\bm{u},\bm{v})\in\mathcal{C}
for all
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Assume that
C
is linear, then for any
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
\Phi(\bm{u}),\Phi(\bm{v}),\Phi(\bm{u}+\bm{v})\in C
.
Thus, by Lemma
1
,
\Phi(pP(\bm{u},\bm{v}))\in C
.
Considering that
\phi(px)=\phi(py)
if and only if
px=py
, then
pP(\bm{u},\bm{v})\in\mathcal{C}
, which is important and occurs many times in this paper.

Conversely, for every
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in C
, there exist
\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\bm{x}=\Phi(\bm{u}^{\prime})
and
\bm{y}=\Phi(\bm{v}^{\prime})
.
Thus,
pP(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
, which implies that
\bm{u}^{\prime}+\bm{v}^{\prime}+(p-1)pP(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
.
Hence, by Lemma
1
,

\Phi(\bm{u}^{\prime}+\bm{v}^{\prime}+(p-1)pP(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}))=\Phi(\bm{u}^{\prime}+\bm{v}^{\prime})+(p-1)\Phi(pP(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}))\in C.

Thus,

\displaystyle\bm{x}+\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\Phi(\bm{u}^{\prime})+\Phi(\bm{v}^{\prime})=\Phi(\bm{u}^{\prime})+\Phi(\bm{v}^{\prime})+p\Phi(pP(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\Phi(\bm{u}^{\prime}+\bm{v}^{\prime})+(p-1)\Phi(pP(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}))\in C,

and we complete the proof.
∎

\bm{u}^{i}
.

a\ast b=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}x^{i}\right)\ast\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}b_{i}x^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}x^{i}.

A\ast B=\{a\ast b|a\in A,b\in B\}

From now on, denote the principal ideal generated by

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle fh+pf\rangle

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}

and
\gcd(n,p)=1
,
then for every positive integer
i
, we have

pI^{i}=p\underbrace{\langle fh\rangle\ast\dots\ast\langle fh\rangle}_{i}=\{p\underbrace{v_{1}\ast\dots\ast v_{i}}_{i}|v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\in\mathcal{C}\},

and

I_{i}=\underbrace{\langle\overline{fh}\rangle_{p}^{n}\ast\dots\ast\langle\overline{fh}\rangle_{p}^{n}}_{i}=\{\underbrace{\overline{v_{1}}\ast\dots\ast\overline{v_{i}}}_{i}|v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

The equalities hold since for every polynomial (codeword)

,

and
\overline{fh}\mid\overline{v}
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle fh+pf\rangle
be a cyclic code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
of length
n
with
fhg=x^{n}-1
and
\gcd(n,p)=1
,
then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is a
p
-ary linear code,

(2)

pP(\overline{r_{1}fh},\overline{r_{2}fh})\in\langle pf\rangle
for any
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}[x]
,

(3)

pI^{\ell}\subseteq\langle pf\rangle
(or equivalently,
I_{\ell}\subseteq\langle\overline{f}\rangle_{p}^{n}
) for every
\ell\in L=\{1+2i|1\leqslant i\leqslant(p-1)/2\}\cup\{p-1\}
.

Proof.

First, we prove
(1)
and
(2)
are equivalent.
It’s easy to check that
pm_{1}m_{2}=p\overline{m}_{1}\overline{m}_{2}
if
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, where
\overline{m}_{1}\equiv m_{1}\pmod{p}
and
\overline{m}_{2}\equiv m_{2}\pmod{p}
.
It’s known that
\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is linear if and only if for any
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathcal{C}
,
pP(\overline{\bm{u}},\overline{\bm{v}})\in\mathcal{C}
,
where
\overline{\bm{u}}\equiv\bm{u}\pmod{p}
and
\overline{\bm{v}}\equiv\bm{v}\pmod{p}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is a cyclic code, then
\bm{u}=r_{1}G
,
\bm{v}=r_{2}G
for some polynomials
r_{1}
,
r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}[x]
, where
G=fh+pf
.
Hence,
pP(\overline{r_{1}G},\overline{r_{2}G})\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
, i.e.,
pP(\overline{r_{1}fh},\overline{r_{2}fh})\in\langle pf\rangle
.

As for
(1)
and
(3)
, note that
I^{\ell}
is the set of all the componentwise products of
\ell
codewords.
If
(3)
holds, then
(2)
holds, hence
\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is linear.
On the other hand, if
(1)
holds, then by
[
17
, Theorem 2]
,
\Phi(pI^{\ell})
,
\ell\in L
are generators of
\Phi(\mathcal{C})
.
Equivalently,
pI^{\ell}\subseteq\langle pf\rangle
.
∎

Let
c
be a divisor of
x^{n}-1\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, then
\left(c\otimes c\otimes\cdots\otimes c\right)_{j}
is defined to be the polynomial whose roots are the products
\xi^{i_{1}}\xi^{i_{2}}\cdots\xi^{i_{j}}
, where

is a positive integer and
\xi^{i_{1}},\xi^{i_{2}},\cdots,\xi^{i_{j}}
are roots of

For convenience, denote

by

, which is called the
j_{th}

circle product
of

.
For example,
c_{2}=(c\otimes c)_{2}
and
c_{3}=(c\otimes c\otimes c)_{3}
.
In general,

since

.
Equivalently, the roots of

form a subset (not necessarily a subgroup) of the group of the
n
-th roots of unity.
In addition,
0\leqslant\deg(c_{i})\leqslant\deg(c_{i+1})\leqslant n
,

.

Denote the set of roots of

by

, then the set of roots of

is a subset of

.

Example 3
.

It’s known that the roots of

are in the extension field
\mathbb{F}_{9}
.
Let

That’s to say, the roots of

and

S_{1}=\{\xi^{-2},\xi^{0},\xi^{2}\}=\{1,-1\}\subseteq U_{4},\quad\textnormal{and}\quad S_{2}=\{\xi^{-3},\xi^{-1},\xi,\xi^{3}\}=\{\xi,\xi^{-1}\}\subseteq U_{4},

Hence,

and

Lemma 3
.

Let
c
and
d
be two polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
such that
x^{n}-1=cd
, where
\gcd(n,p)=1
.
Denote the
j_{th}
circle product of
c
by

and let

be the polynomial such that
c_{j}e_{j}=x^{n}-1
for

Then
J_{i}\subseteq\langle e_{i}\rangle_{p}^{n}
,
where
J=J_{1}=\langle d\rangle_{p}^{n}

J_{i+1}=J_{i}\ast J
for

Proof.

Let
s_{i}(x)=a_{1}^{i}(x)d(x)\ast a_{2}^{i}(x)d(x)\ast\cdots\ast a_{i}^{i}(x)d(x)\in J_{i}

for

be the Mattson-Solomon polynomials of

.
Then, by
[

, Ch.8.§6. Theorem 22]

S_{i}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}s_{i}(z^{k})x^{k}=\frac{1}{n^{i-1}}A_{1}^{i}(x)A_{2}^{i}(x)\cdots A_{i}^{i}(x),

where
z
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity in some extension field of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
A_{j}^{i}(x)
is the Mattson-Solomon polynomials of

with

and

A_{j}^{i}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{j}^{i}(z^{k})d(z^{k})x^{k}.

Since
x^{n}-1=d(x)c(x)
, then
d(z^{k})\neq 0
if and only if
c(z^{k})=0
.
Thus,

\displaystyle S_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{i-1}}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n-1}a_{1}^{i}(z^{k_{1}})d(z^{k_{1}})x^{k_{1}}\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{n-1}a_{i}^{i}(z^{k_{i}})d(z^{k_{i}})x^{k_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{i-1}}\left(\sum_{c(z^{k_{1}})=0}a_{1}^{i}(z^{k_{1}})d(z^{k_{1}})x^{k_{1}}\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{c(z^{k_{i}})=0}a_{i}^{i}(z^{k_{i}})d(z^{k_{i}})x^{k_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{i-1}}\left(\sum_{c_{i}(z^{k})=0}\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}=k}a_{1}^{i}(z^{k_{1}})d(z^{k_{1}})\cdots a_{i}^{i}(z^{k_{i}})d(z^{k_{i}})x^{k}\right).

It follows that if
s_{i}(z^{k})\neq 0
, then
c_{i}(z^{k})=0
for some
0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
.
Equivalently, if
e_{i}(z^{k})=0
, then
s_{i}(z^{k})=0
.
This means
e_{i}(x)
divides
s_{i}(x)
and
s_{i}(x)\in\langle e_{i}(x)\rangle_{p}^{n}
.
Thus,
J_{i}\subseteq\langle e_{i}\rangle_{p}^{n}
.
∎

It’s clear that two polynomials over a field are coprime (over some extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) if and only if their roots are totally distinct.
Using this simple property, we give the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Keep the notations in
Proposition

4
and
Lemma

3
.
If
\overline{f}
is coprime with
\overline{g_{i}}
, then
I_{i}\subseteq\langle\overline{f}\rangle_{p}^{n}
.
Conversely, if there exists a polynomial
s_{i}\in I_{i}\subseteq\langle\overline{f}\rangle_{p}^{n}
such that
s_{i}(z^{j})\neq 0
for all

j\in K_{i}=\{k|\overline{g_{i}}(z^{k})=0,0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\}
, then
\overline{f}
is coprime with
\overline{g_{i}}
,
where
z
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity in some extension field of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and
\overline{g_{i}}
is the
i_{th}
circle product of
\overline{g}
,

.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4
and
[
19
, Theorem 4.13]
, the case
\overline{f}=1
or
\overline{g}=1
is trivial.
From now on, assume that
\overline{f}\neq 1
and
\overline{g_{i}}\neq 1
.
Let
e_{i}
be the polynomial such that
g_{i}e_{i}=\overline{g_{i}}\overline{e_{i}}=x^{n}-1
.

The first statement follows from
Lemma

3
.
So it’s sufficient to consider the last statement.

Obviously,
K_{i}\neq\emptyset
since
1\neq\overline{g}\mid x^{n}-1
.
Let
s_{i}(x)=\overline{\lambda_{i}}(x)\overline{f}(x)\in I_{i}\subseteq\langle\overline{f}\rangle_{p}^{n}
be the polynomial that satisfies the condition.
Using the Mattson-Solomon transform, we obtain

S_{i}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}s_{i}(z^{j})x^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\overline{\lambda_{i}}(z^{j})\overline{f}(z^{j})x^{j}.

Then for every

,
\overline{\lambda_{i}}(z^{k})\overline{f}(z^{k})\neq 0
.
It follows that
\overline{f}(z^{k})\neq 0
.
We deduce that
\overline{g_{i}}(z^{k})=0

\overline{f}(z^{k})\neq 0

Equivalently, if
\overline{f}(z^{k})=0

\overline{g_{i}}(z^{k})\neq 0

\overline{e_{i}}(z^{k})=0

Therefore,
\overline{f}
divides

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}=\langle fh+pf\rangle
be a linear cyclic code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
of length

fhg=x^{n}-1
and
\gcd(n,p)=1
.
Then
\Phi(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})
is linear if and only if
\overline{f}
is coprime with
\overline{g_{\ell}}
for all
\ell\in L
,
where
\overline{g_{\ell}}
is the
\ell_{th}
circle product of
\overline{g}
.

Proof.

The sufficiency follows from
Proposition

4
and
Lemma

4
.

Assume that
\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is linear, then
I_{\ell}\subseteq\langle\overline{f}\rangle_{p}^{n}
for every
\ell\in L
.
Let
s_{i}(x)=s_{i-1}(x)\ast x^{m_{i}}d(x)\in I_{i}
and
S_{i}(x)
be the Mattson-Solomon polynomials of
s_{i}
,
where
s_{i-1}(x)\in I_{i-1}
and
s_{1}(x)=d(x)
,
d=\overline{fh}
, and
0\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant n-1
.
Then for
i\geqslant 2
, by
[
20
, Ch.8.§6. Theorem 22]
, we have

S_{i}(x)=\sum_{k\in K_{i}}s_{i}(z^{k})x^{k}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{t\in K_{i-1}}s_{i-1}(z^{t})x^{t}\right)\left(\sum_{j\in K_{1}}z^{jm_{i}}d(z^{j})x^{j}\right),

where
z
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity in some extension field of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

We claim that there exists

such that
s_{i}(z^{k})\neq 0
for every

.

Use induction on
i
.
The case

holds trivially, since
s_{1}(z^{k})=z^{0}d(z^{k})\neq 0
for every

.
Then assume that

For every

.
Since

Then there exists an integer

.
Thus, the claim holds.

From the claim,
s_{\ell}\in I_{\ell}
is the polynomial such that for every

By

,
\overline{f}
is coprime with
\overline{g_{\ell}}
,
where
\ell\in L
.
∎

Remark 4
.

The Gray images of the codes are called
trivial
in which
f=1
, or
g=1
, or
g=x^{m}-1
for some
m\mid n
,
since the Gray images are always linear.
Assume that
f\neq 1
and let
\Gamma
be the set of roots of
\overline{g}
.
It’s known that
\overline{g_{i}}
is the polynomial whose roots are just in the set
\Gamma^{i}
for some integer
i
, where

\Gamma^{i}=\Gamma\cdots\Gamma=\{\xi_{1}\dots\xi_{i}|\xi_{j}\in\Gamma,1\leqslant j\leqslant i\}\subseteq U_{n}.

It follows that

if

and
b\in\Gamma_{j}
.
Therefore, the nontrivial linear Gray image exists if
\overline{f}\mid(1+x^{e}+x^{2e}+\dots+x^{n-e})
, where

is the order of
\overline{g}
and
e<n
.
Furthermore,
\overline{f}\mid(1+x^{e}+x^{2e}+\dots+x^{n-e})
if and only if
\overline{f}
is coprime with
\overline{g_{i}}
for every positive integer
i
.

In
Section

5
, for some special
n
, the linear Gray images of the cyclic codes obtained from

are all trivial.
Thus, it’s a difficult but interesting topic to find the nontrivial linear Gray image as optimal codes.

(0,c_{2})\in\langle(b^{\prime},fh)\rangle
.
By the above proof,
(0,c_{1})\in\mathcal{I}
.
Therefore,
S\subseteq\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{I}
is cyclic.
∎

Next we will give a necessary and sufficient condition for the Gray image to be linear.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle(a,0),(b,fh+pf)\rangle
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive cyclic code with
fhg=x^{\beta}-1
.
Then
\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is linear if and only if
\overline{f^{\prime}}=\overline{f}a/\gcd(a,\overline{b}g)
is coprime with
\overline{g_{\ell}}
for every
\ell\in L
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{u}=(u,u^{\prime})
,
\bm{w}=(w,w^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
.
Obviously, if
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
, then
pP(\bm{u},\bm{w})=\bm{0}\in\mathcal{C}
.
Note that by Lemma
6
and Lemma
7
,

\mathcal{C}=\langle(a,0),(b\overline{g},pfg)(b^{\prime},fh)\rangle
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}=\langle(a,0),(b\overline{g},pfg)(0,pfh)\rangle
,
where
\lambda h+\mu g=1
.
Then, we only need to consider the codewords that are not in
\mathcal{C}_{p}
and
(b^{\prime},fh)
will always be one generator of such codewords.
Since
pP(\bm{u},\bm{w})=(\bm{0},pP(u^{\prime},w^{\prime}))
, we define the set
S=\{\bm{u}=(\bm{0},u^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}\}

and the subcode
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\langle(b^{\prime},fh),S\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.
Comparing with (
1
) in
Remark

1
, the generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is (permutation-equivalent) in the following form:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc}\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&pT_{1}&pI_{\gamma-\kappa}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&S^{\prime}&S^{\prime\prime}&R&I_{\delta}\\
\end{array}\right).

(7)

For any codeword
\bm{u}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, by (
1
) in
Remark

1
, its order is
p
, then
pP(\bm{u},\bm{w})=(\bm{0},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
Thus, we only need to consider the codewords
\bm{u},\bm{w}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
Obviously,
pP(\bm{u},\bm{w})=(\bm{0},pP(u^{\prime},w^{\prime}))\in S\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.
Therefore,
\Phi(\mathcal{C})
is linear if and only if
pP(\bm{u},\bm{w})\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
for any
\bm{u},\bm{w}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
,
which means, for all
\bm{u},\bm{w}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
,
pP(\bm{u},\bm{w})\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\Phi(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
is linear.

By Lemma
8
,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is cyclic and

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\left\langle(b^{\prime},fh),\left(0,\frac{pfga}{\gcd(a,b\overline{g})}\right)\right\rangle.

Let
f^{\prime}=\frac{fa}{\gcd(a,b\overline{g})}
,
h^{\prime}=\frac{h\gcd(a,b\overline{g})}{a}
and
g^{\prime}=g
.
It’s clear that
f^{\prime}g^{\prime}h^{\prime}=x^{\beta}-1
with
f^{\prime}
,
g^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime}
coprime factors.
Thus, we have
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\langle(b^{\prime},f^{\prime}h^{\prime}),(0,pf^{\prime})\rangle
.
Obviously, the codewords of order
p
in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
belong to
S
, i.e., the left part is all zero vector.
Then,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{p}=\langle(0,pf^{\prime}h^{\prime}),(0,pf^{\prime})\rangle=\langle(0,pf^{\prime})\rangle
is separable since
\langle(0,pf^{\prime})\rangle=\{0\}\times\langle pf^{\prime}\rangle
.
By Lemma
5
,
\Phi(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
is linear if and only if
\Phi(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{Y})
is linear.
Moreover,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{Y}=\langle f^{\prime}h^{\prime},pf^{\prime}\rangle=\langle f^{\prime}h^{\prime}+pf^{\prime}\rangle
since
f^{\prime}h^{\prime}g^{\prime}=x^{\beta}-1
.
According to Theorem
3
,
\Phi(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{Y})=\Phi(\langle f^{\prime}h^{\prime}+pf^{\prime}\rangle)
is linear if and only if

\gcd(\overline{f^{\prime}},\overline{g^{\prime}_{\ell}})=1
for every
\ell\in L
.
∎

Let

,

Assume that

Theorem 5
.

Let
n
be odd prime and
\ell=(n-1)/2
be the multiplicative order of
p
modulo
n
, then the following statements hold:

(1)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, we have
x^{n}-1=(x-1)f_{1}f_{2}
,
where
\overline{f_{1}}
and
\overline{f_{2}}
are two irreducible polynomials of degree
\ell
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
;

(2)

\overline{f}_{i}\otimes\overline{f}_{i}=\frac{x^{n}-1}{x-1}
,
\overline{f}_{i}\otimes\overline{f}_{i}\otimes\overline{f}_{i}=x^{n}-1
and
\gcd(\overline{f_{i}},\overline{f_{j}}\otimes\overline{f_{j}})=\overline{f_{i}}
, where
i=1,2
;

(3)
the Gray image of the cyclic code
\langle fh+pf\rangle
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
is linear if and only if
f=1
or
g\in\{1,x-1\}
, where
fgh=(x-1)f_{1}f_{2}=x^{n}-1
.

Proof.

By
[
18
, Theorem 2.45 & Theorem 2.47]
, it’s known that
x^{n}-1=(x-1)\overline{f_{1}}\overline{f_{2}}
,
where
\overline{f_{1}}
and
\overline{f_{2}}
are two irreducible polynomials of degree
\ell
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Then, using Hensel’s lemma, we can get
x^{n}-1=(x-1)f_{1}f_{2}
and the first statement
(1)
holds.

As for
(2)
,
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
(actually,
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a field) can be partitioned into three parts,
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
and
C_{i}
for some
i

and the sizes of
C_{1}
and
C_{i}
are both
\ell
.
Obviously,
C_{1}=\{1,p,p^{2},\cdots,p^{\ell-1}\}
, since
\ell
is the multiplicative order of
p
modulo
n
.
Note that
p
is the square (
quadratic residue modulo
n
) of some element in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
Thus, all the elements in
C_{1}
are all the squares of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}
.
Equivalently,
C_{i}
is the set of all non-squares (
quadratic non-residue modulo
n
) in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}
.

Recall the
quadratic character

\eta
(see
[
18
, Example 5.10]
) on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}
and the
integer-valued function

\upsilon

(see
[
18
, Definition 6.22]
) of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, where

\eta(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x\ \textnormal{is a square},\\
-1,&x\ \textnormal{is a non-square},\\
\end{cases}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\upsilon(y)=\begin{cases}-1,&y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast},\\
n-1,&y=0.\\
\end{cases}

Let
N(a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}=x_{0})
be the number of solutions of the equation
a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}=x_{0}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{2}
, where
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}
.
Thus, for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, by
[
18
, Lemma 6.24]
, we have

N(a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}=x_{0})=n+\upsilon(x_{0})\eta(-a_{1}a_{2})\geqslant n-(n-1)=1>0.

Hence, every element in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}
is the sum of two squares, and also the sum of two non-squares.
Note that
N(a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}=0)=

Therefore,

C_{1}+C_{1}=C_{i}+C_{i}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast},

where the sum
A+B
of two sets
A
and
B
is the set
\{a+b|a\in A,b\in B\}
.
Therefore, the roots of
\overline{f}_{1}
are also the roots of
\overline{f}_{2}\otimes\overline{f}_{2}
, whose roots are
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\ast}
.

Moreover, it’s easy to check that

C_{1}+C_{1}+C_{1}=C_{i}+C_{i}+C_{i}=\mathbb{Z}_{n},

which implies that
\overline{f}_{i}\otimes\overline{f}_{i}\otimes\overline{f}_{i}=x^{n}-1
,
i=1,2
.

As for
(3)
, it’s easy to check that
\overline{g_{3}}=x^{n}-1
when
g\neq 1
and
g\neq x-1
.
Hence, the Gray image is linear if and only if
f=1
.
∎

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied the
p
-ary images (Gray images) of cyclic codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
.
The main contributions of this paper are the following:

(1)

We defined the polynomial
P(x_{1},x_{2})
, a characteristic function with lower degree than
f(x,y)
defined in
[
19
, Secction IV]
.
Note that the degrees of terms of
P(x_{1},x_{2})
plays an important role in this paper.

(2)

We gave the generator polynomials of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code (See
Theorem

1
), which is a generalization of
[
1
, Theorem 11]
.

(3)

We obtained the minimal spanning set of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-additive cyclic code (See Theorem
2
) and when
k=2
, we gave the relation between its type and generator polynomials (See
Proposition

2
).
And some optimal codes (See
Tables

2
and
1
) are found by different ways from the methods mentioned in
[
15
]
.

(4)

We presented a necessary and sufficient condition of the Gray image of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive cyclic code to be linear
(See Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
).
We determined some special
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-additive cyclic codes with
\gcd(\beta,p)=1
whose Gray images are linear
p
-ary codes.
Specially, if
\alpha=0
, they are the linear cyclic codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
whose Gray images are linear.
As for the linear cyclic Gray image, see
[
19
, Theorem 4.13]
.

(5)

We determined the linearity of the Gray images for two classes of cyclic codes (See
Example

5
and
Theorem

5
).

In fact, for some codes, the Gray image is linear if and only if
\overline{f}
and
\overline{g_{p}}
are coprime.
The readers interested in the Gray images can characterize such cyclic codes and give the condition.
For this work, cyclotomic cosets may be necessary.
Moreover, it’s also an interesting topic to find the condition for the Gray image of the case
p\mid n
or a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
-cyclic code to be linear.

Shapiro steps in charge-density-wave states driven by ultrasound
Michiyasu Mori, Sadamichi Maekawa

Abstract
We show that ultrasound can induce the Shapiro steps (SS) in the charge-density-wave (CDW) state. When ultrasound with frequency ω \omega and a dc voltage are applied, the SS occur at the current I I ∝ \propto n ​ ω n\omega with integer n n . Even and odd multiples of SS are represented by two couplings between the CDW and ultrasound. Although an ac voltage bias with frequency ω \omega induces the SS at I ∝ n ​ ω I\propto n\omega , the ultrasound bias enhances the odd multiples more strongly than the even ones. This is the difference between the ultrasound and the ac voltage. Since the SS cause abrupt peaks in the d ​ V / d ​ I dV/dI , the extreme changes in the I I - V V curve will be applied to a very sensitive ultrasound detector.

The single impurity model can account for several important aspects of CDW transport properties.
This is the limit of strong pinning.
When pinning is weak enough that a single impurity cannot pin the phase of the CDW, the pinning potential is determined by balancing the increase in kinetic energy owing to charge density change

The spatial distortions of the phase in the ground state caused by the random distribution of impurities are an important characteristic of impurity pinning. If

Since electrons move together with lattice distortion, the effective mass of CDW is large and the dynamics of phase motion can be treated classically.

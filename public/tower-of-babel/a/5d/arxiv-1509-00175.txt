Geometric Monodromy around the Tropical Limit
Yuto YAMAMOTO

Abstract
Let { V q } q \{V_{q}\}_{q} be a complex one-parameter family of smooth hypersurfaces in a toric variety. In this paper, we give a concrete description of the monodromy transformation of { V q } q \{V_{q}\}_{q} around q = ∞ q=\infty in terms of tropical geometry. The main tool is the tropical localization introduced by Mikhalkin.

Introduction

Let
K:={\mathbb{C}}\{t\}
be the convergent Laurent series field, equipped with the standard non-archimedean valuation,

\displaystyle\operatorname{val}\colon\ K\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{-\infty\},\qquad k=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{j}t^{j}\mapsto-\min\{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,|\,c_{j}\neq 0\}.

(1.1)

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a natural number and
M
be a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of rank
n+1
. We write
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let further
\Delta\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex lattice polytope, i.e., the convex hull of a finite subset of
M
. We set
A:=\Delta\cap M
.
Let
F=\sum\limits_{m\in A}k_{m}x^{m}\in K\big[x^{\pm}_{1},\dots,x^{\pm}_{n+1}\big]
be a Laurent polynomial over
K
in
n+1
variables such that
k_{m}\neq 0
for all
m\in A
. We fix a sufficiently large
R\in{\mathbb{R}}^{>0}
such that
1/R
is smaller than the radius of convergence of
k_{m}
for all
m\in A
, and set
S_{R}^{1}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,|z|=R\}
. For
q\in S_{R}^{1}
, let
f_{q}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x^{\pm}_{1},\dots,x^{\pm}_{n+1}]
denote the polynomial obtained by substituting
1/q
to
t
in
F
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the normal fan to
\Delta
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
be a unimodular subdivision of
{\mathcal{F}}
. Let
X_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{C}})
denote the toric manifold over
{\mathbb{C}}
associated with
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
. For each
q\in S_{R}^{1}
, we define
V_{q}\subset X_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{C}})
as the hypersurface defined by
f_{q}
in
X_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{C}})
. In this paper, we discuss the monodromy transformation of
\{V_{q}\}_{q\in S_{R}^{1}}
around
q=\infty
. The limit
q\to\infty
is called the tropical limit in this paper. The motivation to address this problem comes from the calculation of monodromies of period maps.

Let
\operatorname{trop}(F)\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the tropicalization of
F
defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{trop}(F)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n+1}):=\max_{m\in A}\big\{{\operatorname{val}}(k_{m})+m_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+m_{n+1}X_{n+1}\big\}.

(1.2)

The non-differentiable locus of
\operatorname{trop}(F)
is called the tropical hypersurface defined by
\operatorname{trop}(F)

and denoted by
V(\operatorname{trop}(F))
. The tropical hypersurface
V(\operatorname{trop}(F))
is a rational polyhedral complex of dimension
n
.
The main theorem of this paper is Theorem
4.5
, which gives a concrete description of the monodromy transformation of
\{V_{q}\}_{q\in S_{R}^{1}}
in terms of the tropical hypersurface
V(\operatorname{trop}(F))
in the case where
V(\operatorname{trop}(F))
is smooth (see Definition
2.7
). The monodromy of
\{V_{q}\}_{q\in S_{R}^{1}}
is also discussed in
[
2
, Appendix B.2]
and Theorem
4.5
is covered by
[
2
, Proposition B.17]
. However, this paper aims to make the relation of the monodromy of
\{V_{q}\}_{q\in S_{R}^{1}}
to tropical geometry clear. We give a self-contained proof and explicit examples.

When
\Delta
is smooth and reflexive and the polynomial
F
gives a central subdivision of
\Delta
, Zharkov
[
10
]
also gave a concrete description of the monodromy transformation of
\{V_{q}\}_{q\in S_{R}^{1}}
. The idea of his description is the same as that of ours. By treating his construction systematically, we generalize his result to the case where
\Delta
is any polytope and the subdivision of
\Delta
given by
F
is not necessarily central.

is technical and it is necessary to make preparations in order to state it, we do not state it here and discuss its corollary in the following. Assume

If

2.1
Tropical toric varieties

Let
M
be a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of rank
n+1
and
N:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
be the dual lattice of
M
.
We set
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. We have a canonical
{\mathbb{R}}
-bilinear pairing

\displaystyle\langle-,-\rangle\colon\ M_{\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. We write the toric variety associated with
{\mathcal{F}}
over
{\mathbb{C}}
as
X_{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{C}})
.
For each cone
\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}
, we set

\displaystyle\sigma^{\vee}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,\langle m,n\rangle\geq 0\mathrm{\ for\ all\ }n\in\sigma\},

\displaystyle\sigma^{\perp}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,\langle m,n\rangle=0\mathrm{\ for\ all\ }n\in\sigma\}.

Let
U_{\sigma}({\mathbb{C}}):=\operatorname{Hom}(\sigma^{\vee}\cap M,{\mathbb{C}})
denote the affine toric variety and
O_{\sigma}({\mathbb{C}}):=\operatorname{Hom}(\sigma^{\perp}\cap M,{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})
denote the torus orbit corresponding to
\sigma
. We write the closure of
O_{\sigma}({\mathbb{C}})
in
X_{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{C}})
as
X_{{\mathcal{F}},\sigma}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}:={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
be the tropical semi-ring, equipped with the following arithmetic operations for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{T}}
;

\displaystyle a\oplus b:=\max\{a,b\},\qquad a\odot b:=a+b.

We can also define the toric variety over
{\mathbb{T}}
as follows. For each cone
\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}
, we define
U_{\sigma}({\mathbb{T}})
as the set of monoid homomorphisms
\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\to({\mathbb{T}},\odot)
,

\displaystyle U_{\sigma}({\mathbb{T}}):=\operatorname{Hom}\big(\sigma^{\vee}\cap M,{\mathbb{T}}\big)

with the compact open topology. For cones
\sigma,\tau\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\sigma\prec\tau
, we have a natural immersion,

\displaystyle U_{\sigma}({\mathbb{T}})\to U_{\tau}({\mathbb{T}}),\qquad\big(v\colon\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\to{\mathbb{T}}\big)\mapsto\big(\tau^{\vee}\cap M\subset\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\xrightarrow{v}{\mathbb{T}}\big),

where
\sigma\prec\tau
means that
\sigma
is a face of
\tau
. By gluing
\{U_{\sigma}({\mathbb{T}})\}_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}}
with each other, we have the tropical toric variety
X_{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{T}})
associated with
{\mathcal{F}}
,

\displaystyle X_{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{T}}):=\bigg(\coprod_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}}U_{\sigma}({\mathbb{T}})\bigg)\bigg/{\sim}.

Tropical toric varieties are first introduced by Kajiwara
[
5
]
, see
[
5
]
or
[
6
]
for details. For a projective toric variety, the associated tropical toric variety is homeomorphic to the moment polytope of it
[
6
, Remark 1.3]
.

Polyhedral complex

We define the product

\displaystyle r\cdot t:=\begin{cases}r\times t,&t\neq-\infty,\\

\displaystyle a\cdot b:=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}\cdot b_{i},

for

and

n+1\}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{T}}_{I}^{n+1}:=\big\{X\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}\,|\,X_{i}=-\infty\ \mathrm{for\ any\ }i\in I\big\}.

.

A subset
\rho
of

is a

if there exist a finite collection
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of half-spaces of the form

\displaystyle H_{j}=\big\{X\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}\,|\,c_{j}\cdot X\leq d_{j}\big\},\qquad c_{j}\in\big({\mathbb{R}}^{\geq 0}\big)^{n+1},\qquad d_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}},

and a subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,n+1\}
such that

\displaystyle\rho=\cap_{j\in J}H_{j}\cap{\mathbb{T}}_{I}^{n+1}.

A subset
\mu
of
\rho
is a
face
of
\rho
if there exist subsets

and
I^{\prime}\subset\{1,\dots,n+1\}
such that

and

\displaystyle\mu=\big\{X\in\rho\,|\,c_{j}\cdot X=d_{j}\ {\rm for\ all}\ j\in J^{\prime},\ X_{i}=-\infty\ {\rm for\ all}\ i\in I^{\prime}\big\}.

We write
\mu\prec\rho
when
\mu
is a face of
\rho
.

.

A subset
\rho

.

A finite set

•

Embedding tensors on 3-Hom-Lie algebras
Wen Teng, Jiulin Jin, Yu Zhang 1. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Guizhou University of Finance and EconomicsGuiyang 550025, P. R. of ChinaEmail: tengwen@mail.gufe.edu.cn(Wen Teng)2. College of Mathematics and Information Science, Guiyang UniversityGuizhou 550005 550005 , P. R. of China

Abstract




Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the notion of embedding tensor on 3-Hom-Lie algebras and naturally induce 3-Hom-Leibniz algebras. Moreover, the cohomology theory
of embedding tensors on 3-Hom-Lie algebras is defined. As an application, we show that if two linear deformations of an embedding tensor on a 3-Hom-Lie algebra are equivalent, then their infinitesimals belong to
the same cohomology class in the first cohomology group.

Key words:
3-Hom-Lie algebra;embedding tensor; representation; cohomology; deformation.

Introduction

The concept of embedded tensors provides a useful tool in the construction of supergravity theories
[
17
]
and higher gauge theories
[
5
]
.
In mathematics, embedding tensor is called average operator. Aguiar
[
1
]
studies the average operator on the associative algebra and Lie algebra.
Further, deformation and cohomology theory of embedding tensors of associative algebra, Lie algebra and 3-Lie algebra were given in
[
8
,
18
,
12
]
. Recently, Das and Makhlouf
[
7
]
introduced the embedding tensor on Hom-Lie algebra, and studied the related properties.

The aim of this paper is to extend the concept of embedded tensors of 3-Lie algebras to Hom-type algebras.
Hom-Lie algebras were introduced by Hartwig, Larsson and Silvestrov
[
11
]

in the study of q-deformations of the Witt and Virasoro algebra.
In the last fifteen years, Hom-type algebras have attracted extensive attention from scholars (see
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
15
,
14
,
13
,
16
,
19
]
). On the other hand, Filippov
[
10
]
introduced 3-Lie algebra and more general
n
-Lie algebra, which can be regarded as a generalization of Lie algebra to higher algebra. In
[
3
]
,
n
-Hom-Lie algebras and various generalizations of n-ary algebras are considered, and the representation and cohomology of
n
-Hom-Lie algebras are first studied in
[
2
]
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we recall the definitions of 3-Hom-Lie algebras. Then we introduce the notion of an embedding tensor on a 3-Hom-Lie algebra, which naturally induces a 3-Hom-Leibniz algebra.
In Section 3, we introduce the representation and cohomology theory of embedding tensor on a 3-Hom-Lie algebra.
In Section 4, we study linear deformations of embedding tensor on a 3-Hom-Lie algebra, we show that if two linear deformations of an embedding tensor on a 3-Hom Lie algebra are equivalent, then their infinitesimals belong
to
the same cohomology class in the first cohomology group.

In this paper, all vector spaces are considered over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic 0.

2
Embedding tensors on 3-Hom-Lie algebras

In this section, we recall some basic definitions of 3-Hom-Lie algebras, Hom-Leibniz algebras and 3-Hom-Leibniz algebras. Then we introduce embedding tensors on 3-Hom-Lie algebras. We show that an embedding
tensor naturally gives rise to a 3-Hom-Leibniz algebra structure. Finally, we provide some examples of embedding tensors on
3-Hom-Lie algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
3
]
) A 3-Hom-Lie algebra is a triple
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
consisting of a vector space
L
, a trilinear
skew-symmetric mapping
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:L\times L\times L\rightarrow L
, and a linear map
\alpha:L\rightarrow L
satisfying
\alpha([x,y,z])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y),\alpha(z)]
and the Hom-Filippov-Jacobi
identity:

\displaystyle[\alpha(a),\alpha(b),[x,y,z]]=[[a,b,x],\alpha(y),\alpha(z)]+[\alpha(x),[a,b,y],\alpha(z)]+[\alpha(x),\alpha(y),[a,b,z]],

(2.1)

for any
a,b,x,y,z\in L
.
Furthermore, if
\alpha:L\rightarrow L
is a vector space automorphism of
L
, then the 3-Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
is called a regular 3-Hom-Lie algebra.

A homomorphism between two 3-Hom-Lie algebras
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
and
(L^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
is a linear map
\psi:L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
satisfying
\psi\circ\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\psi
and

\displaystyle\psi([x,y,z])=[\psi(x),\psi(y),\psi(z)]^{\prime},~~\forall~x,y,z\in L.

In particular, if
\psi
is nondegenerate, then
\psi
is called an isomorphism from
L
to
L^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
14
]
)
A Hom-Leibniz algebra is a vector space
L
together with a
bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot]:L\times L\rightarrow L
and a linear map
\alpha:L\rightarrow L
satisfying
\alpha([x,y])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]
such that

\displaystyle[\alpha(x),[y,z]]=[[x,y],\alpha(z)]+[\alpha(y),[x,y]],

for any
x,y,z\in L
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
be a 3-Hom-Lie algebra, then the elements in
\wedge^{2}L
are called fundamental objects of the 3-Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
. If there is a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
\wedge^{2}L
, which is given by

\displaystyle[X,Y]^{\prime}=[x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}]\wedge\alpha(y_{2})+\alpha(y_{1})\wedge[x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}],\forall X=x_{1}\wedge x_{2},Y=y_{1}\wedge y_{2}\in\wedge^{2}L

and a linear map
\tilde{\alpha}
on
\wedge^{2}L
is defined by
\tilde{\alpha}(X)=\alpha(x_{1})\wedge\alpha(x_{2})
.
Clearly,
(\wedge^{2}L,[\cdot,\cdot]^{\prime},\tilde{\alpha})
is a Hom-Leibniz algebra
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
13
]
)
A representation of a 3-Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
on a vector space
V
with respect
to
\beta\in\mathrm{End}(V)
is a skew-symmetric linear map
\rho:\wedge^{2}L\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
such that

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))\circ\beta=\beta\circ\rho(x,y),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))\rho(a,b)-\rho(\alpha(a),\alpha(b))\rho(x,y)=(\rho([x,y,a],\alpha(b))-\rho([x,y,b],\alpha(a)))\circ\beta,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\rho([x,y,a],\alpha(b))\circ\beta-\rho(\alpha(y),\alpha(a))\rho(x,b)=\rho(\alpha(a),\alpha(x))\rho(y,b)+\rho(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))\rho(a,b),

(2.4)

for any
x,y,a,b\in L
.
Furthermore, if
\beta:V\rightarrow V
is a vector space automorphism of
V
, then
(V;\rho,\beta)
is called a regular representation of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
.

It follows from the above definition that any 3-Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
can be regarded as a representation
of itself, where
\rho=\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{2}L\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(L)
is given by
\mathrm{ad}(x,y)(z):=[x,y,z]
, for
x,y,z\in L
. This is called the adjoint
representation.

(2.5)

for any
a,b,x,y,z\in L

Proposition 2.5

\displaystyle[\alpha(x)+\beta(u),\alpha(y)+\beta(v),\alpha(z)+\beta(w)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle[(\alpha\oplus\beta)(x+u),(\alpha\oplus\beta)(y+v),(\alpha\oplus\beta)(z+w)]_{\rho},

\displaystyle[[a+s,b+t,x+u]_{\rho},(\alpha\oplus\beta)(y+v),(\alpha\oplus\beta)(z+w)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle+[(\alpha\oplus\beta)(x+u),[a+s,b+t,y+v]_{\rho},(\alpha\oplus\beta)(z+w)]_{\rho}

3
The cohomology of embedding tensors on 3-Hom-Lie algebras

In this section, we recall some basic results of representations and cohomologies of
3-Hom-Leibniz algebras. We construct a representation of the 3-Hom-Leibniz algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\beta)

on the vector space
L
, and define the cohomologies of an embedding tensor on 3-Hom-Lie algebras.

Definition 3.1
.

A representation of the 3-Hom-Leibniz algebra
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{L}},\alpha)
is a pair
(V;\beta)
of
vector space
V
and a linear map
\beta:V\rightarrow V
, equipped with 3 actions

\displaystyle l:\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes V\rightarrow V,

\displaystyle m:\mathcal{L}\otimes V\otimes\mathcal{L}\rightarrow V,

\displaystyle r:V\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}\rightarrow V,

satisfying for any
x,y,z,a,b\in\mathcal{L}
and
u\in V

\displaystyle l(\alpha(x),\alpha(y),\beta(u))

\displaystyle=\beta(l(x,y,u)),

\displaystyle m(\alpha(x),\beta(u),\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=\beta(m(x,u,z)),

\displaystyle r(\beta(u),\alpha(y),\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=\beta(r(u,y,z))

and

\displaystyle l([a,b,x]_{\mathcal{L}},\alpha(y),\beta(u))+l(\alpha(x),[a,b,y]_{\mathcal{L}},\beta(u))+l(\alpha(x),\alpha(y),l(a,b,u))

\displaystyle=l(\alpha(a),\alpha(b),l(x,y,u)),

(3.1)

\displaystyle m([a,b,x]_{\mathcal{L}},\beta(u),\alpha(z))+m(\alpha(x),l(a,b,u),\alpha(z))+m(\alpha(x),\beta(u),[a,b,z]_{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=l(\alpha(a),\alpha(b),m(x,u,z)),

(3.2)

\displaystyle r(l(a,b,u),\alpha(y),\alpha(z))+r(\beta(u),[a,b,y]_{\mathcal{L}},\alpha(z))+r(\beta(u),\alpha(y),[a,b,z]_{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=l(\alpha(a),\alpha(b),r(u,y,z)),

(3.3)

\displaystyle r(m(a,u,x),\alpha(y),\alpha(z))+m(\alpha(x),m(a,u,y),\alpha(z))+l(\alpha(x),\alpha(y),m(a,u,z))

\displaystyle=m(\alpha(a),\beta(u),[x,y,z]_{\mathcal{L}}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle r(r(u,b,x),\alpha(y),\alpha(z))+m(\alpha(x),r(u,b,y),\alpha(z))+l(\alpha(x),\alpha(y),r(u,b,z))

\displaystyle=r(\beta(u),\alpha(b),[x,y,z]_{\mathcal{L}}).

(3.5)

An
n
-cochain on a 3-Hom-Leibniz algebra
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{L}},\alpha)
with coefficients in a representation
(V;l,m,r,\beta)

is a linear map

\displaystyle f:\overbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathcal{L}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathcal{L}}^{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{L}\rightarrow V,n\geq 1

such that
\beta\circ f=f\circ(\tilde{\alpha}^{\otimes n-1}\otimes\alpha)
.
The space generated by
n
-cochains is denoted as
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{\mathrm{3HLei}}(\mathcal{L},V)
. The coboundary map
\delta

4
Deformations of embedding tensors on 3-Hom-Lie algebras

In this section, we discuss linear deformations of embedding tensors on 3-Hom-Lie algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T:V\rightarrow L
be an embedding tensor on the 3-Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
with
respect to the representation
(V;\rho,\beta)
, and let
\mathfrak{I}:V\rightarrow L
be a linear map. If
T_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{I}
is a

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
with
respect to the representation
(V;\rho,\beta)
for all
t
,
we say that
\mathfrak{I}
generates a linear deformation of the embedding tensor
T
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{I}
generates a linear deformation of the embedding tensor
T
, then we
have

\displaystyle T_{t}\circ\beta=

\displaystyle\alpha\circ T_{t},

\displaystyle[T_{t}u,T_{t}v,T_{t}w]=

\displaystyle T_{t}\rho(T_{t}u,T_{t}v)w,

for all
u,v,w\in V.
This is equivalent to the following conditions

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}\circ\beta=\alpha\circ\mathfrak{I},

(4.1)

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,\mathfrak{I}w]+[Tu,\mathfrak{I}v,Tw]+[\mathfrak{I}u,Tv,Tw]=\mathfrak{I}\rho(Tu,Tv)w+T\rho(Tu,\mathfrak{I}v)w+T\rho(\mathfrak{I}u,Tv)w,

(4.2)

\displaystyle[Tu,\mathfrak{I}v,\mathfrak{I}w]+[\mathfrak{I}u,Tv,\mathfrak{I}w]+[\mathfrak{I}u,\mathfrak{I}v,Tw]=\mathfrak{I}\rho(Tu,\mathfrak{I}v)w+\mathfrak{I}\rho(\mathfrak{I}u,Tv)w+T\rho(\mathfrak{I}u,\mathfrak{I}v)w,

(4.3)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{I}u,\mathfrak{I}v,\mathfrak{I}w]=\mathfrak{I}\rho(\mathfrak{I}u,\mathfrak{I}v)w,

(4.4)

for all
u,v,w\in V.
Thus,
T_{t}

is a linear deformation of
T
if and only if Eqs. (
4.1

) hold. From Eqs. (
4.1
) and (

map
\mathfrak{I}
is an embedding tensor on the 3-Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
with
respect to the representation
(V;\rho,\beta)
.

Spatially sparse optimization problems in fractional order Sobolev spaces
Anna Lentz a , Daniel Wachsmuth a

Abstract
We investigate time-dependent optimization problems in fractional Sobolev spaces with the sparsity promoting L p L^{p} -pseudo-norm for 0 < p < 1 0<p<1 in the objective functional. In order to avoid computing the fractional Laplacian on the time-space cylinder I × Ω I\times\Omega , we introduce an auxiliary function w ∈ H s ​ ( Ω ) w\in H^{s}(\Omega) that is an upper bound for the function u ∈ L 2 ​ ( I × Ω ) u\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega) . We prove existence and regularity results and derive a necessary optimality condition. This is done by smoothing the L p L^{p} -pseudo-norm and by penalizing the inequality constraint regarding u u and w w . The problem is solved numerically with an iterative scheme whose weak limit points satisfy a weaker form of the necessary optimality condition.

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to investigate time-dependent optimization or optimal control problems
that admit spatially sparse solutions.
Let us describe informally the notion of spatial sparsity.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain and
T>0
a terminal time, and set
I:=(0,T)
.
Given a function
u:I\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
we say that
u
is spatially sparse if the measure of the set
\{x\in\Omega:\ u(\cdot,x)\neq 0\}
is small.

In this paper, we will analyse the following optimization problem:

\underset{u\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega),w\in H^{s}(\Omega)}{\min}f(u)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|w\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}+\gamma\|w\|_{p}^{p}

(1)

subject to the constraints

|u(t,x)|\leq w(x)\quad\textrm{f.a.a.}\,\,(t,x)\in I\times\Omega.

(2)

In this problem,
u
is the unknown control that should be spatially sparse, while
w
is an auxiliary variable.
Let us first explain the various parts of the optimization problem in more detail:

•

f:L^{2}(I\times\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is the objective functional to be minimized,

•

the term
\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}
with
\alpha>0

models the control cost of
u
,

•

the
L^{p}
-pseudo-norm
\gamma\|w\|_{p}^{p}:=\gamma\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
with
p\in(0,1)
and
\gamma>0
promotes sparsity of
w
,

•

the term
\frac{\beta}{2}\|w\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}
with
s\in(0,1)
and
\beta>0
is needed to prove existence of solutions,

•

the constraint (
2
) couples
u
and
w
: if
w
is sparse, i.e. the measure of
\{x\in\Omega:\ w(x)\neq 0\}
is small, then
u
is spatially sparse.

The main results of this paper regarding (
1
)–(
2
) are: existence of solutions, first-order necessary optimality conditions,
and a solution algorithm. The optimality conditions and the algorithm are both based on the same smoothing procedure.

To motivate the form of the functional in (
1
) let us first consider the problem with
\beta=0
. Then we can eliminate
w
by setting

w(x):=\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
, and we obtain the problem

\underset{u\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}{\min}f(u)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}+\gamma\int_{\Omega}\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

(3)

For suitable functions
u
one can prove that for
p\searrow 0
we have the convergence

\int_{\Omega}\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\Omega}\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\left|\{x\in\Omega:\ u(\cdot,x)\neq 0\}\right|

(4)

with the convention
0^{0}:=0
,
where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure.
Note that in (
4
) we can replace the
L^{\infty}(I)
-norm by any
L^{q}(I)
-norm with
q\in[1,+\infty)
.
Hence, we expect solutions of (
3
) or (
1
)–(
2
) to be spatially sparse for
0<p\ll 1
.
The choice
p<1
leads to a functional that is not weakly lower semicontinuous in
L^{q}
-spaces, so that standard existence proofs do not work.

In order to guarantee existence of solutions, one needs to set the problem (
3
) in a function space that compactly embeds into
L^{2}(I\times\Omega)
.
Here, we will use fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
with
0<s\ll 1
.
Then a regularized version of (
3
) reads

\underset{u\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}{\min}f(u)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}+\gamma\int_{\Omega}\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\beta\|u\|_{H^{s}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}.

(5)

Due to compact embeddings of
H^{s}(I\times\Omega)
into
L^{2}(I\times\Omega)
, one can prove existence of solutions of this problem.
To solve the resulting problem numerically, we want to use the majorization-minimization approach from
[
lp_cont
,
sparse
]
.
When applied to (
5
), one would have to solve equations of the type

(-\Delta)^{s}u+au=f
in each iteration, where
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional Laplace on the time-space domain
I\times\Omega
and
a\in L^{\infty}(I\times\Omega)

is a non-constant function that changes during the iterations. Surprisingly, no efficient solution methods are known for these type of equations.
Spectral or Fourier based approaches are only viable if
a\in\mathbb{R}
, since in this case the standard Laplacian
-\Delta
and the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}+a
have the same system of eigenfunctions.
A direct finite-element discretization with mesh-size
h
would lead to a linear system with
h^{-(d+1)}
unknowns and full system matrix,
whose solution would be prohibitively expensive already for
d=2
.
The Caffarelli-Silvestre extension replaces the fractional Laplace equation by a degenerate elliptic problem in
I\times\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+2}
, see
[
StingaTorrea2010
]
,
which again is challenging to solve for
d\geq 2
.

To overcome this difficulty, we introduce the auxiliary variable
w\in H^{s}(\Omega)
. In the resulting optimization problem the sparsity promoting functional is applied to
w
,
and the condition
|u(t,x)|\leq w(x)
enforces spatial sparsity of
u
. Still we need to solve equations involving the fractional Laplacian on a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
instead
on a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
For
s<1/2
the space
H^{s}(\Omega)
contains characteristic functions, so we later work with this choice of
s
.
These considerations lead us to the optimization (
1
)–(
2
) introduced at the beginning.

Let us provide a review of relevant literature.
Actuator placement problems are studied for instance in
[
Munch2008
,
KaliseKunischSturm2018
]
,
where the
L^{0}
-pseudo-norm appears in the functional, and topological gradient methods are used to solve the problem numerically.
Problems with
p\in[0,1)
are studied for instance in
[
Wachsmuth2019
,
sparse
,
lp_cont
]
.
A simple example without solution is presented in
[
Wachsmuth2019
]
.
The regularization in
H^{1}
was proposed in
[
lp_cont
]
, while fractional Sobolev spaces are used in
[
sparse
]
.
For
p\in(0,1)
, majorization-minimization methods are available that are based on approximations of the concave function
s\mapsto s^{p/2}
.
This approach was used in
[
lp_cont
,
sparse
]
, and we will use these ideas as well.
The present paper is a direct extension of
[
sparse
]
to the time-dependent case. Due to the presence of the inequality constraints (
2
)
the analysis is considerably more technical.

The choice
p=1
is very popular in the literature, since then tools from convex analysis can be applied.
Optimal control problems that penalize the
L^{1}
-norm of the controls in the cost functionals were analysed, e.g., in
[
180NavierStokesOCExample
,
181SchloeglExample
,
85l1Sparse
]
.
Problems with spatially sparse controls were considered in
[
81spatTempSparse
,
80dirSparse
]
.
As explained above, we concentrate on the case
p<1
in this paper to stay close to the
L^{0}
-pseudo norm.
For the numerical experiments, we used standard finite elements. More advanced concepts are available to solve the equations of the type
(-\Delta)^{s}u=f
numerically.
We refer only to the review article
[
Hofreither2020
]
. However, these methods do not generalize easily to equations of the type
(-\Delta)^{s}u+au=f
with non-constant
a
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. First, we give a brief overview of fractional Sobolev spaces in Section
2
. Then we prove existence of solutions of the minimization problem in Section
3
and derive an auxiliary problem with a smoothing and penalization scheme in the subsequent Section
4.1
. This auxiliary problem is used in Section
4.2
to obtain an optimality condition for the minimization problem (
1
). In Section
5
we consider an iterative scheme to solve this minimization problem. We conclude by giving some numerical examples in Section
6
.

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes a bounded Lipschitz domain and
I=(0,T)
some time interval with
T>0
. Furthermore, we use the abbreviation

U:=L^{2}(I\times\Omega).

The space
W
will be defined below as a suitable fractional Sobolev space on
\Omega
. We denote by

K:=\{(u,w)\in U\times W:\ |u(t,x)|\leq w(x)\textrm{ for a.a. }(t,x)\in I\times\Omega\}

(6)

the set of admissible functions. Moreover, we use the notation
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
for the inner product of a Hilbert space
V
and
\braket{\cdot,\cdot}_{V}
for its duality product. We use the notation

u_{-}:=\min(u,0)\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{+}:=\max(u,0)

u
.

w

,

w
from

to

for (almost all)
(t,x)\in I\times\Omega
.

2
Fractional Sobolev spaces

In this section, we state some definitions and results about fractional order Sobolev spaces.
There are several possibilities to define a fractional Sobolev space or a fractional Laplace operator on a bounded domain.
For most cases, they lead to to the same spaces with equivalent norms, see (
8
) below.
As the solutions of optimization problems as (
1
) depend on the concrete choice of the norm,
we will discuss several of them.
Important for our analysis are two facts: the compact embedding into
L^{2}(\Omega)
and a certain monotonicity of the inner product of the fractional Sobolev space with respect to
truncated functions, see Lemma
2.3
and
2.4
below.
These are satisfied for the standard choices of fractional Sobolev spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
.
Then the fractional order Sobolev space
H^{s}(\Omega)

is defined as

H^{s}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ (x,y)\mapsto\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)\right\}

with the corresponding norm

\|w\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and inner product

(u,w)_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}uw\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(w(x)-w(y)))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
c_{d,s}:=\frac{s2^{2s}\Gamma(s+\frac{d}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)}.

With this norm, we define the space
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
as

H_{0}^{s}(\Omega):=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{H^{s}(\Omega)},

where
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is the space of infinitely continuously differentiable functions with compact support in
\Omega
.
Moreover, let

\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):w|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega}=0\right\}=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where the latter identity was shown in
[
7densityFrac
]
.
Hence, functions in
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
can be extended by zero to
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
.
The space
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is supplied with the
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-inner product.
For
s=\frac{1}{2}
, the Lions-Magenes space is defined as

H_{00}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}(x)}{\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\}

with the norm

\|w\|_{H_{00}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)}:=\left(\|w\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}(x)}{\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For this space it holds
H_{00}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)\subsetneq H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)
.

Another way to define fractional Sobolev spaces is via spectral theory using eigenvectors and eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator with zero Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
s\geq 0
.
Then the fractional order Sobolev space in spectral form is defined as

\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega):=\left\{w=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w_{n}\phi_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega):\|w\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}^{2}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{s}w_{n}^{2}<\infty\right\},

where
w_{n}:=\int_{\Omega}w\phi_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
, and
\phi_{n}
are the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian with zero Dirichlet boundary conditions to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
.

One can rewrite the norm of
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)
in an integral formulation as done in
[
nonhomBC
, page 1, Appendix A.1]
,
[
anote
, page 5-6]
, namely

\|w\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}|(w(x)-w(y))|^{2}J(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)|w(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

(7)

with inner product

(u,w)_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}(u(x)-u(y))(w(x)-w(y))J(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)uw\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
J
and
\kappa
are measurable non-negative functions, and
J
is symmetric with
J(x,y)=J(y,x)
for a.a.
x,y\in\Omega
.
This formulation of the
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)
-inner product as a double integral will be used in the proof of Lemma
2.4
.

It was shown in
[
105ellipticProb
, Corollary 1.4.4.5]
and
[
lions
, Chapter 1]
or
[
185fracEllipticEq
, page 12]
that
we have the equalities

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}H^{s}(\Omega)=H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)=\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)&\textrm{ if }0<s<\frac{1}{2},\\
H_{00}^{1/2}(\Omega)&\textrm{ if }s=\frac{1}{2},\\
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)=\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)&\textrm{ if }\frac{1}{2}<s<1,\end{cases}

(8)

with equivalence of norms.
From now on, the space
W
refers to a fractional Sobolev space that is covered by the previous result, i.e.,

W\in\{\ H^{s}(\Omega),\ H_{0}^{s}(\Omega),\ \tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega),\ \mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)\ \}

(9)

for some
s\in(0,1)
.
We will work with the space
W
in the remainder of this paper. While all the spaces in (
9
)
share the same analytical properties, the solutions of optimization problems depend on the concrete choice of the space.

The following properties of these fractional Sobolev spaces will be used later on.

Lemma 2.3
.

(i)

The embedding
W\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact.

(ii)

Let
w\in W
and let
z(x):=\max(-1,\min(w(x),1))
. Then
z\in W
and
(w,z)_{W}\geq\|z\|_{W}^{2}
.

(iii)

C_{0}(\Omega)\cap W
is dense in
W
and
C_{0}(\Omega)
.

(iv)

There is
q>2
such that
W
is continuously embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
. More precisely,

\displaystyle W\hookrightarrow\begin{cases}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2s}}(\Omega)&\textrm{if }d>2s,\\
L^{p}(\Omega)\quad\forall p\in[1,\infty)&\textrm{if }d=2s,\\
C^{0,s-\frac{d}{2}}(\bar{\Omega})&\textrm{if }d<2s.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is shown in
[
sparse
, Section 6]
and
[
hitch
, Sections 6, 7]
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
W
as in (
9
) and let
w\in W
. Then
(w,w_{+})_{W}\leq\|w\|_{W}^{2}
.

Proof.

One can directly see that the inequality holds for the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-part in the
W
-norm, i.e.,
(w,w_{+})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
.
Let
w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. Then it holds

(w_{+}(x)-w_{+}(y))\cdot(w(x)-w(y))\leq(w(x)-w(y))^{2}

(10)

for a.a.
x,y\in\Omega
. This can be seen by considering the following cases:

1.

If
w(x)\geq 0
and
w(y)\geq 0
, it holds
(w_{+}(x)-w_{+}(y))\cdot(w(x)-w(y))=(w(x)-w(y))^{2}.

2.

For
w(x)\leq 0
and
w(y)\leq 0
we get
(w_{+}(x)-w_{+}(y))\cdot(w(x)-w(y))=0\leq(w(x)-w(y))^{2}.

3.

If
w(x)\geq 0
and
w(y)\leq 0
, it holds
(w_{+}(x)-w_{+}(y))\cdot(w(x)-w(y))=(w_{+}(x))\cdot(w(x)-w(y))\leq(w(x)-w(y))^{2}.

4.

Similarly, for
w(x)\leq 0
and
w(y)\geq 0
we obtain
(w_{+}(x)-w_{+}(y))\cdot(w(x)-w(y))=(-w_{+}(y))\cdot(w(x)-w(y))\leq(w(x)-w(y))^{2}.

Analogously, we can prove (
10
) for all
w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Multiplying the inequality (
10
) with the kernels and integrating on
\Omega\times\Omega
or
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
proves
the corresponding inequality for the double integrals.
By definition of the inner product in
W
this yields the result, where for the spectral fractional Sobolev space
W=\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)
,
we used the integral formulation (
7
).
∎

3
Existence of Solutions

We now want to prove existence of solutions of the above introduced minimization problem

\displaystyle\underset{(u,w)\in K}{\min}f(u)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|w\|_{W}^{2}+\gamma\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Let us recall the definition of
U=L^{2}(I\times\Omega)
and
W
in (
9
).
Note that the set
K
of admissible functions defined in (
6
) is convex.
First, we state some assumptions that should hold throughout the paper.

Assumption 3.1
.

1.

The function
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
is weakly lower semicontinuous and bounded from below by an affine function, i.e., there are
g\in U^{*}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f(u)\geq g(u)+c
for all
u\in U
.

2.

Furthermore,
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
is continuously Fréchet differentiable.

3.

The constants
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
are positive, and
p\in(0,1)
.

In this paper, we identify
U=L^{2}(I\times\Omega)
with its dual space.
Accordingly,
f^{\prime}(u)\in U^{*}
is identified with its Riesz representative
f^{\prime}(\bar{u})\in U
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Under Assumption (
3.1
), the minimization problem (
1
)–(
2
) admits a solution.

Proof.

This statement can be shown with standard arguments. Let
(u_{n},w_{n})
be a minimizing sequence. Then Assumption
3.1
yields boundedness of
(u_{n})
in
U
and also of
(f(u_{n})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u_{n}\|_{U}^{2})
, which leads to boundedness of
(w_{n})
in
W
. Hence, after possibly extracting a subsequence there are
\bar{u}\in U
and
\bar{w}\in W
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
in
U
and
w_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{w}
in
W
with
w_{n}\to\bar{w}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
This implies
\gamma\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\to\gamma\int_{\Omega}|\bar{w}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
, see
[
lp_cont
, Lemma 5.1]
.
Since
K
is closed and convex and therefore weakly closed, it holds
(\bar{u},\bar{w})\in K
. Thus,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}f(u_{n})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u_{n}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|w_{n}\|_{W}^{2}+\gamma\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq f(\bar{u})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\bar{u}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\bar{w}\|_{W}^{2}+\gamma\int_{\Omega}|\bar{w}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

due to weak lower semicontinuity.
This shows that
(\bar{u},\bar{w})
is a solution of (
1
).
∎

4
Necessary optimality condition

The aim of this section is to derive a necessary optimality condition for problem (
1
).
Throughout this section,
(\bar{u},\bar{w})
will be a local solution of the non-smooth problem (
1
).
Since the
L^{p}
-pseudo-norm is not differentiable, we employ a smoothing scheme.

4.1
Smooth auxiliary problem

Let us construct a sequence of auxiliary minimization problems that are smooth enough to directly obtain necessary optimality conditions. These optimality conditions can then be used to derive an optimality condition for the original problem (
1
).

To do so, we use a smoothing scheme as in
[
sparse
,
lp_cont
]
. There, a smooth approximation of
t\mapsto t^{p/2}
is introduced by

\psi_{\epsilon}(t):=\begin{cases}\frac{p}{2}\frac{t}{\epsilon^{2-p}}+(1-\frac{p}{2})\epsilon^{p}&\textrm{if }t\in[0,\epsilon^{2}),\\
t^{p/2}&\textrm{if }t\geq\epsilon^{2},\end{cases}

for some
\epsilon\geq 0
.
Then

\displaystyle G_{\epsilon}(w):=\int_{\Omega}\psi_{\epsilon}(|w|^{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

is an approximation of
\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
. Note that it holds
G_{0}(w)=\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
.

For the convergence analysis later on we use the following results of
[
sparse
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
sparse
, Lemma 4.1, 4.2, 4.3]
)

Let
p\in(0,2)
. Then
\psi_{\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

has the following properties:

(i)

The function
\psi_{\epsilon}
is concave.

(ii)

\epsilon\mapsto\psi_{\epsilon}(t)
is monotonically increasing for all
t\geq 0
.

(iii)

For sequences
(w_{k})
,
(\epsilon_{k})
such that
w_{k}\to w
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\epsilon_{k}\to\epsilon\geq 0
it holds
G_{\epsilon_{k}}(w_{k})\to G_{\epsilon}(w)
.

(iv)

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then
\psi_{\epsilon}
is continuously differentiable with derivative

\displaystyle\psi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{p}{2}\min(\epsilon^{p-2},t^{\frac{p-2}{2}}),

and
w\mapsto G_{\epsilon}(w)
is Fréchet differentiable from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle G_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(w)h=\int_{\Omega}2w(x)\psi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(w(x)^{2})h(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

We remark that while
\psi_{\epsilon}
is concave, this is not the case for the composition
t\mapsto\psi_{\epsilon}(t^{2})
that we use in the definition of
G
to approximate the
L^{p}
-pseudo-norm.

Now we replace the
L^{p}
-pseudo-norm by its smooth approximation to obtain the auxiliary objective function

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon}(u,w):=f(u)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|w\|_{W}^{2}+\gamma G_{\epsilon}(w).

(11)

For
\epsilon=0
, the function
\Phi_{0}
reduces to the original objective.

Next, we also penalize the violation of the constraint
|u|\leq w
in the original minimization problem. To do so, we relax problem (
1
) to

\underset{(u,w)\in U\times W}{\min}\Phi_{\epsilon}(u,w)+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\|(w-u)_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\|(w+u)_{-}\|_{U}^{2},

adding the
L^{2}(I\times\Omega)
-norm of the parts that violate the constraint and multiplying them with a penalization parameter
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
.
Here, we use the notation

\|(w-u)_{-}\|_{U}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{I}((w(x)-u(t,x))_{-})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

and similarly for
\|(w+u)_{-}\|_{U}^{2}
,
using the constant extension of
w
from
\Omega
to
I\times\Omega
.

Let
(\bar{u},\bar{w})
be a local solution of the non-smooth problem (
1
).
Then there is
\rho>0
such that
\Phi_{0}(\bar{u},\bar{w})\leq\Phi_{0}(u,w)
for all
(u,w)
with
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\rho,\|w-\bar{w}\|_{W}\leq\rho
and
(u,w)\in K
.

In order to enforce convergence of solutions of relaxed problems to
(\bar{u},\bar{w})
, we
consider the auxiliary problem

\begin{gathered}\underset{(u,w)\in U\times W}{\min}\Phi_{\epsilon}(u,w)+\frac{1}{2}\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{U}+\frac{1}{2}\|w-\bar{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\|(w-u)_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\|(w+u)_{-}\|_{U}^{2}\\
\textrm{subject to }\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\rho,\|w-\bar{w}\|_{W}\leq\rho.\end{gathered}

(12)

Existence of solution of this problem can be proven by standard arguments similarly as in Theorem
3.2
.

Note that the penalization is an additional step compared to the procedure in
[
sparse
]
, where the considered minimization problem is unconstrained.
Without the penalization, we were not able to prove boundedness of both the Lagrange multipliers to the inequality constraints (
2
) and the derivatives of the smooth approximation
G_{\epsilon}
in the optimality conditions of a sequence of auxiliary problems (
12
).
The penalization enables us to obtain boundedness results for approximations of the multipliers to the inequality constraints, see Lemma
4.7
below. This then implies also boundedness of the derivatives of
G_{\epsilon}
.

4.2
Passing to the limit

In order to derive an optimality condition for problem (
1
), we pass to the limit
\epsilon\searrow 0
in the optimality condition of problem (
12
). First, we show that solutions of (
12
) converge to the reference solution
(\bar{u},\bar{w})
for
\epsilon\searrow 0
. We start with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
(a^{1}_{k}),\dots,(a^{N}_{k})
be sequences with

\liminf_{k\to\infty}a_{k}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}\quad\forall i=1,\dots,N

and

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a^{i}_{k}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\liminf_{k\to\infty}a^{i}_{k}).

Then all sequences
(a^{1}_{k}),...,(a^{N}_{k})
are convergent with limits in
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The claim is elementary for
N=1
. Let the claim be proven for some
N\geq 1
. Consider
N+1
sequences satisfying the assumptions of the lemma.
Then

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a^{i}_{k}\right)+\liminf_{k\to\infty}a^{N+1}_{k}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}a^{i}_{k}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}(\liminf_{k\to\infty}a^{i}_{k}).

(13)

This implies
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a^{i}_{k}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\liminf_{k\to\infty}a^{i}_{k})
, and hence the sequences
(a^{1}_{k}),...,(a^{N}_{k})

converge by induction hypothesis.
Cancelling these convergent sequences in (
13
) we obtain
\limsup_{k\to\infty}a^{N+1}_{k}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}a^{N+1}_{k}
, so
(a^{N+1}_{k})
converges as well.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\epsilon_{k}\searrow 0
and let
(u_{k},w_{k})
be the solution of (
12
) for the corresponding smoothing parameter
\epsilon_{k}
.
Suppose
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u^{*}
in
U
,
w_{k}\rightharpoonup w^{*}
in
W
,
then

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})\geq\Phi_{0}(u^{*},w^{*}).

If in addition

\Phi_{0}(u^{*},w^{*})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})

(14)

is satisfied, then
u_{k}\to u^{*}
in
U
and
w_{k}\to w^{*}
in
W
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
(iii), it holds
{G_{\epsilon_{k}}(w_{k})\to G_{0}(w^{*})}
, which implies

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})\\
\begin{aligned} &\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}f(u_{k})+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u_{k}\|_{U}^{2}+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\beta}{2}\|w_{k}\|_{W}^{2}+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\gamma G_{\epsilon}(w_{k})\\
&\geq\Phi_{0}(u^{*},w^{*})\end{aligned}

by weak lower semicontinuity of
f
and the squared norms on
U
and
W
. This shows the first part.
For the second statement, we want to apply Lemma
4.2
to the terms in
\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})
. The inequality above together with assumption (
14
) yields

\liminf_{k\to\infty}f(u_{k})+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u_{k}\|_{U}^{2}+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\beta}{2}\|w_{k}\|_{W}^{2}+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\gamma G_{\epsilon_{k}}(w_{k})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k}),

and the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
are satisfied.
Therefore, we obtain convergence of all the terms in
\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})
.
In particular, it holds
\|u_{k}\|_{U}\to\|u^{*}\|_{U}
and
\|w_{k}\|_{W}\to\|w^{*}\|_{W}
which together with weak convergence of
(u_{k})
and
(w_{k})
leads to the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\epsilon_{k}\searrow 0
, and let
(u_{k},w_{k})
be solutions of (
12
) for the corresponding smoothing parameter
\epsilon_{k}
. Then
u_{k}\to\bar{u}
in
U
and
w_{k}\to\bar{w}
in
W
.

Proof.

The sequences
(u_{k})
and
(w_{k})
are bounded in
U
and
W
, respectively, due to the constraints in (
12
).
Hence, there are
u^{*}\in U
,
w^{*}\in W
such that (after extracting a subsequence if necessary)
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u^{*}
in
U
,
w_{k}\rightharpoonup w^{*}
in
W
, and
w_{k}\to w^{*}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Using Lemma
4.1
(iii), it holds
G_{\epsilon_{k}}(\bar{w})\to G_{0}(\bar{w})
, so that
\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(\bar{u},\bar{w})\to\Phi_{0}(\bar{u},\bar{w})
follows.
From optimality of
(u_{k},w_{k})
in (
12
) we get

\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(\bar{u},\bar{w})\geq\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{k}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{U}+\frac{1}{2}\|w_{k}-\bar{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}+u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}.

Passing to the limit superior and using the first claim of Lemma
4.3
leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi_{0}(\bar{u},\bar{w})&\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{k}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{U}+\frac{1}{2}\|w_{k}-\bar{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}+u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}\right)\\
&\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\|u_{k}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{U}+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\|w_{k}-\bar{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\quad+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}+u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}\right)\\
&\geq\Phi_{0}(u^{*},w^{*})+\frac{1}{2}\|u^{*}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{U}+\frac{1}{2}\|w^{*}-\bar{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\quad+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}+u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(15)

From these inequalities we obtain boundedness of the terms
\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}\pm u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}
. Since
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
, it holds
\|(w_{k}\pm u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}\to 0
.
Further, we have
w_{k}-u_{k}-(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\in\{v\in U:v\geq 0\textrm{ a.e. on }I\times\Omega\}
.
This set is weakly closed. Using
w_{k}-u_{k}\rightharpoonup w^{*}-u^{*}
in
U
and
(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\to 0
in
U
, it follows
w^{*}-u^{*}\geq 0
almost everywhere on
I\times\Omega
.
Similarly, one can show
w^{*}+u^{*}\geq 0
almost everywhere on
I\times\Omega
, and therefore
(u^{*},w^{*})
is admissible for problem (
1
)-(
2
).
By local optimality of
(\bar{u},\bar{w})
we get together with the chain of inequalities (
15
)

\Phi_{0}(\bar{u},\bar{w})\geq\Phi_{0}(u^{*},w^{*})+\frac{1}{2}\|u^{*}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{U}+\frac{1}{2}\|w^{*}-\bar{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\Phi_{0}(\bar{u},\bar{w}),

which implies that
(\bar{u},\bar{w})=(u^{*},w^{*})
.
The first inequality in (
15
) also gives
\Phi_{0}(\bar{u},\bar{w})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k},w_{k})
,
which together with
Lemma
4.3
yields strong convergence
u_{k}\to\bar{u}
in
U
and
w_{k}\to\bar{w}
in
W
.
∎

Next, we consider a necessary optimality condition for problem (
12
).

Lemma 4.5
.

For some
\epsilon>0
, let
(u_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon})
be a solution of (
12
) with
\|u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u}\|_{U}<\rho
and
\|w_{\epsilon}-\bar{w}\|_{W}<\rho
.
Then it holds

\begin{gathered}f^{\prime}(u_{\epsilon})v+\alpha(u_{\epsilon},v)_{U}+\beta(w_{\epsilon},z)_{W}+\gamma G_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(w_{\epsilon})z+(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u},v)_{U}+(w_{\epsilon}-\bar{w},z)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((w_{\epsilon}-u_{\epsilon})_{-},\ z-v)_{U}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((w_{\epsilon}+u_{\epsilon})_{-},\ z+v)_{U}=0\ \forall(v,z)\in U\times W.\end{gathered}

(16)

Here, we used the notation

((w_{\epsilon}\pm u_{\epsilon})_{-},\ z\pm v)_{U}:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{I}(w_{\epsilon}(x)\pm u_{\epsilon}(t,x))_{-}\cdot(z(x)\pm v(t,x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

The map
z\mapsto(z_{-})^{2}
is continuously differentiable from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
.
Using dominated convergence, it is easy to verify that
v\mapsto\|v_{-}\|_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}
is directionally
differentiable from
L^{2}(I\times\Omega)
to
\mathbb{R}
.
In addition,
f
and
G_{\epsilon}
are Fréchet differentiable by Assumption
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
.
Then the objective function of problem (
12
) is directionally differentiable, and a necessary optimality condition is

f^{\prime}(u_{\epsilon})(v-u_{\epsilon})+\alpha(u_{\epsilon},v-u_{\epsilon})_{U}+\beta(w_{\epsilon},z-w_{\epsilon})_{W}+\gamma G_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(w_{\epsilon})(z-w_{\epsilon})\\
+(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u},v-u_{\epsilon})_{U}+(w_{\epsilon}-\bar{w},z-w_{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((w_{\epsilon}-u_{\epsilon})_{-},\ z-w_{\epsilon}-(v-u_{\epsilon}))_{U}\\
+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((w_{\epsilon}+u_{\epsilon})_{-},\ z-w_{\epsilon}+v-u_{\epsilon})_{U}\geq 0

for all
(v,z)\in U\times W
with
\|v-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\rho
and
\|z-\bar{w}\|_{W}\leq\rho
. Using the fact that
\|u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u}\|_{U}<\rho
and
\|w_{\epsilon}-\bar{w}\|_{W}<\rho
one obtains the optimality condition (
16
).
∎

Note that for solutions
(u_{k},w_{k})
corresponding to
\epsilon_{k}\searrow 0
, the condition
\|u_{k}-\bar{u}\|_{U}<\rho
and
\|w_{k}-\bar{w}\|_{W}<\rho
is always satisfied for
k
large enough as a consequence of Theorem
4.4
.

The next theorem shows that solutions
(u_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon})
to the penalized and unconstrained optimization problem still satisfy a necessary condition for admissibility.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(u_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon})
be a solution to problem (
12
). Then it holds
w_{\epsilon}\geq 0
.

Proof.

Define
w_{\epsilon}^{-}:=(w_{\epsilon})_{-}
.
Then we get the following inequalities

G_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(w_{\epsilon})w_{\epsilon}^{-}\geq 0,\quad(w_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 0,\quad(-\bar{w},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 0,

where we used the expression of
G_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
from Lemma
4.1
and
\bar{w}\geq 0
.
Furthermore, by Lemma
2.4
we have

(w_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{W}=(w_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon})_{W}-(w_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon}^{+})_{W}\geq 0.

In addition, we get
((w_{\epsilon}\pm u_{\epsilon})_{-},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{U}\geq 0
.
Now, we test (
16
) with
(0,w_{\epsilon}^{-})
to obtain

\beta(w_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{W}+\gamma G_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(w_{\epsilon})w_{\epsilon}^{-}+(w_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\bar{w},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((w_{\epsilon}-u_{\epsilon})_{-}+(w_{\epsilon}+u_{\epsilon})_{-},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{U}=0.

Since by the above considerations every summand in this equation is non-negative,
it follows that all summands are zero.
In particular, this implies

0=(w_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(w_{\epsilon}^{-},w_{\epsilon}^{-})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
so that
w_{\epsilon}^{-}=0
and
w_{\epsilon}\geq 0
almost everywhere on
\Omega
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(u_{k},w_{k})
be the sequence of solutions to the smoothed problem (
12
) corresponding to a sequence
(\epsilon_{k})\searrow 0
and let
\lambda_{k}:=G_{\epsilon_{k}}^{\prime}(w_{k})
,
\mu_{k}^{1}:=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}
and

\mu_{k}^{2}:=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}(w_{k}+u_{k})_{-}
.
Then there are
\bar{\mu}^{1}
and
\bar{\mu}^{2}
in
U

such that for a subsequence
(k_{n})
it holds
\mu_{k_{n}}^{1}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mu}^{1}
and
\mu_{k_{n}}^{2}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mu}^{2}
in
U
. Furthermore, it holds
\lambda_{k_{n}}\to\bar{\lambda}
in
W^{*}
for some
\bar{\lambda}
in
W^{*}
.

Proof.

By testing equation (
16
) with
v:=-(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}
and
z=0
, we get

-f^{\prime}(u_{k})(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}+\alpha(u_{k},-(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-})_{U}+(u_{k}-\bar{u},-(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-})_{U}\\
+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}((w_{k}+u_{k})_{-},-(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-})_{U}=0.

With Lemma
4.6
the last term on the left-hand side vanishes.

Rearranging and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to

\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}\leq\left(\|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{U}+\alpha\|u_{k}\|_{U}+\|u_{k}-\bar{u}\|_{U}\right)\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U},

which implies

\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}\leq\left(\|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{U}+\alpha\|u_{k}\|_{U}+\|u_{k}-\bar{u}\|_{U}\right).

Since
(f^{\prime}(u_{k}))
and
(u_{k})
are bounded sequences in
U
, we obtain boundedness of
\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}
.

Analogously, one obtains boundedness of
\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}+u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}
. Therefore, weak convergence
\mu_{k}^{1}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mu}^{1}
and
\mu_{k}^{2}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mu}^{2}
in
U
along a subsequence
(k_{n})
for some weak limit points
\bar{\mu}^{1},\ \bar{\mu}^{2}\in U
follows.

Using the optimality condition (
16
) with
v\equiv 0
, we get

\gamma\braket{\lambda_{k},z}=-\beta(w_{k},z)_{W}-(w_{k}-\bar{w},z)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\mu_{k}^{1},z)_{U}-(\mu_{k}^{2},z)_{U}\quad\forall z\in W,

where
(\mu_{k}^{i},z)_{U}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{I}\mu_{k}^{i}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\ z(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
for

.
Due to the compact embedding of

into
W^{*}
, all terms on the right-hand side converge in
W^{*}
along the subsequence
(k_{n})
,
and we obtain
\lambda_{k_{n}}\to\bar{\lambda}
in
W^{*}
.
∎

The previous Lemma
4.7
explains the use of the penalization of the constraint
(u,w)\in K
in the auxiliary problem. By first showing boundedness and weak convergence of the sequences
(\mu_{k}^{1}),(\mu_{k}^{2})
, one can quite directly deduce boundedness and convergence of
(\lambda_{k})
. Without the penalization, it would be necessary to somehow separate

and the multipliers corresponding to the constraint.

Concerning the properties of the limit
\bar{\lambda}
, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\epsilon_{k}\searrow 0
, and let
(u_{k},w_{k})
be a sequence of solutions to (

) and

\lambda_{k}
the sequence of multipliers as defined above. Suppose

in

. Then

\displaystyle\braket{\lambda_{k},w_{k}}_{W}\to\braket{\bar{\lambda},\bar{w}}_{W}=p\int_{\Omega}|\bar{w}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

The proof of
[

can be transferred to this setting here,
as it only uses the definition of

via the smoothing scheme and the convergence

∎

Combining the results above, we obtain an optimality condition for the original problem (

).
Here, we will obtain Lagrange multipliers to both constraints encoded in the set

, i.e., to the constraints

and

Define

C:=\{v\in U:\ v\geq 0\},\quad I_{C}(v):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }v\in C,\\

Then

at

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(\bar{u},\bar{w})
be a local solution of the original problem (

). Then there are
\bar{\lambda}\in W^{*}
and
\bar{\mu}^{1},\bar{\mu}^{2}\in U

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\bar{u})v+\alpha(\bar{u},v)_{U}+\beta(\bar{w},z)_{W}+\gamma\braket{\bar{\lambda},z}_{W}+(\bar{\mu}^{1},z-v)_{U}+(\bar{\mu}^{2},z+v)_{U}=0

for all

and it holds

\displaystyle\braket{\bar{\lambda},\bar{w}}_{W}=p\int_{\Omega}|\bar{w}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover,
\bar{\mu}^{1}\in\partial I_{C}(\bar{w}-\bar{u})
and
\bar{\mu}^{2}\in\partial I_{C}(\bar{w}+\bar{u})
.

Here, we used the notation

(\bar{\mu}^{1},z-v)_{U}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{I}\bar{\mu}^{1}(t,x)(z(x)-v(t,x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

for
v\in U
,
z\in W
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{k},w_{k})
be the sequence of solutions to the smoothed problem (
12
) corresponding to a sequence
(\epsilon_{k})\searrow 0
. Equation (

) is a consequence of passing to the limit in (

) along a subsequence using Lemma
4.7
.
Property (

) follows from Lemma
4.8
.
Next, we show

\bar{\mu}^{1}\in\partial I_{C}(\bar{w}-\bar{u})
.
Let

. Using the definition of

\mu_{k}^{1}
as
\mu_{k}^{1}=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\leq 0
in Lemma

,
we get

\displaystyle(\mu_{k}^{1},v-(w_{k}-u_{k}))_{U}=(\mu_{k}^{1},v)_{U}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\|(w_{k}-u_{k})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}\leq 0.

Convergence of
w_{k}\to\bar{w}
in

,
u_{k}\to\bar{u}
in

, and
\mu_{k}^{1}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mu}^{1}

\displaystyle(\bar{\mu}^{1},v-(\bar{w}-\bar{u}))_{U}=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mu_{k}^{1},v-(w_{k}-u_{k}))_{U}\leq 0.

The claim follows now by the definition of the convex subdifferential. The corresponding statement for
\bar{\mu}^{2}
can be shown analogously.
∎

The statements

\bar{\mu}^{1}\in\partial I_{C}(\bar{w}-\bar{u})
and
\bar{\mu}^{2}\in\partial I_{C}(\bar{w}+\bar{u})

are equivalent to the complementarity system

Lemma 5.7

\delta_{n}\searrow 0

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\delta_{n}\searrow 0
. Then it holds
u_{k}^{n}\to u_{k+1}

Proof.

Since
(u_{k}^{n})
,
(w_{k}^{n})
are bounded, there is a weakly convergent subsequence also denoted by
(u_{k}^{n})
,
(w_{k}^{n})

\bar{w}_{k}\in W

\frac{1}{\delta_{n}}\|(w_{k}^{n}+u_{k}^{n})_{-}\|_{U}

By optimality of

\begin{gathered}\varphi_{k}(u_{k+1},w_{k+1})\geq\varphi_{k}(u_{k}^{n},w_{k}^{n})+\frac{1}{2\delta_{n}}\|(w_{k}^{n}-u_{k}^{n})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\delta_{n}}\|(w_{k}^{n}+u_{k}^{n})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}.\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi_{k}(u_{k+1},w_{k+1})&\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\varphi_{k}(u_{k}^{n},w_{k}^{n})+\frac{1}{2\delta_{n}}\|(w_{k}^{n}-u_{k}^{n})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\delta_{n}}\|(w_{k}^{n}+u_{k}^{n})_{-}\|_{U}^{2}\right)\\

\geq\varphi_{k}(u_{k+1},w_{k+1})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{k}(u_{k}^{n},w_{k}^{n}),

where the last inequality is due to (

are satisfied,
and all the individual terms in the inequality above converge. In particular we have

and

, respectively.

For the second statement, suppose we have
\frac{1}{\delta_{n_{j}}}(w_{k}^{n_{j}}-u_{k}^{n_{j}})_{-}\rightharpoonup\mu^{1}_{k}

\begin{split}(\mu_{k}^{1},v-(w_{k+1}-u_{k+1}))_{U}&=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{n_{j}}}((w_{k}^{n_{j}}-u_{k}^{n_{j}})_{-},v-(w_{k}^{n_{j}}-u_{k}^{n_{j}}))_{U}\\

so that

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
(u_{k},w_{k},L_{k})
be a sequence generated by Algorithm

.
Let

Proof.

Let
(u_{k},w_{k})

Then

That is,

of multipliers to be bounded. Therefore, we can a extract a subsequence still denoted by

6
Numerical Results

In this section, we consider some numerical examples to test Algorithm
1
.
For the implementation of the algorithm, we use
P_{1}
-finite elements
to construct the discrete spaces
U_{h}
and
W_{h}
on
I\times\Omega
and
\Omega
.
The domain
\Omega
is triangulated with some shape regular mesh with
N
nodes and mesh-size
h
, the interval
I
is divided by
M
equidistant nodes into intervals of equal length.
Then
W_{h}
is defined as the
P_{1}
-space on the triangulation of
\Omega
, whereas
U_{h}
is the tensor-product space on the subdivisions of
I
and
\Omega
.
It follows
\dim(U_{h})=M\cdot N
and
\dim(W_{n})=N
.
Let
(\phi_{i})
and
(\phi_{i,j})
denote the basis functions of
W_{h}
and
U_{h}
.
Then it holds
(u,w)=(\sum_{i,j}u_{i,j}\varphi_{i,j},\sum_{i}w_{i}\varphi_{i})\in K\cap(U_{h}\times W_{h})
if and only if
|u_{i,j}|\leq w_{i}
for all
i,j
.

For solving subproblem (
24
) in each step of the iterative scheme, we employ a semismooth Newton method as presented in
[
21ss_kanzow
]
. To apply this method, the discretized optimality condition of (
24
) is rewritten as a system of equations in the variables
u\in\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot N},w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mu^{1},\mu^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot N}
using the Fischer-Burmeister function. Here,

\mu^{1},\mu^{2}
are the Lagrange multipliers for the constraint
(u,w)\in K
. The system is then solved
with a globalized semismooth Newton method, where we switch to the gradient step of a merit function whenever the Newton iterate is not well defined or does not satisfy a certain descent condition.

In every step of the semismooth Newton method we solve a linear system to obtain the search direction for the new iterates
u_{k+1},w_{k+1},\mu^{1}_{k+1}
and
\mu^{2}_{k+1}
. The matrix of the linear system is a block matrix consisting mostly of sparse matrices.
Only the block of size
N\times N
that corresponds to the discretization of the
W
inner product
is not sparse but dense.
If we had not introduced the auxiliary function
w
and had directly worked with (
5
),
the resulting system would have a dense block of size
(M\cdot N)\times(M\cdot N)
.

In order to find a parameter
L_{k}
such that the descent condition (
25
) is satisfied, we employ a procedure similar to Armijo line-search in nonlinear optimization.
We solve (
24
) for
L_{k}=Lb^{l}
, where
l=0,1,\dots
is increased until (
25
) is satisfied.
In the following examples we choose the parameters
L=1
and
b=2
in this loop.

Next, we consider two numerical examples where
f
is chosen to be a tracking type functional of the form
f(u)=\frac{a}{2}\|u-u_{d}\|_{U}^{2}
for some given function
u_{d}\in U
and
a>0
. In these examples we choose the space
W=\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
.
For computing the
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
-stiffness matrix, we follow
[
control1dim
]
in the one-dimensional and
[
fe2d
]
in the two-dimensional case.

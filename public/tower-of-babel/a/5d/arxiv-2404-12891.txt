1.
Introduction

If
G
is a finite group and
X,Y
are subsets of
G
, we write
\mathrm{Pr}(X,Y)
for the probability that two random elements
x\in X
and
y\in Y
commute. Thus,

\mathrm{Pr}(X,Y)=\frac{|\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\mid xy=yx\}|}{|X|\,|Y|}.

The number
\mathrm{Pr}(G,G)
is called the commuting probability of
G
. It is well-known that
\mathrm{Pr}(G,G)\leq 5/8
for any nonabelian group
G
. Another important result is the theorem of P. M. Neumann
[
7
]
which states that
if
G
is a finite group and
\epsilon
is a positive number such that
\mathrm{Pr}(G,G)\geq\epsilon
,
then
G
has a nilpotent normal subgroup
R
of nilpotency class at most
2
such that both the index
[G:R]

and the order of the commutator subgroup
[R,R]
are
\epsilon
-bounded (see also
[
4
]
).

Throughout the article we use the expression “
(a,b,\dots)
-bounded” to mean that a quantity is bounded from above by a number depending only on the parameters
a,b,\dots
.

There are several recent papers studying
\mathrm{Pr}(H,G)
, where
H
is a subgroup of
G
(see for example
[
3
,
5
,
6
]
).
In particular, it was proved in
[
3
, Proposition 1.2]
that if
H
is a subgroup of a finite group
G
and
\mathrm{Pr}(H,G)\geq\epsilon>0
,
then there is a normal subgroup
T\leq G
and a subgroup
B\leq H
such that the indices
[G:T]
and
[H:B]
, and the order of the commutator subgroup
[T,B]
are
\epsilon
-bounded.

Lately there also has been a considerable interest in studying approximate subgroups of finite groups.

Let
K
be a positive real number. A subset
A
of a finite group
G
is said to be a
K
-approximate subgroup of
G
, or simply a
K
-approximate
group, if
A
contains
1
and the inverse of each of its elements, and if there exists
E\subseteq G
with
|E|\leq K

such that
A^{2}\subseteq EA
.

Here and throughout, given a positive integer
j
and a subset
X
of a group
G
, we write
X^{j}
for the set of all products
x_{1}\dots x_{j}
, where
x_{i}\in X
. The definition of approximate subgroups was introduced by Tao in
[
9
]
. Since then many important results on the subject have been established. In particular, Breuillard, Green and Tao essentially described the structure of finite approximate subgroups
[
2
]
. The reader is referred to the book
[
10
]
for detailed information on these developments.

In the present paper we examine the relation between the structure of an approximate subgroup and the commuting probability. Using the well known analogy between approximate groups and groups, we aim at extending the above mentioned group-theoretical results to approximate subgroups. On the one side, we employ the group-theoretical machinery already available, and, on the other side, ad hoc techniques for approximate subgroups.

Proposition 1.2 in
[
3
]
says, roughly speaking, that a subgroup
H
of a finite group
G
has many commuting elements with
G
if and only if
H
has a large subgroup which almost commutes with a large normal subgroup of
G
. Replacing
H
with an approximate subgroup
A
we obtain that
A
has many commuting elements with
G
if and only if it is commensurate with another approximate subgroup
B
which generates a subgroup
H
of
G
with the same properties as in the aforementioned proposition. More formally, our result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be a
K
-approximate subgroup of a finite group
G
, and let
\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\geq\epsilon>0
.
There are two
(\epsilon,K)
-bounded positive numbers
\gamma
and
K_{0}

such that
G
contains a normal subgroup
T
and a
K_{0}
-approximate subgroup
B
such that

(i)

|A\cap B|\geq\gamma\;{\mathrm{max}}\{|A|,|B|\}
, and

(ii)

the index
[G:T]
and the order of the subgroup
[T,\langle B\rangle]
are both
(\epsilon,K)
-bounded.

Throughout,
\langle X\rangle
denotes the subgroup generated by a subset
X
of a group.

Next, we study approximate subgroups
A
such that
\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)\geq\epsilon>0
. Roughly speaking, Neumann’s theorem says that if a finite group has many commuting elements then it has a large subgroup which is almost abelian, in the sense that it has a small commutator subgroup. It turns out that if
A
is an approximate subgroup with many commuting elements then a big part of
A^{2}
is contained in a subgroup
C
with small commutator subgroup, and
A
itself is contained in boundedly many cosets of
C
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
be a
K
-approximate subgroup of a finite group
G
and assume that
\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)\geq\epsilon>0
. There are two
(\epsilon,K)
-bounded positive numbers
\gamma
and
s
, and a subgroup
C\leq G
such that

(i)

|C\cap A^{2}|>\gamma|A|
, and

(ii)

the commutator subgroup of
C
is of order at most
s
.

Moreover
A
is contained in the union of at most
\gamma^{-1}K^{2}
left cosets of the group
C
.

Furthermore, we show that each of the above theorems admits an “approximate” converse.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
A,B
be subsets and
T
a subgroup of a finite group
G
. Set
\gamma=|A\cap B|/|A|
,
n=[G:T]
and

m=|[T,\langle B\rangle]|
. Then
\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\geq\frac{\gamma}{nm}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
A
be a
K
-approximate subgroup of a finite group
G
, and let
C\leq G
be a subgroup.
Set
\gamma=|C\cap A^{2}|/|A|
and
s=|C^{\prime}|
.
Then

\mathrm{Pr}(A^{2},A^{2})\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{K^{4}s}.

Note that some properties of the commuting probability of subgroups cannot be extended verbatim to approximate subgroups but they do hold when replacing an approximate subgroup
A
with
A^{2}
(compare Proposition
2.6
and Example
2.8
).
Clearly,
A^{2}
is a

-approximate subgroup, whenever

is a
K
-approximate subgroup (if
A^{2}\subseteq EA
, then
A^{4}\subseteq EA^{3}\subseteq E^{2}A^{2}
).

The next section contains some general comments on the commuting probabilities. In Section 3 we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. The last section is devoted to Propositions

and
1.4
.

2.
General comments on commuting probabilities

Note that if
X,Y
are subsets of a finite group
G
, we have

\mathrm{Pr}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{|Y|}\sum_{y\in Y}\frac{|C_{X}(y)|}{|X|}=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}\frac{|C_{Y}(x)|}{|Y|}.

It was proved in
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
that if
K
and
N
are subgroups of a finite group
G
, with
N
normal in
G
, then

\mathrm{Pr}(K,G)\leq\mathrm{Pr}\left(KN/N,G/N\right)\,\,\mathrm{Pr}(N\cap K,N).

We are interested in finding an “approximate” variant of this result. We start by looking at symmetric subsets.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
N
a normal subgroup of
G
, and assume that
A
is a symmetric subset of
G
.
Then

\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\leq\frac{|A^{5}|}{|A|}\,\mathrm{Pr}\left(\frac{AN}{N},\frac{G}{N}\right)\,\mathrm{Pr}(A^{4}\cap N,N).

Proof.

Let
\bar{G}=G/N
and
\bar{A}=\{hN\mid h\in A\}=\{h_{1}N,\dots,h_{r}N\}
. Note that
A
is contained in the union of the subsets
h_{i}(N\cap A^{2})
, for
i=1,\dots,r
. Indeed,
A\subseteq\bigcup_{i}h_{i}N
and if
a\in h_{i}N
, then
h_{i}^{-1}a=n
for some
n\in N\cap A^{2}
and so
a=h_{i}n\in h_{i}(N\cap A^{2})
.
Therefore

\displaystyle|A|\,|G|\,\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in A}|C_{G}(x)|\leq\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}\left(\sum_{x\in h(N\cap A^{2})}\frac{|NC_{G}(x)|}{|N|}|C_{N}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}\left(\sum_{x\in h(N\cap A^{2})}|C_{\bar{G}}(hN)|\,|C_{N}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}|C_{\bar{G}}(hN)|\left(\sum_{x\in h(N\cap A^{2})}|C_{N}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}|C_{\bar{G}}(hN)|\left(\sum_{y\in N}|C_{h(N\cap A^{2})}(y)|\right).

If
C_{h(N\cap A^{2})}(y)\neq\emptyset
take
y_{0}\in h(N\cap A^{2})\cap C_{G}(y)
and observe that
h(N\cap A^{2})\subseteq y_{0}(N\cap A^{4})
. Indeed,
y_{0}=hu
with
u\in N\cap A^{2}
, whence
h=y_{0}u^{-1}
and
h(N\cap A^{2})\subseteq y_{0}u^{-1}(N\cap A^{2})\subseteq y_{0}(N\cap A^{4})
.
Therefore

\displaystyle C_{h(N\cap A^{2})}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(N\cap A^{2})\cap C_{G}(y)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle y_{0}C_{N\cap A^{4}}(y),

whence
|C_{h(N\cap A^{2})}(y)|\leq|C_{N\cap A^{4}}(y)|
.
It follows that

\displaystyle|A|\,|G|\,\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}|C_{\bar{G}}(hN)|\right)\left(\sum_{y\in N}|C_{N\cap A^{4}}(y)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(|\bar{A}|\,|\bar{G}|\,\mathrm{Pr}(\bar{A},\bar{G})\Big)\Big(|N|\,|N\cap A^{4}|\,\mathrm{Pr}(N\cap A^{4},N)\Big).

Thus

\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\leq\frac{|\bar{A}|\,|N\cap A^{4}|}{|A|}\,\mathrm{Pr}(\bar{A},\bar{G})\,\mathrm{Pr}(N\cap A^{4},N).

As
|\bar{A}|\,|N\cap A^{4}|\leq|A|^{5}
by
[
10
, Lemma 2.6.3]
, the result follows.
∎

The previous proposition says, in particular, that in a homomorphic image
\bar{G}
of
G
the commuting probability of
\bar{A}
in
\bar{G}
is controlled in terms of
\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)
. The next lemma deals with the commuting probability
\mathrm{Pr}(\bar{A},\bar{A})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
N
a normal subgroup of
G
, and assume that
A
is a symmetric subset of
G
. Then

\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)\leq\frac{|A^{3}|\,|A^{5}|}{|A|^{2}}\,\mathrm{Pr}\left(\frac{AN}{N},\frac{AN}{N}\right)\,\mathrm{Pr}(A^{4}\cap N,A^{2}\cap N).

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the proof of Proposition
2.1
. Thus we will sketch it, avoiding repetitions.
Let
\bar{G}=G/N
and
\bar{A}=\{hN\mid h\in A\}=\{h_{1}N,\dots,h_{r}N\}
. As shown in Proposition
2.1
,
A
is contained in the union of the subsets
h_{i}(N\cap A^{2})
, for
i=1,\dots,r
.
Moreover, for
x\in A
,

C_{A}(x)\subseteq\bigcup_{hN\in C_{A}(x)N/N}hC_{N\cap A^{2}}(x),

since, for
a,h\in C_{A}(x)
, the equality
aN=hN
implies
h^{-1}a\in N\cap A^{2}
.
Therefore, as
C_{A}(x)N/N\subseteq C_{\bar{A}}(xN)
, we have

(1)

|C_{A}(x)|\leq|C_{\bar{A}}(xN)|C_{N\cap A^{2}}(x)|.

It follows that

\displaystyle|A|\,|A|\,\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in A}|C_{A}(x)|\leq\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}\left(\sum_{x\in h(N\cap A^{2})}|C_{A}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}\left(\sum_{x\in h(N\cap A^{2})}|C_{\bar{A}}(hN)|\,|C_{N\cap A^{2}}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}|C_{\bar{A}}(hN)|\left(\sum_{x\in h(N\cap A^{2})}|C_{N\cap A^{2}}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}|C_{\bar{A}}(hN)|\left(\sum_{y\in{N\cap A^{2}}}|C_{h(N\cap A^{2})}(y)|\right).

As in the proof of Proposition
2.1
, the inequality
|C_{h(N\cap A^{2})}(y)|\leq|C_{N\cap A^{4}}(y)|
holds for every
h\in A
.
Therefore

|A|\,|A|\,\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)\leq\left(\sum_{hN\in\bar{A}}|C_{\bar{A}}(hN)|\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{N\cap A^{2}}}|C_{N\cap A^{4}}(y)|\right)

=\Big(|\bar{A}|\,|\bar{A}|\,\mathrm{Pr}(\bar{A},\bar{A})\Big)\Big(|{N\cap A^{2}}|\,|N\cap A^{4}|\,\mathrm{Pr}(N\cap A^{4},{N\cap A^{2}})\Big).

Thus

\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)\leq\frac{|\bar{A}|^{2}\,|N\cap A^{4}||{N\cap A^{2}}|}{|A|^{2}}\,\mathrm{Pr}(\bar{A},\bar{A})\,\mathrm{Pr}(N\cap A^{4},{N\cap A^{2}}).

As
|\bar{A}|\,|N\cap A^{4}|\leq|A|^{5}
and
|\bar{A}|\,|N\cap A^{2}|\leq|A|^{3}
by
[
10
, Lemma 2.6.3]
, the result follows.
∎

As
|A^{n}|/|A|\leq K^{n-1}
for any
K
-approximate subgroup
A
and for every integer

, the following corollary is a straightforward consequence of the above propositions.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
N
a normal subgroup of
G
, and assume that
A\subseteq G
is a
K
-approximate subgroup.
Then

•

\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\leq K^{4}\,\mathrm{Pr}(AN/N,G/N)\,\mathrm{Pr}(A^{4}\cap N,N),

•

\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)\leq K^{6}\,\mathrm{Pr}(AN/N,AN/N)\,\mathrm{Pr}(A^{4}\cap N,A^{2}\cap N).

In particular,

•

\mathrm{Pr}(AN/N,G/N)\geq(1/{K^{4}})\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)
,

•

\mathrm{Pr}(AN/N,AN/N)\geq(1/K^{6})\mathrm{Pr}(A,A).

More generally, the above corollary holds in the case where
A
is a symmetric set containing
1
which has small tripling, i.e.
|A^{3}|\leq T|A|
. To see this, observe that
|A^{5}|\leq T^{3}|A|
by
[
10
, Proposition 2.5.3]
.

If
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
are subgroups of a finite group
G
, then

(2)

\mathrm{Pr}(H_{1},G)\geq\mathrm{Pr}(H_{2},G)

(see
[
5
, Theorem 3.7]
).

As we will show in Example
2.8
, the above inequality does not hold if
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
are
K
-approximate subgroups.
Indeed, if
H_{1}
is a
K
-approximate subgroup contained in
H_{2}
, then
\mathrm{Pr}(H_{1},G)
might be arbitrarily small compared to
\mathrm{Pr}(H_{2},G)
(even if
H_{2}
is a subgroup).
We will show however that
\mathrm{Pr}(H_{1}^{2},G)
is bounded away from zero (see Proposition
2.6
).
In the particular case where
H_{1}
is a subgroup of
G
and
H_{2}
is a

-approximate subgroup containing

, we get

\mathrm{Pr}(H_{1},G)\geq\frac{1}{K}\mathrm{Pr}(H_{2},G)

(see Corollary

).

To prove these results, we need a preliminary elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
G
is a finite group,
g\in G
and
A
is a symmetric subset of
G.

Then

(a)

|C_{A}(g)|\,|g^{A}|\leq|A^{2}|
.

(b)

|A|\leq|C_{A^{2}}(g)|{|g^{A}|}
.

Proof.

Note that if
g^{A}=\{g^{a_{1}},\dots,g^{a_{r}}\}
, then the subsets
C_{A}(g)a_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,r
, are pairwise disjoint subsets of
A^{2}
, so

\left|C_{A}(g)\right|\left|g^{A}\right|=\left|\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq r}C_{A}(g)a_{i}\right|\leq\left|A^{2}\right|,

and (a) holds.

To prove (b), observe that whenever
a,b\in A
are such that
g^{a}=g^{b}
, we have
ab^{-1}\in C_{G}(g)\cap A^{2}=C_{A^{2}}(g)
.
∎

The following result holds for approximate subgroups.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
G
is a finite group,
g\in G
and
A\subseteq G
is a
K
-approximate subgroup. Then

|g^{A^{n}}|\leq K^{n-1}|g^{A}|
for every
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

As
A^{2}\subseteq EA
where
|E|\leq K
, we have
A^{n}\subseteq E^{n-1}A
. Since
A
is symmetric, every element
h\in A^{n}
can be written as the inverse of an element
ea\in A^{n}\subseteq E^{n-1}A
, with
e\in E^{n-1}
and
a\in A
. So

g^{h}=g^{(ea)^{-1}}=(g^{a^{-1}})^{e^{-1}},

and since there are at most
K^{n-1}
elements in
E^{n-1}
, we deduce that
|g^{A^{n}}|\leq K^{n-1}|g^{A}|.

∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}
be symmetric subsets of
G
such that
|A_{1}^{2}|\leq K|A_{1}|
and
|A_{2}^{2}|\leq K^{\prime}|A_{2}|
. Then for any subset
B\subseteq G
we have

\mathrm{Pr}(A_{1}^{2},B)\geq\frac{1}{KK^{\prime}}\mathrm{Pr}(A_{2},B).

Proof.

Let
g\in G
. As
|A_{1}^{2}|\leq K|A_{1}|
, it follows from Lemma
2.4
(b) that

\frac{|C_{A_{1}^{2}}(g)|}{|{A_{1}^{2}}|}\geq\frac{1}{K|g^{A_{1}}|}.

Clearly, as
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}
, we have
1/{|g^{A_{1}}|}\geq 1/{|g^{A_{2}}|}
. Moreover, as
|A_{2}^{2}|\leq K^{\prime}|A_{2}|
, we deduce from Lemma
2.4
(a) that

\frac{1}{|g^{A_{2}}|}\geq\frac{|C_{A_{2}}(g)|}{K^{\prime}|{A_{2}}|}.

Therefore

\frac{|C_{A_{1}^{2}}(g)|}{|{A_{1}^{2}}|}\geq\frac{1}{K|g^{A_{1}}|}\geq\frac{1}{K|g^{A_{2}}|}\geq\frac{|C_{A_{2}}(g)|}{KK^{\prime}|{A_{2}}|}.

We conclude that

\mathrm{Pr}(A_{1}^{2},B)=\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{g\in B}\frac{|C_{A_{1}^{2}}(g)|}{|{A_{1}^{2}}|}\geq\frac{1}{KK^{\prime}|B|}\sum_{g\in B}\frac{|C_{A_{2}}(g)|}{|{A_{2}}|}=\frac{1}{KK^{\prime}}\mathrm{Pr}(A_{2},B),

as claimed.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let

be a subgroup contained in a

-approximate subgroup

. Then for any subset

\mathrm{Pr}(H,B)\geq\frac{1}{K}\mathrm{Pr}(A,B).

The result a straightforward consequence of the previous lemma, taking into account that

.

Example 2.8
.

Here we show that for any positive integer

and

\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\leq\epsilon\;\mathrm{Pr}(H,G)

{\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)}/{\mathrm{Pr}(H,G)}

Let

Let

Since

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{a\in A}\frac{|C_{G}(a)|}{|G|}=\frac{1}{|A|}\left(\sum_{1\neq a\in A}\frac{|C_{G}(a)|}{|G|}+\frac{|C_{G}(1)|}{|G|}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A|}\left(\frac{|A|-1}{n}+1\right)=\frac{1}{kz+1}\left(\frac{kz}{n}+1\right)<\frac{kz+n}{kzn}.

As

, which is an abelian subgroup of index

G
, whenever
x\in H
we have
[G:C_{G}(x)]\leq n
. So we can estimate
\mathrm{Pr}(H,G)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(H,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{x\in H}\frac{|C_{G}(x)|}{|G|}=\frac{1}{|H|}\left(\sum_{x\in H\setminus Z}\frac{|C_{G}(x)|}{|G|}+\sum_{x\in Z}\frac{|C_{G}(x)|}{|G|}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|H|}\left(\frac{|H|-z}{n}+z\right)=\frac{1}{2^{k}z}\left(\frac{2^{k}z-z+nz}{n}\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}z}\left(\frac{nz}{n}\right)=\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

Therefore

\frac{\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)}{\mathrm{Pr}(H,G)}<\frac{kz+n}{kzn}\,2^{k}=\left({\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{kz}}\right)\,{2^{k}}.

This can be arbitrarily small if
n
and
|U|
are chosen large enough.

Example 2.9
.

In the previous example
A
was quite small compared to the subgroup
H=\langle A\rangle
, as
{|A|}/{|H|}=({kz+1})/({2^{k}z})<({k+1})/{2^{k}}
.
Now we show that, in the notation of the previous example, we can choose
G
,
A
, and a
K
-approximate subgroup
A_{0}\subseteq G
containing
A
, such that
|A|/|A_{0}|>k/(k+1)
and
\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\leq\mathrm{Pr}(A_{0},G)
. Moreover the value of

\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)/\mathrm{Pr}(A_{0},G)

cannot be bounded away from zero in terms of
|A|/|A_{0}|
and
K
.

Let
G,A,Z
be as in Example
2.8
and let
A_{0}=Z\cup A
. Note that
A^{2}\subseteq BA
and
A_{0}^{2}\subseteq BA_{0}
, where
B=\{1,g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\}
, so both
A
and
A_{0}
are
K
-approximate subgroups.

Note that
|A|=kz+1
and
|A_{0}|=(k+1)z
, where
z=|Z|
, and so
|A|/|A_{0}|=(kz+1)/(k+1)z>k/(k+1)
.
We know from the calculations in Example
2.8
that

\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)<\frac{kz+n}{kzn}=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{kz}.

Moreover, as
A_{0}=A\cup Z
and
|C_{G}(y)|/|G|\geq\frac{1}{n}
for every
y\in A
(see Example
2.8
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(A_{0},G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A_{0}|}\left(\sum_{y\in Z}\frac{|C_{G}(y)|}{|G|}+\sum_{1\neq y\in A}\frac{|C_{G}(y)|}{|G|}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k+1)z}\left(z+\frac{kz}{n}\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}.

Therefore

(3)

\frac{\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)}{\mathrm{Pr}(A_{0},G)}<\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{kz}\right)\left(k+1\right).

If
k
is fixed and the group
G
is chosen with
n
and
|U|=z/2
arbitrary large, the righthand side of (
3
) becomes arbitrary small.

3.
Proof of the main results

We start with two preliminary results, the first one being purely group theoretical.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
, and let
G
be a finite group containing a subgroup
B
such that
[G:C_{G}(x)]\leq m
for all
x\in B
. Then there is a normal subgroup
T\leq G
such that the index
[G:T]
and the order of the commutator subgroup
[T,B]
are
m
-bounded.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(B,G)\geq\frac{1}{m}.

By Proposition 1.2 of
[
3
]
there exists a normal subgroup
R
of
m
-bounded index in
G
and a subgroup
U
of
m
-bounded index in
B
such that
[R,U]
has
m
-bounded order. By Remark 2.6 in
[
3
]
, the normal closure
[R,U]^{G}
has
m
-bounded order.

Passing to the quotient over
[R,U]^{G}
, we can assume that
R\leq C_{G}(U)
.
Take
m
-boundedly many elements
b_{1},\dots,b_{r}
in
B
such that
B=\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{r},U\rangle
. Since
[G:C_{G}(b_{i})]\leq m
for every
i=1,\dots,r
and
C_{G}(U)
has
m
-bounded index in
G
, the intersection
C
of
C_{G}(U)
and all
r
subgroups
C_{G}(b_{i})
has
m
-bounded index in
G
. So
C
contains a normal subgroup
T
of
G
of

m
-bounded index with
[T,B]=1
. This concludes the proof.∎

The following lemma tells us that if
A
is an approximate subgroup and
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}
are elements of
G
having few
A
-conjugates, then any element of the subgroup
\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\rangle
has few
A
-conjugates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
A
a
K
-approximate subgroup of
G
, and
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\in G
elements such that
|g_{i}^{A}|\leq m
for every
i=1,\dots,s
. Then there exists a
(K,m,s)
-bounded integer
u
such that
|g^{A}|\leq u
for every
g\in\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\rangle.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that there exists a
(K,m,s)
-bounded integer
u
and elements
d_{1},\dots,d_{u}\in G
such that

(4)

A\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq u}C_{A^{2^{s}}}(g_{1},\dots,g_{s})d_{i}.

Use induction on
s
.
Let
s=1
and
g_{1}^{A}=\{g_{1}^{a_{1}},\dots,g_{1}^{a_{m}}\}
. If
a\in A
, then there exists
k
such that
g_{1}^{a}=g_{1}^{a_{k}}
, thus
aa_{k}^{-1}\in C_{A^{2}}(g_{1})
and
a=(aa_{k}^{-1})a_{k}
. Hence,

A\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq m}C_{A^{2}}(g_{1})a_{i},

as desired.

Now assume that the result is true for
s-1
, that is, there are
(K,m,s)
-boundedly many elements
h_{1},\dots,h_{v}\in G
such that

A\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq v}C_{A^{2^{s-1}}}(g_{1},\dots,g_{s-1})h_{i}.

Set
D=C_{A^{2^{s-1}}}(g_{1},\dots,g_{s-1})
. Note that by Lemma
2.5
the size of the class
g_{s}^{D}
is
(K,m,s)
-bounded. Write

g_{s}^{D}=\{g_{s}^{b_{1}},\dots,g_{s}^{b_{r}}\}

for suitable
{b_{1}},\dots,{b_{r}}\in D
.

As above, it follows that
D\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq r}C_{D^{2}}(g_{s})b_{i}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle D\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq r}C_{D^{2}}(g_{s})b_{i}\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq r}C_{A^{2^{s}}}(g_{1},\dots,g_{s})b_{i}.

We know that

A\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq v}Dh_{i}
, whence (
4
) follows with
u=rv
.
This establishes the lemma.
∎

Built on the ideas of P. M. Neumann’s theorem
[
7
]
, we get the next proposition, which
contains the core of the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group containing a
K_{1}
-approximate subgroup
H
and a
K_{2}
-approximate subgroup
U
such that
\mathrm{Pr}(H,U)\geq\epsilon>0
. Then there exists a symmetric subset
X
of
H
, with
1\in X
,
and two positive numbers
K_{0}
and
m
depending only on
K_{1},K_{2}
and
\epsilon
such that

•

|X|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}|H|
,

•

X^{2}
is a
K_{0}
-approximate subgroup of
G
,

•

|X^{2}|\leq K_{0}|X|
,

•

|y^{U}|\leq m
for every
y\in\langle X^{2}\rangle
.

Proof.

Set

X=\{x\in H\mid|x^{U}|\leq 2K_{2}/\epsilon\}=\{x\in H\mid 1/|x^{U}|\geq\epsilon/(2K_{2})\}.

As
|U^{2}|\leq K_{2}|U|
, from Lemma
2.4
(a) it follows that, for any
g\in H\setminus X
,

|C_{U}(g)|\leq\frac{K_{2}|U|}{|g^{U}|}\leq\frac{\epsilon K_{2}|U|}{2K_{2}}=\frac{\epsilon|U|}{2},

whence

\displaystyle\epsilon|H|\,|U|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\{(x,y)\in H\times U\mid xy=yx\}|=\sum_{x\in H}|C_{U}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}|U|+\sum_{x\in H\setminus X}\frac{\epsilon}{2}|U|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|X||U|+(|H|-|X|)\frac{\epsilon}{2}|U|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|X||U|+\frac{\epsilon}{2}|H|\,|U|.

Therefore
({\epsilon}/{2})|H|\leq|X|
, that is,

|X|\geq\alpha|H|

for
\alpha={\epsilon}/{2}
.
Thus,

|X^{2}|\leq|H^{2}|\leq K_{1}|H|\leq(K_{1}/\alpha)|X|

and also

|X^{3}|\leq|H^{3}|\leq K_{1}^{2}|H|\leq(K_{1}^{2}/\alpha)|X|,

hence
X
has tripling
K_{1}^{2}/\alpha
.

It follows that
B=X^{2}
is a
K_{0}
-approximate subgroup where
K_{0}
depends on
K_{1}
and
\alpha
only (see Proposition 2.5.5 in
[
10
]
).

Note that
|y^{U}|\leq(K_{2}/\alpha)^{2}
for every
y\in B
.

Let
E
be a minimal subset of
G
such that
B^{2}\subseteq EB
and
|E|\leq K_{0}
.
By minimality of
E
, for every element
e\in E
there are
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B
such that
b_{1}b_{2}=eb_{3}
and so every element
e\in E
can be written as a product of at most
3
elements of
B
.
Therefore
|e^{U}|\leq(K_{2}/\alpha)^{6}
for every
e\in E
.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
that there exists a
(K_{0},K_{2},\alpha)
-bounded integer
n
such that
|g^{U}|\leq n
for every
g\in\langle E\rangle.
Note that
B^{i}\leq\langle E\rangle B
for every
i\geq 2
, and so there exists a
(K_{0},K_{2},\alpha)
-bounded integer
m
such that
|y^{U}|\leq m
for every
y\in\langle B\rangle
.
As
K_{0}
and
\alpha
depend only on
K_{1}
and
\epsilon
, the proof is complete.
∎

Now we are ready to proceed with the proof of our main results. For the reader’s convenience we restate Theorem
1.1
here.

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
A
be a
K
-approximate subgroup of a finite group
G
such that
\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\geq\epsilon>0
.
There are two
(\epsilon,K)
-bounded positive numbers
\gamma
and
K_{0}
such that
G
contains a normal subgroup
T
and a
K_{0}
-approximate subgroup
B
such that

(i)

|A\cap B|\geq\gamma\;{\mathrm{max}}\{|A|,|B|\}
, and

(ii)

the index
[G:T]
and the order of the subgroup
[T,\langle B\rangle]
are both
(\epsilon,K)
-bounded.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
3.3
with
H=A
,
U=G
,
K_{1}=K
and
K_{2}=1
.
Deduce that there exists a subset
X
of
A
and two
(K,\epsilon)
-bounded numbers
K_{0}
and
m
such that

B=X^{2}
is a
K_{0}
-approximate subgroup while
|B\cap A|\geq|X|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}|A|
and
|y^{G}|\leq m
for every
y\in\langle B\rangle
.
Note also that

|B\cap A|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}|A|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2K}|A^{2}|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2K}|B|,

so we can take
\gamma={\epsilon}/({2K})
.

It follows from Lemma
3.1
applied to the subgroup
\langle B\rangle
that there exists a normal subgroup
T\leq G
such that the index
[G:T]
and the order of the commutator subgroup
[T,\langle B\rangle]
are
m
-bounded.
Since
m
is
(K,\epsilon)
-bounded, the result follows.
∎

We now deal with Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
1.2
.

If
A
is a
K
-approximate subgroup of a finite group
G
satisfying
\mathrm{Pr}(A,A)\geq\epsilon>0
, then there are two
(\epsilon,K)
-bounded positive numbers
\gamma
and
s
, and a subgroup
C\leq G
such that
|C\cap A^{2}|>\gamma|A|
and
|C^{\prime}|\leq s
. Moreover,
A
is contained in the union of at most
\gamma^{-1}K^{2}
left cosets of the group
C
.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
3.3
with
H=U=A
and
K_{1}=K_{2}=K
and deduce that there exists a subset
X
of
A
and two positive numbers
K_{0}
and
m
depending only on
K
and
\epsilon
such that
|X|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}|A|
, the set
B=X^{2}
is a
K_{0}
-approximate subgroup, and
|y^{A}|\leq m
for every
y\in\langle B\rangle
.

As
|B^{2}|\leq K_{0}|B|
, it follows from Lemma
2.4
(b) that for every
y\in\langle B\rangle
we have

|C_{B}(y)|\geq\frac{|B|}{K_{0}|y^{X}|}\geq\frac{|B|}{K_{0}|y^{A}|}\geq\eta|B|,

where
\eta=1/({K_{0}m})
.
Since

|C_{B}(y)|\geq\eta|B|,

for every
y\in\langle B\rangle
, we have

\mathrm{Pr}(B,\langle B\rangle)=\frac{1}{|\langle B\rangle|}\sum_{y\in\langle B\rangle}\frac{|C_{B}(y)|}{|B|}\geq\eta.

Now we apply Proposition
3.3
with
H=B
and
U=\langle B\rangle
, where
K_{1}=K_{0}
and
K_{2}=1
, to deduce that there exists a
symmetric
subset
Y
of
B
and two positive numbers
K_{3}
and
n
depending only on
K_{0}
and
\eta
such that
|Y|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}|B|
and
Y^{2}
is a
K_{3}
-approximate subgroup satisfying
|y^{\langle B\rangle}|\leq n
for every
y\in\langle Y^{2}\rangle
.

It follows from Theorem 1.1 in
[
8
]
that the commutator subgroup of the subgroup
\langle(Y^{2})^{\langle B\rangle}\rangle
has
(K_{3},n)
-bounded order. As
\langle Y^{2}\rangle=\langle Y\rangle
, we deduce that the order of the commutator subgroup of
\langle Y\rangle
is
(K_{3},n)
-bounded.

Taking into account that
Y\subseteq B=X^{2}\subseteq A^{2}
, write

|A|\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon}|X|\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon}|X^{2}|\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{2}{\eta}|Y|\right),

and

(5)

|\langle Y\rangle\cap A^{2}|\geq|Y\cap A^{2}|=|Y|\geq\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4}|A|=\gamma|A|,

for
\gamma=\epsilon\eta/4
.

Finally, as

|AY|\leq|A^{3}|\leq K^{2}|A|\leq K^{2}\frac{4}{\epsilon\eta}|Y|,

it follows from
[
10
, Lemma 2.4.4]
(Ruzsa’s covering lemma) that there exists a subset
F\subseteq A
with
|F|\leq 4K^{2}/(\epsilon\eta)
such that
A\subseteq FY^{2}
.
Therefore
A
is contained in the union of at most
4K^{2}/(\epsilon\eta)
left cosets of
\langle Y\rangle
(note that this follows also from (
5
) and Lemma 7.1.5 in
[
10
]
).
∎

4.
The converse statements

In this section we prove Proposition
1.3
and Proposition
1.4
, which are roughly converse to Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, respectively.

We will also often use without mention the fact that if
S,T
are subsets of a finite group
G
and
g\in S
then
|g^{T}|\leq|[S,T]|
, because the map
g^{T}\to\{[g,x]\,|x\in T\}
defined by
g^{x}\mapsto g^{-1}g^{x}
is a bijection.

Proposition
1.3
.
Let
A,B
be subsets and
T
a subgroup of a finite group
G
. Set
\gamma=|A\cap B|/|A|
,
n=[G:T]
and

m=|[T,\langle B\rangle]|
. Then
\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)\geq\frac{\gamma}{nm}
.

Proof.

Note that

|A\cap\langle B\rangle|\geq|A\cap B|\geq\gamma|A|,

so
|A\cap\langle B\rangle|/|A|\geq\gamma
and without loss of generality we can assume that
B
is a subgroup.
Moreover, if
g\in A\cap B
, then

\displaystyle[G:C_{G}(g)]\leq[G:T]\,[T:C_{T}(G)]\leq nm,

since
|[T,\langle B\rangle]|=m
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(A,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{a\in A}\frac{|C_{G}(a)|}{|G|}\geq\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{g\in A\cap B}\frac{|C_{G}(g)|}{|G|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{|A\cap B|}{nm|A|}\geq\frac{\gamma}{nm}.

∎

Proposition
1.4
.

Let
A
be a
K
-approximate subgroup of a finite group
G
, and let
C\leq G
be a subgroup. Set
\gamma=|C\cap A^{2}|/|A|
and
s=|C^{\prime}|
.
Then

\mathrm{Pr}(A^{2},A^{2})\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{K^{4}s}.

Proof.

Since
|C\cap A^{2}|=\gamma|A|
,
it follows from
[
10
, Lemma 7.1.5]
that
A
is contained in the union of
n\leq\gamma^{-1}K^{2}
left cosets of
C
, say

A\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{i}C.

As
A
is symmetric, for every
a\in A
we have
a^{-1}\in\bigcup_{i}f_{i}C
, whence
a=(f_{i}c)^{-1}=c^{-1}f_{i}^{-1}
for some
i\leq n
and
c\in C
. It follows that

A\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}Cf_{i}^{-1}.

Therefore, for every
g\in G
we have

|g^{A}|\leq|g^{\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}Cf_{i}^{-1}}|\leq\sum_{i-1}^{n}|(g^{C})^{f_{i}^{-1}}|=n|g^{C}|.

When
g\in C\cap A^{2}
we also have
|g^{C}|\leq s
, since
|C^{\prime}|=s
,
hence

(6)

\frac{1}{|g^{A}|}\geq\frac{1}{ns}.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.4
(b),
{|g^{A}|}\geq|A|/{|C_{A^{2}}(g)|}\geq|A^{2}|/(K{|C_{A^{2}}(g)|})
, which gives

(7)

\frac{|C_{A^{2}}(g)|}{|A^{2}|}\geq\frac{1}{K|g^{A}|}.

Now, by (
7
) and (
6
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(A^{2},A^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A^{2}|}\sum_{a\in A^{2}}\frac{|C_{A^{2}}(a)|}{|A^{2}|}\geq\frac{1}{|A^{2}|}\sum_{g\in C\cap A^{2}}\frac{|C_{A^{2}}(g)|}{|A^{2}|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A^{2}|}\sum_{g\in C\cap A^{2}}\frac{1}{K|g^{A}|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{|C\cap A^{2}|}{Kns|A^{2}|}.

Moreover, as
|A^{2}|\leq K|A|
,

|C\cap A^{2}|=\gamma|A|\geq\gamma|A^{2}|/K.

Therefore

\mathrm{Pr}(A^{2},A^{2})\geq\frac{|C\cap A^{2}|}{Kns|A^{2}|}\geq\frac{\gamma}{K^{2}ns}.

Since
n\leq\gamma^{-1}K^{2}
, we conclude that
\mathrm{Pr}(A^{2},A^{2})\geq{\gamma^{2}}/({K^{4}s}),
as claimed.
∎

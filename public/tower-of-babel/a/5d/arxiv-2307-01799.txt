Multiparameter universality and intrinsic diversity of critical phenomena in weakly anisotropic systems
Volker Dohm

Abstract
Recently a unified hypothesis of multiparameter universality for the critical behavior of bulk and confined anisotropic systems has been formulated [V. Dohm, Phys. Rev. E 97 , 062128 (2018)]. We prove the validity of this hypothesis in d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 dimensions on the basis of the principle of two-scale-factor universality for isotropic systems. We introduce an angular-dependent correlation vector and a generalized shear transformation that transforms weakly anisotropic systems to isotropic systems. As examples we consider the O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) -symmetric φ 4 \varphi^{4} model, Gaussian model, and n n -vector model. By means of the inverse of the shear transformation we determine the general structure of the bulk order-parameter correlation function, of the singular bulk part of the critical free energy, and of critical bulk amplitude relations of anisotropic systems at and away from T c T_{c} . It is shown that weakly anisotropic systems exhibit a high degree of intrinsic diversity due to d ⁡ ( d + 1 ) / 2 − 1 d(d+1)/2-1 independent parameters that cannot be determined by thermodynamic measurements. Exact results are derived for the d = 2 d=2 Ising universality class and for the spherical and Gaussian universality classes in d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 dimensions. For the d = 3 d=3 Ising universality class we identify the universal scaling function of the isotropic bulk correlation function from the nonuniversal result of the functional renormalization group. A proof is presented for the validity of multiparameter universality of the exact critical free energy and critical Casimir amplitude in a finite rectangular geometry of weakly anisotropic systems with periodic boundary conditions in the Ising universality class. This confirms the validity of recent predictions of self-similar structures of finite-size effects in the ( d = 2 , n = 1 ) (d=2,n=1) universality class at T = T c T=T_{c} derived from conformal field theory [V. Dohm and S. Wessel, Phys. Rev. Lett. 126 , 060601 (2021)]. This also substantiates the previous notion of an effective shear transformation for anisotropic two-dimensional Ising models. Our theory paves the way for a quantitative theory of nonuniversal critical Casimir forces in anisotropic superconductors for which experiments have been proposed by G.A. Williams, Phys. Rev. Lett. 92 ,197003 (2004).

\displaystyle H^{G}_{\text{field}}=\int_{V}d^{d}x\Big[\frac{r_{0}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\frac{A_{\alpha\beta}}{2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{\beta}}\Big]\;\;\;\;\;\;

(4.58)

with the same
anisotropy matrix
{\bf A}
(
2.3
) as for the
\varphi^{4}
model. Correspondingly the isotropic Gaussian Hamiltonian obtained after the special shear transformation (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) reads

\displaystyle H^{\prime G}_{\text{field}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{V^{\prime}}d^{d}x^{\prime}\Big[\frac{r_{0}}{2}\varphi^{\prime}({\bf x}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime})^{2}\Big].\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

(4.59)

The advantage of the special shear transformation (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) is that the transformed Hamiltonian (
4.13
) has the form of the standard isotropic field-theoretic Landau-Ginzburg-Wilson Hamiltonian. This implies that the same renormalization factors (
Z
factors) can be employed for the transformed system as for the established isotropic
\varphi^{4}
field theory.
This has permitted us to perform renormalized perturbation theory
dohm2008
;
dohm2018
for the transformed isotropic system in
2<d<4
dimensions.

The disadvantage of this shear transformation is, however, that it is not directly applicable to other weakly anisotropic systems such as the
n
-vector model where it is unknown how to construct an appropriate continuum Hamiltonian with a large-distance anisotropy matrix as a function of the couplings
E_{i,j}
that plays the same role as
{\bf A}
in the
\varphi^{4}
theory. In the following we introduce a generalized shear transformation that does not need an anisotropy matrix and that is applicable to all weakly anisotropic systems.

IV.2
Generalized shear transformation of the
\varphi^{4}
theory

We aim at introducing a shear transformation of the
\varphi^{4}
theory in the scaling region that differs from the special shear transformation in that the explicit use of the anisotropy matrix
{\bf A}
is avoided.
The principal correlation lengths of the
\varphi^{4}
model above, at, and below
T_{c}
are
directed along the
T
-independent principal unit vectors
{\bf e}^{(\alpha)}
.
Accordingly we introduce the orthogonal set of
d

principal correlation vectors

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{\pm}^{(\alpha)}(t)={\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}|t|^{-\nu}=\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm}|t|^{-\nu}{\bf e}^{(\alpha)},\;\;\alpha=1,...,d\;\;\;

(4.60)

where
\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm}
are the amplitudes of the principal correlation lengths.
We use a Cartesian coordinate system with orthogonal unit vectors
{\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)}
,
\alpha=1,...,d
along the
d
Cartesian axes.
The shear transformation is achieved by a rotation of the vectors
{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}
by means of the orthogonal matrix
{\bf U}\big(\{{\bf e}^{(\alpha)}\}\big)

with matrix elements
U_{\alpha\beta}=e_{\beta}^{(\alpha)}
(which are the same as in the special shear transformation)
such that these vectors point along the direction of the Cartesian axes,

\displaystyle{\bf U}{\bf e}^{(\alpha)}={\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)},\;\;{\bf U}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}=\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm}{\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)},

(4.61)

and by a subsequent rescaling of their lengths by means of a diagonal matrix
{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}^{-1/2}
. This matrix can be chosen to be independent of the temperature, i.e., it can be chosen to be the same above, at, and below
T_{c}
as will be specified below.
This yields the
d
transformed correlation vectors

{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\xi}$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}={\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}^{-1/2}{\bf U}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}=\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{-1/2}\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm}{\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)}

(4.62)

where
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}

are the diagonal elements of
\widetilde{\lambda}
. To obtain a system that is isotropic in the scaling region it is necessary and sufficient that
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{1/2}
is proportional to
\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm}
,

\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{1/2}=\widetilde{c}_{\pm}\;\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm},

(4.63)

where
\widetilde{c}_{+}>0
and
\widetilde{c}_{-}>0

are independent of the direction
\alpha
.
This rescaling guarantees that the lengths
|{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\xi}$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}|
of the transformed correlation vectors (
4.62
)

|{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\xi}$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}|=|{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}^{-1/2}{\bf U}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}|=\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{-1/2}\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm}=\widetilde{c}_{\pm}^{-1}

(4.64)

become independent of the direction
\alpha
.
This implies that
isotropic correlations are obtained in the large-distance scaling regime where the isotropic lengths
\widetilde{c}_{\pm}^{-1}
are as yet unspecified.
The transformation (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) corresponds to the special choice
\widetilde{c}_{\pm}=(\xi^{\prime}_{0\pm})^{-1}

where
\xi^{\prime}_{0\pm}
is the correlation-length amplitude of the isotropic Hamiltonian
H_{\text{field}}^{\prime}
, (
4.13
) and thus depends on the parameters
a_{0}
and
(\det{\bf A})^{1/2}u_{0}
of the original anisotropic
\varphi^{4}
model through (
4.3
). For the explicit expression of
\xi^{\prime}_{0\pm}
see Eq. (2.82) of
dohm-arXiv
.

Here we make a more general and conceptually different choice of
\widetilde{c}_{\pm}

that is independent of any parameter of the original anisotropic system
.
We choose

\displaystyle\widetilde{c}_{+}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+},\;\;\;\widetilde{c}_{-}^{-1}=\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-},

(4.65)

thus

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\xi}$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0\pm}{\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)},\;\;\;|{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\xi}$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}|=\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0\pm},\;\;\alpha=1,...,d\;\;\;

(4.66)

where
\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}
and
\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}
are free parameters, together with the requirement that

\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}=X_{\xi}={\rm universal}.

(4.67)

This requirement is necessary in order to comply with the fact implied by two-scale-factor universality that the ratio of the correlation lengths above and below
T_{c}
of any isotropic system near

must satisfy the universal relation (
3.3
).
According to (
4.31

\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0+}/\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0-}=\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}

\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0-}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}=\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0+}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+},

(4.68)

thus we obtain from (
4.63
)-(
4.67
)
d
different
constants

\displaystyle\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0-}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}=\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0+}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}

(4.69)

which are the same above and below
T_{c}
. By continuity, this

-independent identification of
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}
is applicable also at
T=T_{c}
. This property is analogous to the relation (
4.32

T
-independent eigenvalues
\lambda_{\alpha}(K_{i,j})
of the anisotropy matrix
{\bf A}(K_{i,j})
can be expressed in terms of
\xi^{(\alpha)}_{0\pm}/\xi^{\prime}_{0\pm}
,
thus both diagonal matrices
\widetilde{\lambda}
and
\lambda
are
T
-independent and are related by

\widetilde{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\xi^{\prime}_{0-}}{\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}}\Bigg)^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}=\Bigg(\frac{\xi^{\prime}_{0+}}{\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}}\Bigg)^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}.

(4.70)

We note that the mean correlation length can be used to express
\det{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}
as

\displaystyle\det{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}=(\bar{\xi}_{0\pm}/\xi^{\rm iso}_{0\pm})^{2d}.

(4.71)

We apply this generalized shear transformation to the lattice vectors
{\bf x}
of the anisotropic system

\displaystyle{\bf\widetilde{x}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}^{-1/2}{\bf U}{\bf x},

(4.72)

with
\widetilde{\lambda}
given by (
4.69
). This generates lattice vectors
{\bf\widetilde{x}}
of
an isotropic system with the correlation-length amplitudes
\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0\pm}
in the scaling region. This transformation is applicable not only in the presence of finite correlations lengths
\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{\pm}(t)
for

but also right at

.
We may also consider the inverse shear transformation

(4.73)

Our generalized shear transformation differs significantly from the special transformation (

\varphi

Within this generalized transformation, we are primarily interested in determining the reduced anisotropy matrix
{\bf\bar{A}}^{\rm gen}={\bf U}^{-1}{\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{\rm gen}}{\bf U}
via the alternative definition given in (

) in terms of a

In this transformation the reduced rescaling matrix is defined by

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{\lambda}$}^{\rm gen}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}}{(\det{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}})^{1/d}}\;\;,

(4.74)

\displaystyle\det{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod^{d}_{\alpha=1}\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha},

(4.75)

where the diagonal elements
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}
of
\widetilde{\lambda}
are given by (
4.69
). The rescaling matrix
\widetilde{\lambda}
and the reduced rescaling matrix

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\widetilde{\lambda}$}}^{1/2}\xi^{\rm iso}_{0\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big({\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{\lambda}$}^{\rm gen}}\big)^{1/2}\bar{\xi}_{0\pm}.

(4.76)

We see that the dependence on
\xi^{\rm iso}_{0\pm}
is canceled in the diagonal matrix (
4.74
). This implies that the matrix (

) is the same as that defined in (
4.12
),

{\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{\lambda}$}^{\rm gen}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{\lambda}$}},

(4.77)

with the same

4.53

As a consequence, the reduced anisotropy matrix in the generalized shear transformation

{\bf\bar{A}}^{\rm gen}={\bf\bar{A}}\big(\{\xi_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)},{\bf e}^{(\alpha)}\}\big)

(4.78)

is identical with that given in (
4.56
) within the special shear transformation (
4.1
)-(
4.3

the generalized shear transformation is capable deriving the structure

{\bf\bar{A}}
of the anisotropic
\varphi^{4}

model without explicit knowledge of the anisotropy matrix
{\bf A}
.
Furthermore the generalized shear transformation is simpler than the special shear transformation in that it is a pure coordinate transformation. For this reason this
transformation is applicable also to other weakly anisotropic systems.

The generalized shear transformation introduced above for the
\varphi^{4}

-limit of the
\varphi^{4}
theory
and to the Gaussian model for

4.13

Generalized shear transformation of the
{\bf n}
-vector model

As a representative of weakly anisotropic systems other than the

n
-vector Hamiltonian
H^{\rm sp}
, (
2.22

d

n

model

(d,n)

\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0\pm}

and of the principal unit vectors

{\bf e}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}

E_{i,j}

Our introduction of the generalized shear transformation of the
n

4.60
) - (
4.73

Accordingly we introduce the orthogonal set of
d

{\bf\widehat{U}}={\bf U}\big(\{{\bf e}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\}\big)

(4.82)

with matrix elements
\widehat{U}_{\alpha\beta}=e_{\beta}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle{\bf\widehat{U}}{\bf e}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}={\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)},\;\;{\bf\widehat{U}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0\pm}{\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)}.

(4.83)

As for the
\varphi^{4}
model, the orthogonal matrix
{\bf\widehat{U}}
contains
d(d-1)/2
independent matrix elements which are needed to specify the directions

of the principal axes. These

mutually orthogonal axes can be described by

independent angles
\Omega_{i}

\Omega
in two dimensions, three angles in three dimensions, six angles in four dimensions, etc.,

\displaystyle{\bf U}\big(\{{\bf e}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\}\big)={\bf U}\big(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3},...\big).

(4.84)

A subsequent rescaling of the lengths
\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0\pm}
by means of a diagonal matrix

{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\lambda}$}}^{-1/2}

yields the
d
transformed vectors

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\xi}$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\lambda}$}}^{-1/2}{\bf\widehat{U}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}=\widehat{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{-1/2}\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0\pm}{\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)}.

(4.85)

Here we make the choice, similar to (
4.63
),

\widehat{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{1/2}=\widetilde{c}_{\pm}\;\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0\pm},

(4.86)

together with the same choice for
\widetilde{c}_{\pm}
as specified in (
4.65
) and (
4.67
), with free parameters
\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0\pm}
. Because of (
4.67
) and (
4.80
) this implies

\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0+}/\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0-}=\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}
or

\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0-}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}=\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0+}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+},

(4.87)

thus the diagonal elements of
\widehat{\lambda}
are defined to be
T
-independent parameters given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0-}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}\Big)^{2}=\Big(\xi^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}_{0+}/\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}\Big)^{2},

(4.88)

similar to (
4.32
) and (
4.69
). This transforms the

different principal correlation vectors (

d
vectors
{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\xi}$}}_{0\pm}^{(\alpha)}=\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0\pm}{\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}}^{(\alpha)}
with the angular-independent lengths
\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0+}
and
\xi^{\text{ iso}}_{0-}
above and below
T_{c}
, respectively, representing an isotropic system.

Correspondingly we define the
T
-independent

at fixed couplings

of the anisotropic
n
-vector model by

\displaystyle{\bf\widehat{x}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\lambda}$}}^{-1/2}{\bf\widehat{U}}{\bf x},

(4.89)

with
\widehat{\lambda}
given by (
4.88
) and

defined by (

). This generates an
n
-vector model on a transformed lattice with lattice points

and with isotropic correlations characterized by the correlation length

for which two-scale-factor universality can be invoked.
In constructing this transformation no assumption has been made other than the existence of the principal correlation lengths (
4.79
) and, for the application to

, the universality of the amplitude relation (
4.80
).

Now we have arrived at the position to derive the structure of the reduced anisotropy matrix
{\bf\bar{A}}^{\rm sp}
that will enter the anisotropic correlation function
G^{\rm sp}({\bf x},t)
of the
n
-vector model to be derived in Sec. V. B. We define the reduced rescaling matrix

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{\lambda}$}}^{\rm sp}\big(\{\xi_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\lambda}$}}/\big(\det{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\lambda}$}}\big)^{1/d}\;\;

(4.90)

which has the diagonal elements

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}^{\rm sp}_{\alpha}=\prod^{d}_{\beta=1,\;\beta\neq\alpha}\Big(\frac{\xi_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}}{\xi_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\beta)}}\Big)^{2/d}=\Big(\frac{\xi_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}}{\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0\pm}}\Big)^{2}\;\;\;

with the amplitude
\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0\pm}
of the mean correlation length

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{\pm}(t)=\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0\pm}|t|^{-\nu},\;\;\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0\pm}=\big[\prod^{d}_{\alpha=1}\xi_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\big]^{1/d},

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0+}/\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0-}=X_{\xi}.

(4.93)

4.91

) and (

\displaystyle\det{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\lambda}$}}=(\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0\pm}/\xi^{\rm iso}_{0\pm})^{2d}

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\widehat{\lambda}$}}^{1/2}\xi^{\rm iso}_{0\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big({\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{\lambda}$}}^{\rm sp}\big)^{1/2}\bar{\xi}^{\rm sp}_{0\pm}.

(4.95)

\displaystyle{\bf\bar{A}}^{\rm sp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\bf U}^{-1}{\bf\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}}^{\rm sp}\widehat{\bf U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\bar{A}}\big(\{\xi_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)},{\bf e}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\}\big)

(4.97)

\displaystyle{\bf U(\{{\bf e}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\})}^{-1}{\bf\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}}^{\rm sp}\big(\{\xi_{0\pm}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\}\big){\bf U(\{{\bf e}^{{\rm sp}(\alpha)}\})}\;\;\;\;\;

in (

) for the

model but with the arguments

and

and

.

theory without explicit knowledge of an anisotropy matrix
{\bf A}
as a function of the couplings.

This means that we have identified a temperature-independent universal structure of the reduced anisotropy matrix

whose dependence either on the nonuniversal ratios

and nonuniversal principal unit vectors

or on

model and the

-vector model. We have shown that for the construction of this reduced anisotropy matrix the existence and knowledge of an anisotropy matrix
{\bf A}
is not required, thus our construction of the structure of
{\bf\bar{A}}
is applicable to any weakly anisotropic system. This is an important ingredient for the general validity of multiparameter universality of weakly anisotropic systems.

V
Multiparameter universality of the anisotropic
correlation function

Besides the bulk free energy density the most fundamental physical quantity describing the critical fluctuations is the bulk order-parameter correlation function.
However, in the traditional theory of critical phenomena
fish-1
;
bre-1
;
pelissetto
;
priv
;
pri
;
hohenberg1976
;
cardybuch
;
zinn2007
including the nonperturbative functional renormalization group
metzner2021
;
hassel2007
;
sinner2008
the issue of the general universality properties of weakly anisotropic correlation functions was not addressed. Only special anisotropic
models were studied whose bulk correlation functions
bruce
;
Vaidya1976
;
WuCoy
;
CoyWu
;
Perk1
;
Perk2
;
Perk3
;
Perk4
were presented in a nonuniversal form.
Recently a general scaling form of the anisotropic bulk correlation function of the
O(n)
-symmetric
d
-dimensional
\varphi^{4}
model has been presented
dohm2018
;
dohm2019
in terms of a reduced anisotropy matrix

{\bf\bar{A}}

which was found to violate two-scale-factor universality in both two and three dimensions.
Instead, within the
\varphi^{4}
theory, these results exhibit the feature of multiparameter
universality
dohm2018

and it was hypothesized that this structure is valid quite generally for weakly anisotropic systems beyond
\varphi^{4}
models.
In this section we prove the validity of this hypothesis for the bulk correlation function and point to the intrinsic diversity of this scaling structure.

V.1
Anisotropic correlation function
of the
\varphi^{4}
theory

In

;

\displaystyle G_{\pm}({\bf x},t)=\big(\xi^{\prime}_{0\pm}/\bar{\xi}_{0\pm}\big)^{d}\;G_{\pm}^{\prime}(|{\bf x^{\prime}}|,t),

VI
Multiparameter universality of critical bulk amplitude relations

The universal critical point bulk amplitude relations play an important role in the traditional theory of critical
phenomena
priv
where, however, no attention has been paid to amplitude
relations in the subclass of weakly anisotropic systems within a universality class.
The failure of two-scale-factor universality in this subclass was pointed out in
cd2004
. Subsequently several bulk amplitude relations have been shown to be valid within the weakly anisotropic bulk
\varphi^{4}
theory in
2<d<4
dimensions

;
dohm2008
;
dohm2018
;
dohm2019
with the same universal constants as for the isotropic case, such as

but where, in general, up to
d(d+1)/2+1
independent nonuniversal parameters are involved rather than only two nonuniversal parameters. These properties were called multiparameter universality
dohm2008
for bulk amplitude relations. It was hypothesized
dohm2018
that these properties are valid not only for
\varphi^{4}
models but also for all weakly anisotropic bulk systems other than
\varphi^{4}
models. So far no proof exists for this hypothesis. Our generalized shear transformation introduced in Sec. IV enables us to present such a proof. As a representative of a weakly anisotropic system other than the
\varphi^{4}
model we take the
n
-vector model.

IX.2
Critical Casimir forces in anisotropic superconductors

An experimental verification of critical Casimir forces
has been achieved so far only in isotropic systems, most prominently in superfluid
4
He films
garcia
.
It has been pointed out by Williams
wil-1
that a measurable critical Casimir force should occur also in superconducting films. Superconductors belong to the same universality class as superfluid
4
He and have the same (Dirichlet) boundary conditions but are anisotropic. Williams’ scenario is the following: a superconducting film is connected to a bulk sample of the same material. He argues that below the bulk critical temperature the film-bulk-system can lower its free energy by a transfer of electrons (Cooper pairs) from the film to the bulk system which is analogous to helium atoms moving from the film to the superfluid bulk reservoir. While in the helium system this leads to a thinning of the film the corresponding effect in the superconducting film-bulk-system is a transfer of negative electrical charge from the film to the bulk system. Williams
wil-1
argues that this gives rise to an electrical potential difference which can be related to the free-energy difference (per unit area) between the film and the bulk and from which a Casimir force can be derived. He estimates the voltage difference to have a measurable magnitude.

The critical Casimir force is an observable only if the ordering degrees of freedom can enter and leave the system. Therefore it has been claimed in the literature
toldin
;
diehl2009
;
diehl2010
;
DDreview
that this force can be active only in isotropic fluids and that the issue of spatial anisotropy is not relevant in the context of the critical Casimir force. We argue that, unlike the localized degrees of freedom (magnetic moments) of the order parameter of an anisotropic magnetic material, the ordering degrees of freedom (Cooper pairs) of a superconductor are not localized at lattice points but play the role of an electrical superfluid in an
anisotropic
environment that can leave and enter the film connected to the bulk of the same material, as anticipated by Williams
wil-1
. So far no specific objection has been raised in the literature against this specific argumentation for superconductors, and in a comment
comment
on
wil-1
the measurability of the critical Casimir force in superconductors has not been questioned. Furthermore, we point to the largely unexplored area of thermodynamic Casimir forces in liquid crystals
singh
which exhibit a wide variety of spatial anisotropy and whose ordering degrees of freedom can leave and enter the system.

In closing we note that experimental studies, Monte Carlo simulations, and further theoretical research are called for in view of the fact that at present no experimental or Monte Carlo data and no analytic predictions are available for the critical Casimir force in anisotropic systems with realistic boundary conditions. Also theoretical efforts based on the functional renormalization group
metzner2021
applied to anisotropic confined systems could yield important contributions to this matter. Analytic results for the critical Casimir force in anisotropic films of finite thickness have been presented previously
dohm2018
for the case of periodic boundary conditions which refute earlier results
wil-1
where no anisotropy effect in anisotropic superconductors near
T_{c}
was found. An analytic renormalization-group study in three dimensions with realistic Dirichlet boundary conditions below
T_{c}
without adjustable parameters was performed
dohm2014
that explains the depth and position of the deep minimum of the Casimir force scaling function observed in isotropic
4
He films
garcia
in the temperature regime
T_{\rm c,film}<T<T_{c}
on a semiquantitative level. It is conceivable that this isotropic study can be extended to anisotropic film systems with Dirichlet boundary conditions which could lead to quantitative predictions of the critical Casimir force in real superconductors.

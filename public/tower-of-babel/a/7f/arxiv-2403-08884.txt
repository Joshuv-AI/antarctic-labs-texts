A note on spectral properties of random S S -adic systems Thanks: The research of Solomyak was supported by the Israel Science Foundation grant #1647/23
Boris Solomyak with an appendix by Pascal Hubert and Carlos Matheus

Abstract
. The paper is concerned with random S S -adic systems arising from an i.i.d. sequence of unimodular substitutions. Using equidistribution results of Benoist and Quint, we show in Theorem 3.3 that, under some natural assumptions, if the Lyapunov exponent of the spectral cocycle is strictly less than 1/2 of the Lyapunov exponent of the random walk on S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) SL(2,{\mathbb{R}}) driven by the sequence of substitution matrices, then almost surely the spectrum of the S S -adic ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} -action is singular with respect to any (fixed in advance) continuous measure. Finally, the appendix discusses the weak-mixing property for random S S -adic systems associated to the family of substitutions introduced in Example 4.1.

1.
Introduction

The paper is devoted to the spectral theory of
S
-adic dynamical systems. They are
closely related to
Bratteli-Vershik
(BV) transformations, introduced by A. M. Vershik
[
50
,
51
]
(who called them “adic”), see also
[
52
]
, as universal models of ergodic measure-preserving
systems.
A
substitution
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action
is defined as the shift on the space of sequences obtained by a repeated iteration of a single substitution on a
finite alphabet. Ferenczi
[
35
]
introduced a generalization, in which a sequence of (possibly different) substitutions is applied at each step, in a predetermined order; he called such a
system
S
-adic
. Under some restrictions (most importantly,
recognizability
, see Definition
2.3
below), every
S
-adic
system is measurably isomorphic to a “natural” BV-system, for any fully supported invariant measure, see
[
16
, Theorem 6.5]
.

The literature on Bratteli-Vershik transformations,

S
-adic systems, and their spectral properties is vast; we mention a few papers, but do not attempt to provide an exhaustive survey.
For the background on BV transformations as models for Cantor minimal systems,
see
[
40
,
32
]
and the recent book
[
33
]
; for
S
-adic systems, see
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, as well as
[
33
, 6.4]
.
A detailed analysis of eigenvalues (both measurable and continuous) for Cantor minimal systems
is given in
[
31
]
; see also
[
21
]
and references therein for earlier work on this topic. Often BV systems and/or
S
-adic systems are studied with a motivation
coming from a specific application: e.g., to interval exchange
transformations (IET’s) and translation flows
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
4
]
, or to multi-dimensional continued fraction algorithms and symbolic coding of toral translations

[
15
,
17
]
.
See also
[
19
,
28
,
1
,
30
]

for the study of some special families of
S
-adic systems.
Quite often, individual systems are hard to analyze, and instead one tries to obtain results for “almost every,” in an appropriate sense, system in a particular class.
A famous example of this kind is a theorem of Avila and Forni
[
3
]
saying that almost every IET, that is not a rotation, is weakly mixing. (This was recently upgraded to
“quantitative weak mixing with a polynomial rate” for non-rotation class IET’s in
[
4
]
.)

In this note, following
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, we consider
random
S
-adic systems
.
This means that a sequence of substitutions is chosen randomly from a given “alphabet”; in general, the choice is driven by some ergodic stochastic process, but often it is i.i.d.
Recently, spectral properties of random
S
-adic systems
have been investigated
in several works; among them we should point out the paper
[
17
]
by Berthé, Steiner, and Thuswaldner, where
the emphasis is on the generalized Pisot property and pure discrete spectrum. In contrast, in
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
we were motivated by the study
of translation flows on surfaces of higher genus and IET’s, which are typically weakly mixing.
Earlier, random 2-sided BV-systems were introduced by Bufetov
[
22
,
23
]
, who used them to obtain limit theorems for translation flows on flat surfaces.
There is also work on the spectral properties of “
S
-adic tiling systems”

[
44
,
49
]
.

Our goal here is to extend the results of
[
27
]
, which gave an effective (at least, in some cases) sufficient condition for singularity of the spectrum for substitution

{\mathbb{Z}}
-actions, to the setting of random
S
-adic transformations.
This condition is stated in terms of the top Lyapunov exponent of the spectral (“twisted”) cocycle, introduced in
[
25
]
and extensively studied in recent years.
The main new idea is an application of powerful
equidistribution results, due to Benoist and Quint
[
10
]
. As a bonus, we prove that, under our assumptions,
almost every random
S
-adic system is not just pure singular, but is disjoint from any (chosen in advance) weakly mixing measure-preserving
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action.
We illustrate this result on a class of examples, where the computations were done “by hand”. Using numerical methods on a computer, one should be able to considerably expand the class of
examples, for which this method is applicable.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Substitutions and
S
-adic systems

For the background on substitutions and associated dynamical systems see, e.g.,
[
46
,
36
]
. In describing our set-up we closely follow
[
25
,
26
]
.
Consider the
alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}=\{1,\ldots,d\}
, and denote by
{\mathcal{A}}^{+}
the set of finite (non-empty) words with letters in
{\mathcal{A}}
. A
substitution
is a map
\zeta:\,{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{+}
, extended to
{\mathcal{A}}^{+}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
by concatenation.
The
substitution matrix
is defined by

(2.1)

{\sf S}_{\zeta}(i,j)=\mbox{number of symbols}\ i\ \mbox{in the word}\ \zeta(j).

Let
{\mathfrak{A}}
be the set of substitutions
\zeta
on
{\mathcal{A}}
with the property that all letters appear in the set of words
\{\zeta(a):\,a\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
and there exists
a
such that
|\zeta(a)|>1
.
Consider
{\bf a}=(\zeta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, a 1-sided sequence of substitutions on
{\mathcal{A}}
, called a
directive sequence
. We denote

\zeta^{[n]}:=\zeta_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\zeta_{n},\ \ n\geq 1.

Recall that
{\sf S}_{\zeta_{1}\circ\zeta_{2}}={\sf S}_{\zeta_{1}}{\sf S}_{\zeta_{2}}
.
We will sometimes write

{\sf S}_{j}:={\sf S}_{\zeta_{j}}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ {\sf S}^{[n]}:={\sf S}_{\zeta^{[n]}}.

We will also consider subwords of the sequence
{\bf a}
and the corresponding substitutions obtained by composition. Denote

(2.2)

{\sf S}_{\bf q}={\sf S}_{n}\cdots{\sf S}_{\ell}\ \ \ \mbox{for}\ \ {\bf q}=\zeta_{n}\ldots\zeta_{\ell}.

Given
{\bf a}
, let
X_{{\bf a}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the subspace of all two-sided sequences whose every subword appears as a subword of

\zeta^{[n]}(b)
for some
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
n\geq 1
. Let
T
be the left shift on
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
; then
(X_{{\bf a}},T)
is a topological
S
-adic dynamical system.
We refer to
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
for the background on
S
-adic shifts.
The following will be assumed throughout the paper:

(A1)

There is a word
{\bf q}\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{+}
which appears in
{\bf a}
infinitely often, for which
{\sf S}_{\bf q}
has all entries strictly positive.

Property (A1) implies unique ergodicity of the
S
-adic shift, see
[
14
, Theorems 5.2 and 5.7]
; the claim on unique ergodicity goes back to Furstenberg
[
38
, (16.13)]
.

A directive sequence
{\bf a}=(\zeta_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and the associated
S
-adic system
(X_{\bf a},T)
are said to be
primitive
if for every
n\geq 1
there exists
N>n
such that
{\sf S}_{n}\cdots{\sf S}_{N-1}
has strictly positive entries. Primitivity implies minimality by a result of Durand, see

[
33
, Lemma 6.4.5]
. It is clear that property (A1) implies primitivity.

We will want the
S
-adic system to be
aperiodic
, i.e., to have no periodic points under the shift action.
Aperiodicity is not always easy to check; we quote some sufficient conditions which suit us well.

A substitution
\zeta
on
{\mathcal{A}}
is called
left (resp. right) proper
if all words
\zeta(a),\ a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, start (resp. end) with the same letter.
Another commonly used condition is
strong coincidence

[
5
]
. A substitution
\zeta
is said to satisfy the strong coincidence condition
if there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a letter
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that for every letter
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
we have
\zeta^{k}(a)=W^{a}_{k}\,b\,S^{a}_{k}
, where

W^{a}_{k}
and
S^{a}_{k}
are words, possibly empty, with the property that either all
W^{a}_{k},\ a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, or all
S^{a}_{k},\ a\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
have the same “abelianization”.
It is clear that being left or right proper implies strong coincidence.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
1
, Prop. 2.1]
,
[
2
]
; see also
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Suppose that the directive sequence
{\bf a}=(\zeta_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is primitive,
\det({\sf S}_{\zeta_{j}})\neq 0
for all
j
, and such that either all
\zeta_{j}
are
left proper, or all
\zeta_{j}
are right proper, or
there is a word
{\bf q}
appearing in
{\bf a}
infinitely often, with
\zeta_{\bf q}
satisfying the strong coincidence
condition.
Then the
S
-adic system
(X_{\bf a},T)
is aperiodic.

Remark 2.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

It should be noted that the condition of being proper (or with a strong coincidence)
is missing in the assumptions of
[
1
, Prop. 2.1]
. Without this condition the claim is false: e.g.,
take
\zeta_{j}=\zeta
for all
j
to be
\zeta(0)=010,\ \zeta(1)=101
.

Another important condition we need is
recognizability
.
For a sequence of substitutions this notion was introduced in
[
16
]
; it generalizes
bilateral recognizability
of Mossé
[
43
]
for a single substitution (see Sections 5.5 and 5.6 in
[
46
]
). By the
definition of the space
X_{{\bf a}}
, for every
n\geq 1
, every
x\in X_{{\bf a}}
has a representation of the form

(2.3)

x=T^{k}\bigl(\zeta^{[n]}(x^{\prime})\bigr),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ x^{\prime}\in X_{\sigma^{n}{\bf a}},\ \ 0\leq k<|\zeta^{[n]}(x_{0})|,

where
\sigma
is the left shift on
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathbb{N}}
. The action of a substitution
\zeta
is extended to two-sided sequences in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
by

(2.4)

\zeta(\ldots a_{-1}.a_{0}a_{1}\ldots)=\ldots\zeta(a_{-1}).\zeta(a_{0})\zeta(a_{1})\ldots

Definition 2.3
.

A sequence of substitutions
{\bf a}=(\zeta_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is said to be recognizable if the representation (
2.3
) is unique for all
n\geq 1
.

The following is a special case of
[
16
, Theorem 4.6]
that we need.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
{\bf a}=(\zeta_{j})_{j\geq 1}\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
\det({\sf S}_{\zeta_{j}})\neq 0
for every substitution matrix and
X_{{\bf a}}
is aperiodic. Then
{\bf a}
is recognizable.

2.1.1.
Random
S
-adic systems

Let
\Omega
be a shift-invariant subspace of
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathbb{N}}
, with an invariant measure
{\mathbb{P}}\,
. We suppose that the following properties hold:

(C1)
the system
(\Omega,\sigma,{\mathbb{P}}\,)
is ergodic;

(C2)
the function
{\bf a}\mapsto\log(1+\|{\sf S}_{\zeta_{1}}\|)
is integrable;

(C3)
there is a word
{\bf q}
admissible for sequences in
\Omega
, such that all entries of the matrix
{\sf S}_{\bf q}
are positive and
{\mathbb{P}}\,([{\bf q}])>0
.

(C4)
the set
{\mathfrak{R}}
of substitutions from
{\mathfrak{A}}
which appear in
\Omega
is
finite
, and

(2.5)

\forall\,\zeta\in{\mathfrak{R}},\ \ \det({\sf S}_{\zeta})\neq 0.

Observe that
(C1)
and
(C3)
imply
(A1)
for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
, hence unique ergodicity and primitivity of the random
S
-adic system

(X_{\bf a},T)
holds
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-almost surely. Moreover,
(A1)
, together
with
(C4)
, yield recognizability for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
, by Theorem
2.4
.

2.2.
Spectral cocycle

We recall the definition of the spectral cocycle introduced in
[
25
]
.
Let
\zeta
be a substitution on
d
symbols, with a substitution matrix
{\sf S}_{\zeta}
, having a non-zero determinant.
Then the map

{\mathbf{t}}\mapsto{\sf S}_{\zeta}^{\sf T}\,{\mathbf{t}}\ (\mbox{mod}\ {\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\ {\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, is a toral endomorphism, which preserves the Haar measure
m_{d}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.
Here and below the symbol
{\sf S}^{\sf T}
denotes the matrix transpose of
{\sf S}
.

Let us write the substitution map on symbols explicitly, as follows:

\zeta(b)=u_{1}^{b}\ldots u_{|\zeta(b)|}^{b},\ \ b\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Consider the matrix-valued function
{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
(the space of complex
d\times d
matrices) defined by

(2.6)

{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}({\mathbf{t}})=[{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}(t_{1}\ldots,t_{d})]_{(b,c)}:=\Bigl(\sum_{j\leq|\zeta(b)|,\ u_{j}^{b}=c}\exp\bigl(-2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}t_{u_{k}^{b}}\bigr)\Bigr)_{(b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{2}},\ \ \ {\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Observe that
{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-periodic, so we obtain a continuous matrix-function on the torus, which we denote, by a slight abuse of notation, by the same letter:

{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Note that
{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}({\mathbf{t}})
is a matrix-function whose entries are trigonometric polynomials in
d
variables, with the following properties:

(i)
{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}(0)={\sf S}^{\sf T}_{\zeta}
, and every entry of the matrix
{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}({\mathbf{t}})
is less or equal to the corresponding entry of
{\sf S}^{\sf T}_{\zeta}
in absolute value;

(ii) all the coefficients of the trigonometric polynomials are 0’s and 1’s, and in every row, any given monomial appears at most once;

(iii) the substitution is uniquely determined by

{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}
.

(iv) the cocycle property: for any substitutions
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
on the same alphabet we have

{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta_{1}\circ\zeta_{2}}({\mathbf{t}})={\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta_{2}}({\sf S}^{\sf T}_{\zeta_{1}}{\mathbf{t}}){\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta_{1}}({\mathbf{t}}),

which is verified by a direct computation.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\Omega,\sigma,{\mathbb{P}}\,)
be a measure-preserving shift on
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the properties
(C1)–(C4)
.
Consider the skew product transformation
{\mathbb{G}}:\,\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
defined by

(2.7)

{\mathbb{G}}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})=\bigl(\sigma{\bf a},{\sf S}^{\sf T}_{\zeta_{1}}{\mathbf{t}}\,({\rm mod}\ {\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\bigr),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ {\bf a}=(\zeta_{n})_{n\geq 1}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ {\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})={\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta_{1}}({\mathbf{t}})
be defined by (
2.6
),
where
{\bf a}=(\zeta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.
Then

(2.8)

{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),n):={\mathscr{M}}({\mathbb{G}}^{n-1}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}))\cdot\ldots\cdot{\mathscr{M}}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})

is a complex matrix cocycle over the skew product system
(\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{P}}\,\times m_{d},{\mathbb{G}})
.

We also need the “untwisted” cocycle (the analog of the Rauzy-Veech cocycle from the theory of IET’s), which is simply
{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},0),n)
:

(2.9)

{\mathbb{A}}({\bf a}):={\sf S}_{\zeta_{1}}^{\sf T};\ \ {\mathbb{A}}({\bf a},n):={\mathbb{A}}(\sigma^{n-1}{\bf a})\cdot\ldots\cdot{\mathbb{A}}({\bf a}),

where
{\bf a}=(\zeta_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega
.
This is a
GL(d,{\mathbb{R}})
-cocycle over the ergodic system
(\Omega,\sigma,{\mathbb{P}}\,)
. Property
(C2)
implies that
the
corresponding Lyapunov exponent exists a.e.:

(2.10)

\lambda:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|{\mathbb{A}}({\bf a},n)\|\ \ \mbox{for ${\mathbb{P}}\,$-a.e.\ ${\bf a}\in\Omega$}.

In fact,
\lambda>0
, which can be easily deduced from assumption
(C3)
.

2.3.
Lyapunov exponents of the spectral cocycle

Consider the pointwise upper Lyapunov exponent (which is defined everywhere and is independent of the matrix norm):

(2.11)

{\chi}_{{\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}}^{+}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),n)\|,\ \ {\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}.

Note that the
property (i) of
{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}
above implies

(2.12)

\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}}\leq\lambda\ \ \mbox{for all}\ {\bf a}\in\Omega,\ {\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}.

Now assume that
(\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{P}}\,\times m_{d},{\mathbb{G}})
is ergodic. Then by theorems of Furstenberg-Kesten
[
39
]
and Kingman
[
41
]
there is a “global” Lyapunov exponent

(2.13)

\displaystyle\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),n)\|,\ \ \mbox{for $({\mathbb{P}}\,\times m_{d})$-a.e.}\ ({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{k}\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\bf a}).

The value of the Lyapunov exponent does not depend on the norm; often it will be convenient to use the Frobenius norm of a matrix, defined by

{\|(a_{ij})_{i,j}\|}^{2}_{\rm F}=\sum_{i,j}|a_{ij}|^{2}.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
k\geq 1
, the function
({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})\mapsto\log\|{\mathscr{M}})({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|
is integrable, and

\int_{\Omega}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\bf a})\geq 0.

Thus,
\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})\geq 0
.

Proof.

This an immediate consequence of
[
27
, Lemma 2.3]
, but we recall the argument for the reader’s convenience. By Definition
2.6
, we obtain that for any substitution
\zeta
on
{\mathcal{A}}
, the function
{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}({\mathbf{t}})\|}_{\rm F}^{2}
is a sum of the squares of absolute values of
polynomials in
d
variables
z_{j}=e^{-2\pi it_{j}}
. This expression can be rewritten as a (positive) polynomial in the variables
z_{j}^{\pm 1}
. Multiplying it by

z_{j}^{\ell_{j}}
for some
\ell_{j}\geq 0
to get rid of the negative powers, we obtain that

\log{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}({\mathbf{t}})\|}_{\rm F}^{2}=\log|P_{\zeta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})|

for some polynomial
P_{\zeta}
with integer coefficients, It follows that

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta}({\mathbf{t}})\|}_{\rm F}^{2}\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})=\mathfrak{m}(P_{\zeta})\geq 0,

where
\mathfrak{m}(P_{\zeta})
is the logarithmic Mahler measure of a polynomial, see
[
18
]
. Since

{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)={\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta^{[k]}}({\mathbf{t}}),

where
\zeta^{[k]}
corresponds to the first
k
letters of
{\bf a}
, the claim follows.
∎

It is immediate from (
2.12
) that
\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{\Omega})\leq\lambda
; however, this is not sharp.
The next lemma follows from
[
25
, Corollary 4.5]
, but we give a direct proof here, inspired by the argument in
[
8
, Theorem 3.29]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
(C1)–(C4)
are satisfied and the skew product
(\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{P}}\,\times m_{d},{\mathbb{G}})
is ergodic. Then

\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{\Omega})\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda
.

Proof.

We have by Jensen’s inequality:

(2.14)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{k}:=\frac{1}{k}\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|}_{\rm F}\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\bf a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2k}\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|}^{2}_{\rm F}\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\bf a})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2k}\log\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|}^{2}_{\rm F}\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\bf a}).

By the definition of Frobenius norm and Parseval’s formula,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|}^{2}_{\rm F}\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k))_{(b,c)}|^{2}\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}\sum_{{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|(\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},\cdot),k))_{(b,c)}({\bf n})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}\sum_{{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|(\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},\cdot),k))_{(b,c)}({\bf n})|,

since all the coefficients are 0’s and 1’s. Moreover, the sum of all the coefficients is exactly the sum of all the entries of the corresponding substitution matrix. Thus we obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|}^{2}_{\rm F}\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{b\in{\mathcal{A}}}|\zeta_{\bf a}^{[k]}(b)|=\sum_{(b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{\sf S}_{\zeta_{\bf a}^{[k]}}(b,c)=\|A({\bf a},k)\|_{1,1},

where

{\bigl\|(a_{ij})_{i,j}\bigr\|}_{1,1}=\sum_{i,j}|a_{ij}|.

By Egorov’s Theorem and (
2.10
), for any
{\varepsilon}>0
there exists
E_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega
, with
{\mathbb{P}}\,(E_{\varepsilon})<{\varepsilon}
, and
N=N({\varepsilon})\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that for all
k\geq N
holds

{\bigl\|{\mathbb{A}}({\bf a},k)\bigr\|}_{1,1}\leq e^{k(\lambda+{\varepsilon})}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ {\bf a}\in\Omega\setminus E_{\varepsilon}.

On the other hand, for all
{\bf a}\in\Omega
and all
{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, we have

{\|{\mathscr{M}}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})\|}_{\rm F}\leq C_{1}:=\max_{\zeta\in{\mathfrak{R}}}{\|{\sf S}_{\zeta}\|}_{\rm F}\implies{\|{\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),k)\|}_{\rm F}\leq C_{1}^{k},

since the Frobenius norm is sub-multiplicative.
Thus, splitting the integral over
\Omega
in (
2.14
) into the sum of integrals over
{\mathbb{E}}\,_{\varepsilon}
and over its complement, we obtain

{\mathcal{I}}_{k}\leq{\varepsilon}\log C_{1}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}),\ \ k\geq N({\varepsilon}).

Letting
{\varepsilon}\to 0
yields the desired inequality
\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda
, in view of (
2.13
).
∎

2.4.
Spectral measures, cylindrical functions

Now we return to the random
S
-adic system
(X_{\bf a},T)
under the assumptions
(C1)–(C4)
.
As we have seen, they imply unique ergodicity of
(X_{\bf a},T)
for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
. Denote the unique invariant Borel probability measure by
\mu_{\bf a}
.
We are interested in spectral properties of the measure-preserving system
(X_{{\bf a}},T,\mu_{\bf a})
.
Here and below, by
spectral properties of a measure-preserving
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action
we always mean the properties of the corresponding unitary operator —
the Koopman operator, and similarly, for the
{\mathbb{R}}
-action — the properties of the associated unitary group.

Recall that, given a test function
f\in L^{2}(X_{\bf a},\mu_{\bf a})
,
the
spectral measure

\sigma_{f}
is a finite positive measure on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, determined by its Fourier coefficients:

\widehat{\sigma}_{f}(-k)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{2\pi ik\omega}\,d\sigma_{f}(\omega)=\langle U_{T}^{k}f\,,\,f\rangle,\ \ \ k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

where
U_{T}:f\mapsto f\circ T
is the Koopman operator on
L^{2}(X_{\bf a},\mu_{\bf a})
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}
.
It is well-known that, given a countable complete (i.e., having
a dense linear span) set
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of unit vectors in
L^{2}
, the maximal spectral type of
U_{T}
, equivalently, of the measure-preserving system, is
given by

(2.15)

[\sigma_{\max}]=\textstyle{\bigl[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\sigma_{f_{n}}\bigr]}.

The symbol
[\nu]
denotes the
type
of the measure
\nu
, that is, the equivalence class of
\nu
under the relation of mutual absolute continuity.

The most basic functions in the symbolic space
X_{\bf a}
are the functions depending only on the symbol
x_{0}
. We call them
cylindrical functions of
level
0. Of course, their span is only
d
-dimensional, where
d
is the number of symbols, so in order to “capture” the maximal spectral type we need
functions depending on an arbitrary finite number of symbols. In order to do this efficiently, we make use of renormalization.

Recall that the action of substitutions
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{R}}
is extended to
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
in such a way that
\zeta(x_{0})
starts from the
0
-th position, see
(
2.4
). Consider the subsets
\zeta^{[n]}[a]

of
X_{\bf a}
, where
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
[a]
is the cylinder set of sequences having
x_{0}=a
. We emphasize that
\zeta^{[n]}[a]
may be a proper subset of
[\zeta^{[n]}(a)]
.
Recognizability implies that

{\mathcal{P}}_{n}=\left\{T^{i}(\zeta^{[n]}[a]):a\in{\mathcal{A}},\ 0\leq i<|\zeta^{[n]}(a)|\right\},\ \ n\geq 1,

is a sequence of Kakutani-Rokhlin partitions, which generates the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
X_{\bf a}
, see
[
16
, Lemma 6.3]
. Functions that are
measurable with respect to the partition
{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}
, but not
{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell-1}
, are called
cylindrical functions of level

\ell
. Thus a sequence
f_{n}

of cylindrical functions of levels
\ell_{n}\to\infty
, having the property
that their linear span contains all cylindrical functions of all levels, generates the maximal spectral type
of the
S
-adic system
(X_{\bf a},T)
, as in (
2.15
), for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
.

2.5.
Suspension flows and their spectrum

As in
[
27
]
, we
also need to consider
suspension flows
(or
special flows
, in another terminology), over
S
-adic systems, for which the roof function

\phi
is piecewise-constant and depends only on the symbol
x_{0}
of
x\in X_{\bf a}
:

(2.16)

\phi(x)=s_{x_{0}}\geq 0.

Consider the partition of
X_{\bf a}
into cylinder sets according to the value of
x_{0}
:
X_{{\bf a}}=\bigsqcup_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}[a]
.
Denote
by
({\mathfrak{X}}_{\bf a}^{\vec{s}},h_{\tau},\widetilde{\mu}_{\bf a})
the suspension flow over
(X_{{\bf a}},T,\mu_{\bf a})
, corresponding to the piecewise-constant roof function
(
2.16
), where
\vec{s}=(s_{a})_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
.
The suspension flow is defined on the following union:

{\mathfrak{X}}_{\bf a}^{\vec{s}}=\bigcup_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}[a]\times[0,s_{a}],

with the invariant measure
\widetilde{\mu}_{\bf a}
equal to the normalized sum of
\mu_{\bf a}|_{[a]}\times m|_{[0,a_{a}]}
. Here
m
denotes the Lebesgue measure
on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
We define
a
Lip-cylindrical function of level 0
by the formula:

(2.17)

F(x,\tau)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}{1\!\!1}_{[a]}(x)\cdot\psi_{a}(\tau),\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \psi_{a}\in{\rm Lip}[0,s_{a}],

where
{\rm Lip}
is the space of Lipschitz functions.
Further,
F
is a
Lip-cylindrical function of level
\ell\geq 1
if

(2.18)

F(x,\tau)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}{1\!\!1}_{\zeta^{[\ell]}[a]}(x)\cdot\psi^{(\ell)}_{a}(\tau),\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \psi^{(\ell)}_{a}\in{\rm Lip}[0,s^{(\ell)}_{a}],

where

\vec{s}^{\,(\ell)}=(s^{(\ell)}_{a})_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}:={\sf S}_{\zeta^{[\ell]}}^{\sf T}\vec{s}.

The union of spaces of Lip-cylindrical functions of level
\ell
over
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is dense in
L^{2}({\mathfrak{X}}_{\bf a}^{\vec{s}},\widetilde{\mu}_{\bf a})
,
see
[
25
, Lemma 7.1]
, and hence the maximal spectral type
of
({\mathfrak{X}}_{\bf a}^{\vec{s}},h_{\tau},\widetilde{\mu}_{\bf a})
can be recovered from the spectral measures
\sigma_{F}
,
where
F
runs over Lip-cylindrical functions of an arbitrary level.

Recall that, given a probability measure-preserving flow
h_{\tau}
on a space
{\mathfrak{X}}
and a test function
F\in L^{2}({\mathfrak{X}})
, the spectral measure
\sigma_{F}
is a
finite positive Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
determined by the formula

\widehat{\sigma}_{F}(-\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi i\omega\tau}\,d\sigma_{F}(\omega)=\langle F\circ h_{\tau},F\rangle,\ \ \ \tau\in{\mathbb{R}}.

It is well-known that spectral properties of a (general, probability-preserving)
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action on
(X,\mu)

are closely related to those of the suspension
{\mathbb{R}}
-action
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mu})
,
corresponding to the constant-1 roof function, in particular, when we have
\vec{s}=\vec{1}
in the case of an
S
-adic system.
In fact,
for
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
consider the
function
F\in L^{2}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mu})
defined by

F(x,\tau)=f(x)
. Then
the following relation holds between the spectral measures
\sigma_{F}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\sigma_{f}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
:

(2.19)

d\sigma_{F}(\omega)=\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\omega)}{\pi\omega}\right)^{2}\cdot d\sigma_{f}(e^{2\pi i\omega}),\ \ \omega\in{\mathbb{R}}.

See
[
29
, Prop. 1.1]
for a general treatment and
[
11
, Lem. 5.6]
for our specific case.

We next state a result from
[
25
]
, from which we quote only the relevant part. It provides a lower bound for the pointwise local dimension of
a spectral measure, defined by

\underline{d}(\sigma_{F},\omega):=\liminf_{r\to 0+}\frac{\log\sigma_{F}(B(\omega,r))}{\log r}.

Theorem 2.8

(see
[
25
, Theorems 4.3 and 4.6 ]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\sigma,{\mathbb{P}}\,)
be a measure-preserving shift on
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the properties
(C1)–(C4)
, such that
for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}\in\Omega
, the
S
-adic system
(X_{\bf a},T)
is aperiodic, and hence it is
recognizable and uniquely ergodic for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}\in\Omega
.
Denote by
\mu_{\bf a}
the unique invariant
probability measure.
Fix any
\vec{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
and consider the suspension flow
({\mathfrak{X}}_{\bf a}^{\vec{s}},h_{\tau},\widetilde{\mu}_{\bf a})
over
(X_{\bf a},T,\mu_{\bf a})
,
with the piecewise constant roof function determined by
\vec{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
. Then for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}\in\Omega
the following holds:

Let
F
be a nonzero Lip-cylindrical function on
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\bf a}^{\vec{s}}
(of any level) and let

\sigma_{F}
be the corresponding spectral measure for the flow. Then the lower local dimension of
\sigma_{F}
satisfies for all
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

(2.20)

\underline{d}(\sigma_{F},\omega)\geq 2\min\Bigl\{1,1-\frac{\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}}{\lambda}\Bigr\},

where
\lambda
is from (
2.10
) and
\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}
is the local upper Lyapunov exponent of the spectral cocycle, defined in
(
2.11
), with
{\mathbf{t}}=\omega\vec{s}
mod
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.

Corollary 2.9

(see
[
25
, Corollary 4.5]
)
.

Under the assumptions of the last theorem, we have for any
\vec{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
, for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}\in\Omega
, and for any nonzero Lip-cylindrical function
F
:

(2.21)

\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}\geq\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda\ \ \mbox{for}\ \sigma_{F}\mbox{-a.e.}\ \omega\in{\mathbb{R}}.

We recall the derivation of the corollary. The upper Hausdorff dimension of the measure
\sigma_{F}
is defined by

\dim_{\rm H}^{*}(\sigma_{F})=\inf\{\dim_{\rm H}(E):\ E\subset{\mathbb{R}}\ \mbox{is a Borel set with}\ \sigma_{F}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus E)=0\}.

Obviously,
\dim_{\rm H}^{*}(\sigma_{F})\leq 1
. Combining (
2.20
) with the well-known formula

\dim_{\rm H}^{*}(\sigma_{F})=\inf\{s:\ \underline{d}(\sigma_{F},\omega)\leq s\ \ \mbox{for $\sigma_{F}$-a.e.}\ \ \omega\},

see, e.g.,
[
34
, (10.12)]
, yields the claim. ∎

2.6.
Equidistribution results

The next theorem is a special case of a result of Benoist and Quint
[
10
]
.

Theorem 2.10

(Benoist and Quint)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a semigroup in
SL(d,{\mathbb{R}})
which

(A)
acts strongly irreducibly on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, that is, no finite union of proper vector subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
\Gamma
-invariant;

(B)
contains a proximal element, i.e., a matrix with a dominant eigenvalue.

Let
\nu
be a finitely supported probability measure on
\Gamma
, whose support generates
\Gamma
.
Consider the random walk
g_{n}\ldots g_{1}
, where
g_{i}
are chosen i.i.d., according to the measure
\nu
on
SL(d,{\mathbb{R}})
. Then for every

x_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, the sequence

(g_{n}\ldots g_{1}\cdot x_{0})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is almost surely (i.e., for a.e. realization of the random walk) equidistributed on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

We also need the following proposition, which we state in a special case.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
48
, Prop. 5.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact space,
G
a locally compact 2nd countable group acting continuously on
X
. Suppose that
m
is a
G
-invariant and ergodic measure on
X
, and
\mu
a probability measure with finite support
E
on
G
. Let
B=E^{\mathbb{N}}
,
\beta=\mu^{{\mathbb{N}}}
, and denote by
T
the left shift on
B
. Fix
x_{0}\in X
and suppose that for
\beta
-a.e.
b\in B
, the random path

(g_{b_{n}}\ldots g_{b_{1}}\cdot x_{0})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is equidistributed with respect to the measure
m
on
X
. Then for
\beta
-a.e.
b\in B
, the sequence

(g_{b_{n}}\ldots g_{b_{1}}\cdot x_{0},T^{n}b)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is equidistributed with respect to the measure
m\times\beta
on
X\times B
.

Combining Theorem
2.10
with Proposition
2.11
, we obtain that if
\nu
is a probability measure on
\Gamma
with a finite support
E
, which acts on the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
totally irreducibly a contains a proximal element, then given any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, for
almost every
b\in B
, the sequence
(g_{b_{n}}\ldots g_{b_{1}}\cdot x_{0},T^{n}b)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is equidistributed with respect to
m_{d}\times\beta
.

3.
A new result: application of equidistribution

Theorem 3.1
.

For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, let
\zeta_{j}
be a
substitution on
d
letters. Consider the infinite product space

\Omega=\{\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{\ell}\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, with
\ell\geq 2
, equipped with a fully supported
Bernoulli measure
{\mathbb{P}}\,=\mu^{{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Let
{\sf S}_{j}
be the substitution matrix of
\zeta_{j}
, and let
\Gamma
be the semigroup generated by
{\sf S}_{j}^{\sf T}
,
j\leq\ell
.
We suppose that the following properties hold:

(B1)

for each
j\leq\ell
,
\det({\sf S}_{j})=1
, and for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}\in\Omega
the space
X_{\bf a}
is aperiodic (e.g., all
\zeta_{j}
are left or
right proper);

(B2)

the semigroup
\Gamma<SL(d,{\mathbb{R}})
acts strongly irreducibly on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
;

(B3)

the semigroup
\Gamma
contains a matrix with strictly positive entries.

Let
\lambda
be the top Lyapunov exponent of the cocycle induced by
{\sf S}^{\sf T}
, as in (
2.10
). We have
\lambda>0
.
Consider the skew product transformation, as in (
2.7
):
{\mathbb{G}}:\,\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, defined by

{\mathbb{G}}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})=\bigl(\sigma{\bf a},{\sf S}^{\sf T}_{\zeta_{1}}{\mathbf{t}}\,({\rm mod}\ {\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\bigr),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ {\bf a}=(\zeta_{n})_{n\geq 1}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ {\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Then

(i)
The properties
(C1)–(C4)
are satisfied, and for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}\in\Omega
the corresponding
S
-adic measure-preserving
system
(X_{\bf a},T,\mu_{\bf a})
is aperiodic, recognizable, and uniquely ergodic.

(ii)

The skew product
{\mathbb{G}}
is ergodic, hence the top Lyapunov exponent
\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})
exists, see (
2.13
).

(iii)
Let
\nu
be any measure on
{\mathbb{T}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that the set of rationals has zero measure (in particular, any continuous measure).
If
\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})<\frac{1}{2}\lambda
, then for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
the maximal spectral type of

(X_{\bf a},T,\mu_{\bf a})
is singular with respect to
\nu
. In particular, a.e. such system is spectrally disjoint from any given weakly mixing measure-preserving transformation.

Here (iii) is the main claim of the theorem; (i) and (ii) are essentially a summary of what we have already shown.

Proof.

(i) Properties
(C1)-(C2)
are immediate from the assumptions,
(C3)
follows from
(B3)
, since
{\mathbb{P}}\,
is a Bernoulli
measure, and
(C4)
follows from
(B1)
. The remaining part of the claim was already noted in Section
2.1.1
.

(ii)
Applying Theorem
2.10
and Proposition
2.11
, we obtain that, given any
{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}

the orbit
{\mathbb{G}}^{n}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})
is equidistributed with respect to the measure
{\mathbb{P}}\,\times m_{d}
.
Note also that conditions
(A), (B)

are preserved when passing from
\mu
to
\mu^{*k}
. Thus we have

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the assumptions of the theorem,
given any
{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
, for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the orbit

\{{\mathbb{G}}^{kn}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is equidistributed with respect to the measure
{\mathbb{P}}\,\times m_{d}
.

This equidistribution result implies that the skew product
{\mathbb{G}}
is ergodic,
hence the Lyapunov exponent

\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{\Omega})
of the spectral cocycle is well-defined.

(iii) The main part of the proof is contained in the following

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that all the assumptions of the theorem are satisfied. Let
\omega\vec{s}\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. Then for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}\in\Omega
,

\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}\leq\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})\leq\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda.

Proof of the lemma.

This is an extension of an argument from
[
27
]
(more precisely, of the first part of the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 2.4]
).
Denote

{\mathscr{M}}^{[n]}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}):={\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}}(({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}),n)={\mathscr{M}}({\mathbb{G}}^{n-1}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}))\cdot\ldots\cdot{\mathscr{M}}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})

for convenience. Observe that for any
{\bf a}\in\Omega,{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

(3.1)

\chi_{{\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}}^{+}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{nk}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[nk]}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})\|.

Indeed, the inequality
\geq
is obvious, and the reverse inequality follows from

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[nk+j]}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[nk]}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})\|\cdot\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[j]}({\mathbb{G}}^{nk}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[nk]}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}})\|\cdot\max_{i\leq\ell}\|{\sf S}_{i}\|^{k-1},

for
1\leq j\leq k-1
.

Now, by the definition of the cocycle, in view of (
3.1
), for any
{\bf a}\in\Omega
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.2)

\chi_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}^{+}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{nk}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbb{G}}^{kj}({\bf a},\omega\vec{s}))\|.

Fix any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\omega\vec{s}\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. By (
3.2
) and Lemma
3.2
, for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
,

\displaystyle\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{nk}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log\bigl({\varepsilon}+\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbb{G}}^{kj}({\bf a},{\mathbf{t}}))\|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log\bigl({\varepsilon}+\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|\bigr)\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\mathbf{x}}),

using the definition of equidistribution and the fact that the integrand is continuous.

We claim that we can pass to the limit
{\varepsilon}\to 0
by dominated convergence to obtain for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}\leq\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|\,d{\mathbf{t}}\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\mathbf{x}}),\ \ \mbox{for ${\mathbb{P}}\,$-a.e.\ ${\bf a}$},

hence

(3.3)

\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{s}}\leq\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})\leq\frac{\lambda}{2},\ \ \mbox{for ${\mathbb{P}}\,$-a.e.\ ${\bf a}$},

as desired.
In order to justify passing to the limit
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, we split the integral into two:

\int_{\Omega}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\log\bigl({\varepsilon}+\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|\bigr)\,dm_{d}({\mathbf{t}})\,d{\mathbb{P}}\,({\mathbf{x}})=:\int_{A_{1}}+\int_{A_{2}},

where

A_{1}=\bigl\{({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}:\ \|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|<1/2\bigr\},\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ A_{2}=(\Omega\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d})\setminus A_{1}.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Then for
({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\in A_{1}
,

\bigl|{\varepsilon}+\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|\bigr|=-\log\bigl({\varepsilon}+\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|\bigr)<-\log\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|,

which is positive and integrable, so the Dominated Convergence Theorem applies. On the other hand, for
({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\in A_{2}
we have

\bigl|{\varepsilon}+\|{\mathscr{M}}^{[k]}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{t}})\|\bigr|\leq\max\bigl\{\log 2,\log(1/2+C_{1}^{k})\bigr\},

where
C_{1}=\max_{j\leq\ell}\|{\sf S}_{j}\|
, so again passing to the limit
{\varepsilon}\to 0
is justified.
∎

Now we can conclude the proof of the theorem.
In view of (
2.19
),
it suffices to prove the analogous claim for the suspension flow
({\mathfrak{X}}_{\bf a}^{\vec{s}},h_{\tau},\widetilde{\mu}_{\bf a})
, with the vector of heights
\vec{s}=\vec{1}
, and
assume that
\nu
is a measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, by the discussion in Section
2.5
, it is enough
to show that
for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
we have
\sigma_{f}\perp\nu
for any Lip-cylindrical function
f
(of any level).
By Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, we have that for any irrational
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
, hence for

\nu
-a.e.
\omega
, for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
,

(3.4)

\chi^{+}_{{\bf a},\omega\vec{1}}\leq\chi({\mathscr{M}}_{{}_{\Omega}})<\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda.

Then, by Fubini’s Theorem, for
{\mathbb{P}}\,
-a.e.
{\bf a}
the inequality holds for
\nu
-a.e.
\omega
. Fix such an
{\bf a}\in\Omega
and consider a cylindrical function
f
for the

Consider the family of substitutions

Note that all the assumptions of Theorem

, since the composition of every two substitutions

is left proper.

Step 1.

First we obtain the lower bound for the Lyapunov exponent

{\mathscr{M}}_{\zeta_{m}}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1+z_{0}+\cdots+z_{0}^{2m-1}&z_{0}^{2m}(1+z_{1}+\cdots+z_{1}^{m^{2}-1})&z_{0}^{2m}z_{1}^{m^{2}}\\

A.2.
Some examples

In this section, we show that the assumptions of Theorem
A.1
above applies to the families of substitutions considered by Solomyak in Example 4.1 above. In particular, we solve the question in Remark 4.2 above concerning the positivity of the second Lyapunov exponent attached to these examples.
Note that the composition of every two substitutions
\zeta_{m},\zeta_{n}
is left proper, hence the strong coincidence assumption holds.

A.2.1.
Invariant cones

Let
m\geq 3
be an integer, and recall that the matrices appearing in Example 4.1 above are:

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2m&1&0\\
m^{2}&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{array}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2(m+1)&1&0\\
(m+1)^{2}&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{array}\right).

Note that the inverses of these matrices are

A^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
1&0&-2m\\
0&1&-m^{2}\end{array}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad B^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
1&0&-2(m+1)\\
0&1&-(m+1)^{2}\end{array}\right).

Define the cone

\mathcal{C}=\{(0,0,0)\}\cup\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x_{1}\neq 0,-3m\leq\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\leq-2m,-m^{2}-3m\leq\frac{x_{3}}{x_{1}}\leq-m^{2}+1\right\}.

Proposition A.2
.

The cone
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under the semigroup generated by
A^{-1}
and
B^{-1}
.

Proof.

By definition,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}^{\prime}\\
x_{2}^{\prime}\\
x_{3}^{\prime}\end{array}\right):=A^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
x_{3}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{3}\\
x_{1}-2mx_{3}\\
x_{2}-m^{2}x_{3}\end{array}\right).

Therefore, if
\vec{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}
, then

\frac{x_{2}^{\prime}}{x_{1}^{\prime}}=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{3}}-2m\in\left[-\frac{1}{m^{2}-1}-2m,-\frac{1}{m^{2}+3m}-2m\right]

and

\frac{x_{3}^{\prime}}{x_{1}^{\prime}}=\frac{x_{2}}{x_{3}}-m^{2}=\frac{(-x_{2}/x_{1})}{(-x_{3}/x_{1})}-m^{2}\in\left[\frac{2m}{m^{2}+3m}-m^{2},\frac{3m}{m^{2}-1}-m^{2}\right],

so that
A^{-1}(\vec{x})
falls in the interior of
\mathcal{C}
when
m\geq 3
.

Similarly,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}^{\prime\prime}\\
x_{2}^{\prime\prime}\\
x_{3}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right):=B^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
x_{3}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{3}\\
x_{1}-2(m+1)x_{3}\\
x_{2}-(m+1)^{2}x_{3}\end{array}\right).

Hence, if
\vec{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}
, then

\frac{x_{2}^{\prime\prime}}{x_{1}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{3}}-2-2m\in\left[-\frac{1}{m^{2}-1}-2-2m,-\frac{1}{m^{2}+3m}-2-2m\right]

and

\frac{x_{3}^{\prime\prime}}{x_{1}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{x_{2}}{x_{3}}-(m+1)^{2}=\frac{(-x_{2}/x_{1})}{(-x_{3}/x_{1})}-1-2m-m^{2}\in\left[\frac{2m}{m^{2}+3m}-1-2m-m^{2},\frac{3m}{m^{2}-1}-1-2m-m^{2}\right],

so that
B^{-1}(\vec{x})
falls in the interior of
\mathcal{C}
when
m\geq 3
.
∎

A.2.2.

Once an adequate invariant cone was identified, let us show that the vectors in this cone are expanded:

Proposition A.3
.

For each
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}
, one has

\frac{\|A^{-1}(\vec{x})\|_{L^{1}}}{\|\vec{x}\|_{L^{1}}}\geq\frac{m^{4}+2m^{3}-5m}{m^{2}+6m+1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{\|B^{-1}(\vec{x})\|_{L^{1}}}{\|\vec{x}\|_{L^{1}}}\geq\frac{m^{4}+4m^{3}+3m^{2}-7m-3}{m^{2}+6m+1}

We can assume without loss of generality that

\vec{x}\in\mathcal{C}
implies that
x_{3}<0
and
x_{2}-m^{2}x_{3},x_{2}-(m+1)^{2}x_{3}>0
, so that

\|\vec{x}\|_{L^{1}}=x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3}=x_{1}(1-x_{2}/x_{1}-x_{3}/x_{1})\leq x_{1}(1+6m+m^{2})

and

\displaystyle\|A^{-1}(\vec{x})\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle-x_{3}+x_{1}-2mx_{3}+x_{2}-m^{2}x_{3}=x_{1}\left(1+\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}-(m+1)^{2}\frac{x_{3}}{x_{1}}\right)

Phragmèn-Lindelöf type theorems for elliptic equations on infinite graphs
Stefano Biagi, Fabio Punzo

Abstract
. We investigate the validity of the Phragmèn-Lindelöf principle for a class of elliptic equations with a potential, posed on infinite graphs. Consequently, we get uniqueness, in the class of solutions satisfying a suitable growth condition at infinity. We suppose that the outer degree (or outer curvature) of the graph is bounded from above, and we allow the potential to go to zero at infinity in a controlled way. Finally, we discuss the optimality of the conditions on the potential and on the outer degree on special graphs.

2.
Mathematical framework

2.1.
The graph setting

Let
G
be a countably infinite set, and
let
\mu:G\to(0,+\infty)
be a measure on
G

satisfying
\mu(\{x\})<+\infty
for every
x\in G
(so that
(G,\mu)
becomes a measure space). Furthermore, let

\omega:G\times G\to[0,+\infty)

be a symmetric, with zero diagonal and finite sum function, i.e.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\text{(i)}\,\,\omega(x,y)=\omega(y,x)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all}\,\,\,(x,y)\in G\times G;

\displaystyle\text{(ii)}\,\,\omega(x,x)=0\quad\quad\quad\,\,

\displaystyle\text{for all}\,\,\,x\in G;

\displaystyle\text{(iii)}\,\,\displaystyle\sum_{y\in G}\omega(x,y)<\infty\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all}\,\,\,x\in G\,.

Thus, we define
weighted graph
the triplet
(G,\omega,\mu)
, where
\omega
and
\mu
are the so called
edge weight
and
node measure
, respectively. Observe that assumption
(ii)
corresponds to ask that
G
has no loops.

Let
x,y
be two points in
G
; we say that

•

x
is
connected
to
y
and we write
x\sim y
, whenever
\omega(x,y)>0
;

•

the couple
(x,y)
is an
edge
of the graph and the vertices
x,y
are called the
endpoints
of the edge whenever
x\sim y
;

•

a collection of vertices
\{x_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}\subset G
is a
path
if
x_{k}\sim x_{k+1}
for all
k=0,\ldots,n-1.

We are now ready to list some properties that the weighted graph
(G,\omega,\mu)
may satisfy.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the weighted graph
(G,\omega,\mu)
is

(i)

locally finite if each vertex
x\in G
has only finitely many
y\in G
such that
x\sim y
;

(ii)

connected if, for any two distinct vertices
x,y\in G
there exists a path joining
x
to
y
;

For any
x\in G
, we define

•

the
degree
of
x
as

\operatorname{deg}(x):=\sum_{y\in G}\omega(x,y);

•

the
weighted degree
of
x
as

\operatorname{Deg}(x):=\frac{\operatorname{deg}(x)}{\mu(x)}.

Let
d:G\times G\to[0,+\infty)
be a distance on
G.

For any
x_{0}\in G
and
r>0
we define the ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
with respect to the metric
d
as

B_{r}(x_{0}):=\{x\in G\,:\,d(x,x_{0})<r\}\,.

Sometimes we shall use specific metrics.

In this paper, we always make the following assumption:

(2.2)

(G,\omega,\mu)\text{ is a connected, locally finite, weighted graph}.

2.2.
Difference and Laplace operators

Let
\mathfrak{F}
denote the set of all functions
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
. For any
f\in\mathfrak{F}
and for all
x,y\in G
, let us give the following

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(G,\omega,\mu)
be a weighted graph. For any
f\in\mathfrak{F}
,

•

the difference operator is

\nabla_{xy}f:=f(y)-f(x)\,;

•

the (weighted) Laplace operator on
(G,\omega,\mu)
is

(2.3)

\Delta f(x):=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in G}[f(y)-f(x)]\omega(x,y)\quad\text{ for all }\,x\in G\,.

Clearly,

\Delta f(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in G}(\nabla_{xy}f)\omega(x,y)\quad\text{ for all }x\in G\,.

2.3.
Outer and inner degrees

We introduce some basic definitions following
[
15
, Chapter 9]
. Let
\rho
be the
combinatorial graph distance
on
G
, that is, for any
x,y\in G,

\rho(x,y)
is the least number of edges in a path between the two vertices
x
and
y
.

Let
\Omega\subset G
be finite subset. Define the distance from any
x\in G
to the subset
\Omega

\rho(x,\Omega):=\min_{y\in\Omega}\rho(x,y)\quad\forall x\in G\,.

With an abuse of notation we write
\rho(x,y)
to indicate the distance between the two points
x,y\in G
, and
\rho(x,\Omega)
to denote the distance from the point
x\in G
to the set
\Omega\subset G
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let

S_{r}(\Omega):=\{x\in G\,:\rho(x,\Omega)=r\,\}.

For any
f\in\mathfrak{F}
, if

f(x)=f(y)\quad\text{ whenever }\rho(x,\Omega)=\rho(y,\Omega),

then we say that
f
is
spherically symmetric w.r.t.

\Omega
; moreover, with a slight abuse of notation
we write

f(x)=f(r)\quad\forall x\in S_{r}(\Omega)\,.

For any
x\in G
with
r\equiv r(x):=\rho(x,\Omega)\geq 1
, let

\mathfrak{D}_{+}(x):=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in S_{r+1}(\Omega)}\omega(x,y),\quad\mathfrak{D}_{-}(x):=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in S_{r-1}(\Omega)}\omega(x,y)\,.

The function
\mathfrak{D}_{+}:G\to[0,+\infty)
is called
outer degree
w.r.t.
\Omega
, whereas
\mathfrak{D}_{-}:G\to[0,+\infty)
is called
inner degree
w.r.t.
\Omega.

Sometimes the outer degree and the inner degree are also called
outer curvature
and, respectively,
inner curvature
(see
[
1
]
).

Remark 2.3
.

We explicitly highlight, for a future reference,
the following simple property:
given any
x\in S_{r}(\Omega)

(for some
r\geq 1
) and any
y\in G
, by definition of
\rho
we have

(2.4)

\omega(x,y)>0\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,y\in S_{r-1}(\Omega)\cup S_{r+1}(\Omega).

The weighted graph
(G,\mu,\omega)
, endowed with the combinatorial distance
\rho
, is said to be

weakly spherically symmetric
with
respect to a finite subset
\Omega\subset G
, if the outer and inner degrees
\mathfrak{D}_{\pm}
are spherically symmetric with respect to
\Omega
. Therefore, on a weakly symmetric graph,

\mathfrak{D}_{\pm}(x)=\mathfrak{D}_{\pm}(r)\quad\forall x\in S_{r}(\Omega)\,.

3.
Main Results

We have already stated in (
2.2
)
the main hypotheses on the weighted graph
(G,\omega,\mu)
.
In order to state our main results, we first fix the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq G
be an arbitrary set.

1)

Given any
f:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
, we say
that a function
u\in\mathfrak{F}
is a subsolution

[
resp. supersolution
]
of the equation

\mathcal{L}u=f
in
\mathcal{O}
if,
for every
x\in\mathcal{O}
, we have

(3.1)

\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in G}\omega(x,y)\left[u(y)-u(x)\right]-V(x)u(x)\geq\,[\text{resp.\,\,$\leq$}]\,\,f(x).

Moreover, we say that
u
is a solution of the equation
\mathcal{L}u=f
in
\mathcal{O}

if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution of the same equation.

2)

Given any
f:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:G\setminus\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we say
that a function
u\in\mathfrak{F}
is a subsolution

[
resp. supersolution
]
of the

\mathcal{L}
- Dirichlet problem

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}u=f&\text{in $\mathcal{O}$}\\
u=g&\text{in $G\setminus\mathcal{O}$}\end{cases}

if the following conditions hold:

i)

u
is a subsolution
[
resp. supersolution
]
of the equation
\mathcal{L}u=f

in
\mathcal{O}
;

ii)

u\leq g

[
resp.
u\geq g
]
pointwise in
G\setminus\mathcal{O}
.

Finally, we say that
u
is a solution of problem (
3.2
)

u
is both a subsolution and a supersolution of this problem.

3.1.
Phragmèn-Lindelöf principle

The first main result
of this paper is a general Phragmèn-Lindelöf type principle, which reads as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let assumption (
2.2
) be satisfied. Let
V\in\mathfrak{F},V\geq 0,x_{0}\in G
. Suppose that there exists
Z\in\mathfrak{F}
bounded from above such that

(3.3)

\Delta Z\geq-V\quad\text{ in }G,

(3.4)

\lim_{d(x,x_{0})\to+\infty}Z(x)=-\infty\,.

Let
u
be a subsolution of equation (
1.2
) fulfilling

(3.5)

\limsup_{d(x,x_{0})\to+\infty}\frac{u(x)}{|Z(x)|}\leq 0\,.

Then

u\leq 0\quad\text{ in }G\,.

In particular, on graphs with outer degree bounded from above, we have the next

Theorem 3.3
.

Let assumption (
2.2
) be satisfied. Let
\Omega\subset G
be a finite subset. Suppose that, for some
k_{0}>0
,

(3.6)

0\leq\mathfrak{D}_{+}(x)\leq k_{0}\quad\text{ for all }\,x\in G\text{ with }\rho(x,\Omega)\geq 1\,.

Suppose that

(3.7)

V\in\mathfrak{F},\quad V(x)\geq c_{0}\,[1+\rho(x,\Omega)]^{-\alpha}\quad\textrm{for all}\;\;x\in G,

for some
\alpha\in[0,1],c_{0}>0.

Let
u
be a subsolution of equation (
1.2
) fulfilling

(3.8)

\limsup_{\rho(x,\Omega)\to+\infty}\frac{u(x)}{|\tilde{Z}(x)|}\leq 0\,,

where

(3.9)

\tilde{Z}(x):=\begin{cases}-[\rho(x,\Omega)]^{1-\alpha}&\text{ if }\alpha\in[0,1)\\
-\log\rho(x,\Omega)&\text{ if }\alpha=1\end{cases},\text{ whenever }\rho(x,\Omega)>1.

Then

u(x)\leq 0\quad\forall x\in G.

We can immediately infer the following uniqueness results.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let assumption (
2.2
) be satisfied. Let
V\in\mathfrak{F},V\geq 0,x_{0}\in G
; let
f\in\mathfrak{F}
. Suppose that there exists
Z\in\mathfrak{F}
bounded from above such that (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) hold. Then there exists at most one solution to equation (
1.1
) such that

\lim_{d(x,x_{0})\to+\infty}\frac{u(x)}{Z(x)}=0\,.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let assumption (
2.2
) be satisfied; let
f\in\mathfrak{F}
. Assume that (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) are fulfilled. Then there exists at most one solution to equation (
1.1
) such that

\lim_{d(x,\Omega)\to+\infty}\frac{u(x)}{\tilde{Z}(x)}=0\,,

where
\tilde{Z}
is given by (
3.9
).

3.2.
Optimality on spherically symmetric trees

In this subsection we consider a special kind of weakly symmetric graphs, the so called
spherically symmetric trees
, and we show that the results in Theorem
3.3
and
Corollary
3.5
are sharp. More precisely, we show that
both

the choice
\alpha\in[0,1]
in assumption (
3.7
)
and the boundedness assumption (
3.6
) on

\mathfrak{D}_{+}

are optimal
, that is,
infinitely many bounded solutions exist whenever
\alpha>1
or whenever
\mathfrak{D}_{+}
is not bounded.

Let
(G,\omega,\mu)
be a weakly symmetric graph w.r.t.
\Omega=\{o\}
, for some
fixed point
o\in G

(which is usually referred to as the
root of
G
).
Suppose that

•

\omega:G\times G\to\{0,1\}
;

•

\omega|_{S_{r}(\Omega)\times S_{r}(\Omega)}=0
;

•

\mu(x)=1\text{ for every }x\in G;

•

there exists
b:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, which is called the
branching function
, such that

\mathfrak{D}_{+}(x)=b(r),\quad\mathfrak{D}_{-}(x)=1\quad\text{for every $x\in S_{r}(\Omega)$ and
$r\in\mathbb{N}$}.

As regards the optimality
of the choice
\alpha\in[0,1]

in (
3.7
), from
[
4
, Corollary 7.4]
we immediately
have the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(G,\omega,\mu)
be a spherically symmetric tree as above, with

constant branching function

b(r)=b_{0}\geq 2
. Moreover, let
V\in\mathfrak{F},\,V>0

on
G
. Assume that,

(3.10)

V(x)\leq C_{0}\,\rho^{-\alpha}(x,\Omega)\quad\text{ for any }\;x\in G\setminus B_{R_{0}}\,.

for some
\alpha>1
and some
R_{0}\geq 2
.

Then
there exist infinitely many bounded solutions
u
of problem (
1.2
). In particular, for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\gamma>0
, there exists a solution
u
to equation (
1.2
) such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x)=\gamma.

As regards the optimality of the bounded assumption
(
3.6
), instead, by relying on a general result
established in
[
4
]
(see, precisely, Proposition
4.3
below)
we will establish in the next
Section
5
the following theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(G,\omega,\mu)
be a spherically symmetric tree, with

branching function

b(r)=(r+1)^{p}
, for some
p\geq 1
.

Moreover, let
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
and let

V:G\to{\mathbb{R}},\qquad V(x)=(1+\rho(x,\Omega))^{-\alpha}.

If
\alpha+p>1
, then
there exist infinitely many bounded solutions
u
of problem (
1.2
). In particular, for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\gamma>0
, there exists a solution
u
to equation (
1.2
) such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x)=\gamma.

Remark 3.8
.

Let
(G,\omega,\mu)
be a spherically symmetric tree as above, with branching function

b(r)
. Moreover, let
\alpha\in[0,1]
be fixed, and let

V:G\to{\mathbb{R}},\qquad V(x)=(1+d(x,\Omega))^{-\alpha}.

On account of Theorem
3.3
we see that,
if
b(r)
is bounded
,
then there exists at most one bounded solution
to equation (
1.2
); on the other hand, if

b(r)=(1+r)^{p}
(for some
p\geq 2
),
it follows from Theorem

that there exists

infinitely many bounded solutions
of (
1.2
).

3.3.
Further results on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

Consider the
n-
dimensional
integer lattice graph
. Then
G=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
; furthermore,
x\sim y
if and only if there exists
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
x_{k}=y_{k}\pm 1
and
x_{i}=y_{i}
for
i\neq k
. The edge weight is

\omega:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)

\omega(x,y)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }y\sim x\\
0&\text{ if }y\not\sim x\,,\end{cases}

whereas the node measure is

\mu(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\omega(x,y)=2n\,.

We equip the graph
(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\omega,\mu)
with the euclidean distance

|x-y|=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad(x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n})\,.

Remark 3.9
.

We remark that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with the euclidean distance is not a weakly symmetric graph, since in the definition of weakly symmetric graph only the combinatorial graph distance is considered. Furthermore, it is easily seen that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
endowed with the combinatorial metric is not a weakly symmetric graph.

In this case the condition on
\alpha
made in (
3.7
) is not optimal.
In fact, the critical value is now
\alpha=2
, and not more
\alpha=1
.

More precisely, we can prove the next results.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Suppose that, for some
\alpha\in[0,2]
and
c_{0}>0
,

(3.11)

V\in\mathfrak{F},\quad V(x)\geq c_{0}\,(1+|x|)^{-\alpha}\quad\textrm{for all}\;\;x\in G.

Let
u
be a subsolution of equation (
1.2
) fulfilling
(
3.5
),
with
x_{0}=0
,
d(x,y)=|x-y|
and

(3.12)

Z(x):=\begin{cases}-|x|^{2-\alpha}&\text{ if }\alpha\in[0,2)\\
-\log|x|&\text{ if }\alpha=2\end{cases},\text{ whenever }|x|>2.

Then

u(x)\leq 0\quad\forall x\in G.

A direct consequence of Theorem
3.2
is the following uniqueness result.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}^{n},f\in\mathfrak{F}
. Assume that (
3.11
) holds. Then there exists at most one solution to equation (
1.1
) such that

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{u(x)}{Z(x)}=0\,,

where
Z
is given by (
3.12
).

The value
\alpha=2
in Corollary
3.11
is optimal. This follows from the next nonuniqueness result.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}^{n},n\geq 3
. Assume that

(3.13)

V\in\mathfrak{F},\quad 0<V(x)\leq c_{0}\,(1+|x|)^{-\alpha}\quad\textrm{for all}\;\;x\in G,

for some
\alpha>2
. Then there exist infinitely many bounded solutions to equation (
1.2
). More precisely, for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a bounded solution
u_{\gamma}
such that

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma\,.

4.
Auxiliary Results

In this section we collect some well-known facts
which will be exploited in the proofs of our main results.
To begin with, we prove the following simple lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let assumption (
2.2
) be satisfied. Let
\Omega\subset G
be a finite set and let
f\in\mathfrak{F}
be a spherically symmetric function with respect to
\Omega
.
Then

(4.1)

\Delta f(x)=\mathfrak{D}_{+}(x)[f(r+1)-f(r)]+\mathfrak{D}_{-}(x)[f(r-1)-f(r)]

for any
x\in G
with
r\equiv r(x):=\rho(x,\Omega)\geq 1.

Proof.

Let
x\in G
with
r\equiv r(x):=\rho(x,\Omega)\geq 1.

From (
2.3
) and Remark
2.3
we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Delta f(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta f(r(x))=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in G}[f(y)-f(x)]\omega(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in S_{r+1}(\Omega)\cup S_{r-1}(\Omega)}[f(y)-f(x)]\omega(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\Big\{\sum_{y\in S_{r+1}(\Omega)}[f(y)-f(x)]\omega(x,y)

\displaystyle+\sum_{y\in S_{r-1}(\Omega)}[f(y)-f(x)]\omega(x,y)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in S_{r+1}(\Omega)}[f(r+1)-f(r)]\omega(x,y)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in S_{r-1}(\Omega)}[f(r-1)-f(r)]\omega(x,y)

\displaystyle=[f(r+1)-f(r)]\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in S_{r+1}(\Omega)}\omega(x,y)

\displaystyle+[f(r-1)-f(r)]\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in S_{r-1}(\Omega)}\omega(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}_{+}(x)[f(r+1)-f(r)]+\mathfrak{D}_{-}(x)[f(r-1)-f(r)]\,.

Proof of Theorem

Let

Coalgebraic Geometric Logic: Basic Theory
Nick Bezhanishvili \rsuper a, Jim de Groot ORCID:0000-0003-1375-6758 \rsuper b, Yde Venema \rsuper a

Abstract
. Using the theory of coalgebra, we introduce a uniform framework for adding modalities to the language of propositional geometric logic. Models for this logic are based on coalgebras for an endofunctor on some full subcategory of the category of topological spaces and continuous functions. We investigate derivation systems, soundness and completeness for such geometric modal logics, and we specify a method of lifting an endofunctor on 𝐒𝐞𝐭 \mathbf{Set} , accompanied by a collection of predicate liftings, to an endofunctor on the category of topological spaces, again accompanied by a collection of (open) predicate liftings. Furthermore, we compare the notions of modal equivalence, behavioural equivalence and bisimulation on the resulting class of models, and we provide a final object for the corresponding category.

1.
Introduction

Propositional geometric logic arose at the interface of (pointfree) topology,
logic and theoretical computer science as the logic of
finite
observations

[
Abr87
,
Vic89
]
.
Its language is constructed from a set of proposition letters by applying finite
conjunctions and arbitrary disjunctions, these being the propositional
operations preserving the property of finite observability.
Through an interesting topological connection, formulas of geometric logic
can be interpreted in the frame of open sets of a topological space.
Central to this connection is the well-known dual adjunction between the category
\mathbf{Frm}
of
frames and frame morphisms and the category
\mathbf{Top}
of topological spaces
and continuous maps, which restricts to several interesting Stone-type
dualities
[
Joh82
]
.

Coalgebraic logic is a framework in which generalised versions of modal logics
are developed parametric in the signature of the language and a functor

\mathsf{T}:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}
on some base category
\mathbf{C}
.
With classical propositional logic as base logic, two natural choices for the base
category are
\mathbf{Set}
, the category of sets and functions, and
\mathbf{Stone}
,
the category of Stone spaces and continuous functions, i.e. the
topological dual to the algebraic category of Boolean algebras.
Coalgebraic logic for endofunctors on
\mathbf{Set}
has been well investigated and
still is an active area of research, see e.g.
[
CKP+08
,
KP11
]
.
In this setting, modal operators can be defined using the notion of relation
lifting
[
Mos99
]
or predicate lifting
[
Pat03
]
.
Coalgebraic logic in the category of Stone coalgebras has been studied
in
[
KKV04
,
HK04
,
ES18
,
BEG20
]
, and there is
a fairly extensive literature on the design of a coalgebraic modal logic based
on a general Stone-type duality (or dual adjunction), see for
instance
[
BK05
,
BK06
,
CJ14
,
Kli07
]
and references therein.

In this paper we investigate some links between coalgebraic logic and geometric
logic.
That is, we use methods from coalgebraic logic to introduce modal operators
to the language of geometric logic, with the intention of studying
interpretations of these logics in certain topological coalgebras.
Note that extensions of geometric logic with the basic modalities
\Box
and

\Diamond
, which are closely related to the topological Vietoris construction,
have received much attention in the literature, see
[
Vic89
]
for some early
history.
A first step towards developing coalgebraic geometric logic was taken
in
[
VVV13
]
, where a method is explored to lift a functor on
\mathbf{Set}

to a functor on the category
\mathbf{KHaus}
of compact Hausdorff spaces, and the
connection is investigated between the lifted functor and a relation-lifting
based “cover” modality.

Our aim here is to develop a framework for the coalgebraic geometric logics that
arise if we extend geometric logic with modalities that are induced by
appropriate
predicate liftings
.
Guided by the connection between geometric logic and topological spaces, we
choose the base category of our framework to be
\mathbf{Top}
itself, or one of
its full subcategories such as
\mathbf{Sob}
(sober spaces),
\mathbf{KSob}
(compact
sober spaces) or
\mathbf{KHaus}
(compact Hausdorff spaces).
On this base category
\mathbf{C}
we then consider an arbitrary endofunctor

\mathsf{T}
which serves as the type of our topological coalgebras.
Furthermore, we shall see that if we want our formulas to be interpreted as

open sets
of the coalgebra carrier, we need the predicate liftings that
interpret the modalities of the language to satisfy some natural
openness

condition.
Summarizing, we shall study the coalgebraic geometric logic induced by
(1) a functor
\mathsf{T}:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}
, where
\mathbf{C}
is a full
subcategory of
\mathbf{Top}
, and
(2) a set
\Lambda
of open predicate liftings for
\mathsf{T}
.
As running examples we take the combination of the basic modalities for the
Vietoris functor, and that of the monotone box and diamond modalities for
various topological manifestations of the monotone neighbourhood functor on

\mathbf{Set}
.
The structures providing the semantics for our coalgebraic geometric logics are
the
\mathsf{T}
-models
comprised of a
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra together with a
valuation mapping proposition letters to open sets in the coalgebra carrier.

The main results that we report on here are the following:

•

Section
4
contains a detailed description of the
monotone
neighbourhood functor
on
\mathbf{KHaus}
, which naturally extends the monotone
functor on
\mathbf{Stone}

[
HK04
]
that corresponds to monotone modal logic.

•

In Section
5
we discuss derivation systems for coalgebraic geometric
logic, based on
consequence pairs
, and derive a general completeness result.

•

After that, in Section
6
we adapt the method of
[
KKP04
]
in order to
lift a
\mathbf{Set}
-functor together with a collection of predicate liftings to
an endofunctor on
\mathbf{Top}
.
We obtain the Vietoris functor and monotone functor on
\mathbf{KHaus}
as
restrictions of such lifted functors.

•

In Section
7
, we construct a final object in the
category of
\mathsf{T}
-models, where
\mathsf{T}
is an endofunctor on
\mathbf{Top}

which preserves sobriety and admits a Scott-continuous, characteristic geometric
modal signature.

•

Finally, in Section
8
we transfer the notion of

\Lambda
-bisimilarity from
[
GS13
,
BH17
]
to our setting, and we
compare this to geometric modal equivalence, behavioural equivalence and
Aczel-Mendler bisimilarity.
Our main finding is that on the categories
\mathbf{Top}
,
\mathbf{Sob}
and

\mathbf{KSob}
, the first three notions coincide, provided
\Lambda
and
\mathsf{T}

meet some reasonable conditions.

We finish the paper with listing some questions for further research.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Categories and functors

We use a
\mathbf{bold}

\mathbf{font}
for categories. We assume familiarity with
the following categories and functors:

•

\mathbf{Set}
is the category of sets and functions;

•

\mathbf{Top}
is the category of topological spaces and continuous

•

\mathbf{KHaus}

\mathbf{Stone}
are the full subcategories of

\mathbf{Top}
whose objects are compact Hausdorff spaces and Stone spaces,

•

\mathbf{BA}
is the category of Boolean algebras and Boolean algebra

Categories can be connected by functors. We use a
\mathsf{sans}

\mathsf{serif}

\mathsf{font}
for
functors. In particular, the following functors are regularly used in this

•

\mathsf{U}:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}

\mathbf{Top}

abuse notation and also call it

•

\mathsf{P}:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}

\mathbf{BA}
;

•

is the contravariant functor
that sends a topological space to

Note that
\breve{\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{U}_{\mathbf{BA}}\circ\mathsf{Q}
,
where
\mathsf{U}_{\mathbf{BA}}:\mathbf{BA}\to\mathbf{Set}

More categories and
functors will be defined along the way. We use the symbol
\equiv
for

2.2.
Coalgebra

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a category and
\mathsf{T}
an endofunctor on
\mathbf{C}
. A

\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra
is a pair
(X,\gamma)
where
X
is an object in

\mathbf{C}
and
\gamma:X\to\mathsf{T}X
is a morphism in
\mathbf{C}
.
A
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra morphism
between two
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebras

(X,\gamma)
and
(X^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is a morphism
f:X\to X^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{C}

satisfying
\gamma^{\prime}\circ f=\mathsf{T}f\circ\gamma
.
The collection of
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebras and
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra morphisms forms
a category, which we shall denote by
\mathbf{Coalg}(\mathsf{T})
. The category

\mathbf{C}
is called the
base category
of
\mathbf{Coalg}(\mathsf{T})
.

The notion of an
algebra
for
\mathsf{T}
is defined dually, and gives
rise to the category
\mathbf{Alg}(\mathsf{T})
.

{exa}

[Kripke frames]
Kripke frames correspond 1-1 with
\mathsf{P}
-coalgebras. For a Kripke frame

(X,R)
define
\gamma_{R}:X\to\mathsf{P}X:x\mapsto\{y\mid xRy\}
.
Then
(X,\gamma_{R})
is a
\mathsf{P}
-coalgebra. Conversely, for a

\mathsf{P}
-coalgebra
(X,\gamma)
define
R_{\gamma}
by
xR_{\gamma}y
iff

y\in\gamma(x)
. Then
(X,R_{\gamma})
is a Kripke frame. It is not hard to
see that
R_{\gamma_{R}}=R
and
\gamma_{R_{\gamma}}=\gamma
, so we obtain a
bijection between Kripke frames and
\mathsf{P}
-coalgebras. Moreover, bounded
morphisms between Kripke frames are precisely
\mathsf{P}
-coalgebra morphisms.
Thus, we have

\mathbf{Krip}\cong\mathbf{Coalg}(\mathsf{P}),

where
\mathbf{Krip}
is the category of Kripke frames and bounded morphisms.

{exa}

[Monotone neighbourhood frames]
Let
\mathsf{D}:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
be the functor given on objects by

\mathsf{D}X=\{W\subseteq\mathsf{P}X\mid\text{ if }a\in W\text{ and }a\subseteq b\text{ then }b\in W\},

where
X
is a set. For a morphism
f:X\to X^{\prime}
define

\mathsf{D}f:\mathsf{D}X\to\mathsf{D}X^{\prime}:W\mapsto\{a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{P}X^{\prime}\mid f^{-1}(a^{\prime})\in W\}.

Then the category of monotone frames and bounded morphisms is isomorphic to

\mathbf{Coalg}(\mathsf{D})

[
Che80
,
Han03
,
HK04
]
.

n

\displaystyle\llbracket p\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=V(p),\quad\llbracket\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}\cap\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}},\quad\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=X\setminus\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}},

\displaystyle\llbracket\heartsuit^{\lambda}(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n})\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=\gamma^{-1}(\lambda(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}},\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}})),

2.4.
Geometric logic

Let
\Phi
be a set of proposition letters. The language
\GL(\Phi)
of geometric
formulas is given by

\varphi::=\top\mid\bot\mid p\mid\varphi\wedge\varphi\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}

where
p\in\Phi
and
I
is some index set.
Coherent
formulas are
defined in the same way, but without infinitary disjuctions. These are also known
as the formulas from
positive
logic.

We describe the logical system as a collection of
binary consequence pairs, written as
\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi
.
A
geometric logic
is a collection consequence pairs closed under the
following rules:

identity

\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\varphi,

cut

\dfrac{\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi\quad\psi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\chi}{\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\chi},

the
conjunction rules

\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\top,\qquad\varphi\wedge\psi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\varphi,\qquad\varphi\wedge\psi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi,\qquad\dfrac{\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi\quad\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\chi}{\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi\wedge\chi},

the
disjunction rules

\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\bigvee S\quad(\varphi\in S),\qquad\dfrac{\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi\quad(\text{all }\varphi\in S)}{\bigvee S\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi}

and
frame distributivity

\varphi\wedge\bigvee S\mathrel{\triangleleft}\bigvee\{\varphi\wedge\psi\mid\psi\in S\}.

Note that these are in fact all schemata.
We write
\mathscr{GL}
for the minimal geometric logic, i.e. the
smallest collection of consequence pairs closed under the axioms and rules
given above. We write
\varphi\vdash_{\mathscr{GL}}\psi
if the consequence
pair
\varphi\mathrel{\triangleleft}\psi
is in
\mathscr{GL}
.

Note that frame distributivity allows us to reduce every formula to a
disjunction of finite conjunctions of proposition letters. Therefore, modulo
equivalence, the formulas form a set. A collection
S
of geometric formulas is
called
directed
if for every pair
\varphi,\psi\in S
there exists

\chi\in S
such that
\varphi\vdash\chi
and
\psi\vdash\chi
.

The topological semantics and algebraic semantics of geometric logic are given
by topological spaces and frames.

2.5.
Frames and spaces

A
frame
is a complete lattice
F
in which for all
a\in F
and

S\subseteq F
the infinite distributive law holds:

a\wedge\bigvee S=\bigvee\{a\wedge s\mid s\in S\}.

A
frame homomorphism
is a function between frames that preserves finite
meets and arbitrary joins.

For
a,b\in F
we say that
a
is
well inside

b
, notation:

a\eqslantless b
, if there is a
c\in F
such that
c\wedge a=\bot
and

c\vee b=\top
. An element
a\in F
is called
regular
if

a=\bigvee\{b\in F\mid b\eqslantless a\}
and a frame is called
regular

if all of its elements are regular. The
negation
of
a\in F
is defined
as
{\sim}a=\bigvee\{b\in F\mid a\wedge b=\bot\}
.
A frame is said to be
compact
if
\bigvee S=\top
implies that there
is a finite subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
such that
\bigvee S^{\prime}=\top
.

Lemma 1
.

For all elements
a,b
in a frame
F
we have
a\eqslantless b
iff

{\sim}a\vee b=\top
.

Proof 2.1
.

See
[
Joh82
, III1.1]
.

Lemma 2
.

Finite meets and arbitrary joins of regular elements are regular.

Proof 2.2
.

It is known that
d\leq c\eqslantless a\leq b
implies
d\eqslantless b
.
We first show that
c\eqslantless a
and
d\eqslantless b
implies
c\wedge d\eqslantless a\wedge b
.
It is clear that
c\wedge d\eqslantless a
and
c\wedge d\eqslantless b
. Since

{\sim}(c\wedge d)\vee(a\wedge b)=({\sim}(c\wedge d)\vee a)\wedge({\sim}(c\wedge d)\vee b)=\top\wedge\top=\top
we know
c\wedge d\eqslantless a\wedge b
.

Now suppose
a
and
b
are regular elements, then

a\wedge b=\bigvee\{c\mid c\eqslantless a\}\wedge\bigvee\{d\mid d\eqslantless b\}=\bigvee\{c\wedge d\mid c\eqslantless a,d\eqslantless b\}\leq\bigvee\{c\mid c\eqslantless a\wedge b\}\leq a\wedge b,

so
a\wedge b
is regular. If
a_{i}
is regular for all
i
in some index set

I
, then

\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\Big(\bigvee\{c\mid c\eqslantless a_{i}\}\Big)\leq\bigvee\Big\{c\mid c\eqslantless\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i}\Big\}\leq\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i},

so an arbitrary join of regular elements is regular.

Frames can be presented by generators and relations.

{defi}

A
presentation
is a pair
\langle G,R\rangle
where
G
is a set of
generators and
R
is a collection of relations between expressions constructed
from the generators using arbitrary joins and finite meets.

Let
F
be a frame and
\mathsf{Z}F
its underlying set. We say that

\langle G,R\rangle

presents

F
if there is an assignment

f:G\to\mathsf{Z}F
of the generators such that
1
,
2
and
3
hold:

(1)

The set
\{f(g)\mid g\in G\}
generates
F
,
that is, every element of
F
can be obtained from

\{f(g)\mid g\in G\}
using finite meets and arbitrary joins in
F
.

The assignment
f
can be extended to an assignement
\widetilde{f}
for any
expression
x
build from the generators in
G
using
\wedge
and
\bigvee
.
We require:

(2)

If
x=x^{\prime}
is a relation in
R
, then

\widetilde{f}(x)=\widetilde{f}(x^{\prime})
in
F
.

(3)

For any frame
F^{\prime}
and assignment

satisfying property

there exists a frame homomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Z}F}

.

from a frame

Fact 4
.

Any presentation by generators and relations presents a unique frame.

The collection of open sets of a topological space
\mathbb{X}
forms a frame,
denoted
\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}
. A continuous map
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}^{\prime}

induces
\mathsf{opn}f=f^{-1}:\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\to\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}
and

is a contravariant functor
\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Frm}
. A frame is called
spatial
if it isomorphic to
\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}
for some topological space
\mathbb{X}
.

A
point
of a frame
F
is a frame homomorphism
p:F\to 2
, with

2=\{\top,\bot\}
the two-element frame. Let
\mathsf{pt}F
be the
collection of points of
F
endowed with the topology
\{\widetilde{a}\mid a\in F\}
, where
\widetilde{a}=\{p\in\mathsf{pt}F\mid p(a)=\top\}
. For a frame

define
\mathsf{pt}f:\mathsf{pt}F^{\prime}\to\mathsf{pt}F
by

p\mapsto p\circ f
. The assignment
\mathsf{pt}
defines a contravariant functor

\mathbf{Frm}\to\mathbf{Top}
.
A topological space that arises as the space of points of a lattice is called

sober
. The
sobrification
of a topological space
\mathbb{X}
is

\mathsf{pt}(\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X})
.

We denote by
\mathbf{Sob}
and
\mathbf{KSob}
the full subcategories of
\mathbf{Top}

whose objects are sober spaces and compact sober spaces, respectively. Where

\mathbf{Frm}
is the category of frames and frame homomorphisms,

\mathbf{Frm}

Fact 5
.

The functor
\mathsf{pt}
is dually adjoint to
\mathsf{opn}
.
This adjunction restricts to a duality between the category of spatial frames
and the category of sober spaces,

\mathbf{SFrm}\equiv\mathbf{Sob}^{\op}.

This duality restricts to the dualities

\mathbf{KSFrm}\equiv\mathbf{KSob}^{\op}

and

\mathbf{KRFrm}\equiv\mathbf{KHaus}^{\op}.

For a more thorough exposition of frames and spaces, and a proof of the

]
.
We explicitly mention one isomorphism which is part of this duality, for we
will encounter it later on.

Remark 6
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a sober space. Then Fact

entails that
there is an isomorphism
\mathbb{X}\to\mathsf{pt}(\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X})
. This
isomorphism is given by

, where

p_{x}:\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}\to 2:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a\mapsto\top&\text{ if $x\in a$}\\
a\mapsto\bot&\text{ if $x\notin a$}\end{array}\right.

for all

3.
Logic for topological coalgebras

Although not all of our results can be proved for every full subcategory of

\mathbf{Top}
, we will give the basic definitions in full generality. To this
end, we let
\mathbf{C}
be some full subcategory of
\mathbf{Top}
and define
coalgebraic logic with
\mathbf{C}
as base category.
In particular
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{KHaus}
and
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{Sob}
will be of interest. Throughout this
section
\mathsf{T}
is an arbitrary endofunctor on
\mathbf{C}
.
Recall that
\mathsf{\Omega}:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}
sends a topological
spaces to its set of opens, while
\mathsf{opn}:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Frm}
takes
a space to its collection of opens viewed as a frame.
Also, recall that
\Phi
is an arbitrary but fixed set of proposition letters.
We begin with defining the topological version of a predicate
lifting, called an
open
predicate lifting.

3.1.
Open predicate liftings

{defi}

An
open predicate lifting
for
\mathsf{T}
is a natural transformation

\lambda:\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}\to\mathsf{\Omega}\circ\mathsf{T}.

An open predicate lifting is called
monotone in its
i
-th argument

if for every
\mathbb{X}\in\mathbf{C}
and all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
we have
\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i},\ldots,a_{n})\subseteq\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\cup b,\ldots,a_{n})
, and

monotone
if it is monotone in every argument.
It is called
Scott-continuous
in its
i
-th argument if for every

\mathbb{X}\in\mathbf{C}
and every directed set
A\subseteq\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}

we have

\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,\bigcup A,\ldots,a_{n})=\bigcup_{b\in A}\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,b,\ldots,a_{n})

and
Scott-continuous
if it is Scott-continuous in every argument.

A collection of open predicate liftings for
\mathsf{T}
is called a

geometric modal signature
for
\mathsf{T}
.
A geometric modal signature for a functor
\mathsf{T}
is called
monotone

if every open predicate lifting in it is monotone,
Scott-continuous
if
every open predicate lifting in it is Scott-continuous, and
characteristic

if for every topological space
\mathbb{X}
in
\mathbf{C}
the collection

\{\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\text{ $n$-ary},a_{i}\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}\}

is a sub-base for the
topology on
\mathsf{T}\mathbb{X}
.

Remark 7
.

Using the fact that for any two (open) sets
a,b
the set
\{a,a\cup b\}

is directed, it is easy to see that Scott-continuity implies monotonicity.

Scott-continuity will play a rôle in Section
7
, where it is
used to show that the collection of formulas modulo (semantic) equivalence is
a set, rather than a proper class.

Let
\mathbb{S}
be the Sierpinski space, i.e. the two-element set
2=\{0,1\}

topologised by
\{\emptyset,\{1\},2\}
. For a topological space
\mathbb{X}

and
a\subseteq\mathsf{U}\mathbb{X}
let
\chi_{a}:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{S}
be the
characteristic map (i.e.
\chi_{a}(x)=1
iff
x\in a
). Note that
\chi_{a}
is
continuous if and only if

. Analogously to
predicate liftings for
\mathbf{Set}
-functors
[
Sch05
, Proposition 43]
, one
can classify
n
-ary predicate liftings as open subsets of
\mathsf{T}\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
This elucidates the analogy with predicate liftings for
\mathbf{Set}
-functors.

Proposition 8
.

Suppose
\mathbb{S}\in\mathbf{C}
, then there is a bijective correspondence between

n
-ary open predicate liftings and elements of
\mathsf{\Omega T}\mathbb{S}^{n}
. This
correspondence is given as follows: To an open predicate lifting
\lambda

assign the set

\lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(\{1\}),\ldots,\pi_{n}^{-1}(\{1\}))\in\mathsf{\Omega T}\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}
is the
i
-th
projection, and conversely, for

define

\lambda^{c}:\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}\to\mathsf{\Omega T}
by

\lambda^{c}_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=(\mathsf{T}\langle\chi_{a_{1}},\ldots,\chi_{a_{n}}\rangle)^{-1}(c)
.

Furthermore, there is a bijective correspondence between open
predicate liftings and continuous functions
\mathsf{T}\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}
.
This is established by identifying elements of
\mathsf{\Omega}\mathsf{T}\mathbb{S}^{n}

with their characteristic map
\mathsf{T}\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}
.
This view on predicate liftings has been investigated in
[
BKV15
, Section 7]
.

{defi}

The
language
induced by a geometric modal signature
\Lambda
is the
collection
\GML(\Phi,\Lambda)
of formulas defined by the grammar

\varphi::=\top\mid p\mid\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}\mid\heartsuit^{\lambda}(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}),

where
p
ranges over the set
\Phi
of proposition letters,
I
is some index
set, and
\lambda\in\Lambda
is
n
-ary. Abbreviate
\bot:=\bigvee\emptyset
.
We call a formula in
\GML(\Phi,\Lambda)

if it does not involve any
infinite disjunctions.

3.2.
Interpretation and examples

The language
\GML(\Phi,\Lambda)
is interpreted in so-called geometric

\mathsf{T}
-models.

{defi}

A
geometric
\mathsf{T}
-model
is a triple
\mathfrak{X}=(\mathbb{X},\gamma,V)

where
(\mathbb{X},\gamma)
is a
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra and

V:\Phi\to\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
is a valuation of the proposition letters.
A map
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
is a
geometric
\mathsf{T}
-model morphism

from
(\mathbb{X},\gamma,V)
to
(\mathbb{X}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},V^{\prime})
if
f
is a coalgebra
morphism between the underlying coalgebras and
V=f^{-1}\circ V^{\prime}
. The
collection of geometric
\mathsf{T}
-models and geometric
\mathsf{T}
-model morphisms
forms a category, which we denote by
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathsf{T})
.

{defi}

The
semantics
of
\varphi\in\GML(\Phi,\Lambda)
on a geometric
\mathsf{T}
-model

\mathfrak{X}=(\mathbb{X},\gamma,V)
is given recursively by

\displaystyle\llbracket\top\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=X,\quad\llbracket p\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=V(p),\quad\llbracket\varphi\wedge\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}},\quad\llbracket\bigvee_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=\bigcup_{i\in I}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}},

\displaystyle\llbracket\heartsuit^{\lambda}(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n})\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}=\gamma^{-1}(\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}},\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}})).

We write
\mathfrak{X},x\Vdash\varphi
iff
x\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}
. Two
states
x
and
x^{\prime}
are called
modally equivalent
if they satisfy the
same formulas, notation:
x\equiv_{\Lambda}x^{\prime}
.
We say that
\varphi
is a
semantic consequence
of
\psi
in
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathsf{T})
,
notation:
\varphi\Vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\psi
, if
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}
for all
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathbf{Mod}(\mathsf{T})
.

The following proposition shows that morphisms preserve truth. Its proof is
similar to the proof of Theorem 6.17 in
[
Ven17
]
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
\Lambda
be a geometric modal signature for
\mathsf{T}
.
Let
\mathfrak{X}=(\mathbb{X},\gamma,V)
and
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}=(\mathbb{X}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},V^{\prime})

be geometric
\mathsf{T}
-models and let

be a geometric

\mathsf{T}
-model morphism. Then for all
\varphi\in\GML(\Phi,\Lambda)
and

we have

\mathfrak{X},x\Vdash\varphi\quad\text{iff}\quad\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},f(x)\Vdash\varphi.

We state the notion of behavioural equivalence for future reference.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(\mathbb{X},\gamma,V)

\mathsf{T}

\mathsf{T}

and
\mathsf{T}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Y}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

3.3.
Strong predicate liftings

In Section
8
it turns out to be useful to have a slightly
stronger notion of open predicate liftings, called strong open predicate
liftings, as this allows us to prove that behavioural equivalence implies
so-called
\Lambda
-bisimilarity.
Whereas the action of open predicate liftings is defined only on open subsets,
a strong open predicate lifting acts on
every
subset of elements of a
topological space.
Recall that
\mathsf{U}:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}
denotes the forgetful functor.

{defi}

A
strong open predicate lifting
for
\mathsf{T}:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}
is
a natural transformation

\mu:(\breve{\mathsf{P}}\circ\mathsf{U})^{n}\to\breve{\mathsf{P}}\circ\mathsf{U}\circ\mathsf{T}

such that for all
\mathbb{X}\in\mathbf{C}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
the set
\mu_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is open
in
\mathsf{T}\mathbb{X}
. Monotonicity and Scott-continuity of strong open
predicate liftings are defined in the standard way.

We call an open predicate lifting (from Definition
3.1
)

extendable
if it is the restriction of some
strong open predicate lifting and
monotone extendable
if it is the
restriction of a monotone strong open predicate lifting.
We call a geometric modal signature
\Lambda

(monotone) extendable

if all predicate liftings in it are (monotone) extendable.

Evidently, every strong open predicate lifting restricts to an open predicate
lifting, and it is only this weaker notion of open predicate lifting that has
an effect on the semantics.
Our notion of strong open predicate lifting is similar to the notion of a

topological predicate lifting
for endofunctors on
\mathbf{Stone}
, which
was introduced in
[
ES18
]
.

{exa}

The predicate lifting corresponding to the box modality from Example
3.2

is (monotone) extendable, for it is the restriction of
\mu:\mathsf{U}\to\mathsf{U}\circ\mathsf{V}_{\mathbf{kh}}
given by
\mu_{\mathbb{X}}(u)=\{b\in\mathsf{V}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}\mid b\subseteq u\}
.
Likewise, all other predicate liftings from Examples
3.2
,
3.2
and the monotone functor from Section
4
are extendable
as well.

We devote the remainder of this section to investigating strong open predicate
liftings.
Recall from Example
3.2
that
\mathbbm{2}
denotes the two-element set
with the trivial topology. We claim that natural transformations

\mu:(\breve{\mathsf{P}}\circ\mathsf{U})^{n}\to\breve{\mathsf{P}}\circ\mathsf{U}\circ\mathsf{T}
correspond one-to-one with elements of
\mathsf{\breve{P}UT}\mathbbm{2}
,
provided
\mathbbm{2}\in\mathbf{C}
: To a natural transformation
\mu
associate
the set
\mu_{\mathbbm{2}}(p_{1}^{-1}(\{1\}),\ldots,p_{n}^{-1}(\{1\}))
, where

p_{i}:\mathbbm{2}^{n}\to\mathbbm{2}
denotes the
i
-th projection.
Conversely, for
c\in\mathsf{\breve{P}UT}\mathbbm{2}
define
\mu^{c}
by

\mu^{c}_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=(\mathsf{T}\langle\chi_{a_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,\chi_{a_{n}}^{\prime}\rangle)^{-1}(c)
, where
\mathbb{X}
is a topological space,

a\subseteq\mathsf{U}\mathbb{X}
and
\chi_{a}^{\prime}:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbbm{2}
is the
characteristic map. Note that
\chi^{\prime}_{a}
is continuous regardless of whether
a

is open or not, hence
\mathsf{T}
acts on all
\chi_{a}^{\prime}
. Details of the bijection
are left to the reader.

Proposition 10
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be an endofunctor on
\mathbf{C}
and suppose that
\mathbf{C}
contains
the spaces
\mathbbm{2}
and
\mathbb{S}
.
Let
s:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbbm{2}
be the identity map and let

c\in\mathsf{\breve{P}UT}\mathbbm{2}^{n}
. The natural transformation
\mu^{c}
is a
strong open predicate lifting if and only if
(\mathsf{T}s^{n})^{-1}(c)\subseteq\mathsf{T}\mathbb{S}^{n}
is open.

Proof 3.1
.

We give the proof for the case
n=1
, the general case being similar.
Left to right follows from the fact that
\{1\}
is open in
\mathbb{S}
, hence

\mu_{\mathbb{S}}^{c}(\{1\})=(\mathsf{T}\chi^{\prime}_{\{1\}})^{-1}(c)=(\mathsf{T}s)^{-1}(c)

must be open in
\mathsf{T}\mathbb{S}
.
For the converse, let
\mathbb{X}
be a topological space and

a\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
. We need to show that
\mu^{c}_{\mathbb{X}}(a)
is open.
Since
a
is open, the characteristic map
\chi_{a}:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{S}
is
continuous and hence
\chi^{\prime}_{a}=s\circ\chi_{a}
. We have

\displaystyle\mu^{c}_{\mathbb{X}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{T}\chi_{a}^{\prime})^{-1}(c)

(Definition of
\mu^{c}
)

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{T}(s\circ\chi_{a}))^{-1}(c)

(
\chi^{\prime}_{a}=s\circ\chi_{a}
)

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{T}s\circ\mathsf{T}\chi_{a})^{-1}(c)

(Definition of functors)

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{T}\chi_{a})^{-1}\circ(\mathsf{T}s)^{-1}(c).

(Definition of inverse)

Since
\mathsf{T}\chi_{a}
is continuous and
(\mathsf{T}s)^{-1}(c)
is assumed to be

\mathsf{T}\mathbb{S}
, the set
\mu^{c}_{\mathbb{X}}(a)
is open in

\mathsf{T}\mathbb{X}
.

The following proposition gives two sufficient conditions on
\mathsf{T}
for its
open predicate liftings to be extendable. For a full subcategory
\mathbf{C}
of

\mathbf{Top}
let
\mathbf{preC}
denote the category of topological spaces in

\mathbf{C}
and (not necessarily continuous) functions.

Proposition 11
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be an endofunctor on
\mathbf{C}
and suppose
\mathbbm{2},\mathbb{S}\in\mathbf{C}
.

(1)

If
\mathsf{T}
preserves injective functions then every open predicate
lifting for
\mathsf{T}
is extendable.

(2)

If
\mathsf{T}
extends to
\mathbf{preC}
, then every open predicate
lifting for
\mathsf{T}
is extendable.

Proof 3.2
.

For the first item, let
c\in\mathsf{\Omega T}\mathbb{S}^{n}
determine the
n
-ary
open predicate lifting
\lambda^{c}
. Since
s^{n}
is injective, by assumption

\mathsf{T}s^{n}
is as well, and hence
c=(\mathsf{UT}s^{n})^{-1}((\mathsf{UT}s^{n})[c])
.
Proposition
10
now implies that
\mu^{(\mathsf{UT}s^{n})[c]}
is a
strong open predicate lifting. It is easy to see that
\mu^{(\mathsf{UT}s^{n})[c]}

extends
\lambda^{c}
, hence the latter is extendable.

For the second item we show that, under the assumption,
\mathsf{T}
preserves
injective functions. Let
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
be an injective function
in
\mathbf{C}
, then there exists a (not necessarily continuous) function

g:\mathbb{Y}\to\mathbb{X}
satisfying
g\circ f=\id_{\mathbb{X}}
. Then

\mathsf{T}g\circ\mathsf{T}f=\mathsf{T}(g\circ f)=\mathsf{T}\id_{\mathbb{X}}=\id_{\mathsf{T}\mathbb{X}}
, so
\mathsf{T}f
has a (set-theoretic) left-inverse,
hence is injective.

Monotone open predicate lifting (hence also Scott-continuous ones) for an
endofunctor on
\mathbf{KHaus}
are always extendable:

Proposition 12
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be an endofunctor on
\mathbf{KHaus}
and
\Lambda
a monotone

\mathsf{T}
. Then
\Lambda
is monotone extendable.

Proof 3.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda
. We need to show that
\lambda
is the restriction
of some monotone strong predicate lifting. Define

\widetilde{\lambda}_{\mathbb{X}}:\breve{\mathsf{P}}^{n}\mathsf{U}\mathbb{X}\to\breve{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{UT}\mathbb{X}:(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\mapsto\bigcap\{\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\mid a_{i}\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}\text{ and }a_{i}\supseteq b_{i}\}.

Monotonicity of
\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}
ensures
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\mathbb{X}}(a)=\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(a)
for all
a\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
and

\widetilde{\lambda}
is monotone by construction. So we only need to show that

\widetilde{\lambda}
is indeed a strong open predicate lifting, i.e. a natural
transformation
\breve{\mathsf{P}}^{n}\mathsf{U}\mathbb{X}\to\breve{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{UT}\mathbb{X}
. We assume
\lambda
to be unary,
the general case being similar.

For a continuous map
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
between compact Hausdorff
spaces we need to show that

\widetilde{\lambda}_{\mathbb{X}}\circ f^{-1}=(\mathsf{T}f)^{-1}\circ\widetilde{\lambda}_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}
. Since, by naturality
of
\lambda
, the right hand side is equal to

\bigcap\{\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(f^{-1}(a^{\prime}))\mid a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\text{ and }b^{\prime}\subseteq a^{\prime}\}
,
it suffices to show

\bigcap\{\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(c)\mid c\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}\text{ and }f^{-1}(b^{\prime})\subseteq c\}=\bigcap\{\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(f^{-1}(a^{\prime}))\mid a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\text{ and }b^{\prime}\subseteq a^{\prime}\}.

(2)

If
a^{\prime}
is an open superset of
b^{\prime}
then clearly
f^{-1}(b^{\prime})\subseteq f^{-1}(a^{\prime})
. So every element in the intersection of the right hand side is
contained in the one on the left hand side and therefore we have
\subseteq

in (

).
For the converse, suppose
c\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
and
f^{-1}(b^{\prime})\subseteq c
. Then the set
a^{\prime}=X^{\prime}\setminus f[X\setminus c]
is open,
contains

, and satisfies
f^{-1}(b^{\prime})\subseteq f^{-1}(a^{\prime})\subseteq c
.
Therefore
\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(f^{-1}(a^{\prime}))
is one of the elements in the
intersection on the left hand side of (

\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(f^{-1}(a^{\prime}))\subseteq\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}(c)
this shows
“
\supseteq
” in (
2
).

4.
The monotone neighbourhood functor on
\mathbf{KHaus}

In this section we define the monotone neighbourhood functor on
\mathbf{Frm}
and
show that it (individually) preserves regularity and compactness. This functor
is a variation of the Vietoris Locale
[
Joh85
, Section 1]
.
Subsequently, we give a functor on
\mathbf{KHaus}
which is dual to the
restriction of the monotone neighbourhood functor to
\mathbf{KRFrm}
.

4.1.
The monotone neighbourhood frame

{defi}

For a frame
F
, let
\mathsf{M}F
be the frame generated by
\Box a,\Diamond a
,
where
a
ranges over
F
, subject to the relations

(1)

\Box(a\wedge b)\leq\Box a

(2)

\Box a\wedge\Diamond b=\bot\;\text{ whenever }a\wedge b=\bot

(3)

\Box\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{\Box a\mid a\in A\}

(4)

\Diamond a\leq\Diamond(a\vee b)

(5)

\Box a\vee\Diamond b=\top\;\text{ whenever }a\vee b=\top

(6)

\Diamond\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{\Diamond a\mid a\in A\}
,

where
a,b\in F
and
A
is a directed subset of
F
.
For a homomorphism
f:F\to F^{\prime}
define
\mathsf{M}f:\mathsf{M}F\to\mathsf{M}F^{\prime}
on
generators by
\Box a\mapsto\Box f(a)
and
\Diamond a\mapsto\Diamond f(a)
.
The assignment
\mathsf{M}
defines a functor on
\mathbf{Frm}
.

The proof of the following proposition closely resembles that of Proposition III4.3 in
[
Joh82
]
. In a similar manner one can show that
\mathsf{M}

preserves complete regularity and zero-dimensionaity.

Proposition 13
.

If
F
is a regular frame, then so is
\mathsf{M}F
.

Proof 4.1
.

We need to show that for all
c\in\mathsf{M}F
we have
c=\bigvee\{d\in\mathsf{M}F\mid d\eqslantless c\}
. It follows from Lemma
2
that it
suffices to focus on the generators of
\mathsf{M}F
. Let
a\in F
, then we know

\bigvee\{d\in\mathsf{M}F\mid d\eqslantless\Box a\}\leq\Box a
. Suppose
b\eqslantless a

in
F
, then by Lemma
1

{\sim}b\vee a=\top
and hence

\Diamond{\sim}b\vee\Box a=\top
. Also
{\sim}b\wedge b=\bot
so it
follows from
2
that
\Diamond{\sim}b\wedge\Box b=\bot
. This
proves
\Box b\eqslantless\Box a
, because the element
\Diamond{\sim}b
is such that

\Diamond{\sim}b\vee\Box a=\top
and
\Diamond{\sim}b\wedge\Box b=\bot
. Since
F
is regular and
\{b\in F\mid b\eqslantless a\}
is directed, it
follows that

\Box a=\Box\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{b\in F\mid b\eqslantless a\}=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{\Box b\in\mathsf{M}F\mid b\eqslantless a\}\leq\bigvee\{d\in\mathsf{M}F\mid d\eqslantless\Box a\}\leq\Box a

so
\Box a=\bigvee\{d\in\mathsf{M}F\mid d\eqslantless\Box a\}
. In a similar
fashion one may show that
\Diamond a=\bigvee\{d\in\mathsf{M}F\mid d\eqslantless\Diamond a\}
. This proves the proposition.

We now prove that the functor
\mathsf{M}
preserves compactness. We proceed
in a similar manner as
[
VVV13
, Theorem 4.2]
. This relies on an auxiliary definition
and lemma (Definition
4.1
and Lemma
14
), in
which we give an alternative description of
\mathsf{M}F
. We then prove that
this alternative description preserves compactness.

In
[
Gro18
, Corollary 3.42]
we proved the same result by first giving a duality
result between frames and topological spaces, and then proving preservation
of compactness on the topological side. The main difference between that
proof and the one we present here is that the current one is constructive.

Write
\mathsf{P}_{\omega}
of the finite powerset functor and recall that

\mathsf{Z}:\mathbf{Frm}\to\mathbf{Set}
is the forgetful functor.

{defi}

For a frame
F
define
\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F
to be the free frame generated by

\mathsf{P}_{\omega}\mathsf{Z}F\times\mathsf{P}_{\omega}\mathsf{Z}F
, qua join-semilattice
(that is, the
join in
\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F
is given by
(\gamma,\delta)\vee(\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})=(\gamma\cup\gamma^{\prime},\delta\cup\delta^{\prime})
),
subject to

(1)

(\gamma\cup\{a\wedge b\},\delta)\leq(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta)

(2)

(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta)\wedge(\gamma,\delta\cup\{b\})\leq(\gamma,\delta)

if
a\wedge b=0

(3)

(\gamma\cup\{\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A\},\delta)\leq\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}_{a\in A}(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta)

(4)

(\gamma,\delta\cup\{a\})\leq(\gamma,\delta\cup\{a\vee b\})

(5)

\top\leq(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta\cup\{b\})

if
a\vee b=1

(6)

(\gamma,\{\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A\}\cup\delta)\leq\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}_{a\in A}(\gamma,\{a\}\cup\delta)

This results in a frame isomorphic to
\mathsf{M}F
:

Lemma 14
.

Let
F
be a frame. Then
\mathsf{M}F\cong\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F
.

Proof 4.2
.

Define
\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F\to\mathsf{M}F:(\gamma,\delta)\mapsto\bigvee_{c\in\gamma}\Box c\vee\bigvee_{d\in\delta}\Diamond d
and

\mathsf{M}F\to\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Box a\mapsto(\{a\},\emptyset)\\
\Diamond a\mapsto(\emptyset,\{a\})\end{array}\right.

Clearly these define a bijection. Furthermore it
is straightforward to verify that these maps are well defined by checking that
the images of generators satisfy relations of the respective frame.

Theorem 15
.

Suppose
F
is compact. Then
\mathsf{M}F
is compact.

Proof 4.3
.

The frame
\mathsf{M}D
is compact iff there is a preframe homomorphism

\varphi:\mathsf{M}D\to 2
that is right adjoint to the unique frame homomorphism

!:2\to\mathsf{M}D
, where
2=\{0,1\}
is the two-element frame.

By Proposition
14
we have
\mathsf{M}D\cong\mathsf{M^{\prime}}D
, and since
all the relations in Definition
4.1
are join-stable, we can use the
preframe coverage theorem
[
JV06
, Theorem 5.1]
to find that
\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F
viewed
as a preframe is the preframe generated by
\mathsf{P}_{\omega}\mathsf{Z}F\times\mathsf{P}_{\omega}\mathsf{Z}F
qua poset, subject to the
the relations from Definition
4.1
.

Define

\varphi:\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F\to 2:(\gamma,\delta)\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ iff there exists a $c\in\gamma$ such
that $c\vee(\bigvee\delta)=1$}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right..

First we check that
\varphi
is indeed a pre-frame homomorphism. Since
\varphi
is
defined on generators, it suffices to show that it preservers the
relations
1
to
6
, because if it does it can be lifted in a
unique way to a preframe homomorphism
\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F\to 2
.
It is clear that
\varphi
is a monotone morphism (hence preserves the poset
structure of the generators). We check that
\varphi
preserves the
relations one by one.

(1)

If
\varphi(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta)=0
then
c\vee\bigvee\delta=\top_{F}

for all
c\in\gamma
and
(a\wedge b)\vee\bigvee\delta\leq a\vee\bigvee\delta=\top_{F}
.

(2)

Suppose
\varphi(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta)=1
and
\varphi(\gamma,\delta\cup\{b\})=1
. Then either there is some
c\in\gamma
such that

c\vee\bigvee\delta=\top_{F}
, which implies
\varphi(\gamma,\delta)=1
, or

a\vee\bigvee\delta=\top_{F}
. In the latter case, note that we also have some

c^{\prime}\in\gamma
such that
c^{\prime}\vee\bigvee\delta\vee b=\top_{F}
, so that

c^{\prime}\vee\bigvee\delta=c^{\prime}\vee\bigvee\delta\vee(a\wedge b)=(a\vee\bigvee\delta\vee c^{\prime})\wedge(c^{\prime}\vee\bigvee\delta\vee b)=\top_{F}\wedge\top_{F}=\top_{F}.

The first equality holds because
a\wedge b=\bot_{F}
. Again we find

\varphi(\gamma,\delta)=1
.

(3)

Suppose
\varphi(\gamma\cup\{\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A\},\delta)=1
, then either

c\vee(\bigvee\delta)=\top_{F}
for some
c\in\gamma
, or
\top_{F}=(\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A)\vee(\bigvee\delta)=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}_{a\in A}(a\vee(\bigvee\delta))
(note
that the latter is indeed a directed set, because
A
is). By compactness of

F
this gives
a\vee(\bigvee\delta)=\top_{F}
for some
a\in A
. So both
cases yield
\varphi\big(\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}_{a\in A}(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta)\big)=1
.

(4)

If
\varphi(\gamma,\delta\cup\{a\})=1
, then
c\vee\bigvee(\delta\cup\{a\})=\top_{F}
for some
c\in\gamma
, so
c\vee\bigvee(\delta\cup\{a\vee b\})\geq c\vee\bigvee(\delta\cup\{a\})=\top_{F}
.

(5)

If
a\vee b=\top_{F}
, then
a\vee\bigvee(\delta\cup\{b\})=\top_{F}
so

\varphi(\gamma\cup\{a\},\delta\cup\{b\})=1
.

(6)

Suppose
\varphi(\gamma,\{\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A\}\cup\delta)=1
, then, for some

c\in\gamma
, we have

\top_{F}=c\vee\bigvee(\{\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}A\}\cup\delta)=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}(c\vee a\vee\bigvee\delta)

and by compactness we must have
c\vee\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}(\{a\}\cup\delta)=\top_{F}

for one of the
a
.
(The set
\{c\vee a\vee\bigvee\delta\mid a\in A\}
is directed
and by
4
.)

Lastly, we need to verify that
\varphi
is right-adjoint to
!:2\to\mathsf{M^{\prime}}L

(defined by
1\mapsto\top_{\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F}=(\{\top_{F}\},\{\top_{F}\})
, and

0\mapsto\bot_{\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F}=(\emptyset,\emptyset)
). It suffices to show that

\varphi(!(p))\geq p
and
!(\varphi(\gamma,\delta))\leq(\gamma,\delta)
. For the first,
suppose
p=1
, then
!(p)
is the equivalence class of
(\{\top_{F}\},\{\top_{F}\})

and
\varphi(!(p))=1
. For the second, if
\varphi(\gamma,\delta)=1
, then there are

c\in\gamma
such that
c\vee(\bigvee\delta)=\top_{F}
(in particular

\delta\neq\emptyset
) and hence

\top_{\mathsf{M^{\prime}}F}=(\{\top_{F}\},\delta)=(\{c\vee(\bigvee\delta)\},\delta)\leq(\{c\},\delta)\leq(\gamma,\delta).

The first inequality follows from recalling that
\delta
is a finite set and
applying
6
repeatedly.
This completes the proof.

We now know that
\mathsf{M}
restricts to an endofunctor on
\mathbf{KRFrm}
.
We write
\mathsf{M}_{\mathbf{kr}}
for this restriction.

Remark 16
.

The category
\mathbf{Loc}
of locales and locale morphisms is the opposite of

\mathbf{Frm}
. Therefore, we can also view
\mathsf{M}
as an endofunctor on
locales and
\mathsf{M}A
as the monotone neighbourhood locale, where
A
is
a locale.

4.2.
Monotone neighbourhood functor on
\mathbf{KHaus}

We now describe the topological manifestation of the monotone neighbourhood
functor.

{defi}

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X,\tau)
be a compact Hausdorff space. Let

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
be the collection of sets
W\subseteq\mathsf{P}X

such that
u\in W
iff there exists a closed
c\subseteq u
such that every
open superset of
c
is in
W
. Endow
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
with the
topology generated by the subbase

\boxbar a:=\{W\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}\mid a\in W\},\quad\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{45.0}{$\boxslash$}}a:=\{W\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}\mid X\setminus a\notin W\},

where
a
ranges over
\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
.
For continuous functions
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
define

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f:\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}\to\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}^{\prime}:W\mapsto\{a\in\mathsf{P}X\mid f^{-1}(a)\in W\}
.

Lemma 17
.

If
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
is a morphism in
\mathbf{KHaus}
, then

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f
is a well-defined continuous function from

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
to
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
.

Proof 4.4
.

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f
is well-defined.   Let

W\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
. We need to show that
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f(W)\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
. Suppose
a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f(W)
.
Then
f^{-1}[a^{\prime}]\in W
, so there exists a closed
c\subseteq f^{-1}[a^{\prime}]
such
that
c\in W
. Since
\mathbb{X}
is compact and
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
is Hausdorff,

f[c]
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
. In addition we have
f[c]\subseteq a^{\prime}
.
Suppose
f[c]\subseteq b
for some open
b\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
, then

c\subseteq f^{-1}[b]
so
f^{-1}[b]\in W
and hence

b\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f(W)
. So all open supersets of
f[c]
are in

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f(W)
, and therefore
f[c]\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f(W)
.
Thus, for
a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f(W)
, there exists a closed subset (in
this case
f[c]
) of
a^{\prime}
with the property that every open superset is in

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f(W)
.

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f
is continuous.   For continuity we need to show
that both
(\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f)^{-1}(\boxbar a^{\prime})
and

(\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f)^{-1}(\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{45.0}{$\boxslash$}}a^{\prime})
are open in

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
, whenever
a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{\Omega}(\mathbb{X}^{\prime})
. It
follows from a straightforward computation that
(\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f)^{-1}(\boxbar a^{\prime})=\boxbar f^{-1}(a^{\prime})
, which is open in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}

by definition, and similarly
(\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}f)^{-1}(\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{45.0}{$\boxslash$}}a^{\prime})=\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{45.0}{$\boxslash$}}f^{-1}(a^{\prime})\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
.

For the time being, we regard
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}
as a functor

\mathbf{KHaus}\to\mathbf{Top}
, because we have no evidence yet that it
restricts to an endofunctor on
\mathbf{KHaus}
.
We aim to prove that
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}
is dual to the restriction of

\mathsf{M}
to
\mathbf{KRFrm}
. As a corollary, we then obtain that

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}
indeed restricts to
\mathbf{KHaus}
.

Theorem 18
.

If
\mathbb{X}
is a compact Hausdorff space then

\mathsf{pt}(\mathsf{M}(\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}))\cong\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}.

We temporarily fix a compact Hausdorff space
\mathbb{X}
and define the two maps
constituting a homeomorphism.

{defi}

For a compact Hausdorff space
\mathbb{X}
, define
\zeta:\mathsf{pt}\circ\mathsf{M}\circ\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}\to\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
by sending a prime
filter
p
to

W_{p}:={\uparrow}\{X\setminus a\mid p(\Diamond a)=\bot\}.

We have
W_{p}\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
because it is the up-set of a
collection of closed sets; indeed, for each
b\in W_{p}
there exists a closed
subset
X\setminus a\subseteq b
with
p(\Diamond a)=\bot
and by
definition all open supersets of
X\setminus a
are in
W_{p}
. Therefore

\zeta
is well defined.
In the converse direction we define:

{defi}

For a compact Hausdorff space
\mathbb{X}
, define

\theta:\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}\to\mathsf{pt}\circ\mathsf{M}\circ\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}:W\mapsto p_{W},

where
p_{W}
is given on generators by

p_{W}:\mathsf{M}\circ\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}\to 2:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Box a\mapsto\top&\text{ iff }a\in W\\
\Diamond a\mapsto\bot&\text{ iff }X\setminus a\in W\end{array}\right.

Lemma 19
.

The assignment
\theta
is well defined.

Proof 4.5
.

Since
p_{W}
is a frame homomorphisms defined on generators, it suffices to
check that the
p_{W}(\Box a)
and
p_{W}(\Diamond a)
(where the
a
range over

\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
) satisfy
1
through
6
from
Definition
4.1
. Let us check
1
,
2

and
3
, items
4
,
5

and
6
being similar.

(1)

If
p_{W}(\Box(a\cap b))=\top
then
a\cap b\in W
. Since
W
is
upward closed
a\in W
, so
p_{W}(\Box a)=\top
.

(2)

If
a\cap b=\emptyset
then
a\subseteq X\setminus b
. Suppose

p_{W}(\Box a)=\top
then
a\in W
so
X\setminus b\in W
so

p_{W}(\Diamond b)=\bot
hence
p_{W}(\Box a)\wedge p_{W}(\Diamond b)=\bot
.

(3)

We claim that for all
W\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
and directed
sets
A\subseteq\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
we have
\textstyle{\bigcup^{\hskip-3.1298pt{\uparrow}}}A\in W
iff
there is
a\in A
with
a\in W
. The direction from right to left
follows from the fact that
W
is upwards closed. Conversely, suppose

\textstyle{\bigcup^{\hskip-3.1298pt{\uparrow}}}A\in W
, then there is a closed set
k\subseteq\textstyle{\bigcup^{\hskip-3.1298pt{\uparrow}}}A
with
k\in W
. The elements of
A
now cover the closed therefore
compact set
k
, so there is a finite
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with

k\subseteq\bigcup A^{\prime}
and since
A
is directed there is
a\in A

with
\bigcup A^{\prime}\subseteq a
. As
k\in W
and
k\subseteq a
it
follows that
a\in W
.

Now we have
p_{W}(\Box\textstyle{\bigcup^{\hskip-3.1298pt{\uparrow}}}A)=\top
iff
\textstyle{\bigcup^{\hskip-3.1298pt{\uparrow}}}A\in W
iff
there is
a\in A
with
a\in W
iff
\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{p_{W}(\Box a)\mid a\in A\}=\top
.

The following lemma is key for proving that
\zeta
and
\theta
are
continuous and each other’s inverses.

Lemma 20
.

For all
p\in\mathsf{pt}\circ\mathsf{M}\circ\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}
we have

X\setminus a\in W_{p}
iff
p(\Diamond a)=\bot
and
a\in W_{p}
iff

p(\Box a)=\top
.

Proof 4.6
.

If
p(\Diamond a)=\bot
then
X\setminus a\in W_{p}
. Conversely, Suppose

X\setminus a\in W_{p}
, then there is some
b
with
p(\Diamond b)=\bot
and

X\setminus b\subseteq X\setminus a
. Therefore
a\subseteq b
and

p(\Diamond a)\leq p(\Diamond b)=\bot
. This proves
X\setminus a\in W_{p}

iff
p(\Diamond a)=\bot
.

If
a\in W_{p}
then there is
X\setminus b\subseteq a
in
W_{p}
, so

p(\Diamond b)=\bot
. Then
a\cup b=X
, so it follows
from
5
of Definition
4.1
that
p(\Box a)=\top
.
If
a\notin W_{p}
and
a^{\prime}\eqslantless a
, then there exists
b
with
b\cap a^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
b\cup a=\mathbb{X}
. Since
X\setminus b\subseteq a
, set
set
X\setminus b
is not in
W_{p}
and hence we must have
p(\Diamond b)=\top
. As
a^{\prime}\cap a=\emptyset
it follows from
2
that

p(\Box a^{\prime})=p(\emptyset)=\bot
. Now we use
3
and the fact
that
a=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{a^{\prime}\mid a^{\prime}\eqslantless a\}
(this is true because
\mathbb{X}
is
assumed to be compact Hausdorff so
\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}
is compact regular) to find

p(\Box a)=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{p(\Box a^{\prime})\mid a^{\prime}\eqslantless a\}=\textstyle{\bigvee^{\uparrow}}\{\bot\mid a^{\prime}\eqslantless a\}=\bot.

It follows that
a\in W_{p}
iff
p(\Box a)=\top
.

We have now aquired sufficient knowledge to prove Theorem
18
.

Proof 4.7

(Proof of Theorem
18
)
.

We claim that the maps
\zeta
and
\theta
define a homeomorphism between

\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
and
\mathsf{pt}(\mathsf{M}(\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}))
. First we prove
that they are each other’s inverses, by showing that for all

p\in\mathsf{pt}(\mathsf{M}(\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}))
and
W\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
we
have
p_{W_{p}}=p
and
W_{p_{W}}=W
.

In order to prove that (the frame homomorphisms)
p
and
p_{W_{p}}
coincide, it
suffices to show that they coincide on the generators of

\mathsf{M}(\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X})
. By Definition
4.2
and Lemma
20
we have

p(\Box a)=\top\quad\text{iff}\quad a\in W_{p}\quad\text{iff}\quad p_{W_{p}}(\Box a)=\top

and

p(\Diamond a)=\bot\quad\text{iff}\quad X\setminus a\notin W_{p}\quad\text{iff}\quad p_{W_{p}}(\Diamond a)=\bot.

In order to show that
W=W_{p_{W}}
it suffices to show that

X\setminus a\in W
iff
X\setminus a\in W_{p_{W}}
for all open sets
a
,
because elements of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
are uniquely determined by the
closed sets they contain. This follows immediately from the definitions and
Lemma
20
, as

X\setminus a\in W\quad\text{iff}\quad p_{W}(\Diamond a)=\bot\quad\text{iff}\quad X\setminus a\in W_{p_{W}}.

Therefore
\zeta=\theta^{-1}
.

We complete the proof by showing that
\zeta
and
\theta
are continuous.
The opens of
\mathsf{pt}(\mathsf{M}(\mathsf{opn}\mathbb{X}))
are generated by

\widetilde{\Box a}=\{p\mid p(\Box a)=\top\}
and

\widetilde{\Diamond a}=\{p\mid p(\Diamond a)=\top\}
, for

a\in\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
. We have

\displaystyle\theta^{-1}(\widetilde{\Box a})=\theta^{-1}(\{p\mid p(\Box a)=\top\})=\{W\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}\mid a\in W\}=\boxbar a

and similarly
\theta^{-1}(\widetilde{\Diamond a})=\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{45.0}{$\boxslash$}}a
.
Continuity of
\theta
follows from the fact that
\boxbar a
and
\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{45.0}{$\boxslash$}}a

are open in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
.
Conversely, the opens of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathbf{kh}}\mathbb{X}
are generated by
\boxbar a
and

\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{45.0}{$\boxslash$}}a
, where
a
ranges over
\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbb{X}
. It is routine to
see that

5.2.
Completeness

We keep working within the assumptions of
5.1
.
In order to prove completeness with respect to
\mathbf{Coalg}(\mathsf{T})
, we want
to show that

\varphi\Vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\psi\quad\text{ implies }\quad\varphi\vdash\psi.

That is, if
\varphi\Vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\psi
then

(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathscr{GML}(\Lambda,\Ax)
.
By Lemma
26
it suffices to show that
[\varphi]\leq[\psi]

in the initial
\mathsf{L}
-algebra
\mathcal{L}=(L,\ell)
whenever

\varphi\Vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\psi
.

If
\varphi\Vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\psi
, then we know that
\llparenthesis\,\varphi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}\leq\llparenthesis\,\psi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}
in every complex algebra
\mathfrak{X}^{+}
for

\mathfrak{X}\in\mathbf{Mod}(\mathsf{T})
. However, there is no guarantee that
\mathcal{L}

should be the complex algebra of some
\mathsf{T}
-model, so we do not automatically
get completeness.

The next proposition shows that in order to prove completeness it
suffices to find a
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra
\mathfrak{X}
and an
\mathsf{L}
-algebra morphism

h:\mathfrak{X}^{+}\to\mathcal{L}
.

Proposition 27
.

If there exists a
\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra
\mathfrak{X}
and an

\mathsf{L}
-algebra morphism
h:\mathfrak{X}^{+}\to\mathcal{L}
, then

\varphi\Vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\psi\quad\text{ implies }\quad\varphi\vdash\psi.

Proof 5.1
.

Write
i
for the unique
\mathsf{L}
-algebra morphism
\mathcal{L}\to\mathfrak{X}^{+}
.
Then initiality of
\mathcal{L}
forces
h\circ i=\id_{\mathcal{L}}
.
Suppose
\varphi\Vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\psi
, then we have

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{X}}
, which in turn
implies
\llparenthesis\,\varphi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}\leq\llparenthesis\,\psi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}

by (

).
It follows from monotonicity of

that

[\varphi]=\llparenthesis\,\varphi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathcal{L}}=h\circ i(\llparenthesis\,\varphi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathcal{L}})=h(\llparenthesis\,\varphi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}})\leq h(\llparenthesis\,\psi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}})=h\circ i(\llparenthesis\,\varphi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathcal{L}})=\llparenthesis\,\varphi\,\rrparenthesis_{\mathcal{L}}=[\psi].

This proves the proposition.

Ideally, one would use a duality of functors to establish that such an
\mathfrak{X}

as in Proposition
27
exists. For example,
this is how one can prove completeness for (classical) normal modal logic:
The Vietoris functor on
\mathbf{Stone}
is the Stone dual of the endofunctor
on
\mathbf{BA}
which determines the logic. However, since we do not
start with a dual equivalence (like Stone duality) but rather with a dual

adjunction
, endofunctors on both categories cannot be dual.

To remedy this, we will make use of the fact that the dual adjunction between

\mathbf{Top}
and
\mathbf{Frm}
restricts to several dual equivalences
(see Fact
5
). Note that this does not yet guarantee that the initial

\mathsf{L}
algebra from Lemma

is the complex algebra of some

\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra! Indeed, its underlying frame need not be in the
restricted dual equivalence. We will see that some of the dual
equivalences are good enough to “imitate” frames that fall outside it.

We now investigate under which conditions we can use Proposition

.
As announced, we shall restrict our attention to the case where

This means that there is no need for having valuations, hence

and

The absence of proposition letters need not pose a big deficiency:
proposition letters can simply be introduced via predicate liftings.

of

(2)

7.
A final model construction

We construct a final model in

\mathbf{Sob}

There are various ways of obtaining final

AMV05

MV04

MV06
]

Our strategy for obtaining a final coalgebra is similar to that by
Moss and Viglizzo

]
in the sense that we use a
language (in our case induced by the geometric similarity type) without a
logical system.
Specifically,

\mathsf{T}
on
\mathbf{Top}
, we use a notion of semantic
equivalence to obtain an equivalence relation on
\GML(\Phi,\Lambda)
.
We prove that
\GML(\Phi,\Lambda)

a frame, denoted by
\mathbf{E}
.
Subsequently, we set

\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{opn}\circ\mathsf{T}\circ\mathsf{pt}:\mathbf{Frm}\to\mathbf{Frm}

and endow
\mathbf{E}
with an
\mathsf{L}
-algebra structure
\delta
.

\mathsf{pt}\mathbf{E}
is the state space for the

\delta
gives rise to a
\mathsf{T}
-algebra
structure on
\mathsf{pt}\mathbf{E}
. Finally, we show that with the
canonical valuation this gives rise to a final model in
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathsf{T})
.

We hasten to say that the functor
\mathsf{L}
used in this construction
need not coincide with the one discussed at the end of Section
6

From a more abstract point of view

,
BK06
,
Kli07
]
, the functor
\mathsf{L}

defined by

gives rise

model construction on

in more detail in the conclusion.

It is well known that coalgebras for endofunctors on
\mathbf{Set}
do not
in general have final coalgebras. In particular, there exists no final

-coalgebra,
as this would imply the existence of a set

such that

and

have the same cardinality.
One way to remedy this is by using topology to restrict the collection of
admissible morphisms.
We show that in presence of a Scott-continuous
characteristic geometric modal signature we

final coalgebra. Intuitively, Scott-continuity gives us a finitary handle
on the final coalgebra we construct.
(For comparison, observe that the set of predicate liftings

\Lambda=\{\lambda^{\Box},\lambda^{\Diamond}\}
for the powerset functor

8.
Bisimulations

This section is devoted to bisimulations and bisimilarity between coalgebraic
geometric models.
Bisimulations are important tools in the study of modal logics.
They provide a structural notion of semantic equivalence:
bisimilar worlds satisfy precisely the same logical formulas.
If the converse is also true then the logical language is powerful enough
to distinguish non-bisimilar states. This is called the

Hennessy-Milner property

[
HM85
]
.

We compare two notions of bisimilarity, modal equivalence
(from Definition

) and behavioural equivalence

\mathbf{C}
is a full subcategory of

\mathbf{Top}

\mathsf{T}

restrict our scope to particular instances of
\mathbf{C}
.

9.
Conclusion

We have started building a framework for coalgebraic
geometric
logic and
we have investigated examples of concrete functors. There are still many
unanswered and interesting questions. We outline possible directions for
further research.

Modal equivalence versus behavioural equivalence:

From Theorem
49
we know that modal equivalence and
behavioural equivalence coincide in
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathsf{T})
if
\mathsf{T}
is
an endofunctor on
\mathbf{KSob}
,
\mathbf{Sob}
or an endofunctor on

\mathbf{Top}
which preserves sobriety. A natural question is whether the
same holds when
\mathsf{T}
is an endofunctor on
\mathbf{KHaus}
.

When does a lifted functor restrict to
\mathbf{KHaus}
?:

We know of two examples, namely the powerset functor with the box and
diamond lifting, and the monotone functor on
\mathbf{Set}
with the box and
diamond lifting, where the lifted functor on
\mathbf{Top}
restricts to

\mathbf{KHaus}
. It would be interesting to investigate whether there are
explicit conditions guaranteeing that the KKP lift of a functor restricts to

\mathbf{KHaus}
. These conditions could be either for the
\mathbf{Set}
-functor
one starts with, or the collection of predicate liftings for this functor,
or both.

Modalities and finite observations:

Geometric logic is generally introduced as the logic of finite
observations, and this explains the choice of connectives (
\land
,

\bigvee
and, in the first-order version,
\exists
).
We would like to understand to which degree
modalities
can safely
be added to the base language, without violating the (semantic) intuition
of finite observability.
Clearly there is a connection with the requirement of

Scott-continuity
(preservation of directed joins), and we would
like to make this connection precise, specifically in the topological setting.

Connection to geometric predicate logic:

The extension of geometric logic with predicates is discussed in
e.g.
[
Joh02
, Chapter D1]
and
[
Vic07
]
. In this setting
toposes replace the rôle of frames. This raises the question of
how modal geometric logic and geometric predicate logics relate.
For example, can we find an analogue of the Van Benthem characterisation
theorem, characterising modal geometric logic as a bisimulation-invariant
fragment of geometric predicate logic?

Order-enriched category theory:

The fact that all examples in this paper use open predicate liftings
that are monotone suggests that we could also work in the setting
of order-enriched category theory.
The richer structure of order-enriched category theory was successfully
used in the study of positive coalgebraic logics
[
KKV12
,
BKV15
]
.

Connection with domain theory:

In
[
Abr91
]
Abramsky investigates distributive lattices, rather than frames.
This raises the question which of his examples have extensions to frames.

An abstract view on coalgebraic logic:

If we abstract away from predicate liftings, a logic for an endofunctor

\mathsf{T}
on
\mathbf{Top}
may be viewed as a functor

\mathsf{L}:\mathbf{Frm}\to\mathbf{Frm}
together with a natural transformation

\rho:\mathsf{L}\circ\mathsf{opn}\to\mathsf{opn}\circ\mathsf{T}

[
Kli07
,
KR12
]
.
A generic choice for

\mathsf{L}
is
\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{opn}\circ\mathsf{T}\circ\mathsf{pt}
,
which is also used in Section
7
to construct a final

\mathsf{T}
-coalgebra.
Moreover, in the setting of Section
7
the algebra

(\mathbf{E},\delta)
is the initial
\mathsf{L}
-algebra,
which gives rise to a completeness result.
A natural question is whether or not an endofunctor
\mathsf{L}:\mathbf{Frm}\to\mathbf{Frm}

can be presented by generators and relations.
Similar questions for endofunctors on the categories of Boolean
algebras and distributive lattices are addressed in
[
KR12
]

and
[
BKV15
]
, respectively.

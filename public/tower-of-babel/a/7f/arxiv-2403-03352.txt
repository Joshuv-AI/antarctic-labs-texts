Characterization of admissible quasisymmetries
J. W. Burby \aff 1 \corresp, N. Kallinikos \aff 2 and R. S. MacKay \aff 2

Abstract
We solve “half” the problem of finding three-dimensional quasisymmetric magnetic fields that do not necessarily satisfy force balance. This involves determining which hidden symmetries are admissible as quasisymmetries, and then showing explicitly how to construct quasisymmetric magnetic fields given an admissible symmetry. The admissibility conditions take the form of a system of overdetermined nonlinear partial differential equations involving second derivatives of the symmetry’s infinitesimal generator.

hidden symmetry

Introduction

Quasisymmetry was introduced in
Boozer 1983
as a condition on magnetic fields satisfying force balance,
(\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B}=\nabla p
, that ensures guiding center trajectories enjoy a constant of motion analogous to canonical angular momentum. Subsequently,
Burby & Qin 2013
, and then
Burby et al. 2020
, showed how to remove force balance from the definition of quasisymmetry, leading to

Definition 1

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded spatial domain. A nowhere-vanishing vector field
\bm{B}:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is said to be
quasisymmetric
when there is a non-zero divergence-free vector field
\bm{u}
on
Q
such that

\displaystyle\nabla\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{B})=0,\quad(\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{u}+\nabla(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B})=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=0.

(1)

The field
\bm{u}
is known as the
infinitesimal generator
of the quasisymmetry.

This notion of quasisymmetry is readily shown to be equivalent to existence of a
1
-parameter family of spatial symmetries for a standard truncation (see
Littlejohn 1981
,
Littlejohn 1982
,
Littlejohn 1983
,
Littlejohn 1984
) of Littlejohn’s guiding center Lagrangian. It should be compared with the related notion of weak quasisymmetry, where the second equation in (
1
) is removed, introduced in
Rodriguez et al. 2020
and placed in a more general context in
Burby et al. 2021
; weak quasisymmetry is the weakest condition on
\bm{B}
that ensures guiding centers enjoy an adiabatic invariant analogous to canonical angular momentum, while quasisymmetry is the weakest condition that guarantees a particular truncation of the guiding center equations is integrable. Removing the force balance constraint is of practical concern because, in light of Grad’s conjecture (see
Grad 1967
), the equation
(\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B}=\nabla p
is likely not a correct description of real stellarator equilibria at small enough scales.

Many prior discussions of quasisymmetry attempt to address existence of smooth quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
. For instance, see
Garren & Boozer 1991
,
Landreman & Sengupta 2019
,
Burby et al. 2020
,
Constantin et al. 2021
,
Landreman & Paul 2022
,
Wechsung et al. 2022
,
Sato 2022
. However, as the above definition plainly demonstrates, the magnetic field
\bm{B}
and the infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
, i.e. the symmetry itself, play almost symmetric roles in the theory. Briefly thinking along these lines leads to the notion dual to quasisymmetry,

Definition 2

A divergence-free vector field
\bm{u}
is
admissible
when there is some quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
with
\bm{u}
as its infinitesimal generator.

This Article initiates the study of admissible
\bm{u}
.

Characterization of admissible infinitesimal generators
\bm{u}
plays at least two important roles in the theory of quasisymmetric magnetic fields. The first role concerns the usual existence problem. The process of finding quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
may be split into two subprocesses: (1) find an admissible
\bm{u}
, then (2) find a quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
with that
\bm{u}
as its infinitesimal generator. Of course this decomposition will only be useful if there is some way to find admissible
\bm{u}
without finding
\bm{u}
and
\bm{B}
simultaneously. Determining whether this can be done requires characterizing admissible
\bm{u}
. The second role concerns an important no-go result due to Garren-Boozer in
Garren & Boozer 1991
. Garren-Boozer argued quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
in toroidal
Q
that satisfy force balance cannot exist, except when the
\bm{u}
-flow is a
1
-parameter family of Euclidean isometries. Their result has not been shown to apply when the force balance constraint is removed. In fact, three-dimensional
weakly
quasisymmetric fields out of force balance appear in
Sato 2022
. It is therefore very interesting to determine whether quasisymmetry must always correspond to rigid Euclidean motions, or if there are genuine three-dimensional examples of quasisymmetric magnetic fields. Characterization of admissible
\bm{u}
addresses this problem directly.

An infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
is
Killing
when its flow is a
1
-parameter family of Euclidean isometries. A simple argument shows that a necessary and sufficient condition for
\bm{u}
to be Killing is vanishing of the
strain-rate tensor

\Delta=(\nabla\bm{u}+(\nabla\bm{u})^{T})/2
. Every Killing
\bm{u}
is admissible, as the following argument shows.

Lemma 1

Suppose
\bm{u}
is a nowhere-vanishing vector field on
Q
with vanishing strain-rate tensor,
\Delta=0
. Then all vector fields
\bm{B}
of the form

\displaystyle\bm{B}=\frac{1}{|\bm{u}|^{2}}\bigg(\bm{u}\times\nabla\psi+C\,\bm{u}\bigg),

with
\bm{u}
-invariant functions
\psi,C
(i.e.
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla C=0
), are quasisymmetric with infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
.

Proof 1.1
.

\Delta=0
implies
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0
and
\nabla|\bm{u}|^{2}+(\nabla\times\bm{u})\times\bm{u}=0
. In particular we have
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla|\bm{u}|^{2}=0
.

Let
\bm{w}=\bm{u}\times\bm{B}
. We have

\displaystyle\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\bm{u}|^{2}}\bm{u}\times\bigg(\bm{u}\times\nabla\psi+C\,\bm{u}\bigg)=\frac{1}{|\bm{u}|^{2}}(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi)\,\bm{u}-\nabla\psi=-\nabla\psi.

This immediately gives us
\nabla\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{B})=0
.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}
denotes the linear operator on vector fields
\bm{v}
given by
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\bm{v}=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{v}-\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}=[\bm{u},\bm{v}]
then

\displaystyle[\bm{u},\bm{B}]

\displaystyle=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla|\bm{u}|^{-2}(\bm{u}\times\nabla\psi+C\,\bm{u})

\displaystyle+|\bm{u}|^{-2}\bigg(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\bm{u}\times\nabla\psi+\bm{u}\times\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\nabla\psi+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla C)\bm{u}+C\,\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\bm{u}\bigg)=0,

where we have used the fact that
\bm{u}
is Killing to infer that
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}
is a cross-product derivation that commutes with
\nabla
, i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\nabla\psi=\nabla(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi)
. Therefore

\displaystyle 0=\nabla\times\bm{w}=(\nabla\cdot\bm{B})\,\bm{u}-(\nabla\cdot\bm{u})\,\bm{B}-[\bm{u},\bm{B}]=(\nabla\cdot\bm{B})\,\bm{u},

which implies
\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=0
.

It remains to show that the vector field
\bm{k}=(\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{u}+\nabla(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B})
vanishes. Well-known vector identities imply

\displaystyle\bm{k}=[\bm{u},\bm{B}]+2\,\Delta\cdot\bm{B}.

But we have just shown that the first term vanishes, and the second term vanishes because
\Delta=0
. So
\bm{B}
is quasisymmetric with infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
, as claimed.

We remark that this Lemma can be generalised to fields for which
\psi
is not a global function. Given a
\bm{u}
-invariant function
C
and a irrotational vector field
\bm{a}
such that
\bm{u}\cdot\bm{a}=0
, define a field
\bm{B}
by replacing
\nabla\psi
with
\bm{a}
. Then
\bm{B}
is still divergence-free and quasisymmetric with infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
. This is the most general such
\bm{B}
. To prove this define
\bm{a}=\bm{B}\times\bm{u}
. From
\nabla\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{B})=0
, this field is irrotational. Also
\bm{u}\cdot\bm{a}=0
, so
[\bm{u},\bm{a}]=0
. Define
C=\bm{B}\cdot\bm{u}
. We have
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla C=0
. Thus,
\bm{B}=(\bm{u}\times\bm{a}+C\bm{u})/|\bm{u}|^{2}
.

Every Killing field
\bm{u}
is admissible because such functions
\psi,C
exist (in abundance). For Euclidean metric,
\bm{u}
is contained in the Euclidean Lie algebra, and therefore has the form
\bm{u}(\bm{x})=\bm{U}+\bm{L}\times\bm{x}
in Cartesian coordinates, for some constant vectors
(\bm{U},\bm{L})
. By translation and rotation we can take
\bm{U},\bm{L}
both in the
z
-direction. If
\bm{U}=0
(the case of axisymmetry) then we can choose
\psi,C
to be any smooth functions of
r^{2},z
in cylindrical coordinates
(r,\phi,z)
. In particular we can achieve
\nabla\psi\neq 0
almost everywhere. If
\bm{L}=0
we can choose any smooth functions of
(x,y)
. In the general case
\bm{U},\bm{L}\neq 0
(helical symmetry), can choose any smooth functions of
r^{2},\zeta
with
\zeta=z-c\phi
where
c\neq 0
is the associated pitch (note this requires periodicity in
z
).

Thus, in the study of admissible
\bm{u}
, it is useful to restrict attention to infinitesimal generators with nowhere-vanishing strain-rate tensors. Such fields
\bm{u}
are
non-Killing
. The existence of even one non-Killing admissible
\bm{u}
would imply existence of genuinely-three-dimensional quasisymmetric magnetic fields. We leave out the case of
\bm{u}
for which
\Delta=0
in some places, nonzero in others.

This Article establishes three foundational results concerning admissible non-Killing
\bm{u}
. First, every such
\bm{u}
must satisfy a system of partial differential equations that do not involve
\bm{B}
.

Theorem 1.2

(necessary conditions for admissibility)
.

If
\bm{u}
is non-Killing and admissible then it must satisfy the PDE constraints

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0

(2)

\displaystyle\text{det}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=0

(3)

\displaystyle[\bm{e}_{3},\bm{u}]

\displaystyle=0,

(4)

where
\bm{e}_{3}
is the unique (up to sign) unit vector field that takes values in the null space of
\Delta
, and
[\bm{e}_{3},\bm{u}]=\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}-\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{e}_{3}
denotes the vector field commutator.

See the discussion in Section
5
for more explicit formulations of the equation
[\bm{e}_{3},\bm{u}]=0
. Second, the necessary PDE constraints (
2
)-(
4
) on
\bm{u}
are generally
locally
sufficient for admissibility as well.

Theorem 1.3

(local admissibility)
.

If
\bm{u}
is non-Killing and satisfies the PDE constraints (
2
)-(
4
) then for each
\bm{x}\in Q
such that

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{3}(\bm{x})\times\bm{u}(\bm{x})\neq 0

(5)

there is an open neighborhood
U\ni\bm{x}
and quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
defined within
U
with
\bm{u}
as its infinitesimal generator.

Finally, the local sufficiency condition extends to a global sufficiency condition in toroidal annuli as follows.

Theorem 1.4

(global admissibility)
.

Assume
Q
is diffeomorphic to the toroidal annulus
S^{1}\times S^{1}\times[0,1]
, where
S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi
denotes the circle. If
\bm{B}:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is quasisymmetric with non-Killing infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
,
\bm{u}\times\bm{B}
is nowhere-vanishing in
Q
, and both
\bm{u}
and
\bm{B}
are tangent to
\partial Q
then

(a)

\bm{u}
satisfies Eqs. (
2
)-(
4
),
\bm{u}\times\bm{e}_{3}
is nowhere-vanishing in
Q
, and
\bm{u},\bm{e}_{3}
are tangent to
\partial Q
,

(b)

there is a smooth function
\psi:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with nowhere-vanishing gradient such that
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi=\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla\psi=0
.

Conversely, if
\bm{u}
is non-Killing and satisfies properties (a) and (b) then
\bm{u}
is admissible and any quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
with
\bm{u}
as its infinitesimal generator has
\bm{u}\times\bm{B}
nowhere-vanishing and
\bm{u},\bm{B}
tangent to
\partial Q
.

2
Derivation of the local admissibility constraints

The following self-contained derivation of local admissibility constraints for
\bm{u}
largely isolates elements of the discussion from
Burby et al. 2020
. That these necessary conditions are also sufficient, as will be explained in Sections
3
and
4
, was not recognized in
Burby et al. 2020
.

Suppose that
\bm{B}
is quasisymmetric with non-Killing infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
. The following argument shows that
\bm{u}
must satisfy the PDE constraints (
2
)-(
4
). In addition, the discussion here leads to Lemma
2.5
, which reduces the problem of finding a quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
with given non-Killing
\bm{u}
to a system of PDEs with a single unknown.

The equation system (
1
) defining quasisymmetry is equivalent to

\displaystyle\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}-\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}=0,\quad\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}+\nabla\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=0.

(6)

The well-known fact that
|\bm{B}|
admits a symmetry for quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
then follows from

\displaystyle\bm{u}\cdot\nabla|\bm{B}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\,(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B})\cdot\bm{B}=2\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}=-2\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}=0.

(7)

An immediate consequence of (
6
) is

\displaystyle 2\bm{B}\cdot\Delta=\bm{B}\cdot(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{T})=\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}=0.

(8)

Since, by definition,
\bm{B}
is nowhere-vanishing, this implies
\text{det}(\Delta)=0
, which recovers the constraint (
3
). Clearly it applies to any admissible
\bm{u}
whatsoever, be it Killing, non-Killing, or anything in-between.

By symmetry of the strain-rate tensor, for each
\bm{x}\in Q
there is an orthonormal frame
(\bm{e}_{1}(\bm{x}),\bm{e}_{2}(\bm{x}),\bm{e}_{3}(\bm{x}))
that diagonalizes
\Delta(\bm{x})
:

\Delta(\bm{x})=\lambda_{1}(\bm{x})\,\bm{e}_{1}(\bm{x})\bm{e}_{1}(\bm{x})+\lambda_{2}(\bm{x})\,\bm{e}_{2}(\bm{x})\bm{e}_{2}(\bm{x})+\lambda_{3}(\bm{x})\,\bm{e}_{3}(\bm{x})\bm{e}_{3}(\bm{x}).

The constraint
\text{det}(\Delta)=0
implies that at least one of the eigenvalues, say
\lambda_{3}(\bm{x})
, is zero. The incompressibility constraint
0=\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=\text{tr}(\Delta)
therefore implies the sum of the remaining two eigenvalues vanishes:
\lambda_{1}(\bm{x})+\lambda_{2}(\bm{x})=0
. Upon setting
\lambda(\bm{x})=\lambda_{1}(\bm{x})
, these observations lead to the following expression for
\Delta(\bm{x})
:

\displaystyle\Delta(\bm{x})=\lambda(\bm{x})\,(\bm{e}_{1}(\bm{x})\bm{e}_{1}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{2}(\bm{x})\bm{e}_{2}(\bm{x})).

(9)

Note that the non-Killing assumption played no role in arriving at this formula.

Now invoking the fact that
\bm{u}
is non-Killing reveals that the eigenvalue
\lambda(\bm{x})
must be non-zero to ensure
\Delta(\bm{x})\neq 0
. It follows that
\Delta(\bm{x})
has three distinct eigenvalues
\{0,\lambda(\bm{x}),-\lambda(\bm{x})\}
, with
\lambda
nowhere-vanishing, and that the corresponding unit eigenvectors are uniquely determined up to sign. In this way, the strain-rate tensor of any non-Killing admissible
\bm{u}
determines a unique, smoothly-defined frame field
(\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2},\bm{e}_{3})
for choice of signs of the three unit vectors. The
8
-fold degeneracy in the frame definition may be reduced to
4
by requiring right-handed orientation. Our convention will be that the unit vector
\bm{e}_{1}
is an eigenvector of
\Delta
with positive eigenvalue, while
\bm{e}_{3}
is a null eigenvector of
\Delta
.

By Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
),

\displaystyle\Delta\cdot\bm{B}=\lambda\,(\bm{e}_{1}\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{2}\bm{e}_{2})\cdot\bm{B}=\lambda\,B_{1}\,\bm{e}_{1}-\lambda\,B_{2}\,\bm{e}_{2}=0,\quad\bm{B}=B_{1}\,\bm{e}_{1}+B_{2}\,\bm{e}_{2}+B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}.

This requires
B_{1}=B_{2}=0
because
\lambda
is nowhere-vanishing. Therefore the magnetic field
\bm{B}
must have the following remarkably simple expression in the frame determined by
\Delta
:

\displaystyle\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}.

(10)

In particular,
B_{3}=\pm|\bm{B}|
. From this, the symmetry condition (
7
) for
|\bm{B}|
, and Eq. (
6
), we arrive at the final PDE constraint (
4
) on
\bm{u}
:

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}-\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{e}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B_{3}}\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}-\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\bm{B}}{B_{3}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B_{3}}\bigg(\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}-\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}\bigg)-\bigg(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3}^{-1}\bigg)\bm{B}

\displaystyle=0.

We have thus succeeded in proving Theorem
1.2
.

The preceding argument successfully obtained the PDE constraints (
2
)-(
4
). It also identified a notable representation for quasisymmetric magnetic fields
\bm{B}
that dramatically simplifies the problem of finding a quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
given an admissible
\bm{u}
. This is summarized in the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix a non-Killing
\bm{u}
with associated null eigenvector,
\bm{e}_{3}
, for the
\bm{u}
-strain-rate tensor. Assume
\bm{u}
satisfies the PDE constraints (
2
)-(
4
). The nowhere-vanishing vector field
\bm{B}
is quasisymmetric with infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
if and only if
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
,
B_{3}
is nowhere-vanishing, and
B_{3}
satisfies

\displaystyle\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=0,\quad\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=-B_{3}\,\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}.

(11)

Proof 2.6
.

We have already shown that if
\bm{B}
is nowhere-vanishing and quasisymmetric with non-Killing infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
then (a)
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
and (b)
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=0
. The condition
\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=-B_{3}\,\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}
follows simply from
0=\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=\nabla\cdot(B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3})
.

It is still necessary to show that
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
is quasisymmetric when
B_{3}
satisfies Eq. (
11
). But this follows from the direct calculations,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla B_{3}+B_{3}\,\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}

(12)

\displaystyle\nabla\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{B})

\displaystyle=\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}-\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}=B_{3}\,[\bm{e}_{3},\bm{u}]-(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3})\bm{e}_{3}

(13)

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{u}+\nabla(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B})

\displaystyle=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}+\nabla\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}=(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3})\,\bm{e}_{3}+2B_{3}\,\Delta\cdot\bm{e}_{3},

(14)

together with Eq. (
4
) and the definition of
\bm{e}_{3}
.

3
Local admissibility

Next we provide a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof 3.7
.

The proof proceeds by constructing a smooth nowhere-vanishing solution
B_{3}
of the equations
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=0
and
\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=-B_{3}\,\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}
in a neighborhood of each point
\bm{x}
where
\bm{u}(\bm{x})\times\bm{e}_{3}(\bm{x})\neq 0
. Given such a
B_{3}
, the vector field
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
is quasisymmetric with infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
by Lemma
2.5
.

Suppose
\bm{x}\in Q
and
\bm{u}(\bm{x})\times\bm{e}_{3}(\bm{x})\neq 0
. Let
\gamma:(-1,1)\rightarrow Q
be a parameterized curve with
\gamma(0)=\bm{x}
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=\bm{u}(\bm{x})\times\bm{e}_{3}(\bm{x})
. Denote the flow maps for
\bm{u}
and
\bm{e}_{3}
as
F^{\bm{u}}_{t_{1}}
and
F^{\bm{e}_{3}}_{t_{2}}
, respectively. There is an open set
V\ni 0\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the smooth mapping
\Phi:V\rightarrow Q
given by

\displaystyle\Phi(t_{1},t_{2},s)=F^{\bm{u}}_{t_{1}}(F^{\bm{e}_{3}}_{t_{2}}(\gamma(s)))

is a diffeomorphism onto its image. By vanishing of the commutator
[\bm{e}_{3},\bm{u}]
, the coordinate frame
(\partial_{t_{1}},\partial_{t_{2}},\partial_{s})
associated with
\Phi
is related to
\bm{u}
and
\bm{e}_{3}
according to

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}}=\bm{u},\quad\partial_{t_{2}}=\bm{e}_{3}.

So the PDE system
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=0
and
\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=-B_{3}\,\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}
in
(t_{1},t_{2},s)
coordinates is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}}S=0,\quad\partial_{t_{2}}S=-(\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}).

where
S=\text{ln}\,B_{3}
. Moreover, since

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}(\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3})=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}\Omega}{\Omega}=0,

where
\Omega
is the standard Riemannian volume form on
Q
, the function
\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}=\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}(t_{2},s)
does not depend on
t_{1}
. It follows that a smooth solution for
S
is given by

\displaystyle S(t_{1},t_{2},s)=-\int_{0}^{t_{2}}(\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3})(\overline{t}_{2},s)\,d\overline{t}_{2},

and that
B_{3}=\exp(S)
is a smooth, nowhere-vanishing solution of the original system
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=0
and
\bm{e}_{3}\cdot\nabla B_{3}=-B_{3}\,\nabla\cdot\bm{e}_{3}
, as claimed.

4
Global admissibility

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.4
. This argument is relatively straightforward when expressed using the language of Cartan’s exterior calculus, but relatively opaque when expressed using conventional vector calculus. So, in this part of the Article only, we assume the reader is familiar with exterior calculus. See
Abraham & Marsden 2008
for a full development of this theory and
MacKay 2020
for a more approachable point of entry tailored to an audience of plasma physicists.

Proof 4.8
.

Suppose
\bm{B}
is quasisymmetric with non-Killing infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
. Also assume that
\bm{u}\times\bm{B}
is nowhere vanishing and that both
\bm{u}
and
\bm{B}
are tangent to
\partial Q
. Theorem
1.2
immediately implies
\bm{u}
satisfies (
2
)-(
4
). And Lemma
2.5
implies that
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
, which implies in turn that
\bm{u}\times\bm{e}_{3}
is nowhere-vanishing and that
\bm{e}_{3}
is tangent to
\partial Q
. This establishes (a). For (b), consider the vector field
\bm{w}=\bm{u}\times\bm{B}
. The curl of
\bm{w}
vanishes. Moreover, the line integral of
\bm{w}
around any closed loop in
\partial Q
vanishes because
\bm{u}
and
\bm{B}
provide a basis for vectors tangent to
\partial Q
. Since
Q
is homotopic to either component of
\partial Q
, it follows that
\bm{w}=\nabla\psi
for some single-valued function
\psi:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Clearly
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi=\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\psi=0
. This establishes (b).

Now for the converse. Let
\Omega
denote the standard Riemannian volume form on
Q
. The strategy is to first find a nowhere-vanishing function
B_{3}:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)=0
and then show that
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
is quasisymmetric.

Nowhere-vanishing
\nabla\psi
and
\bm{u},\bm{e}_{3}
tangent to
\partial Q
implies that each component of the boundary
\partial Q
is a level set for
\psi
. Elementary results from Morse theory therefore imply that each level set of
\psi
is connected and diffeomorphic to the
2
-torus
S^{1}\times S^{1}
.

Let
[\psi_{a},\psi_{b}]=\psi(Q)
. Integrating along
\nabla\psi/|\nabla\psi|^{2}
starting from the boundary component
\psi=\psi_{a}
produces a curve
\gamma:[\psi_{a},\psi_{b}]\rightarrow Q
with
\gamma(\psi_{a})\in\partial Q
,
\gamma(\psi_{b})\in\partial Q
, and
\psi(\gamma(\overline{\psi}))=\overline{\psi}
. This curve intersects each level set of
\psi
exactly once; the intersections are each transversal.

Commutativity of the vector fields
\bm{u}
and
\bm{e}_{3}
, and their tangency to
\partial Q
, implies that their corresponding flow maps
F^{\bm{u}}_{\lambda_{1}}:Q\rightarrow Q
,
F^{\bm{e}_{3}}_{\lambda_{2}}:Q\rightarrow Q
, exist and commute. Composing these flows therefore produces an
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-action
\Phi_{\lambda}:Q\rightarrow Q
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, on
Q
, given by
\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x})=F_{\lambda_{1}}^{\bm{u}}(F^{\bm{e}_{3}}_{\lambda_{2}}(\bm{x}))
. The
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-orbits are precisely the level sets of
\psi
.

For each
\overline{\psi}\in[\psi_{a},\psi_{b}]
let
\Lambda_{\overline{\psi}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denote the stabilizer subgroup of the point
\gamma(\overline{\psi})
. Since
\Phi_{\lambda}
is locally free and transitive on each
\psi
-level the group
\Lambda_{\overline{\psi}}
is a lattice with
2
-generators for each
\overline{\psi}
. These generators
\tau_{1}(\overline{\psi}),\tau_{2}(\overline{\psi})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
may be chosen to vary smoothly with
\overline{\psi}
. Therefore there is a
2
-torus action on
Q
,
\Psi_{\theta}:Q\rightarrow Q
,
\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in S^{1}\times S^{1}
, given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{\theta}(\bm{x})=\Phi\bigg(\bm{x},\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\tau_{1}(\psi(\bm{x}))+\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\tau_{2}(\psi(\bm{x}))\bigg).

The associated infinitesimal generators, corresponding to
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
respectively, are given by

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\tau_{1}^{1}(\psi)\,\bm{u}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\tau_{1}^{2}(\psi)\,\bm{e}_{3},\quad\bm{\xi}_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\tau_{2}^{1}(\psi)\,\bm{u}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\tau_{2}^{2}(\psi)\,\bm{e}_{3}.

Because
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
generate a lattice, the
2\times 2
matrix of components
\tau_{i}^{j}
is invertible. See
Markus & Meyer 1974
for introduction of the period lattice method in the study of integrable Hamiltonian systems,
Duistermaat 1980
for its globalization, and
Laurent-Gengoux et al. 2011
for an interesting subsequent application.

By averaging the Riemannian volume form over the group variables
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
, the torus action produces a group-invariant volume form given by

\displaystyle\langle\Omega\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\Psi_{\theta}^{*}\Omega)\,d\theta_{1}\,d\theta_{2}.

(15)

(Note that
\langle\Omega\rangle
is nowhere-vanishing because
\Omega
is nowhere-vanishing.) Group invariance means, in particular, that the flows of the infinitesimal generators
\bm{\xi}_{1},\bm{\xi}_{2}
leave
\langle\Omega\rangle
invariant. The corresponding infinitesimal statement is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\xi}_{1}}\langle\Omega\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\xi}_{2}}\langle\Omega\rangle=0.

Since
\bm{\xi}_{1},\bm{\xi}_{2}
are related to
\bm{u},\bm{e}_{3}
by an invertible matrix with coefficients that only depend on
\psi
it follows that the
\bm{u}
-flow and
\bm{e}_{3}
-flow also leave the averaged volume form invariant,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\langle\Omega\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}\langle\Omega\rangle=0.

(16)

Let
f(\psi)
be any smooth, positive, single-variable function. Set

\displaystyle B_{3}=\frac{f(\psi)\,\langle\Omega\rangle}{\Omega}.

(17)

By Eq. (
16
), this
B_{3}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\bigg(f(\psi)\,\langle\Omega\rangle\bigg)=f(\psi)\,\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}\langle\Omega\rangle=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}\bigg(f(\psi)\,\langle\Omega\rangle\bigg)=f(\psi)\,\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}\langle\Omega\rangle=0.

In fact,
B_{3}
is the general nowhere-vanishing solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)=0
.

The vector field
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
is clearly nowhere-vanishing, and smoothly globally-defined in
Q
. In addition
\bm{u}\times\bm{B}
is nowhere vanishing and
\bm{u},\bm{B}
are each tangent to
\partial Q
. By the algebraic simplifications in (
12
)-(
14
),
\bm{B}
is quasisymmetric with infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
. In fact, because
B_{3}
is the general solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)=\mathcal{L}_{\bm{e}_{3}}(B_{3}\,\Omega)=0
,
\bm{B}=B_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
is the most general quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
with infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
.

5
Discussion

The proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
involve explicit construction of quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
with a given admissible non-Killing infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
. These constructions solve “half” of the problem (step (2) from the introduction) of constructing genuinely-three-dimensional quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
. The other “half” problem remains open — the existence question for smooth non-Killing admissible
\bm{u}
needs to be addressed. A subsequent publication will apply Cartan’s method of prolongation to involution, along with the Cartan-Kähler theorem (see
Bryant et al. 1991
), to resolve at least the local existence problem.

The admissibility problem can also be studied for weak quasisymmetry, as defined in
Rodriguez et al. 2020
. Details of such analysis will appear in future publications.

The PDE system (
2
)-(
4
) that must be satisfied by any admissible non-Killing
\bm{u}
appears at first glance to involve two unknowns,
\bm{u}
and
\bm{e}_{3}
. But, as discussed in Section
2
, the vector field
\bm{e}_{3}
is uniquely (up to a sign) determined by non-Killing
\bm{u}
as the unit null eigenvector for the strain-rate tensor
\Delta=(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{T})/2
. So the equations can, in principle, be written in terms of
\bm{u}
alone. In fact, it is not difficult to show that a non-Killing
\bm{u}
satisfies (
2
)-(
4
) if and only if

\displaystyle\text{det}(\Delta)=0,\quad\text{tr}(\Delta)=0,\quad\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\pi_{\parallel}-\nabla\bm{u}^{T}\cdot\pi_{\parallel}-\pi_{\parallel}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}=0,

(18)

\displaystyle\Delta=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{T}),\quad\pi_{\parallel}=1-\frac{2\Delta\cdot\Delta}{\Delta:\Delta},

which is manifestly a PDE system for
\bm{u}
alone. Notice that
\pi_{\parallel}
is the orthogonal projector onto the kernel of
\Delta
,
\pi_{\parallel}=\bm{e}_{3}\,\bm{e}_{3}
, and so is insensitive to the sign ambiguity of
\bm{e}_{3}
. Alternatively, in Cartesian coordinates
(x,y,z)
and
\bm{u}
non-Killing implies some principal cofactor for
\Delta
is non-zero, so in a region where say the
zz
-cofactor for
\Delta
is non-zero an explicit formula for
\bm{e}_{3}
is

\displaystyle\bm{v}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{xy}\Delta_{yz}-\Delta_{yy}\Delta_{xz}\\
\Delta_{xz}\Delta_{yx}-\Delta_{xx}\Delta_{yz}\\
\Delta_{xx}\Delta_{yy}-\Delta_{xy}^{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{e}_{3}=\bm{v}/|\bm{v}|,

which allows re-writing the condition
[\bm{u},\bm{e}_{3}]=0
as an ordinary PDE in the components of
\bm{u}
, namely
\bm{v}\times[\bm{u},\bm{v}]=0
.

In light of Lemma
2.5
and Eq. (
17
) giving the general solution for
B_{3}
, every quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
with non-Killing infinitesimal generator
\bm{u}
may be written in the form

\displaystyle\bm{B}=\frac{f(\psi)\,\langle\Omega\rangle}{\Omega}\,\bm{e}_{3},

(19)

where
\langle\Omega\rangle
and
\bm{e}_{3}
are determined by
\bm{u}
alone. Thus, once the symmetry is known,
\bm{B}
is determined by a single free flux function
f(\psi)
. Apparently the space of genuinely-three-dimensional quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
is not much larger than the space of non-Killing admissible
\bm{u}
! The representation (
19
) should be compared with the well-known representation given in Lemma
1
.

An alternative route to the equations found here for a non-Killing
\bm{u}
is via Theorems X.2 and X.3 of
Burby et al. 2020
.
\bm{B}
can be eliminated from these by using
\bm{B}=(\bm{u}\times\nabla\psi+C\bm{u})/|\bm{u}|^{2}
with
C=\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}
, and we had proved
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla C=0
. Then
\psi
can be eliminated by
\nabla\psi=|\bm{B}|\bm{u}\times\bm{b}
.

The global characterization of admissible
\bm{u}
provided by Theorem
1.4
requires linear independence of
\bm{u}
and
\bm{e}_{3}
. Since any quasisymmetric
\bm{B}
is parallel to
\bm{e}_{3}
and it is well-known that
\bm{u}
is parallel to
\bm{B}
along a magnetic axis, Theorem
1.4
cannot be applied naively when the spatial domain
Q
is a solid torus instead of a toroidal annulus. Obtaining a global characterization of three-dimensional admissible
\bm{u}
in the presence of magnetic axes will be the subject of future work.

The arguments presented in this Article assumed the spatial domain
Q
is a region in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and that the metric tensor on
Q
is flat. They generalize readily to the more general situation where
Q
is a Riemannian
3
-manifold with possibly-non-flat metric tensor
g
. The strain-rate tensor then becomes
\Delta=g^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}g/2
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}g
denotes the Lie derivative of
g
along
\bm{u}
and
g^{-1}
indicates raising the first index of
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{u}}g
using the metric
g
. Such non-flat metrics have become interesting to consider, even in terrestrial settings, due to recent results from
Cardona et al. 2023
showing that smooth non-symmetric solutions of magnetohydrostatics with non-trivial rotational transform exist for non-flat metrics that approximate the flat metric with arbitrary precision.

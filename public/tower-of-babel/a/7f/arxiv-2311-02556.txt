Local wellposedness for the quasilinear Schrödinger equations via the generalized energy method
Jie Shao Yi Zhou

Abstract




Abstract.
We study the global Cauchy problem of the quasilinear Schrödinger equations, for which KENIG et al. (Invent Math, 2004; Adv
Math, 2006) obtained short time local wellposedness with large data by pseudo-differential techniques and viscosity methods, while MARZUOLA et al. (Adv Math, 2012;
Kyoto J Math, 2014; Arch Ration Mech Anal, 2021) improved the results by dispersive arguments. In this paper, we introduce the generalized energy method that can close the bounds by combining momentum and energy estimates and derive the results by viscosity methods. The whole arguments basically only involve integration by parts and Sobolev embedding inequalities, just like the classical local existence theorem for semilinear Schrödinger equations. For quadratic interaction problem with small data, we derive low regularity local wellposedness in the same function spaces as in the works of Kenig et al. For cubic interaction problem, we obtain the same low regularity results as in Marzuola et al. (Kyoto J Math, 2014).

2020 Mathematics Subject Classifications.
35Q55; 35A01.

Key words and phrases.
Quasilinear Schrödinger equations; Local wellposedness; Generalized energy method; Momentum type estimates

In this paper, we introduce the generalized energy method to study (
1.1
) for both quadratic and cubic interaction problem and the approach is quite different with those of the previous works. The main idea is that we can use the
|\alpha|
order momentum conservation law equality to obtain the momentum type etsimates and get some
|\alpha|+1
order good terms which include weight and integrate over space and time. Although after the type analysis of fractional integration by parts formula, this process will produce some
|\alpha|+\frac{1}{2}
order terms that are to be bounded and have no time integration, the
|\alpha|+1
order good terms can be utilized to control the nonlinear terms
\mathrm{Im}(\overline{\phi}_{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}F)
in the
|\alpha|
order energy estimates and the terms
\mathrm{Im}(D\overline{\phi}_{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{\alpha}F)
in the
|\alpha|+\frac{1}{2}
order energy estimates, if
|\alpha|
is suitable large. Consequently, one can derive both the
|\alpha|
order and the
|\alpha|+\frac{1}{2}
order energy estimates and this in turn can bound the bad
|\alpha|+\frac{1}{2}
order bad terms in the momentum type estimates. In short, by combining the energy estimates and the momentum type estimates, we can close the bounds, which combining with the artificial viscosity method will lead to the desired results. Here, we call the estimates, which are derived from the momentum conservation law type equality, momentum quantity or the variant of them, momentum type estimates, while energy estimates are the usual ones that are derived from the term containing time derivatives in the equation.

At first sight, the momentum conservation law type equality is less useful than the energy conservation law equality. As an example, for the classic semilinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\beta|u|^{\alpha-1}u=0,\quad t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\alpha>1,\beta=\pm 1,

certain is the sign of the mass quantity of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x
, which is one of the bases to investigate all kinds of properties of NLS, while uncertain is the sign of the momentum quantity
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Im}(\overline{u}\nabla u)\mathrm{d}x
, which seems cannot be directly used. However, the momentum type conservation law equality and the momentum type estimates still play a key role in many situation: The classic Morawetz estimate of NLS is founded on the combination of momentum type estimates and energy estimates; The Morawetz-virial estimate in Dodson et al.
[
7
]
is calculated based on a variant of momentum type identity; The crucial interaction Morawetz estimate in Colliander et al.
[
5
]
is also founded on some variant of momentum type quantity
\dots

[

is also the combination of the momentum and energy conservation law equality and this can provide extra bounds more than the energy estimates, which eventually helps to obtain the desired results for the difficult quasilinear problem such as time-like extremal hypersurface and one dimensional Schrödinger map
flow. We remark that the function in the new Div-Curl type Lemma of

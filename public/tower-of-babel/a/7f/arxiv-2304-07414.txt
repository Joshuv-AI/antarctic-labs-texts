On the Riemann problem for the foam displacement in porous media with linear adsorption Thanks: Submitted to the editors August 9, 2026.
Giulia C. Fritis, Pavel S. Paz, Luis F. Lozano, Grigori Chapiro

Abstract
Motivated by the foam displacement in porous media with linear adsorption, we extended the existing framework for two-phase flow containing an active tracer described by a non-strictly hyperbolic system of conservation laws. We solved the global Riemann problem by presenting possible wave sequences that composed this solution. Although the problems are well-posedness for all Riemann data, there is a parameter region where the solution lacks structural stability. We verified that the model implemented on the most used commercial solver for geoscience, CMG-STARS, describing foam displacement in porous media with adsorption satisfies the hypotheses to apply the developed theory, resulting in structural stability loss for some parameter regions.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S+\partial_{x}f(S,C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[(S+\mathcal{A})C\right]+\partial_{x}\left[f(S,C)C\right]

\lambda_{S}(S,C)=\lambda_{C}(S,C)

2.2
Fundamental waves

The typical solution to the Riemann problem is composed of constant states separated by waves moving with different velocities.
In this section, we review the basic wave concepts required to solve the Riemann problem (
1
)-(
2
) and initial condition (
3
). The states on each wave left and right are referred to as
U_{L}
and
U_{R}
, respectively.

To determine the basic waves that compose the solution of system (
1
)-(
2

S
and
C
. The
C
-characteristic field satisfies
\nabla\lambda_{C}\cdot r_{C}=0
, classified as
linearly degenerate
and results in a contact discontinuity solution
[
19
]
. The
S
-characteristic field provides
\nabla\lambda_{S}\cdot r_{S}=\partial_{SS}f
, which gives us a field with a
local linear degeneracies
at the inflection point of

(where
\partial_{SS}f=0
) for constant
C

[
3
]
. The corresponding solution can be a shock discontinuity, a rarefaction wave, or a combination of both.

2.2.1
Rarefaction waves

Rarefaction waves are continuous solutions of the system (
4

and
U_{R}
satisfying

U(x,t)=V(x/t),

(12)

where

is an integral curve, which is locally defined as a tangent in the direction of the eigenvector

associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda

in (
5

[

. The initial velocity of a rarefaction wave is
\lambda(U_{L})
, and the final velocity is
\lambda(U_{R})
. In the
x
-
t
plane, rarefaction waves are characterized through a set of straight lines starting from the origin with slopes between
\lambda(U_{L})
and
\lambda(U_{R})

[
19
]
.
Notice that (
12
) yields a constant solution along the lines with fixed

For the system (
1
)-(
2
), the eigenvector of the
S
-family is
r_{S}=(1,0)^{T}
, yielding the straight line integral curve with constant
C
. Therefore, the
S
-family rarefaction waves maintain the value of
C
constant.

2.2.2
Shock and contact waves

Shock and contact waves are discontinuous solutions satisfying Rankine-Hugoniot (RH) condition, which provides the discontinuity’s propagation velocity
\sigma
given by

\displaystyle f(U_{R})-f(U_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(S_{R}-S_{L}),

(13)

\displaystyle f(U_{R})C_{R}-f(U_{L})C_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma((S_{R}+\mathcal{A})C_{R}-(S_{L}+\mathcal{A})C_{L}).

(14)

In the

-
t

\sigma
, which refers to the solution’s discontinuity.

After some algebraic manipulations, Eqs. (
13
)-(
14

\displaystyle\sigma=(f(U_{R})-f(U_{L}))/(S_{R}-S_{L}),

(15)

\displaystyle(C_{R}-C_{L})(S_{R}f(U_{L})

\displaystyle-S_{L}f(U_{R})-\mathcal{A}(f(U_{R})-f(U_{L})))=0.

(16)

The last relation is satisfied if
C_{L}=C_{R}
, or

\displaystyle\lambda_{C}(U_{L})=\frac{f(U_{L})}{S_{L}+\mathcal{A}}=\frac{f(U_{R})}{S_{R}+\mathcal{A}}=\lambda_{C}(U_{R}).

(17)

When
C_{L}=C_{R}
, we obtain a shock that maintains the value of

constant.
We refer to the Buckley-Leverett type solutions containing shocks and rarefactions as

-waves. On the other hand, if

Next, we analyze the behavior of the curve
\lambda_{C}(S,C(S))=

Therefore, from the definition of sets

Therefore, the curve

constant as a function of

is decreasing in

2.3
Admissibility criteria

Generally, the solution of the system (
1
)-(
2
) with initial data (
3
) is not unique. To obtain physically meaningful solutions, one uses entropy conditions
[
25
]
. In this section, we review the literature about entropy conditions and present the condition adopted in this work. Later, in Section
5.3
we validate the obtained solutions through direct numerical simulations.

Let us consider
\sigma
the propagation velocity of the discontinuity connecting
U_{L}
and
U_{R}
satisfying RH condition (
13
)-(
14
). We say that a characteristic velocity
\lambda
leaves the discontinuity if
\lambda(U_{L})<\sigma
or
\lambda(U_{R})>\sigma
, and enters the discontinuity if
\lambda(U_{L})>\sigma
or
\lambda(U_{R})<\sigma
.

In genuinely nonlinear fields of strictly hyperbolic systems of conservation laws, Lax’s entropy condition states that a shock wave associated with the characteristic field
P
is admissible if the
P
-characteristics enters the shock and exactly one characteristic of the other fields leaves it
[
18
]
. For a linearly degenerate field, the contact wave is admissible if the
P
-characteristics are tangent to the discontinuity and exactly one characteristic of the other fields leaves it. For non-strictly hyperbolic systems, Keyfitz & Kranzer
[
16
]
generalized Lax entropy condition, where their system possesses a linearly degenerate and a genuinely non-linear families. The entropy condition in Barkve’s work
[
3
]
is equivalent to the generalized Lax entropy condition in the absence of degeneracies. Regarding polymer flooding, the works
[
13
,
14
,
27
]
used a similar model to the one considered here. Despite one family possesses degeneracies, generalized Lax or a similar entropy condition was considered. A physically meaningful vanishing adsorption condition was presented by Petrova et al.,
[
23
]
, which implies the standard entropy conditions from
[
13
,
14
,
16
]
. For simplicity, in this work we use the entropy condition presented by Isaacson & Temple
[
14
]
, defined below.

Definition 2
.

A
C
-wave is admissible if connects states on the same side of the transition curve
\mathcal{T}
. An
S
-wave is admissible if satisfies a usual entropy condition for scalar conservation laws.

Once the fractional flow function
f
possesses an inflection point, we adopt Oleinik’s entropy condition for the
S
-waves. This condition states that a discontinuity is admissible if

\displaystyle\frac{f(U)-f(U_{L})}{S-S_{L}}\geq\sigma\geq\frac{f(U_{R})-f(U)}{S_{R}-S}

(20)

for all
U
between
U_{L}
and
U_{R}
, where
\sigma
is the discontinuity velocity, given by Eq. (
15
).
Geometrically, Oleinik’s entropy condition states that if
S_{L}<S_{R}
(or
S_{L}>S_{R}
), the shock is admissible if the secant line connecting
(S_{L},f(U_{L}))
and
(S_{R},f(U_{R}))
lies above (or below) the arc of the graphic of
f
with endpoints at
(S_{L},f(U_{L}))
and
(S_{R},f(U_{R}))

[
8
]
. We emphasize that applying Olenik’s entropy condition for the
S
characteristics field is possible because all waves in this field keep the value o
C
constant.

If
S
tends to
S_{L}
or
S_{R}
in Eq. (
20
), we obtain the following relation (see
[
8
]
for further details):

\displaystyle\lambda_{S}(U_{R})\leq\sigma\leq\lambda_{S}(U_{L}).

(21)

Therefore, the
S
-wave velocity is at least
\lambda_{S}(U_{R})
and at most
\lambda_{S}(U_{L})
; the same result was obtained in
[
13
]
.

In this section, we present conditions for the existence of a solution to the Riemann problem (

The proof of the solution’s uniqueness in the

In this Subsection, we show that the fractional flow function of water phase

a

6
Conclusions

Motivated by the foam displacement in porous media with linear adsorption, we extended the existing framework for the two-phase flow containing an active tracer described by a non-strictly hyperbolic system of conservation laws.
We solved the global Riemann problem by presenting possible wave sequences that composed this solution. Although the problem is well-posed for all Riemann data, we identified parameter regions where the solution lacks structural stability.

We verified that the CMG-STARS model describing foam displacement in porous media with linear adsorption satisfies the hypotheses to apply the developed theory. Therefore, there exists a parameter region where the CMG-STARS model losses the structural stability, which can result in numerical oscillations. Our results provide a possible explanation to some numerical issues appearing in commercial simulators.

Measuring Hawking Radiation from Black Hole Morsels in Astrophysical Black Hole Mergers
Giacomo Cacciapaglia, Stefan Hohenegger, Francesco Sannino

Abstract
We show that it is possible to observe the Hawking radiation emitted by small black holes assumed to form in catastrophic astrophysical events such as black hole mergers. Gamma ray bursts in the TeV range are unique footprints of these asteroid-mass black hole morsels ejected during the merger. The time delay of the gamma ray bursts from the gravitational wave event is correlated to the mass distribution of the morsels. The integrated mass of the morsels allowed by the unaccounted merger mass leads to a Hawking induced radiation in photons that is above the sensitivity of atmospheric Cherenkov telescopes such as HESS, LHAASO and HAWC.



Black Holes (BHs) are extreme objects predicted by Einstein’s theory of general relativity. Their presence in the Universe has been detected indirectly via their gravitational interactions. We now know that super-massive Black Holes (SMBHs) are present in the centre of most galaxies
Kormendy and Richstone 1995
;
Richstone et al. 1998
, with masses between
10^{5}
and several billion solar masses. They reveal their presence by affecting the rotation speed of nearby stars
Ghez et al. 1998
;
Schodel et al. 2002
or producing active galactic nuclei. Recently, the first direct images of the region near a SMBH have been taken
Akiyama et al. 2019
. Stellar mass BHs are produced at the end of heavy star lifetimes, and they are at the origin of gravitational wave signals observed at LIGO/VIRGO interferometers
Abbott et al. 2016
. A distinctive signal from BHs may be due, theoretically, to the emission of particles via the evaporation mechanism introduced by S.Hawking
Hawking 1974
. However, since their effective temperature is inversely proportional to their mass, astrophysical BHs heavier than a few solar masses are stable because they are colder than the cosmic microwave background. Smaller BHs, however, emit radiation that can be potentially observed. These asteroid-mass BHs could have been produced in the early Universe
Hawking 1971
, and constitute a potential candidate for dark matter if sufficiently long-lived
Chapline 1975
;
Meszaros 1975
;
Frampton et al. 2010
;
Villanueva-Domingo et al. 2021
. The Hawking radiation stemming from these primordial BHs is constrained by measuring the diffuse gamma ray background
Kimura et al. 2017
.

In this letter, we explore the observational consequences of the production of a large number of small BH
morsels
during a catastrophic event such as the merger of two astrophysical BHs. As we shall show, the Hawking radiation stemming from these BH
morsels
gives rise to gamma ray bursts (GRBs) possessing a distinctive fingerprint.

Although BHs lighter than a few solar masses are not expected to be produced via gravitational collapse of over-dense stellar matter, one can still envision that, under certain strong non-linearity in the gravitational field within general relativity or beyond, small BHs may form. For an example, primordial BHs are expected to form via quantum space-time fluctuations enhanced by an inflationary period
Kawasaki et al. 1998
;
Kawasaki and Yanagida 1999
;
Harada et al. 2013
. Gravitational non-linearity does occur in astrophysical events, as shown in
Yang et al. 2015
;
Gralla et al. 2016
;
Westernacher-Schneider 2017
;
Bini et al. 2022
for highly rotating BHs, and in
Giesler et al. 2019
;
Okounkova 2020
;
Khera et al. 2023
for BH merger dynamics.
Specifically, non-linearity appears both in merger simulations
Okounkova 2020
and during the successive ringdown phase
Giesler et al. 2019
;
Khera et al. 2023
. BH mergers are especially interesting because they can be detected via gravitational waves. Additionally, the events are expected to be followed by GRBs only in the presence of pre-existing material from their progenitor star
Loeb 2016
;
Woosley 2016
, from supernova remnants
Perna et al. 2016
;
Kimura et al. 2017
;
Murase et al. 2016
or from active galactic nuclei
Bartos et al. 2017
. The emission of small BH morsels provides a novel mechanism for the emission of high-energy particles, within GRBs characterised by the TeV energy scale.

As we remain agnostic about the details of the BH morsel production, we assume the existence of a distribution of masses that escapes the merger. In other words, when the two BHs merge into a single one, regions of space-time characterised by a strong non-linear gravitational field lead to the formation of BH morsels outside the final horizon. The physics of the Hawking radiation of the BH morsels is encoded in their mass distribution function

\rho(M_{\rm Bm})\equiv\frac{dn_{\rm Bm}}{dM_{\rm Bm}}\,.

(1)

Here,
\rho_{\rm Bm}\,d{M_{\rm Bm}}
gives the number of BH morsels in the infinitesimal mass range between
M_{\rm Bm}
and
M_{\rm Bm}+dM_{\rm Bm}
. For now, we neglect the effect of the gravitational field of the merged BH, and consider the morsels to be in a spherically-symmetric shell configuration. Each morsel emits particles with rates that depend on its mass. The emission rates per each particle species
p
follow the master equation
Hawking 1975

\frac{d^{2}\,N_{p}}{dt\;dE_{p}}=\frac{1}{h}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(E_{p},M_{\rm Bm})}{\exp\frac{E_{p}}{k_{B}T_{\rm Bm}}\pm 1}\,,

(2)

where the denominator is the usual Boltzmann statistic factor for BH morsel temperature
T_{\text{Bm}}
(with
\pm 1
for fermions/bosons, respectively) and the numerator contains the specific grey body factors. Hence, the emission rate for the distribution of BH morsels can be computed as

J_{p}=\int_{M_{\rm min}}^{M_{\rm max}}dM_{\rm Bm}\;\frac{dn_{\rm Bm}}{dM_{\rm Bm}}\;\frac{d^{2}\,N_{p}}{dt\;dE_{p}}(\mu_{t}(M_{\text{Bm}},t))\,,

(3)

where
\mu_{t}
is the mass of the morsels at time
t
as a function of the initial mass
M_{\text{Bm}}
.
The above formula yields the differential primary flux for each particle species
p
. We computed the BH evolution and
J_{p}
numerically by use of the open-source public code BlackHawk
Arbey and Auffinger 2019
;
Arbey and Auffinger 2021
, where radiative emission, decays and hadronisation of the primary particles is taken into account by using Pythia
Sjöstrand et al. 2015
.
The hadronisation tables, therefore, allow us to reliably generate secondary radiation for primary particle energies between
5
GeV up to a few TeV.
For the numerical results in this letter, we use as template a population of BH morsels with equal masses and non rotating, as angular momentum dissipates faster than the mass of the evaporating BH
Page 1976
.

For LIGO/VIRGO/KAGRA mergers
Abbott et al. 2019
;
Abbott et al. 2021
;
Abbott et al. 2024
;
Abbott et al. 2023
, the BH masses are of stellar order, ranging from a few to several tens of solar masses with associated distance span between 240 Mpc to 3 Gpc. The masses of the initial and final BHs can be indirectly measured from the gravitational wave spectrum, with errors of a few solar masses, depending on the event. For instance, for GW170814
Abbott et al. 2017
, which is the first BH merger observed by all three detectors of LIGO and VIRGO, the initial BH masses have been determined to be
30.5^{+5.7}_{-3.0}
and
25.3^{+2.8}_{-4.2}
, in units of the solar mass
M_{\odot}
. The final BH mass is
53.2^{+3.2}_{-2.7}\;M_{\odot}
, while the total energy emitted in gravitational waves amounts to
2.7^{+0.4}_{-0.3}\;M_{\odot}
. Taking into account the errors in the parameters, the energy budget of the merger still allows for a total energy of a few solar masses to be emitted in BH morsels. Conservatively, we will take one solar mass as a reference value for the maximal total mass in morsels.

Via evaporation, the BH morsels emit all kinds of SM particles, however only the neutral stable ones, photons and neutrinos, reach detectors on Earth without being deflected by galactic magnetic fields. To compute template photon and neutrino fluxes, we consider as benchmark a BH merger at a distance
D=300
Mpc from Earth, corresponding to the range of the closest detected signals. The photon flux on Earth can be computed as

F_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{4\pi D^{2}}\int dE_{\gamma}\ \frac{1}{2}J_{\gamma}\,,

(4)

where we included a geometric factor of
1/2

For different morsel masses, the evaporation time will change drastically as it is proportional to

10^{9}

As visible signals from BH evaporation always entail photons above the TeV energy, this signal offers a golden opportunity for high energy atmospheric Cherenkov telescopes like HESS, HAWC
Albert et al. 2020a
and LHAASO
Cao et al. 2023a
;

;
Cao et al. 2023b

10^{-13}\div 10^{-11}~\text{erg}\ \text{sec}^{-1}\ \text{cm}^{-2}

Several SMBH binary systems are known in galaxies, probably following the merger of two progenitor galaxies which hosted a SMBH at their centre. It is believed that the galaxy merger generates a binary system at distances down to 1 pc, while further decay leading to a merger is due to the interactions with the star population
Milosavljevic and Merritt 2003
. Such merger would produce gravitational waves in the sensitivity ranges of Pulsar timing array telescopes (PTA)
Hobbs et al. 2010
or LISA. Intriguingly, it has recently been reported that the activity of the galaxy SDSS J1430+2303, at a distance of
378
Mpc from Earth hints at a possible SMBH merger within three years
Jiang et al. 2022
;
Dou et al. 2022
. The system is currently being monitored by HESS
Aharonian et al. 2023
, while a gravitational wave signal will be in the observation window of PTAs.

Because of the much larger masses, a SMBH merger can potentially emit much more mass in BH morsels, hence providing more intense GRBs than a stellar mass merger. Furthermore, the initial photons emitted by heavier morsels could be visible and provide steady signals that would outlive the photons emitted by other astrophysical sources. Another interesting feature is the fact that the BH morsels are produced and evaporate within the strong gravitational field of the merged SMBH, hence their spectrum may record a signature of where the BH morsels have been produced and the mass of the merged SMBH. For illustration purposes, and for simplicity, we computed the gravitational effects considering a Schwarzschild SMBH of mass
10^{8}\;M_{\odot}

50.000

So far, we have presented simple templates based on BH morsels of the same mass emitted radially as a spherically symmetric shell. An extended mass distribution can modulate and prolong the duration of the TeV photon emission. The geometry of the emission, instead, has a smaller impact on the signal, as BHs emit particles in a spherically symmetric manner. Hence, as long as the morsels are not screened by the merged BH, their Hawking radiation will reach our detectors. Some distortions of the spectrum can be expected if the BH morsels travel with relativistic velocity, in which case their direction of motion with respect to the line of sight becomes important. When BH morsels are emitted in a strong gravitational field, such as for SMBH mergers, their Hawking emission may be affected and a dedicated study is required to quantify such effects, which we leave for future investigations. As a final remark, we recall that BH morsels will most intensely emit at times close to their evaporation and with primary energies exceeding the multi-TeV scale. Besides a correct characterisation of the emission of secondary spectra
Bauer et al. 2021
, the Hawking radiation at such energies can be also impacted by the limited validity of the standard model of particle physics, and some new physics effects may induce measurable distortions. The observation of Hawking radiation from BH morsels, therefore, could enlighten us not only about the production of such morsels, but also about particle physics at energies beyond the reach of current and future collider experiments, carrying imprints from new physics based on supersymmetry, composite dynamics, or extra dimensions, to name a few.

In summary, in this letter we have shown that it is possible to observe the Hawking radiation emitted by BH morsels assumed to form in catastrophic astrophysical events such as black hole mergers. GRBs are unique footprints of these asteroid-mass black hole morsels emitted after the merger, with photon energies exceeding the TeV scale. The time delay between the gravitational wave event and the GRB is correlated to the mass distribution of the morsels. We showed that the total morsel mass allowed by the unaccounted merger mass leads to a Hawking induced radiation in photons that is above the sensitivity of atmospheric Cherenkov telescopes such as HESS, LHAASO and HAWC.

1.
Introduction

An overpartition of
n
is a nonincreasing sequence of natural numbers whose sum is
n
in which the first occurrence of a number
may be overlined and
\overline{p}(n)
denotes the number of overpartitions of
n
. For convenience, define
\overline{p}(0)=1
. For example, there are
8
overpartitions of
3
enumerated by
3,\overline{3},2+1,\overline{2}+1,2+\overline{1},\overline{2}+\overline{1},1+1+1,\overline{1}+1+1
. Systematic study of overpartition began with the work of Corteel and Lovejoy
[
4
]
, although it has been studied under different nomenclature that dates back to MacMahon. Analogous to Hardy-Ramanujan-Rademacher formula for partition function (cf.
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
), Zuckerman
[
13
]
gave a formula for
\overline{p}(n)
that reads

\overline{p}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\underset{2\nmid k}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}}\sqrt{k}\underset{(h,k)=1}{\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}}\dfrac{\omega(h,k)^{2}}{\omega(2h,k)}e^{-\frac{2\pi inh}{k}}\dfrac{d}{dn}\biggl(\dfrac{\sinh\frac{\pi\sqrt{n}}{k}}{\sqrt{n}}\biggr),

(1.1)

where

\omega(h,k)=\text{exp}\Biggl(\pi i\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\dfrac{r}{k}\biggl(\dfrac{hr}{k}-\biggl\lfloor\dfrac{hr}{k}\biggr\rfloor-\dfrac{1}{2}\biggr)\Biggr)

for positive integers
h
and
k
. In somewhat a similar spirit as Lehmer
[
8
]
obtained an error bound for the partition function, Engel
[
6
]
provided an error term for
\overline{p}(n)

\overline{p}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\underset{2\nmid k}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}}\sqrt{k}\underset{(h,k)=1}{\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}}\dfrac{\omega(h,k)^{2}}{\omega(2h,k)}e^{-\frac{2\pi inh}{k}}\dfrac{d}{dn}\biggl(\dfrac{\sinh\frac{\pi\sqrt{n}}{k}}{\sqrt{n}}\biggr)+R_{2}(n,N),

(1.2)

where

\bigl|R_{2}(n,N)\bigr|<\dfrac{N^{5/2}}{\pi n^{3/2}}\sinh\biggl(\dfrac{\pi\sqrt{n}}{N}\biggr).

(1.3)

A positive sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is called
\log
-convex if for
n\geq 1
,

a^{2}_{n}-a_{n-1}a_{n+1}\leq 0,

and it is called
\log
-concave if for
n\geq 1
,

a^{2}_{n}-a_{n-1}a_{n+1}\geq 0.

Engel
[
6
]
proved that
\{\overline{p}(n)\}_{n\geq 2}
is
\log
-concave by using the asymptotic formula (
1.2
) with
N=2
followed by (
1.3
). Prior to Engel’s work on overpartitions,
\log
-concavity of partition function
p(n)
and its associated inequalities has been studied in a broad spectrum, for example see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
, and
[
5
]
. Following the same line of studies, Liu and Zhang
[
9
]
proved a list of inequalitites for overpartition function.

Sun
[
11
]
initiated the study on
\log
-convexity problems associated with
p(n)
, later settled by Chen and Zheng
[
3
, Theorem 1.1-1.2]
. In a more general setting, Chen and Zheng studied
\log
-convexity of
\{\sqrt[n]{p(n)/n^{\alpha}}\}_{n\geq n(\alpha)}
(cf.
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
). Moreover, they discovered the asymptotic growth of the sequence
\Delta^{2}\log\sqrt[n]{p(n)}
(cf.
[
3
, Theorem 1.4]
).

The main objective of this paper is to prove all the theorems
[
3
, Theorem 1.1-1.4]
but in context of overpartitions. Our goal is to obtain a much more general inequality, given in Theorem
1.1
, which at once implies
[
3
, Theorem 1.1-1.4]
for
\overline{p}(n)
, presented in Corollary
1.2
-
1.5
. More explicitly, in Theorem
1.1
, we get a somewhat symmetric upper and lower bound of
\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)/n^{\alpha}}
, as shown in (
1.4
). We note that the lower bound presented in (
1.4
) depicts a finer inequality than merely stating
\Delta^{2}\log\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)/n^{\alpha}}>0
which implies
\log
-convexity. In another direction, we note that (
1.4
) readily suggests that
\dfrac{3\pi}{4}
is the best possible constant so as to understand the asymptotic growth of
\Delta^{2}\log\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)/n^{\alpha}}
, given in Corollary
1.5
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, define

r_{\alpha}(n):=\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)/n^{\alpha}}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and

N(\alpha):=\begin{cases}\text{$\max\Biggl\{\Bigl[\dfrac{3490}{\alpha}\Bigr]+2,\Bigl\lceil\Bigl(\dfrac{4(11+5\alpha)}{3\pi}\Bigr)^{4}\Bigr\rceil,5505\Biggr\}$}&\quad\text{if $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$},\\
\text{$4522$}&\quad\text{if $\alpha=0$.}\\
\end{cases}

Then for
n\geq N(\alpha)
,

\log\biggl(1+\frac{3\pi}{4n^{5/2}}-\frac{11+5\alpha}{n^{11/4}}\biggr)<\Delta^{2}\log r_{\alpha}(n-1)<\log\biggl(1+\frac{3\pi}{4n^{5/2}}\biggr).

(1.4)

Corollary 1.2
.

The sequence
\bigl\{\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)/n^{\alpha}}\bigr\}_{n\geq N(\alpha)}
is
\log
-convex.

From (
1.4
), it is immediate that

\dfrac{r_{\alpha}(n+1)r_{\alpha}(n-1)}{r^{2}_{\alpha}(n)}>1+\frac{3\pi}{4n^{5/2}}-\frac{11+5\alpha}{n^{11/4}}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n\geq N(\alpha).

We finish the proof by observing that

1+\frac{3\pi}{4n^{5/2}}-\frac{11+5\alpha}{n^{11/4}}>1\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n\geq N(\alpha).

∎

Corollary 1.3
.

The sequences
\bigl\{\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)/n}\bigr\}_{n\geq 19}
and
\bigl\{\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)}\bigr\}_{n\geq 4}
are
\log
-convex.

In order to prove
\bigl\{\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)/n}\bigr\}_{n\geq 19}
and
\bigl\{\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)}\bigr\}_{n\geq 4}
are
\log
-convex, after corollary
1.2
, it remains to check numerically for
19\leq n\leq 5504
and
4\leq n\leq 4521
, which is done in ‘Mathematica’ interface. ∎

Corollary 1.4
.

For all
n\geq 2
, we have

\dfrac{\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)}}{\sqrt[n+1]{\overline{p}(n+1)}}\biggl(1+\frac{3\pi}{4n^{5/2}}\biggr)>\dfrac{\sqrt[n-1]{\overline{p}(n-1)}}{\sqrt[n]{\overline{p}(n)}}.

(1.5)

It is an immediate implication of (
1.4
) as it is only left over to verify (
1.5
) for
2\leq n\leq 4522
, which we did numerically in ‘Mathematica’. ∎

Corollary 1.5
.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{5/2}\Delta^{2}\log r_{\alpha}(n)=\frac{3\pi}{4}.

(1.6)

Multiplying both side of (
1.4
) by
n^{5/2}
and taking limit as
n
tends to infinity, we get (
1.6
).
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

[
3
, Lemma 2.1]

Suppose
f(x)
has a continuous second derivative for
x\in[n-1,n+1]
. Then there exists
c\in(n-1,n+1)
such that

\Delta^{2}f(n-1)=f(n+1)+f(n-1)-2f(n)=f^{\prime\prime}(c).

(2.1)

If
f(x)
has an increasing second derivative, then

f^{\prime\prime}(n-1)<\Delta^{2}f(n-1)<f^{\prime\prime}(n+1).

(2.2)

Conversely, if
f(x)
has a decreasing second derivative, then

f^{\prime\prime}(n+1)<\Delta^{2}f(n-1)<f^{\prime\prime}(n-1).

(2.3)

We start by laying out a brief outline of Engel’s primary set up
[
6
]
for proving
\log
-concavity of
\{\overline{p}(n)\}_{n\geq 2}
. Setting
N=3
in (
1.2
), we express
\overline{p}(n)
as

\overline{p}(n)=\overline{T}(n)+\overline{R}(n),

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\overline{T}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\overline{c}}{\overline{\mu}(n)^{2}}\biggl(1-\dfrac{1}{\overline{\mu}(n)}\biggr)e^{\overline{\mu}(n)},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\overline{R}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{8n}\biggl(1+\dfrac{1}{\overline{\mu}(n)}\biggr)e^{-\overline{\mu}(n)}+R_{2}(n,3)

(2.6)

with
\overline{c}=\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{8}
and
\overline{\mu}(n)=\pi\sqrt{n}
. In order to estimate the upper and lower bound of
\Delta^{2}\log r_{\alpha}(n-1)
, it is necessary for us to express
\Delta^{2}\log r_{\alpha}(n-1)
in the following form

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\log r_{\alpha}(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\log\overline{p}(n-1)-\alpha\ \Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\log(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\log\overline{T}(n-1)+\Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\log\biggl(1+\dfrac{\overline{R}(n-1)}{\overline{T}(n-1)}\biggr)-\alpha\ \Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\log(n-1).

Define

\overline{E}(n-1)=\log\biggl(1+\dfrac{\overline{R}(n-1)}{\overline{T}(n-1)}\biggr)

(2.8)

and rewrite (

\Delta^{2}\log r_{\alpha}(n-1)=\Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\log\overline{T}(n-1)+\Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\overline{E}(n-1)-\alpha\ \Delta^{2}\dfrac{1}{n-1}\log(n-1)

(2.9)

Therefore, in order to estimate

Lemma 2.2

Let

\displaystyle-\dfrac{3\log\ \overline{\mu}(n)}{n},

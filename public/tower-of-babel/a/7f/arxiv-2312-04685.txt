Properties and special filters of pseudocomplemented posets
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
Investigating the structure of pseudocomplemented lattices started ninety years ago with papers by V. Glivenko, G. Birkhoff and O. Frink and this structure was essentially developed by G. Grätzer. In recent years, some special filters in pseudocomplemented and Stone lattices have been studied by M. Sambasiva Rao. However, in some applications, in particular in non-classical logics with unsharp logical connectives, pseudocomplemented posets instead of lattices are used. This motivated us to develop an algebraic theory of pseudocomplemented posets, i.e. we derive identities and inequalities holding in such posets and we use them in order to characterize the so-called Stone posets. Then we adopt several concepts of special filters and we investigate their properties in pseudocomplemented posets. Moreover, we show how properties of these filters influence algebraic properties of the underlying pseudocomplemented posets.

The aim of this paper is two-fold.

At first, we collect identities known to be valid in pseudocomplemented and Stone lattices and establish their counterparts in pseudocomplemented and Stone posets. It is evident that not all of these identities can be transferred to posets, but a lot can be settled and proved. Section 3 contains an algebraic theory of pseudocomplemented and Stone posets which, to the authors’ knowledge, was not published before.

The second part of the paper (Section 4) is devoted to several special filters in pseudocomplemented and Stone posets. We investigate relationships between these filters and connections between such filters and the structure of the underlying posets.

In the following we identify singletons with their unique element, i.e. we will often write

(ii)

,

(iii)

(iv)

a\leq b^{*}

L(a,b)=0

An element

. Let

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)

∎

We define

\displaystyle A\leq_{1}B

\displaystyle:\Leftrightarrow\text{ for every }a\in A\text{ there exists some }b\in B\text{ with }a\leq b,

\displaystyle A\leq_{2}B

\displaystyle:\Leftrightarrow\text{ for every }b\in B\text{ there exists some }a\in A\text{ with }a\leq b,

\displaystyle A=_{1}B

\displaystyle:\Leftrightarrow A\leq_{1}B\text{ and }B\leq_{1}A,

\displaystyle A=_{2}B

\displaystyle:\Leftrightarrow A\leq_{2}B\text{ and }B\leq_{2}A,

\displaystyle A^{*}

\displaystyle\text{ implies }B^{*}\leq A^{*},

\displaystyle A\leq_{1}B

\displaystyle\text{ implies }B^{*}\leq_{2}A^{*},

In the sequel
\Max A

denotes the set of all maximal and minimal elements of

(DCC, for short) if it has no infinite descending chains. In particular, every finite poset satisfies both the ACC and DCC. Note that if
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the ACC then every non-empty subset

of

has at least one maximal element, i.e. the set
\Max A
is non-empty, and if
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the DCC then every non-empty subset
A
of

has at least one minimal element, i.e. the set
\Min A
is non-empty. If
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the ACC and

then
A\leq_{1}\Max B
. If
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the DCC and
A\subseteq B
then
\Min B\leq_{2}A
. It is evident that the sets
\Max A
and
\Min A
are antichains in
\mathbf{P}
.

Remark 2.4
.

As mentioned above, the sets

are antichains for all

.

The poset depicted in Figure 3

is a poset and

Remark 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset,
A
an antichain of
\mathbf{P}
and
B\subseteq P
and assume
A=_{1}B
. Then
A\subseteq B
. This can be seen as follows: Assume
a\in A
. Since
A\leq_{1}B
there exists some
b\in B
with
a\leq b
. Since
B\leq_{1}A
there exists some
c\in A
with

. Because of transitivity we have

. Since

is an antichain of
\mathbf{P}
we conclude

and hence

. This shows

. In a similar way one can prove that

. If both

. If

. This can be seen as follows: Assume

3
Properties of pseudocomplements in bounded posets

In this section we present identities and inequalities holding in pseudocomplemented posets and compare these identities and inequalities with the corresponding identities holding in pseudocomplemented lattices.

We start with a list of several important properties of bounded pseudocomplemented posets.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset. If
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the
ACC
then the following holds:

(i)

\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\approx_{1}\big(\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{**}
,

(ii)

\Max L\Big(x,\big(\Max L(x^{*},y)\big)^{*}\Big)\approx x
,

(iii)

\Max L(x,y)\leq_{1}\Max L(x^{**},y)\leq_{1}\Max L(x^{**},y^{**})
for all
x,y\in P
,

(iv)

\Max L(x,y)\leq_{1}\Max L(x,y^{**})\leq_{1}\Max L(x^{**},y^{**})
for all
x,y\in P
,

(v)

\big(\Max L(x,y)\big)^{**}\leq_{1}\Max L(x^{**},y^{**})
for all
x,y\in P
.

If
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the
DCC
then the following holds:

(vi)

\Min U(x,y)\leq_{2}\Min U(x^{**},y)\leq_{2}\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})
for all
x,y\in P
,

(vii)

\Min U(x,y)\leq_{2}\Min U(x,y^{**})\leq_{2}\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})
for all
x,y\in P
,

(viii)

\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})\leq_{2}\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{**}
for all
x,y\in P
.

If
\mathbf{P}
satisfies both the
ACC
and the
DCC
then the following holds:

(ix)

\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{*}\approx_{1}\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})
,

(x)

\Min U(x^{*},y^{*})\leq_{2}\big(\Max L(x,y)\big)^{*}
for all
x,y\in P
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in P
.

(i)

\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\leq a^{*}
implies

\big(\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\big)^{**}\leq a^{***}=a^{*}.

Analogously,
\big(\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\big)^{**}\leq b^{*}
. Hence
\big(\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\big)^{**}\subseteq L(a^{*},b^{*})
whence
\big(\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\big)^{**}\leq_{1}\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})
. If
c\in\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})
then
c\leq c^{**}\in\big(\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\big)^{**}
. This shows
\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\leq_{1}\big(\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\big)^{**}
.

(ii)

We have
\Max L(a^{*},b)\leq a^{*}
and hence
a\leq a^{**}\leq\big(\Max L(a^{*},b)\big)^{*}
whence

L\Big(a,\big(\Max L(a^{*},b)\big)^{*}\Big)=L(a).

(iii)

This follows from
L(a,b)\subseteq L(a^{**},b)\subseteq L(a^{**},b^{**})
.

(iv)

This follows from
L(a,b)\subseteq L(a,b^{**})\subseteq L(a^{**},b^{**})
.

(v)

From (ii) and (i) we obtain
\big(\Max L(a,b)\big)^{**}\leq_{1}\big(\Max L(a^{**},b^{**})\big)^{**}=_{1}\Max L(a^{**},b^{**})
.

(vi)

This follows from
U(a^{**},b^{**})\subseteq U(a^{**},b)\subseteq U(a,b)
.

(vii)

This follows from
U(a^{**},b^{**})\subseteq U(a,b^{**})\subseteq U(a,b)
.

(viii)

We have
\Min U(a^{**},b^{**})\leq_{2}\big(\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\big)^{*}=_{2}\big(\Min U(a,b)\big)^{**}
according to (vii) and (vi).

(ix)

Since
a,b\leq\Min U(a,b)
we have
\big(\Min U(a,b)\big)^{*}\leq a^{*},b^{*}
and hence
\big(\Min U(a,b)\big)^{*}\subseteq L(a^{*},b^{*})
whence
\big(\Min U(a,b)\big)^{*}\leq_{1}\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})
. Conversely, let
c\in\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})
. Then
c\leq a^{*},b^{*}
and hence
a,b\leq c^{*}
, i.e.
c^{*}\in U(a,b)
. Therefore there exists some
d\in\Min U(a,b)
with
d\leq c^{*}
. Now
c\leq d^{*}\in\big(\Min U(a,b)\big)^{*}
showing
\Max L(a^{*},b^{*})\leq_{1}\big(\Min U(a,b)\big)^{*}
.

(x)

Let
c\in\big(\Max L(a,b)\big)^{*}
. Then there exists some
d\in\Max L(a,b)
with
d^{*}=c
. Because of
d\leq a,b
we have
a^{*},b^{*}\leq d^{*}
, i.e.
d^{*}\in U(a^{*},b^{*})
. Hence there exists some
e\in\Min U(a^{*},b^{*})
with
e\leq d^{*}
. Therefore
e\leq c
.

∎

The following result is in fact a corollary of Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset satisfying the
ACC
and assume

(1)

\big(\Max L(x,y)\big)^{*}\leq_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{**},y^{**})\big)^{*}
for all
x,y\in P
.

Then
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identities

(i)

\big(\Max L(x,y)\big)^{*}\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{**},y^{**})\big)^{*}
,

(ii)

\big(\Max L(x,y)\big)^{**}\approx_{1}\Max L(x^{**},y^{**})
,

(iii)

\big(\Max L(x,y)\big)^{*}\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{**},y)\big)^{*}\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x,y^{**})\big)^{*}
.

Proof.

(i)

We have

\big(\Max L(a,b)\big)^{*}\leq_{2}\big(\Max L(a^{**},b^{**})\big)^{*}\leq_{2}\big(\Max L(a,b)\big)^{*}

according to the assumption and to (iii) of Theorem
3.1
.

(ii)

We have

\big(\Max L(a,b)\big)^{**}=_{1}\big(\Max L(a^{**},b^{**})\big)^{**}=_{1}\Max L(a^{**},b^{**})

according to (i) and to (i) of Theorem
3.1
.

(iii)

We have

L(a,b)\subseteq L(a^{**},b)\subseteq L(a^{**},b^{**})

and hence

\Max L(a,b)\leq_{1}\Max L(a^{**},b)\leq_{1}\Max L(a^{**},b^{**})

whence

\big(\Max L(a,b)\big)^{*}=_{2}\big(\Max L(a^{**},b^{**})\big)^{*}\leq_{2}\big(\Max L(a^{**},b)\big)^{*}\leq_{2}\big(\Max L(a,b)\big)^{*}

\displaystyle=(\Max\{0,a,b,c,d,e,f,1\})^{*}=\big(\Max L(1,1)\big)^{*}=\big(\Max L(d^{**},e^{**})\big)^{*}.

In the sequel we will often deal with Stone posets. We define

Definition 3.3
.

A Stone poset is a pseudocomplemented poset
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
satisfying both the
ACC
and the
DCC
as well as

\big(\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{*}\leq_{2}\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})\text{ for all }x,y\in P.

Stone posets can be characterized by identities, see the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset satisfying both the
ACC
and the
DCC
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbf{P}
is a Stone poset,

(ii)

\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identity
\big(\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{*}\approx_{2}\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})
,

(iii)

\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identity
\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{**}\approx_{2}\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})
.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii):

This follows from (x) of Theorem
3.1
.

(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii):

According to (ix) of Theorem
3.1
we have
\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{**}\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{*}
.
∎

Assertion (iii) of Theorem
3.4
implies that
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identities

\displaystyle\big(\Min U(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{**}

\displaystyle\approx_{2}\Min U(x^{***},y^{***})\approx\Min U(x^{*},y^{*}),

\displaystyle\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{*}

\displaystyle\approx\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{***}\approx_{1}\big(\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})\big)^{*}.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset satisfying the
ACC
and the
DCC
as well as the identity

(2)

\big(\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})\big)^{**}\approx_{2}\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})
.

Then
\mathbf{P}
is a Stone poset.

Proof.

By (ix) of Theorem
3.1
,
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identity

\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{*}\approx_{1}\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})

and hence also the identity

\big(\Min U(x,y)\big)^{**}\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{*}.

Replacing
x
by
x^{**}
and
y
by
y^{**}
we obtain the identities

\big(\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})\big)^{**}\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{***},y^{***})\big)^{*}=_{1}\big(\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{*}.

Applying identity (2) we get the identity

\Min U(x^{**},y^{**})\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{*},y^{*})\big)^{*}

which by (ii) of Theorem
3.4
is equivalent to
\mathbf{P}
being a Stone poset.
∎

Recall the following well-known concept. A
Stone lattice
is a distributive pseudocomplemented lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
satisfying the identity
x^{*}\vee x^{**}\approx 1
.

In order to justify the name Stone posets for the posets defined in Definition
3.3
we prove that such posets satisfy the identity
U(x^{*},x^{**})\approx 1
which is equivalent to
x^{*}\vee x^{**}\approx 1
. Hence such posets satisfy the same identity as Stone lattices.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a Stone poset. Then
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identity

(3)

U(x^{*},x^{**})\approx 1
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.4
we have

\Min U(x^{*},x^{**})\approx\Min U(x^{***},x^{**})\approx_{2}\big(\Max L(x^{**},x^{*}\big)^{*}\approx(\Max 0)^{*}\approx 0^{*}\approx 1.

Since both
\Min U(x^{*},x^{**})
and
\{1\}
are antichains we obtain
\Min U(x^{*},x^{**})\approx 1
, i.e.
U(x^{*},x^{**})\approx 1
.
∎

Identity (3) is called the
Stone identity
.

(x^{*}\wedge y^{*})^{*}\approx x^{**}\vee y^{**}

(
see Theorem

Remark 3.9

The concept of a filter of a poset was already introduced in
[
3
]
. Recall that a non-empty subset of a poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
is called a
filter
of
\mathbf{P}
if

•

a\in F
,
b\in P
and
a\leq b
together imply
b\in F
,

•

a,b\in F
implies
L(a,b)\cap F\neq\emptyset
.

The
filter

F
is called
proper
if
F\neq P
. For every
x\in P
the set
F_{x}:=\{y\in P\mid x\leq y\}
is a filter of
\mathbf{P}
, the so-called
principal filter
of
\mathbf{P}
generated by
x
. In case
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the DCC every filter of
\mathbf{P}
is principal. This can be seen as follows. Assume
\mathbf{P}
to satisfy the DCC and let
F
be a filter of
\mathbf{P}
and
a\in F
. Because of the DCC there exists some minimal element
b
of
F
with
b\leq a
. Assume that
b
is not the smallest element of
F
. Then there exists some
c\in F
with
b\not\leq c
. Since
F
is a filter of
\mathbf{P}
there exists some
d\in L(b,c)\cap F
. Now
d=b
would imply
b\leq c
, a contradiction. This shows
d<b
contradicting the minimality of
b
. In particular, every filter of a finite pseudocomplemented poset as well as of a Stone poset is principal.

Example 4.1
.

All filters of the poset from Fig. 1 are given by:

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle=\{0,a,b,c,d,1\},

\displaystyle F_{a}

\displaystyle=\{a,c,d,1\},

\displaystyle F_{b}

\displaystyle=\{b,c,d,1\},

\displaystyle F_{c}

\displaystyle=\{c,1\},

\displaystyle F_{d}

\displaystyle=\{d,1\},

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\{1\}.

Hence the poset of all filters of a poset
\mathbf{P}
satisfying the DCC ordered by set inclusion is dually isomorphic to
\mathbf{P}
and hence it is lattice-ordered if and only if
\mathbf{P}
has this property.

Let us note that e.g. the subset

of

from Fig. 1 is not a filter of
\mathbf{P}
since

, but

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset,

a proper filter of
\mathbf{P}
and

. Then the following holds:

(i)

\{a,a^{*}\}\not\subseteq F
,

(ii)

if

and

then
\overline{b}\not\subseteq F
.

Proof.

(i)

The inclusion

would imply

, i.e.

is proper.

(ii)

Assume

. Since

∎

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid U(x^{**},y^{**})=1\text{ for all }y\in A\},

\displaystyle A^{D}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid U(x,y)\subseteq D\text{ for all }y\in A\}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset and

. Then
\overline{\emptyset}=\emptyset^{D}=P
and
\overline{P}=P^{D}=D
.

Proof.

Let

. Then the following are equivalent:

;

for all

;
U(a^{**},0^{**})=1

;

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset,

and

. Then the following holds:

(i)

\overline{A}\subseteq A^{D}
,

(ii)

if

and hence

Observe that

.

(i)

Let

∎

Remark 4.5
.

The pair
(A\mapsto\overline{A},A\mapsto\overline{A})
of mappings is the Galois-correspondence between
(2^{P},\subseteq)
and
(2^{P},\subseteq)
induced by the binary relation
\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid U(x^{**},y^{**})=1\}
on
P
. Hence

is a closure operator on
(2^{P},\subseteq)
and therefore the set
\{\overline{A}\mid A\subseteq P\}
of all subsets of
P
closed under this closure operator forms a closure system of
P
, i.e. it is closed under arbitrary intersections. This means that the closed subsets of
P
form a complete lattice with respect to set inclusion with smallest element
\overline{P}=D
and greatest element
\overline{\emptyset}=P
and we have the following properties for
A,B\subseteq P
:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\subseteq B\text{ implies }\overline{B}\subseteq\overline{A},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{\overline{A}},

\displaystyle\overline{\overline{\overline{A}}}

\displaystyle=\overline{A},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{B}\text{ if and only if }B\subseteq\overline{A}.

Analogously, the pair
(A\mapsto A^{D},A\mapsto A^{D})
is the Galois-correspondence between
(2^{P},\subseteq)
and
(2^{P},\subseteq)
induced by the binary relation
\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid U(x,y)\subseteq D\}
on
P
. Hence the set
\{A^{D}\mid A\subseteq P\}
of all subsets of
P
closed under the closure operator
A\mapsto A^{DD}
forms a complete lattice with respect to set inclusion and we have analogous results as before.

Note that
\overline{A}
as well as

are order filters in

and that for

we have
\overline{F_{a}}=\overline{a}
as well as
(F_{a})^{D}=a^{D}
.

\mathbf{P}
we have
a^{**}\in F
. According to (iii) this implies
\overline{a}\not\subseteq F
.
∎

Finally, we show some properties of median
D
-filters.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset and
F
a median
D
-filter of
\mathbf{P}
. Then the following holds:

(i)

If
F
is prime then for all
x\in P
,
x\in F
if and only if
\overline{\overline{x}}\subseteq F
,

(ii)

F
is coherent.

Proof.

Let
a\in F
. Since
F
is median there exists some
c\in\overline{a}\setminus F
.

(i)

Assume
F
to be prime. Let
b\in\overline{\overline{a}}
. Then
c\in\overline{a}\subseteq\overline{b}
. Since
c\in P\setminus F
we have
\overline{c}\subseteq F
according to Theorem
4.12
. Hence
b\in\overline{\overline{b}}\subseteq\overline{c}\subseteq F
. This shows
\overline{\overline{a}}\subseteq F
. That for
x\in P
,
\overline{\overline{x}}\subseteq F
implies
x\in F
follows from
x\in\overline{\overline{x}}
.

(ii)

Let
b\in P
and assume
\overline{a}=\overline{b}
. Then
c\in\overline{a}=\overline{b}\subseteq b^{D}
according to Lemma
4.4
. This implies
U(b,c)\subseteq D\subseteq F
. Since
F
is prime and
c\not\in F
we conclude
b\in F
.

∎

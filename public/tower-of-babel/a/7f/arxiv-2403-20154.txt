12th Workshop on the CKM Unitarity Triangle Santiago de Compostela, 18-22 September 2023 Rare Strange and Charm Decays at LHCb
Paras Naik paras.naik@cern.ch

Abstract
Thanks to the large cross-section for the production of strange and charm mesons at the Large Hadron Collider, LHCb has recently performed several new explorations of rare transitions that are highly suppressed or forbidden by the Standard Model: mostly flavour changing neutral current decays, but also lepton flavour violating decays. Physics beyond the Standard Model could contribute at tree and loop levels.



1
Rare strange decays at LHCb

1.1
K^{0}_{S}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

The decay
{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
is a flavor-changing neutral current (FCNC) process which has not been observed yet.
In the Standard Model (SM), this decay is highly suppressed
[
1
,
2
]
, with an expected branching fraction
[
3
]
of:

{\cal B}({{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-})_{\rm SM}=(5.18\pm 1.50_{\rm LD}\pm 0.02_{\rm SD})\times 10^{-12},

(1.1)

where LD(SD) uncertainties relate to long-distance(short-distance) effects.

A search for the rare decay
{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
has been performed on a LHCb data set of about
8.6\text{\,fb}^{-1}

[
4
]
.
The data are consistent both with the background-only hypothesis and the combined background and SM signal expectation at the
1.4\sigma
and
1.3\sigma
level, respectively.
We set the most stringent upper limit to date on the
{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
branching fraction —
2.1\,(2.4)\times 10^{-10}
at 90 (95)% confidence level, improving the previous best limit by a factor of four. The obtained results supersede those of our previous publications
[
5
,
6
]
.

We expect to improve limits on
\mathcal{B}(K^{0}_{S}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
by an order of magnitude after LHCb Upgrade I (50 fb
-1
)
[
7
]
. After LHCb Upgrade II (300 fb
-1
) we expect to reach a sensitivity close to the SM expectation
[
7
]
.

1.2
K^{0}_{S(L)}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

The
K^{0}_{S(L)}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays are flavour-changing neutral current processes that have not yet been observed. In the SM, their decay rates are highly suppressed, with expected branching fractions
[
8
]
:

{\cal B}({{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})_{\text{SM}}\sim(1\text{--}4)\times 10^{-14},

(1.2)

{\cal B}({{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})_{\text{SM}}\sim(4\text{--}9)\times 10^{-13}.

(1.3)

Since the expected LHCb acceptance for
{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}
decays is
\sim 2\times 10^{-3}
times smaller than the
{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
one
[
9
]
,
the SM
{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
yield expected in the experiment is small compared to that of the
{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
decay.

Physics beyond the SM can lead to large enhancements of
{\cal B}({{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
with respect to the SM prediction. For instance, proposed dark-sector scenarios can enhance the branching fractions up to around
2\times 10^{-12}

[
10
,
11
]
.

A search for
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S(L)}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
has been performed analysing
5.1\text{\,fb}^{-1}
of LHCb data recorded from 2016 to 2018
[
12
]
. No signal is observed.
The upper limits at 90% C.L. are found to be:

\displaystyle{\cal B}({{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})

\displaystyle<5.1\times 10^{-12},

\displaystyle{\cal B}({{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})

\displaystyle<2.3\times 10^{-9}.

The obtained upper limits are the first reported for the
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S(L)}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay modes. The observed values are close to the maximum values allowed in the dark photon models
[
10
]
.
LHCb could achieve sensitivities at the level of the SM prediction for
{\cal B}({{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
using 300
\text{\,fb}^{-1}
of integrated luminosity (available after LHCb Upgrade II)
[
12
]
.

1.3
\mathchar 28934\relax^{+}\rightarrow p\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

A search for the
\mathchar 28934\relax^{+}\rightarrow p\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay was performed by
LHCb using
3\text{\,fb}^{-1}
of
pp
collisions at centre-of-mass energies
\sqrt{s}=7
and
8\text{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
(Run 1)
[
13
]
. Evidence for the

\mathchar 28934\relax^{+}\rightarrow p\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

A branching fraction

1.4
Future analyses

Besides the analyses discussed above, the following interesting channels are expected to be explored by LHCb in the future:

•

K^{0}_{S}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
— This decay is highly constrained by phase space.

•

K^{0}_{S}\rightarrow(\pi/\mu/e)^{+}(\pi/\mu/e)^{-}e^{+}e^{-}
— These are interesting channels, requiring further data due to low electron efficiencies.

•

K^{0}_{S}\rightarrow e\mu
,
K^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}e\mu
— These channels allow the study of potential lepton flavor violation.

Rare charm decays at LHCb

2.1
Search for rare and forbidden
D^{+}_{(s)}
decays

Decays of the form
{{D}^{+}_{(s)}}\!\rightarrow h^{\pm}\ell^{+}\ell^{(\prime)\mp}
, where
h
is a kaon or pion and
\ell^{(\prime)}
is an electron or muon, are rare or forbidden within the SM, making room for new physics enhancements.
Searches have been made for a total of 25
decays
using
1.6\text{\,}{\mathrm{fb}}^{-1}
of
p

p
collision data collected by the LHCb experiment during 2016
[
14
]
.
No significant deviations from the background-only hypotheses are seen.
The
90\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
CL limits on the branching fractions vary from
1.4\text{\times}{10}^{-8}
to
6.4\text{\times}{10}^{-6}

and represent the world’s best limits for 23 of these decays. In the majority of the channels the improvement on previous limits is more than an order of magnitude, with the largest improvement being a factor of five hundred in
{{D}^{+}_{s}}\!\rightarrow{{K}^{-}}{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{+}}
decays.

2.2
Search for
D^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays

A search for the
{{D}^{0}}\!\rightarrow{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}}
decay in data corresponding to
9\text{\,fb}^{-1}
of
pp
collision data collected by the LHCb experiment is performed
[
15
]
. No excess with respect to the background expectation has been found and an upper limit of

\mathcal{B}({{D}^{0}}\!\rightarrow{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}})<3.1\times 10^{-9}
at 90% CL has been set.
This result represents an improvement of more than a factor two with respect to the previous LHCb result. This measurement constitutes the most stringent limit on the relevant FCNC couplings in the charm sector, allowing additional constraints on physics models beyond the SM that predict the branching fraction of
{{D}^{0}}\!\rightarrow{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}}
.

By the end of LHCb Run 3 (23 fb
-1
) [LHCb Upgrade II (300 fb
-1
)], we expect to achieve a sensitivity of
\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-})<5.9\times 10^{-10}~[1.8\times 10^{-10}
]
[
7
]
.

2.3
D^{0}\rightarrow h^{+}h^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
angular analysis

A measurement of the full set of
C\!P
-averaged angular observables and their
C\!P
asymmetries in
{{D}^{0}}\rightarrow{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
{{D}^{0}}\rightarrow{{K}^{+}}{{K}^{-}}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays is performed
[
16
]
. The analysis uses
p

p
collision data collected with the
LHCb
detector at center-of-mass energies of 7, 8, and 13
TeV
, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 9
\text{\,fb}^{-1}
. This is the first full angular analysis of a rare charm decay ever performed. The full set of
C\!P
-averaged angular observables and their asymmetries are measured as a function of the dimuon invariant mass. The results are consistent with expectations from the SM and with
C\!P
symmetry. The measured null-test observables
are in agreement with the SM null hypothesis with overall
p
values of 79% (0.8%) for
{{D}^{0}}\rightarrow{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
(
{{D}^{0}}\rightarrow{{K}^{+}}{{K}^{-}}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
) decays, corresponding to 0.3 (2.7) Gaussian standard deviations. These measurements will help to constrain the parameter space of physics models extending the SM.

3
Summary

LHCb has worked “like a charm” but seen “nothing strange” yet. LHCb in its first two runs has published several rare strange and charm results, now often providing the best available limits. LHCb is continuing to exploit its unique data set to investigate rare decays. Expect more LHCb rare decay results in the LHCb Upgrade eras.

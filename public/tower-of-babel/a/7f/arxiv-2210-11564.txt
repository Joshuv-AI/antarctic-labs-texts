Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group, and let x x be an element of G G . Denote by Sol G ⁡ ( x ) \Sol_{G}(x) the set of all y ∈ G y\in G such that the group generated by x x and y y is soluble. We investigate the influence of Sol G ⁡ ( x ) \Sol_{G}(x) on the structure of G G .

1.
Introduction

Some of the properties of a finite group
G
are determined by the properties
of its
2
-generated subgroups. For instance, according to Zorn’s theorem in
[
17
]
,
a finite group
G
is nilpotent if and only if every two-generated subgroup of
G
is nilpotent.
In
[
3
]
, Baer proved that
G
is supersoluble if and only if every two-generated subgroup of
G
is supersoluble.
A similar result for the solubility property is provided by John G. Thompson in
[
15
, Corollary 2]

which is an outstanding criterion for the solubility of finite groups. The result reads as follows:

A finite group
G
is soluble if and only if every two-generated subgroup of
G
is soluble.

Given a group
G
, we say that
g\in G
is a
radical element
if for every
x\in G
, the subgroup
generated by
x
and
g
is soluble. For the soluble radical of
G
, which is denoted by
R(G)
,
the following extension of Thompson’s theorem has been proved in
[
8
]
:

The soluble radical
R(G)
of
G
coincides with the collection of all radical elements in
G
.

An interesting topic is the characterization of finite groups in terms of soluble
two-generated subgroups. To achieve this goal, for an element
x\in G
we define the
solubilizer
of
x
in
G
by

\Sol_{G}(x):=\{g\in G~|~\langle x,g\rangle\ \mbox{is soluble}\}.

It might be just a subset of
G
and not a subgroup.
Note that
\Sol_{G}(x)=G
if and only if
x
is a radical element in
G
and equivalently
x\in R(G)
.

In
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
, it is proved that, if for some
x\in G
, the elements of
\Sol_{G}(x)
commute pairwise, then
G
is abelian. In
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
, extending this result it is proved that if for every
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\Sol_{G}(x)
we have the left-normed commutator
[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]=1
, then
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a subgroup. Moreover it is proved that
\Sol_{G}(x)
is nilpotent of class at most
k-1
, and that
G
is nilpotent
of nilpotency class at most
2
if and only if
k=3
. Also, it is raised the question whether we always have the
k
-th term of the lower central series of G,
\gamma_{k}(G)=1
in the case that for every
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\Sol_{G}(x)
it is
[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]=1
.

Our answer to this question is no. For a counter example assume that
G\cong\PGL(2,7)
, then by using GAP
[
5
]
, we see that for all
x\in G
of order
8
,
\Sol_{G}(x)\cong D_{16}
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
.

It is clear that if
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a
2
-subgroup of
G
for some
x\in G
, it must be a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
. Therefore, its structure is expected to be a crucial factor in determining the structure of the insoluble group
G
. The question now arises: in which insoluble groups
G
is
\Sol_{G}(x)
a subgroup?

In this article, we answer this question in the specific case where
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a maximal and meta-cyclic
2
-subgroup of
G
for some
x
in
G
. Additionally, we show that if
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=2p
for an odd prime number
p
, then
G
is a simple group.
Some other results are also given.

2.
Preliminaries and some properties of solubilizers

Now we state some elementary results about the solubilizer of an element in a finite group.

Theorem 2.1

(Deskins, Janko, Huppert)
.

[
10
, Satz 4.7.4]

Assume that
G
has a nilpotent maximal subgroup
M
, such that the nilpotency class of a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
M
is at most
2
. Then
G
is soluble.

Remark 2.2
.

Assume that
G
is an insoluble group and
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a subgroup of
G
. Then, by the above theorem, for any
x\in G
, a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\Sol_{G}(x)
is non-abelian of order at least
16
, otherwise, if
M\leqslant G
such that
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a maximal in
M
, then
M
must be soluble, a contradiction, because
\Sol_{G}(x)
is the maximal soluble subgroup of
G
containing
x
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
1
,
9
]

Let
G
be a finite group and
N\trianglelefteq G
. Then, the following statements hold for any
x\in G
.

(1)

\langle x\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)\cup R(G)\subseteq\Sol_{G}(x)=\bigcup_{H}H,\\

where the union ranges over all soluble subgroups
H
of
G
containing
x
.

(2)

|x|
divides
|\Sol_{G}(x)|
.

(3)

\frac{\Sol_{G}(x)N}{N}\subseteq\Sol_{G/N}(xN)~(
obviously, here
\frac{\Sol_{G}(x)N}{N}:=\{yN~\big|~y\in\Sol_{G}(x)\})
.

(4)

If
N
is soluble, then
\Sol_{\frac{G}{N}}(xN)=\frac{\Sol_{G}(x)}{N}
.

(5)

If
N
is soluble, then
|\Sol_{G}(x)|
is divisible by
|N|
. In particular,
|\Sol_{G}(x)|
is divisible by
|R(G)|
. Furthermore,

|\frac{\Sol_{G}(x)}{N}|=\frac{|\Sol_{G}(x)|}{|N|}
.

(6)

If
G
is insoluble, then
\langle x\rangle
is properly contained in
\Sol_{G}(x)
.

(7)

|\Sol_{G}(x)|
cannot be equal to a prime.

(8)

If
\langle x\rangle=\langle y\rangle
for some
y\in G
, then
\Sol_{G}(x)=\Sol_{G}(y)
.

(9)

For any
g\in G
,
\Sol_{G}(x^{g})=\Sol_{G}(x)^{g}
.

(10)

For every
x\in G
,
|\mathcal{C}_{G}(x)|
divides
|\Sol_{G}(x)|
.

(11)

A finite group
G
is soluble if and only if
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a subgroup of
G
for all
x\in G
.

Similar to Lemma
2.3
-(i),
\mathcal{N}_{G}(H)\subseteq\Sol_{G}(x)
for any soluble subgroup
H
of
G
when
x\in H
, because
\langle x,y\rangle\leqslant\langle H,y\rangle
is soluble for any
y\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(H)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
and
K
be two subgroups of
G
such that
G=HK
and
[H,K]=1
. Assume that
x\in H
, then
\Sol_{G}(x)=K\Sol_{H}(x)
.

Proof.

Obviously
K\Sol_{H}(x)\subseteq\Sol_{G}(x)
. Assume that
g\in\Sol_{G}(x)
, then
g=hk
for some
h\in H
and
k\in K
and
\langle x,hk\rangle
is soluble. Since
\langle x,hk\rangle\leqslant\mathcal{C}_{G}(k)
,
\langle x,h\rangle\leqslant\langle x,hk,k\rangle
is soluble. Therefore
h\in\Sol_{H}(x)
and
g\in K\Sol_{H}(x)
. The assertion is obtained.
∎

3.
\Sol
as a subgroup of
G

While
\Sol_{G}(x)
may not always be a subgroup for a finite group
G
, it is important to note that for an insoluble group
G
, where
G=\langle x^{G}\rangle
for some involution
x
of
G
,
\Sol_{G}(x)
is definitely not a subgroup of
G
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
x
be an arbitrary involution of
G
. If
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a subgroup of
G
, then
x^{G}\subset\Core_{G}(\Sol_{G}(x))
. If in additional
G=\langle x^{G}\rangle
, then
G
is soluble.

Proof.

Since for any
g\in G
, the group
\langle x,x^{g}\rangle
is either cyclic or dihedral, i.e. in particular soluble, we have
x\in\Sol_{G}(x^{g})
. As

\Core_{G}(\Sol_{G}(x))=\bigcap_{g\in G}\Sol_{G}(x)^{g}=\bigcap_{g\in G}\Sol_{G}(x^{g}),

we conclude that
x\in\Core_{G}(\Sol_{G}(x))
. Therefore
x^{G}\subset\Core_{G}(\Sol_{G}(x))
.

Now assume that
G=\langle x^{G}\rangle
. Then
G=\Sol_{G}(x)
, thus
x\in R(G)
and so
G=\langle x^{G}\rangle=R(G)
is soluble.
∎

By the above lemma, if
G
is a simple group and
x\in G
is an involution,
\Sol_{G}(x)
is not a subgroup of
G
. The same holds if
G
is a symmetric group of degree at least 5.

Lemma 3.2
.

Finite insoluble groups do not have self-normalizing subgroups of prime order.

Proof.

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and
\langle x\rangle
be a self-normalizing subgroup of order
p
, where
p
is a prime number. Then
\mathcal{C}_{G}(x)=\langle x\rangle
, hence the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
is of order
p
. Suppose
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
.
Then
\mathcal{N}_{G}(P)=\mathcal{C}_{G}(P)
and so
P
has a normal complement in
G
, say
N
. Since
P=\mathcal{C}_{G}(P)
, so
G=NP
is a Frobenius group with kernel
N
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
x\in G
. Then either
\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)=\Sol_{G}(x)
or
|\Sol_{G}(x)|>\ell|x|
, where
\ell=\min\{|\langle x\rangle:\langle x\rangle\cap\langle x^{y}\rangle|\,|\,y\not\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)\neq\Sol_{G}(x)
and
y\in\Sol_{G}(x)\backslash\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
. Then
\langle x\rangle\langle x^{y}\rangle\subset\langle x,y\rangle
, otherwise
y\in\langle x\rangle
. Hence
\ell|x|<|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq|\Sol_{G}(x)|
.
∎

Assume that
x
is of prime order
p
. In this case
\ell=|x|
, so either
\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)=\Sol_{G}(x)
or
|\Sol_{G}(x)|>p^{2}
. Now immediately we conclude the second main result and Proposition 4.8 of
[
2
]
.

Corollary 3.4
.

[
2
, Theorem B]

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and
x
an element of
G
. Then

|\Sol_{G}(x)|\neq p^{2}
for any prime
p
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Suppose that
P\in\Syl_{p}(G)
for some prime
p
and
x\in P
such that
|x|=\exp(P)
. Then either
\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)=\Sol_{G}(x)
or
|\Sol_{G}(x)|>p\exp(P)
.

Now we have the following structural theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and for some
x\in G
,
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=2p
, where
p
is an odd prime number. Then
G
is simple and
\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)=\Sol_{G}(x)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
-(2), (10), we have
\langle x\rangle=C_{G}(x)
is of prime order (otherwise,
\Sol_{G}(x)=C_{G}(x)
is abelian, a contradiction). Therefore
\langle x\rangle
is a Sylow subgroup of
G
and
|x|=p
, because
G
is insoluble. Also by Lemma
3.3
,
\Sol_{G}(x)=\mathcal{N}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
.

Assume that
G
is not simple and
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. If
p\mid\,|N|
, then
G=N\mathcal{N}_{G}(P)
. So

\mathcal{N}_{N}(P)=P
and by Lemma
3.2
,
N
is soluble, which contradicts the insolubility of
G
. Therefore
p\nmid|N|
, so

P\cap N={1}
and
PN
is a Frobenius group with kernel
N
(because
P=\mathcal{C}_{G}(P)
). Hence
N
is nilpotent and
NP
is soluble. It implies that
NP\leq\Sol_{G}(x)=\mathcal{N}_{G}(P)
, so
|N|=2
and
\Sol_{G}(x)
is abelian, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we can see that if
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=pq
, where
|x|=q>p
are primes, then
G
is simple and
N_{G}(\langle x\rangle)=\Sol_{G}(x)
.

By GAP
[
5
]
we get
|\Sol_{S_{7}}((1~2)(3~4))|=2\cdot 3\cdot 7
,
|\Sol_{S_{5}}((1~2~3)(4~5))|=2^{2}\cdot 3
and
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=2\cdot 3\cdot 7
, where
G=\PSL(2,11)
and
x
is an element of order
3
. These examples show that if the
|\Sol_{G}(x)|
for some
x\in G
is the product of more than two prime numbers, then G is not necessarily simple.

Again by GAP, we observe that
\Sol_{A_{5}}(x)\cong D_{10}
, for some
x
of order
5
and
\Sol_{\PSL(2,7)}(x)\cong C_{7}\rtimes C_{3}
. Now the following problem arises naturally.

Problem
.

Suppose that
p
and
q
are two distinct primes numbers. Find the structure of finite simple groups
G
such that,
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=pq
for some
x\in G
.

In
[
2
, Lemma 4.2]
, the authors show that (with a long proof)
|\Sol_{G}(x)|\neq 8
for all insoluble group
G
and
x\in G
. In the following we give a short proof of the this lemma.

Let
Q\in\Syl_{2}(G)
. Since
Q
is not cyclic and by Theorem
2.1
,
\Sol_{G}(x)
is not a subgroup of
G
, so
2<|Q|\leq|\mathcal{N}_{G}(Q)|<8
. Therefore
Q=\mathcal{N}_{G}(Q)
is of order
4
. Thus
G
is
2
-nilpotent, a contradiction.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and
x\in G
. If
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=16
, then
\Sol_{G}(x)\leqslant G
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Sol_{G}(x)
is not a subgroup of
G
and
Q
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
containing
x
. Then
4\leq|Q|\leq 8
and
|\mathcal{N}_{G}(Q)|<16
. Also
R(G)=1
, otherwise
|\Sol_{G/R(G)}(xR(G))|
is a power of
2
less than or equal to
8
, a contradiction. Now, the following two cases can be distinguished.

Case 1:
|Q|=8
.

Assume that
M
is a subgroup of
G
such that
Q
is a maximal subgroup of
M
. Then
M
is soluble by Theorem
2.1
. Since
|Q|<|M|\leq|\Sol_{G}(x)|
, thus
M=\Sol_{G}(x)
, which is a contradiction.

Case 2:
|Q|=4

Since
Q
is not a self-normalizing subgroup of
G
,
|\mathcal{N}_{G}(Q)|=12
and
\mathcal{C}_{G}(Q)=Q
is elementary abelian. Assume that
G
is not simple and
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Then
2\mid|N|
because
R(G)=1
. If
|Q\cap N|=2
then
N
is
2
-nilpotent and normal
2
-complement of
N
is characteristic of odd order, which is a contradiction. Thus
Q\leqslant N
and
G=N\mathcal{N}_{G}(Q)
, by Frattini argument. Therefore
\mathcal{N}_{N}(Q)=Q=\mathcal{C}_{N}(Q)
, again
N
is
2
-nilpotent, which is a contradiction.

Thus
G
is simple and
Q=\mathcal{C}_{G}(x)
, because
|\mathcal{C}_{G}(x)|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)|
. Now by
[
7
, Theorem 15.2.5]
,
G\cong A_{5}
. Since all involutions in
A_{5}
are conjugate so
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=36
by using GAP, which is final contradiction.
∎

4.
\Sol
as a
2
-subgroup

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and
x
be an element of
G
. By Remark
2.2
,
\Sol_{G}(x)
cannot admits the structure of a
p
-group, where
p
is an odd prime number. Also by Lemma
3.3
,
|\Sol_{G}(x)|\neq p^{n}
if
|x|=p^{n-1}
where
p
is odd.
Now in the following we show that, for some
x\in G
,
\Sol_{G}(x)
can be a
2
-subgroup of
G
of order grater than
8
. Also
\Sol_{G}(x)
can be of size
2^{n}
when
|x|=2^{n-1}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and
\Sol_{G}(x)
be a
2
-subgroup of
G
for some
x\in G
. Then
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
. In addition, if
G/R(G)
is isomorphic to direct product of
\ell
copies of
\PSL(2,p)
, where
p
is prime, then
p\geq 31
is a Mersenne prime number,
|x|\geq 8
and
|\Sol_{G}(x)|=\ell(p+1)|R(G)|
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
-(2),
x
is a
2
-power order too. Let
Q
be a suitable Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
such that
x\in Q=\Sol_{G}(x)
.

Set
\bar{G}=G/R(G)
and
\bar{x}=xR(G)
. As
R(G)\leqslant Q
,
\Sol_{\bar{G}}(\bar{x})
is of
2
-power order. Assume that
G=G_{1}\cdots G_{\ell}
, where
G_{i}\cong\PSL(2,p)
and
[G_{i},G_{j}]=1
for any
1\leq i<j\leq\ell
. By Lemma
2.4
,
\bar{x}\not\in G_{i}
, for all
i\leq\ell
. Let
\bar{x}=x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}
, where
x_{i}\in G_{i}
is an
2
-element.

A Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G_{i}
is isomorphic to either
C_{2}\times C_{2}
, where
p\equiv\pm 3\pmod{8}
or a self-normalizing maximal subgroup of dihedral type, where
p\geq 17
is a Fermat or Mersenne prime number.

In the first case, we can assume that
Q_{i}=\langle x_{i},y_{i}\rangle
is Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G_{i}
and
\mathcal{N}_{G_{i}}(Q_{i})=\langle x_{i},y_{i}\rangle\rtimes\langle s_{i}\rangle
, where
|s_{i}|=3
. Now
\langle\bar{x},\bar{y}\rangle\rtimes\langle\bar{s}\rangle\cong A_{4}
, where
\bar{y}=y_{1}\cdots y_{\ell}
and
\bar{s}=s_{1}\cdots s_{\ell}
. Therefor
3\mid\,|\Sol_{\bar{G}}(\bar{x})|\mid\,|\Sol_{G}(x)|
, a contradiction.

In the second case if
p
is a Fermat prime number, by
[
14
, Theorem 6.25]
,
G_{i}
has a maximal subgroup
M_{i}=\langle s_{i},y_{i}\rangle\cong C_{p}\rtimes C_{(p-1)/2}
, where
|s_{i}|=p
and
y_{i}
is an
2
-element of order
(p-1)/2
. Also
G_{i}
contains a subgroup
D_{i}=\langle s_{i},y_{i}\rangle
isomorphic to
D_{2p}
, where
|s_{i}|=p
and
|y_{i}|=2
. If for some
i
,
\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a maximal cyclic subgroup of order
2
, then
x_{i}\in D_{i}
, otherwise we can assume that
x_{i}\in M_{i}
for all
i
. So in any case
\langle s_{i},x_{i}\rangle
is soluble.
Since
\langle s_{i},x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}\rangle\leqslant\langle s_{i},x_{i},\prod_{i\neq j}x_{j}\rangle
, so it is soluble, thus
p\mid|\Sol_{\bar{G}}(\bar{x})|
, a contradiction.
Thus
p
is a Mersenne prime number.

If for some
i
,
|x_{i}|\leq 4
, then
G_{i}
has a maximal subgroup
M_{i}
isomorphic to
S_{4}
such that
x_{i}\in M_{i}
. Hence for some
i
,
\langle x_{i},s_{i}\rangle
is soluble where
|s_{i}|=3
. Thus
\langle s_{i},x_{i}\cdots x_{\ell}\rangle
is soluble and so
3\mid|\Sol_{\bar{G}}(\bar{x})|
, thus
p\neq 7
. Therefore
p\geq 31

and

\Sol_{G}(x)/R(G)=Q/R(G)\cong\underset{\ell}{\underbrace{D_{p+1}\times\cdots\times D_{p+1}}}.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite minimal insoluble group and
\Sol_{G}(x)
a
2
-subgroup of
G
for some
x\in G
. Then
G/R(G)\cong\PSL(2,p)
, where
p>31
is Mersenne prime and
p\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a
2
-subgroup of
G
. By Lemma
4.1
,
Q:=\Sol_{G}(x)
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
and
R(G)=\Fit(G)
. Since
G
is minimal insoluble,
\Phi(G)=\Fit(G)
, also
Q
is a maximal subgroup of
G
. Hence
G/R(G)
is a minimal simple group with nilpotent maximal subgroup. According to
[
4
, Main Theorem]
and
[
6
, Theorem 2]
,
G/R(G)\cong\PSL(2,p)
with dihedral Sylow
2
-subgroups. By Lemma
4.1
,
p
is a Mersenne prime number, where
p\geq 31
. If
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
, then
\PSL(2,p)
contains
A_{5}
as its maximal subgroup, a contradiction, in particular
p>31
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and
\Sol_{G}(x)
be a
2
-subgroup of
G
for some
x\in G
. If
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a maximal subgroup of
G
, then

N\leqslant G/R(G)\leqslant\Aut(N)
, where
N
is a direct
product of copies of
\PSL(2,p)
, where
p\geq 31
is a Mersenne prime number.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
,
Q:=\Sol_{G}(x)
is Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
, also
\Fit(G)=R(G)\leqslant Q
.

(i) First suppose that
R(G)=1
.
As
\Fit(G)=R(G)=1
, by
[
4
, Main Theorem]
and
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, there exists a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
which is a direct product of copies of
\PSL(2,p)
, where
p\geq 17
is a Mersenne or Fermat prime number. Since

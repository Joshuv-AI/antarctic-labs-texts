Qingguo Hong † , Young-Ju Lee ‡ and Jinchao Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we revisit Korn’s inequality for the piecewise H 1 \displaystyle H^{1} space based on general polygonal or polyhedral decompositions of the domain. Our Korn’s inequality is expressed with minimal jump terms. These minimal jump terms are identified by characterizing the restriction of rigid body mode to edge/face of the partitions. Such minimal jump conditions are shown to be sharp for achieving the Korn’s inequality as well. The sharpness of our result and explicitly given minimal conditions can be used to test whether any given finite element spaces satisfy Korn’s inequality, immediately as well as to build or modify nonconforming finite elements for Korn’s inequality to hold.

|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$})\|_{0}^{2}+(\Phi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}))^{2}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{E}^{o}_{h}}(\hbox{diam}~f)^{d-2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{V}(f)}|[\![\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}]\!]_{f}(p)|^{2}

for all
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}\in\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle V$}_{h}
, where
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$})|_{T}=\mathcal{D}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}|_{T})

for all
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Now, in order to refine the discrete Korn’s inequality, we further impose an addition Assumption for
\displaystyle\Phi
as follows:

(C4)

\displaystyle|\Phi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}-\Pi\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$})|\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$})\|_{0},\quad\forall\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}\in(H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}))^{d}
.

Remark 2.1
.

For
\displaystyle u\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{d}
, Assumption (C1) implies Assumption (C4), but for
\displaystyle u\in(H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}))^{d}
, Assumption (C4) can not be derived from Assumption (C1).

We then obtain the following main result in this paper:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\Phi:(H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}))^{d}\longrightarrow{\rm I\!R}
be a seminorm satisfying the Assumptions (C1), (C2), (C3) and (C4). We have the following results for 2D and 3D, respectively. For
\displaystyle 2D

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$})\|_{0}^{2}+(\Phi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}))^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{E}^{o}_{h}}(\hbox{diam}(f))^{-1}\Big(\|\left[\pi_{1}([\![\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}]\!]_{f}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f})\right]\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f}\|_{0,f}^{2}+\|\left[\pi_{RM^{\perp}(f)}([\![\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}]\!]_{f}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}_{f})\right]\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}_{f}\|_{0,f}^{2}\Big).

For
\displaystyle 3D
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$})\|_{0}^{2}+(\Phi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}))^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{E}^{o}_{h}}(\hbox{diam}(f))^{-1}\Big(\|\left[\pi_{1}([\![\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}]\!]_{f}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f})\right]\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f}\|_{0,f}^{2}+\|\left[\pi_{RM^{\perp}(f)}([\![\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}]\!]_{f}\times\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f})\right]\times\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f}\|_{0,f}^{2}\Big).

Let
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}\in(H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}))^{d}

2.27

2.24

Using condition (C4), we find

Let

be arbitrary and
\displaystyle p\in\mathcal{V}(f)

3.
On sharpness of the Korn’s inequality

In this subsection, we establish that the proposed Korn’s inequality is sharp. We first start with 2D case and move on to 3D case. The goal is to establish that the proposed Korn’s inequality is sharp, see Corollary
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
. More precisely, the above continuity conditions in Corollary
2.1
and Corollary

For a sharpness proof, we shall take examples showing that if any one of the conditions is missing, then the inequality does not hold. These examples are constructed in special 2D and 3D domains as given in Figure

\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E_{CR}$}(T)=({\rm{CR}}(T))^{2}+\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle V_{EC}$}(T),

where
\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle V_{EC}$}}(T)
is the space with the following functions as bases:

(4.14)

\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ij}=b(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ij},~~~\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{jk}=b(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{jk},~~~\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ki}=b(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i})\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}.

The degrees of freedom are given as follows:

(4.15)

\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle t$}_{f}ds\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f}qds,\quad\forall q\in P_{1}(f).

It is simple to prove that the aforementioned degrees of freedom can be equivalently formulated as

(4.16)

\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}ds\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f}qds,\quad\forall q\in P_{1}(f).

We shall now prove that the space
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E_{CR}$}(T)
with the degrees of freedom (
4.16
) is unisolvent.

Lemma 4.2
.

The space
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E_{CR}$}(T)
with the degrees of freedom (
4.16
) is unisolvent.

Proof.

We let
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}\in\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E_{CR}$}(T)
be decomposed into two parts:

(4.17)

\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{0}+\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{0}\in({\rm{CR}}(T))^{2},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}\in\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle V_{EC}$}(T).

We shall assume that
\displaystyle\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}\,ds=0
and
\displaystyle\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{f}q\,ds=0,\,\,\forall q\in P_{1}(f)
, for all
\displaystyle f\in\partial T
and shall show that
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle 0$}
. We first note that for any
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}\in\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle V_{EC}$}(T)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}\,ds=0
, for any
\displaystyle f\in\partial T
, since for all
\displaystyle f\in\partial T
, it holds that
\displaystyle\int_{f}bq\,ds=0,\forall q\in P_{1}(f)
. This measn that
\displaystyle\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{0}\,ds=0
for any
\displaystyle f\in\partial T
and so,
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{0}=0
. Therefore, it is sufficient to show that
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle 0$}
. By the definition of
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle V_{CR}$}(T)
, we can write
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}
as follows for some constants
\displaystyle c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
:

(4.18)

\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}=c_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ij}+c_{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{jk}+c_{3}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ki}.

The fact that
\displaystyle\int_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}\,qds=0,\,\,\forall q\in P_{1}(f)
leads to the following linear system:

(4.19)

\int_{f_{ij}}(c_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ij}+c_{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{jk}+c_{3}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ki})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ij}\lambda_{i}ds&=0,\\
\int_{f_{jk}}(c_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ij}+c_{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{jk}+c_{3}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ki})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{jk}\lambda_{j}ds=0,\\
\int_{f_{ki}}(c_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ij}+c_{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{jk}+c_{3}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\psi$}_{ki})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}\lambda_{k}ds=0.

Or equivalently, we have that

(4.20)

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-2&\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{jk}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ij}&\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ij}\\
\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{jk}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ij}&-2&\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{jk}\\
\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ij}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}&\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{jk}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}&-2\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\\
c_{3}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right).

Since
\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ij}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}|+|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{jk}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle n$}_{ki}|<2
, the coefficient matrix in the equation (
4.20
) is diagonally dominant and so it is invertible, which implies
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle v$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle 0$}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Conclusion

We have proven a Korn’s inequality for the piecewise
\displaystyle H^{1}

. We further have shown some sharpness of the Korn’s inequality. This result has been applied to a number of finite elements to check that they satisfy the Korn’s inequality or not.

Abstract
. Diverging eigenvalues in domain truncations of Schrödinger operators with complex potentials are analyzed and their asymptotic formulas are obtained. Our approach also yields asymptotic formulas for diverging eigenvalues in the strong coupling regime for the imaginary part of the potential.

1.
Introduction

Approximating spectra of non-self-adjoint partial differential operators is a major challenge in spectral analysis even in the case of purely discrete spectra. In this paper, we focus on the spectral convergence of domain truncations for multidimensional Schrödinger operators
-\Delta+Q
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a
complex
potential
Q:\Omega\to{\mathbb{C}}
, the study of which was initiated in
[
12
]
. As an example, which we use here to indicated the questions addressed in this paper and our new results, consider the imaginary oscillator

T_{\infty}=-\Delta_{\rm D}+{\rm i}|x|^{2}

(1.1)

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{B_{1}(0)}
and the Dirichlet boundary condition imposed at
\partial\Omega
. A possible sequence of truncations are
T_{n}=-\Delta_{\rm D}+Q
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{n})
with
\Omega_{n}:=B_{s_{n}}(0)\cap\Omega
,
s_{n}\nearrow+\infty
, and Dirichlet boundary conditions at
\partial\Omega_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The general goal is to determine the relation of spectra of
\{T_{n}\}
and
T_{\infty}
.

It was established in
[
11
]
that for potentials
Q
with
\operatorname{Re}Q\geq 0
,
|Q(x)|\to+\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
and satisfying suitable regularity conditions (hence in particular for (
1.1
)), the truncations
T_{n}
converge to
T_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty
in the norm resolvent sense. Consequently the approximation is
spectrally exact
,
i.e.
all eigenvalues of
T_{\infty}
are approximated by eigenvalues of
T_{n}
and there is no pollution (there are no finite accumulation points of eigenvalues of
\{T_{n}\}
which are not eigenvalues of
T_{\infty}
), see
e.g.

[
12
]
. The results in
[
11
]
include more general cases with different boundary conditions and with potentials having negative real part controlled by
\operatorname{Im}Q
, moreover, the convergence rates of eigenvalues were related to the decay of eigenfunctions of
T_{\infty}
. (For further works on spectral approximations using limiting essential spectra and essential numerical ranges see
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.)

It is however crucial to notice that the spectral exactness does not exclude eigenvalues of the truncations escaping to infinity as
n\to\infty
, which is in our example (
1.1
) illustrated in Figure
1.1
. In fact, many other examples, in particular the one-dimensional imaginary cubic oscillator (
Q(x)={\rm i}x^{3}
) examined in
[
11
,
24
]
, suggest that diverging eigenvalues are rather typical and exhibit quite regular patterns.

Figure 1.1.
Trajectory in
{\mathbb{C}}
of eigenvalues of truncations
T_{n}
of
T_{\infty}
from (
1.1
) for
d=3
,
s_{n}=0.1n
,
n=15,16,\dots,115
; eigenvalues for
l=1
(red),
l=2
(pink),
l=3
(green),
l=4
(purple),
l=5
(brown) only are plotted; see (
1.3
) and Section
6.2
for details. The numerics illustrates the spectral exactness (the clusters of eigenvalues at the ray
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\pi/4}{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
) as well as the eigenvalues escaping to infinity along the blue curves, see (
1.3
) for asymptotic formulas.

The extreme case are the truncations of the imaginary Airy operator
T_{\infty}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+{\rm i}x
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
to
T_{n}
in
L^{2}((-s_{n},s_{n}))
where, due to the established spectral exactness and the fact the spectrum of
T_{\infty}
is empty,
all
eigenvalues of
T_{n}
escape to infinity (see Example
5.1
for details, more results can be found in
[
6
, Thm. 3.1]
).

The main goal of this paper is to analyze the diverging eigenvalues employing an operator convergence and a localization strategy inspired by
[
6
, Thm. 3.1]
.
This approach yields also improvements of the spectral exactness results in
[
11
]
,
moreover, it is applicable in some problems with strongly coupled
\operatorname{Im}Q

T_{g}=-\Delta+\operatorname{Re}Q+{\rm i}g\operatorname{Im}Q,\qquad g\to+\infty,

(1.2)

arising in various contexts like enhanced dissipation,
cf.

[
23
,
38
]
, or
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}
-symmetric phase transitions,
cf.

[
13
,
5
]
(see Section
7
for details).

In particular in example (
1.1
), our results show that the truncations
T_{n}
contain asymptotically the diverging eigenvalues

\lambda_{k,n,l}=(2s_{n})^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\overline{\nu_{k}}+\mathcal{O}_{k,l}\left(s_{n}^{-\frac{4}{3}}\right)\right)+{\rm i}s_{n}^{2},\quad n\to\infty,

(1.3)

where
\{\nu_{k}\}
are eigenvalues of the imaginary Airy operator
-\partial_{x}^{2}+{\rm i}x
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
with Dirichlet boundary condition at
0
(see Section
6.2
, Example
5.2
, Figures
1.1
and
6.3
). To be more precise, by writing that spectra of operators
\{A_{n}\}
contain asymptotically the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k,n}\}_{k}
we mean that

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad\exists n_{k}\in\mathbb{N},\quad\forall n>n_{k},\quad\lambda_{k,n}\in\sigma(A_{n}).

(1.4)

Figures
1.1
and
6.3
, as well as other examples in Section
6
, exhibit a good correspondence of numerics and obtained asymptotics. Moreover, they suggest that all diverging eigenvalues are described (in these examples); however, this remains open.

The improvements in the spectral exactness lie in finding the convergence rate for the resolvent norm in terms of the decay of
|Q|^{-1}
, establishing convergence in Schatten norms and estimating the constants in the convergence rates. Moreover, we can also treat truncations of operators with non-empty essential spectrum like
-\partial_{x}^{2}+x{\rm e}^{x}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
where one truncates the part of the domain where the potential is unbounded,
e.g.
to
(-\infty,s_{n})
with
s_{n}\nearrow+\infty
, see Example
4.4
.

An example of our results for the operators (
1.2
) with a strongly coupled
\operatorname{Im}Q
are the eigenvalues of operators
T_{g}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})

T_{g}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{2}+{\rm i}g(1+|x|^{\kappa})^{-1},

(1.5)

with
\kappa,g>0
, which are known to satisfy
\operatorname{Re}\sigma(T_{g})\geq C_{\kappa}\,g^{\frac{2}{\kappa+2}}
for
g>0
, see
[
38

. Our results show that this bound is exhausted as
g\to+\infty
since the spectra of
T_{g}
contain asymptotically the eigenvalues

\lambda_{k,g}=g^{\frac{2}{\kappa+2}}(\overline{\nu}_{k,\kappa}+o_{k}(1))+{\rm i}g,\quad g\to+\infty,

(1.6)

where
\{\nu_{k,\kappa}\}
are eigenvalues of
-\partial_{x}^{2}+{\rm i}|x|^{\kappa}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(see Example
7.4
for more details and remainder estimates).

1.1.
Notation

We use conventions
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dots\}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\mathbb{N}^{*}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, a subscript in the notation
\mathcal{O}_{a}
indicates that the constant depends on the parameter
a
, and
\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We write
a\lesssim b
to denote that, given
a,b\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of any relevant variable or parameter, such that
a\leq Cb
;
a\gtrsim b
is analogous and
a\approx b
means that
a\lesssim b
and
a\gtrsim b
.

For an open
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and a measurable function
m:\Omega\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we define the corresponding multiplication operator in
L^{2}(\Omega)
on the maximal domain

{\operatorname{Dom}}(m):=\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,:\,mf\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}.

(1.9)

The Dirichlet Laplacian
-\Delta_{\rm D}
is defined via its quadratic form,
i.e.

{\operatorname{Dom}}(\Delta_{\rm D}):=\{f\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,\Delta f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}.

(1.10)

The characteristic function of a set
\Sigma
is denoted by
\chi_{\Sigma}
and

\widetilde{\chi}_{\Sigma}:=1-\chi_{\Sigma}.

2.
Preliminaries

We collect several known results on Schrödinger operators with complex potentials, mostly following
[
3
,
30
,
11
]
; precise references are given at individual claims. We also work out several examples that are used later to illustrate the results.

2.1.
Schrödinger operators with complex potentials

The main basic assumption on the potential
Q
reads as follows.

Assumption 2.1
.

Let
\emptyset\neq\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be open and let
Q\in W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Omega};{\mathbb{C}})
with
\operatorname{Re}Q\geq 0
satisfy

\exists\varepsilon_{\nabla}\in[0,\varepsilon_{\rm crit}),\quad\exists M_{\nabla}\geq 0,\quad|\nabla Q|\leq\varepsilon_{\nabla}|Q|^{\frac{3}{2}}+M_{\nabla}\quad\text{a.e.~in~}\Omega;

(2.1)

here
\varepsilon_{\rm crit}=2-\sqrt{2}
.

\blacksquare

The value of
\varepsilon_{\rm crit}
in Assumption
2.1
is obtained by simple estimates in Lemma
A.1
in Appendix which imply the graph norm estimate (
2.5
). The optimal value of
\varepsilon_{\rm crit}
for the latter in the self-adjoint case is
\varepsilon_{\rm crit}=2
, see
[
21
,
22
]
.
In examples of
Q
, (
2.1
) holds usually with an arbitrarily small
\varepsilon_{\nabla}>0
, see
e.g.
Example
2.3
, and if
Q
is unbounded it suffices to check (
1.8
).

We omit explicit claims, nonetheless, as a consequence (
2.5
) or more precisely (
A.1
), the results summarized in this section generalize straightforwardly when a relatively bounded perturbation with a sufficiently small bound is added.

The Dirichlet realization
T
of
-\Delta+Q
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
can be obtained via the form

\displaystyle t[f]:=\|\nabla f\|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}Q(x)|f(x)|^{2}\,{{\rm d}}x,\quad{\operatorname{Dom}}(t):=W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap{\operatorname{Dom}}(|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}})

(2.2)

invoking the generalization of Lax-Migram theorem due to Almog and Helffer
[
3
]
.
The associated operator is defined in the usual way

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Dom}}(T)

\displaystyle:=\{f\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(t)\,:\,\exists\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega),\ \forall g\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(t),\ t(f,g)=\langle\eta,g\rangle\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle Tf

\displaystyle:=\eta=-\Delta f+Qf.

Under Assumption
2.1
the form
t
is bounded with respect to a natural norm
\|\cdot\|_{t}^{2}:=\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,2}}^{2}+\||Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\|^{2}
, but not coercive in general. Nevertheless, following
[
3
]
, the form
t
exhibits a generalized coercivity

|t_{\alpha}(f,f)|+|t_{\alpha}(\Phi f,f)|\gtrsim\|f\|_{t}^{2},\quad|t_{\alpha}(f,f)|+|t_{\alpha}(f,\Phi f)|\gtrsim\|f\|_{t}^{2},\quad f\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(t),

where
t_{\alpha}:=t+\alpha
with some
\alpha\geq 0
and

\Phi:=\frac{\operatorname{Im}Q}{\sqrt{1+|Q|^{2}}}.

(2.4)

Alternatively, one can introduce
T
by Kato’s theorem
[
20
, Thm. VII.2.5]
, see
[
11
]
.

The following theorem summarizes known properties of
T
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
Q
satisfy Assumption
2.1
and let the operator
T
be defined as in (
2.3
). Then
T
is
m
-accretive, moreover,

(1)

the graph norm of
T
separates,
i.e.
there is a constant
a_{\nabla}>0
, depending only on
\varepsilon_{\nabla}
,
M_{\nabla}
, such that for all
f\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(T)

\|Tf\|^{2}+\|f\|^{2}\geq a_{\nabla}(\|\Delta f\|^{2}+\|Qf\|^{2}+\|f\|^{2}),

(2.5)

hence the domain of
T
separates,
i.e.

{\operatorname{Dom}}(T)={\operatorname{Dom}}(-\Delta_{\rm D})\cap{\operatorname{Dom}}(Q)
;

(2)

T
is
\mathcal{C}
-self-adjoint,
i.e.

T^{*}=\mathcal{C}T\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is the complex conjugation operator
\mathcal{C}f=\overline{f}
,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, thus

\displaystyle T^{*}=-\Delta+\overline{Q},\quad{\operatorname{Dom}}(T^{*})={\operatorname{Dom}}(T);

(2.6)

(3)

if
\Omega
is bounded or if
\Omega
in unbounded and

\lim_{R\to\infty}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{|x|>R,x\in\Omega}|Q(x)|=+\infty,

(2.7)

then
T
has compact resolvent, thus the spectrum of
T
is discrete (consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity);

(4)

denote by
S
the self-adjoint Dirichlet realization of
-\Delta+|Q|
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
i.e.

S:=-\Delta+|Q|
,
{\operatorname{Dom}}(S):={\operatorname{Dom}}(T)
, then (with
p>0
)

(T+1)^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}(L^{2}(\Omega))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(S+1)^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}(L^{2}(\Omega))

(2.8)

and with
k_{1},k_{2}>0
depending only on
\varepsilon_{\nabla},M_{\nabla}
,

k_{1}\|(S+1)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\leq\|(T+1)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\leq k_{2}\|(S+1)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}.

(2.9)

Moreover, let
\partial\Omega\in C^{2,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
. If for
p>0

\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(|\xi|^{2}+|Q(x)|+1)^{-p}\,{{\rm d}}x{{\rm d}}\xi<\infty,

(2.10)

then
(S+1)^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}(L^{2}(\Omega))
.

The proofs can be found in
[
3
,
30
]
where more general forms of
T
are analyzed (
e.g.
with a real magnetic field, complex rotated coefficients, a relatively bounded negative real part of the potentials or singular perturbations controlled by the Laplacian). Estimates on the constant
a_{\nabla}
are in Lemma
A.1
. The equivalences (
2.8
), (
2.9
) are a consequence of the characterization of
{\operatorname{Dom}}(T)
, in particular (
2.5
). Assuming a suitable regularity of
\Omega
, one can also include different boundary conditions (Neumann, Robin), see
[
11
]
for some details.

Example 2.3
.

Let
Q
with
\operatorname{Re}Q\geq 0
be such that

|Q(x)|\approx\langle x\rangle^{\gamma},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(2.11)

with some
\gamma>0
and let (
1.8
) and hence (
2.1
) be satisfied; notice that
e.g.
in the special case of
Q(x)={\rm i}\langle x\rangle^{\gamma}
, we have

|\nabla Q(x)|=\mathcal{O}(|Q(x)||x|^{-1}),\qquad|x|\to\infty.

(2.12)

From Theorem
2.2
, the corresponding Schrödinger operator
T
is m-accretive and has a compact resolvent, moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(T+1)^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{p_{\gamma,d}+\varepsilon}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\qquad p_{\gamma,d}=\frac{2+\gamma}{2\gamma}d;

(2.13)

the latter follows from (
2.10
) by Young’s inequality.

\blacksquare

2.2.
Decay of eigenfunctions

Result on the eigenfunctions decay for accretive Schrödinger operators can be found in
[
30
]
. A slight adaptation of
[
30
, Prop. 4.1]
yields the following, (see Appendix for details).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let Assumption
2.1
be satisfied,
T
be the Dirichlet realizations of
-\Delta+Q
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\Phi
be as in (
2.4
) and define

\widetilde{Q}:=\operatorname{Re}Q+\Phi\operatorname{Im}Q-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}.

(2.14)

Let
\lambda\in\sigma_{\rm p}(T)
and
\psi,\psi_{0}\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(T)
satisfy
T\psi=\lambda\psi+\psi_{0}
.
Suppose that for this
\lambda
, there exist an open
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega
, a constant
\delta>0
and a weight
W\in W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}})
such that
e^{W}\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
e^{W}+|\widetilde{Q}|+|\nabla W|\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1})
and

\widetilde{Q}-3|\nabla W|^{2}-\operatorname{Re}\lambda-|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|\geq 2\delta>0,\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{1}.

(2.15)

Then there exists
C=C(\lambda,\delta,\Omega_{1},W,\widetilde{Q})>0
such that

\|e^{W}\psi\|\leq C\|\psi\|+\delta^{-2}\|{\rm e}^{W}\psi_{0}\|.

(2.16)

(For an estimates of
C
see the proof Theorem
2.4
in Appendix.)

Example 2.5
.

Let
Q
and
T
be as in Example
2.3
. It follows from (
2.11
), (
1.8
) and a straightforward estimate of
|\nabla\Phi|
that there exist
\beta,R>0
such that

\widetilde{Q}(x)\geq\beta|x|^{\gamma},\quad|x|>R.

(2.17)

Suppose that we can find a weight
W\in W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};{\mathbb{R}})
such that

|\nabla W(x)|^{2}\leq\frac{1-\kappa}{3}\widetilde{Q}(x),\quad|x|>R,

(2.18)

where
\kappa\in(0,1)
. Then for all
|x|>R

\widetilde{Q}(x)-3|\nabla W(x)|^{2}-\operatorname{Re}\lambda-|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|\geq\kappa\beta|x|^{\gamma}-\operatorname{Re}\lambda-|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|.

(2.19)

Thus for each
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq 0
, we can find
r_{\lambda}\geq R
such that (
2.15
) is satisfied with
\Omega_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{\lambda}}(0)}
(and some
\delta>0
).

A simple weight
W
satisfying (
2.18
) can be selected as a radial function

W(x):=\sqrt{\beta\frac{1-\kappa}{3}}\int_{0}^{|x|}s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,{{\rm d}}s.

(2.20)

Thus eigenfunctions
\psi
of
T
satisfy
e^{W}\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and the same follows for generalized eigenfunctions by a repeated application of Theorem
2.4
.

Finally, we remark that the result on the eigenfunctions decay will remain valid also in other underlying domains than
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
e.g.
for cones in Example
5.2
below.

\blacksquare

Example 2.6
.

Let
T
be the Schrödinger operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
with the potential
Q:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
that satisfies with some
\gamma>0
and
R_{0}\geq 0
,

|Q(x)|\approx|x|^{\gamma}{\rm e}^{x},\quad|x|>R_{0}

(2.21)

and
|Q^{\prime}(x)|=o(|Q(x)|^{3/2})
as
x\to+\infty
(hence Assumption
2.1
is satisfied with arbitrarily small
\varepsilon_{\nabla}>0
.) Theorem
2.4
applies with
\Omega_{1}=(r_{\lambda},+\infty)
where
r_{\lambda}>0
is sufficiently large so that (
2.15
) holds for the weight (with a sufficiently small
\kappa>0
)

W(x)=\kappa\,\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(x)\int_{0}^{x}s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}{\rm e}^{\frac{s}{2}}\,{{\rm d}}s,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.22)

Thus the (generalized) eigenfunctions of
T
decay fast as
x\to+\infty
.

\blacksquare

with open
\Omega_{j}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Assumption 3.1
.

For
j=1,2
, let
\Omega_{j}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be open and let
Q_{j}
satisfy Assumption
2.1
with constants
\varepsilon_{\nabla,j}
,
M_{\nabla,j}
. Let
T_{j}=-\Delta+Q_{j}
be the Dirichlet realizations in
L^{2}(\Omega_{j})
defined via the forms
t_{j}
given in Theorem
2.2
.
Let
\Omega_{0}:=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
and suppose that
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are such that there exists a cut-off
\xi:\Omega_{0}\to[0,1]
satisfying that
\chi_{\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}}\xi=\xi
on
\Omega_{0}
,
|\nabla\xi|+\Delta\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})
and

\displaystyle\forall f\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(T_{1}),\quad\xi f\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(t_{2}),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\forall g\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(T_{2}),\quad\xi g\in{\operatorname{Dom}}(t_{1});

here we understand
\xi f
as

\begin{cases}\xi(x)f(x),&x\in\Omega_{2}\cap\Omega_{1},\\
0,&x\in\Omega_{2}\setminus\Omega_{1},\end{cases}

(3.2)

and analogously for
\xi g
.

\blacksquare

An illustration of a choice of suitable cut-off
\xi
is in Figure
3.1
.

Figure 3.1.
The domains
\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle|x_{2}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|x_{1}|^{\frac{4}{3}},|x_{1}|\gtrsim 1:

\displaystyle|\nabla Q(x)|

Example 7.2

\left|\frac{Q_{\sigma}(x)-{\rm i}Q_{\infty}(x)}{(Q_{\infty}(x)+1)(Q_{\sigma}(x)+1)}\right|\lesssim\sigma^{2}+\frac{|\sigma^{2+\kappa}gQ_{2}(\sigma x)-Q_{\infty}(\sigma x)|}{|Q_{\infty}(\sigma x)|}\lesssim\sigma^{2}+|h_{0}(\sigma x)|.

Next, when
\sigma^{\beta}\leq|\sigma x|\leq\delta
with
\delta>0
fixed, but sufficiently small, we use inequality (
7.13
) and the properties of
Q_{\infty}
similarly as above to arrive at

\left|\frac{Q_{\sigma}(x)-{\rm i}Q_{\infty}(x)}{(Q_{\infty}(x)+1)(Q_{\sigma}(x)+1)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q_{\infty}(x)|}+\frac{1}{|Q_{\sigma}(x)|}\lesssim\sigma^{\kappa(1-\beta)}.

(7.18)

Finally, for
\delta\leq|\sigma x|\leq R
, we use in addition (
7.2
) and obtain

\left|\frac{Q_{\sigma}(x)-{\rm i}Q_{\infty}(x)}{(Q_{\infty}(x)+1)(Q_{\sigma}(x)+1)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q_{\infty}(x)|}+\frac{1}{|Q_{\sigma}(x)|}\lesssim\sigma^{\kappa}+\frac{\sigma^{\kappa}}{|Q_{2}(\sigma x)|}\lesssim\sigma^{\kappa}.

(7.19)

The estimate of the remaing terms in (
3.31
) is similar. Namely, denoting
\zeta_{\sigma}
the characteristic function of
\operatorname{supp}(1-\xi_{\sigma})
, we obtain

\left\|\frac{\zeta_{\sigma}}{Q_{\sigma}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\sigma^{-1}\Omega)}+\left\|\frac{\zeta_{\sigma}}{Q_{\infty}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\sigma^{\kappa}.

(7.20)

Since all assumptions of Corollary
3.6
are satisfied, we obtain that
S_{\sigma}\to S_{\infty}
as
g\to+\infty
and the conclusion for eigenvalues (
7.5
) follows by spectral mapping. Since the eigenfunctions of
S_{\infty}
decay exponentially, see Example
2.5
, the second term in (
3.35
), entering the estimate of
\rho_{k,g}^{(j)}
, can be omitted.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
be satisfied and let
T=-\Delta+Q
be the Schrödinger operator defined as in Section

\varepsilon_{1}

\varepsilon_{\nabla}
and

such that for all

\|Tf\|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{2-\varepsilon_{\nabla}(2+\sqrt{2})-\varepsilon_{1}}{2-\varepsilon_{\nabla}}\right)(\|\Delta f\|^{2}+\|Qf\|^{2})-C_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{\nabla},M_{\nabla}}\|f\|^{2},

(A.1)

Proof.

By a standard approximation argument, see
e.g.

[
30
]
, it suffices to establish (
A.1
) for

with a bounded support. Integrating by parts we get

\|(-\Delta+Q)f\|^{2}\geq\|\Delta f\|^{2}+\|Qf\|^{2}+2\langle\nabla f,(\operatorname{Re}Q)\nabla f\rangle-2\langle|\nabla f|,|\nabla Q||f|\rangle.

Employing (
2.1
), Cauchy-Schwartz and Young inequalities we get (with
\delta_{1}>0
)

\displaystyle|\langle|\nabla f|,|\nabla Q||f|\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\nabla}\langle|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|,|f|\rangle+M_{\nabla}\langle|\nabla f|,|f|\rangle

Next, integrating by parts and using that

Thus combining (
A.3
) and (
A.4
), using Young inequality (with
\delta_{2}>0

\|\nabla f\|^{2}=\langle-\Delta f,f\rangle\leq\delta_{3}\|\Delta f\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\delta_{3}}\|f\|^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle|\nabla f|,|\nabla Q||f|\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}}\left(\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}\|\Delta f\|\|Qf\|+\frac{\varepsilon_{\nabla}}{4\delta_{1}}\|Qf\|^{2}+M_{\nabla}\|\nabla f\|\|f\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{\nabla}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}+M_{\nabla}\delta_{3}}{1-\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}}\|\Delta f\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon_{\nabla}}{1-\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}}\left(\frac{\delta_{1}}{4\delta_{2}}+\frac{1}{4\delta_{1}}\right)\|Qf\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{M_{\nabla}}{1-\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}}\left(\frac{1}{4\delta_{3}}+\frac{1}{4}\right)\|f\|^{2}.

Inserting the last inequality in (

\displaystyle\|Tf\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{2\varepsilon_{\nabla}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}}-\frac{2M_{\nabla}\delta_{3}}{1-\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}}\right)\|\Delta f\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{2\varepsilon_{\nabla}}{1-\delta_{1}\varepsilon_{\nabla}}\left(\frac{\delta_{1}}{4\delta_{2}}+\frac{1}{4\delta_{1}}\right)\right)\|Qf\|^{2}-C\|f\|^{2}.

can be chosen arbitrarily small and it is hidden in

in (

)

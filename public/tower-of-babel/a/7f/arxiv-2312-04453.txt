Abstract
. We prove the following restricted projection theorem. Let n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and Σ ⊂ S n \Sigma\subset S^{n} be an ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -dimensional C 2 C^{2} manifold such that Σ \Sigma has sectional curvature > 1 >1 . Let Z ⊂ ℝ n + 1 Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} be analytic and let 0 < s < min ⁡ { dim Z , 1 } 0<s<\min\{\dim Z,1\} . Then dim { z ∈ Σ : dim ( Z ⋅ z ) < s } ≤ ( n − 2 ) + s = ( n − 1 ) + ( s − 1 ) < n − 1 . \dim\{z\in\Sigma:\dim(Z\cdot z)<s\}\leq(n-2)+s=(n-1)+(s-1)<n-1. In particular, for almost every z ∈ Σ z\in\Sigma , dim ( Z ⋅ z ) = min ⁡ { dim Z , 1 } \dim(Z\cdot z)=\min\{\dim Z,1\} . The core idea, originated from Käenmäki-Orponen-Venieri, is to transfer the restricted projection problem to the study of the dimension lower bound of Furstenberg sets of cinematic family contained in C 2 ​ ( [ 0 , 1 ] n − 1 ) C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1}) . This cinematic family of functions with multivariables are extensions of those of one variable by Pramanik-Yang-Zahl and Sogge. Since the Furstenberg sets of cinematic family contain the affine Furstenberg sets as a special case, the dimension lower bound of Furstenberg sets improves the one by Héra, Héra-Keleti-Máthé and Dąbrowski-Orponen-Villa. Moreover, our method to show the restricted projection theorem can also give a new proof for the Mattila’s projection theorem in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 .

1.
Introduction

We begin with the following fundamental projection theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel, and
0\leq s<\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z
. Then
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)=\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost every
z\in S^{n-1}
and moreover,

(1.2)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\{z\in S^{n-1}:\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)<s\}\leq(n-2)+s.

In particular,
for almost every
z\in S^{n-1}
,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)=\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
.

In the case
n=2
, Theorem
1.1
is a combination of results in
[
15
,
11
]
by Marstrand and Kaufman. For all
n\geq 2
, Theorem
1.1
is proved by
Mattila
[
16
]
.

Noting that
Z\cdot z
has the same dimension as the orthogonal projection of
Z
to span
\{z\}
, the above theorem states that for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost all directions
z\in S^{n-1}
, the orthogonal projection of
Z
has dimension
\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
.
Since when
n\geq 3
, there are
n-1\geq 2
dimensional many directions, it is natural to study projection properties for some restricted family of directions. Recently, there are a series of results establishing projection theorems to lines restricted to one dimensional family of directions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.

When
n=3
, Pramanik-Yang-Zahl
[
19
]
proved the following theorem, confirming a conjecture in
[
3
]
by Fässler and Orponen.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval and
{\gamma}:I\to S^{2}
be a
C^{2}
curve that satisfies the “non-degenerate” condition:

(1.4)

\text{span}\{{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),\ddot{\gamma}(t)\}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\text{ for all }t\in I.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an analytic set. Then for almost every
t\in I
,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}({\gamma}(t)\cdot Z)=\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
and

(1.5)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\{t\in I:\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot{\gamma}(t))<s\}\leq s.

Before Theorem
1.3
, partial progress was made by Fässler-Orponen
[
3
]
, He
[
6
]
and Käenmäki-Orponen-Venieri
[
13
]
.
When
n>3
, unlike the case in Theorem
1.3
where the intersection of the one dimensional family of directions and
S^{2}
forms a
C^{2}
-curve in
S^{2}
, Gan-Guo-Wang
[
5
]
showed the following theorem using decoupling.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval and
{\gamma}:I\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{\infty}
curve that satisfies:

(1.7)

\text{span}\{{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),\ddot{\gamma}(t),\cdots,{\gamma}^{(n-1)}(t)\}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ for all }t\in I.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an analytic set. Then for almost every
t\in I
,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}({\gamma}(t)\cdot Z)=\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
.

After
[
5
]
, Zahl
[
22
]
reproved Theorem
1.6
and in addition established the same exceptional estimate as in (
1.5
) for Theorem
1.6
.

In this paper, we study the projection theorem to a class of
(n-2)
-dimensional families of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n>3
.
The main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\Sigma\subset S^{n}
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional
C^{2}
manifold such that
\Sigma
has sectional curvature
>1
everywhere. Let
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be analytic and let
0<s<\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
. Then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\{z\in\Sigma:\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)<s\}\leq(n-2)+s.

In particular,
for almost every
z\in\Sigma
,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)=\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
.

Since the regularity assumption of the
(n-1)
-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
is
C^{2}
in Theorem
1.8
,
\Sigma
may not be foliated by an
(n-2)
-dimensional family of
C^{\infty}
curves. Thus Theorem
1.6
does not imply Theorem
1.8
. We also remark that our technique only works for
n\geq 3
in Theorem
1.8
(we refer to the proof of Lemma
1.25
), thus Theorem
1.8
does not contain Theorem
1.3
by Pramanik-Yang-Zahl as a special case.

Example 1.9
.

The typical examples of
\Sigma
in Theorem
1.8
are
\Sigma_{c}:=S^{n}\cap\{x_{n+1}=c\}
for
c\in(0,1)\cup(-1,0)
. Indeed,
\Sigma_{c}
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with radius
\sqrt{1-c^{2}}
and thus has constant sectional curvature
1/(1-c^{2})>1
.

Our method to show Theorem
1.8
can also give a new proof for the Mattila’s projection theorem, i.e. Theorem
1.1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. Indeed, we will show the following more general version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
n\geq 3
, and
\Sigma\subset S^{n-1}
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional manifold. Let
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be analytic and
0<s<\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
. Then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\{z\in\Sigma:\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)<s\}\leq(n-2)+s.

In particular,
for almost every
z\in\Sigma
,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)=\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
.

Obviously, by taking
\Sigma=S^{n-1}
in Theorem
1.10
, we obtain Theorem
1.1
.
Moreover, one may observe that since in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 4
,
S^{n-1}
can be foliated by the family
\{\Sigma_{c}:=S^{n-1}\cap\{x_{n}=c\}\}_{c\in[-1,1]}
,
according to Example
1.9
, applying Theorem
1.8
to each
\Sigma_{c}
(except
c=-1,0,1
), we will deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.8
. However, this way could only show Theorem
1.1
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 4
. Indeed, by a more intrinsic observation, in this paper, we will use the method to show Theorem
1.8
to prove Theorem
1.10
, hence Theorem
1.1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
n\geq 3
.

Remark 1.11
.

We remark that by Gauss’ equation, for any
\Sigma\subset S^{n}
, we have

K_{\xi_{i},\xi_{j}}(x)=\kappa_{i}(x)\kappa_{j}(x)+1,\quad x\in\Sigma

where
\{\xi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\subset T_{x}\Sigma
are the
n-1
principal directions,

K_{\xi_{i},\xi_{j}}(x)
is the sectional curvature of
\Sigma
at
x
spanned by
\xi_{i}
and
\xi_{j}
and
\kappa_{i}(x)
is the principal curvature in the direction
\xi_{i}
. Thus the sectional curvature condition in Theorem
1.8
is equivalent to that the
n-1
principal curvatures at each
x\in\Sigma
,
\kappa_{1}(x),\cdots,\kappa_{n-1}(x)
are non-vanishing and have same sign.

Regarding Remark
1.11
,
the curvature condition in Theorem
1.8
can be seen as a natural generalization of Theorem
1.3
. Indeed, the “non-degenrate” condition (
1.4
) of
{\gamma}
is equivalent to that
{\gamma}
has non-vanishing geodesic curvature
k_{g}
. We explain this below.
Let
{\gamma}\subset S^{2}
satisfy (
1.4
). We assume
{\gamma}
is parameterised by arclength, i.e.
|\dot{\gamma}|\equiv 1
for simplicity.
For each
t\in I
, let
\nu=\nu(t)\in T_{{\gamma}(t)}S^{2}
be the unique (up to
\pm 1
) normal of
{\gamma}
, i.e.
\dot{\gamma}(t)\perp\nu
.
Noting that
{\gamma}(t)\perp T_{{\gamma}(t)}S^{2}
, we know
\{{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),\nu\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Since
|\dot{\gamma}|\equiv 1
implies that

\dot{\gamma}(t)\perp\ddot{\gamma}(t)
for all
t\in I
, we know

\ddot{\gamma}(t)=k_{n}(t){\gamma}(t)+k_{g}(t)\nu
for a unique pair
k_{n}(t),k_{g}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
where
k_{n}(t)
is the normal curvature and
k_{g}(t)
is the geodesic curvature at
{\gamma}(t)
.
By condition (
1.4
), we know
k_{g}(t)\neq 0
. For
n\geq 3
and any hypersurface in
S^{n}
, the corresponding notion of
k_{g}
naturally generalizes to principle curvatures.

Inspired by the above relation between “non-degenerate” condition and curvatures, in higher dimensions, we also introduce a “non-degenerate” condition corresponding to the curvature condition in Theorem
1.8
.

Definition 1.12

(“Non-degenerate” Condition for
(n-1)
-dimensional Manifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional
C^{2}
manifold. We say
\Sigma
satisfies the “non-degenerate” condition if

(i)

there exists a
C^{2}
vector field
\nu:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that

\nu(x)\perp x,\ \nu(x)\perp T_{x}\Sigma\text{ and span}\{x,T_{x}\Sigma,\nu(x)\}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\text{ for all }x\in\Sigma;

(ii)

for any
x\in\Sigma
and any pair of
C^{2}
-curves
{\gamma}_{1},{\gamma}_{2}:[-1,1]\to\Sigma
such that
{\gamma}_{i}(0)=x
and
\dot{\gamma}_{i}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in[-1,1]
and
i=1,2
, it holds

\langle\ddot{\gamma}_{1}(0),\nu(x)\rangle\langle\ddot{\gamma}_{2}(0),\nu(x)\rangle>0.

We will prove the following restricted projection theorem for “non-degenerate” manifolds.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional
C^{2}
manifold satisfying the “non-degenerate” condition. Let
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be analytic and let
0<s<\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
. Then

(1.14)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\{z\in\Sigma:\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)<s\}\leq(n-2)+s.

In particular,
for almost every
z\in\Sigma
,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(Z\cdot z)=\min\{\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z,1\}
.

We will show that Theorem
1.13
includes Theorem
1.8
as a special case and moreover, Theorem
1.13
also contains the result of
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
as a special case. Hence the rest of the paper is devoted to showing Theorem
1.13
.
We outline the ideas of the proof.

1.1.
Restricted projections, cinematic families and Furstenberg sets

Let
Z
and
\Sigma
be as in Theorem
1.13
. By proving the theorem on each chart of
\overline{\Sigma}
(the closure of
\Sigma
), we can assume
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism from
[0,1]^{n-1}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and therefore throughout the paper we assume
\Sigma\in C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
to be a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism.
For each
z\in Z
, define

f_{z}(x):=\langle\Sigma(x),z\rangle,\quad x\in[0,1]^{n-1}.

Then we obtain a family
F=\{f_{z}\}_{z\in Z}\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})
.
Note that for each
x\in[0,1]^{n-1}
, the intersection

\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(\{x\}\times\mathbb{R})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})]=[\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}]\cap[\bigcup_{z\in Z}(x,f_{z}(x))]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

is the projection of
Z
to span
\{\Sigma(x)\}
(up to scaling), i.e.
\langle\Sigma(x),Z\rangle
. Thus the study of the
(n-1)
-dimensional projections can be transferred to the study of the dimension lower bound of the union of
graphs of the functions in
F
. Correspondingly, the proof of exceptional estimate (
1.14
) can also be transferred to that of the dimension lower bound of the union of
certain subsets contained in the graphs of the functions in
F
.
This idea was first discovered by Käenmäki-Orponen-Venieri
[
13
]
to study restricted projections in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and then employed by Pramanik-Yang-Zahl to show Theorem
1.3
.

We depict the heuristic idea of the proof in more details. Fix
0<s<s^{\prime}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z
and choose any subset
X\subset[0,1]^{n-1}
with
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}X=n-2+s^{\prime}
.
Then the fact that Theorem
1.13
holds is equivalent to that

(1.15)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(\langle\Sigma(x),Z\rangle)=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(\{x\}\times\mathbb{R})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})])\geq s,\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-2+s^{\prime}}\mbox{-a.e. }x\in X.

Consider the set

(1.16)

E:=\bigcup_{x\in X}\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(\{x\}\times\mathbb{R})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})]=\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(X\times\mathbb{R})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We will further verify (
1.15
) by showing that
E
has dimension lower bound

(1.17)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E\geq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}X+s=n-2+s^{\prime}+s.

Note that there is no Fubini’s theorem for Hausdorff measures, so instead, we will use a standard
\delta
-discretization argument to show (
1.17
) implies (
1.15
). This is done in Section 5.

To see (
1.17
),
note that
E
can be seen as a union of
(n-2+s^{\prime})
-dimensional subsets from a family of graphs of functions. In general, (
1.17
) can fail for an arbitrary family of graphs of functions. However,
under the “non-degenerate” condition for
\Sigma
, we will show that
F=\{f_{z}\}_{z\in Z}
forms a cinematic family in the following sense and (
1.17
) holds for graphs of a cinematic family.

Definition 1.18

(Cinematic Family)
.

Let
F\subset C^{2}(U)
where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a domain,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We say
F
is a cinematic family of
functions, with cinematic constant
K
, doubling constant
D
and modulus of continuity
\alpha
if the following conditions
hold.

(1)

F
is contained in a ball of diameter
K
under the usual metric on
C^{2}(U)
.

(2)

F
is a doubling metric space, with doubling constant at most
D
.

(3)

For all
f,g\in F
and all
(x,\xi)\in U\times S^{k-1}
, we have

\inf_{x\in U}\{|f(x)-g(x)|+|\nabla f(x)-\nabla g(x)|+|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}f(x)-\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}g(x)|\}\geq K^{-1}\|f-g\|_{C^{2}(U)}.

(4)

There exists an increasing function
\alpha\in C^{0}([0,1],[0,+\infty))
with
\alpha(0)=0
and
0<\alpha(s)\leq K^{-1}s
for all
s\in[0,1]
such that for all
f,g\in F
and all
\xi\in S^{k-1}
, we have for any
\eta>0
sufficiently small and
x,y\in U
with
|x-y|\leq\alpha(\eta)
,

|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)(x)-\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)(y)|\leq\eta.

We call
\alpha
as the modulus of continuity of
F
(which is actually the inverse function of the standard modulus of continuity).

Definition
1.18
can be seen as a natural generalization to functions of multi-variables from a cinematic family of one variable in
[
19
, Definition 1.6]
and furthermore in
[
20
]
by Sogge where the name cinematic comes from. We also remark that the condition (4) in Definition
1.18
is not required in the definition for cinematic family of one variable in
[
19
]
(see the paragraph after Proposition 2.1 therein). This is because the paper
[
19
]
focuses on the specific cinematic family arising from the restricted projection which naturally enjoys condition (4).

With the notion of cinematic family, we define Furstenberg sets of hypersurfaces, which contains the set
E
in (
1.16
) as a special case.

Definition 1.19

(Furstenberg sets of hypersurfaces)
.

Let
s,t>0
and
F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])
be a cinematic family with
cinematic constant
K
, doubling constant
D
and modulus of continuity
\alpha
. A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
(s,t)_{n-1}
-Furstenberg set of
hypersurfaces with parameter set
F
if

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(F)\geq t
and
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(E\cap\mbox{graph}(f))\geq s
for each
f\in F
. Here,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(F)
is the Hausdorff dimension with respect to the distance induced by the
C^{2}
-norm in
C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])
.

Definition
1.19
can be seen as a higher dimensional extension of circular Furstenberg sets studied in
[
14
,
4
]
and Furstenberg sets of curves studied in
[
22
]
. The reason we call
E
a Furstenberg set of
hypersurfaces is because each graph of a function in the parameter set
F
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition
1.19
is also a generalization of the affine Furstenberg-type sets studied by Héra
[
7
]
, Héra-Keleti-Máthé
[
8
]
and Dąbrowski-Orponen-Villa
[
2
]
where the graphs from the parameter set
F
are hyperplanes, i.e.
F
consists of affine functions. Indeed, it is not hard to check that if
f,g\in C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])
are two affine functions, then

\inf_{x\in[0,1]^{n-1}}\{|f(x)-g(x)|+|\nabla f(x)-\nabla g(x)|\}\geq K^{-1}\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})},

for some
K>1
, which shows the family
F
of affine functions are a cinematic family.
In Section 4, we will show the following dimension bound for Furstenberg sets of hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.20
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
0<s,t\leq 1
. Then every
(n-2+s,t)_{n-1}
-Furstenberg set
E
satisfies

(1.21)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(E)\geq n-2+s+\min\{t,s\}.

By verifying
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}F=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z
, we know

E
defined in (
1.16
) is a
(n-2+s^{\prime},\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z)_{n-1}
-Furstenberg set of hypersurfaces. Thus Theorem
1.13
is implied by Theorem
1.20
(or more precisely, Theorem
1.24
, the
\delta
-discretized version of Theorem
1.20
).

Since the Furstenberg sets
E
in Theorem
1.20
contains those considered in
[
7
,
8
,
2
]
, the bound in Theorem
1.20
improves the result of these papers in the corresponding range of
s
and
t
. Also, it is straightforward to see that the bound in Theorem
1.20
is sharp if
0\leq t\leq s\leq 1
. For instance, the equality in (
1.21
) can be achieved when the graphs from
F
are parallel hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

1.2.
\delta
-discretized proof

We will reduce the proof of Theorem
1.20
to a
\delta
-discretized argument. We introduce several definitions.

Definition 1.22

(
(\delta,s)
-set)
.

Let
s\geq 0
,
C>0
,
\delta>0
and
(X,d)
be a metric space. A bounded set
P\subset X
is called a
(\delta,s,C)
-set
if

|P\cap B(x,r)|_{\delta}\leq Cr^{s}|P|_{\delta},\qquad x\in X,\,r\geq\delta.

Here, and in the sequel,
|E|_{\delta}
refers to the
\delta
covering number of
E
.

The following observations are useful to keep in mind about
(\delta,s,C)
-sets. First, if
P
is a non-empty
(\delta,s,C)
-set, then
|P|_{\delta}\geq C^{-1}\delta^{-s}
. This follows by applying the defining condition at scale
r=\delta
. Second, a
(\delta,s,C)
-set is a
(\delta,t,C)
-set for all
0\leq t\leq s
.

The next definition can be considered as the
\delta
-discretized version of Furstenberg sets of hypersurfaces.
In the following, for all
(f,x)\in C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\pi_{1}\colon C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})
stands for the map
\pi_{1}(f,x)=f
.

Definition 1.23
.

Let
s,t\in(0,1]
,
C>0
, and
\delta>0
. A
(\delta,s,t,C)_{n-1}
-configuration
is a set
\Omega\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
F:=\pi_{1}(\Omega)
is a non-empty
(\delta,t,C)
-subset of
C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])
consisting of a cinematic family of functions with
cinematic constant
K
and doubling constant
D
, and
E(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(f,x)\in\Omega\}
is a non-empty
(\delta,n-2+s,C)
-subset of
\mbox{graph}(f)
for all
f\in F
. Additionally, we require that the sets
E(f)
have constant
\delta
-covering number: there exists
M\geq 1
such that
|E(f)|_{\delta}=M
for all
f\in F
.
If the constant
M
is worth emphasising, we will call
\Omega
a
(\delta,s,t,C,M)_{n-1}
-configuration.

We will use the next theorem, in a standard way, to show Theorem
1.20
and Theorem
1.13
.

Theorem 1.24
.

For
0<t\leq s\leq 1
, there exist
\epsilon=\epsilon(s,t,n,K),\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\epsilon)\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
such that the following holds for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
. Let
\Omega
be a
(\delta,s,t,\delta^{-\epsilon},M)_{n-1}
-configuration. Then,
|\mathcal{E}|_{\delta}\geq\delta^{16\epsilon-t-(n-2+s)}
, where

\mathcal{E}:=\bigcup_{f\in F}E(f).

For arbitrary
\delta>0
and
subdomain
D\subset[0,1]^{n-1}
, we
define the
vertical
\delta
-neighborhood

f^{\delta}(D):=\left\{(x,y)=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y)\in D\times\mathbb{R}:f(x)-\delta\leq y\leq f(x)+\delta\right\}.

When
D=[0,1]^{n-1}
, we write
f^{\delta}=f^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-1})
for short.
The idea to show Theorem
1.24
is to upper bound the measure of the intersection
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
for any
f,g\in F
.
By the cinematic property, we can show

Lemma 1.25
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
\delta>0
and

F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})
be a cinematic family. Then for any pair
f,g\in F
with
\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\in[\delta,1]
,
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
has measure

(1.26)

\mathcal{L}^{n}(f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta})\lesssim\frac{\delta^{2}}{\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}}.

Here and throughout the paper,
\mathcal{L}^{k}
denotes the
k
-dim Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
A direct consequence is the following functional estimate.

Theorem 1.27
.

Let
n\geq 3
.
For every
\epsilon>0
and
t\in(0,1]
, there exist
\delta_{0}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
such that the following holds for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
. Let
F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])
be a non-empty
\delta
-separated
(\delta,t,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set of cinematic family.
Then,

(1.28)

\int_{[0,1]^{n}}(\sum_{f\in F}\chi_{f^{\delta}}(x))^{2}\,dx\lesssim\delta^{-2\epsilon}|F|^{2}\delta^{1+t}

where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

When the graphs of functions in
F
in Lemma
1.25
and Theorem
1.27
are
n-1
-spheres, inequalities (
1.26
) and (
1.28
) were known from the work by Kolasa-Wolff
[
12
, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 1’]
.
The proof of Theorem
1.24
is also standard, which can date back to Cordoba
[
1
]
who utilised the functional inequality to show that the planar Kakeya sets always have dimension
2
.

1.3.
Sketch the proof of Lemma
1.25

Finally, we briefly sketch the proof of Lemma
1.25
, which forms the core and the most technical part of the paper.
We will provide the heuristic idea
under the additional assumption
\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\sim 1
for simplicity.

For some technical reason, we will decompose
[0,1]^{n-1}
to dyadic cubes
U
with diameter small enough only depending on the cinematic constant
K
as well as the modulus of continuity
\alpha
and upper bound
\mathcal{L}^{n}[f^{\delta}(U)\cap g^{\delta}(U)]
for all
U
.
We fix
U\subset[0,1]^{n-1}
and write
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}=f^{\delta}(U)\cap g^{\delta}(U)
for short. Noticing that the vertical thickness of
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
is no more than
2\delta
, letting
P_{f,g}
be the orthogonal projection of
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
into the first
n-1
coordinates, it suffices to show

(1.29)

\mathcal{L}^{n-1}(P_{f,g})\lesssim\delta/\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\sim\delta.

The proof consists two cases. Define the “tangency parameter”

(1.30)

\Delta(f,g):=\inf_{x\in U}\{|f(x)-g(x)|+|\nabla f(x)-\nabla g(x)|\}.

The first case is called the transversal case, where
\Delta(f,g)\sim\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\sim 1
, and roughly speaking, the intersection
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}\subset U\times[0,1]
looks like the one of two
c\times\cdots\times c\times\delta
slabs in
U\times[0,1]
where
c\sim\operatorname{diam}U
.
Thus we would expect
\mathcal{L}^{n-1}(P_{f,g})\lesssim\delta
, i.e.
\mathcal{L}^{n}(f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta})\lesssim\delta^{2}
since
\sim\delta^{2}
is the measure of the intersection of two transverse slabs in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Actually, we will show this by foliating
P_{f,g}
by an
(n-2)
-dimensional family of curves
\{{\gamma}_{x}:[0,b_{x}]\to U\}_{x\in\Lambda}
where
\Lambda\subset\partial U
and
{\gamma}_{x}
solves the ODE

(1.31)

\dot{\gamma}_{x}(t)=\frac{\nabla h({\gamma}_{x}(t))}{|\nabla h({\gamma}_{x}(t))|}\quad x\in\Lambda.

Then we define a family of functions
h_{x}:=h\circ{\gamma}_{x}
of one variable. For these functions
h_{x}
, by employing the information that
\Delta(f,g)\sim\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\sim 1
(applying an auxiliary Lemma
2.11
(1) in the real proof), we can show that

|\{s\in[0,b_{x}]:|h_{x}(s)|\leq 2\delta\}|\lesssim\delta.

Noting that

P_{f,g}=\{x\in U:|h(x)|\leq 2\delta\}=\bigcup_{x\in\Lambda}\{{\gamma}_{x}(s):|h_{x}(s)|\leq 2\delta\},

we will deduce (
1.29
) if we can show the map
\Psi:\Omega:=\cup_{x\in\Lambda}(x,[0,b_{x}])\to U
,

(x,s)\mapsto\Psi(x,s):={\gamma}_{x}(s)

is a Lipschitz map with Lipschitz constant
\lesssim 1
. Indeed, since
{\gamma}_{x}
is the solution of (
1.31
), by the continuity dependence of the initial value in ODE theory, together with the
C^{2}
regularity of
h
, we could obtain the desired quantitative bound
\|\mbox{Lip }\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim 1
.

The second case is called the tangent case where the intersection
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
looks like the one of two
\delta
-neighbourhoods of
(n-1)
-spheres “
\lambda
-almost tangent at one point” in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, meaning that the tangency parameter

\Delta(f,g)\sim\lambda\in[0,1]
is much smaller compared with the quantity
\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}
.

Roughly speaking, when
\Delta(f,g)=0
, then there exists some point
x\in U
such that the graphs of
f
and
g
share the same
(n-1)
-dim tangent plane at
x
.

To see
\mathcal{L}^{n-1}(P_{f,g})\lesssim\delta
, we give heuristic ideas in the special case that the graphs of
f
and
g
are (part of) upper-hemispheres and the tangency parameter
\lambda\geq\delta
(the case
\lambda\leq\delta
is easier).
We start with two
2
-dimensional spheres
S^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

For convenience, we focus on the case that two centers of the (upper hemi)-spheres lie on the
x_{3}
-axis in
\mathbb{R}^{3}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\}
and they are the graphs
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{g}
of
f
and
g
, and the condition
\lambda\geq\delta
indicates that they are nearly inner tangent (but not perfectly tangent). See Figure
1
for an illustration.

\begin{overpic}[spheres1]
\end{overpic}

Figure 1.
The intersection of two spheres

The intersection of these two spheres
\Gamma_{f}\cap\Gamma_{g}
is topologically a circle
S^{1}_{f,g}
parallel to
x_{1}ox_{2}
-plane.
Thus
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
is some "neighbourhood" of this
S^{1}_{f,g}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which looks like a solid torus in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Moreover,
P_{f,g}
is some neighbourhood of a circle in
x_{1}ox_{2}
-plane with radius
r
same as the one of
S^{1}_{f,g}
. To estimate
\mathcal{L}^{2}(P_{f,g})
, we just need to estimate two values: the radius
r
of
S^{1}_{f,g}
and the “thickness” of
P_{f,g}
.

To this end,
we decompose this 2-dimensional set
P_{f,g}
to many
1
-dimensional intervals. For any unit vector
\xi
in
x_{1}ox_{2}
-plane (i.e.
\xi\in S^{1}
), define

E_{\xi}:=\{s\geq 0:s\xi\in U\}\mbox{ and }P_{\xi}:=\{s\xi:s\in E_{\xi}\}.

Thus
U=\cup_{\xi\in S^{1}}P_{\xi}
and
P_{f,g}=\cup_{\xi\in S^{1}}[P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}]
. The sets
\{P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}\}
are the desired intervals (where the length
|\{P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}\}|
corresponds to the “thickness” of
P_{f,g}
) and we will show

(1.32)

\max_{x\in\{P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}\}}|x|\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda}\mbox{ and }|P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}|\lesssim\sqrt{\delta/\lambda}\quad\xi\in S^{1}.

Geometrically,
P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}
is the projection of the intersection of the (part of) plane
H_{\xi}=P_{\xi}\times
span
\{(0,0,1)\}
and the set
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
.
Observe that

H_{\xi}\cap f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}

is the intersection of two
\delta
-neighbourhood of (part of) circles
S^{1}
in
H_{\xi}
. We have transferred the estimate of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(P_{f,g})
to that of two
\delta
-neighbourhood of circles. See Figure
2
for an illustration.

\begin{overpic}[spheres2]
\end{overpic}

Figure 2.
The intersection of
H_{\xi}
and two spheres

For the
\delta
-neighbourhood of circles, the estimate (
1.32
) has already been established by Kolasa-Wolff
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
.
Consequently, noting that
(\xi,s)
forms a natural polar coordinate, we use this polar coordinate to compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{2}(P_{f,g})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{2}[\bigcup_{\xi\in\{S^{1}\}}|P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}|]\lesssim\int_{\xi\in S^{1}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}\chi_{P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}}(\xi s)s\,ds\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}|P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}|\sqrt{\lambda}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda}\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\leq\delta.

For higher
(n-1)
-dimensional spheres
S^{n-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in this case the intersection of
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{g}
will be some
(n-2)
-dimensional sphere
S^{n-2}_{f,g}
, so
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
is some neighbourhood of this sphere
S^{n-2}_{f,g}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with vertical thickness
\sim\delta
. Also,
P_{f,g}
will be the neighbourhood of the projection of
S^{n-2}_{f,g}
to span
\{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots x_{n-1}\}
.

To compute
\mathcal{L}^{n-1}(P_{f,g})
, we still use decomposition.
Let
\xi\in S^{n-2}\subset
span
\{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots x_{n-1}\}
and
E_{\xi},P_{\xi},H_{\xi}
be defined similarly as the above case.
Then
P_{\xi}\cap f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
is still
the intersection of two
\delta
-neighbourhood of two circles
S^{1}
in
H_{\xi}
and thus the estimate (
1.32
) holds.
Similarly we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{n-1}(P_{f,g})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{n-1}[\bigcup_{\xi\in\{S^{n-2}\}}|P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}|]\lesssim\int_{\xi\in S^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}\chi_{P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}}(\xi s)s^{n-2}\,ds\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}|P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}|\sqrt{\lambda}^{n-2}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda}^{n-2}\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}}=\sqrt{\lambda}^{n-3}\delta\leq\delta.

Thus we get the desired result.
From this computation, we also notice that the assumption
n\geq 3
is necessary.

In general, the graphs of
f
and
g
may not be (part of)
(n-1)
-dimensional spheres. Motivated by the above special case, we will still decompose
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
into an
(n-2)
-dimensional family of slices
H_{\xi}\cap f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
such that
H_{\xi}\cap f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
looks like the intersection of two
\delta
-neighbourhoods of graphs of functions in a cinematic family of one variable for all
\xi\in S^{n-2}
. This decomposition is guaranteed by the fact that
f-g
is strictly convex/concave in
U
which is further derived from our Definition
1.18
of the cinematic family.
Thus
for each intersection
P_{\xi}\cap P_{f,g}
, we will show that the estimate (
1.32
) holds for a general cinematic family with the help of the auxiliary Lemma
2.11
which is a slight modification of
[
19
, Lemma 3.8]
.
Finally, with the estimate (
1.32
), similar to the above computation, we use the integration under the polar coordinate to deduce Lemma
1.25
as desired.

2.
Preliminaries

The next lemma is a multivariable variant of
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
19
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
\eta>0
,
n\geq 1
.
Let

F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])
be a cinematic family with cinematic constant
K
and modulus of continuity
\alpha
and
f,g\in F
with
t:=\|f-g\|_{C^{2}(I^{n-1})}\leq 1
where
I^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is a dyadic cube.
If
U\subset I^{n-1}
is a (closed) dyadic cube such that its diameter is smaller than
\alpha(\eta/2)
, and
k
is one of the three functions
|f-g|,|\nabla(f-g)|,|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)|
for some
\xi\in S^{n-2}
, then
k
satisfies either one of the following two inequalities:

(S)

k(x)<\eta\|f-g\|_{C^{2}(I^{n-1})}\mbox{ for all }x\in U
.

(L)

k(x)\geq\eta\|f-g\|_{C^{2}(I^{n-1})}/2\mbox{ for all }x\in U
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{diam}U<\alpha(\eta/2)
, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in U
,
|x-x^{\prime}|<\alpha(\eta/2)
.
By Definition
1.18
, we know
that
|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)(x)-\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)(x^{\prime})|<\eta/2
.
Moreover, since
F
is contained in a ball of diameter
K
, we know

\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\leq K
. In particular, we know Lip
(f-g)(x)\leq K
and Lip
|\nabla(f-g)|(x)\leq K
for all
x\in U
. Recalling from Definition
1.18
that
\alpha(s)\leq K^{-1}s
, we know

|k(x)-k(x^{\prime})|\leq\frac{\eta}{2},\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in U,

where
k
is one of the three functions
|f-g|,|\nabla(f-g)|,|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)|
.

Next, fix
U\subset I^{n-1}
with
\operatorname{diam}U<\eta/2
, and suppose that the
alternative (L) fails for
k=f-g
. So there is
x_{0}\in U
such that
|k(x_{0})|<\eta\|f-g\|_{C^{2}(I^{n-1})}/2
. And for any
x\in U
, since
|x-x_{0}|<\alpha(\eta/2)
, by defining
l=k/\|f-g\|_{C^{2}(I^{n-1})}=k/t
, we deduce that Lip
{}_{I^{n-1}}l\leq 1
and

\frac{|k(x)|}{t}\leq\frac{|k(x_{0})|}{t}+\left|\frac{|k(x)|}{t}-\frac{|k(x_{0})|}{t}\right|<\frac{\eta}{2}+|l(x)-l(x_{0})|\leq\frac{\eta}{2}+\mbox{ [Lip${}_{I^{n-1}}l$}]|x-x_{0}|\leq\eta.

Thus the alternative (S) holds for
k
. The proof of the cases
k=|\nabla(f-g)|
and
k=|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)|
are the
same.
∎

We denote by
\lambda U
the cube with the same center as
U
and has side length
\lambda
times as that of
U
for
\lambda>0
.
In the following proof, for technical reasons, given a cinematic family
F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])
with cinematic constant
K
, we first extend
F
to be a cinematic family in
C^{2}([-\frac{\alpha(K^{-1}/6)}{2},1+\frac{\alpha(K^{-1}/6)}{2}]^{n-1})
. Thus
F
will have a new cinematic constant
\bar{K}\geq K
and a new modulus of continuity
\bar{\alpha}\leq\alpha
.
Since
\bar{\alpha},\alpha
are increasing functions,
\bar{\alpha}(\bar{K}^{-1}/6)\leq\alpha(\bar{K}^{-1}/6)\leq\alpha(K^{-1}/6)
. Thus
F
is also a cinematic family in
C^{2}([-\frac{\bar{\alpha}(\bar{K}^{-1}/6)}{2},1+\frac{\bar{\alpha}(\bar{K}^{-1}/6)}{2}]^{n-1})
with cinematic constant
\bar{K}
and modulus of continuity
\bar{\alpha}
.
By abuse of notation, we still write
\bar{K}=K
and
\bar{\alpha}=\alpha
. As a result, letting
I_{F}^{n-1}:=[-\frac{\alpha(K^{-1}/6)}{2},1+\frac{\alpha(K^{-1}/6)}{2}]^{n-1}
, we know
F\subset C^{2}(I_{F}^{n-1})
is a cinematic family of cinematic constant
K
and modulus of continuity
\alpha
.

The above lemma gives the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let

F\subset C^{2}(I_{F}^{n-1})
be a cinematic family with cinematic constant
K
and modulus of continuity
\alpha
,
f,g\in F
and
h:=f-g
. Then for each dyadic cube
U\subset[0,1]^{n-1}\subset I_{F}^{n-1}
with
\operatorname{diam}U<\frac{\alpha(K^{-1}/6)}{2}
,

(A):
at least one of the following three conditions hold:

(i)

|h(x)|\geq\frac{1}{3K}\|h\|_{C^{2}(I_{F}^{n-1})}
for all
x\in 2U
.

(ii)

|\nabla h(x)|\geq\frac{1}{3K}\|h\|_{C^{2}(I_{F}^{n-1})}
for all
x\in 2U
.

(iii)

(2.3)

|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}h(x)|\geq\frac{1}{3K}\|h\|_{C^{2}(I_{F}^{n-1})}\qquad\mbox{for all }x\in 2U,\xi\in S^{n-2}.

(B):
Moreover, if
|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}h|
satisfies (
2.3
), then there exists at most one point
x\in 2U
such that
\nabla h(x)=0
and
h
is strictly convex/concave in
2U
.

Proof.

To see (A), we apply Lemma
2.1
to
h
with
I^{n-1}=I_{F}^{n-1}
and
\eta=K^{-1}/3
. As a result, we know for each dyadic cube
U\subset[0,1]^{n-1}
with diameter
\operatorname{diam}U<\frac{\alpha(\eta/2)}{2}=\frac{\alpha(K^{-1}/6)}{2}
(thus
\operatorname{diam}(2U)<\alpha(\eta/2)
),
either (S) or (L) holds for
k
and all
x\in 2U
(
\subset I_{F}^{n-1}
), where
k
is one of the
functions
h
,
\nabla h
or
\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}h
,
\xi\in S^{n-2}
. On the other hand,
since
F
is a cinematic family of functions, if the alternative (L) does not hold for the functions
h
and
\nabla h
, then by Definition
1.18
(3), for all
\xi\in S^{n-2}
, (L) holds for
\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)
.

To see the assertion (B), assume on the contrary that there exists two points
x,y\in 2U
such that
\nabla h(x)=\nabla h(y)=0
.
In particular, letting
e=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}
, we have
\nabla_{e}h(x)=\nabla_{e}h(y)=0
.
Let
{\gamma}:[0,|x-y|]\to 2U
,
{\gamma}(t)=(1-\frac{t}{|x-y|})x+\frac{t}{|x-y|}y
. Then
h\circ{\gamma}\in C^{2}([0,|x-y|],\mathbb{R})
,
(h\circ{\gamma})^{\prime}(t)=\nabla h({\gamma}(t))\cdot\dot{\gamma}(t)=\nabla_{e}h({\gamma}(t))
and
(h\circ{\gamma})^{\prime\prime}(t)=\nabla_{e}\nabla_{e}h({\gamma}(t))
. By Taylor’s formula, there exists
t_{0}\in[0,|x-y|]
such that

0=\nabla_{e}h(x)-\nabla_{e}h(y)=|x-y|\nabla_{e}\nabla_{e}h({\gamma}(t_{0})).

Hence
\nabla_{e}\nabla_{e}h({\gamma}(t_{0}))=0
, which contradicts (
2.3
).

To see

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
[0,a]\subset\mathbb{R}
and
h\in C^{2}([0,a])
with
\|h\|_{C^{2}([0,a])}=t
.
For
\delta>0
,
write

E_{2\delta}=\{s\in[0,a]:|h(s)|\leq 2\delta\}.

Then, for each
0<\delta<c_{1}t
for some constant
0<c_{1}<1
and
4c_{1}\leq c_{2}<1
,

(1)

if

\lambda_{1}:=\inf_{s\in[0,a]}\{|h(s)|+|h^{\prime}(s)|\}\geq c_{2}t,

then
|E_{2\delta}|\lesssim\delta/t
;

(2)

assume
\lim_{s\to 0+}h^{\prime}(s)=0
and

h^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq c_{2}t,\quad s\in(0,a).

Let

\lambda_{2}:=|h(0)|,

then
E_{\delta}\subset[0,d]
with
d\leq c_{2}^{-1}\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/t}
. Moreover,
E_{\delta}
is a closed interval of length
\lesssim\delta/\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)t}
.

Proof.

First, we show (1). Noticing that for
s\in E_{2\delta}
, we have

c_{2}t\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\min_{s\in E_{2\delta}}\{|h(s)|+|h^{\prime}(s)|\}\leq 2\delta+\min_{s\in E_{2\delta}}\{|h^{\prime}(s)|\}.

By
4c_{1}\leq c_{2}
, we know
\min_{s\in E_{2\delta}}\{|h^{\prime}(s)|\}\gtrsim t
. Thus we get
|E_{2\delta}|\lesssim\delta/t
as desired.

Next, we show (2). Using
h^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq c_{2}t
, we deduce that for all
s\in[0,a]
,
h^{\prime}(s)
does not change sign and

\displaystyle|h(s)|

\displaystyle\geq|\int_{0}^{s}h^{\prime}(\tau)\,d\tau|-\lambda_{2}=\int_{0}^{s}|h^{\prime}(\tau)|\,d\tau-\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{\tau}h^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\,d\rho\,d\tau-\lambda_{2}\geq c_{2}ts^{2}-\lambda_{2}.

Noticing that
s\in E_{2\delta}
implies
|h(s)|\leq 2\delta
, we know

\max E_{2\delta}\leq\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/(c_{2}t)}\leq c_{2}^{-1}\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/t}
.

Finally, we estimate
|E_{2\delta}|
. In the case
\lambda_{2}\leq\delta
, it holds

|E_{2\delta}|\leq d\lesssim\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/t}\sim\frac{\lambda_{2}+\delta}{\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/t}}\sim\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/t}}.

In the case
\lambda_{2}>\delta
, recalling
\lambda_{2}=|h(0)|
, for each
s\in E_{2\delta}\cap[0,a]
, we deduce that

\displaystyle 2\delta

\displaystyle\geq|h(s)|\geq\lambda_{2}-\int_{0}^{s}|h^{\prime}(\tau)|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{2}-\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{\tau}|h^{\prime\prime}(\rho)|\,d\rho\,d\tau\geq\lambda_{2}-ts^{2},

which implies
s\gtrsim\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/t}\sim\sqrt{\lambda_{2}/t}
for all
E_{2\delta}\cap[0,b]
. Using
h^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq c_{2}t
for all
s\in(0,a)
, we have

|h^{\prime}(s)|=|h^{\prime}(s)-h^{\prime}(0)|\gtrsim t|s|\geq\sqrt{\lambda_{2}/t},\quad s\in E_{2\delta}.

As a result, we get
|E_{2\delta}|\lesssim\delta/\sqrt{\lambda_{2}/t}\sim\delta/\sqrt{(\lambda_{2}+\delta)/t}
as desired.
∎

. Thus (1) holds. Also for (4), the modulus of continuity
\alpha
can be chosen as the one of
|\nabla^{2}\Sigma|
.

Fix
y,z\in Z
. We show (3) in Definition
1.18
. Similar as (
5.6
), we have

(5.7)

\|f_{y}-f_{z}\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\lesssim|y-z|.

Write

h:=f_{y}-f_{z}
. We prove (3) by showing

(5.8)

\inf_{x\in[0,1]^{n-1},\xi\in S^{n-2}}\{|h(x)|+|\nabla h(x)|+|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}h(x)|\}\gtrsim|y-z|.

To apply the curvature condition (
5.4
),
define
\tilde{h}\in C^{2}(\Sigma)
by
\tilde{h}(w):=(y-z)\cdot w
,
w\in\Sigma
. Thus
h=\tilde{h}\circ\Sigma
. For each
\xi\in S^{n-2}
, let
\xi_{\ast}:=\nabla\Sigma(x)\cdot\xi\in T_{\Sigma(x)}\Sigma
. We know for all
x\in[0,1]^{n-1}
and
\xi\in S^{n-2}
,

(5.9)

\langle\nabla h(x),\xi\rangle=\langle\nabla\tilde{h}(\Sigma(x)),\xi_{\ast}\rangle

and

(5.10)

\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}h(x)=\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\tilde{h}(\Sigma(x))+\langle\nabla\tilde{h}(\Sigma(x)),\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)(\xi,\xi)\rangle

where
\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)
is a linear map from
T_{x}[0,1]^{n-1}\times T_{x}[0,1]^{n-1}
to
T_{w}\Sigma
.
In particular, noting that
|\nabla\Sigma|
is nowhere vanishing (since
\Sigma
is a diffeomorphism), (
5.9
) implies that

(5.11)

|\nabla h(x)|\sim|\nabla\tilde{h}(\Sigma(x))|,\quad x\in[0,1]^{n-1}

where the implicit constant depends on
\max_{x\in[0,1]^{n-1}}\{|\nabla\Sigma(x)|\}
and
\min_{x\in[0,1]^{n-1}}\{|\nabla\Sigma(x)|\}
.

Fix
x\in[0,1]^{n-1}
and
\xi\in S^{n-2}
in the following and let
w:=\Sigma(x)
. Also, let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}

\subset T\Sigma=\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
be an orthonormal basis on each point in
\Sigma
and
e_{1}(w)=\xi_{\ast}
.
Noting that

\tilde{h}(w)=(y-z)\cdot w
, we have

\nabla_{e_{i}}\tilde{h}(w)=(y-z)\cdot\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(0)
where
\gamma_{i}:[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\to\Sigma
is a
C^{2}
-curve
parametrized by arc-length such that
\gamma_{i}(0)=w
and
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(0)=e_{i}
.
Thus

(5.12)

\nabla\tilde{h}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[(y-z)\cdot\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(0)]e_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[(y-z)\cdot e_{i}]e_{i}.

Since
\Sigma
satisfies Definition
1.12
(i), we know there exists a
C^{2}
vector field
\nu
such that

(5.13)

\nu\perp w,\ \nu\perp T_{w}\Sigma\mbox{ and }\mbox{ span }\{w,T_{w}\Sigma,\nu\}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Furthermore, we note that (
5.14
) implies
w\neq 0
for all
w\in\Sigma
, i.e.
\Sigma
does not contain the origin.
Denoting by
e_{0}:=\frac{w}{|w|}=\frac{\Sigma(x)}{|\Sigma(x)|}
, we have

(5.14)

\nu\perp e_{0},\ \nu\perp T_{w}\Sigma\mbox{ and }\mbox{ span }\{e_{0},T_{w}\Sigma,\nu\}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Thus
y-z
can be uniquely written as

y-z=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}[(y-z)\cdot e_{i}]e_{i}+[(y-z)\cdot\nu]\nu.

Let
I_{\Sigma}
be the first fundamental form of
\Sigma
and

|I_{\Sigma}|=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n-1}|\Gamma_{ij}^{k}|

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\langle\nabla_{e_{j}}e_{i},e_{k}\rangle
are the Christoffel symbols,
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
Define

(5.15)

m:=\max_{p\in\Sigma}\{\langle e_{0},\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}\rangle|_{p}\}.

Recall
\kappa
in (
5.4
) in Remark
5.3
and denote

M:=\frac{\min\{\kappa^{-1},\min_{p\in\Sigma}\{|p|\},1\}}{10n\max\{|I_{\Sigma}|,m,\|\Sigma\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})},1\}}\in(0,\frac{1}{10n}]

where the fact
M>0
is because (
5.14
) implies
\min_{p\in\Sigma}\{|p|\}>0
.
In addition, we notice that
|p|\geq M
for all
p\in\Sigma
.

We consider the following three cases separately.

Case 1.

If
|(y-z)\cdot e_{0}|\geq M|y-z|
, then by
\tilde{h}(w)=(y-z)\cdot(|w|e_{0})
,
we deduce that

|h(x)|=|\tilde{h}(w)|=|w||(y-z)\cdot e_{0}|\geq|w|M|y-z|\geq M^{2}|y-z|\sim|y-z|.

Thus (
5.8
) holds.

Case 2.

If
|(y-z)\cdot e_{i}|\geq M|y-z|
for some
i=1,\cdots,n-1
, then by (
5.11
) and (
5.12
),

|\nabla h(x)|\sim|\nabla\tilde{h}(w)|\geq|(y-z)\cdot e_{i}|\geq M|y-z|\gtrsim|y-z|,

which gives (
5.8
).

Case 3.

|(y-z)\cdot e_{i}|<M|y-z|\leq(10n)^{-1}|y-z|
for all
i=0,\cdots,n-1
, which implies

|(y-z)\cdot\nu|\geq\frac{1}{2}|y-z|
. By direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}\tilde{h}(w)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{e_{i}}[(y-z)\cdot\gamma^{\prime}_{i}(0)]=(y-z)\cdot\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}[(y-z)\cdot e_{j}]\langle e_{j},\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i}\rangle+[(y-z)\cdot\nu]\langle\nu,\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i}\rangle.

Recalling that (
5.15
) and noting that
M\leq\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{10nm}
, we know

(5.16)

|\langle e_{0},\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}\rangle|M\leq m\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{10nm}\leq\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{10n}.

Moreover, for
j=1,\cdots,n-1
, noting that
M\leq\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{10n|I_{\Sigma}|}
, we have

(5.17)

|\langle e_{j},\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i}\rangle|M=|\Gamma_{ji}^{i}|M\leq\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{10n}.

By the definition of of principal curvature of
\Sigma
as well as recalling (
5.4
),

(5.18)

\langle\nu,\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{e_{1}}\nu,e_{1}\rangle\geq\kappa^{-1}.

As a result, recalling
\xi_{\ast}=e_{1}(w)
and combining (
5.16
), (
5.17
) and (
5.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\tilde{h}(w)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}[(y-z)\cdot e_{j}]\langle e_{j},\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\xi_{\ast}\rangle+[(y-z)\cdot\nu]\langle\nu,\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\xi_{\ast}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\geq|[(y-z)\cdot\nu]\langle\nu,\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\xi_{\ast}\rangle|-\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}[(y-z)\cdot e_{j}]\langle e_{j},\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\xi_{\ast}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|y-z|\kappa^{-1}-|y-z|M\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|\langle e_{j},\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\xi_{\ast}\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|y-z|\kappa^{-1}-\frac{1}{10}|y-z|\kappa^{-1}

(5.19)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{5\kappa}|y-z|.

Recalling that
\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)
is a linear map from
T_{x}[0,1]^{n-1}\times T_{x}[0,1]^{n-1}
to
T_{w}\Sigma
, we know
\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)(\xi,\xi)\in T_{w}\Sigma
and thus
\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)(\xi,\xi)\in
span
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}
. Combining (
5.12
) and the standing assumption in
Case 3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\langle\nabla\tilde{h}(\Sigma(x)),\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)(\xi,\xi)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|(y-z)\cdot e_{i}|

\displaystyle<\|\Sigma\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}nM|y-z|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10\kappa}|y-z|.

Consequently, using (
5.10
), we arrive at

|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}h(x)|\geq|\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\nabla_{\xi_{\ast}}\tilde{h}(w)|-|\langle\nabla\tilde{h}(\Sigma(x)),\nabla^{2}\Sigma(x)(\xi,\xi)\rangle|\gtrsim|y-z|,

which gives (
5.8
).
Combining
Cases 1-3
, (3) in Definition
1.18
holds.

Finally, we show (2) in Definition
1.18
. Indeed, combining (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), we deduce that

|y-z|\lesssim\inf_{x\in[0,1]^{n-1},\xi\in S^{n-2}}\{|h(x)|+|\nabla h(x)|+|\nabla_{\xi}\nabla_{\xi}h(x)|\}\leq\|f_{y}-f_{z}\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}\lesssim|y-z|,

which implies that (2) in Definition
1.18
holds and

F
is a doubling space with the same doubling constant as
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. In particular,
F
is biLipschitz homeomorphic to
Z
.

The proof is complete.

.

Let
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z=t
. Without loss of generality, we can assume

. It suffices to show (
1.14
) for all
0<s<t
.
Consider the family

,

F:=\{f_{z}=\langle\Sigma(x),z\rangle\}_{z\in Z}.

Applying Lemma
5.5
, we know
F
is a cinematic family with cinematic constant
K
, doubling constant
D
and modulus of continuity
\alpha
only
depending on
\Sigma
and
n
.

Define
L:=\max_{f_{z}\in F}\{\|f_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1]^{n-1})}\}\leq K

\bar{F}:=\{\frac{1}{2L}(f_{z}+L):f_{z}\in F\}\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-1},[0,1])

. Thus without loss of generality, we can assume

We prove by contradiction. Assume there exists
0<s<t=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}Z
and
X_{s}\subset[0,1]^{n-1}

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}X_{s}>n-2+s

and

(5.20)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(\Sigma(x)\cdot Z)\leq s,\quad x\in X_{s}.

We further formulate this statement in a discrete way.
Fix
s^{\prime}<t
such that
n-2+s^{\prime}\in(n-2+s,\dim_{\mathrm{H}}X_{s})
.
Then
H:=\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}_{\infty}(Z)>0
.
Moreover, we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-2+s^{\prime}}_{\infty}(X_{s})>0

and

\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}(\Sigma(x)\cdot Z)=0,\quad x\in X_{s}.

We apply Theorem
1.24
with

. Then we get a parameter
\epsilon(s^{\prime},n,K^{\prime})
and
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\epsilon)

1.24
holds for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
. Moreover, by decreasing
\delta_{0}
, we fix

such that

(5.21)

\epsilon<\min\{\epsilon(s^{\prime},n,K^{\prime}),\frac{s^{\prime}-s}{48}\},\mbox{ and }\delta_{0}^{\epsilon}<\min\{[\log(1/\delta_{0})]^{-2},C^{-1}\}

where

is a constant to be determined.

Fix
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

, let

We know

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}|I_{x}^{i}|^{s}\leq 1.

Write

for all

and

(5.22)

Z_{x}^{k}:=\{z\in Z:\langle\Sigma(x),z\rangle\in\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{k}}I_{x}^{i}\}.

Thus
\{Z_{x}^{k}\}_{k\geq k_{0}}
forms a cover of
Z
for all
x\in X_{s}
.
Recall
H=\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}_{\infty}(Z)>0
. Thus for each
x\in X_{s}
, by pigeonhole principle, there exists
k_{x}\geq k_{0}
such that

\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}_{\infty}(Z_{x}^{k_{x}})>k_{x}^{-2}\mbox{ and }|\mathcal{I}_{x}^{k_{x}}|\overset{\eqref{sdim}}{\leq}2^{sk_{x}}.

By using a second pigeonhole principle, we can find

, and a subset
\bar{X}_{s}\subset X_{s}
such that

\mathcal{H}^{n-2+s^{\prime}}_{\infty}(\bar{X}_{s})>k_{1}^{-2}\mbox{ and }k_{x}=k_{1}\quad x\in\bar{X}_{s}.

Let
\delta:=2^{-k_{1}}
.

[

, we know that there exists a

-separated

).
As a result, we have

\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}_{\infty}(Z_{x}^{k_{1}})>k_{1}^{-2}=[\log(1/\delta)]^{-2}\mbox{ and }|\mathcal{I}_{x}^{k_{1}}|\leq\delta^{-s}\quad x\in Y.

Since

, again
by
[
18
, Lemma 2.7]
, we can further find a
(n+1)\delta
-separated
(\delta,s^{\prime},C[\log(1/\delta)]^{-2})
-set

satisfying

. Since

(n+1)\delta

\delta
-cubes

in

. So we consider

\delta

still denoting by

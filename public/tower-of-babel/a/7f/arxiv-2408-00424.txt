New Janus interfaces from four-dimensional N = 3 N=3 gauged supergravity


Abstract
We construct new supersymmetric Janus solutions from four-dimensional N = 3 N=3 gauged supergravity coupled to eight vector multiplets with S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SO(3)\times SU(3) gauge group. This gauged supergravity admits a supersymmetric N = 3 N=3 A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} vacuum with the full S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SO(3)\times SU(3) symmetry unbroken and a family of supersymmetric N = 1 , 2 , 3 N=1,2,3 and non-supersymmetric A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} vacua with different residual symmetries. These are expected to be dual to supersymmetric Chern-Simons-Matter (CSM) theories in three dimensions via the AdS/CFT correspondence. By considering a truncation to three complex scalar fields, we find a number of Janus solutions preserving N = 1 N=1 supersymmetry that describes conformal interfaces within N = 3 N=3 CSM theories with S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SO(3)\times SU(3) and S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) diag × U ⁡ ( 1 ) SU(2)_{\textrm{diag}}\times U(1) symmetries.

Introduction

Supersymmetric solutions of gauged supergravity in various dimensions provide a number of insights on different aspects of string/M-theory. In particular, these solutions lead to holographic descriptions of strongly coupled dynamics of superconformal field theories (SCFTs) via the AdS/CFT correspondence. Within this type of holographic solutions,
(d+1)
-dimensional Janus solutions in the form of curved domain walls with
AdS_{d}
slices are one of the most studied holographic solutions. These solutions describe conformal interfaces within higher-dimensional SCFTs and can be found directly in string/M-theory or by uplifting solutions of lower-dimensional gauged supergravity to ten or eleven dimensions, see
[
1
]
-
[
36
]
for an incomplete list. The uplifted solutions are obtained via consistent truncations of string/M-theory to the corresponding gauged supergravity of interest. However, in many cases, the embedding of gauged supergravity in higher dimensions is presently not known. Nevertheless, the study of supersymmetric solutions from gauged supergravity is still useful in the holographic context.

In this paper, we are interested in constructing new supersymmetric Janus solutions from
N=3

[

N=3

The only matter multiplets for

The

{\mathcal{R}[T]_{M}}^{P}{\mathcal{R}[T]_{N}}^{Q}\mathbb{C}_{PQ}=\mathbb{C}_{MN}

(4)

\mathbb{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{0}_{3+n}&\mathbf{I}_{3+n}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{3+n}&\mathbf{0}_{3+n}\end{array}\right).

(5)

\Omega=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{Q_{AB}}^{CD}&P_{ABj}\\

{P_{A}}^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ABC}P^{BCi}=({P_{i}}^{A})^{*}\,.

The coset representative in the symplectic representation

{\mathcal{R}[L]_{M}}^{N}={L_{M}}^{N}\,.

The most general gaugings of the
N=3
supergravity coupled to vector multiplets can be completely determined by the embedding tensor

{X_{(MN}}^{R}\mathbb{C}_{P)R}=0\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\left[X_{M},X_{N}\right]=-{X_{MN}}^{P}X_{P}\,.

(12)

In general, the group
G_{0}

Furthermore, the magnetic vector fields can also participate in the gauging. However, gaugings of this type require the introduction of two-form fields transforming in the adjoint representation of
SU(3,n)

depend only on the coordinate
r
. Using the coset representative computed from
\mathbf{X}^{(i)}
, we find the kinetic term for scalars of the form

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{kin}}=-\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left[{\phi^{\prime}_{a}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sinh^{2}2\phi_{a}{\zeta^{\prime}_{a}}^{2}\right].

(56)

The gravitino-shift matrix takes a diagonal form and is given by

S_{AB}=-\delta_{AB}\left[g_{1}\cosh\phi_{1}\cosh\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{3}-g_{2}e^{i(\zeta_{B}-\zeta_{C}-\zeta_{D})}\sinh\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}\sinh\phi_{3}\right]

(57)

with
B\neq C\neq D
.

To further analyze the BPS conditions, it is useful to introduce the “superpotential” defined by the eigenvalue of
S_{AB}
along the Killing spinors

S_{AB}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{W}_{A}\delta_{AB}\,.

(58)

In general, the three eigenvalues given in (
57
) are not equal, but any of them can be used as the superpotential, as pointed out in
[
48
]
. For definiteness, we will choose
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}
in terms of which the scalar potential can be written as

V=2G^{rs}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\Phi^{r}}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\Phi^{s}}-3W^{2}

(59)

with
W=|\mathcal{W}|
and
\Phi^{r}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})
,
r,s=1,2,3,\ldots 6
. Explicitly, we have

\mathcal{W}=2g_{1}\cosh\phi_{1}\cosh\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{3}-2g_{2}e^{i(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{3})}\sinh\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}\sinh\phi_{3}\,.

(60)

G^{rs}
is the inverse of the scalar metric appearing in the scalar kinetic term as

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{kin}}=-\frac{1}{2}G_{rs}\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{r}\partial^{\mu}\Phi^{s}\,.

(61)

Explicitly, we have
G_{rs}=(2\delta_{ab},\frac{1}{2}\sinh^{2}2\phi_{a}\delta_{a+3,b+3})
,
a,b=1,2,3
. We have also verified that both truncations
i
and
ii
give vanishing Yang-Mills currents implying that all the vector fields can be consistently set to zero.

We now consider supersymmetry transformations of fermions given in (
22
). The analysis is essentially the same as in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
, so we will only give the main results and refer to these references for more detail. We will use Majorana representation with all gamma matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
real and
\gamma_{5}=i\gamma_{\hat{0}}\gamma_{\hat{1}}\gamma_{\hat{\xi}}\gamma_{\hat{r}}

purely imaginary and
\epsilon^{A}=(\epsilon_{A})^{*}
. The Killing spinor in this case is taken to be
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}
. We will also write
\epsilon^{*}=\epsilon^{1}
. We begin with the gravitino variations along
x^{\alpha}
which give

A^{\prime}\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon+\frac{1}{\ell}e^{-A}\gamma_{\hat{\xi}}\epsilon+\mathcal{W}\epsilon^{*}=0\,.

(62)

Following
[
12
]
, we impose the following projectors

\gamma^{\hat{r}}\epsilon=e^{i\Lambda}\epsilon^{*}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\gamma_{\hat{\xi}}\epsilon=i\kappa e^{i\Lambda}\epsilon^{*}

(63)

with a real phase
\Lambda
and
\kappa^{2}=1
. The values of
\kappa=1
or
\kappa=-1
correspond to the chiralities of the Killing spinor on the two-dimensional interface. In the present case, these correspond to
N=(1,0)
or
N=(0,1)
superconformal symmetry, respectively.

Taking the complex conjugate of equation (
62
) and iterating lead to the following equation

A^{\prime 2}=W^{2}-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}e^{-2A}

(64)

which is equivalent to the integrability condition on
\epsilon
along
x^{\alpha}
and
\xi
directions. Using the two projectors in (
63
), we find that equation (
62
) gives the phase function of the form

e^{i\Lambda}=-\frac{\mathcal{W}}{A^{\prime}+\frac{i\kappa}{\ell}e^{-A}}\,.

(65)

The equation coming from
\delta\psi_{A\hat{\xi}}=0
takes the form

e^{-A}\partial_{\xi}\epsilon+\frac{1}{2}A^{\prime}\gamma_{\hat{\xi}\hat{r}}\epsilon+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{W}\gamma_{\hat{\xi}}\epsilon^{*}=0\,.

(66)

Using equation (
62
), we find

\partial_{\xi}\epsilon=\frac{1}{2\ell}\epsilon

(67)

which leads to the solution

\epsilon=e^{\frac{\xi}{2\ell}}\tilde{\epsilon}

(68)

for
\xi
-independent
\tilde{\epsilon}
.

Finally, the gravitino variation along the radial
r
direction gives

\partial_{r}\epsilon+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{W}e^{-i\Lambda}\epsilon=0

(69)

which, by equation (
62
), can be rewritten as

\partial_{r}\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}A^{\prime}\epsilon-\frac{i\kappa}{2\ell}e^{-A}\epsilon=0\,.

(70)

With all these conditions, the Killing spinor can accordingly be written as

\epsilon=e^{\frac{A}{2}+\frac{\xi}{2\ell}+i\frac{\Lambda}{2}}\varepsilon^{(0)}

(71)

in which the spinor
\varepsilon^{(0)}
satisfies

\gamma_{\hat{r}}\varepsilon^{(0)}=\varepsilon^{(0)*}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\gamma_{\hat{\xi}}\varepsilon^{(0)}=i\kappa\varepsilon^{(0)*}\,.

(72)

In general,
\varepsilon^{(0)}
can have an
r
-dependent phase determined by (
70
).

We now move to the supersymmetry transformations of spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fields. Within the truncation under consideration here, it turns out that
N^{A}=0
, so
\delta\chi=0
equation is identically satisfied. Using the
\gamma_{r}
projection given in (
63
), we obtain the following equation from
\delta\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle e^{i\Lambda}(\phi_{1}^{\prime}-i\cosh\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{1}\zeta_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g_{1}\sinh\phi_{1}\cosh\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{3}

(73)

\displaystyle-2g_{2}e^{i(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{3})}\cosh\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}\sinh\phi_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial\phi_{1}}\,.

Similarly, from
\delta\lambda_{iA}
, we find

\displaystyle e^{-i\Lambda}(\phi_{2}^{\prime}-i\cosh\phi_{2}\sinh\phi_{2}\zeta^{\prime}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}^{*}}{\partial\phi_{2}},

(74)

\displaystyle e^{-i\Lambda}(\phi_{3}^{\prime}-i\cosh\phi_{3}\sinh\phi_{3}\zeta^{\prime}_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}^{*}}{\partial\phi_{3}}\,.

(75)

Using the phase function from (
65
), we can solve all these equations and find the BPS equations for scalar fields. These equations are given by

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{1}}-4\textrm{csch}2\phi_{1}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{\ell W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{1}},

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-8\textrm{csch}^{2}2\phi_{1}\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{1}}+4\textrm{csch}2\phi_{1}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{\ell W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle\phi_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{2}}+4\textrm{csch}2\phi_{2}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{\ell W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{2}},

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-8\textrm{csch}^{2}2\phi_{2}\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{2}}-4\textrm{csch}2\phi_{2}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{\ell W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{2}},

\displaystyle\phi_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{3}}+4\textrm{csch}2\phi_{3}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{\ell W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{3}},

\displaystyle\zeta_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-8\textrm{csch}^{2}2\phi_{3}\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{3}}-4\textrm{csch}2\phi_{3}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{\ell W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{3}}

(76)

with

\displaystyle W^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4g_{1}^{2}\cosh^{2}\phi_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\cosh^{2}\phi_{3}+4g_{2}^{2}\sinh^{2}\phi_{1}\sinh^{2}\phi_{2}\sinh^{2}\phi_{3}

(77)

\displaystyle-g_{1}g_{2}\cos(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{3})\sinh 2\phi_{1}\sinh 2\phi_{2}\sinh 2\phi_{3}\,.

We give the explicit form of these equations in the appendix. It can also be verified that these BPS equations together with (
64
) are compatible with the second-order field equations. A similar result can be found in truncation
ii
by setting
g_{2}\rightarrow\frac{g_{2}}{2}
.

4.2
Numerical Janus solutions

The resulting BPS equations are rather complicated to solve for any analytic solutions. Accordingly, we will look for numerical solutions. Unlike the RG flow solutions considered in
[
48
]
in which the phase scalars
\zeta_{a}
vanish, there are six active scalars in the present case. We will look at different subtruncations and consider the full set of BPS equations at the end of this section.

We first consider a truncation to one independent complex scalar by setting

\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=\phi_{3}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\zeta_{2}=\zeta_{3}=-\zeta_{1}\,.

(78)

The BPS equations then reduce to

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sinh 2\phi_{1}}{\ell W^{2}}\left[\ell A^{\prime}(4g_{1}^{2}\cosh^{4}\phi_{1}+4g_{2}^{2}\sinh^{4}\phi_{1}-g_{1}g_{2}\cos 3\zeta_{1}\sinh 4\phi_{1})\right.

(79)

\displaystyle\left.+2e^{-A}g_{1}g_{2}\sin 3\zeta_{1}\sinh 2\phi_{1}\right],

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{-A}}{\ell W^{2}}\left[4(g_{2}^{2}-g_{1}^{2})\cosh 2\phi_{1}-(g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2})(3+\cosh 4\phi_{1})\right.

(80)

\displaystyle\left.+2g_{1}g_{2}\cos 3\zeta_{1}\sinh 4\phi_{1}+4g_{1}g_{2}e^{A}A^{\prime}\ell\sin 3\zeta_{1}\sinh 2\phi_{1}\right]

together with

{A^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}e^{-2A}=W^{2}

(81)

for

W^{2}=4g_{1}^{2}\cosh^{6}\phi_{1}+4g_{2}^{2}\sinh^{6}\phi_{1}-g_{1}g_{2}\cos 3\zeta_{1}\sinh^{3}2\phi_{1}\,.

(82)

In the above equations, we immediately see that for
\zeta_{1}=0
, we need to set
\ell\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, there are no Janus solutions in the absence of pseudoscalars corresponding to imaginary parts of the complex scalars. This is in accordance with other previous works on four-dimensional Janus solutions given in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. It is also useful to point out that setting
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{2}=\zeta_{3}
does not lead to a consistent set of BPS equations unless
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{2}=\zeta_{3}=0
. In particular, the BPS equations imply
\zeta^{\prime}_{1}=-\zeta^{\prime}_{2}
for
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{2}=\zeta_{3}
. This is also in agreement with the result of
[
13
]
in which the non-existence of Janus solutions with
SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}}\times U(1)
symmetry, corresponding to
z_{1}=z_{2}=z_{3}
, has been pointed out.

We are now in a position to give numerical Janus solutions to the above equations. In the numerical analysis, we will choose the following numerical values of various parameters

\kappa=-1,\qquad\ell=1,\qquad g_{1}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad g_{2}=1\,.

(83)

To find the solutions, we use the boundary conditions given by the values of
A=A^{(0)}
,
\phi_{1}=\phi_{1}^{(0)}
and
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{1}^{(0)}
at the turning point of
A
at which
A^{\prime}(r_{0})=0
for a particular value of the radial coordinate
r=r_{0}
. We can also choose
r_{0}=0
. As in other cases, most of the initial values of
(A^{(0)},\phi_{1}^{(0)},\zeta_{1}^{(0)})
generally lead to singular solutions approaching a singular geometry at either
r>0
or
r<0
side or both sides of the turning point. In this paper, we are only interested in regular solutions interpolating between
AdS_{4}
vacua on both side of the interface. We find examples of these Janus solutions as shown in figure

.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have constructed new supersymmetric Janus solutions in
N=3
gauged supergravity coupled to eight vector multiplets with
SO(3)\times SU(3)
gauge group. By considering a truncation to three complex scalars, we have found a number of Janus solutions interpolating between
N=3

AdS_{4}
vacua with
SO(3)\times SU(3)
and
SU(2)_{\textrm{diag}}\times U(1)
symmetries. We have given
N=1
supersymmetric solutions preserving
SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}}\times U(1)
symmetry with one and two independent complex scalars non-vanishing. The solutions with all three complex scalars non-vanishing is
N=1
supersymmetric and invariant only under
U(1)\subset SO(3)\times SU(3)
and a discrete quaternionic group
[
48
]
. These solutions should be holographically dual to various two-dimensional conformal interfaces within
N=3
CSM theories in three dimensions.

It would be interesting to identify the conformal interfaces dual to the supergravity solutions found in this paper in the framework of three-dimensional superconformal field theories. It could also be of particular interest to find possible embedding of the
N=3

SO(3)\times SU(3)
gauged supergravity in ten or eleven dimensions. This would allow us to uplift Janus solutions found in this paper and
[
13
]
and holographic RG flows studied in
[
46
,
48
]
to string/M-theory in which the complete holography can be worked out. This might be achieved by an extension or generalization of the truncation given in
[
50
]
. Using the duality covariant formulation of
N=3
gauged supergravity constructed in
[
48
]
to study other gauge groups and possible symplectic deformations along the line of
[
51
]
is also interesting. Finally, finding other solutions such as
AdS_{4}
black holes from this
N=3
gauged supergravity is also worth considering. We leave these issues to future works.

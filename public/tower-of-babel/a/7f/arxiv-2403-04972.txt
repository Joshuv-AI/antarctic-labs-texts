On abelian extensions in mixed characteristic and ramification in codimension one Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 13B05.
Daniel Katz, Prashanth Sridhar

Abstract
. A theorem of Paul Roberts ( [ Rob80 ] ) states that the integral closure of a regular local ring in a generically abelian extension is Cohen-Macaulay, provided the characteristic of the residue field does not divide the order of the Galois group. An example of Koh in [ Koh86 ] shows the conclusion is false in the modular case. After a modification to the statement concerning ramification over p p in codimension one, we give an extension of Roberts’s theorem to the modular case for unramified regular local rings in mixed characteristic when the p p -torsion of the Galois group is annihilated by p p .



1.
Introduction

It is a classical question in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry to study the variety
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
in terms of the fibres of a finite morphism
f:\operatorname{Spec}(R)\to\operatorname{Spec}(S)
, where
R
is a normal domain and
S
is regular. Such an
S
is available when
R
is a finitely generated algebra over a field or is complete. For example, the purity of branch locus states that if
f
is unramified in codimension one, then
f
is étale, see
[
Zar58
]
,
[
Nag58
]
,
[
Nag59
]
and
[
Aus62
]
. Generalizations and variants of this theorem have been studied extensively by relaxing the hypothesis that
S
is regular and establishing whether good properties of
S
transfer to good properties of
R
when there is no ramification in low codimension, see for example
[
Gro68
]
,
[
Gri87
]
,
[
Gri91
]
,
[
Cut95
]
,
[
Kan99
]
. One such property whose transfer has been studied is that of Cohen-Macaulayness. The work in this paper fits in the framework of a related, but orthogonal question - are there good patterns of ramification in codimension one that result in transfer of good properties (Cohen-Macaulayness) from (regular)
S
to
R
? As far as we know, very little is known in this direction.

Our study is motivated by a theorem of Roberts in
[
Rob80
]
that states that the integral closure of a regular local ring in an abelian extension of its fraction field is Cohen-Macaulay, provided the characteristic of the residue field does not divide the order of the Galois group. This result has seen generalizations/applications to the theory of algebraic monoids, singularity theory and arithmetic schemes with a tame action over an abelian group, see
[
Ren83
,
Ito89
,
CPT09
]
. We explain by means of an alternate proof of this statement (see
3.3
) as to why we view this result as one about “good ramification” in codimension one. Roberts’s theorem fails in the modular case, i.e., when the characteristic of the residue field divides the order of the Galois group. Koh in
[
Koh86
]
gave an example of this phenomenon in mixed characteristic. One way to explain this is to note that Roberts’s theorem relies on Maschke’s theorem and there is no direct analog of such an argument in the modular case. But beyond this, not much seems to be known in this regard; see
[
Gri15
]
for comments. Guided by intuition from our alternate proof of Roberts’s theorem, we ask if there exists some analog of this theorem in mixed characteristic.

Let
\Psi:S\to R
be a map of commutative Noetherian rings and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
a prime integer. Say

is

-unramified

is étale in codimension one over

, i.e., if

is étale for all height one primes

containing

(
2.4
). Similarly, in direct analogue to the notion in algebraic number theory,

. If

is a unit, these conditions are satisfied vacuously. It is reasonable to expect that in mixed characteristic

is generically abelian with

p

. Hence, to get an extension of Roberts’s theorem to mixed characteristic, we turn to Kummer theory. Let

be an indeterminate over

and

a prime integer. Then we say

is

-unramified
(resp.

-unramified (resp. tamely

denotes normalization (

is

-ramified. The definitions are independent of the choice of the root

possesses a primitive

-th root of unity. We characterize these properties in terms of numerical conditions in codimension one using the function

(see

Theorem 1.1

(
4.1
)
.

Let
S
be a regular local ring such that
\mathrm{char}(\mathrm{Frac}(S))=0
. Assume

possesses a primitive
p
-th root of unity for

a prime integer. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

0\neq f\in S
is
p
-unramified.

(2)

0\neq f\in S
is tamely

-ramified.

(3)

either

(a)

is a
p
-th power or

(b)

and for all

\Gamma_{QS_{Q}}(f)\geq[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(1/p^{i})]ord_{Q}(p)=\dfrac{p}{p-1}ord_{Q}(p)

where

is the largest power

admits a
p
-th root in

.

Assume

has mixed characteristic

and that it possesses a primitive

-th root of unity. Given a generically abelian extension

, with

. If in addition to

and

Theorem 1.2

(
5.1
)
.

Let

be an unramified regular local ring of mixed characteristic

with quotient field

. Let

-torsion annihilated by

We discuss how our results apply to Koh’s example, see

that the

-ramified canonical divisors are in some sense the obstruction to such an analog in full generality and present a calculation involving the first

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains preliminary definitions and results that are used later. Section 3 contains the alternate take on Roberts’s theorem. Section 4 consists of the numerical characterization of the

-unramified abelian extensions. Section 5 presents the main result and includes a discussion on Koh’s example. Finally, section 6 comments on the

-ramified case.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
S
be an integrally closed domain such that
\mathrm{char}(\mathrm{Frac}(S))=0
and

is a prime element for some prime integer

. Then

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the case

\Phi_{p^{r}}(x+1)
is irreducible over

. To see this, recall that if

\Phi_{p^{r}}(x)=\Phi_{p}(x^{p^{r-1}})
, thus,

\Phi_{p^{r}}(x+1)=((x+1)^{p^{r-1}})^{p-1}+((x+1)^{p^{r-1}})^{p-2}+\cdots+(x+1)^{p^{r-1}}+1.

For each

is divisible by

is an Eisenstein polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Since

is Eisenstein in

∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let

(i)

(ii)

For

.

Suppose further that

. Then the degree of

is

, where

divides

. In particular, the degree of

over

is a unit in

Proof.

Consider a single expression
\alpha:=\sqrt[n]{q}
, an

-th root of

. If

is a square-free non-unit, then

is irreducible over

over

. Moreover, since

is integrally closed by

Now suppose

is a unit in

. Set

. We now note that

, equivalently,
\alpha^{d}\in L
. Let

denote a primitive

. On the one hand, since

For the general case, one proceeds by induction using the fact that

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
S
be a domain and for each

, let

be module finite extension of domains such that

Proof.

Let
\Psi:R_{1}\otimes_{S}\dots\otimes_{S}R_{n}\rightarrow V
denote the natural surjection of

,

. By hypothesis, there exists an isomorphism of

\mathfrak{R}^{r}

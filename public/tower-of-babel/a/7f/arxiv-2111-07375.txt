Explicit rank-one constructions for irrational rotations
Alexandre I. Danilenko, Mykyta I. Vieprik

Abstract
For each well approximable irrational θ \theta , we provide an explicit rank-one construction of the e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ θ e^{2\pi i\theta} -rotation R θ R_{\theta} on the circle 𝕋 \mathbb{T} . This solves “almost surely” a problem by del Junco. For every irrational θ \theta , we construct explicitly a rank-one transformation with an eigenvalue e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ θ e^{2\pi i\theta} . For every irrational θ \theta , two infinite σ \sigma -finite invariant measures μ θ \mu_{\theta} and μ θ ′ \mu_{\theta}^{\prime} on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} are constructed explicitly such that ( 𝕋 , μ θ , R θ ) (\mathbb{T},\mu_{\theta},R_{\theta}) is rigid and of rank one and ( 𝕋 , μ θ ′ , R θ ) (\mathbb{T},\mu_{\theta}^{\prime},R_{\theta}) is of zero type and of rank one. The centralizer of the latter system consists of just the powers of R θ R_{\theta} . Some versions of the aforementioned results are proved under an extra condition on boundedness of the sequence of cuts in the rank-one construction.

Preliminaries

Continued fractions

We recall some basic facts from the theory of continued fractions.
Every irrational number
\theta
can be represented as an infinite continued fraction

[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
for each
j>0
.
The rational numbers
\displaystyle\frac{p_{k}}{q_{k}}:=[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}]
with
(p_{k},q_{k})=1
are called the
convergents
for
\theta
.
For each
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle p_{k}

\displaystyle=a_{k-1}q_{k-1}+p_{k-2},\text{ }p_{0}=1,\ p_{1}=a_{0},

\displaystyle q_{k}

\displaystyle=a_{k-1}q_{k-1}+q_{k-2},\text{ }q_{0}=0,\ q_{1}=1.

Definition 2.1
.

An irrational number
\theta
is called
badly approximable
if the sequence
(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded.
The irrational numbers that are not badly approximable are called
well approximable
.

We will utilize the following well known results from the theory of continuous fractions (see
[
Kh
]
for the proof).

Fact 2.2
.

(i)

(q_{n}\theta-p_{n})(q_{n+1}\theta-p_{n+1})<0

for each
n>0
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{n}+q_{n+1}}<|\theta q_{n}-p_{n}|<\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{n+1}}
for each
n>0
.

(iii)

|q_{n}\theta-p_{n}|<\min\Big\{|b\theta-a|:{\mathbb{N}\ni b\leq q_{n},a\in\mathbb{Z}},\frac{a}{b}\neq\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\Big\}

for each
n>0
.

(iv)

If there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|q\theta-p|<\min\Big\{|b\theta-a|:{\mathbb{N}\ni b\leq q,a\in\mathbb{Z}},\frac{a}{b}\neq\frac{p}{q}\Big\}

then
\frac{p}{q}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(v)

\theta
is badly approximable if and only if there exists a real
\delta>0
such that

\min_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}|q\theta-p|>\frac{\delta}{q}
for each
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

(vi)

If
\theta
is algebraic of power
n
then there exists a real
\delta>0
such that

\min_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}|q\theta-p|>\frac{\delta}{q^{n}}
for each
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

(vii)

The set of badly approximable numbers has Lebesgue measure zero.

Dynamical systems

We recall that a dynamical system
(X,\mathfrak{B},\mu,T)
(or just
T
) is called

•

ergodic

if each
T
-invariant subset is either
\mu
-null or
\mu
-conull;

•

totally ergodic

if
T^{p}
is ergodic for each
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

rigid
if there is a sequence
n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
such that

\mu(T^{n_{k}}A\cap B)\to\mu(A\cap B)\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$}

for all subsets
A,B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
\mu(A)<\infty
and
\mu(B)<\infty
;

•

of
0
-type
if

\mu(T^{n}A\cap B)\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}

for all subsets
A,B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
\mu(A)<\infty
and
\mu(B)<\infty
;

•

of rank one
if there are subsets
B_{1},B_{2},\dots
of
X
with
\mu(B_{k})<\infty
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence of positive integers
n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
such that
T^{l}B_{k}\cap T^{m}B_{k}=\emptyset
whenever
0\leq l<m<n_{k}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for each subset
A\in\mathfrak{B}
of finite measure,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\min_{J\subset\{0,\dots,n_{k}-1\}}\mu\bigg(A\triangle\bigsqcup_{j\in J}T^{j}B_{k}\bigg)=0.

Of course, if
T
is of 0-type then
\mu(X)=\infty
.
If
T
is of rank one then
T
is ergodic.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose that
T
is ergodic.
A number
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
is called an

if there is a measurable function

such that

.
The function

is called a
\lambda
-eigenfunction
of
T
.
It is defined up to a multiplicative constant from
\mathbb{T}
.
The set of all eigenvalues is called

-spectrum
of

and denoted by

.

Of course,
e(T)
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{T}
.
If
\mu(X)<\infty
then

is countable.
It is straightforward to verify that

is totally ergodic if and only if

is torsion free.

For the
\lambda
-rotation

on

endowed with the Haar measure,

.

The

Of course,

Let

\displaystyle F_{0}=\{0\},\#C_{n}>1,

\displaystyle(F_{n}+c)\cap(F_{n}+c^{\prime})=\varnothing\text{, if $c,c^{\prime}\in C_{n+1}$ and $c\neq c^{\prime}$. }

.
Therefore the inductive limit
X
of the sequence

Moreover,

Given a subset

[A]_{n}:=\{x=(f_{n},c_{n+1},\ldots)\in X_{n},f_{n}\in A\}

-

.
It is open and compact in

.
For brevity, we will write

.
Also, we will write
\mathbf{0}

There exists a unique
\sigma

such that
\mu(X_{0})=1

\mu([f]_{n})=\mu([f^{\prime}]_{n})\text{ for all }f,f^{\prime}\in F_{n},\ n\geq 0.

It is easy to verify that

\mu([A]_{n})=\displaystyle\frac{\#A}{\#C_{1}\cdots\#C_{n}}\text{ for each subset }A\subset F_{n},\ n>0.

We also note that
\mu(X)<\infty
if and only if

(2.4)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\#F_{n+1}-\#F_{n}\#C_{n+1}}{\#F_{n+1}}<\infty.

From now on,

(2.5)

F_{n}=\{0,1,\dots,h_{n}-1\}\quad\text{ for some $h_{n}>0$ and every $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Then we can define a transformation
T
on
X
.
We first note that for

We call

Eigenfunctions of
(C,F)
-equivalence relations

Let
\mathcal{R}
be the
(C,F)
-equivalence relation on
X

We now define a Borel mapping

d(x,y):=f_{n}-f_{n}^{\prime},\quad\text{for each $(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}$}.

It is straightforward to verify that

In other words,

.

We call a complex number

\lambda^{d(x,y)}=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)^{-1}\qquad\text{for all $(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}$ with $x,y\not\in A$.}

We call
\varphi

\lambda

\mathcal{R}
.
It is defined up to a multiplicative constant.
We denote by

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\lambda\in e(\mathcal{R})

If a
\lambda

Moreover, for each

\varphi(x)=\varphi(\mathbf{0})\,\lambda^{f_{n}}\prod\limits_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\lambda^{c_{k}}.

Proof.

We will consider only the case where
\varphi
is continuous at
\mathbf{0}
.
The other cases are considered in a similar way.
Take a point
x\in X
.
Then there is
n>0
such that
x\in X_{n}
and
x=(f_{n},c_{n+1},c_{n+2},\dots)
for some

and
c_{k}\in C_{k}
if

We now set

p_{k}(x):=(0,\dots,0,c_{k+1},c_{k+2},\dots)\in X_{n}

for each

Then
p_{k}(x)\to\mathbf{0}
as

Hence
\varphi(p_{k}(x))\to\varphi(\mathbf{0})
as
k\to\infty
.
On the other hand,
(x,p_{k}(x))\in\mathcal{R}
and

We now have

\lambda^{f_{n}+c_{n+1}+\cdots+c_{k}}=\lambda^{d(x,p_{k}(x))}=\varphi(x)\varphi(p_{k}(x))^{-1}.

The righthand side of this formula tends to

Hence there is a limit of the lefthand side.
Of course, this limit equals
\lambda^{f_{n}}\prod_{k>n}\lambda^{c_{k}}
,

It remains to prove that

Given

Therefore, if

Solution of Problem II

Let
\epsilon>0
.
We say that a finite subset

is an

\max_{z\in\mathbb{T}}\min_{p\in P}|z-p|<\epsilon.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given an element
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
of infinite order, there is an explicit
(C,F)
-construction of a rank-one transformation
T

with finite invariant measure and

.

Proof.

It is well known (and easy to verify) that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a number
j_{n}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\delta_{n}:=|1-\lambda^{j_{n}}|<\frac{2\pi}{n}

\frac{2\pi}{n}
-net.

We now construct inductively a sequence
(C_{n},F_{n-1})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of subsets in
\mathbb{Z}
.
We recall that
F_{0}=\{0\}
and
F_{n}=\{0,1,\dots,h_{n}-1\}
for some
h_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose we have already determined
(C_{k},F_{k})_{k=1}^{n-1}
for some
n>0
.
Suppose, in addition, that an auxiliary condition

(4.1)

h_{n-1}>\frac{n^{4}}{\delta_{n^{2}}}

is satisfied.
Our purpose is to define
C_{n}
and
F_{n}
(or, equivalently,
h_{n}
).
Since
\lambda^{h_{n-1}}\cdot P(\lambda,n^{2})
is a
\frac{2\pi}{n^{2}}
-net, there is a positive integer
k_{1}\leq\frac{2\pi}{\delta_{n^{2}}}
such that

|1-\lambda^{h_{n-1}+k_{1}j_{n^{2}}}|<\frac{2\pi}{n^{2}}.

We now set
a(1):=h_{n-1}+k_{1}j_{n^{2}}
.
In a similar way, there is a positive integer
k_{2}\leq\frac{2\pi}{\delta_{n^{2}}}
such that

|1-\lambda^{a(1)+h_{n-1}+k_{2}j_{n^{2}}}|<\frac{2\pi}{n^{2}}.

We now set
a(2):=a(1)+h_{n-1}+k_{2}j_{n^{2}}
.
Continuing this process infinitely many times we obtain an infinite sequence
a(1),a(2),\dots

such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle a(l)=a(l-1)+h_{n-1}+k_{l}j_{n^{2}}\ \text{ for some positive
$k_{l}\leq\frac{2\pi}{\delta_{n^{2}}}$ and}

(4.3)

\displaystyle|1-\lambda^{a(l)}|<\frac{2\pi}{n^{2}}

for each
l>0
.
Let
r_{n}
be the smallest
l
such that
a(r_{n}-1)+h_{n-1}>\frac{(n+1)^{4}}{\delta_{(n+1)^{2}}}
.
Then we set

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{0,a(1),a(2),\dots,a(r_{n}-1)\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle h_{n}

\displaystyle:=a(r_{n}-1)+h_{n-1}.

Thus, we defined
C_{n}
and
F_{n}
.
Moreover, (
4.1
) holds if we replace

with

.
Continuing this process infinitely many times, we

.
It is straightforward to check that (

-dynamical system

We now verify that

Indeed, applying (

Hence, Corollary

e(T)
is torsion free.
Therefore

T
is totally ergodic.

It remains to prove the final claim of Theorem C.
Let
\omega\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\}
be of infinite order.
Suppose first that there are
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\lambda^{n}=\omega^{m}
.
Then
\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}
are two measures on
\mathbb{T}
that are invariant under
the same transformation
R_{\lambda}^{n}=R_{\omega}^{m}
.
Moreover, this transformation is ergodic with respect to each of these two measures because the systems

(\mathbb{T},\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda},R_{\lambda})
and
(\mathbb{T},\mu^{\prime}_{\omega},R_{\omega})
are totally ergodic.
Hence either
\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}
or
\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\sim\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.
If the latter holds then

\{z\in\mathbb{T}\mid\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}\circ R_{z}\sim\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}=\{z\in\mathbb{T}\mid\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\circ R_{z}\sim\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\}
.
Hence
\{\lambda^{l}\mid l\in\mathbb{Z}\}=\{\omega^{l}\mid l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
This is only possible if either
\omega=\lambda
or
\omega=\lambda^{-1}
.
Thus, we obtain a contradiction.
Hence
\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}
, as claimed.

Suppose now that
\omega
and
\lambda
are independent and
\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\not\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.
Then there is a Borel subset
A\subset\mathbb{T}
such that

\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}(A)>0\text{ and }(\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}\restriction A)\prec\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}.

Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}\restriction A)\circ R_{\lambda}^{n}\prec\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\circ R_{\lambda}^{n}\text{ and }\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\circ R_{\lambda}^{n}\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}.

Hence
(\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}\restriction A)\circ R_{\lambda}^{n}\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}
, i.e.

(\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}\restriction R_{\lambda}^{-n}A)\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}
.
It follows that

\bigg(\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}\restriction\bigcup_{n>0}R_{\lambda}^{-n}A\bigg)\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}

Since
\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}
is nonatomic and ergodic with respect to
R_{\lambda}
, we obtain that

\bigcup_{n>0}R_{\lambda}^{-n}A=\mathbb{T}\,\left(\mbox{\rm mod~}\mu_{\lambda}^{\prime}\right)
.
Hence
\mu^{\prime}_{\omega}\perp\mu^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.
∎

A subset
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{T}
is called
independent

if whenever
\lambda_{1}^{k_{1}}\lambda_{2}^{k_{2}}\ldots\lambda_{n}^{k_{n}}=1

for some
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\Lambda
and

n\in\mathbb{N}

then
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{n}=0
.

Remark 5.1
.

Theorems B and C can be strengthened in the following way.

Adaptive Testing for Alphas in Conditional Factor Models with High Dimensional Assets
Huifang Ma

Abstract
This paper focuses on testing for the presence of alpha in time-varying factor pricing models, specifically when the number of securities N N is larger than the time dimension of the return series T T . We introduce a maximum-type test that performs well in scenarios where the alternative hypothesis is sparse. We establish the limit null distribution of the proposed maximum-type test statistic and demonstrate its asymptotic independence from the sum-type test statistics proposed by Ma et al. 2020 . Additionally, we propose an adaptive test by combining the maximum-type test and sum-type test, and we show its advantages under various alternative hypotheses through simulation studies and two real data applications.



Keywords:
Alpha tests, Conditional factor model, High dimensionality, Sparse alternatives, Spline estimator

Introduction

Tests of alpha have attracted much attention in the econometrics literature. In the traditional linear pricing factor models, e.g. CAPM
(
Sharpe 1964
;
Lintner 1965
)
and the Fama-French three-factor model (abbreviated as FF model hereafter)
(
Fama and
French 1993
;
Fama and
French 2015
)
,
Gibbons
et al. 1989
proposed an exact multivariate F-test under the joint normality assumption. Several extended methods include
MacKinlay and
Richardson 1991
,
Zhou 1993
and
Beaulieu
et al. 2007
, etc. However, their application has been confined to a relatively small number of portfolios. With the development of modern financial markets, thousands of securities are traded. So the assumption of fixed dimension is not appropriate. Consequently, recent efforts have focused on developing tests that allow the number of securities
N
are larger than the time periods
T
. For instance,
Pesaran and
Yamagata 2012
;
Pesaran and
Yamagata 2017
propose a sum-type test statistic by replacing the sample covariance matrix with identity matrix in traditional F-test, which performs well under the dense alternatives. For sparse alternatives,
Feng
et al. 2023
proposed a max-type test statistics.
Yu et al. 2023
employed the thresholding covariance estimator of
Fan
et al. 2013
and proposed a novel Wald-type test. They also proved the asymptotic independence between the new Wald-type test and the maximum-type test and proposed a new Fisher combination test which performs very well under neither dense nor sparse alternatives.

Although these tests have addressed the limitation of
T>N
, they still require the factor loadings be constant over time. This assumption can be quite restrictive in empirical finance. Much empirical evidence indicates that the factor loadings in CAPM and the FF model vary substantially over time even at the portfolio level
(
Lewellen and
Nagel 2006
;
Ang and
Chen 2007
)
. As a result, the aforementioned tests can lead to inaccurate conclusions when the factor loadings are time-varying. To remove the limiting of time-invariant factor loadings,
Li and
Yang 2011
and
Ang and
Kristensen 2012
proposed nonparametric Wald-type tests under the case that
N
is fixed and
T
goes to infinity, and
Gagliardini et al. 2016
developed an econometric methodology under high dimensional case. Furthermore,
Ma
et al. 2020
proposed a sum-type statistic based on the residuals obtained from the null model, which is asymptotically normal-distributed.

The above methods are all sum-of-squares types, generally having good power performance against dense alternatives. However, inefficient market pricing is more likely to occur in exceptional assets rather than systematic mispricing of the entire market, i.e.
\bm{\alpha}
has few nonzero elements with a large
||\bm{\alpha}||_{\infty}
. In this case, it is problematic to use the sum-of-squares type tests, since summarizing information through averaging will weaken the signals carried in the few securities with strong signals. Therefore, it is desirable to develop a test that has good power against sparse alternatives.

Recently, the maximum-type tests are wildly used in high dimensional testing problems.
Cai
et al. 2014
proposed three-types maximum-type tests for high dimensional two-sample location problems.
Chang
et al. 2017
consider testing for high-dimensional white noise using maximum cross-correlations.
In the traditional linear factor pricing models, many studies also show that the maximum-type test statistic performs very well under the sparse alternatives, such as
Gungor and
Luger 2013
;
Feng
et al. 2023
;
Yu et al. 2023
. So, we construct an maximum-type test statistic for the time-varying factor pricing models and establish its theoretical properties. Theoretical results and simulation studies show the proposed maximum-type test statistic also has good performance under sparse alternatives.

It’s worth notable that the underlying truth is usually unknown in real applications, as whether it is dense or sparse depends on the properties of the involved securities. Recently, many literatures showed that the maximum-type test statistic is asymptotically independent with the sum-type test statistic and proposed a corresponding combination test in many high dimensional problems, such as
He et al. 2021
,
Feng
et al. 2023
for high dimensional mean testing problems,
Feng
et al. 2022
for cross-sectional independence test in high dimensinal panel data models,
Yu et al. 2022
for testing of high dimensional covariance matrix,
Wang and Feng 2023
for high dimensional change point inference. In the time-varying factor pricing model, we also demonstrate the asymptotic independence between the maximum-type statistic and the sum-type statistic proposed in
Ma
et al. 2020
. Then, we construct an adaptive testing procedure by combining the information from these two statistics, which would yield high power against various alternatives. We demonstrate the advantages of the proposed tests over existing methods through extensive Monte Carlo experiments and two empirical applications.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the maximum-type statistic and establish its theoretical properties. In Section
3
, we integrate the proposed maximum-type test with an existing sum-type test to obtain an adaptive test. Monte Carlo experiment results are presented in Section

2
The Maximum-type Test

2.1
Econometric Framework and B-spline Approximation

We consider the following conditional time-varying factor model under high-dimensional case,

R_{it}=\alpha_{it}+\bm{\beta}_{it}^{\prime}\bm{f}_{t}+e_{it}=\alpha_{it}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\beta_{ijt}f_{jt}+e_{it},\quad i=1,...,N;t=1,...,T,

(1)

where
R_{it}
denotes the excess returns of the
i
-th asset at time
t
,
\alpha_{it}
is the conditional alpha of asset
i
at time
t
,
\bm{\beta}_{it}=(\beta_{i1t},...,\beta_{idt})^{\prime}
is a
d\times 1
vector of time-variant factor loadings,
\bm{f}_{t}=(f_{it},...,f_{dt})^{\prime}
stands for the risk premium on
d
-dimensional tradable systematic risks at time
t
, and
e_{it}
is the idiosyncratic error term with
\text{Cov}(\bm{e}_{t})=\bm{\Sigma}=(\sigma_{ij})_{N\times N}
, where
\bm{e}_{t}=(e_{1t},...,e_{Nt})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
.

To identify the parameters, we follow
Li and
Yang 2011
and others in imposing a smoothness condition. That is we assume that the sequence of alphas and betas are generated from two smoothing functions of time such that
\alpha_{it}=\alpha_{i}(t/T)
and
\beta_{ijt}=\beta_{ij}(t/T)
. Accordingly, we can rewrite equation (
1
) as

R_{it}=\delta_{i}^{0}+\delta_{i}(t/T)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\beta_{ij}(t/T)f_{jt}+e_{it},

(2)

where
\delta_{i}^{0}=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{it}
and
\delta_{i}(t/T)=\alpha_{i}(t/T)-T^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\alpha_{i}(t/T)
. To test whether the average pricing error for
N
assets across all time periods is equal to zero, we then focus on testing

H_{0}:\delta_{i}^{0}=0\text{ for all }i=1,...,N\quad\text{v.s.}\quad H_{1}:\delta_{i}^{0}\neq 0\text{ for some }i=1,...,N.

(3)

For testing hypothesis in equation (
3
), we employ the polynomial spline approach to estimating the unknown parameters
\delta_{i}(t/T)
and
\beta_{ij}(t/T)
under
H_{0}
. Consider
p
interior knots on
[0,1]
,
0=\ell_{0}<\ell_{1}<...<\ell_{p}<\ell_{p+1}=1
that satisfy

\max_{0\leq i\leq p}|\ell_{i+1}-\ell_{i}|/\min_{0\leq l\leq p}|\ell_{i+1}-\ell_{i}|\leq c

for some finite positive constant
c
, where
p=p(N,T)\to\infty
as
(N,T)\to\infty
. For any
t
, define its location as
l(t)
satisfying
\ell_{l(t)}\leq t/T<\ell_{l(t)+1}
. Consider the space of polynomial splines of order
q
and denote the normalized B spline basis of this space as
\bm{B}(t/T)=\{B_{1}(t/T),...,B_{L}(t/T)\}^{\prime}
,
where
L=p+q

(
de Boor 1978
)
. To estimate
\delta_{i}(\cdot)
, we consider the centered spline basis functions,
\widetilde{B}_{l}(t/T)=B_{l}(t/T)-T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}B_{l}(t/T)
, and denote
\widetilde{\bm{B}}(t/T)=\{\widetilde{B}_{1}(t/T),...,\widetilde{B}_{L}(t/T)\}^{\prime}
. Then, the unknown functions
\delta_{i}(\cdot)
and
\beta_{ij}(\cdot)
can be well approximated by the B-spline functions
(
Schumaker 2007
)
such that

\delta_{i}(t/T)\approx\bm{\lambda}_{i0}^{\prime}\widetilde{\bm{B}}(t/T)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{ij}(t/T)\approx\bm{\lambda}_{ij}^{\prime}\bm{B}(t/T),

where
\bm{\lambda}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times 1}
,
j=0,1,...,d
are the coefficients of the B-spline functions.

Denote
\bm{R}_{i.}=(R_{i1},...,R_{iT})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 1}
,
\bm{e}_{i.}=(e_{i1},...,e_{iT})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 1}
,
\bm{\lambda}_{i}=(\bm{\lambda}_{ij}^{\prime},0\leq j\leq d)^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{(1+d)L\times 1}
,
\bm{Z}_{t}=\{\widetilde{\bm{B}}(t/T)^{\prime},f_{jt}\bm{B}(t/T)^{\prime},1\leq j\leq d\}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{(1+d)L\times 1}
and
\bm{Z}=(\bm{Z}_{1},...,\bm{Z}_{T})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times(1+d)L}
. We then choose estimators
\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}=(\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{ij}^{\prime},0\leq j\leq d)^{\prime}
to minimize the following sum of squared residuals:

\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}=\arg\min_{\bm{\lambda}_{i}}||\bm{R}_{i.}-\bm{Z}\bm{\lambda}_{i}||^{2}=(\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{Z})^{-1}\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{R}_{i.}.

Consequently, the resulting residuals are

\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i.}=\bm{R}_{i.}-\bm{Z}\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}=\bm{M_{Z}R}_{i.}.

(4)

2.2
The Proposed Test

Under
H_{0}
, it can be shown that
\delta_{i}(t/T)-\widehat{\delta}_{i}(t/T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}0
and
\beta_{ij}(t/T)-\widehat{\beta}_{ij}(t/T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}0
. This motivates us to consider the max-type test, with the test statistic constructed as,

M_{NT}=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}t_{i}^{2},

(5)

where

t_{i}^{2}=T^{-1}\widehat{\sigma}_{ii}^{-1}(\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i.}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T})^{2},

(6)

with
\widehat{\sigma}_{ij}=\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i.}^{\prime}\widehat{\bm{e}}_{j.}/(T-d-1)
.

We will establish that when
(N,T)\to\infty
,
M_{NT}-2\log(N)+\log\{\log(N)\}
has a type I extreme value distribution. To proceed, we first introduce some assumptions.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{r}
denote the collection of all functions on
[0,1]
such that the
m
-th order derivative satisfies the Hölder condition of order
n
with
r=m+n
, i.e. there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for each

,

|f^{(m)}(x_{1})-f^{(m)}(x_{2})|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{n}

for any
0\leq x_{1},x_{2}\leq 1
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{NT,t}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated from
\{\bm{f},\{e_{it},e_{i,t-1},...\}_{i=1}^{N}\}
, where
\bm{f}=(\bm{f}_{1}^{\prime},...,\bm{f}_{T}^{\prime})^{\prime}
. Let
\bm{e}_{-t}=\{e_{i1},...,e_{i,t-1},e_{i,t+1},...,e_{iT}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Let
\bm{D}
denote the diagonal matrix of
\bm{\Sigma}
and
\bm{R}=(r_{ij})=\bm{D}^{-1/2}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{D}^{-1/2}
denote the correlation matrix.

Assumption 2.1
.

\delta_{i}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
and
\beta_{ij}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
for some
r>3/2
.

Assumption 2.2
.

(i) There exist constants

such that

c_{0}\leq\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{E}\{(1,\bm{f}_{t}^{\prime})^{\prime}(1,\bm{f}_{t}^{\prime})\})\leq\lambda_{\max}(\mathbb{E}\{(1,\bm{f}_{t}^{\prime})^{\prime}(1,\bm{f}_{t}^{\prime})\})\leq C_{0}

holds uniformly for

(ii) There exist a constant
0<K<\infty
such that
\mathbb{E}||\bm{f}_{t}||^{4(2+\kappa)}\leq K
for some
\kappa>0
;
(iii) The process

is strong mixing with mixing coefficient

satisfying
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha(k)^{\kappa/(2+\kappa)}<\infty

(iv)
\{\bm{f}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
and
\{\bm{e}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
are independent.

Assumption 2.3
.

(i)
\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}
are independently and identically distributed with for each

,
\mathbb{E}(e_{it}|\mathcal{F}_{NT,t-1})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{t}\bm{e}_{t}^{\prime}|\bm{e}_{-t})=\bm{\Sigma}
, where
\bm{\Sigma}
is positive definite and
\sigma_{ii}\in(0,\infty)
for every
1\leq i\leq N
;
(ii)

e_{it}
’s have sub-Gaussian-type tails, i.e. there exist
\eta>0
and
K>0
such that
\mathbb{E}\{\exp(\eta e_{it}^{2}/\sigma_{ii})\}\leq K
for
1\leq i\leq N
.

Assumption 2.4
.

(i)
TL^{-2r}=o(1)
;
(ii) There exists
c_{1}>0
such that
c_{1}^{-1}\leq\lambda_{\min}(\bm{R})\leq\lambda_{\max}(\bm{R})\leq c_{1}
;
(iii) There exists
k>0
such that
\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|r_{ij}|\leq k<1
.

Assumption
2.1
is the common smoothness assumption on the unknown functions
(
He and
Shi 1996
)
. Assumption
2.2
is the typical condition for the regression design matrix. Specially, Assumption
2.2
(i) follows Condition (C2) in
Wang
et al. 2008
, and Assumption
2.2
(iv) follows from Assumption 3.1(ii) in
Fan
et al. 2011
. Moreover, Assumptions
2.2
(ii)-(iii) weaken Assumption 3.2 and 3.3(ii) in
Fan
et al. 2011
. Assumption
2.3
is the moment conditions on the distribution of the error terms. Following
Pesaran and
Yamagata 2012
and others, we assume that
\bm{e}_{t}
’s are independently and identically distributed. Moreover, Assumption
2.3
(i) contains the assumption for a martingale difference sequence, and the homogeneity assumption on the covariance of the error terms. Assumption
2.3
(ii) is equivalent to Condition (C6) in
Cai
et al. 2014
, which allows the theoretical results to hold for error distributions more general than the Gaussian type. Assumption
2.4
(i) is the condition on the number of spline basis functions
L
. Assumption
2.4
(ii) is a common assumption on the eigenvalues in the high dimensional setting, which is equivalent to Assumption 4.1(i) in
Fan
et al. 2015
when
\sigma_{ii}
’s are bounded. Assumption
2.4
(iii) is also mild. For example, if
\max_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|r_{ij}|=1
, then
\bm{\Sigma}
is singular.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions

-
2.4
hold. Assuming

\mathbb{P}_{H_{0}}\left(M_{NT}-2\log(N)+\log\{\log(N)\}\leq x\right)\to F(x)\equiv\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{x}{2}\right)\right\}.

Here,

denotes the probability measure under the null hypothesis
H_{0}
. According to the limiting null distribution derived in, we can easily obtain the
p
-value associated with
M_{NT}
, namely,

p_{M}=1-F(M_{NT}-2\log(N)+\log\{\log(N)\}).

If the
p
-value is below
some pre-specified significant level, say
\gamma\in(0,1)
, then we rejected the null hypothesis that the traded factors are sufficient to price all assets. Next, we turn to analyze the power of the maximum-type testing procedure.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
-
2.4
hold. Assuming

and

, we have as

for some large enough constant

Proposition

shows that the proposed max-type test is effective in detecting sparse alternatives.

3
The Adaptive Test

3.1
The Existing sum-type Test

To detect dense alternatives, we consider the sum-type test statistic proposed by
Ma
et al. 2020
. To wit,

S_{NT}=N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i.}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T})^{2},

(7)

whose mean and variance under
H_{0}
are given below,

\mu_{NT}^{0}=N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(e_{it}^{2})\mathbb{E}(h_{t}^{2})\text{ and }\sigma_{NT}^{2}=2N^{-2}T^{-2}\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\sum_{1\leq t\neq s\leq T}\mathbb{E}(h_{t}^{2}h_{s}^{2}),

where
\bm{h}=(h_{1},...,h_{T})^{\prime}=M_{\bm{Z}}\bm{1}_{T}
. Let
\mu_{NT}=N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_{it}^{2}h_{t}^{2}
be an empirical approximation of the mean. For standardization, they considered the consistent estimator of
\mu_{NT}^{0}
as

\widehat{\mu}_{NT}=N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widehat{e}_{it}^{2}h_{t}^{2}.

(8)

As for
\sigma_{NT}^{2}
, following
Lan
et al. 2014
, they proposed the following estimator

\widehat{\sigma}_{NT}^{2}=2N^{-2}T^{-2}\widehat{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})}\sum_{1\leq t\neq s\leq T}h_{t}^{2}h_{s}^{2},

(9)

where
\widehat{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})}=T^{2}\{T+(1+d)L-1\}^{-1}\{T-(1+d)L\}^{-1}[\text{Tr}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}^{2})-\text{Tr}^{2}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})/\{T-(1+d)L\}]

\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{e}}_{t}-\overline{\bm{e}})(\widehat{\bm{e}}_{t}-\overline{\bm{e}})^{T}

The following Lemma restates the asymptotic null distribution of

derived in

et al. 2020
.

Assumption 3.1
.

(i)
TL^{-2r}N\text{Tr}^{-1/2}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})=o(1)
;
(ii)
\text{Tr}^{-1/2}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\max_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\sigma_{ij}|=o(1)
.

Assumption 3.2
.

(i)
tr(\bm{\Sigma}^{4})=o\{\text{Tr}^{2}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\}
as
N\to\infty
;
(ii)
T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{(}\bm{e}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{e}_{t})^{2}=o\{\text{Tr}^{2}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\}
;
(iii)
T^{-1+\varrho}N^{1+\varrho}L\text{Tr}^{-1/2}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})=O(1)
for an arbitratily small

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
-
2.3
and
3.1
-
3.2

and

. Then, under

, as

By Lemma

, the

3.2
Adaptive Strategy

In practice, we seldom know whether the vector of intercepts is sparse or dense. In order to adapt to various alternative behaviors, we combine the maximum- and sum-type testing procedures. The key message is that both test statistics are asymptotically independent under some mild conditions if
H_{0}
holds.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
-
2.4
and
3.1
-
3.2
hold. Assuming
L^{3}T^{-1}=o(1)
,
\log(N)=o(T^{1/4})
and
L^{r}T^{3/2}=o(1)
, we have as
(N,T)\to\infty
, under
H_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}_{H_{0}}\left(\frac{S_{NT}-\widehat{\mu}_{NT}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{NT}}\leq x,M_{NT}-2\log(N)+\log\{\log(N)\}\leq y\right)\to\Phi(x)F(y).

According to Theorem
3.1
, we suggest combining the corresponding
p
-values by using Fisher’s method
(
Littell and
Folks 1971
)
, to wit,

p_{adp}=1-G(-2\{\log(p_{M})+\log(p_{S})\}),

where
G(\cdot)
is the CDF of the chi-squared distribution with 4 degrees of freedom. The rationality is that
-2\{\log(p_{M})+\log(p_{S})\}
converges in distribution to
G
under
H_{0}
due to Theorem
3.1
. If the final
p
-value is less than some pre-specified significant level
\gamma\in(0,1)
, then we reject
H_{0}
.

Next, we analyze the power of the adaptive testing procedure. We consider the following sequence of alternative hypotheses, to wit,

H_{1,NT}:||\bm{\delta}^{0}||_{0}=o[N/\log^{2}\{\log(N)\}]\text{ and }||\bm{\delta}^{0}||=O\{T^{-1/2}\text{Tr}^{1/4}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\},

where
\bm{\delta}^{0}=(\delta_{1}^{0},...,\delta_{N}^{0})^{T}
with
\delta_{i}^{0}=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{it}
. In fact, the asymptotic independence between the maximum-type and sum-type statistics still hold under the hypotheses given in equation (
10
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Under the same condition as Theorem
3.1
, we have as
(N,T)\to\infty
, under
H_{1,NT}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{NT}-\widehat{\mu}_{NT}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{NT}}\leq x,M_{NT}-2\log(N)+\log\{\log(N)\}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\to\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{NT}-\widehat{\mu}_{NT}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{NT}}\leq x\right)\mathbb{P}\left(M_{NT}-2\log(N)+\log\{\log(N)\}\leq y\right).

Simulation studies show that the power of Fisher’s p-value combination-based test would be comparable to that of the test based on
\min\{p_{M},p_{S}\}
(referred to as the minimal p-value combination), say
\beta_{M\wedge S,\alpha}=P(\min\{{\rm p}_{M},{\rm p}_{S}\}\leq 1-\sqrt{1-\alpha})
. Obviously,

\displaystyle\beta_{M\wedge S,\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq P(\min\{{\rm p}_{M},{\rm p}_{S}\}\leq\alpha/2)

\displaystyle=\beta_{M,\alpha/2}+\beta_{S,\alpha/2}-P({\rm p}_{M}\leq\alpha/2,{\rm p}_{S}\leq\alpha/2)

\displaystyle\geq\max\{\beta_{M,\alpha/2},\beta_{S,\alpha/2}\}.

(11)

On the other hand, under
H_{1}
in (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\beta_{M\wedge S,\alpha}\geq\beta_{M,\alpha/2}+\beta_{S,\alpha/2}-\beta_{M,\alpha/2}\beta_{S,\alpha/2}+o(1),

(12)

due to the asymptotic independence entailed by Theorem
3.2
. For a small
\alpha
, the difference between
\beta_{M,\alpha}
and
\beta_{M,\alpha/2}
should be small, and the same fact applies to
\beta_{S,\alpha}
. Consequently, by (
3.2
)–(
12
), the power of the adaptive test would be no smaller than or even significantly larger than that of either max-type or sum-type test.

4.1
Experiment Settings

Example 1.
Following

Empirical Applications

In this section, we employ the proposed tests to analyze the stocks in Chinese and U.S. stock markets. To demonstrate

5.1
Data Description

First, we consider the stocks in Chinese stock market. We collected the daily returns of all the stocks in A-shares from 05/12/2021 to 03/09/2022, i.e.
T=200
. After eliminating the stocks with missing observations to avoid analyzing an unbalanced panel, we randomly selected
N=600
stocks in our final experiment. The time series data on the safe rate of return, and the market factors are calculated according to the corresponding formulas. The risk-free rate
r_{ft}
is
0.000041
(from China Asset Management Center). The value-weighted return on all the stocks of Shanghai Stock Exchange and Shenzhen Stock Exchange is used as a proxy for the market return
r_{mt}
. The average return on the three small portfolios minus the average return on the three big portfolios
\text{SMB}_{t}
, and the average return on two value portfolios minus the average return on two growth portfolios
\text{HML}_{t}
are calculated based on the stocks listed on Shanghai Stock Exchange and Shenzhen Stock Exchange. We use
r_{it}
to denote the return rate of security
i
on time
t
.

Next, we consider the stocks in the S&P 500 index, which is internationally accepted as a leading indicator of the U.S. equities. Similarly, we compiled returns on all the securities that constitute the S&P 500 index each week over the period from 01/08/2010 to 10/25/2013, i.e.
T=200
. Because the securities that make up the index change over time, we only consider
N=400
securities that were included in the S&P 500 index during the entire period. The time series data on the safe rate of return, and the market factors are obtained from Ken French’s data library web page. The one-month U.S. treasury bill rate is chosen as the risk-free rate

\text{HML}_{t}
are calculated based on the stocks listed on the NYSE, AMEX and NASDAQ.

5.2
Conditional Alpha Test

We consider the following rolling window procedure with window length
h=100
. For each
\tau\in\{1,...,T-h\}
, we separately estimate the conditional CAPM and FF model using the data from period
\tau
to
\tau+h-1
. As a result,

\displaystyle\text{CAPM: }R_{it}=r_{it}-r_{ft}=\widehat{\alpha}_{it}+\widehat{\beta}_{it}(r_{mt}-r_{ft})+\widehat{e}_{it},

\displaystyle\text{FF: }R_{it}=r_{it}-r_{ft}=\widehat{\alpha}_{it}+\widehat{\beta}_{i1t}(r_{mt}-r_{ft})+\widehat{\beta}_{i2t}\text{SMB}_{t}+\widehat{\beta}_{i3t}\text{HML}_{t}+\widehat{e}_{it},

for
1\leq t\leq\tau+h-1
. Based on the estimated residuals
\widehat{e}_{it}
obtained by separately fitting CAPM and the FF model to the data in each window, we calculate the Max, Sum and Adp test statistics and their corresponding
p
-values. Here, the number of interior knots
n
is determined via BIC and the order of B-splines is set at 3 for all estimation windows.

Before applying the involved tests, it is necessary to examine whether alphas and betas are time-varying, given the conclusion of
Li and
Yang 2011
that the conditional CAPM and FF are not always superior to their unconditional counterparts. We apply the constant coefficient test (henceforth the CC test) proposed by
Ma
et al. 2020
. The resulting
p
-values are presented in Figure
3
, which shows that most of the
p
-values of the CC test for 600 (400) stocks in the Chinese (U.S.) dataset is close to
0
, regardless of the model. This provides strong evidence that alphas and betas are indeed time-varying in both the Chinese and U.S. stock markets.

Now, we apply Max, Adp and Sum tests to the panel data of the securities in the Chinese and U.S. stock markets under the conditional CAPM and FF. Figure
4
depicts the
p
-values across the 100 windows. The Box-plot of these
p
-values are presented in Figures
5
. In Chinese stock market, we note from Figure
4
and Figures
5
that the
p
-values of Max and Adp are less than the 5% significance level for the conditional CAPM, which indicates that the markets are inefficient over these window periods. In contrast, the
p
-values obtained from Sum in the corresponding window periods are greater than 5%. For the conditional FF, the averaged
p
-values obtained from Max and Adp are smaller than those from Sum. In addition, most of the
p
-values from FF are larger than 5%, and they are also higher than those from CAPM. Accordingly, FF is better than CAPM in explaining the Chinese stock market. In the U.S. stock market, it is more prominent that the
p
-values are larger than 5% for both CAPM and FF. This suggests that the U.S. stock market is more efficient than the Chinese stock market.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose the Max test for alpha testing under high dimensional case, which aims to simultaneously alleviate the difficulties of the time-variation in the risk-exposure coefficients and the sparse alternatives. Considering the good performance of Sum test under dense alternative, we suggest an adaptive test, which integrates the power advantages of both Max and Sum and is applicable for various alternatives. Moreover, the usefulness of the proposed Max and Adp tests is illustrated by two empirical examples.

To further broaden the usefulness of our proposed tests, we conclude this article by identifying the following possible research avenues. First, if the goal is to identify the significance of alphas for all possible assets, then one can apply our Adp test in a multiple testing procedure to control the false discovery rate
(
Giglio
et al. 2020
)
. Second, the assumption of sub-Gaussian-type tails may be too restrictive for stock data.
Zhao 2023
proposed a robust high-dimensional alpha test based on spatial-sign for conditional time-varying factor models. How to construct an adaptive test for time-varying factor pricing models with heavy-tailed errors deserves some further studies. Finally, for sparse alternative, one may consider an L-statistic which combines the first several largest signals together, see more information in Remark 1 in
Chang
et al. 2022
.

\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}r_{ij}^{2}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}q_{li}^{2}\lambda_{l}^{2}\leq c_{3}^{2}
for some constant
c_{3}
due to Assumption
2.4
(ii). Thus, together with Assumption
2.4
(iii),
\bm{R}
satisfies the condition in Lemma
7.2
.

Define
\mathcal{I}=\{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{t}\leq N:\max_{1\leq k<l\leq t}|\text{Cov}(z_{i_{k}},z_{i_{l}})|\geq N^{-\gamma}\}
, where
\gamma>0
is a sufficiently small number to be specified later. For
2\leq d\leq t-1
, define
\mathcal{I}_{d}=\{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{t}\leq N:\text{Card}(S)=d,\text{ where }S\text{ is the largest subset of }\{i_{1},...,i_{t}\}\text{ such that }\forall i_{k},i_{t}\in S,i_{k}\neq i_{t},|\text{Cov}(z_{i_{k}},z_{i_{t}})|<N^{-\gamma}\}
. Here,
\text{Card}(S)
is the cardinality of
S
. For
d=1
, define
\mathcal{I}_{1}=\{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{t}\leq N:|\text{Cov}(z_{i_{k}},z_{i_{l}})|\geq N^{-\gamma},\forall 1\leq k<l\leq t\}
. Hence,
\mathcal{I}=\cap_{d=1}^{t-1}\mathcal{I}_{d}
. For a fixed subset
S
with
\text{Card}(S)=d
, the number of
i
such that
|\text{Cov}(z_{i},z_{j})|\geq N^{-\gamma}
for some
j\in S
is no more than
c_{4}dN^{2\gamma}
, where
c_{4}
is a constant. Indeed, if the number of such
i
had been larger than
c_{4}dN^{2\gamma}
, then

\sum\nolimits_{1\leq i\leq N}r_{ij}^{2}\geq\sum\nolimits_{\{i:|\text{Cov}(z_{i},z_{j})|\geq N^{-\gamma}\}}r_{ij}^{2}\geq N^{-2\gamma}c_{4}dN^{2\gamma}=c_{4}d>c_{3}^{2},

for
c_{4}>d^{-1}c_{3}^{2}
, which is a contradiction. Note that the total number of
S
with
\text{Card}(S)=d
is
\binom{N}{d}
. This leads to

\displaystyle\text{Card}(\mathcal{I}_{d})\leq\binom{N}{d}\binom{c_{4}dN^{2\gamma}}{t-d}\leq N^{d}(c_{4}d)^{t-d}N^{2\gamma(t-d)}\leq(c_{4}t)^{t}N^{d+2\gamma t},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\text{Card}(\mathcal{I})\leq\sum_{d=1}^{t-1}(c_{4}t)^{t}N^{d+2\gamma t}\leq(c_{4}t)^{t}tN^{2\gamma t+t-1}=O(N^{2\gamma t+t-1}).

Define
\mathcal{I}^{c}=\{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{t}\leq N\}\backslash\mathcal{I}
. Then

\text{Card}(\mathcal{I}^{c})=\binom{N}{t}-O(N^{2\gamma t+t-1})=\{1+o(1)\}\binom{N}{t}

as long as
\gamma<(2t)^{-1}
. Thus, by equation (20) in the proof of Lemma 6 in
Cai
et al. 2014
, we have

P_{i_{1},...,i_{t}}=\{1+o(1)\}\pi^{-t/2}N^{-t}\exp\left(-\frac{tx}{2}\right)

uniformly in
(i_{1},...,i_{t})\in\mathcal{I}^{c}
. Similarly, by equation (21) in the proof of Lemma 6 in
Cai
et al. 2014
, we have for
1\leq d\leq t-1

\sum_{(i_{1},...,i_{t})\in\mathcal{I}_{d}}P_{i_{1},...,i_{t}}\to 0.

Thus,

\displaystyle V_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{1},...,i_{t})\in\mathcal{I}^{c}}P_{i_{1},...,i_{t}}+\sum_{(i_{1},...,i_{t})\in\mathcal{I}}P_{i_{1},...,i_{t}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{1},...,i_{t})\in\mathcal{I}^{c}}P_{i_{1},...,i_{t}}+\sum_{d=1}^{t-1}\sum_{(i_{1},...,i_{t})\in\mathcal{I}_{d}}P_{i_{1},...,i_{t}}

\displaystyle=\{1+o(1)\}\pi^{-t/2}\frac{1}{t!}\exp\left(-\frac{tx}{2}\right).

Combining all facts together, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{2k}(-1)^{t-1}\pi^{-t/2}\frac{1}{t!}\exp\left(-\frac{tx}{2}\right)\{1+o(1)\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|\widehat{W}_{i}|\geq x_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{2k-1}(-1)^{t-1}\pi^{-t/2}\frac{1}{t!}\exp\left(-\frac{tx}{2}\right)\{1+o(1)\}.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we have (
A.3
).

Step 1.2.

Show that

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|W_{i}-\widehat{W}_{i}|\geq\frac{1}{\log(N)}\right)\to 0.

(A.4)

Notice that

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|W_{i}-\widehat{W}_{i}|\geq\frac{1}{\log(N)}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\max_{1\leq t\leq T}|V_{it}|\geq\tau_{NT}\right)\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}\sum_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{P}(|V_{it}|\geq\tau_{NT}).

For any
\zeta_{t}\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|V_{it}|\geq\tau_{NT})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(|e_{it}/\sigma_{ii}^{1/2}|\geq|h_{t}^{-1}|\tau_{NT})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|e_{it}/\sigma_{ii}^{1/2}|\geq|h_{t}^{-1}|\tau_{NT},|h_{t}^{-1}|\geq\zeta_{NT})+\mathbb{P}(|e_{it}/\sigma_{ii}^{1/2}|\geq|h_{t}^{-1}|\tau_{NT},|h_{t}^{-1}|<\zeta_{NT})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|e_{it}/\sigma_{ii}^{1/2}|\geq\tau_{NT}\zeta_{NT})+\mathbb{P}(|h_{t}|\geq\zeta_{NT}^{-1}).

Under Assumption
2.3
(iii), by the Markov inequality, we have

\mathbb{P}(|e_{it}/\sigma_{ii}^{1/2}|\geq\tau_{NT}\zeta_{NT})\leq K\exp(-\eta\zeta_{NT}^{2}\tau_{NT}^{2}).

Next, we consider
\mathbb{P}(|h_{t}|\geq\zeta_{NT}^{-1})
. By Bernstein’s inequality in
Bosq 1996
and the same proof for Lemma A.8 of
Ma and
Yang 2011
, under
L^{3}T^{-1}=o(1)
, we have

||\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T}/T-\mathbb{E}(\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T}/T)||_{\infty}=O_{\text{a.s.}}\{\log(T)/\sqrt{TL}\}.

Further, under Assumption
2.2
(ii), we have

\displaystyle||\mathbb{E}(\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T}/T)||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq l\leq L}T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}|B_{l}(t/T)|\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}|f_{jt}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq MT^{-1}\max_{1\leq l\leq L}\sum_{t\in\{t:|l(t)-l|\leq q-1\}}|B_{l}(t/T)|

\displaystyle\leq ML^{-1}

for some constant
0<M<\infty
, which leads to
||\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T}/T||_{\infty}=O_{\text{a.s.}}(L^{-1})
. By Lemma
7.4
and the result in
Demko 1986
, we have with probability one,

||(\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{Z}/T)^{-1}||_{\infty}\leq c_{5}L,

for some constant
0<c_{5}<\infty
, as
T\to\infty
. Then, using the fact that

\sum_{l=1}^{L}B_{l}(t/T)
is bounded, we have

\displaystyle|h_{t}|

\displaystyle=|1-\bm{Z}_{t}^{\prime}(\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{Z})^{-1}\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T}|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{(1+d)L}|Z_{tk}|\cdot||(\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{Z}/T)^{-1}||_{\infty}\cdot||\bm{Z}^{\prime}\bm{1}_{T}/T||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq dc_{6}(1+||\bm{f}_{t}||),

(A.5)

for some constant
0<c_{6}<\infty
, which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|h_{t}|\geq\zeta_{NT}^{-1})\leq\mathbb{P}\left(||\bm{f}_{t}||>d^{-1}c_{6}^{-1}\zeta_{NT}^{-1}-1\right)\leq M(d^{-1}c_{6}^{-1}\zeta_{NT}^{-1}-1)^{-4(2+\kappa)},

where the last inequality comes from Assumption
2.2
(ii) and the Markov inequality. Combining all facts above and setting
\zeta_{t}=o((NT)^{-1/\{4(2+\kappa)\}})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|W_{i}-\widehat{W}_{i}|\geq 1/\log(N)\right)\leq

\displaystyle NT\left\{M(d^{-1}c_{6}^{-1}\zeta_{NT}^{-1}-1)^{-4(2+\kappa)}+K\exp(-\eta\zeta_{NT}^{2}\tau_{NT}^{2})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle NT\left\{M(d^{-1}c_{6}^{-1}\zeta_{NT}^{-1}-1)^{-4(2+\kappa)}+K(N+T)^{-4}\right\}\to 0.

Hence, the proof of (
A.4
) is complete.

Step 1.3.
Show that

\left|\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\varphi_{i}\widehat{\sigma}_{ii}^{-1}-\max_{1\leq i\leq N}W_{i}^{2}\right|=o_{p}(1).

By (

) and letting

which, together with (

), leads to

\displaystyle\left|\max_{1\leq i\leq N}W_{i}^{2}-\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\widehat{W}_{i}^{2}\right|\leq 2\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|W_{i}|\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|W_{i}-\widehat{W}_{i}|+\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|W_{i}-\widehat{W}_{i}|^{2}=o_{p}(1),

and
\max_{1\leq i\leq N}W_{i}^{2}=O_{p}\{\log(N)\}
.
By Lemma E.2 and Proposition 3.1 in
Fan
et al. 2015
, for some
c>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|\sigma_{ii}-\widehat{\sigma}_{ii}|\geq c\sqrt{\frac{\log(N)}{T}}\right)\to 0\text{ and }\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{4}{9}\leq\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{ii}}{\sigma_{ii}}\leq\frac{9}{4},i=1,...,N\right)\to 1.

(A.7)

Combining the results above, with probability tending to one, we have

\displaystyle\left|\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\varphi_{i}\widehat{\sigma}_{ii}^{-1}-\max_{1\leq i\leq N}W_{i}^{2}\right|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq N}W_{i}^{2}\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\frac{\sigma_{ii}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{ii}}-1\right|=O_{p}\{\log^{3/2}(N)T^{-1/2}\}\to 0,

due to
\log(N)=o(T^{1/3})
. Then (
A.6
) follows, which together with (
A.3
) implies (
A.2
).

Step 2.

\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\widehat{\sigma}_{ii}^{-1}\zeta_{ik}=o_{p}(1),k=1,2.

2.4

7.3

Step 1.

\bm{e}_{t}\sim N(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma})

7.1

S_{NT}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\varphi_{i}+\zeta_{i1}+\zeta_{i2}+\zeta_{i3}+\zeta_{i4}+\zeta_{i5}).

According to the proof of Theorem 1 and 2 in
Ma
et al. 2020
, we have under

,

(S_{NT}-\mu_{NT})/\sigma_{NT}=\varphi_{NT}/\sigma_{NT}+o_{p}(1),

where

According to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have under
H_{0}
,

where
\varphi_{i}=T^{-1}(\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_{it}h_{t})^{2}
. Hence, by Lemma
7.5
, it suffices to show that
\varphi_{NT}/\sigma_{NT}
and
\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\varphi_{i}/\sigma_{ii}

For any fixed

, define

\displaystyle\zeta(N,d)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{d}\leq N}|\mathbb{P}(A_{N}B_{i_{1}}...B_{i_{d}})-\mathbb{P}(A_{N})\mathbb{P}(B_{i_{1}}...B_{i_{d}})|,

\displaystyle H(N,d)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{d}\leq N}|\mathbb{P}(B_{i_{1}}...B_{i_{d}})|.

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{N}A_{N}B_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq N}\mathbb{P}(A_{N}B_{i_{1}})-\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq N}\mathbb{P}(A_{N}B_{i_{1}}B_{i_{2}})+...+\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{2k}\leq N}\mathbb{P}(A_{N}B_{i_{1}}...B_{i_{2k}})

2.1
, we have for each

(C.1)

Then, by letting

\displaystyle\varphi_{NT}

\displaystyle=2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\left(\bm{U}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{U}_{s}+\bm{e}_{(1),t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{(1),s}+2\bm{V}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{U}_{s}+\bm{V}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{V}_{s}\right)h_{t}h_{s}

\displaystyle\Delta_{N,\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{P}\left(A_{N}(x)\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(A_{N}(x+2\epsilon)\right)\right|+\left|\mathbb{P}\left(A_{N}(x)\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(A_{N}(x-2\epsilon)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(A_{N}(x+2\epsilon)\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(A_{N}(x-2\epsilon)\right)

since
\mathbb{P}(A_{N}(x))
is increasing in
x
. By running over all possible combinations of
1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{d}\leq N
, we have

\zeta(N,d)\leq\Delta_{N,\epsilon}\cdot H(N,d)+2\binom{N}{d}\cdot N^{-c}.

Since
\mathbb{P}(A_{N}(x))\to\Phi(x)
, we have
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Delta_{N,\epsilon}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\{\Phi(x+2\epsilon)-\Phi(x-2\epsilon)\}=0
.
Since for each
d\geq 1
,
H(N,d)\to\pi^{-1/2}\exp(-dx/2)/d!
as
N\to\infty
, we have
\limsup_{N\to\infty}H(N,d)<\infty
. Due to the fact that
\binom{N}{d}\leq N^{d}
for fixed
d\geq 1
, first sending
N\to\infty
and then sending
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, we get (
C.1
).

It remain to prove that the claim (
C.2
) indeed holds.

By (B.7) in
Ma
et al. 2020
,
\exists
constants
0<c_{M}<C_{M}<\infty
s.t.

2c_{M}^{2}N^{-2}\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\leq\sigma_{NT}^{2}\leq 2C_{M}^{2}N^{-2}\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\{1+o(1)\}.

(C.3)

Define
\sigma_{d}^{2}=2c^{2}N^{-2}\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}_{11}^{2})
with
c_{M}\leq c\leq C_{M}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\Theta_{1}|\geq\epsilon\sigma_{NT})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bm{e}_{(1),t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{(1),s}h_{t}h_{s}\right|\geq\epsilon\sigma_{NT}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sigma_{d}^{-1}\cdot 2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bm{e}_{(1),t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{(1),s}h_{t}h_{s}\right|\geq\epsilon^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})}{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}_{11}^{2})}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\epsilon^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})}{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}_{11}^{2})}}\right\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left|\sigma_{d}^{-1}\cdot 2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bm{e}_{(1),t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{(1),s}h_{t}h_{s}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\epsilon^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})}{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}_{11}^{2})}}\right\}\cdot\log(T),

where the last inequality follows since

\sigma_{d}^{-1}\cdot 2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bm{e}_{(1),t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{(1),s}h_{t}h_{s}/\log\{\log(T)\}\to 0,\text{ a.s. }

by the law of the iterated logarithm of zero-mean square integrable martingale (see Theorem 4.8 in
Hall
et al. 2014
). Similarly,

\mathbb{P}(|\Theta_{2}|\geq\epsilon\sigma_{NT})\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})}{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}_{22\cdot 1}\bm{\Sigma}_{21}\bm{\Sigma}_{11}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{12})}}\right\}\cdot\log(T),

where
\bm{\Sigma}_{22\cdot 1}=\bm{\Sigma}_{22}-\bm{\Sigma}_{21}\bm{\Sigma}_{11}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{12}
, and

\mathbb{P}(|\Theta_{3}|\geq\epsilon\sigma_{NT})\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})}{\text{Tr}[(\bm{\Sigma}_{21}\bm{\Sigma}_{11}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{12})^{2}]}}\right\}\cdot\log(T).

It is then easy to see that (
C.2
) holds.

Step 2.

Investigate the asymptotic independence of
M_{NT}
and
(S_{NT}-\widehat{\mu}_{NT})/\widehat{\sigma}_{NT}
when
\bm{e}_{t}
’s are sub-Gaussian.

According to (
C.3
), define

W(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T})=\sigma_{s}^{-1}\varphi_{NT}=\sigma_{s}^{-1}\left(2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bm{e}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{s}h_{t}h_{s}\right),

where
\sigma_{s}^{2}=2c^{2}N^{-2}\text{Tr}(\bm{\Sigma}^{2})
with
c_{M}\leq c\leq C_{M}
. For
\bm{X}=(x_{1},...,x_{q})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, we consider a smooth approximation of the maximum function
\bm{X}\to\max_{1\leq i\leq q}x_{i}
, namely,

F_{\beta}(\bm{X})=\beta^{-1}\log\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{q}\exp(\beta x_{i})\right\},

where
\beta>0
is the smoothing parameter that controls the level of approximation. An elementary calculation shows that
\forall\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
,

0\leq F_{\beta}(\bm{X})-\max_{1\leq i\leq q}x_{i}\leq\beta^{-1}\log(q),

see
Chernozhukov et al. 2019
. W.L.O.G. assume that
\sigma_{ii}=1
for
i=1,...,N
. Define

V(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T})=F_{\beta}(\sqrt{\varphi_{1}},...,\sqrt{\varphi_{N}})=\beta^{-1}\log\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\exp\left(\beta T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_{it}h_{t}\right)\right\}.

By Lemma
7.5
and setting
\beta=T^{1/8}\log(N)
, it suffices to show that

\mathbb{P}\left(W(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T})\leq x,V(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T})\leq\sqrt{2\log(N)-\log\{\log(N)\}+y}\right)\to\Phi(x)F(y).

Suppose
\{\bm{z}_{1},...,\bm{z}_{T}\}
are i.i.d. from
N(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma})
, and are independent of
\{\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}\}
. According to the results of step 1, it remains to show that
(W(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}),V(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}))
has the same limiting distribution as
(W(\bm{z}_{1},...,\bm{z}_{T}),V(\bm{z}_{1},...,\bm{z}_{T}))
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{b}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
denote the class of bounded functions with bounded and continuous derivatives up to order 3. It is known that a sequence of random variables
\{Z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly to a random variable
Z
if and only if for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathbb{E}(f(Z_{n}))\to\mathbb{E}(f(Z))
, see, e.g.
Pollard 1984
. It suffices to show that

\mathbb{E}\{f(W(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}),V(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}))\}-\mathbb{E}\{f(W(\bm{z}_{1},...,\bm{z}_{T}),V(\bm{z}_{1},...,\bm{z}_{T}))\}\to 0,

for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
as
(N,T)\to\infty
. We introduce
W_{d}=W(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{d-1},\bm{z}_{d},...,\bm{z}_{T})
and
V_{d}=V(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{d-1},\bm{z}_{d},...,\bm{z}_{T})
for
d=1,...,T+1
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\{f(W(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}),V(\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{T}))\}-\mathbb{E}\{f(W(\bm{z}_{1},...,\bm{z}_{T}),V(\bm{z}_{1},...,\bm{z}_{T}))\}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{d=1}^{T}|\mathbb{E}\{f(W_{d},V_{d})\}-\mathbb{E}\{f(W_{d+1},V_{d+1})\}|.

Let

\displaystyle W_{d,0}=2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=2}^{d-1}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bm{e}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{s}h_{t}h_{s}+\sum_{t=d+2}^{T}\sum_{s=d+1}^{t-1}\bm{z}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{z}_{s}h_{t}h_{s}+\sum_{t=d+1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{d-1}\bm{z}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{s}h_{t}h_{s}\right),

\displaystyle V_{d,0}=\beta^{-1}\log\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\exp\left\{\beta T^{-1/2}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{d-1}e_{it}h_{t}+\sum_{t=d+1}^{T}z_{it}h_{t}\right)\right\}\right],

which only rely on
\mathcal{F}_{d}=\sigma\{\bm{e}_{1},...,\bm{e}_{d-1},\bm{z}_{d+1},...,\bm{z}_{T}\}
. By Taylor’s expansion, we have

\displaystyle f(W_{d},V_{d})-f(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})+f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f_{22}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+O(|W_{d}-W_{d,0}|^{3})+O(|V_{d}-V_{d,0}|^{3}),

and

\displaystyle f(W_{d+1},V_{d+1})-f(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})+f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f_{22}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})

\displaystyle+O(|W_{d+1}-W_{d,0}|^{3})+O(|V_{d+1}-V_{d,0}|^{3}),

where
f=f(x,y)
,
f_{1}=\partial f/\partial x
,
f_{2}=\partial f/\partial y
,
f_{11}=\partial^{2}f/\partial x^{2}
,
f_{22}=\partial^{2}f/\partial y^{2}
and
f_{12}=\partial^{2}f/\partial x\partial y
. Notice that

\displaystyle W_{d}-W_{d,0}=2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{d-1}\bm{z}_{d}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{s}h_{d}h_{s}+\sum_{t=d+1}^{T}\bm{z}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{z}_{d}h_{t}h_{d}\right),\text{ and}

\displaystyle W_{d+1}-W_{d,0}=2N^{-1}T^{-1}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{d-1}\bm{e}_{d}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{s}h_{d}h_{s}+\sum_{t=d+1}^{T}\bm{z}_{t}^{\prime}\bm{e}_{d}h_{t}h_{d}\right).

(C.4)

Due to
\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{t})=\mathbb{E}(\bm{z}_{t})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{t}\bm{e}_{t}^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}(\bm{z}_{t}\bm{z}_{t}^{\prime})
, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(W_{d}-W_{d,0}|\mathcal{F}_{d})=\mathbb{E}(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0}|\mathcal{F}_{d}),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{(W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{d}\}=\mathbb{E}\{(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{d}\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{f_{1}(W_{d},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})\}=\mathbb{E}\{f_{1}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})^{2}\}=\mathbb{E}\{f_{11}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})^{2}\}.

Next consider
V_{d}-V_{d,0}
. Let
v_{d,0,i}=T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{d-1}e_{it}h_{t}+T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=d+1}^{T}z_{it}h_{t}
,
v_{d,i}=v_{d,0,i}+T^{-1/2}z_{id}h_{d}
,
v_{d+1,i}=v_{d,0,i}+T^{-1/2}e_{id}h_{d}
,
\bm{v}_{d,0}=(v_{d,0,1},...,v_{d,0,N})^{\prime}
and
\bm{v}_{d}=(v_{d,1},...,v_{d,N})^{\prime}
. By Taylor’s expansion, we have

\displaystyle V_{d}-V_{d,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{i}F_{\beta}(\bm{v}_{d,0})(v_{d,i}-v_{d,0,i})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}F_{\beta}(\bm{v}_{d,0})(v_{d,i}-v_{d,0,i})(v_{d,j}-v_{d,0,j})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}F_{\beta}(\bm{v}_{d,0}+\delta(v_{d}-v_{d,0}))(v_{d,i}-v_{d,0,i})(v_{d,j}-v_{d,0,j})(v_{d,k}-v_{d,0,k}),

(C.5)

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
. Again, due to
\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{t})=\mathbb{E}(\bm{z}_{t})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{t}\bm{e}_{t}^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}(\bm{z}_{t}\bm{z}_{t}^{\prime})
, it can be verified that

\mathbb{E}(v_{d,i}-v_{d,0,i}|\mathcal{F}_{d})=\mathbb{E}(v_{d+1,i}-v_{d,0,i}|\mathcal{F}_{d}),\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\{(v_{d,i}-v_{d,0,i})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{d}\}=\mathbb{E}\{(v_{d+1,i}-v_{d,0,i})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{d}\}.

By Lemma A.2 in
Chernozhukov et al. 2019
, we have

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}F_{\beta}(\bm{v}_{d,0}+\delta(\bm{v}_{d}-\bm{v}_{d,0}))\right|\leq C\beta^{2}

for some positive constant
C
. By Assumption
2.3
(ii), we have
\mathbb{P}(\max_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq t\leq T}|e_{it}|>C\log(NT))\to 0
, and since
z_{it}\sim N(0,1)
,
\mathbb{P}(\max_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq t\leq T}|z_{it}|>C\log(NT))\to 0
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}F_{\beta}(\bm{v}_{d,0}+\delta(\bm{v}_{d}-\bm{v}_{d,0}))(v_{d,i}-v_{d,0,i})(v_{d,j}-v_{d,0,j})(v_{d,k}-v_{d,0,k})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\beta^{2}T^{-3/2}\log^{3}(NT)

holds with probability approaching one. Consequently, we have with probability approaching one,

\left|\mathbb{E}\{f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})\}-\mathbb{E}\{f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})\}\right|\leq C\beta^{2}T^{-3/2}\log^{3}(NT).

Similarly, it can be verified that

\left|\mathbb{E}\{f_{22}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})^{2}\}-\mathbb{E}\{f_{2}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})^{2}\}\right|\leq C\beta^{2}T^{-3/2}\log^{3}(NT),

and

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\{f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d}-W_{d,0})(V_{d}-V_{d,0})\}-\mathbb{E}\{f_{12}(W_{d,0},V_{d,0})(W_{d+1}-W_{d,0})(V_{d+1}-V_{d,0})\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\beta^{2}T^{-3/2}\log^{3}(NT).

Again, Lemma A.2 in
Chernozhukov et al. 2019
, together with (
7.3
), implies that
\mathbb{E}(|V_{d}-V_{d,0}|^{3})=O(T^{-3/2}\log^{3}(NT))
. According to (
7.3
) and the proof of Lemma A.5 in
Ma
et al. 2020
, we have
\mathbb{E}(|W_{d}-W_{d,0}|^{4})=O(T^{-2})
, thus

\sum_{d=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(|W_{d}-W_{d,0}|^{3})\leq\sum_{d=1}^{T}\{\mathbb{E}(|W_{d}-W_{d,0}|^{4})\}^{3/4}=O(T^{-1/2}).

Combining all facts together, we conclude that

\sum_{d=1}^{T}|\mathbb{E}\{f(W_{d},V_{d})\}-\mathbb{E}\{f(W_{d+1},V_{d+1})\}|=O(\beta^{2}T^{-3/2}\log^{3}(NT))+O(T^{-1/2})\to 0,

as
(N,T)\to\infty
, due to
\log(N)=o(T^{1/4})
. Then the proof of Theorem
3.1
is complete.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
(Z_{1},...,Z_{N})^{\prime}

\bm{\Sigma}=(\sigma_{ij})_{N\times N}

\sigma_{ii}=1

1\leq i\leq N

\max\nolimits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|\sigma_{ij}|\leq r<1

\max\nolimits_{1\leq j\leq N}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{2}\leq c
for some

x\in\mathbb{R}
as

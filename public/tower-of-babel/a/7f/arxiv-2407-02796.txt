Hawking radiation in quantum Hall system with an expanding edge: application of anomaly method
Riku Yoshimoto, Yasusada Nambu

Abstract
The relationship between gravitational anomalies and Hawking radiation of black holes was revealed by Wilczek and Robinson. In this study, we apply their method to an analogue de Sitter spacetime in the quantum Hall system with an expanding edge. Because this system is chiral, there is no need to impose the condition of ingoing modes near the horizon, which was assumed in the original method. Moreover, this system is structured so that the de Sitter space is sandwiched between two flat spaces, and although the effects of the anomaly would not appear in an ordinal de Sitter spacetime, they manifest themselves as boundary conditions between the de Sitter and the flat regions. By performing calculations under these boundary conditions, we obtain the flux of Hawking radiation in the outer flat region with the Gibbons-Hawking temperature of the de Sitter horizon.



I
Introduction

Hawking radiation has been studied extensively as a quantum property of black holes. Because of its low temperature, observing it from actual black holes in the universe is extremely challenging. However, in condensed matter systems, it is possible to construct analog models with properties such as black hole horizons and cosmological horizons. Among them, the analog de Sitter spacetime with quantum Hall (QH) systems is discussed in
Hotta et al. 2022
;
Nambu and Hotta 2023
. The usual experiments on QH systems are performed in a static situation where the edge is not time-varying, but by making it time-varying, the excitations moving along the expanding edge can be a simulator of the chiral scalar field in a (1+1)-dimensional curved spacetime. Also, in
Hotta et al. 2022
;
Nambu and Hotta 2023
, the expanding edge can reproduce a two-dimensional de Sitter universe which has the spacetime structure similar to that of a black hole formation via gravitational collapse. It was shown that the entanglement between two spatial regions decreases due to Hawking radiation from the de Sitter region, and when the detection region is sufficiently large compared to the Hubble length of the de Sitter region, the two regions become separable, and only classical correlations remain as well as the inflationary universe.

In this paper, we focus on the chiral properties of edge excitations rather than on quantum correlations in the QH system. This implies the presence of chiral scalar fields in the analog de Sitter spacetime. In such a case, it is known that there is a gravitational anomaly
Alvarez-Gaume and Witten 1984
;
Bertlmann 1996
;
Bertlmann and Kohlprath 2001
. Hawking radiation has been studied using various approaches beyond Hawking’s original calculation
Hawking 1975
. These include approaches using Euclidean quantum gravity by Hawking and Gibbons
Gibbons and Hawking 1977
, calculations using the trace anomaly by Christensen and Fulling
Christensen and Fulling 1977
;
Birrell and Davies 1984
, and calculations based on quantum tunneling
Parikh and Wilczek 2000
. Robinson and Wilczek provided a new calculation of Hawking radiation using gravitational anomaly
Robinson and Wilczek 2005
. Classical ingoing modes do not affect the dynamics of the external region near the black hole horizon. Thus, when they are ignored, the theory becomes chiral and a gravitational anomaly appears. Since the original theory is general covariant, this anomaly needs to be canceled by quantum effects, leading to the interpretation of obtaining Hawking radiation. This method has been applied not only to Schwarzschild black holes, but also to Kerr black holes, Reissner-Nordström black holes, and others
Iso et al. 2006a
;
Iso et al. 2006b
;
Murata and Soda 2006
;
Jiang et al. 2007
.

However, it is known that this method provides incorrect results for the Unruh effect and cosmological particle creation in the de Sitter spacetime
Akhmedova et al. 2010
;
Zampeli 2013
. In this study, we evaluated the flux of Hawking radiation from the de Sitter horizon in the analog de Sitter spacetime using the anomaly method. In the usual de Sitter spacetime, covariant gravitational anomaly method fails because the anomaly term becomes zero in de Sitter spacetime; because the spacetime curvature is constant and provides no terms to cancel out the anomaly. However, in the present QH system, the anomaly term does not become zero at the boundary. Therefore, the boundary conditions between the Minkowski and de Sitter regions are determined by the anomaly equation, and we can obtain the flux with Gibbons-Hawking temperature.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Section II, we review the coordinate transformations of each region in the expanding edge of QH systems introduced in
Nambu and Hotta 2023
;
Hotta et al. 2022
and organize the coordinates to be used in later sections. In Section III, we calculate the expectation value of the energy-momentum tensor (EMT) in the analog de Sitter spacetime introduced in Section II based on the anomalous conservation law and the boundary conditions obtained from it. Section IV is devoted to summary and conclusions.

II
Quantum Hall system with expanding edge

In this section, we review the coordinate setup for the expanding edge of the QH system
Nambu and Hotta 2023
;
Hotta et al. 2022
. We consider the edge of the QH system that has left-moving edge excitation. Then we divide the entire edge into three regions as shown in Fig.
1
; Region I (
x>L/2
) and region III (
x<-L/2
) are static, while region II (
-L/2<x<L/2
) expands with time.

Figure 1:
Schematic picture of an expanding edge in QH systems. Regions I and III are static and region II is the expanding region.

The analog metric of this system is written as

\displaystyle\text{Region {{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}:}~x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}\leq-L/2,\quad ds^{2}=-dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}\,dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-},

\displaystyle\text{Region {{I\hskip-1.2ptI}}:}~|x|\leq L/2,\quad ds^{2}=-e^{2\Theta(t)}dx^{+}dx^{-},

\displaystyle\text{Region I:}~L/2\leq x_{\text{I}},\quad ds^{2}=-dx_{\text{I}}^{+}dx_{\text{I}}^{-},

where
e^{\Theta(t)}
is the scale factor in region II and
x^{\pm}_{\text{I,{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}=t_{\text{I,{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}\pm x_{\text{I,{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}},~x^{\pm}=t\pm x
are null coordinates in each region.

The conformal flat coordinates in region II become discontinuous at the boundary
x=\pm L/2
, so it is not possible to smoothly extend them to regions I and III. However, it is possible to introduce smooth coordinates
x_{\rm{I}}
(
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}
) that cover both regions I and II (or II and III), while keeping
x_{\rm{I}}=L/2
(
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}=-L/2
).

II.1
Region II and III

First, we perform the coordinate transformation between the regions II and III and express the metric in the region II with
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{\pm}
. The matching condition at the boundary
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}=-L/2
is

x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}\left[t-L/2\right]-x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}\left[t+L/2\right]=-L.

(1)

By taking a derivative, we obtain

\frac{dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}}{dx^{+}}\left[t-L/2\right]=\frac{dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}}{dx^{-}}\left[t+L/2\right].

(2)

Since the conformal factor is unity at
x=x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}=-L/2
, we obtain

\frac{dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}}{dx^{+}}\left[t-L/2\right]=e^{\Theta(t)}.

(3)

By shifting the argument of this function,

\frac{dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}}{dx^{+}}\left[x^{+}\right]=e^{\Theta(x^{+}+L/2)},

(4)

and one finds

\displaystyle x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}\left[x^{+}\right]

\displaystyle=\int^{x^{+}}_{0}dy\,e^{\Theta(y+L/2)}=\int^{x^{+}+L/2}_{L/2}dy\,e^{\Theta(y)}

\displaystyle=\Phi\left[x^{+}+L/2\right]-\Phi\left[L/2\right],

(5)

where the function
\Phi\left[x\right]
is introduced by

\Phi\left[x\right]=\int^{x}_{0}dy\,e^{\Theta(y)}.

(6)

The coordinate
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}
is determined by the matching condition:

\displaystyle x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}\left[x^{-}\right]

\displaystyle=x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}\left[x^{-}-L\right]+L

\displaystyle=\int^{x^{-}-L}_{L/2}dy\,e^{\Theta(y+L/2)}+L

\displaystyle=\Phi\left[x^{-}-L/2\right]-\Phi\left[L/2\right]+L.

(7)

From these equations, the metric in region II can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI}}}

\displaystyle=-e^{2\Theta(t)}dx^{+}dx^{-}

\displaystyle=-\exp\left[2\Theta(t)-\Theta(x^{+}+L/2)-\Theta(x^{-}-L/2)\right]dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}.

(8)

At the boundary,
x^{+}=t-L/2
and
x^{-}=t+L/2
, so the conformal factor becomes unity. Thus, the coordinates
x^{\pm}
in region II can be extended to region III.

II.2
Region I and II

Let us consider the matching conditions at
x=x_{\rm{I}}=L/2
:

x_{\rm{I}}^{+}\left[t+L/2\right]-x_{\rm{I}}^{-}\left[t-L/2\right]=L.

(9)

By similar calculation as matching regions II and III, we obtain

x_{\rm{I}}^{+}\left[x^{+}\right]=\int^{x^{+}}_{0}dy\,e^{\Theta(y-L/2)}=\Phi\left[x^{+}-L/2\right]-\Phi\left[-L/2\right],

(10)

and

\displaystyle x_{\rm{I}}^{-}\left[x^{-}\right]

\displaystyle=x_{\rm{I}}^{+}\left[x^{-}+L\right]-L

\displaystyle=\int^{x^{-}+L}_{0}dy\,e^{\Theta(y-L/2)}-L

\displaystyle=\Phi\left[x^{-}+L/2\right]-\Phi\left[-L/2\right]-L.

(11)

The metric in region II becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI}}}

\displaystyle=-e^{2\Theta(t)}dx^{+}dx^{-}

\displaystyle=-\exp\left[2\Theta(t)-\Theta(x^{+}-L/2)-\Theta(x^{-}+L/2)\right]dx_{\rm{I}}^{+}dx_{\rm{I}}^{-}.

(12)

At the boundary,
x^{+}=t+L/2
and
x^{-}=t-L/2
, so the conformal factor becomes unity, and the coordinates
x^{\pm}
can be extended into region I.

II.3
Region I and III

Using the function
\Phi\left[x\right]
introduced above, we can express the relationship between regions I and III. Coordinates
x_{\rm{I}}^{\pm}
and
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{\pm}
are written as

\displaystyle x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}=

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{+}+L/2)-\Phi(L/2),

(13)

\displaystyle x_{\rm{I}}^{+}=

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{+}-L/2)-\Phi(-L/2),

(14)

\displaystyle x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}=

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{-}-L/2)-\Phi(L/2)+L,

(15)

\displaystyle x_{\rm{I}}^{+}=

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{-}+L/2)-\Phi(-L/2)-L.

(16)

After eliminating
x^{\pm}
, we obtain relations between
x_{\text{I}}^{\pm}
and
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{\pm}
as

\displaystyle x_{\rm{I}}^{+}=\Phi\left[-L+\Phi^{-1}\left[x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}+\Phi\left[L/2\right]\right]\right]-\Phi\left[-L/2\right],

(17)

\displaystyle x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}=\Phi\left[-L+\Phi^{-1}\left[x_{\rm{I}}^{-}+L+\Phi\left[-L/2\right]\right]\right]-\Phi\left[L/2\right]+L.

(18)

II.4
Example of analog spacetime : de Sitter case

For later usage in the analysis of the de Sitter spacetime,
we calculate the specific form of
\Phi\left[x\right]
and
\Phi^{-1}\left[x\right]
(see Fig.
2
for this setup). The conformal factor of the de Sitter spacetime is

e^{\Theta(t)}=\frac{1}{\cos(Ht)}.

(19)

Penrose diagram for the QH system with a de Sitter edge region II. A future horizon and a past horizon exist (time-symmetric eternal case). The vacuum condition is imposed on the past null infinity
\mathcal{I}^{-}
in region I.

Substituting this into the definition of
\Phi\left[x\right]
(

), we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{2H}\ln\frac{1+\sin Hx}{1-\sin Hx},

(20)

\displaystyle\Phi^{-1}(x)=\frac{1}{H}\arcsin\tanh Hx.

(21)

Then the metric in region II is written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI}}}=-\frac{dx_{\rm{I}}^{+}dx_{\rm{I}}^{-}}{\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\rm{I}}-L/2)\right]},

(22)

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI}}}=-\frac{dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}\,dx_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{-}}{\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)\right]}.

(23)

In the following, we will refer to region II covered by
x_{\rm{I}}
as A and region covered by
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}
as B (Fig.

).

Region A in the de Sitter region II is covered by

and region B in the de Sitter region II is covered by

.

III
Application of Anomaly method

This QH system reproduces a left-moving chiral field in de Sitter spacetime. It is known that in such a situation, not only a trace anomaly but also a gravitational anomaly exists. In
(1+1)
-dimensional spacetimes, they are represented by the following expression which indicates a violation of the conservation law for the expectation value of EMT
Alvarez-Gaume and Witten 1984
;
Bertlmann 1996
;
Bertlmann and Kohlprath 2001
:

\displaystyle T^{\alpha}_{\alpha}=\frac{R}{48\pi},

(24)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}_{\nu}=\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\mu}R}{96\pi\sqrt{-g}},

(25)

where
R
denotes the spacetime scalar curvature and
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}
is the totally antisymmetric tensor in two dimensions.
By solving the anomaly equation (
25
), we can observe the emergence of Hawking radiation in region
III
.

III.1
General solution of anomaly equation

First, we determine the general solution of the EMT in each region. The scalar curvature of this system is given by

R=2H^{2}\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)\theta(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)

(26)

where the unit step function defined by

\theta(x)=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{for}\quad x<0,\\
1&\quad\text{for}\quad x\geq 0.\end{cases}

(27)

The EMT is conserved in bulk regions beside boundaries. Thus in regions I and III (Minkowski region), we solve

{T_{i}}_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=0,\quad\partial_{\mu}{T_{i}}^{\mu}_{\nu}=0,\quad(i=\text{I, {I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}).

(28)

We want to consider the general solution of Eq. (
28
). By using
T^{+}_{+}=T^{-}_{-}=1/2(T^{t}_{t}+T^{x}_{x})=1/2\,T^{\alpha}_{\alpha}
, Eq. (
28
) becomes

\partial_{-}{T_{i}}^{-}_{+}=0,\quad\partial_{+}{T_{i}}^{+}_{-}=0.

(29)

Thus, we obtain

{T_{i}}^{-}_{+}=-2{T_{i}}_{++}=F_{i}\left[x^{+}_{i}\right],\quad{T_{i}}^{+}_{-}=-2{T_{i}}_{--}=G_{i}\left[x^{-}_{i}\right],

(30)

where
F_{i}\left[x^{+}_{i}\right]
is an arbitrary function of
x^{+}_{i}
and
G_{i}\left[x^{-}_{i}\right]
is an arbitrary function of
x^{-}_{i}
.

In regions A and B (de Sitter region), the EMT obeys

{T_{j}}_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=\frac{R}{48\pi},\quad\nabla_{\mu}{T_{j}}^{\mu}_{\nu}=\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\mu}R}{96\pi\sqrt{-g_{j}}},\quad(j=A,B).

(31)

The non-zero Christoffel symbols are
{\Gamma_{j}}^{+}_{++}=h_{j}\partial_{+}(1/h_{j})
and
{\Gamma_{j}}^{-}_{--}=h_{j}\partial_{-}(1/h_{j})
with
h_{A}:=\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\rm{I}}-L/2)\right]
and
h_{B}:=\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)\right]
. Therefore, we obtain the following explicit form of the anomaly equation:

\begin{split}\partial_{-}{T_{A}}^{-}_{+}+{\Gamma_{A}}^{-}_{--}{T_{A}}^{-}_{+}&=0,\\
\partial_{+}{T_{A}}^{+}_{-}+{\Gamma_{A}}^{+}_{++}{T_{A}}^{+}_{-}&=-\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi}\delta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2).\end{split}

(32)

\begin{split}\partial_{-}{T_{B}}^{-}_{+}+{\Gamma_{B}}^{-}_{--}{T_{B}}^{-}_{+}&=0,\\
\partial_{+}{T_{B}}^{+}_{-}+{\Gamma_{B}}^{+}_{++}{T_{B}}^{+}_{-}&=\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi}\delta(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}-L/2).\end{split}

(33)

The solution of these equations are given by

\displaystyle{T_{A}}^{-}_{+}

\displaystyle=-2\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\rm{I}}-L/2)\right]{T_{A}}_{++}

\displaystyle=f_{A}\left[x^{+}_{\rm{I}}\right]\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\rm{I}}-L/2)\right],

(34)

\displaystyle{T_{A}}^{+}_{-}

\displaystyle=-2\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\rm{I}}-L/2)\right]{T_{A}}_{--}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{H^{2}}{24\pi}\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)+a\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times g_{A}\left[x^{-}_{\rm{I}}\right]\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\rm{I}}-L/2)\right],

(35)

\displaystyle{T_{B}}^{-}_{+}

\displaystyle=-2\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)\right]{T_{B}}_{++}

\displaystyle=f_{B}\left[x^{+}_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}\right]\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)\right],

(36)

\displaystyle{T_{B}}^{+}_{-}

\displaystyle=-2\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)\right]{T_{B}}_{--}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{H^{2}}{24\pi}\theta(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)+b\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times g_{B}\left[x^{-}_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}\right]\cosh^{2}\left[H(x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}+L/2)\right],

(37)

where
a,b
are integration constants, and
f_{j}\left[x^{+}_{j}\right]
are arbitrary functions of
x^{+}_{j}
,
g_{j}\left[x^{-}_{j}\right]
are arbitrary functions of
x^{-}_{j}
.

In the later section, we will calculate
{T_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}}_{++}
as the left-moving Hawking radiation. As this quantity depends only on the coordinate
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}
, it is determined by the initial condition in region I and the boundary conditions at
x=\pm L/2
, which are provided by the anomaly equation.

III.2
Boundary conditions

We write the total EMT in region I
\,\cup\,
A as

{T_{\rm{Itot}}}^{\mu}_{\nu}={T_{\text{I}}}^{\mu}_{\nu}\left[1-\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)\right]+{T_{\rm{A}}}^{\mu}_{\nu}\,\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2).

(38)

Under the diffeomorphism transformation, the change of effective action
W
is

-\delta_{\lambda}W=\int d^{2}x\sqrt{-g}\,\lambda^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}{T_{\rm{Itot}}}^{\mu}_{\nu},

(39)

where
\lambda^{\nu}
are arbitrary variational parameters. To restore the diffeomorphism covariance, each coefficients of
\lambda^{\nu}
have to vanish. Each coefficient is divergence of the EMT and calculated as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}{T_{\rm{Itot}}}^{\mu}_{+}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}{T_{\text{I}}}^{\mu}_{+}\left[1-\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{\mu}{T_{\rm{A}}}^{\mu}_{+}\,\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)

\displaystyle\quad+({T_{\rm{A}}}^{\mu}_{+}-{T_{\text{I}}}^{\mu}_{+})\partial_{\mu}\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi}+\frac{1}{2}{T_{\rm{A}}}^{-}_{+}-\frac{1}{2}{T_{\rm{I}}}^{-}_{+}\right)\delta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2),

(40)

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}{T_{\rm{Itot}}}^{\mu}_{-}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}{T_{\text{I}}}^{\mu}_{-}\left[1-\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{\mu}{T_{\rm{A}}}^{\mu}_{-}\,\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)

\displaystyle\quad+({T_{\rm{A}}}^{\mu}_{-}-{T_{\text{I}}}^{\mu}_{-})\partial_{\mu}\theta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{2}{T_{\rm{A}}}^{+}_{-}+\frac{1}{2}{T_{\rm{I}}}^{+}_{-}\right)\delta(-x_{\rm{I}}+L/2).

(41)

To make the total EMT anomaly free, the coefficient of the delta function must vanish and boundary conditions for
T_{++}
,
T_{--}
at
x=L/2
become

\begin{split}{T_{\rm{A}}}_{++}-{T_{\rm{I}}}_{++}&=-\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi},\\
{T_{\rm{A}}}_{--}-{T_{\rm{I}}}_{--}&=0,\end{split}

(42)

By performing the same calculation in region III
\,\cup\,
B, we obtain following condition at
x=-L/2
:

\begin{split}{T_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}}_{++}-{T_{B}}_{++}&=\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi},\\
{T_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}}_{--}-{T_{B}}_{--}&=0.\end{split}

(43)

III.3
Calculation of Hawking radiation

From the general solution of the EMT obtained above and the boundary conditions, we can calculate the flux in region III. Since (
30
), (
35

37

T_{++}
in each region does not depend on

{T_{\rm{I}}}_{++}=-\frac{1}{2}F_{\rm{I}}\left[x^{+}_{\rm{I}}\right]=0.

With the EMT in region I, using the matching condition (

{T_{\rm{A}}}_{++}={T_{\rm{I}}}_{++}-\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi}=-\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi}.

(45)

This boundary condition is same as to fix the arbitary function
f_{A}[x_{\text{I}}^{+}]
in Eq. (
34

{T_{\rm{A}}}_{++}
and
{T_{\rm{B}}}_{++}

In the overlaped region A
\cap
B, they are related by the coordinate transformation

T_{B++}=\left(\frac{\partial x_{\rm{I}}^{+}}{\partial x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}}\right)^{2}T_{A++}.

Using the formula (
17

\displaystyle\frac{\partial x_{\rm{I}}^{+}}{\partial x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}^{+}}=

\displaystyle\csc{\left[HL+\arccos{\left[\frac{\cosh{H x_\text{\three}^{+}}\sin{\frac{HL}{2}}+\sinh{Hx_\text{\three}^{+}}}{\cosh{H x_\text{\three}^{+}}+\sin{\frac{HL}{2}}\sinh{Hx_\text{\three}^{+}}}\right]}\right]}

and
{T_{\rm{B}}}_{++}
is determined as

\displaystyle{T_{\rm{B}}}_{++}

\displaystyle=-\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi}\csc^{2}{\left[HL+\arccos{\left[\frac{\cosh{H x_\text{\three}^{+}}\sin{\frac{HL}{2}}+\sinh{Hx_\text{\three}^{+}}}{\cosh{H x_\text{\three}^{+}}+\sin{\frac{HL}{2}}\sinh{Hx_\text{\three}^{+}}}\right]}\right]}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\times\frac{\cos^{2}{\frac{HL}{2}}}{(\cosh{Hx_\text{\three}^{+}}+\sin{\frac{HL}{2}}\sinh{Hx_\text{\three}^{+}})^{2}}.

Finally, by using the boundary condition at
x_{\text{{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI}}}=-L/2
(

), we obtain the flux in region III as a function of

IV
Summary and Conclusion

In this paper, Hawking radiation in an analog de Sitter spacetime in the quantum Hall system was calculated by obtaining the general solution of the anomalous conservation law in each region and considering the boundary conditions between region I and II, and between region II and III. In this system, the effect of gravitational anomaly does not appear in the conservation law of the EMT in the bulk regions, but the effect of anomaly appears as the boundary conditions between bulk regions.
Furthermore, in the original calculation of Hawking radiation using gravitational anomaly, the assumption of the existence of anomaly was necessary. However, in the QH system, the calculation becomes exact because of the inherent property that only chiral fields that exist.

It has been found that Hawking radiation can be obtained by calculating gravitational anomaly, but there are several points needed to be discussed. We found a negatively divergent behavior of

\mathcal{H}^{-}
in region II, but detailed investigation of this issue has not been done.
We assumed that the analog de Sitter spacetime is time-symmetric, which makes boundary conditions simple. However, in order to extract only the effect of Hawking radiation from the future horizon, an analysis using the non-eternal setup

-adic setting. To be specific, for a positive integer

p
-adic setting with
n
pairs of arbitrary parameters essentially depend on

to determine all the possible values and explore their number theoretic consequences.
For a complex number
a

the rising factorial denoted by
(a)_{k}
is defined by

(a)_{k}:=a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+k-1)

For

the classical hypergeometric series

{{}_{r+1}}F_{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1},&a_{2},&\ldots,&a_{r+1}\\

In this series if one of the numerator parameters is equal to a non-positive integer, for example let
a_{1}=-n
, where

then the series terminates and the function is a polynomial of degree
n
in

.

{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-n,&b\\
&c\end{array}\mid z\right)
when

and

are complex arbitrary parameters is of particular interest. Indeed it has not been explored completely, and even when
b,c
are real.
The zero location of special classes of such polynomials with restrictions on the parameters

[

Let

\mathbb{F}_{p}

\overline{\omega}

In these notations, we recall McCarthy’s definition of hypergeometric function in the

Definition 1.1
.

[
18

Let
p
be an odd prime and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
For positive integer

,
b_{k}

Then

\displaystyle{{}_{n}\mathbb{G}_{n}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1},&a_{2},&\ldots,&a_{n}\\
b_{1},&b_{2},&\ldots,&b_{n}\end{array}\mid t\right]:=\frac{-1}{p-1}\sum_{a=0}^{p-2}(-1)^{an}~~\overline{\omega}^{a}(t)

\displaystyle\times\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-p)^{-\lfloor\langle a_{k}\rangle-\frac{a}{p-1}\rfloor-\lfloor\langle-b_{k}\rangle+\frac{a}{p-1}\rfloor}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\langle a_{k}-\frac{a}{p-1}\rangle)}{\Gamma_{p}(\langle a_{k}\rangle)}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\langle-b_{k}+\frac{a}{p-1}\rangle)}{\Gamma_{p}(\langle-b_{k}\rangle)}.

This function is also known as
p
-adic hypergeometric function. It is important to note that the value of this function depends only on the fractional part of the parameters
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
. Therefore, we may assume that
0\leq a_{k},b_{k}<1
.
Consider two families of McCarthy’s hypergeometric functions in the
p
-adic setting

\displaystyle{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t):

\displaystyle={{}_{n}\mathbb{G}_{n}}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\frac{1}{2n},&\frac{3}{2n},&\frac{5}{2n},&\ldots,&\frac{2n-1}{2n}\\
0,&\frac{1}{n},&\frac{2}{n},&\ldots,&\frac{n-1}{n}\end{array}\mid t\right],

and

\displaystyle{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t):

\displaystyle={{}_{n}\mathbb{G}_{n}}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\frac{1}{2},&\frac{1}{2(n-1)},&\frac{3}{2(n-1)},&\ldots,&\frac{2n-3}{2(n-1)}\\
0,&\frac{1}{n},&\frac{2}{n},&\ldots,&\frac{n-1}{n}\end{array}\mid t\right].

In this paper we restrict our attention to these functions. For convenience we denote these functions by
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
, and

{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)
throughout the paper. Classical hypergeometric series possess many powerful identities. For instance,
Gauss’ theorem provides a special value of a general
{{}_{2}F_{1}}(1)
hypergeometric series. To be specific, Gauss established that

\displaystyle{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a,&b\\
&c\end{array}\mid 1\right)=\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)},

provided
R(c-a-b)>0
. In the classical case another evaluation of a
{}_{2}F_{1}(-1)
hypergeometric series is due to Kummer
[
16
]

classical hypergeometric series including Dixon’s theorem
[
21

\displaystyle{{}_{3}F_{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a,&b,&c\\
&1+a-b,&1+a-c\end{array}\mid 1\right)

(1.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1+\frac{a}{2})\Gamma(1+a-b)\Gamma(1+a-c)\Gamma(1+\frac{a}{2}-b-c)}{\Gamma(1+a)\Gamma(1+\frac{a}{2}-b)\Gamma(1+\frac{a}{2}-c)\Gamma(1+a-b-c)},

and Whipple’s theorem
[

\displaystyle{{}_{3}F_{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a,&1-a,&c\\
&b,&1-b+2c\end{array}\mid 1\right)

(1.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\Gamma(b)\Gamma(1-b+2c)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(a+b))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(1+a-b+2c))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(1-a+b))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(2-a-b+2c))}.

These identities give motivation to study values of hypergeometric functions in the
p
-adic setting.

In Section 2 we discuss the values of
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
over finite fields. By investigating the values we obtain zeros of these functions. In Section 3 we explore the values of

2.
Values of
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)

Before we discuss the general case, we first mention that the values of
{{}_{2}G_{2}}(t)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
have been obtained in
[
20
]
. We now investigate the values of
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
when
n\geq 3
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer and
p
be an odd prime such that
p\nmid n
. Let

d=\gcd{(n,p-1)}
and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(1)

If
t^{\frac{p-1}{d}}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, then

{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
a^{n}\equiv t\pmod{p}\end{subarray}}\varphi(a)\varphi(a-1)
.

(2)

If
t^{\frac{p-1}{d}}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)=0
.

By this theorem it follows easily that the function
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
takes only integer values.
It is of interest to examine bounds of hypergeometric functions. From Theorem
2.1
we obtain an obvious bound of the function
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
. We express this in the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
p
be an odd prime such that
p\nmid n
. If
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
d=\gcd{(n,p-1)}
then
-d\leq{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)\leq d
.

There are two questions that emerge from Theorem
2.1
. The first concerns the investigation of zeros of the
p
-adic hypergeometric function
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. If
x\in\mathbb{Q}
is a zero of
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
then the second question is whether
x
is a zero of
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for infinitely many primes
p
. We first discuss a very special case that gives zeros of the function

{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)={{}_{3}\mathbb{G}_{3}}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{2},&\frac{1}{6},&\frac{5}{6}\\
0,&\frac{1}{3},&\frac{2}{3}\end{array}\mid t\right]
. The more general case will be discussed later.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.

(1)

Let
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
g
be a primitive root modulo
p
. If
t\neq 1
then

\displaystyle{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)=0

if and only if
t=g^{i}
with
\gcd{(i,3)}=1
, and if
t=1
then
{{}_{3}G_{3}(1)}=0
if and only if
p\equiv 7\pmod{12}
.

(2)

Let
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. Then
{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)=0
if and only if
t=0,1
. In other words, if
t\neq 0,1
then
{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)\neq 0
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is important to note that 1 is a zero of the function
{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)
for infinitely many primes. If
g\in\mathbb{Z}
is a primitive root modulo
{p}
for infinitely many primes of the form
3k+1
then
g
is a zero of the function
{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)
for infinitely many primes of the form
3k+1
. The existence of such kind of primitive roots certainly related to Artin’s conjecture on primitive roots.

If we put
a=\frac{1}{2}
,
b=\frac{1}{6}
, and
c=\frac{5}{6}
in (
1
) we obtain

\displaystyle{{}_{3}F_{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{2},&\frac{1}{6},&\frac{5}{6}\\
&1+\frac{1}{3},&\frac{2}{3}\end{array}\mid 1\right)=\frac{\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{4})\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{3})\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{12})\Gamma(\frac{5}{12})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}.

This is a particular case of Dixon’s theorem (
1
) and gives motivation to present
p
-adic analogue of
(
2
) in the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
p>3
is a prime then we have

\displaystyle{{}_{3}G_{3}}(1)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm 2,&\hbox{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{12}$;}\\
0,&\hbox{if $p\not\equiv 1\pmod{12}$.}\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if we put
n=3
in Theorem
2.1
then for
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
we obtain the values of
{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)
for all primes
p>3
. To be specific, we obtain

Corollary 2.6
.

(1)

Let
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
t\neq 0,1
.
If
t^{\frac{p-1}{3}}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
then

{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
a^{3}\equiv t\pmod{p}\end{subarray}}\varphi(a(a-1)),
and if
t^{\frac{p-1}{3}}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
then
{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)=0
.

(2)

Let
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
t\neq 0,1
. Then

{{}_{3}G_{3}}(t)=\varphi(t^{\frac{2p-1}{3}})\varphi(t^{\frac{2p-1}{3}}-1)
.

This gives an extension of Corollary
2.5
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and can be understood as some kind of extension over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
of the particular case (
2
) of Dixon’s theorem in the
p
-adic setting.

We now discuss the case for
n>3
. In Theorem
2.1
we show that if
t^{\frac{p-1}{d}}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)=0
for all primes

p\nmid n
, where
d=\gcd{(n,p-1)}
. More generally, it is interesting to examine the zeros of
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer and
p
be an odd prime such that
p\nmid n
. Let
d=\gcd{(n,p-1)}
.
Let
t^{\frac{p-1}{d}}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.

(1)

Let
n
be even. If
t\neq 1
and
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}
are the incongruent solutions of
y^{n}\equiv t\pmod{p}
modulo

and
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(a_{i}(a_{i}-1))=0
then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)=0
. On the other hand if
t=1
then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(1)\neq 0
.

(2)

Let
n
be odd. If
t\neq 1
then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)\neq 0
. On the other hand if
t=1
and
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1}
different from

are the incongruent solutions of the congruence
y^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
modulo
p
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\varphi(a_{i}(a_{i}-1))=0
then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(1)=0
.

The following corollaries provide particular values of

such that
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)=0
for infinitely many primes.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an odd integer. Then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(1)=0
for infinitely many primes
p
such that
\gcd{(n,p(p-1))}=1
. If

t\neq 0,1
then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(t)=\varphi\left(\frac{a}{a-1}\right)\neq 0
for all primes
p
such that
\gcd{(n,p(p-1))}=1
, where

is the unique solution of
y^{n}\equiv t\pmod{p}
modulo
p
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
n\geq 4
be an even integer. Then
{{}_{n}G_{n}}(-1)=0
for infinitely many primes
p
such that
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.

If we consider the family
\{{{}_{p-1}G_{p-1}(t)}:p\geq 3\}
then it turns out that for

this family takes only one value. We state these in the following corollary explicitly.

Corollary 2.10
.

If
t\neq 1
then
{{}_{p-1}G_{p-1}}(t)=0
, and if
t=1
then

{{}_{p-1}G_{p-1}}(1)=-1
for all primes
p\geq 3
.

3.
Values of
{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)

In this section we explore the values of the function
{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)
. We express these values in terms of roots of certain polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer and
p
be an odd prime such that
p\nmid n(n-1)
. For
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
let

f_{t}(y)=y^{n}-y^{n-1}+\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}t}{n^{n}}
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and

\beta_{n}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{if $n$ is odd;}\\
1-(p-1)\varphi((1-n)t),&\hbox{if $n$ is even.}\end{array}\right.

Then we have

\displaystyle{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)=\frac{\beta_{n}(t)-1}{p}+\sum_{f_{t}(a)\equiv 0\pmod{p}}\varphi(a(a-1)).

If we put
a=\frac{1}{4}
,
b=\frac{1}{3}
, and
c=\frac{1}{2}
in (
1
) then we obtain

\displaystyle{{}_{3}F_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{4},&\frac{3}{4},&\frac{1}{2}\\
&\frac{1}{3},&1+\frac{2}{3}\end{array}\mid 1\right)=\frac{\pi\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})\Gamma(\frac{5}{3})}{\Gamma(\frac{7}{24})\Gamma(\frac{23}{24})\Gamma(\frac{13}{24})\Gamma(\frac{29}{24})}.

This is a special case of Whipple’s theorem. Using this identity we motivate our next result.
If we put
n=3
in Theorem
3.1
then we have

{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(t)={{}_{3}\mathbb{G}_{3}}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{4},&\frac{3}{4},&\frac{1}{2}\\
0,&\frac{1}{3},&\frac{2}{3}\end{array}\mid t\right]
and we obtain

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Let
27y^{3}-27y^{2}+4t
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(1)

\displaystyle{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(1)=1+\varphi(-2)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2,&\hbox{if $p\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}$;}\\
0,&\hbox{if $p\equiv 5,7\pmod{8}$.}\end{array}\right.

(2)

Let
t\neq 1
and
27y^{3}-27y^{2}+4t
be irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then

{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(t)=0
.

(3)

Let
t\neq 1
and
27y^{3}-27y^{2}+4t
has one root
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
counting with multiplicity. Then

{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(t)=\varphi(a(a-1))
.

(4)

Let
t\neq 1
and
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
be the roots of the polynomial
27y^{3}-27y^{2}+4t
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then

{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(t)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\varphi(a_{i}(a_{i}-1))
.

(
1
) can be described as a
p
-adic analogue of (
3
) which is a special case of Whipple’s theorem. Indeed this corollary extends (
3
) over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in the
p
-adic setting.

Remark 3.3
.

It is of interest to discuss the zeros of the function
{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For example, if
t=1
then

{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(1)=0
if and only if
p\equiv 5,7\pmod{8}
. Therefore,
t=1
is a non trivial zero of the function

{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(t)
for infinitely many primes
p
. Let
t\in\mathbb{Q}-\{0,1\}
and
27y^{3}-27y^{2}+4t
be irreducible over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
for infinitely primes
p
then
{{}_{3}\widetilde{G}_{3}}(t)=0
for infinitely many primes
p
. However, we do not know whether such
t
exist or not. This will be an interesting question to study in future.

We now investigate two more general cases of zeros of
p
-adic hypergeometric functions. By Theorem
3.1
we obtain

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
n>3
be an even integer and
p
be an odd prime satisfying
p\nmid n(n-1)
.
If
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
then
{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)=0

if and only if
t=0
. In other words, if
t\neq 0
then
{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)\neq 0
for all primes

p\nmid n(n-1)
.

Remark 3.5
.

This corollary is in contrast to Corollary
2.9
. If
p\mid n(n-1)
then the function
{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)
is not defined for these primes. Hence, by Corollary
3.4
we obtain that if
t\neq 0
then there is no prime
p
for which

{{}_{n}\widetilde{G}_{n}}(t)=0
.

4.
Notation and Preliminaries

4.1.
Multiplicative characters:

Let
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}
denote the group of all multiplicative characters of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Let
\overline{\chi}
denote the inverse of a multiplicative character
\chi
. We extend the domain of each
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
by simply setting
\chi(0):=0
including the trivial character
\varepsilon.

We start with a lemma that gives orthogonality relation of multiplicative characters.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
15
, Chapter 8]

Let
p
be an odd prime. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}}\chi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p-1,&\hbox{if $x=1$;}\\
0,&\hbox{if $x\neq 1$.}\end{array}\right.

Let
\zeta_{p}
denote a fixed primitive
p
-th root of unity. For multiplicative character

\chi
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
the Gauss sum is defined by

\displaystyle g(\chi):=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi(x)~\zeta_{p}^{x}.

If
\chi=\varepsilon
then it is easy to see that
g(\varepsilon)=-1
. For more details on the properties of Gauss sum, see
[
11
]
. Let
\delta:\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\mapsto\{0,1\}
be defined by

\displaystyle\delta(\chi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{if $\chi=\varepsilon$;}\\
0,&\hbox{if $\chi\neq\varepsilon$.}\end{array}\right.

We now state a product formula for Gauss sums.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
13

Let

An important product formula of

If

(4.12)

\displaystyle\prod_{h=0}^{m-1}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{x+h}{m}\right)=\omega(m^{(1-x)(1-p)})~\Gamma_{p}(x)\prod_{h=1}^{m-1}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{h}{m}\right).

The next two relations are essentially contained in
[

]
. Let

\displaystyle\omega(t^{tj})\Gamma_{p}\left(\left\langle\frac{tj}{p-1}\right\rangle\right)\prod_{h=1}^{t-1}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{h}{t}\right)=\prod_{h=0}^{t-1}\Gamma_{p}\left(\left\langle\frac{h}{t}+\frac{j}{p-1}\right\rangle\right),

Lemma 4.7

Let

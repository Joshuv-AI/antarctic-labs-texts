Abstract
We study the question of whether a sequence d = ( d 1 , d 2 , … , d n ) d=(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}) of positive integers is the degree sequence of some outerplanar (a.k.a. 1-page book embeddable) graph G G . If so, G G is an outerplanar realization of d d and d d is an outerplanaric sequence. The case where ∑ d ≤ 2 ​ n − 2 \sum d\leq 2n-2 is easy, as d d has a realization by a forest (which is trivially an outerplanar graph). In this paper, we consider the family 𝒟 \mathcal{D} of all sequences d d of even sum 2 ​ n ≤ ∑ d ≤ 4 ​ n − 6 − 2 ​ ω 1 2n\leq\sum d\leq 4n-6-2\omega_{1} , where ω x \omega_{x} is the number of x x ’s in d d . (The second inequality is a necessary condition for a sequence d d with ∑ d ≥ 2 ​ n \sum d\geq 2n to be outerplanaric.) We partition 𝒟 \mathcal{D} into two disjoint subfamilies, 𝒟 = 𝒟 N ​ O ​ P ∪ 𝒟 2 ​ P ​ B ​ E \mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{NOP}\cup\mathcal{D}_{2PBE} , such that every sequence in 𝒟 N ​ O ​ P \mathcal{D}_{NOP} is provably non-outerplanaric, and every sequence in 𝒟 2 ​ P ​ B ​ E \mathcal{D}_{2PBE} is given a realizing graph G G enjoying a 2-page book embedding (and moreover, one of the pages is also bipartite).

Background.

Our study is concerned with degree sequences of graphs.
The degree of a vertex in a graph

A sequence

One of the first characterizations of all graphic degree sequences
was given by Erdös and Gallai
[
6
]
.
About the same time, Havel and Hakimi
[
10
,
11
]
described
an algorithm that, given a sequence
d
, generates a realization
if
d
is graphic or verifies that
d
is not graphic.
Other studies focused on the uniqueness of the realization.
It is known that a sequence can have several non-isomorphic realizations.
As an example, consider the sequence

The realization problem has also been investigated for

In particular, realization by planar graphs was considered
in several papers, but currently a complete solution seems out of reach.
The survey by Rao
[

listed a number of partial results and
studies on related questions.
In this paper, we address the question

Outerplanar graphs are

each other and additionally,

]

A sequence

Similarly, a sequence

Throughout, we denote by

We use the shorthand

By the well-known Handshaking Lemma,

If

We show later (Lemma

Consequently, we focus on the family of nonincreasing sequences of
n

positive integers

\mathcal{D}~=~\left\{d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})~\mid~\sum d~\mbox{is even and}~2n\leq\sum d\leq 4n-6-2\omega_{1}\right\}.

Let

While we are unable to fully characterize the sequences of the family

with respect to outerplanarity, we obtain a classification result that can be
thought of as an “approximate” solution to the outerplanarity problem.
To do that, we employ the concept of

Given a graph
G=(V,E)

The

Definitions and notation.

Given two

Given two sequences

Consider a simple (undirected) graph
G=(V,E)
.
A (directed)

in

is an
ordered set
of vertices

C=\left\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1}\right\}
(with possible repetitions) such that

(v_{i},v_{(i+1)\bmod k})\in E
for every

.
The directed edge set of the circuit

is

E(C)=\{\langle v_{i},v_{(i+1)\bmod k}\rangle\mid i=0,\ldots,k-1\}

and it is required
that no directed edge may appear on it more than once.
If the vertices of
C
are distinct then it is called a
cycle
.
A
triangle
is a cycle consisting of three edges.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, one may define an accompanying embedding in the plane,
namely, a mapping of every vertex to a point in two-dimensional space, with
a curve connecting the two endpoints
u,w

of every edge
(u,w)\in E
.
An
outerplanar
graph

is a graph that has a planar embedding
in which all the vertices occur on the outer face.
(This embedding can be transformed into a 1-page book embedding of

with all vertices placed on the

We refer to the order in which the vertices occur on the outer face as the

of

, and denote it by

.
A

(MOP) graph

is an outerplanar graph
such that adding any new edge to it results in a non-outerplanar graph.
It is

that for a

the cyclic order corresponds to a Hamiltonian cycle in

.

Hereafter, whenever we discuss a given outerplanar (resp., 2PBE) graph,
we implicitly assume that some outerplanar (resp., 2PBE) embedding for it

graph, we also give an outerplanar (resp., 2PBE) embedding for it.

Given an outer-planar embedding, an

An

The other ingredient in the construction of a realization to a forestic

Observation 1
.

A forestic sequence

of positive integers can be
realized by a union of a caterpillar graph and a matching.

Proof.

First consider a treeic sequence

(such that
\sum d=2n-2
).
Assume there are
n-s
vertices of degree 1 in

.
Denote by

the prefix of

that contains all the degrees

.
It follows that
\sum d^{*}+(n-s)=\sum d=2n-2

\sum d^{*}-2s+2=n-s

To get the caterpillar realization of

The path edges contribute

to the volume

The missing

Now assume that

\sum d<2n-2
.
In this case, remove pairs of
1 degrees from

until the volume of the reaming sequence

with

.
This must happen because each pair removal reduces the volume by

to satisfy the

Some known results.

We next summarize known conditions for sequence outerplanarity.

Lemma 2

(
[

]

If

is an outerplanaric degree sequence where

\sum d\leq 4n-6

with equality if and only if

is maximal outerplanaric.

Lemma 3

(
[
21
]

If

is
outerplanaric, then

(i)

d_{n-1}\leq 2
, and

(ii)

d_{n-2}\leq 3
.

Moreover,
d_{n-1}=d_{n}=2
in a maximal outerplanaric sequence

.

Our next observation is that Lemma

can be improved if
\omega_{1}
, the multiplicity of degree
1

is taken into consideration.
Specifically, for the case
\sum d>2n-2
, we have the following.

Lemma 4
.

If

d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})

is outerplanaric and
\sum d>2n-2
, then
\sum d\leq 4n-6-2\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
d
be as in the lemma and let
G=(V,E)
be an outerplanar graph
that realizes

. Construct a graph
G^{\prime}
by deleting from
G
all the
\omega_{1}
vertices
of degree
1
together with their incident edges, and subsequently deleting the vertices whose degree was reduced to zero.
The graph
G^{\prime}
may contain vertices whose degree became 1.
Observe that
G^{\prime}
is outerplanar, and let
d^{\prime}=\deg(G^{\prime})
.
Note that
n^{\prime}\leq n-\omega_{1}
, where
n^{\prime}
the number of vertices in
G^{\prime}
.
Lemma
2
yields the claim, since

\sum d=\sum d^{\prime}+2\omega_{1}\leq 4(n-\omega_{1})-6+2\omega_{1}=4n-6-2\omega_{1}~,

where the first equality comes from the fact that
\omega_{1}
vertices
of degree

together with their incident edges are deleted,
and the inequality is due to Lemma

.

We remark that Lemma

A graph is a MOP

Lemma 5
.

Consider a maximal outerplanar graph
G

(1)

Every two vertices of degree 2 in
G
are nonadjacent.

(2)

there is a outerplanar embedding of

(3)

Removing the edges of
C

The lemma is proved by induction on the number of vertices

The claim clearly holds for the base case of

Now suppose the claim holds for all MOP graphs with at most

and consider a graph

By
Fact

1

G

Adding

Bipartite bracket balancing (BBB) algorithm.

we make use of a simple variant of the
Bracket Balancing
problem,
in which the matched brackets must fit in given positions.
Formally, the problem, referred to as

Bipartite Bracket Balancing (BBB)
, is defined as follows.
We are given two ordered sets
P=\left\langle p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\right\rangle
and
Q=\left\langle q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}\right\rangle
, merged into a
2k

R=\left\langle r_{1},\ldots,r_{2k}\right\rangle
that contains all the elements from
P
and
Q
while maintaining their original order.
The goal is to associate with every
r_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 2k
, a bracket
f(r_{i})\in\left\{[,]\right\}
with the following two properties:

•

The sequence
f(R)=\langle f(r_{1}),\ldots,f(r_{2k})\rangle
is balanced
such that the number of opening brackets (
``[^{\prime\prime}
) in every prefix of
R
is no less than the number of closing brackets (
``]^{\prime\prime}
) in this prefix. Observe that in balanced sequences it is possible to match the opening brackets with the closing brackets
so that the intervals associated with the matched brackets are either nested or disjoint.

•

if
f(r_{i})=[
and
f(r_{j})=]
were matched, for
i<j
, then
r_{i}\in P
and

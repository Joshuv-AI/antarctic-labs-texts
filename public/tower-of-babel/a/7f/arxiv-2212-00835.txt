Abstract
. In this paper we state the following weighted Hardy type inequality for any functions φ \varphi in a weighted Sobolev space and for weight functions μ \mu of a quite general type c N , μ ​ ∫ ℝ N V ​ φ 2 ​ μ ​ ( x ) ​ 𝑑 x ≤ ∫ ℝ N | ∇ φ | 2 ​ μ ​ ( x ) ​ 𝑑 x + C μ ​ ∫ ℝ N W ​ φ 2 ​ μ ​ ( x ) ​ 𝑑 x , c_{N,\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\,\varphi^{2}\mu(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mu(x)dx+C_{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W\varphi^{2}\mu(x)dx, where V V is a multipolar potential and W W is a bounded function from above depending on μ \mu . The method to get the result is based on the introduction of a suitable vector value function and on an integral identity that we state in the paper. We prove that the constant c N , μ c_{N,\mu} in the estimate is optimal by building a suitable sequence of functions.

1.
Introduction

The paper fits into the context of multipolar weighted Hardy type inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The main motivation to investigate weighted Hardy inequalities is the application of the estimates in the study of Kolmogorov operators

(1.1)

Lu=\Delta u+\frac{\nabla\mu}{\mu}\cdot\nabla u,

defined on smooth functions,
\mu>0
is a probability density on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, perturbed by singular potentials and of the related evolution problems

(P)\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=Lu(x,t)+V(x)u(x,t),\quad\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},t>0,\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\geq 0\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mu(x)dx).\end{array}\right.

The classical Hardy inequality was introduced in 1920th
[
15
]
in the one dimensional case (see also

[
16
,
17
]
). For historical reviews see, for example,
[
13
,
21
,
22
]
.

The well known version in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
N\geq 3
, of the inequality is

(1.2)

c_{o}(N)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx

for any functions
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
c_{o}(N)=\bigl(\frac{N-2}{2}\bigr)^{2}
is the optimal constant.
The inequality (
1.2
) does not hold if
c_{o}(N)
is replaced by any
c>c_{o}(N)

(see, e.g.,
[
23
,
24
,
18
]
).

In literature there exist reference papers
in the case of Schrödinger operators with singular potentials

V(x)=\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
,
c>0
. These potentials are interesting for the criticality: they lie at a borderline case where
standard theories such as the strong maximum principle and Gaussian bounds in
[
1
]
fail. It does not belong to the Kato’s class, then it cannot be regarded as a lower order perturbation term.

The operator
\Delta+\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
has the same homogeneity as the Laplacian.
In 1984 P. Baras and J. A. Goldstein in
[
2
]

showed a remarkable result: the evolution problem
(P)
with
L=\Delta
and
V(x)=\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
admits a unique positive solution
if
c\leq c_{o}=\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
and no positive solutions in the sense of distributions exist if
c>c_{o}
.
When it exists, the solution is
exponentially bounded, on the contrary, if
c>c_{o}
, there is the so-called instantaneous blow-up phenomenon.

An analogous result has been obtained by Cabré and Martel in
[
4
]
for more general potentials
0\leq V\in L_{loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and with a different approach.
They showed that the boundedness of

\lambda_{1}(\Delta+V)
, the bottom of the spectrum of the operator
-(L+\Delta)
,

\lambda_{1}(L+V):=\inf_{\varphi\in H^{1}_{\mu}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V\varphi^{2}\,dx}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\varphi^{2}\,dx}\right),

is a necessary
and sufficient condition for the existence of positive exponentially bounded in time
solutions to the associated initial value problem.

These results have been extended to Kolmogorov operators perturbed by inverse square potential
V=\frac{c}{|x|^{2}}
in
[
20
,
19
]
.
Later, for more general drift term, similar result can be found in
[
8
,
10
]
.

If the potentials are of a more general type see

[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

The case of the Schrödinger operator with multipolar inverse square potentials has been investigated in literature.

In particular, for the operator

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{c_{i}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}},

n\geq 2
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, V. Felli, E. M. Marchini and S. Terracini in

[
14
]
proved that the associated quadratic form

Q(\varphi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\,dx

is positive if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{+}<\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
,
c_{i}^{+}=\max\{c_{i},0\}
,
conversely if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{+}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
there exists a configuration of poles such that
Q
is not positive.
Later R. Bosi, J. Dolbeaut and M. J. Esteban in
[
3
]
proved that
for any
c\in\left(0,\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\right]
there exists
a positive constant
K
such that the multipolar Hardy inequality

c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx\\
+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi^{2}\,dx

holds for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
C. Cazacu and E. Zuazua in
[
12
]
, improving a result stated in

[
3
]
, obtained the inequality with optimal constant

\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}\varphi^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx,

for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

For Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type operators

Lu=\Delta u-\sum_{i=1}^{n}A(x-a_{i})\cdot\nabla u,

perturbed by multipolar
inverse square potentials

(1.3)

V(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{c}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}},\quad c>0,\quad a_{1}\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

weighted multipolar Hardy inequalities with optimal constant
and related existence and nonexistence results were stated in
[
9
]
,
with
A
a positive definite real Hermitian
N\times N
matrix,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
In such a case, the invariant measure for these operators is the Gaussian measure

\mu_{A}(x)dx=\kappa e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle A(x-a_{i}),x-a_{i}\right\rangle}dx
,
with a normalization constant
\kappa
. In the paper some results in
[
20
]
have been extended to the multipolar case. We remark that to get weighted Hardy inequalities involving multipolar potentials
V
as in (
1.3
), we need to overcome even the difficulties due to the mutual interaction among the poles.

In
[
11
]
these results have been extended to Kolmogorov operators with a more general drift term using different techniques and following Cabré-Martel’s approach to get existence and nonexistence results.

In this paper we extend the inequality stated in
[
12
]
to the weighted case improving a result stated in
[
11
]
. The potential that we consider is

(1.4)

V(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}.

In particular we state the following inequality

(1.5)

c_{N,\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W\varphi^{2}\,d\mu

for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mu(x)dx)
, where
W
is a bounded function from above which depends on the weight.

The proof is based on the introduction of a suitable vector value function and on an integral identity that we state.

As applications to PDE, we get the existence of positive solutions to (P), with
V
as in (
1.4
), following Cabré-Martel’s approach as in
[
11
]
.
This because the Hardy inequality is related to the estimate of the bottom of the spectrum

\lambda_{1}(L+V)
. In
[
11
]
one can found existence and nonexistence results in the multipolar case when
V\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so the existence results can be extended to potential
V
in (
1.4
).

The constant
c_{N,\mu}
in (
1.5
) is optimal. To prove the optimality
we reason as in
[
12
]
, but the sequence involved to prove the reverse inequality is different.

The paper is organized as follows.

Section 1 is devoted to the introduction of the weight functions and to the weighted Hardy type inequality. In Section 2 we state the optimality of the constant in the estimate.

2.
Weighted inequality

Let
\mu\geq 0
be a weight function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We define the weighted Sobolev space

H^{1}_{\mu}=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mu(x)dx)

as the space of functions in
L^{2}_{\mu}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mu(x)dx)
whose weak derivatives belong to

L_{\mu}^{2}
.

The conditions on
\mu
which we need are the following.

H_{1})

i)

\quad\sqrt{\mu}\in H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

ii)

\quad\mu^{-1}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

H_{2})

there exists constants
C_{\mu},K_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
,
k_{\mu}>2-N
, such that
it holds

(2.1)

W:=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\left[(x-a_{i})\cdot\frac{\nabla\mu}{\mu}-K_{\mu}\right]\leq C_{\mu}.

The class of weight functions
\mu
satisfying conditions
H_{1})
and
H_{2})
was considered in
[
11
]
to get a weighted multipolar Hardy inequality involving inverse square potentials of multipolar type (see also
[
10
]
for a similar conditions in the case of a single pole).

Under the hypotheses
i)
and
ii)
in
H_{1})
the
space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
H_{\mu}^{1}
(see e.g.
[
25
]
).
So we can regard

H_{\mu}^{1}
as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the Sobolev norm

\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}_{\mu}}^{2}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}^{2}.

We need the density result in the proof of Theorem
2.1
below to get the result for any function in
H^{1}_{\mu}
.
Actually, with the additional assumption

H_{3})

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{B(a_{i},\delta)}\mu(x)dx=0\qquad\forall\,i\in\{1,\dots,n\},

then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\})
is dense in
H^{1}_{\mu}

(see
[
11
]
).

A class of weight functions satisfying hypotheses
H_{2})
is the following

(2.2)

\mu(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|x-a_{1}|^{\gamma}}e^{-\delta\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x-a_{j}|^{m}},\qquad\delta\geq 0,\quad m\leq 2,

for suitable values of
\gamma<N-2
(see
[
11
]
for details).

Let

(2.3)

V(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}.

We remark that when
x
tends to the pole
a_{i}
we get
V\sim\frac{n-1}{|x-a_{j}|^{2}}
.

The next result states a weighted Hardy type inequality involving the potential
V
defined in (
2.3
). In
[
12
]
a similar inequality was obtained in the case of Lebesgue measure in a different way and under different hypotheses.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under conditions
H_{1})
and
H_{2})
the following inequality holds

(2.4)

c_{N,\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W\varphi^{2}\,d\mu

for any
\varphi\in H^{1}_{\mu}
, where
\beta=\frac{N+K_{\mu}-2}{n}
and

c_{N,\mu}=\frac{(N+K_{\mu}-2)^{2}}{n^{2}}
.

As a consequence we get

(2.5)

c_{N,\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu+C_{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi^{2}\,d\mu.

Proof.
.

It is enough to prove (
2.4
)
for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
By density argument we extend the result to functions
\varphi\in H^{1}_{\mu}
.

We set

f=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|x-a_{i}|^{\beta}},\qquad\beta>0,

and introduce the function

(2.6)

F(x)=-\frac{\nabla f}{f}\,\mu=-\frac{\nabla f_{i}}{f_{i}}\,\mu:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta\,\frac{x-a_{i}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\mu.

It is useful note that

(2.7)

\begin{split}\frac{\Delta f}{f}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Delta f_{i}}{f_{i}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\nabla f_{i}}{f_{i}}\frac{\nabla f_{j}}{f_{j}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta^{2}-\beta(N-2)}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}+\beta^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(x-a_{i})\cdot(x-a_{j})}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n\beta^{2}-\beta(N-2)}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n\beta^{2}-\beta(N-2)}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}-\beta^{2}V\end{split}

since

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(x-a_{i})\cdot(x-a_{j})}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{|x|^{2}-x\cdot a_{i}-x\cdot a_{j}+a_{i}\cdot a_{j}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\frac{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|x-a_{j}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|x-a_{j}|^{2}}-\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}\right)\\
&=(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}.\end{split}

We observe that

V=-\frac{\Delta f}{f}\qquad\hbox{if}\quad\beta=\frac{N-2}{n}.

We start from the following integral and use (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to obtain

(2.8)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\rm div}&F\,\varphi^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-\frac{\Delta f}{f}\mu+\left|\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right|^{2}\mu-\frac{\nabla f}{f}\cdot\nabla\mu\right)\varphi^{2}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Biggl[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta(N-2)-n\beta^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}+\beta^{2}V+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n\beta^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\\
&-\beta^{2}V+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(x-a_{i})}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\cdot\nabla\mu\Biggr]\varphi^{2}dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{\beta(N-2)}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\mu+\beta\frac{(x-a_{i})}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\cdot\nabla\mu\right]\varphi^{2}dx\end{split}

by observing that the functions

F_{j}
,
\frac{\partial F_{j}}{\partial x_{j}}
, where

F_{j}=\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(x-a_{i})_{j}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\mu
,
belong to
\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let us see in more detail. By means of
Hölder’s inequality and the
classical Hardy’s inequality, taking in mind hypothesis
i)
in
H_{2})
,
for any
K
compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we get

\begin{split}\int_{K}|F_{j}|\,dx&\leq\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{K}\frac{\mu(x)}{|x-a_{i}|}\,dx\leq\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{K}\frac{\mu(x)}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{K}\mu(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{2\beta n}{N-2}\left(\int_{K}\left|\nabla\sqrt{\mu}\right|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{K}\mu(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

For the partial derivative of
F_{j}

\frac{\partial F_{j}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\left\{\left[1-2\frac{(x-a_{i})_{j}^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\right]\mu+(x-a_{i})_{j}\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial x_{j}}\right\}

we obtain the estimate

\begin{split}\int_{K}\left|\frac{\partial F_{j}}{\partial x_{j}}\right|\,dx&\leq 3\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{K}\frac{\mu(x)}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\,dx+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{K}\frac{|\nabla\mu|}{|x-a_{i}|}\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{3\beta n}{c_{o}(N)}\int_{K}\left|\nabla{\sqrt{\mu}}\right|^{2}\,dx+2\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{K}\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{|x-a_{i}|}|\nabla\sqrt{\mu}|\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{3\beta n}{c_{o}(N)}\int_{K}\left|\nabla{\sqrt{\mu}}\right|^{2}\,dx+2\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{K}\frac{\mu(x)}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{K}\left|\nabla\sqrt{\mu}\right|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

where
c_{o}(N)=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
is the best constant in Hardy’s inequality.
Then we apply again Hardy’s inequality on the right-hand side above.

Integrating by parts on the left-hand side in (
2.8
), we obtain

(2.9)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}&{\rm div}F\varphi^{2}\,dx=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\frac{\nabla f}{f}\,\mu\,dx\\
&=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\frac{\varphi}{f}\right|^{2}f^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right|^{2}\varphi^{2}\,d\mu,\end{split}

explained by the fact that

\left|\nabla\frac{\varphi}{f}\right|^{2}=\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{f^{2}}+\left|\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right|^{2}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{f^{2}}-2\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\frac{\nabla f}{f}\frac{1}{f^{2}}.

So, putting together (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we deduce that

(2.10)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}&|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\frac{\varphi}{f}\right|^{2}f^{2}\,d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Delta f}{f}\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\\
&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\nabla f}{f}\cdot\frac{\nabla\mu}{\mu}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\frac{\varphi}{f}\right|^{2}f^{2}\,d\mu\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta(N+K_{\mu}-2)-n\beta^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\varphi^{2}\,d\mu+\beta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\left[(x-a_{i})\cdot\frac{\nabla\mu}{\mu}-K_{\mu}\right]\varphi^{2}\,d\mu.\end{split}

For
\beta=\frac{N+K_{\mu}-2}{n}
in (
2.10
), setting

c_{N,\mu}=\frac{(N+K_{\mu}-2)^{2}}{n^{2}}
,
we get the integral identity

(2.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\frac{\varphi}{f}\right|^{2}f^{2}\,d\mu+c_{N,\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\,\varphi^{2}\,d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W\,\varphi^{2}\,d\mu.

Identity (
2.11
) implies that

c_{N,\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\,\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W\varphi^{2}\,d\mu.

For the class of functions
\mu
satisfying hypothesis (
2.1
) in
H_{2})
,
it follows the inequality (
2.5
).

) allows us to have

(2.12)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta(N+K_{\mu}-2)-n\beta^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\\

.
So we get the inequality

(2.13)

\begin{split}c_{N,n,\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}}\,d\mu+\frac{c_{N,n,\mu}}{2n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{|x-a_{i}|^{2}|x-a_{j}|^{2}}\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu+C_{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi^{2}\,d\mu\end{split}

]

Proof.
.

To state the optimality of the constant

In order to prove (
3.1
), we use the integral inequality (
2.11
). So
we show that there exists a sequence

\varphi_{\varepsilon,i}\in H^{1}_{\mu}
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon,i}}{f}\right|^{2}f^{2}\,d\mu=0.

To this aim,
if
\displaystyle r_{0}\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq n\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}|a_{i}-a_{j}|/2
,
let
R

\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B(a_{i},r_{0})\subset B(0,R)

\varepsilon\leq 1

\theta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }|x|<\frac{R}{\varepsilon},\\
\in[0,1]&\text{ if }\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\leq|x|\leq\frac{2R}{\varepsilon},\\

which belongs to
\in H^{1}_{\mu}

In

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon,i}}{f}\right|^{2}f^{2}\,d\mu&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|x-a_{i}|^{-2\frac{N+K_{\mu}-2}{n}}\,d\mu\\

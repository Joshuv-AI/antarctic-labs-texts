The Constant Width Measure Set, the Spherical Measure Set and isoperimetric equalities for planar ovals
Michał Zwierzyński

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce and study sets: the Constant Width Measure Set and the Spherical Measure Set , which measure the constant width property and the spherical property of planar regular simple curves, respectively. Using these sets and the Wigner caustic, we find the exact relations between the length and the area bounded by a closed regular simple convex planar curve M {M} . Namely, the following equalities are fulfilled: (0.1) L M 2 \displaystyle L_{{M}}^{2} = 4 ​ π ​ A M + 8 ​ π ​ | A ~ E 1 2 ​ ( M ) | + π ​ | A ~ CWMS ​ ( M ) | , \displaystyle=4\pi A_{M}+8\pi\left|\widetilde{A}_{{\text{E}}_{\frac{1}{2}}({M})}\right|+\pi\left|\widetilde{A}_{\text{CWMS}({M})}\right|, (0.2) L M 2 \displaystyle L_{{M}}^{2} = 4 ​ π ​ A M + 4 ​ π ​ | A ~ SMS ​ ( M ) | , \displaystyle=4\pi A_{M}+4\pi\left|\widetilde{A}_{\text{SMS}({M})}\right|, where L M , A M , A ~ E 1 2 ​ ( M ) , A ~ CWMS ​ ( M ) , A ~ SMS ​ ( M ) L_{{M}},A_{{M}},\widetilde{A}_{{\text{E}}_{\frac{1}{2}}({M})},\widetilde{A}_{\text{CWMS}({M})},\widetilde{A}_{\text{SMS}({M})} are the length of M {M} , the area bounded by M {M} , the oriented area of the Wigner caustic of M {M} , the oriented area of the Constant Width Measure Set of M {M} and the oriented area of the Spherical Measure Set of M {M} , respectively. The equality ( 0.2 ) holds also if M {M} is a regular closed simple curve. We also present a new proof of Four–Vertex Theorem for generic ovals based on the number of singular points of the SMS .

This inequality was already known by the ancient Greeks. The first mathematical proof of this famous statement was given in the nineteenth century by Steiner

There are many important inequalities in the convex geometry and differential geometry, such as the isoperimetric inequality, the Brunn–Minkowski inequality, Aleksandrov–Fenchel inequality, Gage’s inequality. The stability property of them are of great interest in geometric analysis, see

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section 2 we present geometric quantities, their Fourier series and affine equidistants, including the Wigner caustic.

Section 3 contains a definition of the Constant Width Measure Set and properties of this set for planar ovals.

In Section 4 we introduce the Spherical Measure Set and present properties of this set for planar simple regular closed curves.

Section 5 is devoted to prove the main results, isoperimetric equalities for planar simple regular closed curves.

In Section 6 we study the stability property of the isoperimetric inequality involving the Constant Width Measure Set and thanks to it we find the lower bounds of the absolute value of the oriented area of the Wigner caustic, the Constant Width Measure Set and the Spherical Measure Set.

Let
{M}

\mathbb{R}^{2}

Definition 2.1

Let

Definition 2.2

A

l(a,b)=\left\{\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b\ \big|\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

An affine

The set

of

.

Note that, for any given

To visualize

Theorem 3.2
.

{M}
is an oval of constant width if and only if
\text{CWMS}({M})=\{\Theta\}
.

Proof.

If
\text{CWMS}({M})=\{\Theta\}
, then
b-a=\overline{w}_{{M}}\cdot\mathbbm{n}(a)
for all parallel pairs
a,b
. This gives that the length of
b-a
is always constant and equal to
\overline{w}_{{M}}
and consequently
{M}
is an oval of constant width.

If
{M}
is an oval of constant width

, then one can notice that if
a,b
is a parallel pair of
{M}
, then
a-b
is perpendicular to the tangent lines to
{M}
at
a
and
b
. This gives
a-b+w_{{M}}\cdot\mathbbm{n}(a)=\Theta
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
{M}
be a positively oriented oval. Let

denote the curvature of
{M}
at

{M}

(ii)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\text{CWMS}({M})}(q)=\frac{\kappa_{{M}}(a)\cdot\kappa_{{M}}(b)}{|\kappa_{{M}}(a)+\kappa_{{M}}(b)-\overline{w}_{{M}}\cdot\kappa_{{M}}(a)\cdot\kappa_{{M}}(b)|}.

Let (

\displaystyle p_{\text{CWMS}({M})}(\theta)=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=2,\\

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(\theta)

\displaystyle\rho_{{M}}(\theta)

6.
The stability of improved isoperimetric inequalities

An
n
– dimensional convex body
is a bounded convex subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is closed and has interior points. Let
\mathcal{C}^{n}
denote the set of all
n
– dimensional convex bodies.

An inequality in the convex geometry can be written as

(6.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(K)\geqslant 0,

where
\Phi:\mathcal{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function and the inequality (
6.1
) holds for all
K
in
\mathcal{C}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{\Phi}
be a subset of
\mathcal{C}^{n}
for which equality in (
6.1
) holds.

Let
L_{\partial K}
denote the length of the boundary of
K
, let
A_{\partial K}
denote the area enclosed by
\partial K
(i.e. the area of
K
).

For example, let
n=2
and let
\Phi(K)=L_{\partial K}^{2}-4\pi A_{\partial K}
. Then the inequality
\Phi(K)\geqslant 0
is the classical isoperimetric inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this case
\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\Phi}
is a set of disks.

In this section we will study stability properties associated with (
6.1
). We ask if
K
must by close to a member of
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{\Phi}
whenever
\Phi(K)
is close to zero. Let
d:\mathcal{C}^{n}\times\mathcal{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
denoted in some sense the deviation between two convex bodies.
d
should satisfy two following conditions:

(i)

d(K,L)\geqslant 0
for all
K,L\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
,

(ii)

d(K,L)=0
if and only if
K=L
.

If
\Phi,\mathcal{C}^{n}_{\Phi}
and
d
are given, then the
stability problem
associated with (
6.1
) is as follows.

Find positive constants
c,\alpha
such that for each
K\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
, there exists
N\in C^{n}_{\Phi}
such that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\Phi(K)\geqslant cg^{\alpha}(K,N).

Let
p_{\partial K}
and
p_{\partial N}
be support functions of convex bodies
K
and
N
, respectively. Usually to measure the deviation between
K
and
N
one can use the
Hausdorff distance
,

(6.3)

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(K,N)=\max_{\theta}\Big|p_{\partial K}(\theta)-p_{\partial N}(\theta)\Big|.

Another such measure is the measure that corresponds to the
L_{2}
-metric in the function space. It is defined by

(6.4)

\displaystyle d_{2}(K,N)=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big|p_{\partial K}(\theta)-p_{\partial N}(\theta)\Big|^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is easy to see that
d_{\infty}(K,N)=0
(or
d_{2}(K,N)=0
) if and only of
K=N
.

Definition 6.1
.

[
46
]

Let
p_{{M}}
be the Minkowski support function of a positively oriented oval
{M}
of length
L_{{M}}
. Then

(6.5)

\displaystyle p_{W_{{M}}}(\theta)=\frac{L_{{M}}}{2\pi}+\frac{p_{{M}}(\theta)-p_{{M}}(\theta+\pi)}{2}

will be the support function of a curve
W_{{M}}
which will be called the
Wigner caustic type curve associated with
{M}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

[

Let

be the Wigner caustic type curve associated with an oval

(ii)

,

(iv)

and the equality holds if and only if

is a curve of constant width,

(v)

{W_{M}}={M}
if and only if
{M}

In

Theorem 6.6

(Improved isoperimetric inequality 2)
Let

\displaystyle L_{{M}}^{2}\geqslant 4\pi A_{M}+\pi\left|\widetilde{A}_{\text{CWMS}({M})}\right|,

where

The equality in (

Definition 6.7

Let
K

\displaystyle S_{K}=\frac{1}{2}(K+(-K))
is centrally symmetric convex body, where

The support function of

\displaystyle p_{\partial S_{K}}(\theta)=\vec{s}(K)+\frac{p_{\partial K}(\theta)+p_{\partial K}(\theta+\pi)}{2}

\displaystyle p_{\partial S_{K}}(\theta)=a_{0}+a_{1}\cos\theta+b_{1}\sin\theta+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=2,\\

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
K
be strictly convex domain of the area

and the perimeter

and let
\widetilde{A}_{\text{CWMS}(\partial K)}
denote the oriented area of the Constant Width Measure Set of
\partial K

be the Steiner symmetral of

(6.14)

\displaystyle L_{\partial K}^{2}-4\pi A_{\partial K}-\pi\left|\widetilde{A}_{\text{CWMS}(\partial K)}\right|\geqslant 8\pi^{2}d_{\infty}^{2}(K,S_{K}),

where equality holds if and only if
\partial K
is centrally symmetric.

Proof.

By (
2.5

2.6
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
), one can get the Fourier series of
\Phi
(see (
6.10

(6.15)

\displaystyle\Phi(K)

\displaystyle=L_{\partial K}^{2}-4\pi A_{\partial K}-8\pi\left|\widetilde{A}_{E_{\frac{1}{2}}({M})}\right|

\displaystyle=2\pi^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=3,\\
n\textit{ is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(n^{2}-1)(a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2}).

One can check that
|a_{n}\cos n\theta+b_{n}\sin n\theta|\leqslant\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2}}
and then by (
6.3
) and Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(K,S_{K})

\displaystyle=\max_{\theta}\Big|p_{\partial K}(\theta)-p_{\partial S_{K}}(\theta)\Big|

\displaystyle=\max_{\theta}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=3,\\
n\textit{ is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(a_{n}\cos n\theta+b_{n}\sin n\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{\theta}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=3,\\
n\textit{ is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}|a_{n}\cos n\theta+b_{n}\sin n\theta|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=3,\\
n\textit{ is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^{2}-1}}\cdot\sqrt{n^{2}-1}\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=3,\\
n\textit{ is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}-1}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=3,\\
n\textit{ is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(n^{2}-1)(a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\Phi(K)}{2\pi^{2}}}.

And the equality holds if and only if

for all

∎

Theorem 6.9

Under the same assumptions of Theorem

By (

n\textit{ is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(n^{2}-1)(a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2})

And the equality holds if and only if

\displaystyle p_{\partial K}(\theta)=a_{0}+a_{1}\cos\theta+b_{1}\sin\theta+a_{3}\cos 3\theta+b_{3}\sin 3\theta+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=2,\\

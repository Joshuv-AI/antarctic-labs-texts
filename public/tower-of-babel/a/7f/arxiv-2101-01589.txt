Asymptotics of a Mathieu-Gaussian series
R. B. Paris

Abstract
We consider the asymptotic expansion of the functional series S μ , γ ​ ( a , λ ) = ∑ n = 1 ∞ n γ e − λ n 2 / a 2 ( n 2 + a 2 ) μ S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\gamma}e^{-\lambda n^{2}/a^{2}}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}} for real values of the parameters γ \gamma , λ > 0 \lambda>0 and μ ≥ 0 \mu\geq 0 as | a | → ∞ |a|\to\infty in the sector | arg ⁡ a | < π / 4 |\arg\,a|<\pi/4 . For general values of γ \gamma the expansion is of algebraic type with terms involving the Riemann zeta function and a terminating confluent hypergeometric function. Of principal interest in this study is the case corresponding to even integer values of γ \gamma , where the algebraic-type expansion consists of a finite number of terms together with a contribution comprising an infinite sequence of increasingly subdominant exponentially small expansions. This situation is analogous to the well-known Poisson-Jacobi formula corresponding to the case μ = γ = 0 \mu=\gamma=0 . Numerical examples are provided to illustrate the accuracy of these expansions. 1. Introduction

The functional series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}

(1.1)

in the case
\mu=2

was introduced by Mathieu in his 1890 book
[
2
]
dealing with the elasticity of solid bodies.
The asymptotic expansion for large
a
of more general functional series has been discussed in
[
4
]
and
[
11
]
.
More recently, Gerhold and Tomovski
[
1
]
extended the asymptotic study of such Mathieu series by introducing in (
1.1
) the factor
z^{n}
, where
|z|\leq 1
. From this result they were able to deduce, in particular, the large-
a
expansions of the trigonometric Mathieu series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n\sin nx}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}},\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n\cos nx}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}.

Subsequently, the above trigonometric series were generalised to include the oscillatory Bessel functions
J_{\nu}(x)
and
Y_{\nu}(x)
with argument proportional to
n/a
, and their large-
a
asymptotics determined in
[
6
]
. In addition, this last study also considered the inclusion of the modified Bessel function
K_{\nu}(x)
of similar argument, which contains the decaying exponential as a special case.

The asymptotic expansion we consider in this paper is the Mathieu series coupled with a Gaussian exponential of the form

S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\gamma}e^{-\lambda n^{2}/a^{2}}}{(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\qquad(\mu\geq 0,\ \lambda>0)

(1.2)

for
|a|\rightarrow\infty
in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\pi/4
. It will be supposed throughout that
\gamma
is real, although the analysis is easily modified to incorporate complex
\gamma
.
We shall employ the Mellin transform approach used in
[
4
,
6
,
11
]
,
where our interest will be primarily concerned with even integer values of
\gamma
(positive or negative). We shall find that the asymptotic expansion of
S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)
with these parameter values for large complex
a
in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\pi/4
consists of a finite algebraic expansion together with an infinite sequence of increasingly subdominant exponentially small contributions.

It is interesting that the apparently simple series (
1.2
) should possess such an intricate
asymptotic structure in the case of even integer values of
\gamma
. This is also found to be the case when
\lambda=0
in (
1.2
); see
[
5
]
for details. A well-known related series corresponding to
\mu=\gamma=0
is the Poisson-Jacobi formula
[
10
, p. 124]

S_{0,0}(a;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda n^{2}/a^{2}}=\frac{a}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}-\frac{1}{2}+a\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}a^{2}/\lambda}.

(1.3)

This sum is also seen to consist of a finite algebraic contribution together with an infinite sum of exponentially small terms when
|a|\to\infty
in the sector
|\arg\,a|<\pi/4
.

In the application of the Mellin transform method to the series in (
1.2
) and its alternating variant we shall require the following estimates for the gamma function and the Riemann zeta function. For real
\sigma
and
t
, we have the estimates

\Gamma(\sigma\pm it)=O(t^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi t}),\qquad|\zeta(\sigma\pm it)|=O(t^{\Omega(\sigma)}\log^{\alpha}t)\quad(t\rightarrow+\infty),

(1.4)

where
\Omega(\sigma)=0
(
\sigma>1
),
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\sigma
(
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
),
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}-\sigma
(
\sigma<0
) and
\alpha=1
(
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
),
\alpha=0
otherwise
[
9
, p. 95]
. The zeta function
\zeta(s)
has a simple pole of unit residue at
s=1
and the evaluations for positive integer

\zeta(0)=-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},\quad\zeta(-2k)=0,\quad\zeta(2k)=\frac{(2\pi)^{2k}}{2(2k)!}|B_{2k}|\ \ (k\geq 1),

(1.5)

where
B_{k}
are the Bernoulli numbers. Finally, we have the well-known functional relation satisfied by
\zeta(s)
given by
[
3
, p. 603]

\zeta(s)=2^{s}\pi^{s-1}\zeta(1-s)\Gamma(1-s)\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi s.

(1.6)

2. An integral representation

The generalised Mathieu series defined in (
1.2
) can be written as

S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)=a^{-\delta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h(n/a),\qquad h(x):=\frac{x^{\gamma}e^{-\lambda x^{2}}}{(1+x^{2})^{\mu}},\quad\delta:=2\mu-\gamma,

(2.1)

where the parameter
\delta
is real and
\lambda>0
. We employ a Mellin transform approach as discussed in
[
7
, Section 4.1.1]
. The Mellin transform of
h(x)
is
{\cal H}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}h(x)\,dx
, where
[
3
, (13.4.4)]

{\cal H}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\gamma+s-1}e^{-\lambda x^{2}}}{(1+x^{2})^{\mu}}\,dx=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$})\,U(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$},1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$}-\mu,\lambda)

(2.2)

in the half-plane
\Re(s)>-\gamma
,
with
U(a,b,z)
being the confluent hypergeometric function of the second kind. The transform
{\cal H}(s)
can be represented alternatively in the form

{\cal H}(s)=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\{{\cal H}_{1}(s)+{\cal H}_{2}(s)\},

(2.3)

where

{\cal H}_{1}(s)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma+s}{2})\Gamma(\mu-\frac{\gamma+s}{2})}{\Gamma(\mu)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$};1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$}-\mu;\lambda),

(2.4)

{\cal H}_{2}(s)=\lambda^{\mu-(\gamma+s)/2}\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$}-\mu)\,{}_{1}F_{1}(\mu;1-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$}+\mu;\lambda).

(2.5)

Using the Mellin inversion theorem (see, for example,
[
7
, p. 118]
), we find

S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)=\frac{a^{-\delta}}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}{\cal H}(s)(n/a)^{-s}ds=\frac{a^{-\delta}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}{\cal H}(s)\zeta(s)a^{s}ds,

(2.6)

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function and
c>\max\{1,-\gamma\}
. The inversion of the order of summation and integration is justified by absolute convergence provided
c
satisfies this condition.
From the estimates in (
1.4
) and the fact that the confluent hypergeometric functions

The expansion (

\mu>0

\gamma

\gamma

Finally, we note that when
\gamma=2p

We shall show that, in addition to a finite algebraic contribution, there is a sequence of increasingly subdominant exponentially small terms in the large-

1.3

3. The exponentially small contribution to
S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)
when
\gamma=2p

Let
\gamma=2p
be an even integer and
\mu\geq 0
. Then the quantity
\delta
defined in (
2.1
) is
\delta=2(\mu-p)
. The number of poles of the sequence
s=-2k-2p
is finite (when
p\leq 0
) or zero (when
p\geq 1
) on account of the trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
. Then we have upon displacement of the integration path

S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)=a^{1-\delta}{\cal H}(1)+H_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)+J(a;\lambda),

(3.1)

where

H_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&(p\geq 1)\\
-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}a^{-\delta}&(p=0)\\
a^{-\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{|p|}\zeta(2k)R_{k}(\mu,|p|)a^{2k}&(p\leq-1).\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

The quantity
R_{k}(\mu,|p|)
denotes the residue of
{\cal H}(s)
at
s=2k
,
0\leq k\leq|p|
when
p\leq-1
given by

R_{k}(\mu,|p|)=\frac{(-)^{q-k}}{(q-k)!}\,U(k-q,1+k-q-\mu,\lambda)\qquad(q=-p,\ 0\leq k\leq q).

Routine calculations show that when
p=-1,-2
, for example, we have

\displaystyle R_{0}(\mu,1)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-(\mu+\lambda),\quad R_{1}(\mu,1)=1,

\displaystyle R_{0}(\mu,2)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\mu(1+\mu)+\mu\lambda+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\lambda^{2},\quad R_{1}(\mu,2)=-(\mu+\lambda),\quad R_{2}(\mu,2)=1.

The integral
J(a;\lambda)
appearing in (
3.1
) is defined by

J(a;\lambda)=\frac{a^{-\delta}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}{\cal H}(-s)\zeta(-s)a^{-s}ds\qquad(c>0),

where we have replaced
s
by
-s
. Use of the functional relation for
\zeta(s)
in (
1.6
) followed by expansion of
\zeta(1+s)
(permissible since
c>0
) leads to

\displaystyle J(a;\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a^{-\delta}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}{\cal H}(-s)\zeta(1+s)\Gamma(1+s)\frac{\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi s}{\pi}\,(2\pi a)^{-s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a^{-\delta}}{\pi}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}{\cal H}(-s)\Gamma(1+s)\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi s\,(2\pi ka)^{-s}ds.

From (
2.2
) and an application of Kummer’s transformation
[
3

we have

{\cal H}(-s)=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\Gamma(p-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)\,\lambda^{\mu-p+s/2}U(\mu,1+\mu-p+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\lambda),

whence

J(a;\lambda)=\frac{(-)^{p}a^{-\delta}}{2\lambda^{p-\mu}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\frac{2^{-s}\Gamma(1+s)}{\Gamma(1-p+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}\,X_{k}^{-s/2}\,U(\mu,1\!+\!\mu\!-\!p\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\lambda)\,ds,

(3.3)

where

X_{k}:=\frac{\pi^{2}k^{2}a^{2}}{\lambda}.

(3.4)

We first consider the case
p=0,1,2,\ldots\,
. Since there are no poles of the integrand in (
3.3
) in
\Re(s)>0
the integration path can be displaced as far to the right as we please such that

is everywhere large on the new path. The quotient of gamma functions in the integrand can then be expanded as

[
3

\frac{2^{-s}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1+s)}{\Gamma(1\!-\!p\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}=\frac{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)\Gamma(1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}{\Gamma(1\!-\!p\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-)^{j}c_{j}\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s+\vartheta-j),

(3.5)

where
\vartheta=p+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
. The coefficients are given explicitly by

c_{j}=\frac{1}{j!}(-p)_{j}(-p+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{j}=\frac{(-2p)_{2j}}{2^{2j}j!}.

(3.6)

We observe that

for

so that the above sum of gamma functions terminates and so is

.
Substitution of the expansion (

) in (

\hskip 85.35826pt\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda t}t^{\mu-1}}{(1+t)^{p}}\biggl(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s+\vartheta-j)\biggl(\frac{X_{k}}{1+t}\biggr)^{\!-s/2}ds\biggr)dt.

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\Gamma(s+\alpha)z^{-s}ds=z^{\alpha}e^{-z}\qquad(|\arg\,z|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi),

Theorem 1

\!\!\!.

\displaystyle\frac{(-)^{m-p}\pi e^{\lambda}}{\Gamma(m)\,\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi s}\,\frac{\Gamma(p-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}{\Gamma(1-m+p-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(1-m;1-m+p-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s;-\lambda)

\displaystyle\frac{(-)^{m-p}\pi e^{\lambda}}{\Gamma(m)\,\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi s}\,\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\frac{(1-m)_{r}(-\lambda)^{r}}{r!}\,\frac{\Gamma(p-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}{\Gamma(1-m+p-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s+r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-)^{p-1}\pi e^{\lambda}}{\Gamma(m)\,\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi s}\,\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\frac{(1-m)_{r}\lambda^{r}}{r!}\,\frac{\Gamma(m-p-r+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}{\Gamma(1-p+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}.

(4.1)

The Pochhammer symbol appearing in (

B_{03}=(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}-p)_{3},\quad B_{13}=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{8}}$}(5-10p+4p^{2}),\quad B_{23}=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}$}(1-p),\quad B_{33}=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{8}}$},\ \ldots\ .

Then we obtain

poles at
s=\pm 2k+\delta
,
\delta=2\mu-\gamma
. We shall show in this appendix that
{\cal H}(s)
is regular at these last points. We have

{\cal H}(s)=\frac{\pi G(s)}{2\sin\pi(\mu-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$})},

where

G(s):=\frac{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$})}{\Gamma(\mu)}{\bf F}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$};1\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$}\!-\!\mu;\lambda)-\lambda^{\mu-(\gamma+s)/2}{\bf F}(\mu;1\!-\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{\gamma+s}{2}}$}\!+\!\mu;\lambda).

Here
{\bf F}
denotes the normalised confluent hypergeometric function defined by

{\bf F}(a;b,z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(b)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(a;b;z),

which is defined for all values of the parameter
b
.

Let
s_{k}=2k+\delta
so that
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(\gamma+s_{k})=k+\mu
. Then

\displaystyle G(s_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu)_{k}{\bf F}(\mu+k;k+1;\lambda)-\lambda^{-k}\sum_{r=k}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}\lambda^{r}}{r!\Gamma(1+r-k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu)_{k}{\bf F}(\mu+k;k+1;\lambda)-\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r+k}\lambda^{r}}{r!\Gamma(1+k+r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu)_{k}{\bf F}(\mu+k;k+1;\lambda)-(\mu)_{k}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu+k)_{r}\lambda^{r}}{r!\Gamma(1+k+r)}\equiv 0.

Hence
{\cal H}(s)
is regular at
s_{k}=2k+\delta
.

A similar argument when
s_{k}=-2k+\delta
shows that

\displaystyle G(s_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\mu-k)}{\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{r=k}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu-k)_{r}\lambda^{r}}{r!\Gamma(1-k+r)}-\lambda^{k}{\bf F}(\mu;1+k;\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\mu-k)}{\Gamma(\mu)}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu-k)_{r+k}\lambda^{r+k}}{r!\Gamma(1+k+r)}-\lambda^{k}{\bf F}(\mu;1+k;\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{k}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu)_{r}\lambda^{r}}{r!\Gamma(1+k+r)}-\lambda^{k}{\bf F}(\mu;1+k;\lambda)\equiv 0,

so that
{\cal H}(s)
is also regular at the points
s_{k}=-2k+\delta
.

Appendix B: The expansion in the case
\gamma=-1

We consider the large-
a
expansion of
S_{\mu,\gamma}(a;\lambda)
given in (
2.7
) in the special case
\gamma=-1
when the singularity of the integrand in (
2.6
) at
s=1
is a double pole.
We set
s=1+\epsilon
, with
\epsilon\to 0
. Then

{\cal H}_{1}(s)=\frac{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon)\Gamma(\mu-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon)}{2\Gamma(\mu)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;1-\mu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;\lambda)

=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\biggl\{1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon(\psi(1)-\psi(\mu))+O(\epsilon^{2})\biggr\}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;1-\mu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;\lambda),

where

{}_{1}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;1-\mu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;\lambda)=1+\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2(1-\mu)}\biggl\{1+\frac{1!\lambda}{(2-\mu)2!}+\frac{2!\lambda^{2}}{(2-\mu)_{2}3!}+\cdots\biggr\}+O(\epsilon^{2})

=1+\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2(1-\mu)}\,{}_{2}F_{2}(1,1;2,2-\mu;\lambda)+O(\epsilon^{2}).

Using the fact that
\zeta(1+\epsilon)=\epsilon^{-1}(1+\gamma_{{}_{E}}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{2}))
and
\psi(1)=-\gamma_{{}_{E}}
, where
\gamma_{{}_{E}}
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant,
we obtain the residue resulting from
{\cal H}_{1}(s)
at
s=1
given by

a\biggl\{\log\,a+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{{}_{E}}-\frac{1}{2}\psi(\mu)+\frac{\lambda}{2(1-\mu)}\,{}_{2}F_{2}(1,1;2,2-\mu;\lambda)\biggr\}\qquad(\mu\neq 1,2,\ldots).

The residue resulting from
{\cal H}_{2}(s)
is

a{\cal H}_{2}(1)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{\mu}\Gamma(1-\mu)\,{}_{1}F_{1}(\mu;1+\mu;\lambda)\qquad(\mu\neq 1,2,\ldots).

Hence, provided
\mu\neq 1,2,\ldots\
,

S_{\mu,-1}(a;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda n^{2}/a^{2}}}{n(n^{2}+a^{2})^{\mu}}\sim a^{1-\delta}\biggl\{\log\,a+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{{}_{E}}-\frac{1}{2}\psi(\mu)+\frac{\lambda}{2(1-\mu)}\,{}_{2}F_{2}(1,1;2,2-\mu;\lambda)\biggr\}

+a^{1-\delta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\zeta(1-2k)\,{}_{1}F_{1}(-k;1-\mu-k;\lambda)a^{-2k}

(B.1)

as
a\to\infty
in
|\arg\,a|<\pi/4
.

When
\mu
is a positive integer a limiting process is required. To illustrate, we consider only the case
\mu=1
.
We find that
{\cal H}_{1}(1+\epsilon)=e^{\lambda}/\epsilon
and

{\cal H}_{2}(1+\epsilon)=-\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon(1-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon)}\Gamma(1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon)\,{}_{1}F_{1}(1;2-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;\lambda)

=-\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon}\biggl\{1+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(1-\gamma_{{}_{E}}-\log\,\lambda)+O(\epsilon^{2})\biggr\}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(1;2-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;\lambda),

where

\displaystyle{}_{1}F_{1}(1;2-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\epsilon;\lambda)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!1\!+\!\frac{\lambda}{2}\biggl(1\!+\!\frac{\epsilon}{4}\biggr)\!+\!\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\cdot 3}\biggl(1\!+\!\frac{\epsilon}{4}\!+\!\frac{\epsilon}{6}\biggr)+\cdots+O(\epsilon^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!{}_{1}F_{1}(1;2;\lambda)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\biggl\{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\cdot 3}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\frac{1}{3}\biggr)\!+\!\frac{\lambda^{3}}{2\cdot 3\cdot 4}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\frac{1}{3}\!+\!\frac{1}{4}\biggr)\!+\!\cdots\biggr\}+O(\epsilon^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\frac{e^{\lambda}-1}{\lambda}\!+\!\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}\tau(n)}{(2)_{n}}+O(\epsilon^{2}),\qquad\tau(n):=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r+1}.

Then we obtain the expansion

S_{1,-1}(a;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda n^{2}/a^{2}}}{n(n^{2}+a^{2})}\sim\frac{a^{1-\delta}}{2}\biggl\{e^{\lambda}(\log\,\lambda+\gamma_{{}_{E}}-1)+\log\,(a^{2}/\lambda)+\gamma_{{}_{E}}+1-\lambda\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}\tau(n)}{(2)_{n}}\biggr\}

+a^{1-\delta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mu)_{k}}{k!}\zeta(1-2k)e_{k}(\lambda)a^{-2k}

(B.2)

as
a\to\infty
in
|\arg\,a|<\pi/4
, where

e_{k}(\lambda):={}_{1}F_{1}(-k;-k;\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}.

Abstract
. Keller and Lenz [ 10 ] define a concept of stochastic completeness at infinity (SCI) for a regular symmetric Dirichlet form ( ℰ , ℱ ) ({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}) . We show that (SCI) can be characterized probabilistically by using the predictable part ζ p \zeta^{p} of the life time ζ \zeta of the symmetric Markov process X = ( 𝐏 x , X t ) X=({\bf P}_{x},X_{t}) generated by ( ℰ , ℱ ) ({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}) , that is, (SCI) is equivalent to 𝐏 x ​ ( ζ = ζ p < ∞ ) = 0 {\mathbf{P}}_{x}(\zeta=\zeta^{p}<\infty)=0 . We define a concept, explosion by killing (EK), by 𝐏 x ​ ( ζ = ζ i < ∞ ) = 1 {\mathbf{P}}_{x}(\zeta=\zeta^{i}<\infty)=1 . Here ζ i \zeta^{i} is the totally inaccessible part of ζ \zeta . We see that (EK) is equivalent to (SCI) and 𝐏 x ​ ( ζ = ∞ ) = 1 {\mathbf{P}}_{x}(\zeta=\infty)=1 . Let X res X^{\rm res} be the resurrected process generated by the resurrected form , a regular Dirichlet form constructed by removing the killing part from ( ℰ , ℱ ) ({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}) . Extending work of Masamune and Schmidt ( [ 15 ] ), we show that (EK) is also equivalent to the ordinary conservation property of time changed process of X res X^{\rm res} by A t k A^{k}_{t} , where the A t k A^{k}_{t} is the positive continuous additive functional in the Revuz correspondence to the killing measure k k in the Beurling-Deny formula (Theorem 2.10 ). We consider the maximum principle for Schrödinger-type operator ℒ μ = ℒ − μ {\mathcal{L}}^{\mu}={\mathcal{L}}-\mu . Here ℒ {\mathcal{L}} is the self-adjoint operator associated with ( ℰ , ℱ ) ({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}) and μ \mu is a Green-tight Kato measure. Let λ ⁡ ( μ ) \lambda(\mu) be the principal eigenvalue of the trace of ( ℰ , ℱ ) ({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}) relative to μ \mu . We prove that if (EK) holds, then λ ⁡ ( μ ) > 1 \lambda(\mu)>1 implies a Liouville property that every bounded solution to ℒ μ ​ u = 0 {\mathcal{L}}^{\mu}u=0 is zero quasi-everywhere and that the refined maximum principle in the sense of Berestycki-Nirenberg-Varadhan [ 2 ] holds for ℒ μ {\mathcal{L}}^{\mu} if and only if λ ⁡ ( μ ) > 1 \lambda(\mu)>1 (Theorem 4.9 ). .

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{F}}\cap C_{0}(E)
. Let

and

a relatively compact open set containing

. Let

be a sequence in

such that

on

\psi_{n}\uparrow 1

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(h\psi_{n},\varphi)={\mathcal{E}}(h,\varphi).

Proof.

On account of
h\psi_{n}=h
on
G
, we see from Beurling-Deny formula
[
9
, Theorem 3.2.1]
that

{\mathcal{E}}(h\psi_{n},\varphi)
is equal to

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{E}d\mu_{\langle h,\varphi\rangle}^{c}+\iint_{K\times K}(h(x)-h(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))J(dx,dy)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt+2\iint_{K\times(K^{c}\cap G)}(h(x)-h(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))J(dx,dy)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt+2\iint_{K\times G^{c}}(h(x)-h(y)\psi_{n}(y))\varphi(x)\,J(dx,dy)+\int_{E}h\varphi\,d\kappa.

Since

\int_{E}\varphi(x)d\mu^{j}_{\langle h\rangle}(x)<\infty,

we see

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ |(h(x)-h(y)\psi_{n}(y))\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|h(x)-h(x)\psi_{n}(y)|\varphi(x)+|h(x)-h(y)||\psi_{n}(y)\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|h(x)|\varphi(x)1_{G^{c}}(y)+|h(x)-h(y)|\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(K\times G^{c};J).

Hence by the
dominated convergence theorem, the fourth term of (

\displaystyle 2\iint_{K\times G^{c}}(h(x)-h(y))\varphi(x)J(dx,dy)

\displaystyle\qquad=2\iint_{K\times G^{c}}(h(x)-h(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))J(dx,dy)

∎

\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\mu,\rm sub}
,

\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\mu,\rm sup}
) under the condition
\lambda(\mu)>1
.

Lemma 4.11
.

For
\mu\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
define

(4.14)

\Lambda(\theta)=\inf\left\{{\mathcal{E}}(u)\,\Big|\,\int_{E}u^{2}d(\mu+\theta m)=1\right\},\ \theta\geq 0.

If the basic measure
m
also belongs to
{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
,
then
\Lambda(\theta)
is continuous.

Proof.

We know from
[
24
, Theorem 4.8]
that for
\nu\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}

the extended Dirichlet space
({\mathcal{F}}_{e},{\mathcal{E}})

is compactly embedded in

L^{2}(E;\nu)
. Hence, for
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\geq 0
there exist functions
u_{1},u_{2}
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{e}
such that

\displaystyle\Lambda(\theta_{1})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}(u_{1}),\ \ \ \int_{E}u_{1}^{2}d(\mu+\theta_{1}m)=1,

\displaystyle\Lambda(\theta_{2})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}(u_{2}),\ \ \ \int_{E}u_{2}^{2}d(\mu+\theta_{2}m)=1.

Put

\displaystyle k_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}u_{1}^{2}d(\mu+\theta_{2}m)=1+(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\int_{E}u_{1}^{2}dm,

\displaystyle k_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}u_{2}^{2}d(\mu+\theta_{1}m)=1+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\int_{E}u_{2}^{2}dm,

and
v_{1}=u_{1}/\sqrt{k_{1}}
,
v_{2}=u_{2}/\sqrt{k_{2}}
.
We then have

\Lambda(\theta_{1})\leq{\mathcal{E}}(v_{2})=\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Lambda(\theta_{2}),\ \ \Lambda(\theta_{2})\leq{\mathcal{E}}(v_{1})=\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Lambda(\theta_{1}),

and thus

k_{1}\Lambda(\theta_{2})\leq\Lambda(\theta_{1})\leq\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Lambda(\theta_{2}).\ \

Therefore, noting that
\lim_{\theta_{2}\to\theta_{1}}k_{1}=1
and
\lim_{\theta_{2}\to\theta_{1}}k_{2}=1
, we have

\varlimsup_{\theta_{2}\to\theta_{1}}\Lambda(\theta_{2})\leq\Lambda(\theta_{1})\leq\varliminf_{\theta_{2}\to\theta_{1}}\Lambda(\theta_{2}),

which implies the continuity of
\Lambda(\theta)
.
∎

Write
\gamma(\mu)
for the principal eigenvalue of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mu}
:

(4.15)

\gamma(\mu)=\inf\left\{{\mathcal{E}}^{\mu}(u)\,\Big|\ u\in{\mathcal{F}},\ \int_{E}u^{2}dm=1\right\}.

Theorem 4.12
.

If
m
and
\mu
are in
{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
, then

(4.16)

\lambda(\mu)>1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \gamma(\mu)>0.

Proof.

If
\lambda(\mu)>1
, there exists
\theta_{0}>0
such that

\inf\left\{{\mathcal{E}}(u)\,\Big|\,\int_{E}u^{2}d(\mu+\theta_{0}m)=1\right\}=1.

Indeed,
denote by
u_{\theta}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{e}
the function attaining the infimum in (
4.14
),
and put
k=\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}dm,\ v_{\theta}=u_{0}/\sqrt{1+k\theta}
. Note that

\int_{E}v_{\theta}^{2}d(\mu+\theta m)=\frac{1}{1+k\theta}\left(\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}d\mu+\theta\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}dm\right)=1.

Then
since

\Lambda(\theta)={\mathcal{E}}(u_{\theta})\leq{\mathcal{E}}(v_{\theta})=\frac{1}{1+k\theta}{\mathcal{E}}(u_{0})=\frac{1}{1+k\theta}\Lambda(0),

we have
\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\Lambda(\theta)=0
. If
\Lambda(0)=\lambda(\mu)>1
, then
there exists
\theta_{0}
such that
\Lambda(\theta_{0})=1
by Lemma
4.11
.

For
\varphi\in{\mathcal{F}}\cap C_{0}(E)
define

G(t)=\frac{{\mathcal{E}}(u_{\theta_{0}}+t\varphi)}{\int_{E}(u_{\theta_{0}}+t\varphi)^{2}d(\mu+\theta_{0}m)}.

Then
G^{\prime}(0)=0
and thus

{\mathcal{E}}(u_{\theta_{0}},\varphi)-\int_{E}u_{\theta_{0}}\varphi d(\mu+\theta_{0}m)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ {\mathcal{E}}^{\mu}(u_{\theta_{0}},\varphi)=\theta_{0}\int_{E}u_{\theta_{0}}\varphi dm.

Hence
\theta_{0}
equals
\gamma(\mu)
, the principal eigenvalue
of the Schrödinger operator
-{\mathcal{L}}^{\mu}
, and
v_{0}=u_{\theta_{0}}/\|u_{\theta_{0}}\|_{m}

is the normalized principal eigenfunction. Hence

\gamma(\mu)={\mathcal{E}}(v_{0})-\int_{E}v_{0}^{2}d\mu\geq(\lambda(\mu)-1)\int_{E}v_{0}^{2}d\mu>0.

Suppose
\gamma(\mu)>0
and let
u_{0}
be the function attaining
\lambda(\mu)
. Then

{\mathcal{E}}(u_{0})-\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}d\mu-\gamma(\mu)\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}dm\geq 0,

and thus

\lambda(\mu)=\frac{{\mathcal{E}}(u_{0})}{\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}d\mu}\geq 1+\gamma(\mu)\frac{\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}dm}{\int_{E}u_{0}^{2}d\mu}>1.

∎

Remark 4.13
.

We suppose that the basic measure
m
is finite and, in addition, that the operator norm
\|p_{t}\|_{1,\infty}
of
the semi-group
p_{t}

is bounded by
C(t)(<\infty)
such that
\int_{\delta}^{\infty}C(t)dt<\infty
for any
\delta>0
.
Here
\|\cdot\|_{1,\infty}
is the operator norm from
L^{1}(E;m)
to
L^{\infty}(E;m)
.
Then for a compact set
K

\displaystyle R1_{E\setminus K}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta}p_{t}1_{E\setminus K}(x)dt+\int_{\delta}^{\infty}p_{t}1_{E\setminus K}(x)dt

\displaystyle=\delta+\left(\int_{\delta}^{\infty}C(t)dt\right)m(E\setminus K).

Hence, for any
\epsilon
there exists
\delta>0
and a compact set
K
such that
the right hand side above is less than
\epsilon
, which implies
m\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
.
In particular, for a uniformly elliptic self-adjoint operator
{\mathcal{L}}
of form

{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(a_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)

in a bounded domain
D\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{d}
, the Lebesgue measure is in
{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
. In this case,
for
\mu\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
,
\gamma(\mu)>0
is equivalent with
\lambda(\mu)>1
.

In general,
\gamma(\mu)>0
implies
\lambda(\mu)>1
; however, the converse does not always
hold (cf.

[
23
, Remark 3.1]
).

We set

{{\mathbb{S}}}^{\prime}=\left\{\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset E\mid\text{For any compact set $K$, $\exists N$ s.t. $x_{n}\not\in K$ $\forall n>N$}\right\}

and put

(4.17)

\partial^{M}h=\inf_{K\in{\mathcal{K}}}\sup_{E\setminus K}h(x),

where
{\mathcal{K}}
is the totality of compact sets of
E
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Suppose that
X
satisfies
({\rm{I}})
,
({\rm{SF}})
and let
\mu\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
with
\lambda(\mu)>1
.
Then for
h\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\mu,\rm sub}\cap C(E)

\partial^{M}h\leq 0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sup_{x\in E}h(x)\leq 0.

In particular, if
\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}h(x_{n})\leq 0\ \text{for any}\ \{x_{n}\}\in{\mathbb{S}}
, then
\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}h(x_{n})\leq 0\ \text{for any}\ \{x_{n}\}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It holds that

\displaystyle\partial^{M}h\leq 0

\displaystyle\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \varlimsup_{n\to\infty}h(x_{n})\leq 0\ \text{for any}\ \{x_{n}\}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ \Longrightarrow\ \varlimsup_{n\to\infty}h(x_{n})\leq 0\ \text{for any}\ \{x_{n}\}\in{\mathbb{S}}

\displaystyle\ \Longrightarrow\ \sup_{x\in E}h(x)\leq 0\ \Longrightarrow\partial^{M}h\leq 0.

∎

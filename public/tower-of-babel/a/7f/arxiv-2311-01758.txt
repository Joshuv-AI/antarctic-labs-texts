On error bounds and optimality conditions at infinity
NGUYEN VAN TUYEN 1

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish sufficient conditions for the existence of error bounds at infinity for lower semicontinuous inequality systems. We also show that the existence of an error bound at infinity of constraint systems plays an important role in deriving necessary optimality conditions at infinity for constrained optimization problems.

49K40, 90C26, 49J52, 90C46

1.
Introduction

Consider the inequality system

(1)

S:=\{x\in\Omega\subset X\;:\;g(x)\leq 0\}

where
X
is a Banach space,
\Omega
is a nonempty and closed subset in
X
, and
g\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a lower semicontinuous function.

We say that
S
has a local error bound at a point
\bar{x}\in S
if there
\tau>0
and a neighborhood
V
of
\bar{x}
such that

(2)

d(x;S)\leq\tau[g(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\cap V,

where
d(x;S)
denotes the distance from
x
to
S
and
[g(x)]_{+}:=\max\{g(x),0\}
. If we can choose
V=X
in (
2
), then we say that
S
has a global/Hoffman error bound.

The concept of error bound plays a center role in many fields of mathematical programming theory including, for example, in analyzing the convergence of algorithms, in studying optimality conditions, stability, sensitivity, subdifferential calculus, and so on. Today, the literature on error bounds is rich and there are many conditions that ensure the existence of error bounds, see, for example
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
23
,
24
,
27
,
28
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, the first result on global error bounds was established by Hoffman
[
8
]
. The author proved that if
g
is a maximum of a finite number of affine functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
S
has a global error bound. In
[
24
]
, Robinson extended the Hoffman’s result to bounded convex differentiable inequality systems in infinite dimensional spaces. The existence of global error bound for unbounded convex inequality systems in reflexive Banach spaces was studied by Deng
[
2
]
. In
[
9
]
, Ioffe presented a sufficient condition
for the existence of a local error bound for nonconvex and nondifferentiable Lipschitz continuous equality system in infinite dimension. The relationship between the existence of local error bounds and Abadie constraint qualification conditions for convex inequality systems can be found in
[
10
,
17
,
18
,
27
,
28
]
. Regarding to the existence of Hölder error bounds, we refer the readers to references
[
3
,
6
,
12
,
14
,
20
,
26
]
.

In mathematical programming, there are numerous problems where the objective function is bounded from below but nevertheless fails to attain its minimum at any finite feasible solution; see, for example,
[
4
]
. When this happens, the Fermat’s rule cannot be applied. However, there exists a sequence of feasible solutions tending to infinity such that the corresponding sequence of objective values tends to its infimum. Therefore, optimality conditions in this case must somehow be taken
“at infinity”
. To do this, very recently, Nguyen and Pham
[
19
]
introduced the notions of Clarke’s tangent, normal cones, subgradients, Lipschitz continuity, …, at infinity and established necessary optimality conditions at infinity. Thereafter, Kim, Nguyen and Pham
[
11
]
proposed the concepts of limiting normal cones at infinity to unbounded sets as well as limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity and then they derived necessary optimality conditions as well as sufficient conditions for the weak sharp minima property at infinity. To obtain necessary optimality conditions at infinity, the authors used a constraint qualification of Mangasarian–Fromovitz type at infinity for Lipschitz at infinity functions. We note here that both assumptions on the Lipschitzness and the Mangasarian–Fromovitz constraint qualification at infinity are rather strict.

To the best of our knowledge, so far there have been no papers studying the concept of error bound at infinity. In this paper, we introduce the concept of error bound at infinity for general constraint systems and derive sufficient conditions for such ones have an error bound at infinity. Then we show that the existence of an error bound at infinity is essential to derive upper estimate for the normal cone at infinity of constraint systems. As a byproduct, we obtain new necessary optimality conditions at infinity for problems which do not attain its minimum.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains some definitions and preliminary results from variational analysis and generalized differentiation. In Section
3
, we derive sufficient conditions for error bounds at infinity. Section
4
is devoted to upper estimates of the normal cone of constraint systems and optimality conditions at infinity.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some notions related to generalized differentiation from
[
11
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
25
]
. The space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equipped with the usual scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the corresponding Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
. The closed unit ball and the nonnegative orthant in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are denoted, respectively, by
\mathbb{B}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. The convex hull of a set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the Euclidean projector of a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
D
are denoted, respectively, by
\mathrm{co}\,D
and
\Pi_{D}(x)
. As usual, we denote
[\alpha]_{+}:=\max\,\{\alpha,0\}
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, and
d(x;D)
is the distance of a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
D
, that is,

d(x;D):=\inf\{\|y-x\|\;:\;y\in D\}.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping. The
domain
and the
graph
of
F
are given, respectively, by

{\rm dom}\,F=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid F(x)\not=\emptyset\}

and

{\rm gph}\,F=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid y\in F(x)\}.

The set-valued mapping is called
F

proper
if
\mbox{\rm dom}\,F\not=\emptyset.
The
Painlevé-Kuratowski outer/upper limit
of
F
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x):=\bigg\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\exists x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},y_{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ y_{k}\in F(x_{k}),\forall k=1,2,....\bigg\}.

2.1.
Normal cones and Subdifferentials

Definition 2.1

(see
[
15
,
16
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
.

(i)

The
regular/Fréchet normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x};\Omega)=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\limsup\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\right\},

where
x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
x\in\Omega
.

(ii)

The
limiting/Mordukhovich normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is given by

\displaystyle N(\bar{x};\Omega)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}(x;\Omega).

When
\bar{x}\not\in\Omega
, we put
\widehat{N}(\bar{x};\Omega)=N(\bar{x};\Omega)=\emptyset
.

By definition, it is clear that

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(x;\Omega)\subset N(x;\Omega),\ \ \ \forall x\in\Omega.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an extend real-valued function. The
effective domain
and the
epigraph
of
f
are denoted, respectively, by

\mbox{dom}\,f:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid f(x)<+\infty\}

and

{\rm{epi}}\,f:=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid\alpha\geq f(x)\}.

We say that
f
is
proper
if its
\mathrm{dom}f
is nonempty.

Definition 2.2

(see
[
15
,
16
]
)
.

Consider a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}f
.

(i)

The
regular/Fréchet subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathrm{epi}f)\}.

(ii)

The
limiting/Mordukovich subdifferential
and the
limiting/Mordukovich singular subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
are defined, respectively, by

\partial f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in{N}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathrm{epi}f)\},

and

\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists v_{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}),\lambda_{k}\downarrow 0,\lambda_{k}v_{k}\to v\}.

It is well-known that

\partial f(\bar{x})=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x}}\widehat{\partial}f(x)\supseteq\widehat{\partial}f(x),

where
x\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x}
means that
x\to\bar{x}
and
f(x)\to f(\bar{x})
. When the function
f
is convex, then the subdifferentials
\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
and
\partial f(\bar{x})
coincide with the subdifferential in the sense of convex analysis.

For the singular subdifferential, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x})\subseteq\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,0)\in N((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathrm{epi}f)\},

and the inclusion holds with equality whenever
f
is locally lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}
; see
[
25
, Theorem 8.9]
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
indicator function

\delta_{\Omega}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of
\Omega
is defined by

\delta_{\Omega}(x):=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{ if }x\in\Omega,\\
+\infty&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

It holds that
\partial\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(x)=N(x;\Omega)
for any
x\in\Omega
; see
[
15
,
16
,
25
]
.

The following result is the nonsmooth versions of Fermat’s rule.

Lemma 2.1

(see
[
15
, Proposition 1.114]
)
.

If a proper function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
has a local minimum at
\bar{x},
then
0\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})\subset\partial f(\bar{x}).

The next result gives sum rules for limiting and singular subgradients of extended-real-valued functions.

Lemma 2.2

(see
[
15
, Theorem 3.36]
)
.

Let
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
,
m\geqq 2
, be l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
and let all but one of these functions be locally Lipschitz around
\bar{x}
. Then we have the following inclusions

\displaystyle\partial(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m})(\bar{x})\subset\partial f_{1}(\bar{x})+\ldots+\partial f_{m}(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m})(\bar{x})\subset\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(\bar{x})+\ldots+\partial^{\infty}f_{m}(\bar{x}).

We now recall the Ekeland variational principle (see
[
5
]
).

Lemma 2.3

(Ekeland variational principle)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper l.s.c. function and bounded from below. Let
\epsilon>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be satisfied

\displaystyle f(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+\epsilon.

Then, for any
\lambda>0
there exists
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(i)

f(x_{1})\leq f(x_{0}),

(ii)

\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|\leq\lambda,
and

(iii)

f(x_{1})\leq f(x)+\dfrac{\epsilon}{\lambda}\|x-x_{1}\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

2.2.
Normal cones and subdifferentials at infinity

Let
\Omega
be a
locally closed
subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., for any
x\in\Omega
there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
such that
\Omega\cap U
is closed. Assume that
\Omega
is unbounded.

Definition 2.3

(see
[
11
]
)
.

The
norm cone to the set
\Omega
at infinity
is defined by

\displaystyle N(\infty;\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty}\widehat{N}(x;\Omega).

The following result is the intersection rule for
normals at infinity.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
11
, Proposition 3.7]
)
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
be locally closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the normal qualification condition at infinity

\displaystyle N(\infty;\Omega_{1})\cap\big(-N(\infty;\Omega_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{0\}.

Then we have the inclusion

\displaystyle N(\infty;\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle N(\infty;\Omega_{1})+N(\infty;\Omega_{2}).

Now let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a l.s.c. function and assume that
f
is
proper at infinity
in the sense that the set
\mathrm{dom}f
is unbounded.

Definition 2.4

(see
[
11
]
)
.

The
limiting/Mordukhovich and the singular subdifferentials
of
f
at infinity are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ (u,-1)\in\mathcal{N}\},

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ (u,0)\in\mathcal{N}\},

where
\mathcal{N}:=\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}{N}((x,f(x));\textrm{epi}f).

The following result gives limiting representations of limiting and singular subgradients at infinity.

Proposition 2.2

(see
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
)
.

The following relationships hold

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\partial f(x)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\widehat{\partial}f(x),

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty,r\downarrow 0}r\partial f(x)\supseteq\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\partial^{\infty}f(x).

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an unbounded and closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then it follows from Proposition
2.2
and
[
16
, Proposition 1.19]
that

\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\infty)=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(\infty)=N(\infty;\Omega).

We now recall the notion of the Lipschitz property at infinity for l.s.c. functions (see
[
11
,
19
]
).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a l.s.c function. We say that
f
is
Lipschitz at infinity
if there exist constants
L>0
and
R>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\|x-x^{\prime}\|\ \ \textrm{ for all }\ \ x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

The following result gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the Lipschitz property at infinity of l.s.c. functions.

Proposition 2.3

(see
[
11
, Proposition 5.2]
)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a l.s.c. function. Then
f
is Lipschitz at infinity if and only if
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)=\{0\}.
In this case,
\partial f(\infty)
is nonempty compact.

The next results present calculus rules for both basic and singular subdifferentials at infinity. These results obtained from
[
11
]
by induction.

Proposition 2.4

(cf.
[
11
, Proposition 4.9]
)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. functions such that the following qualification condition holds

(3)

\left[u_{1}+\ldots+u_{m}=0,u_{i}\in\partial^{\infty}f_{i}(\infty)\right]\Rightarrow u_{i}=0,i=1,\ldots,m,

Then we have

\displaystyle\partial(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m})(\infty)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\partial f_{1}(\infty)+\ldots+\partial f_{m}(\infty),

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m})(\infty)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(\infty)+\ldots+\partial^{\infty}f_{m}(\infty).

Proposition 2.5

(cf.
[
11
, Proposition 4.11]
)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. functions such that the qualification condition (
3
) is satisfied. Then one has the inclusions

\displaystyle\partial(\max\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\})(\infty)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\bigcup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\circ\partial f_{i}(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\in\Delta_{m}\right\},

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}(\max\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\})(\infty)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial^{\infty}f_{i}(\infty),

where
\Delta_{m}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\}
and
\lambda_{i}\circ\partial f_{i}(\infty)
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, are defined as follow

\lambda_{i}\circ\partial f_{i}(\infty)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(\infty)\ \ &\text{if}\ \ \lambda_{i}>0,\\
\partial^{\infty}f_{i}(\infty)\ \ &\text{if}\ \ \lambda_{i}=0.\end{cases}

Remark 2.2
.

By Proposition
2.3
, the qualification condition (
3
) holds if all but one of functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
are Lipschitz at infinity.

Proposition 2.6

(cf.
[
11
, Proposition 4.12]
)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. functions. Then we have

\partial(\min\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\})(\infty)\subset\bigcup\{\partial f_{i}(\infty)\;:\;i=1,\ldots,m\}.

3.
Sufficient conditions for error bounds at infinity

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty and closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a l.s.c. function. Consider the constraint set

(4)

S:=\{x\in\Omega\;:\;g(x)\leq 0\}.

Assume that
\Omega\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,g
is nonempty and unbounded.

Our main purpose in this section is to derive sufficient conditions for the existence of error bounds at infinity for the constraint set
S
in (
4
).

Definition 3.1
.

We say that the constraint set
S
has an error bound at infinity if there exist
\alpha>0
and
R>0
such that

d(x;S)\leq\alpha[g(x)]_{+}

for all
x\in\Omega
with
\|x\|>R
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that

(5)

\partial^{\infty}g(\infty)\cap(-N(\infty;\Omega))=\{0\}

and

(6)

0\notin\partial g(\infty)+N(\infty;\Omega).

Then the constraint set
S
in (
4
) has an error bound at infinity.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
S
has no error bound at infinity. Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
x_{k}\in\Omega
such that
\|x_{k}\|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
and

(7)

d(x_{k};S)>k^{2}[g(x_{k})]_{+}.

This implies that
x_{k}\notin S
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
\epsilon_{k}:=[g(x_{k})]_{+}=g(x_{k})>0
and

[g(x_{k})]_{+}\leq\inf_{y\in\Omega}[g(y)]_{+}+\epsilon_{k}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By the closedness of
\Omega
and the Ekeland variational principle (see Lemma
2.3
), for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda_{k}=k\epsilon_{k}>0
, there exists
y_{k}\in\Omega
such that

(8)

\displaystyle\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|\leq\lambda_{k},

(9)

\displaystyle[g(y_{k})]_{+}\leq[g(y)]_{+}+\frac{1}{k}\|y-y_{k}\|\ \ \text{for all}\ \ y\in\Omega.

It follows from (
7
) that

(10)

\lambda_{k}<\frac{1}{k}d(x_{k};S)\leq d(x_{k};S).

This implies that
y_{k}\notin S
and so
g(y_{k})>0
. Indeed, if otherwise, then

\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|\geq d(x_{k};S)>\lambda_{k},

a contradiction. Now fixed
\bar{x}\in S
, then by (
8
) and (
10
), one has

\displaystyle\|y_{k}\|\geq\|x_{k}\|-\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|\geq\|x_{k}\|-\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle>\|x_{k}\|-\frac{1}{k}d(x_{k};S)

\displaystyle\geq\|x_{k}\|-\frac{1}{k}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}\|\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\left\|\frac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}-\frac{\bar{x}}{\|x_{k}\|}\right\|\right)

and so
\|y_{k}\|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. On the other hand, by (
9
)
y_{k}
is a global minimizer of the l.s.c. function
[g(\cdot)]_{+}+\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{k}\|\cdot-y_{k}\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the l.s.c. property of
g
and the fact that
g(y_{k})>0
there exists a neighborhood of
y_{k}
on which we have

[g(\cdot)]_{+}+\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{k}\|\cdot-y_{k}\|=g(\cdot)+\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)+\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}\|\cdot-y_{k}\|.

By Fermat rule (Lemma
2.1
), we obtain

0\in\partial\left(g(\cdot)+\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{k}\|\cdot-y_{k}\|\right)(y_{k}).

By the Lipschitz property of the function
\|\cdot-y_{k}\|
and the sum rule (Lemma
2.2
), one gets

(11)

0\in\partial(g(\cdot)+\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot))(y_{k})+\frac{1}{k}\partial(\|\cdot-y_{k}\|)(y_{k}).

We now show that condition (
5
) implies that there exists
R>0
such that

(12)

\partial^{\infty}g(x)\cap(-N(x;\Omega))=\{0\}\ \ \text{when}\ \ \|x\|>R.

Indeed, if otherwise, there exist sequences
z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u_{k}\in\partial^{\infty}g(z_{k})\cap(-N(z_{k};\Omega))
such that
\|z_{k}\|\to\infty
and
u_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly,

\frac{u_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}\in\partial^{\infty}g(z_{k})\cap(-N(z_{k};\Omega)).

By passing to a subsequence if necessary we may assume that
\frac{u_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}
converges to some
u
with
\|u\|=1
. Thus, by Proposition
2.2
we get
u\in\partial^{\infty}g(\infty)\cap(-N(\infty;\Omega))
, a contradiction.

By (
12
), (
11
) and the sum rule, we have

(13)

0\in\partial g(y_{k})+\partial\delta_{\Omega}(y_{k})+\frac{1}{k}\partial(\|\cdot-y_{k}\|)(y_{k}).

We note here that
\partial\delta_{\Omega}(y_{k})=N(y_{k};\Omega)
and
\partial(\|\cdot-y_{k}\|)(y_{k})=\mathbb{B}
. Thus (
13
) means that

0\in\partial g(y_{k})+N(y_{k};\Omega)+\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{B}.

Hence, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
u_{k}\in\partial g(y_{k})
and
v_{k}\in N(y_{k};\Omega)
such that

(14)

\|u_{k}+v_{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{k}

and so
\lim_{k\to\infty}(u_{k}+v_{k})=0
.

We have the following two cases.

Case 1. The sequence
u_{k}
is bounded
. Then by (
14
), the sequence
v_{k}
is also bounded. By passing to a subsequence if necessary we may assume that
u_{k}\to u
and
v_{k}\to v
as
k\to\infty
. Thus we arrive at

u\in\partial g(\infty),v\in N(\infty;\Omega)\ \ \text{and}\ \ u+v=0,

which contradicts (
6
).

Case 2. The sequence
u_{k}
is unbounded
. By passing to a subsequence if necessary we may assume that
u_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
and so is
v_{k}
. Furthermore, by (
14
) we have

\|v_{k}\|\leq\|u_{k}+v_{k}\|+\|u_{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{k}+\|u_{k}\|.

Hence,

\frac{\|v_{k}\|}{\|u_{k}\|}\leq 1+\frac{1}{k\|u_{k}\|}.

This implies that the sequence
\frac{v_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}
is bounded and
\frac{v_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}\in N(y_{k};\Omega)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, by passing to a subsequence if necessary we may assume that
\frac{u_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}\to u
and
\frac{v_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}\to v
as
k\to\infty
. By Proposition
2.2
,
u\in\partial^{\infty}g(\infty)
. Clearly,
u\neq 0
,
v\in N(\infty;\Omega)
and
u+v=0
, which contradicts (
5
). The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

By Proposition
2.3
, the condition (
5
) holds automatically when
g
is Lipschitz at infinity or
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The following simple example is to illustrate Theorem
3.1
.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the constraint (
4
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
g(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}
for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then
S=\{(0,0)\}
. It is easy to see that
\partial g(\infty)=\emptyset
. Hence, by Theorem
3.1
,
S
has an error bound at infinity. However, we can check that
S
has no error bound of Hoffman’s type, that is, there exists
\tau>0
such that

(15)

d((x,y);S)\leq\tau[g(x,y)]_{+}\ \ \forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};

see, for example,
[
8
,
14
,
17
]
. Indeed, if otherwise, let
(x_{k},y_{k})=(0,\frac{1}{k})
. Then by (
15
), we obtain

\frac{1}{k}\leq\tau\frac{1}{k^{2}}\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N},

a contradiction.

We now apply Theorem
3.1
to constraint systems described not by a single inequality but possibly by finitely many inequalities.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
S
be a constraint set defined by

(16)

S:=\{x\in\Omega\;:\;g_{i}(x)\leq 0,i=1,\ldots,m\}

where
g_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i\in I:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, are l.s.c. functions,
\Omega
is an unbounded closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Omega\cap(\cap_{i\in I}\,\mbox{\rm dom}\,\,g_{i})
is unbounded. If the following conditions hold

(17)

\left[u_{1}+\ldots+u_{m}+v=0,u_{i}\in\partial^{\infty}g_{i}(\infty),v\in N(\infty;\Omega)\right]\Rightarrow u_{i}=v=0\ \ \forall i\in I,

and

(18)

\nexists\lambda\in\Delta_{m}\ \ \text{such that}\ \ 0\in\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\circ\partial g_{i}(\infty)+N(\infty;\Omega)

then
S
has an error bound at infinity, i.e., there exist
\alpha>0
and
R>0
such that

(19)

d(x;S)\leq\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{m}[g_{i}(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\ \ \text{with}\ \ \|x\|>R.

Proof.

Let
g
be the maximum function defined by
g(x):=\max\,\{g_{i}(x)\;:\;i\in I\}
. Then

S=\{x\in\Omega\;:\;g(x)\leq 0\}.

It follows from (
17
) that

\left[u_{1}+\ldots+u_{m}=0,u_{i}\in\partial^{\infty}g_{i}(\infty)\right]\Rightarrow u_{i}=0\ \ \forall i\in I.

By Proposition
2.5
, one has

\partial g(\infty)\subset\bigcup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\circ\partial f_{i}(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\in\Delta_{m}\right\}

This and (
18
) imply that
0\notin\partial g(\infty)+N(\infty;\Omega)
. Thus, by Theorem
3.1
, there exist
\alpha>0
and
R>0
such that

(20)

d(x;S)\leq\alpha[g(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\ \ \text{with}\ \ \|x\|>R.

It is clear that

[g(x)]_{+}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}[g_{i}(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

which together with (
20
) implies (
19
). The proof is complete.
∎

When
g_{i}
,
i\in I
, are Lipsschitz at infinity, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
S
be given as in (
16
),
where
\Omega
is a nonempty and unbounded closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
g_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i\in I
, are l.s.c. functions and Lipschitz at infinity. If

0\notin\mathrm{co}\,\{\partial g_{i}(\infty)\;:\;i\in I\}+N(\infty;\Omega)

then
S
has an error bound at infinity, i.e., there exist
\alpha>0
and
R>0
such that

d(x;S)\leq\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{m}[g_{i}(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\ \ \text{with}\ \ \|x\|>R.

Proof.

Since
g_{i}
,
i\in I
, are Lipsschitz at infinity, so is for its maxima
g
. Hence, the condition (
5
) is satisfied. Then the desired result follows from Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
2.5
.
∎

The next result gives a sufficient condition for the existence of error bound at infinity for a constraint system described by the pointwise minimum of finitely many l.s.c. functions.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
S
be given as in (
16
),
where
\Omega
is a nonempty and unbounded closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
g_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,

for all

S
has an error bound at infinity, i.e., there exist

and

such that

d(x;S)\leq\alpha[g(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\ \ \text{with}\ \ \|x\|>R.

Proof.

, the condition (

∎

4.
Optimality conditions at infinity

In this section, by using the existence of error bounds at infinity, we derive an upper estimate the normal cone at infinity of the constraint set. This result is instrumental to derive necessary optimality conditions at infinity for constrained optimization problems that have no solution.

We first derive an upper estimate for the normal cone at infinity of a given unbounded subset via the subdifferential of the distance
function to the set in question.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S
be a nonempty, closed and unbounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we have

N(\infty;S)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial d(\infty;S).

Proof.

Let
u\in N(\infty;S)
. Clearly, if
u=0
, then
u\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial d(\infty;S)
. If
u\neq 0
, then there exist sequences
x_{k}\in S
and
u_{k}\in\widehat{N}(x_{k};S)\setminus\{0\}
such that
x_{k}\to\infty
and
u_{k}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. Clearly,
\frac{u_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}\to\frac{u}{\|u\|}
and by
[
15
, Corollary 1.96]
we have

\frac{u_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|}\in\widehat{N}(x_{k};S)\cap\mathbb{B}=\widehat{\partial}d(x_{k};S).

Hence,
\frac{u}{\|u\|}\in\partial d(\infty;S)
and so
u\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial d(\infty;S)
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

In
[
15
, Theorem 1.97]
, Mordukhovich showed that

N(x;S)=\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial d(x;S)\ \ \forall x\in S.

However, this equality does not hold at infinity. For example, let

S=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\;:\;x_{1}\in\mathbb{R},x_{2}=x_{1}^{2}\}.

Then it is easily to check that
N(\infty;S)=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. Furthermore, by
[
16
, Theorem 1.33]
, we have

\partial d(x;S)=\begin{cases}N(x;S)\cap\mathbb{B}\ \ &\text{if}\ \ x\in S,\\
\frac{x-\Pi_{S}(x)}{d(x;S)}\ \ &\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus

\partial d(\infty;S)=(N(\infty;S)\cap\mathbb{B})\cup\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty,\,x\notin S}\frac{x-\Pi_{S}(x)}{d(x;S)}.

Take
x^{k}=(0,-k)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{x^{k}-\Pi_{S}(x^{k})}{d(x^{k};S)}=(0,-1)\in\partial d(\infty;S)

and
(0,-1)\notin N(\infty;S)
.

The following result gives an upper estimate for constraint systems that have an error bound at infinity.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let the constraint set
S
be defined by

(22)

S:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;g(x)\leq 0\},

where
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a l.s.c. function. Assume that
S
has an error bound at infinity. Then we have

N(\infty;S)\subset\bigcup\{\lambda\circ\partial g(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\geq 0\}.

If in addition
g
is Lipschitz at infinity, then

(23)

N(\infty;S)\subset\bigcup\{\lambda\partial g(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\geq 0\}.

Proof.

By assumption, there exist
\alpha>0
and
R>0
such that

d(x;S)\leq\alpha[g(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

Put
f(x):=\alpha[g(x)]_{+}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the function
f
possess the following properties

\displaystyle f(x)=0\ \ \forall x\in S,

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq f(x)\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

Hence, by definition of Fréchet subdiffential and
[
15
, Corollary 1.96]
, we have

\widehat{N}(x;S)\cap\mathbb{B}=\widehat{\partial}d(x,S)\subset\widehat{\partial}f(x)\ \ \forall x\in S\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

This implies that

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{S}\infty}[\widehat{N}(x;S)\cap\mathbb{B}]\subset\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{S}\infty}\widehat{\partial}f(x)\subset\partial f(\infty).

We claim that

N(\infty;S)\cap\mathbb{B}=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{S}\infty}[\widehat{N}(x;S)\cap\mathbb{B}]

and so

(24)

N(\infty;S)\cap\mathbb{B}\subset\partial f(\infty).

Indeed, it is easy to see that

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{S}\infty}[\widehat{N}(x;S)\cap\mathbb{B}]\subset N(\infty;S)\cap\mathbb{B}.

Now take any
u\in N(\infty;S)\cap\mathbb{B}
. If
u=0
, then
u\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{S}\infty}[\widehat{N}(x;S)\cap\mathbb{B}]
. Otherwise, there exist sequences
x_{k}\in S
,
u_{k}\in\widehat{N}(x_{k};S)
such that
x_{k}\to\infty
and
u_{k}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. Since
u\in\mathbb{B}
, we see that

\frac{\|u\|}{\|u_{k}\|}.u_{k}\in\widehat{N}(x_{k};S)\cap\mathbb{B}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{\|u\|}{\|u_{k}\|}.u_{k}\to u

and so
u\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{S}\infty}[\widehat{N}(x;S)\cap\mathbb{B}]
, as required.

We now obtain from (
24
) and Proposition
2.5
that

\displaystyle N(\infty;S)=\mathbb{R}_{+}[N(\infty)\cap\mathbb{B}]

\displaystyle\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial f(\infty)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}_{+}\big[(0,\alpha]\partial g(\infty)\cup\partial^{\infty}g(\infty)\big]

\displaystyle=[\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial g(\infty)]\cup\partial^{\infty}g(\infty)

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{\lambda\circ\partial g(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\geq 0\}.

If
g
is Lipschitz at infinity, then by Proposition
2.3
,
\partial^{\infty}g(\infty)=0
and so (
23
) is valid. The proof is complete.
∎

We now apply the estimate (
23
) to derive necessary optimality conditions at infinity for constrained optimization problems. Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a l.s.c. function and let
S
be a nonempty and closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(A1)
\mbox{\rm dom}\,f\cap S
is unbounded;

(A2)
f
in bounded from below on
S
.

Consider the following minimization problem

(P)

\text{minimize}\,f(x)\ \ \text{such that}\ \ x\in S.

Let us recall necessary optimality conditions at infinity to problem (
P
).

Theorem 4.1

(
[
11
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

If
f
does not attain its infimum on
S
and the following condition holds

(25)

-\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)\cap N(\infty;S)=\{0\},

then

0\in\partial f(\infty)+N(\infty;S).

The following result gives necessary optimality conditions at infinity to problem (
P
) when the constraint set
S
is given as in (
22
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Consider the problem (
P
) with
S
defined as in (
22
). Assume that conditions (A1), (A2) and the following condition hold

(26)

-\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)\cap\bigg[\bigcup\{\lambda\circ\partial g(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\geq 0\}\bigg]=\{0\}.

If
f
does not attains its infimum on
S
and
S
has an error bound at infinity, then there exists
\lambda\geq 0
such that

0\in\partial f(\infty)+\lambda\circ\partial g(\infty).

If in addition
g
is Lipschitz at infinity, then there exists
\lambda\geq 0
such that

0\in\partial f(\infty)+\lambda\partial g(\infty).

Proof.

By assumptions and Proposition
4.2
, we have

(27)

N(\infty;S)\subset\bigcup\{\lambda\circ\partial g(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\geq 0\}.

This and (
26
) imply that the condition (
25
) is satisfied. The desired conclusions follow from (
27
) and Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
f
and
g
are Lipschitz at infinity, then the condition (
26
) holds automatically.

The following example is designed to illustrate Theorem
4.2
.

Example 4.1
.

Consider problem (
P
) with
f(x)=e^{x}+\frac{1}{|x|+1}
,
g(x)=x
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
S=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;g(x)\leq 0\}=\mathbb{R}_{-}
. Clearly,
g
is Lipschitz at infinity and
\partial g(\infty)=1
. Hence, by Theorem
3.1
,
S
has an error bound at infinity. An easy computation shows that
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)=\mathbb{R}_{+}
and so the condition (
26
) is satisfied. Furthermore, the function
f
is bounded from below on
S
but does not attain a minimum. Thus there exists
\lambda\geq 0
such that

0\in\partial f(\infty)+\lambda\partial g(\infty).

The following result is deduced from Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
2.5
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Consider the problem (
P
) with
S
defined as in (
16
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that conditions (A1), (A2), (
17
) and the following condition hold

-\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)\cap\bigg[\bigcup\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\circ\partial g_{i}(\infty)\;:\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\Big\}\bigg]=\{0\}.

If
f
does not attains its infimum on
S
and
S
has an error bound at infinity, then there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
such that

0\in\partial f(\infty)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\circ\partial g_{i}(\infty).

If in addition
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
are Lipschitz at infinity, then there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
such that

0\in\partial f(\infty)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\partial g_{i}(\infty).

Proof.

Let
g
be the function defined by
g(x):=\max\,\{g_{i}(x)\;:\;i=1,\ldots,m\}
. Then the desired result follows from Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
2.5
.
∎

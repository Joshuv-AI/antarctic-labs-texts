On the TAP equations via the cavity approach in the generic mixed p p -spin models
Wei-Kuo Chen, Si Tang

Abstract
In 1977, Thouless, Anderson, and Palmer (TAP) derived a system of consistent equations in terms of the effective magnetization in order to study the free energy in the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) spin glass model. The solutions to their equations were predicted to contain vital information about the landscapes in the SK Hamiltonian and the TAP free energy and moreover have direct connections to Parisi’s replica ansatz. In this work, we aim to investigate the validity of the TAP equations in the generic mixed p p -spin model. By utilizing the ultrametricity of the overlaps, we show that the TAP equations are asymptotically satisfied by the conditional local magnetizations on the asymptotic pure states.

For any

Theorem 1.1

(TAP equations)
.

Assume that the model is generic.
Then the conditional local magnetization
\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\Bigl|\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle^{\alpha}-\tanh\Bigl(X_{N,\beta,N}(\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha})+h-\zeta^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\|\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}\|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(1-\|\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}\|^{2}\bigr)\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle^{\alpha}\Bigr)\Bigr|^{2}\Bigr\rangle=0,\end{split}

(4)

where
\epsilon\downarrow 0
along a sequence such that

\mu_{P}(\{q_{P}-\epsilon\})=0.

4

\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}
satisfies the following asymptotic consistency equations on average over all spin sites

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle^{\alpha}\approx\tanh\Bigl(X_{N,\beta,i}(\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha})+h-\zeta^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\|\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}\|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(1-\|\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}\|^{2}\bigr)\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle^{\alpha}\Bigr).

(5)

These self-consistent equations are the TAP equations. The term
\zeta^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\|\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}\|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(1-\|\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}\|^{2}\bigr)\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle^{\alpha}
is called the Onsager correction term, which distinguishes the disorder and non-disorder spin systems, where the latter, taking the Curie-Weiss model for example, does not involve an Onsager term in the mean-field equation for the spin magnetizations, see, e.g., Equation (II.2) in

]

\Sigma_{N}

\langle\sigma\rangle^{\alpha}\approx\langle\sigma\rangle
and
\langle\sigma\rangle
satisfies the TAP equations (
5
) in the generic mixed
p
-spin model at high temperature. This is similar to the one in the SK model established by Chatterjee
[
12
]
and Talagrand
[
40
]

N
TAP equations asymptotically hold simultaneously with high probability, while we establish the TAP equations in the average sense.
.

In contrast to the high temperature case, it is expected that the TAP equations should have exponentially many solutions in
N
in the low temperature regime. Although an argument for this remains missing in the literature, it can be argued that the TAP equations could have multiple solutions by making use of the ultrametricity (

possesses a nontrivial distribution. Pick

in the support of

Note that in the generic mixed

-spin model, the

with positive probability under

are

Our approach for Theorem
1.1
is based on the cavity method, where the computation utilizes (
2
), the ultrametricity of the overlaps. This property allows us to partition the spin configuration space into random “clusters”
(C_{N,\alpha})_{\alpha\geq 1}
, i.e., the pure states, where the distribution of the weights of the pure states is characterized by the Ruelle Probability Cascades, see, e.g.,
[
29
,
41
]
. More importantly, within each pure state, the overlap of two spin configurations are asymptotically concentrated around

[

Overall we believe that our approach has presented a general receipe in deriving the TAP equations for similar local magnetizations in related spin glass models.

1.1
Related works

The TAP Equations at High Temperature.
The mathematical establishment of the TAP equations appeared firstly in Talagrand’s book
[
40
]
and Chatterjee
[
12
]
, where they showed that the spin magnetization asymptotically satisfies the TAP equations in the SK model at sufficiently high temperature. Later Bolthausen
[
9
]
proposed an iterative scheme and showed that his iteration would converge to one solution of the TAP equations, provided the temperature and external field parameters lie inside the

[
17
]
. In a joint paper,
[
16
]
, the authors showed that Bolthausen’s iteration scheme indeed converges to the spin magnetization whenever the overlap of two independently sampled spin configurations from the Gibbs measure is concentrated locally uniformly in temperature.
Viewing the disorders as Brownian motions and using tools from stochastic calculus, Adhikari-Brennecke-von Soosten-Yau
[
1
]
established the TAP equations for
\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle
and
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle
at sufficiently high temperature for the SK model and a version of the mixed
p
-spin models.

The Mézard-Virasoro Equations.
Recently, Auffinger-Jagannath
[
3

4

considered the generic mixed
p
-spin model and established a system of equations, discovered by Mézard-Virasoro

(6)

The Mézard-Virasoro equations read

Note that it is not always the case that
\langle X_{N,\beta}(\sigma)\rangle^{a}\approx X_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle^{a})

1.2
Further results related to the TAP equations

The TAP equations have many important consequences in the study of mean-field spin glass models and related applications. First of all, based on his iteration, Bolthausen
[
10
]
performed a conditional second moment method to derive the replica symmetry formula for the limiting free energy in the SK model at very high temperature. In a follow-up work, Brennecke-Yau
[
11
]
provided a simplified argument for Bolthausen’s approach and extended the replica symmetry formula to a larger regime. In addition, the TAP equations have played a key role in some optimization problems in spin glasses and statistical inference problems. Most importantly, they naturally give rise to the so-called Approximate Message Passing (AMP) algorithms based on Bolthausen’s iteration scheme
[
9
]
; several generalizations of the AMP algorithms can be found in Bayati-Montanari
[
6
]
and Javanmard-Montanari
[
20
]
. By using the AMP algorithms, Montanari
[
24
]
constructed a polynomial-time random algorithms to produce a near ground state for the SK Hamiltonian under the assumption that the Parisi measure is full replica symmetry breaking. The same construction was also carried out in the mixed
p
-spin model by El Alaoui-Montanari-Sellke
[
18
]
. In the context of Bayesian inferences, various AMPs driven by the TAP equations have also been popularly used, see, e.g.,
[
21
,
25
,
26
,
43
]
.

Proof Sketch

Our approach adapts a similar route as
[
40

, where the TAP equations in the SK model were established at very high temperature relying on the fact that the overlap

between two replicas

is concentrated around a deterministic constant. However, this concentration is no longer valid in the low temperature regime. Our proof utilizes, instead, a conditional concentration property of the overlap, deduced from the ultrametricity (

q_{P}.
In this section, we elaborate some key steps to summarize the main ideas of our arguments. We believe that our approach is potentially applicable to establish the TAP equations in
some other mean-field spin glass models, such as the multi-species model
[

]
and the perceptron model
[
40
, Chapter 2]
.

There are three key steps in our argument:

Step 1: Cavity computation.
In Section

3
, we decompose the Hamiltonian (

N
-th spin. More precisely, if we fix the last spin as the cavity coordinate, then for

we can rewrite

\displaystyle{H}_{N,\beta}(\sigma)={H}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(\tau)+\sigma_{N}\bigl(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h\bigr),

(10)

where
\beta^{\prime}=(\beta_{p}^{\prime})_{p\geq 2}
is a modification of
\beta
defined as
\beta_{p}^{\prime}=\beta_{p}((N-1)/N)^{(p-1)/2}
for each

. In this way, we can express
\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}
as a fraction of averages with respect to the Gibbs measure associated to the reduced Hamiltonian
{H}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
in terms of two constraints,
\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}\bigl((\tau,1)\bigr)
and
\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}\bigl((\tau,-1)\bigr)

\alpha=(\rho,\alpha_{N})
, both of them are approximately
\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau)
due to the fact that

. This will result in

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\approx\frac{\langle\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}.

(11)

Step 2: Central Limit Theorem of cavity fields.

We notice that for any
\rho\in\Sigma_{N-1}
fixed, the disorders,
g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}
, in
X_{N,\beta}
are independent of the conditional Gibbs measure
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
and furthermore, for independent samples
\tau^{1},\tau^{2}\sim G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
, the overlap
R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})
is concentrated around
q_{P}
, due to ultrametricity (
2
). These allow us to show (see Theorem
3.7
) that the first term in the following representation

X_{N,\beta}(\tau)=\bigl(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)-\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigr)+\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

is Gaussian distributed with some variance, approximately
\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})
,
independent of
\tau
.
Consequently, we can represent

X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\approx z\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}+\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho},\quad\text{ for }z\sim N(0,1).

It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\approx\mathbb{E}_{z}\sinh(z\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}+\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h)

\displaystyle=e^{(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))/2}\sinh(\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h),

and

\displaystyle\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\approx\mathbb{E}_{z}\cosh(\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}z+\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h)

\displaystyle\approx e^{(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))/2}\cosh(\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h),

which, combining with (
11

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\approx\tanh(\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h).

(12)

In establishing this Central Limit Theorem, there is a subtle issue that occurs from the fact that
\alpha\in\Sigma_{N}
is not fixed (and neither is

) but sampled from

. Since the Gaussian disorders

appearing in

and

, the first

on the asymptotic pure state

is approximately concentrated around
q_{P}.
This is analogous to the concentration of the overlap

in the SK model at high temperature.

Step 3: Producing the Onsager correction term.
In Section
5

and the product

in

and approximate

and

fixed. More precisely, by definition (

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{p}\sqrt{p!}}{N^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}\langle\sigma_{i_{1}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\cdots\langle\sigma_{i_{p-1}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}.

(13)

We will follow an argument similar to that in our first step to write each conditioned local magnetization in (

\displaystyle\frac{\big\langle\bigl(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}\frac{\beta_{p}\sqrt{p!}}{N^{(p-1)/2}}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}\tau_{i_{1}}^{1}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}^{p-1}\bigr)\,\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{\ell})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\big\langle\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{\ell})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1.\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}.

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}\frac{\beta_{p}\sqrt{p!}}{N^{(p-1)/2}}g_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p-1},N}\tau_{i_{1}}^{1}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}^{p-1},\ \ X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{1}),\ \ldots,\ X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{p-1}),

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})^{p}\bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}=\int_{0}^{1}q^{p}\mu_{P}(dq)

and our claim follows since we assume that our model is generic.

Now we turn to the proof of (
21
). Let
\epsilon^{\prime}
and
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
be any positive numbers such that
\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon<\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
. From these, as long as
N
is large enough, we can bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{\left|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-(q_{P}-\epsilon)\right|<\frac{3}{N-1}\}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})<q_{P}-\epsilon+\frac{3}{N-1}\}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}-\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})\leq q_{P}-\epsilon-\frac{3}{N-1}\}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})\leq q_{P}-\epsilon^{\prime}\}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}-\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})<q_{P}-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}.

Consequently, from the weak convergence of
R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})
to
\mu_{P}
under
\mathbb{E}G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\otimes 2},
we see that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{\left|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-(q_{P}-\epsilon)\right|<\frac{3}{N-1}\}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\leq\mu_{P}([0,q_{P}-\epsilon^{\prime}])-\mu_{P}([0,q_{P}-\epsilon^{\prime\prime})).

Hence, from the continuity of
\mu_{P}
at
q_{P}-\epsilon,
after sending
\epsilon^{\prime}\uparrow\epsilon
and
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\downarrow\epsilon,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{\left|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-(q_{P}-\epsilon)\right|<\frac{3}{N-1}\}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}=0.

As for (
22
) and (
23
), we recall that it is already known in
[
13
, Lemma 3]
that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta}(A_{\ominus}^{\tau})\geq\delta\}}\rangle_{N-1,\beta}=1.

Since the overlap
R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})
under
\mathbb{E}G_{N-1,\beta}^{\otimes 2}
and
\mathbb{E}G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\otimes 2}
converges to the same distribution
\mu_{P}
and both of the measures
\mathbb{E}G_{N-1,\beta}^{\otimes\infty}
and
\mathbb{E}G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\otimes\infty}
satisfy the extended Ghirlanda-Guerra identities, the limiting distributions of the Gram matrix
(R(\tau^{\ell},\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}))_{1\leq\ell<\ell^{\prime}}
corresponding to
(\tau^{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
sampled from either
G_{N-1,\beta}
or
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
are uniquely determined by the overlap distribution
\mu_{P}
and are described by the Ruelle Probability Cascades parametrized by
\mu_{P},
see
[
29
]
. With this, the same argument in
[
13
, Lemma 3]
yields (
22
). For (
23
), note that the extended Ghirlanda-Guerra identities also imply that for independent
\tau^{1},\tau^{2},\tau^{3}\sim G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},
their overlaps must be asymptotically ultrametric due to
[
28
]
. From our claim, we also see that
R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})
is asymptotically less than
q_{P}+\delta
. These together complete the proof of (
23
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
\varepsilon=0,\pm 1
and any
\epsilon>0
such that
q_{P}-\epsilon
is a point of continuity of
\mu_{P},
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\big(\,\langle e^{\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}-\langle e^{\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\,\right\rangle_{N,\beta}=0.

Proof.

The left hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle\langle e^{\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

and by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it is no more than

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\langle e^{2\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\langle e^{2\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\bigl\langle\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\langle e^{2\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl\langle\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}e^{4\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\bigr]\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Here, using the independence between
g_{\cdot N}
and the disorder appearing in the Gibbs expectation
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
, one can get from (
18
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}e^{4\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}

\displaystyle=e^{8\varepsilon^{2}\text{Var}(X_{N,\beta}(\tau))}\leq e^{8\sum_{p\geq 2}p\beta_{p}^{2}}<\infty.

Note that this upper bound holds for
\varepsilon=0
and
\pm 1
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\ \ \mathbb{E}\left\langle\big\langle e^{\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}}(\tau)-e^{\varepsilon X_{N,\beta}(\tau)}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle^{2}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq e^{4\sum_{p\geq 2}p\beta_{p}^{2}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\Big\rangle_{N,\beta}\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{(4+1/4)\sum_{p\geq 2}p\beta_{p}^{2}}\cosh(h)^{1/2}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\left\langle\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\tau^{2}}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\tau^{2}}}(\tau^{1})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=e^{(4+1/4)\sum_{p\geq 2}p\beta_{p}^{2}}\cosh^{1/2}(h)\Big\{\mathbb{E}\left\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{(\tau^{1},\tau^{2}):\,|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-(q_{P}-\epsilon)|\leq 3/(N-1)\}}\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\Big\}^{1/2},

where the second inequality used Lemma
3.2
and
\tau^{1}
and
\tau^{2}
are sampled from the inner and outer Gibbs measures, respectively. Our proof is then completed by applying (
21
).
∎

With the help of the above three lemmas, we can now replace all the indicators in (
19
) with
\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau)
:

Proposition 3.6
.

For
\epsilon>0
such that
q_{P}-\epsilon
is a point of continuity of
\mu_{P},

we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Bigl(\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}-\frac{\langle\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}\Bigr)^{2}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}=0.

(24)

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\big\langle e^{X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}\big((\tau,1)\big)-e^{-X_{N,\beta}(\tau)-h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}\big((\tau,-1)\big)\,\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\big\langle e^{X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}\big((\tau,1)\big)+e^{-X_{N,\beta}(\tau)-h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}\big((\tau,-1)\big)\,\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

\displaystyle A_{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\big\langle\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

\displaystyle B_{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\big\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

and then we can express

\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}=\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\langle\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}=\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}.

For any
\delta>0
, the expectation in (
24
) is no more than

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Bigl(\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\Bigr)^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Big(\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\Big)^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Big(\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\Big)^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Big(\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\Big)^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}.

Using that
|A_{\alpha}/B_{\alpha}|\leq 1
,
|A_{\rho}/B_{\rho}|\leq 1
and combining the first term and the third term, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left\langle\Bigl(\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\Bigr)^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2C\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})<\delta\}}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}+C\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Bigl(\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\Bigr)^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}.

(25)

Since
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})
is independent of
g_{\cdot N}
for any
\rho\in\Sigma_{N-1},
we can use (
21
) to bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})<\delta\}}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\beta_{p}^{2}p}{2}}\cosh(h)\,\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\tau})<\delta\}}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
and then
\delta\downarrow 0,
where the last limit used (
22
).
As for the second expectation in (
25
), we note that, on the event
\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\right\rvert\frac{|B_{\alpha}-B_{\rho}|}{B_{\alpha}}+\frac{|A_{\rho}-A_{\alpha}|}{B_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|B_{\alpha}-B_{\rho}|}{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})}+\frac{|A_{\rho}-A_{\alpha}|}{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})}\leq\delta^{-1}[|B_{\alpha}-B_{\rho}|+|A_{\rho}-A_{\alpha}|],

where we used that
|A_{\rho}/B_{\rho}|\leq 1
and that from (
20
),

\displaystyle B_{\alpha},B_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\big\langle\cosh\big(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h\big)\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\geq\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}=G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho}).

Thus, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Bigl(\frac{A_{\alpha}}{B_{\alpha}}-\frac{A_{\rho}}{B_{\rho}}\Bigr)^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}\leq C\delta^{-2}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\big\langle(A_{\alpha}-A_{\rho})^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}+\mathbb{E}\big\langle(B_{\alpha}-B_{\rho})^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}\Bigr).

Here, from Lemma
3.5
and the inequality in (
20
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle(A_{\alpha}-A_{\rho})^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\big(\,\big\langle e^{X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}-\big\langle e^{X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\quad+C^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\big(\,\big\langle e^{-X_{N,\beta}(\tau)-h}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}-\big\langle e^{-X_{N,\beta}(\tau)-h}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\quad\to 0\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Similarly,
\mathbb{E}\big\langle(B_{\alpha}-B_{\rho})^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}\to 0
.
These imply that the second term in (
25
) also vanishes for any
\delta>0
, completing our proof.
∎

3.2
Central limit theorem for the cavity field

The goal of this section is to derive a central limit theorem for the centered cavity field defined as

\displaystyle\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=X_{N,\beta}(\tau)-\bigl\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\beta_{p}\sqrt{p!}}{N^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}g_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p-1},N}\bigl(\tau_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}-\langle\tau_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigr)

(26)

for any
\rho,\tau\in\Sigma_{N-1}.

Theorem 3.7

(Central Limit Theorem)
.

Assume that
U
is an infinitely differentiable function on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\sup_{0\leq\gamma\leq M}\mathbb{E}|U^{(d)}(\gamma z)|^{k}<\infty,\ \ \forall d,k\in\mathbb{Z},\ 0\leq d,k,M<\infty.

Then, for any integer
r\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Big[\big\langle U\big(\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau)\big)\big\rangle^{\rho}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}-\mathbb{E}U\big(\xi\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}\big)\Big]^{2r}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}=0,

(27)

where
\xi\sim N(0,1)
is independent of all other random variables.

Remark 3.8
.

As we will see later, this theorem is special case of the multivariate central limit theorem stated in Theorem
5.1
below. Here we provide a standalone proof for Theorem
3.7
to illustrate the main steps as a warm-up for the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

The proof of Theorem
3.7
relies on the concentration of the overlap within the pure state established in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9

(Concentration of the overlap)
.

We have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\bigl\langle|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-q_{P}|\bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\bigl\langle|R(\tau,\rho)-q_{P}|\bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=0,

where in both equations, the
\rho
’s are sampled from the outer Gibbs measure and
\tau,\tau^{1},\tau^{2}
are sampled from the inner one.

Proof.

For any
\delta>0
, letting
\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\rho}=\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big\langle\bigl|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-q_{P}|\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big\langle\big|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-q_{P}\big|\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\rho}^{c}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left\langle\frac{\big|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-q_{P}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau^{1})\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau^{2})}{G^{2}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho})}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\rho}}\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\rho}^{c}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-q_{P}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau^{1})\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau^{2})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

(28)

where in the second expectations on the right-hand side,
\rho,\tau^{1},\tau^{2}
are understood as i.i.d. copies from
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}.

From (
22
), the first term in (
28
) vanishes as
N\to\infty
and then
\delta\downarrow 0.
Next, since both
R(\rho,\tau^{1})
and
R(\rho,\tau^{2})
are at least
q_{P}-\epsilon
, it follows from (
23
) that
|R(\tau^{1},\tau^{2})-q_{P}|\leq 2\epsilon
with probability nearly one under
\mathbb{E}G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\otimes 2}.
Hence, the second term in (
28
) also vanishes as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, and this completes the proof of the first assertion. As for the second one, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big\langle\bigl|R(\rho,\tau)-q_{P}|\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\leq 2\mathbb{E}\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\delta,\rho}^{c}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big|R(\rho,\tau)-q_{P}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}.

Again the first term vanishes in the limit due to (
22
).
From (
23
) and the constraint
\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}
, we see that
|R(\rho,\tau)-q_{P}|\leq 2\epsilon
with probability nearly one under
\mathbb{E}G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\otimes 2}.
Hence the second term also vanishes, completing our proof.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Following the same proof, it can also be shown that

\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\bigl|\|\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\|^{2}-q_{P}\bigr|^{2}\bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\bigl|\bigl\langle R(\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2})\bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}-q_{P}\bigr|^{2}\bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}=0.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

We prove the central limit theorem via an interpolation argument.
Fix
r\geq 1
. For every integer
1\leq m\leq 2r
and any
\rho,\tau^{m}\in\Sigma_{N-1}
, consider the interpolation

\displaystyle x_{\rho}^{m}(t)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{t}S_{\rho}^{m}+\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}\xi^{m},\,\,0\leq t\leq 1,

where the
\xi^{m}
’s are i.i.d copies of
\xi
that are also independent of everything else, and
S_{\rho}^{m}:=\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{m}).
Set

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle:=U(x)-\mathbb{E}U(\xi\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}),\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

Recall that for any
\alpha\in\Sigma_{N},

\rho\in\Sigma_{N-1}
represents the first
N-1
spins. For
0\leq t\leq 1,
define

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\bigg\langle\prod_{m=1}^{2r}V(x_{\rho}^{m}(t))\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}.

Evidently the left-hand side of (
27
) equals
\phi_{N}(1)
.

In order to control
\phi_{N}(1)
, we introduce

\psi_{N}(t):=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\bigg\langle\prod_{m=1}^{2r}V(x_{\rho}^{m}(t))\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}.

where
\rho
and
(\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{2r})
are sampled from the outer and inner Gibbs measures,
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
, respectively.
Observe that
\psi_{N}(0)=0
. On the other hand, note that
when
t=1,

x_{\rho}^{m}(1)=\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{m})
and since
\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{2r}
are i.i.d. sampled from
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
, it follows that

\langle V(x_{\rho}^{1}(1))\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}=\langle V(x_{\rho}^{2}(1))\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}=\cdots=\langle V(x_{\rho}^{2r}(1))\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}.

Consequently,

\Bigl\langle\prod_{m=1}^{2r}V(x_{\rho}^{m}(1))\Bigr\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}=\Bigl(\langle V(x_{\rho}^{1}(1))\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\Bigr)^{2r},

which is a nonnegative.
From this, we can adopt a change of measure argument as the one in the proof of Lemma
3.2
to bound

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(1)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\bigg\langle\prod_{m=1}^{2r}V(x_{\rho}^{m}(1))\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\left(\bigg\langle V(x_{\rho}(1))\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right)^{2r}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\left(\bigg\langle V(x_{\rho}(1))\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right)^{2r}\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\bigg\langle\prod_{m=1}^{2r}V(\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{m}))\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}=\psi_{N}(1).

(29)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(1)\leq\psi_{N}(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{N}^{\prime}(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\psi_{N}^{\prime}(t)|dt,

and it suffices to estimate
|\psi^{\prime}_{N}(t)|
.
To this end, we differentiate
\psi_{N}
and use symmetry in
m
to get

\displaystyle\psi_{N}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=r\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big\langle\Phi_{N,t}(\rho,\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{2r})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=r\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big\langle\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N,\xi}}\Phi_{N,t}(\rho,\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{2r})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{N,t}(\rho,\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{2r})

4.2
Removal of the cavity spin constraint II

Fix
2\leq p\leq p_{0}
and recall the definitions of
A_{p}^{\alpha}
and
B_{p}^{\rho}
in (

) and (
42
). Using replicas, we may rewrite
A_{p}^{\alpha}
and
B_{p}^{\rho}
as follows.
For
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{p-1})\in\Sigma_{N-1}^{p-1}
, define

\displaystyle Z_{N,p}(\boldsymbol{\tau})=\frac{\sqrt{p!}}{N^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}\tau^{1}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{p-1}_{i_{p-1}}.

(45)

Define

\displaystyle C_{p}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle Z_{N,p}(\boldsymbol{\tau})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\frac{e^{X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}((\tau^{l},1))+e^{-X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})-h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}((\tau^{l},-1))}{2}\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

\displaystyle C_{p}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle Z_{N,p}(\boldsymbol{\tau})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau^{l})\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

\displaystyle D_{p}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\frac{e^{X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}((\tau^{l},1))+e^{-X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})-h}\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N}^{\alpha}}((\tau^{l},-1))}{2}\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

\displaystyle D_{p}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma_{N-1}^{\rho}}(\tau^{l})\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}.

Then we can write

\displaystyle A_{p}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}\frac{C_{p}^{\alpha}}{D_{p}^{\alpha}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{p}^{\rho}=\beta_{p}\frac{C_{p}^{\rho}}{D_{p}^{\rho}}.

(46)

Denote

\eta_{N}=\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\oplus}^{\rho}\setminus A_{\ominus}^{\rho}}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}.

As we have seen at the end of the proof of Lemma

,
\eta_{N}\to 0
as

.
Our next Lemma bounds the errors when approximating

with
C^{\rho}_{p}
and
D_{p}^{\alpha}
with
D_{p}^{\rho}
, which are the main ingredients when we estimate the difference between

and
B_{p}^{\rho}
. The proof of the lemma is deferred to Appendix

.

Lemma 4.4
.

For each
r\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
, there exist positive constants
K(r,p,\beta,h)
and
K^{\prime}(r,p,\beta,h)

such that for all
N\geq 1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle(C_{p}^{\alpha}-C_{p}^{\rho})^{2r}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{N}K(r,p,\beta,h),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle(D_{p}^{\alpha}-D_{p}^{\rho})^{2r}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{N}K^{\prime}(r,p,\beta,h).

4.3
Proof of Proposition
4.1

First, write

\displaystyle\ \ \ X_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha})-X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})

\displaystyle=\Big(X_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha})-X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}+\Big(X_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha})-X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})<\delta\}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\big(A_{p}^{\alpha}-B_{p}^{\rho}\big)\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}+\Big(\sum_{p>p_{0}}\big(A_{p}^{\alpha}-B_{p}^{\rho}\big)\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}

\displaystyle\qquad+X_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha})\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})<\delta\}}-X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})<\delta\}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\big\langle\big(X_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha})-X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})\big)^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big(\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\big(A_{p}^{\alpha}-B_{p}^{\rho}\big)\Big)^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +C\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big(\sum_{p>p_{0}}A_{p}^{\alpha}\Big)^{2}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big(\sum_{p>p_{0}}B_{p}^{\rho}\Big)^{2}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +C\mathbb{E}\big\langle X^{2}_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha})\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})<\delta\}}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}+C\mathbb{E}\big\langle X^{2}_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})<\delta\}}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}.

By Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
, the last four terms can be made arbitrary small by choosing
\delta>0
small enough and
p_{0}
large enough. Thus, it suffices to show that for any
0<\delta<1
and
p_{0}\geq 2
, the first term vanishes as
N\to\infty
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\bigg[\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\big(A_{p}^{\alpha}-B_{p}^{\rho}\big)\bigg]^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}=0.

(47)

To do this, notice that from (
46
),

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\big(A_{p}^{\alpha}-B_{p}^{\rho}\big)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\beta_{p}\Big(\frac{C_{p}^{\alpha}}{D_{p}^{\alpha}}-\frac{C_{p}^{\rho}}{D_{p}^{\rho}}\Big)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\beta_{p}^{2}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\Big(\frac{C_{p}^{\alpha}}{D_{p}^{\alpha}}-\frac{C_{p}^{\rho}}{D_{p}^{\rho}}\Big)^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{\beta}\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\bigg(\Big(\frac{C_{p}^{\rho}}{D_{p}^{\rho}}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{D_{p}^{\alpha}-D_{p}^{\rho}}{D_{p}^{\alpha}}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{C_{p}^{\alpha}-C_{p}^{\rho}}{D_{p}^{\alpha}}\Big)^{2}\bigg).

Using (
20
) and the fact
\cosh x\geq 1
, we have

D_{p}^{\alpha},D_{p}^{\rho}\geq\delta^{p}

on the event
\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}
and it follows that

\displaystyle\ \ \ \mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\bigg[\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\big(A_{p}^{\alpha}-B_{p}^{\rho}\big)\bigg]^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}(A_{\ominus}^{\rho})\geq\delta\}}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{\beta}}{\delta^{2p_{0}}}\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Big[\Big(\frac{C_{p}^{\rho}}{\delta^{p_{0}}}\Big)^{2}\big(D_{p}^{\alpha}-D_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{2}+\big(C_{p}^{\alpha}-C_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{2}\Big]\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{\beta}}{\delta^{2p_{0}}}\sum_{2\leq p\leq p_{0}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\delta^{2p_{0}}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big(C_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big(D_{p}^{\alpha}-D_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}}+\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big(C_{p}^{\alpha}-C_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}\Bigr).

(48)

By Lemma
4.4
, we have for all
2\leq p\leq p_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big(D_{p}^{\rho}-D_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq K\eta_{N},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big(C_{p}^{\alpha}-C_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq K\sqrt{\eta_{N}},

for all
N\geq 1,
where
K
is a constant depending only on
p_{0},\beta,h.

Also, a slight modification, by dropping the indicators in (
66
), we can bound

\mathbb{E}\langle\big(C_{p}^{\rho}\big)^{4}\rangle_{N,\beta}\leq K^{\prime}
for
2\leq p\leq p_{0}
, where

is a constant depending on
p,\beta,h.

Plugging these into (
48
) and then sending

yields (
47
) and thus completes the proof of (
40
).

We will complete the proof of Theorem

in this section. At the current stage, we have seen that

asymptotically equals

following from Proposition
4.1
, where

is defined in (
42
). Our next step is to handle this sum, via an argument similar to the proof of Proposition
3.11
, by adapting a multivariate central limit theorem for the Gaussian fields,
X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{1}),\ldots,X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{p-1})
, and
Z_{N,p}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
appearing in
C_{p}^{\rho}
and
D_{p}^{\rho}
, see (
46
). We establish this limit theorem in the first subsection.

5.1
A multivariate central limit theorem for the cavity fields

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{p-1})^{T}
be a centered Gaussian (column) vector of length
p
with covariance matrix

\mathbb{E}(\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{T})=\begin{bmatrix}p(1-q_{P}^{p-1})&\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})&\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})&\cdots&\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})&\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})&0&\cdots&0\\
\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})&0&\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})&0&0&\cdots&\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})\end{bmatrix},

that is,
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p-1}
are i.i.d.
N(0,\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))
and
\xi_{0}\sim N(0,p(1-q_{P}^{p-1}))
that has covariance
\beta_{p}pq^{p-2}(1-q)
with each of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p-1}
. Note that this multivariate Gaussian distribution is well-defined. To see this, consider i.i.d. standard normal random variables
z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{p-1}
, and
\boldsymbol{\xi}
can be constructed via linear combinations of
z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{p-1}
as follows:

\displaystyle\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}z_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,p-1,

\displaystyle\xi_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})}{\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}z_{i}+z_{0}\sqrt{p(1-q_{P}^{p-1})-\frac{\beta_{p}^{2}p^{2}(p-1)q_{P}^{2p-4}(1-q_{P})^{2}}{\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P})}},

where the quantity in the last square root is always nonnegative because

\displaystyle p(1-q_{P}^{p-1})(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))-\beta_{p}^{2}p^{2}(p-1)q_{P}^{2p-4}(1-q_{P})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq p(1-q_{P}^{p-1})\cdot\beta_{p}^{2}p(1-q_{P}^{p-1})-\beta_{p}^{2}p^{2}(p-1)q_{P}^{2p-4}(1-q_{P})^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}^{2}p^{2}\bigl((1-q_{P}^{p-1})^{2}-(p-1)q_{P}^{2p-4}(1-q_{P})^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}^{2}p^{2}(1-q_{P})^{2}\bigl((1+q_{P}+\cdots+q_{P}^{p-2})^{2}-(p-1)q_{P}^{2p-4}\bigr)\geq 0.

For
\rho\in\Sigma_{N-1}
, recall from (
26
) that

\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{1}),\ldots,\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{p-1}),\quad\forall\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{p-1}\in\Sigma_{N-1}

are defined as

\displaystyle\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}({\tau}^{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\beta_{p}\sqrt{p!}}{N^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}\bigl(\tau_{i_{1}}^{l}\cdots\tau^{l}_{i_{p-1}}-\big\langle\tau_{i_{1}}^{l}\cdots\tau^{l}_{i_{p-1}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigr).

(49)

Recall
Z_{N,p}
from (
45
). We set the centralized
Z_{N,p}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
as

\displaystyle\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{p!}}{N^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}\bigl(\tau_{i_{1}}^{1}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}^{p-1}-\big\langle\tau_{i_{1}}^{1}\cdots\tau^{p-1}_{i_{p-1}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigr).

(50)

Now we are ready to state the central limit theorem.

Theorem 5.1

(Multivariate Central Limit Theorem)
.

Fix
p\geq 2
. Let
F_{0},\ldots,F_{p-1}
be infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}
such that for
z\sim N(0,1)
, any
M>0
and integers
m,k\geq 0
,

\sup_{0\leq\gamma\leq M}\max\bigl(\mathbb{E}|F_{0}^{(m)}(\gamma z)|^{k},\ldots,\mathbb{E}|F_{p-1}^{(m)}(\gamma z)|^{k}\bigr)<\infty.

Then, for any integer
r\geq 1
fixed, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\,&\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\Big[\big\langle U\big(\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}(\boldsymbol{\tau}),\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{1}),\ldots,\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{p-1})\big)\big\rangle^{\rho}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}-\mathbb{E}U(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Big]^{2r}\Big\rangle_{N,\beta}=0,\end{split}

(51)

where
U(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p-1}):=F_{0}(x_{0})F_{1}(x_{1})\cdots F_{p-1}(x_{p-1})
for
x=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

Proof.

The proof utilizes a similar idea as that of Theorem
3.7
. The main difference is that here we need to deal with the
2r
-th moment of a multivariate function depending on
(p-1)
spin replicas. Thus, we will further consider
2r
independent copies of these
(p-1)
spins,
(\tau^{1},\tau^{2},\ldots,\tau^{p-1})
, and denote them by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tau}^{m}

\displaystyle=(\tau^{m,1},\ldots,\tau^{m,p-1}),\quad m=1,2,\ldots,2r,

where
(\tau^{m,l})_{1\leq m\leq 2r,1\leq p\leq p-1}
are independently sampled from
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
. For
1\leq m\leq 2r
, define

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\rho}^{m}

\displaystyle=(S^{m,0}_{\rho},S^{m,1}_{\rho},\ldots,S^{m,p-1}_{\rho})=\bigl(\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{m}),\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}({\tau}^{m,1}),\ldots,\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}({\tau}^{m,p-1})\bigr).

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{m}=(\xi_{0}^{m},\xi_{1}^{m},\ldots,\xi_{p-1}^{m})
be i.i.d. copies of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
and be independent of everything else.
For
0\leq t\leq 1
, consider the interpolation

\phi_{N}(t):=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\bigg\langle\prod_{m=1}^{2r}V\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t)\big)\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}

where

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl(x_{\rho}^{m,0}(t),\ldots,x_{\rho}^{m,p-1}(t)\bigr):=\sqrt{t}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\rho}^{m}+\sqrt{1-t}\,\xi^{m}

and
V:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\displaystyle V(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p-1})=U(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p-1})-\mathbb{E}U(\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{p-1}).

Note that the left-hand side of (
51
) equals
\phi_{N}(1)
and
\phi_{N}(0)=0.

Following the same argument as (
29
), we have
\phi_{N}(1)\leq\psi_{N}(1)
, where

\displaystyle\psi_{N}(t):=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\bigg\langle\prod_{m=1}^{2r}V\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t)\big)\bigg\rangle_{\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right\rangle_{\!\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\displaystyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}{\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\textstyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}{\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}{\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}}.

It suffices to show that
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\psi_{N}(1)=0.
To justify this limit, observe that
\psi_{N}(0)=0
and we thus aim at showing

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}|\psi_{N}^{\prime}(t)|dt=0.

(52)

First of all, by chain rule,
\psi_{N}^{\prime}(t)
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{2r}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\bigg\langle\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{l}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t))\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{{S}_{\rho}^{m,l}}{\sqrt{t}}-\frac{\xi_{l}^{m}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2r}V\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m^{\prime}}(t)\big)\bigg\rangle_{\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right\rangle_{\!\mathchoice{\raisebox{13.0pt}{$\displaystyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}{\raisebox{13.0pt}{$\textstyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}{\raisebox{13.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}{\raisebox{13.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\!N-1,\beta^{\prime}$}}}.

Note that the Gibbs measures
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
and
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
do not depend on
g_{\cdot N}
, and thus, as before, we can compute this derivative by first taking the expectation (inside the Gibbs averages
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
) only with respect to
g_{\cdot N}
and
(\xi_{l}^{m})_{0\leq l\leq p-1,1\leq m\leq 2r}
and then using the Gaussian integration by parts. With these, for fixed
m
and
l
, the relevant terms in the above display become

\displaystyle\ \ \mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\bigg[\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{l}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t))\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{{S}_{\rho}^{m,l}}{\sqrt{t}}-\frac{\xi_{l}^{m}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2r}V\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m^{\prime}}(t)\big)\bigg]=\text{(I) + (II) + (III)},

where

\displaystyle{\rm(I)}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\bigg[\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{l}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t))\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2r}V\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t)\big)\bigg]\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t}}\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[{S}_{\rho}^{m,l}X_{N,\beta}(\rho)\big],

\displaystyle{\rm(II)}

\displaystyle:=\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l^{\prime}=0\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\bigg[\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x_{l}\partial x_{l^{\prime}}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t))\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2r}V\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m^{\prime}}(t)\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\cdot\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\Big[\Bigl(\frac{S_{\rho}^{m,l}}{\sqrt{t}}-\frac{\xi_{l}^{m}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Bigr)x_{\rho}^{m,l^{\prime}}(t)\Big],

and

\displaystyle{\rm(III)}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l^{\prime}=0\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\bigg[\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{l}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t))\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{l^{\prime}}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m^{\prime}}(t))\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime\prime}\neq m,m^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{2r}V\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m^{\prime\prime}}(t)\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\cdot\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\Big[\Bigl(\frac{S_{\rho}^{m,l}}{\sqrt{t}}-\frac{\xi_{l}^{m}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Bigr)x_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}(t)\Big].

Here
\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
is the expectation with respect to
g_{\cdot N}
and
(\xi_{l}^{m})_{0\leq l\leq p-1,1\leq m\leq 2r}.

To handle these terms, we first note that for any
(\tau^{m,l})_{1\leq m\leq 2r,1\leq l\leq p-1}
sampled from
G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
, coordinates of
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\rho}^{m}
are mean-zero Gaussian random variables with uniformly bounded variance for any
N\geq 1
, and so are the coordinates of
\boldsymbol{x}_{\rho}^{m}(t)
for any
0\leq t\leq 1
. Consequently, from the given assumptions on the functions
F_{0},\ldots,F_{p-1}
and the Hölder inequality, the first expectations in (I), (II), and (III) are bounded by an absolute constant independent of
N
,
t
, and
(\tau^{m,l})_{1\leq m\leq 2r,1\leq l\leq p-1}
. Furthermore, in (II) and (III), using Gaussian integration by parts implies that for all
0<t<1,

1\leq m,m^{\prime}\leq 2r
, and
0\leq l,l^{\prime}\leq p-1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\Big[\Bigl(\frac{S_{\rho}^{m,l}}{\sqrt{t}}-\frac{\xi_{l}^{m}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Bigr)x_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}(t)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[S_{\rho}^{m,l}S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{l}^{m}\xi_{l^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}\big].

From these, we conclude that there exists a positive constant
K_{0}
independent of
N
and
t
such that

\displaystyle|\psi^{\prime}_{N}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{m=1}^{2r}\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[S_{\rho}^{m,l}X_{N,\beta}(\rho)\big]\big|\big\rangle^{\rho}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}

(53)

\displaystyle\ \ +K_{0}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=1}^{2r}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[S_{\rho}^{m,l}S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{l}^{m}\xi_{l^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}\big]\big|\big\rangle^{\rho}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}.

(54)

Note that
t^{-1/2}
is integrable on
(0,1].
It remains to show that the expectations in (
53
) and (
54
) vanish in the limit as
N\to\infty
and then
\epsilon\to 0
, which implies in (
52
).

Estimation of (
53
):
We claim that for any
1\leq m\leq 2r
and
0\leq l\leq p-1,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[S_{\rho}^{m,l}X_{N,\beta}(\rho)\big]\big|\big\rangle^{\rho}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}=0.

(55)

If
l\neq 0
, then recalling
S_{\rho}^{m,l}=\dot{X}_{N,\beta}(\tau^{m,l})
, the same argument as that used in the estimation of (
32
) in the proof of Theorem
3.7
yields (
55
).
As for
l=0
, from
S_{\rho}^{m,0}=\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}(\boldsymbol{\tau}),
we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[S_{\rho}^{m,0}X_{N,\beta}(\rho)\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{p}p}{N^{p-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots i_{p-1}=1\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\bigl(\tau_{i_{1}}^{m,1}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}^{m,p-1}-\big\langle\tau_{i_{1}}^{m,1}\cdots\tau^{m,p-1}_{i_{p-1}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigr)\rho_{i_{1}}\cdots\rho_{i_{p-1}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}p\Bigl(R(\tau^{m,1},\rho)\cdots R(\tau^{m,p-1},\rho)-\big\langle R(\tau^{m,1},\rho)\cdots R(\tau^{m,p-1},\rho)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\Bigr)+o_{N}(1).

Since
\tau^{m,1},\ldots,\tau^{m,p-1}\sim G_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}
, we always have

. This combining with (

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[S_{\rho}^{m,0}X_{N,\beta}(\rho)\big]\big|\big\rangle^{\rho}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\leq\beta_{p}p\bigl((q_{P}+\epsilon)^{p-1}-(q_{P}-\epsilon)^{p-1}\bigr)\leq 2\beta_{p}p(p-1)\epsilon.

Sending
\epsilon\downarrow 0
yields (
55
).

Estimation of (
54
):
We claim that for any
1\leq m,m^{\prime}\leq 2r
and
0\leq l,l^{\prime}\leq p-1,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\big[S_{\rho}^{m,l}S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{l}^{m}\xi_{l^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}\big]\big|\big\rangle^{\rho}_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}=0.

(56)

First consider
l,l^{\prime}\neq 0
. Note that, in this case,

\displaystyle S_{\rho}^{m,l}=\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{m,l})\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}=\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}).

In view of the proof of Theorem
3.7
, when
m=m^{\prime}
and

, the control of

has been implemented in the
Estimation of (

)
; if

, or

, or both, this expectation can also be controlled by the
Estimation of (
31
)
. As a conclusion, we readily see that (
56
) holds under these two cases. It remains to consider the scenarios when at least one of the
l,l^{\prime}
is equal to 0. Without loss of generality, we assume
l=0
and divide our discussion into two cases:

Case 1:
m\neq m^{\prime}.
Note that
\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{0}^{m}\xi_{0}^{m^{\prime}}\big]=0
.

(1a)

When
l^{\prime}=0
,

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[S_{\rho}^{m,0}S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},0}]\big|

\displaystyle=\big\lvert\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}^{m}})\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}^{m^{\prime}}})]\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq p\Big|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}R(\tau^{m,\ell},\tau^{m^{\prime},\ell})-q_{P}^{p-1}\Big|+p\Big|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\big\langle R(\tau^{m,\ell},\tau^{m^{\prime},\ell})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-q_{P}^{p-1}\Big|

\displaystyle\ \ \ +p\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1}=1\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\tau^{m,1}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m,p-1}_{i_{p-1}}\big\langle\tau^{m^{\prime},1}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m^{\prime},p-1}_{i_{p-1}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-q_{P}^{p-1}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\ \ \ +p\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1}=1\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\tau^{m^{\prime},1}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m^{\prime},p-1}_{k_{p-1}}\big\langle\tau^{m,1}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m,p-1}_{i_{p-1}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-q_{P}^{p-1}\Bigg|+o_{N}(1).

Taking Gibbs average with respect to

and using Jensen’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[S_{\rho}^{m,0}S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},0}]\big|\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\leq 4p\bigg\langle\bigg|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}R(\tau^{1,\ell},\tau^{2,\ell})-q_{P}^{p-1}\bigg|\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+o_{N}(1).

(1b)

If

, then

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[S_{\rho}^{m,0}S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}]\big|

\displaystyle=\big\lvert\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}({\tau}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}})]\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{p}p\Big|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}R(\tau^{m,\ell},\tau^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}})-q_{P}^{p-1}\Big|+\beta_{p}p\Big|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\big\langle R(\tau^{m,\ell},\tau^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-q_{P}^{p-1}\Big|

\displaystyle\ \ +\beta_{p}p\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1}=1\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\tau^{m,1}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m,p-1}_{i_{p-1}}\big\langle\tau^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}_{i_{p-1}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-q_{P}^{p-1}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\ \ +\beta_{p}p\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1}=1\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\tau^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}_{i_{p-1}}\big\langle\tau^{m,1}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau^{m,p-1}_{i_{p-1}}\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-q_{P}^{p-1}\Bigg|+o_{N}(1)

and thus,

\displaystyle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[S_{\rho}^{m,0}S_{\rho}^{m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}]\big|\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\leq 4\beta_{p}p\bigg\langle\bigg|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}R(\tau^{1,\ell},\tau^{2,l^{\prime}})-q_{P}^{p-1}\bigg|\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+o_{N}(1).

Case 2:
m=m^{\prime}
.

(2a)

If
l^{\prime}=0
, then
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{0}^{m}\xi_{0}^{m}]=p(1-q_{P}^{p-1})
, and

\displaystyle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[S_{\rho}^{m,0}S_{\rho}^{m,0}]-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{0}^{m}\xi_{0}^{m}]\big|\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\bigl[\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})^{2}\bigr]-p(1-q_{P}^{p-1})\Big|\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq 3p\bigg\langle\Big|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}R(\tau^{1,\ell},\tau^{2,\ell})-q_{P}^{p-1}\Big|\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+o_{N}(1).

(2b)

If
l^{\prime}\neq 0
, then
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{0}^{m}\xi_{l^{\prime}}^{m}]=\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})
, and

\displaystyle\big\langle\big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}[S_{\rho}^{m,0}S_{\rho}^{m,l^{\prime}}]-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{0}^{m}\xi_{l^{\prime}}^{m}]\big|\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Big|\mathbb{E}_{g_{\cdot N}}\bigl[\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}(\boldsymbol{\tau})\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{m,l^{\prime}})\bigr]-\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\Big|\right\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{p}p\bigg\langle\Big|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=1\\
\ell\neq l^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{p-1}R(\tau^{m,\ell},\tau^{m,l^{\prime}})-q_{P}^{p-2}\Big|\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\ \ +3\beta_{p}p\bigg\langle\Big|\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}R(\tau^{1,\ell},\tau^{2,l^{\prime}})-q_{P}^{p-1}\Big|\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+o_{N}(1).

From (1a), (1b), (2a), and (2b), we can use the concentration of the overlap in Lemma
3.9
to conclude that (
56
) holds as long as one of the

equals zero. This completes our proof.

5.2
Derivation of the Onsager correction term

Recall
s^{\rho}
from (
39
). We proceed to show that
X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})
is asymptotically equal to the sum of the cavity field
X_{N,\beta}(\langle\tau\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho})
and the Onsager term
\zeta^{\prime\prime}(q_{P})(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}
by using the multivariate central limit theorem, Theorem
5.1
. This is the first place in our derivation that gives rise to the Onsager correction term.

Proposition 5.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\Big[X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})-X_{N,\beta}(\langle\tau\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho})-\zeta^{\prime\prime}(q_{P})(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big]^{2}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}=0.

We establish the proof of Proposition
5.2
in this subsection. Recall that
X_{N,\beta}(s^{\rho})=\sum_{p\geq 2}B_{p}^{\rho}
, where
B_{p}^{\rho}
is defined in (
42
).
Write

\displaystyle X_{N,\beta}(\langle\tau\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho})+\zeta^{\prime\prime}(q_{P})(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}=\sum_{p\geq 2}\Big(E_{p}^{\rho}+\beta_{p}^{2}p(p-1)q_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big),

where

\displaystyle E_{p}^{\rho}:=\frac{\beta_{p}\sqrt{p!}}{N^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}g_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p-1}N}\langle\tau_{i_{1}}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\cdots\langle\tau_{i_{p-1}}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}.

Thus, to prove Proposition
5.2
, we will show that each
B_{p}^{\rho}
is approximately equal to
E_{p}^{\rho}+\beta_{p}^{2}p(p-1)q_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}
, for each
p\geq 2
fixed. To begin with, we firstly show that the infinite sum in
X_{N,\beta}(\langle\tau\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho})
can be approximated by a finite sum of
E_{p}^{\rho}
, similar to what we have done for
X_{N,\beta}(\langle\sigma\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha})
in Proposition
4.2
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Recall the expression
B_{p}^{\rho}
,
\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}
, and
\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}
from(
46
), (
50
), and (
49
), respectively. Noticing that

\displaystyle\beta_{p}Z_{N,p}(\boldsymbol{\tau})

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})+E_{p}^{\rho},

we can write

\displaystyle B_{p}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}\frac{C_{p}^{\rho}}{D_{p}^{\rho}}=\beta_{p}\frac{\big\langle Z_{N,p}(\boldsymbol{\tau})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\big\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}

\displaystyle=E_{p}^{\rho}+\beta_{p}\frac{\big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\big\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}.

(57)

For the numerator in the second term of (
57
), we can use the relations

\displaystyle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})

\displaystyle=\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{l})+\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\cosh x=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm 1}e^{\varepsilon x}

to rewrite it as

\displaystyle\ \ \Big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh\bigl(\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{l})+\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\bigr)\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p-1}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\exp\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}\big(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\big)\Big)\Big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}e^{\varepsilon_{l}\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{l})}\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho},

where
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{p-1})\in\Sigma_{p-1}
. Applying Theorem
5.1
to the last term with the choices of functions
F_{0}(x)=x
and
F_{l}(x)=e^{\varepsilon_{l}x}
for
l=1,2,\ldots,p-1
, we get, for each
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\Sigma_{p-1}
,

\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\!\bigg[\!\Big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}e^{\varepsilon_{l}\dot{X}_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{l})}\!\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\!-\!\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})e^{\frac{p-1}{2}(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))}\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}\bigg]^{4}\right\rangle_{N,\beta}\!=0,

where the second term comes from

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(F_{0}(\xi_{0})\cdots F_{p-1}(\xi_{p-1})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\xi_{0}\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}e^{\varepsilon_{l}\xi_{l}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}\mathbb{E}(\xi_{0}\xi_{l})\mathbb{E}(e^{\varepsilon_{1}\xi_{1}})\cdots\mathbb{E}(e^{\varepsilon_{p-1}\xi_{p-1}})

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})e^{\frac{p-1}{2}(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))}\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}.

Denote

\displaystyle Q_{N,p}^{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2^{p-1}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\Sigma_{p-1}}\exp\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}\big(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\big)\Big)\mathbb{E}\Big(\xi_{0}\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}e^{\varepsilon_{l}\xi_{l}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}p(p-1)q_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})e^{\frac{p-1}{2}(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(1))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\sinh\bigl(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\bigr)\cosh^{p-2}\bigl(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\bigr).

It then follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\Big[\Big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh\bigl(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h\bigr)\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-Q_{N,p}^{\rho}\Big]^{2}\Big\rangle_{N,\beta}\Bigg\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{p-1}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\Sigma_{p-1}}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\exp\Big(4\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}\big(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\big)\Big)\Big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{1}{2^{p-1}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\Sigma_{p-1}}\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big[\Big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}e^{\varepsilon_{l}X_{N,\beta}^{\rho}(\tau^{l})}\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-\beta_{p}pq_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})e^{\frac{p-1}{2}(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))}\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}\Big]^{4}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta},

where on the right-hand side, the last expectation vanishes as
N\to\infty
and then
\epsilon\downarrow 0
. The first expectation equals

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh^{4}\bigl(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\bigr)\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\rho)+h)\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh^{4}\bigl(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\bigr)\bigg\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}},

where again we adapted a change of measure for
G_{N,\beta}
as in Lemma
3.2
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality to decompose the product inside
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}
and taking the expectation with respect to
g_{\cdot N}
first, we see this term stays bounded for all
N\geq 1
and small enough
\epsilon>0
; this is because
X_{N,\beta}(\tau)
has a variance uniformly bounded by
C_{\beta}
for all
N,\beta
and
\tau\in\Sigma_{N-1}
. We thus conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\Big[\Big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}^{\rho}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh\bigl(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h\bigr)\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-Q_{N,p}^{\rho}\Big]^{2}\Big\rangle_{N,\beta}=0,

(58)

which takes care of the numerator in the second term of (
57
).
For the denominator there, applying Theorem
5.1
to the last term with
F_{0}(x)=1
and
F_{l}(x)=e^{\varepsilon_{l}x}
for
l=1,2,\ldots,p-1
and mimicking the computation for the numerator, we will get

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big[\Big\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh\bigl(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h\bigr)\Big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}-\tilde{Q}_{N,p}^{\rho}\Big]^{2}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}=0,

(59)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{N,p}^{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2^{p-1}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\Sigma_{p-1}}\exp\Big\{\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\varepsilon_{l}\big(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\big)\Big\}\mathbb{E}\Big(\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}e^{\varepsilon_{l}\xi_{l}}\Big)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{p-1}{2}(\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(q_{P}))}\cosh^{p-1}\bigl(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\bigr).

Furthermore, by Proposition
3.11
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big|\frac{Q_{N,p}^{\rho}}{\tilde{Q}_{N,p}^{\rho}}-\beta_{p}p(p-1)q_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Bigr|^{2}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}\\
&=\beta_{p}p(p-1)q_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\\
&\qquad\qquad\cdot\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\big|\tanh\bigl(\big\langle X_{N,\beta}(\tau)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}+h\bigr)-\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\bigr|^{2}\Big\rangle_{N,\beta}=0.\end{split}

(60)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big[B_{p}^{\rho}-E_{p}^{\rho}-\beta_{p}^{2}p(p-1)q_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\big]^{2}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}^{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big[\frac{\big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\big\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}-\beta_{p}p(p-1)q_{P}^{p-2}(1-q_{P})\langle\sigma_{N}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big]^{2}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\beta_{p}^{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big[\frac{\big\langle\dot{Z}_{N,p}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\big\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p-1}\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau^{l})+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}-\frac{Q_{N,p}^{\rho}}{\tilde{Q}_{N,p}^{\rho}}\Big]^{2}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

Appendix A
Proofs of Propositions
4.2
and
4.3

The proofs of Propositions
4.2
and
4.3

are based on the following lemma:

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a constant
K=K(\beta,h)>0
such that for all
N\geq 1
and small
\epsilon>0,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 2}\big(\mathbb{E}\big\langle|A_{p}^{\alpha}|^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}\big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq K\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{p\geq 2}\big(\mathbb{E}\big\langle|B_{p}^{\rho}|^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}\big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq K.

Proof.

For notation simplicity, we suppress the superscript
\alpha
and write
A_{p}^{\alpha}
as
A_{p}
. We handle the series of
A_{p}
first.
Note that for each
p
, we only need to consider the case,
N\geq p
, otherwise
A_{p}=0
by the definition (
41
). Write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle A_{p}^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big(\frac{\beta_{p}^{2}p!}{N^{p-1}}\Big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}\langle\sigma_{i_{1}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\cdots\langle\sigma_{i_{p-1}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big)^{4}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\beta_{p}^{2}p!}{N^{p-1}}\Big)^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{i}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{i}_{4}}\mathbb{E}\Big[G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)\prod_{l=1}^{4}g_{\mathbf{i}_{l},N}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big],

(62)

where the second sum is over all
\mathbf{i}_{l}=(i_{l,1},\cdots,i_{l,p-1})
, for
l=1,2,3,4
that are
(p-1)
-tuples with strictly increasing coordinates from
\{1,2,\ldots,N-1\}^{p-1}
. We note that there are
\binom{N-1}{p-1}
choices for each
\mathbf{i}_{l}
. Write
g_{i_{l,1},\ldots,i_{l,p-1},N}
as
g_{\mathbf{i}_{l},N}
and let
\Delta_{p}:=\beta_{p}\sqrt{p!}{N^{-(p-1)/2}}.
We have

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}^{2}\binom{N-1}{p-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{p}^{2}p!}{N^{p-1}}\,\frac{(N-1)(N-2)\cdots(N-(p-1))}{(p-1)!}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}^{2}p\frac{N-1}{N}\cdot\frac{N-2}{N}\cdots\frac{N-(p-1)}{N}\leq\beta_{p}^{2}p.

Also, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
p,N,\mathbf{i}
such that for any
0\leq k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}\leq 4
, if we let
d=k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4},
then

\displaystyle\Bigl|\partial^{k_{1}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}}\partial^{k_{2}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}}\partial^{k_{3}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{3},N}}\partial^{k_{4}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{4},N}}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta_{p}^{d}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)\leq C\Delta_{p}^{d}(p-1)^{d}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)

(63)

and

\displaystyle\Bigl|\partial^{k_{1}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}}\partial^{k_{2}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}}\partial^{k_{3}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{3},N}}\partial^{k_{4}}_{g_{\mathbf{i}_{4},N}}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta_{p}^{d}(p-1)^{d}.

(64)

These can be established by an induction argument on
d.

Now we divide the collection of
(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{i}_{2},\mathbf{i}_{3},\mathbf{i}_{4})
into three cases and compute, respectively, an upper bound for the summand in (
62
) under each case. In the following discussion,
C_{1},C_{1}^{\prime},C_{2},C_{2}^{\prime},\ldots
are absolute constants independent of
N
and
p.

•

Case I: all 4 tuples are distinct.
Applying Gaussian integration by part and the chain rule, we get

\displaystyle\ \ \ \Big|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{3},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{4},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big|\leq C_{1}\Delta_{p}^{4}(p-1)^{4}\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha).

Since the number of choices for
(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{i}_{2},\mathbf{i}_{3},\mathbf{i}_{4})
in Case I are no more than
\binom{N-1}{p-1}^{4}
, the summation in (
62
) for Case I is bounded by

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}^{4}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{N}}\sum_{{\tiny\rm Case\,\,I}}\Big|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{3},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{4},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{p}^{4}\cdot\binom{N-1}{p-1}^{4}\cdot C_{1}\Delta_{p}^{4}(p-1)^{4}\leq C_{1}\beta_{p}^{8}p^{8}.

•

Case II: there are three distinct tuples in
(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{i}_{2},\mathbf{i}_{3},\mathbf{i}_{4})
.
Without loss of generality, suppose
\mathbf{i}_{1}=\mathbf{i}_{2}
and they are both different from distinct
\mathbf{i}_{3},\mathbf{i}_{4}
. In this case, again using Gaussian integration by part twice and the chain rule, each summand in (
62
) is bounded in absolute value by

\Bigl|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g^{2}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{3},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{4},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Bigr|\leq C_{2}\Delta_{p}^{2}(p-1)^{2}\mathbb{E}g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}^{2}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha).

It follows that

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}^{4}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{N}}\sum_{{\tiny\rm Case\,\,II}}\Big|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{3},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{4},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{\prime}\Delta_{p}^{4}\cdot\binom{N-1}{p-1}^{3}\cdot\Delta_{p}^{2}(p-1)^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{\prime}\Delta_{p}^{6}\binom{N-1}{p-1}^{3}(p-1)^{2}\leq C_{2}^{\prime}\beta_{p}^{6}p^{5}

•

Case III: there are no more than two distinct tuples.
In this case, we have three possibilities, each bounded in absolute value respectively as follows:

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g^{2}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g^{2}_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g^{2}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g^{2}_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N},

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g^{1}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g^{3}_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)\left|g^{1}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g^{3}_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}\right|,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g^{4}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g^{4}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}^{4}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{N}}\sum_{{\tiny\rm Case\,\,III}}\Big|\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)g_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{3},N}g_{\mathbf{i}_{4},N}\prod_{l=1}^{4}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\Delta_{p}^{4}\cdot\binom{N-1}{p-1}^{2}\cdot\left[\mathbb{E}g^{2}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g^{2}_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}+\mathbb{E}\left|g^{1}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}g^{3}_{\mathbf{i}_{2},N}\right|+\mathbb{E}g^{4}_{\mathbf{i}_{1},N}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}^{\prime}\Delta_{p}^{4}\cdot\binom{N-1}{p-1}^{2}\leq C_{3}^{\prime}\beta_{p}^{4}p^{2}.

Combining all three cases, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle A_{p}^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\bigl(\beta_{p}^{4}p^{2}+\beta_{p}^{6}p^{5}+\beta_{p}^{8}p^{8}\bigr).

Since
\sum_{p\geq 2}2^{p}\beta_{p}^{2}<\infty
, we have
\beta_{p}^{2}=o(2^{-p})
as
p\to\infty
. Choosing
p_{0}
large enough such that
\beta_{p}\leq 2^{-p/2}
and
p^{2}<2^{p/4}
for all
p>p_{0}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{p>p_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\big\langle A_{p}^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}\bigr)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\sum_{p>p_{0}}\bigl(\beta_{p}^{4}p^{2}+\beta_{p}^{6}p^{5}+\beta_{p}^{8}p^{8}\bigr)^{1/4}\leq 3C_{4}\sum_{p>p_{0}}\beta_{p}p^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{4}\sum_{p>p_{0}}2^{-p/2}p^{2}\leq 3C_{4}\sum_{p>p_{0}}2^{-p/4}<\infty.

For the summability for the series of
B_{p}^{\rho}
, the proof is essentially the same; the only change is that in (
62
),
\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}
will be replaced by
s_{j}^{\rho}
.
Notice that

|s_{j}^{\rho}|\leq 1

and any partial derivatives of
s_{j}^{\rho}
of degree
d\leq 4
with respect to the variables
(g_{\mathbf{i},N})_{\mathbf{i}}
are bounded by
\Delta_{p}^{d}
up to an absolute constant independent of
p,N
and
\mathbf{i}
. For example,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\partial s_{j}^{\rho}}{\partial g_{\mathbf{i},N}}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\Delta_{p}\frac{\big\langle\tau_{j}\tau_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}\tau_{N}\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\big\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}\Big.

\displaystyle\qquad\Big.-\Delta_{p}\frac{s_{j}^{\rho}\big\langle\tau_{i_{1}}\cdots\tau_{i_{p-1}}\tau_{N}\sinh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}{\big\langle\cosh(X_{N,\beta}(\tau)+h)\big\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}}\bigg|\leq 2\Delta_{p}.

More general partial derivatives can be controlled by an induction argument on the number of differentiations.
This implies that (
64
) with
\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\alpha}
replaced by
s_{j}^{\rho}
is also valid.
We omit the rest of the details.
∎

, but in a slightly simpler manner.
We begin by rewriting

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle|E^{\rho}_{p}|^{4}\big\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\Big(\frac{\beta_{p}^{2}p!}{N^{p-1}}\Big)^{2}\bigg(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p-1}\leq N-1}^{N-1}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1},N}\langle\sigma_{i_{1}}\rangle_{N,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\cdots\langle\sigma_{i_{p-1}}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\bigg)^{4}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\beta_{p}^{2}p!}{N^{p-1}}\Big)^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{i}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{i}_{4}}\mathbb{E}\Big[G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)\prod_{l=1}^{4}g_{\mathbf{i}_{l},N}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\langle\sigma_{i_{l,k}}\rangle_{N-1,\beta^{\prime}}^{\rho}\Big].

When applying Gaussian integration by parts to control the last equation, we only need to differentiate
G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)
with respect to
g_{\mathbf{i}_{l},N}
and the bounds of the partial derivatives of
G_{N,\beta}(\alpha)
given by (
63
). An identical argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
implies our claim (
67
), the summability of
\bigl(\mathbb{E}\langle|E_{p}^{\rho}|^{4}\rangle_{N,\beta}\bigr)^{1/4}
. With this claim, our assertion follows immediately since, similar to (
65
),

\mathbb{E}\bigg\langle\bigg[\sum_{p>p_{0}}E_{p}^{\rho}\bigg]^{4}\bigg\rangle_{N,\beta}\leq\bigg(\sum_{p>p_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\langle|E_{p}^{\rho}|^{4}\rangle_{N,\beta}\bigr)^{1/4}\bigg)^{4},

and the right hand side can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
p_{0}
sufficiently large.
∎

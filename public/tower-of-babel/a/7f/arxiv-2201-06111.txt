Toward explicit Hilbert series of quasi-invariant polynomials in characteristic p p and q q -deformed quasi-invariants
Frank Wang

Abstract
We study the spaces Q m Q_{m} of m m -quasi-invariant polynomials of the symmetric group S n S_{n} in characteristic p p . Using the representation theory of the symmetric group we describe the Hilbert series of Q m Q_{m} for n = 3 n=3 , proving a conjecture of Ren and Xu [ 12 ] . From this we may deduce the palindromicity and highest term of the Hilbert polynomial and the freeness of Q m Q_{m} as a module over the ring of symmetric polynomials, which are conjectured for general n n . We also prove further results in the case n = 3 n=3 that allow us to compute values of m , p m,p for which Q m Q_{m} has a different Hilbert series over characteristic 0 and characteristic p p , and what the degrees of the generators of Q m Q_{m} are in such cases. We also extend various results to the spaces Q m , q Q_{m,q} of q q -deformed m m -quasi-invariants and prove a sufficient condition for the Hilbert series of Q m , q Q_{m,q} to differ from the Hilbert series of Q m Q_{m} .

Introduction

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field, and let
S_{n}
act on
V=\mathbbm{k}^{n}
by permuting the coordinates. A commonly studied object is the space of
invariant
or
symmetric
polynomials, which can be described as the set of
P\in\mathbbm{k}[V]=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that for all transpositions
s_{i,j}\in S_{n}
,
(1-s_{i,j})P=0
. A generalization of the space of invariant polynomials is the space
Q_{m}
of
m
-quasi-invariant polynomials, defined as the set of
P\in\mathbbm{k}[V]
such that for all transpositions
s_{i,j}\in S_{n}
,
(1-s_{i,j})P
is divisible by
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
. Thus
m
-quasi-invariant polynomials are polynomials such that
(1-s_{i,j})P
vanishes on the reflection hyperplane
x_{i}=x_{j}
to some high order, a generalization of invariant polynomials, where
(1-s_{i,j})P
vanishes on this hyperplane to infinite order.

Quasi-invariants were introduced by Chalykh and Veselov
[
5
]
in their work on quantum Calogero-Moser systems. Quasi-invariant polynomials were also found to be useful in the study of rational Cherednik algebras, where they can be used to describe representations of the spherical subalgebra of a rational Cherednik algebra
[
2
]
. These applications in the theory of integrable systems and representation theory have generated interest in the subject, leading to many interesting developments. In
[
7
]
, Feigin and Veselov studied the relation between quasi-invariants and Calogero-Moser systems. In
[
8
]
, Felder and Veselov computed the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}
in characteristic zero using the Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations. Berest and Chalykh later defined quasi-invariant polynomials of a general complex reflection group
[
1
]
. Quasi-invariants in characteristic
p
were first studied by Ren and Xu, where they computed the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}
for
S_{2}
and proved sufficient conditions for the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}
in characteristic
p
to differ from the Hilbert series in characteristic zero
[
12
]
.

The
q
-deformed quasi-invariants are a natural deformation of quasi-invariants introduced by Chalykh
[
4
]
to prove various conjectures about Macdonald polynomials. In
[
3
]
, Braverman, Etingof, and Finkelberg introduced a deformation of the rational Cherednik algebra which they called the cyclotomic DAHA. They showed that the cyclotomic DAHA acts on the space
Q_{m,q}
of
q
-deformed quasi-invariants and used this to show that
Q_{m,q}
is a flat deformation of
Q_{m}
for all but countably many values of
q
. In this paper, we work toward a characterization of the specific values of
q
for which this deformation is not flat, and what the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}
is in such cases.

In Section
2
we give basic definitions and properties of
Q_{m}
for an arbitrary symmetric group. In Section
3
we focus on the case
S_{3}
and prove our main result about the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}
. In Section
4
we discuss how our results in Section
3
might be used to determine the values of
m,p
such that the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}
in characteristic
p
differs from that in characteristic zero and to compute the Hilbert series explicitly in such cases. We also describe some partial progress we made in this direction using the Opdam shift operator. In Section
5
we discuss some preliminary results and conjectures about the Hilbert series of
q
-deformed quasi-invariants, particularly in the case
S_{3}
.

2
Quasi-invariant polynomials for
S_{n}

We start with the definition of quasi-invariant polynomials. Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field, and let
S_{n}
act on
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
by permuting the variables. Let
s_{i,j}\in S_{n}
denote the transposition swapping the
i
-th and
j
-th indices.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. A polynomial
P\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is
m
-quasi-invariant if for all transpositions
s_{i,j}\in S_{n}
,
(1-s_{i,j})P
is divisible by
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
. We denote by
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
the space of all
m
-quasi-invariants in
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
denote the space of polynomials in
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
which are invariant with respect to the action of
S_{n}
. Some useful properties of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
are described in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
6
]
)
.

.

1)

\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}\subset Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
,
Q_{0}(n,\mathbbm{k})=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})\subset Q_{m^{\prime}}(n,\mathbbm{k})
if
m>m^{\prime}
.

2)

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
is a ring.

3)

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
is a finitely generated module over
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
.

Note that
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
admits a grading by the degrees of the polynomials. Thus we may define its Hilbert series.

Definition 2.3
.

The Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
is

H_{m}(t)=\sum_{d\geq 0}\dim Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]t^{d}

where
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]
is the graded component of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
consisting of polynomials of homogeneous degree
d
.

Note that by the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials,
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
is a free polynomial algebra generated by symmetric polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}
of degrees
1,\dots,n
, respectively
[
10
]
. Thus since
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
is a finitely generated module over
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}=\mathbbm{k}[P_{1},\dots,P_{n}]
by Proposition
2.2
, we can write

H_{m}(t)=\frac{G_{m}(t)}{\prod_{d=1}^{n}(1-t^{d})}

where
G_{m}(t)
is a polynomial by Hilbert’s syzygy theorem. We say that
G_{m}(t)
is the Hilbert polynomial associated with
H_{m}(t)
. Note that describing the Hilbert polynomial is related to describing the generators and relations of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
as a
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
-module.

From now on, we will assume the characteristic of
\mathbbm{k}
does not divide
n!
, so that the representation theory of
S_{n}
over
\mathbbm{k}
is non-modular. The following lemma and proposition are standard results in the study of quasi-invariants, for example they a direct consequence of the decomposition of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
as a representation of the spherical rational Cherednik algebra (see
[
2
]
) in the case
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
is a representation of
S_{n}
.

Now, consider a graded component
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]
of the quasi-invariants. It is a subrepresentation of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
, and moreover it is finite dimensional since it is a subspace of the finite dimensional space
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}][d]
. Thus, since we are working in the non-modular case, by Maschke’s theorem it decomposes as a direct sum

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\mathrm{Irrep}(S_{n})}Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]_{\tau}

where
\mathrm{Irrep}(S_{n})
is the set of irreducible representations of
S_{n}
and
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]_{\tau}
is the subspace of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]
on which
S_{n}
acts by a direct sum of copies of
\tau
. Then for
\tau\in\mathrm{Irrep}(S_{n})
, let us define

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\tau}=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]_{\tau}

to be the isotypic components of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
.

Proposition 2.5
.

1)

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\tau}
is a module over
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
.

2)

We have the decomposition

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\mathrm{Irrep}(S_{n})}Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\tau}

as a
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space and hence as a
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
-module as well.

By the above proposition, to study the generators and relations of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
it suffices to study the generators and relations of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\tau}
. This will be our primary method of studying
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
throughout the rest of this paper. Let us denote by triv and sign the trivial and sign representations of
S_{n}
, respectively. We deal with these two cases here.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume
\text{char\,}\mathbbm{k}\neq 2
. As
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
-modules, we have

1)

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{triv}}
is freely generated by
1
.

2)

Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
is freely generated by
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
.

Proof.

1)
Note that the statement that
S_{n}
acts by the trivial representation on
P
is equivalent to the statement that
P
is
S_{n}
-invariant, so
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{triv}}\subset\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
. On the other hand, for any
P\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
and any
s_{i,j}\in S_{n}
we have
(1-s_{i,j})P=P-P=0
is divisible by
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
, so
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{triv}}=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
.

2)
Let
P\in Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
and let
s_{i,j}\in S_{n}
. Then since
P
is in the sign representation we have
s_{i,j}P=-P
, so
(1-s_{i,j})P=2P
is divisible by
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
. So
P
is divisible by
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
for all
i
and
j
, and thus it is divisible by
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
.

Let us write
P=K\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
for some polynomial
K
. Note that

s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}=-\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}

for all
s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\in S_{n}
, so we have

s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}P=-P=-K\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}=s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}K\bigg(s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}\bigg)=-s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}K\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}

so
s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}K=K
. Since
S_{n}
is generated by transpositions,
K
is
S_{n}
-invariant, and thus
P
is in the
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
-module generated by
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
. Conversely, for any
K\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
and
s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\in S_{n}
, we have

s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\Big(K\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}\Big)=-K\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}

and

(1-s_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})\Big(K\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}\Big)=2K\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}

is divisible by
(x_{i^{\prime}}-x_{j^{\prime}})^{2m+1}
, so the
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
-module generated by
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
is contained in
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
. So
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
is exactly this module. Freeness then follows from the fact that
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is an integral domain.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

It was proved in
[
12
]
that the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(2,\mathbbm{k})
is
\frac{1+t^{2m+1}}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}
, regardless of the characteristic of
\mathbbm{k}
. Proposition
2.6
provides an alternate proof of this result in the case
\text{char\,}\mathbbm{k}\neq 2
. Namely, note that the only irreducible representations of
S_{2}
are triv and sign. Thus the generators of
Q_{m}(2,\mathbbm{k})
are 1 (of degree 0) and
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}
(of degree
2m+1
). Since these generators belong to different irreducible representations of
S_{2}
, there are no relations between them, and thus the Hilbert polynomial is
1+t^{2m+1}
and the Hilbert series is
\frac{1+t^{2m+1}}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}
.

H_{m}(t)=\frac{1+2t^{3m+1}+2t^{3m+2}+t^{6m+3}}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})}.

(3.1)

In this section, we work toward an explicit description of the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
in characteristic
p>3
. Note that there are three irreducible representations of
S_{3}
: triv, sign, and the 2-dimensional standard representation which we will denote by std. Proposition
2.6
computes the generators of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{triv}}
and
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
, so to compute the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
we only need to consider the space

This section is dedicated to proving the main result of this paper, which is the following theorem, conjectured in
[
12
]
. Note the similarity to (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field of characteristic
p>3
. Then
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is freely generated by standard representations of degree
d
and
6m+3-d
where
d
is an integer depending on
m,p
that satisfies
2m+1\leq d\leq 3m+1
. In particular, the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
is

H_{m}(t)=\frac{1+2t^{d}+2t^{6m+3-d}+t^{6m+3}}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})}.

To start, let
V
be a copy of the standard representation, and let
s_{i,j}\in S_{3}
. Then
V
contains a one dimensional
-1
eigenspace of
s_{i,j}
. Let us denote this eigenspace by
V_{i,j}^{-}
. Then we have

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V\subset Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
be a copy of the standard representation, and let
P\in V_{i,j}^{-}
. Then we have
P+sP+s^{2}P
=0 where
s=(1\,2\,3)\in S_{3}
and
P=(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}K
for some polynomial
K
that is invariant under the action of
s_{i,j}
. Conversely, let
K
be an
s_{1,2}
-invariant polynomial such that

(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K+(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2m+1}sK+(x_{3}-x_{1})^{2m+1}s^{2}K=0.

Then
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
either belongs to
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
or the
-1
eigenspace of
s_{1,2}
in some copy of std inside
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
.

Proof.

The proof of the first statement is analogous to the proof of Proposition
2.6
. Since
P\in V_{i,j}^{-}
, we have
(1-s_{i,j})P=2P
is divisible by
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
, so
P=(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}K
for some polynomial
K
. Then since
P
and
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
are both
s_{i,j}
-antiinvariant,
K
must be
s_{i,j}
-invariant. The fact that
P+sP+s^{2}P=0
is a direct consequence of
P
being an element of the standard representation.

For the second statement, note that since
K
is
s_{1,2}
-invariant,
sK=s_{1,3}K
and
s^{2}K=s_{2,3}K
. So we have

(1-s_{1,3})\Big((x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K\Big)=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K-(x_{3}-x_{2})^{2m+1}sK=-(x_{3}-x_{1})^{2m+1}s^{2}K,

(1-s_{2,3})\Big((x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K\Big)=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K-(x_{1}-x_{3})^{2m+1}s^{2}K=-(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2m+1}sK

so
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
is
m
-quasi-invariant. On the other hand, since
s_{1,2}
sends the vector space spanned by
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
to itself and
s_{1,3}
and
s_{2,3}
send
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
to polynomials in the space spanned by
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
and
(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2m+1}sK
, the span of the
S_{3}
orbit of
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
is at most 2 dimensional. If it is 1 dimensional, then
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
is contained in a copy of sign inside
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
since
s_{1,2}
acts by
-1
. If it is 2 dimensional, then since the only 2 dimensional irreducible representation of
S_{3}
is std,
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
belongs to some copy of std inside
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
. That it is in the
-1
eigenspace of
s_{1,2}
is clear by applying
s_{1,2}
to
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K
.
∎

Note that since
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is a graded
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
-module, we may assume its generators are homogeneous. We may also describe generating representations of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
, rather than generating elements.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
V
be a generating representation of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
and let
P\in V_{i,j}^{-}
. Let us write
P=(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}K
. Then
K
is not divisible by any nonconstant symmetric polynomial.

Proof.

Since
P
is in
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
, it satisfies
P+sP+s^{2}P=0
where
s=(1\,2\,3)
. Assume for contradiction that
K
is divisible by some symmetric polynomial
Q
. Then it is clear that
P/Q+s(P/Q)+s^{2}(P/Q)=0
and
P/Q=(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}(K/Q)
, so
P/Q
is contained in
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
by Lemma

. But then
P
is in the
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
-module generated by

, so it is generated by an element of lower degree and thus is not contained in a generating representation.
∎

For a standard representation
V
consisting of homogeneous polynomials of degree

\deg V=d
. The following lemma makes use of this, and will prove to be useful both in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be distinct generating representations of

. Let

. Then

is a nonzero element of

As_{2,3}B-Bs_{2,3}A
is contained in

because the quasi-invariants form a ring by Proposition

. It is also contained in a sign representation since it spans the representation

inside

. Thus it is contained in

By Lemma

we can write

for

-invariant polynomials

\displaystyle As_{2,3}B-Bs_{2,3}A

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K(x_{1}-x_{3})^{2m+1}s_{2,3}L-(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}L(x_{1}-x_{3})^{2m+1}s_{2,3}K

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}(x_{1}-x_{3})^{2m+1}(Ks_{2,3}L-Ls_{2,3}K).

So
As_{2,3}B-Bs_{2,3}A

Ks_{2,3}L

s_{2,3}
-invariant. Let us assume that

. Since
Ks_{2,3}L

s_{2,3}L
. If it is a factor of

, then

s_{2,3}Q
is a factor of
K
. Then we also have that
s_{1,2}Q
and
s_{1,2}s_{2,3}Q
are factors of
K

K
is
s_{1,2}
-invariant. Then
s_{2,3}s_{1,2}Q
is a factor of
Ks_{2,3}L
. If it is a factor in
s_{2,3}L
, then note that
s_{1,3}s_{2,3}s_{1,2}Q
is a factor of
s_{2,3}L

is
s_{1,3}
-invariant. But we have
s_{1,3}s_{2,3}s_{1,2}=s_{2,3}
, so
s_{2,3}Q
is a factor of
s_{2,3}L
, which implies
Q
is a factor of
L
, as desired. So we may assume
s_{2,3}s_{1,2}Q
is a factor of
K
. Then we also have
s_{1,2}s_{2,3}s_{1,2}Q=s_{1,3}Q
is a factor of
K
since
K
is
s_{1,2}
-invariant, and thus
K
is divisible by
sQ
for all
s\in S_{3}
.

So
K
is divisible by the least common multiple of all of the
sQ
, which we will denote by
S
.
S_{3}
fixes
S
as a factor of
K
, so it must act on
S
by scalars. It cannot act on
S
trivially, as then
S
would be a symmetric polynomial factor of
K
, and
K
has no symmetric polynomial factors by Corollary
3.3
. So
S_{3}
must act on
S
by sign. Note also that we can only have one such factor of
K
on which
S_{3}
acts by sign, as otherwise their product would be a symmetric polynomial factor of
K
. So every factor of
K
is also a factor of
L
except for possibly a factor in the sign representation. We may apply everything above to
L
as well to get the same result in the other direction, yielding a bijection between all irreducible factors of
K
and
L
except maybe a factor of the sign representation in either case. Since
K
and
L
are both
s_{1,2}
-invariant, acting on both of them by
s_{1,2}
makes it clear that either they both have an extra factor of the sign representation or they both do not.

If
K
and
L
do not have an additional factor in the sign representation, then we have a bijection between all irreducible factors of
K
and
L
, and thus they are equal up to a scalar, as desired. Otherwise, note that by Proposition
2.2
, the sign factors lie in
Q_{0}(3,\mathbbm{k})
, and by Proposition
2.6
they are symmetric polynomial multiples of
(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{2}-x_{3})(x_{3}-x_{1})
. But these symmetric polynomial multiples must be 1, as otherwise
K
and
L
would be divisible by nonconstant symmetric polynomials, which violates Corollary
3.3
. So the sign factors are both
(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{2}-x_{3})(x_{3}-x_{1})
. Thus we still have a bijection between the factors of
K
and
L
, so
K
and
L
are equal up to a scalar, as desired.

Note that
As_{2,3}B-Bs_{2,3}A
has degree
\deg V+\deg W
. Since
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{sign}}
is generated by a polynomial of degree
6m+3
by Proposition
2.6
, we have
\deg V+\deg W\geq 6m+3
.
∎

The above lemma brings us closer to the proof of Theorem

, however we still need a couple more lemmas.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that there exist generating representations
V,W
of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
such that
\deg V+\deg W=6m+3
. Then
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is a free module over
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
generated by
V
and
W
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that there exists some other generating representation
U
of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}

3.4
we have
\deg U\geq\deg W
. Let

we have

As_{2,3}B-Bs_{2,3}A=c\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}

where

polynomial in

As_{2,3}C-Cs_{2,3}A=Q\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}

for some symmetric polynomial
Q
. But then instead of
U
, we may take the representation generated by

as the generating representation of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
. But we have

\displaystyle As_{2,3}(cC-QB)-(cC-QB)s_{2,3}A

\displaystyle=c(As_{2,3}C-Cs_{2,3}A)-Q(As_{2,3}B-Bs_{2,3}A)

\displaystyle=(cQ-Qc)\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}

\displaystyle=0

which contradicts Lemma
3.4
. If
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}

R_{1}As_{2,3}(R_{2}B)-R_{2}Bs_{2,3}(R_{1}A)=R_{1}As_{2,3}(R_{1}A)-R_{1}As_{2,3}(R_{1}A)=0

∎

Now, note that we have
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]

we may write it as

, where
P^{\prime}
is divisible by
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}
and

. Since
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}
is symmetric, the action of

preserves this decomposition, so for any

we have
(1-s_{i,j})P=(1-s_{i,j})P^{\prime}+(1-s_{i,j})P^{\prime\prime}
. Then since
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]
, it follows that

. Moreover, if

is a generator of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
, then

is also a generator since

is generated by

. So we may assume all generators of

belong to
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]

Lemma 3.6

Let

be generating representations of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
such that
\deg V<\deg W
and
\deg W-\deg V>1
. Then we may take the generating representations to be

such that either

or

is in

.

Proof.

Let
A\in V_{1,2}^{-},B\in V_{1,2}^{-}
and let

. We may assume
K,L\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]
. Note that since

are invariant with respect to

are symmetric in
x_{1}-x_{3}
and
x_{2}-x_{3}
. Recall from Section

that by the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials,
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{n}}

P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}

, respectively. We may take

P_{2}=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2}+(x_{3}-x_{1})^{2},\quad P_{3}=(x_{1}+x_{2}-2x_{3})(x_{2}+x_{3}-2x_{1})(x_{3}+x_{1}-2x_{2})

so that
P_{2},P_{3}\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]
. Because
\deg L-\deg K=\deg W-\deg V>1
, there exists some nonzero symmetric polynomial
P
generated by
P_{2},P_{3}
of degree
\deg L-\deg K
. Moreover, note that

P_{2}^{3}-2P_{3}^{2}=54\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}

so if we choose

to be a monomial in
P_{2},P_{3}
then it will not be divisible by
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}
since there are no polynomial relations between
P_{2},P_{3}
.
Let us fix such a polynomial

.

Note that
A,B\in Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})
if and only if
K,L
are divisible by
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]

and
L
in

, since

QK=c_{K}(x_{1}+x_{2}-2x_{3})(x_{1}-x_{3})^{d}(x_{2}-x_{3})^{d},\quad L=c_{L}(x_{1}+x_{2}-2x_{3})(x_{1}-x_{3})^{d}(x_{2}-x_{3})^{d}

to be the representation generated by

.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By (
3.1
), the Hilbert polynomial of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
is
1+2t^{3m+1}+2t^{3m+2}+t^{6m+3}
, and a result of Ren and Xu
[
12
]
says that the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
is at least as big as the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
in all degrees. In particular, this implies that
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
contains a generator of degree at most
3m+1
.

We will first show that
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is freely generated by representations of degree
d
and
{6m+3-d}
for some
d
. We proceed by induction on
m
. The base case
m=0
is a direct consequence of the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, so we proceed directly to the inductive step.

Assume that
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is freely generated by generators
V,W
of degree
d
and
e
respectively where
d+e=6m+3
and
d<e
. If
e-d>1
then by Lemma
3.6
we may assume one of
V,W
is in
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
. Let us assume it is
W
. Then note that
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}V
and
W
are both contained in
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
, and the sum of their degrees is
6m+3+6=6(m+1)+3
. If they are not generators then they must be generated by the same generator, as otherwise their respective generators would violate Lemma
3.4
. But this would imply that they are not independent over
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
, which cannot happen since
V
and
W
are independent over
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
. So they are generators, and by Lemma
3.5

Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is a free module generated by them. The same proof also works in the case when
V
is in
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})
.

Now, consider when
e-d=1
. There exists a generator
U
of
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
of degree at most
3(m+1)+1=d+3
by the above. Note also that
U
is generated by
V,W
since it is contained in
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
by Proposition
2.2
, so
\deg U\geq d
. We will proceed by casework on
\deg U
. If
\deg U=d
, then we must have
U=V
since
V
is the only standard representation in
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
of degree
d
. Then note that both
V
and
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}W
are in
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
, and they freely generate
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
by the same argument as above. If
\deg U=d+1
then we have
U=QV+W
for some symmetric polynomial

of degree 1. But recall that all generators of the quasi-invariants lie in
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]
, and the only symmetric polynomial of degree 1 is
P_{1}=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}
, which does not lie in
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]
. Thus we must have
Q=0
and
U=W
. Then
W
lies in
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})
, so the same proof as above holds. If
\deg U=d+2
, then since there are no symmetric polynomials of degree 1 in
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]
we must have
U=P_{2}V
. But then any
P\in U
is divisible by
P_{2}
, which contradicts Corollary
3.3
. If
\deg U=d+3
, then by Lemma
3.4
there exists no other generators of
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
of degree at most
d+3
. But then there must exist a generator of degree
d+4=3(m+1)+2
since otherwise the Hilbert series of
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbb{C})
would be larger than that of
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})
in degree
3(m+1)+2
. Then by Lemma
3.5

Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is freely generated by these two generators.

Thus
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
is generated by representations of degree
d
and
6m+3-d
. Note that one of
d,6m+3-d
is at most
3m+1
, so we may assume

. Let

be of degree
d
. Then
(1-s_{i,j})P
is also of degree

and is nonzero for some

since

is not in the trivial representation. But it is divisible by
(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}
, so it must be of degree at least

. So we get

. Note that

only depends on

and not on the ground field since quasi-invariants are defined by systems of linear equations with integer coefficients. Since std has dimension 2, we get that the Hilbert polynomial is

so the Hilbert series is
\frac{1+2t^{d}+2t^{6m+3-d}+t^{6m+3}}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})}
.
∎

4
Conditions for the Hilbert series to differ from characteristic 0

A question of interest that was studied in
[
12
]
was to find the values of
m,p
such that
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
has a different Hilbert series than
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
, where
\text{char\,}\mathbbm{k}=p
. The following theorem brings us closer to solving this problem for
n=3
in the non-modular case.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V,W
be the generating representations of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
and let
A\in V_{1,2}^{-},B\in W_{1,2}^{-}
where the coefficients of each of
A,B
are coprime integers. Let

As_{1,2}B-Bs_{1,2}A=c\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2m+1}.

Let
\mathrm{char}\,\mathbbm{k}=p>3
. Then the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
is different from the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
if and only if
p
divides
c
.

Proof.

First, assume that the Hilbert series is different in characteristic
p
. Then note that since the Hilbert polynomial over characteristic zero is
1+2t^{3m+1}+2t^{3m+2}+t^{6m+3}
by (
3.1
),
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
must be generated by representations of degree
d
and
6m+3-d
for
d<3m+1
by Theorem
3.1
. Then note that
6m+3-d>3m+2
, so the images of
V
and
W
in characteristic
p
are generated by the generator of degree
d
. But then
A,B
are not independent over
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
, so we have
As_{1,2}B-Bs_{1,2}A=0
, and we must have
p|c
.

Now, assume that
p|c
. Then
A,B
are not both generators of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
by Lemma
3.4
, and they are nonzero since their coefficients are coprime. Let
\deg A=3m+1,\deg B=3m+2
. If
A
is not a generator then it is generated by some representation of degree less than
3m+1
, so the Hilbert series in characteristic
p
is different from that in characteristic zero since no such generator exists in characteristic zero. If
B
is not a generator then it is generated by some representation of degree less than
3m+2
. But note that it cannot be generated by a representation of degree
3m+1
, as the only symmetric polynomial in degree 1 is
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}
and
B\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]
. So there must be a generator of degree less than
3m+1
, and the Hilbert series is different in characteristic
p
.
∎

Using the methods from the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we were able to calculate the generators
A,B
as above recursively. Let us be more precise by calling the generators of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})

A_{m}
and
B_{m}
. Let
A_{m}=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}K_{m},B_{m}=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2m+1}L_{m}
. Then we expressed
K_{m},L_{m}
as polynomials in
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}
with coefficients that are scalar multiples of

(x_{1}-x_{3})^{r}(x_{2}-x_{3})^{r},(x_{1}+x_{2}-2x_{3})(x_{1}-x_{3})^{r}(x_{2}-x_{3})^{r}

for some
r
. Recalling that the generators of
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
are
P_{2},P_{3}
, note that
K_{m+1}
is a linear combination of
P_{3}K_{m}
and
P_{2}L_{m}
and
L_{m+1}
is a linear combination of
P_{2}^{2}K_{m}
and
P_{3}L_{m}
. Thus by choosing appropriate linear combinations such that the constant terms as polynomials in
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}
vanish, we were able to construct the generators
K_{m+1},L_{m+1}
. We were then able to construct the coefficients
c
in Theorem
4.1
and compute their prime factors to find the set of
p>3
for which the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
differs from the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})
when
\text{char\,}\mathbbm{k}=p
.

In
[
12
]
Ren and Xu proved a sufficient condition, that if there exists integers
a,k
that satisfy

\frac{mn(n-2)+{n\choose{2}}}{n(n-2)k+{n\choose{2}}-1}\leq p^{a}\leq\frac{mn}{nk+1}

(4.1)

then the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is different from that of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
. They also conjectured that this condition is necessary. Using their programs, they confirmed this conjecture for
n=3,
m\leq 15,p<50
. Using the method described above, we were able to confirm the conjecture for a much larger range of values, namely
n=3,m\leq 3000,3<p<\infty
. Thus we have strong evidence that the conjecture is true, at least in the case
n=3,p>3
.

Closer inspection of the inductive step in the proof of Theorem
3.1
also provides a way to compute the degrees of the generators in the cases where they are different in characteristic

. Let
V,W
be the generating representations of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
, and assume that they are of degree
3m+1
and

. Then from the proof of Theorem
3.1
we see that the generators of
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
are of degree
3(m+1)+1
and
3(m+1)+2
unless either
V
or
W
is in
Q_{m+1}(3,\mathbbm{k})_{\mathrm{std}}
. For the sake of this argument it will not matter which representation it is, so let us say that it is

be the maximum positive integer such that

. For all

, we have that the minimal degree generator of

is

, and for all

, so the Hilbert series is equal to the Hilbert series in characteristic 0.

Note that in

12

the authors explicitly computed elements of

4.1
The Opdam Shift Operator

In this section we will describe some partial results towards proving the conjecture of Ren and Xu described above using the Opdam shift operators
O_{m}
introduced in
[

Definition 4.2
.

The Opdam shift operator
O_{m}
is the differential operator (i.e. operator in the algebra
\mathbb{Z}\left[x_{i},\partial_{x_{i}},\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{j}}\right]
) whose action on symmetric polynomials is given by

\prod_{i<j}\left(D_{i}(m)-D_{j}(m)\right)\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})

(4.2)

where

D_{i}(k)=\partial_{x_{i}}-k\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}(1-s_{ij})

is the Dunkl operator.

Note that even though
O_{m}
is defined according to
4.2
, its action on polynomials is not given by
4.2
since this is not a differential operator. Instead, to compute explicitly
O_{m}
as a differential operator one must observe how
O_{m}
acts on symmetric polynomials as a differential operator, and then take that differential operator as the definition of
O_{m}
.

Example 4.3
.

In 3 variables, one can compute explicitly that

O_{m}=(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}-x_{3})(x_{2}-x_{3})(\partial_{x_{1}}-\partial_{x_{2}})(\partial_{x_{1}}-\partial_{x_{3}})(\partial_{x_{2}}-\partial_{x_{3}})+6(1-2m)(1-3m)(2-3m)

+\sum_{cyc}\Bigg[\left((-2+3m)(x_{1}-x_{3})(x_{2}-x_{3})+(1-2m)(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\right)(\partial_{x_{1}}-\partial_{x_{2}})^{2}

+\left((6-22m+20m^{2})(x_{1}-x_{2})+4m(-1+m)\left(\frac{(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2}}{x_{3}-x_{1}}-\frac{(x_{1}-x_{3})^{2}}{x_{3}-x_{2}}\right)\right)(\partial_{x_{1}}-\partial_{x_{2}})\Bigg].

The connection between Opdam shift operators and quasi-invariant polynomials was discovered in

Theorem 4.4

(

We have

5
q
-deformed quasi-invariants

For this section we will mainly work over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 5.1
.

For a nonzero complex number
q
, a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is called
q
-deformed
m
-quasi-invariant if for all transpositions
s_{i,j}\in S_{n}
,
(1-s_{i,j})P
is divisible by
\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})
. We denote by
Q_{m,q}(n)
the space of
q
-deformed
m
-quasi-invariants.

Note that when
q=1
we recover ordinary quasi-invariants, i.e.
Q_{m,1}(n)=Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
. In
[
3
]
it was proved that
Q_{m,q}(n)
is a flat deformation of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
when
q
is a formal parameter, i.e. the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(n)
over
\mathbb{C}(q)
is equal to the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
. This implies that if
q\in\mathbb{C}
, the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(n)
is equal to the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
for all but a countable set of values for
q
. The main goal of this section is to work toward understanding the values of
q
for which the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(n)
differs. The following lemma uses the above to relate the various forms of quasi-invariants introduced in this paper.

Lemma 5.2
.

1)

The Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(n)
is at least as big as the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
in all degrees.

2)

Let
p
be a prime,
q
a
p
-th root of unity, and
\mathbbm{k}
a field of characteristic
p
. Then the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
is at least as big as the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(n)
in all degrees.

Proof.

Fix a degree
d
, and consider the space
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[d]
over
\mathbb{C}(\textbf{q})
where
q
is a formal parameter. Note that the conditions for a polynomial to be in
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[d]
can be described as a system of linear equations with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[\textbf{q},\textbf{q}^{-1}]
, so
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[d]
can be described as the nullspace of a matrix with entries in
\mathbb{Z}[\textbf{q},\textbf{q}^{-1}]
.
Q_{m,q}(n)[d]
is then the nullspace of the same matrix when we specialize
\textbf{q}=q
. Specializing a matrix can only increase the dimension of its nullspace, which proves
1)
.

Now, consider when
q
is a
p
-th root of unity. Then note that
Q_{m,q}(n)[d]
is the nullspace of a matrix with entries in
\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
. If we specialize this matrix to a matrix in
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
, the nullspace of this matrix becomes the space of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}][x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that
(1-s_{i,j})P
is divisible by
{\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})}
for all
s_{i,j}
. But
q
satisfies
q^{p}-1=0
, and in characteristic
p
we have
q^{p}-1=(q-1)^{p}=0
, so
q=1
. Thus we obtain that this nullspace is exactly
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})[d]
, which proves
2)
.
∎

Many basic properties of quasi-invariants also hold for

-deformed quasi-invariants with the obvious modifications and analogous proofs. Proposition

, Lemma

, and Proposition

directly translate from

to

. Analogously to Proposition 2.6, we have

Proposition 5.3
.

As
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
-modules, we have

1)

Q_{m,q}(n)_{\mathrm{triv}}
is freely generated by
1
.

2)

is freely generated by
\prod_{i<j}\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})
.

Thus by the same argument as in Remark
2.7
, we have

Corollary 5.4
.

The Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(2)
is
\frac{1+t^{2m+1}}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}
for all
q
.

Similarly, Lemma
3.2
, Corollary

, Lemma

, and Lemma
3.5
all hold for

as well if all instances of

are replaced with
\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})
with the same proofs. We also have the following theorem analogous to Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
V,W
be the generating representations of
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(3)
and let
A\in V_{1,2}^{-},B\in W_{1,2}^{-}
where the coefficients of each of
A,B
are coprime elements of
\mathbb{C}[\textbf{q},\textbf{q}^{-1}]
. Let

As_{1,2}B-Bs_{1,2}A=c\prod_{i<j}\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j}).

where
c\in\mathbb{C}[\textbf{q},\textbf{q}^{-1}]
. Then the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(3)
is different from the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
if and only if
q
is a root of
c
.

Proof.

First, assume that the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(3)
is different from the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
. Then one of the specializations of
A,B
in
Q_{m,q}(3)
is not a generating representation, so it is generated by a representation of lower degree. Without loss of generality, say that
A
is generated by
C
. Then since
\deg A+\deg B=6m+3
,
\deg C+\deg B<6m+3
, so by Lemma
3.4

B
is also generated by
C
. But then
A,B
are not independent over
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{S_{3}}
, so
As_{1,2}B-Bs_{1,2}A=0
and thus
c(q)=0
.

Now, assume
q
is a root of
c
. Then the specializations of
A,B
in
Q_{m,q}(3)
are not both generators by Lemma 3.4, and they are nonzero since their coefficients are coprime. Let us assume
\deg A=3m+1,\deg B=3m+2
. If
A
is not a generator then it is generated by some representation of degree less than
3m+1
, so the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(3)
is larger than the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
since no such generator exists in
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
. If
B
is not a generator then it is generated by some generator of degree less than
3m+2
. If this representation is not
A
then we are done by the same argument as above. Note that if it is
A
, then up to a scalar we have
(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})A=B
in
Q_{m,q}(3)
since
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}
is the only symmetric polynomial of degree 1. We will show that this cannot happen, which will complete the proof.

Let
M=Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[3m+2]\cap\mathbb{C}[\textbf{q}][x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a
\mathbb{C}[\textbf{q}]
-module. Then
N=M/(\textbf{q}-q)M
is a module over
\mathbb{C}[\textbf{q}]/(\textbf{q}-q)\cong\mathbb{C}
, and it is easy to see that it is a submodule of
Q_{m,q}(n)[3m+2]
that contains the specializations of
(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})A,B
to
Q_{m,q}(n)
.

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in M
be a basis of
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[3m+2]
such that the images of
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
in
N
are nonzero. We will construct a basis of
k
elements in
N
using infinite descent on the sum of the degrees of
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
in
q
. Let
\bar{e}_{1},\dots,\bar{e}_{k}
be the images of
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
in
N
and assume
\bar{e}_{1},\dots,\bar{e}_{k}
satisfy some linear relation
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\bar{e}_{i}=0
where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
j
be an index such that
e_{j}
has the largest degree in
q
out of all
e_{i}
such that
a_{i}\neq 0
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}
is divisible by
\textbf{q}-q
and has degree at most the degree of
e_{j}
. Since
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
form a basis of
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[3m+2]
, it follows that
e_{1},\dots,\hat{e_{j}},\dots,e_{k},\frac{1}{\textbf{q}-q}\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}
is also a basis of
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[3m+2]
that lies in
M
.
\frac{1}{\textbf{q}-q}\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}
has a lower degree than
e_{j}
, so we have constructed a basis of
Q_{m,\textbf{q}}(n)[3m+2]
that has a lower sum of degrees in
q
than
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
. Since the sum of degrees must be nonnegative, this process must eventually terminate, after which there can be no relations between
\bar{e}_{1},\dots,\bar{e}_{k}
. After this process, since
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
form a basis of
M
,
\bar{e}_{1},\dots,\bar{e}_{k}
span
N
, so
N
has dimension equal to
M
. Note that this implies
(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})A
and
B
cannot be linearly dependent in
N
, as they are linearly independent in
M
, so we have the desired result.
∎

However, the proof of Lemma
3.6
does not immediately generalize to the
q
-deformed case. This is because unlike

is not contained in a subalgebra of

and thus we do not know if Theorem

holds for the

indeed holds for

.

Recall that in
[
12

Ren and Xu proved a sufficient condition for the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbbm{k})
to differ from the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Using their methods, we proved a similar sufficient condition in the
q
-deformed case.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
m\geq 0
,
n\geq 3
be integers, and let
q
be a primitive
p
-th root of unity, where
p
is an integer such that

\frac{mn(n-2)+{n\choose{2}}}{{n\choose{2}}-1}\leq p\leq mn.

Then the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(n)
is different from the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
.

Note the similarity between the inequality in this theorem and
4.1
. Namely, this inequality is obtained from
4.1
when we set
a=1,k=0
, but without the restriction that
p
must be prime.

Proof.

In
[
8
]
it was proved that the smallest positive degree term in the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(n,\mathbb{C})
has degree
mn+1
. For the values of
q
described above, we will exhibit a nonsymmetric
q
-deformed
m
-quasi-invariant of degree less than
mn+1
, which suffices for the proof.

Let
q
satisfy the condition in the theorem, and consider the polynomial

P_{m,q}=\begin{cases}x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p}&p\geq 2m+1\\
(x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p})\prod_{i\neq j}\prod_{k=\frac{p+1}{2}}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})&p<2m+1,p\text{ odd}\\
(x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p})\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}+x_{j})\prod_{i\neq j}\prod_{k=\frac{p+2}{2}}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})&p<2m+1,p\text{ even}\end{cases}.

(5.1)

Note that in both cases where
p<2m+1
, we have

\deg P_{m,q}=p+{n\choose{2}}(2m+1-p)\leq{n\choose{2}}(2m+1)-mn(n-2)-{n\choose{2}}=mn

so
P_{m,q}
always has degree less than
mn+1
. It suffices to show that
P_{m,q}\in Q_{m,q}(n)
. First, note that since
q
is a primitive
p
-th root of unity, we have

x_{i}^{p}-x_{j}^{p}=\prod_{k=-\frac{p-1}{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})

if
p
is odd and

x_{i}^{p}-x_{j}^{p}=\prod_{k=-\frac{p}{2}}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})

if
p
is even. If
p\geq 2m+1
, then this implies
x_{i}^{p}-x_{j}^{p}
is divisible by
\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})
. If
p<2m+1
and
p
is odd, then
P_{m,q}
is divisible by

\prod_{k=-\frac{p-1}{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})\prod_{k=\frac{p+1}{2}}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})(x_{2}-q^{k}x_{1})

=\pm q^{l}\prod_{k=-\frac{p-1}{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})\prod_{k=\frac{p+1}{2}}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})(x_{1}-q^{-k}x_{2})=\pm q^{l}\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})

for some integer
l
, and if
p
is even
P_{m,q}
is divisible by

\prod_{k=-\frac{p}{2}}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{2})\prod_{k=\frac{p+2}{2}}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})(x_{2}-q^{k}x_{1})

=\pm q^{l}\prod_{k=-\frac{p}{2}}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})(x_{1}-q^{\frac{p}{2}}x_{2})\prod_{k=\frac{p+2}{2}}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})(x_{1}-q^{-k}x_{2})=\pm q^{l}\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2}).

Now, we will show that
P_{m,q}\in Q_{m,q}(n)
when
p\geq 2m+1
. Let
i,j>2
. Then we have

(1-s_{1,2})(x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p})=2(x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p}),\quad(1-s_{1,i})(x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p})=x_{1}^{p}-x_{i}^{p},

(1-s_{2,i})(x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p})=x_{i}^{p}-x_{2}^{p},\quad(1-s_{i,j})(x_{1}^{p}-x_{2}^{p})=0,

and the desired result follows. Note that the products

\prod_{i\neq j}\prod_{k=\frac{p+1}{2}}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j}),\quad\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}+x_{j})\prod_{i\neq j}\prod_{k=\frac{p+2}{2}}^{m}(x_{i}-q^{k}x_{j})

are symmetric, so the cases where
p<2m+1
follow similarly.
∎

q
for which the Hilbert series of
Q_{m,q}(3)
differs from the Hilbert series of
Q_{m}(3,\mathbb{C})
. We conclude this section with a lemma about the lowest degree generator in the standard representation part of the
n=3
case.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
n=3
and
p,q
satisfy the condition in Theorem
5.6
. Then the polynomial
P_{m,q}
in
5.1
is the minimal degree generator of
Q_{m,q}(3)_{\mathrm{std}}
.

Proof.

Let us write
P_{m,q}=L\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})
for a polynomial
L
. Then assume for contradiction that there exists a generator of
Q_{m,q}(3)_{\mathrm{std}}
of lower degree than
P_{m,q}
. By Lemma
3.5
there can only be one such generator, and by Lemma
3.2
this generator can be expressed as
K\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})
for a polynomial
K
. Since this is the only generator of degree at most the degree of
P_{m,q}
,
P_{m,q}
must be a symmetric polynomial multiple of
K\prod_{k=-m}^{m}(x_{1}-q^{k}x_{2})
, and thus
L
must be a symmetric polynomial multiple of
K
. But it is easy to see directly from the definition of
P_{m,q}
that
L
is not divisible by any nontrivial symmetric polynomial, so we have a contradiction.
∎

Stability estimate for a partial data inverse problem for the convection-diffusion equation
Soumen Senapati † and Manmohan Vashisth ∗

Abstract
. In this article, we study the stability in the inverse problem of determining the time-dependent convection term and density coefficient appearing in the convection-diffusion equation, from partial boundary measurements. For dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , we show the convection term (modulo the gauge term) admits log-log stability, whereas log-log-log stability estimate is obtained for the density coefficient.



Keywords:
Inverse problems, partial Dirichlet to Neumann map, parabolic equation, Carleman estimates, stability estimate.

Mathematics subject classification 2010:
35R30, 35K20.

1.
Introduction

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded simply connected domain having
C^{2}
smooth boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. For
T>0
, let us introduce the parabolic operator
\mathcal{L}_{A,q}
in the cylinder

by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A,q}:=\partial_{t}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\partial_{j}+{A_{j}(t,x)}\right)^{2}+q(t,x),

(1.1)

where

and

We consider an initial boundary value problem (IBVP) known for the convection-diffusion equation which models physical processes like mass or heat transfer within a body and, also appears in probabilistic study of diffusion process (like, the Fokker-Planck and Kolmogorov equations), finance (like, the Black–Scholes or the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes) and chemical engineering (for describing the movement of macro-particles)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{A,q}u(t,x)=0,\ \ (t,x)\in Q,\\
u(0,x)=0,\ \ x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=f(t,x),\ \ (t,x)\in\Sigma:=(0,T)\times\Gamma.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(1.2)

We first briefly discuss some well-posedness results regarding the above IBVP. Following
[
16
]

1.2
). However, in the context of our article, we assume more regularity on the coefficients in the operator (

m>0

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(m)=\big\{(A,q)\in(W^{2,\infty}(Q))^{n}\times W^{1,\infty}(Q);\|A\|_{W^{2,\infty}(Q)}+\|q\|_{W^{1,\infty}(Q)}\leq m\big\}.

\displaystyle H^{p,q}\left((0,T)\times M\right):=L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{p}(M)\right)\cap H^{q}\left(0,T;L^{2}(M)\right),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{p,q}\left((0,T)\times M\right)}=\|u\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{p}(M)\right)}+\|u\|_{H^{q}\left(0,T;L^{2}(M)\right)}.

Further, we denote
H^{p,q}_{0}(\Sigma):=\{f\in H^{p,q}(\Sigma);f(0,x)=0,\ x\in\Omega\}

We prove here a boundary Carleman estimate involving for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{A,q}

5.6
) where no information is given. Now, we choose

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\phi(t,x)=\lambda^{2}t+\lambda x\cdot\omega

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{Q}e^{-2\phi(t,x)}\left(\lambda^{2}\lvert u(t,x)\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla_{x}u(t,x)\rvert^{2}\right)\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Omega}e^{-2\phi(T,x)}\left(\lambda\lvert u(T,x)\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla_{x}u(T,x)\rvert^{2}\right)\ \mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\lambda\int_{\Sigma_{+,\omega}}e^{-2\phi(t,x)}\omega\cdot\nu(x)|\partial_{\nu}u(t,x)|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}S_{x}\mathrm{d}t\leq C\int_{Q}e^{-2\phi(t,x)}\lvert\mathcal{L}_{A,q}u(t,x)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+C\lambda\int_{\Sigma_{-,\omega}}e^{-2\phi(t,x)}\lvert\omega\cdot\nu(x)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\nu}u(t,x)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}S_{x}\mathrm{d}t\end{aligned}

Proof.

We have to convexify the Carleman weight

[

]
where the following convexified weight has been considered for

\displaystyle\phi_{s}(t,x):=\lambda^{2}t+\lambda x\cdot\omega-\frac{s\left((x+x_{0})\cdot\omega\right)^{2}}{2}

where

is as mentioned in the line just before the statement of Theorm

.
∎

We write down the interior Carleman estimate which easily follows from Theorem

Corollary 3.2

(Interior Carleman estimate).

For

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{Q}e^{-2\phi(t,x)}\left(\lambda^{2}\lvert u(t,x)\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla_{x}u(t,x)\rvert^{2}\right)\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\int_{Q}e^{-2\phi(t,x)}\lvert\mathcal{L}_{A,q}u(t,x)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{aligned}

Construction of geometric optics solutions

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{A,q}=\mathcal{L}_{-A,\overline{q}}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &u(t,x)=e^{\phi(t,x)}\left(B_{g}+R_{g}\right)(t,x),\ \ \ \\
\textnormal{and}\ \ &v(t,x)=e^{-\phi(t,x)}\left(B_{d}+R_{d}\right)(t,x).\end{aligned}

\delta
accordingly.
But first we observe the following estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{\delta}^{2}A\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}=\left(\int_{B(0,R)}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y+\int_{B(0,R)^{c}}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leq\left(\underset{T_{1}}{\underbrace{\int_{B(0,R)}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+\left(\underset{T_{2}}{\underbrace{\int_{B(0,R)^{c}}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}.

(5.25)

T_{2}
can be easily estimated after using the apriori assumptions for the potentials. We see

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} T_{2}&=\int_{B(0,R)^{c}}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \leq\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}\langle\tau,\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\leq\ \frac{1}{R^{2}}\|\eta_{\delta}^{2}A\|_{H^{1}(Q)}^{2}\leq\ \frac{C}{\delta^{2}R^{2}}.\end{aligned}

(5.26)

To estimate
T_{1}
, we use lemma
5.2
. We break
T_{1}
into two parts. We consider
T_{1}=T_{11}+T_{12}
, where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} T_{11}&:=\int_{B(0,R)\cap\{(s,y);\ |y|\leq R^{-\frac{3}{n}}\}}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leq\ \|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{|y|\leq R^{-\frac{3}{n}}}\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\leq\ CR^{-2}\end{aligned}

(5.27)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} T_{12}&:=\int_{B(0,R)\cap\left\{(s,y);\ |y|>R^{-\frac{3}{n}}\right\}}|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}A}|^{2}(s,y)\ \mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leq e^{2R(1-\theta)}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+e^{\beta\lambda}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|\right)^{2\theta}\delta^{-12\theta}R^{6\theta+n+1+\frac{6}{n}}.\end{aligned}

(5.28)

Because of the support condition of
\{\eta_{\delta}\}_{\delta>0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\leq\|\eta_{\delta}^{2}A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\ C\delta.

(5.29)

Taking
\alpha=6+\frac{n^{2}+n+6}{n\theta}
and combining (
5.25
)-(
5.28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{R^{\alpha}}{\delta^{12}}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+e^{2\beta\lambda}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2}{\theta}}R^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}+\delta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\underset{I}{\underbrace{\frac{R^{\alpha}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}}}{\lambda\delta^{12}}}}+\underset{II}{\underbrace{\frac{R^{\alpha\theta}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}+2\beta\lambda}}{\delta^{12}}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}}}+\underset{III}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2}{\theta}}R^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}}}+\underset{IV}{\underbrace{\delta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}}\right).

(5.30)

We now choose
\lambda,\delta
and
R
in a way so that the terms (I), (III) and (IV) in (
5.30
) are comparable. That is when

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{R^{\frac{2}{3}}}\ \ \textnormal{and}\ \ \lambda=R^{\alpha+8+\frac{2}{3\theta}}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}}

(5.31)

and hence there exists
\kappa>0
(independent of
R
) such that II of (
5.30
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle e^{e^{\kappa R}}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}.

(5.32)

Combining (
5.30
)-(
5.32
), it is clear that

\displaystyle\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{R^{\frac{2}{3\theta}}}+e^{e^{\kappa R}}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}\right).

(5.33)

We now choose
R>0
large enough (which in turn depends on the smallness of partial DN map) which is
R=\frac{1}{\kappa}\log\big|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|\big|
, so that (
5.33
) becomes

\displaystyle\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\leq C\left(\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|+\left(\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\right)^{-\frac{2}{3\theta}}\right).

(5.34)

We note that, (
5.34
) can be easily derived for the case when
\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|
is not so small. This concludes the proof for stability of first order coefficients from the partial DN map.

Now we establish the stability result for the zeroth order term. There will be no zeroth order term left in (
5.6
) once we divide it by large
\lambda
. So we have to make necessary changes for deriving Fourier estimates. We explicitly use here the stability result for the first order terms (
5.34
). We consider a different exponentially growing solutions for
\mathcal{L}_{A_{2},q_{2}}
, whereas the geometric optics for
\mathcal{L}_{-A_{1},\overline{q}_{1}}
is same as before which is (
5.2
). We have

\displaystyle u_{2}(t,x)=e^{\phi(t,x)}\left(B_{2}+R_{2}\right)(t,x)

where

\displaystyle B_{2}(t,x)=e^{-i\left(t\tau+x\cdot\xi\right)}\eta_{\delta}(t)e^{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega\cdot A_{2}(t,x+s\omega)\ \mathrm{d}s\right)},

and
R_{2}\in L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\right)
satisfying for
k\in\{0,1,2\}

\displaystyle\|R_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{k}_{\lambda}(\Omega))}\leq C\lambda^{-1+k}\delta^{-3}\langle\tau,\xi\rangle^{3}.

(5.35)

For convenience, we rewrite the integral inequality

\displaystyle 2\int_{Q}(A\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{2})(t,x)\overline{v}(t,x)\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{Q}\widetilde{q}(t,x)u_{2}(t,x)\overline{v}(t,x)\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\Sigma}\overline{v}(t,x)\partial_{\nu}u(t,x)\ \mathrm{d}S_{x}\mathrm{d}t.\quad

(5.36)

First, we simplify all the terms present in left hand side of (
5.36
). We observe

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widetilde{q}(t,x)u_{2}(t,x)\overline{v}(t,x)=\widetilde{q}e^{-it\tau-ix\cdot\xi}\eta_{\delta}^{2}(t)e^{-\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega\cdot A(t,x+s\omega)\mathrm{d}s}+B_{2}(t,x)R(t,x)+B(t,x)R_{2}(t,x).\end{aligned}

(5.37)

We use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality alongwith (
5.3
) and (
5.35
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|B_{2}R\|_{L^{1}(Q)}+\|BR_{2}\|_{L^{1}(Q)}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\delta^{-3}\langle\tau,\xi\rangle^{3}

(5.38)

The other term present in the L.H.S of (
5.36
) is

\displaystyle 2(A\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{2})(t,x)\overline{v}(t,x)=2\left(\lambda\omega\cdot AB_{2}+\lambda\omega\cdot AR_{2}+A\cdot\nabla_{x}B_{2}+A\cdot\nabla_{x}R_{2}\right)(t,x)\left(\overline{B}+\overline{R}\right)(t,x)

(5.39)

which is estimated by using the remainder term estimates given in (
5.3
) and (
5.35
)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q}(A\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{2})(t,x)\overline{v}(t,x)\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq C\lambda\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\delta^{-3}\langle\tau,\xi\rangle^{3}.

(5.40)

To estimate the boundary term in the R.H.S of (
5.36
), we proceed as before and obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}\overline{v}(t,x)\partial_{\nu}u(t,x)\ \mathrm{d}S_{x}\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq C\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\lambda}}+\delta^{-6}\langle\tau,\xi\rangle^{3}e^{\beta\lambda}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|\right)

(5.41)

Here
\mathcal{K}
is the R.H.S of the boundary Carleman estimate (
3.1
) applied to
\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},q_{1}}
for
u
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\int_{Q}e^{-2\phi}|\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},q_{1}}u|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\lambda\int_{\Sigma_{-}(\omega)}e^{-2\phi}|\omega\cdot\nu(x)||\partial_{\nu}u|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}S_{x}\mathrm{d}t.

(5.42)

From (
5.5
) we have,
\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},q_{1}}u=2A\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{2}+\tilde{q}u_{2}
. Hence we see

\displaystyle e^{-\phi}\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},q_{1}}u=2\left(A\cdot\nabla_{x}B_{2}+\lambda\omega\cdot AR_{2}+A\cdot\nabla_{x}B_{2}+A\cdot\nabla_{x}R_{2}\right)+\tilde{q}(B_{2}+R_{2}).

(5.43)

Thus, we use (
5.35
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\leq C\left(\lambda^{2}\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+1+e^{\beta\lambda}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}\right)\delta^{-12}\langle\tau,\xi\rangle^{6}.

(5.44)

Combining (
5.36
)-(
5.44
), we conclude for
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\widehat{\eta_{\delta}^{2}\widetilde{q}}(\tau,\xi)\right|&=\left|\int_{Q}e^{-i\left(t\tau+x\cdot\xi\right)}\eta_{\delta}^{2}(t)\widetilde{q}(t,x)\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \leq C\left(\lambda\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+e^{\beta\lambda}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|\right)\delta^{-6}\langle\tau,\xi\rangle^{3}.\end{aligned}

(5.45)

Basically (
5.45
) gives estimate for the Fourier transform of
\eta_{\delta}^{2}\widetilde{q}
over the cone
\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{H}
in
\mathbb{R}^{1+n}
. So we apply Vessella’s conditional stability result
[
55
]
as done before to obtain this estimate over arbitrary large balls. Mimicing the arguments presented before, we get the following estimate similar to (
5.30
)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{q}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\leq C\left(\frac{R^{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\delta^{12}}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}}\left(\lambda^{2}\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+1+e^{\beta\lambda}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2}{\theta}}R^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}+\delta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \leq C\left(\underset{I}{\underbrace{\frac{R^{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}}}{\delta^{12}}\lambda^{2}\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}}}+\underset{II}{\underbrace{\frac{R^{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}}}{\delta^{12}}}}+\underset{III}{\underbrace{\frac{R^{\alpha\theta}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}+\beta\lambda}}{\delta^{12}}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}}}+\underset{IV}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2}{\theta}}R^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}}}+\underset{V}{\underbrace{\delta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}}\right)

(5.46)

We choose
\delta
and
R
such that (II),(IV) and (V) of (
5.46
) are comparable. That is when

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{R^{\frac{2}{3}}}\ \textnormal{and}\ \lambda=R^{\alpha^{\prime}+8+\frac{2}{3\theta}}e^{\frac{2R(1-\theta)}{\theta}}

here
\alpha^{\prime}=6+\frac{n+1}{\theta}
.
Now using the stability result (
5.34
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{q}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\leq C\left({e^{\kappa R}}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2\mu_{1}}+{e^{\kappa R}}\left|\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\right|^{-2\mu_{2}}+e^{e^{\kappa R}}\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{R^{\frac{2}{3\theta}}}\right)

(5.47)

for some constants
\kappa>0
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
.
Taking
R=\frac{\mu_{1}}{\kappa}\log\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\||
, we get from (
5.47
) that
\|\widetilde{q}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}
has the upper bound

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2\mu_{1}}\left(\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\right)^{-2\mu_{2}}+\left(\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\right)^{-\mu_{2}}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{2}|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||+\left(\log\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\right)^{-\frac{2}{3\theta}}.\end{aligned}

(5.48)

We note that our choice of
R
related to the smallness of
\delta
and largeness of
\lambda
. Hence, the estimate (
5.48
) is valid only when
\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|
is small enough. The other case follows easily. Also, we need smallness of
\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|
such that the following hold

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{\mu_{1}}\left(\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\right)^{-2\mu_{2}}+\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\leq C.

Thus for both the cases, we arrive at the following estimate where
C,\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}>0

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{q}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq C\left(\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\alpha_{1}}+\left|\log\left|\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\|\right|\right|^{-\alpha_{2}}\right).

(5.49)

Now we want to prove the stability result for
q:=q_{1}-q_{2}
. We recall

\displaystyle q(t,x)=\widetilde{q}(t,x)+\nabla_{x}\cdot A(t,x)+\left(|A_{1}|^{2}-|A_{2}|^{2}\right)(t,x).

Hence, we obtain the following

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq\|\widetilde{q}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+(2m+1)\|A\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}.

(5.50)

Since our assumptions on the first order perturbations are more than
H^{1}
, we can translate the
L^{2}
norm estimates to that of
H^{1}
using logarithmic convexity for Sobolev norms. Thus there exist
C>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
depending only on
m
and
Q
so that we have

\displaystyle\|A\|_{H^{1}(Q)}\leq C\|A\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{\theta}

(5.51)

Using (
5.51
), the
L^{2}
stability results in (
5.34
) and (
5.49
) in (
5.50
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq C\left(\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\|^{\beta_{1}}+\left|\log|\log|\log\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\||\right|^{-\beta_{2}}\right).

for some
C,\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}>0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

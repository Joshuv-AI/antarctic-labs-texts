Revisiting timelike geodesics in the Fisher/Janis–Newman–Winicour–Wyman spacetime Preprint: RUP-21-24
Keisuke Ota, Shinpei Kobayashi, Keisuke Nakashi

Abstract
We investigate the timelike geodesics and the periapsis precession of orbits in the Fisher-Janis-Newman-Winicour-Wyman spacetime. This spacetime represents the naked singularity spacetime in the Einstein-massless scalar system. We revisit the results in the previous studies and relax the assumptions about the eccentricity of a bound orbit and the size of a semilatus. We find that the negative periapsis precession occurs when the spacetime sufficiently deviates from the Schwarzschild spacetime. In particular, for the small eccentric orbits, we show the negative periapsis precession occurs for γ < 1 / 2 \gamma<1/2 , where γ \gamma is the deviation parameter from the Schwarzschild spacetime. We also obtain the analytical solutions for the special cases of γ = 0 , 1 / 2 , 1 / 4 \gamma=0,1/2,1/4 . Then, we show that the negative precession never occurs for γ = 1 / 2 \gamma=1/2 .



I
Introduction

According to general relativity, at the late stage of the gravitational collapse, the spacetime singularity is generally formed.
It is called the singularity theorem and was proved by Penrose and Hawking
Penrose 1965
;
Hawking 1967
;
Hawking and Penrose 1970
.
The spacetime singularity that can be observed is called a naked singularity.
If naked singularities are formed frequently, we can distinguish them from black holes by observing characteristic phenomena of the naked singularity.
With regards to this, Penrose proposed the cosmic censorship conjecture
Penrose 1969
;
Penrose 1979
.
This conjecture states that all spacetime singularities are hidden by black hole horizons:
there cannot be naked singularities.
However, despite many researchers attempt to prove it, no one has mathematically and rigorously proved it yet.
Then, researchers have searched for a candidate of the counterexamples.
In the last few decades, the possibilities of forming naked singularities at the final state of the gravitational collapse in some models have been pointed out (see for example,
Harada et al. 2002
;
Joshi and Malafarina 2011
and references therein).

There are many spacetimes representing naked singularities as solutions to the Einstein equation.
In this paper, we focus on a specific naked singularity spacetime:
the Fisher-Janis-Newman-Winicour-Wyman (FJNWW) spacetime.
This spacetime was first discovered by Fisher
Fisher 1948
,
and it was rediscovered by Janis, Newman, and Winicour
Janis et al. 1968
,
and independently by Wyman
Wyman 1981
.
Virbhadra showed Weyman’s solution is equivalent to the solution of Janis
et al.

Virbhadra 1997
.
For these reasons,
we call this spacetime the FJNWW spacetime, though it is often called the JNW spacetime.
The FJNWW spacetime is the most general static, spherically symmetric, and asymptotically flat spacetime in the Einstein-massless scalar systems in arbitrary dimensions
Roberts 1993
;
Xanthopoulos and Zannias 1989
.
The FJNWW spacetime reproduces the Schwarzschild spacetime by adjusting the scalar “charge” to zero, which is a conserved quantity associated with the scalar field.
Therefore, the FJNWW spacetime is useful for discussing the characteristic phenomena of naked singularities.

Since a naked singularity is not covered by a horizon,
it shows some characteristic observational signatures different from the black holes.
For this reason, many researchers have investigated observational signatures
of the FJNWW spacetime, e.g., gravitational lensing
Virbhadra et al. 1998
,
the observable images of shadows and thin accretion disks
Gyulchev et al. 2019
;
Sau et al. 2020
,
and the behavior of circular geodesics and the properties of accretion disks
Chowdhury et al. 2012
.
In particular, recent studies have shown that a naked singularity can exhibit a negative periapsis precession
Dey et al. 2019
;
Bambhaniya et al. 2019
;
Joshi et al. 2019
;
Dey et al. 2020
;
Solanki et al. 2021
.
The negative periapsis precession is that the periapsis precessions in the direction opposite to the direction of a particle motion.
If we find the negative periapsis precession near the gravitational source in future observations, it would be strong evidence of the existence of a naked singularity.
For example, the stellar motions in the Milky Way Galactic Center are continuously observed by GRAVITY and SINFONI
Abuter et al. 2017
;
Abuter and et al. 2020
;
Abuter et al. 2019
.
If there is a naked singularity at the Galactic Center, the periapsis precession may be observed as a smaller value.

In
Bambhaniya et al. 2019
;
Joshi et al. 2019
;
Dey et al. 2020
, the authors have investigated the periapsis precession of orbits with small eccentricity in the weak field approximation in the FJNWW spacetime.
Furthermore, in
Solanki et al. 2021
, the authors numerically calculated and showed that the periapsis precession can take a negative value in the rotating FJNWW spacetime.
In this paper, we revisit the results in
Joshi et al. 2019
, where the authors focus on orbits with large semilatus and small eccentricity.
We relax the assumptions about the eccentricity or the semilatus of orbits to determine the parameter region in which the negative periapsis precession occurs.
As the result, we show that when the deviation from the Schwarzschild spacetime is large enough,
the negative periapsis precession can occur even if the orbit is far away from the singularity.
Also, for the orbits with the small eccentricity, we show that the negative periapsis precession appears for
\gamma<1/2
, where
\gamma
is the deviation parameter from the Schwarzschild spacetime, which takes
0<\gamma\leq 1
.
As we give the definition in Sec.
II
,

\gamma
is related to the ADM mass and the scalar charge,
and the case of
\gamma=1
corresponds to the Schwarzschild spacetime.

We also discuss analytical solutions to the geodesic equation for the FJNWW spacetime with some special values of the deviation parameter
\gamma
.
For the orbits without any assumptions, numerical calculations are often used when ones examine whether the negative periapsis precession occurs or not (for example, Refs.
Solanki et al. 2021
;
Bambhaniya et al. 2021
).
Although the numerical analysis is a powerful tool, we can more directly determine the parameter region in which the negative periapsis precession occurs if we can obtain analytical solutions.
In particular, we obtain the analytical solution of the geodesic equation for the case of
\gamma=1/2
and show that the negative precession never occurs.
The value
\gamma=1/2
is the critical value of disappearing the negative periapsis precession for the small eccentricity orbits.
This result supports our results that for the orbits with small eccentricity, the negative periapsis precession does not happen for
\gamma\geq 1/2
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
,
we give a short review of the FJNWW spacetime
and the behavior of geodesics there.
In Sec.
III
,
we revisit the analysis performed in
Joshi et al. 2019
and investigate
the conditions in which the periapsis precession becomes negative
with considering the validity of the approximation in detail.
In Sec.
IV
, to investigate the orbits close to the singularity,
we solve the geodesic equation approximately for either small eccentricity or large semilatus.
In Sec.
V
, we derive the exact periapsis precession for the FJNWW spacetime for
\gamma=1/2
and compare it to each approximate result.
The last section is devoted to the conclusion and the discussion.
In this paper, we work in unit where
c=G=1
.
In the Appendix, we give analytic solutions to the geodesic equations for some particular values of
\gamma
.

II
A short review of the FJNWW spacetime and timelike geodesics

In this section, we give a short review of the FJNWW spacetime.
We consider the following action,

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(R-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\mu}\varphi\right).

(1)

The field equations are written as

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\varphi\partial^{\alpha}\varphi,

(2)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi=0.

(3)

Assuming a static and
spherically symmetric spacetime, we obtain the following metric
of the FJNWW spacetime

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-f^{\gamma}dt^{2}+f^{-\gamma}dr^{2}+f^{1-\gamma}r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

(4)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{r_{g}}{r},

(5)

where
r_{g}=2\sqrt{M^{2}+q^{2}}
,
\gamma=2M/r_{g}\in[0,1]
.

M
is the ADM mass
and
q
is a parameter that relates to the scalar field
\varphi
as

\displaystyle\varphi=\frac{2q}{r_{g}}\log f=\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}\log f.

(6)

The FJNWW spacetime has a naked singularity at
r=r_{g}
.
Indeed, the Kretschmann invariant and the scalar field diverges at

r=r_{g}
as

\displaystyle R^{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}=\frac{r_{g}^{2}}{4r^{8}}f^{2\gamma-4}\bigl[48r^{2}\gamma^{2}-16rr_{g}\gamma(1+\gamma)(1+2\gamma)+r_{g}^{2}(1+\gamma)^{2}(3+2\gamma+7\gamma^{2})\bigr],

(7)

and it is known that this is a strong, globally naked singularity
Virbhadra et al. 1997
.

Now we review the timelike geodesics in the FJNWW spacetime.
Since the FJNWW spacetime is static and spherically symmetric,
it has the following conserved quantities

\displaystyle E=f^{\gamma}\dot{t},\quad L=r^{2}f^{1-\gamma}\sin^{2}\theta\dot{\phi},

(8)

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to the affine parameter.
We normalize the four-velocity

v^{\mu}v_{\mu}=-\epsilon
, where
\epsilon=1
for timelike geodesics and
\epsilon=0
for null geodesics, respectively.
The conserved quantities

and

can be interpreted as the energy and the angular momentum per unit rest mass of a test particle, respectively.
Due to the spherical symmetry, we can restrict the particle motions on the equatorial plane (
\theta=\pi/2

From the normalization condition

8

\displaystyle\dot{r}^{2}=E^{2}-V_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2},

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}=f^{\gamma}\left(\epsilon+f^{\gamma-1}\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right).

(10)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{dr}{d\phi}\Bigr)^{2}=\frac{r^{4}}{L^{2}}f^{2(1-\gamma)}\Bigl(E^{2}-V_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}\Bigr).

III
Periapsis precession of the orbit with small eccentricity in the weak field approximation

III.1
Revisiting the previous studies so far

The definition of the periapsis precession is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{per}}=2\int_{r_{\text{per}}}^{r_{\text{ap}}}\frac{d\phi}{dr}dr-2\pi,

(17)

where
r_{\text{per}}
and
r_{\text{ap}}

The negative periapsis precession (

Using the geodesic equation (
11

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{per}}=2\int_{r_{\text{per}}}^{r_{\text{ap}}}\frac{Ldr}{r^{2}f^{1-\gamma}\sqrt{E^{2}-f^{\gamma}-\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}f^{2\gamma-1}}}-2\pi.

(18)

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=-\mu\lambda(1-\gamma)+\frac{\lambda}{2}(2-\gamma),

(21)

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mu\lambda(1-\gamma)(1-2\gamma)+\frac{\lambda}{2}(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)+1,

\displaystyle=\mu\lambda\gamma(1-\gamma)(1-2\gamma)-\frac{\lambda}{4}\gamma(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)+\frac{3}{2}.

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{per}}=2\pi(m^{-1}-1),

\displaystyle m=(f_{1}^{2}-4f_{0}f_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(27)

IV.2
The periapsis precession of orbits slightly deviated from

Next, we consider bound orbits slightly deviated from a circular orbit

The geodesic deviation describes the deviation of two adjacent particle orbits.
We represent geodesics with

The four-velocity and the deviation vector are defined as

\displaystyle v^{\mu}=\frac{\partial x^{\mu}}{\partial s},\quad n^{\mu}=\frac{\partial x^{\mu}}{\partial p},

V
Exact expression of the periapsis precession for

\gamma=1/2

In this section, we focus on the
\gamma=1/2
case,
where we can solve the geodesic equation analytically
in terms of the elliptic function.
There are two advantages to investigate the analytic solution
for the geodesics equation in the FJNWW spacetime with
\gamma=1/2
.
First, in the previous section, we found that the negative precession does not occur in the
FJNWW spacetime with
\gamma\geq 1/2
using the small eccentricity approximation.
However, it is not obvious if the negative periapsis precession does not occur in the range where the approximation is not valid.
In order to reveal that, we need to solve the geodesic equation analytically.
If we prove analytically that the negative precession does not occur in the
FJNWW spacetime with
\gamma=1/2
, which is the critical value of occurring the negative precession, for an arbitrary eccentricity, then it would be circumstantial evidence that the negative precession does not occur in the FJNWW spacetime with
\gamma\geq 1/2
.
In fact, in this section, we show that the negative periapsis precession does not occur in the FJNWW spacetime with
\gamma=1/2
by obtaining the analytic solution to the geodesic equation.
Second, by taking appropriate approximations for the orbits
in the FJNWW spacetime with
\gamma=1/2
,
we can show the validity of the results we have obtained so far
using some approximation methods.

As summarized in the Appendix,
the periapsis precession for
\gamma=1/2
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{per}}=4\int_{y_{\text{per}}}^{y_{\text{ap}}}\frac{dy}{\sqrt{P(y)}}-2\pi,

(94)

where

\displaystyle P(y)\equiv-y^{4}+2y^{2}-\lambda y+(\lambda\mu-1),

(95)

and we introduced a new variable

.
Here,
y_{\text{per}}
and
y_{\text{ap}}
correspond to the periapsis and the apoapsis of a bound orbit defined as

\displaystyle y_{\text{per}}\equiv\sqrt{1-u_{0}(1+e)},\ y_{\text{ap}}\equiv\sqrt{1-u_{0}(1-e)}.

(96)

The polynomial
P(y)
can be factorized to

\displaystyle P(y)=-(y-y_{\text{ap}})(y-y_{\text{per}})(y-y_{1})(y-y_{2}).

(97)

The parameters
\mu
and
\lambda
and the values

\displaystyle=-\frac{y_{\text{ap}}+y_{\text{per}}}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{6u_{0}-2y_{\text{ap}}y_{\text{per}}+2},

(98)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=2u_{0}(y_{\text{ap}}+y_{\text{per}}),

(99)

\displaystyle\mu\lambda

\displaystyle=2u_{0}-u_{0}^{2}(1-e^{2})+2u_{0}y_{\text{ap}}y_{\text{per}}.

(100)

VI
Summary and Discussion

In this paper, we studied the timelike geodesics and evaluated the periapsis precession in the FJNWW spacetime under the several approximations.
We complemented the result of
Joshi et al. 2019
for the FJNWW spacetime under the weak field and the small eccentricity approximation.
We showed that the negative periapsis precessions occur when the spacetime sufficiently deviates from the Schwarzschild spacetime: the parameter
\gamma
is small enough.
Next we suggested two approximation methods to relax the approximation
conditions used in
Joshi et al. 2019
.
First, employing only the weak field approximation
(u_{0}\ll 1)
, we obtained the analytical solution of the approximate geodesic equation (
20
).
This allowed us to find the periapsis precession for orbits
with arbitrary eccentricity.
Second, we assumed only small eccentricity
(e\ll 1)

and solved the geodesic deviation equation around the circular orbit.
In this case, we showed that the negative periapsis precession can occur
when
\gamma
is smaller than
1/2
, where particles can move around the vicinity of the singularity.
This implies that it is important that a particle moves around close enough to the singularity for the periapsis precession to be negative.
Our results indicate that a naked singularity spacetime may exhibit the characteristic behaviors in the periapsis precession.
Although with the observations of the periapsis precession, it is difficult to distinguish the FJNWW spacetime from other naked singularity spacetimes, it is possible to test the models of naked singularity spacetimes as have been done in
Dey et al. 2019
.

Furthermore, we found that the analytical expression of the periapsis precession in terms of the elliptic integral for
\gamma=1/2
.
We saw that for
\gamma=1/2
, the negative periapsis precession never occurs:
\Delta_{\mathrm{per}}
is always positive.
We confirmed that the approximate solutions are consistent with the analytical solution for at least
\gamma=1/2
.
As shown in the Appendix, for some particular values of
\gamma
,
the analytical solution can be obtained by the elliptic function,
which is expected to be analyzed for the more general case of
\gamma
by using hyperelliptic functions
Hackmann and Lammerzahl 2008
;
Enolski et al. 2011
.
The investigation and utilization of such analytic solutions
are left for a future work.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2}=P_{3,4}(y),

(110)

where
P_{3,4}(y)
is a third or fourth order polynomial.
This is realized for
(\alpha,\gamma)=(\forall\alpha,0),(2,1/2),(2,1/4)

A.1
Timelike and null geodesics for
\gamma=0

When
\gamma=0
, we can obtain an analytic solution of
the geodesic equation for arbitrary
\alpha
, so we set here
\alpha=1
.
Then Eq. (
11
) reduces to the following differential equation:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{dy}{d\phi}\right)^{2}=-y^{3}+Ay^{2}-y,

(111)

where
A\equiv\lambda(\mu-\epsilon)+2
.
We note that for
\gamma=0
, the difference between the timelike geodesics (
\epsilon=1
) and the null geodesics (
\epsilon=0
) appears as the constant shift of
A
.
That is, both the timelike geodesics and the null geodesics are represented by the same equation (
111
).
With a substitution
y=-4z+A/3
, Eq. (
111
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{dz}{d\phi}\right)^{2}=4z^{3}-g_{2}z-g_{3},

(112)

where

\displaystyle g_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{3}A^{2}-1\right),

(113)

\displaystyle g_{3}=\frac{1}{48}A\left(1-\frac{2}{9}A^{2}\right).

(114)

This equation has a solution represented by the Weierstrass function as

\displaystyle z(\phi)=\wp(\phi-\phi_{\mathrm{in}};g_{2},g_{3}),

(115)

where

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathrm{in}}=\phi_{0}+\int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{4z^{3}-g_{2}z-g_{3}}},\quad z_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{r_{g}}{r_{0}}-1+\frac{1}{3}A\right).

(116)

Finally we find the analytical solution of the geodesic equation in the FJNWW spacetime with
\gamma=0
as

\displaystyle r(\phi)=r_{g}\left[4\wp(\phi-\phi_{\mathrm{in}})+1-\frac{1}{3}A\right]^{-1}.

(117)

(118)

Substituting
y=\xi^{-1}+y_{\mathrm{zero}}
,
where
y_{\mathrm{zero}}
is the algebraic roots of
Y_{1/2}(y)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d\xi}{d\phi}\right)^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{3}a_{j}\xi^{j},\ \ a_{j}=\frac{1}{(4-j)!}\left.\frac{d^{4-j}Y_{1/2}(y)}{dy^{4-j}}\right|_{y=y_{\mathrm{zero}}}.

Applying a transformation as

\xi=a_{3}^{-1}(4z-a_{2}/3)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{dz}{d\phi}\right)^{2}=4z^{3}-g_{2}z-g_{3},

(120)

where

\displaystyle g_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{3}a_{2}^{2}-a_{1}a_{3}\right),

(121)

(122)

\gamma=1/2

(123)

where

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathrm{in}}=\phi_{0}+\int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{4z^{3}-g_{2}z-g_{3}}},\quad z_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\left[\frac{a_{3}}{(1-\frac{r_{g}}{r_{0}})^{\frac{1}{2}}-y_{\mathrm{zero}}}+\frac{a_{2}}{3}\right].

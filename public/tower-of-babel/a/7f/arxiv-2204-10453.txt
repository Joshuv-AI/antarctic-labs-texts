Fixed-Time Parameter Adaptation for Safe Control Synthesis footnoteinfo
Mitchell Black, Ehsan Arabi, Dimitra Panagou

Abstract
This paper introduces a parameter adaptation-based control law for a class of nonlinear, control-affine, safety-critical systems subject to additive, parameter-affine model uncertainty. It is shown that the uncertainty is learned in fixed-time, i.e., within a finite time independent of the initial parameter estimates, without requiring persistence of excitation or rank conditions on the associated regressor matrix. A monotonically decreasing bound on the error of the estimated uncertainty is derived and used in the proposed control barrier function-based control law for guaranteed safety of the system trajectories. It is then proven that the parameter adaptation law is robust to bounded measurement noise and non-parametric perturbations to the system in that the estimated uncertainty still converges to a known neighborhood of the true uncertainty in fixed-time. The advantages of the approach are highlighted in a comparative numerical study, and the method is validated on a quadrotor trajectory-tracking problem subject to safety requirements and an unknown wind field.

control of constrained systems; robust control of nonlinear systems; safety-critical systems; fixed-time stability.

1
Introduction

Synthesizing safe controllers for dynamical systems under uncertainty is challenging. The rise of control barrier functions (CBFs)
[
2
]
,
a model-based tool for synthesizing safety with stabilization objectives online via quadratic programs (QPs), has advanced the state-of-the art in the field. However, inaccurate system models may cause safety violations in CBF-based control. Typically, model uncertainty is compensated for either by 1) taking a robust approach
[
34
,
14
]
, assuming a worst-case bound on system perturbations; by 2) using adaptive techniques
[
31
,
17
,
35
]
, updating estimates of unknown system parameters online; or by 3) employing learning algorithms
[
32
,
13
]
, using data to learn a representation of system behavior. Categorically, these approaches possess both strengths and weaknesses: robust controllers protect against bounded perturbations in exchange for conservatism; adaptation-based parameter estimators adjust to changes in the system dynamics online, but often require persistently excited (PE) signals; and while learning algorithms have found empirical success, they often lack guarantees of safety or stabilization. This paper proposes a control framework that leverages the reactivity of adaptation and the protection of robust control to guarantee that 1) a class of additive, parameter-affine perturbations to the system dynamics is learned within a finite time, and 2) the system trajectories remain safe at all times despite model uncertainty. Integral to the proposed framework is the notion of fixed-time stability.

While asymptotic (AS) and exponential (ES) stability imply convergence of the trajectories of a dynamical system to an equilibrium as time tends to infinity, fixed-time stability (FxTS)
[
21
]
guarantees convergence to the equilibrium
within a finite time, independent of the initial conditions
.
Much recent work has been dedicated to FxTS for control design
[
20
,
22
]
, estimation
[
25
,
19
,
30
]
, and optimization
[
23
,
11
]
, though little that utilizes adaptation for control of safety-critical systems. For example,

[
29
]
and
[
25
]
design FxTS parameter estimators for
neural networks and perturbed linear regression systems respectively, but control design is not addressed.
Recently, the dynamic regressor extension and mixing algorithm has received significant attention in the field of parameter estimation for its promise of monotonicity of each element of the parameter error vector without the often restrictive persistence of excitation condition
[
4
,
3
]
. As shown in
[
3
]
, however, it still requires the PE condition for global convergence of the parameter error.

Meanwhile, existing work on safe control for systems with additive, parameter-affine uncertainty has focused on safety at the exclusion of stabilization. In
[
31
]
the adaptive CBF is introduced for system-level safety, not for learning model uncertainty.
This open problem was addressed in
[
17
]
by combining adaptive CBFs with a data-driven set membership identification (SMID)-based parameter update law, which, though leading to improved empirical results, lacks guarantees of convergence of the parameter estimates. To the best of our knowledge, the first design of a safety-critical fixed-time adaptive control law was proposed in our prior work
[
6
]
, though the robustness of the approach to measurement error and unmodelled disturbances was not investigated.

In this paper, a fixed-time adaptive control law is introduced to reduce conservatism in control of nonlinear, control- and parameter-affine safety-critical systems. This is achieved through a novel parameter adaptation law derived to identify the parameter-affine perturbation to the system in fixed-time, notably without requiring the PE condition. The robustness of the proposed adaptation law with respect to bounded measurement error or exogenous perturbations is analyzed, and a fixed-time domain of attraction is derived. Then, the monotonically-decreasing bound on the error of the perturbation estimation is used to synthesize a CBF condition that is both robust to present levels of uncertainty and adaptive to the changing parameter estimates. It is shown how the satisfaction of this condition via control design guarantees safety at all times. The advantages of the proposed method are highlighted in comparison with related works from the literature on a single-integrator example, and are further demonstrated on a simulated quadrotor system in unknown wind fields.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
reviews preliminaries and introduces the problem. Section
3
contains the main results on FxTS parameter adaptation, while Section
4
analyzes their robustness against a class of bounded perturbations and proposes the new CBF condition. Section
5
contains the results of the case studies, and Section
6
concludes with summaries and directions for future work.

The following notation is used.
\mathbb{R}
denotes the set of real numbers. The ones matrix of size

\bm{1}_{n\times m}
.

singular values of a matrix

are denoted
\sigma_{min}(\bm{M})
,
\sigma_{max}(\bm{M})
, and
\sigma_{r}(\bm{M})
. Further,
\underaccent{\bar}{\sigma}(\bm{M},T)\triangleq\inf_{t\leq T}\sigma_{r}(\bm{M}(t))
. The rowspace and nullspace are
\mathcal{R}(\bm{M})
and
\mathcal{N}(\bm{M})
respectively. When
n=m
the minimum (resp. maximum) eigenvalue is
\lambda_{min}(\bm{M})
(resp.
\lambda_{max}(\bm{M})
).
The boundary of a closed set
S
is denoted
\partial S
, and
\textrm{int}(S)
is the interior. The Lie derivative of a continuously differentiable function
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
along a vector field

at a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

.

Consider the following class of nonlinear, control- and parameter-affine systems:

\dot{\bm{x}}=f(\bm{x})+g(\bm{x})\bm{u}+\Delta(\bm{x})\bm{\theta}^{*},\;\bm{x}(0)=\bm{x}_{0},

(1)

where
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the state,
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
the control input, and
\bm{\theta}^{*}\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
a vector of unknown, static parameters. The sets
\mathcal{U}
and
\Theta
are the input constraint and admissible parameter sets respectively, where both are known and
\Theta
is a polytope. The fields
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, and regressor
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
are known and continuous such that for a continuous control input (

d(\bm{x})=\Delta(\bm{x})\bm{\theta}^{*}
may describe, e.g., modelling errors or disturbances in the system dynamics. Note that nothing is assumed about the rank of
\Delta(\bm{x})
, and thus there may be other vectors
\bm{\theta}
that satisfy
\Delta(\bm{x})\bm{\theta}=d(\bm{x})
. As such, define the following state- and parameter-dependent set,
\Omega(\bm{x},\bm{\theta}^{*})
, containing such vectors
\bm{\theta}
:

\Omega(\bm{x},\bm{\theta}^{*})=\{\bm{\theta}\in\Theta:\Delta(\bm{x})\bm{\theta}=\Delta(\bm{x})\bm{\theta}^{*}\}.

(2)

In the remainder,
\Omega
is used for conciseness. The estimated parameter vector is
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
so that the parameter estimation error vector is
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}=\bm{\theta}^{*}-\hat{\bm{\theta}}
. Note that
\hat{\bm{\theta}}\in\Omega
whenever
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathcal{N}(\Delta(\bm{x}))
.
The parameter estimation error dynamics are

\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}=-\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}},\;\;\tilde{\bm{\theta}}(0)=\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{0}.

(3)

With
\Theta
a polytope, for any
\hat{\bm{\theta}}\in\Theta
it follows that
\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}\leq\vartheta(t)
, where

\vartheta(t)\triangleq\sup_{\bm{\theta}_{1},\bm{\theta}_{2}\in\Lambda(t)}(\|\bm{\theta}_{1}-\bm{\theta}_{2}\|_{\infty})
and
\Lambda(t)\subseteq\Theta
denotes the set of admissible parameters at time
t
(which may shrink as measurements are gathered).

Let
\bm{\vartheta}=\vartheta\cdot\bm{1}_{p\times 1}
,
and consider a set of states defined with respect to a constraint function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
and positive-definite robustness margin
m:\mathbb{R}^{p}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
, both continuously differentiable:

S=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(\bm{x})\geq m(\bm{\vartheta})\right\}.

(4)

Let
m(\bm{\vartheta})\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\bm{\vartheta}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}^{-1}\bm{\vartheta}
for a constant, positive-definite matrix
\bm{\Gamma}
such that
h(\bm{x}_{0})\geq m(\bm{\vartheta})
, and assume that
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\bm{x}}\neq 0
,
\forall\bm{x}\in\partial S
. The following, known as Nagumo’s Theorem
[
8
]
, provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the forward invariance of the set (
4
) under the dynamics (
1
).

Lemma 1
.

The set
S
is forward-invariant for (
1
) if and only if
L_{f}h(\bm{x})+L_{g}h(\bm{x})\bm{u}+L_{\Delta}h(\bm{x})\bm{\theta}^{*}-\dot{m}(\bm{\vartheta})\geq 0
for all
\bm{x}\in\partial S
.

Let
F(\bm{x})=f(\bm{x})+\Delta(\bm{x})\bm{\theta}^{*}
.
One way to render
S
forward-invariant is to use CBFs for control design
[
2
]
.

Definition 1
.

Given a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by (
4
) for a continuously differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, the function
h
is a
control barrier function
(CBF) defined on a set
D
, where
S\subseteq D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if there exists an extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
such that,
\forall\bm{x}\in D
,

\sup_{\bm{u}\in\mathcal{U}}\big[L_{F}h(\bm{x})+L_{g}h(\bm{x})\bm{u}\big]\geq-\alpha\big(h(\bm{x})-m(\bm{\vartheta})\big)+\dot{m}(\bm{\vartheta}).

(5)

Note that if
\dot{m}({\bm{\vartheta}})=0
and
\bm{\vartheta}\neq 0
, then (
5
) becomes
\dot{h}\geq-\alpha(h)+\gamma
for
\gamma>0
, a robust CBF condition
[
14
]
.

Consider now the autonomous, nonlinear system

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=f(\bm{x}(t)),\quad\bm{x}(0)=\bm{x}_{0},

(6)

where
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuous such that (
6
) admits a unique solution, and it is assumed without loss of generality that the origin is an equilibrium, i.e.

.

Definition 2
.

[

The origin of (

) is

stable in the sense of Lyapunov and any solution

reaches the origin with uniformly bounded settling-time
T(\bm{x}_{0})\leq T_{b}<\infty
,
\forall\bm{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1
.

[
21
, Lemma 1]
Suppose that there exists a continuous, positive definite, radially unbounded function
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
for the system (
6
) such that

\displaystyle\dot{V}(\bm{x})\leq-c_{1}V^{\gamma_{1}}(\bm{x})-c_{2}V^{\gamma_{2}}(\bm{x}),

(7)

for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, where

,

, and

. Then, the origin of (

) is fixed-time stable with settling time,

, bounded for all

by

\displaystyle T(\bm{x}_{0})\leq T_{b}=\frac{1}{c_{1}(\gamma_{1}-1)}+\frac{1}{c_{2}(1-\gamma_{2})}.

(8)

It has been shown that learning
\bm{\theta}^{*}
in fixed-time leads to favorable disturbance rejection properties

]
.
For control design, however, identifying

). Design adaptation and control laws,

3
Parameter Adaptation

The following is a common, albeit restrictive, assumption in the parameter identification literature (e.g.,
[
18
,
25
]
).

Assumption 1
.

For all time
t\geq 0
there is a known vector,
\bm{v}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, and a known, full column rank matrix,
\bm{M}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times p}
,
such that
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}(t)
is a solution to

\bm{M}(t)\tilde{\bm{\theta}}(t)=\bm{v}(t).

(9)

The above more commonly appears in the form
\sigma_{min}(\bm{M}(t))>s>0
,
\forall t\geq 0
, and is equivalent to system identifiability
[
16
, Def. 4.6]
. Its utility is in the design of adaptation laws that provide stability guarantees (e.g., ES
[
18
]
or FTS/FxTS
[
25
]
) when (
9
) is perturbed, i.e., when

\bm{M}(t)\tilde{\bm{\theta}}(t)=\bm{v}(t)-\bm{\delta}(t),

(10)

for some unknown
\bm{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
.
The authors of
[
18
]
further note that Assumption
1
can be satisfied when the regressor,
\Delta
, of system (
1
) satisfies the PE condition. The following represents a relaxation of Assumption
1
.

Assumption 2
.

For all
t\geq 0
there is a known vector,
\bm{v}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and a known matrix,
\bm{M}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
, that jointly satisfy

1.

\tilde{\bm{\theta}}(t)
is one (not necessarily unique) solution to (
9
)

2.

\bm{v}(t)
and
\bm{M}(t)
are bounded for bounded
\bm{x}(t)

3.

\mathcal{N}(\bm{M}(t))=\mathcal{N}(\Delta(\bm{x}(t)))
.

Remark 1
.

In contrast to Assumption
1
, Assumption
2
does not require that
\bm{M}
be full column-rank. Instead, it requires that
\mathcal{N}(\bm{M})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{\Delta})
, which relaxes restrictions imposed by previous works
[
18
,
25
]
.

Remark 2
.

Note that Assumption
2
is satisfied with
\bm{M}
and
\bm{v}
obtained by rewriting the system dynamics (
1
) as

\displaystyle\underbrace{\Delta(\bm{x})}_{\text{$\bm{M}$}}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}=\underbrace{\dot{\bm{x}}-f(\bm{x})-g(\bm{x})\bm{u}-\Delta(\bm{x})\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{\text{$\bm{v}$}}.

(11)

Clearly, (
11
) requires that the state and its time-derivative are available. When measurements of
\dot{\bm{x}}
are not available, they can be approximated using the state measurements and numerical differentiation. Such schemes, however, can introduce approximation error into (
11
) even under perfect state measurements, so that it takes the form (
10
). Such errors along with the consideration that the state measurements are in general imperfect are addressed in Section
4
. While there are other viable approaches, including the observer introduced by
[
1
]
, the details are beyond the scope of this paper.

The following is one of the main results of the paper, an adaptation law that renders
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
FxTS to the set (
2
).

Theorem 2
.

Consider a dynamical system of the form (
1
) and suppose that Assumption
2
holds. If
\mathcal{N}(\Delta(\bm{x}(t)))=\mathcal{N}(\Delta(\bm{x}(0)))
,
\forall t\in[0,T]
, where

T=\frac{\mu\pi}{2k_{V}^{2}\sqrt{ab}},

(12)

with
a,b>0
,
\mu>2
, and

k_{V}=\underaccent{\bar}{\sigma}(\bm{M},T)\sqrt{2\lambda_{max}(\bm{\Gamma})},

(13)

where
\bm{\Gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is a constant, positive-definite, gain matrix,
then under the ensuing adaptation law,

\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}=\bm{\Gamma}\bm{M}^{T}\bm{v}\left(a\|\bm{v}\|^{\frac{2}{\mu}}+b\|\bm{v}\|^{-\frac{2}{\mu}}\right),

(14)

the estimated parameter vector,
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
, converges to
\Omega
defined by (
2
) within a fixed time
T
, i.e.
\hat{\bm{\theta}}(t)\rightarrow\Omega
and
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}(t)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(\Delta(\bm{x}(t))
as
t\rightarrow T_{c}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}(0))\leq T
,
\forall\hat{\bm{\theta}}(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

Proof.

It will first be shown that (
14
) is well-defined, and then proven that
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
converges to
\Omega
within fixed-time
T
.

It is obvious by boundedness of
\bm{v}
and
\bm{M}
that (
14
) is bounded whenever
0<\|\bm{v}\|<\infty
, so consider when
\|\bm{v}\|\rightarrow 0
. Note that (
14
) can be rewritten as
\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}=G_{1}(\bm{M},\bm{v})+G_{2}(\bm{M},\bm{v})
, where
G_{1}(\bm{M},\bm{v})=a\bm{\Gamma}\bm{M}^{T}\bm{v}\|\bm{v}\|^{\frac{2}{\mu}}
and
G_{2}(\bm{M},\bm{v})=b\bm{\Gamma}\bm{M}^{T}\bm{v}\|\bm{v}\|^{-\frac{2}{\mu}}
. It is evident that
G_{1}(\bm{M},\bm{v})\rightarrow 0
as
\|\bm{v}\|\rightarrow 0
, however, for

it follows that
\|G_{2}(\bm{M},\bm{v})\|^{2}=b^{2}\|\bm{v}\|^{-4/\mu}\bm{v}^{T}\left(\bm{M}\bm{\Gamma}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}\bm{M}^{T}\right)\bm{v}
.
Then, defining
\bm{N}=\bm{M}\bm{\Gamma}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}\bm{M}^{T}
, it holds that,
\forall t\leq T
,

\displaystyle b^{2}\sigma_{min}(\bm{N})\|\bm{v}\|^{\phi}\leq\|G_{2}(\bm{M},\bm{v})\|^{2}\leq b^{2}\sigma_{max}(\bm{N})\|\bm{v}\|^{\phi},

with
\phi=2-4/\mu
.
Since
0\leq\sigma_{min}(\bm{N}),\sigma_{max}(\bm{N})<\infty
, it follows that if
\mu>2
, then
\lim_{\|\bm{v}\|\rightarrow 0}\|G_{2}(\bm{M},\bm{v})\|=0
.
Thus, with
\mu>2
,
(
14
) is well-defined.

For convergence of
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
to
\Omega
, observe that
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}
,
\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}
, and
\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}
may be expressed as the following linear combinations:
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}=\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}+\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{N}
,
\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}=\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}_{R}+\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}_{N}
, and
\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}=\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{R}+\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{N}
,
where
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R},\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}_{R},\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{R}\in\mathcal{R}(\bm{M})

\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{N},\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}_{N},\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{N}\in\mathcal{N}(\bm{M})

as orthogonal complements, such a decomposition always exists

27

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Gamma}\bm{M}^{T}\bm{v}\left(a\|\bm{v}\|^{\frac{2}{\mu}}+b\|\bm{v}\|^{-\frac{2}{\mu}}\right)

Since
\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}=\bm{M}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}+\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{N})=\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}

\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}=\bm{\Gamma}\bm{M}^{T}\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\left(a\|\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\|^{\frac{2}{\mu}}+b\|\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\|^{\frac{-2}{\mu}}\right)=\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{R}

\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{N}=\bm{0}_{p\times 1}
and thus by (

\dot{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}_{R}=-\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{R},\;\;\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}(0)=\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R,0}.

(15)

By Assumption

\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{N}(0)\in\mathcal{N}(\Delta(\bm{x}(0)))

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}^{-1}\dot{\hat{\bm{\theta}}}_{R}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}^{T}\bm{M}^{T}\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\left(a\|\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\|^{\frac{2}{\mu}}+b\|\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\|^{-\frac{2}{\mu}}\right)

\displaystyle=-a\|\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\|^{2+\frac{2}{\mu}}-b\|\bm{M}\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{R}\|^{2-\frac{2}{\mu}}.

When

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\leq-a\Big(\underaccent{\bar}{\sigma}(\bm{M},T)L(V)\Big)^{2+\frac{2}{\mu}}-b\Big(\underaccent{\bar}{\sigma}(\bm{M},T)L(V)\Big)^{2-\frac{2}{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq-c_{1}V^{1+\frac{1}{\mu}}-c_{2}V^{1-\frac{1}{\mu}},

(16)

which takes the form (

6
Conclusion

In this study on the merits of fixed-time adaptation for safe control under parametric model uncertainty, an adaptation law was introduced to learn additive, parametric uncertainty associated with a class of nonlinear, control-affine systems within a fixed time without requiring rank or persistence of excitation conditions on the regressor. The robustness of the proposed law to a class of bounded disturbances was analyzed, and a robust, adaptive CBF-based control law was synthesized after deriving a time-varying bound on the disturbance estimation error. A comparative study on a 2D single integrator system was conducted, and it was shown that the proposed approach succeeds where others from the literature fail. The value of the approach was further demonstrated on a safe trajectory-tracking problem using a quadrotor dynamical model in wind fields both known and unknown.

In the future, fixed-time adaptation for safe control of systems with nonlinearly parameterized and non-parametric additive disturbances to the system dynamics will be studied.

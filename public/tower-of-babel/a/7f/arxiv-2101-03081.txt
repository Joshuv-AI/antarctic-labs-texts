Products of polymatroids with the strong exchange property
Lisa Nicklasson

Abstract
. It was conjectured by White in 1980 that the toric ring associated to a matroid is defined by symmetric exchange relations. This conjecture was extended to discrete polymatroids by Herzog and Hibi, and they prove that the conjecture holds for polymatroids with the so called strong exchange property . In this paper we generalize their result to polymatroids that are products of polymatroids with the strong exchange property. This also extends a result by Conca on transversal polymatroids.



1.
Polymatroidal bases

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field, and let
\mathbb{K}[X]
denote the polynomial ring over
\mathbb{K}
on the set of variables
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. We equip
\mathbb{K}[X]
with the standard grading, meaning that
\deg(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}})=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}
. We will also use the notation
\deg_{i}(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}})=\alpha_{i}
.

Definition 1.1
.

We call a set
B
of monomials in
\mathbb{K}[X]
a
polymatroidal basis
if all the monomials in
B
are of the same degree and the following property holds. If
f
and
g
are monomials in
B
with
\deg_{i}f>\deg_{i}g
for some
i
, then there is a
j
such that
\deg_{j}f<\deg_{j}g
and
\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}f\in B
.

The name
polymatroidal
comes from the fact that a polymatroidal basis is the set of base elements of a discrete polymatroid, phrased in the language of monomials. An ideal generated by a polymatroidal basis is called a
polymatroidal ideal.
The structure of a polymatroidal basis is actually more symmetric than the definition reveals.

Theorem 1.2

(Symmetric exchange property)
.

Let
B
be a polymatroidal base, and suppose
f,g\in B
with
\deg_{i}f>\deg_{i}g
. Then there is a
j
with
\deg_{j}f<\deg_{j}g
such that
\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}f,\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}g\in B
.

For a proof of this theorem, as well as a more extensive background on discrete polymatroids, see
[
5
]
.

To a polymatroidal basis
B
we can associate the toric ring
\mathbb{K}[B]
. This is the subring of
\mathbb{K}[X]
generated by the monomials in
B
.
In the language of discrete polymatroids
\mathbb{K}[B]
is also known as the base ring of
B
, or in the language of monomial ideals as the fiber ring of the ideal generated by
B
. If
B=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}
, let
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
and define a homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{K}[Y]\to\mathbb{K}[X]
by
\varphi(y_{i})=f_{i}
. Then
\mathcal{J}_{B}:=\ker\varphi
is called the
toric ideal of
B
, and
\mathbb{K}[B]\cong\mathbb{K}[Y]/\mathcal{J}_{B}
. It is a well known fact that such an ideal is generated by binomials, more precisely by differences of monomials. If we take two variables
y_{r},y_{s}\in Y
, Theorem
1.2
induces binomials of degree two in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
in the following way. Say
\varphi(y_{r})=f
and
\varphi(y_{s})=g
. Assuming
f\neq g
Theorem
1.2
tells us that there are
i
and
j
such that
\deg_{i}f>\deg_{i}g
,
\deg_{j}f<\deg_{j}g
, and
\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}f,\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}g\in B
. Then there are some
y_{t},y_{u}\in Y
so that
\varphi(y_{t})=\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}f
and
\varphi(y_{u})=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}g
and
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{t}y_{u}\in\mathcal{J}_{B}
. The elements in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
of this type are called
symmetric exchange relations
. To be precise,
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{t}y_{u}
is a symmetric exchange relation if there are
i
and
j
such that

\displaystyle\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{r})>\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{s}),\ \ \deg_{j}\varphi(y_{r})<\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{s}),

\displaystyle\varphi(y_{t})=\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\varphi(y_{r}),\ \mbox{ and }\varphi(y_{u})=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\varphi(y_{s}).

Conjecture 1.3

(White 1980, Herzog-Hibi 2002)
.

The toric ideal
\mathcal{J}_{B}
of a polymatroidal basis
B
is generated by the symmetric exchange relations.

This conjecture was originally posed in
[
9
]
for matroids, which correspond to squarefree polymatroidal bases, and for discrete polymatroids in
[
5
]
. Since the symmetric exchange relations are quadratic it is natural to ask whether the algebra
\mathbb{K}[B]
is Koszul, and if the toric ideal has a quadratic Gröbner basis. Recall that a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
R
is Koszul if
\mathbb{K}
has a linear free resolution over
R
. Every Koszul algebra is defined by an ideal generated in degree two, and every algebra defined by an ideal with a quadratic Gröbner basis is Koszul.

Example 1.4
.

The set
B=\{{x}_{1}{x}_{2}{x}_{3},{x}_{1}{x}_{3}^{2},{x}_{2}^{2}{x}_{3},{x}_{2}{x}_{3}^{2},{x}_{2}{x}_{3}{x}_{4},{x}_{3}^{2}{x}_{4}\}
is a polymatroidal basis. The toric ideal
\mathcal{J}_{B}
is the kernel of the homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{K}[X]\to\mathbb{K}[Y]
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi(y_{1})={x}_{1}{x}_{2}{x}_{3},\ \ \varphi(y_{2})=x_{1}x_{3}^{2},\ \ \varphi(y_{3})=x_{2}^{2}x_{3},

\displaystyle\varphi(y_{4})=x_{2}x_{3}^{2},\ \ \varphi(y_{5})=x_{2}x_{3}x_{4},\ \ \varphi(y_{6})=x_{3}^{2}x_{4}.

A computation in Macaulay2
[
4
]
gives that

\mathcal{J}_{B}=(y_{1}y_{4}-y_{2}y_{3},\ y_{1}y_{6}-y_{2}y_{5},\ y_{3}y_{6}-y_{4}y_{5}).

This generating set is a Gröbner basis w. r. t. the Lex order. We also check that the generators are symmetric exchange relations. The fact that
\deg_{1}(x_{1}x_{3}^{2})>\deg_{1}(x_{2}^{2}x_{3})
,
\deg_{2}(x_{1}x_{3}^{2})<\deg_{2}(x_{2}^{2}x_{3})
, and

\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}x_{1}x_{3}^{3}=x_{2}x_{3}^{2},\ \ \frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\ \ \mbox{both belongs to }B

gives rise to the symmetric exchange relation
y_{2}y_{3}-y_{1}y_{4}
. Moreover,
\deg_{1}(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})>\deg_{1}(x_{3}^{2}x_{4})
,
\deg_{4}(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})<\deg_{4}(x_{3}^{2}x_{4})
, and

\frac{x_{4}}{x_{1}}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}=x_{2}x_{3}x_{4},\ \ \frac{x_{1}}{x_{4}}x_{3}^{2}x_{4}=x_{1}x_{3}^{2}\ \ \mbox{both belongs to }B

gives the symmetric exchange relation
y_{1}y_{6}-y_{2}y_{5}
. Last,
y_{3}y_{6}-y_{4}y_{5}
is a symmetric exchange relation which comes from the fact that
\deg_{2}(x_{2}^{2}x_{3})>\deg_{2}(x_{3}^{2}x_{4})
,
\deg_{3}(x_{2}^{2}x_{3})<\deg_{3}(x_{3}^{2}x_{4})
, and

\frac{x_{3}}{x_{2}}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}=x_{2}x_{3}^{2},\ \ \frac{x_{2}}{x_{3}}x_{3}^{2}x_{4}=x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}\ \ \mbox{both belongs to }B.\qed

We finish this section with a lemma regarding the symmetric exchange relations, which we will need later.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
B
be a polymatroidal basis and let
\mathcal{E}_{B}
be the ideal generated by the symmetric exchange relations. Suppose we have
y_{r},y_{s},y_{t},y_{u}
such that
\varphi(y_{t})=\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\varphi(y_{r})
and
\varphi(y_{u})=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\varphi(y_{s})
for some
i
and
j
. Then
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{t}y_{u}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
.

Proof.

If
\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{r})>\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{s})
and
\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{r})<\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{s})
then
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{t}y_{u}
is a symmetric exchange relation.

Suppose
\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{r})>\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{s})
and
\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{r})\geq\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{s})
. Since
B
is polymatroidal there is some index
k
such that
\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{r})<\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{s})
and
\frac{x_{k}}{x_{i}}\varphi(y_{r})
,
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{k}}\varphi(y_{s})\in B
. Then there are some
y_{v},y_{w}
such that
\varphi(y_{v})=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{i}}\varphi(y_{r})
and
\varphi(y_{w})=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{k}}\varphi(y_{s})
. It follows that
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{v}y_{w}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
. We also have
y_{t}y_{u}-y_{v}y_{w}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
because

\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{t})=\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{r})+1>\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{s})-1=\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{u}),

\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{t})=\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{r})<\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{s})=\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{u}),

\varphi(y_{v})=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{i}}\varphi(y_{r})=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{i}}\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\varphi(y_{t})=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{j}}\varphi(y_{t})\ \mbox{and similarily}\ \varphi(y_{w})=\frac{x_{j}}{x_{k}}\varphi(y_{u})

Then
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{t}y_{u}=(y_{r}y_{s}-y_{v}y_{w})-(y_{t}y_{u}-y_{v}y_{w})\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
.

Suppose instead
\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{r})\leq\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{s})
and
\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{r})<\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{s})
. Since
B
is polymatroidal,
\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{s})>\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{r})
implies that there is some index
k
such that
\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{s})<\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{r})
and
\frac{x_{k}}{x_{j}}\varphi(y_{s}),\frac{x_{j}}{x_{k}}\varphi(y_{s})\in B
. Then there are
y_{v}
and
y_{w}
such that
\varphi(y_{v})=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{j}}\varphi(y_{s})
and
\varphi(y_{w})=\frac{x_{j}}{x_{k}}\varphi(y_{r})
, and we have
y_{s}y_{r}-y_{v}y_{w}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
. In a similar way as in the previous case one can verify that
\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{u})>\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{t})
,
\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{u})<\deg_{k}\varphi(y_{t})
,
\varphi(y_{v})=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{i}}\varphi(y_{u})
and
\varphi(y_{w})=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{k}}\varphi(y_{t})
, so that
y_{u}y_{t}-y_{v}y_{w}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
.
Then
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{t}y_{u}=(y_{r}y_{s}-y_{v}y_{w})-(y_{t}y_{u}-y_{v}y_{w})\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
.

Last we consider the case
\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{r})\leq\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{s})
and
\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{r})\geq\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{s})
. In this case the binomial
y_{t}y_{u}-y_{r}y_{s}
is a symmetric exchange relation, as
\varphi(y_{r})=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\varphi(y_{t})
,
\varphi(y_{s})=\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\varphi(y_{u})
,
\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{t})<\deg_{i}\varphi(y_{u})
, and
\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{t})>\deg_{j}\varphi(y_{u})
. We have now seen that
y_{r}y_{s}-y_{t}y_{u}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
in all possible cases.∎

2.
The Strong Exchange Property

It was proved in
[
5
]
that Conjecture
1.3
holds for discrete polymatroids with the so called
strong exchange property
.

Definition 2.1
.

A set
B
of monomials in
\mathbb{K}[X]
is called a basis with the
strong exchange property (SEP)
if all the monomials in
B
are of the same degree and if
f,g\in B
with
\deg_{i}f>\deg_{i}g
and
\deg_{j}f<\deg_{j}g
, for some
i
and
j
, then
\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}f\in B
.

Notice that we will also have
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}g\in B
in the above definition.

Theorem 2.2

(Herzog-Hibi 2002)
.

If
B
is a basis with the SEP then the toric ideal
\mathcal{J}_{B}
is generated by the symmetric exchange relations, and has a quadratic Gröbner basis.

We say that a monomial ideal has the SEP if it is generated by a basis with the SEP. Every basis with the SEP is polymatroidal, but the converse does not hold. It was proved in
[
6
]
that bases with the SEP are isomorphic to bases of Veronese type. In Theorem
2.3
we make this characterization a bit more explicit. For a basis
B
with the SEP, let
\max_{B}(i)=\max\{\deg_{i}(f)\ |\ f\in B\}
and
\min_{B}(i)=\min\{\deg_{i}(f)\ |\ f\in B\}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
B
be a basis with the SEP of monomials of degree
d
. Then

B=\left\{x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\ \Big|\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=d,\ {\min}_{B}(i)\leq\alpha_{i}\leq{\max}_{B}(i)\right\}

Proof.

We begin by proving the following claim: For any
i
and
j
there is a monomial
f\in B
with
\deg_{i}f=\min_{B}(i)
and
\deg_{j}f=\max_{B}(j)
. To prove this, let
f
be a monomial in
B
such that
\deg_{i}f=\min_{B}(i)
and
\deg_{j}f
is as high as possible for a monomial
f
with
\deg_{i}f=\min_{B}(i)
. If
\deg_{j}f<\max_{B}(j)
, take
g\in B
with
\deg_{j}g=\max_{B}(j)
. Then
\deg_{j}g>\deg_{j}f
and there is a
k
with
\deg_{k}g<\deg_{k}f
and
\frac{x_{j}}{x_{k}}f\in B
. But this contradicts our choice of
f
, and we can conclude that
\deg_{j}f=\max_{B}(j)
.

A consequence of the claim is that if
f\in B
and
\deg_{i}f>\min_{B}(i)
and
\deg_{j}f<\max_{B}(j)
we have
\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}f\in B
. Now let
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
be a monomial of degree
d
such that
\min_{B}(i)\leq\alpha_{i}\leq\max_{B}(i)
for each
i
. Take any monomial
x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\in B
. If
x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\neq x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
then
\beta_{i}>\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{j}<\alpha_{j}
for some
i
and
j
, say
i=1
and
j=2
. Then
\beta_{1}>\min_{B}(1)
and
\beta_{2}<\max_{B}(2)
, and we have
x_{1}^{\beta_{1}-1}x_{2}^{\beta_{2}+1}x_{3}^{\beta_{3}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\in B
. If
x_{1}^{\beta_{1}-1}x_{2}^{\beta_{2}+1}x_{3}^{\beta_{3}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\neq x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
we repeat the same argument. Eventually we will get that
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\in B
, which is what we wanted to prove.
∎

We define the product of two polymatroidal bases
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
as

B_{1}B_{2}=\{b_{1}b_{2}\ |\ b_{1}\in B_{1},b_{2}\in B_{2}\}.

If we consider
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
as generating sets of ideals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, then clearly
B_{1}B_{2}
is a generating set for the ideal
I_{1}I_{2}
. A nice feature of polymatroidal bases, proved in
[
2
]
, is that their products are again polymatroidal. Products of bases with the SEP does not necessarily have the SEP. For example
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\{x_{3},x_{4}\}=\{x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4},x_{2}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4}\}
does not have the SEP. However, powers of bases with the SEP do have the SEP.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
B
has the SEP, so does
B^{k}
for any positive integer
k
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3

B=\Big\{x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\ |\ {\min}_{B}(i)\leq\alpha_{i}\leq{\max}_{B}(i),\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=d\Big\}.

Let

\mathcal{M}=\Big\{x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\ |\ k{\min}_{B}(i)\leq\alpha_{i}\leq k{\max}_{B}(i),\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=dk\Big\}

We can see that
\mathcal{M}
is a basis with the SEP, and
B^{k}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. We shall prove
B^{k}\supseteq\mathcal{M}
. Let
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\in\mathcal{M}
, and write
\alpha_{i}=k\beta_{i}+r_{i}
with
0\leq r_{i}<k
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=dk
, the number
\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}
must also be divisible by
k
. Since
r_{i}<k
we can factorize
x_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{r_{n}}=M_{1}\cdots M_{k}
where
M_{1},\ldots M_{k}
are squarefree monomials all of the same degree. Then we have a factorization

x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}M_{i})

and
x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}M_{i}\in B
since

{\min}_{B}(j)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha_{j}}{k}\right\rfloor\leq\deg_{j}(x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}M_{i})\leq\left\lceil\frac{\alpha_{j}}{k}\right\rceil\leq{\max}_{B}(j).

We have now proved that
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\in B^{k}
, and hence
B^{k}
has the SEP.
∎

The main results of this paper is Theorem
2.5
which describes the structure of the toric ring
\mathbb{K}[B]
when
B
is a product of bases with the SEP. In particular, we show that Conjecture
1.3
holds in this case.

For two graded
\mathbb{K}
-algebras
R=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}
and
S=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}S_{i}
we let
R\circ S
denote their
Segre product
, i. e.
R\circ S=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}S_{i}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
B=B_{1}\cdots B_{s}
where
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
are bases with the SEP. Then

(1)

\mathcal{J}_{B}
is generated by symmetric exchange relations, and

(2)

\mathbb{K}[B]\cong\mathbb{K}[B_{1}]\circ\dots\circ\mathbb{K}[B_{s}]/L
where
L
is generated by binomials of degree one.

The theorem is proved in Section
3
. Here we should point out that Conjecture
1.3
is still open, since not every polymatroidal basis is the product of bases with the SEP. Consider for example the polymatroidal basis

B=\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4},x_{3}x_{4}\}.

It is not of the form in Theorem
2.3
, so it does not have the SEP. If
B
would be a product of two bases, it would be the product of two sets of variables, but one easily checks that this is not the case.

In the proof of Theorem
2.5
we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
B
be a basis with the SEP. Suppose
f,g,\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}f,\frac{x_{k}}{x_{\ell}}g\in B
. Then there is an index
m
such that
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{m}}f,\frac{x_{\ell}}{x_{m}}f,\frac{x_{m}}{x_{\ell}}g\in B
.

Proof.

If
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{\ell}}f\in B
we are done, as we can choose
m=\ell
. Suppose
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{\ell}}f\notin B
. Since
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}f\in B
we have
\deg_{i}f<\max_{B}(i)
. Then
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{\ell}}f\notin B
implies
\deg_{\ell}f=\min_{B}(\ell)
. As
\frac{x_{k}}{x_{\ell}}g\in B
we have
\deg_{\ell}g>\min(\ell)=\deg_{\ell}f
. Then there is an
m
such that
\deg_{m}g<\deg_{m}f
and
\frac{x_{m}}{x_{\ell}}g,\frac{x_{\ell}}{x_{m}}f\in B
. Also,
\deg_{m}f>\min_{B}(m)
and
\deg_{i}f<\max_{B}(i)
gives
\frac{x_{i}}{x_{m}}f\in B
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.5

Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
be bases in
\mathbb{K}[X]
with the SEP, and let
B=B_{1}\cdots B_{s}
. We let
Y
be the set of
\prod_{j=1}^{s}|B_{j}|
variables
y_{v}
indexed by vectors
v=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
where
f_{j}\in B_{j}
. We define our homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{K}[Y]\to\mathbb{K}[X]
so that
y_{v}
maps to the product of the entries in the vector
v
. For a monomial
y_{v_{1}}\cdots y_{v_{d}}\in\mathbb{K}[Y]
we associate a
d\times s
matrix with row vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
. The matrix associated to a monomial is unique up to permutations of the rows, and
\varphi(y_{v_{1}}\cdots y_{v_{d}})
is the product of all the entries in this matrix.

Notice that the toric ideal
\mathcal{J}_{B}=\ker\varphi
, as we defined it here, may contain binomials of degree one, as it can happen that
\varphi(y_{v})=\varphi(y_{w})
even though
v\neq w
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{B}
denote the ideal generated by the binomials of degree one in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{B}
be the ideal generated by the symmetric exchange relations in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
. Also, let
\mathcal{P}_{B}
be the ideal generated by the binomials
F-G\in\mathcal{J}_{B}
such that the matrices of
F
and
G
differ in at most one column (up to a permutation of the rows). We will see that
\mathcal{J}_{B}=\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}=\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}
. This proves that
\mathcal{J}_{B}
is generated by the symmetric exchange relations, as the given generating set of
\mathcal{E}_{B}
will still correspond to symmetric exchange relations after reducing modulo
\mathcal{L}_{B}
. The fact that
\mathcal{J}_{B}=\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}
proves the second part of Theorem
2.5
, as

\frac{\mathbb{K}[Y]}{\mathcal{P}_{B}}\cong\frac{\mathbb{K}[Y_{1}]}{\mathcal{J}_{B_{1}}}\circ\dots\circ\frac{\mathbb{K}[Y_{s}]}{\mathcal{J}_{B_{s}}}.

Now it remains to prove
\mathcal{J}_{B}=\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}=\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}
, or equivalently
\mathcal{J}_{B}\subseteq(\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F
be a monomial in
\mathbb{K}[Y]
, and let
(f_{ij})
be the associated matrix. Let
F^{\prime}
be the monomial in
\mathbb{K}[Y]
with the associated matrix
(f^{\prime}_{ij})
obtained from
(f_{ij})
by permuting the elements in one column. Then
F-F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}\cap\mathcal{P}_{B}
.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{B}
that
F-F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{B}
, so we need to prove that
F-F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
.
As any permutation can by performed as a sequence of transpositions, it is enough to consider a transposition of elements in a column. Without loss of generality, we assume that
f^{\prime}_{11}=f_{21}
,
f^{\prime}_{21}=f_{11}
, and otherwise
f^{\prime}_{ij}=f_{ij}
. We define
u_{i}=(f_{i1},\ldots,f_{is})
and
v_{i}=(f^{\prime}_{i1},\ldots,f^{\prime}_{is})
, so that
F=y_{u_{1}}\cdots y_{u_{d}}
and
F^{\prime}=y_{v_{1}}\cdots y_{v_{d}}
. Then

F-F^{\prime}=y_{u_{1}}\cdots y_{u_{d}}-y_{v_{1}}\cdots y_{v_{d}}=(y_{u_{1}}y_{u_{2}}-y_{v_{1}}y_{v_{2}})y_{u_{3}}\cdots y_{u_{d}},

and we want to prove that
y_{u_{1}}y_{u_{2}}-y_{v_{1}}y_{v_{2}}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}
. Recall that
f_{11},f_{21}\in B_{1}
, which is a basis with the SEP. We have

f_{21}=\frac{x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}}{x_{b_{1}}x_{b_{2}}}\cdots\frac{x_{a_{k}}}{x_{b_{k}}}f_{11}

for some
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},b_{1},\ldots b_{k}
, and we may assume
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\cap\{b_{1},\ldots b_{k}\}=\emptyset
, so that no variable occurs both in the numerator and in the denominator. By Theorem
2.3

f_{11}^{(m)}:=\frac{x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}}{x_{b_{1}}x_{b_{2}}}\cdots\frac{x_{a_{m}}}{x_{b_{m}}}f_{11}\in B_{1},\ \ m=1,\ldots,k,

since with this construction
\deg f_{11}^{(m)}=\deg f_{11}
and

{\min}_{B_{1}}\!(i)\leq\min(\deg_{i}\!f_{11},\deg_{i}\!f_{21})\leq\deg_{i}\!f_{11}^{(m)}\leq\max(\deg_{i}\!f_{11},\deg_{i}\!f_{21})\leq{\max}_{B_{1}}\!(i).

In the same way

f_{21}^{(m)}:=\frac{x_{b_{1}}x_{b_{2}}}{x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}}\cdots\frac{x_{b_{m}}}{x_{a_{m}}}f_{21}\in B_{1},\ \ m=1,\ldots,k.

Let

u_{1}^{(m)}=(f_{11}^{(m)},f_{12},\ldots,f_{1s})\ \mbox{and}\ u_{2}^{(m)}=(f_{21}^{(m)},f_{22},\ldots,f_{2s}),\ \mbox{for}\ m=1,\ldots,k

and notice that
u_{1}^{(k)}=v_{1}
and
u_{2}^{(k)}=v_{2}
. By Lemma
1.5

y_{u_{1}}y_{u_{2}}=y_{u_{1}^{(1)}}y_{u_{2}^{(1)}}=y_{u_{1}^{(2)}}y_{u_{2}^{(2)}}=\ldots=y_{u_{1}^{(k)}}y_{u_{2}^{(k)}}=y_{v_{1}}y_{v_{2}}\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{K}[Y]/\mathcal{E}_{B},

and it follows that
F=F^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{K}[Y]/\mathcal{E}_{B}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
F
and
G
be monomials in
\mathbb{K}[Y]
such that their matrices are identical in the first
m<s
columns, and
F-G\in\mathcal{J}_{B}
. If
\mathcal{J}_{B^{\prime}}=\mathcal{E}_{B^{\prime}}+\mathcal{L}_{B^{\prime}}=\mathcal{P}_{B^{\prime}}+\mathcal{L}_{B^{\prime}}
, where
B^{\prime}=B_{m+1}\cdots B_{s}
, then
F-G\in(\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})
.

The analogous statement is also true if the matrices of
F
and
G
are identical in the last
m
columns, or more generally in any subset of the columns. The proof in this situation follows the same recipe.

Proof.

As the first
m
columns of the matrices
(f_{ij})
and
(g_{ij})
representing
F
and
G
are identical, and
F-G\in\mathcal{J}_{B}
, we have

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
m<j\leq s\end{subarray}}f_{ij}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
m<j\leq s\end{subarray}}g_{ij}

which corresponds to a relation in
\mathcal{J}_{B^{\prime}}
. Then there is a monomial
H\in\mathbb{K}[Y]
such that
G=H
in
\mathbb{K}[Y]/((\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}))
and
H
is identical to
F
and
G
in the first
m
columns, and identical to
F
in the last
s-m
columns after possibly permuting the rows. Then we can apply Lemma
3.1
and get that
G=H=F
in
\mathbb{K}[Y]/((\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}))
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F
be a monomial in
\mathbb{K}[Y]
such that
\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\varphi(F)\in B^{d}
. Then there are monomials
F^{\prime}
and
H
such that
F-F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{B}\cap\mathcal{P}_{B}
,
\varphi(H)=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\varphi(F^{\prime})
, and the matrices associated to
F^{\prime}
and
H
differ in at most one entry in each column.

Proof.

Let
(f_{ij})
be the matrix associated to
F
. Since
\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\varphi(F)\in B^{d}
there are
h_{ij}
’s, such that

\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\varphi(F)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}h_{ij},\ \ \ h_{ij}\in B_{j}.

For each pair
i,j
there is a multiset
\Lambda_{ij}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}^{2}
such that

h_{ij}=\Big(\prod_{(a,b)\in\Lambda_{ij}}\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}\Big)f_{ij}.

The choice of the
h_{ij}
’s is not unique, and the goal is the choose them so that for each
j
there is only one
i
for which
\Lambda_{ij}
in nonempty. First note that we can choose
\Lambda_{ij}
so that for each
(a,b)\in\Lambda_{ij}
it holds that

\deg_{a}f_{ij}<\deg_{a}h_{ij}\ \text{and}\ \deg_{b}f_{ij}>\deg_{b},h_{ij}.

It follows that
\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}f_{ij}\in B_{j}
, since
B_{j}
has the SEP. As

\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}\prod_{(a,b)\in\Lambda_{ij}}\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}\Big)\varphi(F)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}\prod_{(a,b)\in\Lambda_{ij}}\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}f_{ij}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}h_{ij}=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\varphi(F)

we have

\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}\prod_{(a,b)\in\Lambda_{ij}}\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}\Big)=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}

and we may order the factors
\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}
in the left hand side so that

(1)

\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}\prod_{(a,b)\in\Lambda_{ij}}\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}\Big)=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{a_{1}}}\frac{x_{a_{1}}}{x_{a_{2}}}\frac{x_{a_{2}}}{x_{a_{3}}}\cdots\frac{x_{a_{k-1}}}{x_{a_{k}}}\frac{x_{a_{k}}}{x_{2}}

Each factor in the right hand side still corresponds to an index pair in some
\Lambda_{ij}
.
Now, if there are two different values of
i
such that
\Lambda_{i1}
is nonempty we do the following. In the right hand side of (
1
) consider the leftmost and rightmost factors corresponding to index pairs from any
\Lambda_{i1}
. Say for instance that it is
(a_{1},a_{2})\in\Lambda_{11}
and
(a_{5},a_{6})\in\Lambda_{21}
. Then

f_{11},f_{21},\frac{x_{a_{1}}}{x_{a_{2}}}f_{11},\ \text{and}\ \frac{x_{a_{5}}}{x_{a_{6}}}f_{21}\ \mbox{all belong to}\ B_{1}.

By Lemma
2.6
there is an
m
such that

\frac{x_{a_{1}}}{x_{m}}f_{11},\frac{x_{a_{6}}}{x_{m}}f_{11},\ \text{and}\ \frac{x_{m}}{x_{a_{6}}}f_{21}\in B_{1}.

Now we define
F^{\prime}
as the matrix
(f^{\prime}_{ij})
with
f^{\prime}_{ij}=f_{ij}
except
f^{\prime}_{11}=\frac{x_{a_{6}}}{x_{m}}f_{11}
and
f^{\prime}_{21}=\frac{x_{m}}{x_{a_{6}}}f_{21}
. Notice that
F^{\prime}-F\in\mathcal{E}_{B}\cap\mathcal{P}_{B}
. In
\Lambda_{11}
we replace
(a_{1},a_{2})
by
(a_{1},a_{6})
, and we remove
(a_{2},a_{3}),(a_{3},a_{4}),(a_{4},a_{5}),
and
(a_{5},a_{6})
from their corresponding
\Lambda_{ij}
’s. This means that
\Lambda_{11}=\{(a_{1},a_{6})\}
and
\Lambda_{i1}=\emptyset
for
i>1
. We also redefine the
h_{ij}
’s as

h_{ij}=\Big(\prod_{(a,b)\in\Lambda_{ij}}\frac{x_{a}}{x_{b}}\Big)f^{\prime}_{ij}.

Notice that we still have
h_{ij}\in B_{j}
, in particular

h_{11}=\frac{x_{a_{1}}}{x_{a_{6}}}f^{\prime}_{11}=\frac{x_{a_{1}}}{x_{a_{6}}}\frac{x_{a_{6}}}{x_{m}}f_{11}=\frac{x_{a_{1}}}{x_{m}}f_{11}\in B_{1}

and

h_{i1}=f^{\prime}_{i1}\in B_{1}\ \text{for}\ i>1.

Now we do the same procedure for
j=2
, but with
F
replaced by
F^{\prime}
and the right hand side of (
1
) replaced by

\frac{x_{1}}{x_{a_{1}}}\frac{x_{a_{1}}}{x_{a_{6}}}\frac{x_{a_{6}}}{x_{a_{7}}}\cdots\frac{x_{a_{k-1}}}{x_{a_{k}}}\frac{x_{a_{k}}}{x_{2}}.

We continue with this for all values of
j
. Finally we end up with
h_{ij}
’s that for each
j
there is at most one
i
for which
h_{ij}\neq f^{\prime}_{ij}
. Then
H
given by the matrix
(h_{ij})
together with
F^{\prime}
has the desired properties.
∎

Now we are ready to prove that
\mathcal{J}_{B}\subseteq(\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})
. Suppose we have monomials
F
and
G
of degree

in
\mathbb{K}[Y]
such that their difference is in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
. We shall prove that
F=G
in
\mathbb{K}[Y]/((\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}))
by induction over
s
. The base case

is clear, as then

has the SEP, and the matrices have only one column. Now suppose the statement is true for any product of fewer than
s
bases with the SEP. The idea of the proof is the following. Let
(f_{ij})
and
(g_{ij})
be the matrices corresponding to
F
and
G
. Say we are in the special situation where
\prod_{i=1}^{d}f_{i1}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}g_{i1}.
Then we also have
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}f_{ij}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}g_{ij}
. Let
H
be the monomial in
\mathbb{K}[Y]
associated to the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}g_{11}&f_{12}&\cdots&f_{1s}\\
g_{21}&f_{22}&\cdots&f_{2s}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
g_{d1}&f_{d2}&\cdots&f_{ds}\end{pmatrix}

We have

3.2
we have

in
\mathbb{K}[Y]/((\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}))
. If

for each

3.2
one more time to

and

, since they are identical in the last column. It then follows that

in
\mathbb{K}[Y]/((\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B})\cap(\mathcal{P}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{B}))
.

Now, what if we are not in the special situation with
\prod_{i=1}^{d}f_{i1}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}g_{i1}
? If
\prod_{i=1}^{d}f_{i1}\neq\prod_{i=1}^{d}g_{i1}
then also
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}f_{ij}\neq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}g_{ij}
. Since

is polymatroidal there are say
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
so that

(2)

\deg_{1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}f_{ij}<\deg_{1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}g_{ij}\ \text{and}\ \deg_{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}f_{ij}>\deg_{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}g_{ij}

and

\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}f_{ij}\in B_{2}^{d}\cdots B_{s}^{d}.

By Lemma
3.3
we can, after replacing
F

\mathcal{E}_{B}\cap\mathcal{P}_{B}
, find
h_{ij}
’s
1\leq i\leq d
,
2\leq j\leq s
so that

\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}f_{ij}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
2\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}h_{ij},\ \ \ h_{ij}\in B_{j}

such that for each
j
there is at most one

’s for which
h_{ij}\neq f_{ij}
.

Because of (
2
) we also have

\deg_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{d}f_{i1}>\deg_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{d}g_{i1}\ \text{and}\ \deg_{2},\prod_{i=1}^{d}f_{i1}<\deg_{2},\prod_{i=1}^{d}g_{i1}.

By Proposition
2.4

B_{1}^{d}
has the SEP, and it follows that

\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}f_{i1}\in B_{1}^{d}.

Here we can apply Lemma
3.3
once more, to get that

\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}f_{i1}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{i1},\ \ h_{i1}\in B_{1}

where
h_{i1}\neq f_{i1}
for at most one
i
. Now, let
H
be the monomial with the associated matrix
(h_{ij})
,
1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq j\leq s
. For each
j
there is at most one
i
for which
h_{ij}\neq f_{ij}
. We can use Lemma
3.1
to, for one

’s so that the

is

. Since now

and

. If
\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{i1}\neq\prod_{i=1}^{d}g_{i1}
we can repeat this process, now with
H
and
G
. After finitely many repetitions we will get

4.
Rees algebras

An object closely related to the toric ring
\mathbb{K}[B]
is the
Rees algebra
of the monomial ideal generated by
B
. In general, the Rees algebra of an ideal
I\subseteq\mathbb{K}[X]
is defined as
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}I^{j}t^{j}
. If
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
we have
\mathcal{R}(I)=\mathbb{K}[X][f_{1}t,\ldots,f_{m}t]
. Let
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
and let
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]
be the polynomial ring on the variables
X\cup Y
with the bigrading
\deg x_{i}=(1,0)
and
\deg y_{i}=(0,1)
. We say that an element has degree
(k,*)
if it has degree
(k,s)
for any
s
, and similarly for
(*,k)
.
We can present
\mathcal{R}(I)
as
\mathbb{K}[X,Y]/\ker\phi
where
\phi:\mathbb{K}[X,Y]\to\mathbb{K}[X][t]
is the homomorphism defined by
\phi(x_{i})=x_{i}
and
\phi(y_{i})=f_{i}t
. The defining ideal
\ker\phi
is called the
Rees ideal
of
I
. An ideal
I
is said to be of
fiber type
if its Rees ideal is generated in degrees
(0,*)
and
(*,1)
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

If
I
has linear free resolution and is of fiber type then its Rees ideal is generated in degrees
(0,*)
and
(1,1)
.

Polymatroidal ideals are of fiber type and have linear free resolution, see
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, so Lemma
4.1
applies to polymatroidal ideals. If
I
has the SEP then its Rees ideal is generated in degrees
(0,2)
and
(1,1)
, see
[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
. We can now extend this theorem to products of ideals with the SEP.

Theorem 4.2
.

The Rees ideal of a product of ideals with the SEP is generated in degrees
(0,2)
and
(1,1)
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a product of bases with the SEP, and let
\mathcal{I}
be the Rees ideal of the ideal generated by
B
. By Lemma
4.1

\mathcal{I}
is generated in degrees
(0,*)
and
(1,1)
. The set of elements of degree
(0,*)
in
\mathcal{I}
is precisely the toric ideal
\mathcal{J}_{B}
. Then the result follows by Theorem
2.5
.
∎

As the generators of the Rees ideals in Theorem
4.2
has total degree 2 we conclude this section with the following question.

Question 4.3
.

Is the Rees algebra of a product of ideals with the SEP Koszul?

5.
Gröbner bases and transversal polymatroidal bases

Polymatroids that are given as products of subsets of
X
are called
transversal
. This is a special case of the class of polymatroids considered in this paper, as subsets of
X
indeed have the SEP. Theorem
2.5
(
2
) was proved for transversal polymatroids in
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
, which in this case says that
\mathbb{K}[B]
is isomorphic to the Segre product of polynomial rings modulo an ideal of linear binomials. Moreover
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
states that
\mathbb{K}[B]
is Koszul when
B
is transversal. By Theorem
2.5
(
1
) we now also know that White’s conjecture holds for transversal polymatroids. However, the following questions are still open.

Question 5.1
.

Does the toric ideal of a transversal polymatroid, or more generally a product of bases with the SEP, have a quadratic Gröbner basis?

Question 5.2
.

If
B
is a product of bases with the SEP, is
\mathbb{K}[B]
Koszul?

It is not obvious which monomial order to use when searching for a quadratic Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{J}_{B}
. Let
B=X_{1}\cdots X_{s}
, where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{s}
are subsets of
X
. Then
\mathcal{J}_{B}
is generated by the so called
Hibi relations
together with binomials of degree one, see
[
1
, Proposition 3.7]
. The Hibi relations are defined in the following way. We fix an order of the variables in each
X_{i}
. The orderings do not have to be compatible with each other. Then we let
Y
be the set of variables indexed by integer vectors
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})
so that
1\leq\alpha_{j}\leq|X_{j}|
. The toric ideal
\mathcal{J}_{B}
is the kernel of
\varphi:K[Y]\to\mathbb{K}[X]
where
\varphi(y_{\alpha})
is defined as the product of the
\alpha_{1}
-th element of
X_{1}
, the
\alpha_{2}
-th element of
X_{2}
, and so on. The Hibi relations are all relations
y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-y_{\alpha\wedge\beta}y_{\alpha\vee\beta}
where
\alpha\wedge\beta=(\max(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),\ldots,\max(\alpha_{s},\beta_{s}))
and
\alpha\vee\beta=(\min(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),\ldots,\min(\alpha_{s},\beta_{s})).

We have a partial ordering on the variables
Y
by
y_{\alpha}\leq y_{\beta}
if
\alpha_{i}\leq\beta_{i}
for all
i
. Notice that the Hibi relation
y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-y_{\alpha\wedge\beta}y_{\alpha\vee\beta}
is nonzero only if
y_{\alpha}
and
y_{\beta}
are incomparable. The Hibi relations are a Gröbner basis w. r. t. the DegRevLex order with any order on
Y
extending the partial order. The leading term of
y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-y_{\alpha\wedge\beta}y_{\alpha\vee\beta}
is
y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}
. We make the following observation.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
B=X_{1}\cdots X_{s}
be a transversal polymatroidal basis such that
\mathcal{J}_{B}
is generated by the Hibi relations together with one binomial of degree one. Then
\mathcal{J}_{B}
has a quadratic Gröbner basis. Moreover, if
|X_{1}|=\dots=|X_{s}|
then
\mathbb{K}[B]
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

We may order the variables in each
X_{i}
so that the one binomial of degree one in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
is
y_{\alpha}-y_{\beta}
with
\alpha=(|X_{1}|,\ldots,|X_{s}|)
and
\beta=(1,1,\ldots,1)
. The Hibi relations are a Gröbner basis, and we shall prove that they remain so after replacing every occurrence of
y_{\alpha}
by
y_{\beta}
. First notice that
y_{\alpha}
does not divide the leading term of any Hibi relation. Indeed,
y_{\alpha}\geq y_{\gamma}\geq y_{\beta}
for any
y_{\gamma}\in Y
. This shows that replacing
y_{\alpha}
by
y_{\beta}
does not change the leading term of any binomial in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
.

Now assume
|X_{1}|=\dots=|X_{s}|=m
. Then
\mathbb{K}[B]\cong S/L
there
S
is the Segre product of
s
copies of the polynomial ring in
m
variables, and
L
is generated by a binomial of degree one. The Segre product of several of copies of a Gorenstein ring is again Gorenstein, by a result in
[
3
]
. The polynomial ring is Gorenstein, so
T
is Gorenstein.
T
is also a domain, and hence
T/L
is Gorenstein.
∎

Example 5.4
.

Let
B=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\{x_{2},x_{3}\}\{x_{3},x_{4}\}\{x_{4},x_{5}\}\{x_{5},x_{1}\}
. Then
Y
is the set of variables indexed by integer vectors
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{5})
where
1\leq\alpha_{i}\leq 2
. We have
\varphi(y_{11111})=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
and
\varphi(y_{22222})=x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}x_{1}
, and
y_{22222}-y_{11111}
is the only linear relation in
\mathcal{J}_{B}
. Hence the Hibi relations, with every occurrence of
y_{22222}
replaced by
y_{11111}
, is a quadratic Gröbner basis defining
\mathbb{K}[B]
. One example of a Hibi relation is
y_{11122}y_{12221}-y_{11121}y_{12222}
where

\displaystyle\varphi(y_{11122})=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{5}x_{1},\ \ \varphi(y_{12221})=x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}x_{5},

\displaystyle\varphi(y_{11121})=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{5}x_{5},\ \mbox{and}\ \varphi(y_{12222})=x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}x_{1}.

The Segre product of five polynomial rings in two variables is a Gorenstein algebra with Hilbert series

\frac{1+26t+66t^{2}+26t^{3}+t^{4}}{(1-t)^{6}}.

Hence
K[B]
is Gorenstein with Hilbert series

\frac{1+26t+66t^{2}+26t^{3}+t^{4}}{(1-t)^{5}}.\qed

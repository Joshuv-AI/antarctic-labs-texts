Generalized polymorphisms
Gilad Chase, Yuval Filmus

Abstract
We find all functions f 0 , f 1 , … , f m : { 0 , 1 } n → { 0 , 1 } f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\} and g 0 , g 1 , … , g n : { 0 , 1 } m → { 0 , 1 } g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\colon\{0,1\}^{m}\to\{0,1\} satisfying the following identity for all n × m n\times m matrices ( z i ​ j ) ∈ { 0 , 1 } n × m (z_{ij})\in\{0,1\}^{n\times m} : f 0 ​ ( g 1 ​ ( z 11 , … , z 1 ​ m ) , … , g n ​ ( z n ​ 1 , … , z n ​ m ) ) = g 0 ​ ( f 1 ​ ( z 11 , … , z n ​ 1 ) , … , f m ​ ( z 1 ​ m , … , z n ​ m ) ) . f_{0}(g_{1}(z_{11},\dots,z_{1m}),\dots,g_{n}(z_{n1},\dots,z_{nm}))=g_{0}(f_{1}(z_{11},\dots,z_{n1}),\dots,f_{m}(z_{1m},\dots,z_{nm})). Our results generalize work of Dokow and Holzman (2010), which considered the case g 0 = g 1 = ⋯ = g n g_{0}=g_{1}=\cdots=g_{n} , and of Chase, Filmus, Minzer, Mossel and Saurabh (2022), which considered the case g 0 ≠ g 1 = ⋯ = g n g_{0}\neq g_{1}=\cdots=g_{n} .

In social choice theory, the predicate

Paper organization

The rest of the paper constitutes the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. To avoid trivialities, we assume throughout that

The proof follows the approach used in Chase et al.
[

]
, which uses Fourier analysis. For this reason, we switch from
\{0,1\}
to
\{-1,1\}

.

The coefficients

A set

Throughout the paper, we identify functions over

In the introduction, we defined a generalized polymorphism to be a set of Boolean functions satisfying

Definition 2.1

(Generalized polymorphism)
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
be
n

g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
be

. If all of these functions are also Boolean, then they constitute a

As explained in

Let

.

A function

The second property follows immediately from the observation that shifting preserves

Let

For each

, the corresponding rows:
\col(W,j)=\{i:(i,j)\in W\}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Duality lemma)
.

Suppose
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m},g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
is a generalized polymorphism, where
f_{1},\dots,f_{m},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
are centered and non-zero.

Let
W\subseteq[n]\times[m]
. Then:

1.

\rows(W)\in\supp(f_{0})
and
\row(W,i)\in\supp(g_{i})
for all
i\in\rows(W)
if and only if

\cols(W)\in\supp(g_{0})
and
\col(W,j)\in\supp(f_{j})
for all
j\in\cols(W)
.

2.

\rows(W)\in\imsupp(f_{0})
and
\row(W,i)\in\imsupp(g_{i})
for all
i\in\rows(W)
if and only if

\cols(W)\in\imsupp(g_{0})
and
\col(W,j)\in\imsupp(f_{j})
for all
j\in\cols(W)
.

Proof.

Substituting the Fourier expansions of the various functions in
Equation

1.1
, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq[n]\\
\text{for all }i\in A\colon\\
B_{i}\subseteq[m],B_{i}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{0}(A)\prod_{i\in A}\hat{g}_{i}(B_{i})\prod_{j\in B_{i}}z_{ij}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq[m]\\
\text{for all }j\in B\colon\\
A_{j}\subseteq[n],A_{j}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\hat{g}_{0}(B)\prod_{j\in B}\hat{f}_{j}(A_{j})\prod_{i\in A_{j}}z_{ij},

where we can assume that
B_{i},A_{j}\neq\emptyset
since
f_{1},\dots,f_{m},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
are centered.
Since
B_{i},A_{j}\neq\emptyset
, every monomial appears exactly once on each side of this equation.

Extracting the coefficient of the monomial
\prod_{(i,j)\in W}z_{ij}
, we obtain

\hat{f}_{0}(\rows(W))\prod_{i\in\rows(W)}\hat{g}_{i}(\row(W,i))=\hat{g}_{0}(\cols(W))\prod_{j\in\cols(W)}\hat{f}_{j}(\col(W,j)).

This immediately implies part (a).

We prove part (b) by contradiction. Suppose that
\rows(W)\in\imsupp(f_{0})
and
\row(W,i)\in\imsupp(g_{i})
for all
i\in\rows(W)
. We first show that
\col(W,j)
is inclusion-maximal for all
j\in\cols(W)
, and then that
\cols(W)
itself is inclusion-maximal.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
\col(W,j_{0})
is not inclusion-maximal, for some
j_{0}\in\cols(W)
. Then there exists a set

in the support of
\hat{f}_{j_{0}}
. Let
W^{\prime}
be obtained by adding to
W
the points
(i,j_{0})
for
i\in S\setminus\col(W,j_{0})
, and choose an arbitrary
i_{0}\in S\setminus\col(W,j_{0})
.

We now consider two cases. If
i_{0}\notin\rows(W)
then applying part (a) (in the
\leftarrow
direction) to
W^{\prime}
, we deduce that
\rows(W^{\prime})=\rows(W)\cup\{i_{0}\}\in\supp(\hat{f}_{0})
, contradicting the inclusion-maximality of
\rows(W)
. If
i_{0}\in\rows(W)
then applying part (a), we deduce that
\row(W^{\prime},i_{0})=\row(W,i_{0})\cup\{j_{0}\}\in\supp(\hat{g}_{i_{0}})
, contradicting the inclusion-maximality of
\row(W,i_{0})
.

Suppose now that
\cols(W)
is not inclusion-maximal. Let

be in the support of
\hat{g}_{0}
. For each
j\in T\setminus\cols(W)

(this is possible since
f_{j}
is non-zero). Let
W^{\prime}
be obtained by adding to
W
the points
(i,j)
for each

and

Apply part (a) (in the
\leftarrow
direction) to
W^{\prime}
to deduce that
\rows(W^{\prime})=\rows(W)\cup\bigcup_{j\in T\setminus\cols(W)}S_{j}\in\supp(\hat{f}_{0})
. By inclusion-maximality,

, and so

for all
j\in T\setminus\cols(W)
.

Part (a) also implies that for every
i\in\rows(W)
, we have
\row(W^{\prime},i)=\row(W,i)\cup\{j\in T\setminus\cols(W):i\in S_{j}\}\in\supp(\hat{g}_{i})

, and so

must be disjoint from

for all
j\in T\setminus\cols(W)

Put together, we see that
S_{j}=\emptyset
for all
j\in T\setminus\cols(W)

is centered, this is impossible.
∎

2.2
Reachability lemma

Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m},g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
be a generalized polymorphism. If
\emptyset\subsetneq I\subsetneq\rows(Z_{\ell})
then since
Z_{\ell}
is a connected component, we can find

and
i_{1}\in\rows(Z_{\ell})\setminus I
that appear on the same column

. We will need a refinement of this observations.

Lemma 2.4

(Reachability lemma)
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m},g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
be a generalized polymorphism, with connected components

.

Let
\ell\in[k]

be a set which intersects
\rows(Z_{\ell})
but doesn’t contain
\rowstwo(Z_{\ell})
. Then there exist

i_{1}\in\rowstwo(Z_{\ell})\setminus I
such that
i_{0},i_{1}
appear on the same column
j
for some
j\in\cols(Z_{\ell})
, that is
(i_{0},j),(i_{1},j)\in Z_{\ell}
.

Proof.

We can assume, without loss of generality, that
I\subseteq\rows(Z_{\ell})
.
Extend
I
to the set

I^{\prime}=I\cup\{i^{\prime}\in\rowsone(Z_{\ell}):\text{$i^{\prime}$ appears on the same column as some $i\in I$}\}.

Since
I
doesn’t contain all of
\rowstwo(Z_{\ell})
, the same holds for
I^{\prime}
, and in particular,
\emptyset\subsetneq I^{\prime}\subsetneq\rows(Z_{\ell})
. Since
Z_{\ell}
is a connected component, there must exist
i^{\prime}_{0}\in I^{\prime}
and
i^{\prime}_{1}\in\rows(Z_{\ell})\setminus I^{\prime}
that appear on the same column
j
for some
j\in\cols(Z_{\ell})
.

We consider two cases:
i^{\prime}_{0}\in I
and
i^{\prime}_{0}\notin I
. Suppose first that
i^{\prime}_{0}\in I
. We claim that
i^{\prime}_{1}\in\rowstwo(Z_{\ell})
. Indeed, if
i^{\prime}_{1}\in\rowsone(Z_{\ell})
then
i^{\prime}_{1}
appears on the same column as
i^{\prime}_{0}\in I
, and so
i^{\prime}_{1}\in I^{\prime}
, contradicting the definition of
i^{\prime}_{1}
. We can therefore take
i_{0}=i^{\prime}_{0}
and
i_{1}=i^{\prime}_{1}
.

Suppose next that
i^{\prime}_{0}\notin I
. By definition of
I^{\prime}
, we have
i^{\prime}_{0}\in\rowsone(Z_{\ell})
, and there exists

such that

and
i^{\prime\prime}_{0}
are on the same column, which must be column

since

. As in the preceding case,

i_{0}=i^{\prime\prime}_{0}
and

.
∎

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
a\geq 0
and
b\geq 1
be integers such that
a+b\geq 2
. Suppose that

depends on all coordinates and is given by

where
\phi\colon\{-1,1\}^{b}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Then one of the following cases holds:

•

Case 1:
\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{b}=B=0
and
\phi
is
\{-1,1\}
-valued.

•

Case 2:
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{b},B\in\{-1,1\}
, and
\phi
is
\{0,C\}
-valued, where

C=\frac{-2B}{\prod_{i=1}^{b}(2\kappa_{i})}.

Proof.

Suppose first that
a=0
(and so
b\geq 2
), and let
A=\phi()
. Since
f
depends on all coordinates, necessarily
A\neq 0
. We think of
f
as a function of
v
alone. Choose
V_{1},\dots,V_{b}\in\{-1,1\}
such that
V_{i}\neq-\kappa_{i}
for all
i\in[b]
. For
S\subseteq[b]
, let
V^{\oplus S}
be obtained from
V
by flipping the coordinates in
S
.

Observe that

f(V^{\oplus S})=F\prod_{i\in S}\rho_{i}+B,\text{ where }F=A\prod_{i=1}^{b}(V_{i}+\kappa_{i})\neq 0\text{ and }\rho_{i}=\frac{-V_{i}+\kappa_{i}}{V_{i}+\kappa_{i}}\neq 1.

Since
F\neq 0
and
\rho_{i}\neq 1
, for every
i\in[b]
we have

, and so

. This implies that
\rho_{i}=-2B/F-1
is independent of
i
. Denote the common value by
\rho
.

Recall that
b\geq 2
. If

then
\rho^{2}=1
, and so
\rho=-1
. For all
i\in[b]
we have

, and so
\kappa_{i}=0
. In view of the formula for

, in this case

. This is Case 1.

If

then
\rho^{2}=\rho
, and so
\rho=0
. Therefore
\kappa_{i}=V_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
for all

. Since

, we see that

. Finally,

, and so we are in Case 2, with

.

Now suppose that
a\geq 1
. If
b\geq 2

f
depends on all coordinates, there must be an input

\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{b},B\in\{-1,1\}
and
\phi(U_{1})=C
.

\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{b}=B=0
then
\phi(U)\neq 0
for all

, since

is Boolean. Therefore the foregoing shows that
\phi
is
\{-1,1\}
-valued, which is Case 1.

Conversely, if
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{b},B\in\{-1,1\}
then the foregoing shows that for each

, if

\phi(U)=C
. Thus
\phi
is
\{0,C\}
-valued, which is Case 2.

Finally, suppose that
a\geq 1
and

If

-valued. This is Case 1.

If

, such that

\displaystyle f_{0}^{(\ell)}(x)

\displaystyle=2\prod_{i\in\rowstwo(Z_{\ell})}\frac{x_{i}-D_{i}}{-2D_{i}}\prod_{j\in\colstwo(Z_{\ell})}\frac{\phi_{j}(x|_{\colone(j)})+1}{2}-1,

\displaystyle=-2D_{i}\frac{\gamma_{i}(y|_{\rowsone(i)})+1}{2}\prod_{j\in\rowtwo(i)}\frac{y_{j}+\kappa_{ij}}{2\kappa_{ij}}+D_{i},

\displaystyle g_{0}^{(\ell)}(y)

\displaystyle f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=-2B_{j}\frac{\phi_{j}(x|_{\colsone(i)})+1}{2}\prod_{i\in\coltwo(j)}\frac{x_{i}+\kappa_{ij}}{2\kappa_{ij}}+B_{j}.

Proof.

F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{m},G_{1},\dots,G_{n}

have the structure described below.

We start with the following observation:
\kappa_{ij}=0

unless
|\row(i)|,|\col(j)|\geq 2

D_{i}=0
unless
|\row(i)|\geq 2
, and
B_{j}=0

.

If

. Otherwise,

\displaystyle f_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=h(f_{0}^{(1)}(x|_{\rows(Z_{1})}),\dots,f_{0}^{(k)}(x|_{\rows(Z_{k})})),

\displaystyle f_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=h(g_{0}^{(1)}(y|_{\cols(Z_{1})}),\dots,g_{0}^{(k)}(y|_{\cols(Z_{k})})),

where the functions

have the following form. If

and

\displaystyle f_{0}^{(\ell)}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in\rowstwo(Z_{\ell})}(x_{i}-D_{i})\cdot\prod_{j\in\colstwo(Z_{\ell})}\phi_{j}(x|_{\colone(j)}),

\displaystyle g_{0}^{(\ell)}(y)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in\colstwo(Z_{\ell})}(y_{j}-B_{j})\cdot\prod_{i\in\rowstwo(Z_{\ell})}\gamma_{i}(y|_{\rowone(i)}).

The above already handles connected components which are singletons.
In order to handle the remaining components, we need (in some cases) to apply (invertible) affine shifts to the functions

Next, we handle connected components whose points are all on the same row or column. Suppose that

\displaystyle=\gamma_{i}(y|_{\row(i)})+D_{i},

\displaystyle=x_{i}-D_{i},

\displaystyle=\gamma_{i}(y|_{\row(i)}).

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{i}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{i}+D_{i},

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{0}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=f_{0}^{(\ell)}+D_{i}=x_{i},

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{0}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=g_{0}^{(\ell)}+D_{i}=\tilde{\gamma}_{i},

so that

Finally, suppose that
Z_{\ell}
is a non-singleton component in which not all points are on the same row or column. This implies that for each

\rowtwo(i)\neq\emptyset

\coltwo(i)\neq\emptyset
.

a=|\rowone(i)|

For every

We now split into two cases, depending on whether Case 1 holds or Case 2 holds. If Case 1 holds then
\kappa_{ij}=0

for all

If Case 2 holds then

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{i}

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{i}/C_{i}-1,

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{j}

\displaystyle=2\phi_{j}/A_{j}-1,

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{i}}{2}(\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(y|_{\rowsone(i)})+1)\prod_{j\in\rowtwo(i)}(y_{j}+\kappa_{ij})+D_{i}=-2D_{i}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(y|_{\rowsone(i)})+1}{2}\prod_{j\in\rowtwo(i)}\frac{y_{j}+\kappa_{ij}}{2\kappa_{ij}}+D_{i},

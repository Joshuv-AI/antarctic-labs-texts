II
Formalisms

We consider the weak radiative decays of anti-triplet bottom baryons induced by the quark transitions of
b\to f\gamma
with
f=(s,d)
.
We will ignore the contributions from
W
-exchange diagrams since they are suppressed by the CKM elements.
The effective Hamiltonians from the transitions are given by
Buchalla:1995vs

\mathcal{H}_{eff}(b\to f\gamma)=-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{e}{4\pi^{2}}V^{*}_{tf}V_{tb}C_{7\gamma}^{eff}(\mu_{b})m_{b}[\bar{f}i\sigma^{\mu k}(1+\gamma^{5})b]\epsilon_{\mu},

(2)

where
\sigma^{\mu k}=\frac{i}{2}(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})k_{\nu}
, and
C_{7\gamma}^{eff}(\mu_{b})
corresponds to the effective Wilson coefficient at the scale of
\mu_{b}
with

C_{7\gamma}^{eff}(5.09~\rm{GeV})=-0.303
.
The decay amplitudes are obtained by sandwiching
\mathcal{H}_{eff}
with the initial and final states

\mathcal{M}({\bf B}_{b}\to{\bf B}_{n}\gamma)=-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{e}{4\pi^{2}}V^{*}_{tf}V_{tb}C_{7\gamma}^{eff}(\mu_{b})m_{b}\langle{\bf B}_{n}|\bar{f}i\sigma^{\mu k}(1+\gamma^{5})b|{\bf B}_{b}\rangle\epsilon_{\mu}.

(3)

The matrix elements above can be parametrized in terms of the tensor form factors, given by

\displaystyle\langle{\bf B}_{n}|\bar{f}i\sigma^{\mu k}b|{\bf B}_{b}\rangle

\displaystyle=\overline{u}_{{\bf B}_{n}}[f_{1}^{TV}(k^{2})(\gamma^{\mu}k^{2}-k^{\mu}\not{k})/M_{{\bf B}_{b}}-f_{2}^{TV}(k^{2})i\sigma^{\mu k}]u_{{\bf B}_{b}},

(4)

\displaystyle\langle{\bf B}_{n}|\bar{f}i\sigma^{\mu k}\gamma^{5}b|{\bf B}_{b}\rangle

\displaystyle=\overline{u}_{{\bf B}_{n}}[f_{1}^{TA}(k^{2})(\gamma^{\mu}k^{2}-k^{\mu}\not{k})/M_{{\bf B}_{b}}-f_{2}^{TA}(k^{2})i\sigma^{\mu k}]\gamma^{5}u_{{\bf B}_{b}}.

where
u_{{\bf B}_{b(n)}}
stands for the Dirac spinor of
{\bf B}_{b(n)}
,
M
denotes the baryon mass, and
k^{\mu}
is the momentum transfer between the initial and final states. In our case of the radiative decays,
k^{2}=0
. We will neglect the contributions associated with the form factors of

f_{1}^{TV}(k^{2})
and
f_{1}^{TA}(k^{2})

unless particularly noted in the rest of this paper.
Consequently, the decay rates are given as

\Gamma({\bf B}_{b}\to{\bf B}_{n}\gamma)=\frac{\alpha_{em}}{64\pi^{4}}G_{F}^{2}m_{b}^{2}M_{{\bf B}_{b}}^{3}|V^{*}_{tf}V_{tb}|^{2}(C_{7\gamma}^{eff})^{2}\left(1-\frac{M_{{\bf B}_{n}}^{2}}{M_{{\bf B}_{b}}^{2}}\right)^{3}(|f^{TV}_{2}|^{2}+|f^{TA}_{2}|^{2}).

(5)

with
\alpha_{em}
the fine-structure constant of the electromagnetic interaction.

Unfortunately, to calculate the form factors of
f_{2}^{TV,TA}(k^{2})
, the baryon wave functions are required,
which can not be reliably obtained from the first principle due to the nonperturbative effect.
In this work, we use the approach in the LFQM for the baryon wave functions, in which a baryon state
with momentum
P
and spin
(S,S_{z})
is expressed as
Zhang:1994ti
;
Schlumpf:1992vq
;
Geng:2020fng
;
Geng:2021nkl
;
Cheng:2004cc
;
Ke:2019smy
;
Ke:2007tg
;
Ke:2012wa

\displaystyle|B,P,S,S_{z}\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}\int\left\{d^{3}\tilde{p}_{1}\right\}\left\{d^{3}\tilde{p}_{2}\right\}\left\{d^{3}\tilde{p}_{3}\right\}2(2\pi)^{3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{P^{+}}}\delta^{3}\left(\tilde{P}-\tilde{p}_{1}-\tilde{p}_{2}-\tilde{p}_{3}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle\times\Psi^{SS_{z}}\left(\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{p}_{2},\tilde{p}_{3},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\right)C^{\alpha\beta\gamma}F_{abc}|q_{\alpha}^{a}\left(\tilde{p}_{1},\lambda_{1}\right)q_{\beta}^{b}\left(\tilde{p}_{2},\lambda_{2}\right)q_{\gamma}^{c}\left(\tilde{p}_{3},\lambda_{3}\right)\rangle,

where
\Psi
represents the vertex function between the baryon and quarks,
C^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\ (F_{abc})
corresponds to the color (flavor) factor with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\ (a,b,c)
being its indices, and
\tilde{p}_{i}
are the light-front 3-momenta of the
i
-th constituent quark, defined by

p_{i}=(p_{i}^{-},p_{i}^{+},p_{i}^{1},p_{i}^{2})=(p_{i}^{-},\tilde{p}_{i})=(p_{i}^{-},p_{i}^{+},p_{i\perp}),

(7)

with
p_{i}^{\pm}=p_{i}^{0}\pm p_{i}^{3}
and
p_{i}^{-}p_{i}^{+}-p^{2}_{i\perp}=m_{i}^{2}
. Here,
the integration measure and Delta function are given as

\differential^{3}\tilde{p}_{i}\equiv\frac{dp_{i}^{+}d^{2}p_{i\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}},\quad\delta^{3}(\tilde{p})=\delta\left(p^{+}\right)\delta^{2}\left(p_{\perp}\right).

(8)

respectively, along with the normalization

\displaystyle\innerproduct{q_{\alpha^\prime}^{a^\prime}(\tilde{p}_i^\prime, \lambda^\prime)}{q_{\alpha}^{a}(\tilde{p}_i, \lambda)}=2(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}\left(\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{i}-\tilde{p}_{i}\right)\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha}\delta^{a^{\prime}a}\,,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbf{B},P^{\prime},S^{\prime},S_{z}^{\prime}\mid\mathbf{B},P,S,S_{z}\right\rangle=2(2\pi)^{3}P^{+}\delta^{3}(\tilde{P}^{\prime}-\tilde{P})\delta_{S_{z}^{\prime}S_{z}}.

(9)

The vertex function can be further decomposed as

;
Schlumpf:1992vq
;
Geng:1997ws

\Psi^{SS_{z}}\left(\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{p}_{2},\tilde{p}_{3},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\right)=\Phi\left(\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{p}_{2},\tilde{p}_{3}\right)\Xi^{SS_{z}}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\right),

(10)

in which
\Phi
is the momentum distribution function,
\Xi^{SS_{z}}
stands for the helicity wave function given as

\Xi^{SS_{z}}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\right)=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\langle\lambda_{i}|R_{i}^{\dagger}|s_{i}\rangle\innerproduct{\frac{1}{2} s_{1}, \frac{1}{2} s_{2}, \frac{1}{2} s_{3}}{S S_{z}},

(11)

where

R_{i}
is the Melosh matrix, which brings the
i
-th quark from its spin state to a helicity state, and
\innerproduct{\frac{1}{2} s_{1}, \frac{1}{2} s_{2}, \frac{1}{2} s_{3}}{S S_{z}}
is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient, embodied in the spin wave function.

The explicit forms of
\Phi
and
R_{i}
depend on the parametrization scheme of the internal motions of the constituent quarks.
In our calculation, we choose the diquark scheme with the first two quarks being coupled to each other,
while the other coupling schemes can be easily done by the permutations
Geng:2020fng
. The kinematic variables are given as

\displaystyle\tilde{P}

\displaystyle=\tilde{p}_{1}+\tilde{p}_{2}+\tilde{p}_{3},\qquad\xi_{3}=\frac{p_{1}^{+}}{p_{1}^{+}+p_{2}^{+}},\qquad\eta_{3}=1-\frac{p_{3}^{+}}{P^{+}},

(12)

\displaystyle q_{3\perp}

\displaystyle=(1-\xi_{3})p_{1\perp}-\xi_{3}p_{2\perp},\quad Q_{3\perp}=(1-\eta_{3})(p_{1\perp}+p_{2\perp})-\eta_{3}p_{3\perp}.

Note that
(\xi_{3},q_{3\perp})
describe the internal motion within the diquark system,
while
(\eta_{3},Q_{3\perp})
the relative motion between the diquark and the third quark
Bakker:1979eg
.
The invariant masses are then given as

\displaystyle M_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{3\perp}^{2}}{\xi_{3}(1-\xi_{3})}+\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{\xi_{3}}+\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{1-\xi_{3}},

(13)

\displaystyle M^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{3\perp}^{2}}{\eta_{3}(1-\eta_{3})}+\frac{M_{3}^{2}}{\eta_{3}}+\frac{m_{3}^{2}}{1-\eta_{3}}.

In this work, we adopt the Gaussian-type momentum wave function for the ground state baryon
Schlumpf:1992vq
;
Geng:2020fng
;
Geng:2021nkl
. In this particular set of kinematic variables, we have that

\phi_{3}\equiv\Phi(\xi_{3},q_{3\perp},\eta_{3},Q_{3\perp})=\mathcal{N}\sqrt{\frac{\partial q_{3z}}{\partial\xi_{3}}\frac{\partial Q_{3z}}{\partial\eta_{3}}}\exp(-\frac{\vec{Q}_3^2}{2\beta_Q^2}-\frac{\vec{q}_3\!^{2}}{2\beta_{qq'}^2}),

(14)

with
\mathcal{N}=\left(\beta_{qq^{\prime}}\beta_{Q}\pi\right)^{-3/2}
and

\displaystyle q_{3z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi_{3}M_{3}}{2}-\frac{m_{1}^{2}+q_{3\perp}^{2}}{2\xi_{3}M_{3}},\quad\,\vec{q}_{3}\!^{2}=q_{3\perp}^{2}+q_{3z}^{2},

(15)

\displaystyle Q_{3z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta_{3}M}{2}-\frac{m_{3}^{2}+Q_{3\perp}^{2}}{2\eta_{3}M},\quad\vec{Q}_{3}^{2}=Q_{3\perp}^{2}+Q_{3z}^{2},

where
\beta_{qq^{\prime}}
and
\beta_{Q}
are the confinement energy scales within the diquak system and between the diquark and third quark, respectively.
Note that we take the shape parameters as the internal kinematic freedoms to describe the diquark systems
instead of the diquark masses.

If the integration variables
(\vec{q},\vec{Q})
are used instead of
(\xi_{3},q_{3\perp},\eta_{3},Q_{3\perp})
, we obtain that

\int\differential\xi_{3}\differential\eta_{3}\differential^{2}q_{3\perp}\differential^{2}Q_{3\perp}\left|\phi_{3}\right|^{2}=\int\differential^{3}\vec{q}_{3}\differential^{3}\vec{Q}_{3}{\cal N}^{2}\exp(-\frac{\vec{Q}_3^2}{2\beta_Q^2}-\frac{\vec{q}_3\!^{2}}{2\beta_{qq'}^2})=1,

(16)

where the wave functions are clearly Gaussian.
On the other hand, the angular dependencies are embodied in
R_{i}
, given as

\begin{array}[]{l}R_{1}=R_{M}\left(\eta_{3},Q_{3\perp},M_{3},M\right)R_{M}\left(\xi_{3},q_{3\perp},m_{1},M_{3}\right),\\
R_{2}=R_{M}\left(\eta_{3},Q_{3\perp},M_{3},M\right)R_{M}\left(1-\xi_{3},-q_{3\perp},m_{2},M_{3}\right),\\
R_{3}=R_{M}\left(1-\eta_{3},-Q_{3\perp},m_{3},M\right),\end{array}

(17)

with

R_{M}\left(\xi,q_{\perp},m,M\right)=\frac{m+\xi M-i\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{n}\times\vec{q})}{\sqrt{(m+\xi M)^{2}+q_{\perp}^{2}}},

(18)

where
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2},\sigma^{3})
, representing the Pauli matrices, and
\vec{n}=(0,0,1)
. We emphasize that the explicit forms of
R_{i}
depend on the parametrization schemes.

\displaystyle\ket{\Lambda_b}

In our study, since diquark clusters are viewed as effective particles, they are chosen in a way to acquire definite angular momenta. For
\Lambda

\displaystyle\ket{udb}\to\ket{uds},~\ket{dbu}\to\ket{dsu},~\ket{bud}\to\ket{sud},

The equivalence can be understood intuitively in terms of the valence quark framework, in which the spin direction of
\Lambda

H_{+}=0\,,

(29)

where
H_{+(-)}

\Lambda

Without carrying out the numerical detail, Eq. (

\Lambda_{b}

and the definitions of the angles are given in FIG. I , in which

\Lambda_{b}

IV
Conclusions

We have performed a systematic analysis of
{\bf B}_{b}\to{\bf B}_{n}\gamma
based on the LFQM.
We have obtained
{\cal B}(\Lambda_{b}\to\Lambda\gamma)=(7.1\pm 0.3)\times 10^{-6}
,
{\cal B}(\Xi_{b}^{0}\to\Xi^{0}\gamma)=(1.0\pm 0.1)\times 10^{-5}\,,
and
{\cal B}(\Xi_{b}^{-}\to\Xi^{-}\gamma)=(1.1\pm 0.1)\times 10^{-5}
. Our results agree with the current experimental data and are consistent with other theoretical values in the literature.
In addition, for
b\to s\gamma
we have found that our results in the LFQM are in good agreement with those based on
the
SU(3)_{F}
symmetry. Moreover, we have demonstrated that the
SU(3)_{F}
breaking effects for
b\to d\gamma
are as large as
20\%
.

We have also explicitly shown that
f_{2}^{TV}=f_{2}^{TA}
at
k^{2}=0
in the LFQM, resulting in that
P_{L}=-\alpha=-1+{\cal O}(m_{s(d)}^{2}/m_{b}^{2})
for
b\to s(d)\gamma
, which are independent of the theoretical input. A dedicated experimental measurement of the angular distribution of
\Lambda_{b}\to\Lambda(\to p\pi^{-})\gamma

are strongly recommended for testing the SM and probing possible effects from new physics.

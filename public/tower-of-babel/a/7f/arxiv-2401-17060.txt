1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Despite its simplicity, apparently one of the most difficult questions in the theory of invariant subspaces in separable, infinite dimensional complex Hilbert spaces
H
is the problem of the existence of non trivial closed invariant subspaces for a compact perturbation of a selfadjoint operator. Livsic̆ solved this problem for nuclear perturbations, Sahnovic̆ for Hilbert-Schmidt perturbations, and Gohberg and Krein, Macaev, and Schwartz for the perturbation being in the Schatten von Neumann class
\mathcal{C}_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
(see
[
3
]
for more on the subject). In 1992, Lomonosov
[
12
]
proved the existence of real invariant subspaces for compact perturbations of selfadjoint operators, but it is still an open question if every compact perturbation of a selfadjoint operator has a non trivial closed invariant subspace.

The situation is still even hopeless if one considers compact perturbations of a bit broader class of operators, namely
normal operators
. In such a case, if a bounded linear operator
T
acting on
H
is the sum of a normal operator
N
and a compact operator
K
, the results ensuring the existence of non trivial closed invariant subspaces of
T
have been mostly restricted to cases when the spectrum of
N
is contained in a Jordan curve
\gamma
of certain regularity and
K
satisfies additional compactness criteria (like belonging to the Schatten von Neumann class
\mathcal{C}_{p}
) (see the pioneering work
[
10
]
, or the works
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
2
]
as well as the references therein).

In this general framework, in 1975 Pearcy posed the following explicit problem (see
[
13
, Problem K]
)

Suppose
N
is a diagonal normal operator whose eigenvalues constitute a dense subset of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. Show that every operator of the
form
N+F
has a non trivial invariant subspace, where
F
is an operator of rank one.

In 2007, motivated by such a question, Foiaş, Jung, Ko and Pearcy undertook a significant study of what they claimed is
a stubbornly intractable problem
. In particular, they proved in
[
5
]
that there is a large class of rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators having, actually, non trivial closed
hyperinvariant subspaces
, that is, closed subspaces which are invariant under every operator in the commutant of
T
. By recalling that any rank-one operator can be expressed by
u\otimes v
, where
u,v
are non zero vectors in
H
and

u\otimes v(x)=\langle x,v\rangle u,\quad\text{ for }x\in H,

they proved the following:

Theorem

(Foias, Jung, Ko and Pearcy,
[
5
]
)
.

Let
H
be an infinite dimensional, separable, complex Hilbert space and
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and orthonormal basis of
H
. Let
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}
and
v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}e_{n}
be non zero vectors in
H
. Suppose
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is any bounded sequence of the complex plane and
D_{\Lambda}
the diagonal operator with respect to
\mathcal{E}
associated to
\Lambda
, namely,
D_{\Lambda}e_{n}=\lambda_{n}e_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Then, the rank-one perturbation of
D_{\Lambda}

T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v

has non trivial closed invariant subspaces provided that

(1)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2/3}+|\beta_{n}|^{2/3}<\infty.

Moreover,
T
has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces whenever it is not a scalar multiple of the identity.

In 2012, Fang and Xia were able to strengthen the techniques of Foiaş, Jung, Ko and Pearcy also for finite rank perturbations of
D_{\Lambda}
, proving that the exponent “2/3” in (
1
) could be pushed further up to 1. In particular, their result for rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators states the following:

Theorem

(Fang and Xia,
[
4
]
)
.

With the notation as introduced above, the linear bounded operator
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v
has non trivial closed invariant subspaces whenever

(2)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|\alpha_{n}|+|\beta_{n}|\right)<\infty.

Moreover,
T
has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces whenever it is not a scalar multiple of the identity.

.

Recently, the authors have been able to describe spectral subspaces associated to closed sets of the complex plane of finite rank perturbations of diagonal operators and, in particular, of normal operators of multiplicity one whose eigenvectors span
H
. Such a description has allowed them to exhibit proper closed hyperinvariant subspaces
as far as such spectral subspaces are both non zero and non-dense. As a consequence, they have been able to enlarge the class of finite rank perturbations of a diagonalizable normal operator which are known to have non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces. In particular, they proved the following in the case of rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators:

Theorem

(Gallardo-Gutiérrez and González-Doña,
[
8
]
)
.

With the notation as introduced above, the linear bounded operator
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v
has non trivial closed invariant subspaces provided that either

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|<\infty

or

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\beta_{n}|<\infty.

Moreover,
T
has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces whenever it is not a scalar multiple of the identity.

.

By considering the pairs
(p,q)\in(0,2]\times(0,2]
such that the condition

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|\alpha_{n}|^{p}+|\beta_{n}|^{q})<\infty,

implies the existence of non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces of the non-scalar operator
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v
, Foias, Jung, Ko and Pearcy Theorem states that this is the case when
(p,q)\in(0,2/3]\times(0,2/3]
, Fang and Xia Theorem if
(p,q)\in(0,1]\times(0,1]
and the authors’ theorem whenever
(p,q)\in(0,2]\times(0,1]\cup(0,1]\times(0,2]
(see the Figures
1
,
2
and
3
).

The goal of the present manuscript is taking further the previous results and proving, in particular, the following result:

Theorem

(Theorem
2.1
)
.

With the notation as introduced above, the linear bounded operator
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v
has non trivial closed invariant subspaces provided that either
u
or
v
have a Fourier coefficient which is zero or
u
and
v
have non zero Fourier coefficients and

(3)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|\alpha_{n}|}+|\beta_{n}|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|\beta_{n}|}<\infty.

Moreover, if
T
is not a scalar multiple of the identity, it has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces.

It is worthy to remark that in the case that
u
or
v
have a zero Fourier coefficient, Ionascu
[
9
]
proved that either
T
or its adjoint
T^{*}
have an eigenvalue. So the relevant part of the statement is when
u
and
v
have non zero Fourier coefficients. In such a case, the summability condition (
3
) along with authors’ theorem yields, in particular, that the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|\alpha_{n}|^{p}+|\beta_{n}|^{q})

converges for every

(p,q)\in(0,2]\times(0,2]\setminus\{(2,r),(r,2):\;r\in(1,2]\}.

Figure 4
.

Accordingly, as a consequence, if
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v
is non-scalar, it has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces as far as

(p,q)\not\in\{(2,r),(r,2):\;r\in(1,2]\}

(see Figure
4
). Clearly, this enlarges considerably the class of rank-one perturbations of diagonalizable normal operators which are known to have non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces.

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
, we strengthen the methods in
[
8
]
by exhibiting non zero and non-dense spectral subspaces in Section
2
. A significant role in our approach here will be also played by
quasisimilar operators
and Theorem 2.5 in the work
[
6
]
, where the authors proved that the commutants of rank-one perturbations of a diagonalizable normal operators are abelian.

Likewise, in Section
3
we will present an example showing the threshold of our approach which, in particular, is not covering those pairs
(p,q)\in\{(2,r),(r,2):\;r\in(1,2]\}
.

Finally, in Section
4
we deal with finite rank perturbations of diagonal operators proving Theorem
4.1
. Some of the results previously proved for rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators are generalized to this setting, pointing out the main difficulties. This result generalizes Fang and Xia Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
8

and involves the analysis of an
ad-hoc
matrix associated to
T
.

We close this introductory section by recalling some preliminaries and relevant results addressed in the aforementioned works which will be of help to follow the present manuscript.

1.1.
The framework

In what follows,
H
will denote an infinite dimensional separable complex Hilbert space,
\mathcal{L}(H)
the Banach algebra of all bounded linear operators on
H
and
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
an (ordered) orthonormal basis of
H
fixed.

If
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is any bounded sequence in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, the diagonal operator
D_{\Lambda}
with respect to
\mathcal{E}
associated to
\Lambda
is defined by

D_{\Lambda}e_{n}=\lambda_{n}e_{n},\qquad(n\geq 1).

Any rank-one perturbation of
D_{\Lambda}
can be written as

(4)

T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v,

where
u,
and
v
are non zero vectors in
H
. It is important to note that (
4
) is not unique as far as rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators concern but, if
u
and
v
have non zero components for every
n\geq 1
, uniqueness in (
4
) follows (see
[
9
, Proposition 1.1]
).

Note that rank-one perturbations of normal operators whose eigenvectors span
H
are unitarily equivalent to those expressed by (
4
), and consequently, it suffices to study them from the standpoint of invariant subspaces.

1.2.
(\mathcal{RO})
operators

In
[
5
]
, the authors introduced the class of operators
(\mathcal{RO})
as follows:

Definition (Class (
\mathcal{RO}
))
.

Let
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded sequence and
D_{\Lambda}
the diagonal operator associated to
\Lambda
with respect to
\mathcal{E}
. The rank-one perturbation of
D_{\Lambda}

T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v,

where
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}
and
v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
, belongs to the class
(\mathcal{RO})
if:

(i)

\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}\neq 0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(ii)

the map
n\in\mathbb{N}\mapsto\lambda_{n}\in\Lambda
is injective;

(iii)

the derived set
\Lambda^{\prime}
is not a singleton.

In particular, they proved that rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators not belonging to
(\mathcal{RO})
have always non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces as far as they are non-scalar (see
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
).

1.2.1.
Spectrum of
(\mathcal{RO})
operators

Both the spectrum
\sigma(T)
and the point spectrum
\sigma_{p}(T)
of operators
T
belonging to the class
(\mathcal{RO})
were characterized by Ionascu
[
9
]
in terms of Borel series. More precisely, let
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v\in(\mathcal{RO})
, where
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathbb{C}
,
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}
and
v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
. Denote by
f_{T}
the Borel series associated to
T
, namely

(5)

f_{T}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n}\overline{\beta_{n}}}{\lambda_{n}-z},

for those
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that the series converges. Clearly,
f_{T}
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Lambda}
and next result states that, in some sense, it encodes the point spectrum of
T
.

Theorem

(Ionascu
[
9
]
)
.

With the introduced notation,
z\in\mathbb{C}
belongs to
\sigma_{p}(T)
if and only if

(i)

z\not\in\Lambda
,

(ii)

u\in\textnormal{ran}(D_{\Lambda}-zI),
or equivalently

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|z-\lambda_{n}|^{2}}<\infty,

(iii)

f_{T}(z)+1=0.

Moreover, the spectrum of
T
is given by

(6)

\sigma(T)=\Lambda^{\prime}\cup\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Lambda}:f_{T}(z)+1=0\right\}.

1.3.
Spectral subspaces of
(\mathcal{RO})
operators

In order to prove both Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
4.1
, we will exhibit non zero and non-dense spectral subspaces associated to certain closed sets in
\mathbb{C}
. Spectral subspaces arise, in a natural way, scanning the possible decompositions of the spectrum of an operator and play a role in the
Local Spectral Theory
. For the sake of completeness, we collect some of the basic tools and refer to the monograph
[
11
]
for more on the subject.

Recall that an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
has the
single-valued extension property
(SVEP) if for every connected open set
G\subset\mathbb{C}
and every analytic function
f:G\to X
such that
(T-\lambda I)f(\lambda)\equiv 0
on
G
, one has
f\equiv 0
on
G
. Note that every operator
T
such that
\sigma_{p}(T)
has empty interior has the SVEP.

If
T
has the SVEP and
x\in H
, the
local resolvent

\rho_{T}(x)
of
T
at
x
is the union of all open sets
U\subset\mathbb{C}
such that there exists a vector-valued holomorphic function
f_{x}:U\rightarrow H
such that

(7)

(T-zI)f_{x}(z)=x\quad(z\in U).

The
local spectrum

\sigma_{T}(x)
at
x
is defined as the complement of
\rho_{T}(x)
:

\sigma_{T}(x)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho_{T}(x).

Among many other properties, the local spectrum satisfies
\sigma_{T}(x)\subseteq\sigma(T)
for every
x\in X
and
\sigma_{T}(0)=\emptyset
.

For every subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
,
the local spectral subspace of
T
associated with
\Omega
is

H_{T}(\Omega)=\{x\in H:\;\sigma_{T}(x)\subseteq\Omega\}.

If
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
then
H_{T}(\Omega_{1})\subset H_{T}(\Omega_{2})
and
H_{T}(\Omega)=H_{T}(\Omega\cap\sigma(T))
.
It is important to note that
H_{T}(\Omega)
is a linear manifold that is hyperinvariant for
T
but not necessarily closed even for closed subsets (indeed, even in the case that
T
has the SVEP, see
[
1
, Chapter 2]
for instance). The following proposition deals with spectral subspaces associated to closed sets (see
[
11
, Proposition 2.5.1]
):

Proposition
.

Assume
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
and both
T
and its adjoint
T^{*}
have the SVEP. Let
F_{1},F_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}
be disjoint closed sets. Then

(1)

H_{T}(F_{1}\cup F_{2})=H_{T}(F_{1})\oplus H_{T}(F_{2})
holds as an algebraic sum.

(2)

H_{T}(F_{1})\subseteq H_{T^{*}}(F_{2}^{*})^{\perp},
where
F_{2}^{*}=\{\overline{z}:\;z\in F_{2}\}
.

Note that the difference of the statement (2) and Proposition 2.5.1 in
[
11
]
is due to the consideration of the Hilbert adjoint operator
T^{*}
. As a consequence, we have the following

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be such that both
T
and
T^{*}
have the SVEP. Suppose
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are disjoint closed sets in
\mathbb{C}
such that
H_{T}(F_{1})
and
H_{T^{*}}(F_{2}^{*})
are non zero linear manifolds. Then
\overline{H_{T}(F_{1})}
is a non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspace for
T
.

We end this section by stating a result which ensures the existence of non zero vectors belonging to some particular spectral subspaces of operators in the class
(\mathcal{RO})
.

Theorem

(Theorem 3.2,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded sequence,
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}
,
v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}e_{n}
non zero vectors in
H
and
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v
a bounded linear operator in the class
(\mathcal{RO})
. Assume
\sigma(T)
is connected and both
\sigma_{p}(T)
and
\sigma_{p}(T^{*})
are empty. Suppose further that there exists a closed, simple, piecewise differentiable curve
\gamma
in
\mathbb{C}
not intersecting
\Lambda
such that

(i)

\sigma(T)\cap\textnormal{int}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

The map

\xi\in\gamma\mapsto\frac{1}{1+f_{T}(\xi)}

is well defined and continuous on
\gamma.

(iii)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\gamma}\frac{|d\xi|}{|\lambda_{n}-\xi|}\right)^{2}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}<\infty.

Then, the spectral subspace
H_{T}(\overline{\textnormal{int}(\gamma)})
is non zero.

In condition (iii),
|d\xi|
denotes the arc length measure.

2.
Rank-one perturbations of diagonalizable normal operators

As pointed in the Introduction, the aim of this section is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
be any rank-one perturbation of a diagonal normal operator
D_{\Lambda}
with respect to an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
where
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}
and
v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}e_{n}
. Then
T
has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces provided that either
u
or
v
have a Fourier coefficient which is zero or
u
and
v
have non zero Fourier coefficients and

(8)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|\alpha_{n}|}+|\beta_{n}|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|\beta_{n}|}<\infty.

Moreover, if
T\in(\mathcal{RO})
,
\sigma(T)
is connected and
\sigma_{p}(T)\cup\sigma_{p}(T^{*})=\emptyset
, it follows that
T
does have non zero spectral subspaces which are not dense.

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
, we require some preliminary work. First, we consider the following lemma which generalizes Lemma 2.1 in
[
4
]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}
be any bounded sequence of distinct complex numbers and
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
a sequence such that

(9)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\,\log\frac{1}{|\alpha_{n}|}<\infty.

Then, for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n}):n\in\mathbb{N}\}

(10)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-x|}<\infty.

Though the proof is inspired by that of
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
, the conclusion is strengthened.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the clousure
\overline{\Lambda}
is contained in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. Accordingly, the series in (
10
) converges for every
|x|>1.
Denote by
\Theta
the set of
x\in[-1,1]\setminus\{\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n}):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-x|}

diverges. We will show that the Lebesgue measure of
\Theta
is zero.

Fix
0<\varepsilon<1
and let
\delta>0
be so that

2\delta\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}<\varepsilon.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the intervals

I_{n}=[\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-\delta|\alpha_{n}|^{2},\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})+\delta|\alpha_{n}|^{2}]\cap[-1,1]

and the non-negative functions

f_{n}(x):=\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-x|}\chi_{[-1,1]\setminus I_{n}}(x),\qquad(x\in[-1,1]),

where
\chi_{[-1,1]\setminus I_{n}}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
[-1,1]\setminus I_{n}
.

In order to estimate
\int_{[-1,1]}f_{n}(x)\,dx
, we observe that
for any
0<a<1
and
b<a
,

\int_{a<x<1}\frac{1}{x-b}\,dx=\log(1-b)+\log\frac{1}{a-b}.

This along with a bit of computation yields that

(11)

\begin{split}\int_{[-1,1]}f_{n}(x)\,dx&\leq|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\left(2\,\log\frac{1}{\delta|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}+\log\left|1-\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})^{2}\right|\right)\\
\\
&\leq M|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\Big(1+\log\frac{1}{|\alpha_{n}|}\Big)\end{split}

for some constant
M>0
(here we have used that
\overline{\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{D}
).

Now, define the function

F(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x),\qquad(x\in[-1,1]).

Upon applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem along with (
11
) and (
9
) we deduce that the integral

\int_{[-1,1]}F(x)dx

is finite. Accordingly
F(x)
is finite for almost every
x\in[-1,1].

If
\Omega=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{n}
, we observe that its Lebesgue measure satisfies

m(\Omega)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m(I_{n})\leq 2\delta\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}<\varepsilon.

Note that if
x\in[-1,1]\setminus\Omega,
then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-x|}=F(x),

so
\Theta\subset\Omega\cup\{x\in[-1,1]:F(x)=\infty\}
. Since
m(\{x\in[-1,1]:F(x)=\infty\})=0
, we deduce that
m(\Theta)<\varepsilon
for every arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Thus
m(\Theta)=0,
and the proof is finished.
∎

Likewise, we will make use of Lemma 4.3 in

\textnormal{int}(\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+})

\sigma(T)

To show condition (ii) of

The proof runs mostly as in

The obstruction of Theorem
2.1

A natural question which arises having Corollary
2.8
at hands is if it is always possible to find
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
with
a<x_{1}<x_{2}<b
such that the summability condition (
20
) holds. Observe that if this is the case, due to the discussed reductions, every rank-one perturbation of a diagonalizable normal operator would have non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces.

Next, we provide an example showing that such a set could be empty. In particular, we exhibit a bounded sequence of real numbers
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
dense in
[-1,1]
and a non zero vector
u
in the classical
\ell^{2}
space with Fourier coefficients
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
with respect to the canonical basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|r_{n}-x|}=\infty

for every
x\in(-1,1)
.

Note that, in such a case, if
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is any bounded sequence in
\mathbb{C}
such that
\textnormal{Re}(\Lambda)
contains
\{r_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
D_{\Lambda}
is the corresponding diagonal operator with respect to
\mathcal{E}
acting on
\ell^{2}
then any rank-one perturbation
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})
, with
v\in\ell^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, no longer meets the hypotheses of Corollary
2.8
. Roughly speaking, this example shows the blue obstruction of the discussed approach in order to provide non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces.

Likewise, observe that by taking
\Lambda=\{r_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
u,v\in\ell^{2}\setminus\{0\}
such that their Fourier coefficients
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfy

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\alpha_{n}\,\overline{\beta_{n}}}{r_{n}-x}\right|=\infty

for every
x\in(-1,1)
, the operators
T=D_{\Lambda}+u\otimes v\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})
are examples of rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators such that their Borel series
f_{T}
are not absolutely convergent at any point in
\sigma(T)
. Nevertheless, a theorem of Radjabalipour and Radjavi
[
16
]
yields that such
T
’s are indeed decomposable, and hence, have a rich lattice of invariant subspaces. Accordingly, these examples clearly show an edge in order to apply previous approaches to produce invariant subspaces since they rely on the finiteness of associated Borel series to the operator.

S_{m}
be

S_{m}=\{2^{m}+k:0\leq k\leq 2^{m}-1\}.

It is clear that
\mathbb{N}=\bigsqcup_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}.
Now, define the sequence
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
recursively taking into account the defined partition as follows: for each
n\in S_{m}
, if
n=2^{m}+k
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0\leq k\leq 2^{m}-1

r_{n}=r_{2^{m}+k}:=-1+\frac{1}{2^{m}}+\frac{k}{2^{m-1}}.

Observe that
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a well defined sequence contained in
(-1,1)
with particular values:

r_{1}=0;\;r_{2}=-\frac{1}{2};\;r_{3}=\frac{1}{2};\;r_{4}=-\frac{3}{4};\;r_{5}=-\frac{1}{4};\;r_{6}=\frac{1}{4};\;r_{7}=\frac{3}{4};\;\cdots

Likewise,
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in
[-1,1].
Let
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{C}
be a sequence such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}<\infty\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\,2^{n}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}=\infty.

This can be accomplished by taking, for instance,
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}=0
and
\gamma_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n/2}\sqrt{n}\log(n)}
for every
n\geq 2.

Now, let us consider the vector
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}
defined recursively as follows: for each
n\in S_{m}
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let

\alpha_{n}=\gamma_{m}.

Note that for each
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, by definition,
\alpha_{n}
takes the same value for every
n\in S_{m}
. Observe that
u\in\ell^{2}
since

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{m}-1}|\gamma_{m}|^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{m}|\gamma_{m}|^{2}<\infty.

Now, we show that

\varphi(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|r_{n}-x|}=\infty

for every
x\in(-1,1).

Let
-1<x\leq 0
; the case
0<x<1
is analogous (indeed,
\varphi(-x)=\varphi(x)
). There exists a positive integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
n\geq N

-1+\frac{1}{2^{n}}<x.

For each
n\geq N
, there exists a non-negative integer
k_{0}(n)
depending on
n
, with
1<k_{0}(n)\leq 2^{n-1}
, such that

-1+\frac{1}{2^{n}}+\frac{k_{0}(n)-1}{2^{n-1}}\leq x<-1+\frac{1}{2^{n}}+\frac{k_{0}(n)}{2^{n-1}}

or equivalently

r_{2^{n}+k_{0}(n)-1}\leq x<r_{2^{n}+k_{0}(n)}.

Note that for each
k=k_{0}(n),\,k_{0}(n)+1,\,k_{0}(n)+2,\cdots,k_{0}(n)+2^{n-1}-1
,

\frac{k_{0}(n)-1-k}{2^{n-1}}\leq x-r_{2^{n}+k}<\frac{k_{0}(n)-k}{2^{n-1}},

so

|x-r_{2^{n}+k}|\leq\frac{k-k_{0}(n)+1}{2^{n-1}}.

Then

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{|r_{n}-x|}&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{m}-1}\frac{|\gamma_{m}|^{2}}{|x-r_{2^{m}+k}|}\\
&\geq\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}\,\sum_{k=k_{0(m)}}^{k_{0}(m)+2^{m-1}-1}\frac{|\gamma_{m}|^{2}}{k-k_{0}(m)+1}2^{m-1}\\
&=\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}|\gamma_{m}|^{2}\,2^{m-1}\,\sum_{k=1}^{2^{m-1}-1}\frac{1}{k}\\
&>\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}|\gamma_{m}|^{2}\,2^{m-1}\log(2^{m-1})\\
&=\log(2)\sum_{m=M}^{\infty}|\gamma_{m}|^{2}\,2^{m-1}\cdot(m-1),\end{split}

which diverges because of the choice of the sequence
\{\gamma_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
. This concludes the example.

4.
Finite rank perturbations of diagonalizable normal operators

In this section, we consider finite rank perturbations of diagonal operators, namely,

T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H),

where, as before,
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{C}
is a bounded sequence of complex numbers,
D_{\Lambda}
is a diagonal operator with respect to an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
with eigenvalues
\Lambda
and
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
are non zero vectors in
H
for
1\leq k\leq N,
where
N\geq 2
is fixed. We will denote the Fourier coefficients of the vectors
u_{k},v_{k}
with respect to
\mathcal{E}
as

u_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n},\quad v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}\qquad(1\leq k\leq N).

Our main goal in this section is generalizing Theorem
2.1
in this setting proving the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
be any finite rank perturbation of a diagonal normal operator
D_{\Lambda}
with respect to an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
where
u_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
and
v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
. Then
T
has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces provided that for each
1\leq k\leq N

(21)

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{k}}}\left|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}\right|^{2}\log\frac{1}{\left|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}\right|}+\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{v_{k}}}\left|\beta_{n}^{(k)}\right|^{2}\log\frac{1}{\left|\beta_{n}^{(k)}\right|}<\infty,

where

(22)

\mathcal{N}_{u_{k}}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\;\alpha_{n}^{(k)}\neq 0\},\quad\mathcal{N}_{v_{k}}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\;\beta_{n}^{(k)}\neq 0\}.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
consists of an appropriate generalization of the approach carried over for rank-one perturbations of diagonal operators. Nevertheless, there are some technical issues which are not straightforward and we will mainly address them instead of repeating again some of the ideas.

We start by generalizing the class
(\mathcal{RO})
in the setting of finite rank perturbations of diagonal operators:

Definition 4.2
.

With the notation as introduced above, the operator
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
belongs to the class
(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
if:

(i)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
1\leq k_{1},k_{2}\leq N
such that
\alpha_{n}^{(k_{1})}\beta_{n}^{(k_{2})}\neq 0.

(ii)

Each
\lambda\in\Lambda
has multiplicity less or equal than
N
.

(iii)

The derive set
\Lambda^{\prime}
is not reduced to a singleton.

As it was shown in
[
8
, Proposition 6.1]
, if
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
with
u_{k},\,v_{k}\in H
for
1\leq k\leq N
does not belong to the corresponding class
(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
, then it has non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspaces. Accordingly, from the standpoint of view of studying invariant subspaces, we restrict ourselves to this particular subclass of operators.

We begin with an appropriate generalization of Borel series associated to finite rank perturbations of diagonal operators.

Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
be an operator in
(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
, where
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathbb{C}
. For each
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, let us define

f_{T}^{(i,j)}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n}^{(i)}\overline{\beta_{n}^{(j)}}}{\lambda_{n}-z}

for those complex numbers
z
such that the series converges. Clearly, each
f_{T}^{(i,j)}
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Lambda}
. Let
M_{T}
be the
N\times N
matrix function

M_{T}(z)=\left(\begin{matrix}f_{T}^{(1,1)}(z)+1&f_{T}^{(1,2)}(z)&\cdots&f_{T}^{(1,N)}(z)\\
f_{T}^{(2,1)}(z)&f_{T}^{(2,2)}(z)+1&\cdots&f_{T}^{(2,N)}(z)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
f_{T}^{(N,1)}(z)&f_{T}^{(N,2)}(z)&\cdots&f_{T}^{(N,N)}(z)+1\end{matrix}\right)

for those
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
f_{T}^{(i,j)}(z)
is defined for every
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
. This matrix function plays a significant role regarding the existence
of eigenvalues of the operator
T
:

Lemma 4.3
.

(
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
) Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
be a finite rank perturbation of a diagonal normal operator
D_{\Lambda}
with respect to an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
where
u_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
and
v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
for
1\leq k\leq N
. Assume further that
T\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(i)

z\notin\Lambda.

(ii)

For every
1\leq k\leq N
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}\right|^{2}}{|\lambda_{n}-z|^{2}}<\infty.

(iii)

\ker(T-zI)=\{0\}.

Then
M_{T}(z)
is invertible.

We stress here that
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
also asks that the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\beta_{n}^{(k)}\right|^{2}}{|\lambda_{n}-z|^{2}}<\infty,

converge for every
1\leq k\leq N
, but a careful reading of the proof shows that this condition is no longer necessary to yield the conclusion.

Next result, which we borrow from
[
8
]
, allows us to exhibit non zero spectral subspaces of operators
T\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}.

Theorem 4.4
.

(
[
8
, Theorem 6.8]
)
Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
with
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
and
v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
non zero vectors in
H
for each
1\leq k\leq N.
Assume
\sigma_{p}(T)\cup\sigma_{p}(T^{*})=\emptyset
and
\sigma(T)
is connected. Suppose, further, that there exists a closed, simple, piecewise differentiable curve
\gamma
in
\mathbb{C}
not intersecting
\Lambda
such that

(a)

\sigma(T)\cap\textnormal{int}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
and
\textnormal{int}(\gamma)\cap\rho(T)\neq\emptyset.

(b)

The maps

\xi\in\gamma\mapsto f_{T}^{(i,j)}(\xi),\quad(1\leq i,j\leq N)

and

\xi\in\gamma\mapsto\frac{1}{\det(M_{T}(\xi))}

are well defined and continuous on
\gamma.

(c)

The series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\gamma}\frac{|d\xi|}{|\lambda_{n}-\xi|}\right)^{2}|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}|^{2}<\infty,

converge for every
1\leq k\leq N.

Then,
H_{T}(\overline{\textnormal{int}(\gamma)})
is a non zero spectral subspace.

Next definition is the generalization of Definition
2.4
for operators
T\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
:

Definition 4.5
.

Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
be a finite rank perturbation of a diagonal normal operator
D_{\Lambda}
with respect to an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, where
u_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
and
v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
for
1\leq k\leq N
. Let

a=\min\limits_{\lambda\in\sigma(T)}\textnormal{Re}(\lambda),\quad\text{ and }\quad b=\max\limits_{\lambda\in\sigma(T)}\textnormal{Re}(\lambda).

The
relevant set
of
T
consists of

\Omega_{N}(T)=\Big\{x\in\textnormal{Re}(\sigma(T))\cap(a,b):\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}\right|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-x|}+\frac{\left|\beta_{n}^{(k)}\right|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-x|}<\infty,\quad\textnormal{for every}\ 1\leq k\leq N\Big\}.

Note that if
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
have non zero Fourier coefficients for every
1\leq k\leq N
, the index sets in (
22
) coincides with
\mathbb{N}
. Hence, if (
21
) holds for every
1\leq k\leq N
, Lemma
2.2
yields that almost every point in
(a,b)
belongs to
\Omega_{N}(T).

On the other hand, if
u_{k}
(or
v_{k}
) has a Fourier coefficient which is zero for some
1\leq k\leq N
, say
\alpha_{n_{0}}^{(k)}
for instance, though the index set
\mathcal{N}_{u_{k}}\subsetneq\mathbb{N}
, the conclusion of Lemma
2.2
clearly holds if (
9
) is replaced by

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{k}}}|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}|^{2}\,\log\frac{1}{\left|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}\right|}<\infty.

Accordingly, in such a case, under the hypotheses (
21
), it also follows that almost every point in
(a,b)
belongs to
\Omega_{N}(T).

Thus, in both cases, if
T\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
and
\sigma(T)
is a connected set not contained in any vertical line in the complex plane then
\Omega_{N}(T)
contains infinitely many points.

Next result generalizes Theorem
2.5
to this context:

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(H)\setminus\mathbb{C}\,Id_{H}
be a finite rank perturbation of a diagonal normal operator
D_{\Lambda}
with respect to an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, where
u_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
and
v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
for
1\leq k\leq N
. Assume
T\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
,
\sigma(T)
is a connected set not contained in any vertical line in
\mathbb{C}
and
\sigma_{p}(T)\cup\sigma_{p}(T^{*})=\emptyset
. For
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)
let
F_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}
and
F_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{-}
denote the sets

F_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\sigma(T):\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\geq\textnormal{{x}}\},

F_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{-}=\{\lambda\in\sigma(T):\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\textnormal{{x}}\}.

If
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)
satisfies

(23)

\det(M_{T}(\textnormal{{x}}+iy))\neq 0\quad\textnormal{for every}\ \textnormal{{x}}+iy\in\sigma(T),

then
H_{T}(F_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+})
and
H_{T}(F_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{-})
are non zero spectral subspaces.

The proof follows the lines of Theorem
2.5
and hence, we focus on those details that differs from it.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\overline{\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{D}_{+}
and prove that
H_{T}(F^{+})
is non zero (the argument for
H_{T}(F^{-})
is analogous).

Taking
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)
satisfying (
23
) and arguing as is Theorem (
2.5
), it suffices to show that
H_{T}(\overline{\textnormal{int}(\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+})})
is non zero by checking that conditions (a),(b) and (c) of Theorem
4.4
are fulfilled.
Conditions (a) and (c) follows as in Theorem
2.5
, so we are left to prove that condition (b) also holds.

First, let us show that the maps

\xi\in\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}\mapsto f_{T}^{(i,j)}(\xi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n}^{(i)}\overline{\beta_{n}^{(j)}}}{\lambda_{n}-\xi}

are well defined and are continuous for
1\leq i,j\leq N.
Since these functions are holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Lambda},
it suffices to prove that they are well defined and are continuous in
\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}\cap\sigma(T)
.

Note that for every
\xi=\textnormal{{x}}+iy\in\sigma(T)

|f_{T}^{(i,j)}(\xi)|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\alpha_{n}^{(i)}\overline{\beta_{n}^{(j)}}}{\lambda_{n}-\xi}\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\alpha_{n}^{(i)}\overline{\beta_{n}^{(j)}}}{\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-\textnormal{{x}}}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\alpha_{n}^{(i)}\right|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-\textnormal{{x}}|}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\beta^{(j)}_{n}\right|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-\textnormal{{x}}|}\right)^{1/2}\hskip-5.69046pt<\infty

since
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)
. This shows that each
f_{T}^{(i,j)}
is well defined and is continuous on
\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}
.

In order to prove that the map

\xi\in\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}\mapsto\frac{1}{\det(M_{T}(\xi))}

is well defined and is continuous, observe that
\xi\in\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}\mapsto\det(M_{T}(\xi))
is well defined and it is continuous because each
f_{T}^{(i,j)}
is. Likewise,
\det(M_{T}(\xi))\neq 0
for every
\xi\in\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}
. Indeed, this latter fact follows for those
\xi\in\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}\cap\sigma(T)
by hypothesis (equation (
23
)), while for those
\xi\in\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+}\cap\rho(T)
from Lemma
4.3
, since
\sigma_{p}(T)
is empty and the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}|^{2}}{|\lambda_{n}-z|^{2}}

is convergent for each
1\leq k\leq N
and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Lambda}
. Accordingly, condition (b) in Theorem
4.4
is fulfilled and hence
H_{T}(\overline{\textnormal{int}(\gamma_{\textnormal{{x}}}^{+})})
is non zero as wished to prove.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
, where
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
and
v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
for each
1\leq k\leq N
. Assume that there exist
\textnormal{{x}}_{1}
and
\textnormal{{x}}_{2}
in the relevant set
\Omega(T)
such that
\textnormal{{x}}_{1}>\textnormal{{x}}_{2}
and

\det(M_{T}(\textnormal{{x}}_{1}+iy))\neq 0\quad\textnormal{for every}\ \textnormal{{x}}_{1}+iy\in\sigma(T),

\det(M_{T}(\textnormal{{x}}_{2}+iy))\neq 0\quad\textnormal{for every}\ \textnormal{{x}}_{2}+iy\in\sigma(T).

Then,
T
has a non trivial closed hyperinvariant subspace.

The proof of Theorem
4.7
follows the lines of that of Theorem
2.6
having in mind that for any
\textnormal{{x}}_{2}+iy\in\sigma(T^{*})

\det(M_{T}^{*}(\textnormal{{x}}_{2}+iy))=\det(M_{T}(\textnormal{{x}}_{2}-iy))

where
\textnormal{{x}}_{2}-iy\in\sigma(T)
.

The last ingredient in order to prove Theorem
4.1
is a result in the spirit of

[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
regarding quasisimilar operators in the class
(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
T=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k}\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
, where
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
and
v_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{(k)}e_{n}
are non zero vectors in
H
for each
1\leq k\leq N
. Suppose that
0\in\Lambda^{\prime}\setminus(\Lambda\cup\sigma_{p}(T)\cup\sigma_{p}(T^{*}))
and let
D_{\Lambda}^{1/2}
be any fixed square root of the normal operator
D_{\Lambda}.
Assume that
u_{k}
belongs to the range of
D_{\Lambda}^{1/2}
for every
1\leq k\leq N.
Then,
T
and
\tilde{T}=D_{\Lambda}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(D_{\Lambda}^{-1/2}u_{k})\otimes(D_{\Lambda}^{1/2})^{*}v_{k}
are quasisimilar.

The proof is similar to the one of
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
and is included for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

By hypotheses, both operators
D_{\Lambda}^{1/2}
and
T
are quasiaffinities, namely,

\ker(D_{\Lambda}^{1/2})=\ker((D_{\Lambda}^{1/2})^{*})=\ker(T)=\ker(T^{*})=\{0\}.

Likewise,
U:=D_{\Lambda}^{-1/2}T=D_{\Lambda}^{1/2}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(D_{\Lambda}^{-1/2}u_{k})\otimes v_{k}
is a bounded operator being a quasiaffinity.

Observe that

T=D_{\Lambda}^{1/2}U,\quad\tilde{T}=UD_{\Lambda}^{1/2},

so

TD_{\Lambda}^{1/2}=D_{\Lambda}^{1/2}UD_{\Lambda}^{1/2}=D_{\Lambda}^{1/2}\tilde{T}

and

UT=UD^{1/2}U=\tilde{T}U.

Consequently,
T
and
\tilde{T}
are quasisimilar operators and the statement follows.
∎

Finally we are in position to prove Theorem
4.1
as a byproduct of theorems
4.7
and
4.8
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
T\in(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
,
\sigma(T)
is a connected set not lying in any vertical line of
\mathbb{C}
and
\sigma_{p}(T)\cup\sigma_{p}(T^{*})=\emptyset
. Then (
21
) along with lemmas
2.3
and
2.2
yield that the relevant set
\Omega_{N}(T)
contains infinitely many points.

Let
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)
be fixed and let us show that
\det(M_{T}(\textnormal{{x}}+iy))\neq 0
for every
\textnormal{{x}}+iy\in\sigma(T)
following some of the ideas in the proof of the Theorem
2.1
.

Assume, by contradiction, that there exists
\xi=\textnormal{{x}}+iy\in\sigma(T)
such that
\det(M_{T}(\xi))=0
and consider the operator

T-\xi I=(D_{\Lambda}-\xi I)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes v_{k},

which belongs to
(\mathcal{RO})_{N}
. As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we may assume that no eigenvalues of
D_{\Lambda}-\xi I
lies on
(-\infty,0]
. In particular, denoting
\sqrt{\quad}
the principal value of the square root, we fix
D_{\sqrt{\Lambda-\xi}}
as a square root of
D_{\lambda}-\xi I
, denoted by
(D_{\Lambda}-\xi I)^{1/2}
.
Since
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}\right|^{2}}{|\lambda_{n}-\xi|}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n}^{(k)}|^{2}}{|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda_{n})-\textnormal{{x}}|}<\infty,\qquad(1\leq k\leq N),

so the vectors
u_{k}
belongs to the range of
(D_{\Lambda}-\xi I)^{1/2}
for
1\leq k\leq N.
Upon applying Theorem
4.8
it follows that
T-\xi I
and

S:=(D_{\Lambda}-\xi I)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(D_{\Lambda}-\xi I)^{-1/2}u_{k}\otimes(D_{\Lambda}^{*}-\overline{\xi}I)^{1/2}v_{k}

are quasisimilar operators. Then,
T^{*}-\overline{\xi}I
and
S^{*}=D_{\Lambda}^{*}-\overline{\xi}I+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(D_{\Lambda}^{*}-\overline{\xi}I)^{1/2}v_{k}\otimes(D_{\Lambda}-\xi I)^{-1/2}u_{k}
are also quasisimilar what implies, in particular, that
\sigma_{p}(S^{*})=\emptyset
.

On the other hand, since
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)
the vectors
(D_{\Lambda}^{*}-\overline{\xi}I)^{1/2}v_{k}
belong to the range of
(D_{\Lambda}^{*}-\overline{\xi}I)
and hence, by Lemma
4.3
, the determinant
\det(M_{S^{*}}(0))\neq 0
. Note that for any
1\leq i,j\leq N

\begin{split}f_{S^{*}}^{(i,j)}(0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\overline{\lambda_{n}-\xi})^{1/2}\beta_{n}^{(i)}(\overline{\lambda_{n}-\xi})^{-1/2}\overline{\alpha_{n}^{(j)}}}{\overline{\lambda_{n}}-\overline{\xi}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{n}^{(i)}\overline{\alpha_{n}^{(j)}}}{\overline{\lambda_{n}}-\overline{\xi}}=\overline{f_{T}^{(j,i)}(\xi)},\end{split}

so
M_{S^{*}}(0)=M_{T}^{*}(\xi)
. By assumption,
\det(M_{T}(\xi))=0
so
\det(M_{S^{*}}(0))=0
as well, what yields a contradiction.

Accordingly,
\det(M_{T}(\textnormal{{x}}+iy))\neq 0
for every
\textnormal{{x}}+iy\in\sigma(T)
with
\textnormal{{x}}\in\Omega_{N}(T)
. Upon applying Theorem
4.7
the proof of Theorem
4.1
follows.

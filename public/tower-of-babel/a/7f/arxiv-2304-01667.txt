The lifting property for C ∗ C^{*} -algebras : from local to global ?
by

Abstract
This note is motivated by Kirchberg’s conjecture that the local lifting property (LLP) implies the lifting property (LP) for separable C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. The author recently constructed by a “local” method an example of C ∗ C^{*} -algebra with the LLP and the weak expectation property (WEP) which might be a counterexample. We give here several conditions all equivalent to the validity of the implication LLP ⇒ \Rightarrow LP for WEP C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, or equivalently for quotients of WEP C ∗ C^{*} -algebras (QWEP), that hopefully clarify the nature of the problem. But unfortunately we cannot decide whether they hold true. These conditions highlight the notion of “controllable” finite dimensional (f.d.) operator space, in connection with certain C ∗ C^{*} -tensor products. The latter led us to a closely related variant of local reflexivity.

Let
A,C
be
C^{*}
-algebras. Let
I\subset C
be a closed self-adjoint two-sided ideal so that the quotient
C/I
is a
C^{*}
-algebra.
Assuming
A
unital, let
S\subset A
be an operator system, meaning a self-adjoint subspace
containing the unit.
Consider a map
u:S\to C/I
.
We say that
u
is liftable if there is a unital completely positive
(u.c.p. in short) map

\hat{u}:S\to C
lifting
u
. We say that
u
is locally liftable
if for any finite dimensional (f.d. in short) operator system
E\subset A

the restriction
u_{|E}
is liftable.

In
[
13
]
Kirchberg defined the lifting property (LP in short) and the local lifting property (LLP in short) for a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
as follows:

The algebra
A
has the LP (resp. LLP) if any u.c.p.
map
u:A\to C/I
into an arbitrary quotient is liftable (resp. locally liftable).

When
A
is not unital,
A
has the LP (resp. LLP) if its unitization does.

In some sense
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
is the fundamental example of LP
for
C^{*}
-algebras. It plays an analogous role to
that of
\ell_{1}
-spaces in Banach space theory.

In the remarkable last section of his outstanding paper
[
13
]
, Kirchberg
formulated seven equivalent conjectures (labelled as B1,
\cdots
, B7) all equivalent
to the validity of the Connes embedding problem (labelled as B1)
which asks whether any finite von Neumann algebra
on
\ell_{2}
embeds in an ultraproduct of matrix algebras.
One of these conjectures (namely B3) states that there is a unique
C^{*}
-norm on
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})\otimes C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
where
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
denotes the free group
with infinitely countably many generators.
In the paper

[
11
]
(see also
[
27
]
) a negative solution is proposed, based on the equivalence
of B3 with an equivalent conjecture of Tsirelson developed in quantum information theory. See
[
20
]
for a detailed account of the equivalence of these various conjectures.

In
[
13
]
Kirchberg proved that his (equivalent) B-conjectures
imply his conjecture D which states that for separable
C^{*}
-algebras
the LLP implies the LP (in other words they are equivalent).
The latter conjecture is the motivation for the present paper.
Although we do not reach a conclusion, we hope that our results
may advance the situation.
The difficulty is that,
apart from the nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras, very
few examples of LLP
C^{*}
-algebras are known except for
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})

and algebras derived from it.
However, we recently constructed
in
[
21
]
a new type of non-nuclear LLP
C^{*}
-algebras,
for which it does not seem clear at all that they have the LP;
the failure of all our attempts to either prove or disprove that is what led to the present paper.
The special feature of the latter examples is that they have Lance’s weak expectation property (WEP in short), which by another of Kirchberg’s results in
[
13
]
is equivalent to the statement that, if
A
denotes the
C^{*}
-algebra,
there is a unique
C^{*}
-norm on
A\otimes C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
.
Kirchberg’s conjecture B3 states that
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
has the WEP or equivalently that any separable
C^{*}
-algebra is a quotient of a WEP

C^{*}
-algebra (these are called QWEP).
He also proved in
[
13
]
that
A
has the LLP if and only if
there is a unique
C^{*}
-norm on
A\otimes B(\ell_{2})
. Thus the question whether the algebras we constructed in
[
21
]
have the LP
seemed like a very natural way to tackle Kirchberg’s conjecture D.
Two roads opened to us:

\bullet
The most ambitious one would be
to prove that one of these algebras (which have LLP and WEP but are not nuclear) fails the LP. This would invalidate conjecture D and hence give an alternate and hopefully much simpler solution to the Connes embedding problem.

\bullet
A much less ambitious project would be to prove that
conjecture D actually implies (conversely) the (equivalent) conjectures B. In that case
one would deduce a negative answer for D from a negative
solution to the Connes embedding problem.

This leaves aside the possibility that conjecture D might hold at least for all
WEP (or all QWEP)
C^{*}
-algebras in which case our examples in
[
21
]

would have the LP.
However, if it turns out that
conjecture D is not valid, exhibiting a WEP
C^{*}
-algebra with the LP remains
a quite interesting goal, considerably strengthening
[
21
]
.

Unfortunately, we have been unable to decide any of these three alternatives.
Nevertheless, we obtained several equivalent forms
of the validity of LLP
\Rightarrow
LP for WEP (and separable)

C^{*}
-algebras, which seem very close to the goal of
at least the less ambitious
project.

For instance, we show that if a single one of our examples in
[
21
]

has the LP then all WEP and LLP (separable)
C^{*}
-algebras also have LP.
Moreover, denoting for simplicity

\mathscr{C}=C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})

we prove the equivalence of the following (conjectural) assertions:

(i) For WEP (and separable)

C^{*}
-algebras, the LLP implies the LP.

(ii) There is a completely isometric embedding
f:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
such that the minimal and maximal
C^{*}
-norms
coincide on the algebraic tensor product

f(\mathscr{C})\otimes\mathscr{C}
.

(iii) Any (or some) completely isometric embedding

j:\mathscr{C}\to B(\ell_{2})
is such that
the map

Id_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})}\otimes j:\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes\mathscr{C}\to\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes B(\ell_{2})

is contractive from the minimal tensor product to the maximal one.

We feel (ii) is very close to Kirchberg’s B3 conjecture, which asserts that
the min and max norms are equivalent on
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}
. Indeed, the latter is just
(ii) for a surjective
f
.

On the discouraging side, even if there is a a
*
-homomorphism
f

as in (ii)
we do not know
whether B3 follows.

Our work also leads to some surprising classes of f.d. operator subspaces
of
\mathscr{C}
.
Let us say that a f.d. subspace
E\subset\mathscr{C}
is
rigid

if any completely contractive map
u:E\to\mathscr{C}
is the restriction to
E
of a
*
-homomorphism. If
E\subset\mathscr{C}
the dual operator space

E^{*}
also embeds completely isometrically in
\mathscr{C}
(and conversely). The spaces
that are completely isometric to a space with rigid dual
are called
controllable
.
We show that (i) is equivalent to the assertion that
all

f.d. subspaces
E\subset\mathscr{C}
are completely isometric to rigid ones (see Lemma
2.14
and Theorem
3.1
). Equivalently all subspaces
E\subset\mathscr{C}
are controllable.
The controllable spaces form an interesting class of f.d. operator subspaces of
\mathscr{C}
that probably deserves more investigation.
It can be shown that if the dual
E^{*}
is 1-exact then
E
is completely isometric to a rigid space but
at the time of this writing we could not decide whether the latter is true when
E
is the

n
-dimensional commutative
C^{*}
-algebra
\ell_{\infty}^{n}
.
We conjecture it is not, already for
n=3
i.e. for
\ell_{\infty}^{3}
. Equivalently,
we conjecture that its operator space dual
\ell_{1}^{3}
is not controllable.
Actually, the conjecture that
M_{k}(\ell_{1}^{3})
is controllable for all
k\geq 1
is equivalent
to the validity of the assertion (i) by the linearization
trick of
[
18
]
(see Remark
2.6
).

We will always restrict our considerations of the LP to the separable case.
In the non-separable case very little seems to be known on the LP.
It is apparently open whether
C^{*}(\mathbb{F})
has the LP when
\mathbb{F}
is an
uncountable free group. In the non-separable case,
it is natural to consider (as e.g. in
[
24
, §7]
) the class of
C^{*}
-algebras
A

such that all separable
C^{*}
-subalgebras
B\subset A

are contained in a larger separable one with LP.

We end the paper with a generalization of the local reflexivity (LR in short) property
on which our previous paper
[
24
]
is based.

Notation and background

As often, we abbreviate completely bounded by c.b.,
completely positive by c.p. and completely contractive by c.c.
We will also abbreviate operator space (or operator spaces) by o.s.
and finite dimensional by f.d.
We abbreviate completely isometric
and completely isometrically by c.i.

An o.s. is a closed subspace of a
C^{*}
-algebra or of
B(H)
.
The duality of o.s. is a consequence of Ruan’s characterization
(see e.g.
[
9
]
) of the sequences of norms on
(M_{n}(E))_{n\geq 1}
(where
M_{n}(E)
denotes the space of
n\times n
-matrices with entries in a vector space
E
) that come from
an embedding of
E
into
B(H)
for some
H
.
Given an o.s.
E\subset B(H)
with (Banach space sense) dual
E^{*}
there is an
\mathcal{H}
and an isometric embedding

j:E^{*}\to B(\mathcal{H})
that induces
isometric isomorphisms
M_{n}(j(E^{*}))\simeq CB(E,M_{n})
for all
n
.
The embedding
j
allows one to consider
E^{*}
as an o.s.
This is what is called the dual o.s. structure on
E^{*}
.
We will often refer to it as the o.s. dual of
E
. An important property due independently to
Effros-Ruan and Blecher-Paulsen (see e.g.
[
9
, p. 42]
or
[
19
, p. 41]
)
is that
E\simeq(E^{*})^{*}
c.i. for any f.d.
E
.

We denote by
Id_{E}
the identity map on
E
.

We reserve the notation
E\otimes F
for the
algebraic
tensor product of two o.s.

Recall that if
E,F
are completely isomorphic operator spaces, we set

d_{cb}(E,F)=\inf\{\|u\|_{cb}\|u^{-1}\|_{cb}\}

where the inf runs over all the complete isomorphisms
u:\ E\to F
.

Definition 0.1
.

We will say that an o.s.
X
locally embeds in an o.s.
Y
if
for any f.d.
E\subset X
and any
\varepsilon>0
there is
F\subset Y
such that

d_{cb}(E,F)<1+\varepsilon
.

We refer to
[
9
,
19
]
for more background on operator spaces.

Let
(D_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family
of
C^{*}
-algebras. We will denote by
\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{i}\mid\in I\})
or simply by

\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{i}\})
the
C^{*}
-algebra formed of the families

d=(d_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\prod_{i\in I}D_{i}
such that
\sup\nolimits_{i\in I}\|d_{i}\|<\infty
, equipped
with the norm
d\mapsto\sup\nolimits_{i\in I}\|d_{i}\|
.

The following classical fact will be invoked repeatedly.

Lemma 0.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be any uncountable free group and let
X\subset C^{*}(\mathbb{F})

be a separable subspace. There is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\subset C^{*}(\mathbb{F})

such that
X\subset\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, the algebra

\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is (unitally) isomorphic to
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
and there is a unital
c.p. and contractive projection
P:C^{*}(\mathbb{F})\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. Consequently,
for any other
C^{*}
-algebra
A
the norm induced on
A\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime}

(algebraic tensor product)
by
A\otimes_{\max}C^{*}(\mathbb{F})
coincides with the norm in
A\otimes_{\max}\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is separable it lies in the closure
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
of
a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}
generated by a countably infinite set of free generators.
The latter is a copy of
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
. As is well known (see e.g.
[
20
]
) there is a conditional expectation operator
P:C^{*}(\mathbb{F})\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 0.3

(On Arveson’s principle)
.

We will often invoke Arveson’s principle. This
deals with certain classes
\mathcal{F}(X,C)
(that we call admissible) formed of maps from a separable
o.s.
X
to a
C^{*}
-algebra
C
. Let
q:C\to Q
be a surjective
*
-homomorphism
between
C^{*}
-algebras. A map

u:A\to Q
is called
\mathcal{F}
-liftable if there is
\hat{u}\in\mathcal{F}(X,C)
such that

u=q\hat{u}
.
Arveson’s principle (see
[
1
, p. 351]
) says that pointwise limits of
\mathcal{F}
-liftable maps are
\mathcal{F}
-liftable.
The typical example of admissible
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathcal{F}(X,C)=B_{CB(X,C)}
.
Assuming
X
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, then
\mathcal{F}(X,C)=\{u\text{ c.c. and c.p. }\}
is another basic example.
By definition a class is admissible if
\mathcal{F}(X,C)
is bounded in norm, pointwise closed
and satisfies the following form of non-commutative convexity:
for any pair
f,g
in
\mathcal{F}

and any
\sigma\in C_{+}
with
\|\sigma\|\leq 1

the mapping

x\mapsto\sigma^{1/2}f(x)\sigma^{1/2}+(1-\sigma)^{1/2}g(x)(1-\sigma)^{1/2}

belongs to
\mathcal{F}
.

Remark 0.4

(Examples)
.

Choi and Effros

proved that all separable nuclear

when

[

That second proof can be equivalently rewritten by combining the proof of
[

with the criterion involving the nor-tensor product in
[

.
These two proofs seem to be the only known ones that

Remark 0.5

(Stability under free product)
.

In
[
18
]
we proved that the free product of
an arbitrary family of unital
C^{*}
-algebras
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
with LLP
also has the LLP. Here and below by “free product” we mean
the full (or maximal) unital free product.

Let
A
be a separable unital
C^{*}
-algebra and let
q:C^{*}(\mathbb{F})\to A
be a surjective unital
*
-homomorphism. Then

A
has the LP if and only if there is a u.c.p. map

u:A\to C^{*}(\mathbb{F})
lifting
q
. Indeed, if this holds the LP
will be inherited by
A
from
C^{*}(\mathbb{F})
.
Taking the LP of
C^{*}(\mathbb{F})
for granted, Boca proved in
[
4
]
that the LP is stable by full countable free products of unital
C^{*}
-algebra
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
. He uses a simple
application of his remarkable result on free products of u.c.p. maps
in
[
2
]
(see also
[
3
,
7
]
).
This can be applied
to an arbitrary family of unital
C^{*}
-algebras
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
and
arbitrary free groups
with surjective unital
*
-homomorphisms
q_{i}:C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{i})\to A_{i}
admitting
u.c.p. liftings
u_{i}:A_{i}\to C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
.
Boca’s theorem from
[
2
]
yields a u.c.p. lifting
for the quotient map
\ast_{i\in I}q_{i}:C^{*}(\ast_{i\in I}\mathbb{F}_{i})\to\ast_{i\in I}A_{i}
.
In the separable case this shows that the LP is stable by countable free products,
but one first needs to know that
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
has the LP
(for which two proofs are described in Remark
0.4
).

A direct proof (from scratch) can be derived from the more recent criterion in Theorem
1.1

and the linearization trick of

[
18
]
. This is similar to the second proof in Remark
0.4
.

Given this, the best way to unify the Choi-Effros and Kirchberg theorems
is perhaps to state that any
countable free product of separable unital
nuclear

C^{*}
-algebras has the LP.

Remark 0.6

(Counterexamples)
.

It is natural to say that a discrete group
G
has the LP
if
C^{*}(G)
has it.
In the present remark all groups are assumed
countable
.
By the preceding discussion we know that free groups and amenable groups have the LP. Thus it is not at all obvious to find groups failing the LP or the LLP. As it turns out all the known counterexamples to the LP or the LLP use Kazhdan’s property (T). Ozawa
[
17
]
proved that a continuum of (T) groups failing the LP
exists but without producing an explicit example. Later on Thom

[
25
]
produced an explicit example failing the LLP.
More recently, Ioana, Spaas and Wiersma
showed that
SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
fails the LLP for all
n\geq 3
.
They also showed that
any infinitely presented group
with property (T) must fail the LP. By
[
17
]
there is a continuum of such groups.
It remains an open question whether all discrete
groups with property (T) fail the LP or even the LLP.

Preliminaries

We start by recalling the main result from
[
24
]
on the LP.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}=C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
.
A separable
C^{*}
-algebra
A
has the LP if and only if
for any set
I
, any f.d.
E\subset A
and any
family
t=(t_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\mathscr{C}\otimes E
we have

(1.1)

\|t\|_{\ell_{\infty}(I,\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\max}A}\leq\sup_{i\in I}\|t_{i}\|_{\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\max}A}.

For this to hold it suffices to check the case when
I=\mathbb{N}
.

Moreover, (
1.1
) holds whenever
A=C^{*}(\mathbb{F})
for any
(not necessarly countable) free group.

Remark 1.2
.

Assume that the Kirchberg conjecture (and the Connes embedding problem) holds.
We will show that LLP
\Rightarrow
LP for separable
C^{*}
-algebras
(which is the same as conjecture (B3)
\Rightarrow
(D) in Kirchberg’s
[
13
]
).
The conjecture is that
\mathscr{C}
has the WEP.
By a general result
\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})
also has it (see
[
20
, p. 193]
).
By Kirchberg’s fundamental theorem (see
[
20
, p. 195]
) we have

B\otimes_{\min}C=B\otimes_{\max}C
whenever
C
has LLP and
B
has WEP. Therefore

\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\min}C=\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\max}C
.
Using this last identity, Theorem
1.1
shows that
C
has the LP if it is separable and has the LLP.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that
\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})
fails the LLP.
Indeed, by
[
12
]

the algebra
\ell_{\infty}(\{M_{n}\mid n\geq 1\})

(which clearly embeds c.i. in
\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})
) does not locally embed in
\mathscr{C}
.
More generally any
C^{*}
-algebra
C
that contains c.i. copies of
M_{n}
for all

is such that
\ell_{\infty}(C)
fails LLP.

In companion to
\mathscr{C}=C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
, let
\mathscr{B}=B(\ell_{2})
.
We denote by

j:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{B}

an isometric
*
-homomorphism.

Let
A,B,C
be
C^{*}
-algebras.
Let
E\subset A
. We say that a mapping
u:E\to B

is

-nuclear if

\|id_{C}\otimes u:C\otimes_{\min}E\to C\otimes_{\max}B\|=1,

or equivalently if
\|u\otimes id_{C}:E\otimes_{\min}C\to B\otimes_{\max}C\|=1.

With this terminology, a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is WEP (resp. LLP) if and only if
Id_{A}
is
\mathscr{C}
-nuclear
(resp.
\mathscr{B}
-nuclear). See
[
20
]
for an exposition of these topics.

Lemma 1.4
.

Assume that
B
satisfies (
1.1
) (e.g.
B=\mathscr{C}
or
B=C^{*}(\mathbb{F})
for any
free group
\mathbb{F}
). Then any
\mathscr{C}
-nuclear
mapping
u:E\to B
is
\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})
-nuclear.

Proof.

Consider

Id\otimes u:\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes E\to\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes B.

Note that we have an isometric inclusion

\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\min}E\subset\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\min}E)

while by (
1.1
) we have an isometric inclusion

\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\max}B\subset\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\max}B).

If
u
is
\mathscr{C}
-nuclear, obviously
Id_{\mathscr{C}}\otimes u
defines a contractive map

\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\min}E)\to\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\max}B),

from which the lemma follows by restriction to
\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes E
.
∎

Let
E\subset A
and
u:E\to B
. We will denote the norm that controls

\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})
-nuclearity by
\|u\|_{mM}
, i.e. we set

(1.2)

\|u\|_{mM}=\|Id_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})}\otimes u:\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\min}E\to\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\max}B\|.

Thus Lemma
1.4
tells us that if
B
satisfies (
1.1
)

(1.3)

\|u\|_{mM}=\|Id_{\mathscr{C}}\otimes u:\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\min}E\to\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\max}B\|.

If moreover
A
has the WEP (with
B
still satisfying (
1.1
)) then for any
u:A\to B
we have

(1.4)

\|u\|_{mM}=\|Id_{\mathscr{C}}\otimes u:\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\max}A\to\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\max}B\|.

Proposition 1.5
.

A separable WEP
C^{*}
-algebra
A
has the LP
if and only if
Id_{A}
is
\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})
-nuclear, or equivalently if and only if

\|Id_{A}\|_{mM}=1.

Proof.

Assume
\|Id_{A}\|_{mM}=1.
A fortiori the pair
(A,\mathscr{C})

is nuclear so
A
is WEP. In addition
we have isometrically

\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\max}A=\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\min}A\subset\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\min}A)=\ell_{\infty}(\mathscr{C}\otimes_{\max}A),

which means
A
has the LP by Theorem
1.1
.

Conversely, if
A
has both LP and WEP, then
(A,\mathscr{C})
is a nuclear pair

be a copy of
\mathscr{C}
.
The equivalent properties in Theorem
3.1
hold if and only if
there is a c.i. embedding
f:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}_{1}

such that the min and max norms (induced by
the respective ones on
\mathscr{C}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{C}
)
coincide on
f(\mathscr{C})\otimes\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{C}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

Assume that
f(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\min}\mathscr{C}=f(\mathscr{C})\otimes_{\max}\mathscr{C}
.
By (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(v) in Theorem
2.9
,
any f.d.
E\subset\mathscr{C}
is controllable, which is (vi) in
Theorem
3.1
. This proves the if part. Conversely, by Proposition
3.4
, the assertion (vi) in
Theorem
3.1
implies the existence of
f
as in Corollary
3.6
.
∎

For convenience,
let us say that a
C^{*}
-algebra
D
is
j
-nuclear
if
j
is
D
-nuclear.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
A
be any separable WEP, LLP

C^{*}
-algebra such that
\mathscr{C}
locally embeds in
A
.
Let
(D_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
be an arbitrary family of

j
-nuclear separable
C^{*}
-algebras. If
A
has the LP
then
(A,\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{\alpha}\}))
is a nuclear pair
and
j
is
\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{\alpha}\})
-nuclear. Equivalently

\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{\alpha}\})
is
j
-nuclear.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\in I
. Assume that
D_{\alpha}
is separable
and
j
-nuclear. By the construction in
[
21
]
there is a
WEP and LLP separable
C^{*}
-algebra
A_{\alpha}
such that
\mathscr{C}
locally embeds
in
A_{\alpha}
and is such that the pair
(A_{\alpha},D_{\alpha})
is nuclear.
By Corollary
1.12
the pairs

are nuclear
for any
\alpha\in I
.
By Theorem
1.1
the pair

(A,\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{\alpha}\}))
is nuclear.
We now apply Proposition
3.3
to
X=\mathscr{C}
with

in place of

.
Recalling that

(see (

)) we observe

is

(since

itself
is

4
A “local reflexivity” theorem

In Banach space theory, the local reflexivity (LR) principle from
[
15
]
plays
an important role. For
C^{*}
-algebras, following seminal work
by Archbold and Batty, Effros and Haagerup studied in
[
8
]

the cb-version of that principle. In
[
24
]
we proved an
mb-version of the same principle. What follows is a more general version of the one in
[
24
]
, suggested by our use of the norm
mM
in the preceding section.

Let
E\subset A
be a finite dimensional subspace of a
C^{*}
-algebra

A
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
E\subset A
be any f.d. subspace of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
.
Then for any separable
C^{*}
-algebra
C

we have a contractive inclusion

(4.1)

{mM}(E,C^{**})\to{mM}(E,C)^{**}.

In other words any
u
in the unit ball of
{mM}(E,C^{**})
is the weak* limit
of a net
(u_{i})
in the unit ball of
{mM}(E,C)
.

More generally, consider a class of
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{D}

that is stable by arbitrary
\ell_{\infty}
-sum, i.e. for any family

(D_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\mathcal{D}
we have
\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{i}\})\in\mathcal{D}
.

Then we define

\|u\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{D}}\|id_{D}\otimes u:D\otimes_{\min}E\to D\otimes_{\max}C\|,

and we denote by
{mM_{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)
the space of all
u:E\to C

equipped with this norm.

When
\mathcal{D}=\{\ell_{\infty}(I,\mathscr{C})\mid I\ arbitrary\}
and
C
has LP then

\|u\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}=\|u\|_{mM}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}
stable by arbitrary
\ell_{\infty}
-sums.
Let
E\subset A
be any f.d. subspace of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
.
Then for any
C^{*}
-algebra
C

we have a contractive inclusion

(4.2)

{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C^{**})\to{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)^{**}.

Proof.

This will follow from the bipolar theorem.
We first need to identify the dual of

{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)
. As a vector space

{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)\simeq C\otimes E^{*}
and
hence
{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)^{*}\simeq C^{*}\otimes E

(or say
({C^{*}})^{\dim(E)}
).
We equip
C^{*}\otimes E
with the norm
\alpha
defined as follows.
We call “admissible” any
C^{*}
-algebra
D
in
\mathcal{D}
.
Let
K\subset{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)^{*}

denote the set of those
f\in{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)^{*}
for which
there is an admissible
D
, a functional
w
in the unit ball of

{(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}}

and

t\in B_{D\otimes_{\min}E}
,
so that

\forall u\in{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)\quad f(u)=\langle w,[Id_{D}\otimes u](t)\rangle.

By the very definition of
\|u\|_{{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)}
we have

(4.3)

\|u\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}=\sup\{|f(u)|\mid f\in K\}.

This implies that the unit ball of the dual of
{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)
is the bipolar of
K
.
We will show that actually
K
is the unit ball of

{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)^{*}
.

First we show that
K
is convex.
Let
D_{1},D_{2}
be admissible
C^{*}
-algebras. Let
D=D_{1}\oplus D_{2}

with the usual
C^{*}
-norm. Note that
D
is admissible.
Using the easily checked identities
(here the direct sum is in the
\ell_{\infty}
-sense)

D\otimes_{\min}E=(D_{1}\otimes_{\min}E)\oplus(D_{2}\otimes_{\min}E),\text{ and }{D\otimes_{\max}C}=(D_{1}\otimes_{\max}C)\oplus(D_{2}\otimes_{\max}C),

and hence
{(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}}=(D_{1}\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}\oplus_{1}(D_{2}\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}

(direct sum in the
\ell_{1}
-sense), it is easy to check that

K
is convex and hence that
K

is the unit ball of some norm
\alpha
on
{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)^{*}
.

Our main point is the claim that
K
is weak* closed.
To prove this, let
(f_{i})
be a net in
K
converging
weak* to some
f\in{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)^{*}
.
Let
D_{i}
be admissible
C^{*}
-algebras,
w_{i}\in B_{(D_{i}\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}}

and
t_{i}\in B_{D_{i}\otimes_{\min}E}
such that we have

\forall u\in{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)\quad f_{i}(u)=\langle w_{i},[Id_{D_{i}}\otimes u](t_{i})\rangle.

Let
D=\ell_{\infty}(\{D_{i}\})
and
let
t\in D\otimes E
be associated to
(t_{i})
.
Clearly
\|t\|_{\min}\leq 1
.
Let
p_{i}:D\to D_{i}
denote the canonical coordinate projection,
and let
v_{i}\in(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}
be the functional
defined by
v_{i}(x)=w_{i}([p_{i}\otimes Id_{C}](x))
.
Clearly
v_{i}\in B_{(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}}
and

f_{i}(u)=\langle v_{i},[Id_{D}\otimes u](t)\rangle.

Let
w
be a weak* limit point of
(v_{i})
(or the limit point if we pass to an
ultrafilter refining our net).
By weak* compactness,
w\in B_{(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}}
.
Then

f(u)=\lim f_{i}(u)=\langle w,[Id_{D}\otimes u](t)\rangle.

Thus we conclude
f\in K
, which proves our claim.

By (
4.3
) the unit ball of the dual of
{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C)
is the bipolar of
K
,
which is equal to its weak* closure. By what precedes,
the latter coincides with
K
. Thus the gauge of
K
is the
announced dual norm
\alpha=\|\ \|^{*}_{{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}}
.

Let
u^{\prime\prime}\in{{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}(E,C^{**})}
with
\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}\leq 1
. By the bipolar theorem,
to complete the proof it suffices to show that

u^{\prime\prime}
belongs to the polar of
K
, or equivalently that

|f(u^{\prime\prime})|\leq 1
for any
f\in K
.
To show this consider
f\in K
taking

u\in mM^{\mathcal{D}}(E,C)
to
f(u)=\langle w,[Id_{D}\otimes u](t)\rangle

with
D
admissible,
w\in B_{(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}}

and
t\in B_{D\otimes_{\min}E}
.

Observe that

[Id_{D}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}](t)\in D\otimes C^{**}\subset(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{**}
.
Recall that
mM^{\mathcal{D}}(E,C^{**})\simeq mM^{\mathcal{D}}(E,C)^{**}\simeq(C^{**})^{\dim(E)}

as vector spaces. Thus we may view
f\in mM^{\mathcal{D}}(E,C)^{*}

as a weak* continuous functional on
mM^{\mathcal{D}}(E,C^{**})
to define

f(u^{\prime\prime})
.
We claim that

(4.4)

f(u^{\prime\prime})=\langle w,[Id_{D}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}](t)\rangle,

where the duality is relative to
the pair
\langle(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*},(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{**}\rangle
.
From this claim the conclusion is immediate. Indeed,
we have

\|[Id_{D}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}](t)\|_{D\otimes_{\max}C^{**}}\leq\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}\leq 1
, and
by the maximality of the max-norm on
D\otimes C^{**}

we have a fortiori

\|[Id_{D}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}](t)\|_{(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{**}}\leq\|[Id_{D}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}](t)\|_{D\otimes_{\max}C^{**}}\leq 1.

Therefore

|f(u^{\prime\prime})|=|\langle w,[Id_{D}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}](t)\rangle|\leq\|w\|_{(D\otimes_{\max}C)^{*}}\leq 1
, which completes the proof modulo our claim (
4.4
).

To verify the claim, note that
the identity (
4.4
) holds for any
u^{\prime\prime}\in mM^{\mathcal{D}}(E,C)
.
Thus it suffices to prove that the right-hand side of (
4.4
)
is a weak* continuous function of
u^{\prime\prime}
(which is obvious for the left-hand side).
To check this
one way is to note that
t\in D\otimes E
can be written
as a finite sum
t=\sum d_{k}\otimes e_{k}
(
d_{k}\in D,e_{k}\in E
) and
if we denote by
\dot{w}:D\to C^{*}
the linear map associated to
w

we have

\langle w,[Id_{D}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}](t)\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{k}\langle w,[d_{k}\otimes u^{\prime\prime}(e_{k})]\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{k}\langle\dot{w}(d_{k}),u^{\prime\prime}(e_{k})\rangle,

and since
\dot{w}(d_{k})\in C^{*}
the weak* continuity as a function of
u^{\prime\prime}
is obvious,
completing the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let
E\subset A
be f.d.
and
let
\mathcal{D}
be as before stable by arbitrary
\ell_{\infty}
-sums. For the sake of the comparison we wish to make below, we define for any
u:E\to C
,

\gamma_{1}(u)=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{D}}\|Id_{D}\otimes u:{D}\otimes_{\min}E\to{D}\otimes_{\max}C\|.

For instance, if

\mathcal{D}
is formed of WEP
C^{*}
-algebras and if
\{M_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}
locally embeds in
\mathcal{D}
, then

\gamma_{1}(u)=\|Id_{\mathscr{B}}\otimes u:\mathscr{B}\otimes_{\min}E\to\mathscr{B}\otimes_{\max}C\|.

In that case
\gamma_{1}
is related to the LLP.
We also wish to consider

\gamma_{2}(u)=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{D}}\|Id_{D}\otimes u:{D}\otimes_{\max}E\to{D}\otimes_{\max}C\|.

The preceding proof (resp. the similar one in
[
24
]
) shows that, without any additional assumption on
A,D,C
, for
\gamma=\gamma_{1}
(resp. for
\gamma=\gamma_{2}
)
we have a contractive inclusion

(4.5)

{\Gamma}(E,C^{**})\to{\Gamma}(E,C)^{**},

where
{\Gamma}(E,C)
denotes the space of
all maps
u:E\to C
equipped with the norm
\gamma
.

Assume
A
has the LP or satisfies (
1.1
). Then when either
\mathcal{D}=\{\mathscr{C}\}
or

\mathcal{D}=\{\ell_{\infty}(I,\mathscr{C})\mid I\ arbitrary\}

(or when
\mathcal{D}
is the class of all
C^{*}
-algebras) we have

\gamma_{2}(u)=\|u\|_{mb}
. In that case (
4.5
) is nothing but
the LR principle from
[
24
, Th. 5.2]
, which is equivalent to the LP of
A
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is the collection formed of all
\ell_{\infty}(I)
’s (i.e. we replace
\mathscr{C}
by
\mathbb{C}
!)
then
\gamma_{1}(u)=\gamma_{2}(u)=\|u\|
and we recover the classical LR principle for Banach spaces (see
[
15
]
).

If
C
(resp.
A
) is nuclear and if
\{M_{n}\}
locally embeds in
\mathcal{D}
, then

\gamma_{1}(u)=\|u\|_{cb}
(resp.
\gamma_{2}(u)=\gamma_{1}(u)
for all
\mathcal{D}
)
which is consistent with the fact that
nuclearity implies LR (resp. LP).

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be as in Theorem
4.2
.
Let
C/\mathcal{I}
be a quotient
C^{*}
-algebra with quotient map
q:C\to C/\mathcal{I}
.
Let
E\subset A
a f.d. subspace.
Then
any
u:E\to C/\mathcal{I}

admits a lifting
\hat{u}:E\to C
such that

\|\hat{u}\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}=\|u\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}.

Proof.

Replacing
C/\mathcal{I}
by
(C/\mathcal{I})^{**}

and using the morphism lifting
(C/\mathcal{I})^{**}
up to
C^{**}

we can lift
u

to a map
v:E\to C^{**}
with
\|v\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}=\|u\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}
.
Then by Theorem
4.2

there is a net
v_{i}:E\to C

with
\|v_{i}\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}\leq 1
tending pointwise weak* to
v
.
By the usual Mazur argument passing to suitable convex hulls
we get a net such that
qv_{i}
tends pointwise (in norm) to
u
.
For the final point we use Arveson’s principle, observing that
the classes

\mathcal{F}(E,C)=\{u:E\to C\mid\|u\|_{mM^{\mathcal{D}}}\leq 1\}

are admissible.
∎

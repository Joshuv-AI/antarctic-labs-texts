Introduction

In birational geometry, minimal varieties of general type form one of the basic building blocks of the minimal model program. A crucial step toward classifying these varieties is to study the distribution of their birational invariants. Since every smooth variety of general type has a minimal model, this will give the distribution of invariants in the smooth case as well. Throughout, we will work over any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

We begin by recalling a few examples of birational invariants. Let
n\geq 2
be any integer and
X
be a minimal projective
n
-fold of general type, so that
X
has at worst
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities and the canonical divisor

is a measure of the asymptotic growth of the plurigenera, defined as

which is a positive rational number. Since

is minimal, so that

is nef, the Riemann-Roch formula shows that

, the top intersection number of

. We define the

of

r_{s}(X):=\text{min}\{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}|\varphi_{m,X}\ \text{is birational for all}\ m\geq p\},

Taking the limit, as

(4.1)

Case
4.1
.2

n=3k+1
,

Take

(4.2)

4.2.
The canonical stability index

As we have seen, for
3\leq i\leq n
,
|N_{i}|
is not composed with a pencil. Applying the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem in the similar way to that of (

) and (

), for any

and any

\displaystyle|mK_{X^{\prime}}||_{Z_{j}}

\displaystyle\succcurlyeq

\displaystyle|K_{X^{\prime}}+\lceil{(m-n+2)\pi^{*}(K_{X})}\rceil+(n-3)M_{1}||_{Z_{j}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\succcurlyeq

\displaystyle|{M_{1}}|_{Z_{j}}|.

Hence

distinguishes different generic irreducible elements of
|N_{j}|
whenever

and

.

We study the situation on

, which is composed with a pencil of curves. If

is composed with a rational pencil of curves, as long as

,

|N_{2}|
since

. If

,
C_{2}
of

(M_{1}|_{Z_{2}}-C_{1}-C_{2})

(M_{1}|_{Z_{2}}-C_{i})|_{C_{i}}\sim 0

for

. Hence, by the vanishing theorem and for any

, one has the relation:

\displaystyle|mK_{X^{\prime}}||_{Z_{2}}

\displaystyle\succcurlyeq

\displaystyle|K_{X^{\prime}}+\lceil{(m-n)\pi^{*}(K_{X})}\rceil+(n-1)M_{1}|

\displaystyle\succcurlyeq

\displaystyle|K_{Z_{2}}+\lceil{(m-n)\pi^{*}(K_{X})|_{Z_{2}}}\rceil+{M_{1}}|_{Z_{2}}|

and the surjective map:

\displaystyle H^{0}(Z_{2},K_{Z_{2}}+\lceil{(m-n)\pi^{*}(K_{X})|_{Z_{2}}}\rceil+{M_{1}}|_{Z_{2}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle H^{0}(C_{1},K_{C_{1}}+D_{1})\oplus H^{0}(C_{2},K_{C_{2}}+D_{2})

can distinguish different generic irreducible elements of

in this case.
In summary, for any

, Conditions (1) and (2) of Corollary

5.
Geometry of Optimal Examples

In this section, we summarize an alternate approach proving the volume bound in Theorem
1.1
(2). This method also shows that any
X
realizing the minimal value of
v_{n,n-1}
must strongly resemble one of the weighted projective hypersurfaces in Section
4.3
. We omit some of the proof details and continue to use the notation of Section
3.1
.

Claim 5.1
.

Let
X
be a minimal variety of general type with dimension
n\geq 3
and canonical dimension
n-1
such that

\text{\rm Vol}(X)=\frac{6}{2n+(n\bmod 3)}.

(5.1)

Then the canonical map of
X
is a dominant rational map to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
with general fiber a genus
2
curve. Furthermore, the general surface
Z_{2}
has minimal volume in the sense of Proposition
5.2
below. Its minimal model
Z_{2,0}
contains a tree of rational curves contracted by the morphism from
X^{\prime}
to its canonical model, described in Cases
5
.1,
5
.2, and
5
.3.

Proof.

Let
X
be a minimal variety realizing (
5.1
) as above. We immediately see that
g(Z_{1})=2
, or else
\xi\geq\frac{4}{n}
by (
2.2
). Further,
p_{g}(X)=n
, or else
\beta_{2}\geq 2
, so that
K_{X}^{n}\geq 2\cdot\frac{2}{n}=\frac{4}{n}
by (
2.3
). The image
\overline{\varphi_{1}(X)}
of the canonical map is then a subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
of dimension
n-1
, so
\overline{\varphi_{1}(X)}={\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
. Therefore, the canonical map is a dominant rational map to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
whose general fiber is a smooth genus 2 curve.

We can also say a great deal about
Z_{2}
and the properties of the birational contraction
\pi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X_{\can}
, restricted to
Z_{2}
. Here
X_{\can}=\mathrm{Proj}\bigoplus_{m=1}^{\infty}H^{0}(X,mK_{X})
is the canonical model of the variety
X
(equivalently, of
X^{\prime}
) and
K_{X_{\can}}^{n}=K_{X}^{n}
.

The key idea is to find a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\can}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{2,0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\can}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

where
g:Z_{2}\rightarrow Z_{2,0}
is the minimal model,
S
is a projective normal surface with
K_{S}

{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier, and
f:Z_{2,0}\rightarrow S
is a birational morphism whose exceptional divisors have nonpositive discrepancies.

By inequality (
3.3
),
(n-1)\pi|_{Z_{2}}^{*}(K_{X})\geq g^{*}(K_{Z_{2,0}})
. Taking the pushforward to
S
gives
(n-1)\psi^{*}(K_{X_{\can}})\geq K_{S}
by the commutativity of the diagram and because
K_{Z_{2,0}}
and
f^{*}(K_{S})
differ by exceptional divisors contracted by
f
. Both
\psi^{*}(K_{X_{\can}})
and
K_{S}
are nef divisors, the latter by the assumption on discrepancies. Therefore,

((n-1)\psi^{*}(K_{X_{\can}}))^{2}\geq(n-1)\psi^{*}(K_{X_{\can}})\cdot K_{S}\geq K_{S}^{2}.

(5.2)

Combining the inequality (
5.2
) with
K_{X}^{n}\geq(\pi_{\can}^{*}(K_{X_{\can}})|_{Z_{2}})^{2}
, we get

(n-1)^{2}K_{X}^{n}\geq((n-1)\pi_{\can}^{*}(K_{X_{\can}})|_{Z_{2}})^{2}=((n-1)\psi^{*}(K_{X_{\can}}))^{2}\geq K_{S}^{2}.

Moreover, the intersection number
K_{S}^{2}
is determined by the volume of the minimal surface
Z_{2,0}
and the divisors are contracted by
f
. We may obtain sharper bounds on
K_{X}^{n}
via this method than from only considering smooth models of
Z_{2}
because often,
K_{S}^{2}>\text{\rm Vol}(Z_{2})
.
Therefore, we seek to factor
\pi_{\can}|_{Z_{2}}:Z_{2}\rightarrow X_{\can}
through contractions to certain singular surfaces
S
.

The minimal model
Z_{2,0}
of
Z_{2}
is a fibration of genus 2 curves over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. The general fiber is a genus 2 curve
Z_{1}
.
We know by the adjunction formula that
Z_{2}
satisfies
K_{Z_{2}}\geq(n-1)Z_{1}
. Supposing
n\geq 3
,
p_{g}(Z_{2})\geq h^{0}(Z_{2},(n-1)Z_{1})\geq n\geq 3
. Under these assumptions, we have the following properties (cf.
[
7
, Proposition 2.9]
):

Proposition 5.2
.

\text{\rm Vol}(Z_{2})=(K_{Z_{2,0}})^{2}\geq\begin{cases}\frac{8n-16}{3},&n\equiv 2\bmod 3\\
\frac{8n-14}{3},&n\equiv 1\bmod 3\\
\frac{8n-12}{3},&n\equiv 0\bmod 3\text{ .}\end{cases}

(5.3)

The fibration
Z_{2}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
factors through
Z_{2,0}
and
K_{Z_{2,0}}=(n-1)Z_{1}+G
, where
G
is an effective divisor.

If we have equality in (
5.3
), then
G=V+2E
, where
V
is a vertical divisor,
E\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a section of the fibration, and
E^{2}=-\frac{1}{3}(n+1+V\cdot E)
. The divisor
V
satisfies
K_{Z_{2,0}}\cdot V=0
so that all irreducible components of
V
are
(-2)
-curves contained in fibers. Finally,
V\cdot E=0,1,2
, in the cases
n=3k+2
,
n=3k+1
, and
n=3k
, respectively.

One can show that if
K_{X}^{n}
is small enough to compete with the examples in Section
4.3
, then
\text{\rm Vol}(Z_{2})
also must be equal to the minimum in (
5.3
). Further,
\pi_{\can}|_{Z_{2}}:Z_{2}\rightarrow X_{\can}
factors through the minimal model
g:Z_{2}\rightarrow Z_{2,0}
. Using Proposition
5.2
, we may identify some additional curves on
Z_{2,0}
which are contracted by
\pi_{\can}
. In each case, we will contract a connected curve of arithmetic genus
0
with negative-definite self-intersection matrix. This results in a projective surface with rational singularities (see
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
and
[
2
, Proposition 1]
), which is therefore
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein.

Case
5
.1.

n=3k+2
,
k\geq 1
.

This is the easiest case, because we can define the appropriate
f:Z_{2,0}\rightarrow S
by only contracting the curve
E
of Proposition
5.2
on
Z_{2,0}
. This gives
K_{Z_{2,0}}^{2}=f^{*}K_{S}+aE
with
a=\frac{-k+1}{k+1}\leq 0
. Note that the
n=2
case must be treated separately. Assuming that
K_{Z_{2,0}}^{2}
is its minimum of
\frac{8n-16}{3}
, we may compute

(n-1)^{2}K_{X}^{n}\geq K_{S}^{2}=\frac{8n-16}{3}-(aE)^{2}=\frac{3(n-1)^{2}}{n+1}.

Therefore,
K_{X}^{n}\geq\frac{3}{n+1}
and we recover the same volume bound.

We can see this curve
E
explicitly in Example
4.3
: the general weighted projective hypersurface

X_{10(k+1)}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}(1^{(n)},2(k+1),5(k+1))

has canonical dimension
n-1
, with the canonical map a rational map
X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
having generic fiber a curve of genus 2.

Now, consider the intersection of
n-2
general sections of the reflexive sheaf
K_{X}
. This is a general surface
S=S_{10(k+1)}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}(1,1,2(k+1),5(k+1))
with one singular point
p
, which is a quotient singularity of type
\frac{1}{k+1}(1,1)
. This is a cone over the rational normal curve of degree
k+1
, so we can explicitly resolve it with a single blowup at
p
. Denote this blowup as
f:Z_{2,0}\rightarrow S
. This has the same numerical properties as in the general case above. From the description as a weighted projective hypersurface, we know
K_{S}^{2}=\frac{(3k+1)^{2}}{k+1}
. Therefore,

K_{Z_{2,0}}^{2}=\frac{8n-16}{3},

so that the smooth model has volume matching the minimum in Proposition
5.2
. As the notation suggests, this is the same

obtained by desingularizing

with a blowup and applying the machinery of Section

.

Case
5
.2.

n=3k+1
,
k\geq 1
.
If
K_{Z_{2,0}}^{2}
achieves the minimum of
\frac{8n-14}{3}
, then by Proposition

,
V\cdot E=1
. In general,
V
could have several
(-2)
-curves as irreducible components, but a unique one, say

, intersects

in a single point. There is a birational morphism

contracting exactly the connected curve

.

The morphism
\pi_{\can}:Z_{2}\rightarrow X_{\can}
contracts both these curves on the minimal model, so it factors through

and

, where
a=\frac{1-k}{2k+1}
and

are both nonpositive. One can calculate

(n-1)^{2}K_{X}^{n}\geq K_{S}^{2}=\frac{8n-14}{3}-(aE+bV_{1})^{2}=\frac{6(n-1)^{2}}{2n+1},

so that

, in agreement with Corollary

.

Once again, these exceptional curves appear in Example
4.4
. The general weighted projective hypersurface

X_{10k+6}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}(1^{(n)},2k+1,5k+3)

has canonical dimension
n-1
with the canonical map a rational map

to obtain

has only one singular point, at

of

(see, for example,
[

is the continued fraction representation. This means that the exceptional locus has two rational curves as irreducible components, with self-intersections
-2
and

, corresponding to

and

above, respectively. It’s straightforward to verify (e.g. using

Case

,

,

, as desired.

Example
4.5
again displays the same behavior. For any positive integer

,
k\geq 1

This surface

The results of this section notwithstanding, not all minimal varieties satisfying (
5.1
) are necessarily quasi-smooth weighted projective hypersurfaces. This is illustrated by the following example.

Example 5.3
.

Let
n=3k
for
k\geq 1
. The general hypersurface

X_{10k+1}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(1^{(3k)},2k,5k)

is quasi-smooth and has only one non-canonical singularity of type
\frac{1}{5k}(1^{(3k-1)},2k)
, which satisfies the “nefness criterion” of Chen-Jiang-Li
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
. So, after performing a weighted blowup at this point, by
[
13
, Proposition 2.9]
one gets a minimal variety
Y
of dimension
n
with canonical volume
\frac{1}{k}=\frac{3}{n}=\frac{6}{2n+(n\bmod 3)}
. This example does not have
\mathrm{Pic}(X)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
, so it is not a quasi-smooth weighted projective hypersurface (see
[
14
, Theorem 3.2.4]
).

We study the canonical stability index of this example. Denote by
x_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,3k
) the coordinates of weight
1
, by
w
the coordinate with weight
2k
, and by
t
the coordinate with weight
5k
in
{\mathbb{P}}(1^{(3k)},2k,5k)
. The defining equation can be written:

f_{0}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{3k},w)+f_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{3k},w)t+(a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{3k}x_{3k})t^{2}=0.

Denote by
\theta:Y\rightarrow X:=X_{10k+1}
the weighted blowup with weights
(1^{(3k-1)},2k)
. Then we have
5kK_{Y}=\theta^{*}(5kK_{X})-E
with
E
the exceptional divisor and

\theta(E)=Q\not\in(t=0)
. This means that there are the following injective maps:

H^{0}(Y,5kK_{Y})\hookrightarrow\Lambda\hookrightarrow H^{0}(X,5kK_{X})

where
\Lambda
is the vector space spanned by products of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{3k},w
of weighted degree
5k
. As
\Lambda
does not give a birational map of
X
(since
t
is missing),
\varphi_{5k,Y}
is non-birational. Hence
r_{s}(Y)=5k+1=\frac{5n+3}{3}=r_{n,n-1}
.

Remark 5.4
.

In the explicit examples in Section
4.3
and Example

Z_{2,0}

Question 5.5
.

If
X
is a minimal variety of general type of dimension

, and smallest possible volume, is the minimal model of the associated general surface

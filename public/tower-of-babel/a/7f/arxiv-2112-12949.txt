Non-trivial bounds on 2, 3, 4, and 5-torsion in class groups of number fields, conditional on standard L L -function conjectures
Arul Shankar, Jacob Tsimerman

Abstract
We prove new conditional bounds on the m m -torsion subgroups of class groups of number fields of any fixed degree, for m = 2 m=2 , 3 3 , 4 4 , and 5 5 . Our methods first recast the problem in the language of class groups of Galois modules, which allows us to relate these torsion subgroups to Selmer groups of elliptic curves. We then obtain a global estimate using the refined BSD conjecture, in a similar way to how one normally uses the Brauer-Siegel bound. Our methods are potentially very general, but rely on the existence of motives with very special ℤ / m ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}} -cohomology. In particular, the restriction to m ≤ 5 m\leq 5 stems from needing an elliptic curve over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} with m m -torsion subgroup isomorphic to ℤ / m ​ ℤ ⊕ μ m {\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\mu_{m} .

Introduction

History and main results

Let
n,m\geq 2

m

h_{m}(K)=O_{n,\epsilon}(|{\rm Disc}(K)|^{1/2+\epsilon}),

(1)

h(K)

of the entire class group, and applying the trivial inequality

h_{m}(K)\leq h(K)

1

The first such result was obtained independently by Pierce

[
17

[
5

O_{\epsilon}(|{\rm Disc}(K)|^{1/3+\epsilon})
for the cases
(n,m)=(3,3)

and
(4,3)

Recent work of Bhargava, Taniguchi, Thorne, Zhao and the two authors

[

Remark 3

We make the following three observations regarding Theorem
1
and its proof.

(a)

To our knowledge, this is the first (even conditional) result which gives pointwise bounds, where the quality of the exponent does not degrade with the degree
n
of the number field.

(b)

To obtain the conclusion of Theorem
1
, we need to assume something weaker than the three conjectures for all elliptic curves
E/K
. In fact, we only use base changes of a single elliptic curve.

(c)

If, for a fixed elliptic curve
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
, we had a way of bounding the rank of
E/K
by
o(\log|{\rm Disc}(K)|)
unconditionally, we would not need to assume the generalized Riemann Hypothesis for the
L
-function of
E/K
. Assuming the Lindelöf conjecture for this
L
-function would be sufficient. Moreover, any subconvex estimate on the central value of this
L
-function would also imply a power saving bound on the torsion in the class group.

Method of proof

Our perspective is as follows.

1.

First, for an integer
m
, we express the group

{\rm Cl}(K)[m]
as an unramified cohomology group of a finite
Galois module
M
. In fact,
M
is simply
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}[{\rm Hom}(K,\bar{{\mathbb{Q}}})]
. We denote this unramified cohomology group by
{\rm Cl}(M)
.

2.

Our main idea (philosophically, and somewhat imprecisely) is to find other ‘motives’
X
whose mod
m
-reduction is (essentially) isomorphic to a power of
M
. Then we may hope to reinterpret
{\rm Cl}(M)
as the
m
-torsion of a motivic class group
Cl(X)
. There should be a global class number formula for
Cl(X)
with a trivial bound that one can use to bound
Cl(M).
In a sense, we are obtaining better “trivial bounds” for
{\rm Cl}(M)
by embedding
M
into different motives.

3.

Concretely, we find an elliptic curve
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
E[m]
is isomorphic to
\mu_{m}\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
as Galois modules over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. It is known that such elliptic curves only exist when
m
is
2
,
3
,
4
, and
5
, hence our results are limited to these four values of
m
. Our motive is essentially the Abelian variety
A=\textrm{Res}_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}E_{K}
and its class group corresponds to the Tate-Shafarevich group. The torsion group scheme
A[n]
is isomorphic to
M\oplus M\otimes\mu_{m}
, and we show that
{\rm Cl}(M)
and
{\rm Cl}(M\otimes\mu_{m})
are approximately the same size (in fact they are dual to each other up to small error).

4.

The Refined BSD conjecture plays the role of the class number formula which allows us a method for computing the size of
\Sh_{A}
, assuming we have control on the rank of
A
and the size of the central value of the
L
-function. This is where the generalized Riemann hypothesis enters. We then use GRH to provide an upper bound for
\Sh_{A}
. This in turn provides a trivial upper bound for
\Sh_{A}[m]
, which we show is essentially the same size as
{\rm Cl}(A[m])
(once the size of the rank has been controlled). Combining these observations gives an upper bound for
{\rm Cl}(M)
.

In fact, we find it easier to work with
E/K
directly and not form the Weil-restriction down to
{\mathbb{Q}}
, but this is a minor point done for technical convenience.

Estimating the terms in the refined BSD conjecture

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
K
be a degree-
n
number field. Let
L(E/K,s)
denote the Hasse–Weil
L
-function of
E
over
K
, normalized so that the critical line is
\Re(s)=1
. We denote the completed
L
-function of
E/K
by
\Lambda(E/K,s)
:

\Lambda(E/K,s):={\rm Norm}_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(N_{E/K})^{s}|{\rm Disc}(K)|^{2s}\Gamma_{K}(s)L(E/K,s),

where
N_{E/K}
is the conductor ideal of
E
over
K
and
\Gamma_{K}(s):=((2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s))^{n}
.
We begin by recalling three standard conjectures regarding
E/K
and its
L
-function.
First, the Hasse–Weil conjecture (HWC) for
E/K
is the following:

Conjecture 2.1

The function
L(E/K,s)
has an analytic continuation to the entire complex plane, and satisfies the function equation

\Lambda(E/K,s)=w(E/K)\Lambda(E/K,2-s),

where
w(E/K)
denotes the root number of
E/K
.

Denote the algebraic rank of
E/K
by
r_{E/K}
. The refined Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture for
E/K
is the following:

Conjecture 2.2

The algebraic rank
r_{E/K}
is equal to the order of vanishing of
L(E/K,s)
at
s=1
. Moreover, the leading term of the Taylor series of
L(E/K,s)
at
s=1
is given by

\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{L(E/K,s)}{(s-1)^{r_{E/K}}}=|{\rm Disc}(K)|^{-1/2}\cdot\#\Sh_{E/K}\cdot R_{E/K}\cdot\frac{\Omega_{E/K}C_{E/K}}{\#E(K)_{{\rm tor}}^{2}}.

(2)

Above,
{\rm Disc}(K)
denotes the discriminant of
K
,
\Sh_{E/K}
is the Tate–Shafarevich group of
E
over
K
,
R_{E/K}
is the regulator of
E
over
K
,
\Omega_{E,K}:=\Omega_{+}^{r_{1}}\Omega_{-}^{r_{2}}
is the product of the two periods of
E
raised to the number of real and complex embeddings of
K
, respectively, and
C_{E/K}
is given by

C_{E/K}:=\prod_{v}c_{v}(E/K)\Big|\frac{\Delta_{E,v,\min}}{\Delta_{E,v}}\Big|_{v}^{\frac{1}{12}},

where the product is over all finite places
v
of
K
, and where
\Delta_{E,v,\min}
denotes the discriminant of a minimal model for
E
over
K_{v}
.

Finally, we have the Grand Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for the
L
-function of
E/K
.

Conjecture 2.3

The nontrivial zeroes of
L(E/K,s)
lie on the line
\Re(s)=1
.

In the remainder of the section, we obtain bounds on the rank
r_{E/K}
of
E/K
and the size of the Tate–Shafarevich group
\Sh_{E/K}
conditional on
the above three conjectures on
E/K
.
Our bounds are in the setting where
E
and the degree
n
of the number field
K
are fixed, while
K
is varying.

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4

We have

1\ll_{n,E}\frac{\Omega_{E/K}C_{E/K}}{\#E(K)_{{\rm tor}}^{2}}\ll_{n,E,\epsilon}1.

Proof:

The quantity
\Omega_{E/K}
depends only on
E
and the number of real and complex embeddings of
K
. By Merel’s generalization
[
14
]
of Mazur’s theorem
[
13
]
, it follows that we have

\#E(K)_{{\rm tor}}\ll_{n}1
. We also have the inequalities

1\leq\prod_{v}\Big|\frac{\Delta_{E,v,\min}}{\Delta_{E}}\Big|_{v}\ll_{n,E}1,\quad\quad 1\leq\prod_{v}c_{v}(E/K)\ll_{n,E}1,

where the first two inequalities are immediate, and the second two follow from an argument identical to
the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 6.2.1]
.

\Box

Next, we we have the following conditional bounds on the rank of
E/K
from
[
8
, Proposition 5.21]
.

Lemma 2.5

Assume that
E/K
satisfies the HWC and the BSD conjecture, and that

r_{E/K}\ll_{n,E}\frac{\log|{\rm Disc}(K)|}{\log\log|{\rm Disc}(K)|}.

Next, we obtain a lower bound on the regulator depending on the rank of
E/K
:

Lemma 2.6

With notation as above, we have

R_{E/K}\gg_{n,E}\Bigl(\frac{\log|{\rm Disc}(K)|}{r_{E/K}}\Bigr)^{r_{E/K}}.

Proof:

Let
P=(x,y)\in E(K)
be a rational point. Let
F={\mathbb{Q}}(x,y)
be the subfield of

generated by

and

. We know that the canonical height
\hat{h}(P)
of

of the Weil height of

. Since

. Moreover, by Lemma

\ll_{n,E}\log|{\rm Disc}(F)|

\Box

Remark 2.7

We note that sophisticated lower bounds for the canonical heights of nontorsion points in elliptic curves

\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{L(E/K,s)}{(s-1)^{r_{E/K}}}\ll_{E,\epsilon}|{\rm Disc}(K)|^{1/2-\eta+\epsilon}.

Proof:

Denote

L^{(r)}(E/K,1)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|s|=\theta}\frac{L(E/K,1+s)}{s^{r+1}}ds

for every

L^{(r)}(E/K,1)\ll_{E,\epsilon}|{\rm Disc}(K)|^{\frac{1}{2}-\eta+\epsilon},

(3)

as necessary.

3.1
Cohomology of finite Galois modules over number fields

Let
A
be a finite Galois module over a field
F
, i.e., a finite
abelian group
A
, along with an action of the absolute Galois group

G_{F}
of
F
on
A
. We let
H^{i}(F,A)
denote the
i
’th cohomology
group arising from this action.
Now let
A
be a finite Galois module over a local field
K_{v}
(with residue field denoted
{\mathbb{F}}_{v}
). Let
I_{v}
denote
the intertia subgroup of
G_{K_{v}}
. We say that
A
is
unramified
if
I_{v}
acts trivially on
A
. When
A
is unramified,
there is a natural action of
G_{K_{v}}/I_{p}\cong G_{{\mathbb{F}}_{v}}
on
A
, and
we have an injection

H^{1}({\mathbb{F}}_{v},A)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(K_{v},A).

Define the subgroup of unramified cohomology classes by

H^{1}_{\rm ur}(K_{v},A):=\ker\bigl(H^{1}(K_{v},A)\to H^{1}(I_{v},A)\bigr).

When
A
is unramified, it follows from the inflation-restriction
exact sequence that the subgroup of unramified cohomology classes is
exactly given by the image of
H^{1}({\mathbb{F}}_{v},A)
(see
[
18
, Lemma
3.2]
).

Next, let
A
be a finite Galois module over a number field
K
. Let
v
be a place of
K
. The absolute Galois
group
G_{K_{v}}
of
K_{v}
naturally injects into
G_{K}
, yielding natural maps
H^{1}(K,A)\to H^{1}(K_{v},A)
. When
v
is a finite place, we say that
A
is
unramified at
v
if
A
is unramified as a
K_{v}
-Galois module. We define the class group
{\rm Cl}(A)
of
A
to
be the set of elements
\sigma\in H^{1}(K,A)
such that for every
v

at which
A
is unramified, the image of
\sigma
in
H^{1}(K_{v},A)

is an unramified cohomology class. That is

{\rm Cl}(A):=\ker\Bigl(H^{1}(K,A)\to\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{v}H^{1}(K_{v},A)/H_{\rm ur}^{1}(K_{v},A)\Bigr),

where the product is over all finite places
v
at which
A
is unramified.

3.2
Selmer groups of elliptic curves

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a field
F
and let
m
be a
positive integer. We have the exact sequence

0\to E[m]\to E\to E\to 0,

of
G_{F}
-modules, where

E[m]
is the
m
-torsion subgroup of
E
. This gives rise to the
following exact sequence of Galois cohomology groups:

0\to E(F)/mE(F)\to H^{1}(F,E[m])\to H^{1}(F,E)\to 0.

(4)

We now specialize to the arithmetic case. Let
E
be an elliptic curve
over a number field
K
. We have the exact sequence (
4
) for the fields

F=K
and
F=K_{v}
for all completions
K_{v}
of
K
.
The
m
-Selmer
group

\Sel_{m}(E)
is then defined to be

\Sel_{m}(E):=\ker\Bigl(H^{1}(K,E[m])\to\prod_{v}H^{1}(K_{v},E)\Bigr).

(5)

Above, the product is over all places
v
of
K
, and the map

H^{1}(K,E[m])\to H^{1}(K_{v},E)
is given by composing the map

H^{1}(K,E[m])\to H^{1}(K_{v},E[m])
with the map
H^{1}(K_{v},E[m])\to H^{1}(K_{v},E)
of (
4
).

Our next results compare the
m
-Selmer group of
E
with the class group of the finite Galois module
E[m]
. We start with
the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
, and let
m>1
be an
integer. Let
v
be a place not dividing
m
at which
E[m]
is
unramified and at which
E
has good reduction. Then

\ker\left(H^{1}(K_{v},E[m])\to H^{1}(K_{v},E)\right)=H_{\rm ur}^{1}(K_{v},E[m]).

That is, the image of a class
\sigma\in H^{1}(K_{v},E[m])
in

H^{1}(K_{v},E)
is soluble at
p
if and only if it is unramified.

Proof:

This is precisely the content of
[
4
, Lemma 19.3]
.

\Box

Proposition 3.2

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
of degree
n
, and let
m>1
. Let
s
be the number of places of
K
dividing
m\Delta(E)
. Then we have

\log\Bigl(\frac{\#\Sel_{m}(E)}{\#{\rm Cl}(E[m])}\Bigr)=O_{m,n}(s).

Proof:

Both the groups
\Sel_{m}(E)
and
{\rm Cl}(E[m])
are subgroups of the Galois cohomology group
H^{1}(K,E[m])
. The Galois module
E[m]
is unramified away from the prime dividing
m\Delta(E)
. It follows from Lemma
3.1
that the Selmer structures defining
\Sel_{m}(E)
and
{\rm Cl}(E[m])
differ at most at
s+n
‘bad’ local places (the
n
coming from the infinite place of
K
). Thus
\log\frac{\#\Sel_{m}(E)}{\#{\rm Cl}(E[m])}
is bounded by
{\rm ln}\prod_{v\textrm{ bad}}|H^{1}(K_{v},E[m])|
. We are thus finished if we prove that
|H^{1}(K_{v},E[m])|=O_{m,n}(1)
.

Let
N
denote the subgroup of
G_{K_{v}}
which fixes
E[m]
. Then by the inflation-restriction exact sequence, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|H^{1}(K_{v},E[m])|&\leq&|H^{1}(G/N,E[m])|\cdot|H^{1}(N,E[m])|\\[3.61371pt]
&=&|H^{1}(G/N,E[m])|\cdot|{\rm Hom}(N,E[m]|.\end{array}

The first term in the second line of the above equation is
O_{m,n}(1)
by virtue of having finitely many possibilities for
G/N
and its action. On the other hand
|{\rm Hom}(N,E[m])|=|N[m]|
is bounded by
m
times the number of degree
m
extensions of
L=\overline{K_{v}}^{N}
and hence by the number of degree
\leq d
extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, where
d=mn\cdot\#{\rm Aut}E[m]
. This is well-known to be uniformly bounded.

\Box

3.3
Class groups of number fields

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
m>1
be a (fixed) integer. Class field theory provides a bijection between index-
m
subgroups of
{\rm Cl}(K)
and degree-
m
abelian extensions of
K
which are unramified at every place. Let
v
be a place of
K
. The set of degree-
m
abelian extensions of
K
(resp.
K_{v}
) is parametrized by
H^{1}(K,{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
(resp.
H^{1}(K_{v},{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
). Let
L
be the extension of
K
corresponding to
\sigma\in H^{1}(K,\mu_{m})
. Then
L_{v}:=L\otimes_{K}K_{v}
corresponds to the image of
\sigma
under the natural map
H^{1}(K,{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H^{1}(K_{v},{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Furthermore, the extension
L_{v}
of
K_{v}
is unramified if and only if it corresponds to an unramified class in
H^{1}(K_{v},{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
. Therefore, the size of the
m
-torsion of the class group of
K
is
equal to the size of
H^{1}_{\rm ur}(K,{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})={\rm Cl}({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Meanwhile, we have the isomorphism
H^{1}(K,\mu_{m})\cong K^{\times}/(K^{\times})^{m}
and, for a finite place
v
of
K
not dividing
m
, a class in

Critical phase dualities in 1D exactly-solvable quasiperiodic models
Miguel Gonçalves, Bruno Amorim, Eduardo V. Castro, Pedro Ribeiro

Abstract
We propose a solvable class of 1D quasiperiodic tight-binding models encompassing extended, localized, and critical phases, separated by nontrivial mobility edges. Limiting cases include the Aubry-André model and the models of PRL 114, 146601 and PRL 104, 070601. The analytical treatment follows from recognizing these models as a novel type of fixed-points of the renormalization group procedure recently proposed in arXiv:2206.13549 for characterizing phases of quasiperiodic structures. Beyond known limits, the proposed class of models extends previously encountered localized-delocalized duality transformations to points within multifractal critical phases. Besides an experimental confirmation of multifractal duality, realizing the proposed class of models in optical lattices allows stabilizing multifractal critical phases and non-trivial mobility edges without the need for the unbounded potentials required by previous proposals.

As the existence of energy-dependent critical-to-extended or critical-to-localized
transitions has not been experimentally reported so far, more models
with such physics, no need for diverging potentials, and with analytically
exact phase diagrams, are of topical and practical interest for experimental
implementations.

Here, we propose a class of 1D quasiperiodic tight binding models
that includes extended, localized and critical phases and determine
its phase diagram analytically in the thermodynamic limit. Physically
motivated by previous experimentally realizations in optical lattices,
our models contain exponentially decaying hoppings and quasiperiodic
harmonics, with a tunable decay length. As limiting cases, this class
contains the Aubry-André model and the model in Ref.
(
Ganeshan et al. 2015

Roati et al. 2008

An et al. 2021
)
,
and the model in Ref.
(
Biddle and Das
Sarma 2010
)
. Away from
these limits, our class of models contains novel features, not found
in any of the limiting cases: critical phases that extend over a considerable
region of parameters and energy-dependent transitions between critical
and extended or localized phases.

Model and methods.—

We consider a family of models parameterized by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle t\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}e^{i\alpha(n-n^{\prime})}e^{-p|n-n^{\prime}|}c_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n^{\prime}}

(1)

\displaystyle+2V\sum_{n}\sum_{l=1}^{+\infty}e^{-ql}\cos[l(2\pi\tau n+\phi)]c_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}

where
c_{n}^{\dagger}
creates a particle at site
n
. The first
term describes hoppings modulated by a magnetic flux
\alpha
with
an exponential decay determined by
p
. The second term represents
a quasiperiodic potential, incommensurate with the lattice for
\tau\neq\mathbb{Q}
,
obtained by summing harmonics of the incommensurate wavenumber
2\pi\tau

with exponentially decaying amplitudes controlled by the parameter

q
. In the following we set
t=1
unless otherwise stated. The
model in Eq. (
1
) reduces to that in
(
Biddle and Das
Sarma 2010
)

in the
q\to\infty
limit and
\alpha=0
after the replacing
t\rightarrow te^{p}

and
V\rightarrow Ve^{q}
. Similarly, it reduces to the model in

(
Ganeshan et al. 2015
)
for large
p
, and to the Aubry-André
model when both
p
and
q
are large.

We consider finite systems with
L
sites. In order to avoid boundary
defects, we consider rational approximants of the irrational parameter

\tau
. We chose
1/\tau
as the golden ratio in the numerical calculations,
but our analytical results for the phase diagram are independent of

\tau
. The rational approximants are written as
\tau_{c}^{(n)}=F_{n-1}/F_{n}
,
where
F_{n}
is a Fibonacci number defining the number of sites

L
in the unit cell, with
L=F_{n}

(
Azbel 1979
;
Kohmoto 1983
)
.
We impose twisted boundary conditions, with phase twists
k
which
is the same as working in a fixed momentum sector of the Hamiltonian
in the Bloch basis defined as
c_{n}\rightarrow c_{m,r}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{k}e^{ik(m+rL)}\tilde{c}_{m,k}
,
where
m=0,\cdots,L-1
runs over the
L
sites of the unit cell,
and
r=0,\cdots,N
is the unit cell index, with
N\rightarrow\infty

the total number of unit cells. The Hamiltonian for a fixed
k
-sector
becomes

\begin{aligned} H(k)=&t\sum_{r=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=0}^{L-1}e^{-p|rL+m-m^{\prime}|}e^{i(\alpha-k)(m+rL-m^{\prime})}\tilde{c}_{m,k}^{\dagger}\tilde{c}_{m^{\prime},k}\\
&+2V\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}\sum_{l=1}^{+\infty}e^{-ql}\cos[l(2\pi\tau_{c}m+\phi)]\tilde{c}_{m,k}^{\dagger}\tilde{c}_{m,k}\end{aligned}.

(2)

which is just the Hamiltonian of a system with
L
sites and a phase
twist
k
. For the analytical calculations, we study commensurate
approximants (CA) defined by
\tau_{c}=L^{\prime}/L
, where
L^{\prime}
and
L

are co-prime integers, in the
L\rightarrow\infty
limit (infinite
unit cell size/quasiperiodic limit). In particular, we use the methods
introduced in Ref.
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022a
)
and an exact generalized
duality that we prove below.

Our analytical results are confirmed numerically through the real-space
and momentum-space inverse participation ratios, respectively
{\rm IPR}

and
{\rm IPR}_{k}
. For an eigenstate
\left|\psi(E)\right\rangle=\sum_{n}\psi_{n}(E)\left|n\right\rangle
,
where
\{\left|n\right\rangle\}
is a basis localized at each site, these quantities
are defined as
{\rm IPR}_{(k)}(E)=(\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}^{(k)}(E)|^{2})^{-2}\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}^{(k)}(E)|^{4}

(
Aulbach et al. 2004
)
, where
\psi_{n}^{k}(E)
are the amplitudes
of the discrete Fourier transform of the set
\left\{\psi_{n}(E)\right\}
.
In the extended phase, the
{\rm IPR}
scales as
L^{-1}
and
{\rm IPR}_{k}

is
L
-independent, while in the localized phase, the
{\rm IPR}_{k}

scales as
L^{-1}
while the
{\rm IPR}
is
L
-independent (for
large enough
L
). At a critical point or critical phase, the wave
function is multifractal: it is delocalized in real and momentum-space
and both the
{\rm IPR}
and
{\rm IPR}_{k}
scale down with
L

(
Aulbach et al. 2004
)
.

Exact duality.—

The Schrödinger equation for the model in Eq. (
2
)
with phase twists
k
can be written as

h_{n}u_{n}-\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(\alpha-k)(n-m)}e^{-p|n-m|}u_{m}=0\,,

(3)

where
h_{n}=\eta-V\chi(q,2\pi\tau n+\phi)
,
\eta=E+t+V
and
\chi(\lambda,x)=\sum_{l}e^{-\lambda|l|}e^{ilx}=\sinh(\lambda)[\cosh(\lambda)-\cos(x)]^{-1}
.
At dual points
P(t,V,p,q,\alpha,E;\phi,k)
and
P^{\prime}(t^{\prime},V^{\prime},p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},E^{\prime};\phi^{\prime},k^{\prime})

this equation can be mapped into a dual equation under the duality
transformation (see
(
SM
)
for proof):

\tilde{u}_{n}=\sum_{m}e^{i2\pi\tau nm}W(2\pi\tau m)u_{m},

(4)

where
W(x)=\chi(q^{\prime},x+\phi^{\prime})\chi^{-1}(p,x+k-\alpha)
. The dual points

P
and
P^{\prime}
satisfy

\begin{cases}\phi^{\prime}=k-\alpha+\pi\frac{(s-1)}{2},\textrm{ }-k^{\prime}+\alpha^{\prime}=\phi+\pi\frac{(s-1)}{2}\\
\frac{D(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=s\frac{D(V,\eta,p,q)}{A(V,\eta,q)};\textrm{ }\frac{A(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=\frac{B(V,\eta,p)}{A(V,\eta,q)}\\
\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=s\frac{\eta}{A(V,\eta,q)}\end{cases}

(5)

where

and

\displaystyle A(V,\eta,q)=

\displaystyle-\eta\cosh q+V\sinh q

(6)

\displaystyle B(V,\eta,p)=

\displaystyle-\eta\cosh p+t\sinh p

\displaystyle D(V,\eta,p,q)=

\displaystyle\eta\cosh p\cosh q-t\cosh q\sinh p

\displaystyle-V\cosh p\sinh q\,.

E=\frac{V\sinh q\mp t\sinh p}{\cosh q\mp\cosh p}-t-V.

(7)

Phase diagram.—

We now analytically obtain the complete phase diagram. The transitions
between extended and localized phases obtained through the
{\rm IPR/IPR_{k}}

calculations perfectly match the SD points described by Eq. (
7
).
Examples are shown in Fig.
1
(a) for
p=q
, when
Eq. (
7
) reduces to the Aubry-André energy independent
SD line
V=t
, and Fig.
3
(a,b)
for
p\neq q
. However, the SD points can also occur within the critical
phase, in which case they are not associated with any transition.
This implies that the phase boundaries of the critical phase are not
described by SD points.

To obtain the full phase diagram analytically we make use of the renormalization-group
approach developed in Ref.
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022a
)
. In fact, the
model studied here is a fixed-point model according to the classification
in
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022a
)
. Its characteristic polynomial
\mathcal{P}_{L}\left(\varphi,\kappa\right)\equiv\det[H_{L}(\varphi,\kappa)-E]
,
with
H_{L}(\varphi,\kappa)
the Hamiltonian for a CA with
L
sites,
is (see
(
SM
)
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{L}(\varphi,\kappa)=V_{L}\cos(\varphi)+t_{L}\cos(\kappa)+C_{L}\cos(\varphi)\cos(\kappa)+D_{L}

(8)

where
\varphi=L\phi
,
\kappa=Lk
and
V_{L},t_{L},C_{L}
and

D_{L}
are renormalized couplings. For the simplest CA (one site
per unit cell), we have, using the definitions in Eq. (
6
),
that
t_{1}=A(V,\eta,q)
,
V_{1}=B(V,\eta,p)
,
C_{1}=\eta
and

D_{1}=D(V,\eta,p,q)
. The ratios between the renormalized couplings

V_{L},t_{L}
and
C_{L}
can be computed exactly. If
|t_{1}/C_{1}|>1

or
|V_{1}/C_{1}|>1
, we have, respectively

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{t_{L}}{C_{L}}\Big|=

\displaystyle\frac{\Big|g_{L}^{+}\Big(\frac{t_{1}}{C_{1}}\Big)+g_{L}^{-}\Big(\frac{t_{1}}{C_{1}}\Big)\Big|}{2}\textrm{ },\textrm{ }\Big|\frac{V_{L}}{C_{L}}\Big|=\frac{\Big|g_{L}^{+}\Big(\frac{V_{1}}{C_{1}}\Big)+g_{L}^{-}\Big(\frac{V_{1}}{C_{1}}\Big)\Big|}{2}

(9)

where
g_{L}^{\pm}(x)=\Big(x\pm\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\Big)^{L}
. On the
other hand, if
|C_{1}/t_{1}|>1
or
|C_{1}/V_{1}|>1
we have, respectively

|t_{L}/C_{L}|=|T_{L}(t_{1}/C_{1})|;\textrm{ }|V_{L}/C_{L}|=|T_{L}(V_{1}/C_{1})|,

(10)

where
T_{L}(x)
is the
L
-th order Chebyshev polynomial. It is
easy to see that if
|t_{1}/V_{1}|,|t_{1}/C_{1}|>1
we have that

|t_{L}/V_{L}|,|t_{L}/C_{L}|\rightarrow\infty
exponentially in
L

as
L\rightarrow\infty
, i.e. we are in the extended phase. For
|V_{1}/t_{1}|,|V_{1}/C_{1}|>1
,
we have
|V_{L}/t_{L}|,|V_{L}/C_{L}|\rightarrow\infty
and the phase
is localized. Finally,
|C_{1}/t_{1}|,|C_{1}/V_{1}|>1
ensures that

|C_{L}/t_{L}|,|C_{L}/V_{L}|>1
for any
L
(a property of Chebyshev
polynomials), and the system is in a critical phase. Therefore, the
phases and phase boundaries are fully determined through the previous
conditions by knowing the functions in Eq. (
6
).
Summarizing, phases and phase boundaries are analytically given by

\displaystyle|A/B|,|A/\eta|>1

\displaystyle\textrm{, Ext.}

(11)

\displaystyle|B/A|,|B/\eta|>1

\displaystyle\textrm{, Loc.}

\displaystyle|\eta/A|,|\eta/B|>1

\displaystyle\textrm{, Crit.}

\displaystyle|A|=|B|,\textrm{ }|A|,|B|>|\eta|

\displaystyle\textrm{, Ext.-to-Loc.}

(12)

\displaystyle|A|=|\eta|,\textrm{ }|A|,|\eta|>|B|

\displaystyle\textrm{, Crit.-to-Ext.}

\displaystyle|B|=|\eta|,\textrm{ }|B|,|\eta|>|A|

\displaystyle\textrm{, Crit.-to-Loc.}

where we omitted the parameter dependence for clarity. From the ratios

and

\eta

)

we also carried out a multifractal analysis at some
points in the critical phase to show the non-linear behaviour of the
fractal dimension that characterizes multifractal phases
(
Janssen 2004
)
.

Discussion.—

We analytically obtained the phase diagram of the richest family of
1D quasiperiodic solvable models, to our knowledge, hosting (i) critical
multifractal phases in addition to localized and extended ones, and
energy-dependent transitions between all these phases; and (ii) a
rich generalized duality symmetry that includes dualities inside the
critical phase.

From a practical perspective, the family of models we propose can
be experimentally realized with currently available techniques. The
model in
(
Ganeshan et al. 2015
)
have exactly the quasiperiodic
potential considered here and was already experimentally realized
using a synthetic lattice of laser-coupled momentum modes
(
An et al. 2021
)
.
Our model simply requires additional longer-range hoppings (but still
exponentially-decaying), a possibility put forward in
(
Gadway 2015
)
.
It can also be simulated in conventional optical lattices, where the
exponential hopping decay rate can be directly estimated
(
Biddle and Das
Sarma 2010
)
.
A single incommensurate potential (our large
q
limit) was already
realized in optical lattices by applying a second laser beam with
a wave vector
\tau
that is incommensurate with that of the primary
lattice. Additional quasiperiodic harmonics can be introduced by adding
new laser beams with wave vectors that are multiples of
\tau
, as
proposed in
(
Ganeshan et al. 2015
)
. The engineering of optical
lattices with kicked kinetic energy or quasiperiodic potential is
also a possible way to implement our model, as proposed in
(
Liu et al. 2022
)
.
An advantage of our model is that the critical multifractal phase
can be realized without the need of unbounded potentials. We also
note that the existence of exact dualities has direct experimental
relevance: critical-extended transitions are dual of critical-localized
transitions, implying that the detailed experimental characterization
of one transition can give us information on both. The impact of interactions
on the phase diagram of this model is an interesting question for
future research.

S1
Derivation of generalized global duality transformation

Our starting point is the Schrödinger equation

h_{n}u_{n}=\sum_{m}e^{i(\alpha-k)(n-m)}f(|n-m|)u_{m},

(S1)

where
h_{n}=\eta-V\chi_{n}(q,\phi)
,
\eta=E+t+V
,
f(|n-m|)=te^{-p|n-m|}

and
\chi_{m}(q,\phi)=\frac{\sinh q}{\cosh q-\cos(2\pi\tau m+\phi)}
.

k
is a phase twist while
\alpha
was chosen to be a parameter
of the model. From here on we will absorb the parameter
\alpha

in the twist
k
, that is
k-\alpha\rightarrow k
. In what follows
we will also use the useful identity

\chi_{m}(q,\phi)=\frac{\sinh q}{\cosh q-\cos(2\pi\tau m+\phi)}=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-q|l|}e^{i\left(2\pi\tau m+\phi\right)l}.

(S2)

In Ref.
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022b
)
we have seen that generic duality
transformations for Aubry-André-like systems may be defined as

\tilde{u}_{n}=\sum_{m}e^{i2\pi\tau nm}W_{m}u_{m}.

(S3)

Here we will find the form of
W_{m}
for which an exact global duality
of the type in Eq. (
S3
) can be defined. The
inverse transformation is

u_{m}=\frac{1}{N}W_{m}^{-1}\sum_{n}e^{-i2\pi\tau nm}\tilde{u}_{n},

(S4)

where
N
is the total number of sites in the system. Writing Eq. (
S1
)
in terms of the dual wave function
\tilde{u}_{n}
, we have

\begin{aligned} h_{n}W_{n}^{-1}\sum_{m}e^{-i2\pi\tau nm}\tilde{u}_{m}=\sum_{m}e^{-ik(n-m)}f(|n-m|)W_{m}^{-1}\sum_{l}e^{-i2\pi\tau ml}\tilde{u}_{l}\end{aligned}.

(S5)

Multiplying by
e^{i2\pi\tau n\mu}
and summing over

, we get

\sum_{m}\Bigg[\sum_{n}e^{i2\pi\tau n(\mu-m)}W_{n}^{-1}\Big(h_{n}-\chi_{\mu}(p,-k)\Big)\Bigg]\tilde{u}_{m}=0.

(S6)

Our aim now is to find
W_{n}
such that Eq. (
S6
)
for some point
P(t,V,p,q,\alpha,E;\phi,k)

S1
)
at a dual point
P^{\prime}(t^{\prime},V^{\prime},p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},E^{\prime};\phi^{\prime},k^{\prime})

we write Eq. (
S1

\Lambda_{\mu}\Big(\sum_{m}[h_{m}\delta_{m,\mu}-e^{-ik(\mu-m)}e^{-p|\mu-m|}]u_{m}\Big)=0.

(S7)

Notice that
\Lambda_{\mu}
is an additional degree of freedom that
does not affect the solution of Eq. (
S1

we want to inspect when equations (
S1
)
and (
S6

\Lambda_{k}

Imposing an equality for these equations term by term, we have

\Lambda_{\mu}\Big(h_{m}\delta_{m,\mu}-e^{-ik(\mu-m)}e^{-p|\mu-m|}\Big)=\sum_{n}e^{i2\pi\tau n(\mu-m)}W_{n}^{-1}\Big(h_{n}^{\prime}-\chi_{\mu}(p^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\Big).

(S8)

From Eq. (
S8
), we obtain

W_{l}=\gamma_{\mu}\frac{h_{l}^{\prime}-\chi_{\mu}(p^{\prime},-k^{\prime})}{h_{\mu}-\chi_{l}(p,k)}=\gamma_{\mu}\frac{\eta^{\prime}-V^{\prime}\chi_{l}(q^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})-\chi_{\mu}(p^{\prime},-k^{\prime})}{\eta-V\chi_{\mu}(q,\phi)-\chi_{l}(p,k)}\equiv\gamma_{\mu}P_{\mu l},

(S9)

where
\gamma_{\mu}=\Lambda_{\mu}^{-1}

is a tensor. This means that
W_{l}
is only well defined, that is,
a duality transformation of the type in Eq. (
S3
)
only exists, if we can write
P_{\mu l}=p_{\mu}W_{l}

\gamma_{\mu}

can be chosen as
p_{\mu}^{-1}

Defining
c_{\mu,k}=\cos(2\pi\tau\mu+k)
and
c_{\mu,\phi}=\cos(2\pi\tau\mu+\phi)

we have

\displaystyle W_{l}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mu}\Bigg(\frac{c_{\mu,\phi}-\cosh q}{c_{\mu,-k^{\prime}}-\cosh p^{\prime}}\Bigg)\Bigg(\frac{c_{l,k}-\cosh p}{c_{l,\phi^{\prime}}-\cosh q^{\prime}}\Bigg)\frac{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}{A(V,\eta,q)}\Bigg(\frac{\frac{D(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}+\frac{A(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}c_{\mu,-k^{\prime}}+c_{l,\phi^{\prime}}+\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}c_{\mu,-k^{\prime}}c_{l,\phi^{\prime}}}{\frac{D(V,\eta,p,q)}{A(V,\eta,q)}+\frac{B(V,\eta,p)}{A(V,\eta,q)}c_{\mu,\phi}+c_{l,k}+\frac{\eta}{A(V,\eta,q)}c_{\mu,\phi}c_{l,k}}\Bigg)

(S10)

where, as in the main text, we have

\begin{aligned} A(V,\eta,q)=&-\eta\cosh q+V\sinh q\\
B(V,\eta,p)=&-\eta\cosh p+\sinh p\\
D(V,\eta,p,q)=&\eta\cosh p\cosh q-\cosh q\sinh p-V\cosh p\sinh q\end{aligned}.

(S11)

The last term in Eq. (

\mu
and

if we require the numerator and denominator to be equal, this term

\begin{cases}k^{\prime}=-\phi;{\rm\textrm{ }}\phi^{\prime}=k\\
\frac{D(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=\frac{D(V,\eta,p,q)}{A(V,\eta,q)}\\
\frac{A(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=\frac{B(V,\eta,p)}{A(V,\eta,q)}\\
\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=\frac{\eta}{A(V,\eta,q)}\end{cases}.

(S12)

The choice

\begin{cases}k^{\prime}=-\phi+\pi;\textrm{ }\phi^{\prime}=k+\pi\\
\frac{D(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=-\frac{D(V,\eta,p,q)}{A(V,\eta,q)}\\
\frac{A(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},q^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=\frac{B(V,\eta,p)}{A(V,\eta,q)}\\
\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})}=-\frac{\eta}{A(V,\eta,q)}\end{cases}

Alternatively, we could choose

\phi^{\prime}=k

Finally, if we choose

\gamma_{\mu}=\frac{A(V,\eta,q)\sinh q(c_{\mu,-k^{\prime}}-\cosh p^{\prime})}{B(V^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},p^{\prime})\sinh p(c_{\mu,\phi}-\cosh q)},

(S14)

we get, from Eq. (

The dual points can also be obtained from Eqs. (

S2
Details on the calculation of local dualities using commensurate

To compute the data points used in Fig. 2(b),

Using the definition of dual points in Ref.

\tau_{c}=L^{\prime}/L

u_{n}^{\text{d}}(P)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}mn}u_{n}^{\text{r}}(P).

\mathcal{O}_{c}[T^{n}u^{\text{d}}(P)]=T^{n}u^{\text{r}}(P^{\prime}),\hskip 10.00002ptn=0,\cdots,L-1,

\psi^{\prime}_{i}=\psi_{\mod(i+1,L)}

\mathcal{O}_{c}=U^{\dagger}W^{\prime}U

u^{\text{r}}(P^{\prime})=U^{\dagger}W^{\prime}u^{\text{r}}(P)\leftrightarrow u_{\mu}^{\text{r}}(P^{\prime})=\sum_{\nu=0}^{L-1}e^{2\pi i\tau_{c}\mu\nu}w^{\prime}_{\nu}u_{\nu}^{\text{r}}(P).

(S19)

The eigenvalues

are, as seen in Ref.
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022b

evaluations of a function

.
This function is continuously sampled in the limit that
\tau_{c}\rightarrow\tau
,
that is, as

. At global dual points defined by
Eq. 5 of the main text, the exact duality
transformation is given by

\tilde{u}_{\mu}(P^{\prime})=\sum_{\nu}e^{i2\pi\tau\mu\nu}W(\tau\nu)u_{\nu}(P)

(S20)

where
W(x)=\chi(q^{\prime},x)\chi^{-1}(p,x)

\chi(\lambda,x)=\sinh\lambda[\cosh\lambda-\cos(2\pi x)]^{-1}
.
In the commensurate limit, replacing
\tau\rightarrow\tau_{c}

Eq. (
S20

w^{\prime}_{\nu}=W^{\prime}(\tau_{c}\nu)\propto W(\tau_{c}\nu).

(S21)

The duality function
W(x)
can therefore be sampled at
L
different
points by computing the eigenvalues

for CA with
L

in the unit cell. They were computed and shown as data points in Fig. 2(b)

to confirm the validity of the latter.

In the case of local dualities, the exact duality function is unknown
and the only way to access it is by computing the eigenvalues

at (locally) dual points. In this case, dual points were computed
numerically as explained in Ref.
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022b
)
. As shown
in one of the examples in Fig. 2(b)
of the main text, the duality function for the local dualities can
be highly non-trivial.

We finish by remarking that it is important to precisely compute the
dual points to get a meaningful duality function. While for the global
dualities the dual points can be calculated exactly, this is not the
case for the local dualities for which there are unavoidable numerical

S1
we show an example
where it can be seen that even a small numerical error in the calculation

we must therefore slightly change the dual point with respect to the

W^{\prime}(x)
. In this
example we checked that the dual point was miscalculated with an error

to present the results of Fig. 2(b)
of the main text.

Figure S1:

obtained by linear interpolation of the eigenvalues

\tau_{c}=89/144

S3
Derivation of ratios between renormalized couplings

Our starting point is Eq. (
S1
)
for a commensurate system defined by
\tau=\tau_{c}=L^{\prime}/L
, where

L^{\prime}
and
L
two co-prime integers and
L
defines the number of
sites in the unit cell. In this case, we have
h_{n+rL}=h_{n}
and
Bloch’s theorem warrants
u_{n+rL}=u_{n},n=0,\cdots,L-1,{\rm}r\in\mathbb{Z}

We start by considering the example of one site per unit cell, that
is,
\tau_{c}=1
. In this case, the Schrödinger equation becomes

\left[h_{0}-t\sum_{m}e^{ikm}f(|m|)\right]u_{0}=0,

which can be written as

\left[\eta-V\chi_{0}(q,\phi)-t\chi_{0}(p,k)\right]u_{0}=0,

with
\chi_{0}
given by Eq. (
S2

[\cosh q-\cos(\phi)][\cosh p-\cos(k)]\neq 0

\left[A(V,\eta,q)\cos(k)+B(V,\eta,p)\cos(\phi)+\eta\cos(\phi)\cos(k)+D(V,\eta,p,q)\right]=0.

(S22)

Equation. (
S22
) defines the characteristic polynomial
for the simplest CA. To characterize higher-order CA, we start by
applying the transformation
\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}n\mu}=\frac{L}{N}\sum_{n}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}n\mu}
,
where
N
is the total number of sites in the system. The first term
in Eq. (
S1
) becomes

\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}n\mu}h_{n}u_{n}=\frac{L}{N}\sum_{m}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}m\mu}h_{m}u_{m},{\rm}\textrm{with }h_{m+rL}=h_{m},r\in\mathbb{Z}.

(S23)

For the second term we have (absorbing again
\alpha
in the phase
twist
k
):

\displaystyle\frac{L}{N}\sum_{n}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}n\mu}\sum_{m}e^{-ik(n-m)}f(|n-m|)u_{m}

(S24)

\displaystyle\textrm{Using $m^{\prime}=n-m$:}

\displaystyle\frac{L}{N}\sum_{m^{\prime}}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}\mu m^{\prime}}e^{-ikm^{\prime}}f(|m^{\prime}|)\sum_{m}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}m\mu}u_{m}=\frac{L}{N}\chi(p,2\pi\tau_{c}\mu-k)\sum_{m}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}m\mu}u_{m},

where we used Eq. (
S2

\chi(\lambda,x)\equiv\chi_{0}(\lambda,x)

at a discrete set of points

Combining Eqs. (
S23

S24

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{m}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}m\mu}[h_{m}-\chi(p,2\pi\tau_{c}\mu-k)]u_{m}

\displaystyle=0

(S25)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\sum_{m}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}m\mu}\Bigg(\frac{A(V,\eta,q)c_{\mu,-k}+B(V,\eta,p)c_{m,\phi}+\eta c_{m,\phi}c_{\mu,-k}+D(V,\eta,p,q)}{[\cosh q-c_{m,\phi}][\cosh p-c_{\mu,-k}]}\Bigg)u_{m}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow M\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0

where we used the short-hand notation
c_{m,\phi}=\cos(2\pi\tau_{c}m+\phi)

and
c_{\mu,k}=\cos(2\pi\tau_{c}\mu+k)
and
\bm{u}=(u_{0},\cdots,u_{L-1})
.
Note that each component
M_{\mu m}
is now written in a form that
resembles Eq. (
S22

we essentially made appear the characteristic polynomial for this

\phi

is associated with index

and the phase
-k

\mu
.

We will now compute the
\phi

of matrix

the ratios between renormalized couplings
|t_{L}/C_{L}|,|V_{L}/C_{L}|

and
|t_{L}/V_{L}|
, where these couplings are defined, for the characteristic
polynomial of a CA with
L
sites in the unit cell,
\mathcal{P}_{L}(\varphi,\kappa)
,
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{L}(\varphi,\kappa)=

\displaystyle V_{L}\cos(\varphi)+t_{L}\cos(\kappa)+C_{L}\cos(\varphi)\cos(\kappa)+\cdots

(S26)

where
\varphi=L\phi
and
\kappa=Lk

explain why only the fundamental harmonics in

\kappa

We start by noting that the denominator of each term

T_{\mu\phi}T^{\prime}_{mk}=[\cosh q-c_{\mu,\phi}][\cosh p-c_{m,-k}]

The Leibniz formula for the determinant is

{\rm det}(M)=\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}M_{\mu,\sigma_{\mu}}

(S27)

S_{L}
is the set of permutations of indexes
i=1,\cdots,L
.
Therefore for each term in the sum we get all the possible combinations
of
T_{\mu\phi}T^{\prime}_{mk}
, that is if we write
M_{\mu m}=P_{\mu m}/(T_{\mu\phi}T^{\prime}_{mk})
,
we have

{\rm det}(M)=\frac{1}{\prod_{\mu}T_{\mu\phi}T^{\prime}_{\mu k}}\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}P_{\mu,\sigma_{\mu}}\propto\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}P_{\mu,\sigma_{\mu}}.

(S28)

Let us focus on the
|t_{L}/C_{L}|
ratio. We have

{\rm det}(M)\propto\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}\mu\sigma_{\mu}}\Bigg(\eta c_{\mu,-k}(\frac{A}{\eta}+c_{\sigma_{\mu},\phi})+Bc_{\sigma_{\mu},\phi}+D\Bigg).

(S29)

We first realize that only products of terms
\eta c_{\mu,-k}(A/\eta+c_{\sigma_{\mu},\phi})

matter to get the terms
t_{L}\cos(\kappa)+C_{L}\cos(\kappa)\cos(\varphi)

of
\mathcal{P}_{L}(\varphi,\kappa)
. This is because to obtain terms
with
L
times the original frequency
k
or
\phi
(recall the
definitions
\varphi=L\phi
and
\kappa=Lk
), we need to have
L

products of terms
c_{\mu,-k}
and
c_{\mu,\phi}
. The only way
to accomplish this is by multiplying
L
terms of the type
\eta c_{\mu,-k}(A/\eta+c_{\sigma_{\mu},\phi})
.
At this point, we can also ask why terms of the type
\cos(nx)
with

x=\phi,k
and
n\in\mathbb{N}<L
do not appear in
\mathcal{P}_{L}(\varphi,\kappa)
.
This would give rise to periodicities in
\phi
and
k
,
\Delta\phi,\Delta k>2\pi/L
,
that are forbidden. The twist
k
is nothing more than a Bloch momentum
associated with the unit cell of size
L
: the reciprocal lattice
vector for this unit cell is
G=2\pi/L
and therefore the energy
bands should repeat with a period
\Delta k=2\pi/L
. In the case
of phase
\phi
, shifts
\Delta\phi=2\pi/L
in a CA with a unit
cell with
L
sites are just re-definitions of this unit cell
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022b
)
.
The energy bands should therefore be periodic upon these shifts. Finally,
we can also ask why we cannot have terms of the type
\cos(nx)
with

x=\phi,k
and
n\in\mathbb{N}>L
. The reason is that such terms
would require a number of
n>L
products of terms
c_{\mu,-k}
and

c_{\mu,\phi}
that do not appear in the determinant in Eq.
S29
.
Therefore, we only have fundamental harmonics in
\varphi
and
\kappa
,
as previously stated.

To make further progress, we will need the following identity for

L,L^{\prime}
two co-prime integers
(
Inc.
)
,

\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}\Bigg[\cos(y)-\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi L^{\prime}\mu}{L}+\phi\Big)\Bigg]=2^{1-L}\Big[\cos(Ly)-\cos(L\phi)\Big],

(S30)

which we can apply to the term
\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}\mu\sigma_{\mu}}\eta c_{\mu,-k}(\frac{A}{\eta}+c_{\sigma_{\mu},\phi})

in Eq. (
S29
), as long as
|A/\eta|<1
(which occurs
inside the critical phase) by identifying
y=\arccos A/\eta
. We
then obtain

\displaystyle{\rm det}(M)\propto

\displaystyle\gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}\Big(-\cos(L\pi/2)+\cos(Lk)\Big)\Big(\cos[L\arccos(A/\eta)]-\cos(L\phi)\Big)+\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}\mu\sigma_{\mu}}\Big(Bc_{\sigma_{\mu},\phi}+D\Big)

(S31)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\cos[L\arccos(A/\eta)]\cos(Lk)-\cos(Lk)\cos(L\phi)+\cdots

where we identified

\gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}=\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}\mu\sigma_{\mu}}.

(S32)

We therefore have that

|t_{L}/C_{L}|=|\cos[L\arccos(A/\eta)]|=|T_{L}(A/\eta)|

(S33)

where
T_{L}(x)
is the Chebyshev polynomial of order
L
. It is
very interesting to realize that there is no well-defined limit of

|t_{L}/C_{L}|
for large
L
. In fact, even though we always have

|t_{L}/C_{L}|<1
, its value oscillates with
L
with a period that
becomes larger the closer
|A/\eta|
is to
1
(being infinity at

|A/\eta|=1
). Examples are shown in Fig.
S2
.

Figure S2:
t_{L}/C_{L}
for different
|A/\eta|<1
.

What about
|A|\geq|\eta|
? In this case, it is useful to use the
following property
(
Inc.
)
:

\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}\Bigg[x\pm y\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi L^{\prime}\mu}{L}+\phi\Big)\Bigg]=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\Bigg[\Bigg(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-y^{2}}\Bigg)^{L}+\Bigg(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-y^{2}}\Bigg)^{L}-2(\mp y)^{L}\cos(L\phi)\Bigg].

(S34)

This may again be applied to the term
\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in S_{L}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{\mu=0}^{L-1}e^{i2\pi\tau_{c}\mu\sigma_{\mu}}\eta c_{\mu,-k}(\frac{A}{\eta}+c_{\sigma_{\mu},\phi})

in Eq. (
S29
), by identifying
x=A/\eta
and
y=1
.
In this case we obtain

|C_{L}/t_{L}|=\frac{2}{\Bigg(|\frac{A}{\eta}|+\sqrt{(\frac{A}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}+\Bigg(|\frac{A}{\eta}|-\sqrt{(\frac{A}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}}.

(S35)

Note that with
|A/\eta|>1
this gives an exponential decay for
|C_{L}/t_{L}|
.
This is just what we expect in the extended phase, where the dominant
coupling is
t_{L}
, regarding that it also dominates over
V_{L}
.
At large
L
we have
|C_{L}/t_{L}|\sim e^{-L/\xi_{c}}
, and the
decay length
\xi_{{\rm EC}}
is

\begin{aligned} \xi_{{\rm EC}}=\frac{1}{\log\Bigg[|A/\eta|+\sqrt{(A/\eta)^{2}-1}\Bigg]}\end{aligned},

(S36)

which is finite for any
|A/\eta|>1
and of course diverges for
|A/\eta|=1

(transition between extended and critical phase).

With identical calculations, we can work out the expressions for
|C_{L}/V_{L}|

just by replacing
A
with
B
everywhere above. This yields

\begin{aligned} |V_{L}/C_{L}|=&\begin{cases}|T_{L}(B/\eta)|&,\textrm{ }|B/\eta|<1\\
\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\Bigg(|\frac{B}{\eta}|+\sqrt{(\frac{B}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}+\Bigg(|\frac{B}{\eta}|-\sqrt{(\frac{B}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}\Bigg]&,\textrm{ }|B/\eta|\geq 1\end{cases}\end{aligned}.

(S37)

For
|B/\eta|\geq 1
we get the following correlation length characterizing
the localized-critical transition:

\begin{aligned} \xi_{{\rm LC}}=\frac{1}{\log\Bigg[|B/\eta|+\sqrt{(B/\eta)^{2}-1}\Bigg]}\end{aligned}.

(S38)

The ratio
|t_{L}/V_{L}|
may then be obtained through the previous
expressions. For
|V_{L}/C_{L}|,|t_{L}/C_{L}|>1
(outside the critical
phase), we have

\begin{aligned} |t_{L}/V_{L}|=&\frac{\Bigg(|\frac{A}{\eta}|+\sqrt{(\frac{A}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}+\Bigg(|\frac{A}{\eta}|-\sqrt{(\frac{A}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}}{\Bigg(|\frac{B}{\eta}|+\sqrt{(\frac{B}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}+\Bigg(|\frac{B}{\eta}|-\sqrt{(\frac{B}{\eta})^{2}-1}\Bigg)^{L}}\end{aligned}.

(S39)

We have seen in Ref.
(
Gonçalves et al. 2022a
)
that the correlation
or localization length can be inferred by the scaling of the ratio

|t_{L}/V_{L}|
with
L
. We first assume that
|A|<|B|
(localized
phase). Taking the large
L
limit we obtain

\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned} |t_{L}/V_{L}|&=e^{-L/\xi_{{\rm LE}}},\textrm{ {with }$\xi_{{\rm LE}}=1/$}\log\left[\frac{|\frac{B}{\eta}|+\sqrt{(\frac{B}{\eta})^{2}-1}}{|\frac{A}{\eta}|+\sqrt{(\frac{A}{\eta})^{2}-1}}\right]\end{aligned}\end{aligned},

(S40)

where
\xi_{{\rm LE}}
is the localization length. We can also compute
the correlation length in the extended phase for
|A|>|B|
. This
can be done just by interchanging
A
and
B
in the expression
above, that is:

\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned} |V_{L}/t_{L}|&=e^{-L/\xi_{{\rm EL}}},\textrm{ {with }$\xi_{{\rm EL}}$=1/$\log\left[\frac{|\frac{A}{\eta}|+\sqrt{\left(\frac{A}{\eta}\right)^{2}-1}}{|\frac{B}{\eta}|+\sqrt{\left(\frac{B}{\eta}\right)^{2}-1}}\right]$}\end{aligned}\end{aligned},

(S41)

where
\xi_{{\rm EL}}
is the correlation length.

With all the ratios computed, we can summarize the different phases
as in the main text, with the phase diagram being entirely analytically
determined and only depending on
t_{1}\equiv A(V,\eta,q)
,
V_{1}\equiv B(V,\eta,p)
,

C_{1}\equiv\eta
.

We will finish this section by cross-checking our correlation length
results for the Aubry-André model. To get exactly the Hamiltonian
in the original paper
(
Aubry and André 1980
)
, we need to make
t\rightarrow te^{p}

and
V\rightarrow Ve^{q}/2
and then take the large
p
limit, with

p=q
. After doing so, we obtain

\begin{aligned} \frac{A}{\eta}=-\cosh p+\frac{Ve^{p}}{2\eta}\sinh p\approx\frac{Ve^{2p}}{4\eta}\\
\frac{B}{\eta}=-\cosh p+\frac{te^{p}}{\eta}\sinh p\approx\frac{te^{2p}}{2\eta}\end{aligned}.

(S42)

This implies that

\begin{aligned} \xi_{{\rm LE}}=\frac{1}{\log\Big(\frac{V}{2t}\Big)}\\
\xi_{{\rm EL}}=\frac{1}{\log\Big(\frac{2t}{V}\Big)}\end{aligned},

(S43)

which are exactly the correlation lengths previously derived in
(
Aubry and André 1980
)
.

S4
Multifractal analysis

In this section, we carry out a multifractal analysis for some points
within the critical phase. To do so, we compute the real- and momentum-space
generalized inverse participation ratios, respectively
{\rm IPR}(q)

and
{\rm IPR}_{k}(q)
. For an eigenstate
\left|\psi(E)\right\rangle=\sum_{n}\psi_{n}(E)\left|n\right\rangle
,
where
\{\left|n\right\rangle\}
is a basis localized at each site, these quantities
are defined as,

\displaystyle{\rm IPR}(q)=\Bigl(\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}|^{2}\Bigr)^{-1}\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}|^{2q}\propto L^{-\tau_{r}(q)}

(S44)

\displaystyle{\rm IPR}_{k}(q)=\Bigl(\sum_{m}|\psi_{m}^{k}|^{2}\Bigr)^{-1}\sum_{m}|\psi_{m}^{k}|^{2q}\propto L^{-\tau_{k}(q)}

where
\psi_{m}^{k}=L^{-1}\sum_{n}e^{2\pi imm}\psi_{n}
. The size
dependence is characterized by
q
-dependent exponents,
\tau_{r}

and
\tau_{k}
, defined in terms of the generalized fractal dimensions,

D_{r}(q)
and
D_{k}(q)
, as
\tau_{r/k}(q)=D_{r/k}(q)(q-1)
.
Fully extended (localized) states are characterized by
D_{k}(q)=0

(
D_{r}(q)=0
) for
q>0
, and
D_{r}(q)=d
(
D_{k}(q)=d
). In
these cases
D_{k}(q)
is constant, and the system is a single-fractal.
Multifractals correspond to cases where
D_{r}(q)
or
D_{k}(q)

depends on
q

(
Janssen 2004
)
. In Fig. (
S3
)
we show that within the critical phase, the generalized fractal dimensions
depend on
q
as expected in a phase with multifractal properties,
which can be inferred from the non-linear behaviour of
\tau_{r}

and
\tau_{k}
with
q
. The wave function is therefore a multifractal
in both real- and momentum-space.

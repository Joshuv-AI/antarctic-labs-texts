An H α \alpha Impression of Ly α \alpha Galaxies at z ≃ 6 z\simeq 6 with Deep JWST /NIRCam Imaging Journal: ApJL Facilities: HST (WFC3/IR), JWST (NIRCam)
Yuanhang Ning, Zheng Cai, Linhua Jiang, Xiaojing Lin, Shuqi Fu, Daniele Spinoso

Abstract
We present a study of seven spectroscopically confirmed (Ly α \alpha emitting) galaxies at redshift z ≃ 6 z\simeq 6 using the JWST /NIRCam imaging data. These galaxies, with a wide range of Ly α \alpha luminosities, were recently observed in a series of NIRCam broad- and medium-bands. We constrain the rest-frame UV/optical continua and measure the H α \alpha line emission of the galaxies using the combination of the JWST /NIRCam and archival HST /WFC3 infrared photometry. We further estimate their escape fractions of Ly α \alpha photons ( f esc Ly ​ α f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha} ) and the production efficiency of ionizing photons ( ξ ion \xi_{\rm ion} ). Among the sample, 6/7 galaxies have Ly α \alpha escape fractions of ≲ 10 % {\lesssim}10\% , which might be the status for most of star-forming galaxies at z ≃ 6 z\simeq 6 . One UV-faint Ly α \alpha galaxy with an extremely blue UV slope owns a large value of f esc Ly ​ α f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha} reaching ≃ 50 % {\simeq}50\% . These galaxies spread a broad range of ξ ion \xi_{\rm ion} over log 10 ξ ion , 0 \xi_{\rm ion,0} (Hz erg -1 ) ∼ 25.0 − 26.5 \sim 25.0-26.5 . We find that UV-fainter galaxies with bluer UV continuum slopes likely have higher escape fractions of Ly α \alpha photons. We also find that galaxies with higher Ly α \alpha line emission tend to produce ionizing photons more efficiently. The most Ly α \alpha -luminous galaxy in the sample has a very high ξ ion , 0 \xi_{\rm ion,0} of log 10 ξ ion , 0 \xi_{\rm ion,0} (Hz erg -1 ) > 26 >26 . Our results support that Ly α \alpha galaxies may have served as an important contributor to the cosmic reionization. Blue and bright Ly α \alpha galaxies are excellent targets for JWST follow-up spectroscopic observations.



I
Introduction

The
James Webb Space Telescope
(
JWST
;
Gardner et al. 2006
) has begun to explore the very distant Universe, allowing us to gain deep insight on the high-redshift (high-
z
) objects at the epoch of reionization (EoR). The major sources of reionization are presumably star-forming (SF) galaxies
(
Robertson et al. 2015
;
Finkelstein et al. 2019
;
Yung et al. 2020a
, e.g.,)
. This viewpoint has been conclusively demonstrated by
Jiang et al. 2022
. As a population of SF galaxies, Ly
\alpha
emitting galaxies are generally low-mass with low metallicity and dust content
(
Hao et al. 2018
;
Arrabal Haro et al. 2020
;
Santos et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. They should play a non-negligible role to drive the reionization because the processes responsible for the emission of Ly
\alpha
and Lyman continuum (LyC) photons relate to each other
(
Dijkstra 2014
;
Verhamme et al. 2015
;
de Barros et al. 2016
;
Dijkstra et al. 2016
, e.g.,)
while Ly
\alpha
escape fraction (
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
) generally exceeds LyC escape fraction
(
Izotov et al. 2016
;
Izotov et al. 2020
;
Leitherer et al. 2016
;
Shapley et al. 2016
;
Verhamme et al. 2017
;
Flury et al. 2022
,
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}
; e.g.,)
.

Large ground-based telescopes and
Hubble Space Telescope
(
HST
) helped us to find a large number of Ly
\alpha
-emitting galaxies with redshift reaching
z\gtrsim 6-7
, corresponding to the end of EoR. Most of them are Ly
\alpha
emitters (LAEs) selected by the narrowband (Ly
\alpha
) technique
(
Kashikawa et al. 2006
;
Kashikawa et al. 2011
;
Hu et al. 2010
;
Shibuya et al. 2018
;
Taylor et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
. The rest of them are Lyman-break galaxies (LBGs) selected by the dropout technique and identified by Ly
\alpha
lines
(
Steidel et al. 1996
;
Jones et al. 2012
;
Inami et al. 2017
;
Pentericci et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. The LAEs and LBGs (with Ly
\alpha
lines) are probably indistinguishable in terms of their intrinsic properties such as age, stellar mass, and star formation rate
(
Dayal & Ferrara 2012
;
Jiang et al. 2016
;
de La Vieuville et al. 2020
, SFR; e.g.,)
. We thus call both of them as Ly
\alpha
galaxies in the following text.

To understand how SF galaxies contribute to the ionizing photon budget, their rest-frame optical properties (continua and line emission) are necessary to be constrained. However, this task is difficult to execute before
JWST
era, especially for
z\simeq 6
galaxies. For example, due to a lack of near-/mid- infrared (IR) bands, it is challenging to break the degeneracy between prominent nebular emission from young galaxies and strong Balmer breaks from old galaxies
(
Schaerer & de Barros 2009
;
Jiang et al. 2016
, e.g.)
. On the other hand, even if the galaxies are spectroscopically confirmed (by Ly
\alpha
line for instance) at
z\simeq 6
, the optical emission lines (mainly [O
iii]
+H
\beta
and H
\alpha
+[N
ii
]) simultaneously boost the IRAC1 and IRAC2 channels of
Spitzer Space Telescope

(
Faisst et al. 2016
;
Harikane et al. 2018
;
Stefanon et al. 2021
, e.g.)
, leaving lines and continua coupled together. Such problems are being well solved in the current
JWST
era.

For building a large and homogeneous sample of high-
z
galaxies, we have carried out the Magellan M2FS spectroscopic survey to identify LAEs and LBGs at
z\simeq 6
(
Jiang et al. 2017
;
Ning et al. 2020
;
Ning et al. 2022
; Fu et al. in preparation). A fraction of them will be covered by the upcoming
JWST
imaging survey, such as COSMOS-Webb
(
Kartaltepe et al. 2021
;
Casey et al. 2022
, GO 1727;)
and PRIMER
(
Dunlop et al. 2021
, GO 1837;)
. The multiple IR bands can reveal their individual properties in detail. Recently, one of our identified LBGs has been covered by parallel
JWST
/NIRCam imaging of PRIMER MIRI observations. In this work, we compare it with spectroscopically confirmed galaxies at
z\simeq 6
from from previous literatures
(
Pentericci et al. 2018
)
to give a pilot investigation on the H
\alpha
properties of luminous Ly
\alpha
galaxies.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly present the sample of Ly
\alpha
galaxies,
JWST
/NIRCam imaging observations, data reduction, and photometry. In Section 3, we give the measurement results of the Ly
\alpha
, ultraviolet (UV), and H
\alpha
-related properties of the galaxy sample. We discuss their Ly
\alpha
escape fractions and ionizing photon production rates in Section 4. We summarize this work in Section 5. Throughout the paper, we use a standard flat cosmology with
H_{0}=\rm{70\ km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}}
,
\Omega_{m}=0.3
and
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
. All magnitudes refer to the AB system
(
Oke 1974
)
.

II.1
Sample of Ly
\alpha
Galaxies at
z\simeq 6

The sample includes seven spectroscopically confirmed galaxies at
z\simeq 6
. The first one (SC-1) is confirmed at redshift
z=6.087
with a strong Ly
\alpha
line by our spectroscopic survey (see the upper panel of Figure
1
; Fu et al. in preparation). In this survey, we carried out spectroscopic observations using the fiber-fed, multi-object spectrograph Michigan/Magellan Fiber System (M2FS;
Mateo et al. 2012
) on the 6.5 m Magellan Clay telescope. The science goal is to build a large and homogeneous sample of high-
z
galaxies (see
Jiang et al. 2017
for an overview of the program), including LAEs at
z\approx 5.7
and 6.6
(
Ning et al. 2020
;
Ning et al. 2022
)
, and LBGs at
5.5<z<6.8
(Fu et al. in preparation). These high-
z
galaxies are located in the famous fields including the Subaru
XMM-Newton
Deep Survey (SXDS), the Extended
Chandra
Deep Field-South (ECDFS), A370, COSMOS, and SSA22. The total sky area is around
2
deg
2
. SC-1 has an estimated Ly
\alpha
equivalent width of EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
)
\gtrsim 100
Å. It is thus one of the largest-EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) LBGs in a wide area covered by our spectroscopic survey.

The rest six sources (SC-2–7) are from a previous work,
Pentericci et al. 2018
. They used VLT/FORS2 to conduct the CANDELSz7 survey, an ESO Large Program, to spectroscopically confirm SF galaxies at
z\gtrsim 6
in three
HST
CANDELS Legacy fields (GOODS-South, UDS, and COSMOS). SC-2 and SC-3 locate in the CANDELS UDS field while SC-4–7 locate in the CANDELS GOODS-South (GOODS-S) field. Among them, SC-2 was detected with no emission line and a continuum discontinuity which is interpreted as a Ly
\alpha
break (see P18). Due to the limited sensitivity of instruments and depth of observations, we treat this source as a Ly
\alpha
galaxy with an upper limit of EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) from P18. Figure
1
shows redshift distribution of the sample in the lower panel.

II.2
Imaging Data

The three sources SC-1–3 in/around the CANDELS UDS field are covered by the
JWST
Cycle-1 program (GO 1837), Public Release IMaging for Extragalactic Research (PRIMER;
Dunlop et al. 2021
). The PRIMER survey pays attention to the two key equatorial
HST
CANDELS Fields (COSMOS and UDS) by delivering 10-band NIRCam+MIRI imaging observations. It owns the parallel NIRCam imaging in eight bands including F090W, F115W, F150W, and F200W in the short wavelength (SW), and F277W, F356W, F444W, and F410M in the long wavelength (LW). Note that the F410M
-
F444W color is a good indication of H
\alpha
emission of the SF galaxies at
z\sim 5-6.6
. SC-1–3 have been imaged by PRIMER #14 and #22 observations with individual exposure lengths of
{\sim}1.9
hours in the SW bands and
{\sim}0.5
hours in the LW bands.

The other four sources SC-4–7 are covered by the
JWST
Cycle-1 program (GO 1963), UDF medium-band survey (UDF-MB;
Williams et al. 2021
). The UDF-MB survey images the (Hubble) Ultra Deep Field (UDF) with a single NIRCam pointing (field of view
\sim 2\times 2\arcmin.2\times 2\arcmin.2
) in a series of medium-bands including F182M, F210M, F430M, F460M, and F480M (NIRISS F430M and F480M in parallel). Total integration time reaches
{\sim}7.8
hours for each F182M, F210M, and F480M and
{\sim}3.9
hours for each F430M and F460M. In Figure
1
, we plot the transmission curves of the five
JWST
/NIRCam LW filters to compare and illustrate the H
\alpha
locations of the galaxies in the observed-wavelength frame.

Except SC-1 and 2, other sources are not covered by enough
JWST
SW bands especially SC-3 is located in the gap region of NIRCam SW imaging. We thus utilize the archival
HST
/WFC3 (Wide Field Camera 3) near-IR imaging data from the CANDELS program
(
Grogin et al. 2011
;
Koekemoer et al. 2011
;
Guo et al. 2013
;
Galametz et al. 2013
)
. We download the data products provided by the High Level Science Products
1
1

1

https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/candels
. SC-2 and SC-3 have
HST
/WFC3 F125W and F160W observations while SC-1 is a little bit outside the CANDELS UDS region. SC-4–7 in the CANDELS GOODS-S region have
HST
/WFC3 observations in three near-IR bands (F105W, F125W and F160W).

II.3
Data Reduction and Photometry

We reduced the NIRCam imaging data with the standard
JWST
pipeline
2
2

2

https://github.com/spacetelescope/jwst
(v1.7.2) up to stage 2 using the reference files “jwst_0999.pmap” for PRIMER and “jwst_1008.pmap” for UDF-MB. Then we use the
Grizli
3
3

3

https://github.com/gbrammer/grizli
reduction pipeline to process the output images.
Grizli
mitigates 1/f noises and mask the “snowball” artifacts from cosmic rays
(
Rigby et al. 2022
)
. It further converts the world coordinate system (WCS) information in the headers to the SIP format for each exposure so that images can be drizzled and combined with Astrodrizzle
4
4

4

https://drizzlepac.readthedocs.io/en/latest/astrodrizzle.html
. For the SW and LW images, the WCS of final mosaics are registered based on the catalogs of DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys Data Release 9 and the pixel scale was resampled to 0.03″ with pixfrac
=0.8
. We also subtract an additional background on the final mosaics. Figure
2
a shows the thumbnail images of the sample in a series of the
JWST
/NIRCam (and/or
HST
/WFC3 near-IR) bands.

We run
SExtractor

(
Bertin & Arnouts 1996
)
to perform photometry in the
JWST
/NIRCam multi-band images. The aperture has a radius of triple FWHMs of the point-spread function (PSF) in each wavelength band. The aperture correction is calculated from the PSF in each band. We first obtain initial measurements by matching the output catalogs to the targets within a distance tolerance of a FWHM. For each target, we select its brightest band to feed the detection image. Specifically, we adopt the F444W band for three sources covered by the PRIMER survey and the F460W band for the four sources covered by the UDF-MB survey because their H
\alpha
lines boost these bands. We then rerun
SExtractor
in the dual image mode with the detection images. For each measurement image, we also adopt an aperture with a radius of triple PSF FWHMs in this band. Only for the SC-7 source, we use a radius of 1.5 PSF FWHM, in order to minimize the amount of abnormal pixels within the photometric aperture, caused by the fact that SC-7 is located very close to the image edge. III
Results

In this section, we give the measured results of UV and H
\alpha
quantities of the galaxy sample. Their UV properties are derived from
JWST
/NIRCam SW bands and/or
HST
/WFC3 near-IR bands. We utilize the NIRCam medium-bands to constrain their (rest-frame) optical continuum. We then measure their H
\alpha
flux by combining the corresponding LW broad- or medium-bands. We further obtain their H
\alpha
-related properties including the Ly
\alpha
escape fraction and the ionizing photon production efficiency. The results are listed in Table
2
.

III.1
UV Continua

We measure the UV continuum of the galaxies with the commonly used method
(
Pentericci et al. 2018
;
Jiang et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. As in these works, we assume a power-law form for the UV continuum of each source, i.e.:
f_{\lambda}\propto\lambda^{\beta}
. As we work in AB magnitude units, we fit a linear relation
m_{\rm AB}\propto(\beta+2)\times{\rm log}(\lambda)
to the SW photometric data, from which we obtain the UV continuum slope
\beta_{\rm UV}
and the absolute UV magnitude
M_{\rm UV}
at the rest-frame wavelength 1500Å.

For a galaxy at
z\simeq 6
, its observed Ly
\alpha
line locates in the wavelength range of the Subaru/
z^{\prime}
and
JWST
/F090W bands. The corresponding broad-band flux usually differs from the flux level of UV continuum due to the Ly
\alpha
emission or break (IGM absorption bluewards of Ly
\alpha
). So in the measurements for UV continua, we abandon the F090W photometric data for SC-1 and SC-2. We then subtract the fit power-law UV continuum from the
z^{\prime}
- or F090W-band photometry to constrain Ly
\alpha
flux and EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) for SC-1. The IGM continuum absorption blueward of Ly
\alpha
line is considered in the computation
(
Madau 1995
)
. For SC-3–7, the CANDELz7 galaxies, we directly adopt the observed Ly
\alpha
flux given by P18 to compute the Ly
\alpha
luminosity and EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) with our obtained power-law UV continua. For SC-2 which is undetected in Ly
\alpha
, we use the upper limit of EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) given by P18 to compute its Ly
\alpha
flux. Note that Ly
\alpha
flux may be slightly underestimated due to the potential Ly
\alpha
emission from the circumgalactic medium
(
Cai et al. 2019
;
Wu et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.

III.2
H
\alpha
Line Emission

We combine the medium- and broad-bands (covering the rest-frame optical wavelength at
z\simeq 6
) to estimate the flux and EW of H
\alpha
emission lines. In Figure
2
, the (red) color of F410M
-
F444W and F430M
-
F460M clearly show the flux excess due to strong H
\alpha
lines. In the F444W broad-band which covers the F410M, F430M, F460M, and F480M medium-bands, H
\alpha
+[N
ii
] lines dominate the flux estimation
(
Anders & Fritze-v. Alvensleben 2003
, e.g.,)
. We thus ignore other optical lines except H
\alpha
+[N
ii
]. We assume that [N
ii
] contribute line flux at the H
\alpha
wavelength due to the small wavelength difference relative to the wavelength range of
{>}4\ \micron
. We also assume that the emission line has a gaussian profile with FWHM = 300 km s
-1
(see the black lines in Figure
2
b).

SC-4–7 are covered by the UDF-MB survey with five NIRCam medium-bands. For SC-4, 5, and 7, we use the F430M and F480M magnitude (
3\sigma
upper limit for SC-7) to constrain the rest-frame optical continuum with a power-law form
f_{\nu}\propto\nu^{\alpha}
because the H
\alpha
+[N
ii
] emission only fall into the F460M band. For SC-6, the H
\alpha
+[N
ii
] emission fall into the F460M and F480M bands. We thus match the continuum plus line model to the three LW medium-band photometric data. Figure
2
b illustrates the above procedure in the lower panel. For these four sources, we obtain similar power-law indices with a median value of
\alpha\sim-0.6
. SC-1–3 are covered by the PRIMER NIRCam multi-band (7 broad + 1 medium) observations. As no strong nebular lines fall into the F410M band, we use the F410M magnitude to constrain the (rest-frame) optical continuum and the F410M
-
F444W color to estimate H
\alpha
+[N
ii
] flux. We start with a power-law continuum with an index of
\alpha_{0}=-0.6
(from the other four sources) to match the F410M flux density. Then we integrate the known continuum plus unknown line emission weighted by the F444W filter transmission curve to match the F444M flux density and compute the H
\alpha
+[N
ii
] flux. We also vary the continuum slope
\alpha
in a reasonable range of
\alpha_{0}\pm 0.5
to obtain the deviations of the measured line flux which would be included into the errors of the final values. Figure
2
b illustrates the above procedure in the upper panel.

After estimating the line flux, we assume that H
\alpha
accounts for 85% of the H
\alpha
+[N
ii
] flux, which is similar to previous studies
(
Rasappu et al. 2016
;
Faisst et al. 2019
;
Sun et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. To be conservative, we also feed the 10% flux into the error of the final H
\alpha
flux. With the measured flux of H
\alpha
line, we obtain the SFR(H
\alpha
) using the canonical H
\alpha
-SFR calibration relation
(
Hao et al. 2011
;
Murphy et al. 2011
;
Kennicutt & Evans 2012
, listed in Column 11 of Table
2
;)
. We further compute the EW
0
(H
\alpha
) with the rest-frame optical continuum level. The results are plotted in the second and third rows of Figure
3
. Note that the H
\alpha
flux and EW may be underestimated because the optical continuum is overestimated due to the existence of some faint optical lines. But such an underestimation is supposed to be included into the enlarged measurement errors. EW
0
(H
\alpha
) indicates the specific SFR (sSFR) of galaxies. Our
z\simeq 6
sample spread a larger range of EW
0
(H
\alpha
) than the low-
z
LAEs (
Matthee et al. 2021
) and local analogs (
Yang et al. 2017
). Recently,
Sun et al. 2022
serendipitously found a sample of strong H
\alpha
/[O
iii]
emitters in the
JWST
/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) data. Our median EW
0
(H
\alpha
) is twice higher than theirs because in our sample the 6/7 galaxies emitting Ly
\alpha
lines are supposed to have higher sSFR while the SF galaxies of
Sun et al. 2022
are found based on H
\alpha
/[O
iii]
detections. SC-7 is undetected in Ly
\alpha
and its EW
0
(H
\alpha
) is similar to the lowest one of the sample in
Sun et al. 2022
.

III.3
H
\alpha
-related Properties

III.3.1
Escape Fraction of Ly
\alpha
Photons

We estimate the escape fraction of Ly
\alpha
photons (
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
) for the sample. With the obtained H
\alpha
flux, we adopt the canonical ratio
L({\rm Ly\alpha})/L({\rm H\alpha})=8.7

(
Henry et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
to calculate the intrinsic Ly
\alpha
flux and obtain:

\displaystyle f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}=\frac{L_{\rm obs}({\rm Ly\alpha})}{L({\rm H\alpha})\times 8.7}

(1)

under the assumption of case-B recombination in
T_{e}=10^{4}K

(
Osterbrock & Ferland 2006
)
. We also apply a dust correction using the reddening law of
Calzetti et al. 2000
. We can not well constrain the extinction
E(B-V)
for nebulae due to a lack of the Balmer decrement (H
\alpha
/H
\beta
) information. Thus, we perform SED fitting using
BAGPIPES

(
Carnall et al. 2018
)
and obtain
A_{V}=0.8
for SC-2 thanks to the abundance of multi-band photometric data for this source. For others, we adopt a lower and modest value of
A_{V}=0.4
as a reasonable assumption because they have higher EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) with lower dust content. Note that
A_{V}
is supposed to be smaller for the six galaxies but the difference is only
\lesssim 0.1
dex for the computed
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
. The
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
results are listed in the Column 7 of Table
2
. We can see that 6/7 galaxies have
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

\lesssim 10\%
even though they spread over a large range of EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) and EW
0
(H
\alpha
). This may imply the upper limit of
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
for most of galaxies at
z\simeq 6
. Note that
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}
is supposed to be smaller than the
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

(
Dijkstra et al. 2016
;
Izotov et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. SC-7 has the largest value of
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
reaching
{\simeq}50\%
while it is relatively faint in the rest-frame UV (
M_{\rm UV}

\simeq-19
) with an extremely blue UV slope (
\beta_{\rm UV}

\simeq-3
). In Section 4.1, we discuss the relevant, potential trends.

III.3.2
Production Efficiency of Ionizing Photons

With the measured flux of UV continua and H
\alpha
line, we estimate the Hydrogen ionizing photon production efficiency
\xi_{\rm ion}
by

\displaystyle\xi_{\rm ion}=\frac{\dot{N}_{\rm ion}}{L_{\nu}^{\rm UV}},

(2)

where
\dot{N}_{\rm ion}
(s
-1
) is the intrinsic production rate of Hydrogen ionizing photons from stellar populations and
L_{\nu}^{\rm UV}
(erg s
-1
Hz
-1
) is the (mono-chromatic) UV continuum luminosity per photon frequency which can be derived from the above measured
M_{\rm UV}
.
\xi_{\rm ion}
intrinsically depends on the assumed stellar-population model
(
Robertson et al. 2013
;
Eldridge et al. 2017
;
Yung et al. 2020b
, e.g.,)
.
\dot{N}_{\rm ion}
can be computed from H
\alpha
emission by

\displaystyle\dot{N}_{\rm ion}=\frac{L({\rm H\alpha})}{1-f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}}\times 7.35\times 10^{11}\ {\rm erg^{-1}},

(3)

in the (
T_{e}=10^{4}K
) case-B recombination
(
Kennicutt et al. 1994
;
Leitherer & Heckman 1995
;
Madau et al. 1998
)
. We then obtain the production efficiency of ionizing photons which do not escape from the galaxy,
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
assuming
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}
= 0.

The (dust-uncorrected) results are shown in the downmost row of Figure
3
. Note that the dust-corrected values are lower by
{\lesssim}0.1
dex assuming the canonical stellar/nebular extinction ratio of 0.44 which is obtained from local starbursts
(
Calzetti et al. 2000
)
. The
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
results are listed in the Column 10 of Table
2
. Our obtained
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
distribute over a broad range of log
10

\xi_{\rm ion,0}

\sim 25.0-26.5
. The median value is consistent with that of a large UV-faint galaxy sample from
Prieto-Lyon et al. 2022
. This median is also close to that of a H
\alpha
-emitter sample from
Sun et al. 2022
. Among our sample, SC-1 is the most luminous in Ly
\alpha
and has the highest
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
reaching log
10

\xi_{\rm ion,0}

\sim 26.5
while SC-2 is not detected in Ly
\alpha
and has the lowest
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
. We further give an extensive discussion in next section.

IV
Discussion

Thanks to the excellent capability of
JWST

(
Rigby et al. 2022
)
, we have measured the H
\alpha
-related properties of the SF galaxies at
z\simeq 6
. In this section, we discuss the obtained properties of the galaxy sample. We compare our results with previous studies in Figure
3
. The H
\alpha
-related properties are assigned by rows and Ly
\alpha
/UV quantities are assigned by columns. The
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
is assigned again at the rightmost column to compare with the H
\alpha
-related properties.

IV.1
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
-UV Correlation

We compare the obtained
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
in the upmost row and rightmost column of Figure
3
. The measured results show that
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
positively correlates with
L
(Ly
\alpha
) and EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
), and negatively correlates with
M_{\rm UV}
and
\beta_{\rm UV}
(in the upmost row). The positive trends are natural because
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
is inferred through Ly
\alpha
flux, which is similar to those found from the low-
z
samples
(
Hayes et al. 2014
;
Yang et al. 2017
;
Matthee et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
.
Yang et al. 2017
(and
Kim et al. 2021
) use a statistical sample of local Green Pea galaxies as high-
z
analogs to reveal that.
Matthee et al. 2021
use a LAE sample at
z\sim 2
and also found such a relation. For the negative trends of
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
changing as UV properties, we obtain a linear relation between
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
and
M_{\rm UV}
,

{\rm log_{10}}\,f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}=(0.64\pm 0.08)\,M_{\rm UV}+(11.8\pm 1.7),

(4)

shown as a red dotted line in panel 0-0; we also obtain another linear relation between
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
and
\beta_{\rm UV}
,

{\rm log_{10}}\,f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}=(-0.66\pm 0.23)\,\beta_{\rm UV}-(2.5\pm 0.5),

(5)

shown as a red dotted line in panel 0-1.
The
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
-
M_{\rm UV}
relation seems to exist in the current high-
z
sample, although the sign is weak for the two samples of Ly
\alpha
galaxies at lower redshifts.
Chisholm et al. 2022
found that
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}
increases for fainter
M_{\rm UV}
using a sample of LyC-leaking SF galaxies at
z\simeq 0.3
, which is overall lower than our obtained
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
-
M_{\rm UV}
trend. Such difference is consistent with the scenario of
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}

\lesssim

f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

(
de Barros et al. 2016
;
Dijkstra et al. 2016
, e.g.,)
.

The
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
-
\beta_{\rm UV}
trend may exist at
z\simeq 6
like at
z\sim 2

(
Snapp-Kolas et al. 2022
;
Prieto-Lyon et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. The reason can be simply interpreted as that bluer galaxies have younger stellar population and/or lower dust content. They emit harder ionizing photons suffering lower extinction, which accounts for higher escape fraction of Ly
\alpha
photons. The
z\simeq 6
trend is steeper, which is consistent with the current consensus that galaxies at higher redshift are bluer
(
Bouwens et al. 2014
;
Jiang et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.
Chisholm et al. 2022
shows that
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}
scales strongly with
\beta_{\rm UV}
for the LyC-leaker sample at
z\simeq 0.3
, while those with
\beta_{\rm UV}

=-2
have an averaged
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}

\lesssim 5\%
which is smaller than
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

\lesssim 10\%
estimated from our linear fit (panel 0-1 of Figure
3
). SC-7 is the bluest one in our sample with
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

\sim 50\%
. This galaxy and the luminous LAEs with very blue UV continua (
\beta_{\rm UV}

\lesssim 3
) reported by
Jiang et al. 2020
are supposed to be strong LyC leakers to contribute ionizing photons. They are thus excellent targets to carry out
JWST
IR spectroscopic followup for the ionization lines.

IV.2
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
-Ly
\alpha
Correlation

The panel 3-2 (3-3) of Figure
3
shows a potential correlation between the
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
and Ly
\alpha
luminosity (Ly
\alpha
equivalent width) for the
z\simeq 6
sample in this work. We obtain a linear relation between
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
and
L({\rm Ly\alpha})
:

{\rm log_{10}}\,\xi_{\rm ion,0}=(0.87\pm 0.19)\,{\rm log_{10}}\,L({\rm Ly\alpha})-(11.3\pm 8.2),

(6)

shown as a red dotted line in panel 3-2.
Saldana-Lopez et al. 2022
also found a similar trend in a sample of SF galaxies at
3\leq z\leq 5
. We notice that such correlations does not always keep for the LAEs at
z\sim 2
from
Matthee et al. 2021
, especially for those with relative bright Ly
\alpha
flux. A direct reason is that the
z\sim 2
LAEs in
Matthee et al. 2021
generally have higher
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
, even reaching
\gtrsim 1
.
Matthee et al. 2017b
also obtained several galaxies have
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

\gtrsim 1
and they explained it using the mechanisms including, for example, Ly
\alpha
emission is produced by a different mechanism (like cooling radiation;
Dijkstra 2014
). We look forward to compare their results updated with the additional H
\alpha
information.

The
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
-
L({\rm Ly\alpha})
trend can be reasonably expected because harder ionizing radiation of more young stellar populations (higher sSFR shown by the panel 2-2) tend to produce more ionizing photons per non-ionizing photons
(
Balestra et al. 2013
;
Mainali et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
and ionize the ISM more efficiently, eventually enhancing Ly
\alpha
production, transfer, and escape. Note that EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) can be inferred to be proportional to
\xi_{\rm ion}
\times
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

(
Harikane et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. This trend implies that galaxies with
{\rm log}_{10}\,L
(Ly
\alpha
)
\gtrsim 43.5
(at the bright end of Ly
\alpha
luminosity function) may have a high ionizing photon production efficiency reaching log
10

\xi_{\rm ion,0}

\gtrsim 27
. Conversely speaking, SF galaxies with low
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
(
{\lesssim}25
) may tend to emit negligible Ly
\alpha
emission. If such a trend exists, the very (Ly
\alpha
) luminous galaxies could fully ionize their surrounding neutral H
i
gas with a modest or even small
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}
value. This situation is also consistent in which the scenario that luminous Ly
\alpha
galaxies in the EoR power large ionizing bubbles
(
Zheng et al. 2017
;
Yajima et al. 2018
;
Hu et al. 2021
;
Ning et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.

IV.3
Implications of High
\xi_{\rm ion}

We plot the redshift evolution of
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
for the SF galaxies in Figure
4
. The red circle symbols indicate our results for the Ly
\alpha
galaxies at the end of EoR. The figure shows that the
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
of SF galaxies increases with redshift. Among the seven Ly
\alpha
galaxies, three of them (SC-1, 3, and 7) have high production efficiency of ionizing photons of log
10

\xi_{\rm ion,0}

\gtrsim 26
. This fact reveals the possibility that a portion of galaxies have high values of
\xi_{\rm ion}
.
Maseda et al. 2020
also found an elevated mean
\xi_{\rm ion}
(log
10

\xi_{\rm ion}

\gtrsim 26
) in a sample of UV-faint and high-EW LAEs at
z\approx 4-5
.
Finkelstein et al. 2019
presented a reionization model dominated by low-mass and UV-faint galaxies, which would requires such high
\xi_{\rm ion}
. Our results provide an observation evidence. The high values of
\xi_{\rm ion}
have not been well explained in galaxy simulations. For example,
Wilkins et al. 2016
explored the
\xi_{\rm ion}
range of EoR galaxies in the BlueTides simulation, in which they used low-metallicity stellar population (with binary systems) models to obtain their highest
\xi_{\rm ion}
of log
10

\xi_{\rm ion}

\lesssim 26
.
Yung et al. 2020b
could not also provide such high
\xi_{\rm ion}
with the semi-analytical models of galaxy formation. Note that their models only predict a rather weak dependence of
\xi_{\rm ion}
with redshift, which means we have not well understood the physical processes responsible for the redshift evolution shown in Figure
4
(the gray lines). In the meanwhile, a larger sample of fainter galaxies is necessary to be built to overcome the sampling bias at the higher redshift (
z>5
).

The median
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
of our Ly
\alpha
galaxy sample is consistent the overall trend (gray lines). Previous results indicate EoR SF galaxies with
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
on the overall trend can provide enough ionizing photon budget
(
Harikane et al. 2018
;
Stefanon et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. Our sample implies that Ly
\alpha
galaxies may also play a similar role and contribute to the total balance of ionizing photons at
z\simeq 6
. Note that the most luminous Ly
\alpha
galaxy in the sample, SC-1 (identified by our Magellan M2FS survey), has a very high
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
(log
10

\xi_{\rm ion,0}

\sim 26.5
) relative to other SF galaxies even though its Ly
\alpha
luminosity of
{\rm log}_{10}\,L
(Ly
\alpha
)
\simeq 43
is only a little larger than the characteristic luminosity of Ly
\alpha
luminosity function
(
Hu et al. 2010
;
Kashikawa et al. 2011
;
Zheng et al. 2017
;
Ning et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. Its nature need to be revealed based on further spectroscopic observations. Our results support that Ly
\alpha
galaxies, especially those with intrinsically high EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
), may significantly contribute the ionizing photons during the EoR. We look forward to comparing our findings with the ionizing photon production efficiency estimated for other Ly
\alpha
-luminous galaxies
(
Ning et al. 2020
;
Ning et al. 2022
, from, e.g.,)
which will be covered by the
JWST
observations.

V
Summary

In this work, we present a pilot study of a spectroscopically confirmed sample of (Ly
\alpha
emitting) galaxies at redshift
z\simeq 6
based on the
JWST
/NIRCam imaging data. The sample includes seven targets: one identified by our Magellan/M2FS spectroscopic survey and six observed by the previous CANDELSz7 survey. All the seven sources we analyze are Lyman break galaxies showing large differences in their Ly
\alpha
luminosity, ranging from no observed Ly
\alpha
line up to strong Ly
\alpha
line of
{\rm log}_{10}\,L
(Ly
\alpha
)
\simeq 43.3
. These objects have been covered by two
JWST
/NIRCam imaging surveys, PRIMER and UDF-MB, which employ a serious of SW and LW bands.

We have obtained their Ly
\alpha
- and H
\alpha
-related properties by combining the NIRCam broad- and/or medium-bands. Based on the results, we also revealed the potential correlations among the properties of Ly
\alpha
galaxies at
z\simeq 6
. We summarize our findings as follows:

•

6/7 galaxies have Ly
\alpha
escape fractions of
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

\lesssim 10\%
regardless of their EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
) and EW
0
(H
\alpha
), which might be the status for most of star-forming galaxies at
z\simeq 6
.

•

One Ly
\alpha
galaxy which is relatively faint in the rest-frame UV (
M_{\rm UV}

\simeq-19
) with an extremely blue UV slope (
\beta_{\rm UV}

\simeq-3
) has a Ly
\alpha
escape fraction reaching
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

\simeq 50\%
.

•

Ly
\alpha
galaxies with fainter rest-frame UV continua and/or bluer UV slopes tend to have higher escape fraction of Ly
\alpha
photons (Equations [
4
] & [
5
]).

•

Our sample spread over a broad range of the ionizing photon production efficiency over log
10

\xi_{\rm ion,0}

\sim 25.0-26.5
with a median value close to those of the galaxy samples at similar redshifts from other studies.

•

Galaxies with more luminous Ly
\alpha
emission probably have higher production efficiency of ionizing photons (Equation [
6
]).

•

Our identified source (SC-1 analyzed in this study) which is very luminous in Ly
\alpha
has a very high ionizing photon production efficiency of log
10

\xi_{\rm ion,0}
(Hz erg
-1
)
>26
. Its nature merits further investigation.

Our results agree with the scenario in which Ly
\alpha
galaxies may serve as a significant contributor to cosmic reionization. The bluer and/or more luminous Ly
\alpha
galaxies are ideal targets for
JWST
spectroscopic followup observations. We need a larger sample of Ly
\alpha
galaxies observed by
JWST
for further analysis on the reionization sources.

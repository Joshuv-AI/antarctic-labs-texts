PDL on Steroids: on Expressive Extensions of PDL with Intersection and Converse
Diego Figueira, Santiago Figueira, Edwin Pin

Abstract
. We introduce CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} , a family of expressive logics rooted in Propositional Dynamic Logic ( PDL ). In terms of expressive power, CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} strictly contains PDL extended with intersection and converse ( a.k.a. ICPDL ) as well as Conjunctive Queries (CQ), Conjunctive Regular Path Queries (CRPQ), or some known extensions thereof (Regular Queries and CQPDL). We investigate the expressive power, indistinguishability via bisimulations, satisfiability, and model checking for CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} . We argue that natural subclasses of CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} can be defined in terms of the tree-width of the underlying graphs of the formulas. We show that the class of CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} formulas of tree-width 2 is equivalent to ICPDL , and that it also coincides with CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} formulas of tree-width 1. However, beyond tree-width 2, incrementing the tree-width strictly increases the expressive power. We characterize the expressive power for every class of fixed tree-width formulas in terms of a bisimulation game with pebbles. Based on this characterization, we show that CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} has a tree-like model property. We prove that the satisfiability problem is decidable in 2 ExpTime on fixed tree-width formulas, coinciding with the complexity of ICPDL . We also exhibit classes for which satisfiability is reduced to ExpTime . Finally, we establish that the model checking problem for fixed tree-width formulas is in PTime , contrary to the full class CPDL + {\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{} .

1.
Introduction

This paper can be seen as a proof of concept.
Since
Kripke structures
and
graph databases
(
i.e.
, structures over a binary signature) are essentially the same: is it possible to amalgamate the expressive logics (for
Kripke structures
) and query languages (for
graph databases
) into one well-behaved framework?

Our point of departure is Propositional Dynamic Logic, or
PDL
, extended with intersection and converse. This is a well-studied logic, and amongst the most expressive decidable logics on
Kripke structures
. But on the other hand, we want to be able to test for
conjunctive queries
, or more generally
CRPQs
, which are the basic building block for query languages for
graph databases
.
The outcome is an arguably natural and highly expressive logic, which inherits all good computational and model-theoretical behaviors of
PDL
and
CRPQs
, which has the flavor of
PDL
but allows for richer “conjunctive” tests.
We name this logic “
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
”.

PDL
was originally conceived as a logic for reasoning about programs
(
Fischer and Ladner 1979
)
. However, variants of
PDL
are nowadays used in various areas of computer science, in particular in description logics, epistemic logics, program verification, or for querying datasets (see
(
Lange 2006
;
Göller and Lohrey 2006
)
for more applications).
One of the most studied extensions of
PDL
is the addition of converse navigation,
program intersection
and program complement.
In particular, adding converse and
program intersection
operators results in a well-behaved logic, known as
ICPDL
(‘I’ for
intersection
, ‘C’ for converse).
ICPDL
has decidable
satisfiability
, polynomial time
model checking
, and enjoys a “tree-like” model property
(
Göller and Lohrey 2006
)
.
A simpler version of
program intersection
studied before is
CPDL
extended with
program looping
, which can state that a program starts and ends at the same point, known as
loop-CPDL
, and it is strictly less expressive than
ICPDL
.
However, adding the complement of programs results in a logic with a highly undecidable satisfiability problem
(
Harel et al. 2001
;
Göller and Lohrey 2006
)
, although its model checking is still polynomial time
(
Lange 2006
)
.

In this work we explore a family of logics which in particular generalizes
program intersection
.
The intuition is that the intersection of two
PDL
programs
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
could be viewed as the conjunction of two atoms
R_{\pi}(x,y)\land R_{\pi^{\prime}}(x,y)
over the binary relations denoted by the programs
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
(
i.e.
, essentially a
conjunctive query
). Pursuing this idea, we introduce an extension of
CPDL
that we call
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
which allows for testing of any arbitrary number of atoms. For example, a formula can test for an
n
-clique of
\pi
-related elements with
\bigwedge_{1\leq i<j\leq n}R_{\pi}(x_{i},x_{j})
. Further, these tests can be nested, composed, or iterated just like conventional
PDL
programs.
The resulting logic seems appealing from an expressiveness point of view: it captures not only
ICPDL
but also several
graph database
query languages studied lately in the quest for finding well-behaved expressive query languages. These include
C2RPQ
,
Regular Queries
and
CQPDL
. See
Figure

1
for a general idea of where
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
sits.

Further, subclasses of
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
can be naturally defined by restricting the allowed underlying graphs (
a.k.a.
Gaifman graph) of these new kind of tests. Thus,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!(\mathcal{G}){}{}
is the restriction to tests whose underlying graphs are in the class of graphs
\mathcal{G}
. In particular, for suitable (and very simple) classes we find
ICPDL
and
loop-CPDL
.

, which is exponential. For the query languages
CQ

C2RPQ

By

, is the set of all

, introduced in
(

and defined in

.

1.1.
Summary of results

Expressivity

As already mentioned,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
contains some previously studied graph database query languages (see
Figure

1
). Further, depending on the class
\mathcal{G}
of graphs
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!(\mathcal{G}){}{}
can be identified with different previous formalisms. In particular,
CPDL
corresponds to
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!(\emptyset){}{}
on the empty class.

In
Section

4
we show that, on the other hand, the logic
loop-CPDL
can also be expressed as
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathcal{G}_{\circlearrowleft}}{}{}){}{}
, where
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathcal{G}_{\circlearrowleft}}{}{}
is the class with just one
graph
having a self-looping vertex (
Proposition

1
).
It is also easy to see that
ICPDL
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathcal{G}_{\cap}}{}{}){}{}
are
equi-expressive
, where
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathcal{G}_{\cap}}{}{}
is the class having just one
graph
containing two vertices and one
edge
between them. What is more, we also show that
ICPDL
is
equi-expressive
to
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{2\!}}{}{}){}{}
. Hence,
formulas
and
programs
of
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{1\!}}{}{}){}{}
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{2\!}}{}{}){}{}
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathcal{G}_{\cap}}{}{}){}{}
, and
ICPDL
define the same unary and binary relations, respectively (
Theorem

6
).

However, as we show in
Section

5
, beyond
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{2\!}}{}{}
the expressive power starts increasing in an infinite hierarchy, where
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
is strictly contained in
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k+1\!}}{}{}){}{}
in terms of expressive power, for every
k\geq 2
(
Theorem

1
).

Indistinguishability

We characterize model-indistinguishability for
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
in terms of a
k
-pebble local consistency game. That is, we study the conditions under which a pair of models cannot be distinguished by a
formula
or
program
from
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
.
In Section
5
we characterize both the indistinguishability via
positive

formulas
(
Theorem

1
) or arbitrary
formulas
(
Theorem

2
).
These notions correspond, in some sense, to the lifting of the simulation relation from Modal Logics to
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
(though we defer this connection to
). We show that
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
enjoys the
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}
-model property
: if a
formula
of
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
is satisfiable, it is satisfiable in a
model
of
tree-width

k
(
Corollary

3
).

Satisfiability

In
Section

7
we study the
satisfiability problem
, that is, whether for a given formula there exists a
model
and
element
thereof which satisfy it. We show that, for any
k
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
is decidable in
2
ExpTime
,
i.e.
, the same complexity as
ICPDL
and
loop-CPDL

(
Göller et al. 2009
)
. We also identify a hierarchy, based on what we call
conjunctive width
instead of
tree-width
, so that any class of bounded
conjunctive width
is decidable in
ExpTime
,
i.e.
, the complexity of
CPDL
.
For the unrestricted logic, namely
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
, we obtain a
3
ExpTime
upper bound (
Theorem

6
). These results exploit the
bounded tree-width model property
of
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
above.

Model checking

Finally, we argue in
Section

8
that
model checking
on finite
structures
is still in polynomial time for any class
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}\!({\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{TW\!}_{k\!}}{}{}){}{}
of bounded
tree-width
, unlike the unrestricted logic
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
(
Theorem

2
).

All missing proofs are contained in the appendix.

1.2.
Related logics

As mentioned earlier,
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{CPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
can be seen as an “umbrella logic” capturing both expressive extensions of
PDL
, such as
ICPDL
, and expressive extensions of
conjunctive queries
studied in the context of
graph databases
. We next detail some query languages related to
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\textup{ICPDL}^{\!+}}{}{}
.

A
graph database
is often abstracted as a finite, edge-labeled graph, where labels come from some finite alphabet of relations (see
e.g.

(
Baeza 2013
)
for more details), but via trivial adaptations one can use
graph
query languages on
Kripke structures
, or vice-versa.

Conjunctive queries
, or
CQs
, are conjunctions of relational atoms, closed under projection. For example, the
CQ
“
q(x,x,y)=R(x,y)\land S(y,z)\land T(z,x)
” returns, on an edge-labelled graph, all triplets of vertices
(u,u,v)
such that
u,v
participates in an
R
-
S
-
T
-cycle.

In analogy to
conjunctive queries
, the class of
conjunctive regular path queries
, or
CRPQs
, allow for atoms of the form
x\xrightarrow{L}y
, where
L
is a regular expression over the alphabet of the relations, with the semantics that there exists a path from
x
to
y
reading a word of relation names in
L

(
Calvanese et al. 2000
)
. This language is often considered as the backbone of any reasonable graph query language
(
Baeza 2013
)
. The extension of

conjunctive two-way
regular path queries
, or
C2RPQs
allows
L
to be built over the alphabet augmented with the converse of the relations.

Regular queries

(
Reutter et al. 2017
)
, also known as
Nested Positive 2RPQ

(
Bourhis et al. 2014
)
, can be seen as the closure under Kleene star, union and converse of
C2RPQs
.Its containment problem has been shown to be decidable in elementary time, contrary to some more ambitious generalizations of
C2RPQs

(
Rudolph and Krötzsch 2013
;
Bourhis et al. 2014
)
.

Perhaps the language closest in spirit to this work is
CQPDL

(
Benedikt et al. 2016
)
, denoting
conjunctive queries
whose atoms relations can be defined by
CPDL

programs
. It subsumes
C2RPQs
in expressive power, but it is incomparable with
Regular queries
.

3.
PDL and its extensions

CPDL

Expressions

Let

In the rest of the section we work with

We use

{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\Sigma_{N}}{}{}
-labeled
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\Sigma_{E}}{}{}

.
If

The trees accepted by

Klein’s Fundamental 2-Form of Second Kind for the 𝑪 𝒂 ​ 𝒃 \boldsymbol{C_{ab}} Curves
Joe SUZUKI

Abstract
In this paper, we derive the exact formula of Klein’s fundamental 2-form of second kind for the so-called C a ​ b C_{ab} curves. The problem was initially solved by Klein in the 19th century for the hyper-elliptic curves, but little progress had been seen for its extension for more than 100 years. Recently, it has been addressed by several authors, and was solved for subclasses of the C a ​ b C_{ab} curves whereas they found a way to find its individual solution numerically. The formula gives a standard cohomological basis for the curves, and has many applications in algebraic geometry, physics, and applied mathematics, not just analyzing sigma functions in a general way.

Introduction

Since Abel, Jacobi, Poincaré, and Riemann established its framework, theories of Abelian and modular functions associated with algebraic curves have been of crucial importance in algebraic geometry, physics, and applied mathematics. Algebraic curves in this paper are intended as compact Riemann surfaces.

The study of the hyper-elliptic curves goes back to the beginning of the 20th century, and these appear in much detail in advanced text-books such as Baker
[
2
]
and Cassels and Flynn
[
9
]
. However, little has been considered for more general curves than the hyper-elliptic curves. In this paper, we consider a class of curves (
C_{ab}
curves) in the form

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}c_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}=0,

(1.1)

where
(i,j)
range over
ai+by\leq ab
,
0\leq i\leq b
,
0\leq j\leq a
for some mutually prime integers
a
,
b
, and
c_{i,j}
are constants such that (
1.1
) implies
\sum\limits_{i,j}ic_{i,j}x^{i-1}y^{j}\not=0
or
\sum\limits_{i,j}jc_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j-1}\not=0
.

This paper studies cohomologies of
C_{ab}
curves in terms of which the Klein–Weierstrass construction of multivariate Abelian and sigma functions is made possible. It does not seek further theories but focuses on the description of cohomologies of these curves.

According to the Riemann–Roch theorem, the entire holomorphic differentials make a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
of dimension
g
. For example, we can take them as

\displaystyle du_{i}(x,y)=\frac{x^{i-1}dx}{2y},

(1.2)

i=1,2,\dots,g
for the hyper-elliptic curve

\displaystyle y^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{2g+1}c_{i}x^{i}

(1.3)

with
c_{i,0}=c_{i}
,
c_{i,1}=0
,
i=0,\dots,2g+1
.

We consider a 2-form
((x,y),(z,w))\mapsto R((x,y),(z,w))dxdz
over
C\times C
that has a (double) pole only at
(z,w)=(x,y)
and is normalized:

\displaystyle\lim_{(z,w)\rightarrow(x,y)}R((x,y),(z,w))(x-z)^{2}=1.

Such a 2-form can be written as

\displaystyle R((x,y),(z,w)):=\frac{d}{dz}\Omega((x,y),(z,w))+\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{du_{i}(x,y)}{dx}\frac{dr_{i}(z,w)}{dz},

(1.4)

using differentials
\{dr_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
of the second kind that have poles only at infinity and a meromorphic function
\Omega((x,y),(z,w))
. If we further require symmetry:

\displaystyle R((x,y),(z,w))=R((z,w),(x,y)),

then such an
\{dr_{i}\}
is unique modulo operation of
{\rm Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
and the space generated by
\{du_{i}\}
(see
[
5
]
for hyper-elliptic curves and
[
8
,
11
]
for general curves). We call such a normalized symmetric
R((x,y),(z,w))dxdz
Klein’s fundamental 2-form of second kind
[
14
,
15
]
.

For example, for the hyper-elliptic curve (
1.3
), such a 2-form can be obtained using the canonical meromorphic differentials
\{dr_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}

\displaystyle dr_{i}(z,w)=\sum_{k=i}^{2g-i}c_{k+1+i}(k+1-i)\frac{z^{k}}{2w}dz

(1.5)

and 1-form

\displaystyle\Omega((x,y),(z,w)):=\frac{y+w}{2(x-z)y}dx.

It is known that the normalized symmetric bilinear
R((x,y),(z,w))
can be expressed using (
1.4
) for
C_{ab}

[
17
]
and telescopic curves
[
1
]
although
\Omega((x,y),(z,w))
needs more extensions. However, for the canonical meromorphic differentials
\{dr_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
, no exact formula has been given. For example,
C_{34}
and
C_{35}
curves and
C_{45}
curves were studied in
[
6
]
and
[
12
]
, respectively.
Recently, the reference
[
7
]
addressed
C_{3,g+1}
curves with genus
g
. These results dealt with a specific class of curves and failed to obtain the formula for general
C_{ab}
curves with arbitrary mutually prime
a
,
b
. As a result, still many researchers are either numerically calculating or algorithmically computing
\{dr_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
given
\{c_{i,j}\}
and
\{du_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
. This paper extends (
1.5
) and obtains the general formula in a closed form for the
C_{ab}
curves. It contains all the existing results and has many applications including algebraic expression of sigma functions
[
17
]
, defining equations of the Jacobian varieties, etc.
[
5
,
10
]
.

This paper is organized as follows: Section
2
sets up the holomorphic differentials
\{du_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
and gives background of this paper.
Section
3
states and proves the main result (formula) and gives two typical examples both of which extend the previous cases. In the last section, we raise an open problem.

Background

Let
a
,
b
be mutually prime positive integers, and
C
the curve defined by

\displaystyle F(x,y):=\sum_{i,j}c_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}=0

with a unique point
\cal O

and

are

or
\sum\limits_{i,j}jc_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j-1}\not=0
for each

on the curve.

We consider the set of 1-forms
[
17

\displaystyle du_{i,j}=\frac{x^{i}y^{j}}{\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}(x,y)}dx,

where
(i,j)
range over

\displaystyle J(a,b):=\{(i,j)\,|\,i,j\geq 0,\,ai+bj\leq ab-a-b\}.

We know by the general theory that for
g

(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{g},y_{g})
on
C
, the sum of integrals from
\cal O
to those
g
points

\displaystyle u=(u_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in J(a,b)}=\sum_{(i,j)\in J(a,b)}\int_{\cal O}^{(x_{i},y_{i})}du_{i,j}

fill the whole space
{\mathbb{C}}^{g}
, where the weights of the variables
u_{i,j}
are
ab-a(i+1)-b(j+1)
. If we regard the weight of each coefficient
c_{i,j}
in (
1.1
) is
ai+bi-ab
, the weights assigned to the differentials render
F(x,y)
homogeneous.

Proposition 2.1
.

The set
J(a,b)
has

\displaystyle\#J(a,b)=g=\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}{2}

elements, and the zero orders of
\{du_{i,j}\}
are nonnegative and different.

Definition 2.2
.

The 2-form
R((x,y),(z,w))dxdz
on
C\times C
is called the fundamental 2-form of the second kind if the following conditions are satisfied

1)

it is symmetric:
R((x,y),(z,w))=R((z,w),(x,y))
,

2)

it has its only pole along the diagonal of
C\times C
, and

3)

in the vicinity of each point, it is expanded in power series as

\displaystyle R((x,y),(z,w))dxdz=\frac{dt_{xy}dt_{zw}}{(t_{x,y}-t_{z,w})^{2}}+O(1)

as
(z,w)\rightarrow(x,y)
, where
t_{x,y}
and
t_{z,w}
are local coordinates of points
(x,y)
and
(z,w)
, respectively.

We shall look for a realization of
R((x,y),(z,w))
in the form

\displaystyle R((x,y),(z,w))=\frac{G((x,y),(z,w))}{(x-z)^{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}(x,y)\frac{\partial F}{\partial w}(z,w)},

where
G((x,y),(z,w))
is a symmetric polynomial in its variables.

The fundamental 2-form of the second kind for the
\boldsymbol{C_{ab}}
curves

Hereafter, we denote
F_{z}:=\frac{\partial F(z,w)}{\partial z}
,
F_{w}:=\frac{\partial F(z,w)}{\partial w}
,
H:=\frac{F(y,z)-F(w,z)}{y-z}
,
H_{z}:=\frac{\partial H}{\partial z}
, and
H_{w}:=\frac{\partial H}{\partial w}
.

We find symmetric
R((x,y),(z,w))
for the meromorphic function

\displaystyle\Omega((x,y),(z,w)):=\frac{H}{(x-z)F_{y}}.

To this end, if we note
\frac{dw}{dz}=-\frac{F_{z}}{F_{w}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\Omega}{dz}=\frac{(H_{z}+H_{w}\frac{dw}{dz})(x-z)+H}{(x-z)^{2}F_{y}}=\frac{(H_{z}F_{w}-H_{w}F_{z})(x-z)+HF_{w}}{(x-z)^{2}F_{y}F_{w}}.

(3.1)

Let
g_{j}:=\sum\limits_{i\colon\,0\leq ai+bj\leq ab}c_{i,j}z^{i}

g_{j}^{\prime}:=\frac{\partial g_{j}}{\partial z}

h_{j}:=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j-1}w^{i}y^{j-1-i}

\displaystyle F_{w}=\sum_{j=0}jw^{j-1}g_{j},\qquad H=\sum_{j=0}^{a}h_{j}g_{j},\qquad H_{z}=\sum_{j=0}^{a}h_{j}g_{j}^{\prime},\qquad H_{w}=\sum_{j=0}^{a}\frac{\partial h_{j}}{\partial w}g_{j}.

(3.2)

Let

\displaystyle I(i,j):=\left(-w^{i}\frac{\partial h_{j}}{\partial w}+jw^{j-1}h_{i}-\frac{\partial{h}_{i}}{\partial y}w^{j}\right)g_{i}^{\prime}g_{j}(x-z)+\big(jw^{j-1}h_{i}-jh_{j-1}w^{i}\big)g_{i}g_{j}

for

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{a}\sum_{j=0}^{a}I(i,j)=\sum_{i=0}^{a}\left\{-w^{i}g_{i}^{\prime}H_{w}+h_{i}g_{i}^{\prime}F_{w}-\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial y}g_{i}^{\prime}\sum_{j=0}^{a}w^{j}g^{j}\right\}(x-z)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\sum_{i=0}^a\sum_{j=0}^a I(i,j)=}{}+\sum_{i=0}^{a}\left\{h_{i}g_{i}F_{w}-w^{i}g_{i}\sum_{j=0}^{a}jh_{j-1}g_{j}\right\},

which coincides with the numerator of (

We seek

\displaystyle R((x,y),(z,w)):=\frac{d\Omega_{(z,w)}}{dz}+\sum_{(i,j)\in J(a,b)}\frac{du_{i,j}}{dx}\frac{dr_{i,j}}{dz}

is symmetric, in other words, its numerator

\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{a}\sum_{v=0}^{a}I(u,v)+(x-z)^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in J(a,b)}u_{i,j}(x,y)r_{i,j}(z,w)

\displaystyle\frac{dr_{i,j}}{dz}=\frac{r_{i,j}(z,w)}{F_{w}}

\displaystyle\frac{du_{i,j}}{dx}=\frac{u_{i,j}(x,y)}{F_{y}}

with
u_{i,j}(x,y)=x^{i}y^{j}

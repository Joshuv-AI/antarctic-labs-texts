Codes Correcting Two Bursts of Exactly b b Deletions
Zuo Ye, Yubo Sun, Wenjun Yu, Gennian Ge, Ohad Elishco

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate codes designed to correct two bursts of deletions, where each burst has a length of exactly b b , where b > 1 b>1 . The previous best construction, achieved through the syndrome compression technique, had a redundancy of at most 7 ​ log ⁡ n + O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n / log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 7\log n+O\left(\log n/\log\log n\right) bits. In contrast, our work introduces a novel approach for constructing q q -ary codes that attain a redundancy of at most 5 ​ log ⁡ n + O ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 5\log n+O(\log\log n) bits for all b > 1 b>1 and q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 . Additionally, for the case where b = 1 b=1 , we present a new construction of q q -ary two-deletion correcting codes with a redundancy of 5 ​ log ⁡ n + O ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 5\log n+O(\log\log n) bits, for all q > 2 q>2 .

Remark II.1

There is another possibility: the two bursts might overlap and result in a single burst that is shorter than
2b

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we present constructions of
q
-ary codes capable of correcting two bursts of exactly
b
deletions with redundancy at most
5\log n+O(\log\log n)
for all
b\geq 2
and
q\geq 2
, which improves the redundancy of codes derived from the syndrome compression technique. Inspired by these ideas, we provide a new construction of
q
-ary two-deletion correcting codes with redundancy
5\log n+O(\log\log n)
for all
q>2
.

In this work, it is required that the two bursts have the same length. Allowing the two deletion-bursts to have different sizes would make the error model more general and applicable to a wider range of practical scenarios. Indeed, such a generalization would be more realistic, as burst deletions in real-world applications (e.g., communication systems or storage systems) may not always have equal lengths. The case where the two bursts have different lengths seems more involved. Extending our work to handle bursts of different sizes is a promising direction for future research.

A more complex problem is to construct codes capable of correcting at most two bursts of deletions, where each burst has a length at most
b
. Our technique fails in these setup since we can not even know the number of bursts or the length of each burst occurring in a received sequence. This problem is also left for future research.

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{\ln q}\left[\ln\left(1-\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\frac{1}{q}+\epsilon\right)\ln\left(\frac{1-(\epsilon q)/(q-1)}{1+\epsilon q}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{\ln q}\left[-\ln\left(1-\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{q}+\epsilon\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\frac{1}{q}+\epsilon\right)\ln\left(1+\epsilon q\right)\right].

Since
\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{q-1}{2q}\right\}
, we have
0<\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}<\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\epsilon q<1
. Then it follows from
Fact

A.1
that
-\ln\left(1-\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2(q-1)^{2}}+\frac{2\epsilon^{3}q^{3}}{(q-1)^{3}}
,
\ln\left(1-\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}\right)\leq-\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2(q-1)^{2}}
and
-\ln(1+\epsilon q)\leq-\epsilon q+\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2}
. Now by
Equation

24
, we have

\displaystyle H_{q}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}-\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\epsilon^{3}q^{3}}{(q-1)^{3}\ln q}+\frac{1}{\ln q}\left[\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2(q-1)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\frac{1}{q}+\epsilon\right)\left(-\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2(q-1)^{2}}-\epsilon q+\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\epsilon^{3}q^{3}}{(q-1)^{3}\ln q}+\frac{1}{\ln q}\left[\frac{\epsilon q}{q-1}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2(q-1)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\frac{1}{q}+\epsilon\right)\left(-\frac{\epsilon q^{2}}{q-1}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{3}(q-2)}{2(q-1)^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\epsilon^{3}q^{3}(q^{2}-3q+6)}{2(q-1)^{3}\ln q}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\ln q}\left[\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2(q-1)^{2}}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{q-1}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}(q-2)}{2(q-1)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}q^{2}}{2(q-1)\ln q}+\frac{\epsilon^{3}q^{3}(q^{2}-3q+6)}{2(q-1)^{3}\ln q}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4},

where (a) follows from the fact that
\epsilon<\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q^{2}-3q+6}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{(q-1)\ln q}{2q^{3}}\right)
.
∎

Appendix B
Encoding sequences into regular sequences

In this section, we give two methods to encode a sequence into a
d
-regular sequence using only one redundant symbol. The first method is an instantiation of the encoding idea in the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 11]
. The second method relies on the sequence replacement technique. The first method works for more flexible parameters
n
and
d
. The time complexity of the second method is lower.

For any
q\geq 2
, let
Q\subseteq\Sigma_{q}^{m}
be the set of sequences containing two consecutive coordinates smaller than
\left\lceil q/2\right\rceil
and two consecutive coordinates no less than
\left\lceil q/2\right\rceil
. According to
[
19
, Lemma 10]
, it holds that
\left|Q\right|\geq 2^{m}-2\times(1.62)^{m+2}
when
q=2
. By the definition of
q
-ary
d
-regular sequences, it is clear that
\left|Q\right|\geq q^{m}-2\times(1.62)^{2}\times(0.81q)^{m}
for any even
q
. In
Lemma

IV.4
, it is shown that
\left|Q\right|\geq q^{m}-2\times(0.87q)^{m}
.

B-A

The first method

Suppose that
d
and
n
satisfy conditions in
Lemma

IV.4
. In this subsection, let
m=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\log n\right\rfloor
,
k=\left\lfloor n/m\right\rfloor
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\bigg\{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{(0)}\cdots\mathbf{x}^{(k-1)}

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{(k)}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}^{(i)}\in Q,\forall 0\leq i\leq k-1\\
\mathbf{x}^{(k)}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n-km}\end{array}\bigg\}.

Then in the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 11]
and
Lemma

IV.4
, it is shown that each sequence in
d
-regular and
\left|\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right|\geq q^{n-1}
.
We aim to encode a sequence of length
n-1
into
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. Before showing the algorithm, we need to define some functions.

For
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n-1}
, define
f_{\textup{dec}}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}q^{i-1}
. It is clear that
f_{\textup{dec}}(\mathbf{x})\in\left[0,q^{n-1}-1\right]
and
f_{\textup{dec}}(\mathbf{x})
can be computed in
O(n)
time.

We further assume that
n
is sufficiently large such that
m\geq 8
when
q
is odd, or such that
m\geq 12
when
q
is even. Under this assumption, it is easy to verify that
\left|Q\right|\geq q^{m-1}\geq q^{n-km}
.
Since
\left|Q\right|^{k}-1+\left|Q\right|^{k}\left(q^{n-km}-1\right)=\left|\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right|-1\geq q^{n-1}-1
, each integer
a
in
\left[0,q^{n-1}-1\right]
can be uniquely written as
a=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}\left|Q\right|^{i}
, where
0\leq a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1}<\left|Q\right|
and
0\leq a_{k}<q^{n-km}\leq\left|Q\right|
. Denote
g_{Q}(a)=\left(a_{0},\ldots,a_{k-1},a_{k}\right)
. The time complexity of computing
g_{Q}(a)
is
O\left(\log_{\left|Q\right|}\left(q^{n-1}-1\right)\right)=O\left(\log\left(q^{n-1}-1\right)/\log\left|Q\right|\right)=O\left(n/m\right)
.

Since
\left|Q\right|\leq q^{m}=O\left(n^{d\log q/2}\right)
, we can efficiently construct the set
Q
by brute-force. There is a bijection between the set
Q
and the integer set
\left[0,\left|Q\right|-1\right]
. We can build a lookup table which gives such a bijection. Then for each sequence in
Q
we can find its corresponding integer in
\left[0,\left|Q\right|-1\right]
in
O\left(n^{d\log q/2}\right)
time. Similarly, there is a bijection between
\Sigma_{q}^{n-km}
and the set
\left[0,q^{n-km}-1\right]
and for each sequence in
\Sigma_{q}^{n-km}
, we can find its corresponding integer in
\left[0,q^{n-km}-1\right]
in
O\left(n^{d\log q/2}\right)
time by a lookup table.

Our encoding algorithm is described as follows. For a sequence
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n-1}
, compute
f_{\textup{dec}}(\mathbf{x})
and
g_{Q}\left(f_{\textup{dec}}(\mathbf{x})\right)
. Suppose that
g_{Q}\left(f_{\textup{dec}}(\mathbf{x})\right)=\left(a_{0},\ldots,a_{k-1},a_{k}\right)
, where
0\leq a_{0},\ldots,a_{k-1}<\left|Q\right|
and
0\leq a_{k}<q^{n-km}
. As analyzed above, we can map
a_{i}
to a sequence
\mathbf{y}^{(i)}
in
Q
for each
0\leq i\leq k-1
, and map
a_{k}
to a sequence
\mathbf{y}^{(k+1)}
in
\Sigma_{q}^{n-km}
. At last, concatenate these sequences and we get the output
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{y}^{(0)}\cdots\mathbf{y}^{(k-1)}\mathbf{y}^{(k)}
, which is
d
-regular. Clearly, the time complexity of the encoding process is
O\left((k+1)n^{d\log q/2}\right)=O\left(n^{d\log q/2+1}/m\right)
.

Decoding
\mathbf{y}
to the original sequence is straightforward. And the time complexity is also
O\left(n^{d\log q/2+1}/m\right)
.

B-B

The second method

In this subsection, we give a new encoding algorithm based on the sequence replacement technique. This technique has been widely used in the literature to encode a sequence to a sequence without forbidden substrings
[
29
,
44
,
36
,
38
]
.

Let
m=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{3}\log n\right\rfloor
(where
d
will be specified later) and
Q
be the set defined at the beginning of this section. Then we have that
\left|\Sigma_{q}^{m}\setminus Q\right|\leq(c\cdot q)^{m}
for some
0<c<1
. Therefore, there is an injective mapping
f_{Q}
from
\Sigma_{q}^{m}\setminus Q
to the set
\Sigma_{q}^{\left\lceil m\left(1+\log_{q}c\right)\right\rceil}
. Then the inverse mapping
f_{Q}^{-1}:\left\{f_{Q}:\mathbf{z}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}\setminus Q\right\}\rightarrow\Sigma_{q}^{m}\setminus Q
is well-defined. In addition, by building a lookup table, both
f_{Q}
and
f_{Q}^{-1}
can be computed in
O\left((cq)^{m}\right)=O\left(n^{\frac{d}{3}\log(cq)}\right)
time.

In the rest of this subsection, denote
k=\left\lfloor n/m\right\rfloor
. Fix a suitable
d
such that when
n
is sufficiently large, we have
m\geq\left\lceil m\left(1+\log_{q}c\right)\right\rceil+\left\lceil\log_{q}k\right\rceil+4
.

For a sequence
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n-1}
, we first append a symbol
0
to
\mathbf{x}
and get the sequence
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}0
. This appended symbol
0
will help to indicate when our decoding algorithm ends. Next, we partition sequence
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
as
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)}\cdots\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k)}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}
, where
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}
for each
1\leq i\leq k
and
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n-km}
. For any symbol
\alpha\in\Sigma_{q}
and integer
\ell\geq 1
, let
\alpha^{\ell}
denote the sequence consisting of
\ell
symbols
\alpha
. For simpler notations, denote
m_{1}=\left\lfloor\frac{m-\left\lceil m\left(1+\log_{q}c\right)\right\rceil-\left\lceil\log_{q}k\right\rceil}{2}\right\rfloor
and
m_{2}=\left\lceil\frac{m-\left\lceil m\left(1+\log_{q}c\right)\right\rceil-\left\lceil\log_{q}k\right\rceil}{2}\right\rceil
.

The idea behind the encoding algorithm is as follows.

Step
1

For each
1\leq i\leq k
, check if
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\in Q
. If not, delete
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}
from
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
and still denote the resulted sequence by
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
.

Step
2

Then we append the sequence
f_{Q}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\right)b_{q}(i)0^{m_{1}}(q-1)^{m_{2}}
to the end of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
. Here, we use
b_{q}(i)
to denote the
q
-ary representation of integer

. Clearly,
b_{q}(i)\in\Sigma_{q}^{\left\lceil\log_{q}k\right\rceil}
.

Step
3

Note that the substring
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}
will never be deleted after Step 1 and Step 2. Continue Step 1 and Step 2 until the left of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}
is empty or all
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}
to the left of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}
is in

.

Since
m\geq\left\lceil m\left(1+\log_{q}c\right)\right\rceil+\left\lceil\log_{q}k\right\rceil+4
, we have
m_{2}\geq m_{1}\geq 2
. Therefore, once Step 2 is executed, a sequence in

is appended to the end. Note that the length of

is
m
. This implies that the length of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
does not change after Step 2.
Denote the sequence at the end of this algorithm by
\mathbf{y}
. The fact that

implies

\mathbf{y}
of length at least

\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}
(which is on the left of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}
) or contains a substring of the form

(which is on the right of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}

\mathbf{y}
is

-regular.

\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j+1)}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}

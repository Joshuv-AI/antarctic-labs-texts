Eisenstein cohomology classes for GL N \displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{N} over imaginary quadratic fields
Nicolas Bergeron, Pierre Charollois, Luis E. Garcia

Abstract
. We study the arithmetic of degree N − 1 \displaystyle N-1 Eisenstein cohomology classes for the locally symmetric spaces attached to GL N \displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{N} over an imaginary quadratic field k \displaystyle k . Under natural conditions we evaluate these classes on ( N − 1 ) \displaystyle(N-1) -cycles associated to degree N \displaystyle N extensions L / k \displaystyle L/k as linear combinations of generalised Dedekind sums. As a consequence we prove a remarkable conjecture of Sczech and Colmez expressing critical values of L \displaystyle L -functions attached to Hecke characters of F \displaystyle F as polynomials in Kronecker–Eisenstein series evaluated at torsion points on elliptic curves with multiplication by k \displaystyle k . We recover in particular the algebraicity of these critical values.



1.
Introduction

The relationship between Eisenstein series, the cohomology of arithmetic groups and special values of
\displaystyle L
-functions has been studied extensively.

A classical example is that of weight
\displaystyle 2
Eisenstein series associated to a pair
\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)\in(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. Such a series can be defined as limits of finite sums:

E_{2,(\alpha,\beta)}(\tau)=\lim_{M\to+\infty}\sum_{m=-M}^{M}\left(\lim_{N\to+\infty}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n=-N}^{N}\frac{e^{2i\pi(m\alpha+n\beta)}}{(m\tau+n)^{2}}\right)\quad(\tau\in\mathscr{H}).

Here the prime on the sum means that we exclude the term
\displaystyle(m,n)=(0,0)
. When
\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
, the holomorphic
\displaystyle 1
-form

\displaystyle E_{2,(\alpha,\beta)}(\tau)d\tau
on Poincaré’s upper half-plane
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}

is invariant under any subgroup
\displaystyle\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
that fixes
\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)
modulo
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. This holomorphic form then represents a cohomology class in
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})
. A remarkable feature of these classes is that they are rational and even almost integral.

A convenient and compact way to state the precise integrality properties of these cohomology classes is to consider for each prime integer the ‘
\displaystyle p
-smoothed Eisenstein series’

\begin{split}E_{2,(\alpha,\beta)}^{(p)}(\tau)&=\sum_{j=1}^{p}E_{2,(\alpha+j/p,\beta)}(\tau)-pE_{2,(\alpha,\beta)}(\tau)\\
&=p(E_{2,(p\alpha,\beta)}(p\tau)-E_{2,(\alpha,\beta)}(\tau)),\end{split}

and suppose furthermore that
\displaystyle\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{0}(p)
. Then
\displaystyle E_{2,(\alpha,\beta)}^{(p)}(\tau)
yields a homomorphism
\displaystyle\Phi_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{(p)}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}

by the rule

\Phi_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{(p)}(\gamma)=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\gamma\tau_{0}}E_{2,(\alpha,\beta)}^{(p)}(\tau)d\tau,

for any base point
\displaystyle\tau_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
, and it is classical (see e.g.
[
36
, Theorem 13]
) that we have:

(1.1)

\Phi_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{(p)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{if }c=0,\\
(2\pi)^{2}\cdot\mathrm{sign}(c)\cdot\left(D_{p\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{pa}{|c|}\right)-pD_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{a}{|c|}\right)\right),&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Here
\displaystyle D_{\alpha,\beta}
denotes the
generalised Dedekind sum

D_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{c}\left(\left(\frac{j-\beta}{c}\right)\right)\ \left(\left(\frac{a(j-\beta)}{c}-\alpha\right)\right)\quad\mbox{for}\quad c>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad(a,c)=1,

where the symbol
\displaystyle((x))
is defined by

((x))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x-[x]-1/2&\mbox{if }x\mbox{ is not an integer},\\
0&\mbox{if }x\mbox{ is an integer}.\end{array}\right.

These sums define rational numbers and enjoy many beautiful arithmetical properties, see e.g.
[
29
]
. On the other hand a formula of Siegel
[
36
]
expresses the values at nonpositive integers of the
\displaystyle\zeta
-functions attached to real quadratic fields as periods of Eisenstein series. The expression (
1.1
) can therefore be turned into a very explicit expression for these special values. This implies in particular that they are essentially integral which is the key input in the construction by Coates and Sinnott
[
10
]
of
\displaystyle p
-adic
\displaystyle L
-functions over real quadratic fields.

Using Selberg’s and Langlands’ theory of Eisenstein series Harder has vastly generalised the above mentioned ‘Eisenstein cohomology classes.’ In
[
19
]
he constructed a complement to the cuspidal cohomology for the group
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{2}
over number fields and manage to construct rational representatives. In a more recent work Harder even managed to address some integrality properties of these classes, see
[
22
]
. However for the group
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{N}
it is hard to check that Eisenstein classes are rational and the automorphic form theory is not yet adapted to the study of integrality properties of these classes.

For
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{N}
over the field of rational numbers, Nori
[
27
]
and Sczech
[
32
]
have proposed constructions of Eisenstein cohomology classes that have turned out to be very efficient in practice to study the fine arithmetical properties of
\displaystyle L
-functions over totally real number fields, see e.g.
[
18
,
7
,
8
,
2
]
. Sczech’s approach more generally gives formulas analogous to (
1.1
). The goal of this paper is to prove similar formulas for the group
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{N}
over an imaginary quadratic field
\displaystyle k
. As a consequence we prove a remarkable conjecture of Sczech and Colmez
[
11
, Conjecture p. 205]
expressing critical values of
\displaystyle L
-functions attached to Hecke characters of finite extension of
\displaystyle k
as polynomials in Kronecker–Eisenstein series evaluated at torsion points on elliptic curves with multiplication by
\displaystyle k
.

We now describe in more details our main results.

1.1.
An Eisenstein cocycle for imaginary quadratic fields

Fix a positive integer
\displaystyle N\geq 2
. Let
\displaystyle k
be a quadratic imaginary field with ring of integers
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
and let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathscr{O}
be an ideal of prime norm
\displaystyle\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}
. Our first main result is the construction of an
\displaystyle(N-1)
-cocycle for the level
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
congruence subgroup

(1.2)

\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&{{}^{t}}b\\
c&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathscr{O})\ \right|\ a\in\mathscr{O},b\in\mathscr{O}^{N-1},c\in\mathfrak{p}^{N-1},D\in M_{N-1}(\mathscr{O})\right\}

of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathscr{O})
taking values in the space of polynomials in certain classical series called Kronecker–Eisenstein series.

Let us recall the definition of Kronecker–Eisenstein series (see
[
37
, VIII.§12]
). We fix once and for all an embedding
\displaystyle\sigma:k\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. For a fractional ideal
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}
of
\displaystyle k
and non-negative integers
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle q
, define

(1.3)

K^{p,q}(z,\mathfrak{I},s)=p!\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\mathfrak{I})}\frac{\overline{z+\lambda}^{q}}{(z+\lambda)^{p+1}|z+\lambda|^{s}}

where we assume that
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\displaystyle z\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{I})
. The series converges when
\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(s)>1+q-p
and has analytic continuation to
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{C}
that is regular at
\displaystyle s=0
. It is a classical result due to Damerell
[
13
]
that the values at
\displaystyle s=0
have the following algebraicity property:

(1.4)

K^{p,q}(z_{0},\mathfrak{I},0)\in\Omega_{\infty}^{1+p+q}\pi^{-q}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\quad\text{for }z_{0}\in k\backslash\sigma(\mathfrak{I}).

Here
\displaystyle\Omega_{\infty}
denotes any period of a
\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-rational holomorphic differential against a non-zero rational homology class on an elliptic curve with CM by
\displaystyle k
defined over
\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. In fact these series have almost integral values; we refer to
[
25
]
for precise results.

Next we introduce polynomials in the series
\displaystyle K^{p,q}(z,\mathfrak{I},s)
. For fractional ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{I}_{N}
and multi-indices
\displaystyle I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
and
\displaystyle J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, we set

(1.5)

K^{I,J}(z,\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N},s)=K^{i_{1},j_{1}}(z_{1},\mathfrak{I}_{1},s)\cdots K^{i_{N},j_{N}}(z_{N},\mathfrak{I}_{N},s).

More generally, for an
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
-lattice
\displaystyle\Lambda\subset k^{N}
, we pick fractional ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{I}_{N}
such that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N}
has finite index in
\displaystyle\Lambda
and set

(1.6)

\begin{split}K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,s)&=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda/\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N}}K^{I,J}(z+\sigma(\lambda),\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N},s)\\
&=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\prod_{1\leq k\leq N}i_{k}!\frac{\overline{z_{k}+\sigma(\lambda_{k})}^{j_{k}}}{(z_{k}+\sigma(\lambda_{k}))^{i_{k}+1}|z_{k}+\sigma(\lambda_{k})|^{s}}.\end{split}

As the last expression shows,
\displaystyle K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,s)
does not depend on the choice of the fractional ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{k}
. We set

(1.7)

K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda):=K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,0).

Each
\displaystyle K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda)
defines a smooth function on an open subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
obtained by removing all
\displaystyle\Lambda
-translates of a finite number of hyperplanes. We write

(1.8)

\mathscr{F}=\langle K^{I,J}(\gamma z,\Lambda)\ |\ \gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k),\Lambda\subset k^{N}\text{ an }\mathscr{O}-\text{lattice}\rangle

for the
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
-span of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
-translates of all functions
\displaystyle K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda)
.

Next we introduce the
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
-smoothed series

(1.9)

K^{I,J}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\mathscr{O}^{N})=K^{I,J}(z,\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\oplus\mathscr{O}^{N-1})-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\cdot K^{I,J}(z,\mathscr{O}^{N})

and, for
\displaystyle A\in M_{N}(\mathscr{O})
, we define the generalised Dedekind sum
\displaystyle D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{I,J}(z,A)
by

(1.10)

D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{I,J}(z,A)=\det A^{-1}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{I,J}(A^{-1}z,A^{-1}\mathscr{O}^{N})

if
\displaystyle A
is invertible and set
\displaystyle D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{I,J}(z,A)=0
otherwise. These sums are natural generalisations of Dedekind sums for imaginary quadratic fields and
\displaystyle N
variables.

Our first theorem shows that the series
\displaystyle D^{I,J}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,A)
can be combined into a homogeneous
\displaystyle(N-1)
-cocycle for
\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
. In the following statement, for a multi-index
\displaystyle I\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, we write
\displaystyle|I|=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{N}
. When
\displaystyle I
(resp.
\displaystyle J
) runs over multi-indices with
\displaystyle|I|=p
(resp.
\displaystyle|J|=q
), the vectors

e^{I}:=e_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots e_{N}^{i_{n}}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{p}\mathbb{C}^{N}\quad(\text{resp. }\overline{e}^{J}:=\overline{e_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{e_{N}}^{j_{N}}\in\overline{\mathrm{Sym}^{q}\mathbb{C}^{N}})

form a basis of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}^{p}\mathbb{C}^{N}
(resp. of
\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Sym}^{q}\mathbb{C}^{N}})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
\displaystyle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
, define

A(\underline{\gamma})=(\gamma_{1}e_{1}|\cdots|\gamma_{N}e_{1})\in M_{N}(\mathscr{O}).

The map

\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})^{N}\to\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{p}{\mathbb{C}^{N}}\otimes\overline{\mathrm{Sym}^{q}\mathbb{C}^{N}}

given by

\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\underline{\gamma})=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{I,J}(z,A(\underline{\gamma}))\otimes A(\underline{\gamma})(e^{I}\otimes\overline{e}^{J})

is a homogeneous
\displaystyle(N-1)
-cocycle. Here the sum runs over all multi-indices
\displaystyle I,J\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
with
\displaystyle|I|=p
and
\displaystyle|J|=q
.

More concretely, the cocycle property of
\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}
means that

(1.11)

\begin{split}\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\gamma z,\gamma\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma\gamma_{N})&=\gamma\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\gamma z,\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N})\\
&=\sum_{|I|=p,\ |J|=q}D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{I,J}(z,A(\underline{\gamma}))\otimes\gamma A(\underline{\gamma})(e^{I}\otimes\overline{e}^{J})\end{split}

for any
\displaystyle\gamma,\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
, and

\sum_{1\leq k\leq N+1}(-1)^{k-1}\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\gamma_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\gamma_{k}},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1})=0

for any
\displaystyle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
(here as usual the notation
\displaystyle\widehat{\gamma_{k}}
means that the term
\displaystyle\gamma_{k}
is to be omitted).

More generally, in the body of the paper we introduce a cocycle
\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\underline{\gamma},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
for the
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
-lattice
\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})=\mathfrak{I}^{-1}\oplus\mathscr{O}^{N-1}
of
\displaystyle k^{N}
.

1.2.
Application to critical values of Hecke
\displaystyle L
-functions

We refer to
[
15
, Section 1]
or
[
9
,
34
,
30
]
for generalities on Hecke characters.
Let
\displaystyle L/k
be a field extension of degree
\displaystyle N>1
and let
\displaystyle n:L\to k
denote the norm map. We fix an algebraic Hecke character
\displaystyle\psi_{k}
of
\displaystyle k
of infinity type
\displaystyle(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and a Dirichlet character
\displaystyle\chi
of
\displaystyle L
,
and consider the algebraic Hecke character

(1.12)

\phi=\chi\cdot(\psi_{k}\circ n)

of
\displaystyle L
. We denote the conductor of
\displaystyle\phi
by
\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}
, so that for
\displaystyle\alpha\equiv 1\mod\mathfrak{f}
we have

\phi((\alpha))=n(\alpha)^{p}\overline{n(\alpha)}^{q}.

Note that if
\displaystyle k
is a maximal CM field in
\displaystyle L
then any algebraic Hecke character
\displaystyle\phi
of
\displaystyle L
is of the above form.

The Hecke
\displaystyle L
-function of
\displaystyle\phi
is

L(\phi,s)=\prod_{(\mathfrak{P},\mathfrak{f})=1}(1-\phi(\mathfrak{P})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{P}^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{f})=1}\phi(\mathfrak{a})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{-s},

where the sum, resp. the product, runs over integral ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}
, resp. prime ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}
, of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{L}
coprime to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}
. The global L-function of
\displaystyle\phi
is
\displaystyle\Lambda(\phi,s)=L_{\infty}(\phi,s)L(\phi,s)
, where

L_{\infty}(\phi,s)=\prod_{v|\infty}\Gamma(\phi_{v},s).

Here each
\displaystyle\phi_{v}
with
\displaystyle v|\infty
is of the form

z^{p}\overline{z}^{q}=(z\overline{z})^{w/2}\left(\frac{z}{\overline{z}}\right)^{(p-q)/2},

with
\displaystyle w=p+q
(the weight), and

\Gamma(\phi_{v},s)=2(2\pi)^{-(s-w/2+|p-q|/2)}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{w}{2}+\frac{|p-q|}{2}\right).

The value
\displaystyle L(\phi,s_{0})
at an integer
\displaystyle s_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
is said to be critical if and only if

\mathrm{ord}_{s=s_{0}}L_{\infty}(\phi,s)=\mathrm{ord}_{s=s_{0}}L_{\infty}(\phi^{-1},1-s)=0.

In our case this is equivalent to

\frac{w}{2}-\frac{|p-q|}{2}<s_{0}<1+\frac{w}{2}+\frac{|p-q|}{2}.

Our second main result is that for critical
\displaystyle s_{0}
the value
\displaystyle L(\phi,s_{0})
can be expressed as an explicit polynomial in Kronecker–Eisenstein series; answering positively a conjecture of Sczech and Colmez
[
11
, Conjecture p. 205]
. Note that the complex conjugate
\displaystyle\overline{\psi_{k}}
has weight
\displaystyle(q,p)
and that multiplying
\displaystyle\phi
by an integral power of the norm character shifts
\displaystyle s
by an integer. Thus we may assume that
\displaystyle p<0
and
\displaystyle q\geq 0
and consider only the critical value
\displaystyle L(\phi,0)
.

Our result is more conveniently expressed in terms of partial zeta functions, as follows. For integers
\displaystyle p,q
and integral ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}
,
\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}
of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{L}
, define

(1.13)

\zeta_{\mathfrak{f}}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{a},s)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{x\in U(\mathfrak{f})\backslash 1+\mathfrak{fa}^{-1}}\frac{\overline{n(x)}^{q}}{n(x)^{p+1}|n(x)|^{2s}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)\gg 0.

Here
\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{f})
denotes the group of units of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{L}^{\times}
that are congruent to
\displaystyle 1
modulo
\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}
.
(Since
\displaystyle u\overline{u}=1
for every
\displaystyle u\in\mathscr{O}^{\times}
, this is well-defined provided that
\displaystyle p+q+1
is divisible by the order of the subgroup
\displaystyle n(U(\mathfrak{f}))
of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}^{\times}
, which we assume.) Choosing integral ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{r}
giving a system of representatives for the ray class group
\displaystyle C_{\mathfrak{f}}
we can write

L(\phi,s)=\sum_{j}\phi(\mathfrak{a}_{j})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{-s}\zeta^{-p-1,q}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathfrak{a}_{j},s).

Given two distinct prime ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}
of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{L}
coprime to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}
and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}
, we define also the ‘smoothed’ partial zeta functions

(1.14)

\begin{split}\zeta^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{a},s)&=\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{P}^{-s}\zeta^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathfrak{aP},s)-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{P}^{1-s}\zeta^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathfrak{a},s)\\
\zeta^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{P},\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(\mathfrak{a},s)&=\mathrm{N}\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}^{-s}\zeta^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{f,\mathfrak{P}}}(\mathfrak{a\tilde{P}},s)-\mathrm{N}\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}^{-s}\zeta^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{a},s).\end{split}

These modified zeta functions appear in an expression for
\displaystyle L(\phi,s)
with modified Euler factors at
\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}
. Namely, setting

L_{\mathfrak{P}}(\phi\cdot\mathrm{N},s)=(1-\phi(\mathfrak{P})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{P}^{1-s})^{-1},\quad L_{\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(\phi,s)=(1-\phi(\tilde{\mathfrak{P}})\mathrm{N}\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}^{-s})^{-1},

and using the fact that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{P},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{r}\mathfrak{P}
is also a system of representatives of
\displaystyle C_{\mathfrak{f}}
, we have

(1.15)

\begin{split}L_{\mathfrak{P}}(\phi\cdot\mathrm{N},s)^{-1}L(\phi,s)&=\sum_{j}\phi(\mathfrak{a}_{j}\mathfrak{P})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{-s}\zeta^{-p-1,q}_{\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{a}_{j},s),\\
L_{\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(\phi,s)^{-1}L_{\mathfrak{P}}(\phi\cdot\mathrm{N},s)^{-1}L(\phi,s)&=\sum_{j}\phi(\mathfrak{a}_{j}\mathfrak{P}\tilde{\mathfrak{P}})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{-s}\zeta^{-p-1,q}_{\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{P},\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(\mathfrak{a}_{j},s),\end{split}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
\displaystyle\phi
be an algebraic Hecke character of the form (
1.12
). Assume that
\displaystyle p<0
and
\displaystyle q\geq 0
. Then

L(\phi,0)\in\Omega_{\infty}^{N(q-p)}\pi^{-Nq}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}.

Remark.
As was pointed out to us by Don Blasius, one can take
\displaystyle\Omega_{\infty}
to be a period of a holomorphic differential on an elliptic curve defined over
\displaystyle k_{ab}
— the maximal abelian extension of
\displaystyle k
. Then

L(\phi,0)\in\Omega_{\infty}^{N(q-p)}\pi^{-Nq}k_{ab}E,

where
\displaystyle E
is the CM field generated by the values of
\displaystyle\phi
. This follows from the fact that the ratio of two arithmetic automorphic functions with Fourier coefficients in
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{ab}
, and having the same weight, takes value in
\displaystyle k_{ab}
when evaluated at a CM point.

In fact, one can be more precise: Blasius
[
5
]
proves a reciprocity law for values at CM points of modular forms which generalizes that of Shimura for functions. According to it, if a value transforms by a Hecke character, then it is the Deligne period of the motive attached to the Hecke character. Since Theorem
1.2
expresses the
\displaystyle L(\phi,0)
as a linear combination of products of values of
\displaystyle L
-functions of modular Eisenstein series, the general law of Blasius should apply to show the following: Let
\displaystyle M(\phi)
be the motive — defined over
\displaystyle L
, with coefficients in
\displaystyle E
, and of rank one — attached to
\displaystyle\phi
and let
\displaystyle c^{+}\mathrm{Res}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}M(\phi)
be the period attached by Deligne
[
14
, §8]
, we have

L(\mathrm{Res}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}M(\phi))=c^{+}\mathrm{Res}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}M(\phi)\in(E\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\times}/E^{\times}

as conjectured by Deligne
[
14
]
as part of a much more general picture.

2.
Differential forms on the symmetric space of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})

Fix an integer
\displaystyle N\geq 2
and let
\displaystyle V=\mathbb{C}^{N}
(column vectors). We identify the points of the symmetric space

X:=G/K

of
\displaystyle G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
with positive definite hermitian
\displaystyle N
-by-
\displaystyle N
matrices
\displaystyle h
of unit determinant via the map

gK\mapsto h:={{}^{t}}\overline{g}^{-1}\cdot g^{-1};

under this identification the action of
\displaystyle g\in G
on
\displaystyle X
by left multiplication corresponds to the action
\displaystyle g\cdot h:={{}^{t}}\overline{g}^{-1}hg^{-1}
. A matrix
\displaystyle h\in X
defines a positive definite hermitian form on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
given by
\displaystyle v\mapsto v^{*}hv
. The entries
\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle(1\leq i,j\leq N)
of
\displaystyle h
define smooth functions
\displaystyle h_{ij}:X\to\mathbb{C}
.

We write
\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(V)
for the space of Schwartz functions on
\displaystyle V
.
For
\displaystyle p,q\geq 0
, let

(2.1)

V^{p,q}=\mathrm{Sym}^{p}V^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{q}\overline{V};

it is naturally a representation of
\displaystyle G
. We will identify elements of
\displaystyle V^{p,q}
with linear functionals on the tensor product of the complex vector spaces
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}^{p}V
(homogeneous holomorphic polynomials of degree
\displaystyle p
on
\displaystyle V^{\vee}
) and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}^{q}\overline{V}^{\vee}
(homogeneous anti-holomorphic polynomials of degree
\displaystyle q
on
\displaystyle V
).

The natural action of
\displaystyle G
on
\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(V)
defined by
\displaystyle(g\cdot f)(v)=f(g^{-1}v)
turns
\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(V)\otimes V^{p,q}
into a smooth
\displaystyle G
-module. Let
\displaystyle A^{*}(X;\mathscr{S}(V)\otimes V^{p,q})
be the space of differential forms on
\displaystyle X
valued in
\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(V)\otimes V^{p,q}
. This space carries an action of
\displaystyle G
given by

(g,\omega(x,Y))\mapsto g\cdot\omega(g^{-1}x,g^{-1}Y),\quad x\in X,\ Y\in\land T_{x}X.

In this section we introduce
\displaystyle G
-invariant differential forms

(2.2)

\begin{split}\psi^{p,q}\in A^{N-1}(X;\mathscr{S}(V)\otimes V^{p,q})^{G}\end{split}

valued in this
\displaystyle G
-module.

2.1.
Polynomial forms

Fix a vector
\displaystyle v\in V
. We write
\displaystyle(hv)_{1},\ldots,(hv)_{N}
(resp.
\displaystyle(dhv)_{1},\ldots,(dhv)_{N})
for the components of the vector
\displaystyle hv
(resp.
\displaystyle dhv
):

(2.3)

\begin{split}(hv)_{i}&=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}h_{ij}v_{j}\in C^{\infty}(X).\\
(dhv)_{i}&=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}dh_{ij}v_{j}\in A^{1}(X).\end{split}

Define

(2.4)

p(v)=2(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}(hv)_{i}(dhv)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(dhv)_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(dhv)_{1}\in A^{N-1}(X).

(Here, as usual, the term under the symbol
\displaystyle\widehat{\quad}
is to be omitted). Note that, as a function of
\displaystyle v
,
\displaystyle p
is a (holomorphic) polynomial of degree
\displaystyle N
and so defines a form

p\in A^{N-1}(X;\mathbb{C}[V]).

The conjugate polynomial
\displaystyle\overline{p(v)}
defines a form in
\displaystyle A^{N-1}(X;\mathbb{C}[\overline{V}])
. Since
\displaystyle h
is hermitian, we have

\overline{(hv)_{i}}=\sum_{j}\overline{h_{ij}v_{j}}=\sum_{j}\overline{v_{j}}h_{ji}=(v^{*}h)_{i}

and so we can write

(2.5)

\overline{p(v)}=2(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}(v^{*}h)_{i}(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(v^{*}dh)_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}.

Lemma 2.1
.

The form
\displaystyle\overline{p}
is
\displaystyle G
-invariant. That is, for
\displaystyle g\in G
we have

g^{*}\overline{p(gv)}=\overline{p(v)},\qquad v\in V.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\displaystyle v\neq 0
(the case
\displaystyle v=0
is obvious). The statement then follows from the fact that given a representation of a group
\displaystyle G
on an
\displaystyle N
-dimensional complex vector space
\displaystyle W
, and a basis
\displaystyle e_{1},\ldots,e_{N}
of
\displaystyle W
with dual basis
\displaystyle e_{1}^{\vee},\ldots,e_{N}^{\vee}\in W^{\vee}
, the element
\displaystyle e_{1}\otimes e_{1}^{\vee}+\cdots+e_{N}\otimes e_{N}^{\vee}
of
\displaystyle W\otimes W^{\vee}
is
\displaystyle G
-invariant.

Namely, consider the
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\displaystyle W\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
spanned by
\displaystyle(v^{*}h)_{1},\ldots,(v^{*}h)_{N}
. For
\displaystyle g\in G
we have

(gv)^{*}(g\cdot h)=v^{*}{{}^{t}}\overline{g}({{}^{t}}\overline{g}^{-1}hg^{-1})=v^{*}hg^{-1}.

This shows that
\displaystyle W
is naturally a representation of
\displaystyle G
that is isomorphic to the dual
\displaystyle V^{\vee}
of
\displaystyle V
. The same statement (with same proof) holds for the
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\displaystyle\tilde{W}\subset A^{1}(X)
spanned by
\displaystyle(v^{*}dh)_{1},\ldots,(v^{*}dh)_{N}
.

Consider the map

W\otimes\wedge^{N-1}\tilde{W}\to\wedge^{N}\tilde{W},\quad w\otimes\tilde{w}=dw\wedge\tilde{w}.

Here
\displaystyle\wedge^{N}\tilde{W}\simeq\mathbb{C}\cdot(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\ldots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}
is isomorphic to the trivial
\displaystyle G
-representation via the map
\displaystyle z\cdot(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\ldots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\mapsto z
. Thus we obtain a pairing
\displaystyle W\otimes\wedge^{N-1}\tilde{W}\to\mathbb{C}
. A direct check shows that the basis
\displaystyle(-1)^{N-i}(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(v^{*}dh)_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}
(
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq N
) of
\displaystyle\wedge^{N-1}\tilde{W}
is dual to the basis
\displaystyle(v^{*}h)_{i}
(
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq N
) of
\displaystyle W
, and the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle v\neq 0
. Then the form

(v^{*}hv)^{-N}\overline{p(v)}\in A^{N-1}(X)

is closed.

Proof.

An equivalent statement is the equality

(2.6)

Nd(v^{*}hv)\wedge\overline{p(v)}=(v^{*}hv)d\overline{p(v)}.

Differentiating (
2.5
) we obtain

(2.7)

\begin{split}d\overline{p(v)}&=2(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}d\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}(v^{*}h)_{i}(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(v^{*}dh)_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\right)\\
&=2N(-1)^{(N+2)(N-1)/2}(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}.\end{split}

On the other hand we have
\displaystyle d(v^{*}hv)=\sum_{j}(v^{*}dh)_{j}v_{j}
and hence

Nd(v^{*}hv)\wedge\overline{p(v)}\\
\begin{split}&=2N(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\left(\sum_{j}(v^{*}dh)_{j}v_{j}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}(v^{*}h)_{i}(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(v^{*}dh)_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\right)\\
&=2N(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\sum_{j}(-1)^{j-1}(v^{*}dh)_{j}v_{j}(v^{*}h)_{j}(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(v^{*}dh)_{j}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\\
&=2N(-1)^{(N+2)(N-1)/2}\left(\sum_{j}v_{j}(v^{*}h)_{j}\right)(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\\
&=(v^{*}hv)d\overline{p(v)}.\end{split}

and the assertion follows.
∎

2.2.
Schwartz forms

We can now define the forms
\displaystyle\psi^{p,q}
mentioned in the introduction to this section.
First we consider the case
\displaystyle p=q=0
: for
\displaystyle v\in V
, we define

(2.8)

\psi^{0,0}(v)=e^{-v^{*}hv}\overline{p(v)}.

Remark.
The form
\displaystyle\psi^{0,0}
arises naturally as a component of a characteristic form defined by Mathai and Quillen. More precisely, the vector bundle
\displaystyle\mathscr{V}=X\times V
over
\displaystyle X
with fiber
\displaystyle V
carries a tautological metric, and the main result of
[
26
]
is the construction of a canonical Thom form
\displaystyle U\in A^{2N}(X\times V)
and an infinitesimal transgression
\displaystyle\tilde{U}
of
\displaystyle U
in
\displaystyle A^{2N-1}(X)
(denoted
\displaystyle-i_{X}U_{t}
in
[
26
, §7]
). A vector
\displaystyle v\in V
defines a section of
\displaystyle\mathscr{V}
over
\displaystyle X
, and the form
\displaystyle\psi^{0,0}(v)
is essentially obtained from
\displaystyle\tilde{U}
by contracting with the vector fields
\displaystyle\partial_{z_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{z_{N}}
(this gives a form in
\displaystyle A^{N-1}(X\times V)
) and then pulling back by this section. We refer to
[
4
]
for more details on this perspective.

Note that the hermitian form
\displaystyle v\mapsto v^{*}hv
on
\displaystyle V
is positive definite and so
\displaystyle\psi^{0,0}
, as a function of
\displaystyle v
, belongs to the Schwartz space
\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(V)
. Also note that, for any
\displaystyle g\in G
, the expression
\displaystyle v^{*}hv
is invariant upon replacing
\displaystyle h
with
\displaystyle g^{*}h
and
\displaystyle v
with
\displaystyle gv
, and so
Section 2.1
implies that
\displaystyle\psi^{0,0}
is
\displaystyle G
-invariant:

(2.9)

g^{*}\psi^{0,0}(gv)=\psi^{0,0}(v),\qquad g\in G.

Thus
\displaystyle\psi^{0,0}\in A^{N-1}(X;\mathscr{S}(V))^{G}
.

For arbitrary
\displaystyle p,q\geq 0
we define

\psi^{p,q}\in A^{N-1}(X;\mathscr{S}(V)\otimes V^{p,q})^{G}

so that its value on
\displaystyle P\otimes\overline{Q}
, where
\displaystyle P
(resp.
\displaystyle Q
) is a holomorphic polynomial of degree
\displaystyle p
on
\displaystyle V^{\vee}
(resp. a holomorphic polynomial of degree
\displaystyle q
on
\displaystyle V
), is given by

(2.10)

\begin{split}\psi^{p,q}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q})=\overline{Q}(\overline{v})P(-\partial_{z_{1}},\ldots,-\partial_{z_{N}})\psi^{0,0}(v)\end{split}

From now on we often omit the indices
\displaystyle p,q
and simply write
\displaystyle\psi(v,P\otimes\overline{Q})
.
One can give a more explicit expression for
\displaystyle\psi(v,P\otimes\overline{Q})
: the identity

(2.11)

-\partial_{z_{i}}\left(e^{-v^{*}hv}\right)=(v^{*}h)_{i}e^{-v^{*}hv}

gives

(2.12)

P(-\partial_{z_{1}},\ldots,-\partial_{z_{N}})\left(e^{-v^{*}hv}\right)=P((v^{*}h)_{1},\ldots,(v^{*}h)_{N})e^{-v^{*}hv};

since if
\displaystyle\overline{p(v)}
is an anti-holomorphic polynomial we have
\displaystyle\partial_{z_{i}}\overline{p(v)}=0
for all
\displaystyle i
, and we conclude that

(2.13)

\psi(v,P\otimes\overline{Q})=e^{-v^{*}hv}\overline{p(v,P,Q)},

with

(2.14)

\overline{p(v,P,Q)}:=\overline{Q(v)}P(v^{*}h)\overline{p(v)}.

Note that
\displaystyle\overline{p(\cdot,P,Q)}
is an anti-holomorphic polynomial in
\displaystyle v
of degree
\displaystyle N+p+q
. This expression shows that, generalizing the invariance property (
2.9
), we have

(2.15)

\begin{split}g^{*}\psi(gv,gP\otimes\overline{gQ})&=\psi(v,P\otimes\overline{Q}).\end{split}

The following is a generalization of
Section 2.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle v\neq 0
. For any
\displaystyle P\in\mathrm{Sym}^{p}V
and
\displaystyle Q\in\mathrm{Sym}^{q}V^{\vee}
, the form

(v^{*}hv)^{-N-p}\overline{p(v,P,Q)}\in A^{N-1}(X)

is closed.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle d\overline{Q(v)}=0
, it suffices to assume that
\displaystyle Q=1
and that
\displaystyle P
is monomial, say
\displaystyle P=e^{I}
for some multi-index
\displaystyle I
of degree
\displaystyle p
. Then

\displaystyle P(v^{*}h)=(v^{*}h)_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots(v^{*}h)_{N}^{i_{N}}
and

dP(v^{*}h)=\left(\sum_{j}i_{j}\frac{d(v^{*}h)_{j}}{(v^{*}h)_{j}}\right)P(v^{*}h),

and so

dP(v^{*}h)\wedge\overline{p(v)}\\
\begin{split}&=2(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}P(v^{*}h)\left(\sum_{j}i_{j}\frac{d(v^{*}h)_{j}}{(v^{*}h)_{j}}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}(v^{*}h)_{i}(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(v^{*}dh)_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\right)\\
&=2(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}P(v^{*}h)\sum_{j}i_{j}(-1)^{j-1}(v^{*}dh)_{j}\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(v^{*}dh)_{j}}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\\
&=2(-1)^{(N+2)(N-1)/2}\left(\sum_{j}i_{j}\right)P(v^{*}h)(v^{*}dh)_{N}\wedge\cdots\wedge(v^{*}dh)_{1}\\
&=pN^{-1}P(v^{*}h)d\overline{p(v)},\end{split}

where the last equality follows from (
2.7
). Using (
2.6
) we compute

d((v^{*}hv)^{-N-p}P(v^{*}h)\overline{p(v)})\\
\begin{split}&=(v^{*}hv)^{-N-p-1}\left[-(N+p)d(v^{*}hv)\wedge P(v^{*}h)\overline{p(v)}+(v^{*}hv)dP(v^{*}h)\wedge\overline{p(v)}+(v^{*}hv)P(v^{*}h)d\overline{p(v)}\ \right]\\
&=(v^{*}hv)^{-N-p}\left[-(N+p)N^{-1}+pN^{-1}+1\right]P(v^{*}h)d\overline{p(v)}\\
&=0.\end{split}

∎

2.3.
Mellin transform

We define
\displaystyle\eta(v,s)
to be the Mellin transform of
\displaystyle\psi(v)
; that is, for holomorphic polynomials
\displaystyle P
and
\displaystyle Q
define

(2.16)

\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tv,P\otimes\overline{Q})t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}.

Then

(2.17)

g^{*}\eta(gv,gP\otimes\overline{gQ},s)=\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s),\quad g\in G

because
\displaystyle\psi
is
\displaystyle G
-invariant. Since
\displaystyle\overline{p(tv,P,Q)}=t^{N+p+q}\overline{p(v,P,Q)}
, we have

(2.18)

\begin{split}\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}v^{*}hv}t^{s+2N+2p}\frac{dt}{t}\overline{p(v,P,Q)}\\
&=2^{-1}\Gamma(N+p+s/2)(v^{*}hv)^{-s/2-N-p}\overline{p(v,P,Q)}.\end{split}

Lemma 2.4
.

We have

d\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)=c(s)(v^{*}hv)^{-s/2-N-p}\overline{Q(v)}P(v^{*}h)d\overline{p(v)},

where
\displaystyle c(s)=(-4N)^{-1}s\Gamma(N+p+s/2)
.

Proof.

By
Section 2.2
we have
\displaystyle d((v^{*}hv)^{-N-p}\overline{p(v,P,Q)})=0
. Using (
2.6
), we compute

\begin{split}2\Gamma(N+p+s/2)^{-1}&d\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)\\
&=d((v^{*}hv)^{-s/2}(v^{*}hv)^{-N-p}\overline{p(v,P,Q)})\\
&=-2^{-1}s(v^{*}hv)^{-1-s/2}d(v^{*}hv)\wedge(v^{*}hv)^{-N-p}\overline{p(v,P,Q)}\\
&=-(2N)^{-1}s(v^{*}hv)^{-s/2-N-p}\overline{Q(v)}P(v^{*}h)d\overline{p(v)}.\end{split}

∎

Using
Section 2.3
we can represent the form
\displaystyle d\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)
as a Mellin transform: we define

\phi\in A^{N}(X;\mathscr{S}(V)\otimes V^{p,q})^{G}

by

(2.19)

\phi(v,P\otimes\overline{Q})=e^{-v^{*}hv}\overline{Q(v)}P(v^{*}h)d\overline{p(v)};

then the above lemma implies that

(2.20)

d\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)=-\frac{s}{2N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(tv,P\otimes\overline{Q})t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}.

For further reference we note the homogeneity property (which follows from (
2.18
)):

(2.21)

\eta(zv,P(z\cdot)\otimes\overline{Q(z^{-1}\cdot)},s)=|z|^{-s}z^{-N}\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)

for
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

2.4.
Example: the case
\displaystyle N=2

We compute the form
\displaystyle\psi^{0,0}(v)
when
\displaystyle N=2
. We have

(2.22)

\begin{split}\psi^{0,0}(v)&=-2e^{-v^{*}hv}(\overline{(hv)}_{1}(\overline{dhv})_{2}-\overline{(hv)}_{2}(\overline{dhv})_{1})\\
&=-2e^{-v^{*}hv}(\omega_{11}\overline{v_{1}}^{2}+\omega_{12}\overline{v_{1}v_{2}}+\omega_{22}\overline{v_{2}}^{2}),\end{split}

with

(2.23)

\begin{split}\omega_{11}&=\overline{h_{11}}d\overline{h_{21}}-\overline{h_{21}}d{\overline{h_{11}}}=h_{11}dh_{12}-h_{12}dh_{11},\\
\omega_{12}&=h_{11}dh_{22}-h_{12}dh_{21}+h_{21}dh_{12}-h_{22}dh_{11},\\
\omega_{22}&=h_{21}dh_{22}-h_{22}dh_{21}.\end{split}

Let us rewrite the expression in classical coordinates. For
\displaystyle\tau=(z,y)\in\mathbb{H}^{3}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, write
\displaystyle g_{\tau}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}y^{1/2}&zy^{-1/2}\\
0&y^{-1/2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. The map
\displaystyle\tau\mapsto g_{\tau}K
identifies
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{3}
with
\displaystyle X=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/\mathrm{SU}(2)
. In these coordinates we have

h_{\tau}={{}^{t}}\overline{g_{\tau}}^{-1}g_{\tau}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}y^{-1/2}&0\\
-\overline{z}y^{-1/2}&y^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}y^{-1/2}&-zy^{-1/2}\\
0&y^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}=y^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&-z\\
-\overline{z}&y^{2}+|z|^{2}\end{pmatrix},

and

v^{*}h_{\tau}v=|g_{\tau}^{-1}v|^{2}=y^{-1}(|v_{1}-zv_{2}|^{2}+|yv_{2}|^{2}).

Hence

(2.24)

\begin{split}dh_{\tau}&=-y^{-2}dy\begin{pmatrix}1&-z\\
-\overline{z}&y^{2}+|z|^{2}\end{pmatrix}+y^{-1}d\begin{pmatrix}1&-z\\
-\overline{z}&y^{2}+|z|^{2}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=-h_{\tau}y^{-1}dy+y^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&-dz\\
-d\overline{z}&2ydy+zd\overline{z}+\overline{z}dz\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

and we compute

(2.25)

\begin{split}\omega_{11}&=y^{-2}dz,\\
\omega_{12}&=-2(y^{-1}dy+y^{-2}\overline{z}dz),\\
\omega_{22}&=2\overline{z}y^{-1}dy-d\overline{z}+\overline{z}^{2}y^{-2}dz.\end{split}

Writing
\displaystyle\psi^{0,0}(v)=\psi(v)_{y}dy+\psi(v)_{z}dz+\psi(v)_{\overline{z}}d\overline{z}
we obtain

(2.26)

\begin{split}\psi(v)_{\overline{z}}&=2e^{-v^{*}h_{\tau}v}\cdot\overline{v_{2}}^{2}\\
\psi(v)_{y}&=-2e^{-v^{*}h_{\tau}v}(-2y^{-1}\overline{v_{1}v_{2}}+2\overline{z}y^{-1}\overline{v_{2}}^{2})\\
&=4y^{-1}e^{-v^{*}h_{\tau}v}\overline{(v_{1}-zv_{2})v_{2}}\\
\psi(v)_{z}&=-2y^{-2}e^{-v^{*}h_{\tau}v}(\overline{v_{1}}^{2}-2\overline{z}\overline{v_{1}v_{2}}+\overline{z}^{2}\overline{v_{2}}^{2})\\
&=-2y^{-2}e^{-v^{*}h_{\tau}v}\overline{(v_{1}-zv_{2})}^{2}.\end{split}

The Mellin transform
\displaystyle\eta^{0,0}(v,s)=\eta(v,s)_{y}dy+\eta(v,s)_{z}dz+\eta(v,s)_{\overline{z}}d\overline{z}
(defined in (
2.16
) below) is then given by:

(2.27)

\begin{split}\eta(v,s)_{\overline{z}}&=\Gamma(s/2+2)y^{s/2}\frac{(\overline{yv_{2}})^{2}}{(|v_{1}-zv_{2}|^{2}+|yv_{2}|^{2})^{s/2+2}}\\
\eta(v,s)_{y}&=2\Gamma(s/2+2)y^{s/2}\frac{\overline{(v_{1}-zv_{2})yv_{2}}}{(|v_{1}-zv_{2}|^{2}+|yv_{2}|^{2})^{s/2+2}}\\
\eta(v,s)_{z}&=-\Gamma(s/2+2)y^{s/2}\frac{\overline{(v_{1}-zv_{2})}^{2}}{(|v_{1}-zv_{2}|^{2}+|yv_{2}|^{2})^{s/2+2}}\end{split}

Thus we recover the form introduced by Ito in
[
23
]
.

2.5.
Fourier transform

Recall that the Cartan decomposition
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
identifies the tangent space
\displaystyle T_{eK}X
at the point
\displaystyle eK\in X
with
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
. Given
\displaystyle Y\in\wedge^{N-1}\mathfrak{p}
and polynomials
\displaystyle P
and
\displaystyle Q
, evaluation at
\displaystyle Y
defines a Schwartz function

\psi(Y,P\otimes\overline{Q})\in\mathscr{S}(V)

given explicitly by

\psi(v,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q})=e^{-v^{*}v}\overline{p(v,Y;P,Q)},

with
\displaystyle\overline{p(v,Y;P,Q)}=\overline{Q(v)}P(v^{*})\overline{p(v,Y)}
.

We write
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the scalar product on
\displaystyle V
defined by
\displaystyle\langle v,w\rangle=2\mathrm{Re}(w^{*}v)
and given a Schwartz form
\displaystyle f\in\mathscr{S}(V)
, we define its Fourier transform
\displaystyle\mathscr{F}f\in\mathscr{S}(V)
by

\mathscr{F}f(v)=\int_{V}f(w)e^{2\pi i\langle v,w\rangle}dw,

where
\displaystyle dw
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Since the polynomial
\displaystyle\overline{p(v,Y;P,Q)}
is anti-holomorphic, it is also harmonic and hence we have

(2.28)

\mathscr{F}\psi(Y,P\otimes\overline{Q})=C\psi(Y,P\otimes\overline{Q})

for some constant
\displaystyle C
satisfying
\displaystyle C^{4}=1
. In particular,
\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\psi(0,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q})=\psi(0,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q})=0
. Similar statements hold for
\displaystyle\phi(Y,P\otimes\overline{Q})
for any
\displaystyle Y\in\wedge^{N}\mathfrak{p}
.

2.6.
Integral on a maximal torus

Let
\displaystyle T\subset G
be the torus of diagonal matrices. The inclusion of
\displaystyle T
in
\displaystyle G
induces an embedding

T/T\cap K\rightarrow X

identifying
\displaystyle T/T\cap K
with the submanifold of
\displaystyle X
consisting of diagonal hermitian matrices. This submanifold is diffeomorphic to
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N-1}
: writing

(2.29)

C=\left\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{N})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}\ |\ t_{1}\cdots t_{N}=1\right\},

the map
\displaystyle(t_{1},\ldots,t_{N})\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{N}^{-1})T\cap K
identifies
\displaystyle C\simeq T/T\cap K
. We use this identification to orient
\displaystyle T/T\cap K
as follows: forgetting the coordinate
\displaystyle t_{N}
gives a diffeomorphism
\displaystyle C\simeq\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N-1}
. We orient
\displaystyle C
, and hence
\displaystyle T/T\cap K
, by pulling back the standard orientation of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N-1}
(given by the volume form
\displaystyle\tfrac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\tfrac{dt_{N-1}}{t_{N-1}}
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\displaystyle v\neq 0
. If
\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(s)+2N+2p>0
, then the form
\displaystyle\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)
is integrable on
\displaystyle T/T\cap K
. For
\displaystyle P=e^{I}
and
\displaystyle Q=z^{J}
monomial with
\displaystyle I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})
and
\displaystyle J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{N})
multi-indices, we have

\int_{T/T\cap K}\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2N}+1+i_{k}\right)\frac{\overline{v_{k}}^{j_{k}}}{|v_{k}|^{s/N}v_{k}^{i_{k}+1}}.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)=\overline{Q(v)}\eta(v,P\otimes 1,s)
, we may assume that
\displaystyle Q=1
. In the above coordinates for
\displaystyle C
we have
\displaystyle h=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1}^{2},\ldots,t_{N}^{2})
and
\displaystyle dh=2\mathrm{diag}(t_{1}dt_{1},\ldots,t_{N}dt_{N})
, and so the restriction of
\displaystyle P(v^{*}h)\overline{p(v)}
to
\displaystyle T/T\cap K
is given by

\prod_{j=1}^{N}(t_{j}^{2}\overline{v_{j}})^{i_{j}}\cdot 2(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\sum_{j}(-1)^{j-1}\overline{v_{j}}t_{j}^{2}(\overline{v_{N}}2t_{N}dt_{N})\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{(\overline{v_{j}}2t_{j}dt_{j})}\wedge\cdots\wedge(\overline{v_{1}}2t_{1}dt_{1})\\
=2^{N}(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}t_{j}^{2i_{j}}\overline{v_{j}}^{i_{j}+1}\sum_{j}(-1)^{j-1}\frac{dt_{N}}{t_{N}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\frac{dt_{j}}{t_{j}}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\qquad(\text{since }t_{1}\cdots t_{N}=1).

For
\displaystyle t\in C
and
\displaystyle u>0
, set
\displaystyle u_{i}=t_{i}u
. This gives
\displaystyle u_{1}\cdots u_{N}=u^{N}
and
\displaystyle\tfrac{du_{i}}{u_{i}}=\tfrac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}+\tfrac{du}{u}
, and hence

\left(\sum_{j}(-1)^{j-1}\frac{dt_{N}}{t_{N}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{\frac{dt_{j}}{t_{j}}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\right)\wedge\frac{du}{u}=\frac{du_{N}}{u_{N}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{du_{1}}{u_{1}}.

The map
\displaystyle((t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}),u)\mapsto(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})
induces a diffeomorphism
\displaystyle C\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\simeq\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}
. Using this as change of variables, we compute

\begin{split}\int_{T/T\cap K}\eta(v,P\otimes 1,s)&=\int_{C}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(uv,P\otimes 1)u^{s+N+p}\frac{du}{u}\\
&=2^{N}(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}}e^{-\sum u_{j}^{2}|v_{j}|^{2}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}t_{j}^{2i_{j}}u^{i_{j}+1}\overline{v_{j}}^{i_{j}+1}\right)u^{s+N+p}\frac{du_{N}}{u_{N}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{du_{1}}{u_{1}}\\
&=2^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\overline{v_{j}}^{i_{j}+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u_{j}^{2}|v_{j}|^{2}}u_{j}^{(s+2N)/N+2i_{j}}\frac{du_{j}}{u_{j}}\\
&=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2N}+1+i_{j}\right)\frac{\overline{v_{j}}^{i_{j}+1}}{|v_{j}|^{s/N+2+2i_{j}}}.\end{split}

∎

The above lemma shows that the integral of
\displaystyle\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)
on
\displaystyle T/T\cap K
has meromorphic continuation to
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{C}
that is regular at
\displaystyle s=0
. Its value at
\displaystyle s=0
for
\displaystyle P=e^{I}
and
\displaystyle Q=z^{J}
is

(2.30)

\left.\int_{T/T\cap K}\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)\right|_{s=0}=\prod_{k=1}^{N}i_{k}!\frac{\overline{v_{k}}^{j_{k}}}{v_{k}^{i_{k}+1}}.

It follows easily that for arbitrary
\displaystyle P
we can write

\int_{T/T\cap K}\eta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)=C(s)\overline{Q(v)}P(-\partial_{z_{1}},\ldots,-\partial_{z_{N}})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\overline{v_{j}}}{|v_{j}|^{s/N+2}}\right),

for some meromorphic function
\displaystyle C(s)
such that
\displaystyle C(0)=1
.

3.
Eisenstein cocycle

Let
\displaystyle k
be an imaginary quadratic field with ring of integers
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
. We fix an integer
\displaystyle N\geq 2
and let
\displaystyle V_{k}=k^{N}
and
\displaystyle G_{k}=\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
(recall that
\displaystyle V=\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\displaystyle G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
). We also fix an embedding
\displaystyle\sigma:k\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, which makes
\displaystyle V
a
\displaystyle k
-module and induces inclusions
\displaystyle V_{k}\subset V
and
\displaystyle G_{k}\subset G
.

Given a non-zero ideal
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}
of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
, define

(3.1)

\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})=\mathfrak{I}^{-1}\oplus\mathscr{O}^{N-1}.

It is an
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
-submodule of
\displaystyle k^{N}
that we regard as a lattice in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
via the embedding
\displaystyle k^{N}\to\mathbb{C}^{N}
induced by
\displaystyle\sigma
. We write
\displaystyle\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
for the intersection of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathscr{O}}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
with
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
; more explicitly

(3.2)

\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&{{}^{t}}b\\
c&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)\right|a\in\mathscr{O},D\in M_{N-1}(\mathscr{O}),b\in(\mathfrak{I}^{-1})^{N-1},c\in\mathfrak{I}^{N-1}\right\}.

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathscr{O}
be a prime ideal coprime to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}
. We define a congruence subgroup
\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
by

(3.3)

\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&{{}^{t}}b\\
c&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))\ \right|\ c\in(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{I})^{N-1}\right\};

thus
\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI}))\cap\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
. When
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}=\mathscr{O}
we have
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathscr{O})=\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathscr{O})
and
\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathscr{O}))=\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
is the standard level
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
subgroup of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathscr{O})
.

In this section we prove
Theorem 1.1
. We first define a more general cocycle

(3.4)

\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})):\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))^{N}\to\mathscr{F}\otimes V^{p,q},

where
\displaystyle V^{p,q}
is given in (
2.1
) and
\displaystyle\mathscr{F}
is a certain space of functions defined on complements of unions of affine hyperplanes in
\displaystyle V
, endowed with a natural action of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
. In the last section we will show that its cohomology class is non-trivial by computing its value explicitly on the units of degree
\displaystyle N
field extensions of
\displaystyle k
.

3.1.
Definition of the cocycle

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}\subset k
be a fractional ideal. Then
\displaystyle\sigma(\mathfrak{I})\subset\mathbb{C}
is a lattice.
Given a pair of non-negative integers
\displaystyle p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}
, define the Kronecker–Eisenstein series

(3.5)

K^{p,q}(z,\mathfrak{I},s)=p!\sum_{a\in\sigma(\mathfrak{I})}\frac{\overline{z+a}^{q}}{(z+a)^{p+1}|z+a|^{s}},\qquad z\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{I}).

The sum converges absolutely for
\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(s)>1+q-p
and for
\displaystyle z
in a compact subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
. The series
\displaystyle K^{p,q}(z,\mathfrak{a},s)
has an analytic continuation to the whole
\displaystyle s
-plane that is regular at
\displaystyle s=0
, see e.g.
[
37
,
11
,
12
]
.

More generally, for an
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
-lattice
\displaystyle\Lambda\subset k^{N}
,
let
\displaystyle U(\Lambda)
be the open subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
obtained by removing all translates of coordinate hyperplanes by
\displaystyle\lambda\in\sigma(\Lambda)
. For
\displaystyle I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})
and
\displaystyle J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{N})
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
and
\displaystyle z\in U(\Lambda)
, define

(3.6)

K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Lambda)}\prod_{1\leq k\leq N}i_{k}!\frac{\overline{z_{k}+\lambda_{k}}^{j_{k}}}{(z_{k}+\lambda_{k})^{i_{k}+1}|z_{k}+\lambda_{k}|^{s}}.

The function
\displaystyle K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,s)
can be expressed as a homogeneous degree
\displaystyle N
polynomial of Kronecker–Eisenstein series: pick non-zero fractional ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{I}_{N}
of
\displaystyle k
such that
\displaystyle\Lambda\supseteq\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N}
. Then

(3.7)

K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda/\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N}}K^{I,J}(z+\sigma(\lambda),\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N},s),

and

(3.8)

K^{I,J}(z,\mathfrak{I}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{I}_{N},s)=\prod_{1\leq k\leq N}K^{i_{k},j_{k}}(z_{k},\mathfrak{I}_{k},s).

Thus
\displaystyle K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,s)
converges absolutely for
\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(s)>1+\max\{j_{k}-i_{k}\}
and has analytic continuation to all
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{C}
that is regular at
\displaystyle s=0
. We set

(3.9)

K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda)=K^{I,J}(z,\Lambda,0)

and define

\mathscr{F}:=\mathrm{span}\langle K^{I,J}(\gamma^{-1}z,\Lambda)\ |\ \gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k),\Lambda\text{ an }\mathscr{O}\text{-lattice in }k^{N}\rangle,

which carries a natural action of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle A
be a matrix in
\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))\cap\mathrm{GL}_{N}(k)
. Then
\displaystyle A^{-1}\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})\supseteq\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})
and we define the generalized Dedekind sum

\begin{split}D^{I,J}(z,A,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))&=\det A^{-1}K^{I,J}(A^{-1}z,A^{-1}\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))\\
&=\det A^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})/A\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})}K^{I,J}(A^{-1}(z+\sigma(\lambda)),\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})).\end{split}

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
be a proper ideal of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
coprime to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}
be its norm. Define

D^{I,J}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,A,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=D^{I,J}(z,A,\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI}))-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\cdot D^{I,J}(z,A,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})).

If
\displaystyle A\in\mathrm{End}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
but
\displaystyle A
is not invertible, set

D^{I,J}(z,A,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=D^{I,J}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,A,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=0.

For
\displaystyle p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, recall the
\displaystyle G
-representation
\displaystyle V^{p,q}
introduced in (
2.1
). A basis of
\displaystyle V^{p,q}
is given by the vectors

(3.10)

e^{I,J}:=(({{}^{t}}e_{1})^{i_{1}}\cdots({{}^{t}}e_{N})^{i_{N}})\otimes(\overline{e_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{e_{N}}^{j_{N}}),

where
\displaystyle I,J\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
satisfy
\displaystyle i_{1}+\ldots+i_{N}=p
and
\displaystyle j_{1}+\ldots+j_{N}=q
.

Recall that given a group
\displaystyle\Gamma
and a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
-module
\displaystyle M
, a map
\displaystyle\alpha:\Gamma^{N}\to M
is said to be a homogeneous
\displaystyle(N-1)
-cocycle if it is equivariant, that is,

(3.11)

\alpha(\gamma\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma\gamma_{N})=\gamma\alpha(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}),\qquad\gamma,\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}\in\Gamma,

and satisfies

(3.12)

\sum_{1\leq i\leq N+1}(-1)^{i-1}\alpha(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{i-1},\gamma_{i+1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1})=0,\qquad\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1}\in\Gamma.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I},\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathscr{O}
be non-zero coprime ideals of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
and assume that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathscr{O}
. Given
\displaystyle\underline{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N})\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))^{N}
, let

A(\underline{\gamma})=(\gamma_{1}e_{1}|\cdots|\gamma_{N}e_{1})\in M_{N}(\mathscr{O})

be the matrix formed by the first columns of
\displaystyle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}
. Then
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})\in\mathrm{End}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
. For fixed
\displaystyle p,q
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define a map

\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})):\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))^{N}\to\mathscr{F}\otimes V^{p,q}

by

\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\underline{\gamma},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|=p\\
|J|=q\end{subarray}}D^{I,J}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,A(\underline{\gamma}),\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))\otimes A(\underline{\gamma})e^{I,J}.

Then
\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
is a homogeneous
\displaystyle(N-1)
-cocycle.

Note that the first row of the matrix
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})
in the statement has entries in
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
whereas all its other rows have entries in
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}
. The statement that
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})\in\mathrm{End}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
follows. Note also that

(3.13)

A(\gamma\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma\gamma_{N})=\gamma A(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}),\qquad\gamma,\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})).

The equivariance property (
3.11
) of
\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
follows from this. Thus it remains to show the cocycle property (
3.12
). To prove it we will next define — as an Eisenstein series — a closed
\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
-invariant differential form

(3.14)

E_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\psi^{p,q},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))\in A^{N-1}(X)\otimes V^{p,q}

and
\displaystyle(N-1)
-dimensional submanifolds

(3.15)

\Delta(\underline{\gamma})\subset X,\qquad\underline{\gamma}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))^{N},

such that

(3.16)

\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\underline{\gamma},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=\int_{\Delta(\underline{\gamma})}E_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\psi^{p,q},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})).

The cocycle property will follow from the fact that for
\displaystyle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
we can find a simplex

(3.17)

\Delta(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1})\subset X

with boundary

(3.18)

\partial\Delta(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1})=\sum_{1\leq i\leq N+1}(-1)^{i-1}\Delta(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{i-1},\gamma_{i+1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1})

and such that
\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{p}}(z,\psi^{p,q},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
decreases rapidly on
\displaystyle\Delta(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N+1})
for fixed
\displaystyle z
.

3.2.
Eisenstein series

For
\displaystyle v\in V
, an
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
-lattice
\displaystyle\Lambda\subset V_{k}
and a holomorphic polynomial
\displaystyle P
(resp.
\displaystyle Q
) on
\displaystyle V^{\vee}
(resp. on
\displaystyle V
), consider the theta series

(3.19)

\theta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\psi(v+\lambda,P\otimes\overline{Q}).

The series converges rapidly as
\displaystyle\psi(v,P\otimes\overline{Q})
is rapidly decreasing. By (
2.15
), we obtain a differential form
\displaystyle\theta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda)\in A^{N-1}(X)
satisfying

(3.20)

\gamma^{*}\theta(\gamma v,\gamma P\otimes\overline{\gamma Q};\psi,\Lambda)=\theta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda),\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma(\Lambda):=\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathscr{O}}(\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k).

The Mellin transform of
\displaystyle\theta(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda)
is the Eisenstein series

(3.21)

\begin{split}E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda,s)&:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\theta(tv,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,t\Lambda)t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}\\
&=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\eta(v+\lambda,P\otimes\overline{Q},s),\end{split}

where
\displaystyle\eta(v,s)
is given by (
2.18
). Here the sum converges when
\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(s)\gg 0
but can be analytically continued to the whole
\displaystyle s
-plane in a standard way using Poisson summation. To do this, consider the scalar product
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
given by

(3.22)

\langle v,w\rangle=2\mathrm{Re}(v\cdot w^{*})

and define

(3.23)

\begin{split}\Lambda^{\vee}&=\left\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\ |\ \langle v,w\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\forall v\in\Lambda\right\}.\end{split}

Given
\displaystyle g\in G
and a tangent vector
\displaystyle Y\in\mathfrak{p}=T_{eK}X
we can define a vector
\displaystyle g_{*}Y\in T_{gK}X
. The invariance property (
2.15
) can be rewritten as

\psi(gv,g_{*}Y;gP\otimes\overline{gQ})=\psi(v,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q}).

By (
2.28
),
\displaystyle\psi(\cdot,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q})\in\mathscr{S}(V)
is an eigenvector for the Fourier transform and so Poisson sumation gives

(3.24)

\begin{split}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\psi(t(v+\lambda),g_{*}Y;P\otimes\overline{Q})&=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\psi(tg^{-1}(v+\lambda),Y;g^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q}))\\
&=C\ \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\Lambda)^{-1}t^{-2N}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}}e^{2\pi i\langle v,\lambda\rangle}\psi(t^{-1}g^{*}\lambda,Y;g^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q})).\end{split}

Using this we can write

(3.25)

E(v,g_{*}Y;P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda,s)\\
\begin{split}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta(tv,g_{*}Y;P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,t\Lambda)t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}\\
&=\int_{1}^{\infty}\theta(tv,g_{*}Y;P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,t\Lambda)t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}+C\ \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\Lambda)^{-1}\end{split}\\
\cdot\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}}e^{2\pi i\langle v,\lambda\rangle}\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(tg^{*}\lambda,Y;g^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q}))t^{-s+N+q-p}\frac{dt}{t}.

The last expression converges for all
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{C}
and gives the desired analytic continuation (with no poles since
\displaystyle\psi(0)=\mathscr{F}\psi(0)=0
) of
\displaystyle E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda,s)
. We set

(3.26)

E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda)=E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda,0)\in A^{N-1}(X).

Proposition 3.3
.

For fixed
\displaystyle v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and polynomials
\displaystyle P
and
\displaystyle Q
, the form
\displaystyle E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda)
is closed.

Proof.

For
\displaystyle t>0
define the theta series

\theta(tv,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi,t\Lambda)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\phi(t(v+\lambda),P\otimes\overline{Q}),

where
\displaystyle\phi
is given in (
2.19
). The same argument used above shows that

E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi,\Lambda,s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta(tv,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi,t\Lambda)t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)\gg 0,

admits analytic continuation to
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{C}
(with no poles). The relation

(3.27)

dE(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda,s)=-\frac{s}{2N}E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi,\Lambda,s),

which follows from (
2.20
), proves the claim.
∎

Thus we may regard
\displaystyle E(\cdot,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda)
as a closed differential
\displaystyle(N-1)
-form on
\displaystyle X
valued on the space of smooth functions
\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(V)
, and by (
2.17
) we have the equivariance property

(3.28)

\gamma^{*}E(\gamma v,\gamma P\otimes\overline{\gamma Q};\psi,\Lambda)=E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda),\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma(\Lambda).

For
\displaystyle\Lambda=\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})
defined in (
3.1
), we set

(3.29)

E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}),s)=E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI}),s)-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\cdot E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}),s)

and

(3.30)

E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}),0).

Again we regard
\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{p}}(\cdot,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
as a closed differential
\displaystyle(N-1)
-form on
\displaystyle X
valued in
\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(V)
, equivariant under
\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))(=\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI}))\cap\Gamma(\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})))
.

3.3.
Behaviour on Siegel sets

Fix two coprime ideals
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}
of
\displaystyle\mathscr{O}
with
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
of prime norm. Recall that proper rational parabolics of
\displaystyle G_{k}=\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
are in bijection with proper flags

(3.31)

W_{\bullet}:0\subsetneq W_{0}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq W_{r}\subsetneq k^{N},\quad r\geq 0.

Before stating our next result we recall the definition of Siegel sets. For a strictly increasing sequence
\displaystyle\mathbf{J}=\{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{r}\}
of integers in
\displaystyle\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, let
\displaystyle W_{j_{k}}=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{j_{k}}\rangle
and
\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{J}}
be the standard parabolic of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
stabilizing the flag

W_{\mathbf{J}}:0\subsetneq W_{j_{1}}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq W_{j_{r}}\subsetneq V.

We can write
\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{J}}=NMA
, where (setting
\displaystyle j_{0}=0
and
\displaystyle j_{r+1}=N
)

(3.32)

\begin{split}N&=N_{\mathbf{J}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1_{j_{1}}&*&\cdots&*\\
0&1_{j_{2}-j_{1}}&\cdots&*\\
0&0&\ddots&*\\
0&0&0&1_{j_{r+1}-j_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\right\}\\
M&=M_{\mathbf{J}}=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&A_{2}&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\ddots&0\\
0&0&0&A_{r+1}\end{pmatrix}\ \right|\ A_{k}\in\mathrm{GL}_{j_{k}-j_{k-1}}(\mathbb{C}),\ |\det(A_{k})|=1\right\}\\
A&=A_{\mathbf{J}}=\left\{\left.a(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r+1}):=\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}1_{j_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&t_{2}1_{j_{2}-j_{1}}&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\ddots&0\\
0&0&0&t_{r+1}1_{j_{r+1}-j_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\ \right|\ t_{k}>0,\ \det a(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r+1})=1\right\}.\end{split}

An element
\displaystyle g\in G
can be written as

g=nmak,\quad n\in N,\ m\in M,\ a\in A,\ k\in\mathrm{SU}(N).

In this decomposition
\displaystyle n
and
\displaystyle a
are uniquely determined by
\displaystyle g
and
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle k
are determined up to an element of
\displaystyle M\cap\mathrm{SU}(N)
.

For
\displaystyle t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let

A_{t}=\{a(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r+1})\in A\ |\ t_{k}/t_{k+1}\geq t\text{ for all }k\}.

The Siegel set determined by
\displaystyle t>0
and a relatively compact set
\displaystyle\omega\subset NM
is

(3.33)

S(t,\omega):=\omega A_{t}\cdot\mathrm{SU}(N)\subset\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C});

we refer to its image in
\displaystyle X
also as a Siegel set.

More generally, suppose that
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
is a proper flag of
\displaystyle k^{N}
. A
Siegel set for the cusp defined by
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
is a set of the form

S(g,t,\omega):=g^{-1}\omega A_{t}\cdot\mathrm{SU}(N)

where
\displaystyle g\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(k)
is such that
\displaystyle gW_{\bullet}
is a standard flag (i.e. of the form
\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{J}}
for some
\displaystyle\mathbf{J}
).

We say that
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}

defines a good cusp
if
\displaystyle\gamma e_{1}\in W_{0}
for some
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
defines a good cusp. If
\displaystyle v\in k^{N}
satisfies
\displaystyle(v+\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI}))\cap W_{r}=\emptyset
, then
\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
is rapidly decreasing on every Siegel set for the cusp defined by
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
.

For the proof it will be convenient to work with adeles.
Given a finite Schwartz function
\displaystyle\phi_{f}\in\mathscr{S}(V_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{f}))
and
\displaystyle t>0
, let

(3.34)

\theta(v,t,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi_{f}\otimes\psi)=\sum_{\lambda\in k^{N}}\phi_{f}(\lambda)\psi(t(v+\lambda),P\otimes\overline{Q})

and

(3.35)

\begin{split}E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi_{f}\otimes\psi,s)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta(v,t,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi_{f}\otimes\psi)t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}\\
&=\sum_{\lambda\in k^{N}}\phi_{f}(\lambda)\eta(v+\lambda,P\otimes\overline{Q},s).\end{split}

Using Poisson summation as in
Section 3.2
, for
\displaystyle Y\in\wedge^{N-1}\mathfrak{p}
we may write

(3.36)

\theta(v,g_{*}Y,t,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi_{f}\otimes\psi)\\
=C\ \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))^{-1}t^{-2N}\sum_{\lambda\in V_{k}}\widehat{\phi_{f}}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\langle v,\lambda\rangle}\psi(t^{-1}g^{*}\lambda,Y;g^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q}))

and

(3.37)

E(v,g_{*}Y,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi_{f}\otimes\psi,s)\\
\begin{split}&=\sum_{\lambda\in V_{k}}\phi_{f}(\lambda)\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(tg^{-1}(v+\lambda),Y;g^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q}))t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}\\
&\quad+C\ \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in V_{k}}\widehat{\phi_{f}}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\langle v,\lambda\rangle}\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(tg^{*}\lambda,Y;g^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q}))t^{-s+N+q-p}\frac{dt}{t},\end{split}

showing that
\displaystyle E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi_{f}\otimes\psi,s)
admits analytic continuation to
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{C}
that is regular at
\displaystyle s=0
.
Note that we can write

(3.38)

E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\phi_{f}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{I})\otimes\psi,s)|_{s=0}

where
\displaystyle\phi_{f}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{I})\in\mathscr{S}(V_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{f}))
is given by

(3.39)

\phi_{f}(\lambda;\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{I})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&\text{ if }\lambda\notin\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}},\\
1,&\text{ if }\lambda\in\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}\text{ and }\lambda_{1}\notin\mathfrak{I}^{-1}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}\\
1-\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p}),&\text{ if }\lambda\in\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}\text{ and }\lambda_{1}\in\mathfrak{I}^{-1}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}.\end{array}\right.

Proof of
Section 3.3
.

Fix
\displaystyle Y\in\wedge^{N-1}\mathfrak{p}
, and a vector
\displaystyle v\in k^{N}
and define
\displaystyle\tilde{\phi_{f}}\in\mathscr{S}(V_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{f}))
by
\displaystyle\tilde{\phi_{f}}(\lambda)=\phi_{f}(\lambda-v;\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{I})
. For
\displaystyle g=(g_{f},g_{\infty})\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{A}_{k})
, polynomials
\displaystyle P
and
\displaystyle\overline{Q}
and
\displaystyle t>0
, define

\theta_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}(g,t)=\sum_{\lambda\in k^{N}}\tilde{\phi_{f}}(g_{f}^{-1}\lambda)\psi(tg_{\infty}^{-1}\lambda,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q})

and

\begin{split}E_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}(g,s)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}(g,t)t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}\\
&=\sum_{\lambda\in k^{N}}\tilde{\phi_{f}}(g_{f}^{-1}\lambda)\eta(tg_{\infty}^{-1}\lambda,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q},s).\end{split}

Then our Eisenstein series
\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
satisfies

E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,(g_{\infty})_{*}Y;P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}),s)=E_{g_{\infty}^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q})}((g_{f}=1,g_{\infty}),s).

Since
\displaystyle E_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}
is linear in
\displaystyle P
and
\displaystyle Q
and
\displaystyle V^{p,q}
is a polynomial representation of
\displaystyle G
, the expression
\displaystyle g_{\infty}^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q})
grows at most polynomially on any Siegel set. It follows that it suffices to show that
\displaystyle E_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}((g_{f}=1,g_{\infty}),s)
is rapidly decreasing on every Siegel set for the cusp defined by
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
for all
\displaystyle P\otimes\overline{Q}\in V^{p,q}
; since
\displaystyle E_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}(g,s)
is an automorphic form, we can check this by showing that the constant term

E_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}((g_{f}=1,g_{\infty}),s)_{\mathbf{N}}=\int_{\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{A})}E_{P\otimes\overline{Q}}(n(g_{f}=1,g_{\infty}),s)dn

vanishes, where
\displaystyle\mathbf{N}
denotes the unipotent radical of the parabolic
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
corresponding to
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
. Let us fix
\displaystyle P
and
\displaystyle Q
and drop
\displaystyle P\otimes\overline{Q}
from the notation and write simply
\displaystyle E(g,s)
and
\displaystyle E(g,s)_{\mathbf{N}}
. By transitivity of constant terms, we may assume that
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
is maximal, i.e. that the flag
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
consists of just one proper subspace
\displaystyle W_{0}
of
\displaystyle k^{N}
. Note that under our assumptions on
\displaystyle v
we have
\displaystyle\tilde{\phi_{f}}(\lambda)=0
for
\displaystyle\lambda\in W_{0}
. For
\displaystyle\lambda\in k^{N}-W_{0}
, the orbit of
\displaystyle\lambda
under
\displaystyle\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{Q})
is
\displaystyle\lambda+W_{0}
. Writing
\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})
for the stabilizer of
\displaystyle\lambda
in
\displaystyle\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{Q})
, we have

\begin{split}E&((g_{f}=1,g_{\infty}),s)_{\mathbf{N}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{A})}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in k^{N}-W_{0}}\tilde{\phi_{f}}(n_{f}^{-1}\lambda)\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tg_{\infty}^{-1}n_{\infty}^{-1}\lambda,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q})t^{s+N+p-q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)dn\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in k^{N}/W_{0}\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{A})}\left(\sum_{n^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{Q})}\tilde{\phi_{f}}((n^{\prime}n_{f})^{-1}\lambda)\eta(tg_{\infty}^{-1}(n^{\prime}n_{\infty})^{-1}\lambda,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q},s)\right)dn\\
&=\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{A}_{f}))\\
&\quad\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in k^{N}/W_{0}\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\backslash\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{A}_{f})}\tilde{\phi_{f}}(n_{f}^{-1}\lambda)dn_{f}\int_{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{R})}\eta(tg_{\infty}^{-1}n_{\infty}^{-1}\lambda,Y;P\otimes\overline{Q},s)dn_{\infty}.\end{split}

Let
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
such that
\displaystyle l:=\langle\gamma e_{1}\rangle\subseteq W_{0}
. Since the Schwartz function
\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{I})
, the
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}
-component of
\displaystyle\phi_{f}
, satisfies

(3.40)

\int_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}(w+xe_{1};\mathfrak{I})dx=0,\qquad w\in V_{k}(k_{\mathfrak{p}}),

using that
\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{I})

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\backslash\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{A}_{f})}\tilde{\phi_{f}}(n_{f}^{-1}\lambda)dn_{\mathfrak{p}}&=\int_{W_{0}(\mathbb{A}_{f})}\tilde{\phi_{f}}(\lambda+w)dw\\

showing that indeed the constant term is zero.
∎

3.4.
Tits compactification and modular symbols

First recall that the Tits building
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{G})
is a simplicial set whose non-degenerate simplices are in bijection with (proper) rational parabolic subgroups
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}
, or equivalently with proper
\displaystyle k
-rational flags

W_{\bullet}:0\subsetneq W_{0}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq W_{r}\subsetneq k^{N},\quad r\geq 0.

The stabilizer
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(W_{\bullet})
of this flag is a rational parabolic of
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}
that defines an
\displaystyle r
-simplex in
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{G})
. Its
\displaystyle i
-th face is the simplex corresponding to the flag obtained from
\displaystyle W_{\bullet}
by deleting
\displaystyle W_{i}
(degenerate simplices correspond to proper flags where we allow
\displaystyle W_{i}=W_{i+1}
for any
\displaystyle i
).

For a parabolic subgroup
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
, denote by
\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\mathbf{P}}
its unipotent radical and write
\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{P}}=\mathbf{P}/\mathbf{N}_{\mathbf{P}}
for its Levi quotient,
\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{P}}
for the maximal
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-split torus in the center of
\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{P}}
and
\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{P}}=\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbb{R})^{0}
for the identity component of the real points of
\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{P}}
. Writing
\displaystyle X(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{P}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
for the group of rational characters of
\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{P}}
, we define
\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{P}}=\cap_{\alpha\in X(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{P}})_{\mathbb{Q}}}\ker\alpha^{2}
. Then we have the direct product decomposition

\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbb{R})A_{\mathbf{P}}.

The simplex in
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{G})
corresponding to
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
admits a natural geometric realization. To define it, let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}
and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{\mathbf{P}}
be the Lie algebras of
\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{P}}
and
\displaystyle N_{\mathbf{P}}
respectively, and let
\displaystyle\Phi^{+}(P,A_{\mathbf{P}})
be the set of roots for the adjoint action of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}
on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{\mathbf{P}}
.
These roots define a positive chamber

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}=\left\{H\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}\ |\ \alpha(H)>0,\quad\alpha\in\Phi^{+}(P,A_{\mathbf{P}})\right\}.

Writing
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the Killing form on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
, we define an open simplex

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}(\infty)=\left\{H\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}\ |\ \langle H,H\rangle=1\right\}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}

and a closed simplex

\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}}(\infty)=\left\{H\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}\ |\ \alpha(H)\geq 0,\ \langle H,H\rangle=1,\quad\alpha\in\Phi^{+}(P,A_{\mathbf{P}})\right\}

in
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}
. Note that for
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
maximal the Lie algebra
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}
is one-dimensional and so
\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}}(\infty)
is just a point. Moreover, if
\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}
is another rational parabolic, then
\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{+}}(\infty)
is a face of
\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}}(\infty)
if and only if
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\subseteq\mathbf{Q}
. It follows that
\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}}(\infty)
gives a geometric realization of the simplex in
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{G})
corresponding to
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
, and so the Tits building
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{G})
admits the geometric realization

(3.41)

|\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{G})|\sim\coprod_{\mathbf{P}}\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}}(\infty)/\sim,

where the union runs over all proper rational parabolics
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}
and
\displaystyle\sim
is the equivalence relation induced by the identification of
\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{+}}(\infty)
with a face of
\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}}(\infty)
whenever
\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\subseteq\mathbf{Q}
. As a set we may write

|\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{G})|=\coprod_{\mathbf{P}}\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}(\infty)

as a disjoint union of open simplexes
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{P}}^{+}(\infty)
.

Tits compactification

3.5.
Evaluation on modular symbols and the cocycle property

We can now relate the Eisenstein series
\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
and the Eisenstein cocycle.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that
\displaystyle v
does not lie in any
\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathfrak{pI})
-translate of a proper subspace of
\displaystyle V
of the form
\displaystyle\langle\gamma_{i}e_{1}\ |\ i\in I\rangle
for
\displaystyle I\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
. Then
\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\cdot,\underline{\gamma},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))
is defined at
\displaystyle v

\mathbf{\Phi}^{p,q}_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,\underline{\gamma},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))(P\otimes\overline{Q})=\int_{\Delta^{\circ}(\underline{\gamma})}E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi^{p,q},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})).

Proof.

Consider the matrix
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})=(\gamma_{0}e_{1}|\ldots|\gamma_{N-1}e_{1})
. If
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})
is not invertible, then both sides are zero by definition. Now assume that
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})
is invertible and take
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}P
and
\displaystyle\overline{A(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}Q}
to be monomial, say
\displaystyle A(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}P(z)=z^{I}=z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots z_{N}^{i_{N}}
and
\displaystyle\overline{A(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}Q}(\overline{z})=\overline{z}^{J}=\overline{z_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{z_{N}}^{j_{N}}
; it suffices to show that with this choice of
\displaystyle P
and
\displaystyle\overline{Q}
we have

\int_{\Delta^{\circ}(\underline{\gamma})}E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi^{p,q},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}))=D^{I,J}_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,A(\underline{\gamma}),\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})).

Note that the proof of Proposition
3.3
shows that, for any
\displaystyle s
, the Eisenstein series
\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}),s)
is rapidly decreasing on any Siegel set corresponding to a good cusp; since
\displaystyle\Delta^{\circ}(\underline{\gamma})
admits a finite cover by such Siegel sets, it follows that
\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{p}}(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi,\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}),s)
is integrable over
\displaystyle\Delta^{\circ}(\underline{\gamma})
. For
\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(s)\gg 0
, we compute

\int_{\Delta^{\circ}(\underline{\gamma})}E(v,P\otimes\overline{Q};\psi^{p,q},\Lambda(\mathfrak{I}),s)\\
\begin{split}&=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})}\int_{\Delta^{\circ}(\underline{\gamma})}\eta^{p,q}(v+\lambda,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)\\
&=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})}\int_{T/T\cap K}a(\underline{\gamma})^{*}\eta^{p,q}(v+\lambda,P\otimes\overline{Q},s)\\
&=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})}\int_{T/T\cap K}\eta^{p,q}(a(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}(v+\lambda),a(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q}),s)\\
&=|\det A(\underline{\gamma})|^{-s/N}\det A(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mathfrak{I})}\int_{T/T\cap K}\eta^{p,q}(A(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}(v+\lambda),A(\underline{\gamma})^{-1}(P\otimes\overline{Q}),s),\end{split}

where the last equality follows from the homogeneity property (
2.21
). The desired identity follows by analytic continuation from
Section 2.6
.

4.4.
Smoothing and evaluation

We can use the above results to express values of partial zeta functions as polynomials in Kronecker–Eisenstein series, by using the fact that the Eisenstein series

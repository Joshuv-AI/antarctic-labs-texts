Abstract
. This paper is the first of a pair that aims to classify a large number of the type I ​ I II quantum subgroups of the categories 𝒞 ⁡ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 r + 1 , k ) \mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k) . We find that the symmetries are all non-exceptional except for four cases (up to level-rank duality). Introduction

One of the oldest open problems in quantum algebra has been the program to classify the
quantum subgroups
(or module categories, or Morita equivalence classes of algebra objects) of the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g},k)
. This program was initially investigated in the language of
conformal field theory
by Cappelli, Itzykson, and Zuber
[
6
]
. They used physical reasoning to argue that a quantum subgroup of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g},k)
is precisely the data needed to extend a
chiral Wess-Zumino-Witten
conformal field theory (constructed from
\mathfrak{g}
and
k
) up to a
full conformal field theory
. With this motivation in hand they were then able to give a combinatorial classification of the quantum subgroups of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
. Their results were unexpected and exciting, falling into an
A-D-E
pattern. The two infinite families
A
and
D
were expected, but far more intriguing were the three exceptional examples
E_{6}
,
E_{7}
, and
E_{8}
.

Inspired by the richness of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
classification, there was a flurry of activity to give classification results for the higher rank Lie algebras
[
50
,
8
,
9
]
. However this proved far more difficult than the rank one case. Despite the intense research activity directed towards the problem, very few new classification results were achieved. Once the dust had settled, a combinatorial classification for
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
had been given by Gannon
[
28
]
, and
\mathfrak{sl}_{4}
had been claimed by Ocneanu
[
51
]
, but without supplied proof. It was here that the project stagnated, with many considering it to be intractable.

In a more general setting, the problem of extending chiral conformal field theory up to full conformal field theory was studied rigorously by Fuchs, Runkel, and Schweigert
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,

It was proven that the data to extend a chiral conformal field theory is precisely a module category over the representation category of the chiral theory. However, a module category is more than just its combinatorics, which is what was classified in
[
6
]
and
[
28
]
. There is also the categorical data of the module category, which is captured by the 6-j symbols, or equivalently the associator, of the module. Thus classification for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and

There is a fundamental bifurification in classification program of quantum subgroups for any modular tensor category. This split occurs between the
type
I
quantum subgroups
, and the
type
II
quantum subgroups
. These sub-classes of quantum subgroups are most easily defined using the Morita equivalence classes of algebra objects formalism. A quantum subgroup is called type
I
if the Morita equivalence class of algebra objects contains a commutative representative, and it is called type
II
if there is no such commutative representative. The differences between these two cases means that different classification techniques are needed for each case. There is also the distinction between
non-exceptional
quantum subgroups, and
exceptional
quantum subgroups. We say a quantum subgroup of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
is non-exceptional if it can be obtained as the category of modules of an algebra of the form
\operatorname{Fun}(G)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
, where
G
is a finite group (necessarily a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}
). A quantum subgroup is then exceptional if it is not non-exceptional.

Recently there has been a massive revitalisation in the program to classifying quantum subgroups of the higher rank Lie algebras. This began with work of Schopieray
[
56
]
, which gave level bounds on which categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g},k)
could have exceptional type
I
quantum subgroups for the rank two Lie algebras. These techniques were then drastically improved upon by Gannon
[
27
]
, where effective level bounds were determined for all Lie algebras. In short, this allowed for a computer search to find all type
I
quantum subgroups for any Lie algebra. These computer searches were performed by Gannon, and type
I
classification was given for all ranks less than 7, a dramatic improvement on the state of knowledge. For these examples it was found that there are the expected infinite families of de-equivariantisation (or orbifold) type
I
quantum subgroups, a finite number of type
I
quantum subgroups coming from conformal inclusions of Lie groups
[
60
]
, and four new examples not related to conformal inclusions of Lie groups. We will refer to these latter four quantum subgroups as the
truly exceptional
quantum subgroups.

Thus the type
I
case has essentially been solved, and classification up to higher ranks is now a matter of computer power, rather than mathematical insight. However, the type
II
case (which comprise all remaining examples) still remains entirely open. This paper is the first in a pair to classify the type
II
quantum subgroups for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
. The techniques developed in these papers will generalise to the other classical algebras. However we restrict our attention now to the type
A
case for three reasons. First is that the details of working through the generalisation will require substantial effort that would push the length of these papers beyond a readable limit. Second is that combinatorial evidence suggests that type
A
has the richest behaviour with type
II
quantum subgroups, so we can expect to find the most interesting results by studying this case. Finally, historically the type
A

A

With the motivation and main theorem of this paper described, let us move on to describing the structure of the article.

In Section
2
we introduce the background required to begin this paper. We introduce the combinatorics of the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. In particular we give the formula for the dimensions of the simples, and prove useful inequalities which they obey. We describe the structure of the orbifold
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, and of the local modules
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. We explicitly determine useful structure of the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, including the parametrisation of the simples, the group of invertibles, and the adjoint subcategory.

In Section
3
we determine the so called
non-exceptional
braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. These are the braided auto-equivalences which fix the image of the adjoint representation under the free module functor. The end result is the expected one, i.e. we show all non-exceptional braided auto-equivalences are either charge conjugation, simple current auto-equivalence, or come from the canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action. Here a simple current auto-equivalence is a symmetry of the category constructed via the action of invertible elements, see
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
for additional details). Proving this result is highly technical, and requires several powerful techniques. The difficulty here is not surprising, as determining the non-exceptional braided auto-equivalences has troubled researchers working on this same problem in the past. To begin we develop skein theory for the adjoint subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. Our methods here are general, and will allow one to find skein theory for de-equivariantisation by an abelian group of any braided category, given that skein theory of the original category is known. With this skein theory in hand we can then use standard planar algebra techniques to find the non-exceptional braided auto-equivalence group of the adjoint subcategory. To extend these auto-equivalences to the entire category we use the techniques developed by the author in
[
16
]
. These techniques give an upper bound on the number of auto-equivalences which may extend an auto-equivalence on the adjoint subcategory. By a happy coincidence, this upper bound is precisely realised by simple current auto-equivalences, introduced in the authors work
[
15
]
, which was inspired by combinatorics from conformal field theory. This happy coincidence suggests the potential for a general theorem.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a modular tensor category,
\mathcal{C}^{\text{ad}}
its adjoint subcategory, and
\mathcal{F}
an auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}
which restricts to the identity on
\mathcal{C}^{\text{ad}}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to a simple current auto-equivalence.

The validity of this general conjecture remains to be investigated. All together, the results of this section fully classify all non-exceptional braided auto-equivalences of the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
.

In Section
4
we investigate the combinatorics of the exceptional braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. We show that with a finite number of exceptions, that every braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is non-exceptional, and hence is covered by the results of the previous section. Our main observation here is simple. If there were an exceptional braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, then its image of the adjoint would have the same dimension and twist. This puts massive combinatorial restrictions on the objects of the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. By studying these restrictions in a case by case analysis, we are able to obtain a series of inequalities which imply that both the rank and level must be small. From here we can computer search to find the finite cases where
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
has an exceptional braided auto-equivalence, at the level of the fusion ring and twists. Up to level-rank duality we find four possible candidates for exceptional braided auto-equivalences. These are
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
. While the case by case analysis is messy, and a uniform approach to this section would be desired, the exceptional examples which are discovered mean that such a uniform approach is unlikely to exist.

In Section

2.1.
Quantum Integers, Dimensions, and Inequalities

The main object of study in this paper will be the modular tensor categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
, the category of level
k
integrable representations of
\hat{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}}
. For an overview us these categories see
[
57
]
. For our purposes we will only require some basic combinatorics of these categories. The simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
are parametrised by

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i}\quad\text{ where }\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}=k.

Often we will omit the
\lambda_{0}
term of a simple object, as its value can be deduced from the remaining
\lambda_{i}
’s. For example, the vector representation
(k-1)\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{1}
will usually be written simply as
\Lambda_{1}
. A special subset of these simples are the
r+1
invertibles (or simple currents), which are the objects

\{k\Lambda_{i}:i\in\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}\}.

To describe the quantum dimensions of the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
we will need two ingredients. The first are the quantum integers.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
n
-th quantum integer (as a function of
r
and
k
) as

[n]_{r,k}:=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad q=e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{2(1+k+r)}}.

The second ingredient is the
hook formula
, which gives the quantum dimension of a simple of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
in terms of quantum integers. To describe this formula, we have to introduce the tableaux of a simple object. Let
X=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i}
be a simple object, and define a
r\times k
tableaux
T(X)
whose
j
-th row contains
\sum_{i=j}^{r}\lambda_{i}
boxes. For each box
(x,y)
in the tableaux
T(X)
we can define the content, which is the quantum integer
[r+1+-x+y]_{r,k}
, and the hook length, which is the quantum integer
[h]_{r,k}
, where
h
is the number of boxes with the same
x
or
y
coordinate. The quantum dimension of
X
is the product over all the boxes of
T(X)
of the contents divided by the hooks. For a quick example, we have that the tableaux for the object
\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
has two boxes in row one, and one box in row two. Thus the contents are

[r+1]_{r,k},\quad[r]_{r,k},\quad\text{and}\quad[r+2]_{r,k},

and the hooks are

[3]_{r,k},\quad[1]_{r,k},\quad\text{and}\quad[1]_{r,k}.

Therefore the hook formula tells us that the quantum dimension of
\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}
is
\frac{[r]_{r,k}[r+1]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}}{[3]_{r,k}}
.

There are two natural actions of the simples of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
that preserve the dimensions. These are
charge-conjugation
which sends

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{-i}.

The fact that this map preserves dimensions can be deduced from the hook formula. The other action comes from simple currents, which sends

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i+a}\text{ for }a\in\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}.

This map preserves dimensions as it is simply tensoring by the invertible
k\Lambda_{a}
. For an object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
we write
[X]
for its orbit under the action of simple currents.

For a given object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
it will be useful to know which subgroup of invertibles fix
X
. To that end we introduce the following notation.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
a simple object. Given
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
a subgroup of the invertibles of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
, we define

\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{d}}(X):=\{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}:g\otimes X\cong X\}.

The quantum dimensions of the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
satisfy a variety of useful equalities and inequalities.

Our main tool is the fact that the dimensions of the simples of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
respect the geometry of the truncated Weyl chamber in a nice manner. Namely if one draws a convex hull in the truncated Weyl chamber, then the minimum of the dimensions in this hull will occur at the corners.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
29
]

For
1\leq i\leq N
, let
X_{i}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
simple objects, and
t_{i}\in[0,1]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}t_{i}=1
. Then

\dim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}t_{i}X_{i}\right)\geq\min\left\{\dim(X_{i}):1\leq i\leq N\right\},

with equality occurring exactly at the corners of the convex hull.

We also have the following inequalities of quantum integers which occur due to the cut-off of the level
k
in the truncated Weyl chamber.

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
1\leq n\leq r+k
we have

[n]_{r,k}<[n]_{r+1,k}\quad\text{ and }\quad[n]_{r,k}<[n]_{r,k+1}.

Proof.

The second inequality holds as
[n]_{r,k}
is equal to
[n]_{n-1,r+k-n+1}
. The value
[n]_{n-1,r+k-n+1}
is precisely the graph norm of the fusion graph for
\Lambda_{1}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n},r+k-n+1)
. This fusion graph embeds in the fusion graph for
\Lambda_{1}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n},r+k-n+2)
, which has graph norm
[n]_{n-1,r+k-n+2}
. As graph norms respect inclusions, we get that

[n]_{n-1,r+k-n+1}<[n]_{n-1,r+k-n+2},

which is equivalent to

[n]_{r,k}<[n]_{r,k+1}.

The first inequality now holds as
[n]_{r,k}=[n]_{k,r}
.
∎

Often it will be useful to bound a quantum integer by a simpler function of
n
. The following inequalities allow us exactly that. The first bounds the quantum integer above.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
56
]

For all
n\geq 1
we have

[n]_{r,k}\leq n.

The second bounds the quantum integer below.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
56
]

Suppose that
1\leq n\leq\frac{c-1}{c}(1+r+k)
for some
c\in\mathbb{N}
, then

[n]_{r,k}\geq\frac{1}{c}.

With these general inequalities in hand, we can now prove a collection of useful inequalities on the dimensions of the simples of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For all
0\leq h\leq k
and
0\leq a\leq r
we have

\dim\left(h\Lambda_{a}\right)=\dim\left((k-h)\Lambda_{a}\right).

Proof.

By applying a simple current symmetry, we see that the objects
(h\Lambda_{a})
and
((k-h)\Lambda_{-a})
have the same dimension. Applying charge conjugation then gives the result.
∎

Our first true inequality gives bounds on the symmetric powers of the fundamental representations.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
1\leq a\leq r+1
and
1\leq\lambda_{a}\leq\frac{k-1}{2}
. If
\lambda_{a}\leq j\leq k-\lambda_{a}
then

\dim(j\Lambda_{a})\geq\dim(\lambda_{a}\Lambda_{a}).

Proof.

From Lemma
2.7
we have

\dim\left(\lambda_{a}\Lambda_{a}\right)=\dim\left((k-\lambda_{a})\Lambda_{a})\right).

We can write

j\Lambda_{a}=\left(1-\frac{j-\lambda_{a}}{k-2\lambda_{a}}\right)\cdot\lambda_{a}\Lambda_{a}+\frac{j-\lambda_{a}}{k-2\lambda_{a}}\cdot(k-\lambda_{a})\Lambda_{a}.

Thus the result follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Applying level-rank duality to the above bound, we can also obtain the following. Together, these bounds allow us to understand the ordering on the dimensions of the symmetric powers of the fundamental representations.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
1\leq a\leq\frac{r}{2}
and
1\leq\lambda_{a}\leq k
. If
a\leq j\leq r+1-a
then

\dim(\lambda_{a}\Lambda_{j})\geq\dim(\lambda_{a}\Lambda_{a}).

Proof.

Via a level-rank duality, we have that the dimension of
(\lambda_{a}\Lambda_{j})
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
is equal to the dimension of
(j\Lambda_{\lambda_{a}})
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{k},r+1)
. The result then follows from Lemma
2.8
.
∎

The last bound we will give applies to objects that are fixed by the invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. If this stabaliser subgroup of an object is non-trivial, then the following lemma gives strong restrictions on the dimension of that object.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
with
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)=\mathbb{Z}_{d}
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0\leq a\leq k
. Then

\dim(X)\geq\dim\left(a\Lambda_{m\frac{r+1}{d}}\right).

Proof.

As
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)=\mathbb{Z}_{d}
we have that
X
is fixed by
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{d}}
. Thus
X
is of the form

\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{d}}\lambda_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Lambda_{i+j\frac{r+1}{d}},

with

\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{d}}\lambda_{i}=\frac{k}{d}.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0\leq a\leq k
and define for
1\leq i\leq r+1
the objects

P_{i}:=(k-a)\Lambda_{i}+a\Lambda_{i+m\frac{r+1}{d}}.

As
k\Lambda_{1}\otimes P_{i}=P_{i+1}
, we have that
\dim(P_{i})=\dim(P_{j})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq r+1
.

We claim that

X=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{d}}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{i+j\frac{r+1}{d}}.

To see this we count the multiplicity of an arbitary
\Lambda_{\ell}
in both sides of the above equation. In the object
X
, the multiplicity of
\ell
is equal to
\lambda_{\ell\pmod{\frac{r+1}{d}}}
. On the right hand side,
\Lambda_{\ell}
will appear in the
P_{\ell}
term where it appears with multiplicity
a\frac{\lambda_{\ell\pmod{\frac{r+1}{d}}}}{k}
, and in the
P_{\ell-m\frac{r+1}{d}}
term where it appears with multiplicity
(k-a)\frac{\lambda_{\ell-m\frac{r+1}{d}\pmod{\frac{r+1}{d}}}}{k}=(k-a)\frac{\lambda_{\ell\pmod{\frac{r+1}{d}}}}{k}
. Thus in the entire right hand side,
\Lambda_{\ell}
appears with multiplicity
\lambda_{\ell\pmod{\frac{r+1}{d}}}
, and so the claim is valid.

As
\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{d}}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{d}1=1
, we can use Lemma
2.3
to see that

\dim(X)\geq\min(\dim(P_{i}):1\leq i\leq r+1)=\dim(P_{0})=\dim(a\Lambda_{m\frac{r+1}{d}})

as desired.

∎

2.2.
De-equivariantization

Our main focus of study in this paper will be the orbifold type
I
quantum subgroups, and their local modules. These are constructed as
de-equivariantisations
of the modular categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
.

In general let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided tensor category, and choose a distinguished subcategory braided equivalent to
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
for
G
a finite group. We can consider the function algebra
\operatorname{Fun}(G)\subset\operatorname{Rep}(G)\to\mathcal{C}
, which lifts to a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
via the braiding. We write
(\operatorname{Fun}(G),\sigma)
for this commutative central algebra object.

Definition 2.11
.

The
de-equivariantisation
of
\mathcal{C}
by
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is defined as the category of
\operatorname{Fun}(G)
modules, which can be endowed with the structure of a
G
-crossed braided category via
\sigma
. We write
\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{Rep}(G)}
for this de-equivariantisation.

The category
\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{Rep}(G)}
has the canonical structure of a
G
-crossed braided category (see
[
49
,
42
,
59
]
). The
G
-action is given by left translation of the algebra
\operatorname{Fun}(G)
i.e. multiplication by group elements. The category of local modules of the commutative central algebra object
(\operatorname{Fun}(G),\sigma)
is the trivially graded subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{Rep}(G)}
with respect to the
G
-crossed structure. We write
\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{Rep}(G)}^{0}
for this category of local modules.

With the generalities out of the way, let us now focus on the specific de-equivariantisations of interest for this paper. The Tannakian subcategories of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
are completely understood and classified. These subcategories of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
are parametrised by
m
a divisor of
r+1
satisfying
m^{2}\divides k(r+1)
if
r
is even, and
2m^{2}\divides k(r+1)
if
r
is odd. The corresponding Tannakian subcategory is equivalent to
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})
, and is generated by the invertible object
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m}}
.

Our goal is to describe the basic structure of the modular tensor category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. We begin by looking at the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-crossed braided category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. The objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
are of the form
(Y,\rho)
, where
Y
is an object which is fixed by tensoring by
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m}}
, and
\rho
is a choice of isomorphism
Y\to k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m}}\otimes Y
satisfying a standard coherence condition. We have the free module functor

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}:\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}

given by tensoring with the algebra object
\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})=\oplus_{i}k\Lambda_{i\frac{r+1}{m}}
. It is known that the functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}
is dominant
[
4
, Proposition 5.5]
. The adjoint to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}
is the lax monoidal functor given by forgetting the isomorphism
\rho
. That is

\mathcal{F}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}((Y,\rho))=Y.

In order to simplify our proofs and computations later, it is necessary to give a more elementary description of the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. Our skein theoretic version of the proof can be found in Lemma
3.6
.

Lemma 2.12
.

[
47
, Corollary 5.3]

The simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
are parameterised by pairs
(X,\chi_{X})
, where
X
is a simple object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
considered up to action by
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m}}
, and
\chi_{X}
is a character of the group
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)
.

The dimension of the simple object
(X,\chi_{X})
is given by

\dim(X,\chi_{X})=\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)|}

The canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action on these simples is given by multiplication of
\chi_{X}
by the standard character
g^{j}\mapsto e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)|}}
.

Under this parametrisation, we can explicitly describe the free module functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}
. We have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\hat{G}}(X,\chi):\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}.

Note that the restriction of the adjoint
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}^{*}:\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
to the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}^{0}
is a ribbon lax monoidal functor
[
47
, Lemma 3.10]
or
[
3
, Lemme 3.3]
. In practice, this means that the twist of
(X,\chi)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}^{0}
is equal to the twist on
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
.

To obtain the simple objects of the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
we must take the objects which are
0
-graded in the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-graded category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-grading on the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is inherited from the
\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}
-grading on the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. Thus a simple object
(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i},\chi)
will live in the
\sum_{i=1}^{r}i\lambda_{i}
graded component of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, taken modulo
m
. This gives the following.

Lemma 2.13
.

The simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
are parametrised by pairs
(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i},\chi)
where
\sum_{i=1}^{r}i\lambda_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{m}
, and
\chi
is a character of
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i})
.

Let us single out a distinguished object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. Consider the object
(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
. We can take the image of this object under the functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}
to obtain an object in
\left(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k))_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}\right)^{ad}\subset\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
.

Definition 2.14
.

We define the object

\Omega:=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}.

The distinguished simple object
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
satisfies several nice properties that will make it useful for us in our computations later. Immediately we have that
\Omega
is self-dual, its dimension is
[r]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}
, and there exists a map
\Omega\otimes\Omega\to\Omega
.

We now compute some useful information about the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. Let us define
m^{\prime}=\gcd(m,k)
and
m^{\prime\prime}:=\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}
.

For the remainder of the paper we will constantly encounter three exceptions in nearly all of our lemmas and proofs. These are the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
. To put them to rest, we deal with them now.

Lemma 2.15
.

The claims of theorem
1.4
hold for the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
.

Proof.

From the formula of the dimensions of the simples of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
we immediately see that each of these cases is pointed. By considering twists we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{1,e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{3}},e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{3}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,-1,-1,-1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{6},\{1,e^{2\pi i\frac{5}{12}},e^{2\pi i\frac{2}{3}},e^{2\pi i\frac{3}{4}},e^{2\pi i\frac{2}{3}},e^{2\pi i\frac{5}{12}}\},

where the second argument describes the non-degenerate quadratic form on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. With these explicit presentations, it is straight-forward to verify that they satisfy the claims of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Remark 2.16
.

In order to keep the statements of various lemma tidy, for the remainder of this paper we will implicitly assume that the three cases
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
are ignored.

With the special cases mentioned in the above remark excluded, we can show that the object
\Omega
is simple.

Lemma 2.17
.

The object
\Omega
is a simple object in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
.

Proof.

This lemma is equivalent to showing that
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})
is trivial. Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, then the corresponding invertible object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
is
k\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{m}}
. We compute

k\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{m}}\otimes(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})\cong\left((k-2)\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{m}}+\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{m}+1}+\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{m}-1}\right).

A case by case analysis, where we consider
k\geq 4
,
k=3
, and
k=2
gives the desired result.
∎

Let us study the group of invertibles of the modular category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
.

Lemma 2.18
.

We have

\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})})=\left\{(k\Lambda_{\ell m^{\prime\prime}},1):\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}\right\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}.

Proof.

From the formula for the dimensions of the simples of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, it is clear that
(X,\chi_{X})\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
will be invertible if and only if
X
has integer dimension, and lives in a graded component of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
which is a multiple of
m
. The objects with integer dimension in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
have been classified
[
55
]
, and aside from the special cases we have discarded, the only such objects are the invertibles.

The invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
are of the form
k\Lambda_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}
. These invertible objects live in the graded component
ki
of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. Hence, to find the invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, we need to see which
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}
satisfy the equation
ki=Nm
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We can write this equation as
\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}i=Nm^{\prime\prime}
. As a consequence of the definition of
m^{\prime}
and
m^{\prime\prime}
, we have that
\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}
and
m^{\prime\prime}
are coprime. Thus,
m^{\prime\prime}
divides
i
, and so
i
is a multiple of
m^{\prime\prime}
. This tells us the group of invertibles of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is generated by the object
(k\Lambda_{m^{\prime\prime}},1)
, and hence form a group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
.
∎

The category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is modular, which implies the universal grading group is isomorphic to the group of invertibles. Hence we get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.19
.

The universal grading group of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is the group
\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
.

Let us now identify the adjoint subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. Knowing this subcategory will allow us to use powerful graded category techniques. A natural guess would be that
\left({\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}}\right)^{\text{ad}}\simeq\left(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}\right)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. However this doesn’t even typecheck, as
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})
is not necessarily always a subcategory of
\left(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}\right)
i.e. consider
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{8},3)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
. Instead we find that the adjoint subcategory is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. As
m^{\prime}
divides
k
, we have that
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
.

Lemma 2.20
.

We have

\left({\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}}\right)^{\text{ad}}\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}

Proof.

The category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
naturally embeds in
{\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}}
via the identity functor. As
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is generated by the simple object
\Omega
, and
\operatorname{dimHom}(\Omega\otimes\Omega\to\Omega)\geq 1
, the adjoint subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is itself. Hence the adjoint subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}}
contains
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. The global dimension of
\left({\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}}\right)^{\text{ad}}
and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
are the same, thus we have an equivalence.
∎

Finally, we study the invertible objects of the adjoint subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
.

Lemma 2.21
.

We have

\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Ad}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k))_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}},

where
n^{\prime}:=\gcd(r+1,k)
.

Proof.

This proof is fairly similar to the proof of Lemma
2.18
. The same idea shows that any invertible of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
will be of the form
(k\Lambda_{i},1)
where
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
and
ki\equiv 0\pmod{r+1}
. This implies that
i
has to be a multiple of
\frac{r+1}{n^{\prime}}
. Thus the invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
are of the form
(k\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{n^{\prime}}},1)
, where
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}
.
∎

The invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
act transitively on the simple object
\Omega
in all but one special case.

Lemma 2.22
.

Suppose
(r,k,m)\nin\{(1,4,1),(2,3,1),(3,2,1)\}
, and let
g\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})})
. Then

g\otimes\Omega\cong\Omega\implies g\cong\mathbf{1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.18
we have that
g\cong(k\Lambda_{\ell m^{\prime\prime}},1)
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
. As
g\otimes\Omega\cong\Omega
, we get

k\Lambda_{\ell m^{\prime\prime}}\otimes(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})\quad\text{ and }\quad(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})

live in the same orbit under the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. Thus there exists a
j
such that

(1)

\Lambda_{-1+\ell m^{\prime\prime}}+(k-2)\Lambda_{\ell m^{\prime\prime}}+\Lambda_{\ell m^{\prime\prime}+1}=\Lambda_{-1+j\frac{r+1}{m}}+(k-2)\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{m}}+\Lambda_{1+j\frac{r+1}{m}}.

We first deal with the special case of
r=1
. If
m=2
, then we have that
\ell\equiv 0\pmod{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
, and so
g\cong\mathbf{1}
. Otherwise
m=1
, and Equation (
1
) becomes

2\Lambda_{\ell+1}+(k-2)\Lambda_{\ell}=2\Lambda_{1}+(k-2)\Lambda_{0}.

Either
\ell=0
and the desired result is immediate, or
\ell=1
, which forces
k=4
(an excluded case in the statement of the lemma).

With the case of
r=1
dealt with, we can assume that

\Lambda_{-1+\ell m^{\prime\prime}}\neq\Lambda_{\ell m^{\prime\prime}+1}\quad\text{and }\quad\Lambda_{-1+j\frac{r+1}{m}}\neq\Lambda_{1+j\frac{r+1}{m}}.

We now break into cases depending on
k
.

If
k>3
, then Equation (
1
) gives that
\ell m^{\prime\prime}\equiv j\frac{r+1}{m}\pmod{r+1}
. Hence
\ell\equiv 0\pmod{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
and so
g\cong\mathbf{1}
as desired.

If
k=3
, then Equation (
1
) gives that
\ell m^{\prime\prime}\equiv\{-1+j\frac{r+1}{m},j\frac{r+1}{m},1+j\frac{r+1}{m}\}\pmod{r+1}\}
. If
\ell m^{\prime\prime}\equiv j\frac{r+1}{m}\pmod{r+1}
, then
\ell\equiv 0\pmod{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
, and we have
g\cong\mathbf{1}
. If
\ell m^{\prime\prime}\equiv\pm 1+j\frac{r+1}{m}\pmod{r+1}
, then Equation (
1
) gives
3\equiv 0\pmod{r+1}
, and so
r=2
. Either we have
m=1
, in which case we are excluded by the statement of the lemma, or
m=3
, in which case we are excluded by Remark
2.16
.

If
k=2
then Equation (
1
) gives that either
\ell m^{\prime\prime}\equiv j\frac{r+1}{m}\pmod{r+1}
or
\ell m^{\prime\prime}\equiv 2+j\frac{r+1}{m}\equiv-2+j\frac{r+1}{m}\pmod{r+1}
. In the first case, we have that
\ell\equiv 0\pmod{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
and we are done. Inthe latter case, we have that
4\equiv 0\pmod{r+1}
, and so
r=3
. We either have
m=2
, in which case we are excluded by Remark
2.16
, or
m=1
, in which case we are excluded by the statement of the lemma.
∎

We will make
\Omega
the base point of our auto-equivalence computations, and distinguish auto-equivalences based on whether they fix or move this object.

Definition 2.23
.

We say an auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is
non-exceptional
if it maps
\Omega
to an image of
\Omega
under simple currents. We will say the auto-equivalence is
exceptional
if it is not non-exceptional.

We will see in the bulk of this paper the surprising result that only a finite number of auto-equivalences are exceptional, and that every non-exceptional auto-equivalence comes from either a simple current auto-equivalence, charge conjugation, or from the canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action.

While the definition of a non-exceptional auto-equivalence allows for the object
\Omega
to be moved, the following lemma shows this is not the case.

Lemma 2.24
.

A non-exceptional auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
must fix
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
a non-exceptional auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. Then there exists an invertible element
g
of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
such that
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)\cong g\otimes\Omega
. As
g
is of the form
(k\Lambda_{nm^{\prime\prime}},1)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we compute that

g\otimes\Omega\cong((k-2)\Lambda_{nm^{\prime\prime}}+\Lambda_{nm^{\prime\prime}+1}+\Lambda_{nm^{\prime\prime}-1},1).

The object
\Omega
is self-dual, and so
g\otimes\Omega
must be as well, thus
g\otimes\Omega\cong g^{*}\otimes\Omega
. Assuming that we are not in the excluded cases of Lemma
2.22
, we can apply this lemma to obtain
g^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathbf{1}
, and so
g\cong(k\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{2m}},1)
for
j\in\{0,1\}
. If we are in one of the three excluded cases, then a direct calculation shows that
g\otimes\Omega\cong\Omega
for all
g\in\operatorname{Inv}\left(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}\right)
and hence
\mathcal{F}
fixes
\Omega
.

For the generic case of
r\geq 2
and
k\geq 3
, we know that
\dim\hom(\Omega\otimes\Omega\to\Omega)=2
, and thus
\dim\hom(g\otimes\Omega\otimes g\otimes\Omega\to g\otimes\Omega)=2
. Using the braiding on the category, along with the fact that
g
has order two, we see that
\dim\hom(\Omega\otimes\Omega\to g\otimes\Omega)=2
. We explicitly compute the simple decomposition of
\Omega\otimes\Omega
as

\mathbf{1}\oplus 2\Omega\oplus(\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{r},1)\oplus(\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{r-1},1)\oplus(2\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r-1},1)\oplus(2\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{r},1).

As
g\otimes\Omega=((k-2)\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{2m}}+\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{2m}+1}+\Lambda_{j\frac{r+1}{2m}-1},1)
must appear in this decomposition, we can immediately deduce that
j=0
, i.e.
g
must be the identity.

For the remaining cases, the proof is almost identical, except the decomposition of
\Omega\otimes\Omega
is smaller, and in some cases the stabaliser subgroup of the simples in the decomposition is non-trivial, so the characters of the stabaliser groups must be changed.
∎

In light of the above result we make the following definition.

Definition 2.25
.

We write
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})};\Omega)
for the group of braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
which fix
\Omega
, or equivalently, the group of non-exceptional auto-equivalences.

2.3.
Planar Algebras

A key tool for the results of this paper are
planar algebras
. Roughly speaking a planar algebra
\mathcal{P}
is a collection of vector spaces
\{\mathcal{P}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, along with a multi-linear action of planar tangles. The full definition can be found in
[
39
]
, and illuminating examples in
[
45
]
.

We will be interested in planar algebras constructed from symmetrically self-dual objects in pivotal fusion categories. Let
X\in\mathcal{C}
be such an object. Then we can define a planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{X}
by

(\mathcal{P}_{X})_{n}:=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{1}\to X^{\otimes n}).

Supposing the object
X
generated
\mathcal{C}
, then we can recover
\mathcal{C}
by taking the idempotent completion of
\mathcal{P}_{X}
. Here the objects are idempotents in the algebras
(\mathcal{P}_{X})_{2n}
(where we have
n
legs pointing up, and
n
legs pointing down) with vertical stacking as the multiplication. The morphisms between two idempotents are elements of the planar algebra which intertwine the two idempotents. The tensor product is given by horizontal juxtaposition, and direct sums are added formally. Additional information on these two constructions can be found in
[
45
]
.

It is proven in
[
33
, Theorem A]
that the above bijection between planar algebras and symmetrically self-dual objects
X\in\mathcal{C}
is functorial. That is there is an isomorphism between automorphisms of the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{X}
, and pivotal auto-equivalences of the category
\mathcal{C}
which fix
X
.

2.4.
Simple Current Auto-equivalences

A useful class of auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
are given by
simple current auto-equivalences
. These are graded auto-equivalences which permute the simple objects by tensoring with certain invertible objects in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. The precise definition is as follows.

Lemma 2.26
.

[
17
, Lemma 2.4]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a modular tensor category, and
g
an invertible object of order
M
. Set
q
equal to the unique integer (modulo
2M
) such that

\sigma_{g,g}=e^{2\pi i\frac{q}{2M}}\operatorname{id}_{g\otimes g},

(see
[
54
, Proposition 2.5.1]
)
and choose
a\in\{0,1,\cdots,M-1\}
such that

1+aq\quad\text{ is coprime to }M.

Then there exists a monoidal auto-equivalence
\mathcal{F}_{g,a}
of
\mathcal{C}
defined on objects by

\mathcal{F}_{g,a}(X)=g^{-an}\otimes X,

where
n
is the unique integer (modulo
M
) such that
\sigma_{X,g}\sigma_{g,X}=e^{2\pi i\frac{n}{M}}\operatorname{id}_{g\otimes X}
. The monoidal auto-equivalence
\mathcal{F}_{g,a}
is braided if and only if

a+\frac{a^{2}q}{2}\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

As
\Omega
is in the adjoint subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, we have that any simple current auto-equivalence fixes
\Omega
, and hence is non-exceptional.

3.
Non-exceptional auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}

In this section we will determine the braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
that fix the distinguished object
\Omega
. In terms of the notation introduced in this paper, we will determine the group
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})};\Omega)
. We show that non-exceptional auto-equivalences (in the formal definition of this paper) are non-exceptional (in the layman terms). That is, every non-exceptional braided auto-equivalence is either charge conjugation, simple current, or comes from the canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action on
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
.

Let us outline the arguments of this section. To begin, we initially focus our attention on the distinguished subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. The subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
has two nice features that will assist with the results of this section. First is that it has trivial universal grading group, and hence has a unique pivotal structure, and second the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}
is generated by the distinguished object
\Omega
. Together these facts will allow us powerful planar algebra techniques to determine the non-exceptional symmetries.

With the above in mind, we give a presentation of the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, i.e. the planar algebra generated by the object
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. To achieve this, we observe that
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
contains the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
, i.e. the planar algebra generated by the object
\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
. The planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
is well understood, and is known to be generated by two trivalent vertices. We can then find an additional generator in
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, which together with the two trivalent vertices, generate all of
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
. The idea here is that the group
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
is singly generated, which allows us to understand skein theory for de-equivariantisation in terms of the addition of one additional generator. With the generators of
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
identified, we can then find relations that these generators satisfy.

Remark 3.1
.

While it is not explicit in this paper, the techniques we have briefly described above (and will explain in detail in the remainder of this section), can be used to give skein theory for any de-equivariantisation by an abelian group.

With the presentation of the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
in hand, we can use it to give an upper bound for the group of braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
which fix
\Omega
. We find that there are at most
2m^{\prime}
of these auto-equivalences, which compose to form a group isomorphic to
D_{m^{\prime}}
. Further, we explicitly identify how these potential auto-equivalences act on the simples of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. We then construct these
2m^{\prime}
potential auto-equivalences by the charge conjugation auto-equivalence, which gives us a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
subgroup, and by the canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
-action on
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
which comes from de-equivariantisation.

To obtain the auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
which fix
\Omega
, we appeal to the techniques developed in
[
16
]
. These techniques allow us to give an upper bound for
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})};\Omega)
in terms of
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}};\Omega)
and some cohomogical data. While there is no reason that this bound should be sharp (the techniques involve verifying that certain obstructions vanish in order to show that auto-equivalences lift) we are able to show that the theoretical upper bound is realised by simple current auto-equivalences.

All together we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
m
a divisor of
r+1
such that
m^{2}\divides k(r+1)
. Set
m^{\prime}=\gcd(m,k)
and
m^{\prime\prime}=\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}
. Then we have the following isomorphism of groups

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})};\Omega)\cong\begin{cases}\{e\}\text{ if $k=2$ and $r=1$}\\
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{p+t}\text{ if $k=2$ or $r=1$}\\
D_{m^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{p+t}\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

where

•

p
is the number of distinct odd primes dividing
\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}
but not
\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}
, and

•

t=\begin{cases}0\text{ if $\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}$ is odd, or if $\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}\equiv 0\pmod{4}$, or if both $\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}$ is odd, and $\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\equiv 2\pmod{4}$}\\
1\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

With the high-level arguments in mind, let us begin with the details of proving the above theorem.

Consider the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
. As
\Omega
generates, and their exists a map
\Omega\otimes\Omega\to\Omega
, we have that
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
has trivial universal grading group, and thus also has a unique pivotal structure. Therefore we have that
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}},\Omega)
is isomorphic to the group of braided planar algebra automorphisms of
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
. Our goal is thus to specify as much of the structure of this planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
as possible in order to understand its auto-equivalence group.

As the free module functor
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is dominant, and maps
\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}
to
\Omega
, we obtain a planar algebra embedding

\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}\to\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}.

The planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
is well understood
[
38
,
17
]
. It is generated by two trivalent vertices satisfying the Thurston relations (see
[
38
, Lemma 3.2]
). Hence the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
also contains two trivalent vertices

and

satisfying these same Thurston relations. However, there are going to be additional generators in this planar algebra. These additional generators come from the de-equivariantization by
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})
.

Remark 3.3
.

For the remainder of this section we will identify
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
as the idempotent completion of the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
as the idempotent completion of the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
. This means that we regard simple objects of these categories as minimal idempotents of the planar algebras, and morphisms as elements of the planar algebra which commute with the idempotents.

Let us write
p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}
for the minimal idempotent of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
corresponding to the simple object
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
. From the inclusion of planar algebras
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}\to\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, we have that this idempotent
p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}
also exists in
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
.

The free module functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}:\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
sends
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
to the tensor unit. Therefore in the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, the trivial idempotent and
p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}
are isomorphic. Thus there exists an invertible element
S\in\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
(which we draw as a circle to differentiate it from the other planar algebra elements) satisfying

\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{SstarS.pdf}}=1\quad\text{ and}\quad\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{SSstar.pdf}}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{p+.pdf}}.

The element
S
lives in the
n
-box space of
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, where
n
is the smallest
n
such that
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
appears in the decomposition of
(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})^{\otimes n}

We claim that
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
is generated by the two trivalent vertices, along with the new element
S
.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have that
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
is generated by the two Thurston trivalent vertices, and the element
S
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{S}
be the sub-planar algebra of
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
generated by these three elements, and
\mathcal{C}_{S}
the corresponding category. Then we have a chain of embeddings

\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}\to\mathcal{P}_{S}\to\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}.

This gives us dominant monoidal functors

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}\to\mathcal{C}_{S}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{C}_{S}\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}},

and their adjoints

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{*}_{1}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{C}_{S}\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{*}_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}\to\mathcal{C}_{S}.

From
[
5
]
, we have that
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{1}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{S}})
is a commutative central algebra object, and that
\mathcal{C}_{S}
is equivalent to the category of
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{1}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{S}})
-modules in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
.

As these dominant functors
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are just the inclusions of idempotents, we have that the composition of these two dominant functors is equal on the nose to the dominant functor
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
induced by the planar algebra inclusion

\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}\to\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}.

This induced functor
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}\to\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is precisely the free module functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}
. Hence we have that

\mathcal{F}_{2}\circ\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}},

which implies that

\mathcal{F}^{*}_{1}\circ\mathcal{F}^{*}_{2}=\mathcal{F}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}.

From this fact we see

\mathcal{F}^{*}_{1}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{S}})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{*}_{1}\circ\mathcal{F}^{*}_{2}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}}\right)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}^{*}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}}\right)=\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}),

as a central commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
. In particular we get that
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{1}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{S}})\cong\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
where
\ell\divides m^{\prime}
. As
\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})
is the central commutative algebra object in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
corresponding to the de-equivariantisation by the
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})
subcategory, the central structure is given by the braiding of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
. Hence the central structure on
\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is also given by the braiding. This gives that
\mathcal{C}_{S}
is a de-equivariantisation of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
by
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
, i.e.

\mathcal{C}_{S}\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})}.

In
\mathcal{C}_{S}
we know that
S
gives an isomorphism from
\mathbf{1}\to p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}
which implies that
m^{\prime}\divides\ell
. Thus

\mathcal{C}_{S}\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}

which gives the desired isomorphism of planar algebras

\mathcal{P}_{S}\cong\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}.

∎

In order to study the planar algebra automorphisms of
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
we need to study the element
S
further, and deduce further relations that it satisfies.

Remark 3.5
.

To simplify notation, we will now draw multiple strands of a planar algebra as a single strand in our graphical diagrams. It will be clear from context how many strands are meant by the diagram.

In the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
we have that
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}^{\otimes m^{\prime}}\cong\mathbf{1}
. Thus the object
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
generates a subcategory with the fusion rules of
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
. We are given that this subcategory is Tannakian (as it is the subcategory we are de-equivariantating by), so it is braided equivalent to
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
, let
p_{k\Lambda_{r\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
be the unique (by the fusion rules) projection onto
k\Lambda_{n\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
appearing in the smallest possible box-space of
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
. Note that as
\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}
is self-dual, we get that
k\Lambda_{n\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
and
k\Lambda_{-n\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
live in the same box space. We can choose a system of trivalent vertices

t_{n,p}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{triv}}\quad:p_{k\Lambda_{n\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}\otimes p_{k\Lambda_{p\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}\to p_{k\Lambda_{(n+p)\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}

in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
with trivial 6-j symbols, and such that the charge conjugation auto-equivalence
k\Lambda_{n\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}\mapsto k\Lambda_{-n\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
maps
t_{n,p}\mapsto t_{-n,-p}
.

We can build an isomorphism

j:=t_{1,1}t_{2,1}\cdots t_{m^{\prime}-1,1}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{j}}\quad:p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}^{\otimes m^{\prime}}}\to\mathbf{1}.

Hence we have that
j
is a map from the idempotent
p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}^{\otimes m^{\prime}}
to the trivial idempotent in the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
. In the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
we have that
S^{\otimes m^{\prime}}
is an isomorphism from the idempotent
p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}^{\otimes m^{\prime}}
to the trivial idempotent. Thus we have that

S^{\otimes m^{\prime}}\circ j

lives in the 0-box space of
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
and is non-zero. This allows us to normalise
S
so that we get the relation

(2)

\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{Smp}}\quad=\quad\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{jinv}},

in the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
.

This explicit presentation of the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
is sufficient to compute the minimal idempotents up to equivalence, and thus the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
are parametrised (up to isomorphism) by

(X,\chi_{X})

where
X
is a simple object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
(up to action by
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
) and
\chi_{X}
is a character of the group
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)
. The quantum dimension of
(X,\chi_{X})
is equal to
\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)|}
.

Proof.

The free module functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}
is dominant, therefore every simple object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is a sub-object of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)
for some
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
. Let
p_{X}
be the minimal projection in the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
corresponding to
X
. As
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is idempotent complete, each simple sub-object of
X
will correspond (up to isomorphism) to a minimal sub-idempotent of
p_{X}
.

Assume that
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, then there exists an isomorphism
f_{X}:p_{X}\otimes p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}^{\otimes\frac{m^{\prime}}{d}}}\to p_{X}
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
we define isomorphisms
r_{X,n}:p_{X}\to p_{X}
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
by

r_{X,n}:=f_{X}^{n}\circ S^{\otimes n\frac{m^{\prime}}{d}}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{rxn}}.

By design we have that
r_{X,n}r_{X,n^{\prime}}=r_{X,n+n^{\prime}}
. Furthermore, by relation
2
we have that
r_{X,d}:p_{X}\to p_{X}
lives in
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
. As
p_{X}
is simple in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
, we have that
r_{X,d}
must be a scaler multiple of
p_{X}
. We normalise our choice of the isomorphism
f_{X}
to ensure that
r_{X,d}=p_{X}
. Thus we have that
\operatorname{End}(p_{X})
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{d}]
. It is a classical result that the minimal idempotents are indexed by characters
\chi
of
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
with

p_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)|}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\chi(n)r_{X,n}.

The quantum dimension of the minimal idempotent
p_{\chi}
is given by the trace. Note that the trace of
r_{X,n}
is
0
, unless
n=0
, as otherwise we could build a non-trivial morphism

k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}^{\otimes\frac{nm^{\prime}}{d}}\to\mathbf{1}.

If
n=0
, then the trace of
r_{X,n}
is the quantum dimension of
X
. Hence the trace of
p_{\chi}
is equal to the quantum dimension of
X
divided by
|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)|
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

For ease of notation, let us fix isomorphisms
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\to\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{N}}
by

n\mapsto\chi_{n}:=a\mapsto e^{2\pi i\frac{na}{N}}.

We can now determine an upper bound for the group
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega})
, and hence also for the group
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}};\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

We have that

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}};\Omega)\subseteq D_{m^{\prime}},

with generators

(X,\chi_{n})\mapsto(X,\chi_{n+1})

and

(X,\chi_{n})\mapsto(X^{*},\chi_{-n}).

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega})
be a braided automorphism. Then
\phi
is determined by where it sends the three generators. Recall, we have two trivalent vertices satisfying the Thurston relations, and the generator
S
which lives in the
n
-box space, where
n
is the smallest
n
such that
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
appears in the decomposition of
(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})^{\otimes n}
. By explicitly expanding
(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})^{\otimes 3}
we can see that
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
appears as a summand only in the case
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},4)^{\text{ad}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},3)^{\text{ad}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},2)^{\text{ad}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
. These cases have already been excluded and dealt with previously in the paper.

Let us deal with the remaining cases. As
S
does not live in the three box space, we know that there are scalers
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\phi\left(\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{UPTLTRIV}}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{1}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{UPTLTRIV}}+c_{2}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{UPSV}}

\displaystyle\phi\left(\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{UPSV}}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{3}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{UPTLTRIV}}+c_{4}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{UPSV}}.

The coefficients
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}
for which
\phi
preserve the Thurston relations are solved for in
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
. With the condition that
\phi
is braided, there are two solutions, which we denote
\phi_{\text{id}}
and
\phi_{\text{cc}}
. These planar algebra automorphisms on the sub-planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
are explicitly identified in the cited paper, where it is found that
\phi_{\text{cc}}
corresponds to the charge conjugation auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
.

Now the charge-conjugation auto-equivalence maps
k\Lambda_{\pm\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}\mapsto k\Lambda_{\mp\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
, thus we have the following in the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
:

\phi_{\text{id}}\left(p_{k\Lambda_{\pm\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}\right)=p_{k\Lambda_{\pm\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}

and

\phi_{\text{cc}}\left(p_{k\Lambda_{\pm\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}\right)=p_{k\Lambda_{\mp\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}.

As the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
canonically embeds in
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, we also have these relations in the larger planar algebra.

To see when these auto-equivalences
\phi_{\text{id}}
and
\phi_{\text{cc}}
extend to the full planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
we must determine if (and how) these automorphisms act on the generators
S
.

Let us define isomorphisms in
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
by

S_{n}:=S^{\otimes n}\circ t_{1,1}t_{2,1}\cdots t_{n-1,1}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{sn}}\quad:\mathbf{1}\to p_{k\Lambda_{n\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}.

Note that trivially we have
S_{1}=S
, and by relation (
2
) we have that
S_{m^{\prime}}=1
.

To see when
\phi_{\text{id}}
extends to
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, observe that
\phi_{\text{id}}(S)
is an isomorphism from
\mathbf{1}\to p_{k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}}
. As this morphism space is 1-dimensional, we must have that
\phi_{\text{id}}(S)=\beta S
for some non-zero scaler
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. Applying the potential automorphism to relation (
2
) gives that
\beta
must be an
m^{\prime}
-th root of unity.

To see when
\phi_{\text{cc}}
extends to
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, observe that
\phi_{\text{cc}}(S)
is an isomorphism from
\mathbf{1}\to p_{k\Lambda_{-\frac{r+1}{m}}}
. This implies that
\phi_{\text{cc}}(S)=\hat{\beta}S_{m^{\prime}-1}
for some non-zero scaler
\hat{\beta}\in\mathbb{C}
. We apply this potential automorphism to relation (
2
) to obtain

\hat{\beta}^{m^{\prime}}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{SmpConj}}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{jinvConj}}.

From this equation we expand out the
S_{m^{\prime}-1}
terms to obtain an equation with
(m^{\prime}-1)m^{\prime}
of
S
terms. We then apply relation (
2
) to get an equation purely in terms of the trivalent vertices
t
. From here we then use that the trivalent vertices
t
have trivial 6-j symbols to obtain
\hat{\beta}^{m^{\prime}}=1
. Thus
\hat{\beta}
must be an
m^{\prime}
-root of unity.

With the explicit presentation of how the
2m^{\prime}
potential automorphisms act on the generator
S
, it is straight forward to determine that if these automorphisms existed, then they would form a group isomorphic to
D_{m^{\prime}}
. Note that the two automorphisms corresponding to
\beta=e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}}
, and
\hat{\beta}=1
are generators for the entire automorphism group.

We now determine how these
D_{m^{\prime}}
worth of potential automorphisms would act on the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. Let
(X,\chi_{n})
be a simple object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
, where
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
for some
d\mid m^{\prime}
, and
\chi_{n}\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
(using the isomorphism of Remark
3.7
).

For the planar algebra automorphisms sending
S
to
\beta S
we pick the generator
\beta=e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}}
to study.We compute that
r_{X,j}\mapsto\beta^{j\frac{m^{\prime}}{d}}r_{X,j}=e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{d}}r_{X,j}
. Therefore under this planar algebra automorphism, we have

p_{(X,\chi_{n})}\mapsto\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\chi_{n}(j)e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{d}}r_{X,j}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}e^{2\pi i\frac{nj}{d}}e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{d}}r_{X,j}=p_{(X,\chi_{n+1})}.

Thus the auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
corresponding to the planar algebra automorphism for
\beta=e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}}
maps

(X,\chi_{n})\mapsto(X^{*},\chi_{-n}).

For the planar algebra automorphism sending
S\mapsto\hat{\beta}S_{m^{\prime}-1}
we have to work a little harder to determine where it sends the simple object
(X,\chi_{n})
. We pick the generator
\hat{\beta}=1
to study. Recall that this planar algebra automorphism restricts to
\phi_{\text{cc}}
on the sub-planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}}
. Thus we know how this automorphism acts on the trivalent vertices
t
, so we can compute that

f_{X}\mapsto\gamma f_{X^{*}}^{m^{\prime}-1}\circ(t_{1,1}^{-1}t_{2,1}^{-1}\cdots t_{m^{\prime}-2,1}^{-1})^{\otimes d}

for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
. By simultaneously rescaling the trivalent vertices
t
, we can ensure that
\gamma=1
. With this information we compute that

r_{X,j}\mapsto r_{X^{*},-j},

and hence

p_{(X,\chi_{n})}\mapsto\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\chi_{n}(j)r_{X^{*},-j}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\chi_{n}(-j)r_{X^{*},j}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\chi_{-n}(j)r_{X^{*},j}=p_{(X^{*},\chi_{-n})}.

Thus the auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
corresponding to the planar algebra automorphism for
\hat{\beta}=1
maps

(X,\chi_{n})\mapsto(X^{*},\chi_{-n}).

∎

The above lemma gives an upper bound on the braided auto-equivalence group (which fix
\Omega
) for the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. In theory we could determine a complete set of relations for the planar algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, and verify that the auto-equivalences exist by checking that they preserve all relations. However this requires additional work which is beyond the scope of this paper. Instead we construct
2m^{\prime}
worth of braided auto-equivalences of the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
directly, realising the upper bound.

Lemma 3.9
.

We have

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}};\Omega)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}\text{ if $k=2$ or $r=1$ }\\
D_{m^{\prime}}\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

Proof.

Let us begin by constructing the
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
worth of braided auto-equivalences. Via construction, we have that
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
acts on
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
via the map

(X,\chi_{n})\mapsto(X,\chi_{n+1}).

To obtain the full
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
worth of auto-equivalences we need to show this action is faithful. This is equivalent to finding an object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
with
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)=\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
. If
m^{\prime}
is odd, then the object

X=\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}\Lambda_{i\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}

satisfies
X\otimes k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}=X
, and lives in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
as

\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}i\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}=(r+1)\frac{k(m^{\prime}+1)}{2m^{\prime}}\equiv 0\pmod{r+1}.

When
m^{\prime}
is even, the above object does not live in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
. To choose a suitable object, we observe that
r+1
is even in this setting (as
m\mid r+1
), and so
2m^{2}\mid(r+1)k
. In particular
\frac{(r+1)k}{2(m^{\prime})^{2}}
is an integer. For this case we pick the object

X=\left(\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}\Lambda_{i\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}\Lambda_{i\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}+\frac{(r+1)k}{2(m^{\prime})^{2}}}.

This object satisfies
X\otimes k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}=X
as desired, and lives in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
as

\left(\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}i\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}i\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}+\frac{(r+1)k}{2(m^{\prime})^{2}}=(r+1)\left(\frac{k(m^{\prime}+1)}{2m^{\prime}}+\frac{k}{2m^{\prime}}\right)=(r+1)\left(\frac{k(m^{\prime}+2)}{2m^{\prime}}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{r+1}.

In either case, we have an object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
with
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}}(X)=\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
as desired.

To construct the remaining auto-equivalences, we observe that the charge-conjugation auto-equivalences exist for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
when
k\geq 3
and
r\geq 2

[
17
]
. These auto-equivalences preserve the
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})
subcategory, and hence descend to auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
.

To finish the proof, we must show that the charge conjugation auto-equivalence never coincides with the
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
action. Thus we have to find an object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{\text{ad}}
such that
X^{*}
is not in the orbit of the action of
k\Lambda_{\frac{r+1}{m^{\prime}}}
. This object is given by

X=\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{r-1}.

This satisfies the required properties when
r\geq 3
and
k\geq 3
.

If
r=2
and
k\geq 3
, then we can use the object

X=3\Lambda_{1}.

If
k=2
or
r=1
, then
m^{\prime}\in\{1,2\}
and the result is given in
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

Now that we understand the auto-equivalences of the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
which fix
\Omega
, we can leverage this to determine the auto-equivalences of the full category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. The idea here is to use the fact that
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
-graded extension of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. This allows us to apply the results of
[
16
]
to classify the auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
extending a given auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. To convenience the reader, the results of
[
16
]
state for a
G
-graded category
\oplus_{G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, the number of auto-equivalences extending
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Eq}(\mathcal{C}_{e})
is bounded above by

|\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G):\mathcal{C}_{g}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\phi(g)}\text{ as $\mathcal{C}_{e}$-bimodules for all $g\in G$}|\cdot|H^{1}(G,\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{e})))|\cdot|H^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})|.

With this bound, we can determine the following result.

Lemma 3.10
.

The group of auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
extending the identity on the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is isomorphic to the group

\left\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}:1+a\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}\text{ is coprime to }\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\right\},

unless
r=1
,
k=2
, and
m=1
, in which case the group is trivial.

Proof.

We begin with the group
\{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}):\mathcal{C}_{g}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\phi(g)}\text{ as $\mathcal{C}_{e}$-bimodules for all $g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}$}\}
. As the
\mathcal{C}_{e}
bimodules form a group, we have that
\mathcal{C}_{g}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\phi(g)}\text{ as $\mathcal{C}_{e}$-bimodules for all $g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}$}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\phi(g)g^{-1}}
is equivalent to the trivial
\mathcal{C}_{e}
-bimodule, and so only if
\mathcal{C}_{\phi(g)g^{-1}}
contains an invertible object. Recall that the invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
are generated by the object
(k\Lambda_{m^{\prime\prime}},1)
, which lives in the graded component
\mathcal{C}_{\frac{km^{\prime\prime}}{m}}=\mathcal{C}_{\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}}
. Therefore the invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
live in the graded components
\mathcal{C}_{N\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}^{\times}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{cg-g}
contains an invertible object if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{c-1}
does. For this to happen, we need that
c\equiv 1+N\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Using Bezout’s identity, this is equivalent to having
c\equiv 1\pmod{\gcd(\frac{k}{m^{\prime}},\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}})}
. A direct prime by prime computation reveals that
\gcd(\frac{k}{m^{\prime}},\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}})=\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}
, where we recall that
n^{\prime}=\gcd(r+1,k)
. Thus together we have a bound

|\{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}):\mathcal{C}_{g}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\phi(g)}\text{ as $\mathcal{C}_{e}$-bimodules for all $g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}$}\}|\leq|\{c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}^{\times}:c\equiv 1\pmod{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}\}|.

Now we count the group
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}},\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}))\right)
. As a 1-cocycle is determined by its value on the generator, we have that the size of this group is bounded above by the size of
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}))
. As the universal grading group of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is trivial, we can use
[
32
]
to see that every invertible of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
has at most one lift to the centre. Further, as
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is braided each invertible object has a lift to the centre via the braiding. Therefore

\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}))\cong\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}},

and so the size of the group
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}},\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}))\right)
is bounded above by
\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}
.

It is a classical group theory result that
H^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}},\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)
is trivial.

Thus there are at most

\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}\cdot\left|\{c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}^{\times}:c\equiv 1\pmod{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}\}\right|

auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
extending the identity on the
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
subcategory. We now show this bound is sharp by constructing enough distinct auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
to realise the upper bound. We will construct these auto-equivalences as simple current auto-equivalences. For the definition of simple current auto-equivalences we use in this paper, see
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
.

To construct simple current auto-equivalences, we pick out the invertible object
(k\Lambda_{m^{\prime\prime}},1)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. This object has order
\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}
, and has self-braiding eigenvalue equal to
e^{2\pi i\frac{mm^{\prime\prime}q}{2(r+1)}}
where
q=\frac{rk}{m^{\prime}}
. Thus we get simple current auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
for each element of the set

\left\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}:1+a\frac{rk}{m^{\prime}}\text{ is coprime to }\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\right\}.

To see that these simple current auto-equivalences are distinct, note that they form a group. Therefore, we need to show that for each
a\neq 0
, the corresponding simple current auto-equivalence acts non-trivially. Consider
(\Lambda_{m^{\prime}},1)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. Then we have that the simple current auto-equivalence sends

(\Lambda_{m^{\prime}},1)\mapsto(\Lambda_{m^{\prime}},1)\otimes(k\Lambda_{m^{\prime\prime}},1)=((k-1)\Lambda_{m^{\prime\prime}}+\Lambda_{m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}}),1).

To verify that this action in non-trivial, we have to check that
(k-1)\Lambda_{m^{\prime\prime}}+\Lambda_{m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}})
does not live in the orbit of
\Lambda_{m^{\prime}}
under the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. Supposing this was the case, then there would exist a
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(k-1)\Lambda_{m^{\prime\prime}}+\Lambda_{m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}})=(k-1)\Lambda_{t\frac{r+1}{m}}+\Lambda_{t\frac{r+1}{m}+m^{\prime}}.

Assuming
k>2
, we get the equation

t\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\equiv 1\pmod{r+1},

which is nonsense, as
\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}
is clearly not invertible in
\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}
.

If
k=2
, then we get that
2m^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{r+1}
, and thus
m^{\prime}=\frac{r+1}{2}
. As
m^{\prime}\divides k
, we see that either
r=1
and
m^{\prime}=1
, or
r=3
and
m^{\prime}=2
. The latter case is one of the excluded cases. For the former case, it is known that the simple current auto-equivalence acts trivially
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
.

The same argument used in
[
17
, Lemma A.2]
shows that the set of simple current auto-equivalences, and the set

\left\{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}:b\equiv 1\pmod{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}\right\}

have the same size. We give a bijection

\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}\cdot\left\{c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}^{\times}:c\equiv 1\pmod{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}\right\}\to\left\{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}:b\equiv 1\pmod{\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}}\right\}

by sending

(N,c)\mapsto c+N\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}.

As the simple current auto-equivalences are all distinct (except for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{1})}
), and the number of them is equal to the upper bound of auto-equivalences extending the identity on the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
, we therefore have that every auto-equivalences extending the identity on the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is isomorphic to the group of simple current auto-equivalences, which is

\left\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}:1+a\frac{rk}{m^{\prime}}\text{ is coprime to }\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\right\}.

∎

A-priori
there should be no reason that the upper bound on the number of auto-equivalences we construct should be tight. We suspect that something deep going on here that deserves to be investigated.

As a corollary, we can determine which auto-equivalence which extend the identity are braided.

Corollary 3.11
.

The group of braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
extending the identity on the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
is isomorphic to the group

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{p+t},

where

•

p
is the number of distinct odd primes dividing
\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}
but not
\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}
, and

•

t=\begin{cases}0\text{ if $\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}$ is odd, or if $\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}\equiv 0\pmod{4}$, or if both $\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}$ is odd, and $\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}\equiv 2\pmod{4}$}\\
1\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

unless
r=1
,
k=2
, and
m=1
, in which case the group is trivial.

Proof.

We know that a simple current auto-equivalence is braided precisely when
a^{2}\frac{rk}{m^{\prime}}-2a\equiv 0\pmod{2\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}}
. It is then immediate that the group of simple currents for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is isomorphic to the group of simple currents for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}},\frac{k}{m^{\prime}})
(with compositions as in
[
17
, Appendix A]
). The claim then follows from
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
, where we ignore the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{c}
factor of
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{\frac{r+1}{mm^{\prime\prime}}},\frac{k}{m^{\prime}}))
which corresponds to a non simple current auto-equivalence.

∎

Now that we completely understand the braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
which extend the identity on the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
, we can use a torsor argument to fairly easily leverage this information to understand the auto-equivalences extending the charge-conjugation auto-equivalence on the subcategory
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
which fix the distinguished object
\Omega
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.2
, the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

All that remains to be done is to show that there exists a braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
which restricts to give the charge-conjugation auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})}^{\text{ad}}
. This follows from the fact that charge conjugation exists for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
, and it preserves the
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})
subcategory. Therefore it descends to the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
.

∎

4.
Candidates for exceptional auto-equivalences

In the previous section we were able to completely determine all non-exceptional braided auto-equivalences of the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
. That is, we could determine all braided auto-equivalences which fixed the distinguished object
\Omega
. For this section we will focus on determining the braided auto-equivalences which move
\Omega
. This section will be combinatorial in nature, making use of the rich combinatorics of the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. Let us outline the arguments of this section.

Our main tool to determine when the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
has an exceptional auto-equivalence will be the following Lemma, which gives very restrictive necessary conditions.

Lemma 4.1
.

The category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
has a braided exceptional auto-equivalence only if there exists a object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
such that

•

X\nin[\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}]
, and

•

the orbit of
X
under the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is closed under charge-conjugation,

•

we have

[r]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}=\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)|}\geq\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)|},

and

•

the twist of
X
is equal to the twist of
\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
has a braided exceptional auto-equivalence, then by definition there is an object
(X,\chi_{X})\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
such that
\Omega
is mapped to
(X,\chi_{X})
under the exceptional auto-equivalence, and
(X,\chi_{X})
is not in the orbit of
\Omega
under simple currents.

As
\Omega
is self-dual, we have that
(X,\chi_{X})
is self-dual, and hence the orbit of
X
under
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is closed under conjugation.

To obtain the dimension bound for
X
, we note that

\dim(\Omega)=\dim((X,\chi_{X})).

From this we can obtain the inequality

\dim(\Omega)=\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)|}\geq\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)|}.

The dimension of the object
\Omega
is
[r]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}
, hence we have the result.

To get the condition on the twist of
X
, note that a braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
will preserve twists by
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
. The twist of an object
(X,\chi_{X})\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is equal to the twist of
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. The condition is then immediate.
∎

The key restriction here is the existence of an object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
with

[r]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}\geq\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)|}.

If
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)
is non-trivial, then we can use Lemma
2.10
to bound the dimension of
X
below by the dimension of a simpler object in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
, say for example
2\Lambda_{2}
. We can then use the hook formula to write the dimension of this simpler object as a product of quantum integers. For our
2\Lambda_{2}
example we would have the dimension is
\frac{[r]_{r,k}[r+1]_{r,k}^{2}[r+2]_{r,k}}{[3]_{r,k}[2]^{2}_{r,k}}
. This then gives us an inequality of quantum integers that must be obeyed for there to exist an exceptional auto-equivalence. By suitably bounding this inequality we can then obtain strong restrictions on the rank and level of the category. With this approach we are able to show that there are only a finite number of cases where the inequality may hold. From here we can then directly search for
X
where the condition

[r]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}=\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)|}

holds. This yields a very small number of candidates for exceptional auto-equivalences.

When
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)
is trivial, we search for objects
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
which satisfy

[r]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}=\dim(X).

Here there are many candidates for
X
. In particular, any object in
[\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}]
will satisfy this condition. However, when paired with the condition that
X\nin[\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}]
, we can again reduce the list of candidates down to a finite list via similar techniques as before. This case is a bit more fiddly than the case with non-trivial stabaliser group, as now we have to carefully avoid the objects in the orbit of
\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}
, however the technical details remain the same.

In order to suitably bound the inequalities of quantum integers, we have to assume that
k\geq r+1
in order to apply Lemma
2.6
. To deal with the
k<r+1
cases, we use level-rank duality to reduce it to the
k\geq r+1
case.

All together we can give a complete list of objects
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
such that
X\nin[\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}]
and such that

[r]_{r,k}[r+2]_{r,k}=\frac{\dim(X)}{|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}(X)|}.

From this finite list we then search for objects which satisfy the remaining conditions of Lemma
4.1
to obtain an even smaller list.

Finally, we computer search the fusion rings of these remaining candidates, looking for fusion ring automorphisms which preserve the twists of the simples. This yeilds the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
and
m
a divisor of
r+1
satisfying
m^{2}\divides k(r+1)
if
r
is even, and
2m^{2}\divides k(r+1)
if
r
is odd. Then except for the cases

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})},\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})},\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})},\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{8},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})},\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{9},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})},\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}

every braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
is non-exceptional.

For the first four cases, we have that there are two possibilities for the group of braided auto-equivalences:

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})

\displaystyle\in\{\mathbb{Z}_{2},S_{3}\},\qquad

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})\in\{D_{3},S_{4}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})

\displaystyle\in\{D_{4},S_{4}\},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})\in\{D_{5},A_{5}\}.

For the remaining four cases, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{8},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{9},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}).

With the high-level arguments and end goal in mind. Let us proceed with the fine details of the arguments. Let
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
be a category with an exceptional braided auto-equivalence. Then by Lemma
4.1
we get an object
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
satisfying the conditions of the lemma. Our goal is show that
r
,
k
, and
m
are severely constrained. We will have to split into several cases, depending on the stabaliser group of
X\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
, and on the size of
k
compared to
r+1
.

Case:
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)=r+1

Let us first deal with the case where
X
has full stabaliser subgroup, i.e.
\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)=\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}
. As
|\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathbb{Z}_{r+1}}(X)|
divides
k
, we necessarily have
k\geq r+1
in this case. From Lemma
2.10
we can deduce that

\dim(X)\geq\dim(2\Lambda_{2}),\quad\dim(X)\geq\dim(6\Lambda_{1}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\dim(X)\geq\dim(3\Lambda_{3}).

These inequalities hold when
k\geq 2
,
k\geq 6
, and
k\geq 3
respectively. Recalling

There are many interpretations of level-rank duality (see

Taking the transpose of this tableaux gives a

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{k},r+1)
are identified by

where

\hat{\lambda_{\ell}}:=\left|1\leq j\leq r:\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\lambda_{i}=k-\ell\right|.

Using the hook formula, along with the fact that

we see that

\dim(X)=\dim\left(X^{T}\right).

Lemma 4.6

We will first show that

Suppose that

Suppose

for all

Suppose we have
0\leq j\leq r
contributes to

and

(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{0})\mapsto(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{1})\mapsto(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{2}),

and

\Omega\mapsto(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{0})\mapsto(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{1})\mapsto(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{2}).

From Theorem
3.2
we know that the first two generators are realised as braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
, and form a group isomorphic to
D_{3}
. Further, this theorem tells us that any braided auto-equivalence which fixes
\Omega
must be in the subgroup generated by the first two generators. Thus
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})
is an intermediate subgroup of
D_{3}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times S_{4}
with the property that if
\Omega
is fixed by an auto-equivalence, then this auto-equivalence lives in the
D_{3}
subgroup. With this knowledge, we can study the intermediate subgroups of
D_{3}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times S_{4}
to see that there are only two such subgroups with this property. These are
D_{3}
with the first two generators, and
S_{4}
with the first two generators, and the new generator

\Omega\leftrightarrow(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{0})\quad,\quad(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{1})\leftrightarrow(3\Lambda_{1}+3\Lambda_{2},\chi_{2}).

Thus
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})
is isomorphic to either
D_{3}
or
S_{4}
.

The remaining two cases fall to the same argument. For
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
the group of braided fusion ring symmetries is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times S_{4}
. We have that the generators

(4\Lambda_{2},\chi_{0})\leftrightarrow(4\Lambda_{2},\chi_{1})\quad,\quad(2\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},\chi_{i})\mapsto(2\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},\chi_{i+1})

and

(2\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2},1)\leftrightarrow(\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},1)\quad,\quad(2\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},\chi_{1})\leftrightarrow(2\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},\chi_{3})

form a
D_{4}
subgroup of
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})
, and that any braided auto-equivalence which fixes
\Omega
must be in this subgroup. Analysing the subgroup structure between
D_{4}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times S_{4}
shows that at most there can be one more generator

\Omega\leftrightarrow(4\Lambda_{2},\chi_{0})\quad,\quad(2\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2},1)\mapsto(2\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},\chi_{0})\mapsto(\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},1)\mapsto(2\Lambda_{1}+2\Lambda_{2}+2\Lambda_{3},\chi_{2}),

in the braided auto-equivalence group, which would form a group isomorphic to
S_{4}
. Thus
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})
is either isomorphic to
D_{4}
or
S_{4}
.

Finally for the case
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
we have that the group of braided fusion symmetries is
S_{6}
. We have that the generators

(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{i})\mapsto(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{i+1})

and

(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{i})\mapsto(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{-i})

form a
D_{5}
subgroup of
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})
, and that any braided auto-equivalence which fixes
\Omega
must be in this subgroup. Analysing the subgroup structure between
D_{5}
and
S_{6}
shows that at most there can be one more generator

\displaystyle\Omega\mapsto(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{1})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{0}),

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{2})\mapsto(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{3})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},\chi_{4})

in the braided auto-equivalence group, which would form a group isomorphic to
A_{5}
. Thus
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})
is either isomorphic to
D_{5}
or
A_{5}
.
∎

To finish off the proof of the main theorem of this section, we need to deal with the remaining four cases. These can be dealt with easily by a level-rank duality argument.

Proposition 4.9
.

We have the following braided equivalences:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}

\displaystyle\simeq(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})^{\text{rev}}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,e^{2\pi i\frac{3}{4}}\}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}

\displaystyle\simeq(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})^{\text{rev}}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{8},\{1,e^{2\pi i\frac{15}{16}},e^{2\pi i\frac{3}{4}},e^{2\pi i\frac{7}{16}},1,e^{2\pi i\frac{7}{16}},e^{2\pi i\frac{3}{4}},e^{2\pi i\frac{15}{16}}\}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{9},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}

\displaystyle\simeq(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})^{\text{rev}}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{1,e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{3}},e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{3}}\}\right),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{8},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}

\displaystyle\simeq(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})^{\text{rev}}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,e^{2\pi i\frac{3}{4}}\}\right).

Proof.

Let us do the computation for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
, as the other cases follow in a similar fashion.

From Lemma
2.20
we know that
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
has a subcategory braided equivalent to
(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{\text{ad}})_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
. Via level-rank duality
[
53
]
, we have a braided equivalence

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{\text{ad}}\to(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{\text{ad}})^{\text{rev}}.

Thus together we have a braided subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
equivalent to
((\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{\text{ad}})^{\text{rev}})_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
. Taking the reverse braiding on the category commutes with taking the de-equivariantization (as
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{\text{rev}}=\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
), thus we have a subcategory braided equivalent to
(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})^{\text{rev}}
. As
(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})^{\text{rev}}
is modular, Mugers theorem
[
48
, Theorem 4.2 ]
give us that the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
factors as

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}\simeq(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})^{\text{rev}}\boxtimes\mathcal{D},

where
\mathcal{D}
is a modular category with global dimension 2. To identify
\mathcal{D}
we study the object
([2\Lambda_{2}],1)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
. The twist of this object is
e^{2\pi i\frac{3}{4}}
, and the order of the object is
2
. Thus
([2\Lambda_{2}],1)
generates a modular subcategory equivalent to
\operatorname{Vec}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{2},e^{2\pi i\frac{3}{4}}\right)
. As the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
has no non-trivial invertible objects, we must have that
([2\Lambda_{2}],1)
generates
\mathcal{D}
, which completes the claim.

∎

Corollary 4.10
.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{16},2)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{9},3)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{8},4)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}).

Proof.

We use the canonical embedding

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C})\times\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{D})\to\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}).

By analysing the fusion rings and twists of the the Deligne products from the previous proposition, we see that this embedding is an isomorphism.
∎

5.
Realisation of the exceptionals

In the previous section we identified a finite list of the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
which may have an exceptional braided auto-equivalence, and furthermore, gave upper bounds for the number of such auto-equivalences that may exist. In this section, our goal is to construct all the exceptional braided auto-equivalences of these finite number of categories. That is, we want to compute the braided auto-equivalence groups of the categories

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})},\qquad\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})},\qquad\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}.

While it is not immediate from the above list, there are two situations in play here. The first is for the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
, where the exceptional auto-equivalences come from the coincidence of categories

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},3)\simeq(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})})^{\text{rev}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},1).

The
S_{3}
worth of braided exceptional auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
is then naturally seen due to the triality of the Dynkin diagram
D_{4}
. This connection was initially discovered in
[
46
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
46
, Theorem 4.3]

We have

\operatorname{EqBr}\left(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}\right)=S_{3}.

The far more interesting situation occurs with the other three categories. Studying the dimensions of these three examples, one notices that they are all defined over small quadratic fields. For
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
the dimensions live in
\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{3}]
, for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
the dimensions live in
\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{3}]
, and for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
the dimensions live in
\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{5}]
. Such behaviour with the dimensions does not hold for general
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})}
, and suggests that these dimensions may have something to do with the potential exceptional auto-equivalences of these categories.

A large class of categories with objects living in quadratic fields are the quadratic categories, where the simple objects consist of the group of invertibles, and an object
\rho
, along with the orbit of
\rho
under the action of the invertibles. The natural suspicion to draw, is that the three categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
should in some way be connected to quadratic categories. The naive guess, that these three categories are quadratic categories on the nose, is immediately thwarted by fact that the dimensions in these examples take on more than two values. Further, quadratic categories are almost never modular, where as our three examples are. However, this lack of modularity suggests the next place to look for a connection.

Taking the Drinfeld centre of a quadratic category gives a modular category whose dimensions lie in the same field as the quadratic category. While in the quadratic category, the dimensions of the simples can only have two possible values, the dimensions of the simples in the centre can be any integer combination of
1
and the dimension of the non-invertible of the quadratic. This provides strong evidence that the three categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
may be related to Drinfeld centres of quadratic categories.

Now that we have an idea of what to look for, we can make educated guesses as to the identity of the quadratic categories. For example, the dimensions of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
are

1,\quad 7+4\sqrt{3},\quad 8+4\sqrt{3},\quad\text{and}\quad 3+2\sqrt{3}\quad\text{(6 times)}.

If
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
were the Drinfeld centre of a quadratic category, then a natural guess for the dimension of the non-invertible would be
3+2\sqrt{3}
, as all the above dimensions can be constructed as integer combinations of
1
and
3+2\sqrt{3}
. There is a known quadratic category with an object of this dimension, which is a near-group category with group of invertibles
\mathbb{Z}_{3}=\{\mathbf{1},g,g^{2}\}
, and a single non-invertible with fusion

\rho\otimes\rho\cong\mathbf{1}\oplus g\oplus g^{2}\oplus 6\rho.

The global dimension of this category is
24+12\sqrt{3}
, so the global dimension of its Drinfeld centre is
1008+576\sqrt{3}
. Where as the global dimension of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
is
336+192\sqrt{3}
. These global dimensions are off by a factor of three, which suggests a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
factor is involved. From all this we conjecture that there is a quadratic category
\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3}
with fusion as above such that

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3})\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{1,e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}},e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}}\}).

Using similar reasoning we conjecture the existence of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{4,8,4}
with invertibles
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\mathbf{1},e,m,em\}
and non-invertibles
\{\rho,m\rho\}
with fusion

\rho\otimes\rho\cong\mathbf{1}\oplus e\oplus 6\rho\oplus 4m\rho,

such that

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{4,8,4})\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,e^{2i\pi\frac{3}{4}},-1,e^{2i\pi\frac{3}{4}}\}),

and a fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{5,5,5}
with invertibles
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\mathbf{1},e,m,em\}
and non-invertibles
\{\rho,e\rho,m\rho,em\rho\}
with fusion

\rho\otimes\rho\cong\mathbf{1}\oplus\rho\oplus e\rho\oplus m\rho\oplus em\rho,

such that

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{5,5,5})\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,-1,-1,-1\}).

We make all this precise with the following Theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exist unitary fusion categories
\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3}
,
\mathcal{C}_{4,8,3}
, and
\mathcal{C}_{5,5,5}
with combinatorics as above, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{1,e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}},e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}}\})

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{4,8,4})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,e^{2i\pi\frac{3}{4}},-1,e^{2i\pi\frac{3}{4}}\}),\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{5,5,5})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,-1,-1,-1\}).

Proof.

Let us begin with the case
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
. It is well known that there is a conformal inclusion
SU(3)_{9}\subset(E_{6})_{1}
which induces a commutative algebra object
A\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)
. The category
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9),A)
is then a unitary fusion category, as described in
[
7
, Figure 4]
. The category
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9),A)
is
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-graded. Let
\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3}
be the trivially graded subcategory of this grading.

By
[
2
, Corollary 4.8]
we have that

\mathcal{Z}(\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9),A))\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)\boxtimes(\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9),A)^{0})^{\text{rev}}.

For this particular case, we have that
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9),A)^{0}\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{e}_{6},1)\simeq\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{1,e^{2i\pi\frac{2}{3}},e^{2i\pi\frac{2}{3}}\})
.

Now the results of
[
30
]
allow us to compute the Drinfeld centre of the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3}
in terms of the Drinfeld centre of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9),A)
. This gives that

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3})\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{1,e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}},e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}}\})

as desired.

The case of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
is almost identical, except now we use the conformal inclusion
SU(4)_{8}\subset Spin(20)_{1}
. The structure of the associated algebra object, and category of modules, can be found in
[
9
, Section 2.6]
.

The case of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
should follow in the same manner, where now we work with the conformal inclusion
SU(5)_{5}\subset Spin(24)_{1}
. However, the author was unable to find a suitable description of the category of modules in this case. Instead we have
[
61
, Theorem 3.2]
which proves the precise statement in this case. They show that
\mathcal{C}_{5,5,5}
is the even part of the
3^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
subfactor.
∎

With these alternate identifications of the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
, and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
identified, we now have the tools to construct their exceptional braided auto-equivalences, and hence determine their braided auto-equivalence groups. We are able to complete this for the cases
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}
in this paper. Let us begin with
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})=S_{4}

Proof.

From Theorem
4.2
we have that
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})
is either
D_{3}
or
S_{4}

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3})\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{1,e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}},e^{2i\pi\frac{1}{3}}\})

we see that

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3}))=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

It is proven in
[
36

that
\operatorname{Out}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3})=D_{4}
. From
[
44
]
we have an embedding

\operatorname{Out}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3})\to\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{3,9,3})).

Thus
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{3})})=S_{4}

∎

We now deal with the case of

,

Lemma 5.4
.

We have

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})=A_{5}.

From Theorem
4.2
we have that
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})
is either
D_{5}
or

. By analysing the fusion rings and twists of

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{5,5,5})\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})}\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{1,-1,-1,-1\}).

we see that

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{5,5,5}))=\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})\times S_{3}.

It is proven in

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})\times S_{3}

A_{4}
. This is only possible if
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5},5)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})})=A_{5}

∎

Unfortunately we are unable to use the quadratic category
\mathcal{C}_{4,8,4}
to construct the exceptional braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
. This is because an explicit construction of the quadratic category

Instead we construct the exceptional braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
using a coincidence of categories to connect it to
\mathfrak{so}_{8}
, similar to the
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},16)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
case. Recall that
\mathfrak{sl}_{4}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}_{6}
. Hence the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)
can be viewed as
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)
. Now level-rank duality will give a (non-trivial) connection between
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)
and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)
. Via this connection we can pull back the triality of
\mathfrak{so}_{8}
in order to obtain an order 3 auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
, which forces
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})=S_{4}
.

Let us expand more on this connection between
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)
and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)
. These categories have different ranks, so even as abelian categories they are not equivalent. Thus there are several steps we must take to get some sort of equivalence. First let
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
generated by the “vector representation”
\Lambda_{1}
. To get “orthogonal categories”, where level-rank duality applies we have to ‘’add in” the determinant representation by taking the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariantisation via the
D_{n}
Dynkin diagram symmetry. This gives us the braided equivalence

\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\simeq[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]^{-\text{rev}}.

Where
-\text{rev}
means to take the reverse the braiding, and to negate it on the non-trivial piece of the grading. However this equivalence doesn’t preserve the determinant representations, so we can’t de-equivariantise by a single
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
subcategory to obtain a braided equivalence. Instead we must de-equivariantise by the maximal Tannakian subcategory. This gives us a braided equivalence

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}=\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)_{\langle 8\Lambda_{1}\rangle}\simeq[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)_{\langle 6\Lambda_{1}\rangle}]^{-\text{rev}}=[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{-\text{rev}}.

However triality doesn’t preserve the
\langle 8\Lambda_{1}\rangle
subcategory of
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)
, so it won’t descend to
[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{-\text{rev}}
. Thus we have to take local modules with respect to the remaining
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
subcategory to get

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}\simeq[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{\text{rev}}.

Triality now preserves the
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)
and hence descends to give us an order 3 auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}=\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
.

To make this all precise we begin the following lemma, which formalises type
D
-
D
level-rank duality.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
N
and
k
be even integers. We have a braided equivalence

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{0}_{\langle k\Lambda_{1}\rangle}\simeq[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{0}_{\langle N\Lambda_{1}\rangle}]^{-\text{rev}}

Proof.

Let

X_{N,k}:=(\Lambda_{1},+)\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}},

and

X_{k,N}:=(\Lambda_{1},+)\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}.

From
[
58
]
we have that

\mathcal{P}_{X_{N,k}}=\overline{\text{BMW}\left(e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{2(N+k-2)}},e^{2\pi i\frac{N-1}{2(N+k-2)}}\right)},

and

\mathcal{P}_{X_{k,N}}=\overline{\text{BMW}\left(e^{2\pi i\frac{1}{2(N+k-2)}},e^{2\pi i\frac{k-1}{2(N+k-2)}}\right)},

where
\overline{\text{BMW}(q,r)}
is the semi-simplified planar algebra generated by a single crossing and the Kauffman relation (see
[
58
, Section 7]

[
17
, Definition 2.12]
). It is routine to verify that

\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{BMWpositivecrossing}}\mapsto-\raisebox{-0.5pt}{ \includegraphics[scale]{BMWnegitivecrossing}}

gives an isomorphism of planar algebras

\mathcal{P}_{X_{N,k}}\to\mathcal{P}_{X_{k,N}}.

Via
[
33
, Theorem A]
this induces a braided equivalence

\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\to[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]^{-\text{rev}}.

Lets define the two groups

G_{N,k}:=\operatorname{Inv}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\right)=\{(\mathbf{1},+),\quad(\mathbf{1},-),\quad(k\Lambda_{1},+),\quad\text{and}\quad(k\Lambda_{1},-)\},

and

G_{k,N}:=\operatorname{Inv}\left([\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]^{-\text{rev}}\right)=\{(\mathbf{1},+),\quad(\mathbf{1},-),\quad(N\Lambda_{1},+),\quad\text{and}\quad(N\Lambda_{1},-)\}.

The above braided equivalence will preserve the group of invertibles, so we have that it maps
G_{N,k}
to
G_{k,N}
.

As
k\Lambda_{1}
and
N\Lambda_{1}
are symmetric objects in their respective categories and have trivial twist (these facts requires both
N
and
k
even), we get that

\operatorname{Rep}(G_{N,k})\subseteq Z_{2}(\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}),

and

\operatorname{Rep}(G_{k,N})\subseteq Z_{2}([\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]^{-\text{rev}}).

Thus we can de-equivariantise to obtain a braided isomorphism

\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(G_{N,k})}\to[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]_{\operatorname{Rep}(G_{k,N})}^{-\text{rev}}.

From
[
12
, Theorem 4.9]
we have that
\langle(\mathbf{1},-)\rangle
generates a copy of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
(resp.
[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]^{-\text{rev}}
), and that de-equivaraiantising
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
(resp.
[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]^{-\text{rev}}
) by this copy of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
recovers
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
(resp.
[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)]^{-\text{rev}})
up to braided equivalence. As
\langle(\mathbf{1},-)\rangle
is normal in
G_{N,k}
and
G_{k,N}
respectively we can use the above fact, along with
[
47
, Proposition 4.12]
to get braided equivalences

\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\operatorname{Rep}(G_{N,k})}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)_{\langle k\Lambda_{1}\rangle},

and

[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}]_{\operatorname{Rep}(G_{k,N})}^{-\text{rev}}\simeq[\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)]_{\langle N\Lambda_{1}\rangle}^{-\text{rev}}.

As
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
is modular, and
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
is the adjoint subcategory with respect to a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
, we have that
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)=Z_{2}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k),H)
for some

(this is a consequence of the isomorphism
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{C})

[

). From the modular data of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
we can see that

is symmetric in
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
, and the invertibles
k\Lambda_{N}
and
k\Lambda_{N-1}
do not centralise
\Lambda_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)
. Thus
H=\langle k\Lambda_{1}\rangle
, and so
\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)\simeq Z_{2}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k),\langle k\Lambda_{1}\rangle)
. This implies that

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)^{0}_{\langle k\Lambda_{1}\rangle}=\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{N},k)_{\langle k\Lambda_{1}\rangle}.

The same argument works to show that

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)^{0}_{\langle N\Lambda_{1}\rangle}=\mathcal{C}^{\text{Vec}}(\mathfrak{so}_{k},N)_{\langle N\Lambda_{1}\rangle},

which completes the proof.
∎

As a corollary we get our desired exceptional braided auto-equivalence.

Corollary 5.6
.

We have

\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})=S_{4}.

Proof.

Via a coincidence of Dynkin diagrams we have

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)\simeq\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{6},8).

Thus the above Lemma gives a braided equivalence

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}\simeq[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{-\text{rev}}.

By taking local-modules with respect to the remaining
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
subcategory we obtain

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}\simeq[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{-\text{rev}}=[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{\text{rev}}.

The triality of the
D_{4}
Dynkin diagram induces an order 3 braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{\text{- rev}}
by
[
17
, Corollary 2.7]
. This auto-equivalence preserves the
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
subcategory, and thus descends to
[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{\text{rev}}
. To see that this braided auto-equivalence is non-trivial, we observe that triality will send

2\Lambda_{1}\to 2\Lambda_{3}

in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{\text{- rev}}
, which implies that the induced braided auto-equivalence will map

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(2\Lambda_{1})\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(2\Lambda_{3})

in
[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{\text{rev}}
. However the two objects
2\Lambda_{1}
and
2\Lambda_{3}
are not in the same orbit under the simple currents of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{\text{-rev}}
which implies

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(2\Lambda_{1})\ncong\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(2\Lambda_{3}).

Thus the induced braided auto-equivalence of
[\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{so}_{8},6)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})}]^{\text{rev}}
is non-trivial.

Recall from Theorem
4.2
that
\operatorname{EqBr}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})})
is either
D_{4}
or
S_{4}
. As we know there exists an order 3 braided auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4},8)^{0}_{\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})}
, we can only have the latter option.
∎

Appendix A
Coincidences of Small Dimensions

By Terry Gannon

In this appendix, we prove the following result regarding coincidences of dimensions in the categories
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
X
be a simple object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
such that
\dim(X)=\dim(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r})
. Then either
X\in[\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{r}]
, or

\displaystyle(r,k)=(8,3),(8,15)\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad X\in[\Lambda_{3}],

or

\displaystyle(r,k)=(2,9),(14,9)\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad X\in[3\Lambda_{1}],

or

\displaystyle(r,k)=(3,6),(5,4)\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad X\in[2\Lambda_{2}],

or

\displaystyle(r,k)=(7,4),(7,6)\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad X\in[\Lambda_{4}],

or

\displaystyle(r,k)=(3,8),(5,8)\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad X\in[4\Lambda_{1}].

The main technical tool we use to prove this result is the following lemma, which allows us to shuffle around the Dynkin labels of a simple object
X
to decrease its dimension.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i}
be a simple object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
. For any two indices
0\leq j,l\leq r
, and integers
0\leq c_{j}\leq\lambda_{j}
and
0\leq c_{l}\leq\lambda_{l}
we have that

\dim(X)\geq\min(\dim(X-c_{j}\Lambda_{j}+c_{j}\Lambda_{l}),\dim(X+c_{l}\Lambda_{j}-c_{l}\Lambda_{l})),

with equality if and only if
c_{j}=0
or
c_{l}=0
.

Proof.

Note that we can write

X=\frac{c_{l}}{c_{j}+c_{l}}(X-c_{j}\Lambda_{j}+c_{j}\Lambda_{l})+\frac{c_{j}}{c_{j}+c_{l}}(X+c_{l}\Lambda_{j}-c_{l}\Lambda_{l}).

Thus the result follows immediately from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

With this tool we can now prove Proposition
A.1
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a simple object of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)

Now suppose

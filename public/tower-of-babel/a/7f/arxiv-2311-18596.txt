Positive eigenvectors and simple nonlinear maps
Marta Calanchi, Carlos Tomei

Abstract
For linear operators L , T \displaystyle L,T and nonlinear maps P \displaystyle P , we describe classes of simple maps F = I − P ​ T \displaystyle F=I-PT , F = L − P \displaystyle F=L-P between Banach and Hilbert spaces, for which no point has more than two preimages. The classes encompass known examples (homeomorphisms, global folds) and the weaker, geometric, hypotheses suggest new ones. The operator L \displaystyle L may be the Laplacian with various boundary conditions, as in the original Ambrosetti-Prodi theorem, or the operators associated with the quantum harmonic oscillator, the hydrogen atom, a spectral fractional Laplacian, elliptic operators in non-divergent form. The maps P \displaystyle P include the Nemitskii map P ⁡ ( u ) = f ⁡ ( u ) \displaystyle P(u)=f(u) but may be non-local, even non-variational. For self-adjoint operators L \displaystyle L , we employ familiar results on the nondegeneracy of the ground state. On Banach spaces, we use a variation of the Krein-Rutman theorem.

Ambrosetti-Prodi theorem, folds, Krein-Rutman theorem, positivity preserving semigroups.

Both results are inspirational, in the specification of hypotheses and in the techniques of proof. Our results are more geometric and more general. We take a different point of view: simple maps are obtained from nonlinear perturbationsof special linear operators. As for the theorems above, the positivity of an eigenvector of the Jacobian, or some appropriate linearization, is an essential ingredient. We use the preservation of positive eigenfunctions associated with the spectral radius (Krein-Rutman type arguments, as in Section 2), or with the smallest eigenvalue of a self-adjoint map (the perturbation theory of nondegenerate groundstates, Section 3). On a global scale, the asymptotic linearity of the nonlinear terms yields a certain uniformity of the linearizations, which belong to a set with privileged compactness properties.

.

(v)

There is an eigenvector
\displaystyle\phi^{\ast}\in K^{\ast}
of
\displaystyle S^{\ast}
associated with
\displaystyle r(S)
,
\displaystyle\phi^{\ast}>0
.

(vi)

If
\displaystyle\mu>r(S)
, then
\displaystyle(\mu I-S)^{-1}\in{{\cal B}(Y)}
and
\displaystyle(\mu I-S)^{-1}K\subset K
.

We prove Theorem
4
from this theorem.
In Section
2.1
,
\displaystyle Y
is a real Banach space,
\displaystyle K\subset Y
is a normal, reproducing cone.
A reproducing cone is unflattened (from the Krein-Smulian theorem,
[
39
]
) and unflattened cones are B-cones (
[
33
]
). Also,
\displaystyle S\in{{\cal B}(Y)}
is compact and ergodic with
\displaystyle r(S)>0
. Ergodicity implies that
\displaystyle S
is positive, nonsupporting. From the Krein-Rutman theorem,
\displaystyle r(S)>0
and the spectral theory of compact operators implies that
\displaystyle r(S)
is a pole of the resolvent map. Hence, the hypotheses of Theorem
4
imply those of Theorem
10
.

We show that
\displaystyle r
is a basic eigenvalue of
\displaystyle S
.
From (1) and (5),
\displaystyle S
and
\displaystyle S^{\ast}
have eigenvectors
\displaystyle\phi_{m}>0
and
\displaystyle\phi_{m}^{\ast}>0
associated with
\displaystyle r(S)
: this is property (b-1).
By the compactness of
\displaystyle S
and
\displaystyle S^{\ast}
, the invariant subspaces
\displaystyle V_{r}
and
\displaystyle V^{\ast}
associated to
\displaystyle r(S)
are finite dimensional and it is easy to see that
\displaystyle\dim V=\dim V^{\ast}
. The Jordan theorem for matrices applied to the restriction of
\displaystyle S
in
\displaystyle V
implies that the resolvent map
\displaystyle\lambda\to(S-\lambda I)^{-1}
has a pole at
\displaystyle r(S)
of multiplicity given by the dimension of
\displaystyle V
. By (2), then,
\displaystyle V
is one dimensional. This proves properties (b-2) and (b-3):
\displaystyle r(S)
is a basic eigenvalue of
\displaystyle S
. The remaining claims of Theorem
4
are explicit consequences of Theorem
10
.

Interface fluctuations for 1 1 D stochastic Allen-Cahn equation revisited
Weijun Xu 1 Wenhao Zhao 2 Shuhan Zhou 3

Abstract
We revisit the interface fluctuation problem for the 1 1 D Allen-Cahn equation perturbed by a small space-time white noise. We show that if the initial data is a standing wave solution to the deterministic equation, then under proper long time scale, the solution is still close to the family of traveling wave solutions. Furthermore, the motion of the interface converges to an explicit stochastic differential equation. This extends the classical result in [ Fun95 ] to full small noise regime, and recovers the result in [ BBDMP98 ] . The proof builds on the analytic framework in [ Fun95 ] . Our main novelty is the construction of a series of functional correctors that are designed to recursively cancel potential divergences. Moreover, to show these correctors are well-behaved, we develop a systematic decomposition of Fréchet derivatives of the deterministic Allen-Cahn flow of all orders. This decomposition is of its own interest, and may be useful in other situations as well.

Introduction

In this article, we study the long-time interface behavior of the following
1
D stochastic Allen-Cahn equation:

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon^{\gamma}a_{\varepsilon}\dot{W}\;.

(1.1)

Here,
\gamma>0
is a fixed parameter,
a_{\varepsilon}=a(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\cdot)
for some smooth function

with compact support in
(-1,1)
,
\dot{W}
is a one-dimensional space-time white noise (time derivative of the
\mathcal{L}^{2}
cylindrical Wiener process

), and
f
satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption 1.1
.

The function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is smooth with all derivatives bounded (including
f
itself), and satisfies the followings:

1.

f
has exactly three zeros:
\pm 1
and
0
, and
f^{\prime}(\pm 1)<0
,
f^{\prime}(0)>0
.

2.

f
is odd, that is,
-f(u)=f(-u)

The assumption that
f
has bounded derivatives is mainly for technical simplicity. It can be relaxed to requiring that
f
has polynomial growth and its first derivative is bounded from above (see
[
Fun95
, Theorem 2.1]
for details). The choice of the cutoff on the interval of length
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{\varepsilon})
is to be consistent with the set up in
[
Fun95
]
. Indeed, by a change of parameter, one can make the cutoff to be
a\big(\varepsilon^{\theta}\cdot\big)
for every
\theta>0
.

The Allen-Cahn equation is a popular model to study dynamical phase separation. The deterministic part of (
1.1
) has been well understood. Let
m:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[-1,1]
be the unique increasing solution of the steady-state problem

\Delta m+f(m)=0\;,\quad m(\pm\infty)=\pm 1\;,\quad m(0)=0\;.

(1.2)

For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, write
m_{\theta}:=m(\cdot-\theta)
. The solution space for (
1.2
) without the restriction
m(0)=0
is the one dimensional manifold

\mathcal{M}:=\{m_{\theta}=m(\cdot-\theta)\,:\;\;\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}\;.

If there is no noise in the equation, solutions starting close enough to
\mathcal{M}
will converge to
\mathcal{M}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty

\mathcal{M}
do not evolve with time and stay there forever.
[
CP89
,
FH89
]
studied the deterministic part of (
1.1

[-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}]

m_{\theta}
on the interval
[-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}]
for some
\theta

e^{-c/\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
for some
c>0

The situation is different with the effect of noise. Even though one starts with
u_{\varepsilon}[0]\in\mathcal{M}
for (
1.1

\mathcal{M}
. One expects that at proper long time (polynomial in
\varepsilon
this time), the solution is still close to
\mathcal{M}
, but the location of the interface moves according to an approximate diffusion process.

To investigate the right time scale to observe the above behavior, we note that due to the factor
\varepsilon^{\gamma}
, it takes
\varepsilon^{-2\gamma}
time for the effect of noise to accumulate to size
\mathcal{O}(1)
.
[
BDMP95
]
showed that there exists a process
\theta_{t}^{\varepsilon}
converging to Brownian motion as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
such that
u[\varepsilon^{-2\gamma}t]
is close to
m_{\theta_{t}^{\varepsilon}}
. On the other hand, since noise exists on the interval
[-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}]

\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{\varepsilon})

[
Fun95
]

\theta_{t}^{\varepsilon}
converging in law to a limiting diffusion such that
u[\varepsilon^{-2\gamma-1}t]
is close to
m_{\theta_{t}^{\varepsilon}/\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
. There was a restriction
\gamma>5
in
[
Fun95
]
due to some potentially divergent terms arising from the derivation of the SDE, and one assumes the noise to be sufficiently small to balance them out.

Later,
[
BBDMP98
]

\varepsilon^{-2\gamma-1}

\gamma>0

\gamma

[
Fun95
]

In this article, we show that the methods in
[
Fun95

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem

We now briefly discuss our strategy to prove Theorem
1.2
. Since
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha^{*},p^{*}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
if
\alpha^{*}p^{*}>1
, by choosing
\kappa^{\prime}<\kappa
, Theorem
1.3
immediately implies the existence of a process
\xi_{t}^{\varepsilon}
such that (
1.3
) holds. However, proving convergence of the interface process to the limiting diffusion is much more challenging. In order to emphasize our main new inputs, we first assume Theorem
1.3

1.4
).

As mentioned above, the main challenge at the time scale
\varepsilon^{-2\gamma-1}
is that there are potentially divergent terms with negative powers of
\varepsilon
in the derivation of the limiting equation. They arise from the quadratic variation process when applying Itô’s formula. Our main idea to deal with them is to introduce a series of functional correctors, which are constructed to cancel out the potential divergences inductively. The idea is similar in spirit to homogenization, but with significant practical differences. The construction of the correctors here involves solving infinite-dimensional PDEs (for unknown
\Psi:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
) of the type

\langle D\Psi(v),\mathcal{N}(v)\rangle=\mathcal{H}(v)\;,

where
\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a functional on Banach space
\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{N})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is an (unbounded and nonlinear) operator on
\mathcal{B}
with certain nice properties. This is carried out in Section
2
. Similar ideas have been used in
[
Hai09
,
Energy_dissipation_slow
]
to construct Lyapunov function for finite-dimensional Hamiltonian systems with noise, and also in
[
Lorenz_noise
]
to “correct” the choice of Lyapunov function for a stochastically perturbed Lorenz system. One difference in our infinite-dimensional situation is that showing the well-posedness of the“correctors” is much more sophisticated. This is the main content of Sections
and
, where we systematically analyze the Fréchet derivatives of all orders of the deterministic solution flow. We hope the techniques developed here may play a role in the study of the long-time behavior of other stochastic PDEs (especially singular ones).

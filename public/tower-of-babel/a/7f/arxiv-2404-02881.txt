On computing approximate Lewis weights
Simon Apers, Sander Gribling, Aaron Sidford

Abstract
In this note we provide and analyze a simple method that given an n × d n\times d matrix, outputs approximate ℓ p \ell_{p} -Lewis weights, a natural measure of the importance of the rows with respect to the ℓ p \ell_{p} norm, for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . More precisely, we provide a simple post-processing procedure that turns natural one-sided approximate ℓ p \ell_{p} -Lewis weights into two-sided approximations. When combined with a simple one-sided approximation algorithm presented by Lee (PhD thesis, ‘16) this yields an algorithm for computing two-sided approximations of the ℓ p \ell_{p} -Lewis weights of an n × d n\times d -matrix using poly ⁡ ( d , p ) \mathrm{poly}(d,p) approximate leverage score computations. While efficient high-accuracy algorithms for approximating ℓ p \ell_{p} -Lewis had been established previously by Fazel, Lee, Padmanabhan and Sidford (SODA ‘22), the simple structure and approximation tolerance of our algorithm may make it of use for different applications.

Introduction

For a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, its
leverage scores

\sigma(\mathbf{A})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, are a prominent importance measure of its rows.
The leverage score of row
i

\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A})=[\sigma(\mathbf{A})]_{i}
is defined as

\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A}):=a_{i}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{+}a_{i},\quad\forall i\in[n],

where
a_{i}^{\top}
is the
i
-th row of
A
and
(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{+}
is the pseudoinverse of
\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
.
It holds that
\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A})\leq d
, with equality if
A
has rank
d
.
Leverage scores have widespread applications in statistics
[
RV07
]
, randomized linear algebra
[
DMIMW12
]
, and graph algorithms
[
SS08
]
.

While leverage scores measure row importance with respect to the
\ell_{2}
-norm, there is a generalized notion called

that measures row importances with respect to the
\ell_{p}
-norm
[
Lew78
,
BLM89
,
CP15
]
.
For finite
p>0
, the
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weights of
\mathbf{A}
are defined as the unique vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
that satisfies

w=\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A}),

(1)

We denote the
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weights of
\mathbf{A}
by
w_{p}(\mathbf{A})
, and note that
w_{2}(\mathbf{A})=\sigma(\mathbf{A})
.
Lewis weights are connected to computational geometry
[
Tod16
]
, and have applications in
\ell_{p}
-regression, e.g.,
[
CP15
,
JLS22
]
, and convex optimization, e.g.,
[
LS19
,
vdBLL+21
]
.

Lewis weights lack an explicit description, and computing them is somewhat more involved than the
p=2
case of leverage scores. For
p\in(0,4)
there exists a (simple) iterative scheme, based on computing leverage scores
[
CP15
]
. For
p\geq 4
the task is more challenging, see for example the recent works
[
CP15
,
LS19
,
FLPS22
,
WY23
]
.
Luckily, as for leverage scores, an
\varepsilon
-estimate
of the form

(1-\varepsilon)w_{p}(\mathbf{A})\leq v\leq(1+\varepsilon)w_{p}(\mathbf{A}),

(2)

where the inequality is elementwise,
often suffices in applications such
\ell_{p}
-embedding and -regression problems
[
Tal90
,
CP15
,
WY23
]
, approximating John ellipsoids
[

]
, or interior point methods
[

]
.

The implicit definition in

1

For example, we call a vector

a

\varepsilon
-approximation

) in the following way:

(1-\varepsilon)\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\leq w\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A}).

It was shown in

that a two-sided

-approximation is also a

-estimate of the true Lewis weights.
An even weaker

\varepsilon
-approximation

(1-\varepsilon)\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\leq w\quad\text{ and }\quad\|w\|_{1}\leq(1+\varepsilon)d,

(4)

and, as is argued in

,

]
, one-sided approximation suffices for
\ell_{p}
-embedding and -regression problems, even when

.

Correspondingly, a simple algorithm to obtain such a one-sided approximation was proposed by Lee
[
Lee16
]
; it allows one to construct a one-sided
\varepsilon
-approximation by computing
\widetilde{O}(1/\varepsilon)
many
O(\varepsilon)
-approximate leverage scores.

In

Their algorithm is related to the aforementioned algorithm by Cohen and Peng

Crucially however, their algorithm is limited to

More recently, Fazel, Lee, Padmanabhan and Sidford

provided an algorithm for computing an

-estimate of the

, and to high precision, using

A naive analysis of this last algorithm suggests that approximate leverage scores suffice, albeit with a precision that is
\mathrm{poly}(\varepsilon,1/d,1/n)
.
We generally have that

, and such inverse polynomial dependence on

In this note we focus on the

We show that it is possible to achieve two-sided and even multiplicative Lewis weight approximations by making

-approximate leverage score computations, as is implied by the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Low-precision Lewis weights)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
1>\varepsilon>0
and
p\geq 2
.
Using
\widetilde{O}(pd/\varepsilon)
many
O(\varepsilon/(pd))
-approximate leverage score computations it is possible to compute a two-sided
\varepsilon
-approximation (
3
) of the
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weights of

.

Concretely, we show that running one “fixed point iteration” (as used e.g. in the algorithm in
[

]
) can turn a one-sided
\varepsilon
-approximation into a two-sided
O(pd\varepsilon)
-approximation of the Lewis weights.
By using Lee’s algorithm to obtain the one-sided approximation, we obtain the stated theorem.
By

Recently, two of the authors provided an improved quantum algorithm for computing approximate leverage scores, see

Throughout, we use plain italic for vectors

If there can be no confusion, we use a boldface capital to denote the diagonal matrix corresponding to its lowercase counterpart, e.g.,

For vectors

we write

as shorthand for the entrywise inequality

We formally define the following three notions of approximate Lewis weights.

Definition 1

(Lewis weight approximations)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
,
1>\varepsilon>0
and
p\geq 2
.

•

w
is a one-sided
\varepsilon
-approximate
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weight of
\mathbf{A}
if
\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\leq(1+\varepsilon)w
and
\|w_{i}\|_{1}\leq(1+\varepsilon)d
.

•

w
is a two-sided
\varepsilon
-approximate
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weight of
\mathbf{A}
if
(1-\varepsilon)\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\leq w\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})
.

•

w
is an
\varepsilon
-estimate
of
w_{p}(\mathbf{A})
if
(1-\varepsilon)w_{p}(\mathbf{A})\leq w\leq(1+\varepsilon)w_{p}(\mathbf{A})
.

Note that when
p=2
, the case of leverage scores, the last two notions of approximation coincide.

Next, we prove two simple technical lemmas that we use.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and
\delta>0
are such that
y\leq(1+\delta)x
entrywise and
\|x\|_{1}\leq(1+\delta)\|y\|_{1}
. Then
\|x-y\|_{1}\leq 3\delta\|y\|_{1}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from writing
x-y=(x-\frac{1}{1+\delta}y)-\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}y
and applying the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[n]}\left|x_{i}-\frac{1}{1+\delta}y_{i}\right|+\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\sum_{i\in[n]}|y_{i}|=\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}-\frac{1}{1+\delta}y_{i}+\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\sum_{i\in[n]}y_{i}

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{1}-\frac{1}{1+\delta}\|y\|_{1}+\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\|y\|_{1}.

Finally, using
\|x\|_{1}\leq(1+\delta)\|y\|_{1}
we obtain
\|x-y\|_{1}\leq\left(\frac{(1+\delta)^{2}-1+\delta}{1+\delta}\right)\|y\|_{1}\leq 3\delta\|y\|_{1}
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

|1-x^{c}|\leq c\cdot\mathrm{max}\{1,x\}^{c-1}\cdot|1-x|
for all
x\geq 0
and
c\geq 1
.

Proof.

As the claim is trivial when
c=1
, we assume
c>1
. In this case,

|1-x^{c}|=\left|\int_{1}^{x}c\cdot\alpha^{c-1}d\alpha\right|\leq\left|\int_{1}^{x}c\cdot\mathrm{max}\{1,x\}^{c-1}d\alpha\right|=c\cdot\mathrm{max}\{1,x\}^{c-1}\cdot|1-x|\,.\qed

Main result

We first prove a key lemma that allows us to compare quadratic forms associated with
\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)
and
w

Lemma 4
.

For any
w\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
and

\alpha=\big\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)-\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\big\|_{1}
, we have

(1-\alpha)\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A}\preceq\mathbf{A}^{\top}\text{\rm Diag}(\mathcal{T}_{p}(w))^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A}\preceq(1+\alpha)\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A}\,.

Proof.

Let
\Delta:=\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}-w^{1-\frac{2}{p}}
and let
\Delta_{+}:=\mathrm{max}\{\Delta,\mathbf{0}\}

and
\Delta_{-}:=\mathrm{max}\{-\Delta,\mathbf{0}\}
entrywise so that
\Delta_{+},\Delta_{-}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
,

\Delta=\Delta_{+}-\Delta_{-}
, and
\|\Delta\|_{1}=\|\Delta_{+}\|_{1}+\|\Delta_{-}\|_{1}
.
Letting
\mathbf{H}:=\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A}
we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\Delta\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\Delta_{+}\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\Delta_{-}\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\right\|_{2}\,.

We then use the fact
\Delta_{+}
is positive semidefinite, and therefore so is
\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\Delta_{+}\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}
, to upper bound the spectral norm by the trace:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\Delta_{+}\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\Delta_{+}\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\right]=\sum_{i\in[n]}\left[\Delta_{+}\right]_{i}\left[\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right]_{ii}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n]}\left[\Delta_{+}\right]_{i}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{w_{i}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\,.

By symmetry the same bound holds for
\Delta_{-}
.
Combining the two bounds yields that

\left\|\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\Delta\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\right\|_{2}\leq\sum_{i\in[n]}\left|\Delta_{i}\right|\cdot\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{w_{i}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}

Recalling that
\Delta=\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}-w^{1-\frac{2}{p}}
we obtain the desired upper bound:

\sum_{i\in[n]}\left|\Delta_{i}\right|\cdot\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{w_{i}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}=\big\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)-\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\big\|_{1}\,.\qed

Next, we show how to bound the approximation factor
\alpha=\big\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)-\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\big\|_{1}
when

\mathbf{A}

Lemma 5
.

If
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
is a one-sided
\varepsilon
-approximate
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weight of
\mathbf{A}
for

\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)-\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\|_{1}\leq 3\varepsilon(\frac{p}{2}-1)(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}d.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)_{i}=\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})(\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})/w_{i})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)
and
\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})
are non-negative, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)-\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\left|\left(\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{w_{i}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{p}{2}-1)(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\|w-\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\|_{1}\,,

where in the last step we applied

and that
\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\leq(1+\varepsilon)w
. The result then follows from the fact that
\|w-\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\|_{1}\leq 3\varepsilon\|\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\|_{1}=3\varepsilon d

.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main technical theorem.

Theorem 2

(1- to 2-sided)
.

Let
w
be a one-sided
\varepsilon
-approximate
\ell_{p}
-Lewis weight of
\mathbf{A}
and let

v=\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)=w^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Then, for
\alpha=3\varepsilon(\frac{p}{2}-1)(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}d

(1-\alpha)\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{V}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\leq v_{i}\leq(1+\alpha)\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{V}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})

We have that

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(\mathbf{V}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})_{i}}{v_{i}}

\displaystyle=v_{i}^{-\frac{2}{p}}\cdot\left[\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right]_{ii}=\frac{\left[\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{+}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right]_{ii}}{\left[\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{+}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right]_{ii}}\,.

(1-\alpha)\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A}\preceq\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A}\preceq(1+\alpha)\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A}.

By
Lemma

4
and
Lemma

\alpha=\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(w)-\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})\|_{1}\leq 3\varepsilon(\frac{p}{2}-1)(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}d
.

Obtaining multiplicative estimates of Lewis weights

Through

We first prove a simple lemma that shows that two-sided approximation is “stable” with respect to a multiplicative change (i.e., if

Lemma 6
.

Let
\gamma\geq 1
.
Let
v,\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
be such that
\gamma^{-1}\tilde{v}_{i}\leq v_{i}\leq\gamma\tilde{v}_{i}
for all

\gamma^{-1}\frac{\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{\tilde{v}_{i}}\leq\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{V}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{v_{i}}\leq\gamma\frac{\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{\tilde{v}_{i}}

Proof.

We first prove the first inequality. We have that

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{\tilde{v}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{v}_{i}^{-\frac{2}{p}}\cdot\left[\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{+}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right]_{ii}\leq\gamma\,v_{i}^{-\frac{2}{p}}\cdot\left[\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{+}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right]_{ii}=\gamma\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{V}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{A})}{v_{i}}

where the inequality uses the estimates
\tilde{v}_{i}^{-\frac{2}{p}}\leq\gamma^{\frac{2}{p}}v_{i}^{-\frac{2}{p}}
and
(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{+}\preceq\gamma^{1-\frac{2}{p}}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\mathbf{A})^{+}
. The second inequality of the lemma follows by exchanging the roles of
v
and
\tilde{v}
.
∎

Abstract
. We prove a wavelet T ⁡ ( 1 ) T(1) theorem for compactness of multilinear Calderón-Zygmund (CZ) operators. Our approach characterizes compactness in terms of testing conditions and yields a representation theorem for compact CZ forms in terms of wavelet and paraproduct forms that reflect the compact nature of the operator.



1.
Introduction

A continuous
(m+1)
-linear form
\Lambda
defined on the
(m+1)
-fold product of the Schwarz space
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a singular integral form if its off-diagonal kernel satisfies the standard size and smoothness estimates (see Definition
3.1
below). A singular integral form
\Lambda
is bounded on
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all

(1.1)

1<p_{j}\leq\infty,\quad\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}=1,

if and only if
\Lambda
is
Calderón-Zygmund
, which we take to mean that it satisfies the weak boundedness property (see Definition
3.2
below) and the following multilinear
T(1)
condition: There exists
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for every
\phi
in
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with mean zero,

(1.2)

\Lambda^{*,j}(\phi,1,\ldots,1)=\langle\phi,\mathfrak{b}_{j}\rangle,

where
\Lambda^{*,j}
permutes the
0
th and
j
th argument (see (
1.3
) below). When
m=1
, this is the celebrated
T(1)
theorem of David and Journé
[
10
]
which was extended to
m\geq 2
by Grafakos and Torres
[
18
]
(see also the earlier works
[
8
,
9
]
) and more recently developed in, for example,
[
13
,
14
,
23
,
26
,
27
]
.

Our goal here is to prove a
T(1)
representation theorem for compactness of multilinear singular integral forms. The first distinction between the boundedness problem and compactness problem is that compactness is a property of operators, while boundedness in the reflexive range (
1.1
) can be equivalently stated in terms of forms. Accordingly for each
j=0,1,\ldots,m
, we associate to
\Lambda
the
m
-linear adjoint operators
T^{*,j}
and transposed forms
\Lambda^{*,j}
by

(1.3)

\langle f_{0},T^{*,j}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\rangle=\Lambda^{*,j}(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\Lambda(f_{j},f_{1},\ldots,f_{j-1},f_{0},f_{j+1},\ldots,f_{m}).

If
\Lambda
is Calderón-Zygmund, then we say each
T^{*,j}
is an
m
-linear Calderón-Zygmund operator (CZO). Often we use the shorthand
T=T^{*,0}
.

In the linear case, Villaroya in
[
43
]
gave a complete characterization of compact Calderón-Zygmund operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which was further developed in
[
34
,
35
,
39
,
44
]
. Recently, Mitkovski and Stockdale in
[
30
]
gave a simplified formulation of the
T(1)
theorem for compactness of Villaroya. More precisely, they showed that a CZO
T
is compact if and only if
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{1}
both belong to
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a vanishing version of the weak boundedness property, called the weak compactness property, is satisfied. Even more recently, Bényi, Li, Oh, and Torres
[
2
]
have characterized compact CZOs in terms of
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
testing conditions, in the spirit of Stein’s formulation of the
T(1)
-theorem
[
38
]
. These
T(1)
theorems for compactness are complemented by a compact Rubio De Francia theory of extrapolation due to Hytönen and Lappas in
[
19
,
21
]
, and subsequently developed by others in
[
6
,
7
]
. Particularly, in
[
6
]
, Cao, Olivo, and Yabuta extended the bilinear results of
[
19
]
to the multilinear setting and to the quasi-Banach range, in which case the target space can be
L^{r}
with
r>\frac{1}{m}
. However, due to the difficulties of extrapolating to the upper endpoint in the multilinear setting
[
25
,
32
]
, the results of
[
6
,
19
]
do not consider the case where one (or more) input spaces is
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We point out that our results below do yield compactness when an input space is
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Our main result is Theorem
2
below which builds on the literature discussed above in the following two noteworthy manners. First, it provides a
T(1)
-type characterization of compact multilinear CZOs. Though extrapolation of compactness had been developed in the multilinear setting as mentioned above, a
T(1)
-type characterization in the vein of Villaroya
[
43
]
or the simplified formulation of Mitkovski and Stockdale
[
30
]
was heretofore unknown. Our characterization is indeed in the simplified form of
[
30
]
(not relying on compact CZ kernel estimates) while at the same time provides a stronger result than both
[
2
]
and
[
30
]
when
m=1
.

Second, we provide a representation formula for all compact CZOs. This is one answer to the search for examples of compact CZOs. Frankly, from Theorem
2
, we now know all the examples of
m
-linear compact CZOs. This contribution is new even in the linear case. In words, we can describe our wavelet representation formula in terms of two building blocks whose adjoint operators are well-known examples of compact CZOs. The first class of buiding blocks consists of what we call compact wavelet forms which are almost-orthogonal perturbations of compact approximations of the wavelet resolution of the identity. The second class of building blocks consists of paraproduct forms with
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
symbol. The wavelet representation theorem we will prove says that all compact CZOs are finite linear combinations of these two building blocks. We refer to Theorem
2
and Definitions
2.6
and
5.1
below for precise statements.

The strategy of representing CZOs as simpler model operators has been very successful in understanding more refined properties of CZOs, most famously in providing sharp quantitative weighted estimates. Dyadic representation theorems, finding their roots in the works of Figiel
[
16
]
; Nazarov, Treil, and Volberg
[
31
]
; and Petermichl
[
36
]
among others, have led to a powerful and comprehensive theory of quantitative
L^{p}
estimates for singular integrals, culminating with Hytönen’s proof of the
A_{2}
conjecture
[
22
]
. See
[
1
,
20
,
26
,
28
]
and references therein for more recent extensions. Smooth wavelet representations, which may be traced back to David and Journé’s original proof of the
T(1)
theorem, have recently been developed by Di Plinio and some of the authors in a series of papers
[
11
,
12
,
15
]
in order to study various Sobolev space estimates for CZOs.

For simplicity, here in the introduction we state only the bilinear testing characterization, without the representation. It remains only to introduce the “weak compactness property” in the bilinear setting. For the function
\varphi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
t>0
, define

\varphi_{z}=\frac{1}{t^{d}}\varphi\left(\frac{\cdot-w}{t}\right),\quad z=(w,t).

Moreover, let
\mathbb{B}(z)
denote a hyperbolic ball in the upper half space of radius
1
centered at
z=(w,t)
(see (
2.3
) below for the precise definition).
Fixing three functions
\phi,\psi^{1},\psi^{2}
, define

(1.4)

\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}(z)=t^{2d}\cdot\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{B}(z)}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{3}\\
i=1,2\end{subarray}}\left|\Lambda^{\sigma}\left(\phi_{z_{1}},\psi^{i}_{z_{2}},\phi_{z}\right)\right|,

where for each
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m+1}
, the permutation group on
\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
, we define

(1.5)

\Lambda^{\sigma}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})=\Lambda(f_{\sigma(0)},\ldots,f_{\sigma(m)}).

Theorem 1
.

Let
T
be a bilinear CZO,
\Lambda
the associated trilinear form, and
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined by (
1.2
). Then, the following are equivalent.

A.

For each
1<p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
, and
\tfrac{1}{2}<r<\infty
satisfying
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{r}
,
T
extends to a compact bilinear operator from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and from
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

B.

\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}(z)=0
and for each
j=0,1,2
,
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

A broad outline of the proof is to use a wavelet averaging procedure to obtain the representation formula in §
3
and then use the Riesz-Kolmogorov criterion to obtain the precompactness of the image of the unit ball under the adjoint operators to the wavelet and paraproduct forms in §
4
and §
5
. These results are combined in §
6
to state our main result, Theorem
2
including the representation theorem and a simplified statement in the linear case, Theorem
3
. There we also outline a sample application.

2.
Wavelets

In this section we will review some preliminaries regarding wavelets and introduce one of the building blocks of our representation, wavelet forms.

2.1.
Analysis of the parameter space

Introduce the parameter space

Z^{d}=\{z=(w,t):w\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t>0\},

whose elements
z=(w,t)
act on functions
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
functions by the formula

{\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}f=\frac{1}{t^{d}}f\left(\frac{\cdot-w}{t}\right).

Let
\mu
be the measure on
Z^{d}
given by

\int\displaylimits_{Z^{d}}F(z)\,{\rm d}\mu(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(w,t)\,\frac{{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}t}{t},\quad F\in{\mathrm{C}}_{0}(Z^{d}).

Notice that
\mu
is invariant under
{\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}
.
To analyze multilinear operators, we will use wavelets adapted to two parameters, one in
Z^{md}
and the other in
Z^{d}
. First, given
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{md}
and
w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

(2.1)

|\mathbf{w}-w_{0}|_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|w_{i}-w_{0}|^{2}},\quad\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}),\quad w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{w},s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{md}
and
\zeta=(w_{0},t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, define for any
0<\delta\leq 1
,

(2.2)

[\mathbf{z},\zeta]_{\delta}=\frac{\min\{s,t\}^{\delta}}{\max\{t,s,|\mathbf{w}-w_{0}|_{2}\}^{md+\delta}}.

We will also say
\mathbf{z}\geq\zeta
if
s\geq t
. Notice that if
\delta\geq\delta^{\prime}
then
[\mathbf{z},\zeta]_{\delta}\leq[\mathbf{z},\zeta]_{\delta^{\prime}}.

For
M\geq 1
and
\zeta\in Z^{d}
, introduce
\mathbb{B}_{M}^{m}(\zeta)
which are the following approximate balls in the hyperbolic metric,

(2.3)

\mathbb{B}_{M}^{m}(\zeta)=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in Z^{md}:t2^{-M}\leq s\leq t2^{M},\,\left|\mathbf{w}-w_{0}\right|_{2}\leq t2^{M}\right\}

when
\zeta=(0,1)
,
M=1
, or
m=1
, those parameters are omitted from the notation.
Given a function
F:Z^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
we say
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}F(z)=L
if

\lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_{z\not\in\mathbb{B}_{M}}|F(z)-L|=0.

2.2.
Wavelet classes and forms

The building blocks of our representation theorem are wavelets for which we are going to introduce notation and relevant classes as well as the averaging lemmata from
[
12
,
15
]
.
We denote the space of Schwartz functions by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the mean-zero subspace

{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int\varphi(x)\,{\rm d}x=0\right\}.

We fix a radial function
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, supported in a ball and appropriately normalized which we will call the mother wavelet,
in which case the Calderón reproducing formula holds, namely

(2.4)

f=\int_{Z^{d}}\langle f,\phi_{z}\rangle\phi_{z}\,{\rm d}\mu(z)\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

For the convenience of the reader, we restate the setup from
[
12
]
on which we will base our analysis.
For
0<\delta\leq 1
we introduce the norm on functions
\varphi\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{md})
,

(2.5)

\|\varphi\|_{\star,\delta}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,h\in\mathbb{R}^{md}\\
0<|h|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\langle x\rangle^{md+\delta}\left(|\varphi(x)|+\frac{|\varphi(x+h)-\varphi(x)|}{|h|^{\delta}}\right),\quad\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}.

Definition 2.3
.

For
z=(w,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the wavelet class
\Psi^{m,\delta}_{z}
is defined by

\Psi^{m,\delta}_{z}=\left\{\varphi\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{md}):\|({\mathsf{Sy}}_{\mathbf{z}})^{-1}\varphi\|_{\star,\delta}\leq 1\right\},\quad\mathbf{z}=(w,\ldots,w,t)\in Z^{md},

and its cancellative subclass, for
j=1,\ldots,m
is denoted by
\Psi^{m,\delta;j}_{z}
and consists of
\varphi\in\Psi^{m,\delta}_{z}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})\,{\rm d}x_{j}=0.

Let
\chi_{z}
denote the
L^{\infty}
-normalized decay factor adapted to the parameter
z=(w,t)
,

\chi_{z}(x)=\left\langle\frac{x-w}{t}\right\rangle^{-1}.

With this notation, we can recast
\varphi\in\Psi^{m,\delta}_{z}
as

(2.6)

|\varphi|\leq\frac{1}{t^{md}}\chi_{\mathbf{z}}^{md+\delta},\quad|\varphi-\varphi(\cdot+h)|\leq\frac{|h|^{\delta}}{t^{md+\delta}}\chi_{\mathbf{z}}^{md+\delta}.

Aiming to make this paper as self contained as possible, as well as to set the stage for the representation in Proposition
3.11
, we state the averaging lemmata from
[
12
]
and a refinement of the averaging procedure in
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\phi
be the mother wavelet and
k\geq 0
. There exist functions
\psi^{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4
, satisfying

(i)

{\mathrm{supp}\,}\psi^{i}\subset B(0,1)
;

(ii)

\psi^{1},\psi^{3}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(iii)

\psi^{2},\psi^{4}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(iv)

For any
s>0
and
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{r\geq s}\int_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle f,\phi_{u,r}\rangle\phi_{u,r}\dfrac{{\rm d}u\ {\rm d}r}{r}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle f,\psi_{u,s}^{1}\rangle\psi_{u,s}^{2}+\langle f,\psi_{u,s}^{3}\rangle\psi_{u,s}^{4}\,{\rm d}u.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\varphi_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
j=1,\ldots,m
and
0<\eta<\delta\leq 1
. There exists
C>0
such that for any
H:Z^{md}\times Z^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\left|H(\mathbf{z},\zeta)\right|\leq\left[\mathbf{z},\zeta\right]_{\delta},

there holds

(2.7)

\nu_{\zeta}=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{z}\in Z^{md}\\
\mathbf{z}\geq\zeta\end{subarray}}H(\mathbf{z},\zeta)\left(\varphi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_{m}\right)_{\mathbf{z}}{\rm d}\mu(\mathbf{z})\in C\Psi^{m,\eta}_{\zeta}.

Furthermore, if
\varphi_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
\nu_{\zeta}\in C\Psi^{m,\eta;j}_{\zeta}
.

Finally, we end this section by introducing the wavelet forms, which will be used to systematically study
m
-linear CZOs.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a collection
\{\varepsilon_{z}\in\mathbb{C},\nu_{z}\in\Psi^{m,\delta;1}_{z}:z\in Z^{d}\}
define the associated
(m+1)
-linear
canonical wavelet form
by

\int_{Z^{d}}\varepsilon_{z}\left\langle f_{0},\phi_{z}\right\rangle\left\langle f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{m},\nu_{z}\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mu(z).

More generally, we say
U
is a wavelet form if
U^{\sigma}
is a canonical wavelet form for some
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m+1}
. We say
U
is a
bounded wavelet form
if
\sup_{z\in Z^{d}}\left|\varepsilon_{z}\right|<\infty
. Additionally, we say
U
is a
compact wavelet form
if it is a bounded wavelet form for which

\lim_{z\to\infty}\varepsilon_{z}=0.

Wavelet forms may be viewed as a generalization of the Calderón-Toeplitz operators considered in
[
33
,
37
]
. More generally though, all cancellative CZ forms (see Definition
3.2
below) can be realized as wavelet forms
[
12
,
15
]
. Let us proceed to describe in what sense bounded wavelet forms are indeed bounded. They themselves are cancellative CZ forms so Proposition
2.8
below follows from any number of results
[
18
,
23
,
25
,
27
,
32
]
. See also
[
12
]
*Proposition 5.1 for a direct proof of the sparse
(1,\ldots,1)
bound for bounded wavelet forms. Let us introduce some bookkeeping to concisely describe the full range of Lebesgue space estimates for CZOs.

Definition 2.7
.

Let

P=\left\{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}):1<p_{j}\leq\infty\right\},\quad{P_{\circ}=\left\{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}):1<p_{j}<\infty\right\}}.

We introduce the shorthand for
\vec{p}\in P
,

L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigtimes_{j=1}^{m}L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mathcal{B}^{\vec{p}}=\left\{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in L^{\vec{p}}:\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\right\},\quad r(\vec{p})=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)^{-1}.

Then the admissible classes of Hölder tuples we consider are

Q=\left\{(\vec{p},r(\vec{p})):\vec{p}\in P,\ r(\vec{p})<\infty\right\},\quad{Q_{\mathrm{ref}}=\left\{(\vec{p},r(\vec{p}):\vec{p}\in P_{\circ},\ r(\vec{p})>1\right\}}.

Given
(\vec{p},r)\in Q
, and an
m
-linear operator
T
, denote by
\left\|T\right\|_{\vec{p},r}
the operator norm from
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e.

\left\|T\right\|_{\vec{p},r}=\sup_{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in\mathcal{B}^{\vec{p}}}\left\|T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Furthermore, we define the following modification at the endpoint,

\left\|T\right\|_{\infty,\mathrm{BMO}}=\sup_{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in\mathcal{B}^{\vec{p}}}\left\|T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\vec{p}=(\infty,\ldots,\infty),

where the
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm is defined by

(2.8)

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sup_{Q\,\mathrm{cube}}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)-f_{Q}\right|\,{\rm d}x,\quad f_{Q}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(y)\,{\rm d}y.

Proposition 2.8
.

For each
(\vec{p},r)\in Q
, there exists
C_{\vec{p},r}>0
such that for any
T
such that
U(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})=\left\langle f_{0},T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\rangle
is a bounded wavelet form,

(2.9)

\left\|T\right\|_{\vec{p},r}\leq C_{\vec{p},r}\sup_{z\in Z^{d}}\left|\varepsilon_{z}\right|.

Furthermore, there exists
C_{\infty}>0
and
\bar{T}
an extension of
T
such that

(2.10)

\left\|\bar{T}\right\|_{\infty,\mathrm{BMO}}\leq C_{\infty}\sup_{z\in Z^{d}}\left|\varepsilon_{z}\right|.

3.
Wavelet representation of compact Calderón-Zygmund forms

Let us make rigorous the informal definitions given in the introduction.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
K\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{(m+1)d}\setminus\{x\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m+1}:x_{0}=\cdots=x_{m}\})
is a
\delta
-
singular integral kernel
if there exist
C_{K},\delta>0
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle|K(x_{0},\ldots,x_{m})|\leq\dfrac{C_{K}}{\left|x_{0}-\mathbf{x}\right|_{2}^{md}},\quad\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\max_{j=0,\ldots,m}|\Delta^{j}_{h}K(x_{0},\ldots,x_{m})|\leq\dfrac{C_{K}|h|^{\delta}}{\left|x_{0}-\mathbf{x}\right|_{2}^{md+\delta}},

where
\Delta_{h}^{j}
denotes the difference operator in the
j
-th position , and
\left|\cdot\right|_{2}
is defined in (
2.1
).
Let
\Lambda:\times_{j=0}^{m}{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous
(m+1)
-linear form. We say
\Lambda
is an
(m+1)
-linear
singular integral form
if there exists a singular integral kernel
K
such that for any
f_{0},\ldots,f_{m}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\cap_{j=0}^{m}{\mathrm{supp}\,}f_{j}=\varnothing
one has

\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m+1}}K(x)\prod_{j=0}^{m}f_{j}(x_{j})\,{\rm d}x.

When
m
is understood, we simply say
\Lambda
is a singular integral form.

Let
{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of all continuous functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}f(x)=0
. Then, the Banach space of functions with continuous mean oscillation,
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is defined to be the closure of
{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, defined in (
2.8
) above.

Definition 3.2
.

We say a singular integral form
\Lambda
is a
Calderón-Zygmund (CZ) form
if there exists
C_{\mathsf{W}}
such that

t^{md}\left|\Lambda({\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}\varphi_{0},\ldots,{\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}\varphi_{m})\right|\leq C_{\mathsf{W}},\quad\forall\varphi_{j}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(B(0,1))\cap\Psi^{1,1}_{(0,1)},

and furthermore, there exist
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that (
1.2
) holds. The rigorous definition of (
1.2
) is as follows. Let
\theta\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\theta(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
. Then, for
t>0
, set
\theta_{t}=\theta(\cdot t)
. We say (
1.2
) holds if for all
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(3.3)

\lim_{t\to 0}\Lambda^{*,j}(\varphi,\theta_{t},\theta_{t},\ldots,\theta_{t})=\left\langle\varphi,\mathfrak{b}_{j}\right\rangle.

If
T
is a continuous
m
-linear operator from
\times_{j=1}^{m}{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
such that
\left\langle f_{0},T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\rangle=\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})
for some CZ form
\Lambda
, then
T
is said to be a Calderón-Zygmund operator (CZO).
Furthermore, let
\phi
be a mother wavelet and
\psi^{2},\psi^{4}
be the Schwartz functions from Lemma
2.4
. For
j=1,\ldots,m
define

(3.4)

\vec{\psi}_{j}=\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{j-1}\psi^{2}\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=j}^{m-1}\psi^{4}\right)\otimes\phi.

For each

, define

(3.5)

\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}^{M}(\zeta)=\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{B}^{m}_{M}(\zeta)}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m+1}\\
j=1,\ldots,m\end{subarray}}\left|\Lambda^{\sigma}\left((\vec{\psi}_{j})_{\mathbf{z}},\phi_{\zeta}\right)\right|t^{md},\quad\zeta=(w,t).

A CZ form

is said to be a compact CZ form if for some (all)

\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\lim\limits_{\zeta\to\infty}\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}^{M}(\zeta)=0.

Finally, we say a CZ form is cancellative if

.

Remark 3.3
.

By the symmetry in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
above, if
\Lambda
is a CZ form or compact CZ form, then so is
\Lambda^{\sigma}
for any

is a CZO, so is

for any

.
It is not too difficult to see that if

.

Remark 3.4
.

Compact wavelet forms, as defined in Definition
2.6
are compact CZ forms. Almost orthogonality of elements of the wavelet classes, e.g.
[
15
]
*Lemma 2.3, and the fact that
\varepsilon_{z}\to 0
readily shows
\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}(z)\to 0
for such forms. Furthermore, since wavelet forms possess cancellation in
two
inputs, all the symbols
\mathfrak{b}_{j}
are zero.
Compact paraproduct forms, the subject of §
5
below, are also compact CZ forms. For these,
\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}(z)\to 0
for the same reason as the wavelet forms. Furthermore,
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
is the paraproduct symbol while
\mathfrak{b}_{j}=0
for
j=1,\ldots,m
.
Our representation theorem (Theorem
2
part C. below) states that in fact all compact CZ forms are a linear combination of forms from these two classes.

Now we justify our description of such forms as compact.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
T
be an
m
-linear CZO. We say
T
is a compact CZO if for each
(\vec{p},r)\in Q
, there exists an extension of
T
,
\bar{T}
, such that
\bar{T}(\mathcal{B}^{\vec{p}})
is precompact in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and at the upper endpoint, there exists an extension
\bar{\bar{T}}
such that
\bar{\bar{T}}(\mathcal{B}^{\infty})
is precompact in
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, if the weaker condition, for all
(p,r)\in Q_{\mathrm{ref}}

T(\mathcal{B}^{{\vec{p}}})
is precompact in

, holds, then we say

is a compact CZO in the reflexive range.

Remark 3.6
.

The classical definition of compactness of an abstract
m
-linear operator on quasi-normed spaces
[
4
,
6
]
is that it maps bounded sets to precompact sets. In Definition
3.5
, we are imposing this definition of compactness on
T
acting from
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
all

(\vec{p},r)
in
Q
and at the upper endpoint because our testing conditions allow us to conclude compactness in this full range. It is tempting to only require
T:L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be compact for a single
(\vec{p},r)
, but the current state of multilinear extrapolation of compactness
[
6
,
19
]
does not include the endpoints.

An important concept closely related to compact operators is that of a weakly convergent sequence. We give a straightforward sufficient condition for generating such families in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
parameterized by
z\in Z^{d}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
1\leq q_{-}<r<q_{+}\leq\infty
and
\{f_{z}\in L^{q_{-}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{q_{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):z\in Z^{d}\}
satisfy

(i)

\left\|f_{z}\right\|_{L^{r}}
is uniformly bounded,

(ii)

there exists
\beta>0
such that
\left\|f_{z}\right\|_{L^{q_{-}}}\lesssim t^{\beta}
,
\left\|f_{z}\right\|_{L^{q_{+}}}\lesssim t^{-\beta}
,

(iii)

for
|x-w|\gtrsim t
,
\left|f_{z}(x)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{r}}\chi_{z}(x)
.

Then for any
g\in L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\left\langle f_{z},g\right\rangle\to 0
.

Proof.

By (i) and a standard density argument, it is enough to prove the conclusion for
g\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose
{\mathrm{supp}\,}g\subset B(0,R)
. The main step is the trivial consequence of Hölder’s inequality.

\left|\left\langle f_{z},g\right\rangle\right|\leq\min\left\{\left\|f_{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,R))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{1}},\left\|f_{z}\right\|_{L^{q_{-}}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{q_{-}^{\prime}}},\left\|f_{z}\right\|_{L^{q_{+}}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{q_{+}^{\prime}}}\right\}.

(iii) ensures that the first quantity is smaller when
z=(w,t)
,
t\sim 1
, and
|w|
is much larger than
R
. (ii) controls the latter two quantities when
t
is small or large.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
T
be an
m
-linear CZO. First, if
T
is compact in the reflexive range, then for any
\varphi^{1},\ldots,\varphi^{m}\in L^{\infty}
with compact support, and any

(3.6)

\lim_{z\to\infty}t^{md-\frac{d}{2}}\left\|T^{*,j}(\varphi^{1}_{z},\ldots,\varphi^{m}_{z})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0,\quad\varphi_{z}^{i}={\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}\varphi^{i}.

Second, if (
3.6
) holds, then each
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for

, and for each
M\geq 1

(3.7)

\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}^{M}(\zeta)=0.

Proof.

To prove the first conclusion, set
b_{z}^{j}=t^{md-\frac{d}{2}}T^{*,j}(\varphi^{1}_{z},\ldots,\varphi^{m}_{z})
. Since
T^{*,j}
is bounded from
\times_{j=1}^{m}L^{qm}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
1<q<\infty
(being a CZO) we have

(3.8)

\left\|b_{z}^{j}\right\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim t^{\frac{d}{q}-\frac{d}{2}}.

Furthermore,

(3.9)

\left\|b_{z}^{j}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=t^{md-\frac{d}{2}}\left\langle b_{z}^{j},T^{*,j}(\varphi^{1}_{z},\ldots,\varphi^{m}_{z})\right\rangle=\left\langle f^{0}_{z},T(f^{1}_{z},\ldots,f^{m}_{z})\right\rangle,

for
f^{i}_{z}
which are appropriate permutations and normalizations of
\varphi^{i}_{z}
and
b^{j}_{z}
. To assign them precisely, we distinguish between
j=0
and
j=1,\ldots,m
. In the first case, we can simply take

f_{z}^{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t^{d(1-\frac{1}{2m})}\varphi_{z}^{i}&i=1,\ldots,m\\
b_{z}^{0}&i=0;\end{array}\right.\quad p_{i}=2m,\ i=1,\ldots,m;\quad r=2.

And for
j=1,\ldots,m
then

f_{z}^{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t^{d(1-\frac{1}{4(m-1)})}\varphi_{z}^{i}&i\not\in\{0,j\}\\
t^{d(1-\frac{1}{4})}\varphi_{z}^{j}&i=0\\
b_{z}^{j}&i=j;\end{array}\right.\quad p_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}4(m-1)&i\not\in\{0,j\}\\
2&i=j;\end{array}\right.\quad r=4

In either case, as defined,

\{(f^{1}_{z},\ldots,f^{m}_{z}),z\in Z^{d}\}
is a bounded set in
L^{\vec{p}}
. Crucially, by Lemma
3.7
,
f^{0}_{z}
converges weakly to zero in
L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In the case
f^{0}_{z}=t^{d(1-\frac{1}{r})}\varphi^{j}_{z}
the conditions are readily verified for

and
q_{+}=\infty

(the case

and

), also satisfies the assumptions of Lemma

. Indeed, (i) and (ii) are consequences of (
3.8

3.1
). Now suppose (
3.6

\varepsilon>0
and

such that

(3.10)

\left|\left\langle f^{0}_{z_{n}},T(f^{1}_{z_{n}},\ldots,f^{m}_{z_{n}})\right\rangle\right|\geq\varepsilon.

As

was assumed to be compact from

, there exists a subsequence

To prove the second statement, Cauchy-Schwarz immediately tells us that (

[
18

*Lemma 1. Notice that when

Let

satisfy

and, in the sense of (

Using the kernel estimate (

for

\mathsf{osc}_{z}(T[\varphi_{\zeta},\ldots,\varphi_{\zeta}])=\mathsf{osc}_{z}(b^{j}_{\zeta})\lesssim_{a}\left\|b^{j}_{\zeta}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, given

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\Lambda

\delta
-CZ form,

, and
\psi_{j}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(B(0,1))

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\Lambda
be a cancellative compact
\delta
-CZ form and
\eta\in(0,\delta)
. Let
\phi
be the mother wavelet and let
\vec{\psi}_{j}
be defined as in (

C>0
and
\left\{\varepsilon_{\zeta}\in\mathbb{C}:\zeta\in Z^{d}\right\}
with the properties that

(3.12)

\sup_{\zeta\in Z^{d}}\left|\varepsilon_{\zeta}\right|\leq C,\quad\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\left|\varepsilon_{\zeta}\right|=0,\quad\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m+1}\\
j=1,\ldots,m\end{subarray}}\left|\Lambda^{\sigma}\left((\vec{\psi}_{j})_{\mathbf{z}},\phi_{\zeta}\right)\right|\leq\left|\varepsilon_{\zeta}\right|\left[\mathbf{z},\zeta\right]_{\eta},\quad\mathbf{z}\geq\zeta.

Proof.

By symmetry, we can assume
\sigma
is the identity element and

. Furthermore, set
\vec{\psi}=\vec{\psi}_{1}
to declutter the notation. For each

, we will construct

and

\displaystyle\sup_{\zeta\in Z^{d}}|\varepsilon_{\zeta}^{n}|\lesssim 1,\quad\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}|\varepsilon_{\zeta}^{n}|=0,

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda\left(\vec{\psi}_{\mathbf{z}},\phi_{\zeta}\right)\right|\lesssim 2^{-\rho n}\left|\varepsilon^{n}_{\zeta}\right|\left[\mathbf{z},\zeta\right]_{\eta},\quad\mathbf{z}\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{B}_{1}^{m}(\zeta)&n=1,\\
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{m}(\zeta)\setminus\mathbb{B}_{{n-1}}^{m}(\zeta)&n\geq 2,\end{array}\right.\quad\mathbf{z}\geq\zeta.

Assuming we have such
\varepsilon_{\zeta}^{n}
, define

\varepsilon_{\zeta}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-\rho n}\varepsilon^{n}_{\zeta}.

The first and third properties of
\varepsilon_{\zeta}
in (
3.12

approaches zero. Now, to construct

, let
\delta_{j}>0
such that
\eta<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<\delta
. By Lemma

and the definition of

\left|\Lambda\left(\vec{\psi}_{\mathbf{z}},\phi_{\zeta}\right)\right|\lesssim\left(t^{-md}\right)^{1-\theta}\left[\mathbf{z},\zeta\right]_{\delta_{2}}^{\theta}\min\{1,\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}^{n}(\zeta)\}^{1-\theta},\quad\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{B}^{m}_{n}(\zeta),\ \mathbf{z}\geq\zeta.

We choose
\theta

With this specific choice of

\left(t^{-md}\right)^{1-\theta}[\mathbf{z},\zeta]_{\delta_{2}}^{\theta}=[\mathbf{z},\zeta]_{\delta_{1}}

\varepsilon_{\zeta}^{1}=\min\{1,\mathsf{W}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\zeta)\}^{1-\theta}
handles the case

\left[\mathbf{z},\zeta\right]_{\delta_{1}}=\left(\frac{t}{\max\left\{s,|\mathbf{w}-w_{0}|\right\}}\right)^{\rho}\left[\mathbf{z},\zeta\right]_{\eta}.

Therefore, one only needs to verify that for

4.
Compact wavelet forms

In this section we will prove the compact analogue of Proposition

.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T
be such that
U(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})=\left\langle f_{0},T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\rangle
is a compact wavelet form. Then

is a compact CZO.

Proof.

T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)
is either of the form

(4.1)

\int_{Z^{d}}\varepsilon_{z}\left\langle f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{m},\nu_{z}\right\rangle\phi_{z}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu(z)\quad\mathrm{or}\,\int_{Z^{d}}\varepsilon_{z}\left\langle f_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{\sigma(m)},\nu_{z}(x,\cdot)\otimes\phi_{z}\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mu(z)

for some
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m}
and
\nu_{z}\in\Psi^{m,\delta;j}_{z}
; by
\nu_{z}(x,\cdot)
we mean for each
x
it returns the function
(x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})\mapsto\nu_{z}(x,x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})
. We will only handle the second case, and we will reduce, by symmetry, to the case where
\sigma
is the identity. The first case in (
4.1
) is simpler, though in fact they are handled in exactly the same fashion. Let
\rho>0
and split the integral defining
T
over
\mathbb{B}_{M}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{M}
where
M
is chosen large enough that
|\varepsilon_{z}|\leq\rho
for
z\not\in\mathbb{B}_{M}
. Therefore, the operator norm of the second component is, by (
2.9
) controlled by
\rho
. The proof will be concluded if we can show
R_{\rho}
defined by

R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{M}}\left\langle f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{m},\nu_{z}(x,\cdot)\otimes\phi_{z}\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mu(z),\quad\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}),

is compact. By the Riesz-Kolmogorov compactness criteria (see e.g.
[
29
]
and
[
41
]
for the case

), we need to prove that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{B}^{\vec{p}}}\int_{|x|>N}|R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}(x)|^{r}{\rm d}x=0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\sup_{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{B}^{\vec{p}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}(x+h)-R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}(x)|^{r}{\rm d}x=0.

To this end we will give suitable pointwise bounds on the operator

and the differences induced by it. Since
M
is fixed, we will crucially use that

satisfies

(4.4)

where
\chi=\chi_{(0,1)}
while

and

now denote comparability with constants depending on

). To this end, introduce, for

,

\lambda_{0}=\frac{d}{r}+\eta,\quad\lambda_{j}=\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}}+\eta,\quad\eta=\frac{1}{m}\left(\frac{d}{p_{m}^{\prime}}+\delta\right)>0.

It is easy to check that
md+\delta=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}
,
\lambda_{0}r>d
, and
\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{\prime}>d
, thus for
z\in\mathbb{B}_{M}
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left|\langle f_{m},\phi_{z}\rangle\right|\leq\left\|f_{m}\right\|_{p_{m}}\left\|\phi_{z}\right\|_{p_{m}^{\prime}}\lesssim 1

\displaystyle\left|\langle v_{z}(x,\cdot),f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{m-1}\rangle\right|\leq\frac{1}{t^{d}}\chi_{z}(x)^{\lambda_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{p_{j}}\left\|\frac{1}{t^{d}}\chi_{z}^{\lambda_{j}}\right\|_{p_{j}^{\prime}}\lesssim\chi(x)^{\lambda_{0}}.

With these estimates in hand we have that
|R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}|\lesssim\chi^{\lambda_{0}}
and therefore (
4.2
) holds since
\lambda_{0}r>d
. In the same way, we have that

\left|\langle v_{z}(x,\cdot),f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{m-1}\rangle-\langle v_{z}(x+h,\cdot),f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{m-1}\rangle\right|\lesssim|h|^{\delta}\chi(x)^{\lambda_{0}}

and therefore
|R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}(x)-R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}(x-h)|\lesssim|h|^{\delta}\chi(x)^{\lambda_{0}}
from which (
4.3
) now follows. It remains to handle the endpoint case. First note that
R_{\rho}
is clearly bounded on
\mathcal{B}^{\infty}
. We claim it suffices to establish that for each sequence
\{\mathbf{f}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}
,
R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}_{n}
has a convergent subsequence in
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Indeed, given such an
\{\mathbf{f}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, by a diagonalization argument, we may extract a subsequence
\{\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for each
\rho_{n}=\frac{1}{n}
,
\{R_{\rho_{n}}\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
pick
n
large enough that

(4.6)

\left\|{(T-R_{\rho_{n}})[\cdot]}\right\|_{\infty,\mathrm{BMO}}<\frac{\epsilon}{3},

recalling the definition (
3.11
).
Such an
n
exists by the condition
\varepsilon_{z}\to 0
and (
2.10
). Then, pick
N
large enough that for all
i,k\geq N
,
\left\|R_{\rho_{n}}\mathbf{f}_{n_{i}}-R_{\rho_{n}}\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\frac{\epsilon}{3}
. Therefore, by the triangle inequality, (
4.6
), and recalling that
R_{\rho}[\mathbf{f_{n}}]
and
R_{\rho}(\mathbf{f}_{n})
only differ by a constant,

\left\|T[\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}]-T[\mathbf{f}_{n_{i}}]\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\epsilon

whence
\{T[\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}]\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy and has a limit in
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Now it remains to show each
R_{\rho}
is compact. Applying (
4.5
) with all
p_{j}=\infty
implies the same pointwise estimates as above, which implies
R_{\rho}:{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}\to{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that the family
\{R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}:\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}\}
is equicontinuous. Therefore, by the Arzela-Ascoli theorem and a diagonalization argument, given a sequence
\{\mathbf{f}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}
, we can obtain a subsequence such that
\{R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\infty}([-n,n]^{d})}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. However, the pointwise estimate for
R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}
shows that given
\epsilon>0
we can find
n
large enough that
\left|R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}(x)\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{3}
for
x
outside
[-n,n]^{d}
. Combining these two facts with the triangle inequality shows that
\{R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
which is a stronger norm than
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Finally, recalling that
R_{\rho}\mathbf{f}_{n_{k}}\in{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
establishes that the limit belongs to
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

5.
Compactness of paraproducts

In this section we will deal with the compactness of the paraproducts that arise from our representation theorem. Specifically, we will prove that the membership of the symbols in
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is sufficient for the compactness of the associated paraproduct by giving an essential norm estimate. Given
(\vec{p},r)\in Q
, and an
m
-linear operator
T
, define the essential norm

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{ess}(\vec{p},r)}=\inf_{K\,\mathrm{compact}}\left\|T-K\right\|_{\vec{p},r},

and the natural modification at the endpoint which we denote by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{ess}(\infty,\mathrm{BMO})}
where the operator norm of
T-K
is measured in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty,\mathrm{BMO}}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\vartheta\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(B(0,1))
with
\int\vartheta(x)\,{\rm d}x=1
. For each
z\in Z^{d}
, set
\vartheta_{z}={\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}\vartheta
. Given
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define the
(m+1)
-linear form
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
by

\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\int_{Z^{d}}\left\langle\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\right\rangle\left\langle f_{0},\phi_{z}\right\rangle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left\langle f_{j},\vartheta_{z}\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mu(z).

\Pi_{b}
is called a paraproduct form with symbol
\mathfrak{b}
. Any
m
-linear operator
S_{\mathfrak{b}}
such that

\left\langle f_{0},S_{\mathfrak{b}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\rangle=\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\sigma}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})

for some
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m+1}
is called a paraproduct with symbol
\mathfrak{b}
.

The main goal of this section is to prove the following.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then any paraproduct
S_{\mathfrak{b}}
is a compact CZO.

Our proof of Proposition
5.2
will argue along the same lines as our proof of Proposition
4.1
from the previous section. For different line of reasoning, see
[
3
]
.

To prepare for the proof, let us review the standard boundedness theory of paraproducts, analogous to Proposition
2.8
for wavelet forms. To do so, it is convenient to view
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
as an
(m+2)
-linear form, where the extra input function is
\mathfrak{b}
itself. In fact, in this way
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})=U(\mathfrak{b},f_{0},\ldots,f_{m})
where
U
is a canonical
(m+2)
-linear wavelet form with
\left|\varepsilon_{z}\right|\lesssim 1
. Such forms are cancellative in the first and second positions, for which a slight strengthening of (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) holds: If
T
satisfies

\left\langle f_{0},T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\rangle=U(\mathfrak{b},f_{\sigma(0)},\ldots,f_{\sigma(m)})

for some
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m+1}
and canonical wavelet form
U
, then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\|T\right\|_{\vec{p},r}\leq C_{\vec{p},r}\left\|\mathfrak{b}\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\left\|T\right\|_{\infty,\mathrm{BMO}}\leq C_{\infty}\left\|\mathfrak{b}\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad(\vec{p},r)\in Q.

In particular, (
5.1
) applies to
T=S_{\mathfrak{b}}
.
A proof of (
5.1
) is omitted since it follows from standard considerations; see e.g. the proofs and comments following Propositions 2.5 and 2.7 in
[
15
]
.

Now, to give a description of
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is more amenable to
S_{\mathfrak{b}}
, let us introduce an orthonormal wavelet system
\left\{\psi_{I}\right\}_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}}
. Here
{\mathcal{D}}
is a dyadic grid on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for each
I\in{\mathcal{D}}
, set
\zeta(I)=(c(I),\ell(I))\in Z^{d}
, where
c(I)
is center of the cube
I
and
\ell(I)
the side length. Then
\psi_{I}={\mathsf{Sy}}_{\zeta(I)}\psi
for a specific
\psi\in C\Psi^{1,1;1}_{(0,1)}
. We have kept
\psi_{I}
to be
L^{1}
-normalized, so the reproducing formula is

f=\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}}|I|\left\langle f,\psi_{I}\right\rangle\psi_{I}.

Now introduce the family of orthogonal projections for
M\geq 1
,

P_{M}f=\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}}|I|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I},\quad\mathcal{D}_{M}\coloneqq\left\{I\in{\mathcal{D}}:\zeta(I)\in\mathbb{B}_{M}\right\},\quad P_{M}^{\perp}=\mathrm{Id}-P_{M}.

From
[
43
]
*Lemma 2.20, an equivalent characterization of
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is that

(5.2)

\lim_{M\to\infty}\left\|P^{\perp}_{M}\mathfrak{b}\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

Therefore, Proposition
5.2
will be a consequence of the following essential norm estimate.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
S_{\mathfrak{b}}
be a paraproduct with symbol
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then for each
(\vec{p},r)\in Q\cup\{(\infty,\mathrm{BMO})\}
,

(5.3)

\left\|S_{\mathfrak{b}}\right\|_{\mathrm{ess}(\vec{p},r)}\lesssim\liminf_{M\to\infty}\left\|P_{M}^{\perp}\mathfrak{b}\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Let
M\geq 1
large, and perform the splitting
S_{\mathfrak{b}}=R_{M}+T_{M}
where

T_{M}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\int_{Z^{d}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{100M}}\left\langle\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\right\rangle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left\langle f_{j},\vartheta_{z}\right\rangle\phi_{z}\,{\rm d}\mu(z),

and
R_{M}
is same but the integration is taken over
\mathbb{B}_{100M}
. The operator
R_{M}
is clearly compact by repeating the discussion made in §
4
to show that
R_{\rho}
there was compact. Therefore the essential norm of
S_{\mathfrak{b}}
is controlled by the operator norm of
T_{M}.
Now, for
z\not\in\mathbb{B}_{100M},
we can calculate the pairings appearing in the above equation by expanding
\mathfrak{b}
with the aid of the wavelet basis
\left\{\psi_{I}\right\}_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}}
:

\langle\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\rangle=\langle P_{M}\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\rangle+\langle P_{M}^{\perp}\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\rangle.

For the first term we will employ the linear wavelet averaging process from Lemma
2.5
. We expand

\langle P_{M}\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\rangle=\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}}|I|\langle\mathfrak{b},\psi_{I}\rangle\langle\psi_{I},\phi_{z}\rangle=\left\langle\mathfrak{b},\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}}|I|\langle\psi_{I},\phi_{z}\rangle\psi_{I}\right\rangle.

Since
\psi_{I}\in\Psi^{1,1;1}_{\zeta(I)}
and
\phi_{z}\in\Psi^{1,1;1}_{z}
, one can compute
\left|\langle\psi_{I},\phi_{z}\rangle\right|\lesssim[z,\zeta(I)]_{\frac{1}{2}}
(see e.g.
[
15
]
*Lemma 2.3 or
[
17
]
*Appendix, Lemmata 2 and 4). Furthermore, since
\zeta(I)\in\mathbb{B}_{M}
and
z\not\in\mathbb{B}_{100M}
,
[z,\zeta]_{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim M^{-\frac{1}{4}}[z,\zeta]_{\frac{1}{4}}
. Now, to apply Lemma
2.5
, rewrite

\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}}|I|\langle\psi_{I},\phi_{z}\rangle\psi_{I}=\int_{Z^{d}}H(\zeta,z)\tilde{\psi}_{\zeta}\,{\rm d}\mu(\zeta),\quad

where for each
I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}
and
\zeta=(w,t)\in I\times(\tfrac{\ell(I)}{2},\ell(I)]
, we define

\tilde{\psi}_{\zeta}=\psi_{I},\quad H(\zeta,z)=\frac{|I|}{\mu(I\times(\tfrac{\ell(I)}{2},\ell(I)])}\langle\psi_{I},\phi_{z}\rangle,

and
H(\zeta,z)=0
if
\zeta\not\in\cup_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}}I\times(\tfrac{\ell(I)}{2},\ell(I)]
. Since
\left|H(\zeta,z)\right|\lesssim M^{-\frac{1}{4}}[z,\zeta]_{\frac{1}{4}}
, Lemma
2.5
provides a universal constant
C
and
\lambda_{z}\in C\Psi_{z}^{1,\frac{1}{8};1}
such that

T_{M}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\int_{Z^{d}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{100M}}\left(M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left\langle\mathfrak{b},\lambda_{z}\right\rangle+\left\langle P_{M}^{\perp}\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\right\rangle\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left\langle f_{j},\vartheta_{z}\right\rangle\phi_{z}\,d\mu(z).

Therefore, (
5.3
) follows by the triangle inequality and (
5.1
).
∎

6.
Main result and proof

Let us now put together the pieces from the previous sections to prove the following compact
T(1)
wavelet representation theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
T
is an
m
-linear CZO,
\Lambda
the associated
(m+1)
-linear form, with
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
j=0,1,\ldots,m
satisfying (
1.2
). The following are equivalent.

A.

T
is a compact CZO in the reflexive range.

B.

For any
\varphi^{1},\ldots,\varphi^{m}\in L^{\infty}
with compact support, and every
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,

\lim_{z\to\infty}t^{md-\frac{d}{2}}\left\|T^{*,j}(\varphi^{1}_{z},\ldots,\varphi^{m}_{z})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0,\quad\varphi_{z}^{i}={\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}\varphi^{i}.

C.

\Lambda
is a compact CZ form, i.e.
\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}^{M}(z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty
and
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

D.

There exist compact wavelet forms
\{U_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K_{m}}
and compact paraproduct forms
\{\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}\}_{j=0}^{m}
such that for all
f_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\Lambda(\mathbf{f})=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{m}}U_{k}(\mathbf{f})+\sum_{j=0}^{m}\Pi^{*,j}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}(\mathbf{f}),\quad\mathbf{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m}).

E.

T^{*,j}
is a compact CZO for each
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

It is trivial that E. implies A., and the path (A.
\implies
B.
\implies
C.) is precisely the content of Proposition
3.8
.
To show C. implies D. we isolate the cancellative part of
\Lambda
, namely

\Lambda_{\mathsf{c}}=\Lambda-\Pi_{\Lambda},\quad\Pi_{\Lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\Pi^{*,j}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}.

\Lambda_{\mathsf{c}}
is definitely a CZ form, and to verify that it is cancellative, simply note that since
\left\langle\vartheta,1\right\rangle=1
, by the reproducing formula (
2.4
), for
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(\varphi,1,\ldots,1)=\int_{Z^{d}}\left\langle\mathfrak{b},\phi_{z}\right\rangle\left\langle\phi_{z},\varphi\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mu(z)=\left\langle\mathfrak{b},\varphi\right\rangle,

and since
\phi
is cancellative,
\Pi^{*,j}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\varphi,1,\ldots,1)=0
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. We want to apply Proposition
3.11
to
\Lambda_{\mathsf{c}}
so we must establish that it is a compact CZ form. Since
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by Remarks
3.3
and
3.4
,
\Pi_{\Lambda}
is a compact CZ form. Since we also know that
\Lambda
is a compact CZ form,
\Lambda_{\mathsf{c}}
must indeed be compact, and B. follows by applying Proposition
3.11
to
\Lambda_{\mathsf{c}}
. D. implies E. is a consequence of Propositions
4.1
and
5.2
.∎

A sixth equivalent condition in Theorem
2
would be the compactness of the adjoint operators on
weighted
Lebesgue spaces for weights belonging to the multilinear Muckenhoupt classes
[
24
,
32
]
. If one is only concerned with weights corresponding to
p_{j}<\infty
, then this follows immediately from
[
6
]
. Alternatively, to handle the full range of
Q
, including when some
p_{j}=\infty
, one can verify the conditions of the weighted Riesz-Kolmogorov theorem
[
6
]
*Proposition 2.9 as we did in Propositions
4.1
and
5.2
by hand.

Furthermore, let us briefly recall the notion of a compact CZ kernel, which has been ubiquitous in the compact CZO literature until
[
30
]
recently removed this condition. A compact CZ kernel satisfies the kernel estimates (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), but in addition to the uniform bound provided by
C_{K}
, one imposes an additional prefactor on the RHS,

o\left(\max\left\{\left|x_{0}-\mathbf{x}\right|_{2},\left|x_{0}-\mathbf{x}\right|_{2}^{-1},\left|x_{0}+\mathbf{x}\right|_{2}^{-1}\right\}\right).

By standard arguments
[
40
]
*Lecture 7, if one assumes
T
has a compact CZ kernel and satisfies the
diagonal
weak compactness property

(6.1)

\lim_{z\to\infty}\Lambda(\mathbf{1}_{B(w,t)},\ldots,\mathbf{1}_{B(w,t)})=0,

then condition B. in Theorem
2
holds from whence all the other conclusions follow. This perspective, using the compact kernel and (
6.1
), was recently undertaken in
[
5
]
in the bilinear setting.

We finally note that Theorem
2
applies to the linear case as well and a few simplifications can be made due to the greater symmetry enjoyed in this setting. Let us restate Theorem
2
when
m=1
for additional clarity.

Theorem 3
.

Let
T
be a linear CZO. The following are equivalent.

A.

T
is compact on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

B.

For any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}_{c}
,

\lim_{z\to\infty}t^{\frac{d}{2}}\left\|T(\varphi_{z})\right\|_{L^{2}}+t^{\frac{d}{2}}\left\|T^{*}(\varphi_{z})\right\|_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\varphi_{z}={\mathsf{Sy}}_{z}\varphi.

C.

T(1),T^{*}(1)\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{B}(\zeta)}t^{d}\left|\left\langle T\phi_{z},\phi_{\zeta}\right\rangle\right|=0.

D.

There exists a compact wavelet form
U
and compact paraproduct forms
\Pi_{T(1)},\Pi_{T^{*}(1)}
such that for all
f,g\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left\langle Tf,g\right\rangle=U(f,g)+\Pi_{T(1)}(f,g)+\Pi_{T^{*}(1)}(g,f).

E.

T
and
T^{*}
are compact CZOs.

Proof.

Only A. and C. differ from their multilinear statements in Theorem
2
. To compare C. with its
m
-linear analogue, one only needs to note that
z\in\mathbb{B}_{M}(\zeta)
is equivalent to
\zeta\in\mathbb{B}_{M^{\prime}}(z)
as long as
M\sim M^{\prime}
. Therefore, (B.
\implies
C.
\implies
D.
\implies
E.) follows from Theorem
2
. E. trivially implies A., so it remains to establish A. implies B. where the true force of the linear situation is used. Namely,
t^{\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{z}
converges weakly to zero and therefore, since
T
is compact
TT^{*}(t^{\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{z})
and
T^{*}T(t^{\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{z})
both converge strongly to zero, which implies B.
∎

Finally, we conclude with a short, simple application.

Example

Given functions
a,b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, consider the operators of multiplication on the Fourier and space sides by

T_{a}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi,\quad M_{b}f(x)=b(x)f(x).

It is easy to see that unless
a
or
b
is identically zero, such operators can never be compact. This even persists for the multilinear analogues, in particular the
m
-linear Fourier multiplier

T_{a}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}a(\xi)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\hat{f}_{j}(\xi_{j})e^{ix(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m})}\,d\xi,\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}),\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Nonetheless, by the uncertainty principle, we expect compositions of
T_{a}
and
M_{b}
to be compact. The most well-known example is in the linear case when
a,b\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then trivially
T_{a}M_{b}
is Hilbert-Schmidt under no smoothness assumptions on
a
and
b
.

We must impose some conditions on
a
and functions
b_{0},\ldots,b_{m}
so that suitable compositions of
T_{a}
and
M_{b_{j}}
are CZOs. Assume there exists
\delta>0
such that
b_{j}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
a
belongs to
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{md}\setminus\{0\})
such that

(6.2)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}a(\xi)\right|\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|},\quad\left|b_{j}(x)\right|+\frac{\left|b_{j}(x)-b_{j}(x+h)\right|}{\left|h\right|^{\delta}}\lesssim(1+\left|x\right|)^{-\delta}.

It is a routine exercise to check that under these conditions, the operator

S(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=M_{b_{0}}T_{a}(M_{b_{1}}f_{1},\ldots,M_{b_{m}}f_{m})

is an
m
-linear CZO satisfying
\mathfrak{b}_{j}\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, by Theorem
2
, compactness of
S
is characterized by
\mathsf{W}_{\Lambda}(z)\to 0
. To check that, we examine quantities of the form

(6.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\widehat{c_{0}g^{0}_{z_{0}}}(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\widehat{c_{j}g^{j}_{z_{j}}}(\xi_{j})\,d\xi,\quad z_{j}\in\mathbb{B}(z),

where
(c_{0},\ldots,c_{m})
is some permutation of
(b_{0},\ldots,b_{m})
and each
g^{i}
is either
\phi
,
\psi^{1}
, or
\psi^{3}
. One can rewrite, for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\widehat{c_{j}g^{j}_{z_{j}}}(\eta)=\left\langle e^{i\eta\cdot}c_{j},g^{j}_{z_{j}}\right\rangle

to see that for each
\xi
, the integrand in (
6.3
) goes to zero as
z\to\infty
. Then, if
a\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{md})
, by dominated convergence, the entire quantity (
6.3
) goes to zero, and
S
is a compact CZO.

Planar Turán number for balanced double stars
Xin Xu, Qiang Zhou, Tong Li, Guiying Yan

Abstract
Planar Turán number, denoted by ex 𝒫 ​ ( n , H ) \mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,H) , is the maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex planar graph which does not contain H H as a subgraph. Ghosh, Győri, Paulos and Xiao initiated the topic of the planar Turán number for double stars. For balanced double star, S 3 , 3 S_{3,3} is the only remaining graph need to be considered. In this paper, we give the exact value of ex 𝒫 ​ ( n , S 3 , 3 ) \mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{3,3}) , forcing the planar Turán number for all balanced double stars completely determined. Keywords: Planar Turán number, Balanced double stars, Extremal graphs.

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected and simple. Let
V(G)
,
v(G)
,
E(G)
,
e(G)
,
\delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)
denote the vertex set, number of vertices, edge set, number of edges, minimum degree and maximum degree of a graph

, respectively. For any subset
S\subset V(G)
, the subgraph induced on
S
is denoted by
G[S]
. We denote by
G\backslash S
the subgraph induced on
V(G)\backslash S
. If
S=\{v\}
, we simply write
G\backslash v
. We use
e[S,T]
to denote the number of edges between
S
and
T
, where
S
,
T
are subsets of
V(G)
.

Let
H
be a graph, a graph is called
H
-free if it does not contain
H
as a subgraph. One of the most classical problems in extremal graph theory, nowadays so-called Turán-type
problem is:

Problem 1.1

(Turán Problem)
.

What is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
H
-free graph
G
?

We use
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
to denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
H
-free graph. In 1941, Turán
[
24
]
gave the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{r})
and the extremal graph, where
K_{r}
is a complete graph with
r
vertices. Later in 1946, Erdős and Stone
[
5
]
extended this result by replacing
K_{r}
by an arbitrary graph
H
and showed that
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=(1-\frac{1}{\chi(H)-1}+o(1))\binom{n}{2}
, where
\chi(H)
denotes the chromatic number of
H
. This is latter called the “fundamental theorem of extremal graph theory”
[
1
]
.

In 2016, Dowden
[
3
]
initiated the study of Turán-type problems when host graphs are planar graphs:

Problem 1.2

(Planar Turán Problem)
.

What is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
H
-free planar graph
G
?

We use
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,H)
to denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
H
-free planar graph. Dowden studied the planar Turán number of
C_{4}
and
C_{5}
, where
C_{k}
is a cycle with
k
vertices. Ghosh, Győri, Martin, Paulos and Xiao
[
9
]
gave the exact value for
C_{6}
. Shi, Walsh and Yu
[
22
]
, Győri, Li and Zhou
[
12
]
gave the exact value for
C_{7}
. The planar Turán number of

is still unknown for
k\geq 8
. Cranston, Lidický, Liu and Shantanam
[
2
]
first gave both lower and upper bound for general cycles, Lan and Song
[
20
]
improved the lower bound. Recently, Shi, Walsh and Yu
[
23
]
improved the upper bound, Győri, Varga and Zhu
[
14
]
gave a new construction and improved the lower bound. Lan, Shi and Song
[
17
]
gave a sufficient condition for graphs with planar Turán number
3n-6
. We refer the interested readers to more results on paths, theta graphs and other graphs
[
16
,
18
,
11
,
15
,
26
,
6
,
7
,

,

,

,

]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
double star

S_{m,l}
is the graph obtained by taking an edge
xy
and joining
x
with
m
vertices,
y
with
l
vertices which are different from the
m
vertices.

Figure 1:
The double star
S_{m,l}
.

In 2022, Ghosh, Győri, Paulos and Xiao
[
10
]
studied the planar Turán number for
S_{2,2}
,
S_{2,3}
,
S_{2,4}
,
S_{2,5}
,
S_{3,3}
and
S_{3,4}
. Moreover, they gave the exact value for
S_{2,2}
and
S_{2,3}
. Later, The first author of this paper improved the upper bound for
S_{2,5}

[
25
]
.

We say a double star is a
balanced double star
if
m=l
. For
m\geq 4
,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{m,m})=3n-6
since a double wheel graph (a graph with two non-adjacent vertices connecting to all vertices on the cycle
C_{n-2}
) does not contain
S_{m,m}
as a subgraph. Since
S_{1,1}
is a path on 4 vertices and every graph without a path on 4 vertices must be a planar graph, the planar Turán number of
S_{1,1}
is equal to its Turán number. By the result of Faudree and Schelp
[
8
]
,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{1,1})=\mathrm{ex}(n,S_{1,1})\leq n
and the equality holds for
3|n
. Ghosh, Győri, Paulos and Xiao
[
10
]
proved that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{2,2})=2n-4
when
n\geq 16
and
\lfloor 5n/2\rfloor-5\leq\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{3,3})\leq\lfloor 5n/2\rfloor-2
when
n\geq 3
. Moreover, they gave the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{3,3})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}3n-6&\text{if }3\leq n\leq 7,\\
16&\text{if }n=8,\\
18&\text{if }n=9,\\
\lfloor 5n/2\rfloor-5&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

In this paper, we solve this conjecture by a new method and thus the planar Turán number for all balanced double stars is determined:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any integer
n\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{m,m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n&\text{if }m=1\text{ and }3|n,\\
n-1&\text{if }m=1\text{ and }3\nmid n,\\
2n-4&\text{if }m=2\text{ and }n\geq 16,\\
\lfloor 5n/2\rfloor-5&\text{if }m=3\text{ and }n\geq 10,\\
3n-6&\text{if }m\geq 4.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

Definition 2.5
.

Let

be a subgraph of

. The

\mathcal{B}
of

is the set consisting of star-blocks satisfying:

(a)

;

(b)

\forall B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
, if

. Moreover, the common peripheral vertices are called

It should be noticed that for any shared vertex

. In fact, if

,

belongs to one star-block and can not be shared. If

, then an

is easily found.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
. If
\mathcal{B}
is a star-block base of
G_{1}
and any vertex in
V(G_{2})
has degree at most
4
in

, then we say

has a
star-block partition
.
Let
d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)
be the number of star-blocks in base
\mathcal{B}
containing

.
For any star-block
B\in\mathcal{B}
, let
d_{s}(B)
denote the number of shared vertices of degree at most
3
in
B
and let
d_{s^{\prime}}(B)
denote the number of shared vertices of degree
4
in
B
.

It is easy to see that
G
must have a star-block partition. Specially, if
\Delta(G)\leq 4
, then
G=G_{2}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
H
be a subgraph of
G
. The
primary weight
of
H
, denoted by
\boldsymbol{w_{0}(H)}
, is defined as

\boldsymbol{w_{0}(H)}\coloneqq e(H)+\frac{1}{2}(e[H,G\backslash H])=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(H)}d(v).

Definition 2.8
.

Let
G
have a star-block partition and a star-block base
\mathcal{B}
. For any
B\in\mathcal{B}
,
the
modified weight
of
B
, denoted by
\boldsymbol{w(B)}
, is defined as

\boldsymbol{w(B)}\coloneqq w_{0}(B)+\frac{s}{2}+\frac{s^{\prime}}{4}+\mathbf{1}_{B},

where
s=d_{s}(B)
,
s^{\prime}=d_{s^{\prime}}(B)
, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{1}_{B}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if }d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=3\text{ for some }v\in B,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right.\end{split}

Obviously, if there is a star-block partition
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
, then
e(G)=w_{0}(G)=w_{0}(G_{1})+w_{0}(G_{2})
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
G
have a star-block base
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,T\}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be the star-block base obtained from
\mathcal{B}
satisfying:
(a)

|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{B}|
;
(b)
for all
1\leq i\leq T
, there exist
B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
and
B^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
such that
V(B_{i})\subset V(B^{\prime}_{i})
and
w(B^{\prime}_{i})/v(B^{\prime}_{i})\leq w(B_{i})/v(B_{i})
, where at least one inequality is strict.
Then we say
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is the
refinement
of
\mathcal{B}
.

For the sake of convenience in subsequent discussion, we categorize the star-blocks into three types:

\bullet

\mathcal{B}_{0}\coloneqq\{B\in\mathcal{B}\ |\ d_{s}(B)+d_{s^{\prime}}(B)=0\}
,

\bullet

\mathcal{B}_{1}\coloneqq\{B\in\mathcal{B}\ |\ d_{s}(B)+d_{s^{\prime}}(B)\geq 1\ and\ d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)\leq 2\ for\ each\ v\in B\}
,

\bullet

\mathcal{B}_{2}\coloneqq\{B\in\mathcal{B}\ |\ d_{s}(B)+d_{s^{\prime}}(B)\geq 1\ and\ d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=3\ for\ some\ v\in B\}
.

We show that
w(B)
can be constrained for each star-block in some base
\mathcal{B}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be an
S_{3,3}
-free planar graph on
n
vertices. Then there exists a star-block partition
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
such that all star-blocks in the base
\mathcal{B}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\begin{split}w(B)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{5}{2}v(B)-\frac{5}{2}&\text{when }B\in\mathcal{B}_{0},\\
\frac{5}{2}v(B)-\frac{5}{t}&\text{when }B\in\mathcal{B}_{1},\\
\frac{5}{2}v(B)-1&\text{when }B\in\mathcal{B}_{2},\end{array}\right.\end{split}

where
t=|\mathcal{B}_{1}|+|\mathcal{B}_{2}|
.

This lemma is the highlight of this paper, which make it possible to prove the following two lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be an
S_{3,3}
-free planar graph on
n\geq 7
vertices. If
G
contains only one star-block, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}e(G)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}15&\text{when }n=7,\\
16&\text{when }n=8,\\
18&\text{when }n=9,\\
5n/2-5&\text{when }n\geq 10.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be an
S_{3,3}
-free planar graph with a star-block partition
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
and a star-block base
\mathcal{B}
. If
|\mathcal{B}|\geq 2
, then
e(G)\leq 5n/2-5
.

Together with the extremal graphs achieving the bound, Theorem
1.1
can be deduced from these two lemmas, which will be proved in the following sections.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
and Theorem
1.1

In this section, we first prove the Lemma
2.3
, and then provide the proof of upper bound in theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Note that if there is a vertex of degree at least
5
, then it must be contained in some star-block. Since
G
has a star-block partition
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
, it follows that

e(G)=w_{0}(G_{1})+w_{0}(G_{2})\leq w_{0}(G_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{v\in V(G_{2})}4=w_{0}(G_{1})+2v(G_{2}).

It suffices to prove that

w_{0}(G_{1})\leq\frac{5}{2}v(G_{1})-5.

Recall that
G_{1}
has a star-block base
\mathcal{B}
. Let

•

r_{1}\coloneqq\big|\{v\in V(G_{1}):d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=2\ and\ d(v)\leq 3\}\big|
,

•

r_{2}\coloneqq\big|\{v\in V(G_{1}):d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=2\ and\ d(v)=4\}\big|
,

•

r_{3}\coloneqq\big|\{v\in V(G_{1}):d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=3\}\big|
.

Then we have

\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}w_{0}(B)=w_{0}(G_{1})+\frac{3}{2}r_{1}+2r_{2}+3r_{3}.

Let
t_{0}=|\mathcal{B}_{0}|
,
t_{1}=|\mathcal{B}_{1}|
,
t_{2}=|\mathcal{B}_{2}|
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}w_{0}(B)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}(w(B)-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{s^{\prime}}{4}-\mathbf{1}_{B})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}(w(B))-\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{s^{\prime}}{4}+\mathbf{1}_{B})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{2}\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}v(B)-\frac{5}{2}t_{0}-\frac{5t_{1}}{t}-t_{2}-(r_{1}+\frac{1}{2}r_{2}+\frac{3}{2}r_{3}+t_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{2}(v(G_{1})+r_{1}+r_{2}+2r_{3})-\frac{5}{2}t_{0}-\frac{5t_{1}}{t}-t_{2}-(r_{1}+\frac{1}{2}r_{2}+\frac{3}{2}r_{3}+t_{2}).

Combining the results, we conclude that

w_{0}(G_{1})\leq\frac{5}{2}v(G_{1})+(\frac{1}{2}r_{3}-t_{2})-\frac{5}{2}t_{0}-\frac{5t_{1}}{t}-t_{2}.

Next we will show that
r_{3}\leq 2t_{2}-4
when
t_{2}\neq 0
. Recall that
r_{3}
is the number of shared vertices in
G_{1}
with
d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=3
and
t_{2}
is the number of star-blocks in
\mathcal{B}
containing such type of vertices. We construct an auxiliary bipartite graph
(X,Y)
such that
|X|=r_{3}
and
|Y|=t_{2}
. Each vertex in
X
represents a vertex
v
in
G_{1}
with
d_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=3
and each vertex in
Y
represents a star-block in
\mathcal{B}_{2}
. Moreover, the edge
xy
means that
x
is contained in the star-block
y
for
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. It is easy to check that the auxiliary bipartite graph is a planar graph since
G
is a planar graph and every vertex in
X
has degree exactly
3
. So we have
3r_{3}\leq 2(r_{3}+t_{2})-4
. This implies
r_{3}\leq 2t_{2}-4
.

Hence we have

\displaystyle w_{0}(G_{1})\leq\frac{5}{2}v(G_{1})-2\cdot\mathbf{1}_{t_{2}}-\frac{5}{2}t_{0}-\frac{5t_{1}}{t}-t_{2},

where
\mathbf{1}_{t_{2}}
is the characteristic function of
t_{2}
.

If
t_{2}>0
, then
t_{2}\geq 3
. It follows that
w_{0}(G_{1})\leq\frac{5}{2}v(G_{1})-5
. Assume that
t_{2}=0
. If
t_{1}>0
, then
w_{0}(G_{1})\leq\frac{5}{2}v(G_{1})-5
since
t_{1}=t_{1}+t_{2}=t
. If
t_{1}=t_{2}=0
, then
w_{0}(G_{1})\leq\frac{5}{2}v(G_{1})-5
since
t_{0}=|\mathcal{B}|\geq 2
.

It should be noted that we did not consider vertices of degree 2 in our proof. In fact, the calculations for
w(B)
were based on the assumption that all vertices in
B
have degree at least
3
. Assume there exists a
2
-degree vertex
w
in
G
. When
d_{\mathcal{B}}(w)=1
, it can be easily verified. When
d_{\mathcal{B}}(w)=2
, we show that this will not affect our proof either.

Note that the core of the proof is the following inequality

w_{0}(G_{1})+\frac{3}{2}r_{1}+2r_{2}+3r_{3}\leq\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}(w(B)-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{s^{\prime}}{4}-\mathbf{1}_{B}).

If there exists a shared vertex
w
of degree
2
, then the value of
w_{0}(G_{1})+\frac{3}{2}r_{1}+2r_{2}+3r_{3}
would be reduced by
1/2
. However
w(B)
would be reduced by
1/2
if
w\in B
. And there are two star-blocks containing
w
. Thus
\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{B}}(w(B)-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{s^{\prime}}{4}-\mathbf{1}_{B})
would be reduced by at least
1
. Hence, ignoring each shared vertex of degree

results in an increase of
\frac{1}{2}
to the value on the left side of the inequality, and an increase of
1
to the right side. This does not affect our calculation above.
∎

Now we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Given any planar graph
G
on

obviously. Thus

.

By Lemma
2.2
and Lemma

When

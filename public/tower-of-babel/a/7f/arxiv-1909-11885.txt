Hitchin fibrations, abelian surfaces, and the P=W conjecture
Mark Andrea de Cataldo, Davesh Maulik, Junliang Shen

Abstract
. We study the topology of Hitchin fibrations via abelian surfaces. We establish the P=W conjecture for genus 2 2 curves and arbitrary rank. In higher genus and arbitrary rank, we prove that P=W holds for the subalgebra of cohomology generated by even tautological classes. Furthermore, we show that all tautological generators lie in the correct pieces of the perverse filtration as predicted by the P=W conjecture. In combination with recent work of Mellit, this reduces the full conjecture to the multiplicativity of the perverse filtration. Our main technique is to study the Hitchin fibration as a degeneration of the Hilbert–Chow morphism associated with the moduli space of certain torsion sheaves on an abelian surface, where the symmetries induced by Markman’s monodromy operators play a crucial role.

0.5.
Idea of the proof

The key idea in proving our results
is to first study perverse filtrations for certain
compact
hyperkähler manifolds, motivated in part by earlier work of the third author and Yin
[
45
]
.
That is, we consider an abelian surface
A
with ample curve class
\beta\in H_{2}(A,{\mathbb{Z}})
, and prove analogs of
our main result for the moduli space

{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A}
of one-dimensional sheaves with support in the class
\beta
; see Theorem
2.1
for the precise statement.
The advantage of the compact geometry is that we can use
results of Markman
[
41
]
on monodromy operators for
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A}
. These are symmetries, arising from parallel transport and Fourier-Mukai transforms, that do not appear in the Hitchin setting and which heavily constrain tautological classes of universal families. We show that these operators relate perverse filtrations and tautological classes for different choices of
\beta
and, in particular, allow us to pass from the case of imprimitive
\beta
to primitive
\beta
, which can be studied directly using
[
52
,
46
]
.

In order to apply this to Hitchin fibrations, we consider the degeneration to the normal cone of an embedding
of a genus
g
curve
C
into an abelian surface

j_{C}:C\hookrightarrow A,

and study the specialization map on cohomology of the associated moduli spaces. In general, there is a great deal of information loss in this specialization because the family is non-proper. Nevertheless, we are able to show its compatibility with tautological classes and perverse splittings. Finally we use these compatibilities to deduce our main theorems for such a curve
C
; this implies our results hold for all curves in view of
[
15
]
.

\ \widetilde{G}_{k}H^{d}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta^{\prime},A^{\prime}},{\mathbb{C}})=\phi(\widetilde{G}_{k}H^{d}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A},{\mathbb{C}}))

also splits the perverse filtration for
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta^{\prime},A^{\prime}}
, and the corresponding tautological classes lie in its correct pieces by condition (iii) in the definition of isomorphism of perverse package.
∎

If
(A,\beta)
is perversely isomorphic to
(A^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
, then condition (ii) implies that

(57)

\beta^{2}=\mathrm{dim}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A})-2=\mathrm{dim}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta^{\prime},A^{\prime}})-2=\beta^{\prime 2}.

The following theorem, which we deduce from Theorem

, establishes the converse
to (

are larger than the equivalence classes
given by the deformation types of the

Theorem 2.8
.

Any two pairs
(A,\beta)
and

satisfying

\beta^{2}=\beta^{\prime 2}

are perversely isomorphic.

Proof.

As we explain at the end of the proof, it will be sufficient to find an isomorphism of graded
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector spaces

(58)

g:H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{C}})\to H^{*}(A^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}})

satisfying the following two properties:

(a)

g
preserves the intersection pairing;

(b)

g
satisfies the following identities

(59)

\begin{split}g(0,0,1)&=(0,0,1),\\
g(1,0,0)&=(1,0,0),\\
g(0,\beta,0)&=(0,\beta^{\prime},0),\end{split}

and a graded
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra isomorphism

(60)

\phi:H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A},{\mathbb{C}})\to H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta^{\prime},A^{\prime}},{\mathbb{C}})

satisfying condition (iii) in the definition of isomorphism of perverse packages.

We construct
(g,\phi)
in two steps.

We note that

, and

constructed below automatically satisfy condition (a)
by construction.

Firstly, since the Mukai vectors (

v_{\beta}
and

are primitive with the same Mukai length,

we can apply
[

(which follows from Yoshioka
[

]
) to find

g_{1}:H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(A^{\prime},{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad\phi_{1}:H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta^{\prime},A^{\prime}},{\mathbb{Q}})

such that
g_{1}(v_{\beta})=v_{\beta^{\prime}}
and

(61)

(g_{1}\otimes\phi_{1})\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{F}}_{\beta})=\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{F}}_{\beta^{\prime}})

with
[{\mathcal{F}}_{\beta^{\prime}}]
a universal class on

. Moreover, the morphism

satisfies the evident variant of Remark

,

If

In particular, for any class

Splittings of perverse filtrations

3.1.
Overview

In this section, we study splittings of the perverse filtration associated with a proper surjective morphism
\pi:X\to Y
with
X
and
Y
nonsingular. As an application, we strengthen Theorem
2.1
by requiring that the decomposition given by said theorem

(63)

H^{\ast}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A},{\mathbb{C}})=\bigoplus_{i,d}\widetilde{G}_{i}H^{d}({\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,A},{\mathbb{C}}).

is induced by a “Lefschetz class” via the mechanisms introduced in
[
21
,
11
,
20
]
; see Theorem
3.8
. As discussed in Remark
4.5
, this is crucial in the study of the specialization morphism (
82
) in Section 4.

Throughout, we work with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-coefficients except for Section
3.5
, where we need to switch to
{\mathbb{C}}
-coefficients in order to apply results in Section
2.7
. However, we note that all discussions in

3.2
–

remain valid if we replace
{\mathbb{Q}}
by

.

3.2.
Lefschetz classes

We consider the perverse filtration associated with
\pi:X\to Y
,

(64)

P_{0}H^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\subset P_{1}H^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\dots\subset P_{2{R}}H^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),

where
{R}
is the defect of semismallness of
\pi
(
13
). The action of a class
\eta\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
on the cohomology
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
via cup product satisfies

(65)

\eta:P_{k}H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to P_{k+2}H^{i+2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),

which further induces an action on

{\mathbb{H}}=\bigoplus_{\star,\bullet\geq 0}\mathrm{Gr}_{\star}^{P}H^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{\star,\bullet\geq 0}\left(P_{\star}H^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})/P_{\star-1}H^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\right).

We say that
\eta\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is a
\pi
-Lefschetz class
if its induced action on
{\mathbb{H}}
satisfies the hard Lefschetz-type condition in the sense of
[
11
, Assumption 2.3.1]
,
i.e.
, the actions

(66)

\eta^{k}:\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{{R}-k}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{{R}+k}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\forall k\geq 0,

are isomorphisms. As a typical example, the relative Hard Lefschetz Theorem
[
7
]
with respect to
\pi:X\to Y
implies that a relatively ample class for
\pi
is a
\pi
-Lefschetz class. The following lemma gives examples of Lefschetz classes other than relatively ample classes.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
m
. Any class
\eta\in H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfying

(67)

\eta^{m}\neq 0

is a Lefschetz class with respect to
\pi:A\to\mathrm{pt}
.

Proof.

Let
W=H^{1}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
. We identify
H^{k}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
with
\wedge^{k}W
, and therefore
\eta\in\wedge^{2}W
. The condition (
67
) implies that
\eta
defines a (constant) symplectic form on
W^{*}
. In particular, we can write
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{i}\wedge f_{i}
under a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{m},f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
of
W
. Hence the pair
(W\otimes{\mathbb{C}},\eta)
is the tensor product of
m
copies of the symplectic plane
({\mathbb{C}}^{2},e\wedge f)
, for which the statement is clear.
∎

Recall the Hitchin fibration
h:{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\to\Lambda
. The following proposition is a numerical criterion for Lefschetz classes with respect to
h
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
F
be a closed fiber of
h
. A class
\eta\in H^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})
is a Lefschetz class with respect to
h
if and only if the following cap product with the fundamental class of the connected fiber
F
(counting components with multiplicities) is non-trivial:

(68)

0\neq\eta^{\mathrm{dim(F)}}\cap[F]\in H_{0}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}.

Proof.

We denote by
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
the corresponding moduli space of stable
\mathrm{PGL}_{r}
-Higgs bundles with

\hat{h}:\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\to\hat{\Lambda}

the Hitchin fibration. By
[
30
,
37
]
, we have

H^{2}(\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbb{Q}}\cdot\alpha

where
\alpha
is the first Chern class of an
\hat{h}
-ample line bundle on
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
, and therefore is
a
\hat{h}
-Lefschetz class. By the discussion in
[
13
, Section 2.4]
, we have an isomorpism

(69)

H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{*}(\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes H^{*}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),{\mathbb{Q}})

satisfying that

(70)

P_{k}H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{i+j=k}P_{i}H^{*}(\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes H^{j}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),{\mathbb{Q}}).

Under the identification

H^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{2}(\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus H^{2}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),{\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbb{Q}}\cdot\alpha\oplus H^{2}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)),

induced by (
69
), we can express any class
\eta\in H^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})
as

(71)

\eta=\mu{\alpha\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi},\quad\mu\in{\mathbb{Q}},~\xi\in H^{2}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),{\mathbb{Q}}).

Lemma
3.1
combined with
[
11
, Appendix]
implies that the class
\eta
is Lefschetz if and only if

(72)

\mu\neq 0,\quad\xi^{g}\neq 0.

Therefore, it suffices to show that (
72
) is equivalent to the condition (
68
).

We first consider traceless Higgs bundles

{\mathcal{M}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Dol}}=\{({\mathcal{E}},\theta)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}:\mathrm{trace}(\theta)=0\}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}.

Recall from
[
13
, Proposition 2.4.3]
that there is a finite morphism

q:\check{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)\to{\mathcal{M}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Dol}}

with
\check{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
the corresponding Dolbeault moduli space of stable
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
-Higgs bundles. The preimage of a closed fiber
F\subset{\mathcal{M}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
of the restricted Hitchin fibration
h|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Dol}}}
is the product

q^{-1}(F)=\check{F}\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),

where
\check{F}
is the corresponding closed fiber of the
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
Hitchin fibration. The pullback of a class (
71
) along

\check{F}\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)\hookrightarrow\check{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)\xrightarrow{q}{\mathcal{M}}^{0}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}

is of the type

\check{\eta}=\mu\check{\alpha}\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi\in H^{2}(\check{F},{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus H^{2}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),{\mathbb{Q}})\subset H^{2}(\check{F}\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),{\mathbb{Q}})

with
\check{\alpha}
an ample class on
\check{F}
. Since
q:\check{F}\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)\to F
is finite
and surjective, the condition (
68
) is equivalent to

0\neq\check{\eta}^{\mathrm{dim}(\check{F}\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C))}\cap[\check{F}]=\check{\eta}^{\mathrm{dim}(F)}\cap[\check{F}],

which, in turn, is equivalent to (
72
). This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
The first Deligne splitting

Given a projective morphism
\pi:X\to Y
and a
\pi
-ample line bundle on
X
, Deligne
[
21
]

constructs three splittings of the direct image
R\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}
(resp.
R\pi_{*}\mathrm{IC}_{X}
if
X
is singular), which induce splittings of the perverse filtration on the (resp. intersection) cohomology groups
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. In this paper, we need the variant
[
11
]
of this construction
where one starts with a
\pi
-Lefschetz class
\eta\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
,
i.e.
one that does not necessarily satisfy the relative Hard Lefschetz Theorem in the derived category
D^{b}_{c}(Y)
, but nonetheless satisfies the cohomological version (
66
).
We need only the first of the resulting three splittings, which we name the
first Deligne splitting

(
[
21
,
20
,
11
]
).

According to
[
11
]
, the first Deligne splitting of the perverse filtration (
64
)

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{i}G_{i}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

associated with the
\pi
-Lefschetz class
\eta
(
i.e.
(
66
) holds)
can be described using
only
the action of
\eta
on
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. We explain this description explicitly as follows.

For
i\geq 0
, we let
\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{i}:=P_{i}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})/P_{i-1}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
denote the graded spaces, which are subquotients of cohomology, and let
G_{i}:=G_{i}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
denote the corresponding image of
\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{i}
via the first Deligne splitting, i.e. these are the summands in the last paragraph, which are subspaces of cohomology that we want to characterize.

For
0\leq k\leq{R}
, let

\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{k}\supseteq Q^{\prime}_{k}:=\ker\{\eta^{{R}-k+1}:\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{k}\to\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{2{R}-k+2}\}

be the associated graded-primitive spaces (here
\eta
acts on the graded spaces of the perverse filtration on cohomology).
Let
Q_{k}\subseteq G_{k}
be the image of
Q^{\prime}_{k}
via the first Deligne splitting. We have

G_{k}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\eta^{i}Q_{k-2i}
for
0\leq k\leq{R}
, and
G_{{R}+\kappa}=\eta^{k}G_{{R}-\kappa}

(here
\eta
acts on cohomology).

It follows that, in order to have a complete description of the first Deligne splitting that involves solely
the action of
\eta
in cohomology, we only need to describe
Q_{k}\subseteq H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
in such terms.

We describe
Q_{k}
following
[
20
, Section 2.7]
. Note that its context is the one of the fist Deligne splitting arising from working in the derived category, but the proof works verbatim in the present context of cohomology acted upon by a
\pi
-Lefschetz class. By (
65
), we have

\eta^{j}P_{k}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\subset P_{k+2j}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Let
\Phi_{0}(\eta)
be the composition of the morphisms

\Phi_{0}(\eta):P_{k}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\eta^{{R}-k+1}}P_{2{R}-k+2}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2{R}-k+2}^{P}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),

where the first map is cup product and the second map is the projection to the graded piece. We obtain the sub-vector space

\mathrm{Ker}(\Phi_{0}(\eta))\subset P_{k}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

The morphisms
\Phi_{m}(\eta)
are defined inductively for
m\geq 0
as follows:

\Phi_{m}(\eta):\mathrm{Ker}(\Phi_{m-1}(\eta))\xrightarrow{\eta^{{R}-k+m}}P_{2{R}-k+2m}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2{R}-k+2m}^{P}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Therefore for fixed
k
, we obtain a sequence of sub-vector spaces

\cdots\subset\mathrm{Ker}(\Phi_{1}(\eta))\subset\mathrm{Ker}(\Phi_{0}(\eta))\subset P_{k}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

According to
[
20
, Proposition 2.7.1]
, we have the desired description

Q_{k}=Q_{k}(\eta)=\mathrm{Ker}(\Phi_{k}(\eta))\subset P_{k}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\forall 0\leq k\leq{R}.

The description of the first Deligne splitting yields immediately the following comparison lemma, which expresses a kind of functoriality for the first Deligne splitting.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X_{i}

(i=1,2)
be nonsingular varieties with proper surjective morphisms
f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y_{i}
, and let
P_{\star}H^{*}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})
be the corresponding perverse filtrations. Let

\phi:H^{*}(X_{1},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Q}})

be a morphism
of graded
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebras satisfying

(a)

\phi(P_{k}H^{d}(X_{1},{\mathbb{Q}}))\subset P_{k}H^{d}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Q}})
;

(b)

\phi(\eta_{1})=\eta_{2}
, with
\eta_{i}\in H^{2}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})
an
f_{i}
-Lefschetz class for
i=1,2
.

Then we have

\phi(G_{k}H^{*}(X_{1},{\mathbb{Q}}))\subset G_{k}H^{*}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\forall k\geq 0

with
H^{*}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})=\oplus_{k\geq 0}G_{k}H^{*}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})
the first Deligne splitting associated with
\eta_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

Proof.

It follows from the description of the first Deligne splittings

H^{*}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{0\leq k\leq r}\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}\left(\eta^{j}_{i}Q_{k-2j}(\eta_{i})\bigoplus\eta^{r-k+j}_{i}Q_{k-2j}(\eta_{i})\right)\quad i=1,2

summarized above, and the fact that the sub-vector spaces

\mathrm{Ker}(\Phi_{k}(\eta_{i}))=Q_{k}(\eta_{i})\subset P_{k}H^{*}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})

are preserved under the
P
-filtered morphism
\phi
.
∎

3.4.
Semismall maps and Hilbert schemes

In this section, we study the situation where our morphism
\pi:X\to Y
can be factored as

X\xrightarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{g}Y

with
f:X\to Z
semismall and surjective; in particular,
f
is generically finite and
\dim(X)=\dim(Z)
. We further assume that there are closed irreducible sub-varieties
Z_{i}\subset Z
such that the decomposition theorem for
f
takes the form of a canonical finite direct sum decomposition

(74)

Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{IC}_{Z_{i}}[-\mathrm{dim}(X)],

where each
\mathrm{IC}_{Z_{i}}
is the (perverse) intersection cohomology complex of
Z_{i}
. Note that we have the following identity concerning intersection cohomology groups

\mathrm{IH}^{d}(Z_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{d-\dim Z_{i}}(Z_{i},\mathrm{IC}_{Z_{i}})
.

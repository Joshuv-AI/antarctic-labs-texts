Higher Gauge Theory and Integrability
Hank Chen, Joaquin Liniado

Abstract
In recent years, significant progress has been made in the study of integrable systems from a gauge theoretic perspective. This development originated with the introduction of 4 4 d Chern-Simons theory with defects, which provided a systematic framework for constructing two-dimensional integrable systems. In this article, we propose a novel approach to studying higher-dimensional integrable models employing techniques from higher category theory. Starting with higher Chern-Simons theory on the 4 4 -manifold ℝ × Y \mathbb{R}\times Y , we complexify and compactify the real line to ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}P^{1} and introduce the disorder defect ω = z − 1 ​ d ​ z \omega=z^{-1}\mathrm{d}z . This procedure defines a holomorphic five-dimensional variant of higher Chern-Simons theory, which, when endowed with suitable boundary conditions, allows for the localisation to a three-dimensional theory on Y Y . The equations of motion of the resulting model are equivalent to the flatness of a 2 2 -connection ( L , H ) (L,H) , that we then use to construct the corresponding higher holonomies. We prove that these are invariants of homotopies relative boundary, which enables the construction of conserved quantities. The latter are labelled by both the categorical characters of a Lie crossed-module and the infinite number of homotopy classes of surfaces relative boundary in Y Y . Moreover, we also demonstrate that the 3 3 d theory has left and right acting symmetries whose current algebra is given by an infinite dimensional centrally extended affine Lie 2-algebra. Both of these conditions are direct higher homotopy analogues of the properties satisfied by the 2d Wess-Zumino-Witten CFT, which we therefore interpret as facets of integrable structures.

Introduction

Integrable models represent a unique domain of exploration where complex systems exhibit a remarkable level of order. This is typically expressed through an infinite number of symmetries, which in turn, allow for the construction of an infinite number of independently conserved charges. The existence of such a large number of conserved quantities imposes stringent constraints on the system, which may render it soluble to some extent; it is precisely this solvable character what makes integrable systems so special.

However, the strength of integrable theories is also their greatest weakness: identifying the infinite number of conserved charges is at the same time, the most complicated challenge. Significant progress was made in this direction with the introduction of the Lax formalism
[
1
]
for two-dimensional models. Indeed, this framework provides a systematic way to identify and construct the conserved charges. Specifically, in the case of two dimensional field theories, one looks for a
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}

Similarly, in the context of four-dimensional systems, integrability has also been expressed in terms of the
on-shell
flatness of some connection; the prototypical example being the anti-self-dual Yang-Mills (ASDYM) equations. In fact, it has long been established that two-dimensional integrable models arise as symmetry reductions of the ASDYM equations. Of course, anti-self-duality is not quite a flatness condition on the curvature, but instead, the requirement
F=-\star F

[
2

identifies solutions to the ASDYM equations on four-dimensional space-time, with holomorphic vector bundles over twistor space. In particular, for a vector bundle to be holomorphic, the corresponding connection must be flat.

Despite the inherent elegance of these geometric characterizations of integrability, both suffer from the same issue: although they provide a systematic procedure for constructing the conserved charges once the on-shell flat connection is found, they offer no instruction whatsoever on how to find the connection in the first place.

Definition 2.4
.

We define the
2-Maurer-Cartan action
as the action functional

S_{\text{2MC}}[\mathcal{A}]\coloneqq\sum_{m=1}^{2}\frac{1}{(m+1)!}\int_{X}\langle\mathcal{A},\ell_{m}(\mathcal{A},\dots,\mathcal{A})\rangle\,.

(2.8)

In particular, for an arbitrary variation
\delta{\cal A}
we find that
\delta S_{\mathrm{2MC}}[{\cal A}]=0
if and only if
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{A})=0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
{\cal A}=(A,B)\in\mathscr{L}_{1}
consists of a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form
A
and an
\mathfrak{h}
-valued
2
-form
B
. Similarly
\mathscr{F}=(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})\in\mathscr{L}_{2}
consists of a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
2
-form
{\cal F}
and an
\mathfrak{h}
-valued
3
-form
{\cal G}
. Since the pairing on the balanced Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
pairs elements of
\mathfrak{g}
with those of
\mathfrak{h}
, we conclude that the integrand in (
2.8
) is a
4
-form, and thus
X
must be a
4
-manifold. This explains why dg Lie algebras (dgla’s), at the very least, are
required
to define a 4d analogue of Chern-Simons theory.

Going back to (
2.8
), we make use of the invariance property (
2.5
) to bring the 2-Maurer-Cartan action into the form

\displaystyle S_{\text{2CS}}[\mathcal{A}]=\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A,A]-\mu_{1}(B),B\rangle\,,

(2.9)

which is what we shall refer to in the rest of the paper as
2-Chern-Simons theory
(2CS)
[
60
]
. This theory is also called the "4d BF-BB theory" in some literature
[
79
,
80
,
81
]
. By construction, the equations of motion of (
2.9
) will be precisely
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{A})=0
. Explicitly, it is simple to check that

\displaystyle\delta_{B}S_{\text{2CS}}[A,B]=0\,:

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A,A]-\mu_{1}(B)=0\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{A}S_{\text{2CS}}[A,B]=0\,:

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{G}=\mathrm{d}B+\mu_{2}(A,B)=0\,.

2.2
Gauge Symmetries of 2CS Theory

As in any gauge theory, we will be interested in gauge symmetries of the action. These are given by a higher analogue of usual gauge transformations and are characterized by a smooth map
h\in C^{\infty}(X,G)
and a
1
-form
\Gamma\in\Omega^{1}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{h}
which act on the gauge fields as
[
52
]

\displaystyle

\displaystyle A\mapsto A^{(h,\Gamma)}=\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}A+h^{-1}\mathrm{d}h+\mu_{1}(\Gamma)

(2.10)

\displaystyle B\mapsto B^{(h,\Gamma)}=h^{-1}\rhd B+\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\mu_{2}(A^{(h,\Gamma)},\Gamma)-\tfrac{1}{2}[\Gamma,\Gamma]\,.

We call the simultaneous transformations of the gauge fields a
2
-gauge transformation
. Note that the above expressions correspond to finite gauge transformations of the gauge fields, which in this article will play a crucial role. In the literature people often consider only infinitesimal gauge transformations, which can be obtained by a formal expansion
h\sim 1+\alpha
and neglecting terms quadratic in
\alpha
and
\Gamma
, see for instance
Prop. 2.9
of
[
52
]
. With a simple computation, it can be shown that under these gauge transformations, the fake-curvature and the
2
-curvature transform covariantly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(A^{(h,\Gamma)},B^{(h,\Gamma)}\big)=\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{F}(A,B)\,,\qquad\mathcal{G}\big(A^{(h,\Gamma)},B^{(h,\Gamma)}\big)=h^{-1}\rhd\mathcal{G}(A,B)+\mu_{2}(\mathcal{F}(A,B),\Gamma)\,,

whence the transformations (
2.10
) leave the subspace
\mathsf{MC}\subset\Omega^{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{G}
of Maurer-Cartan elements invariant.

Remark 2.3
.

Notice (
2.10
) includes a class of transformations that translates the 1-form field. As such, the value of
A
is only defined modulo the image of
\mu_{1}
up to 2-gauge redundancy. However, to properly treat this seemingly large gauge freedom in the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) formalism, one would have to make use of
homotopy transfer
. Given the BV(-BRST)
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathscr{L}
encoding all of the fields in the theory, it was argued in
[
82
]
that integrating out degrees of freedom is equivalent to projecting onto its cohomology
H(\mathscr{L})
. If
H(\mathscr{L})\neq 0
is not trivial, then the homotopy transfer theorem states that higher homotopy brackets — in particular a trilinear tertiary "homotopy map"
\tilde{\ell}_{3}

[
83
]
— will be induced on
H(\mathscr{L})
through this projection
[
84
,
82
]
.

We shall explain the mathematics behind homotopy transfer in §
B
. But the upshot is that integrating out/gauging this shift symmetry
A\rightarrow A+\mu_{1}\Gamma
away will in general lead to a non-trivial deformation of the theory, and the 2-Chern-Simons theory will end up differing perturbatively from the 4d BF theory. Though if
\mu_{1}

were
invertible, then
A
can indeed be completely gauged away. In this case, the cohomology of the BV-BRST complex
\mathscr{L}
would be trivial, hence performing a homotopy transfer to project away the field
A
leads to a trivial theory, at least perturbatively. In this case, the 2CS theory becomes the so-called 4d BF-BB theory, which is conjectured to coincide with the Crane-Yetter-Broda TQFT
[
79
]
that hosts only non-perturbative degrees-of-freedom. It is sensitive only to the (
G
-)bordism class of the underlying 4-manifold.

We are interested in the variation of 2CS theory under a gauge transformation, which we describe in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the 2-gauge transformations (
2.10
) the 2CS action transforms as

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{2CS}}\big[A^{(h,\Gamma)},B^{(h,\Gamma)}\big]

\displaystyle=S_{\mathrm{2CS}}[A,B]

(2.11)

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\mathrm{d}\big[\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}F(A),\Gamma\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}A+h^{-1}\mathrm{d}h,[\Gamma,\Gamma]\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}L_{\mathrm{CS}}(\Gamma)\big]

where

L_{\mathrm{CS}}(\Gamma)=\langle\mu_{1}(\Gamma),\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\tfrac{1}{3}[\Gamma,\Gamma]\rangle\,.

(2.12)

Proof.

Let us begin by simplifying the expressions that appear in the Lagrangian
L_{\mathrm{2CS}}\big[A^{(h,\Gamma)},B^{(h,\Gamma)}\big]
conveniently. First, we note that

F\big(A^{(h,\Gamma)}\big)=F(A^{h})+F(\Gamma)+[A^{h},\mu_{1}(\Gamma)]

(2.13)

where
A^{h}=h^{-1}Ah+h^{-1}\mathrm{d}h
. On the other hand, we can write

B^{(h,\Gamma)}=h^{-1}\rhd B+F(\Gamma)+\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma)\,.

(2.14)

Thus, we find that

F\big(A^{(h,\Gamma)}\big)-\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{1}\big(B^{(h,\Gamma)}\big)=\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}\big[F(A)-\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{1}(B)\big]+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\mu_{1}(F(\Gamma))+[A^{h},\mu_{1}\Gamma]\big)\,,

(2.15)

where we have used the covariance
F(A^{h})=\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}F(A)
of the usual curvature. Hence we have

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{2CS}}\big[A^{(h,\Gamma)},B^{(h,\Gamma)}\big]=L_{\mathrm{2CS}}[A,B]

\displaystyle+\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}\big[F(A)-\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{1}(B)\big],F(\Gamma)+\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma)\rangle

(2.16)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}\mu_{1}(B)+F(\Gamma)+\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma),F(\Gamma)+\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma)\rangle\,.

The terms containing
B
cancel each other. Expanding the last term and doing some algebraic manipulations we get

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\langle F(\Gamma)+\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma),F(\Gamma)

\displaystyle+\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma)\rangle=

(2.17)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{CS}}(\Gamma)+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle[A^{h},\mu_{1}(\Gamma)],\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma)\rangle+\langle\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma)\rangle\,,

where we have used the Jacobi identity to eliminate terms cubic in
\Gamma
and
L_{\mathrm{CS}}(\Gamma)
is given in (
2.12
). The remaining term is after some manipulations,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}F(A),F(\Gamma)+\mu_{2}(A,\Gamma)\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\langle A^{h},\tfrac{1}{2}[\Gamma,\Gamma]\rangle+\mathrm{d}\langle F(A^{h}),\Gamma\rangle-\langle\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\mu_{2}(A^{h},\Gamma)\rangle

(2.18)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{4}\langle[A^{h},A^{h}],[\Gamma,\Gamma]\rangle

where we have used the Bianchi identity
\mathrm{d}F(A^{h})+[A^{h},F(A^{h})]=0
. Putting together (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) we see that the non-exact terms cancel using the graded Jacobi identity (
2.3
), and we arrive to the desired result.

∎

Note that the gauge variation is a total boundary term. In other words, violation to gauge non-invariance of 2-Chern-Simons theory is completely hologrpahic, in contrast with CS
3
whose gauge variation contains the well-known Wess-Zumino-Witten term, which must be defined in the
3
d bulk.

2.2.1
Secondary gauge transformations

A special feature of 2-gauge theory in general is that there are redundancies in the
2-gauge transformations

(h,\Gamma)
(
2.10
) itself
[
38
,
73
,
85
,
33
]
. This can be attributed to the fact that the gauge symmetries in (
2.9
) form in actuality a 2-group, whose objects are the gauge parameters
(h,\Gamma)
.
4
4

4

This is an example of a categorical
gauge
symmetry, which is distinct from the categorical global symmetries that have been discussed recently in various places, eg.
[
32
,
39
]
.
We call these

, which can be formalized in a categorical context as in
[

,

,

More explicitly, these secondary gauge transformations are in essence deformations/"morphisms"
(h,\Gamma)\rightarrow(h^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
such that
(h^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
remains a 2-gauge transformation as in (
2.10
),

(A^{(h,\Gamma)},B^{(h,\Gamma)})\rightarrow(A^{(h^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})},B^{(h^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})})

(2.19)

In full generality, these morphisms are labelled by a pair of fields (the so-called "ghosts-of-ghosts")
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)\otimes\mathsf{H},E\in\Omega^{1}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{h}.
Essentially, the 0-form
\varphi
induces a translation

along the base while the 1-form
E
induces a translation

along the faces. We leave the interested reader to find the detailed differential constraints these fields must satisfy in the relevant literature
[
85
,
52
]
.

As we are in the context of a
strict
2-gauge theory, it is safe to not make crucial use of these data. For
weak/semistrict
2-gauge theories, on the other hand, it has been noted that they in fact play a key role
[
73
,
85
]
, even in the case where the 2-group is finite
[
33
]
. Though this requires one to always be on-shell of the fake-flatness condition
F-\mu_{1}B=0

[
54
]
, which is undesirable in our considerations.

2.3
The derived superfield formulation

We pause here momentarily to comment that the above higher-gauge theory computations can be reformulated using the general operational framework developed in
[
86
,
87
,
60
]
. This formalism makes use of
derived
structures, and provides a direct link with ordinary gauge theory. We will content ourselves with presenting the key expressions, the details can be found in
[
60
]
.

Given the Lie
2
-group
\mathbb{G}
we define the derived Lie
2
-group
\mathsf{D}\mathbb{G}
as the set of maps
\mathbb{R}[1]\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
labelled by the semidirect product
G\ltimes\mathfrak{h}
,

\alpha\mapsto(x,e^{\alpha\cdot{\mathsf{y}}})\,,\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,,\qquad x\in G,\penalty\ {\mathsf{y}}\in\mathfrak{h}\,.

(2.20)

In particular,
\mathsf{D}\mathbb{G}
is a graded Lie group; it shall make appearance again in §
7
. Similarly, one can also define the corresponding derived Lie 2-algebra
\mathsf{D}\mathfrak{G}
as the space of maps
\mathbb{R}[1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}
.
We may now describe our higher connection and higher gauge transformations as elements valued in
\mathsf{D}\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathsf{D}\mathbb{G}
respectively. Explicitly, for the
2
-connection we have a degree-
1
polyform

{\cal A}(\alpha)=(A,\alpha\cdot B)\in(\Omega^{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathsf{D}\mathfrak{G})_{1}\,,

(2.21)

whereas for the
2
-gauge transformation, a degree-
0
polyform

U(\alpha)=(h,e^{\alpha\cdot\Gamma})\in(\Omega^{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathsf{D}\mathbb{G})_{0}\,.

(2.22)

Note
\Gamma\in\Omega^{1}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{h}
, and
e^{\alpha\cdot\Gamma}=1+\alpha\cdot\Gamma+\frac{1}{2}[\alpha\cdot\Gamma,\alpha\cdot\Gamma]
terminates due to the Jacobi identity
[
52
]
. The tuples
(A,B),(h,\Gamma)
that we have used previously can therefore be understood respectively as the labels for these derived polyforms
\mathcal{A},U
.

With these expressions at hand, we may write a general
2
-gauge transformation (
2.10
) in the compact form

\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{U}=\penalty\ _{2}\operatorname{Ad}_{U}^{-1}\mathcal{A}+U^{-1}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}U\,

(2.23)

as a function of the derived parameter
\alpha
, where the expressions for the
2
-adjoint action
{}_{2}\operatorname{Ad}
and the Maurer-Cartan form
U^{-1}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}U
are given, respectively, in eqs. (3.2.8) and (3.2.14) of
[
60
]
, and
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}=\mathrm{d}-\mu_{1}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}
. Finally, we may induce a pairing
(-,-)
on
\mathsf{D}\mathfrak{G}
from the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle
on
\mathfrak{G}
(see eq. (3.2.15) in
[
60
]
) to write the four-dimensional
2
CS action (
2.9
) as

S_{2\mathrm{CS}}[{\cal A}]=\int_{X}({\cal A},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}{\cal A}+\tfrac{1}{3}[{\cal A},{\cal A}])\,.

(2.24)

As such, the derived superfield formalism allows us to rewrite expressions in higher gauge theory as analogues of those in ordinary gauge theory.

In addition to the compactness of this derived operational framework, it also introduces an
internal
grading attached to the graded field algebra: in contrast to ordinary gauge theory, derived gauge theory has two gradings
[
86
,
87
]
.
This additional grading endows both connections and gauge transformations with
ghostlike
partners, in a way similar to the AKSZ formulation of BV theory
[
88
,
89
,
90
]
. The derived formalism therefore gives a useful and unifying description of higher gauge theory, also known as "non-Abelian bundle gerbes with connection"
[
52
,
91
,
92
]
.

3
Holomorphic 2-Chern-Simons Theory

Having introduced 2-Chern-Simons theory together with its equations of motion and its behaviour under gauge transformations, we are in conditions to define its holomorphic variant. Following
[
7
]
, we take our
4
-manifold
X=\mathbb{R}\times Y
and we complexify and compactify the copy of
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. Taking coordinates
z,\bar{z}
on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and
x^{i}
with
i=1,2,3
on
Y
, we define
holomorphic 2-Chern-Simons theory
(h2CS) by the action functional

S_{\text{h2CS}}[A,B]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{X}\omega\wedge\langle F(A)-\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{1}(B),B\rangle

(3.1)

where
\omega\in\Omega^{(1,0)}(X)
is a meromorphic
(1,0)
-form on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. In this article we will make a particular choice of
\omega
given by

\omega=\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,.

(3.2)

Note that
\omega
is nowhere vanishing and has simple poles at
z=0
and
z=\infty
. For future use, we write down the gauge fields in components as

\displaystyle A=A_{\bar{z}}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}+A_{i}\mathrm{d}x^{i}

(3.3)

\displaystyle B=B_{i\bar{z}}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}+B_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,,

(3.4)

where we have ignored the
z
-components of the gauge fields provided
\omega\in\Omega^{(1,0)}(X)
.

Without any additional constraints, the action (
3.1
) is not well defined: although the singularities in
\omega
do not break the local integrability of the action (see
Lemma 2.1
in
[
93
]
), boundary terms arise under an arbitrary field variation. Indeed, we have

\delta S_{\mathrm{h2CS}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{X}\omega\wedge\langle\delta\mathcal{F},B\rangle-\omega\wedge\langle\delta A,\mathcal{G}\rangle+\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\langle\delta A,B\rangle.

(3.5)

The first two terms are bulk terms which give rise to the equations of motion. The third term, however, is a distribution localized at the poles of
\omega
. Indeed, we can integrate along
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, to find (see
Lemma 2.2
in
[
9
]
)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times Y}\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\langle\delta A,B\rangle=\int_{Y}\iota_{Y}^{*}\langle\delta A,B\rangle|_{z=0}-\iota_{Y}^{*}\langle\delta A,B\rangle_{z=\infty}\,,

(3.6)

where
\iota_{Y}:Y\hookrightarrow X
is an embedding of the 3-manifold
Y
into
X=\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times Y
.
Thus, to have a well defined action principle, namely
\delta S_{\text{h2CS}}=0
, we must impose boundary conditions on the gauge fields
A
and
B
at
z=0,\infty
. Throughout the bulk of the article we will make different choices of boundary conditions depending on the additional structure we introduce on
Y
. However, the main example to which we will consistently come back to simplify discussions is given by the choice

\displaystyle

\displaystyle A_{1}|_{z=0}=A_{2}|_{z=0}=B_{12}|_{z=0}=0\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle A_{3}|_{z=\infty}=B_{13}|_{z=\infty}=B_{23}|_{z=\infty}=0\,,

and we restrict to variations satisfying the same boundary conditions. It is immediate to verify that this choice of boundary conditions makes (
3.6
) vanish.

Remark 3.1
.

The quantity (
3.6
) defines the
symplectic form

\varpi_{\text{blk}}
on the on-shell fields (i.e. Maurer-Cartan elements, c.f. §
2.1
)
\mathsf{MC}_{\text{blk}}\subset\iota_{Y}^{*}\Omega(X)\otimes\mathfrak{G}
of the bulk
5
d theory. In particular, a choice of boundary conditions such that
\delta S_{\mathrm{h2CS}}=0
is then equivalent to a choice of a Lagrangian subspace
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathsf{MC}_{\text{blk}}
such that

\varpi_{\text{blk}}|_{\mathcal{L}}=0.

Indeed, fields
(\iota_{Y}^{*}A,\iota_{Y}^{*}B)\in\mathcal{L}
by definition will satisfy
\delta S_{\text{2CS}}[A,B]=0
. The variational quantities such as
\delta A,\delta B
, are naturally interpreted as covectors on
field space
. This is made precise in the covariant phase space formalism
[
94
,
80
]
.

In the above language, the boundary conditions (
3.7
) correspond to the choice of Lagrangian subspace

\mathcal{L}=\operatorname{Span}\{A_{3},B_{23},B_{13}\}|_{z=0}\cup\operatorname{Span}\{A_{1},A_{2},B_{12}\}|_{z=\infty}\,,

which is simply given by the components of the fields that survive at the respective poles
z=0,z=\infty
. We shall see in §
4
that this example is in fact part of a covariant family of such "chiral" Lagrangian subspaces
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\subset\mathsf{MC}_{\text{blk}}
, which are parameterized by a unit vector
\ell\in S^{2}
called the
chirality
. Moreover, in §
4.1
we shall see that, if additional structure such as a
transverse holormophic foliation
is imposed on
Y
, the different choices of the chirality vector
\ell
can lead to very different three dimensional actions.

Going back to our h2CS action, the bulk equations of motion are readily obtained from (
3.5
). Indeed, they imply the Maurer-Cartan condition — namely the fake- and 2-flatness, see §
2.1
— of the 2-connection
\mathcal{A}=(A,B)
. In components, these are

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{i}-\partial_{i}A_{\bar{z}}+[A_{\bar{z}},A_{i}]-\mu_{1}(B_{\bar{z}i})=0

(3.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}A_{i}-\partial_{i}A_{j}+[A_{j},A_{i}]-\mu_{1}(B_{ij})=0

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}B_{j\bar{z}}+\partial_{\bar{z}}B_{ij}+\mu_{2}(A_{i},B_{j\bar{z}})+\mu_{2}(A_{\bar{z}},B_{ij})=0

(3.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}B_{jk}+\mu_{2}(A_{i},B_{jk})=0

(3.11)

where
i,j,k\in\{1,2,3\}
and where we have explicitly separated the
\bar{z}
component equations for later convenience. Preempting what will happen after localisation to a three-dimensional boundary theory, equations (
3.8
) and (
3.10
) will imply that
A
and
B
are holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and therefore constant along
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. On the other hand, equations (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) will result on fake flatness and 2-flatness of the three-dimensional localised fields.

3.1
Gauge Symmetries of
\mathrm{h2CS}

As discussed in §
2.2
, 2-Chern-Simons theory is invariant (modulo boundary terms) under an arbitrary 2-gauge transformation. In the holomorphic case, the introduction of the meromorphic
1
-form
\omega
breaks this gauge symmetry, in the sense that only a subset of gauge transformations leave (
3.1
) invariant. In particular, it is precisely the breaking of this gauge symmetry which gives rise to the edge-modes on the boundary, which will be the fields of our three-dimensional theory. Moreover, the transformations which remain symmetries of the 5d action will descend to symmetries of the 3d boundary theory.

This is entirely analogous to what happens in the case of 4d Chern-Simons theory, as well as in holomorphic Chern-Simons theory on Twistor space. Notably, in these cases, the gauge transformations which remain symmetries of the action after the introduction of
\omega
are those which preserve the boundary conditions imposed on the gauge fields. We will show that this is not true any more in h2CS theory.

Indeed, in proposition
2.1
we have shown that under the 2-gauge transformation (
2.10
) the variation is a total boundary term

\Omega=\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}F(A),\Gamma\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{-1}A+h^{-1}\mathrm{d}h,[\Gamma,\Gamma]\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}L_{\mathrm{CS}}(\Gamma)\,.

(3.12)

With the introduction of the meromorphic
1
-form we have that under a finite gauge transformation

\begin{split}S_{\text{h2CS}}\big[A^{(h,\Gamma)},B^{(h,\Gamma)}\big]&=S_{\text{h2CS}}[A,B]+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{X}\omega\wedge\mathrm{d}\Omega[A,B,h,\Gamma]\\
&=S_{\text{h2CS}}[A,B]+\int_{Y}\iota_{Y}^{*}\Omega[A,B,h,\Gamma]|_{z=0}-\iota_{Y}^{*}\Omega[A,B,h,\Gamma]|_{z=\infty}\,,\end{split}

(3.13)

where in the second line we have integrated by parts to get the distribution
\mathrm{d}\omega
and then we have integrated along
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
to localize at the poles. Let us consider the boundary conditions (
3.7
) and restrict to gauge transformations which preserve these boundary conditions. In other words, we constraint our gauge parameters
(h,\Gamma)
by requiring that
A^{(h,\Gamma)}
and
B^{(h,\Gamma)}
satisfy the same boundary conditions than
A
and
B
. From (
2.10
) we find the set of constraints

\displaystyle z=0:\quad

\displaystyle h^{-1}\partial_{1}h+\mu_{1}(\Gamma_{1})=h^{-1}\partial_{2}h+\mu_{1}(\Gamma_{2})=\partial_{1}\Gamma_{2}-\partial_{2}\Gamma_{1}-[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}]=0

(3.14)

\displaystyle z=\infty:\quad

\displaystyle h^{-1}\partial_{3}h+\mu_{1}(\Gamma_{3})=\partial_{i}\Gamma_{3}-\partial_{3}\Gamma_{i}+\mu_{2}(A^{(h,\Gamma)}_{i},\Gamma_{3})-[\Gamma_{i},\Gamma_{3}]=0\,,\quad i=1,2\,.

(3.15)

Writing down
\iota_{Y}^{*}\Omega
explicitly, using the boundary conditions (
3.7
) and the constraints (
3.14
) and (
3.15

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\iota_{Y}^{*}\Omega[A,B,h,\Gamma]|_{z=0}

Field Reparametrization

We now begin the localization procedure for 5d h2CS theory (

Towards this, we first introduce new field variables

On the other hand, the reparametrisations (

Proposition 3.2
.

Given

\displaystyle\hat{g}\mapsto u^{-1}\hat{g},\hskip 86.78099ptA^{\prime}\mapsto u^{-1}A^{\prime}u+u^{-1}\mathrm{d}u+\mu_{1}(\Lambda)

(3.23)

\displaystyle\hat{\Theta}\mapsto\hat{\Theta}-(\hat{g}^{-1}u)\rhd\Lambda\qquad\qquad B^{\prime}\mapsto u^{-1}\rhd B^{\prime}+d\Lambda+\mu_{2}(A^{\prime(u,\Lambda)},\Lambda)-\tfrac{1}{2}[\Lambda,\Lambda].

(3.24)

leave the gauge fields

and
B

Proof.

The proof follows by explicit computation. Indeed, from equation (
3.21
) we find

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{-1}A^{\prime}\hat{g}

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{g}^{-1}A^{\prime}\hat{g}+\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat{g}}^{-1}\mathrm{d}uu^{-1}+\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat{g}^{-1}u}\mu_{1}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}

\displaystyle\mapsto-\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat{g}}^{-1}\mathrm{d}uu^{-1}+\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Theta)

\displaystyle\mapsto\mu_{1}(\Theta)-\mathrm{Ad}_{\hat{g}^{-1}u}\mu_{1}(\Lambda)\,,

from where invariance of

follows. Similarly, from equation (

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{-1}\rhd B^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{g}^{-1}\rhd\big[B^{\prime}+u\rhd(\mathrm{d}\Lambda+\tfrac{1}{2}[\Lambda,\Lambda])+\rhd\mu_{2}(A^{\prime},u\rhd\Lambda)\big]+\mu_{2}(\mathrm{d}uu^{-1},u\rhd\Lambda)

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\hat{\Theta}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{d}\hat{\Theta}+\mu_{2}(\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g},\hat{g}u^{-1}\rhd\Lambda)-\mu_{2}(\mathrm{d}uu^{-1},u\rhd\Lambda)-\hat{g}^{-1}u\rhd\mathrm{d}\Lambda

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(A,\hat{\Theta})

\displaystyle\mapsto\mu_{2}(A,\hat{\Theta})-\mu_{2}(A,\hat{g}^{-1}u\rhd\Lambda)

(3.27)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}[\hat{\Theta},\hat{\Theta}]

\displaystyle\mapsto-\tfrac{1}{2}[\hat{\Theta},\hat{\Theta}]+\mu_{2}(\mu_{1}(\hat{\Theta}),\hat{g}^{-1}u\rhd\Lambda)-\hat{g}^{-1}u\rhd\tfrac{1}{2}[\Lambda,\Lambda]\,.

(3.28)

Adding all of these together we are left after some cancellations with

B\mapsto B-\mu_{2}(A,\hat{g}^{-1}u\rhd\Lambda)+\mu_{2}(\hat{g}^{-1}A^{\prime}\hat{g}+\hat{g}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\hat{g}+\mu_{1}(\hat{\Theta}),\hat{g}^{-1}u\rhd\Lambda)=B\,,

(3.29)

where the last equality follows from the definition of

in (

Proof.

If we introduce the covariant derivatives on

Y:\nabla^{L^{\prime}}_{i}=\partial_{i}+\mu_{2}(L^{\prime}_{i},-)\,,\qquad\mathbb{C}P^{1}:\bar{\partial}^{L^{\prime}}=\partial_{\bar{z}}+\mu_{2}(L^{\prime}_{\bar{z}},-)\,,

4.1
Covariant family of boundary conditions

We begin with a particularly convenient family of boundary conditions/Lagrangian subspaces
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathsf{MC}_{\text{blk}}
, which can be understood as a generalization of the example (
3.7
). First, recall that the 2-Chern-Simons action is symmetric under global Poincaré transformations. For a product manifold

\operatorname{Iso}(Y)
of
Y

\operatorname{Iso}(Y)

(

Here, we are going to introduce a family of boundary conditions that breaks only the linear part of the isometries

S^{2}\hookrightarrow O(3)\rightarrow O(2).

(4.5)

The fibre

of Lagrangian subspaces of
\mathsf{MC}_{\text{blk}}
. We call this data

the

under a right-action. These quantities indeed coincide.

7.2.1
The differential in
\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}

This graded algebra
\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}
is equipped with the differential
\hat{\mathrm{d}}:\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet+1}
that satisfies the usual graded Leibniz rule, but also a compatibility with whiskering
\rhd
. Since
\mathfrak{G}
only has two terms, so does each degree of
\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}
. On elements
(g,g\rhd\Theta)
of degree-0, we can explicitly write

\hat{\mathrm{d}}(g,\Theta^{g})=(\overrightarrow{\Delta}g-\mu_{1}\Theta^{g},\mathrm{d}\Theta^{g}-\Theta^{g}\wedge\Theta^{g}),

(7.5)

where
\overrightarrow{\Delta}g=-\mathrm{d}gg^{-1}
is the right Maurer-Cartan form. Note this produces the covariant derivative associated to a connection
\Theta^{g}
on a
\mathsf{H}
-bundle over
X
(recall
\mathsf{H}
is a
G
-module).

For
g,h\in C^{\infty}(Y)\otimes G,
and
\Theta\in\Omega^{1}(Y)\otimes\mathfrak{h}
, the compatibility with a whiskering by
g
can then be written as

g\rhd\hat{\mathrm{d}}(h,h\rhd\Theta)=\hat{D}(gh,(gh)\rhd\Theta)\,,\qquad\hat{D}=(\mathrm{d}_{1}-[\partial gg^{-1},-]-\mu_{1},\mathrm{d}_{2}-\mu_{2}(\partial gg^{-1},-)),

The nilpotency of the differential,
\hat{\mathrm{d}}^{2}=0
, is equivalent to the fake- and 2-flatness of
(L,H)
. This then allows us to identify the (off-shell) currents

(L,H)=g\rhd\hat{\mathrm{d}}(1,\Theta)=\hat{D}(g,\Theta^{g})\in\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{1}

as a degree-1 element in this derived complex.

7.2.2
Characterizing the currents

To characterize the on-shell 2-currents, we slice up the dg algebra
\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}
into two according to the projection
\operatorname{proj}_{\ell}
and its complement. This induces a fibration

\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}_{\perp}^{\bullet}\cong(\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}/\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}_{\ell}^{\bullet})\hookrightarrow\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}_{\ell}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}_{\ell}^{\bullet}=\Omega_{\ell}^{\bullet}(Y)\otimes\mathsf{D}\mathbb{G}
. This splits the differential
\hat{\mathrm{d}}=\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\perp}+\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\ell}
, which are individually nilpotent
\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\perp}^{2},\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\ell}^{2}=0
on the fields
(g,\Theta^{g})\in\mathsf{D}\mathscr{L}^{0}
, as a consequence of the 2-Lax equations (
7.2
) and (
7.4
). We can then characterize the current algebras as the following subspaces:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}

8
Outlook

In this paper, we have constructed a 3d integrable field theory from a higher-gauge theory by following through with the Costello-Yamazaki localization procedure
[
7
]
. We also studied in detail the properties of this 3d field theory, and proved several very interesting facts about its properties. We believe that there are many more open questions to be answered in regards to this theory, and the connection between higher-gauge theories and higher-dimensional integrable field theories in general. Here, we list a few of them in this section.

Higher Monodromies.

Recall that the conserved 2-monodromy matrices (
6.16
) in our 3d field theory come labelled by the categorical characters
\mathcal{X}_{k}
of the Lie 2-group
\mathbb{G}
and homotopy classes
[\Sigma]
of surfaces in
Y
. We are lead to two very interesting problems to tackle when considering the properties of these higher monodromies.

1.

The label
[\Sigma]
: differential gerbes and 2-tangle invariants.
In 3d TQFTs, it is known that the algebra of the Wilson lines define invariants of framed links
[
57
]
. The usual singular homology is not enough to capture such data: indeed, framed link invariants satisfy certain knot and satellite relations that are seen only by differential — possibly Dolbeault, when the chirality and foliation are misaligned — cohomology
[
124
]
(eg. the
Deligne-Beilinson
cohomology
[
58
]
in Abelian Chern-Simons theory). As such, the label
[\Sigma]
should be treated as differential non-Abelian gerbes
[
91
]
which classify invariants of framed 2-tangles
[
29
,
125
]
in
Y
(or its thickening
Y\times[0,1]
).

2.

The label
k
: orthogonality of categorical characters.
In regular representation theory, one can prove that there are countably many orthogonal irreducible characters of a compact Lie group that span the representation ring
[
126
]
. Correspondingly, the label
k
should run over all indecomposable
\mathbb{G}
-module categories, but categorical character theory is not yet powerful enough to prove this (see eg. §9.6 in
[
108
]
and
Proposition 1.2.19
in
[
50
]
). Moreover, while an inner product is known to exist for categorical characters
[
67
]
, there is no satisfactory notion of "orthogonality" for them at the moment. One of the authors (HC) of this paper is currently investigating this problem.

Resolving these two issues are of major interest in recent literature. For instance, higher-tangle invariants would help define 4d non-semisimple TQFTs (which have a chance of definition exotic 4-manifold invariants
[
49
]
), and categorical character theory would have very explicit applications in the construction of 4d gapped phases in condensed matter theory
[
127
,
39
]
.

Quantization of
S_{3d}
: the 2-Kac-Moody group.

An obvious next step to take would be to try and quantize the theory

. Towards this, one of the authors (HC) has devised in

Appendix A
Classification of Lie 2-groups and Lie 2-algebras

Let us begin with a brief overview of the notion of 2-groups, Lie 2-algebras and 2-gauge theory. Recall a 2-group
\mathbb{G}
is, by definition, a monoidal groupoid
\Gamma\rightrightarrows G
in which all objects are invertible. There are many different but equivalent characterizations of a 2-group, namely as a pointed 2-groupoid, as a group object internal to the category of groups, or as a 2-term crossed-complex of groups as in
Definition
2.1
.

The fundamental theorem of Lie 2-groups state that there is a one-to-one correspondence between connected, simply-connected Lie 2-groups and Lie 2-algebras.
Definition
2.2
gives Lie 2-algebras as a differential crossed-complex
[
75
]
, which is equivalent to the
L_{2}
-algebra definition by simply identifying
\mu_{1}=t
and
\mu_{2}=(\rhd,[-,-])
. The homotopy Jacobi identities are equivalent to (
2.2
) and the 2-Jacobi identities.

The goal of this section is to briefly recall some classification results for Lie 2-groups and Lie 2-algebras. If
\mathbb{G},\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
are two Lie 2-groups, a
Lie 2-group homomorphism

(\Phi,\Psi):\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
is a tuple of Lie group maps
\Phi:G\rightarrow G^{\prime},\Psi:\mathsf{H}\rightarrow\mathsf{H}^{\prime}
such that

1.

\bar{\mu}_{1}^{\prime}\circ\Phi=\Psi\circ\bar{\mu}_{1}
, and

2.

\Phi(x\rhd y)=(\Psi x)\rhd^{\prime}(\Phi y)
for all
x\in G,y\in\mathsf{H}
.

Similarly, If
\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
are two Lie 2-algebras, then a Lie 2-algebra homomorphism
(\phi,\psi):\mathfrak{G}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
is a tuple of Lie algebra maps
\phi:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},\psi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
such that

1.

\mu_{1}^{\prime}\circ\phi=\psi\circ\mu_{1}
, and

2.

\phi({\mathsf{x}}\rhd{\mathsf{y}})=(\psi{\mathsf{x}})\rhd^{\prime}(\phi{\mathsf{y}})
for each
{\mathsf{x}}\in\mathfrak{g},{\mathsf{y}}\in\mathfrak{h}
.

As in the Lie 1-algebra case, this notion transports to maps between principal 2-bundles
\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}

[
52
,
78
]
.

In contrast to Lie groups, the notion of "isomorphism" for Lie groupoids is categorical equivalence
[
30
]
. A bit more explicitly, we say
\mathbb{G}\simeq\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
are equivalent iff there exist Lie 2-group maps
(\Phi,\Psi):\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
and
(\Phi,\Psi)^{-1}:\mathbb{G}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
such that there are invertible natural transformations

\mathcal{H}:(\Phi,\Psi)^{-1}\circ(\Phi,\Psi)\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{G}},\qquad\mathcal{E}:(\Phi,\Psi)\circ(\Phi,\Psi)^{-1}\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{G}^{\prime}}.

The following is a classic result first proven by Hoàng Xuân Sính
[
134
,
103
,
33
,
135
]
, who is a Vietnamese mathematician taught by Alexander Grothendieck.

Theorem A.1
.

2-groups
\mathbb{G}
are classified by the following data: a group
\Pi_{1}=\operatorname{coker}\bar{\mu}_{1}
, a
\Pi_{1}
-module
\Pi_{2}=\operatorname{ker}\bar{\mu}_{1}
, and a degree-3 group cohomology class
\tau\in H^{3}(\Pi_{1},\Pi_{0})
called the
Postnikov class
.

Note
\Pi_{2}\subset\mathsf{H}
must be an Abelian group by the Peiffer identity (
2.2
). The tuple
(\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2},\tau)
is often called the
Hoàng data
of
\mathbb{G}
.

An analogous result holds for Lie 2-algebras. Here, one can state equivalence in the context of chain complexes. We say
\mathfrak{G}\simeq\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
are equivalent iff there exist Lie 2-algebra maps
(\phi,\psi):\mathfrak{G}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}^{\prime},(\phi,\psi)^{-1}:\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}
and invertible chain homotopies

\eta:(\phi,\psi)^{-1}\circ(\phi,\psi)\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{G}},\qquad\varepsilon:(\phi,\psi)\circ(\phi,\psi)^{-1}\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}}.

The following is also a classic result, which was claimed to be first proven by Gerstenhaber by Wagemann in
[
105
]
.

Theorem A.2
.

Lie 2-algebras are classified up to equivalence by the following data: a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}=\operatorname{coker}\mu_{1}
, a
\mathfrak{n}
-module
V=\operatorname{ker}\mu_{1}
and a degree-3 Lie algebra cohomology class
\kappa\in H^{3}(\mathfrak{n},V)
, called the
Postnikov class
.

One can show that, if two principal 2-bundles
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rightarrow X
have structure 2-groups
\mathbb{G},\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
that have distinct Postnikov classes, then they cannot be isomorphic 2-bundles over
X
.

Appendix B
Homotopy transfer

The BV-BRST formalism is a way to organize the algebraic and geometric data in a field theory with gauge symmetry into cochain complexes, which are designed such that their cohomologies describes precisely the gauge-invariant on-shell quantities. The mathematical structure that arises out of this construction is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
, which can be described as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded differential complex

\mathfrak{L}\cong\dots\rightarrow\mathfrak{l}_{-2}\xrightarrow{\ell_{1}}\mathfrak{l}_{-1}\xrightarrow{\mu_{1}}\mathfrak{l}_{0}\xrightarrow{\ell_{1}}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\xrightarrow{\ell_{1}}\mathfrak{l}_{2}\rightarrow\dots

equipped with higher-brackets
\ell_{n}\in\operatorname{Hom}^{2-n}(\mathscr{L}^{n\otimes},\mathscr{L})
for
n\geq 2
satisfying the Koszul-Jacobi identities. In gauge field theories with structure (2-)group
\mathbb{G}
, such as the homotopy Maurer-Cartan theories
[
76
]
, for instance, this complex is given by
\mathscr{L}=\Omega^{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{G}
where
\mathfrak{G}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathbb{G}
.

Now from a purely mathematical perspective, it is known that
L_{\infty}
-algebras retracts onto its cohomology
[
84
,
98
]
.
18
18

18

L_{\infty}
-algebras are objects in the derived category of Lie algebras. More generally in any derived Abelian category, equivalences are precisely quasi-isomorphisms, ie. derived chain maps that induce isomorphisms in cohomologies.
In other words, given a
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathscr{L}
, the inclusion
\iota:H(\mathscr{L})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{L}
treating
H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{L})\cong\operatorname{ker}\ell_{1}/\operatorname{im}\ell_{1}
as a subcomplex of
\mathscr{L}
admits a homotopy inverse (ie. an adjunction) given by the projection
p:\mathscr{L}\rightarrow H(\mathscr{L})
— there exists chain homotopies
\eta:\iota\circ p\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{G}}
,
\varepsilon:p\circ\iota\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{H(\mathfrak{G})}
that "witness" this retraction. To be a chain homotopy, the map
\eta=(\eta_{n})
— where
\eta_{n}:\mathscr{L}_{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{L}_{n-1}
— for instance must satisfy

\iota\circ p-1=\eta\circ\ell_{1}+\ell_{1}\circ\eta

as linear maps on
\mathscr{L}
.

In this context, it can be shown that a
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure, ie. the higher brackets
\tilde{\ell}=(\tilde{\ell}_{n})_{n\geq 2}
, on
H(\mathscr{L})
can be induced from that
\ell=(\ell_{n})_{n\geq 2}
on
\mathscr{L}
by using the projector
p
; notice, by construction, the cohomology
H(\mathscr{L})
has trivial differential. This is given by the formula

\tilde{\ell}=p\circ(\ell+\ell\circ\eta\circ\ell+\ell\circ\eta\circ\ell\circ\eta\circ\ell+\dots)\circ\iota,

(B.1)

and it can be proven that
\tilde{\ell}
indeed satisfies the Koszul-Jacobi identities on
H(\mathscr{L})

[
84
,
82
]
. This is the celebrated
homotopy transfer theorem
for
L_{\infty}
-algebras: the
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\mathscr{L}
is transferred to a
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on its cohomology
H(\mathscr{L})
through the projection quasi-isomorphism
p
.

An interesting case occurs when
\mathscr{L}
is only a dgla, such that
\ell_{n}=0
for all
n>2
. The formula (
B.1
) implies that homotopy transfer in fact induces higher homotopy brackets on
H(\mathscr{L})
. Indeed, at degree-3, we see that we have a generally non-trivial contribution given for each
{\mathsf{x}}_{1},{\mathsf{x}}_{2},{\mathsf{x}}_{3}\in\mathscr{L}
by

\tilde{\ell}_{3}([{\mathsf{x}}_{1}],[{\mathsf{x}}_{2}],[{\mathsf{x}}_{3}])=\penalty\ \circlearrowright[\ell_{2}(\eta(\ell_{2}([{\mathsf{x}}_{1}],[{\mathsf{x}}_{2}])),\eta([{\mathsf{x}}_{3}]))],

where
\circlearrowright
denotes a cyclic sum and we have denoted the cohomology class of an element
{\mathsf{x}}\in\mathscr{L}
by
p({\mathsf{x}})=[{\mathsf{x}}]\in H(\mathscr{L})
. This map
\tilde{\ell}_{3}
is usually called the
homotopy map
in the literature
[
83
]
, and can be seen to measure the failure of the chain homotopy
\eta
to respect the graded Jacobi identity. This is relevant for us, as given a strict Lie 2-algebra
\mathfrak{G}
, the associated BV-BRST
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathscr{L}=\Omega^{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{G}
is indeed a dgla with
\ell_{1}=d-\mu_{1}
. However, upon performing a homotopy transfer, we see that a degree-3 homotopy map is induced on the cohomology
H(\mathscr{L})
.

This idea can be used to immediately to prove Gerstenhaber’s theorem in §
A
. The cohomology
H(\mathfrak{G})
of a Lie 2-algebra
\mathfrak{G}
consist of
M\xrightarrow{0}\mathfrak{n}
, and the homotopy map
\tilde{\ell}_{3}
is a 3-cocycle representing the Postnikov class
\kappa\in H^{3}(\mathfrak{n},M)
.

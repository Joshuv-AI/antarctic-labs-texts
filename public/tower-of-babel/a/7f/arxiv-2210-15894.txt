Sublacunary sequences that are strong sweeping out
Sovanlal Mondal, Madhumita Roy and Máté Wierdl Department of Mathematical Sciences, University of Memphis Memphis, TN 38152, USA email smondal@memphis.edu

Abstract
An increasing sequence ( a n ) (a_{n}) of positive integers which satisfies a n + 1 a n > 1 + η \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}>1+\eta for some positive η \eta is called a lacunary sequence. It has been known for over twenty years that every lacunary sequence is strong sweeping out which means that in every aperiodic dynamical system we can find a set E E of arbitrary small measure so that lim sup N 1 N ​ ∑ n ≤ N 𝟙 E ​ ( T n ​ x ) = 1 \limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\leq N}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{n}x)=1 and lim inf N 1 N ​ ∑ n ≤ N 𝟙 E ​ ( T n ​ x ) = 0 \liminf_{N}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\leq N}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{n}x)=0 almost everywhere. In this paper we improve this result by showing that if ( a n ) (a_{n}) satisfies only a n + 1 a n > 1 + 1 ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 1 − η \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}>1+\frac{1}{(\log\log n)^{1-\eta}} for some positive η \eta then it is already strong sweeping out.

Introduction and main results:

Throughout this paper, we use the notation

[N]\coloneqq\left\{1,2,\dots,N\right\}

(1.1)

Let
T
be a measure preserving transformation on the probability
space
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
. After Birkhoff’s pointwise ergodic theorem
was proved, naturally the question was raised whether it is possible
to generalize the theorem along any subsequence
(a_{n})
of integers
instead of taking the entire sequence
(n)
. Krengel
(
Krengel 1971
)
was
the one who first constructed a strictly increasing sequence
(a_{n})

of positive integers so that in every aperiodic system the ergodic
averages
\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{a_{n}}x)
diverge
almost everywhere. Soon after, Bellow
(
Bellow 1983
)
showed that if

(a_{n})
is a
lacunary sequence
, that is, it satisfies

\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}\geq 1+\eta
for some positive
\eta
, then the
ergodic averages along
(a_{n})
diverge a.e. in every aperiodic system,
though she showed divergence only for
f\in L^{p}
for
p<\infty
. Our
first main result provides a growth condition for a sequence to be

pointwise bad
which applies to
non lacunary sequences

as well, that is sequences for which
\lim_{n}\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}=1
.

Theorem 1

(label=thm:1)
.

Strong sweeping out with deterministic
conditionSuppose
(a_{n})
is a sequence which satisfies

\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}\geq 1+{\frac{1}{(\log\log n)^{1-\eta}}}

(1.2)

for some
\eta>0
.

Then in every aperiodic dynamical system
(X,\Sigma,\mu,T)
and
every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a set
E\in\Sigma
with

\mu(E)<\epsilon
such that for almost every
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{a_{n}}x)

\displaystyle=1

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{a_{n}}x)

\displaystyle=0

(1.4)

An example of a sequence
(a_{n})
which satisfies the growth condition
in
eq.

is

a_{n}=\lfloor e^{\frac{n}{(\log\log n)^{1-\eta}}}\rfloor
, for some

\eta>0
.

Definition 2

(label=defn:3)
.

Pointwise good and bad sequencesLet

and
(a_{n})
be a sequence of positive
integers.

We say

is
pointwise good
for

if for every
measure preserving system
(X,\Sigma,\mu,T)
and every

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{a_{n}}x)
exists a.e.

We say the sequence
(a_{n})
is
pointwise bad
for

if for
every aperiodic measure preserving system
(X,\Sigma,\mu,T)
there
exists a function

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{a_{n}}x)

Whether a sequence will be pointwise good or bad for
L^{p}
depends on
many factors, such as, the speed of the sequence
(a_{n})
, the value of

p
, and sometimes the intrinsic arithmetic properties of the sequence

(a_{n})
. From
, we can see that if a sequence grows very
fast, then it will be pointwise bad even for
L^{\infty}
. On the other
extreme, if a sequence grows slower than any positive power of
n
,
for example
(a_{n})=\big((\log n)^{c}\big)
for some

, then it is
again pointwise bad for
L^{\infty}
as shown in Jones and Wierdl

(
Jones & Wierdl 1994
, Example 2.18)
. See also a
related result of Loyd
(
Loyd 2022
)
. Bellow
(
Bellow 1989
)
and

(
Reinhold-Larsson 1994

proved that whether a sequence will

or not can depend on the value of

(a_{n})
which are
pointwise good for
L^{q}
but pointwise bad for
L^{p}
.
Parrish
(

)
gives refinements of these results in terms
of Orlicz spaces.
In general, neither the growth rate of the sequence, nor the value of

alone determines whether the sequence is pointwise good or bad.
In some cases, one has to analyze the intrinsic arithmetic
properties of the sequence
(a_{n})
. One such curious example is

(n^{k})_{n}
. A celebrated result of Bourgain
(
Bourgain 1988
, Theorem 2)
says
that the sequence
(n^{k})_{n}
is pointwise good for

when

is a
positive integer. On the other hand, the sequence

\left\lparen\lfloor n^{k}+\log n\rfloor\right\rparen
is known to be pointwise bad for

when

is a positive integer
(
Boshernitzan et al. 2005
, Theorem C)
.

After showing that polynomials are pointwise good for
L^{2}
, Bourgain
showed
(
Bourgain 1989
)
that a polynomial sequence is pointwise
good for
L^{p}
for every

and Wierdl
(
Wierdl 1988
)
showed the
same for the sequence of primes. However, the sequence of squares is
pointwise bad for
L^{1}
, as was shown by
Buczolich-Mauldin
(
Buczolich & Mauldin 2010
)
, and
LaVictoire
(
LaVictoire 2011
)
showed the same for the sequence

(n^{k})
of

th powers for a fixed positive integer

and for the
sequence of primes. It was largely believed that there cannot be any
sequence
(a_{n})
which is pointwise good for
L^{1}
and satisfies

(a_{n+1}-a_{n})\to\infty
as

, but
Buczolich
(

)
disproved this conjecture.
LaVictoire
(
LaVictoire 2009
)
showed that a large class or random

Urban and Zienkiewicz
(
Urban & Zienkiewicz 2007
)
showed that

\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor,c\in(1,1.001)
is pointwise good for
L^{1}
. The
current best result is due to Mirek
(
Mirek 2015
)
who showed that

\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor,c\in(1,\frac{30}{29})
is pointwise good for

L^{1}
, see also
(
Trojan 2021
)
. It would be interesting to know if
the latter result can be extended to all positive non integer
c
.
The case of
L^{2}
is known
(
Boshernitzan et al. 2005
)
as well as
L^{p}
,

.

For further exposition in this area, the reader is referred to the
survey article of
(
Rosenblatt & Wierdl 1995
)
.

The extreme case scenario of non-convergence is the strong sweeping
out property of a sequence.

(label=defn:1)

We say

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{a_{n}}x)

\displaystyle=1

(1.5)

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{a_{n}}x)

\displaystyle=0

(1.6)

Akcoglu etal
(
Akcoglu et al. 1996
)
showed that every lacunary sequence is
strong sweeping out, and our
extends their result.

can also be interpreted from a different viewpoint.
Jones-Wierdl
(
Jones & Wierdl 1994
, Corollary 2.14)
proved that for

if a sequence
(a_{n})
satisfies

\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}\geq 1+{\frac{1}{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{p}-\eta}}}

(a_{n})
is pointwise bad, and our

can be viewed as an extension of this result to not only

(

)
on lacunary polynomials.

In the next theorem, we will give a probabilistic condition for a
sequence to be strong sweeping out. Before we state the result, let us
explain the notion of a randomly generated sequence. Suppose

(\sigma_{n})
is a sequence of positive numbers;
\sigma_{n}
is the
probability with which
n
is chosen into the random sequence. More
precisely, let
Y_{n}
be a sequence of
0-1

the probability space
(\Omega,\beta,P)
so that
P(Y_{n}=1)=\sigma_{n}

and
P(Y_{n}=0)=1-\sigma_{n}.
For each
\omega\in\Omega
, let
A^{\omega}

be the sequence defined by the property that

if and
only if
Y_{n}(\omega)=1.
Our second main result is the following:

Theorem 4

(label=thm:3)
.

Strong sweeping out with probabilistic
conditionLet
\eta>0
be arbitrary and

\sigma_{n}=\frac{(\log\log\log n)^{1-\eta}}{n}
.

Then for a.e.
\omega
the random sequence

is strong sweeping out.

This result is an improvement of a result of
Jones-Lacey-Wierdl
(
Jones et al. 1999

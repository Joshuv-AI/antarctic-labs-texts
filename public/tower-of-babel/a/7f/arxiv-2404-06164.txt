A counterexample to the PIA conjecture for minimal log discrepancies
Yusuke Nakamura, Kohsuke Shibata

Abstract
. We give a counterexample to the PIA (precise inversion of adjunction) conjecture for minimal log discrepancies. We also give a counterexample to the LSC conjecture for families.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14E30; Secondary 14B05, 14E18, 14N30

1.
Introduction

The minimal log discrepancy is an invariant of singularities in birational geometry.
The PIA (precise inversion of adjunction) conjecture is a fundamental conjecture on the minimal log discrepancy,
as well as the ACC (ascending chain condition) conjecture and the LSC (lower semi-continuity) conjecture.
In this paper, we always work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero.

Conjecture 1.1

(PIA conjecture,
[
12
]
*17.3.1)
.

Let
(X,\mathfrak{a})
be a log pair over
k
and let
D
be a normal Cartier prime divisor on
X
.
Let
x\in D
be a closed point.
Suppose that
D
is not contained in the cosupport of the
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}
.
Then

\operatorname{mld}_{x}\bigl(X,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\bigr)=\operatorname{mld}_{x}(D,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{D})

holds.

The PIA conjecture is known to be true in the following cases:

(
1.1
.1)

the case when
\dim X=2
by Shokurov (
[
21
]
).

(
1.1
.2)

the case when
X
is smooth by Ein, Mustaţǎ, and Yasuda (
[
3
]
).

(
1.1
.3)

the case when
X
is a locally complete intersection variety by Ein and Mustaţǎ (
[
4
]
).

(
1.1
.4)

the case when
\dim X=3
(see Proposition
7.1
).

(
1.1
.5)

the case when
X
has only quotient singularities and
D
is klt at
x
by the authors (
[
19
,
16
]
).

(
1.1
.6)

In
[
19
,
16
]
(see also
[
17
]
), the authors more generally prove the PIA conjecture for the case when
X
is a subvariety of a variety
Y
such that

–

Y
has only quotient singularities at
x
,

–

X
is locally defined by
c
equations in
Y
at
x
for
c:=\dim Y-\dim X
, and

–

X
and
D
are klt at
x
.

The purpose of this paper is to give a counterexample to the PIA conjecture.

Example 1.2
.

Let
\xi\in k
be a primitive third root of unity.
Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
We define
R:=k[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+3}]
and

f:=x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}\in R,\qquad\gamma:=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(1,\xi,\xi^{2},\overbrace{\xi,\cdots,\xi}^{n}\bigr)\in\operatorname{GL}_{n+3}(k).

We define
G:=\langle\gamma\rangle=\{1,\gamma,\gamma^{2}\}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{n+3}(k)
, and

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Spec}(R)=\mathbb{A}^{n+3}_{k},

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:=\overline{A}_{n}/G=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(R^{G}\bigr),

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Spec}(R/(f)),

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle:=\overline{B}_{n}/G=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(R^{G}/(f)\bigr).

Let
\overline{x}_{0}=(0,\cdots,0)
denote the origin of
\overline{A}_{n}
,
and let
x_{0}\in A_{n}
denote the image of
\overline{x}_{0}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

In Example
1.2
, we have the following assertions.

(1)

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}(A_{n},B_{n})\leq 1+\frac{n}{3}
(see Section
5
for the exact value).

(2)

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}(B_{n})=n
.

(3)

When
n\geq 2
,
the PIA conjecture (Conjecture
1.1
) does not hold
for
(X,D)=(A_{n},B_{n})
and
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{O}_{A_{n}}
.

Thus, the PIA conjecture has a counterexample when
\dim X\geq 5
.
The conjecture is still open when
\dim X=4
(see Question
7.2
).
Note that
B_{n}
is not klt at
x_{0}
.
Therefore, it shows that the assumption “
D
is klt at
x
” in the authors’ result (
1.1
.5) above was necessary.
It also suggests that the PIA conjecture might be modified to add the additional assumption “
D
is klt at
x
” (see Question
7.3
).

The same example can be used to construct a counterexample to the LSC conjecture for families (see Example
6.1
).

An interesting point of this example is that we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}(B_{n})=\operatorname{mld}_{\overline{x}_{0}}(\overline{B}_{n})

even though the
G
-action on
\overline{B}_{n}
is not free.
To explain this phenomenon (
\spadesuit
), we introduce a new concept called “virtually free” for a finite group action (see Section
3
).
This concept lies between “free” and “free in codimension one”.
We prove that the minimal log discrepancy remains unchanged after taking the quotient of a virtually free action (Proposition
3.5
).
In order to prove the
G
-action on
\overline{B}_{n}
is virtually free,
we prove a permanence property of the virtual freeness when taking the product of varieties (Theorem
3.9
).

We prove that the virtual freeness is equivalent to the freeness when the considered variety is klt (Theorem
3.10
).
This fact explains why it is not possible to make a similar counterexample with only klt singularities.
We note that the phenomenon
(\spadesuit)
can also be explained from the perspective of arc space theory (see Remark
4.2
).

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
3
, we introduce the new concept called “virtual freeness” and discuss its properties.
In Section
4
, we give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.
In Section
5
, we determine the exact value of
\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}(A_{n},B_{n})
.
In Section
6
, we discuss two pathological examples related to the semi-continuity of minimal log discrepancies (Examples
6.1
,
6.2
).
In Section
7
, we give a proof of the PIA conjecture in dimension three, which seems to be well-known to experts (Proposition
7.1
).
We also give some related questions (Questions
7.2
,
7.3
).

2.2.
Log pairs

A
log pair

(X,\mathfrak{a})
is a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety
X
over
k
and
an
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}
on
X
.
Here, an
\mathbb{R}
-
ideal sheaf

\mathfrak{a}
on
X
is a formal product

\mathfrak{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{r_{i}}
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{s}
are non-zero coherent ideal sheaves on
X
and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{s}
are positive real numbers.
For a morphism
Y\to X
and an
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{r_{i}}
,
let
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
denote the
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\prod_{i=1}^{s}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{Y})^{r_{i}}
on
Y
.

Let
(X,\mathfrak{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{r_{i}})
be a log pair.
Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper birational morphism from a normal variety
X^{\prime}
, and let
E
be a prime divisor on
X^{\prime}
.
Then, the
log discrepancy
of
(X,\mathfrak{a})
at
E
is defined as

a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=1+\operatorname{coeff}_{E}(K_{X^{\prime}}-f^{*}K_{X})-\operatorname{ord}_{E}(\mathfrak{a}),

where
\operatorname{ord}_{E}(\mathfrak{a}):=\sum_{i=1}^{s}r_{i}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(\mathfrak{a}_{i})
.
The image
f(E)
is called the
center
of
E
on
X
and denoted by
c_{X}(E)
.
For a closed subset
W\subset X
, the
minimal log discrepancy
along
W
is defined as

\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=\inf_{c_{X}(E)\subset W}a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a})

if
\dim X\geq 2
, where the infimum is taken over all prime divisors
E
over
X
with center
c_{X}(E)\subset W
.
When
\dim X=1
, we define
\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=\inf_{c_{X}(E)\subset W}a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a})

if the infimum is non-negative and

\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=-\infty
otherwise.
It is known that
\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X,\mathfrak{a})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\}
(cf.
[
13
]
*Corollary 2.31).

Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
Take
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\ell D
is Cartier.
Then, we define
a_{E}(X,D):=a_{E}\bigl(X,(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\ell D))^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\bigr)
and
\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X,D):=\operatorname{mld}_{W}\bigl(X,(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\ell D))^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\bigr)
.
Note that these values do not depend on the choice of
\ell
.

When
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, we define
a_{E}(X):=a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a})
and
\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X):=\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X,\mathfrak{a})
for simplicity.
When
W=\{x\}
for some closed point
x\in X
,
we denote
\operatorname{mld}_{\{x\}}
by
\operatorname{mld}_{x}
for simplicity.

3.
Virtually free action

In this section, we introduce a new concept called “virtually free” for a finite group action.
This concept lies between “free” and “free in codimension one”.
In Proposition
3.5
, we prove that the minimal log discrepancy remains unchanged after taking the quotient of a virtually free action.
In Proposition
3.8
, we provide several characterizations of the virtual freeness.
In Theorem
3.9
, we prove a permanence property of the virtual freeness when taking the product of varieties.
In Theorem
3.10
, we show that the virtual freeness is equivalent to the freeness when the considered variety is klt.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
X
be a normal variety.
We say that a
G
-action on
X
is
virtually free
if the following condition holds:

•

For any
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y
with a
G
-action,
the
G
-action on
Y
is free in codimension one.

Remark 3.2
.

By the definition, we have implications
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
among the following three conditions:

(1)

a
G
-action on
X
is free.

(2)

a
G
-action on
X
is virtually free.

(3)

a
G
-action on
X
is free in codimension one.

In Theorem
3.10
, we will see
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
when
X
is klt.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
Let
E
be a divisor over
X
.
Then, there exists a
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y
with a
G
-action such that
E
is a prime divisor on
Y
.

Proof.

By
[
13
]
*Lemma 2.45, the desired
Y
is obtained by repeating the blow-up along the
G
-orbit of the center of
E
and taking the normalization.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
Let
W\to X/G
be a proper birational morphism from a normal variety
W
.
Let
W^{\prime}
be the normalization of
W
in the field
k(X)
of fractions of
X
.
Then, the
G
-action on
X
extends to
W^{\prime}
, and we have
W^{\prime}/G\simeq W
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
. Suppose that the
G
-action on
X
is virtually free.
Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a non-zero
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf on
X/G
.
Then, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{X})=\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(X/G,\mathfrak{a})

for any closed point
x\in X
and its image
x^{\prime}
on the quotient variety
X/G
.

Proof.

Let
Y\to X
be any
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism from a normal variety
Y
.
Let
E
be a prime divisor on
Y
, and
F
the image of
E
on
Y/G
.
Since
X\to X/G
and
Y\to Y/G
are étale in codimension one, we have
a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{X})=a_{F}(X/G,\mathfrak{a})
.
Therefore, the assertion follows from Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a group. Let
f_{1}:Y_{1}\to X
and
f_{2}:Y_{2}\to X
be two
G
-equivariant proper birational morphisms between normal varieties
X
,
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
with
G
-actions.
Suppose that prime divisors
E_{1}
on
Y_{1}
and
E_{2}
on

are the strict transforms of each other (by the maps
f_{2}^{-1}\circ f_{1}
and
f_{1}^{-1}\circ f_{2}
).
Then, for
\gamma\in G
,
E_{1}
is point-wise fixed by
\gamma
if and only if
E_{2}
is point-wise fixed by
\gamma
.

Proof.

We can take a normal variety
W
with a
G
-action equipped with
G
-equivariant proper birational morphisms

and
W\to Y_{2}
that are compatible with
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
.
Therefore, we may assume that
f_{2}^{-1}\circ f_{1}:Y_{1}\dasharrow Y_{2}
is a morphism from the beginning.
Since
Y_{2}
is normal, the morphism
f_{2}^{-1}\circ f_{1}
is isomorphic at the generic point of
E_{2}
, which proves the assertion.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
W
be a variety of dimension
N
and let
x\in W
be a closed point.
Suppose that
W
is smooth at
x
.
Let
\gamma:W\to W
be an automorphism of
W
with finite order
d
.
Suppose that
\gamma
fixes
x
.
Let
\xi\in k
be a primitive
d
-th root of unity.

(1)

Then, there exist a regular system of parameters
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in\mathcal{O}_{W,x}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
such that

\gamma^{*}(f_{i})=\xi^{a_{i}}f_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq N
.

(2)

Let
W^{\prime}
be the weighted blow-up of
W
at
x
with respect to the regular system of parameters
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
and the weight
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})
in (1).
That is,
W^{\prime}=\operatorname{Proj}_{W}\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
is the ideal generated by all monomials
f_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots f_{N}^{c_{N}}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}c_{i}\geq\ell
.
Then, the automorphism
\gamma
on
W
extends to
W^{\prime}
and the exceptional divisor of
W^{\prime}\to W
is point-wise fixed by
\gamma
.

Proof.

The assertion (1) follows from the proof of
[
11
]
*Thorem 2.2.4 (see
[
11
]
*Remark 2.2.5).

Next, we prove (2).
Let
S=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
.

W^{\prime}
is covered by open affine subsets
D_{+}(f_{1}),\ldots,D_{+}(f_{N})
corresponding to the elements
f_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{a_{i}}
.
Note that each
D_{+}(f_{i})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f_{i})}
, where
S_{(f_{i})}
is the subring of
S_{f_{i}}
consisting of degree zero elements.
Under this isomorphism, the composition map
D_{+}(f_{i})\hookrightarrow W^{\prime}\to W
is corresponding to the inclusion
S_{(f_{i})}=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}f_{i}^{-m}\mathcal{I}_{ma_{i}}\subset k(W)
.
The
\gamma
induces an automorphism
\gamma^{*}
on the field
k(W)
, and we have
\gamma^{*}\bigl(S_{(f_{i})}\bigr)=S_{(f_{i})}
for each
i
.
Therefore,
\gamma
induces an automorphism on
D_{+}(f_{i})
.

Let
E
be the exceptional divisor of
W^{\prime}\to W
.
By symmetry, it is sufficient to prove that all closed points
x\in E\cap D_{+}(f_{N})
are fixed by
\gamma
.
Let
\varphi:S_{(f_{N})}\to k
be the ring homomorphism corresponding to such
x
.
As an
\mathcal{O}_{W}
-algebra,
S_{(f_{N})}
is generated by the elements
h=f_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots f_{N}^{b_{N}}
satisfying
a_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+a_{N}b_{N}\geq 0
and

.
Therefore, it is sufficient to show that
\varphi\circ\gamma^{*}(h)=\varphi(h)
for such

If

.
In this case, we have

If

\gamma^{*}(h)=h
.
In both cases, we conclude

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The
G
-action on
X
is virtually free.

(2)

There exists a
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y
such that the
G
-action on
Y
is free.

(3)

For any
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a smooth variety
Y
, the
G
-action on
Y
is free.

Proof.

Since we can always take a
G
-equivariant resolution of
X
, the implication
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
is clear.

We prove
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
.
Suppose that (1) does not hold.
Then, there exist a
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y

and a prime divisor
E
on
Y
such that
E
is point-wise fixed by some
\gamma\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
.
Let
Y^{\prime}\to X
be any
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism from a normal variety
Y^{\prime}
.
We shall prove that the
G
-action on
Y^{\prime}
is not free.
We take a normal variety
W
with a
G
-action equipped with
G
-equivariant proper birational morphisms
W\to Y
and
W\to Y^{\prime}
that commute with
Y\to X
and
Y^{\prime}\to X
.
Since
W\to Y
is proper and
Y
is normal,
W\to Y
is isomorphic outside a codimension two closed subset of
Y
.
Therefore, the strict transform
E_{W}
of
E
on
W
is point-wise fixed by
\gamma
.
Since
W
has a
\gamma
-fixed point, we conclude that
Y^{\prime}
also has a
\gamma
-fixed point, which proves that the
G
-action on
Y^{\prime}
is not free.

We prove
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
.
Suppose that (3) does not hold.
Then, there exists a
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a smooth variety
Y
such that
some closed point
y\in Y
is fixed by some
\gamma\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
.
Let
Y^{\prime}\to Y
be the weighted blow-up corresponding to the action of
\gamma
as in Lemma
3.7
.
Then, the
\gamma
-action on
Y
extends to
Y^{\prime}
and the exceptional divisor
E
is point-wise fixed by
\gamma
.
Note here that the entire
G
-action on
Y
does not necessarily lift to
Y^{\prime}
.
However, by Lemma
3.3
, we can take a
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
W\to X
with a prime divisor
E_{W}
on
W
such that
E_{W}
is the strict transform of
E
.
By Lemma
3.6
,
E_{W}
is also point-wise fixed by
\gamma
.
Therefore, the
G
-action on
W
is not free in codimension one,
and we conclude that the
G
-action on
X
is not virtually free.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
G

Then, for any normal variety

Proof.

The assertion follows from the characterization (2) in Proposition

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a klt variety
X
.
If the
G
-action on
X
is virtually free, then this action is free.

Proof.

We may assume that
k=\mathbb{C}
.

Suppose that the action is not free.
Then, there exists a closed point

fixed by some
\gamma\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}

Let
Y\to X
be any
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism from a normal variety
Y
.
By Proposition
3.8
, it is sufficient to prove that
Y
always has a
\gamma
-fixed point.
Take a resolution

of the singularities of
Y/G
.
Let
W^{\prime}
be the normalization of
W
in the field
k(Y)
. Then,
G

and we have

(cf. Lemma

Let
f
and

\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y/G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X/G}

Since

is klt at

,

is also klt at the image

A Langlands duality of elliptic Hecke algebras
Gufang Zhao, Changlong Zhong

Abstract
. Associated to any root datum, there is an elliptic affine Hecke algebra defined by Ginzburg, Kapranov, and Vasserot. In this study, we establish a Fourier-Mukai functor from the representation category of the elliptic affine Hecke algebra to the corresponding category associated with the Langlands dual root datum. To achieve this connection, we employ the elliptic Hecke algebra with dynamical parameters as an intermediary.



0.
Introduction

The notion of an elliptic affine Hecke algebra, associated to a root datum, was introduced by Ginzburg, Kapranov, and Vasserot
[
GKV97
]
as a coherent sheaf on an abelian variety. In this paper, we refer to this algebra as the GKV elliptic Hecke algebra or simply the GKV algebra. Notably, a representation of this algebra has an underlying coherent sheaf on an abelian variety. This observation naturally leads us to the question of whether the Fourier-Mukai transform of the abelian variety can be extended to the representation categories of elliptic affine Hecke algebras. In this work, we provide an affirmative answer to this question.

One crucial component of our study involves the introduction of a version of elliptic affine Hecke algebras with dynamical parameters.
This algebra is a sheaf defined on the product of the aforementioned abelian variety and its dual. An essential feature of this algebra is that the correct notion of polynomial representation is given by (a translate of) the Poincaré line bundle.
Various components of this algebra have already appeared in literature. Notably,
Demazure-Lusztig operators with dynamical parameters were originally discovered by Rimányi and Weber in the context of elliptic Schubert calculus
[
RW20
]
. A remarkable consequence of their findings is that these operators satisfy the braid relation. Building upon these ideas, in
[
ZZ22
]
, it was observed that these operators can be embedded within an elliptic Hecke algebra with dynamical parameters, a variant of the GKV algebra with the aforementioned polynomial representation, partially inspired by Felder’s dynamical elliptic
R
-matrices as well as
[
RW20
]
.
However, for the purpose of representation theory, it is necessary to find a suitable integral structure.
In §
3
, we provide a GKV-style definition of the elliptic Hecke algebra with dynamical parameters (see Definition
3.2
), as well as a definition of the representation category associated to this algebra.

Utilizing the Poincaré line bundle, we construct a functor from the representation category of the GKV algebra to that of the dynamical elliptic Hecke algebra (see Theorem
4.1
). Furthermore, we elucidate the Weyl group relations of the dynamical Demazure-Lusztig operators in terms of the Langlands dual root datum (see Theorem
4.5
). By combining these results, we demonstrate that the Fourier-Mukai transform on abelian varieties can be extended to the representation categories of GKV algebras (see Corollary
5.1
).
Various consequences deduced using this functor are discussed in §
5
.

1.
The dynamical elliptic twisted group algebra

1.1.
Setup

We recall the notion of root datum following
[
SGA3
, Exp. XXI, § 1.1]
.
A root datum of rank
n
consists of two lattices
X^{*}
and
X_{*}
of rank
n
with a perfect
{\mathbb{Z}}
-values pairing
\langle-,-\rangle
, non-empty finite subsets
\Phi\subset X^{*}
, and
\Phi^{\vee}\subset X_{*}
with a bijection
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\vee}
.
For simplicity, we do not distinguish an abstract root datum and that of a connected, complex reductive group. If the group has maximal torus
T
, the lattices above are respectively
{\mathbb{X}}^{*}(T)
and
{\mathbb{X}}_{*}(T)
, the group of characters and co-characters of
T
.
In the present paper, we fix a set
\Sigma
of simple roots
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}
, with simple reflections
s_{i}:=s_{\alpha_{i}}
. The Weyl group is denoted by
W
. For each
w\in W
, define
\Phi(w)=w\Phi^{-}\cap\Phi^{+}
.
Let
\rho
be the half-sum of all positive roots.

Throughout this paper,
E
is an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{C}}
of the form

E\cong{\mathbb{C}}/({\mathbb{Z}}+{\mathbb{Z}}\tau)\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{*}/q^{\mathbb{Z}},

where
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\im\tau>0
, and
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
.
We identify
E^{\vee}=\Pic^{0}(E)
with
E
itself, via the map
E\to E^{\vee}
,
\lambda\mapsto\{\lambda\}-\{0\}
.
In particular, on
E\times E
, there is a universal line bundle
{\mathcal{P}}
, the Poincaré line bundle.

Consider the origin
0\in E
. When the line bundle

{\mathcal{O}}(\{0\})
is lifted to the cover
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, it becomes trivial. This lift is denoted by
\tilde{L}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, and it comes with a lifted section
\tilde{s}:{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\to\tilde{L}
.
The trivialization

\textstyle{\tilde{L}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{s}}

can be uniquely determined based on two conditions:
\varphi
must commute with multiplication by

q^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and the derivative of
\varphi\circ\tilde{s}
should be equal to 1 at
1\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

[
S80
, p.38]
.
Recall the Jacobi Theta function

\vartheta(u)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}(u^{1/2}-u^{-1/2})\prod_{s>0}(1-q^{s}u)(1-q^{s}u^{-1})\cdot\prod_{s>0}(1-q^{s})^{-2},\quad u\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}.

Assume
|q|<1
, this series converges and defines a holomorphic function on a double cover on
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
It vanishes of order 1 at
q^{\mathbb{Z}}
, is non-zero everywhere else, and its derivative at
1\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is equal to 1.

Let us denote the coordinates of
E
and
E^{\vee}
as
z
and
\lambda
, respectively. The following function

\frac{\vartheta(z-\lambda)}{\vartheta(z)}

becomes a rational section of

{\mathcal{P}}
.

1.2.
The (dynamical) elliptic twisted group algebra

Define
\mathfrak{A}=X_{*}\otimes E
. We have an isomorphism

(1)

\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}\cong X^{*}\otimes E.

Each
\mu\in X^{*}
defines a map
\chi_{\mu}:\mathfrak{A}\to E
. Denote
z_{\mu}=\chi_{\mu}(z),z\in\mathfrak{A}
. Dually, each
\mu^{\vee}\in X_{*}
defines a map
\chi_{\mu^{\vee}}:\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}\to E
, and denote
\lambda_{\mu^{\vee}}=\chi_{\mu^{\vee}}(\lambda),\lambda\in\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
.
To make the isomorphism (
1
) explicit, we consider a special point
\alpha\otimes t\in X^{*}\otimes E
, with
\alpha\in X^{*}
and
t\in E
.
The point
\alpha
defines a map
\chi_{\alpha}:\mathfrak{A}\to E
, and
t\in E=E^{\vee}
defines a degree 0 line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}(t)
on
E
.
Under the above isomorphism, the line bundle on
\mathfrak{A}
corresponding to
\alpha\otimes t
is
\chi_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(t)
.
In what follows, we write
\alpha\otimes t
simply as
t\alpha
.
For each
z\in\mathfrak{A},\lambda\in\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
, the associated line bundle over
\mathfrak{A}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)
.

The Weyl group
W
acts on both
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
, and hence on the product
\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
, we have the action of product of two Weyl groups. To avoid confusion, we write the factor of
W
acting on
\mathfrak{A}
as
W
and the factor acting on
\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
as
W^{\operatorname{d}}
. Similarly, for any element
w\in W
, we write the element in
W^{\operatorname{d}}
as
w^{\operatorname{d}}
.

We define the category
\Coh^{W\times W^{\operatorname{d}}}(\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee})
of
W\times W^{\operatorname{d}}
-graded coherent sheaves on
\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
. The objects are coherent sheaves with a direct-sum decomposition
{\mathcal{F}}=\oplus_{w\in W,v\in W^{\operatorname{d}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{w,v}
with
{\mathcal{F}}_{w,v}
a coherent sheaf, called the degree-
(w,v)
piece of
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Morphisms in this category respect the grading.
We then define a monoidal structure on this category. It suffices to work with each graded piece. We define

{\mathcal{F}}_{w,v}\star{\mathcal{F}}_{w^{\prime},v^{\prime}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{F}}_{w,v}\otimes(w^{-1})^{*}(v^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{w^{\prime},v^{\prime}},

which is set to be of degree
(ww^{\prime},vv^{\prime})
.
The object
{\mathcal{O}}
concentrated in degree 0 is the unit object.
The verification of the monoidal axioms are straightforward.

On
\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
there is a Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{P}}
. See
[
P03
, § 9.3]
for its defining properties. Define

{\mathbb{L}}:={\mathcal{P}}\otimes({\mathcal{O}}(\hbar\rho)\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}(-\hbar\rho^{\vee})),

and define
{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}={\mathbb{L}}\otimes(w^{-1})^{*}(v^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}
.
The object
{\mathbb{S}}_{W\times W^{\operatorname{d}}}:=\bigoplus_{w,v\in W}{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}
has the structure of a monad (algebra) object in the category
\Coh^{W\times W^{\operatorname{d}}}(\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee})
given by the identity map

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}\star{\mathbb{S}}_{x,y}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}\otimes(w^{-1})^{*}(v^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathbb{S}}_{x,y}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{L}}\otimes(w^{-1})^{*}(v^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}\otimes(w^{-1})^{*}(v^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathbb{L}}\otimes(w^{-1})^{*}(v^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}(x^{-1})^{*}(y^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\cong{\mathbb{L}}\otimes((wx)^{-1})^{*}((vy)^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{S}}_{wx,vy},

called the
(dynamical) elliptic twisted group algebra
.
We also write
{\mathbb{S}}
for short.

Notice also that the monoidal category
\Coh^{W\times W^{\operatorname{d}}}(\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee})
acts on
\Coh(\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee})
. On graded pieces, the action is given by

{\mathcal{F}}_{w,v}\star{\mathcal{G}}:={\mathcal{F}}_{w,v}\otimes(w^{-1})^{*}(v^{-1})^{{\operatorname{d}}*}{\mathcal{G}}.

The object
{\mathbb{L}}
in
\Coh(\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee})
with the identity map

{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}\star{\mathbb{L}}\to{\mathbb{L}}

is a module over the algebra object
{\mathbb{S}}
. We refer interested readers to
[
LZZ23
]
for more details of this monoidal category, the algebra object, as well as their applications in elliptic Schubert calculus.

Similarly we have the monoidal categories
\Coh^{W}(\mathfrak{A})
and
\Coh^{W^{\operatorname{d}}}(\mathfrak{A}^{\vee})
. Obviously, the twisted group algebra

(2)

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes W:=\bigoplus_{w\in W}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{A}}

is an algebra object in
\Coh^{W}(\mathfrak{A})
. Similar for
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}}\rtimes W^{\operatorname{d}}
.

3.
Residue definition of the dynamical Hecke algebra

We define the elliptic Hecke algebra with dynamical parameters.
This algebra is not only the key ingredient in construction of the Fourier-Mukai functor, but also interesting on its own. The images of this algebra under various localization functors have already appeared in
[
ZZ22
,
LZZ23
]
. However, for the purpose of representation theory, it is necessary to find a suitable integral structure, which we carry out in this section, following
[
GKV97
, Definition 1.3]
.

3.1.
The residues of rational sections of line bundles

Let
U\subseteq\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}^{\vee}
be an open subset, with
f
a rational section of
{\mathcal{L}}
on
U
, with a pole along
D_{\alpha}
of order at most 1.
The residue of
f
along
D_{\alpha}
is denoted by

\Res_{\alpha}(f)=(\vartheta(z_{\alpha})f)|_{D_{\alpha}}.

In words, taking residue is the same as evaluating
\vartheta(z_{\alpha})f
at
z_{\alpha}=0
. In particular, the result is a rational section of
({\mathcal{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(-D_{\alpha}))|_{D_{\alpha}}
on
D_{\alpha}\cap U
.
If
a,b
are rational sections of line bundles
{\mathcal{L}}_{1},{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
, together with a canonical isomorphism
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}|_{D_{\alpha}}\cong{\mathcal{L}}_{2}|_{D_{\alpha}}
, then
a|_{D_{\alpha}}+b|_{D_{\alpha}}
is defined via this isomorphism. In this case, the following definition makes sense:

\Res_{\alpha}(a+b):=\Res_{\alpha}(a)+\Res_{\alpha}(b).

Lemma 3.1
.

(1)

We have canonical isomorphisms
s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}^{-1}
and
{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}|_{D_{\alpha}}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha}w,v}|_{D_{\alpha}}
.

(2)

If
f_{\alpha}
is a rational section of
{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}
, and
a
is a rational section of
{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha}w,v}
, then
{}^{s_{\alpha}}a\cdot f_{\alpha}
is a rational section of
{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}
.

Proof.

(1).The first one follows from the definition
{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}={\mathbb{L}}\otimes s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}
. For the second one, recall that

{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha}w,v}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}\otimes s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}.

Note that
s_{\alpha}
fixes
D_{\alpha}
, so

{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}|_{D_{\alpha}}=({\mathbb{L}}\otimes s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1})|_{D_{\alpha}}={\mathbb{L}}|_{D_{\alpha}}\otimes(s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1})|_{D_{\alpha}}\cong{\mathbb{L}}|_{D_{\alpha}}\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}|_{D_{\alpha}}={\mathcal{O}}|_{D_{\alpha}}.

Similarly,
(s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v})|_{D_{\alpha}}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}|_{D_{\alpha}}
. Therefore,
{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}|_{D_{\alpha}}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha}w,v}|_{D_{\alpha}}
.

(2). The isomorphism of (1) gives

s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha}w,v}\cong s_{\alpha}^{*}{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}\otimes{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}\cong{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha},e}^{-1}\otimes{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}.

The conclusion then follows.
∎

Consequently,
if
f
is a rational section of
{\mathbb{S}}_{w,v}
and
g
is a rational section of
{\mathbb{S}}_{s_{\alpha}w,v}
,

\Res_{\alpha}(f+g)=\Res_{\alpha}(f)+\Res_{\alpha}(g)
makes sense.

3.2.
The elliptic Hecke algebra with dynamical parameters

Consider

As in

Definition 3.2
.

Let

\Res_{\alpha}(a_{w,v}+a_{s_{\alpha}w,v})=0.

ii’)

\Res_{\alpha^{\vee}}(a_{w,v}+a_{w,s_{\alpha}v})=0
.

iii)

For any

The conditions on an expression (
4

and

are coherent sheaves.

Recall that for a coherent sheaf on

and

Theorem 3.4

The subsheaves

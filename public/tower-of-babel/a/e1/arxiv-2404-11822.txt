Shiliang Wu, Deren Han, Cuixia Li

Abstract
In this paper, by using | x | = 2 ​ max ⁡ { 0 , x } − x |x|=2\max\{0,x\}-x , a class of maximum-based iteration methods is established to solve the generalized absolute value equation A ​ x − B ​ | x | = b Ax-B|x|=b . Some convergence conditions of the proposed method are presented. By some numerical experiments, the effectiveness and feasibility of the proposed method are confirmed. Keywords: Generalized absolute value equation; iteration method; convergence AMS classification: 65F10

Introduction

The generalized absolute value equation (GAVE) is to look for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

Ax-B|x|=b\ \mbox{with}\ A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\mbox{and}\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

where
|\cdot|
denotes the absolute value. The GAVE (
1.1
)
often arises in diverse fields, like flows in porous media, linear
programming, nonnegative constrained least squares problems,
quadratic programming, complementarity problem, bimatrix games, and
others, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

Nowdays, for the theoretical analysis of the GAVE (
1.1
),
there exist many interesting results. For example, Wu and Shen in

[
7
]
presented some necessary and sufficient conditions for
the unique solvability of the GAVE (
1.1
). Wu and Li

[
8
]
discussed the error and perturbation bounds of the GAVE
(
1.1
). Hladík in
[
9
]
investigated the
properties of the solution set of the GAVE (
1.1
) with
B=I

(the didentity matrix). Zamani and Hladík in
[
10
]

discussed its error bounds and a condition number.

Besides that, to efficiently solve the GAVE (
1.1
), many
efficient iteration methods have been designed, including the
successive linearization method
[
2
]
, the sign accord
method
[
11
]
, the Picard and Picard-HSS methods

[
12
]
, the generalized Newton (GN) method

[
13
]
, and so on. Recently, Wang et al. in

[
14
]
designed the modified Newton (MN) method, see Method 1.

Method 1
Assume that
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
arbitrary initial guess. For
k=0,1,2,\ldots
until the iteration
sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is convergent,
computing
x^{(k+1)}
by

x^{k+1}=(A+\Omega)^{-1}(\Omega x^{k}+B|x^{k}|+b),k=0,1,,2,\ldots,

(1.2)

where
A+\Omega
is invertible and
\Omega
is positive
semi-definite.

To expedite the convergence speed of Method 1, by making use of the
matrix splitting technique, i.e., by expressing
A
as
A=M-N
, Zhou

et al.
in
[
15
]
presented the following Newton-based
matrix splitting (NMS) method, see Method 2.

Method 2
Assume that
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
arbitrary initial guess. For
k=0,1,2,\ldots
until the iteration
sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is convergent,
computing
x^{k+1}
by

\begin{split}x^{k+1}=(M+\Omega)^{-1}((N+\Omega)x^{k}+B|x^{k}|+b),k=0,1,2\ldots,\end{split}

(1.3)

where
\Omega+M
is invertible and
\Omega
is a given matrix.

Comparing Method 2 with Method 1, the former not only covers the
latter, but also may be performed easily by choosing the suitable
matrix
\Omega
and matrix splitting of
A
, moreover, may be fit
for the indefinite matrix
A
as well.

In this paper, we continuously develop the numerical method for
solving the GAVE (
1.1
) on condition that the GAVE
(
1.1
) has a unique solution (see
[
7
]
). By using

|x|=2\max\{0,x\}-x
for the GAVE (
1.1
), a class of
maximum-based iteration methods is established, and is completely
different from the above existing numerical methods. Not only that,
the building new iteration method does not contain
|x|
, and takes
full advantage of information of matrix
B
. In particular, under
certain condition, determining the solution of the absolute value
equation is equal to determining the solution of the linear systems.
We investigate the convergence properties of the proposed method.
Further, by using some numerical experiments, the effectiveness and
feasibility of the proposed method are confirmed.

The remainder of the paper is organized below. In Section 2, a
class of maximum-based iteration methods is established, and its
convergence properties are discussed. In Section 3, the numerical
results for the proposed method are reported. In Section 4, we draw
some conclusions to finish this paper.

2
A class of maximum-based iteration methods

Firstly, for the later discussion, we recall some necessary
terminology and lemmas.

For
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, it is called as a

Z
-matrix if
a_{ij}\leq 0
for
i\neq j
; a nonsingular
M
-matrix
if
A^{-1}\geq 0
and
A
is a
Z
-matrix; an
H
-matrix if its
comparison matrix
\langle A\rangle=(\langle a_{ij}\rangle)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
(
\langle a_{ii}\rangle=|a_{ii}|
and

\langle a_{ij}\rangle=-|a_{ij}|
for
i\neq j
) is a nonsingular

M
-matrix; an
H_{+}
-matrix if it is an
H
-matrix with positive
diagonal entries, see
[
16
]
.
\rho(A)
and
\|A\|_{2}
in order
denote the spectral radius and the 2-norm of the matrix
A
.

Let
A=M-N
with
\mbox{det}(M)\neq 0
. Then it is named an

M
-splitting if
N\geq 0
and
M
is a nonsingular
M
-matrix.
Further, if
A=M-N
is an
M
-splitting with
A
being a
nonsingular
M
-matrix, then
\rho(M^{-1}N)<1
, see
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
18
]

Let
A=D-B
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is an
H
-matrix
and
D
is its diagonal part. Then matrices
A
and
|D|
are
nonsingular,
|A^{-1}|\leq\langle A\rangle^{-1}
.

Secondly, we will establish a class of maximum-based iteration
methods to solve the GAVE (
1.1
). To this end, by using

|x|=2\max\{0,x\}-x
for the GAVE (
1.1
), the GAVE
(
1.1
) is equal to

(A+B+\Omega)x=\Omega x+2B\max\{0,x\}+b,

(2.1)

where
A+B+\Omega
is invertible. Hence, based on Eq. (
2.1
),
a class of maximum-based iteration methods can be established, see
Method 3.

Method 3
Assume that
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
arbitrary initial guess. For
k=0,1,2,\ldots
until the iteration
sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is convergent,
computing
x^{k+1}
by

\begin{split}x^{k+1}=(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}(\Omega x^{k}+2B\max\{0,x^{k}\}+b),\end{split}

(2.2)

where matrix
A+B+\Omega
is invertible.

Clearly, Method 3 gives a new general framework for solving the GAVE
(
1.1
). Similar to Method 2, some relaxation methods are
generated by selecting the matrix splitting of matrix
A+B
for
(
2.1
). Here is omitted. It is easy to see that Method 3 does
not contain
|x|
and takes full advantage of information of matrix

B
.

In addition, it is noted that if
x
is non positive, i.e.,
x\leq 0
, then Eq. (
2.1
) reduces to the linear systems

(A+B+\Omega)x=\Omega x+b\ \mbox{or}\ (A+B)x=b;

if
x
is positive, i.e.,
x>0
, then Eq. (
2.1
) reduces to
the linear systems

(A-B+\Omega)x=\Omega x+b\ \mbox{or}\ (A-B)x=b.

This shows that under certain condition, determining the solution of
the absolute value equation is equal to determining the solution of
the linear system. That happens all the time, see the next section
(Examples 3.1 and 3.2). In such case, we can employ some numerical
methods, like Krylov subspace methods, for solving the corresponding
linear systems. That is to say, once we judge that the solution
x

of the GAVE (
1.1
) is non positive or positive, i.e.,
x\leq 0
or
x>0
, we only needs to deal with the linear systems, no
longer deal with the GAVE (
1.1
).

Finally, we turn to discuss the convergence property of Method 3.
Theorem
2.1
presents a general convergence condition of
Method 3 for solving the GAVE (
1.1
) when the related matrix
is nonsingular.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A+B+\Omega
be invertible. Define

f_{\Omega}=|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}\Omega|,g_{\Omega}=2|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}B|.

If

\rho(f_{\Omega}+g_{\Omega})<1,

(2.3)

then Method 3 is convergent.

Proof.
Assume that a solution of the GVAE (
1.1
) is

x^{\ast}
. Combining (
2.1
) with (
2.2
), we get

x^{k+1}-x^{\ast}=(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}(\Omega(x^{k}-x^{\ast})+2B(\max\{0,x^{k}\}-\max\{0,x^{\ast}\}).

(2.4)

Together with Corollary 2.1 in
[
19
]
, using the absolute value
for (
2.4
) leads to

\displaystyle|x^{k+1}-x^{\ast}|=

\displaystyle|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}(\Omega(x^{k}-x^{\ast})+2B(\max\{0,x^{k}\}-\max\{0,x^{\ast}\})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}\Omega(x^{k}-x^{\ast})|+2|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}B(\max\{0,x^{k}\}-\max\{0,x^{\ast}\})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}\Omega||x^{k}-x^{\ast}|+2|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}B||x^{k}-x^{\ast}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f_{\Omega}+g_{\Omega})|x^{k}-x^{\ast}|.

Clearly, Method 3 is convergent under the condition (
2.3
).

\hfill{}\Box

Based on Theorem
2.1
, it is easy to see that Method 3 is also
convergent if
A,B
and
\Omega
satisfy

\|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}\|_{2}(\|\Omega\|_{2}+2\|B\|_{2})<1.

Further, together with Banach perturbation Lemma in
[
20
]
,
Theorem
2.2
can be given.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
A+B
and
A+B+\Omega
be invertible. If

\|(A+B)^{-1}\|_{2}<(2\|\Omega\|_{2}+2\|B\|_{2})^{-1},

(2.5)

then Method 3 is convergent.

Proof.
Based on the Banach perturbation lemma in

[
20
]
, we have

\displaystyle\|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|(A+B)^{-1}\|_{2}}{1-\|(A+B)^{-1}\|_{2}\|\Omega\|_{2}}<\frac{\frac{1}{2\|B\|_{2}+2\|\Omega\|_{2}}}{1-\frac{\|\Omega\|_{2}}{2\|B\|_{2}+2\|\Omega\|_{2}}}=\frac{1}{\|\Omega\|_{2}+2\|B\|_{2}}.

This shows that Method 3 is convergent under the condition
(
2.5
).
\hfill{}\Box

In addition, we consider two cases: (1) matrix
A+B
is symmetric
positive definite; (2) matrix
A+B
is an
H_{+}
-matrix.

When matrix
A+B
is symmetric positive definite, for
\Omega=\omega I
with
\omega>0
, Theorem
2.3
can be obtained.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
A+B
be symmetric positive definite,
2\|B\|_{2}=\tau
and

\Omega=\omega I
with
\omega>0
. Further, let
\mu_{\min}
be the
smallest eigenvalue of the matrix
A+B
. If

\tau<\mu_{\min},

(2.6)

then Method 3 is convergent.

Proof.
By assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}\|_{2}(\|\Omega\|_{2}+2\|B\|_{2})

\displaystyle=\|(A+B+\omega I)^{-1}\|_{2}(\|\omega I\|_{2}+2\|B\|_{2})=\frac{\omega+\tau}{\mu_{\min}+\omega}.

It is not difficult to check that Method 3 is convergent under the
condition (
2.6
).
\hfill{}\Box

The advantage of Theorem
2.3
is that it does not require that

A
and
B
must be symmetric positive definite.

When matrix
A+B
is an
H_{+}
-matrix, Theorem
2.4
can be
derived.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A+B
be an
H_{+}
-matrix, and
\Omega
be a positive diagonal
matrix. If
\langle A+B\rangle-2|B|
is an
M
-matrix, then Method 3
is convergent.

Proof.
By assumptions and Lemma 2.1, we know

|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}|\leq(\langle A+B\rangle+\Omega)^{-1}.

Based on the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|x^{k+1}-x^{\ast}|

\displaystyle\leq|(A+B+\Omega)^{-1}|\cdot(\Omega+2|B|)|x^{k}-x^{\ast}\leq(\langle A+B\rangle+\Omega)^{-1}(\Omega+2|B|)|x^{k}-x^{\ast}|.

Therefore, Method 3 is convergent if
\langle A+B\rangle-2|B|
is an

M
-matrix.
\hfill{}\Box

Similar to Theorem
2.3
, the advantage of Theorem
2.4
is
that it does not require that
A
and
B
must be an
H_{+}
-matrix.
In particular, for
x^{\ast}
being non positive (it is equal to the
nonlinear term vanished in Method 3), when
A+B
is an

H_{+}
-matrix, Method 3 is convergent for any positive diagonal
matrix
\Omega
.

4
Conclusions

In this paper, a class of maximum-based iteration methods has been
designed to solve the GAVE (
1.1
) by using

|x|=2\max\{0,x\}-x
. Its convergence conditions are presented. What
we find interesting is that under certain condition determining the
solution of the absolute value equation is equal to determining the
solution of the linear system. Numerical experiments verify that
this new iteration method are superior to some existing iteration
methods, including the modified Newton method, some Krylov subspace
methods like GMRES(20).

Graph States and the Variety of Principal Minors
Vincenzo Galgano, Frédéric Holweck

Abstract
In Quantum Information theory, graph states are quantum states defined by graphs. In this work we exhibit a correspondence between graph states and the variety of binary symmetric principal minors, in particular their corresponding orbits under the action of S ​ L ​ ( 2 , 𝔽 2 ) × n ⋊ 𝔖 n SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n} . We start by approaching the topic more widely, that is by studying the orbits of maximal abelian subgroups of the n n -fold Pauli group under the action of 𝒞 n loc ⋊ 𝔖 n \mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n} , where 𝒞 n loc \mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}} is the n n -fold local Clifford group: we show that this action corresponds to the natural action of S ​ L ​ ( 2 , 𝔽 2 ) × n ⋊ 𝔖 n SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n} on the variety 𝒵 n ⊂ ℙ ⁡ ( 𝔽 2 2 n ) \mathcal{Z}_{n}\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{n}}) of principal minors of binary symmetric n × n n\times n matrices. The crucial step in this correspondence is in translating the action of S ​ L ​ ( 2 , 𝔽 2 ) × n SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n} into an action of the local symplectic group S ​ p 2 ​ n loc ​ ( 𝔽 2 ) Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2}) on the Lagrangian Grassmannian L ​ G 𝔽 2 ​ ( n , 2 ​ n ) LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n) . We conclude by studying how the former action restricts onto stabilizer groups and stabilizer states, and finally what happens in the case of graph states. Key-words: graph states, stabilizer states, Pauli group, local Clifford group, local symplectic group, Lagrangian Grassmannian, symmetric principal minors.

Introduction

In Quantum Information, stabilizer states are quantum states known in particular for quantum error correcting codes
[
8
]
. In the stabilizer formalism, a stabilizer state is described by a maximal
n
-fold abelian subgroup
\mathcal{S}_{M_{1},\dots,M_{n}}
of the
n
-fold Pauli group
\mathcal{P}_{n}
(see Sec.
2
for definitions) that stabilizes it. Graph states are a special class of quantum stabilizer states elegantly described by a graph
G=(V,E)
that encodes its stablizer group. Graph states have many applications in quantum information processing
[
12
]
: they are in particular useful for Measured Based Quantum Computation (MBQC)
[
12
,
3
,
21
]
, for quantum error correcting codes
[
8
]
and for secret sharing
[
20
,
1
]
.
As a resource for quantum information, it is interesting to propose classification of graph states. A natural framework to classify quantum states is to consider the group of local unitary operations LU. However for stabilizer states (and graph states), one usually restricts to considering the group of local unitaries within the Clifford group
[
26
,
27
]
. We will denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\text{loc}}_{n}\subset\text{LU}
the group of local Clifford acting on
n
qubit states. Under the action of
\mathcal{C}^{\text{loc}}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, graph states have been classified up to
n=12
qubits
[
12
,
5
,
4
]
.

The variety
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
of principal minors for
n\times n
symmetric matrices over a field
\mathbb{K}

[
23
]
is an algebraic variety of
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{K}^{2^{n}})
introduced by Holtz and Sturmfels
[
13
]
in order to study relations among principal minors of symmetric matrices. The existence problem of a matrix statisfying predefinite conditions on its principal minors has many applications to matrix theory, probability, statistical physics and computer vision
[
9
,
15
,
24
]
. The goal of this paper is to show another potential application of the study of this variety over the two-elements field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
by establishing a bijection between classes of graph states and orbits of the variety
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
. More precisely, the main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

The Lagrangian mapping induces a bijection between the

\left(\mathcal{C}^{\text{loc}}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\right)
-orbits of maximal abelian subgroups of
\mathcal{P}_{n}

and the
\left(\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\right)
-orbits of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{n}})
. In particular, there is a one to one correspondence between representatives of the graph states classification, up to

\left(\mathcal{C}^{\text{loc}}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\right)
-action, and the representatives of the
\left(\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\right)
-orbits of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
.

Regarding the cardinality of the orbits, the Lagrangian mapping (Sec.
2
) shows that, if
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is a
\left(\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\right)
-orbit of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, then the number of corresponding stabilizer states is
4^{n}|\mathcal{O}_{i}|
.

Maximal (
n
-fold) abelian subgroups of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
correspond to subspaces of maximal dimension in the symplectic polar space
\mathcal{W}(2n-1,2)
of rank
n
and order
2
(see Sec.
2
). The bijection induced by the Lagrangian mapping between subspaces of maximal dimension in
\mathcal{W}(2n-1,2)
and points of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
was already established in
[
14
]
in order to generalize observations made in
[
19
]
regarding the case
n=3
and its connection with the so-called
black-holes/qubits correspondence
. More recently, that same bijection was also considered in
[
28
]
with motivating examples from supergravity theory. It was proven
[
28
]
that, over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is the image of the Spinor variety and thus a
\text{Spin}(2n+1)
-orbit. However, the correspondence of orbits as established in Theorem
1
was not proven in the former papers, neither was the connection with graph states classification.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
we recall the basic definitions regarding the
n
-qubit Pauli group, the symplectic polar space and the Lagrangian mapping. In Sec.
3
and
4
we show how the
\left(\mathcal{C}^{\text{loc}}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\right)
action on the symplectic polar space translates into an action on the variety of principal minors, proving the first part of Theorem
1
. In Sec.
5
and
6
we recall the definitions and basic properties of stabilizer states and graph states, and we complete the proof of our Theorem. Finally Sec.
7
is dedicated to applications of our correspondence.

2.1
The Pauli group
\mathcal{P}_{n}

The group of the elementary

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\langle iX,iZ,iY\rangle\subset\U(2,\mathbb{C})

X=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\ \ ,\ \ Z=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\

We notice that

XZ=-ZX=-iY\ \ ,\ \ XY=-YX=iZ\ \ ,\ \ ZY=-YZ=-iX\ .

Moreover,

V_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{1}/Z(\mathcal{P}_{1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

Definition

From the commutation rules above it is clear that

\mathcal{P}_{n}=\big\{\pm Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}},\pm iZ^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}}\ \big|\ \mu_{i},\nu_{i}\in\{0,1\}\big\}\ .

In particular, we can exhibit the following generators

\mathcal{P}_{n}=\left\langle I\otimes\ldots\otimes\underbrace{Z}_{l-th}\otimes\ldots\otimes I,\ I\otimes\ldots\otimes\underbrace{X}_{s-th}\otimes\ldots\otimes I\ \bigg|\ 1\leq l,s\leq n\right\rangle\ .

(1)

Notice that
\#\mathcal{P}_{n}=4\cdot 4^{n}
and its center is
Z(\mathcal{P}_{n})=\{\pm I^{\otimes n},\pm iI^{\otimes n}\}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}

Remark 2.1

The quotient

(2)

Clearly, this correspondence is not unique, but it depends on the coordinates we choose in

2.2
The symplectic polar space
\mathcal{W}(2n-1,2)
and
\mathcal{I}^{n}

By Remark
2.1
we know that the Pauli group quotient
V_{n}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
. Let us denote the binary projective space
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n})

\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}
and let us fix the coordinates (

\begin{matrix}&\mathcal{P}_{n}&\dashrightarrow&\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}&\\
M=&\alpha Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}}&\mapsto&[\mu_{1}:\ldots:\mu_{n}:\nu_{1}:\ldots:\nu_{n}]&=P_{M}\end{matrix}

(6)

where the arrow is dashed since the above map is actually not defined in

Up to the coefficients
\alpha

\mathcal{P}_{n}
by projective points as a set, but it losts the commutation information we have in
\mathcal{P}_{n}

Let
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and let
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}
be the corresponding points. By a simple count it comes out that

M_{1}M_{2}=M_{2}M_{1}\iff\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mu_{i}^{(1)}\nu_{i}^{(2)}-\mu_{i}^{(2)}\nu_{i}^{(1)}\big)=0\ .

Consider the symplectic bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{J}
on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
(hence on
\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}
) where

J=\begin{bmatrix}&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&\end{bmatrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{F}_{2}}}{{=}}\begin{bmatrix}&I_{n}\\
I_{n}&\end{bmatrix}\ .

Since
\langle P_{1},P_{2}\rangle_{J}=\!^{t}P_{1}JP_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mu_{i}^{(1)}\nu_{i}^{(2)}-\mu_{i}^{(2)}\nu_{i}^{(1)}\big)
, the previous relation is equivalent to

M_{1}M_{2}=M_{2}M_{1}\iff\langle P_{1},P_{2}\rangle_{J}=0

that is
commuting Pauli group elements correspond to isotropic points with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{J}
.

Definition
.

The
symplectic polar space
of rank
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
(with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{J}
) is the set of (fully) isotropic subspaces of
\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}

\mathcal{W}(2n-1,2)=\big\{W\subset\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}\ \big|\ \forall P,Q\in W,\ \langle P,Q\rangle_{J}=0\big\}\ .

Let
M\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and let
P\in\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}
be its associated point: then
P
defines the hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}

H_{P}=\big\{Q\in\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}\ \big|\ \langle P,Q\rangle_{J}=0\big\}\ .

Clearly, this hyperplane is not fully isotropic. However, we can extend this construction to any set of Pauli group elements with the only condition that they mutually commute:

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{P}_{n}&\dashrightarrow&\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vee}}{{\rightarrow}}&\big(\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}\big)^{\vee}\\
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}&\mapsto&P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}&\mapsto&H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}}=\big\{Q\in\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}\ \big|\ \langle P_{i},Q\rangle_{J}=0,\ \forall i=1:k\big\}\end{matrix}

The condition for the
M_{i}
’s to be mutually commuting implies that the
P_{i}
’s are two-by-two isotropic, but the subspace
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}}
is not fully isotropic in general.

We know that, for any
P\in\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}
,

is a hyperplane, hence it has (projective) dimension
2n-2
. Generally, given
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}
two-by-two isotropic, the subspace
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}}
does not have dimension
2n-1-k
, but it holds so if we start from
k

M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
with the additional condition to be
independent

M_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot M_{k}^{c_{k}}=I^{\otimes n}\iff\forall i=1:k,\ c_{i}=0\ .

Let

be

(i)

(ii)

In this section we extend the previous group actions to the semidirect product with the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
in order to prove the first part of Theorem

4.1
The actions
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\curvearrowright\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\curvearrowright\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}

By definition of the
n
-fold Pauli group

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}\ |\ A_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\right\}

(30)

\displaystyle=\big\{\pm Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}},\pm iZ^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}}\ \big|\ \mu_{i},\nu_{i}\in\{0,1\}\big\}\ ,

(31)

there is a natural action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
permuting the tensor entries:

\forall\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n},\ \ \ \sigma\cdot(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})=A_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma(n)}\ .

Each permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
induces a transformation
\tilde{\sigma}\in U(2^{n},\mathbb{C})
permuting the basis vectors, so that for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and for any
A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
one gets

\sigma\cdot(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})=A_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma(n)}=\tilde{\sigma}(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})\tilde{\sigma}^{\dagger}\ .

(32)

Notice that
\tilde{\sigma}^{\dagger}=\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}
. In particular, the above action preserves
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, thus for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
it holds
\tilde{\sigma}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}=N_{U(2^{n},\mathbb{C})}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
: it follows that there is an injective homomorphism

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}&\hookrightarrow&\mathcal{C}_{n}&\subset U(2^{n},\mathbb{C})\\
\sigma&\mapsto&\tilde{\sigma}\end{matrix}

which allows to identify
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as a subgroup of the Clifford group
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. Moreover, the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
naturally acts on the local Clifford group
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
by conjugacy

\begin{matrix}\phi:&\mathfrak{S}_{n}&\longrightarrow&\Aut\left(\mathcal{C}_{n}^{loc}\right)\\
&\sigma&\mapsto&\big(\ \phi_{\sigma}:U\mapsto\ ^{\sigma}\!U=\tilde{\sigma}U\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}\ \big)\end{matrix}\ ,

(33)

where
{}^{\sigma}\!U=\tilde{\sigma}U\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}=U_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{\sigma(n)}
for any
U=U_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The action (
33
) is actually well defined. Indeed, given
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
U=U_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
and
A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, we have

{}^{\sigma}\!U(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})^{\sigma}\!U^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\ =(\tilde{\sigma}U\tilde{\sigma}^{-1})(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})(\tilde{\sigma}U\tilde{\sigma}^{-1})^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\ =(\tilde{\sigma}U\tilde{\sigma}^{-1})(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})(\tilde{\sigma}U^{\dagger}\tilde{\sigma}^{-1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{S_n acting on P_n}}}{{=}}\tilde{\sigma}U\left(A_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}\right)U^{\dagger}\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}

\displaystyle\ =\tilde{\sigma}\left(U_{1}A_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}U_{1}^{\dagger}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}A_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}U_{n}^{\dagger}\right)\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{S_n acting on C_n^loc}}}{{=}}U_{\sigma(1)}A_{1}U_{\sigma(1)}^{\dagger}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{\sigma(n)}A_{n}U_{\sigma(n)}^{\dagger}\ \in\mathcal{P}_{n}\ .

It follows that the subgroups
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(the second up to isomorphism) generate a subgroup in
\mathcal{C}_{n}
which is isomorphic to the semidirect product
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\ ,\ \mathfrak{S}_{n}\right\rangle&\subset\mathcal{C}_{n}\\
(U,\sigma)&\mapsto&U\tilde{\sigma}\end{matrix}\ ,

acting on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
as follows

\begin{matrix}\big(\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\big)\times\mathcal{P}_{n}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{P}_{n}\\
\big((U,\sigma),A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}\big)&\mapsto&U\cdot\left(\sigma\cdot(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})\right)\end{matrix}

(34)

where, if
U=U_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}
,

\displaystyle U\cdot\left(\sigma\cdot(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=U\left(A_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma(n)}\right)U^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=U_{1}A_{\sigma(1)}U_{1}^{\dagger}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}A_{\sigma(n)}U_{n}^{\dagger}\ .

Remark 4.2
.

We recall that the operation in the semidirect product is

(U,\sigma)\cdot_{\phi}(U^{\prime},\tau)=\big(U\cdot\phi_{\sigma}(U^{\prime}),\sigma\cdot\tau\big)

(35)

and the following commutation rule holds

\big(I,\sigma\big)\cdot_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}_{\sigma}(U),id)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\ (U,\sigma)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\ (U,id)\cdot_{\phi}(I,\sigma)\ .

Since
\phi_{\sigma}^{-1}=\phi_{\sigma^{-1}}
, by equality
(a)
it follows (in agreement with (
34
))

\displaystyle(U,\sigma)\cdot(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\big(I,\sigma\big)\cdot_{\phi}(\phi^{-1}_{\sigma}(U),id)\bigg)\cdot(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})

\displaystyle=(I,\sigma)\cdot\bigg((^{\sigma^{-1}}U)(A_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n})(^{\sigma^{-1}}U)^{\dagger}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(I,\sigma)\cdot\bigg(U_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}A_{1}U_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}^{\dagger}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}A_{n}U_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}^{\dagger}\bigg)

\displaystyle=U_{1}A_{\sigma(1)}U_{1}^{\dagger}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}A_{\sigma(n)}U_{n}^{\dagger}\ .

Since the elements in
\mathcal{P}_{n}
are of the form
\alpha Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}}
for
\alpha\in\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
, the action (
32
) of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
can be equivalently described by

\sigma\cdot(\alpha Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}})=\alpha Z^{\mu_{\sigma(1)}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{\sigma(n)}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(n)}}\ .

(36)

Finally, from (
21
) we know that the action of a given
U=U_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
on
Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}}
corresponds to an action of a certain
\left({\tiny\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{bmatrix}},\ldots,{\tiny\begin{bmatrix}a_{n}&b_{n}\\
c_{n}&d_{n}\end{bmatrix}}\right)\in\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}
, where each
{\tiny\begin{bmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{bmatrix}}
depends on
U_{i}
and it acts on the coefficients
\mu_{i},\nu_{i}
’s. In particular, in the same notation, we have

\displaystyle U(Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}})U^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=U_{1}Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}U_{1}^{\dagger}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}}U_{n}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=Z^{a_{1}\mu_{1}+b_{1}\nu_{1}}X^{c_{1}\mu_{1}+d_{1}\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{a_{n}\mu_{n}+b_{n}\nu_{n}}X^{c_{n}\mu_{n}+d_{n}\nu_{n}}

and the action (
34
) of the semidirect product
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
can be rephrased as

(U,\sigma)\cdot(Z^{\mu_{1}}X^{\nu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}}X^{\nu_{n}})\ =\ U(Z^{\mu_{\sigma(1)}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{\sigma(n)}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(n)}})U^{\dagger}=

(37)

Z^{a_{1}\mu_{\sigma(1)}+b_{1}\nu_{\sigma(1)}}X^{c_{1}\mu_{\sigma(1)}+d_{1}\nu_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{a_{n}\mu_{\sigma(n)}+b_{n}\nu_{\sigma(n)}}X^{c_{n}\mu_{\sigma(n)}+d_{n}\nu_{\sigma(n)}}\ .

The above formula shows that the action of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
induces an action on the vectors
(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n},\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
: more precisely, the action (
37

Remark 4.3

Remark 4.4

\mathcal{P}_{n}
)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{P}_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{P}_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{P}_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ {\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{4.07pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\textstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.12001pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.80057pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.33333pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.80057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ .

4.2
The actions
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\curvearrowright\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
and
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\curvearrowright\mathcal{I}^{n}

The next step is to extend the orbit correspondence (
43
) to an orbit correspondence between the set of
n
-fold maximal abelian subgroups in
\mathcal{P}_{n}

\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})=\left\{\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle<\mathcal{P}_{n}\ |\ M_{i}^{\prime}s\ \text{independent \& mutually commuting}\right\}

and the set of maximal fully isotropic subspaces in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
(with respect to the symplectic form
J
)

\mathcal{I}^{n}=\left\{W\subset\mathbb{P}_{2}^{2n-1}\ |\ \langle P,Q\rangle_{J}=0\ \forall P,Q\in W,\ \dim_{\mathbb{P}}=n-1\right\}\ ,

which are in bijection via (
7
). From diagram (
23
) we already have the correspondence between the orbits

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}}$}}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ {\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\textstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})}$}}{\raisebox{2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.80057pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})}$}}{\raisebox{1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.80057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})}$}}}\ .

Action on
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
.

Given
M_{i}=A_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we denote

{}^{\sigma}\!M_{i}\ =\ \sigma\cdot(A_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}^{(i)})\ =\ A_{\sigma(1)}^{(i)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma(n)}^{(i)}\ .

Let
S_{M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}}=\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
be a maximal abelian subgroup of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
with

M_{i}=\alpha_{i}Z^{\mu_{1}^{(i)}}X^{\nu_{1}^{(i)}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}^{(i)}}X^{\nu_{n}^{(i)}}\ \ \ ,\ \alpha_{i}\in\{\pm 1,\pm i\}\ .

Then, for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the observables
{}^{\sigma}M_{i}
’s are such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\mu_{\sigma(j)}^{(h)}\nu_{\sigma(j)}^{(k)}-\mu_{\sigma(j)}^{(k)}\nu_{\sigma(j)}^{(h)}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l=\sigma^{-1}(j)}}{{=}}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\big(\mu_{l}^{(h)}\nu_{l}^{(k)}-\mu_{l}^{(k)}\nu_{l}^{(h)}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\clubsuit)}}{{=}}0

and

\displaystyle(^{\sigma}M_{1})^{c_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot(^{\sigma}M_{n})^{c_{n}}

\displaystyle=(A_{\sigma(1)}^{(1)})^{c_{1}}\cdots(A_{\sigma(1)}^{(n)})^{c_{n}}\otimes\ldots\otimes(A_{\sigma(n)}^{(1)})^{c_{1}}\cdots(A_{\sigma(n)}^{(n)})^{c_{n}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\spadesuit)}}{{=}}I_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes I_{2}=I^{\otimes n}\ ,

where the equalities
(\clubsuit)
and
(\spadesuit)
respectively follow from the commutation and the independence of the
M_{i}
’s. It follows that
{}^{\sigma}\!M_{1},\ldots,^{\sigma}\!M_{n}
are independent and mutually commuting too: thus we get the following well-defined action

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})\\
\big(\sigma\ ,\ S_{M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}}\big)&\mapsto&S_{{}^{\sigma}\!M_{1},\ldots,^{\sigma}\!M_{n}}\end{matrix}\ .

Actually, since the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on the generators of a maximal abelian subgroup, the above action coincides with the one permuting
both
the entries of any generators
and
the generators among them, that is

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})\\
\big(\sigma\ ,\ \langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\big)&\mapsto&\langle^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(n)}\rangle\end{matrix}

(44)

where

{}^{\sigma}M_{\sigma(i)}=Z^{\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}}\ .

We conclude that the action of the group
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
extends to the action

\begin{matrix}\bigg(\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\bigg)\times\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})\\
\left((U,\sigma)\ ,\ \langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\right)&\mapsto&\langle\ U(^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(1)})U^{\dagger},\ldots,U(^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(n)})U^{\dagger}\ \rangle\end{matrix}

(45)

where, for
U=U_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
and
M_{i}=Z^{\mu_{1}^{(i)}}X^{\nu_{1}^{(i)}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\mu_{n}^{(i)}}X^{\nu_{n}^{(i)}}
,

U(^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(i)})U^{\dagger}=U_{1}Z^{\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))}}U_{1}^{\dagger}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}Z^{\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}}X^{\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}}U_{n}^{\dagger}\ .

Action on
\mathcal{I}^{n}
.

From the correspondence (
7
) we know that any maximal abelian subgroup
\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}(P_{n})
corresponds to the maximal isotropic subspace
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}}\in\mathcal{I}^{n}
, where
P_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
is defined by the exponents in
M_{i}
. Moreover, by Proposition
2.2
and by the linear independence of the
M_{i}
’s, it holds
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}}^{\vee}=H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}}
, thus
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}}=\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
.

By putting together the actions (
41
) and (
45
), one gets the action

\begin{matrix}\bigg(\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\bigg)\times\mathcal{I}^{n}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{I}^{n}\\
\left((\tilde{U},\sigma)\ ,\ H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}}\right)&\mapsto&H_{\tilde{U}S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\tilde{U}S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(n)}}\end{matrix}\

(46)

where, for
P_{i}=(\mu_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,\mu_{n}^{(i)},\nu_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,\nu_{n}^{(i)})
,

\tilde{U}S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(i)}\ =\ \tilde{U}S_{\sigma}\left(\mu_{1}^{(\sigma(i))},\ldots,\mu_{n}^{(\sigma(i))},\nu_{1}^{(\sigma(i))},\ldots,\nu_{n}^{(\sigma(i))}\right)=

(47)

=\ \tilde{U}\left(\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))},\ldots,\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))},\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))},\ldots,\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}\right)\ =

\big(a_{1}\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))}+b_{1}\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))},\ \ldots,\ a_{n}\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}+b_{n}\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))},\ c_{1}\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))}+d_{1}\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(i))},\ \ldots,\ c_{n}\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}+d_{n}\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(i))}\big)\ .

Notice that each point
\tilde{U}S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
corresponds to the observable
U(^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(i)})U^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
. We conclude that the correspondence (
43
) extends to a correspondence between the orbits

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ {\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\textstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.80057pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{I}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.80057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ .

(48)

4.3
The actions of
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)
and
\mathcal{Z}_{n}

We are close to prove the correspondence between orbits in
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and orbits in the variety of binary symmetric principal minors
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
with respect to
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
: it only remains to translate the action (
46
) into an action on the Lagrangian Grassmannian
\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)
, and later on
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
via the diagram (
15
).

Action on
\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)
.

By definition,
\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)=\Pl(\mathcal{I}^{n})\subset\mathbb{P}\big(\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}\big)
, where
\Pl
is the Plücker embedding: in particular, a maximal fully isotropic subspace
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}}=\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\in\mathcal{I}^{n}
corresponds to the point
[P_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge P_{n}]\in\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)
. Moreover, one can write the point
[P_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge P_{n}]
in coordinates in
\mathbb{P}_{2}^{\binom{2n}{n}-1}
: given
N=\big[P_{1}|\ldots|P_{n}\big]
the
2n\times n
matrix representing the subspace
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}}
, the coordinates of
[P_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge P_{n}]
are given by the maximal minors (i.e. of size
n\times n
) of
N
, that is

\begin{matrix}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}\right)&\longleftrightarrow&\mathbb{P}_{2}^{\binom{2n}{n}-1}\\
[P_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge P_{n}]&\longleftrightarrow&\left[N_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}:N_{\{1,\ldots,n-1,n+1\}}:\ldots:N_{\{n+1,\ldots,2n\}}\right]\end{matrix}

where
N_{I}
is the minor of
N
given by the
I
-indexed rows and all the
n
columns.

It is straightforward that the action (
46
) of
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathcal{I}^{n}
is equivalent to the following action on the Lagrangian Grassmannian (which extends the action (
27
)):

\begin{matrix}\bigg(\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\bigg)\times\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)&\longrightarrow&\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)\\
\left((\tilde{U},\sigma)\ ,\ [P_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge P_{n}]\right)&\mapsto&\left[\tilde{U}S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\ldots\wedge\tilde{U}S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(n)}\right]\end{matrix}

(49)

where
\tilde{U}S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(i)}
are as in (
47
).

Remark 4.5
.

From Remark
3.7
we know that the action of
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
does not preserve the open subsets
LU_{I}
defined in (
13
), hence the above action does not preserve them either.

We can translate the action (
49
) on the Lagrangian Grassmannian into an action on the set of full-rank
2n\times n
matrices. By (
25
) we already know that
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
acts on the full-rank
2n\times n
matrices by left-multiplication, that is a transformation
\tilde{U}={\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}}\in\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
maps a certain full-rank
2n\times n
matrix
N={\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}}
into the full-rank
2n\times n
matrix

\tilde{U}\cdot N\ =\ \begin{bmatrix}AF+BG\\
CF+DG\end{bmatrix}\ .

(50)

By substituting the identity matrix
\tilde{U}=I
in (
47
) we deduce that a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on a full-rank
2n\times n
matrix
{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}}=[P_{1}|\ldots|P_{n}]
as

\sigma\cdot\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}=\left[S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(1)}|\ldots|S_{\sigma}P_{\sigma(n)}\right]={\tiny\begin{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(1))}&\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(2))}&\cdots&\mu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(n))}\\
\mu_{\sigma(2)}^{(\sigma(1))}&\mu_{\sigma(2)}^{(\sigma(2))}&\cdots&\mu_{\sigma(2)}^{(\sigma(n))}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(1))}&\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(2))}&\cdots&\mu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(n))}\end{matrix}\\
---------------\\
\begin{matrix}\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(1))}&\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(2))}&\cdots&\nu_{\sigma(1)}^{(\sigma(n))}\\
\nu_{\sigma(2)}^{(\sigma(1))}&\nu_{\sigma(2)}^{(\sigma(2))}&\cdots&\nu_{\sigma(2)}^{(\sigma(n))}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(1))}&\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(2))}&\cdots&\nu_{\sigma(n)}^{(\sigma(n))}\end{matrix}\end{bmatrix}}=\begin{bmatrix}{}^{\sigma}\!F\\
{}^{\sigma}\!G\end{bmatrix}

(51)

where the
n\times n
matrix
{}^{\sigma}\!F
(resp.
{}^{\sigma}\!G
) is obtained by the
n\times n
matrix
F
(resp.
G
) by permuting both columns and rows by
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
: more precisely, if
S_{\sigma}={\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}A_{\sigma}\\
&A_{\sigma}\end{bmatrix}}
where
A_{\sigma}
is the
n\times n
permutation matrix defined by
\sigma
, then the action of
\sigma
onto
{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}}
corresponds to the conjugacy action by
A_{\sigma}
onto
F
and
G
separately, that is

\sigma\cdot\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{}^{\sigma}\!F\\
{}^{\sigma}\!G\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{\sigma}FA_{\sigma}^{-1}\\
A_{\sigma}GA_{\sigma}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\ .

Remark 4.6
.

By (
51
) the above action preserves the full-rankness. Moreover, from the matrix setting we deduce that, analogously to Remark (
3.7
), the action (
49
) of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)
does not preserve the open subsets
LU_{I}
either. For instance, denote by
E_{ij}
the
n\times n
matrix having
1
in the entry
(i,j)
and zero everywhere else: then
{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}E_{ii}+E_{jj}\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}}

lies
in the open subset
LU_{\{i,j,n+1,\ldots,2n\}\setminus\{n+i,n+j\}}
but the permutation
\sigma=(ik)(j\ell)
maps it into the matrix
{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}E_{kk}+E_{\ell\ell}\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}}
which does not lie in
LU_{\{i,j,n+1,\ldots,2n\}\setminus\{n+i,n+j\}}
. However, the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
preserves the open subset
LU_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
: indeed,
\sigma\cdot{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}\\
S\end{bmatrix}}={\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}{}^{\sigma}\!I_{n}\\
{}^{\sigma}\!S\end{bmatrix}}={\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}\\
{}^{\sigma}\!S\end{bmatrix}}
.

By putting together (
50
) and (
51
) we conclude that the action (
49
) of
\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)
is equivalent to the restriction onto full-rank matrices of the action

\begin{matrix}\bigg(\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\bigg)\times\Mat_{2n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})&\longrightarrow&\Mat_{2n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\\
\left((\tilde{U},\sigma)\ ,\ {\footnotesize\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}}\right)&\mapsto&{\footnotesize\begin{bmatrix}A(^{\sigma}\!F)+B(^{\sigma}\!G)\\
C(^{\sigma}\!F)+D(^{\sigma}\!G)\end{bmatrix}}\end{matrix}

(52)

where
\tilde{U}={\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}}
.

\big(|i_{1}\ldots i_{n}\rangle=v_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{i_{n}}\ |\ i_{k}\in\{0,1\}\big)

5
Stabilizer states and their orbits under
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}

In this section we focus on a subset of the set
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
of the maximal (
n
-fold) abelian subgroups of the Pauli group
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, and show that the action (
45
) restricts to an action on this subset.

Definition
.

A subgroup
S<\mathcal{P}_{n}
is called
stabilizer group
if it is abelian and
-I^{\otimes n}\notin S
. In particular,
S\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
is a
maximal stabilizer group
if it does not contain
-I^{\otimes n}
. We denote the set of maximal stabilizer state groups in
\mathcal{P}_{n}
by
\mathscr{S}^{+}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
, or simply
\mathscr{S}^{+}_{n}
.

Let
S=\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
: since the
M_{i}
’s are mutually commuting, they admit (at least) one common eigenvector
|\phi\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
. Moreover, recall that for any
M\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
it holds
M^{2}=\pm I^{\otimes n}
: in particular, for any
M\in S
one gets
M^{2}=I^{\otimes n}
since
-I^{\otimes n}\notin S
and
M^{2}\in S
. It follows that elements in
S
can only have eigenvalues
+1
or
-1
.

For any abelian subgroup
S<\mathcal{P}_{n}
not containing
-I^{\otimes n}
the set of the common eigenvectors with eigenvalue
+1
of
S
forms a subspace
V_{S}
, called
stabilized subspace
or
stabilizer code
of
S
, and its dimension is
\dim V_{S}=2^{n}/|S|

[
6
, Sec. III.B, III.C]
: in particular, if
S\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
,
V_{S}
is one-dimensional.

Definition
.

A (
n
-qubit)
stabilizer state
is the (unique up to phase) common eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
of a maximal stabilizer state
S\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
. We denote by
\Phi_{1}^{n}
the set of
n
-qubit stabilizer states.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between
\Phi_{1}^{n}
and
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
: we denote by
S_{|\phi\rangle}
the maximal stabilizer group associated to the stabilizer state
|\phi\rangle
. Let us study how
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
and
\Phi_{1}^{n}
.

First of all, the action (
16
) of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
preserves
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
: indeed, given
U\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
and
S_{|\phi\rangle}=\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
, it holds

UM_{i}U^{\dagger}(U|\phi\rangle)=UM_{i}|\phi\rangle=U|\phi\rangle\ \ \ ,\forall i=1:n

that is
U|\phi\rangle
is common eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
of
S_{UM_{1}U^{\dagger},\ldots,UM_{n}U^{\dagger}}
.

In particular, the action of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
on
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
is given by

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\ \times\ \mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\\
(U\ ,\ S_{|\phi\rangle})&\mapsto&S_{U|\phi\rangle}\end{matrix}

(57)

and it is equivalent to the action of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
on
\Phi_{1}^{n}
given by

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\ \times\ \Phi_{1}^{n}&\longrightarrow&\Phi_{1}^{n}\\
(U\ ,\ |\phi\rangle)&\mapsto&U|\phi\rangle\end{matrix}

(58)

where, if
U=U_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}
and
|\phi\rangle=\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle
(with
|\phi_{j}^{(k)}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
),

U|\phi\rangle=\sum_{j}U_{1}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle\ .

On the other hand, also the action (
44
) of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
preserves
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
: indeed, given
S=\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
(which by definition does not contain
-I^{\otimes n}
), if
{}^{\sigma}\!S=\langle^{\sigma}M_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(n)}\rangle
contained
-I^{\otimes n}
, then
\sigma^{-1}\cdot(-I^{\otimes n})=-I^{\otimes n}
would be in
{}^{\sigma^{-1}}(^{\sigma}\!S)=S
which is a contradiction. Thus we get the induced action

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ \times\ \mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\\
\big(\sigma\ ,\ S_{|\phi\rangle}\big)&\mapsto&{}^{\sigma}\!(S_{|\phi\rangle})\end{matrix}\ .

Actually, we can exhibit the stabilizer state corresponding to
{}^{\sigma}\!(S_{|\phi\rangle})
: given
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
|\phi\rangle=\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle\in\Phi_{1}^{n}
, our candidate is

^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle=\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(1))}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(n))}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}

(59)

that is the element obtained by permuting via
\sigma
the tensor entries of
|\phi\rangle
. Notice that this is precisely the one we should expect: indeed,
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on a maximal stabilizer group by permuting both its generators and their tensor entries, but the eigenvector is invariant under reordering of the generators, so the only non-trivial action is the one permuting the tensor entries.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any stabilizer state
|\phi\rangle\in\Phi_{1}^{n}
and any permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the tensor element
{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle
in (
59
) is also a stabilizer state, i.e.
{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle\in\Phi_{1}^{n}
. In particular,

{}^{\sigma}\!(S_{|\phi\rangle})=S_{{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle}\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\ .

Proof.

Let
|\phi\rangle=\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle\in\Phi_{1}^{n}
be a stabilizer state and
S_{|\phi\rangle}=\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
the corresponding maximal stabilizer group. If
M_{i}=A_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}^{(n)}
for any
i=1:n
, then by definition

\displaystyle\sum_{j}A_{1}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle

\displaystyle=(A_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}^{(i)})|\phi\rangle=M_{i}|\phi\rangle=|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle

for any
i=1:n
. By applying
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
we get for any
i=1:n

\displaystyle\sum_{j}A_{\sigma(1)}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(1))}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma(n)}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(n))}\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\clubsuit)}}{{=}}\sum_{j}\sigma\cdot\left(A_{1}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma\cdot\left(\sum_{j}A_{1}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{n}^{(i)}|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma\cdot\left(\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(1))}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(n))}\rangle\ ,

where
(\clubsuit)
follows by definition of the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
. By (
59
) the above chain of equalities gives

{}^{\sigma}\!M_{i}(^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle)=\left(A_{\sigma(1)}^{(i)})\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma(n)}^{(i)}\right)(^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle)\ =\ ^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle\ ,

that is
{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle
is eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
for
{}^{\sigma}\!M_{i}=A_{\sigma(1)}^{(i)}\otimes\ldots\otimes A_{\sigma(n)}^{(i)}
. Since this holds for any
i=1:n
, then
{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle
is a common eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
of
\langle^{\sigma}\!M_{1},\ldots,^{\sigma}\!M_{n}\rangle=\langle^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(n)}\rangle=\ ^{\sigma}\!(S_{|\phi\rangle})
. But
{}^{\sigma}\!(S_{|\phi\rangle})
is a maximal stabilizer group, thus
{}^{\sigma}|\phi\rangle
actually is its
unique
eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
, that is
{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle\in\Phi_{1}^{n}
and
{}^{\sigma}\!(S_{|\phi\rangle})=S_{{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle}
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

The action (
44
) of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
preserves
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
and induces the action

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ \times\ \mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\\
\big(\sigma\ ,\ S_{|\phi\rangle}\big)&\mapsto&{}^{\sigma}\!(S_{|\phi\rangle})=S_{{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle}\end{matrix}\ .

(60)

Moreover, the above action corresponds to an action on the stabilizer states

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ \times\ \Phi_{1}^{n}&\longrightarrow&\Phi_{1}^{n}\\
\big(\sigma\ ,\ |\phi\rangle\big)&\mapsto&{}^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle\end{matrix}\ .

(61)

By putting together the previous actions we get that the action (
45
) of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
restricts to the action

\begin{matrix}\big(\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\big)\ \times\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\\
\big((U,\sigma)\ ,\ S_{|\phi\rangle}\big)&\mapsto&S_{U(^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle)}=U(^{\sigma}\!S_{|\phi\rangle})U^{\dagger}\end{matrix}

(62)

In particular, this action corresponds to the action on the stabilizer states

\begin{matrix}\big(\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\big)\times\ \Phi_{1}^{n}&\longrightarrow&\Phi_{1}^{n}\\
\big((U,\sigma)\ ,\ |\phi\rangle\big)&\mapsto&U(^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle)\end{matrix}

(63)

where, if
U=U_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}
and
|\phi\rangle=\sum_{j}|\phi_{j}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes|\phi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle
, then

U(^{\sigma}\!|\phi\rangle)=\sum_{j}U_{1}|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(1))}\rangle\otimes\ldots\otimes U_{n}|\phi_{j}^{(\sigma(n))}\rangle\ .

Conclusion.

This section proves an intermediate step to the second statement of Theorem
1
. From Section 4 we have the one-to-one correspondence (
56
) between the orbits of maximal abelian subgroups under
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and the orbits in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
under
\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. But the Lagrangian mapping (Sec.
2
) associates maximal fully isotropic subspaces in
\mathcal{I}^{n}
to maximal abelian subgroups in
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})

up to phasis
(since we work into the quotient
V_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n}/Z(\mathcal{P}_{n})
): this means that the Lagrangian mapping does not distinguish between maximal abelian subgroups containing
-I^{\otimes n}
and maximal stabilizer states. It follows that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the orbits of stabilizer states under
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and the orbits in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
under
\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
:

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ {\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.23112pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.98112pt}{$\textstyle{\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.17558pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.3889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ {\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.23112pt}{$\displaystyle{\SL(2,\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ .

6
Graph states and their orbits under
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}

In this section we investigate how the action (
63
) of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\Phi_{1}^{n}
behaves with respect to a privileged subset of stabilizer states, the so-called
graph states
.

Remark 6.1
.

In
[
26
]
it is pointed out that the action of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
does not preserve this subset, hence the action (
63
) does not either: in particular, one cannot talk about
orbits of graph states under
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. However, we will improperly refer to “orbit” of a graph state as to the set of all graph states belonging to the same orbit in
\Phi_{1}^{n}
: in order to get an actual action on the graph states, one should consider
only
certain local Clifford operations corresponding to graph transformations (cf.
[
26
, Sec. IV, Definition 1]
).

Consider a (non-oriented) graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
defined by a finite set of vertices
V=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and a set of edges
E\subset V\times V
. We can associate to
\Gamma
a unique symmetric matrix
\theta\in\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})
such that

\theta_{ij}=1\iff(i,j)\in E

(the matrix is indeed symmetric since the graph is not oriented). The graph
\Gamma
(or equivalently its matrix
\theta
) defines
n
elements in the Pauli group
\mathcal{P}_{n}

\begin{matrix}M_{1}=&Z^{\theta_{11}}X&\otimes&Z^{\theta_{12}}&\otimes&\ldots&\otimes&Z^{\theta_{1n}}\\
\vdots\\
M_{n}=&Z^{\theta_{1n}}&\otimes&\ldots&\otimes&Z^{\theta_{n-1,n}}&\otimes&Z^{\theta_{nn}}X\end{matrix}\ ,

or equivalently, in a more compact notation,

M_{i}=Z^{\theta_{i1}}X^{\delta_{1i}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\theta_{in}}X^{\delta_{ni}}\ \ \ ,\ \forall i=1:n\ ,

(65)

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol: these elements are independent and mutually commuting, thus they generate a maximal abelian subgroup which we denote by
S_{\Gamma}=S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
. Moreover, the maximal fully isotropic subspace
H_{\theta}\in\mathcal{I}^{n}
corresponding to
S_{\theta}
is described (in the coordinates (
3
)) by the
2n\times n
matrix
{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}\theta\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}}
(it is already in a Plücker form) and belongs to the chart
LU_{\{n+1,\ldots,2n\}}\subset\LG_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(n,2n)
.

Remark 6.2
.

The association
\Gamma\mapsto S_{\theta}
is not unique: indeed, instead of defining the elements
M_{i}
’s in (
65
), one could choose to associate to
\Gamma
the elements

M_{i}^{\prime}=Z^{\delta_{1i}}X^{\theta_{i1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\delta_{ni}}X^{\theta_{in}}\

defining the subspace
H_{\theta}^{\prime}\in LU_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
described by the matrix
{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}\\
\theta\end{bmatrix}}
. However, this ambiguity does not affect our interests and results since the two different associations
\Gamma\mapsto M_{i}
and
\Gamma\mapsto M_{i}^{\prime}
give maximal abelian subgroups
S_{\theta}
and
S_{\theta}^{\prime}
which are in the same
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
-orbit: indeed,

J{\footnotesize\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}\\
\theta\end{bmatrix}}\ =\ {\footnotesize\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}\\
\theta\end{bmatrix}}\ =\ {\footnotesize\begin{bmatrix}\theta\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}}

and
J\in\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. On one hand, the choice of using the
M_{i}^{\prime}
allows to have an immediate transcription in the variety of symmetric principal minors
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
: via the Lagrangian mapping, the maximal abelian subgroup
S_{\theta}^{\prime}
corresponds to the point
[1:\theta_{ii}:\theta_{[i,j]}:\ldots:\det\theta]\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}
. On the other hand, the choice of using the
M_{i}
is more common in the literature. Thus in this section we are going to work with the association
\Gamma\mapsto S_{\theta}
, but in Section 7 we will use the one
\Gamma\mapsto S_{\theta}^{\prime}
for exhibiting examples.

Now we wonder if the maximal abelian subgroup
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
defined by a graph
\Gamma_{\theta}
(with adjacent matrix
\theta
) is a maximal stabilizer group. In general, the answer is negative unless we add the assumption that the graph is
loopless
(a.k.a
simple
), i.e.
\theta_{ii}=0
for any
i=1:n
. This fact seems to be well known and implicit in the literature but we haven’t be able to find a proof, thus we propose one.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma_{\theta}
be a (non-oriented) graph and let
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
be the associated maximal abelian group
S_{\theta}
via (
65
). Then
S_{\theta}
is a maximal stabilizer group
if and only if
the graph
\Gamma_{\theta}
is loopless:

S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\ \iff\ \theta_{ii}=0\ \ ,\forall i=1:n\ .

Proof.

Recall that
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\ \iff\ \left(\ -I^{\otimes n}\notin S_{\theta}\ \ \wedge\ \ S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P}_{n})\ \right)
.

[\Rightarrow]
Assume that
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
and that, by contradiction, the graph has at least one loop, that is there exists
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\theta_{i_{0}i_{0}}=1
. Then the element
M_{i_{0}}=Z^{\theta_{i_{0}1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes ZX\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\theta_{i_{0}n}}=Z^{\theta_{i_{0}1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes(iY)\otimes\ldots\otimes Z^{\theta_{i_{0}n}}
squares to
M_{i_{0}}^{2}=I\otimes\ldots\otimes(iY)^{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes I=-I^{\otimes n}
, hence
-I^{\otimes n}\in S_{\theta}
, leading to a contradiction.
↝

[\Leftarrow]
Assume
\Gamma_{\theta}
to be loopless. Then for any
i=1:n
it holds
M_{i}^{2}=I^{\otimes n}
, that is
M_{i}
can only have eigenvalues
+1
and
-1
. Since the
M_{i}
’s are mutually commuting, there exists (at least) one common eigenvector, say
|\gamma\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
: then for any
i=1:n
we get
M_{i}|\gamma\rangle=(-1)^{c_{i}}|\gamma\rangle
. In particular, for any
i=1:n
it holds
(-1)^{c_{i}}M_{i}|\gamma\rangle=|\gamma\rangle
, that is
|\gamma\rangle
is common eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
of the
n
elements
(-1)^{c_{i}}M_{i}
’s. But we can always find a local Clifford element
U\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
such that
U(-1)^{c_{i}}M_{i}U^{\dagger}=M_{i}
for all
i=1:n
: if
c_{i_{1}},\ldots,c_{i_{k}}
are the only non-zero exponents, then by (
5
) the local Clifford tranformation
\hat{U}=H_{i_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot H_{i_{k}}
(where
H
is the Hadamard matrix and
H_{j}=I\otimes\ldots\otimes\underbrace{H}_{j-th}\otimes\ldots\otimes I
) is the one doing the work. It follows that
\hat{U}|\gamma\rangle
is common eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
of
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
, hence of
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
.
∎

Since we are interested in the so-called
graph states
(which are stabilizer states) and how
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on them,
from now on we restrict to considering only the maximal abelian subgroups which are defined by loopless graphs
: we underline once again that graphs with loops can only define maximal abelian subgroups which are not stabilizer, thus in these cases one cannot talk about graph states as stabilizer states.

Definition
.

A (
n
-qubit)
graph state

|\Gamma\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
is the stabilizer state (i.e. common eigenvector with eigenvalue
+1
) of a maximal stabilizer group
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
defined by a
n
-vertex (
loopless
) graph
\Gamma_{\theta}
. We denote the subset of
n
-qubit graph states by
\Theta_{n}\subset\Phi_{1}^{n}
.

Moreover, we define a
graph group
to be a maximal stabilizer group defined by a (
loopless
) graph and we denote the set of such subgroups by
\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}\subset\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
.

The one-to-one correspondence
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}\leftrightarrow\Phi_{1}^{n}
restricts to a one-to-one correspondence
\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}\leftrightarrow\Theta_{n}
. Next, we wonder if the actions of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
on
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{+}
preserve
\Theta_{n}
: let us investigate the two actions separately.

Action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

Let
|\Gamma\rangle\in\Theta_{n}
be a graph state defined by a graph
\Gamma
with adjacent matrix
\theta
. The graph group
S_{\theta}=\langle M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}
(as in (
65
)) is described by the
2n\times n
matrix
\begin{bmatrix}\theta\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}
. Given
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, by (
51
) we know that
\sigma\cdot{\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}\theta\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}}={\scriptsize\begin{bmatrix}{}^{\sigma}\!\theta\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}}
which describes the subgroup
\sigma\cdot S_{\theta}=\ ^{\sigma}\!(S_{\theta})=\langle^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\ ^{\sigma}\!M_{\sigma(n)}\rangle
: the matrix
{}^{\sigma}\!\theta
is still symmetric and uniquely defines a graph
{}^{\sigma}\!\Gamma=\Gamma_{{}^{\sigma}\!\theta}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
preserves
\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}
and the action (
60
) restricts to the action

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ \times\ \mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}\\
(\sigma\ ,\ S_{\theta})&\mapsto&{}^{\sigma}\!(S_{\theta})=S_{{}^{\sigma}\!\theta}\end{matrix}\ .

(66)

Remark 6.4
.

The action (
66
) reflects an action on the
n
-graphs described as follows: given a graph
\Gamma
and a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the graph
{}^{\sigma}\!\Gamma
is obtained simply by renaming the vertices from
\{1,\ldots,n\}
to
\{\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(n)\}
, without changing the edges. Roughly speaking, two graphs are in the same
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-orbit if they have the same drawing representation as “dots-edges”.

By correspondence, it follows that the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action (
61
) preserves the subset of graph states
\Theta_{n}
, hence

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ \times\ \Theta_{n}&\longrightarrow&\Theta_{n}\\
(\sigma\ ,\ |\Gamma\rangle)&\mapsto&{}^{\sigma}\!|\Gamma\rangle=|^{\sigma}\!\Gamma\rangle\end{matrix}

(67)

where
|^{\sigma}\Gamma\rangle
is obtained simply by permuting via
\sigma
the tensor entries of
|\Gamma\rangle
as in (
59
).

Action of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
.

As already spoilered in Remark
6.1
, the situation with respect to the action of the local Clifford group is a little more tricky. Given a local Clifford transformation
U\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
and a graph group
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}
, we can consider the corresponding local symplectic transformation
\tilde{U}={\tiny\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}}\in\Sp_{2n}^{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
of type (
18
) and the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}\theta\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}
describing
S_{\theta}
: then the action
U\cdot S_{\theta}
corresponds to the linear transformation

\tilde{U}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\theta\\
I_{n}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A\theta+B\\
C\theta+D\end{bmatrix}

that is, in general,
U\cdot S_{\theta}
is not a graph group (but a maximal stabilizer group of course). This explains Remark
6.1
.

However, the local Clifford operations preserving
\Theta_{n}
are known
[
12
, Proposition 5.3]
to correspond to operations on
n
-graphs, called
local complementations
: for a formal definition and a graphical description we refer to
[
2
, Sec. 1]
,
[
12
]
and
[
26
, Sec. IV, VI]
.

Remark 6.5
.

The local complementations together with Remark
6.4
give a graphical description of the action of (a subgroup of)
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on the graph states.

If we denote by
G_{n}<\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
the subgroup of the local complementations, and by
u
the graph transformation corresponding to
U\in G_{n}
, then one gets the actions

\begin{matrix}G_{n}\ \times\ \mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}\\
(U\ ,\ S_{\theta})&\mapsto&S_{u\cdot\theta}\end{matrix}\ \ \ \ \ ,\ \ \ \ \ \begin{matrix}G_{n}\ \times\ \Theta_{n}&\longrightarrow&\Theta_{n}\\
(U\ ,\ |\Gamma\rangle)&\mapsto&U|\Gamma\rangle\end{matrix}

(68)

which, together with (
66
) and (
67
), give the actions of the semidirect product
G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}

\begin{matrix}\big(G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\big)\ \times\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}\\
\big((U,\sigma)\ ,\ S_{\theta}\big)&\mapsto&S_{u\cdot(^{\sigma}\!\theta)}\end{matrix}\ \ \ \ ,\ \ \ \ \begin{matrix}\big(G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}\big)\times\ \Theta_{n}&\longrightarrow&\Theta_{n}\\
\big((U,\sigma)\ ,\ |\Gamma\big)&\mapsto&U|^{\sigma}\!\Gamma\rangle\end{matrix}\ .

(69)

We have seen that, for any graph group
S_{\theta}\in\mathscr{S}_{\Theta_{n}}
and any
U\in\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
, the subgroup
U\cdot S_{\theta}
is still a stabilizer group, thus
any graph group (resp. graph state) is the representative of a
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
-orbit of maximal stabilizer groups (resp. stabilizer states)
. But we can say more: by
[
26
, Theorem 1]
, each maximal stabilizer group (resp. stabilizer state) is
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
-equivalent to a graph group (resp. graph state), thus
any
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}
-orbit of stabilizer states admits a representative which is a graph states
. This means that studying the orbits of stabilizer states under the action of
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the same as studying the orbits of graph states under the action of
G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
:

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Phi_{1}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{C_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ {\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\Theta_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\Theta_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Theta_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Theta_{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}}$}}}\ .

(70)

Conclusion:

This section achieves the proof of Theorem
1
, proving that the classification (up to
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\text{loc}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
action) of (loopless)

-graph states is in a one-to-one correspondence with the orbits in

under the action of

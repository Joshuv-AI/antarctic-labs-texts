Nonexistence of time-periodic solutions of the Dirac equation in nonextreme Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetime
Yaohua Wang, Xiao Zhang

Abstract
. In non-extreme Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetime, we prove that there is no nontrivial Dirac particle which is L p L^{p} for 0 < p ≤ 4 3 0<p\leq\frac{4}{3} with arbitrary eigenvalue λ \lambda , and for 4 3 < p ≤ 4 3 − 2 ​ q \frac{4}{3}<p\leq\frac{4}{3-2q} , 0 < q < 3 2 0<q<\frac{3}{2} with eigenvalue | λ | > q ​ κ |\lambda|>q\kappa , outside and away from the event horizon. By taking q = 1 2 q=\frac{1}{2} , we show that there is no normalizable massive Dirac particle with mass greater than κ 2 \frac{\kappa}{2} outside and away from the event horizon in non-extreme Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetime, and they must either disappear into the black hole or escape to infinity, and this recovers the same result of Belgiorno and Cacciatori in the case of Q = 0 Q=0 obtained by using spectral methods. Furthermore, we prove that any Dirac particle with eigenvalue | λ | < κ 2 |\lambda|<\frac{\kappa}{2} must be L 2 L^{2} outside and away from the event horizon.

&+i\left[-i\alpha_{1}X_{-}-(i\beta_{1}-\gamma_{1})X_{+}\right]\bar{X}_{+}\\
&-i\left[i\alpha_{1}X_{+}+(i\beta_{1}+\gamma_{1})X_{-}\right]\bar{X}_{-}\\
&-i\left[i\alpha_{1}\bar{X}_{-}+(i\beta_{1}+\gamma_{1})\bar{X}_{+}\right]X_{+}\\
=&2\alpha_{1}(\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}+\bar{X}_{-}X_{+})+2\beta_{1}(|X_{+}|^{2}+|X_{-}|^{2})\\
\geq&2(\beta_{1}-|\alpha_{1}|)(|X_{+}|^{2}+|X_{-}|^{2}).\end{aligned}

As
\alpha_{1}\sim o(\frac{1}{r})
,
\beta_{1}\sim\frac{\lambda}{\kappa r}
for sufficiently large
r
, there exists a sufficiently large
r_{2}>r_{1}

such that, for
r\geq r_{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left[i\left(\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}-\bar{X}_{-}X_{+}\right)\right]\geq\frac{2q}{r}(|X_{+}|^{2}+|X_{-}|^{2}).

(3.4)

We have already proved that

\displaystyle\left|X_{+}\right|^{2}-\left|X_{-}\right|^{2}=C

for some constant
C
. If
C=0
, then
\Psi\equiv 0
from the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Now we assume
C\neq 0
, then (
3.4
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left[i\left(\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}-\bar{X}_{-}X_{+}\right)\right]\geq\frac{2q|C|}{r}

for
r\geq r_{2}
. Integrating it, we obtain

\displaystyle i\left[\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}-\bar{X}_{-}X_{+}\right]\geq 2q|C|\ln r+C^{\prime}

for
r\geq r_{2}
. Thus there exists
r_{3}>r_{2}
such that, for
r\geq r_{3}
,

\displaystyle i(\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}-\bar{X}_{-}X_{+})>0.

On the other hand, (
3.4
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left[i\left(\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}-\bar{X}_{-}X_{+}\right)\right]\geq\frac{2q}{r}i\left[\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}-\bar{X}_{-}X_{+}\right]

for
r\geq r_{e}
. Therefore, for
r\geq r_{3}
,

\displaystyle\left|X\right|^{2}\geq i\left(\bar{X}_{+}X_{-}-\bar{X}_{-}X_{+}\right)\geq C_{1}r^{2q}

for certain constant
C_{1}>0
.
Thus, for
0<p\leq\frac{4}{3-2q}
,

\displaystyle\int_{M_{r_{1}}}\left|\Psi\right|^{p}d\mu>C^{\prime}\int_{r_{3}}^{\infty}r^{1+(q-\frac{3}{2})p}dr=\infty

This gives a contradiction. Hence
\Psi\equiv 0
for
r\geq r_{e}
. Then, similar to the discussion in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\Psi\equiv 0
for
r>0
(Theorem
3.1
already gives that
\Psi\equiv 0
for
0<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
). Q.E.D.

Corollary 3.1
.

By taking
q=\frac{1}{2}
in Theorem
3.2
, we obtain that there is no normalizable massive Dirac particle with mass greater than
\frac{\kappa}{2}
outside and away from the event horizon in non-extreme Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetime, and they must either disappear into the black hole or escape to infinity.

Note that this recovers the same result of Belgiorno and Cacciatori
[
1
]
in the case of
Q=0
obtained by using spectral methods.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Psi
be the solution of (
1.1
) taking the form (
1.2
) on
r>0
in non-extreme Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetime.
Any nontrivial
\Psi
for
|\lambda|<\frac{\kappa}{2}
must be
L^{2}
over
M_{r_{1}}
with some
r_{1}>r_{e}
.

Proof:
In terms of (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\left|X_{+}\right|^{2}+\left|X_{-}\right|^{2}\right)=&\Re\left(\frac{dX_{+}}{dr}\bar{X}_{+}\right)+\Re\left(\frac{dX_{-}}{dr}\bar{X}_{-}\right)\\
=&\Re\left(\left(i\alpha_{1}X_{+}+(i\beta_{1}+\gamma_{1})X_{-}\right)\bar{X}_{+}\right)\\
&+\Re\left(\left(-i\alpha_{1}X_{-}+(-i\beta_{1}+\gamma_{1})X_{+}\right)\bar{X}_{-}\right)\\
=&\Re\left(i\alpha_{1}\left(X_{+}\bar{X}_{+}-X_{-}\bar{X}_{-}\right)\right)\\
&+\Re\left(i\beta_{1}\left(X_{-}\bar{X}_{+}-X_{+}\bar{X}_{-}\right)\right)\\
&+\Re\left(\gamma_{1}\left(X_{-}\bar{X}_{+}+X_{+}\bar{X}_{-}\right)\right)\\
=&i\beta_{1}\left(X_{-}\bar{X}_{+}-X_{+}\bar{X}_{-}\right)+\gamma_{1}\left(X_{-}\bar{X}_{+}+X_{+}\bar{X}_{-}\right).\end{aligned}

As
\beta_{1}\sim\frac{\lambda}{\kappa r}
and
\gamma_{1}\sim\frac{\epsilon}{\kappa r^{2}}
for sufficiently large
r
, and

|\lambda|<\frac{\kappa}{2}
, there exists a sufficiently large
r_{2}>r_{1}
such that, for
r\geq r_{2}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dr}\left(\left|X_{+}\right|^{2}+\left|X_{-}\right|^{2}\right)\leq\frac{a}{r}(\left|X_{+}\right|^{2}+\left|X_{-}\right|^{2})\end{aligned}

for some constant
0<a<1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|X_{+}\right|^{2}+\left|X_{-}\right|^{2}\leq Cr^{a}\end{aligned}

for some constant
C
. Thus

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\{r>r_{1}\}}\left|\Psi\right|^{2}d\mu\leq C^{\prime}\int_{r_{1}}^{\infty}r^{a-2}dr<\infty.\end{aligned}

Q.E.D.

Finally, we guess that there exists normalizable massive Dirac particle with mass less than
\frac{\kappa}{2}
in non-extreme
Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetime. For the special case
a=0
and spacetime is Schwarzschild-AdS:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widetilde{g}_{SAdS}=&-Wd{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{W}d{r}^{2}+r^{2}(d{\theta}^{2}+\sin^{2}{\theta}d{\varphi}^{2}),\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} W&=1-\frac{2m}{r}+\kappa^{2}r^{2}=\frac{\Delta_{r}}{r^{2}},\end{aligned}

there does exist static, normalizable massless Dirac particle

\Psi=e^{\frac{i}{2}\varphi}S^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\sinh\big(-\int_{r_{0}}^{r}r^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{2}}dr\big)\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\cosh\big(-\int_{r_{0}}^{r}r^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{2}}dr\big)\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\cosh\big(-\int_{r_{0}}^{r}r^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{2}}dr\big)\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\sinh\big(-\int_{r_{0}}^{r}r^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{2}}dr\big)\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\end{pmatrix}

with
k=-1
,
\varepsilon=-1
,
\omega=\lambda=0
.

4.
General stationary axisymmetric spacetimes

In this section, we use the argument in
[
7
]
to study the analogous nonexistence for general stationary axisymmetric spacetimes
with negative cosmological constant. The general stationary axisymmetric solutions for the Einstein field equations can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{\Delta_{\mu}(d\chi+\nu^{2}d\psi)^{2}-\Delta_{\nu}(d\chi-\mu^{2}d\psi)^{2}}{\nu^{2}+\mu^{2}}+(\nu^{2}+\mu^{2})\Big(\frac{d\nu^{2}}{\Delta_{\nu}}+\frac{d\mu^{2}}{\Delta_{\mu}}\Big),

(4.1)

with the potential

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}+\mu^{2}}\big(H(\nu)(d\chi-\mu^{2}d\psi)+K(\mu)(d\chi+\nu^{2}d\psi)\big),

(4.2)

where
\Delta_{\mu}
,
\Delta_{\nu}
are smooth functions depending only on
\mu
,
\nu
respectively,

-\infty<\chi,\ \nu<\infty
,
0\leq\psi<2\pi
,
-a<\mu<a
for some positive constant
a
.
We refer to
[
3
]
for choices of these functions for Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetime.

Consider the Dirac equation

\left(D+i\lambda\right)\Psi=0,\quad\Psi=S^{-1}\Phi,\quad\Phi=e^{-i\left(\omega\chi+k\psi\right)}\begin{pmatrix}X_{-}(\nu)Y_{-}(\mu)\\
X_{+}(\nu)Y_{+}(\mu)\\
X_{+}(\nu)Y_{-}(\mu)\\
X_{-}(\nu)Y_{+}(\mu)\end{pmatrix}

(4.3)

for real eigenvalue
\lambda
and diagonal matrix

S=\left|\Delta_{\nu}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\mbox{diag}\big((\nu+i\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}},\big((\nu+i\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}},\big((\nu-i\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}},\big((\nu-i\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

It is equivalent to the following equations in the region
\Delta_{\nu}>0
(c.f.
[
7
]
)

\mathcal{D}_{1}\phi=\mathcal{D}_{2}\phi,\qquad\phi=\begin{pmatrix}X_{-}(\nu)Y_{-}(\mu)\\
X_{+}(\nu)Y_{+}(\mu)\\
X_{+}(\nu)Y_{-}(\mu)\\
X_{-}(\nu)Y_{+}(\mu)\end{pmatrix}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-i\lambda\nu&0&\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}}D_{\nu+}&0\\
0&i\lambda\nu&0&\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}}D_{\nu-}\\
\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}}D_{\nu-}&0&i\lambda\nu&0\\
0&\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}}D_{\nu+}&0&-i\lambda\nu\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\mu&0&0&\sqrt{\Delta_{\mu}}L_{\mu+}\\
0&-\lambda\mu&-\sqrt{\Delta_{\mu}}L_{\mu-}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\Delta_{\mu}}L_{\mu+}&\lambda\mu&0\\
-\sqrt{\Delta_{\mu}}L_{\mu-}&0&0&-\lambda\mu\end{pmatrix}

with

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{\nu\pm}=&\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mp\frac{i}{\Delta_{\nu}}\left(\omega\nu^{2}+H(\nu)-k\right).\end{aligned}

Thus there exists a real constant
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{dX_{+}(\nu)}{d\nu}-i\alpha X_{+}(\nu)-(i\beta+\gamma)X_{-}(\nu)&=0,\\
\frac{dX_{-}(\nu)}{d\nu}+i\alpha X_{-}(\nu)+(i\beta-\gamma)X_{+}(\nu)&=0\end{aligned}

(4.4)

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \alpha=&\frac{1}{\Delta_{\nu}}\Big(\omega\nu^{2}+H(\nu)-k\Big),\ \ \beta=\frac{\lambda\nu}{\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}}},\ \ \gamma=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}}}.\end{aligned}

Similar equations can also be obtained in the region
\Delta_{\nu}<0
. Now we introduce the following conditions:

(A)

the asymptotic behavior
0<lim_{\nu\rightarrow\pm\infty}(\Delta_{\mu}-\nu^{-4}\mu^{4}\Delta_{\nu})<\infty
since the the slice

\{\chi=constant\}
is asymptotically AdS;

(B)

\Delta_{\nu}
has only simple roots
\nu_{1}<\cdots<\nu_{e}
, which are the horizons;

(C)

X_{+}=0
or
X_{-}=0
on the horizons which can match the solution inside and outside the horizons.

Theorem 4.1
.

Denote
M_{\nu_{*}}
the slice
\{\chi=constant\}
of the spacetime with
\nu\geq\nu_{*}>\nu_{e}
for some
\nu_{*}>0
. Under the conditions
(A)
,
(B)
and
(C)
, any solution
\Psi
of (
4.3
) which is
L^{p}
over
M_{\nu_{*}}
for
0<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
must be zero.

Proof:
In terms of (
4.4
) and
(B)
, we obtain: (i)
|\Phi|<\infty
on
\nu=\nu_{e}
; (ii)
|\Phi|=0
on
\nu=\nu_{e}
implies that
|\Phi|=0
on
\nu_{e}<\nu\leq\nu_{*}
.

On the other hand, (
4.4
) gives that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{d\nu}\left(\left|X_{+}\right|^{2}-\left|X_{-}\right|^{2}\right)=0.\end{aligned}

This implies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|X_{+}\right|^{2}-\left|X_{-}\right|^{2}=C\end{aligned}

for some constant
C
. If
C\neq 0
, we can assume
C>0
without loss of generality. Thus

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{M_{\nu_{*}}}\left|\Psi\right|^{p}dv>C^{\prime}\int_{\nu_{*}}^{\infty}\nu^{1-\frac{3p}{2}}d\nu=\infty\end{aligned}

for
0<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
under the condition
(A)
. This contradicts the assumption that
\Psi
is
L^{p}
. Thus
C=0
.
Hence
\left|X_{+}\right|^{2}=\left|X_{-}\right|^{2}
for
\nu>\nu_{e}
. The above discussion shows that
\left|X_{\pm}\right|

exists on
\nu=\nu_{e}
. So the condition
(C)
gives
\left|X_{+}\right|=\left|X_{-}\right|=0
on
\nu=\nu_{e}
. Therefore

\Phi\equiv 0
for
\nu\geq\nu_{e}
. The similar argument can show that
\Phi\equiv 0
on the whole slice. Q.E.D.

5.
Conclusion and future work

For non-extreme Kerr-Newman-AdS spacetimes, we proved that there is no nontrivial Dirac particle which is
L^{p}
for
0<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
with arbitrary eigenvalue
\lambda
, and for
\frac{4}{3}<p\leq\frac{4}{3-2q}
,
0<q<\frac{3}{2}
with eigenvalue
|\lambda|>q\kappa
, outside and away from the event horizon. We also proved that any Dirac particle with eigenvalue
|\lambda|<\frac{\kappa}{2}
must be
L^{2}
outside and away from the event horizon. It concludes that there is no normalizable time-period Dirac particle with mass greater than
\frac{\kappa}{2}
. In the future, we shall study whether there exists nontrivial normalizable time-period Dirac particle with mass less than or equal to
\frac{\kappa}{2}
?

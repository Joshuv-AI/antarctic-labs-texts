Preservation of log-Hölder coefficients of the vorticity in the transport equation
Dongho Chae, In-Jee Jeong

Abstract
We show that the log-Hölder coefficients of a solution to the transport equation is preserved in time. AMS Subject Classification Number: 35Q31, 76B03, 76W05 Keywords : Transport equation, incompressible Euler equations, modulus of continuity, vorticity

Introduction

We are concerned on the transport equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(1.1)

\theta_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0\quad\text{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T),

where
u=u(x,t)=(u_{1}(x,t),\cdots,u_{n}(x,t))
for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,+\infty)
, and
\theta=\theta(x,t)
is a scalar
with
\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x)
.

We say
f
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma\in(0,1)
at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

if there exists
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|f(x)-f(x_{0})|}{|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}}<+\infty.

In this case we define the Hölder coefficient
[f]_{\gamma;x_{0}}
by

(1.2)

[f]_{\gamma;x_{0}}=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|f(x)-f(x_{0})|}{|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}},

and denote

C^{\gamma}(x_{0})=\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,|\,[f]_{\gamma;x_{0}}<+\infty\}.

Note that we are following the notation of
[
1
, pp.52]
.
One can generalize the above notion to a general case of functions defining the modulus of continuity
\delta=\delta(s)
, and denote

(1.3)

[f]_{\delta(s);x_{0}}:=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|f(x)-f(x_{0})|}{\delta(|x-x_{0}|)},

for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and write

C^{\delta(s)}(x_{0})=\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,|\,[f]_{\delta(s);x_{0}}<+\infty\}.

Given a vector field
u=u(x,t)\in L^{1}(0,T;Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, we introduce the particle trajectory mapping
x_{0}\mapsto\varphi(x_{0},t)
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
generated by
u=u(x,t)
, which is defined as the solution of the ordinary differential equation,

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\varphi(x_{0},t)}{\partial t}=u(\varphi(x_{0},t),t)\quad\text{on}\quad(0,T),\\
&\varphi(x_{0},0)=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Our main result states that for the transport equation, the log-Hölder coefficient is constant in time.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in L^{1}(0,T;Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\theta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
satisfies (
2.15
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)
.
Suppose there exists
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
such that
\theta_{0}\in C^{(\log\frac{1}{s})^{-\gamma}}(x_{0})
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then,

(1.5)

[\theta(\cdot,t)]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};\varphi(x_{0},t)}=[\theta_{0}]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};x_{0}}\quad\forall t\in[0,T),

where
\varphi(x_{0},t)
denotes the particle trajectory defined in (
1.4
). In particular, we have

(1.6)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\theta(t)]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma},x}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\theta_{0}]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma},x}.

As an application we consider the 2D Euler equations on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,+\infty)
.

(E)\left\{\begin{aligned} &\omega_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\omega=0,\\
&\nabla\cdot u=0,\quad\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1}=\omega,\\
&\omega(x,0)=\omega_{0}(x).\end{aligned}\right.

For bounded initial data
\omega_{0}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, well known Yudovich theory gives a unique solution to
(E)
in
\omega(t,x)\in L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})_{x}.
If
\omega_{0}
has a little bit of additional regularity, then it is guaranteed that
u
is Lipschitz continuous in space (
[
6
]
).

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
u\in L^{1}(0,T;Lip(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
be a solution to (E),
Suppose
\omega_{0}\in C^{(\log\frac{1}{s})^{-\gamma}}(x_{0})
for some
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then,

(1.7)

[\omega(\cdot,t)]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};\varphi(x_{0},t)}=[\omega_{0}]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};x_{0}}\quad\forall t\in[0,T).

Remark 1.1
In the proof, we observe the following inequalities.

(1.8)

\displaystyle[\omega_{0}]_{\beta;x_{0}}\exp\left(-\beta\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla u(\varphi(x_{0},s),s)|ds\right)

(1.9)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq[\omega(\cdot,t)]_{\beta;\varphi(x_{0},t)}\leq[\omega_{0}]_{\beta;x_{0}}\exp\left(\beta\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla u(\varphi(x_{0},s),s)|ds\right)\quad\forall\beta\in(0,1]

for all
t\in[0,T)
. We could understand that (
1.7
) is a limiting relation of (
1.8
) as
\beta\to 0
.

Remark 1.2
It would be interesting to compare (
1.7
) with the result of the double exponential growth in time of the vorticity gradient by Kieselev and Šverák
[
5
]
for the 2D Euler equations. As far as the authors know we do not know if the growth of
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}[\omega(t)]_{\gamma,x}
(or
\|\omega(t)\|_{C^{0,\gamma}}
) is slower than the double exponential one. The above result says that for the log-Hölder coefficient it is constant in time.

Remark 1.3

\begin{split}|\omega_{0}(x)|\simeq\log(\frac{1}{|x|})^{-\gamma},\qquad 0<|x|\ll 1\end{split}

for some
\gamma>0
together with odd-odd symmetry with respect to both axes and satisfying
\omega_{0}\geq 0
on
(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}
was considered in
[
2
]
.

This type of data could be used to prove
ill-posedness
of the 2D Euler equations in the critical Sobolev space
\omega\in H^{1}

Our main result shows that whenever
\gamma\leq 1
,
if
the corresponding velocity field belongs to
L^{1}(0,T;Lip)
for any
T>0
, it is forced that
|\omega(x,t)|\simeq\log(\frac{1}{|x|})^{-\gamma}
. Here, the origin is fixed by the flow due to the odd symmetry. But then, using this asymptotics with the explicit formula

\begin{split}(\partial_{x_{1}}u_{1})(0,t)=c_{0}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}}\frac{y_{1}y_{2}}{|y|^{4}}\omega(y,t)\,dy\gtrsim\int_{r(t)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r(\log\frac{1}{r})^{\gamma}}\,dr,\end{split}

one can prove that

for

, which is a contradiction to the

for any
T>0
. In turn, this forces the

to be divergent;
|\partial_{x_{1}}\Phi_{1}(0,t)|=+\infty
for

suggests that
\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\simeq ct^{-1}
for some absolute constant
c>0
, which is barely non-integrable in time. To clarify, in general (without the odd-odd assumption) it is possible that
|\omega(x,t)|\simeq\log(\frac{1}{|x|})^{-\gamma}
and
u(x,t)\in L^{1}_{t}Lip
hold at the same time for any
\gamma>0
. This happens for instance the vorticity satisfies a rotational symmetry; see
[

]
.

For the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations, we have the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(u,\omega)
be a solution to the 3D Euler equations on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T)
, namely

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\omega\cdot\nabla u,\\
&\nabla\cdot u=0,\quad\nabla\times u=\omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\gamma>0
, and suppose there exists
\gamma_{1}>\gamma
such that

\int_{0}^{t}[u(\tau)]_{1;\varphi(x_{0},\tau)}[\nabla u(\tau)]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma_{1}};\varphi(x_{0},\tau)}d\tau<+\infty

holds for some
0<t<T
.
Then, we have

(1.11)

[\omega(\cdot,t)]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};\varphi(x_{0},t)}=[\omega_{0}]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};x_{0}}\quad\forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In particular,

(1.12)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\omega(\cdot,t)]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};x}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\omega_{0}]_{{\log(\frac{1}{s})}^{-\gamma};x}.

The Proof of the Main Theorem

Proof of Theorem 1.1
In the proof, we assume that
u(x,t)
is
C^{1}
–smooth in
x
, so that the flow map
\varphi(\cdot,t)
defines a
C^{1}
–diffeomorphism on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
0\leq t<T
. The case of
u(x,t)\in L^{1}(0,T;Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
then follows from a simple approximation argument.

From
\varphi(\alpha,t)=\alpha+\int_{0}^{t}u(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)ds
and the triangle inequality,

(2.1)

\displaystyle|\alpha-\beta|-\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(u(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)-u(\varphi(\beta,t),t)\right)ds\right|

(2.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|\leq|\alpha-\beta|+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(u(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)-u(\varphi(\beta,t),t)\right)ds\right|

from which, diving it by
|\alpha-\beta|\not=0
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle 1-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|u(\varphi(\alpha,s),s)-u(\varphi(\beta,s),s)|}{|\varphi(\alpha,s)-\varphi(\beta,s)|}\frac{|\varphi(\alpha,s)-\varphi(\beta,s)|}{|\alpha-\beta|}ds

(2.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}{|\alpha-\beta|}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 1+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|u(\varphi(\alpha,s),s)-u(\varphi(\beta,s),s)|}{|\varphi(\alpha,s)-\varphi(\beta,s)|}\frac{|\varphi(\alpha,s)-\varphi(\beta,s)|}{|\alpha-\beta|}ds,

and

(2.6)

\displaystyle 1-\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{|\varphi(\alpha,s)-\varphi(\beta,s)|}{|\alpha-\beta|}ds

(2.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}{|\alpha-\beta|}\leq 1+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{|\varphi(\alpha,s)-\varphi(\beta,s)|}{|\alpha-\beta|}ds.

By Gronwall’s inequality we obtain

(2.8)

\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\leq\frac{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}{|\alpha-\beta|}\leq\mu(t),\qquad\mu(t)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}ds}

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This shows that

\beta\in B_{\frac{r}{\mu(t)}}(\alpha)\Rightarrow\varphi(\beta,t)\in B_{r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))\Rightarrow\beta\in B_{\mu(t)r}(\alpha)\quad\forall r>0,

which implies that

(2.9)

\varphi(B_{r}(\alpha),t)\subset B_{\mu(t)r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))\quad\text{and}\quad B_{r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))\subset\varphi(B_{\mu(t)r}(\alpha),t).

From this we infer

(2.10)

\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}f(\varphi(\beta,t))=\sup_{\varphi(\beta,t)\in\varphi(B_{r}(\alpha),t)}f(\varphi(\beta,t))\leq\sup_{y\in B_{\mu(t)r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))}f(y),

and

(2.11)

\sup_{y\in B_{r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))}f(y)\leq\sup_{y\in\varphi(B_{\mu(t)r}(\alpha),t)}f(y)=\sup_{\beta\in B_{\mu(t)r}(\alpha)}f(\varphi(\beta,t)).

Combining (
2.10
) with (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}f(\varphi(\beta,t))

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{y\in B_{\mu(t)r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))}f(y)=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{y\in B_{r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))}f(y)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{\mu(t)r}(\alpha)}f(\varphi(\beta,t))=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}f(\varphi(\beta,t)).

Hence,

(2.12)

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}f(\varphi(\beta,t))=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{y\in B_{r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))}f(y).

From (
2.8
) one has

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\log\mu(t)}{\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}}\right)^{-\gamma}\leq\frac{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}\right)^{-\gamma}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}\leq\left(1-\frac{\log\mu(t)}{\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}}\right)^{-\gamma},

and thus

(2.14)

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}\frac{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}\right)^{-\gamma}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}=1.

From
\theta(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)=\theta_{0}(\alpha)
we deduce

(2.15)

\displaystyle[\theta_{0}]_{(\log\frac{1}{s})^{-\gamma},\alpha}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}\frac{|\theta_{0}(\alpha)-\theta_{0}(\beta)|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}\frac{|\theta(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)-\theta(\varphi(\beta,t),t)|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}\right)^{-\gamma}}\frac{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}\right)^{-\gamma}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}\frac{|\theta(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)-\theta(\varphi(\beta,t),t)|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-\varphi(\beta,t)|}\right)^{-\gamma}}

(2.18)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{y\in B_{r}(\varphi(\alpha,t))}\frac{|\theta(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)-\theta(y,t)|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,t)-y|}\right)^{-\gamma}}

(2.19)

\displaystyle=[\theta(t)]_{(\log\frac{1}{s})^{-\gamma},\varphi(\alpha,t)},

where we used (
2.14
) in the third equality and (
2.12
) in the fourth equality respectively.
\square

Remark 2.1
The result is sharp in the case of the 2D Euler equations in the sense that for any
\beta>0
, there exists initial data
\omega_{0}\in C^{\beta}(x_{0})
with
[\omega(\cdot,t)]_{\beta;\varphi(x_{0},t)}
growing at least exponentially in time. To see this, we take
\omega_{0}(x)\in C^{\beta}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
which is odd with respect to both axes, non-negative on the first quadrant and satisfies

\omega_{0}(x)=\frac{2x_{1}x_{2}}{4x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}|x|^{\beta}

for
|x|\leq 1
. There is a unique global in time solution (with Lipschitz continuous velocity) for any
\beta>0
. From the symmetry,
\varphi(0,t)=0
for all
t
. For fixed
|x|\leq 1
, the maximum of
\omega_{0}(x)
is achieved for
2x_{1}=x_{2}
. Using the version of Kiselev-Sverak “Key Lemma” appeared in Zlatos
[
7
]
, one can prove at least for a short time interval that

\begin{split}\varphi_{1}(x,t)\simeq r\exp\left((\frac{C_{0}}{\beta}+O(1))t\right),\qquad\varphi_{2}(x,t)\simeq 2r\exp\left(-(\frac{C_{0}}{\beta}+O(1))t\right),\end{split}

for all
x=(r,2r)
with
0<r<1/10
. In particular,
|\varphi(x,t)|
is decreasing in time. Then, using

\omega(\varphi(x,t),t)=\omega_{0}(x)=\frac{2x_{1}x_{2}}{4x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}|x|^{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{5}r)^{\beta}

for
x=(r,2r)
and

\begin{split}[\omega(\cdot,t)]_{\beta;0}\geq\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{(\sqrt{5}r)^{\beta}}{2|\varphi(x,t)|^{\beta}},\qquad x=(r,2r),\end{split}

we can show local in time growth of
[\omega(\cdot,t)]_{\beta;0}
. This argument could work to prove growth of moduli of continuity which lie “between” Hölder and log-Hölder.

Proof of Theorem 1.3

From the vorticity form of the Euler equations

\omega_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\omega\cdot\nabla u,\quad\nabla\times u=\omega,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0,

\omega(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)=\omega_{0}(\alpha)+\int_{0}^{t}(\omega\cdot\nabla)u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)d\tau.

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{|\omega(\varphi(\alpha,t),t)-\omega(\varphi(\beta,t),t)|}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}

(2.22)

We note that

(2.23)

\displaystyle|\omega\cdot\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)-\omega\cdot\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)|

(2.24)

\displaystyle\leq|\omega(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)||\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)-\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)|

\displaystyle\qquad+|\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)||\omega(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)-\omega(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)|

(2.26)

\displaystyle\leq 2|\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)||\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)-\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)|

\displaystyle\qquad+2|\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)||\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)-\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)|

Hence, we have

(2.29)

\displaystyle\leq 4\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)|\frac{(|\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)-\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)|)}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}d\tau

(2.31)

\displaystyle\leq 4\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)|\times

(2.32)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{(|\nabla u(\varphi(\alpha,\tau),\tau)-\nabla u(\varphi(\beta,\tau),\tau)|)}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,s)-\varphi(\beta,\tau)|}\right)^{-\gamma_{1}}}\frac{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,\tau)-\varphi(\beta,\tau)|}\right)^{-\gamma_{1}}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{0}^{t}[u(t)]_{1;\varphi(\alpha,\tau)}[\nabla u]_{(\log\frac{1}{s})^{-\gamma_{1}};\varphi(\alpha,\tau)}\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\beta\in B_{r}(\alpha)}\frac{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(\alpha,\tau)-\varphi(\beta,\tau)|}\right)^{-\gamma_{1}}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|\alpha-\beta|}\right)^{-\gamma}}d\tau

\displaystyle=0.

on (

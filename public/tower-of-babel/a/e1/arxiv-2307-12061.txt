Torus shadow formalism and exact global conformal blocks
Konstantin Alkalaev and, Semyon Mandrygin

Abstract
Using the shadow formalism we find global conformal blocks of torus CFT 2 . It is shown that n n -point torus blocks in the “necklace” channel (a loop with n n legs) are expressed in terms of a hypergeometric-type function which we refer to as the necklace function.

), see Fig.
3
. Note that a coordinate transformation that moves
z
out of the domain (
3.2
) must be accompanied by some modular transformation of
q
which properly changes the inner boundary
q\bar{q}
(and vice versa), thereby guaranteeing that we stay on the same torus.

Let us introduce the group-theoretic notation. Global conformal symmetries in CFT
2
are governed by
sl(2,\mathbb{C})
with commutation relations
[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}
(plus the same for
\bar{L}_{m}
), where
m,n=0,1,-1
. The (chiral) Verma module
\mathcal{V}_{h}
is spanned by the basis vectors (at level
m
)
|m,h\rangle=L_{-1}^{m}|h\rangle
, with
|h\rangle
being the highest-weight vector,
L_{0}|h\rangle=h|h\rangle
. The Gram matrix in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
is denoted by
\left(B_{h}\right)^{mn}=\langle h,m|n,h\rangle=\delta_{mn}m!(2h)_{m}
, where
(a)_{m}=\Gamma(a+m)/\Gamma(a)
is the Pochhammer symbol.

3.1
Torus correlation functions

Torus correlation functions of
n
primary operators
\phi_{i}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
of the (anti)holomorphic dimensions
h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}
can be written as

\big\langle Y({\bf z},\bar{{\bf z}})\big\rangle_{\tau}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\left[Y({\bf z},\bar{{\bf z}})\,q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}}\right].

(3.3)

Here, we denoted
Y({\bf z},\bar{{\bf z}})\equiv\phi_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})...\,\phi_{n}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})
and
{\bf z}=\{z_{1},...\,,z_{n}\}
are points on
\mathbb{C}
,
the trace is taken over the Hilbert space of states

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{h,\bar{h}\in D}\mathcal{V}_{h}\otimes\bar{\mathcal{V}}_{\bar{h}}\,,

(3.4)

where the domain
D
of admissible dimensions is determined by choosing a particular CFT
2
. Expanding the trace in (
3.3
) yields

\big\langle Y({\bf z},\bar{{\bf z}})\big\rangle_{\tau}=\sum_{\tilde{h},\bar{\tilde{h}}\in D}\sum_{m,\bar{m}=0}^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{h}}^{-1}\right)^{mm}\left(B_{\bar{\tilde{h}}}^{-1}\right)^{\bar{m}\bar{m}}\langle\tilde{h},\bar{\tilde{h}},m,\bar{m}|Y({\bf z},\bar{{\bf z}})\,q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}}|m,\bar{m},\tilde{h},\bar{\tilde{h}}\rangle\,.

(3.5)

Thus, the
n
-point torus correlation function is expressed through the
(n+2)
-point plane matrix elements. Since
L_{0}|m,h\rangle=(m+h)|m,h\rangle
, then the torus correlation function (
3.5
) is a power series in
q
. At
q\rightarrow 0
(when the torus decompactifies onto a plane) all higher-level matrix elements in (
3.5
) are suppressed and the torus
n
-point correlation function becomes equal to the plane
(n+2)
-point correlation function (see
[
11
]
for a detailed discussion). When considering the torus conformal block as a part of the torus CPW we will treat this behaviour as the asymptotic condition:

\mathcal{F}_{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},...,\Delta_{n}}^{h_{1},h_{2},...,h_{n}}(q\rightarrow 0,z_{1},z_{2},...\,,z_{n})\,\sim\,q^{\Delta_{1}}G^{\Delta_{1},h_{1},...,h_{n},\Delta_{1}}_{\Delta_{2},...,\Delta_{n}}(\infty,z_{1},...\,,z_{n},0)\,,

(3.6)

where
\mathcal{F}
is the
n
-point torus conformal block,
G
is the
(n+2)
-point plane block and
\Delta_{1}\in D
is the torus intermediate dimension coming from the trace (see the next section).

The torus geometry breaks the global conformal symmetry of the plane such that the global torus symmetry is only translational,
u(1)\oplus u(1)\subset sl(2,\mathbb{C})
, generated by
L_{0},\bar{L}_{0}
(in the plane parametrization these transformations become dilatations). It constrains the torus correlation functions by implementing the global Ward identities. The holomorphic Ward identity reads as

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}\big\langle\phi_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\ldots\phi_{i}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\ldots\phi_{n}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\big\rangle_{\tau}=0\,,

(3.7)

where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is an action of
L_{0}
on primary operators (the superscript means acting on
i
-th coordinate)

\left[L_{m},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=\mathcal{L}_{m}\phi(z,\bar{z}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{m}=z^{m}\left(z\partial_{z}+h(m+1)\right)\,,\quad m=0,\pm 1\,,

(3.8)

along with the same relations for
\bar{L}_{m}
.

Torus shadow formalism

In this section we define a torus CPW within the shadow formalism which will be the first step towards the calculation of torus conformal blocks.

Conformal partial waves in thermal CFT

To this end, consider first 1-point torus functions. The respective CPW can be obtained by inserting the projector (

\sum_{\tilde{h},\bar{\tilde{h}}\in D}\sum_{m,\bar{m}=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\rm d}^{2}w\left(B_{\tilde{h}}^{-1}\right)^{mm}\left(B_{\bar{\tilde{h}}}^{-1}\right)^{\bar{m}\bar{m}}\langle\tilde{h},\bar{\tilde{h}},m,\bar{m}|\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{{w}})|0\rangle\\

Representing the primary operator in any point as

\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{{w}})=e^{wL_{-1}+\bar{{w}}\bar{L}_{-1}}\,\mathcal{O}(0,0)\,e^{-(wL_{-1}+\bar{{w}}\bar{L}_{-1})}\,,

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{{w}})|0\rangle&=\sum_{n,\bar{n}=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}\frac{\bar{{w}}^{\bar{n}}}{\bar{n}!}L_{-1}^{n}\bar{L}_{-1}^{\bar{n}}|\Delta,\bar{\Delta}\rangle=\sum_{n,\bar{n}=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}\frac{\bar{{w}}^{\bar{n}}}{\bar{n}!}|n,\Delta\rangle\otimes|\bar{n},\bar{\Delta}\rangle\,,\end{split}

(3.11)

where we used the conformal invariance of the vacuum state,
L_{m}|0\rangle=0
and
\bar{L}_{m}\ket{0}=0

) can be rewritten as (to keep things simple we suppress
\bar{z}

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\sum_{\tilde{h}\in D}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int{\rm d}w\frac{w^{n}}{n!}\left(B_{\tilde{h}}^{-1}\right)^{mm}\langle\tilde{h},m|n,\Delta\rangle\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(w)\phi_{1}(z_{1})q^{L_{0}}|m,\tilde{h}\rangle\\
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\int{\rm d}w\big\langle\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(w)\phi_{1}(z_{1})q^{L_{0}}\mathcal{O}(w)\big\rangle.\end{array}

(3.12)

Here, we used
\langle\tilde{h},m|n,\Delta\rangle=\delta_{\tilde{h},\Delta}\left(B_{\Delta}\right)^{mn}=\delta_{\tilde{h},\Delta}\delta_{m,n}m!(2\Delta)_{m}

\tilde{h}=\Delta
and
m=n
; after that, using (
3.11

\mathcal{O}
. One can go further and expand

\begin{split}q^{L_{0}}\mathcal{O}(w)|0\rangle&=\sum_{k,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi i\tau\,L_{0})^{k}}{k!}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}|n,\Delta\rangle=\sum_{k,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi i\tau(\Delta+n))^{k}}{k!}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}|n,\Delta\rangle\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\Delta+n}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}|n,\Delta\rangle=q^{\Delta}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(qw)^{n}}{n!}|n,\Delta\rangle=q^{\Delta}\mathcal{O}(qw)|0\rangle\,,\end{split}

(3.13)

where we again used (
3.11
) and
L_{0}|n,\Delta\rangle=(\Delta+n)|n,\Delta\rangle
. Now, substituting this relation into (
3.12

\Upsilon_{\Delta,\bar{\Delta}}^{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(q,\bar{q},z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})=q^{\Delta}\bar{q}^{\bar{\Delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\rm d}^{2}w\,V_{1-\Delta,h_{1},\Delta}(w,z_{1},qw)\bar{V}_{1-\bar{\Delta},\bar{h}_{1},\bar{\Delta}}(\bar{{w}},\bar{z}_{1},\bar{q}\bar{{w}})\,,

with the 3-point functions

given by (

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\Upsilon_{{\bf\Delta},\bar{{\bf\Delta}}}^{{\bf h},\bar{\bf h}}(q,\bar{q},{\bf z},\bar{{\bf z}})=q^{\Delta_{1}}\bar{q}^{\bar{\Delta}_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left[{\rm d}w\right]_{1}^{n}\left[{\rm d}\bar{{w}}\right]_{1}^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}V_{1-\Delta_{j},h_{j},\Delta_{j+1}}(w_{j},z_{j},w_{j+1})\right)\\
\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\times\left(\prod_{\bar{j}=1}^{n}\bar{V}_{1-\bar{\Delta}_{\bar{j}},\bar{h}_{\bar{j}},\bar{\Delta}_{\bar{j}+1}}(\bar{{w}}_{\bar{j}},\bar{z}_{\bar{j}},\bar{{w}}_{\bar{j}+1})\right).\end{array}

(3.15)

Here, the antiholomorphic measure is obtained from (

) by substituting

From now on we consider the coordinates and the modular parameter to be real,

Torus cross-ratios.

These particular combinations of

(4.26)

\hskip 11.38109ptz_{i}\rightarrow z^{-1}_{i},\qquad q\rightarrow q^{-1}\,.

A few comments are in order. First, in terms of the modulus

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have found exact functions of the global torus CFT
1

n
-point conformal blocks in the necklace channel. Up to the leg factor these blocks are given by the necklace functions which are hypergeometric-type functions of
n
torus cross-ratios defined as particular combinations of the locations of the insertion points of primary operators as well as the modular parameter. To this end, we have elaborated the torus shadow formalism which is a version of the standard shadow formalism. Given the (anti-)holomorphic factorization of the CFT
2
torus blocks, the necklace functions also define the CFT
2
global necklace blocks.

Our results can be naturally extended in a few directions. First of all, we note that both the comb functions in plane CFT
2
and the necklace functions in torus CFT
2
belong to the same family of the hypergeometric-type series. It is tempting to speculate that considering CFT
2
on higher genus-
g
Riemann surfaces the conformal block functions in the channels which generalize the comb and necklace channels are members of the same hypergeometric-type family of functions. The second natural direction is to use the elaborated machinery to calculate conformal blocks in thermal CFT
d
and their asymptotics, see e.g.
[
31
,
16
,
32
,
33
]
. Third, up to a truncated Virasoro character the global torus blocks are the large-
c
asymptotics of the Virasoro torus blocks (the so-called light blocks)
[
6
,
9
]
. Then, the Zamolodchikov’s
c
-recursion in torus CFT
2
should be rephrased in terms of the torus cross-ratios which proved to be an efficient parameterization of torus blocks. Finally, it would be interesting to find an integral representation for the necklace function along with various Kummer-type and Pfaff-type transformations that could be useful for applications.

B.1

The necklace function is defined to be

(B.1)

where
n=1,2,...\,

\hskip 17.07164ptc_{1}\end{array}\bigg|\;4x_{1}\right],

(B.2)

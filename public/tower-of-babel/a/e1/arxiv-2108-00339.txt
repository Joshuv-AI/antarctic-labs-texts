A direct proof of Stahl’s theorem for a generic class of algebraic functions
Sergey P. Suetin

Abstract
. Under the assumption of the existence of Stahl’s S S -compact set we give a short proof of the limit zeros distribution of Padé polynomials and convergence in capacity of diagonal Padé approximants for a generic class of algebraic functions. The proof is direct but not from the opposite as Stahl’s original proof is. The generic class means in particular that all branch points of the multi-sheeted Riemann surface of the algebraic function are of the first order (i.e., we assume the surface is such that all branch points are of square root type). We do not use the relations of orthogonality at all. The proof is based on the maximum principle only. Bibliography: [ 13 ] titles.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

The seminal Stahl’s Theory (see
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
1
]
and the bibliography therein) on the convergence of diagonal Padé approximants for the multivalued analytic functions consists of two parts. Namely, geometrical part and analytical part. In the first part it is proven that for a multi-valued function there exists a unique admissible compact set
S
possesses Stahl’s symmetric property. Traditionally it is refereed to as an
S
-compact set or briefly
S
-curve (see
[
12
]
and
[
9
]
for details). In the original Stahl’s Theory it is supposed that the set of singularities of a multivalued analytic function is of zero (logarithmic) capacity. Here we restrict our investigation to the case of finite number of singular points only. Note that a short proof of the existence of an
S
-curve for a multivalued analytic function with a finite number of singular points was proposed by E. Rakhmanov in 1994 in the unpublished paper
[
8
]
(see also
[
6
]
). Rakhmanov’s proof is based on the connection between capacity and potential of equilibrium measure of a compact set.

In the second part of the Stahl’s Theory it is proven that there exists zeros limit distribution of Padé polynomials and convergence in capacity of the diagonal Padé approximants takes place as well. The original Stahl’s proof of the existence of limit zeros distribution of Padé polynomials is based on the method of proof from the opposite. In the current paper we give a short and direct proof of the second part of Stahl’s Theory for a generic class of algebraic functions and under the assumption that the first geometrical component is valid.
We do not use the relations of orthogonality at all.
The proof is based on the maximum principle only (cf.
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
).

Let introduce a generic class
\mathscr{F}
of admissible multivalued functions which was mentioned above.

We set that
f\in\mathscr{F}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)
f
is an algebraic function with all the branch points of
the first order (i.e., we assume that all the branch points are of square root type);

(2)
S
-curve of
f
consists of a finite number of non-intersected closed analytic arcs each of which contains exactly two branch points of
f
– the ends of the arc.

Some discussion on this assumptions are given in Section
3
.

1.2.

Let
f\in\mathscr{F}
,
f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{H}(\infty)
and
S=S(f_{\infty})
be the corresponding Stahl’s compact set.
Let
\lambda_{S}
be the probability equilibrium measure supported on
S
and
V^{\lambda_{S}}(z)
be the corresponding logarithmic potential of
\lambda_{S}
:

(1)

V^{\lambda_{S}}(z)=\gamma_{S}-g_{S}(z,\infty),\quad z\in D:=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S,

g_{S}(z,\infty)
is the Green function for the domain
D
with the logarithmic singularity at the infinity point
z=\infty
and
\gamma_{S}
is the Robin constant for
D
.

Set
\mathbb{P}_{n}:=\mathbb{C}_{n}[z]
be the set of all algebraic polynomials with complex coefficients of order
\leqslant{n}
. For arbitrary polynomial
Q\in\mathbb{C}[z]
,
Q\not\equiv 0
, we denote by
\chi(Q)
the zero counting measure of
Q
:
\chi(Q):=\sum\limits_{\zeta:Q(\zeta)=0}\delta_{\zeta}
, where each zero of
Q
is represented with respect to its multiplicity.

For
f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{H}(\infty)
and arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
Padé polynomials
P_{n},Q_{n}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
,
Q_{n}\not\equiv 0
, are defined (non-uniquely) from the following relation:

(2)

R_{n}(z):=(Q_{n}f_{\infty}-P_{n})(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{n+1}}\right),\quad z\to\infty;

R_{n}(z)
is the so-called error function.

The following result holds (see
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{F}
and
f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{H}(\infty)
. Then as
n\to\infty

(3)

\frac{1}{n}\chi(Q_{n}),\frac{1}{n}\chi(P_{n})\overset{*}{\longrightarrow}\lambda_{S}.

Set
Q_{n}(z)=z^{k_{n}}+\dotsb
, then as
n\to\infty

(4)

\displaystyle|R_{n}(z)|^{1/n}\overset{\operatorname{cap}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{cap}(S)e^{-g_{S}(z,\infty)}\quad\text{inside $D$},

(5)

\displaystyle\bigl|f(z)-[n/n]_{f}(z)\bigr|^{1/n}\overset{\operatorname{cap}}{\longrightarrow}e^{-2g_{S}(z,\infty)}\quad\text{inside $D$}.

Recall that the existence of an
S
-compact set for a function
f\in\mathscr{F}
follows from
[
8
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

2.1.

For two positive sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}
the relation
\alpha_{n}\asymp\beta_{n}
means that
0<C_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}\leqslant C_{2}<\infty
for
n=1,2,\dots
and some constants
C_{1},C_{2}
which do not depend on
n
. For two sequences
\{\alpha_{n}(z)\}
and
\{\beta_{n}(z)\}
of functions holomorphic in a domain
\Omega
the relation
\alpha_{n}\asymp\beta_{n}
means that for each compact set
K\subset\Omega
and
n=1,2,\dots

0<C_{1}\leqslant|\alpha_{n}(z)/\beta_{n}(z)|\leqslant C_{2}<\infty
for
z\in K
where the constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depend on
K
but do not depend on
n
and
z\in K
. Evidently that for such pairs of sequences and functions we have that
|\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}|^{1/n}\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.

Since
f\in\mathscr{F}
, we have that
S=S(f)=\bigsqcup\limits_{j=1}^{p}S_{j}
, where
S_{j}=\operatorname{arc}(a_{2j-1},a_{2j})
.
Set
w^{2}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}(z-a_{2j-1})(z-a_{2j})
. Then the two-sheeted hyperelliptic Riemann surface (RS)
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
of the function
w
is the RS
associated with
f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{H}(\infty)
,
f\in\mathscr{F}
, in accordance with Stahl’s Theory. Point
\mathbf{z}
on
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
is given by
\mathbf{z}=(z,w)
. The RS
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
may be considered as a two-sheeted covering of the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with the canonical projection
\pi
,
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
\pi(\mathbf{z})=z
. Let
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}:=\pi^{-1}(S)
. Then
\Gamma
shares
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
into two domains, We shall call them the (open) sheets of
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
.
The function
w
is a single-valued function on this RS and has opposite signs on the sheets. We denote by
\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(0)}
the sheet of
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
where
w(z)/z^{p}\to 1
as
z\to\infty
and refereed to it as zero sheet. Another sheet will be denoted by
\mathfrak{R}^{(1)}_{2}
and refereed to as first sheet of
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
. Then we have
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)=\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(0)}\sqcup\Gamma\sqcup\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(1)}
. Points on the sheets will be denoted by
z^{(j)}
,
j=0,1
, respectively. Evidently
\pi(\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(j)})=D
. We shall identify
\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(0)}
with the Stahl’s domain
D=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{S}
and
\infty^{(0)}
with
\infty
and consider the germ
f_{\infty}
as the germ
f_{\infty^{(0)}}
. In general
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
is not the Riemann surface of
f
and thus
f
is not a single-valued on
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
. But since
f\in\mathscr{F}
the germ
f_{\infty^{(0)}}
can be extended from the infinity point
\infty^{(0)}
to the whole zero sheet
\mathfrak{R}^{(0)}_{2}
and even more to a small enough neighborhood
V^{(0,1)}
of
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
,
V^{(0,1)}\cap\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(1)}\neq\varnothing
. In accordance with Stahl’s Theory (see also
[
8
]
) the Green function
g_{S}(z,\infty)
of
D
can be lifted to RS
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
as
g(\mathbf{z})
with the following properties:
g(z^{(0)})=g(z,\infty)
,
g(z^{(1)})=-g(z^{(0})<0
. From now on we shall suppose that
V^{(0,1)}
is such that
\partial V^{(0,1)}\cap\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(1)}=\{z^{(1)}:g(z,\infty)=\log{R}\}
,
R>1
. Set

\mathfrak{D}:=\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(0)}\cup V^{(0,1)}
be a domain on
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(w)
and
f_{\infty^{(0)}}
continues in
\mathfrak{D}
as a meromorphic (analytic and single-valued) function,
f\in\mathscr{M}(\mathfrak{D})
.
The function
R_{n}(z)
is also extended to
\mathfrak{D}
as a meromorphic function
R_{n}(\mathbf{z})
.
Let a polynomial
q_{m}(z)=z^{m}+\dotsb
is such that the set of its zeros coincide with the projection of poles of
f
in
\mathfrak{D}
. Then
\widetilde{f}:=q_{n}f
and
q_{m}R_{n}
are holomorphic functions in
\mathfrak{D}
.

2.2.

For each
\rho\in(1,R)
we denote by
\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rho}
the set of points
z^{(1)}
such that
g_{S}(z,\infty)=\log\rho
as
z^{(1)}\in\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rho}
. Clearly we have
g(z^{(1)})=-\log\rho
. The set
\Gamma^{(0)}_{\rho}
is defined similarly and we have on that set
g(z^{(0)})=\log\rho
.
For
\rho\in(1,R)
let
D^{(1)}_{\rho}
be a subdomain of
\mathfrak{D}
with
\partial D^{(1)}_{\rho}=\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rho}
,
\infty^{(0)}\in D^{(1)}
. Similarly
D^{(0)}_{\rho}\subset\mathfrak{D}
,
\partial D^{(0)}=\Gamma^{(0)}_{\rho}
,
\infty^{(0)}\in D^{(0)}
. Set

(6)

u_{n}(\mathbf{z}):=\log|q_{m}(z)R_{n}(\mathbf{z})|+(n+1-m)g(\mathbf{z}),\quad\mathbf{z}\in\mathfrak{D}.

Function
u_{n}
is a subharmonic function in
\mathfrak{D}
. Thus for each
\rho\in(1,R)
we have that

(7)

u_{n}(\mathbf{z})\leqslant\max_{\mathbf{\zeta}\in\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rho}}u_{n}(\zeta),\quad\mathbf{z}\in D^{(1)}_{\rho}.

From (
7
) it follows that

(8)

\bigl|R_{n}(z^{(0)})q_{m}(z)\bigr|\leqslant\frac{1}{\rho^{2n+2-2m}}M_{n,1}(\rho),\qquad z\in\Gamma_{\rho}.

where we set
M_{n,1}(\rho):=\max\limits_{z\in\Gamma_{\rho}}|R_{n}(z^{(1)})q_{m}(z)|
.
Relations (
6
) and (
7
) imply that for
1<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<R
we have

(9)

M_{n,1}(\rho_{1})\leqslant\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{n-m+1}M_{n,1}(\rho_{2}).

The following identity holds true:

\displaystyle R_{n}(z^{(0)})

\displaystyle=Q_{n}(z)f(z^{(0)})-P_{n}(z)=Q_{n}(z)f(z^{(1)})-P_{n}(z)+Q_{n}(z)[f(z^{(0)})-f(z^{(1)})]

(10)

\displaystyle=R_{n}(z^{(1)})+Q_{n}(z)[f(z^{(0)})-f(z^{(1)})],\quad z\in\Gamma_{\rho}.

From (
8
) and (
9
) it follows that

(11)

\max_{z\in\Gamma_{\rho}}|Q_{n}(z)[f(z^{(0)})-f(z^{(1)})]q_{m}(z)|=M_{n,1}(\rho)(1+\varepsilon_{n}),\quad\varepsilon_{n}\to 0.

From now on we shall consider only such
\rho\in(1,R)
that
|q_{m}(z)[f(z^{(0)})-f(z^{(1)})]|\geqslant C(\rho)>0
as
z\in\Gamma_{\rho}
(clearly,
f(z^{(0)})-f(z^{(1)})\not\equiv 0
).
Set
m_{n}(\rho):=\max\limits_{z\in\Gamma_{\rho}}|Q_{n}(z)|
. Then from (
11
) it follows that

(12)

m_{n}(\rho)\asymp M_{n,1}(\rho).

Since
\operatorname{deg}{Q_{n}}\leqslant{n}
, then Bernstein–Walsh theorem gives us the inequality:

(13)

|Q_{n}(z)|\leqslant e^{ng_{\Gamma_{\rho_{1}}}(z,\infty)}m_{n}(\rho_{1}),\quad z\in\Gamma_{\rho_{2}},\quad\rho_{2}>\rho_{1},

where
g_{\Gamma_{\rho_{1}}}(z,\infty)
is the Green function for the domain
g_{S}(z,\infty)>\log\rho_{1}
. Clearly,
g_{\Gamma_{\rho_{1}}}(z,\infty)=g_{S}(z,\infty)-\log\rho_{1}
. From (
13
) we obtain the estimate

(14)

m_{n}(\rho_{2})\leqslant\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{n}m_{n}(\rho_{1}).

After combining the relations (
9
), (
12
) and (
14
) we obtain that

(15)

m_{n}(\rho_{2})\asymp\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{n}m_{n}(\rho_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad M_{n,1}(\rho_{2})\asymp\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{n}M_{n,1}(\rho_{1}).

Let
Q_{n}(z)=z^{k_{n}}+\dotsb
,
k_{n}=\operatorname{deg}{Q_{n}}\leqslant{n}
.
Again Bernstein–Walsh theorem gives us the inequality:

(16)

|Q_{n}(z)|\leqslant e^{k_{n}g_{\Gamma_{\rho_{1}}}(z,\infty)}m_{n}(\rho_{1}),\quad z\in\Gamma_{\rho_{2}},\quad\rho_{2}>\rho_{1}.

From this relation it follows that

(17)

m_{n}(\rho_{2})\leqslant\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{k_{n}}m_{n}(\rho_{1}).

But in accordance with (
15
)
m_{n}(\rho_{2})\asymp(\rho_{2}/\rho_{1})^{n}m_{n}(\rho_{1})
. From here and (
17
) we obtain the inequality

(18)

\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{n}\leqslant C\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{k_{n}},

where
C=C(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
,
k_{n}\leqslant{n}
,
1<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
. It directly follows from (
18
) that

(19)

\operatorname{deg}{Q_{n}}/n\to 1,\quad n\to\infty.

2.3.

Let
D_{\rho}:=\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}:g_{S}(z,)>\log\rho\}
,
\rho>1
,
g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\infty)
be the Green function for
D_{\rho}
. Then
g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\infty)=g_{S}(z,\infty)-\log\rho=\log{|z|}+\gamma_{\rho}+o(1)=\log|z|+\gamma_{S}-\log\rho+o(1)
. Thus
\gamma_{\rho}=\gamma_{S}-\log\rho
.

Set

(20)

u_{n}(z):=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\log|Q_{n}(z)|-g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\infty)-\frac{1}{k_{n}}\log m_{n}(\rho).

Since function
u_{n}
is subharmonic in the domain
D_{\rho}
and
u_{n}\leqslant 0
on
\Gamma_{\rho}
, then
u_{n}\leqslant 0
in
D_{\rho}
and
u_{n}(\infty)\leqslant 0
. Thus we have that
\log\rho-\gamma_{S}\leqslant\log m_{n}(\rho)^{1/k_{n}}
and finally obtain

(21)

m_{n}(\rho)^{1/k_{n}}\geqslant\rho e^{-\gamma_{S}}=\rho\operatorname{cap}(S).

For
z\in K\Subset D\rho
and
\zeta\in\Gamma_{\rho}
we have by definition
g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\zeta)=0
. Let now extend the function
g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\zeta)
inside
\Gamma_{\rho}
by identity
g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\zeta)\equiv 0
when
z\in D_{\rho}
,
\zeta\in\operatorname{int}\Gamma_{\rho}
.

Let
Q_{n}(z)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}}(z-\zeta_{j})
,

(22)

v_{n}(z):=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\log|Q_{n}(z)|-g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\infty)+\frac{1}{k_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\zeta_{j})-\frac{1}{k_{n}}\log m_{n}(\rho),

z\in D_{\rho}
. Then
v_{n}
is a harmonic function in
D_{\rho}
and
v_{n}\leqslant 0
on
\Gamma_{\rho}
. From this it follows that for
z\in\Gamma_{\rho_{2}}
,
\rho<\rho_{2}<R
, we have

(23)

|Q_{n}(z)|\exp\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\zeta_{j})\right\}\leqslant m_{n}(\rho)\exp\biggl\{k_{n}g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\infty)\biggr\}.

Let
z_{n}^{*}\in\Gamma_{\rho_{2}}
is such that
|Q_{n}(z_{n}^{*})|=m_{n}(\rho_{2})
. Then from (
23
) we obtain that

(24)

m_{n}(\rho_{2})\exp\biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z_{n}^{*},\zeta_{j})\biggr\}\leqslant m_{n}(\rho)\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho}\right)^{k_{n}}.

Ultimately from (
24
) and (
15
) we obtain that

(25)

\exp\biggl\{\frac{1}{k_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z_{n}^{*},\zeta_{j})\biggr\}\leqslant C(\rho,\rho_{2})^{1/k_{n}},\quad\varepsilon_{n}\to 0.

Let now
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{N}
is a subsequence such that
z_{n}^{*}\to z^{*}\in\Gamma_{\rho_{2}}
and
\frac{1}{k_{n}}\chi(Q_{n})\to\mu
as
n\to\infty
,
n\in\Lambda
,
\mu(1)=1
.
Then according to the descendence principle (see
[
5
, Chapter I, § 3, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
10
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
), we have that

(26)

\int g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z^{*},\zeta)\,d\mu(\zeta)\leqslant 0,\quad z^{*}\in\Gamma_{\rho_{2}},\quad\rho_{2}>\rho.

It directly follows from (
26
) that
\operatorname{supp}{\mu}\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus D_{\rho}
. Since it is true for every
\rho>1
than we have that
\operatorname{supp}{\mu}\subset S
.

2.4.

So we have got that
k_{n}/n\to 1
and each limit point
\mu
of the sequence
\{\frac{1}{k_{n}}\chi(Q_{n})\}
satisfies the condition
\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset S
.

Since
\operatorname{supp}{\mu}\subset{S}
we have that

\varlimsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}m_{n}(\rho)^{1/k_{n}}\leqslant C_{2}<\infty.

Also from (
21
) it follows that

\varliminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}m_{n}(\rho)^{1/k_{n}}\geqslant C_{1}>0.

Set

u_{n}(z):=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\log\frac{1}{|Q_{n}(z)|}-\frac{1}{k_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\zeta_{j})+g_{\Gamma_{\rho}}(z,\infty),\quad z\in D_{\rho}.

Then
\{u_{n}\}
is a sequence of harmonic functions in
D_{\rho}
.
Let
u(z)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(z)
. Then
u
is a harmonic function with the following properties (see (
15
)):

\displaystyle u(z)=V^{\mu}(z)-V^{\lambda_{S}}(z)+\operatorname{const},

\displaystyle\min_{z\in\Gamma_{\rho_{1}}}u(z)=\min_{z\in\Gamma_{\rho_{2}}}u(z)\quad\text{for each}\quad\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>\rho.

From this it follows that
u(z)=\operatorname{const}
and thus
V^{\mu}(z)=V^{\lambda_{S}}(z)
for
z\in D_{\rho}
. Since
\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset S
, then
V^{\mu}(z)=V^{\lambda_{S}}
for
z\in D
. Finally
\mu=\lambda_{S}
because
S
has no inner points.

Eventually we have that for each
\rho>1
there exist the limits

(27)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}m_{n}(\rho)^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n,1}(\rho)^{1/n}=\rho\operatorname{cap}{S},\quad\rho>1,

(28)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{z\in S}|Q_{n}(z)|^{1/n}=\operatorname{cap}{S}=e^{-\gamma_{S}},\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{z\in\Gamma_{\rho}}|R_{n}(z^{(0)})|^{1/n}=\frac{1}{\rho}e^{-\gamma_{S}}.

From the above it easy to prove that

(29)

|R_{n}(z)|^{1/n}\overset{\operatorname{cap}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{cap}(S)e^{-g_{S}(z,\infty)},\quad n\to\infty.

Indeed, let

(30)

v_{n}(z):=\frac{1}{n-m+1}\log|q_{m}(z)R_{n}(z)|+g_{S}(z,\infty)+\gamma_{S}.

Then for each
\rho>1
function
v_{n}
is a subharmonic function in
D_{\rho}
and from (
28
) it follows that for
z\in\Gamma_{\rho}
we have

(31)

v_{n}(z)\leqslant C_{n},

where
C_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. From (
31
) we obtain that for
z\in D_{\rho}

(32)

|q_{m}(z)R_{m}(z)|^{1/(n-m+1)}\leqslant\operatorname{cap}(S)e^{-g_{S}(z,\infty)}e^{C_{n}}.

Thus

(33)

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}|R_{n}(z)|^{1/n}\leqslant\operatorname{cap}(S)e^{-g_{S}(z,\infty)}.

Relation (
29
) follows from (
28
) and (
33
) by two-constants theorem (cf.
[
4
, § 3, subsection 8, (31)–(36)]
).

Since
\frac{1}{n}\chi(Q_{n})\to\lambda_{S}
, then inside
D

(34)

|Q_{n}(z)|^{1/n}\overset{\operatorname{cap}}{\longrightarrow}e^{-V^{\lambda_{S}}(z)},\quad n\to\infty.

From (
29
) and (
34
) the relation (
5
) follows.

Theorem
1
is proven.

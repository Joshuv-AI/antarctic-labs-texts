Domain Walls in the Heisenberg-Ising Spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} Chain
Axel Saenz, Craig A. Tracy, Harold Widom

Abstract
In this paper we obtain formulas for the distribution of the left-most up-spin in the Heisenberg-Ising spin-1/2 chain with anisotropy parameter Δ \Delta , also known as the XXZ spin-1/2 chain, on the one-dimensional lattice ℤ \mathbb{Z} with domain wall initial conditions. We use the Bethe Ansatz to solve the Schrödinger equation and a recent antisymmetrization identity of Cantini, Colomo, and Pronko [ 9 ] to simplify the marginal distribution of the left-most up-spin. In the Δ = 0 \Delta=0 case, the distribution F 2 F_{2} arises. In the Δ ≠ 0 \Delta\neq 0 case, we propose a conjectural series expansion type formula based on a saddle point analysis. The conjectural formula turns out to be a Fredholm series expansion in the Δ → 0 \Delta\rightarrow 0 limit and recovers the result for Δ = 0 \Delta=0 .

The definition of the quantum spin chain Hamiltonian on the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
requires some explanation since there is the problem of making sense of infinite tensor products in the construction of a Hilbert space of states. The general construction uses the Gelfand-Naimark-Segal (GNS) construction; but in the case considered here, there is an elementary treatment

[

]
which we now describe.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbb{C}
. For each positive integer

\mathcal{X}_{N}:=\left\{X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N}\right\}

\mathcal{H}_{N}:=\ell^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{N}).

The Hilbert space of states is

\mathcal{H}:=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{N}.

The normalized state
\Omega=1\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the ground state of all spins down. In physicists’ notation

\Omega=|\cdots\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\cdots\rangle.

Given
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}
, define
e_{X}\in\mathcal{H}_{N}
by

e_{X}(Y)=\delta_{X,Y}.

The set
\{e_{X}\}_{X\in\mathcal{X}_{N}}
defines a natural orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
. The physical interpretation of

e_{X}
is the state with up spins at
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N}
in a sea of down spins:

e_{X}=|\cdots\underset{x_{1}}{\uparrow}\cdots\underset{x_{2}}{\uparrow}\cdots\underset{x_{N}}{\uparrow}\cdots\rangle.

This is a model
of a quantum lattice gas (see, for example, §6.1.6 of
[
25
]
). We will frequently use this particle interpretation.

We introduce the

\sigma_{j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{3}e_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hskip 6.45831pt\>e_{X}&\textrm{if}\>\>\>j\in X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\},\\
-\>e_{X}&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{+}e_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\textrm{if}\>\>\>j\in\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\},\\
e_{X^{+}}&\textrm{where}\>\>\>X^{+}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},j,x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N}\},\>x_{k}<j<x_{k+1}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{-}e_{X}

\sigma^{-1}(i)={\sigma^{\prime}}^{-1}(j)
and
\sigma^{-1}(j)={\sigma^{\prime}}^{-1}(i)
, and
\sigma^{-1}(k)={\sigma^{\prime}}^{-1}(k)
when

. The factor (
120
) is clearly the same for both when
\xi_{i}=\xi_{j}
, and we shall show that the
\sigma
- and
\sigma^{\prime}
-factors in (

) are negatives of each other when

.

Assume for definiteness that

i<j\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \sigma^{-1}(i)<\sigma^{-1}(j).

(124)

(Otherwise we reverse the roles of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
.) Then the factor

\sigma
in (
121
) but it does appear for
\sigma^{\prime}
.
This factor equals
-1
when
\xi_{i}=\xi_{j}
.

To complete the proof it is enough to show that for any

the product of
S
-factors involving
k
and either
i
or
j
is the same for
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
when
\xi_{i}=\xi_{j}
. There are nine cases, depending on the position of
k
relative to
i
and
j
and the position of

\sigma^{-1}(k)
relative to
\sigma^{-1}(i)
and
\sigma^{-1}(j)
.
If
k
is outside the interval
[i,\,j]
and
\sigma^{-1}(k)
is outside the interval
[\sigma^{-1}(i),\,\sigma^{-1}(j)]
then the products of
S
-factors for
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are clearly the same. There are five remaining cases, with the results displayed in the table below. The first column gives the position of
k
relative to
i
and
j
, the second column gives the position of
\sigma^{-1}(k)
relative to

\sigma^{-1}(i)
and
\sigma^{-1}(j)
, the third column gives the product of
S
-factors involving
k

for
\sigma

) in mind.

\begin{array}[]{llllll}i<k<j&\si(k)<\si(i)&&S(\xi_{k},\xi_{i})&&S(\xi_{k},\xi_{j})\\

We give more detail into the derivation of the approximations (

B.1
Approximation of

We give more details for the approximation (

). Take the definition of the function

and

\begin{split}&\frac{\prod_{j\in J_{1},k\in K_{1}}(\xi_{j}+\zeta_{k}-2\Delta\xi_{j}\zeta_{k})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<k\\

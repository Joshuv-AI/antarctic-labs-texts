Self-adaptive-type CQ algorithms for split equality problems
Songxiao Li 1, Bing Tan 1, Zheng Zhou 1,∗

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is concerned with the approximate solution of split equality problems. We introduce two types of algorithms and a new self-adaptive stepsize without prior knowledge of operator norms. The corresponding strong convergence theorems are obtained under mild conditions. Finally, some numerical experiments demonstrate the efficiency of our results and compare them with the existing results.

Introduction

As an extension of the split feasibility problem (for short, SFP), in 2013, Moudafi
moudafi2013relaxed
;
moudafi2014alternating
introduced the following split equality problem (for short, SEP) that is applied to decomposition for PDEs
attouch2008alternating
and intensity-modulated radiation therapy
censor2006unified
. Let
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
and
H_{3}
be Hilbert spaces and
C\subset H_{1}
,
Q\subset H_{2}
be nonempty closed convex subsets. Let
A:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{3}
and
B:H_{2}\rightarrow H_{3}
be bounded linear operators. The split equality problem is to find

x^{*}\in C,y^{*}\in Q\ {\text{ such\ that}}\ \ Ax^{*}=By^{*}.

(1.1)

In particular, when
B=I
and
H_{2}=H_{3}
, the SEP can be considered as well-known the split feasibility problem (Censor and Elfving introduced in
s2
), which is to find
x^{*}\in C
such that
Ax^{*}\in Q
. Naturally,
x^{*}
is a solution of the split feasibility problem if and only if
x^{*}
is a solution of the equation
x^{*}=P_{C}(I-\gamma A^{*}(I-P_{Q})A)x^{*}
, where
P_{C}:H_{1}\rightarrow C
and
P_{Q}:H_{2}\rightarrow Q
are metric projection operators,
A^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
A
. By virtue of the fixed point algorithm, Byrne
s1
came up with CQ algorithm to approximate a solution of the split feasibility problem by the recursive procedure
x_{n+1}=P_{C}(I-\gamma A^{\mathrm{T}}(I-P_{Q})A)x_{n}
, where
A^{\mathrm{T}}
is the matrix transposition of
A
and
\gamma\in(0,2/L)
with
L
the largest eigenvalue of matrix
A^{\mathrm{T}}A
. Subsequently, Wang
wang2017new
, Yao, Liou and Postolache
yao2018self
studied the following new iterative algorithm

x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}[(I-P_{C})+A^{*}(I-P_{Q})A)]x_{n},\ \forall n\geq 0,

(1.2)

where
\{\gamma_{n}\}
is a self-adaptive stepsize sequence without prior knowledge of
operator norms. It is worth noting that only the weak convergence of split feasibility problem were obtained by Byrne’s algorithm and the algorithm (
1.2
). To fill this gap, Halpern algorithms and viscosity algorithms were studied by many authors and were applied to various aspects, such as fixed point problems
qin2009convergence
;
qin2008strong
;
syoaml2011
;
halpern1967fixed
;
luo2020
;
tan2020strong
, variational inequality problems
ansariaa2020
;
Chang209for
;
choacta2012
;
chojia2013
;
kraikaew2014strong
;
takahahsifpt2018
, equilibrium problems
takahashi2007viscosity
, split feasibility problems
sahuo
;
xu2006variable
, and so on. Based on the idea of Halpern algorithm (Halpern introduced in
halpern1967fixed
), Xu
xu2006variable
proposed the following modified algorithm and obtained strong convergence of the split feasibility problem

x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}u+(1-\alpha_{n})P_{C}(I-\gamma A^{*}(I-P_{Q})A)x_{n},

(1.3)

where
\gamma
is a constant in
(0,{2}/{\|A\|^{2}})
and
u
is a fixed point. Further, Takahashi
takahashi2017mann
;
takahashi2018weak
proposed a modified Halpern algorithm, which uses a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
converges strongly to
u
, and obtained the corresponding strong convergence theorems. The viscosity algorithm was introduced by Moudafi
moudafi2000viscosity
in 2000, which uses a contraction mapping to approximate a solution of the fixed point problem.

On the other hand, to approximate the solutions of SEP, Moudafi
moudafi2014alternating
presented the following alternating CQ algorithm (for short, ACQA)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{x_{n+1}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\gamma_{n}A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n}))},\\
&{y_{n+1}=P_{Q}(y_{n}+\gamma_{n}B^{*}(Ax_{n+1}-By_{n})),}\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

where
\{\gamma_{n}\}
is a sequence in
(\varepsilon,\min\{\frac{1}{\|A\|^{2}},\frac{1}{\|B\|^{2}}\}-\varepsilon)
(
\varepsilon
is a small enough nonnegative real number). Further, Byrne and Moudafi
byrne2012extensions
came up with the following simultaneous CQ algorithm (for short, SCQA) to solve SEP, for
\varepsilon<\gamma_{n}<\frac{2}{\|A\|^{2}+\|B\|^{2}}-\varepsilon
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{x_{n+1}=P_{C}(x_{n}-\gamma_{n}A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n}))},\\
&{y_{n+1}=P_{Q}(y_{n}+\gamma_{n}B^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})).}\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

Due to expensive calculation of the projection operators
P_{C}
and
P_{Q}
, Moudafi considered level sets to solve SEP, in which the level set of convex function is easy to implement, that is,
C
and
Q
are replaced with level sets of convex and subdifferentiable functions
\mathfrak{f}:H_{1}\rightarrow R
and
\mathfrak{g}:H_{2}\rightarrow R
, respectively, i.e.,

C=\{x\in H_{1}:\mathfrak{f}(x)\leq 0\}
, and
Q=\{y\in H_{2}:\mathfrak{g}(y)\leq 0\}
.
In this situation, Moudafi
moudafi2013relaxed
put forward the relaxed alternating CQ algorithm (for short, RACQA)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{x_{n+1}=P_{C_{n}}(x_{n}-\gamma A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n}))},\\
&{y_{n+1}=P_{Q_{n}}(y_{n}+\gamma B^{*}(Ax_{n+1}-By_{n})),}\end{aligned}\right.

where
\gamma
is a constant in
(0,\min\{\frac{1}{\|A\|^{2}},\frac{1}{\|B\|^{2}}\})
,

C_{n}=\{x\in H_{1}|\mathfrak{f}(x_{n})+\langle\xi_{n},x-x_{n}\rangle\leq 0\},\ \xi_{n}\in\partial\mathfrak{f}(x_{n})
,
and
Q_{n}=\{y\in H_{2}|\mathfrak{g}(y_{n})+\langle\eta_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle\leq 0\},\ \eta_{n}\in\partial\mathfrak{g}(y_{n})
.
Especially, ACQA, SCQA and RACQA only got weak convergence properties of the split equality problem. Inspiration and motivation through these work in Moudafi
moudafi2013relaxed
;
moudafi2014alternating
;
moudafi2000viscosity
and Takahashi
takahashi2017mann
;
takahashi2018weak
, we introduce two modified self-adaptive-type iterative algorithms to solve the split equality problem (
1.1
) in infinite Hilbert spaces by the Halpern algorithm and the viscosity algorithm. The corresponding strong convergence theorems are obtained without prior knowledge of operator norms. Furthermore, some numerical experiments are used to demonstrate and show the efficiency of our main results.

The present is built up as follows. Some basic properties and relevant lemmas will be introduced in Section
2
, which will be used in the proof for the convergence of the proposed algorithms. The main results and some corollaries of this paper are contained in Sections
3
and
4
. The last section, in Section
5
, some numerical experiments demonstrate the efficiency of our results and compare them with the existing alternating CQ algorithm (ACQA) in Moudafi
moudafi2014alternating
, simultaneous CQ algorithm (SCQA) in Byrne and Moudafi
byrne2012extensions
, Dong, He and Zhao algorithm in
dong2015solving
.

Preliminaries

For the convenience and standard in the rest of this article, we use the notations
\rightarrow
and
\rightharpoonup
to represent strong convergence and weak convergence, respectively. The fixed point set of the mapping
T
is represented by
F(T)
. Some well-known basic properties are as follows:

(P1)

P_{C}
is denoted metric projection from
H
onto
C
, that is,
P_{C}x=\operatorname{argmin}_{y\in C}\|x-y\|,\ \forall x\in H
. It has such an equivalent form
\langle P_{C}x-x,P_{C}x-y\rangle\leq 0,\forall y\in C
, and can also be converted to
\|y-P_{C}x\|^{2}+\|x-P_{C}x\|^{2}\leq\|x-y\|^{2}
;

(P2)

The mapping
T:H\rightarrow H
with
F(T)\neq\emptyset
and
I-T
is demiclosed at 0, i.e., for any sequence
\{x_{n}\}\in H
, if
\{x_{n}\}
weakly converges to
x
and
(I-T)x_{n}
strongly converges to
0
, then
x\in F(T)
;

(P3)

The mapping
T:H\rightarrow H
is a contraction with constant
\lambda
, that is,

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\lambda\|x-y\|,\ \forall x,y\in H,\ \lambda\in[0,1);

(P4)

\partial\mathfrak{f}
is denoted the subdifferential of convex function
\mathfrak{f}:H\rightarrow R
at
x
, that is,

\partial\mathfrak{f}(x)=\{\varpi:\mathfrak{f}(y)\geq\mathfrak{f}(x)+\langle\varpi,y-x\rangle,\ \forall y\in H\}.

(P5)

For any
x,y\in H
, the following properties hold

\|x+y\|^{2}\leq\|x\|^{2}+2\langle y,x+y\rangle;

\|\kappa x+(1-\kappa)y\|^{2}=\kappa\|x\|^{2}+(1-\kappa)\|y\|^{2}-\kappa(1-\kappa)\|x-y\|^{2},\ \forall\kappa\in R.

Lemma 1

Let the solution set of the split equality problem (
1.1
) is nonempty. For any
\gamma>0
, a solution of SEP is equivalent to a solution of the following equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &x=x-\gamma\left((I-P_{C})x+A^{*}(Ax-By)\right),\\
&y=y-\gamma\left((I-P_{Q})y-B^{*}(Ax-By)\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Proof

Obviously, any solution of the problem (
1.1
) is the solution of equations (
2.1
). On the other hand, put any element
(x,y)
in the solution set of equations (
2.1
), we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &0=(I-P_{C})x+A^{*}(Ax-By),\\
&0=(I-P_{Q})y-B^{*}(Ax-By).\end{aligned}\right.

For any
(x^{*},y^{*})
in the solution set of SEP, that is,
x^{*}\in C
,
y^{*}\in Q
and
Ax^{*}=By^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle(I-P_{C})x+A^{*}(Ax-By),x-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x-P_{C}x,x-P_{C}x\rangle+\langle x-P_{C}x,P_{C}x-x^{*}\rangle+\langle Ax-By,Ax-Ax^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\|x-P_{C}x\|^{2}+\langle Ax-By,Ax-Ax^{*}\rangle,

and

\displaystyle 0=\langle(I-P_{Q})y-B^{*}(Ax-By),y-y^{*}\rangle\geq\|y-P_{Q}y\|^{2}-\langle Ax-By,By-By^{*}\rangle.

Combine the above two formulas we get
0\geq\|x-P_{C}x\|^{2}+\|y-P_{Q}y\|^{2}+\|Ax-By\|^{2}
. This implies that
x\in C
,
y\in Q
and
Ax=By
, i.e.,
(x,y)
is a solution of the split equality problems (
1.1
).

1.1

1.1

\Omega=\{(x^{*},y^{*})\in C\times Q|Ax^{*}=By^{*}\}\neq\emptyset

(A1)

H_{1}
,
H_{2}
and
H_{3}
are Hilbert spaces, and
C\subset H_{1}
,
Q\subset H_{2}
are nonempty closed convex subsets;

(A2)

A:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{3}
,
B:H_{2}\rightarrow H_{3}
are bounded linear operators,
A^{*}
and
B^{*}
are the adjoint operators of
A
and
B

(A3)

\{u_{n}\}\subset H_{1}
,
\{v_{n}\}\subset H_{2}

u_{n}\rightarrow u
and
v_{n}\rightarrow v

3.1
Self-adaptive Halpern-type CQ algorithm (SHCQA)

Through the aforementioned Halpern-type algorithm in
takahashi2017mann
;
takahashi2018weak
, the iterative sequence
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}
of the split equality problem (
1.1
) is generated by the following recursive procedure

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\widehat{x}_{n}=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}\left[(I-P_{C})x_{n}+A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\right],}\\
&{x_{n+1}=\delta_{n}u_{n}+(1-\delta_{n})\widehat{x}_{n},}\\
&{\widehat{y}_{n}=y_{n}-\gamma_{n}\left[(I-P_{Q})y_{n}-B^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\right],}\\
&{y_{n+1}=\delta_{n}v_{n}+(1-\delta_{n})\widehat{y}_{n},n\geq 0.}\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

The corresponding parameters satisfy the following restrictions

(R1)

If
Ax_{n}\neq By_{n}
, the self-adaptive stepsize

\gamma_{n}=\alpha_{n}\min\left\{1,\frac{\|Ax_{n}-By_{n}\|^{2}}{\|A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\|^{2}+\|B^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\|^{2}}\right\}\ {\text{with}}\ \alpha_{n}\in(0,1).

Otherwise, the self-adaptive stepsize
\gamma_{n}=\alpha_{n}
;

(R2)

\{\delta_{n}\}
is real number sequence in
(0,1)
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{n}=0
and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}=\infty
.

Theorem 3.1

Given the assumptions (A1)-(A3) and conditions (R1)-(R2). The iterative sequence
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}
generated by algorithm (
3.1
) converges strongly to
P_{\Omega}(u,v)\in\Omega

Proof

Take
(x^{*},y^{*})=P_{\Omega}(u,v)\in\Omega
, that is,
x^{*}\in C
,
y^{*}\in Q
and
Ax^{*}=By^{*}

3.1

\displaystyle\|\widehat{x}_{n}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma_{n}\langle(I-P_{C})x_{n}+A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n}),x_{n}-x^{*}\rangle

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{n}^{2}\|(I-P_{C})x_{n}+A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma_{n}\|(I-P_{C})x_{n}\|^{2}-2\gamma_{n}\langle Ax_{n}-By_{n},Ax_{n}-Ax^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\gamma_{n}^{2}\left(\|(I-P_{C})x_{n}\|^{2}+\|A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\|^{2}\right).

Similarly, the following inequality is available

\displaystyle\|\widehat{y}_{n}-y^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{n}-y^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma_{n}\|(I-P_{Q})y_{n}\|^{2}+2\gamma_{n}\langle Ax_{n}-By_{n},By_{n}-By^{*}\rangle

(3.3)

\displaystyle+2\gamma_{n}^{2}\left(\|(I-P_{Q})y_{n}\|+\|B^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\|^{2}\right).

On the other hand, we get

2\langle Ax_{n}-By_{n},By_{n}-By^{*}\rangle-2\langle Ax_{n}-By_{n},Ax_{n}-Ax^{*}\rangle=-2\|Ax_{n}-By_{n}\|^{2}.

From the definition of
\gamma_{n}

\gamma_{n}(1-\gamma_{n})>0

\displaystyle\|\widehat{x}_{n}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\|\widehat{y}_{n}-y^{*}\|^{2}

\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\ \langle f(x^{*})-x^{*},x_{n_{k}}-x^{*}\rangle=\underset{j\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\langle f(x^{*})-x^{*},x_{n_{k_{j}}}-x^{*}\rangle=\langle f(x^{*})-x^{*},\bar{x}-x^{*}\rangle\leq 0,

\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\ \langle g(y^{*})-y^{*},y_{n_{k}}-y^{*}\rangle=\underset{j\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\langle g(y^{*})-y^{*},y_{n_{k_{j}}}-y^{*}\rangle=\langle g(y^{*})-y^{*},\bar{y}-y^{*}\rangle\leq 0.

According to condition (E1) and (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{x}_{n_{k}}-x_{n_{k}}\|\leq\gamma_{n}(\|(I-P_{C})x_{{n_{k}}}\|+\|A\|\|Ax_{{n_{k}}}-By_{{n_{k}}}\|)\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty,

\|\widehat{y}_{n_{k}}-y_{n_{k}}\|\leq\gamma_{n}(\|(I-P_{Q})y_{{n_{k}}}\|+\|B\|\|Ax_{{n_{k}}}-By_{{n_{k}}}\|)\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty.

Further, we get

\|x_{{n_{k}}+1}-x_{n_{k}}\|=\delta_{n_{k}}\|f(x_{n_{k}})-x_{n_{k}}\|+(1-\delta_{n_{k}})\|\widehat{x}_{n_{k}}-x_{n_{k}}\|\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty,

(4.6)

\|y_{{n_{k}}+1}-y_{n_{k}}\|=\delta_{n_{k}}\|g(y_{n_{k}})-y_{n_{k}}\|+(1-\delta_{n_{k}})\|\widehat{y}_{n_{k}}-y_{n_{k}}\|\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty.

(4.7)

Hence, we have
\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\ \langle f(x^{*})-x^{*},x_{n_{k}+1}-x^{*}\rangle\leq 0
and
\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\ \langle g(y^{*})-y^{*},y_{n_{k}+1}-y^{*}\rangle\leq 0
, which implies
\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\ \tau_{n_{k}}\leq 0
. By Lemma
2
, we obtain
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\theta_{n}=0
. This implies that
(x_{n},y_{n})\rightarrow(x^{*},y^{*})
.∎

4.2

In this section, we first set the same
C
,
Q
,
C_{n}
and
Q_{n}
as in Theorem
3.2
. In addition, the combination of the relaxed CQ algorithm and the viscosity-type algorithm has the following algorithm

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\widehat{x}_{n}=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}\left[(I-P_{C_{n}})x_{n}+A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\right],}\\
&{x_{n+1}=\delta_{n}f(\widehat{x}_{n})+(1-\delta_{n})\widehat{x}_{n},}\\
&{\widehat{y}_{n}=y_{n}-\gamma_{n}\left[(I-P_{Q_{n}})y_{n}-B^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\right],}\\
&{y_{n+1}=\delta_{n}g(\widehat{y}_{n})+(1-\delta_{n})\widehat{y}_{n},n\geq 0.}\end{aligned}\right.

(4.8)

The corresponding parameters satisfy the following restrictions

(E3)

If
Ax_{n}\neq By_{n}
, the self-adaptive stepsize

\gamma_{n}=\alpha_{n}\min\left\{1,\frac{\|Ax_{n}-By_{n}\|^{2}}{\|A^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\|^{2}+\|B^{*}(Ax_{n}-By_{n})\|^{2}}\right\}\ \text{with}\ \alpha_{n}\in(0,1).

Otherwise, the self-adaptive stepsize
\gamma_{n}=\alpha_{n}
;

(E4)

\{\delta_{n}\}
is real number sequence in
(0,1)
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{n}=0
and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}=\infty
.

6
Conclusion

The first conclusion from Sections
3
and
4
is that we propose four self-adaptive CQ algorithms by using the methods of the Halpern algorithm and the viscosity algorithm for solving the SEP under the condition of the self-adaptive stepsize sequence. A point should be stressed is that such a self-adaptive stepsize sequence does not depend on the prior knowledge of operator norms. The second conclusion from the numerical results in Section
5
is that the convergence of our algorithm is validity and authenticity. Meanwhile, our proposed self-adaptive viscosity-type CQ algorithm (
4.1
) (SVCQA) improves and extends the existing results.

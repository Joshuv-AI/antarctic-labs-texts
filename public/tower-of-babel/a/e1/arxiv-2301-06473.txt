Abstract
. We investigate the structure of transversely Kähler foliations with quasi-negative tranverse Ricci curvature. In particular, we prove a de Rham type decomposition theorem on the leaf space where we characterize each factor. This can be seen as a foliated analog of Nadel and Frankel’s uniformization theorem for canonically polarized manifold. This is also related to the works of the second named author on codimension one singular foliations with transverse hyperbolic structure. Further properties are established when the ambient manifold is compact Kähler and the foliation is holomorphic.

Introduction

Preliminary warning

Unless otherwise stated, the objects considered here such as manifolds, foliations, functions, tensors
\dots
are supposed to be smooth. For the sake of notational simplicity, we will denote by the same symbol (typically
\mathcal{F}
) a foliation/distribution and its tangent bundle.

1.1.
Statement of the main results

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a transversely Kähler foliation of complex codimension
n
on
X
a compact (differentiable) manifold; it is worth mentioning here that
X
is not necessarily endowed with a complex structure. We denote by
J_{\mathcal{F}}
the holomorphic transverse structure (see §
3.3
) and by
\overline{g}
the transverse Kähler metric. In some holomorphic coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
parameterizing the local space of leaves, it reads

\overline{g}=\sum_{i,{j}}g_{i\bar{j}}dz_{i}d\bar{z_{j}}

where
g_{i\bar{j}}
depends only of the transverse variables
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
. The foliation is thus equipped with two basic closed
(1,1)
forms. Namely the fundamental form
\omega
of
\overline{g}
and the (transverse) Ricci form
\gamma=\mathrm{Ric}(\overline{g})
respectively defined in the previous local transverse coordinates as

\omega=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i,j}g_{i\bar{j}}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z_{j}}

and

\gamma=\mathrm{Ric}(\overline{g})=-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log\left(\frac{\omega^{n}}{{|dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}|}^{2}}\right)=-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log\left(\textrm{det}(g_{i\bar{j}})\right).

In this paper, we will make use of the following assumptions.

(A1)

The tranverse Ricci form
\mathrm{Ric}(\overline{g})
is
quasi-negative
:
\mathrm{Ric}(\overline{g})\leq 0
and
\mathrm{Ric}(\overline{g})<0
in the transverse direction (
i.e.
has maximal rank
n
) somewhere.

(A2)

\mathcal{F}
is
homologically orientable
,
i.e.
the top basic cohomology group
H^{2n}(X/\mathcal{F})
is non-trivial, and then generated over
\mathbb{R}
by the class of the transverse volume
\omega^{n}
(see Theorem
3.3
).

Motivated by Nadel/Frankel’s uniformization results (see the paragraph below Theorem
C
) and by the codimension one situation (see §
1.4
), we can then prove the following statement.

Theorem A
.

Let us assume that the pair
(X,\mathcal{F})
satisfies
(A1)
and
(A2)
above.

(1)

There exist on
X
two regular foliations
\mathcal{G}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
containing
\mathcal{F}
and such that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{G}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

(2)

The foliations
\mathcal{G}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
are holomorphic with respect to the complex structure
J_{\mathcal{F}}
on the normal bundle
N\mathcal{F}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}/\mathcal{F}
are orthogonal and parallel with respect to
\overline{g}
(as subbundles of
N\mathcal{F}
).

(3)

The leaves of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
are the topological closure of the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, they are closed. The leaf space
X/\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is a compact Kähler orbifold with quasi-negative Ricci curvature, in particular of general type.

We denote by
\widetilde{\bullet}
the lift on the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
of any object previously defined. To get a more in-depth description of the foliations
\mathcal{G}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
we first formulate the following result.

Theorem B
.

The leaves of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
are closed and the leaf space
\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is a complete Kähler orbifold (with respect to the metric
\tilde{\overline{g}}
).

Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
be the foliations induced on
\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}
. Note that they induce an infinitesimal splitting
(in the orbifold category):

T(\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})=\mathcal{G}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{G}_{2}.

We have then the following geometric description of these foliations.

Theorem C
.

(1)

The leaves of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are all isometric to a Hermitian symmetric space
\mathscr{H}
of non-compact type.

(2)

The leaves of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
are all isometric to a Kähler complete orbifold
\mathscr{K}
with quasi-negative Ricci curvature.

(3)

The aforementioned infinitesimal splitting gives rise to a global decomposition (de Rham decomposition)

\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\mathscr{K}\times\mathscr{H}.

(4)

Let us consider the natural diagonal and isometric action of
\pi_{1}(X)
(with respect to the decomposition above). Then this action is minimal (
i.e.
dense) on the second factor and discrete cocompact on the first factor
\mathscr{K}
.

The most interesting situation occurs when the Hermitian symmetric factor, equivalently the bounded symmetric domain
\mathscr{H}
is not reduced to a point in the decomposition of
\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
. Let
\Isom(\mathscr{H})
be the Lie group of isometries of
\mathscr{H}
with respect to its Bergman metric. The elements of the identity component
G=\Isom^{0}(\mathscr{H})
, a semi-simple real algebraic group without compact factors, are holomorphic transformation maps of
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{H}
is identified with the coset space
G/K
where
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. The leaves of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
coincide with the fibers of a submersion
f:\widetilde{X}\to\mathscr{H}
. Possibly after replacing
X
by a finite étale cover,
f
is equivariant with respect to some representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to G
with dense image and which is in addition transversely holomorphic (see §
3.4
, as well as Theorems
6.4
and
6.5
). Actually, as it will be highlighted in the sequel, this map
f
is the unique
\rho
-equivariant map with respect to (the lift of) some suitable bundle-like metric on
X
.

These statements shall be seen as foliated analogues of results obtained by Nadel
[
Nad90
]
and Frankel
[
Fra95
]
. Both authors have studied the geometry of the universal covering
\widetilde{X}
of
X
a complex projective manifold with ample canonical bundle.

Let us recall that it is equivalent to saying that
X
admits a Kähler–Einstein metric with negative Ricci curvature.
Nadel proved that
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(\widetilde{X})
is a semi-simple Lie group having no compact factors and he conjectured the existence of a splitting
\widetilde{X}\simeq\mathscr{K}\times\mathscr{H}
with
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(\mathscr{K})=\{\mathrm{Id}\}

and then in full generality

1.2.
Examples

To illustrate the above-mentioned statements, we give examples with increasing complexity.

(a)

The most basic construction consists in considering the foliation defined by a submersion
X\to M
with connected fibers of a compact manifold onto a compact Kähler manifold
(M,g_{M})
with quasi-negative Ricci curvature. Note that
M
is of the general type (
i.e.
, the canonical bundle
K_{M}
is big) by virtue of Riemenschneider’s Theorem
[
Rie73
]
.

(b)

Another family of examples is given by the so-called suspension process. Let
N
be a compact manifold,
\widetilde{N}
its universal cover and let us consider a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(N)\to\textrm{Aut}(M)
of the fundamental group of
N
into the holomorphic transformation group of
M
with quasi-negative Ricci curvature as above. Recall that the latter group is finite. Up to averaging
g_{M}
, one can suppose that
M
is equipped with a
\textrm{Aut}(M)
-invariant Kähler metric. Let
X
be the quotient manifold
(\tilde{N}\times M)/\pi_{1}(N)
defined by the natural diagonal action of
\pi_{1}(N)
. This latter has a structure of fiber bundle
M\rightarrow X\rightarrow N
over
N
and carries a natural foliation
\mathcal{F}_{H}
transverse to the fibers, namely the projection of the horizontal foliation on
N\times M
. This foliation has the sough properties but this construction does not gives rise to an interesting example from the dynamical viewpoint: the leaf space
X/\mathcal{F}_{H}
identifies with the Kähler orbifold
M/\textrm{Im}\ \rho
.

(c)

Now, suppose given on a compact manifold
N
a
minimal foliation
(
i.e.
with dense leaves)
\mathcal{F}_{N}
whose transverse geometry is locally modeled on a Hermitian symmetric space of non-compact type
\mathscr{H}=G/K
. The foliation carries a natural transverse invariant metric
\overline{g_{N}}
induced by the
G
-invariant Kähler–Einstein metric on
\mathscr{H}
. Let
(M,g_{M})
be a compact Kähler manifold with quasi-negative Ricci curvature. The product
X=M\times N
is equipped with an induced foliation
\mathcal{F}
of the same rank which restricts to each vertical fiber
\{m\}\times N\simeq N
to
\mathcal{F}_{N}
. This foliation is transversely Kähler with respect to the transverse metric
g_{M}\oplus\overline{g_{N}}
and satisfies moreover hypothesis
(A1)
and
(A2)
. Here, the leaves closure are precisely the vertical fibers. Moreover, if we fix a Hermitian symmetric space of non-compact type
\mathscr{H}
, it is possible to exhibit a compact foliated manifold
(N,\mathcal{F}_{N})
as above. Indeed, let
\mathscr{H}={\mathscr{H}}_{1}\times\cdots\times{\mathscr{H}}_{p}
be the decomposition of
\mathscr{H}
into irreducible symmetric factors. It is well known since Borel’s work
[
Bor63
]
(see also
[
Mar91
, Section IX.4.7, Theorem C]
and
[
Mor15
, Corollary 18.7.4]
) that for every
i
, there exists a discrete torsion free subgroup of holomorphic isometries
\Gamma_{i}
of

\mathscr{H}_{i}\times\mathscr{H}_{i}
acting cocompactly and diagonally and such that
\Gamma_{i}
acts densely on each factor. It is then sufficient to consider the projective manifold
N=(\mathscr{H}\times\mathscr{H})/\prod_{i}\Gamma_{i}
and to take as

\mathcal{F}_{N}
the holomorphic foliation which lift to the horizontal (or vertical) one on the universal cover
\mathscr{H}\times\mathscr{H}
. One can also mix with the suspension construction by considering the foliation
\mathcal{F}
lying on
{N\times M}
obtained as the intersection
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{H}\cap{\pi^{*}}\mathcal{F}_{N}
. With the notations of Theorem
A
,
\mathcal{F}_{H}
and
{\pi^{*}}\mathcal{F}_{N}
correspond respectively to
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{G}
.

Apart this use of irreducible uniform lattices, we are not aware of other examples of somewhat different nature. In this setting, it is worth mentionning that, under special circumstances, Zimmer has shown that the “holonomy group” of a minimal Riemannian foliation on a compact manifold with non-compact semi-simple structural Lie algebra is of “arithmetic nature”
[
Zim88
]
.
As pointed out in the following subsection, when the ambient manifold is algebraic/Kähler, we have at our disposal a powerful machinery that enable to connect the representation
\rho
(see te paragraph after Theorem
C
) to arithmetic lattices in semi-simple Lie group.

1.3.
The case where
X
is Kähler

When
X
is a compact complex manifold and
\mathcal{F}
is a holomorphic foliation satisfying the conditions
(A1)
(with respect to the natural holomorphic transverse structure) and
(A2)
above. The second item of Theorem
A
implies that
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are also holomorphic (the tranverse complex structure being the one induced by the complex structure of the ambient manifold
X
). Moreover, if
X
is Kähler, the homological orientability is automatically fullfilled. In this case, the
\rho
-equivariant map
f
is holomorphic. Examples are provided by foliations defined by a subline bundle
L\subset\Omega_{X}^{p}
such that
c_{1}(L)
is semi-positive with the maximal possible rank
=p
everywhere, see Proposition
9.5
.

Assume now for simplicity that
X
is projective and that
G=H_{\mathbb{R}}
consists of the real points of a simple algebraic group
H
.
As
f
has maximal rank, the representation
\rho
tends to be rigid in as an element of
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),H_{\mathbb{C}})
. The foliation
\mathcal{G}_{1}
is defined as the kernel of the differential
d^{1,0}f
, which can be regarded as the Higgs field attached to the representation
\pi_{1}(X)\to G_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{Aut}^{0}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
The minimality of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
prevents from the existence of a morphism
\varphi:X\to Y
,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(Y)\leq\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}{H}
through which
\rho
factors. With this at hands, together with similar results valid in the context of Zariski dense representations of the fundamental group

to
p
-adic simple group, we can infer that the local system defined by
\rho
is a direct factor of some
\mathbb{Z}
-variation of Hodge structures. In particular, thanks to the metric properties of period domains, this enables to show that

cannot support a Zariski dense entire curve, see §

.

Theorem D
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated compact Kähler manifold (
\mathcal{F}
being holomorphic). Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is transversely Kähler with quasi-negative Ricci curvature and that
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Then every entire curve on

is contained in a proper analytic subset of
X
.

1.5.
The case where
\mathcal{F}
has real dimension one

Let
X
a
n
-dimensional compact manifold equipped with a 1-dimensional Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
transversely modelled on
(M,\Isom(M))
where
M
is a simply connected complete Riemannian manifold together with its group
\Isom(M)
of isometries. Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\Isom(M)
the holonomy representation and denote by
H_{0}
the identity component of the closure of the image of
\rho
. It is easy to see that the following assertions are equivalent:

•

Every leaf of
\mathcal{F}
is closed.

•

The group
H_{0}
is trivial.

According to a result of Thurston when
M={\mathbb{H}}^{n-1}
and generalized afterwards by Carrière
[
Mol88
, Appendix A, Theorem 1.1]
, the group
H_{0}
is
abelian
. This implies that, if
\mathcal{F}
satisfies properties
(A1)
and
(A2)
, it cannot fit into this category unless
H_{0}
is trivial. Indeed, suppose by contradiction that
\mathcal{F}
possesses a non compact leaf
\mathcal{L}
. Then, according to Theorems
A
,
B
, and
C
, the topological closure
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
is a submanifold of
M
and the restriction
{\mathcal{F}}_{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}
is transversely modelled on a symmetric space
\mathscr{H}
. Moreover, the group
H_{0}
attached in this case to the representation
\pi_{1}(\bar{\mathcal{L}})\to\Isom(\mathscr{H})
is nothing but the identity component of the target, a semi-simple Lie group.

Important examples of 1-dimensional transversely Kähler foliations are provided by the orbits of the so-called Reeb vector field on Sasakian manifold. These foliations are known to satisfy property
(A2)
(this is a general feature for foliations defined by a killing vector field) so that the leaves are automatically closed whenever they satisfy
(A1)
. On the opposite side, Epstein has given in
[
Eps84
]
examples of
1
-dimensional transversely hyperbolic foliation on closed 3-dimensional manifolds
M
such that the leaf closure is 2-dimensional torus and the holonomy representation
\pi_{1}(M)\to\Isom(\mathbb{H}^{2})
takes values in the affine subgroup. In fact, these foliations are not transversely homologically orientable and their dynamical and arithmetic behavior present some strong similarities with that of holomorphic foliations described in item (
4
) of the following subsection.

1.6.
Remarks and counterexamples

We give now some basic examples of transversely Kähler foliations which exhibit different behaviors when the assumptions
(A1)
or
(A2)
are dropped.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Riemannian foliation on a compact manifold
X
. In general the leaves of the lifted foliation on the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
are not necessarily closed.

(2)

The conclusions of Theorem
A
do not necessarily hold if we only require the Ricci form to be only semi-negative. Actually, it may happen that the topological closure of the leaves of a linear foliation on a complex torus are real hypersurfaces. In this setting, the natural transverse metric
\overline{g}
is flat and the Ricci form
\gamma
vanishes identically.

(3)

For general transversely Kähler holomorphic foliations on compact Kähler manifolds, the dimension of the topological closure of the leaves is likely to vary. A simple instance of this phenomenon is the Riccati foliation constructed on a ruled surface
S
over a curve
C
of genus
g\geq 1
by the datum of a dense representation

\pi_{1}(C)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\subset\textrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}).

In this situation, there exists exactly two closed leaves and the closure of the other leaves are Levi-Flat hypersurfaces.The natural transverse metric is induced by the Fubiny-study metric on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and thus coincides with its Ricci form. We don’t know if this equidimensionality defect can occur when the Ricci form is semi-negative (but not quasi-negative).

(4)

We cannot drop the homological orientability assumption, even if
X
is complex and
\mathcal{F}
is holomorphic.

In order to justify this assertion, let us consider the examples of non-Kähler compact complex manifolds associated to number fields as constructed in
[
OT05
]
generalizing some examples of Inoue surfaces
[
Ino74
]
. We retain the presentation given in
[
OT05
]
and we refer to
loc.cit.
for details. This example has been previously considered in
[
LBPRT22
, §5.5]
in relation with the study of the transverse action of the automorphisms group of a foliation (see the next subsection).

Let
K
be a number field, let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{s}
be its real embeddings and
\sigma_{s+1},\dots,\sigma_{s+2t}
its complex embeddings (
\sigma_{s+t+i}=\overline{\sigma_{s+i}}
). Let us assume that
s,t>0
. Let
\mathbb{H}
be the Poincaré upper half-plane. Let
a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
acting on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
as a translation by the vector
(\sigma_{1}(a),\dots,\sigma_{s+t}(a))
. Let
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}
be a totally positive unit (
i.e.

\sigma_{i}(u)>0
for all real places). Then
u
acts on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
by
u\cdot(z_{1},\dots,z_{s+t})=(\sigma_{1}(u)z_{1},\dots,\sigma_{s+t}(u)z_{s+t}).
Moreover, as
t>0
, the set
\{(\sigma_{1}(a),\dots,\sigma_{s}(a))\mid\ a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\}
is dense in
{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
. For any subgroup
U
of totally positive units, the semi-direct product
U\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{K}
acts freely on
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
. This subgroup
U
is called admissible if the quotient space
X(K,U)
is a compact complex manifold. In particular, admissible groups must have rank
s
. We can always find such admissible subgroups.

The admissible group
U
being given, the corresponding compact complex manifold support a transversely Hermitian symmetric foliation of complex codimension
s
transversely locally modeled on
\mathbb{H}^{s}
and which lifts to the vertical foliation on the universal cover
\mathbb{H}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
. The leaves closure are thus codimension
s
real submanifolds of
X
, namely
(2t+s)
-dimensional real tori fibering over the real
s
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{s}
. In any cases, these submanifolds fail to be holomorphic.

Here, the representation
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}:\pi_{1}(X(K,U))\to{\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})}^{s}
associated to the transverse hyperbolic structure takes values in the product of affine subgroups
{\Aff(2,\mathbb{R})}^{s}
and its linear part
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}:\pi_{1}(X(K,U))\to({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{s},\times)
has non-trivial image.

The product of Poincaré metrics (defining the transverse hyperbolic metric) on
{\mathbb{H}}^{s}
is given (up to a multiplicative factor) as
\omega=dd^{c}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\log(\operatorname{Im}(z_{i}))\right)
and thus descends to
X
as an exact two form, namely the differential of the
\pi_{1}(X)
-invariant one form
d^{c}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\log(\operatorname{Im}(z_{i}))\right)
. Hence, this foliation is not homologically orientable (albeit satisfying
(A1)
).

1.7.
Transverse action of the group of automorphisms

Consider now a foliated compact complex manifold
(X,\mathcal{F})
and
let
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\mathcal{F})
be the group of biholomorphisms of
X
preserving the foliation
\mathcal{F}
. This group contains the normal subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{Fix}}(X,\mathcal{F})
of biholomorphisms
f
preserving the foliation leafwise. That is,
f\in\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{Fix}}(X,\mathcal{F})
iff for every
x\in X
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{x}={\mathcal{L}}_{f(x)}
where
{\mathcal{L}}_{x}
denotes the leaf through
x
.

Definition 1.1
.

We will say that the transverse action of
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\mathcal{F})
is finite whenever the quotient
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\mathcal{F})/\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{Fix}}(X,\mathcal{F})
is finite.

In other words, the action is transersely finite if the set theoretic action
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\mathcal{F})\times X/\mathcal{F}\to X/\mathcal{F}
on the leaf space
X/\mathcal{F}
is
finite
(
i.e.
has finite image in the symmetric group of
X/\mathcal{F}
).

Assume now that
\mathcal{F}
is transversely Kähler and satisfies the assumption
(A1)
. In the classical (unfoliated) situation, the quasi-negativity of the Ricci curvature implies that
X
is of general type
[
Rie73
]
. As a byproduct, the group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
of biholomorphisms of
X
is nessessarily finite. In our foliated setting, it is therefore natural to inquire whether the transverse action is finite. Actually, we cannot expect it to hold in full generality. This is illustrated by the example given in §
1.6
-(
4
) above. Indeed, retaining the same notations, we know from Dirichlet’s units theorem that
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
is a group of rank
s+t-1
. Elements of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*,+}/U
induce automorphisms of
X(K,U)
. Therefore as soon as
t>1
, we obtain automorphisms with infinite transverse order.

We now turn our attention to the particular case where
X
is itself Kähler. As we can see, the situation is much better.

Theorem E
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated compact Kähler manifold (
\mathcal{F}
being holomorphic). Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is transversely Kähler with quasi-negative Ricci curvature. Then the transverse action of
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\mathcal{F})
is finite.

Remark 1.2
.

We are not aware of any example satisfying
(A1)
and
(A2)
with
X
complex non-Kähler such that the transverse action of
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\mathcal{F})
is infinite.

2.
Outline of the proof

Let us now describe the strategy that we employ to establish the main Theorems
A
and
C
. As already mentioned in the introduction, the techniques are widely inspired by previous results of Nadel
[

On the other hand, it is well known that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\forall u,v\in\Gamma(TX),\quad\nabla_{u}^{g}v-\nabla_{v}^{g}u

\displaystyle=[u,v],

(3.3)

\displaystyle\forall u,v,w\in\Gamma(TX),\quad u\cdot\langle v,w\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{u}^{g}v,w\rangle_{g}+\langle v,\nabla_{u}^{g}w\rangle_{g}.

Let

be a local orthonormal frame of

If

Lemma 3.12

For every

Proof.

Let

4.4.
The transverse frame bundle of a Riemannian foliation

As it was alluded to above, the structure of Riemannian foliations can be understood from the viewpoint of parallelizable ones.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},g)
be a compact Riemannian foliated manifold (see §
3.2
for the definition). As usual, we set
m=\rg(\mathcal{F})
and
m+n=\dim(X)
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}
the foliation constructed as the lift of
\mathcal{F}
on the direct orthonormal transverse frame bundle
X^{\sharp}
(see
[
Mol88
, §2.5]
). Both foliations have the same rank,
\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}
projects onto
\mathcal{F}
via the natural projection map

p^{\sharp}:X^{\sharp}\longrightarrow X

so that the differential
p^{\sharp}_{*}
induces a surjective morphism
\overline{p^{\sharp}_{*}}
between the normal bundles
N{\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}}
and
(p^{\sharp})^{*}N\mathcal{F}
.

The space

is naturally endowed with a structure of
\SO(n)
-principal bundle. As in the classical setting, the transverse Levi-Civita connection associated to
\mathcal{F}
is defined on

by a horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
on

. By construction, the foliation

is tangent to

and
\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}
are invariant under the right action of

. Moreover, the foliated manifold

is equipped with a canonical transverse paralellism. More precisely let us first fix a basis
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
of
\mathfrak{so}(n)
(with

) identified with the Lie algebra of fundamental vector fields (with respect to the action of
\SO(n)
). On the other hand, pick a basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
of

and denote by

the horizontal transverse vector field on
X^{\sharp}
such that for every

, the projection
\overline{p_{*}}(\overline{u_{i}}(z))
has coordinate vector

in the transverse frame

. Let
\overline{\lambda_{i}}
be the projection of
\lambda_{i}
on

. It turns out that the transverse vector fields
\overline{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\lambda_{k}},\overline{u_{1}},\ldots,\overline{u_{n}}

and thus define a transverse parallelism for the lifted foliation
\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}
(
cf.

[
Mol88
, §3.3]
). We can thus apply Theorem

.

Definition 4.12

The structural Lie algebra

4.5.
The semi-simple without compact factors case

We explain in this paragraph how the various structure results for TC foliations (Theorem
4.4
and Lemma
4.11
) can be used when the structural Lie algebra is semi-simple without compact factors. In this setting, we can produce a basic map on the universal cover of the ambient manifold that is equivariant with respect to the monodromy representation and that takes values in a symmetric space.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a TC foliation on a manifold
X
and let us assume that the structural Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{g}\left(X,\mathcal{F}\right)
is semi-simple without compact factors. From Lemma
4.11
, we know that
\mathcal{F}
is developable and the leaves of the foliation
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
on
\widetilde{X}
are thus given by the fibers of a submersion with connected fibers

\pi_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}\colon\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow P:=\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}.

We have a commutative diagram of basic fibrations:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mathcal{F}}}

The map

is a submersion and it has the structure of a principal bundle over
W
. Let us now explain where this structure comes from. The Lie algebra
\ell(X,\mathcal{F})
is naturally a Lie subalgebra of
\Chi(W)
and
\ell(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})
can be identified (through
\pi_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
) with
\Chi(P)
. Using

we can thus consider
\ell(X,\mathcal{F})
as a subalgebra of
\Chi(P)
.

Let
G

(

acting in the fibers of
\alpha
) that fix each element of
\ell(X,\mathcal{F})
(seen as vector fields on

).

Remark 4.13
.

The group
G
is a Lie group that can have infinitely many connected components.

The manifold
P
is then endowed with an action of
G
and it is not hard to see that it makes
\alpha\colon G\to W
a principal
G

By its very definition, it is straightforward to check that
\Lie(G)\simeq\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, its adjoint representation gives rise to a morphism

\mathrm{Ad}_{G}\colon G\longrightarrow G_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}).

The latter group is a real algebraic group and has thus finitely many connected components. We can consider
K\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}
a maximal compact subgroup: the homogeneous space
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}=G_{\mathfrak{g}}/K
is thus a Riemannian symmetric space of the non compact type (
cf.
for instance
[
Hel01
, Chapter VI]
and
[
Bor98
, Chapter IV]
). This is a contractible space (diffeomorphic to a Euclidean space) and if

denotes the subgroup

, the space

is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}

[
Bor98
, Chapter IV, Proposition 4.10, Chapter VII, Theorem 3.7]
.

It is now a classical fact in the theory of fiber bundles: the homogenous space
G/H
being contractible, we can reduce the structure group of
P
to
H
. Equivalently, it means that we can find

\Phi\colon P\longrightarrow G/H

such that

(4.4)

\forall\,x\in P,\ \Phi(x\cdot g)=g^{-1}\Phi(x).

We gather here some useful observations.

(1)

The group
\pi_{1}(X)
of deck transformations of
\widetilde{X}
acts vertically on

with respect to

This morphism maps
\pi_{1}(X)
onto a

. To see this, pick a point

and let

be a connected component of the fiber

. Let

be the component of the identity in

. As

, it is sufficient to prove that
\pi_{1}(X)\cap G^{0}
acts densely on

. But this last point immediately results from the fact that
\mathcal{F}
is minimal (any leaf is dense) in restriction to the fibers of the basic fibration
\pi_{\mathcal{F}}:X\to W
, according to Proposition

.

(2)

Let
\Phi:P\to G/H
be the map constructed above (it depends on the choice of a section of the map

). Let us consider

\Psi=\Phi\circ\pi_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}\colon\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow G/H.

By construction,

is an equivariant locally trivial fibration where equivariant means

\forall\,\gamma\in\pi_{1}(X),\ \forall\,x\in\widetilde{X},\ \Psi(\gamma(x))=\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\gamma)\cdot\Psi(x).

(3)

Conversely any

and satisfying the equivariance property (

) can be obtained in this way. Indeed, let

be the map factorizing

. Then
\Phi
satisfies the equivariance property (
4.4
) for a dense subgroup of

(namely
\pi_{1}(X)

5.
Evolution equation for harmonic maps

The main goal of this section is to prove that the hamonic flow used to produce harmonic maps preserves the property of being basic (with respect to a given Riemannian foliation). To do so, we introduce the notion of tangential energy (§
5.1
below) and study its behavior along the harmonic flow.

5.1.
Tangential energy

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},g)
be a foliated Riemannian manifold and
f:X\to Y
be a smooth function with values in a Riemannian manifold
(Y,h)
. Let us denote by
f_{*}^{T}
the restriction of the differential
f_{*}
to
\mathcal{F}
and let us define the

(with respect to
\mathcal{F}

e_{T}(f):\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}X&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{+}\\
x&\longmapsto&\frac{1}{2}{\|{f}_{*}^{T}\|}_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Trace}\left({(f_{*}^{T})}^{\star}{f_{*}^{T}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}{{|f_{*}(e_{i})|}_{h}^{2}}\end{array}\right.

where
(e_{i})
is any orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{F}_{x}
. Here, the star exponent stands for the adjoint.

Tension field and basic maps

We maintain notation/assumptions form §

{f}^{*}(T{Y})

In more layman terms, once we have fixed a local orthonormal frame
(e_{i})
of
TX
,

(5.1)

\tau(f)=\sum_{i}\nabla_{{e_{i}}}f_{*}{e_{i}}-f_{*}(\sum_{i}{\nabla}_{{e_{i}}}^{{g}}{e_{i}}).

Let us now focus on the case where

(

\mathscr{R}(v,w)=\nabla_{v}\nabla_{w}-\nabla_{w}\nabla_{v}-\nabla_{[v,w]}

for any local foliated vector field

are constant along the leaves and that

is tangent to
\mathcal{F}
, the vector field

Remark that for every local foliated vector field

(e_{i})
is any local orthonormal frame of

.

As noticed before we can restrict the operator

More explicitly, let us consider

Proof.

This is a completely standard application of the maximum principle in the presence of subsolutions of the heat operator

6.
Construction of equivariant foliated harmonic and holomorphic maps

In this section, we put together results from Sections
4
and
5
to prove existence of harmonic or holomorphic maps invariant under some foliations.

6.1.
The Riemaniann case

We first state a general existence result for some TC foliations over a compact base.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold where
X
is compact and
\mathcal{F}
is TC with structural Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{g}\left(X,\mathcal{F}\right)
semi-simple without compact factors. Let us consider
g
a metric on
X
which is bundle-like with respect to
\mathcal{F}
and such that the mean curvature vector of the leaves is foliated,
i.e.

(\mathcal{F},g)
is tense (see Theorem
3.6

\Psi
that is harmonic with respect to the lift
\widetilde{g}
of the metric on
\widetilde{X}
and to the Killing metric on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
(the symmetric space associated with
\mathfrak{g}
).

Proof.

Thanks to the item
2
of Theorem
4.14
, there exists a
basic
(in other words
\mathcal{F}
-invariant)
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
-equivariant map

f_{0}=\Psi:\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}

where
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\Isom(\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is the monodromy of the commuting sheaf whose image is dense in the identity component according to Proposition
4.8
.

We can now apply the existence criterion of twisted harmonic map given in
[
Lab91
]
or
[
Cor88
]
. As the image of
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
does not fix any point on the boundary of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we can apply
[
Lab91
, Théorèmes 0.1 et 0.2]
to infer the existence of a
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
-equivariant
harmonic
map
f_{\infty}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
which is obtained as the limit of a subsequence of
(f_{t_{n}}),\ t_{n}\longmapsto+\infty
where
f_{t}
is a solution of the evolution equation (
5.4
) at time
t
with initial datum
f_{t_{0}}=f_{0}
. Moreover,
f_{\infty}
remains
\mathcal{F}
-invariant according to Corollary
5.5
.

Concerning the uniqueness, we can first assume, up to passing to a finite étale cover of
X
that the representation takes values in
\Isom^{0}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, the neutral component of
\Isom(\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{S}_{p}
the decomposition of
\mathcal{S}
as a Riemannian product of irreducible symmetric spaces. The image of
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
acts diagonally and isometrically with respect to this decomposition. By projection to each factor, we inherit a
\rho_{i}
-equivariant harmonic map
\Psi_{i}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathcal{S}_{i}
where
\rho_{i}:\pi_{1}(X)\to\Isom^{0}(\mathcal{S}_{i})
is the corresponding representation. By density, the image of
\rho_{i}
does not preserve any non-trivial subspace of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}:=\Lie(\Isom^{0}(\mathcal{S}_{i}))
. By virtue of Corlette’s uniqueness Theorem
[
Cor88
, Theorem 3.4]
, the
\Psi_{i}
’s are the only ones
\rho_{i}
-equivariant harmonic maps. In particular
\Psi
is the unique
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
-equivariant harmonic map, as desired.
∎

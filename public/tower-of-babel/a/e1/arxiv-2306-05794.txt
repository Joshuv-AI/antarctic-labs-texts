Abstract
. The aim of this article is to obtain variations on the classical theorems of Schur and Baer on finiteness of commutator subgroups, valid in the contexts of Lie algebras and Leibniz algebras over a field. Using non-abelian tensor products and exterior products, we prove Schur’s Theorem for finitely generated Leibniz algebras, both Schur’s Theorem and Baer’s Theorem for finitely generated Lie algebras, and a version of these theorems for finitely presented Lie algebras.

1.
Introduction

Schur’s Theorem
[
12
,
6
]
states that, for a group
G
and
C\leq Z(G)
, if
G/C
is a finite group, then the derived subgroup
G^{\prime}
is finite. Baer
[
1
]
extended this by proving that, if for a natural number
i
the quotient
G/Z_{i}(G)
is finite, then
\gamma_{i+1}(G)
is finite. This result is commonly known as Baer’s Theorem.

Several kinds of variations of these results, for groups and for other types of algebraic objects, exist in the literature. Closely related to our current work are, for instance, a version of Schur’s theorem for Lie algebras obtained in
[
10
]
: here the author proves that if
\mathfrak{l}/Z(\mathfrak{l})
is finite-dimensional, then so is the commutator ideal
\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}
. An analogue of Baer’s theorem for Lie algebras occurs in
[
11
]
: if
\mathfrak{l}/Z_{i}(\mathfrak{l})
is finite-dimensional, then so is
\gamma_{i+1}(\mathfrak{l})
.

Our ultimate aim is to work towards a categorical approach to such results, using the tensor product of
[
5
]
, which captures the non-abelian tensor product introduced by Brown and Loday
[
3
,
2
]
, as well as, for instance, Ellis’s version of it for Lie algebras
[
7
]
.

The present article is a preliminary step in this direction. We use techniques from homological algebra, with in particular the non-abelian tensor products (as well as the closely related exterior products) of Lie and Leibniz algebras respectively introduced in
[
7
]
and in
[
9
]
to prove Schur’s Theorem for finitely generated Leibniz algebras, both Schur’s Theorem and Baer’s Theorem for finitely generated Lie algebras, and a version of these theorems for finitely presented Lie algebras.

The text is structured as follows. In Section
2
we consider the case of Leibniz algebras. After the technically important Theorem
2.5
, here the main result is a Schur-type theorem, which says that given a finitely generated Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{m}
, the commutator
[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]
is finitely generated if and only if for every central extension
0\to\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{l}\to\mathfrak{m}\to 0
of Leibniz algebras, the commutator
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
is finitely generated (Theorem
2.7
).

This is used to obtain two kinds of Schur/Baer-type result for Lie algebras. In Section
3
we consider the case of finitely generated Lie algebras. We first prove Theorem
3.10
, which says that if
\mathfrak{m}
is a finitely generated Lie algebra and
i
a natural number, then
\gamma_{i+1}(\mathfrak{m})
is finitely generated if and only if so is
\mathfrak{m}^{\otimes i+1}
. This is then used in Theorem
3.12
stating that if
\mathfrak{m}
is a finitely generated Lie algebra and
i
a natural number, then
\gamma_{i+1}(\mathfrak{m})
is finitely generated if and only if for every
i

0\to\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{l}\to\mathfrak{m}\to 0
of Lie algebras,
\gamma_{i+1}(\mathfrak{l})
is finitely generated. Section
4
treats similar results for
finitely presented
Lie algebras: Theorem
4.2
and Theorem

Throughout the text, we let
\mathbb{K}
be a field, and consider Lie and Leibniz algebras with respect to this field.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}

\tau_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n})

We will prove the “if” part of the theorem. The other part is trivial.

We have natural morphisms
(\tau_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n}))\star\mathfrak{n}\to\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n}

{\mathfrak{m}\star(\tau_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n}))\to\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n}}

\tau_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n})

\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
,

M_{1}=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}

\mathfrak{m}
and
N_{1}=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{l}\}
a set of generators of
\mathfrak{n}

Since
\mathfrak{m}/\tau_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n})

\mathfrak{n}/\tau_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{n})

(1)

there is a finitely generated

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a finitely generated Leibniz algebra. Then
\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{m}
is finitely generated
iff the Leibniz commutator
[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]
is finitely generated. ∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a finitely generated Leibniz algebra. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]
is finitely generated;

(ii)

there exists a central extension
0\to\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{l}\to\mathfrak{m}\to 0
of Leibniz algebras, where
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
is finitely generated;

(iii)

for every central extension
0\to\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{l}\to\mathfrak{m}\to 0
of Leibniz algebras,
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
is finitely generated.

Proof.

The implications (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) are obvious, so let us show that (i) implies (iii). Consider a central extension as in (iii). We have a short exact sequence
of Leibniz algebras

\displaystyle 0\to(\mathfrak{r}\star\mathfrak{l})\vee(\mathfrak{l}\star\mathfrak{r})\to\mathfrak{l}\star\mathfrak{l}\to\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{m}\to 0,

(2.2)

where
\mathfrak{r}\star\mathfrak{l}
and
\mathfrak{l}\star\mathfrak{r}
are identified with their images into
\mathfrak{l}\star\mathfrak{l}
.
Observe that
\tau_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{m})=[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]
. Moreover, since the extension
0\to\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{l}\to\mathfrak{m}\to 0

is central, we have that
\tau_{\mathfrak{m}}((\mathfrak{r}\star\mathfrak{l})\vee(\mathfrak{l}\star\mathfrak{r}))=0
. By (
2.2
) we get a surjection
\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{m}\to[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
. By the previous corollary,
\mathfrak{m}\star\mathfrak{m}
is finitely
generated. This proves the theorem.
∎

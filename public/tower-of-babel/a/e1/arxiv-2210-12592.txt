Supersymmetric non-Hermitian topological interface laser
Motohiko Ezawa, Natsuko Ishida, Yasutomo Ota, Satoshi Iwamoto

Abstract
We investigate laser emission at the interface of a topological and trivial phases with loss and gain. The system is described by a Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model with site-dependent hopping parameters. We study numerically and analytically the interface states. The ground state is described by the Jackiw-Rebbi mode with a pure imaginary energy, reflecting the non-Hermiticity of the system. It is strictly localized only at the A sites. We also find a series of analytic solutions of excited states based on SUSY quantum mechanics, where the A and B sites of the bipartite lattice form SUSY partners. We then study the system containing loss and gain with saturation. The Jackiw-Rebbi mode is extended to a nonlinear theory, where B sites are also excited. The relative phases between A and B sites are fixed, and hence it will serve as a large area coherent laser.

;

A representation of the algebra is constructed as follows.

We define the operators

H_{A}\equiv A^{\dagger}A,\qquad H_{B}\equiv AA^{\dagger}.

(38)

The eigenvalue equations are

H_{A}\phi_{p}^{A}=E_{p}^{A}\phi_{p}^{A},\qquad H_{B}\phi_{p}^{B}=E_{p}^{B}\phi_{p}^{B}.

(39)

Using these we obtain

\displaystyle H_{B}(A\phi_{p}^{A})

\displaystyle=AA^{\dagger}A\phi_{p}^{A}=E_{p}^{A}(A\phi_{p}^{A}),

(40)

\displaystyle H_{A}(A^{\dagger}\phi_{p}^{B})

\displaystyle=A^{\dagger}AA^{\dagger}\phi_{p}^{B}=E_{p}^{B}(A^{\dagger}\phi_{p}^{B}),

(41)

and hence,
A\phi_{q}^{A}
is an eigenstate of
H_{B}
with the eigenvalue
E_{q}^{A}
. If we assume
E_{0}^{A}=0
and
E_{0}^{B}\neq 0
, we may choose
q=p+1
. Then,
\phi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right)\varpropto A\phi_{p+1}^{A}\left(x\right)
and
\phi_{p+1}^{A}\left(x\right)\varpropto A^{\dagger}\phi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right)
so that

E_{p}^{B}=E_{p+1}^{A},\qquad E_{0}^{A}=0.

(42)

The wavefunctions give a representation of the SUSY algebra, as illustrated
in Fig.
5
.

We now show that the present model presents a non-Hermitian representation
of the SUSY algebra. We may use Eq.(
24
) for
A
and
A^{\dagger}
.
For the Hamiltonian (
23
) we find

\overline{H}^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\bar{\gamma}^{2}+A^{\dagger}A&0\\
0&-\bar{\gamma}^{2}+AA^{\dagger}\end{array}\right).

(43)

On the other hand, from

\overline{H}^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Psi_{p}^{A}\left(x\right)\\
\Psi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right)\end{array}\right)=\overline{E}_{n}^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Psi_{p}^{A}\left(x\right)\\
\Psi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right)\end{array}\right),

(44)

we find a set of decoupled equations,

\displaystyle H_{A}\Psi_{p}^{A}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=(\overline{E}_{p}^{2}+\bar{\gamma}^{2})\Psi_{p}^{A}\left(x\right),

(45)

\displaystyle H_{B}\Psi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=(\overline{E}_{p}^{2}+\bar{\gamma}^{2})\Psi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right).

(46)

When we set

\displaystyle\phi_{p}^{A}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{p}^{A}\left(x\right),\qquad\phi_{p-1}^{B}=\Psi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right),

(47)

\displaystyle E_{p-1}^{B}

\displaystyle=E_{p}^{A}=\overline{E}_{p}^{2}+\bar{\gamma}^{2},

(48)

Eq.(
39
) is satisfied. Hence, the SUSY algebra is satisfied. The SUSY
partners are the wavefunctions on the A and B sites.

Explicit solutions of non-Hermitian SSH model

We next seek the explicit solutions of the non-Hermitian model (
26
). This can be done by simplifying the function (
25
). When
\xi
is large, we can approximate the gap function by a linear function as

\Delta\left(x\right)=(\kappa\lambda/\xi)x,

(49)

where we set
x=0
at the interface. The Hamiltonian is given by

\overline{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}i\bar{\gamma}&A^{\dagger}\\
A&-i\bar{\gamma}\end{array}\right),

(50)

or

\widetilde{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}i\gamma\chi-i\gamma&A^{\dagger}\\
A&-i\gamma\end{array}\right),

(51)

where

A\equiv(\kappa\lambda/\xi)x+\kappa\partial_{x},\qquad A^{\dagger}\equiv(\kappa\lambda/\xi)x-\kappa\partial_{x}.

(52)

The commutator of the SUSY operators is calculated as

\left[A,A^{\dagger}\right]=\alpha,\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha\equiv 2\kappa^{2}\lambda/\xi.

(53)

The standard commutation relation of the annihilation and creation operators
follows,

\left[b,b^{\dagger}\right]=1,

(54)

in terms of the scaled operators
b
and
b^{\dagger}
defined by

A\equiv\sqrt{\alpha}b,\quad A^{\dagger}\equiv\sqrt{\alpha}b^{\dagger}.

(55)

Eqs.(
45
) and (
46
) are rewritten as

\alpha b^{\dagger}b\Psi_{p}^{A}=(\overline{E}_{p}^{2}+\bar{\gamma}^{2})\Psi_{p}^{A},\quad\alpha(1+b^{\dagger}b)\Psi_{p}^{B}=(\overline{E}_{p}^{2}+\bar{\gamma}^{2})\Psi_{p}^{B}.

(56)

These are solved as

\displaystyle\Psi_{p}^{A}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=h_{p}^{A}\langle x|p\rangle,\quad\Psi_{p}^{B}\left(x\right)=h_{p}^{B}\langle x|p-1\rangle,

(57)

\displaystyle\overline{E}_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\bar{\gamma}^{2}+\alpha p,

(58)

for
p\geq 1
, where
h_{p}^{A}
and
h_{p}^{B}
are c-numbers. For
p=0
,
we have
\overline{E}_{0}=i\bar{\gamma}
, and the wavefunctions are given by
the non-Hermitian Jackiw-Rebbi solutions (
29
) and (
30
).

We note that the energy
\overline{E}_{p}
of the
p
th level is pure
imaginary when

p<\frac{\bar{\gamma}^{2}}{\alpha}=\frac{\gamma\chi\xi}{4\kappa^{2}\lambda}.

(59)

We call the mode
|p\rangle
the SUSY Jackiw-Rebbi mode, because we create
it from the Jackiw-Rebbi mode
|0\rangle
by the operation of
b^{\dagger}
. The SUSY Jackiw-Rebbi modes are supersymmetric, while the Jackiw-Rebbi
mode breaks it.

We determine the relation between two c-numbers
h_{p}^{A}
and
h_{p}^{B}
.
We write down the eigenvalue equations (
26
) explicitly,

i\bar{\gamma}\Psi_{p}^{A}+A^{\dagger}\Psi_{p}^{B}=\overline{E}_{p}\Psi_{p}^{A},\quad A\Psi_{p}^{A}-i\bar{\gamma}\Psi_{p}^{B}=\overline{E}_{p}\Psi_{p}^{B},

(60)

which we rewrite with the use of (
57
) as

\displaystyle i\bar{\gamma}h_{p}^{A}|p\rangle+\sqrt{\alpha}h_{p}^{B}b^{\dagger}|p-1\rangle

\displaystyle=\overline{E}_{p}h_{p}^{A}|p\rangle,

(61)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}h_{p}^{A}b|p\rangle-i\bar{\gamma}h_{p}^{B}|p-1\rangle

\displaystyle=\overline{E}_{p}h_{p}^{B}|p-1\rangle.

(62)

It follows that

\displaystyle i\bar{\gamma}h_{p}^{A}+\sqrt{\alpha p}h_{p}^{B}

\displaystyle=\overline{E}_{p}h_{p}^{A},

(63)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha p}h_{p}^{A}-i\bar{\gamma}h_{p}^{B}

\displaystyle=\overline{E}_{p}h_{p}^{B}.

(64)

or

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha p}h_{p}^{B}

\displaystyle=(\overline{E}_{p}-i\bar{\gamma})h_{p}^{A},

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha p}h_{p}^{A}

\displaystyle=(\overline{E}_{p}+i\bar{\gamma})h_{p}^{B},

\Psi_{p}^{B}

\Psi_{p}^{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{E}_{p}^{+}-i\bar{\gamma}}{\overline{E}_{p}^{+}+i\bar{\gamma}}\ll 1\quad\text{for}\quad\overline{E}_{p}^{+}=i\bar{\gamma}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\gg|\Psi_{p}^{B}|\quad\text{for the series}\quad\overline{E}_{p}^{+},

\displaystyle|\Psi_{p}^{A}|

\displaystyle\ll|\Psi_{p}^{B}|\quad\text{for the series}\quad\overline{E}_{p}^{-}.

(71)

Quench dynamics

\eta
finite in Eq.(
1
). We show
the results in Fig.

(c1), (c2) and (c3). The amplitudes

|\psi_{n_{\text{IF}}}|

(c4).

We show the spatial profile of the saturated amplitude

Nonlinear Jackiw-Rebbi theory

We have numerically revealed the excitations at the B sites in the presence

VIII
Conclusion and Discussion

We have explored a SUSY structure in the SSH model with a topological
interface as a model of topological interface laser with gain and loss. By
extending a SUSY quantum mechanics to non-Hermitian systems, we have found a
series of analytic solutions which extend the original Jackiw-Rebbi
solution. They have pure imaginary energies and their wavefunctions are
given by those of a harmonic oscillator. We also derived an analytic form of
the Jackiw-Rebbi mode in nonlinear regime by using a mean-field
approximation.

We have applied quench dynamics to investigate a topological interface laser
with gain and loss. However, it may be hard to observe the time evolution in
actual optical experiments because the time scale is too short. The same
physics is executed by the coupled-wave-guide arrays along the
z
direction
Longhi
, simply by replacing time
t
by coordinate
z
in the
equation of motion.

We have developed an analysis based on the basic equation (
1
). On
the other hand, it is well known that the dynamics of a laser is described
by the rate equations. It is actually possible to derive Eq.(
1
)
from the rate equations in a certain limit provided the carrier population
is saturated. See details for Appendix C.

Large-area single-mode lasers are realized by suppressing the appearance of
higher order modes. In order to increase the output power of the laser, it
is necessary to enlarge the emitting area. However, it causes the multi-mode
and degrades the brightness at the same time in general. There are several
proposals on the single mode laser using photonic crystals have been
reported by using double-lattice photonic-crystal resonators
Yoshida
,
accidental Dirac-point
Chua
;
Contra
and Kekulé modulation
Gao
;
Yang

in the photonic lattice mostly over the past few years. Our results give a
deeper understanding of a large area single mode laser from a topological
interface
Ishida
.

Appendix A
Topological property of the non-Hermitian SSH model

We consider a homogeneous system. The Hamiltonian in the momentum space
corresponding to the hopping matrix (
8
) is

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-i\gamma\left(1-\chi\right)&\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B}e^{-iak}\\
\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B}e^{iak}&-i\gamma\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle=-i\gamma\left(1-\frac{\chi}{2}\right)I_{2}+\overline{H}_{\text{SSH}}

(84)

with
a
the lattice constant and

\overline{H}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}i\gamma\chi/2&\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B}e^{-iak}\\
\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B}e^{iak}&-i\gamma\chi/2\end{array}\right).

(85)

The Hamiltonian
\overline{H}_{\text{SSH}}
is non-Hermitian for
\gamma\neq 0
. The relation between the eigenenergy of the Hamiltonians (
84
) and (
85
) is

\widetilde{E}=-i\gamma\left(1-\frac{\chi}{2}\right)+\overline{E}_{\text{SSH}}.

(86)

The energy spectrum reads

\overline{E}\left(k\right)=\pm\sqrt{\kappa_{A}^{2}+\kappa_{B}^{2}+2\kappa_{A}\kappa_{B}\cos ak-\gamma^{2}}.

(87)

Especially, we have

\overline{E}\left(\pi/a\right)=\pm\sqrt{\left(\kappa_{A}-\kappa_{B}\right)^{2}-\gamma^{2}}.

(88)

The system is the PT preserved phase for
\gamma<\left|\kappa_{A}-\kappa_{B}\right|
, where the bulk energy is real even though the
system is non-Hermitian, while the system is the PT broken phase for
\gamma>\left|\kappa_{A}-\kappa_{B}\right|
, where the bulk
energy becomes pure imaginary for a certain range of the momentum
k
.

We recall that the PT symmetry operation is defined by

PT=\sigma_{x}K,

(89)

with K the complex conjugation. Since we have

PT\overline{H}\left(k\right)\left(PT\right)^{-1}=\overline{H}\left(k\right),

(90)

and hence
\overline{H}_{\text{SSH}}
is a PT symmetric Hamiltonian.

The topological number is defined with respect to the Hamiltonian (
85
). We define the right and left eigenvectors by

H\left|\psi^{\text{R}}\right\rangle=E\left|\psi^{\text{R}}\right\rangle,\quad H^{\dagger}\left|\psi^{\text{L}}\right\rangle=E\left|\psi^{\text{L}}\right\rangle.

(91)

The non-Hermitian Zak phase is a topological number
Lieu

W\equiv\frac{i}{2\pi/a}\int_{0}^{2\pi/a}\left\langle\psi^{\text{L}}\right|\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\left|\psi^{\text{R}}\right\rangle dk.

(92)

It is straightforward to show that
W=1
for
\kappa_{A}<\kappa_{B}
and
W=0
for
\kappa_{A}>\kappa_{B}
irrespective of
\gamma
. Hence, the
system is topological for
\kappa_{A}<\kappa_{B}
and trivial for
\kappa_{A}>\kappa_{B}
.

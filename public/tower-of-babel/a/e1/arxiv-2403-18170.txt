Formal deformations, cohomology theory and L ∞ ​ [ 1 ] L_{\infty}[1] -structures for differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight
Weiguo Lyu, Zihao Qi, Jian Yang and Guodong Zhou

Abstract
. Generalising a previous work of Jiang and Sheng, a cohomology theory for differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight is introduced. The underlying L ∞ ​ [ 1 ] L_{\infty}[1] -structure on the cochain complex is also determined via a generalised version of higher derived brackets. The equivalence between L ∞ ​ [ 1 ] L_{\infty}[1] -structures for absolute and relative differential Lie algebras are established. Formal deformations and abelian extensions are interpreted by using lower degree cohomology groups. Also we introduce the homotopy differential Lie algebras. In a forthcoming paper, we will show that the operad of homotopy (relative) differential Lie algebras is the minimal model of the operad of (relative) differential Lie algebras.

Introduction

This paper studies the cohomology theory, abelian extensions, formal deformations, and
L_{\infty}
-structures for differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight.

0.1.
Deformation theory and minimal models

An important method to study a mathematical object is to investigate properties of this object under small deformations.
Algebraic deformation theory originated from the deformation theory of complex structures due to Kodaira and Spencer and evolved from ideas of Grothendieck, Illusie, Gerstenhaber, Nijenhuis, Richardson, Deligne, Schlessinger, Stasheff, Goldman, Millson, etc.
A fundamental idea of deformation theory is that the deformation theory
of any given mathematical object can be governed by a certain differential graded Lie algebra or more
generally an
L_{\infty}
-algebra associated to this mathematical object (whose underlying complex is called the deformation complex).
This philosophy has been realised as a theorem in characteristic zero by Lurie
[
15
]
and Pridham
[
19
]
. It is an important problem to determine explicitly this differential graded Lie algebra or
L_{\infty}
-algebra governing deformation theory of this mathematical object.

Another important problem about algebraic structures is to study their homotopy versions, just like
A_{\infty}
-algebras for usual associative algebras.
Expected result would be providing a minimal model of the operad governing this algebraic structure.
When this operad is Koszul, the Koszul duality for operads
[
7
,
16
,
13
]
gives a perfect solution. More precisely,
the cobar construction of the Koszul dual cooperad of the operad in question is the minimal model of this operad. However, when the operad is NOT Koszul, essential difficulties arise and few examples of minimal models have been worked out.

These two problems, say, describing controlling
L_{\infty}
-algebras and constructing minimal models or more generally cofibrant resolutions, are closed related. In fact, given a cofibrant resolution, in particular, a minimal model, of the operad in question, one can deduce from the cofibrant resolution the deformation complex as well as its
L_{\infty}
-structure as explained by Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
12
]
.

However, in practice, a minimal model or a small cofibrant resolution is not known a priori. Wang and Zhou
[
25
,
26
]
recently found a method to solve these two problems for a large class of non-Koszul operads. The method is in fact the original method of Gerstenhaber
[
4
,
5
]
. We start by studying formal deformations of the algebraic structure in question and construct the deformation complex as well as
L_{\infty}
-structure on it from deformation equations. Then we consider the homotopy version which corresponds to Maurer-Cartan elements in the
L_{\infty}
-structure when appropriate spaces are graded. At last we show the differential graded operad governing the homotopy version is the minimal model of the operad of this algebraic structure, and we also found the Koszul dual which, in this case, is usually a homotopy cooperad.

We aim to deal with differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight in this paper and its sequel by using the method of
[
25
,
26
]
as well as derived bracket technique.

0.2.
Differential Lie algebras, old and new

As is well known, differential operators appeared in Calculus several centuries ago. The study of differential algebras themselves, as the algebraic study of differential equations, only began in the 1930s at the hands of Ritt
[
20
,
21
]
. From then on, by the work of many mathematicians in the following decades, the subject has been fully developed into a vast area in mathematics.

By definition a
differential algebra
is an associative commutative algebra
A
endowed with a linear operator
{\mathrm{d}}
subject to the Leibniz rule:

{\mathrm{d}}(xy)={\mathrm{d}}(x)y+x{\mathrm{d}}(y),x,y\in A.

The discretisation of differential operators are

difference operators
. The action of the difference operator
\Delta
on a real function
f
defined by:

(\Delta f)(x)=f(x+\Delta x)-f(x),

which satisfies

\Delta(fg)=\Delta(f)g+f\Delta(g)+\Delta(f)\Delta(g).

In order to unify the study of differential operator and difference operator, Guo and Keigher introduced weighted differential operators
[
9
]
.

Definition 0.1
.

(
[
9
]
)
Let
\lambda\in{\mathbf{k}}
be a fixed element. A
differential associative algebra of weight
\lambda
is an associative algebra
A
together with a linear operator
{\mathrm{d}}:A\rightarrow A
such that

{\mathrm{d}}(xy)={\mathrm{d}}(x)y+x{\mathrm{d}}(y)+\lambda{\mathrm{d}}(x){\mathrm{d}}(y),x,y\in A.

So an usual differential operator is a differential operator of weight
0
and a difference operator is a differential operator of weight
1
.

One of the motivations of Guo and his collaborators to introduce the concept of weighted differential operators is to study Rota’s program. Rota
[
22
]
proposed to classify “interesting” operators on an algebra. In order to promote this program, Guo et al. introduced the Gröbner-Shirshov basis theory for algebras endowed with linear operators
[
8
,
1
,
10
,
3
]
, and focused on two types of operator algebras: Rota-Baxter type algebras
[
27
]
and differential type algebras
[
10
]
. Weighted differential algebras fall into the second type.

Inspired by the above research, Guo and Keigher
[
9
]
introduced differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight.

Definition 0.2
.

(
[
9
]
)
Let
\lambda\in{\mathbf{k}}
be a fixed element. A
differential Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
is a Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},[~,~])
together with a linear operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,y])=[{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),y]+[x,{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)]+\lambda[{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)],\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

A related notion is that of relative difference Lie algebras.

Definition 0.3

(
[
2
,
11
]
)
.

A
LieAct triple
is the triple
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}},\rho)
, where
({\mathfrak{g}},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{h}})
are Lie algebras and
\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}({\mathfrak{h}})
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras, where
\mathrm{Der}({\mathfrak{h}})
is the space of derivations on
{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}},\rho)
be a LieAct triple. A linear map
D:{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,{\mathfrak{h}}
is called a
relative differential operator of weight

\lambda
if the following equality holds:

D([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho(x)(D(y))-\rho(y)(D(x))+\lambda[D(x),D(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

There has been some papers working on differential Lie algebras with nonzero weight before, but they mainly focus on relative differential Lie algebras of weight
1
, also called relative difference algebras. When studying non-Abelian extensions of Lie algebras, Lue
[
14
]
introduced the concept of cross homomorphisms which are just relative difference operators of weight
1
.
Caseiro and Costa
[
2
]
studied the framework for the existence of cross homomorphisms: LieAct triple
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}},\rho)
, and gave a differential graded Lie algebra where the Maurer-Cartan elements correspond one-to-one to Lie action triples.
Pei, Sheng, Tang, and Zhao
[
18
]
defined the cohomology theory of cross homomorphisms on Lie algebras, and used the derived bracket technique to find the deformation of a differential graded Lie algebra where the Maurer-Cartan elements correspond one-to-one to the cross homomorphisms.
Jiang and Sheng constructed an
L_{\infty}
-algebra for relative difference Lie algebra using the technique of derived brackets in
[
11
]
and they also introduced cohomology theory for relative difference Lie algebras and absolute difference Lie algebras.

This paper is different from papers of Sheng and his collaborators, firstly generalising from weight
1
to arbitrary weight. Moreover, our ultimate goal is to develop systematically the homotopy theory for both absolute and relative differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight from an operadic viewpoint, including cohomology theory (or deformation complexes),
L_{\infty}[1]
-structures, homotopy versions, minimal models, and Koszul dual homotopy cooperads.
This task will be completed in two papers. This paper is the elementary part of this project, which contains only cohomology theory (or deformation complexes),
L_{\infty}[1]
-structures and homotopy versions.
In a forthcoming paper, we shall prove the operad of homotopy absolute (resp. relative) differential Lie algebras is the minimal model of the operad of absolute (resp. relative) differential Lie algebras, thus justifying the homotopy version we found is the right one.

Apart from the cohomology theory and
L_{\infty}[1]
-structures, in this paper we introduce a generalised version of derived bracket technique which replace Lie subalgebras by injective maps of Lie algebras. Moreover, this generalised version permits us to establish the equivalence between
L_{\infty}[1]
-structures for relative and absolute differential Lie algebras. This equivalence is believed by experts and firstly presented explicitly in this paper for differential Lie algebras. In the forthcoming paper, we will establish the equivalence between minimal models for relative and absolute differential Lie algebras by using a coloring and decoloring procedure for (coloured) operads.

0.3.
Layout of the paper

The paper is organized as follows.

The first section is of preliminary nature, which contains results which are more or less known. After fixing some notations in Subsection
1.1
, we recall basic notions and facts about differential Lie algebra and their representations in Subsection
1.2
. In particular, a key descending property for differential representations Lemma
1.7
is presented.
A differential Lie algebra is the combination of the underlying Lie algebra and the differential operator. In this light we build the cohomology theory of a differential Lie algebra by combining its components from the Lie algebra and from the differential operator.
In Subsection
1.3
, we establish the cohomology theory for differential operators of arbitrary weight, which is quite different from the one for the underlying algebra unless the weight is zero.
In Subsection
1.4
, we combine the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology for Lie algebras and the just established cohomology for differential operators of arbitrary weight to define the cohomology of differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight, with the cochain maps again posing extra challenges when the weight is not zero. The proofs of this section will be given in Subsection
5.1
, once the

-structure has been established in Section
4
.

As applications and further justification of our cohomology theory for differential Lie algebras, in Section

Further, in Section
3
, we apply the above cohomology theory to study formal deformations of differential Lie algebras of arbitrary weight. In particular, we show that if the second cohomology group of a differential Lie algebra with coefficients in the regular representation is trivial, then this differential Lie algebra is rigid.

To deal with the weight case and get

-structure in Section

, after a reminder on

-structures in Subsection
4.1
, a generalised version of the derived bracket technique is introduced in Subsection

, and the weight

With this generalised version at hand, we build the

-structure of relative differential Lie algebras in Subsection
4.3
,
generalising the result of Jiang and Sheng
[
11
]
from weight
1
to arbitrary weight.
In Subsection
4.4
, we build the
L_{\infty}[1]
-structure of (absolute) differential Lie algebras. The comparison with the
L_{\infty}[1]
-structures for relative and absolute differential Lie algebras is provided in Subsection
4.5
.

At last in Section
5
, based on the

-structure of (absolute) differential Lie algebras found in Section

is the right one in Subsection

-structure of (absolute) differential Lie algebras when the spaces involved are graded.

1.1.

Throughout this paper, let

We will employ Koszul sign rule to determine signs, that is, when exchanging the positions of two graded objects in an expression, we have to multiply the expression by a power of

The suspension of a graded space

x_{1}\odot x_{2}\odot\dots\odot x_{n}=\varepsilon(\sigma)x_{\sigma(1)}\odot x_{\sigma(2)}\odot\dots\odot x_{\sigma(n)}\in S(V).

Denote by

Let

1.2.
Differential Lie algebras with weight and their representations

In this subsection, we recall basic notions about differential Lie algebras with weight and their representations.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
9
]
)
Let
\lambda\in{\mathbf{k}}
be a fixed element. A
differential Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
is a Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},~[~,~])
together with a linear operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

(2)

{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,y])=[{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),y]+[x,{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)]+\lambda[{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)],\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Such an operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
differential operator of weight
\lambda
or a
derivation of weight
\lambda
.

Given two differential Lie algebras
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}),\,({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{h}})
of the same weight
\lambda
, a
homomorphism of differential Lie algebras
from
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
to
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\varphi:{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
\varphi\circ{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\varphi
.
We denote by
\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}
the category of differential Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
.

A differential operator of weight
1
is also called a crossed homomorphism and a differential Lie algebra of weight
1
is also called a difference Lie algebra
[
18
,
11
]
.

Recall that a representation of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a pair
(V,\rho)
, where
V
is a vector space and
\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V),~x\mapsto(v\mapsto\rho(x)v)
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
v\in V
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a differential Lie algebra.

(i)

A
representation
over the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
or a
differential representation
is a triple
(V,\rho,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
, where
{\mathrm{d}}_{V}\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, and
(V,\rho)
is a representation over the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, such that for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},v\in V,

the following equality holds:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(\rho(x)v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))v+\rho(x){\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v)+\lambda\rho({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)){\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v).

(ii)

Given two representations
(U,\rho^{U},{\mathrm{d}}_{U}),\,(V,\rho^{V},{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
over
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, a linear map
f:U\,\rightarrow\,V
is called a
homomorphism
of representations, if

f\circ\rho^{U}(x)=\rho^{V}(x)\circ f,\quad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\mathrm{and}\quad f\circ{\mathrm{d}}_{U}={\mathrm{d}}_{V}\circ f.

.

For a differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, the usual adjoint representation

{\rm ad}:{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{gl}({\mathfrak{g}}),\,x\mapsto(y\mapsto[x,y])

over the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}

It is also called the

over the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, denoted by
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\rm ad}}
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra and
V
be a representation of it. Then
({\mathfrak{g}},\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a differential Lie algebra of weight
-1
and the pair

Example 1.5
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a differential Lie algebra together with a differential representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
. Then for arbitrary nonzero scalar

,
({\mathfrak{g}},\kappa{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a differential Lie algebra of weight

and the pair

It is straightforward to obtain the following result:

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
(V,\rho,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
be a representation of the differential Lie algebra

({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V,{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+{\mathrm{d}}_{V})

1.3.
Cohomology of differential operators

In this subsection, we define a cohomology theory of differential operators.

Recall that
the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with coefficients in a representation
(V,\rho)
is the cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{{\mathrm{Lie}}}^{*}),

where for
n\geq 0
,
{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)={\rm Hom}(\wedge^{n}{\mathfrak{g}},V)
(in particular,
{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)=V
) and the coboundary operator

\partial_{{\mathrm{Lie}}}^{n}:{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{C}}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),n\geq 0

is given by

\begin{split}\partial_{{\mathrm{Lie}}}^{n}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})&=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+n}\rho(x_{i})f(x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n+1})\\
&+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j+n+1}f([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,\hat{x}_{j},\dots,x_{n+1}),\end{split}

for all
f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),~x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
\hat{x_{i}}
means deleting this element. The corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology is denoted by
{\mathrm{H}}^{*}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
.

When
V
is the adjoint representation
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\rm ad}}
, we write
{\mathrm{H}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}}):={\mathrm{H}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),n\geq 0
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a differential Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
and
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
be a differential representation over
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
The
cochain complex of the differential operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
with coefficients in the differential representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
is defined to be the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with coefficients in the new differential representation
V_{\lambda}
, denoted by
({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{*}):=({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\rm Lie}({\mathfrak{g}},V_{\lambda}),\partial_{\rm Lie}^{*})
.
The corresponding cohomology, denoted by
{\mathrm{H}}^{*}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
, is called the
cohomology of the differential operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
with coefficients in the representation

(V_{\lambda},{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
.

More precisely, for
n\geq 0
,
{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)={\rm Hom}(\wedge^{n}{\mathfrak{g}},V)
and the coboundary operator

\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{n}:{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)\to{\mathrm{C}}^{n+1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{n}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+n}\rho_{\lambda}(x_{i})f(x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j+n+1}f([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,\hat{x}_{j},\dots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+n}\rho(x_{i}+\lambda{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{i}))f(x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j+n+1}f([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,\hat{x}_{j},\dots,x_{n+1}),

for all
f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),~x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

1.4.
Cohomology of differential Lie algebras

We now combine the classical Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of Lie algebras and the newly defined cohomology of differential operators of weight
\lambda
to define the cohomology of the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
. In fact, we will define the cochain complex of a differential Lie algebras as, up to shift and signs, the mapping cone of a cochain map from the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of the Lie algebra to the cochain complex of the differential operators of weight
\lambda
.

Notice that
[
11
]
has introduced the cohomology theory of a difference Lie algebra with coefficients in a representation. We in fact generalise their construction from weight
1
to arbitrary weight.

Introduce the linear maps

\delta^{n}:{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)

by

\displaystyle\delta^{n}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda^{k-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}f(x_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{i_{1}}),\dots,{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{i_{k}}),\dots,x_{n})-{\mathrm{d}}_{V}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

for
f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
,
n\geq 1
and

\delta^{0}v={-}{\mathrm{d}}_{V}{(v)},\quad\forall v\in{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)=V.

Proposition 1.9
.

The linear map
\delta
is a cochain map from the cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{*})
to
({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{*})
.

Although a direct proof is possible, we shall deduce the above result from the
L_{\infty}[1]
-structure; see Proposition
5.2
.

Remark 1.10
.

Note that
{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
equals to
{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
as linear spaces but they are not equal as cochain complexes unless
\lambda=0
. When
\lambda
is not zero, a new representation structure is needed to define
\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{*}
which eventually leads to the rather long and technical argument in order to establish the cochain map in Proposition
1.9
.

Now we can define the cochain complex of a differential Lie algebras
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in the differential representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
.

Definition 1.11
.

Define the
cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial^{*}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}})
of the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in the differential representation

(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
to be the negative shift of
the mapping cone of the cochain map
\delta^{*}:({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),{\partial}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{*})\rightarrow({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),{\partial}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{*})
. More precisely, the space of
n
-cochains is given by

{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},V):=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)\oplus{\mathrm{C}}^{n-1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),&n\geq 1,\\
{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)=V,&n=0.\end{cases}

and the differential
\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}:{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}(g,V)\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n+1}(g,V)
by

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}(f,g)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{n}f,-\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{n-1}g-\delta^{n}f),\quad\forall f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\,g\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n-1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\quad n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{0}{v}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{0}{v},\delta^{0}v),\quad\forall{v}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)=V.

Definition 1.12
.

The cohomology of the cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial^{*}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}})
, denoted by
{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
, is called the
cohomology of the differential Lie algebra

({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in the differential representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
.

In the next two sections, we will use the following remarks.

Remark 1.13
.

We compute 0-cocycles, 1-cocycles and 2-cocycles of the cochain complex

{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
.

It is obvious that for all
v\in V
,
\partial^{0}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}v=0
if and only if

\partial^{0}_{\mathsf{Lie}}v=0,\quad{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v)=0.

For all
(f,v)\in{\rm Hom}({\mathfrak{g}},V)\oplus V
,
\partial^{1}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}(f,v)=0
if and only if
\partial^{1}_{\mathsf{Lie}}f=0
and

\rho_{\lambda}(x)v=f({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))-{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(f(x)),\quad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

For all
(f,g)\in{\rm Hom}(\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{g}},V)\oplus{\rm Hom}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
,
\partial^{2}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}(f,g)=0
if and only if
\partial^{2}_{\mathsf{Lie}}f=0,
and

(3)

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(x)g(y)-\rho_{\lambda}(y)g(x)-g([x,y])=-\lambda f({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))-f({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),y)-f(x,{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))+{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(f(x,y)),\

for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Remark 1.14
.

we shall need a subcomplex of the cochain complex
{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
. Let

\tilde{{\mathrm{C}}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},V):=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V)\oplus{\mathrm{C}}^{n-1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),&n\geq 2,\\
{\mathrm{C}}^{1}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},V),&n=1,\\
0,&n=0.\end{cases}

Then it is obvious that
(\tilde{{\mathrm{C}}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde{{\mathrm{C}}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*})
is a subcomplex of the cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*})
. We denote its cohomology by
\tilde{{\mathrm{H}}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
. Obviously,
\tilde{{\mathrm{H}}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},V)={{\mathrm{H}}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
for
n>2
.

2.
Abelian extensions of differential Lie algebras

In this section, we study abelian extensions of differential Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
and show that they are classified by the second cohomology, as one would expect of a good cohomology theory.

2.1.
Abelian extensions

Notice that a vector space
V
together with a linear endomorphism
{\mathrm{d}}_{V}
can be considered as a
trivial differential Lie algebra of weight

\lambda

endowed with the trivial Lie bracket
[u,v]=0
for all
u,v\in V
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
abelian extension
of differential Lie algebras is a short exact sequence of homomorphisms of differential Lie algebras

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>{V}@>{i}>{}>\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}@>{p}>{}>{\mathfrak{g}}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{{\mathrm{d}}_{V}}V{}V@V{{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}}V{}V@V{{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>{V}@>{i}>{}>\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}@>{p}>{}>{\mathfrak{g}}@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

where
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
is a trivial differential Lie algebra.
We will call
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}})
an
abelian extension
of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}})
and
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}})
be two abelian extensions of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
. They are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists an isomorphism of differential Lie algebras
\zeta:(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}})\rightarrow(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}})
such that the following commutative diagram holds:

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>{(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})}@>{i}>{}>(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}})@>{p}>{}>({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})@>{}>{}>0\\
\Big\|@V{\zeta}V{}V\Big\|\\
0@>{}>{}>{(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})}@>{i}>{}>(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}})@>{p}>{}>({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

A
section
of an abelian extension
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}})
of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
is a linear map
s:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
such that
p\circ s={\rm Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Now for an abelian extension
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}})
of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
with a section
s:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, then there exist a unique linear map
t:\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}\rightarrow V
such that:

t\circ i=\mathrm{Id}_{V},t\circ s=0,i\circ t+s\circ p=\mathrm{Id}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}},

then we can define

\rho(x)v:=t[s(x),i(v)]_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}},\quad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}},v\in V.

For convenience, we denote it by
\rho(s(x))v,
and observe that
[s(x),i(v)]_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}\in i(V)
, and we identify
V
with
i(V).

Now we get a linear map
\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V),~x\mapsto(v\mapsto\rho(x)v).

Proposition 2.3
.

With the above notations,
(V,\rho,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
is a representation over the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Proof.

For arbitrary
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},v\in V
, since
s([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})-[s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}\in V
implies
s([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})v=[s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}v
, we have

\rho([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})(v)=\rho(s([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}))v=\rho([s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}})v{\mathrel{\to}^{\mbox{Jacobi identity}}}\rho(x)\rho(y)(v)-\rho(y)\rho(x)(v).

Hence,
\rho
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Moreover,
{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s(x))-s({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\in V
means that

\rho({\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s(x)))v=\rho(s({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))v.

Thus we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(\rho(x)v)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{V}(\rho(s(x))v)={\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(\rho(s(x))v)

\displaystyle=\rho({\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s(x)))v+\rho(s(x)){\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(v)+\lambda\rho({\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s(x))){\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(v)

\displaystyle=\rho(s({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))v+\rho(s(x)){\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v)+\lambda\rho(s({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))){\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v)

\displaystyle=\rho({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))v+\rho(x){\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v)+\lambda\rho({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)){\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v).

Hence,
(V,\rho,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
is a representation over
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

We further define linear maps
\psi:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow V
and
\chi:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow V
respectively by

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)

\displaystyle=[s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}-s([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}),\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle\chi(x)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s(x))-s({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)),\quad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

We transfer the differential Lie algebra structure on
\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
to
{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V
by endowing
{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V
with a multiplication
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\psi}
and a differential operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi}
defined by

(4)

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v]_{\psi}

\displaystyle=[x,y]+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u+\psi(x,y),\,\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},\,u,v\in V,

(5)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi}(x+v)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)+\chi(x)+{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(v),\,\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}},\,v\in V.

Proposition 2.4
.

The triple
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\psi},{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi})
is a differential Lie algebra if and only if

(\psi,\chi)
is a 2-cocycle of the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with the coefficient in
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
.

Proof.

If
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\psi},{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi})
is a differential Lie algebra, then
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\psi}
implies

(6)

\psi(x,[y,z])+\rho(x)\psi(y,z)+\psi(y,[z,x])\\
+\rho(y)\psi(z,x)+\psi(z,[x,y])+\rho(z)\psi(x,y)=0,

for arbitrary
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since
{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi}
satisfies (
2
), we deduce that

(7)

\chi([x,y])-\rho_{\lambda}(x)\chi(y)+\rho_{\lambda}(y)\chi(x)+{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(\psi(x,y))-\psi({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),y)-\psi(x,{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))-\lambda\psi({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))=0.

Hence,
(\psi,\chi)
is a 2-cocycle, see Eq. (
3
).

Conversely, if
(\psi,\chi)
satisfies equalities (
6
) and (
7
), one can easily check that
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\psi},{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi})
is a differential Lie algebra.
∎

2.2.
Classificaiton for Abelian extensions

Now we are ready to classify abelian extensions of a differential Lie algebra.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
{\mathrm{d}}_{V}\in{\rm End}_{\mathbf{k}}(V)
.
Then abelian extensions of a differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
are classified by the second cohomology group
{\rm\tilde{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
.

Proof.

Let
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}})
be an abelian extension of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
. We choose a section
s:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
to obtain a 2-cocycle
(\psi,\chi)
by Proposition
2.4
. We first show that the cohomological class of
(\psi,\chi)
does not depend on the choice of sections. Indeed, let
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
be two distinct sections providing 2-cocycles
(\psi_{1},\chi_{1})
and
(\psi_{2},\chi_{2})
respectively. We define
\phi:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow V
by
\phi(x)=s_{1}(x)-s_{2}(x)
. Then

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[s_{1}(x),s_{1}(y)]-s_{1}([x,y])

\displaystyle=[s_{2}(x)+\phi(x),s_{2}(y)+\phi(y)]-(s_{2}([x,y])+\phi([x,y]))

\displaystyle=([s_{2}(x),s_{2}(y)]-s_{2}([x,y]))+[s_{2}(x),\phi(y)]+[\phi(x),s_{2}(y)]-\phi([x,y])

\displaystyle=([s_{2}(x),s_{2}(y)]-s_{2}([x,y]))+[x,\phi(y)]+[\phi(x),y]-\phi([x,y])

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}(x,y)+\partial^{1}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\phi(x,y),

and

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s_{1}(x))-s_{1}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s_{2}(x)+\phi(x))-(s_{2}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))+\phi({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))

\displaystyle=({\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(s_{2}(x))-s_{2}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))+{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(\phi(x))-\phi({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))

\displaystyle=\chi_{2}(x)+{\mathrm{d}}_{V}(\phi(x))-\phi({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))

\displaystyle=\chi_{2}(x)-\delta^{1}\phi(x).

That is,
(\psi_{1},\chi_{1})=(\psi_{2},\chi_{2})+\partial^{1}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}(\phi)
. Thus
(\psi_{1},\chi_{1})
and
(\psi_{2},\chi_{2})
are in the same cohomological class in
\tilde{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
.

Next we prove that isomorphic abelian extensions give rise to the same element in
\tilde{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
. Assume that
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}})
and
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}})
are two isomorphic abelian extensions of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
with the associated homomorphism
\zeta:(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}})\rightarrow(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}})
. Let
s_{1}
be a section of
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}})
. As
p_{2}\circ\zeta=p_{1}
, we have

p_{2}\circ(\zeta\circ s_{1})=p_{1}\circ s_{1}={\rm Id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}.

Therefore,
\zeta\circ s_{1}
is a section of
(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}})
. Denote
s_{2}:=\zeta\circ s_{1}
. Since
\zeta
is a homomorphism of differential Lie algebras such that
\zeta|_{V}={\rm Id}_{V}
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[s_{2}(x),s_{2}(y)]-s_{2}([x,y])=[\zeta(s_{1}(x)),\zeta(s_{1}(y))]-\zeta(s_{1}([x,y]))

\displaystyle=\zeta([s_{1}(x),s_{1}(y)]-s_{1}([x,y]))=\zeta(\psi_{1}(x,y))

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}(x,y),

and

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}}(s_{2}(x))-s_{2}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))={\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{2}}(\zeta(s_{1}(x)))-\zeta(s_{1}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))

\displaystyle=\zeta({\mathrm{d}}_{\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}}(s_{1}(x))-s_{1}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))=\zeta(\chi_{1}(x))

\displaystyle=\chi_{1}(x).

Consequently, all isomorphic abelian extensions give rise to the same element in
\tilde{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
.

Conversely, given two 2-cocycles
(\psi_{1},\chi_{1})
and
(\psi_{2},\chi_{2})
, we can construct two abelian extensions
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\psi_{1}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi_{1}})
and
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\psi_{2}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\chi_{2}})
via equalities (
4
) and (
5
). If they represent the same cohomological class in
\tilde{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
, then there exists a linear map
\phi:{\mathfrak{g}}\to V
such that

(\psi_{1},\chi_{1})=(\psi_{2},\chi_{2})+\partial^{1}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}(\phi).

Define
\zeta:{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V
by

\zeta(x,v):=x+\phi(x)+v.

Then
\zeta
is an isomorphism of these two abelian extensions.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

In particular, any vector space
V
with linear endomorphism
{\mathrm{d}}_{V}
can serve as a trivial representation of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. In this situation, central extensions of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
are classified by the second cohomology group
{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with the coefficient in the trivial representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
. Note that for a trivial representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
, since
\partial^{1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}v=0
for all
v\in V
, we have

{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V)=\tilde{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},V).

3.
Deformations of differential Lie algebras

In this section, we study formal deformations of a differential Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
. In particular, we show that if the second cohomology group
\tilde{\rm{H}}^{2}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}})=0
, then the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is rigid.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a differential Lie algebra. Denote by
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}
the multiplication of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Consider the 1-parameterized family

\mu_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mu_{i}t^{i},\,\,\mu_{i}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{2}_{{\mathrm{Lie}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad d_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{i}t^{i},\,\,d_{i}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}).

Definition 3.1
.

A
1-parameter formal deformation
of a differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a pair
(\mu_{t},d_{t})
, which endows the
{\mathbf{k}}[[t]]
-module
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\mu_{t},d_{t})
with a differential Lie algebra structure such that
(\mu_{0},d_{0})=(\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

The pair
(\mu_{t},d_{t})
generates a 1-parameter formal deformation of the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
if and only if for all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the following equalities hold:

(8)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(x,\mu_{t}(y,z))+\mu_{t}(y,\mu_{t}(z,x))+\mu_{t}(z,\mu_{t}(x,y)),

(9)

\displaystyle d_{t}(\mu_{t}(x,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(d_{t}(x),y)+\mu_{t}(x,d_{t}(y))+\lambda\mu_{t}(d_{t}(x),d_{t}(y)).

Expanding these equations and collecting coefficients of
t^{n}
, we see that Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
) are equivalent to the systems of equations: for each
n\geq 0
,

(10)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mu_{i}(x,\mu_{n-i}(y,z))+\mu_{i}(y,\mu_{n-i}(z,x))+\mu_{i}(z,\mu_{n-i}(x,y)),

(11)

\displaystyle 0

.

Definition 3.5
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\mu_{t},d_{t})

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\mu}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t}),

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{d}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}.

Theorem 3.7
.

The infinitesimals of two equivalent
1
-parameter formal deformations of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
are in the same cohomology class in
\tilde{\rm{H}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}})

Proof.

Let
\Phi_{t}:({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\bar{\mu}_{t},\bar{d}_{t})\,\rightarrow\,({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\mu_{t},d_{t})
be a formal isomorphism. For all

, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\mu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t})(x,y),

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{d}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}(x).

Expanding the above identities and comparing the coefficients of
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(x,y)+[\phi_{1}(x),y]+[x,\phi_{1}(y)]-\phi_{1}([x,y]),

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}(x)+{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\phi_{1}(x))-\phi_{1}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)).

Thus, we have

in

and

Definition 3.8

-parameter formal deformation

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t}),

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\Phi_{t}.

Definition 3.9
.

A differential Lie algebra

-parameter formal deformation is trivial.

Theorem 3.10
.

If
\tilde{\rm{H}}^{2}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\rm ad})=0
, then the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\mu_{t},d_{t})
be a
1
-parameter formal deformation of
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. By Proposition
3.3
,
(\mu_{1},d_{1})
is a 2-cocycle. By
\tilde{\rm{H}}^{2}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\rm ad})=0
, there exists a 1-cochain
\phi_{1}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{1}_{\mathsf{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}})
such that

(14)

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},d_{1})=-\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{1}(\phi_{1}).

Then setting
\Phi_{t}={\rm Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{1}t
, we have a 1-parameter formal deformation
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\bar{\mu}_{t},\bar{d}_{t})
, where

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Phi_{t}^{-1}\circ\mu_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t})\big)(x,y),

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Phi_{t}^{-1}\circ d_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}\big)(x).

Thus,
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\bar{\mu}_{t},\bar{d}_{t})
is equivalent to
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\mu_{t},d_{t})
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\rm Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}-\phi_{1}t+\phi_{1}^{2}t^{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{i}\phi_{1}^{i}t^{i}+\cdots)(\mu_{t}(x+\phi_{1}(x)t,y+\phi_{1}(y)t)),

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\rm Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}-\phi_{1}t+\phi_{1}^{2}t^{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{i}\phi_{1}^{i}t^{i}+\cdots)(d_{t}(x+\phi_{1}(x)t)).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]+(\mu_{1}(x,y)+[x,\phi_{1}(y)]+[\phi_{1}(x),y]-\phi_{1}([x,y]))t+\bar{\mu}_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}(x)+(d_{A}(\phi_{1}(x))+d_{1}(x)-\phi_{1}({\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))t+\bar{d}_{2}(x)t^{2}+\cdots.

By Eq. (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]+\bar{\mu}_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)+\bar{d}_{2}(x)t^{2}+\cdots.

Then by repeating the argument, we can show that
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\mu_{t},d_{t})
is equivalent to
({\mathfrak{g}}[[t]],\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Thus,
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is rigid.
∎

4.
L_{\infty}[1]
-structure for (relative and absolute) differential Lie algebras

In this section, we study the
L_{\infty}[1]
-structure for differential Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
.
In order to deal with absolute differential Lie algebras, we introduce a generalised version of derived bracket technique; moreover, to consider the weight case, we incorporate the weight
\lambda
into the statements. These are the main differences of this paper with
[
18
,
11
]
, since the latter papers consider difference operators, say, relative differential operators of weight
1
.

4.1.
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebras

In this subsection, we recall some preliminaries on
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

An
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra
is a graded vector space
{\mathfrak{L}}=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathfrak{L}}^{i}
endowed with a family of graded linear maps
l_{n}:{\mathfrak{L}}^{\otimes n}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{L}},n\geq 1
of degree
1
satisfying the following equations:
for arbitrary
n\geq 1
,
\sigma\in S_{n}
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathfrak{L}}
,

(i)

(graded symmetry)

l_{n}(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)})=\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

(ii)

(generalised Jacobi identity)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(i,n-i)}\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0.

Remark 4.2
.

Let us consider the generalised Jacobi identity for
n\leq 3
with the assumption of generalised symmetry.

(i)

For
n=1
, then
l_{1}\circ l_{1}=0
, that is,
l_{1}
is a differential.

(ii)

For
n=2
, then
l_{1}\circ l_{2}+l_{2}\circ(l_{1}\otimes{\rm Id}+{\rm Id}\otimes l_{1})=0
, that is ,
l_{1}
is a derivation with respect to
l_{2}
.

(iii)

For
n=3
and arbitrary homogeneous elements
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in L
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&l_{2}(l_{2}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})\otimes x_{3})+(-1)^{|x_{1}|(|x_{2}|+|x_{3}|)}l_{2}(l_{2}(x_{2}\otimes x_{3})\otimes x_{1})+(-1)^{|x_{3}|(|x_{1}|+|x_{2}|)}l_{2}(l_{2}(x_{3}\otimes x_{1})\otimes x_{2})\\
=&-\Big(l_{1}(l_{3}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes x_{3}))+l_{3}(l_{1}(x_{1})\otimes x_{2}\otimes x_{3})+(-1)^{|x_{1}|}l_{3}(x_{1}\otimes l_{1}(x_{2})\otimes x_{3})+\\
&(-1)^{|x_{1}|+|x_{2}|}l_{3}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes l_{1}(x_{3}))\Big),\end{array}

that is,
l_{2}
satisfies the Jacobi identity up to homotopy.

Definition 4.3
.

A
Maurer-Cartan element
of an
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra
({\mathfrak{L}},\{l_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
is an element
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{L}}^{0}
satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}l_{n}(\alpha^{\otimes n})=0,

whenever this infinite sum exists. Denote
\mathcal{MC}({\mathfrak{L}}):=\{\mbox{Maurer-Cartan elements of}~{\mathfrak{L}}\}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

[
6
, Twisting procedure]

Let
\alpha
be a Maurer-Cartan element of
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra
{\mathfrak{L}}
, The twisted
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra is given by
l_{n}^{\alpha}:{\mathfrak{L}}^{\otimes n}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{L}}
which is defined as follows
\colon

\displaystyle l^{\alpha}_{n}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}l_{n+i}(\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}),\ \forall x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathfrak{L}},

whenever these infinite sums exist.

The following simple observation with immediate proof will be very useful while considering differential operators of a given weight
\lambda
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}=({\mathfrak{L}},\{l_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
be an
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra and
\lambda\in{\bf k}
.
Consider
l_{n}^{\prime}:{\mathfrak{L}}^{\otimes n}\to{\mathfrak{L}},n\geq 1
given by

\displaystyle l^{\prime}_{n}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=\lambda^{n-1}l_{n}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}),\ \forall x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathfrak{L}},

then
({\mathfrak{L}},\{l_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\geq 1})
is also an
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra.

If
l_{1}=0
, then imposing
l_{1}^{\prime}=0
and
l_{n}^{\prime}=\lambda^{n-2}l_{n}
for
n\geq 2
also gives a new
L_{\infty}[1]
-structure on
{\mathfrak{L}}
.

4.2.
A generalised version of derived bracket technique

Now we introduce a generalised version of the derived bracket technique invented by Voronov

differential Lie algebras with weight

Definition 4.6
.

A
generalised V-data
is a septuple
({\mathfrak{L}},{\mathfrak{M}},\iota_{\mathfrak{M}},{\mathfrak{A}},\iota_{\mathfrak{A}},P,\Delta)
, where

(1)

({\mathfrak{L}},[-,-])
is a graded Lie algebra,

(2)

({\mathfrak{M}},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{M}})

(3)

{\mathfrak{A}}

\iota_{\mathfrak{A}}:{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathfrak{L}}

and

is a graded Lie subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{L}}

\Delta\in\mathrm{Ker}(P)^{1}
satisfying

4.3.

We recall the classical Nijenhuis-Richardson brackets and basic facts about LieAct triples; for details, see

Given a vector space
V
, its exterior algebra is
\bigwedge(V):=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\wedge^{k}V
and the reduced version is
\bar{\bigwedge}(V):=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty}\wedge^{k}V

\mathrm{Hom}(\bar{\bigwedge}(V),V)

[

under the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket
[~,~]_{{\rm NR}}
which is defined as follows:
for arbitrary
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{p+1}V,V)
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{q+1}V,V)

[f,g]_{{\rm NR}}:=f\bar{\circ}g-(-1)^{pq}g\bar{\circ}f,

where
f\bar{\circ}g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{p+q+1}V,V)
is given by

\displaystyle f\bar{\circ}g(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(q+1,p)}{\mathrm{sgn}}(\sigma)f(g(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(q+1)}),x_{\sigma(q+2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p+q+1)}).

Definition 4.11

A
LieAct triple

({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}},\rho)

({\mathfrak{g}},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{h}})
are Lie algebras and
\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}({\mathfrak{h}})
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras.

Remark 4.12
.

Given a LieAct triple
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}},\rho)

\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}({\mathfrak{h}})
means that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Lie
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module and there exists an action
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}}\,\rightarrow\,{\mathfrak{h}}
given by

x\cdot[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[x\cdot u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,x\cdot v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}~\mbox{and}~u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

Given two vector spaces
V
and
W

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varphi:\wedge^{n}(V\oplus W)&\longrightarrow&\bigoplus_{k+l=n,k,l\geq 0}\wedge^{k}V\otimes\wedge^{l}W\\
(v_{1}+w_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(v_{n}+w_{n})&\mapsto&\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(k,n-k)}{\mathrm{sgn}}(\sigma)v_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\sigma(k)}\otimes w_{\sigma(k+1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{\sigma(n)},\end{array}

we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}(V\oplus W),V\oplus W)\cong\big(\oplus_{k+l=n}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}V\otimes\wedge^{l}W,V)\oplus(\oplus_{k+l=n}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}V\otimes\wedge^{l}W,W))\big).

Theorem 4.21
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a vector space. Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}})
,

if and only if
({\mathfrak{g}},\pi,D)
is a differential Lie algebra of weight
\lambda\in{\bf k}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.10
,

(s\pi,D)\in\mathcal{MC}(s\mathfrak{M}\oplus{\mathfrak{A}})
if and only if

[\pi,\pi]_{{\rm NR}}=0
(that is,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)!}l_{k}(s\pi,\underbrace{D,\dots,D}_{(k-1)\ \mathrm{times}})

\displaystyle=l_{2}(s\pi,D)+\frac{\lambda}{2}l_{3}(s\pi,D,D)

\displaystyle=\pi\bar{\circ}D-D\bar{\circ}\pi+\frac{\lambda}{2}l_{3}(s\pi,D,D).

For arbitrary

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\big(\pi\bar{\circ}D-D\bar{\circ}\pi+\frac{\lambda}{2}l_{3}(s\pi,D,D)\big)(x,y)

\displaystyle=[D(x),y]+[x,D(y)]-D([x,y])+\lambda[D(x),D(y)].

is a differential operator of weight
\lambda

∎

Proof.

It suffices to make explicit the differential

For

\displaystyle l_{1}^{(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}})}(sf,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k+1}(\underbrace{(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}}),\cdots,(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}})}_{k\ \mathrm{times}},(sf,g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{2}((s\mu,{\mathrm{d}}),(sf,g))+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k+1}(\underbrace{(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}}),\cdots,(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}})}_{k\ \mathrm{times}},(sf,g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(l_{2}(s\mu,sf),l_{2}(s\mu,g)+l_{3}(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}},g)+l_{2}(sf,{\mathrm{d}})+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k+1}(sf,\underbrace{{\mathrm{d}},\cdots,{\mathrm{d}}}_{k\ \mathrm{times}})\big).

It is easy to see that

-s\partial_{\rm Lie}^{n}(f)

Let us compute

.

, we have

\displaystyle(l_{2}(s\mu,g)+l_{3}(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}},g))(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu,g]_{\rm NR}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})+\lambda\sum\limits_{\tau\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(1,n-1)}\mu\big(g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}\big)\tau^{-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+n-1}\rho(x_{i})g(x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}^{n-1}(-1)^{i+j+n}g([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,\hat{x}_{j},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+n-1}\rho({\mathrm{d}}(x_{i}))g(x_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{n-1}(g)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle l_{2}(sf,{\mathrm{d}})+\sum\limits_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k+1}(sf,\underbrace{{\mathrm{d}},\cdots,{\mathrm{d}}}_{k\ \mathrm{times}})(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(-{\mathrm{d}}\circ f+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda^{k-1}f\bar{\circ}\{\underbrace{{\mathrm{d}},\cdots,{\mathrm{d}}}_{k\ \mathrm{times}}\}\big)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\mathrm{d}}(f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\tau\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(\underbrace{1,\cdots,1}_{k\ \mathrm{times}},n-k)}\lambda^{k-1}f(\underbrace{{\mathrm{d}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathrm{d}}}_{k\ \mathrm{times}}\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes n-k}\big)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(k,n-k)}{\mathrm{sgn}}(\sigma)f_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i_{1}}},\cdots,\hat{x_{i_{k}}},\cdots,x_{n},{\mathrm{d}}(x_{i_{1}}),\cdots,{\mathrm{d}}(x_{i_{k}}))-{\mathrm{d}}(f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{n}(f).

That is, we have

l_{1}^{(s\mu,{\mathrm{d}})}(sf,g)=\big(-s\partial_{\rm Lie}^{n}(f),\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{n-1}(g)+\delta^{n}(f)\big)=-\partial^{n}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}(f,g).

∎

Let
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
be a representation of the differential Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{{\mathfrak{g}}},{\mathrm{d}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}})
with weight
\lambda
.
Now we justify the cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*})
of the differential Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{{\mathfrak{g}}},{\mathrm{d}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
introduced in Definition
1.11
and Proposition
1.9
.

To this end, we consider the trivial extension
{\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes V
of the differential Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathrm{d}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}})
by the representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
in Proposition
1.6
, and we get the complex
({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes V,({\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes V)_{\rm ad}),\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*})
, by Proposition
5.1
. The following result can be proved by direct inspection.

Proposition 5.2
.

The cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*})
of the differential Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{{\mathfrak{g}}},{\mathrm{d}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,{\mathrm{d}}_{V})
introduced in Definition
1.11
is a subcomplex of

({\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes V,({\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes V)_{\rm ad}),\partial_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{*})
.

Proof.

For vector spaces
U
and
W
, we have an isomorphism

\wedge^{p}(U\oplus W)\simeq\wedge^{p}U\oplus(\oplus_{i=1}^{p-1}\wedge^{i}U\otimes\wedge^{p-i}W)\oplus\wedge^{p}W.

Hence,
there exists a natural injection
from

{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}{\mathfrak{g}},V)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}{\mathfrak{g}},V)

to

{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DL}_{\lambda}}({\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes V,({\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes V)_{\rm ad})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V),{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V),{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus V).

Now it suffices to show that this map commutes with differentials.

∎

5.2.
Homotopy differential Lie algebras with weight

In this section, we need to use graded Nijenhuis-Richardson brackets.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a graded vector space and denote
{\mathfrak{l}}=s{\mathfrak{g}}
. Consider the graded vector space
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}):=\mathrm{Hom}(S({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})
.

Recall that by Eq. (
1
),
S^{n}(s{\mathfrak{g}})
is isomorphic to
s^{n}\wedge^{n}{\mathfrak{g}}
.
When
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{0}=V
is ungraded, then
{\rm Hom}(S^{n}(sV),sV)
, which has degree
n-1
, is isomorphic to
{\rm Hom}(\wedge^{n}V,V)
as vector spaces. This justifies why we have imposed degree
n-1
on the latter in Subsection
4.3
.

The graded Nijenhuis-Richardson
bracket
[-,-]_{{\rm NR}}
on the graded vector space
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}})
can be defined as follows:
for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(S^{n}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})
of degree
p
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(S^{m}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})
of degree
q
,

[f,g]_{{\rm NR}}:=f\bar{\circ}g-(-1)^{pq}g\bar{\circ}f,

where
f\bar{\circ}g\in\mathrm{Hom}(S^{m+n-1}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})
of degree
p+q
is defined by

\displaystyle f\bar{\circ}g(v_{1},\dots,v_{m+n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(m,n-1)}\varepsilon(\sigma)f(g(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(m)}),v_{\sigma(m+1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(m+n-1)}),

for
v_{1},\dots,v_{m+n-1}\in{\mathfrak{l}}
.
It is well known that
({\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}),[~,~]_{\rm NR})
is a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements correspond bijectively to
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra structures on
{\mathfrak{l}}
.

We consider the graded Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{L}}:={\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathrm{Lie}}({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g},{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g})

endowed with the graded Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket
[~,~]_{{\rm NR}}
.

Let

{\mathfrak{M}}:=\mathrm{Hom}(\bar{S}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})\ \mathrm{and}\ {\mathfrak{A}}:={\rm Hom}(\bar{S}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}}).

Endow
{\mathfrak{M}}
with the graded Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket and
{\mathfrak{A}}
with the trivial bracket.
With the same analysis in Subsection
4.4
, we have:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a graded vector space.
Then we get a generalised V-datum
({\mathfrak{L}},{\mathfrak{M}},\iota_{\mathfrak{M}},{\mathfrak{A}}
,
\iota_{\mathfrak{A}},P,\Delta=0)

and there is an
L_{\infty}
[1]-algebra
s{\mathfrak{M}}\oplus{\mathfrak{A}}
given below:

l_{2}(sf,sg)=(-1)^{|f|}s[f,g]_{{\rm NR}},\ \ l_{2}(sf,\xi)=[f,\xi]_{{\rm NR}},

and for
3\leq i\leq n+2
,

l_{i}(sf,\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{i-1})=\sum\limits_{\tau\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(m_{i-1}+1,\dots,m_{1}+1,n+2-i)}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}(|\xi_{1}|+\cdots+|\xi_{j-1}|)|\xi_{j}|}\lambda^{i-2}f\big(\xi_{i-1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes n+2-i}\big)\tau^{-1},

for homogeneous elements

f\in{\rm Hom}(S^{n+1}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
,
g\in{\rm Hom}(S^{m+1}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
,
\xi\in{\rm Hom}(S^{m+1}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{A}}
, and
\xi_{j}\in{\rm Hom}(S^{m_{j}+1}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathfrak{l}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{A}}
,
1\leq j\leq i-1
, and all others components vanish.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a graded vector space.
A structure of
homotopy differential Lie algebra with weight

\lambda
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined to be a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
[1]-algebra
s{\mathfrak{M}}\oplus{\mathfrak{A}}
introduced in Proposition
5.3
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a graded vector space and denote
{\mathfrak{l}}=s{\mathfrak{g}}
.
A homotopy differential Lie algebra with weight
\lambda\in{\bf k}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is equivalent to the pair
(\mu=\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\geq 1},D=\{D_{i}\}_{i\geq 1})
, where for each
i\geq 1
,
\mu_{i}:S^{i}({\mathfrak{l}})\to{\mathfrak{l}}
is a degree
1
map such that
({\mathfrak{g}},\mu)
is an
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra and for each
i\geq 1
,
D_{i}:S^{i}({\mathfrak{l}})\to{\mathfrak{l}}
is of degree
0
which form a homotopy differential operator of weight
\lambda
. More precisely, for
n\geq 1
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathfrak{l}}
, we have

(17)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(i,n-i)}\varepsilon(\sigma)\mu_{n-i+1}(\mu_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0,

and

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 2}\sum_{t=p-1}^{n}\sum_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{p-1}=t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(m_{p-1},\cdots,m_{1},n-t)\\
\sigma|_{t}\in\mathrm{PSh}(m_{p-1},\cdots,m_{1})\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(\sigma)\lambda^{p-2}\mu_{n-t+p-1}(D_{m_{p-1}}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m_{p-1})}),\cdots,

\displaystyle\cdots,D_{m_{1}}(x_{\sigma(m_{2}+\cdots+m_{p-1}+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(t)}),x_{\sigma(t+1)}\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(j,n-j)}\varepsilon(\sigma)D_{n-j+1}(\mu_{j}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(j)}),x_{\sigma(j+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0.

Proof.

Let
\mu=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu_{i}:\bar{S}({\mathfrak{l}})\,\rightarrow\,{\mathfrak{l}}
, with
\mu_{i}:S^{i}({\mathfrak{l}})\,\rightarrow\,{\mathfrak{l}}
and
D=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{i}:\bar{S}({\mathfrak{l}})\,\rightarrow\,{\mathfrak{l}}
, with
D_{i}:S^{i}({\mathfrak{l}})\,\rightarrow\,{\mathfrak{l}}
.
Then by Proposition
4.10
,
(s\mu,D)\in\mathcal{MC}(s\mathfrak{M}\oplus{\mathfrak{A}})
is equivalent to

[\mu,\mu]_{{\rm NR}}=0\ \mathrm{and}\ \sum\limits_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)!}l_{p}(s\mu,\underbrace{D,\dots,D}_{(p-1)\ \mathrm{times}})=0.

It is well known that, see for instance,
[
2
, Theorem 4.2]
, that
[\mu,\mu]_{{\rm NR}}=0
if and only if Eq. (
17
) holds if and only if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is an
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)!}l_{p}(s\mu,\underbrace{D,\dots,D}_{(p-1)\ \mathrm{times}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)!}l_{p}(s\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mu_{m},\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}D_{m_{1}},\dots,\sum_{m_{p-1}=1}^{\infty}D_{m_{p-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\sum_{m,m_{1},\dots,m_{p-1}=1}^{\infty}l_{p}(s\mu_{m},D_{m_{1}},\dots,D_{m_{p-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[\mu_{m},D_{j}]_{\rm NR}+\sum_{p=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\sum_{m,m_{1},\dots,m_{p-1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\tau\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(m_{p-1},\dots,m_{1},m+1-p)}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p-1}(|D_{m_{1}}|+\dots+|D_{m_{j-1}}|)|D_{m_{j}}|}

\displaystyle\lambda^{p-2}\mu_{m}(D_{m_{p-1}}\otimes\dots\otimes D_{m_{1}}\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes m+1-p})\tau^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[\mu_{m},D_{j}]_{\rm NR}

\displaystyle+\sum_{p=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\sum_{m,m_{1},\dots,m_{p-1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\tau\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(m_{p-1},\dots,m_{1},m+1-p)}\lambda^{p-2}\mu_{m}(D_{m_{p-1}}\otimes\dots\otimes D_{m_{1}}\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes m+1-p})\tau^{-1},

which is equivalent to Eq. (
18
).
∎

Example 5.6
.

Since Eq. (
17
) is the well known
L_{\infty}[1]
-structure,
we only need to focus on homotopy differential operators.

When
n=1
, Eq. (
18
) gives

\displaystyle\mu_{1}D_{1}=D_{1}\mu_{1}.

which means that
D_{1}
is a cochain map.

When
n=2
, Eq. (
18
) gives

\displaystyle D_{1}(\mu_{2}(x,y))-\mu_{2}(D_{1}(x),y)-\mu_{2}(x,D_{1}(y))-\lambda\mu_{2}(D_{1}(x),D_{1}(y))

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}(D_{2}(x,y))-D_{2}(\mu_{1}(x),y)-D_{2}(x,\mu_{1}(y)).

which means that
D_{1}
is a differential operator (with respect to the multiplication
\mu_{2}
), but only up to the homotopy given by
D_{2}
.

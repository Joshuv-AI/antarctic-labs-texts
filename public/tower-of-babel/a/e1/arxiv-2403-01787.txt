Abstract
. We prove an effective multidimensional Ω \Omega -result of Voronin in short intervals [ T , T + H ] [T,T+H] with T 27 82 ≤ H ≤ T T^{\frac{27}{82}}\leq H\leq T and derive an effective weak universality theorem of the Riemann zeta-function. Furthermore, we provide a few remarks for these results under the assumption of the Riemann hypothesis.

1.
Statement of the Main Results

In 1975, Sergei Voronin
[
vor
]
proved his celebrated universality theorem for the Riemann zeta-function
\zeta
which states that, roughly speaking, every non-vanishing analytic function
g
, defined on a disk
D\coloneqq\{s:|s|\leq r\}
of sufficiently small radius
r
, can be uniformly approximated by shifts
\zeta(s+i\tau)
of the Riemann zeta-function. Moreover, given
\varepsilon>0
, the set of real
\tau>0
satisfying

\max_{s\in D}\bigg|\zeta\Big(s+\frac{3}{4}+i\tau\Big)-g(s)\bigg|<\varepsilon

has a positive lower density. Two years earlier, Voronin
[
voroninj
]
had proved a multi-dimensional denseness theorem which continued earlier work by Harald Bohr and may be considered as a first step towards the later universality theorem (cf.
[
glmss
]
). In 1988, Voronin
[
voronin1989
]
obtained an
effective
version of the latter result which implies a weak effective version of universality as shown by Ramūnas Garunkštis et al.
[
glmss
]
(and which was probably known to Voronin as well). Here
effective
means that there exists an explicit upper bound for the shift
\tau
.

In this note, we extend these results to
short
intervals
[T,T+H]
for the shifts
\tau
, a concept that was recently introduced by Antanas Laurinčikas
[
laurincikas
]
. Note that
short
here means that
H=o(T)
. For treating short intervals, let
N(\alpha;T,H)
be the number of zeros
\beta+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
in the rectangle
\alpha<\beta
and
T\leq t\leq T+H
(counting multiplicities). We shall use an advanced density theorem due to Ramachandran Balasubramanian
[
balasubramanian1978
]
, which is stated that

N(\alpha;T,H)\ll H^{\frac{4(1-\alpha)}{3-2\alpha}}(\log H)^{100},

(1)

uniformly in
\alpha
and
H
, for all
H
satisfying
T^{\frac{27}{82}}\leq H\leq T
.

Our main results are the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{N-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be arbitrary but fixed. Then, the system of inequalities

\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\zeta(s)\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+i\tau}-a_{k}\bigg|<\varepsilon\qquad\mbox{for}\quad k=0,1,\ldots,N-1

has a solution
\tau\in[T,T+H]
provided that
T^{\frac{27}{82}}\leq H\leq T
and

T\geq\exp_{2}\bigg(C_{1}(N,\sigma_{0})\Big(\|\boldsymbol{a}\|+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{8}{1-\sigma_{0}}+{\frac{8}{\sigma_{0}-{\frac{1}{2}}}}}\bigg),

where
C_{1}(N,\sigma_{0})
is a positive, effectively computable constant depending only on
N,\sigma_{0}
(and not
H
!), and
\|\boldsymbol{a}\|\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq k\leq N-1}|a_{k}|
.

Here
\exp_{2}
is the double iterated exponential function (i.e.,
\exp_{2}=\exp\circ\exp
). The logarithm is a multivalued-function. Here, we define, as usual,
\log\zeta
as the principal branch of the logarithm on the real line segment
(1,\infty)
and elsewhere by continuous variation along line segments (as in
[
titchmarsh1986
, p. 210]
).

We also provide a version of this result for the zeta-function in place of its logarithm (see Theorem
5
below).
Applying the first theorem to a partial sum of the Taylor expansion of the zeta-function and an admissible target function, we shall derive

Theorem 2
.

Let
s_{0}=\sigma_{0}+it_{0}
be a fixed complex number with
\sigma_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and for
r>0
let
g\,:\,\mathcal{K}=\{s\in\mathbb{C}:|s-s_{0}|\leq r\}\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous non-vanishing function which is analytic in the interior. Let
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{1,|g(s_{0})|\})
. Assume that
N=N(\delta_{0},\varepsilon)
is a positive integer for which

\Big(\max_{|s-s_{0}|=r}|g(s)|\Big)\frac{\delta_{0}^{N}}{1-\delta_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Then, there exists
\tau\in[T-t_{0},T+H-t_{0}]
for which

T^{\frac{27}{82}}\leq H\leq T
and

\max_{|s-s_{0}|\leq\delta r}\big|\zeta(s+i\tau)-g(s)\big|<\varepsilon

for any
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
satisfying

\Big(\max_{|s-s_{0}|=r}|\zeta(s+i\tau)|\Big)\frac{\delta^{N}}{1-\delta}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

provided that

T\geq\max\bigg\{\exp_{2}\bigg(C_{2}(N,\sigma_{0})\Big(B(N,g,s_{0},\delta_{0},r,\varepsilon)\Big)^{{\frac{8}{1-\sigma_{0}}}+{\frac{8}{\sigma_{0}-{\frac{1}{2}}}}}\bigg),r\bigg\},

Here
C_{2}(N,\sigma_{0})
is a positive, effectively computable constant depending on
N,\sigma_{0}
, and

B(N,g,s_{0},\delta_{0},r,\varepsilon)\coloneqq|\log g(s_{0})|+\frac{(1+|g(s_{0})|)\exp(\delta_{0}r)}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{\|\boldsymbol{G}\|}{|g(s_{0})|}\Big)^{(N-1)^{2}},

where
\|\boldsymbol{G}\|\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq k\leq N-1}\big|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)|_{s=s_{0}}\big|
.

The proofs of these results will be given in the following two sections. Our reasoning follows Voronin’s proof
[
voronin1992
,
voronin1989
]
; note that these sources are not easy to read due to many typos and inaccuracies. In the final section, we consider what can be proved under the assumption of the Riemann hypothesis and conclude with a few final remarks.

2.
The Proof of Theorem
1

Let
\Omega
be the set of all infinite vectors
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\dots,\theta_{p},\dots)
, where each
\theta_{p}
is a real number and the index
p
runs through the set of all primes
\mathbb{P}
in ascending order.
We begin with an auxiliary result and introduce the following notation: let
\widehat{Q}
denote the set of prime numbers
p\leq Q
and

\zeta_{\mathcal{P}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta})\coloneqq\prod_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}\Big(1-\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p})}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}

(2)

for a set of primes
{\mathcal{P}}
. Our auxiliary result can be stated as follows:

Proposition 3
.

Under the assumptions from Theorem
1
, there exists an effective computable constant
c_{1}(\sigma_{0},N)>0
(in terms of functions of
\sigma_{0}
and
N
) such that if

Q\geq c_{1}(\sigma_{0},N)\Big(\|\boldsymbol{a}\|+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{8}{1-\sigma_{0}}+\frac{8}{\sigma_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}},

(3)

then there exists
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}=(\theta_{2}^{(0)},\theta_{3}^{(0)},\dots,\theta_{p}^{(0)},\dots)\in\Omega
such that

\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\zeta_{\widehat{Q}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-a_{k}\bigg|<\varepsilon

for
k=0,1,\dots,N-1
.

Proof.

Define
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}=(\theta_{2}^{(1)},\theta_{3}^{(1)}\dots,\theta_{p}^{(1)},\dots)\coloneqq(0,{\frac{1}{2}},0,{\frac{1}{2}},\ldots)
. Observe that for each
k=0,1,\dots,N-1
, the series

\sum_{p}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}

converges to a complex number, which we will denote by
\gamma_{k}
.
For a non-empty set of primes
{\mathcal{P}}
, define

\varphi_{\mathcal{P}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta})\coloneqq\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p})}{p^{s}}.

Let
U_{0}>200^{N}
and put
U_{j}\coloneqq U_{0}2^{j}
for
j<N
. Furthermore, let
V<U_{0}
and
\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=0}^{N-1}M_{j}
, where

M_{j}\coloneqq\big\{p\in\mathbb{P}:U_{j}\leq p<U_{j}+V\big\}

for
j=0,1,\dots,N-1
.
Then, for each
j,k=0,1,\dots,N-1
, we have

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial s^{k}}\varphi_{M_{j}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta})=(-\log U_{j})^{k}\varphi_{M_{j}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta})+R_{j,k},

where

R_{j,k}\ll_{N}\frac{V^{2}(\log U_{0})^{N-1}}{U_{0}^{\sigma_{0}+1}}

(4)

follows from the mean-value theorem.
Here and in the sequel
\ll_{N}
means that the implicit constant depends only on
N
.
We consider the following system of linear equations in the unknowns
z_{j}
:

\sum_{0\leq j<N}(-\log U_{j})^{k}z_{j}=a_{k}-\gamma_{k}

for
k=0,1,\dots,N-1
. Observe that the coefficient matrix of this system is a Vandermonde matrix with a non-vanishing determinant. Applying Cramer’s rule, there exists a unique solution
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{N-1})
satisfying

\|\boldsymbol{z}\|\ll_{N}(\log U_{0})^{N-1}\|\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}\|,

(5)

where
\|\boldsymbol{z}\|\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|z_{k}|
and
\|\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}\|\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|a_{k}-\gamma_{k}|
. A precise detail can be found in
[
endo2023
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Next, we determine how
U_{0}
must be chosen in order that the equations

\varphi_{M_{j}}(\sigma_{0},\boldsymbol{\theta})=z_{j}

(6)

for
j=0,1,\dots,N-1
can be solved for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.
If
M_{j}
contains at least three primes, then the set of values of
\varphi_{M_{j}}(\sigma_{0},\boldsymbol{\theta})
as
\boldsymbol{\theta}
varies is a disk of radius

\sum_{p\in M_{j}}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma_{0}}}.

(7)

We need a lower bound for this quantity. This follows from a geometric argument; see
[
titchmarsh1986
, §11.5]
.

For this purpose, we apply a prime number theorem for short intervals due to Martin Huxley. If
\pi(x)
is counting the prime numbers
p\leq x
, then

\pi(x+h)-\pi(x)\sim\frac{h}{\log x}

(8)

for any
h\geq x^{\theta}
with
\theta>{\frac{7}{12}}
as
x\to\infty
; a proof can be found in
[
huxley
, Chapter 28]
.

Using this in combination with partial summation implies that if
U_{0}
and
V
satisfy

U_{0}\gg_{N,\sigma_{0}}\big(\|\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}\|+1\big)^{\frac{8}{1-\sigma_{0}}}\quad\text{and}\quad V\coloneqq U_{0}^{\frac{1+3\sigma_{0}}{4}},

(9)

then

\sum_{p\in M_{j}}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma_{0}}}\gg_{N}\sum_{p\in M_{j}}\frac{1}{U_{0}^{\sigma_{0}}}\gg_{N}\frac{V}{U_{0}^{\sigma_{0}}\log U_{0}}\gg_{N,\sigma_{0}}(\log U_{0})^{N-1}(\|\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}\|+1).

(10)

Note that
\frac{1+3\sigma_{0}}{4}>\frac{7}{12}
, hence Huxley’s prime number theorem is applicable.

In view of (
5
), (
7
) and (
10
) it follows that the system of linear equations (
6
) can be solved for
\boldsymbol{\theta}=\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\coloneqq(\theta_{2}^{(2)},\theta_{3}^{(2)},\dots,\theta_{p}^{(2)},\dots)
provided that (
9
) holds.
In addition, if

U_{0}\gg_{N,\sigma_{0}}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{4}{1-\sigma_{0}}},

(11)

then (
4
) implies that

\sum_{0\leq j<N}|R_{j,k}|\ll_{N}U_{0}^{-\frac{1-\sigma_{0}}{2}}(\log U_{0})^{N-1}\ll_{N,\sigma_{0}}\varepsilon.

Thus, the condition that
U_{0}
satisfies (
9
) and (
11
) implies

\bigg|\sum_{0\leq j<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\varphi_{M_{j}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-(a_{k}-\gamma_{k})\bigg|=\bigg|\sum_{0\leq j<N}R_{j,k}\bigg|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Since
\gamma_{k}
is an absolute constant, it suffices to assume

U_{0}\gg_{N,\sigma_{0}}\Big(\|\boldsymbol{a}\|+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{8}{1-\sigma_{0}}}

(12)

instead of (
9
) and (
11
).

Obviously, the logarithm of
\zeta_{\mathcal{P}}
is related to
\varphi_{\mathcal{P}}
.
It follows from the Taylor expansion and partial summation that

\bigg|\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial s^{k}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p})}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial s^{k}}\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p})}{p^{s}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|\ll_{N}\frac{(\log p)^{k}}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}.

(13)

This implies that for any
j=0,1,\dots,N-1
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial s^{k}}\log\zeta_{M_{j}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta})\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial s^{k}}\varphi_{M_{j}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\ll_{N}\sum_{p\in M_{j}}\frac{(\log p)^{k}}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}\ll_{N}U_{0}^{-\frac{3}{8}}(\log U_{0})^{N-1}.

Hence, by possibly increasing the implicit constant, the system of inequalities

\bigg|\sum_{0\leq j<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log{\zeta_{M_{j}}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-(a_{k}-\gamma_{k})\bigg|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

(14)

is solvable provided (
12
) holds.

We claim that for
Q>U_{0}2^{N}
,

\sum_{p\in\widehat{Q}\setminus\mathcal{M}}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}=\gamma_{k}+O_{N}\Big(U_{0}^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log U_{0})^{N}\Big).

(15)

To see this, it suffices to show that

\bigg\{\sum_{p>Q}+\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}}\bigg\}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\ll_{N}U_{0}^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log U_{0})^{N}.

By (
13
), the prime number theorem, and partial summation, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\{\sum_{p>Q}+\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}}\bigg\}\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{p>Q}\frac{(\log p)^{k}}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}+\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{(\log p)^{k}}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}\ll Q^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{k-1}+U_{0}^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log U_{0})^{N-1}.

In addition, it is not difficult to see that

\sum_{p>Q}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}=\sum_{p>Q}\frac{(-\log p)^{k}\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{\sigma_{0}}}\ll\frac{(\log Q)^{k}}{Q^{\sigma_{0}}}

and

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{(-\log p)^{k}\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{\sigma_{0}}}\ll_{N}\frac{(\log U_{0})^{k}}{U_{0}^{\sigma_{0}}}.

Hence, we obtain (
15
) and then we have

\bigg|\sum_{p\in\widehat{Q}\setminus\mathcal{M}}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\exp(-2\pi i\theta_{p}^{(1)})}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-\gamma_{k}\bigg|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

(16)

provided that

U_{0}\gg_{N,\sigma_{0}}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

(17)

Consequently, we choose
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}\coloneqq(\theta_{2}^{(0)},\theta_{3}^{(0)},\dots,\theta_{p}^{(0)},\dots)
defined by

\theta_{p}^{(0)}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\theta_{p}^{(1)}&\text{for }p\in\widehat{Q}\setminus\mathcal{M},\\
\theta_{p}^{(2)}&\text{for }p\in\mathcal{M}.\end{cases}

Then, by (
14
) and (
16
), we obtain, for
k=0,\dots,N-1
, that

\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\zeta_{\widehat{Q}}(s,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}-a_{k}\bigg|<\varepsilon

provided that (
12
) and (
17
) hold. Thus, we prove the proposition.
∎

Following the original proof of Voronin, the next step involves some Fourier analysis. To do this, let
\lambda
be a real-valued, infinitely differentiable function satisfying

0\leq\lambda(x)\leq 1,\quad\supp\lambda\subseteq[-1,1],\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lambda(x)dx=1.

For
\delta=\delta(Q)\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\theta_{p}\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
, define

L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\coloneqq\prod_{p\in\widehat{Q}}\lambda_{\delta}(\theta_{p}),

where
\lambda_{\delta}
is given by

\lambda_{\delta}(\theta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\delta}\lambda\Big(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\Big).

Recall that
\widehat{Q}
is the set of all primes not exceeding
Q
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\dots,\theta_{p},\dots)\in\Omega
.
Now we extend
L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
to all of
\Omega
by periodicity with period
1
in each variable
\theta_{p}
. Then, the function
\frac{1}{\delta}\lambda(\frac{\theta}{\delta})
, also extended with period
1
to
\mathbb{R}
, can be represented as a Fourier series

\frac{1}{\delta}\lambda\Big(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\Big)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\exp(2\pi in\theta),

where
\alpha_{0}=1
and
\alpha_{n}\ll n^{-2}\delta^{-3}
for every non-zero integer
n
. The last relation follows from integration by parts twice and the implicit constant depends only on our choice of
\lambda
. It thus follows that
L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
has a Fourier expansion,

L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}}\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}\exp(2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\theta}\rangle),

where
\boldsymbol{n}
is extended from
(n_{p})_{p\in\widehat{Q}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\pi(Q)}
by setting
n_{p}\coloneqq 0
for all primes
p>Q
and

\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}\coloneqq\prod_{p\in\widehat{Q}}\alpha_{n_{p}}.

Hence, for every positive integer
M
,

\displaystyle L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\sum_{\max|n_{p}|\leq M}\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}\exp\big(2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{n},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}\rangle\big)+

\displaystyle+O\bigg(\pi(Q)\Big(\sum_{|n_{p}|>M}|\alpha_{n_{p}}|\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\alpha_{n}|\Big)^{\pi(Q)-1}\bigg).

Obviously,

\sum_{|n|>M}|\alpha_{n}|\ll\frac{1}{\delta^{3}M}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n}|\alpha_{n}|\ll\frac{1}{\delta^{3}},

as well as

\beta_{\boldsymbol{0}}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}\ll\prod_{p\leq Q}\min\{1,\delta^{-3}n_{p}^{-2}\}.

Hence, we have

L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max{|n_{p}|}\leq M\\
\boldsymbol{n}\neq\boldsymbol{0}\end{subarray}}\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}\exp(2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\theta}\rangle)+O\bigg(\frac{Q\exp(3\pi(Q)\log{\frac{1}{\delta}})}{M\log Q}\bigg)

(18)

and

\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}}|\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}|\ll\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\min\{1,\delta^{-3}n^{-2}\}\bigg)^{\pi(Q)}\ll\exp(3Q).

(19)

Our next result deals with the integral of
L_{Q}
with respect to a uniformly distributed curve. For this purpose, define the curve
\boldsymbol{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}\to\Omega
by

\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)\coloneqq\left(\frac{\log 2}{2\pi}t,\frac{\log 3}{2\pi}t,\dots,\frac{\log p}{2\pi}t,\dots\right).

(20)

Proposition 4
.

According to the above notation, let
\delta=Q^{-1}
,
T^{\nu}\leq H\leq T
, where
0<\nu<1
, and
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega
. Then, for
M\gg Q^{2}\exp(3Q)
and
T\gg\big(Q\exp((3+M)Q)\big)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}
,

\bigg|\frac{1}{H}\int_{T}^{T+H}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)dt-1\bigg|<\frac{1}{Q}.

Proof.

We begin with substituting
\delta=Q^{-1}
in the Fourier expansion (
18
) and using the prime number theorem to obtain

L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta})=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max{|n_{p}|}\leq M\\
\boldsymbol{n}\neq\boldsymbol{0}\end{subarray}}\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}\exp\big(2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\rangle\big)+O\Big(\frac{Q\exp(3Q)}{M\log Q}\Big).

(21)

To continue, recall that

\sum_{0<m<n<T}\frac{1}{m^{\sigma}n^{\sigma}\log nm^{-1}}\ll T^{2-2\sigma}\log T

(22)

for
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq 1
, and uniformly for
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{0}<1
. Moreover,

\Big|\log\frac{m}{n}\Big|>\frac{1}{\max(m,n)}

(23)

for distinct positive integers
m,n
(see
[
titchmarsh1986
, §7.2, p. 139 and §8.8, p. 193]
).

Then, for each
\boldsymbol{n}
such that
\max|n_{p}|\leq M
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{T+H}\exp\big(2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\rangle\big)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{T+H}\exp\bigg(2\pi i\Big(\sum_{p\leq Q}\frac{n_{p}(\log p)t}{2\pi}+n_{p}\theta_{p}\Big)\bigg)dt

\displaystyle\ll\bigg|\int_{T}^{T+H}\exp\Big(it\sum_{p\leq Q}n_{p}\log p\Big)dt\bigg|

\displaystyle\ll\bigg(\sum_{p\leq Q}n_{p}\log p\bigg)^{-1}\ll\exp(MQ)

(24)

by (
23
) and the prime number theorem.
Hence, by (
19
) and (
21
), we get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{H}\int_{T}^{T+H}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)dt-1

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\exp(MQ)}{H}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max{|n_{p}|}\leq M\\
\boldsymbol{n}\neq\boldsymbol{0}\end{subarray}}|\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}|\bigg)+\frac{Q\exp(3Q)}{M\log Q}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\exp((3+M)Q)}{H}+\frac{Q\exp(3Q)}{M\log Q}.

For
M\gg Q^{2}\exp(3Q)
and
T\gg\big(Q\exp\big((3+M)Q\big)\big)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}
, we deduce the desired inequality.
∎

For the next step, assume that there is
\sigma^{*}\in(\frac{1}{2},\sigma_{0})
such that
\omega(\sigma^{*})<1
, where

N(\alpha;T,H)\ll H^{\omega(\alpha)}(\log H)^{\eta(\alpha)}

(25)

uniformly in
\alpha
and
H
, for all
H
satisfying
T^{\nu}\leq H\leq T
. We consider the integral

\displaystyle I\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{D_{T,H}}\sum_{0\leq k<N}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\zeta(s)\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}-\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}(\log\zeta_{\widehat{Q}}(s+it,\boldsymbol{0}))\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|^{2}dt,

where
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega
and
D_{T,H}\subseteq[T,T+H]
is defined as follows: Around every hypothetical exceptional zero
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
in
\sigma>\sigma^{*}
, where we write
s=\sigma+it
, as usual, we draw a rectangle

P_{\rho}^{(h)}\coloneqq\big\{s=\sigma+it:\sigma^{*}<\sigma<2,|t-\gamma|\leq h\big\},

where
h\in[10,H)
is a parameter. Then, define

D_{T,H}\coloneqq\Big\{t\in[T,T+H]:\sigma_{0}+it\notin\bigcup_{\rho}P_{\rho}^{(h)}\Big\}.

Our aim is to bound
I
from above, for suitable
Q
and
T
, by

I\ll_{N,\sigma,\nu}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)dt.

For this purpose, we use an explicit formula due to Atle Selberg
[
selbe
]
, that is

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n\leq x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}+\frac{x^{2(1-s)}-x^{1-s}}{(1-s)^{2}\log x}+

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{q\geq 1}\frac{x^{-2q-s}-x^{-2(2q+s)}}{(2q+s)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}-x^{2(\rho-s)}}{(s-\rho)^{2}},

(26)

where

\Lambda_{x}(n)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\Lambda(n)&\text{if $1\leq n<x$},\\
\Lambda(n){\frac{\log(x^{2}/n)}{\log x}}&\text{if $x\leq n\leq x^{2}$},\\
0&\text{if $n>x^{2}$},\end{cases}

and
\Lambda
is the von Mangoldt
\Lambda
-function (see
[
titchmarsh1986
]
).
Integrating this identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\log\zeta(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}\log n}+\sum_{n>x}\frac{\Lambda(n)-\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s+10}\log n}+

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{\log x}F(s,1)+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\rho}F(s,\rho)+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{q\geq 1}F(s,-2q),

(27)

where

F(s,z)\coloneqq\int_{s+10}^{s}\frac{x^{z-w}-x^{2(z-w)}}{(w-z)^{2}}dw.

When
k=1,2,\dots,N-1
, we use (
26
) to estimate
I
, and when
k=0
, we use (
27
).

We choose
Q,x
and
T
such that
Q<x\ll H^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Defining

I_{k}\coloneqq\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\zeta(s)\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}-\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\log\zeta_{\widehat{Q}}(s+it,\boldsymbol{0})\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|^{2}dt,

we have

By (
26
), we conclude that

I_{k}\ll A_{k}+B_{k}+C_{k}+D_{k}+E_{k},

where

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{m\leq x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(m)}{m^{s}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}-\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{p^{\ell}\leq Q}\frac{\log p}{p^{\ell(s+it)}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|^{2}dt,

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\ell}>Q\\
p\leq Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{\ell(s+it)}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|^{2}dt,

\displaystyle C_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{q\geq 1}\frac{x^{-2q-s}-x^{-2(2q+s)}}{(2q+s)^{2}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}\bigg|^{2}dt,

\displaystyle D_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta})\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\frac{x^{2(1-s)}-x^{1-s}}{(1-s)^{2}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}\bigg|^{2}dt,

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta})\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}-x^{2(\rho-s)}}{(s-\rho)^{2}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}\bigg|^{2}dt.

We assume the assumption in Proposition
4
: let
\delta=Q^{-1}
,
M\gg Q^{2}\exp(3Q)
and
T\gg(Q\exp((3+M)Q))^{\frac{1}{\nu}}
.
From the definition of
\Lambda_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{m\leq x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(m)}{m^{s}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}-\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{p^{\ell}\leq Q}\frac{\log p}{p^{\ell(s+it)}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q<p\leq x^{2}}\frac{a_{p}}{p^{{\sigma_{0}+it}}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q<p^{\ell}\leq x^{2}\\
\ell\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{p,\ell}}{p^{\ell(\sigma_{0}+it)}}

with implicitly defined coefficients
a_{p}
and
a_{p,\ell}
. We define
A_{k,1}
and
A_{k,2}
according to this splitting such that

A_{k}\ll A_{k,1}+A_{k,2}.

To estimate
A_{k,1}
, we use again (
22
) and (

). These give, in combination with partial summation and the prime number theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{T+H}

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{Q<p\leq x^{2}}\frac{a_{p}}{p^{\sigma_{0}+it}}\bigg|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q<p_{1},p_{2}\leq x^{2}}\frac{a_{p_{1}}\overline{a}_{p_{2}}}{(p_{1}p_{2})^{\sigma_{0}}}\bigg|\int_{T}^{T+H}\exp\big(-it\log(p_{1}p_{2}^{-1})\big)dt\bigg|

\displaystyle=H\sum_{Q<p\leq x^{2}}\frac{|a_{p}|^{2}}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}+O\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q<p_{1},p_{2}\leq x^{2}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|a_{p_{1}}||a_{p_{2}}|}{(p_{1}p_{2})^{\sigma_{0}}\log(p_{1}p_{2}^{-1})}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll_{N}HQ^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{2k-1}+x^{2(2-2\sigma_{0})}(\log x)^{2k+1}.

In view of (
24
) we note that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{T}^{T+H}\exp\big(2\pi i\langle\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\rangle\big)\Big|\sum_{Q<p\leq x^{2}}\frac{a_{p}}{p^{\sigma_{0}+it}}\Big|^{2}dt\bigg|\ll x^{4}(\log x)^{2k}Q^{M\pi(Q)-2\sigma_{0}}.

By Fourier expansion (
21
) together with (
19
), the prime number theorem and
M\gg Q^{2}\exp(3Q)
, we have

\displaystyle A_{k,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{T}^{T+H}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)\bigg|\sum_{Q<p\leq x^{2}}\frac{a_{p}}{p^{\sigma_{0}+it}}\bigg|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll_{N}HQ^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{2k}+x^{2(2-2\sigma_{0})}(\log x)^{2k+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+x^{4}(\log x)^{2k}Q^{M\pi(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max{|n_{p}|}\leq M\\
\boldsymbol{n}\neq\boldsymbol{0}\end{subarray}}|\beta_{\boldsymbol{n}}|

\displaystyle\ll_{N}HQ^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{2k}+x^{4}(\log x)^{2k+1}\exp\big((3+M)Q\big).

To estimate
A_{k,2}
, we apply partial summation together with the prime number theorem, and then get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q<p^{\ell}<x^{2}\\
\ell\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{p,\ell}}{p^{\ell({\sigma_{0}+it})}}

\displaystyle\ll_{N}\sum_{p\leq\sqrt{Q}}\sum_{\ell>\frac{\log Q}{\log p}}\frac{(\ell\log p)^{k}}{p^{\ell\sigma_{0}}}+\sum_{p>\sqrt{Q}}\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{(\ell\log p)^{k}}{p^{\ell\sigma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\ll_{N}\sum_{p\leq\sqrt{Q}}\frac{(\log p)^{k}}{Q^{\sigma_{0}}}+\sum_{p>\sqrt{Q}}\frac{(\log p)^{k}}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}\ll_{N}Q^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{k-1}.

Hence,

A_{k}\ll_{N}HQ^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{2k}+x^{4}(\log x)^{2k+1}\exp\big((3+M)Q\big).

(28)

Next, we shall estimate
B_{k}
. By partial summation formula and the prime number theorem, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{p^{\ell}>Q}\\
p\leq Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{\ell(s+it)}}\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p^{\ell}>Q}\frac{\ell^{k-1}(\log p)^{k}}{p^{\ell\sigma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p\leq\sqrt{Q}}\sum_{\ell>\frac{\log Q}{\log p}}\frac{\ell^{k-1}(\log p)^{k}}{p^{\ell\sigma_{0}}}+\sum_{p>\sqrt{Q}}\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{\ell^{k-1}(\log p)^{k}}{p^{\ell\sigma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\ll_{N}Q^{{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{k-1}.

By a trivial estimation, we thus obtain

B_{k}\ll_{N}Q^{1-2\sigma_{0}}(\log Q)^{2k-2}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta})dt.

(29)

It remains to estimate integrals of the form

By the Cauchy integral formula, the estimation

\displaystyle\max_{t\in D_{T,H}}\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}f(s)\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in D_{T,H}}\bigg|\frac{(k-1)!}{2\pi i}\oint_{\widetilde{D}_{\sigma_{0}+it}}\frac{f(z)}{(z-\sigma_{0}-it)^{k}}dz\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{N,\sigma_{0}}\max_{s\in\widetilde{D}}|f(s)|^{2}

holds for any holomorphic function
f(s)
on

\widetilde{D}\coloneqq\Big\{s=\sigma+it:|\sigma-\sigma_{0}|\leq r_{\sigma_{0}},\Big|t-T-\frac{H}{2}\Big|\leq\frac{H}{2}+r_{\sigma_{0}},\text{ and }s\notin\bigcup_{\rho}P_{\rho}^{(h-r_{\sigma_{0}})}\Big\},

where
r_{\sigma_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{*}+\sigma_{0})

and
\widetilde{D}_{\sigma_{0}+it}
is a circle with center
\sigma_{0}+it
and radius

.
By a trivial estimation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)\bigg|\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}f(s)\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it}

\displaystyle\bigg|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll_{N,\sigma_{0}}\max_{s\in\widetilde{D}}|f(s)|^{2}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}\big(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta}\big)dt.

and

. Note that

\bigg|\sum_{q\geq 1}\frac{x^{-2q-s}-x^{-2(2q+s)}}{(2q+s)^{2}}\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{(T-r_{\sigma_{0}})^{2}}\Big(\frac{x^{-2-\sigma}}{1-x^{-2}}+\frac{x^{-4-2\sigma}}{1-x^{-4}}\Big)\ll x^{-{\frac{5}{2}}}T^{-2}

for every

. This implies that

C_{k}\ll_{N,\sigma_{0}}\frac{1}{x^{5}T^{4}(\log x)^{2}}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta})dt

(30)

and

D_{k}\ll_{N,\sigma_{0}}\frac{x^{2}}{T^{4}(\log x)^{2}}\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta})dt.

(31)

It remains to consider

.
For each

, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{*}+\sigma_{0})\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle|s-\rho|^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq n\leq 2T+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta<\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{*}+\sigma_{0})\end{subarray}}|s-\rho|^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq n\leq 2T+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\

Hence, for each

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}

\displaystyle\frac{x^{\rho-s}-x^{2(\rho-s)}}{(s-\rho)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\

\beta<\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{*}+\sigma_{0})\end{subarray}}|s-\rho|^{-2}

E_{k}\ll_{N,\sigma_{0}}\Big(\frac{x^{2}(\log T)^{2}}{h^{2}(\log x)^{2}}+x^{\sigma^{*}-\sigma_{0}}(\log T)^{2}\Big)\int_{D_{T,H}}L_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)-\boldsymbol{\theta})dt.

The
weak
universality result for the Riemann zeta-function was first published in
[
glmss
]
. Recently, the result for other functions for long intervals has been done by Kenta Endo
[
endo2023
]
(which also contains a small correction of a small inaccuracy in
[
glmss
]
); here we derive the result of the Riemann zeta-function which is restricted to short intervals.

First of all, we provide a version of Theorem
1
for the Riemann zeta-function in place of its logarithm. The proof follows easily from the original paper.

Theorem 5
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{N-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
with
b_{0}\neq 0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be arbitrary but fixed. Then the system of inequalities

\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\zeta(s)\Big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+i\tau}-b_{k}\bigg|<\varepsilon\qquad\mbox{for}\quad k=0,1,\ldots,N-1

has a solution
\tau\in[T,T+H]
provided that
T^{\frac{27}{82}}\leq H\leq T
and

T\geq\exp_{2}\bigg(C_{3}(N,\sigma_{0})\Big(|\log b_{0}|+\frac{1+|b_{0}|}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{\|\boldsymbol{b}\|}{|b_{0}|}\Big)^{(N-1)^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{8}{1-\sigma_{0}}+{\frac{8}{\sigma_{0}-{\frac{1}{2}}}}}\bigg),

where
C_{3}(N,\sigma_{0})
is a positive, effectively computable constant depending only on
N
,
\sigma
and
\|\boldsymbol{b}\|\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq k\leq N-1}|b_{k}|
.

We now come to the proof of Theorem
2
which involves the Taylor’s expansion of
g
and Theorem
5
. By the Cauchy integral formula

\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-s_{0}|=r}\frac{g(z)}{(z-s_{0})^{k+1}}dz,

we obtain that

\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}(s-s_{0})^{k}\bigg|\leq k!M_{g}\delta_{0}^{k}

for
|s-s_{0}|\leq\delta_{0}r
, where
M_{g}\coloneqq\max_{|s-s_{0}|=r}|g(s)|
. Hence, by Taylor’s expansion, we have

\bigg|g(s)-\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}\frac{(s-s_{0})^{k}}{k!}\bigg|\leq M_{g}\sum_{k\geq N}\delta_{0}^{k}=M_{g}\frac{\delta_{0}^{N}}{1-\delta_{0}}

for
|s-s_{0}|\leq\delta_{0}r
. We choose
N
(depending on
g,\delta_{0}
) for which

M_{g}\frac{\delta_{0}^{N}}{1-\delta_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Thus, for
|s-s_{0}|\leq\delta_{0}r
, we have

\bigg|g(s)-\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}\frac{(s-s_{0})^{k}}{k!}\bigg|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

(41)

Next, by Theorem
5
, for any
\delta_{1}\in(0,1)
,
there exists a number
t_{1}\in[T,T+H]
such that

\Bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\zeta(s)\big|_{s=\sigma_{0}+it_{1}}-\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)\big|_{s=s_{0}}\Bigg|<\delta_{1}

for
k=0,1,\dots,N-1
and

T\geq\exp_{2}\bigg(C_{2}(N,\sigma_{0})\Big(|\log g(s_{0})|+\frac{1+|g(s_{0})|}{\delta_{1}}\Big(\frac{\|\boldsymbol{G}\|}{|g(s_{0})|}\Big)^{(N-1)^{2}}\Big)^{{\frac{8}{1-\sigma_{0}}}+{\frac{8}{\sigma_{0}-{\frac{1}{2}}}}}\bigg),

where

\|\boldsymbol{G}\|\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq k\leq N-1}\big|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)|_{s=s_{0}}\big|
.
Put
\tau\coloneqq t_{1}-t_{0}
. Then
\sigma_{0}+i\tau=s_{0}+it_{1}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\zeta(s)\Big|_{s=s_{0}+i\tau}\frac{(s-s_{0})^{k}}{k!}-\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}g(s)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}\frac{(s-s_{0})^{k}}{k!}\bigg|\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle<\delta_{1}\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{(\delta_{0}r)^{k}}{k!}=\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

(42)

provided that
|s-s_{0}|\leq\delta_{0}r
and

\delta_{1}\coloneqq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\exp(-\delta_{0}r)\in(0,1)
.

Finally, we use the Taylor expansion (again) for
\zeta(s)
on the shifted disk
\mathcal{K}+i\tau
. Here, we need to exclude all poles. Hence, we assume that
T>r
.
By the Cauchy integral formula (again)

\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\zeta(s+i\tau)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-s_{0}|=r}\frac{\zeta(z+i\tau)}{(z-s_{0})^{k+1}}dz,

we obtain that

\bigg|\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\zeta(s+i\tau)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}(s-s_{0})^{k}\bigg|\leq k!M_{\zeta}(\tau)\delta^{k}

for
|s-s_{0}|\leq\delta r
and
0\leq\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, where
M_{\zeta}(\tau)\coloneqq\max_{|s-s_{0}|=r}|\zeta(s+i\tau)|
. By Taylor’s expansion, we have

\bigg|\zeta(s+i\tau)-\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\zeta(s+i\tau)\Big|_{s=s_{0}}\frac{(s-s_{0})^{k}}{k!}\bigg|\leq M_{\zeta}(\tau)\frac{\delta^{N}}{1-\delta}

for
|s-s_{0}|\leq\delta r
. Choosing
\delta
for which

M_{\zeta}(\tau)\frac{\delta^{N}}{1-\delta}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},

we have

\bigg|\zeta(s+i\tau)-\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\zeta(s)\Big|_{s=s_{0}+i\tau}\frac{(s-s_{0})^{k}}{k!}\bigg|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

(43)

Combining (
41
), (
42
) and (
43
) yields Theorem
2
.

4.
Concluding Remarks

Since the domain of approximation is restricted and there is no information about the size of the set of shifts, Theorem
2
is considered a
weak
universality theorem for short intervals. The more advanced though ineffective case of universality in the sense of Voronin’s result from
[
vor
]
was considered first by
[
laurincikas
]
with respect to short intervals, and in a recent note
[
short
]
conditional and unconditional improvements were given.

The results in
[
short
]
and this note focus on different points. More precisely, the results in
[
short
]
focus on an ineffective case based on the bounded mean-square for
\zeta(s)
together with the result of Bourgain and Watt
[
BourWatt2017
]
(and the method of exponent pairs) for unconditional results; the result of Sankaranarayanan and Srinivas
[
SanSri1993
]
for conditional results. Meanwhile, those in this note focus on an effective case based on multidimensional
\Omega
-result of Voronin.

Observe that the proof of Theorem
1
deals with hypothetical zeros to the right and this proof should be much easier if there are no such zeros. It is reasonable to study the same under conditional assumptions such as the unproven
Riemann hypothesis
, which implies that there are no zeros to the right of the critical line, i.e.
N(\sigma^{*};T,H)=0
for all
\sigma^{*}>\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, under the
Riemann hypothesis
, our results for short interval
[T,T+H]
still hold but the length of intervals becomes for every
H
satisfying

T^{\nu}\leq H\leq T

(for any fixed
\nu>0
) and its effective constants depend on parameters
N,\sigma_{0}
, and
\nu
.

On this note, the constants in the main results are effectively computable. Explicit bounds, however, are not too easy to establish. The probably hardest work would be to make the error term in Huxley’s prime number theorem (
8
) explicit.

Finally, this can also be done for elements of the Selberg class but needs some slightly more advanced details; for long intervals, this has recently been done by Kenta Endo
[
endo2023
]
.

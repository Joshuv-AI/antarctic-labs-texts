Motivic Modularity of CM K3 Surfaces
Rikuto Ito

Abstract
. The main result of this paper is the explicit construction of algebraic Hecke characters of K3 surfaces defined over a number field k ⊂ ℂ k\subset\mathbb{C} with complex multiplication. In particular, we give the explicit determination of the algebraic Hecke characters of Kummer surfaces associated to simple abelian surfaces with complex multiplication.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
n
with CM (complex multiplication) defined over a number field
k\subset\mathbb{C}
. Shimura and Taniyama
[
25
]
showed that there exist algebraic Hecke characters
\chi_{1},\cdots,\chi_{2n}
over
k
such that

\displaystyle L(s,A)=\prod^{2n}_{i=1}L(s,\chi_{i}),

(1.1)

where
L(s,A)
is the Hasse-Weil
L
-function of
A
and
L(s,\chi_{i})
are the Hecke
L
-functions of
\chi_{i}
. This statement is a consequence of
the main theorem of complex multiplication
for abelian varieties.

Piatetski-Shapiro and Shafarevich
[
21
]
proved an analogue of (1.1) for
K3 surfaces with complex multiplication (CM K3 surfaces, in short)
using their Kuga-Satake constructions and Shimura-Taniyama’s result.

In some arithmetic problems, e.g., the calculation of Brauer groups, it is important to know the explicit description of the algebraic Hecke characters. The explicit descriptions of Hecke characters associated to CM K3 surfaces have been obtained in the following cases:

•

K3 surfaces with non-symplectic group actions.
Livné, Schütt, and Yui
[
14
]
discussed the fields of definition and the Hecke characters of K3 surfaces with non-symplectic group actions. In such a class, K3 surfaces with the minimal Picard rank are of CM type (Example
2.20
(2)). They showed that the fields of definition of these K3 surfaces are cyclotomic fields over
\mathbb{Q}
and the associated algebraic Hecke characters are given by
Jacobi sums
.

•

Kummer surfaces associated to the self-product of a CM elliptic curve.
Deuring
[
6
]
constructed the algebraic Hecke characters for CM elliptic curves defined over number fields, and proved the equality (1.1) for
n=1
. Shioda and Inose
[
26
]
showed that the Hecke characters of the Kummer surface associated to the self-product of a CM elliptic curve are given by the square of the Hecke character of the CM elliptic curve.

•

Kummer surfaces associated to the product of two CM elliptic curves.
Rizov
[
23
]
proved the main theorem of complex multiplication for the products of two elliptic curves. Using the Kummer construction, one can compute the algebraic Hecke characters of the associated Kummer surfaces.

•

Certain K3 surfaces with Picard rank 16.

Costa, Elsenhans, Jahnel, and Voight
[
3
]
introduced three explicit K3 surfaces of Picard rank 16, realized as double planes. They conjectured that these surfaces have CM by CM fields obtained by adjoining
\sqrt{-1}
to certain totally real cubic fields. Under the assumption that the surfaces are of
CM type
, they proved that the 6-dimensional Galois representations associated with their transcendental parts are isomorphic to representations induced from algebraic Hecke characters over the corresponding CM fields. In particular, after restriction to these CM fields, the representations decompose as direct sums of algebraic Hecke characters and their conjugates.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that K3 surfaces in the above cases do not have the Picard rank 8 or 14 as shown in
[
14
, Theorem 1 and 2]
and
[
9
, Corollary 2.3, Proposition 2.4, and Corollary 2.6]
. But Taelman
[
29
]
proved that for any CM field
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
of degree at most 20, there exists a K3 surface over
\mathbb{C}
with complex multiplication by
E
. The existence of CM K3 surfaces with Picard rank 8 or 14 follows from
[
29
]
.

The contribution of this paper is to provide a formula (Theorem
1.3
) for the algebraic Hecke characters of CM K3 surfaces over number fields, including those with Picard rank 8 and 14. Moreover, this formula includes the following new examples:

•

Kummer surfaces associated to a simple CM abelian surface (Proposition
4.7
).
This result is independent of Shioda-Inose
[
26
]
and Rizov
[
23
]
. By applying the main theorem of complex multiplication for simple abelian surfaces, one can determine the Hecke characters of the associated Kummer surfaces by a method analogous to those of Shioda-Inose and Rizov, and show that they coincide with those given in Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 1.2
.

Although Theorem
1.3
does not prove that the three K3 surfaces in
[
3
]
are of CM type, it shows that, under this assumption, their transcendental Galois representations are governed by algebraic Hecke characters in the sense of this paper. In this way, their result
[
3
, Theorem 1.2(a)]
are recovered by our general construction once the CM type assumption is imposed.

1.1.
Main theorem

Let
k
be a number field, and let
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}
denote the idele group of
k
. Fix a rational prime
l
and an isomorphism of fields
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{l}}\cong\mathbb{C}
. By class field theory, there exists a one-to-one correspondence (see Theorem
2.12
):

\displaystyle\{1\textendash{\rm dimensional~locally~algebraic~representation}\}

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\{{\rm algebraic~Hecke~character}\}

\displaystyle(\rho_{l,\chi}:{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{l}}^{\times})

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow(\chi:\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}).

An
n
-dimensional semisimple
l
-adic abelian representation
\rho_{l}
is said to be
modular
if there exist algebraic Hecke characters
\chi_{1},\cdots,\chi_{n}
such that
\rho_{l}\cong\rho_{l,\chi_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\rho_{l,\chi_{n}}
.

Let
k
be a number field and let
\overline{k}
be an algebraic closure of
k
. Let
X
be a K3 surface over
k
, i.e., a smooth projective surface over
k
such that
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})=0
and
\Omega_{X/k}^{2}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
. Since the Galois group
{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
acts on
X_{\overline{k}}:=X\times_{{\rm Spec}(k)}{\rm Spec}(\overline{k})
, we obtain the 22-dimensional
l
-adic Galois representation

\displaystyle\rho_{l}:{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\to GL(H^{2}(X_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{l}(1))).

(1.2)

Fix an embedding
\overline{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Then there are isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{2}(X_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{l}(1))\cong H^{2}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q}(1))\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l}\cong({\rm NS}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l})\oplus(T(X_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l}),

where
T(X_{\mathbb{C}}):={\rm NS}(X_{\mathbb{C}})^{\bot}\subset H^{2}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z}(1))
. Since
{\rm NS}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\rm NS}(X_{\overline{k}})
, the group
{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
acts on
{\rm NS}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l}
. Hence we obtain a
(22-\rho(X_{\overline{k}}))
-dimensional Galois representation

\displaystyle{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\to GL(T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l}),

(1.3)

where
T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}}):=T(X_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{l}
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{l}
-module
T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})
is called the
l
-adic transcendental lattice
of
X
.

We obtain the following new result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface defined over a number field
k\subset\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that
X
has CM over
k
by a CM field
E
, i.e.,
X_{\mathbb{C}}
is of CM type and
k\supset E
. Then there exists a unique locally constant homomorphism
u:\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}\to E^{\times}\subset\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E}
satisfying the following properties (i)-(iii):

(i) Fix an embedding
\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}
. The function

\displaystyle\chi_{\tau}:\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}\xrightarrow{u(-)\cdot\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(-)}/{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(-)}\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E}\xrightarrow{\tau-{\rm projection}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}

is an algebraic Hecke character, where
{\rm Nm}_{k/E}:\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}\to\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E}
denotes the norm map for the extension
k/E
, and
\overline{(-)}:\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E}\to\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E}
denotes the complex conjugation.

(ii) Let

\displaystyle\rho_{l}:G_{k}\to GL(T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l})

be the
(22-\rho(X_{\overline{k}}))
-dimensional
l
-adic representation. Then
\rho_{l}
is diagonalized by the algebraic Hecke characters
\chi_{\tau}
, where
\tau
runs over
{\rm Hom}(E,\mathbb{C})
.

(iii) Fix an embedding
\tau\in{\rm Hom}(E,\mathbb{C})
and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset k
with
(l,\mathfrak{p})=1
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a) The
l
-adic representation
\rho_{l}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e., if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the inertia group of
\mathfrak{p}
, then
\rho_{l}(I_{\mathfrak{p}})=\{id\}
.

(b) The Hecke character
\chi_{\tau}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e., if
\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the ring of integers of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then
\chi_{\tau}(\mathscr{O}^{\times}_{\mathfrak{p}})=1.

Remark 1.4
.

If
k
does not contain
E
, Theorem
1.3
can be extended to a K3 surface
X
over
kE
, and Theorem
1.3
is valid for K3 surface
X
over
kE
.

By Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following equality

\displaystyle L(s,T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l})=\prod_{\tau\in{\rm Hom}(E,\mathbb{C})}L(s,\chi_{\tau}).

(1.4)

Here
L(s,T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l})
denotes the
L
-function of
\rho_{l}
(see
[
30
, Section 2]
). We call the equality (1.4) the
Motivic modularity
of
X
.

A key point in the proof of Theorem
1.3
is to construct algebraic Hecke characters
\chi_{\tau}
satisfying condition (i) of Theorem
1.3
. The fact that the characters
\chi_{\tau}
satisfy the property (ii) follows from the semisimplicity of
\rho_{l}
. These are applications of the main theorem of complex multiplication for K3 surfaces by Rizov
[
23
]
.

On the other hand, the semisimplicity also follows from the theory of absolute Hodge cycles by Deligne
[
5
]
; Indeed, let
X
be a K3 surface with CM over
k
by a CM field
E
. The Hodge cycles

\displaystyle E:={\rm End}_{Hdg}T(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset H^{4}(X_{\mathbb{C}}\times X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q})(2)

are absolute Hodge cycles (as a consequence of Deligne’s Principle B
[
5
, I, Theorem 2.12]
and the Kuga-Satake construction
[
4
, Proposition 6.5]
). Hence the action of
{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
on
T(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{A}_{f}}
commutes with the action of
E
. It follows that the image of the Galois representation
\rho:{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\to GL(T(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{A}_{f}})
is contained in
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E,f}
. Since
T(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{A}_{f}}
is a rank-1
\mathbb{A}_{E,f}
-module, it follows that
\rho
is semisimple.

Remark 1.5
.

In general, Zarhin and Skorobogatov
[
28
]
proved that the Galois representation (1.2) is semisimple for any K3 surface. Their result is based on the semisimplicity of the Galois representations for abelian varieties due to Faltings
[
8
]
.

In the final section, we compute, as an example of Theorem
1.3
, the algebraic Hecke characters in Theorem
1.3
for Kummer surfaces associated with CM simple abelian surfaces. The main theorem of complex multiplication for simple abelian surfaces describes the Galois action on the
Tate module
(Milne
[
17
]
). Using this result, we compute the Galois action on the
transcendental part
of a CM simple abelian surface (see Proposition
4.4
). Finally, we show that the Hecke characters obtained from Proposition
4.4
coincide with those given in Theorem
1.3
.

be a rational Hodge structure of weight

. In other words, there exists a decomposition
V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}V^{p,q}
with
\overline{V^{p,q}}=V^{q,p}
. This gives a real representation

\displaystyle\rho:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to GL(V_{\mathbb{R}}),z\mapsto\rho(z):v\mapsto(z^{-p}\overline{z}^{-q})v

for
v\in V^{p,q}
. In particular, for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
, we have
\rho(t)=t^{-n}:v\mapsto t^{-n}v
. Conversely, if
V
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space and
\rho:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to GL(V_{\mathbb{R}})
is a real representation such that
\rho(t)=t^{-n}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
, then the
\mathbb{C}
-subspaces

\displaystyle V^{p,q}:=\{v\in V_{\mathbb{C}}|~\rho(z)(v)=z^{-p}\overline{z}^{-q}v,{\rm for~all}~z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\},~~~p+q=n

define a rational Hodge structure of weight
n
on
V
. Hence we obtain a one-to-one correspondence:

\displaystyle\{{\rm rational~Hodge~structure~of~weight}~n\}\leftrightarrow\{{\rm real~representation}~\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to GL(V_{\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle{\rm such~that}~t\mapsto t^{-n}\cdot(-)~{\rm on}~\mathbb{R}^{\times}\}.

On the other hand, a real representation
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to GL(V_{\mathbb{R}})
can be regarded as a representation of the Deligne torus
\mathbb{S}:={\rm Res}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{C}})
. This is an
\mathbb{R}
algebraic group defined by

\displaystyle\{\mathbb{R}\textendash{\rm algebras}\}\ni A\mapsto\mathbb{S}(A):=(A\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})^{\times}\in\{{\rm groups}\}.

Hence, a rational Hodge structure on
V
of weight
n
corresponds to an algebraic
\mathbb{R}
-morphism
\rho:\mathbb{S}\to GL(V_{\mathbb{R}})
such that
\rho(t)=t^{-n}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.

Definition 2.13
.

(Mumford-Tate groups)

Let
V
be a rational Hodge structure, and let
\rho:\mathbb{S}\to GL(V_{\mathbb{R}})
be the algebraic
\mathbb{R}
-representation associated with
V
. The
Mumford-Tate group

{\rm MT}(V)
of
V
is the smallest
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic subgroup of
GL(V)
such that
\rho(\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}))\subset{\rm MT}(V)(\mathbb{R})
.

Example 2.14
.

(i) Let
X
be an algebraic K3 surface over
\mathbb{C}
. Consider the second cohomology
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. This is a Hodge structure of weight
2
, hence the Tate twist
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})(1)
is of weight 0. By the Lefschetz
(1,1)
-theorem (see
[
10
, Chapter III, Proposition 3.3.2]
), the Néron-Severi group
{\rm NS}(X)
is isomorphic to
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})(1)\cap H^{1,1}(X)
. Hence the orthogonal complement
T(X)
is a sub-Hodge structure of weight
0
.

(ii) Let
A
be an abelian surface over
\mathbb{C}
. Then the second cohomology

is a Hodge structure of weight

2.3.
K3 surfaces with CM

To explain CM, we first define CM fields.

Definition 2.15
.

(CM fields) Let
K
be a number field.

(i)

is said to be
totally real
if
\sigma(K)\subset\mathbb{R}
for all inclusions
\sigma:K\to\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

is said to be
totally imaginary
if
\sigma(K)\not\subset\mathbb{R}
for all inclusions
\sigma:K\to\mathbb{C}
.

(iii) A

is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field.

Example 2.16
.

(1) An imaginary quadratic field is a CM field. In fact,
\mathbb{Q}
is totally real, and the imaginary quadratic extension is not contained in
\mathbb{R}
.

(2) Let

be a prime
\neq 2

be a

-th root of unity. Consider the field extensions
\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
has degree

is totally real because

\displaystyle\sigma(\zeta_{p}+\zeta_{p}^{-1})=\zeta_{p}^{n}+\zeta_{p}^{-n}=2\cos{\left(\frac{2n\pi}{p}\right)}\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
is a CM field.

Let

be an algebraic K3 surface over
\mathbb{C}

(2.3)

Proof.

See

3.2.
Construction of Hecke characters

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.3
. First, we prove the following lemma from algebraic number theory.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
E
be a CM field. Then the group

\displaystyle G(\mathbb{Q})=\{x\in E^{\times}|~x\overline{x}=1\}

is a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E,f}

Proof.

Let
F
be the maximal totally real field of
E
. By Dirichlet’s unit theorem, the group of units
\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\times}
has a finite index in
\mathscr{O}_{E}^{\times}
since
E
is a CM field. Moreover, under the natural inclusion
E\to\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E,f}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{E}=E\cap(\prod_{v}\mathscr{O}_{v}).

Since
\prod_{v}\mathscr{O}_{v}
is open in
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E,f}
, it follows that
\mathscr{O}_{E}
is open in
E
. Hence
\mathscr{O}_{F}
is also open in
E
. On the other hand,
G(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathscr{O}_{F}=\{\pm 1\}
. Therefore, since
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E,f}
is Hausdorff, it follows that
G(\mathbb{Q})
is a discrete subgroup.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
X
is a K3 surface with CM over a number field
k\subset\mathbb{C}
. Let
E
be the reflex field. Then there exists a unique locally constant homomorphism
u:\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}\to E^{\times}
satisfying the following condition:

For all
t\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\rho^{ab}({\rm art}_{k}(t))=u(t)\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)}/{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)\in G(\mathbb{A}_{f}).

Proof.

Under the notation of Remark
3.3
, the correspondence
t\to u
defines a map from
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}
to
G(\mathbb{Q})=\{x\in E^{\times}|~x\overline{x}=1\}
, which we denote by
u
. The function
u
is continuous, since it is defined by a functional equation involving the nonzero continuous functions
\rho^{ab}
and
{\rm Nm}_{k/E}
. Next, we show that
u
is a group homomorphism. Let
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}
. By Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{ab}({\rm art}_{k}(t_{1}){\rm art}_{k}(t_{2}))

\displaystyle=\rho^{ab}({\rm art}_{k}(t_{1}))\rho^{ab}({\rm art}_{k}(t_{2}))

\displaystyle=u(t_{1})\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t_{1})}{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t_{1})^{-1}u(t_{2})\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t_{2})}{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=u(t_{1})u(t_{2})\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t_{1}t_{2})}{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t_{1}t_{2})^{-1}.

On the other hand, since the Artin map
{\rm art}_{k}:\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}\to{\rm Gal}(k^{ab}/k)
is a homomorphism, we have
{\rm art}_{k}(t_{1}){\rm art}_{k}(t_{2})={\rm art}_{k}(t_{1}t_{2})
. Hence, by uniqueness, we obtain
u(t_{1}t_{2})=u(t_{1})u(t_{2})
. Therefore,
u
is a homomorphism from
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}
to
G(\mathbb{Q})
.
∎

Let
\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}
be an inclusion. We define a character
\chi_{\tau}:\mathbb{A}_{k}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by

\displaystyle\chi_{\tau}(t):=\left(u(t)\cdot\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)}/{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)\right)_{\tau},

where
(-)_{\tau}
denotes the
\tau
-component in
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{E}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

(i) The characters
\chi_{\tau}
are algebraic Hecke characters of
k
.

(ii)
Let
l
be a prime, and let
\rho_{l}:{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\to GL(T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l}))
be the l-adic Galois representation. Fix a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset k
such that
(l,\mathfrak{p})=1
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a) The
l
-adic representation
\rho_{l}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
.

(b) The Hecke character
\chi_{\tau}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e., if
\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the ring of integers of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then
\chi_{\tau}(\mathscr{O}^{\times}_{\mathfrak{p}})=1.

Proof.

(i): Let
t\in k^{\times}
. Since
k^{\times}
is contained in the kernel of the Artin map by Theorem
2.3
(i), we have
{\rm art}_{k}(t)=id_{k^{ab}}
. Since
t\in k
, we have
{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)=N_{k/E}(t)
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(id_{\overline{k}})=u(t)\cdot\overline{N_{k/E}(t)}N_{k/E}(t)^{-1}.

Thus we have
\chi_{\tau}(t)=1
. This shows that
\chi_{\tau}
is a Hecke character.

(ii) By the Artin map,
\mathscr{O}^{\times}_{\mathfrak{p}}
corresponds to
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
(Theorem
2.3
(iii)). Let
t\in{\rm Im}(\mathscr{O}^{\times}_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k})
.

(a)\Rightarrow(b):
Suppose that
\rho_{l}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
. By Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{ab}({\rm art}_{k}(t))=u(t)\cdot\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)}{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)^{-1}.

By assumption,
\rho^{ab}({\rm art}_{k}(t))
acts trivially on the
l
-adic transcendental lattice
T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}}).
On the other hand, by Proposition
3.4
,
\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)}/{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)
also acts trivially. Hence
u(t)=1.
By the definition of
t
, we have

\displaystyle(\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t)}/{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(t))_{\tau}=1,

and hence
\chi_{\tau}(t)=1.

(b)\Rightarrow(a)

Conversely, suppose that
\chi_{\tau}(t)=1.
Then
u(t)=1
. Let
x\in I_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the image of
t
under the Artin map. It suffices to show that
\rho_{l}(x)
acts trivially on
T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})
. This follows from Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal with
(l,\mathfrak{p})=1
, and let
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a uniformizer. Suppose that
\rho_{l}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
. Then the action of
u(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1})
on
T_{l}(X_{\overline{k}})
coincides with the Frobenius action
\rho_{l}({\rm Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Proof.

The Artin map
{\rm art}_{k}
sends
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}
to
{\rm Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\rho({\rm Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})=u(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1})\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1})}/{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}).

Taking the
l
-component, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{l}({\rm Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})=u(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}).

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that
(l,\mathfrak{p})=1
and that
\rho_{l}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
. Then the eigenvalues of
{\rm Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are given by

\displaystyle\{\chi_{\tau}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1})~|~\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}~~~inclusions~~~\}.

In particular, the Galois representation
\rho_{l}
is rational.

Proof.

Since
X
has CM over
k
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}T(X_{\mathbb{C}})=[E:\mathbb{Q}].

Hence the characteristic polynomial of
{\rm Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
has degree
[E:\mathbb{Q}].
By Proposition
3.8
, the set

\displaystyle\{u(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1})^{\tau}~|~\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}~~~inclusions~~~\}

gives all the eigenvalues of
{\rm Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle(\overline{{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}})/{\rm Nm}_{k/E}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}))_{\tau}=1.

Hence this set coincides with

\displaystyle\{\chi_{\tau}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1})~|~\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}~~~\}.

∎

Remark 3.10
.

The rationality is due to Dwork
[
7
]
. However, we prove this for CM K3 surfaces without Dwork’s result.

Corollary 3.11
.

For each
\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}
, let
\rho_{l,\tau}
be the one-dimensional
l
-adic representation associated with the Hecke character
\chi_{\tau}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\rho_{l}\cong\bigoplus_{\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}}\rho_{l,\tau}.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
3.9
, Remark
3.2
, Chebotarev’s density theorem.
∎

By Corollary
3.11
, we obtain Theorem
1.3
.

We now specialize Theorem

with those arising from the transcendental part of the abelian surface.

Following Chai-Conrad-Oort
[

]
, we recall the definition of CM abelian varieties. Let
K
be a

, i.e., a finite product of CM fields. If

, then we have
{\rm Hom}(K,\mathbb{C})=\coprod_{i}{\rm Hom}(K_{i},\mathbb{C})
. In particular, when

, the set

consists of exactly

-elements. A

such that

, where
\overline{(-)}
denotes complex conjugation of
\mathbb{C}
. The pair

is called a

. If
(K,\Phi)
is a CM type of

, then the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Phi}\mathbb{C}
has a canonical structure of a

-vector space via the diagonal map

.

Definition 4.1
.

(CM abelian varieties)
Let
K
be a CM-algebra with
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}K=g
, let
(K,\Phi)
be a CM type, and let

be a

-dimensional abelian variety over a subfield

. Suppose that an embedding

is given, and there is an isomorphism

as
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
-modules, where
T_{0}A
is the tangent space of
A
at 0. The pair
(A,i)
is said to be a
CM abelian variety
over
k
of CM type
(K,\Phi)
.

If
(A,i)
is a CM abelian variety over
k
of type
(K,\Phi)
, then

contains the reflex field

\displaystyle K^{\ast}:=\mathbb{Q}(\sum_{\sigma\in\Phi}\sigma(x)|x\in K).

Indeed, by the definition of a CM abelian variety, there is a diagonal action
(\sigma(x))_{\sigma\in\Phi}
on the

-vector space

for every

. Hence the trace
\sum_{\sigma\in\Phi}\sigma(x)
lies in
k
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(A,i)
be a CM abelian surface over
k\subset\mathbb{C}
of CM type
(K,\Phi)
. Then the Kummer surface
X:={\rm Km}(A)
has CM over
k
by the reflex field
K^{\ast}\subset\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\}.
By the construction of Kummer surfaces, there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge isometry (up to the double twist)

\displaystyle H^{2}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q}(1))\cong H^{2}(A_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q}(1))\oplus\bigoplus^{16}_{i=1}[E_{i}],

where
[E_{i}]
are the cohomology classes of the exceptional divisors of the minimal resolution
X\to A/\langle-1\rangle
.
In particular, we obtain a Hodge isometry (up to the double twist)
T(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus it suffices to prove that the reflex field of
T(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is
K^{\ast}
.

Via the composition

\displaystyle K\xrightarrow{i}{\rm End}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\to{\rm End}_{Hdg}T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\rm End}_{\mathbb{C}}H^{2,0}(A_{\mathbb{C}})=\mathbb{C},

(4.1)

the field
K
acts on
H^{2,0}(A_{\mathbb{C}})
.
For
x\in K
, the element
i(x)
acts on
H^{2,0}(A)
as
\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)
. In fact, since
T_{0}(A_{\mathbb{C}})\cong T_{0}A\otimes_{k}\mathbb{C}\cong\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Phi}\mathbb{C}
, the eigenvalues of
i(x)
on
T_{0}(A_{\mathbb{C}})
are
\sigma_{1}(x)
and
\sigma_{2}(x)
. Hence the eigenvalue of
i(x)
on
H^{2,0}(A_{\mathbb{C}})=\wedge^{2}H^{1,0}(A_{\mathbb{C}})
is
\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)
. Thus the image of the composition (
4.1
) is
K^{{}^{\prime}}:=\mathbb{Q}(\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)|~x\in K)\subset\mathbb{C}
. On the other hand,
K^{{}^{\prime}}
coincides with the reflex field
K^{\ast}
. Indeed, we have the relations

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(x)+\sigma_{2}(x)=\sigma_{1}(x+1)\sigma_{2}(x+1)-\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)-1,

\displaystyle 2\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)=(\sigma_{1}(x)+\sigma_{2}(x))^{2}-(\sigma_{1}(x^{2})+\sigma_{2}(x^{2})),

which show that
K^{{}^{\prime}}=K^{\ast}
.

Let
E(T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}})
denote the reflex field of
T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. To show
K^{\ast}=E(T(A)_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we consider the following three cases.

(i) If
A_{\mathbb{C}}
is simple, then the ring
{\rm End}(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a 4-dimensional CM field (see
[
2
, 1.3.6.4. Proposition]
). Hence
K
is a CM-field. By (
4.1
), the degree
[K^{\ast}:\mathbb{Q}]
is equal to 4. Since the Picard number of
A_{\mathbb{C}}
is
2
(see
[
19
, Lemma 3.3]
), we have
[E(T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}):\mathbb{Q}]={\rm dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}=4
, and hence
K^{\ast}=E(T(A)_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

(ii) If
A_{\mathbb{C}}
is isogenous to a product of elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, and if
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are not isogenous, then we have a canonical isomorphism of rings

\displaystyle{\rm End}(E_{1})_{\mathbb{Q}}\times{\rm End}(E_{2})_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\rm End}(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

(4.2)

given by
(f_{1},f_{2})\mapsto f_{1}\times f_{2}
.
In fact, let
\varphi(x,y)=(f_{1}(x,y),f_{2}(x,y))\in{\rm End}(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}.
Then

\displaystyle f_{i}(x,y)=f_{i}(x,0)+f_{i}(0,y).

In particular,
f_{1}(0,y)
is a homomorphism
E_{2}\to E_{1}
. Since
E_{2}
and
E_{1}
are not isogenous, it follows that
f_{1}(0,y)=0
for all
y\in E_{2}
. By the same argument,
f_{2}(x,0)=0
for all
x\in E_{1}
. Hence the morphism (
4.2
) is an isomorphism.

In particular, the CM algebra
K
is of the form
K_{1}\times K_{2}
, where
K_{i}
are imaginary quadratic fields that are not isomorphic. Hence the reflex field
K^{\ast}
is generated by two non-isomorphic imaginary quadratic fields, and thus
[K^{\ast}:\mathbb{Q}]=4
. On the other hand, since
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}T(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}=4
(see
[
9
, Corollary 2.3]
), we obtain
[E(T(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}):\mathbb{Q}]=4
. Hence
K^{\ast}=E(T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

(iii) Suppose that
A_{\mathbb{C}}
is isogenous to a product of two elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
which are isogenous. Then
K
is a product of two isomorphic imaginary quadratic fields. In particular, the reflex field
K^{\ast}
is a 2-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{Q}
. Since the Picard number of
A_{\mathbb{C}}
is
4
(see
[
9
, Corollary 2.6]
), we have
[E(T(A_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}):\mathbb{Q}]=2
. Thus
K^{\ast}=E(T(A)_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
∎

As a consequence of Milne’s complex multiplication theory

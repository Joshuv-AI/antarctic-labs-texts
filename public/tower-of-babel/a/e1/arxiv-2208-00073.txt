Geometric model for module categories of Dynkin quivers via hearts of total stability conditions
Wen Chang, Yu Qiu, Xiaoting Zhang

Abstract
. We derive a geometric model for the module category mod ⁡ 𝐤 ​ Q \operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q of a Dynkin quiver Q Q via the heart of a total stability condition on the bounded derived category of mod ⁡ 𝐤 ​ Q \operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q . As an application, we prove Reineke’s conjecture that there is a stability function on mod ⁡ 𝐤 ​ Q \operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q making any indecomposables stable. Key words: total stability condition, Dynkin diagram, module category, geometric model, Reineke’s conjecture



1.
Introduction

The stability structures, e.g. King’s
\theta
-stability on abelian categories and Bridgeland’s stability conditions on triangulated categories, play an important role in many areas, such as, geometric invariant theory, Donaldson-Thomas theory, cluster theory, etc.

In the prequel
[
QZ22
]
, Qiu-Zhang give a geometric model for the root category of the bounded derived category
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)

associated to a Dynkin diagram and describe the space
\operatorname{ToSt}(Q)
of all total stability conditions on
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)
.
In this paper, we describe the heart
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
for
\sigma\in\operatorname{ToSt}(Q)
on the geometric model and hence obtain a model
for the module category of certain Dynkin quiver whose underlying diagram is the same as
Q
.
In fact, we show that such a heart can realize the module category of a Dynkin quiver with any given orientation.
As an application, we prove Reineke’s conjecture that, for any Dynkin quiver
Q
,
there is a stability function on
\operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q
such that all indecomposable objects are stable.

1.1.
Stability structure on abelian and triangulated categories

An algebraic version of the notion of stability in geometric invariant theory is introduced by King
[
K94
]
in the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras.
The King’s
\theta
-stability is generalized by Rudakov
[
R97
]
to any abelian category.
A stability function on the module category
\mathcal{H}(Q)\colon=\operatorname{mod}\mathbf{k}Q
of a quiver
Q

is a group homomorphism
Z\colon K\mathcal{H}(Q)\to\mathbb{C}
.
The associated slope function is

\mu_{Z}(\alpha)=\frac{\operatorname{Im}Z(\alpha)}{\operatorname{Re}Z(\alpha)}.

An object
M
is
Z
-stable (resp.
Z
-semistable) if and only if

\mu_{Z}(L)<\mu_{Z}(M),\quad\text{(resp. $\mu_{Z}(L)\leq\mu_{Z}(M)$)}

for any subobject
0\neq L\subsetneq M
.

Later, motivated by
\Pi
-stability from string theory,
Bridgeland
[
B07
]
introduce the notion of stability conditions on a triangulated category.
To be more precise, a stability condition
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{P})
on a triangulated category
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
consists of
a group homomorphism
Z\colon K\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathbb{C}
, called the central charge,
and an
\mathbb{R}
-collection of abelian subcategories
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
of
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
,
known as the slicing, which satisfy certain conditions.
The objects in each
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
are called semistable
and the simple objects there are called stable.
Such a notion is a kind of triangulated generalization of the stability for an abelian category mentioned above.
Indeed, a stability condition
\sigma
on
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
induces
a stability function
Z
on the abelian category, i.e. its heart
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
.
Conversely, a stability function
Z
on a heart
\mathcal{H}
of
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
,
satisfying the so-called Harder-Narasimhan property, is equivalent to a stability condition with heart
\mathcal{H}
on
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
.

1.2.
Reineke’s conjecture and total stability

Denote by
\operatorname{Sim}\mathcal{H}(Q)
the set of simples in
\mathcal{H}(Q)
and

\mathcal{A}_{Q}
be the quantum affine space

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/2})\langle y^{S}\mid S\in\operatorname{Sim}\mathcal{H}(Q)\rangle\big/(y^{S_{i}}y^{S_{j}}-q^{\lambda_{Q}(i,j)}y^{S_{j}}y^{S_{i}}),

where
q^{1/2}
is an indeterminate and
\lambda_{Q}(i,j)=\langle S_{j},S_{i}\rangle-\langle S_{i},S_{j}\rangle
with

\langle-,-\rangle
given by the Euler form associated to
Q
.
One can obtain the completion
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{Q}
of
\mathcal{A}_{Q}

with respect to the ideal generated by
y^{S},\forall S\in\operatorname{Sim}\mathcal{H}(Q)
.
Suppose that a stability function
Z
on
\mathcal{H}(Q)
is discrete,
i.e.
\mu_{Z}(M)\neq\mu_{Z}(L)
for any indecomposable
M\not\cong L
in
\mathcal{H}(Q)
.
Then an invariant
\operatorname{DT}(\mathcal{H}(Q))
(cf. e.g.
[
Ke11
]
) can be calculated as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{DT}(\mathcal{H})\colon=\prod^{\longleftarrow}_{\text{$M$ stable}}\mathbb{E}(y^{\dim M})\quad\in\mathcal{A}_{Q}

where the product is taking in phase-decreasing order and
\mathbb{E}
is the quantum dilogarithm defined as the formal series

\mathbb{E}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{j^{2}/2}X^{j}}{\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}(q^{j}-q^{k})}.

The formula
1.1
for calculating quantum dilogarithm identities is proved by Reineke
[
R03
]
,
using Hall algebra and the integration map,
for
\mathcal{H}(Q)
of a Dynkin quiver
Q
.
An alternative proof can be found in
[
Q15
]
which uses the fundamental group of exchange graph of hearts.
In the setting of Calabi-Yau-
3
categories,
this invariant produces the Donladson-Thomas invariants,
e.g. studied by Kontsevich-Sobliman
[
KS08
]
.
In
[
R03
]
,
Reineke conjectures the following.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For any Dynkin quiver
Q
, there is a stability function
Z
such that
the number of factors in the product (
1.1
) reaches maximal, i.e. equals
n\cdot h_{Q}/2
,
where
h_{Q}
is the Coxeter number associated to
Q
.
In other words, any indecomposable object in
\mathcal{H}(Q)
is
Z
-stable.

Such a stability function
Z
is called a total stability function.
This conjecture has been confirmed for any quiver
Q
of type
A_{n}
(with arbitrary orientation) in several papers via different approaches, such as
[
BGMS19
,
AI20
,
HH20
,
Ki20
]
, and also for any quiver
Q
of type
D_{n}
in
[
A22
]
.

In this paper, we prove Reineke’s conjecture from the point of view of triangulated categories via geometric models.
In the triangulated setting, one also has the notion of total stability for a Bridgeland’s stability condition
\sigma
, (cf.
[
Q15
,
Q18
]
),
i.e. requiring all indecomposable objects in the triangulated category
\sigma
-stable.
The existence of such total stability conditions is rare, if and only if under the circumstance that
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}=\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)
for a Dynkin diagram
Q
(cf.
[
KOT19
,
Q20
]
).
Clearly, a total stability condition
\sigma
on
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
will induce a total stability function on its heart
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
.
The subtle point is to describe the heart
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
so that one can obtain the module category of any Dynkin quiver (with arbitrary orientation).
The geometric description (cf.
[
QZ22
]
) of the space
\operatorname{ToSt}(Q)
is the key here,
that is we have a geometric model for any module category of Dynkin type.

In type
A_{n}
, our approach is essentially the same as the one in
[
BGMS19
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that in Reineke’s original paper
[
R03
]
,
he takes
\operatorname{Re}Z(M)=\dim M
to be the dimension function of
M
for any
M\in\mathcal{H}(Q)
, which is just for simplicity.
Very recently, it is proved in
[
M22
]
that, when restricted to
\operatorname{Re}Z(M)=\dim M
,
Reineke’s conjecture fails for the linear quiver
Q
of type
E_{7}
.
However, such a constrain is unnecessary from the modern point of view.
Our approach proves the correct version of Reineke’s conjecture.

Another remark is that Hille-Juteau also have a proof of Reineke’s conjecture (unpublished) according to
[
Ke11
, § 1]
.

1.3.
Geometric model for module category of a Dynkin diagram

There are many works on geometric models for various categories in representation theory of algebras.
Such models are in some sense, certain type of Fukaya categories, cf.
[
HKK17
,
OPS18
,
HZZ20
,
BS21
]
.
The geometric model allows one to use surface combinatorics/ topology to produce many applications, i.e.
realizing arcs as indecomposables, intersections as morphisms, (total) rotations of arcs as Auslander-Reiten translations, etc.
In particular, Qiu-Zhang
[
QZ22
]
construct a geometric model for the root category
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)/[2]
of each Dynkin diagram
Q
. In details, for a Dynkin diagram
Q
,
they construct stable
h_{Q}
-gons
\mathbf{V}
, which provide geometric models for
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)/[2]
, in the sense that each indecomposable object in
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)/[2]
is interpreted as an oriented admissible diagonal in
\mathbf{V}
, and naturally induce a central charge of
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)/[2]
.
Note that by upgrading orientation (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading) to
\mathbb{Z}
-grading for admissible diagonals,
the model also works for the bounded derived category
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(Q)
.
Furthermore, by the construction, contractible triangles in the geometric model imply triangles in the associated triangulated categories.

We will derive a geometric model for any heart of a total stability condition in
\operatorname{ToSt}(Q)

from the geometric model of the root category. In Proposition
3.2
, we realize each admissible upward diagonal of a stable
h_{Q}
-gon associated to a total stability condition
\sigma
as an indecomposable object in the heart
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
. Moreover, we locate all the simples in the stable
h_{Q}
-gon and obtain the following.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorems
4.4
,
4.9
and
4.15
)
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{ToSt}(Q)
for a quiver
Q
of Dynkin type and
\mathbf{V}_{\sigma}
be the stable
h_{Q}
-gon of type
Q
associated to
\sigma
.
Then there is a bijection

\mathfrak{M}\colon\mathrm{UD}(\mathbf{V}_{\sigma})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ind}\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}

sending an upward diagonal
\ell
in
\mathbf{V}_{\sigma}
to an indecomposable object
\mathfrak{M}(\ell)
in
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
.

Moreover, it restricts to a bijection

\operatorname{Sim}\mathbf{V}_{\sigma}\to\operatorname{Sim}\mathcal{H}_{\sigma},

between the set of
\operatorname{Im}Z
-Ind diagonals of
\mathbf{V}_{\sigma}
and the set of simples of
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
.
As a consequence,

\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
is equivalent to the module category of the (ungraded) intersection quiver of the
\operatorname{Im}Z
-Ind diagonals of
\mathbf{V}_{\sigma}
.

Furthermore, we shall use such a model to prove Reineke’s conjecture.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
5.6
)
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds.

In type
A_{n}
and
D_{n}
, the proof bases on building stable
h_{Q}
-gon directly.
In type
E_{n\in\{6,7,8\}}
,
we show that essentially we only need to give
2^{n-5}
concrete examples (of stable
h_{Q}
-gons) to verify the conjecture.

Hearts and triangles

3.1.
Hearts of total stability conditions

Recall that a stability condition
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{P})
consists of the central charge
Z
and the slicing
\mathcal{P}
,
which is a
\mathbb{R}
-collection of abelian subcategory

Its heart

In particular, if

Define

(3.1)

\displaystyle\upharpoonleft\hskip-3.61371pt{VW}\hskip-3.61371pt\upharpoonright\colon=\begin{cases}VW&\text{if~}~V<W,\\
WV&\text{if~}~W<V.\end{cases}

Then we obtain
\operatorname{arg}(\upharpoonleft\hskip-3.61371pt{VW}\hskip-3.61371pt\upharpoonright)\in[0,\pi)
.

.

An admissible diagonal

-gon

of type

Denote by

For any admissible diagonal

,
where

Extended Gevrey regularity via weighted matrices
Nenad Teofanov, Filip Tomić

Abstract
. The main aim of this paper is to compare two recent approaches for investigating the interspace between the union of Gevrey spaces 𝒢 t ​ ( U ) \mathcal{G}_{t}(U) and the space of smooth functions C ∞ ​ ( U ) C^{\infty}(U) . The first approach in the style of Komatsu is based on the properties of two parameter sequences M p = p τ ​ p σ M_{p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}} , τ > 0 \tau>0 , σ > 1 \sigma>1 . The other one uses weight matrices defined by certain weight functions. We prove the equivalence of the corresponding spaces in the Beurling case by taking projective limits with respect to matrix parameters, while in the Roumieu case we need to consider a larger space then the one obtained as the inductive limit of extended Gevrey classes.

1.
Introduction

Classes of ultradifferentable functions (also known as smooth functions of ultra-rapid decay)
are usually studied in the framework of one of the two most widely used approaches.
The first one is based on the properties of the defining sequences
M_{p}
,
p\in\mathbf{N}
, which control the derivatives of the functions,
[
8
]
. For the same purpose, the other approach uses weights with the certain asymptotical properties,
[
2
,
10
]
.
The relation between these weights and the so-called associated functions to
M_{p}
sequences provides a way to compare the theories of ultradifferentable functions and their dual spaces of ultradistributions. In many situations these approaches are equivalent. For example, it is proved in
[
10
]

that the corresponding classes of functions are equal
if the sequence
M_{p}
satisfies Komatsu’s conditions
(M.1)
,
(M.2)
and
(M.3)
, see Section
2
. These conditions are relaxed in
[
1
]
where
(M.3)
is replaced by

(\exists Q\in\mathbf{N})\quad\liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{m_{Qp}}{m_{p}}>1,

(1.1)

with
\displaystyle m_{p}=M_{p}/M_{p-1}
.

In this paper we study the equivalence of the approaches by considering specific sequences which do not satisfy
(M.2)
. To that end we exploit the powerful technique based on weight matrices introduced in
[
14
]
. Broadly speaking, weight matrices are families of sequences. For instance,
\{p!^{t}\}_{t>0}
is a weight matrix that consists of Gevrey sequences. More generally, for a given weight function
\omega
(see Subsection
1.1
for the definition)
one can observe matrices of the form
\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}=\{M_{p}^{H}\}_{H>0}
where

\displaystyle M_{p}^{H}=e^{\frac{1}{H}\varphi^{*}(Hp)},\quad p\in\mathbf{N},

(1.2)

and
\varphi^{*}
is the Young conjugate of
\displaystyle\varphi(t)=\omega(e^{t})
, see (
1.6
).
This approach allows to prove that the corresponding classes of functions
are equivalent in certain situations even if
(M.2)
is violated, see
[
14
,
6
]
.

We consider the two parameter defining sequences of the form
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, cf.
[
11
]
. Such sequences do not satisfy
(M.2)
for any choice of parameters
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
, hence the corresponding classes of functions
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
(extended Gevrey classes) are not ultradifferentiable. However, related ultradifferentiable classes can be obtained by taking their unions and intersections (inductive and projective limits) with respect to the parameter
\tau
(this follows from Proposition
2.1

iv)
).

Extended Gevrey regularity turned out to be convenient when describing certain aspects of some
hyperbolic PDE’s. In particular,
\mathcal{E}_{1,2}(U)
appears in the study of local solvability of strictly hyperbolic PDE’s, for which the initial value problem is ill posed in the Gevrey settings (see
[
4
]
). In addition, sequences
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
for
1<\sigma\leq 2
are recently used in
[
7
]
to study the surjectivity of Borel maps for ultraholomorphic classes. For more details concerning
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
we refer to
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

In this paper we prove that the extended Gevrey classes are the special case of classes investigated
[
6
,
14
]
only when considering projective and inductive limits with respect to the (matrix) parameter
\tau
. More precisely, in the Beurling case we prove the equality of the corresponding spaces, while in the Roumieu case the equivalence holds when the corresponding inductive limit is replaced by a larger space of test functions (see (
2.14
) and (
3.7
)).

We start by proving that the function
T_{\tau,\sigma,h}(k)
associated to the sequence
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
is equivalent to a weight function in the sense of
[
1
]
(see Theorem
3.1
). For that purpose we need to estimate
T_{\tau,\sigma,h}(k)
. This is done in
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
by using the properties of the Lambert
W
function. In Proposition
3.1
(see also Lemma
2.2
) we use another technique to obtain similar estimates.
Consequently, we conclude that
\displaystyle\{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}\}_{\tau>0}
and
\displaystyle\{e^{\frac{1}{H}\varphi_{\sigma}^{*}(Hp)}\}_{H>0}
are equivalent matrices for a suitable function
\varphi_{\sigma}
, which implies that the corresponding classes of functions
given by these matrices coincide.

Although Theorem
3.1
, as the main result of the paper, connects the approach from
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
with the one given in
[
14
,
6
]
, let us mention an important difference between them.
In contrast to the usual Carleman classes and corresponding part in
[
14
,
6
]
, in the norm (
2.8
) we consider
h^{|\alpha|^{\sigma}}
,
\sigma>1
, in denominator. Thus the parameter
\sigma
plays an important role in our construction which can not be revealed by using the techniques from

[
14
,
6
]
. For example, the spaces
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
are closed under finite order differentiation for any choice of parameters
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
.
In addition, the parameters
h
and
\sigma
provide a ”fine tuning” in the gap between the union of Gevrey spaces and
C^{\infty}
(see Proposition
2.1

i)
).

We end this introductory section with a review of some basic notions.

1.1.
Basic notions and notation

We will use the standat notation
{\bf N}
,
\mathbf{Z}_{+}
,
{\bf R}
,
{\bf R}_{+}
,
{\bf C}
, for the sets of nonnegative
integers, positive integers, real numbers, positive real numbers and complex numbers, respectively.
The floor function of
x\in{\bf R}_{+}
is denoted by
\lfloor x\rfloor:=\max\{m\in\mathbf{N}\,:\,m\leq x\}
.
For a multi-index

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in{\bf N}^{d}
we write

\partial^{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\partial^{\alpha_{d}}
and

|\alpha|=|\alpha_{1}|+\dots|\alpha_{d}|
. By
\#A
we denote the number of elements of the finite set
A
. We write
\ln_{+}x=\max\{0,\ln x\}
,
x>0
.

An essential role in our analysis is played by the
Lambert
W
function
, which is defined as the inverse of
ze^{z}
,
z\in{\bf C}
. By
W(x)
,
x\geq 0
, we denote the restriction of its principal branch, and we review some of its basic properties as follows:

(W1)\quad

W(0)=0
,
W(e)=1
,
W(x)
is continuous, increasing and concave on
[0,\infty)
,

(W2)\quad

W(xe^{x})=x
and
x=W(x)e^{W(x)}
,
x\geq 0
,

(W3)\quad

\displaystyle\ln x-\ln(\ln x)\leq W(x)\leq\ln x-\frac{1}{2}\ln(\ln x)
,
x\geq e
.

Note that
(W2)
implies

W(x\ln x)=\ln x,\quad x>1.

(1.3)

By using
(W3)
we obtain

W(x)\sim\ln x,\quad x\to\infty,

(1.4)

and therefore

W(Cx)\sim W(x),\quad x\to\infty,

(1.5)

for any
C>0
. We refer to
[
5
]
for more details concerning the Lambert function.

Recall (see
[
1
]
), a non-negative, continuous, even and increasing function
\omega
defined on
\mathbf{R}
,
\omega(0)=0
, is called
weight function
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(
\alpha
)

\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t)),\quad t\to\infty,

(
\beta
)

\omega(t)=O(t),\quad t\to\infty

(
\gamma
)

o(\omega(t))=\log t,\quad t\to\infty,

(
\delta
)

\varphi(t)=w(e^{t}),\quad{\rm is\,convex}.

Young’s conjugate of the function
\varphi
(defined as above) is given by

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(k)=\sup_{t>0}(kt-\varphi(t)),\quad k\geq 0.

(1.6)

Some classical examples of weight functions are

\omega(t)=\ln^{s}_{+}|t|,\quad\quad\omega(t)=\frac{|t|}{\ln^{s-1}(e+|t|)},\quad s>1,\,t\in\mathbf{R}.

(1.7)

Moreover,
\omega(t)=|t|^{s}
is a weight function if and only if
0<s\leq 1
. Note that by (
1.4
) it follows that
\omega(t)=W(|t|)
is not a weight functions since the condition
(\gamma)
is not satisfied.

Functions
f
and
g
are equivalent if
f=O(g)
and
g=O(f)
, and we will write
f\asymp g
. In particular, if
\omega
is a weight function and
\omega_{1}\asymp\omega
then

A\varphi^{*}(y/A)\leq\varphi_{1}^{*}(y)\leq B\varphi^{*}(y/B)\quad y>0,

(1.8)

for some
A,B>0
, where
\varphi(t)=\omega(e^{t})
,
\varphi_{1}(t)=\omega_{1}(e^{t})
and
\varphi^{*}
,
\varphi_{1}^{*}
are their Young’s conjugates, respectively (see
[
10
]
).

Throughout the paper we assume that
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
, unless stated otherwise.

2.1.
Weight sequences

In this subsection we consider sequences of the form
M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{0}=1
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
. Since
\displaystyle(M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p})^{1/p}\to\infty
when

such sequences are examples of weight sequences considered in
[

]
.

Note that

M^{\tau_{1},\sigma_{1}}_{p}\leq M^{\tau_{2},\sigma_{2}}_{p},\quad 0<\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2},\quad 1<\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2},\quad p\in\mathbf{N}.

(2.1)

Moreover,
M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
\tau>0
,

(

),
satisfies the following conditions (see
[
11
]
for the proof):

(M.1)

\displaystyle({M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}})^{2}\leq{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma}}
,

,

\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}

(\exists C>0)\quad M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}
,
p\in\mathbf{N}
,

\widetilde{(M.2)}

(\exists C>0)

M_{p+q}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}+q^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{2^{\sigma-1}\tau,\sigma}M_{q}^{2^{\sigma-1}\tau,\sigma},\quad p,q\in\mathbf{N},

(M.3)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}<\infty,

\widetilde{(M.4)}

\displaystyle(\forall h>0)\,(\exists C>0)\quad M^{\tau_{1},\sigma}_{p}\leq C\,h^{p^{\sigma}}M^{\tau_{2},\sigma}_{p},\quad 0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2},\quad\sigma>1

\widetilde{(M.5)}

\displaystyle(\forall h>0)\,(\exists C>0)\quad M^{\tau_{1},\sigma_{1}}_{p}\leq Ch^{p^{\sigma_{2}}}M^{\tau_{2},\sigma_{2}}_{p},\,\,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}>0,\,1<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}.

Note that

Ch^{p^{\sigma}}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}\geq M^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{p}\geq 1,\quad C,h>0,\quad p\in\mathbf{N},

and hence we obtain weaker condition

(\forall h>0)\,(\exists C>0)\quad{h^{p^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}\geq C,\quad p\in\mathbf{N}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us briefly comment the case
\sigma=1
. Then the conditions
\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{(M.2)}
are classical Komtasu’s
{(M.2)^{\prime}}
and
{(M.2)}
(respectively) for the Gevrey sequence
M_{p}=p!^{\tau}
. Moreover,

also holds. The theory of Gevrey functions is a classical one (see
[

15

and references therein), hence we are interested in the case

Note that

is also true for the case

(see
[

]

Recall (see
[
6
]
), a family of weight sequences
{\mathcal{M}}
is called

if

(\forall M_{p},N_{p}\in{\mathcal{M}})\quad M_{p}\leq N_{p}\,\,\vee\,\,N_{p}\leq M_{p},\quad p\in\mathbf{N}.

(2.2)

Example 2.1
.

For fixed

and
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}=\sigma
, (

Nevertheless, if we observe

we can only prove that

for a large positive constant

does not satisfy (

).

For two weight matrices

(\forall M_{p}\in{\mathcal{M}})\,(\exists N_{p}\in{\mathcal{N}})\,(\exists C>0)\quad M_{p}\leq C^{p}N_{p},\quad p\in\mathbf{N}.

We say that

]
).

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function and
\omega_{1}
equivalent to

. Notice that
\displaystyle\{M_{p}^{H}=e^{\frac{1}{H}\varphi^{*}(Hp)}\}_{H>0}
and

Put

\displaystyle m^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}=\frac{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}},\qquad p\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}.

(2.3)

By
(M.1)
it follows that

is an increasing sequence.
Moreover, the following Lemma holds.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{0}=1
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, and let

be given by

). Then the following inequalities hold

\Big(\frac{e}{2^{\sigma}}\Big)^{\frac{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}{2^{\sigma-1}}}p^{\frac{\tau\sigma p^{\sigma-1}}{2^{\sigma-1}}}\leq m^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}\leq e^{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}p^{\tau\sigma p^{\sigma-1}},\quad p\geq 2.

(2.4)

Proof.

Set
\displaystyle f_{\tau,\sigma}(x)=\tau x^{\sigma}\ln x
,
x>0
. By the mean value theorem, for every

there exists
\theta_{p}
such that

f_{\tau,\sigma}(p)-f_{\tau,\sigma}(p-1)=\tau\theta_{p}^{\sigma-1}\ln(e\theta_{p}^{\sigma}),\quad p-1<\theta_{p}<p.

(2.5)

For

, we obtain

\frac{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}{2^{\sigma-1}}\ln\frac{ep^{\sigma}}{2^{\sigma}}\leq\tau(p-1)^{\sigma-1}\ln(e(p-1)^{\sigma})<\tau\theta_{p}^{\sigma-1}\ln(e\theta_{p}^{\sigma})<\tau p^{\sigma-1}\ln(ep^{\sigma}),

\frac{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}{2^{\sigma-1}}\ln\frac{ep^{\sigma}}{2^{\sigma}}\leq\tau p^{\sigma}\ln p-\tau(p-1)^{\sigma}\ln(p-1)\leq\tau p^{\sigma-1}\ln(ep^{\sigma}),\quad p\geq 2.

Then (

∎

.

Note that

2.2.

In this subsection we recall the definition and some elementary properties of

2.3.
Extended Gevrey classes

In this subsection we recall the definition of extended Gevrey classes and some of their basic properties. We consider general sequences which satisfy conditions
(M.1)-\widetilde{(M.5)}
.

Let
U
be an open set in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
and
K\subset\subset U
be a regular compact set. We denote by
\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma,h}(K)
the Banach space of functions
\phi\in C^{\infty}(K)
such that

\|\phi\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma,h}(K)}=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbf{N}^{d}}\sup_{x\in K}\frac{|\partial^{\alpha}\phi(x)|}{h^{|\alpha|^{\sigma}}M_{|\alpha|}^{\tau,\sigma}}<\infty.\,

(2.8)

Note that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau_{1},\sigma_{1},h_{1}}(K)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau_{2},\sigma_{2},h_{2}}(K),\;\;\;0<h_{1}<h_{2},\;0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2},\;1<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2},

where
\hookrightarrow
denotes the strict and dense inclusion. We define spaces of Roumieu and Beurling type
by introducing the following inductive and projective limit topologies (respectively)

{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\tau,\sigma\}}(U)=\varprojlim_{K\subset\subset U}\varinjlim_{h\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma,h}(K),

(2.9)

{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma)}(U)=\varprojlim_{K\subset\subset U}\varprojlim_{h\to 0}{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma,h}(K).

(2.10)

We omit the brackets if we consider either
\{\tau,\sigma\}
or
(\tau,\sigma)
.

Remark 2.5
.

The condition
(M.3)^{\prime}
implies that
{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
contains compactly supported functions. The construction of smooth compactly supported functions which are not in Gevrey classes but which belong to
{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
can be found in
[
11
]
.

Extended Gevrey classes given by (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) are studied in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we collect some of their basic properties in the following Proposition. Recall, the Gevrey class of index
t>1
is given by
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}(U)=\mathcal{E}_{\{t,1\}}(U)
, see (
2.9
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
U
be an open set in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\tau,\sigma\}}(U)
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma)}(U)
be given by (

) and (

) respectively, and let

and
\varprojlim
denote the corresponding inductive and projective limits respectively.
Then the following is true:

i)

For
\sigma_{2}>\sigma_{1}>1
we have

\varinjlim_{t\to\infty}{\mathcal{G}}_{t}(U)\hookrightarrow\varprojlim_{\tau\to 0}{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\tau,\sigma_{1}\}}(U)=\varprojlim_{\tau\to 0}{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma_{1})}(U)\hookrightarrow\varinjlim_{\tau\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma_{1})}(U)\\
=\varinjlim_{\tau\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\tau,\sigma_{1}\}}(U)\hookrightarrow\varprojlim_{\tau\to 0}{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\tau,\sigma_{2}\}}(U)\hookrightarrow C^{\infty}(U).

(2.11)

ii)

{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
are closed under the pontwise multiplication.

iii)

{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
are closed under finite order derivation.

iv)

For
a_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma)}(U)

3.
Main result

In this section we first give an estimate for
T_{\tau,\sigma}(k)=T_{\tau,\sigma,1}(k)
which is introduced in Subsection
2.2
. Note that
T_{\tau,\sigma}(k)
satisfies estimates (
2.7
) when
h=1
. Therefore the following Proposition follows directly from
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
. However, here we give an independent proof.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T_{\tau,\sigma}(k)=T_{\tau,\sigma,1}(k)
be given by (
2.6
) with
h=1
, and let

W(t)
,
t>0
, denote the restriction of the principal branch of the Lambert
W
function.
If

\displaystyle\varphi_{\sigma}(t)=\frac{t^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(t)}
,
t>0
, and
\varphi_{\sigma}(0)=0
, then we have

B_{\tau,\sigma}\varphi_{\sigma}(\ln_{+}k)+\widetilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}\leq T_{\tau,\sigma}(k)\leq A_{\tau,\sigma}\varphi_{\sigma}(\ln_{+}k)+\widetilde{A}_{\tau,\sigma},\quad k>0,

for suitable constants
A_{\tau,\sigma},B_{\tau,\sigma}>0
and
\widetilde{A}_{\tau,\sigma},\widetilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}\in\mathbf{R}
.

Proof.

For
\lambda>0
we let

\displaystyle m_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)=\#\{p\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}\,|\,m^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}\leq\lambda\},

and note that
m_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)=0
for all
0<\lambda<1
. This is due to the fact that
m_{1}=1
and
m^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
is increasing.

Since
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
satisfies
(M.1)
we can write (see
[
8
,
3
]
)

T_{\tau,\sigma}(k)=\int_{0}^{k}\frac{m_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)}{\lambda}d\lambda=\int_{1}^{k}\frac{m_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)}{\lambda}d\lambda.

In the sequel we estimate
m_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)
when
\lambda\geq 1
.

Put

m^{C}_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)=\#\{p\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}\,|\,C^{p^{\sigma-1}}p^{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}\leq\lambda\},\quad C>0.

Then (
2.4
) implies that

m^{C_{1}}_{(\tau\sigma),\sigma}(\lambda)\leq m_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)\leq m^{C_{2}}_{(\tau\sigma)/2^{\sigma-1},\sigma}(\lambda),\quad\lambda\geq 1,

where
C_{1}=e^{\tau}
and
C_{2}=(e/2^{\sigma})^{\tau/2^{\sigma-1}}
. In particular,

\int_{1}^{k}\frac{m^{C_{1}}_{(\tau\sigma),\sigma}(\lambda)}{\lambda}d\lambda\leq T_{\tau,\sigma}(k)\leq\int_{1}^{k}\frac{m^{C_{2}}_{(\tau\sigma)/2^{\sigma-1},\sigma}(\lambda)}{\lambda}d\lambda,\quad k>0.

(3.1)

Next we note that

C^{p^{\sigma-1}}p^{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}\leq\lambda\iff C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}p^{\sigma-1}\ln(C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}p^{\sigma-1})\leq C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}\ln\lambda\\
\iff\ln(C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}p^{\sigma-1})\leq W(C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}\ln\lambda)\iff\\
p\leq C^{-\frac{1}{\tau}}{e}^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}W(C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}\ln\lambda)},\quad C>0,\,\,\lambda\geq 1,

(3.2)

where for the second equivalence we used property (
1.3
) of the Lambert function.

This calculation shows that

m^{C}_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)=\Big\lfloor C^{-\frac{1}{\tau}}{e}^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}W(C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}\ln\lambda)}\Big\rfloor,\quad\lambda\geq 1.

and therefore

\int_{1}^{k}\frac{m^{C}_{\tau,\sigma}(\lambda)}{\lambda}d\lambda\sim C^{-\frac{1}{\tau}}\int_{1}^{k}\frac{{e}^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}W(C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}\ln\lambda)}}{\lambda}d\lambda,\,\quad k\to\infty.

It remains to compute

I^{C}_{\tau,\sigma}(k):=C^{-\frac{1}{\tau}}\int_{1}^{k}\frac{{e}^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}W(C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}\ln\lambda)}}{\lambda}d\lambda,\quad C>0.

Set
C_{\tau,\sigma}=C^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\tau}
. Note that after the substitution
t=C_{\tau,\sigma}\ln\lambda
we obtain

I^{C}_{\tau,\sigma}(k)=C^{-\frac{\sigma}{\tau}}\frac{\tau}{\sigma-1}\int_{0}^{C_{\tau,\sigma}\ln k}{e}^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}W(t)}dt.

(3.3)

Another change of variables
W(t)=s

(t=se^{s},dt=(s+1)e^{s}ds)
, and integration by parts yields

\int{e}^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}W(t)}dt=\int{e}^{\frac{\sigma s}{\sigma-1}}(s+1)ds={e}^{\frac{\sigma s}{\sigma-1}}\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}\Big(s+\frac{1}{\sigma}\Big),

(3.4)

where we use indefinite integral just for the notational convenience.

Now using
(W2)
property of the Lambert function and (
1.5
), by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we have

I^{C}_{\tau,\sigma}(k)=\frac{\tau}{\sigma}C^{-\frac{\sigma}{\tau}}{e}^{\frac{\sigma s}{\sigma-1}}\Big(s+\frac{1}{\sigma}\Big)\Big|_{0}^{W(t)}=\\
\frac{\tau}{\sigma}C^{-\frac{\sigma}{\tau}}\Big(\frac{t}{W(t)}\Big)^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(W(t)+\frac{1}{\sigma})\Big|_{0}^{C_{\tau,\sigma}\ln k}\asymp\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\varphi_{\sigma}(\ln_{+}k)+\widetilde{C}_{\tau,\sigma},\ k>0,

(3.5)

for some
\widetilde{C}_{\tau,\sigma}\in\mathbf{R}
, where the hidden constants are depending only on
\sigma
.

More precisely, using (
3.1
) and (
3.5
) we conclude

B_{\sigma}\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\varphi_{\sigma}(\ln_{+}k)+\widetilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}\leq T_{\tau,\sigma}(k)\leq A_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{\tau}{2^{\sigma-1}}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\varphi_{\sigma}(\ln_{+}k)+\widetilde{A}_{\tau,\sigma},\,\,k>0,

(3.6)

for suitable
A_{\sigma},B_{\sigma}>0
and
\widetilde{A}_{\tau,\sigma},\widetilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}\in\mathbf{R}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Following
[
6
,
14
]
we introduce the Banach space
{\mathcal{B}}_{H,\sigma}(K)
,
K\subset\subset U
, with the norm

\|\phi\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{H,\sigma}(K)}=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbf{N}^{d}}\sup_{x\in K}{|\partial^{\alpha}\phi(x)|}e^{-\frac{1}{H}\varphi_{\sigma}^{*}(Hp)},\quad H>0,

where
\varphi^{*}_{\sigma}
is Youngs conjugate of the function

introduced in Proposition

.

We introduce the corresponding Roumieu and Beurling classes as follows:

{\mathcal{B}}_{\{\sigma\}}(U)=\varprojlim_{K\subset\subset U}\varinjlim_{H\to\infty}{\mathcal{B}}_{H,\sigma}(U),\quad{\mathcal{B}}_{(\sigma)}(U)=\varprojlim_{K\subset\subset U}\varprojlim_{H\to 0}{\mathcal{B}}_{H,\sigma}(U),

respectively.

Now we can formulate the main result of the paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix
\sigma>1
and let
\displaystyle\varphi_{\sigma}(t)
be as in Proposition

. Moreover, let

i)

The function

is equivalent to a weight function. Moreover, for every

and

ii)

The weight matrices

and
{\mathcal{N}}_{\sigma}=\{\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{H}\varphi_{\sigma}^{*}(Hp)}\}_{H>0}
are equivalent. In particular,

{\mathcal{B}}_{(\sigma)}(U)={\mathcal{E}}_{(\sigma)}^{B}(U),\quad{\mathcal{B}}_{\{\sigma\}}(U)={\mathcal{E}}_{\{\sigma\}}^{R}(U)

(3.7)

where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{(\sigma)}^{B}(U)
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\sigma\}}^{R}(U)
are given in (
2.13
).

Proof.

i)
By Proposition
3.1
it follows that functions
T_{\tau,\sigma}(|k|)\asymp\varphi_{\sigma}(\ln_{+}|k|)
. Thus, it is sufficient to show that
\displaystyle T_{\tau,\sigma}(|k|)
is a weight functions (see Remark
2.2
).

Since
T_{\tau,\sigma}
is the function associated to

, by
[
1
, Lemma 12]
it is sufficient to show that

given by

satisfies (

Note that Lemma

\frac{m^{\tau,\sigma}_{3p}}{m^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}}\geq\frac{\Big(\frac{e}{2^{\sigma}}\Big)^{\frac{\tau(3p)^{\sigma-1}}{2^{\sigma-1}}}(3p)^{\frac{\tau\sigma(3p)^{\sigma-1}}{2^{\sigma-1}}}}{e^{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}p^{\tau\sigma p^{\sigma-1}}}\\
=\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)^{\tau\sigma(\frac{3p}{2})^{\sigma-1}}\exp\Big\{\tau\Big(({3}/{2})^{\sigma-1}-1\Big)p^{\sigma-1}\Big\}p^{\tau\sigma\Big(({3}/{2})^{\sigma-1}-1\Big)p^{\sigma-1}}\to\infty,\,\,p\to\infty,

and (

) follows when

.

ii)
Let

and

4.
Discussion

The equivalence of theories of ultradifferentiable functions given by Komatsu’s
or the Braun-Meise-Taylor approach are well established in the most classical situations.
Recent approach based on weighted matrices seems to offer a very general construction,
see
[
14
,
6
]
. In parallel, it is demonstrated in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
that the two-parameter sequences of the form
M_{p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, provide a useful extension of the Gevrey type spaces.

In this paper we show that the projective limits of extended Gevrey classes
can be viewed as a part of the construction based on the weighted matrices.
The same conclusion holds when the inductive limits of extended Gevrey classes
are replaced by certain slightly larger spaces.
At the same time, extended Gevrey classes
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
for fixed
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
,
can not be characterized by weight matrices used in
[
14
,
6
]

due to the particular role played by the parameter
\sigma
.

While finishing the paper the authors learned about the work in progress
”A comparison of two ways to generalize ultradifferentiable
classes defined by weight sequences” by J. Jiménez-Garrido, D. N. Nenning, and G. Schindl,
which is devoted to similar topic considered from a different point of view.
We thank the authors for their fruitful comments on the first version of this paper.

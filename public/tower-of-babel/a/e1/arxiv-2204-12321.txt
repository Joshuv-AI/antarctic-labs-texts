Abstract:

The statistically unbounded
p
-convergence is an abstraction of the statistical order, unbounded order, and
p
-convergences. We investigate the concept of the statistically unbounded convergence on lattice-normed Riesz spaces with respect to statistical p-decreasing sequences. Also, we get some relations between this concept and the other kinds of statistical convergences on Riesz spaces.

1
Introduction

A Riesz space is an ordered vector space, which was introduced by Riesz in
[
21
]
. Most of the spaces encountered in the analysis are Riesz spaces. Moreover, Riesz spaces have many applications in measure theory, operator theory, optimization, problems of Banach spaces, measure theory, and applications in economics (cf.
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
19
,
25
]
).

The
order convergence
is one of the fundamental concepts in the study of Riesz spaces, which is not topological in general (cf.
[
16
]
). However, even without a topological structure, several kinds of continuous operators can be defined. Another fundamental concept in the theory of Riesz spaces is
unbounded order convergence
, which was firstly introduced in
[
20
]
under the name individual convergence. A lot of work has been done since then (cf.
[
9
,
10
,
15
,
24
]
). The unbounded order convergence will be the basic tool of this paper. Lattice-valued norms on vector lattices provide natural and efficient tools in the theory of vector lattices. It is enough to mention the theory of lattice-normed vector lattices (cf.
[
11
,
18
,
17
]
).

The theory of the
statistical convergence
is an active area of research, which is a generalization of the ordinary convergence of a real sequence, and the idea of the statistical convergence was firstly introduced by Zygmund
[
26
]
. After then, Fast
[
13
]
and Steinhaus
[
22
]
independently improved that idea. Several applications and generalizations of the statistical convergence of sequences have been investigated by several authors (cf.
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
14
,
23
]
. Most of the works on the theory of the statistical convergence are handled with respect to a given topology. But, Ercan introduced a concept of the statistical convergence in Riesz spaces without topology in
[
12
]
.Then Şençimen and Pehlivan extended this concept to Riesz spaces with respect to the order convergence; see
[
23
]
. Recently, Aydın et al., have investigated some studies about the statistical convergence on Riesz spaces, Riesz algebras, and locally solid Riesz spaces; see
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. In the present paper, we aim to introduce the concept of the statistically unbounded
p
-convergence on lattice-normed spaces and illustrate the usefulness of lattice-valued norms for the investigation of different types of statistical convergence in Riesz spaces.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we give several notions related to lattice-normed spaces and statistical convergence. In Section 3, we introduce the concept of the statistically unbounded convergence in lattice-normed spaces. In the last section, we show some main results.

Definition 2.1
.

A real-valued vector space

\leq

(a)

(b)

\lambda x\leq\lambda y
for every
0\leq\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

An ordered vector space

is called a
Riesz space
or a

if, for any two vectors

x\wedge y=\inf\{x,y\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ x\vee y=\sup\{x,y\}

exist in
E
, respectively. A Riesz space is called
Dedekind complete
if every nonempty bounded above subset has a supremum (or, equivalently, whenever every nonempty bounded below subset has an infimum). For an element
x
in a Riesz spaces
E
,
the positive part
,
the negative part
, and

of
x

x^{+}:=x\vee 0,\ \ \ x^{-}:=(-x)\vee 0\ \ and\ \ |x|:=x\vee(-x).

In the present paper, the vertical bar
|\cdot|
of elements of the Riesz spaces will stand for the module of the given elements. A Riesz space
E
is called

\frac{1}{n}x\downarrow 0
holds in

. Unless otherwise stated, we assume that all vector lattices are real and Archimedean.

A sequence
(x_{n})
in a Riesz space

is said to be

whenever
x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots
, and

if
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots
. Then we denote them by

and
x_{n}\downarrow

x_{n}\uparrow

x_{n}\uparrow x
. Similarly, if
x_{n}\downarrow

\inf x_{n}=x

x_{n}\downarrow x
. Then we call that
(x_{n})
is increasing or decreasing as

. Moreover, we remind that a sequence
(x_{n})
in a Riesz space

is called

-

order convergent
to

(

, for short

(q_{n})\downarrow 0
in

-

It is clear that order convergence implies

c_{0}
or

then

Definition 2.2

Let

3
Statistically unbounded
p
-convergence

We introduce the statistically unbounded convergence on
LNRS
s in this section. Recall that a sequence
(x_{n})
in an
LNRS

(X,p,E)
is said to be
unbounded
p
-convergent
to
x\in X
(shortly,

x_{n}\xrightarrow{up}x
), if
p(|x_{n}-x|\wedge u)\oc 0
for all
u\in X_{+};
see
[
9
, Def.6]
. The following definition is motivated by the notion of
up
-convergence.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,p,E)
be an
LNRS
and
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
X
. Then
(x_{n})
is said to be
statistically unbounded
p
-convergence
to
x
if, for any positive element
u\in X_{+}
, there exists a sequence
q_{n}\stpd 0
in
X
with an index set
K=\{n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta(K)=1
and

p(|x_{k_{n}}-x|\wedge u)\leq q_{k_{n}}

for all
k_{n}\in K
. Then we write
x_{n}\stupc x
.

It is clear that a sequence
x_{n}\stupc x
holds in an
LNRS

(X,p,E)
if and only if
(x_{n}-x)\stupc 0
if and only if
p(|x_{n}-x|\wedge u)\sto 0
in
E
for all
u\in X_{+}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
X^{*}
be the algebraic dual of a Riesz space
X
and
Y
be a sublattice of
X^{*}
such that
\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle
is a dual system. Then define an
LNRS

(X,p,{\mathbb{R}}^{Y})
with a lattice norm
p:X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{Y}
denoted by
p(x)[f]:=|f|(|x|)
. Take a sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
. Thus, it is clear that
|x_{n}|\wedge u\xrightarrow{|\sigma|(X,Y)}0
for every
u\in X_{+}
, and so, we have
x_{n}\stupc 0
.

The next observations follow directly from the basic definitions and results, and so, we omit their proofs.

Remark 3.3
.

(i)

It follows from the inequality
|a-b|\wedge u\leq|a-b|
in Riesz spaces and by the monotonicity of
p
in
LNRS

p
-convergence implies
st
-
up
-convergence.

(ii)

Statistically unbounded
p
-convergence coincides with the notion of statistical
p
-convergence for order bounded sequences.

(iii)

Consider an
LNRS

(L_{p}(\mu),\lVert\cdot\rVert,\mathbb{R})
for
1\leq p<\infty
, where
\mu
is a finite measure. Then the convergence in measure implies
st
-
up
-convergence of sequences in
L_{p}(\mu)
; see
[
24

(iv)

Take the

(L_{p},\lvert\cdot\rvert,L_{p})

-spaces implies
st
-

-convergence of sequences in
L_{p}
; see
[
15
]
.

(v)

Consider an
LNRS

(E,|\cdot|,E)
for an arbitrary Riesz space

. Then statistically unbounded order convergence and

-

-convergence agree.

(vi)

Every

-convergent sequence is statistically unbounded

-convergent to its
up
-limit.

(vii)

A statistical

-decreasing sequence is
st
-

-convergent.

(viii)

Take an
LNRS

(X,p,E)
, where

is

(

),

or

. Then the coordinate-wise convergence of a sequence implies

-
up
-convergence.

(ix)

An order convergent sequence in

st
-
up
-convergent to its order limit.

The converse of the properties of Remark

Consider the

(c_{0},|\cdot|,c_{0})

Example 3.5

Let’s consider the

(\mathbb{R}^{2},|\cdot|,\mathbb{R}^{2})

\mathbb{R}^{2}

(x_{n})
in

c

Theorem 3.8

If

Proof.

Let

q_{n}\stpd 0
and
t_{n}\stpd 0
with
\delta(K)=\delta(M)=1

p|(x_{k_{n}}-x|\wedge u)\leq q_{k_{n}}

p(|x_{m_{n}}-y|\wedge u)\leq t_{m_{n}}

k_{n}\in K
and
m_{n}\in M
. By using the inequality

(cf.

\displaystyle p(|x_{n}\vee y_{n}-x\vee y|\wedge u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(|x_{n}\vee y_{n}-x_{n}\vee y|\wedge u)+p(|x_{n}\vee y-x\vee y|\wedge u)

\displaystyle p(|y_{n}-y|\wedge u)+p(|x_{n}-x|\wedge u)

(q_{j_{n}}+t_{j_{n}})\downarrow 0

.

Let
(X,p,E)
be an
LNRS

x_{n}\stupc x

(i)

(v)

The properties

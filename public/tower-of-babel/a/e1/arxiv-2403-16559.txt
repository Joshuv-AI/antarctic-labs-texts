On divergent on average trajectories for higher rank actions
Wooyeon Kim

Abstract
. We say that a pair ( ξ 1 , ξ 2 ) ∈ ℝ 2 (\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2} satisfies the inhomogeneous Littlewood conjecture if lim inf q → ∞ q ​ ‖ q ​ ξ 1 − θ 1 ‖ ℤ ​ ‖ q ​ ξ 2 − θ 2 ‖ ℤ = 0 \liminf_{q\to\infty}q\|q\xi_{1}-\theta_{1}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\|q\xi_{2}-\theta_{2}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}=0 for all ( θ 1 , θ 2 ) ∈ ℝ 2 (\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2} , where ∥ ⋅ ∥ ℤ \|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{Z}} denotes the distance to the nearest integer. 1.
Introduction

The study of orbits in the space of lattices, identified with
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
, offers a rich interplay between geometry, topology, and arithmetic. As this homogeneous space is non-compact, understanding the behavior of orbits near the cusp is particularly significant, both as an intrinsic interest and due to its profound connections to number theory.

For
d\geq 2
and a one-parameter unipotent subgroup
\left\{u_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, Margulis proved that the trajectory
u_{t}x
is non-divergent for any
x\in\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})

[
Mrg71
]
. This result was used as an ingredient in his proof of the arithmeticity of nonuniform lattices in semisimple Lie groups of higher rank
[
Mrg75
]
. Building on this, Dani strengthened Margulis’s non-divergence theorem by showing that the mass of the trajectory
u_{t}x
does not escape to infinity
[
Dan79
,
Dan86
]
. More precisely, he showed that for any
\eta>0
there exists a compact set
K\subset\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

(1.1)

{m_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\{t\in[0,T]:\;u_{t}x\notin K\}\big)<\eta T}

for sufficiently large
T
and
x\in K
, where
m_{\mathbb{R}}
stands for the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. This result laid the groundwork for Ratner’s celebrated theorem, which provides a complete classification of invariant measures and orbit closures for unipotent flows.

In contrast, the dynamics of diagonalizable subgroup actions can exhibit much wilder behavior. For instance, there are divergent orbits and those whose mass escapes to infinity (divergent on average) for diagonalizable subgroups. This naturally leads to the question: What is the Hausdorff dimension of such escaping orbits? The Hausdorff dimension of the set of divergent (or divergent on-average) points for one-dimensional diagonalizable subgroups has been extensively studied by many authors, as will be detailed in the next subsection.

Although one-dimensional diagonalizable subgroups do not exhibit the measure rigidity phenomena exemplified by Ratner’s theorem, Furstenberg, Margulis, and others conjectured that higher-rank diagonalizable actions have rigidity properties analogous to those of unipotent flows. Over the last two decades, there has been significant progress towards the conjectural rigidity properties of higher-rank actions. In particular, Einsiedler, Katok, and Lindenstrauss characterized invariant and ergodic measures for higher-rank actions under the assumption of positive entropy
[
EKL06
,
EL15
,
EL18
,
EL23
]
. These rigidity results have led to striking consequences in number theory, including a significant advance towards the Littlewood conjecture in
[
EKL06
]
.

However, unlike unipotent dynamics, the link between individual orbits and invariant measures for higher-rank diagonalizable actions is weaker, primarily due to the absence of a non-divergence theorem analogous to that in unipotent dynamics. Consequently, characterizing divergent on-average orbits becomes essential for understanding individual orbits in higher-rank actions. The goal of this paper is to estimate the Hausdorff dimension of the set of divergent on-average points for higher-rank actions. As an application, we use this result to determine the Hausdorff dimension of the set of exceptions to the inhomogeneous uniform version of the Littlewood conjecture.

1.1.
one-dimensional diagonal flows

We begin by reviewing previous results on the Hausdorff dimension of divergent trajectories (or those that are divergent on average) for one-dimensional diagonal flows. For
d\geq 3
, let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
X=G/\Gamma
. We consider a one-parameter diagonal subgroup
\{g_{t}\}<G
given by
g_{t}:=\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{t},\ldots,e^{t},e^{-(d-1)t}\right)
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
x\in X
.

(1)

We say that
x
is
g_{t}
-divergent if for any compact set
K\subset X
there exists
T(K)>0
such that
g_{t}x\notin K
for all
t\geq T(K)
.

(2)

We say that
x
is
g_{t}
-divergent on average if

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\#\left\{t\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}:g_{t}x\in K\right\}=0

for any compact set
K\subset X
.

Let
U
be the (unstable) horospherical subgroup for
g_{t}
, i.e.

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\left\{g\in G:g_{t}gg_{-t}\rightarrow e\quad\textrm{as}\quad t\to-\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\left(\begin{matrix}\operatorname{Id}_{d-1}&\boldsymbol{\xi}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\in G:\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\right\}.

It is well known from the Dani correspondence that Diophantine properties of a vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
are characterized by dynamical properties of the
g_{t}
-orbit of the point
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
on the submanifold
U\Gamma
of
X

[
Dan85
,
KM98
]
. In particular,
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
g_{t}
-divergent if and only if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
singular
, i.e. for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
T_{0}
such that for all
T>T_{0}
the system of inequalities

\|q\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}<\epsilon T^{-\frac{1}{d}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad 0<q<T

admits an integral solution
q\in\mathbb{N}

[
Dan85
, Theorem 2.14]
. Similarly,
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
g_{t}
-divergent on average if and only if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
singular on average
, i.e. for every
\epsilon>0

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left|\left\{l\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}:\textrm{there exists }q\in\mathbb{N}\ \text{s.t.}\ \|q\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}<\epsilon 2^{-l}\ \textrm{and}\ 0<q<2^{l}\right\}\right|=1.

It is natural to ask about the set of singular (or singular on average) vectors, or equivalently the set of
g_{t}
-divergent (or divergent on average) points in the space of lattices. In the last decade the Hausdorff dimension of these sets has been calculated by several authors:
[
Che11
]
proved that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:\boldsymbol{\xi}\textrm{ is singular}\right\}

\displaystyle=\dim_{H}\left\{x\in U\Gamma:x\textrm{ is }g_{t}\textrm{-divergent}\right\}

\displaystyle=d-1-\frac{d-1}{d}

for
d=3
, and
[
CC16
]
extended this result for all
d\geq 3
. For the set of singular on average vectors,
[
EK12
]
showed that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:\boldsymbol{\xi}\textrm{ is singular on average}\right\}

\displaystyle=\dim_{H}\left\{x\in U\Gamma:x\textrm{ is }g_{t}\textrm{-divergent on average}\right\}

\displaystyle=d-1-\frac{d-1}{d}

for
d=3
, and
[
KKLM17
]
extended this for all
d\geq 3
. More generally,
[
KKLM17
]
considered the set of singular (or singular on average) linear forms and gave an upper bound for the Hausdorff dimension, and
[
DFSU19
]
later proved that this upper bound is indeed sharp. See also
[
LSST19
,
Sol21
,
KP22
]
for the results for weighted
1
-dimensional diagonal flows on the space of lattices, and
[
GS20
,
AGMS21
]
for the results on more general homogeneous spaces.

1.2.
Higher rank diagonal actions

We now turn to higher-rank diagonal actions. We denote

a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:=\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{\tau_{1}},\ldots,e^{\tau_{d-1}},e^{-(\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{d-1})}\right)

for
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
Let
A<G
denote the group of
d\times d
positive diagonal matrices, i.e.
A=\left\{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\right\}
. We also denote
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}_{>0}
the set of vectors with positive entries, and let
A^{+}
be the subsemigroup
\left\{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d-1}\right\}
of
A
. Note that
A^{+}
consists of the diagonal elements in
A
expanding the horospherical subgroup
U
. As we defined for
1
-dimensional diagonal subgroups, let us define the notion of divergent (divergent on average) trajectories for higher-rank diagonal actions.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
x\in X
.

(1)

We say that
x
is
A^{+}
-divergent if for any compact set
K\subset X
there exists
T(K)>0
such that
a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}x\notin K
for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}
with
\displaystyle\min_{1\leq i\leq d-1}\tau_{i}\geq T(K)
.

(2)

We say that
x
is
A^{+}
-divergent on average if

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-1}}\#\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}^{d-1}:a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}x\in K\right\}=0

for any compact set
K\subset X
.

The set of divergent trajectories for higher-rank actions can be characterized algebraically. Margulis showed that
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
A^{+}
-divergent if and only if
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d-1}
. In general homogeneous spaces of reductive real algebraic groups,
[
TW03
,
ST23
]
showed that every divergent trajectory of a real torus diverges due to a purely algebraic reason if the dimension of the torus is at least the
\mathbb{Q}
-rank of the lattice subgroup.

In contrast to the set of divergent trajectories, not much has been known about the set of divergent on average trajectories for higher-rank actions. The first main result of this paper is an upper bound for the Hausdorff dimension of the set of
A^{+}
-divergent on average points on the closed horosphere
U\Gamma
.

Theorem 1.3
.

We have

{\dim_{H}\left\{x\in U\Gamma:x\textrm{ is }A^{+}\textrm{-divergent on average}\right\}\leq\frac{d-1}{2}.}

We remark that the upper bound in Theorem
1.3
is indeed sharp for
d=3
. Let us denote by
\mathcal{R}_{d}
the set of
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1})\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}
such that

{\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(1,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1})\leq 2.}

Since
\mathcal{R}_{d}
is a countable union of rational lines, we have
\dim_{H}\mathcal{R}_{d}=1
for all
d\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathcal{R}_{d}
,
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
A^{+}
-divergent on average.

Combining Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
we have

(1.2)

{1\leq\dim_{H}\left\{x\in U\Gamma:x\textrm{ is }A^{+}\textrm{-divergent on average}\right\}\leq\frac{d-1}{2},}

hence for
d=3
the Hausdorff dimension is exactly
1
.

1.3.
Inhomogeneous uniform version of Littlewood conjecture

Theorem
1.3
has number-theoretic consequences, particularly in Diophantine approximation. Around 1930, Littlewood conjectured that

(1.3)

{\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\|q\xi_{1}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\|q\xi_{2}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}=0}

for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Although Littlewood conjecture remains open,
[
EKL06
]
established that the set of exceptions to Littlewood conjecture has Hausdorff dimension zero. The key ingredient to this result was their classification theorem of ergodic
A
-invariant measures with positive entropy.

The Littlewood conjecture can be extended in a natural way to inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation. Shapira
[
Sha11
]
proved that almost every pair of real numbers
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
satisfies the following inhomogeneous uniform version of Littlewood conjecture:

(1.4)

\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\|q\xi_{1}-\theta_{1}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\|q\xi_{2}-\theta_{2}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}=0\;\textrm{ for all }\;\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

answering a question of Cassels
[
Cas72
, p.307]
. Furthermore,
[
Sha11
]
also proved that (
1.4
) holds if
1,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
forms a basis of a real cubic field, and does not hold if
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{R}_{3}
, i.e.
1,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{Q}
. In
[
GV17
]
, Gorodnik and Vishe obtained a quantitative refinement of (
1.4
) by a factor of
(\log\log\log\log\log q)^{\lambda}
for almost every pair, for some constant
\lambda>0
. Recently, in
[
CT23
]
Chow and Technau established an inhomogeneous analog of Gallagher’s theorem, i.e. a quantitative refinement of (
1.4
) by a factor of
(\log q)^{2}
for almost every pair.

Motivated by the results in
[
Sha11
]
, Bugeaud asked the following question:

Question 1.5
.

[
Bug14
, Problem 24]

Is it true that, we have (
1.4
) for all
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\notin\mathcal{R}_{3}
?

Theorem
1.3
, as well as its refinement which will be described in §5, has the following implication toward Question
1.5
, in the sense of the Hausdorff dimension of the set of exceptions.

Theorem 1.6
.

For
d\geq 3
, let
\Xi
be the set of
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
such that

(1.5)

\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}>0\;\textrm{ for some }\;\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d-1}\in\mathbb{R}.

Then we have
1\leq\dim_{H}\Xi\leq\frac{d-1}{2}
.

In particular, for
d=3
the Hausdorff dimension of the set of exceptions to the inhomogeneous uniform version of Littlewood conjecture is
1
, which is equal to the Hausdorff dimension of the conjectural set of exceptions
\mathcal{R}_{3}
.

1.4.
Discussion of the proofs

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\left\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d-1}\right\}
the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. For
1\leq i\leq d-1
we let

A_{i}:=\left\{a_{t\mathbf{e}_{i}}\in G:t\in\mathbb{R}\right\},\qquad\qquad U_{i}:=\left\{u_{i}(s):=u(s\mathbf{e}_{i})\in G:s\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

We also let
U_{i}^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal complement of
U_{i}
in
U
, and let
H_{i}
denote the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-copy in
G
containing
A_{i}
and
U_{i}
.

The proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.6
rely heavily on various height functions and their associated contraction properties. In §2, we construct height functions for one-dimensional diagonal subgroups and establish their contraction hypotheses. Let
0<\lambda<1
be fixed, sufficiently close to 1, and
t
be sufficiently large depending on
\lambda
. For each
1\leq i\leq d-1
, the Benoist-Quint height function
\alpha_{i}:X\to[1,\infty]
satisfies

(1)

(Subharmonic estimate) For any
x\in X
,

\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{i}(a_{t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds\leq e^{-\lambda t}\alpha_{i}(x)+C,

(2)

(Log-Lipschitz property) For any
g\in B^{H_{i}}(\operatorname{id},1)
and
x\in X
,

C^{-1}\alpha_{i}(x)\leq\alpha_{i}(gx)\leq C\alpha_{i}(x),

where
C>1
is a constant depending only on
\lambda
and
t

[
BQ12
,
Shi20
,
Kha20
]
. Notably, the log-Lipschitz property for
\alpha_{i}
is restricted to perturbation under the subgroup
H_{i}
, which is insufficient for our purposes. To address this limitation, we define modified height functions
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}:X^{\prime}\to[1,\infty]
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
, where
X^{\prime}=A^{+}U\Gamma
. While these functions are defined only on a subset
X^{\prime}
of
X
, their log-Lipschitz property extends beyond
H_{i}
to the subgroup
U
as well.

We then construct
dynamical height functions

\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}:X^{\prime}\to[1,\infty]
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\delta<1
,
1\leq i\leq d-1
, and
\mathsf{h}\geq 1
. These functions
\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}
capture the averaged behavior of
A_{i}
-trajectories near the cusp. Specifically,
\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(x)
is positive only if the trajectory
\left\{a_{t\mathbf{e}_{i}}x,\ldots,a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}x\right\}
remains close to the cusp most of time, as measured by
\delta
(representing the proportion of mass escaping to infinity as
1-\delta
) and a cut-off height
\mathsf{h}
defined in terms of
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}
. In particular, if
x
is
A_{i}
-divergent on average, then

(1.6)

{x\in\bigcap_{\delta>0}\bigcap_{\mathsf{h}>1}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Supp}\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}.}

The following contraction hypothesis for
\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}
(Proposition
2.7
) is derived from the above contraction hypothesis for
\alpha_{i}
(or for
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}
): if
\mathsf{h}
is sufficiently large, then

(1.7)

{\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(u_{i}(s)x)ds\leq e^{-(\lambda^{2}-O(\delta))Nt}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(x).}

The proof of Proposition
2.7
is inspired by the approach used in
[
KKLM17
, Theorem 1.5]
. The above inequality (
1.7
) provides a covering number estimate for the sets
\operatorname{Supp}\big(\boldsymbol{\xi}\mapsto\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)\big)
. In view of (
1.6
), this establishes that the Hausdorff dimension of the set of points on
Ux
that are
A_{i}
-divergent on average is at most
\dim U-\frac{1}{2}
.

In §4.3 we introduce a dynamical height function
\psi_{N,\delta,\mathsf{h}}
for
A^{+}
and prove its contraction hypothesis. Constructed from
\beta_{N,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}
’s,
\psi_{N,\delta,\mathsf{h}}
detects the averaged behavior of an
A^{+}
-trajectory near the cusp. Specifically,
\psi_{N,\delta,\mathsf{h}}(x)
is positive only if the
A^{+}
-trajectory
\left\{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}x:\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\left\{t,\ldots,dNt\right\}^{d-1}\right\}
spends most of the time close to the cusp. Furthermore, if the
A^{+}
-trajectory exhibits an escape of mass exceeding
1-\delta
, then there exists
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}\in(t\mathbb{N})^{d-1}
with
\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}\|=O(\delta Nt)
such that
\left\{a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}+t\mathbf{e}_{i}}x,\ldots,a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}+dNt\mathbf{e}_{i}}\right\}
also has an escape of mass
1-O(\delta)
for every
1\leq i\leq d-1
. To summarize, for fixed
x\in X^{\prime}
if
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x
is
A^{+}
-divergent then

(1.8)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\in\bigcap_{\delta>0}\bigcap_{\mathsf{h}>1}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Supp}\big(\boldsymbol{\xi}\mapsto\psi_{N,\delta,\mathsf{h}}(u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)\big)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcap_{\delta>0}\bigcap_{\mathsf{h}>1}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigcap_{i=1}^{d-1}\operatorname{Supp}\big(\boldsymbol{\xi}\mapsto\beta_{dN,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)\big).

In other words, to calculate the Hausdorff dimension of
A^{+}
-divergent on average points on
Ux
it suffices to estimate

\mathsf{Q}:=\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\beta_{dN,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)d\boldsymbol{\xi}.

For a moment, let us assume that the maps
\boldsymbol{\xi}\mapsto\beta_{dN,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
are independent. In combination with (
1.7
) one can deduce that

\mathsf{Q}\asymp\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\beta_{dN,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)d\boldsymbol{\xi}\ll e^{-d(d-1)(\lambda^{2}-o(1))Nt}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(x).

This in turn implies that for
x\in X^{\prime}
the Hausdorff dimension of the set of points on
Ux
that are
A^{+}
-divergent on average is at most
\dim U-\frac{d-1}{2}=\frac{d-1}{2}
. Therefore, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is reduced to verifying a certain independence among the maps
\boldsymbol{\xi}\mapsto\beta_{dN,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
, which constitutes the technical heart of §3. The key observation for proving Proposition
3.4
is that different one-dimensional subgroups in
A^{+}
expand each direction in
U
at different expansion rates. Namely, a diagonal element
a_{t\mathbf{e}_{i}}
in
A_{i}
expands
U_{j}
at a rate of
e^{2t}
if
i=j
, and
e^{t}
otherwise. Using this, we approximate
\operatorname{Supp}\big(\boldsymbol{\xi}\mapsto\beta_{dN,\delta,i,\mathsf{h}}(a_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)\big)
as the union of boxes in
U
with side lengths
e^{-2dNt}
along
U_{i}
and
e^{-dNt}
along
U_{j}
for
j\neq i
. Then the intersection of these supports for
1\leq i\leq d-1
is then described as the union of cubes of side length
e^{-2dNt}
. Computing the volume of this intersection yields the optimal contraction rate, which is crucial for bounding the Hausdorff dimension. The proof of Theorem
1.3
, based on this optimal contraction rate, is completed in §4.4.

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.6
. We identify
\widehat{X}=\operatorname{ASL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{ASL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
with the space of unimodular affine lattices in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The proof relies on a measure rigidity theorem from
[
EL18
]
, which applies to
A
-action on this space.

For
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
we let
\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}\in\widehat{X}
denote the corresponding affine lattice
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\mathbb{Z}^{d}-\boldsymbol{\theta}
. By Dani’s correspondence on the space of affine lattices (Lemma
6.1
), if
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1})
satisfies Littelwood’s conjecture and
\displaystyle\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}>0
for some
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d-1})
, then there exist
\epsilon>0
such that
a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
for any sufficiently large
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}_{>0}
, where

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}:=\left\{\Lambda\in\widehat{X}:\Lambda\cap B^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,\epsilon)=\emptyset\right\}.

Note that the set
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is not compact for any
\epsilon>0
. In contrast, in the setting of homogeneous Diophantine approximation, the analogous set

\left\{\Lambda\in X:\Lambda\cap B^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,\epsilon)=\left\{0\right\}\right\}

in the space of lattices is compact by Mahler’s compactness criterion. This is the principal reason why a more careful treatment on the escape of mass is necessary in the setting of the inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
\dim_{H}\Xi>\frac{d-1}{2}
. Theorem
1.3
implies that most of the points in the set
\Xi
are not
A^{+}
-divergent on average. From the
A^{+}
-orbits of such points, one can construct an
A
-invariant measure
\mu
on
\widehat{X}
supported on
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
. Moreover,
\mu
is nonzero, as it is constructed from the points that are not
A^{+}
-divergent on average. This measure construction procedure is similar in spirit to those in
[
EKL06
,
LSS19
,
KKL
]
.

In Lemma
6.4
we show that for any
\epsilon>0
there is no homogeneous
A
-invariant measure supported on
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
. This contradicts the measure rigidity theorem
[
EL18
, Theorem 1.3]
, hence we conclude that
\dim_{H}\Xi\leq\frac{d-1}{2}
.

However, to apply
[
EL18
, Theorem 1.3]
, we must verify that
h_{\mu}(a)>0
for some
a\in A
, as the measure rigidity theorem requires a positive entropy assumption. To establish this, in §5 we strengthen the results in §2 and §3. Specifically, instead of focusing on the behavior of trajectories approaching the cusp, we construct dynamical height functions that capture the interaction of trajectories relative to a given reference trajectory. The contraction properties of these dynamical height functions ensure that the measure
\mu
not only has positive mass on
\widehat{X}
, but also exhibits positive entropy for some diagonal element
a\in A
. This allows us to apply the measure rigidity theorem and derive the desired dimension bound.

1.5.
Structure of the paper

This paper is organized as follows.

In Section 2 we modify the Benoist-Quint height function constructed in
[
BQ12
]
. We then use these modified Benoist-Quint height functions to construct

for one-dimensional diagonal subgroups and establish the contraction hypotheses for those dynamical height functions (Proposition

). The proof of Proposition

is inspired by
[

, Proof of Theorem 1.5]
.

In Section 3 we introduce dynamical height functions for higher-rank actions and establish the contraction hypothesis for higher-rank actions (Proposition

).

In Section 4 we establish the upper bound for the Hausdorff dimension of

-divergent on average trajectories (Theorem
1.3
) using Proposition

) by employing Dani’s correspondence in Diophantine approximation.

In Section 5 we discuss variants of the dynamical height functions defined in Sections 2 and 3. We then establish the contraction hypotheses for these dynamical height functions for a given trajectory in Proposition

In Section 6 we deduce Theorem

3.3.
Contraction for higher rank actions

We are now ready to prove the following inductive contraction inequality for higher-rank dynamical height functions.

Proposition 3.4

(Inductive contraction for
\psi
)
.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\delta<2^{-(d-1)}
,
\mathsf{h}>E_{\ref{101},\lambda}e^{t}
, and

\displaystyle\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\psi_{2N,\delta,D(\mathsf{h})}(u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)d\boldsymbol{\xi}\leq E_{\ref{104},\lambda}

\displaystyle N^{d^{2}}(8C_{\ref{5}})^{d(d-1)N}e^{-\{d(d-1)\lambda^{2}-C_{\ref{10}}\delta\}Nt}

\displaystyle\times\kappa(x)^{8}\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\psi_{N,2^{d-1}\delta,\mathsf{h}}(u(\boldsymbol{\xi})x)d\boldsymbol{\xi},

for some constants

\mathcal{D}_{N}\setminus\Omega(N,\mathsf{h},x)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2N}\setminus\Omega(2N,\mathsf{h},x)

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\#\Omega(N,\mathsf{h},x)}{\#\mathcal{D}_{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\#\big(\mathcal{D}_{2N}\setminus\Omega(2N,\mathsf{h},x)\big)}{\#\mathcal{D}_{N}}

\displaystyle=2^{d-1}\frac{\#\big(\mathcal{D}_{2N}\setminus\Omega(2N,\mathsf{h},x)\big)}{\#\mathcal{D}_{2N}}=2^{d-1}\left(1-\frac{\#\Omega(2N,\mathsf{h},x)}{\#\mathcal{D}_{2N}}\right),

for any

, hence
\operatorname{Supp}\psi_{2N,\delta,D(\mathsf{h})}\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}\psi_{N,2^{d-1}\delta,D(\mathsf{h})}
.

Let
F:[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

, and
\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{M}\right\}
be a maximal
e^{-(2d+4\delta)Nt}
-separated set of
\operatorname{Supp}F
as in Lemma
3.2
. Note that

(3.22)

{\operatorname{Supp}F\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}B(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k},e^{-(2d+4\delta)Nt}).}

By Lemma
3.1
, for each
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\operatorname{Supp}F
we can find some

\big(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\tau}_{d-1}\big)\in\mathcal{E}_{N,2\delta,1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{E}_{N,2\delta,d-1}

we obtain

\displaystyle M(N^{\prime},\delta,K_{D(\mathsf{h})})\leq

\displaystyle(2E_{\ref{104},\lambda})^{d^{2}l^{2}}\mathsf{M}(x)N^{d^{2}l}(8C_{\ref{5}})^{d(d-1)2^{l}N}

\displaystyle\times e^{\{d(d-1)(2^{l+1}-2^{l}\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{2})+(C_{\ref{10}}2^{dl}\delta+C_{\ref{11}})\}Nt}.

Note that the constants
C_{\ref{5}},E_{\ref{104},\lambda}
and
\mathsf{M}(\lambda)
are independent of
N,l
, and
\delta
while they might depend on
t
and
\lambda
. Therefore, for any
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0<\delta<2^{-(d-1)l}
,

\displaystyle\dim_{H}

\displaystyle\left(\bigcap_{N\geq N_{0}}Z_{x}(2^{l}N,\delta,K_{D(\mathsf{h})})\right)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\operatorname{box}}\left(\bigcap_{N\geq N_{0}}Z_{x}(2^{l}N,\delta,K_{D(\mathsf{h})})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log M(N^{\prime},\delta,K_{D(\mathsf{h})})}{-\log(e^{-2dN^{\prime}t})}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{d^{2}l\log N+d(d-1)2^{l}N\log(8C_{\ref{5}})}{2^{l+1}dNt}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{\{d(d-1)(2^{l+1}-2^{l}\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{2})+(C_{\ref{10}}2^{dl}\delta+C_{\ref{11}})\}Nt}{2^{l+1}dNt}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-1)\log(8C_{\ref{5}})}{2t}+\frac{(d-1)(2^{l+1}-2^{l}\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{2})}{2^{l+1}}+\frac{C_{\ref{10}}2^{dl}\delta+C_{\ref{11}}}{2^{l+1}d}.

Using (
4.4
) and taking
\delta\to 0
,
l\to\infty
we have

{\dim_{H}Z_{x}\leq\frac{(d-1)\log(8C_{\ref{5}})}{2t}+\frac{(d-1)(2-\lambda^{2})}{2}.}

Since this estimate holds for any
0<\lambda<1
and
t=t_{\lambda}\to\infty
as
\lambda\to 1
, we finally obtain

{\dim_{H}Z_{x}\leq\frac{d-1}{2}.}

∎

4.2.
Lower bound

In this subsection we prove Theorem
1.4
, which directly implies that for any
d\geq 3

{\dim_{H}\left\{x\in U\Gamma:x\textrm{ is }A^{+}\textrm{-divergent on average}\right\}\geq 1.}

Recall that
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
g_{t}
-divergent (or
g_{t}
-divergent on average) if and only if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is singular (or singular on average). We can extend this notion to define analogous concepts of singular (or singular on average) vectors for higher-rank actions, and use them to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

(1)

We say that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
multiplicatively singular
if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
T(\epsilon)>0
such that for any
n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1}\in\mathbb{N}
with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d-1}>T(\epsilon)
there exists
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.9)

{\|q\xi_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\epsilon e^{-n_{i}}\textrm{ for all }1\leq i\leq d-1\textrm{ and }0<q<\epsilon e^{\sum n_{i}}.}

(2)

We say that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
multiplicatively singular on average
if

{\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-1}}\left|\left\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}^{d-1}:\textrm{ there exists }q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\textrm{ s.t. }\eqref{simulsing}\textrm{ holds}\right\}\right|=1}

for any
\epsilon>0
.

The following lemma is a version of Dani’s correspondence between divergent on average trajectories for higher rank actions and multiplicatively singular on average vectors.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
A^{+}
-divergent on average if and only if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is multiplicatively singular on average.

Proof.

Suppose that
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
A^{+}
-divergent on average. For
\epsilon>0
we denote

\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}:=\left\{g\Gamma\in X:\min_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\|gv\|\geq\epsilon\right\}.

Then for any
\epsilon>0
the set
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
is compact by the classical Mahler’s criterion, thus we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-1}}\#\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}^{d-1}:a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma\notin\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}\right\}=1.

For
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})
the condition
a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma\notin\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
implies that there exists a vector
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d-1},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
such that

v_{(m_{1},\cdots,m_{d-1},q)}=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{n_{1}}(m_{1}+q\xi_{1})\\
\vdots\\
e^{n_{d-1}}(m_{d-1}+q\xi_{d-1})\\
e^{-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d-1})}q\end{matrix}\right)

satisfies
\|v_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d-1},q)}\|<\epsilon
, hence also implies the condition (
4.9
) for
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})
. Thus
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is multiplicatively singular on average. The proof of the converse direction is similar.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

If
\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(1,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d-1})\leq 2
, then there exists
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
such that for any
1\leq i\leq d-1

there exist
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
satisfying
\xi_{i}=a_{i}\xi+b_{i}
. Let
D
be the l.c.m of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d-1},b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-1}
and write
a_{i}=\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{D}
and
b_{i}=\frac{b_{i}^{\prime}}{D}
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
.

We claim that for any
\epsilon>0
if
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})
satisfies

(4.10)

{\max_{1\leq i\leq d-1}n_{i}\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq d-1}n_{i}-\log\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq d-1}Da_{i}^{\prime}\right),}

then
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})
also satisfies (
4.9
). To see this, let
T=\sum_{i}n_{i}
and
Q=\lfloor\frac{\epsilon e^{T}}{D}\rfloor
. By Dirichlet’s theorem, there exists
1\leq q\leq Q
such that
\|q\xi\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\frac{1}{Q}
. It follows that for any
1\leq i\leq d-1
we have
\|(Dq)\xi_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{Q}
. Assuming (
4.10
) for
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})
, we have

{\|(Dq)\xi_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{Q}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq d-1}Da_{i}^{\prime}}{\epsilon e^{T+1}}\leq\epsilon e^{-n_{i}}}

for any
i
. Then (
4.9
) holds for
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})
since
Dq\leq DQ\leq\epsilon e^{T}
. Hence the claim is proved.

For any
\epsilon>0
, the density of the set of
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
satisfying (
4.10
) is
1
. By Lemma
4.4
and the above claim,
u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma
is
A^{+}
-divergent on average.
∎

5.
Dynamical height functions for a reference trajectory

5.1.
Injectivity radius

For
x\in X
we denote by
\operatorname{inj}(x)
the maximal injectivity of radius of
x\in X
, which is the supremum of
r>0
such that the map
g\mapsto gx
is injective in the ball
B^{G}(\operatorname{id},r)
. According to
[
KM12
, Proposition 3.5]
there exists a constant
C_{\ref{12}}>1
such that the following holds: for any
x\in X
there exists a nonzero
x
-integral vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\|v\|<C_{\ref{12}}\operatorname{inj}(x)^{\frac{1}{d}}
.

Let
0<\lambda<1
be given, let
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}:=\widetilde{\alpha}_{i,\lambda,t_{\lambda}}
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
be as in (
2.10
).

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
1\leq i\leq d-1
and
x\in X^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{inj}(x)\leq C_{\ref{12}}^{-d}\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d(d-1)}
,

\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(x)>C_{\ref{12}}^{-1}\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}\operatorname{inj}(x)^{-\frac{\lambda}{d}}.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{inj}(x)\leq C_{\ref{12}}^{-d}\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d(d-1)}
, there exists an
x
-integral vector
v
such that
\|v\|<C_{\ref{12}}\operatorname{inj}(x)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}
, hence
\|\pi_{i,1,0}(v)\|\leq\|v\|<\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}
. It follows that

\varphi_{i,1,\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}}(v)=\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}\|\pi_{i,1,1}(v)\|^{-\lambda}\geq\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}\|v\|^{-\lambda}>C_{\ref{12}}^{-1}\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}\operatorname{inj}(x)^{-\frac{\lambda}{d}}.

By definition of
\alpha_{i,\lambda,t_{0}}=\alpha_{i,\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}}
we have

\alpha_{i,\lambda,t_{0}}(x)\geq\varphi_{i,1,\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}}(v)>C_{\ref{12}}^{-1}\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}\operatorname{inj}(x)^{-\frac{\lambda}{d}}.

On the other hand, the
Claim
in the proof of Proposition
2.3
implies

\alpha_{i,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)\geq\|v\|^{-\lambda}>C_{\ref{12}}^{-1}\operatorname{inj}(x)^{-\frac{\lambda}{d}}.

Since
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(x)\geq\min\left\{\alpha_{i,\lambda,t_{0}}(x),\alpha_{i,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)\right\}
, we obtain
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(x)>C_{\ref{12}}^{-1}\epsilon_{\lambda,t_{\lambda}}^{d-1}\operatorname{inj}(x)^{-\frac{\lambda}{d}}
.
∎

5.2.
Dynamical height function for a reference trajectory under one-dimensional action

This subsection aims to construct dynamical height functions for a given reference trajectory under one-dimensional diagonal actions and to establish the contraction hypotheses for these height functions.

Given
0<\lambda<1
let us choose and fix

such that
1-2^{-l_{\lambda}+1}>\lambda

Denote by

\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}g^{\prime}&0\\

of

(5.1)

{\mathsf{B}_{t,\eta}:=B^{U}(\operatorname{id},\eta e^{-2t})B^{Q}(\operatorname{id},\eta e^{-\frac{t}{2}}),\quad\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}_{t,\eta}:=B^{U}(\operatorname{id},\eta e^{-\frac{t}{2}})B^{Q}(\operatorname{id},\eta e^{-\frac{t}{2}}).}

Observe that the boxes
\mathsf{B}_{t,\eta}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}_{t,\eta}
are open sets in
G
and satisfy the following property: for any
1\leq i\leq d-1
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G

\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}:u_{i}(s)g_{1}\in\mathsf{B}_{t,\eta}g_{2}\right\}

\eta e^{-2t}
, and similarly, the set
\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}:u_{i}(s)g_{1}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}_{t,\eta}g_{2}\right\}
is either the empty set or an interval of length
\eta e^{-\frac{t}{2}}
. Note that it also implies the analogous property for
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
with
\operatorname{inj}(x_{1}),\operatorname{inj}(x_{2})>\eta e^{-\frac{t}{2}}
in the place of
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
.

For
1\leq i\leq d-1
,
t\in t_{\lambda}\mathbb{N}
, and

let us consider the following three sets:

J_{i,t,\operatorname{low}}(y):=\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{jt\mathbf{e}_{i}}y)\leq e^{\frac{t}{3d}}\right\},

J_{i,t,\operatorname{mid}}(y):=\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}:e^{\frac{t}{3d}}<\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{jt\mathbf{e}_{i}}y)\leq\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(t)\right\},

J_{i,t,\operatorname{high}}(y):=\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{jt\mathbf{e}_{i}}y)>\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(t)\right\}.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\eta\leq 1
, and
0\leq l\leq l_{\lambda}
we denote by
\Upsilon_{N,i,t,\eta,l}(y)\subseteq X
the set of

such that

\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{jt\mathbf{e}_{i}}x)>D^{(l)}\big(E_{\ref{101},\lambda}(3C_{\ref{5}})^{t}\big)\textrm{ for all }j\in J_{i,t,\operatorname{high}}(y)\cap\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}.

We now define a proper lower semi-continuous function
\beta_{N,i,t,l}^{\prime}(\;\cdot\;;y):X^{\prime}\to[1,\infty)
by

{\beta_{N,i,t,l}^{\prime}(x;y)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}x)&\text{if $x\in\Upsilon_{N,i,t,1,l}(y)$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}}

We note that
0\leq\beta_{N,i,t,l}^{\prime}\leq\beta_{N,i,t,l^{\prime}}^{\prime}
for any
0\leq l^{\prime}\leq l\leq l_{\lambda}
, as the sets
\Upsilon_{N,i,t,1,l}(y)
are decreasing in
l
.

We state the contraction hypothesis for
\beta^{\prime}_{N,i,t,l}
.

Proposition 5.2

(Contraction for
\beta^{\prime}
)
.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq i\leq d-1
,
t\in t_{\lambda}\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq l\leq l_{\lambda}
,
y\in X
and

(5.2)

{\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{N,i,t,l}^{\prime}(u_{i}(s)x;y)ds\leq 2C_{\ref{5}}E_{\ref{101},\lambda}e^{t}(3C_{5}E_{\ref{101},\lambda})^{|\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}\cap J_{i,t,\operatorname{mid}}(y)|t}e^{-\lambda^{2}Nt}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(x).}

Proof.

We may assume
l=0
, since
\beta_{N,i,t,l}^{\prime}\leq\beta_{N,i,t,0}^{\prime}
holds for any
0\leq l\leq l_{\lambda}
. We denote
\varpi(N)=0
if
N\in J_{i,t,\operatorname{mid}}(y)\cup J_{i,t,\operatorname{high}}(y)
and
\varpi(N)=1
if
N\in J_{i,t,\operatorname{low}}(y)
. The desired inequality (
5.2
) immediately follows the following claim:

Claim.
The support of the map
s\mapsto\beta_{N,i,t,l}^{\prime}(u_{i}(s)x;y)
is covered by
M_{N}
intervals
I_{N,1},\ldots,I_{N,M_{N}}
, where

with
\frac{1}{2}e^{-2(N+\varpi(N))t}
-separated

for

, such that

\sum_{k=1}^{M_{N}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s_{N,k})x)e^{-2(N+\varpi(N))t}\leq 2E_{\ref{101},\lambda}e^{t}(3C_{5}E_{\ref{101},\lambda})^{|\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}\cap J_{i,t,\operatorname{mid}}(y)|t}e^{-\lambda^{2}Nt}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(x).

Claim

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{N,i,t,l}^{\prime}(u_{i}(s)x;y)ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M_{N}}\int_{I_{N,k}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds

Claim
holds for
N
, and prove
Claim
for
N+1
. The support of the map
s\mapsto\beta_{N+1,i,t}^{\prime}(u_{i}(s)x;y)
is contained in
\bigcup_{k=1}^{M_{N}}I_{N,k}
. For each
1\leq k\leq M_{N}
, by (
2.11
) we have

(5.3)

{\int_{I_{N,k}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds\leq E_{\ref{101},\lambda}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s_{N,k})x).}

Moreover, if
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s_{N,k})x)\geq E_{\ref{101},\lambda}e^{t}
, then by (
2.12
)

(5.4)

{\int_{I_{N,k}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds\leq 2e^{-\lambda^{2}t}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s_{N,k})x).}

Case 1.

N+1\in J_{i,t,\operatorname{low}}(y)
.

In this case
N
is either in
J_{i,t,\operatorname{low}}(y)
or
J_{i,t,\operatorname{mid}}(y)
. Indeed,

{\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{Nt\mathbf{e}_{i}}y)\leq C_{\ref{5}}^{t}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}y)\leq C_{\ref{5}}^{t}e^{\frac{t}{3d}}\leq E_{\ref{101},\lambda}(3C_{\ref{5}})^{t},}

hence

. Let us denote by

\mathcal{T}
is a union of disjoint intervals of length

, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
is a union of disjoint intervals of length

. Moreover, each subinterval of
\mathcal{T}
is contained in exactly one subinterval of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
. It follows from (
2.13
) that for any
1\leq k\leq M_{N}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{N,k}\cap\mathcal{T}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{\ref{5}}e^{-\frac{3t}{2}}\int_{I_{N,k}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{\ref{5}}e^{-\frac{3t}{2}}\int_{I_{N,k}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds.

Subcase 1-1.

N\in J_{i,t,\operatorname{low}}(y)
.

In this subcase

is an interval of length

(5.6)

{\int_{I_{N,k}\cap\mathcal{T}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds\leq C_{\ref{5}}^{3}e^{-(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{3d})t}|I_{N,k}|}

for any

. We may assume that

hence

\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)\leq C_{\ref{5}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}y)\leq C_{\ref{5}}e^{\frac{t}{3d}}.

{\int_{I_{N,k}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s)x)ds\leq C_{\ref{5}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s_{N,k}^{\prime})x)|I_{N,k}|\leq C_{\ref{5}}^{2}e^{\frac{t}{3d}}|I_{N,k}|.}

Thus, combining with (

) we get (
5.6
).

Note that each connected component of

is an interval of length at most

. Let us define

so that each

for

is of length

and contains exactly one connected component of
\bigcup_{k=1}^{M_{k}}I_{N,k}\cap\mathcal{T}
. Then using (

) and the inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{M_{N+1}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{(N+1)t\mathbf{e}_{i}}u_{i}(s_{N+1,l})x)e^{-2(N+2)t}

The set of exceptions to the inhomogeneous Littlewood conjecture

6.1.
The space of grids and Dani correspondence

In this subsection, we introduce the space of grids and give a dynamical reformulation of the inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation problem.

Let us denote by
\widehat{G}=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
the group of volume-preserving affine transformations and denote by
\widehat{\Gamma}=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\operatorname{Stab}_{\widehat{G}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the stabilizer of the standard lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We view
\widehat{G}
as a subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
by
\widehat{G}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}g&v\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right):g\in G,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}
. We also view
G
as a subgroup of
\widehat{G}
and take a lift of the element
g\in G
to
\widehat{G}\subset\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
by
g\mapsto\left(\begin{matrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)
, denoted again by
g
.

Let
\widehat{X}=\widehat{G}/\widehat{\Gamma}
and denote by
\pi:\widehat{X}\to X
the canonical projection from
\widehat{X}
to
X
. One can view the homogeneous space
\widehat{X}
as the space of unimodular
grids
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e. unimodular lattices translated by a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. More explicitly, for
\widehat{x}=\left(\begin{matrix}g&v\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\widehat{\Gamma}\in\widehat{X}
with
g\in G
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we identify
\widehat{x}
to the corresponding unimodular grid
\Lambda_{\widehat{x}}:=g\mathbb{Z}^{d}+v
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For
\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
we denote

x_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=\left(\begin{matrix}\operatorname{Id}_{d-1}&\boldsymbol{\xi}\\
&1\end{matrix}\right)\Gamma\in X,\qquad\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=\left(\begin{matrix}\operatorname{Id}_{d-1}&\boldsymbol{\xi}&-\boldsymbol{\theta}\\
&1&0\\
&&1\end{matrix}\right)\widehat{\Gamma}\in\widehat{X}.

Note that
\Lambda_{\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}}=u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\mathbb{Z}^{d}-(\boldsymbol{\theta},0)
. As in the classical Dani correspondence for homogeneous Diophantine approximation, inhomogeneous Diophantine properties of
(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta})
are characterized by dynamical properties of the orbits of
\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}
in the space
\widehat{X}
.

We say that a pair
(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
is
rational
if there exist some
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
q\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\theta}+\mathbf{p}=0
, and
irrational
otherwise. Let us define

A^{+}_{\geq T}:=\left\{a_{(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d-1})}\in A:\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d-1}\geq T\right\}
for
T>0
, and

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}:=\left\{\widehat{x}\in\widehat{X}:\Lambda_{\widehat{x}}\cap B^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,\epsilon)=\emptyset\right\}
for
\epsilon>0
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
satisfies Littlewood’s conjecture, i.e. (
1.3
) holds. Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. If
\displaystyle\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}>0
, then there exists
T>0
such that
A^{+}_{\geq T}\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

We first consider the case of
(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta})
is rational. Then there exist
\mathbf{p}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
and
q_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
q_{0}\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\theta}+\mathbf{p}=0
. It follows that

\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}=\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|(q-q_{0})\xi_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}=\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}=0,

as we are assuming that
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
satisfies Littlewood’s conjecture.

We now suppose that
(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta})
is irrational, and for any
\epsilon>0
and
T>0
there exists
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in A_{\geq T}^{+}
such that
a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}\notin\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
. By a matrix calculation, it implies that there exists a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|v\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
of the form

v=\big(e^{\tau_{1}}(q\xi_{1}-\theta_{1}-p_{1}),\ldots,e^{\tau_{d-1}}(q\xi_{d-1}-\theta_{d-1}-p_{d-1}),e^{-(\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{d-1})}q)\big),

where
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that

\|q\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}-\theta_{i}\|\leq\epsilon e^{-\tau_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
and
|q|\leq\epsilon e^{\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{d-1}}
, hence
q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\epsilon^{d}
. Since
(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta})
is irrational, the
q
satisfying
\|q\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\epsilon e^{-T}
is unbounded as
T\to\infty
. Thus we get
\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\epsilon^{d}
, and conclude that
\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\|q\xi_{i}-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}=0
as
\epsilon
is arbitrary.

∎

By Theorem

[

, §5]
classified which homogeneous measures may occur in the positive base entropy case. Either

m_{j}^{\prime}:=\lfloor(1-\sigma)m_{j}\rfloor
.

Lemma 6.7
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}
,
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, and
1\leq i\leq d-1
we have

\kappa_{i}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma)\leq e^{(d-1)\sigma m_{j}t},\qquad\alpha_{i}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma)\leq E_{\ref{101},\lambda}e^{2(d-1)\sigma m_{j}t}.

Proof.

By definition of
\kappa_{i}
we have
\kappa_{i}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma)\leq e^{\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{d-1}}\leq e^{(d-1)\sigma m_{j}t}
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
. It follows that

\alpha_{i,\lambda}^{\prime}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma)=\kappa_{i}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma)\operatorname{ht}_{\lambda}\big(\bar{a}_{\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{d-1}}\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma\big)\leq e^{2(\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{d-1})}\leq e^{2(d-1)\sigma m_{j}t}

for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
. The definition (
2.10
) of
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i,\lambda}
implies that

\widetilde{\alpha}_{i,\lambda}(x)\leq\max\left\{\kappa_{i}(x)E_{\ref{101},\lambda},\alpha_{i,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)\right\}
for any

. Hence we obtain

\widetilde{\alpha}_{i,\lambda}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma)\leq E_{\ref{101},\lambda}e^{2(d-1)\sigma m_{j}t}
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}
, and
y\in X
we have

\big((a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}\big)\big([y]_{\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime})}}\big)\leq E_{\ref{109},\lambda}e^{-(\lambda^{5}-\lambda^{6})(d-1)m_{j}t}

for some constant
E_{\ref{109},\lambda}>0
.

Proof.

Since
(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}
is the normalized counting measure on the set
\left\{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma:\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}\right\}
, we have

\big((a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}\big)\big([y]_{\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime})}}\big)=\frac{1}{\#S_{j}}\#\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}:a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma\in[y]_{\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime})}}\right\}.

Let us define
F:[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
by
F(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\widetilde{\psi}_{m_{j}^{\prime},2^{-l_{\lambda}},l_{\lambda}}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma;y)
. Observe that

(6.4)

{\#\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}:a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma\in[y]_{\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime})}}\right\}\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}}F(\boldsymbol{\xi})}

by Lemma
6.5
. By a similar argument to (2) of Lemma
5.6
we obtain

(6.5)

{\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}}F(\boldsymbol{\xi})\leq E_{\ref{102},\lambda}^{d-1}2^{2(d-1)l_{\lambda}}\kappa(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\Gamma)^{2}e^{2(d-1)m_{j}^{\prime}t}\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{m_{j}^{\prime},2^{-l_{\lambda}+1},l_{\lambda}-1}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma;y)d\boldsymbol{\xi}.}

Let
\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{M}\right\}
be a maximal
\frac{1}{2}e^{-2(1-\sigma)m_{j}^{\prime}t}
-separated set so that
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}
is covered by
B(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k},\frac{1}{2}e^{-2(1-\sigma)m_{j}^{\prime}t})
, and
M\ll e^{2(d-1)\sigma m_{j}^{\prime}t}
. Then we have

(6.6)

\displaystyle\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{m_{j}^{\prime},2^{-l_{\lambda}+1},l_{\lambda}-1}

\displaystyle(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma;y)d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{m_{j}^{\prime},2^{-l_{\lambda}+1},l_{\lambda}-1}(u(\boldsymbol{\xi})a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k})\Gamma;y)d\boldsymbol{\xi}.

For each

we apply Proposition
5.5
with

,

,

, and

. Then we have

(6.7)

\displaystyle\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d-1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{m_{j}^{\prime},2^{-l_{\lambda}+1},l_{\lambda}-1}

\displaystyle(u(\boldsymbol{\xi})a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k})\Gamma;y)d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq E_{\ref{107},\lambda}\kappa(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k})\Gamma)^{8l_{\lambda}}e^{-\lambda^{4}(d-1)m_{j}^{\prime}t}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k})\Gamma)

for some constant
E_{\ref{107},\lambda}>0
. Here we are using the fact from our choice of

that

(2^{l_{\lambda}-1}-1)N=(1-2^{-\lambda+1})m_{j}^{\prime}\geq\lambda m_{j}^{\prime}.

Combining (
6.4
), (
6.5
), (
6.6
), (
6.7
), and the estimates from Lemma

altogether we obtain

\displaystyle\#\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}:a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Gamma\in[y]_{\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime})}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq E_{\ref{108},\lambda}e^{(2-\lambda^{4}+20dl_{\lambda}\sigma)(d-1)m_{j}^{\prime}t}

\displaystyle\leq E_{\ref{108},\lambda}e^{(2-\lambda^{4}+20dl_{\lambda}\sigma)(1-\sigma)(d-1)m_{j}t}

for some constant

. Recall that

∎

Proof of Proposition
6.6
.

(1) Since
(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\mu_{j}-\mu_{j}
goes to zero measure as
j\to\infty
,
\mu
is
a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
-invariant for any
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in(t\mathbb{Z})^{d-1}
.

(2) For any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}
we have

\frac{1}{m_{j}^{d-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\{1,\ldots,m_{j}\}^{d-1}}\delta_{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}}(K)\geq\delta

since
\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\in\mathcal{Z}_{K,\delta,m_{j}}
. It follows that

\mu_{j}(K)=\frac{1}{\#S_{j}}\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}}\frac{1}{m_{j}^{d-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\{1,\ldots,m_{j}\}^{d-1}}\delta_{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}}(K)\geq\delta

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
\mu(\widehat{X})\geq\delta
.

(3) We show that
\mu(\widehat{X}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})=0
. Recall that
a\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\in\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}
and
a\in A^{+}_{\geq T}
. It follows that

\frac{1}{m_{j}^{d-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\{1,\ldots,m_{j}\}^{d-1}}\delta_{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}}(\widehat{X}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})\leq\frac{dTm_{j}}{m_{j}^{2}}=\frac{dT}{m_{j}}

for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}
. It implies that

\mu_{j}(\widehat{X}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})=\frac{1}{\#S_{j}}\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S_{j}}\frac{1}{m_{j}^{d-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\{1,\ldots,m_{j}\}^{d-1}}\delta_{a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\widehat{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}}(\widehat{X}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})\leq\frac{dT}{m_{j}}

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, hence
\mu(\widehat{X}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})=0
.

(4) For
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}
and
1\leq i\leq d-1
we define

\mu_{j,\boldsymbol{\tau},i}:=\frac{1}{\lfloor(1-\sigma)m_{j}\rfloor}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(1-\sigma)m_{j}\rfloor}(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}+kt\mathbf{e}_{i}})_{*}\nu_{j}.

Then for any

(6.8)

{\sigma^{d-1}\mu_{j}\leq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}|}\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}}\mu_{j,\boldsymbol{\tau},i}.}

By Proposition
6.8
we have

(6.9)

{\big((a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}\big)\big([y]_{\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime})}}\big)\leq E_{\ref{109},\lambda}e^{-(\lambda^{5}-\lambda^{6})(d-1)m_{j}t}.}

We refer the reader to
[
ELW
, Chapter 1 & 2]
for the definitions and basic properties of the entropy. For all sufficiently large
j\in\mathbb{N}
, (
6.9
) impies that

(6.10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}H_{(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime},i)}\big)

\displaystyle\geq H_{(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}}\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{d-1}\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime},i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda^{5}-\lambda^{6})(d-1)m_{j}t-\log E_{\ref{109},\lambda}\geq(\lambda^{5}-\lambda^{6})m_{j}t.

For

and large

by

m_{j}^{\prime}-1=qm^{\prime}+r,\textrm{ with }r\in\left\{0,\ldots,q-1\right\}.

By subadditivity of the entropy with respect to the partition,

{H_{(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime},i)}\big)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m^{\prime}}H_{(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}+(kq+r)t\mathbf{e}_{i}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(q,i)}\big)+2q\log|\mathcal{Q}|}

for each

. Summing those inequalities for

, and using the concavity of entropy with respect to measure, we get

\displaystyle qH_{(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime},i)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m_{j}^{\prime}-1}H_{(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}+kt\mathbf{e}_{i}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(q,i)}\big)+2q^{2}\log|\mathcal{Q}|

\displaystyle\leq m_{j}^{\prime}H_{\pi_{*}\mu_{j,\boldsymbol{\tau},i}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(q,i)}\big)+2q^{2}\log|\mathcal{Q}|.

It follows from (

) that for any

and
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}H_{\pi_{*}\mu_{j,\boldsymbol{\tau},i}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(q,i)}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{m_{j}^{\prime}}H_{(a_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_{*}\overline{\nu_{j}}}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{(m_{j}^{\prime},i)}\big)-\frac{2q\log|\mathcal{P}|}{m_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{q}H_{\overline{\mu_{j}}}(\mathcal{Q}^{(q,i)})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma^{d-1}}{|\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}|}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}^{*}}\frac{1}{q}H_{\pi_{*}\mu_{j,\boldsymbol{\tau},i}}(\mathcal{Q}^{(q,i)})+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq\sigma^{d-1}\left((\lambda^{5}-\lambda^{6})t-\frac{2q\log|\mathcal{P}|}{m_{j}^{\prime}}\right).

In particular, by taking

∎

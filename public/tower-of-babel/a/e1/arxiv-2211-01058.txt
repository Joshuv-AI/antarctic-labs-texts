A Note on the Ramanujan Machine
Éric Brier, David Naccache, Ofer Yifrach-Stav

Abstract
The Ramanujan Machine project detects new expressions related to constants of interest, such as ζ \zeta function values, γ \gamma and algebraic numbers (to name a few). In particular the project lists a number of conjectures involving even and odd ζ \zeta function values, logarithms etc. We show that many relations detected by the Ramanujan Machine Project stem from a specific algebraic observation and show how to generate infinitely many. This provides an automated proof and/or an explanation of many of the relations listed as conjectures by the project (although not all of them).

Theoretical Preamble

Consider continued fractions defined by the formulae

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} p_{n}&=b_{n}p_{n-1}+a_{n}p_{n-2}\\
q_{n}&=b_{n}q_{n-1}+a_{n}q_{n-2}\\
\end{aligned}\right.\mbox{~~with the initial conditions:}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} p_{-1}&=1\\
p_{0}&=b_{0}\\
\end{aligned}\right.\mbox{~~and~~}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} q_{-1}&=0\\
q_{0}&=1\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Let
f
and
g
be two functions from which we build
\forall n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{n}&=-f(n)^{2}\\
b_{n}&=\frac{f(n+1)g(n+1)+f(n)g(n-1)}{g(n)}\\
\end{aligned}\right.\mbox{~~with the initial condition:~~}b_{0}=f(1)g(1)

We further require
f
and
g
to be nonzero for positive integers.

Define the sequence
\mathbf{F}_{n}
by:

F_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}f(i)\mbox{~~with the initial conditions:~~}\mathbf{F}_{-1}=1\mbox{~~and~~}\mathbf{F}_{0}=f(1)

We will now prove by induction that

\forall n\geq-1,p_{n}=\mathbf{F}_{n}g(n+1)

The above initial conditions ensure this equality for
n=-1
and
n=0
. Assume that the hypothesis holds true for all values below
n
and let us compute:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} p_{n}&=b_{n}p_{n-1}+a_{n}p_{n-2}\\
&=b_{n}\mathbf{F}_{n-1}g(n)-f(n)^{2}\mathbf{F}_{n-2}g(n-1)\\
&=\mathbf{F}_{n-1}(f(n+1)g(n+1)+f(n)g(n-1))-f(n)\mathbf{F}_{n-1}g(n-1)\\
&=\mathbf{F}_{n-1}f(n+1)g(n+1)\\
&=\mathbf{F}_{n}g(n+1)\\
\end{aligned}

as desired, thereby proving a closed form for the convergents
p_{n}
.

We will now handle
q_{n}
by first remarking that:

\frac{q_{n}}{p_{n}}-\frac{q_{n-1}}{p_{n-1}}=\frac{q_{n}p_{n-1}-q_{n-1}p_{n}}{p_{n}p_{n-1}}

The recurrence conditions for
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
ensure that:

q_{n}p_{n-1}-q_{n-1}p_{n}=-a_{n}(q_{n-1}p_{n-2}-q_{n-2}p_{n-1})

Induction yields:

q_{n}p_{n-1}-q_{n-1}p_{n}=(\prod_{i=1}^{n}-a_{n})(q_{-1}p_{-2}-q_{-2}p_{-1})

The last term is equal to 1 given the initial conditions. The
n
-term product is, by the definition of the sequence
a_{n}
, equal to
(\mathbf{F}_{n-1})^{2}
.

We hence get:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{q_{n}}{p_{n}}-\frac{q_{n-1}}{p_{n-1}}&=\frac{(\mathbf{F}_{n-1})^{2}}{p_{n}p_{n-1}}\\
&=\frac{(\mathbf{F}_{n-1})^{2}}{\mathbf{F}_{n}g(n+1)\mathbf{F}_{n-1}g(n)}\\
&=\frac{1}{f(n+1)g(n)g(n+1)}\\
\end{aligned}

Once again, by induction (taking into account the initial values), we get:

\frac{q_{n}}{p_{n}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{f(i+1)g(i)g(i+1)}

The limit
L
of the continued fraction is thus given by the equation:

\dfrac{1}{L}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{f(n+1)g(n)g(n+1)}

It suffices now to resort to standard partial fraction decomposition to get relations such as those given by the Ramanujan Machine Project. This is done automatically by symbolic computation software such as Mathematica to avoid tedious yet standard formula manipulation by hand.

The last step connecting the observation and the Ramanujan Machine Project is somewhat technical we hence break it down into successive steps:

Step 1:

The Ramanujan Machine Project considers that the
a_{n},b_{n}
defining the continued fraction are polynomials. Because nothing in our analysis imposes that the

are polynomials we can write:

\dfrac{1}{L}=\frac{f(1)g(1)+f(0)g(-1)}{g(0)}-{\cfrac{f(1)^{2}}{\frac{f(2)g(2)+f(1)g(0)}{g(1)}-{\cfrac{f(2)^{2}}{\frac{f(3)g(3)+f(2)g(1)}{g(2)}-{\cfrac{f(3)^{2}}{\frac{f(4)g(4)+f(3)g(2)}{g(3)}-{\cfrac{f(4)^{2}}{\frac{f(5)g(5)+f(4)g(3)}{g(4)}-\cdots}}}}}}}}

\dfrac{1}{L}=\frac{f(1)g(1)+f(0)g(-1)}{g(0)}-{\cfrac{g(1)f(1)^{2}}{\frac{f(2)g(2)+f(1)g(0)}{1}-{\cfrac{g(2)g(1)f(2)^{2}}{\frac{f(3)g(3)+f(2)g(1)}{1}-{\cfrac{g(3)g(2)f(3)^{2}}{\frac{f(4)g(4)+f(3)g(2)}{1}-{\cfrac{g(4)g(3)f(4)^{2}}{\frac{f(5)g(5)+f(4)g(3)}{1}-\cdots}}}}}}}}

\dfrac{g(0)}{L}=\frac{f(1)g(1)+f(0)g(-1)}{1}-{\cfrac{g(1)g(0)f(1)^{2}}{\frac{f(2)g(2)+f(1)g(0)}{1}-{\cfrac{g(2)g(1)f(2)^{2}}{\frac{f(3)g(3)+f(2)g(1)}{1}-{\cfrac{g(3)g(2)f(3)^{2}}{\frac{f(4)g(4)+f(3)g(2)}{1}-{\cfrac{g(4)g(3)f(4)^{2}}{\frac{f(5)g(5)+f(4)g(3)}{1}-\cdots}}}}}}}}

\dfrac{g(0)}{L}={f(1)g(1)+f(0)g(-1)}-{\cfrac{g(1)g(0)f(1)^{2}}{{f(2)g(2)+f(1)g(0)}-{\cfrac{g(2)g(1)f(2)^{2}}{{f(3)g(3)+f(2)g(1)}-{\cfrac{g(3)g(2)f(3)^{2}}{{f(4)g(4)+f(3)g(2)}-\cdots}}}}}}

Step 2:

In the Ramanujan Project many formulae have an
a_{n}
(in our notations, i.e.
b_{n}
in theirs) of the form
Q=(n+\alpha)^{2}(n+\beta)(n+\gamma)
, i.e. with a total degree of 4 and a term having a power of two. In many other cases the
a_{n}
can be written as
\phi_{1}(n)^{2}\phi_{2}(n)\phi_{3}(n)
for some functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3}
. The form (
f(n)^{2}g(n-1)g(n)
) at the numerator of the continued fraction obtained in Step 1 explains why:

Step 3:

Operate the change of variable
n_{0}=n+\alpha
to get:

Q={n_{0}}^{2}(n_{0}-\alpha+\beta)(n_{0}-\alpha+\gamma)

Step 4:

Operate the change of variables
\beta_{1}=-\alpha+\beta
,
\gamma_{1}=-\alpha+\gamma
to get:

Q={n_{0}}^{2}(n_{0}+\beta_{1})(n_{0}+\gamma_{1})

Step 5:

Operate the change of variable
n_{0}=n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})
to get:

Q={(n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1}))}^{2}(n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})+\beta_{1})(n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})+\gamma_{1})

Step 6:

Kick the parasite factor
(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})^{2}
out of the continued fraction and integrate it in the limit. We get:

Q^{\prime}=\frac{Q}{(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})^{2}}={n_{1}}^{2}(n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})+\beta_{1})(n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})+\gamma_{1})

Step 5:

Declare
f(n_{1})=\mbox{ID}(n_{1})=n_{1}
and
g(n_{1})=n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})+\beta_{1}
to get:

Q^{\prime}=f(n_{1})^{2}g(n_{1})(n_{1}(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})+\gamma_{1})

Step 7:

Observe that:

g(n_{1}-1)=(n_{1}-1)(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})+\gamma_{1}=(\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1})n_{1}+\beta_{1}

and hence:

Q^{\prime}=f(n_{1})^{2}g(n_{1})g(n_{1}-1)

Step 8:

Replace in the simplified continued fraction the terms of the form:

f(n)g(n)+f(n-1)g(n-2)

by their variable-changed expression in
n_{1}
.

Step 9:

Light an altar candle hoping that the variable changes did not alter the initial conditions. If so a new relation of the form
a_{n}=(n+\alpha)^{2}(n+\beta)(n+\gamma)
and
b_{n}=\mbox{polynomial}
was found.

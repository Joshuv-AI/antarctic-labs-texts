Abstract
. This paper presents categorical formulations of Turing, Medvedev, Muchnik, and Weihrauch reducibilities in Computability Theory, utilizing Lawvere doctrines. While the first notions lend themselves to a smooth categorical presentation, essentially dualizing the traditional idea of realizability doctrines, Weihrauch reducibility and its extensions to represented and multi-represented spaces require a separate investigation. Our abstract analysis of these concepts highlights a shared characteristic among all these reducibilities. Specifically, we demonstrate that all these doctrines stemming from computability concepts can be proven to be instances of completions of quantifiers for doctrines, analogous to what occurs for doctrines for realizability. As a corollary of these results, we will be able to formally compare Weihrauch reducibility with the dialectica doctrine constructed from a doctrine representing Turing degrees.

Introduction

Categorical methods and language have been employed in many areas of Mathematics. In Mathematical Logic, they are widely used in Model Theory and Proof Theory. In Recursion or Computability Theory, there is a long tradition of categorical methods in Realizability studies, expounded in van Oosten’s book
[
vO08
]
.
One of the key tools employed in such a setting is the notion of
tripos
, introduced by Hyland, Johnstone, and Pitts in
[
HJP80
]
, which is a specific instance of the notion of
hyperdoctrine
introduced by Lawvere
[
Law06
,
Law69
,
Law70
]
to synthesize the structural properties of logical systems.

The use of categorical methods in Realizability includes, for example, Hofstra’s work
[
Hof06
]
, where he proved that most well-known realizability-like triposes, (e.g. the “effective” tripos
[
HJP80
]
, the “modified realizability” tripos
[
vO97
]
and the “dialectica” tripos
[
Bie08
]
) are instances of a more general notion of tripos associated to a given ordered partial combinatory algebra (PCA) equipped with a filter. It is worth recalling that the notion of PCA presents a generalization of both Kleene’s first and second models.
His point was that all these triposes can be presented as “triposes for a given PCA”, hence all these notions differ only in the choice of the associated PCA, and we could say that all realizability is relative to a choice of a PCA equipped with a filter.

Over the years, several authors observed that realizability triposes are instances of a free construction adding “generalized existential quantifiers” to a given doctrine. For instance, we refer to Hofstra’s work
[
Hof06
]
, Trotta and Maietti’s work
[
MT23
]
, and Frey’s works
[
Fre14
,
Fre23
]
. So every realizability tripos is obtained by freely adding left adjoints along the class of all morphisms of the base category. Hence, combining this result with the previous analysis by Hofstra, we have that realizability-like triposes are instances of the generalized existential completion. These results show the abstract, structural property that lies behind all the various forms of realizability.

Despite the long tradition of studying realizability and its variants from a categorical perspective, a systematic and in-depth analysis of computability-like reducibilities, such as Turing
[
Odi89
,
Soa87
]
, Medvedev
[
Sor96
,
Hin12
]
, Muchnik
[
Hin12
]
, and Weihrauch reducibilities
[
BG11
]
, from a categorical perspective is still lacking.
A categorical presentation of these notions is useful to highlight some of their abstract structural properties. Moreover, this work provides a common language for the categorical logic community and the computability theorists. These are two fields that traditionally employ very different languages and notations, so a categorical understanding of reducibility in computability with categorical logic descriptions in terms of doctrines has the positive effect of fostering collaborations between the two communities.

A first approach to Medvedev and Muchnik reducibility via hyperdoctrines has been introduced in
[
Kuy15
]
, while Weihrauch reducibility for assemblies (or multi-represented spaces) has been introduced only very recently in
[
Kih22
,
Sch22
]
through the notion of realizer-based Weihrauch reducibility. In
[
Bau22
]
, Bauer introduced an abstract notion of reducibility between predicates, called instance reducibility, which commonly appears in reverse constructive mathematics. In a relative realizability topos, the instance degrees correspond to a generalization of (realizer-based) Weihrauch reducibility, called extended Weihrauch degrees, and the “classical” Weihrauch degrees correspond precisely to the
\lnot\lnot
-dense modest instance degrees in Kleene-Vesley realizability. Upon closer inspection, it is not hard to check that realizer-based Weihrauch reducibility is a particular case of Bauer’s notion.

In this paper, we want to extend the use of the categorical tools of doctrines to computability, by presenting a specific doctrine for every previously mentioned notion of reducibility and studying their universal properties. In detail, we first introduce a doctrine for each of the notions of Turing, Medvedev, and Muchnik reducibility. After showing that these doctrines provide a proper categorification of these notions, we prove that both Medvedev and Muchnik reducibility are instances of the categorical construction called “universal completion” which freely adds “generalized universal quantifiers” to a given doctrine. This construction is a natural generalization of the “pure universal completion” used to characterize dialectica doctrines in
[
TSdP22a
,
TSdP23
]
. As a corollary of these results, we show that the Medvedev doctrine is obtained as the full universal completion of the doctrine of Turing degrees.

We then focus on the notion of Weihrauch reducibility (and its strong version) from a categorical perspective. Following along the same lines, we start our analysis by defining a doctrine abstracting the ordinary notion of Weihrauch reducibility, and then we prove that this doctrine can be obtained as the pure existential completion of a more basic doctrine. The crucial difference between this setting and the previous one concerns the base category of the doctrine we consider. Indeed, while for Medvedev and Muchnik reducibility, the base category of the doctrines is the category
\mathsf{Set}
of sets and functions, for Weihrauch we will use the category
\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathsf{p}}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
whose objects are subsets of the PCA
\mathbb{A}
, and whose morphisms are computable functions. This change of perspective is necessary to fully abstract the details of a Weihrauch reduction (in particular, it captures the role of the
forward functional
).

The last part of our work is devoted to studying Weihrauch reducibility in the more general context of partial multi-valued functions on represented spaces, a common setting for people working in computable analysis. However, the problem of generalizing our previous approach to this setting is non-trivial.

In order to fully (and smoothly) abstract Weihrauch reducibility for represented spaces in the language of doctrines, we introduce a new base-category
\mathsf{extAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
for our doctrines, that will turn out to be equivalent to the ordinary category of partition assemblies. The objects of
\mathsf{extAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
are assemblies, but the morphisms are better suited to abstract the properties of the forward functional. Even if the category of partition assemblies is well-known and studied in the literature, using the category
\mathsf{extAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
as base-category for doctrines abstracting Weihrauch reducibility has two main advantages: from a conceptual point of view, its objects and morphisms have an immediate and clear connection with the usual notions involved in the ordinary presentation of generalizations of Weihrauch reducibility to assemblies. Then, from a purely technical perspective, employing
\mathsf{extAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
will make the proofs of our main results quite smooth.

Once the issue concerning the base category is resolved and the doctrines are appropriately defined, we finally demonstrate that all these doctrines are instances of pure existential completion, following the same lines adopted for the first Weihrauch doctrine we introduced.

Our abstract analysis systematizes all these variants of Weihrauch reducibility and shows a clear connection between them. In particular, we highlight how extended Weihrauch reducibility can be seen as the most general variant, and all the others can be obtained from it by considering suitable restrictions of this doctrine.

We conclude our work by highlighting an interesting connection between Medvedev and extended Weihrauch reducibility, and discussing how the notion of extended strong Weihrauch is related to Gödel’s Dialectica interpretation
[
Göd90
]
. In particular, our presentation allows us to provide a formal explanation of the resemblance between the structure of Dialectica categories and some known notions of computability (extended Weihrauch reducibility), which has been observed by several authors over the years.

The outline of this paper is as follows: in Section
2
and Section
3
, we recall the background notions of partial combinatory algebra and quantifier completions. In Section
4
we reformulate Medvedev and Muchnik reducibility in categorical terms. In Section
5
we categorify Weihrauch reducibility and, in Section
6
, we consider the just mentioned generalizations of Weihrauch reducibility. Finally, we draw some conclusions in Section
7
.

2.
Partial combinatory algebras

Realizability theory originated with Kleene’s interpretation of intuitionistic number theory
[
Kle45
]
and has since developed into a large body of work in logic and theoretical computer science. We focus on two basic flavors of realizability, number realizability, and function realizability, which were both due to Kleene.

In this section, we recall some standard notions within realizability and computability. We follow the approach suggested by van Oosten
[
vO08
]
, as we want to fix a suitable notation for both category theorists and computability logicians.

We describe
partial combinatory algebras
and discuss some important examples. For more details, we refer the reader to van Oosten’s work on categorical realizability (see
[
vO08
]
and the references therein).

We start by introducing the basic concept of a
partial applicative structure
or PAS, due to Feferman, which may be viewed as a universe for computation.

Definition
\thethm

(PAS)
.

A
partial applicative structure
, or PAS for short, is a set
\mathbb{A}
equipped with a

partial
binary operation

\cdot:\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}
.

Some conventions and terminology: given two elements
a,b
in
\mathbb{A}
, we think of
a\cdot b
(which we often abbreviate
ab
) as “
a
applied to
b
”. The partiality of the operation
\cdot
means that this application need not always be defined. We write
f:\subseteq A\to B
to say that
f
is a partial function with domain a subset of
A
,
\operatorname{dom}(f)\subseteq A
and range
\operatorname{ran}(f)=B
.
If
(a,b)\in\operatorname{dom}(\cdot)
, that is, when the application is defined, then we write
a\cdot b\downarrow
or
ab\downarrow
.

We usually omit brackets, assuming associativity of application to the left. Thus
abc
stands for
(ab)c
. Moreover, for two expressions
x
and
y
we write
x\simeq y
to indicate that
x
is defined whenever
y
is, in which case they are equal.

Even though these partial applicative structures do not possess many interesting properties (they have no axioms for application), they already highlight one of the key features of combinatorial structures, namely the fact that we have a domain of elements that can act both as functions and as arguments, just as in untyped
\lambda
-calculus.
This behavior can be traced back to Von Neumann’s idea that programs (functions, operations) live in the same realm and are represented in the same way as the data (arguments) that they act upon.
In particular, programs can act on other programs.

Definition
\thethm

(PCA)
.

A
partial combinatory algebra
(PCA) is a PAS
\mathbb{A}
for which there exist elements
k,s\in\mathbb{A}
such that for all
a,b,c\in\mathbb{A}
we have that

ka\downarrow\mbox{ and }kab\simeq a

and

sa\downarrow\mbox{, }sab\downarrow\mbox{, and }sabc\simeq ac(bc)

The elements
k
and
s
are generalizations of the homonymous combinators in Combinatory Logic. Note that appropriate elements
k,s
are not considered part of the structure of the partial combinatory algebra, so they need not be preserved under homomorphisms.

Every PCA
\mathbb{A}
is combinatory complete in the sense of
[
Fef75
,
vO08
]
, namely: for every term
t(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})
built from variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1}
, constants
\bar{c}
for
c\in\mathbb{A}
, and application operator
\cdot
, there is an element
a\in\mathbb{A}
such that for all elements
b_{1},\dots,b_{n+1}\in\mathbb{A}
we have that
ab_{1}\cdots b_{n}\downarrow
and
ab_{1}\cdots b_{n+1}\simeq t(b_{1},\dots,b_{n+1})
.

In particular, we can use this result and the elements
k
and
s
to construct elements
\mathsf{pair},\mathsf{fst},\mathsf{snd}
of
\mathbb{A}
so that
(a,b)\mapsto\mathsf{pair}\cdot a\cdot b
is an injection of
\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{A}
with left inverse
a\mapsto(\mathsf{fst}\cdot a,\mathsf{snd}\cdot a)
. Hence, we can use
\mathsf{pair}ab
as an element of
\mathbb{A}
which codes the pair
(a,b)
. For this reason, the elements
\mathsf{pair},\mathsf{fst},\mathsf{snd}
are usually called
pairing
and
projection
operators. For the sake of readability, we write
\langle a,b\rangle
in place of
\mathsf{pair}ab
(as it is more customary in computability theory).

Using
s
and
k
, we can prove the analogues of the Universal Turing Machine (UTM) and the SMN theorems in computability in an arbitrary PCA.
A PCA
\mathbb{A}
is called
extensional
if for all
x
in
\mathbb{A}
,
(ax\simeq bx)
implies
a=b
.

By definition, in every extensional PCA, if two elements represent the same partial function, then they must be equal.

Next we recall the notion of
elementary sub-PCA
(see for example
[
vO08
, Sec. 2.6.9]
). Many definitions in the computability context refer to a concept and a subset of the given concept, as one needs to pay attention to the computable functions (and elements) included in the original concept.

Definition
\thethm

(elementary sub-PCA)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA. A subset
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
is called an
elementary sub-PCA
of
\mathbb{A}
if
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
is a PCA with the partial applicative structure induced by
\mathbb{A}
(namely, the elements
k
and
s
as in
can be found in
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
). In particular, it is closed under the application of
\mathbb{A}
, that means: if
a,b\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
and
ab\downarrow
in
\mathbb{A}
then
ab\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
.

In particular, elements of the sub-PCA will play the role of the computable functions.

Example
\thethm

(
Kleene’s first model
)
.

Fix an effective enumeration
(\varphi_{a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}}
of the partial recursive functions
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
(i.e. a Gödel numbering). The set
\mathbb{N}
with partial recursive application
(a,b)\mapsto\varphi_{a}(b)
is a PCA, and it is called
Kleene’s first model

\mathcal{K}_{1}
(see e.g.
[
Soa87
]
).

Example
\thethm

(
Kleene’s second model
)
.

The PCA
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is often used for function realizability
[
vO08
, Sec. 1.4.3]
. This PCA is given by the Baire space
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, endowed with the product topology.
The partial binary operation of application
\cdot:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
corresponds to the one used in Type-
2
Theory of Effectivity
[
Wei00
]
.
This can be described as follows. Let
\alpha[n]
denote the string
(\alpha(0),\ldots,\alpha(n-1))
.
Every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
induces a function
F_{\alpha}:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}
defined as
F_{\alpha}(\beta)=k
if there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha(\langle\beta[n]\rangle)=k+1
and
(\forall m<n)(\alpha(\langle\beta[n]\rangle)=0)
, and undefined otherwise. The application
\alpha\cdot\beta
can then be defined as the map
n\mapsto F_{\alpha}((n)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}\beta)
, where
(n)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}\beta
is the string
\sigma
defined as
\sigma(0):=n
and
\sigma(k+1):=\beta(k)
.

When working with Kleene’s second model, we usually consider the elementary sub-PCA
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{rec}
consisting of all the
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\beta\mapsto\alpha\cdot\beta
is computable. For more details and examples, also of non-elementary sub-PCAs for Kleene’s second model, we refer to
[
vO11
]
.

Remark
\thethm
.

Kleene’s
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
presented above are not extensional, since there are many codes (programs) that compute the same function. In fact, every function has infinitely many representatives.

Categorical Doctrines

We want to connect the notions of computability as described in the previous subsection to work on (logical) categorical doctrines in
[
TSdP22a
]

Several generalizations of the notion of a (Lawvere) hyperdoctrine have been considered recently, we refer, for example, to the works of Rosolini and Maietti
[
MPR17

MR13

Definition
\thethm

(doctrine)
.

A
doctrine
is a contravariant functor:

\textstyle{P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}

where the category
\mathcal{C}
has finite products and
\mathsf{Pos}
is the category of (partially ordered sets or) posets.

Definition
\thethm

(morphism of doctrines)
.

A morphism of doctrines is a pair
\mathfrak{L}:=(F,\mathfrak{b})

\textstyle{\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{P}
\scriptstyle{F^{\operatorname{op}}}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{R}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{b}}

such that

is a finite product preserving functor and:

Example
\thethm
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA.
We can define a functor
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}^{(-)}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
assigning to a set
X

\mathbb{A}^{X}
of functions from
X
to
\mathbb{A}
.
By standard properties of PCAs, given two elements
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{A}^{X}

\alpha\leq\beta
if there exists an element
a\in\mathbb{A}
such that for every

The doctrine given by considering the poset reflection of such a preorder is the

.

Given a PCA

dPdS20
]
.
in the style of Kolmogorov
[
Kol91
]
, and now it is well established in the computability literature. We briefly introduce the main notions and definitions on the topic. For a more thorough presentation, the reader is referred to
[
Sor96
,
Hin12
]
.

A set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
is sometimes called a
mass problem
. The intuition is that a mass problem corresponds to the set of solutions for a specific computational problem. For example, the problem of deciding membership in a particular
P\subseteq\mathbb{N}
corresponds to the mass problem
\{\chi_{P}\}
, where
\chi_{P}
is the characteristic function of
P
. Similarly, the problem of enumerating
P
corresponds to the family
\{f\colon\mathbb{N}\to P:f\text{ is surjective}\}
.

While Medvedev reducibility is usually defined in the context of Type-
2
computability, we can give a slightly more general definition in the context of PCAs.

Definition
\thethm

(Medvedev reducible set)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA and let
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
be an elementary sub-PCA of
\mathbb{A}
. If
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{A}
, we say that
A
is
Medvedev reducible to

B
, and write
A\leq_{\mathrm{M}}B
, if there is an effective functional
\Phi\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that
\Phi(B)\subseteq A
, i.e.
(\forall b\in B)(\Phi(b)\in A)
.

The notion of Medvedev reducibility induces a preorder on the powerset of
\mathbb{A}
, whose quotient is the
Medvedev lattice
.
In the following, whenever there is no ambiguity, we identify a degree with any of its representatives.

We will now introduce a
Medvedev doctrine
that generalizes the notion of Medvedev reducibility. Intuitively, the Medvedev doctrine maps every singleton
X
to an isomorphic copy of the Medvedev lattice. However, if
X
is not a singleton, we obtain a somewhat different structure, corresponding to having several Medvedev reductions all witnessed by the same map.

To define the Medvedev doctrine we first introduce the
Turing doctrine
.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA and
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
be an elementary sub-PCA. We can define a functor
\textstyle{\mathfrak{T}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
mapping a set
X
to the set
\mathbb{A}^{X}
of functions from
X
to
\mathbb{A}
. Given two elements
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{T}(X)
, we define
\alpha\leq_{\mathsf{T}}\beta
if there exists an element
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that for every
x\in X
we have that
\overline{a}\cdot\beta(x)
is defined and
\overline{a}\cdot\beta(x)=\alpha(x)
. The functor
\textstyle{\mathfrak{T}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
is called
Turing doctrine
.

Remark
\thethm
.

The name “Turing doctrine” is motivated by the fact that, when working with Kleene’s second model, then
\mathfrak{T}(1)
can be identified with
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and the reduction
\alpha\leq_{\mathsf{T}}\beta
holds whenever there is a computable functional
\Phi
such that
\Phi(\beta)=\alpha
, i.e.
(\forall n)(\Phi(\beta)(n)=\alpha(n))
. This corresponds precisely to the notion of Turing reducibility between functions
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
.

Notice also that, in the previous example, it is important to consider Kleene’s second model. Indeed, if we instead work with Kleene’s first model,
\mathfrak{T}(1)
would be trivial (all numbers are computable) and
\mathfrak{T}(\mathbb{N})
would give rise to a stronger notion of reducibility than Turing reducibility: given
f,g\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
,
f\leq_{\mathsf{T}}g
iff there is a computable functional
\Phi
such that
(\forall n)(\Phi(g(n))=f(n))
, hence only the value of
g(n)
is needed to compute
f(n)
.

Definition
\thethm

(Medvedev doctrine)
.

Given a PCA
\mathbb{A}
with elementary sub-PCA
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
, we define the
Medvedev doctrine

\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
over
\mathsf{Set}
as follows: for every set
X
and every pair of functions
\varphi,\psi
in
\raisebox{1.70717pt}{\Large$\wp$}(\mathbb{A})^{X}
, we define

\displaystyle\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi:\iff

\displaystyle(\exists\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime})(\forall x\in X)(\forall b\in\psi(x))(\exists a\in\varphi(x))(\overline{a}\cdot b=a)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\exists\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime})(\forall x\in X)(\overline{a}\cdot\psi(x)\subseteq\varphi(x)).

This preorder induces an equivalence relation on functions in
\raisebox{1.70717pt}{\Large$\wp$}(\mathbb{A})^{X}
.
The poset
\mathfrak{M}(X)
is defined as the quotient of
\raisebox{1.70717pt}{\Large$\wp$}(\mathbb{A})^{X}
by the equivalence relation generated by
\leq_{\mathrm{M}}
. The partial order on the equivalence classes
[\varphi]
is the one induced by the Medvedev order
\leq_{\mathrm{M}}
. Moreover, given a function
f\colon X\to Y
, the functor
\mathfrak{M}_{f}\colon\mathfrak{M}(Y)\to\mathfrak{M}(X)
is defined as
\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi):=\psi\circ f
.

Observe that, when working with Kleene’s second PCA, if
X
is a singleton then
\mathfrak{M}(X)
corresponds exactly to the Medvedev degrees. For an arbitrary set
X
, the reduction
\varphi,\psi\in\mathfrak{M}(X)
corresponds to a uniform Medvedev reducibility between
\varphi(x)
and
\psi(x)
, where
x\in X
. In particular, if
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we can think of
\varphi
and
\psi
as computational problems on the Baire space. In this case, the reduction
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi
corresponds to a strong Weihrauch reduction where the forward functional is simply the identity function. We will discuss strong Weihrauch reducibility and its connections with the Medvedev doctrine in more detail in Sections
5.1
,
6
.

We also show that, for every
X
,
\mathfrak{M}(X)
is a distributive lattice with
\bot=\{\mathbb{A}\}
and
\top=\emptyset
, where the join and the meet of the lattice are induced respectively by the following operation on mass problems:

•

A\lor B:=\{\langle a,b\rangle:a\in A\text{ and }b\in B\}
,

•

A\land B:=A\sqcup B=\{(0)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}a:a\in A\}\cup\{(1)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}b:b\in B\}
, where
(n)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}f(0):=n
and
(n)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}f(i+1):=f(i)
.

Next we want to show that, for every
X
,
\mathfrak{M}(X)
is a co-Heyting algebra (i.e. a Brouwer algebra, see
[
Sor96
, Thm. 9.1]
), where the subtraction operation is defined as
A\backslash B:=\min\{C:B\leq A\lor B\}
. In other words, this subtraction is an ‘implication’ with respect to the join of the lattice. The fact that, in general,
\mathfrak{M}(X)
is not a Heyting algebra follows from the fact that
\mathfrak{M}(1)
is not (see
[
Sor96
, Thm. 9.2]
).

Proposition
\thethm

(Medvedev co-Heyting algebra)
.

For every set
X
,
\mathfrak{M}(X)
is a co-Heyting algebra, where:

(1)

\bot:=x\mapsto\mathbb{A}

(2)

\top:=x\mapsto\emptyset
;

(3)

(\varphi\wedge\psi)(x):=\{\langle p_{1},a\rangle:a\in\varphi(x)\}\cup\{\langle p_{2},b\rangle:b\in\psi(x)\}
, where
p_{1},p_{2}
are two fixed (different) elements in
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
.

(4)

(\varphi\vee\psi)(x):=\{\langle a,b\rangle:a\in\varphi(x)\text{ and }b\in\psi(x)\}
;

(5)

(\varphi\backslash\psi)(x):=\{c\in\mathbb{A}:(\forall b\in\psi(x))(c\cdot b\in\varphi(x))\}
.

Proof.

This proposition can be proved essentially the same way one proves that the Medvedev degrees form a co-Heyting algebra (see
[
Sor96
, Thm. 1.3]
). Let
\varphi,\psi\in\mathfrak{M}(X)
.

(1)

The reduction
\bot\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi
is witnessed by the identity functional.

(2)

The reduction
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\top
is trivially witnessed by any
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
, as the quantification on
b\in\top(x)
is vacuously true.

(3)

The reductions
\varphi\land\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi
and
\varphi\land\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi
are witnessed respectively by the maps
a\mapsto\langle p_{1},a\rangle
and
b\mapsto\langle p_{2},b\rangle
. Moreover, if
\rho\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi
via
a_{\varphi}
and
\rho\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi
via
a_{\psi}
then the reduction
\rho\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi\land\psi
is witnessed by the map that, upon input
\langle p,c\rangle
, if
p=p_{1}
returns
a_{\varphi}\cdot c
, otherwise returns
a_{\psi}\cdot c
.

(4)

The reductions
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi\lor\psi
and
\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi\lor\psi
are witnessed by the projections. Moreover, if
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\rho
via
a_{\varphi}
and
\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\rho
via
a_{\psi}
then
\varphi\lor\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\rho
is witnessed by the map
x\mapsto\langle a_{\varphi}\cdot x,a_{\psi}\cdot x\rangle
.

(5)

We need to show that, for every
\rho
,
\varphi\backslash\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\rho\iff\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi\lor\rho
. To prove the left-to-right implication, observe that if
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
witnesses the reduction
\varphi\backslash\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\rho
, then, for every
\langle b,d\rangle\in(\psi\lor\rho)(x)
,
\overline{a}\cdot d\in(\varphi\backslash\psi)(x)
, and therefore
(\overline{a}\cdot d)\cdot b\in\varphi(x)
. To prove the right-to-left implication, notice that if
\overline{b}
witnesses
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi\lor\rho
, then, by definition, for every
\langle b,c\rangle\in(\psi\lor\rho)(x)
,
\overline{b}\cdot\langle b,c\rangle\in\varphi(x)
. This implies that the map
b\mapsto\overline{b}\cdot\langle b,c\rangle\in(\varphi\backslash\psi)(x)
, therefore concluding the proof. ∎

Now we want to show some structural properties of Medvedev’s doctrines.
Using the two theorems we recalled from previous work, we can show:

Proposition
\thethm
.

The Medvedev doctrine
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
is a full universal and pure existential doctrine. In particular, for every function
f\colon X\to Y
, the morphism
\forall_{f}\colon\mathfrak{M}(X)\to\mathfrak{M}(Y)
sending an element
\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}(X)
to the element
\forall_{f}(\varphi)\in\mathfrak{M}(Y)
defined as

\forall_{f}(\varphi)(y):=\bigcup_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\varphi(x)

is right adjoint to
\mathfrak{M}_{f}
, i.e.
\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\forall_{f}(\varphi)\iff\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi
for any
\psi\in\mathfrak{M}(Y)
and
\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}(X)
.

Similarly, if
f
is surjective then the assignment

\exists_{f}(\varphi)(y):=\bigcap_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\varphi(x)

determines a left adjoint to
\mathfrak{M}_{f}
, i.e.
\exists_{f}(\varphi)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi\iff\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)
for any
\psi\in\mathfrak{M}(Y)
and
\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}(X)
.

Proof.

This is essentially a definition-chasing exercise. Let us first show that, for every
\psi\in\mathfrak{M}(Y)
and
\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}(X)
,
\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\forall_{f}(\varphi)\iff\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi
. Assume first that the reduction
\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\forall_{f}(\varphi)
is witnessed by
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
. The same
\overline{a}
witnesses
\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi
: indeed, for every
x\in X
and every
a\in\varphi(x)
, we have
a\in\forall_{f}(\varphi)(f(x))
, and therefore
\overline{a}\cdot a\in\psi(f(x))=\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)(x)
. On the other hand, assume
\overline{b}
witnesses
\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\varphi
. For every
y\in Y
, if
b\in\forall_{f}(\varphi)(y)
then
b\in\varphi(x)
for some
x\in f^{-1}(y)
. In particular,
\overline{b}\cdot b\in\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)(x)=\psi(y)
, i.e.
\overline{b}
witnesses
\psi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\forall_{f}(\varphi)
.

The second part of the statement is proved analogously. Assume first that
\exists_{f}(\varphi)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi
is witnessed by
\overline{c}
. Fix
x\in X
and
a\in\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)(x)=\psi(f(x))
. In particular,
\overline{c}\cdot a\in\exists_{f}(\varphi)(f(x))
, and hence
\overline{c}\cdot a\in\varphi(x)
. Finally, assume
\overline{d}
witnesses
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\mathfrak{M}_{f}(\psi)
, and let
y\in Y
and
b\in\psi(y)
. Since
f
is surjective, there is
x\in X
s.t.
f(x)=y
. Moreover, for every
x\in f^{-1}(y)
we have
b\in\psi(f(x))
and, hence,
\overline{d}\cdot b\in\varphi(x)
. This implies that
\overline{d}\cdot b\in\bigcap_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\varphi(x)=\exists_{f}(\varphi)(y)
.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to studying abstract
universal
properties of Medvedev doctrines.

Theorem
\thethm

(Medvedev isomorphism)
.

Let
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
be the Medvedev doctrine for a given PCA
\mathbb{A}
. Then we have the isomorphism of full universal doctrines

\mathfrak{M}\equiv{\mathfrak{T}}^{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}.

In particular, for every set
X
, the map

(f,\alpha)\mapsto\alpha\circ f^{-1}

is a surjective (pre)order homomorphism between
({\mathfrak{T}}^{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}(X),\leq_{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}})
and
(\mathfrak{M}(X),\leq_{\mathrm{M}})
.

Proof.

Observe that the full universal completion
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}
of
\mathfrak{T}
can be described as follows:

•

{\mathfrak{T}}^{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}(X)=\{(f,\alpha):f\colon Y\to X\text{ and }\alpha\in\mathfrak{T}(Y)\},

•

if
f\colon Y\to X
,
\alpha\in\mathfrak{T}(Y)
,
g\colon Z\to X
, and
\beta\in\mathfrak{T}(Z)
, then

(f,\alpha)\leq_{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}(g,\beta)\iff(\exists h\colon Z\to Y)((\forall z\in Z)(g(z)=f\circ h(z))\text{ and }(\mathfrak{T})_{h}(\alpha)\leq_{\mathsf{T}}\beta).

In other words,
(f,\alpha)\leq_{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}(g,\beta)
if and only if there are
h\colon Z\to Y
and
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}
such that the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{a}}

If we define
\varphi:=\alpha\circ f^{-1}
and
\psi:=\beta\circ g^{-1}
. we obtain the following diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{a}}

where
\varphi
and
\psi
are represented as double arrows to stress the fact that they are maps into
\raisebox{1.70717pt}{\Large$\wp$}(\mathbb{A})
.

Let us first show that
(f,\alpha)\leq_{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}(g,\beta)
implies
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi
. Fix two witnesses
h\colon Z\to Y
and
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
for
(f,\alpha)\leq_{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}(g,\beta)
and fix
x\in X
. If
x\notin\operatorname{ran}(g)
then
\psi(x)=\emptyset
, hence there is nothing to prove. Assume therefore that
x\in\operatorname{ran}(g)
. By definition,
\psi(x)=\beta(g^{-1}(x))\neq\emptyset
. Fix
b\in\psi(x)
and let
z\in g^{-1}(x)
be s.t.
\beta(z)=b
. Since
(f,\alpha)\leq_{\forall_{\mathsf{f}}}(g,\beta)
, we can write

\overline{a}\cdot b=\overline{a}\cdot\beta(z)=\alpha\circ h(z)=\alpha(y),

for some
y\in f^{-1}(x)
. In particular,
\alpha(y)\in\varphi(x)
, and therefore
\overline{a}
witnesses
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi
.

Let us now prove the other direction. Let
\overline{b}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
be a witness for
\varphi\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\psi
. Fix
z\in Z
. Clearly, letting
x_{z}:=g(z)
,
\psi(x_{z})\neq\emptyset
. Moreover,
\psi(x_{z})\neq\emptyset
implies
\varphi(x_{z})=\alpha(f^{-1}(x_{z}))\neq\emptyset
(as
\overline{b}\cdot\psi(x_{z})\subseteq\varphi(x_{z})
).
We define
h
as a choice function that maps

to some

such that
\alpha(y)\in\overline{b}\cdot\psi(x_{z})
. Observe that

is well-defined as if

then

for some

. This also shows that

and
\overline{b}
witness

.

Finally, to show that the homomorphism is surjective it is enough to notice that every
\varphi\colon X\to\raisebox{1.70717pt}{\Large$\wp$}(\mathbb{A})
is the image of the pair
(\pi_{X},\pi_{\mathbb{A}})
, where

, and
\pi_{X}\colon Y\to X
and
\pi_{\mathbb{A}}\colon Y\to\mathbb{A}
are the two projections.
∎

In other words, when working with Kleene’s second model, the Medvedev degrees are isomorphic to the full universal completion of the Turing doctrine.

4.1.
Muchnik Doctrines

Using a very similar strategy, we now show that the
Muchnik lattice
is isomorphic to the full universal completion of a doctrine. The notion of
Muchnik reducibility
formally dates back to 1963 (but it was probably known earlier) and can be thought of as the non-uniform version of Medvedev reducibility. More precisely, given two mass problems
P,Q\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we say that
P
is Muchnik reducible to
Q
, and write
P\leq_{w}Q
, if for every
q\in Q
there is a functional
\Phi
such that
\Phi(q)\in P
. In other words,
P\leq_{w}Q
if every element of
Q
computes some element of
P
. Muchnik reducibility is sometimes called “weak reducibility” (which motivates the choice of the symbol
\leq_{w}
) to contrast it with Medvedev reducibility, sometimes called “strong reducibility”
[
Hin12
]
.

Similarly to the Medvedev degrees, the Muchnik degrees form a distributive lattice, where the join and the meet operations are induced by the same operations on subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
that induce the join and the meet in the Medvedev degrees. Unlike the Medvedev lattice, the Muchnik lattice is both a Heyting and a co-Heyting algebra
[
Hin12
, Prop. 4.3 and 4.7]
.

Definition
\thethm

(Muchnik doctrine of singletons)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA and let
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
be an elementary sub-PCA. We can define a doctrine
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}_{w}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
mapping a set
X
to the set
\mathbb{A}^{X}
of functions from
X
to
\mathbb{A}
. Given two elements
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{A}_{w}(X)
, we define
\alpha\leq_{\mathsf{dw}}\beta
if

(\forall x\in X)(\exists\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime})(\overline{a}\cdot\beta(x)=\alpha(x)).

The functor
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}_{w}\colon\mathsf{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Pos}}
is called
Muchnik doctrine of singletons
.

Observe that the order
\leq_{\mathsf{dw}}
is just the non-uniform version of the order
\leq_{\mathsf{T}}
induced by the Turing doctrine. In particular, if
X
is a singleton then
\mathbb{A}_{w}(X)=\mathfrak{T}(X)
. This implies that Remark
4
applies to the Muchnik doctrine of singletons as well. On the other hand, both
\mathbb{A}_{w}(1)

5.
Weihrauch Doctrines

Weihrauch reducibility is a notion of reducibility between computational problems that is useful to calibrate the uniform computational strength of a multi-valued function. It complements the analysis of mathematical theorems done in reverse mathematics, as multi-valued functions on represented spaces can be considered as realizers of theorems in a natural way. Weihrauch reducibility provides a framework where one can formalize questions such as “which theorems can be transformed continuously or computably into another?”
[
BG11
]

Given the connection between Medvedev and Weihrauch reducibility, it is natural to ask whether, with a similar approach as the one from the last section, we can also describe Weihrauch reducibility as the completion of some existential doctrine.

This observation would suggest that, to present a doctrine abstracting the notion of Weihrauch reducibility for represented spaces and assemblies, a natural choice could be that of considering a category whose objects are represented spaces and whose morphisms are multi-valued functions. However, this approach does not align well with the categorical framework of doctrines, primarily because represented spaces and multi-valued functions lack the structure of cartesian products.

Indeed, the category

]

In summary, the challenge in extending our approach to assemblies and modest sets lies in defining a new notion of “morphism of assemblies”, so as to obtain a new category with cartesian products whose morphisms allow for a categorical presentation of realizer-based Weihrauch reducibility.

We conclude this section by highlighting an interesting connection between Medvedev and extended Weihrauch reducibility. The definition of extended Weihrauch reducibility can be naturally strengthened (to obtain its

To establish such a link, we employ a result due to Hofstra

7.
Conclusions

We set out to and managed to categorify the notions of Turing, Medvedev, Muchnik, and Weihrauch reducibility and their variants.

To show the categorification works, we proved the Medvedev isomorphism theorem, the Muchnik isomorphism theorem, and the Weihrauch isomorphism theorems.
We also showed how the respective Medvedev, Muchnik, and Weihrauch doctrines relate to existential and universal completions. Then, we consider several generalizations of Weihrauch reducibility for represented spaces and assemblies, extending our previous approach to these settings.
In future work, we aim to delve more deeply into the algebraic structures of these doctrines. If the structure proves to be sufficiently suitable, we intend to apply the tripos-to-topos construction to these doctrines and study the resulting categories.

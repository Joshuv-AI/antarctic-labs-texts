Scalar product for the XXZ spin chain with general integrable boundaries
Samuel Belliard ∗, Rodrigo A. Pimenta ∗∗, Nikita A. Slavnov ∗∗∗

Abstract
. We calculate the scalar product of Bethe states of the XXZ spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} chain with general integrable boundary conditions. The off-shell equations satisfied by the transfer matrix and the off-shell Bethe vectors allow one to derive a linear system for the scalar product of off-shell and on-shell Bethe states. We show that this linear system can be solved in terms of a compact determinant formula that involves the Jacobian of the transfer matrix eigenvalue and certain q-Pochhammer polynomials of the boundary couplings.



Introduction.

The anisotropic Heisenberg XXZ spin chain is a paradigmatic model of interacting many body quantum system. It is an integrable model within the quantum inverse scattering method
[
31
,
30
]
. In this paper, we consider the XXZ spin chain on the segment with general integrable boundaries given by the Hamiltonian

(1)

\displaystyle H=\epsilon\,\sigma^{3}_{1}+\kappa^{-}\,\sigma^{-}_{1}+\kappa^{+}\,\sigma^{+}_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\Big(\sigma^{x}_{k}\otimes\sigma^{x}_{k+1}+\sigma^{y}_{k}\otimes\sigma^{y}_{k+1}+\Delta\sigma^{3}_{k}\otimes\sigma^{3}_{k+1}\Big)+\nu\,\sigma^{3}_{N}+\tau^{-}\,\sigma^{-}_{N}+\tau^{+}\,\sigma^{+}_{N},

where
N
is the length of the chain,
\sigma_{i}^{\pm,x,y,3}
are the standard Pauli matrices acting non-trivially in the
i
-th site of the quantum space
\mathcal{H}=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The anisotropy parameter is
\Delta=\frac{q+q^{-1}}{2}
with generic
q
. Left
\{\epsilon,\kappa^{\pm}\}
and right

\{\nu,\tau^{\pm}\}
boundary couplings are also generic. The aim of this
paper is to compute the scalar product of on-shell and off-shell Bethe
states of the Hamiltonian (
1
).

The building blocks of the quantum inverse scattering method are the R-matrix, which solves the Yang–Baxter equations, and the K-matrices, which solve the reflection equation
[
19
,
35
]
. They can be used to construct commuting transfer matrices, which contain the XXZ Hamiltonian with arbitrary boundary couplings (
1
) as a conserved charge. Solving the spectral problem of the transfer matrix is the primordial aim, followed by the computation of scalar products, form factors and correlation functions (see,
e.g.
,
[
20
]
).
One of the most powerful methods available to handle such problems is the algebraic Bethe ansatz:
it allows the construction of off-shell Bethe states of the transfer matrix as well as certain scalar products in terms of
compact formulas (see
[
32
]
for a recent review).

Let us recall that the K-matrices are associated with boundary fields at each end of the spin chain.
The boundaries in general can break the
U(1)
symmetry of the bulk Hamiltonian, and
it happens when the K-matrices have a general non-diagonal form.
This breaking prevents the application of the standard Bethe ansatz technique to analyze the
spectral problem of the transfer matrix, unless some constraints on the boundary couplings are imposed (see e.g.
[
13
,
40
,
26
,
28
,
6
,
29
,
15
]
)

R-matrix and K-matrices.

We recall the basic ingredients of the quantum inverse scattering method. The main object is the R-matrix, given by

\displaystyle R(u)=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}qu-q^{-1}u^{-1}&0&0&0\\
0&u-u^{-1}&q-q^{-1}&0\\
0&q-q^{-1}&u-u^{-1}&0\\
0&0&0&qu-q^{-1}u^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)\,.

The R-matrix acts on the space
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
and is a solution of the Yang–Baxter
equation in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle R_{12}(u/v)R_{13}(u)R_{23}(v)=R_{23}(v)R_{13}(u)R_{12}(u/v)\,.

Here the indices
ij
in
R_{ij}(u)
indicate the vector spaces where it acts non-trivially. Next, in order to consider the boundary fields, one introduces the reflection matrices

\displaystyle K^{-}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\nu_{-}u+\nu_{+}u^{-1}&\tau^{2}(u^{2}-u^{-2})\\
\tilde{\tau}^{2}(u^{2}-u^{-2})&\nu_{-}u^{-1}+\nu_{+}u\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

\displaystyle K^{+}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon_{+}qu+\epsilon_{-}q^{-1}u^{-1}&\tilde{\kappa}^{2}(q^{2}u^{2}-q^{-2}u^{-2})\\
\kappa^{2}(q^{2}u^{2}-q^{-2}u^{-2})&\epsilon_{-}qu+\epsilon_{+}q^{-1}u^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right),

where
\{\nu_{\pm},\tau,\tilde{\tau},\epsilon_{\pm},\kappa,\tilde{\kappa}\}
are arbitrary parameters
[
19
,
35
]
. They are the most general solutions of
the reflection equation and of the dual reflection equation
[
30
]
, which hold in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
:

(14)

\displaystyle R_{12}(u/v)K^{-}_{1}(u)R_{12}(uv)K^{-}_{2}(v)=K^{-}_{2}(v)R_{12}(uv)K^{-}_{1}(u)R_{12}(u/v),

(15)

\displaystyle R_{12}(v/u)K^{+}_{1}(u)R_{12}(q^{-2}u^{-1}v^{-1})K^{+}_{2}(v)=K^{+}_{2}(v)R_{12}(q^{-2}u^{-1}v^{-1})K^{+}_{1}(u)R_{12}(v/u).

From the
R
and
K^{\pm}
matrices, one can define the so-called double-row transfer matrix

(16)

\displaystyle t(u)=\operatorname{Tr}_{a}(K_{a}^{+}(u)T_{a}(u)K_{a}^{-}(u)\hat{T}_{a}(u)),

where
a
denotes an auxiliary
\mathbb{C}^{2}
vector space, and

(17)

\displaystyle T_{a}(u)=R_{a1}(u/x_{1})R_{a2}(u/x_{2})\cdots R_{aN}(u/x_{N})\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{a}(u)=R_{aN}(ux_{N})R_{aN-1}(ux_{N-1})\cdots R_{a1}(ux_{1})\,,

are called
single-row monodromy matrices. They depend on arbitrary parameters
x_{i}
called inhomogeneities. We denote

\displaystyle K_{a}(u)=T_{a}(u)K_{a}^{-}(u)\hat{T}_{a}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathscr{A}(u)&\mathscr{B}(u)\\
\mathscr{C}(u)&\mathscr{D}(u)+\frac{q-q^{-1}}{qu^{2}-q^{-1}u^{-2}}\mathscr{A}(u)\end{array}\right)_{a},

where
\mathscr{A}(u),\mathscr{B}(u),\mathscr{C}(u),\mathscr{D}(u)
act on
\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{C}^{2}
and are called double-row monodromy operators.
It turns out that the transfer matrix (
16
)
enjoys the fundamental property

(22)

\displaystyle\left[t(u),t(v)\right]=0\,,

for arbitrary
u
and
v
. Thus, the transfer matrix (
16
) is a generating function of conserved charges of the model that allows one to reconstruct the Hamiltonian (
1
) through the relation

(23)

\displaystyle H=\frac{q-q^{-1}}{2}\frac{d}{du}\ln(t(u))\Big|_{u=1,w_{i}=1}-\left(N~\frac{q+q^{-1}}{2}+\frac{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}{2(q+q^{-1})}\right).

In terms of the boundary parameters of the
K^{\pm}
-matrices,
the couplings of the Hamiltonian (
1
) are expressed as

(24)

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{(q-q^{-1})}{2}\frac{(\epsilon_{+}-\epsilon_{-})}{(\epsilon_{+}+\epsilon_{-})},\quad\kappa^{-}=\frac{2(q-q^{-1})}{(\epsilon_{+}+\epsilon_{-})}\kappa^{2},\quad\kappa^{+}=\frac{2(q-q^{-1})}{(\epsilon_{+}+\epsilon_{-})}\tilde{\kappa}^{2},

(25)

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{(q-q^{-1})}{2}\frac{(\nu_{-}-\nu_{+})}{(\nu_{+}+\nu_{-})},\quad\tau^{-}=\frac{2(q-q^{-1})}{(\nu_{+}+\nu_{-})}\tilde{\tau}^{2},\quad\tau^{+}=\frac{2(q-q^{-1})}{(\nu_{+}+\nu_{-})}\tau^{2}.

For convenience, we will use the following parametrization for the boundary parameters:

\displaystyle\nu_{-}=i\tilde{\tau}\tau\big(\mu/\tilde{\mu}+\tilde{\mu}/\mu\big),\quad\nu_{+}=i\tilde{\tau}\tau\big(\mu\tilde{\mu}+1/(\mu\tilde{\mu})\big)\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{-}=i\tilde{\kappa}\kappa\big(\xi/\tilde{\xi}+\tilde{\xi}/\xi\big),\quad\epsilon_{+}=i\tilde{\kappa}\kappa\big(\xi\tilde{\xi}+1/(\tilde{\xi}\xi)\big).

The transfer matrix (
16
) is the object that can be diagonalized by means of the modified algebraic Bethe ansatz
[
7
,
8
,
1
]
which extend
the usual boundary algebraic Bethe ansatz
[
30
,
13
]
.

Modified Bethe vectors.

Usually, in the Bethe ansatz with
U(1)
symmetry, the eigenvectors of the transfer matrix are generated using the operators

\mathscr{B}(u)
or
\mathscr{C}(u)
. In the general case, one needs modified
creation and annihilation operators (see
[
1
]
) given by

(27)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}(u,m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{qu}{\gamma_{m+1}}\Big(\mathscr{B}(u)+\beta q^{m}\Big(\frac{qu\left(u^{2}-u^{-2}\right)}{\left(qu^{2}-q^{-1}u^{-2}\right)}\mathscr{A}(u)-u^{-1}\mathscr{D}(u)\Big)-\beta^{2}q^{2m}\mathscr{C}(u)\Big),

(28)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(u,m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{qu}{\gamma_{m-1}}\Big(\mathscr{B}(u)+\alpha q^{-m}\Big(\frac{qu\left(u^{2}-u^{-2}\right)}{\left(qu^{2}-q^{-1}u^{-2}\right)}\mathscr{A}(u)-u^{-1}\mathscr{D}(u)\Big)-\alpha^{2}q^{-2m}\mathscr{C}(u)\Big).

Here

(29)

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}=\alpha q^{-m}-\beta q^{m},\quad\beta=-i\frac{\tilde{\kappa}\tilde{\xi}}{\kappa\xi}q^{1-2N},\quad\alpha=-i\frac{\tilde{\kappa}\xi}{\kappa\tilde{\xi}}q^{1+2N}.

Let

\displaystyle|N\rangle=\otimes_{j=1}^{N}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}iq^{N-j}\frac{\mu\tau}{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\tau}x_{j}}\\
1\end{array}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle N|=\otimes_{j=1}^{N}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}iq^{N-j}\frac{\mu\tilde{\tau}x_{j}}{\tilde{\mu}\tau}&1\\
\end{array}\right)\,.

Acting on these vectors with the products of the modified
creation and annihilation operators we obtain the modified Bethe vectors

(34)

\displaystyle|\Psi(\bar{u})\rangle=\mathscr{B}(u_{1},2(N-1))\mathscr{B}(u_{2},2(N-2))\cdots\mathscr{B}(u_{N},0)|N\rangle,

and the dual ones

(35)

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|=\langle N|\mathscr{C}(v_{1},2)\mathscr{C}(v_{2},4)\cdots\mathscr{C}(v_{N},2N).

We denote
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
the set of Bethe parameters
\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N}\}
and
\{v_{1},u_{2},\dots,v_{N}\}
. When the sets
\bar{u}
or
\bar{v}

satisfy the Bethe ansatz equations (
\mathcal{Y}(u_{i}|\bar{u})=0
or
\mathcal{Y}(v_{i}|\bar{v})=0
, see (
40
) below), the vectors (
34
,
35
) are called on-shell
Bethe vectors. For arbitrary
parameters
\bar{u}
or
\bar{v}
, the vectors (
34
,
35
) are called off-shell Bethe vectors.

Off-shell equations of the transfer matrix.

Now we are in position to write down the action of the transfer matrix (
16
) on the off-shell Bethe vectors (
34
,
35
), following
[
1
]
. For that, it is convenient to introduce some shorthand notation, namely,

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}\backslash u_{i}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1},u_{i+1},\dots,u_{N}\}\,,\quad\text{for~the~set~with~the~}u_{i}\text{~element~removed}\,,

\displaystyle\{u,\bar{u}_{i}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1},u,u_{i+1},\dots,u_{N}\}\,,\quad\text{for~the~set~with~the~}u_{i}\text{~element~replaced~by~}u\,,

\displaystyle Q(u,\bar{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{N}Q(u,u_{j})\,,\quad\text{for~the~product~of~a~two-variable~function~over~the~set~}\bar{u}\,,

\displaystyle Q(u,\bar{u}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}Q(u,u_{j})\,,\quad\text{for~the~product~of~a~two-variable~function~over~the~set~}\bar{u}_{i}\,.

If no product is involved we denote,
e.g.
,

\displaystyle\Lambda(u|\bar{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda(u|{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}})\,,

for multi-variable functions.
Using this notation, we have the following compact off-shell relations:

(36)

\displaystyle t(u)|\Psi(\bar{u})\rangle=\Lambda(u|\bar{u})|\Psi(\bar{u})\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{F(u)}{F(u_{i})}\frac{\mathcal{Y}(u_{i}|\bar{u})}{Q(u_{i},\{u,\bar{u}_{i}\})}|\Psi(\{u,\bar{u}_{i}\})\rangle,

and

(37)

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|t(u)=\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|\Lambda(u|\bar{v})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{F(u)}{F(v_{i})}\frac{\mathcal{Y}(v_{i}|\bar{v})}{Q(v_{i},\{u,\bar{v}_{i}\})}\langle\Psi(\{u,\bar{v}_{i}\})|\,.

Here

(38)

\displaystyle F(u)=u^{-1}\frac{q^{2}u^{2}-q^{-2}u^{-2}}{q-q^{-1}}\,,\quad Q(u,v)=U(u)-U(v)\,,\quad U(u)=\frac{qu^{2}+q^{-1}u^{-2}}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle\Lambda(u|\bar{u})=\phi(u)\frac{Q(q^{-1}u,\bar{u})}{Q(u,\bar{u})}+\phi(q^{-1}u^{-1})\frac{Q(qu,\bar{u})}{Q(u,\bar{u})}+\frac{H(u)}{Q(u,\bar{u})},

and

(40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}(u|\bar{u})={Q(u,\bar{u})}\Lambda(u|\bar{u}).

The functions
\phi(u)
and
H(u)
are given by

(41)

\displaystyle\phi(u)=-\kappa\tilde{\kappa}\tau\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{\xi}u+{\tilde{\xi}}^{-1}u^{-1})(\xi^{-1}u+\xi u^{-1})(\mu u+\mu^{-1}u^{-1})({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}u+{\tilde{\mu}}u^{-1})\frac{q^{2}u^{2}-q^{-2}u^{-2}}{qu^{2}-q^{-1}u^{-2}}V(u),

(42)

\displaystyle H(u)=\left((\kappa\tau)^{2}+(\tilde{\kappa}\tilde{\tau})^{2}+\kappa\tilde{\kappa}\tau\tilde{\tau}\left(\frac{\xi\tilde{\mu}}{\tilde{\xi}\mu}q^{N+1}+\frac{\tilde{\xi}\mu}{\xi\tilde{\mu}}q^{-N-1}\right)\right)(u^{2}-u^{-2})(q^{2}u^{2}-q^{-2}u^{-2})V(u)V(q^{-1}u^{-1}),

where

(43)

\displaystyle V(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}Q(q^{1/2}u,q^{-1/2}x_{i}).

In the formulas above,
\Lambda(u|\bar{u})
is the eigenvalue of
the transfer matrix and
\mathcal{Y}(u|\bar{u})
is called the Bethe function, forming the Baxter TQ equation.
We can see that the Bethe function is a linear symmetric function of Bethe parameter
U(u_{i})
. The structure of the eigenvalues is similar to the one of the closed XXX case but with this new variable
U(u)
, that is, the Baxter TQ equation can be written solely
in terms of
U
. It reflects the crossing invariance of the transfer matrix
t(u)=t(q^{-1}u^{-1})
. Therefore, many calculations below are similar to those in
[
12
]
. The differences appear in the asymptotic calculations which involve the model dependent functions
\phi(u)
and
H(u)
.

Homogeneous linear system for the scalar product.

Now we can follow
[
12
]
to fix the general form of the scalar product of on-shell and off-shell Bethe vectors.
First of all, we consider the enlarged set
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N+1}\}
and define the following important products:

\displaystyle F(\bar{u})=\prod_{u_{i}\in\bar{u}}F(u_{i})\,,\quad\Delta(\bar{u})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{i},u_{j}\in\bar{u}\\
i<j\end{subarray}}Q(u_{i},u_{j})\,,\quad\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{i},u_{j}\in\bar{u}\\
i>j\end{subarray}}Q(u_{i},u_{j})\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\partial U(u)=\partial_{u}U(u)\,,\quad\partial U(\bar{u})=\prod_{u_{i}\in\bar{u}}\partial U(u_{i})\,.

Then we introduce variables
X_{k}=\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|\Psi(\bar{u}_{k})\rangle
, where
\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|
is an on-shell vector. Let us create an
N+1
dimensional vector
X=(X_{1},...,X_{N+1})
. It follows from the off-shell equations (
36
,
37
) for the transfer matrices and from
\mathcal{Y}(v_{i}|\bar{v})=0
with
i=1,\dots,N
(since
\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|
is on-shell) that

(45)

\displaystyle LX=0,

where
L
is the
(N+1)\times(N+1)
matrix with entries

(46)

\displaystyle L_{kj}=\delta_{kj}\Lambda(u_{j}|\bar{v})-\frac{F(u_{k})}{F(u_{j})}\frac{\mathcal{Y}(u_{j}|\bar{u}_{k})}{Q(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}.

The homogeneous system (
45
) has non-trivial solution if
\det_{N+1}(L)=0
, and we now prove that this is indeed the case for (
46
). Recall that
\det_{N+1}(L)=0
implies that
\text{rank}(L)\leq N
.

In order to prove that
\det_{N+1}(L)=0
, let us introduce a nonsingular
(N+1)\times(N+1)
matrix
W

with entries

(47)

\displaystyle W_{ik}=\frac{Q(u_{k},\bar{w}_{i})}{F(u_{k})Q(u_{k},\bar{u}_{k})},

where
\bar{w}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{N+1}\}
are generic pairwise distinct parameters. The determinant of
W
is given by

(48)

\displaystyle\det_{N+1}(W)=\frac{1}{F(\bar{u})}\frac{\Delta(\bar{w})}{\Delta(\bar{u})},

The product
\Omega=WL
has the following entries:

(49)

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(u_{j})Q(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}\Big(\Lambda(u_{j}|\bar{v})Q(u_{j},\bar{w}_{i})-\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}\frac{Q(u_{k},\bar{w}_{i})}{Q(u_{k},\bar{u}_{k})}\mathcal{Y}(u_{j}|\bar{u}_{k})\Big).

To calculate the sum in (
49
), we use identities

(50)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}\frac{Q(u_{k},\bar{w}_{i})}{Q(u_{k},\bar{u}_{k})}Q(au_{j},\bar{u}_{k})=Q(au_{j},\bar{w}_{i})\,,\quad\quad\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}\frac{Q(u_{k},\bar{w}_{i})}{Q(u_{k},\bar{u}_{k})}=1\,,

where
a
is an arbitrary complex number. Setting
a=q
and
a=q^{-1}
in (
50
) and using (
40
), we obtain

(51)

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(u_{j})Q(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}\Big(\Lambda(u_{j}|\bar{v})Q(u_{j},\bar{w}_{i})-\mathcal{Y}(u_{j}|\bar{w}_{i})\Big).

Setting
\bar{w}_{N+1}=\bar{v}
we find that the row
\Omega_{N+1,j}
vanishes. Thus, we have
\det_{N+1}(\Omega)=0
. Therefore, it follows that
\det_{N+1}(W)\det_{N+1}(L)=0
and
\det_{N+1}(L)=0
.

We now find non-trivial solutions to the system (
45
). Note that the rows of
\Omega_{ij}
with
i\leq N
are given by

(52)

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(u_{j})Q(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}\Big(\mathcal{Y}(u_{j}|\bar{v})\frac{Q(u_{j},w)}{Q(u_{j},v_{i})}-\mathcal{Y}(u_{j}|\{\bar{v}_{i},w\})\Big),

with
w=w_{N+1}
. It is a simple exercise to show that
\Omega_{ij}
can be rewritten as

(53)

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}=\frac{Q(v_{i},w)}{F(u_{j})Q(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})Q(u_{j},v_{i})}\mathcal{Y}(u_{j}|\{\bar{v}_{i},u_{j}\})=\frac{Q(v_{i},w)}{\partial U(v_{i})}\frac{Q(u_{j},\bar{v})}{F(u_{j})Q(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}\partial_{v_{i}}\Lambda(u_{j}|\bar{v})\,.

Now define
\tilde{\Omega}=V\Omega
where
V
is a
(N+1)\times(N+1)
diagonal matrix with entries
V_{ii}=\frac{(1-\delta_{N+1,i})}{Q(v_{i},w)}
. We have,

(54)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{ij}=\frac{M_{ij}}{\partial U(v_{i})F(u_{j})Q(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})},\quad M_{ij}=Q(u_{j},\bar{v})\partial_{v_{i}}\Lambda(u_{j}|\bar{v}),

The system (
45
) is equivalent to
\tilde{\Omega}X=0
. Let us assume that the rank of
\tilde{\Omega}
is
N
and consider the reduced matrix
\tilde{\Omega}_{l}=\tilde{\Omega}_{(N+1),(l)}
which is obtained from
\tilde{\Omega}
by removing the row
N+1
and the column
l
. Then it follows from linear algebra
[
38
]
that the system is
solved in terms of cofactors

(55)

\displaystyle X_{l}=\tilde{G}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})(-1)^{N+1+l}\det_{N}(\tilde{\Omega}_{l})\,,

where
\tilde{G}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})
is an arbitrary function of
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots u_{N+1}\}
and
\bar{v}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{N}\}
. Defining similarly the reduced matrix
M_{l}=M_{(N+1),(l)}
for the matrix
M
in (
54
) with the removed row
N+1
and column
l
, we have

(56)

\displaystyle X_{l}

\displaystyle=

Conclusion.

In this paper, we computed
the scalar product of on-shell and off-shell modified Bethe vectors
associated with the XXZ spin chain with general integrable boundary conditions.
Similarly to the
U(1)
-invariant models, the compact formula is
given by a determinant involving the Jacobian of the transfer
matrix eigenvalue. Breaking the
U(1)
symmetry implies the expectation value
\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|N\rangle
, while the “dynamic” nature of the modified creation operators generates q-Pochhammer symbols involving the boundary parameters fields. The first point to be investigated from here is the computation of the expectation
value
\langle\Psi(\bar{v})|N\rangle
and check whether it can be written as a boundary case of the q-deformed modified Izergin determinant
[
11
,
34
,
18
]
. The next natural step is to compute
the action of local operators on the modified Bethe vector, and then derive building
blocks of correlation functions, trying to generalize the known results for the diagonal boundary case
[
21
,
22
]
. Certainly, the solution of these problems will
bring advances to the solution of the inverse
problem for models without
U(1)
symmetry, providing new tools
for the calculation of physical quantities.

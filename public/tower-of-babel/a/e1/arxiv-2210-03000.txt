On isometric immersions of almost k k -product manifolds
Vladimir Rovenski, Paweł Walczak

Abstract
A Riemannian manifold endowed with k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 complementary pairwise orthogonal distributions is called a Riemannian almost k k -product manifold. In the article, we study the following problem: find a relationship between intrinsic and extrinsic invariants of a Riemannian almost k k -product manifold isometrically immersed in another Riemannian manifold. For such immersions, we establish an inequality that includes the mixed scalar curvature and the square of the mean curvature. Although Riemannian curvature tensor belongs to intrinsic geometry, a special part called the mixed curvature is also related to the extrinsic geometry of a Riemannian almost k k -product manifold. Our inequality also contains mixed scalar curvature type invariants related to B.-Y Chen’s δ \delta -invariants. Applications are given for isometric immersions of multiply twisted and warped products (we improve some known inequalities by replacing the sectional curvature with our invariant) and to problems of non-immersion and non-existence of compact leaves of foliated submanifolds. Keywords : almost product manifold, distribution, mixed scalar curvature, isometric immersion, mean curvature vector, multiply twisted product Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C12; 53C15; 53C42

Introduction

A fundamental problem (based on the famous embedding theorem of J. F. Nash,
[
8
]
) is to find a simple connection between the intrinsic
and extrinsic invariants of a submanifold.
The problem is particularly interesting
if we assume additional structures, for example, web of foliations, almost
k
-product structure or multiply warped product structure, on a Riemannian submanifold.

Multiply warped products have been studied from extrinsic geometry point of view
in a series of articles, see
[
4
,
5
]
for an overview.
B.Y. Chen and F. Dillen
[
6
]
proved an optimal inequality
(which involves the Laplacian of the warping function and the squared mean curvature)
for warped product manifolds isometrically immersed in arbitrary Riemannian manifolds,
in particular, in Riemannian spaces of constant sectional curvature.
Corresponding inequalities for isometrically immersed doubly warped product manifolds have been obtained by A. Olteanu in
[
10
]
.
A sufficient condition for an isometric immersion of a warped product in a Euclidean space to split into a product immersion
was given by S. Nölker
[
9
]
.

In this article, we consider a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
endowed with an
almost
k
-product structure

(more general than a multiply warped product), i.e.,

k\geq 2
complementary pairwise orthogonal distributions
{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}
(subbundles of the tangent bundle
TM
),
e.g.,
[
12
]
.
Such
(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
is called a Riemannian almost
k
-product manifold.
Its important example is a multiply twisted or warped product manifold.
One can ask when
(M,g,{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
splits
into the product of
k
manifolds.
The best known answer to this question is the de Rham’s Decomposition theorem, for example,
[
7
]
:
if each

{\cal D}_{i}
is parallel (with respect to the Levi-Civita connection), then
M

splits
, i.e.,
each point
x\in M
has a neighborhood
U
,
which
is isometric to the direct product of the maximal integral manifolds for the distributions
obtained by parallel translation of
\,{\cal D}_{i}(x)
over
U
.
If
M
is complete and simply connected, the statement is true with
U=M
.

We study the following problem (more general than
[
3
, Problem 2]
):

find a relationship between intrinsic and extrinsic curvature invariants of

(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
isometrically immersed in another Riemannian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
.
For such immersions, we prove an inequality that includes the mixed scalar curvature of

(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
and the square of the mean curvature of the immersion,
and give applications for multiply twisted products and to problems non-immersion and absence of compact leaves of
foliated submanifolds.
Although Riemannian curvature tensor belongs to intrinsic geometry, a special part called the
mixed curvature
is also related to the extrinsic geometry of
(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
,
see
[
14
]
.
Our inequality also contains mixed scalar curvature type invariants related to B.-Y Chen’s
\delta
-invariants for
k\geq 2
, for example,
[
4
,
5
]
.

The results of this article can be applied also to so-called
adapted
isometric immersions

f:(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})\to(\bar{M},\bar{g};\bar{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\cal D}_{k})
, i.e.,

f_{*}({\cal D}_{i})\subset\bar{\cal D}_{i\,|f(M)}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
Obviously, there are many Riemannian almost
k
-product manifolds (already with integrable distributions, e.g., a
k
-dimensional torus with irrational winding), which cannot be isometrically immersed adapted way
into some Euclidean space endowed with the standard structure of the product of
k
Euclidean spaces.
Therefore, we supplement the above problem with the following:
characterize almost
k
-product manifolds
(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
admitting adapted isometric immersions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}}
with a Euclidean metric for some
N

and
N_{i}
satisfying
N=\sum_{i}N_{i}
.

The Riemannian almost
k
-product structure on an open simply connected manifold
M
can be obtained by a special
immersion in
(\bar{M},\bar{g};\bar{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\cal D}_{k})
.
Let
f_{*}(TM)
intersects transversally with every distribution
\bar{\cal D}_{i}
restricted to
f(M)
. Then

f:M\to\bar{M}
induces pairwise orthogonal distributions
{\cal D}_{i}
on
M
with induced metric
g
,
i.e.,
f_{*}({\cal D}_{i})\subset\bar{\cal D}_{i\,|f(M)}
and
TM=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\,k}{\cal D}_{i}
.
There are topological obstacles to the existence of an almost
k
-product structure on a smooth closed manifold
M
.
For example, if
n_{i}=1
for some
i
, then the Euler characteristic
\chi(M)
is zero;
and if
n_{i}=1
for all
i
and
M
is an odd-dimensional sphere (thus,
\chi(M)=0
), then maximal
k
is the (known in topology) number of linear independent vector fields on an
n
-dimensional sphere.
We do not consider here (no less complicated) topological obstacles for a closed manifold
M^{n}
with
n=\sum_{i}n_{i}
to admit a special immersion in
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}}
for some
N=\sum_{i}N_{i}
such that
f_{*}(TM)
has an
n_{i}
-dimensional transversal intersection with every distribution
\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The article is organized as follows.
Section
2
contains necessary definitions (e.g., this of the mixed scalar curvature) for a Riemannian almost
k
-product structure.
Section
3
contains the main theorem providing an
inequality (which involves the mixed scalar curvature and the squared mean curvature)
for isometric immersions of such manifolds,
and several corollaries with applications to problems of non-immersing and non-existence of compact leaves of foliated submanifolds.
Section
4
contains applications to isometric immersions of multiply twisted and warped products.

2
Preliminaries

Let an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
and the curvature tensor
R

be endowed with
k\geq 2
pairwise orthogonal
n_{i}
-dimensional distributions
{\cal D}_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq k)

(subbundles of the tangent bundle
TM
) of ranks
n_{i}
such that
\sum_{\,i=1}^{k}n_{i}=n
.
Let
{\cal D}^{\bot}_{i}
be the orthogonal complement to
{\cal D}_{i}
in
TM
and
n^{\bot}_{i}=n-n_{i}
.

Let
P^{\bot}_{i}:TM\to{\cal D}^{\bot}_{i}
be the orthoprojector. The 2nd fundamental form
{h}_{i}:{\cal D}_{i}\times{\cal D}_{i}\to{\cal D}^{\bot}_{i}
and
the integrability tensor
{T}_{i}:{\cal D}_{i}\times{\cal D}_{i}\to{\cal D}^{\bot}_{i}
of
{\cal D}_{i}
(and similarly,
h^{\bot}_{i}
and
T^{\bot}_{i}
) are defined by

2\,h_{i}(X,Y)=P^{\bot}_{i}(\nabla_{X}Y+\nabla_{Y}X),\quad 2\,T_{i}(X,Y)=P^{\bot}_{i}(\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X)=P^{\bot}_{i}([X,Y]).

(1)

Then
H_{i}=\operatorname{trace}_{g}h_{i}
is called the mean curvature vector field of the distribution
{\cal D}_{i}
.
A distribution
{\cal D}_{i}
is integrable (or involutive) if
T_{i}=0
,
and
{\cal D}_{i}
is
totally umbilical
,
minimal
, or
totally geodesic
,
if
{h}_{i}=({H}_{i}/n_{i})\,g,\ {H}_{i}=0
, or
{h}_{i}=0
, respectively.

A plane that nontrivially intersects two of the available
k
distributions on
(M,g)

and its sectional curvature are called mixed.
Any unit vectors
X\in{\cal D}_{i}(x)
and
Y\in{\cal D}_{j}(x)
for
x\in M
and
i\neq j
define a mixed plane, and

K(X,Y)=g(R(X,Y)\,Y,\,X)
is its
mixed sectional curvature
.

The
mixed scalar curvature
of
(M,g)
equipped with two complementary orthogonal distributions
({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2})
is an averaged mixed sectional curvature (of planes that intersect
{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}={\cal D}^{\bot}_{1}
):

{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i\leq n_{1},\,j>n_{1}}K(e_{i},{e}_{j}).

Here,

\{e_{i}\}

is an adapted local orthonormal frame on
TM={\cal D}_{1}\oplus{\cal D}_{2}
, i.e.,

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}}\}\subset{{\cal D}_{1}}
.
If
{\cal D}_{1}
or
{{\cal D}_{2}}
is spanned by a unit vector
N
then
{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2})=\operatorname{Ric}_{\,N,N}
.
This concept can be extended for
k>2
distributions as follows, see
[
12
]
.

Definition 1
.

Let
x\in M
and
\{e_{i}\}
on
(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})

be an adapted orthonormal frame on
T_{x}M
, i.e.,

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}}\}\subset{{\cal D}_{1}(x)},\ \ldots\ \{e_{n_{k-1}+1},\ldots,e_{n_{k}}\}\subset{{\cal D}_{k}(x)}
.
The
mixed scalar curvature
of
(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})

is an averaged mixed sectional curvature, the function on
M
defined by

{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{i},{\cal D}_{j}).

Here,
{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{i},{\cal D}_{j})
is the
mixed scalar curvature
of the pair
({\cal D}_{i},{\cal D}_{j})

given at
x\in M
by

{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{i}(x),{\cal D}_{j}(x))=\sum\nolimits_{\,n_{i-1}<a\,\leq n_{i},\ n_{j-1}<b\leq n_{j}}K(e_{a},{e}_{b}),\quad i\neq j,

and it does not depend on the choice of frames.

By Definition
1
, we get

2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{i},{\cal D}^{\bot}_{i}),

(2)

where the coefficient 2 is due to the fact that each
{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{i},{\cal D}_{j})
participates twice in the sum.
For example, if
k=3
, then

{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}^{\bot}_{1})={\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2})+{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{3})
,
etc., hence,

{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}^{\bot}_{1}){+}{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{2},{\cal D}^{\bot}_{2}){+}{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{3},{\cal D}^{\bot}_{3})=2\big({\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}){+}{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{3}){+}{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{2},{\cal D}_{3})\big).

The scalar curvature
\tau=\operatorname{trace}_{g}\operatorname{Ric}
(the trace of the Ricci tensor) of
(M,g)
can be represented as

\tau=2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})+\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}\tau(\,{\cal D}_{i}),

(3)

where
\tau(\,{\cal D}_{i})
are scalar curvatures (functions on
M
) of distributions
{\cal D}_{i}
.
Thus, if all distributions
{\cal D}_{i}
are one-dimensional then
2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})=\tau
.

The divergence of a vector field
X
on
(M,g)
is defined using a local orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
by

\operatorname{div}X=\operatorname{trace}(Y\to\nabla_{Y}X)=\sum\nolimits_{\,i}g(\nabla_{e_{i}}X,\,e_{i}).

The following formula
[
15
]
has many applications:

\operatorname{div}(H_{1}+H^{\bot}_{1})={\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}^{\bot}_{1})+\|h_{1}\|^{2}+\|h^{\bot}_{1}\|^{2}-\|H_{1}\|^{2}-\|H^{\bot}_{1}\|^{2}-\|T_{1}\|^{2}-\|T^{\bot}_{1}\|^{2};

(4)

from (
2
) and (
4
), for
(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
we obtain, see
[
12
,
14
]
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}\big(H_{i}+H_{i}^{\bot}\big)=2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}\big(\|h_{i}\|^{2}-\|H_{i}\|^{2}-\|T_{i}\|^{2}+\|h_{i}^{\bot}\|^{2}-\|H_{i}^{\bot}\|^{2}-\|T_{i}^{\bot}\|^{2}\big).

(5)

If all distributions
{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}
are totally umbilical, then, see
[
14
]
,

\|h_{i}\|^{2}-\|H_{i}\|^{2}=-\frac{n_{i}-1}{n_{i}}\,\|H_{i}\|^{2},\quad\|h^{\bot}_{i}\|^{2}-\|H^{\bot}_{i}\|^{2}=-\frac{n^{\bot}_{i}-1}{n^{\bot}_{i}}\,\|H^{\bot}_{i}\|^{2};

from this and (
4
) we get

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{div}\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}\big(H_{i}+H_{i}^{\bot}\big)=2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})

\displaystyle-\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{n_{i}-1}{n_{i}}\,\|H_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{n^{\bot}_{i}-1}{n^{\bot}_{i}}\,\|H^{\bot}_{i}\|^{2}+\|T_{i}\|^{2}+\|T^{\bot}_{i}\|^{2}\Big).

(6)

3
Main results

Let
\bar{h}:TM\times TM\to TM^{\bot}
be the second fundamental form of
an isometric immersion
f:(M^{n},g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})\to(\bar{M}^{m},\bar{g})
with

\dim{\cal D}_{i}=n_{i}
satisfying
\sum\,_{i=1}^{k}\,n_{i}=n<m
.
We will identify
M
with its image
f(M)
and
put a top “bar” for the objects related to
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
.
The second fundamental forms
\bar{h}_{i}:{\cal D}_{i}\times{\cal D}_{i}\to TM^{\bot}

are defined similarly to (
1
), as restrictions of
\bar{h}
on
{\cal D}_{i}
.
The mean curvature vector
\bar{H}=\operatorname{trace}_{\,\bar{g}}\,\bar{h}

of
f
decomposes as
\bar{H}=\sum_{\,i}\bar{H}_{i}
, where
\bar{H}_{i}=\operatorname{trace}_{\,\bar{g}}\,\bar{h}_{i}

is the mean curvature vector of
{\cal D}_{i}
.
An isometric immersion
f
is called
mixed totally geodesic
if

\bar{h}(X,Y)=0\quad{\rm for\ all}\ \ X\in{\cal D}_{i},\ Y\in{\cal D}_{j},\ \ i\neq j.

The Gauss equation for our immersion
f
(a submanifold
M\subset\bar{M}
) has the following form:

g(\bar{R}(Y,Z)U,V)=g(R(Y,Z)U,V)+g(\bar{h}(Y,U),\bar{h}(Z,V))-g(\bar{h}(Z,U),\bar{h}(Y,V)),

(7)

where

U,V,Y,Z\in TM
and

\bar{R}
and
R
are the curvature tensors of
(\bar{M}^{m},\bar{g})
and
(M,g)
, respectively.

Definition 2
.

Let
V_{i}\ (i=1,\ldots,k)
be pairwise orthogonal subspaces of
T_{x}\bar{M}
at a point
x\in\bar{M}

with
\dim V_{i}=n_{i}
.
Let
\{e_{i}\}
be an orthonormal frame on
T_{x}\bar{M}
such that

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}}\}\subset V_{1}
,
…,

\{e_{n_{k-1}+1},\ldots,e_{n_{k}}\}\subset V_{k}
.
Define

\bar{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}\sum\nolimits_{\,n_{i-1}<a\,\leq n_{i},\ n_{j-1}<b\leq n_{j}}\bar{K}(e_{a},{e}_{b})

(see Definition
1
),
and observe that these quantities do not depend on the choice of frames.
Set

\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})=\sup\{\bar{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}):\dim V_{i}=n_{i}\}.

If the sectional curvature of
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
satisfies
\bar{K}\leq C
and
\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}n_{i}=n
, then

\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots n_{k})\leq C\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}n_{i}\,n_{j}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,C\,k\,n^{2}.

Remark 1
.

The
\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}
-invariants are related with B.-Y Chen’s
\delta
-invariants, e.g.,
[
5
]
.
Indeed,
if
k\geq 2
and the sectional curvature
\bar{K}\geq 0
, then
\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\leq\bar{\delta}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, where for
x\in\bar{M}

2\bar{\delta}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})(x)=\bar{\tau}(x)-\inf\,\{\bar{\tau}({\cal D}_{1})(x)+\ldots+\bar{\tau}({\cal D}_{k})(x)\}.

Also, for
\sum\,_{i=1}^{k}\,n_{i}=\dim\bar{M}
and
k\geq 2
, from (
3
) we get

\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})=\bar{\delta}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
.
Note that B.-Y.Chen defines the scalar curvature as half of ”trace Ricci”, i.e.,
\tau=\sum_{\,i<j}K(e_{i},e_{j})
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:(M,g;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})\to(\bar{M},\bar{g})
be an isometric immersion
of an almost
k
-product manifold in a Riemannian manifold.
Then

{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})\leq\frac{k-1}{2\,k}\,\|\bar{H}\|^{2}+\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}).

(8)

The equality in (
8
) holds if and only if
f
is mixed totally geodesic,

\bar{H}_{1}=\ldots=\bar{H}_{k}

and
\bar{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1}(x)
,
\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}(x))=\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
at each point
x\in M
.

Proof.

Tracing the Gauss equation (
7
) for the immersion
f
yields the equality

\bar{\tau}_{\,|M}-\tau=\|\bar{h}\|^{2}-\|\bar{H}\|^{2},

(9)

where
\bar{\tau}_{\,|M}
and
\tau
are the scalar curvatures of
M
for the curvature tensors
\bar{R}
and
R
, respectively.
Tracing the Gauss equation (
7
) on
M
along
{\cal D}_{i}
, we get

\bar{\tau}(\,{\cal D}_{i})-\tau(\,{\cal D}_{i})=\|\bar{h}_{i}\|^{2}-\|\bar{H}_{i}\|^{2},

(10)

where
\bar{\tau}(\,{\cal D}_{i})
and
\tau(\,{\cal D}_{i})
are the scalar curvatures of
{\cal D}_{i}

for the curvature tensors
\bar{R}
and
R
.

Suppose that
\bar{H}\neq 0
on an open set
U\subset M
and
extend over
U
an adapted local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
(M,g)
by
e_{n+1}
parallel to
\bar{H}
.
Using the algebraic inequality
a_{1}^{2}+\ldots+a_{k}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{k}\,(a_{1}+\ldots+a_{k})^{2}

for
a_{i}=g(\bar{H}_{i},e_{n+1})
, we find

\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\|\bar{H}_{i}\|^{2}\geq\sum\nolimits_{\,i}g(\bar{H}_{i},e_{n+1})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{k}\,\|\bar{H}\|^{2},

(11)

and the equality holds if and only if
\bar{H}_{1}=\ldots=\bar{H}_{k}
.
The above inequality is trivially satisfied for
\bar{H}=0
, hence it is valid on
M
.
Note also that

\|\bar{h}\|^{2}=\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\|\bar{h}_{i}\|^{2}+\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}\|\bar{h}^{\rm mix}_{ij}\|^{2}\geq\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\|\bar{h}_{i}\|^{2},

(12)

where

\|\bar{h}^{\rm mix}_{ij}\|^{2}=\sum_{\,e_{a}\in{\cal D}_{i},\,e_{b}\in{\cal D}_{j}}\|\bar{h}(e_{a},e_{b})\|^{2}
,
and
\bar{h}^{\rm mix}_{ij}=0\ (\forall\,i<j)
if and only if
f
is mixed totally geodesic.
By (
9
), (
10
), (
11
), (
12
) and the equalities

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}_{\,|M}=2\,\bar{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{\tau}(\,{\cal D}_{i}),

\displaystyle\tau=2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\tau(\,{\cal D}_{i}),

see (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})=2\,\bar{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}(\bar{\tau}(\,{\cal D}_{i})-\tau(\,{\cal D}_{i}))+\|\bar{H}\|^{2}-\|\bar{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})-(\|\bar{h}\|^{2}-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\|\bar{h}_{i}\|^{2})+(\|\bar{H}\|^{2}-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\|\bar{H}_{i}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})+\frac{k-1}{k}\,\|\bar{H}\|^{2},

(and the equality holds in the second line if and only if

\bar{\rm S}_{\,\rm mix}({\cal D}_{1}(x),\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}(x))=\bar{\delta}_{\rm mix}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
at each point
x\in M
)
that proves (
8
).
∎

Example 1
.

Consider distributions

on a domain
M
on a unit sphere

in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

Using coordinate charts, we can take integrable distributions

,
and

Extend the distributions

to distributions

on

.
Thus

.

Definition 3

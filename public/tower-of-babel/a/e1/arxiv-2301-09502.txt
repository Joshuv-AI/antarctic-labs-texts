The Identity Problem in the special affine group of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2}
Ruiwen Dong

Abstract
We consider semigroup algorithmic problems in the Special Affine group 𝖲𝖠 ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) = ℤ 2 ⋊ 𝖲𝖫 ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) \mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes\mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) , which is the group of affine transformations of the lattice ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} that preserve orientation. Our paper focuses on two decision problems introduced by Choffrut and Karhumäki (2005): the Identity Problem (does a semigroup contain a neutral element?) and the Group Problem (is a semigroup a group?) for finitely generated sub-semigroups of 𝖲𝖠 ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) \mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z}) . We show that both problems are decidable and NP -complete. Since 𝖲𝖫 ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) ≤ 𝖲𝖠 ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) ≤ 𝖲𝖫 ⁡ ( 3 , ℤ ) \mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\leq\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})\leq\mathsf{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z}) , our result extends that of Bell, Hirvensalo and Potapov (SODA 2017) on the NP -completeness of both problems in 𝖲𝖫 ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) \mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) , and contributes a first step towards the open problems in 𝖲𝖫 ⁡ ( 3 , ℤ ) \mathsf{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z}) .

The Special Affine group
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

In this paper we focus on an intermediate group between
\mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathsf{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
: the
Special Affine group

\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
This affine analogue of
\mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that preserve orientation.
Written as matrices, elements of
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
are

integer matrices of the following form.

\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&\boldsymbol{a}\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}\;\middle|\;A\in\mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}),\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right\}.

To be precise, elements of
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

We denote by
(A,\boldsymbol{a})
the element

\begin{pmatrix}A&\boldsymbol{a}\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}
, then the neutral element of
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
is
(I,\boldsymbol{0})
; multiplication in
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
is given by

(A,\boldsymbol{a})\cdot(B,\boldsymbol{b})=(AB,A\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{a}).

Naturally,
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
has a subgroup
\{(A,\boldsymbol{0})\mid A\in\mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\}

While both problems as well as many of their variations have been shown to be decidable in dimension one and undecidable for dimension three

Since the study of these reachability problems in dimension two necessitates the study of sub-semigroups of

Currently, among the four algorithmic problems introduced in the beginning, the only known result in
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

This can be deduced from the recent work of Delgado

In this paper, we step further by considering sub-semigroups of
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

We show decidability and

\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

This extends the
NP

\mathsf{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

The

\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

In order to prove decidability and the

Given a finitely generated sub-semigroup
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle

\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

If

Our proof combines two viewpoints: a group theoretic viewpoint of

as a virtually free group, and a geometric viewpoint of
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})

Beyond the Identity Problem and the Group Problem, we will discuss some obstacles to generalizing our results to the Semigroup Membership problem in

Our results actually show that Semigroup Membership in

Fix a set

The following lemma shows that, if the semigroup

Lemma 7

Let
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\{(A_{1},\boldsymbol{a}_{1}),\ldots,(A_{K},\boldsymbol{a}_{K})\}

If the semigroup

Proof.

If

Thus
\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{K}\rangle
is a group.
∎

Suppose now that

Theorem 11

The Group Problem and the Identity Problem in

Proof.

VI
Extensions and obstacles to Semigroup Membership

In previous sections we showed decidability and NP-completeness of the Identity Problem and the Group Problem in
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
In this section we discuss possible extensions of our result and obstacles to solving the Semigroup Membership problem in
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\{(A_{1},\boldsymbol{a}_{1}),\ldots,(A_{K},\boldsymbol{a}_{K})\}
be a set of elements of
\mathsf{SA}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
This first obvious obstacle for deciding Semigroup Membership for
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
is that we can no longer suppose
G\coloneqq\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{K}\rangle
to be a group, which we could do for the Identity Problem and the Group Problem.
However, if we restrict the target element to be of the form
(I,\boldsymbol{a})
, that is, if we want to deciding whether
(I,\boldsymbol{a})\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
, then we can still suppose
G
to be a group.

Indeed, if
G
is not a group, then at least one of the
A_{i}
is not invertible in
G
. Therefore, a word over
\mathcal{G}
representing
(I,\boldsymbol{a})
cannot contain the letter
(A_{i},\boldsymbol{a}_{i})
.
We can thus delete
(A_{i},\boldsymbol{a}_{i})
from the alphabet
\mathcal{G}
without changing whether
(I,\boldsymbol{a})\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
.
One can repeat this process until

becomes a group.

Under the additional assumption that

Recall Theorem
8

contains a non-abelian free subgroup, then

If

Case (ii), (iii) and (v) are easy since

becomes a group.
In case (i), deciding whether

On the commutant of B ⁡ ( H ) B(H) in its ultrapower
Emmanuel Chetcuti, Beatriz Zamora-Aviles

Abstract
. Let B ⁡ ( H ) B(H) be the algebra of bounded linear operators on a separable infinite-dimesional Hilbert space H H . We study the commutant of B ⁡ ( H ) B(H) in its ultrapower. We characterize the class of non-principal ultrafilters for which this commutant is non-trivial. Additionally, we extend the class of ultrafilters for which the commutant is trivial.

1.
Introduction

Let
\alpha
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathds{N}
and let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. We define,

\displaystyle A_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(x_{n})\in\prod_{n}A:\sup_{n}\|x_{n}\|<\infty\right\}

and

\displaystyle N_{\alpha}:=\biggl\{(x_{n})\in A_{\infty}:\lim_{\alpha}\|x_{n}\|=0\biggr\}.

The quotient of
A_{\infty}
by

is a C*-algebra and it is called the
ultrapower of
A
w.r.t.
\alpha
. We denote this C*- algebra by
C_{\alpha}(A)
. Note that if

is self-adjoint, then

F_{\alpha}(K):=\{\mathbf{b}\in C_{\alpha}(A):\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0}\ \forall\mathbf{a}\in K\}

is a C*-subalgebra of
C_{\alpha}(A)
and it is called the commutant of
K
in
C_{\alpha}(A)
. It is easily seen that the algebra
A
embeds isometrically in
C_{\alpha}(A)
which implies that
A
and
F_{\alpha}(A)
can be identified as C*-subalgebras of
C_{\alpha}(A)
. The invariant
F_{\alpha}(A)
is important for the classification of separable nuclear unital purely infinite simple
C^{*}
-algebras, some of these applications can be found in
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
16
]
. If
A
is separable and the Continuum Hypothesis is assumed then the isomorphism class of

is independent of the choice of
\alpha
, see
[
9
]
. In the absence of the Continuum Hypothesis (CH),
F_{\alpha}(A)
depends on the choice of
\alpha
for every infinite-dimensional separable C*-algebra

, see
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

Let
B(H)
be the algebra of bounded operators on a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space and let
K(H)
be the ideal of compact operators on
B(H)
. For the Calkin algebra
C(H)=B(H)/K(H)
(which is non-separable) Kirchberg proved in
[
11

that

for any ultrafilter
\alpha
. This implies that
F_{\alpha}(B(H))\subset\mathds{C}+C_{\alpha}(K(H))
. Kirchberg asked whether
F_{\alpha}(B(H))=\mathds{C}
for any ultrafilter
\alpha
.

It was surprising when I. Farah, C. Philips and J. Steprans in
[
8
]
proved that under the Continuum Hypothesis,

depends on the choice of the ultrafilter
\alpha
. More specifically, they proved that if
\alpha
is a selective ultrafilter then
F_{\alpha}(B(H))=\mathds{C}
. They also constructed an ultrafilter in
ZFC
for which
F_{\alpha}(B(H))\neq\mathds{C}
that they called
flat
. J. Steprans also proved that
F_{\alpha}(B(H))=\mathds{C}
for any P-point ultrafilter. Recall that every selective ultrafilter is a P-point and neither the existence of selective nor P-point ultrafilters are provable from the axioms of ZFC; Shelah showed that it is consistent that there are no P-point ultrafilters, see
[
21
]
. However, they exist assuming the Continuum Hypothesis,
[
15
]
and
[
18
]
.

The authors of
[
8
]
posed the following two questions:

1.

Is there an ultrafilter
\alpha
on
\mathds{N}
in ZFC such that
F_{\alpha}(B(H))=\mathds{C}
?

2.

If an ultrafilter
\alpha
satisfies
F_{\alpha}(B(H))\neq\mathds{C}
, does if follow that
\alpha
is flat?

In this paper we give a positive answer to the second question. In addition, we make other advancements with regard to the first question by introducing the class of
quasi P-point ultrafilters
and show that for such ultrafilters the relative commutant of

is trivial. In our analysis we also show that the relative commutant of
B(H)
is trivial for ultrafilters satisfying the 3f-property. The following diagram summarizes these results.

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}&&&&\text{Quasi P-point}&&\\
&&&\nearrow&&\searrow&\\
\text{Selective}&\rightarrow&\text{P-point}&&&&\text{Non-flat}\leftrightarrow F_{\alpha}(B(H))=\mathds{C}\\
&&&\searrow&&\nearrow&\\
&&&&\text{3f-Property}&&\end{array}

It is known that the existence of ultrafilters with the 3f-Property (and therefore of selective ultrafilters and P-points) cannot be proved in ZFC.

In order to prove
F_{\alpha}(B(H))=\mathds{C}
for every selective ultrafilter
\alpha
, the authors in
[
8
]
used a result by Sherman
[
20
]
about central sequences in

. They also used a non-trivial characterization of selective ultrafilters by Mathias in
[
15
]
. It is worthwhile to mention that our methods are different in that they are totally elementary.

This paper is organized as follows: In section

3.1.
Selective ultrafilters and P-points

We recall that for an ultrafilter

(ii)

(iii)

For every

(iv)

(iii)

(i)

Lemma 2
.

For

3.2.
Ultrafilters on a countable metric space.

Let

.

\alpha

.

(i)

Definition 1
.

An ultrafilter
\alpha\in\beta\omega\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\omega
is called a
quasi P-point
if for every countable and decreasing uniformity base
(E_{k})
on
\mathds{N}

(i)

\exists X\in\alpha\quad\exists k\in\mathds{N}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad X^{2}\cap E_{k}\mathbin{\fgebackslash}D=\emptyset
, where
D
equals the diagonal of

,

(ii)

\forall k\in\mathds{N}\quad\exists X\in\alpha\quad\text{s.t.}\quad X^{2}\subset E_{k}
,

∎

4.
Ultrapowers of a metric space - some considerations

Let
(M,\rho)
be a metric space and let
M
denote the set of all bounded sequences in
M
. Denote the members of
M
by
\mathbf{x}=(x_{n})
,
\mathbf{y}=(y_{n})
and so on. The following is easily seen to be an equivalence relation on
M

\Delta:=\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ :\ \{n\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x_{n},y_{n})<\varepsilon\}\in F\ \forall\varepsilon>0\},

and associated to the ultrafilter
\alpha\in\beta\omega\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\omega
we further have the following equivalence relations:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\alpha}_{0}:=

\displaystyle\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ :\ \{n\in\mathds{N}:x_{n}=y_{n}\}\in\alpha\},

\displaystyle\Delta^{\alpha}_{1}:=

\displaystyle\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ :\ \exists A\in\alpha\ \text{s.t.}\ \{n\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x_{n},y_{n})<\varepsilon\}\in F(A)\quad\forall\varepsilon>0\},

\displaystyle\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}:=

\displaystyle\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ :\ \{n\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x_{n},y_{n})<\varepsilon\}\in\alpha\ \forall\varepsilon>0\}.

The functions

\rho_{\infty}:M\times M\to[0,1]:\bigl((x_{n}),(y_{n})\bigr)\mapsto\sup_{n\in\mathds{N}}\rho(x_{n},y_{n}),

and

\rho_{\alpha}:M\times M\to[0,1]:\bigl((x_{n}),(y_{n})\bigr)\mapsto\lim_{\alpha}\rho(x_{n},y_{n}),

define, respectively, a metric and a pseudo-metric on
M
.

The following inclusions can easily be verified.

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&{\Delta}&&\\
&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\supset$}&&\\
{\Delta^{\alpha}_{0}}&{\subset}&\Delta^{\alpha}_{1}&\subset&\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}\end{array}

In the complete lattice of all the equivalence relations on
M
(ordered by set-theoretic inclusion) one has the equality
\Delta^{\alpha}_{1}=\Delta\vee\Delta^{\alpha}_{0}
.

Proposition 17
.

The closure of
\Delta^{\alpha}_{0}
is equal to
\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}
w.r.t. both metrics
\rho_{\infty}\times\rho_{\infty}
and
\rho_{\alpha}\times\rho_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\Delta_{0}^{\alpha}
is dense in
\Delta_{2}^{\alpha}
w.r.t.
\rho_{\infty}\times\rho_{\infty}
(and therefore w.r.t.
\rho_{\alpha}\times\rho_{\alpha}
). We show that
\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}
is closed w.r.t.
\rho_{\alpha}\times\rho_{\alpha}
. (This will imply that
\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}
is closed w.r.t.
\rho_{\infty}\times\rho_{\infty}
.) Suppose that
(\mathbf{x}^{n})
,
(\mathbf{y}^{n})
are sequences in
M
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{x}^{n}=\mathbf{x}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{y}^{n}=\mathbf{y}
(w.r.t.
\rho_{\alpha}
) and suppose that
(\mathbf{x}^{n},\mathbf{y}^{n})\in\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}
for every
n\in\mathds{N}
. Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. We show that
\{i\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x_{i},y_{i})<\varepsilon\}\in\alpha
. Let
\delta:=\varepsilon/3
. The assumption
(\mathbf{x}^{n},\mathbf{y}^{n})\in\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}
implies that
\{i\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x^{n}_{i},y^{n}_{i})<\delta\}\in\alpha
. We also have that

J:=\{n\in\mathds{N}:\rho_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}^{n},\mathbf{x})\vee\rho_{\alpha}(\mathbf{y}^{n},\mathbf{y})<\delta\}\in F,

and so, for every
n\in J
we have

\{i\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x^{n}_{i},x_{i})\vee\rho(x^{n}_{i},y^{n}_{i})\vee\rho(y^{n}_{i},y_{i})<\delta\}\in\alpha.

Hence,
\{i\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x_{i},y_{i})<\varepsilon\}\in\alpha
.
∎

Proposition 18
.

If
\alpha
is a P-point, then
\Delta^{\alpha}_{1}=\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\Delta^{\alpha}_{2}
. Let
\varepsilon_{i}\downarrow 0
in
\mathds{R}
and let
A_{i}:=\{j\in\mathds{N}:\rho(x_{j},y_{j})<\varepsilon_{i}\}
. Then
A_{i}\in\alpha
and
A_{i}\downarrow
. If
\bigcap_{i\in\mathds{N}}A_{i}\in\alpha
, then
\{i\in\mathds{N}:x_{i}=y_{i}\}\in\alpha
and
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\Delta^{\alpha}_{0}
. So, suppose that
\bigcap_{i\in\mathds{N}}A_{i}\notin\alpha
and let
B_{j}:=A_{j}\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\bigcap_{i\in\mathds{N}}A_{i}
. Then
B_{j}\downarrow\emptyset
in
\alpha
and – being a P-point –
\alpha
contains
X
such that
X\cap(B_{j}\mathbin{\fgebackslash}B_{j+1})
is finite, for every
j\in\mathds{N}
. This implies that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in X\\
j\to\infty\end{subarray}}\rho(x_{j},y_{j})=0,

i.e.
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\Delta^{\alpha}_{1}
.
∎

Theorem 19
.

Let
X
be a separable topological space, and suppose that
(f_{n})
is an equicontinuous sequence of functions from
X
into the metric space
Y
, converging pointwise to the function
f
along the ultrafilter
\alpha\in\beta\omega\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\omega
.
If
\alpha
is a P-point, then there exists
N\in\alpha
such that

(2)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in N\end{subarray}}f_{n}(x)=f(x)\quad\forall x\in X.

Proof.

If
U
is an equicontinuous set of functions mapping the separable space
X
into
Y
, then, the restriction to
U
of the product topology on
Y^{X}
is metrizable. So the assertion follows by Proposition
18
.
∎

When the ultrafilter is selective, one can use deep Mathias’s Theorem to prove that the same conclusion can be drawn when one relaxes equicontinuity to measurability. The proof of the next theorem follows the same arguments of
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
. We prefer to present it in this more general setup because we believe that it could be interesting in its own right.

Theorem 20
.

[
8
, Theorem 1.5]

Let
X
be a Polish space, and suppose that
(f_{n})
is a sequence of Borel functions from
X
into a metric space
Y
, converging pointwise to the Borel function
f
along the ultrafilter
\alpha
. If
\alpha\in\beta\omega\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\omega
is selective, then there exists
N\in\alpha
such that

(3)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in N\end{subarray}}f_{n}(x)=f(x)\qquad\forall x\in X.

Proof.

Let
\rho
denote the metric function on
Y
. Consider the function

\displaystyle\Phi:X\times\mathds{N}

\displaystyle\to P(\mathds{N})

\displaystyle(x,i)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left\{n\in\mathds{N}:\rho\bigl(f_{n}(x),f(x)\bigr)\,>\,1/i\right\},

and define

E:=\{N\in[\mathds{N}]^{\infty}:\exists(x,i)\in X\times\mathds{N}\ \text{s.t.}\ N\subset\Phi(x,i)\}.

If one where to show that
E
is analytic, the result would follow by Mathias’s Theorem as follows: There is
N\in\alpha

such that
[N]^{\infty}\subset E
or
[N]^{\infty}\cap E=\emptyset
. Since we cannot have the first possibility because of our assumption

\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n}(x)=f(x)
for every
x\in X
, it follows that
[N]^{\infty}\cap E=\emptyset
; i.e. for every
(x,i)\in X\times\mathds{N}
the set

\left\{n\in\mathds{N}:\rho\bigl(f_{n}(x),f(x)\bigr)\,>\,1/i\right\}

is finite. So (
3
) would follow.

We show that
\Phi
is a Borel function – since
X\times\mathds{N}
is a Polish space, and
[\mathds{N}]^{\infty}
(being a co-countable set in a metric space) is a
Borel subset of
P(\mathds{N})
, this would imply that
E=[\mathds{N}]^{\infty}\cap\Phi(X\times\mathds{N})
is analytic.

For any pair
(F,G)\in[\mathds{N}]^{<\omega}\times[\mathds{N}]^{<\omega}
define

U(F,G):=\left\{N\in P(\mathds{N}):N\cap F=\emptyset\text{ and }N\cap G=G\right\}.

It is clear that
\{U(F,G):(F,G)\in[\mathds{N}]^{<\omega}\times[\mathds{N}]^{<\omega}\}
is a countable base for

. For our purpose, therefore, it suffices to show that

\Phi^{-1}\bigl(U(F,G)\bigr)
is a Borel set. This follows because

\Phi^{-1}\bigl(U(F,G)\bigr)=\bigcup_{i}X_{i}\times\{i\},

where

X_{i}:=\bigcap_{n\in F}\bigr\{x\in X:\rho\bigl(f_{n}(x),f(x)\bigr)\leq 1/i\bigr\}\ \cap\ \bigcap_{n\in G}\bigl\{x\in X:\rho\bigl(f_{n}(x),f(x)\bigr)>1/i\bigr\}

and the later is a Borel set by the assumption on the Borel-measurability of the functions
f_{n}
and
f
.
∎

5.
The commutant of

in its ultrapower.

\alpha\in\beta\omega\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\omega

in its ultrapower w.r.t.

5.1.
Non-trivial commutant – Flat ultrafilters.

Theorem 22
.

[
8
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
(s_{n})
be a flatness scale for the ultrafilter
\alpha\in\beta\omega\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\omega
, satisfying
(i)-(iii)
of Proposition
10
. Let
\{e_{k}:k\in\mathds{N}\}
be the set of pairwise orthogonal, one-dimensional projections onto the canonical basis of
\ell^{2}\ (=H)
. For every
n\in\mathds{N}
let

a_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(k)\,e_{k}

and let
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{n})
. Then
\mathbf{a}
is a non-trivial element of
F_{\alpha}(B(H))
.

The proof makes use of a ‘stratification’ property of
B(H)
as described in the following interesting lemma. This lemma was originally proved in
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
and generalised in
[
8
, Lemma 4.6]
. For every
g\in N^{\uparrow}
and
k\in\mathds{N}

p^{g}(k):=\sum_{i=g^{k-1}(1)}^{g^{k}(1)-1}e_{i},

and let

D(g):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ p^{g}(k)\,B(H)\,p^{g}(k).

Lemma 23
.

For every
a\in B(H)
and
\delta>0
, there exist
g_{0},g_{1}\in N^{\uparrow}
,
a^{0}\in D(g_{0})
and
a^{1}\in D(g_{1})
such that
a-a^{0}-a^{1}
is compact,
\|a^{i}\|\leq 2\|a\|
for

, and
\|a-a^{0}-a^{1}\|<\delta/2
.

Lemma
23
implies that the span of
\bigcup_{g\in N^{\uparrow}}D(g)
is norm dense in
B(H)
. It follows, therefore, that a sufficient (and necessary) condition for a given
(a_{n})

is that
\lim_{\alpha}\|a_{n}b-ba_{n}\|=0
for every

22
we fix an arbitrary

. Observe that
y_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ s_{n}(k)p^{g}(k)
is in the centre of
D(g)
. The flatness property of
\alpha
implies that
\{n\in\mathds{N}:\|a_{n}-y_{n}\|\leq\varepsilon\}\in\alpha
for each
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, since each
a_{n}
is compact and of norm one, the sequence
(a_{n})
is non-trivial, i.e.
(a_{n})
is a non-trivial element of
F_{\alpha}(B(H))
.

We shall now proceed to prove the necessity of the flatness property for
F_{\alpha}(B(H))
to be non-trivial. This answers
[
8
, Question 5.2]
.

Remark 24
.

Note that if
F_{\alpha}(B(H))
is non-trivial, then, in view of
[
11
, Corollary 2.21]
, and since the set of finite rank operators

is norm dense in

, there is
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{a}\in F_{\alpha}(B(H))\cap C_{\alpha}(F(H))

is of norm one and self-adjoint, i.e. if

has norm one, is of finite rank and is self-adjoint. In addition, by making a small perturbation (if necessary) we can further assume that all the

with an ultrafilter on

.

Theorem 25
.

Let
\alpha\in\beta\omega\mathbin{\fgebackslash}\omega
. Then

is non-trivial if and only if
\alpha
is flat.

Proof.

In Theorem
22
it was already shown that if
\alpha
is flat, then

is not trivial. Here we show the converse.

Suppose that
F_{\alpha}(B(H))
is not trivial. In view of Remark
24
, there exists
\mathbf{a}=(a_{i})\in F_{\alpha}(B(H))
such that every
a_{i}
has norm one and has finite rank. Let
\{\xi_{i}:i\in\mathds{N}\}
be a fixed orthonormal basis of
H
. For each
k\in\mathds{N}
let
q_{k}
denote the projection of
H
onto
\spn\{\xi_{i}:i<k\}
. Note that
q_{1}=0
.
For every
n,m\in\mathds{N}
define
s_{n}(m):=\|a_{n}(\mathds{1}-q_{m})\|
.
Observe that the sequence
(s_{n})
satisfies (i) and (ii) of Proposition
10
. So, either
(s_{n})
is a flatness scale for
\alpha
and we’re done, or there is
\varepsilon>0
and a strictly increasing sequence
(m_{k})
in
\mathds{N}
such that

A:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\{n\in\mathds{N}:s_{n}(m_{k})-s_{n}(m_{k+1})>\varepsilon\}

belongs to
\alpha
. Without loss of generality we can suppose that
(m_{k})
increases in a way so that the sequence
(d_{k})
where
d_{k}:=m_{k+1}-m_{k}
is also strictly increasing.
Observe that if
s_{n}(m_{k})-s_{n}(m_{k+1})>\varepsilon

\|a_{n}(q_{m_{k+1}}-q_{m_{k}})\|=\|a_{n}(\mathds{1}-q_{m_{k}})-a_{n}(\mathds{1}-q_{m_{k+1}})\|\geq s_{n}(m_{k})-s_{n}(m_{k+1})>\varepsilon.

For every

is a sequence of pairwise orthogonal, finite-rank projections and for every

for every

and

is flatness scale for

be a strictly increasing sequence in

It is harmless to assume that

\displaystyle\delta<t_{n}(\ell_{i})-t_{n}(\ell_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\|a_{n}p_{\mu(n,i)}\|-t_{n}(\ell_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\|ua_{n}p_{\mu(n,i)}\|-\sup_{k\geq\ell_{j+1}}\|a_{n}p_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|ua_{n}-a_{n}u\|.

\mathbf{a}=(a_{i})\in F_{\alpha}(B(H))

(p_{j})

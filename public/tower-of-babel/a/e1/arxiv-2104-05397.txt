Multigraded algebras and multigraded linear series
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Fatemeh Mohammadi, Leonid Monin

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the study of multigraded algebras and multigraded linear series. For an ℕ s \mathbb{N}^{s} -graded algebra A A , we define and study its volume function F A : ℕ + s → ℝ F_{A}:\mathbb{N}_{+}^{s}\to\mathbb{R} , which computes the asymptotics of the Hilbert function of A A . We relate the volume function F A F_{A} to the volume of the fibers of the global Newton-Okounkov body Δ ⁡ ( A ) \Delta(A) of A A . Unlike the classical case of standard multigraded algebras, the volume function F A F_{A} is not a polynomial in general. However, in the case when the algebra A A has a decomposable grading, we show that the volume function F A F_{A} is a polynomial with non-negative coefficients. We then define mixed multiplicities in this case and provide a full characterization for their positivity. Furthermore, we apply our results on multigraded algebras to multigraded linear series. Our work recovers and unifies recent developments on mixed multiplicities. In particular, we recover results on the existence of mixed multiplicities for (not necessarily Noetherian) graded families of ideals and on the positivity of the multidegrees of multiprojective varieties.

1.
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to study and extend the current theory of
graded algebras of almost integral type
and
graded linear series
to
multigraded algebras of almost integral type
and
multigraded linear series
, respectively. The asymptotic behavior of graded algebras of almost integral type and graded linear series was extensively studied in the foundational papers of Kaveh and Khovanskii
[
20
]
and of Lazarsfeld and Mustaţă
[
24
]
.
Following ideas from seminal works of Okounkov
[
29
,
30
]
, the authors of
[
20
]
and
[
24
]
associated a convex body
\Delta(A)
to a graded algebra
A
equipped with a valuation with one-dimensional leaves. The convex body
\Delta(A)
is called the
Newton-Okounkov body
of the algebra
A
.
One of the main results of
[
20
,
24
]
relates the asymptotic growth of the Hilbert function of a graded algebra of almost integral type with the corresponding Newton-Okounkov body.

More precisely, let
\mathbb{k}
be an algebraically closed field and
\mathbb{F}
be a field containing
\mathbb{k}
.
Recall that a graded
\mathbb{k}
-subalgebra
A\subset\mathbb{F}[t]
of the polynomial ring in one variable is of
integral type
if
A
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
and is a finitely generated module over the subalgebra generated by
[A]_{1}
.
A graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A\subset\mathbb{F}[t]
is of
almost integral type
if
A\subset B\subset\mathbb{F}[t]
, where
B
is a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of integral type. Then, one has the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
20
,
24
]
)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}[t]
be a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type, and let
q=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta(A))
.
Then the Hilbert function of
A
has a polynomial growth.
Moreover, the main term of asymptotics has degree
q
and its coefficient is given by the volume of
\Delta(A)
.

We generalize
Theorem 1.1
to a far-reaching general case of algebras of almost integral type. We consider an arbitrary field
\mathbb{k}
(not necessarily algebraically closed) and we replace the field
\mathbb{F}
with any reduced
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
R
. We also relate the dimension of the Newton-Okounkov body of
A\subset R[t]
to the Krull dimension of
A
.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 3.6
)
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R
be a reduced
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
A\subset R[t]
be a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type.
Suppose
d=\dim(A)>0
.
Then, there exists an integer
m>0
such that the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{nm}\right)}{n^{d-1}}\;\in\;\mathbb{R}_{+}

exists and it is a positive real number.

We prove this result by analyzing the graded algebras equipped with a valuation having leaves of bounded dimension.
In particular, we show that any algebra
A\subset R[t]
of almost integral type has a valuation with bounded leaves (
§3.2
).
Our treatment of singly graded algebras also profited from the works of Cutkosky
[
11
,
9
]
.

We now focus on our developments of the multigraded case of algebras and linear series.

1.1.
Multigraded algebras of almost integral type

Here we describe our main results regarding arbitrary multigraded algebras of almost integral type.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be an arbitrary field and
R
be a
\mathbb{k}
-domain.
Our results in principle could cover the case when
R
is an arbitrary reduced ring (see the general reductions used in
Section 3
), however, in doing so the notation would be quite cumbersome in the notion of volume function that we define below.

First, we introduce our main object of study, which provides the multigraded extension of graded algebras of almost integral type (as introduced in
[
20
]
).
Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}
be new variables over
R
and consider
R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
as a standard
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded polynomial ring where
\deg(t_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
and
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th elementary vector
(0,\ldots,1,\dots,0)
.
We have the following notions:

(i)

An
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}[A]_{{\mathbf{n}}}\subset R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
is said to be of
integral type
if
A
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
and is a finite module over the subalgebra generated by
[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{2}},\ldots,[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}
.

(ii)

An
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}[A]_{{\mathbf{n}}}\subset R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
is said to be of
almost integral type
if
A\subset B\subset R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
, where
B
is an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra of integral type.

Let
A\subset R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
be an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra of almost integral type.
To simplify notation, we also need to assume that
[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.
One has that the Krull dimension of
A
is finite (see
Theorem 2.5
), i.e.,
\dim(A)<\infty
.
Let
d=\dim(A)
and
q=d-s
.
Our main focus is on the
volume function
of
A
, which is defined as

(1)

F_{A}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\quad F_{A}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\Big([A]_{(nn_{1},\ldots,nn_{s})}\Big)}{n^{q}}.

As a direct consequence of
Theorem 3.11
, we obtain that the limit defining the volume function
F_{A}
always exists.
Note that, when
A
is a standard
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra then the volume function
F_{A}
encodes the mixed multiplicities of
A
(see
Section 4
).

In a similar fashion to
[
24
]
, we relate the volume function
F_{A}
of
A
to a global Newton-Okounkov body that we define below.
For that, we can safely assume that
R
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
(see
Section 3
) and that there exists a valuation
\nu:\text{Quot}(R)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
with certain good properties (see
§3.2
).
Of particular importance is the fact that the valuation
\nu
has leaves of bounded dimension.
Let
\Gamma_{A}
be the
valued semigroup
of the
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
A
:

\Gamma_{A}\;=\;\big\{(\nu(a),{\mathbf{m}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\times\mathbb{N}^{s}\;\mid\;0\neq a\in[A]_{\mathbf{m}}\big\}.

We define

\Delta(A)\;=\;{\text{Con}}(\Gamma_{A})\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}

to be the closed convex cone generated by
\Gamma_{A}
and we call it the
global Newton-Okounkov body
of
A
.
We denote the index of
A
by
{\text{ind}}(A)
and the maximal dimension of leaves of
A
by
\ell_{A}
(these invariants refer to a uniform behavior of the Veronese subalgebras
A^{({\mathbf{n}})}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}[A]_{n{\mathbf{n}}}
; for more details, see
§4.1
).
One has the diagram below
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}^{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}}
\pi_{1}
\pi_{2}

where
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}
and
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
denote the natural projections.
We denote the
fiber
of the global Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A)
over
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
by
\Delta(A)_{x}=\Delta(A)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(x)
.

The following theorem contains our main results regarding the relations between the volume function
F_{A}
and the global Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A)
.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 4.7
,
§4.1
)
.

Under the notations above the following results hold:

(i)

The fiber
\Delta(A)_{{\mathbf{n}}}
of the global Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A)\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
coincides with the Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A^{({\mathbf{n}})})
for each
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
, that is:

\pi_{1}\left(\Delta(A)_{\mathbf{n}}\right)\times\{1\}\;=\;\pi_{1}\left(\Delta(A)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}({\mathbf{n}})\right)\times\{1\}\;=\;\Delta(A^{({\mathbf{n}})})\;\subset\;\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\{1\}.

(ii)

There exists a unique continuous homogeneous function of degree
q
extending the volume function
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
defined in
(1)
to the positive orthant
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
.
This function is given by

F_{A}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad x\mapsto\ell_{A}\cdot\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{q}(\Delta(A)_{x})}{{\text{ind}}(A)}.

Moreover, the function
F_{A}
is log-concave:

F_{A}(x+y)^{\frac{1}{q}}\;\geq\;F_{A}(x)^{\frac{1}{q}}+F_{A}(y)^{\frac{1}{q}}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}.

We illustrate the concrete computation of the volume functions in
Section 4
and
§4.1
.
In particular, we show that in general, part
(ii)
of
Theorem B
is the strongest result that can be obtained in this setting: for any non-negative, homogeneous of degree
1
, concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{s}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
we construct an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
A
whose volume function is
f
(see
§4.1
).

1.2.
Algebras with decomposable grading

An important question is to determine the following:

•

When is the volume function
F_{A}
a polynomial?

By mimicking the classical case of standard multigraded algebras, the existence of such a polynomial yields a natural notion of mixed multiplicities for the algebra
A
(see
Section 4
).
It turns out that the volume function
F_{A}
is not always a polynomial (see, e.g.,
Section 4
,
§4.1
and
§4.1
). However, we show that the volume function is a polynomial for the family of multigraded algebras with decomposable gradings.

An
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
A
is said to have a
decomposable grading
if the equality

[A]_{(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{s})}=[A]_{n_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}\cdot[A]_{n_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}\cdot\,\cdots \,\cdot[A]_{n_{s}{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}

holds for each
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
.

Now, we additionally assume that the
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
A\subset R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
of almost integral type has a decomposable grading.
For each
p\geq 1
, let

\widehat{A}_{[p]}\;=\;\mathbb{k}\left[[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}\right]\subset A

be the
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra generated by
[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}
, and denote by
\widetilde{A}_{[p]}=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\left[\widehat{A}_{[p]}\right]_{p{\mathbf{n}}}
the standard
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra obtained by regrading
\widehat{A}_{[p]}
.

The following theorem deals with the case of algebras with a decomposable grading.

Theorem C

(
Theorem 5.5
)
.

Assume the notations above with
A
having a decomposable grading.
Then, there exists a homogeneous polynomial
G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{R}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}]
of degree
q
with non-negative real coefficients such that

F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\quad\text{ for all }\quad{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}.

Additionally, we have

G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\;=\;\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}.

We illustrate
Theorem C
with an example of the Cox ring of a full flag variety (
Section 5
).

Moreover, we can write the polynomial
G_{A}
of
Theorem C
as follows

G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\sum_{|{\mathbf{d}}|=q}\frac{1}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\,e({\mathbf{d}};A)\,{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}.

Then, for each
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=q
, we define the non-negative real number

e({\mathbf{d}};A)\geq 0

to be the
mixed multiplicity of type
{\mathbf{d}}
of
A
.

In
Section 5
, we provide an extension into a multigraded setting of the Fujita approximation theorem for graded algebras given in
[
20
, Theorem 2.35]
.
We show the following equalities

e({\mathbf{d}};A)\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)}{p^{q}}\;=\;\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)}{p^{q}}.

Furthermore, in
Theorem 5.9
, we provide a full characterization for the positivity of the mixed multiplicities
e({\mathbf{d}};A)
of
A
.

In our findings, we got that a number of interesting applications follow rather easily by studying certain multigraded algebras with decomposable grading.
The list of applications includes the following:

(1)

In
Section 6
, we recover some results from
[
7
,
6
,
10
]
by showing the existence of mixed multiplicities for (not necessarily Noetherian) graded families of ideals.

(2)

In
§6.1
, we provide a full characterization for the positivity of the mixed multiplicities of graded families of equally generated ideals.

(3)

In
§6.2
, we recover a classical characterization for the positivity of the mixed volumes of convex bodies.

1.3.
Multigraded linear series

Finally, we are interested in the notion of multigraded linear series.
Let
\mathbb{k}
be an arbitrary field and
X
be a proper variety over
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
be a sequence of Cartier divisors on
X
, and consider the corresponding section ring

\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})\;=\;\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}(n_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}D_{s})\big).

A
multigraded linear series
associated to the divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
is an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-subalgebra
W
of the section ring
\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
.
In particular,
W
is of almost integral type (see
Section 7
).
The
Kodaira-Itaka dimension
of
W
is given by

\kappa(W)\;=\;\dim(W)-s,

where as before
\dim(W)
denotes the Krull dimension of
W
.
As simple consequence of our developments for multigraded algebras, we have the following theorem for multigraded linear series (which we enunciate below for the sake of completeness).

Theorem D

(
Theorem 7.4
)
.

Under the above notations, let
W\subset\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
be a multigraded linear series and suppose that
[W]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

The volume function

F_{W}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([W]_{n{\mathbf{n}}}\big)}{n^{\kappa(W)}}

of
W
is well-defined for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.

(ii)

There exists a unique continuous function that is homogeneous of degree
\kappa(W)
and log-concave and that extends the volume function
F_{W}({\mathbf{n}})
to the positive orthant
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
.
This function is given by

F_{W}\,:\,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad x\,\mapsto\,\ell_{W}\cdot\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{\kappa(W)}\big(\Delta(W)_{x}\big)}{{\text{ind}}(W)}.

(iii)

If
W
has a decomposable grading, then there exists a homogeneous polynomial
G_{W}({\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{R}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}]
of degree
\kappa(W)
with non-negative real coefficients such that

F_{W}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;G_{W}({\mathbf{n}})

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.

Finally, in
Theorem 7.5
, we express the mixed multiplicities of a multigraded linear series with decomposable grading in terms of the multidegrees of the multiprojective varieties obtained as the image of certain Kodaira rational maps.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

In this preparatory section, we fix our notation and recall some important results to be used throughout the paper.
We denote the set of non-negative integers by
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
and the set of positive integers by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
.
Let
d\geq 1
.
For a vector
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we denote by
|\mathbf{n}|
the sum of its entries. We also denote by
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
the
i
-th elementary vector
(0,\ldots,1,\dots,0)
.
For
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})
in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
we write
\mathbf{n}\geqslant\mathbf{m}
if
n_{i}\geqslant m_{i}
for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
.
The null vector
(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
.
Moreover, we write
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
if
n_{i}\geqslant 0
for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
.
We also use the abbreviations
\mathbf{n}!=n_{1}!\cdots n_{d}!
and
\mathbf{n}^{\mathbf{m}}=n_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{m_{d}}
.

We first describe the notions and methods of Newton-Okounkov bodies and recall some important results from
[
20
]
.
Let
d\geq 1
.
Suppose that
S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1}
is a semigroup in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1}
.
Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the projection into the last component.
Let
L=L(S)
be the linear subspace of
R^{d+1}
which is generated by
S
.
Let
M=M(S)
be the rational half-space
M(S):=L(S)\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})=L(S)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right)
, and let
\partial M_{\mathbb{Z}}=\partial M\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1}
.
Let
{\text{Con}}(S)\subset L(S)
be the closed convex cone which is the closure of the set of all linear combinations
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}s_{i}
with
s_{i}\in S
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
.
Let
G(S)\subset L(S)
be the group generated by
S
.

We say that the semigroup
S
is
non-negative
if
S\subset M
;
additionally, if
{\text{Con}}(S)
is strictly convex and intersects the space
\partial M
only at the origin, then
S
is
strongly non-negative
(see
[
20
, Definition 1.9 and §1.4]
).

Following
[
20
]
, when
S
is non-negative we fix the following notation:

–

[S]_{n}:=S\cap\pi^{-1}(n)=S\cap\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\times\{n\}\right)
.

–

m(S):=\left[{\mathbb{Z}}:\pi(G(S))\right]
.

–

{\text{ind}}(S):=\left[\partial M_{\mathbb{Z}}:G(S)\cap\partial M\right]
.

–

\Delta(S):={\text{Con}}(S)\cap\pi^{-1}(m(S))
(the Newton-Okounkov body of
S
).

–

{\text{Vol}}_{q}(\Delta(S))
is the
integral volume
of
\Delta(M,S)
(see
[
20
, Definition 1.13]
); this volume is computed using the
translation of the
integral measure
on
\partial M
.

The following result is of remarkable importance for us.

Theorem 2.1

(Kaveh-Khovanskii,
[
20
, Corollary 1.16]
)
.

Suppose that
S
is strongly non-negative.
Let
m=m(S)
and
q=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta(S))
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#[S]_{nm}}{n^{q}}\;=\;\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{q}(\Delta(S))}{{\text{ind}}(S)}.

For a subset
U\subset S
of
S
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

n\star U\;:=\;\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\,\mid\,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in U\Big\}.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\widehat{S}_{p}
the subsemigroup of
S
generated by
[S]_{p}
, that is,

\left[\widehat{S}_{p}\right]_{np}=n\star[S]_{p}\quad\;\text{ and }\quad\;\left[\widehat{S}_{p}\right]_{n}=0\text{ if }p\text{ does not divide }n.

The following approximation theorem relates the semigroup
S
with the semigroups
\widehat{S}_{pm}
for
p
big enough.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
20
, Theorem 1.27]
,
[
24
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Suppose that
S
is strongly non-negative.
Let
m=m(S)
and
q=\dim(\Delta(S))
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be a positive real number.
Then, for
p\gg 0
the following statements hold:

(i)

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta(\widehat{S}_{pm}))=q
.

(ii)

{\text{ind}}(\widehat{S}_{pm})={\text{ind}}(S)
.

(iii)

We have the inequalities

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#[S]_{nm}}{n^{q}}-\varepsilon\;\;\leq\;\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\left(n\star[S]_{pm}\right)}{n^{q}p^{q}}\;\;\leq\;\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#[S]_{nm}}{n^{q}}.

We now briefly recall Minkowski’s theorem and the notion of mixed volume of convex bodies.
Let
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{s})
be a sequence of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For any sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
of non-negative integers, we denote by
\lambda\mathbf{K}
the Minkowski sum
\lambda\mathbf{K}:=\lambda_{1}K_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}K_{s}
and by
\mathbf{K}_{\lambda}
the multiset
\mathbf{K}_{\lambda}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}\{K_{i}\}
of
\lambda_{i}
copies of
K_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
.
Below is the classical Minkowski’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
31
, Theorem 5.1.7]
).

Theorem 2.3

(Minkowski)
.

{\text{Vol}}_{d}(\lambda\mathbf{K})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
.

We write the polynomial
{\text{Vol}}_{d}(\lambda\mathbf{K})
as

{\text{Vol}}_{d}(\lambda\mathbf{K})=\sum_{{{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}\;\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=d}}\;\frac{1}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\,{\text{MV}}_{d}(\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{d}})\,\lambda^{\mathbf{d}},

where
{\text{MV}}_{d}(-)
denotes the
mixed volume
.

Next, we concentrate on describing the dimension of a subalgebra of an algebra finitely generated over a field.
Following the terminology of
[
23
]
, we say that these algebras are
subfinite
.
Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
A
be a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
We shall always denote by
\dim(A)
the Krull dimension of
A
.
We say that
A
is
subfinite over
\mathbb{k}
if there exists a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
B
containing
A
.
Denote by
\delta_{\mathbb{k}}(A)
the maximal number of elements in
A
which are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{k}
, that is,

\delta_{\mathbb{k}}(A):=\sup\big\{d\mid\text{there exists }x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\in A\text{ which are algebraically independent over }\mathbb{k}\big\}.

Equivalently,
\delta_{\mathbb{k}}(A)=d
if
d
is the largest integer such that there is an inclusion
\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\hookrightarrow A
where
\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
is a polynomial ring over
\mathbb{k}
.

Remark 2.4
.

If
A
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
, then
\dim(A)=\delta_{\mathbb{k}}(A)
.
This is a classical result that follows for instance from the Noether normalization theorem (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 13.3]
).

The following theorem extends the above remark to algebras that are subfinite over
\mathbb{k}
(cf.
[
14
]
).

Theorem 2.5

(
[
23
, Theorem 4.6, Corollary 4.7]
)
.

Let
A
be a subfinite algebra over
\mathbb{k}
.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

\dim(A)=\delta_{\mathbb{k}}(A)
.

(ii)

If
B\supset A
is a subfinite algebra over
\mathbb{k}
, then
\dim(B)\geq\dim(A)
.

3.
Singly graded algebras of almost integral type

In this section, we extend the results of
[
20
, Part II]
regarding the asymptotic behavior of algebras of
almost integral type
.
Our results are slightly more complete than the ones in
[
20
, Part II]
with respect to the following points:

(1)

We consider an arbitrary field
\mathbb{k}
instead of assuming that
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed.

(2)

We substitute the field
\mathbb{F}
by any reduced
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
R
.

(3)

We show that the asymptotic growth of an algebra of almost integral type is determined by its Krull dimension.

Similar results to the ones in this section were also obtained in
[
9
]
for graded linear series.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be an arbitrary field and
R
be a reduced
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
R[t]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over
R
.
Below we introduce the notion of almost integral type in our current setting.

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

A graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A\subset R[t]
is called of
integral type
if
A
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
and is a finitely generated module over the subalgebra generated by
[A]_{1}
.

(ii)

A graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A\subset R[t]
is called of
almost integral type
if
A\subset B\subset R[t]
, where
B
is a graded algebra of integral type.

Notice that, if
A\subset R[t]
is a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral, then
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{n}\right)<\infty
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
By definition, one has that a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type is a subfinite algebra over
\mathbb{k}
.
For any positively graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A
which is subfinite over
\mathbb{k}
, we denote by
G^{i}(A)
the finitely generated graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra that is generated by the graded components
[A]_{0},\ldots,[A]_{i}
, that is,

G^{i}(A)\,:=\,\mathbb{k}\big[[A]_{0},\ldots,[A]_{i}\big]\,\subset\,A.

First, we discuss some properties of graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebras that are subfinite over
\mathbb{k}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a positively graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra which is subfinite over
\mathbb{k}
.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

There exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\dim(A)=\dim\left(G^{i}(A)\right)
for all
i\geq i_{0}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
d=\dim(A)>0
. Then there exists
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([A]_{nh}\big)>\alpha n^{d-1}

for all
n>0
.

Proof.

(i) This part follows from
Theorem 2.5
.

(ii) Let
C=G^{i}(A)
such that
\dim(C)=d
.
Since
C
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
, by
[
15
, Lemma 13.10, Remark 13.11]
, one has a positive integer
h>0
such that the Veronese subalgebra
C^{(h)}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}[C]_{nh}
is a standard graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
The ring
[A]_{0}
is Artinian because by assumption it is a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{k}
.
As
\dim(C^{(h)})=\dim(C)=d
, by
[
27
, Corollary of Theorem 13.2]
the Hilbert polynomial of
C^{(h)}
has degree
d-1
and it coincides with
\text{length}_{[A]_{0}}\left([C]_{nh}\right)
for
n\gg 0
.
Therefore, there exists a positive real number
\alpha
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([A]_{nh})\geq\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([C]_{nh})>\alpha n^{d-1}
for all
n>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
,
B
and
C
be graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebras which are subfinite over
\mathbb{k}
.
Suppose there exists
n_{0}\geq 0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
we have a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow[A]_{n}\rightarrow[B]_{n}\rightarrow[C]_{n}\rightarrow 0
.
Then
\dim(B)=\max\{\dim(A),\dim(C)\}
.

Proof.

After choosing some
h\geq n_{0}
, we substitute
A
,
B
and
C
by the Veronese subalgebras
A^{(h)}
,
B^{(h)}
and
C^{(h)}
, respectively.
Thus we may assume that
0\rightarrow[A]_{n}\rightarrow[B]_{n}\rightarrow[C]_{n}\rightarrow 0
is exact for all
n\geq 1
.

By invoking
Theorem 2.5
, we choose
i>0
such that
\dim(G^{i}(B)\cap A)=\dim(A)
,
\dim(G^{i}(B))=\dim(B)
and
\dim(G^{i}(C))=\dim(C)
.
Notice that we have the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\left[G^{i}(B)\cap A\right]_{+}\rightarrow\left[G^{i}(B)\right]_{+}\rightarrow\left[G^{i}(C)\right]_{+}\rightarrow 0,

where
[S]_{+}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}[S]_{n}
for any graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
S
.
From the above short exact sequence, we obtain that the ideal
\left[G^{i}(B)\cap A\right]_{+}
is finitely generated (as it is an ideal over
G^{i}(B)
), which implies that
G^{i}(B)\cap A
is a finitely generated graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Proposition 1.5.4]
).
We substitute
A
,
B
and
C
by
G^{i}(B)\cap A
,
G^{i}(B)
and
G^{i}(C)
, respectively.
So, we assume that
A
,
B
and
C
are finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebras.

Finally, by standard arguments we can show that
\dim(B)=\max\{\dim(A),\dim(C)\}
.
We choose
h>0
such that
0\rightarrow A^{(h)}\rightarrow B^{(h)}\rightarrow C^{(h)}\rightarrow 0
is a short exact sequence of standard graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebras (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Lemma 13.10, Remark 13.11]
), and then the result follows from the additivity of Hilbert polynomials and
[
27
, Corollary of Theorem 13.2]
.
∎

The following easy remark shows that we can always substitute
R
by a reduced finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra (cf.
[
20
, Proposition 2.25]
).

Remark 3.4
.

Let
A\subset R[t]
be a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type.
By definition, let
B\supset A
be a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of integral type and suppose that
f_{i_{1}}t^{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{m}}t^{i_{m}}
are generators of
B
as a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
R^{\prime}\subset R
be the reduced
\mathbb{k}
-algebra generated by the elements
f_{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{m}}\in R
.
So, one has that
A\subset R^{\prime}[t]
with
R^{\prime}
being a reduced finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.

Thus, as a consequence of the above remark, we now safely assume that
R
is a reduced finitely generated algebra.
Hence, let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell}\in\text{Spec}(R)
be the minimal primes of
R
.
Since
R
is assumed to be reduced, we have a canonical inclusion

R[t]\,\hookrightarrow\,\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}[t]
.
Let
A\subset R[t]
be a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type.
For each
1\leq i\leq\ell
, let
B^{i}
be the
\mathbb{k}
-subalgebra defined by

(2)

\left[B^{i}\right]_{n}\,:=\,\begin{cases}\mathbb{k}\qquad\quad\;\,\text{ if }n=0\\
\left[M^{i}\right]_{n}\quad\,\text{ if }n>0\end{cases}

where

M^{i}:=\left(A\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}R[t]\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}R[t]\right)/\left(A\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}R[t]\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}R[t]\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}R[t]\right)
.
By construction,
B^{i}
is a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type and we have a canonical inclusion
B^{i}\hookrightarrow R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}[t]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Under the above notation, the following statements hold:

(i)

\dim\left(A\right)=\max\big\{\dim\left(B^{i}\right)\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\big\}
.

(ii)

For all
n>0
, one has
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{n}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[B^{i}\right]_{n}\right)
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq\ell
we have the short exact sequence

0\,\rightarrow\,M^{i}\,\rightarrow\,\frac{A}{A\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}R[t]\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}R[t]\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}R[t]}\,\rightarrow\,\frac{A}{A\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}R[t]\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}R[t]}\rightarrow 0.

Since
R
is reduced,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}R[t]\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell}R[t]=0
.
Therefore, part (ii) is clear and part (i) follows from
Section 3
.
∎

The following theorem contains the main result of this section.
By using the general arguments above, the first idea in the proof is to reduce to the case when
R
is a domain.
For organizational purposes, in the next subsection, we encapsulate a proof of
Theorem 3.6
under the assumptions of
R
being a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-domain (see
Theorem 3.11
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R
be a reduced
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
A\subset R[t]
be a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type.
Suppose
d=\dim(A)>0
.
Then, there exists an integer
m>0
such that the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{nm}\right)}{n^{d-1}}\;\in\;\mathbb{R}_{+}

exists and it is a positive real number.

Proof.

By
Section 3
, we assume that
R
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
.
Suppose that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell}
are the minimal primes of
R
, and consider the algebras
B^{i}
constructed in
(2)
.
Since each
B^{i}\subset R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}[t]
is an algebra of almost integral type,
Theorem 3.11
yields the existence of the following limits

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[B^{i}\right]_{nm_{i}}\right)}{n^{d_{i}-1}}\;\in\;\mathbb{R}_{+}

where
m_{i}=m(B^{i})
and
d_{i}=\dim(B^{i})
.
Therefore, after taking
m=\text{lcm}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell})
, the result of the theorem follows from
Section 3
.
∎

3.1.
The case where
R
is a domain

The setup below is used throughout this subsection.

Setup 3.7
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-domain and
r={\rm trdeg}_{\mathbb{k}}(\text{Quot}(R))
.
Let
A\subset R[t]
be a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type.
Let
d=\dim(A)
.
Let
m(A)
be the index
m(A):=[{\mathbb{Z}}:G]
of the subgroup
G
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
generated by
\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid[A]_{n}\neq 0\}
.
For any
p>0
, we denote by
\widehat{A}_{p}
the graded
\mathbb{k}
-subalgebra
\widehat{A}_{p}:=\mathbb{k}\big[[A]_{p}\big]\subset A
generated by the graded part
[A]_{p}
.
By regrading the algebra
\widehat{A}_{p}
, we obtain the standard graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
\widetilde{A}_{p}
defined by
{[\widetilde{A}_{p}]}_{n}:={[\widehat{A}_{p}]}_{np}
for all
n\geq 0
.
As customary, let

e(\widetilde{A}_{p})\;:=\;(\dim(\widetilde{A}_{p})-1)!\;\cdot\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big({[\widetilde{A}_{p}]}_{n}\big)}{n^{\dim(\widetilde{A}_{p})-1}}

be the multiplicity of the standard graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
\widetilde{A}_{p}
.

Remark 3.8
.

We fix an order on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
which is compatible with an addition, i.e., for any
{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{m}},\mathbf{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
with
{\mathbf{n}}<{\mathbf{m}}
, we have
{\mathbf{n}}+\mathbf{p}<{\mathbf{m}}+\mathbf{p}
.
One way to do this is to fix a linear function
l
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
with rationally independent coefficients and set
{\mathbf{n}}<{\mathbf{m}}\iff l({\mathbf{n}})<l({\mathbf{m}})
for
{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
.

We assume that
\text{Quot}(R)
admits a valuation
\nu:\text{Quot}(R)\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
such that
\nu(\alpha)=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{k}\subset\text{Quot}(R)
.
We further suppose that
\nu:\text{Quot}(R)\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
is
faithful
and has
leaves of bounded dimension
.
By
§3.2
we can always construct such a valuation.
The faithfulness of
\nu
means that
\nu(\text{Quot}(R))={\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
.
For any
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
, we define

K_{\mathbf{n}}:=\{f\in\text{Quot}(R)\mid\nu(f)\geqslant{\mathbf{n}}\}

and

K_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}:=\{f\in\text{Quot}(R)\mid\nu(f)>{\mathbf{n}}\}
,
and we say that the
leaf with value
{\mathbf{n}}
is given by

L_{\mathbf{n}}\;:=\;K_{\mathbf{n}}/K_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}.

We say that
\nu
has leaves with bounded dimension if
\sup_{{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}}\left(\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(L_{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)<\infty
.
Let
\ell
be the maximal dimension of the leaves of
\nu
:

\ell\;:=\;\max_{{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}}\big(\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(L_{\mathbf{n}}\right)\big).

For every
t\geq 1
, we define

(3)

\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}\,:=\;\Big\{({\mathbf{n}},n)=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r},n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\times\mathbb{N}\;\mid\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(K_{{\mathbf{n}}}\cap[A]_{n}/K_{{\mathbf{n}}}^{+}\cap[A]_{n}\right)\geq t\Big\}.

For any
n\geq 0
and
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
one has the inequalities

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(K_{\mathbf{n}}\cap[A]_{n}/K_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\cap[A]_{n}\right)\;\leqslant\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(L_{\mathbf{n}}\right)\;\leqslant\;\ell.

We consider the integer

(4)

\ell_{A}\;:=\;\max\big\{t\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}\not\subseteq\{0\}\big\}\;\leq\;\ell.

Then, we have the following equalities

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{n}\right)&=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}}\left(K_{\mathbf{n}}\cap[A]_{n}/K_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\cap[A]_{n}\right)\right)\\
&=\sum_{t=1}^{\ell_{A}}\#\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}\right]_{n}\end{split}

for all
n\geq 0
.

We have the following general lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\times\mathbb{N}
be a strongly non-negative semigroup and let
\Gamma\subset S
be a non-trivial semigroup ideal (i.e.,
a+S\subset\Gamma
for all
a\in S
), then

(i)

\Delta(\Gamma)=\Delta(S)
.

(ii)

{\text{ind}}(\Gamma)={\text{ind}}(S)
.

(iii)

m(\Gamma)=m(S)

Proof.

Let
a\in\Gamma
be any point, then we have an inclusion
a+S\subset\Gamma
, and hence
G(S)=G(\Gamma)
and, in particular,
{\text{ind}}(\Gamma)={\text{ind}}(S)
and
m(\Gamma)=m(S)
.
Moreover, we obtain inclusions of closed convex cones

{\text{Con}}(S)+a\subset{\text{Con}}(\Gamma)\subset{\text{Con}}(S),

which guaranties the equalities
{\text{Con}}(S)={\text{Con}}(\Gamma)
and
\Delta(\Gamma)=\Delta(S)
.
∎

In the next proposition, we study some properties of
\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}
.
For the special case
t=1
, we simply write
\Gamma_{A}:=\Gamma^{(1)}_{A}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose that
1\leq t\leq\ell_{A}
(that is,
\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}\not\subseteq\{0\}
).
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}\subseteq\Gamma_{A}
.

(ii)

\Gamma_{A}
is a semigroup, and
\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}
is a semigroup ideal of
\Gamma_{A}
.
In particular, one has that

\Delta(\Gamma^{(t)}_{A})=\Delta(\Gamma_{A}),\quad{\text{ind}}(\Gamma^{(t)}_{A})={\text{ind}}(\Gamma_{A})\quad\text{and}\quad m(\Gamma^{(t)}_{A})=m(A).

(iii)

\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}
is strongly non-negative.

Proof.

(i) This part is clear.

(ii) Let
({\mathbf{n}},n)=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r},n)\in\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}
and
({\mathbf{m}},m)=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r},m)\in\Gamma_{A}
.
Then we can choose elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}\in[A]_{n}
such that
\nu(f_{i})={\mathbf{n}}
and the classes
\overline{f_{1}},\ldots,\overline{f_{t}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{k}
in
K_{\mathbf{n}}\cap[A]_{n}/K_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\cap[A]_{n}
.
Similarly, let
g\in[A]_{m}
such that
\nu(g)={\mathbf{m}}
.

By contradiction, suppose that the classes of
gf_{1},\ldots,gf_{t}\in[A]_{n+m}
are
\mathbb{k}
-linearly dependent in

K_{{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{m}}}\cap[A]_{n+m}/K_{{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{m}}}^{+}\cap[A]_{n+m}.

Thus, there exist
a_{1}\ldots,a_{t}\in\mathbb{k}
such that
\nu(a_{1}gf_{1}+\cdots+a_{t}gf_{t})>{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{m}}
, which implies that
\nu(a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{t}f_{t})>{\mathbf{n}}
.
But, the last inequality
\nu(a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{t}f_{t})>{\mathbf{n}}
yields the contradiction
a_{1}\overline{f_{1}}+\cdots+a_{t}\overline{f_{t}}=0\in K_{\mathbf{n}}\cap[A]_{n}/K_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\cap[A]_{n}
.
So, as required we have
({\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{m}},n+m)\in\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}
.

Finally,
§3.1
implies that
\Delta(\Gamma^{(t)}_{A})=\Delta(\Gamma_{A})
,
{\text{ind}}(\Gamma^{(t)}_{A})={\text{ind}}(\Gamma_{A})
and
m(\Gamma^{(t)}_{A})=m(\Gamma_{A})
.
Also, notice that
m(\Gamma_{A})=m(A)
.

(iii) Since
\Gamma^{(t)}_{A}\subset\Gamma_{A}
, it is enough to show that

\Gamma_{A}

is strongly non-negative.
By definition, there is a graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
B\subset R[t]
of integral type such that
A\subset B
.
Consider the semigroup

\Gamma_{B}:=\big\{({\mathbf{n}},n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\times\mathbb{N}\;\mid\;K_{{\mathbf{n}}}\cap[B]_{n}/K_{{\mathbf{n}}}^{+}\cap[B]_{n}\neq 0\big\}

determined by
B
.
After possibly extending
B
by a bigger algebra of integral type, we can assume that
G(\Gamma_{B})={\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}
(see
[
20
, Lemma 2.29]
).
Since
B
is a finitely generated module over a standard graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra and
\dim(B)\leq r+1
, it follows that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([B]_{n})
becomes a polynomial of degree bounded by
r
for
n\gg 0
.
So,
[
20
, Theorem 1.18]
implies that
\Gamma_{B}
is strongly non-negative, and since
\Gamma_{A}\subset\Gamma_{B}
, the result follows.
∎

The following theorem deals with the asymptotic behavior of the growth of
A
.
The semigroup
\Gamma_{A}
is given by

\Gamma_{A}\;=\;\Gamma^{(1)}_{A}\;=\;\big\{(\nu(a),n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\times\mathbb{N}\;\mid\;0\neq a\in[A]_{n}\big\}

and we call it the
valued semigroup
of the graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A
.
To simplify notation, we denote
\Delta(\Gamma_{A})
and
{\text{ind}}(\Gamma_{A})
by
\Delta(A)
and
{\text{ind}}(A)
, respectively.

Theorem 3.11
.

Assume
§3.1
, set
m=m(A)
and let

and
\ell_{A}
be as in
(4)
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{nm}\right)}{n^{d-1}}\;=\;\ell_{A}\cdot\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{d-1}(\Delta(A))}{{\text{ind}}(A)},

and we have the equality

Proof.

For any

By using the inclusions

(6)

and

, we can choose

From

From

4.
Multigraded algebras of almost integral type

Here we define and study a volume function for multigraded algebras of almost integral type (see

), and we relate this function to certain global Newton-Okounkov bodies.
In
[
24
]
, similar volume functions have been considered for the case of multigraded linear series in an irreducible projective variety over an algebraically closed field.
First, for the sake of completeness, we recall the notion of mixed multiplicities for the well-studied case of standard multigraded algebras, and we explain how this volume function encodes the mixed multiplicities.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
A
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra (i.e., it is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
by elements of degree
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
).
Suppose
A
is a domain and set
d=\dim(A)
.
Let
P_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=P_{A}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})
be the multigraded Hilbert polynomial of
A
(see, e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
).
Then, the degree of
P_{A}
is equal to
q=d-s
and

P_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{\mathbf{n}}\right)

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
such that
{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Furthermore, if we write

P_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=\sum_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{s}\geq 0}e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})\binom{n_{1}+d_{1}}{d_{1}}\cdots\binom{n_{s}+d_{s}}{d_{s}},

then
0\leq e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=q
.
For
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=q
, we say that

e({\mathbf{d}};A)\;:=\;e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})

is the
mixed multiplicity of
A
of type
{\mathbf{d}}
.
We define a function

F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;:=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{n{\mathbf{n}}}\right)}{n^{q}}\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([A]_{(nn_{1},\ldots,nn_{s})}\right)}{n^{q}}.

For all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
, we have
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
where
G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
is the homogeneous polynomial

G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;:=\;\sum_{|{\mathbf{d}}|=q}\frac{1}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\,e({\mathbf{d}};A)\,{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}\;=\;\sum_{d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=q}\frac{e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s};A)}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{s}!}\,n_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots n_{s}^{d_{s}}.

So, the function
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
encodes the mixed multiplicities of
A
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R
be a
\mathbb{k}
-domain.
We introduce new variables

The following setup is used throughout the rest of this section.

Setup 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and

\mathbb{k}

be the maximal dimension of leaves of
A
which is the constant value of
\ell_{A^{({\mathbf{n}})}}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.

The corollary below gives an important characterization of the volume function of
A
in terms of the global Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A)
.

Corollary 4.11
.

Assume
Section 4
.
There exists a unique continuous homogeneous function of degree
q
extending the volume function
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
defined in
(9)
to the positive orthant
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
.
This function is given by

F_{A}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad x\mapsto\ell_{A}\cdot\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{q}(\Delta(A)_{x})}{{\text{ind}}(A)}.

Moreover, the function
F_{A}
is log-concave:

F_{A}(x+y)^{\frac{1}{q}}\;\geq\;F_{A}(x)^{\frac{1}{q}}+F_{A}(y)^{\frac{1}{q}}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}.

Proof.

By combining
Theorem 3.11
,
Theorem 4.7
and
§4.1
, it follows that
F_{A}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}\to\mathbb{R},\;x\mapsto\ell_{A}\cdot\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{q}(\Delta(A)_{x})}{{\text{ind}}(A)}
extends the function
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
defined in
(9)
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.

Now, the homogeneity follows from the fact that
\Delta(A)_{\lambda\cdot x}=\lambda\cdot\Delta(A)_{x}
. Since
\Delta(A)
is convex we have
\Delta(A)_{x}+\Delta(A)_{y}\subset\Delta(A)_{x+y}
and hence the log-concavity follows from Brunn-Minkowski inequalities for the volume of convex bodies.
∎

As our next example shows, in general,
§4.1
is the most general statement about volume function
F_{A}
for some multigraded algebra
A
.

Example 4.12

(Every concave function is a volume function)
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[u]
be a polynomial ring and consider the polynomial ring
R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
.
Let further
f:\mathbb{R}^{s}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be any non-negative, homogeneous of degree
1
, concave function. We define a family of vector spaces indexed by
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
:

[A]_{\mathbf{n}}=\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq f(\mathbf{n})}\mathbb{k}\cdot u^{i}\right)\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}\subset\Big[R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]\Big]_{\mathbf{n}}

The algebra
A_{f}:=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}[A]_{\mathbf{n}}\subset R[t_{1},\dots,t_{s}]
is naturally an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra. Since
f
is concave and homogeneous, the global Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A_{f})
of
A_{f}
is a cone in
\mathbb{R}^{s+1}
given by:

\Delta(A_{f})=\left\{(y,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}_{\geq 0}\,\mid\,0\leq y\leq f(x)\right\}.

In particular, we get
F_{A_{f}}(x)=f(x)
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{s}_{\geq 0}
.

However in some cases one can say more about function
F_{A}(x)
.
Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
be a convex cone and consider a family
\{\Delta_{x}\}_{x\in C}
of convex bodies of dimension
q
parametrised by
C
. We say
\{\Delta_{x}\}_{x\in C}
is
linear
if
\Delta_{\lambda_{1}x+\lambda_{2}y}=\lambda_{1}\Delta_{x}+\lambda_{2}\Delta_{y}
for any
x,y\in C
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
. According to Minkowski’s theorem (see
Theorem 2.3
), the volume of a linear family of convex bodies is a homogeneous polynomial. Thus we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.13
.

Assume that the fibers of the global Newton-Okounkov body form a linear family of convex bodies. Then the function
F_{A}(x)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
q
.

Proof.

Since

F_{A}(x)=\ell_{A}\cdot\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{q}(\Delta(A)_{x})}{{\text{ind}}(A)},

the statement of the proposition follows from Minkowski’s theorem and the fact that

\Delta_{x}=x_{1}\Delta_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}+\cdots+x_{s}\Delta_{{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}

for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
.
∎

Another important case is when the global Newton-Okounkov body is a polyhedral cone. In this case, the multiplicity function is a piecewise polynomial with respect to some fan supported on the positive orthant.

Proposition 4.14
.

Assume that the global Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A)
is a polyhedral cone. Then there exists a fan
\Sigma
supported on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
such that the function
F_{A}(x)
is polynomial at each cone of
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Indeed, by
[
21
, Proposition 4.1]
, for any polyhedral cone
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}
with
\pi_{2}(C)=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
, the family
\{C\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(x)\}_{x\in C}
of polytopes
is piecewise linear with respect to some fan
\Sigma
supported on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
. Therefore, the proposition follows from Minkowski’s theorem.
∎

Example 4.15
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[u]
be a polynomial ring and consider the polynomial ring
R[t_{1},t_{2}]
.
Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function defined by

f(x_{1},x_{2})=\min(x_{1},x_{2}).

We define a family of vector spaces:

[A]_{(n_{1},n_{2})}=\left(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{f(n_{1},n_{2})}\mathbb{k}u^{i}\right)t_{1}^{n_{1}}t_{2}^{n_{2}}\subset\Big[R[t_{1},t_{2}]\Big]_{(n_{1},n_{2})}

The algebra
A=\bigoplus_{(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\subset R[t_{1},t_{2}]
is naturally an
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-graded algebra. The global Newton-Okounkov body of
A
is a cone
C
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
generated by vectors
(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(1,1,1)
and, as in
§4.1
,

F_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})=f(x_{1},x_{2})=\min(x_{1},x_{2}).

5.
Multigraded algebras of almost integral type with decomposable grading

In this section, we introduce and study the notion of mixed multiplicities for certain multigraded algebras of almost integral type.
We treat a family of algebras that we call
algebras with decomposable grading
.
Our approach is inspired by the methods used in
[
7
,
6
,
10
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

An
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
A
is said to have a
decomposable grading
if we have the equality

[A]_{(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{s})}=[A]_{n_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}\cdot[A]_{n_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}\cdot\,\cdots \,\cdot[A]_{n_{s}{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}

for all
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
.

We now proceed to define the mixed multiplicities of a multigraded algebra
A
of almost integral type with decomposable grading.
Here we extend
Section 4
: our approach relies on showing that the corresponding function
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
coincides with a polynomial
G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
when
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.
We use this polynomial to define the mixed multiplicities of
A
.
For the rest of this section we use the following setting.

Setup 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R
be a
\mathbb{k}
-domain.
Let
A\subset R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}]
be an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of almost integral type with decomposable grading.
Set
d=\dim(A)
and
q=d-s
.
We assume that
[A]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.

For each
p\geq 1
, let

\widehat{A}_{[p]}\;:=\;\mathbb{k}\left[[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}\right]\subset A

be the
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra generated by
[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\ldots,[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}
, and denote by
\widetilde{A}_{[p]}:=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\left[\widehat{A}_{[p]}\right]_{p{\mathbf{n}}}
the standard
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra obtained by regrading
\widehat{A}_{[p]}
.
For
p\gg 0
,
Theorem 2.5
and the fact that
A
has a decomposable grading imply that
\dim\big(\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)=d
.
Then, by
Section 4
the function
F_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})
coincides with the homogeneous polynomial

(10)

G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})=\sum_{|{\mathbf{d}}|=q}\,\frac{1}{{\mathbf{d}}!}e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)\,{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
, where
q=d-s
and
e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)
denotes the mixed multiplicity of
\widetilde{A}_{[p]}
of type
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
.

For each
a\geq 1
, we consider the
a
-truncation

G^{[a]}(A):=\mathbb{k}\left[\cup_{i=1}^{s}\cup_{j=1}^{a}[A]_{j{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\right]\subset A
of
A
which is the subalgebra generated by the graded components
[A]_{j{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}
with
1\leq i\leq s,1\leq j\leq a
.
The next proposition says that the
a
-truncations can be used to approximate
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume
Section 5
.
For each
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
, we have the equality

F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\lim_{a\to\infty}F_{G^{[a]}(A)}({\mathbf{n}}).

Proof.

Fix
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.
To simplify notation, set
C=A^{({\mathbf{n}})}
and
D^{a}=\left(G^{[a]}(A)\right)^{({\mathbf{n}})}
.
Note that
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([C]_{n})}{n^{q}}
and
F_{G^{[a]}(A)}({\mathbf{n}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([D^{a}]_{n})}{n^{q}}
.
By following the same steps as in
§3.1
, we can define strongly non-negative semigroups

\Gamma_{C}^{(t)}\,\subset\,\mathbb{N}^{r+1}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\Gamma_{D^{a}}^{(t)}\,\subset\,\mathbb{N}^{r+1},

such that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([C]_{n}\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{\ell_{C}}\#\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{C}\right]_{n}
and
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([D^{a}]_{n}\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{\ell_{D^{a}}}\#\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{D^{a}}\right]_{n}
.
Consider the integer
a^{\prime}=\lfloor a/\max\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}\}\rfloor
(where
\lfloor\beta\rfloor
denotes the floor function of
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
) and the inclusions

n\star\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{C}\right]_{a^{\prime}}\;\subset\;\left[\Gamma_{D^{a}}^{(t)}\right]_{na^{\prime}}\;\subset\;\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{C}\right]_{na^{\prime}}.

Then,
Theorem 2.2
implies that

\lim_{a\to\infty}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\big[\Gamma_{D^{a}}^{(t)}\big]_{n}}{n^{q}}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\big[\Gamma_{C}^{(t)}\big]_{n}}{n^{q}},

and so the result follows.
∎

The following proposition deals with the case when
A
is also a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume
Section 5
with
A
being finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
.
For each
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
, we have the equality

F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{F_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}.

Proof.

Fix
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.
We define the graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra

B\,:=\,\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}[A]_{n{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdot[A]_{n{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdot\,\cdots\,\cdot[A]_{n{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}^{n_{s}}.

For each
p\geq 1
, let
C^{p}
be the graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra

C^{p}\,:=\,\Big(\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\Big)^{({\mathbf{n}})}\,=\,\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\widehat{A}_{[p]}\right]_{np{\mathbf{n}}}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}^{nn_{1}}\cdot[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}^{nn_{2}}\cdot\,\cdots\,\cdot[A]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}^{nn_{s}}.

Once again, we can define strongly non-negative semigroups
\Gamma_{B}^{(t)}\subset\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
and
\Gamma_{C^{p}}^{(t)}\subset\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([B]_{n}\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{\ell_{B}}\#\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{B}\right]_{n}
and
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([C^{p}]_{n}\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{\ell_{C^{p}}}\#\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{C^{p}}\right]_{n}
.

Since
\left([B]_{p}\right)^{n}=\left[C^{p}\right]_{n}\subset[B]_{np}
, we obtain the corresponding inclusions

n\star\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{B}\right]_{p}\;\subset\;\left[\Gamma_{C^{p}}^{(t)}\right]_{n}\;\subset\;\left[\Gamma^{(t)}_{B}\right]_{np}.

By
Theorem 2.2
, it follows that

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{p^{q}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\big[\Gamma_{C^{p}}^{(t)}\big]_{n}}{n^{q}}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\big[\Gamma_{B}^{(t)}\big]_{n}}{n^{q}}.

As a consequence, we get
\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{F_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([B]_{n}\big)}{n^{q}}
.
To finish the proof, it remains to show the equality
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([B]_{n}\big)}{n^{q}}
.

As
A
has a decomposable grading, the algebras
A^{({\mathbf{e}}_{1})},\ldots,A^{({\mathbf{e}}_{s})}
are also finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
.
Hence, by
[
15
, Lemma 13.10]
we can choose
h>0
such that

[A]_{nh{\mathbf{n}}}=[A]_{nn_{1}h{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}\cdot[A]_{nn_{2}h{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}\cdot\cdots\cdot[A]_{nn_{s}h{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}=[A]_{nh{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdot[A]_{nh{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdot\,\cdots\,\cdot[A]_{nh{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}^{n_{s}}=[B]_{nh}

for all
n\geq 0
.
We then obtain

F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([A]_{nh{\mathbf{n}}}\big)}{n^{q}h^{q}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([B]_{nh}\big)}{n^{q}h^{q}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([B]_{n}\big)}{n^{q}}.

So, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

The next theorem contains the main result of this section.
It shows that
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
is a polynomial like function when
A
has a decomposable grading.

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Then, there exists a homogeneous polynomial
G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{R}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}]
of degree
q
with non-negative real coefficients such that

F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\quad\text{ for all }\quad{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}.

Additionally, we have

G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\;=\;\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}.

Proof.

Fix
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.
For any
a\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
, we have the following inequalities

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{F_{\widetilde{B^{a}}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\;\leq\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{F_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\;\leq\;F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})

where
B^{a}=G^{[a]}(A)
.
Therefore, by combining
Section 5
and
Section 5
, we obtain the equalities

F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{F_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\;=\;\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{F_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}.

From
Section 4
, the function
F_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})
coincides with the polynomial
G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})=\sum_{|{\mathbf{d}}|=q}\frac{1}{{\mathbf{d}}!}e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big){\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}
in
(10)
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.
It then necessarily follows that, for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
,
F_{A}({\mathbf{n}})=G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
where
G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{R}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}]
is the polynomial

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})&\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\;=\;\sum_{|{\mathbf{d}}|=q}\left(\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)}{p^{q}}\right)\,\frac{{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\\
&\;=\;\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{G_{\widetilde{A}_{[p]}}({\mathbf{n}})}{p^{q}}\;=\;\sum_{|{\mathbf{d}}|=q}\left(\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)}{p^{q}}\right)\,\frac{{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\end{split}

(see, e.g.,
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
).
So, the result of the theorem follows.
∎

Below we have an example of a family of algebras with decomposable grading.

Example 5.6
.

Let
G
be a complex semisimple group and let
B\subset G
be a Borel subgroup with a character lattice
M
. Denote by
A
the Cox ring of
G/B
, i.e.

A=\bigoplus_{L\in{\text{Pic}}(G/B)}\text{H}^{0}(G/B,L),

with a product induced by the natural maps:

\text{H}^{0}(G/B,L_{1})\otimes\text{H}^{0}(G/B,L_{2})\to\text{H}^{0}(G/B,L_{1}\otimes L_{2}).

The Cox ring
A
is naturally an algebra graded by the Picard group
{\text{Pic}}(G/B)
of the flag variety
G/B
. By Borel’s theorem,
{\text{Pic}}(G/B)
is isomorphic to
M
, for a character
\lambda\in M
, we will denote by
L_{\lambda}\in{\text{Pic}}(G/B)
the corresponding line bundle. Moreover, by Borel-Weil theorem one has

\text{H}^{0}(G/B,L_{\lambda})=\begin{cases}V_{\lambda}\quad\text{ if }\lambda\text{ is a dominant weight}\\
0\quad\;\;\;\;\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore, the Cox ring
A
of
G/B
is given by

A=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}}V_{\lambda},

where
\Lambda^{+}
is the positive Weyl chamber, i.e., the direct sum is over dominant weights.

The product maps
[A]_{\lambda}\otimes[A]_{\mu}\to[A]_{\lambda+\mu}
can be described in the following way. The tensor product
[A]_{\lambda}\otimes[A]_{\mu}=V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}
decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible representations of
G
with
V_{\lambda+\mu}
appearing with multiplicity one. Then the multiplication map
[A]_{\lambda}\otimes[A]_{\mu}\to[A]_{\lambda+\mu}
is the projection on
V_{\lambda+\mu}
in the above decomposition. In particular, we have

[A]_{\sum n_{i}\omega_{i}}=[A]_{n_{1}\omega_{1}}\cdot\,\cdots\,\cdot[A]_{n_{s}\omega_{s}},

where
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{s}
are fundamental weights of
G
.
Therefore, the Cox ring
A
has decomposable grading and by
Theorem 5.5
the volume function
F_{A}
is a polynomial.

Finally the global Newton-Okounkov body of
A
has a nice description. By
[
22
]
there exists a valuation
\nu
on
A
such that for any dominant weight
\lambda
, the Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A^{(\lambda)})
is the string polytope
St_{\lambda}
. Therefore, the global Newton-Okounkov body
\Delta(A)
is the weighted string cone, which is in particular a polyhedral cone (
[
26
,
1
]
).

Note that, in general, string polytopes
St_{\lambda}
provide only piecewise linear family of polytopes on the positive Weyl chamber. So a priory by
§4.1
, the function
F_{A}
is only piecewise polynomial with respect to some fan decomposition of the positive Weyl chamber. However, using virtual polytopes, one can construct a linear family of
virtual string polytopes
which makes the polynomiality of
F_{A}
evident. See
[
19
, Section 10]
for more details.

With
Theorem 5.5
in hand we are now able to define the mixed multiplicities of
A
.

Definition 5.7
.

Assume
Section 5
and let
G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})
be as in
Theorem 5.5
.
Write

G_{A}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\sum_{|{\mathbf{d}}|=q}\frac{1}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\,e({\mathbf{d}};A)\,{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}.

For each
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=q
, we define the non-negative real number

e({\mathbf{d}};A)\geq 0

to be the
mixed multiplicity of type
{\mathbf{d}}
of
A
.

The next straightforward corollary shows that the mixed multiplicities
e({\mathbf{d}};A)
of
A
can be expressed as a limit that depends on the multiplicities
e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)
of the standard multigraded algebras
\widetilde{A}_{[p]}
.
It can be seen as an extension into a multigraded setting of the Fujita approximation theorem for graded algebras given in
[
20
, Theorem 2.35]
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Then, the following equalities hold

e({\mathbf{d}};A)\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)}{p^{q}}\;=\;\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{e\big({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\big)}{p^{q}}.

Proof.

It follows directly from
(11)
.
∎

Finally, we provide a complete characterization for the positivity of the mixed multiplicities of a multigraded algebra of almost integral type with decomposable grading.
This result is a direct consequence of
Section 5
and the general criterion of
[
5
]
.

Following the notation of
[
5
]
, for an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
T
and for each subset
\mathfrak{J}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
denote by
T_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
the
\mathbb{N}^{k}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra given by

T_{({\mathfrak{J}})}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,i_{s}\geq 0\\
i_{j}=0\text{ if }j\not\in{\mathfrak{J}}\end{subarray}}{\left[T\right]}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s})}\;\subset\;T.

We obtain a full characterization for the positivity of the mixed multiplicities
e({\mathbf{d}},A)
of
A
in terms of the dimensions
\dim\left(A_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)
.

Theorem 5.9
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
such that
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=q
.
Then,
e({\mathbf{d}},A)>0
if and only if for each
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
the inequality

d_{j_{1}}+\cdots+d_{j_{k}}\;\leq\;\dim\left(A_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)-k

holds.

Proof.

For each
p\geq 1
,
[
5
, Theorem B]
characterizes the positivity of the mixed multiplicities of the standard
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
\widetilde{A}_{[p]}
, namely:

e\left({\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\right)>0\;\;\,\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\,d_{j_{1}}+\cdots+d_{j_{k}}\;\leq\;\dim\left(\left(\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\right)_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)-k\;\text{ for each }\;{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}.

Since
A
is an algebra with decomposable grading, by
Theorem 2.5
, we can choose
p
big enough such that

\dim\left(A_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)\;=\;\dim\left(\left(\widetilde{A}_{[p]}\right)_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)

for all
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
Therefore, the result follows from
Section 5
.
∎

6.
Application to graded families of ideals

In this section, we apply the results of
Section 5
to the case of graded families of ideals and we recover some results from
[
7
,
6
,
10
]
.
We also obtain a characterization for the positivity of mixed multiplicities of certain graded families of ideals.
First, we recall the notion of mixed multiplicities introduced by Bhattacharya in
[
2
]
for the case of ideals, and extended for (not necessarily Noetherian) graded families of ideals in
[
7
]
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
\mathbb{k}
-domain.
We denote the graded irrelevant ideal of
R
by
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}[R]_{n}\subset R
.
Assume that
R
has positive dimension
d=\dim(R)>0
.

Remark 6.1
.

Let
I,J_{1},\ldots,J_{s}
be non-zero homogeneous ideals in
R
such that
I
is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary.
Then, for
n_{0}\gg 0
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\gg\mathbf{0}
the Bhattacharya function
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}(I^{n_{0}}J_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots J_{s}^{n_{s}}/I^{n_{0}+1}J_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots J_{s}^{n_{s}})
coincides with a polynomial of total degree
d-1
whose homogeneous term in degree
d-1
can be written as

B_{(I;J_{1},\ldots,J_{s})}(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\;:=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d_{0},\mathbf{d})=(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}\\
d_{0}+|\mathbf{d}|=d-1\end{subarray}}\frac{e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}(I\mid J_{1},\ldots,J_{s})}{d_{0}!d_{1}!\cdots d_{s}!}n_{0}^{d_{0}}n_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots,n_{s}^{d_{s}}.

Using standard techniques (see
[
6
, proof of Lemma 4.2]
), one may show that for each
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(J_{1}^{nn_{1}}\cdots J_{s}^{nn_{s}}/I^{nn_{0}}J_{1}^{nn_{1}}\cdots J_{s}^{nn_{s}}\right)}{n^{d}}

exists and coincides with the following polynomial

G_{(I;J_{1},\ldots,J_{s})}(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\;:=\;\sum_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})\in\mathbb{N}^{s+1},d_{0}+|\mathbf{d}|=d-1}\frac{e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}(I\mid J_{1},\ldots,J_{s})}{(d_{0}+1)!d_{1}!\cdots d_{s}!}\,n_{0}^{d_{0}+1}n_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots n_{s}^{d_{s}}.

The numbers
e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}(I\mid J_{1},\ldots,J_{s})
are non-negative integers called the
mixed multiplicities
of
J_{1},\ldots,J_{s}
with respect to
I
.

A sequence of ideals
\mathbb{I}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
graded family
if
I_{0}=R
and
I_{i}I_{j}\subseteq I_{i+j}
for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.
The graded family is
Noetherian
if the corresponding Rees algebra
R[\mathbb{I}t]=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n}t^{n}\subseteq R[t]
is Noetherian.
The graded family is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary
when each
I_{n}
is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary, and it is a
filtration
when
I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a homogeneous ideal
J\subset R
we denote
{\rm maxdeg}(J):=\max\{j\mid\left[J\otimes_{R}R/{\mathfrak{m}}\right]_{j}\neq 0\}
, that is, the maximum degree of a minimal set of homogeneous generators of
J
.

Throughout this section, we assume the following setup.

Setup 6.2
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
\mathbb{k}
-domain.
Let
\mathbb{I}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a (not necessarily Noetherian) graded family of
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary homogeneous ideals in
R
.
Let
\mathbb{J}(1)=\{J(1)_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\ldots
,
\mathbb{J}(s)=\{J(s)_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be (not necessarily Noetherian) graded families of non-zero homogeneous ideals in
R
.
We assume that there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

(12)

{\rm maxdeg}(J(i)_{n})\leq\beta n\quad\text{for all}\quad 1\leq i\leq s\ \ \text{and}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Remark 6.3
.

The condition
(12)
is automatically satisfied in the case of
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary graded families of ideals.
Similar assumptions to the one in
(12)
have been considered in previous works regarding limits of graded families of ideals
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
.

To extend the discussions of
Section 6
, we need to study the following function

(13)

F_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;:=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(J(1)_{nn_{1}}\cdots J(s)_{nn_{s}}/I_{nn_{0}}J(1)_{nn_{1}}\cdots J(s)_{nn_{s}}\right)}{n^{d}}

for all
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{s}
.
For a vector
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
, we abbreviate
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{n}}=J(1)_{n_{1}}\cdots J(s)_{n_{s}}
.
By
[
6
, Lemma 3.9]
, there exists
c>\beta
such that

{\mathfrak{m}}^{c(n_{0}+|{\mathbf{n}}|)}\,\cap\,\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{n}}\;=\;{\mathfrak{m}}^{c(n_{0}+|{\mathbf{n}}|)}\,\cap\,I_{n_{0}}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{n}}

for all
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
.
Then, we have the equality

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;:=\;&\lim_{n\to\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\mathbf{J}_{n{\mathbf{n}}}/\big({\mathfrak{m}}^{cn(n_{0}+|{\mathbf{n}}|)+1}\,\cap\,\mathbf{J}_{n{\mathbf{n}}}\big)\right)\big/n^{d}\\
&\qquad\quad-\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(I_{nn_{0}}\mathbf{J}_{n{\mathbf{n}}}/\big({\mathfrak{m}}^{cn(n_{0}+|{\mathbf{n}}|)+1}\,\cap\,I_{nn_{0}}\mathbf{J}_{n{\mathbf{n}}}\big)\right)\big/n^{d}\end{split}

for all
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
.
We consider the multi-Rees algebras

\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{I},\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s)):=\bigoplus_{n_{0}\in\mathbb{N},{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}I_{n_{0}}\mathbf{J}_{{\mathbf{n}}}t_{0}^{n_{0}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{R},\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s)):=\bigoplus_{n_{0}\in\mathbb{N},{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\mathbf{J}_{{\mathbf{n}}}t_{0}^{n_{0}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}

where
{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}=t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{s}^{n_{s}}
and
\mathfrak{R}
is the trivial filtration of identity ideals, i.e.,
\mathfrak{R}=\{\mathfrak{R}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\mathfrak{R}_{n}=R
.
We have the corresponding
\mathbb{N}^{s+1}
-graded subalgebras

A\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n_{0}\in\mathbb{N},{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{c(n_{0}+|{\mathbf{n}}|)}{\big[I_{n_{0}}\mathbf{J}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\big]}_{k}\right)t_{0}^{n_{0}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\;\subset\;\;\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{I},\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))

and

B\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n_{0}\in\mathbb{N},{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{c(n_{0}+|{\mathbf{n}}|)}{\big[\mathbf{J}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\big]}_{k}\right)t_{0}^{n_{0}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\;\subset\;\;\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{R},\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s)).

Notice that both
A
and
B
are of almost integral type and have decomposable gradings.
Since
\dim(A)=\dim(B)=d+s+1
, we can rewrite
(14)
in terms of the volume functions of
A
and
B
(see
(9)
), that is

(15)

F_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;F_{B}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})-F_{A}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}}).

Therefore, as a simple consequence of
Theorem 5.5
we obtain the following result, which extends
Section 6
and allows us to define mixed multiplicities for graded families of ideals.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume
Section 6
.
Then, there exists a homogeneous polynomial
G_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{R}[n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}]
of degree
d
with non-negative real coefficients such that

F_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;G_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\quad\text{ for all }\quad n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\,{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s},

where
F_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})
is the function in
(13)
.
Additionally, we have

G_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{G_{(I_{p};J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})}{p^{d}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\,{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s},

where
G_{(I_{p};J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})
is the corresponding polynomial of the ideals
I_{p},J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p}
(as in
Section 6
).

Proof.

By using the equality
(15)
, the result follows by applying
Theorem 5.5
to the
\mathbb{N}^{s+1}
-graded algebras of almost integral type
A
and
B
that have decomposable grading.
∎

As a consequence of
Theorem 6.4
we have the following definition.

Definition 6.5
.

Assume
Section 6
and let
G_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})
be as in
Theorem 6.4
.
Write

G_{(\mathbb{I};\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;\sum_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})\in\mathbb{N}^{s+1},d_{0}+|\mathbf{d}|=d-1}\frac{e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}(\mathbb{I}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))}{(d_{0}+1)!d_{1}!\cdots d_{s}!}\,n_{0}^{d_{0}+1}{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}.

The numbers
e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}(\mathbb{I}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))
are non-negative integers called the
mixed multiplicities
of
\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s)
with respect to
\mathbb{I}
.

The following result recovers the “Volume = Multiplicity formula” for the mixed multiplicities of graded families of ideals (see
[
7
,
6
]
).

Corollary 6.6
.

Assume
Section 6
.
Then, the following equalities hold

e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}(\mathbb{I}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}(I_{p}\mid J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}{p^{d}}.

Proof.

It follows from
Theorem 6.4
and
Section 6
.
∎

6.1.
Positivity for graded families of equally generated ideals

Here, we restrict to the following graded families of ideals and we provide a criterion for the positivity of their mixed multiplicities.

Setup 6.7
.

Assume
Section 6
.
Let
\mathbb{M}
be the filtration
\mathbb{M}:=\{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
For each
1\leq i\leq s
, assume that there is an integer
h_{i}\geq 1
such that the ideal
J(i)_{n}
is generated in degree
nh_{i}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, that is,

J(i)_{n}\;=\;\left(\big[J(i)_{n}\big]_{nh_{i}}\right)\quad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Our focus is on characterizing the positivity of the mixed multiplicities
e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))
.
We define the following
\mathbb{N}^{s+1}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra

(16)

T\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n_{0},\ldots,n_{s}\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}}J(1)_{n_{1}}\cdots J(s)_{n_{s}}\Big/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}+1}J(1)_{n_{1}}\cdots J(s)_{n_{s}}.

Since each ideal
J(i)_{n_{i}}
is generated in degree
n_{i}h_{i}
, by Nakayama’s lemma we get the isomorphism

T\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{n_{0},\ldots,n_{s}\in\mathbb{N}}\Big[{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}}\Big]_{n_{0}}\Big[J(1)_{n_{1}}\Big]_{n_{1}h_{1}}\cdots\Big[J(s)_{n_{s}}\Big]_{n_{s}h_{s}}\quad\subset\quad\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{M},\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s)).

As a consequence we obtain that
T
is a
\mathbb{k}
-domain of almost integral type with decomposable grading, and so by
Theorem 5.5
and
Section 5
we can consider its mixed multiplicities
e\big(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};T\big)
for
d_{0}\in\mathbb{N},{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
.
We have that
\dim(T)=\dim(R)+s
.
The following proposition shows that the mixed multiplicities of
T
coincide with the mixed multiplicities
e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))
.

Proposition 6.8
.

For each
d_{0}\in\mathbb{N},\,{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
with
d_{0}+|{\mathbf{d}}|=d-1
, we have the equality

e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))\;=\;e\big(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};T\big).

Proof.

For each
p\geq 1
, we define the following standard
\mathbb{N}^{s+1}
-graded algebra

C^{p}\;=\;\bigoplus_{n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}\in\mathbb{N}^{s+1}}{\Big[\mathbb{k}\big[[T]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{0}},[T]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\cdots,[T]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}\big]\Big]}_{(n_{0},n_{1}p,\ldots,n_{s}p)}

(i.e., the regrading of the algebra generated by the graded parts
[T]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{0}},[T]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\cdots,[T]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}
).
Notice that the polynomial
G_{C^{p}}
of
C^{p}
(see
Section 4
) equals the polynomial
B_{({\mathfrak{m}};J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}
corresponding to the ideals
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p}
(see
Section 6
), that is,

G_{C^{p}}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;B_{({\mathfrak{m}};J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}}).

Hence
e\left(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};C^{p}\right)=e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}\left({\mathfrak{m}}\mid J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p}\right)
.

Since the following polynomials are equal

G_{C^{p}}(pn_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;G_{\widetilde{T}_{[p]}}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}}),

by comparing the coefficients, we obtain
e\big(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{T}_{[p]}\big)=p^{d_{0}}e\left(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};C^{p}\right)
(recall that
\widetilde{T}_{[p]}
denotes the regrading of the algebra generated by the graded parts
[T]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{0}},[T]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}},\cdots,[T]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}}
).
Similarly, we have the equality of polynomials

G_{({\mathfrak{m}};J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}(pn_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\;=\;G_{({\mathfrak{m}}^{p};J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})\qquad(\text{see \autoref{rem_mixed_mult_ideals}})

that implies
e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}\left({\mathfrak{m}}^{p}\mid J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p}\right)=p^{d_{0}+1}e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}\left({\mathfrak{m}}\mid J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p}\right)
.

By combining the above equalities with
Section 5
and
Section 6
, we get

\displaystyle e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))\;

\displaystyle=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}({\mathfrak{m}}^{p}\mid J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}{p^{d}}

\displaystyle=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}({\mathfrak{m}}\mid J(1)_{p},\ldots,J(s)_{p})}{p^{d-1-d_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e\left(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};C^{p}\right)}{p^{d-1-d_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{e\big(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{T}_{[p]}\big)}{p^{d-1}}

\displaystyle=\;e\big(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};T\big).

Therefore, the result follows.
∎

The
analytic spread
of an ideal
I\subset R
is given by
\ell(I):=\dim\Big(\mathcal{R}(I)/{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{R}(I)\Big)
, where
\mathcal{R}(I)
denotes the Rees algebra of
I
.
When the ideal
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{e})\subset R
is generated by homogeneous elements of the same degree, one has that
\mathcal{R}(I)/{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{R}(I)\cong\mathbb{k}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{e}]
and so
\ell(I)=\dim\left(\mathbb{k}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{e}]\right)
.
The following positivity criterion extends the result of
[
5
, Theorem 4.4]
.
It characterizes the positivity of the mixed multiplicities
e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Assume
§6.1
.
Let
d_{0}\in\mathbb{N},\,{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
such that
d_{0}+\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=d-1
.
Then,

e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))>0

if and only if for all
p\gg 0
and
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
the inequality

d_{j_{1}}+\cdots+d_{j_{k}}\;\leq\;\ell\Big(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}\Big)-1

holds.

Proof.

By using
§6.1
and
Section 5
, to show
e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))>0
, it is enough to show that for
p\gg 0
one has

e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}\big({\mathfrak{m}}\mid J(j_{1})_{p},\ldots,J(j_{k})_{p}\big)=e\big(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}};\widetilde{T}_{[p]}\big)>0.

For any
p>0
,
[
5
, Theorem 4.4]
implies that the inequality
e_{(d_{0},\mathbf{d})}\big({\mathfrak{m}}\mid J(j_{1})_{p},\ldots,J(j_{k})_{p}\big)>0
is equivalent to the condition of having
d_{j_{1}}+\cdots+d_{j_{k}}\;\leq\;\ell\Big(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}\Big)-1

for all
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
So, the result follows.
∎

6.2.
Positivity for mixed volumes of convex bodies

In this subsection, by exploiting the known close relation between mixed multiplicities and mixed volumes (see
[
33
,
6
]
), we provide a positivity criteria for mixed volumes.
Following the notation of
[
6
]
, we use the setup below.

Setup 6.10
.

Let
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{s})
be a sequence of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d}
.
Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R
be the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d+1}]
, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d+1}\right)
.
Let

\pi_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\,(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d},\alpha_{d+1})\mapsto(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
be the projection into the first
d
factors.
Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the linear map

\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d},\alpha_{d+1})\mapsto\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}+\alpha_{d+1}.
For
1\leq i\leq s
, choose
h_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
a positive integer such that
K_{i}\subset\pi_{1}\big(\pi^{-1}(h_{i})\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}\big)
.
The corresponding
homogenization
of
K_{i}
(with respect to
h_{i}
) is defined as the convex body

\widetilde{K_{i}}\;:=\;\left(K_{i}\times\mathbb{R}\right)\cap\pi^{-1}(h_{i})\;\subset\;\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}.

Let
C_{K_{i}}
be the corresponding cone
C_{K_{i}}:=\text{Cone}(\widetilde{K_{i}})
.
Consider the semigroup
S_{K_{i}}\subset\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
given by

S_{K_{i}}:=C_{K_{i}}\cap\mathbb{N}^{d+1}\cap\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi^{-1}\left(kh_{i}\right)\right).

We consider the (not necessarily Noetherian) graded family of monomial ideals

\mathbb{J}(i)\;:=\;\{J(i)_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\quad\text{where }\quad J(i)_{n}\;:=\;\Big({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}\mid{\mathbf{m}}\in S_{K_{i}}\text{ and }\lvert{\mathbf{m}}\rvert=nh_{i}\Big)\subset R.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be the filtration
\mathbb{M}:=\{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

As a direct consequence of our previous developments, we recover a classical criterion for the positivity of mixed volumes (see
[
31
, Theorem 5.1.8]
).

Theorem 6.11
.

Assume
§6.2
.
Let
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
such that
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=d
.
Then,
{\text{MV}}_{d}\left(\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{d}}}\right)>0
if and only if for each
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
the inequality

d_{j_{1}}+\cdots+d_{j_{k}}\;\leq\;\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}K_{j_{i}}\right)

holds.

Proof.

From
[
6
, Theorem 5.4]
we have that
{\text{MV}}_{d}\left(\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{d}}}\right)=e_{(0,{\mathbf{d}})}(\mathbb{M}\mid\mathbb{J}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{J}(s))
.
Hence,
Theorem 6.9
implies that
{\text{MV}}_{d}\left(\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{d}}}\right)>0
if and only if for all
p\gg 0
and
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
the inequality

d_{j_{1}}+\cdots+d_{j_{k}}\;\leq\;\ell\Big(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}\Big)-1

holds.
For each ideal
J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}
there corresponds the convex body

K\big(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}\big)\;:=\;\pi_{1}\left(\text{conv}\Big\{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}\mid{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}\in\big[J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}\big]_{ph_{j_{1}}+\cdots+ph_{j_{k}}}\Big\}\right)\;\subset\;\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d}.

Notice that
\ell(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p})-1=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(K(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}))
.
For
p\gg 0
, one obtains the equality
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(K(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p}))=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\sum_{i=1}^{k}K_{j_{i}})
, as the convex bodies
K(J(j_{1})_{p}\cdots J(j_{k})_{p})
approximate the Minkowski sum
\sum_{i=1}^{k}K_{j_{i}}
.
Therefore, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

7.
Multigraded linear series

In this section, we apply our main results from the previous sections to the case of multigraded linear series.
Here, we extend the results of
[
24
]
on the multigraded linear series.
Our main improvement is the fact that we deal with valuations with leaves of bounded dimension instead of restricting to valuations with only one-dimensional leaves.
Furthermore, with the family of multigraded linear series with decomposable grading, we provide an interesting family for which the volume function is a polynomial.

Following the notation of
[
17
]
, we say that
X
is a
variety
over a field
\mathbb{k}
if
X
is a reduced and irreducible separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{k}
.

Throughout this section the following setup is used.

Setup 7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
X
be a proper variety over
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
be a sequence of Cartier divisors on
X
.

We consider the section ring of the divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
, which given by

\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})\;:=\;\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}(n_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}D_{s})\big).

Notice that
\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
is by construction an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
To simplify notation, for any
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
, we denote the divisor
n_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}D_{s}
by

{\mathbf{n}}D\;:=\;n_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}D_{s}.

The following basic result shows that the section ring of
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
is an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra of almost integral type (in the sense of
Section 4
).
For the single graded case, see
[
20
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Proposition 7.2
.

\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
is an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra of almost integral type.

Proof.

First, by Chow’s lemma (see, e.g.
[
15
, Theorem 13.100]
or
[
17
, Exercise II.4.10]
), there exists a proper birational morphism
\pi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
where
X^{\prime}
is a normal projective variety over
\mathbb{k}
.
Since for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
we have
\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}({\mathbf{n}}D)\big)\hookrightarrow\text{H}^{0}\big(X^{\prime},\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}({\mathbf{n}}D)\big)
, it suffices to assume that
X
is a normal projective variety over
\mathbb{k}
, and we do so.

We can find a very ample divisor
H
on
X
such that
D_{i}\leq H
, and so
\mathcal{O}({\mathbf{n}}D)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(|{\mathbf{n}}|H)
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
(see, e.g.,
[
25
, Example 1.2.10]
and
[
20
, Theorem 3.9]
).
From the fact that the section ring
\mathcal{S}(H)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(nH))
is of integral type (see
[
17
, Exercise II.5.14]
), we obtain that
\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
is an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra of almost integral type.
∎

Here, we study multigraded linear series as defined below.

Definition 7.3
.

A
multigraded linear series
associated to the divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
is an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-subalgebra
W
of the section ring
\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
.

Let
W\subseteq\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
be a multigraded linear series and suppose that
[W]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.
By
Section 7
, it follows that
W
is an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra of almost integral type.
The
Kodaira-Itaka dimension
of
W
is denoted and given by

\kappa(W)\;:=\;\dim(W)-s,

where as before
\dim(W)
denotes the Krull dimension of the
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra
W
of almost integral type.
This value was the correct asymptotic for the volume function of an
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra of almost integral type (see
(9)
).
Additionally, notice that this agrees with the definition used in
[
9
, Section 7]
for the case of singly graded linear series.
Following
§4.1
, let
\Delta(W)
be the global Newton-Okounkov body of
W
.
As in
§4.1
, consider the integers
{\text{ind}}(W)
and
\ell_{W}
.

Our main result regarding multigraded linear series is the theorem below, and it follows rather easily from our previous developments.

Theorem 7.4
.

Assume
Section 7
.
Let
W\subset\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
be a multigraded linear series and suppose that
[W]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

The volume function

F_{W}({\mathbf{n}})\;:=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big([W]_{n{\mathbf{n}}}\big)}{n^{\kappa(W)}}

of
W
is well-defined for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.

(ii)

There exists a unique continuous function that is homogeneous of degree
\kappa(W)
and log-concave and that extends the volume function
F_{W}({\mathbf{n}})
of part (i) to the positive orthant
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}
.
This function is given by

F_{W}\,:\,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{s}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad x\,\mapsto\,\ell_{W}\cdot\frac{{\text{Vol}}_{\kappa(W)}\big(\Delta(W)_{x}\big)}{{\text{ind}}(W)}.

(iii)

If
W
has a decomposable grading, then there exists a homogeneous polynomial
G_{W}({\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{R}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}]
of degree
\kappa(W)
with non-negative real coefficients such that

F_{W}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;G_{W}({\mathbf{n}})

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
.

Proof.

(i) The function is obtained as in
(9)
.

(ii) It follows from
§4.1
.

(iii) It follows from
Theorem 5.5
.
∎

For a closed subscheme
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{s}}
of a multiprojective space over
\mathbb{k}
, we can consider the
multidegrees
of
Y
.
These fundamental invariants go back to the work of van der Waerden
[
34
]
.
If
S
is a standard
\mathbb{N}^{s}
-graded algebra that coincides with the multihomogeneous coordinate ring of
Y
, then for each
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=\dim(Y)
, one way of defining the multidegree of
Y
of type
{\mathbf{d}}
is by setting

\deg({\mathbf{d}};Y)\;:=\;e({\mathbf{d}};S).

The following theorem is an extension of
[
20
, Theorem 3.3]
to a multigraded setting.
It expresses the mixed multiplicities of a multigraded linear series with decomposable grading in terms of the multidegrees of the image of the corresponding Kodaira rational maps.

Theorem 7.5
.

Assume
Section 7
.
Let
W\subset\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
be a multigraded linear series with decomposable grading and suppose that
[W]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. For each
p\geq 1
, consider the corresponding Kodaira rational map

\Pi_{[W]_{(p,\ldots,p)}}\;:\;X\;\dashrightarrow\;{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([W]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{1}})-1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([W]_{p{\mathbf{e}}_{s}})-1}.

Let
Y_{[W]_{(p,\ldots,p)}}
be the closure of the image of
\Pi_{[W]_{(p,\ldots,p)}}
.
Then, for each
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=\kappa(W)
, we have the following equalities

e({\mathbf{d}};W)\;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\deg\big({\mathbf{d}};Y_{[W]_{(p,\ldots,p)}}\big)}{p^{\kappa(W)}}\;=\;\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{\deg\big({\mathbf{d}};Y_{[W]_{(p,\ldots,p)}}\big)}{p^{\kappa(W)}}.

Proof.

The result follows by applying
Section 5
to the multigraded linear series
W
that has a decomposable grading.
∎

Finally, below we have an example where the section ring has a decomposable grading.

Example 7.6
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective algebraic curve of genus
g
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{k}
. Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
be divisors on
C
with
\deg(D_{i})\geq 2g+1
. Then by
[
28
, Theorem 6]
, the tensor product map

\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}(n_{1}D_{1})\big)\otimes\cdots\otimes\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}(n_{s}D_{s})\big)\to\text{H}^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}(n_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}D_{s})\big)

is surjective. Hence, the multigraded linear series
W=\mathcal{S}(D_{1},\ldots,D_{s})
has a decomposable grading and by part (iii) of
Theorem 7.4
the function
F_{W}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\kappa(W)
.

Finally, we would like to remark that the conditions on
C
and
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
can be relaxed slightly by considering generalizations of Mumford’s theorem, see
[
16
,
12
,
4
]
for details.

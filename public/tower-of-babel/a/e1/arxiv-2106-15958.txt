Quadratic non-stochastic operators: Examples of splitted chaos
U.A. Rozikov, S.S. Xudayarov

Abstract
. There is one-to-one correspondence between quadratic operators (mapping ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} to itself) and cubic matrices. It is known that any quadratic operator corresponding to a stochastic (in a fixed sense) cubic matrix preserves the standard simplex. In this paper we find conditions on the (non-stochastic) cubic matrix ensuring that corresponding quadratic operator preserves simplex. Moreover, we construct several quadratic non-stochastic operators which generate chaotic dynamical systems on the simplex. These chaotic behaviors are splitted meaning that the simplex is partitioned into uncountably many invariant (with respect to quadratic operator) subsets and the restriction of the dynamical system on each invariant set is chaos in the sense of Devaney.

2.
Quadratic non-stochastic operators

The following theorem gives conditions for coefficients of
V
to preserve the simplex.

Theorem 1
.

For a quadratic operator
V
(given by (
1.5
)), to preserve a simplex
S^{m-1}
it is
sufficient
that

i)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}P_{ij,k}=1,\,\,\ i,j=1,\dots,m;

ii)

0\leq P_{ii,k}\leq 1,\,\,\ i,k=1,\dots,m;

iii)

-{1\over m-1}\sqrt{P_{ii,k}P_{jj,k}}\leq P_{ij,k}.

and
necessary
that the conditions i), ii) and

iii’)

-\sqrt{P_{ii,k}P_{jj,k}}\leq P_{ij,k}\leq 1+\sqrt{(1-P_{ii,k})(1-P_{jj,k})}

are satisfied.

Proof.

Sufficiency.
Let
x\in S^{m-1}
. We show that
x^{\prime}=V(x)=(x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{m}^{\prime})\in S^{m-1}
.
Using condition i) we get

\sum_{k=1}^{m}x^{\prime}_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{m}P_{ij,k}x_{i}x_{j}=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}P_{ij,k}\right)x_{i}x_{j}

=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{m}x_{i}x_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}=1,\ \ \forall k=1,\dots,m.

The condition ii) is needed to have
V(e_{i})\in S^{m-1}
, for vertices
e_{i}=(0,...,0,1,0,...0)
(here 1 is the
i
th coordinate).
Because in this case
V(e_{i})=(P_{ii,1},\dots,P_{ii,m}).

Now using iii) we show that
x_{k}^{\prime}\geq 0
:

x^{\prime}_{k}=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{m}P_{ij,k}x_{i}x_{j}=\sum_{i<j}\left({P_{ii,k}\over m-1}x_{i}^{2}+2P_{ij,k}x_{i}x_{j}+{P_{jj,k}\over m-1}x_{j}^{2}\right)

\geq\sum_{i<j}\left({P_{ii,k}\over m-1}x_{i}^{2}-{2\over m-1}\sqrt{P_{ii,k}P_{jj,k}}x_{i}x_{j}+{P_{jj,k}\over m-1}x_{j}^{2}\right)

=\sum_{i<j}\left(\sqrt{P_{ii,k}\over m-1}x_{i}-\sqrt{P_{jj,k}\over m-1}x_{j}\right)^{2}\geq 0.

Therefore the quadratic operator
V
preserves the simplex.

Definition 1
.

A quadratic operator (
1.5
), preserving a simplex, is called non-stochastic
if at least one of its coefficients

Definition 2
.

Let

be a quadratic non-stochastic operator (QnSO). It is called a Volterra QnSO (VQnVO) if

iv)

P_{ij,k}=0,
for all

Theorem 2
.

If for a quadratic operator the conditions i)-iii) of Theorem
1
are satisfied then it is not a VQnSO.

Proof.

Assume i)-iii) are satisfied then we get

P_{kk,k}=1,\ \ \ P_{ki,k}+P_{ik,k}=1.

P_{ii,k}=0,\,\,\ i\neq k.

Moreover, by iii) we have

-{1\over m-1}\sqrt{P_{ii,k}}\sqrt{P_{kk,k}}\leq P_{ik,k}\quad\Rightarrow 0\leq P_{ik,k},\,\ i\neq k.

Similarly we get

. Thus all coefficients of Volterra quadratic operator are non-negative. Hence it is not VQnSO.

The Eisenstein cycles and Manin-Drinfeld properties
Debargha Banerjee, Loïc Merel

Abstract
. Let Γ \Gamma be a subgroup of finite index of SL 2 ​ ( 𝐙 ) {\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\bf Z}) . We give an analytic criterion for a cuspidal divisor to be torsion in the Jacobian J Γ J_{\Gamma} of the corresponding modular curve X Γ X_{\Gamma} . By Belyi’s theorem, such a criterion would apply to any curve over a number field. Our main tool is the explicit description, in terms of modular symbols, of what we call Eisenstein cycles . The latter are representations of relative homology classes over which integration of any holomorphic differential forms vanishes. Our approach relies in an essential way on the specific case Γ ⊂ Γ ⁡ ( 2 ) \Gamma\subset\Gamma(2) , where we can consider convenient generalized Jacobians instead of J Γ J_{\Gamma} . The Eisenstein classes are the real part of certain homology classes with complex coefficients. The imaginary part of those classes are related to the scattering constants attached to Eisenstein series. Finally, we illustrate our theory by considering Fermat curves.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a subroup of finite index of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\bf Z})
, acting on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
. Consider the modular curve
Y_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
. Let
\partial_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\bf Q})
be the set of cusps and
X_{\Gamma}
be the compact modular curve obtained from
Y_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
by adding the cusps.
We have a long exact sequence of relative homology:

0\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma};{\bf Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\delta}{\bf Z}[\partial_{\Gamma}]\rightarrow{\bf Z}\rightarrow 0,

where the map
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\delta}{\bf Z}[\partial_{\Gamma}]
is obtained from the boundary map
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{0}(\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf Z})
.
Let

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X_{\Gamma},\Omega^{1})

denote the complex vector space of global sections of the sheaf of holomorphic differentials on the Riemann surface
X_{\Gamma}
.
Let
D=\sum_{j\in\partial_{\Gamma}}m_{j}[j]
be a divisor of degree
0
supported on
\partial_{\Gamma}
.
We call
Eisenstein classes
corresponding to
D
to be the unique element
{\mathcal{E}}_{D}\in\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf R})
such that
\int_{{\mathcal{E}}_{D}}\omega=0
for all
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(X_{\Gamma},\Omega^{1})
and
\delta({\mathcal{E}}_{D})=D
. We will determine this class in this article, with the following application in mind: the divisor
D
is torsion in the Jacobian
J_{\Gamma}
of
X_{\Gamma}
if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}_{D}\in\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf Q})
.

It is known since Manin and Drinfeld that
D
is torsion when
\Gamma
is a congruence subgroup
[
9
,
16
]
. Scholl
[
23
]
, Murty-Ramakrishnan
[
19
]
and recently Burrin
[
5
]
have given criteria for
D
being torsion in
J_{\Gamma}
, without appealing to
{\mathcal{E}}_{D}
.
Our approach to this question is different from those authors and is a continuation of
[
4
]
and
[
17
]
(where we limited ourselves to congruence subgroups).

We impose a more specific setting (as we will see, without losing generality), in connection with the theory of Belyi maps and dessins d’enfants, see section
2
. We assume in this article that
\Gamma
is contained in
\Gamma(2)
(the principal congruence subgroup of level
2
) and that
-{\rm Id}\in\Gamma
. The corresponding modular curve
X(2)=X_{\Gamma(2)}
has three cusps corresponding to the classes of
0
,
1
and
\infty
. Denote by
\partial_{\Gamma}^{+}
(resp.
\partial_{\Gamma}^{-}
) the cusps above either
0
or
\infty
(resp. above
1
) via the canonical morphism
X_{\Gamma}\rightarrow X(2)
.

We reformulate the question of the Eisenstein cycles in the mixed homology group
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{-},\partial_{\Gamma}^{+};{\bf R})
and its dual
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{+},\partial_{\Gamma}^{-};{\bf R})
.
Let
\alpha
,
\beta
be rational numbers with odd numerators and denominators (resp. with either even numerator or even denominator). Denote by
{\{\alpha,\beta\}}^{-}
(resp.
\{\alpha,\beta\}^{+}
) the class in
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{+},\partial_{\Gamma}^{-};{\bf R})
(resp.
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{-},\partial_{\Gamma}^{+};{\bf R})
) of the geodesic path in
\mathbb{H}
from
\alpha
to
\beta
.
Such a consideration is justified by the fact that the elements
\{g0,g\infty\}^{+}
and
\{g(1),g(-1)\}^{-}
form bases of the free
{\bf Z}
-modules
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{-},\partial_{\Gamma}^{+};{\bf Z})
and
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{+},\partial_{\Gamma}^{-};{\bf Z})

respectively when
g
runs though a set of representatives of
\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)

[
17
]
, and see section
4
.

For
j\in\partial_{\Gamma}
, let
\Gamma_{j}
the stabilizer of
\sigma_{j}(\infty)
in
\Gamma
. For
x\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\bf Q})
, such that
m_{\Gamma x}=0
, set

\mathcal{S}_{D}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\lim_{s\rightarrow 1^{+}}\sum_{j\in\partial_{\Gamma}}m_{j}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_{j}\backslash\Gamma/\penalty\Gamma_{j}}\frac{e^{2\pi ir(x+\frac{d}{c})}}{|c|^{2s}},

where the entries
c
and
d
are such that
\sigma_{j}^{-1}\sigma\sigma_{j}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\star&\star\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
c
is required to be
>0
.
The internal sum over
\sigma
is sometimes called a Kloosterman zeta function
[
13
, Chapter 9, p. 121]
,
[
23
, p. 14]
.
A priori
, such Dirichlet series converge absolutely for
\Re(s)>1
. Their values at
1
exist since
D
is of degree
0
.

Let
D^{+}\in\text{Div}^{0}(\partial_{\Gamma}^{+})
(resp.
D^{-}\in\text{Div}^{0}(\partial_{\Gamma}^{-})
). Define
F_{D^{+}}
and
F_{D^{+}}
:
\Gamma(2)\rightarrow{\bf C}
by

F_{D^{+}}(h)=\mathcal{S}_{D^{+}}(h(-1))-\mathcal{S}_{D^{+}}(h(1))\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274ptF_{D^{-}}(h)=\mathcal{S}_{D^{-}}(h(\infty))-\mathcal{S}_{D^{-}}(h(0)).

We will see that those
\Gamma
-invariant quantities can be understood as periods of certain Eisenstein series.
Set

{\mathcal{E}}_{D^{+}}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)}F_{D^{+}}(g)\{g0,g\infty\}^{+}\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt{\mathcal{E}}_{D^{-}}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)}F_{D^{-}}(g)\{g(1),g(-1)\}^{-}

in
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{-},\partial_{\Gamma}^{+};{{\bf C}})
and
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{+},\partial_{\Gamma}^{-};{{\bf C}})
respectively.
We decompose them into their real and imaginary parts:

{\mathcal{E}}_{D^{+}}=\mathcal{R}_{D^{+}}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\mathcal{I}_{D^{+}}

and

{\mathcal{E}}_{D^{-}}=\mathcal{R}_{D^{-}}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\mathcal{I}_{D^{-}}
.

Theorem 1
.

For all differential forms of the third kind
\omega
on
X_{\Gamma}
with poles in
\partial_{\Gamma}^{-}
(resp.
\partial_{\Gamma}^{+}
), one has
\int_{\mathcal{R}_{D^{+}}}\omega=0
(resp.
\int_{\mathcal{R}_{D^{-}}}\omega=0
) and the boundary of
\mathcal{R}_{D^{+}}
(resp.
\mathcal{R}_{D^{-}}
) is
-D^{+}
(resp.
-D^{-}
). Moreover
{\mathcal{E}}_{D^{+}}
(resp.
{\mathcal{E}}_{D^{-}}
) has boundary
D^{+}
(resp.
D^{-}
).

It follows that the images of
\mathcal{R}_{D^{+}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{D^{-}}
in
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf R})
are Eisenstein classes. They are the classes of what we call
Eisenstein cycles
. We will explain in section
10
how to derive all Eisenstein classes in
\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\partial_{\Gamma};{\bf R})
from such images. We show in section
7
that
\mathcal{I}_{D^{-}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{D^{+}}
are deduced from the scattering constants, obtained from specializing scattering matrices of Eisenstein series at
s=1
.

We have a canonical map
\lambda^{+}
:
\mathrm{H}_{0}(\partial_{\Gamma}^{-},{\bf R})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{-},\partial_{\Gamma}^{+},{\bf R})
(resp.

\lambda^{-}
:
\mathrm{H}_{0}(\partial_{\Gamma}^{+},{\bf R})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\Gamma}^{+},\partial_{\Gamma}^{-},{\bf R})
) dual to the boundary map. Its image contains
\mathcal{I}_{D^{+}}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}_{D^{-}}
), see section
8
.
Let
J_{\Gamma}^{-}
(resp.
J_{\Gamma}^{+}
) be the generalized Jacobian of
X_{\Gamma}
with respect to the set
\partial_{\Gamma}^{-}
(resp.
\partial_{\Gamma}^{+}
).

Theorem 2
.

The class of
D^{+}
(resp.
D^{-}
) in
J_{\Gamma}^{-}
(resp.
J_{\Gamma}^{+}
) is torsion if and only if for every
g\in\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)
, one has
F_{D^{+}}(g)\in{\bf Q}
(resp.
F_{D^{-}}(g)\in{\bf Q}
).

The class of
D^{+}
(resp.
D^{-}
) in
J_{\Gamma}
is torsion if and only if one has
\sum_{g\in\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)}F_{D^{+}}(g)[g]\in{\bf Q}[\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)]+\lambda^{+}({\bf R}[\partial_{\Gamma}^{-}])
(resp.
\sum_{g\in\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)}F_{D^{-}}(g)[g]\in{\bf Q}[\Gamma\backslash\Gamma(2)]+\lambda^{-}({\bf R}[\partial_{\Gamma}^{+}])
.

The first criterion is similar to the following statement:
e^{2\pi iz}
is torsion in
{\bf C}^{\times}
if and only if the real part of
z
is rational, and the imaginary part of
z
is
0
.
Note that the first statement is substantial even if
X_{\Gamma}
has genus
0
.
The second criterion can be derived from the first. The maps
\lambda^{+}
and
\lambda^{-}
are computed explicitly by proposition
5
.

The principal weakness of our approach resides in the fact that the functions
F_{D^{+}}
and
F_{D^{-}}
are not easily computable for two reasons :
(i)
the convergence of Dirichlet series is notoriously difficult to understand, and
(ii)
we have no convenient way to pass from a combinatorial description of
P\Gamma(2)=\Gamma(2)/\{\pm 1\}
as a subgroup of the free group on two generators
P\Gamma(2)
to a description of the elements of
\Gamma
as
2\times 2
matrices. But it is expected that the Eisenstein classes are of a transcendental nature in general, and we hope that our method cast a new light on this nature.

We computed the functions
F_{D}^{+}
and
F_{D}^{-}
when
\Gamma
is congruence subgroup
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Here is a classical instance of a non-congruence subgroup where the computation of these formulas is possible.

Let
N
be an integer
>0
. Let
\Phi_{N}
be the kernel of the composed maps
\Gamma(2)\rightarrow\Gamma(2)^{\rm ab}\rightarrow\Gamma(2)^{\rm ab}/N
, where the first map is the abelianization. It is a non-congruence subgroup, when
N>8
. The corresponding modular curve
X_{\Phi_{N}}
is the
N
-th Fermat curve (
i.e.
it admits the model
X^{N}+Y^{N}=1
, the cusps correspond to the points such that
X^{N}=0
,
1
or
\infty
).
Rohrlich has shown that every cuspidal divisor of degree
0
is torsion in
J_{\Phi_{N}}

[
22
]
. As an application of our theory, we describe the cuspidal subgroups of
J_{\Phi_{N}}^{+}
and
J_{\Phi_{N}}^{-}
; as expected they are finite.

2.
The combinatorics of Belyi maps

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
{{\bf C}}
.
A
Belyi
map is a morphism
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\bf C})
unramified outside
0
,
1
,
\infty

[
25
]
.

A
dessin d’enfant

[
12
]
is a connected graph, composed of a finite set of vertices
V
and a finite set of edges
E
, with the additional structures :

•

a map from
V
to the pair
\{0,\infty\}
(the graph is bicolored)

•

the extremities of a given edge have different colors

•

the set of of edges adjacent to a given vertice is endowed with a cyclic ordering (
i.e.
a transitive action of
{\bf Z}
).

The dessin d’enfant associated to
f
is the bicolored graph
G
whose vertices of color
0
(resp.
\infty
) constitute
f^{-1}(0)
(resp.
f^{-1}(\infty)
).
The edges of the graph are the components the inverse images by
f
of the arc form
\infty
to
0
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\bf C})
(we depart here from the usual convention, where
0
and
1
are used instead of
0
and
\infty
).

Consider the function on the upper half-plane
[
6
, p. 86 and p. 105]
:

\lambda(z)=16e^{2\pi iz}\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{1+e^{2n\pi iz}}{1+e^{(2n-1)\pi iz}}\right)^{8}.

It identifies the modular curve
X(2)
to the projective line. We resist the tentation to use
1/\lambda
; instead we follow here the customary convention so that
\lambda(\infty)=0
,
\lambda(0)=\infty
,
\lambda(1)=\lambda(-1)=1
.
Thus the arc from
\infty
to
0
on the projective line is the image by
\lambda
of the arc from
0
to
\infty
in the upper half-plane.

Since
\Gamma(2)/\{-1,1\}
identifes to the fundamental group of the affine modular curve
Y(2)
, and the upper half-plane is the universal covering of
Y(2)
, the morphism
f
can be regarded as the morphism
X_{\Gamma}\rightarrow X(2)
, for an appropriate subgroup
\Gamma\subset\Gamma(2)
of finite index, and containing
-{\rm Id}
. The group
\Gamma(2)/\{-1,1\}
is freely generated by
\{A,B\}
where
A
is the image of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
B
is the image of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
2&1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

The dessin d’enfant attached to
X_{\Gamma}
is the graph whose vertices are the cusps of
X_{\Gamma}
above
0
(of colour
\infty
) and
\infty
(of colour
0
). The edges are the translates by
\Gamma(2)
of the image in
X_{\Gamma}
of the arc from
0
to
\infty
(in the upper half-plane). The action of
{\bf Z}
on the edges attached to a vertice of color
0
(resp.
\infty
) follows from the action of
A
(resp.
B^{-1}
).

Conversely,
\Gamma
can be recovered as follows from the dessin. It acts on the edges of the dessin: consider an edge
e
attached to a pair of vertices
v_{e}(0)
and
v_{e}(\infty)
(coloured
0
and
\infty
respectively), the image of
e
by
A
(resp.
B
) is the successor (resp. predecessor) of
e
along the vertice
v_{e}(0)
(resp.
v_{e}(\infty)
). The stabilizers of all edges constitute
\Gamma
. Thus a dessin d’enfant encodes the situation considered in this article.

Question
: Is there a natural interpretation of the Kloosterman zeta function purely in terms of the geometry of the dessin, without involving the entries of matrices in
\Gamma(2)
?

3.
Preliminaries and previous work

Let
X
be a compact, connected, non-empty Riemann surface. Let
S
be a finite subset of
X
. Let
D
be a divisor of degree
0
supported on
S
. Recall that a differential of the third kind on
X
is a meromorphic differential whose poles are simple and whose residues are integers.

Let
\omega
be a differential of the third kind on
X
, with divisor of poles equal to
D
. It exists by the Riemann Roch theorem.
There exists a unique holomorphic differential form
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}(X)
such that, for every
c\in{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(X,S;{\bf Z})
, one has
{\rm Re}(\int_{c}\omega)={\rm Re}(\int_{c}\omega^{\prime})
.
Therefore there exists a unique differential of the third kind
\omega_{D}
of residue divisor
D
such that
{\rm Re}(\int_{c}\omega_{D})=0
for every
c\in{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(X,S;{\bf Z})
.
This is the
canonical differential of the third kind
associated to
D
. The notion extends obviously to the situation where
D
is a divisor of degree
0
with real coefficients.

Let
f_{D}
be a multivalued function on
X-S
such that
df_{D}=\omega_{D}
. Since the periods of
\omega_{D}
are imaginary, the real part
g_{D}
of
f_{D}
is single-valued and harmonic.

We turn now to the specific situation of modular curves. Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of finite index of the modular group.
The cases of noncongruence subgroups has been studied since Atkin and Swinnerton-Dyer
[
1
]
.
Suppose
X
is the modular curve
X_{\Gamma}
associated to
\Gamma
, and
S
is the set
\partial_{\Gamma}
of cusps.
The pullbacks to the upper half-plane of differential of the third kind care of the form
2\pi if(z)\,dz
, where
f
is a holomorphic modular form of weight
2
for
\Gamma
.
The pullback of the
canonical differential of the third kind
associated to
D
is of the form
2\pi iG_{D}(z)\,dz
, where
G_{D}
is by definition the
Eisenstein series
associated to
D
.

The Eisenstein series
G_{D}
has been determined explicitly see section
6
. We find convenient to follow Scholl’s account, which in turn follows Hecke, Selberg, Kubota etc.

Let
j\in\partial_{\Gamma}
and choose
\sigma_{j}\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\bf Z})
such that
\sigma_{j}\infty\in j
and denote by
\Gamma_{j}
the stabilizer of
\sigma_{j}\infty
in
\Gamma
.
For
s\in{\bf C}
, with
{\rm Re}(s)>1
and

, set

E_{j}(z,s)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_{j}\backslash\Gamma}{\rm Im}(\sigma_{j}^{-1}\sigma(z))^{s}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_{j}\backslash\Gamma}\frac{y^{s}}{|cz+d|^{s}},

where
(c,d)
is the lower row of
\sigma_{j}^{-1}\sigma
. As a function of
s
,
E_{j}(z,s)
admits a meromorphic continuation to
{\bf C}
, with a simple pole at

.

Its study involves the corresponding Kloosterman zeta function at the pair of cusps
(j,k)
, which has been introduced by Selberg, even though the terminology seems due in print to Goldfeld and Sarnak
[
10
]
, see also the monographies of Kubota
[
14
, Chapter 2, p. 16]
and Iwaniec
[
13

, it is defined as

\phi_{jk,r}(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_{j}\backslash\Gamma/\penalty\Gamma_{k}}\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi ird}{c}}}{|c|^{2s}},

where the entries
c
and
d
are such that
\sigma_{j}^{-1}\sigma\sigma_{k}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\star&\star\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
c
is required to be
>0
.
The series is absolutely convergent if
{\rm Re}(s)>1
, and can be extended to a meromorphic function on the complex plane, which is due to Selberg
[
24
]
, see for instance
[
13
, Chapter 9, p. 122]
.
It has a pole at
s=1
when
r=0
, and extends holomorphically to the neighbourhood of
s=1
, when
r\neq 0
.
The notation
\phi_{jk,r}
comes from Kubota’s book
[
14
]
, and has been retained by Goldstein and Scholl
[
11
,
23
]
, and has been modified by Iwaniec in
[
13
]
.
The expansion of
E_{j}
at
\sigma_{k}(\infty)
is given by the formula :

(3.1)

E_{j}(\sigma_{k}(z),s)=\sum_{r\in{\bf Z}}a_{jk,r}(y,s)e^{2\pi irx}.

where, for
r\geq 1
,

(3.2)

a_{jk,r}(y,s)=\phi_{jk,r}(s)F_{r}(y,s),

and

(3.3)

a_{jk,0}(y,s)=\delta_{j,k}y^{s}+\phi_{jk,0}(s)y^{1-s}\pi^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma(s-1/2)}{\Gamma(s)}

here
\delta_{j,k}
is the Kronecker symbol, and
F_{r}(y,s)
is built out of Bessel functions, and does not concern us, except for the value at

.
Note that Scholl’s formula for

seems to contain a misprint: it involves

instead of

, contra Kubota and Iwaniec.
The coefficients
\phi_{jk,0}(s)\pi^{1/2}{\Gamma(s-1/2)}/{\Gamma(s)}
are the entries of the

at the pair

.
Such expressions have all a simple pole at

C_{j,k}=\lim_{s\to 1}(\phi_{jk,0}(s)\pi^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma(s-1/2)}{\Gamma(s)}-\frac{2}{\pi|\Gamma(2)/\Gamma|(s-1)})=\lim_{s\to 1}(\pi\phi_{jk,0}(s)-\frac{2}{\pi|\Gamma(2)/\Gamma|(s-1)}).

It is the
scattering constant
, see
[
15
]
, at the pair
(i,j)
. As a remark on the terminology, we note that the term seems to be of more recent use than the calculations that justifies its existence. It does not appear in
[

]
, for instance.
The constants could be, and have been
[
20
]

\zeta(s)

Following Scholl

Furthermore, write

where

,

,

Write

The modular form
G_{D}

It is holomorphic since

0

Proposition 7

Note that the formula makes sense.

One has a disjoint union (up to a negligible subset)

We turn now to the computation. One has

\int_{X}\omega_{+}\wedge\bar{\omega}_{-}=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{q\in Q_{v}}\int_{\tau(v,q)}\omega_{+}\wedge\bar{\omega}_{-}=\sum_{v\in V}\int_{\cup_{q\in Q_{v}}\tau(v,q)}\omega_{+}\wedge\bar{\omega}_{-}.

.

We have an equality of differential forms

Proof.

We compare the

When

On the other side we use the identity

\displaystyle G_{D}(z)\,dz=\frac{2}{mi}\,d\eta_{\Gamma,D}(z)=2\pi i\sum_{j\in\partial_{\Gamma}}m_{j}\,d(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\phi_{jj,r}(1)q^{r}).

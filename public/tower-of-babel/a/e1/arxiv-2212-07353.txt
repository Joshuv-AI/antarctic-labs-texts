1.
Introduction

The celebrated Brezis-Oswald theorem
[
6
]
states, among other things, that the problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f(x,u)&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\\
u\geq 0&\text{in $\Omega$}\end{cases}

in a connected smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
has a unique solution whenever
f(\cdot,t)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
t\geq 0
and
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t
is strictly decreasing in
t>0
, for a. e.
x\in\Omega
. This result has later been generalised to the quasi-linear setting in
[
13
]
, to the nonlocal one (see
[
18
]
) and even to mixed local-nonlocal problems in
[
1
]
, producing countless applications in nonlinear analysis.

The general principle underlying Brezis-Oswald theorem appears to be the following. If
L
denotes a (pseudo)-differential elliptic operator enjoying a strong minimum principle and which is
homogeneous
of degree
q
, then uniqueness of the solution

(1.2)

\begin{cases}L(u)=f(x,u)&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\\
u\geq 0&\text{in $\Omega$}\end{cases}

should follow under the assumption that
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{q}
is strictly decreasing. These kind of results, therefore, are called in the literature ”Brezis-Oswald uniqueness theorems”.

A number of additional assumptions, however, are generally required for such statements to hold, and we discuss here a few of them.

(1)

Regularity of the domain
.
Often, the domain
\Omega
is assumed to have smooth (say,
C^{1,\alpha}
) boundary, so that a Hopf-type maximum principle holds true for the corresponding solutions. This usually requires rather fine regularity theory for solutions of the corresponding problems, and is usually avoided requiring additional technical assumptions on the reaction (see e. g.
[
1
]
).

(2)

Growth conditions on
f
.
The monotonicity assumption on
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{q}
only grants an upper bound on the growth of
f
. Even if the operator
L
is variational, namely
L=dE
for a suitable differentiable convex functional
E:X\to\mathbb{R}
on a Banach space
X\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
, this poses delicate issues. Indeed, it is then natural to consider solutions of (
1.2
) as critical points of the functional

J(v)=E(v)-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx,\qquad F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\,ds

but without assuming any further lower growth control on
f
,
J
is, typically, only lower semicontinuous. This problem is often side-stepped by either requiring a-priori boundedness in the notion of solution of (
1.2
) (so that actually
f
can be truncated), or by directly requiring a two sided growth condition on
f
.

(3)

Monotonicity assumption
. It is clear that uniqueness fails even for (
1.1
) without
strict
monotonicity, as seen by considering the case
f(x,t)=\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\,t
, where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the first eigenfunction of the Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions. In this case
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t
is only non-increasing and the corresponding problem (
1.1
) has infinitely many positive solutions, namely all first eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet Laplacian. However, at least for autonomous reactions independent of
x
, this is essentially the only obstruction to uniqueness. Indeed, in
[
2
, Proposition 3.8]
it is proved that, for
L=-\Delta_{p}
and
t\mapsto f(t)/t^{p-1}
only
non-increasing
in
t>0
, either there is a unique solution to the corresponding problem (
1.2
), or all solutions are first eigenfunctions for the Dirichlet
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
. In particular, uniqueness holds true if
t\mapsto f(t)/t^{p-1}
is non-increasing and strictly decreasing only on an arbitrarily small interval
]0,\delta[
,
\delta>0
.

(4)

Regularity of
L
. As anticipated in point (2) above, the typical framework for a variational Brezis-Oswald uniqueness result is
L(u)=dE(u)
, for
E(u)=\|u\|_{X}^{p}
and
\|\ \|_{X}
is the norm of a Banach space
X\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
, smooth away from the origin. Differentiability of the norm is typically used to have a single valued operator
L
in equation (
1.2
). Considering non-differentiable and only locally Lipschitz energies
E
yields a differential inclusion formally of the form

f(x,u)\in\partial E(u)

for critical points of the relevant functional, where here
\partial
denotes the multivalued convex subdifferential. In this more general setting, uniqueness results are clearly stronger statements.

1.1.
Main results

In this manuscript, we plan to address the previous themes, aiming to obtain a general version of the uniqueness part of Brezis-Oswald theorem. In particular, we seek for a classification of the critical points of functionals of the form

(1.3)

J(v)=\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)-F(x,v)\,dx,\qquad v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

under fairly general conditions on
\Omega
,
H
, and
f(x,t)=\partial_{t}F(x,t)
. Our main result reads as follows (for a more complete statement, see Theorem
5.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open, connected and of finite measure.
For some
p>1
suppose that

i)

H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty[
is strictly convex and positively
p
-homogeneous

ii)

f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Caratheodory and fulfills

\sup_{x\in\Omega,|s|\leq t}|f(x,s)|<\infty\qquad\forall t>0

iii)

The map
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is non-increasing on
]0,\infty[
.

For
J
as in (
1.3
) with

F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\,ds,

let
C_{+}(J)
be the set of its non-negative critical points
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
. Then

(1)

All elements in
C_{+}(J)
are multiple of each other and each of them minimise
J
among non-negative functions in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

(2)

If
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is strictly decreasing on
t>0
,
C_{+}(J)
contains at most one element

(3)

If
f
does not depend on
x
and
C_{+}(J)
contains more than one element, each function in
C_{+}(J)
minimises the Rayleigh quotient

(1.4)

\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}v^{p}\,dx}}

among all
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
,
v\geq 0
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let us make some comments on the features of the previous theorem.

•

No regularity assumptions are made on the boundary of the open set
\Omega
. The finite measure assumption could be further weakened requiring additional conditions on the behaviour of
f(x,t)
near
0
, but we chose not to analyse this case in order to have a cleaner statement. Notice that in this setting, there is no hope to have a Hopf maximum principle available.

•

The stated assumption on
H
enforces the two sided bound

c\,|z|^{p}\leq H(z)\leq d\,|z|^{p},\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for some
0<c\leq d<\infty
. However, since
H
is not supposed to be
C^{1}
, the first term of
J
in (
1.3
) is only locally Lipschitz, with multi-valued differential. Notice also that
H
is not supposed to be even. The strict convexity of
H
is also not needed in assertion
(2)
, as well as in the second statement of assertion
(1)
.

•

Conditions
ii)
and
iii)
on the reaction
f
are equivalent to

f(\cdot,t)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\qquad f(x,t)\leq C\,(1+t^{p-1}),\qquad t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}\ \text{non-increasing}

for
t>0
, which are more commonly found in Brezis-Oswald type results.

•

Under the sole assumptions
ii)
and
iii)
, even for smooth
H
,
J
is only l. s. c., and the notion of critical point has to be dealt with care. We employ here the notion of
energy critical point
used in
[
10
]
, meaning those
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
which, roughly speaking, minimise the corresponding semi-linearised convex functional

v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)-f(x,u)\,v\,dx.

For the sake of this introductory discussion, let us note that the usually encountered notions of critical point, when available, yield an energy critical point.
For more details, we refer to Definition
2.4
and Remark
2.5
.

•

Notice that we don’t give any condition ensuring that
C_{+}(J)
is actually not empty. One of the main features of the original Brezis-Oswald result (and of the related literature on the subject) was actually to provide necessary and sufficient condition for
J
to have non-negative, non-trivial critical points, under a strict monotonicity assumption on
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
. We won’t investigate this theme here.

•

Under assumption
iii)
alone, the structure of
C_{+}(J)
is described in an optimal way by conclusion
(1)
of the theorem. Indeed, when
H(z)=|z|^{2}
, given a first positive eigenfunction
u
of the Dirichlet Laplacian in the unit ball
B_{1}
and an interval
[a,b]\subseteq[0,+\infty[
, any reaction
f
obeying
f(x,t)=\lambda_{1}(B_{1})\,t
if
a\,u(x)\leq t\leq b\,u(x)
and
f(x,t)/t
strictly decreasing otherwise has
C_{+}(J)=\{t\,u:t\in[a,b]\}
.

•

A similar statement holds true for non-positive critical points of
J
, with obvious modifications to
ii)
and
iii)
. Notice, however, that since we are not assuming that
H
is even, the infimum of the ratios (
1.4
) may change under the corresponding constrain
v\leq 0
. This feature of possibly non-even, positively
p
-homogeneous energies
H
and their corresponding first Dirichlet eigenvalues is discussed in section
4
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we need to develop basic regularity theory for energy critical points. The main step is contained in the following
a-priori
bound, which is of independent interest. In its statement, we let as usual
p^{*}=N\,p/(N-p)
if
p<N
,
p^{*}=\infty
if
p>N
and
p^{*}
be any finite number if
p=N
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
have finite measure and, for given
p\geq 1
, suppose that

i)

H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty[
is convex and fulfills
H(z)\geq c\,|z|^{p}
for some
c>0
and all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

ii)

f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Caratheodory and fulfills

\sup_{x\in\Omega,|s|\leq t}|f(x,s)|<\infty\qquad\forall t>0

iii)

There exist
\mu,\theta\geq 0
and
1\leq q\leq p^{*}
such that

(1.5)

{\rm sign}\,(t)f(x,t)\leq\mu\,(|t|+\theta)^{q}\qquad\text{for a.\,e.\,$x\in\Omega$ and all $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Then, any critical point
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
of
J

is bounded. If additionally
q<p^{*}
, the estimate

(1.6)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\max\left\{\theta,C\,\left((\mu/c)^{\frac{N}{p}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p\,q+(p-q)\,N}}\right\}

holds true for some
C=C(N,p,q)
which, for any
N
, is positive and continuous in its other arguments.

Remark 1.4
.

Regarding this second result, the following should be noted.

•

Not only
H
is only assumed to be locally Lipschitz, but no growth control from above is required in order to obtain boundedness of the critical points of
J
.

•

Assumption
iii)
is understood to be empty in the case
q=\infty
, which forces
p>N
thanks to
q\leq p^{*}
. Clearly, in this case boundedness is trivial thanks to Morrey embedding. Anyway, (
1.6
) only holds for
q<p^{*}
and therefore for finite
q
’s.

•

Assumption
ii)
and
iii)
are required in order for the functional
J
in (
1.3
) to be well defined (possibly assuming the value
+\infty
) on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. In any case, under these assumptions
J
turns out to be only l. s. c. on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with the strong topology, and again we understand by critical point an energy one (see subsection
2.4
).

•

The form (
1.6
) of the sup bound is optimal, as discussed in Remark
3.4
. Moreover, it is invariant by scaling and scalar multiples, meaning the following. A function
u
is a critical point for
J
on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
if and only if for any
\lambda,r>0

u_{\lambda,r}(x)=\lambda\,u(x/r)
is a critical point for a suitable functional
J_{\lambda,r}
on
W^{1,p}_{0}(r\,\Omega)
(where
r\,\Omega=\{r\,x:x\in\Omega\}
). More precisely, the functional
J_{\lambda,r}
is of the form (
1.3
) for a reaction
\hat{f}
obeying (
1.5
) with
\hat{\theta}=\lambda\,\theta
,
\hat{\mu}=\mu/\lambda^{q}
and and
\hat{H}
obeying
\hat{H}(z)\geq\hat{c}\,|z|^{p}
with
\hat{c}=c\,\lambda/r
. The corresponding estimates (
1.6
) turn out to be independent of
\lambda
and
r
.

•

Regarding the continuity statement for the constant
C
in the last part of the theorem, it is to be understood in its domain
p\geq 1
,
q<p^{*}
. As is to be expected, the constant
C
blows up as
q\uparrow p^{*}
.

1.2.
Related results and motivations

Applications and extensions of the Brezis-Oswald theorem are countless and it would be impossible to list all the contributions on the subject.
The principal part of the operator considered in the various generalisation can be classical quasi-linear operators, as in
[
13
]
, nonlocal operators as in
[
18
]
and even mixed local-nonlocal ones as in
[
1
]
. In all these cases the operator is
(p-1)
-homogeneous and the corresponding monotonicity assumption on the reaction pertains
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
. Non-homogeneous operators of Orlicz type are considered in
[
7
]
. The case when
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is non-increasing but not strictly decreasing is naturally related to weighted nonlinear eigenvalue problems, where
f(x,t)=a(x)\,t^{p-1}
. Corresponding eigenfunctions are considered in
[
4
,
25
,
26
]
when the energy density
H
is
p
-homogeneous and
C^{1}
, while the case where
H
may be non-differentiable is studied in
[
21
]
. Various applications to nonlinear parabolic problems, encompassing the non-differentiable setting, are described in
[
12
]
.

A complete Brezis-Oswald theorem usually consists of two statements. The first one is devoted to derive necessary and sufficient condition for the solvability of (
1.2
), under the aforementioned monotonicity assumption of
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
, as is done in
[
6
,
13
,
18
]
. The second one regards uniqueness of the solution, and is clearly the focus of this research.

As briefly outlined in the first section, our main intent with this investigation was to clarify how the various technical assumptions which usually appear in Brezis-Oswald type result could be weakened to have a cleaner picture, especially regarding its uniqueness part. It is clear, for instance, that the regularity of the domain or of
H
is not necessary for the uniqueness statement to hold. Our interest in this part of Brezis-Oswald theorem, however, lies mainly in its applications to the qualitative study of solutions to PDE’s.

Indeed, uniqueness is the basic step needed to effectively approximate the particular PDE problem under scrutiny of the form (
1.2
) with solutions of regularised problems

(1.7)

L_{n}(u)=f_{n}(x,u),

each having a smooth solution
u_{n}
allowing computations can be more effectively performed. The sequence
(u_{n})
then shown to enjoy uniform bounds and therefore to converge up to subsequences to a solution of the limiting, original problem (
1.2
). Then, uniqueness of solutions of (
1.2
) is the key point ensuring that the approximating solutions actually converge to the original one, which then (hopefully) inherits some properties of the
u_{n}
’s. This scheme of proof is nowadays standard in both regularity theory and qualitative properties of solution to PDE’s.

As a typical example, let us consider positive solutions of the quasi-linear problem

-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)

with Dirichlet boundary conditions and a qualitative property of these solutions known as
quasi-concavity
, meaning that the super-level sets of
u
are convex.
It is quite fortunate that the monotonicity assumption on
t\mapsto f(t)/t^{p-1}
is strictly connected to the general conditions found in
[
2
]
ensuring quasi-concavity of the aforementioned solutions. Actually, the results in
[
2
,
3
]
are proved via the smoothing technique outlined above under the assumption that the domain is convex with
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary, but Theorem
1.1
allow to extend the results to general convex domains.

As a motivation for Theorem
1.3
, let us also stress that, in order for the previously described approximation scheme to work, uniform bounds are also essential to ensure compactness. This is especially true when, in the family of problems (
1.7
), both the operators
L_{n}
and the reactions
f_{n}
may have a varying growth control, only qualitatively bounded. In this regard, Theorem
1.3
is inspired by
[
5
]
, where the stability of the available a-priori bounds had to be treated with extreme care for the employed contradiction argument to carry on. In particular, (
1.6
) should be compared with
[
5
, Proposition 2.4]
, corresponding to the case
\theta=0
in (
1.5
). In
[
5
]
, stability with respect to
p
wasn’t an issue, and the a-priori bound was derived via a Moser iteration technique involving the
p
-Sobolev inequality. Since the constant in the latter blows up as
p\uparrow N
, the obtained bounds in
[
5
]
are not stable in this limit. To be honest, we initially hoped for this apparent instability to be substantial, suggesting the possibility of interesting phenomena exhibited by solutions of, say, the
p
-Laplace equations as
p\uparrow N
. Unfortunately, a different and, we think, more streamlined proof of the
L^{\infty}
bound in the spirit of De Giorgi method, shows that this is not the case.

Let us finally mention that our proof of Theorem
1.1
, detailed in the next section, relies on a non-smooth version of the Picone inequality which relies only on the positive
p
-homogeneity of
H
. This should be compared to
[
19
]
where, however, differentiable convex
H
are considered. A natural alternative route to the Brezis-Oswald uniqueness result, as in
[
12
,
13
,
26
]
, is to consider the hidden convexity properties of the functional
J
in (
1.3
). Indeed, the core assumption that
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
be non-increasing is implied by the weaker hypothesis that
t\mapsto F(x,t^{1/p})
is concave. Notice that this latter condition does not require
F
to be differentiable in its second variable, nor any bound on its derivative is needed to define the functional
J
. Concavity of
t\mapsto F(x,t^{1/p})
implies
hidden convexity
of

J_{F}(v)=-\int_{\Omega}F(x,v)\,dx,

on the cone of non-negative
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, which means convexity of
J_{F}
along the curves

t\mapsto\left(t\,u^{p}+(1-t)\,v^{p}\right)^{1/p}\qquad\forall u,v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\quad u,v\geq 0.

On the other hand, by
[
4
]
, our non-smooth Picone inequality is equivalent to the hidden convexity of
J_{H}
. Hence the whole
J
turns out to be hidden convex on the cone of nonnegative functions in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and it is only natural to expect few critical points from such a functional. We chose not to adopt this approach since our notion of energy critical point, while very weak, requires at least that the reaction
f(x,t)=\partial_{t}F(x,t)
is well defined and Caratheodory, but we point out that the interplay between hidden convexity and the notion of critical point for l. s. c. functionals in the sense of
[
9
]
has been initiated in
[
11
]
in the framework of nonlinear eigenvalue problems.

1.3.
Outline of the proof

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is split in two main parts: regularity and uniqueness. The first obstacle to regularity is the lack of a proper Euler-Lagrange equation to work with, in particular when dealing with critical points of the l. s. c. functional
J
. Here we take advantage of the variational characterisation of critical points
u
as minima of the semi-linearised functional

J_{u}(v)=\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)-f(x,u)\,v\,dx.

This allows to test the minimality of
J_{u}(u)
against
J_{u}(u_{k})
, where
u_{k}:=\max\{k,u\}
for
k\geq 0
, and
J_{u}(u)\leq J_{u}(u_{k})
reads

\int_{\Omega}H(D(u-k)_{+})\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,(u-k)_{+}\,dx.

This inequality and the one-sided growth control on
f
suffice to derive a ”fast decay” relation of the
L^{q}
norms of
(u-k)_{+}
much in the spirit of De Giorgi regularity proof. However, in order to ensure stability of the estimates in
p
, we avoid the natural Sobolev inequality and instead use an interpolation inequality
á la
Gagliardo-Nirenberg with a more tamed constant (see Proposition
2.2
). Once the
L^{\infty}
bound of Theorem
1.3
is proved, the reaction is bounded and Giaquinta-Giusti hole-filling method
[
15
]
allows to prove that the solutions belong to suitable De Giorgi classes, thus providing continuity and a strong minimum principle for non-negative critical points. In particular, an Euler-Lagrange equation (or rather, inclusion) is satisfied by critical points, namely

(1.8)

\int_{\Omega}(\xi,D\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,\varphi\,dx\qquad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

where
\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a measurable choice of the multifunction
x\mapsto\partial H(Du(x))
and
(\ ,\ )
denotes the Euclidean scalar product.

With these regularity tools at our disposal we can prove the uniqueness part. First we derive a non-smooth version of Picone inequality in Lemma
5.1
. If
H
positively
p
-homogeneous and convex, the point-wise inequality

(1.9)

p\,H(z)\geq(\xi,z),\qquad\forall\xi\in\partial H(z)

holds true.
The non-smooth Picone inequality follows from the latter and reads as follows: if
u
and
v
are positive functions in
W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, then a. e. in
\Omega
it holds

p\,H(Dv)\geq\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right),

for any measurable
\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\xi(x)\in\partial H(Du(x))
a. e.. Now, if
H
is differentiable its convex subdifferential is single-valued, i. e.
\partial H=\{DH\}
and actually (
1.9
) is an equality, so the smooth Picone inequality becomes

\left(DH(Dv),Dv\right)\geq\left(DH(Du),D\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right).

If both
u
and
v
are positive critical points, (
1.8
) gives (cheating a little bit, since we are testing it for
u
with
\varphi=v^{p}/u^{p-1}
)

\int_{\Omega}f(x,v)\,v\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\,dx.

Exchanging the role of
u
and
v
and summing the corresponding inequalities, one arrives at

(1.10)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}-\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}\right)(u-v)\,dx\geq 0

which yields uniqueness when
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is strictly decreasing.

The point, in the non-differentiable case, is that in general the inequality in (
1.9
) can be strict. However, it turns out that the measurable selection
\xi
of
\partial H(Du)
arising from (
1.8
) actually attains equality in (
1.9
) almost everywhere. This property is related to our variational notion of critical point, and allows to carry on the proof as us usual.
Finally, the characterisation of the set of critical points described in Theorem
1.1
under the non-increasing assumption on
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is based on the analysis of the equality case in the non-smooth Picone inequality, derived from the identity

\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}=\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}

which must hold a. e. if
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is non-increasing, thanks to (
1.10
).

1.4.
Structure of the paper

Section
2
contains preliminary material: we collect the notations used in the paper, provide some basic functional inequalities, derive basic properties of convex, positively
p
homogeneous functions and describe the notion of energy critical points and its basic features. Section
3
contains the proof of Theorem
1.3
and some of its consequences, such as continuity and minimum principles for energy critical points. In Section
4
we define and study the first Dirichlet eigenvalues associated to possibly non-even convex energies, aiming at the proof of point
(3)
of Theorem
1.1
. In the final section we prove the non-smooth Picone inequality and Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

Throughout the paper we will make use of the following notations.

Given a number
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
and
a_{-}=-\min\{0,a\}
and similarly for real valued functions.
For every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we denote by
|v|
its Euclidean norm while
(v,w)
stands for the scalar product,
v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The symbol
B
denotes a generic ball with unspecified center and radius while
B_{r}
denotes a ball of radius
r
centred at the origin.
S_{1}
stands for the unit sphere
\partial B_{1}
.

For a Lebesgue measurable
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|E|
denotes its measure and we set
\omega_{N}=|B_{1}|
. We will omit the domain of integration when it is the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, if this cause no confusion. For a measurable
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
{\mathcal{L}}(E;\mathbb{R}^{M})
will denote the set of Lebesgue measurable functions from
E
to
\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Given an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f
will denote a Caratheodory function
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and

(2.1)

F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\,ds\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Given
u\in{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, we’ll denote by
f(\cdot,u)
the measurable function
x\mapsto f(x,u(x))
and similarly for
F(\cdot,u)
. The Lebesgue spaces are denoted as usual by
L^{p}(E)
for
p\geq 1
, with norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(E)}
, while
\sup_{E}u
and
\inf_{E}u
stand for the essential supremum and infimum respectively. The Lebesgue spaces of vector valued functions
u:E\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
, will be denoted by
\left(L^{p}(E)\right)^{M}
with norms
\||u|\|_{L^{p}(E)}
, where
|u|
is the standard Euclidean norm of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Furthermore, we set for the sake of notational simplicity
\|f\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Given
p\in[1,\infty]
,
p^{\prime}
denotes its conjugate, namely
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
if
p\in\,]1,\infty[
,
1^{\prime}=\infty
and
\infty^{\prime}=1
. Given an integer
N\geq 2
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by

p^{*}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{N\,p}{N-p}&\text{if $p<N$}\\
\text{any positive number}&\text{if $p=N$}\\
\infty&\text{if $p>N$}.\end{cases}

For an open
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with finite measure, the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
w. r. t. the seminorm
\|Du\|_{p}
. In the finite measure case,
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a Banach space, continuously embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,p^{*}]
. Its dual will be denoted by
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, with duality pairing denoted by
\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle
for
\Lambda\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
If
p<N
, the homogeneous Sobolev space
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the space of functions in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with distributional derivative in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and which
vanish at infinity
, meaning that

|\{|u|>k\}|<\infty\qquad\text{ for all $k>0$}.

2.2.
Functional inequalities

Functions in
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
1\leq p<N
enjoy the Sobolev inequality

(2.2)

\|u\|_{p^{*}}\leq C(N,p)\,\|Du\|_{p}.

We are particularly interested in the
p=1
case, for which
C(N,1)=N^{-1}\,\omega_{N}^{-1/N}
for all
N\geq 2
.

The following result is probably well known, but we couldn’t find a precise references and thus provide its proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
1\leq p,q<\infty
, let
X_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the vector space of functions in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with distributional derivative in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, equipped with the norm
\|u\|=\|Du\|_{p}+\|u\|_{q}
. Then
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
X_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

The set
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap X_{p,q}
is dense in
X_{p,q}
by known results (see
[
22
, Theorem 1, Section 1.1.5]
), so it suffices to show that any
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap X_{p,q}
can be approximated by functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Fix a cutoff
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2};[0,1])
such that
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{1}
and, for any
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap X_{p,q}
, define for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\eta_{k}(x)=\eta(x/k),\qquad u_{k}=u\,\eta_{k},\qquad A_{k}=B_{2\,k}\setminus B_{k},

so that
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
{\rm supp}\,(D\eta_{k})\subseteq A_{k}
and
\|D\eta_{k}\|_{\infty}=\|D\eta\|_{\infty}/k
. Clearly
\|u-u_{k}\|_{q}\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{k})}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, so we only have to estimate
\|D(u-u_{k})\|_{p}
. It holds

(2.3)

\begin{split}\int|Du-Du_{k}|^{p}\,dx&\leq C_{p}\int|Du|^{p}\,(1-\eta_{k})^{p}\,dx+C_{p}\,\int|u|^{p}\,|D\eta|^{p}\,dx\\
&\leq C_{p}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{k}}|Du|^{p}\,dx+\frac{C_{p}\,\|D\eta\|_{\infty}^{p}}{k^{p}}\,\int_{A_{k}}|u|^{p}\,dx.\end{split}

and the first term on the right vanishes for
k\to\infty
since
|Du|\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so we are left to estimate the second term.

The case
p<N
is standard: by Chebichev inequality any
u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
vanishes at infinity, hence
X_{p,q}\subseteq D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subseteq L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Therefore, by Hölder inequality

\int_{A_{k}}|u|^{p}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{A_{k}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\,|A_{k}|^{1-\frac{p}{p^{*}}}=C\,k^{p}\,\left(\int_{A_{k}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}},

and the last factor on the right vanishes as
k\to\infty
due to
u\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, hence also the last term in (
2.3
) does so. If
p\geq N
, we use the inequality

\int_{B_{2}\setminus B_{1}}|v|^{p}\,dx\leq C\,\int_{B_{2}\setminus B_{1}}|Dv|^{p}\,dx+C\,\left(\int_{B_{2}\setminus B_{1}}|v|^{q}\,dx\right)^{p/q}

which is trivial by Hölder when
q\geq p
and holds true thanks to Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality when
q<p
. Applying it to
v(x)=u(k\,x)
and changing variables, we get

\frac{1}{k^{p}}\,\int_{A_{k}}|u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\,\int_{A_{k}}|Du|^{p}\,dx+\frac{C\,k^{N\,\left(1-\frac{p}{q}\right)}}{k^{p}}\left(\int_{A_{k}}|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{p/q}.

As
k\to\infty
, the first term on the right vanishes again, as well as the second, since

N\left(1-\frac{p}{q}\right)<p\qquad\forall p\geq N,q<\infty.

∎

The next proposition is a particular case of the general Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. In order to get stable
L^{\infty}
bounds in Section
3
, however, we have to pay special attention to the dependance from the parameters and thus give a direct proof depending only on (
2.2
).

Proposition 2.2

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg)
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
1\leq p,q<\infty
. Then for any
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
it holds

(2.4)

\left(\int|u|^{\frac{N\,(q\,p+p-q)}{(N-1)\,p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq G_{p,q,N}\left(\int|Du|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}},

where

G_{p,q,N}=\frac{1}{N\,\omega_{N}^{1/N}}\,\frac{q\,p+p-q}{p}.

Proof.

We can suppose that the right-hand side is finite, which amounts to
u\in X_{p,q}
. By the previous Proposition we can also suppose that
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
For
p=1
(
2.4
) reduces to (
2.2
), so we can suppose that
p>1
.
Let

r=\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}+1=\frac{q\,p+p-q}{p}

and apply (
2.2
) to
v=|u|^{r}
and then Hölder inequality, to get

\begin{split}\left(\int|u|^{\frac{q\,p+p-q}{p}1^{*}}\,dx\right)^{1/1^{*}}&\leq\frac{1}{N\,\omega_{N}^{1/N}}\,\int|D|u|^{r}|\,dx=\frac{1}{N\,\omega_{N}^{1/N}}\,r\,\int|u|^{r-1}\,|Du|\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{N\,\omega_{N}^{1/N}}\,r\,\left(\int|Du|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}.\end{split}

∎

2.3.
Convex
p
-homogeneous functions

Let
X
be a Banach space in duality with
X^{*}
via the duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Given a continuous convex function
C:X\to\mathbb{R}
(we will alway restrict to this setting) its
subdifferential
at
x\in X
is

\partial C(x)=\left\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:C(x+v)-C(x)\geq\langle x^{*},v\rangle\ \forall v\in X\right\}.

When
X=\mathbb{R}^{N}
we’ll identify
X
with
X^{*}
via the scalar product as a duality pairing, hence in this case
\partial C(x)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Throughout the paper,
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty[
will denote a convex function. Since its domain is
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, any such function is locally Lipschitz. The function
H
is positively
p
-homogeneous for some
p\geq 1
if

H(\lambda\,z)=\lambda^{p}\,H(z)\qquad\text{ for all $\lambda>0$ and $z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

Notice that we do not assume regularity of
H
, nor that
H
is even.
In the positively
p
-homogeneous case the whole
H
is completely determined by its
associated ball
, i. e. the convex closed set

B_{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:H(z)\leq 1\},

which in general is not symmetric around the origin but always contains
0
in its interior. Vice-versa, given any convex
K
containing
0
in its interior, the function

H_{K}(z)=\left(M_{K}(z)\right)^{p}

where
M_{K}
is the Minkowski functional of
K

M_{K}(z)=\inf\{t>0:z/t\in K\},

is a convex, positively
p
-homogeneous function with associated ball
K
. The set of symmetries of
H
is the set of symmetries of its associated ball, namely

{\rm Sym}\,(H)=\{T\in O(N):T(B_{H})=B_{H}\}

where
O(N)
is the orthogonal group in dimension
N
. By construction, for any
T\in{\rm Sym}\,(H)
it holds
H\circ T=H
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty[
be convex and positively
p
-homogeneous for some
p\geq 1
. Then for any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\xi\in\partial H(w)
, it holds

(2.5)

p\,H(z)\geq(\xi,z)\qquad\forall\xi\in\partial H(z).

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\partial H(z)
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By
p
-homogeneity and convexity

t^{p}\,H(z)=H(t\,z)\geq H(z)+(\xi,t\,z-z)

for all
t\geq 1
, while the left hand side coincides with the right hand side when
t=1
. Therefore

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\big[t^{p}\,H(z)-H(z)-(\xi,t\,z-z)\big]\right|_{t=1}\geq 0,

which gives (
2.5
).

∎

2.4.
Energy critical points

In this section we consider functionals of the form (
1.3
) with more general structural conditions than those required in Theorem
1.1
. More precisely, we assume that

(2.6)

\begin{split}\text{ $H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,+\infty[$ is convex and for some $p\geq 1$ and $0\leq c<d\leq\infty$ obeys}\\
c\,|z|^{p}\leq H(z)\leq d\,|z|^{p}\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\end{split}

(2.7)

\text{$f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is Caratheodory and}\quad\sup_{|s|\leq t}\|f(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty\quad\forall t>0,

(2.8)

\text{there exist $\theta,\mu\geq 0$, $1\leq q\leq p^{*}$ s.\,t. }{\rm sign}\,(t)\,f(x,t)\leq\mu\,(|t|+\theta)^{q-1}\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Regarding
F
as per (
2.1
), it follows from (
2.7
) that when
p>N
then
F(\cdot,v)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for any
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
by Morrey embedding, while, when
p\leq N
, from (
2.8
) we readily obtain the one-sided estimate

\sup_{\Omega}F(\cdot,t)\leq\frac{\mu}{q}\,\left(|t|+\theta\right)^{q}

for a finite
q
.
Therefore, as long as
\Omega
has finite measure (which will be assumed henceforth),

(2.9)

\left(H(Dv)-F(\cdot,v)\right)_{-}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

and the functional
J
in (
1.3
) is well defined on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, eventually assuming the value
\infty
if
F(\cdot,v)\notin L^{1}(\Omega)
. Fatou’s lemma ensures that
J
is l. s. c. on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with the strong topology (regardless of
p\geq 1
), hence the non-smooth critical point theory of
[
9
]
can be applied. Notice also that if (
2.8
) is strengthened to hold for both
f
and
-f
,
then
J
turns out to be locally Lipschitz, since its second term is actually
C^{1}
on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, allowing to employ Clarke critical point theory
[
8
]
. We will not pursue these approaches here for sake of brevity and will use the following notion of critical point, taken from
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Energy critical point)
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
has finite measure. Under assumptions (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and, if
p\leq N
, (
2.8
), we say that
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is an
energy critical point
for
J
, if
J(u)<\infty
and
u
minimises the convex functional

(2.10)

J_{u}(v)=\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)-f(x,u)\,v\,dx

defined on the space

V_{u}=\left\{v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega):f(\cdot,u)\,v\in L^{1}(\Omega)\right\}.

A few remarks on this notion of critical point are in order.

Remark 2.5
.

•

Notice that an energy critical point
u
is
assumed
to lie in
V_{u}
, i. e.
f(\cdot,u)\,u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. The role of
p
in the previous definition is connected to the validity of (
2.9
) (granted, as noted, by (
2.8
)), so that
J
is at least well defined. It should be remarked that, at this level of generality, it has nothing to do with (
2.6
), where one could also have
c=0
and
d=\infty
.
Regardless, from
H\geq 0
and the assumption
J(u)<\infty
, we see that any energy critical point
u
fulfills
H(Du)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. In particular, if
c
in (
2.6
) is finite and positive and
p\leq N
, then
u\in L^{q}(\Omega)
by the finite measure assumption on
\Omega
and Sobolev embedding. Let us also remark that if
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is assumed, or proved, to be bounded, assumption (
2.8
) is irrelevant as
J
is always well defined as long as
\Omega
has finite measure and in this case
V_{u}=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

•

We stress here that the notion of energy critical point is a very general one as it does not involve any topological structure on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. As such, it is useless if one wants to develop an effective critical point theory, where topological considerations are paramount.
Under conditions (
2.6
) with
c>0
and
p>1
, as well as (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) if
p\leq N
, a critical point for
J
in the sense of
[
9
]
(with the strong topology on
L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)
) is an energy critical point (see
[
10
, Theorem 5]
). Similarly, if
d<\infty
and
p>1
in (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) holds for both
f
and
-f
, then any critical point in the sense of Clarke for
J
is an energy critical point.

•

In general, (
2.7
) and the finite measure assumption on
\Omega
ensure that
f(x,u)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, hence
V_{u}
contains
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and is dense in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. In the case
d<\infty
of (
2.6
), being

(2.11)

J_{H}(v):=\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx

convex and finite on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, it is locally Lipschitz, hence for any energy critical point
u
it holds

\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,(v-u)\,dx\leq J_{H}(v)-J_{H}(u)\leq C_{r}\,\|D(v-u)\|_{p}

for all
v\in V_{u}
with
\|D(v-u)\|_{p}\leq r
. Therefore the linear functional

V_{u}\ni v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,v\,dx

has a unique extension
T
to
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
as an element in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
. Notice that while
\langle T,v\rangle
is finite for all
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, this does not imply that
f(\cdot,u)\,v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, unless a one-sided bound on
f(x,u)\,v
holds true, enabling to apply Brezis-Browder theorem.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open with finite measure and assume (
2.6
) with
d<\infty
, (
2.7
) and, if
p\leq N
, (
2.8
).
If
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is an energy critical point for
J
, there exists
\xi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(2.12)

\begin{cases}\text{$\xi(x)\in\partial H(Du(x))$}&\text{ for a.\,e.\,$x\in\Omega$}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi,D\varphi\right)-f(x,u)\,\varphi\,dx=0}&\ \forall\varphi\in V_{u}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us compute
\partial J_{H}
(see (
2.11
)), following
[
23
]
. The operator

W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\ni v\mapsto A(v)=Dv\in\left(L^{p}(\Omega)\right)^{N}

is linear and continuous with transpose

\big(L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\big)^{N}\ni g\mapsto A^{*}(g)={\rm div}\,g\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)

where, as usual,
{\rm div}\,g\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
acts as

(2.13)

\langle{\rm div}\,g,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\left(g,D\varphi\right)\,dx\qquad\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

The convex functional

\left(L^{p}(\Omega)\right)^{N}\ni g\mapsto I_{H}(g)=\int_{\Omega}H(g)\,dx

is locally Lipschitz thanks to
d<\infty
in (
2.6
) and has convex subdifferential (see
[
24
, Theorem 21]
)

\partial I_{H}(g)=\left\{\xi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}):\text{$\xi(x)\in\partial H(g(x))$ for a.\,e.\,$x\in\Omega$}\right\}.

Clearly
J_{H}=I_{H}\circ A
and by
[
24
, Theorem 19]
it holds

\partial\left(I_{H}\circ A\right)(u)=A^{*}\left(\partial I_{H}(A(u))\right).

It follows that at any
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\partial J_{H}(v)=\left\{{\rm div}\,\xi:\xi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ and $\xi(x)\in\partial H(Dv(x))$ a.\,e.\,in $\Omega$}\right\},

where an element of the set on the right acts as in (
2.13
). To conclude, let
T\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
be the unique extension of

v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}f(\cdot,u)\,v\,dx,\qquad v\in V_{u}

granted by Remark
2.5
.
Then
u
also minimises
v\mapsto J_{H}(v)-\langle T,v\rangle
on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, by the continuity of
J_{H}-T
and the density of
V_{u}
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Therefore

0\in\partial(J_{H}-T)(u)=\partial J_{H}(u)-T

which implies (
2.12
) when bracketed with
v\in V_{u}
.

∎

The following easy consequence will be useful later.

Corollary 2.7
.

Suppose that the assumptions of the previous Proposition hold true and additionally that
H
is positively
p
-homogeneous.
Then any energy critical point
u
for
J
fulfills

(2.14)

(\xi,Du)=p\,H(Du)\qquad\text{a.\,e.\,in $\Omega$}

for any
\xi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
obeying (
2.12
). In particular,

(2.15)

p\,\int_{\Omega}H(Du)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,u\,dx.

Proof.

Since
u
minimises the convex functional
J_{u}
defined in (
2.10
), it does so on the half-line through
u
, namely on
\{t\,u:t>0\}\subseteq V_{u}
. The function

h(t):=J_{u}(t\,u)=t^{p}\,\int_{\Omega}H(Du)\,dx-t\,\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,u\,dx

is smooth and convex on
]0,\infty[
, therefore the previous statement forces
h^{\prime}(1)=0
, which is (
2.15
). Let now
\xi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
fulfil (
2.12
), so that testing with
\varphi=u\in V_{u}
, we get

\int_{\Omega}(\xi,Du)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,u\,dx.

By using (
2.15
), we deduce

\int_{\Omega}p\,H(Du)-(\xi,Du)\,dx=0,

but by (
2.5
) the integrand is non-negative, therefore it must vanish a. e. in
\Omega
, proving (
2.14
).
∎

3.
Regularity

The following lemma enucleates the De Giorgi method for proving
L^{\infty}
bounds.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
q\geq 1
and
h_{0}>0
. Let
u
be such that
(u-h_{0})_{+}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
and
u
satisfies the inequalities

(3.1)

Proof.

Let

to be determined and set

We can then apply (

for

By

Remark 3.4

(
On the form of (
3.4
))
.

It is worth analysing the optimality of the form (
3.4
) of the
L^{\infty}
bound in the simple case of the Dirichlet energy, the main point being that one cannot expect the simpler bound

(3.11)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\left((\mu/c)^{\frac{N}{p}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p\,q+(p-q)\,N}}

to be true when
\theta>0
with a constant
C=C(N,p,q)
independent of
\theta
.

Let
\mu\in\ ]0,\lambda_{1}[
, where
\lambda_{1}
is the first eigenvalue of the negative Dirichlet Laplacian in the ball
B_{1}
. For any
\theta>0
, the problem

(3.12)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\mu\,(u+\theta)&\text{in $B_{1}$},\\
u=0&\text{in $\partial B_{1}$}\end{cases}

has, by standard methods, a unique positive solution, which minimises a functional obeying the assumptions of the previous Theorem with
c=1
,
q=p=2
and
\mu,\theta
as in (
2.8
). By comparison,
u
is greater or equal than the solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=\mu\,\theta&\text{in $B_{1}$},\\
v=0&\text{in $\partial B_{1}$},\end{cases}

which is
v(x)=\mu\,\theta\,(1-|x|^{2})/(2\,N)
, therefore

(3.13)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\geq\frac{\mu\,\theta}{2\,N}.

On the other hand, by testing the weak form of (
4.6
) with
u
and by the definition of
\lambda_{1}
, we get

\mu\,\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}+\theta\,u\,dx=\int_{B_{1}}|Du|^{2}\,dx\geq\lambda_{1}\,\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}\,dx

so that, rearranging and using Hölder inequality, we find

(\lambda_{1}-\mu)\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}\,dx\leq\mu\,\theta\,\int_{B_{1}}u\,dx\leq\mu\,\theta\,\left(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\omega_{N}^{1/2}.

The latter gives the bound

\left(\mu^{N/2}\,\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq\omega_{N}^{1/2}\,\frac{\mu^{1+\frac{N}{4}}\,\theta}{\lambda_{1}-\mu},

so that, if

C(N,2,2)\,\omega_{N}^{1/2}\,\frac{\mu^{\frac{N}{4}}}{\lambda_{1}-\mu}<\frac{1}{2\,N},

by (

) we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}>C(N,2,2)\left(\mu^{N/2}\,\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

and the bound (
3.11
) cannot hold.

Once boundedness is proved, assumption (
2.7
) ensures that
f(\cdot,u)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and therefore
V_{u}=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Even if (

) is just a differential inclusion, basic regularity theory is nevertheless at hand, thanks to the theory of quasi-minima developed by Giaquinta and Giusti in
[
15
]
.

Corollary 3.5

(Regularity)
.

Suppose (

) holds true with

and

, as well as (
2.7
). Let

be a bounded energy critical point for

on

,

,

,

,

,

.
If
\Omega

and
\Omega

To make the statement precise, we rewrite (

\sup_{|s|\leq t}\|f(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq h(t)\qquad\forall t>0

for a smooth, increasing

, through the function

which in turn depends on

.
As

is bounded, it minimises the convex functional

defined in (

) over the whole

,
we can adapt the proof of
[
17

to our setting, focusing on the Dirichlet boundary condition.
We focus on the first statement, since the second one will follow from it by choosing smooth

3.14
).

Consider the case

.
For any ball
B_{R}
, not necessarily contained in
\Omega
and

. Notice that

regardless of the relative position of

By using the growth conditions on

Corollary 3.6

(Minimum principles)
.

Let (
2.6
) hold with
0<c<d<\infty
and
p>1
, as well as (
2.7
), and let
u
be a bounded energy critical point for
J
on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
has finite measure.

(1)

If
f(\cdot,t)\geq 0
for
t\leq 0
,
then
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

(2)

If
u\geq 0
and there exist
A\geq 0
,
\delta>0
such that for all
t\in\,]0,\delta[

(3.17)

\inf_{\Omega}f(\cdot,t)\geq-A\,t^{p-1},

then, in each connected component of
\Omega
,
u
is either strictly positive or vanishes identically.

Proof.

We test the minimality with respect to
J_{u}
of
u
against
u_{+}
, to get

\int_{\{u<0\}}H(Du)\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,(u-u_{+})\,dx=-\int_{\{u\leq 0\}}f(x,u)\,u_{-}\,dx

and since
f(\cdot,u)\geq 0
on
u\leq 0
, the term on the left must vanish, implying that
u\geq 0
a. e.. To prove the strict positivity of
u
we further test the minimality of
u
with
v=u+\eta\,(k-u)_{+}
for
k\geq 0
and
\eta
as in (
3.15
) with
B_{R}\subseteq\Omega
. Since

{\rm supp}\,(u-v)\subseteq\{x\in B_{R}:u(x)\leq k\}=:{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)

the minimality relation
J_{u}(u)\leq J_{u}(v)
reads

\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)}H(Du)\,dx\leq\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)}H(Dv)+f(x,u)\,(u-v)\,dx.

Since
u
is bounded, we can assume (
3.17
) holds true for all
t\geq 0
, with a bigger constant
A
also depending on
\delta
and
\|u\|_{\infty}
.
Since
u-v=-\eta\,(k-u)_{+}\leq 0
, (
3.17
) gives

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)}H(Du)\,dx&\leq\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)}H(Dv)+A\,u^{p-1}\,\eta\,(k-u)_{+}\,dx.\\
&\leq\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)}H(Dv)\,dx+A\,k^{p}\,|{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)|.\end{split}

Proceeding with the same calculations of the previous proof one arrives via hole-filling at

\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(k,r)}|Du|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{p}}\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)}(k-u)_{+}^{p}\,dx+A\,k^{p}\,|{\mathcal{B}}(k,R)|

for all
k\geq 0
. Therefore
u\geq 0
belongs to the homogeneous De Giorgi class
DG^{-}_{p}(\Omega)
as in
[
14
]
. A weak Harnack inequality holds true in this setting (see
[
14
, Theorem 2]
), from which the strong minimum principle readily follows.
∎

4.
Dirichlet eigenvalues

For
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty[
convex and obeying (
2.6
) we can define its
first Dirichlet eigenvalue
as

(4.1)

\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx:v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx=1\right\}.

A
first normalized Dirichlet eigenfunction
for
\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)
is a function solving the minimum problem for
\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)
with its constraints. When
\Omega
has finite measure, the embedding
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
is compact, therefore, if in (
2.6
) we assume
c>0
and
p>1
, problem (
4.1
) is always solvable and its value is positive.

The value (
4.1
) makes sense in general, but we focus here on the
p
-positively homogeneous setting for
p>1
. In this case,
\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)
is related to the best constant for the inequality

(4.2)

\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx\leq C_{\rm opt}(H,\Omega)\,\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

which, by positive
p
-homogeneity, satisfies
C_{\rm opt}(H,\Omega)=1/\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if
u
solves problem (
4.1
), any
positive
multiple of
u
attains the best constant in (
4.2
), and will be called a
first Dirichlet eigenfunction
.
Notice that we are not assuming any regularity on
H
except the natural local Lipschitz one and, more substantially, we do not assume that
H
is even.
In the possibly non-even setting, it is convenient to define

(4.3)

\begin{split}\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)&=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx:v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx=1,\ v\geq 0\right\}\\
\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)&=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx:v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx=1,\ v\leq 0\right\},\end{split}

which may in principle be different. We say that
u\geq 0
is a
first normalized Dirichlet positive eigenfunction
for
\lambda^{+}_{1,H}(\Omega)
if
u
realises the minimum for
\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)
(with its constraints) and that
u\leq 0
is a
first normailzed negative Dirichlet eigenfunction
if it does so for
\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)
. Similar considerations as in the unconstrained eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)
are valid for sign-constrained ones and we will drop the term normalized accordingly.

Notice that, without any evennes assumption on
H
, it may
a-priori
no longer be true that if
u
is a first positive eigenfunction then
-u
is a first negative eigenfunction. Moreover, it holds

(4.4)

\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1,\check{H}}^{+}(\Omega),\qquad\check{H}(z)=H(-z),

since
v
obeys the constraints involving
\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)
if and only if
-v
does so for
\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty[
is continuous and positively
p
-homogeneoues, for
p\geq 1
. Then, for any open
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}

(4.5)

\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)=\min\left\{\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)\right\}.

Proof.

Clearly

\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)\leq\min\left\{\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)\right\},

so we prove the opposite inequality. To this end, notice that all the eigenvalues are finite and, for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, choose
v
such that
\|v\|_{p}=1
and

\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx<\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)+\varepsilon.

If
v_{-}\equiv 0
a. e., then
v\geq 0
and

\min\left\{\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)\right\}\leq\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)\leq\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx<\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)+\varepsilon,

so that letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
we have

(4.6)

\min\left\{\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)\right\}\leq\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega).

A similar conclusion holds if
v_{+}=0
, so we can assume that both
v_{+}
and
v_{-}
do not vanish identically. Thus
v_{+}/\|v_{+}\|_{p}
and
-v_{-}/\|v_{-}\|_{p}
are admissible competitors for the corresponding problems (
4.3
), giving, by
p
-homgeneity

\lambda_{1,H}^{\pm}(\Omega)\,\|v_{\pm}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}H(D(\pm v_{\pm}))\,dx.

From
1=\|v\|_{p}=\|v_{+}\|_{p}+\|v_{-}\|_{p}
and
H(0)=0
we infer

\begin{split}\min\left\{\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)\right\}&=\min\left\{\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)\right\}\left(\|v_{+}\|_{p}^{p}+\|v_{-}\|_{p}^{p}\right)\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}H(Dv_{+})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H(D(-v_{-}))\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx\leq\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)+\varepsilon\end{split}

so that (
4.6
) follows again by letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

∎

In general, we do not expect that the two values in (
4.3
) coincide, although we have not been able to produce an explicit example. If equality is always true, however, it cannot follow from convexity,
p
-homogeneity and modularity of the energy alone, as the following toy example shows.

Example 4.2
.

Let
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty[
be

H(x,y)=a\,x_{+}^{2}+x_{-}^{2}+a\,y_{+}^{2}+y_{-}^{2}

for some
a>1
.
We look at
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as the set of functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with
\Omega=\{0,1,2\}
, whose ”gradient” is
Du:=(u(0)-u(1),u(1)-u(2))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The corresponding positive and negative Dirichlet eigenvalue problems are, respectively,

\lambda_{1,H}^{\pm}(\Omega)=\inf\left\{\sum H(Du):u(0)=u(2)=0,u(1)=\pm 1\right\}.

The function
H
is convex, positively
2
-homogeneous and modular, meaning

H(Du)+H(Dv)=H(D\max\{u,v\})+H(D\min\{u,v\}),

due to
[
16
, Remark 2.2]
, so the truncation arguments employed in the previous proof still stand. However

\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)=1<a=\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega).

A similar construction can clearly be generalised to finite sets
\Omega
containing an arbitrary number of points.

A simple case where the sign-constrained eigenvalues are equal is when
\Omega
enjoys a symmetry opposite with
H
.
In order to make a precise statement, let

{\rm Sym}\,(\Omega)=\left\{T\in O(N):T(\Omega)=\Omega+x_{0}\text{ for some $x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\right\}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of the previous proposition, suppose that

(4.7)

{\rm Sym}\,(\Omega)\cap-{\rm Sym}\,(H)\neq\emptyset.

Then

(4.8)

\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega).

Proof.

Fix
T\in{\rm Sym}\,(\Omega)\cap-{\rm Sym}\,(H)
, so that it holds

H(-T(z))=H(z)\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then, for any
v\geq 0
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|v\|_{p}=1
, the function
w=-v\circ T
is non positive, belongs to
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega-x_{0})
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and fulfills
\|w\|_{p}=1
. By translation invariance

\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega-x_{0})

and

\int_{\Omega-x_{0}}H(Dw)\,dx=\int_{\Omega-x_{0}}H(-TDv\circ T)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx.

Therefore
\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)
. Proceeding symmetrically from
v\leq 0
we get the opposite inequality, while applying (
4.5
) concludes the proof of (
4.8
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

By the translation invariance of the energy, the same conclusion holds true if each connected component of
\Omega
separately fulfills assumption (
4.7
).
In particular, (
4.8
) holds true if each connected component has at least one axis of symmetry. Using this fact it is readily shown that (
4.8
) always holds true for arbitrary open
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, open connected subset of
\mathbb{R}
are intervals, half-lines or the whole
\mathbb{R}
. But if
\Omega
has an unbounded connected component, a scaling argument shows that all eigenvalues vanish, while if
\Omega
has only bounded connected components the previous Proposition applies, since open intervals are symmetric with respect to their midpoint.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,+\infty[
is convex, positively
p
-homogeneous for some
p>1
, vanishing only at the origin and that
\Omega
is connected with finite measure. If
u
is a first Dirichlet eigenfunction (sign-constrained or not), then
u\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega)
and never vanishes in
\Omega
.

Proof.

We first observe that any eigenfunction is a critical point for a functional of the form (
1.3
) obeying (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
). Since
H
vanishes only at the origin and
H
is continuous

0<\inf_{|z|=1}H(z)\leq\sup_{|z|=1}H(z)<\infty,

so that by the positive
p
-homogeneity of
H
, (
2.6
) holds with
0<c\leq d<\infty
.
In the unconstrained case, for the functional

J(v)=\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)-\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)|v|^{p}\,dx

it holds
J\geq 0
on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
by construction and
J(u)=0
, hence
u
minimises
J
and is therefore an energy critical point for it. In the sign-constrained case, by considering
\check{H}
instead of
H
and using (
4.4
), it suffices to consider the case when
u
is a positive eigenfunction.
The functional

J_{+}(v)=\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)-\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)\,v_{+}^{p}\,dx

vanishes at
u
and it holds
J_{+}(v)\geq J_{+}(v_{+})
. But
J_{+}(v_{+})\geq 0
by the definition of
\lambda^{+}_{1,H}(\Omega)
, so again
u
minimises
J_{+}
. Clearly in all cases (
2.8
) holds true with
q=p
and Theorem
3.2
implies boundedness, and thus continuity by Corollary
3.5
, of all eigenfunctions. Moreover, Corollary
3.6
applies for positive eigenfunction, since in this case
f(x,t)=t_{+}^{p-1}\geq 0
, yielding the first statement for positive eigenfunctions.

For an arbitrary eigenfunction
u
, we claim that either
u_{+}
or
-u_{-}
are eigenfunctions for
\lambda^{\pm}_{1,H}(\Omega)
respectively, regardless of any assumption on
\Omega
. Indeed, it suffices to prove it for normalized eigenfunctions, so let
u
be a first Dirichlet eigenfunction for
\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)
such that
\|u\|_{p}=1
. If
u
is of constant sign, the claim is trivial. Otherwise, both
u_{+}
and
u_{-}
are non trivial and then
\pm u_{\pm}/\|u_{\pm}\|_{p}
are admissible for the corresponding problems in (
4.3
). By the positive
p
-homogeneity of
H

\lambda_{1,H}^{\pm}(\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{\|u_{\pm}\|_{p}^{p}}\,\int_{\Omega}H(D(\pm u_{\pm}))\,dx

which, summed up, imply

\begin{split}\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)&=\min\left\{\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega)^{+},\lambda_{1,H}^{-}(\Omega)\right\}\left(\|u_{+}\|_{p}^{p}+\|u_{-}\|_{p}^{p}\right)\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}H(Du_{+})+H(D(-u_{-}))\,dx=\lambda_{1,H}(\Omega),\end{split}

so that all inequalities above are actually equalities, proving the claim. It follows that if
u_{+}\neq 0
, then
u_{+}
is a positive eigenfunction and therefore never vanishes by the previous part of the proof, so
u_{-}=0
and
u=u_{+}>0
in
\Omega
. A similar conclusion holds true if
u_{-}\neq 0
, yielding
u=-u_{-}<0
in
\Omega
and concluding the proof.

∎

5.
Main result

In order to prove a Brezis-Oswald comparison principle, we’ll need the following generalisation to the non-smooth setting of the Picone inequality.

Lemma 5.1

(Picone inequality)
.

Suppose
H
is convex and positively
p
-homogeneous. Let
u,v\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
be such that
\inf_{\Omega}u>0
,
\inf_{\Omega}v\geq 0
and let
\xi\in{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\xi(x)\in\partial H(Du(x))
a. e.. Then it holds

(5.1)

\frac{1}{p}\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right)\leq H(Dv)\qquad\text{a.\,e.\,in $\Omega$. }

Suppose additionally that
H
is strictly convex,
\Omega
is connected and equality holds a. e. in (
5.1
). Then
v=k\,u
for some
k>0
.

Proof.

By considering
v+\varepsilon
instead of
v
and then passing to the limit for
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we can suppose that
\inf_{\Omega}v>0
.
First observe that by the assumptions
v^{p}/u^{p-1}\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and Liebnitz rule for weak derivatives ensures

(5.2)

D\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}=(1-p)\,\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p}}\,Du+p\,\frac{v^{p-1}}{u^{p-1}}\,Dv.

Let
\xi\in\partial H(w)
for some
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The following computation are henceforth made at a point
x\in\Omega
such that
\xi=\xi(x)\in\partial H(Du(x))
. In the inequality

H(z)\geq H(Du)+(\xi,z-Du)\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

we choose
z=(u/v)\,Dv
to get, by
p
-homogeneity,

\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p}\,H(Dv)=H\left(\frac{u}{v}\,Dv\right)\geq H(Du)+\frac{u}{v}\,(\xi,Dv)-(\xi,Du)

and by (
2.5
) at
w=Du(x)

\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p}\,H(Dv)\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)(\xi,Du)+\frac{u}{v}\,(\xi,Dv).

Multiplying both sides by
(v/u)^{p}
and using (
5.2
) we finally get

H(Dv)\geq\frac{1}{p}\left(\xi,(1-p)\,\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p}}\,Du+p\,\frac{v^{p-1}}{u^{p-1}}\,Dv\right)=\frac{1}{p}\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right)

and (
5.1
) is proved.

To prove the second statement, suppose equality holds in (
5.1
) a. e. in
\Omega
for some choice of
\xi
obeying
\xi\in\partial H(Du)
a. e.. Then all the previous inequalities are equalities and in particular

H\left(\frac{u}{v}\,Dv\right)=H(Du)+\left(\xi,\frac{u}{v}\,Dv-Du\right)

holds for a. e.
x\in\Omega
. Let, for any such
x
,
w=Du(x)
and
z=(u(x)/v(x))\,Dv(x)
and suppose that
w\neq z
. From
H(z)=H(w)+(\xi,z-w)
and the convexity of
H
we infer

H\big(t\,z+(1-t)\,w\big)\leq t\,H(z)+(1-t)\,H(w)=H(w)+\big(\xi,t\,(z-w)\big),

while from
\xi\in\partial H(z)
we deduce

H(w)+\big(\xi,t\,(z-w)\big)=H(w)+\big(\xi,(t\,z+(1-t)\,w)-w\big)\leq H\big(t\,z+(1-t)\,w\big).

Therefore
H
is linear on the segment from
z
to
w
, contradicting the strict convexity of
H
. Therefore
z=w
, hence

D\log u=D\log v

a. e. in
\Omega
, which implies that
u
and
v
are proportional by the connectedness of
\Omega
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

By inspecting the proof, it is readily seen that assumption
\inf_{\Omega}u>0
can be weakened to

\inf_{\Omega^{\prime}}u>0,\qquad\forall\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega.

We can now prove our main result, Theorem
1.1
, which we now restate in a form convenient for its proof.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open, connected and of finite measure.
For
p>1
suppose that

i)

H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty[
is convex, positively
p
-homogeneous and vanishes only at the origin

ii)

f
fulfills (
2.7
)

iii)

The map
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is non-increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
for a. e.
x\in\Omega
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be a non-negative energy critical point for
J
. The following assertions hold.

(1)

u
minimises
J
among non-negative functions in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(2)

If
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is strictly decreasing,
u
is the unique positive energy critical point for
J
.

(3)

If
H
is strictly convex, any other non-negative energy critical point is a constant multiple of
u
.

(4)

If
H
is strictly convex and
f
doesn’t depend on
x
, either
u
is the unique positive energy critical point for
J
or
u
is a first Dirichlet positive eigenfunction.

Proof.

Since
u\geq 0
by assumption, we can assume
f(x,t)\equiv f(x,0)
for all
t\leq 0
.
Let

h(t)=\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(\cdot,s)\|_{\infty}.

By the monotonicity assumption
iii)
and (
2.7
)

(5.3)

f(x,t)\leq\begin{cases}h(1)\,t^{p-1}&\forall t\geq 1\\
h(1)&\forall t\leq 1.\end{cases}

so that Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.5
apply and
u\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover

(5.4)

f(x,t)\geq\frac{f(x,\|u\|_{\infty})}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}}\,t^{p-1}\geq-\frac{h(\|u\|_{\infty})}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}}\,t^{p-1}\qquad\forall t\in[0,\|u\|_{\infty}]

so that the connectedness of
\Omega
and Corollary
3.6
ensure that
u>0
in
\Omega
.

Since
u
is bounded, it holds
V_{u}=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, hence its Euler-Lagrange equation (
2.12
) holds true for any
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let
\xi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that

\xi(x)\in\partial H(Du(x))\quad\text{a.\,e.},\qquad\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi,D\varphi\right)-f(x,u)\,\varphi\,dx=0\quad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

and let
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be non-negative. It is readily checked that for any
\varepsilon>0
the function
\varphi=v^{p}/(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}
is an admissible test function in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, hence

\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,\frac{v^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\,dx

and by Picone inequality (
5.1
) (notice that
\xi\in\partial H(Du)=\partial H(D(u+\varepsilon))
), we have

(5.5)

p\,\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,\frac{v^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\,dx.

We claim that

(5.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u)}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\,v^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\,v^{p}\,dx.

Indeed, let

\Omega_{+}=\big\{x\in\Omega:f(x,u(x))\geq 0\big\},\qquad\Omega_{-}=\big\{x\in\Omega:f(x,u(x))<0\big\}.

By Beppo-Levi monotone convergence theorem, (
5.6
) holds true with
\Omega_{+}
instead of
\Omega
, while on
\Omega_{-}
it holds, by (
5.4
),

0\geq\frac{f(x,u)}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\,v^{p}\geq-\frac{h(\|u\|_{\infty})}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}}\,\frac{u^{p-1}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\,v^{p}\geq-\frac{h(\|u\|_{\infty})}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}}\,\|v\|_{\infty}^{p}

so that dominated convergence yields (
5.6
) on
\Omega_{-}
as well.
Therefore (
5.6
) is proved, and passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
in (
5.5
) yields

(5.7)

p\,\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\,v^{p}\,dx.

Another monotone convergence argument (separately on
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
) allows to remove the assumption
v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, so that (
5.7
) holds for all
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
v\geq 0
. As a byproduct of this argument, let us notice that the integrand on the right of (
5.7
) is always in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, for any
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
v\geq 0
.

Let us prove assertion
(1)
. To this end, recall that
F(\cdot,v)_{+}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
for all non-negative
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, thanks to the one-sided growth condition (
5.3
). Therefore it suffices to compare
J(u)
with
J(v)
for non-negative
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
F(\cdot,v)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
We rewrite (
5.7
) as

J(v)\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\,\frac{v^{p}}{p}-F(x,v)\,dx

and recall that, by (
2.15
),

J(u)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p}\,f(x,u)\,u-F(x,u)\,dx,

so we have

J(v)-J(u)\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\,\left(\frac{v^{p}}{p}-\frac{u^{p}}{p}\right)+F(x,u)-F(x,v)\,dx.

We claim that the integrand is non-negative. Indeed, for a. e.
x\in\Omega
it holds

F(x,t)-F(x,s)=\int_{s}^{t}\frac{f(x,\sigma)}{\sigma^{p-1}}\,\sigma^{p-1}\,d\sigma

so, considering separately the two cases
t\geq s\geq 0
and
0\leq t\leq s
and using the monotonicity of
\sigma\mapsto f(x,\sigma)/\sigma^{p-1}
, we get

F(x,t)-F(x,s)\geq\frac{f(t)}{t^{p-1}}\,\left(\frac{t^{p}}{p}-\frac{s^{p}}{p}\right)

for all
t,s\geq 0
. Setting
t=u(x)
and
s=v(x)
proves the claim, therefore
J(v)\geq J(u)
for all
v\geq 0
,
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, concluding the proof of the first statement.

To prove statement
(2)
, suppose now that
v
is another nontrivial, non-negative critical point for
J
, so that (
5.7
) holds true with
u
and
v
exchanged. Recalling (
2.15
) we get

\int_{\Omega}f(x,v)\,v\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\,v^{p}\,dx,\qquad\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,u\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}\,u^{p}\,dx

which, summed up, give

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}-\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}\right)\,(u^{p}-v^{p})\,dx\geq 0.

By assumption
ii)
, the latter forces

(5.8)

\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}=\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}

a. e. in
\Omega
, which implies that
u=v
if
t\mapsto f(x,t)/t^{p-1}
is strictly decreasing.

To prove assertion
(3)
, recall that by Picone inequality

p\,H(Dv)-\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)\geq 0,

for all
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Hence, chaining Fatou lemma, the weak form of the equation for
u
, (
5.6
), (
5.8
) and (
2.15
), we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}p\,H(Dv)-\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right)\,dx&\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}p\,H(Dv)-\left(\xi,D\frac{v^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)\,dx\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}p\,H(Dv)-f(x,u)\,\frac{v^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}p\,H(Dv)-\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\,v^{p}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}p\,H(Dv)-f(x,v)\,v\,dx=0\end{split}

so that equality must occur a. e. in the Picone inequality. If
H
is strictly convex, Lemma
5.1
ensures that

(5.9)

u=k\,v\,,\qquad\mbox{for some $k>0$,}

giving assertion
(3)
.

Finally, statement
(4)
amounts to prove that, in the case
f(x,t)=f(t)
, the validity of (
5.9
) for some
k\neq 1
forces
u
to be a first Dirichlet positive eigenfunction.
Let
g(t):=f(t)/t^{p-1}
and assume that
k>1
in (
5.9
). For any fixed
\delta\in\ ]0,\|u\|_{\infty}[
, by continuity
u(\Omega)
is connected and contains
]0,\|u\|_{\infty}-\delta]
. Hence for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
x_{n}\in\Omega
such that

u(x_{n})=\frac{\|u\|_{\infty}-\delta}{k^{n}}

and thus (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) give

\begin{split}g(\|u\|_{\infty}-\delta)&=g(u(x_{0}))=g(v(x_{0}))=g(u(x_{0})/k)=g((\|u\|_{\infty}-\delta)/k)=g(u(x_{1}))\\
&=g(v(x_{1}))=g(u(x_{1})/k)=g((\|u\|_{\infty}-\delta)/k^{2})=\dots=g((\|u\|_{\infty}-\delta)/k^{n})\end{split}

for any
n\geq 0
. Therefore
g
, being continuous and non-increasing, is constantly equal to some
\lambda\geq 0
on
]0,\|u\|_{\infty}-\delta]
. By letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, the same conclusion holds on
]0,\|u\|_{\infty}]
. Similarly, if
0<k<1
, we infer that
g\equiv\lambda\geq 0
on

]0,\|v\|_{\infty}]=\ ]0,\|u\|_{\infty}/k]\supseteq\ ]0,\|u\|_{\infty}].

Notice that then
f(t)=\lambda\,t^{p-1}
for
t\in\left[0,\|u\|_{\infty}\right]
, hence

(5.10)

F(t)=\frac{\lambda}{p}\,t^{p}\qquad\text{ for all $t\in[0,\|u\|_{\infty}]$}.

By (
2.15
) it holds

J(u)=\int_{\Omega}H(Du)-\frac{\lambda}{p}\,u^{p}\,dx=0

and by the first assertion of the Theorem

J(v)\geq J(u)\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\ v\geq 0.

If
0\leq v\leq\|u\|_{\infty}
, from the last two displays and (
5.10
) we infer

\frac{\lambda}{p}\int_{\Omega}v^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}H(Dv)\,dx.

By positive
p
-homogeneity, the latter holds for any bounded
v\geq 0
,
v\neq 0
, (simply writing it for
w=v\,\|u\|_{\infty}/\|v\|_{\infty}
, which fulfills
0\leq w\leq\|u\|_{\infty}
) and thus for any
v\geq 0
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
by a monotone convergence argument. It follows that
\lambda/p\leq\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)
and
J(u)=0
implies

\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)\,\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}H(Du)\,dx=\frac{\lambda}{p}\,\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\,dx\leq\lambda_{1,H}^{+}(\Omega)\,\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\,dx

i. e.,
u
is a first Dirichlet positive eigenfunction, as claimed.

Another Approach to Juhl’s Conformally Covariant Differential Operators from 𝑺 𝒏 \boldsymbol{S^{n}} to 𝑺 𝒏 − 𝟏 \boldsymbol{S^{n-1}}
Jean-Louis CLERC

Abstract
A family ( 𝐃 λ ) λ ∈ ℂ ({\mathbf{D}}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}} of differential operators on the sphere S n S^{n} is constructed. The operators are conformally covariant for the action of the subgroup of conformal transformations of S n S^{n} which preserve the smaller sphere S n − 1 ⊂ S n S^{n-1}\subset S^{n} . The family of conformally covariant differential operators from S n S^{n} to S n − 1 S^{n-1} introduced by A. Juhl is obtained by composing these operators on S n S^{n} and taking restrictions to S n − 1 S^{n-1} .

Let

be the

\displaystyle S=\big\{x=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\ x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}=1\big\}.

Let
\mathbf{E}
be the vector space

\displaystyle Q(\mathbf{x})=[(t,x),(t,x)]=t^{2}-|x|^{2}\qquad\text{for}\quad\mathbf{x}=(t,x),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad x\in E.

For

\displaystyle t({\bf x})=t,\qquad{\bf x}_{E}=x.

The space of isotropic lines

\displaystyle S\ni x\longmapsto d_{x}=\mathbb{R}(1,x)\in\mathcal{S},\qquad\mathcal{S}\ni d\longmapsto d\cap\{t=1\}.

Let

\displaystyle\big(1,g(x)\big)=t\big(g.(1,x)\big)^{-1}g.(1,x).

Set, for

and

\displaystyle\kappa(g,x)=t\big(g.(1,x)\big)^{-1}.

Clearly

satisfies the

,

and any

\displaystyle\kappa(g_{1}g_{2},x)=\kappa\big(g_{1},g_{2}(x)\big)\kappa(g_{2},x).

This action of

on

is known to be

\displaystyle|Dg(x)\xi|=\kappa(g,x)|\xi|,

and hence

of

at

.

Associated to the action of
G
on
S

, which, from the point of view of harmonic analysis is the

of

. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda}(g)f(x)=\kappa\big(g^{-1},x\big)^{\lambda}f\big(g^{-1}(x)\big).

The formula defines a (smooth) representation

of

The Knapp–Stein intertwining operators are a major tool in harmonic analysis of

(as of any semi-simple Lie group, see, e.g.,

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda-\frac{n}{2})}\int_{S}|x-y|^{-2n+2\lambda}f(y)dy,

4
The expression of
\boldsymbol{{\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}}_{\lambda}}
in the non-compact picture

Consider the point
-{\mathbf{1}}=(-1,0,\dots,0)\in S
. The stereographic projection with source at
-{\mathbf{1}}
provides a diffeomorphism from
S\setminus\{-\mathbf{1}\}
onto the hyperplane
\{x_{n}=1\}
. The inverse map (up to a scaling by a factor 2)
c\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow S
is given by

\displaystyle c(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{1-|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\dfrac{2\xi_{1}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\\
\vdots\\
\dfrac{2\xi_{n}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.1)

When using this local chart on
S
, we refer to the
non-compact picture
, as a reference to semi-simple harmonic analysis.

Geometric considerations (or an elementary computation) show that, for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle|c(\xi)-c(\eta)|^{2}=\kappa(c,\xi)|\xi-\eta|^{2}\kappa(c,\eta),

where, for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

\displaystyle\kappa(c,\xi)=2\big(1+|\xi|^{2}\big)^{-1}.

There is an infinitesimal version of this result, namely

\displaystyle|Dc(\xi)\eta|=\kappa(c,\xi)|\eta|

for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This last statement shows that
c
is conformal from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with its standard Euclidean structure into
S
.

The action of
g
on
S
can be transferred as a (rational) action of
G
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, namely
c^{-1}\circ g\circ c
. For notational convenience, we still denote this action on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
(g,\xi)\longmapsto g(\xi)
,
g\in G
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As the map
c
is conformal, the transferred action of
G
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is still conformal. For
g\in G
defined at
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we let
\kappa(g,\xi)
be the corresponding conformal factor of
g
at
\xi
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. For
f\in C^{\infty}(S)
let
C_{\lambda}(f)
be defined by

\displaystyle C_{\lambda}(f)(\xi)=\kappa(c,\xi)^{\lambda}f(c(\xi)),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and let
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
be the image of
C_{\lambda}
. It is easily proved that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\subset\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big),

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
stands for the Schwartz space on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for its dual, the space of tempered distributions.

The representation
\pi_{\lambda}
can be transferred in the non-compact model, using
C_{\lambda}
as intertwining map, i.e., set

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(g)=C_{\lambda}\circ\pi_{\lambda}(g)\circ C_{\lambda}^{-1}.

Using the cocycle property of
\kappa
,
\rho_{\lambda}
can be realized as

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(g)f(\xi)=\kappa\big(g^{-1},\xi\big)^{\lambda}f\big(g^{-1}(\xi)\big),

where
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
and
g\in G
.

Similarly, the Knapp–Stein operators can be transferred to the non-compact picture. For
s\in\mathbb{C}
, consider the expression

\displaystyle h_{s}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\big)}|\xi|^{s},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>-n
,
h_{s}
is locally summable with moderate growth at infinity, hence defines a tempered distribution. The (
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued) function
s\mapsto h_{s}
can be extended by analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
and the
\Gamma
factor in the definition of
h_{s}
is so chosen that it extends as an
entire
function with values in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(for more details see, e.g.,
[
5
]
).

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the Knapp–Stein operator
J_{\lambda}
is given by

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}f=h_{-2n+2\lambda}\star f,

or more concretely

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}f(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}\big)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi-\eta|^{-2n+2\lambda}f(\eta)d\eta.

As for any
s\in\mathbb{C}

h_{s}
is a tempered distribution,
J_{\lambda}
maps
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}f=\big(C_{n-\lambda}\circ I_{\lambda}\circ C_{\lambda}^{-1}\big)f.

As
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

-\frac{n}{2}+1
and which coincide on
\Xi^{+}
. Then
\square F_{1}
and
\square F_{2}
coincide on
\Xi^{+}
.

Proof.

The function
F_{1}-F_{2}
vanishes on
\Xi^{+}
. Notice that
dQ(\mathbf{x})\neq 0
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi^{+}
. Hence, there exists a smooth function
G
defined on a neighborhood of
\Xi^{+}
such that

\displaystyle F_{1}(\mathbf{x})-F_{2}(\mathbf{x})=Q(\mathbf{x})G(\mathbf{x}).

Moreover,
G
is positively homogeneous of degree
-\frac{n}{2}-1
. Now, for any smooth function
H
on
\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle\square(QH)=2(n+2)H+4EH+Q\square H,

where
E=t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
is the Euler operator. As
G
is homogeneous of degree
-\frac{n}{2}-1
,

\displaystyle EG(\mathbf{x})=\left(-\frac{n}{2}-1\right)G(\mathbf{x}),

and hence
\square(QG)(x)=0
for
x\in\Xi^{+}
. The lemma follows.
∎

The next result is a reformulation of the previous lemma.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{H}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
. Extend
F
smoothly to a positively homogeneous function of degree
-\frac{n}{2}+1
to neighborhood of
\Xi^{+}
. Then the restriction to
\Xi^{+}
of
\square F
does not depend on the extension.

The operator
\square
induces a map from
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{n}{2}+1}
and intertwines the action of
G
. Let
\Delta_{S}
be the operator defined on
C^{\infty}(S)
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{S}=R_{\frac{n}{2}+1}\circ\square\circ R^{-1}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}.

The invariance of
\square
(see (
7.2
)) and the covariance of
R_{\lambda}
(see (
7.1
)) imply the following proposition.

Proposition 7.3
.

The operator
\Delta_{S}

(
conformal Laplacian or Yamabe operator on
S)
is a differential operator on
S
which is covariant with respect to
(\pi_{\frac{n}{2}-1},\pi_{\frac{n}{2}+1})
.

Let
\mathbf{B}_{\mu}
be the differential operator on
\mathbf{E}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\mu}F(\mathbf{x})=x_{n}\square F(\mathbf{x})-2\mu\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{n}}.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
F
be a smooth function on
\mathbf{E}
. Then, on
\{x_{n}\neq 0\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\mu}F(\mathbf{x})=x_{n}|x_{n}|^{-\mu}\square\big(|x_{n}|^{\mu}F\big)(\mathbf{x})+\mu(\mu-1)\frac{1}{x_{n}}F(\mathbf{x}).

(7.3)

Proof.

By an elementary calculation,

\displaystyle\square\big(|x_{n}|^{\mu}F\big)(\mathbf{x})=|x_{n}|^{\mu}\square F(\mathbf{x})-2\mu\sgn(x_{n})|x_{n}|^{\mu-1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{n}}(\mathbf{x})-\mu(\mu-1)|x_{n}|^{\mu-2}F(\mathbf{x}),

so that

\displaystyle\square\big(|x_{n}|^{\mu}F\big)+\mu(\mu-1)|x_{n}|^{\mu-2}F=\sgn(x_{n})|x_{n}|^{\mu-1}\mathbf{B}_{\mu}F.

The conclusion follows, by noticing that
x_{n}=\sgn(x_{n})|x_{n}|
.
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
g\in G^{\prime}
. Then for
F
a smooth function on
\mathbf{E}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\mu}(F\circ g)=(\mathbf{B}_{\mu}F)\circ g.

Proof.

As
g\in G^{\prime}
, the coordinate
x_{n}
is unchanged by the action of
g
, and the action of
g
commutes with
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
and with
\square
. The result follows.
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
. Extend
F
smoothly to a neighborhood of
\Xi^{+}
as a positively homogeneous function of degree
-\lambda
. Then the restriction to
\Xi^{+}
of
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F

does not depend on the extension.

Proof.

The function
|x_{n}|^{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(\mathbf{x})
is homogenous of degree
-\frac{n}{2}+1
, and hence, by Lemma
7.2
, for
x\in\Xi^{+}
,
\square\big(|x_{n}|^{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F\big)(\mathbf{x})
only depend on the values of
F
on
\Xi^{+}
. Hence, by (
7.3
), for
\mathbf{x}
in
\Xi^{+}
,
x_{n}\neq 0
,
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(\mathbf{x})
does not depend on the extension of
F
. The result follows by continuity.
∎

Proposition 7.7
.

The differential operator
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}
induces a map from
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
into
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda+1}
, which commutes with the action of
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The invariance follows from Proposition
7.5
.
∎

Having constructed a covariant operator in the ambient space model, it is possible to express it both in the non-compact and in the compact picture.

Proposition 7.8
.

The local expression of the operator
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}
in the non compact picture is equal to
-E_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
f
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall the map
c
(cf. (
4.1
)) which realizes the passage from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
S
. Its inverse is given by

\displaystyle S\setminus\{-\mathbf{1}\}\ni(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\longmapsto\left(\frac{x_{1}}{1+x_{0}},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{1+x_{0}}\right).

So map
f
to a function on
S
by

\displaystyle C_{\lambda}^{-1}f(x)=(1+x_{0})^{-\lambda}f\left(\frac{x_{1}}{1+x_{0}},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{1+x_{0}}\right).

Consider the function
F
on
\mathbf{E}
defined by

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x})=(t+x_{0})^{-\lambda}f\left(\frac{x_{1}}{t+x_{0}},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{t+x_{0}}\right).

Then
F
is homogenous of degree
-\lambda
and coincide on
S
with
C_{\lambda}^{-1}f
. To compute
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F
, first observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{0}},\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial t^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial x_{0}^{2}},

so that

\displaystyle\square F=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(\mathbf{x})=-(t+x_{0})^{-\lambda-2}x_{n}(\Delta f)\left(\frac{x_{1}}{t+x_{0}},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{t+x_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(\mathbf{x}) =}{}-2\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1\right)(t+x_{0})^{-\lambda}(t+x_{0})^{-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi_{n}}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{t+x_{0}},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{t+x_{0}}\right).

Now letting
\mathbf{x}=(1,c(\xi))
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(1,c(\xi))=-\xi_{n}\Delta f(\xi)-(2\lambda-n+2)\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi_{n}}(\xi).

A comparison with (
4.6
) implies the result.
∎

Proposition 7.9
.

The expression of the operator
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}
on
S
is given by

\displaystyle x_{n}|x_{n}|^{-\lambda+\frac{n}{2}-1}\Delta_{S}\circ|x_{n}|^{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}+\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1\right)\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}\right)\frac{1}{x_{n}}.

The expression, a priori defined on
x_{n}\neq 0
can be continued continuously to all of
S
.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(S)
. Then

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x})=\big(x_{0}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{-\lambda}f\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}}},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}}}\right)

is a function defined on
\mathbf{E}\setminus\{0\}
which is positively homogeneous of degree
-\lambda
and such that for
x\in S
,

\displaystyle F(1,x)=f(x).

By (
7.3
) with
\mu=\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1
and for
\mathbf{x}\neq 0
,
x_{n}\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(\mathbf{x})=x_{n}|x_{n}|^{-\lambda+\frac{n}{2}+1}\square\big(|x_{n}|^{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F\big)(\mathbf{x})+\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1\right)\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}\right)\frac{1}{x_{n}}F(\mathbf{x}).

The function
|x_{n}|^{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(\mathbf{x})
is positively homogeneous of degree
-\frac{n}{2}+1
. Thus, by Lemma
7.2
and the definition of the Yamabe operator
\Delta_{S}
, for
x\in S
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}F(1,x)=x_{n}|x_{n}|^{-\lambda+\frac{n}{2}-1}\Delta_{S}\big(|x_{n}|^{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}f\big)(x)+\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1\right)\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}\right)\frac{1}{x_{n}}f(x),

from which the statement follows, at least for
x_{n}\neq 0
. As
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1}
induces a smooth differential operator on
S
, the formula determines the operator on all of
S
by continuity.
∎

Exponential convergence of recursive least squares with forgetting factor for multiple-output systems
Sven Brüggemann, Robert R. Bitmead

Abstract
We extend results of the recursive-least-squares-with-forgetting-factor identifier for single-input-single-output systems to the multiple-output case by deriving the corresponding minimized objective function and by showing exponential convergence of the estimation error.

Introduction

This work revolves around the popular recursive-least-squares-with-forgetting-factor (RLSFF) estimation algorithm for multiple-output (MO) systems. The idea of a recursive formulation goes back to
[
4
]
, where it is shown that the estimate converges to a value which minimizes the sum of output errors, whereby the outputs’ weights reduce exponentially with respect to their time of measurement; hence, the name forgetting factor. These results are established for single-input-single-output (SISO) systems and extended to MO systems in
[
8
]
. Since, the RLSFF algorithm has been proposed in many different variations, particulalry for the SISO case (see e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
). Also, exponential convergence of the estimation error under the influence of the RLSFF identifier from
[
4
]
is shown in
[
9
]
. The latter work contains a deterministic analysis considering SISO systems without noise.

Despite the popularity of the RLSFF algorithm, to our best knowledge, a concise convergence result of the RLSFF for the MO case has not been published. Given the literature on adaptive control algorithms using a recursive-least-squares identifier in one way or the other, whose analysis is restricted to SISO systems (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
), especially within the framework of model predictive control (see e.g.
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
); and the recent article
[
8
]
dealing with MO systems, we are convinced that SISO-equivalent results for the MO case are of interest and facilitate further research in adaptive control; especially in the area of adaptive model predictive control, which has attracted relatively little attention
[
11
]
.

Our note therefore closes the gap between the scalar and the MO case by extending the results from
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
. Under the usual assumption of persistence of excitation, we show that the RLSFF algorithm for MO systems minimizes a criterion for which the multiple outputs are weighted not only by their time of measurement (through the forgetting factor), but also among each other (via a user-defined weighting matrix). Furthermore, we demonstrate that the estimation error convergences exponentially to the origin.

2
Main results

Consider the system

\displaystyle y_{k+1}=\psi^{T}_{k}\theta,

(1)

where
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the measurable output,
\psi_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
the regressor matrix, respectively at time
k
, and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the constant parameter vector. Suppose that the parameter vector is uncertain and, thus, to be estimated. Toward this end, consider the following RLSFF algorithm.

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{k+1}=\hat{\theta}_{k}+P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}\left(y_{k+1}-\psi_{k}^{T}\hat{\theta}_{k}\right),

(2)

where
D_{k}=\lambda T+\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}
with
T=T^{T}>0\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and constant forgetting factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and

\displaystyle P_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\Big(I-P_{k}\psi_{k+1}D_{k+1}^{-1}\psi_{k+1}^{T}\Big)P_{k},

(3)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle P_{k-1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\lambda P^{-1}_{k-2}+\psi_{k-1}T^{-1}\psi_{k-1}^{T}.

(4)

where
P_{-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is symmetric positive definite. Define persistence of excitation as follows.

Definition 1
.

The matrix sequence
\{\psi_{k}\}
is said to be persistently exciting (PE) if for some constant
S
and all
j
there exist positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

\displaystyle 0<\alpha I\leq\sum_{i=j}^{j+S}\psi_{i}\psi_{i}^{T}\leq\beta I<\infty.

The following lemma is akin to the result in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
\{\psi_{k}\}
is PE. Then, for
k\geq S
, the algorithm in (
2
) and (
3
) converges to the value
\theta
which minimizes

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda^{k-i}|y_{i}-\psi^{T}_{i-1}\theta|^{2}_{T^{-1}}.

.

PROOF.

The proof is analogous to that of
[
8
, Theorem 2]
and hence omitted for brevity.

\blacksquare

We have thus presented the objective which is minimized by the RLSFF algorithm in (
2
) and (
3
). In contrast to the existing literature, we explicitly incorporate a weight for each respective output via the matrix
T
. We now wish to obtain an exponentially stable estimation error

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}_{k}

\displaystyle=\theta-\hat{\theta}_{k}.

The corresponding theorem as an extension of
[
9
]
follows.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\{\psi_{k}\}
is PE. Then, for any initial condition
\tilde{\theta}_{0}
, the estimation error
\tilde{\theta}_{k}
converges exponentially to
\theta
, i.e. for any
\tilde{\theta}_{0}
there exist
\gamma>0
such that for all
k\geq S

\displaystyle|\tilde{\theta}_{k}|^{2}\leq\gamma\lambda^{k}|\tilde{\theta}_{0}|^{2}.

PROOF.

The proof is divided into two parts. Part I establishes a lower bound on
P^{-1}_{k}
. This is then used in part II to show exponential stability of the estimation error.

Part I
:
Recollect that if
B
is symmetric, then for any matrix
A
,

\displaystyle ABA^{T}\geq\lambda_{min}(B)AA^{T}.

This follows by definition of a positive definite matrix;

\displaystyle x^{T}(ABA^{T}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{min}(B)AA^{T})x

\displaystyle=x^{T}ABA^{T}x-\lambda_{min}(B)x^{T}AA^{T}x

\displaystyle=(A^{T}x)B(A^{T}x)-\lambda_{min}(B)x^{T}AA^{T}x

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{min}(B)(A^{T}x)A^{T}x-\lambda_{min}(B)x^{T}AA^{T}x

\displaystyle=0,

where we use the fact that
\lambda_{min}(B)|x|^{2}\leq|x|_{B}^{2}
. It follows that if
\{\psi_{k}\}
is PE, then

\displaystyle P^{-1}_{j-1}+\dots+P^{-1}_{j+S-1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle(\mkern-1.5mu\ref{eq:PinvRecursion}\mkern-1.5mu)}}{{\geq}}\sum_{k=j}^{j+S}\psi_{k-1}T^{-1}\psi_{k-1}^{T}

\displaystyle~\geq\lambda_{min}(T^{-1})\alpha I.

for all
k\geq S
. Following
[
9
, Lemma 1]
leads to the lower bound

\displaystyle P^{-1}_{k-1}\geq\frac{\lambda_{min}(T^{-1})\alpha(\lambda^{-1}-1)}{\lambda^{-(S+1)}-1}>0

(5)

for all
k\geq S
.

Part II
:
By (
2
), one can write recursively the estimation error as

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}_{k+1}=\left(I-P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}\psi^{T}_{k}\right)\tilde{\theta}_{k}.

(6)

Consider the Lyapunov function candidate

\displaystyle W_{k}=\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}^{-1}\tilde{\theta}_{k}.

(7)

Then, using the recursions in (
4
) and (
6
) yields

\displaystyle W_{k+1}-W_{k}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}_{k+1}^{T}P_{k}^{-1}\tilde{\theta}_{k+1}-\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}^{-1}\tilde{\theta}_{k}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{T}\left[(\lambda-1)P_{k-1}^{-1}-\lambda\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}+C\right]\tilde{\theta}_{k},

(8)

where

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\psi_{k}\left[T^{-1}-D_{k}^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}T^{-1}-\lambda D_{k}^{-1}\right.

\displaystyle\quad-T^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}+\lambda D_{k}^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\left.+D^{-1}_{k}\psi^{T}_{k}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}T^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}\right]\psi_{k}^{T}.

We demonstrate now that
C
is equal to the zero matrix. To this end, multiply the inner term of
C
by
T^{1/2}
from both sides respectively and obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle-T^{1/2}D_{k}^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}T^{-1/2}-\lambda T^{1/2}D_{k}^{-1}T^{1/2}

\displaystyle-T^{-1/2}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}T^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\lambda T^{1/2}D_{k}^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}T^{1/2}

\displaystyle+T^{1/2}D_{k}^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}T^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}T^{1/2}.

(9)

By defining

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=\psi_{k}T^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\bar{D}

\displaystyle=T^{-1/2}D_{k}T^{-1/2}=\lambda I+\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}

(10)

one has that

\displaystyle T^{1/2}D_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=(D_{k}T^{-1/2})^{-1}=(\lambda T^{1/2}+\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}T^{-1/2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(T^{1/2}(\lambda+T^{-1/2}\psi_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}\psi_{k}T^{-1/2})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(T^{1/2}(\lambda+\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\bar{D}^{-1}T^{-1/2},

so that for (
2
) it follows:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle-\bar{D}^{-1}\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}-\lambda\bar{D}^{-1}

\displaystyle-\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}\bar{D}^{-1}+\lambda\bar{D}^{-1}\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}\bar{D}^{-1}

\displaystyle+\bar{D}^{-1}\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}\bar{D}^{-1}.

Observe that this can be reformulated as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle-\bar{D}^{-1}\overbrace{\left(\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}+\lambda I\right)}^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle(\mkern-1.5mu\ref{eq:Dbar}\mkern-1.5mu)}}{{=}}\bar{D}}

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{D}^{-1}\left(-\bar{D}\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}+\lambda\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.+\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}\bar{\psi}^{T}P_{k-1}\bar{\psi}\right)\bar{D}^{-1},

which is clearly zero and, thus, so is
C
.

Therefore, the difference related to the Lyapunov function candidate in (
2
)

\displaystyle W_{k+1}-W_{k}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{T}\left[(\lambda-1)P_{k-1}^{-1}-\lambda\psi_{k}D_{k}^{-1}\psi_{k}^{T}\right]\tilde{\theta}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda-1)\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{T}P_{k-1}^{-1}\tilde{\theta}_{k}

\displaystyle=(\lambda-1)W_{k},

so that

\displaystyle W_{k+1}\leq\lambda W_{k}\leq\lambda^{k+1}W_{0}=\lambda^{k+1}\tilde{\theta}_{0}^{T}P_{-1}^{-1}\tilde{\theta}_{0}.

Finally, combining this inequality with the definition of
W_{k}
in (
7
) and the lower bound of
P_{k-1}^{-1}
in (
5
) leads to

\displaystyle|\tilde{\theta}_{k}|^{2}\leq\underbrace{\frac{\lambda^{-(S+1)}-1}{\lambda_{min}(T^{-1})\alpha(\lambda^{-1}-1)}\lambda_{max}\left(P_{-1}^{-1}\right)}_{\eqqcolon\gamma}\lambda^{k}|\tilde{\theta}_{k}|^{2}

for all
k\geq S
.
\blacksquare

Remark 4
.

Note that exponential stability of the estimation error implies a bounded error in the presence of bounded additive noise. Thus, for additive bounded noise on system (
1
), the estimation error converges to a ball centered on the true parameter vector with a radius proportional to the bound on the disturbance.

3
Conclusion

We have shown that the RLSFF estimation algorithm for MO systems shares the properties of those for single output systems, i.e. it minimizes a similar cost function, where outputs are weighted among one another by a given matrix, and it induces exponential convergence of the estimate to the true parameter vector.

The polarimeter vector for τ → 3 ​ π ​ ν τ \tau\to 3\pi\nu_{\tau} decays
Vladimir Cherepanov, Christian Veelken

Abstract
The polarimeter vector of the τ \tau represents an optimal observable for the measurement of the τ \tau spin. In this paper we present an algorithm for the computation of the τ \tau polarimeter vector for the decay channels τ − → π − ​ π + ​ π − ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau} and τ − → π − ​ π 0 ​ π 0 ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau} . The algorithm is based on a model for the hadronic current in these decay channels, which was fitted to data recorded by the CLEO experiment [ 1 ] .



1
Introduction

The polarimeter vector
h
of the
\tau
lepton represents an optimal observable for the measurement of the
\tau
spin orientation
s
.
Measurements of
s
allow the determination of the Weinberg angle
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
and of the
CP
properties of the Higgs boson
[
8
,
9
]
.
The term
optimal
refers to the fact that
h
provides equal or higher sensitivity for the measurement of
s
compared to alternative observables
[
10
]
.
The polarimeter vector is a function of the momenta of the particles produced in the
\tau
decay and needs to be computed in the restframe of the
\tau
lepton.

While the charged decay products of the
\tau
are typically reconstructed with high efficiency and purity, the reconstruction of the neutral decay products is experimentally more challenging. The reconstruction of the hadronic
\tau
decay mode further requires the classification of individual charged and neutral particles as either resulting from the hadronic
\tau
decay or not, which constitutes a non-trivial task, in particular at the LHC. See Refs.
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
for recent results on the
\tau
decay mode reconstruction. The computation of the
\tau
lepton restframe requires the reconstruction of the neutrino produced in the
\tau
decay. The use of energy and momentum conservation allows for a direct reconstruction of the neutrino momentum in electron–positron collisions
[
19
,
20
]
, while more sophisticated methods are in use at the LHC
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

It has been demonstrated
[
25
]
that all hadronic decay channels of the
\tau
lepton provide the same optimal sensitivity, referred to as
\tau
spin analyzing power
, provided that the momentum of the
\tau
lepton is known and all mesons produced in the
\tau
decay are reconstructed and measured with negligible experimental resolution. The spin analyzing power of leptonic
\tau
decays is limited to about
40\%
relative to the sensitivity of hadronic
\tau
decays
[
10
]
.

Expressions for the
\tau
polarimeter vector for the decay channels
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
are given in Ref.
[
26
]
.
In this paper, we describe an algorithm for computing the polarimeter vector for the decay channels
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
. Both decay channels proceed predominantly through an intermediate
\textrm{a}_{1}\textrm{(1260)}
resonance and are related by isospin symmetry.
We collectively refer to both channels as
\tau\to 3\pi\nu_{\tau}
decays.
Corresponding expressions for
\tau^{+}
decays can be obtained via charge conjugation.
The computation of
h
proceeds in two steps. The first step is specific to the decay channels
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
. It pertains to the computation of the hadronic current and is detailed in Section
2
.
The second step is identical for all hadronic
\tau
decays and is described in Section
3
.
We conclude the paper with a summary in Section
4
.
The code of the algorithm presented in this paper is available at Ref.
[
27
]
.

2
Computation of the hadronic current

The hadronic current, denoted by the symbol
J
, encapsulates the dynamics of hadronic interactions that are specific to each
\tau
decay channel.
For
\tau\to 3\pi\nu_{\tau}
decays,
it is not possible to derive an analytic expression for
J
based on theory.
Instead, one needs to assume a model and then determine the parameters of the model by a fit to experimental data.
Different models for the decay
\tau\to 3\pi\nu_{\tau}
have been proposed
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
and fitted to data
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
.
Our algorithm is based on the fit performed by the CLEO collaboration to data recorded in the decay channel
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}

[
1
]
.
The CLEO fit and the fit based on the Resonance Chiral theory
[
33
,
34
,
40
,
41
]
are implemented in recent versions of the Monte Carlo (MC) program
TAUOLA

[
26
,
38
,
39
]
. See Ref.
[
42
]
for a comparison of both fits. The CLEO fit is also used to compute the hadronic current for the decay channels
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
in the program TauSpinner
[
43
]
. The latter allows to simulate different
\tau
polarization and spin correlation states via MC reweighting.

The CLEO fit includes seven resonances to model the substructure of the
\textrm{a}_{1}\textrm{(1260)}
decay and makes the following ansatz for the contribution of these resonances to the hadronic current:

\textrm{J}^{\mu}=B_{\textrm{a}_{1}}(s)\,\sum_{i}\,\beta_{i}\,j_{i}^{\mu}\,,

(1)

where the symbol
B_{\textrm{a}_{1}}(s)
denotes the Breit-Wigner function of the
\textrm{a}_{1}\textrm{(1260)}
meson,
\beta_{i}
are complex coupling constants, and the symbols
j_{i}^{\mu}
refer to the amplitudes of the seven resonances.

The Breit-Wigner function
B_{\textrm{a}_{1}}(s)
of the
\textrm{a}_{1}\textrm{(1260)}
meson in Eq.(
1
) is given by:

B_{\textrm{a}_{1}}(s)=\frac{1}{s-m_{0\textrm{a}_{1}}^{2}+i\,m_{0\textrm{a}_{1}}^{2}\Gamma_{\textrm{tot}}^{\textrm{a}_{1}}(s)}\,,

(2)

where
s=a^{2}
corresponds to the square of the center-of-mass energy of the three-pion system.
The symbol
a
denotes the four-momentum of the
3\pi
system:
a=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}
,
where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
refer to the two pions of same charge and
p_{3}
refers to the pion of opposite charge.
We use
m_{0\textrm{a}_{1}}^{2}=1.331

GeV
, given in Table VI of Ref.
[
1
]
, for the mass of the
\textrm{a}_{1}\textrm{(1260)}
resonance.

The width
\Gamma_{\textrm{tot}}^{\textrm{a}_{1}}(s)
increases with
s
as new decay channels open up.
We have digitized the graph for
\Gamma_{\textrm{tot}}^{\textrm{a}_{1}}(s)
shown in Fig. 9 (b) of Ref.
[
1
]
and interpolate linearly between the digitized values.
Up to an overall sign, which cancels when computing the polarimeter vector
h
, the hadronic current is symmetric with respect to the interchange
p_{1}\leftrightarrow p_{2}
.

The complex coupling constants
\beta_{i}
as well as the masses and widths of the seven resonances are parameters of the fit.
The values determined by the fit to CLEO data for the decay channel
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
are given in Tables

and
2

L
denotes the angular momentum of the resonances, where
\textrm{L}=0

2

where

q_{1}=p_{2}-p_{3}
,
q_{2}=p_{3}-p_{1}
,
q_{3}=p_{1}-p_{2}
,

h_{i}=p_{j}+p_{k}
(with
i\not=j\not=k\not=i

Q_{i}=h_{i}-p_{i}
, and
s_{i}=h_{i}^{2}

\rho^{\prime}
refers to the
\rho\textrm{(1450)}

The tensor
T^{\mu\nu}

T^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-a^{\mu}a^{\nu}/a^{2}

g^{\mu\nu}
is the metric tensor.

s_{i}

B_{\textrm{Y}}^{\textrm{L}}(s_{i})=\frac{m_{0\textrm{Y}}^{2}}{(m_{0\textrm{Y}}^{2}-s_{i})-i\,m_{0\textrm{Y}}\,\Gamma^{\textrm{Y},\textrm{L}}(s_{i})}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\Gamma^{\textrm{Y},\textrm{L}}(s_{i})=\Gamma_{0}^{\textrm{Y}}\,\left(\frac{k^{\prime}_{i}}{k^{\prime}_{0}}\right)^{2\textrm{L}+1}\,\frac{m_{0\textrm{Y}}}{\sqrt{s_{i}}}\,.

(4)

The decay momentum
k^{\prime}_{i}
is given by:

k^{\prime}_{i}=\frac{\sqrt{\left(s_{i}-(m_{j}+m_{k})^{2}\right)\,\left(s_{i}-(m_{j}-m_{k})^{2}\right)}}{2\sqrt{s_{i}}}\quad\mbox{(with $i\not=j\not=k\not=i$)}\,.

(5)

The symbol
k^{\prime}_{0}
refers to the decay momentum evaluated at
s_{i}=m_{0\textrm{Y}}^{2}
.

\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
are related by isospin symmetry, one expects this model to be valid for the decay channel
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
also.
A subsequent CLEO analysis has confirmed this assumption
[
44
]
.

3
Computation of the polarimeter vector

Given the hadronic current
J
in Eq. (
1
),
the polarimeter vector
h
of the
\tau
is computed using the relations
[
26
,
45
]
:

\textrm{h}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{m_{\tau}\,\omega}\,\left(m_{\tau}^{2}\,\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}-\textrm{P}_{\mu}\textrm{P}^{\nu}\right)\,\left(\Pi^{5}_{\nu}-\gamma_{\textrm{va}}\,\Pi_{\nu}\right)\,,

(6)

with:

\Pi_{\mu}=2\left((\textrm{J}^{\ast}\cdot\textrm{N})\,\textrm{J}_{\mu}+(\textrm{J}\cdot\textrm{N})\,\textrm{J}^{\ast}_{\mu}-(\textrm{J}^{\ast}\cdot\textrm{J})\,\textrm{N}_{\mu}\right)\,,\quad\quad\Pi^{5}_{\mu}=2\,\operatorname{Im}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\textrm{J}^{\ast}_{\nu}\textrm{J}_{\rho}\textrm{N}_{\sigma}

(7)

and

\omega=\textrm{P}^{\mu}\left(\Pi_{\mu}-\gamma_{\textrm{va}}\,\Pi^{5}_{\mu}\right)\,.

(8)

In these formulas,
m_{\tau}
denotes the mass of the
\tau
lepton,

P
the
\tau
lepton momentum, and
N
the momentum of the
\tau
neutrino. The latter can be computed by taking the difference between
P
and the momenta of the charged and neutral mesons produced in the
\tau
decay.
The ratio of the vector and axial-vector couplings of the
\tau
lepton,
\gamma_{\textrm{va}}
, is assumed to have the Standard Model value
\gamma_{\textrm{va}}=1
.
The relations given by Eqs. (
6
) to (
8
) are valid for decays of
\tau^{-}
. For decays of
\tau^{+}
the terms proportional to
\gamma_{\textrm{va}}
in Eqs. (
6
) and (
8
) reverse sign, cf. the text following Eq. (3.16) in Ref.
[
26
]
.

The code for the computation of the polarimeter vector in
\tau\to 3\pi\nu_{\tau}
decays
has been validated in the context of studies of the longitudinal
\tau
polarization in the process
\textrm{Z}/\gamma^{\ast}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}

[
46
]
and of transverse spin correlations between the
\tau^{+}
and the
\tau^{-}
in the process
\textrm{H}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}

[
47
]
, using simulated proton-proton collisions at the LHC.

4
Summary

The polarimeter vector
h
represents an optimal observable for measurements of the
\tau
spin orientation.
Expressions for
h
for the decay channels
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
are given in Ref.
[
26
]
. In this paper, we have presented an algorithm that allows the computation of
h
for the decay channels
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
, collectively referred to as
\tau\to 3\pi\nu_{\tau}
decays. The computation is based on a model for the hadronic current
J
, the parameters of which have been determined by a fit to data recorded in the decay channel
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
by the CLEO experiment. The model is expected to be valid for the decay channel
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
also, due to isospin symmetry.
A subsequent CLEO analysis has confirmed this assumption.
From our perspective as experimentalists at the LHC, it would be very useful if a similar fit to data in the decay channel
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{\textrm{0}}\nu_{\tau}
could be made.
The polarimeter vector for the leptonic decay channels
\tau^{-}\to\textrm{e}^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\textrm{e}}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\mu^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\nu_{\tau}
has not been discussed in this paper. The spin analyzing power of these channels is limited to about
40\%
relative to the sensitivity of hadronic
\tau
decays.

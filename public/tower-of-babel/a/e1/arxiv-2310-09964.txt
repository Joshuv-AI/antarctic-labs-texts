Structural Controllability of Polysystems via Directed Hypergraph Representation
Joshua Pickard

Abstract
This document explores structural controllability of polynomial dynamical systems or polysystems. We extend Lin’s concept of structural controllability for linear systems, offering hypergraph-theoretic methods to rapidly assess strong controllability. Our main result establishes that a polysytem is structurally controllable when its hypergraph representation contains no hyperedge dilation and no inaccessible vertices. We propose two efficient, scalable algorithms to validate these conditions.

Necessary

The next step is to show that the presence of a hyperedge dilation or inaccessible vertex is a necessary condition for

Proposition 5
.

Suppose
(\textsf{A},\mathbf{B})
is not structurally controllable. Then
\mathcal{H}(\textsf{A},\mathbf{B})
contains either a hyperedge dilation or inaccessible vertex.

Proof.

If
(\textsf{A},\mathbf{B})
is not structurally controllable, then
rank(\mathbf{C}(\textsf{A},\mathbf{B}))<n
for all entries in the set
(\hat{\textsf{A}},\hat{\mathbf{B}}).
This can occur either if
rank(\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\textsf{A}}_{(k)}&\hat{\mathbf{B}}\end{bmatrix})<n
or if

where
\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\textsf{A}}_{(k)}&\hat{\mathbf{B}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n(k-1)+m}.
Beginning with the case
rank(\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\textsf{A}}_{(k)}&\hat{\mathbf{B}}\end{bmatrix})<n,
if this matrix is generally rank deficient, then there exist at least one row containing all zeros – corresponding to an inaccessible vertex – or if there are at least
n(k-2)+m+1

Consider the alternative case where

\mathbf{B}.

\hat{\textsf{A}}_{(k)}

\mathbf{B},

\mathbf{B}.
Since there is a vertex that is not individually accessible, there must exist either a inaccessible vertex or a hyperedge dilation. ∎

5
A Test for Structural Controllability

This section presents 2 algorithms to verify the separate conditions of
2
, namely the existence of inaccessible nodes and hyperedge dilations. Checking for dilations is equivalent to solving a maximum matching problem, which can be accomplished in
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}|\sum_{i}|e_{i}^{h}|),
and verifying the existence of inaccessible nodes can by performing a walk beginning at the control vertices over all hyperedges, which occurs in
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{E}|).
The complexity to run both algorithms and verify
2
is
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}|\sum_{i}|e_{i}^{h}|+|\mathcal{E}|).

5.1
Maximum Matching to Detect Dilations

Maximum matching was utilized by
Liu et al. 2011
to determine structural controllability of graphs/linear systems. We can map the problem of detecting hypergraph dilations to solving a maximum matching problem in a directed star expansion.

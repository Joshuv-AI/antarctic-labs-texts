1.
Introduction and Preliminary results

A number is perfect if the sum of its divisors equals twice the number itself. In 2001, T. Leinster
[
7
]
, developed and studied a group theoretic analogue of perfect numbers. A finite group is said to be a perfect group (not to be confused with the one which is equal to its commutator subgroup) or an immaculate group or a Leinster group if the sum of the orders of its normal subgroups equals twice the order of the group itself. The Leinster groups have a beautiful connection with the perfect numbers. Obviously, in the case of cyclic groups and less obviously in the case of dihedral groups. Clearly, a finite cyclic group
C_{n}
is Leinster if and only if its order
n
is a perfect number. In fact, the nilpotent Leinster groups are precisely the finite cyclic groups whose orders are perfect numbers. On the other hand, the Leinster dihedral groups are in one to one correspondence with odd perfect numbers. It may be mentioned here that, till now it is not known whether there are infinitely many Leinster groups or not. Another interesting fact is that upto now, only one odd order Leinster group is known, namely
(C_{127}\rtimes C_{7})\times C_{3^{4}.{11}^{2}.{19}^{2}.113}
. It was discovered by F. Brunault, just one day after the question on existence of odd order Leinster groups was asked by Tom Leinster in Mathoverflow

2.
Leinster groups of order
p^{2}qr

It is easy to verify that
Q_{20}\times C_{19}
and
Q_{28}\times C_{13}
are Leinster groups. In this connection, a natural question arises: Is there any other Leinster group whose order is of the form
p^{2}qr
,
p<q<r
being primes? We obtain the answer of this question and found that
Q_{20}\times C_{19}
and
Q_{28}\times C_{13}
are the only Leinster groups whose orders are of the form
p^{2}qr
.

We begin with the following elementary remark:

Remark 2.1
.

If
G
is a Leinster group, then the number of odd order normal subgroups of
G
must be even. Hence, for any odd order Leinster group
G
,
\tau(G)
is even.

The following Lemmas will be used to get the main result of this section.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
G
is a Leinster group of order
p^{2}qr
, where
p<q<r
are primes, then
T_{p}\ntriangleleft G
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
1.2
,
G
is non-abelian. Now, if
T_{p}\lhd G
, then by Correspondence theorem,
G
has an abelian normal centralizer say
N
of index
q
and consequently, by Theorem
1.5
, we have
p^{2}qr=q\mid G^{\prime}\mid\mid Z(G)\mid
.

Now, if
q>3
, then
G
has an abelian centralizer say
K
of index
r
. Clearly,
N\cap K=Z(G)
and
G=NK
. Therefore
\mid Z(G)\mid=p^{2}
and hence
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=r
. But then,
G
has a normal subgroup of order
qr
, which implies
G\cong T_{p}\times(C_{r}\rtimes C_{q})
. In the present scenario, if
T_{p}=C_{p^{2}}
, then by Theorem
1.3
,
\tau(G)=9
and consequently, by Remark
2.1
,
\mid G\mid=4qr
. Now, using Theorem
1.3
again, we have
8qr=\sigma(G)=\sigma(T_{2})\times\sigma(C_{r}\rtimes C_{q})=7(1+r+qr)
, which is impossible. Again, if
T_{p}=C_{p}\times C_{p}
, then
\sigma(G)>2\mid G\mid
.

Next, suppose
q=3
. Then we have
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\mid Z(G)\mid=4r
. Now, if
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=4
, then
G
has a normal subgroup of order
12
and consequently,
G\cong A_{4}\times C_{r},Q_{12}\times C_{r}
or
D_{12}\times C_{r}
, which are not Leinster. Again, if
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=4r
, then
\sigma(G)<2\mid G\mid
. Finally, if
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=2,r
or
2r
, then by Correspondence theorem,
G
has a normal subgroup of index
2
, which is a contradiction. Hence
T_{p}\ntriangleleft G
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
G
is a Leinster group of order
p^{2}qr
,
p<q<r
being primes, then
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq r
.

Proof.

If
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=r
, then in view of Theorem
1.1
, Lemma
2.2
and Correspondence theorem,
G
has unique normal subgroup for each of the orders
r,pr,qr,p^{2}r,pqr
and
G
cannot have any normal subgroup of order
p^{2},p^{2}q
. Note that
G
can have at the most one normal subgroup of order
pq
. Suppose
G
has a normal subgroup
N
of order
pq
. Let
K
be the normal subgroup of order
pr
. Now, if
\mid N\cap K\mid=1
, then exactly one of
N
or
K
is cyclic, noting that
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=r
. Consequently,
\tau(G)=10
. Again, if
\mid N\cap K\mid=p
, then also we have
\tau(G)=10
. Therefore from the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+p+q+pq+r+pr+qr+p^{2}r+pqr
. But then
(p-1)pqr=(1+p)(1+q)+(1+p+q+p^{2})r
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
G
cannot have a normal subgroup of order
pq
. Consequently, in view of Theorem
1.4
, using the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+p+r+pr+qr+p^{2}r+pqr
or
1+q+r+pr+qr+p^{2}r+pqr
, which is again a contradiction. Hence
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq r
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
G
is a Leinster group of order
p^{2}qr
,
p<q<r
being primes, then
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq qr
.

Proof.

If
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=qr
, then in view of Theorem
1.1
, Lemma
2.2
and Correspondence theorem,
G
has unique normal subgroup for each of the orders
qr,pqr
and
G
cannot have normal subgroup of order
p^{2},p^{2}q,p^{2}r
. Note that
G
can have at the most one normal subgroup for each of the orders
pq,pr
. If
G
has no normal subgroup of order
pq
or
pr
, then
\tau(G)\leq 7
, which is a contradiction to Theorem
1.4
. Therefore
G
must have a normal subgroup
N
of order
pq
and a normal subgroup
K
of order
pr
. Now, if
\mid N\cap K\mid=1
, then
G=(C_{q}\rtimes C_{p})\times(C_{r}\rtimes C_{p})
. Therefore using the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+q+r+pq+pr+qr+pqr
, which implies
r=\frac{1+(p+1)q}{q(p^{2}-p-1)-(p+1)}
. In the present situation, one can easily verify that
p>7
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
\mid N\cap K\mid=p
and consequently, from the definition of Leinster groups, we have

p^{2}qr=1+p+q+r+pq+qr+pr+pqr=(1+p)(1+q)(1+r)
, which is again impossible.
Hence
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq qr
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
G
is a Leinster group of order
p^{2}qr
,
p<q<r
being primes, then
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq pq
.

Proof.

If
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=pq
, then in view of Theorem
1.1
and Correspondence theorem,
G
has unique normal subgroups, say,
H
and
K
of order
p^{2}q
and
pqr
respectively. Moreover,
G
cannot have normal subgroups of order
qr
and
p^{2}r
. Note that
G
has unique normal subgroup of order
pq
since
K
is unique and can have at the most one normal subgroup of order
pr
. Now, suppose
G
has a normal subgroup
N
of order
pr
. Then
\mid G^{\prime}\cap N\mid=p
, otherwise
G=G^{\prime}\times N
, which is a contradiction since
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=pq
. Therefore
G
has a normal subgroup of order
p
. Consequently,
G^{\prime}
is cyclic and hence
G
has a cyclic normal subgroup of index
p
. It now follows from Theorem
1.5
, that
\mid Z(G)\mid=r
and consequently,
T_{q}\ntriangleleft G
. In the present scenario, in view of Lemma

, using the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+p+r+pq+pr+p^{2}q+pqr
and hence
r=\frac{p^{2}q+pq+p+1}{p^{2}-pq-p-1}
. In this case, one can verify that

, which is impossible. Therefore
G
cannot have a normal subgroup of order
pr
. But then
\tau(G)\leq 7
, which is again impossible by Theorem
1.4
. Hence
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq pq
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

If
G
is a Leinster group of order
p^{2}qr
,
p<q<r
being primes, then
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq pr
.

Proof.

If
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=pr
, then in view of Theorem
1.1
and Correspondence theorem,
G
has unique normal subgroup for each of the orders
p^{2}r
and
pqr
. Moreover,
G
cannot have normal subgroups of order
qr
and
p^{2}q
. Let
K
be the normal subgroup of order
pqr
. It is easy to see that

can have at the most one normal subgroup of order
pq
and
pr
, noting that

Now, if
N
is a normal subgroup of

of order

, then
\mid G^{\prime}\cap N\mid=p
, otherwise

, which is a contradiction since
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=pr
. It now follows that

has a normal subgroup of order

and hence

, then

. Therefore by Theorem

, we have
\mid Z(G)\mid=q

and Lemma

, using the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+p+q+pq+pr+p^{2}r+pqr=(1+p)(1+q)+r(p+p^{2}+pq)

cannot have a normal subgroup of order
pq
. But then, again we have
\tau(G)\leq 7

. Hence
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq pr
.
∎

Now, we are ready to state the following theorem:

Theorem 2.7
.

If
G
is a Leinster group of order
p^{2}qr
, where

In view of Theorem

,

.

Now, if
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=pqr
or

, then
\tau(G)\leq 7
, which is impossible by Theorem

.

Next, suppose
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=p^{2}q

has more than one normal subgroup of order

, then
T_{q}\lhd G
and hence
T_{r}\ntriangleleft G
. Now, from the definition of Leinster group, we have
\mid G\mid=12r
and consequently,
\mid G\mid=60

[
11
]
. Therefore

can have at the most one normal subgroup of order
pq
. But then
\tau(G)\leq 7
, which is again impossible by Theorem

.

Again, if
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=p
, then by Theorem

and Correspondence theorem,
T_{p}\vartriangleleft G
, which is a contradiction to Lemma

. Therefore, in view of Lemma

, Lemma

, Lemma

, Lemma
2.5
, Lemma

, we have
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=q
. In the present scenario, by Theorem

and Correspondence theorem,

has unique normal subgroup for each of the following orders

. Also, note that
G
cannot have normal subgroup of order
p^{2}r
.

Let
N
be the normal subgroup of

of order
p^{2}q
. Now, if
T_{r}\ntriangleleft G
, then
G
cannot have a normal subgroup of order
pr
. In this situation, from the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+p+q+pq+qr+p^{2}q+pqr=(1+p)(1+q)+q(r+p^{2}+pr)
, noting that by Theorem
1.4
, we have
\tau(G)>7
, which is impossible. Therefore
T_{r}\lhd G
. In the present scenario, if
G
don’t have a normal subgroup of order
p
, then from the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+q+r+pr+pq+qr+p^{2}q+pqr
, i.e.,

or
p^{2}qr=1+q+r+pq+qr+p^{2}q+pqr
, i.e.,
r=\frac{p^{2}q+pq+q+1}{p^{2}q-pq-q-1}
. But in both the cases, one can verify that

, which is impossible. Therefore

must have a normal subgroup of order
p
and consequently, using the definition of Leinster groups, we have
p^{2}qr=1+p+q+r+pq+qr+pr+p^{2}q+pqr
. In the present situation, one can verify that

and consequently,

, which implies

. Hence

or

. Now, using GAP

or
Q_{28}\times C_{13}
.
∎

3.
Leinster groups of order
pqrs

Given any primes
p<q<r<s
, by
[
1
, Corollary 3.2, Theorem 3.6]
, the only Leinster group of order
pq
is

and the only Leinster group of order

is

. In this section, we consider the groups of order

and prove that no group of order

is Leinster. The following lemmas will be used to establish our result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H
be a group of squarefree order and
p
be any odd prime,
p\nmid\mid H\mid
. If
G=H\times C_{2p}
, then
G
is not Leinster.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
1.2
,
G
is non-abelian. Now, if
\mid H\mid
is even, then
H
has a subgroup
N
of index
2
. In the present scenario,
N\times C_{2p}
and
H\times C_{p}
are two distinct subgroups of
G
of index
2
and hence
G
is not Leinster. Next, suppose
\mid H\mid
is odd. Then using Theorem
1.3
, we have
3\mid\sigma(G)
and so if
G
is Leinster, then
3\mid\mid G\mid
. Therefore
G
will have a normal subgroup of index
2
and a normal subgroup of index
3
. Hence
G
is not Leinster.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
G
is a Leinster group of order
pqrs
,
p<q<r<s
being primes, then
8\leq\tau(G)\leq 10
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
1.2
,
G
is non abelian. Consequently,
\tau(G)\leq 12
, noting that every normal subgroup of
G
is uniquely determined by its order.

Now, suppose
\tau(G)=11
. In view of Theorem
1.3
,
G
can have at the most
3
normal subgroups of prime index and at the most
3
normal subgroups of order product of two primes. Consequently,
G
is not leinster.

Next, suppose
\tau(G)=12
. Then we have
G=N_{ab}\times C_{cd}
, where

is the unique normal subgroup of order

and

. Now, if
\mid G\mid
is odd, then using Theorem
1.3
, we have

and hence

is not Leinster. Next, suppose
\mid G\mid
is even. In the present situation, if
\mid C_{cd}\mid
is odd, then
\mid N_{ab}\mid
must be even and by Theorem

, we have

, and hence

is not Leinster. On the other hand if

.
∎

Let

\mid G^{\prime}\mid=qr,qs
or

(b)

.

(c)

.

\mid G^{\prime}\mid

, we have
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=qrs

.

, which is again impossible.

Next, suppose

Again, if

Finally, if

, we have
\{y,z\}=\{3,r\}

\{3,s\}

, noting that in the present scenario, we cannot have a normal subgroup of order

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If

Proof.

If

, then in view of Remark
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid\leq 1+\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{5}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{10}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{14}+\frac{2\mid G\mid}{22}<\mid G\mid.

Again, if
qr<2s
, then in view of Remark
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid\leq 1+\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{5}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{10}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{14}+\frac{2\mid G\mid}{35}<\mid G\mid.

∎

Now, we consider the case where
\tau(G)=9
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
G
be a group of order
pqrs
,
p<q<r<s
being primes and
\tau(G)=9
. If
G
is Leinster, then we have:

(a)

p=2
.

(b)

T_{2}\ntriangleleft G
.

(c)

\mid G^{\prime}\mid=qr,qs
or
rs
.

(d)

G
has exactly
2
normal subgroups of prime index and has exactly
2
normal subgroups of prime order.

(e)

3\nmid\mid G\mid
.

Proof.

a) It follows from Remark
2.1
.

b) It follows from Theorem
1.3
, noting that by Theorem
1.6
,
G
has a normal subgroup of index
2
.

c) Since
\tau(G)=9
, therefore
\mid G^{\prime}\mid
cannot be prime. Again, if
G^{\prime}
is of prime index, then in view of Theorem
1.6
, we have
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=qrs
. In the present scenario, by Theorem
1.5
,
\mid Z(G)\mid=1
, and hence
G
is a dihedral group, which is impossible by
[
7
, Example 2.4]
. Therefore we must have
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=qr,qs
or
rs
, noting that we cannot have
G=G^{\prime}
.

d) In view of (c),
G
has exactly two normal subgroups of prime index. In the present scenario, if
G
has less than two normal subgroups of prime order, then using Theorem
1.3
, we have
\tau(G)>9
. Hence,
G
has exactly
2
normal subgroups of prime order, noting that if
G
has more than
2
normal subgroup of prime order, then also we have
\tau(G)>9
.

e) If
\mid G\mid=2.3rs
, then in view of (c), we have
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3r
or
3s
.
Now, suppose
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3r
. In the present situation, if
G
has a normal subgroup of order
rs
, then in view of (d) and Remark
2.1
, from the definition of Leinster groups, we have

\displaystyle 6rs=1+3rs+2.3r+3r+rs+r+2.3+3,

which is impossible.

Next, suppose
G
has a normal subgroup of order
3s
. Then in view of (d) and Remark
2.1
, from the definition of Leinster groups, we have

\displaystyle 6rs=1+3rs+2.3r+3r+3s+3+x+y,

where
x,y
are the orders of the remaining normal subgroups such that
x
is a product of two primes. But then, clearly,
x
cannot be odd, otherwise
y
will also be odd. Again,
x\neq 2r,2s
, noting that
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3r
. Therefore
x=2.3
, which is again impossible.

Therefore, we must have
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3s
. In the present situation, if
G
has a normal subgroup of order
rs
, then in view of (d) and Remark
2.1
, from the definition of Leinster groups, we have

\displaystyle 6rs=1+3rs+2.3s+3s+rs+s+2.3+3,

which is impossible.

Next, suppose
G
has a normal subgroup of order
3r
. Then in view of (d) and Remark
2.1
, from the definition of Leinster groups, we have

\displaystyle 6rs=1+3rs+2.3s+3s+3r+3+x+y,

where
x,y
are the orders of the remaining normal subgroups such that
x
is a product of two primes. But then, clearly,
x
cannot be odd, otherwise
y
will also be odd. Again,
x\neq 2r,2s
, noting that
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3s
. Therefore
x=2.3
, which is again impossible. Therefore we have
3\nmid\mid G\mid
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
G
is a group of order
pqrs
,
p<q<r<s
being primes, and
\tau(G)=9
, then
G
is not Leinster.

Proof.

If
qr>2s
, then in view of Remark
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid\leq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{5}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{10}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{14}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{22}+\frac{3\mid G\mid}{70}<\mid G\mid.

Again, if
qr<2s
, then in view of (e) and (f) of Remark
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid\leq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{5}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{10}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{14}+\frac{\mid G\mid}{35}+\frac{3\mid G\mid}{70}<\mid G\mid.

∎

Finally, we consider the case where
\tau(G)=10
.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
G
be a group of order
pqrs
,
p<q<r<s
being primes and
\tau(G)=10
. If
G
is Leinster, then we have:

(a)

p=2
.

(b)

3\nmid\mid G\mid
.

(c)

T_{2}\ntriangleleft G
.

(d)

\mid G^{\prime}\mid=qr,qs
or
rs
.

Proof.

a) Suppose,
p>2
. It is easy to see that
G^{\prime}
cannot be of prime index, noting that in view of Theorem
1.3
,
G
can have at the most three normal subgroup whose order is product of two primes.

Now, suppose
\mid G^{\prime}\mid
is prime. Note that, in view of Theorem
1.6
,
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq p
. Next, suppose
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=q
. In this situation, if
T_{p}\lhd G
, then
q\mid p+1
, which is a contradiction. Again, if
T_{r}\lhd G
or
T_{s}\lhd G
, then by Correspondence theorem, we have a normal subgroup of order
rs
, which is impossible. Therefore
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq q
. On the other hand, if
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=s
, then we have
s\leq{r+1}
, which is impossible. Therefore
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=r
and consequently, in view of Theorem
1.3
, from the definition of Leinster groups, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid=1+r+s+pr+qr+rs+pqr+prs+qrs.

Now, suppose
pqr>rs
. Then
pr<qr<rs<pqr<prs<qrs
. In the present situation, clearly we have
qrs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{3},prs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{5},pqr\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{11},rs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{15},qr\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{33}
and
pr\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{55}
. But then,
\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid<\mid G\mid
.

Next, suppose
pqr<rs
. Then
pr<qr<pqr<rs<prs<qrs
. In the present situation, clearly we have
qrs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{3},prs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{5},rs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{15},,pqr\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{17},qr\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{51}
and
pr\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{85}
. But then also, we have
\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid<\mid G\mid
.

Therefore
\mid G^{\prime}\mid
has to be product of two primes. In the present scenario,
G
can have exactly two normal subgroups of prime index and in view of Theorem
1.3
,
G
can have exactly three normal subgroups of order product of two primes.

Now, suppose
qr>ps
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid\leq 1+q+r+s+qr+qs+rs+prs+qrs.

In the present situation, clearly we have
qrs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{3},prs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{5},rs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{15},qs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{21},qr\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{33}
and
s\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{105}
. But then,
\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid<\mid G\mid
.

Therefore we must have
qr<ps
, and consequently,

\displaystyle\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid\leq 1+q+r+s+ps+qs+rs+prs+qrs.

In the present situation, clearly we have
qrs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{3},prs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{5},rs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{15},qs\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{21},ps\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{35}
and
s\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{105}
. But then also, we have
\sigma(G)-\mid G\mid<\mid G\mid
. Therefore
p=2
.

b) In view of (a), suppose
\mid G\mid=2.3rs
. Note that, by Correspondence theorem,
T_{2}\ntriangleleft G
. In the present scenario, in view of Theorem
1.6
, one can verify that
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3r
or
3s
, and consequently,
T_{3},T_{r},T_{s}\lhd G
, noting that
\mid G^{\prime}\mid\neq 3,r,s
.

Now, suppose
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3r
. Then we have
G=T_{s}\times(C_{3.r}\rtimes C_{2})
. But then, from the definition of Leinster groups, using Theorem
1.3
, we have
12rs=(s+1)(1+r+3+3r+6r)
, which is a contradiction.

Next, suppose
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=3s
. Then we have
G=T_{r}\times(C_{3s}\rtimes C_{2})
. But then, again from the definition of Leinster groups, using Theorem
1.3
, we have
12rs=(r+1)(1+s+3+3s+6s)
, which is again a contradiction. Therefore
3\nmid\mid G\mid
.

c) It follows from (b), using Theorem
1.3
.

d) Clearly,
G^{\prime}
cannot be of prime index. Now, suppose
\mid G^{\prime}\mid
be a prime. In the present situation, one can verify that
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=q
or
r
and consequently,
s=\frac{1+r+3q+3qr}{qr-3q}
or
s=\frac{1+3r+3qr}{qr-3r-1}
. But in both the cases, one can verify that
q>13
, which is impossible. Hence the result follows, noting that we cannot have
G=G^{\prime}
.
∎

Conclusion 3.11
.

The objective of this research was to investigate the structure of Leinster groups of order
pqrs
for any primes
p,q,r,s
not necessarily distinct. It is well known that no group of order
p^{2}q^{2}
is Leinster
[
1
, Proposition 2.4]
. On the other hand, in view of Proposition
3.10
, we have
C_{7}\rtimes C_{8}
is the only Leinster group of order
p^{3}q
. In this paper, for any primes
p<q<r<s
, we have studied the cases where order of
G
is
p^{2}qr
or
pqrs
. However, we were unable to give any information for Leinster groups of order
pq^{2}r
and
pqr^{2}
. As such we leave it as an open question to our readers.

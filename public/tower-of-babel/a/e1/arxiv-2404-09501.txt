The double phase problems on lattice graphs
Zhentao He, Chao Ji

Abstract
. In this paper, we first develop the theory of Musielak-Orlicz spaces on locally finite graphs, including completeness, reflexivity, separability, and so on. Then, we give some elementary properties of double phase operators on locally finite graphs. Finally, as applications of previous theory, we prove some existence results of solutions to double phase problems on lattice graphs.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J20, 35J60, 35B33.

1.
Introduction and main results

In the past decades, a considerable amount of literature has been devoted to double phase problems. These problems are motivated by various physical phenomena. Firstly, the double phase operator has been utilized to describe steady-state solutions of reaction-diffusion problems in various fields such as biophysics, plasma physics, and chemical reaction analysis. The prototype equation for these models can be expressed as:

u_{t}=\Delta_{p}^{a}u(z)+\Delta_{q}u+g(x,u)\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{T},

where
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
denotes the space-time cylinder, and
\Omega
denotes a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geqslant 2
and
t\in(0,T)
with
T>0
. In this context, the function
u
typically represents a concentration, the term
\Delta_{p}^{a}u(z)+\Delta_{q}u
accounts for diffusion with coefficients determined by
a(z)|\nabla u|^{p-2}+|\nabla u|^{q-2}
, and
g(x,u)
describes the reaction term associated with source and loss processes. For further details, please refer to Cherfils & Il’yasov
[
5
]
and Singer
[
38
]
. Secondly, such operators offer a valuable framework for explaining the behavior of highly anisotropic materials. These materials exhibit varying hardening properties, which are influenced by the exponent governing the propagation of the gradient variable. These variations occur depending on the point of interest, where the modulating coefficient
a(z)
influences the geometry of a composite composed of two different materials. For instance, this structure is used to describe a composite material having on
\{z\in\Omega:\alpha(z)=0\}
an energy density with
q
-growth, but on
\{z\in\Omega:\alpha(z)>0\}
the energy density has
p
-growth.

This operator is related to the double phase energy functional, which is defined by

u\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}\Big(a(z)|\nabla u|^{p}+|\nabla u|^{q}\Big)dz.

It is noteworthy that if the weight function
a(\cdot)
is not bounded away from zero, i.e.,
\underset{\Omega}{\text{ess}\inf}\,a\geq 0
, the density function of the above integral functional, denoted as the integrand
\eta(z,t)=a(z)t^{p}+t^{q}
, exhibits unbalanced growth, which can be characterized by:

t^{q}\leq\eta(z,t)\leq c_{0}(1+t^{p}),\,\,\text{for a.e.}\,\,z\in\Omega,\,\,\text{all}\,\,t\geq 0,\,\,\text{some}\,\,c_{0}>0.

Such integral functionals were first considered by Marcellini
[
30
,
31
]
and Zhikov
[
44
,
45
]
in the context of problems in the calculus of variations (including the Lavrentiev gap phenomenon) and of nonlinear elasticity theory. For problems with unbalanced growth, only local regularity results exist (please see the survey papers
[
32
]
due to Marcellini and
[
33
]
due to Mingione and Rǎdulescu), and there is no global regularity theory (i.e., regularity up to the boundary). This limitation restricts the use of many powerful technical tools available for problems with balanced growth and the nonlinearity
f
satisfies some subcritical growth conditions. For further results about existence, multiplicity, regularity and qualitative properties of the solutions to double phase problems, please see
[
19
,
2
,
5
,
28
,
35
,
7
,
8
,
29
,
34
,
14
,
15
]
and the references therein.

In recent years, the nonlinear Schrödinger equations on graphs has received considerable attention (see
[
23
,
24
,
41
,
16
,
17
,
4
,
42
,
36
,
18
,
37
,
20
,

,

,

]
and the references therein).
In particular, in the monograph
[
16
]
, Grigor’yan introduced discrete Laplace operator on finite and infinite graphs. In
[
17
]
, Grigor’yan, Lin and Yang considered the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

-\Delta u+h(x)u=f(x,u)

on locally finite graph
G=(V,E)
, where
f
satisfies some growth conditions and the potential function
h
satisfies
1/h\in\ell^{1}(V)
or
h(x)\to\infty
as
d(x,x_{0})\to\infty
for some
x_{0}\in V
. Under the assumptions on
h
, they proved Sobolev compact embeddings and obtained the existence of positive solution to equation (
1.1
) via variational methods. In
[
23
]
, applying the Nehari method, Hua and Xu studied the existence of ground states of equation (
1.1
) on the lattice graphs, where
f
satisfies some growth conditions and the potential function
h
is periodic or bounded, and they extended their results to quasi-transitive graphs.

The following Sobolev inequality in lattice graph
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is well-known

(1.2)

\|u\|_{r}\leqslant C_{r}\|\nabla u\|_{p},\quad\forall u\in D^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{N}\right),

where
N\geqslant 2
,
1\leqslant p<N
,
r\geqslant p^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-p}
, see
[
22
, Theorem 3.6]
for the detailed proof. In
[
20
]
, by minimizing
\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}
on
\left\{u\in D^{1,p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}):\lVert u\rVert_{r}=1\right\}
, Hua and Li proved the existence of extremal function for
the
Sobolev inequality (
1.2
), and then they obtained a positive solution to the equation

-\Delta_{p}u=u^{r-1}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}^{N},

where
N\geqslant 3
,
1\leqslant p<N
,
r>p^{*}
.

However, as far as we know, there are no any
results on double phase problems on graphs. In this paper, we will make some significant attempts and establish some fundamental theories and results in this direction. More precisely, we are studying the double phase problems on the lattice graphs for the first time.
To investigate these problems, we first develop the theory of Musielak-Orlicz spaces on locally finite graphs, including completeness,
reflexivity, separability, and so on. Then, we give some elementary properties of double phase operators on locally finite graphs.
We think that these have independent interest and can be applied to the study of related problems.

In the continuous case
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the following double phase problem has been extensively studied

(1.3)

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)=g(u),&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(x)\to 0&\text{ as }\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to+\infty,\end{cases}

where
N\geqslant 3
,
1<p<N
and
p<q
. The assumption that
a(x)
is a positive constant is crucial to obtain the compactness of Palais-Smale sequences, see
[
35
,
19
]
. When
a(x)
is not a positive constant, to the best of our knowledge, there is nothing about the existence of solutions of (
1.3
) in the literature. The present paper
is the first contribution to the double phase problem on lattice graphs under different assumptions on
a
. Moreover, our work space is continuously embedded in
\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
for all
r\geqslant p^{*}
, while it is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
r\in[p^{*},q^{*}]
in continuous setting.

Now we recall some basic setting on graphs from
[
20
,
42
]
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected locally finite graph,
where
V
denotes the vertex set and
E\subset V\times V
denotes the edge set. A graph is called locally finite if each vertex has finitely many neighbours and is called connected if for any two vertices
x,y\in V
, there is a path (with finite length) connecting
x
and
y
. The graph distance
d(x,y)
of two vertices
x,y\in V
is defined by the minimal number of edges which connect these two vertices. Denote
C(V)
as the set of all real functions defined on
V
.

Throughout this paper, we regard
E
as a subset of
V\times V
. Therefore, for any
(x,y)\in E
,
(x,y)
and
(y,x)
are different elements in
E
while they stand for the same edge. Let
\mu\colon V\to(0,+\infty)
be a measure on
V
, and
w\colon V\times V\to[0,+\infty)
be an edge weight function satisfying

w(x,y)=w(y,x),\quad\text{for all }(x,y)\in E,

and for any
(x,y)\in V\times V
,

(x,y)\in E\Leftrightarrow w(x,y)>0.

To simplify the notation, in the following, if
(x,y)\in E
, we also write
xy\in E
or
y\sim x
, and define
w_{xy}=w(x,y)
.

The
N
-dimensional integer lattice graph,
denoted by
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, is the graph consisting of the set of
vertices
V=\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and the set of edges

E=\left\{(x,y):x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},\mathop{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left\lvert x_{i}-y_{i}\right\rvert=1}\right\}.

For any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, define
\left\lvert x\right\rvert:=d(x,0)
. On lattice graphs, we always assume that
\mu\equiv 1
and
w\equiv 1
.

In this paper, we shall study the following double phase problem on the lattice graph

(1.4)

-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)=f(x,u)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}^{N},

where
N\geqslant 2
,
1<p<N
,
p<q<\infty
,
a\in C(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
,
a(x)\geqslant 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
,
and the double phase operator on the lattice graph is denoted by

\displaystyle L(u)(x_{0}):=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{y\sim{x_{0}}}\left\lvert u(y)-u(x_{0})\right\rvert^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x_{0}))

\displaystyle-\sum_{y\sim{x_{0}}}\frac{a(x_{0})+a(y)}{2}\left\lvert u(y)-u(x_{0})\right\rvert^{q-2}(u(y)-u(x_{0}))

for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
u\in C(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. It is natural to consider the integral

(1.5)

I(u):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xy\in E}\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert u(y)-u(x)\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{a(x)}{q}\left\lvert u(y)-u(x)\right\rvert^{q}\right)

to be the functional corresponding to the double phase operator
L
. If
a\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, then by Remark
2.18
below,
I
is well-defined and of class
C^{1}
on
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. However, if
a
is unbounded, then, for each
1\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\infty
, (
1.5
) could be infinite for some
u\in D^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and thus
I
is no longer a well-defined functional on
D^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Therefore, we introduce the Musielak-Orlicz space
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
in Subsection
2.4
to ensure (
1.5
) is well defined and of class
C^{1}
on
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Therefore, we can deal with problem
(
1.4
)
under different assumptions on
a
uniformly.

Now, we present the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
f(x,t)=f(x)
,
f\in\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}\geqslant 1
,
then problem (
1.4
) has a unique solution.

Remark 1.2
.

Since there is no similar results in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we compare Theorem
1.1
to
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
(double phase problems on bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
). In Theorem
1.1
, the assumption on
r
is
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}\leqslant 1
, because we have the embedding
\ell^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\hookrightarrow\ell^{\frac{r}{r-1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
for all
\frac{r}{r-1}\geqslant p^{*}
by Proposition
2.17
(i) below. While in
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
, the assumption on
r
is
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}\leqslant 1
, because
L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{r}{r-1}}(\Omega)
for all
1\leqslant\frac{r}{r-1}\leqslant p^{*}
.

Next, let
r>p^{*}
. Define

\displaystyle J\colon D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{r}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}{\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{r}}\,d\mu:=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert u(x)\right\rvert^{r}.

For given
t>0
, let

M_{t}:=\left\{u\in D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}):J(u)=t\right\}.

We consider the following constrained minimization problem

(1.6)

S_{t}:=\inf_{u\in M_{t}}I(u).

u_{0}
is called a solution of problem (
1.6
) if
\in M_{t}
and
I(u_{0})=\inf_{u\in M_{t}}I(u)
.

Moreover, due to the theorem of Lagrange multipliers and the integration by parts formula (
2.7
), if
u
is a solution of the problem (
1.6
), then there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\langle I^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\lambda\langle J^{\prime}(u),v\rangle
for all
v\in D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, that is,
u
is a nontrivial weak solution of the eigenvalue problem

(1.7)

-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)=\lambda\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{r-2}u\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

For any fixed
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, take the test function
v
to be

\delta_{y}(x):=\begin{cases}1,&x=y,\\
0,&x\neq y,\end{cases}

then we obtain

-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)(y)=\lambda\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{r-2}u(y),

and thus
u
is also a pointwise solution of (
1.7
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r>p^{*}
. If
a
satisfies

\lim_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to\infty}a(x)=+\infty,

then for each
t>0
, problem (
1.6
) (or (
1.7
)) has a positive solution
u
.

We observe that Theorem

and

can be extended to more general graphs. Let

be a uniformly locally finite graph satisfying

. Because in our proof, the only property of

we used is

for all

, see Proposition

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r>p^{*}
,
r\geqslant q
and
a\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
.
Let
\{v_{n}\}\subset D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
be a minimizing sequence of (

) for any

, and
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be an arbitrary sequence ensuring
\{x_{n}-y_{n}\}
bounded in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, where
\{x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
\left\lvert v_{n}(x_{n})\right\rvert=\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{\infty}
. Then
\{u_{n}(x):=v_{n}(x+y_{n})\}
contains a convergent subsequence, still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}
, that converges to

.
Moreover,

satisfies

(1.8)

\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{\tilde{a}(x)}{q}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw=S_{t}

and solves

(1.9)

-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+\tilde{a}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)=\lambda\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{r-2}u\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}^{N},

where

and

is a positive constant. Moreover, if

are non-negative, then

The existence of
\tilde{a}
is provided in Lemma

. In general,

is not equal to

since

varies depending on the choice of

in Theorem

For
T\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we call that a function

b(x+Te_{i})=b(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},1\leqslant i\leqslant N,

We call that a function

on

be bounded potential, if

0\leqslant b(x)\leqslant b_{\infty}:=\lim_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to\infty}b(x)<\infty,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

.

Let

and

. If

) (or (

.

\left\lvert x\right\rvert

]
.

Finally, motivated by

]
, we study the asymptotic behaviour of Lagrangian multiplier

. If

, then (

) boils down to the following

-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u)=\lambda\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{r-2}u\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce some notations on locally finite graphs. Denote
C(V\times V)
as the set of all real functions defined on
V\times V
. For any
g\in C(V\times V)
and fixed
x_{0}\in V
, assume that
\{y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{k}\}
be all neighbours of
x_{0}
, we write

\sum_{y\sim{x_{0}}}g(x_{0},y):=\sum_{\{y\in V\ :\ yx_{0}\in E\}}g(x_{0},y)=g(x_{0},y_{1})+g(x_{0},y_{2})+\cdots+g(x_{0},y_{k}).

Define the difference operator

(2.1)

\displaystyle\nabla\colon C(V)

\displaystyle\to C(V\times V)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\to(\nabla u)(x,y):=u(y)-u(x)

for all
(x,y)\in V\times V
.

The divergence operator is denoted by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\colon C(V\times V)

\displaystyle\to C(V)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\to(\mathrm{div}f)(x):=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim{x}}w_{xy}(f(x,y)-f(y,x))

for all
x\in V
.

Remark 2.1
.

The operators
\nabla
and
\mathrm{div}
are introduced in

[
22
]
and
[
9
]
, and they are employed to establish Proposition
2.15
, which presents Green’s formula on graphs.

The
p
-Laplacian on graphs (see
[
20
]
) of
u\in C(V)
can be defined as follows

\Delta_{p}u(x):=\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u)(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim{x}}w_{xy}\left\lvert u(y)-u(x)\right\rvert^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))

for all
x\in V
with
1<p<\infty
.

Now we define the double phase operator on graphs of
u\in C(V)
as follows

(2.3)

\displaystyle L(u)(x_{0}):=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mu(x_{0})}\sum_{y\sim{x_{0}}}w_{x_{0}y}\left\lvert u(y)-u(x_{0})\right\rvert^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x_{0}))

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mu(x_{0})}\sum_{y\sim{x_{0}}}w_{x_{0}y}\frac{a(x_{0})+a(y)}{2}\left\lvert u(y)-u(x_{0})\right\rvert^{q-2}(u(y)-u(x_{0}))

for all
x_{0}\in V
,
1<p<q<\infty
and

2.1.
The Musielak-Orlicz space

In this subsection, we recall some basic definitions and properties of Musielak-Orlicz space

,
where
(A,\Sigma,\mu)
is a
\sigma
-finite, complete measure space.
For more details, please see
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A convex, left-continuous function
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]

with

\varphi(0)=0,\quad\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\varphi(t)=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi(t)=\infty

is called a
\Phi
-function.
It is called positive if
\varphi(t)>0
for all
t>0
.

Let
(A,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite, complete measure space.
A real function
\varphi\colon A\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is said to be a generalized
\Phi
-function on

(A,\Sigma,\mu)
, denoted by
\varphi\in\Phi(A,\mu)
. If

(i)

\varphi(y,\cdot)
is a
\Phi
-function for every
y\in A
.

(ii)

y\to\varphi(y,t)
is measurable for every
t\geqslant 0
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi(A,\mu)
and let
\varrho_{\varphi}
be given by

\varrho_{\varphi}(f):=\int_{A}\varphi(y,\left\lvert f(y)\right\rvert)\,d\mu(y)

for all
\mu
-measurable real functions

on

. Then the Musielak-Orlicz space
is defined by

L^{\varphi}(A,\mu):=\left\{f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}\penalty\ \text{measurable}:\varrho_{\varphi}(\lambda f)<\infty\penalty\ \text{for some}\penalty\ \lambda>0\right\},

equipped with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{\varphi}:=\mathrm{inf}\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{\varphi}(\frac{f}{\lambda})\leqslant 1\right\}.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi(A,\mu)
, then
L^{\varphi}(A,\mu)
is a Banach space.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that

-condition if

\varphi(y,2t)\leqslant K\varphi(y,t)

for all
y\in A
and all

. The smallest such

is called the
\Delta_{2}
-constant
of
\varphi
.

A
\Phi
-function
\varphi
is said to be an N-function if it is continuous and positive and satisfies

\lim_{t\to 0}{\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}{\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}}=\infty.

A function
\varphi\in\Phi(A,\mu)
is said to be a generalized N-function if

\varphi(y,\cdot)
is an N-function for every
y\in A
, denoted by
\varphi\in N(A,\mu)
.

A function
\varphi\in N(A,\mu)
is called uniformly convex if for any

\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\left\lvert t_{1}-t_{2}\right\rvert\leqslant\varepsilon\max{\{t_{1},t_{2}\}}\quad\text{or}\quad\varphi\left(y,\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}\right)\leqslant(1-\delta)\frac{\varphi(y,t_{1})+\varphi(y,t_{2})}{2}

for all

and every

.

Proposition 2.7
.

If

is uniformly convex and satisfies the

-condition,
then the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\varphi}
is uniformly convex. Hence,

is also uniformly convex.

Definition 2.8

A measure

is called separable if there exists a sequence

with the following properties:

(i)

\mu(E_{k})<\infty
for all

(ii)

for every

, where

.

A function

is called locally integrable on

\varrho_{\varphi}(t\chi_{E})<\infty

for all

and all
\mu
-measurable

.

Proposition 2.9
.

If
\varphi\in\Phi(A,\mu)
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, then

\displaystyle L^{\varphi}(A,\mu)

\displaystyle=E^{\varphi}(A,\mu):=\left\{f\in L^{\varphi}(A,\mu):\varrho_{\varphi}(\lambda f)<\infty\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ \lambda>0\right\}

\displaystyle=L_{OC}^{\varphi}(A,\mu):=\left\{f\in L^{\varphi}(A,\mu):\rho_{\varphi}(f)<\infty\right\}.

Proposition 2.10

Let

is separable. If we further assume

\displaystyle+\int_{E}a(x)\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q-2}\nabla u_{n}\right)\left(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u\right)dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{E}\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)dw

\displaystyle-\int_{E}\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla u+a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla u\right)\,dw

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{E}\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)dw-\int_{E}\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p-1}|\nabla u|+a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q-1}|\nabla u|\right)\,dw

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{E}\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)dw-\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{E}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}\,dw-\frac{1}{p}\int_{E}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dw

\displaystyle-\frac{q-1}{q}\int_{E}a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\,dw-\frac{1}{q}\int_{E}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)dw-\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right)dw.

Noting that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle L(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle=0,

we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle L\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}-u\right\rangle=0,

that is,

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)dw\leqslant\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right)dw.

On the other hand, it follows from Fatou’s lemma that

\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)dw\geqslant\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right)dw.

Thus, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)dw=\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right)dw,

Since

\displaystyle\left|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u\right|^{p}+a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u\right|^{q}\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{1}{p}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle+C\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right),

it follows from a variant of Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{E}\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u\right|^{p}+a(x)\left|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u\right|^{q}\right)dw=0.

Hence, we obtain that
u_{n}\to u
in
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(V)
.

(iii) Since
L
is strictly monotone and

\lim_{\|u\|\to\infty}\frac{\langle L(u),u\rangle}{\|u\|}=\frac{\int_{E}\left(|\nabla u|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right)\,dw}{\|u\|}=+\infty,

that is,
L
is coercive, we know

is a surjection in view of Minty-Browder theorem (see
[
43
, Theorem 26A]
). Hence

has an inverse mapping
L^{-1}:\left(D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(V)\right)^{*}\to D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(V)
. Therefore, in order to complete the proof of (iii), it suffices to prove that
L^{-1}
is continuous. If
f_{n},f\in\left(D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(V)\right)^{*},f_{n}\to f
, letting
u_{n}=L^{-1}\left(f_{n}\right),u=L^{-1}(f)
, then
L\left(u_{n}\right)=f_{n},L(u)=f
. Note that
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is bounded in
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(V)
. Therefore, there is a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}
that converges weakly to a limit, say
u_{0}
. We conclude from

that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\langle L\left(u_{n_{k}}\right)-L\left(u_{0}\right),u_{n_{k}}-u_{0}\right\rangle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\langle f_{n_{k}},u_{n_{k}}-u_{0}\right\rangle=0.

Since

is of type
(S)_{+}
,
u_{n_{k}}\to u_{0}
. Then, by injectivity of

, we have
u_{0}=u
. Reasoning as above, we know that every subsequence of
\{u_{n}\}
contains a convergent subsequence that converges to

. Hence,
u_{n}\to u
, and

is continuous.
∎

Now, by adapting the argument of

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
u\in D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(V)
and
v\in C_{0}(V)
. Then

(2.7)

\left\langle L(u),v\right\rangle=-\int_{V}{v\cdot\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)}\,d\mu

Proof.

Let
f:=\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle L(u),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{E}{f(x,y)(\nabla v)(x,y)}\,dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xy\in E}w_{xy}{f(x,y)(\nabla v)(x,y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}f(x,y)(v(y)-v(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}f(x,y)v(y)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}f(x,y)v(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in V}\sum_{x\sim y}w_{yx}f(y,x)v(x)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}f(x,y)v(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{yx\in E}w_{yx}f(y,x)v(x)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}f(x,y)v(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xy\in E}w_{xy}f(y,x)v(x)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}f(x,y)v(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(f(x,y)-f(y,x))v(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{V}{v\cdot(\mathrm{div}f)(x)}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=-\int_{V}{v\cdot\mathrm{div}(\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}\nabla u)}\,d\mu.

∎

whence it follows that
u_{n}\to u\neq 0
pointwise in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
since
\Omega
is bounded.

We claim that
\lVert u\rVert_{r}=1
, i.e.,
u\in M_{\frac{1}{r}}
. Suppose that this is not true, i.e.,
0<\lVert u\rVert_{r}<1
.
Hence, by Brézis-Lieb lemma, we obtain
0<\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}<1
and
\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{r}+\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{r}=1
.
Since
r\geqslant q>p
, we get
\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{p}>\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{q}
,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{p}>\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{q}
and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{q}+\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{q}\geqslant 1
.
Moreover, by (
4.2
), (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and Brézis-Lieb lemma, passing to a subsequence if necessary, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla u_{n}\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{a_{n}(x)}{q}\left\lvert\nabla u_{n}\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla(u_{n}-u)\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\left\lvert a_{n}(x)^{\frac{1}{q}}\nabla u_{n}-\tilde{a}(x)^{\frac{1}{q}}\nabla u)\right\rvert^{q}+\frac{\tilde{a}(x)}{q}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left({\frac{\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla(u_{n}-u)\right\rvert^{p}}\,dw}{p\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{p}}}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{p}+\frac{\int_{E}{a_{n}(x)\left\lvert\nabla(u_{n}-u)\right\rvert^{q}}\,dw}{q\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{q}}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle+{\frac{\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}}\,dw}{p\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{p}}}\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{p}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\int_{E}{a_{n}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q}}\,dw}{q\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{q}}\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{q}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle S\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{r}^{q}+\lVert u\rVert_{r}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle S

which is an contradiction and we prove that
\lVert u\rVert_{r}=1
. Moreover, (
4.5
) yields

S\geqslant\frac{1}{p}\lim_{n\to\infty}{\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla(u_{n}-u)\right\rvert^{p}\,dw}}+\lim_{n\to\infty}{\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{a_{n}(x)}{q}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw}\geqslant\frac{1}{p}\lim_{n\to\infty}{\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla(u_{n}-u)\right\rvert^{p}\,dw}}+S.

Hence, we conclude that
u_{n}\to u
in
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
by Remark
2.18
. Therefore, by using a variant of Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, Proposition
2.17
(ii)
implies

(4.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{E}{a_{n}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u_{n}\right\rvert^{q}}\,dw=\int_{E}{\tilde{a}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q}}\,dw.

Hence
u
satisfies (
1.8
).

Furthermore, by Ekeland’s variational principle (see
[
11
]
), we obtain
I(v_{n})\to S
and

(4.7)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(I^{\prime}(v_{n})-\lambda_{n}J^{\prime}(v_{n})\right)=0,

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a Lagrange multiplier sequence. Thus, by (
4.6
) and the boundedness of
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
in
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert v_{n}\right\rvert^{r}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u_{n}\right\rvert^{p}}\,dw+\int_{E}{a_{n}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u_{n}\right\rvert^{q}}\,dw\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}}\,dw+\int_{E}{\tilde{a}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q}}\,dw.

Let
\lambda:=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}
. It follows from
r>p^{*}
,
\lVert u\rVert_{r}=1
and the Sobolev inequality (
1.2
) that
\lambda>0
. For any
\varphi\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, we have
\left\{\varphi_{n}(x):=\varphi(x-y_{n})\right\}
is bounded in
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
by Remark
2.18
. Let

\langle\tilde{I}^{\prime}(u),\varphi\rangle:=\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p-2}{\nabla u}{\nabla\varphi}}\,dw+\int_{E}{\tilde{a}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q-2}{\nabla u}{\nabla\varphi}}\,dw.

Then it follows from (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{I}^{\prime}(u),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u_{n}\right\rvert^{p-2}{\nabla u_{n}}{\nabla\varphi}}\,dw+\int_{E}{a_{n}(x)\left\lvert\nabla u_{n}\right\rvert^{q-2}{\nabla u_{n}}{\nabla\varphi}}\,dw\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}I^{\prime}(v_{n})\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}J^{\prime}(v_{n})\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert u_{n}\right\rvert^{r-2}u_{n}\varphi\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{r-2}u\varphi\,d\mu,

that is,
u
is a nontrivial weak solution of (
1.9
). It is clear that
u
is a pointwise solution of (
1.9
). Similar to the arguments in Theorem
1.3
, one can show that if
v_{n}
are non-negative, then
u
is positive.
∎

Now we provide the proof of Corollary
1.5
.

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

The proof is split in two steps.

(i)

a
is
T
-periodic.

Let
\{v_{n}\}\subset D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
be a minimizing sequence of (
1.6
) and
{x_{n}}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be such that
\left\lvert v_{n}(x_{n})\right\rvert=\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{\infty}
. Since
\{\left\lvert v_{n}\right\rvert\}
is also a minimizing sequence, we may assume that

Let
\{k_{n}:=(k_{n}^{1},...,k_{n}^{N})\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
to ensure that
\{x_{n}-Tk_{n}\}\subset\Omega

\Omega=[0,T)^{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Let
\left\{u_{n}(x):=v_{n}(x+Tk_{n})\right\}
. Then, by Theorem
1.4

u_{n}\to u
in
D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, where
u
satisfies (
1.8
) and solves (

u
is a positive minimizer for (
1.6
) and pointwise solution of (
1.7
), since
\tilde{a}(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a(x+Tk_{n})=a(x)
.

(ii)

a
is a bounded potential.

We consider the case that
a\not\equiv a_{\infty}:=\lim\limits_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to\infty}a(x)
, since the case that
a
is a constant is contained in the proof above. Then there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
a(x_{0})<a_{\infty}
.
Let

(4.8)

S_{\infty}:=\inf_{u\in M_{\frac{1}{r}}}\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{a_{\infty}}{q}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw.

We claim that
S<S_{\infty}
. In fact, it follows from the first part of this proof that problem (
4.8
) has a positive solution
u_{\infty}
. Then there exists
x_{1}y_{1}\in E
such that
(\nabla u_{\infty})(x_{1},y_{1})\neq 0
. Hence, for
v(x):=u_{\infty}(x+x_{1}-x_{0})

S_{\infty}=\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla v\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{a_{\infty}}{q}\left\lvert\nabla v\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw>\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla v\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{a(x)}{q}\left\lvert\nabla v\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw\geq S.

Now, let
\{v_{n}\}\subset D^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
be a minimizing sequence of (
1.6
) and
{x_{n}}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}

\left\lvert v_{n}(x_{n})\right\rvert=\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{\infty}

\{\left\lvert v_{n}\right\rvert\}

are non-negative. Let

u_{n}\to\tilde{u}

S=\int_{E}{\left(\frac{1}{p}\left\lvert\nabla\tilde{u}\right\rvert^{p}+\frac{\tilde{a}(x)}{q}\left\lvert\nabla\tilde{u}\right\rvert^{q}\right)}\,dw.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

In the final section, by adapting the arguments of
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
, we shall study the asymptotic behaviour of Lagrangian multiplier
\lambda
with respect to
t
. It follows from (
1.7
) that

\lambda\left(u_{t}\right)=\frac{\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u_{t}\right\rvert^{p}\,dw}+\int_{E}{a(x)\left\lvert\nabla u_{t}\right\rvert^{q}\,dw}}{\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert u_{t}\right\rvert^{r}\,d\mu}.

Since
I(u_{t})=S_{t}
and
\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert u_{t}\right\rvert^{r}=rt
, we have

\frac{pS_{t}}{rt}\leqslant\lambda\left(u_{t}\right)\leqslant\frac{qS_{t}}{rt}.

Hence, for the proof of Theorem
1.6
, it suffices to show

\frac{S_{t}}{t}\to 0\text{ as }t\to\infty,\text{ and }\frac{S_{t}}{t}\to\infty\text{ as }t\to 0.

Proof of theorem
1.6
.

The proof is split in two steps.

(i)

Let
r>q
.

First, fix
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Thus, for
t>1
, we have

\frac{S_{t}}{t}\leqslant\frac{I\left((rt)^{\frac{1}{r}}\delta_{y}\right)}{t}\leqslant(rt)^{\frac{q}{r}-1}I\left(\delta_{y}\right).

Hence
\frac{S_{t}}{t}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
because
r>q
.

Now, let
t\in(0,1)
. Take
u_{t}\in A_{t}
. Then, by
the
Sobolev inequality (
1.2
), we obtain

\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u_{t}\right\rvert^{p}\,dw}\geqslant C_{r}^{p}t^{\frac{p}{r}}.

Thus

\frac{S_{t}}{t}=\frac{I(u_{t})}{t}\geqslant\frac{{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{E}{\left\lvert\nabla u_{t}\right\rvert^{p}\,dw}}{t}\geqslant\frac{1}{p}C_{r}^{p}t^{\frac{p}{r}-1},

hence
\frac{S_{t}}{t}\to\infty
as
t\to 0
because
r>p^{*}>p
.

(ii)

Let
r=q
and
a
satisfies the hypotheses of Corollary
1.5
.

Since
r>p
still holds, we only need to show

\frac{S_{t}}{t}\to\infty\text{ as }t\to 0.

Let

w_{n}(x):=\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{N}{r}},&x\in\left[-\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}\right)^{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N},\\
0,&else.\end{cases}

Then
\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert w_{n}\right\rvert^{r}=1
and

I(w_{n})\leqslant\frac{2}{p}Nn^{N-1}n^{-\frac{Np}{r}}+\frac{2}{q}\lVert a\rVert_{\infty}Nn^{N-1}n^{-\frac{Nq}{r}}\leqslant C(n^{N(1-\frac{p}{r})-1}+n^{-1}),

Thus, for
t>1
and
t^{\frac{1}{N}}-1\leqslant n<t^{\frac{1}{N}}
,

\frac{S_{t}}{t}\leqslant\frac{I\left((rt)^{\frac{1}{r}}w_{n}\right)}{t}\leqslant\frac{C_{1}(t^{\frac{p}{r}}n^{N(1-\frac{p}{r})-1}+t^{\frac{q}{r}}n^{-1})}{t}<C_{2}(t^{-\frac{1}{N}}+(t^{\frac{1}{N}}-1)^{-1}).

Hence
\frac{S_{t}}{t}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

∎

Remark 5.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
with

, our second conclusion, i.e.,

Remark 5.2

Theorem

can be extended to more general graphs. Let

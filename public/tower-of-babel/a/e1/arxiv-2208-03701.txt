Zero-sum continuous-time Markov games with one-side stopping
Yurii Averboukh

Abstract
The paper is concerned with a variant of the continuous-time finite state Markov game of control and stopping where both players can affect transition rates, while only one player can choose a stopping time. We use the dynamic programming principle and reduce this problem to a system of ODEs with unilateral constraints. This system plays the role of the Bellman equation. We show that its solution provides the optimal strategies of the players. Additionally, we prove the existence and uniqueness theorem for the deduced system of ODEs with unilateral constraints. MSC Classification (2020): 91A05, 91A25, 90C40, 60G40, 60J27. Keywords: continuous-time Markov games, dynamic programming, verification theorem, stopping time.

Introduction

The continuous-time Markov processes can be controlled in two ways. First, the decision maker can affect transition rates. The second way involves the choice of the stopping time. The case where the transition rates are controlled refers to the Markov decision problem (see
[
7
]
,
[

[

[

4

where the players choose stopping times and the games of control and stopping where all players can affect the transition rates as well as the stopping time
[

. In the paper, we consider one-side stopping Markov game that implies that one player chooses a control and a stopping time, while the other one affects only the control. Our interest to this problem is motivated by the possible application of these games to the differential games with minimal cost studied in
[
2

13

We study the continuous-time finite state Markov chain governed by two decision makers with opposite purposes. Without loss of generality, we assume that the state space is
\mathcal{S}=\{1,\ldots,d\}
. The transition rates are described by the Kolmogorov matrix
Q(t,u,v)=(Q_{i,j}(t,u,v))_{i,j=1}^{d}
, i.e., for each

Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)\geq 0\text{ for }i\neq j,

while

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}Q_{i,j}=0.

Here

(respectively,

) is a control of the first (respectively, second) player. It is assumed that
u\in U
,
v\in V
, while
t\in[0,T]
. We assume that the stopping of the game is chosen by the first player. If

is an initial time,

is a state of the Markov chain at time
t
, while
\tau
is a stopping time, then the quality of the examined one-side Markov game is evaluated by

g(\tau,X(\tau))+\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}h(t^{\prime},X(t^{\prime}),u(t^{\prime}),v(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}.

(1)

Here
\mathbb{E}
stands for the expectation. We assume that the first player tries to minimize functional (
1
), while the second player wishes to maximize it.

The universal tool for the study of Markov decision processes, Markov games as well as optimal stopping problems is the dynamic programming principle. It primary relies on the feedback strategies and reduces the problem of design of optimal strategies to a certain Bellman equation. In the case of the finite horizon Markov decision processes and the Markov games, the Bellman equation takes a form of system of ODEs. For the game theoretical case, this system was derived in paper by Zachrisson
[
14
]
. Thus, we call it a Zachrisson equation.

In the paper, we use the dynamic programming principle and show the players’ optimal strategies can be constructed based on the solution of the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints. Thus, the existence result for the one-side stopping Markov game is reduced to the existence theorem for the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints that is also obtained in the paper.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we formalize the concept of one-side stopping Markov game and introduce the classes of strategies. The main results are formulated in Section
3
. Here, we, first, claim that, given a solution of the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints, one can design optimal players’ strategies. Additionally, in this section, we formulate the existence and uniqueness result for the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints. These statements imply the existence of the value function of the examined one-side stopping Markov game. Section
4
is concerned with the proof of the first result by the verification argument. The proof of the existence and uniqueness theorem for the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints is given in Section
5

3
Main result

We impose the following assumptions:

(H1)

The control spaces
U
and
V
are compact;

(H2)

the transition rates
Q_{i,j}
and the payoff functions
g(\cdot,i)
,
h(\cdot,i,\cdot,\cdot)
are continuous for each
i
and
j
;

(H3)

the function
g
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. the time variable;

(H4)

for every
t\in[0,T]
,
i\in\mathcal{S}
and each
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\begin{split}\min_{u\in U}\max_{v\in V}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}&{}\Bigl[Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)w_{j}+h(t,i,u,v)\Bigr]\\
&=\max_{v\in V}\min_{u\in U}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}\Bigl[Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)w_{j}+h(t,i,u,v)\Bigr].\end{split}

Notice that assumption
(H4)
can be considered as an analog of the Isaacs’ condition in the theory of differential games
[
10
]
.

We consider the upper and lower values of the game those corresponds to the upper and lower games. We assume that within the upper game the first player chooses his/her strategy (that includes both controls and stopping time) from the class of feedback strategies, while the response of the second player is from the class of strategies with memory. Formally, this means the advantage of the second player. In the lower game, we give the advantage to the first player. The second player’s choice is restricted to the class of feedback strategies, while the first player chooses his/her policy from the class of strategies with memory.

Therefore, the upper and lower values of the one-side stopping Markov game are defined by the rules:

\operatorname{Val}^{+}(t_{0},i_{0})\triangleq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\operatorname{fb}}}\inf_{\vartheta\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{fb}}}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal{V}_{t_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{t_{0},i_{0}}^{u,\beta}G_{t_{0}}^{u,\beta}(\bar{\tau}_{t_{0}}[\vartheta]),

\operatorname{Val}^{-}(t_{0},i_{0})\triangleq\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\operatorname{fb}}}\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{U}_{t_{0}}}\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{t_{0},i_{0}}^{\alpha,v}G_{t_{0}}^{\alpha,v}(\tau).

Notice that due to the fact that, for each
t_{0}\in[0,T]
,
i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
,
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\operatorname{fb}}
,
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\operatorname{fb}}
, the probability
\mathbb{P}^{u,v}_{t_{0},i_{0}}
is well-defined, we have that all aforementioned values are finite.

Moreover,

\operatorname{Val}^{+}(t_{0},i_{0})\geq\operatorname{Val}^{-}(t_{0},i_{0}).

(3)

Below, we show that the one-side stopping Markov games has a value. Additionally, we will show that this value can be obtained as a solution of the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints. The latter object is defined as follows. For
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
i\in\mathcal{S}
, set

H_{i}(t,w)\triangleq\min_{u\in U}\max_{v\in V}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}\Bigl[Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)w_{j}+h(t,i,u,v)\Bigr].

(4)

Notice that, due to assumption
(H4)
,

H_{i}(t,w)=\max_{v\in V}\min_{u\in U}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}\Bigl[Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)w_{j}+h(t,i,u,v)\Bigr].

Below, if
\phi:[0,T]\times\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the
\phi(t)
stands for the vector
(\phi(t,1),\ldots,\phi(t,d))^{T}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{AC}
satisfies the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints if, for each
i\in\mathcal{S}
,

(Z1)

\varphi(T,i)=g(T,i)
;

(Z2)

\varphi(t,i)\leq g(t,i)
for
t\in[0,T]
;

(Z3)

for every point
t\in[0,T]
such that
\frac{d}{dt}\varphi(t,i)
is well-defined and
\varphi(t,i)<g(t,i)
, one has

\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t,i)\leq-H_{i}(t,\varphi(t));

(Z4)

if
\phi(\cdot,i)
is differentiable at
t\in[0,T]
, then

\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t,i)\geq-H_{i}(t,\varphi(t)).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\varphi
satisfy the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints. Then at every
t_{0}\in[0,T]
,
i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
,

\operatorname{Val}^{+}(t_{0},i_{0})=\operatorname{Val}^{-}(t_{0},i_{0})=\varphi(t_{0},i_{0}).

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists a unique function
\varphi
satisfying the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints.

These two theorems obviously yield the following.

Corollary 3.4
.

There exists the value of the one-side stopping Markov game.

Theorem
3.2
and
3.3
are proved in Sections
4
and
5
respectively.

[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
:

\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{N}^{k}(t,i)=-H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t)),\ \ \psi_{N}^{k}(t^{k+1}_{N},i)=\varphi_{N}(t^{k+1}_{N},i),\ \ i\in\mathcal{S}.

(11)

Finally, set, for
t\in[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
,

\varphi_{N}(t,i)\triangleq\psi_{N}^{k}(t,i)\wedge g(t,i).

(12)

Now examine the properties of the sequence
\{\varphi_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}
such that, for every natural
N
,
t\in[0,T]
,
i\in\mathcal{S}
,

|\varphi_{N}(t,i)|\leq C_{1}.

Proof.

First, denote

M_{Q}\triangleq\max\{|Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)|:i,j\in\mathcal{S},\,t\in[0,T],\,u\in U,\,v\in V\},

(13)

M_{h}\triangleq\max\{|h(t,i,u,v)|:t\in[0,T],\,i\in\mathcal{S},\,u\in U,\,v\in V\},

(14)

M_{g}\triangleq\max\{|g(t,i)|:t\in[0,T],\,i\in\mathcal{S}\}.

(15)

Due to assumptions
(H1)
,
(H2)
, the quantities
M_{Q}
,
M_{h}
and
M_{g}
are finite.

Put

\mathcal{M}(t)\triangleq(M_{g}+M_{h}(T-t))e^{dM_{Q}(T-t)}.

(16)

We will prove the estimate

|\varphi_{N}(t,i)|\leq\mathcal{M}(t)

(17)

by the backward induction.

By the definition of the function
\mathcal{M}
, (
17
) holds true at
t=T
.

Further, assume that (
17
) is fulfilled on
[t^{k+1}_{N},T]
. Let us show that the functions

\xi_{N}^{k}(t)\triangleq\max_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|\psi^{k}_{N}(t,i)|

(18)

satisfies on
[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
the following inequality:

\xi_{N}^{k}(t)\leq\mathcal{M}(t).

(19)

Indeed, by (
11
) and (
4
) we have that

\xi^{k}_{N}(t)\leq\mathcal{M}(t^{k+1}_{N})+\int_{[t,t^{k+1}_{N}]}dM_{Q}\xi^{k}_{N}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}+M_{h}(t^{k+1}_{N}-t).

Applying the Gronwall’s inequality and definition of the function
\mathcal{M}
(see (
16
)), we conclude that (
19
) holds true on
[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
. The definition of
\varphi_{N}
on
[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
(see (
12
)) and the fact that
g
is bounded by
M_{g}
, while
\mathcal{M}(t)\geq M_{g}
yield estimate (
17
) on
[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
.

To complete the proof, let

C_{1}\triangleq\mathcal{M}(0).

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

If
k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
,
s,r\in[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
, then

|\psi^{k}_{N}(s,i)-\psi^{k}_{N}(r,i)|\leq C_{2}|s-r|,

where
C_{2}
is a constant depending only on
g
,
h
and
Q
,

Proof.

Since
\psi_{N}^{k}
satisfies ODE (
11
), it suffices to estimate the right-hand side of this equation. First, due to the definition of the Hamiltonian and the constants
M_{Q}
,
M_{h}
(see (
4
), (
13
), (
14
)), we have that

|H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k})|\leq dM_{Q}\max_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|\psi^{k}_{N}(t,i)|+M_{h}.

Using the estimate of
\max_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|\psi^{k}_{N}(t,i)|
by the function
\mathcal{M}
(see (
18
), (
19
)) and the fact that
\mathcal{M}(t)\leq C_{1}
, we obtain the statement of the lemma with
C_{2}\triangleq dM_{Q}C_{1}+M_{h}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

There exists a constant
C_{3}
such that, for every natural
N
,
s,r\in[0,T]
,
i\in\mathcal{S}
,

|\varphi_{N}(s,i)-\varphi_{N}(r,i)|\leq C_{3}|s-r|.

(20)

Proof.

Recall (see assumption
(H3)
) the function
g
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. the time variable. Let
L_{g}
denote this Lipschitz constant. Put

C_{3}\triangleq L_{g}\vee C_{2}.

(21)

Further, let us consider time instants
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}
such that they lies in the same time interval
[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
for some
k
. Without loss of generality, one can assume that
\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)>\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)
. To estimate
|\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)-\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)|=\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)-\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)
, we consider two cases.

1.

\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)<g(t^{\prime\prime},i)
. Therefore,
\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)=\psi_{N}^{k}(t^{\prime\prime},i)
, while
\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)\leq\psi_{N}^{k}(t^{\prime},i)
. The latter inequality directly follows from the definition of the function
\varphi_{N}
(see (
12
)). By Lemma
5.2
, we have that

\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)-\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)\leq\psi^{k}_{N}(t^{\prime},i)-\psi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)\leq C_{2}|t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime}|\leq C_{3}|t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime}|.

2.

\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)=g(t^{\prime\prime},i)
. As above, by definition of the function
\varphi_{N}
(see (
12
)),
\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)\leq g(t^{\prime},i)
. Hence,

\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)-\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime\prime},i)\leq g(t^{\prime},i)-g(t^{\prime\prime},i)\leq L_{g}|t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime}|\leq C_{3}|t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime}|.

Therefore, we derive (
20
) for
s,r
lying in some interval
[t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N}]
. To complete the proof it suffices to sum up inequalities (
20
) on small time intervals.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
t\in[0,T]
and
i\in\mathcal{S}
be such that
\phi_{N}(t,i)<g(t,i)
and
\varphi_{N}
is differentiable at
t
. Then

\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{N}(t,i)\leq-H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t)).

(22)

Proof.

First, let us consider the case where
t\in(t^{k}_{N},t^{k+1}_{N})
for some
k
. Due to the assumption that
\phi_{N}(t,i)<g(t,i)
, we have that

\phi_{N}(t,i)=\psi_{N}^{k}(t,i).

Moreover, the inequality
\phi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)<g(t^{\prime},i)
holds in some neighborhood of
t
. Thus,

\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{N}(t,i)=\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{N}^{k}(t).

By construction of the function
\psi_{N}^{k}
, we have that

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{N}(t,i)=\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{N}^{k}(t)=-H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t)).

Further, notice that
\varphi_{N}(t,j)\leq\psi_{N}^{k}(t,j)
,
Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)\geq 0
for
i\neq j
. This and the equality
\phi_{N}(t,i)=\psi_{N}^{k}(t,i)
imply that, for every
u\in U
and
v\in V
,

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)\varphi_{N}(t,j)+h_{i}(t,i,u,v)\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}Q_{i,j}(t,u,v)\psi_{N}^{k}(t,j)+h_{i}(t,i,u,v).

(23)

Therefore, using the definition of the Hamiltonian (see (
4
)), we obtain the inequality

H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))\leq H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t)).

Combining this with (
5
), we conclude that (
22
) holds true for
t\neq t^{k}_{N}
,

Now let us consider the case when
t=t^{k+1}_{N}
for some
k
. By construction of the function
\varphi_{N}
, we have that
\varphi_{N}(t_{N}^{k+1})=\psi_{N}^{k}(t^{k+1}_{N})
. Additionally, due to the assumption
\varphi_{N}(t_{N}^{k+1},i)<g(t_{N}^{k+1},i)
and the continuity of these functions, we conclude that

\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)<g(t^{\prime},i)
when
t^{\prime}\in(t_{N}^{k+1}-\delta,t_{N}^{k+1}]
. Hence, the construction of the function
\varphi_{N}
gives that
\varphi_{N}(t^{\prime},i)=\psi_{N}^{k}(t^{\prime},i)
on
(t_{N}^{k+1}-\delta,t_{N}^{k+1}]
. Therefore,

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{N}(t^{k+1}_{N},i)=\frac{d^{-}}{dt^{-}}\psi_{N}^{k}(t_{N}^{k+1},i)=-H_{i}(t_{N}^{k+1},\psi_{N}^{k-1}(t_{N}^{k+1})).

Here
\frac{d^{-}}{dt-}
stands for the left-hand derivative. This and (
23
) yield that (
22
) holds true in the case when
t\in\{t^{k}_{N}\}_{k=1}^{N-1}
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists a constant
C_{4}
such that, for every
s,r\in[0,T]
,
s<r
, and
i\in\mathcal{S}
, the following inequality holds true:

\varphi_{N}(r,i)-\varphi_{N}(s,i)\geq\int_{s}^{r}H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))dt-\frac{C_{4}}{N}

Proof.

Let
m,n\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
be such that
s\in[t^{m}_{N},t^{m+1}_{N})
,
r\in(t^{n}_{N},t^{n+1}_{N}]
.

We have that

\begin{split}\varphi_{N}(r,i)-\varphi_{N}(s,i)=\varphi_{N}(r,i)-\varphi_{N}(&t^{n}_{N})+\varphi_{N}(t^{m+1}_{N},i)-\varphi_{N}(s,i)\\
&+\sum_{k=m+1}^{n-1}(\varphi_{N}(t^{k+1}_{N},i)-\varphi_{N}(t^{k}_{N},i))\\
\geq\varphi_{N}(r,i)-\varphi_{N}(&t^{n}_{N})+\varphi_{N}(t^{m+1}_{N},i)-\varphi_{N}(s,i)\\
&+\sum_{k=m+1}^{n-1}(\psi_{N}^{k}(t^{k+1}_{N},i)-\psi^{k}_{N}(t^{k}_{N},i)).\end{split}

In the latter inequality, we used the definition of the functions
\psi_{N}^{k}
and
\varphi_{N}
(see (
11
) and (
12
)). Further, due to Lemma
5.3
,

|\varphi_{N}(r,i)-\varphi_{N}(t^{n}_{N})|,\,|\varphi_{N}(t^{m+1}_{N},i)-\varphi_{N}(s,i)|\leq C_{3}T/N
. Using this and the fact that each function
\psi^{k}_{N}(\cdot)
satisfies (
11
), we conclude that, for each
k
,

\varphi_{N}(r,i)-\varphi_{N}(s,i)\geq-2C_{3}\frac{T}{N}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{n-1}\int^{t^{k+1}_{N}}_{t^{k}_{N}}H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t))dt.

(24)

By the equality
\psi_{N}^{k}(t_{N}^{k+1})=\varphi_{N}(t_{N}^{k+1})
, we have that

\begin{split}H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t))=H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))+(H_{i}&(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t))-H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t_{N}^{k+1})))\\
&+(H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t_{N}^{k+1}))-H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))).\end{split}

Lemmas
5.2
,
5.3
and the fact that
H_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. the second variable with the constant
dM_{Q}
give that, for
t\in[t_{N}^{k},t^{k+1}_{N}]
,

H_{i}(t,\psi_{N}^{k}(t))\geq H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}^{k}(t))-(C_{3}+C_{2})dM_{Q}\frac{T}{N}.

(25)

Finally, using the definition of Hamiltonian (see (
4
)) and Lemma
5.1
, we conclude that

|H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))|\leq C^{\prime}.

Here
C^{\prime}
is a constant dependent only on the examined game. This, inequalities (
24
) and (
25
) and the fact that
r-t_{N}^{n},t^{m+1}_{N}-s\leq T/N
imply that

\begin{split}\varphi_{N}(r,i)-\varphi_{N}(s,i)\geq-(3C_{3}+&C_{2}+C^{\prime})\frac{T}{N}+\int_{s}^{t^{m+1}_{N}}H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))dt\\
&+\sum_{k=m+1}^{n-1}\int^{t^{k+1}_{N}}_{t^{k}_{N}}H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))dt+\int^{r}_{t^{n}_{N}}H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))dt\\
=-(3C_{3}+&C_{2}+C^{\prime})\frac{T}{N}+\int_{s}^{r}H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N}(t))dt.\end{split}

This gives the statement of the lemma.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

As it was mentioned above, to prove the existence of the Zachrisson equation with unilateral constraints, we use the sequence of functions
\{\varphi_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
. Due to Lemma
5.1
, these functions are uniformly bounded. Lemma
5.3
says that these functions are Lipschitz continuous with the same constant. By the Arzela–Ascoli theorem, there exist a sequence
\{\varphi_{N_{l}}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
and a function
\varphi
such that
\varphi_{N_{l}}\rightarrow\varphi
as
l\rightarrow\infty
. The limiting function
\varphi
is bounded by the constant
C_{1}
and is Lipschitz continuous with the constant
C_{3}
. Hence,

\varphi\in\mathcal{AC}.

Further, by construction, each function
\varphi_{N_{l}}
satisfies the boundary condition

\varphi_{N_{l}}(T)=g(T).

Passing to the limit, we obtain that
\varphi
satisfies condition
(Z1)
. Analogously, (
12
) implies that

\varphi_{N_{l}}(t,i)\leq g(t,i),\ \ i\in\mathcal{S}.

Therefore, condition
(Z2)
is fulfilled for the limiting function
\varphi
.

Let
i\in\mathcal{S}
and let
t\in(0,T)
be a point of differentiability of the functions
\varphi(\cdot,i)
such that
\varphi(t,i)<g(t,i)
. Choose
\varepsilon
satisfying

3\varepsilon\leq g(t,i)-\varphi(t,i).

Due to the continuity of the functions
g
and
\varphi
, we have that there exists
\delta>0
such that, for
t^{\prime}\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)
,

\varphi(t^{\prime},i)+2\varepsilon<g(t^{\prime},i).

Using the fact that
\varphi_{N_{l}}\rightarrow\varphi
as
l\rightarrow\infty
, we have that, for sufficiently large
l
and every
t^{\prime}\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)
,

\varphi_{N_{l}}(t^{\prime},i)+\varepsilon<g(t^{\prime},i).

Lemma
5.4
implies that, if
\hat{t}\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)
,

\varphi_{N_{l}}(\hat{t},i)-\varphi_{N_{l}}(t)\leq-\int_{t}^{\hat{t}}H_{i}(t^{\prime},\varphi_{N_{l}}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}.

Passing to the limit, we conclude that

\varphi(\hat{t},i)-\varphi(t)\leq-\int_{t}^{\hat{t}}H_{i}(t^{\prime},\varphi(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}

when
\hat{t}\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)
. Since
t\in(0,T)
is a point of differentiability of the function
\varphi(\cdot,i)
, we arrive at condition
(Z3)
.

To prove that
\varphi
satisfies
(Z4)
, notice that Lemma
5.5
implies that, for every
s,r\in[0,T]
,
s<r
,

\varphi_{N_{l}}(r)-\varphi_{N_{l}}(s)\geq-\int_{s}^{r}H_{i}(t,\varphi_{N_{l}}(t))dt-C_{4}/N.

Passing to the limit, we have

\varphi(r)-\varphi(s)\geq-\int_{s}^{r}H_{i}(t,\varphi(t))dt.

This gives condition
(Z4)
at every point of differentiability of the function
\varphi
.

The uniqueness follows from Theorem
3.2
. Indeed, each functions satisfying (
3.1
) coincides with both upper and lower values.

∎

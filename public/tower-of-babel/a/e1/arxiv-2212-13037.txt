Equivalence and automorphism groups of two families of maximum scattered linear sets Thanks: This is an English translation of the original Chinese version published in Scientia Sinica Mathematica. DOI: 10.1360/SSM-2022-0073
Wei Tang 1, Yue Zhou 1

Abstract
. Linear set in projective spaces over finite fields plays central roles in the study of blocking sets, semifields, rank-metric codes and etc. A linear set with the largest possible cardinality and the maximum rank is called maximum scattered. Despite two decades of study, there are only a few number of known maximum scattered linear sets in projective lines, including two families constructed in [ 5 ] , and [ 6 , 14 ] , respectively. This paper aims to solve the equivalence problem of the linear sets in each of these families and to determine their automorphism groups.



1.
Introduction

The concept of
linear set
was introduced by Lunardon
[
12
]
, which is natural generalization of subgeometrie. In the pasting two decades after this work, linear sets have been intensively investigated and applied to construct and characterize various objects in finite geometry and coding theory, including blocking sets, two-intersection sets, translation spreads of the Cayley generalized Hexagon, translation ovoids of polar spaces, semifields and rank-metric codes. We refer to
[
2
,
8
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we only concern with the linear sets in a projective line. Let
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(V)=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
, where
V
is a
2
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then
\Lambda
is a projective line. A point set
L
of
\Lambda
is said to be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
of rank
k
if it is defined by the non-zero vectors of a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector subspace
U
of
V
, i.e.

L=L_{U}:=\{\langle{\bf u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}:{\bf u}\in U\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}\}.

For any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set

L_{U}
with rank
k
, it is not difficult to see that

|L|\leq\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}.

If the equality holds,
L_{U}
is called a scattered linear set. In
[
3
]
, Blokhuis and Lavrauw show that a scattered linear set has rank at most
n
, and they call a scattered linear set of rank
k=n
in
\Lambda

maximum
.

Under the action of
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
, we can always assume that the linear set
L_{U}
of rank
n
in
\Lambda
does not contain the point
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
. Consequently,
U
and
L_{U}
can be written as

U_{f}=\{(x,f(x))\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\},

and

L_{f}=\left\{\langle(x,f(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\},

where
f
is a
q
-polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, that is
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
. It is not difficult to show that
L_{f}
is scattered if and only if for any
z,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
the condition

\frac{f(z)}{z}=\frac{f(y)}{y}

implies that
z
and
y
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly dependent. Hence,
q
-polynomials satisfying this condition are called
scattered
by Sheekey in
[
18
]
.

Up to now, there are only three families of maximum scattered linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
for infinitely many
n
. We list the corresponding scattered polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
below.

(i)

f=X^{q^{s}}
with
\gcd(s,n)=1
, and
L_{f}
is called the linear set of pseudoregulus type; see
[
3
]
.

(ii)

f=X^{q^{s}}+\delta X^{q^{n-s}}
with
n\geq 4
,
N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)\notin\{0,1\}
and
\gcd(s,n)=1
which was found by Lunardon and Polverino
[
13
]
and later generalized by Sheekey
[
18
]
.

(iii)

f=X^{q^{s}}+X^{q^{s(m-1)}}+h^{1+q^{s}}X^{q^{s(m+1)}}+h^{1-q^{2m-s}}X^{q^{2m-s}}
with
q
odd,
n=2m\geq 6
,
h^{q^{m}+1}=-1
and
\gcd(s,n)=1
. This family was first introduced by Longobardi and Zanella in
[
11
]
and later generalized to a big family in
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in several families of maximum scattered linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{6})
and
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{8})
. In the following we list their corresponding scattered polynomials.

(a)

x^{q}+x^{q^{3}}+\delta x^{q^{5}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
with
\delta^{2}+\delta=1
and
q
is odd; see
[
6
,
14
]
.

(b)

x^{q^{s}}+\delta x^{q^{s+m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2m}}
with
m\in\{3,4\}
,
\gcd(s,m)=1
and some conditions on
\delta
; see
[
5
]
.

For convenience, we use
\mathscr{F}_{6}
and
\mathscr{F}_{8}
to denote the families of linear sets given in (b) for
m=3
and
4
, respectively. Let
f_{\delta,m}
stand for a member with parameter
(\delta,m)
in
\mathscr{F}_{6}
or
\mathscr{F}_{8}
.

For
m=3
, it has been shown in
[
5
]
that for
q>4
and
N_{{q^{6}}/{q^{3}}}(\delta)\neq 1
we can always find
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
such that
f_{\delta,3}
is scattered. The exact number of such
\delta
has been determined by Bartoli, Csajbók and Montanucci in
[
1
]
.

For
m=4
, it also has been proved in
[
5
]
that for odd
q
,
N_{{q^{8}}/{q^{4}}}(\delta)\neq 1
and
\delta^{2}=-1
,
f_{\delta,4}
is scattered. Moreover, Timpanella and Zini
[
20
]
show that
L_{\delta,4}
is maximum scattered if and only if
\delta^{1+q^{4}}=-1
, under the extra assumption that the odd prime power
q\geq 1039891
.

For two linear sets
L_{U}
and
L_{W}
of
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
, if there is an element
\varphi
in
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
such that
L_{U}^{\varphi}=L_{W}
, then we say
L_{U}
and
L_{W}
are
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-equivalent
. For given
q
and
n
, it is obvious that there is only one maximum scattered linear set of pseudoregulus type, because

\{\langle(1,x^{q^{s}-1})\rangle:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}=\{\langle(1,x^{q-1})\rangle:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}

for any integer
s
satisfying
\gcd(s,n)=1
. The equivalence of different members in the Lunardon-Polverino family has been recently solved by the authors and Zullo in
[
19
]
. The equivalence for the scattered quadrinomials from the third infinite family has been partially determined in
[
11
]
.

In this paper, we continue to consider the equivalence among the members in the families (a) and (b) defined on
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{6})
and
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{8})
, respectively. Firstly, in Section 3, we prove that (a) contains only one element up to
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-equivalence, and we completely determine its automorphism group. Then, in Section 4, the necessary and sufficient conditions for the equivalence of the elements in
\mathscr{F}_{6}
and
\mathscr{F}_{8}
are given, and their automorphism groups are further determined. The main ideas for proving the above results are as follows: First, we transform the
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-equivalence problem into the equivalence of image sets of the corresponding polynomial maps. By further analyzing and verifying some necessary conditions on the coefficients of these polynomials, and considering some properties of their adjoint polynomials, we can determine the equivalence between the linear sets and their automorphism groups.

The rest part of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce some preliminaries and auxiliary results for the equivalence of maximum scattered linear sets. In Section
3
, we focus on the equivalence problem for the linear sets derived from the scattered polynomials given in (a) and determine their automorphism groups. In Section
4
, we turn to the equivalence problem of linear sets in
\mathscr{F}_{6}
and
\mathscr{F}_{8}
. In section
5
, we briefly summarized the contents of this paper.

2.
Preliminaries

A
q
-
polynomial
(or a
linearized polynomial
) over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is a polynomial of the following form

f=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}X^{q^{i}},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
k
is a positive integer.

We will denote by
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
the set of all
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with degree less than
q^{n}
. There is a bijection between
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
and the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
For more details on linearized polynomials we refer to
[
9
, Chapter 3, Section 4]
.

Consider the non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

\langle x,y\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(xy),

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and where
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)=x+\cdots+x^{q^{n-1}}
.
The
adjoint polynomial

\hat{f}
of the
q
-polynomial
\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}
with respect to the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, i.e. the unique function over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
satisfying

\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(yf(z))=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(z\hat{f}(y))

for every
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, is given by

\hat{f}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}^{q^{n-i}}X^{q^{n-i}}.

The following result concerning the linear set defined by the adjoint of a
q
-polynomial was proved in
[
2
, Lemma 2.6]
and
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
in different ways.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be a
\sigma
-polynomial in
\mathcal{L}_{n,\sigma}
.Then for any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, there is

\#\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}:\frac{f(x)}{x}=b\right\}=\#\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}:\frac{\hat{f}(y)}{y}=b\right\}.

In particular,
L_{f}=L_{\hat{f}}
.

Two linear sets
L_{U}
and
L_{U^{\prime}}
of
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
are said to be
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-
equivalent
(or
projectively equivalent
) if there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
such that
L_{U}^{\varphi}=L_{U^{\prime}}
.

Let
L_{U}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set of rank
n
in
\Lambda
. Under the action of
{\rm P\Gamma L}(1,q^{n})
on
\Lambda
, we may assume that
L_{U}
does not contain the point
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
, so that
L_{U}
can be written as

L_{f}=\{\langle(x,f(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\},

where
f
is a
q
-polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, that is
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
.
Sheekey in
[
18
]
called
f

scattered
if
L_{f}
turns out to be a scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set.

For two
q
-polynomials
f
and
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, we say
U_{f}=\{(x,f(x)):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
and
U_{g}=\{(x,g(x)):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}
-
equivalent
if there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
such that
U_{f}^{\varphi}=U_{g}
. From the definitions, we get the following straightforward result.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
q
-polynomials
f
and
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, if
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}
-equivalent, then
L_{f}
and
L_{g}
are
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-equivalent.

However, the converse statement is not always true. For instance, if
f(x)=x^{q}
and
g(x)=x^{q^{s}}
with
s\neq 1
and
\gcd(s,n)=1
, then
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are not
\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent, but obviously
L_{f}=\left\{\langle(1,x^{q-1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\}=L_{g}
. For more results on the equivalence problems, we refer to
[
5
,
7
]
.

In general, the equivalence problem between
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
is easier than the equivalence between
L_{f}
and
L_{g}
. For instance, the equivalence between
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
with
f
,
g
from family (b) has been completely determined; see the following lemma. However, the equivalence between
L_{f}
and
L_{g}
is still open and will be solved in Section
4
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
5
, Proposition 5.1]

Define two
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces
U_{\delta,s_{1}}=\{(x,x^{q^{s_{1}}}+\delta x^{q^{s_{1}+m}}):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2m}}\}
and
U_{\theta,s_{2}}=\{(x,x^{q^{s_{2}}}+\theta x^{s_{2}+m}):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2m}}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2m}}^{2}
with
\delta
,
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2m}}^{*}
. Assume that
N_{q^{2m}/{q^{m}}}(\delta)\neq 1
,
N_{q^{2m}/{q^{m}}}(\theta)\neq 1
,
1\leq s_{1},s_{2}<m
and
\gcd(m,s_{1})=\gcd(m,s_{2})=1
. The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces
U_{\delta,s_{1}},U_{\theta,s_{2}}
are

\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{2m})
-equivalent if and only if either

s_{1}=s_{2}\text{ and }N_{q^{2m}/{q^{m}}}(\delta)=N_{q^{2m}/{q^{m}}}(\theta)^{\sigma},

or

s_{1}+s_{2}=m\text{ and }N_{q^{2m}/{q^{m}}}(\delta)N_{q^{2m}/{q^{m}}}(\theta)^{\sigma}=1,

for some automorphism
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
.

Given two (multi-)subsets
S
and
T\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
,
S=T
implies that

\sum_{x\in S}x^{d}=\sum_{x\in T}x^{d},

for any non-negative integer
d
. As a direct consequence, we can get the following necessary result for two identical linear sets.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
4
, Lemma 3.4]

Let
f
and
g
be two linearized polynomials. If
L_{f}=L_{g}
, then for each non-negative integer
d
the following holds

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}\left(\frac{g(x)}{x}\right)^{d}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}\left(\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)^{d}.

The following result is well-known.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any prime power
q
and integer
d
we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}x^{d}=\begin{cases}-1,&q-1\mid d;\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By applying Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, one can prove the following criterion on the identity of two linear sets.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
4
, Lemma 3.6]

Let
f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}
and
g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x^{q^{i}}
be two
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, such that
L_{f}=L_{g}
. Then

(1)

a_{0}=b_{0};

for
k=1,2,\cdots,n-1
,

(2)

a_{k}a_{n-k}^{q^{k}}=b_{k}b_{n-k}^{q^{k}};

for
k=2,3,\cdots,n-1
,

(3)

a_{1}a_{k-1}^{q}a_{n-k}^{q^{k}}+a_{k}a_{n-1}^{q}a_{n-k+1}^{q^{k}}=b_{1}b_{k-1}^{q}b_{n-k}^{q^{k}}+b_{k}b_{n-1}^{q}b_{n-k+1}^{q^{k}}.

In general, the restrictions on the coefficients of
f
and
g
in Lemma
2.6
are not enough to determine
g
from
f
. In Sections
3
and
4
, we will provide some extra restrictions for
f
and
g
in (a) or (b).

3.
Csajbók-Marino-Montanucci-Zullo family

In this section, we consider the equivalence problem for the family of maximum scattered linear sets which is found for odd prime power
q\equiv\pm 1,0\pmod{5}
by Csajbók, Marino and Zullo in
[
6
]
and later completely proved for any odd prime power
q
by Marino, Montanucci and Zullo in
[
14
]
. Recall that the linear sets in this family are of the shape

\left\{\langle(1,,x^{q-1}+x^{q^{3}-1}+\theta x^{q^{5}-1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}}:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}^{*}\right\},

where
\theta^{2}+\theta=1
.
By definition, there are at most two elements in this family for given
q
which is odd. The following simple lemma tells us whether
\theta
is in the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For positive integer
n
and odd prime power
q
, suppose that
x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}=x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}=1
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then

(i)

x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, if
q\equiv 0,\pm 1\pmod{5}
;

(ii)

x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, if
q\equiv\pm 2\pmod{5}
.

Moreover,
x_{1}^{2q-1}=x_{2}^{2q-1}
if and only if
x_{1}=x_{2}
. In particular, when
q\equiv 0\pmod{5}
,
x_{1}=x_{2}=2
.

Proof.

Let
q=p^{m}
such that
p
is prime. By the law of quadratic reciprocity,
5
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
if and only if
p=0,1
or
4\pmod{5}
.

Suppose that
p=5
. The two roots
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
of the polynomial
X^{2}+X-1
are identical and
x_{1}=x_{2}=2\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Suppose that
q\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
. When
p=1
or
4\pmod{5}
,
5
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
whence
5
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. When
p=2
or
3\pmod{5}
which means
q=p^{2k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
5
is also a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Suppose that
q\equiv\pm 2\pmod{5}
. Then
p=2
or
3\pmod{5}
and
q=p^{2k+1}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
5
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Next we show that the necessary and sufficient condition of
x_{1}^{2q-1}=x_{2}^{2q-1}
is
x_{1}=x_{2}
.
The sufficiency is obvious.

Assume that
x_{1}^{2q-1}=x_{2}^{2q-1}
. If
q\equiv 0,\pm 1\pmod{5}
, by (a) we get
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. From
x_{1}=x_{1}^{2q-1}=x_{2}^{2q-1}=x_{2}
, we derive
x_{1}=x_{2}
.

If
q\equiv\pm 2\pmod{5}
, by (b) we get
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. As
x_{1},x_{2}
are two (not necessarily distinct) roots of
x^{2}+x-1
,
x_{1}=x_{2}
or
x_{1}=x_{2}^{q}
. Assume that
x_{1}=x_{2}^{q}
, by
x_{1}^{2q-1}=x_{2}^{2q-1}
and
x_{2}^{q+1}=-1
we get
x_{2}^{6}=-1
. As
x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}=1
,

(1-x_{2})^{3}=-x_{2}^{3}+3x_{2}^{2}-3x_{2}+1=x_{2}(4x_{2}-4)+1=5-8x_{2}=-1,

which implies

x_{2}=3/4.

However, this contradicts
x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}=1
under the assumption that
p\neq 2,5
. Therefore
x_{1}
must be identical to
x_{2}
.
∎

Before looking at maximum scattered linear sets in the Csajbók-Marino-Montanucci-Zullo family, we first consider the equivalence between the
6
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces
U_{f}
associated with different
L_{f}
from this family. Depending on the value of
q
mod
5
, our result are divided into two lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

For odd prime power
q
with
q\equiv\pm 2\pmod{5}
, define functions
f(x)=x^{q}+x^{q^{3}}+\theta x^{q^{5}}
,
g(x)=x^{q}+x^{q^{3}}+\delta x^{q^{5}}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
with
\theta
,
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
satisfying
\theta^{2}+\theta=1
and
\delta^{2}+\delta=1
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}
-equivalent.

Proof.

As
\theta,\delta
are the roots of
x^{2}+x-1
which is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\theta=\delta
or
\theta=\delta^{q}
.

If
\theta=\delta
, then
U_{f}=U_{g}
; if
\theta=\delta^{q}
, then

\left\{(x^{q},(x^{q}+x^{q^{3}}+\delta x^{q^{5}})^{q}):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}\right\}=\left\{(x,x^{q}+x^{q^{3}}+\delta^{q}x^{q^{5}}):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}\right\}=U_{f}.

Therefore,
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}
-equivalent.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For odd prime power
q
with
q\equiv 0,\pm 1\pmod{5}
, define functions
f(x)=x^{q}+x^{q^{3}}+\theta x^{q^{5}}
,
g(x)=x^{q}+x^{q^{3}}+\delta x^{q^{5}}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
with
\theta
,
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
satisfying
\theta^{2}+\theta=1
and
\delta^{2}+\delta=1
. If
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}
-equivalent,
then
\theta=\delta.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}
-equivalent.
By Lemma
3.1
,
\theta,\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We only have to consider the existence of invertible matrix
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

Now

We apply Lemma
2.6
on the above equation. By (

(33)

r_{0}=-d.

Moreover, by (

By (

, which means

r_{m}=0.

By (

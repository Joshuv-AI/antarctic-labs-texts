Abstract
. We study log canonical models of foliated surfaces of general type. In particular, we show that log canonical models of general type and their minimal partial du Val resolutions are bounded. Moreover, we show the valuative criteria of separatedness and properness and a property related to local-closedness for the moduli functor ℳ P sm {\mathcal{M}}_{P}^{\textnormal{sm}} which parametrizes the stable smoothable foliated surfaces pairs. On the way, we also show a result on the invariance of plurigenera.

Introduction

The study of foliated surfaces of general type has seen significant progress in recent years.
By work of McQuillan and Brunella (see
[
McQ08
]
,
[
Bru15
]
, and the reference therein), it is known that foliated surfaces of general type with only canonical foliation singularities admit a unique canonical model.
Thus it is natural to study the moduli theory for these canonical models, for instance, the existence of the moduli functor.

A first step toward this was achieved in
[
HL21
]
where it is shown that canonical models of foliated surfaces of general type with fixed Hilbert function are birationally bounded.
Notice that, by a result of McQuillan,
K_{\mathscr{F}}
is not
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier for any canonical model
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
with cusp singularities.
Thus, the moduli functor is expected not to be algebraic.
However, if we consider the moduli functor for some natural birational modifications of these canonical models, then it is better behaved.
For instance, in
[
Che21
]
, it is shown that minimal partial du Val resolutions (see Definition
3.5
) of these canonical models are bounded.
As a consequence, the boundedness of these canonical models also follows.

The next natural property to investigate is the local-closedness.
We recall the following example.

Example 0.1

(
[
Bru01
, page 114]
)
.

Consider
\pi:{\mathbb{C}}^{2}_{x,\,y}\times({\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}\setminus\{0,1\})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}\setminus\{0,1\}
and a family of foliations given by

x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\lambda y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

We can extend this family to a family
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0,1\})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0,1\}
.
Note that the singularity is reduced (see Definition
1.14
) if and only if
\lambda\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
.

In light of this example, the moduli functor for canonical models (or their minimal partial du Val resolutions) is
not
locally closed.
To solve this issue, we allow some special
log canonical
foliation singularities and consider
log canonical
models (see Definition
2.1
).
As in
[
HL21
]
and
[
Che21
]
, we first show the following boundedness for minimal partial du Val resolutions of log canonical models. (See Theorem
3.9
.)

Theorem 0.2
.

Fix a function
P:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{P}
consisting of minimal partial du Val resolutions
(Y,{\mathscr{G}},D)
(see Definition
3.5
) of log canonical models
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
of general type with Hilbert function
\chi(mK_{\mathscr{F}})=P(m)
is bounded.
More precisely, there exists a projective morphism
\mu:{\mathcal{Y}}\longrightarrow T
and a family of foliations
{\mathcal{F}}
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
over
T
(see Definition
3.8
) where
{\mathcal{Y}}
and
T
are quasi-projective varieties of finite type such that, for any
(Y,{\mathscr{G}},D)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{P}
, there is a
t\in T
and an isomorphism
\rho:Y\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
such that
{\mathscr{G}}(-D)\cong\rho^{*}({\mathcal{F}}|_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}})
.

Using the techniques in
[
CF18
]
, we also show the following invariance of plurigenera for families of minimal partial du Val resolutions of log canonical models of general type with fixed Hilbert function. (See Theorem
4.2
.)
Note that condition
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)_{t}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{P}
puts a very strong restriction on

.

Theorem 0.3
.

Given any family of foliated triples
f:({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)\longrightarrow T
where

is connected
and

for all

(see Definition

is independent of

. (See Lemma

Next, for a fixed function

, we use the techniques from
[

]
and
[
CS21
]
, to establish the semi-stable

-minimal model program for the semi-stable families of foliated triples. (See Section 4 and Theorem

Theorem 0.4
.

Let

1.
Preliminaries

In this paper, we always work over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Throughout the paper, a scheme will refer to a scheme of finite type over
{\mathbb{C}}
and a variety is a complex analytic space.

In this section, we recall several definitions and results which will be used later.

1.1.
Intersection theory on normal complete surfaces

We will use Mumford’s intersection pairing on normal surfaces (see, e.g.,
[
Sak84
]
) and some of its properties.

Let
X
be a normal complete surface and
f:Y\longrightarrow X
be a proper birational morphism from a smooth surface
Y
, for instance, the minimal resolution of singularities.
Let
E=\cup_{j}E_{j}
be the exceptional divisor of
f
.
Since the intersection matrix
(E_{i}\cdot E_{j})
is negative definite, for any Weil
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
D
on
X
, we can define
f^{*}D
as
\widetilde{D}+\sum_{j}a_{j}E_{j}
where
\widetilde{D}
is the proper transform of
D
and the
a_{j}
’s are real numbers uniquely determined by the property that
(\widetilde{D}+\sum_{j}a_{j}E_{j})\cdot E_{i}=0
for every
i
.

For any two Weil
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
on
X
, we define the intersection number
D_{1}\cdot D_{2}:=(f^{*}D_{1})\cdot(f^{*}D_{2})
.
Note that if
D_{1}
is Cartier and
D_{2}
is an irreducible curve, then
D_{1}\cdot D_{2}
agrees with the degree of
{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{2}}(D_{1})
.

1.4.
Foliations on normal varieties

1.5.1.
Non-invariant curves

We first consider the non-invariant curves and define the tangency order for them.

Definition 1.18

(Tangency order)
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated normal surface and
p\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})\setminus\textnormal{Sing}(X)
.
Let
v
be the vector field generating
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
and
C
be a
non-invariant
curve through
p
.
Let
f
be the local defining function of

at
p
.
We define the
tangency order
of
{\mathscr{F}}
along

at

to be

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}}{\langle f,v(f)\rangle}.

Note that
\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)\geq 0
and is independent of the choices of

and

.
Moreover, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is transverse to

at

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0
.

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C):=\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p).

Theorem 1.19

(Adjunction for non-invariant divisors,
[
Bru97
]
,
[
Bru15
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface

.
Let

be a non-invariant irreducible curve on

be the normalization of

.
Then there is an effective divisor
\Delta
on

.

Let

be a non-invariant curve on a foliated surface

.

1.5.2.
Invariant curves

Now we consider the invariant curves.

Definition 1.21
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface and
p\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})\setminus\textnormal{Sing}(X)
.
Let
\omega
be a
1
-form generating
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
.
If
C
is an invariant curve and
f
is the local defining function at
p
, then we can write

g\omega=h\textnormal{d}f+f\eta

where
g
and
h
are holomorphic functions,
\eta
is a holomorphic
1
-form, and
h,f
are relatively prime functions.

We define the index
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
to be the vanishing order of
\frac{h}{g}|_{C}
at
p
.
Also we define Camacho-Sad index
\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
to be the residue of
\frac{-1}{h}\eta|_{C}
at
p
.
These two definitions are independent of the choices of
f
,
g
,
h
,
\omega
,
\eta
.
(For a reference, see
[
Bru15
, page 15 in Chapter 2 and page 27 in Chapter 3]
.)

Note that if
p\not\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})
, then
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0=\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
.
Therefore, if
C
is compact, then we can define

\displaystyle\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)\textnormal{ and }

\displaystyle\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p).

Theorem 1.22

(Adjunction for invariant divisors,
[
Bru97
]
,
[
Bru15
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface
X
.
Let
C
be an invariant irreducible curve on
X
and
C^{\nu}
be its normalization.
Then there is an effective divisor
\Delta
on
C^{\nu}
such that
K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{C^{\nu}}=K_{C^{\nu}}+\Delta
with
\deg\Delta=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C)+\deg\textnormal{Diff}_{C}(0)
where
\textnormal{Diff}_{C}(0)
is the different with
(K_{X}+C)|_{C^{\nu}}=K_{C^{\nu}}+\textnormal{Diff}_{C}(0)
.
In particular, we have
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot C=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C)+2p_{a}(C)-2
where
p_{a}(C)
is the arithmetic genus of
C
.

Theorem 1.23

(Camacho-Sad formula,
[
CS82
]
,
[
LN88
]
,
[
Suw98
]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface
X
, and
C
be an invariant curve on
X
.
Then we have
C^{2}=\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C)
.

Lemma 1.24
.

Given a foliated normal surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.
Let
p\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})\setminus\textnormal{Sing}(X)
and
\omega
be a
1
-form generating
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
.

(1)

Suppose
p
is reduced and non-degenerate singularity.
Then we may assume that
\omega=\lambda y(1+o(1))\textnormal{d}x-x(1+o(1))\textnormal{d}y
around
p
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=\frac{1}{\lambda},\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=\lambda\mbox{, and }

\displaystyle\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=1=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p).

(2)

Suppose
p
is a saddle-node.
Then we may assume that

\omega=y^{k+1}\textnormal{d}x-(x(1+\nu y^{k})+yo(k))\textnormal{d}y

around
p
. Hence,
\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=0
and
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=1
.
Suppose there exists a weak separatrix, then
\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=\nu\mbox{ and }\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=k+1
.

(3)

Suppose
{\mathscr{F}}
has a positive rational eigenvalue
\lambda=\frac{m}{n}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
in the reduced form at
p
.
Then we may assume
\omega=\lambda y(1+o(1))\textnormal{d}x-x(1+o(1))\textnormal{d}y
around
p
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=\frac{1}{\lambda},\,\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=\lambda,\,\mbox{and }

\displaystyle\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=1=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p).

Moreover, for the invariant curve
C
through
p=(0,0)
which is locally defined by
ax^{m}(1+o(1))-y^{n}(1+o(1))=0
, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=mn\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=1-(m-1)(n-1).

Proof
.

We will only show the case
(3)
.
For the first two cases, the computations are similar.
(See also
[
Bru15
, page 30-31 in Chapter 3]
.)

For the case
f=x
, note that
1\cdot\omega=(\lambda y(1+o(1)))\textnormal{d}x+x(-(1+o(1))\textnormal{d}y)
.
Then we take
g=1
,
h=\lambda y(1+o(1))
, and
\eta=-(1+o(1))\textnormal{d}y
.
Thus we have

\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=\textnormal{res}_{p}\frac{-1}{h}\eta|_{f=0}=\textnormal{res}_{p}\frac{1}{\lambda y(1+o(1))}(1+o(1))\textnormal{d}y|_{x=0}=\frac{1}{\lambda}

and

\displaystyle Z({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{vanishing order of }\frac{h}{g}|_{f=0}

\displaystyle=\textnormal{vanishing order of }\frac{\lambda y(1+o(1))}{1}|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=1.

For the case
f=y
, it is similar.

For the case
C=(ax^{m}(1+o(1))-y^{n}(1+o(1))=0)
, we may assume, for simplicity, that
C=(f=ax^{m}-y^{n}=0)
and
\omega=my\textnormal{d}x-nx\textnormal{d}y
.
Note that

\displaystyle ax^{m-1}\omega

\displaystyle=amx^{m-1}y\textnormal{d}x-anx^{m}\textnormal{d}y

\displaystyle=amx^{m-1}y\textnormal{d}x-ny^{n}\textnormal{d}y-nf\textnormal{d}y

\displaystyle=y\textnormal{d}f-nf\textnormal{d}y.

So we take
g=ax^{m-1}
,
h=y
, and
\eta=-n\textnormal{d}y
.
Thus,

\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=\textnormal{res}_{p}\frac{-1}{h}\eta|_{f=0}=\textnormal{res}_{p}\,\frac{1}{y}n\textnormal{d}y|_{f=0}=mn

and

\displaystyle\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{vanishing order of }\frac{h}{g}|_{f=0}

\displaystyle=\textnormal{vanishing order of }\frac{y}{ax^{m-1}}|_{f=0}

\displaystyle=1-(n-1)(m-1).

∎

-canonical singularities

From Example

, we know that there is no Seidenberg theorem for a family.
Nevertheless, in
[
PS19
]
, we can still have a Seidenberg-type theorem for a family by allowing

Definition 1.25

(
[
PS19
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface with

smooth and let
\pi:Y\longrightarrow X

Denote
{\mathscr{G}}
be the pullback foliation of
{\mathscr{F}}
via
\pi
.
If
E
is a prime exceptional divisor of
\pi
, then we define the
adjoint discrepancy
of
{\mathscr{F}}
along

to be the function

a({\mathscr{F}},E):[0,\infty)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

which sends

to
\mbox{\rm ord}_{E}\big((1-t)(K_{\mathscr{G}}-\pi^{*}K_{\mathscr{F}})+t(K_{Y}-\pi^{*}K_{X})\big)

(
[
PS19
, Definition 4.2]
)
.

A point

with

\varepsilon
-canonical if the adjoint discrepancy of
{\mathscr{F}}
along any divisor

over

.

Lemma 1.27

(
[
PS19
, Lemma 7.3]
)
.

Let
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{F}})
be a smooth family over an algebraic variety

such that all fibers are smooth projective surfaces.
If

, then the subset of
T
corresponding to foliations with isolated and
\varepsilon
-canonical singularities is a Zariski open subset of

.

Proposition 1.28

(
[
PS19
, Proposition 7.4]
)
.

Given a smooth family
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{F}})
over
T
such that all fibers are smooth projective surfaces and a real number

with

.
Then there exists a non-empty Zariski open subset
U\subset T
and a family
({\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{G}})
over
U
obtained from
{\mathcal{F}}|_{U}
by a finite composition of blowups over (multi)-sections such that, for all

, the foliation
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}

In particular,

.

In this section, we recall some properties of Quot schemes.

Definition 1.29
.

Let
X\longrightarrow S
be a scheme of finite type over a noetherian base scheme
S
and
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent sheaf on

{\mathcal{Q}}uot_{{\mathcal{E}}/X/S}(T)=\left\{({\mathcal{F}},q)\Big|\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathcal{F}}\in\textnormal{Coh}(X_{T}),\mbox{{\rm Supp}}{{\mathcal{F}}}\textnormal{ is proper over }T\\

where

under the projection

, and

.

.

Let

S

be a relatively ample line bundle.
For any coherent sheaf

.

.

Fix any relatively ample line bundle
{\mathcal{A}}
on

There is a natural stratification:

{\mathcal{Q}}uot_{{\mathcal{E}}/X/S}=\coprod_{\Phi\in{\mathbb{Q}}[\lambda]}{\mathcal{Q}}uot_{{\mathcal{E}}/X/S}^{\Phi,{\mathcal{A}}}

.

2.
Log canonical models

In light of the Example
0.1
, we would like to allow some mild singularities in order to get local-closedness in a family.
We introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A foliated normal surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
is called a
log canonical model
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

{\mathscr{F}}
has
mild
log canonical foliation singularities, that is,
{\mathscr{F}}
is a foliation with only log canonical foliation singularities such that all points at which
{\mathscr{F}}
is
not
canonical are either smooth points of
X
or cyclic quotient singularities of
X
.

(2)

K_{\mathscr{F}}
is nef.

(3)

K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot C=0
implies

for any irreducible curve
C
.

In Lemma
2.7
and Remark

, we study more properties of mild log canonical foliation singularities.
Actually, there are log canonical foliation singularities which are not mild.

Example 2.2

(

)

We consider the germ of the normal surface
(x^{n+2}+y^{n+1}+xz^{n}=0)

It is not a rational singularity but the foliation given by

Definition 2.3
.

We say a foliated normal surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
is a

if it is a log canonical model with only canonical foliation singularities.

Let

be a log canonical model.

is numerically ample, that is

The proof follows closely from the proof of

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}},p)
be a germ of a foliated surface with
p\in X
a smooth point.
Suppose
{\mathscr{F}}
at
p
has the positive rational eigenvalue.
Then there is a sequence of blowups
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}},p)
with
{\mathscr{G}}
having only reduced singularities on the exceptional divisor
E
.
Moreover we have the following:

(1)

The support of the exceptional divisor
\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}E_{i}
is a string,

(2)

All
E_{i}
’s except for one
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
are invariant; while this

is non-invariant with
\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{G}},E_{j})=0

(3)

The string
\bigcup_{i=1}^{j-1}E_{i}
(resp.
\bigcup_{i=j+1}^{s}E_{i}

{\mathscr{G}}
-chain with the first curve

(resp.

(4)

\pi^{*}K_{\mathscr{F}}=K_{\mathscr{G}}+E_{j}

Proof
.

Since the eigenvalue is defined up to reciprocal, we may assume that the eigenvalue is
\frac{m}{n}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
with
\textnormal{gcd}(m,n)=1
and
m\geq n
.
We proceed by induction on the pair
(m,n)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
(by lexicographic ordering).

(1)

We may assume that
p=(0,0)
and that
{\mathscr{F}}
is generated by

around
p
.
Let
\varphi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be the blowup at

and
E_{0}
be the exceptional divisor.
Notice that there are two singularities on the exceptional curve locally generated by

v_{1}=nx^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\prime}}+(m-n)y^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\prime}}

3.1.

From now on, we assume that the foliation

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Rei87
]
,
[
Lan00
, Section 3]
)
.

Let

at

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)
at

.

If

has only quotient singularities, then the theorem follows from
[

]
.
In general, the theorem follows from
[
Lan00
, Section 3]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
m
be a non-negative integer.

(1)

If
D
is Cartier at
x
, then
a(x,D)=0
.

(2)

If
(X,{\mathscr{F}},x)
is a mild log canonical non-canonical foliation singularity, then

a(x,mK_{\mathscr{F}})=0\mbox{ for all }m.

(3)

If
(X,{\mathscr{F}},x)
is a canonical foliation singularity, which is

-Gorentein but it is not terminal.
Then we have two cases:

(a)

{\mathscr{F}}
is Gorenstein and

(b)

-Gorenstein and

\frac{-1}{2}&\mbox{ for $m$ odd.}\end{array}\right.

(4)

(X,{\mathscr{F}},x)
is a canonical foliation singularity and

is not

-Gorenstein at

Remark 3.3

Proposition 3.4
.

Fix a function

.
There exist constants

,
B_{2}
,
B_{3}
, and

.

The proof follows closely from the proof of
[
HL21
, Proposition 4.1]

For the reader’s convenience, we include the proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we have

P(m)=\chi(mK_{\mathscr{F}})=\frac{1}{2}mK_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot(mK_{\mathscr{F}}-K_{X})+\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})+\sum_{x\in\textnormal{Sing}(X)}a(x,mK_{\mathscr{F}})

Since

is the quadratic term of

and
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot K_{X}
is the linear term of
P
, both of them are fixed.
Also
\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})=P(0)
is also fixed.
If we put
m=1
, then we get the number

is fixed.

Let

be the set of terminal foliation singularities,
\Sigma_{2}
be the set of dihedral quotient singularities, and
\Sigma_{3}
be the set of cusp singularities.
Also let

Then by Proposition

Finally, let

Hence, the number of cusps is fixed.

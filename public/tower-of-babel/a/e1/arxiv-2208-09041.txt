Polarization tensor in de Sitter gauge gravity
R. Raziani, M.V. Takook

Abstract
. The gauge theory of the de Sitter group, SO ( 1 , 4 ) (1,4) , in the ambient space formalism has been considered in this article. This method is essential to constructing the de Sitter super-conformal gravity and Quantum gravity. 10 10 gauge vector fields are needed, corresponding to 10 10 generators of the de Sitter group. Using the gauge-invariant Lagrangian, the field equation of these vector fields has been obtained. The gauge vector field solutions are recalled. Then, the spin- 2 2 gauge potentials are constructed from the gauge vector field. There are two possibilities for presenting this tensor field: rank- 2 2 symmetric and mixed symmetry rank- 3 3 tensor fields. To preserve the conformal transformation, a spin- 2 2 field must be represented by a mixed symmetry rank- 3 3 tensor field, 𝒦 α ​ β ​ γ \mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta\gamma} . This tensor field has been rewritten using a generalized polarization tensor field and a de Sitter plane wave. This generalized polarization tensor field has been calculated as a combination of vector polarization, ℰ α \mathcal{E}_{\alpha} , and tensor polarization of rank- 2 2 , ℰ α ​ β \mathcal{E}_{\alpha\beta} , which can be used in the gravitational wave consideration. For the construction of this polarization tensor, the arbitrary constant vector fields appear. We fix it so that, in the limit, H = 0 H=0 , one obtains the polarization tensor in Minkowski space-time. It has been shown that under some simple conditions, the spin- 2 2 mixed symmetry rank- 3 3 tensor field can be simultaneously transformed by the unitary irreducible representation of de Sitter and conformal groups, SO ( 2 , 4 ) (2,4) .

2.
de Sitter ambient space formalism

The dS space-time is a maximally symmetric solution of the vacuum Einstein equations with a positive cosmological constant
\Lambda
. It can be viewed as a one-sheeted hyperboloid embedded in a five-dimensional Minkowski space-time:

(2.1)

M_{H}=\left\{x\in\mathrm{I\hskip-1.49994ptR}^{5}|\;\;x\cdot x=\eta_{\alpha\beta}x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=-H^{-2}\right\},

where,
\eta_{\alpha\beta}=
diag
(1,-1,-1,-1,-1)
,
\alpha,\beta=0,1,2,3,4,

and
H
is the Hubble constant parameter. Hereafter, for simplicity, we set
H=1
. The dS metric is

(2.2)

ds^{2}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}dx^{\alpha}dx^{\beta}|_{x\cdot x=-1}=g_{\mu\nu}^{dS}dX^{\mu}dX^{\nu},\;\;\mu=0,1,2,3,

where
x^{\alpha}
stands for ambient space formalism, and
X^{\mu}
is used
for dS intrinsic coordinates. We use the ambient space formalism for ease of manipulation, which is very similar to Minkowski space. The main points of this formalism can be listed as follows:

•

(A) The projection operator from
5
-dimensional Minkowski space to
4
-dimensional dS hyperboloid is defined as:

(2.3)

\theta_{\alpha\beta}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}+x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}.

•

(B) The transverse-covariant derivative acting on a tensor field of rank-
l
is defined by:

(2.4)

\nabla^{\top}_{\beta}T_{\alpha_{1}....\alpha_{l}}\equiv\partial^{\top}_{\beta}T_{\alpha_{1}....\alpha_{l}}-\sum_{n=1}^{l}x_{\alpha_{n}}T_{\alpha_{1}..\alpha_{n-1}\beta\alpha_{n+1}..\alpha_{l}},

where
\partial^{\top}_{\beta}=\theta_{\beta}^{\;\;\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}=\partial_{\beta}+H^{2}x_{\alpha}x\cdot\partial\,,\;\;x\cdot\partial^{\top}=0
.

•

(C) It is worth to mention that:
x\cdot\nabla^{\top}=0
.

The dS kinematical group is the
10
-parameters group SO
(1,4)
. There are two Casimir operators for this group
[
15
,
16
]
, the second-order Casimir operator is:

(2.5)

Q^{(1)}=-\frac{1}{2}L_{\alpha\beta}L^{\alpha\beta},\;\;\alpha,\beta=0,1,2,3,4,

and the fourth-order Casimir operator is:

(2.6)

Q^{(2)}=-W_{\alpha}W^{\alpha}\;\;,\;\;W_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{8}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\eta}L^{\beta\gamma}L^{\delta\eta},

where
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\eta}
is the antisymmetric tensor in
5
-dimensional Minkowski space.

L_{\alpha\beta}
are the
infinitesimal generators of dS group,
L_{\alpha\beta}=M_{\alpha\beta}+S_{\alpha\beta}
. The orbital part
M_{\alpha\beta}
, is defined as follows:

(2.7)

M_{\alpha\beta}=-i(x_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}-x_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha})=-i(x_{\alpha}\partial^{\top}_{\beta}-x_{\beta}\partial^{\top}_{\alpha}).

The spinorial
part,
S_{\alpha\beta}
, with an integer spin
s=l=0,1,\cdots
, is read as
[
17
,
18
]
:

(2.8)

S_{\alpha\beta}^{(l)}\mathcal{K}_{\gamma_{1}......\gamma_{l}}=-i\sum^{l}_{i=1}\left(\eta_{\alpha\gamma_{i}}\mathcal{K}_{\gamma_{1}....(\gamma_{i}\rightarrow\beta)....\gamma_{l}}-\eta_{\beta\gamma_{i}}\mathcal{K}_{\gamma_{1}....(\gamma_{i}\rightarrow\alpha)....\gamma_{l}}\right),

where
(\gamma_{i}\rightarrow\beta)
means
\gamma_{i}
is replaced
by
\beta
.

3.
Gauge-invariant action

The local or gauge symmetry is a fundamental basis for explaining the electromagnetic, weak, and strong nuclear interactions by the gauge vector fields in Minkowski space-time. Here, we construct the gauge theory in dS ambient space formalism for the dS group SO
(1,4)
.
The notation is changed for simplicity, and the dS group generators are defined as follows:

L_{\alpha\beta}=M_{\alpha\beta}+S_{\alpha\beta}\equiv X_{A},\;\;A=1,2,...,10.

In this case, the commutation relation can be reformulated as:

[X_{A},X_{B}]=f_{BA}^{\;\;\;\;\;C}X_{C},

where
f_{BA}^{\;\;\;\;\;C}
is the Lie algebra structure constant, which is explicitly presented in
[
13
]
. We have
10
gauge vector fields
K_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}
. As these gauge vector fields exist on the dS
hyperboloid, they should be transverse:
x\cdot K^{A}\equiv x^{\alpha}K_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}=0
.
From the gauge theory point of view, the transverse gauge-covariant derivative in the ambient space notation can be defined as:

(3.1)

D^{K}_{\alpha}=\nabla^{\top}_{\alpha}+iK_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}X_{A},

where
\nabla^{\top}_{\alpha}
is defined in equation (
2.4
).
There exist
40
degrees of freedom for these gauge vector
fields. So, they can be recognized as the connection coefficients in the general
relativity framework. The construction of the gauge theory in its canonical manner can be reproduced. Under a local infinitesimal gauge transformation generated by
\epsilon^{A}(x)X_{A}
, one can obtain the following gauge transformation:

(3.2)

\delta_{\epsilon}{K}_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}=D^{K}_{\alpha}\epsilon^{A}=\nabla^{\top}_{\alpha}\epsilon^{A}+f_{CB}^{\;\;\;\;A}{K}_{\alpha}^{\;\;C}\epsilon^{B}.

The curvature
\mathcal{R}
, with values in the Lie algebra of the dS group,
is defined by:

\mathcal{R}(D^{K}_{\alpha},D^{K}_{\beta})=-[D^{K}_{\alpha},D^{K}_{\beta}]=R_{\alpha\beta}^{\;\;\;\;A}X_{A},

where

(3.3)

R_{\alpha\beta}^{\;\;\;\;A}=\nabla^{\top}_{\alpha}K_{\beta}^{\;\;A}-\nabla^{\top}_{\beta}K_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}+K_{\beta}^{\;\;B}K_{\alpha}^{\;\;C}f_{BC}^{\;\;\;\;A}.

In the ambient space formalism, the gauge-invariant action or Lagrangian density for this curvature can be defined as
[
14
,
19
,
20
]
:

(3.4)

S[K]=\int d\mu(x)\left(R_{\alpha\beta}^{\;\;\;\;\;A}q_{AB}R^{\alpha\beta B}\right),

where
d\mu(x)
is dS invariant volume element and
q_{AB}
is a numerical constant.

4.
Gauge fields

Using the Euler-Lagrange equation for (
3.4
), the field equation can be obtained as (see appendix A):

(4.1)

-Q_{0}K_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}+2K_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}-x_{\alpha}\partial^{\top}\cdot K^{\;A}-\partial^{\top}_{\alpha}\partial^{\top}\cdot K^{\;A}=0,

where

Q_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}M_{\alpha\beta}M^{\alpha\beta}=-\partial^{\top}\cdot\partial^{\top}.

If these vector fields are desired to propagate on the dS hyperboloid and transform as a UIR of the dS group, they must satisfy the transversality and the divergencelessness conditions
[
7
]
,

x\cdot K^{\;A}=0,\;\;\partial^{\top}\cdot K^{\;A}=0,

then we have:

(4.2)

(Q_{0}-2)K_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}=0.

The four generators
L_{4\mu},(\mu=0,1,2,3)
become the translation generators
P^{\mu}
in the flat space-time limit, and the gauge fields
E_{\alpha}^{\mu}
, which correspond to them, play the role of the linear gravitational wave. The six generators
L_{\mu\nu}
in the null curvature limit are related to the Lorentz group generators, SO
(1,3)

W_{\alpha}^{\mu\nu}
correspond to the generators
L_{\mu\nu}

(4.3)

D^{K}_{\alpha}=\nabla^{\top}_{\alpha}+i{K}_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}X_{A}\equiv\nabla^{\top}_{\alpha}+i\left(E_{\alpha}^{\;\;\mu}L_{4\mu}+W_{\alpha}^{\mu\nu}L_{\mu\nu}\right),\;\;{K}_{\alpha}^{\;\;A}\equiv(E_{\alpha}^{\;\;\mu},W_{\alpha}^{\mu\nu}).

The solution of the field equation (
4.2
) is recalled in the following subsection, and then the tensor fields of different ranks are presented.

5.
conclusion

The gauge principle governs the interactions between the elementary systems in the universe. These interactions are formulated through the gauge-covariant derivative. They are defined as the quantities that preserve the gauge-invariant transformation of the Lagrangian. In this paper, the spin-
2
gauge field has been presented from the gauge vector fields of the dS group in the line of the Yang-Mills gauge theory. It is important to note that the rank-
2
symmetric tensor field is written regarding a combination of the vector gauge fields. Unfortunately, the usual rank-
2
symmetric tensor field breaks the conformal transformation, although it can lead to the super-gravity gauge theory in dS universe
[
29
]
. To preserve the conformal invariant, a mixed symmetry rank-
3
gauge field must be used. The mixed symmetry rank-
3
tensor field can be written in rank-
2
symmetric tensor and vector gauge fields. These results help us to formulate the super-conformal gravity in the dS ambient space formalism
[
30
]
.

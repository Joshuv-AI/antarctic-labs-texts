Abstract

A Hermitian form ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) (\cdot,\cdot) on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is called invariant if ( Z ​ a , b ) = ( a , b ​ Z − 1 ) (Za,b)=\big(a,bZ^{-1}\big) , ( u ​ a , b ) = ( a , s ​ b ​ v ) (ua,b)=(a,sbv) , ( v ​ a , b ) = ( a , s − 1 ​ b ​ u ) (va,b)=\big(a,s^{-1}bu\big) for some s ∈ ℂ s\in\mathbb{C} with | s | = 1 |s|=1 and all a , b ∈ 𝒜 a,b\in\mathcal{A} . In this paper we classify positive definite invariant Hermitian forms on generalized q q -Weyl algebras.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra over
\mathbb{C}
,
g
be an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
. We say that a linear map
T\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
g
-twisted trace if
T(ab)=T(bg(a))
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
\rho
be an antilinear automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
. We say that a linear map
T\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is a positive trace if
(a,b)_{T}=T(a\rho(b))
is a positive definite Hermitian form on
\mathcal{A}
. The hermitian condition implies that
T
is indeed a
\rho^{2}
-twisted trace.

The classification of positive traces on quantizations is an important and interesting question. For example, let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex simple Lie algebra,
G
be the corresponding simply connected group. For central quotients of
U(\mathfrak{g})
this question is equivalent to the classification of spherical unitarizable
G
-modules of principle series.

Let
C_{n}
be a cyclic subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,\mathbb{C})
that consist of matrices
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\
0&\varepsilon^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
such that
\varepsilon^{n}=1
. It naturally acts on a polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
. We can consider the subalgebra of invariant polynomials
\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{C_{n}}
, the associated algebraic variety is called a Kleinian singularity of type
A
. Positive traces on quantizations of Kleinian singularities of type
A
were studied in
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
8
]
. The paper
[
6
]
gives a complete classification of positive traces.

We will consider the natural
q
-analogue of that situation and study positive traces on
q
-deformations of Kleinian singularities of type
A
. The algebra
\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{C_{n}}
admits only one Poisson bracket up to a scaling, so we use terms “deformation” and “quantization” interchangeably.

The
q
-analogue of a deformation of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{C_{n}}
is the following algebra
\mathcal{A}
. It is generated by
u
,
v
,
Z
,
Z^{-1}
with relations
ZuZ^{-1}=q^{2}u
,
ZvZ^{-1}=q^{-2}v
,
uv=P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)
,
vu=P(qZ)
, where
P
is a Laurent polynomial. These algebras are also called generalized
q
-Weyl algebras
[
1
]
: when
P(z)=1
,
\mathcal{A}
is a
q
-Weyl algebra.

When
P(z)=az^{-1}+b+cz
, the algebra
\mathcal{A}
is a central reduction of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. We expect that there exists a connection between our results and the classification of unitarizable Harish-Chandra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-bimodules
[
11
]
.

Positive traces for deformations of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{C_{n}}
appear in 3-dimensional superconformal field theories
[
2
]
. Positive traces for
q
-deformations of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{C_{n}}
appear in the study of the Coulomb branch of 4-dimensional superconformal field theories
[
4
]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we prove analytic formulas for twisted traces on
q
-deformations similar to those in
[
6
, Section 3]
. The main difference is that now
T(R)=\int_{0}^{1}R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w(x)\,{\rm d}x
plus some additional terms, where
w
is quasiperiodic in two directions:
w(x+1)=w(x)
,
w(x+\tau)=tw(x)
. Here
q={\rm e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}
.
After that we classify positive traces. The conjugation
\rho
that we use is defined only for real
q
. Since
q\neq 1
and
q
,
-q
,
q^{-1}
all give isomorphic algebras we assume that
0<q<1
in Sections
3
and
4
.
Positive traces naturally form a convex cone, so we will often use the sentence “the cone of positive traces” instead of “the set of positive traces”. When we say that it is isomorphic to another cone we mean that there is a bijection that preserves addition of elements and multiplication by a positive real number.

In Section
3
we classify positive traces in the case when all roots
\alpha
of
P
satisfy
q<|\alpha|<q^{-1}
. The answer is a cone of quasiperiodic functions that are positive on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfy a certain condition on
\mathbb{R}+\frac{\tau}{2}
. More precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem
3.7
.

The cone
C
of positive
g_{t}
-twisted traces has dimension
n
. It is isomorphic to the cone of entire functions
f
such that

f(x+1)=f(x),\qquad f(x+\tau)={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}(2nx+n\tau-2\sum\beta_{j}-2c)}f(x)

and

f(x),\qquad{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}(-nx+\sum\beta_{j}+m_{0}+c)}f\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)

are nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
. At the level of sets
C
consists of functions

\lambda\frac{\vartheta(z-a_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-a_{n})}{\vartheta(z-\beta_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-\beta_{n})},

where
\lambda>0
,
\sum a_{i}-\sum\beta_{i}-c-m_{0}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and all
a_{i}
are divided into pairs
(a_{i},a_{j})
with
a_{i}=\overline{a_{j}}
. In particular, if
Q
is another Laurent polynomial with the same number of nonzero roots
n
counted with multiplicities,
s
is a nonzero complex number with
\lvert s\rvert=1
, cones
C_{P,t}
and
C_{Q,s}
are isomorphic.

Here
c
is a number such that
t={\rm e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}c}
,
m_{0}
is either
0
or
1
depending on the behavior of
P
on
S^{1}
. The main difference with
[
6
]
is that the dimension of the cone always equals to the number of roots of
P
counted with multiplicities.

In Section
4
we classify positive traces in the general case. The reasoning in this section is very similar to the reasoning in
[
6
, Sections 4.3–4.4]
with some simplifications because we integrate over the compact set
S^{1}
instead of
\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
. We get the following answer.

Theorem
4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
q
-deformation with parameter
P
. Let
t\in S^{1}
,
\rho_{t}
be the corresponding conjugation. Let
l
be the number of roots
\alpha
of
P
such that
q<|\alpha|<q^{-1}
,
r
be the number of distinct roots
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=q
. Then the cone
\mathcal{C}_{+}
of positive definite
\rho_{t}
-equivariant traces is isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{l}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{l}
consists of nonzero entire functions
f
such that

(1)

f(x+1)=f(x)
,
f(x+\tau)={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}n(\tau+2x)}f(x)
,

(2)

f(x)
and
{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}nx}f\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)
are nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
.

In Section
5
we assume that
P
belongs to
\mathbb{C}[z]
. In this case we can consider the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{+}
generated by
u
,
v
,
Z
. Algebra
\mathcal{A}_{+}
is filtered and
A_{+}=\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits\mathcal{A}_{+}
depends only on the degree
m
of
P
. Trace
T
on a filtered algebra
\mathcal{B}
is called nondegenerate if bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)\colon\mathcal{B}_{\leq k}\times\mathcal{B}_{\leq k}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
(a,b)=T(ab)
is nondegenerate for all positive integers
k
. Motivation for considering nondegenerate traces comes from paper
[
7
]
that connects nondegenerate traces on
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and short star-products on
A_{+}
. We obtain the following result:

Theorem
5.6
.

Let
\lvert q\rvert\neq 1
. Then we can find a countable subset
Z
of
\mathbb{C}
containing
1,q^{-2},\allowbreak q^{-4},\dots
with the following property. For any
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus Z
there exists a countable union of algebraic hypersurfaces
X
in
\mathbb{C}^{2n+1}
such that for any
(c_{0},\dots,c_{n},t_{0},\dots,t_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{2n+1}\setminus X
the
g_{t}
-twisted trace
T
given by
T(Z^{i})=t_{i}
on the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{+}
with parameter
P(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}x^{i}
is nondegenerate.

In Section
6
we study in more details the central reductions of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

2
An analytic construction of traces

2.1
Preliminaries

Let
q
be a complex number,
P
be a Laurent polynomial. Then
\mathcal{A}
is an algebra generated by
u
,
v
,
Z
with relations
ZuZ^{-1}=q^{2}u
,
ZvZ^{-1}=q^{-2}v
,
uv=P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)
,
vu=P(qZ)
. We assume that
\lvert q\rvert\neq 1
. Note that
q
and
q^{-1}
give isomorphic algebras: the isomorphism interchanges
u
and
v
and sends
Z
to itself. So we will assume that
\lvert q\rvert<1
.
Sometimes we will write “polynomial” instead of “Laurent polynomial”.

Let
n
be the number of nonzero roots of
P
counted with multiplicities. In other words, for
P=ax^{k}+\cdots+bx^{l}
with
a
,
b
nonzero and
k\geq l
we have
n=k-l
. We will assume that
n>0
.

We have

\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}_{i},

ZaZ^{-1}=q^{2i}a
for
a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
. The linear subspace
\mathcal{A}_{i}
equals to
u^{i}\mathbb{C}\big[Z,Z^{-1}\big]
for
i\geq 0
and
v^{-i}\mathbb{C}\big[Z,Z^{-1}\big]
for
i\leq 0
.

Let
t
be a nonzero complex number. There exists an automorphism
g_{t}
of
\mathcal{A}
such that
g_{t}(u)=tu
,
g_{t}(v)=t^{-1}v
,
g_{t}(Z)=Z
. A linear map
T\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
g_{t}
-twisted trace if
T(ab)=T(bg_{t}(a))
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

T
is a
g_{t}
-twisted trace if and only if
T
is supported on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and

T\big(P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)R\big(q^{-1}Z\big)-tP(qZ)R(qZ)\big)=0

for all
R\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
. The space of
g_{t}
-twisted traces has dimension
n
.

Proof.

It is enough to check that
T(ab)=T(bg_{t}(a))
when
a=u,v,Z
.
The condition
T(Zb)=T(bZ)
is equivalent to
T
being supported on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. So we have to check that
T(ub)=tT(bu)
for
b=vR\big(q^{-1}Z\big)
. For this
b
we have

\displaystyle ub=uvR\big(q^{-1}Z\big)=P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)R\big(q^{-1}Z\big),

\displaystyle bu=vR\big(q^{-1}Z\big)u=vuR(qZ)=P(qZ)R(qZ).

This gives

\big(P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)R\big(q^{-1}Z\big)-tP(qZ)R(qZ)\big)=0.

We also have to check that
T(vb)=t^{-1}T(bv)
for
b=uR(qZ)
. For this
b
we have

\displaystyle vb=vuR(qZ)=P(qZ)R(qZ),

\displaystyle bv=uR(qZ)v=uvR\big(q^{-1}Z\big)=P\big(q^{-1}ZR(qZ)\big).

This gives

T\big(t^{-1}P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)R\big(q^{-1}Z\big)-P(qZ)R(qZ)\big)=0.

Hence we can view
g_{t}
-twisted traces as functions

T\colon\ \mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\to\mathbb{C}

satisfying

T\big(P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tP(qz)R(qz)\big)=0.

We turn to the statement about dimension. Consider the map
\phi\colon\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\to\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
given by
\phi(S(z))=S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)
. We have
\phi\big(z^{k}\big)=q^{-k}\big(1-tq^{2k}\big)z^{k}
.

In the case when
t
is not an integer power of
q^{2}
the map
\phi
is a linear isomorphism. Hence the codimension of
\phi\big(P(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\big)
in
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
equals to the codimension of
P(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
in
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
which is equal to
n
.

In the case when
t=q^{-2k}
map
\phi
has a kernel
z^{k}
, the image of
\phi
does not contain
z^{k}
and has codimension
1
in
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
. Since
P
has nonzero roots
z^{k}
does not belong to
P(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
and the set
z^{k}+P(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
has codimension
n-1
in
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
. It follows that
\phi\big(P(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\big)
has codimension
n-1
in the image of
\phi
. We deduce that
\phi\big(P(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\big)
has codimension
n
in
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
.

In both cases we get that
\phi(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
has codimension
n
in
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
. The only condition on
T
is that
T
equals to zero on
\phi(P(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
, hence the space of such maps has dimension
n
.
∎

We also note that the set
\big\{P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tP(qz)R(qz)\mid R(z)\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\big\}
does not change when we multiply
P
by a nonzero complex number or an integer power of
z
, hence the set of
g_{t}
-twisted traces will be the same

2.2
The case when all roots of
\boldsymbol{P}
satisfy
\boldsymbol{\lvert q\rvert<\lvert z\rvert<\lvert q\rvert^{-1}}

Choose
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
such that
q=\exp(\pi{\rm i}\tau)
. Let

\vartheta(x)=\vartheta(x;\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{n^{2}}{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}xn}

be the Jacobi theta function. Let
U=\big\{\lvert q\rvert<\lvert z\rvert<\lvert q\rvert^{-1}\big\}
,
V=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\ln U
,
D
be a fundamental region of
\vartheta(x)
,
D_{0}
its interior,
\overline{D}
its closure. We choose
D
so that
\overline{D}
is the parallelogram on vertices
-\frac{\tau}{2}
,
\frac{\tau}{2}
,
\frac{\tau}{2}+1
,
1-\frac{\tau}{2}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
w
is a function such that

\displaystyle w(x)=w(x+1),

\displaystyle w(x+\tau)=tw(x),

and
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
is holomorphic when
x\in\overline{V}
. Then

T(R(z))=\int_{0}^{1}w(x)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x

is a
g_{t}
-twisted trace. Moreover, if all roots of
P(x)
belong to
U
then any twisted trace can be obtained in this way.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle T\big(P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tP(qz)R(qz)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{0}^{1}w(x)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\left(x-\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\left(x-\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\big)\,{\rm d}x-t\int_{0}^{1}w(x)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{-\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1-\frac{\tau}{2}}w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x-t\int_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1+\frac{\tau}{2}}w\bigg(x-\frac{\tau}{2}\bigg)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x.

Recall that
\partial D
is the parallelogram with vertices
-\frac{\tau}{2}
,
\frac{\tau}{2}
,
1+\frac{\tau}{2}
,
1-\frac{\tau}{2}
. We can continue as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1-\frac{\tau}{2}}w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x-t\int_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1+\frac{\tau}{2}}w\bigg(x-\frac{\tau}{2}\bigg)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{-\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1-\frac{\tau}{2}}w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1+\frac{\tau}{2}}w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{\partial D}w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}-\int_{1-\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1+\frac{\tau}{2}}w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\partial D}\!w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\,{\rm d}x=0.

We used that
R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is
1
-periodic and
P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is holomorphic on
\overline{D}
.

Suppose that all roots of
P(x)
belong to
U
. It remains to prove that any trace
T
can be obtained from a function
w
as above. Since different
w
give different traces it is enough to prove that the space of functions
w
satisfying the conditions of the theorem has dimension
n
.
All roots of
P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
belong to the strip
V
. Therefore there are
n
roots of
P({\rm e}^{{\rm i}x})
in
D_{0}
. Denote these roots by
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
. Let
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}
.
Recall that
\vartheta(z)
has zeroes at points
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}+\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
and
\vartheta(z+\tau)={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}(\tau+2z)}\vartheta(z)
. For the proof of this fact see, for example,
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Let
t={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}c}
. We get the result from the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
S
be the set of functions
w
that satisfy

w=\lambda\frac{\vartheta(z-a_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-a_{n})}{\vartheta(z-\beta_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-\beta_{n})},

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\sum a_{i}-\sum\beta_{i}\in c+\mathbb{Z}
. Then any
w\in S
satisfies conditions of Theorem
2.2
and
S
is a linear space of dimension
n
.

Proof.

Let

w(z)=\lambda\frac{\vartheta(z-a_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-a_{n})}{\vartheta(z-\beta_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-\beta_{n})}

be an element of
S
. This function satisfies
w(x+1)=w(x)
and

\displaystyle w(x+\tau)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{n}{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}(\tau+2z-2a_{k})}{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}(\tau+2z-2\beta_{k})}w(x)={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}(\sum a_{k}-\sum\beta_{k})}w(x)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}c}w(x)=tw(x).

Since
\vartheta
has zeros at points
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}+\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
,
w(z+\frac{\tau}{2})
has poles at points
\alpha_{i}+\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
. It follows that
P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is holomorphic on
V
.

Multiplication by
\frac{\vartheta(z-\beta_{1})}{\vartheta(z-\beta_{1}-c)}
gives a bijection between
S
and the space of elliptic functions with poles in
\alpha_{1}+c,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}
. It follows that
S
is a linear space of dimension
n
.
∎

2.3
General case

We start with the case when all roots
\alpha
of
P
satisfy
\lvert q\rvert\leq|\alpha|\leq\frac{1}{\lvert q\rvert}
. We will mainly use analytic formulas for traces in the case when
P(z)=\overline{P}\big(z^{-1}\big)
. So we assume that roots of
P
on
qS^{1}\cup q^{-1}S^{1}
are symmetric with respect to the map
z\mapsto\overline{z^{-1}}
. In this case we can find polynomials
P_{*}
,
Q
such that
P(x)=P_{*}(x)Q(qx)\overline{Q}\big(qx^{-1}\big)
, all nonzero roots of
P_{*}
belong to
U
and all roots of
Q
belong to
S^{1}
.

Let us choose a linear subspace
S
of
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
such that the composition
S\subset\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\to\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]/(Q)
is an isomorphism. For any
R\in\mathbb{C}\big[x,x^{-1}\big]
we define
R_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
,
R_{0}\in S
by
R(x)=R_{1}Q(x)+R_{0}(x)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Any
g_{t}
-twisted trace
T
on
\mathcal{A}
can be written as

T(R)=\int_{0}^{1}R_{1}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)Q\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w(x)\,{\rm d}x+l(R_{0}),

where
R_{0}
,
R_{1}
are defined above,
w
is a meromorphic function such that
w(x+1)=w(x)
,
w(x+\tau)=tw(x)
,
Q\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w(x)
has no poles on
[0,1]
,
P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
has no poles
V
, and
l
is an arbitrary linear functional on
S
.

Proof.

For
z\in S^{1}
we have
\overline{Q(z)}=\overline{Q}\big(z^{-1}\big)
. Since all roots of
Q
belong to
S^{1}
it follows that
\overline{Q}\big(z^{-1}\big)
has the same roots as
Q(z)
with the same multiplicities. Therefore
Q(z)
divides
\overline{Q}\big(z^{-1}\big)
.

First we prove that any pair of
w
and
l
gives a trace. We have to show that

T\big(R\big(q^{-1}z\big)P\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tR(qz)P(qz)\big)=0

for any
R\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
. We note that
Q(z)
divides

P\big(q^{-1}z\big)=P_{*}\big(q^{-1}z\big)Q(z)\overline{Q}\big(q^{2}z\big)

and

P(qz)=P_{*}(qz)Q\big(q^{2}z\big)\overline{Q}\big(z^{-1}\big).

It follows that

\displaystyle T\big(R\big(q^{-1}z\big)P\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tR(qz)P(qz)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{0}^{1}T\big(R\big(q^{-1}{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)P\big(q^{-1}{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)-tR\big(q{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)P\big(q{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\big)w(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
2.2
we deduce that this is zero.

It is easy to see that different pairs of
w
and
l
give different traces. Now we compute the dimension of the space of pairs. For convenience we denote by
\deg R
the number of nonzero roots of a Laurent polynomial
R
counted with multiplicities. The space of functionals
l
has dimension
\deg Q
. The space of functions
w
has dimension
\deg Q+\deg P_{*}
because we allow poles at roots of
Q\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
and shifted roots of
P_{*}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
. So the overall dimension is
\deg P_{*}+2\deg Q=\deg P=n
as we wanted.
∎

Now we turn to the general case.
Let
z
be a complex number. If
\lvert z\rvert>\frac{1}{\lvert q\rvert}
we find a minimal positive integer
k
such that
\big\lvert q^{2k}z\big\rvert\leq\frac{1}{\lvert q\rvert}
and denote
q^{2k}z
by
\widetilde{z}
. If
\lvert z\rvert<\lvert q\rvert
we similarly find the smallest
k
such that
\big|q^{-2k}z\big|\geq\lvert q\rvert
and denote
q^{-2k}z
by
\widetilde{z}
.

Suppose that
P
has roots
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k+1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
, where
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
belong to
\overline{U}=\big\{\lvert q\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert\leq\frac{1}{\lvert q\rvert}\big\}
and
\alpha_{k+1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
do not belong to
\overline{U}
. We are counting roots with multiplicities, so some of
\alpha_{i}
may be equal to each other. Let
\widetilde{P}
be a polynomial with roots
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k},\widetilde{\alpha_{k+1}},\dots,\widetilde{\alpha_{n}}
,
P_{\circ}
be a polynomial with roots
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Any trace
T
can be represented as
T=\widetilde{T}+\Phi
, where
\Phi
is a linear functional of the form

\Phi(R)=\sum_{a\notin S^{1},k\geq 0}c_{ak}R^{(k)}(a)

and
\widetilde{T}
is a trace for a
q
-deformation
\mathcal{A}_{\widetilde{P}}
corresponding to
\widetilde{P}
. We will abbreviate this sentence to “
\widetilde{T}
is a trace for
\widetilde{P}
”. Furthermore, if
\Phi=0
then
T
is a trace for
P_{\circ}
.

Remark 2.6
.

We note that the space of traces for
\widetilde{P}
does not change when we multiply this polynomial by nonzero complex number, similarly for
P_{\circ}
.

Proof.

In this proof we will denote the coefficient of
z^{l}
in a polynomial
R
by
R_{l}
.
Consider
T^{\prime}(S(x))=T\big(S\big(q^{-1}x\big)-tS(qx)\big)
. The map
T^{\prime}
is linear and satisfies
T^{\prime}(P(x)R(x))\allowbreak=0
for all
R(x)\in\mathbb{C}\big[x,x^{-1}\big]
. If
t=q^{2l}
for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}
we also have
T^{\prime}\big(x^{-l}\big)=0
. From
T^{\prime}\big(P(x)\mathbb{C}\big[x,x^{-1}\big]\big)=\{0\}
we get that
T^{\prime}(S(x))=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}S^{(j)}(\alpha_{i})
for some
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
. Here
\alpha_{i}
are different roots of
P
and
j
is not bigger than the multiplicity of
\alpha_{i}
as a root of
P
.

Let
z
be a complex number with
\lvert z\rvert>\frac{1}{\lvert q\rvert}
. Let
\widetilde{z}=q^{2k}z
. Let
R
,
S
be polynomials such that
R(z)=S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)
. Then

S(z)-t^{k}S(\widetilde{z})=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}t^{l}\big(S\big(q^{2l}z\big)-tS\big(q^{2l+2}z\big)\big)=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}t^{l}R\big(q^{2l+1}z\big).

For
l=0,\dots,k-1
we have
\big\lvert q^{2l+1}z\big\rvert>1
by the definition of
\widetilde{z}
.

Similarly for
z
with
\lvert z\rvert<\lvert q\rvert
,
\widetilde{z}=q^{-2k}z
we have

S(z)-t^{-k}S\big(q^{-2k}z\big)=-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}t^{-l-1}R\big(q^{-1-2l}z\big).

Differentiating we get similar formulas for
S^{(j)}
and
R^{(j)}
:

S^{(j)}(z)-t^{k}q^{2kj}S^{(j)}(\widetilde{z})=\sum_{|w|\neq 1}s_{w}R^{(j)}(w).

(2.1)

Let
\widetilde{\alpha_{i}}=z^{2k_{i}}\alpha_{i}
. Consider

T_{1}(S(x))=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}t^{k_{i}}q^{2k_{i}j}S^{(j)}(\widetilde{\alpha_{i}}).

We have
T_{1}\big(S(x)\widetilde{P}(x)\big)=0
for all
S(x)\in\mathbb{C}\big[x,x^{-1}\big]
.
Using (
2.1
) we see that
T^{\prime}(S)-T_{1}(S)
depends only on

R(x)=S\big(q^{-1}x\big)-tS(qx)

and has the form
\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{b}s_{z_{i},j}R^{(j)}(z_{i})
, where
\lvert z_{i}\rvert\neq 1
for all
i
. We denote this linear functional of
R
by
\Phi(R)
.

If
t=q^{2l}
for some integer
l
then
T_{1}\big(z^{-l}\big)=T^{\prime}\big(z^{-l}\big)=0
.
For any polynomial
R
with
R_{-l}=0
there exists a polynomial
S
such that
R(z)=S\big(q^{-1}z\big)\allowbreak-tS(qz)
.
S
is defined up to adding
z^{-l}
. So we get
T
from
T^{\prime}
as

T(R)=R_{-l}T\big(z^{-l}\big)+T^{\prime}\big(S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)\big),

where

S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)=R(z)-R_{-l}z^{-l}.

It follows that

\widetilde{T}(R)=R_{-l}T\big(z^{-l}\big)+T_{1}\big(S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)\big),

where

S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)=R(z)-R_{-l}z^{-l},

is a well-defined linear map. Since
T_{1}
is zero on
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\widetilde{P}(x)
, the map
\widetilde{T}
is a trace for
\widetilde{P}
.

In the case when there does not exist
l
such that
t=q^{2l}
, for any polynomial
R
there exists a unique polynomial
S
such that
R(z)=S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)
. In this case we define
\widetilde{T}(R)=T_{1}\big(S\big(q^{-1}z\big)-tS(qz)\big)
. The map
\widetilde{T}
is a trace for
\widetilde{P}
.
So we get
T=\widetilde{T}+\Phi
. This proves the first statement of the theorem.

Suppose that
\Phi=0
. This linear functional was a linear combination of

l_{z,j}(R)=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}t^{l}\big(R\big(q^{2l+1}z\big)\big)^{(j)}

for
\lvert z\rvert>\frac{1}{\lvert q\rvert}
and similar functionals for
\lvert z\rvert<\lvert q\rvert
. Here
k
is defined by
\widetilde{z}=q^{2k}z
.
It is easy to see that
l_{z,j}
are linearly independent for different
z
,
j
. From
\Phi=0
we deduce that in the sum
T^{\prime}(S(x))=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}S^{(j)}(\alpha_{i})
we have
c_{ij}=0
for indices
i
such that
\alpha_{i}\notin\overline{U}
. So
T^{\prime}
uses only roots of
P_{\circ}
. It follows that
T
is a trace for
P_{\circ}
.
∎

3
Positivity of traces in the case when all roots of
\boldsymbol{P}

satisfy
\boldsymbol{q<\lvert z\rvert<q^{-1}}

3.1
Positivity for twisted traces via quasiperiodic functions

In this section we assume that
0<q<1
.
Suppose that
\rho(u)=av
,
\rho(v)=bu
,
\rho(Z)=Z^{-1}
is an antilinear automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
such that
\rho^{2}=g_{t}
. Rescaling
v
we may assume that
|a|=1
. We have
\rho^{2}(u)=\overline{a}bu
,
\rho^{2}(v)=\overline{b}av
and
\rho^{2}=g_{t}
, where
g_{t}(u)=tu
,
g_{t}(v)=t^{-1}v
. It follows that
a\overline{a}b\overline{b}=1
, so
|b|=1
.
Since
t=\overline{a}b
we have
|t|=1
. Let
c\in[0,1)
be a real number such that
t={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}c}
.
If we change
u
to
zu
,
\lvert z\rvert=1
, then
a
changes to
\overline{z}a=z^{-1}a
,
b
changes to
z^{-1}b
. Therefore we can assume that
a={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}
. It follows that
b={\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}c}
.
So we will assume that
\rho
is an antilinear isomorphism such that
\rho(u)={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}v
,
\rho(v)={\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}c}u
,
\rho(Z)=Z^{-1}
, where
t={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}c}
.
The conjugation
\rho
exists when

P\big(q^{-1}Z^{-1}\big)=\rho\big(P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)\big)=\rho(uv)=\rho(u)\rho(v)=vu=P(qZ).

This is equivalent to
P(z)
being real on
S^{1}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T
be a
g_{t}
-twisted trace. We say that
T
is positive if the sesquilinear form
(a,b)_{T}=T(a\rho(b))
,
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, is positive definite.
We note that any positive definite sesquilinear form is Hermitian.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}_{i}
, where for
a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
we have
ZaZ^{-1}=q^{2i}a
. Since
T
is supported on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, the decomposition
\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is orthogonal with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{T}
. Therefore it is enough to check positive definiteness for homogeneous elements
a
.
Recall that since
T
is supported on
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathbb{C}\big[Z,Z^{-1}\big]
we write
T
both for linear functional on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and on
\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

1.

A
g_{t}
-twisted trace
T
is positive if and only if
T(a\rho(a))>0
for any nonzero
a\in\mathcal{A}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

2.

A
g_{t}
-twisted trace
T
is positive if and only if

T\big(R(z)\overline{R}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}T\big(P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(qz^{-1}\big)\big)>0

for all nonzero Laurent polynomials
R
.

Proof.

1. Suppose that
(\cdot,\cdot)_{T}
is positive definite on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
a
is a homogeneous element of degree
i>1
. Then there exists an element
b
of degree
0
or
1
and a positive integer
k
such that
a=u^{k}bu^{k}
. We have

\rho(a)=\rho(u)^{k}\rho(b)\rho(u)^{k}={\rm e}^{-2k\pi{\rm i}c}v^{k}\rho(b)v^{k}=t^{-k}v^{k}\rho(b)v^{k}.

Therefore

\displaystyle T(a\rho(a))=t^{-k}T\big(u^{k}bu^{k}v^{k}\rho(b)v^{k}\big)=t^{-k}T\big(bu^{k}v^{k}\rho(b)v^{k}g_{t}\big(u^{k}\big)\big)=T\big(bu^{k}v^{k}\rho(b)v^{k}u^{k}\big).

Let
s=bu^{k}v^{k}
. Note that

\rho(s)=\rho\big(bu^{k}v^{k}\big)=\rho(b){\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}kc}v^{k}{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}kc}u^{k}=\rho(b)v^{k}u^{k}.

So we proved that
T(a\rho(a))=T(s\rho(s))
, where
s
has degree
0
or
1
. So
T(s\rho(s))>0
and
T(a\rho(a))>0
.

If
a
has negative degree then
a=\rho(b)
for some
b
homogeneous of positive degree. Then

T(a\rho(a))=T(\rho(b)g_{t}(b))=T(b\rho(b))>0.

2. It is enough to take
a
of degree
0
or
1
. Let
a\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
. Then there exists
R\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
such that
a=R(Z)
. Therefore

T(a\rho(a))=T\big(R(Z)\overline{R}\big(Z^{-1}\big)\big)=T\big(R(z)\overline{R}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big).

Let
a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Then there exists
R\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
such that
a=uR(qZ)
. Then

\displaystyle a\rho(a)

\displaystyle=uR(Z){\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}v\overline{R}\big(qZ^{-1}\big)={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}R\big(q^{-1}Z\big)uvR\big(qZ^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}R\big(q^{-1}Z\big)P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)R\big(qZ^{-1}\big).

It follows that

\displaystyle T(a\rho(a))={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}T\big(R\big(q^{-1}z\big)P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(qz^{-1}\big)\big).

∎

Now we want to reformulate positivity conditions in terms of quasiperiodic functions in the case when all roots of
P
belong to
U
. Let
w
be a nonzero function such that
w(x+1)=w(x)
,
w(x+\tau)=tw(x)
and
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P(x)
is holomorphic on
\overline{D}=[0,1]\times\big[{-}\frac{\tau}{2},\frac{\tau}{2}\big]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T(R(z))=\int_{0}^{1}R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w(x)\,{\rm d}x
, where
w(x)
is as above. Then
T
is positive if and only if
w
and
{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
are nonnegative on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

We will use the following lemma in the proof.

Lemma 3.4
.

The closure of the set
\big\{R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\overline{R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)}\big\}
in
C[0,1]
is the set of functions
f
that are nonnegative on
[0,1]
and satisfy
f(0)=f(1)
.

Proof.

Let
f\geq 0
,
f(0)=f(1)
. Then we can find
g\in C\big(S^{1}\big)
such that
f=g\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)^{2}
. Since trigonometric polynomials are dense in
C\big(S^{1}\big)
, there exists a sequence of polynomials
R_{n}\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
such that
R_{n}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
tends to
\sqrt{f}
in
C[0,1]
. It follows that
\overline{R_{n}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)}
also tends to
\sqrt{f}
, so
R_{n}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\overline{R_{n}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)}
tends to
f
.
∎

It follows from Lemma
3.2
that
T
is positive if and only if

T\big(R(z)\overline{R}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)>0

and

{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}T\big(P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(q^{-1}z\big)\overline{R}\big(qz^{-1}\big)\big)>0

for all nonzero
R\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
.
We have

T\big(R(z)\overline{R}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)=\int_{0}^{1}R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\overline{R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)}w(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Using Lemma
3.4
we see that this expression is positive for all nonzero Laurent polynomials
R
if and only if
w
is nonnegative on
[0,1]
.
Recall that
q={\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}\tau}
. We have

\displaystyle T\big(P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(q^{-1}z\big)\overline{R}\big(qz^{-1}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}(x-\frac{\tau}{2})}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}(x-\frac{\tau}{2})}\big)\overline{R}\big({\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}(x-\frac{\tau}{2})}\big)w(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{\tau}{2}}^{1-\frac{\tau}{2}}P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\overline{R}\big({\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\overline{R}\big({\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right){\rm d}x.

In the last equality we used that
P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is holomorphic on
\overline{D}
.
Using Lemma
3.4
we see that

{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}T\big(P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R\big(q^{-1}z\big)\overline{R}\big(qz^{-1}\big)\big)

is positive for all nonzero Laurent polynomials
R
if and only if

{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)

is nonnegative on
[0,1]
.
∎

3.2
Positivity conditions for quasiperiodic functions

In this section we will describe the set of quasiperiodic functions
w
that give positive traces.
Recall that
q={\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}\tau}
,
U=\big\{\frac{1}{q}<|x|<q\big\}
,
V=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\ln U=\mathbb{R}\times\big({-}\frac{\tau}{2},\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
,
D
is a fundamental region for lattice generated by
1,\tau
such that
\overline{D}
is a parallelogram on vertices
-\frac{\tau}{2}
,
\frac{\tau}{2}
,
1+\frac{\tau}{2}
,
1-\frac{\tau}{2}
. Since
0<q<1
, we have
\tau\in\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
,
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\tau>0
.
Recall that
g_{t}
-twisted traces
T
are in one-to-one correspondence with quasiperiodic functions
w
such that
w(x+1)=w(x)
,
w(x+\tau)=tw(x)
and
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
is holomorphic on
\overline{V}
. Denote the linear space of these functions by
L
.

Suppose that
w
is a quasiperiodic function that is real on
\mathbb{R}
. Since
w
is meromorphic, we have
w(\overline{z})=\overline{w(z)}
. For
z=a-\frac{\tau}{2}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
this gives

w(\overline{z})=w\bigg(a+\frac{\tau}{2}\bigg)=\overline{w\bigg(a-\frac{\tau}{2}\bigg)}=\overline{t}^{-1}\overline{w\bigg(a+\frac{\tau}{2}\bigg)}=tw(\overline{z}).

Therefore
{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is real when
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits z=\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\tau
.

Denote the space of such functions by

. Theorem

says in particular that positive traces correspond to functions from

.

Since all roots of

belong to

, there are

in

, the interior of

. Denote them by

. Roots of

, and singletons

, so

are in pairs
\alpha
,

and singletons
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Let

. They satisfy the same symmetry condition as

.

Recall that
\vartheta(z)=\vartheta(z;\tau)
is the Jacobi theta function. Lemma

says that

L=\bigg\{\lambda\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-a_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-\beta_{i})}\bigg|\sum a_{i}-\sum\beta_{i}-c\in\mathbb{Z},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\bigg\}.

We want to describe the behavior of

and

on the real line.

Lemma 3.5
.

1.

Suppose that
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\vartheta(z-a)
and

\frac{{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}(z-a)}\vartheta\big(z-a+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)}{\cos(\pi(z-a))}

are positive on
\mathbb{R}
.

2.

Suppose that
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits a\notin\mathbb{Z}{\rm i}\tau
. Then
\vartheta(z-a)\vartheta(z-\overline{a})
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
and

{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}(z-\operatorname{Re}a)}\vartheta\left(z-a+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\vartheta\left(z-\overline{a}+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)

\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We will use Jacobi triple product identity

\vartheta(z)=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(1-{\rm e}^{2m\pi{\rm i}\tau}\big)\big(1+{\rm e}^{(2m-1)\pi{\rm i}\tau+2\pi{\rm i}z}\big)\big(1+{\rm e}^{(2m-1)\pi{\rm i}\tau-2\pi{\rm i}z}\big).

(3.1)

It follows that

\displaystyle\vartheta\left(z+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(1-{\rm e}^{2m\pi{\rm i}\tau}\big)\big(1+{\rm e}^{2m\pi{\rm i}\tau+2\pi{\rm i}z}\big)\big(1+{\rm e}^{(2m-2)\pi{\rm i}\tau-2\pi{\rm i}z}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(1+{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}z}\big)\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(1-{\rm e}^{2m\pi{\rm i}\tau}\big)\big(1+{\rm e}^{2m\pi{\rm i}\tau+2\pi{\rm i}z}\big)\big(1+{\rm e}^{2m\pi{\rm i}\tau-2\pi{\rm i}z}\big).

(3.2)

We note that

\overline{1+{\rm e}^{k\pi{\rm i}\tau\pm 2\pi{\rm i}z}}=1+{\rm e}^{k\pi{\rm i}\tau\mp 2\pi{\rm i}z}

(3.3)

for all integers

. Also when

,
z\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\big\lvert{\rm e}^{k\pi{\rm i}\tau\pm 2\pi{\rm i}z}\big\rvert={\rm e}^{k\pi{\rm i}\tau}\neq 1

is nonzero when

.

1. Comparing (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) we see that
\vartheta(z)
is a product of two nonzero conjugate numbers when

Comparing (

) and (
3.3
) we see that

\big(1+{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}z}\big)^{-1}\vartheta\left(z+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)

is a product of two nonzero conjugate numbers when

. We have

1+{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}z}=2{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}z}\cos(\pi{\rm i}z).

This proves the first part of the lemma.

2. We have

\big|{\rm e}^{k\pi{\rm i}\tau\pm 2\pi{\rm i}(z-a)}\big|=\big|{\rm e}^{k\pi{\rm i}\tau\pm 2\pi\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits a}\big|\neq 1

for all real

is nonzero when

and

Comparing (

) and (
3.3
) we see that
\vartheta(z-a)\vartheta(z-\overline{a})

.
Comparing (

) and (

\big(1+{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}(z-a)}\big)^{-1}\big(1+{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}(z-\overline{a})}\big)^{-1}\vartheta\left(z+\frac{\tau}{2}-a\right)\vartheta\left(z+\frac{\tau}{2}-\overline{a}\right)

is a product of two nonzero conjugate numbers. Similarly to the above we have

The second statement of the lemma follows.
∎

It follows from Lemma
3.5
that the denominator of

. In this case

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that

does not change sign on
\mathbb{R}
. Then there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
divided into singletons

and pairs

such that

. Moreover, we can choose

so that they satisfy
\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits b_{j}\rvert<\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\tau
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{f(z)}=f(\overline{z})
for any

we have
f\big(\overline{a_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)=0
. So there exists
k
such that
\overline{a_{j}}-a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
. Comparing multiplicities of roots, we can divide
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
into singletons
a_{j}-\overline{a_{j}}\in\mathbb{Z}\tau
and pairs
a_{j}-\overline{a_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
. Since
\vartheta(z)=\vartheta(z+1)
, we may assume that in pairs
a_{j}-\overline{a_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}\tau
.
Shifting all
a_{j}
by some integer multiple of
\tau
, we get a set
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
divided into singletons

and pairs

such that

where

,

. Moreover, we may choose
d_{j}
so that they satisfy
\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits d_{j}\rvert<\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\tau\rvert
.
Suppose that
d_{j}\in\mathbb{R}+\frac{\tau}{2}
. Then
\vartheta(z-d_{j})
has simple zeros on
\mathbb{R}
. Since all roots of
f
on
\mathbb{R}
have even multiplicity, there exists an even number of

from

to

with

. Shifting half of

by

, we obtain a new sequence
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
divided into singletons
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and pairs
b_{j}=\overline{b_{k}}
such that

, where

,

.
It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
\vartheta(z)
is positive on
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore both

and
\vartheta(z-b_{1})\cdots\allowbreak\vartheta(z-b_{n})
are real on

and do not change sign. So

and

.
∎

Before we describe the cone of positive traces we need to introduce an additional parameter that distinguishes the parameters

and
-P
. Since
P
and

give isomorphic algebras, the difference between

and

is in the choice of another conjugation on the same algebra.

Note that

\frac{P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)}{\prod\limits_{j\colon\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(x-\beta_{j})}

is a continuous function that is real on the real line and has no roots on
\mathbb{R}
, so it does not change sign on
\mathbb{R}
. Let
m_{0}=1
in the case when this fraction is negative on
\mathbb{R}
and
m_{0}=0
in the case when this fraction is positive on
\mathbb{R}
. In other words,

(-1)^{m_{0}}\frac{P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)}{\prod\limits_{j\colon\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(x-\beta_{j})}

is positive on

.

Now we are ready to describe the cone of functions that give a positive trace.

Theorem 3.7
.

The cone
C
of positive
g_{t}
-twisted traces has dimension
n
. It is isomorphic to the set of entire functions
f
such that

f(x+1)=f(x),\qquad f(x+\tau)={\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}(2nx+n\tau-2\sum\beta_{j}-2c)}f(x)

and

f(x),\qquad{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}(-nx+\sum\beta_{j}+m_{0}+c)}f\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)

are nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
. At the level of sets
C
consists of functions

\lambda\frac{\vartheta(z-a_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-a_{n})}{\vartheta(z-\beta_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-\beta_{n})},

where
\lambda>0
,
\sum a_{i}-\sum\beta_{i}-c-m_{0}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and all
a_{i}
are divided into pairs
(a_{i},a_{j})
with
a_{i}=\overline{a_{j}}
. In particular, if
Q
is another Laurent polynomial with the same number of nonzero roots
n
counted with multiplicities,
s
is a nonzero complex number with
\lvert s\rvert=1
, cones
C_{P,t}
and
C_{Q,s}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

A quasiperiodic function
w\in L
gives a positive trace if and only if
w
and
{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\times w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
are nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
.
We know that

w=\lambda\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-a_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-\beta_{i})},

where
\sum a_{i}-\sum\beta_{i}-c\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.
It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-\beta_{i})
is positive on
\mathbb{R}
. Since
w
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
we deduce that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-a_{i})
does not change sign on
\mathbb{R}
. Using Lemma
3.6
we may assume that
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are divided into singletons
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and pairs
a_{i}=\overline{a_{j}}
. Since
w
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
we get that
\lambda>0
.

On the other hand, suppose that
\lambda>0
,

w=\lambda\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-a_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta(z-\beta_{i})}

is an element of
L
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are symmetric with respect to
\mathbb{R}
. It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
\vartheta(z-a_{i})
is positive on
\mathbb{R}
when
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. We deduce that in this case
w
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
.

We now study the behavior of
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
on
\mathbb{R}
. We say that two meromorphic functions
f
and
g
are equivalent if
\frac{f}{g}
is a positive function when restricted to
\mathbb{R}
.
For all
i
we have
|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\beta_{i}|<\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\tau
and
|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits a_{i}|<\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\tau
.
It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta\big(z-\beta_{i}+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is equivalent to

{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}(nz-\sum\beta_{j})}\prod_{j\colon\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(\pi(z-\beta_{j}))

and
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta\big(z-a_{i}+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is equivalent to

{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}(nz-\sum a_{j})}\prod_{j\colon a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(\pi(z-a_{j})).

Let
\sum a_{i}-\sum\beta_{j}=c+m
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore
w(z+\frac{\tau}{2})
is equivalent to

{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}c+\pi{\rm i}m}\frac{\prod_{a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(\pi(z-a_{j}))}{\prod_{\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(\pi(z-\beta_{j}))}.

Recall that
P(x)
is equivalent to
(-1)^{m_{0}}\prod_{\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(x-\beta_{j})
.
It follows that
{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is equivalent to

(-1)^{m+m_{0}}\prod_{a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(\pi(z-a_{j})).

This function is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
if and only if each real number appears even number of times among

and
m
has the same parity as
m_{0}
. This gives a description of
C
as a set.

Now we describe
C
in terms of the numerator

f(z)=\vartheta(z-a_{1})\cdots\vartheta(z-a_{n}).

The function

w(z)=\frac{f(z)}{\vartheta(z-\beta_{1})\cdots(z-\beta_{n})}

belongs to
C
only if
w(x)
and
{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
are nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
. It follows from Lemma
3.6
that
w(x)
is equivalent to
f(x)
on
\mathbb{R}
. Recall that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta\big(z-\beta_{i}+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)
is equivalent to

\exp\Big({-}\pi{\rm i}\Big(nz-\sum\beta_{j}\Big)\Big)\prod_{\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}\cos(\pi(z-\beta_{j})).

It follows that
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
is equivalent to

(-1)^{m_{0}}f\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right){\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}\left(nz-\sum\beta_{j}\right)}.

So
{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
if and only if

f\left(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\right){\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}\left(nz+m_{0}+c-\sum\beta_{j}\right)}

is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
.
We see from this statement that there exists an isomorphism between any two cones
C_{P,t}
and
C_{Q,s}
given by the map
f(x)\mapsto f(x+x_{0})
for
x_{0}=\sum\beta_{P,i}+c_{t}+m_{P,0}-\sum\beta_{Q,i}-c_{s}-m_{Q,0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We deduce the statement about dimension from the example below.
∎

Example 3.8
.

Suppose that
P
is positive on
S^{1}
,

. Then the space
L
consists of elliptic functions
w
such that

is holomorphic when
\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits x\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\lvert\tau\rvert
. In particular,

. The linear map

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
at nonzero points and to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
at zero.

By definition

consists of functions
w
such that

,

and

says that

and

.

∎

Example 3.9
.

Let

. In this case

is isomorphic to

.
Indeed, extremal points of

are given by functions that have roots on

or

or

Positivity of traces in the general case

Now we describe the cone of positive traces in the general case. The argument here is very similar to

4.1
The case when all roots of

\boldsymbol{q\leqslant\lvert z\rvert\leqslant q^{-1}}

Let

belong to

, all roots of

belong to

. For any

from some fixed linear space of representatives modulo

Proposition

says that in this case any trace

is a quasiperiodic function such that

Suppose that

is a positive trace as above. First we will prove that

satisfies the same positivity conditions as in Theorem

. Then we will prove that

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that

is a positive trace as above. Then

.

Proof.

Since

is positive we deduce from Lemma
3.2
that

T\big(R_{1}(z)\overline{R_{1}}\big(z^{-1}\big)Q(z)\overline{Q}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)>0

{\rm e}^{-\pi{\rm i}c}T\big(P\big(q^{-1}z\big)R_{1}\big(q^{-1}z\big)R_{q}\big(qz^{-1}\big)\big)>0

for any nonzero

. These polynomials are divisible by

. So in this case the

is given by integration. Now we deduce from Lemma

similarly to the proof of Theorem

that
w(x)Q\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\overline{Q\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)}

. It follows that

is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

be a trace as above. If

has poles on

does not give a positive definite form.

Proof.

Since
\overline{R}\big(z^{-1}\big)=\overline{R(z)}
for

\overline{R}\big(z^{-1}\big)
by

Suppose that

has poles on
\mathbb{R}
and

is positive. We deduce that
w
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
, so all poles of

have even order. Suppose that
w
has a pole of order

.
Let
f
be an element of

,
R_{n}
be a sequence of polynomials that tends to

in
L^{2}\big(S^{1}\big)
. Let

be a real number. Consider
S_{n}=(R_{n}Q+b)\overline{(R_{n}Q+b)}=Q\big(R_{n}\overline{R_{n}}\overline{Q}+b\overline{R_{n}}+bR_{n}\frac{\overline{Q}}{Q}\big)+b^{2}
. We have

T(S_{n})=\int_{0}^{1}w\cdot Q\cdot\bigg(R_{n}\overline{R_{n}}\overline{Q}+b\overline{R_{n}}+bR_{n}\frac{\overline{Q}}{Q}\bigg){\rm d}x+Cb^{2},

where
C
is some constant. If
C
is negative then the statement is clear, so we assume that
C\geq 0
.
Since
R_{n}
tends to
f
,
\overline{R_{n}}
tends to
\overline{f}
in
L^{2}\big(S^{1}\big)
and
R_{n}\overline{R_{n}}
tends to
f\overline{f}
in
L^{1}\big(S^{1}\big)
. We deduce that
T(S_{n})
tends to

\int_{0}^{1}w\cdot Q\cdot\bigg(f\overline{f}\overline{Q}+b\overline{f}+bf\frac{\overline{Q}}{Q}\bigg)+Cb^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}w\big(Q\overline{Q}f\overline{f}+b\overline{f}Q+bf\overline{Q}\big){\rm d}x+Cb^{2}.

Let
Q
have a zero of order
M
at point
x_{0}
. Then
wQ\overline{Q}
has a zero of order
2M-N
at
x_{0}
,
w\overline{Q}
and
wQ
have a zero of order
M-N
at
x_{0}
.
Let
a
be a complex number,
\varepsilon>0
. We define
f
so that
f\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)=a\chi_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]}(x)
for
x\in[0,1]
. When
x_{0}=0
we set
f\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)=a\chi_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]}(x)
for
x\in\big[{-}\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\big]
.
We deduce that

\int_{0}^{1}f\overline{f}Q\overline{Q}w\,{\rm d}x=c_{1}\varepsilon^{2N-2M+1},\qquad\int_{0}^{1}\overline{f}Qw\,{\rm d}x=c_{2}\varepsilon^{N-M+1},

where
c_{1}=c_{1}(\varepsilon)
has strictly positive limit at
\varepsilon=0
,
c_{2}=c_{2}(\varepsilon)
has nonzero limit at
\varepsilon=0
. We choose
a
so that
c_{2}
has strictly positive limit at
\varepsilon=0
.
We deduce that

\lim_{n\to\infty}T(S_{n})=c_{1}\varepsilon^{2M-N+1}+2c_{2}\varepsilon^{M-N+1}b+Cb^{2}.

This is a quadratic polynomial in
b
with discriminant

D=4c_{2}^{2}\varepsilon{2M-2N+2}-4Cc_{1}\varepsilon^{2M-N+1}=\varepsilon^{2M-2N+2}\big(4c_{2}^{2}-4Cc_{1}\varepsilon^{N-1}\big).

Since
N\geq 2
for small
\varepsilon
this discriminant is positive. So there exists
b
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(S_{n})\allowbreak<0
. Therefore
T\big((R_{n}Q+b)\overline{(R_{n}Q+b)}\big)=T(S_{n})<0
for some
n
, a contradiction.
∎

Now we are left with the case when
w
has no poles on
\mathbb{R}
. In this case
T(R(z))=\int_{0}^{1}R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\times w(x)\,{\rm d}x+\eta(R_{0})
, where
\eta
is some linear functional.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
T
be a trace as above. Then
T
gives a positive definite form if and only if
\eta(R_{0})=\sum c_{j}R_{0}(z_{j})
, where
c_{j}\geq 0
,
z_{j}\in S^{1}
are roots of
Q
.

Proof.

Suppose that this is not the case. Then it is easy to find a polynomial
S
such that
\eta\big(\big(S\overline{S}\big)_{0}\big)<0
. Consider a sequence of continuous functions
f_{n}
such that
f_{n}Q+S
tends to zero in
L^{2}\big(S^{1}\big)
. Since polynomials are dense in
C\big(S^{1}\big)
, we can find a sequence of polynomials
G_{n}
such that
G_{n}Q+S
tends to zero in
L^{2}\big(S^{1}\big)
. We deduce that

T\big((G_{n}Q+S)\overline{(G_{n}Q+S)}\big)\to\eta\big(\big(S\overline{S}\big)_{0}\big)<0.

This gives a contradiction.
∎

4.2
General case

Now we deal with the general case. Let
\widetilde{P}
,
P_{\circ}
be polynomials defined in Section
2.3
. The roots of
\widetilde{P}
and
P_{\circ}
belong to the set
\overline{U}=\big\{\lvert q\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert\leq\frac{1}{\lvert q\rvert}\big\}
. The roots of
\widetilde{P}
are obtained from the roots of
P
by multiplication by
q^{2k}
with minimal possible
|k|
. The multiplicity of
\alpha\in\overline{U}
as a root of
P_{\circ}
equals to the multiplicity of
\alpha
as a root of
P
.
Theorem
2.5
says that in this case any trace
T
can be represented as
T=\widetilde{T}+\Phi
, where
\widetilde{T}
is a trace for
\widetilde{P}
and
\Phi
is a linear functional such that

\Phi(R)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k}c_{jk}R^{(k)}(z_{j}),

where
z_{j}\notin S^{1}
. Furthermore, if
\Phi=0
then
T
is a trace for
P_{\circ}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
T
be a trace such that
\Phi\neq 0
. Then
T
does not give a positive definite form.

Proof.

We denote
\overline{S}\big(z^{-1}\big)
by
\overline{S}
.
For big enough
k
we have
\Phi\big((z-z_{1})^{k}\cdots(z-z_{m})^{k}\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\big)=\{0\}
. Recall that there exists a polynomial
Q
such that for any linear space
S
of representatives modulo
Q
and any polynomial
R=R_{1}Q+R_{0}
,
R_{0}\in S
, we have
\widetilde{T}(R)=\int_{0}^{1}R_{1}Qw\,{\rm d}x+\psi(R_{0})
, where
w
is a function and
\psi
is some linear functional. Moreover, all roots of
Q
belong to
S^{1}
. We can still represent
\widetilde{T}
in this form if we change
Q
to
Q_{*}=Q\overline{Q}
. So we may assume that
Q
is nonnegative on
S^{1}
. Let
U
be a polynomial divisible by
Q
and real on
S^{1}
such that
\Phi\big(U(z)\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\big)=\{0\}
.

Let
F
be any polynomial. It is easy to find a sequence of continuous functions
f_{n}
such that
f_{n}U-F
tends to zero in
L^{2}\big(S^{1}\big)
. Approximating
f_{n}
with polynomials, we find a sequence of polynomials
R_{n}
such that
UR_{n}-F
tends to zero in
L^{2}\big(S^{1}\big)
. It follows that
H_{n}=(UR_{n}-F)\times\overline{(UR_{n}-F)}
tends to zero in
L^{1}\big(S^{1}\big)
. We deduce that
\widetilde{T}(H_{n}Q)=\int_{0}^{1}H_{n}Q\cdot w\,{\rm d}x
tends to zero when
n
tends to infinity.
It follows that
T(Q(z)H_{n}(z))
tends to
\Phi(Q(z)H_{n}(z))=\Phi\big(Q(z)F(z)\overline{F}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)
. Since
QH_{n}
is nonnegative on
S^{1}
, we have
T(QH_{n})>0
. Now we get a contradiction with

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists
F(z)\in\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]
such that

\Phi\big(Q(z)F(z)\overline{F}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)\notin\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

Proof.

Let
r
be the biggest number such that there exists
j
with
c_{jr}\neq 0
. Fix this
j
. If there exists
z_{s}=\overline{z_{j}}^{-1}
, we denote this index
s
by
j^{*}
. Let

F(z)=G(z)(z-z_{1})^{r+1}\cdots(z-z_{j})^{r}\cdots\widehat{(z-z_{j^{*}})}\cdots(z-z_{m})^{r+1}.

Notation
\widehat{(z-z_{j^{*}})}
means that we omit
z-z_{j^{*}}
in this product.
We note that

c_{ik}\big(Q(z)F(z)\overline{F}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)^{(k)}|_{z=z_{i}}=0

for all
i
,
k
except
k=r
and
i=j
or
i=j^{*}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\Phi\big(Q(z)F(z)\overline{F}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=c_{jk}\big(Q(z)F(z)\overline{F}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)^{(k)}(z_{j})+c_{j^{*}k}\big(Q(z)F(z)\overline{F}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)(z_{j^{*}})

\displaystyle=c_{jr}Q(z_{j})F^{(k)}(z_{j})\overline{F}\big(z_{j}^{-1}\big)+c_{j^{*}k}Q(z_{j^{*}})F(z_{j^{*}})(-z_{j^{*}})^{-2k}\overline{F}^{(k)}(z_{j^{*}})

\displaystyle=sa+t\overline{a},

where
a=Q(z_{j})F^{(k)}(z_{j})\overline{F}\big(z_{j}^{-1}\big)
,
s,t\in\mathbb{C}
are not simultaneously zero. Pick
a\in\mathbb{C}
so that
sa+t\overline{a}\notin\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and choose
G\in\mathbb{C}[z]
which gives this value of
a
. For example, we can choose
G
linear. Then
\Phi\big(Q(z)F(z)\overline{F}\big(z^{-1}\big)\big)\notin\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, as desired.
∎

Consider a
q
-deformation
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
with parameter
P_{\circ}
. We choose
P_{\circ}
so that
\frac{P}{P_{\circ}}
is positive on
S^{1}
.

Corollary 4.6
.

The cone of positive definite forms for
\mathcal{A}
coincides with the cone of positive definite forms for
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
. Namely,
T\colon\mathbb{C}\big[z,z^{-1}\big]\to\mathbb{C}
gives a positive definite form for
\mathcal{A}
if and only if it gives a positive definite form for
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
.

Proof.

Let
T=\widetilde{T}+\Phi
be a positive trace. Using Proposition
4.4
we deduce that
\Phi=0
. Therefore
T
is a trace for
P_{\circ}
. Writing
P_{\circ}(z)=P_{*}(z)Q(z)\overline{Q}\big(z^{-1}\big)
and choosing a linear space of representatives
S
modulo
Q
we can write
T(R)=\int_{0}^{1}R_{1}Qw\,{\rm d}x+l(R_{0})
, where
R=R_{1}Q+R_{0}
,
R_{0}\in S
.
Using Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
we deduce that
w
has no poles on
\mathbb{R}
and that
w(x)
and
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
are nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
. Using Proposition
4.3
we get that
T(R)=\int_{0}^{1}R\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)\times w(x)\,{\rm d}x+\eta(R),
where
\eta(R)=\sum c_{j}R(z_{j})
,
c_{j}\geq 0
and
z_{j}
is a root of
Q
. On the other hand, from such
w
and
\eta
we obtain a positive trace for
P
.
Using this argument for
P_{\circ}
instead of
P
we get that
T
is positive for
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
if and only if
T=\int_{0}^{1}Rw\,{\rm d}x+\eta
, where
w(x)
and
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P_{\circ}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x}\big)
are nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
,
\eta(R)=\sum c_{j}R(z_{j})
,
c_{j}\geq 0
,
z_{j}
is a root of
Q
.
Since
\frac{P}{P_{\circ}}
is positive on
S^{1}
, the function
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P(x)
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
w\big(x+\frac{\tau}{2}\big)P_{\circ}(x)
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
. It follows that
T
is positive for
P
if and only if it is positive for
P_{\circ}
.
∎

So we have proved the following theorem.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
q
-deformation with parameter
P
. Let
t\in S^{1}
,
\rho_{t}
be the corresponding conjugation. Let
l
be the number of roots
\alpha
of

such that
q<|\alpha|<q^{-1}
,

be the number of distinct roots

with

. Then the cone
\mathcal{C}_{+}
of positive definite

-equivariant traces is isomorphic to

, where
\mathcal{C}_{l}
consists of nonzero entire functions
f
such that

(1)

,

.

5
Short star-products

5.1
Classification of deformations and automorphisms

Let
A
be an arbitrary
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-graded algebra such that
A_{0}=\mathbb{C}
. A
short star-product

[
7
]
on
A
is an associative product
*
on
A
such that for all nonnegative integers
k
,
m
and
a\in A_{k}
,
b\in A_{m}
we have

a*b=c_{0}(a,b)+c_{2}(a,b)+c_{4}(a,b)+\cdots,

where
c_{0}(a,b)=ab
,
c_{i}(a,b)\in A_{k+m-2i}
,
c_{i}
is a bilinear map and
c_{i}=0
for
i>\min(k,m)
. A short star-product is nondegenerate if the form
(\cdot,\cdot)\colon A_{k}\times A_{k}\to\mathbb{C}
,
(a,b)=c_{k}(a,b)=CT(a*b)
is nondegenerate for all
k
. Here
CT
means taking constant term.

We see that
A
with the multiplication given by
*
is a filtered algebra and associate graded algebra of
(A,*)
is naturally isomorphic to
A
. Consider the operator
s\colon A\to A
that acts on
A_{i}
as
(-1)^{i}
. It follows from definitions that
s
is an automorphism of
A
and an automorphism of
(A,*)
. We say that a filtered algebra
\mathcal{A}
equipped with an involution
S
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant deformation of
A
if
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits S=s
. We see that
(A,*)
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant deformation of
A
.

In
[
7
]
it is proved that nondegenerate short star-products correspond to triples
(\mathcal{A},g,T)
. Here
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant deformation of
A
,
g
is a filtered automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
and
T
is a nondegenerate
g
-twisted trace. A trace is called nondegenerate if the form
(\cdot,\cdot)\colon\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}\times\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
(a,b)=T(ab)
is nondegenerate for all
i\geq 0
.

In
[
7
]
is assumed that
A
is commutative and equipped with Poisson bracket, but the proof should be the same. In this paper we need this correspondence only as a motivation to classify deformations and study nondegenerate traces, so we will not prove this result for noncommutative graded algebras
A
. We will prove this result in a new paper
[
9
]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a generalized
q
-Weyl algebra.
In this section we assume that
P
belongs to
\mathbb{C}[z]
and has degree
n
. We no longer assume that
q
is positive, but we assume that
0<\lvert q\rvert<1
.
Consider the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{+}
of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
u
,
v
,
Z
. There is a filtration on
\mathcal{A}_{+}
defined by
\deg u=\deg v=n
,
\deg Z=2
. We have
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits\mathcal{A}_{+}=A_{+}
, where
A_{+}
is generated by
u
,
v
,
Z
with relations
Zu=q^{2}uZ
,
Zv=q^{-2}vZ
,
uv=q^{-n}Z^{n}
,
vu=q^{n}Z^{n}
,
\deg u=\deg v=n
,
\deg Z=2
.
We will do the following in this section. First, we will prove that any deformation
\mathcal{B}
of
A_{+}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{+}
for some parameter
P
. Then we will prove that if
n>2
and
T
is a nondegenerate
g
-twisted trace on
\mathcal{A}_{+}
then
g=g_{t}
for some
t
. Finally we will classify the
g_{t}
-twisted traces on
\mathcal{A}_{+}
in the case when
t\neq 1
,
q^{-2},q^{-4},\dots
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a filtered deformation of
A_{+}
. Then there exists parameter
P
such that
\mathcal{B}\cong\mathcal{A}_{+}
.

Proof.

Choose any lifts of
Z
,
u
,
v
in
\mathcal{B}
. Denote them by
Z_{1}
,
u_{1}
,
v_{1}
. Since
Z^{i}u^{j}
and
Z^{i}v^{j}
form a basis of
A_{+}
, elements
Z_{1}^{i}u_{1}^{j}
and
Z_{1}^{i}v_{1}^{j}
form a basis of
\mathcal{B}
.
By definition,
u_{1}v_{1}
equals to
q^{-n}Z_{1}^{n}+R
, where
R\in\mathcal{B}_{\leq 2n-2}
. Therefore
u_{1}v_{1}-q^{-n}Z_{1}^{n}
belongs to the linear span of
Z_{1}^{i}u_{1}^{j}
and
Z_{1}^{i}v_{1}^{j}
, where
2i+nj<2n-2
. In particular,
j\leq 1
, so we write
u_{1}v_{1}-q^{-n}Z_{1}^{n}=R_{1}+u_{1}R_{2}+v_{1}R_{3}
, where
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}\in\mathbb{C}[Z_{1}]
and
R_{2},R_{3}\in\mathcal{B}_{\leq n-2}
. Consider
u=u_{1}-R_{3}
,
v=v_{1}-R_{2}
. We see that
uv
belongs to
\mathbb{C}[Z_{1}]
.
Because of commutation relations in
A_{+}
we have
Z_{1}u-q^{2}uZ_{1}=B\in\mathcal{B}_{\leq n}
,
q^{2}Z_{1}v-vZ_{1}=C\allowbreak\in\mathcal{B}_{\leq n}
. In particular,
B
,
C
belong to the span of
u,v,1,Z_{1},Z_{1}^{2},\dots
. Let
B=b_{1}u+b_{2}v+f(Z_{1})
,
C=c_{1}u+c_{2}v+g(Z_{1})
.
Since
uv\in\mathbb{C}[Z_{1}]
we have
Z_{1}uv=Z_{1}vu
. On the other hand,

Z_{1}uv=\big(q^{2}uZ_{1}+B\big)v=q^{2}uZ_{1}v+Bv=uvZ_{1}+uC+Bv.

It follows that
uC+Bv=0
, so

c_{1}u^{2}+c_{2}uv+ug(Z_{1})+b_{1}uv+b_{2}v^{2}+f(Z_{1})v=0.

Since
u^{2}
,
v^{2}
,
uv
,
uZ^{i}
,
Z^{i}v
are linearly independent in
A_{+}
, elements
u^{2}
,
v^{2}
,
uv
,
uZ_{1}^{i}
,
Z_{1}^{i}v
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{B}
. Therefore
B=b_{1}u
,
C=c_{2}v
and
b_{1}+c_{2}=0
. Taking
Z=Z_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{q^{2}-1}
we get
Zu=q^{2}uZ
,
Zv=q^{-2}vZ
.
Hence
vu
also commutes with
Z
, so
vu\in\mathbb{C}[Z]
. Let
P
be a polynomial such that
vu=P(qz)
. From
(uv)u=u(vu)
we get
uv=P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)
. Taking this parameter
P
we obtain isomorphism between
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\mathcal{B}
that sends
u
,
v
,
Z
to
u
,
v
,
Z
respectively.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Deformation
\mathcal{A}_{+}
is automatically
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded with
\deg u=\deg v=n\pmod{2}
,
\deg Z=0
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
n>2
and
P(z)\neq z^{n}
. Let
g
be an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}_{+}
. Then
g(Z)=\lambda Z
,
g(u)=tu
,
g(v)=\lambda^{n}t^{-1}v
, where
\lambda,t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. If there exists a
g
-twisted nondegenerate trace then
\lambda=1
and
g=g_{t}
.

Proof.

In this proof we will denote
k
-th filtration subspace of
\mathcal{A}_{+}
by
\mathcal{A}_{\leq k}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}_{\leq 2}=\mathbb{C}\cdot Z\oplus\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
, we have
g(Z)=\lambda Z+b
, where
\lambda\neq 0
. For a positive integer
k
consider the operator

\phi\colon\ a\mapsto g(Z)a-q^{2k}ag(Z).

Since the operator
a\mapsto Za-q^{2k}aZ
has a kernel, namely
u^{k}\mathbb{C}[Z]
,
\phi
also has a kernel. We have

\displaystyle\phi\big(u^{n}R(z)\big)

\displaystyle=(\lambda Z+b)u^{n}R(Z)-q^{2k}u^{n}R(Z)(\lambda Z+b)

\displaystyle=u^{n}\big(\big(q^{2n}-q^{2k}\big)\lambda Z+\big(1-q^{2k}\big)b\big)R(z),

where
n
is a nonnegative integer,
R
is any polynomial. We have a similar formula for
v^{n}R(z)
. It follows that
\phi
preserves the decomposition
\mathcal{A}_{+}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}u^{i}\mathbb{C}[Z]\oplus\bigoplus_{i>0}v^{i}\mathbb{C}[Z]
. So if
\phi
has a kernel then for some integer
n
we have
\big(q^{2n}-q^{2k}\big)\lambda Z+\big(1-q^{2k}\big)b=0
. In particular,
\big(1-q^{2k}\big)b=0
. It follows that
b=0
.
So
g(Z)=\lambda Z
. We have

\mathbb{C}u=\mathcal{A}_{\leq n}\cap\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}\colon Za=q^{2}aZ\big\},

so
g(u)=tu
for some
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Similarly
g(v)=sv
for some
s\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Therefore

P\big(q^{-1}\lambda Z\big)=g\big(P\big(q^{-1}Z\big)\big)g(uv)=g(u)g(v)=tsuv=tsP\big(q^{-1}Z\big).

Suppose that the coefficient of
z^{k}
in
P
is nonzero for some
k<n
. We get
\lambda^{n}=ts
,
\lambda^{k}=ts
. So
\lambda^{n-k}=1
,
ts=\lambda^{n}
.
If
\lambda=1
we get
g=g_{t}
.
Suppose that
\lambda\neq 1

Proof.

We may assume that

is a monic polynomial of degree

and
s=\pm 1
so that the leading coefficient of
a^{2}P(sx)
equals to
\frac{1}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}
. Then we change
P(x)
to
a^{2}P(sx)
. Define
c
to be the coefficient of
P
on
1
minus
\frac{2}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}
. It follows from
P(x)=\overline{P}\big(x^{-1}\big)
that

P(x)=-\frac{x+x^{-1}-2}{\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{2}}+c.

Therefore

\mathcal{A}\cong U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})/(C-c)

and this isomorphism respects conjugation.
∎

Recall that
\mathcal{A}
has a positive definite invariant form if and only if both roots
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
of
P
satisfy
q<\lvert\alpha_{i}\rvert<q^{-1}
,
i=1,2
.
By Vieta’s formulas we have
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=1
,
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=c\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{2}+2
. In particular,
q<\lvert\alpha_{i}\rvert<q^{-1}
if and only if
\big|c\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{2}+2\big|<q+q^{-1}
. This is equivalent to
c\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{2}+2\in\big({-}q-q^{-1},q+q^{-1}\big)
. We get
c\in\big(\frac{-q-q^{-1}-2}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}},\frac{q+q^{-1}-2}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}\big)
.
We also have
\lvert\alpha_{1}\rvert=\lvert\alpha_{2}\rvert=1
if and only if
\big|c\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{2}+2\big|\leq 2
. So when
c\in\big({-}\frac{4}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}},0\big)
both roots lie on the circle.

6.2
Computation of the cone of positive functions in a fixed basis

We assume that
c\in\big(\frac{-q-q^{-1}-2}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}},\frac{q+q^{-1}-2}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}\big)
. Denote the roots of
P(x)
by
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
. Since
P(x)=\overline{P}(x)=P\big(x^{-1}\big)
we have
z_{1}z_{2}=1
and either
z_{1}=\overline{z_{2}}
or
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
The linear space of traces on
\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic to the space
L
of elliptic functions
w
with period
q^{2}
such that
P(z)w(qz)
is holomorphic on the set
\big\{z\mid q\leq\lvert z\rvert\leq q^{-1}\big\}
. This space has dimension
2
. Function
w
corresponds to the trace
T(R)=\int_{S^{1}}R(z)w(z)\lvert{\rm d}z\rvert
.

Let
\wp
be a Weierstrass elliptic function with periods
2\ln q
,
2\pi{\rm i}
and
w_{0}(z)=\wp(\ln z)
. Since
z_{1}z_{2}=1
, we have
w_{0}(qz_{1})=w_{0}(qz_{2})
. Since
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
belong to
S^{1}
or
\mathbb{R}
, we also have
c_{0}=w_{0}(qz_{1})\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
L
has a basis
1
,
\frac{1}{w_{0}-c_{0}}
.
Consider a real linear subspace
L_{\mathbb{R}}
consisting of functions
w
real on
S^{1}
. It can be written as

L_{\mathbb{R}}=\bigg\{a+\frac{b}{w_{0}-c_{0}}\,\bigg|\,a,b\in\mathbb{R}\bigg\}.

We have
2\ln q\in\mathbb{R}
,
2\pi{\rm i}\in{\rm i}\mathbb{R}
. In this case it follows from the definition that
\wp(x)
is real when
x\in\ln q\mathbb{Z}+{\rm i}\mathbb{R}
or
x\in\pi{\rm i}\mathbb{Z}+{\rm i}\mathbb{R}
. This means that rays
I_{1}=w((0,2\ln q])
,
I_{2}=w((0,2\pi\mathrm{i}])
and intervals
I_{3}=w([\pi\mathrm{i},\pi\mathrm{i}+2\ln q])
,
I_{4}=w([\ln q,\ln q+2\pi\mathrm{i}])
belong to the real line.

The derivative
\wp^{\prime}
of
\wp
has roots at
\pi\mathrm{i}
,
\ln q
and
\pi\mathrm{i}+\ln q
. It follows that
\wp
takes each value in the interior of
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
,
I_{4}
two times. Since
\wp
takes each value at most two times we deduce that interiors of
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
,
I_{4}
do not intersect.
It follows from the definition of
\wp
that
\wp
is positive on
(0,\varepsilon)
and negative on
(0,\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)
for small enough
\varepsilon>0
. Hence
I_{2}=(-\infty,e_{1}]
and
I_{1}=[e_{3},\infty)
. Since
I_{3}
intersects with
I_{2}
only in the end point we can write
I_{3}=[e_{3},e_{2}]
, where
e_{3}<e_{2}
. Since
I_{4}
interects with
I_{1}
,
I_{3}
only in the end points we can write
I_{2}=[e_{2},e_{1}]
, so that
e_{2}<e_{1}
.
We deduce that

\displaystyle w_{0}\big(qS^{1}\big)=\wp([\ln q,\ln q+2\pi\mathrm{i}])=[e_{2},e_{3}],

\displaystyle w_{0}\big(S^{1}\big)=\wp([0,2\pi\mathrm{i}])=[-\infty,e_{1}],

\displaystyle w_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})=\wp([0,2\ln q])=[e_{3},\infty],

\displaystyle w_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{<0})=\wp([\pi\mathrm{i},\pi\mathrm{i}+2\ln q])=[e_{1},e_{2}].

There exists a transcendental formula that expresses
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
,
e_{3}
through
2\pi{\rm i}
and
2\ln q
.
Theorem
3.3
says that elliptic function
w
gives a positive definite trace when
w(z)
and
P(z)w(qz)
are nonnegative on
S^{1}
.
There are six cases depending on the leading sign of
P
and
c_{0}
:
c_{0}\in[e_{3},\infty)
,
c_{0}\in[e_{2},e_{3}]
,
c_{0}\in(e_{1},e_{2}]
. This corresponds to roots on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
S^{1}
,
\mathbb{R}_{<0}
respectively.

Let
w_{1}=\frac{1}{w_{0}-c_{0}}
.
Consider the case when
c_{0}\in(e_{2},e_{3})
. In this case the roots
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
divide
S^{1}
into two contours
C_{+}
and
C_{-}
. Then
w_{1}\big(S^{1}\big)=\big[\frac{1}{e_{1}-c_{0}},0\big)
,
w_{1}(qC_{+})=\big[\frac{1}{e_{3}-c_{0}},\infty\big)
,
w_{1}(qC_{-})=\big({-}\infty,\frac{1}{e_{2}-c_{0}}\big)
.
The positivity condition for the elliptic function

is as follows:
w
is nonnegative on

,

is nonnegative on

and nonpositive on

, or vice versa, depending on the leading sign of

.
The space

consists of functions

for

. In the case when

the answer is

\bigg\{\lambda(a+w_{1})\bigg|\lambda>0,a\in\bigg[\frac{1}{c_{0}-e_{1}},\frac{1}{c_{0}-e_{2}}\bigg]\bigg\}.

In the case when

The other cases of

6.3
Nondegeneracy of
\boldsymbol{U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})}
-invariant traces

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a central reduction of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
:

\mathcal{A}=U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})/(C-c_{0}),

where
C=FE+\frac{Kq+K^{-1}q^{-1}}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}
. The adjoint action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on itself gives adjoint action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\mathcal{A}
. The subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{+}
of locally finite elements is generated by
F
,
K^{-1}
and
EK^{-1}
. Consider a trace
T_{0}
on
\mathcal{A}
and a
g_{q^{-2}}
-twisted trace
T(a)=T_{0}\big(K^{-1}a\big)=T_{0}\big(aK^{-1}\big)
. We note that
T
is invariant with respect to the adjoint action:

\displaystyle T\big(KaK^{-1}\big)=T(a)=\varepsilon(K)T(a),

\displaystyle T\big(Ea-KaK^{-1}E\big)=T_{0}\big(K^{-1}Ea-aK^{-1}E\big)=0,

\displaystyle T((Fa-aF)K)=T_{0}(Fa-aF)=0.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{+}
is the sum of all finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of type
1
and odd dimension, we deduce that
T|_{\mathcal{A}_{+}}
is uniquely defined up to a constant. This is easy to see directly: a space of
g_{q^{-2}}
-twisted traces on
\mathcal{A}
is two-dimensional, but one of the traces sends
\sum a_{i}K^{i}
to
a_{1}
, so the restriction of this trace on
\mathcal{A}_{+}
is zero.

We turn to the question when
T
gives a nondegenerate short star-product.

Proposition 6.2
.

Consider the bilinear form
(a,b)=T(ab)
. For
c_{0}
outside of a countable subset of
\mathbb{C}
the restriction of this form to
\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}
is nondegenerate for all
i
. More precisely, the following three statements are equivalent:

1.

The restriction of
(\cdot,\cdot)
to
\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}
is nondegenerate for all
i
.

2.

(\cdot,\cdot)
has trivial kernel.

3.

\mathcal{A}
does not have finite-dimensional representations.

In particular, for generic
c_{0}
the unique
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-invariant trace gives a nondegenerate short star-product on
A=\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits\mathcal{A}_{+}
.

Proof.

There are two representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of a given dimension
d
. Hence the set of
c_{0}
such that
\mathcal{A}
has a finite-dimensional representation is countable. So it is enough to prove equivalence of the three statements.

We start with
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
. It is enough to prove that
(1)
follows from
(2)
. Suppose that this is not the case and
(\cdot,\cdot)|_{\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}}
is degenerate for some
i
.
The multiplication map is
\operatorname{ad}
-equivariant, so the map
\phi\colon a\otimes b\mapsto T(ab)
is an
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-equivariant map from
\mathcal{A}_{+}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{+}
to
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{+}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}V_{2k}
, where
V_{l}
is a type
1
irreducible representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of dimension
l+1
. It follows from Schur lemma that the restriction of
\phi
to
V_{k}\otimes V_{l}
is either zero or nondegenerate bilinear map. In particular, for
k\neq l
it is zero.
It follows that
(\cdot,\cdot)
is denegerate on some
V_{i}
if and only if
T(ab)=0
for all
a\in V_{i}
,
b\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
. Note that
\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}=V_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{i}
. So if
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricted to
\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}
is degenerate for some
i
it follows that
(\cdot,\cdot)
has a nontrivial kernel.

Now we prove
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
. We start with
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
.
Suppose that
(\cdot,\cdot)
has a kernel
I
. Since
(a,b)=T(ab)
, for any
\operatorname{ad}K
-homogeneous
c\in\mathcal{A}
we have

\displaystyle(ac,b)=T(acb)=(a,cb),\qquad(ca,b)=T(cab)=\lambda T(abc)=(a,bc)

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. In particular,
I
is an ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{+}
. From the proof of
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
we see that
I
has a finite codimension in
\mathcal{A}_{+}
. Also
I
contains
V_{k}
for some
k
, hence it contains some powers of
E
and
FK^{-1}
.
Let
V
be a finite-dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathcal{A}_{+}
in the composition series of
\mathcal{A}_{+}/I
. We see that
E
and
FK^{-1}
act nilpotently on
V
.
Let
V_{0}
be the kernel of
K^{-1}
. It is easy to see that
V_{0}
is a subrepresentation of
\mathcal{A}_{+}
. So
V_{0}=V
or
V_{0}=\{0\}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathcal{A}_{+}
localized in
K^{-1}
we see that in the second case
V_{0}
is a finite-dimensional representation of
\mathcal{A}
.
Now we prove that the first case is impossible. Let
R
be a polynomial such that
EFK^{-1}=R\big(K^{-1}\big)
. We have
R(x)=\frac{x^{2}q+q^{-1}}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}+c_{0}x
. But
FK^{-1}
is nilpotent operator, so
EFK^{-1}
has a nontrivial kernel on
V_{0}
, while
R\big(K^{-1}\big)=\frac{q^{-1}}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}
does not. We get a contradiction.

It remains to prove
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
. Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
has a finite-dimensional representation
V
. Denote by
I
the intersection of its kernel with
\mathcal{A}_{+}
. This is a two-sided ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{+}
.
The quantum trace on
V
is an
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-invariant map from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathbb{C}
that is nonzero on
\mathbb{C}\big[K^{-1}\big]
. Therefore its restriction to
\mathcal{A}_{+}
coincides with
T
up to a multiplication by a scalar. It follows that
I
belongs to the kernel of
T
. The proposition follows.
∎

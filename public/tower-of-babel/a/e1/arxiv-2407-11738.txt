Strictly outer actions of locally compact groups: beyond the full factor case
Basile Morando

Abstract
We show that, given a continuous action α \alpha of a locally compact group G G on a factor M M , the relative commutant M ′ ∩ ( M ⋊ α G ) M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G) is contained in M ⋊ α H M\rtimes_{\alpha}H where H H is the subgroup of elements acting without spectral gap. As a corollary, we answer a question of Marrakchi and Vaes by showing that if M M is semifinite and α g \alpha_{g} is not approximately inner for all g ≠ 1 g\neq 1 , then M ′ ∩ ( M ⋊ α G ) = ℂ M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)=\mathbb{C} .



1
Introduction and results

Given a continuous action
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
of a locally compact group
G
on a von Neumann algebra
M
, we can build the
crossed product

M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
: this algebra is generated by a copy of
M
and a unitary representation of
G
such that, given
u_{g}
in the representation of
G
and
x
in the copy of
M
, we have
u_{g}xu_{g}^{*}=\alpha_{g}(x)
. Understanding how properties of the action reflect into properties of the algebra
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
and of the inclusion
M\subset M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a fundamental question in the theory of non-commutative dynamical systems. In this article, we focus on the following question:

Problem 1.

Find a criterion on
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
a continuous action of a locally compact group on a factor
M
, ensuring that
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
is trivial. Such an action is said to be
strictly outer
, and this property trivially implies factoriality of
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
.

When the group
G
is discrete, many properties of the crossed product can be deduced from the
Fourier decomposition
: every element of
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is uniquely written as a sum
\sum_{\gamma\in G}x_{\gamma}u_{\gamma}
(where
x_{\gamma}
and
u_{\gamma}
respectively refers to elements of the copy of
M
and
G
inside
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
). It allows in particular to answer problem
1
.

•

When
M=\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}\rtimes G
is just the group von Neumann algebra
L(G)
. This algebra is a factor if and only if
G
has infinite non-trivial conjugacy classes.

•

When
M
is a factor, the relative commutant
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
is trivial if and only if
\alpha
is
outer
: for all
\gamma\neq 1
in
G
, there is no unitary
u\in M
such that
\alpha_{\gamma}=\Ad(u)
.

•

More generally, consider the subgroup
H=\{\gamma\in G\mid\alpha_{\gamma}\text{ is inner}\}
. Then
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)\subset M\rtimes_{\alpha}H
. When
M
is a type
\II_{1}
factor, it implies that
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor if and only if the subgroup
H
of inner-acting elements of
G
is
G
-icc (for every
h\in H\setminus\{1\}
, the set
\{\gamma h\gamma^{-1}\mid\gamma\in G\}
is infinite).

In the non-discrete case, these questions become much more subtle:

•

Even in the simplest case where
M=\mathbb{C}
, we do not have any criterion for factoriality of
\mathbb{C}\rtimes G=L(G)
.

•

When
M
is a factor, it is still true that
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)=\mathbb{C}
implies that
\alpha
is outer. However, the converse is no longer true. For example, if
M
is a factor of type
\III_{0}
with trivial
T
-invariant, any action
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}^{+*}\curvearrowright M
by modular automorphisms is outer, but
M\rtimes_{\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}}\mathbb{R}^{+*}
is not even a factor. As explained in
[
12
, Example 8]
, there also exist outer actions
\alpha:\mathbb{S}^{1}\curvearrowright R
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
on the hyperfinite
\II_{1}
factor such that
R\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{S}^{1}
is not a factor either.

•

Consider
(M,\tau)
a
\II_{\infty}

being a semifinite faithful trace on

, and
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{+*}\curvearrowright M
a continuous action. Assume that

scales the trace, meaning that for all

. It trivially implies that

is outer, but the fact that

is a deep result of modular theory, the

[
8

(see also

As pointed out before, Theorem

Question 1 (
[
12

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be a continuous action of a locally compact group on a factor. Assume that for all

\alpha_{g}\notin\overline{\Inn}(M)

In this paper, we solve this question at least when
M
is a semifinite factor. This is a consequence of the following main theorem.

Theorem A.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be an action of a locally compact group on a
\sigma
-finite factor. Let
H=\{g\in G\mid L^{2}(\id)\prec L^{2}(\alpha_{g})\}<G
be the subgroup of elements acting without spectral gap. Then

M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)\subset M\rtimes_{\alpha}H.

This theorem does not answer Question
1
when
M
is a type
\III
factor because not every topologically outer action on a type
\III
factor is spectrally outer. However, this holds true when
M
is of type
\II_{1}
, which implies the following corollary:

Corollary B.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be a continuous action of a locally compact group on a
\sigma
-finite type
\II
factor. If
\alpha
is topologically outer (i.e. for all
g\in G\setminus\{1\}
,
\alpha_{g}\notin\overline{\Inn}(M)
), then
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)=\mathbb{C}
.

If
\alpha
is a trace scaling action on a
\II_{\infty}
factor, it is topologically outer. Therefore, Corollary
B
generalizes Connes-Takesaki’s relative commutant theorem. However, our proof of this corollary uses Connes-Takesaki’s result, and therefore we do not get a new proof of this deep result.

To establish Theorem
A
the key step is the following
uniform spectral gap for group actions
result:

Theorem C.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be a continuous action of a locally compact group on a
\sigma
-finite factor.
Let
g\in G
such that
L^{2}(\id)\nprec L^{2}(\alpha_{g})
. Then there exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
of
g
in
G
such that
L^{2}(\id)\nprec\bigoplus_{h\in\mathcal{O}}L^{2}(\alpha_{h})
.

Specializing this result in the
\II_{1}
factors case makes it clearer: saying that
g
is such that
L^{2}(\id)\nprec L^{2}(\alpha_{g})
is exactly saying that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in M
such that

\forall\xi\in L^{2}M,\sum_{i}\left\|\alpha_{g}(a_{i})\xi-\xi a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq\varepsilon\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}^{2}.

By Connes’ result, this is equivalent to
\alpha_{g}\notin\overline{\Inn}(M)
. Therefore, by continuity of the action, there is an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
of
g
in
G
such that for all
h\in\mathcal{O}
,
\alpha_{h}
possesses a similar spectral gap property, but a priori with
\varepsilon
and
a_{1},...,a_{n}
depending on the group element. Theorem
C
exactly says that it is possible to choose a neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
such that the spectral gap is uniform over
\mathcal{O}
: there exists
\varepsilon>0
,
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in M
such that for all
h
in
\mathcal{O}
,

\forall\xi\in L^{2}M,\sum_{i}\left\|\alpha_{h}(a_{i})\xi-\xi a_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq\varepsilon\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Whereas in the full case, this result (see
[
12
, lemma 1]
) is a direct consequence of a uniform spectral gap result
at the automorphism group level
by Jones
[
10
]
, it is not clear at all that such a property (or a satisfying weakening of it) remains true for non-full factors. We obtain Theorem
C
using different methods: we rely on the locally compact group structure of G to apply smoothing procedures to well-chosen states.

Preliminaries

Topologies on von Neumann algebras.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra,
M_{*}
its predual. By weak topology on
M
we mean the weak-
*
topology relative to
M_{*}
. The strong topology (resp. the
*
-strong topology) is the topology induced by the seminorms
\left\|x\right\|_{\varphi}\doteq\varphi(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
(resp.
\left\|x\right\|_{\varphi}^{\sharp}\doteq\varphi(x^{*}x)^{1/2}+\varphi(xx^{*})^{1/2}
) for
\varphi\in M_{*}^{+}
.

Automorphisms group.

The
automorphism group

\Aut(M)
acts on
M_{*}
by
\theta(\varphi)=\varphi\circ\theta
for
\theta\in\Aut(M)
and
\varphi\in M_{*}
. Following
[
9
]
, it is endowed with the
u
-topology which is the topology of pointwise norm convergence on
M_{*}
: a net
(\theta_{i})_{i\in I}
converges to
\theta
if and only if for all
\varphi\in M_{*}
,
\left\|\theta_{i}(\varphi)-\theta(\varphi)\right\|\to 0
. With this topology
\Aut(M)
is a topological group that is Polish when
M_{*}
is separable. The map

\displaystyle\Ad:\mathcal{U}(M)

\displaystyle\to\Aut(M)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto[x\mapsto uxu^{*}]

is continuous, and its image is the subgroup of
inner
automorphisms denoted by
\Inn(M)
. When
M
is a factor, we say that it is
full
if the map
\Ad
is open on its range
[
5
]
. When
M_{*}
is separable,
M
is full if and only if
\Inn(M)=\overline{\Inn}(M)
, where
\overline{\Inn}(M)
is the closure of
\Inn(M)
in the
u
-topology. The topological group of classes of outer automorphisms is defined by
\Out(M)=\Aut(M)/\Inn(M)
, and the quotient map is denoted by
p:\Aut(M)\to\Out(M)
. When
\Inn(M)
is not closed, we will consider
\underline{\Out}(M)=\Aut(M)/\overline{\Inn}(M)
and the corresponding quotient map
\underline{p}:\Aut(M)\to\underline{\Out}(M)
.

Crossed products.

We refer to
[
14
, Chapter X,§1]
for proofs and details.
Let
G
be a locally compact group. We denote by
\mu
one left Haar measure on it, by
\lambda:G\to L^{2}(G,\mu)
(resp.
\rho:G\to L^{2}(G,\mu)
) its left (resp. right) regular representation and by
L(G)
(resp.
R(G)
) the von Neumann algebra
\lambda(G)^{\prime\prime}
(resp.
\rho(G)^{\prime\prime}
). A
continuous action

\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
of
G
on a von Neumann algebra
M
is a continuous group homomorphism
\alpha:G\to\Aut(M)
.
The
crossed product
associated to the action
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
is the von Neumann algebra generated by a copy of
M
and a family of unitaries
\{u_{g}\mid g\in G\}
verifying
u_{g}xu_{g}^{*}=\alpha_{g}(x)
for
g\in G
and
x\in M
that admits a representation
\alpha:M\rtimes_{\alpha}G\to M\overline{\otimes}B(L^{2}G)
such that
\alpha(u_{g})=1\otimes\lambda_{g}
and
\alpha(x)=[s\mapsto\alpha_{s^{-1}}(x)]\in M\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}(G)\subset M\overline{\otimes}B(L^{2}G)
.

Smoothing procedure for von Neumann algebra valued functions.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be a continuous action on a von Neumann algebra. Given
x\in M
, the map
g\mapsto\alpha_{g}(x)
is weakly continuous, but generally not norm continuous. Consider
M_{\alpha}=\{x\in M\mid g\mapsto\alpha_{g}(x)\text{ is norm continuous}\}
:
M_{\alpha}
is an
\alpha
invariant
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
M
, and it turns out that
M_{\alpha}
is
*
-strongly dense in
M
. This is a consequence of the following useful smoothing procedure.

First, recall that we can integrate some von Neumann algebra valued functions, even if they are not Bochner measurable. Let
f:(X,\mu)\to M
be a map from a measure space to a von Neumann algebra with
x\mapsto\left\|f(x)\right\|
integrable. It is
weakly measurable
if for all
\omega\in M_{*}
,
\omega\circ f:X\to\mathbb{C}
is measurable. For such a function, there exists a unique element
\int_{X}fd\mu\in M
such that for all
\omega\in M_{*}
,
\omega(\int_{X}fd\mu)=\int_{X}(\omega\circ f)d\mu
. For all
x\in M
,
h\mapsto\alpha_{h}(x)
is weakly integrable and therefore, for any Borel
X\subset G
of non-zero finite Haar measure, we can define the
smoothing map

\alpha_{X}
by

\displaystyle\alpha_{X}:M

\displaystyle\rightarrow M

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}\alpha_{h}(x)d\mu(h).

For all
X\subset G
,
0<\mu(X)<\infty
and for all
x\in M
,
\alpha_{X}(x)\in M_{\alpha}
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{B}
is a neighborhood basis of the identity in
G
,
\lim_{U\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha_{U}(x)=x
in weak topology which implies the
*
-strong density of
M_{\alpha}
in
M
. Note that for any
x\in M_{\alpha}
,
h\mapsto\alpha_{h}(x)
is norm-continuous. Therefore, it is Bochner measurable which implies that for all
\omega\in M^{*}
and
x\in M_{\alpha}
,
\omega(\alpha_{X}(x))=\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}\omega(\alpha_{h}(x))d\mu(h)
(this does not hold for a generic element in
M
).

-cyclic unit vector

\omega\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{K}}=\varphi\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{H}}
.

•

\omega\circ\rho_{\mathcal{K}}=\varphi\circ\rho_{\mathcal{H}}
.

•

there exist
*
-strongly dense subsets
M_{0}\subset M
and
N_{0}\subset N
such that
\omega\circ\pi_{\mathcal{K}}=\varphi\circ\pi_{\mathcal{H}}
on
M_{0}\odot N_{0}^{\op}
.

Then
\omega\circ\pi_{\mathcal{K}}=\varphi\circ\pi_{\mathcal{H}}
on
M\odot N^{\op}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in M
,
b\in N
. Consider two nets
(a_{i})_{i\in I}\in(M_{0})^{I}
and
(b_{i})_{i\in I}\in(N_{0})^{I}
such that
a_{i}\displaystyle\mathop{\overset{*\text{-strongly}}{\longrightarrow}}_{i\rightarrow\infty}a
and
b_{i}\displaystyle\mathop{\overset{*\text{-strongly}}{\longrightarrow}}_{i\rightarrow\infty}b
. Then:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\omega(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i}^{\op}))-\omega(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;=\left\lvert\omega(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}((a_{i}-a)\otimes b_{i}^{\op}))+\omega(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(a\otimes(b_{i}-b)^{\op}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\left\|a_{i}-a\right\|_{\omega\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{K}}}\left\|(b_{i}^{\op})^{*}\right\|_{\omega\circ\rho_{\mathcal{K}}}+\left\|a\right\|_{\omega\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{K}}}\left\|(b_{i}^{\op}-b^{\op})^{*}\right\|_{\omega\circ\rho_{\mathcal{K}}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\left\|a_{i}-a\right\|_{\varphi\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}\left\|b_{i}^{\op}\right\|_{\varphi\circ\rho_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}+\left\|a\right\|_{\varphi\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}\left\|(b_{i}-b)^{\op}\right\|_{\varphi\circ\rho_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}\displaystyle\mathop{\overset{}{\longrightarrow}}_{i\rightarrow\infty}0

\displaystyle\left\lvert\omega(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i}^{\op}))-\varphi(\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;=\left\lvert\varphi(\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i}^{\op}))-\varphi(\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\left\lvert\varphi(\pi_{\mathcal{H}}((a_{i}-a)\otimes b_{i}^{\op}))+\varphi(\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(a\otimes(b_{i}-b^{\op})))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\left\|a_{i}-a\right\|_{\varphi\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}\left\|b_{i}^{\op}\right\|_{\varphi\circ\rho_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}+\left\|a\right\|_{\varphi\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}\left\|(b_{i}-b)^{\op}\right\|_{\varphi\circ\rho_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sharp}\displaystyle\mathop{\overset{}{\longrightarrow}}_{i\rightarrow\infty}0.

∎

Proof of Theorem
C
.

Let
g\in G
, and denote by
\mathcal{B}
a basis of open neighborhoods of
g
in
G
. Assume that for all
\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{B}
,
L^{2}(\id)\prec\bigoplus_{\theta\in\mathcal{O}}L^{2}(\theta)
or equivalently that
L^{2}(\id)\prec L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}})
. Because
M
is
\sigma
-finite, we can choose
\varphi\in M_{*}^{+}
a faithful normal state on
M
. Using Proposition
2.1
we choose for each
\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{B}
a state
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}\in B(L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}}))^{*}
such that
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))=\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle
for all
a,b\in M
. It induces a net
(\tilde{\omega}_{\mathcal{O}})_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{B}}
of states in
B(L^{2}M)^{*}
defined by
\tilde{\omega}_{\mathcal{O}}(x)\doteq\omega_{\mathcal{O}}(1_{\mathcal{O}}\otimes x)
for
x\in B(L^{2}M)
. Consider
\tilde{\omega}
a weak-
*
accumulation point of
(\tilde{\omega}_{\mathcal{O}})_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{B}}
. Let
a\in M_{\alpha}
,
b\in M
: for any
\varepsilon>0
, we can find
\Omega\in\mathcal{B}
small enough such that for all
h\in\Omega
,
\left\|\alpha_{g}^{-1}(a)-\alpha_{h}^{-1}(a)\right\|\leq\varepsilon
. Then:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))-\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{O}\to\infty}\left\lvert\omega_{\mathcal{O}}(1_{\mathcal{O}}\otimes\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))-\omega_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{O}\to\infty}\left\lvert\omega_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\big[1_{\mathcal{O}}\otimes\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes 1)-\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}}}(a\otimes 1)\big](\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}}}(1\otimes b^{\op}))\Big)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{O}\to\infty}\left\|(1_{\mathcal{O}}\otimes\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes 1)-\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}}}(a\otimes 1))\right\|\left\|\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\mathcal{O}}}(1\otimes b^{\op})\right\|

\displaystyle\;\leq\left\|1_{\Omega}\otimes\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes 1)-\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\Omega}}(a\otimes 1)\right\|\left\|\pi_{\alpha_{\mid\Omega}}(1\otimes b^{\op})\right\|

\displaystyle\;\leq\varepsilon\left\|1\otimes b^{\op}\right\|.

This being true for all
\varepsilon>0
, we get that for all
a\in M_{\alpha}
and
b\in M
,

\tilde{\omega}(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))=\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle.

(3.1)

Now we smooth the state
\tilde{\omega}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a base of open neighborhoods of
1
in
G
, and consider
\omega
an accumulation point of
(\tilde{\omega}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathcal{V}})_{\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{C}}
.

For
b\in M
:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\omega(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(1\otimes b^{\op}))-\langle\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathcal{V}}(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(1\otimes b^{\op}))-\langle\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\left\lvert\langle\varphi^{1/2}(\alpha_{\mathcal{V}}(b)-b),\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;=0.

For
a\in M
:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\omega(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes 1))-\langle a\varphi^{1/2},\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathcal{V}}(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes 1))-\langle a\varphi^{1/2},\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\left\lvert\langle(\alpha_{g\mathcal{V}g^{-1}}(a)-a)\varphi^{1/2},\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;=0.

For
a,b\in M_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\omega(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}(a\otimes b^{\op}))-\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert=\left\lvert\omega(\pi_{\id}(\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a)\otimes b^{\op}))-\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(\mathcal{V})}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\tilde{\alpha}_{h}[\pi_{\id}(\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a)\otimes b^{\op})]d\mu(h)\right)-\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\left\lvert\frac{1}{\mu(\mathcal{V})}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\tilde{\omega}\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{h}[\pi_{\id}(\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a)\otimes b^{\op})]\right)d\mu(h)-\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

(since
\pi_{\id}(\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a)\otimes b^{\op})\in B(L^{2}M)_{\tilde{\alpha}}
)

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\left\lvert\frac{1}{\mu(\mathcal{V})}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\tilde{\omega}\Big(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}\Big(\alpha_{ghg^{-1}}(a)\otimes\alpha_{h}(b)^{\op}\Big)\Big)d\mu(h)-\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mu(\mathcal{V})}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}\Big(\pi_{\alpha_{g}}\Big(\alpha_{ghg^{-1}}(a)\otimes\alpha_{h}(b)^{\op}\Big)\Big)-\langle a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert d\mu(h)

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mu(\mathcal{V})}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\left\lvert\langle\alpha_{ghg^{-1}}(a)\varphi^{1/2}\alpha_{h}(b)-a\varphi^{1/2}b,\varphi^{1/2}\rangle\right\rvert d\mu(h)

(by (

), since

\displaystyle\;\leq\limsup_{\mathcal{V}\to\infty}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{V}}\left(\|\alpha_{ghg^{-1}}(a)-a\|\left\|\alpha_{h}(b)\right\|+\left\|a\right\|\left\|\alpha_{h}(b)-b\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\;=0.

Now we can apply lemma

:

on

and thus

The following result explains how structure on the algebra of intertwiners of the action bimodule can be used to locate the relative commutant
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
in the crossed product by a subgroup
M\rtimes_{\alpha}H
.

Lemma 3.2.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be a continuous action of a locally compact group on a factor. Let
H<G
be a closed subgroup such that
1\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}(H\setminus G)\subset\mathcal{Z}(\End(L^{2}(\alpha)))
. Then

M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)\subset M\rtimes_{\alpha}H.

Proof.

Recall that

\End(L^{2}(\alpha))=\alpha(M)^{\prime}\cap(M^{\op}\otimes 1)^{\prime}\cap(B(L^{2}M)\overline{\otimes}B(L^{2}G))=\alpha(M)^{\prime}\cap(M\overline{\otimes}B(L^{2}G))

and that the crossed product
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
can be realized as the algebra of fixed points in
M\overline{\otimes}B(L^{2}G)
under the action
\alpha\otimes\Ad(\rho)
(cf
[
14
, X.1.22]
). Using the
\alpha\otimes\Ad(\rho)
invariance of
\alpha(M)
, we obtain that
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)=\End(L^{2}(\alpha))^{\alpha\otimes\Ad(\rho)}
. Then:

\displaystyle M^{\prime}

\displaystyle\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)=(\End(L^{2}(\alpha))\cap\mathcal{Z}(\End(L^{2}(\alpha)))^{\prime})^{\alpha\otimes\Ad(\rho)}

\displaystyle\subset\alpha(M)^{\prime}\cap(1\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}(H\setminus G))^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)

\displaystyle\subset(1\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}(H\setminus G))^{\prime}\cap M\rtimes_{\alpha}G

\displaystyle=M\rtimes_{\alpha}H

(
by
[
4
, Prop. 2.2]
.)

∎

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Using Theorem
C
we show that if
H=\{g\in G\mid L^{2}(\id)\prec L^{2}(\alpha_{g})\}
, then

\mathbb{C}\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}(G/H)\subset\mathcal{Z}(\End(L^{2}(\alpha))).

Noticing that
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
, lemma
3.2
concludes the proof of Theorem
A
.

Set
\mathcal{N}\doteq\End(L^{2}(\alpha))
. Take
K
a compact subset of
G
. We want to establish that
1\otimes 1_{KH}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{N})
. Because
M
is a factor,
\mathcal{N}\cap(1\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}G)^{\prime}=(1\overline{\otimes}L^{\infty}G)
and consequently
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{N})\subset 1\otimes L^{\infty}(G)
. Consider
A\subset G
such that
z(1\otimes 1_{KH})=1\otimes 1_{A}
, where
z(\cdot)
is the central support in
\mathcal{N}
. Let
L
be the support of
\mu_{\mid A}
, and take
g_{0}\in L
: then
L^{2}(\alpha_{g_{0}})\prec L^{2}(\alpha_{L})\sim L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid A})\sim L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid KH})
(because
L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid A})
and
L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid KH})
are quasi-equivalent by construction).

Now assume that
g_{0}\notin KH
. Take any
g\in KH
:
g_{0}^{-1}g\notin H
. By Theorem
C
there exists an open neighborhood
U_{g}
of
g
such that
L^{2}(\id)\nprec L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid g_{0}^{-1}U_{g}})
. Taking the tensor product by
L^{2}(\alpha_{g_{0}})
we get an open covering
(U_{g})_{g\in G}
of
KH
such that for all
g\in G
,
L^{2}(\alpha_{g_{0}})\nprec L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid U_{g}})
. Denote by
q:G\to G/H
the quotient map, and recall that it is continuous and open. Hence, our open covering induces an open covering
q(K)\subset\bigcup_{g\in G}q(U_{g})
. We extract a finite covering
q(K)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{g_{i}}
, and pull it back:
KH\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{g_{i}}H
. For
1\leq i\leq n
,
L^{2}(\alpha_{g_{0}})\nprec L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid U_{g_{i}}})\sim L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid U_{g_{i}}H})
(lemma
2.5
), hence
L^{2}(\alpha_{g_{0}})\nprec\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid U_{g_{i}}H})\sim L^{2}(\alpha_{\mid\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{g_{i}}H})
, and therefore
L^{2}(\alpha_{g_{0}})\nprec L^{2}(KH)
, which is a contradiction.

Thus,
g_{0}\in KH
, so
L\subset KH
,
\mu(A\setminus KH)=0
and finally
1\otimes 1_{KH}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{N})
.
∎

Proof of Corollary

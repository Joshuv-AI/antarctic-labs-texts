A primal dual formulation through a proximal approach for non-convex variational optimization
Fabio Silva Botelho

Abstract
This article develops a primal dual formulation for a primal proximal approach suitable for a large class of non-convex models in the calculus of variations. The results are established through standard tools of functional analysis, convex analysis and duality theory and are applied to a Ginzburg-Landau type model. Finally, in the last two sections, we present concerning optimality conditions and another related duality principle for the model in question.



1
Introduction

We start this article by justifying the suitability of the proximal approach for the concerning model.

Consider a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the functional
J:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
where

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u^{2}-\beta)^{2}\;dx

(1)

\displaystyle-\langle u,f\rangle_{L^{2}},\;\forall u\in U=W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

We could write such a functional as

J(u)=G_{1}(u,0)+F_{1}(u),\;\forall u\in U,

where

G_{1}(u,v)=\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u^{2}-\beta+v)^{2}\;dx-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx,

and

F_{1}(u)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx-\langle u,f\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Among other possibilities, we could define the dual functional as

J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*})=-G^{*}_{1}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*})-F^{*}(v^{*}),

where

G_{1}^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(v^{*})^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)}\;dx+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}(v_{0}^{*})^{2}\;dx+\beta\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{*}\;dx,

and

F^{*}_{1}(v^{*})=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}(v^{*}-f)^{2}\;dx

Through the variation in
v_{0}^{*}
we obtain

\frac{(v^{*})^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)^{2}}-\frac{v_{0}^{*}}{\alpha}-\beta=\mathbf{0},

intending to obtain conditions for a solution
v_{0}^{*}(v^{*})
and thus to obtain a final functional as a function of
v^{*}
with a possible large region of convexity (in fact concavity) due the term

F^{*}_{1}(v^{*})=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}(v^{*}-f)^{2}\;dx

with a small value for
\varepsilon>0
.

The issue is that if the term

-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon

corresponds to an undefined matrix (this is a common situation for the case of local minima for the primal formulation) we may not have the hypothesis of the implicit function theorem satisfied so that critical points of the dual formulation may not correspond to critical points of the primal one and reciprocally.

Indeed, we may obtain for the second variation of
J^{*}
in
v_{0}^{*}

\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(v_{0}^{*})}{\partial(v_{0}^{*})^{2}}=-4\frac{(v^{*})^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)^{3}}-\frac{1}{\alpha},

Observe that for a critical point denoting

u=\frac{(v^{*})}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)}

we have

v_{0}^{*}=\alpha\left(\left(\frac{(v^{*})}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)}\right)^{2}-\beta\right)=\alpha(u^{2}-\beta),

so that

\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(v_{0}^{*})}{\partial(v_{0}^{*})^{2}}=-4\frac{u^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)}-\frac{1}{\alpha}

and thus

\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(v_{0}^{*})}{\partial(v_{0}^{*})^{2}}=\frac{-4\alpha u^{2}+\gamma\nabla^{2}-2v_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)\alpha}=\frac{-\delta^{2}J(u)+\varepsilon}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon)\alpha}.

Therefore if for a critical point where

\delta^{2}J(u)-\varepsilon>\mathbf{0}

the term

-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}-\varepsilon

corresponds to an undefined matrix, we have that

\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(v_{0}^{*})}{\partial(v_{0}^{*})^{2}}

is also undefined and the hypothesis of the implicit function theorem may not be satisfied, in order to obtain
v_{0}^{*}(v^{*})
. The other issue is that

\delta^{2}J(v^{*},v_{0}^{*})

may also be undefined at a critical point, so that we do not have a qualitative correspondence between the primal and dual critical points.

So this may lead us, for a large class of similar models, through such a formulation, to wrong results concerning the equivalence of critical points for the primal and dual formulations.

In order to solve this problem, in this article we propose a kind of proximal variational formulation with exact penalization. Thus, with such facts in mind, we propose as the primal dual equivalent formulation for the original primal problem in question, the following functional
\hat{J}:U\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
where

\displaystyle\hat{J}(u,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u^{2}-\beta)^{2}\;dx

(2)

\displaystyle+\frac{K}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-p)^{2}\;dx-\langle u,f\rangle_{L^{2}}

We highlight the proximal term

\frac{K}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-p)^{2}\;dx

makes the primal formulation convex in
u
for appropriate values of
K>0
.

In the next section we present the theoretical results for a duality principle concerning such a proximal formulation. We believe through an analysis of the proof of the next theorem the suitability of such a proximal
formulation will be clarified.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an open, bounded and connected set with a regular (Lipschitzian)
boundary denoted by
\partial\Omega
. Even though we have not relabeled the functionals and operators, consider a finite dimensional approximation for the model in question, in a finite elements or finite differences context, where we define the functionals
\hat{J}:U\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

and
J:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, by

\displaystyle\hat{J}(u,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u^{2}-\beta)^{2}\;dx

(3)

\displaystyle+\frac{K}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-p)^{2}\;dx-\langle u,f\rangle_{L^{2}}

and

J(u)=\hat{J}(u,u),

where

U=W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega),

Y=Y^{*}=L^{2}(\Omega),

\alpha>0,\beta>0,\gamma>0
,
K>0
and
f\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}).

Furthermore, for a sufficiently small parameter
\varepsilon>0
, define
G:U\times Y\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle G(u,v,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u^{2}-\beta+v)^{2}\;dx

(4)

\displaystyle-\langle u,Kp\rangle_{L^{2}}+\frac{K}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx,

F:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

F(u)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx+\langle u,f\rangle_{L^{2}}

and
H:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

H(p)=\frac{K}{2}\int_{\Omega}p^{2}\;dx,

so that

\hat{J}(u,p)=G(u,0,p)-F(u)+H(p),\;\forall(u,p)\in U\times Y.

Define also,
G^{*}:Y^{*}\times Y^{*}\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle G^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in U}\sup_{v\in Y}\{\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle v,v_{0}^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}-G(u,v,p)\}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(v^{*}+Kp)^{2}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}\;dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}(v_{0}^{*})^{2}\;dx+\beta\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{*}\;dx,

if
v_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}
where

B^{*}=\left\{v_{0}^{*}\in Y^{*}\;:\;-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon>\frac{K}{2}\right\},

F^{*}:Y^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
where

\displaystyle F^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in Y}\{\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}-F(u)\}

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}(v^{*}-f)^{2}\;dx.

and
J^{*}:Y^{*}\times B^{*}\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)=-G^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)+F^{*}(v^{*})+H(p),\;\forall(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)\in Y^{*}\times B^{*}\times Y.

Under such hypotheses,

1.

Assume
u_{0}\in U
is such that
\delta J(u_{0})=\mathbf{0}
and define

\hat{v}_{0}^{*}=\alpha(u_{0}^{2}-\beta),

\hat{v}^{*}=\varepsilon u_{0}+f,

\hat{p}=u_{0}

under such assumptions,

\delta J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})=\mathbf{0}.

(a)

Assume also
\delta^{2}J(u_{0})>\mathbf{0}
and
\hat{v}_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}
.
Under such additional hypotheses, there exist
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in B_{r_{1}}(u_{0})}J(u)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v^{*}\in B_{r_{3}}(\hat{v}^{*})}\left\{\inf_{p\in B_{r_{2}}(\hat{p})}\left\{\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p}).

Moreover, defining
J_{3}^{*}:B_{r_{3}}(\hat{v}^{*})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J_{3}^{*}(v^{*})=\inf_{p\in B_{r_{2}}(\hat{p})}\left\{\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)\right\}

we have that

\delta J^{*}_{3}(\hat{v}^{*})=\mathbf{0}

\delta^{2}J_{3}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})>\mathbf{0}

so that

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in B_{r_{1}}(u_{0})}J(u)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v^{*}\in B_{r_{3}}(\hat{v}^{*})}J_{3}^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{3}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*}).

(b)

Suppose
\delta^{2}J(u_{0})<\mathbf{0}
and
\hat{v}_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}
.
Under such additional hypotheses, there exist
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in B_{r_{1}}(u_{0})}J(u)

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v^{*}\in B_{r_{3}}(\hat{v}^{*})}\left\{\sup_{p\in B_{r_{2}}(\hat{p})}\left\{\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p}).

Moreover, defining
J_{5}^{*}:B_{r_{3}}(\hat{v}^{*})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J_{5}^{*}(v^{*})=\sup_{p\in B_{r_{2}}(\hat{p})}\left\{\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)\right\}

we have

\delta J_{5}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})=\mathbf{0}

\delta^{2}J_{5}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})>\mathbf{0}

so that

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in B_{r_{1}}(u_{0})}J(u)

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v^{*}\in B_{r_{3}}(\hat{v}^{*})}J_{5}^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{5}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*}).

(c)

For this item define
A^{+}
by

A^{+}=\{v_{0}^{*}\in Y^{*}\;:\;-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2v_{0}^{*}>\mathbf{0}\}.

Assume
\hat{v}_{0}^{*}\in A^{+}\cap B^{*}
.

Under such additional assumptions and definitions, we have

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in U}J(u)

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{(v^{*},p)\in Y^{*}\times Y}\left\{\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in A^{+}\cap B^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p}).

Moreover, defining
J_{7}^{*}:Y^{*}\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J_{7}^{*}(v^{*},p)=\left\{\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in A^{+}\cap B^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p)\right\}

we have

\delta J_{7}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})=\mathbf{0}

\delta^{2}J_{7}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})>\mathbf{0}

so that

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in U}J(u)

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{(v^{*},p)\in Y^{*}\times Y}J_{7}^{*}(v^{*},p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{7}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p}).

Proof.

Suppose
u_{0}\in U
is such that
\delta J(u_{0})=\mathbf{0}.

We shall start by proving that

\delta J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})=\mathbf{0}.

Observe that from

\delta J(u_{0})=0

we have that

-\gamma\nabla^{2}u_{0}+2\alpha(u_{0}^{2}-\beta)u_{0}-f=0,\text{ in }\Omega,

so that

-\gamma\nabla^{2}u_{0}+2\alpha(u_{0}^{2}-\beta)u_{0}-\varepsilon u_{0}+Ku_{0}+\varepsilon u_{0}-Ku_{0}-f=0,

that is

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}=\varepsilon u_{0}+f+Ku_{0}=-\gamma\nabla^{2}u_{0}+2\alpha(u_{0}^{2}-\beta)u_{0}+\varepsilon u_{0}+Ku_{0}.

(13)

Thus,

u_{0}=\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon},

so that

u_{0}=\frac{\hat{v}^{*}-f}{\varepsilon}=\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}.

Therefore

\frac{\hat{v}^{*}-f}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}=\mathbf{0},

and consequently we may infer that

\frac{\partial J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial v^{*}}=\mathbf{0}.

On the other hand

\frac{\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\alpha}=(u_{0}^{2}-\beta)=\left(\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}-\beta,

so that

-\frac{\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\alpha}+\left(\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}-\beta=0,

that is,

\frac{\partial J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial\hat{v}^{*}_{0}}=\mathbf{0}.

Moreover

K\hat{p}=Ku_{0}=K\left(\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}\right),

so that

K\hat{p}-K\left(\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}\right)=0,

that is,

\frac{\partial J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p}=\mathbf{0}.

From these last results, we have that

\delta J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})=\mathbf{0}.

Also

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J^{*}_{3}(\hat{v}^{*})}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial v^{*}}

(14)

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial v^{*}_{0}}\frac{\partial\hat{v}^{*}_{0}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p}\frac{\partial\hat{p}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}.

Similarly we may obtain

\frac{\partial J^{*}_{5}(\hat{v}^{*})}{\partial v^{*}}=\mathbf{0},

and

\delta J^{*}_{7}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})=\mathbf{0}.

From the relations between the primal and dual variables, as a by-product of the Legendre transform proprieties we may obtain

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-G^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})+F^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})+H(\hat{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(u_{0},\mathbf{0},\hat{p})-F(u_{0})+H(\hat{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{J}(u_{0},\hat{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J(u_{0}).

Suppose now

\delta^{2}J(u_{0})>\mathbf{0}.

Define
J_{8}^{*}:Y^{*}\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J_{8}^{*}(v^{*},p)=\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},v_{0}^{*},p).

In particular we have got

J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})=\sup_{v_{0}^{*}\in B^{*}}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},v_{0}^{*},\hat{p})=J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*}.\hat{p}).

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p^{2}}

(16)

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p\partial v_{0}^{*}}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial p}.

At this point we recall that

\frac{\partial J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial v_{0}^{*}}=\mathbf{0},

so that

\left(\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}-\frac{\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\alpha}-\beta=\mathbf{0}

Hence, taking the variation in
p
of such a last equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2K(\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p})}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle-4\frac{(\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p})^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)^{3}}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial p}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial p}=\mathbf{0}.

(17)

so that

\displaystyle\frac{2Ku_{0}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle-4\frac{(u_{0})^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial p}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial p}=\mathbf{0}.

(18)

and thus

\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial p}=\frac{2\alpha Ku_{0}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}.

From this we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p^{2}}

(19)

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p\partial v_{0}^{*}}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K-\frac{K^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2(\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p})K}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)^{2}}\frac{2\alpha Ku_{0}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K-\frac{K^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

(20)

\displaystyle+\frac{4\alpha K^{2}u_{0}^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

so that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K-\frac{K^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon)}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K\frac{\delta^{2}J(u_{0})+\varepsilon}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}.

Summarizing,

\frac{\partial^{2}J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial p^{2}}>\mathbf{0}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}

(22)

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial v^{*}\partial v_{0}^{*}}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}.

As above indicated,

\left(\frac{\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p}}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}-\frac{\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\alpha}-\beta=\mathbf{0}

Hence, taking the variation in
v^{*}
of such a last equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2(\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p})}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle-4\frac{(\hat{v}^{*}+K\hat{p})^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)^{3}}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}=\mathbf{0}.

(23)

so that

\displaystyle\frac{2u_{0}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle-4\frac{(u_{0})^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}\frac{\partial v_{0}^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}=\mathbf{0}.

(24)

so that

\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}=\frac{2\alpha u_{0}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+4\alpha u_{0}^{2}+2\hat{v}_{0}^{*}+K+\varepsilon)}.

From this we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J_{8}^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}

(25)

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*},\hat{v}_{0}^{*},\hat{p})}{\partial v^{*}\partial v_{0}^{*}}\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{0}^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(v^{*}+f)^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+K)}\;dx,

F^{*}:D^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

F^{*}(v^{*})=\sup_{u\in U_{1}\cap B^{+}}\{\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}-F(u)\}

and
J^{*}:D^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J^{*}(v^{*})=-G^{*}(v^{*})+F^{*}(v^{*})

Assume now either

f(x)>0,\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}

or

f(x)<0,\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Define

A^{+}=\{u\in U_{1}\;:\;u\;f>0\text{ in }\Omega\}

and define also

D_{1}^{*}=\left\{v^{*}\in D^{*}\;:\;\hat{u}=\frac{\partial F_{1}^{*}(v^{*})}{\partial v^{*}}\in A^{+}\right\},

where

F_{1}^{*}(v^{*})=\sup_{u\in U}\{\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}-F(u)\}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the hypotheses state above
J^{*}
is convex on
D_{1}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
v^{*}\in D^{*}_{1}
.

Thus

\displaystyle F^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in U_{1}\cap B^{+}}\{\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}-F(u)\}

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in U}\{\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}-F(u)

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\;dx+6\alpha\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\varphi^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle-2\alpha\beta\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\;dx-\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{2}(u^{2}-\sqrt[2]{K})\;dx\},

for some appropriate Lagrange multipliers
(\varphi,\varphi_{1})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega).

The last supremum is attained for some
\hat{u}\in U
such that

v^{*}-\frac{\partial F(\hat{u})}{\partial u}+\varphi^{2}(12)\alpha\hat{u}-\varphi_{1}^{2}(2\hat{u})=0,\text{ in }\Omega.

Taking the variation in
v^{*}
in this last equation, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}-\frac{\partial^{2}F(\hat{u})}{\partial u^{2}}\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle+\varphi^{2}(12\alpha)\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}-\varphi_{1}^{2}2\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle+24\alpha\varphi\partial_{v^{*}}\varphi\hat{u}-12\varphi_{1}\partial_{v^{*}}\varphi_{1}\hat{u}=0,\text{ in }\Omega.

(59)

On the other hand we have the following necessary condition to be satisfied

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\;dx+6\alpha\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\varphi^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle-2\alpha\beta\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\;dx=0,

(60)

so that

2\varphi\partial_{u}\varphi(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}-2\alpha\beta)+\varphi^{2}(12\alpha\hat{u})=0

\text{ in }\Omega
so that

\varphi^{2}(12\alpha\hat{u})=0\text{ in }\Omega.

And also, we must have

\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{2}(u^{2}-\sqrt[2]{K})\;dx=0,

so that

2\varphi_{1}\partial_{u}\varphi_{1}(\hat{u}^{2}-\sqrt[2]{K})+2\varphi_{1}^{2}\hat{u}=0,\text{ in }\Omega,

and thus

2\varphi_{1}^{2}\hat{u}=0,\text{ in }\Omega.

Since
v^{*}\in D^{*}
we may assume
\varphi_{1}=0
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v^{*}}{\partial v^{*}}-\frac{\partial^{2}F(\hat{u})}{\partial u^{2}}\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

(61)

\displaystyle+\varphi^{2}(12\alpha)\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}-\varphi_{1}^{2}2\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle+24\alpha\varphi\partial_{v^{*}}\varphi\hat{u}-12\varphi_{1}\partial_{v^{*}}\varphi_{1}\hat{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\partial^{2}F(\hat{u})}{\partial u^{2}}\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle+\varphi^{2}(12\alpha)\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}=0,\text{ in }\Omega.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\frac{\partial^{2}F(\hat{u})}{\partial u^{2}}-12\alpha\varphi^{2}}

(62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}+2\alpha\beta-12\alpha\varphi^{2}+K}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle F^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{u},v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}-F(\hat{u})

(63)

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\;dx+\int_{\Omega}6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}\varphi^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle-2\alpha\beta\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\;dx-\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{2}(\hat{u}^{2}-\sqrt[2]{K})\;dx,

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F^{*}(v^{*})}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{u}+\left(v^{*}-\frac{\partial F(\hat{u})}{\partial u}\right.

(64)

\displaystyle\left.+\varphi^{2}12\alpha\hat{u}-\varphi_{1}^{2}(2\hat{u})\right)\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

\displaystyle 2\varphi\partial_{v^{*}}\varphi(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}-2\alpha\beta)

\displaystyle-2\varphi_{1}\partial_{v^{*}}\varphi_{1}(\hat{u}^{2}-\sqrt[2]{K})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{u}.

Therefore, we may infer that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}F^{*}(v^{*})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{u}}{\partial v^{*}}

(65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}+2\alpha\beta-12\alpha\varphi^{2}+K}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}J^{*}(v^{*})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}G^{*}(v^{*})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}F^{*}(v^{*})}{\partial(v^{*})^{2}}

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+K}+\frac{1}{-6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}+2\alpha\beta-12\alpha\varphi^{2}+K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\gamma\nabla^{2}+6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}-2\alpha\beta+12\alpha\varphi^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+K)(-6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}+2\alpha\beta-12\alpha\varphi^{2}+K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}J(\hat{u})+12\alpha\varphi^{2}}{(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+K)(-6\alpha\hat{u}^{2}+2\alpha\beta-12\alpha\varphi^{2}+K)}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\mathbf{0},\;\forall v^{*}\in D_{1}^{*}

From this we may infer that
J^{*}
is convex on
D_{1}^{*}
.

∎

In the next lines we present our main result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\hat{v}^{*}\in D^{*}_{1}
be such that

\delta J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})=\mathbf{0}.

Assume either

f(x)>0,\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}

or

f(x)<0,\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Define

A^{+}=\{u\in U_{1}\;:\;u\;f>0\text{ in }\Omega\}

and

u_{0}=(-\gamma\nabla^{2}+K)^{-1}\hat{v}^{*}

Assume also

u_{0}\in A^{+}\cap B^{+}

and recall that

D_{1}^{*}=\left\{v^{*}\in D^{*}\;:\;\hat{u}=\frac{\partial F_{1}^{*}(v^{*})}{\partial v^{*}}\in A^{+}\right\}.

Under such hypotheses

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in U_{1}}J(u)

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v^{*}\in D^{*}_{1}}J^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*}).

Proof.

From the last theorem
J^{*}
is convex in
D_{1}^{*}
so that

J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})=\inf_{v^{*}\in D_{1}^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle J^{*}(v^{*})

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-G^{*}(v^{*})+F^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}+G(u)+F_{1}^{*}(v^{*})\;\forall u\in U_{1},\;v^{*}\in D^{*}_{1}.

Hence

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{v^{*}\in D_{1}^{*}}\{-\langle u,v^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}+G(u)+F_{1}^{*}(v^{*})\}

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(u)-F(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J(u),\;\forall u\in A^{+}.

Similarly as in the previous theorems proofs we may obtain

\inf_{u\in U_{1}}J(u)=\inf_{u\in A^{+}}J(u)\geq\inf_{v^{*}\in D_{1}^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*}).

(70)

On the other hand, also similarly as the proofs of the previous theorems we may obtain

\delta J(u_{0})=\mathbf{0}

and

J(u_{0})=J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*}).

From this and (
70
) we may infer that

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in U_{1}}J(u)

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v^{*}\in D^{*}_{1}}J^{*}(v^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{*}(\hat{v}^{*}).

The proof is complete.
∎

6
A final dual variational formulation

This final duality principle is summarized by the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
U,Y
be a Banach spaces such that
Y=Y^{*}
and let
\Lambda:U\rightarrow Y
be a bounded linear operator.

Consider the functional
J:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
expressed by

J(u)=G_{K}(\Lambda u)-F(\Lambda u)-\langle u,f\rangle_{U},

where
G_{K}:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

G_{K}(\Lambda u)=G(\Lambda u)+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u,\Lambda u\rangle_{Y}
where
G:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a coercive, Fréchet differentiable and possibly non-convex functional. Moreover
f\in U^{*}
and
F:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is such that

F(\Lambda u)=\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u,\Lambda u\rangle_{Y},

so that

J(u)=G(\Lambda u)-\langle u,f\rangle_{U}.

Assume

\inf_{u\in U}J(u)=\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

and
K>0
is such that
G_{K}
is convex.

Define the polar functionals
G^{*}_{K}:Y^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
F^{*}:Y^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

G_{K}^{*}(v^{*}+z^{*})=\sup_{v\in Y}\{\langle v,v^{*}+z^{*}\rangle_{Y}-G_{K}(v)\},

and

F^{*}(z^{*})=\sup_{v\in Y}\{\langle v,z^{*}\rangle_{Y}-F(v)\},

respectively

Define also
A^{*}=\{v^{*}\in Y^{*}\>:\;\Lambda^{*}v^{*}-f=\mathbf{0}\}

J^{*}(v^{*},z^{*})=-G^{*}_{K}(v^{*}+z^{*})+F^{*}(z^{*})

Suppose
(u_{0},v_{0}^{*},z_{0}^{*})\in U\times Y^{*}\times Y^{*}
is such that

\delta(J^{*}(v_{0}^{*},z^{*}_{0})+\langle u_{0},\Lambda^{*}v_{0}^{*}-f\rangle_{U})=\mathbf{0}.

Under such hypotheses, we have

\delta J(u_{0})=\mathbf{0}

and

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{u\in U}\left\{J(u)+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}\right\}

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{v^{*}\in A^{*}}\left\{J^{*}(v^{*},z^{*}_{0})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{*}(v_{0}^{*},z_{0}^{*}).

Proof.

Observe that from the variation of
J^{*}
in
u
we obtain

\Lambda^{*}v_{0}^{*}-f=\mathbf{0}

so that
v_{0}^{*}\in A^{*}.

Moreover from the variation of
J^{*}
in
v^{*}
we have

\frac{\partial G_{K}^{*}(v_{0}^{*}+z^{*}_{0})}{\partial v^{*}}=\Lambda u_{0}.

Also, from the variation of
J^{*}
in
z^{*}
we have

-\frac{\partial G_{K}^{*}(v_{0}^{*}+z^{*}_{0})}{\partial z^{*}}+\frac{\partial F^{*}(z_{0}^{*})}{\partial z^{*}}=\mathbf{0}.

Therefore

\Lambda u_{0}=\frac{\partial F^{*}(z_{0}^{*})}{\partial z^{*}},

so that from the Legendre transform properties

z_{0}^{*}=\frac{\partial F(\Lambda u_{0})}{\partial v}=K\Lambda u_{0}

where
v=\Lambda u.
Hence,

F^{*}(z_{0}^{*})=\langle\Lambda u_{0},z_{0}^{*}\rangle-F(\Lambda u_{0})=\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}.

Also from the Legendre transform properties we may obtain

v_{0}^{*}+z_{0}^{*}=\frac{\partial G_{K}(\Lambda u_{0})}{\partial v},

so that

\displaystyle v_{0}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G_{K}(\Lambda u_{0})}{\partial v}-z_{0}^{*}

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G_{K}(\Lambda u_{0})}{\partial v}-K\Lambda u_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G(\Lambda u_{0})}{\partial v}.

From this and

\Lambda^{*}v_{0}^{*}-f=\mathbf{0}

we have

\Lambda^{*}\left(\frac{\partial G(\Lambda u_{0})}{\partial v}\right)-f=\mathbf{0},

that is

\delta J(u_{0})=\mathbf{0}.

Once more through the Legendre transform properties, we get

G_{K}^{*}(v_{0}^{*}+z_{0}^{*})=\langle\Lambda u_{0},v_{0}^{*}+z_{0}^{*}\rangle_{Y}-G_{K}(\Lambda u_{0}),

and

F^{*}(z_{0}^{*})=\langle\Lambda u_{0},z_{0}^{*}\rangle_{Y}-F(\Lambda u_{0}),

so that

\displaystyle J^{*}(v_{0}^{*},z_{0}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-G_{K}^{*}(v_{0}^{*}+z_{0}^{*})+F^{*}(z_{0}^{*})

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle u_{0},\Lambda^{*}v_{0}^{*}\rangle_{U}+G_{K}(\Lambda u_{0})-F(\Lambda u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle u_{0},f\rangle_{U}+G(\Lambda u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J(u_{0}).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle J^{*}(v_{0}^{*},z_{0}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{-G_{K}^{*}(v^{*}_{0})+F^{*}(z^{*}_{0})\}

(75)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\langle\Lambda u,v_{0}^{*}+z_{0}^{*}\rangle_{Y}+G_{K}(\Lambda u)+F^{*}(z^{*}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\Lambda u)+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u,\Lambda u\rangle_{Y}-\langle u,f\rangle_{Y}-\langle\Lambda u,z_{0}^{*}\rangle_{Y}+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\Lambda u)-\langle u,f\rangle_{U}+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u,\Lambda u\rangle_{Y}-K\langle\Lambda u,\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\Lambda u)-\langle u,f\rangle_{U}+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J(u)+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y},\;\forall u\in U.

Summarizing, we have got

J^{*}(v_{0}^{*},z_{0}^{*})\leq\inf_{u\in U}\left\{J(u)+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}\right\}

(76)

Therefore, from

\delta J(u_{0})=\mathbf{0},

J(u_{0})=J^{*}(v_{0},z_{0}^{*}),

from (
76
) and the concavity of
J^{*}
in
v^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{u\in U}\left\{J(u)+\frac{K}{2}\langle\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0},\Lambda u-\Lambda u_{0}\rangle_{Y}\right\}

(77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{v^{*}\in A^{*}}J^{*}(v^{*},z^{*}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{*}(v_{0}^{*},z_{0}^{*}).

The proof is complete.
∎

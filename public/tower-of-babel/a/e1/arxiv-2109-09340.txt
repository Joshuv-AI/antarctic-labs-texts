Orthogonal pair and a rigidity problem for Segre maps between hyperquadrics
Yun Gao

Abstract
Being motivated by the orthogonal maps studied in [ GN1 ] , orthogonal pairs between the projective spaces equipped with possibly degenerate Hermitian forms were introduced. In addition, orthogonal pairs are generalizations of holomorphic Segre maps between Segre families of real hyperquadrics. We showed that non-degenerate holomorphic orthogonal pairs also have certain rigidity properties under a necessary codimension restriction. As an application, we got a rigidity theorem for Segre maps related to Heisenberg hypersurfaces obtained by Zhang in [ Zh ] .



1
Introduction

Let
U
be an open subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
M
be a real analytic
hypersurface in
U
with a defining function
\psi(z,\bar{z})
. The
complexification of
M
is defined as:

\mathcal{M}:=\{(z,\xi)\in U\times\mbox{Conj}(U):\psi(z,\xi)=0\}
.
Define the complex analytic varieties
Q_{\xi}:=\{z\in U:\psi(z,\xi)=0\}

for
\xi\in\mbox{Conj}(U)
and
\hat{Q}_{z}:=\{\xi\in\mbox{Conj}(U):\psi(z,\xi)=0\}

for
z\in M
. Then
Q_{\xi}
and
\widehat{Q}_{z}
are called Segre varieties
associated with
M
. And the complex hypersurface
\mathcal{M}
is called
the Segre family of
M
. The term “family” comes from the
fact that
\mathcal{M}
is foliated by
\{Q_{\xi}\}
as
\xi
varies
over
U
. Segre varieties have been basic tools in the study of CR
mappings between real analytic hypersurfaces as originated in the
paper of Webster
[
We
]
. (See also [Fa1], [BER], [Hu1], [Hu2],
[Zh],etc, for many related references.)

Let
\widetilde{M}
be also a real analytic hypersurface
in
\widetilde{U}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
be its associated
Segre family. Let
F
be a holomorphic map from
\mathcal{M}
into

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. If for
(z,\xi)\in\mathcal{M}
,
there exists
(z^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}

such that
F((\{z\},\hat{Q}_{z}))\subset(\{z^{\prime}\},\hat{Q}_{z^{\prime}})

and
F((Q_{\xi},\{\xi\}))\subset(Q_{\xi^{\prime}},\{\xi^{\prime}\})
,
then
F
is called a
holomorphic Segre map
. It is known that

F
is always of the form

F(z,\xi)=(f_{1}(z),f_{2}(\xi))
(
[
Fa2
]
, (
[
HJ
]
, Lemma 6.1)). A basic property for Segre families is its invariance for
holomorphic maps from
M
into
\tilde{M}
. Namely, a holomorphic
map from
M
into
\widetilde{M}
induces a holomorphic Segre map
from
\mathcal{M}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. Segre maps, as well
as their rigidity problems, have been studied by many authors. In
[
Zh
]
, Zhang proved a rigidity theorem for holomorphic Segre
maps from
\mathcal{H}^{n}
to
\mathcal{H}^{N}
where
\mathcal{H}^{n}

denotes the Segre family associated to the Heisenberg hypersurface

\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n-1},\zeta)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:Im(\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}z_{j}\bar{z}_{j}\}
.
In

[
An2
]
and
[
An2
]
, Angle gave several sufficient conditions insuring that
the Segre maps between the complexification of real analytic
hypersurfaces are Segre transversal or null.

In
[
GN1
]
, the authors studied various rigidity properties for
orthogonal maps that generalize holomorphic proper maps between
hyperquadrics. To be more specific,
we let
r,s,t\in\mathbb{N}
and denote by
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
for the
Euclidean space equipped with the standard (possibly degenerate)
Hermitian bilinear form whose eigenvalues are
+1
,
-1
and
0

with multiplicities
r
,
s
and
t
, respectively. Write its
projectivization as
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}:=\mathbb{P}\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
.
When
t=0
, we use the notation
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
instead of
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,0}
. A local holomorphic map
f
from
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}

into
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
is called an local orthogonal map if
f(z)\perp f(w)
for any
z
,
w\in U
such that
z\perp w
.
Motivated by this definition, we then define a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}

into
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
to be a pair of local holomorphic maps

f_{1}
,
f_{2}
from
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
into
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
such that
f_{1}(z)\perp f_{2}(w)
whenever
z\perp w
for

z,w\in U
. It is easy to find that
f
is a local orthogonal map from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
if and only if
(f,f)
is a local orthogonal pair.
In addition, a Segre map is easily seen to be a local
orthogonal pair in this sense. Our main rigidity result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}\leq 2(r+s)-3
. Then any local orthogonal pair
from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
is either null or
quasi-standard.

Here, a local orthogonal pair
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
is called a null pair
if
f_{1}(z)\perp f_{2}(w)
for any
(z,w)
in domain of definition,
namely the Segre transversality fails at each point.
F
is called
quasi-standard if it is a “direct sum” of two parts, of which one
comes from a linear isometry pair up to a constant from
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
into
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
and the other is null. A more
precise definition will be given in Section
3
. The theorem above generalizes the following result of Zhang:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Zh
]
)
.

Suppose
N\leq 2n-2
. Then every local Segre map from
\mathcal{H}^{n}

into
\mathcal{H}^{N}
is either null or quasi-standard.

Since
f
is a local orthogonal map from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
if and only if
(f,f)
is a local orthogonal pair, therefore Theorem
1.1
also generalizes the analogous result for orthogonal maps obtained in
[
GN1
]
.

Notice that Segre families of hyperquadrics of any signature are
Segre isomorphic to each other. The novelty of our theorem is that
we allow the target space to have degenerate bilinear form. Also,
our proof is different and simpler from the one employed in
[
Zh
]
. In addition, we can apply our results to orthogonal maps between generalized balls.

Our method of the proof is based on an analysis of linear subspaces
theory initiated in the paper of Faran(
[
Fa1
]
).
First, the orthogonal
complements (which are well defined on
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
) are
linear subspaces that are reserved by local orthogonal pairs. Then
the dimension of the linear span of the image of any general

k
-dimensional linear subspace under
f_{1}
controls the
corresponding dimension of the image of its orthogonal complement
under
f_{2}
for
1<k<r+s+t
and vice versa. This mutual
restriction between
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
together with the dimensional
formula obtained in
[
GN2
]
(see Lemma
5.1
) that
is used to estimate the size of the linear spans of the image of a
linear subspace in any dimension, forces the linearity (or
quasi-linearity) of the orthogonal pair under the stated assumptions
for
r,s,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}
.

2
Notations and conventions

For non-negative integers
r,s,t
, consider the standard Hermitian
form of signature
(r;s;t)
on
\mathbb{C}^{r+s+t}
whose the
eigenvalues are
1
,
-1
and
0
with multiplicities
r
,
s
and

t
, respectively, which is represented by the matrix

H_{r,s,t}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{r}&0&0\\
0&-I_{s}&0\\
0&0&0_{t}\end{pmatrix}
under the standard coordinates. Let
n=r+s+t
. Define the (possibly degenerate) indefinite inner product of signature
(r;s;t)
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as

\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}=z_{1}\bar{w}_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}\bar{w}_{r}-z_{r+1}\bar{w}_{r+1}-\cdots-z_{r+s}\bar{w}_{r+s},

where
z=[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
and
w=[w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}]
. We denote
such a
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the aforementioned Hermitian inner
product by
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
and write its projectivization by

\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}:=\mathbb{P}\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
. If
\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}=0
, we say that
z
is orthogonal to
w
and write

z\perp w
. When
z\perp z
, we say that
z
is a
null
point
. In addition, we also write the
orthogonal
complement
of
z
as

z^{\perp}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}\mid\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}=0\}.

The inner product
\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}
does not descend to

\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
. However, the orthogonality and the orthogonal
complement are well defined over
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
.

More generally,
let
V
be an
n
-dimensional complex vector space equipped with an abstract
Hermitian inner product
H_{V}
(possibly degenerate or indefinite)
of signature
(r;s;t)
, where
n=r+s+t
. Let

\mathbb{P}V
be its projectivization.
The notion of orthogonality can be similarly defined on
\mathbb{P}V
.
In addition, a linear isometric isomorphism
F:\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}\rightarrow V

induces a biholomorphic map
\widetilde{F}:\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}V
.
For our purpose here, it is sufficient for us to simply identify any
such projective space
\mathbb{P}V
with
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
.

Now let
H
be a
k
-dimensional complex vector subspace
in
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
and suppose the restriction of

H_{r,s,t}
on
H
is of signature
(a;b;c)
.
Then
\mathbb{P}H\cong\mathbb{P}^{a,b,c}
.
We call
\mathbb{P}H
an

(a,b,c)
-subspace
.
(When
c=0
, we will simply call
\mathbb{P}H
an
(a,b)
-subspace.)
For brevity, we denote an
(a,b,c)
-subspace by
H^{a,b,c}
.
Clearly,
we have
0\leq a\leq r
,
0\leq b\leq s
,
0\leq c\leq\min\{r-a,s-b\}+t
and
a+b+c=k
.

In this article, when we say a
general point
,
general line
, etc., we mean our choice of the point or line, etc. is anything outside some finite union of complex analytic subvarieties.

3
Local orthogonal pair

For real hyperquadrics in a complex projective space, we note that
Segre maps preserve Segre varieties which are the orthogonal
complements to points with respect to the orthogonality described
before. Motivated by this property, we give the following
definition:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
be a connected open set containing a null point
and let

f_{1}
,
f_{2}
be two holomorphic maps from
U
into
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. We call
F:=(f_{1},f_{2})
an orthogonal pair if

f_{1}(z)\perp f_{2}(w)
for any
z
,
w\in U
with
z\perp w
. We also
call
F
a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to

\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
.

We remark that when
U
does not contain any null point,
the orthogonality condition in the above definition could be vacuous, for
there might not be any pair of orthogonal points in
U
.

In what follows,
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
is said to map a line (or, a

k
-plane) to a line (respectively, a
k^{\prime}
-plane) if it maps the
intersection of a line with
U
(or, a
k
-plane with
U
) into a
line (or a
k^{\prime}
-plane, respectively).

For brevity, we also give the following definitions.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be two linear maps from
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
.
The pair
(\sigma,\tau)
is called an conformal pair
from
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
if there exists a non-zero
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

such that
\langle\sigma(z),\tau(w)\rangle_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}=\lambda\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}

for any
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. We call
F
a standard pair
if it is induced by a conformal pair from
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
to

\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. We call call
F
a null pair if

f_{1}(z)\perp f_{2}(w)
for any
z,w
.

For a non-trivial orthogonal direct sum decomposition
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}=A\oplus B
, there are two standard projections

\pi^{A}:\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}\dasharrow\mathbb{P}A
and
\pi^{B}:\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}\dasharrow\mathbb{P}B
(as rational maps). For a pair of
holomorphic maps
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
, we will write
(\pi^{A}\circ f_{1},\pi^{A}\circ f_{2})
for
(\pi^{A},\pi^{A})\circ F
and write similarly

(\pi^{B},\pi^{B})\circ F
(whenever the compositions are defined as
rational maps). By the same token, for a proper vector subspace

S\subset\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
such that the restriction of the
Hermitian form on
S
is non-degenerate, we can define unambiguously
the projection
\pi^{S}:\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\dasharrow\mathbb{P}S
, as

\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}=S\oplus S^{\perp}
. Thus, we can also define

(\pi^{S},\pi^{S})\circ F
in this case.

An orthogonal pair interacts naturally with an orthogonal decomposition:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
and let
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}=A\oplus B
be an orthogonal decomposition.
Assume that the union of the images of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
is not
contained in
\mathbb{P}A\cup\mathbb{P}B
. If
(\pi^{A},\pi^{A})\circ F

is a local orthogonal pair, then so is
(\pi^{B},\pi^{B})\circ F
. In
addition, if
(\pi^{A},\pi^{A})\circ F
is null, then

(\pi^{B},\pi^{B})\circ F
is null if and only if
F
is null.

Proof.

Let
U
be the domain of definition for
F
. Let
V_{1}:=U\setminus f_{1}^{-1}(\mathbb{P}A\cup\mathbb{P}B)
,
V_{2}:=U\setminus f_{2}^{-1}(\mathbb{P}A\cup\mathbb{P}B)
and
V=V_{1}\cap V_{2}
. Let
p,q\in V
be two orthogonal points and
p^{\prime}=f_{1}(p)
,
q^{\prime}=f_{2}(q)
.

Let
\ell_{p^{\prime}}
,
\ell_{q^{\prime}}
,
\ell^{A}_{p^{\prime}}
,
\ell^{A}_{q^{\prime}}
,

\ell^{B}_{p^{\prime}}
and
\ell^{B}_{q^{\prime}}
be the lines in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}

corresponding to
p^{\prime}
,
q^{\prime}
,
\pi^{A}(p^{\prime})
,
\pi^{A}(q^{\prime})
,
\pi^{B}(p^{\prime})
and
\pi^{B}(q^{\prime})
,
respectively. Then
\ell_{p^{\prime}}^{A}\perp\ell_{q^{\prime}}^{B}
and
\ell_{q^{\prime}}^{A}\perp\ell_{p^{\prime}}^{B}
by definition, and by the orthogonality of
F
and
(\pi^{A},\pi^{A})\circ F
, we also have
\ell_{p^{\prime}}\perp\ell_{q^{\prime}}
and
\ell^{A}_{p^{\prime}}\perp\ell^{A}_{q^{\prime}}
.

For non-zero vectors
v_{p^{\prime}}\in\ell_{p^{\prime}}
and
v_{q^{\prime}}\in\ell_{q^{\prime}}
,
write
v_{p^{\prime}}=v_{p^{\prime}}^{A}+v_{p^{\prime}}^{B}
and
v_{q^{\prime}}=v_{q^{\prime}}^{A}+v_{q^{\prime}}^{B}
for
the decompositions with respect to
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}=A\oplus B
.
Since
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\not\in\mathbb{P}A\cup\mathbb{P}B
, it follows that

v_{p^{\prime}}^{A},v_{p^{\prime}}^{B},v_{q^{\prime}}^{A},v_{q^{\prime}}^{B}
are non-zero. From the
previous paragraph, we know that
v_{p^{\prime}}\perp v_{q^{\prime}}
,

v_{p^{\prime}}^{A}\perp v_{q^{\prime}}^{B}
,
v_{p^{\prime}}^{B}\perp v_{q^{\prime}}^{A}
, and

v^{A}_{p^{\prime}}\perp v^{A}_{q^{\prime}}
. Thus, we have
v^{B}_{p^{\prime}}\perp v^{B}_{q^{\prime}}
and
thus
\ell^{B}_{p^{\prime}}\perp\ell^{B}_{q^{\prime}}
. Hence
(\pi_{B},\pi_{B})\circ F
is
an orthogonal pair.

If
(\pi^{A},\pi^{A})\circ F
is null, then
v^{A}_{p^{\prime}}\perp v^{A}_{q^{\prime}}
for
any
p,q\in V
(not just orthogonal pair
(p,q)
).
Therefore, for any
p,q\in V
, we have
v^{B}_{p^{\prime}}\perp v^{B}_{q^{\prime}}

if and only if
v_{p^{\prime}}\perp v_{q^{\prime}}
.
∎

Applying a similar argument, one also obtains the following lemma:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
F:=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. If the image of
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
is contained in an
(a,b)
-subspace
H^{a,b}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
, then either
F
is null or
(\pi,\pi)\circ F
is a non-null local orthogonal pair, where
\pi
is the projection from
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
to
H^{a,b}
.

We now make the following definition:

Definition 3.6
.

Let
F:=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
into
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. We call
F
quasi-standard
if one of the following scenarios occur:

(1) There exists a vector subspace
A\subset\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
such that the image of
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
is contained in
\mathbb{P}A
and
(\pi^{A},\pi^{A})\circ F
is standard.

(2) There exists a non-trivial orthogonal decomposition
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}=A\oplus B
such that
(\pi^{A},\pi^{A})\circ F
is standard and
(\pi^{B},\pi^{B})\circ F
is null.

Example.
If we split the homogeneous coordinates on

\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
as
[z_{1},\ldots,z_{r+s}]=[z^{+},z^{-}]
,
where
z^{+}=[z_{1},\cdots,z_{r}]
,
z^{-}=[z_{r+1},\cdots,z_{r+s}]
and let
f_{1}

f_{2}
be
the two holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

(with
r<r^{\prime}
and
s<s^{\prime}
) defined by
f_{1}[z^{+},z^{-}]=[z^{+},0,z^{-},0]

and
f_{2}[w^{+},w^{-}]=[\phi[w]w^{+},\phi_{1}[w],\phi[w]w^{-},\phi_{2}[w]]
,
where
\phi
,
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
are homogeneous polynomials of the same degree,
then
(f_{1},f_{2})
is quasi-standard.

Write the standard
(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
-subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
as
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. From the following lemma, a local orthogonal pair
F
from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
naturally gives rise to a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
unless the image of
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
lies entirely in

(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{P}^{0,0,t^{\prime}}
, the set of indeterminacy for the projection.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
F
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
and

\pi
,
\pi^{\perp}
be the projections from
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
and
\mathbb{P}^{0,0,t^{\prime}}
respectively. Then,
(\pi,\pi)
is standard,
(\pi,\pi)\circ F
is orthogonal and
(\pi^{\perp},\pi^{\perp})\circ F
is null whenever the compositions are defined. In addition,
F
is null if and only if
(\pi,\pi)\circ F
is null.

Proof.

The proof follows by applying the above lemma to
the projection
\pi:\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

Let
F:=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. If the dimension of the linear span of the image of
\pi\circ f_{1}
or
\pi\circ f_{2}
is less than
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})
, where
\pi
is the projection from
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
, then we say that
F
is
degenerate.

Proposition 3.9
.

Every degenerate local orthogonal pair is null.

Proof.

Let
F:=(f_{1},f_{2})
be an orthogonal pair from a connected open
set
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
into
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
.

First, we consider the case
t^{\prime}=0
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that the linear span of
f_{1}(U)
is a
d
-dimensional linear subspace
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
, where
d<\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})=r+s-1
.
Let
d^{\prime}
be the dimension of the linear span of
f_{1}(H\cap U)
for a
general hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
. Then
d^{\prime}\leq d<r+s-1
.

If
d^{\prime}=d
, then for a general point
\alpha\in U
such that its orthogonal
complement is a hyperplane
H
intersecting
U
,
the linear span of
f_{1}(H\cap U)
is
\Sigma
and hence
f_{2}(\alpha)

lies in
\Sigma^{\perp}
. Since
this holds for each point in a neighborhood of
\alpha
,
we conclude that
F
is a null pair by uniqueness of holomorphic functions.

If
d^{\prime}<d
, then
d^{\prime}<\dim(H)
. From Lemma
5.1
(
ii
),
the linear span of
f_{1}(U)
is contained in a
d^{\prime}
-dimensional linear subspace.
This contradicts to the assumption that
d
is the dimension of the
linear span of
f_{1}(U)
.

Hence we see that when
t^{\prime}=0
and
F
is degenerate,
F
is a null
pair.

When
t^{\prime}>0
, by Lemma
3.7
,
(\pi,\pi)\circ F
is a local orthogonal pair
and
(\pi^{\perp},\pi^{\perp})\circ F
is null, where
\pi
and
\pi^{\perp}
are
the projections from
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
or
\mathbb{P}^{0,0,t^{\prime}}

respectively. And
(\pi,\pi)\circ F
is a null pair from the argument above.
Therefore
F
is a null pair.
∎

From the proposition above, it is easy to get the following result.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
F:=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
. If
r+s>r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}
, then
F
is null.

The following result provides a tool for us to carry out a reduction
argument.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
F
be an orthogonal pair from a connected open set
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
. Suppose the linear span of
f_{1}(U)
is an
(a,b,c)
-subspace
H^{a,b,c}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
such that
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
and
\dim(H^{a,b,c})\leq 2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-2
. Then, either
F
is null or
(\pi,\pi)\circ F
is a non-null local orthogonal pair, where
\pi
is the projection from
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
to any
(a,b)
-subspace
H^{a,b}
of
H^{a,b,c}
.

Proof.

Let
H^{a+c,b+c}
be an
(a+c,b+c)
-subspace in
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

containing
H^{a,b,c}
. From Lemma
3.5
, we may, after
replacing
F
by its composition with the projection to
H^{a+c,b+c}
, assume that
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\mathbb{P}^{a+c,b+c}
.
Fix an
(a,b)
-subspace
H^{a,b}\subset H^{a,b,c}
and let
\pi
and

\pi^{\perp}
be the projections from
\mathbb{P}^{a+c,b+c}
to

H^{a,b}
and
(H^{a,b})^{\perp}
, respectively.

Let
U^{\prime}\subset U
be the open subset consisting of
\beta^{\prime}s
such
that
\beta^{\perp}\cap U\neq\varnothing
.
By
Definition
3.1
,
U^{\prime}
is non-empty. For
\beta\in U^{\prime}
, let

d(\beta)
be the dimension of the linear span of

f_{1}(U\cap\beta^{\perp})
. If
d(\beta)=\dim(H^{a,b,c})
, then the
linear span of
f_{1}(U\cap\beta^{\perp})
is exactly
H^{a,b,c}
. Thus,

f_{2}(\beta)\in(H^{a,b,c})^{\perp}
by the orthogonality of
F
. If it
is true for a general choice of
\beta
, then we immediately see
that
F
is null.

If for each
\beta\in U^{\prime}
, we have
d(\beta)\leq\dim(H^{a,b,c})-2
, then
d(\beta)\leq 2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-4\leq 2\dim(\beta^{\perp})-2
. Since any hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
is
the orthogonal complement of some point, we deduce by
Lemma
5.1
(
i
) that the dimension of the linear
span of
f_{1}(U)
is
d+1\leq\dim(H^{a,b,c})-1
, contradicting our
definition of
H^{a,b,c}
.

It remains to handle the case where
d(\beta)=\dim(H^{a,b,c})-1
for
a general
\beta\in U^{\prime}
. In this case, if we let
H(\beta)
be the
linear span of
f_{1}(U\cap\beta^{\perp})
, then
H(\beta)
is a hyperplane in
H^{a,b,c}
.
We claim that
H(\beta)^{\perp}\subset H^{a,b,c}
. Assuming
the claim for the moment, then as
f_{2}(\beta)\in H(\beta)^{\perp}\subset H^{a,b,c}
for a general
\beta
, which implies that
H^{a,b,c}
contains the image of
f_{2}
, we see that
F
is a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
H^{a,b,c}
. The desired result now follows Lemma
3.7
.

To prove the claim, let
Z:=(H^{a,b,c})^{\perp}
. Since the ambient space is
\mathbb{P}^{a+c,b+c}
, it follows easily that
Z\subset H^{a,b,c}
and
Z
is indeed just the null space in
H^{a,b,c}
corresponding to the zero eigenvalue. As
H(\beta)
is a hyperplane in
H^{a,b,c}
, we have
H(\beta)^{\perp}\supset(H^{a,b,c})^{\perp}=Z
and

\dim(H(\beta)^{\perp})\leq\dim(Z)+1.
On the other hand,
Let
\hat{H}^{a,b,c}\subset\mathbb{C}^{a+c,b+c}
be the
Hermitian vector subspace of signature
(a,b,c)
such that

\mathbb{P}\hat{H}^{a,b,c}=H^{a,b,c}
and similar notation for
\hat{Z}
,
\hat{H}(\beta)
.
Now
\hat{H}(\beta)
is a hyperplane in
\hat{H}^{a,b,c}
and thus its
image under the canonical projection to the quotient space
\hat{H}^{a,b,c}/\hat{Z}

is either full or of codimension 1. This implies that
\hat{H}(\beta)^{\perp}=(\hat{H}^{a,b,c})^{\perp}=\hat{Z}
and
\hat{H}(\beta)^{\perp}\varsupsetneq\hat{Z}

with
\dim(\hat{H}(\beta)^{\perp}\cap\hat{H}^{a,b,c})=\dim(\hat{Z})+1
. Combining these
with the inequality
\dim(H(\beta)^{\perp})\leq\dim(Z)+1
obtained earlier,
it follows that we always have
\hat{H}(\beta)^{\perp}\subset\hat{H}^{a,b,c}

and hence the claim follows.
∎

Remark.
When
\dim(H^{a,b,c})>2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-2
, the conclusion of Lemma
3.11
may not hold. For example, let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be the holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{P}^{2,2}
to
\mathbb{P}^{3,4}
defined as follows

f_{1}[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}]=[z_{1}^{2},z_{2}^{2},z_{1}z_{2},z_{3}^{2},z_{4}^{2},\sqrt{2}z_{3}z_{4},z_{1}z_{2}]

f_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4}]=[w_{1}^{2},w_{2}^{2},w_{1}w_{2},w_{3}^{2},w_{4}^{2},\sqrt{2}w_{3}w_{4},-w_{1}w_{2}].

Then
(f_{1},f_{2})
is an orthogonal pair and the linear span of
f_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2,2})
is an
(2,3,1)
-subspace
H^{2,3,1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{3,4}
. But
(\pi\circ f_{1},\pi\circ f_{2})
is not an orthogonal pair, where
\pi
is the projection from
\mathbb{P}^{3,4}
to
H^{2,3}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
and suppose
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})=\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})
. If
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
is linear, then
F
is either null or standard.

Proof.

Suppose
F
is not null and
f_{1}
is linear. Then by Proposition
3.9
,
F
is
non-degenerate. Thus,
f_{1}
is a linear biholomorphism. By the orthogonality of
F
,
it follows immediately that
f_{2}
maps lines to lines and hence it is also a
linear biholomorphism since
F
is non-degenerate (Lemma
5.1
(
ii
)).
Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be two matrices representing
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
respectively under
the standard homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
and
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
.
By the orthogonality of
F
, for any
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
, we have

\bar{u}^{t}\bar{A}_{1}^{t}H_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}A_{2}v=0
if and only if
\bar{u}^{t}H_{r,s}v=0
, where
H_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
and
H_{r,s}
are the Hermitian matrices representing the inner products in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
and
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
. This implies that
\bar{A}_{1}^{t}H_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}A_{2}=\lambda H_{r,s}
for some non-zero
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and hence
F
is standard.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main theorem.

Theorem 3.13
.

If
r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}\leq 2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-1
, every local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
is either null or quasi-standard.

Proof.

As in Proposition
3.9
, we only need to prove the result for the
case
t^{\prime}=0
.
Let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
be a local orthogonal pair from
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
into

\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
and
F
is not null. From Proposition
3.9
,
F
is non-degenerate.

The hypothesis is equivalent to that
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})\leq 2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-2
and so everything is trivial unless

\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})\geq 2
. We will consider the case

\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})\geq 3
first and handle the two dimensional
case later.

If
f_{1}
is not linear, then the dimension (denoted by
d
) of the linear span

S(L)
of
f_{1}(U\cap L)
for a general line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
intersecting
U

is at least 2. Note that
\dim(L^{\perp})=\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-2=r+s-3\geq 1

and
\dim(S(L)^{\perp})=\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime}s^{\prime}})-d-1=r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}-d-2
for a general line
L
.
In addition, since any
(r+s-3)
-plane in
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
is the orthogonal
complement of some line (for the Hermitian form is non-degenerate), we see that
f_{2}

maps
(r+s-3)
-planes into
(r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}-d-2)
-planes by the orthogonality of
F
. As

r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}-d-2\leq r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}-4\leq 2(r+s)-7=2(r+s-3)-1,

Lemma
5.1
(
i
) implies that the image of
f_{2}
is
contained
in a proper linear subspace
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
such
that
\dim(H)\leq r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}-d\leq\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})-1
.

Suppose
H\cong\mathbb{P}^{a,b,c}
for some
a,b,c
.
Then
c\leq\min\{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\}\leq\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-1

since
r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}\leq 2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-1
. From this we see that

(a,b)\neq(0,0)
since otherwise it would contradict the fact that
F
is non-degenerate.
As
\dim(H)\leq\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})-1\leq 2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-3
, by Lemma
3.11

F_{K}:=(\pi^{K},\pi^{K})\circ F
is a non-null local orthogonal pair, where
\pi^{K}:\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\dasharrow K
is the projection to any choice of
(a,b)
-subspace
K\subset H
.

The hypotheses of the theorem still hold for
F_{K}
. We can therefore
repeat the arguments and deduce inductively that
there exists some
(r^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime\prime})
-subspace
\Phi\subset\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

with projection
\pi_{\Phi}:\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\dasharrow\Phi
,
such that
F_{\Phi}:=(\pi^{\Phi}\circ{f_{1}},\pi^{\Phi}\circ{f_{2}})
is a non-null
local orthogonal pair of which at least one component, say
\pi^{\Phi}\circ{f_{1}}
,
is linear. Combining with Proposition
3.9
and Lemma
3.11
, we may further assume that
\dim(\Phi)=\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})
and
\pi^{\Phi}\circ{f_{1}}
is a linear biholomorphism.

Here we note that the previous conclusion is also true when
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})=2
(from which the hypothesis implies
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})\leq 2
). It is because in this case if we replace a general line
L
in the argument above by a general point, we see immediately
by orthogonality that
f_{2}
maps lines to lines and hence by non-degeneracy
f_{2}
is a linear biholomorphism onto
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
.

Lemma
3.12
now implies that
F_{\Phi}
is standard (and
F
itself is standard when
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})=2
). If
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})\geq 3
, we have
\dim(\Phi^{\perp})\leq\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-3
since
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})\leq 2\dim(\mathbb{P}^{r,s})-2
by hypothesis. Hence, if the images of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are not contained in
\Phi
, then
(\pi^{\Phi^{\perp}},\pi^{\Phi^{\perp}})\circ F
is defined and null by Corollary
3.10
. Therefore,
F
is quasi-standard.
∎

4
Segre map

In
[
Zh
]
, Zhang considered the holomorphic Segre maps from the Segre family
\mathcal{H}^{n}
of the Heisenberg hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined by
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n-1},\zeta)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:Im(\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}z_{j}\bar{z}_{j}\}
. It is well-known that under the Cayley transform,
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the intersection of the set of null points in
\mathbb{P}^{1,n}
with an affine coordinate chart.

Lemma 4.1
.

A local holomorphic Segre map from
\mathcal{H}^{n}
into
\mathcal{H}^{N}
is a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{1,n}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1,N}
.

Proof.

The Segre family
\mathcal{H}^{n}
is holomorphically foliated by
Q_{\xi}\times\{\xi\}
and
z\times\hat{Q}_{z}
, where

Q_{\xi}\times\{\xi\}\cong\{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1,n}:\langle z,\xi\rangle_{1,n}=0\}

z\times\hat{Q}_{z}\cong\{\xi\in\mathbb{P}^{1,n}:\langle z,\xi\rangle_{1,n}=0\}.

A local holomorphic Segre map
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
from
\mathcal{H}^{n}
into
\mathcal{H}^{N}
satisfies
f_{1}(Q_{\xi})\subset\hat{Q}_{f_{2}(\xi)}
and
f_{2}(\hat{Q}_{z})\subset Q_{f_{1}(z)}
.

Hence,
f_{1}
maps
\xi^{\perp}
into
(f_{2}(\xi))^{\perp}
and
f_{2}

maps
z^{\perp}
into
(f_{2}(z))^{\perp}
. Therefore

\langle f_{1}(z),f_{2}(w)\rangle_{1,N}=0

for any
z
,
w\in\mathbb{P}^{1,n}

such that
\langle z,w\rangle_{1,n}=0
. Hence
F
is a local
orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{1,n}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1,N}
.
∎

The following rigidity of the Segre maps from
\mathcal{H}^{n}
into
\mathcal{H}^{N}
obtained by Zhang in
[
Zh
]
is a special case of our Theorem
3.13
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
N\leq 2n-2
. Then every local Segre map from
\mathcal{H}^{n}
into
\mathcal{H}^{N}
is either null or quasi-standard.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.13
and Lemma
4.1
.
∎

5
Appendix

The following result about the estimate of the dimension of the holomorphic mappings is the special case of main theorem in
[
GN2
]
which is proved by using Green hyperplane restriction Theorem. In order to make the paper more self-contained, we give an analytic proof here, which is inspired by
[
Fa1
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}
be a connected open set and
F:U\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{m^{\prime}}
be a holomorphic map. Suppose
F
maps
l
-planes to
l^{\prime}
-planes, where
1\leq l\leq m-1
.

(i).
If
l^{\prime}\leq 2l-1
, then
F
maps
(l+k)
-planes into
(l^{\prime}+k)
-planes for
k\geq 0
.

(ii).
If
l^{\prime}\leq l
, then either
F
is linear or the image of
F
is contained in an
l^{\prime}
-plane.

Proof.

At first, we will prove that
F
maps
(l+1)
-planes into
(l^{\prime}+1)
-planes. Let
q
be the maximum of the rank of the Jacobian matrix
DF
of
F
. We consider two cases:

(Case I:
q\geq l+1
)
In this case, by restricting to a general
q
-dimensional linear subspace (i.e. those with tangent spaces intersecting trivially with the kernel of
DF
at some point), we get a map (still denoted by
F
) from
\mathbb{P}^{q}
to
\mathbb{P}^{m^{\prime}}
mapping
l
-planes into
l^{\prime}
-planes and such that at a general point, the Jacobian matrix of
F
is of rank
q
. It suffices to show that this
F
maps
(l+1)
-planes into
(l^{\prime}+1)
-planes since it then follows that a general (and hence every)
(l+1)
-plane of
\mathbb{P}^{m}
is mapped by (the original)
F
into an
(l^{\prime}+1)
-plane.

Suppose on the contrary the linear span of the image of a general
(l+1)
-plane
E\subset\mathbb{P}^{q}
is of dimension at least
(l^{\prime}+2)
.

Then, for a general point in
x\in E
, there exist multi-indices
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{l^{\prime}+1-l}
and holomorphic tangent vectors
v_{1},\cdots,v_{l^{\prime}+1-l}
in
T_{x}E
so that
DF(T_{x}E)
is of dimension
l+1
and

DF(T_{x}E)+\textrm{Span}\{D^{\alpha_{j}}F(x)(v_{j}^{|\alpha_{j}|}):1\leq j\leq l^{\prime}+1-l\}

is of dimension
l^{\prime}+2
. Note that
l^{\prime}+1-l\leq l
. If we consider an
l
-plane
\mathcal{L}\subset E
passing through
x
such that
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
,
\cdots
,
v_{l^{\prime}+1-l}
are all contained in
T_{x}\mathcal{L}
, then the linear span of the image of
\mathcal{L}
by
F
contains

DF(T_{x}\mathcal{L})+\textrm{Span}\{D^{\alpha_{j}}F(x)(v_{j}^{|\alpha_{j}|}):1\leq j\leq l^{\prime}+1-l\},

which is of dimension
l^{\prime}+1
. Thus the image of the
l
-plane
\mathcal{L}
is not contained in any
l^{\prime}
-plane, a contradiction. Therefore,
F
maps
(l+1)
-planes into
(l^{\prime}+1)
-planes.

(Case II:
q<l+1
)
We can choose a point
x\in\mathbb{P}^{m}
at which the rank of the Jacobian
DF
is constantly
q
in a neighborhood
U\ni x
and such that there is a neighborhood
U^{\prime}
at
F(x)
in which the image of
F(U)
is a
q
-dimensional complex manifold in
U^{\prime}
. Then, as
q\leq l
, for a general
l
-plane
\mathcal{L}
, we have
F(\mathcal{L}\cap U)=F(U)
, which by hypotheses, is contained in an
l^{\prime}
-plane and thus it follows trivially that
F
maps
(l+1)
-planes into
(l^{\prime}+1)
-planes.

Combining
Case I
and
Case II
, we conclude that
F
maps
(l+1)
-planes to
(l^{\prime}+1)
-planes and thus by induction
F
maps
(l+k)
-planes into
(l^{\prime}+k)
-planes for
0\leq k\leq\min\{m-l,m^{\prime}-l^{\prime}\}
. The proof for part (i) is complete.

To prove part (ii), let
d
be the dimension of the linear span of the image of
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}
for a general pair of
l
-planes
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
such that intersection
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}
is an
(l-1)
-plane. Thus,
d\leq l^{\prime}
.

If
d=l^{\prime}
, then the images of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
must be contained in the same
l^{\prime}
-plane. Since
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are chosen generally, so
F
must map every
l
-plane into this
l^{\prime}
-plane. Therefore, the image of
F
must be contained in this
l^{\prime}
-plane.

If
d<l^{\prime}
, then it means
F
maps
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}
into an
(l^{\prime}-1)
-plane. Since
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
are chosen generally, it follows that
F
maps
(l-1)
-planes to
(l^{\prime}-1)
-planes.

Inductively, we deduce that either
F
maps
(l-l^{\prime}+1)
-planes to 1-planes (lines) or the image of
F
is contained in an
k
-plane,
1\leq k\leq l^{\prime}
.

If
l^{\prime}=l
, then either
F
maps lines to lines or the image of
F

is contained in an
l^{\prime}
-plane. Thus
F
is linear or the image of

F
is contained in an
l^{\prime}
-plane.

If
l^{\prime}<l
, then either
F
maps
(l-l^{\prime})
-planes to 0-planes (points) or the image of

F
is contained in an
l^{\prime}
-plane. The first possibility implies that the image of

F
is a single point. Thus, the image of
F
must be contained in an
l^{\prime}
-plane in
any case.
∎

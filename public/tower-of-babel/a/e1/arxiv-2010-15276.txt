Ladder Operators and Hidden Algebras for Shape Invariant Nonseparable and Nondiagonalizable Models with Quadratic Complex Interaction. II. Three-Dimensional Model
Ian MARQUETTE a and Christiane QUESNE b

Abstract
A shape invariant nonseparable and nondiagonalizable three-dimensional model with quadratic complex interaction was introduced by Bardavelidze, Cannata, Ioffe, and Nishnianidze. However, the complete hidden symmetry algebra and the description of the associated states that form Jordan blocks remained to be studied. We present a set of six operators { A ± , B ± , C ± } \{A^{\pm},B^{\pm},C^{\pm}\} that can be combined to build a 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 3 ) {\mathfrak{gl}}(3) hidden algebra. The latter can be embedded in an 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 6 ) {\mathfrak{sp}}(6) algebra, as well as in an 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 / 6 ) {\mathfrak{osp}}(1/6) superalgebra. The states associated with the eigenstates and making Jordan blocks are induced in different ways by combinations of operators acting on the ground state. We present the action of these operators and study the construction of an extended biorthogonal basis. These rely on establishing various nontrivial polynomial and commutator identities. We also make a connection between the hidden symmetry and the underlying superintegrability property of the model. Interestingly, the integrals generate a cubic algebra. This work demonstrates how various concepts that have been applied widely to Hermitian Hamiltonians, such as hidden symmetries, superintegrability, and ladder operators, extend to the pseudo-Hermitian case with many differences.

Introduction

Over the years, a large body of literature has been devoted to non-Hermitian Hamiltonians with a real spectrum, in particular PT-symmetric systems
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
or, more generally, pseudo-Hermitian ones, for which
\eta H\eta^{-1}=H^{\dagger}

[

[

]
and later on by Scholtz, Geyer, and Hahne as quasi-Hermiticity
[
23
]
.

However, mainly the one-dimensional case has been explored and only a few two- and three-dimensional systems have been obtained (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
6
,
8
,
13
,
21
]
) and their study may still be incomplete.

In the previous paper of this series
[
15
]
, we re-examined a non-Hermitian two-dimensional system
[
8
]
which is exactly solvable due to its shape invariance
[
1
,
7
,
10
,
12
,
14
]

hidden symmetry algebra and that an underlying
{\mathfrak{sp}}(4)

[

can be constructed, as well as an
{\mathfrak{osp}}(1/4)
superalgebra
[
11
]
. In contrast with the two-dimensional Hermitian harmonic oscillator, however, the algebraic structure is related to integrability, but not superintegrability.

The main purpose of the present paper is to consider another non-Hermitian system with a quadratic complex interaction, which is exactly solvable, although not separable nor diagonalizable
[
2
]
. This system being in three dimensions has a more complicated structure of the Jordan blocks that are needed to form a complete basis. This is the reason it deserves a separate study.

Another purpose of this paper is to highlight properties of non-Hermitian systems in higher dimensions, such as exact solvability without separation of variables and hidden symmetry that does not lead to superintegrability in the usual way. We would also like to emphasize that the ladder operators constructed from partial differential operators may have distinct properties, such as the possibility of infinitely zero modes.

2
Shape invariant model with quadratic complex interaction

Let us consider the three-dimensional model with complex oscillator Hamiltonian
[
2
]

\displaystyle H=-\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2}-\partial_{3}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\big(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}\big)+g^{2}\big(x_{1}^{2}-2{\rm i}x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}^{2}\big)-4\lambda g(x_{1}-{\rm i}x_{2})x_{3}-3\lambda,

where
g
and
\lambda
are two real parameters such that
\lambda>|g|
. This Hamiltonian can be rewritten as

\displaystyle H=-4\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}-\partial_{3}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\big(z\bar{z}+x_{3}^{2}\big)+g^{2}\bar{z}^{2}-4\lambda g\bar{z}x_{3}-3\lambda,

(2.1)

where
z=x_{1}+{\rm i}x_{2}
and
\bar{z}=x_{1}-{\rm i}x_{2}
. It satisfies pseudo-Hermiticity with
\eta
chosen as
P_{2}
, which is the operator changing
x_{2}
into
-x_{2}
.

With the operators

\displaystyle A^{\pm}=2\partial_{z}\mp\lambda\bar{z}.

(2.2)

the Hamiltonian (
2.1
) satisfies the intertwining properties

\displaystyle HA^{+}=A^{+}(H+2\lambda),\qquad A^{-}H=(H+2\lambda)A^{-},

showing its self-isospectral shape invariance. These equations can also be expressed in the form of ladder-type relations

\displaystyle[H,A^{\pm}]=\pm 2\lambda A^{\pm}.

(2.3)

However, unlike ladder operators for the Hermitian harmonic oscillator, the operators (
2.2
) satisfy the relation

\displaystyle[A^{-},A^{+}]=0.

(2.4)

This algebra can be seen as a Euclidean e(2), which differs from the Heisenberg algebra encountered in the Hermitian case.

Another set of ladder operators can be introduced
[
2
]
,

\displaystyle Q^{\pm}=4\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}-\partial_{3}^{2}\mp 2\lambda(z\partial_{z}+\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}-x_{3}\partial_{3})\pm 4gx_{3}\partial_{z}\mp 2g\bar{z}\partial_{3}

\displaystyle\hphantom{Q^{\pm} =}{}+\lambda^{2}\big(z\bar{z}-x_{3}^{2}\big)-g^{2}\bar{z}^{2}\mp\lambda.

(2.5)

They satisfy the following commutation relations with
H
,

\displaystyle[H,Q^{\pm}]=\pm 4\lambda Q^{\pm},

(2.6)

which can also be interpreted as intertwining relations, characteristic of shape invariance. Additional commutation relations read

\displaystyle[A^{\pm},Q^{\mp}]=\pm 4\lambda A^{\pm},

(2.7)

\displaystyle[A^{\pm},Q^{\pm}]=0,

(2.8)

\displaystyle[Q^{-},Q^{+}]=-2\tilde{R}_{1}=8\big(\lambda H-2gR_{1}+3\lambda^{2}\big),

(2.9)

where

\displaystyle R_{1}=2\partial_{z}\partial_{3}+\lambda\bar{z}(g\bar{z}-\lambda x_{3}).

The Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle H\Psi({\bf x})=E\Psi({\bf x})

can be solved in terms of the operators
A^{\pm}
and
Q^{\pm}

[
2
]
. The (unnormalized) ground-state wavefunction
\Psi_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle A^{-}\Psi_{0}=Q^{-}\Psi_{0}=0,

is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}(z,\bar{z},x_{3})={\rm e}^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(z\bar{z}+x_{3}^{2})+g\bar{z}x_{3}}

(2.10)

with corresponding energy

\displaystyle E_{0}=0.

The operators
A^{+}
and
Q^{+}
allow to obtain from it the excited-state wavefunctions

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,n,0}(z,\bar{z},x_{3})=c_{k,n}(Q^{+})^{k}(A^{+})^{n}\Psi_{0}(z,\bar{z},x_{3}),

(2.11)

corresponding to

\displaystyle E_{k,n}=2\lambda(2k+n).

Here,
k
and
n
run over 0, 1, 2, …, and
c_{k,n}
is some normalization coefficient. The determination of the latter, which is far more complicated than in the two-dimensional case, will be studied in detail in Section
6
.

The action of
A^{\pm}
and
Q^{\pm}
on the wavefunctions can be easily calculated and is given by

\displaystyle A^{+}\Psi_{k,n,0}=\frac{c_{k,n}}{c_{k,n+1}}\Psi_{k,n+1,0},

(2.12)

\displaystyle A^{-}\Psi_{k,n,0}=-\frac{4\lambda kc_{k,n}}{c_{k-1,n+1}}\Psi_{k-1,n+1,0},

(2.13)

\displaystyle Q^{+}\Psi_{k,n,0}=\frac{c_{k,n}}{c_{k+1,n}}\Psi_{k+1,n,0},

\displaystyle Q^{-}\Psi_{k,n,0}=\frac{8\lambda^{2}k(2n+2k+1)c_{k,n}}{c_{k-1,n}}\Psi_{k-1,n,0}-\frac{16g^{2}k(k-1)c_{k,n}}{c_{k-2,n+2}}\Psi_{k-2,n+2,0}.

The algebra generated by
H
,
A^{\pm}
, and
Q^{\pm}
, and whose commutation relations are given in equations (
2.3
), (
2.4
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
), and (
2.9
), is not a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. If the operators
A^{\pm}
and
Q^{\pm}
are useful for building the Hamiltonian wavefunctions, they do not lead to the hidden symmetry algebra. Furthermore, the wavefunctions
\Psi_{k,n,0}(z,\bar{z},x_{3})
with
n\neq 0
are self-orthogonal, which signals that
H
is nondiagonalizable, so that some associated functions must be introduced to complete the basis and to get a resolution of identity. To try to solve these problems, it will prove convenient to introduce some additional ladder operators. This will be the purpose of Section
3
.

3
Construction of additional sets of ladder operators

If we consider the subset
\{\Psi_{0,n,0}\,|\,n=0,1,2,\dots\}
of wavefunctions, it is clear from equations (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) that we have a raising operator
A^{+}
, but no lowering one since
A^{-}
annihilates
\Psi_{0,n,0}
. This is actually another difference with the Hermitian case. The fact that the ladder operators are given by differential operators depending on more than one variable allows the existence of infinitely many zero modes.

Such a lowering operator is provided by

\displaystyle B^{-}=\partial_{\bar{z}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}z-gx_{3},

for which

\displaystyle B^{-}\Psi_{0,n,0}\propto\bar{z}^{n-1}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(z\bar{z}+x_{3}^{2})+g\bar{z}x_{3}}\propto\Psi_{0,n-1,0}.

This allows to consider another operators
B^{+}
, defined by

\displaystyle B^{+}=\partial_{\bar{z}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}z+gx_{3}.

With
H
, the pair of operators
B^{\pm}
satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[H,B^{\pm}]=\pm 2\lambda B^{\pm}\mp 2gC^{\pm},

(3.1)

where there appear two new operators

\displaystyle C^{\pm}=\partial_{3}\pm g\bar{z}\mp\lambda x_{3}.

The other commutation relations are given by

\displaystyle[H,C^{\pm}]=\mp 2gA^{\pm}\pm 2\lambda C^{\pm},

(3.2)

\displaystyle[A^{-},B^{+}]=[B^{-},A^{+}]=[C^{-},C^{+}]=-2\lambda,

(3.3)

\displaystyle[B^{-},C^{+}]=[C^{-},B^{+}]=2g.

(3.4)

From the three sets of operators
A^{\pm}
,
B^{\pm}
, and
C^{\pm}
, we can generate the operators
Q^{\pm}
, defined in (
2.5
), since

\displaystyle Q^{\pm}=2A^{\pm}B^{\pm}-(C^{\pm})^{2}.

(3.5)

From equation (
3.5
) and the commutation relations (
2.4
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle[B^{\pm},Q^{\pm}]=[C^{\pm},Q^{\pm}]=0,

\displaystyle[B^{\pm},Q^{\mp}]=\pm 4\lambda B^{\mp}\pm 4gC^{\mp},

\displaystyle[C^{\pm},Q^{\mp}]=\mp 4gA^{\mp}\mp 4\lambda C^{\mp},

together with the set of relations (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
).

In contrast with the algebra generated by
H
,
A^{\pm}
, and
Q^{\pm}
, the one generated by
H
,
A^{\pm}
,
B^{\pm}
,
C^{\pm}
, and whose commutation relations are given by equations (
2.3
), (
2.4
), (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
), is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. It has, however, a rather complicated structure, so that some additional transformations have to be carried out, as shown in Section
4
.

4
Construction of the hidden symmetry algebra

In order to get more insight into the structure of the hidden symmetry algebra of this non-Hermitian Hamiltonian, let us introduce a set of nine bilinear operators

\displaystyle R=A^{+}A^{-},\qquad S=B^{+}B^{-},\qquad T=C^{+}C^{-},

\displaystyle U=A^{+}B^{-}+B^{+}A^{-},\qquad V=A^{+}C^{-}+C^{+}A^{-},\qquad W=B^{+}C^{-}+C^{+}B^{-},

(4.1)

\displaystyle X=A^{+}B^{-}-B^{+}A^{-},\qquad Y=A^{+}C^{-}-C^{+}A^{-},\qquad Z=B^{+}C^{-}-C^{+}B^{-},

which have the following differential operator realizations

\displaystyle R=4\partial_{z}^{2}-\lambda^{2}\bar{z}^{2},\qquad S=\partial_{\bar{z}}^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}z^{2}+\lambda gzx_{3}-g^{2}x_{3}^{2},

\displaystyle T=\partial_{3}^{2}-g^{2}\bar{z}^{2}+2\lambda g\bar{z}x_{3}-\lambda^{2}x_{3}^{2}+\lambda,

\displaystyle U=4\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}-\lambda^{2}z\bar{z}+2\lambda g\bar{z}x_{3}+2\lambda,\qquad V=4\partial_{z}\partial_{3}+2\lambda g\bar{z}^{2}-2\lambda^{2}\bar{z}x_{3},

\displaystyle W=2\partial_{\bar{z}}\partial_{3}+\lambda gz\bar{z}-\lambda^{2}zx_{3}-2g^{2}\bar{z}x_{3}+2\lambda gx_{3}^{2}-2g,\qquad X=2(\lambda z-2gx_{3})\partial_{z}-2\lambda\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}},

\displaystyle Y=-4(g\bar{z}-\lambda x_{3})\partial_{z}-2\lambda\bar{z}\partial_{3},\qquad Z=-2(g\bar{z}-\lambda x_{3})\partial_{\bar{z}}-(\lambda z-2gx_{3})\partial_{z}.

They satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[R,S]=-2\lambda X,\qquad[R,T]=0,\qquad[R,U]=0,\qquad[R,V]=0,

\displaystyle[R,W]=-2\lambda Y,\qquad[R,X]=4\lambda R,\qquad[R,Y]=0,\qquad[R,Z]=-2\lambda V,

\displaystyle[S,T]=2gZ,\qquad[S,U]=0,\qquad[S,V]=-2\lambda Z-2gX,\qquad[S,W]=0,

\displaystyle[S,X]=-4\lambda S,\qquad[S,Y]=-2\lambda W-2gU,\qquad[S,Z]=-4gS,

\displaystyle[T,U]=-2gY,\qquad[T,V]=2\lambda Y,\qquad[T,W]=2\lambda Z,\qquad[T,X]=-2gV,

\displaystyle[T,Y]=2\lambda V,\qquad[T,Z]=2\lambda W+4gT,\qquad[U,V]=-2\lambda Y,

(4.2)

\displaystyle[U,W]=-2\lambda Z+2gX,\qquad[U,X]=0,\qquad[U,Y]=-2\lambda V-4gR,

\displaystyle[U,Z]=-2\lambda W-2gU,\qquad[V,W]=-2\lambda X+2gY,\qquad[V,X]=2\lambda V-4gR,

\displaystyle[V,Y]=4\lambda R,\qquad[V,Z]=2\lambda(U-2T)+2gV,\qquad[W,X]=-2\lambda W-2gU,

\displaystyle[W,Y]=2\lambda(U-2T)-2gV,\qquad[W,Z]=4\lambda S,\qquad[X,Y]=-2\lambda Y,

\displaystyle[X,Z]=2\lambda Z-2gX,\qquad[Y,Z]=2\lambda X+2gY,

and therefore generate a nine-dimensional Lie algebra.

These bilinear operators can be related to the Hamiltonian
H
through the equation

\displaystyle H=-U-T.

This relation, which connects the Hamiltonian with the generators of the nine-dimensional Lie algebra, proves that the latter is a hidden symmetry algebra. Note, however, a distinction with respect to the Hermitian three-dimensional oscillator, for which
H
is expressed in terms of three commuting components in involution, while here the components
U
and
T
have a nonvanishing commutator.

We may also point out the commutators of
H
with the nine operators (
4.1
),

\displaystyle[H,R]=0,\qquad[H,S]=2gZ,\qquad[H,T]=-2gY,

\displaystyle[H,U]=2gY,\qquad[H,V]=0,\qquad[H,W]=-2gX,

(4.3)

\displaystyle[H,X]=2gV,\qquad[H,Y]=4gR,\qquad[H,Z]=2g(U-2T),

which will be useful in further calculations.

4.1
Connection with
\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)}
and bosonic operators

We can re-express the nine operators (
4.1
) in terms of
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
generators
E_{ij}
,
i,j=1,2,3
, satisfying the commutation relations

\displaystyle[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{j,k}E_{il}-\delta_{i,l}E_{kj}.

We indeed get the following relations

\displaystyle E_{11}=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}T+\frac{1}{2},\qquad E_{22}=\frac{\lambda}{2g^{2}}S+\frac{1}{2\lambda}T+\frac{1}{2g}W+\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle E_{33}=-\frac{g^{2}}{2\lambda^{3}}R-\frac{\lambda}{2g^{2}}S-\frac{1}{2\lambda}T-\frac{1}{2\lambda}U-\frac{g}{2\lambda^{2}}V-\frac{1}{2g}W+\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle E_{12}={\rm i}\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda}T+\frac{1}{4g}W-\frac{1}{4g}Z\right),\qquad E_{21}={\rm i}\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda}T+\frac{1}{4g}W+\frac{1}{4g}Z\right),

\displaystyle E_{13}=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}T-\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}V-\frac{1}{4g}W+\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}Y+\frac{1}{4g}Z,

\displaystyle E_{31}=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}T-\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}V-\frac{1}{4g}W-\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}Y-\frac{1}{4g}Z,

\displaystyle E_{23}={\rm i}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2g^{2}}S+\frac{1}{2\lambda}T+\frac{1}{4\lambda}U+\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}V+\frac{1}{2g}W-\frac{1}{4\lambda}X-\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}Y\right),

\displaystyle E_{32}={\rm i}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2g^{2}}S+\frac{1}{2\lambda}T+\frac{1}{4\lambda}U+\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}V+\frac{1}{2g}W+\frac{1}{4\lambda}X+\frac{g}{4\lambda^{2}}Y\right).

The
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
linear Casimir operator corresponds to

\displaystyle E_{11}+E_{22}+E_{33}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(T+U+\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}R+\frac{g}{\lambda}V-3\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(H-\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}R-\frac{g}{\lambda}V+3\lambda\right),

(4.4)

so that it is a linear combination of the three commuting operators
H
,
R
, and
V
, up to some additive constant.

Rewriting the generators
E_{ij}
in terms of
A^{\pm}
,
B^{\pm}
, and
C^{\pm}
, as done in Appendix
A
, allows to reveal some underlying structure in terms of bosonic operators
a^{\pm}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, satisfying the well-known commutation relations

\displaystyle[a_{i}^{-},a_{j}^{+}]=\delta_{i,j},\qquad[a_{i}^{\pm},a_{j}^{\pm}]=0.

On using the transformation

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{1}^{\pm}=\frac{\rm i}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}C^{\pm},\qquad a_{2}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\left(C^{\pm}+\frac{\lambda}{g}B^{\pm}\right),\\
&a_{3}^{\pm}=\frac{\rm i}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\left(C^{\pm}+\frac{\lambda}{g}B^{\pm}+\frac{g}{\lambda}A^{\pm}\right),\end{split}

(4.5)

it is indeed possible to rewrite
E_{ij}
as

\displaystyle E_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\{a_{i}^{+},a_{j}^{-}\}=a_{i}^{+}a_{j}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}.

From the inverse transformation of (
4.5
),

\displaystyle A^{\pm}=-\frac{\lambda}{g}\sqrt{2\lambda}(a_{2}^{\pm}+{\rm i}a_{3}^{\pm}),\qquad B^{\pm}=\frac{g}{\lambda}\sqrt{2\lambda}(a_{2}^{\pm}+{\rm i}a_{1}^{\pm}),\qquad C^{\pm}=-{\rm i}\sqrt{2\lambda}a_{1}^{\pm},

we can also express
Q^{\pm}
and
H
in terms of the bosonic operators,

\displaystyle Q^{\pm}=2\lambda\big[(a_{1}^{\pm})^{2}-2(a_{2}^{\pm})^{2}-2{\rm i}a_{1}^{\pm}a_{2}^{\pm}+2a_{1}^{\pm}a_{3}^{\pm}-2{\rm i}a_{2}^{\pm}a_{3}^{\pm}\big],

\displaystyle H=2\lambda\big[a_{1}^{+}a_{1}^{-}+2a_{2}^{+}a_{2}^{-}+{\rm i}(a_{1}^{+}a_{2}^{-}+a_{2}^{+}a_{1}^{-})-(a_{1}^{+}a_{3}^{-}+a_{3}^{+}a_{1}^{-})+{\rm i}(a_{2}^{+}a_{3}^{-}+a_{3}^{+}a_{2}^{-})\big].

The
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
hidden symmetry algebra can be embedded into an
{\mathfrak{sp}}(6)
algebra by considering the additional generators
[
16
]

\displaystyle D_{ij}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\{a_{i}^{+},a_{j}^{+}\},\qquad D_{ij}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}\{a_{i}^{-},a_{j}^{-}\}.

Together with the bosonic operators, the operators
E_{ij}
,
D^{+}_{ij}
, and
D^{-}_{ij}
then make rise to an
{\mathfrak{osp}}(1/6)
superalgebra
[
11
]
(see
[
15
]
for more details).

Finally, we may also point out the nonstandard differential operator realization of the bosonic operators,

\displaystyle a_{1}^{\pm}=\frac{\rm i}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}(\partial_{3}\pm g\bar{z}\mp\lambda x_{3}),\quad a_{2}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{g}\partial_{\bar{z}}+\partial_{3}\mp\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2g}z\pm g\bar{z}\right),

\displaystyle a_{3}^{\pm}=\frac{\rm i}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\left(2\frac{g}{\lambda}\partial_{z}+\frac{\lambda}{g}\partial_{\bar{z}}+\partial_{3}\mp\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2g}z\right).

This completes the description of the hidden symmetry algebra. As compared with the Hermitian case, the analysis carried out here has shown that the Hamiltonian connects in a different way with the algebra Casimir operator and that a nonstandard realization of bosonic operators makes its appearance. Further progress on these ideas might provide a way to classify some classes of nonseparable and nondiagonalizable models.

4.2
Superintegrability and cubic algebra

In
[
2
]
, four independent operators
R_{0}
,
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
, and
R_{3}
commuting with
H
were identified, the first two being mutually commuting. Here we plan to relate such a superintegrability property with the hidden symmetry algebra.

On expressing the operators of
[
2
]
in terms of the bilinear operators (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle R_{0}=A^{+}A^{-}=R,

\displaystyle R_{1}=\frac{1}{8g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}[Q^{+},Q^{-}]+4\lambda H+12\lambda^{2}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}V,

\displaystyle R_{2}=\frac{1}{8\lambda}[A^{+}A^{-},Q^{+}Q^{-}]=-R(X-2\lambda)+\frac{1}{4}\{V,Y\},

\displaystyle R_{3}=Q^{+}(A^{-})^{2}=R(U-X+4\lambda)-\frac{1}{4}\big(V^{2}+Y^{2}-\{V,Y\}\big).

From the commutation relations (
4.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle[H,R_{0}]=[H,R_{1}]=[H,R_{2}]=[H,R_{3}]=0,

(4.6)

while equation (
4.2
) leads to

\displaystyle[R_{0},R_{1}]=0,\qquad[R_{0},R_{2}]=-4\lambda R_{0}^{2},\qquad[R_{1},R_{2}]=2gR_{0}^{2},

(4.7)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[R_{0},R_{3}]-4\lambda R_{0}^{2},\qquad[R_{1},R_{3}]=2gR_{0}^{2},\\
&[R_{2},R_{3}]=8gR_{1}R_{0}^{2}+4\lambda(R_{3}-R_{2})R_{0}+8\lambda R_{1}^{2}R_{0}.\end{split}

(4.8)

Equations (
4.6
) and(
4.7
) agree with some results derived in
[
2
]
, while equation (
4.8
) is new and shows that the integrals of motion generate a cubic algebra.

Note that the superintegrability property of the present model contrasts with what was obtained in
[
15
]
for the two-dimensional pseudo-Hermitian oscillator, which was proved to be only integrable. This points out that in the context of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians, the number of integrals of motion may be affected by the structure of the problem.

5
Nondiagonalizability and construction of associated functions

As pointed out above, the Hamiltonian
H
being nondiagonalizable, the excited wavefunctions
\Psi_{k,n,0}
with
n\neq 0
have to be accompanied with some associated functions
\Psi_{k,n,m}
,
m=1,2,\dots,p_{n}-1
, completing the Jordan blocks. By definition, these functions obey the relation

\displaystyle(H-E_{k,n})\Psi_{k,n,m}=\Psi_{k,n,m-1},\qquad m=1,2,\dots,p_{n}-1.

(5.1)

It is the purpose of the present section to determine them and to establish that the dimension of the Jordan blocks is
p_{n}=2n+1
. We plan to rely on the new sets of ladder operators of Section
3
to provide an algebraic construction of these associated functions. Furthermore, we will also build a subset of them in terms of multivariate polynomials. Finally, we will determine the action of the ladder operators and of the
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
Casimir operator on the associated functions.

5.1
Algebraic construction of associated functions

Let us start by noting some equivalences among polynomials of the operators acting on the ground state,

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda)B^{+}\Psi_{0}=-2gC^{+}\Psi_{0},\qquad(H-2\lambda)^{2}B^{+}\Psi_{0}=4g^{2}A^{+}\Psi_{0}\propto\Psi_{0,1,0}.

Hence
(H-2\lambda)B^{+}\Psi_{0}\propto\Psi_{0,1,1}
and
B^{+}\Psi_{0}\propto\Psi_{0,1,2}
.
We would like to extend these results by showing that

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda n)^{2n}(B^{+})^{n}\Psi_{0}\propto(A^{+})^{n}\Psi_{0}\propto\Psi_{0,n,0},

and more generally that

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda n)^{2p}(B^{+})^{n}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(B^{+})^{n-2p}\sum_{q=0}^{p}a_{q}^{(n,p)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2p-2q}\Psi_{0},\qquad p=0,1,\dots,n,

(5.2)

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda n)^{2p+1}(B^{+})^{n}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(B^{+})^{n-2p-1}\sum_{q=0}^{p}b_{q}^{(n,p)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2p+1-2q}\Psi_{0},\qquad p=0,1,\dots,n-1,

(5.3)

for some coefficients
a^{(n,p)}_{q}
and
b^{(n,p)}_{q}
to be determined.

Let us first point out some auxiliary results,

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda n)(A^{+})^{p}=(A^{+})^{p}[H-2\lambda(n-p)],

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda n)(B^{+})^{p}=(B^{+})^{p}[H-2\lambda(n-p)]-2pq(B^{+})^{p-1}C^{+},

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda n)(C^{+})^{p}=(C^{+})^{p}[H-2\lambda(n-p)]-2pgA^{+}(C^{+})^{p-1},

which directly follow from the commutation relations established in Sections
2
and
3
.

By acting on (
5.2
) with
(H-2\lambda n)
and identifying the result with equation (
5.3
), we get the coefficients
b_{q}^{(n,p)}
in terms of
a_{r}^{(n,p)}
,

\displaystyle b_{0}^{(n,p)}=-2g(n-2p)a_{0}^{(n,p)},

\displaystyle b_{q}^{(n,p)}=-2g\big[(2p-2q+2)a_{q-1}^{(n,p)}+(n-2p+q)a_{q}^{(n,p)}\big],\qquad q=1,2,\dots,p.

Alternatively, by acting on (
5.3
) with
(H-2\lambda n)
and identifying the result with equation (
5.2
), where
p
is replaced by
p+1
, we find the coefficients
a_{q}^{(n,p+1)}
in terms of
b_{r}^{(n,p)}
,

\displaystyle a_{0}^{(n,p+1)}=-2g(n-2p-1)b_{0}^{(n,p)},

\displaystyle a_{q}^{(n,p+1)}=-2g\big[(2p+3-2q)b_{q-1}^{(n,p)}+(n-2p+q-1)b_{q}^{(n,p)}\big],\quad q=1,2,\dots,p,

\displaystyle a_{p+1}^{(n,p+1)}=-2gb_{p}^{(n,p)}.

On eliminating
b_{q}^{(n,p)}
or
a_{q}^{(n,p)}
between the two sets of relations, we obtain recursion relations for
a_{q}^{(n,p)}
or
b_{q}^{(n,p)}
,

\displaystyle a_{0}^{(n,p+1)}=4g^{2}(n-2p-1)(n-2p)a_{0}^{(n,p)},

\displaystyle a_{1}^{(n,p+1)}=4g^{2}(n-2p)\big[(4p+1)a_{0}^{(n,p)}+(n-2p+1)a_{1}^{(n,p)}\big],

\displaystyle a_{q}^{(n,p+1)}=4g^{2}\big[(2p-2q+3)(2p-2q+4)a_{q-2}^{(n,p)}+(n-2p+q-1)(4p-4q+5)a_{q-1}^{(n,p)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{a_q^{(n,p+1)}=}{}+(n-2p+q-1)(n-2p+q)a_{q}^{(n,p)}\big],\quad q=2,3,\dots,p,

\displaystyle a_{p+1}^{(n,p+1)}=4g^{2}\big[2a_{p-1}^{(n,p)}+(n-p)a_{p}^{(n,p)}\big],

and

\displaystyle b_{0}^{(n,p+1)}=4g^{2}(n-2p-2)(n-2p-1)b_{0}^{(n,p)},

\displaystyle b_{1}^{(n,p+1)}=4g^{2}(n-2p-1)\big[(4p+3)b_{0}^{(n,p)}+(n-2p)b_{1}^{(n,p)}\big],

\displaystyle b_{q}^{(n,p+1)}=4g^{2}\big[(2p-2q+4)(2p-2q+5)b_{q-2}^{(n,p)}+(n-2p+q-2)(4p-4q+7)b_{q-1}^{(n,p)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{b_q^{(n,p+1)}=}{}+(n-2p+q-2)(n-2p+q-1)b_{q}^{(n,p)}\big],\quad q=2,3,\dots,p,

\displaystyle b_{p+1}^{(n,p+1)}=12g^{2}\big[2b_{p-1}^{(n,p)}+(n-p-1)b_{p}^{(n,p)}\big],

respectively.

The solutions of the recursion relations are given by

\displaystyle a_{q}^{(n,p)}=(2g)^{2p}\frac{n!}{(n-2p+q)!}\frac{p!}{q!(p-q)!}\frac{(2p-1)!!}{(2p-2q-1)!!}

(5.4)

and

\displaystyle b_{q}^{(n,p)}=(-2g)^{2p+1}\frac{n!}{(n-2p+q-1)!}\frac{p!}{q!(p-q)!}\frac{(2p+1)!!}{(2p-2q+1)!!}.

(5.5)

At this stage, it is worth noting that in equations (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), the summations over
q
do not really go from
0
to
p
because the exponents of the operators have to be nonnegative, hence
q
may not be smaller than
2p-n
and
2p+1-n
, respectively. In fact, the summations run from max
(0,2p-n)
to
p
or max
(0,2p+1-n)
to
p
. This property is accounted for by the presence of
(n-2p+q)!
or
(n-2p+q-1)!
in the denominator for
a_{q}^{(n,p)}
or
b_{q}^{(n,p)}
. We may indeed interpret these factorials as
\Gamma(n-2p+q+1)
or
\Gamma(n-2p+q)

or
q\leq 2p-n
and produce the vanishing of the corresponding
a_{q}^{(n,p)}
or
b_{q}^{(n,p)}
.

More generally, on applying
(Q^{+})^{k}
to both sides of equations (
5.2
) and (
5.3

[H,Q^{+}]=4\lambda Q^{+}
and
[Q^{+},A^{+}]=[Q^{+},B^{+}]=[Q^{+},C^{+}]=0

\displaystyle[H-2\lambda(n+2k)]^{2p}(B^{+})^{n}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(B^{+})^{n-2p}\sum_{q=0}^{p}a_{q}^{(n,p)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2p-2q}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0},\qquad p=0,1,\dots,n,

and

\displaystyle[H-2\lambda(n+2k)]^{2p+1}(B^{+})^{n}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(B^{+})^{n-2p-1}\sum_{q=0}^{p}b_{q}^{(n,p)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2p+1-2q}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0},\qquad p=0,1,\dots,n-1,

so that in particular

\displaystyle[H-2\lambda(n+2k)]^{2n}(B^{+})^{n}Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}=a^{(n,n)}_{n}(A^{+})^{n}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}\propto\Psi_{k,n,0}.

(5.6)

with

\displaystyle a^{(n,n)}_{n}=(2g)^{2n}n!(2n-1)!!.

This establishes that the dimension of the Jordan blocks is
p_{n}=2n+1
and that the associated functions can be obtained from the ground state in the form

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,n,m}=d_{k,n}[H-2\lambda(n+2k))]^{2n-m}(B^{+})^{n}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0},\qquad m=1,2,\dots,2n.

(5.7)

Their detailed expressions in terms of the ladder operators are given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{k,n,m}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle d_{k,n}(B^{+})^{2\mu-n}\sum\limits_{q=\max(0,n-2\mu)}^{n-\mu}a_{q}^{(n,n-\mu)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}&\\
\displaystyle\qquad{}\times(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}&\text{if $m=2\mu$},\\
\displaystyle d_{k,n}(B^{+})^{2\mu+1-n}\sum\limits_{q=\max(0,n-2\mu-1)}^{n-\mu-1}b_{q}^{(n,n-\mu-1)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}&\\
\displaystyle\qquad{}\times(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q-1}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}&\text{if $m=2\mu+1$},\end{cases}

(5.8)

where the coefficients are expressed in (
5.4
) and (

), and p has been replaced by
n-\mu
or

5.2
Some associated functions in terms of multivariate polynomials

After presenting an algebraic description of the associated functions, we will now use an alternative approach wherein some of them will be written in terms of polynomials in the variables
z
,
\bar{z}
, and
x_{3}
.

To start with, it is useful to introduce a new set of variables,

\displaystyle u=\bar{z},\qquad v=-\lambda z+2gx_{3},\qquad w=g\bar{z}-\lambda x_{3},

which are directly related to the action of the raising operators
A^{+}
,
B^{+}
, and
C^{+}
on
\Psi_{0}
,

\displaystyle A^{+}\Psi_{0}=-2\lambda u\Psi_{0},\qquad B^{+}\Psi_{0}=v\Psi_{0},\qquad C^{+}\Psi_{0}=2w\Psi_{0}.

The Jacobian of the transformation is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(u,v,w)}{\partial(z,\bar{z},x_{3})}=\left|\begin{matrix}0&1&0\\
-\lambda&0&2g\\
0&g&-\lambda\end{matrix}\right|=-\lambda^{2}

and the inverse transformation writes

\displaystyle z=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(2g^{2}u-\lambda v-2gw),\qquad\bar{z}=u,\qquad x_{3}=\frac{1}{\lambda}(gu-w).

Furthermore, we may introduce a new operator
D_{p}
, defined by

\displaystyle(H-2\lambda p)\Psi_{0}=\Psi_{0}D_{p},

and whose explicit expression in terms of the new variables is

\displaystyle D_{p}=4\lambda\partial^{2}_{uv}-4g^{2}\partial^{2}_{v}+8\lambda g\partial^{2}_{vw}-\lambda^{2}\partial^{2}_{w}+2\lambda(u\partial_{u}+v\partial_{v}+w\partial_{w}-p)-4gw\partial_{v}+2\lambda gu\partial_{w}.

Its action on the monomials
u^{i}
,
v^{i}
, and
w^{i}
is given by

\displaystyle D_{p}u^{i}=u^{i}D_{p}+2\lambda iu^{i-1}(2\partial_{v}+u),

\displaystyle D_{p}v^{i}=v^{i}D_{p}+iv^{i-1}\big(4\lambda\partial_{u}-8g^{2}\partial_{v}+8\lambda g\partial_{w}+2\lambda v-4gw\big)-4i(i-1)g^{2}v^{i-2},

\displaystyle D_{p}w^{i}=w^{i}D_{p}+2\lambda iw^{i-1}(4g\partial_{v}-\lambda\partial_{w}+gu+w)-i(i-1)\lambda^{2}w^{i-2}.

Let us consider the set of functions belonging to a Jordan block with
k=0
and any
n
(or, in other words, those belonging to the lower lattice in Figure
1
),

\displaystyle\Psi_{0,n,2n-p}=d_{0,n}(H-E_{0n})^{p}v^{n}\Psi_{0},\qquad p=0,1,\dots,2n,

and introduce the notation

\displaystyle v_{i}^{(n)}=\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n-i+1)}v^{n-i},\qquad i=0,1,\dots,n,

so that
v_{0}^{(n)}=v^{n}
,
v_{i}^{(n)}=n(n-1)\cdots(n-i+1)v^{n-i}
, with
i=1,2,\dots,n
, and

\displaystyle D_{n}v_{i}^{(n)}=-2\lambda iv_{i}^{(n)}-4gv_{i+1}^{(n)}w-4g^{2}v_{i+2}^{(n)}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi_{0,n,2n-p}=d_{0,n}\Psi_{0}\sum_{q=[\frac{p+1}{2}]}^{2p}v_{q}^{(n)}f_{q}^{(n,p)}(u,w),

where

\displaystyle f_{q}^{(n,p)}(u,w)=\sum_{r,s}\alpha_{r,s}^{(n,p,q)}u^{r}w^{s}

is a polynomial of total degree
2p-q
and total parity
(-1)^{q}
in the cubic variable
u
and the linear one
w
. This means that
3r+s\leq 2p-q
and
(-1)^{3r+s}=(-1)^{q}
. The coefficients
\alpha^{(n,p,q)}_{r,s}
satisfy the recursion relation

\displaystyle\alpha_{r,s}^{(n,p+1,q)}=\lambda\big[2(r+s-q)\alpha_{r,s}^{(n,p,q)}+2q(s+1)\alpha_{r-1,s+1}^{(n,p,q)}-\lambda(s+1)(s+2)\alpha_{r,s+2}^{(n,p,q)}\big]

\displaystyle\hphantom{\alpha_{r,s}^{(n,p+1,q)} =}{}+4\big[{-}g\alpha_{r,s-1}^{(n,p,q-1)}+2\lambda g(s+1)\alpha_{r,s+1}^{(n,p,q-1)}+\lambda(r+1)\alpha_{r+1,s}^{(n,p,q-1)}\big]-4g^{2}\alpha_{r,s}^{(n,p,q-2)},

which can be solved for low values of
p
and
q
. Some examples of polynomials
f^{(n,p)}_{q}(u,w)
are presented in Appendix
B
.

5.3
Action of the ladder operators and of the
\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)}
Casimir operator

on
\boldsymbol{\Psi_{k,n,m}}

The structure of the functions
\Psi_{k,n,m}
is complicated, but using our algebraic description in terms of the ladder operators
A^{\pm}
,
B^{\pm}
, and
C^{\pm}
allows us to establish explicit formulas for the action of these operators.

For
A^{+}
, we get

\displaystyle A^{+}\psi_{k,n,m}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,m)}\psi_{k,n+1,m}+\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,m)}\psi_{k+1,n-1,m-2},

where the coefficients
\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,m)}
and
\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,m)}
are given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,m)}=\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n+1}}\frac{1}{4g^{2}(n+1)(2n+1)},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,m)}=\begin{cases}\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k+1,n-1}}\frac{n}{2n+1}&\text{if $m\geq 2$},\\
0&\text{if $m=0$ or 1},\end{cases}

(5.11)

in terms of the normalization coefficients, whose calculation will be discussed in Section
6
.

This result can be demonstrated by direct calculations. Let us provide some details on the proof.

Case 1
:
m=2\mu
. Assuming
n\geq 2\mu
, we get

\displaystyle A^{+}\Psi_{k,n,2\mu}=d_{k,n}(B^{+})^{2\mu-n-1}\sum_{q=n-2\mu}^{n-\mu}a_{q}^{(n,n-\mu)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q+1}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,2\mu)}d_{k,n+1}(B^{+})^{2\mu-n-1}\sum_{q=n-2\mu+1}^{n-\mu+1}a_{q}^{(n+1,n-\mu+1)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q+2}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,2\mu)}d_{k+1,n-1}(B^{+})^{2\mu-n-1}\Biggl[2\sum_{q=n-2\mu+2}^{n-\mu+1}a_{q-1}^{(n-1,n-\mu)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q+2}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sum_{q=n-2\mu+1}^{n-\mu}a_{q}^{(n-1,n-\mu)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q+2}\Biggr](Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}.

By equating both expressions, we obtain the following constraints to be satisfied:

\displaystyle d_{k,n}a_{n-2\mu}^{(n,n-\mu)}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,2\mu)}d_{k,n+1}a_{n-2\mu+1}^{(n+1,n-\mu+1)}-\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,2\mu)}d_{k+1,n-1}a_{n-2\mu+1}^{(n-1,n-\mu)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{k,n} a_{n-2\mu}^{(n,n-\mu)} =}{}\text{if $q=n-2\mu+1$},

\displaystyle d_{k,n}a_{q-1}^{(n,n-\mu)}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,2\mu)}d_{k,n+1}a_{q}^{(n+1,n-\mu+1)}+\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,2\mu)}d_{k+1,n-1}\big(2a_{q-1}^{(n-1,n-\mu)}-a_{q}^{(n-1,n-\mu)}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{k,n} a_{q-1}^{(n,n-\mu)} =}{}\text{if $n-2\mu+2\leq q\leq n-\mu$},

\displaystyle d_{k,n}a_{n-\mu}^{(n,n-\mu)}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n)}d_{k,n+1}a_{n-\mu+1}^{(n+1,n-\mu+1)}+2\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,2\mu)}d_{k+1,n-1}a_{n-\mu}^{(n-1,n-\mu)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{k,n} a_{n-\mu}^{(n,n-\mu)}}{}\text{if $q=n-\mu+1$}.

These are fulfilled due to the definitions of
\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,m)}
,
\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,m)}
, and
a_{q}^{(n,n-\mu)}
. Whenever
n<2\mu
, a similar procedure leads to the proof of equations (
5.10
) and (
5.11
).

Case 2
:
m=2\mu+1
.
Assuming
n\geq 2\mu+1
, we get

\displaystyle A^{+}\Psi_{k,n,2\mu+1}=d_{k,n}(B^{+})^{2\mu-n}\sum_{q=n-2\mu}^{n-\mu}b_{q-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q+1}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle{}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,2\mu+1}d_{k,n+1}(B^{+})^{2\mu-n}\sum_{q=n-2\mu}^{n-\mu}b_{q}^{(n+1,n-\mu)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q+1}(Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,2\mu+1)}d_{k+1,n-1}(B^{+})^{2\mu-n}\Biggl[2\sum_{q=n-2\mu+1}^{n-\mu}b_{q-1}^{(n-1,n-\mu-1)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q+1}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sum_{q=n-2\mu}^{n-\mu-1}b_{q}^{(n-1,n-\mu-1)}(A^{+}B^{+})^{q}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q+1}\Biggr](Q^{+})^{k}\Psi_{0},

thus leading to the following set of constraints by equating similar terms:

\displaystyle d_{k,n}b_{n-2\mu-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,2\mu+1)}d_{k,n+1}b_{n-2\mu}^{(n+1,n-\mu)}-\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,2\mu+1)}d_{k+1,n-1}b_{n-2\mu}^{(n-1,n-\mu-1)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{k,n} b_{n-2\mu-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)} =}{}\text{if $q=n-2\mu$},

\displaystyle d_{k,n}b_{q-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,2\mu+1)}d_{k,n+1}b_{q}^{(n+1,n-\mu)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{k,n} b_{q-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)} =}{}+\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,2\mu+1)}d_{k+1,n-1}\big(2b_{q-1}^{(n-1,n-\mu-1)}-b_{q}^{(n-1,n-\mu-1)}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{k,n} b_{q-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)} =}{}\text{if $n-2\mu+1\leq q\leq n-\mu-1$},

\displaystyle d_{k,n}b_{n-\mu-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)}=\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,2\mu+1)}d_{k,n+1}b_{n-\mu}^{(n+1,n-\mu)}+2\alpha_{1}^{(k,,n,2\mu+1)}d_{k+1,n-1}b_{n-\mu-1}^{(n-1,n-\mu-1)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{k,n} b_{n-\mu-1}^{(n,n-\mu-1)} =}{}\text{if $q=n-\mu$}.

These can be shown to be satisfied by using the explicit formulas for
\alpha_{0}^{(k,n,m)}
,
\alpha_{1}^{(k,n,m)}
, and
b_{q}^{(n,n-\mu)}
. After considering the case where
n<2\mu+1
in a similar way, the proof of the action of
A^{+}
on the states
\Psi_{k,n,m}
forming Jordan blocks is complete.

The action of
B^{+}
and
C^{+}
can be obtained in the same way by considering appropriate subcases for the index and deriving a set of constraints. We will only present the final results:

\displaystyle B^{+}\Psi_{k,n,m}=\beta_{0}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k,n+1,m+2}+\beta_{1}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k+1,n-1,m},

where

\displaystyle\beta_{0}^{(k,n,m)}=\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n+1}}\frac{(m+1)(m+2)}{2(n+1)(2n+1)},

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{(k,nm)}=\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k+1,n-1}}\frac{2g^{2}n(2n-m-1)(2n-m)}{2n+1},

and

\displaystyle C^{+}\Psi_{k,n,m}=\gamma_{0}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k,n+1,m+1}+\gamma_{1}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k+1,n-1,m-1},

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}^{(k,n,m)}=-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n+1}}\frac{m+1}{2g(n+1)(2n+1)},

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}^{(k,nm)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k+1,n-1}}\frac{2gn(2n-m)}{2n+1}&\text{if $m\geq 1$},\\
0&\text{if $m=0$}.\end{cases}

The action of the operators
A^{-}
,
B^{-}
, and
C^{-}
is more complicated, because we have to use their commutation relations with
A^{+}
,
B^{+}
,
C^{+}
, and
Q^{+}
, given in Sections
2
and
3
, but it can be straightforwardly established. The results are given by

\displaystyle A^{-}\Psi_{k,n,m}=\bar{\alpha}_{0}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k-1,n+1,m}+\bar{\alpha}_{1}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k,n-1,m-2},

where

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{0}^{(k,n,m)}=-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k-1,n+1}}\frac{\lambda k}{g^{2}(n+1)(2n+1)},

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{1}^{(k,n,m)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n-1}}\frac{2\lambda n(2k+2n+1)}{2n+1}&\text{if $m\geq 2$},\\
0&\text{if $m=0$ or 1},\end{cases}

\displaystyle B^{-}\Psi_{k,n,m}=\bar{\beta}_{0}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k-1,n+1,m+1}+\bar{\beta}_{1}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k-1,n+1,m+2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{B^{-}\Psi_{k,n,m}=}{}+\bar{\beta}_{2}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k,n-1,m-1}+\bar{\beta}_{3}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k,n-1,m},

where

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}_{0}^{(k,n,m)}=\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k-1,n+1}}\frac{2k(m+1)}{(n+1)(2n+1)},

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}_{1}^{(k,n,m)}=-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k-1,n+1}}\frac{2\lambda k(m+1)(m+2)}{(n+1)(2n+1)},

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}_{2}^{(k,n,m)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n-1}}\frac{4g^{2}n(2n-m)(2k+2n+1)}{2n+1}&\text{if $m\geq 1$},\\
0&\text{if $m=0$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}_{3}^{(k,n,m)}=-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n-1}}\frac{4\lambda g^{2}n(2n-m)(2n-m-1)(2k+2n+1)}{2n+1},

and

\displaystyle C^{-}\Psi_{k,n,m}=\bar{\gamma}_{0}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k-1,n+1,m}+\bar{\gamma}_{1}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k-1,n+1,m+1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{C^{-}\Psi_{k,n,m}=}{}+\bar{\gamma}_{2}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k,n-1,m-2}+\bar{\gamma}_{3}^{(k,n,m)}\Psi_{k,n-1,m-1},

where

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}_{0}^{(k,n,m)}=\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k-1,n+1}}\frac{k}{g(n+1)(2n+1)},

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}_{1}^{(k,n,m)}=-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k-1,n+1}}\frac{2\lambda k(m+1)}{g(n+1)(2n+1)},

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}_{2}^{(k,n,m)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n-1}}\frac{2gn(2k+2n+1)}{2n+1}&\text{if $m\geq 2$},\\
0&\text{if $m=0$ or 1},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}_{3}^{(k,n,m)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k,n-1}}\frac{4\lambda g(2k+2n+1)n(2n-m)}{2n+1}&\text{if $m\geq 1$},\\
0&\text{if $m=0$}.\end{cases}

Finally, let us consider the action of the
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
linear Casimir operator, which, from (
4.4
), can be expressed in terms of the operators
H
,
R=A^{+}A^{-}
, and
V=A^{+}C^{-}+C^{+}A^{-}
. From equation (
5.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle H\Psi_{k,n,m}=2\lambda(2k+n)\Psi_{k,n,m}+\Psi_{k,n,m-1},

while the results obtained above can be combined to yield

\displaystyle R\Psi_{k,n,m}=-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k-1,n+2}}\frac{\lambda k}{4g^{4}(n+1)(n+2)(2n+1)(2n+3)}\Psi_{k-1,n+2,m}

\displaystyle\hphantom{R \Psi_{k,n,m} =}{}-\frac{\lambda(4k+2n+3)}{2g^{2}(2n-1)(2n+3)}\Psi_{k,n,m-2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{R \Psi_{k,n,m} =}{}-\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k+1,n-2}}\frac{2\lambda n(n-1)(2k+2n+1)}{(2n-1)(2n+1)}\Psi_{k+1,n-2,m-4}

and

\displaystyle V\Psi_{k,n,m}=\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k-1,n+2}}\frac{k}{4g^{3}(n+1)(n+2)(2n+1)(2n+3)}\Psi_{k-1,n+2,m}

\displaystyle\hphantom{V\Psi_{k,n,m} =}{}+\frac{4k+2n+3}{2g(2n-1)(2n+3)}\psi_{k,n,m-2}+\frac{\lambda}{g}\Psi_{k,n,m-1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{V\Psi_{k,n,m} =}{}+\frac{d_{k,n}}{d_{k+1,n-2}}\frac{2gn(n-1)(2k+2n+1)}{(2n-1)(2n+1)}\Psi_{k+1,n-2,m-4}.

These equations lead to the following action for the linear Casimir operator

\displaystyle(E_{11}+E_{22}+E_{33})\Psi_{k,n,m}=\left(2k+n+\frac{3}{2}\right)\Psi_{k,n,m}.

We conclude that, in spite of the presence of Jordan blocks, the Casimir operator has the same action on their member states as on the eigenstates of the Hermitian harmonic oscillator.

Construction of an extended biorthogonal basis

The Hamiltonian being pseudo-Hermitian with
\eta=P_{2}
, one has to use a new scalar product
[

4

,

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|\eta|\Phi\rangle=\langle\langle\Psi|\Phi\rangle\rangle=\int\Psi\Phi\,{\rm d}^{3}x.

For the ground state (

\langle\langle\Psi_{0}|\Psi_{0}\rangle\rangle=\sqrt{\big(\frac{\pi}{\lambda}\big)^{3}}

makes it necessary to add some associated functions to the wavefunctions in order to complete the Jordan blocks, thereby resulting in a set of functions

for

[

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Psi_{k,n,m}|\Psi_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle=\langle\tilde{\Psi}_{k,n,m}|\Psi_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\rangle=\int\Psi_{k,n,m}\Psi_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\,{\rm d}^{3}x=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{m,2n-m^{\prime}}.

The purpose of this section is twofold: first, to determine the normalization coefficient

of the functions
\Psi_{k,n,m}
from the relation

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Psi_{k,n,m}|\Psi_{k,n,2n-m}\rangle\rangle=1

(6.1)

and second, to check the orthogonality of the functions
\Psi_{k,n,m}
and
\Psi_{k,n,m^{\prime}}
with

. In the calculations, we will rely on the commutation relations between
A^{\pm}
,
B^{\pm}
,
C^{\pm}
,
Q^{\pm}
and use the following results:

\displaystyle\langle\langle{\cal O}\Psi_{0}|{\cal O}^{\prime}\Psi_{0}\rangle\rangle=\langle\Psi_{0}|{\cal O}^{\dagger}\eta{\cal O}^{\prime}|\Psi_{0}\rangle,

\displaystyle(A^{+})^{\dagger}\eta=-\eta A^{-},\qquad(B^{+})^{\dagger}\eta=-\eta B^{-},\qquad(C^{+})^{\dagger}\eta=-\eta C^{-},\qquad(Q^{+})^{\dagger}\eta=\eta Q^{-},

\displaystyle A^{-}\Psi_{0}=B^{-}\Psi_{0}=C^{-}\Psi_{0}=Q^{-}\Psi_{0}=0.

6.1.1

The simplest calculation of

As a check, we have proved that the conditions

\displaystyle\qquad=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $q>n-2\mu+r$},\\
\displaystyle(-2\lambda)^{q}\frac{(n-2\mu+r)!}{(n-2\mu+r-q)!}\langle\Psi_{0}|\eta(C^{-})^{2n-2\mu-2q}(B^{-})^{2\mu-n+q}&\\

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{0}|\eta(C^{-})^{2n-2\mu-2q}(A^{+})^{s}(C^{+})^{2n-2\mu-2q-s}|\Psi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\delta_{s,0}(-2\lambda)^{2n-2\mu-2q}(2n-2\mu-2q)!\langle\langle\Psi_{0}|\Psi_{0}\rangle\rangle,

we get

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Psi_{0,n,2\mu}|\Psi_{0,n,2n-2\mu}\rangle\rangle=d_{0,n}^{2}\sum_{q=n-2\mu}^{n-\mu}a_{q}^{(n,n-\mu)}a_{2\mu-n+q}^{(n,\mu)}(2\lambda)^{n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\langle\langle\Psi_{0,n,2\mu}|\Psi_{0,n,2n-2\mu}\rangle\rangle=}{}\times q!(2\mu-n+q)!(2n-2\mu-2q)!\langle\langle\Psi_{0}|\Psi_{0}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Psi_{0,1,0}|\Psi_{0,1,0}\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle\Psi_{0,1,0}|\Psi_{0,1,1}\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle\Psi_{0,1,2}|\Psi_{0,1,2}\rangle\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Psi_{0,1,1}|\Psi_{0,1,2}\rangle\rangle=\frac{1}{2\lambda},

which show that the three states do not form an orthogonal basis, as it was expected. It is therefore necessary to orthogonalize it, which amounts to going from
\Psi_{0,1,m}
to
\Phi_{0,1,m}
, defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,1,0}=\Psi_{0,1,0},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,1,1}=\Psi_{0,1,1}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\Psi_{0,1,0},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,1,2}=\Psi_{0,1,2},

and such that
\langle\langle\Phi_{0,1,m}|\Phi_{0,1,m^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle=\delta_{m,2-m^{\prime}}
for
m,m^{\prime}=0,1,2
.

We have studied in the same way some other Jordan blocks and constructed, from the
\Psi_{k,n,m}
functions, new functions
\Phi_{k,n,m}
satisfying the condition

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Phi_{k,n,m}|\Phi_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{m,2n-m^{\prime}}.

For
k=0
and
n=2
, for instance,

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,2,0}=\Psi_{0,2,0},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,2,1}=\Psi_{0,2,1}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\Psi_{0,2,0},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,2,2}=\Psi_{0,2,2}+\frac{1}{6\lambda^{2}}\Psi_{0,2,0}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\Psi_{0,2,1},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,2,3}=\Psi_{0,2,3}+\frac{1}{48\lambda^{3}}\Psi_{0,2,0}-\frac{1}{24\lambda^{2}}\Psi_{0,2,1},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,2,4}=\Psi_{0,2,4},

while, for
k=0
and
n=3
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,3,0}=\Psi_{0,3,0},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,3,1}=\Psi_{0,3,1}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\Psi_{0,3,0},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,3,2}=\Psi_{0,3,2}+\frac{3}{20\lambda^{2}}\Psi_{0,3,0}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\Psi_{0,3,1},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,3,3}=\Psi_{0,3,3}-\frac{1}{30\lambda^{3}}\Psi_{0,3,0}+\frac{3}{20\lambda^{2}}\Psi_{0,3,1}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\Psi_{0,3,2},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,3,4}=\Psi_{0,3,4}-\frac{1}{300\lambda^{4}}\Psi_{0,3,0}+\frac{1}{60\lambda^{3}}\Psi_{0,3,1}-\frac{1}{20\lambda^{2}}\Psi_{0,3,2},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,3,5}=\Psi_{0,3,5},

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,3,6}=\Psi_{0,3,6}.

In this section, we have pointed out that the associated functions of the Jordan blocks for nondiagonalizable non-Hermitian Hamiltonians require much more involved calculations when going from two to three dimensions. The use of new ladder operators has, nevertheless, allowed us to conjecture the form of their general normalization coefficient and to enhance the need for an orthogonalization of the basis.

7
Conclusion

In the present paper, we have demonstrated that three sets of canonical ladder operators exist for the three-dimensional nonseparable and nondiagonalizable pseudo-Hermitian oscillator of
[
2
]
. They can be introduced from their action on the wavefunctions belonging to the lower lattice in Figure
1
.

These ladder operators have allowed us to show the existence of a nine-dimensional hidden symmetry algebra, which can be written in terms of
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
generators. The latter can be expressed in terms of bosonic operators in a nonstandard realization, which serve to embed
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
into an
{\mathfrak{sp}}(6)
algebra, as well as into an
{\mathfrak{osp}}(1/6)
superalgebra. Furthermore, we have connected the hidden symmetry algebra with the integrals responsible for the superintegrability of the model and established that the latter generate a cubic algebra.

The ladder operators have served to construct the associated functions completing the Jordan blocks, whose dimension has been established. We have also presented the action of these ladder operators on the associated functions, proved that the latter are eigenfunctions of the
{\mathfrak{gl}}(3)
linear Casimir operator, and written a subset of associated functions as multivariate polynomials.

Finally, we have studied in detail the construction of an extended biorthogonal basis and shown that its structure is more complicated than that considered in
[
15
]
for the corresponding two-dimensional model. Nevertheless, we have been able to conjecture the form of the associated function normalization coefficient and to establish the need for an orthogonalization of the basis.

The results obtained here point out some similarities between the present pseudo-Hermitian oscillator and the usual three-dimensional oscillator, but also indicate their very different nature. The method established in this paper may allow a broader understanding of these pseudo-Hermitian models. So far, only particular examples have been considered and no classification has been provided. Our understanding of the underlying hidden symmetry algebra may allow to provide other ideas to obtain and classify such models. Studies of supersymmetric quantum mechanics, superintegrability, separation of variables and hidden algebra have been restricted mostly to models whose states are eigenstates of the Hamiltonian. The present paper points out that models with Jordan blocks have features that make them interesting from a mathematical physics perspective. Among open problems, there is the application of those methods to pseudo-Hermitian anharmonic oscillator models
[
9
]
.

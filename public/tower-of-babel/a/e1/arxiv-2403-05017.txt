A weighted decoupling inequality and its application to the maximal Bochner-Riesz problem
Shengwen Gan, Shukun Wu

Abstract
. We prove some weighted L p ​ ℓ p L^{p}\ell^{p} -decoupling estimates when p = 2 ​ n / ( n − 1 ) p=2n/(n-1) . As an application, we give a result beyond the real interpolation exponents for the maximal Bochner-Riesz operator in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . We also make an improvement in the planar case.



1.
Introduction

Let
S
be a compact
C^{2}
hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with positive second fundamental form. For simplicity, let us assume that
S
has the following expression:

\{(\bar{x},\psi(\bar{x})):\bar{x}\in[0,1]^{n-1}\},

where
\psi
is a
C^{2}
bounded function and
D^{2}\psi(\bar{x})
is positive definite for every
x^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{n-1}
. A typical example is
\psi(\bar{x})=|\bar{x}|^{2}
, in which
S
is a truncated paraboloid.

For
R\geq 1
, denote by
N_{R^{-1}}(S)
the
R^{-1}
-neighborhood of
S
. We partition
N_{R^{-1}}(S)
into rectangular parallelepipeds
\Theta=\{\theta\}

of dimensions
R^{-1}\times R^{-1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{-1/2}
, each of which is called a
R^{-1/2}
-cap. For any function
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote by
f_{\theta}
the Fourier restriction of
f
onto
\theta
, that is,
f_{\theta}=({\bf{1}}_{\theta}\widehat{f})^{\vee}
. A celebrated decoupling theorem by Bourgain and Demeter
[
BD15
]
states that

(1.1)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n-1}{2}\cdot\frac{p-2}{2}}\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{p}^{p}

for
2\leq p\leq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
and any function
f
such that
{\rm supp}\widehat{f}\subset N_{R^{-1}}(S)
.

It is natural to replace the integration domain
B_{R}
on the left-hand side with a subset
Y\subset B_{R}
, and ask for a refined estimate for
\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}
.
This kind of estimate appeared in many references and has applications to other problems. For example, in
[
DGL17
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
DZ19
, Theorem 1.6]
, the set
Y
is chosen to be a union of unit balls in
B_{R}
that resembles an
(n-1)
-dimensional set, and it has an application to the maximal Schrödinger problem. Another example is in
[
GIOW20
, Theorem 4.2]
, where
Y
is a union of
R^{1/2}
-balls in
B_{R}
that have controlled number of intersections with wave packets of
f
. This type of estimate has application to the Falconer distance problem. See also the recent work in
[
DORZ23
]
.

In this paper, we will study another variant of the decoupling inequality for
\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}
, and apply it to the maximal Bochner-Riesz problem. The nature of the Bochner-Riesz problem needs an estimate for
\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}
with
Y
being an arbitrary subset of
B_{R}
. We will show that if
p
is strictly smaller than
2(n+1)/(n-1)
, the decoupling endpoint, then whenever
Y
is a small subset of
B_{R}
, there is a refinement of the classical decoupling estimate (
1.1
).

Specifically, let
p_{n}=\frac{2n}{n-1}
be the (multilinear) restriction endpoint. If plugging in
p=p_{n}
to (
1.1
), we obtain

(1.2)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(B_{R})}^{p_{n}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}R^{1/2}\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}.

If
B_{R}
is replaced by a subset
Y
, we expect some stronger decoupling inequality than (
1.2
). More concretely, we hope that for some
\alpha_{n}>0
,

(1.3)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}^{p_{n}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{|Y|}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}.

It is clear that when
\alpha_{n}
is bigger, (
1.3
) is stronger. When
\alpha_{n}=0
, it is just (
1.2
).

The goal is to find the biggest
\alpha_{n}
so that for any
Y\subset B_{R}
, (
1.3
) holds. Since
f
is locally constant at scale
1
(
\widehat{f}
is supported on in the unit ball), we just need to study the case when
Y
is a disjoint union of unit balls. Our result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
Y
is a union of unit balls in
B_{R}
. Denote
\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{2n(3n-1)}
when
n\geq 3
and
\alpha_{n}=\frac{n-1}{4n^{2}}=\frac{1}{16}
when
n=2
. Also denote
p_{n}=\frac{2n}{n-1}
. Then for any function
f
such that
{\rm supp}\widehat{f}\subset N_{R^{-1}}(S)
, we have

(1.4)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}^{p_{n}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{|Y|}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}.

Theorem
1.1
immediately implies the following result by pigeonholing and noting that
p_{n}\alpha_{n}\leq 1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
Y\subset B_{R}
. Denote
\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{2n(3n-1)}
when
n\geq 3
and
\alpha_{n}=\frac{n-1}{4n^{2}}=\frac{1}{16}
when
n=2
, and denote
p_{n}=\frac{2n}{n-1}
. Then for any function
f
such that
{\rm supp}\widehat{f}\subset N_{R^{-1}}(S)
, we have

(1.5)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}^{p_{n}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{|Y|}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
assuming Theorem
1.1
.

Partition
B_{R}
into a set of unit balls
B_{R}=\sqcup B_{1}
. For each dyadic number
\lambda\leq 1
, define
{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}=\{B_{1}:|B_{1}\cap Y|\sim\lambda|B_{1}|\}
and
Y_{\lambda}=Y\cap(\cup_{B_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}}B_{1})
. By pigeonholing, there exists a
\lambda
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}^{p_{n}}\lessapprox\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\lambda})}^{p_{n}}.

Since
f
is locally constant on each
B_{1}
, we morally have

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y\cap B_{1})}^{p_{n}}\lesssim\frac{|Y\cap B_{1}|}{|B_{1}|}\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(B_{1})}^{p_{n}}.

Summing over
B_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}
, we get

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\lambda})}^{p_{n}}\lesssim\lambda\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(\cup_{B_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}}B_{1})}^{p_{n}}.

Applying Theorem
1.1
with the set
\cup_{B_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}}B_{1}
and noting that
|\cup_{B_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}}B_{1}|=\lambda^{-1}|Y|
, we obtain

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\lambda})}^{p_{n}}\leq\lambda C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{-1}|Y|}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}.

Finally, we just need to note that
p_{n}\alpha_{n}\leq 1
.
∎

Let us look at two examples. Here is the first one: Let
Y=B(0,1)
which is the unit ball centered at the origin. For each
\theta
, let
f_{\theta}
be a single wave packet passing through
Y
. Then on the one hand,

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}^{p_{n}}\sim(\#\theta)^{p_{n}}=R^{\frac{n-1}{2}p_{n}}=R^{n}.

On the other hand,

\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}=R^{\frac{n-1}{2}}R^{\frac{n+1}{2}}=R^{n}.

Thus, if (
1.4
) is true, then we must need

R^{np_{n}\alpha_{n}}\lessapprox R^{1/2}.

This implies

\alpha_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2np_{n}}=\frac{n-1}{4n^{2}}.

The first example shows that our Theorem
1.1
is sharp when
n=2
. Also we conjecture that for
n\geq 3
, (
1.4
) should hold for
\alpha_{n}=\frac{n-1}{4n^{2}}
. However, it is hard to prove the exponent
\alpha_{n}=\frac{n-1}{4n^{2}}
, because of the similar difficulties that arise in the restriction conjecture. For the purpose of the paper, we do not expand our discussion on its relationship with restriction conjecture.

The second example is in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by the exponential sum (see also
[
Bou16
]
). Assume
R^{1/2}
is an integer. Let

(1.6)

f(x)=\psi_{B_{R}}(x)\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{R^{1/2}}e\Big(x_{1}\frac{k}{R^{1/2}}+x_{2}\frac{k^{2}}{R}\Big)

where
\psi_{B_{R}}
is a smooth bump function adapted to
B_{R}
, and
e(t):=e^{2\pi it}
.
If
\theta
is a cap centered at where
(k/R^{1/2},k^{2}/R)
, then
f_{\theta}
has the form

(1.7)

f_{\theta}(x)=\psi_{B_{R}}(x)\cdot e\Big(x_{1}\frac{k}{R^{1/2}}+x_{2}\frac{k^{2}}{R}\Big).

Direct calculation shows

\|f_{\theta}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}\sim R^{2}.

On the other hand, if
x=(\frac{l}{R^{1/2}},0)
where
l\in[0,R^{1/2}]
is an integer, then

|f(x)|\sim R^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Actually, this is still true when
x
varies within distance
\leq 1/100
, by the uncertainty principle. There are
\sim R^{\frac{1}{2}}
such points in
B_{R}
. Let
Y
be the union of unit balls centered at these points. Then

|Y|\sim R^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(Y)}^{p_{2}}\sim R^{\frac{1}{2}(p_{2}+1)}.

To satisfy (
1.4
), we must need

\alpha_{2}\leq 1/12.

The second example shows that when
|Y|
is big, we may expect a bigger value of
\alpha_{n}
. In fact, if we consider level sets of the Gauss sum (
1.6
) other than the biggest one
\{x:|f|\sim R^{1/2}\}
, we will still get the same exponent
1/12
to satisfy (
1.4
). Thus, in the absence of unforeseen examples, we might venture to propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Suppose
n=2
. If
|Y|\geq R^{1/2}
, then (
1.4
) is true for
\alpha=1/12
.

Next, we introduce the maximal Bochner-Riesz problem. The
n
-dimensional Bochner-Riesz operator is defined as

(1.8)

T_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(1-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{t^{2}}\Big)^{\lambda}_{+}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi\,,

and the associated maximal operator is given by

(1.9)

T^{\lambda}_{\ast}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|T_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)|.

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of the maximal Bochner-Riesz operator is closely related to the almost-everywhere convergence of Bochner-Riesz mean, that is,
\lim_{t\to\infty}T_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)
. In fact, for the same
\lambda
, Stein’s maximal principle implies the following: When
1\leq p\leq 2
, the almost-everywhere convergence statement,
\lim_{t\to\infty}T_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.e.}}{{=}}f(x)
for every
L^{p}
function
f
, is equivalent to the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the maximal Bochenr-Riesz operator
T^{\lambda}_{\ast}
. When
p\geq 2
, the almost-everywhere convergence problem is completely solved in
[
CRdFV88
]
.

Regarding the
L^{p}
behavior of
T^{\lambda}_{\ast}
, Tao made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.4

(
[
Tao98
]
)
.

When
1<p<2
and for any
\lambda>\frac{2n-1}{2p}-\frac{n}{2}
,

(1.10)

\big\|T^{\lambda}_{\ast}f\big\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}\,.

By real interpolation, (
1.10
) is true when
\lambda>(n-1)(1/p-1/2)
. So far there are only two results (
[
Tao02
]
,
[
LW20
]
) beyond the interpolation exponent
(n-1)(1/p-1/2)
, and both of them only consider the planar case. Here we give a new result for the planar case and give a first improvement over the interpolation exponents for the three-dimensional case.

Theorem 1.5
.

When
n=2
, (
1.10
) holds for
p=10/7
,
\lambda=21/145
. When
n=3
, (
1.10
) holds for
p=3/2
,
\lambda=107/325
.

Theorem
1.5
improves the interpolation exponent in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
slightly from
\lambda=1/3
to
\lambda=107/325
. It also improves the result in
[
Tao02
]
slightly from
\lambda=3/20
to
\lambda=21/145
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.5
is built on the framework developed in
[
LW20
]
. The new input is the weighted decoupling estimate (
1.4
), which allows us to pick up some local information of the maximal operator. To compare, the argument in
[
Tao02
]
essentially uses the classical decoupling estimate (
1.1
) when
p=4
.

Remark 1.6
.

Unlike the Bochner-Riesz operator
T^{\lambda}
, there is no duality for the maximal operator
T^{\lambda}_{\ast}
between
p<2
and
p>2
. Thus, methods built for
p>2
do not work well when
p<2
. See
[
GOW21
]
for results related to
T^{\lambda}_{\ast}
when
p>2
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We begin with a standard wave packet decomposition of
f
. For each
\theta
, let
\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}
be a collection of parallel, finitely overlapping
R^{1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{1/2}\times R
-tubes, whose direction is the normal direction of the center of
S\cap\theta
. (Later, we will use
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
to denote a refinement of
\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}
). Let
\{{\bf{1}}_{T}^{\ast}\}_{T\in\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}}
be an associated smooth partition of unity of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\widehat{{\bf{1}}_{T}^{\ast}}
is supported in
2\theta
,
{\bf{1}}_{T}^{\ast}\sim 1
on
T
, and
{\bf{1}}_{T}^{*}
decays rapidly outside
T
. Therefore, we can partition
f
as

(2.1)

f=\sum_{\theta}f_{\theta}=\sum_{\theta}\sum_{T\in\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}}f_{\theta}{\bf{1}}_{T}^{\ast}=:\sum_{\theta}\sum_{T\in\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}}f_{T}.

Each
f_{T}
is called a wave packet, whose Fourier support is contained in
3\theta
. A similar decomposition can be found in
[
Wu23
]
Section 3.

The direction of an
R^{1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{1/2}\times R
-tube
T
is defined as the direction of its coreline. As a convention, we also called an
R^{-1/2}
-cap the direction of
T
, if the coreline of
T
is parallel to the normal vector of some point in
S\cap\theta
.

2.1.
When
n\geq 3

Let us first prove Theorem
1.1
for
n\geq 3
.

Step 1. Wave packets and dyadic pigeonholing

Recall the wave packet decomposition (
2.1
). For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
K=R^{\varepsilon^{10}}
so
1\ll K\ll R
. Let
\{\tau\}
be the set of
K^{-1}
-caps that form a covering of the
K^{-2}
-neighborhood of
S
. By dyadic pigeonholing, we can find: a function
F
, which is a sum of wave packets
f_{T}
; a refinement of set of
R^{-1/2}
-caps, still denoted by
\Theta
; for each
\theta\in\Theta
, a set of tubes
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}\subset\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}
; two dyadic numbers
\mu,\sigma
. They satisfy the following conditions.

(1)

F=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}F_{\theta}
.

(2)

\#\{\theta\subset\tau\}\sim\sigma
for any
K^{-1}
-cap
\tau
that contains some
\theta\in\Theta
.

(3)

For each
\theta\in\Theta
,
F_{\theta}=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{\theta}}f_{T}
,
\|f_{T}\|_{\infty}
are about the same for all
T\in\bigcup_{\theta}\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
,
|\mathbb{T}_{\theta}|\sim\mu
uniformly in
\theta
.

(4)

We have the estimate

(2.2)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}\lesssim(\log R)^{C}\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}.

Let us assume
\|f_{T}\|_{\infty}\sim 1
without loss of generality.

Recall that
F
is locally constant on each unit ball.
We do dyadic pigeonholing with respect to the magnitude of
|F|
to find a dyadic number
\lambda
and a set
Y_{\lambda}\subset Y
which is a union of unit balls so that for any unit ball
B\subset Y_{\lambda}
we have

(2.3)

\|F{\bf{1}}_{B}\|_{\infty}\sim\lambda

and

(2.4)

\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}\lesssim(\log R)\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\lambda})}.

To simplify the notation, still denote
Y_{\lambda}
by
Y
.

Step 2. Broad-narrow decomposition

For a
K^{-1}
-cap
\tau
in the frequency space, let
F_{\tau}=\sum_{\theta\subset\tau}F_{\theta}
. For each
K^{2}
-ball
B_{K^{2}}\subset B_{R}
in the physical space, define the significant set of
K^{-1}
-caps as follows

(2.5)

{\mathcal{T}}(B_{K^{2}})=\{\tau:\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{n}}(B_{K^{2}})}\geq(10nK)^{-n}\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y\cap B_{K^{2}})}\}.

Then by the triangle inequality, we have

(2.6)

\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y\cap B_{K^{2}})}\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}(B_{K^{2}})}F_{\tau}\Big\|_{L^{p_{n}}(B_{K^{2}})}.

Definition 2.1
.

We call a physical
K^{2}
-ball
B_{K^{2}}

narrow
if there is an
(n-1)
-dimensional hyperplane
\Gamma
of form
\Pi\times\mathbb{R}
(where
\Pi
is a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
), so that

(2.7)

\bigcup_{\tau\subset{\mathcal{T}}(B_{K^{2}})}\tau\subset N_{10K^{-1}}(\Gamma).

Otherwise, we call
B_{K^{2}}

broad
.

If
B_{K^{2}}
is a broad
K^{2}
-ball, then we must have (see
[
DZ19
]
Section 3.1)

(2.8)

\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y\cap B_{K^{2}})}\lesssim K^{O(1)}\Big(\int_{B_{K^{2}}}\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{n}F_{\tau_{j},v_{j}}\Big|^{\frac{p_{n}}{n}}\Big)^{1/p_{n}},

for some
\tau_{j}\in{\mathcal{T}}(B_{K^{2}})
and some
v_{j}
, where
v_{j}=O(K^{2})
is an integer, and
F_{\tau_{j},v_{j}}(x)=F_{\tau_{j}}(x+v_{j})
is a translation of
F_{\tau_{j}}
.

Finally, decompose

Y=Y_{\textup{narrow}}\bigsqcup Y_{\textup{broad}},

where

Y_{\textup{narrow}}=\bigcup_{B_{K^{2}}\text{ is narrow}}(Y\cap B_{K^{2}})

and

Y_{\textup{broad}}=\bigcup_{B_{K^{2}}\text{ is broad}}(Y\cap B_{K^{2}}).

Hence we have the decomposition

(2.9)

\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y)}=\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\textup{narrow}})}+\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\textup{broad}})}.

Step 3. Narrow case

We use induction for the narrow case. For each narrow ball
B_{K^{2}}
, apply the
(n-1)
-dimensional decoupling estimate (
1.1
) with
R
replaced by
K^{2}
,
n
replaced by
n-1
and
p
replaced by
p_{n}
to obtain

(2.10)

\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\textup{narrow}}\cap B_{K^{2}})}^{p_{n}}\leq\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(B_{K^{2}})}^{p_{n}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}K^{\varepsilon^{2}}K^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\sum_{\tau}\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{n}}(B_{K^{2}})}^{p_{n}}.

Summing up all the
B_{K^{2}}
that is contained in
Y_{\textup{narrow}}
, we get

(2.11)

\|F\|_{L^{p_{n}}(Y_{\textup{narrow}})}^{p_{n}}\leq K^{\varepsilon^{2}}K^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\sum_{\tau}\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{n}}(N_{K^{2}}(Y))}^{p_{n}}.

We will bound
\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{n}}(N_{K^{2}}(Y))}^{p_{n}}
by induction and parabolic rescaling. Note that
\widehat{F}_{\tau}
is supported in
N_{R^{-1}}(S)\cap\tau
. Without loss of generality, let us assume that
S
contains the origin and that
\tau
is the
K^{-1}
-cap centered at the origin. Moreover, the tangent plane of
S
at the origin is the horizontal plane
\{\xi_{n}=0\}
. Hence
N_{R^{-1}}(S)\cap\tau
is contained in the set

(2.12)

\{(\bar{\xi},\xi_{n}):|\xi_{n}-\phi(\bar{\xi})|\leq R^{-1}\text{ and }|\bar{\xi}|\leq K^{-1}\},

where
\phi
is a
C^{2}
function whose graph is
S
on
\{|\bar{\xi}|\leq K^{-1}\}
, and
\nabla\phi(0)=0
. Define
\mathcal{L}
to be the parabolic rescaling

(2.13)

\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},x_{n})=(K^{-1}\bar{x},K^{-2}x_{n}).

Then, for some other
C^{2}
function whose Hessian is also positive-definite, the Fourier transform of
F_{\tau}\circ\mathcal{L}
is contained in the set

(2.14)

\{(\bar{\xi},\xi_{n}):|\xi_{n}-\phi^{\prime}(\bar{\xi})|\lesssim K^{2}(R)^{-1}\text{ and }|\bar{\xi}|\lesssim 1\},

which is
K^{2}R^{-1}
-neighborhood for some
C^{2}
surface with positive second functamental form. Partition
B_{R}
into parallel
R/K\times\cdots\times R/K\times R
fat tubes with direction
\tau
, so that under the map
\mathcal{L}
, those fat tubes become
R/K^{2}
-balls. We denote these
R/K^{2}
-balls by
\{Q\}
. Define
Y_{Q}=\mathcal{L}(N_{K^{2}}(Y))\cap Q
as the portion of the image of
N_{K^{2}}(Y)
under
\mathcal{L}
that is contained in
Q
. Clearly, we have

(2.15)

\max_{Q}|Y_{Q}|\lesssim K^{-(n+1)}|N_{K^{2}}(Y)|\lesssim K^{n-1}|Y|.

In the last inequality, we used that
Y
is a union of unit balls.

Thus, we can use induction to have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{n}}(N_{K^{2}}(Y))}^{p_{n}}

(3.4)

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, we can assume in the expression
S(\psi_{Q}f)=R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\int e^{it|x-y|}a(x,y,t)(\psi_{Q}f)(y)dy
that the direction
(x-y)/|x-y|
is within a
1/(10n)
-neighborhood of
e_{n}
, the vertical direction that corresponds to
x_{n}
.

Denote by
x=(\bar{x},x_{n})
. By freezing the variable
x_{n}
, it suffices to prove that

(3.5)

\|S(\psi_{Q}f)\|_{L^{2}_{\bar{x}}L^{\infty}_{t}}\lesssim|Q|^{\frac{1}{2n}}R^{-1}\|f\|_{2}.

Via the
TT^{\ast}
-method, this is equivalent to prove

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Big\|R^{-(n+1)}\int e^{it|x-y|-t^{\prime}|x^{\prime}-y|}

\displaystyle a(x,y,t)a(x^{\prime},y,t^{\prime})\psi_{Q}^{2}(y)dyF(x^{\prime},t)d\bar{x^{\prime}}dt\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\bar{x}}L^{\infty}_{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|^{\frac{1}{n}}R^{-2}\|F\|_{L^{2}_{\bar{x}}L^{1}_{t}}.

The kernel
\bar{K}(x,t,x^{\prime},t^{\prime})=R^{-(n+1)}\int e^{it|x-y|-t^{\prime}|x^{\prime}-y|}a(x,y,t)a(x^{\prime},y,t^{\prime})\psi_{Q}^{2}(y)dy
has a very good pointwise estimate. In fact, note that
\nabla_{y}(t|x-y|-t^{\prime}|x^{\prime}-y|)\gtrsim R^{-1}|x-x^{\prime}|
whenever
t,t^{\prime}\sim 1
. By the method of stationary phase we have

(3.7)

\bar{K}\leq C_{N}R^{-(n+1)}|Q|(1+R^{-1}|x-x^{\prime}|\cdot|Q|^{1/n})^{-N},

yielding
\int|\bar{K}|d\bar{x},\int|\bar{K}|d\bar{x^{\prime}}\lesssim R^{-2}|Q|^{1/2}
. This proves (
3.6
) by Schur’s test.
∎

As a function of
(x,t)
, the Fourier transform of
Sf
is supported in
[-CR,CR]\times B^{n}(0,1)
. Hence
Sf
is essentially constant on

, where

is an interval of length

, and

is a unit ball contained in

. Therefore, after linearizing the maximal operator

, we can find a collection of
R^{-1}
-separated points
\{t_{j}\}
, and for each
t_{j}
, a set

that is a union of unit balls so that

(3.8)

\|S^{*}f\|_{p}\lessapprox\Big\|\sum_{j}Sf(\cdot,t_{j}){\bf{1}}_{F_{j}}(\cdot)\Big\|_{p}.

By dyadic pigeonholing, there is a dyadic number
\gamma
and a set
\mathcal{J}
such that

(3.9)

|F_{j}|\sim\gamma

for all

, and

(3.10)

\|S^{*}f\|_{p}\lessapprox\Big\|\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}Sf(\cdot,t_{j}){\bf{1}}_{F_{j}}(\cdot)\Big\|_{p}.

For each
t_{j}
, the Fourier transform of the kernel
K_{j}(x)=R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}e^{it_{j}|x|}
is a smooth function with bounded
L^{\infty}
-norm that is essentially supported on the annulus
A_{j}=\{R^{-1}\leq|\xi|-t_{j}\leq R^{-1}\}
. Let
\Theta_{j}
be a collection of

-caps that forms a finitely overlapping cover of

, and let
\{\widehat{\varphi}_{\theta,j}\}_{\theta}
be a set of functions so that each
\widehat{\varphi}_{\theta,j}
is a bump function on
\theta
,
\|\widehat{\varphi}_{\theta,j}\|_{\infty}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}
, and, up to negligible loss, we have
K_{j}=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{j}}\varphi_{\theta,j}
. This gives a partition to our kernel

.

For each
\varphi_{\theta,j}
, let
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
be a collection of parallel, finitely overlapping
R^{1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{1/2}\times R
-tubes with direction
\theta
. Let
\{{\bf{1}}_{T}^{\ast}\}_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{\theta}}
be an associated smooth partition of unity of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\widehat{{\bf{1}}_{T}^{\ast}}
is supported in
2\theta
, and
{\bf{1}}_{T}^{\ast}\sim 1
on
T
. Then, up to a negligible loss, we have the wave packet decomposition

(3.11)

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}Sf(x,t_{j}){\bf{1}}_{F_{j}}(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{j}}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{\theta}}(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast f)(x){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}(x){\bf{1}}_{F_{j}}(x)=:Gf(x).

Each
(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast f){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}
is a wave packet and is essentially constant.

We follow the strategy in
[

]
(which dates back to
[

]
) to study the two operators
S^{\ast}
and
G
. Introduce two parameters
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
to be chosen later. First,

is used to partition the set

according to its density in a maximal dyadic cube: Let

be a collection of maximal dyadic cubes such that

|E\cap Q|\geq\beta_{2}|Q|^{1/n}.

Denote by

(3.13)

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle:=E\setminus\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}Q,

(3.14)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle:=E\setminus E_{1}.

Then,
\beta_{1}
is used to partition the set of triples

according to the magnitude of the wave packet

(3.15)

\displaystyle\Xi_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi:|(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}|<\beta_{1}\},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Xi_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi:|(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}|\geq\beta_{1}\}.

This partition
S^{\ast}{\bf{1}}_{E}
as
S^{\ast}{\bf{1}}_{E}\leq S^{\ast}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}+S^{\ast}{\bf{1}}_{E_{2}}
, and partition
G{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}
as
G{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}=G_{1}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}+G_{2}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}
, where

(3.17)

G_{k}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\sum_{(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi_{k}}(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}{\bf{1}}_{F_{j}}.

We will plug in

in (

) and (

).

Similar to

]
, we study the maximal operator

, which will be given in the next three lemmas. The first lemma is where we will invoke the weighted decoupling estimate (

Lemma 3.2
.

Recall (
3.9
). We have

(3.18)

\|G_{1}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}\lessapprox\Big(\frac{\gamma}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\beta_{1}^{p_{n}-2}|E|,

where
\alpha_{n}
is defined in Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Since
F_{j}
’s are all disjoint,

(3.19)

\|G_{1}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}\leq\sum_{j}\int_{F_{j}}\Big|\sum_{(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi_{1}}(\varphi_{\theta,k}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}\Big|^{p_{n}}.

We apply Theorem
1.1
to the right-hand side so that

(3.20)

\|G_{1}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}\lessapprox\Big(\frac{\gamma}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\sum_{j}\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{j}}\sum_{T:(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi_{1}}|(\varphi_{\theta,k}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}|^{p_{n}}.

Since the wave packet
(\varphi_{\theta,k}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}
is an element in
\Xi_{1}
, we thus have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|G_{1}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{\gamma}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\beta_{1}^{p_{n}-2}\sum_{T:(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi_{1}}|(\varphi_{\theta,k}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}|^{2}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\gamma}{R^{n}}\Big)^{p_{n}\alpha_{n}}R^{1/2}\beta_{1}^{p_{n}-2}\|{\bf{1}}_{E}\|_{2}^{2}.

The last inequality follows from Plancherel.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

(3.23)

\|G_{2}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{1}\lessapprox\beta_{1}^{-1}\beta_{2}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}|E|.

Proof.

Note that
\varphi_{\theta,j}
is essentially supported in a
R^{1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{1/2}\times R
-tube with direction
\theta
, centered at the origin. Hence
(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}
is essentially supported on
T
. By the definition of
\beta_{1}
,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|G_{2}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int\beta_{1}^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\sum_{(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi_{2}}|(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}^{\ast}_{T}|^{2}{\bf{1}}_{F_{j}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\lessapprox\beta_{1}^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\sum_{(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi_{2}}|(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}_{T}|^{2}|T\cap F_{j}|.

Since
|\varphi_{\theta,j}|\lesssim R^{-(n+1)/2}
, we have
|(\varphi_{\theta,j}\ast{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}){\bf{1}}_{T}|\lessapprox|E\cap T|
. Hence (
3.25
) is

(3.26)

\displaystyle\lessapprox R^{-(n+1)}\beta_{1}^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\sum_{(\theta,j,T)\in\Xi_{2}}|E_{1}\cap T|^{2}|T\cap F_{j}|.

Since
F_{j}
are disjoint subsets of
B_{R}
, we can sum up all

so that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\|G_{2}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{1}\lessapprox R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\beta_{1}^{-1}\sum_{T}|E_{1}\cap T|^{2}.

The last summation can be expressed as

(3.28)

R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\beta_{1}^{-1}\int{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}(x)\int\sum_{T}{\bf{1}}_{T}(x){\bf{1}}_{T}(y){\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}(y)dydx.

Recalling the definition of
\beta_{2}
, the inner integral can be bounded as

(3.29)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{1}}\sum_{T}{\bf{1}}_{T}(x){\bf{1}}_{T}(y)dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,2^{r}\leq R\end{subarray}}\int_{|x-y|\sim 2^{r}}\int_{E_{1}}\sum_{T}{\bf{1}}_{T}(x){\bf{1}}_{T}(y)dydx

(3.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{2}\sum_{r}2^{r}\min\{(R/2^{-r})^{n-1},R^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\}

(3.31)

\displaystyle\lessapprox\beta_{2}R^{\frac{n}{2}}.

The second inequality follows from the observation that for fixed
x,y
with
|x-y|\sim 2^{r}
, there are
\lesssim\sum_{r}2^{r}\min\{(R/2^{-r})^{n-1},R^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\}

R^{1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{1/2}\times R
-tubes
T
containing both of them.

Plugging the above estimate back, we therefore get

(3.32)

\|G_{2}{\bf{1}}_{E_{1}}\|_{1}\lessapprox R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{1}^{-1}\beta_{2}|E|

as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
\alpha>0
,

(3.33)

\big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:S^{*}{\bf{1}}_{E_{2}}(x)\geq\alpha\}\big|\lesssim\alpha^{-2}R^{-1}\beta_{2}^{-1}|E|^{2}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality and the local

3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|S^{*}({\bf{1}}_{E_{2}})\|_{2}\lesssim\big\|S^{*}\big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}{\bf{1}}_{E\cap Q}\big)\big\|_{2}\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\|S^{*}(\chi_{E\cap Q})\|_{2}

The desired estimate follows from the Chebyshev’s inequality.
∎

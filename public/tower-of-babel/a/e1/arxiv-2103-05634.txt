Zeros of Rankin–Selberg L L -functions in families
Peter Humphries, Jesse Thorner

Abstract
. Let 𝔉 n \mathfrak{F}_{n} be the set of all cuspidal automorphic representations π \pi of GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} with unitary central character over a number field F F . We prove the first unconditional zero density estimate for the set 𝒮 = { L ⁡ ( s , π × π ′ ) : π ∈ 𝔉 n } \mathcal{S}=\{L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\colon\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\} of Rankin–Selberg L L -functions, where π ′ ∈ 𝔉 n ′ \pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}} is fixed. We use this density estimate to establish (i) a hybrid-aspect subconvexity bound at s = 1 2 s=\frac{1}{2} for almost all L ⁡ ( s , π × π ′ ) ∈ 𝒮 L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S} , (ii) a strong on-average form of effective multiplicity one for almost all π ∈ 𝔉 n \pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n} , and (iii) a positive level of distribution for L ⁡ ( s , π × π ~ ) L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}) , in the sense of Bombieri–Vinogradov, for each π ∈ 𝔉 n \pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n} .

Since such strong zero-free regions for Rankin–Selberg

To quantify our notion of “sparse”, we define for

Note that

2.1.
Bounds for Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

It is a classical problem for Dirichlet
L
-functions to find strong bounds on the critical line
\textup{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. The Phragmén–Lindelöf convexity principle shows that if
q_{\chi}
is the conductor of a primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
, then
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi)\ll q^{1/4}
; improving this bound by replacing
1/4
with a smaller exponent is known as a
subconvex
bound. The multiplicative version of the classical large sieve inequality combined with an approximate functional equation shows that for almost all
\chi
, we have the bound
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)\ll_{\varepsilon}q_{\chi}^{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
, consistent with the generalized Lindelöf hypothesis (GLH).

For
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
, GLH predicts that
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi)\ll_{\varepsilon}C(\pi)^{\varepsilon}
. Michel and Venkatesh
[
38
]
proved that there exists a fixed positive
\delta>0
such that
L(\frac{1}{2},\pi)\ll_{F}C(\pi)^{1/4-\delta}
, the culmination of several decades of research. When
F=\mathbb{Q}
, a sharp mean value estimate for Hecke eigenvalues proved by Deshouillers and Iwaniec
[
14
]
, in conjunction with the approximate functional equation, implies the bound
L(\frac{1}{2},\pi)\ll_{\varepsilon}(qT)^{\varepsilon}
for almost all
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
of arithmetic conductor
q
, trivial central character, and archimedean complexity (Laplace eigenvalue or weight squared) lying in the dyadic interval
[T,2T]
. Note that in this case,
C(\pi)\asymp qT
.

For
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
with
n\geq 3
, the best uniform result towards the bound
L(\frac{1}{2},\pi)\ll_{\varepsilon}C(\pi)^{\varepsilon}
predicted by GLH is that of Soundararajan and Thorner
[
45
, Corollary 2.7]
, namely

(2.1)

L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi)\ll C(\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log C(\pi))^{-1/(10^{17}n^{3})}.

We mention three results that improve upon (
2.1
) in an average sense, each having complementary strengths. Jana
[
29
, Theorem 6]
extended the GLH-on-average bound of Deshouillers and Iwaniec to the family of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
of arithmetic conductor 1 and growing analytic conductor. Blomer
[
3
, Corollary 5]
proved the corresponding result for the family of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
of a large given prime arithmetic conductor
q
, trivial central character, and whose archimedean components are principal series representations confined to a compact subset of the unitary dual. Thorner and Zaman
[
47
, Theorem 1.3]
proved that there exists a constant
=\Cr{TZ_{g}ln}(n,[F:\mathbb{Q}])>0
such that if
\varepsilon>0
, then

(2.2)

|\{\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)\colon|L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi)|\geq\Cr{TZ_{g}ln}C(\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\varepsilon}{10^{16}n^{3}}}\}|\ll_{F}|\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)|^{\varepsilon}.

Unlike the preceding results, (
2.2
) is uniform in both the arithmetic conductor and spectral aspects and holds over number fields other than
\mathbb{Q}
, but the savings over (
2.1
) is not comparable to GLH on average.

Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, Soundararajan and Thorner
[
45
, Corollary 2.7]
proved when
F=\mathbb{Q}
that if
C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is the analytic conductor of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
, then

(2.3)

L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll|L(\tfrac{3}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|^{2}C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime}))^{-1/(10^{17}(n^{\prime}n)^{3})}.

As of now, the best general upper bound for
|L(\tfrac{3}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|^{2}
is larger than any fixed power of
\log C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})

[
33
, Theorem 2]
. The factor of
|L(\tfrac{3}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|^{2}
can be removed under certain partial progress toward GRC. The bound
L(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll_{\varepsilon}C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}
is predicted by GLH.

In order to improve (
2.3
) on average with uniformity in
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
, one might first try to mimic the approach that worked well for Dirichlet
L
-functions and
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

L
-functions using trace formulae, approximate functional equations, the spectral large sieve, Voronoĭ summation, etc. While such methods have seen great success for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n^{\prime}}
with
n,n^{\prime}\in\{1,2\}
, suitably uniform and flexible versions of these tools do not appear to be available yet in the general setting. The special case where
|n-n^{\prime}|\leq 1
exhibits some nice structural properties, lending itself to approaches via period integrals that completely avoids the aforementioned tools. To describe work in this direction, let
F=\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the subset of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
of arithmetic conductor 1, and
\mathcal{F}_{n}(Q)=\{\pi\in\mathcal{F}_{n}\colon C(\pi)\leq Q\}
. It follows from work of Jana
[
30
, Corollary 2.2]
that if
\varepsilon>0
,
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, and the spectral parameters of
\pi^{\prime}
have real part at least
-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}
(which is far stronger than the best known unconditional lower bound
-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}
due to Luo, Rudnick, and Sarnak
[
37
]
), then

(2.4)

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}_{n}(Q)}|L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|^{2}\ll_{\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon}|\mathcal{F}_{n}(Q)|^{1+\varepsilon}.

Therefore, for fixed
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, the generalized Lindelöf hypothesis for
L(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
holds on average over the
\pi\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Using Chebyshev’s inequality, we conclude that for all
\delta>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\pi^{\prime},\delta}>0
such that

(2.5)

|\{\pi\in\mathcal{F}_{n}(Q)\colon|L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|\geq c_{\pi^{\prime},\delta}C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{2\delta}\}|\ll_{\pi^{\prime},\delta}|\mathcal{F}_{n}(Q)|^{1-\delta}.

See also the work of Blomer
[
2
, Theorem 2]
, which proves a variant of (
2.4
) for families of “spectrally close” Hecke–Maaß newforms on
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Along the same lines as (

Let

2.2.
Effective multiplicity one

Let
\pi=\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
and
\pi^{\prime}=\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}^{\prime}
be cuspidal automorphic representations in
\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)
. Under the assumption of GRH for
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
and
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
and that
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
are tempered for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}
, it is known that if
Y=(\log Q)^{2}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}
with
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\ll Y
, then
\pi=\pi^{\prime}

[
25
, Proposition 5.22]
. Brumley
[
8
]
, improving on work of Moreno
[
40
]
, proved that there exists a constant
B_{n}>0
such that this result holds unconditionally with
Y=Q^{B_{n}}
. This result makes effective the multiplicity one theorems of Jacquet and Shalika
[
28
, Theorem 4.8]
and Piatetski-Shapiro
[
43
]
. Any fixed
B_{n}>2n
suffices
[
35
]
.

When
n=2
, we have an average result that nearly achieves what GRH predicts. Specifically, let
\mathfrak{F}_{2}^{\flat}
be the subset of
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
with squarefree conductor and trivial central character, and let
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}^{\flat}
. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an effectively computable constant
N_{\varepsilon}>0
, depending at most on
\varepsilon
and
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
, such that

(2.6)

|\{\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}^{\flat}(Q)\colon\textup{$\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}$ for all $\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}$ with $\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leq(\log Q)^{N_{\varepsilon}}$}\}|\ll_{\varepsilon}Q^{\varepsilon}.

In particular, the implied constant does not depend on
\pi^{\prime}
. This was proved by Duke and Kowalski
[
15
, Theorem 3]
when
F=\mathbb{Q}
in a stronger form under the assumption of GRC. See Brumley’s Ph.D. thesis
[
7
, Corollary 5.2.2]
for a proof that does not use GRC.

If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
and
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
is the contragredient, then
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})=\zeta_{F}(s)L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})
, where
\zeta_{F}(s)
is the Dedekind zeta function of
F
and
\mathrm{Ad}
is the adjoint square lift from a representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
to a representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. The arguments in
[
7
,
15
]
rely on two key results:

(1)

Gelbart and Jacquet
[
18
]
proved that if
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
, then
L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})
is the
L
-function of an automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, denoted
\mathrm{Ad}\,\pi
, complete with a criterion by which one can determine whether
\mathrm{Ad}\,\pi
is cuspidal (and lies in
\mathfrak{F}_{3}
).

(2)

At most
O_{\varepsilon}(Q^{1/2+\varepsilon})
representations
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}(Q)
have the same adjoint lift. Also, Ramakrishnan (
[
15
, Appendix]
and
[
44
]
) proved that
\mathrm{Ad}\colon\mathfrak{F}_{2}^{\flat}\to\mathfrak{F}_{3}
is injective.

In attempting to generalize the strategy of Duke and Kowalski to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for
n\geq 3
, one encounters some deep open problems. If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, then for
\textup{Re}(s)
sufficiently large,
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
factors as
\zeta_{F}(s)L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Ad}\,)
, where
\mathrm{Ad}\,
is the adjoint square lift from
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{n^{2}-1}
. Apart from some special cases, the following obstacles arise:

(1)

The adjoint lift is not yet known to be automorphic for
n\geq 3
, and if it were, there is no known criterion for cuspidality.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)
be the subset of
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)
such that
\mathrm{Ad}\,\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{2}-1}
. It is not known how many
\pi\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
have the same adjoint lift.

Despite these setbacks, we can use (
1.7
) (more specifically,

below) to prove a

analogue of (

depends mildly on

Theorem 2.3

Let
n\geq 3
and
Q\geq 1
. There exists an absolute, effectively computable constant

Remark 2.4
.

Given a subset
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, a similar result can be proved for
\pi\in\mathcal{S}(Q)
using
Theorem

1.1
. Such a result would depend effectively on
\delta_{\mathcal{S}}
in (
1.4
).

The proof is very flexible. For example, if
\varepsilon>0
and
C(\pi^{\prime})\ll_{\varepsilon}Q^{\Cr{multone_{1}}\varepsilon^{2}/(n^{2}[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{2})}
, then the same proof with minor changes in choices of parameters produces the bound

|\{\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}(Q)\colon\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}~\textup{for all}~\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}~\textup{with}~\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leq Q^{\varepsilon}\}|\ll_{\varepsilon}Q^{\varepsilon}.

While the number of
\mathfrak{p}
for which one needs to check that
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
is larger, the range of
C(\pi^{\prime})
is greatly extended, and the threshold
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leq Q^{\varepsilon}
(reminiscent of Vinogradov’s conjecture on the size of the least quadratic nonresidue) still greatly improves on the unconditional range
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\ll_{\varepsilon}Q^{2n+\varepsilon}
from
[
35
]
.

2.3.
Automorphic level of distribution

Let
\Lambda(m)
be the von Mangoldt function, equal to
\log p
if
m
is a power of a prime
p
and zero otherwise. The celebrated Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem states that if
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
is fixed, then for all
A>0
, we have

(2.7)

\sum_{q\leq x^{\theta}}~\max_{\gcd(a,q)=1}\max_{y\leq x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq y\\
m\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)-\frac{y}{\varphi(q)}\Big|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

This may be viewed as an average form of GRH for Dirichlet
L
-functions. As part of his proof
[
6
]
, Bombieri proved a strong form of the zero density estimate in
Theorem

1.1
for Dirichlet
L
-functions. We call any
\theta
for which (
2.7
) holds a
level of distribution
for the primes. Elliott and Halberstam conjectured that any fixed
\theta<1
is a level of distribution for the primes.

Number theorists have proved several interesting extensions and variations of (
2.7
). For example, Murty and Murty
[
42
]
proved that primes in the Chebotarev density theorem have a positive level of distribution. To describe a different direction for automorphic representations over
\mathbb{Q}
, we let
n\geq 2
and consider
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
with conductor
q_{\pi}
. Let
\Lambda(m)
be the von Mangoldt function, and define the numbers
a_{\pi}(m)
by

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)=\sum_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)^{k}\log p}{p^{ks}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi}(m)\Lambda(m)}{m^{s}},\qquad\textup{Re}(s)>1.

Note that
a_{\pi}(p)=\lambda_{\pi}(p)
. For fixed
\theta<\frac{1}{n^{2}-2}
, Wong
[
49
, Theorem 9]
proved that if
\pi
satisfies GRC and
L(s,\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi))
has no Landau–Siegel zero for all Dirichlet characters
\chi
, then for any
A>0
,

(2.8)

\sum_{q\leq x^{\theta}}~\max_{\gcd(a,q)=1}\max_{y\leq x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq y\\
\gcd(m,q_{\pi})=1\\
m\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)\end{subarray}}|a_{\pi}(m)|^{2}\Lambda(m)-\frac{y}{\varphi(q)}\Big|\ll_{A,\pi}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

This conditionally endows
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
with a positive level of distribution
\theta
. The hypotheses for (
2.8
) hold for
\pi
attached to non-CM holomorphic cuspidal newforms on congruence subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. Using (
1.7
), we unconditionally endow
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
with a notion of positive level of distribution. In particular, we avoid recourse to unproven progress toward GRC or the absence of Landau–Siegel zeros.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
F=\mathbb{Q}
and
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. Fix
\theta<1/(9n^{3})
. If
A>0
, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{\theta}\\
\gcd(q,q_{\pi})=1\end{subarray}}\max_{\gcd(a,q)=1}\max_{y\leq x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq y\\
m\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)\end{subarray}}|a_{\pi}(m)|^{2}\Lambda(m)-\frac{y}{\varphi(q)}\Big|\ll_{A,\pi}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

The implied constants are ineffective.

Remark 2.6

One can prove an analogue of
Theorem

2.5
with
F\neq\mathbb{Q}
, replacing residue classes modulo
q
with ray classes modulo
\mathfrak{q}
. We restrict to
F=\mathbb{Q}
for notational simplicity.

3.1.
Standard
L
-functions

Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, let
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the contragredient representation and
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
be the conductor of
\pi
. We express
\pi
as a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth admissible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
, where
v
varies over places of
F
. When
v
is a nonarchimedean place corresponding with a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, then the local
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})
is defined in terms of the Satake parameters
A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})=\{\alpha_{1,\pi}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,\alpha_{n,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\}
by

(3.1)

L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}.

We have
\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
for all
j
whenever
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
, and when
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
, it might be the case that there exist
j
such that
\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})=0
. The standard
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
associated to
\pi
is of the form

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}}.

The Euler product and Dirichlet series converge absolutely when
\textup{Re}(s)>1
.

At each archimedean place
v
of
F
, there are
n
Langlands parameters
\mu_{j,\pi}(v)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

L(s,\pi_{\infty})=\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)),\qquad\Gamma_{v}(s)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{C}$.}\end{cases}

By combining the work in
[
4
,
5
,
37
,
41
]
, we know that there exists

(3.2)

0\leq\theta_{n}\leq\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $n=1$,}\\
7/64&\mbox{if $n=2$,}\\
5/14&\mbox{if $n=3$,}\\
9/22&\mbox{if $n=4$,}\\
1/2-1/(n^{2}+1)&\mbox{if $n\geq 5$}\end{cases}

such that

(3.3)

|\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})|\leq\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{\theta_{n}}\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad\textup{Re}(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))\geq-\theta_{n}.

GRC asserts that in (
3.2
), one may take
\theta_{n}=0
. We have
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}=\mathfrak{q}_{\widetilde{\pi}}
, and for each
\mathfrak{p}
and each
v
, we have the equalities of sets
\{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{p})\}=\{\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})}\}
and
\{\mu_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}=\{\overline{\mu_{j,\pi}(v)}\}
.

Let
r_{\pi}
be the order of the pole of
L(s,\pi)
at
s=1
. The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\pi)=(s(s-1))^{r_{\pi}}(D_{F}^{n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})^{s/2}L(s,\pi)L(s,\pi_{\infty})

is entire of order 1, and there exists a complex number
W(\pi)
of modulus 1 such that for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we have the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\pi)=W(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi})
. Let
d(v)=1
if
F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
and
d(v)=2
if
F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
. The analytic conductor of
\pi

[
26
]
is given by

(3.4)

C(\pi,t)\coloneqq D_{F}^{n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(3+|it+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)|^{d(v)}),\qquad C(\pi)\coloneqq C(\pi,0).

Since
\Lambda(s,\pi)
is entire of order 1, there exist complex numbers
a_{\pi}
and
b_{\pi}
such that

\Lambda(s,\pi)=e^{a_{\pi}+b_{\pi}s}\prod_{\Lambda(\rho,\pi)=0}\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big)e^{s/\rho}.

The zeros
\rho
in the above Hadamard product are the nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi)
, and the zeros of
L(s,\pi)
that arise as poles of
s^{r_{\pi}}L(s,\pi_{\infty})
are the trivial zeros.

3.2.
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. At each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
27
]
associate to
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
a local Rankin–Selberg
L
-function

(3.5)

L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}(1-\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}

and a local conductor
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}
. If
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}
, then we have the equality of sets

(3.6)

\{\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\}=\{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\alpha_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\}.

The Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
associated to
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
and its arithmetic conductor are

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}},\qquad\mathfrak{q}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}.

At an archimedean place
v
of
F
, Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika associate
n^{\prime}n
complex Langlands parameters
\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)
to
\pi_{v}
and
\pi_{v}^{\prime}
, from which one defines

L(s,\pi_{\infty}\times\pi_{\infty}^{\prime})=\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)).

Using the explicit descriptions of
\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)
in
[
23
,
45
]
, one sees that

(3.7)

|\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})|\leq\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{\theta_{n}+\theta_{n^{\prime}}},\qquad\textup{Re}(\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v))\geq-\theta_{n}-\theta_{n^{\prime}}.

Let
r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=-\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
. By our normalization for the central characters of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
, we have that
r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=0
if and only if
\pi\neq\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}
, and
r_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}=1
otherwise. The function

(3.8)

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=(s(s-1))^{r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}(D_{F}^{n^{\prime}n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}})^{s/2}L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})L(s,\pi_{\infty}\times\pi_{\infty}^{\prime})

is entire of order 1, and there exists a complex number
W(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
of modulus 1 such that
\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
satisfies the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=W(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
. As with
L(s,\pi)
, the analytic conductor of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is given by

(3.9)

C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)\coloneqq D_{F}^{n^{\prime}n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}(3+|it+\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)|^{d(v)}),\qquad C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\coloneqq C(0,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}).

The combined work of Bushnell and Henniart
[
13
]
and Brumley
[
23
, Appendix]
yields

(3.10)

C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)\ll C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})(3+|t|)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]n^{\prime}n},\qquad C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll C(\pi)^{n^{\prime}}C(\pi^{\prime})^{n}.

Since
\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is entire of order 1, there exist complex numbers
a_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}
and
b_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}
such that the Hadamard factorization

(3.11)

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=e^{a_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}+b_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}s}\prod_{\Lambda(\rho,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=0}\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big)e^{s/\rho}

holds. The zeros
\rho
in (
3.11
) are the nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
, and the zeros of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
that arise as poles of
s^{r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}L(s,\pi_{\infty}\times\pi_{\infty}^{\prime})
are the trivial zeros.

It follows from work of Li
[
33
, Theorem 2]
(with minor adjustments when
F\neq\mathbb{Q}
) that there exists an absolute and effectively computable constant
>0
, which we assume to be sufficiently large for future convenience, such that

(3.12)

\lim_{\sigma_{0}\to\sigma}(\sigma_{0}-1)^{r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}L(\sigma_{0},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll\exp\Big(\Cr{Li}n^{\prime}n[F:\mathbb{Q}]\frac{\log C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})}{\log\log C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})}\Big),\quad\sigma\in[1,3].

We can change
\pi^{\prime}
to
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\left|\det\right|^{it}
; at the archimedean places, this has the effect of adding
it
to each
\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)
. We then apply functional equation, the Phragmén–Lindelöf convexity principle, and (
3.10
) to obtain for all
\sigma\geq 0

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma_{0}\to\sigma}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{0}+it-1}{\sigma_{0}+it+1}\Big)^{r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}L(\sigma_{0}+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)^{\frac{\max\{1-\sigma,0\}}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{\prime}n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}(C(\pi)^{n^{\prime}}C(\pi^{\prime})^{n}(3+|t|)^{n^{\prime}n[F:\mathbb{Q}]})^{\frac{\max\{1-\sigma,0\}}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{\prime}n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}}.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
,
X\geq 3
, and
\varepsilon>0
, then
\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leq X}\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})/\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\ll_{\varepsilon}C(\pi)^{\varepsilon}\log X
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})\geq 0
for all
\mathfrak{n}
by
[
21
, Lemma a]
, we observe by (
3.12
) that

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leq X}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}\leq e\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{1+\frac{1}{\log X}}}\ll(\log X)\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}).

The desired bounded now follows from (
3.13
) with
\sigma=1
,
t=0
, and
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
J\geq 1
be an integer. For all
j\in\{1,\ldots,J\}
, let
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
;
n_{j},n_{j}^{\prime}
be positive integers; and
\pi_{j}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n_{j}}
and
\pi_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n_{j}^{\prime}}
. Let

D(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{J}L(s+it_{j},\pi_{j}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{j}^{\prime}),\qquad\Delta(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{J}\Lambda(s+it_{j},\pi_{j}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{j}^{\prime}),\qquad Q=\prod_{j=1}^{J}C(\pi_{j})^{n_{j}^{\prime}}C(\pi_{j}^{\prime})^{n_{j}}.

Let
R=-\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}D(s)
. If
1<\sigma<2
and the
\mathfrak{n}
-th Dirichlet coefficient of
-\frac{D^{\prime}}{D}(s)
is nonnegative when
\gcd(\mathfrak{n},\prod_{j=1}^{J}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi_{j}}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi_{j}^{\prime}})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
, then

\sum_{\Delta(\rho)=0}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho}\Big)<\frac{R}{\sigma-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq J\\
\pi_{j}=\pi_{j}^{\prime},~t_{j}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma-1}{(\sigma-1)^{2}+t_{j}^{2}}+O(\log Q).

Proof.

Let
1<\sigma<2
and

D_{\infty}(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{J}L(s,(\pi_{j})_{\infty}\times(\widetilde{\pi}_{j}^{\prime})_{\infty}),\qquad\mathfrak{q}_{D}=\prod_{j=1}^{J}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi_{j}\times\widetilde{\pi}_{j}^{\prime}}.

By comparing the logarithmic derivative of
\Delta(s)
with the logarithmic derivative of its Hadamard factorization

\Delta(s)=e^{a_{D}+b_{D}s}\prod_{\Delta(\rho)=0}\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big)e^{s/\rho},

we find that

\sum_{\Delta(\rho)=0}\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)+b_{D}=\frac{D^{\prime}}{D}(\sigma)+\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{D}}{2}+\frac{R}{\sigma-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq J\\
\pi_{j}=\pi_{j}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma+it_{j}-1}+\frac{1}{\sigma+it_{j}}\Big)+\frac{D_{\infty}^{\prime}}{D_{\infty}}(\sigma).

Since
\textup{Re}(b_{D})=-\sum_{\Delta(\rho)=0}\textup{Re}(\rho^{-1})

[
25
, Proposition 5.7(3)]
, we take real parts and obtain

\sum_{\Delta(\rho)=0}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho}\Big)=\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{D^{\prime}}{D}(s)+\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{D}}{2}+\frac{R}{\sigma-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq J\\
\pi_{j}=\pi_{j}^{\prime}\\
t_{j}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma-1}{(\sigma-1)^{2}+t_{j}^{2}}+\frac{D_{\infty}^{\prime}}{D_{\infty}}(s)\Big)+O(1).

By (
3.3
), (
3.7
), and (
3.10
), the bound
\textup{Re}(\frac{D^{\prime}}{D}(\sigma))\ll\log Q
(resp.
\frac{D_{\infty}^{\prime}}{D_{\infty}}(\sigma)\ll\log Q
) follows from our hypothesis on the Dirichlet coefficients of
-\frac{D^{\prime}}{D}(s)
(resp. Stirling’s formula).
∎

3.3.
Rankin–Selberg combinatorics

A partition
\mu=(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of nonincreasing nonnegative integers
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots
with only finitely many nonzero entries. For a partition
\mu
, let
\ell(\mu)
be the number of nonzero
\mu_{i}
, and let
|\mu|=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu_{i}
. For a set
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
of real numbers and a partition
\mu
with
\ell(\mu)\leq n
, let
s_{\mu}(\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\})
be the Schur polynomial
\det[(\alpha_{i}^{\lambda(j)+n-j})_{ij}]/\det[(\alpha_{i}^{n-j})_{ij}]
associated to
\mu
. If
|\mu|=0
, then
s_{\mu}(\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\})
is identically one. By convention, if
\ell(\mu)>n
, then
s_{\mu}(\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\})
is identically zero.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. By (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and Cauchy’s identity
[
12
, Theorem 38.1]
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}=L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mu}\frac{s_{\mu}(A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))s_{\mu}(A_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p}))}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s|\mu|}},\quad\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}},

where the sum ranges over all partitions. This yields

\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})=\sum_{|\mu|=k}s_{\mu}(A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))s_{\mu}(A_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})),\qquad\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}.

For an integral ideal
\mathfrak{n}
with factorization
\mathfrak{n}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{n})}
(with
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{n})=0
for all but finitely many
\mathfrak{p}
), the multiplicativity of
\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})
tells us that if
\gcd(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
, then

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{n})})=\sum_{(\mu_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\underline{\mu}[\mathfrak{n}]}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}s_{\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}}(A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))s_{\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}}(A_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})),

where
(\mu_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes a sequence of partitions indexed by prime ideals and

(3.15)

\underline{\mu}[\mathfrak{n}]\coloneqq\{(\mu_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon|\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}|=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{n})\textup{ for all $\mathfrak{p}$}\}.

Define the numbers
\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})
on unramified prime powers by

where
-A_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})=\{-\alpha_{1,\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,-\alpha_{n,\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\}

\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})=\sum_{(\mu_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\underline{\mu}[\mathfrak{n}]}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}s_{\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}}(A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))s_{\mu^{*}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(-A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})),\qquad\gcd(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}})=\mathcal{O}_{F}.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\gcd(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}})=\mathcal{O}_{F}

We apply the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means to (

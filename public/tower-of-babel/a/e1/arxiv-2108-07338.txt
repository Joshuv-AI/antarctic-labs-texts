Diganta Das, Jaydeb Das

Abstract
We study lepton number violating and lepton number conserving semi-leptonic decays of heavy baryons Λ b 0 \Lambda_{b}^{0} to three charged leptons and a neutrino. The decays occur through two intermediate quasi-degenerate GeV-scale sterile neutrinos of either Majorana or Dirac type that can be on-shell. Interference between the intermediate heavy neutrinos leads to CP violation in the final states. Effect of neutrino oscillations between the heavy states are also considered in observables of interests, i.e., branching ratio, and the CP-asymmetry. Given the present constraints on the heavy-to-light mixing elements | V e ​ N | |V_{eN}| and | V μ ​ N | |V_{\mu N}| , CP-averaged branching ratio of Λ b 0 → ( Λ c , p ) ​ μ ​ μ ​ e ​ ν \Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c},p)\mu\mu e\nu with intermediate Majorana neutrinos is almost two orders of magnitude larger than for the same with Dirac neutrinos, whereas, CP-averaged branching ratio of Λ b 0 → ( Λ c , p ) ​ e ​ e ​ μ ​ ν \Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c},p)ee\mu\nu is of the same order of magnitude for both Majorana and Dirac neutrino induced decays. CP-violation is found to be appreciable when the neutrino mass difference is comparable with the average decay widths.

L

Formalism

Our calculations are based on a model where the left-handed neutrinos of the SM
SU(2)
doublets are accompanied by two right-handed sterile neutrinos
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. The relation between the SM flavor eigenstates
\nu_{\ell_{i}L}
and the mass eigenstates
\nu_{i,L},N_{1},N_{2}
is

\nu_{\ell_{i}L}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}V_{\ell_{i}\nu_{j}}\nu_{j,L}+V_{\ell_{i}N_{1}}N_{1}+V_{\ell_{i}N_{2}}N_{2},\,\,\ \ell_{i}=e^{-},\mu^{-},\tau^{-}.

(1)

The heavy to light mixing elements
V_{\ell_{i}N_{1}}
and
V_{\ell_{i}N_{2}}
are the free parameters of our model which can be constrained by experimental data. The parameters in general can be complex. We assume a convention that
V_{\ell_{i}N_{j}}
is the mixing between a negatively charged lepton and a sterile neutrino and define a CP-odd phase
\phi_{\ell_{i}N_{j}}
as

V_{\ell_{i}N_{j}}=|V_{\ell_{i}N_{j}}|e^{i\phi_{\ell_{i}N_{j}}}\,,\quad j=1,2

(2)

Under the assumption of CPT conservation, a Majorana neutrino differs from a Dirac neutrino in the fact that the former needs only two degrees of freedom (two helicities) compared to the latter’s four (two helicities each for particle and anti-particle). Another important difference between the two lies in how the PMNS mixing matrix is parametrized for them. For Dirac neutrinos, the PMNS matrix can be parametrized in terms of three mixing angles and one CP-violating phase which is called the Dirac phase
Giunti:2007ry
. For Majorana neutrinos, two additional CP phases, called Majorana phases, are needed.

Our interest is to calculate
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c}^{+},p^{+})\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu
decays and their conjugate modes. Depending on the flavor of the neutrinos that are unobserved, the decays can be either LNV or LNC. For example,
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c}^{+},p^{+})\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu
is LNV if
\nu

\nu_{\ell_{3}}
, but it is LNC if
\nu

\bar{\nu}_{\ell_{2}}
. The LNV decays occur due to Majorana neutrinos whereas LNC decays can happen either due to Majorana or a Dirac. At the LHC, the reconstruction efficiency of tau leptons are is smaller than muon and electrons. Moreover, the tau leptons in the final state are also expected to give severe phase space suppressions. For these reasons, we neglect tau leptons. To reduce the dominant electromagnetic backgrounds we also avoid the events where two leptons of opposite charges are present. Therefore, we will study only the following combinations of leptons in the
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\rightarrow(\Lambda_{c}^{+},p^{+})\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu
decays. Depending on the flavor of the unobserved neutrinos the final states can be either LNV or LNC.

\frac{{\rm Im}\widehat{\Gamma}(XX^{\ast})_{12}}{\widehat{\Gamma}(XX^{\ast})_{jj}}&=2\frac{\eta(y)}{y}\frac{\Gamma_{N_{j}}}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}},\,\,\ X=C,D;j=1,2\end{split}

(12)

where
y=\Delta M_{N}/\Gamma_{N}
, and
\Gamma_{N}=(\Gamma_{N_{1}}+\Gamma_{N_{2}})/2
is the average decay width. Numerically we find

\eta(y)=\frac{y^{2}}{y^{2}+1}\,,\quad\delta(y)=\frac{1}{y^{2}+1}\,.

(13)

which is in agreement with similar decays in mesons

. Physically,
\delta(y)
and
\eta(y)
are correction terms for non-negligible overlap of the two neutrinos. In figure

\eta
and
\delta
as a function of
y
.
{\rm Re}\widehat{\Gamma}(XX^{\ast})_{12}

are also sources of a CP-even phase
\Delta\xi=\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}
where

\tan\xi_{1}=\frac{m_{N_{1}}\Gamma_{N_{1}}}{k_{N}^{2}-m_{N_{1}}^{2}},\,\,\ \tan\xi_{2}=\frac{m_{N_{2}}\Gamma_{N_{2}}}{k_{N}^{2}-m_{N_{2}}^{2}}\,.\quad

(14)

where
k_{N}
is the momentum of the intermediate neutrinos. The cosine and sine of the angles
\Delta\xi
come from the
{\rm Re}\widehat{\Gamma}(XX^{\ast})_{12}
and
{\rm Im}\widehat{\Gamma}(XX^{\ast})_{12}
parts, respectively

.

2.2
Neutrino oscillation

In the previous expressions of decay widths, two effects are missing, acceptance factor
P_{N}
, which is a probability that the sterile neutrino decays within the detector in its flight, and the oscillation between
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
states. The effect of heavy neutrino oscillation is that the decay rates depend on the length of flight
L
of the heavy neutrinos between production and decay vertex
Cvetic:2015ura
. The acceptance factors is given by

\displaystyle P_{N_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{\tau_{N_{j}}\gamma_{N_{j}}}\Big)=1-\exp\Big(-\frac{L\Gamma_{N_{j}}}{\beta_{N_{j}}\gamma_{N_{j}}}\Big)\,,

(15)

where
\tau_{N_{j}}
is the lifetime of
N_{j}
,
\beta_{N_{j}}
is the velocity in the lab frame, and
\gamma_{N_{j}}=(1-\beta_{N_{j}}^{2})^{-1/2}
. Though in practice
P_{N_{j}}
can be different for two neutrinos, they being degenerate in mass we assume
\Gamma_{N_{1}}\approx\Gamma_{N_{2}}=\Gamma_{N}
,
\beta_{N_{1}}=\beta_{N_{2}}\equiv\beta_{N}
. The effective decay widths including the acceptance factor are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV(LNC),eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(1-\exp\Big(-\frac{L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M/D}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\Big)\bigg)\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV(LNC)}

(16)

where the expressions of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV(LNC)}
are given in the previous section, and
\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M/D}
indicate decay rates of Majorana (M) or Dirac (D) neutrinos. For the rest of this section up to the end, we will show the derivations only for the LNV mode. For the LNC mode, only the final expression will be given but the steps of derivations are similar. The effective differential decay width with respect to
L
for LNV mode is given as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV,eff}}{dL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\ \bigg)\bar{\Gamma}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}N)\bar{\Gamma}(N\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}})\times\bigg\{\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}}

(17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}+\frac{4\big(|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|\big)}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}\Big(\delta(y)\cos(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV})

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\frac{\eta(y)}{y}\sin(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV})\Big)\bigg\}\,.~~

To obtain (
17
) from (
16
) we have used (
2.1
), (
11
) and (
12
).

We now consider a scenario where the neutrinos are degenerate, ie
\Delta M_{N}\ll m_{N_{i,j}}
, but
\Delta M_{N}\gg\Gamma_{N}
. In this case
y\gg 1
and hence the functions
\delta(y)
and
\eta(y)/y
are negligibly small. Physically, this corresponds to a scenario where the overlap between the two neutrinos is negligibly small. In the
y\gg 1
limit the effective differential decay rates for LNV modes with respect to
L
are

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV,eff}}{dL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\ \bigg)\bar{\Gamma}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}N)\bar{\Gamma}(N\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}})\times\bigg\{\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}}

(18)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}\bigg\}\,.~~

To introduce effects of
N_{1}-N_{2}
oscillation we note that while a neutrino travels from the production vertex to the decay vertex, it picks up a phase
\exp(-ip_{N_{j}}\cdot z)
where
p_{N_{j}}
is the four momentum of
N_{j}
,
z=(t,0,0,L)
is the space-time separation between the two vertices, and
L\approx\beta_{N}t
is the length traveled by the neutrinos between production and decay vertex
Cohen:2008qb
. This amplitudes is

\mathcal{A}^{\rm LNV}_{\rm osc}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}};L)\sim V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}\exp(-ip_{N_{1}}\cdot z)+V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}\exp(-ip_{N_{2}}\cdot z)\,.

(19)

The expressions for
\mathcal{A}^{\rm LNV}_{\rm osc}(\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{2}\ell_{1}^{+}\ell_{2}^{+}\ell_{3}^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\ell_{3}};L)
is obtained by taking complex conjugate of the
V_{\ell N}
elements in the above formulas. The differential with respect to
L
of the decay rates that include oscillation effect is proportional to the modulus squared of the amplitudes and we can write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dL}\Gamma^{\rm LNV}_{\rm osc}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}};L)\sim\big|\mathcal{A}^{\rm LNV}_{\rm osc}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}};L)\big|^{2}\

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{i=1}^{2}|V_{\ell_{1}N_{i}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{i}}|^{2}+2{\rm Re}\Big(V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}V^{\ast}_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}V^{\ast}_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}\exp[i(p_{N_{2}}-p_{N_{1}}).z]\Big)

(20)

The superscript “osc” indicates that the oscillation effect is included in the effective differential decay width. Comparing with the the differential decay widths (
18
) and (
2.2
) we get the differential expressions of decay rates that includes oscillation as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV,osc}}{dL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\ \bigg)\bar{\Gamma}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}N)\bar{\Gamma}(N\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}})\times\bigg\{\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}}

(21)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}+\frac{4|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi L}{L_{\rm osc}}\mp\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV}\Big)\bigg\}\,.

To simplify the above equations we have used
(p_{N_{2}}-p_{N_{1}}).z=2\pi L/L_{\rm osc}
, and
L_{\rm osc}=2\pi\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}/\Delta M_{N}=2\pi\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}/(y\Gamma_{N})
. Combining (
17
) and (
21
) we get the complete expression of differential decay width that includes both the acceptance factor and the oscillation effects

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV,osc}}{dL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}\ \bigg)\bar{\Gamma}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}N)\bar{\Gamma}(N\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}})\times\bigg\{\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}}

(22)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}+\frac{4\big(|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|\big)}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}\Big(\delta(y)\cos(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV})\pm\frac{\eta(y)}{y}\sin(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV})\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi L}{L_{\rm osc}}\mp\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV}\Big)\bigg\}\,,

where we have used the equations (
2.1
)-(
12
).
Integrating the above equations with respect to
L
we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm LNV,osc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}})=\bar{\Gamma}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}N)\bar{\Gamma}(N\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu_{\ell_{3}})

(23)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bigg\{\bigg[\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}}+\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|\Big(\delta(y)\cos(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV})

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\frac{\eta(y)}{y}\sin(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV})\Big)\bigg]\Big(1-e^{\frac{-L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}}\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm M}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm M}}\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{2}N_{2}}|\bigg(e^{\frac{-L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm M}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}}\Big[y\sin\Big(\frac{2\pi L}{L_{osc}}\mp\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV}\Big)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi L}{L_{osc}}\mp\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV}\Big)\Big]+\Big[\cos\big(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV}\big)\pm y\sin\big(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNV}\big)\Big]\bigg)\bigg\}

Following similar steps, we give the final expression for LNC decay

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}/\bar{\mathcal{B}_{1}}}^{\rm F,LNC,osc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\bar{\nu}_{\ell_{2}})=\bar{\Gamma}(\mathcal{B}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{2}\ell_{1}^{-}N)\bar{\Gamma}(N\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\bar{\nu}_{\ell_{2}})

(24)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bigg\{\bigg[\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{3}N_{1}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm F}}+\frac{|V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}|^{2}|V_{\ell_{3}N_{2}}|^{2}}{\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm F}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm F}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm F}}|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{3}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{3}N_{2}}|\Big(\delta(y)\cos(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNC})

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\frac{\eta(y)}{y}\sin(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNC})\Big)\bigg]\Big(1-e^{\frac{-L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm F}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}}\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\Gamma_{N_{1}}^{\rm F}+\Gamma_{N_{2}}^{\rm F}}\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}|V_{\ell_{1}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{3}N_{1}}||V_{\ell_{1}N_{2}}||V_{\ell_{3}N_{2}}|\bigg(e^{\frac{-L\Gamma_{N}^{\rm F}}{\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}}}\Big[y\sin\Big(\frac{2\pi L}{L_{osc}}\mp\theta_{21}^{\rm LNC}\Big)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi L}{L_{osc}}\mp\theta_{21}^{\rm LNC}\Big)\Big]+\Big[\cos\big(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNC}\big)\pm y\sin\big(\theta_{21}^{\rm LNC}\big)\Big]\bigg)\bigg\}\,.

where F = M (Majorana) or D (Dirac).

3
Numerical analysis

For numerical analysis,
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}
and
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to p^{+}
form factors are taken from the lattice QCD calculations presented in
Detmold:2015aaa
. We leave the decay widths of the sterile neutrinos as phenomenological parameters that can be measured in the experiment. Since the sterile neutrinos are almost degenerate, we will assume their lifetimes to be approximately equal,
\tau_{N_{1}}\approx\tau_{N_{2}}=\tau_{N}
. We present our analysis for
\tau_{N}=[100,1000]
ps
Das:2021prm
. For nearly degenerate neutrinos, it is also natural to assume
|V_{\ell N_{1}}|\sim|V_{\ell N_{2}}|\equiv|V_{\ell N}|
. Present experimental bounds on the heavy-to-light mixing are
|V_{eN}|^{2}\leq 10^{-7}
and
|V_{\mu N}|^{2}\leq 10^{-5}

Abada:2017jjx
;
Atre:2009rg
. The neutrinos are expected to travel nearly at the speed of light and we assume that the velocity
\beta_{N}\geq 0.9
corresponds to
\beta_{N}\gamma_{N}\geq 2
.

The observables of interest are the CP-averaged branching ratio and the CP-asymmetry. If sterile neutrinos involved are of Majorana types then the final states will include both LNV and LNC states and the CP-averaged branching ratio is

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}r^{\rm M}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\mathcal{B}r_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNC,osc}+\mathcal{B}r_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNV,osc}+\mathcal{B}r_{\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNC,osc}+\mathcal{B}r_{\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNV,osc}\Bigg)\,.

(25)

The branching ratios are obtained from decay rates by dividing with the total decay rates of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
baryons. For simplicity, we have assumed that
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
have the same decay rates and that is why we have used
\approx
in the above equation. The CP-asymmetry observable for Majorana induced decay is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{CP}^{\rm M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Big(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNC,osc}+\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNV,osc}\Big)-\Big(\Gamma_{\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNC,osc}+\Gamma_{\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNV,osc}\Big)}{\Big(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNC,osc}+\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNV,osc}\Big)+\Big(\Gamma_{\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNC,osc}+\Gamma_{\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{1}}^{\rm M,LNV,osc}\Big)}\,.

(26)

When Dirac neutrino is involved then there are only LNC final states and CP-averaged branching ratio and the CP-asymmetry are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}r^{\rm D}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\mathcal{B}r_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm D,LNC,osc}+\mathcal{B}r_{\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{\rm D,LNC,osc}\Bigg),\,\,\ \mathcal{A}_{CP}^{\rm D}=\frac{\Big(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm D,LNC,osc}-\Gamma_{\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{1}}^{\rm D,LNC,osc}\Big)}{\Big(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\rm D,LNC,osc}+\Gamma_{\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{1}}^{\rm D,LNC,osc}\Big)}\,.

(27)

4
Summary

In this paper we have studied
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c}^{+},p^{+})\ell_{1}^{-}\ell_{2}^{-}\ell_{3}^{+}\nu
and its conjugate decays mediated by two quasi-degenerate GeV-scale sterile neutrinos. Both Majorana and Dirac neutrinos have been considered and we have focused on kinematical regions where the neutrinos can be on-shell. The heavy neutrino acceptance factor and effects of oscillations are included in the decay rates. We find that the CP-averaged branching ratio for
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c},p)\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\nu
is one orders of magnitude suppressed compared to
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c},p)\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\nu
due to Cabbibo-Kobayashi-Maskawa suppression. For the
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c},p)\mu\mu e\nu
mode, the Majorana induced transition is two orders of magnitude larger than the Dirac induced transition. For the
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to(\Lambda_{c},p)ee\mu\nu
mode, the branching ratios for Majorana and Dirac induced transitions are of the same order of magnitude. Numerically, we have explored the possibility of CP-violation and find that it is appreciable when the neutrino mass difference is of the order of their average decay widths.

Shi-Liang Wu, Cui-Xia Li

Abstract
To our knowledge, the error and perturbation bounds of the general absolute value equations are not discussed. In order to fill in this study gap, in this paper, by introducing a class of absolute value functions, we study the error and perturbation bounds of two types of the general absolute value equations (AVEs): A ​ x − B ​ | x | = b Ax-B|x|=b and A ​ x − | B ​ x | = b Ax-|Bx|=b . Some useful error bounds and perturbation bounds of the above two types of absolute value equations are provided. Without limiting the matrix type, some computable estimates for the above upper bounds are given. By applying the absolute value equations, a new approach for some existing perturbation bounds of the linear complementarity problem (LCP) in (SIAM J. Optim., 18 (2007), pp. 1250-1265) is provided. Some numerical examples for the AVEs from the LCP are given to show the feasibility of the perturbation bounds. Keywords: Absolute value equations; the error bound; the perturbation bound; linear complementarity problem AMS classification: 65G50, 90C33

Error bound

In this section, without further illustration, we always assume that
the AVEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2

this premise, we can give the framework of error bounds on the
distance between the approximate solutions and the exact solutions
of the AVEs (
1.1

2.1
Framework of error bounds for AVEs

In this subsection, the framework of error bounds for the AVEs is
obtained. To achieve our goal, the following absolute value function
is introduced, see Lemma 2.1.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})^{T},b=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n})^{T}
be any two vectors in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there exist
d_{i}\in[-1,1]
such that

|a_{i}|-|b_{i}|=d_{i}(a_{i}-b_{i}),\mbox{for\ all}\ i=1,2,\ldots,n.

(2.1)

Proof.
.

Its proof is straightforward, which is omitted.

\square

Let

r(x)=Ax-B|x|-b.

Clearly,
x^{\ast}
is a solution of the AVEs (
1.1
) if and
only if
r(x^{\ast})=0
. The function
r(x)
is called the natural
residual of the AVEs (
1.1
). Let
x^{\ast}
be the unique
solution of the AVEs (
1.1
). Then from Lemma 2.1 we have

\displaystyle r(x)=

\displaystyle Ax-B|x|-b-(Ax^{\ast}-B|x^{\ast}|-b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(x-x^{\ast})-B(|x|-|x^{\ast}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(x-x^{\ast})-B\tilde{D}(x-x^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-B\tilde{D})(x-x^{\ast}),

where
\tilde{D}=diag(\tilde{d}_{i})
with
\tilde{d}_{i}\in[-1,1]
, which promptly results in the error bounds for the AVEs
(
1.1
), see Theorem 2.1.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
x^{*}
be the unique solution of AVEs (
1.1
). Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{1}{\underline{\alpha}}\|r(x)\|\leq\|x-x^{\ast}\|\leq\overline{\alpha}\|r(x)\|,

(2.2)

where

\underline{\alpha}=\max\{\|A-BD\|:D=\mbox{diag}(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}

and

\overline{\alpha}=\max\{\|(A-BD)^{-1}\|:D=\mbox{diag}(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}.

In Theorem 2.1,
\underline{\alpha}\overline{\alpha}\geq 1
. In fact,

\displaystyle\underline{\alpha}\overline{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\max\{\|A-BD\|\|(A-BD)^{-1}\|:D=diag(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}

\displaystyle\geq\max\{\|(A-BD)(A-BD)^{-1}\|:D=diag(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}

\displaystyle=1.

Similarly, the following involved results, i.e., Theorem 2.2 and
Corollary 2.1, are completely analogical.

By using the same technique for the AVEs (
1.2
), we have

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
x^{*}
be the unique solution of AVEs (
1.2
). Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{1}{\underline{\beta}}\|r(x)\|\leq\|x-x^{\ast}\|\leq\overline{\beta}\|r(x)\|,

(2.3)

where

\underline{\beta}=\max\{\|A-DB\|:D=\mbox{diag}(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}

and

\overline{\beta}=\max\{\|(A-DB)^{-1}\|:D=diag(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}.

When
B=I
in Theorem 2.1 or Theorem 2.2, the error bounds for the
AVEs (
1.3

Corollary 2.1

Let

\frac{1}{\underline{\gamma}}\|r(x)\|\leq\|x-x^{\ast}\|\leq\overline{\gamma}\|r(x)\|,

\underline{\gamma}=\max\{\|A-D\|:D=diag(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}

\overline{\gamma}=\max\{\|(A-D)^{-1}\|:D=diag(d_{i})~\mbox{with}~d_{i}\in[-1,1]\}.

.

Clearly, Corollary 2.1 provides the lower and upper error bounds for

In

Theorem 2.3
.

(Theorem 4.1 in
[
7

) Let

be the unique solution of AVEs (
1.3

\frac{1}{\underline{C}}\|r(x)\|\leq\|x-x^{\ast}\|\leq\overline{C}\|r(x)\|,

(2.5)

where

\underline{C}=\|A+I\|+\|A-I\|\ \mbox{and}\ \overline{C}=\frac{\|A+I\|+\|A-I\|}{\sigma_{\min}(A)^{2}-1}\ \mbox{with}\ \sigma_{\min}(A)>1.

Now, we show that the error bounds in Corollary 2.1 are sharper than
that in Theorem 2.3. Here, we consider
\|\cdot\|_{2}
for
Corollary 2.1 and Theorem 2.3.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let the assumptions of Corollary 2.1 and Theorem 2.3 be
satisfied. Then for any diagonal matrix
D=diag(d_{i})
with

d_{i}\in[-1,1]
,

\|A+I\|_{2}+\|A-I\|_{2}\geq\max\|A-D\|_{2}

(2.6)

and

\max\|(A-D)^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\|A+I\|_{2}+\|A-I\|_{2}}{\sigma_{\min}(A)^{2}-1}.

(2.7)

Proof.
.

Firstly, we prove (
2.6
). Since

\displaystyle\max\|A-D\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\max\|A+I-I-D\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max(\|A+I\|_{2}+\|I+D\|_{2})

\displaystyle=\|A+I\|_{2}+\max\|I+D\|_{2}

\displaystyle=2+\|A+I\|_{2}

(2.8)

and

\displaystyle\max\|A-D\|_{2}\leq 2+\|A-I\|_{2}.

(2.9)

Combining (
Proof.
) and (
2.9
) leads to

\max\|A-D\|_{2}\leq 2+\frac{\|A-I\|_{2}+\|A+I\|_{2}}{2}\leq\|A-I\|_{2}+\|A+I\|_{2},

the latter inequality holds because

2=\|2I\|_{2}=\|I+A+I-A\|_{2}\leq\|I+A\|_{2}+\|I-A\|_{2}.

This shows that (
2.6
) holds.

Next, we prove (
2.7
). It only needs to prove

(\sigma_{\min}(A)^{2}-1)\max\|(A-D)^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\|A+I\|_{2}+\|A-I\|_{2}.

From
\sigma_{\min}(A)>1
, we know
\|A^{-1}\|_{2}<1
. Further,

\|A^{-1}\|_{2}\|D\|_{2}<1
. By Banach Perturbation Lemma in

[
26
]
, clearly,

\|(A-D)^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\|A^{-1}\|_{2}}{1-\|A^{-1}\|_{2}\|D\|_{2}}\leq\frac{\|A^{-1}\|_{2}}{1-\|A^{-1}\|_{2}}.

By the simple computation, we have

\displaystyle\|A^{-1}\|_{2}(\sigma_{\min}(A)^{2}-1)=

\displaystyle\|A^{-1}\|_{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}(A^{-1})^{2}}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|A^{-1}\|_{2}(\frac{1}{\|A^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\|A^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|A^{-1}\|_{2}}.

So, we get

(\sigma_{\min}(A)^{2}-1)\max\|(A-D)^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1+\|A^{-1}\|_{2},

this shows that then we only show that

1+\|A^{-1}\|_{2}\leq|A+I\|_{2}+\|A-I\|_{2}.

In fact, together with
\|A^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle 1+\|A^{-1}\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle 2=\|2I\|_{2}=\|I+A^{-1}+I-A^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|I+A^{-1}\|_{2}+\|I-A^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|A^{-1}(I+A)\|_{2}+\|A^{-1}(I-A)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A^{-1}\|_{2}(\|A+I\|_{2}+\|A-I\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A+I\|_{2}+\|A-I\|_{2}.

This proves (
2.7
).
\square

(A,b)

x^{\ast}

the LCP
(A,b)

(B,c)

or,

\frac{I+\tilde{D}+(I-\tilde{D})A}{2}(x^{\ast}-y^{\ast})=-\frac{(I-\tilde{D})(A-B)}{2}y^{\ast}-\frac{(I-\tilde{D})(b-c)}{2},

\displaystyle\|x^{\ast}-y^{\ast}\|=

\displaystyle\|(I-\tilde{\Lambda}+\tilde{\Lambda}A)^{-1}\tilde{\Lambda}\|\|(A-B)y^{\ast}+b-c\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(I-\tilde{\Lambda}+\tilde{\Lambda}A)^{-1}\tilde{\Lambda}\|(\|A-B\|\|y^{\ast}\|+\|b-c\|)

(B,c_{+})

(4.9)

6
Conclusion

In this paper, by introducing a class of absolute value functions,
the frameworks of error and perturbation bounds for two types of
absolute value equations (AVEs) have been established. Besides,
without limiting the matrix type, some computable estimates for the
above upper bounds are given. As an application, a new approach for
some existing perturbation bounds of the LCP in
[
32
]
is
provided. In addition, some numerical examples for absolute value
equations from the LCP are given to show the feasibility of the
proposed perturbation bounds.

Renormalized solutions for the Maxwell–Stefan system with an application to uniqueness of weak solutions
Stefanos Georgiadis, Hoyoun Kim, Athanasios E. Tzavaras

Abstract
. We give conditions that guarantee uniqueness of renormalized solutions for the Maxwell-Stefan system. The proof is based on an identity for the evolution of the symmetrized relative entropy. Using the method of doubling the variables we derive the identity for two renormalized solutions and use information on the spectrum of the Maxwell-Stefan matrix to estimate the symmetrized relative entropy and show uniqueness. We then show that weak solutions for the Maxwell-Stefan system have sufficient regularity to produce renormalized solutions. Combining the two results yields a uniqueness result for weak solutions of the Maxwell-Stefan system with bounded fluxes.

Introduction

The Maxwell-Stefan system describes diffusive phenomena in multicomponent systems of gases. It appears in applications from various domains,
for instance in dialysis, ion exchange, sedimentation and electrolysis. The problem of existence of solutions has been extensively studied in the literature,
where local-in-time strong solutions
[
2
,
9
,
10
]
and global-in-time weak solutions
[
14
]
have been established. The uniqueness of strong solutions has been shown in
[
10
,
13
]
, however for weak solutions the problem has been open, with the only available result
being the weak-strong uniqueness property
[
12
]
. We show here that weak solutions to the Maxwell-Stefan system are unique, provided the molar fluxes are essentially bounded.

The Maxwell-Stefan system describes the evolution of the vector function
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})
where
c_{i}(x,t)
is the concentration and
u_{i}(x,t)
the velocity of the
i
-th component,
i=1,\dots,n
.
Their dynamics is described by
n
equations for the conservation of mass,

(1.1)

\partial_{t}c_{i}+\nabla\cdot J_{i}=0\,,

with
J_{i}=c_{i}u_{i}
the molar fluxes and
u_{i}
the diffusional velocities. System (
1.1
) is complemented by the constrained linear system

(1.2)

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace]-\sum_{{j=1},{j\not=i}}^{n}\frac{c_{j}J_{i}-c_{i}J_{j}}{D_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\nabla c_{i}\quad\quad i=1,\dots,n\,,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}J_{i}

\displaystyle=0,

for determining the molar fluxes
J_{i}(x,t)
, where
D_{ij}=D_{ji}>0
,
i\not=j
, are the diffusion coefficients modeling binary interactions between the components.
We will consider the problem on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(0,T)
, where
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
is the
3
-dimensional torus and
T>0
.
The system is completed with the initial conditions,

(1.4)

c_{i}(x,0)=c_{i}^{0}(x)\quad\text{ on }{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\,.

The initial concentrations
c_{i}^{0}(x)\geq 0
are such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{0}(x)=1
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
, then the constraint (
1.3
) propagates
and implies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=1
, for
x\in{\mathbb{T}^{3}}
,
t>0
. The property
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=1
provides a consistency condition for the constrained linear system (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) which is uniquely solvable;
see
[
9
,
2
,
14
,
11
,
12
]
for solvability properties of the linear system (
1.2
)-(
1.3
).

The system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) is endowed with an entropy functional, which for simple gases takes the form

(1.5)

H(\mathbf{c})=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}(\ln c_{i}-1)\textnormal{d}x\,.

The functional is well-defined even for
c_{i}=0
and under natural conditions on the initial data leads
to a global existence theory
[
14
]
for weak solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
),
by exploiting the entropy structure in conjunction with the properties of the friction operator.

A commonly used method for stability proceeds by computing the evolution of the relative entropy, a method
applicable when one of the solutions is a strong solution. It provides
[
12
]
a weak-strong uniqueness result
under the assumptions that the strong solution is sufficiently smooth and presents no anomalous diffusion (concentrations in the entropy identity).
The required smoothness of the strong solution is considerably more than what is provided by the global existence result in
[
14
]

and regularity results for weak solutions (even if expected) are currently not available. The strong solution in
[
2
]
is only locally defined in time
and the weak-strong uniqueness result
[
12
]
yields uniqueness so long as a strong solution exists.

To exploit the relative entropy under reduced regularity assumptions two new tools are introduced: (i) the relative entropy is replaced by the
symmetrized relative entropy

(1.6)

H^{sym}(\mathbf{c},\bar{\mathbf{c}}):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\ln{c_{i}}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\ \textnormal{d}x

which satisfies a nice formal identity (see (
2.13
)). (ii) For the rigorous derivation of the relative entropy identity we introduce
renormalized solutions for the Maxwell-Stefan system. (iii) Finally, the singular behavior at the origin needs to be approximated, and this is achieved by introducing
a companion Maxwell-Stefan system for shifted solutions.

Renormalized solutions were introduced in the study of the Boltzmann equation
[
5
]
and
extended to transport equations
[
4
]
, nonlinear elliptic problems
[
17
]
, equations of fluid mechanics
[
16
]
, nonlinear parabolic problems
[
1
]
. Closest to the use here are the references
[
16
, pp. 227]
,
[
6
, Sec 4.1.5]
concerning the compressible Navier-Stokes system.
To motivate the definition of a renormalized solution, compute the evolution of a smooth function
\beta(c_{i})
. Formal multiplication of (
1.1
) by
\beta^{\prime}(c_{i})
gives:

(1.7)

\partial_{t}\beta(c_{i})+\nabla\cdot(\beta^{\prime}(c_{i})c_{i}u_{i})-2c_{i}\beta^{\prime\prime}(c_{i})\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\cdot\sqrt{c_{i}}u_{i}=0

From the existence theory of weak solutions
[
14
]
, we know that
\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\cdot\sqrt{c_{i}}u_{i}\in L^{1}
and use this information
to give meaning to (
1.7
).

The formal identity (
2.13
) leads after integration to

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\sum_{i}(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\Big)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\underbrace{\sum_{i,j}{\frac{1}{2D_{ij}}(c_{i}c_{j}+\bar{c}_{i}\bar{c}_{j})\big|(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})-(u_{j}-\bar{u}_{j})\big|^{2}}}_{Q}dx=E_{1}+E_{2}

where
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
are error terms.
Deriving the identity (
1.8
) is the main difficulty; in the process we employ renormalized solutions
and an adaptation of Krushkov’s method of doubling of variables.
It is also proved that weak solutions of the regularity class provided by the existence theory
[
14
]
yield renormalized solutions.
As an application of the latter result, we obtain uniqueness of weak solutions.
The obtained uniqueness theorem is conditional, in the sense that it holds for global weak solutions of additional regularity than that provided in
[
14
]
;
namely, it is valid for solutions of regularity
\nabla c_{i}\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}
versus the regularity

\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\in L^{2}_{t,x}
provided by the existence theory. On the other hand, it appears to exploit the full capacity of the relative entropy identity, see the discussion in section
8
.

An outline of the contents follows: In section
2
, we state the formal estimates,
motivated by the transport equation structure, leading to the transport identity (

) for the symmetrized relative entropy.
Renormalized solutions are used to give a meaning to (

obtained results are outlined in section

In section

induce renormalized solutions. This hinges on two lemmas, inspired by the work
[

on renormalized solutions of reaction-diffusion systems,
proved in section

In section

stated in Theorem

Then we have to resolve the following problem. The derived identity (
3.5
) holds for test functions
\beta\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
and this class does not include the
logarithm due to the singularity at the origin. To compensate, we introduce in section

an approximation at the origin and the observation that a shifted solution
satisfies a companion Maxwell-Stefan system.
The last step, performed in section
7
, combines information from
[
12
]
on the spectral gap of an operator associated to the frictional relative work

with a delicate estimation of the error terms

and

It leads to a uniqueness result for renormalized solutions of the Maxwell-Stefan system, see Theorem

2.
Formal comparison of solutions of transport equations

The entropy (
1.5
) of the Maxwell-Stefan system plays an important role in the existence theory of weak solutions
[
14
]
,
and the relative entropy

(2.1)

H(\mathbf{c}|\bar{\mathbf{c}})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(c_{i}\ln\frac{c_{i}}{\bar{c}_{i}}-(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\Big)\textnormal{d}x

was used in
[
12
]
to estimate
the distance between two solutions
\mathbf{c}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
. Here, we will employ instead a symmetrized version of the relative entropy

(2.2)

H^{sym}(\mathbf{c},\bar{\mathbf{c}}):=H(\mathbf{c}|\bar{\mathbf{c}})+H(\bar{\mathbf{c}}|\mathbf{c})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\textnormal{d}x

The quantity
(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})
is positive for
0<c_{i},\bar{c}_{i}<1
when
c_{i}\neq\bar{c}_{i}
. It takes the value
\infty

when one out of
c_{i}
or
\bar{c_{i}}
vanishes, and it can be defined to be
+\infty
when both vanish. Hence, it can serve to measure
the distance between two solutions.

For a renormalized solution corresponding to (
1.7
), an entropy functional is defined by

(2.3)

H_{B}(\mathbf{c})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{c_{i}}\beta(s)\textnormal{d}s\,\textnormal{d}x\,.

Setting
B(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\beta(\tau)\textnormal{d}\tau
, the symmetrized relative entropy of two renormalized solutions
\mathbf{c}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
is given by

(2.4)

H^{sym}_{B}(\mathbf{c},\bar{\mathbf{c}})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\beta(c_{i})-\beta(\bar{c}_{i}))(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\,\textnormal{d}x\,.

When
\beta
is a monotone map, the symmetrized relative entropy is nonnegative and offers a suitable tool to measure the
distance between
\mathbf{c}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
.

2.1.
Uniqueness for the heat equation

In this section we present a uniqueness/stability estimate for the heat equation.
The heat equation is first written in the form suggested by the so-called Otto calculus
[
18
]

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}\rho u=0\qquad u=-\nabla\ln\rho.

We present an identity based only on the transport equation,
\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}\rho u=0
,
which yields a relative entropy comparison between two solutions of (
2.5
).
Let
(\rho,u)
and
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u})
be two solutions of the transport equation and we write down the identities

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}\rho u

\displaystyle=0

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\ln\bar{\rho}+\operatorname{div}\bar{u}+\nabla\ln\bar{\rho}\cdot\bar{u}

\displaystyle=0.

We multiply (
2.6
) by
\ln\bar{\rho}
and (
2.7
) by
\rho
and add to obtain

(2.8)

\partial_{t}(\rho\ln\bar{\rho})+\operatorname{div}(\rho\ln\bar{\rho}\,u+\rho\bar{u})+\nabla\ln\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho}\cdot\rho\bar{u}-\nabla\ln\bar{\rho}\cdot\rho u=0.

In particular, we have

(2.9)

\partial_{t}(\rho\ln\rho)+\operatorname{div}(\rho\ln\rho\,u+\rho u)-\nabla\ln\rho\cdot\rho u=0.

Next, we add (
2.9
) with the corresponding equation for
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u})
, and then from the result we subtract (
2.8
) and the analog
of (
2.8
) with
(\rho,u)
and
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u})
interchanged. After re-arranging the terms we obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\big((\ln\rho-\ln\bar{\rho})(\rho-\bar{\rho})\big)

\displaystyle+\operatorname{div}\big((\ln\rho-\ln\bar{\rho})(\rho u-\bar{\rho}\bar{u})+(\rho-\bar{\rho})(u-\bar{u})\big)

\displaystyle-(\rho+\bar{\rho})\nabla(\ln\rho-\ln\bar{\rho})\cdot(u-\bar{u})=0.

We emphasize that up to here we only used the transport equation (
2.6
).

Now, consider the heat equation (
2.5
) and introduce the formula
u=-\nabla\ln\rho
to (
2.10
).
After an integration, we obtain the identity

(2.11)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\big((\ln\rho-\ln\bar{\rho})(\rho-\bar{\rho})\big)\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(\rho+\bar{\rho})|\nabla(\ln\rho-\ln\bar{\rho})|^{2}\textnormal{d}x=0

which provides an alternative approach to show uniqueness and stability for the heat equation, based on its entropy structure.

2.2.
The symmetrized relative entropy identity for the Maxwell-Stefan system

Consider next the Maxwell-Stefan system and return to the notation
c_{i}
for the densities and
J_{i}=c_{i}u_{i}
for the fluxes. Using
the transport equations (
1.1
) for the individual components and following the derivation of section
2.1
we obtain the identity

(2.12)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}&\Big(\sum_{i}(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\Big)\\
&+\operatorname{div}\Big(\sum_{i}(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}u_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i})+(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\Big)\\
&\qquad-\sum_{i}\Big(\nabla(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})\cdot(c_{i}+\bar{c}_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\Big)=0.\end{split}

Next, we use the algebraic system (
1.2
),

-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}c_{j}(u_{i}-u_{j})=\nabla\ln c_{i}

and the symmetry of
D_{ij}
to express

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}(c_{i}+\bar{c}_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\cdot\nabla(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})\cdot(c_{i}+\bar{c}_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{i,j}\tfrac{1}{2}c_{i}c_{j}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}\big|(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})-(u_{j}-\bar{u}_{j})\big|^{2}+\sum_{i,j}(c_{j}-\bar{c}_{j})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\cdot\frac{c_{i}}{D_{ij}}(\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{j})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j}\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{c}_{i}\bar{c}_{j}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}\big|(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})-(u_{j}-\bar{u}_{j})\big|^{2}+\sum_{i,j}(c_{j}-\bar{c}_{j})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\cdot\frac{\bar{c}_{i}}{D_{ij}}(u_{i}-u_{j}).

Combining with (
2.12
) we arrive at the (formal) identity

(2.13)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}&\Big(\sum_{i}(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\Big)\\
&\quad+\operatorname{div}\Big(\sum_{i}(\ln c_{i}-\ln\bar{c}_{i})(c_{i}u_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i})+(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\Big)\\
&\quad+\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2D_{ij}}(c_{i}c_{j}+\bar{c}_{i}\bar{c}_{j})\big|(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})-(u_{j}-\bar{u}_{j})\big|^{2}\\
&=-\sum_{i,j}(c_{j}-\bar{c}_{j})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\cdot\frac{1}{D_{ij}}\Big(c_{i}(\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{j})+\bar{c}_{i}(u_{i}-u_{j})\Big).\end{split}

The identity (
2.13
) will guide the analysis of uniqueness in the following sections. It bears analogies to the identity used
for weak-strong uniqueness in
[
12
]
, but here the evolution is computed for the symmetrized relative entropy.
It should be compared to Corollary
5
for
\delta=0
.
In section
7.2
it is complemented with
spectral properties of the matrix in (
1.2
) that will quantify the effect of friction.

3.
Description of results

We work on the torus
{\mathbb{T}^{3}}
with initial data that satisfy

(A)

0\leq c_{i}^{0}\in L^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}})\,,\;\;i=1,\dots,n\,;\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{0}=1\,;\qquad H(\mathbf{c}^{0})<+\infty\,.

The solution propagates from the data the constraint
\sum_{i}c_{i}=1

Definition 1
.

A function

{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=1}

(ii)

\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\in L^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(0,\infty))\,,\;\sqrt{c_{i}}u_{i}\in L^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(0,\infty)))
,

(iii)

for any test function
\phi_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times[0,\infty))
, we have

(3.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}c_{i}^{0}(x)\phi_{i}(x,0)\textnormal{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}c_{i}\partial_{t}\phi_{i}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}c_{i}u_{i}\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t=0

and

1.2
)-(
1.3
).

(iv)

{\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|c_{i}(x,t)-c_{i}^{0}(x)|dx\to 0.}

Existence of weak solutions is established in

(3.2)

For test functions

Definition 2
.

A function
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})
is a renormalized solution of (

)-(

), if for

it satisfies:

(i)

0\leq c_{i}\leq 1

{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=1}

(ii)

\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\in L^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(0,\infty))\,,\;\sqrt{c_{i}}u_{i}\in L^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(0,\infty)))
,

(iii)

for any test function
\phi_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times[0,\infty))
and for
\beta\in C^{2}([0,\infty))

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(c_{i}^{0})\phi_{i}(x,0)\textnormal{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(c_{i})\partial_{t}\phi_{i}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\

{\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\beta(c_{i}(x,t))-\beta(c_{i}^{0}(x))|\textnormal{d}x\to 0}

\beta\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
all terms in (

To this degree, we start by studying the relation between weak and renormalized solutions. We show that the weak solutions constructed in
[
14
]
have sufficient regularity to induce renormalized solutions in the sense of Definition
2
:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
be a weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
1

3.2

Then
\mathbf{c}

Then, the problem of uniqueness of weak solutions reduces to showing uniqueness for renormalized solutions. The objective is to make the derivation of the symmetrized relative entropy identity (
2.12
) (equivalently (

1.6

Thus, we introduce a symmetrized relative entropy, which is regularized so as to also allow for zero concentrations:

(3.4)

F(\mathbf{c},\bar{\mathbf{c}}):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\ln{(c_{i}+\delta)}-\ln(\bar{c}_{i}+\delta))(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\ \textnormal{d}x

where

The first step is to derive an identity for the symmetrized relative entropy between two renormalized solutions, using the method of doubling the variables.
This method, due to Kruzhkov
[
15
]
, was further developed in
[
3
]
, and allows to treat one solution as a constant with respect to the evolution of the other,
in order to derive a relative entropy identity, overcoming the lack of regularity. Compared to the use of doubling of variables in
hyperbolic problems, we here employ also the regularity properties emerging from the existence theory.

The work proceeds as follows: First we establish a symmetrized relative entropy inequality between two renormalized solutions:

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
be renormalized solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) emanating from the same initial data.
Then for any

\beta\in C^{2}([0,\infty))

(3.5)

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\big(\beta(c_{i})-\beta(\bar{c}_{i})\big)(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\,\textnormal{d}x\Big|_{t=T}\\
&\quad=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\nabla\left(\beta(c_{i})-\beta(\bar{c}_{i})\right)\cdot(c_{i}u_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i})\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\Big(\nabla\big[(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\beta^{\prime}(c_{i})\big]\cdot c_{i}u_{i}-\nabla\big[(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\beta^{\prime}(\bar{c}_{i})\big]\cdot\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}\Big)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.\end{split}

Now, the choice of
\beta(s)=\ln(s+\delta)
, which is indeed in
C^{2}([0,\infty))
, in (
3.5
) implies the following result:

Corollary 5
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
be renormalized solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2

\delta>0

(3.6)

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{c},\bar{\mathbf{c}})(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(c_{i}+\bar{c}_{i}+2\delta)\ \nabla\big(\ln(c_{i}+\delta)-\ln(\bar{c}_{i}+\delta)\big)\cdot\left(\frac{c_{i}u_{i}}{c_{i}+\delta}-\frac{\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}}{\bar{c}_{i}+\delta}\right)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.

We remark that up to Corollary
5
only the transport structure (
1.1
) was used. The question becomes how the
formal identity (
2.12
) is affected by the introduction of the corrections in the logarithmic terms.
To achieve this, a change of variables is quite helpful, namely, the functions
d_{i}=c_{i}+\delta
and
v_{i}=\frac{c_{i}u_{i}}{c_{i}+\delta}

satisfy an approximate Maxwell-Stefan system in the form (
6.1
). This motivates to express the error estimate for the new system.
A careful estimation of the terms involved in (
3.6
) yields the uniqueness of nonnegative renormalized solutions:

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
be renormalized solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) emanating from the same initial data. Then, if
c_{i}u_{i}
and
\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}
are in
L^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(0,\infty))
, there exists at most one nonnegative renormalized solution to the Maxwell-Stefan system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

As a consequence, the uniqueness of weak solutions follows:

Corollary 7
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
be weak solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) emanating from the same initial data and suppose that

c_{i}u_{i}
and
\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}
are in
L^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(0,\infty))
.
Then,
\mathbf{c}=\bar{\mathbf{c}}
, i.e. weak solutions are unique.

The paper is organized as follows: In section
4
we show that weak and renormalized solutions coincide (cf. Theorem
3
). For the proof of Theorem
3
, we make use of two lemmas for generic transport equations. In section
5
, the method of doubling the variables is used to derive a relative entropy identity for renormalized solutions, thus showing Theorem
4
. In section
6
, we perform the calculations that deal with vanishing solutions and lead to Corollary
5
, and we present the necessary preparations introducing the shifted solution. Then in section
7
we exploit the frictional structure of the Maxwell-Stefan inversion matrix, in order to show Theorem
6
. We conclude the paper with a brief discussion of the results and their implications in section
8
.

4.
Equivalence between weak and renormalized solutions

It is evident from Definition
2
that weak solutions are a special case of renormalized solutions.
In this section, we investigate the other direction (weak
\Rightarrow
renormalized) and prove Theorem
3
.

We first prove a result for generic transport equations, which is inspired by
[
7
, Lemmas 4 and 5]
and which is used in the proof of Theorem
3
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
u\in C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
, with
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
and
u^{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3})
, and
z\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))^{3})
, be such that for all
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times[0,T))

(4.1)

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u\partial_{t}\psi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u^{0}\psi(x,0)\textnormal{d}x=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}z\cdot\nabla\psi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\beta\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathbb{R}})\textnormal{ s.t. }|\beta(w)|\leq C|w|^{2},\;|\beta^{\prime}(w)|\leq C|w|,\;|\beta^{\prime\prime}(w)|\leq C\}
. Then, for
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times[0,T))
, there holds

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(u)\partial_{t}\psi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(u^{0})\psi(x,0)\textnormal{d}x\\
&\phantom{xxxxxxxxxx}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime}(u)z\cdot\nabla\psi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\psi\beta^{\prime\prime}(u)\nabla u\cdot z\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
\rho_{\sigma}
denote a standard symmetric mollifier with respect to space. We choose the test function
\rho_{\sigma}*\psi
in (
4.1
) and integrate by parts (in space) on the right-hand side, to obtain for all
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times[0,T))

(4.2)

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\partial_{t}\psi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\rho_{\sigma}*u^{0})\psi(x,0)\textnormal{d}x=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\operatorname{div}(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\psi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.

Define the functions
f^{\sigma},g^{\sigma}:{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times[-T,T]\to\mathbb{R}
by:

(4.3)

f^{\sigma}(x,t)=\begin{cases}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)(x,t),&\quad t>0\\
(\rho_{\sigma}*u^{0})(x),&\quad t<0\end{cases}

and

(4.4)

g^{\sigma}(x,t)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{div}(\rho_{\sigma}*z)(x,t),&\quad t>0\\
0,&\quad t<0\end{cases}

and notice that
f^{\sigma},g^{\sigma}\in L^{2}(-T,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
. Then, for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(-T,T))
,
f^{\sigma}
and
g^{\sigma}
satisfy, due to (
4.2
),

(4.5)

-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}f^{\sigma}(x,t)\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t=-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}g^{\sigma}(x,t)\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.

Let, now,
\rho_{\tau}
be a standard symmetric mollifier with respect to time. Using
\rho_{\tau}*\varphi
as a test function in (
4.5
), we get for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(-T+2\tau,T+2\tau))

-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t=-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma})\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t,

which implies that
\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}\in C^{1}((-T+\tau,T-\tau);L^{1}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}))
, with the representation

\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})=-\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma},\quad\textnormal{in the $L^{1}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}})$ sense}.

Now, for
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
, we have by the chain rule

(4.6)

-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t=-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})(\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma})\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(-T+2\tau,T-2\tau))
.

Keeping
\sigma>0
fixed, we let
\tau\to 0
. For the term on the left-hand side,
\beta
is continuous and
\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}\to f^{\sigma}
a.e., hence
\beta(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})\to\beta(f^{\sigma})
a.e. and due to
f^{\sigma}
being in
L^{2}(-T,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
and the growth of
\beta
, we have

\displaystyle|\beta(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})\partial_{t}\varphi|\leq C|\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}|^{2}\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty}\quad\textnormal{and}

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}\to f^{\sigma},\textnormal{ in $L^{2}(K)$ for any compact $K\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(-T,T)$}.

Similarly, for the right-hand side, since
\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma}\to g^{\sigma}
a.e. and
\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}\to f^{\sigma}
a.e. and
\beta^{\prime}
is continuous, we have
\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})(\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma})\to\beta^{\prime}(f^{\sigma})g^{\sigma}
a.e. and due to the growth of
\beta^{\prime}
and
f^{\sigma},g^{\sigma}
being in
L^{2}(-T,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
, we have

\displaystyle|\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})(\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma})\varphi|\leq C|\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}||\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma}|\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\quad\textnormal{and}

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}\to f^{\sigma},\textnormal{ in $L^{2}(K)$ for any compact $K\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(-T,T)$},

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}*g^{\sigma}\to g^{\sigma},\textnormal{ in $L^{2}(K)$ for any compact $K\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(-T,T)$}.

Letting
\tau\to 0
we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to deduce that

-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(f^{\sigma})\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t=-\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime}(f^{\sigma})(g^{\sigma})\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t,

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\times(-T,T))
and because of (
4.3
)-(
4.4
) we recover

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(\rho_{\sigma}*u^{0})\varphi(x,0)\,\textnormal{d}x=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\operatorname{div}(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.

We, now, integrate by parts on the right-hand side

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(\rho_{\sigma}*u^{0})\varphi(x,0)\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\phantom{xx}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\nabla(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\cdot(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\end{split}

and let
\sigma\to 0
. For the terms on the left-hand side, we have
\rho_{\sigma}*u\to u
a.e. and the continuity of
\beta
implies that
\beta(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\to\beta(u)
a.e.. Moreover, by
u\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
and the growth condition on
\beta
, we see that

\displaystyle|\beta(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\partial_{t}\varphi|\leq C|\rho_{\sigma}*u|^{2}\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty},\textnormal{ where}

\displaystyle\rho_{\sigma}*u\to u\textnormal{ in $L^{2}(K)$, for any compact $K\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(0,T)$}.

For the first term on the right-hand side, we have
\beta^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\nabla(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\cdot(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\to\beta^{\prime\prime}(u)\nabla u\cdot z
a.e. and use the growth condition
|\beta^{\prime\prime}(w)|\leq C
and the fact that
z,\nabla u\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))^{3})
to get

\displaystyle|\beta^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\nabla(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\cdot(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\varphi|\leq C|\nabla(\rho_{\sigma}*u)||\rho_{\sigma}*z|\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\textnormal{ where}

\displaystyle\nabla(\rho_{\sigma}*u)\to\nabla u\textnormal{ and }\rho_{\sigma}*z\to z\textnormal{ in $L^{2}(K)$ for any compact $K\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(0,T)$}.

Finally, for the last term on the right-hand side
\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\to\beta^{\prime}(u)z
a.e. and since
|\beta^{\prime}(w)|\leq C|w|
,
u\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
and
z\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))^{3})
we obtain

\displaystyle|\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\sigma}*u)(\rho_{\sigma}*z)\cdot\nabla\varphi|\leq C|\rho_{\sigma}*u||\rho_{\sigma}*z|\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty},\textnormal{ where}

\displaystyle\rho_{\sigma}*u\to u\textnormal{ and }\rho_{\sigma}*z\to z\textnormal{ in $L^{2}(K)$ for any compact $K\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times(0,T)$}.

By the dominated convergence theorem, taking the limit
\sigma\to 0
, we conclude

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(u)\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(u^{0})\varphi(x,0)\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\phantom{xxxxxxxx}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime\prime}(u)\nabla u\cdot z\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime}(u)z\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.\end{split}

∎

If the solution satisfies the bounds
0\leq u\leq M
, for some
M>0
, the growth conditions on
\beta
and its derivatives are not needed:

Lemma 9
.

Let
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
, with
0\leq u(x,t)\leq M
, for some
M>0
,
u^{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3})
and
z\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))^{3})
satisfy for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times[0,T))

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u^{0}\varphi(x,0)\textnormal{d}x=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}z\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.

Let
\beta\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
. Then, for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}\times[0,T))
, there holds

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(u)\partial_{t}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta(u^{0})\varphi(x,0)\textnormal{d}x\\
&\phantom{xxxxxxxxxx}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime}(u)z\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\beta^{\prime\prime}(u)\nabla u\cdot z\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t.\end{split}

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
8
, we derive (
4.6
) and we pass to the limit
\tau\to 0
.
The limit is done via a similar (but simpler) argument using the bound
0\leq u\leq M
and the continuity of
\beta,\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}
. In particular, since
u
and
u^{0}
take values between
0
and
M
, the same holds for
\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma}
, and the continuity of
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
implies that
\beta(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})
and
\beta^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau}*f^{\sigma})
are bounded. This is enough to apply the dominated convergence theorem to (
4.6
) and pass to the limit
\tau\to 0
. We, then, proceed as in the proof of Lemma
8
and use the same idea for the limit
\sigma\to 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
a weak solution satisfying (i)-(iv) of Definition
1
. For
\mathbf{c}
to be a renormalized solution, it has to satisfy (i)-(iv) of Definition
2
. Conditions (i), (ii) are clear and imply
c_{i}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
and
c_{i}u_{i}\in L^{2}(0,T;(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))^{3})
. Moreover,
c_{i}\in C([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))
(see (
3.2
)). This allows for a direct application of Lemma
9
with
u=c_{i}
and
z=c_{i}u_{i}
, which implies that
\mathbf{c}
satisfies (
3.3
) (notice that
2c_{i}\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\cdot\sqrt{c_{i}}u_{i}=\nabla c_{i}\cdot c_{i}u_{i}
). Finally, since
\beta\in C^{2}
, we have
\beta
is Lipschitz continuous, hence (iv) of Definition
2
follows due to condition (iv) of Definition
1
. Hence,
\mathbf{c}
is a renormalized solution.

\square

5.
Derivation of a symmetrized relative entropy identity

Let
c_{i},\bar{c}_{i}
be two renormalized solutions and we proceed to establish Theorem
4
. By the weak formulation for the difference
A(x,t):=c_{i}(x,t)-\bar{c}_{i}(x,t)
, corresponding to
\beta=\textnormal{id}
, we have

(5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,0)\phi(x,0)\textnormal{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}[A(x,t)\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)+C(x,t)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)]\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t=0,

where
C(x,t):=c_{i}u_{i}(x,t)-\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}(x,t)
and
\phi(x,t)\in C^{1}_{c}(Q_{\infty})
a test function compactly supported in

Q_{\infty}=\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,\infty)
.
Likewise, write the weak formulation for the difference
B(y,\tau):=\beta(c_{i}(y,\tau))-\beta(\bar{c}_{i}(y,\tau))
, for a generic
\beta
, namely

(5.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}B(y,0)\phi(y,0)\textnormal{d}y&+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}B(y,\tau)\partial_{\tau}\phi(y,\tau)+D(y,\tau)\cdot\nabla_{y}\phi(y,\tau)]\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau\\
&\phantom{xxx}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}E(y,\tau)\phi(y,\tau)\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau=0,\end{split}

where
\phi(y,\tau)\in C^{1}_{c}(Q_{\infty})
,

\displaystyle D(y,\tau)

\displaystyle=\beta^{\prime}(c_{i}(y,\tau))c_{i}u_{i}(y,\tau)-\beta^{\prime}(\bar{c}_{i}(y,\tau))\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}(y,\tau)

\displaystyle E(y,\tau)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{c_{i}}\beta^{\prime\prime}(c_{i})\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\cdot c_{i}u_{i}-2\sqrt{\bar{c}_{i}}\beta^{\prime\prime}(\bar{c}_{i})\nabla\sqrt{\bar{c}_{i}}\cdot\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}.

Let
\varphi(z,s)\in C^{1}_{c}(Q_{\infty})
, that is
\phi
is periodic in space and compactly supported on
{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\times[0,\infty)
.
Let
\rho(\sigma)\geq 0
be a symmetric mollifier, supported in the ball centered at
0
with radius
1
, with
\int\rho(\sigma)d\sigma=1
.
Define

(5.3)

\begin{split}{\Phi}(x,t;y,\tau)&=\varphi\left(\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{t+\tau}{2}\right)\;\frac{1}{\epsilon}\rho\left(\frac{t-\tau}{\epsilon}\right)\prod_{\alpha=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\rho\left(\frac{x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}}{\epsilon}\right)\\
&=:\varphi\left(\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{t+\tau}{2}\right)\;\phi^{\epsilon}(x,y,t,\tau)\end{split}

and note that
\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}(Q_{\infty}\times Q_{\infty})
and if
z
and
s
denote the first and second argument of
\varphi
respectively, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y}){\Phi}=(\nabla_{z}\varphi)\Big(\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{t+\tau}{2}\Big)\;\phi^{\epsilon}

(5.5)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\partial_{\tau}){\Phi}=(\partial_{s}\varphi)\Big(\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{t+\tau}{2}\Big)\;\phi^{\epsilon}.

\Phi
is jointly periodic in space: if
p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a period then
\Phi(x+p,t;y+p,\tau)=\Phi(x,t;y,\tau)
.

Now, in (
5.1
) we employ the test function
\phi_{B}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}B(y,\tau)\Phi(x,t;y,\tau)\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau

which is in
C_{c}^{1}(Q_{\infty})
, since

\displaystyle\phi_{B}(x+p,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}B(y,\tau)\Phi(x+p,t;y,\tau)\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}-p}B(y+p,\tau)\Phi(x+p,t;y+p,\tau)\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}B(y,\tau)\Phi(x,t;y,\tau)\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\phi_{B}(x,t).

The resulting
identity is integrated over
{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\times[0,\infty)
and gives, using (
5.4
),

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,t)B(y,\tau)(\partial_{t}\Phi)+C(x,t)B(y,\tau)\cdot\Big(\nabla_{z}\varphi\big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\tfrac{t+\tau}{2}\big)\phi^{\epsilon}-\nabla_{y}{\Phi}\Big)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau\\
&\qquad=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,0)B(y,\tau)\Phi(x,0;y,\tau)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau\,.\\
\end{split}

Since
B(y,\tau)=\beta(c_{i}(y,\tau))-\beta(\bar{c}_{i}(y,\tau))\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{3}})
for
\tau
fixed, we can integrate by parts in
y
and use the periodic boundary conditions
to get

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,0)B(y,\tau)\varphi\big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\tfrac{\tau}{2}\big)\phi^{\epsilon}(x,y,0,\tau)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,t)B(y,\tau)\partial_{t}\big(\Phi(x,t;y,\tau)\big)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau\\
&-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left[C(x,t)B(y,\tau)\cdot(\nabla_{z}\varphi)\big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\tfrac{t+\tau}{2}\big)\phi^{\epsilon}+C(x,t)\cdot\nabla_{y}B(y,\tau){\Phi}\right]\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau.\end{split}

Similarly, we choose the test function
\phi_{A}(y,\tau)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,t)\Phi(x,t;y,\tau)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t
in (
5.2
), which is
C_{c}^{1}(Q_{\infty})

and follow similar steps to arrive at

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,t)B(y,0)\varphi\big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\tfrac{\tau}{2}\big)\phi^{\epsilon}(x,y,t,0)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}A(x,t)B(y,\tau)\partial_{\tau}\big(\Phi(x,t;y,\tau)\big)+A(x,t)E(y,\tau){\Phi}\;\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau\\
&-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left[A(x,t)D(y,\tau)\cdot(\nabla_{z}\varphi)\big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\tfrac{t+\tau}{2}\big)\phi^{\epsilon}+D(y,\tau)\cdot\nabla_{x}A(x,t){\Phi}\right]\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau.\end{split}

Adding the two equations and using (
5.5
), we get

Lemma 10
.

Let
f(x,t)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times(0,\infty))
. Then

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(y,\tau)\varphi\left(\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{t+\tau}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\epsilon}\rho\left(\frac{t-\tau}{\epsilon}\right)\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\tau\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\phantom{xxxxxxx}\to\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times[0,\infty)),\end{split}

where we denote

\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}\rho\left(\frac{x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}}{\epsilon}\right).

Proof.

Extend
f
by setting

\bar{f}(x,t)=\begin{cases}f(x,t)&t>0\\
0&\textnormal{else}\end{cases}

and set
X=(x,t)
and
Y=(y,\tau)
. Then, it is enough to show that for all

Indeed, using the change of variables
Y=X+\epsilon Z
,

\begin{split}I&=\left|\iint\bar{f}(Y)\varphi\left(\frac{X+Y}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d+1}}\rho\left(\frac{X-Y}{\epsilon}\right)\textnormal{d}Y\textnormal{d}X-\int\bar{f}(X)\varphi(X)\textnormal{d}X\right|\\
&=\left|\iint\left(\bar{f}(X+\epsilon Z)\varphi\left(X+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon Y\right)-\bar{f}(X)\varphi(X)\right)\rho\left(Z\right)\textnormal{d}Z\textnormal{d}X\right|\\
&\leq\iint|\bar{f}(X+\epsilon Z)-\bar{f}(X)|\left|\varphi\left(X+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon Z\right)\right|\rho(Z)\textnormal{d}Z\textnormal{d}X\\
&\phantom{xxxx}+\iint\left|\varphi\left(X+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon Z\right)-\varphi(X)\right||\bar{f}(X)|\rho(Z)\textnormal{d}Z\textnormal{d}X,\end{split}

where both terms tend to zero by the dominated convergence theorem since

∎

Lemma 11

Let

Proof.

We compare the difference between

&\phantom{xxx}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\frac{\bar{d}_{i}}{D_{ij}}(d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j})(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot(v_{i}-v_{j})\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\phantom{xxx}+\delta~\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}\Big)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\phantom{xxx}-\delta~\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot\left(\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}v_{j}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\bar{v}_{j}\right)\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\end{split}

and we aim to control the terms on the right-hand side by the relative entropy and the dissipation on the left-hand side.

7.1.
Estimation of the dissipation

Following the ideas of
[
12
]
, we use the symmetry of
D_{ij}
to rewrite the dissipation terms as follows:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}&\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{2D_{ij}}|(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})-(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j})|^{2}=\phantom{x}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{D_{ij}}((v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})-(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}))\cdot(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\\
&=\phantom{x}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{D_{ij}}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}-\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{D_{ij}}(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j})\cdot(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\right]\\
&=\phantom{x}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{j}}{D_{ij}}\sqrt{d_{i}}(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot\sqrt{d_{i}}(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})-\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}d_{j}}}{D_{ij}}\sqrt{d_{i}}(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot\sqrt{d_{j}}(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j})\right]\\
&=\phantom{x}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\mathbf{d})\sqrt{d_{i}}(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot\sqrt{d_{j}}(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}).\end{split}

where
A_{ij}(d)
is the matrix in (
6.6
).
Let
\bm{Y}=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
, where
Y_{i}=\sqrt{d_{i}}(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})
. By (
6.10
),

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\mathbf{d})\sqrt{d_{i}}(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot\sqrt{d_{j}}(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j})\geq(1+n\delta)\mu|\mathbb{P}_{L}(\mathbf{d})\bm{Y}|^{2}\geq\mu|\mathbb{P}_{L}(\mathbf{d})\bm{Y}|^{2},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}|\mathbb{P}_{L}(\mathbf{d})\bm{Y}|^{2}&=|\bm{Y}|^{2}-|\mathbb{P}_{L^{\perp}}(\mathbf{d})\bm{Y}|^{2}\end{split}

with

|\bm{Y}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}

and

\begin{split}(\mathbb{P}_{L^{\perp}}(\mathbf{d})\bm{Y})_{i}&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}d_{j}}}{1+n\delta}\sqrt{d_{j}}(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j})=\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}}}{1+n\delta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{j}v_{j}-d_{j}\bar{v}_{j})\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}}}{1+n\delta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bar{d}_{j}\bar{v}_{j}-d_{j}\bar{v}_{j})=\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}}}{1+n\delta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bar{d}_{j}-d_{j})\bar{v}_{j}\end{split}

where we have used the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}v_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{d}_{i}\bar{v}_{i}=0
.

Then, by (
6.5
),

\begin{split}|\mathbb{P}_{L^{\perp}}(\mathbf{d})Y|^{2}&=\frac{1}{1+n\delta}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bar{d}_{j}-d_{j})\bar{v}_{j}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{n}{1+n\delta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}|\bar{v}_{j}|^{2}\\
&\leq n\|\bar{v}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}\leq\frac{n}{\delta^{2}}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{2D_{ij}}|(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})-(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j})|^{2}\geq\mu\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}-\frac{\mu n}{\delta^{2}}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}

and similarly

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\bar{d}_{i}\bar{d}_{j}}{2D_{ij}}|(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})-(v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j})|^{2}\geq\mu\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{d}_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}-\frac{\mu n}{\delta^{2}}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}.

Putting everything together, we find:

(7.3)

{\begin{split}&F(\mathbf{d},\bar{\mathbf{d}})\Big|_{t=T}+\mu\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\leq\frac{2n\mu}{\delta^{2}}\|cu\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\phantom{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\underbrace{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{i}}{D_{ij}}(d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j})(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot(\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{v}_{j})}_{J_{1}}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\phantom{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\underbrace{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\bar{d}_{i}}{D_{ij}}(d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j})(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot(v_{i}-v_{j})}_{J_{2}}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\phantom{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\underbrace{\delta~\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}\Big)(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}}_{J_{3}}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\\
&\phantom{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\underbrace{-\delta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})(v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i})\cdot\left(\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}v_{j}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\bar{v}_{j}\right)}_{J_{4}}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\end{split}}

and we need to estimate the error terms
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}
and
J_{4}
.

7.2.
Estimation of the error terms

We start with
J_{3}
. Setting
M=\max\limits_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{D_{ij}}
, we get

(7.4)

J_{3}\leq n\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}.

Regarding the terms
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
, we have:

\begin{split}J_{1}&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{i}}{D_{ij}}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}||v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}||\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{v}_{j}|\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{d_{i}}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}}}{D_{ij}}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}||\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{v}_{j}|\end{split}

and using Young’s and Jensen’s inequality

\begin{split}J_{1}&\leq\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}}}{D_{ij}}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}||\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{v}_{j}|\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{8n^{2}M^{2}}{\mu}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2},\end{split}

where
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Finally, by (
6.5
):

\begin{split}J_{1}&\leq\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{8n^{2}M^{2}}{\mu\delta^{2}}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}\end{split}

and similarly,

J_{2}\leq\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{d}_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{8n^{2}M^{2}}{\mu\delta^{2}}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}.

Hence, we see that

(7.5)

J_{1}+J_{2}\leq\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{C_{1}}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}

and
C_{1}>0
depends on
n
,
\mu
,
M
, and
\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}
, but not on
\delta
.

For
J_{4}
, we first split it into two parts

\begin{split}J_{4}&\leq\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}v_{j}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\bar{v}_{j}\right|\\
&\leq\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}d_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\big(\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}v_{j}-\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}\bar{v}_{j}\right|+\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}\bar{v}_{j}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\bar{v}_{j}\right|\big)\\
&\phantom{xx}+\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\bar{d}_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\big(\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}v_{j}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}v_{j}\right|+\left|\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}v_{j}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\bar{v}_{j}\right|\big)\\
&=\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}d_{j}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}\right|+\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}d_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}||\bar{v}_{j}|\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\right|\\
&\phantom{xx}+\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\bar{d}_{i}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}||v_{j}|\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\right|+\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\bar{d}_{j}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}\right|\\
&=\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}(d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}\right|\\
&\phantom{xx}+\delta M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}(d_{i}|\bar{v}_{j}|+\bar{d}_{i}|v_{j}|)|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\right|\\
&=:J_{4}^{1}+J_{4}^{2}.\end{split}

The first term is handled by Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle J_{4}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sqrt{d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j}}|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}|\left(\delta M\sqrt{d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j})\left|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}\right|^{2}+\frac{4n\delta^{2}M^{2}}{\mu}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\right)^{2}

then Jensen’s inequality in the second term:

J_{4}^{1}\leq\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j})\left|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}\right|^{2}+\frac{4n^{2}\delta^{2}M^{2}}{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}

and finally we multiply and divide by
d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i}
:

\displaystyle J_{4}^{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j})\left|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}\right|^{2}+\frac{4n^{2}\delta^{2}M^{2}}{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}\frac{1}{d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j})\left|v_{j}-\bar{v}_{j}\right|^{2}+\frac{2n^{2}\delta M^{2}}{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}

where in the last inequality we used that
d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i}\geq 2\delta
.

The second term is estimated in a similar way:

\begin{split}J_{4}^{2}&\leq\delta M\max\{\|\bar{v}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|v_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\right|\\
&\overset{\eqref{estimation:v}}{\leq}M\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\right|\end{split}

and using triangle inequality

\begin{split}J_{4}^{2}&\leq M\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\Big(\left|\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}}-\frac{d_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\right|+\left|\frac{d_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}-\frac{\bar{d}_{j}}{\bar{d}_{i}}\right|\Big)\\
&=M\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\Big(\frac{d_{j}}{d_{i}\bar{d}_{i}}\left|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}\right|+\frac{1}{\bar{d}_{i}}\left|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}\right|\Big)\\
&\leq M\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\Big(\frac{2}{\delta^{2}}\left|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}\right|+\frac{1}{\delta}\left|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}\right|\Big)\end{split}

because
\delta\leq d_{i},\bar{d}_{i}\leq 2
and thus
\frac{1}{d_{i}\bar{d}_{i}}=\frac{1}{(c_{i}+\delta)(\bar{c}_{i}+\delta)}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}
. Then,

\begin{split}J_{4}^{2}&=\frac{2M\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}\right|\\
&\phantom{xx}+\frac{M\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}\right|=:J_{4}^{2,1}+J_{4}^{2,2}\,.\end{split}

Using Young’s inequality the first term gives

\begin{split}J_{4}^{2,1}&=\frac{2nM\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\left|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}\right|\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i}}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\frac{2nM\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\delta^{2}}\sqrt{d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|\\
&\leq\frac{\mu}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{16n^{2}M^{2}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{\mu\delta^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\,.\end{split}

Similarly, Young’s and Jensen’s inequalities give

\begin{split}J_{4}^{2,2}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i}}|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|\frac{M\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\delta}\sqrt{d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|\\
&\leq\frac{\mu}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{2M^{2}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{\mu\delta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\mu}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{8M^{2}n^{2}\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{\mu\delta^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}.\end{split}

Summarizing,

(7.6)

J_{4}\leq\left(\frac{\mu}{2}+C_{2}\delta\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{C_{3}}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|d_{j}-\bar{d}_{j}|^{2}

where
C_{2}>0
depends only on
n,\mu
and
M
and
C_{3}>0
on
n,\mu,M
and
\|cu\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
and neither of them depends on
\delta
.

Finally, putting together (
7.3
), (
7.4
), (
7.5
) and (
7.6
), we obtain the differential inequality

F(\mathbf{d},\bar{\mathbf{d}})\Big|_{t=T}+\left(\frac{\mu}{4}-C_{4}\delta\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i})|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\leq\frac{C_{5}}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t

where
C_{4}
and
C_{5}
are positive constants independent of
\delta
.

Next, by selecting
0<\delta<\min\{1,\frac{\mu}{4C_{4}}\}
, we obtain

(7.7)

F(\mathbf{d},\bar{\mathbf{d}})\Big|_{t=T}\leq\frac{C_{5}}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t,

where

F(\mathbf{d},\bar{\mathbf{d}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\ln d_{i}-\ln\bar{d}_{i})(d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i})\textnormal{d}x.

By Taylor’s theorem,

(\ln d_{i}-\ln\bar{d}_{i})(d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i})=\frac{1}{\xi}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2},

for some
\xi\in(\min\{d_{i},\bar{d}_{i}\},\max\{d_{i},\bar{d}_{i}\})\subset(\delta,1+\delta)
, i.e.

(7.8)

(\ln d_{i}-\ln\bar{d}_{i})(d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i})\geq\frac{1}{1+\delta}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}.

Therefore, (
7.7
) and (
7.8
) imply that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\Big|_{t=T}\leq C_{5}\frac{1+\delta}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t

and a direct application of Grönwall’s Lemma gives

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\ \textnormal{d}x\Big|_{t=T}\leq 0

i.e.
d_{i}=\bar{d}_{i}
and thus
c_{i}=\bar{c}_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
. This completes the proof.

8.
Discussion of the hypotheses for uniqueness

We discuss here the role of the assumption
c_{i}u_{i}\in L^{\infty}
that is necessary for the uniqueness result. Refering to (
7.3
), note that the terms
J_{1},J_{2}
and
J_{4}
need to be estimated in such a way that they produce a term that is dominated by the integral of the symmetrized relative entropy and a term that is absorbed by the dissipation on the left-hand side. The former term should be a multiple of
\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i}|^{2}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t
, as (
7.8
) suggests, while the latter should be a multiple of the dissipation. To do so, we need to apply Young’s inequality in each of
J_{1},J_{2},J_{4}
and this requires the differences
v_{i}-v_{j}
and
\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{v}_{j}
to be bounded (which follows if
c_{i}u_{i}
and
\bar{c}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}
are in
L^{\infty}_{x,t}
). In this sense, the assumption
c_{i}u_{i}\in L^{\infty}
is necessary to exploit the full capacity of the relative entropy identity.

The assumption
c_{i}u_{i}\in L^{\infty}_{x,t}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, is equivalent to
\nabla c_{i}\in L^{\infty}_{x,t}
,
i=1,\dots,n
. One direction of this implication
is immediate from (
1.2
) and the fact that
0\leq c_{i}\leq 1
.
For the converse, assume that
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}
are nonzero and
c_{m+1},\dots,c_{n}
are zero (for this we might need to relabel them). By inverting the Maxwell-Stefan system (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) (see
[
8
, Prop. 9, 11]
)
c_{i}u_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{ik}\nabla c_{k}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
c_{i}u_{i}=-B_{i}\nabla c_{i}
for
i\in\{m+1,\dots,n\}
, where
A_{ik}
and
B_{i}
are possibly zero but well-defined coefficients that depend on
D_{ij}
and the positive mass fractions.

We recall that the regularity offered by the existence theory of
[
14
]
is
\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\in L^{2}_{x,t}
, which implies
\nabla c_{i}\in L^{2}_{x,t}
due to
c_{i}
being bounded and is less than what is required for uniqueness.

CLT for β \beta -ensembles at high-temperature, and for integrable systems: a transfer operator approach.
G. Mazzuca, R. Memin

Abstract
In this paper, we prove a polynomial Central Limit Theorem for several integrable models, and for the β \beta -ensembles at high-temperature with polynomial potential. Furthermore, we connect the mean values, the variances and the correlations of the moments of the Lax matrices of these integrable systems with the ones of the β \beta -ensembles. Moreover, we show that the local functions’ space-correlations decay exponentially fast for the considered integrable systems. For these models, we also established a Berry–Esseen type bound.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we establish a Central Limit Theorem for eigenvalue fluctuations of the Lax matrices of several integrable models, and for the classical
\beta
-ensembles of random matrix theory in the so-called high-temperature regime. Motivated by the recent observation that both topics are closely related, we connect, for the considered integrable systems, the limiting variance in the CLT to the one of the associated
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime, by establishing a relation between their free energies. Furthermore, we establish exponential decay of space correlation for the considered integrable models and a Berry–Esseen type bound.

We start by describing the case of the Toda chain, which will serve both as a motivation for the results and as a way to introduce the key objects of the present work.

1.1
The Toda chain: an introductory case

The classical Toda chain (or Toda lattice)
[
68
]
is the dynamical system described by the following Hamiltonian:

H_{T}({\bf p},{\bf q}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{p_{j}^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{T}(q_{j+1}-q_{j})\ ,\quad V_{T}(x)=e^{-x}+x-1\,,

(1.1)

with periodic boundary conditions
\,q_{j+N}=q_{j}+\Omega\quad\forall\,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\Omega>0
is fixed. Its equations of motion take the form

\dot{q}_{j}=\dfrac{\partial H_{T}}{\partial p_{j}}=p_{j},\quad\dot{p}_{j}=-\dfrac{\partial H_{T}}{\partial q_{j}}=V_{T}^{\prime}(q_{j+1}-q_{j})-V_{T}^{\prime}(q_{j}-q_{j-1}),\;\;j=1,\dots,N\,.

(1.2)

It is well known that the Toda chain is an integrable system
[
68
,
39
]
. One way to prove it is to put the Toda equations in Lax pair form. This was obtained by Flaschka
[
26
]
, and Manakov
[
50
]
through the following
non-canonical
change of coordinates:

a_{j}:=-p_{j}\,,\qquad b_{j}:=e^{\frac{1}{2}(q_{j}-q_{j+1})}\equiv e^{-\frac{1}{2}r_{j}},\qquad 1\leq j\leq N\,,

(1.3)

where
r_{j}=q_{j+1}-q_{j}
is the relative distance between particles
j
and
j+1
.

Defining the Lax operator
L
as the periodic Jacobi matrix

[
70
]

L:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{1}&b_{1}&0&\ldots&b_{N}\\
b_{1}&a_{2}&b_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&b_{2}&a_{3}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&b_{N-1}\\
b_{N}&\ldots&0&b_{N-1}&a_{N}\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

(1.4)

and the antisymmetric matrix
B

B:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&b_{1}&0&\ldots&-b_{N}\\
-b_{1}&0&b_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&-b_{2}&0&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&b_{N-1}\\
b_{N}&\ldots&0&-b_{N-1}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

(1.5)

a straightforward calculation shows that the equations of motions (
1.2
) are equivalent to

\dot{L}=\left[B;L\right]\,,

(1.6)

where
[B;L]=BL-LB
is the commutator of two matrices.
This form implies that the eigenvalues of
L
are conserved, and therefore
\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{k}\right)
,
k=1,\ldots,N
are constants of motions for the Toda lattice, so the system is integrable
[
8
]
. We call these quantities
conserved fields
. Also, note that the sum of the streches
\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}=\Omega

is conserved along the dynamic.

Let
\alpha>0
and
V:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Lebesgue measurable function such that for some
C\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\frac{V(x)}{x^{2}}>0
and
V(x)>C
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
We introduce, after
[
64
]
, the probability measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}

{\rm d}\mu_{N,\text{Toda}}^{\alpha,V}({\bf a},{\bf b}):=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{\text{Toda}}(\alpha,V)}\ \prod_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}^{2\alpha-1}\mathbbm{1}_{b_{j}>0}e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(L)\right)}{\rm d}{\bf a}\,{\rm d}{\bf b}\,,

(1.7)

where
L
is a periodic tridiagonal matrix of the form (
1.4
) and

\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(L)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}V(\lambda_{j}(L))\,,

denoting by
\lambda_{j}(L)
,
1\leq j\leq N
the eigenvalues of
L
.

The measure
\mu_{N,\text{Toda}}^{\alpha,V}
is called
Generalized Gibbs Ensemble
(GGE) of the Toda chain with pressure parameter
\alpha>0
and potential
V
. Because both quantities
\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(L)\right)
and

\prod_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}\right\}

are constants of motion,
\mu_{N,T}^{\alpha,V}
is invariant under the Toda flow, this is a consequence of a more general result, see
[
37
,
33
]
.

Note that the conditions on
V(x)
are enough to ensure that the normalizing constant
Z^{\text{Toda}}_{N}(\alpha,V)
is finite. It is called the
partition function
of the system, and it reads

Z^{\text{Toda}}_{N}(\alpha,V)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}^{2\alpha-1}\mathbbm{1}_{b_{j}>0}e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(L)\right)}{\rm d}{\bf a}\,{\rm d}{\bf b}\,.

In the framework of generalized hydrodynamics, it is expected that for generic initial data, the long-time distribution of the Toda chain converges towards a GGE whose pressure
\alpha
and potential
V
are prescribed by the initial condition. Formally, it is expected that for sufficiently regular observables
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
we have as
t\to+\infty

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f({\bf a}(s)),{\bf b}(s)){\rm d}s\to{\mathbb{E}}_{N}^{\alpha,V}[f({\bf a},{\bf b})]\,,

where the expectation is taken under
\mu^{\alpha,V}_{N,T}
, and where
\alpha
,
V
depend on
({\bf a}(0),{\bf b}(0))
. From there, understanding the GGE with arbitrary potential and pressure parameter is key to understanding the Toda lattice’s long-time behaviour.

Under
\mu^{\alpha,V}_{N,\text{Toda}}
,
L
is a random matrix whose law is invariant under the Toda flow. It turns out, as was observed by Spohn in
[
64
]
, that its spectral properties can be linked with the ones of the real
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime.

Fix
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\beta>0
and
V:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
Lebesgue measurable such that for some
\delta>0
,
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}

V(x)\geq(N\beta+1+\delta)\log|x|+c\,.

The real
\beta
-ensemble of size
N
with potential
V
is the probability measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
given by

d\mathbf{P}^{\beta,V}_{N}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})=\frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}^{\beta,V}_{N}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}V(\lambda_{j})}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\,.

(1.8)

When
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
and
V
is quadratic, this measure is the joint law of the (unordered) spectrum of respectively the GOE, GUE and GSE (see
[
6
, Section 2.5]
for example), giving matrix representation for the
\beta
-ensemble in those specific cases. In
[
19
]
, Dumitriu and Edelman gave a tridiagonal matrix model for the
\beta
-ensemble of size
N
with quadratic potential for
any choice of

\boldsymbol{\beta>0}
:

Let
T
be the following random tridiagonal matrix with independent coefficients (up to the symmetry):

•

The diagonal entries
T_{i,i}
are standard Gaussians

•

For
1\leq i\leq N-1
, the off-diagonal entry
T_{i,i+1}=T_{i+1,i}
is distributed as
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\chi_{\beta(N-i)}
,

Where for
a>0
, the
\chi_{a}
law is given by the density

f_{a}(x)=\frac{2^{1-a/2}}{\Gamma(a/2)}x^{a-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,.

Then,
[
19
, Theorem 2.12]
asserts that the distribution of the eigenvalues of
T
is given by the
\beta
-ensemble of size
N
with potential
V(x)=x^{2}/2
.

With this last point point in mind, let

T:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{1}&b_{1}&0&\ldots&0\\
b_{1}&a_{2}&b_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&b_{2}&a_{3}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&b_{N-1}\\
0&\ldots&0&b_{N-1}&a_{N}\\
\end{array}\right)\,

(1.9)

be the (nonperiodic) tridiagonal matrix whose entries are distributed according to the measure

{\rm d}\mu^{\beta,V}_{N,\text{beta}}=\frac{1}{Z^{\text{beta}}_{N}(\beta,V)}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}b_{j}^{\beta(N-j)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{b_{j}>0}e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(T)\right)}{\rm d}{\bf a}{\rm d}{\bf b}\,,

(1.10)

with
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})
and
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{N-1})
. Then, the eigenvalues of
T
are distributed according to the
\beta
-ensemble (
1.8
) with potential
V
, the case
V=\frac{x^{2}}{2}
being the case where
T
has independent entries described above.

The parameter
\beta
is allowed to be
N
-dependent and can be interpreted as an inverse temperature. In this paper, we call the
high-temperature regime
the framework where
\beta=\frac{2\alpha}{N}
for some fixed
\alpha>0
. We then define the high temperature version of
\mu^{\beta,V}_{N,\text{beta}}
as

{\rm d}\mu^{\alpha,V}_{N,\text{HT}}:={\rm d}\mu^{2\alpha/N,V}_{N,\text{beta}}=\frac{1}{Z^{\text{HT}}_{N}(\alpha,V)}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}b_{j}^{2\alpha\frac{N-j}{N}-1}\mathbbm{1}_{b_{j}>0}e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(T)\right)}{\rm d}{\bf a}{\rm d}{\bf b}\,.

(1.11)

We can now sketch the link between the Toda chain distributed according to a GGE and the high temperature real
\beta
-ensemble. Consider the GGE (
1.7
) with quadratic potential
V(x)=x^{2}/2
. Then, under
\mu^{\alpha,x^{2}/2}_{N,\text{Toda}}
, the entries of
L
become independent (up to the symmetry) and their law is given by

•

the diagonal entries
L_{i,i}
are standard Gaussian

•

the off diagonal entries
L_{i,i+1}=L_{i+1,i}
,
1\leq i\leq N
(where the indices are taken modulo
N
) are distributed as
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\chi_{2\alpha}
.

With the choice
\beta=2\alpha/N
in (
1.8
), the entries near the top left corner of Dumitriu-Edelman representation (
i.e.
the matrix
T
distributed according to
\mu^{\alpha,x^{2}/2}_{N,\text{HT}}
) resemble the ones of the top left corner of
L
when
({\bf a},{\bf b})
is distributed according to
\mu^{\alpha,x^{2}/2}_{N,\text{Toda}}
. One can then hope to pull this link between both models for more general choices of
V
. As already mentioned, this fundamental remark was made by Spohn in
[
64
]
where the author was able to derive the limit of the empirical measure of eigenvalues of the Toda Lax matrix
L
under the GGE in terms of the limiting measure of the
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime, both with polynomial potential
V
. Namely, denoting the empirical measure of eigenvalues of any
N\times N
complex matrix
A

\nu_{N}(A)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{j}(A)},

(1.12)

Spohn argued that with
({\bf a},{\bf b})
distributed according to
\mu^{\alpha,V}_{N,\text{Toda}}
, the empirical measure
\nu_{N}(L)
converges almost surely as
N\to\infty
towards some deterministic measure
\nu^{V}_{\alpha}
, which is given in terms of the limiting empirical measure
\mu^{V}_{\alpha}
of the high temperature
\beta
-ensemble,
\beta=\frac{2\alpha}{N}
, by

\nu^{V}_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}\left(\alpha\mu^{V}_{\alpha}\right)\,,

(1.13)

where the last equality is understood in the weak sense, i.e. tested against bounded continuous functions. This was proved for the quadratic potential in
[
51
]
and generalized to potentials with polynomial growth in
[
36
]
, where the authors established large deviations for the empirical measure of the Toda Lax matrix and related the associated rate function with the one of the
\beta
-ensemble at high temperature.

In the present paper, as an application of Theorem
1.5
, we push the comparison between both models further by linking the
fluctuations
of the empirical measure of the Toda chain to the ones of the high temperature
\beta
-ensemble.

Namely, we show that taking a
polynomial potential

V
, considering

case 1:

the periodic tridiagonal matrix
L
distributed according to
\mu_{N,\text{Toda}}^{\alpha,V}
, eq. (
1.7
)

case 2:

the tridiagonal matrix
T
distributed according to
\mu^{\alpha,V}_{N,\text{HT}}
, eq. (
1.11
),

we have for some
\sigma^{2}_{\text{Toda}}(\alpha,V,g)>0
and
\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(\alpha,V,g)>0
the following convergences in distribution:

in case 1
,

\sqrt{N}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}g{\rm d}\nu_{N}(L)-\lim_{N\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}g{\rm d}\nu_{N}(L)\right)\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\text{Toda}}(\alpha,V,g))

where
\nu_{N}(L)
(
1.12
) is the empirical measure of
L
.

in case 2
,

\sqrt{N}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}g{\rm d}\nu_{N}(T)-\lim_{N\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}g{\rm d}\nu_{N}(T)\right)\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(\alpha,V,g))

where
\nu_{N}(T)
(
1.12
) is the empirical measure of
T
.

Furthermore, we establish that both limiting variances are linked through

\sigma^{2}_{\text{Toda}}(\alpha,V,g)=\partial_{\alpha}\left(\alpha\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(\alpha,V,g)\right)\,,

(1.14)

which is the analogue of
1.13
at the level of fluctuations of the empirical measures of eigenvalues.

This result is particularly relevant in the context of the so-called
Generalized Hydrodynamics
, a recent physical theory that allows computing the correlation functions for classical integrable models, for an introduction to the subject see
[
18
,
66
]
. According to this theory, one of the main ingredients to compute the correlation functions for the integrable model at hand is to be able to calculate the correlation functions for the conserved fields at time
0
. We are able to access these quantities as a consequence of Theorem
1.5
. We show how to do it in Section
2
. We also mention the recent work
[
52
]
, where the authors made molecular dynamics simulations of the correlation functions of the Toda lattice, and they compared them with the predictions of linear Generalized Hydrodynamics, showing an astonishing agreement.

The relation between the Toda chain and the high-temperature regime of the real
\beta
-ensemble is not a unique case — it is an instance of a broader picture linking two types of models:

Type 1)

discrete space, continuous time integrable system with nearest neighbour interaction endowed with some GGE

Type 2)

high temperature
\beta
-ensemble on some given curve of the complex plane.

Indeed, an analogue result to the one of Spohn for the Toda lattice holds true also for several other pairs integrable system/

-ensemble at high-temperature matrix model as it was described in
[

66
,

.

The periodic matrices (Type 1) that we consider are the

Statement of the results.

We come to precise statements of the main results of the present paper. To do that, we introduce the concept of

Definition 1.1

Let

where

N
for

a
seed
of

,
\widetilde{y}
a
weed
of
Y
, and

the

of

. When we are not interested in the specific circular index of the function

Remark 1.2
.

We notice that

•

The seed of a function is not unique, indeed

-circular function

is a seed of

2.1
). We notice that this implies that we can compute the susceptibility matrix of the Toda lattice (
1.44
) in terms of the derivative of the Free energy.

2.1.1
The Toda chain’s currents

Since the conserved fields are local quantities, they must satisfy a local conservation law. Following the notation of
[
67
]
, we define

Q^{[n],N}_{j}=L^{n}_{j,j}\,,

(2.11)

where
L\in\textrm{Mat}(N,{\mathbb{R}})
is (
1.4
). We can easily compute the evolution equation for such quantities as

\frac{d}{dt}Q^{[n],N}_{j}=(BL^{n}-L^{n}B)=b_{j-1}L^{n}_{j,j-1}-b_{j}L^{n}_{j+1,j}\,.

(2.12)

Defining
J^{[n],N}_{j}=b_{j-1}L^{n}_{j,j-1}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}Q^{[n],N}_{j}=J^{[n],N}_{j}-J^{[n],N}_{j+1}

and we say that
J^{[n],N}_{j}
is the current of the local conserved field
Q^{[n],N}_{j}
. In particular, defining the matrix
L^{\downarrow}
as

L^{\downarrow}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}L_{i,j}\quad\text{if}\;j<i\;\text{or}\;i=1,j=N\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,

(2.13)

we can recast the previous definition as

J^{[n],N}_{j}=(L^{n}L^{\downarrow})_{j,j}\,.

(2.14)

We notice that both
Q^{[n],N}_{j}
and
J^{[n],N}_{j}
depend on time, and we adopt the convention that if not explicitly written the evaluation is at time
0
. Furthermore, we define

Q^{[n],N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q^{[n],N}_{j}\,,\qquad J^{[n],N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}J^{[n],N}_{j}\,,

(2.15)

and we refer to
Q^{[n],N}
as the
n^{th}
-conserved field, and to
J^{[n],N}
as the
n^{th}
-total current.

The evaluation of the expected values of both the currents
J^{[n],N}_{j}
and the total current
J^{[n],N}
according to the Generalized Gibbs ensemble (
2.1
) is one of the crucial steps to apply the theory of Generalized Hydrodynamics to the Toda lattice, as it is explained in
[
67
]
. In this manuscript, the author used some heuristic arguments to explicitly derive the expression for these quantities, here we rigorously justify his argument applying Theorem
1.11
.

First, we extend the definition of
Q_{j}^{[n],N}
and
J_{j}^{[n],N}
for
n=0
, setting
Q_{j}^{[0],N}=r_{j}
, and
J_{j}^{[0],N}=-p_{j}=-Q_{j}^{[1],N}
. We notice that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}^{[0],N}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{j}^{[1],N}
is still a conserved field. We are now in position to show how to compute the limiting Toda average current

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[J^{[n],N}\right]\,,

(2.16)

in terms of the susceptibility matrix (
1.44
) of the Toda chain, so in particular of the derivative of the Free energy (
2.10
). Indeed, we prove the following:

Lemma 2.5
.

Consider the Lax matrix L (
1.4
) of the Toda lattice distributed according to the Generalized Gibbs Ensemble (
2.1
), and assume that
P(x)
is a polynomial of even degree with positive leading order coefficient. Then, for any fixed
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
defining the total currents
J^{[n],N}
as in (
2.15
) we have the following equality

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left[J^{[n],N}\right]=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\partial_{t_{1}}\partial_{t_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}_{T}(s,P+it_{1}x+it_{2}x^{n})_{|_{t_{1},t_{2}=0}}{\rm d}s.

(2.17)

Proof.

In view of the cyclic structure of the measure
\mu_{T}
and of the total current, we deduce that

\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[J^{[n],N}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]\,.

(2.18)

Furthermore, for any fixed
N
, we deduce, by differentiating with respect to the parameter
\alpha
, the following equality

\partial_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]=-\mathrm{Cov}\left(J^{[n],N}_{1}\ ;\ \sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}\right)=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Cov}\left(J^{[n],N}_{1}\ ;\ Q^{[0],N}_{j}\right)\,,

(2.19)

where we defined for any functions
f,g\in L^{2}(X^{N},\mu_{T})

\textrm{Cov}(f\,;\,g)=\mathbb{E}\left[fg\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[f\right]\mathbb{E}\left[g\right]\,.

(2.20)

We show now that the following limits coincide

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Cov}\left(J^{[n],N}_{1}\,;\,Q^{[0],N}_{j}\right)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Cov}\left(J^{[0],N}_{1}\,;\,Q^{[n],N}_{N-j+2}\right)\,.

(2.21)

Indeed, for any
n,m\geq 0
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

\begin{split}\mathrm{Cov}\left(J_{j+1}^{[n],N}(t)-J_{j}^{[n],N}(t)\ ;\ Q^{[m],N}_{1}(0)\right)&=-\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Cov}\left(Q^{[n],N}_{j}(t)\ ;\ Q^{[m],N}_{1}(0)\right)\\
&=-\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Cov}\left(Q^{[n],N}_{1}(0)\ ;\ Q^{[m],N}_{N-j+2}(-t)\right)\\
&=\mathrm{Cov}\left(Q^{[n],N}_{1}(0)\ ;\ J^{[m],N}_{N-j+3}(-t)-J^{[m],N}_{N-j+2}(-t)\right),\end{split}

(2.22)

where we used that
s\mapsto Q^{[n],N}_{j}(t+s)Q^{[m],N}_{1}(s)
is constant in law under the Toda dynamic, and the periodicity of the matrix
L
(
1.4
). Denoting the difference operator
\partial_{j}f(j)=f(j+1)-f(j)
, equation (
2.22
) shows that

\partial_{j}\left(\mathrm{Cov}\left(J_{j}^{[n],N}(t)\ ;\ Q^{[m],N}_{1}(0)\right)-\mathrm{Cov}\left(Q^{[n],N}_{1}(0);\ J^{[m],N}_{N-j+2}(-t)\right)\right)=0

(2.23)

Evaluating the previous expression at
t=0
, we deduce that there is some constant
c_{N}
, independent of
j
, such that

\mathrm{Cov}\left(J_{j}^{[n],N}\ ;\ Q^{[m],N}_{1}\right)-\mathrm{Cov}\left(Q^{[n],N}_{1};\ J^{[m],N}_{N-j+2}\right)=c_{N}\,.

(2.24)

Furthermore, since both
Q^{[n],N}_{j}
, and
J^{[m],N}_{j}
are local quantities, in view of Theorem
1.11
, we deduce that
\lim_{N\to\infty}Nc_{N}=0
.
So, evaluating the previous expression for
m=0
, we deduce (
2.21
). Thus, in the large
N
limit, we can recast (
2.19
) as

\lim_{N\to\infty}\partial_{\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Cov}\left(J^{[0],N}_{1}\ ;\ Q_{j}^{[n],N}\right)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Cov}\left(Q^{[1],N}_{1}\ ;\ Q_{j}^{[n],N}\right)\,.

(2.25)

Moreover, in view of the periodicity properties ot the conserved fields and (
2.10
)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\partial_{\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Cov}\left(Q^{[1],N}\ ;\ Q^{[n],N}\right)=\partial_{t_{1}}\partial_{t_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}_{T}(\alpha,P+it_{1}x+it_{2}x^{n})_{|_{t_{1},t_{2}=0}}\,.

(2.26)

Noticing that
\lim_{\alpha\to 0}{\mathbb{E}}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]=0
, and that we can always uniformly bound
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]
by a constant independent of
N
, the previous equation implies that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\partial_{t_{1}}\partial_{t_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}_{T}(s,P+it_{1}x+it_{2}x^{n})_{|_{t_{1},t_{2}=0}}{\rm d}s.

(2.27)

So, we conclude.
∎

The real
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime.

We consider the real
\beta
-ensemble introduced in Section
1.1
as the probability measure on the real line

{\rm d}\mathbf{P}^{\beta,V}_{N}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})=\frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}^{\beta,V}_{N}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}V(\lambda_{j})}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\,,

(2.28)

where
\beta>0
and
V
is a continuous function such that the partition function

\mathbf{Z}^{\beta,V}_{N}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}|^{\beta}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}V(\lambda_{j})}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\lambda}

is finite. This is the case if
V
grows to infinity fast enough, namely if for some
\beta^{\prime}>\max(1,\beta)
,

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{N\beta^{\prime}\ln|x|}>1\,,

(2.29)

see
[
6
, equation (2.6.2)]
.

Dumitriu and Edelman showed in
[
19
]
that the
\beta
-ensemble admits a tridiagonal representation

H=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&&&0\\
b_{1}&a_{2}&b_{2}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&b_{N-1}\\
0&&&b_{N-1}&a_{N}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.30)

where the entries of the matrix are distributed according to the following probability measure

{\rm d}\mu^{\beta,V}_{N,\text{beta}}=\frac{1}{Z^{\text{beta}}_{N}(\beta,V)}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}b_{j}^{\beta(N-j)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{b_{j}\geq 0}\exp\left(-\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(H)\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf a}{\rm d}{\bf b}\,.

(2.31)

Then, the eigenvalues of
H
are distributed according to
{\rm d}\mathbf{P}^{\beta,V}_{N}
(
2.28
). An important example is the case
V(x)=x^{2}/2
for which we recover the classical
Gaussian
\beta
-ensemble
(also called Hermite
\beta
-ensemble), see
[
6
, Section 2.5]
, and the distribution
\mu^{\beta,V}_{N,\text{beta}}
factorizes in the following way: the entries of
H
can be seen to be independent (modulo the symmetry of the matrix), Gaussian
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
on the diagonal, and the law of the off-diagonal elements is given by renormalized chi variables

b_{j}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\chi_{(N-j)\beta}\,,

where the variable
X
is
\chi_{\kappa}
-distributed if its law is given by the density function

f(x)=\frac{x^{\kappa-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}}{2^{\kappa/2-1}\Gamma(\kappa/2)}\,.

We are interested in the so-called
high-temperature regime
for this model, specifically, we are interested in the infinite size
N
limit, in such a way that
\beta=\frac{2\alpha}{N}
for some
\alpha>0
. In this regime the probability distribution (
2.31
) becomes
\mu_{N,\text{HT}}^{\alpha,V}
(
1.11
), which we recall:

{\rm d}\mu_{N,\text{HT}}^{\alpha,V}=\frac{1}{Z^{\text{HT}}_{N}(\beta,V)}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}b_{j}^{2\alpha\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{b_{j}\geq 0}\exp\left(-\mathrm{Tr}\left(V(H)\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf a}{\rm d}{\bf b}\,.

(2.32)

This regime has drawn a lot of attention from the random matrix and statistical physics communities lately. Introducing the empirical measure by

{\rm d}\nu_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}\,,

this model was first considered in
[
4
]
, where the authors were able to compute the limiting empirical measure for this model when
V(x)=x^{2}/2
. Recently, Garcia-Zelada showed in
[
29
]
that under a general choice of
V
, the sequence of empirical measures satisfies a large deviation principle with strictly convex rate function, ensuring the convergence of
\nu_{N}
. Although the limiting measure is not explicit, its density
\rho^{V}_{\alpha}
satisfies for almost every
x
the nonlinear equation

V(x)-2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|x-y|\rho^{V}_{\alpha}(y){\rm d}y+\log\rho^{V}_{\alpha}(x)=\lambda^{V}_{\alpha}

for some constant
\lambda^{V}_{\alpha}
, see
[
36
, Lemma 3.2]
for example.

The fluctuations of the eigenvalues in the bulk and at the edge of a configuration were studied for example in
[
9
,
56
,
57
,
60
,
47
]
. These fluctuations were shown to be described by Poisson statistics in this regime. With the choice
V(x)=x^{2}/2
, Nakano and Trinh proved in
[
56
]
a Central Limit theorem for this ensemble, namely y they proved that for smooth enough
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the random variables

\sqrt{N}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f{\rm d}\nu_{N}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\rho^{V}_{\alpha}{\rm d}x\right)

converge towards a centred Gaussian random variable with variance depending both on
\alpha
and
f
. In
[
23
]
, the authors showed this central limit theorem for general confining potentials and smooth enough, decaying at infinity test functions. In this paper, we consider the case where
V
is a polynomial of even degree greater than
2
as in Hypotheses
1.1
. We deduce a central limit theorem for polynomial test functions from section
4
.

Indeed, we notice that the matrix
H
(
2.30
) is a Toeplitz-like matrix (see Appendix
A
), thus, in view of Lemma
1.3
and Corollary
2.2
, we can apply Theorem
1.5
to the real
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime, thus we deduce that

Corollary 2.6

(CLT for real, high temperature
\beta
-ensemble)
.

Consider the matrix representation (
2.30
) of the real
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime with potential
P
, polynomial of even degree with positive leading order coefficient. Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{HT}}(\alpha,P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{HT}}(\alpha,P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln(Z^{\mathrm{HT}}_{N}(\beta,P))\,,

(2.33)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(H^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(H^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})\,,

(2.34)

where

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(H^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{HT}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{HT}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|\,.

(2.35)

Thus, we obtained a central limit theorem for the real
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime with polynomial potential.

Furthermore, all the hypotheses to apply the second part of our results are satisfied, so we deduce the following identities

\begin{split}&\partial_{\alpha}(\alpha\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{HT}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}})=\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{T}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\\
&\partial_{\alpha}(\alpha\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{HT}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}})=\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{T}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\end{split}

(2.36)

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
is the free energy of the Toda lattice (
2.7
).
So we can compute both the moments and their variances of the Toda lattice starting from the one of the real
\beta
-ensemble at high temperature.

Remark 2.7
.

Applying the second part of Theorem
1.5
, we deduce the following equality valid for the currents of the Toda lattice:

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[J^{[n],N}_{1}\right]=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\partial_{t_{1}}\partial_{t_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}_{T}(s,P+it_{1}x+it_{2}x^{n})_{|_{t_{1},t_{2}=0}}{\rm d}s=\alpha\partial_{t_{1}}\partial_{t_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{HT}}(\alpha,P+it_{1}x+it_{2}x^{n})_{|_{t_{1},t_{2}=0}}.

(2.37)

2.2
The exponential Toda lattice, and the Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature

In this subsection, we focus on the Exponential Toda lattice and its relation with the Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime
[
28
]
. These two systems were considered in
[
33
]
. In this paper, the authors considered the classical Gibbs ensemble for the Exponential Toda lattice and were able to compute the density of states for this model connecting it to the Laguerre
\alpha
ensemble
[
51
]
, which is related to the classical
\beta
one in the high-temperature regime. Here we consider both the Generalized Gibbs ensemble for the integrable lattice and the Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature with polynomial potential, and we obtain a CLT for both systems, furthermore, we connect the two in the same way as we did for the Toda lattice and the real
\beta
-ensemble.

The exponential Toda lattice.

The exponential Toda lattice is the Hamiltonian system on
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
described by the Hamiltonian

H_{E}({\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-p_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{q_{j}-q_{j+1}}\,,\quad p_{j},q_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,

(2.38)

with canonical Poisson bracket.
Here, we consider periodic boundary conditions

q_{j+N}=q_{j}+\Omega,\quad p_{j+N}=p_{j},\qquad\forall\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}},

(2.39)

and
\Omega\geq 0
is an arbitrary constant. The equations of motion are given in Hamiltonian form as

\begin{split}&\dot{q}_{j}=\frac{\partial H_{E}}{\partial p_{j}}=-e^{-p_{j}}\,,\\
&\dot{p}_{j}=-\frac{\partial H_{E}}{\partial q_{j}}=e^{q_{j-1}-q_{j}}-e^{q_{j}-q_{j+1}}\,.\end{split}

(2.40)

Following
[
33
]
, we perform the non-canonical change of coordinates

x_{j}=e^{-\frac{p_{j}}{2}},\quad y_{j}=e^{\frac{q_{j}-q_{j+1}}{2}}=e^{-\frac{r_{j}}{2}},\quad r_{j}=q_{j+1}-q_{j},\quad j=1,\dots,N,

(2.41)

to obtain a Lax Pair for this system.
Indeed, in these variables, the Hamiltonian (
2.38
) transform into

H_{E}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})\,,

(2.42)

and the Hamilton’s equations (
2.40
) become

\dot{x_{j}}=\frac{x_{j}}{2}\left(y_{j}^{2}-y_{j-1}^{2}\right),\quad\dot{y_{j}}=\frac{y_{j}}{2}\left(x_{j+1}^{2}-x_{j}^{2}\right),\quad j=1,\dots,N,

(2.43)

where
x_{N+1}=x_{1},\,y_{0}=y_{N}
.

Let us introduce the matrices
L,A\in\textrm{Mat}(N)
as

\displaystyle L=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{2}+y_{N}^{2}&x_{1}y_{1}&&&x_{N}y_{N}\\
x_{1}y_{1}&x_{2}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}&x_{2}y_{2}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&x_{N-1}y_{N-1}\\
x_{N}y_{N}&&&x_{N-1}y_{N-1}&x_{N}^{2}+y_{N-1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.44)

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{1}y_{1}&&&-x_{N}y_{N}\\
-x_{1}y_{1}&0&x_{2}y_{2}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&x_{N-1}y_{N-1}\\
x_{N}y_{N}&&&-x_{N-1}y_{N-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.45)

The system of equations (
2.43
) admits the Lax representation

\dot{L}=[A,L].

(2.46)

Hence, the quantities
H_{m}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{m}\right)
,
m=1,\ldots,N
are constants of motion as well as the eigenvalues of
L
.
For this integrable model, we define the generalized Gibbs ensemble as

{\rm d}\mu_{ET}=\frac{1}{Z^{H_{E}}_{N}(\alpha,P)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{2\alpha-1}y_{j}^{2\alpha-1}\mathbbm{1}_{x_{j}\geq 0}\mathbbm{1}_{y_{j}\geq 0}e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P(L)\right)}{\rm d}{\bf x}{\rm d}{\bf y}\,,

(2.47)

where
\alpha>0
, and
P
is a real-valued polynomial with positive leading coefficient.
Z_{N}^{H_{E}}(\alpha,P)
is the normalization constant. We notice that the matrix
L
is of type 1-
ii
.

Remark 2.8
.

The definition of our Gibbs ensemble is more general than the one given in
[
33
]
, indeed there the authors were considering just the classical Gibbs ensemble for this model, so the case
P(x)=x/2
.

We notice that the structure of (
2.47
) resembles the one of
\mu_{N}^{(1)}
(
1.31
), thus we want to apply Theorem
1.5
. To do this, we have to identify the functions
F,W
. First, as an application of Lemma
1.3
and Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the following corollary

Corollary 2.9
.

Fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and consider the matrix
L
(
2.44
). Then for
N
big enough, there exists some
k=k(m)\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{m}\right)
is a
k
-circular function, with seed
V
and weed
\widetilde{V}
. Furthermore, both
V,\widetilde{V}
are bounded from below away from
-\infty
.

As in the Toda lattice case, if we naively set
F(x,y)=x^{2\alpha-1}y^{2\alpha-1}
, this would not fit in the hypotheses of our theorem. As in the previous case, we have just to consider a slight modification of the measure:

{\rm d}\mu_{ET}=\frac{1}{Z^{H_{E}}_{N}(\alpha,P)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{2\alpha-1}y_{j}^{2\alpha-1}\exp\left(-\varepsilon\frac{x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}}{2}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{x_{j}\geq 0}\mathbbm{1}_{y_{j}\geq 0}e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P(L)\right)+\varepsilon\frac{x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}}{2}}{\rm d}{\bf x}{\rm d}{\bf y}\,,

(2.48)

for fixed
\varepsilon>0
, but small. In this way, defining
F(x,y,\alpha)=x^{2\alpha-1}y^{2\alpha-1}\exp(-\varepsilon\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2})
, and
W({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1},{\bf x}_{2},{\bf y}_{2})=V({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1},{\bf x}_{2},{\bf y}_{2})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2k}x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}
we are in the same hypotheses as Theorem
1.5
, thus we deduce the following corollary

Corollary 2.10

(CLT for the Exponential Toda lattice)
.

Consider the Lax matrix L (
2.44
) of the Exponential Toda lattice distributed according to the Generalized Gibbs Ensemble (
2.47
). Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{HE}(\alpha,P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{ET}(\alpha,P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln(Z^{H_{E}}_{N}(\alpha,P))\,,

(2.49)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})\,,

(2.50)

where

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{ET}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{ET}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|

(2.51)

The Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime.

The Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble is a random matrix ensemble introduced by Dumitriu and Edelman in
[
19
]
. It has the following matrix representation

Q=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{2}&x_{1}y_{1}&&&\\
x_{1}y_{1}&x_{2}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}&x_{2}y_{2}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&x_{N-1}y_{N-1}\\
&&&x_{N-1}y_{N-1}&x_{N}^{2}+y_{N-1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.52)

where the entries of
Q
are distributed according to

{\rm d}\mu_{L}=\frac{1}{Z^{L}_{N}(\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{\beta(M-j+1)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{x_{j}\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}y^{\beta(N-j)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{y_{j}\geq 0}\exp\left(-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P(Q)\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf x}{\rm d}{\bf y}\,,

(2.53)

here we consider the case
M=N

P
can be any continuous function such that the partition function is well-defined. For our purpose we consider

to be a polynomial of degree greater than one with positive leading coefficient, as in Hypotheses

The remarkable property of this ensemble is that it is possible to explicitly compute the joint eigenvalue density as

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{L}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{Z}^{L}_{N}(\beta,P)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\beta}{2}\left(M-N+1\right)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{\lambda_{j}\geq 0}\prod_{j<i}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}|^{\beta}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}P(\lambda_{j})}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\,.

We are interested in the so-called high-temperature limit, i.e. when
\beta=\frac{2\alpha}{N}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, which was considered in
[
5

, where the authors were able to compute the density of states for the particular case

.

In this regime, the density (
2.53
) takes the form

{\rm d}\mu_{L}=\frac{1}{Z^{L}_{N}(\alpha,P)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{2\alpha\left(1-\frac{j+1}{N}\right)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{x_{j}\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}y_{j}^{2\alpha\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{y_{j}\geq 0}\exp\left(-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P(Q)\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf x}{\rm d}{\bf y}\,.

(2.55)

We notice that the structure of this density resembles the one of
\mu^{(2)}_{N}
(
1.32
). Furthermore, the matrix
Q
is Toeplitz-like and tridiagonal, thus applying Lemma
1.3
and Theorem
2.1
, we deduce the following corollary

Corollary 2.11

(CLT for Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble)
.

Consider the matrix representation (
2.52
) of the Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime, and let
P
be a real polynomial of degree at least
1
with positive leading coefficient. Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{L}(\alpha,P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{L}(\alpha,P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln(Z^{L}_{N}(\alpha,P))\,,

(2.56)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})\,,

(2.57)

where

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{L}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{L}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|

(2.58)

Which is the perfect analogue of the result for the Exponential Toda lattice.
Furthermore, we are ready to apply the second part of our result, indeed we can deduce the following identities

\begin{split}&\partial_{\alpha}(\alpha\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{L}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}})=\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{ET}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\\
&\partial_{\alpha}(\alpha\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{L}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}})=\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{ET}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\end{split}

(2.59)

thus, we can compute all the quantities involved in the previous theorems from the Free Energy of the Laguerre ensemble.

2.3
The Volterra lattice, and the antisymmetric
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature

In this subsection, we focus on the Volterra lattice and its relation with the Antisymmetric
\beta
-ensemble
[
21
]
in the high-temperature regime
[
28
]
. These two systems were considered in
[
33
]
. In this paper, the authors considered the classical Gibbs ensemble for the Volterra lattice and were able to compute the density of states for this model connecting it to the Antisymmetric
\alpha
ensemble
[
28
]
, which is related to the classical
\beta
one introduced by Dumitriu and Forrester
[
21
]
.

The Volterra Lattice.

The Volterra lattice (or
discrete KdV equation
) is the following systems of
N
coupled ODEs

\dot{a_{j}}=a_{j}\left(a_{j+1}-a_{j-1}\right),\qquad j=1,\dots,N,

(2.60)

here
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
, and we consider periodic boundary conditions
a_{j}=a_{j+N}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Volterra introduced it to study
evolution of populations in a hierarchical system of competing species. This system was considered by Kac and van Moerbeke in
[
43
]
, who solved it explicitly using a discrete version of the inverse scattering transform introduced by Flaschka
[
25
]
.

Introducing on the phase space
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
the following Poisson bracket

\{a_{j},a_{i}\}_{\textrm{Volt}}=a_{j}a_{i}(\delta_{i,j+1}-\delta_{i,j-1})\,,

(2.61)

and defining the Hamiltonian
H_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\,
, we can rewrite the equations of motion (
2.60
) in Hamiltonian form as

\dot{a}_{j}=\{a_{j},H_{1}\}_{\textrm{Volt}}\,.

(2.62)

An elementary constant of motion for the system is
H_{0}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}
which is independent of
H_{1}
.

The Volterra lattice is a completely integrable system, and it admits several equivalents
Lax representations
, see e.g.
[
43
,
54
,
33
]
. We use the one presented in
[
33
]
. Specifically, we introduce the matrices
L,A\in\textrm{Mat}({\mathbb{R}},N)
as

\displaystyle L=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{a_{1}}&&&-\sqrt{a_{N}}\\
-\sqrt{a_{1}}&0&\sqrt{a_{2}}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\sqrt{a_{N-1}}\\
\sqrt{a_{N}}&&&-\sqrt{a_{N-1}}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.63)

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sqrt{a_{j}a_{j+1}}(E_{j,j+2}-E_{j+2,j})\,,

(2.64)

where
E_{r,s}
is defined as
\left(E_{r,s}\right)_{ij}=\delta^{i}_{r}\delta^{j}_{s}
and
E_{j+N,i}=E_{j,i+N}=E_{j,i}
. Then, it follows that the equations of motion (
2.62
) are equivalent to

\dot{L}=[L;A]\,.

(2.65)

In view of this Lax pair, we know that
\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{k}\right)
are constant of motion for the model.

Following
[
33
]
, we introduce the Generalized Gibbs Ensemble of the Volterra lattice (
2.60
) as

{\rm d}\mu_{\textrm{Volt}}({\bf a})=\frac{e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P(L)\right)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}^{\alpha-1}\mathbbm{1}_{a_{j}>0}{\rm d}{\bf a}}{Z_{N}^{\textrm{Volt}}(\alpha,P)}\,,

(2.66)

where
\alpha>0
,
P
is a polynomial as in Hypotheses
1.1
of the form
P(x)=(-1)^{j}x^{2j}+\textrm{l.o.t}
, where
l.o.t
stands for lower order terms. Moreover, we notice that, given the antisymmetric nature of
L
,
\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{2j+1}\right)=0
. For this reason, we perform the change of coordinates
\sqrt{a_{j}}=x_{j}
, where we take just the positive root, so the previous measure read

{\rm d}\mu_{\textrm{Volt}}({\bf x})=\frac{e^{-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P(L)\right)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{2\alpha-1}\mathbbm{1}_{x_{j}>0}{\rm d}{\bf x}}{Z_{N}^{\textrm{Volt}}(\alpha,P)}\,.

(2.67)

This Generalized Gibbs ensemble resembles the structure of
\mu_{N}^{(1)}
(
1.31
), we have just to identify
F,W
. We notice that it is possible to generalize Theorem
2.1
also for the antisymmetric situation, moreover, the matrix
L
is circular-like. Thus, by applying Lemma
1.3
and Theorem
2.1
, we deduce the following Corollary:

Corollary 2.13
.

Fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and consider the matrix
L
(
2.63
). Then for
N
big enough, there exists some
k=k(m)\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{m}\right)
is a
k
-circular function, with seed
V
and weed
\widetilde{V}
. Furthermore, both
V,\widetilde{V}
are bounded from below away from
-\infty
.

Thus, following the same kind of reasoning as in the Toda lattice, section
2.1
, and the Exponential Toda lattice, section
2.2
, we deduce the following:

Corollary 2.14

(CLT for Volterra lattice)
.

Consider the Lax matrix L (
2.63
) of the Volterra lattice distributed according to the Generalized Gibbs Ensemble (
2.66
). Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{Volt}}(\alpha,P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{Volt}}(\alpha,P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln(Z^{\textrm{Volt}}_{N}(\alpha,P))\,,

(2.68)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})\,,

(2.69)

where

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(L^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{Volt}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{Volt}}(\alpha,P+tx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|\,.

(2.70)

The Antisymmetric
\beta

The Antisymmetric
\beta
-ensemble is a random matrix ensemble introduced by Dumitriu and Forrester in
[
21
]
; it has the following matrix representation

Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{1}&&&\\

here
P

2.72

1.1

P
polynomial of the form
P(x)=(-1)^{j}x^{2j}+\textrm{l.o.t}
.

As in the previous cases, we are interested in the high-temperature regime for this model, so we set
\beta=\frac{2\alpha}{N}
, and we rewrite the previous density as

{\rm d}\mu_{AG}=\frac{1}{Z^{AG}_{N}(\alpha,P)}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}x_{j}^{2\alpha\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)-1}\mathbbm{1}_{x_{j}\geq 0}\exp(-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P(Q)\right)){\rm d}{\bf x}\,.

(2.73)

This regime was introduced in
[
33
]
, where the author computed the density of states for this model in the case
P(x)=-x^{2}/2
.
The structure of this last density (
2.73
) resembles the one of
\mu^{(2)}_{N}
(
1.32
), indeed proceeding as in the case of the Volterra lattice, we deduce the following corollary

Corollary 2.15

(CLT for Antisymmetric
\beta
-ensemble)
.

Consider the matrix representation (
2.71
) of the Antisymmetric
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime, endowed with the probability distribution
{\rm d}\mu_{AG}
(
2.73
), and let
P(x)
be a polynomial of the form
P(x)=(-1)^{\ell}x^{2\ell}+\textrm{l.o.t.}
. Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{AG}(\alpha,P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{AG}(\alpha,P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln(Z^{AG}_{N}(\alpha,P))\,,

(2.74)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})\,.

(2.75)

where

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{AG}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{AG}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|\,.

(2.76)

Which is the perfect analogue of the result for the Volterra lattice.

2.4
The defocusing Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, and the Circular
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature

In this subsection, we focus on the defocusing Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, and its relation to the Circular
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature
[
38
,
35
,
67
]
.
This relation was highlighted by one of the present authors and T. Grava
[
35
]
, and independently by H. Spohn
[
67
]
. In these papers, the authors were able to characterize the density of states of the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice in terms of the one of the Circular
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime. Moreover, in
[
35
]
the authors were able to compute explicitly the density of states in the case of linear potential in terms of the solution of the Double Confluent Heun Equation
[
17
]
highlighting a connection with the Painlevé equations
[
49
,
27
]
. In
[
53
]
, the two present authors obtained a large deviations principles for the empirical spectral measure for any continuous and bounded potential.

The defocusing Ablowitz-Ladik lattice.

The defocusing Ablowitz-Ladik (dAL) lattice is defined by the following system of nonlinear equations

i\dot{a}_{j}=-(a_{j+1}+a_{j-1}-2a_{j})+|a_{j}|^{2}(a_{j-1}+a_{j+1})\,,

(2.78)

where
a_{j}(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We assume
N
-periodic boundary conditions
a_{j+N}=a_{j}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The dAL lattice was introduced by Ablowitz and Ladik
[
2
,
3
]
as the spatial integrable discretization of the defocusing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger Equation for the complex function

\psi(x,t)
,
x\in S^{1}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)=-\partial^{2}_{x}\psi(x,t)+2\lvert\psi(x,t)\rvert^{2}\psi(x,t).

(2.79)

As for the others dynamical systems that we considered, the dAL is an integrable system. Its integrability was proved by Ablowitz and Ladik by discretizing the
2\times 2
Zakharov-Shabat Lax pair
[
1
,
2
]
of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation.
Furthermore, Nenciu and Simon
[
59
,
62
]
constructed a new Lax pair for this lattice. Following their construction we double the size of the chain according to the periodic boundary condition, thus we consider a chain of
2N
particles
a_{1},\ldots,a_{2N}
such that
a_{j}=a_{j+N}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
.
Define the
2\times 2
unitary matrix
\Xi_{j}

\Xi_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}{\overline{a}}_{j}&\rho_{j}\\
\rho_{j}&-a_{j}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad j=1,\dots,2N\,,\quad\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}

(2.80)

and the
2N\times 2N
matrices

{\mathcal{M}}=\begin{pmatrix}-a_{2N}&&&&&\rho_{2N}\\
&\Xi_{2}\\
&&\Xi_{4}\\
&&&\ddots\\
&&&&\Xi_{2N-2}\\
\rho_{2N}&&&&&{\overline{a}}_{2N}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad{\mathcal{L}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Xi_{1}\\
&\Xi_{3}\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&\Xi_{2N-1}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.81)

Now let us define the unitary Lax matrix

{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{L}}{\mathcal{M}}\,,

(2.82)

that has the structure of a
5
-band periodic diagonal matrix.
The matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
is a periodic CMV matrix
[
14
]
.
The equations of motion (
2.78
) are equivalent to the following Lax equation for the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
:

\dot{\mathcal{E}}=i\left[{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{+}+({\mathcal{E}}^{+})^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(2.83)

where
†
stands for hermitian conjugate and

{\mathcal{E}}^{+}_{j,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}_{j,j}\quad j=k\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{j,k}\quad k=j+1\,{\,{\rm mod}\,}\,2N\,\mbox{or}\,k=j+2\,{\,{\rm mod}\,}\,2N\\
0\quad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.84)

The matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
is a circular-like matrix, this can be deduced from the fact that both
{\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{M}}
are circular-like (
2.81
), and the entries are continuous functions on a compact set, so by applying Lemma
1.3
, we deduce the following:

Corollary 2.17
.

Fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and consider the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
(
2.82
). Then for
N
big enough, there exists some
k=k(m)\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{m}\right)
is a
k
-circular function, with seed
V
and weed
\widetilde{V}
.

Following
[
67
,
35
,
53
]
, we notice that the quantity
K_{0}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})
is conserved, so this means that if
|a_{j}(0)|<1
for all
j=1,\dots,N
then
|a_{j}(t)|<1
for all
j=1,\dots,N
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, so we can consider
{\mathbb{D}}^{N}
as our phase space, here
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,|z|<1
}. On this phase space, we introduce the Generalized Gibbs ensemble for the defocusing AL lattice as

{\rm d}\mu_{\textrm{dAL}}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathbbm{1}_{a_{j}\in{\mathbb{D}}}\exp\left(-\mbox{Tr}\left(\Re P\left({\mathcal{E}}\right)\right)\right){\rm d}^{2}{\bf a}}{Z_{N}^{\textrm{dAL}}(\alpha,\Re P)}\,,

(2.85)

where
P\in{\mathbb{C}}[X]
is a polynomial. In view of Corollary
2.17
, we are in the hypotheses of Theorem
1.5
, thus we deduce the following:

Corollary 2.18

(CLT for defocusing Ablowitz–Ladik lattice)
.

Consider the Lax matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
(
2.82
) of the defocusing Ablowitz–Ladik lattice distributed according to the Generalized Gibbs Ensemble (
2.85
). Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dAL}}(\alpha,\Re P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dAL}}(\alpha,\Re P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ln(Z^{\textrm{dAL}}_{N}(\alpha,\Re P))}{2N}\,,

(2.86)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limits

\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Re{\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\Re\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma_{\text{real}}^{2})\,,\\
&\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Im{\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\Im\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma_{\text{imag}}^{2})\,,\end{split}

(2.87)

where

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Re{\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dAL}}(\alpha,\Re P+it\Re x^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma_{\text{real}}^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dAL}}(\alpha,\Re P+it\Re x^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|\,.\\
&\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Im{\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dAL}}(\alpha,\Re P+it\Im x^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma_{\text{imag}}^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dAL}}(\alpha,\Re P+it\Im x^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|\,.\end{split}

(2.88)

(2.94)

for all

2.5
The defocusing Schur flow, and the Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature

In this subsection, we focus on the defocusing Schur flow
[
31
]
, and its relation to the Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature
[
28
]
. This relation was first noticed in
[
67
]
, and then the two present authors obtained a large deviations principles for the empirical spectral measure for the defocusing Schur flow, and they were able to link it to the one of the Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime
[
53
]
.

The defocusing Schur flow.

The defocusing Schur flow is the system of ODEs
[
31
]

\dot{a_{j}}=\rho_{j}^{2}(a_{j+1}-a_{j-1})\,,\quad\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}\,,

(2.98)

and, as before, we consider periodic boundary conditions, namely
a_{j}=a_{j+N}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We notice that, if one chooses an initial data such that
a_{j}(0)\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
j=1,\ldots,N
, then
a_{j}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all times. Moreover, it is straightforward to verify that
K_{0}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|a_{j}|^{2}\right)
is conserved along the Schur flow. This implies that we can choose as phase space for the Schur flow the
N
-cube
\mathbb{I}^{N}
, where
{\mathbb{I}}:=(-1,1)
.
Furthermore, it was shown in
[
31
]
, that the Schur flow has the same Lax matrix as the focusing Ablowitz–Laddik lattice.

Following
[
67
,
33
]
, on
{\mathbb{I}}^{N}
we define the finite volume limit GGE as

{\rm d}\mu_{\textrm{dS}}({\bf a})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-a_{j}^{2}\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathbbm{1}_{a_{j}\in{\mathbb{I}}}\exp\left(-\mbox{Tr}\left(P\left({\mathcal{E}}\right)\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf a}}{Z_{N}^{\textrm{dS}}(\alpha,P)}\,,

(2.99)

where
P\,:\,{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a polynomial. Thanks to Corollary
2.17
, we can apply Theorem
1.5
obtaining a CLT theorem for the defocusing Schur flow

Corollary 2.21

(CLT for defocusing Schur flow)
.

Consider the Lax matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
(
2.82
) of the defocusing Schur flow distributed according to the Generalized Gibbs Ensemble (
2.99
). Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dS}}(\alpha,P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dS}}(\alpha,P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ln(Z^{\textrm{dS}}_{N}(\alpha,P))}{2N}\,,

(2.100)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})\,,

(2.101)

where

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dS}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dS}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|\,.

(2.102)

The Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime.

The Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble is a random matrix ensemble introduced by Killip and Nenciu in
[
45
]
. It has two slightly different matrix representations. The first one is the same as the Circular
\beta
-ensemble (
2.90
), but the distribution of the entries of the matrix is

{\rm d}\mu_{J}({\bf a})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{2N-1}\left(1-a_{j}^{2}\right)^{\beta(2N-j)/4-1}\prod_{j=1}^{2N-1}(1-a_{j})^{{\mathtt{a}}+1-\beta/4}(1+(-1)^{j}a_{j})^{{\mathtt{b}}+1-\beta/4}\mathbbm{1}_{a_{j}\in{\mathbb{I}}}\exp\left(-\mbox{Tr}\left(P\left({\mathfrak{E}}\right)\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf a}}{Z_{N}^{J}(\beta,P)}\,,

(2.103)

where
{\mathtt{a}},{\mathtt{b}}>-1
,
P\in{\mathbb{R}}[X]
is a polynomial. We notice that we are considering an
even
number of random variables, and
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
; for these reasons, all the eigenvalues of
{\mathfrak{E}}
come in pairs, meaning that if
e^{i\theta}
is an eigenvalue, then
e^{-i\theta}
is another one. Exploiting this symmetry, Killip and Nenciu found another matrix representation for this ensemble

J=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}&b_{1}&&&\\
b_{1}&c_{2}&b_{2}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&b_{N-1}\\
&&&b_{N-1}&c_{N}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\begin{cases}b_{j}=\left((1-a_{2j-2})(1-a_{2j-1}^{2})(1+a_{2j})\right)^{1/2}\\
c_{j}=(1-a_{2j-2})a_{2j-1}-(1+a_{2j-2})a_{2j-3}\end{cases}\,,

(2.104)

where
a_{0}=a_{2N}=-1
, and the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of
J
are related to the one of
{\mathfrak{E}}
as
\lambda_{j}=\cos(\theta_{j})
.

Also, in this case, it is possible to compute explicitly the joint eigenvalue density for this model as

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{J}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{Z}^{J}_{N}(\beta,P)}\prod_{j<\ell}|\cos(\theta_{j})-\cos(\theta_{\ell})|^{\beta}\mathbbm{1}_{\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{T}}e^{-2\sum_{j=1}^{N}P(\cos(\theta_{j}))}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\theta}\,.

(2.105)

As in the previous cases, we are interested in the high-temperature regime for this ensemble, so we wet
\beta=\frac{2\alpha}{N}
, thus the measure (
2.103
) read

{\rm d}\mu_{J}({\bf a})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{2N-1}\left(1-a_{j}^{2}\right)^{\alpha\left(1-\frac{j}{2N}\right)}\prod_{j=1}^{2N-1}(1-a_{j})^{{\mathtt{a}}+1-\frac{\alpha}{2N}}(1+(-1)^{j}a_{j})^{{\mathtt{b}}+1-\frac{\alpha}{2N}}\mathbbm{1}_{a_{j}\in{\mathbb{I}}}\exp\left(-\mbox{Tr}\left(P\left({\mathfrak{E}}\right)\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf a}}{Z_{N}^{J}(\beta,P)}\,.

(2.106)

This regime was considered in
[
69
]
and in the recent paper
[
55
]
, where the authors established a CLT for polynomial test functions in the absence of external potential (
P=0
in (
2.106
) ) by considering orthogonal polynomials, obtaining an explicit recurrence relation for the limiting variance.

Again, thanks to Corollary
2.17
, we can apply Theorem
1.5
deducing the following

Corollary 2.22

(CLT for Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature)
.

Consider the matrix representation
{\mathfrak{E}}
(
2.90
) of the Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime (
2.106
) . Then, defining the Free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{J}(\alpha,P)
as

{\mathcal{F}}_{J}(\alpha,P)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ln(Z^{J}_{N}(\alpha,P))}{N}\,,

(2.107)

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, we have the following weak limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathfrak{E}}^{j}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathfrak{E}}^{j}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{N}}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})\,,

(2.108)

where

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left({\mathfrak{E}}^{j}\right)\right]=iN\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{J}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\quad\sigma^{2}=|\partial^{2}_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{J}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}|\,.

(2.109)

Remark 2.23
.

We notice that for
N
even, for
{\mathtt{a}}+{\mathtt{b}}=-1+\frac{\beta}{4}
we can apply the final part of Theorem

, thus we deduce that

\begin{split}&\partial_{\alpha}(\alpha\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{J}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}})=\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{dS}}(\alpha,P+itx^{j})_{|_{t=0}}\,,\\

2.6
The Itoh–Narita–Bogoyavleskii lattices

In this section, we apply our results to two families of integrable lattices with short-range interaction that generalize the Volterra one (
2.60
). These families are described in
[
11
]
(see also
[
10
,
40
,
58
]
).

One is called
additive Itoh–Narita–Bogoyavleskii (INB)
r
-lattice
and is defined by the following equations

\displaystyle\dot{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{i+j}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{i-j}\right),\quad i=1,\dots,N,\;N\geq r\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2.111)

The second family is called the
multiplicative Itoh–Narita–Bogoyavleskii (INB)
r
-lattice
and is defined by the equations

\displaystyle\dot{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=a_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}a_{i+j}-\prod_{j=1}^{r}a_{i-j}\right),\quad i=1,\dots,N,\;N\geq r\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2.112)

In both cases we consider the periodicity condition

a_{j+N}=a_{j}
. We notice that setting
r=1
, we recover in both cases the Volterra lattice.
Moreover, both families admit the KdV equation as continuum limits, see
[
11
]
.

In both cases the interaction is short-range, but in the additive case (
2.111
) the nonlinearity is quadratic as in the Volterra lattice, instead in the multiplicative one (
2.112
) it is of polynomial order.

r\in{\mathbb{N}}

4
Nagaev–Guivarc’h theory

In this section, we adapt some standard results from the fluctuation theory of Nagaev–Guivarc’h
[
32
]
to our situation. Specifically, we prove the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of real random variables with partial sums
(S_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.
Assume that there exists
\varepsilon>0
, two functions
\lambda\in C^{1}([0,\varepsilon))
,
c\in C^{0}([0,\varepsilon))
and
h_{n}\in C^{0}([0,\varepsilon))
, such that for all
t\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
, and all
n\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-itS_{n}}\right]=c(t)\lambda(t)^{m}\left(1+h_{n}(t)\right)\,,

(4.1)

Where
\lim_{n\to\infty}n/m=k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 1}
.

Furthermore, assume that:

a.

there exists
A,\,\sigma^{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\lambda(t)=\exp\left(-iAt-\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2+o(t^{3})\right)
as
t\to 0
;

b.

h_{n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
uniformly in
[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
, and
h_{n}(0)=0
;

c.

c(0)=1
.

Then
A\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\sigma^{2}\geq 0
, and
\left(S_{n}-nA/k\right)/\sqrt{n}
converges to a Gaussian distribution
{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2}/k)
as
n
tends to infinity.

Proof.

First, evaluating (
4.1
) at
t=0
, we deduce that
\lambda(0)=1
. Then, we use the asymptotic expansion of
\lambda(t)
, and properties b.-c. to prove that

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-it\frac{S_{n}-mA}{n}\right)\right]\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}1\,.

Thus, by Lévy theorem
[
71
]
, we deduce that
S_{n}/n-A/k
converges in distribution to
0
. So, since
S_{n}
is real, then
A\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Exploiting again the asymptotic expansion of
\lambda(t)
and properties b.-c., we show that
\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-it\frac{S_{n}-mA}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]
converges to the function
\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}t^{2}}{2k}\right)
. By Lévy theorem
[
71
]
, this must be the characteristic function of a real random variable, proving that
\sigma^{2}\geq 0
, and that
(S_{n}-nA/k)/\sqrt{n}
converges to a Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}/k)
.
∎

Further, we prove the following:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of random variables with partial sums
(S_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Assume that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and functions
(x,t)\mapsto\lambda(x,t)\in C^{1,0}([0,1)\times{\mathbb{R}})
,
c_{n}\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
h_{n}
continuous at
0
, such that for all
t\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
, and all
n\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-itS_{n}}\right]=c_{n}(t)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\lambda\left(j/m,t\right)\right)\left(1+h_{n}(t)\right)\,,

(4.2)

where
\lim_{n\to\infty}n/m=k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 1}
.

Furthermore, assume that:

a.

there exists two continuous functions
A,\,\sigma^{2}\,:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\lambda(x,t)=\exp\left(-iA(x)t-\sigma^{2}(x)t^{2}/2+o(t^{2})\right)
as
t\to 0
;

b.

||h_{n}||_{\infty}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
uniformly in
[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
, and
h_{n}(0)=0
;

c.

c_{n}(0)=1
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}(t/\sqrt{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}(t/n)=1
.

Then
\int_{0}^{1}A(x){\rm d}x\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\int_{0}^{1}\sigma^{2}(x){\rm d}x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, and
\sqrt{n}\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n}-\frac{\int_{0}^{1}A(x){\rm d}x}{k}\right)
converges to a Gaussian distribution
{\mathcal{N}}\left(0,\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\sigma^{2}(x){\rm d}x}{k}\right)
as
n
tends to infinity.

Proof.

First, let
t={\widetilde{t}}/n
, then by hypothesis c.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-i{\widetilde{t}}\left(S_{n}/n-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}A\left(\ell/m\right)\right)}\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-i{\widetilde{t}}\left(S_{n}/n-\frac{\int_{0}^{1}A(x){\rm d}x}{k}\right)}\right]=1\,.

(4.3)

Thus, by Levy theorem,
S_{n}/n\to\frac{\int_{0}^{1}A(x){\rm d}x}{k}
almost surely, thus, since
S_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, this implies that
\int_{0}^{1}A(x){\rm d}x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Consider now
t={\widetilde{t}}/\sqrt{n}
, following the same reasoning one conclude that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-i{\widetilde{t}}\sqrt{n}\left(S_{n}/n-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}A\left(\ell/m\right)\right)}\right]&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-i{\widetilde{t}}\sqrt{n}\left(S_{n}/n-\frac{\int_{0}^{1}A(x){\rm d}x}{k}\right)}\right]\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{-\frac{{\widetilde{t}}^{2}}{2n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\sigma^{2}(\ell/m)}=e^{-\frac{{\widetilde{t}}^{2}}{2k}\int_{0}^{1}\sigma^{2}(x){\rm d}x}\,,\end{split}

(4.4)

thus, by Lévy theorem
[
71
]
,
e^{-\frac{{\widetilde{t}}^{2}}{2k}\int_{0}^{1}\sigma^{2}(x){\rm d}x}
must be the characteristic function of a real random variable, proving that
\int_{0}^{1}\sigma^{2}(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

Under Hypotheses
1.2
. Consider
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and the measure
\mu_{N}^{(1)}
(
1.31
), let
W,h
be the common seeds of
\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Re P(L)\right)
and

be their weeds. Assume that for some

A\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(6.44)

Proof.

We adapt the arguments of
[
32
, Theorem 3.7]
. By
[
24

, there exists a constant

is a constant depending on
k,\ell

.
By Theorem
1.5
, there exists an

N
goes to infinity
X
converges to
{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})

Since
t^{-1}
is not integrable at
0

[0,N^{-1}]
. In this interval, we have the following estimate:

\begin{split}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-itX}\right]-e^{-it\sqrt{N}A}\right|&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ration_BE}}}{{=}}\frac{\lvert Z_{N}^{(1)}\left(\alpha,\Re P+i\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\Re z^{s}\right)-Z_{N}^{(1)}\left(\alpha,\Re P\right)\rvert}{Z_{N}^{(1)}\left(\alpha,\Re P\right)}\\

(6.45)

with the convention that the empty product is equal to one.
Here we defined

\begin{split}\mathfrak{F}({\bf x})=&\prod_{j=1}^{N}F(x_{j},\alpha)\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}W({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{j+1})-{\widetilde{W}}({\bf x}_{M},x_{kM+1},\ldots,x_{kM+\ell},{\bf x}_{1})\right)\\

(6.46)

Thus, since

(6.47)

&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:small_set_2}}}{{\leq}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}\frac{C\sqrt{N}t+t\sqrt{N}A+\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2}{t}{\rm d}t\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\end{split}

.

We now consider the integral on
[1/N,\varepsilon\sqrt{N}]

\mathbb{E}_{1}[e^{itX}]

1.5
the following third order expansion and that there exist two continuous functions

\varepsilon>0

, and for

\int_{\frac{1}{N}}^{\varepsilon\sqrt{N}}\frac{|\mathbb{E}_{1}[e^{-itX}]-e^{-\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2}|}{t}{\rm d}t=\int_{\frac{1}{N}}^{\varepsilon\sqrt{N}}\frac{\left|c_{k,\ell}(\alpha,t/\sqrt{N})e^{-\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2+t^{3}p(t/\sqrt{N})/\sqrt{N}}(1+o(N^{-q}))-e^{-\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2}\right|}{t}{\rm d}t\,,

7
Conclusion and Outlooks

In this paper, we proved a general Central Limit Theorem type result and we apply it to several models in random matrix theory and integrable systems. By doing this, we strengthen the connection between these two subjects. Specifically, we could connect the expected values and the variances of the moments of each classical
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime with one specific integrable model, see Table
1
.

The results that we have obtained are relevant for two main reasons. Under the random matrix theory perspective, we were able to develop a general framework to prove polynomial central limit theorems for the classical
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime, based on their band matrix representation and on the transfer operator technique. Under the integrable systems’ theory point of view, our result enables the explicit computation of the so-called
susceptibility
matrix, which is a fundamental object in the theory of Generalized Hydrodynamics to compute the correlation functions for integrable models. Furthermore, we can prove rigorously the exponential decay of correlation for short-range interacting systems with polynomial potential.

It would be fascinating to generalize our result to a wider class of potential and functions and to obtain a Berry-Esseen bound for the classical
\beta
-ensemble in the high-temperature regime. Furthermore, defining a new
\beta
-ensemble related to the INB lattice would be interesting. Finally, we point out that it would be interesting to obtain large deviation principles for the Exponential Toda lattice and the Volterra one in the spirit of
[
36
,
53
]
.

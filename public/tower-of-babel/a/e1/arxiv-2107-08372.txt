Remarks on the Steady Prandtl Boundary Layer Expansions
Chen Gao, Liqun Zhang

Abstract
. We continue the study of the validity of the Prandtl boundary layer expansions in [ 4 ] , where by estimating the stream-function of the remainder, we proved if the Euler flow is perturbation of shear flows when the width of domain is small. In this paper, we obtain a new derivatives estimate of stream-function away from the boundary layer and then prove the validity of expansions for any non-shear Euler flow, provided the width of domain is small.



1.
Introduction

We consider the stationary incompressible Navier-Stokes equations

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &U^{\varepsilon}U^{\varepsilon}_{X}+V^{\varepsilon}U^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-\varepsilon\Delta U^{\varepsilon}+P^{\varepsilon}_{X}=0,\\
&U^{\varepsilon}V^{\varepsilon}_{X}+V^{\varepsilon}V^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-\varepsilon\Delta V^{\varepsilon}+P^{\varepsilon}_{Y}=0,\\
&U^{\varepsilon}_{X}+V^{\varepsilon}_{Y}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

posed in a two dimensional domain
\Omega=\{(X,Y):0<X<L,Y>0\}.
The no-slip boundary conditions are set on the boundary
Y=0
:

U^{\varepsilon}(X,0)=0,\hskip 8.53581ptV^{\varepsilon}(X,0)=0.

We are concerned with the asymptotic behavior of solution
[U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon}]
when
\varepsilon
is small. A formal limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
should lead to the Euler flow
[U^{0},V^{0}]
inside
\Omega
:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &U^{0}U^{0}_{X}+V^{0}U^{0}_{Y}+P^{0}_{X}=0,\\
&U^{0}V^{0}_{X}+V^{0}V^{0}_{Y}+P^{0}_{Y}=0,\\
&U^{0}_{X}+V^{0}_{Y}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Naturally, we pose the system (
1.2
) with no penetration boundary condition on
Y=0
:

V^{0}(X,0)=0.

Generically, there is a mismatch between the tangential velocities of the Euler flow
U^{0}(X,0)\neq 0
and the prescribed Navier-Stokes flow
U^{\varepsilon}(X,0)=0
on the boundary, because of the difference of boundary conditions imposed on the two systems.

According to the classical Prandtl boundary layer theory, there exists a thin layer which connect with
U^{\varepsilon}(X,0)
and
U^{\varepsilon}(X,0)
. Precisely, we take the Prandtl’s variables:

(1.3)

\displaystyle x=X,\qquad y=\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}.

In these variables, we express the solution of the NS equations
[U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon}]
via
[u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon}]
as

\begin{split}[U^{\varepsilon}(X,Y),V^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)]=[u^{\varepsilon}(x,y),\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}(x,y)]\end{split}

in which we note that the scaled normal velocity
v^{\varepsilon}
is
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
of the original velocity
V^{\varepsilon}
to satisfy the divergence-free condition. Similarly,
P^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)=p^{\varepsilon}(x,y)
. In these new variables, the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) now read

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{x}+v^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{y}+p^{\varepsilon}_{x}&=u^{\varepsilon}_{yy}+\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}_{xx},\\
\varepsilon[u^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}_{x}+v^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}_{y}]+p^{\varepsilon}_{y}&=\varepsilon[v^{\varepsilon}_{yy}+\varepsilon v^{\varepsilon}_{xx}],\\
u^{\varepsilon}_{x}+v^{\varepsilon}_{y}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, it leads to the Prandtl equations:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{0}_{p}u^{0}_{px}+v^{0}_{p}u^{0}_{py}-u^{0}_{pyy}+p^{0}_{px}=0,\\
&p^{0}_{py}=0,\\
&u^{0}_{px}+v^{0}_{py}=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

with
u^{0}_{p}|_{y=0}=0
. Prandtl hypothesized that when viscosity
\varepsilon
is small the Navier-Stokes flow can be approximately decomposed into two parts:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &U^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)-u^{0}_{e}(X,0)+u^{0}_{p}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),\\
&V^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{0}_{p}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),\end{aligned}

in which
[u^{0}_{e},v^{0}_{e}]=[U^{0},V^{0}]
denotes the Euler flow.

The verification of the viscosity vanishing limits is a challenging problem in general. There are lots of studies in recent year. For non-stationary case, the problem in the analytic case was proved in
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
. In 2014, Maekawa
[
21
]
proved the convergence under the assumption on the initial vorticity vanishing in the neighbourhood of boundary. Fei, Tao and Zhang
[
3
]
generalized this result to 3D case by energy methods. In
[
5
]
, Gerard-Varet, Maekawa and Masmoudi established the Gevrey stability for Prandtl type shear flows. Chen, Wu and Zhang gave a new proof of Gevrey stability for steady profile by resolvent estimate method in
[
1
]
. Later, Gerard-Varet, Maekawa and Masmoudi
[
6
]
showed the Prandtl expansion around concave boundary layer in Gevrey space. There are some results of instablity in Sobolev space, cf.
[
8
]
-
[
10
]
.

For the steady case, the problem is considered in Sobolev space. In this situation, the Euler flow
[u^{0}_{e},v^{0}_{e}]
in expansion (
1.6
) is always shear flow
[u^{0}_{e}(Y),0]
. An important progress was made by Guo, Nguyen
[
13
]
for Prandtl boundary layer expansions for the steady Navier-Stokes flows over a moving plate. Then Iyer
[
14
]
extended this result into a rotating disk. They considered the Euler flow is shear or rotating shear, Prandtl profile is strictly positive and the width of region or the angle of sector is small. Later, Iyer
[
15
]
generalized the result in
[
13
]
for the perturbation of shear flow. After that, Iyer in
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
justified the steady Prandtl expansion over a moving plane in
(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
under the assumption of constant shear Euler flow and smallness of the boundary layer profile. In 2018, a significant work by Guo and Iyer
[
12
]
showed the convergence result for no-slip boundary conditions in shear Euler flows in the case the width of the region
L
is small. Inspired by the methods in this work, we introduced a new quantity, the quotient of steam-function and the approximate solution, in
[
4
]
. By estimating that quantity, we justified the validity of expansion (
1.6
) for the perturbation of shear Euler flow when
L
is small. Moreover, we showed when the Euler flow is shear and Prandtl profile is in monotonic class, (
1.6
) is right even
L
is large. Recently, Iyer and Masmoudi in
[
19
]
also estimated the quantity introduced in
[
4
]
to show the stability of the Prandtl expansion in domain
(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
. In that work, the Euler flow is considered as special shear flow
[1,0]
and the solution of Prandtl equation is famous Blasius flow in monotonic class. There are also the stability results for Prandtl type shear flow of Navier-Stokes equations with force term in
X
-periodic domain cf.
[

and
[

.

In this paper, we continue our earlier work in
[
4
]
. We assume that the outside Euler flow
[u^{0}_{e}(X,Y),v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)]
satisfying the following hypothesis:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &0<c_{0}\leqslant u^{0}_{e}\leqslant C_{0}<\infty,\\
&\|\langle Y\rangle^{k}\nabla^{m}[u^{0}_{e},v^{0}_{e}]\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty\text{ for }m\geqslant 1.\end{aligned}

Here
\langle Y\rangle=Y+1
and
k
is a large constant. The special case for Euler flow is shear flow
[u^{0}_{e}(Y),0]
which discussed in
[
12
]
and
[
4
]
.

We consider the Prandtl equations with the positive data.

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{0}_{p}u^{0}_{px}+v_{p}^{0}u^{0}_{py}-u^{0}_{pyy}+p^{0}_{px}=0,\hskip 8.53581ptp^{0}_{py}=0,\hskip 8.53581ptu_{px}^{0}+v_{py}^{0}=0,\hskip 8.53581pt(x,y)\in(0,L)\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u^{0}_{p}|_{x=0}=U^{0}_{P}(y),\hskip 14.22636ptu^{0}_{p}|_{y=0}=v^{0}_{p}|_{y=0}=0,\hskip 14.22636ptu^{0}_{p}|_{y\uparrow\infty}=u^{0}_{e}|_{Y=0}.\end{aligned}\right.

U^{0}_{P}
is a prescribing smooth function such that

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &U^{0}_{P}>0\text{ for }y>0,\hskip 8.53581pt\partial_{y}U^{0}_{P}(0)>0,\hskip 8.53581pt\partial_{y}^{2}U^{0}_{P}-u^{0}_{e}(x,0)u^{0}_{ex}(x,0)\sim y^{2}\text{ near }y=0,\\
&\partial_{y}^{m}\{U^{0}_{P}-u^{0}_{e}(x,0)\}\text{ decay fast for any }m\geqslant 0.\end{aligned}

By the classical result in
[
22
]
, under above conditions on
U^{0}_{P}
, if
L
is small enough, equations (
1.8
) admit a classical solution
[u^{0}_{p},v^{0}_{p}]
satisfying:

(1.10)

\displaystyle u^{0}_{p}>0\text{ for }y>0,\hskip 8.53581ptu^{0}_{py}|_{y=0}>0,

\displaystyle\nabla^{m}\{u^{0}_{p}-u^{0}_{e}(0)\}\text{ decay fast as $y\rightarrow\infty$ for any }m\geqslant 0.

Now we state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume the Euler flow
[u^{0}_{e},v^{0}_{e}]
satisfies (
1.7
), the Prandtl profile satisfies (
1.10
),
L
is a constant small enough,
then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}(L)>0
depending on
L
, such that for
0<\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
, equations (
1.1
) admits a solution
[U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon}]\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
, satisfying:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|U^{\varepsilon}-u^{0}_{e}+u^{0}_{e}|_{Y=0}-u^{0}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\sqrt{\varepsilon},\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\|V^{\varepsilon}-v^{0}_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\sqrt{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}

with the following boundary conditions:

(1.12)

\displaystyle[U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon}]|_{Y=0}=0,

\displaystyle[U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon}]|_{X=0}=[u^{0}_{e}(0,Y)-u^{0}_{e}(0,0)+u^{0}_{p}(0,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}a_{0},v^{0}_{e}(0,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}b_{0}],

\displaystyle[U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon}]_{X=L}=[u^{0}_{e}(L,Y)-u^{0}_{e}(L,0)+u^{0}_{p}(L,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}a_{L},v^{0}_{e}(L,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}b_{L}].

Here
C
is a constant independent of
L
and
\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle a_{0}(Y)=u^{1}_{e}(0,Y)+u^{1}_{b}(0,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}u^{2}_{e}(0,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\hat{u}^{2}_{b}(0,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

\displaystyle a_{L}(Y)=u^{1}_{e}(L,Y)+u^{1}_{b}(L,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}u^{2}_{e}(L,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\hat{u}^{2}_{b}(L,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

\displaystyle b_{0}(Y)=v^{0}_{b}(0,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{1}_{e}(0,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{1}_{b}(0,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{2}_{e}(0,Y)+\varepsilon\hat{v}^{2}_{b}(0,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

\displaystyle b_{L}(Y)=v^{0}_{b}(L,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{1}_{e}(L,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{1}_{b}(L,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{2}_{e}(L,Y)+\varepsilon\hat{v}^{2}_{b}(L,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

are smooth functions constructed in Proposition
4.4
.

Unlike the assumptions of shear flows or their perturbation in previous works, this theorem shows the expansions (
1.6
) for any non-shear Euler flow which
u^{0}_{e}
is strictly positive. This result and the second result in
[
4
]
coincide with the classical results of Oleinik and Samokhin
[
22
]
for solutions of Prandtl’s equation. They show the local well-posedness (
L
is small) of Prandtl equation for any
u^{0}_{e}(X,0)
is strictly positive and the global well-posedness (
L
is any constant) for
u^{0}_{eX}(X,0)\geqslant 0
. And we show the Prandtl expansions for non-shear Euler flow when
L\ll 1
in this paper and shear Euler flow when
L
is any given constant in
[
4
]
.

To prove the theorem, we first construct the approximate solutions
\mathbf{U}_{s}=[U_{s},V_{s}]
of Navier-Stokes equations, which is similar to
[
4
]
. The main difficulty is estimating the remainders
\mathbf{U}:=\mathbf{U}^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{U}_{s}
, where
\mathbf{U}
satisfies the following linearized Navier-Stokes equations:

(1.13)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{U}_{s}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}_{s}+\nabla P=\mathbf{F}.

But when Euler flows is non-shear,
V_{s}\approx v^{0}_{e}
is not small. We notice that
v^{0}_{e}(X,0)=0
, so
v^{0}_{e}
is small near the boundary
\{(X,Y)|Y=0\}
. It leads us to estimating the stream-function away from the boundary layers. We estimate the derivatives of stream-function in outer area, i.e.
\{Y\geqslant\delta>0\}
, and combine this estimate with some estimates we obtained in
[
4
]
, to show the stream-function can be dominating by
\mathbf{F}
, which essentially leads to the proof the theorem.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we show the main profile of the approximation solution. In Section 3, we estimate the stream-function of remainder. In Section 4, we prove the main theorem. The construction of the high-order approximation solutions is in Appendix.

2.
Construction of the approximate solution

The construct of the approximate solutions is similar to
[
4
]
. We will need higher order expansions, as compared to (
1.6
), in order to control the remainder. Actually, the approximate solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations are as the following form:

(2.1)

\displaystyle U^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx

\displaystyle u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[u^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon[u^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})],

\displaystyle V^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx

\displaystyle v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[v^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{1}_{e}(X,Y)]+\varepsilon[v^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{2}_{e}(X,Y)]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}v^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

\displaystyle P^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx

\displaystyle p^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[p^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon[p^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}p^{3}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

in which
[u^{j}_{e},v^{j}_{e},p^{j}_{e}]
and
[u^{j}_{b},v^{j}_{b},p^{j}_{b}]
, with
j=0,1,2,
denoting the Euler profiles and boundary layer profiles, respectively. Here, we note that these profile solutions also depend on
\varepsilon
. And the Euler flows are always evaluated at
(X,Y)
, whereas the boundary layer profiles are at
(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})
.

Notation.

For convenience, we will introduce some notation here. we write

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}_{X,Y}},

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Y=0}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}_{X}(Y=0)},

\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{X,Y}}

and

\|\cdot\|_{\infty}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{X,Y}}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}.

We denote
a\lesssim b
which means there exist a positive constant
C_{0}
, s.t.
a\leqslant C_{0}b
, here
C_{0}
is independent on
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and
L
. And we write
a=O\big(b\big)
as
|a|\lesssim b
.

2.1.
The leading order of approximate solution

Recall the Euler flow
[u^{0}_{e},v^{0}_{e}]
. Let
\psi
be the stream-function of
[u^{0}_{e},v^{0}_{e}]

(2.2)

\displaystyle\psi(X,Y):=\int_{0}^{Y}u^{0}_{e}(X,Y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}Y^{\prime},\hskip 8.53581pt\psi_{Y}=u^{0}_{e},\hskip 8.53581pt\psi_{X}=-v^{0}_{e},

then Euler equations (
1.2
) are equivalent to:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta\psi=F_{e}(\psi).

From the assumptions in (
1.7
), we can know that
F_{e}
together with sufficiently many derivatives are bounded and decaying in its argument.

For Prandtl equations, there is a famous result due to Oleinik
[
22
]
:

Proposition 2.1

(Oleinik)
.

Assume boundary data
U^{0}_{P}\in C^{\infty}
satisfies (
1.9
), then for some
L>0
, equations (
1.8
) exists a solution
[u^{0}_{p},v^{0}_{p}]
, satisfying, for some
y_{0},m_{0}>0
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{(0,L)\times(0,\infty)}|u^{0}_{p},\partial_{y}u^{0}_{p},\partial_{yy}u^{0}_{p},\partial_{x}u^{0}_{p}|\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\inf_{(0,L)\times(0,y_{0})}\partial_{y}u^{0}_{p}>m_{0}>0,

\displaystyle u^{0}_{p}>0,\hskip 2.84526pt\text{ for }y>0.

In fact, if
U^{0}_{P}
satisfies high order parabolic compatibility conditions at the corner
(0,0)
, then
[u^{0}_{p},v^{0}_{p}]
are smooth enough. The compatibility conditions are discussed in our previous works
[
4
]
. Following the proof of Oleinik in
[
22
]
, we have:

Lemma 2.2
.

If
U^{0}_{P}\in C^{\infty}
satisfies (
1.9
) and high order parabolic compatibility conditions, then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|\langle y\rangle^{M}\nabla^{k}(u^{0}_{p}(x,y)-u^{0}_{e}(x,0))\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1,\hskip 5.69054pt\text{ for }\hskip 5.69054pt0\leqslant k\leqslant K,

here
K
and
M
are constants.

After we solved Prandtl’s equation (
1.8
), we set

u^{0}_{b}(x,y)=u^{0}_{p}(x,y)-u^{0}_{e}(x,0),

v^{0}_{b}(x,y)=\int_{y}^{\infty}u^{0}_{bx}(x,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

and
p^{0}_{b}=0.
The construction of high-order approximate solutions
[u^{1}_{e},v^{1}_{e}]
,
[u^{2}_{e},v^{2}_{e}]
,
[u^{1}_{b},v^{1}_{b}]
and
[\hat{u}^{2}_{b},\hat{v}^{2}_{b}]
is in Appendix. We denote the
[U_{s},V_{s}]
as the following:

(2.6)

\displaystyle U_{s}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[u^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon[u^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+\hat{u}^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})],

\displaystyle V_{s}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[v^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{1}_{e}(X,Y)]+\varepsilon[v^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{2}_{e}(X,Y)]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\hat{v}^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

\displaystyle P_{s}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle p^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[p^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon[p^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}p^{3}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}).

Then the errors

(2.7)

\displaystyle R_{1}:=U_{s}U_{sX}+V_{s}U_{sY}-\varepsilon\Delta U_{s}+P_{sX},

\displaystyle R_{2}:=U_{s}V_{sX}+V_{s}V_{sY}-\varepsilon\Delta V_{s}+P_{sY},

satisfy

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|R_{1}\|+\|R_{2}\|\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Next we show the main profile of
[U_{s},V_{s}]
.

2.2.
The main profile of approximate solution

In order to obtain the estimates of the remainder, we need to know more information about
[U_{s},V_{s}]
.

\begin{split}&U_{s}(X,Y)=u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+O\big(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big),\\
&V_{s}(X,Y)=v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}(v^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{1}_{e}(X,Y))+O\big(\varepsilon\big).\end{split}

Since
u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)
is strictly positive,
u^{0}_{p}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})>0
for
Y>0
,
u^{0}_{p}(X,0)=0
and
u^{0}_{py}(X,0)>0
, when
\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\leqslant 1
,

\begin{split}u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})=u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)-u^{0}_{e}(X,0)+u^{0}_{p}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\gtrsim-Y+\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\gtrsim\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},\end{split}

when
1\leqslant\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\leqslant\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{4}}
,

\begin{split}u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})=u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)-u^{0}_{e}(X,0)+u^{0}_{p}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\gtrsim-Y+1\gtrsim 1,\end{split}

when
\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{4}}
,

\begin{split}u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\gtrsim 1.\end{split}

So
U_{s}\sim\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},
when
Y\leqslant\sqrt{\varepsilon},
and
U_{s}\sim 1,
when
Y\geqslant\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

One can easily see for
i,j\geqslant 0
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\partial^{j}_{X}U_{s}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1,\hskip 5.69054pt\|\partial^{j}_{X}V_{s}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}\|Y^{j}\partial^{i+j}_{Y}U_{s}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}\|Y^{j}\partial^{i+j}_{Y}u^{0}_{e}\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}\|\frac{y^{j}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}}\partial^{i+j}_{y}u^{0}_{b}\|_{\infty}+O\big(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big)\lesssim 1,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}\|Y^{j}\partial^{i+j+1}_{Y}V_{s}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}\|Y^{j}\partial^{i+j+1}_{Y}u^{0}_{e}\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}\|\frac{y^{j}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}}\partial^{i+j+1}_{y}u^{0}_{b}\|_{\infty}+O\big(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big)\lesssim 1.\end{aligned}

Let
\delta
be a positive constant satisfying
\delta\geqslant\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}
, when
Y\leqslant\delta
, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |V_{s}|\lesssim\delta U_{s}.\end{aligned}

In fact, when
Y\leqslant\sqrt{\varepsilon}
,

\begin{split}|v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)|\lesssim Y=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon}U_{s}\lesssim\delta U_{s},\end{split}

when
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leqslant Y\leqslant\delta
,

\begin{split}|v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)|\lesssim Y\lesssim YU_{s}\lesssim\delta U_{s}.\end{split}

When
Y\geqslant\delta
, for any
j\geqslant 0
,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial^{j}_{Y}U_{s}(X,Y)=\partial^{j}_{Y}u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+O\big(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big),\\
&\partial^{j}_{Y}V_{s}(X,Y)=\partial^{j}_{Y}v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+O\big(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big).\end{aligned}

In fact since
u^{j}_{b}(X,y),v^{j}_{b}(X,y)
decay fast when
y\rightarrow\infty
, for some large
M
,

\begin{split}|u^{j}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})|\lesssim\big(\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\big)^{-M}\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{M}\delta^{-M}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{M}{4}},\\

Estimates of the remainder

In this section, we show the estimates of the remainder. Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle U^{\varepsilon}=U_{s}+U,\hskip 8.53581ptV^{\varepsilon}=V_{s}+V.

Then

(3.2)

Our critical estimates is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the assumptions in theorem
1.1
, if

[U,V]\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

satisfies the equations (
3.3

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}U_{X},\sqrt{\varepsilon}U_{Y},\sqrt{\varepsilon}V_{X},\sqrt{\varepsilon}V_{Y},U,V\|\leqslant C(\|F_{1}\|+\|F_{2}\|).

Let

According to the boundary conditions
[U,V]_{\Omega}=0

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Phi|_{X=0}=\Phi|_{X=L}=\Phi|_{Y=0}=0,\\

If

Because
[U,V]

It is the fourth-order elliptic equation for

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathbb{Y}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon\{\|\sqrt{U}_{s}G_{XX}\|^{2}+2\|\sqrt{U}_{s}G_{XY}\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{U}_{s}G_{YY}\|^{2}\}.

We start with the following Hardy-type’s inequality in

Lemma 3.1
.

If
H\in W^{1,2}(0,\infty)
,
0<\xi\leqslant 1
, then

\|H\|_{L^{2}_{Y}}^{2}\leqslant C\xi\varepsilon\|\sqrt{U_{s}}H_{Y}\|_{L^{2}_{Y}}^{2}+\frac{C}{\xi^{2}}\|U_{s}H\|_{L^{2}_{Y}}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\chi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth cut-off function supported in
[0,2]
, and
\chi|_{[0,1]}=1
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{2}\chi^{2}(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\mathrm{d}Y+\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{2}(1-\chi(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}}))^{2}\mathrm{d}Y.

Recall the leading profile of
U_{s}
,

\displaystyle U_{s}\sim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},\hskip 5.69054ptif\hskip 2.84526ptY\leqslant\sqrt{\varepsilon},\\
&1,\hskip 5.69054ptif\hskip 2.84526ptY\geqslant\sqrt{\varepsilon}.\end{aligned}\right.

So when
\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\leqslant 1
,
1-\chi(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\lesssim\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\frac{U_{s}}{\xi}
, and when
\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\geqslant 1
,
1-\chi(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\lesssim 1\lesssim\frac{U_{s}}{\xi}
. We have

\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{2}(1-\chi(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}}))^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\lesssim\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}U_{s}^{2}H^{2}\mathrm{d}Y.

And

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{2}\chi^{2}(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{\infty}YHH_{Y}\chi^{2}(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\mathrm{d}Y-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}}H^{2}\chi^{\prime}(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\chi(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}Y^{2}H_{Y}^{2}\chi^{2}(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\mathrm{d}Y+\int_{0}^{\infty}({\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}}})^{2}H^{2}|\chi^{\prime}(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})|\chi(\frac{Y}{\xi\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle\lesssim\xi\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}U_{s}H_{Y}^{2}\mathrm{d}Y+\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}U_{s}^{2}H^{2}\mathrm{d}Y.

The proof is complete.
∎

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}\|\lesssim\|\mathbf{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\mathbf{U}\|\lesssim\|\mathbf{U}\|^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\mathbf{U}\|^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}\mathbf{U}\|^{\frac{1}{4}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{9}{8}}\|\mathbf{U}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}.\end{split}

It implies

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leqslant C_{2}\|\mathbf{F}\|+C_{3}\varepsilon^{-\frac{9}{8}}\|\mathbf{U}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}\leqslant(C_{1}C_{2}+C_{3}C^{2}_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{8}})\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

Select
C_{0}=C_{1}C_{2}+1
,
\mathcal{T}(B)\subset B
when
\varepsilon
is small enough. And if
\mathbf{U}_{1},\mathbf{U}_{2}\in B
,

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2})\|_{\mathbb{Z}}&\leqslant C_{2}\|\mathbf{U}_{1}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}_{2}\|\\
&\leqslant C_{2}\|(\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}_{1}\|+\|\mathbf{U}_{2}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2})\|\\
&\leqslant C_{2}\|(\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2})\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\mathbf{U}_{1}\|+\|\mathbf{U}_{2}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla(\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2})\|\\
&\leqslant C_{3}\varepsilon^{-\frac{9}{8}}(\|\mathbf{U}_{1}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}+\|\mathbf{U}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{Z}})\|\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\\
&\leqslant 2C_{0}C_{3}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{8}}\|\mathbf{U}_{1}-\mathbf{U}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{Z}},\end{split}

so
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction mapping on
B
when
\varepsilon
is small enough, we can conclude equations (
4.1
) admits a solution and

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{5}{8}}\|\mathbf{U}\|_{\mathbb{Z}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{8}}.\end{split}

So we have

\begin{split}&|U^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)-u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)-u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})|\\
=&|\sqrt{\varepsilon}u^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}u^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\varepsilon u^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+\varepsilon\hat{u}^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+U(X,Y)|\\
\lesssim&\sqrt{\varepsilon},\\
&|V^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)-v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)|\\
=&|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+\varepsilon v^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\varepsilon v^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\hat{v}^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+V(X,Y)|\\
\lesssim&\sqrt{\varepsilon},\end{split}

which ends the proof.
∎

Sharp decay rate for the damped wave equation with convex-shaped damping
Chenmin Sun

Abstract
. We revisit the damped wave equation on two-dimensional torus where the damped region does not satisfy the geometric control condition. It was shown in [ AL14 ] that, for sufficiently regular damping, the damped wave equation is stale at a rate sufficiently close to t − 1 t^{-1} . We show that if the damping vanishes like a Hölder function | x | β |x|^{\beta} , and in addition, the boundary of the damped region is locally strictly convex with positive curvature, the wave is stable at rate t − 1 + 2 2 ​ β + 7 t^{-1+\frac{2}{2\beta+7}} , which is better than the known optimal decay rate t − 1 + 1 β + 3 t^{-1+\frac{1}{\beta+3}} for strip-shaped dampings of the same Hölder regularity. Moreover, we show by example that the decay rate is optimal. This illustrates the fact that the sharp energy decay rate depends not only on the order of vanishing of the damping, but also on the shape of the damped region. The main ingredient of the proof is the averaging method (normal form reduction) developed by Hitrik and Sjöstrand ( [ Hi1 ] [ Sj ] ).

). Since by definition,

Next we assume that

is ergodic, then by Weyl’s equidistribution theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{v}(f)(z)=\widehat{f}(0)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}f(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}.

(4.2)

To prove this,
by the assumption of

. The proof of Lemma

is now complete.

Throughout this section, we denote by

We need the following basic lemma for exponentials of bounded linear operators:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(G_{h})_{0<h<1}
be a family of
h
-dependent, uniformly bounded operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

e^{sG_{h}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(sG_{h})^{k}}{k!},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

Then the operator

is invertible with inverse
e^{-sG_{h}}
. Moreover,

\|e^{sG_{h}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leq e^{|s|\|G_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}}<\infty.

For any linear operator
B

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}(e^{sG_{h}}Be^{-sG_{h}})=e^{sG_{h}}\mathrm{ad}_{G_{h}}(B)e^{-sG_{h}},

(5.1)

where
\mathrm{ad}_{A}(B):=[A,B]
. Consequently, for any bounded operator
B
on
\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\|[e^{sG_{h}},B]\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leq|s|e^{3|s|\|G_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}}\|\mathrm{ad}_{G_{h}}(B)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}.

(5.2)

Finally, we have the Taylor expansion

\displaystyle e^{sG_{h}}Be^{-sG_{h}}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}\mathrm{ad}_{G_{h}}^{k}(B)+\frac{1}{(N-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{N-1}e^{sG_{h}}\mathrm{ad}_{G_{h}}(B)e^{-sG_{h}}ds,\quad\forall N\in\mathbb{N},

(5.3)

where
\mathrm{ad}_{A}^{k}(B)=\mathrm{ad}_{A}(\mathrm{ad}_{A}^{k-1}(B))
,
\mathrm{ad}_{A}^{0}(B)=B
.

Proof.

Assume that
\|G_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leq M
for all
h\in(0,1)
. The series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|(sG_{h})^{k}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}}{k!}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|s|^{k}M^{k}}{k!}=e^{|s|M}

converges absolutely. Therefore,

\|e^{sG_{h}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leq e^{|s|\|G_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}}.

To show that

is invertible, again, by the absolute convergence of the series

, we deduce that

\displaystyle e^{-sG_{h}}e^{sG_{h}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{k}G_{h}^{k}}{k!}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}(-1)^{k_{1}}\cdot\frac{k!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}=\mathrm{Id}.

Therefore,

is invertible with inverse

. One easily verifies that

(5.4)

Taking the operator norm we obtain (
5.2
). The last identity (

\displaystyle h^{-\frac{3+\delta}{2}}\|r_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}+h^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|Wv_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E})^{\prime}-(\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\prime}v_{h,E}\|_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{T})}.

Since
(\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\prime}\lesssim W^{\frac{1}{4}}
,
the last term on the right hand side is bounded by

C\|W^{\frac{1}{4}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Young’s inequality, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

\bullet
(C) Low energy hyperbolic regime:
c_{1}h^{2}<E\leq h^{1+\delta}

Again we denote by
\lambda=h^{-1}E^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then
\lambda\leq h^{-\frac{1-\delta}{2}}
. In this situation, the non self-adjoint term
h^{2}\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime}
can be absorb to the right as a remainder:

Lemma 6.8
.

Assume that
E\leq h^{1+\delta}
, then

\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim h^{-2}\|r_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}+h^{-\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}+\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

With the notation
\lambda=h^{-1}E^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
v_{h,E}
solves the equation

\displaystyle-v_{h,E}^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{2}v_{h,E}+ih^{-1}Wv_{h,E}=h^{-2}r_{h,E}-\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime}.

(6.9)

Doing the integration by part and inserting the equation (
6.9
), we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}Wv_{h,E}^{\prime}\overline{v}_{h,E}^{\prime}dx=-

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime}v_{h,E}^{\prime}\overline{v}_{h,E}dx-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}Wv_{h,E}^{\prime\prime}\overline{v}_{h,E}dx

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime}v_{h,E}^{\prime}\overline{v}_{h,E}dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\overline{v}_{h,E}(-\lambda^{2}v_{h,E}+ih^{-1}Wv_{h,E}-h^{-2}r_{h,E}+\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime})dx.

By writing
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(v_{h,E}^{\prime}\overline{v}_{h,E})=\frac{1}{2}(|v_{h,E}|^{2})^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime}v_{h,E}^{\prime}\overline{v}_{h,E}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime\prime}|v_{h,E}|^{2}dx\lesssim\|W^{\frac{1}{4}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}},

where we used
|W^{\prime\prime}|\lesssim W^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Writing

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\overline{v}_{h,E}\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\frac{3}{2}}\kappa_{h,E}(|v_{h,E}|^{2})^{\prime}dx=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{3}{2}})^{\prime}|v_{h,E}|^{2}dx,

one verifies that

\Big|\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\overline{v}_{h,E}\cdot\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime}dx\Big|\lesssim\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\lambda^{2}\leq h^{-(1-\delta)}
, we have

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{3}{2}}\lambda^{2}|v_{h,E}|^{2}dx\Big|\lesssim h^{-(1-\delta)}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The last term

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\overline{v}_{h,E}(-ih^{-1}Wv_{h,E}-h^{-2}r_{h,E})dx=h^{-2}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\overline{v}_{h,E}r_{h,E}dx,

and it can be easily controlled by

h^{-2}\|r_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}.

Putting the bounds together, we complete the proof of Lemma
6.8
.
∎

The importance of Lemma
6.8
is that, in the low energy hyperbolic regime, the term
h^{2}\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime}
has the same size
o(h^{2+\delta})
in
L^{2}
, and thus can be absorbed as a remainder.

At this state, we are able to apply the following 1D resolvent estimate for the Hölder-like damping in
[
DKl
]
:

Proposition 6.9

(
[
DKl
]
)
.

Let
\gamma\geq 0
. Assume that
W=W(x)\geq 0
and
\{W>0\}
is disjoint unions of intervals
I_{j}=(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}),j=1,2,\cdots,l
and that for each
j\in\{1,\cdots,l\}
,

C_{1}V_{j}(x)\leq W(x)\leq C_{2}V_{j}(x)\text{ on }(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}),

where
V_{j}(x)>0
are continuous functions on
(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})
such that

V_{j}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(x-\alpha_{j})^{\gamma},\;\alpha_{j}<x<\frac{3\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}}{4}\\
&(\beta_{j}-x)^{\gamma},\;\frac{\alpha_{j}+3\beta_{j}}{4}<x<\beta_{j}.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.10)

Then there exist
h_{0}>0
,
c_{1}>0
,
C>0
, such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
,
\sqrt{c_{1}}\leq\lambda\leq h^{-\frac{1-\delta}{2}}
and all solutions
v_{h,\lambda}
of the equation

-v_{h,\lambda}^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{2}v_{h,\lambda}+ih^{-1}W(x)v_{h,\lambda}=r_{h,\lambda},

we have

\displaystyle\|v_{h,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{-\frac{1}{\gamma+2}}\|r_{h,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.11)

The proof of Proposition
6.9
will be given in Appendix
A
.

Now applying Proposition
6.9
for
\gamma=\frac{1}{\theta}
(then
\frac{1}{\gamma+2}=\delta=\frac{\theta}{2\theta+1}
),
v_{h,\lambda}=v_{h,E}
and
r_{h,\lambda}=h^{-2}r_{h,E}-\kappa_{h,E}W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}^{\prime}
in our previous setting, combining with Lemma

, we deduce that
when
c_{1}h^{2}\leq E<h^{1+\delta}
,

\displaystyle\|v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{-2-\delta}\|r_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}+h^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}+h^{-\delta}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{h,E}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.12)

Finally, to get a contradiction, we denote three index sets for three regimes:

\mathcal{E}_{A}:=\{n:E_{n}\leq c_{1}h_{n}^{2}\},\;\mathcal{E}_{B}:=\{n:E_{n}>h_{n}^{1+\delta}\},\;\mathcal{E}_{C}:=\{n:c_{1}h_{n}^{2}<E_{n}\leq h_{n}^{1+\delta}\}.

Clearly,
\mathbb{N}=\mathcal{E}_{A}\cup\mathcal{E}_{B}\cup\mathcal{E}_{C}
. From Lemma
6.5
, Corollary
6.7
and (

), we deduce that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\rightarrow\infty\\
n\in S\end{subarray}}\|v_{h_{n},E_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\forall S\in\{A,B,C\},

and this implies that
\|v_{h_{n},E_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}=o(1)
, a contradiction to (
6.7
).
This finishes the proof of Proposition
6.1
. In summary, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is now complete.

7.
Optimality

Let
r_{0}\in(0,\pi)
and
\beta\geq 10
. Consider the function

a_{0}(x,y)=c_{\beta}\frac{(r_{0}^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2})_{+}^{\beta}}{(r_{0}+|x|)^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad c_{\beta}=2\pi\Big(\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\rho^{2})^{\beta}\Big)^{-1}.

Obviously, the damped region of
a_{0}
is the disc
\omega_{0}:=\{|(x,y)|<r_{0}\}
, which is strictly convex with positive curvature. Moreover, close to
|(x,y)|=r_{0}
,
a_{0}(x,y)
vanishes exactly of order
(r_{0}-|(x,y)|)_{+}^{\beta}
. Therefore,
a_{0}
verifies all the hypothesis of the damping function in Theorem
1.2
.

The averaged damping of
a_{0}
along
\mathrm{e}_{2}
is given by

b_{0}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}a_{0}(x,y)dy,

and direct computation yields

b_{0}(x)=(r_{0}-|x|)_{+}^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{ as }|x|\text{ close to }r_{0}.

The goal of this section is to show that

\displaystyle\|(-h^{2}\Delta-1+iha_{0})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}\gtrsim h^{-2-\frac{1}{\gamma+2}},\;\text{ with }\gamma=\beta+\frac{1}{2}.

(7.1)

The idea is to use the quasimodes constructed in
[
Kl
]
that saturates the lower bound of

\|(-h^{2}\Delta-1+ihb_{0}(x))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}

and use the averaging argument to transform back to obtain the desired quasimodes for the operator
-h^{2}\Delta-1+iha_{0}(z)
. In order to control remainders appearing in the normal form analysis, rather than using the construction in
[
Kl
]
as a blackbox, we should keep track of the regularity (anisotropic) of the quasimodes. For this reason, we will briefly review the construction of
[
Kl
]
and prove some extra estimates in the next subsection.

7.1.
Estimates for the
\mathbb{T}^{2}
quasimodes

First we review the construction of quasimodes of
-h^{2}\Delta-1+ihb_{0}(x)
in
[
Kl
]
. The original idea for the construction dates back to the appendix in
[
AL14
]
by Nonnenmacher.

Recall that
b_{0}(x)=(r_{0}-|x|)_{+}^{\gamma}
, we consider the ansatz

u_{k}(x,y):=\mathrm{e}^{iky}v_{h_{k}}(x),

with
0<h_{k}\lesssim\frac{1}{|k|}
to be specified later. We fix
\delta=\frac{1}{\gamma+2}
throughout this section. As before, we will drop the dependence in
k
and write simply
h=h_{k},v_{h}=v_{h_{k}}
. Plugging into the equation

(-h^{2}\Delta-1+ihb_{0}(x))u_{h}=O_{L^{2}}(h^{2+\delta}),

we would like
v_{h}
to satisfy

\displaystyle-h^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{h}+ihb_{0}(x)v_{h}=(1-h^{2}k^{2})v_{h}+O_{L^{2}}(h^{2+\delta}),\quad\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\sim 1.

(7.2)

This amounts to solve the eigenvalue problem:

\displaystyle-h^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{h}+ihb_{0}(x)v_{h}=\lambda_{h}^{2}v_{h}

(7.3)

with
\lambda_{h}=Ch+O(h^{1+\delta})
. We consider the even eigenfunction
v_{h}(x)=v_{h}(|x|)
with

\displaystyle v_{h}(x)=\begin{cases}&\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{h,l}(x),\;0\leq x<r_{0},\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{h,r}(x),\;r_{0}\leq x<\pi\end{cases}

where

v_{h,r}(x)=\cos\big(\frac{\lambda_{h}}{h}x\big).
The left function
v_{h,l}
should satisfy the equation

\displaystyle-h^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{h,l}+ih(r_{0}-x)_{+}^{\gamma}v_{h,l}=\lambda_{h}^{2}v_{h,l},\;0\leq x<r_{0}.

(7.4)

In order to ensure
v_{h}\in H^{2}
, the function
v_{h,l}
should satisfy the compatibility condition

\displaystyle v_{h,l}(r_{0})=\cos\big(\frac{\lambda_{h}r_{0}}{h}\big),\quad v^{\prime}_{h,l}(r_{0})=-\frac{\lambda_{h}}{h}\sin\big(\frac{\lambda_{h}r_{0}}{h}\big).

(7.5)

Since the mass in the damped region is very small compared with the total mass, and the amplitude of the transmitted mass should be the same size as the reflected mass, so we expect that
|v_{h,l}^{\prime}(r_{0})|\gg|v_{h,l}(r_{0})|
. As
\frac{\lambda_{h}}{h}
is of size
O(1)
, the principal part of the argument
\frac{\lambda_{h}r_{0}}{h}
must belong to
\pi\big(l+\frac{1}{2}\big),l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, we take the following ansatz for
\lambda_{h}
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}=\frac{\pi\big(l+\frac{1}{2}\big)h}{y_{0}}+O(h^{1+\delta}).

(7.6)

Next, denote by
F(x;\theta)
the
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
solution of the Neumann problem (with a parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\!\!\!\!-F^{\prime\prime}(x)+ix^{\gamma}F-\theta F=0,\;x>0\\
&\!\!\!\!F^{\prime}(0)=1,\end{cases}

(7.7)

then
v_{h,l}(x)
takes the form
h^{\delta}\alpha_{h}F\big(\frac{r_{0}-x}{h^{\delta}};\theta_{h}\big)
, with
\theta_{h}=h^{-\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma+2}}\lambda_{h}^{2}
, and a constant
O(1)=\alpha_{h}\in\mathbb{C}
to be determined in order to match the compatibility condition (
7.5
).

Denote by
\mu_{0}
be the lowest Neumann eigenvalue of the operator
-\partial_{y}^{2}+x^{\gamma}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
F_{0}
the Dirichelet trace
F(x;0)|_{x=0}
. It was shown that
\mu_{0}>0
(Lemma 4.1 of
[
Kl
]
). Moreover, by using the implicit function theory (Lemma 4.2 of
[
Kl
]
), there exists a uniform constant
C_{0}>0
, such that for all
|\theta|\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}
, the (unique) solution
F(y;\theta)
of the Neumann problem (
7.7
) satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{0}}\leq|F(0;\theta)|\leq C_{0}.

(7.8)

In particular,
F_{0}=F(0;0)\neq 0
.

Now we are ready to solve (
7.4
) with the compatibility condition (
7.5
). In view of (
7.6
), we precise the ansatz of
\lambda_{h}
as

\lambda_{h}=\frac{\pi\big(l+\frac{1}{2}\big)h}{r_{0}}+\gamma_{h}h^{1+\delta},\quad O(1)=\gamma_{h}\in\mathbb{C}.

Now
\alpha_{h},\gamma_{h}
are the parameters to be determined.
Plugging into (
7.5
) with

v_{h,l}(x)=h^{\delta}\alpha_{h}F\big(\frac{r_{0}-x}{h^{\delta}};\theta_{h}\big),

we obtain a system

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}F(0;\theta_{h})=(-1)^{l+1}h^{-\delta}\sin(\gamma_{h}r_{0}h^{\delta}),

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}=(-1)^{l}\big[\frac{\pi\big(l+\frac{1}{2}\big)}{r_{0}}+\gamma_{h}h^{\delta}\big]\cos(\gamma_{h}r_{0}h^{\delta}).

(7.9)

Since
|\theta_{h}|\sim h^{\frac{2}{\gamma+2}}
, by Taylor expansion of sin and cos, the leading term of
\gamma_{h}
should be
\gamma_{0}:=\frac{\pi\big(l+\frac{1}{2}\big)F_{0}}{r_{0}^{2}}
and the leading term for
\alpha_{h}
should be
\alpha_{0}=(-1)^{l}\frac{\pi\big(l+\frac{1}{2}\big)}{y_{0}}
. Fix the number
l
, by using the implicit function theorem, the solution
(\alpha_{h},\gamma_{h})
to (
7.1
) exists for
0\leq h\ll 1
and

|(\alpha_{h},\gamma_{h})-(\alpha_{0},\gamma_{0})|\lesssim h^{\delta}.

For the detailed argument, we refer Lemma 4.3 and Lemma 4.4 of
[
Kl
]
.

Finally, we take
h=h_{k}=\frac{r_{0}}{\sqrt{k^{2}r_{0}^{2}+\pi^{2}(l+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}}
, then
1-h_{k}^{2}k^{2}=\lambda_{h_{k}}^{2}+O(h_{k}^{2+\delta})
, hence

\displaystyle u_{h}(x,y)=e^{iky}v_{h}(x)=e^{iky}\big[\cos\big(\frac{\lambda_{h}|x|}{h}\big)\mathbf{1}_{r_{0}<|x|\leq\pi}+h^{\delta}\alpha_{h}F\big(\frac{r_{0}-|x|}{h^{\delta}};\eta_{h}\big)\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq r_{0}}\big]

(7.10)

are the desired
\mathbb{T}^{2}
quasimodes, satisfying

-h^{2}\Delta u_{h}-u_{h}+ihb_{0}(x)u_{h}=O_{L^{2}}(h^{2+\delta}).

We are going to prove more
estimates on
u_{h}
as well as its transformations:

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
u_{h}
is given by (
7.10
) and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a bump function supported near
0
. Then
\widetilde{u}_{h}:=\psi(h^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}D_{x})u_{h}
satisfies

(-h^{2}\Delta-1+ihb_{0}(x))\widetilde{u}_{h}=O_{L^{2}}(h^{2+\delta}).

Moreover, there exist uniform constants
h_{0}>0
,
C_{1}>0
, such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
:

\mathrm{(a)}

\frac{1}{C_{1}}\leq\|\widetilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1},\quad

\|b_{0}(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}h^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}
.

\mathrm{(b)}

\|h^{j}\partial_{x}^{j}\widetilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}h^{j(1-\delta)+\frac{\delta}{2}}
and

\mathrm{(c)}

.

Moreover, for any
S^{0}
symbol
g(x,y,\eta)
in
(y,\eta)
, smoothly depending on
x
, if
\widetilde{v}_{h}=e^{-\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{w}(g)}\widetilde{u}_{h}
, the above estimates
\mathrm{(a)},\mathrm{(b)},\mathrm{(c)}
still hold, up to some different uniform constant
C_{2}>0
.

We need a Lemma:

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mu_{0}
be the least eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{\gamma}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
associated to the Neumann boundary condition. Then there exists a uniform constant
C>0
, such that for all
|\theta|\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{4}
, the solution
F(x;\theta)
of (
7.7
) satisfies

\|F\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C.

Proof.

Take
H\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that
H(0)=0
and
H^{\prime}(0)=1
. Consider
\widetilde{F}:=F-H
, then

-\widetilde{F}^{\prime\prime}+ix^{\gamma}\widetilde{F}-\theta\widetilde{F}=W,

with
W=H^{\prime\prime}-ix^{\gamma}H+\theta H
. Multiplying by
\overline{\widetilde{F}}
and doing the integration by part, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widetilde{F}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx+i\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma}|\widetilde{F}(x)|^{2}dx-\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widetilde{F}(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x)\overline{\widetilde{F}}(x)dx.

Taking the real part and imaginary part, we get

\|\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\leq|\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\theta|\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}+\|W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},

and

\|x^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\leq|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\theta|\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}+\|W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Adding two inequalities above, using
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}-\mu_{0}\geq 0
and the fact that
|\theta|\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{4}
, we get

\mu_{0}\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}+2\|W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

This proves the boundedness of
\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\partial_{x}\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|x^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
in terms of
\|W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
. Replacing
\widetilde{F}
by
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}
, the same argument yields the boundedness of
\|\widetilde{F}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
in terms of
\|W\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
. Since the
H^{1}
bound for
W
is uniform with respect to
|\theta|\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{4}
, the proof of Lemma
7.2
is complete.

∎

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Denote by
\hbar=h^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}
as before. The fact that

, up to changing

At the first step, we prove the same estimates (a),(b),(c) for

.
For (b), since
|k|\sim\frac{1}{h}
, we have
\|h^{j}\partial_{y}^{j}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1
for all

.
The derivatives of

in

|\partial_{x}^{j}u_{h}|\lesssim 1+\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq r_{0}}h^{-(j-1)\delta}|(\partial_{x}^{j}F)\big(\frac{r_{0}-|x|}{h^{\delta}};\theta_{h}\big)|,

.
Finally, since

on supp

\displaystyle\|b_{0}^{\prime}(x)\partial_{x}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\big\|(r_{0}-|x|)_{+}^{\gamma-1}F^{\prime}\big(\frac{r_{0}-|x|}{h^{\delta}};\theta_{h}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim h^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\cdot h^{\delta(\gamma-1)}=h^{\frac{\gamma-1/2}{\gamma+2}}=h^{1-\frac{5\delta}{2}}\leq h^{\delta},

(7.11)

thanks to

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\int_{-\pi}^{y}(\partial_{x}a_{0}(x,y^{\prime})-b_{0}^{\prime}(x))dy^{\prime}\Big)\partial_{x}u_{h}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

the same computation as (

\displaystyle C\Big|\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}\chi_{h}^{\prime}\overline{v}\Phi_{h}(x)\chi_{h}v^{\prime\prime}dx\Big|+C\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\chi_{h}^{\prime}(\Phi_{h}\chi_{h})^{\prime}\overline{v}v^{\prime}|dx+C\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\chi_{h}^{\prime}\overline{v}(\Phi_{h}(x)\chi_{h}^{\prime}v)^{\prime}|dx,

(A.5)

where the terms in the third line of the right side is obtained by integration by part of

\Big|\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi_{h}(x)\overline{v}_{1}^{\prime}(\chi_{h}^{\prime}v)^{\prime}dx\Big|.

Here we keep the real part for this term in order to perform some cancellation when replacing
v^{\prime\prime}
by
-\lambda^{2}v+ih^{-1}Wv-r
later on, after using the equation (
A.1
) of
v
. Denote by
\widetilde{I}_{h}=\cup_{j=1}^{l}\widetilde{I}_{j,h}
and
I_{h}=\cup_{j=1}^{l}I_{j,h}
. Using (
A.2
) and the facts that
\lambda^{2}\leq h^{-\delta}
,
W\sim h^{\alpha}
on
\widetilde{I}_{h}
, we can control terms on the right hand side of (
A
) by the sum of following types:

\displaystyle\text{Type I}:\;\mathrm{I}=h^{-\delta}\|v\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{I}_{h}}\|_{L^{2}}\|r\|_{L^{2}}+\|r\|_{L^{2}}\|v^{\prime}\chi_{h}\|_{L^{2}};

\displaystyle\text{Type II}:\;\mathrm{II}=h^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W|vv^{\prime}|\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{I}_{h}}dx+h^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\chi_{h}^{2}|vv^{\prime}|dx;

\displaystyle\text{Type III}:\;\mathrm{III}=h^{-(1+\delta)}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{I}_{h}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We analyze the type II term. By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}\lesssim h^{-1}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v\mathbf{1}_{I_{h}}\|_{L^{2}}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{h}}v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}.

(A.6)

Note that on
I_{h}
,
\Theta(x)=h^{-\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}\lesssim h^{-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{I_{h}}v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq h^{-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Theta(x)e_{0}(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\alpha+2\delta=1
, using Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}\leq\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Theta(x)e_{0}(x)dx+C_{\epsilon}h^{-(1+\delta)}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\forall\epsilon>0.

Plugging into (
A
), we get

\displaystyle\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Theta(x)e_{0}(x)dx\lesssim h^{-(1+\delta)}\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h^{-\delta}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|r\|_{L^{2}}+\|r\|_{L^{2}}\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
\lambda\leq h^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}
, by (a) of Lemma
A.1
,

\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq o(h^{\delta})+\lambda^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq o(h^{-\delta}).

Recall that
\|r\|_{L^{2}}=o(h^{\delta})
and
\|W^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}=o(h^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}})
, we get

\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Theta(x)e_{0}(x)dx\lesssim o(1).

Since we have already shown that
\|v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}=o(1)
, this is a contradiction to our assumption that
\|v\|_{L^{2}}=1
. The proof of Proposition
6.9
is now complete.

Appendix B
Semiclassical pseudo-differential calculus

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
, the symbol class
S^{m}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of smooth functions
c(z,\zeta)
such that

|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\partial_{\zeta}^{\beta}c(z,\zeta)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\zeta\rangle^{m-|\alpha|}.

Given a symbol
c(z,\zeta)
, we associate it with the Weyl quantization
\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{w}(c)
:

f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mapsto\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{w}(f)(z):=\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{d}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}c\big(\frac{z+z^{\prime}}{2},\zeta\big)e^{\frac{i(z-z^{\prime})\cdot\zeta}{h}}f(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}d\zeta.

Most of the time we will use the standard quantization
\mathrm{Op}_{h}(c)
:

\mathrm{Op}_{h}(c)(f)(z):=\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{d}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}c(z,\zeta)e^{\frac{i(z-z^{\prime})\cdot\zeta}{h}}f(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}d\zeta.

An important mapping property is the following theorem due to Calderón-Vaillancourt:

Theorem B.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any function
c
on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
with uniformly bounded derivatives up to order
d
, we have

\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}(c)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|\mathrm{Op}^{w}_{h}(c)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq d}h^{|\beta|}\|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\partial_{\zeta}^{\beta}c\|_{L^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We use frequently the sharp G
\mathring{\mathrm{a}}
rding inequality:

Theorem B.2

(Sharp G
\mathring{\mathrm{a}}
rding’s inequality)
.

Assume that
c\in S^{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
c(z,\zeta)\geq 0
for all
(z,\zeta)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Then there exist
C>0
and
h_{0}>0
such that for all
0<h\leq h_{0}
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\mathrm{Op}_{h}(c)f,f)_{L^{2}}\geq-Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}.

Since we deal with symbols (damping) of limited regularity in this article, we need additional estimates. First we recall the following boundedness property on
L^{2}

Lemma B.1
.

Assume that

, such that for all
|\alpha|\leq d+1

|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}b(x,y,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(|\alpha|+\mu_{0})}.

Then the operator
T_{h}

K_{h}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b(x,y,\xi)e^{\frac{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}{h}}d\xi

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, uniformly in
h\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

The proof with
\mu_{0}=1
can be found in Lemma A.1 of
[
BS20
]
. The same argument works for all
\mu_{0}>0
.
∎

A direct consequence is the following commutator estimates for Lipschitz functions:

Corollary B.2
.

Assume that
\kappa\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
b\in S^{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then there exists
C>0
such that

\|[\kappa,\mathrm{Op}_{h}(b)]\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|[\kappa,\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{w}(b)]\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq Ch.

Moreover generally, for some
m\geq 1
, we have

\|\mathrm{ad}_{\kappa}^{m}(\mathrm{Op}_{h}(b))\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|\mathrm{ad}_{\kappa}^{m}(\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{w}(b))\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq Ch^{m}.

Proof.

We do the proof for the standard quantization. The same proof applied to the Weyl quantization. Denote by
T_{j}=\mathrm{ad}_{\kappa}^{j}(\mathrm{Op}_{h}(b))
and
K_{j}(x,y)
the Schwartz kernel of
T_{j}
. Then

K_{j}(x,y)=(\kappa(x)-\kappa(y))^{j}K_{0}(x,y),

where

K_{0}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b(x,\xi)e^{\frac{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}{h}}d\xi

is the Schwartz kernel of
\mathrm{Op}_{h}(b)
. Since
\kappa\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists
\Psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that

\kappa(x)-\kappa(y)=(x-y)\cdot\Psi(x,y)=\sum_{l=1}^{d}(x_{l}-y_{l})\Psi^{(l)}(x,y),

where
\Psi^{(l)}
is the
l
-th component of
\Psi
.
Thus by integration by part,

\displaystyle K_{m}(x,y)=\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{m}}\frac{h^{m}}{(2\pi h)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\frac{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}{h}}(D_{\xi_{1}}\cdots D_{\xi_{m}}b)(x,\xi)\prod_{k=1}^{m}\Psi^{(j_{k})}(x,y)d\xi.

Applying Lemma
B.1
, we deduce that
\|T_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq Ch^{m}
.
∎

We also need a special version of symbolic calculus, used in the proof of Lemma

:

Lemma B.3
.

Let
\kappa\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

\displaystyle(a)\hskip 8.5359pt\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}(c)b_{1}-\mathrm{Op}_{h}(cb_{1})\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C_{1}h;

\displaystyle(b)\hskip 8.5359pt\big\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}(c)b_{2}-\mathrm{Op}_{h}(cb_{2})-\frac{h}{i}\mathrm{Op}_{h}(\partial_{\xi}c\cdot\partial_{x}b_{2})\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C_{2}h^{2},

where the constants
C_{1}
depend only on
\|\kappa\|_{W^{1,\infty}},\|b_{1}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
and

\|\kappa\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
and
\|b_{2}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}

Proof.

Since
c
does not depend on
\eta

as operators acting on

and prove the one-dimensional estimate. Hence we will not display the dependence in
y

For

\displaystyle c(x,\xi+h\xi^{\prime})=c(x,\xi)+h\xi^{\prime}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\xi}c(x,\xi+th\xi^{\prime})dt,

\displaystyle c_{1}(x,\xi)=c(x,\xi)b_{1}(x)+h\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{1,t}(x,\xi)dt

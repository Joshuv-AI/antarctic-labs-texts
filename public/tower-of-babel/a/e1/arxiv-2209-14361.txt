Metric spaces in which many triangles are degenerate
Vašek Chvátal Ida Kantor

Abstract
Richmond and Richmond ( American Mathematical Monthly 104 (1997), 713–719) proved the following theorem: If, in a metric space with at least five points, all triangles are degenerate, then the space is isometric to a subset of the real line. We prove that the hypothesis is unnecessarily strong: In a metric space on n n points, ( n 3 ) − n + 5 \binom{n}{3}-n+5 arbitrarily placed or 3 ​ ( n − 2 2 ) + 1 3\binom{n-2}{2}+1 suitably placed degenerate triangles suffice.

Results.

Given a metric space
(V,\mbox{\rm dist\/})
, we follow
[
1
]
in writing
[rst]
to signify that

r,s,t
are pairwise distinct points of
V
and
\mbox{\rm dist\/}(r,s)+\mbox{\rm dist\/}(s,t)=\mbox{\rm dist\/}(r,t)
. Following
[
15
]
, we refer to three-point subsets of
V
as
triangles;
if
[rst]
, then the triangle
\{r,s,t\}
is called
degenerate.

Now let
(V,\mbox{\rm dist\/})
be a metric space.
Trivially, if there is a linear order
\preceq
on
V
such that
r\prec s\prec t\;\Rightarrow[rst]
, then all triangles in
V
are degenerate. Richmond and Richmond
[
15
]
proved the converse under a mild lower bound on
\lvert V\rvert
:

Theorem 1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
(V,\mbox{\rm dist\/})
be a metric space such that
\lvert V\rvert\geq 5
. If all triangles in
V
are degenerate, then there is a linear order
\preceq
on
V
such that
r\prec s\prec t\;\Rightarrow[rst]
.

Here, the lower bound on
\lvert V\rvert
cannot be reduced: consider
V=\{a,b,c,d\}
and

\mbox{\rm dist\/}(a,b)\!=\!dist(b,c)\!=\!\mbox{\rm dist\/}(c,d)\!=\!\mbox{\rm dist\/}(d,a)\!=\!1
,
\mbox{\rm dist\/}(a,c)\!=\!\mbox{\rm dist\/}(b,d)\!=\!2
.

The purpose of this note is to prove that the hypothesis of Theorem
1
can be relaxed as soon as
\lvert V\rvert=6
and that it can be relaxed further and further as
\lvert V\rvert
gets larger and larger. To state these results, let us call a set
{\cal E}
of three-point subsets of a set
V
an
anchor in
V
if, for every metric space
(V,\mbox{\rm dist\/})
, the assumption that all triangles in
{\cal E}
are degenerate implies a linear order
\preceq
on
V
such that
r\prec s\prec t\;\Rightarrow[rst]
. In this terminology, Theorem
1
asserts that whenever
\lvert V\rvert=n\geq 5
, the set of all
\binom{n}{3}
three-point subsets of
V
is an anchor in
V
.

Theorem 2
.

If
\lvert V\rvert=n
, then every set of
\binom{n}{3}-n+5
three-point subsets of
V
is an anchor in
V
.

The
\binom{n}{3}-n+5
in Theorem
2
cannot be replaced by
\binom{n}{3}-n+4
. To see this, consider the graph with vertices

1,2,\ldots n
and edges
\{1,3\}
,
\{1,4\}
,
\{2,3\}
,
\{2,4\}
and
\{i,i+1\}
with
i=4,5,\ldots n-1
. In the metric space induced by this graph (in the usual way, where edges have unit lengths), the only nondegenerate triangles are
\{1,2,i\}
with
i=5,6,\ldots n
.

Theorem 3
.

If
\lvert V\rvert=n\geq 5
, then there is an anchor in
V
consisting of
3\binom{n-2}{2}+1
three-point subsets of
V
.

We do not know whether or not the
3\binom{n-2}{2}+1
in Theorem
3
can be reduced.

2
Proofs.

Our arguments involve the following algorithm that, given a set
{\cal E}
of triangles in
V
, produces a certain sequence
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots T_{m}
of pairwise distinct triangles outside
{\cal E}
:

Set
m=0
.

While some six-point subset
S
of
V
contains precisely
19
members of
{\cal E}\cup\{T_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
,

increment
m
by one and then let
T_{m}
be the
20
th three-point subset of
S
.

In an iteration of this algorithm, more than one
S
may be available, and so there may be more than one candidate for
T_{m}
. Nevertheless, candidates that are rejected now remain available in the next iteration and so, when the algorithm terminates, the set
\{T_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
is uniquely determined. We let
{\rm cl}\,{\cal E}
denote its union with
{\cal E}
. The role of this notion is explained by the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
(V,\mbox{\rm dist\/})
be a metric space and let
{\cal E}
be a set of triangles in
V
. If all triangles that belong to
{\cal E}
are degenerate, then all triangles that belong to
{\rm cl}\,{\cal E}
are degenerate.

Most of the work involved in proving Lemma
4
is subsumed in its following special case:

Lemma 5
.

Let
(V,\mbox{\rm dist\/})
be a metric space such that
\lvert V\rvert=6
. If
19
triangles in
V
are degenerate, then all
20
triangles in
V
are degenerate.

Proof.

By assumption, there is a triangle
T
in
V
such that the
19
triangles distinct from
T
are degenerate. In particular, for each of the three elements
\lambda
of
T
, all triangles in
V-\{\lambda\}
are degenerate and so,
by Theorem
1
, there is a linear order
\preceq_{\lambda}
on
V-\{\lambda\}
such that

r\prec_{\lambda}s\prec_{\lambda}t\;\Rightarrow[rst].

(1)

Having chosen a
\mu
in
T
, let us label the elements of
V-T
as
u,v,w
in such a way that

u\prec_{\mu}v\prec_{\mu}w
, and so
[uvw]
. Now for each of the remaining two elements
\nu
of
T
, property (
1
) implies
u\prec_{\nu}v\prec_{\nu}w
or
w\prec_{\nu}v\prec_{\nu}u
; reversing the orders if necessary, we may assume that
u\prec_{\nu}v\prec_{\nu}w
, and so

u\prec_{\lambda}v\prec_{\lambda}w\;\text{ for all three $\lambda$ in $T$.}

(2)

Next, let us label the elements of
T
temporarily as
a,b,c
in such a way that
a\prec_{c}b
and then permanently as
x,y,z
: If
b\prec_{a}c
, then
x=a,y=b,z=c
; else either
x=a,y=c,z=b
(in case
a\prec_{b}c
) or
x=c,y=a,z=b
(in case
c\prec_{b}a
). Now we have

x\prec_{z}y\;\text{ and }\;y\prec_{x}z.

(3)

For future reference, let us note that

the restrictions of
\preceq_{x}
and
\preceq_{z}
on
\{u,v,w,y\}
are identical.

(4)

(Analogous statements apply to
x,y
in place of
x,z
and to
y,z
in place of
x,z
, but we will not need these variations.) To see this, observe that, by virtue of (
2
) and (
1
), our metric space determines the rank of
y
in the restriction of
\preceq_{x}
on
\{u,v,w,y\}
in the same way as it determines the rank of
y
in the restriction of
\preceq_{z}
on
\{u,v,w,y\}
.

The remainder of the proof relies on the fact that

[\alpha\beta\gamma]\text{ and }[\alpha\gamma\delta]\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;[\alpha\beta\delta]\text{ and }[\beta\gamma\delta],

(5)

which has been pointed out by Menger
[
13
]
. (By the way, extensions of (
5
) are discussed in
[
6

Finally, we are ready to prove that the triangle
\{x,y,z\}
is also degenerate. Actually, we are going to prove
[xyz]
. For this purpose, let us distinguish between three cases.

Case 1:

u\prec_{z}x
. By (
3
), we have
u\prec_{z}x\prec_{z}y
, and so
[uxy]
; by (
4
) and (

), we have
u\prec_{x}y\prec_{x}z
, and so
[uyz]
. Now we have
[xyz]

Case 2:

z\prec_{x}w

y\prec_{x}z\prec_{x}w

[yzw]

x\prec_{z}y\prec_{z}w

[xyw]

[xyz]

Case 3:

u\prec_{x}v\prec_{x}w\prec_{x}z

[xuv],[xvw],[vwz],[uvz].

Here we have neither
[vxz]
(else

[xzv]
(else

If

Lemma 7
.

Every
3
-uniform hypergraph with
n

\binom{n}{3}-n+5
hyperedges is weakly
K^{3}_{6}
-saturated.

Proof.

Consider a
3
-uniform hypergraph
(V,{\cal E})
such that
\lvert{\cal E}\rvert\geq\binom{n}{3}-n+5
, where

. Since

\lvert{\cal E}\rvert\leq\binom{n}{3}

consists of all three-point subsets of
V
; now let us assume that

T
of
V

. This is trivial when
T\in{\cal E}
; to prove it when

,
it suffices to find a six-point subset

of
V
such that
T
is the unique three-point subset of
S
not belonging to

{\cal E}
.
For this purpose, take one vertex from each

such that

is a three-point subset of
V

{\cal E}
and

{\cal E}
; since

A note on circular wirelength for hypercubes
Qinghui Liu, Zhiyi Tang

Abstract
. We study embeddings of the n n -dimensional hypercube into the circuit with 2 n 2^{n} vertices. We prove that the circular wirelength attains minimum by gray coding, which is called the CT conjecture by Chavez and Trapp (Discrete Applied Mathematics, 1998). This problem had claimed to be settled by Ching-Jung Guu in her doctor dissertation “The circular wirelength problem for hypercubes” (University of California, Riverside, 1997). Many people argue there are gaps in her proof. We eliminate gaps in her dissertation. Keywords: circular wirelength; hypercube; gray coding. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C60, 68R10.

Let

An embedding (

to

(ii).

\{P_{\eta}(\eta(u),\eta(v))\ :\ P_{\eta}(\eta(u),\eta(v))\mbox{ is a path in $H$ between $\eta(u)$ and }\eta(v)\}.

Note that in this paper, we always take a shortest path from

The wirelength of an embedding

in

Then, the minimum wirelength of

For

, the hypercube of dimension

For any

We want to study the minimal wirelength of

into

Theorem 1.1
.

For any

For each

2.
preliminaries

2.1.
Cubal

Take
S\subset V(Q_{n})
with
|S|=k
, where we write

(6)

k=\sum_{i=1}^{N}2^{c_{i}},\ N\geq 1,\ 0\leq c_{1}<c_{2}<\cdots<c_{N}\leq n,

and
c_{N}=\left\lfloor\log_{2}k\right\rfloor
.
If
S
is a disjoint union of
c_{i}
-subcubes,
i=1,\cdots,N
,
such that each
c_{i}
-subcube lies in a neighbor of every
c_{j}
-subcube for
j<i
, then
S
is called a
k
-cubal
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
S\subset V(Q_{n})
be a cubal with
k
vertices, and write
k
as (
6
). Then

\theta(n,S)=\theta(n,k)=n\cdot k-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left((N-i)2^{c_{i}}+c_{i}2^{c_{i}-1}\right).

As a corollary, we have

(7)

\theta(n,k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\theta(n-1,k)+k&\text{if}\quad 0\leq k\leq 2^{n-1};\\
&\theta(n,2^{n}-k)&\text{if}\quad 2^{n-1}\leq k\leq 2^{n}.\end{array}\right.

and

(8)

\theta(n+1,2k)=2\theta(n,k),\quad 0\leq k\leq 2^{n}.

Lemma 2.2
.

\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n-1}}\theta(n,i)=2^{2n-2}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. For
n=1
,

\sum_{i=0}^{1}\theta(1,i)=\theta(1,0)+\theta(1,1)=1.

Suppose,

\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n-2}}\theta(n-1,i)=2^{2n-4}.

Since, for
2^{n-2}<i\leq 2^{n-1}
,
\theta(n-1,i)=\theta(n-1,2^{n-1}-i)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n-1}}\theta(n,i)&=&\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n-1}}(\theta(n-1,i)+i)\\
&=&2\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n-2}}\theta(n-1,i)-\theta(n-1,2^{n-2})+\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n-1}}i\\
&=&2\cdot 2^{2n-4}-2^{n-2}+\frac{2^{n-1}(1+2^{n-1})}{2}=2^{2n-2}.\end{array}

∎

2.2.
Partition path

We define the
derived network
of the embedding problem as the vertex-weighted graph

D=(V(D),E(D)),

where
V(D)
consists of all subsets of
V(Q_{n})
of cardinality
2^{n-1}
,
and
e\in E(D)
with
e=\{U,W\},
if
|U\Delta W|=2
, where
|U\Delta W|=|U\backslash W|+|W\backslash U|
.
The weight of each vertex
U\in V(D)
is
\theta(n,U)
.

For any
U,W\in V(D)
, define

d_{D}(U,W):=|U\Delta W|.

It is direct to verify that
d_{D}
is a metric on
V(D)
.

We call a simple path

F=(F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{2^{n-1}},F_{1}^{c})

a
partition path
of length
2^{n-1}
in
D
,
if it start from any vertex set
P_{1}
and end at its complement
F_{1}^{c}
,
where for a subset
S\subset V(Q_{n})
,
S^{c}=V(Q_{n})\backslash S
.

By definition, for any partition path
F=(F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{2^{n-1}},F_{1}^{c})
,

\bigcup_{i=1}^{2^{n-1}}(F_{i}\backslash F_{i+1})\cup(F_{i+1}\backslash F_{i})=V(Q_{n}),

where
F_{2^{n-1}+1}=F_{1}^{c}
.
Hence the partition path
F
corresponds to an embedding
\eta_{F}:V(Q_{n})\rightarrow V(C_{2^{n}})

in the following way, for
1\leq i\leq 2^{n-1}
,

\eta_{F}(F_{i}\backslash F_{i+1})=i,\ \text{and}\ \ \eta_{F}(F_{i+1}\backslash F_{i})=i+2^{n-1},

and,

F_{i}=\eta_{F}^{-1}\{i,i+1,\cdots,2^{n-1}+i-1\}.

By (
1
),

(9)

WL(Q_{n},C_{2^{n}};\eta_{F})=\theta(n,F_{1})+\theta(n,F_{2})+\cdots+\theta(n,F_{2^{n-1}}).

We say the partition path
F=(F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{2^{n-1}},F_{1}^{c})
has weight
WL(Q_{n},C_{2^{n}};\eta_{F})
.

By the above analysis, partition paths and embeddings are 1-1 corresponding.
Solving the circular wirelength problem is the same as finding a partition path
in the derived network that has minimum weight.

2.3.
The type

We discuss some properties of type defined in (
2
).
A subset
S\subset V(Q_{n})
with
|S|=2^{n-1}
is called of small type, if
0\leq\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)\leq 2^{n-3}
;
otherwise, it is called of big type.

For a subset
S\subset V(Q_{n})
with
|S|=k
and
\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)=t
. By definition, there is a half plane
H\in\mathscr{H}_{n}
, such that

|S\cap H|=t
and
|S\cap H^{c}|=k-t
. Let

A=\{\{u,v\}\in E(Q_{n})\ :\ u\in S,v\not\in S\}.

Then
|A\cap E(Q_{n})|_{H}|\geq\theta(n-1,t)
,
|A\cap E(Q_{n})|_{H^{c}}|\geq\theta(n-1,k-t)
, and

|A\cap\{\{u,v\}\in E(Q_{n})\ :\ u\in H,v\not\in H\}|\geq k-2t.

So, we have (see also
[
6
]
)

(10)

\theta(n,k,t)\geq\theta(n-1,t)+\theta(n-1,k-t)+k-2t.

Especially, as in (
7
), for
0\leq t\leq 2^{n-3}
,

(11)

\theta(n,2^{n-1},t)\geq 2\theta(n-2,t)+2^{n-1}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
S\subset V(Q_{n})
with
|S|=2^{n-1}
and
\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)=t
.
If
t<2^{n-3}
, then there exists a unique half plane
H\in\mathscr{H}_{n}
such that

|S\cap H|=t,

and for any other half plane
H^{\prime}\in\mathscr{H}_{n}
,

|S\cap H^{\prime}|\geq 2^{n-2}-t>2^{n-3}.

Proof.

By definition of type, there is a half plane
H
such that

|S\cap H|=t
. Then

|S\cap H^{c}|=2^{n-1}-t>2^{n-2}.

Take any other half plane
H^{\prime}\in\mathscr{H}_{n}
with
H^{\prime}\neq H
and
H^{\prime}\neq H^{c}
. We have

|S\cap H^{c}|=|S\cap H^{c}\cap H^{\prime}|+|S\cap H^{c}\cap(H^{\prime})^{c}|.

Since

|S\cap H^{c}\cap(H^{\prime})^{c}|\leq|H^{c}\cap(H^{\prime})^{c}|=2^{n-2},

we have

|S\cap H^{c}\cap H^{\prime}|=|S\cap H^{c}|-|S\cap H^{c}\cap(H^{\prime})^{c}|\geq 2^{n-1}-t-2^{n-2}=2^{n-2}-t.

Therefore,

|S\cap H^{\prime}|\geq|S\cap H^{c}\cap H^{\prime}|\geq 2^{n-2}-t>2^{n-3}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
S\subset V(Q_{n})
with
|S|=2^{n-1}
.
If there exists a half plane
H\in\mathscr{H}_{n}
such that

|S\cap H|\leq 2^{n-3},
then
\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)=|S\cap H|
.

Proof.

If
|S\cap H|<2^{n-3}
, then by Lemma
2.3
,
\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)=|S\cap H|
.
Assume
|S\cap H|=2^{n-3}
.
Suppose
\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)<|S\cap H|
, then
\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)<2^{n-3}
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
|S\cap H|>2^{n-3}
, which is a contradiction. So, we have
\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(S)=|S\cap H|
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Assume
U,W
are neighbors in
V(D)
and are both of type strictly smaller than
2^{n-3}
.
There exists a unique half plane
H\in\mathscr{H}_{n}
such that

|U\cap H|=\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(U),\quad|W\cap H|=\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(W).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a half plane
H
such that

|U\cap H|=\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(U).

Since
U
,
W
are neighbors, i.e.
|U\Delta W|=2
, we have

|W\cap H|\leq|U\cap H|+1\leq 2^{n-3}.

By Corollary
2.4
,
|W\cap H|=\mathop{\rm Type}\nolimits(W)
.
∎

Proposition 2.6

(

Let
F
be a partition path and

T_{F}=(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{2^{n-1}})

be its type sequence. We have

(1)

for any
1\leq i<2^{n-1}
,
|t_{i}-t_{i+1}|\leq 1
;

(2)

there exist at least two
i
with
1\leq i\leq 2^{n-1}
such that
t_{i}\geq 2^{n-3}
.

Proof.

(1) We only prove
|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq 1
. The others are same.
Without loose of generality, assume
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
.
Then there exists a half plane
H\in\mathscr{H}_{n}
such that

t_{1}=|H\cap F_{1}|.

So,

|H\cap(F_{1}\cap F_{2})|=|H\cap F_{1}|-|H\cap(F_{1}\backslash F_{2})|\leq t_{1}.

Due to
|F_{1}\Delta F_{2}|=2
and
|F_{1}|=|F_{2}|
, we have

|F_{1}\backslash F_{2}|=|F_{2}\backslash F_{1}|=1

Moreover,

t_{2}\leq|H\cap F_{2}|=|H\cap(F_{1}\cap F_{2})|+|H\cap(F_{2}\backslash F_{1})|\leq t_{1}+1,

so
t_{2}-t_{1}=1
or 0.

(2) We only need to prove that for any
1\leq i\leq 2^{n-2}
, there exists
j
with
i\leq j<i+2^{n-2}
such that
t_{j}\geq 2^{n-3}
.
Otherwise, suppose, for all
i\leq j<i+2^{n-2}
,
t_{j}<2^{n-3}
.
By Corollary
2.5
, there exists a half plane
H
such that, for all
i\leq j<i+2^{n-2}
,

|F_{j}\cap H|=t_{j},

and hence
d_{D}(F_{j},H^{c})=2|F_{j}\backslash H^{c}|=2|F_{j}\cap H|=2t_{j}
.
Since
d_{D}
is a metric, we have

d_{D}(F_{i},F_{i+2^{n-2}})\leq d_{D}(F_{i},H^{c})+d_{D}(F_{i+2^{n-2}},H^{c})<2^{n-1}.

This contradicts the fact that
d_{D}(F_{i},F_{i+2^{n-2}})=2^{n-1}.

∎

2.4.
The Gray code

A gray code is an ordering of
2^{n}
binary numbers such that only one bit changes from one entry to the next.
It is also a Hamilton cycle on an
n
-dimensional hypercube.
Each gray code corresponds to an embedding from
Q_{n}
to
C_{2^{n}}
.
The following embedding
\xi_{n}
corresponds to reflected Gray code.
Take any
n
-tuple
x=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.
Let
y=y_{1}\cdots y_{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
be defined as, for any
1\leq i\leq n
,

y_{i}=0
, if
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{i}
is even,
y_{i}=1
otherwise. Then

\xi_{n}(x)=(y)_{2}+1,

where
(y)_{2}
is the integer with binary expansion
y
.

For any
1\leq i\leq n
, define mappings

g_{n,i,0},g_{n,i,1}:\ \{0,1\}^{n-1}\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{n},

such that, for any
x_{1}\cdots x_{n-1}\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}
,

(12)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}g_{n,i,0}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n-1})&=&x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}0x_{i}\cdots x_{n-1},\\
g_{n,i,1}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n-1})&=&x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}1x_{i}\cdots x_{n-1}.\end{array}

For any
1\leq i\leq n
, define

\begin{array}[]{rcl}H_{n,i,0}&=&\{x_{1}\cdots x_{i}\cdots x_{n}\in V(Q_{n}):x_{i}=0\}=g_{n,i,0}(\{0,1\}^{n-1}),\\
H_{n,i,1}&=&\{x_{1}\cdots x_{i}\cdots x_{n}\in V(Q_{n}):x_{i}=1\}=g_{n,i,1}(\{0,1\}^{n-1}).\end{array}

Then the set of half planes can be written as

\mathscr{H}_{n}=\{H_{n,i,j}\ :\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ j=0,1\}.

By definition of
\xi_{n}
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\xi_{n}(H_{n,1,0})&=&\{1,\cdots,2^{n-1}\},\\
\xi_{n}(H_{n,1,1})&=&\{2^{n-1}+1,\cdots,2^{n}\},\\
\xi_{n}(H_{n,2,0})&=&\{1,\cdots,2^{n-2},2^{n}-2^{n-2}+1,\cdots,2^{n}\},\\
\xi_{n}(H_{n,2,1})&=&\{2^{n-2}+1,\cdots,2^{n}-2^{n-2}\}.\end{array}

For any

, let

Then

is a partition path in

corresponding to the reflected Gray code.

Take any

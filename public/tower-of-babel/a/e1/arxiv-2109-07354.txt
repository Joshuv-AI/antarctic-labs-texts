The Replica Symmetric Formula for the SK Model Revisited
Christian Brennecke, Horng-Tzer YauInstitute for Applied Mathematics, University of Bonn, Endenicher Allee 60, 53115 Bonn, GermanyDepartment of Mathematics, Harvard University, One Oxford Street, Cambridge MA 02138, USA

Abstract
We provide a simple extension of Bolthausen’s Morita type proof of the replica symmetric formula [E. Bolthausen, Stat. Mech. of Classical and Disordered Systems, pp. 63-93 (2018)] for the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model and prove the replica symmetry for all ( β , h ) (\beta,h) that satisfy β 2 ​ E ​ sech 2 ⁡ ( β ​ q ​ Z + h ) ≤ 1 \beta^{2}E\sech^{2}(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)\leq 1 , where q = E ​ tanh 2 ⁡ ( β ​ q ​ Z + h ) q=E\tanh^{2}(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h) . Compared to [E. Bolthausen, Stat. Mech. of Classical and Disordered Systems, pp. 63-93 (2018)], the key of the argument is to apply the conditional second moment method to a suitably reduced partition function.



1
Introduction

We study systems of
N
spins
\sigma_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, with values in
\{-1,1\}
and with the Hamiltonian
H_{N}:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

H_{N}(\sigma)=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}.

(1.1)

The interactions
\{g_{ij}\}
are i.i.d. centered Gaussians of variance
1/N
for
i\neq j
and we set
g_{ii}\equiv 0
.
\beta\geq 0
denotes the inverse temperature and
h>0
the external field strength.

Eq. (
1.1
) corresponds to the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) spin glass model
[
21
]
and we are interested in its free energy
f_{N}
at high temperature, where

f_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N},\,\,\text{ }\,\,Z_{N}=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}.

(1.2)

The mathematical understanding of the SK model has required substantial efforts until the famous Parisi formula
[
18
,
19
]
was rigorously established by Guerra
[
10
]
and Talagrand
[
22
]
. Later, Panchenko
[
15
,
17
]
gave another proof based on the ultrametricity of generic models. For a thorough introduction to the topic, we refer to
[
14
,
23
,
24
,
16
]
.

Despite the validity of the Parisi formula, it is an interesting question to prove the replica symmetry of the SK model at high temperature, as predicted by de Almeida and Thouless
[
3
]
. Replica symmetry is expected for all
(\beta,h)
that satisfy

\beta^{2}E\sech^{4}(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)<1,

(1.3)

where
q
denotes the unique solution of the self-consistent equation

q=E\tanh^{2}(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h).

(1.4)

In both cases
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
denotes a standard Gaussian and
E
the expectation over
Z
.

In the special case that the external field in direction of
\sigma_{i}
is a centered Gaussian random variable,
h_{i}=hg_{i}
for i.i.d.
g_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
(independent of the
g_{ij}
), replica symmetry has been recently shown in
[
9
]
for all
(\beta,h)
that satisfy (
1.3
) (in which case
h
in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) is replaced by
hZ^{\prime}
for some
Z^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
independent of
Z
); see also
[
20
]
for previous results in this case. For Hamiltonians
H_{N}
as in (
1.1
) (or, more generally, Hamiltonians with non-centered random external field), however, replica symmetry is to date only known above the AT line up to a bounded region in the
(\beta,h)
-phase diagram. This has been analyzed in
[
13
]
. Like
[
9
]
, this analysis is based on the Parisi variational problem and we refer to
[
13
]
for the details. For previously obtained results based on the Parisi formula, see also
[
24
, Chapter 13]
.

In this note, instead of analyzing the high temperature regime in view of the Parisi variational problem, we give a simple extension of Bolthausen’s argument
[
6
]
and prove the replica symmetric formula for all
(\beta,h)
that satisfy

\beta^{2}E\sech^{2}(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)\leq 1.

(1.5)

Although (
1.5
) is clearly stronger than the condition (
1.3
), it already covers a fairly large region of the high temperature regime, see Fig.
1
for a schematic. It improves upon the inverse temperature range from
[
6
]
, where
\beta
was assumed to be sufficiently small.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
(\beta,h)
satisfies (
1.5
), then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}=\log 2+E\log\cosh(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}(1-q)^{2}.

(1.6)

Remarks:

1)

From
[
11
]
, it is well-known that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}
exists and that almost surely
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}
.

2)

It follows from the results of
[
10
]
that the right hand side in (
1.6
) provides an upper bound to the free energy
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}
, for all inverse temperatures and external fields
(\beta,h)
. To establish Theorem
1.1
, it is therefore sufficient to prove that the right hand side in (
1.6
) provides a lower bound to
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}
.

We conclude this introduction with a quick heuristic outline of the main argument. To this end, consider first the case
h=0
where the critical temperature corresponds to
\beta=1
. In this case, it is straight forward (see e.g.
[
7
, Chapter 1, Section 3]
) to see that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}Z_{N}=\log 2+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4},\hskip 14.22636pt\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}Z_{N}^{2}=2\log 2+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}

for all
\beta<1
. The replica symmetric formula thus follows from the second moment method, using the Gaussian concentration of the free energy. In fact, for
h=0
, also the fluctuations of
Z_{N}/\mathbb{E}Z_{N}
have been known for a long time
[
2
]
.

Clearly, it would be desirable to extend this simple argument to the case
h>0
, but a direct application of the second moment method does not work here. However, as suggested in
[
6
]
, one may hope to obtain a model similar to the case
h=0
by centering the spins around suitable magnetizations and viewing
Z_{N}
, up to normalization, as an average over the corresponding coin-tossing measure. To center the spins correctly, recall that at high temperature one expects the TAP equations
[
25
]
to hold, that is

m_{i}\approx\tanh\Big(h+\beta\sum_{j\neq i}\bar{g}_{ij}m_{j}-\beta^{2}(1-q)m_{i}\Big)\hskip 14.22636pt(\text{for }i=1,\dots,N),

(1.7)

where
\bar{g}_{ij}=(g_{ij}+g_{ji})/\sqrt{2}
and
m_{i}=Z_{N}^{-1}\sum_{\sigma}\sigma_{i}\,e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}
. The validity of (
1.7
) is known for sufficiently small
\beta
(see
[
23
,
8
]
and, more recently,
[
1
]
; see also
[
12
]
on the TAP equations for generic models, valid at all temperatures) and expected to be true under (
1.3
). In
[
5
,
6
]
, Bolthausen has provided an iterative construction
(\textbf{m}^{(s)})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
of the solution to (
1.7
) that converges (in a suitable sense) in the full high temperature regime (
1.3
). The main result of
[
6
]
is a novel proof of (
1.6
) for
\beta
small enough, based on a conditional second moment argument, given the approximate solutions
(\textbf{m}^{(s)})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
. It has remained an open question, however, if the approach can be extended to the region (
1.3
).

In this note, while we are not able to resolve this question for all
(\beta,h)
satisfying (
1.3
), we improve the range of
(\beta,h)
to (
1.5
) as follows.
[
5
,
6
]
show, roughly speaking, that
\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\approx\sum_{s=1}^{k}\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)}
for certain orthonormal vectors
\phi^{(s)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and deterministic numbers
\gamma_{s}\,(\,\approx\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle
with high probability), where
\langle\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\rangle=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}y_{i}
for
\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. One also has
\textbf{g}=\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}\rho^{(s)}
for the interaction
\textbf{g}=(g_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
, where the
\rho^{(s)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
are measurable w.r.t.
(\textbf{m}^{(s)})_{s\leq k+1}
and where the modified interaction
\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}
is Gaussian, conditionally on the
(\textbf{m}^{(s)})_{s\leq k+1}
, with the property that
\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)}=0
. Up to negligible errors, one obtains with
\widehat{\sigma}=\sigma-\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}
that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}\approx&\,\log 2+E\log\cosh(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)\\
&+\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\exp\bigg[\frac{N\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\big\langle\widehat{\sigma},\textbf{g}^{(k)}\widehat{\sigma}\big\rangle+N\mathcal{O}\big(\max_{s}|\gamma_{s}-\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle|\big)\bigg],\end{split}

(1.8)

where
p_{\text{free}}
denotes the product measure for which
\sum_{\sigma}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\,\sigma=\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}
. A simple observation is now that we can ignore the error
N\mathcal{O}(\max_{s}|\gamma_{s}-\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle|)
in (
1.8
) by restricting the modified partition function to those
\sigma
with
\max_{s}|\gamma_{s}-\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle|\approx 0
. Note that the probability of the complement of this set is small under
p_{\text{free}}
, because
\gamma_{s}\approx\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle
. This yields a simple lower bound on
\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}
and we can apply the conditional second moment argument to the restricted partition function. We show that its first conditional moment equals
\beta^{2}(1-q)^{2}/4
(up to negligible errors) in the full high temperature regime (
1.3
). To dominate its second moment by the square of the first, on the other hand, we need to impose the stronger condition (
1.5
).

Notice that imposing similar orthogonality restrictions on the partition function has been proved useful before for obtaining lower bounds on the free energy, like in the TAP analysis of the spherical SK model
[
4
]
.

Figure 1:
Schematic of the
(T,h)
phase diagram, where
T=\frac{1}{\beta}
denotes the temperature. In the blue region, whose boundary corresponds to the AT line (
1.3
), the SK model is known to be replica symmetry breaking. The boundary of the green region corresponds to the condition (
1.5
). Theorem
1.1
proves the replica symmetry in the green region.

Although (
1.5
) covers already a comparably large region of the high temperature phase as schematically shown in Fig.
1
, it remains an open question whether the second moment argument can be extended to the full high temperature regime (
1.3
), see also related comments in
[
6
, Section 6]
.

The paper is structured as follows. In the next two sections we set up the notation and recall Bolthausen’s iterative construction of the magnetization
[
5
,
6
]
. In Section
4
, we define the reduced partition function and compute its first and second moments. In the last section, we apply the conditional second moment method to prove Theorem
1.1
.

2
Notation

In this section, we introduce basic notation and conventions. We follow closely
[
6
]
.

We usually denote vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by boldface or greek letters. If
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define
g(\textbf{x})
in the componentwise sense. By
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote the normalized inner product

\langle\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}y_{i}

and by
\|\cdot\|=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
the induced norm. We also normalize the tensor product
\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{y}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
of two vectors
\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
so that for all
\textbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{y})(\textbf{z})=\langle\textbf{y},\textbf{z}\rangle\,\textbf{x}.

Given a matrix
\textbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
{\textbf{A}}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
denotes its transpose and
\bar{\textbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
denotes its symmetrization

\bar{\textbf{A}}=\frac{\textbf{A}+\textbf{A}^{T}}{\sqrt{2}}.

We mostly use the letters
Z,Z^{\prime},Z_{1},Z_{2},
etc. to denote standard Gaussian random variables independent of the disorder
\{g_{ij}\}
and independent from one another. When we average over such Gaussians, we denote the corresponding expectation by
E
, to distinguish it from the expectation
\mathbb{E}
with respect to the disorder
\{g_{ij}\}
. Unless specified otherwise, we consider all Gaussians to be centered.

Finally, given two sequences of random variables
(X_{N})_{N\geq 1},(Y_{N})_{N\geq 1}
that may depend on parameters like
\beta,h,
etc. we say that

X_{N}\simeq Y_{N}

if and only if there exist positive constants
c,C>0
, which may depend on the parameters, but which are independent of
N
, such that for every
t>0
we have

\mathbb{P}(|X_{N}-Y_{N}|>t)\leq Ce^{-ct^{2}N}.

3
Bolthausen’s Construction of the Local Magnetizations

In this section, we recall Bolthausen’s iterative construction of the solution to the TAP equations
[
5
,
6
]
and list the properties that we will need for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We follow here the conventions of
[
6
]
and we refer to
[
6
, Sections 2, 4 & 5]
for the proofs of the following statements.

First of all, we define three sequences
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(\gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Set

\alpha_{1}=\sqrt{q}\gamma_{1},\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma_{1}=E\tanh(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h),\hskip 14.22636pt\Gamma_{1}^{2}=\gamma_{1}^{2},

where here and in the following
q
denotes the unique solution of (
1.4
). Then, we define
\psi:[0,q]\to[0,q]
by

\psi(t)=E\tanh(\beta\sqrt{t}Z+\beta\sqrt{q-t}Z^{\prime}+h)\tanh(\beta\sqrt{t}Z+\beta\sqrt{q-t}Z^{\prime\prime}+h)

and set recursively

\alpha_{k}=\psi(\alpha_{k-1}),\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma_{k}=\frac{\alpha_{k}-\Gamma^{2}_{k-1}}{\sqrt{q-\Gamma^{2}_{k-1}}},\hskip 14.22636pt\Gamma_{k}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{k}^{2}.

The following lemma collects important properties of
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(\gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
5
, Lemma 2.2, Corollary 2.3, Lemma 2.4]
,
[
6
, Lemma 2]
)

1)

\psi
is strictly increasing and convex in
[0,q]
with
0<\psi(0)<\psi(q)=q
. If (
1.3
) is satisfied, then
q
is the unique fixed point of
\psi
in
[0,q]
.

2)

The sequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing and
\alpha_{k}>0
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If (
1.3
) is satisfied, then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_{k}=q
and if (
1.3
) is satisfied with a strict inequality, the convergence is exponentially fast.

3)

For all
k\geq 2
, we have that
0<\Gamma_{k-1}^{2}<\alpha_{k}<q
and that
0<\gamma_{k}<\sqrt{q-\Gamma_{k-1}^{2}}
. If (
1.3
) is true, then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{k}^{2}=q
and, as a consequence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{k}=0
.

Next, we recall Bolthausen’s modified interaction matrix. We define

\textbf{g}^{(1)}=\textbf{g},\hskip 14.22636pt\phi^{(1)}=\textbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\hskip 14.22636pt\textbf{m}^{(1)}=\sqrt{q}\textbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Assuming
\textbf{g}^{(s)},\phi^{(s)},\textbf{m}^{(s)}
are defined for
1\leq s\leq k
, we set

\zeta^{(s)}=\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(s)}\phi^{(s)}

and we define the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}_{k}
through

\mathcal{G}_{k}=\sigma\big(\textbf{g}^{(s)}\phi^{(s)},(\textbf{g}^{(s)})^{T}\phi^{(s)}:1\leq s\leq k\big).

Expectations with respect to
\mathcal{G}_{k}
are denoted by
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}
. Furthermore, we set

\begin{split}\textbf{h}^{(k+1)}&=h\textbf{1}+\beta\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\gamma_{s}\zeta^{(s)}+\beta\sqrt{q-\Gamma_{k-1}^{2}}\zeta^{(k)},\hskip 14.22636pt\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}=\tanh(\textbf{h}^{(k+1)}),\\
\phi^{(k+1)}&=\frac{\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle\phi^{(s)}}{\Big\|\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle\phi^{(s)}\Big\|}\end{split}

(3.1)

and we note that
\phi^{(k+1)}
is
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
well-defined for all
k
if
k<N

[
6
, Lemma 5]
. Finally, the modified interaction matrix
\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}
is defined by

\begin{split}\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}=&\,\textbf{g}^{(k)}-\rho^{(k)},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{where}\\
\rho^{(k)}=&\,\textbf{g}^{(k)}\phi^{(k)}\otimes\phi^{(k)}+\phi^{(k)}\otimes(\textbf{g}^{(k)})^{T}\phi^{(k)}-\langle\textbf{g}^{(k)}\phi^{(s)},\phi^{(k)}\rangle\,\phi^{(k)}\otimes\phi^{(k)}.\end{split}

In particular, this means that
\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}
is equal to

\begin{split}\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}=\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k)}-\bar{\rho}^{(k)},\hskip 14.22636pt\bar{\rho}^{(k)}=\zeta^{(k)}\otimes\phi^{(k)}+\phi^{(k)}\otimes\zeta^{(k)}-\langle\zeta^{(k)},\phi^{(k)}\rangle\,\phi^{(k)}\otimes\phi^{(k)}.\end{split}

It is clear that
(\phi^{(s)})_{s=1}^{k}
forms an orthonormal sequence of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and we denote by
\textbf{P}^{(k)}
and
\textbf{Q}^{(k)}
the corresponding orthogonal projections in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, that is

\textbf{P}^{(k)}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\phi^{(s)}\otimes\phi^{(s)}=(P^{(k)}_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N},\hskip 14.22636pt\textbf{Q}^{(k)}=\textbf{1}-\textbf{P}^{(k)}=(Q^{(k)}_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

By
[
6
, Lemma 3]
,
\textbf{m}^{(k)}
and
\phi^{(k)}
are
\mathcal{G}_{k-1}
-measurable for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and we also have that

\textbf{g}^{(k)}\phi^{(s)}=(\textbf{g}^{(k)})^{T}\phi^{(s)}=\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k)}\phi^{(s)}=0,\,\,\forall\,\,s<k.

Proposition 3.2
.

(
[
6
, Prop. 4]
)

1)

Conditionally on
\mathcal{G}_{k-2}
,
\textbf{g}^{(k)}
and

2)

\mathcal{G}_{k-2}

\textbf{g}^{(k)}
is independent of
\mathcal{G}_{k-1}
. In particular, conditionally on

3)

Conditionally on
\mathcal{G}_{k-1}
, the random variables

are Gaussian with

The main result of

]
is summarized in the following proposition.

.

(
[
5
, Prop. 2.5]

[

For every

and

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[

1)

4
Conditional Moments of Reduced Partition Function

Using Bolthausen’s magnetizations, we compute in this section the first two conditional moments of a suitably reduced partition function onto a subset
S\subset\{-1,1\}^{N}
defined below. This will suffice to establish Theorem
1.1
, as explained in Section
5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. We define the set
S_{\varepsilon,k}\subset\{-1,1\}^{N}
through

\begin{split}S_{\varepsilon,k}=\Big\{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}:|\langle\sigma-\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle|\leq\varepsilon/k,\,\,\,\forall\,\,1\leq s\leq k\Big\},\end{split}

(4.1)

with
\phi^{(s)},\gamma_{s}
from Section
3
. We define the reduced partition function
Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})
by

\begin{split}Z^{(k+1)}_{N}(S_{\varepsilon,k})&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\exp\bigg(\frac{N\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\langle\sigma,\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle\bigg)\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\exp\bigg(\frac{N\beta}{2}\langle\sigma,\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle\bigg),\end{split}

(4.2)

where
p_{\text{free}}:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to(0,1)
denotes the coin-tossing measure

p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\exp\big(h_{i}^{(k+1)}\sigma_{i}\big)}{\cosh\big(h_{i}^{(k+1)}\big)}.

(4.3)

The following lemma records that
p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k}^{c})
is exponentially small in
N
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
S_{\varepsilon,k}
and
p_{\text{free}}
be defined as in (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), respectively. Then, there exist
c,C>0
, independent of
N
and
\varepsilon
, such that

p_{\emph{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\geq 1-Ce^{-cN\varepsilon^{2}}.

(4.4)

Proof.

By a standard union bound, we have that

\begin{split}p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k}^{c})\leq&\,k\max_{s=1,\dots,k}p_{\text{free}}\Big(\Big\{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}:\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle-\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle>\frac{\varepsilon}{k}\Big\}\Big)\\
&\,+k\max_{s=1,\dots,k}p_{\text{free}}\Big(\Big\{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}:\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle-\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle>\frac{\varepsilon}{k}\Big\}\Big),\end{split}

which implies

\begin{split}&p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k}^{c})\\
&\leq k\max_{s=1,\dots,k}\inf_{\lambda\geq 0}\exp\bigg[-N\bigg(\frac{\lambda\varepsilon}{k}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\frac{\cosh\big(h_{i}^{(k+1)}+\lambda\phi_{i}^{(s)}\big)}{\cosh\big(h_{i}^{(k+1)}\big)}+\lambda\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle\bigg)\bigg]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+k\max_{s=1,\dots,k}\inf_{\lambda\geq 0}\exp\bigg[-N\bigg(\frac{\lambda\varepsilon}{k}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\frac{\cosh\big(h_{i}^{(k+1)}-\lambda\phi_{i}^{(s)}\big)}{\cosh\big(h_{i}^{(k+1)}\big)}-\lambda\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle\bigg)\bigg].\\
\end{split}

Using the pointwise bound
\log\cosh(x+y)\leq\log\cosh(x)+y\tanh(x)+\frac{y^{2}}{2}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\langle\phi^{(s)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle=1
and the identity
\tanh\big(\textbf{h}^{(k+1)}\big)=\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}
, we obtain

\begin{split}p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k}^{c})\leq&\,2k\inf_{\lambda\geq 0}\exp\bigg[-\frac{N\lambda\varepsilon}{k}+\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{2}\bigg]=2ke^{-N\varepsilon^{2}/(2k^{2})}.\end{split}

This concludes (
4.4
) for
c=c_{k}=1/(2k^{2}),C=C_{k}=2k
. ∎

We notice that the constants
c,C>0
in (
4.4
) are independent of the realization of the disorder
\{g_{ij}\}
. Thus,
a.s.
in the disorder (so that
\phi^{(s)}
,
s=1,\dots,k
, and hence
\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}
as well as
p_{\text{free}}
are well-defined),
S_{\varepsilon,k}\neq\emptyset
for
N
large enough.

The next lemma determines the first conditional moment of the reduced partition function
Z^{(k+1)}_{N}(S_{\varepsilon,k})
and is valid in the full high temperature regime (
1.3
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
S_{\varepsilon,k}
,
Z^{(k+1)}_{N}(S_{\varepsilon,k})
and
p_{\text{free}}
be as in (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively. Assume that
(\beta,h)
satisfy the AT condition (
1.3
). Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\,Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}(1-q)^{2}\bigg|=0

(4.5)

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, we have that

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}Z^{(k+1)}_{N}(S_{\varepsilon,k})&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\exp\bigg(\frac{\beta^{2}N^{2}}{4}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle\sigma,\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle^{2}\bigg)\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{4}\bigg(1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg].\end{split}

Centering around
\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}
yields

\begin{split}1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}&=1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}-2\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma-\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma-\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}&\sup_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}\bigg|\bigg(1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\bigg)-\bigg(1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\bigg)\bigg|\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\leq C\sup_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}\sum_{s=1}^{k}|\langle\sigma-\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle|\leq C\varepsilon\end{split}

for some
C>0
, independent of
N
and
k
. This implies with Lemma
4.1
that

\begin{split}&\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}Z^{(k+1)}_{N}(S_{\varepsilon,k})-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\bigg(1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg|\leq C\beta^{2}\varepsilon+\frac{C}{N}|\log(1-Ce^{-cN\varepsilon^{2}})|.\end{split}

Moreover, by Proposition
3.3
, we have that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}=\Gamma_{k}^{2}

in
L^{p}(d\mathbb{P})
, for any
p\in[1;\infty)
, so that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}Z^{(k+1)}_{N}(S_{\varepsilon,k})-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\big(1-\Gamma_{k}^{2}\big)^{2}\bigg|\leq C\beta^{2}\varepsilon.

Finally, since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{k}^{2}=q
under the AT condition (
1.3
), by Lemma
3.1
, we let
N\to\infty
, then
k\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
which implies

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\,Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}(1-q)^{2}\bigg|=0.

∎

The following lemma computes the second conditional moment of
Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})
under the stronger high temperature condition (
1.5
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
S_{\varepsilon,k}
,
Z^{(k+1)}_{N}(S_{\varepsilon,k})
and
p_{\text{free}}
be as in (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively. Assume that
(\beta,h)
satisfy the condition (
1.5
). Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\,\Big[\big(Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\big)^{2}\Big]-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}(1-q)^{2}\bigg|=0.

(4.6)

Proof.

Proceeding as in the previous proposition, we compute

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\,\Big[\big(Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\big)^{2}\Big]\\
&=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)p_{\text{free}}(\tau)\exp\bigg(\frac{\beta^{2}N^{2}}{4}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\Big(\langle\sigma,\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle+\langle\tau,\textbf{g}^{(k+1)}\tau\rangle\Big)^{2}\bigg)\\
&=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)p_{\text{free}}(\tau)\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{4}\bigg(1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}N}{4}\bigg(1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\tau,\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\\
&\hskip 108.12054pt\times\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}\bigg(\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma,\phi^{(s)}\rangle\langle\phi^{(s)}\tau\rangle\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\\
&=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)p_{\text{free}}(\tau)\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}\langle\sigma,\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle^{2}\bigg]\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt\times\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}\bigg(1-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{2}+N\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2}\varepsilon)\bigg].\end{split}

Arguing as in the previous lemma, we therefore see that it is enough to show that

{\mathbb{E}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)p_{\text{free}}(\tau)\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}\langle\sigma,\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle^{2}\bigg]\bigg|

vanishes when
N\to\infty
. To this end, recall that by definition of
S_{\varepsilon,k}
, we have that

\sup_{\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}\big|\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\|^{2}-\big(1-\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\textbf{P}^{(k)}\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\rangle\big)\big|\leq C\varepsilon

(4.7)

for some
C>0
independent of
N
and
k
. For fixed
\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}
, we then have

\begin{split}p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k})&\leq\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}\langle\sigma,\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle^{2}\bigg]\\
&\leq p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k})+\int_{0}^{1}dt\,N\beta^{2}t\,e^{\frac{N}{2}\beta^{2}t^{2}}\,p_{\text{free}}\big(\big\{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}:|\langle\sigma,\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle|>t\big\}\big).\end{split}

Setting
\lambda=t/(1-q)
and using that
\log\cosh(x+y)\leq\log\cosh(x)+y\tanh(x)+y^{2}/2
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we can estimate the tail probability in the integral by

\begin{split}&p_{\text{free}}\big(\big\{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}:|\langle\sigma,\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle|>t\big\}\big)\\
&\leq\exp\bigg[-N\lambda t+N\lambda\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle+\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{2}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\|^{2}\bigg]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\exp\bigg[-N\lambda t-N\lambda\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle+\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{2}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\|^{2}\bigg]\\
&\leq 2\exp\bigg[-\frac{Nt^{2}}{1-q}+\frac{Nt^{2}}{2}\frac{\big\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\big\|^{2}}{(1-q)^{2}}\bigg]\exp\bigg[\frac{Nt}{1-q}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\|\bigg],\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k})&\leq\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}\langle\sigma,\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle^{2}\bigg]\\
&\leq p_{\text{free}}(S_{\varepsilon,k})+2\int_{0}^{1}dt\,N\beta^{2}t\,e^{\frac{Nt^{2}}{2(1-q)}\big(\beta^{2}(1-q)-2+\frac{\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\|^{2}}{(1-q)}\big)}e^{\frac{Nt}{1-q}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\|}.\end{split}

In particular, by (
4.7
) and because

\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\textbf{P}^{(k)}\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\rangle=\Gamma_{k}^{2},\hskip 14.22636pt\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\|^{2}=q-\Gamma_{k}^{2},

in
L^{p}(d\mathbb{P})
for
p\in[1;\infty)
, we obtain under the condition (
1.5
), i.e.
\beta^{2}(1-q)\leq 1
, that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)p_{\text{free}}(\tau)\exp\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}\langle\sigma,\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\tau\rangle^{2}\bigg]\bigg|=0,

which implies (
4.6
).
∎

Remark:
The key difficulty in the proof of Lemma
4.3
is to obtain a strong concentration bound on the overlap
(\sigma,\tau)\mapsto\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
under the product measure
p_{\text{free}}^{\otimes 2}
, restricted to
S_{\varepsilon,k}\times S_{\varepsilon,k}\subset\{-1,1\}^{N}\times\{-1,1\}^{N}
. In our proof, the condition
\beta^{2}(1-q)\leq 1
emerges due to the restrictions on one of the spin variables, say,
\tau\in S_{\varepsilon,k}
. In principle, one may be able to find the optimal temperature condition by taking into account the restrictions on the other spin variable as well using standard large deviation variational estimates, but an exact solution seems difficult. We hope to get back to this point in future work.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
, based on Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
. Before we start, let us first re-center the Hamiltonian
H_{N}
appropriately, as outlined in the introduction.

Using the notation of Section
3
, we have that

\begin{split}\frac{H_{N}(\sigma)}{N}&=\frac{\beta}{2}\langle\sigma,\bar{\textbf{g}}\sigma\rangle+\langle h,\sigma\rangle=\frac{\beta}{2}\langle\sigma,\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\sigma,\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\sigma\rangle+\langle h,\sigma\rangle.\end{split}

In contrast to
[
6
]
, instead of centering the spins
\sigma
around
\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}
, we center the spins in
\langle\sigma,\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\sigma\rangle
around
\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)}
in order to produce the right cavity field
\textbf{h}^{(k+1)}
. Notice that the remaining term
\langle\sigma,\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle
contains automatically centered spins around
\sum_{s=1}^{k}\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)}
(which approximately equals
\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}
), as
\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}\phi^{(s)}=0
for
s<k+1
. We thus write

\begin{split}\langle\sigma,\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\sigma\rangle&=2\gamma_{s}\langle\sigma,\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\phi^{(s)}\rangle+\langle\sigma-\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)},\bar{\rho}^{(s)}(\sigma-\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)})\rangle-\gamma_{s}^{2}\langle\phi^{(s)},\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\phi^{(s)}\rangle\\
&=2\gamma_{s}\langle\sigma,\zeta^{(s)}\rangle+\langle\widehat{\sigma}^{(s)},\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\widehat{\sigma}^{(s)}\rangle-\gamma_{s}^{2}\langle\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle,\end{split}

which follows from
\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\phi^{(s)}=\zeta^{(s)}
and where we set
\widehat{\sigma}^{(s)}=\sigma-\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)}
. Hence

\begin{split}\frac{H_{N}(\sigma)}{N}&=\frac{\beta}{2}\langle\sigma,\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle+\langle\textbf{h}^{(k+1)},\sigma\rangle\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle\widehat{\sigma}^{(s)},\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\widehat{\sigma}^{(s)}\rangle-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\gamma_{s}^{2}\langle\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle+\beta\Big(\gamma_{k}-\sqrt{q-\Gamma_{k-1}^{2}}\Big)\langle\sigma,\zeta^{(k)}\rangle.\end{split}

Since an exact evaluation of the free energy seems rather involved, let us notice here that for configurations
\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}
as defined in (
4.1
) instead, we have approximately
H_{N}(\sigma)/N\approx\frac{\beta}{2}\langle\sigma,\bar{\textbf{g}}^{(k+1)}\sigma\rangle+\langle\textbf{h}^{(k+1)},\sigma\rangle
. Indeed, we find that

\begin{split}\langle\widehat{\sigma}^{(s)},\bar{\rho}^{(s)}\widehat{\sigma}^{(s)}\rangle=2\langle\sigma-\gamma_{s}\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle(\langle\phi^{(s)},\sigma\rangle-\gamma_{s})-\langle\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle(\langle\phi^{(s)},\sigma\rangle-\gamma_{s})^{2}\end{split}

with
\gamma_{s}\simeq\langle\textbf{m}^{(k+1)},\phi^{(s)}\rangle
. Similarly, recall that
\langle\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,2/N)
for each
s
and

\Big|\Big(\gamma_{k}-\sqrt{q-\Gamma_{k}^{2}}\Big)\sup_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}\langle\sigma,\zeta^{(k)}\rangle\Big|\leq\Big(\gamma_{k}+\sqrt{q-\Gamma_{k}^{2}}\Big)\|\zeta^{(k)}\|

with
\lim_{k\to\infty}|q-\Gamma_{k}^{2}|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{k}=0
under (
1.3
), by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.4
.

Thus, we obtain the simple lower bound

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}&=\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}\\
&=\log 2+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh(h^{(k+1)}_{i})+\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)e^{H_{N}(\sigma)-N\langle\textbf{h}^{(k+1)},\sigma\rangle}\\
&\geq\log 2+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh(h^{(k+1)}_{i})+\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\varepsilon,k}}p_{\text{free}}(\sigma)e^{H_{N}(\sigma)-N\langle\textbf{h}^{(k+1)},\sigma\rangle}\\
\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}&\geq\log 2+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh(h^{(k+1)}_{i})+\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-C\frac{\beta\varepsilon}{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\|\zeta^{(s)}\|-C\beta\sum_{s=1}^{k}\|\zeta^{(s)}\|\big|\langle\phi^{(s)},\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\rangle-\gamma_{s}\big|\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\gamma_{s}^{2}\langle\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle-\beta\gamma_{k}\|\zeta^{(k)}\|-\beta\sqrt{q-\Gamma_{k}^{2}}\|\zeta^{(k)}\|.\end{split}

(5.1)

We have now all necessary preparations for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Up to minor modifications, we follow
[
6
, Section 3]
and we also abbreviate
RS(\beta,h)=\log 2+E\log\cosh(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)+\beta^{2}(1-q)^{2}/4.

By the Paley-Zygmund inequality, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{k}\Big(Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\geq{\mathbb{E}}_{k}Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})/2\Big)\geq\frac{\big({\mathbb{E}}_{k}(Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\big)^{2}}{4{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\big[(Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k}))^{2}\big]}

Given
\delta_{1}>0
, Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
imply

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{2}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\,Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\geq\frac{1}{N}\log 4\,{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\big(Z_{N}^{2}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\big)-\delta_{1}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{2}

if we choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,
k
sufficiently large and
N\geq N_{1}(\varepsilon,k)\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. This also implies that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mathbb{P}_{k}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\geq\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})-\frac{\log 2}{N}\bigg)\geq e^{-\delta_{1}N}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{2}.\end{split}

On the other hand, applying Lemma
3.4

3)
, we note that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\|\zeta^{(s)}\|>2\bigg)\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\zeta^{(s)}\|>2\Big)=0

and that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\zeta^{(k+1)}\|>2\Big)=0.

Moreover, Lemma
3.4
and the fact that
\langle\phi^{(s)},\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\rangle\simeq\gamma_{s}
by Proposition
3.3
imply

\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigg(C\beta\sum_{s=1}^{k}\|\zeta^{(s)}\|\big|\langle\phi^{(s)},\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\rangle-\gamma_{s}\big|+\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\gamma_{s}^{2}\langle\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle\bigg)=0

as well as

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh\big(h^{(k+1)}_{i}\big)=E\log\cosh(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)

in probability. Now, the lower bound (
5.1
) implies that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mathbb{P}_{k}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}\geq\log 2+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})-\frac{\log 2}{N}-\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,k}\bigg)\geq e^{-\delta_{1}N}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{2},\end{split}

where we defined the error
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,k}
by

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,k}&=\frac{C\beta\varepsilon}{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\|\zeta^{(s)}\|+C\beta\sum_{s=1}^{k}\|\zeta^{(s)}\|\big|\langle\phi^{(s)},\textbf{m}^{(k+1)}\rangle-\gamma_{s}\big|\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt+\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\gamma_{s}^{2}\langle\phi^{(s)},\zeta^{(s)}\rangle+\beta\gamma_{k}\|\zeta^{(k)}\|+\beta\sqrt{q-\Gamma_{k}^{2}}\|\zeta^{(k)}\|.\end{split}

Given
\delta_{2}>0
, we may choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large and
N\geq N_{2}(k,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large such that

\mathbb{P}\bigg(|\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,k}|\leq\frac{\delta_{2}}{4},\,\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh(h^{(k+1)}_{i})-E\log\cosh(\beta\sqrt{q}Z+h)\bigg|\leq\frac{\delta_{2}}{4}\bigg)\geq\frac{7}{8}

and, by Lemma
4.2
, also such that

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{k}Z_{N}^{(k+1)}(S_{\varepsilon,k})\geq\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}(1-q)^{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{4}\bigg)\geq\frac{7}{8}.

Combining the above observations, we find that

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mathbb{P}_{k}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}\geq RS(\beta,h)-\delta_{2}\bigg)\geq e^{-\delta_{1}N}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{4}

for all
N\geq\max(N_{1}(\varepsilon,k),N_{2}(\varepsilon,k),4\log 2/\delta_{2})
. This implies

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}\geq RS(\beta,h)-\delta_{2}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{4}e^{-\delta_{1}N}.

By Gaussian concentration of the free energy, i.e.

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}-\frac{1}{N}{\mathbb{E}}\log Z_{N}\bigg|\leq\delta_{3}\bigg)\geq 1-e^{-N\delta_{3}^{2}/\beta^{2}},

we may choose
\delta_{1}<\delta_{3}^{2}/(2\beta^{2})
to conclude for large enough

that

{\mathbb{E}}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}\geq RS(\beta,h)-\delta_{2}-\delta_{3}.

Since
\delta_{2},\delta_{3}>0
were arbitrary, this shows that the right hand side in (
1.6
) is a lower bound to
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N}
and by

2), this proves Theorem
1.1
.

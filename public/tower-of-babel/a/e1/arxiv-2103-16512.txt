The tropical symplectic Grassmannian
George Balla and Jorge Alberto Olarte

Abstract
. We launch the study of the tropicalization of the symplectic Grassmannian, that is, the space of all linear subspaces isotropic with respect to a fixed symplectic form. We formulate tropical analogues of several equivalent characterizations of the symplectic Grassmannian and determine all implications between them. In the process, we show that the Plücker and symplectic relations form a tropical basis if and only if the rank is at most 2. We provide plenty of examples that show that several features of the symplectic Grassmannian do not hold after tropicalizing. We show exactly when do conormal fans of matroids satisfy these characterizations, as well as doing the same for a valuated generalization. Finally, we propose several directions to extend the study of the tropical symplectic Grassmannian.



1.
Introduction

Given a field
\mathbb{K}
, the Grassmannian, denoted by
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(k,n)
, is the space of all linear subspaces of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
of dimension
k
. Let
\omega
denote the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{K}^{2n}
. The symplectic Grassmannian, which will be denoted by
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(k,2n)
, is the subset of the Grassmannian consisting of all isotropic subspaces of
\mathbb{K}^{2n}
, that is, spaces whose elements are orthogonal to each other with respect to
\omega
.
We consider the Grassmannian under its Plücker embedding and the symplectic Grassmannian as a subset under the same embedding. The defining ideal of the latter variety with respect to this embedding is generated by Plücker and certain linear relations that we term symplectic relations
[
Dec79
]
. We will therefore refer to this ideal as the Plücker-Symplectic ideal.
For a given valuation
\operatorname{val}:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, the tropicalization of the Grassmannian is the space of tropicalizations of linear subspaces
[
SS04
]
. We define the tropical symplectic Grassmannian as the tropicalization of the symplectic Grassmannian with respect to the Plücker-Symplectic ideal. Therefore, it is a subset of the tropical Grassmannian consisting of tropicalizations of isotropic linear subspaces. We will denote it by
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,2n)
, where
p
is the characteristic of the field
\mathbb{K}
.

It is known that the geometry of the tropical Grassmannian is governed by the combinatorics of matroids
[
Spe08
]
. Coxeter matroids are generalizations of matroids to different root systems, where the type
A
Coxeter matroids are the usual matroids
[
BGW03
]
. In
[
Rin12
]
, Rincón studied the tropicalization of the type
D
Grassmannian, known as the Spinor variety, using the type
D
Coxeter matroids, also known as
\Delta
-matroids, to obtain similar results as those in
[
Spe08
]
for type
A
. The symplectic Grassmannian can be interpreted as the type
C
Grassmannian. However, very little is known about the type
C
Coxeter matroids, known as symplectic matroids (see
[
BGW98
]
and
[
BGW03
, Chapter 3]
for what is known about them). In particular, there is no good characterization of symplectic matroids in terms of axioms for their bases. This makes it more challenging to build a theory of their valuated counterparts as tropical Plücker vectors similar to the ones for types
A
and
D
. So, we take a different approach, relying on the already rich theory of type
A
valuated matroids.

There are several equivalent ways of saying that a linear space belongs to the symplectic Grassmannian, among them:

(1)

Its Plücker vector satisfies all polynomials in the Plücker-Symplectic ideal.

(2)

Its Plücker vector satisfies the symplectic relations.

(3)

It is isotropic.

(4)

It has a basis of pairwise orthogonal vectors (with respect to
\omega
).

(5)

It is the row span of a matrix
(A|B)
such that
A\cdot B^{T}
is symmetric, where
A,B\in\mathbb{K}^{k\times n}
.

There is a natural way of obtaining a tropical analogue for each of the statements above, by imposing a condition on a given tropical linear space. The first is the most obvious one, as it just asks to be in the tropicalization of the symplectic Grassmannian. Just like the Dressian is the tropical prevariety consisting of vectors satisfying the tropical Plücker relations, we define the symplectic Dressian, which will be denoted by
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
, to be the prevariety consisting of vectors satisfying the tropical symplectic relations as well as the tropical Plücker relations.

A tropical notion of isotropic linear spaces was already studied in
[
Rin12
]
. Rincón’s motivation was in type
D
, that is, in the context of isotropic spaces with respect to a symmetric bilinear relation. However, the sign that distinguishes that context to ours vanishes after tropicalizing, so his definition is the relevant one for us too. For the last two characterizations, we use the tropical Stiefel map from
[
FR15
]
as an analogue of being the span of a collection of vectors. However, we recognize the limitations of this last analogy, since it forces us to restrict ourselves to tropical linear spaces of this kind.
Thus we obtain the following conditions on a given tropical linear space that can be considered tropical analogues of the characterizations of the symplectic Grassmannian above.

(1)

It is in the tropical symplectic Grassmannian.

(2)

It is in the symplectic Dressian.

(3)

It is tropically isotropic.

(4)

It is the image under the tropical Stiefel map of a matrix with (tropically) orthogonal rows.

(5)

It is the image under the tropical Stiefel map of a matrix
(A|B)
such that
A\odot B^{T}
is symmetric, where
A,B\in\mathbb{T}^{k\times n}
.

Our main result (
Theorem

4.16
), determines for each
k
(dimension of the tropical linear space) and
n
(dimension of the ambient space), which of the above statements implies another. It is fully described in
Figure

1
below.

. Dashed arrows represent implications for specific cases. They come with a label indicating the conditions on
k

.

A particularly interesting case covered in our main theorem is the rank two case. We see that in this case, the tropical symplectic Grassmannian
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(2,2n)
and the symplectic Dressian
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
coincide (
Theorem

4.8
). This result also implies that the Plücker and symplectic relations form a tropical basis for the Plücker-Symplectic ideal in this case. This is analogous to the type
A
result by Maclagan and Sturmfels
[
MS15
]
. Furthermore, Speyer and Sturmfels
[
SS04
]
showed that the tropical Grassmannian in rank two coincides with the space of phylogenetic trees. We show that this coincidence holds in the symplectic case as well, after quotienting
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(2,2n)
by the tropical hyperplane corresponding to the single tropical symplectic relation (Proposition
5.1
). We then go on to obtain enumerative information on this fan, namely, the number of rays and facets (Corollary
5.2
) and the Betti numbers for the corresponding simplicial complex (
Corollary

5.3
).

Another case of particular interest is the Lagrangian case, that is, when
k=n
. In
[
ADH20
]
, the authors proved that the
h
-vector of the independence complex and the broken circuit complex of a matroid is log-concave, solving conjectures that were standing since the 80’s. One of their main tools is what they call the conormal fan of a matroid
M
, which has the same support as the product of the Bergman fan of
M
and its dual. The conormal fan has a natural generalization to any valuated matroid, forming a class of linear spaces which make sense to study under our context. We show that indeed, they are tropically isotropic, they satisfy the tropical symplectic relations, and provided the involved valuated matroids are realizable, they are in the tropical symplectic Grassmannian (
Theorem

8.2
).

The paper is organized as follows:
Section

2
provides background on the classical Grassmannian as well as the symplectic Grassmannian while
Section

3
provides background on the tropical Grassmannian and its connection to valuated matroids. In
Section

4
, we formulate each of the tropical analogues of characterizations of the symplectic Grassmannian and show the implications between them.
Section

5
deals with the rank two case and in particular, we show that for this case, the symplectic and Plücker relations form a tropical basis for the symplectic Grassmannian.
Section

7
contains the other side of the main result, by exhibiting all counterexamples needed to finish the proof, plus some other interesting examples that exhibit some pathologies that can occur. Before that,
Section

6
explains why the list given in
Section

7
is complete to prove
Theorem

4.16
.
In
Section

8
, we answer the question: where do conormal bundles fit in
Figure

1
?
Finally,
Section

9
suggests several directions for future work, including the connection to symplectic matroids and applications to toric degenerations of flag varieties.

2.
The Symplectic Grassmannian

We begin by fixing some notation that we will be using. Let
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
[2n]:=\{1,\ldots,n,\overline{1},\ldots,\overline{n}\}
ordered as
1<\cdots<n<\overline{1}<\cdots<\overline{n}
. Adding a bar is a dual operation, so that
\bar{\bar{i}}=i
.
For a set
S
, we write
Si:=S\cup\{i\}
and
\overline{S}:=\{\bar{i}\mid i\in S\}
.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
.
For a matrix
M\in\mathbb{K}^{k\times n}
we write
M^{T}
for the transpose and for a sub-sequence
\textsc{J}\subseteq[n]
let
M_{\textsc{J}}
denote the sub-matrix of
M
with columns indexed by
J
.

The
Grassmannian

\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(k,n)
is the space of all
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
.
Whenever the field
\mathbb{K}
is not relevant, we omit it from the subscript and write just
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
.
A subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
can be identified by its
Plücker coordinates

\textsc{X}_{\textsc{J}}
, which are determinants of the sub-matrices
M_{\textsc{J}}
for every
\textsc{J}\in\binom{[n]}{k}
, where
M
is a
(k\times n)
-matrix whose rows span
L
. A
Plücker vector

[\textsc{X}]\in\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}
is a vector whose coordinates are the
\binom{n}{k}
Plücker coordinates. The Grassmannian
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
can be identified with the set of all Plücker vectors in
\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}
via the
Plücker embedding
. It is a projective variety of dimension
k(n-k)
that satisfies the
Plücker relations
:

(2.1)

\forall\,\,S\in\binom{[n]}{k-1},\quad\forall\,\,T\in\binom{[n]}{k+1},\quad\sum_{i\in T\backslash S}\operatorname{sign}(i,T\backslash S)\textsc{X}_{T\setminus i}\cdot\textsc{X}_{Si}=0,

where the sign
\operatorname{sign}(i,T\backslash S)\in\{-1,1\}
alternates with respect to the ordering of
T\backslash S
. We write
\mathcal{P}_{k,n}(\mathbb{K})
for the ideal generated by the above relations, called the
Plücker ideal
.

The subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
can be recovered from the Plücker vector
\textsc{X}\in\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}
as follows:

(2.2)

L=\left\{x\in\mathbb{K}^{n}\mid\forall\,\,T\in\binom{[n]}{k+1},\quad\sum_{i\in T}\operatorname{sign}(i,T)\textsc{X}_{T\setminus i}\cdot x_{i}=0\right\}.

A symplectic vector space
W
is a vector space with a symplectic form, which is a bilinear form
\omega:W\times W\to\mathbb{K}
satisfying:

•

\forall\,\,v\in W\kern 5.0pt\omega(v,v)=0
(alternating).

•

if
\omega(u,v)=0
for every
u\in W
, then
v=0
(non-degenerate).

The first condition implies that
\omega(u,v)=-\omega(v,u)\,\,\forall\,\,u,v\in W
, however the converse only holds if
p\neq 2
.

It is well known that any symplectic vector space has even dimension and that there is a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n},e_{\bar{1}},\dots,e_{\bar{n}}\}
such that
\omega
is given by
\omega(e_{i},e_{\bar{i}})=1
(for
i<\bar{i}
) and
\omega(e_{i},e_{j})=0
for
j\neq\bar{i}
. In other words,
\omega
can be written as

(2.3)

\begin{pmatrix}0&\operatorname{Id}_{n}\\
-\operatorname{Id}_{n}&0\end{pmatrix},

with respect to this basis, where
\operatorname{Id}_{n}
is the
n\times n
matrix with
1
’s on the diagonal and zeros elsewhere.

We say that two vectors
u
and
v
are
orthogonal
if
\omega(u,v)=0
. A
k
-dimensional linear subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{2n}
is called
isotropic
if every two vectors in
L
are orthogonal.
By bilinearity, it is enough that
L
has a basis consisting of pairwise orthogonal vectors. If we write such a basis as rows of a matrix
(A|B)
with
A
and
B
two
k\times n
matrices,
it is straightforward to see that the basis is orthogonal if and only if
A\cdot B^{T}
is symmetric. If
L
is isotropic then it follows that
k\leq n
. When
\dim(L)=n
, we also call
L

Lagrangian
.

The
symplectic Grassmannian

\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(k,2n)
is the sub-variety of
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(k,2n)
consisting of all isotropic linear subspaces of
\mathbb{K}^{2n}
. Again, we omit the subscript
\mathbb{K}
when the field does not play a role and write
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
. It is an irreducible projective variety of dimension
\frac{k(4n-3k+1)}{2}
(see for example,
[
MWZ20
]
).
It follows from the work of De Concini
[
Dec79
]
that the defining ideal of
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
, which we denote by
S_{k,2n}(\mathbb{K})
, is generated by the Plücker relations together with the
symplectic relations
:

(2.4)

\forall\,\,S\in\binom{[2n]}{k-2},\quad\sum_{i}\textsc{X}_{Si\overline{i}}=0,

with respect to the Plücker embedding, where the sum goes over all
i\in[n]
such that
\{i,\overline{i}\}\cap S=\emptyset
.
We call
S_{k,2n}(\mathbb{K})
the
Plücker-Symplectic ideal
.
We include the following short proof that the symplectic relations really define the symplectic Grassmannian, for completeness. It was originally written by Carrillo-Pacheco and Zaldivar
[
CPZ11
]
for the Lagrangian case
k=n
, but it works for any
k
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The defining ideal of
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
with respect to the Plücker embedding is the ideal
S_{k,2n}(\mathbb{K})
.

Proof.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, let
\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{K}^{2n}
be the
k
-th exterior power over
\mathbb{K}^{2n}
. Define a linear map:

\displaystyle f:\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{K}^{2n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{k-2}\mathbb{K}^{2n},

\displaystyle v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq k}(-1)^{r+s}\omega(v_{r},v_{s})v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{v}_{r}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{v}_{s}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k},

where
\hat{v}
means that the corresponding term is omitted.
Notice that the sign
(-1)^{r+s}
is necessary for
f
to be well defined with respect to commuting the terms in the wedge product (this sign is missing in
[
CPZ11
]
).
Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the kernel of
f
and denote by
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K})
the projectivization of
\mathcal{K}
. Then
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K})
is a closed irreducible subset of
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{K}^{2n}\right)
under the Plücker embedding.

Claim 1:
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)=\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)\cap\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K})
. It is clear that
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)\subseteq\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)\cap\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K})
. For the opposite inclusion, if
v\in\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)\cap\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K})
, then
v
is the equivalence class of
v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}
with
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
linearly independent and
f(v)=0
, that is:

f(v)=\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq k}(-1)^{r+s}\omega(v_{r},v_{s})v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{v}_{r}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{v}_{s}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}=0,

and thus
\omega(v_{r},v_{s})=0
for all
1\leq r<s\leq k
, and hence the claim follows.

Claim 2:
v\in\mathcal{K}
if and only if the linear relations (
2.4
) vanish.
For this, we use the linearity of
f
. Write
v
with its Plücker coordinates, that is,

v=\sum\limits_{I\in\binom{[2n]}{k}}\textsc{X}_{I}e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{k}}

where
I=\{i_{1}\dots i_{k}\}
. So

f(v)=\sum\limits_{I\in\binom{[2n]}{k}}\textsc{X}_{I}f(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{k}}).

Notice that
f(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{k}})
vanishes, unless
\{i,\bar{i}\}\subseteq I
for some
i
. Let
S=\{s_{1}\dots s_{k-2}\}\in\binom{[2n]}{k-2}
. The coefficient of
e_{s_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{s_{k}}
in
f(v)
is

\sum_{\{i,\bar{i}\}\cap S=\emptyset}\textsc{X}_{Si\bar{i}}(-1)^{2k-1}.

So
f(v)=0
if and only if the coefficient above is
0
for every
S
, which are precisely the linear relations (
2.4
).
∎

To summarize, we have established five equivalent conditions for a linear space
L
to be in the symplectic Grassmannian
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
:

(1)

All polynomials in the Plücker-Symplectic ideal
S_{k,2n}(\mathbb{K})
vanish on the Plücker coordinates of
L
.

(2)

The Plücker coordinates of
L
satisfy the symplectic relations (
Equation

2.4
).

(3)

For every pair
u,v\in L
, we have
\omega(u,v)=0
.

(4)

There is a basis of
L
,
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
, such that
\forall\,\,i,j,\kern 5.0pt\omega(u_{i},u_{j})=0
.

(5)

If
L
is the row-span of a matrix
(A|B)
, then
A\cdot B^{T}
is symmetric, where
A,B\in\mathbb{K}^{k\times n}
.

3.
The Tropical Grassmannian

In this section, we recall notions related to the tropical Grassmannian, which was first introduced and studied by Speyer and Sturmfels
[
SS04
]
. We refer the reader to Maclagan and Sturmfels
[
MS15
]
for a comprehensive introduction to tropical geometry.

To get started, we recall a couple of key tropical notions and fix some conventions. Let
\mathbb{T}
be the semi-field of tropical numbers
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
with operations
\oplus:=\min
and
\odot:=+
.
Let
\operatorname{val}:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{T}
be a non-Archimidean valuation.
Given an ideal
\textsc{I}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\textsc{X}_{1},\ldots,\textsc{X}_{n}]
, let
V(\textsc{I})
denote the algebraic variety corresponding to the ideal
I
. The
tropical variety

\operatorname{trop}(V(\textsc{I}))
is the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces
\operatorname{trop}(V(f))
for
all

f\in\textsc{I}
.

On the other hand, a
tropical prevariety
is the intersection of finitely many tropical hypersurfaces, which is not necessarily a tropical variety.
A finite set of polynomials is said to be a
tropical basis
of the ideal it generates if the tropical prevariety it defines is equal to the tropical variety of that ideal.

3.1.
The Tropical Grassmannian and the Dressian

First, we recall the
tropical projective space

\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}:=\left(\mathbb{T}^{\binom{n}{k}}\setminus(\infty,\dots,\infty)\right)/\mathbb{R}(1,\dots,1).

The
tropical Grassmannian
, which we denote by
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,n)
, is the tropicalization of
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
i.e.,
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,n)=\operatorname{trop}(\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,n))
.
We want to emphasize that we use the notation
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,n)
because the tropicalization of the Plücker ideal only depends on the characteristic
p
of
\mathbb{K}
. However, similarly as before, whenever we say something about the tropical Grassmannian that holds for every characteristic, we omit
p
from the subscript and write
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
.

The
Dressian

\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,n)\supseteq\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
is the prevariety given by all the Plücker relations, i.e., all vectors in
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}
that satisfy the
tropical Plücker relations
:

\forall\,\,S\in\binom{[n]}{k-1},\kern 5.0pt\,\,\forall\,\,T\in\binom{[n]}{k+1},\,\,\text{the minimum in}\bigoplus_{i\in T\setminus S}(x_{T\setminus i}\odot x_{Si})

is achieved at least twice (or is equal to
\infty
).

A vector
\mu\in\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
is called a
valuated matroid

or a
tropical Plücker vector
. A
matroid
can be defined to be a tropical Plücker vector consisting of just
0
and
\infty
as entries. The set of subsets whose corresponding coordinate is
0
is the usual set of
bases
of the matroid (see Oxley
[
Oxl06
]
for more on the theory of ordinary matroids).

The difference between the tropical Grassmannian and the Dressian is an extension of representable matroids vs non-representable matroids. When the bases of a matroid are the set of non-zero coordinates of a point in the Grassmannian
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(k,n)
, we say that the matroid is
representable
or
realizable
over the field
\mathbb{K}
. The existence of non-realizable matroids for fields of a given characteristic, such as the Fano matroid (for characteristic different than 2) and non-Fano matroid (for characteristic equal to 2), implies that the tropical Grassmannian indeed depends on the characteristic and that the Plücker relations (
2.1
) do not form a tropical basis for the Plücker ideal
\mathcal{P}_{k,n}
.
In other words, the tropical Grassmannian
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,n)
and the Dressian
\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
do not coincide in general, except in some cases:

Theorem 3.1

(Maclagan-Sturmfels,
[
MS15
]
)
.

The Dressian
\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(2,n)
and the tropical Grassmannian
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,n)
coincide.

In an analogous fashion as before, a
tropical linear space
can be recovered from a tropical Plücker vector
\mu
:

(3.1)

L_{\mu}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid\forall\,\,T\in\binom{[n]}{k+1},\text{ the minimum in}\,\,\bigoplus\limits_{i\in T}\mu_{T\setminus i}\odot x_{i}\,\,\text{is A.A.T or equals }\infty\right\},

where A.A.T means “achieved at least twice”. If
\mu\in\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,n)
has rational coefficients and
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed, then by the Fundamental Theorem of Tropical Algebraic Geometry
[
MS15
, Theorem 3.2.3]
,
\mu=\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X})
for some Plücker vector
X
over
\mathbb{K}
.

The tropical linear space corresponding to
\mu
coincides with the tropicalization of the linear space corresponding to
X
, i.e.,
\operatorname{trop}(L_{\textsc{X}})=L_{\mu}

[
SS04
, Theorem 3.8]
.
If
\mu
is a valuated matroid which is not in the tropical Grassmannian,
L_{\mu}
is not the tropicalization of a linear space, however there exist reasons to still call such
L_{\mu}
a tropical linear space (see, for example,
[
Fin13
, Theorem 6.5]
).

The study of tropical linear spaces equals the study of the valuated matroids, which relies heavily on a polyhedral view of matroids. Valuated matroids are characterized by being vectors which induce a regular subdivision of a matroid polytope into matroid polytopes (Speyer
[
Spe08
]
). The tropical linear space of an unvaluated matroid (such as the case where the valuation is trivial) is a fan whose support is the same as a fan known as the
Bergman fan
.

The condition that a linear subspace contains another can be read off from their Plücker vectors via the so called incidence relations.
In
[
Haq12
]
, Haque shows that the incidence relations tropicalize well:

Proposition 3.2

(
[
Haq12
]
)
.

Let
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
be two valuated matroids on
[n]
of rank
k_{1}\leq k_{2}
. Then
L_{\mu_{1}}\subseteq L_{\mu_{2}}
if and only if

\forall S\in\binom{[n]}{k_{1}-1},\kern 5.0ptT\in\binom{[n]}{k_{2}+1}\text{ the minimum in }\bigoplus_{i\in T\setminus S}(\mu_{1})_{Si}\odot(\mu_{2})_{T\backslash i}

is achieved at least twice.

3.2.
The Tropical Stiefel Map

The classical Stiefel map sends full rank
k\times n
matrices to their row span.
In
[
FR15
]
, Fink and Rincón introduced a tropical analogue. Let
A\in\mathbb{T}^{k\times n}
be a matrix with tropical entries. For
\textsc{J}\in\binom{[n]}{k}
, let
A_{\textsc{J}}
be the sub-matrix with columns indexed by
J
. Then:

Definition 3.3

(
[
FR15
]
)
.

The
tropical Stiefel map

\pi:\mathbb{T}^{k\times n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{\binom{[n]}{k}}
is given by

\pi(A)_{\textsc{J}}:=\operatorname{tdet}(A_{\textsc{J}}),

where
\operatorname{tdet}
denotes the tropical determinant, that is, the usual determinant computed with tropical operations. It is only defined for matrices such that at least one of such determinants is different from
\infty
, which is one notion of tropical full rank
[
DSS05
]
.

Not all valuated matroids arise this way. The matroids that arise this way are known as
transversal matroids
and the corresponding valuated matroids are a natural generalization so they are also called
transversal
. For a general introduction to (unvaluated) transversal matroids, we recommend
[
Bru87
]
.

For some valuated matroid
\mu
, a matrix
A
such that
\pi(A)=\mu
is called a
presentation
of
\mu
. We naturally have
\pi(A)\in\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{0}(k,n)
but not all valuated matroids that are representable over characteristic
0
are of this form (see, for example,
[
FR15
, Figure 1]
). The space of transversal valuated matroids in
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{0}(k,n)
is called the
Stiefel image
.

The fibers of the tropical Stiefel map were explicitly described by Fink and the second author
[
FO19
, Theorem 6.6]
. This generalizes the characterization of presentations of valuated matroids by Brualdi and Dinolt
[
BD72
, Theorem 4.7]
. It is easy to see that any row of
A
is a point in
L_{\pi(A)}
. In general, the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\mu)
is the orbit of a certain fan in
L_{\mu}^{k}
under the action of permuting rows.

4.
Tropical Analogies

In this section, we describe the tropical analogues of the five equivalent classical realizations of isotropic subspaces discussed in Section
2
following section
3
, and then we go on to discuss the analogies between them. The key point to catch is that these five realizations are no longer tropically equivalent in general.

4.1.
Tropical Analogues of the Five Equivalent Characterizations of Classical Isotropic Subspaces

We start by introducing the analogue of the tropical Grassmannian; the
tropical symplectic Grassmannian
which will be denoted by
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
tropical symplectic Grassmannian

\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,2n)
is the tropicalization of the symplectic Grassmannian
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{K}}
. That is

\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,2n)=\{w\in\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{2n}{k}-1}\mid w\in\operatorname{trop}(V(f))\quad\forall\,\,f\in S_{k,2n}(\mathbb{K})\}.

Again, the tropical symplectic Grassmannian depends on the characteristic
p
of the field
\mathbb{K}
, but we omit the subscript when it is not relevant.

The following is a consequence of the Structure Theorem
[
MS15
, Theorem 3.3.5]
:

Corollary 4.2
.

The tropical symplectic Grassmannian
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
is a pure polyhedral fan in
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{2n}{k}-1}
of dimension
\frac{k(4n-3k+1)}{2}
.

Below we provide explicit examples of the fan
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
for small
k
and
n
that we compute using the
Tropical
package (Améndola et al
[
AKL+17
]
) based on
Gfan
(Jensen
[
Jen08
]
) in
Macaulay2
(Grayson and Stillman
[
GS97
]
). [We use the same software for all other computational examples in this paper.]

Example 4.3

(k=2, n=3)
.

The ideal
S_{2,6}(\mathbb{K})
of
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,6)
is generated by the corresponding Plücker relations and the symplectic relation:
\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1}}+\textsc{X}_{2,\overline{2}}+\textsc{X}_{3,\overline{3}}
. Here
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,6)
is a pure
7
-dimensional fan in
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{14}
with a lineality space of dimension 3. Its f vector modulo the lineality space is
(28,180,420,315)
, so it has 28 rays and 315 maximal cones.

Example 4.4

(k=3, n=3)
.

For
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{0}(3,6)
, the ideal
S_{3,6}(\mathbb{C})
is generated by the respective Plücker relations and the following set of symplectic relations:

\small\textsc{X}_{2,\overline{1},\overline{2}}+\textsc{X}_{3,\overline{1},\overline{3}},\,\,\textsc{X}_{1,3,\overline{1}}+\textsc{X}_{2,3,\overline{2}},\,\,-\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1},\overline{2}}+\textsc{X}_{3,\overline{2},\overline{3}},

\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1},\overline{3}}+\textsc{X}_{2,\overline{2},\overline{3}},\,\,\textsc{X}_{1,2,\overline{1}}+\textsc{X}_{2,3,\overline{3}},\,\,\textsc{X}_{1,2,\overline{1}}-\textsc{X}_{2,3,\overline{3}}.

The fan
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{0}(3,6)
is pure of dimension 6 in
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{19}
, with a 3-dimensional lineality space. Its f vector modulo the lineality space is
(35,157,159)
.

We now define the symplectic counterpart of the usual Dressian
\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
; the
symplectic Dressian
which we denote by
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
:

Definition 4.5
.

The
symplectic Dressian

\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)\supseteq\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
is the prevariety corresponding to the ideal
S_{k,2n}(\mathbb{K})
, i.e., the set of valuated matroids (tropical Plücker vectors)
\mu
satisfying:

(4.1)

\forall\,\,S\in\binom{[2n]}{k-2},\text{ the minimum in }\quad\bigoplus_{i}\mu_{Si\overline{i}}\quad\text{is achieved at least twice.}

We call the elements of
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
,
tropical symplectic Plücker vectors
.

We have seen the notion of a tropical linear space
L_{\mu}
associated to a valuated matroid
\mu\in\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
in Section
3
. The following definition is a tropical analogue of being isotropic, which was first introduced by Rincón in
[
Rin12
]
. Under the scope of Coxeter matroids (Borovik et al
[
BGW03
]
), Rincón
[
Rin12
]
studies valuated matroids corresponding to type
D
, while here we are considering type
C
. However, orthogonality and isotropicity in type
D
vs type
C
differ by just a sign, which becomes irrelevant under tropicalization, so his definition remains the relevant one for us:

Definition 4.6
.

We say that two points
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{2n}
are
(tropically) orthogonal
if the minimum is achieved at least twice in

\bigoplus_{i\in[2n]}x_{i}\odot y_{\overline{i}}.

A tropical linear space
L\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{2n}
is
isotropic
if all of its points are pairwise
orthogonal
.

Recall the tropical Stiefel map
\pi
from Definition
3.3
. In the following definition, we establish two more analogies for transversal valuated matroids:

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mu\in\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
and suppose
\mu=\pi(A|B)
, for
A,B\in\mathbb{T}^{k\times n}
.

(1)

If the rows of
(A|B)
are (tropically) orthogonal, we say
\mu
has a
row-orthogonal
presentation.

(2)

If
A\odot B^{T}
is symmetric, we say
\mu
has a
symmetric
presentation.

To summarize, we have the following tropical analogues of the five equivalent characterizations of isotropic linear subspaces with respect to a symplectic form:

(1)

\mu\in\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,2n)
.

(2)

\mu\in\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
.

(3)

L_{\mu}
is isotropic.

(4)

\mu
has a row-orthogonal presentation.

(5)

\mu
has a symmetric presentation.

Since it turns out they do not agree in general, it is convenient to give a name to each of the sets they define, respectively, the:

(1)

tropical symplectic Grassmannian.

(2)

symplectic Dressian.

(3)

isotropic region.

(4)

row-orthogonal Stiefel image.

(5)

symmetric Stiefel image.

4.2.
Implications between the Five Tropical Analogues

We are now set to examine when does one of the analogues above imply another.

To begin with, we examine the cases when
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
is equal to
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
. It turns out that the two fans are equal when
k=2
, as stated in the following theorem. We keep the proof for Section
5
, where we study the rank two case in detail.

Theorem 4.8
.

The set of Plücker and symplectic relations forms a tropical basis for the ideal
S_{2,2n}(\mathbb{K})
, i.e., the symplectic Dressian
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
and the tropical symplectic Grassmannian
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(2,2n)
coincide. In particular,
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(2,2n)
does not depend on
p
.

In
A
of the matroid zoo (
Section

7
), we describe an explicit point that is in
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
but not in
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
, hence showing that these two fans are not equal.
This is in contrast with type
{\tt A}
, where
\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(3,6)=\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
.
With the help of this example, we also find the missing relation to obtain a tropical basis for
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{2}(3,6)
. The computational information of the fan
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
is shown below:

Example 4.9
.

The symplectic Dressian
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
is a non-pure non-simplicial 7-dimensional polyhedral fan in
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{19}
with f vector
\{35,153,155,2\}
. It has 149 maximal cones; of these, 2 are of dimension 7 while 147 are of dimension 6. However, the rays of
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
are the rays of
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
.

We will show in
Theorem

4.16
, that the non-equality of the two fans for
k=n=3
discussed above holds for all
k\geq 3,n\geq 3
.

We now go on to study connections between the tropical symplectic Grassmannian, the symplectic Dressian and isotropic tropical linear spaces. We first show that a tropical linear space corresponding to any valuated matroid in
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,n)
is isotropic:

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\mu\in\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,2n)
be a valuated matroid in the tropical symplectic Grassmannian. Then
L_{\mu}
is isotropic.

Proof.

Consider
\mathbb{K}
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
such that
\operatorname{val}:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{T}
is surjective, for example the field of formal Hahn series (see for example
[
JS21
]
). Since
\mu\in\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(k,2n)
, then there exists an isotropic linear space
L\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{2n}
realizing
\mu
, that is, its Plücker vector
X
satisfies
\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X})=\mu
(here we are using that
\mathbb{K}
has a surjective valuation). Since
L
is given by
Equation

2.2
and
L_{\mu}
is given by
Equation

3.1
, then by
[
MS15
, Theorem 3.2.5 and Proposition 4.1.6]
, we have that
L_{\mu}=\operatorname{val}(L)
. Since any two points
x,y\in L
satisfy
\omega(x,y)=0
, we have that
\operatorname{val}(x)
and
\operatorname{val}(y)
are orthogonal, so
L_{\mu}
is isotropic.
∎

The following is a result due to Rincón
[
Rin12
]
. It gives a sufficient condition for a tropical linear space to be isotropic in the Lagrangian case.

Proposition 4.11

(
[
Rin12
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(n,2n)
be a valuated matroid. Then
L_{\mu}
is isotropic if and only if
\mu_{\overline{\textsc{J}}}=\mu_{[2n]\setminus\textsc{J}}
for all
\textsc{J}\in\binom{[2n]}{n}
.

Notice that for
k=n\leq 3
, the relations
\mu_{\overline{\textsc{J}}}=\mu_{[2n]\setminus\textsc{J}}
are equivalent to the symplectic relations (in other words, these relations are trivially true), so we have the following result:

Proposition 4.12
.

For
k=n\leq 3
,
L_{\mu}
is isotropic if and only if
\mu\in\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(n,2n)
.

Remark 4.13
.

The equations from
Proposition

4.11
are also known to cut out the Lagrangian Grassmannian in the non-tropical case for any
n
. That is, a Plücker vector
X
satisfies
\textsc{X}_{\overline{\textsc{J}}}=\textsc{X}_{[2n]\setminus\textsc{J}}
if and only if
\textsc{X}\in\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(n,2n)

[
Kar18
, Theorem 5.16]
.

The proof of
Proposition

4.11
is based on the observation that the dual linear space
L_{\mu^{*}}
(where
\mu^{*}_{\textsc{J}}:=\mu_{[2n]\setminus\textsc{J}}
) consists of all points that are orthogonal to
L_{\mu}
with respect to the tropical dot product. So being isotropic means that
L_{\overline{\mu}}\subseteq L_{\mu^{*}}
, where
\overline{\mu}_{\textsc{J}}:=\mu_{\overline{\textsc{J}}}
. For the Lagrangian case,
\dim(L_{\overline{\mu}})=\dim(L_{\mu^{*}})=n
so they must be the same, hence
Proposition

4.11
. However, for
k<n
, we can combine this approach with the
Proposition

3.2
to obtain a characterization of isotropic tropical linear spaces in terms of the Plücker coordinates, although not as clean as
Proposition

4.11
.

Proposition 4.14
.

A tropical linear space
L_{\mu}
is isotropic if and only if

\forall\,\,S_{1},S_{2}\in\binom{[2n]}{k-1},\text{ the minimum in }\bigoplus_{i}\mu_{S_{1}i}\odot\mu_{S_{2}\bar{i}}

is achieved twice (if
i\in S_{1}
or
\bar{i}\in S_{2}
we assume
\mu_{S_{1}i}=\infty
or
\mu_{S_{2}\bar{i}}=\infty
respectively).

Finally, we show that the symmetric Stiefel image is contained in the row-orthogonal Stiefel image:

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{T}^{k\times n}
such that
A\odot B^{T}
is symmetric. Then the rows of
(A|B)
are (tropically) orthogonal.

Proof.

Since
A\odot B^{T}
is a
k\times k
symmetric matrix, we have that for any
i,j
:

\bigoplus_{l\in[n]}A_{il}\odot B_{jl}=\bigoplus_{l\in[n]}A_{jl}\odot B_{il}.

This implies that rows
i
and
j
of
(A|B)
are orthogonal, since

\bigoplus_{l\in[2n]}(A|B)_{il}\odot(A|B)_{j\bar{l}}=\left(\bigoplus_{l\in[n]}A_{il}\odot B_{jl}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{l\in[n]}B_{il}\odot A_{jl}\right).

∎

The following is our main theorem. It combines the results from this section and Sections
5
,
6

7
to give a full description of all the implications.

.

None of the statements (1)-(5) are equivalent in general. The following is a complete list of the implications between them for each

(a)

In general,

and
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
.

(b)

For transversal valuated matroids,

and
(5)\Rightarrow(4)
.

(c)

For

(d)

For

.

(e)

For

.

There is an example showing that any other implication fails (even when restricting to transversal valuated matroids, for the cases involving (4) and (5)).

Notice that set

Proof.

The proof is summarized in

5.
Rank Two Case

In this section, we prove Theorem
4.8
and deduce from it enumerative information for
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
.

5.1.
Coincidence of the Tropical Symplectic Grassmannian with the Symplectic Dressian

Notice that the ideal
\textsc{I}_{2,2n}
for
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
is generated by the following
\binom{2n}{4}
Plücker relations

(5.1)

\textsc{X}_{i,j}\textsc{X}_{k,l}-\textsc{X}_{i,k}\textsc{X}_{j,l}+\textsc{X}_{i,l}\textsc{X}_{j,k}=0\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i<j<k<l\leq\overline{n},

and a single linear relation

(5.2)

\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1}}+\cdots+\textsc{X}_{n,\overline{n}}=0.

Proof of Theorem
4.8

We want to prove that
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)=\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(2,2n)
.
We know that isotropic linear spaces satisfy a single linear relation (
5.2
) when
k=2
.

Obviously,
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(2,2n)\subseteq\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
, so let
\mu\in\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
. We have that the minimum in
\mu_{1,\overline{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mu_{n,\overline{n}}
is achieved twice. By Theorem
3.1
we know that
\mu\in\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
and
\mathbb{K}\{\!\!\{t\}\!\!\}
be the field of Puiseux series with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose
\mu
has rational entries. Then there exists a linear subspace
L\subseteq(\mathbb{K}\{\!\!\{t\}\!\!\})^{2n}
whose Plücker vector
X
satisfies
\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{I})=\mu_{I}
for every
I\in\binom{[2n]}{2}
.

It may be that
L
is not isotropic, i.e.,
X
does not satisfy
Equation

5.2
. However, we have that the minimum of

\bigoplus\limits_{i\in[n]}\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}})

is achieved twice. Let
i
be such that
\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}})
achieves the minimum and assume it is not infinite (otherwise we would already have that
L
is isotropic). Consider a matrix
A\subseteq\mathbb{K}\{\!\!\{t\}\!\!\}^{2\times 2n}
such that its rows form a basis of
L
.

Notice that multiplying column
i
by a factor
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}\{\!\!\{t\}\!\!\}
with valuation
0
, changes
\textsc{X}_{I}
by a factor of
\alpha
if
i\in I
, otherwise it remains unchanged. However, since
\operatorname{val}(\alpha)=0
we observe that
\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{I})
remains unchanged anyway. We are going to show that there exists such
\alpha
such that the tropical linear space
L^{\prime}
, generated by the rows of the matrix
A^{\prime}
which is the result of multiplying column
i
of
A
by
\alpha
, is isotropic. By the discussion above, the valuation of the Plücker vector of
L^{\prime}
is
\mu
and this will show that
\mu
is in the tropical symplectic Grassmannian.

Let
a=\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1}}+\dots+\textsc{X}_{n,\overline{n}})
. If
a=\infty
we have already that
L
is isotropic. So suppose
a
is finite. By the axioms of valuation,
a\geq\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}})
.
If
a=\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}})
, consider

b=\frac{\overline{-t^{-a}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}^{n}\textsc{X}_{j,\overline{j}}}}{\overline{t^{-a}\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}}}}

where
\overline{x}
denotes the value of
x
in the residue field (which in this case is
\mathbb{K}
). Since the minimum is achieved twice for
\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{j,\overline{j}})
, without loss of generality we can assume that
i
is such that
b\neq 0
. We have now that

\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1}}+\cdots+b\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}}+\cdots+\textsc{X}_{n,\overline{n}})>\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}}).

Let
m
be least common denominator of all the exponents of
t
in all entries of
A
. We are going to construct
\alpha
recursively as follows:

(1)

Start with
\alpha_{1}=b
where
b=1
if
a>\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}})
and let
S_{1}=\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{1}\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}}+\cdots+\textsc{X}_{n,\overline{n}}
.

(2)

Let
\lambda_{j}=\operatorname{val}(S_{j})-\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}})
which is a positive rational and let
c_{j}=\overline{-t^{-\operatorname{val}(S_{j})}S_{j}}
.

(3)

Define
\alpha_{j+1}=\alpha_{j}+t^{\lambda_{j}}c_{j}
and
S_{j+1}=\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{j+1}\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}}+\cdots+\textsc{X}_{n,\overline{n}}
.

(4)

Repeat the two previous steps infinitely (or until
S_{j}=0
).

(5)

Let
\alpha
be the resulting series, that is

\alpha:=b+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}t^{\lambda_{j}}.

We have that
\alpha
is a Puiseux series since all
\lambda_{j}
share the denominator
m
. Furthermore
\operatorname{val}(\alpha)=0
. Notice that
\operatorname{val}(S_{j+1})\geq\operatorname{val}(S_{j})+\dfrac{1}{m}
, so we must have that
\operatorname{val}(\textsc{X}_{1,\overline{1}}+\cdots+\alpha\textsc{X}_{i,\overline{i}}+\cdots+\textsc{X}_{n,\overline{n}})=\infty
as we wanted.
∎

5.2.
Coincidence with the Space of Phylogenetic Trees

We consider the space of phylogenetic trees with
2n
labeled leaves (studied by Billera et al
[
BHV01
]
) and its underlying simplicial complex which we denote by
\mathbf{T}_{2n}
; first introduced by Buneman
[
Bun74
]
and later studied by Robinson and Whitehouse
[
RW96
]
and Vogtmann
[
Vog90
]
.

We prove the following proposition which relates
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
to the simplicial complex
\mathbf{T}_{2n}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The tropical symplectic Grassmannian
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
is isomorphic as a fan to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times H_{n-2}\times\mathbf{T}_{2n}
where
H_{n-2}
denotes a generic hyperplane in
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

Let
Y
denote the tropical hyperplane given by
\mu_{1,\overline{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mu_{n,\overline{n}}
, which is the tropicalization of the linear relation (
5.2
). Recall that the lineality space
L
of
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
is the largest linear space contained in every cone of
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
. It is the
(2n-1)
-dimensional vector space spanned by the vectors:

u_{i}=\sum\limits_{B\ni i}e_{B}

for every
i\in[2n]
(the span of this vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{\binom{2n}{2}}
contains the line
\mathbb{R}(1,\dots,1)
which is collapsed in the tropical projective plane).

Theorem

4.8
tells us that
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
is the intersection of
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
with the tropical hyperplane
Y
. The intersection
Y\cap L
is a fan with a
n
-dimensional lineality space
L^{\prime}
spanned by the vectors:

u_{i}-u_{\overline{i}}

Every other cone of
L\cap Y
is of the form
L^{\prime}+\operatorname{cone}\{u_{i}\mid i\in I\}
for any subset
I\subseteq[n]
of size at most
n-2
. So
L\cap Y
is isomorphic as a fan to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times H_{n-2}
.

For any point
\mu\in\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{{\binom{2n}{2}}-1}
, it is straightforward to see that there is a point
\mu^{\prime}\in\mu+L
where the minimum in
Equation

5.2
is achieved at every term. From this we can deduce that
(\mu+L)\cap Y=\mu^{\prime}+(L\cap Y)
.
One way to interpret the last equation is that
Y
intersects
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
through its lineality space.
As a fan,
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
is isomorphic to
L\times\mathbf{T}_{2n}

[
SS04
, Theorem 3.4]
. So we conclude that

\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)\cong(L\cap Y)\times\mathbf{T}_{2n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}\times H_{n-2}\times\mathbf{T}_{2n}.

∎

We can now extract enumerative information of
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
:

Corollary 5.2
.

The fan
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
has
2^{2n-1}-n-1
rays and
\binom{n}{2}[(4n-5)!!]
facets.

Proof.

The number of rays of
H_{n-2}
is
n
and the number of rays in
\mathbf{T}_{2n}
is
2^{2n-1}-2n-1

[
BHV01
]
, so the number of rays of
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
is
n+2^{2n-1}-2n-1=2^{2n-1}-n-1
.

The number of facets of
H_{n-2}
is
\binom{n}{2}
and the number of facets in
\mathbf{T}_{2n}
is
(4n-5)!!=1\times 3\times 5\times\cdots\times(4n-5)

[
RW96
,
Vog90
]
, so the number of facets of
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
is
\binom{n}{2}[(4n-5)!!]
.
∎

One can turn any simplicial fan into a simplicial complex by quotienting out the lineality space and intersecting with the sphere of radius 1. We say that the homology of the simplicial fan is the simplicial homology of this complex. The following corollary specifies some topological information of
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
; its homotopy type in particular:

Corollary 5.3
.

The Betti numbers for the simplicial complex corresponding to the fan
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
are as follows: for
n\geq 4
the only positive Betti numbers are
b_{0}=1
,
b_{n-3}=n-1
,
b_{2n-4}=(2n-2)!
and
b_{3n-7}=(n-1)(2n-2)!
; for
n=3
they are
b_{0}=3
and
b_{2}=72
and for
n=2
we only have
b_{0}=3
.

Proof.

The simplicial complex
\mathbf{T}_{2n}
has the homotopy type of a bouquet of
(2n-2)!
spheres of dimension
2n-4

[
RW96
,
Vog90
]
while the complex corresponding to
H_{n-2}
has the homotopy type of a bouquet of
n-1
spheres of dimension
n-3
. Now we use Künneth formula for product of spaces
[
Kün23
]
to compute the Betti numbers of the simplicial complex of
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,2n)
as stated in the corollary.
∎

6.
Direct Sums

Let
\mu_{1}\in\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k_{1},n_{1})
and
\mu_{2}\in\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k_{2},n_{2})
be two valuated matroids. The
direct sum
between them
\mu_{1}\oplus\mu_{2}\in\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k_{1}+k_{2},n_{1}+n_{2})
is defined as

(\mu_{1}\oplus\mu_{2})_{\textsc{J}_{1}\sqcup\textsc{J}_{2}}:={\mu_{1}}_{\textsc{J}_{1}}+{\mu_{2}}_{\textsc{J}_{2}}

where
\textsc{J}_{1}\subset[n_{1}]
and
\textsc{J}_{2}\subset[n_{2}]
. Notice that this implies that
(\mu_{1}\oplus\mu_{2})_{\textsc{J}}=\infty
if
|\textsc{J}\cap n_{1}|\neq k_{1}
.

The main motivation behind this construction is that if
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are representable valuated matroids, represented by
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, then
\mu_{1}\oplus\mu_{2}
is representable and represented by
L_{1}\times L_{2}
. It is straightforward to see that

(6.1)

L_{\mu_{1}\oplus\mu_{2}}=L_{\mu_{1}}\times L_{\mu_{2}}

and that if
\mu_{1}=\pi(A_{1})
and
\mu_{2}=\pi(A_{2})
then

(6.2)

\mu_{1}\oplus\mu_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi(A_{1})&\infty\\
\infty&\pi(A_{2})\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, the converse is also true: every presentation of
\mu_{1}\oplus\mu_{2}
is of this form. This follows from
Equation

6.1
and
[
FO19
, Theorem 6.6]
.

From the observations above we can prove the following statements which help us to extend the counter-examples from
Section

7
to counter-examples for all suitable
k
and
n
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
X
be any of the five sets considered in
Theorem

4.16
for all
k
and
n
and let
\mu
be a valuated matroid. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mu\in X
,

(2)

\mu\oplus U_{0,2}\in X
,

(3)

\mu\oplus U_{1,2}\in X
,

where
U_{0,2}=(0)\in\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(0,2)
and
U_{1,2}=(0,0)\in\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(1,2)
are the uniform matroids.

Proof.

We are going to examine each set that we are considering one by one:

•

The tropical symplectic Grassmannian:
If
\mu\in\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
then there is an isotropic linear space
L\subset\mathbb{K}^{2n}
such that
\mu=\operatorname{val}(L)
. Then the space
L^{\prime}=L\times\{(0,0)\}\subset\mathbb{K}^{2n+2}
is obviously isotropic (when pairing the last two coordinates) and represents
L\oplus U_{0,2}
. Any representation of
L\oplus U_{0,2}
is of this form so the other direction holds.

Similarly,
L^{\prime\prime}=L\times\{(x,x)\mid x\in\mathbb{K}\}
is a
(k+1)
-dimensional vector space representing
\mu\oplus U_{1,2}
which is isotropic if and only if
L
is isotropic.

•

The symplectic Dressian:
Since
(\mu\oplus U_{0,2})_{\textsc{J}}=\infty
if
\textsc{J}\cap\{2n+1,2n+2\}\neq\emptyset
, the only non-trivial symplectic relations occur when
S
in
Equation

4.1
does not contain
2n+1
or
2n+2
and they are equivalent to the symplectic relations on
\mu
.

For
\mu\oplus U_{1,2}
, notice that
(\mu\oplus U_{1,2})_{\textsc{J}}=\infty
unless
|\textsc{J}\cap\{2n+1,2n+2\}|=1
. So again we have that the only non-trivial symplectic relation occurs when
|S\cap\{2n+1,2n+2\}|=1
and they are again equivalent to those for
\mu
(since
(\mu\oplus U_{1,2})_{\textsc{J}}=\mu_{\textsc{J}\setminus k}
for
k\in\{2n+1,2n+2\}
.

•

The isotropic region:
By
Equation

6.1
,
L_{\mu\oplus U_{0,2}}=L_{\mu}\times\{(\infty,\infty)\}
and
L_{\mu\oplus U_{1,2}}=L_{\mu}\times\{(x,x)\mid x\in\mathbb{T}\}
, which are clearly isotropic if and only if
L_{\mu}
is isotropic.

•

Row-orthogonal Stiefel image:
Suppose
\mu
is a transversal valuated matroid. We have that
\mu=\pi(A)
for a tropical matrix
A\in\mathbb{K}^{k\times 2n}
if and only if the matrix
A^{\prime}
which is obtained by adding two columns to
A
with all entries equal to
\infty
satisfies
\pi(A^{\prime})=\mu\oplus U_{0,2}
. Moreover, all presentations of
\mu\oplus U_{0,2}
are of this form.

Similarly, consider the matrix
A^{\prime\prime}
that consists of adding a row to
A^{\prime}
whose first
2n
entries are equal to
\infty
and the last two are equal to
0
. We have that
\pi(A)=\mu\Leftrightarrow\pi(A^{\prime\prime})=\mu\oplus U_{1,2}
and all presentations are of this form.

Now it is clear that the rows of
A
are orthogonal if and only if the rows of
A^{\prime}
are orthogonal and if and only if the rows of
A^{\prime\prime}
are orthogonal.

•

Symmetric Stiefel image:
Let us write a presentation of
\mu
as
(A|B)
and the corresponding presentations of
\mu\oplus U_{0,2}
and
\mu\oplus U_{1,2}
as
(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})
and
(A^{\prime\prime}|B^{\prime\prime})
, so for example

A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}A&\infty\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{T}^{k\times(n+1)}

and

A^{\prime\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}A&\infty\\
\infty&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{T}^{(k+1)\times(n+1)}.

Then
A^{\prime}\odot B^{\prime T}=A\odot B
and

A^{\prime\prime}\odot B^{\prime\prime T}=\begin{pmatrix}A\odot B^{T}&\infty\\
\infty&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{T}^{(k+1)\times(k+1)},

from which the desired statement follows.

∎

The Matroid Zoo

Welcome to the matroid zoo, a section devoted to show examples that exhibit different pathologies.
Examples

4.16

•

If we were to complete this symplectic matroid to a usual matroid (i.e., determining its bases for non-admissible sets), we would necessarily have that
13\bar{3}
and
\bar{1}2\bar{2}
are the two parallel classes. But then the symplectic relation
x_{1\bar{1}}\oplus x_{2\bar{2}}\oplus x_{3\bar{3}}
achieves its minimum once, so such matroid is not in the symplectic Dressian.

8.
Conormal Bundles

Recall that the dual of a valuated matroid
\mu
on
[n]
of rank
k
is a valuated matroid
\mu^{*}
on
[n]
of rank
n-k
where
\mu^{*}_{B}=\mu_{[n]\backslash B}
.

In
[
ADH20
]
, Ardila, Denham and Huh studied the
conormal fan
of a matroid,
\Sigma_{M,M^{*}}
, whose support equals
L_{M\oplus M^{*}}
. The latter is a tropical linear space of dimension
n
on
[2n]
. The conormal fan serves as a tropical analogue of conic Lagrangian subvarieties. Therefore, a natural question is: where does this kind of tropical linear space fit in our picture (that is, in
Figure

1
)?

We answer this question for a more general class of tropical linear spaces, namely for the following natural generalization to valuated matroids:

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\mu
be a valuated matroid. The
conormal bundle
of
\mu
is the tropical linear space
L_{\mu\oplus\mu^{*}}
.

We remark that this is a generalization of conormal fans only in the same way as tropical linear spaces generalize Bergman fans: for unvaluated matroids the conormal bundle coincides with the support of the conormal fan, but the conormal fan may have a finer subdivision. As the name suggests, for valuated matroids the conormal bundle may not be a fan.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\mu
be a valuated matroid. Then:

(1)

if
\mu
is realizable over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
p
, then
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}\in\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(n,2n)
.

(2)

for any
\mu
,
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}\in\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(n,2n)
, i.e.,
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}
satisfies the tropical symplectic relations.

(3)

the conormal bundle
L_{\mu\oplus\mu^{*}}
is isotropic.

(4)

if
\mu
and
\mu^{*}
are transversal,
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}
has a symmetric presentation if and only if
\mu
has a single basis.

Proof.

For the first statement, if
\mu=\operatorname{trop}(V)
for a linear subspace
V\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
, then the orthogonal complement
V^{\perp}
satisfies that
\operatorname{trop}(V^{\perp})=\mu^{*}
and
V\times V^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{2n}
is isotropic with respect to the canonical symplectic form given by
Equation

2.3
. So
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}=\operatorname{trop}(V\times V^{\perp})\in\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{p}(n,2n)
.

Next, let
\mu
be any valuated matroid (not necessarily realizable). Notice that the tropical symplectic relations for
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}
are exactly the same as the tropical Plücker relations for
\mu
:

\bigoplus_{i}(\mu\oplus\mu^{*})_{Si\bar{i}}=\bigoplus_{i}\mu_{(S\cap[n])\cup i}\odot\mu^{*}_{S\backslash[n]\cup\bar{i}}=\bigoplus_{i}\mu_{S^{\prime}i}\odot\mu_{T\backslash i}

where
S^{\prime}=S\cap[n]
and
T=[n]\backslash\bar{S}
.

For the third statement, we use
Proposition

4.11
. Let
J=B_{1}\sqcup B_{2}\in\binom{2n}{n}
where
B_{1}=J\cap[n]
. Then

(\mu\oplus\mu^{*})_{[2n]\backslash J}=\mu_{[n]\backslash B_{1}}\odot\mu^{*}_{[n]\backslash B_{2}}=\mu_{B_{2}}\odot\mu^{*}_{B_{1}}=(\mu\oplus\mu^{*})_{\bar{J}}.

Finally, to relate conormal bundles with the Stiefel image, we need to work under the assumption that both
\mu
and
\mu^{*}
are transversal (transversality is closed under direct sums but not under duality). If such is the case, then by part (2) we have that
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}
has row-orthogonal presentation.

Now, if
\mu
has a single basis, it means it is the direct sum of loops and coloops (an element
i
is a loop of
\mu
if it is not in any basis and it is a coloop if it is a loop of
\mu^{*}
). Therefore,
\mu^{*}
and
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}
are also of this form. Then, if column
i
of a presentation
(A|B)
of
\mu\oplus\mu^{*}
has a finite entry, the column
\bar{i}
must have all entries equal to
\infty
.
From this it follows that
A\odot B^{T}
has all infinity entries for a presentation
(A|B)
and in particular it is symmetric.

If
\mu
is not of this form, then there is an element
x\in[n]
which is not a loop in
\mu
or in
\mu^{*}
. Therefore, there are rows
i
and
j
in
(A|B)
with a finite entry at
x
and
\bar{x}
respectively. This implies that the
(i,j)
-entry of
A\odot B^{T}
is finite. However, the
(j,i)
entry of
A\odot B^{T}
is given by the tropical dot product of two all
\infty
vectors, so it is equal to
\infty
. Therefore
A\odot B^{T}
is not symmetric.
∎

9.
Future Bridges

In this section, we discuss several notions including the tropical symplectic flag variety, symplectic flag Dressian, flag Stiefel image, degenerations of symplectic flag varieties and total positivity and pose several questions about them.

9.1.
Symplectic Matroids

We follow Borovik et al
[
BGW98
,
BGW03
]
and we refer the reader there for details on symplectic matroids.

We begin by recalling that we call a subset
\textsc{J}\subset[2n]

admissible
if for every
i\in\textsc{J}
,
\bar{i}\notin\textsc{J}
.
Let
e_{\bar{i}}:=-e_{i}
and for an admissible set
\textsc{J}\subseteq[2n]
consider
e_{\textsc{J}}:=\sum\limits_{i\in\textsc{J}}e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{[n]}
.

Definition 9.1
.

A
symplectic matroid
is a collection
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\binom{[2n]}{k}
of admissible sets such that every edge of the polytope
\operatorname{conv}\{e_{\textsc{J}}\mid\textsc{J}\in\mathcal{B}\}
is either a translation of
e_{i}-e_{j}
for some
i,j
or of
2e_{i}
for some
i
.

The main motivation behind this definition is the following:

Theorem 9.2

(
[
BGW98
]
)
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{[2n]}
be an isotropic linear space. Then the collection of admissible bases of
L
is a symplectic matroid.

A symplectic matroid constructed as above is called
representable
. From
Theorem

9.2
, it follows that any realizable symplectic matroid can be completed to a matroid which is in the tropical symplectic Grassmannian (as opposed to
I
).

One possible direction would be to find axioms for which one can enhance a symplectic matroid with a valuation on the tropical numbers, which includes every vector in the tropical symplectic Grassmannian
\textsc{T}\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
and such that symplectic matroids are exactly the valuations that take values in
\{0,\infty\}
. However, even the
\{0,\infty\}
symplectic matroids do not always satisfy the tropical symplectic relations (see
Section

7
above), so this would not be contained in the symplectic Dressian
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
.

In general, the connection of symplectic matroids with the tropical symplectic Grassmannian remains largely not understood. One reason for this is that the symplectic relations (
Equation

4.1
) are not in terms of variables corresponding to admissible sets. However, there are equations in the ideal generated by them which are purely in terms of variables corresponding to admissible sets (at least in characteristic 2, see
A
). Finding more of these relations and studying their combinatorics might lead to a better understanding of this connection.

9.2.
Characterization in terms of Matroid Subdivisions

It would be desirable to obtain a characterization of tropical symplectic Plücker vectors in terms of polyhedral subdivisions, similar to Speyer’s theorem for type
A

[
Spe08
, Proposition 2.2]
, generalized by Rincón for any matroid polytope in type
A
as well as type
D

[
Rin12
, Theorem 5.14]
and in
[
BEZ20
, Theorem A]
to tropical flags.

However, such a characterization is, in principle, not possible,
since satisfying the tropical symplectic relations is not closed under translations. This implies that being a tropical symplectic Plücker vector can not be determined from the matroid subdivision it induces.
But one could still ask which matroid subdivisions are induced by vectors from
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}(k,2n)
.

9.3.
The Tropical Symplectic Flag Variety and Symplectic Flag Dressian

The complete symplectic flag variety which will be denoted by
\textsc{SpF}_{2n}
, is the set of all full flags
\{\textsc{U}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\textsc{U}_{n},\,\,\dim\,\,\textsc{U}_{k}=k\}
, with the extra condition that each
\textsc{U}_{k}
is isotropic with respect to the symplectic form
\omega
. We consider the Plücker embedding of
\textsc{SpF}_{2n}
into the product of projective spaces
\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{2n}\Big)
, for
1\leq k\leq n
. It is an irreducible projective variety of dimension
n^{2}
. With respect to this embedding, its defining ideal which we denote by
S_{2n}
, is seen to be generated by the corresponding Plücker and symplectic relations following the work of De Concini
[
Dec79
]
. See also
[
Mak20
]
and
[
Bal20
]
for a further description and some examples of this ideal. Let
G_{2n}
denote the set of the generators of the ideal
S_{2n}
.

Let
\textsc{TF}_{n}
denote the usual tropical flag variety, i.e., the tropicalization of the (type
A
n-1
) complete flag variety with respect to the Plücker embedding. Let
N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{2n}{i}
denote the number of Plücker coordinates on
\textsc{SpF}_{2n}
with respect to the Plücker embedding as discussed above. We introduce the symplectic counterpart of
\textsc{TF}_{n}
; the
tropical symplectic flag variety
, which will be denoted by
\textsc{T}\textsc{SpF}_{2n}
.

Definition 9.3
.

The
tropical symplectic flag variety

\textsc{T}\textsc{SpF}_{2n}
, is the tropicalization of the symplectic flag variety
\textsc{SpF}_{2n}
. That is

\textsc{T}\textsc{SpF}_{2n}=\{\mu\in\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\mid\mu\in\operatorname{trop}(V(f))\quad\forall\,\,f\in S_{2n}\}.

Example 9.4

(n=2)
.

The tropical symplectic flag variety
\textsc{T}\textsc{SpF}_{4}
is a pure 4-dimensional fan in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
with a 2-dimensional lineality space. It has 10 rays and 15 maximal cones.

In the following, we define the
symplectic flag Dressian
which we denote by
\textsc{SpF}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}_{2n}
. It is the analogue of the usual flag Dressian; introduced and studied by Brandt, Eur and Zhang
[
BEZ20
]
.

Definition 9.5
.

The
symplectic flag Dressian

\textsc{SpF}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}_{2n}
is given by:

\textsc{SpF}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}_{2n}=\{\mu\in\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\mid\mu\in\operatorname{trop}(V(f))\quad\forall\,\,f\in G_{2n}\}.

As it would be expected, the tropical symplectic flag variety
\textsc{T}\textsc{SpF}_{2n}
is not equal to
\textsc{SpF}\textsc{D}\mathsf{r}_{2n}
in general. However, the two fans coincide computationally for
n=2
. It is important to note that the issue of representability of symplectic flags is subtle, in that there are flags with symplectically representable components but the flags themselves are not symplectically representable. Such a scenario is illustrated in Example
H
of the matroid zoo.

As in the classical setting, we can also obtain a flag of tropical linear spaces out of an
n\times n
matrix, by considering all minors (not necessarily maximal) whose rows are indexed by
[k]
for some
k\leq n
.

Definition 9.6
.

The
tropical flag Stiefel map
for type
{\tt A}
,

\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\tt A}:\mathbb{T}^{n\times n}\dashrightarrow\textsc{TF}_{n},

sends a matrix to the vector where for every non-empty proper subset
S\subset[n]
,
\pi_{\mathcal{F}}(A)_{S}
is the tropical determinant of the submatrix of
A
with columns indexed by
S
and the first
|S|
rows.

Similarly, we have a
tropical flag Stiefel map
for type
{\tt C}
,

\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\tt C}:\mathbb{T}^{n\times 2n}\dashrightarrow\textsc{T}\textsc{SpF}_{2n},

where we consider the minors indexed by non-empty subsets
S\subset[2n]
of size at most
n
.

The image under the above maps we call
flag Stiefel image
.

9.4.
Degenerations of Symplectic Grassmann/Flag Varieties and Tropical Geometry

A cone of a tropical variety is said to be prime if the corresponding initial ideal is a prime ideal.
Prime maximal cones of tropicalizations of algebraic varieties give rise to a nice class of toric degenerations, namely, those that are irreducible. According to
[
SS04
]
, all maximal cones of
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(2,n)
are prime, but not all those of
\textsc{T}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(3,6)
are. On the other hand, Bossinger, Lambogila, Mincheva, and Mohammadi in
[
BLMM17
]
, computed toric degenerations of the type
A
flag variety corresponding to both prime and non-prime maximal cones in the tropicalization. So, it is natural to ask which of the maximal cones of the tropical symplectic Grassmann or flag variety are prime cones.

Now, an explicit computation of tropicalizations of flag varieties is very cumbersome, in that, it is only possible for small
n
(see again
[
BLMM17
]
). However, a full facet description of a maximal prime cone with nice features in the type
A
tropical flag variety has been given in
[
FFFM19
]
, and connections made to various degenerations of flag varieties. For example, some of its facets correspond to linear degenerations
[
CFF+19
]
.

We point out that such a description can be explained through the tropical flag Stiefel map discussed above. Consider the cone
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{n\times n}
of upper triangular matrices
A
such that:

•

A_{i,i}=0
,

•

A_{i,j}=\infty,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad
for
j<i
,

•

A_{i,j}+A_{i+1,j+1}\geq A_{i+1,j}+A_{i,j+1},\quad\,
for
1\leq i<j\leq n-2
.

The original description of the cone in
[
FFFM19
]
was stated by setting the tropical Plücker coordinates as

\mu_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}A_{p_{i},q_{i}}

where
p_{1}>\dots>p_{l}
are the elements of
B\setminus[k]
,
q_{1}<\dots<q_{l}
are the elements of
[k]\setminus B
and
k=|B|
. However, we notice that this corresponds precisely to the minimal term in the tropical determinant of the submatrix with rows
[k]
and columns
B
got by adding the 0-terms
A_{i,i}
for
i\in[k]\cap B
. To see that, notice that this is the lexicographic maximal permutation using finite coordinates and the third condition ensures that this is the minimal term. So we conclude that the maximal prime cone described in
[
FFFM19
]
is actually
\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\tt A}(\mathcal{A})
, where
\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\tt A}
is the type
A
tropical flag Stiefel map (
Definition

9.6
). We see therefore that the valuated matroids within this cone are transversal.

A symplectic counterpart of this cone is under construction in
[
BF21
]
and we conjecture that it also lives in the tropical flag Stiefel image for type
C
.

9.5.
Total Positivity

A major object of study has been the non-negative Grassmannian
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\geq 0}(k,n)
, that is, the set of all Plücker vectors in
\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,n)
with non-negative entries. Extensive research has been conducted on its positroid decomposition, specially motivated by its connections to quantum physics, such as computing Feynman integrals in the supersymmetric-Yang-Mills model (see, for example,
[
ABC+16
]
for an overview). The cells of the positroid decomposition are indexed by a variety of combinatorial gadgets and can be parametrized using networks through the boundary measurement map
[
Pos06
]
. Adaptations of the combinatorics of the non-negative Grassmannian have been constructed both for type
C
, more precisely, for the non-negative Lagrangian Grassmannian
\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\geq 0}(n,2n):=\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}_{\geq 0}(n,2n)\cap\textsc{Sp}\textsc{G}\mathsf{r}(n,2n)

[
Kar18
]
. On the other hand, the study of the totally positive tropical Grassmannian as defined in
[
SW05
]
has gained a lot of momentum recently (see for example
[
ALS21
,
LPW20
,
SW21
]
) where the combinatorics of positroids play again a protagonist role. Therefore we believe it is likely that the combinatorial objects used in
[
Kar18
]
, such as symmetric plabic graphs, can be used in a tropical setting to provide information about the non-negative part of the tropical symplectic Grassmannian.

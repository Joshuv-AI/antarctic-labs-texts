Abstract
. In this article, we discuss subspace duals of a frame of translates by an action of a closed abelian subgroup Γ \Gamma of a locally compact group 𝒢 . \mathscr{G}. These subspace duals are not required to lie in the space generated by the frame. We characterise translation-generated subspace duals of a frame/Riesz basis involving the Zak transform for the pair ( 𝒢 , Γ ) . (\mathscr{G},\Gamma). We continue our discussion on the orthogonality of two translation-generated Bessel pairs using the Zak transform, which allows us to explore the dual of super-frames. As an example, we extend our findings to splines, Gabor systems, p p -adic fields ℚ ​ p , \mathbb{Q}p, locally compact abelian groups using the fiberization map.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex Hilbert space, and let
(\mathscr{M},\sum_{\mathscr{M}},\mu_{\mathscr{M}})
be a measure space, where
\Sigma_{\mathscr{M}}
denotes
\sigma
-algebra and
\mu_{\mathscr{M}}
the non-negative measure.

Definition 2.1
.

A family of vectors
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
in
\mathcal{H}
is called a
continuous
\mathcal{K}
-subspace frame
(simply call as
\mathcal{K}
-subspace frame

[
32
]
, or
basic frame

[
25
]
) with respect to
(\mathscr{M},\sum_{\mathscr{M}},\mu_{\mathscr{M}})
if

k\mapsto f_{k}
is weakly measurable, i.e., the map
k\mapsto\langle f,f_{k}\rangle
is measurable for each
f\in\mathcal{H}
, and
there exist constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

(2.1)

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathscr{M}}|\langle f,f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\ d{\mu_{\mathscr{M}}}(k)\leq B\|f\|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{K}.

If
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{H},
then
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
is a
frame for
\mathcal{H}
(or
total frame

[
25
]
), and it is
Bessel
in
\mathcal{H}
when only upper bound holds in (
2.1
), and
complete
when
\overline{\Span}\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}=\mathcal{H}.

Definition 2.2
.

For two Bessel families
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
and
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
in
\mathcal{H},
if
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
satisfies the following reproducing formula:

(2.2)

\int_{\mathscr{M}}\langle f,g_{k}\rangle f_{k}\ d{\mu_{\mathscr{M}}}(k)=f\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{K},

then
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
is called a
\mathcal{K}
-subspace dual
to
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}.

Note that
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
need not be a
\mathcal{K}
-subspace dual to
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}},
but the family
\{g_{k}+h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
is a
\mathcal{K}
-subspace dual to
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
provided

(2.3)

\int_{\mathscr{M}}\langle f,h_{k}\rangle g_{k}\ d{\mu_{\mathscr{M}}}(k)=0\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{K},

where
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
is Bessel in
\mathcal{H}.
Such
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
satisfying (
2.3
) is known as
\mathcal{K}
-subspace orthogonal
to
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}

[
32
]
.

Every frame or a Bessel family is associated with an analysis operator, the range of which carries out a lot of information of a signal/image or function. Given a Bessel family
\mathcal{X}=\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
in
\mathcal{H}
we define a bounded linear operator
T_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathscr{M}),
known as
analysis operator
, by

(2.4)

T_{\mathcal{X}}(f)(k)=\langle f,f_{k}\rangle\ \mbox{for all}\ k\in\mathscr{M}\ \mbox{and}\ f\in\mathcal{H},

and its adjoint operator
T_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}:L^{2}(\mathscr{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{H},
known as
synthesis operator
, by

(2.5)

T_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\psi=\int_{\mathscr{M}}\psi(k)f_{k}\ d{\mu_{\mathscr{M}}}(k)\ \mbox{for all}\ \psi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{M}),

in the weak sense. For two Bessel families
\mathcal{X}=\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
and
\mathcal{Y}:=\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
in
\mathcal{H}
the operator
T_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}T_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by
f\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle f,g_{k}\rangle f_{k}\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(k)}
is known as
mixed dual Gramian
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
and
\mathcal{Y}:=\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{M}}
be two Bessel families in
\mathcal{H}.

(i)

If

and
\mathcal{X},

\mathcal{Y}
are the frames for
\overline{\Span}\mathcal{X}=\overline{\Span}\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{H},
then
\mathcal{X},

\mathcal{Y}
are called as
dual frames
to each other, where

is an identity operator on
\overline{\Span}\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

If
T_{\mathcal{X}}^{*}T_{\mathcal{Y}}=0
on
\overline{\Span}\mathcal{X}=\overline{\Span}\mathcal{Y},
then we call
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are

.

Orthogonality plays a prominent role to generalize dual frames for super Hilbert spaces

2.2.
Zak transform and Bracket

The
Zak transform

\mathcal{Z}
of
f\in L^{1}(\mathscr{G})\cap L^{2}(\mathscr{G})
for the pair
(\mathscr{G},\Gamma)
is defined by

(2.6)

(\mathcal{Z}f)(\alpha,\Gamma x)=\widehat{f^{\Gamma x}}(\alpha)=\int_{\Gamma}f^{\Gamma x}(\gamma)\alpha(\gamma^{-1})\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}\ \mbox{a.e.}\ \alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}\ \mbox{and}\ \Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G},

which is a unitary linear transformation from
L^{2}(\mathscr{G})
to
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma}\times\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G})

[
22
]
. The space
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma}\times\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G})
is measure theoretic isomorphic to
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma};L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G})).
For
x\in\mathscr{G},

\Gamma x
is a
right coset
of
\Gamma
in
\mathscr{G}
with respect to
x.
The set
\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}=\{\Gamma x:x\in\mathscr{G}\}
denotes a collection of all right cosets of
\Gamma
in
\mathscr{G}.

For a function
f:\mathscr{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
a complex valued function
f^{\Gamma x}
on
\Gamma
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle f^{\Gamma x}(\gamma)=f(\gamma\,\Xi(\Gamma x)),\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,

where
\Xi:\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
is a
Borel section
for the quotient space
\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}
whose existence is guaranteed by
[
29
, Lemma 1.1]
. The
dual group

\widehat{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
is a collection of continuous homomorphisms from
\Gamma
to the circle group
\mathbb{T}.

In case of locally compact abelian (LCA) group
{\mathscr{G}},
the Zak transform
\mathcal{Z}
has a sibling named as the
fiberization map

\mathscr{T},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}:

\displaystyle L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow L^{2}(\widehat{{\mathscr{G}}}/\Gamma^{\perp};L^{2}(\Gamma^{\perp}))\ \mbox{ defined by}

\displaystyle(\mathscr{T}f)(\omega\Gamma^{\perp})(k)=\widehat{f}(\Theta(w\Gamma^{\perp})k)

is unitary, where
f\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})

w\Gamma^{\perp}\in\widehat{{\mathscr{G}}}/\Gamma^{\perp},
and
k\in\Gamma^{\perp}.

The group
\Gamma^{\perp}=\{\omega\in\widehat{\mathscr{G}}:\omega(\gamma)=1\ \mbox{ for all}\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}
and the quotient group
{\mathscr{G}}/\Gamma
is same as
\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}.
The Borel section
\Theta:\widehat{{\mathscr{G}}}/\Gamma^{\perp}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathscr{G}}}
sends compact sets to pre-compact sets.
In the setup of discrete group
\Gamma,
the Zak transform
\mathcal{Z}
for the pair
({\mathscr{G}},\Gamma)
can be rewritten from (
2.6
) as follows: for
f\in L^{1}({\mathscr{G}})\cap L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),

(\mathcal{Z}f)(\alpha,\Gamma x)=\widehat{f^{\Gamma x}}(\alpha)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f^{\Gamma x}(\gamma)\alpha(\gamma^{-1})\ \mbox{for}\ \alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}\ \mbox{and}\ \Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}.

We begin with the notion of matrix elements for the left regular representation
[
14
, Section 5.2]
.

Definition 2.4
.

For
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{G}),
let
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function defined by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi)(\gamma)=\left\langle\psi,L_{\gamma}\varphi\right\rangle,\ \gamma\in\Gamma.

Then
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi
is known as a
matrix element
of the left regular representation associated with
\varphi
and
\psi.

In the sequel, we require the discrete-time Fourier transform of
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi
at a point of
\widehat{\Gamma}.
Recall a
discrete-time Fourier transform

\widehat{z}(\alpha)
of a sequence
z=(z(\gamma))\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
at a point
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},
defined by
\widehat{z}(\alpha)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}z(\gamma)\overline{\alpha(\gamma)}.
The convergence of the series is interpreted as its limit in
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma}).
We describe below the discrete-time Fourier transform of
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi
in terms of the Zak transform associated with the right coset
\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{G})
be such that the associated matrix element
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi
is a member of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma).
Then the discrete-time Fourier transform of
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi
at
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}
is

\widehat{(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi)}(\alpha)=[\psi,\varphi](\alpha),

provided
[\psi,\varphi](\cdot)\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma}),

where the complex valued function
[\psi,\varphi](\cdot)
on
\widehat{\Gamma},
known as bracket map, is given by

(2.9)

[\psi,\varphi](\alpha):=\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}}\mathcal{Z}\psi(\alpha,\Gamma x)\,\overline{\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}}}(\Gamma x)\ \mbox{for}\ \alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}.

Moreover, for a Bessel sequence
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)=\{L_{\gamma}\varphi:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
in
L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),

\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi
and
[\psi,\varphi](\cdot)
are members of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
and
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma}),
respectively, and hence the above result holds true.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{Z}
is unitary, the discrete-time Fourier transform of
\{\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi(\gamma)\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
at
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}
is,

\displaystyle\widehat{(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi)}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\langle\psi,L_{\gamma}\varphi\rangle\alpha(\gamma^{-1})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\langle\mathcal{Z}\psi,\mathcal{Z}(L_{\gamma}\varphi)\rangle_{L^{2}(\hat{\Gamma}\times\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}})}\ \alpha(\gamma^{-1}).

Employing the intertwining property of the Zak transform
\mathcal{Z}
on the left translation with modulation, i.e., for
f\in L^{1}({\mathscr{G}})\cap L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),

\gamma\in\Gamma,

\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},

x\in{\mathscr{G}},
and using (
2.6
) and (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(L_{\gamma}f)(\alpha,\Gamma x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma}(L_{\gamma}f)^{\Gamma x}(\lambda)\alpha(\lambda^{-1})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma}f((\gamma^{-1}\lambda)\Xi(\Gamma x))\alpha(\lambda^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma}f^{\Gamma x}(\gamma^{-1}\lambda)\alpha(\lambda^{-1})=\alpha(\gamma^{-1})\mathcal{Z}f(\alpha,\Gamma x).

Then we obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle\widehat{(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi)}(\alpha)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\alpha(\gamma^{-1})\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\beta(\gamma)\langle\mathcal{Z}\psi(\beta),\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\beta)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}})}\ d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\beta)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\alpha(\gamma^{-1})\zeta(\gamma),

where for
\gamma\in\Gamma,
the function
\zeta(\gamma):=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}{\beta(\gamma)}\langle\mathcal{Z}\psi(\beta),\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\beta)\rangle\ d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\beta)
is identical with
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi(\gamma).

The sequence
\{\zeta(\gamma)\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
since
\{\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi(\gamma)\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma).

Further, we can write (
2.10
) for
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}
as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi)}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\alpha(\gamma^{-1})\zeta(\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}{\beta(\gamma)}\langle\mathcal{Z}\psi(\beta),\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\beta)\rangle\ d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\beta)\right)\alpha(\gamma^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}{\beta(\gamma)}\left(\int_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\mathcal{Z}\psi(\beta,\Gamma x)\overline{\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\beta,\Gamma x)}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}}(\Gamma x)\right)d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\beta)\right)\alpha(\gamma^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}[\psi,\varphi](\beta){\beta(\gamma)}\ d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\beta)\right)\alpha(\gamma^{-1}),

where for
\beta\in\widehat{\Gamma},

[\psi,\varphi](\beta)
is defined by

[\psi,\varphi](\beta)=\int_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\mathcal{Z}\psi(\beta,\Gamma x)\overline{\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\beta,\Gamma x)}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}}(\Gamma x).

Also by identifying
\Gamma
to
\widehat{\widehat{\Gamma}}
as
\gamma\mapsto\widehat{\gamma}
and noting that
\widehat{\widehat{\Gamma}}
is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma}),
we can write

\displaystyle\widehat{(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi)}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}[\psi,\varphi](\beta)\widehat{\gamma}(\beta)\ d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\beta)\right)\overline{\widehat{\gamma}(\alpha)}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left\langle[\psi,\varphi](\cdot),\overline{\widehat{\gamma}}(\cdot)\right\rangle\overline{\widehat{\gamma}(\alpha)}=[\psi,\varphi](\alpha),

provided
[\psi,\varphi](\cdot)\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma}).

For the moreover part, assume that the
\Gamma
-TG system
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is Bessel. Then for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),
we have the inequality
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|<f,L_{\gamma}\varphi>|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}

for some constant
B>0,
and hence by choosing
f=\psi,
we get
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\psi
as a member of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma).
Also the Bessel property of
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
implies
[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\leq B
a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}

[
22
]
, and hence using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\Big|[\psi,\varphi](\alpha)\Big|^{2}\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\Big|\int_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\mathcal{Z}\psi(\alpha,\Gamma x)\overline{\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)}\ {d{\mu_{\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}}}(\Gamma x)}\Big|^{2}{d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle\leq

are orthogonal pair if and only if for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},

[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)[\psi,\psi](\alpha)=0.

(v)

If the functions
\varphi
and
\psi
satisfy
(\supp\mathcal{Z}\varphi)\cap(\supp\mathcal{Z}\psi)=0
a.e., then
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace orthogonal to
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi),
where
\supp\mathcal{Z}\varphi
denotes the support of
\mathcal{Z}\varphi
by considering the map
\mathcal{Z}\varphi:\widehat{\Gamma}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}).

Proof.

(i) The expression
[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)=0
follows by observing the estimate from (
3.1
) for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},

\displaystyle|[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)|\leq\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}}\left|\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)\overline{\mathcal{Z}\psi(\alpha,\Gamma x)}\right|\ {d{\mu_{\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}}}(\Gamma x)}\leq([\varphi,\varphi](\alpha))^{1/2}([\psi,\psi](\alpha))^{1/2}

using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. From Corollary
3.8
,
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace orthogonal to
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).

(ii) For
\tilde{\psi}\in S^{\Gamma}(\psi),
we can write
\mathcal{Z}\tilde{\psi}(\alpha,\Gamma x)=\mathfrak{m}(\alpha)\mathcal{Z}\psi(\alpha,\Gamma x)
for a.e.
\ {\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}},
and
\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}
due to Proposition
3.6
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is a member of the weighted space
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma},[\psi,\psi]).
Then, we get

[\varphi,\tilde{\psi}](\alpha)=\overline{\mathfrak{m}(\alpha)}[\varphi,\psi](\alpha),
and hence the result follows since the system
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace orthogonal to
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi),
equivalently,
[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)=0
a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}
by Corollary
3.8
.

(iii) This follows easily by Corollary
3.8
.

(iv) It is enough to show the orthogonality of the Bessel pairs
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
implies the expression
[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)[\psi,\psi](\alpha)
become zero for a.e.
\alpha
in view of Corollary
3.8
and part (i). For this, we proceed similar to the part (i) of Corollary
3.8
by considering
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi=0
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)=\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\psi).
Then we get
[\psi,\psi](\alpha)\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)=0
by choosing
f=\psi
and hence, we obtain either
[\psi,\psi](\alpha)=0
or
[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)=0
for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}.
This proves the result.

(v) Observe that the set
\{\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}:\{\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)\}_{\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\neq 0\}
is same as the set
\{\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}:\|\{\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)\}_{\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\|\neq 0\}
which is further equal to
\{\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}:[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\neq 0\}.
Therefore, the support of
\mathcal{Z}\varphi
is same as the support of
[\varphi,\varphi].
Hence
(\mbox{supp}\ \mathcal{Z}\varphi)\cap(\mbox{supp}\ \mathcal{Z}\psi)=0
a.e. implies
(\mbox{supp}\ [\varphi,\varphi])\cap(\mbox{supp}\ [\psi,\psi])=0
a.e. Thus, we get
[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)[\psi,\psi](\alpha)=0
for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}.
Now from part (i) we get result. ∎

Theorem 3.10

\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).

Moreover,
\tilde{\psi}\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\psi)

In particular, the
\tilde{\psi}\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\psi)

[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)\,(\mathcal{Z}\tilde{\psi})(\alpha,\Gamma x)=(\mathcal{Z}\psi)(\alpha,\Gamma x)\,\chi_{\Omega_{\varphi}}(\alpha)\ \mbox{for all}\ \Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}.

Proof.

)
as

respectively

\tilde{\psi}\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
if and only if for a.e.

for all

from Proposition

. From Corollary

4.
Translation generated Biorthogonal system and subspace Riesz basis

For two non-zero functions
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),
the
\Gamma
-TG systems
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
are said to be
biorthogonal
if
\langle L_{\gamma}\varphi,L_{\gamma^{\prime}}\psi\rangle=\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}
for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma.
Throughout the section, we assume
\Gamma
to be discrete abelian subgroup of
{\mathscr{G}}.
The following result characterizes biorthogonal systems in terms of the Zak transform and describes when a translation generated system will be linearly independent.
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is
linearly independent
if the expression
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}L_{\gamma}\varphi=0
for some
\{c_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
implies
c_{\gamma}=0
for all
\gamma.

Theorem 4.1
.

For non-zero functions
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),
the
\Gamma
-TG systems
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
are biorthogonal if and only if for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},

[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)=1.
In case of either one holds, then

(i)

The systems
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
are linearly independent.

(ii)

\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace dual to
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
for compactly supported
\varphi
and
\psi.

Proof.

Firstly observe that the biorthogonal relation between the
\Gamma
-TG systems
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is equivalent to
\langle L_{\gamma}\varphi,\psi\rangle=\delta_{\gamma,0}
for
\gamma\in\Gamma.
Now

\displaystyle\delta_{\gamma,0}=\langle\mathcal{Z}L_{\gamma}\varphi,\mathcal{Z}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma};L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}))}=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\int_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)\overline{\mathcal{Z}\psi(\alpha,\Gamma x)\alpha(\gamma)}\ {d{\mu_{\Gamma\backslash\mathscr{G}}}(\Gamma x)}\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)\overline{\alpha(\gamma)}\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}.

Thus the result follows by the uniqueness of the Fourier coefficients.

For the remaining part of (i), let
\{c_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
be such that

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}L_{\gamma}\varphi=0.
Then for each
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma,

0=\langle 0,L_{\gamma^{\prime}}\psi\rangle=\langle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}L_{\gamma}\varphi,L_{\gamma^{\prime}}\psi\rangle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\langle L_{\gamma}\varphi,L_{\gamma^{\prime}}\psi\rangle=c_{\gamma^{\prime}}

by the biorthogonal relation between
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi),
and hence all
c_{\gamma}
’s are zero. Thus
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is linearly independent. Similarly,
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is also linearly independent.

(ii) Due to
\langle\varphi,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle=\delta_{\gamma,0}
for
\gamma\in\Gamma,
we have
f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi
for all
f\in\mbox{span}\ \mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\Gamma_{1}
is a finite subset of
\Gamma,
which holds for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
in view of compactly supported functions
\varphi
and
\psi,
and the continuity of the function
f\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi.
Note that
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
are Bessel families since
\{[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}}
and
\{[\psi,\psi](\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}}
are bounded sets for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}

[
22
]
. The boundedness of
\{[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}}
follows by observing the continuity of the function
\alpha\mapsto[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)
from the compact set
\widehat{\Gamma}
to
\mathbb{R}.
Indeed
[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)
is a polynomial for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},
that can be realised by writing it in the form of Fourier series expansion where only finitely many Fourier coefficients are non-zero in view of the compact support of
\varphi.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{G})
be such that for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},

C\leq[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\leq D
for some constants
0<C\leq D<\infty.
Then there is a
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{G})
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
are biorthogonal systems. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is linearly independent.

Proof.

For
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{G}),
choose
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathscr{G})
satisfying
\mathcal{Z}\varphi(\alpha,\Gamma x)=\mathcal{Z}\psi(\alpha,\Gamma x)[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)
for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}
and
\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}.
Then
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
are biorthogonal systems in view of Theorem
4.1
since
[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)=1
for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}.
The moreover part also follows by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Our next goal is to fix a function
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
and want to find
\psi\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace dual to
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
by following the idea of Theorem
4.1
. To find such
\psi,
we will assume
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
with compact support such that
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace Riesz basis in the next result.
By an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace Riesz basis
, we mean
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace frame and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is linearly independent. Equivalently, there are
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that
A\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|^{2}\leq\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}L_{\gamma}\varphi\|^{2}\leq B\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|^{2}
for some sequence
\{c_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
having finitely many non-zero terms. We refer
[
8
,
22
,
31
]
for more details.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
be a function with compact support such that
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace Riesz basis. If there exists a function
\psi\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is biorthogonal to
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi),

\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is an
S^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace dual to
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).
Moreover,
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
is also an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
-subspace Riesz basis.

Proof.

Firstly note that for any
f\in\mbox{span}\ \mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi),
we can write
f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}}\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi
due to the biorthogonality of
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
for some finite set
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma.
We need to show the expression for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).
For this let

f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).
Then there is an element
g\in\mbox{span}\ \mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
such that
\|f-g\|<\epsilon
for
\epsilon>0.
By writing

g=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi
where
\Gamma_{1}
is a finite subset of
\Gamma,
we have
f-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi=(f-g)+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\langle(g-f),L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi,
and by taking norm on the both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|+\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\langle(g-f),L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|+\sqrt{B}\Big(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}|\langle(g-f),L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{B^{\prime}B})\|f-g\|<(1+\sqrt{B^{\prime}B})\epsilon

for some
B,B^{\prime}>0
since
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace Riesz basis. The last inequality holds true provided
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2}\leq B^{\prime}\|f\|^{2}
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).
For this, let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).
Then there is a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mbox{span}\,\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\|f_{n}-f\|=0,
and also by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}
\langle f_{n},L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle=\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma.
Hence for any finite set
\Gamma_{1}
of
\Gamma,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}|\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\langle f_{n},L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}|\langle f_{n},L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2}\leq B^{\prime}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}\|^{2}=B^{\prime}\|f\|^{2},

provided
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2}\leq B^{\prime}\|f\|^{2}
for all
f\in\mbox{span}\,\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi).
For this we proceed as follows.

By writing
f\in\mbox{span}\,\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
in the form
f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}L_{\gamma}\varphi
with finitely many non-zeros
\{c_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell^{2},
we get
\|f\|^{2}=\|\mathcal{Z}f\|^{2}=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}|\widehat{c}(\alpha)|^{2}[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}
by following the steps of (
3.3
). Since
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
is an
\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
-subspace Riesz basis, we have
C\leq[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\leq D
for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma}
and some
0<C\leq D<\infty

[
22
, Remark 5.6]
, and hence
C\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}|\widehat{c}(\alpha)|^{2}{d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}\leq\|f\|^{2}\leq D\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}|\widehat{c}(\gamma)|^{2}\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}.
Thus, we get

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}|\widehat{c}(\alpha)|^{2}\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}\leq\frac{1}{C}\|f\|^{2}.
Further due to the biorthogonality of the sets
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\psi)
and Parseval’s formula, we write

\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}|\widehat{c}(\alpha)|^{2}\ {d{\mu_{\widehat{\Gamma}}}(\alpha)}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2},
which gives the inequality

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\langle f,L_{\gamma}\psi\rangle|^{2}\leq B^{\prime}\|f\|^{2}
for
f\in\mbox{span}\,\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)
and
B^{\prime}=\frac{1}{C}.
Thus the result follows.
∎

\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),
we mean it is a collection of functions of the form
\{\oplus_{n=1}^{N}f^{(n)}:=(f^{(1)},f^{(2)},\cdots,f^{(N)}):f^{(n)}\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),1\leq n\leq N\}
with the inner product
\langle\oplus_{n=1}^{N}f^{(n)},\oplus_{n=1}^{N}g^{(n)}\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle f^{(n)},g^{(n)}\rangle.
Indeed,
\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
is nothing but the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}),
where
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
is an abelian group with modulo
N.

Analogous to the classical trend, we state the following characterization result for dual frames of translates in the super Hilbert space
\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
(named as super dual frame).

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{N}
be an
\sigma
-finite measure space with counting measure. For
1\leq n\leq N,
let
\{\varphi_{t}^{(n)}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N}}
and
\{\psi_{t}^{(n)}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N}}
be two collections of functions in
L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
such that
\{L_{\gamma}\varphi_{t}^{(n)}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
and
\{L_{\gamma}\psi_{t}^{(n)}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
are Bessel. For each
\gamma\in\Gamma,
define the translation operator
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}:=\oplus^{N}L_{\gamma}
which acts on an element
\oplus_{n=1}^{N}f^{(n)}
by
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}f^{(n)})=\oplus_{n=1}^{N}L_{\gamma}f^{(n)}.
Then
\{\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
and
\{\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\psi_{t}^{(n)})\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
are (super) dual frames in
\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
if and only if for a.e.
\alpha\in\widehat{\Gamma},
(i) the systems

\left\{\{\mathcal{Z}\varphi_{t}^{(n)}(\alpha,\Gamma x)\}_{\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\right\}_{t\in\mathcal{N}}
and
\left\{\{\mathcal{Z}\psi_{t}^{(n)}(\alpha,\Gamma x)\}_{\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\right\}_{t\in\mathcal{N}}
are dual frames in
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}})
for
1\leq n\leq N,
and
(ii)
\left\{\{\mathcal{Z}\varphi_{t}^{(n_{1})}(\alpha,\Gamma x)\}_{\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}\right\}_{t\in\mathcal{N}}
and
\left\{\{\mathcal{Z}\psi_{t}^{(n_{2})}(\alpha,\Gamma x)\}_{\Gamma x\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathscr{G}}}:t\in\mathcal{N}\right\}
are orthogonal pair for
1\leq n_{1}\neq n_{2}\leq N.

Proof.

Assume the systems
\{\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
and
\{\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\psi_{t}^{(n)})\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
are (super) dual frames in
\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}).
Then for each
1\leq n\leq N,
(i) follows by just applying the orthogonal projection
P_{n}
on it and Corollary
5.3
. For the part (ii), let
1\leq n_{1}\neq n_{2}\leq N
and
h\in\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}).
Then we have
P_{n_{1}}(P_{n_{2}}h)=0,
where
P_{n_{1}}(P_{n_{2}}h)
is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}\langle P_{n_{2}}h,P_{n_{2}}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\rangle P_{n_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\psi_{t}^{(n)})\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)=\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}\langle P_{n_{2}}h,L_{\gamma}\varphi_{t}^{(n_{2})}\rangle L_{\gamma}\psi_{t}^{(n_{1})}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t).

Hence,
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\{\varphi_{t}^{(n_{2})}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N}})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}(\{\psi_{t}^{(n_{1})}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N}})
are orthogonal pair. Therefore (ii) follows.

Conversely, let us assume (i) and (ii) hold. Then notice that the both
\{\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
and
\{\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\psi_{t}^{(n)})\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
are Bessel families in
\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}}),
follows by the below calculations for
h\in L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})^{N}
using the Bessel property of
\{L_{\gamma}\varphi_{t}^{(n)}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
with Bessel bound
B^{(n)}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle|\langle h,\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\rangle|^{2}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)=\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}|\langle\oplus_{n=1}^{N}P_{n}h,\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\rangle|^{2}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle P_{n}h,L_{\gamma}\varphi_{t}^{(n)}\rangle\Big|^{2}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)\leq C\|h\|^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}B^{(n)},

for some constant
C>0
(similarly for
\{L_{\gamma}\psi_{t}^{(n)}\}_{t\in\mathcal{N},\gamma\in\Gamma}
). Thus we have the result using Theorem
5.3
, by just looking the reproducing formula for each
h\in\oplus^{N}L^{2}({\mathscr{G}})
and writing
h=\oplus_{n=1}^{N}P_{n}h
in the below calculations:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}\langle h,\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\psi_{t}^{(n)})\rangle\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)=\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle P_{n}h,L_{\gamma}\psi_{t}^{(n)}\rangle\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}(\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{(n)})\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle P_{n}h,L_{\gamma}\psi_{t}^{(n)}\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi_{t}^{(1)}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)\oplus\cdots\oplus\int_{\mathcal{N}}\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle P_{n}h,L_{\gamma}\psi_{t}^{(n)}\rangle L_{\gamma}\varphi_{t}^{(N)}\ d{\mu_{\Gamma}}(\gamma)\ d{\mu_{\mathcal{N}}}(t)

\displaystyle=P_{1}h\oplus\dots\oplus P_{N}h=h.

∎

6.4.1.

\mathcal{Z}f(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f(y+\xi)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\ d{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}(\xi)

6.4.2.
Semidirect product of LCA groups

For LCA groups
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2},
let us consider a locally compact group
\mathscr{G}_{\tau}=\Gamma_{1}\rtimes_{\tau}\Gamma_{2}
by the semidirect product of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
with the binary operation

(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).(\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime})=(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}\tau_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{2})),
where
\gamma_{1}\mapsto\tau_{\gamma_{1}}
is a group homomorphism from
\Gamma_{1}
to the set of all automorphisms on
\Gamma_{2},
such that
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mapsto\tau_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{2})
from
\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}\mapsto\Gamma_{2}
is continuous. Then the Zak transform
\mathcal{Z}
is defined by
\mathcal{Z}f(\gamma_{1},w)=\int_{\Gamma_{2}}f(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\overline{w(\gamma_{2})}\delta(\gamma_{1})\ d{\mu_{\Gamma_{2}}}(\gamma_{2})
for
f\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{1}\times_{\tau}\Gamma_{2}),

(\gamma_{1},\omega)\in\Gamma_{1}\rtimes_{\widehat{\tau}}\widehat{\Gamma_{2}},
where
\delta
is a positive homeomorphism on
\Gamma_{1}
given by

d{\mu_{\Gamma_{2}}}(\gamma_{2})=\delta(\gamma_{1})d{\mu_{\Gamma_{2}}}({\tau_{\gamma_{1}}}(\gamma_{2})).
It can be extended
from
L^{2}(\Gamma_{1}\times_{\tau}\Gamma_{2})
to
L^{2}(\Gamma_{1}\rtimes_{\widehat{\tau}}\widehat{\Gamma_{2}}).

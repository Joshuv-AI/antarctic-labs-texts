A Generalized Hamming Distance of Sequence Patterns
Pengyu Liu 1,2, Jingzhou Na 2

Abstract
We define sequence patterns of length n n and level ℓ \ell to be equivalence classes of sequences that have n n elements from the set of ℓ \ell integer symbols { 1 , 2 , … , ℓ } \left\{1,2,\ldots,\ell\right\} with no restriction on repetition, where the equivalence relation is induced by symbol relabeling without swapping positions of symbols. We define a distance for a set of k k sequence patterns of length n n and level ℓ \ell by generalizing the Hamming distance between sequences. We compute the maximal distance for k k sequence patterns of length n n and level ℓ \ell and demonstrate how to calculate the exact distance between a pair of length- n n level- ℓ \ell sequence patterns.

3.2
Metric spaces of sequence patterns

It is well known that the Hamming distance between two sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
is a metric.
Namely, the Hamming distance has the following three properties.

1.

Identity: For any sequences
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
, we have
d(q,p)=0
if and only if
q=p
.

2.

Symmetry: For any sequences
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
, we have
d(q,p)=d(p,q)
;

3.

Triangle inequality: For any sequences
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\in\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
, the inequality
d(q_{1},q_{3})+d(q_{2},q_{3})\geq d(q_{1},q_{2})
holds.

It is trivial to check that the generalized Hamming distance of two sequence patterns in
\mathcal{T}_{n,\ell}
satisfies the first two properties.
Here, we show that the triangle inequality holds.
Consider three sequence patterns
t_{1}=\langle q_{1}\rangle,t_{2}=\langle q_{2}\rangle
and
t_{3}=\langle q_{3}\rangle
in
\mathcal{T}_{n,\ell}
.
Suppose that
d(t_{1},t_{3})=d(\phi_{1}(q_{1}),q_{3})
for a permutation
\phi_{1}\in S_{\ell}
and that
d(t_{2},t_{3})=d(\phi_{2}(q_{2}),q_{3})
for a permutation
\phi_{2}\in S_{\ell}
.
We have
d(t_{1},t_{3})+d(t_{2},t_{3})=d(\phi_{1}(q_{1}),q_{3})+d(\phi_{2}(q_{2}),q_{3})\geq d(\phi_{1}(q_{1}),\phi_{2}(q_{2}))
.
Note that
d(t_{1},t_{2})
is defined to be the minimal distance of
d(\phi_{a}(q_{1}),\phi_{b}(q_{2}))
over all
\phi_{a},\phi_{b}\in S_{\ell}
.
We have
d(\phi_{1}(q_{1}),\phi_{2}(q_{2}))\geq d(t_{1},t_{2})
and
d(t_{1},t_{3})+d(t_{2},t_{3})\geq d(t_{1},t_{2})
.
Therefore, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

The generalized Hamming distance defined for a pair of sequences patterns in
\mathcal{T}_{n,\ell}
is a metric, and all sequence patterns in
\mathcal{T}_{n,\ell}
together with the generalized Hamming distance for two sequence patterns form a metric space.

.
Since the cross sections
\Phi(c)
and
\Phi(c^{\prime})
are constant in
\Phi(Q)
, we have
\phi_{i}(a_{i})=\phi_{j}(a_{j})
and
\phi_{i}(b_{i})=\phi_{j}(b_{j})
.
Furthermore, because
a_{i}=b_{i}
, we have
\phi_{i}(a_{i})=\phi_{i}(b_{i})
.
These imply that
\phi_{j}(a_{j})=\phi_{j}(b_{j})
, which contradicts
a_{j}\neq b_{j}
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
2
can be generalized for a set of pairwise connected cross sections.

Lemma 3
.

Let
Q=\left[q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{k}\right]
be a set of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
.
If
Q
contains
m
pairwise connected cross sections, then there exists a sequence of permutations
\Phi^{*}\in S_{\ell}^{k}
such that the
m
pairwise connected cross sections are mapped to
m
constant cross sections by
\Phi^{*}
.

Proof.

For any
1\leq i\leq m
, let
c^{i}=\left[a^{i}_{1},a^{i}_{2},\ldots,a^{i}_{k}\right]
be one of the
m
pairwise connected cross sections in
Q
.
Since connected cross sections can be identical, we assume that there are
w\leq m
unique cross sections among them.
Without loss of generality, let
c^{1},c^{2},\ldots,c^{w}
be the unique cross sections.
Because they are pairwise incompatible, the elements
a^{1}_{j}
,
a^{2}_{j}
,…
a^{w}_{j}
are
w
distinct symbols for any
1\leq j\leq k
. We define a sequence of permutations
\Phi^{*}=\left[\phi^{*}_{1},\phi^{*}_{2},\ldots,\phi^{*}_{k}\right]\in S_{\ell}^{k}
by the two-line notation in Formula (
16
)

\phi^{*}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{1}_{j}&a^{2}_{j}&\dots&a^{w-1}_{j}&a^{w}_{j}&\cdots\\
a^{1}_{1}&a^{2}_{1}&\dots&a^{w-1}_{1}&a^{w}_{1}&\cdots\end{pmatrix}

(16)

Note that
c^{1},c^{2},\ldots,c^{w}
being pairwise incompatible implies that the sequence of permutations
\Phi^{*}
is well defined.
It is trivial to check that the
m
connected cross sections are mapped to
m
constant cross sections by
\Phi^{*}
.
∎

We examine the connectedness of cross sections in
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell}
.
There are in total
\ell^{k}
cross sections in
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell}
.
We divide
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell}
into
\ell
subsets based on their first elements, and we denote the subset of cross sections with first element
i
by
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|i}
.
Let
c
be a cross section in
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|i}
and
c^{\prime}
be a cross section in
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|i+1}
.
We say that
c
is
linked
to
c^{\prime}
if
c^{\prime}=\psi(c)
, where
\psi=(12\ldots\ell)\in S_{\ell}
.
Let
c
be a cross section in
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|1}
.
We define a
link

\mathcal{L}(c)
generated by
c
to be a subset of
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell}
such that
\mathcal{L}(c)=\left\{c,\psi(c),\psi^{2}(c),\ldots,\psi^{\ell-1}(c)\right\}
.
For instance, we display a link of
\mathcal{C}_{5,3}
in Example (
17
).

c=[1,2,3,1,1]\quad\psi(c)=[2,3,1,2,2]\quad\psi^{2}(c)=[3,1,2,3,3]

(17)

It is clear that for any cross section
c\in\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|1}
, every subset
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|i}
has one and only one element in
\mathcal{L}(c)
.
It is also trivial that for different cross sections
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|1}
, we have
\mathcal{L}(c)\cap\mathcal{L}(c^{\prime})=\emptyset
.
So the links partition
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell}
, and there are
\ell^{k-1}
links in
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell}
.
Moreover, since
\psi
increases every symbol by
1
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
(where we set
0=\ell
), every pair of cross sections in a link is incompatible, hence connected.

We say that a set
Q
of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
is
complete
if
\Phi(Q)
contains one and only one constant cross section for any sequence of permutations
\Phi\in S_{\ell}^{k}
.
We say that
Q
is
semi-complete
if
\Phi(Q)
contains at most one constant cross section for any sequence of permutations
\Phi\in S_{\ell}^{k}
.
Note that there exist totally
\ell^{k-1}
cross sections in
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|1}
.
We list these cross sections in lexicographic order of the elements in them and construct a set
M=\left[q^{*}_{1},q^{*}_{2},\ldots,q^{*}_{k}\right]
of sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell^{k-1},\ell}
such that the cross sections in
M
are the cross sections from
\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|1}
in lexicographic order. See Formula (
18
).
For a positive integer
r<\ell^{k-1}
, we define
M_{r}
to be the set of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{r,\ell}
that contains only the first
r
cross sections in
M
.

M=\left[q^{*}_{1},q^{*}_{2},\ldots,q^{*}_{k}\right]=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\ldots&1&1\\
1&1&\ldots&1&2\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&1&\ldots&1&\ell\\
1&1&\ldots&2&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\ell&\ldots&\ell&\ell\\
\end{bmatrix}

(18)

Lemma 4
.

The set
M
of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell^{k-1},\ell}
is complete, and the set
M_{r}
of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{r,\ell}
is semi-complete.

Proof.

Because
q^{*}_{1}
is a constant sequence and there is no identical cross sections in
M
or
M_{r}
, no pair of cross sections in
M
or
M_{r}
is connected.
By Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
, we have
d(\langle M\rangle)=\ell^{k-1}-1
, and
d(\langle M_{r}\rangle)=r-1
.
So
M
and
M_{r}
are semi-complete.

Let
M^{\prime}=\left[q^{*}_{2},q^{*}_{3},\ldots,q^{*}_{k}\right]
be a set of
k-1
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell^{k-1},\ell}
.
The cross sections in
M^{\prime}
contain all combinations of assigning
\ell
symbols to
k-1
positions.
So there are
\ell
unique constant cross sections in
M^{\prime}
, namely
\left[1,1,\ldots,1\right]
,
\left[2,2,\ldots,2\right]
,…,
\left[\ell,\ell,\ldots,\ell\right]
.
For any sequence of permutations
\Phi^{\prime}=\left[\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\ldots,\phi_{k}\right]\in S_{\ell}^{k-1}
, these constant cross sections persist in
\Phi^{\prime}(M^{\prime})
.
So for any sequence of permutations
\Phi=\left[\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{k}\right]
in
S_{\ell}^{k}
, no matter what
\phi_{1}(1)\in L
in
\Phi(M)
is, there always exists a constant cross section in
\Phi(M)
.
Therefore,
M
is complete.
∎

Theorem 4
.

The maximal distance of
k
sequence patterns of length
n
and level
\ell
is given by Formula (
19
).

D_{n,\ell,k}=n-{\left\lceil\frac{n}{\ell^{k-1}}\right\rceil}

(19)

Proof.

For any set
Q
of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
, there exists a link
\mathcal{L}(c)
generated by a cross section
c\in\mathcal{C}_{k,\ell|1}
such that
Q
has at least
{\left\lceil n/\ell^{k-1}\right\rceil}
cross sections of
\mathcal{L}(c)
due to the pigeonhole principle.
These cross sections in
\mathcal{L}(c)
are connected, so they are constant cross sections in
\Phi(Q)
for a sequence of permutations
\Phi\in S_{\ell}
by Lemma
3
.
Therefore, we have
s(\Phi(Q))\geq{\left\lceil n/\ell^{k-1}\right\rceil}
and
D_{n,\ell,k}\leq n-{\left\lceil n/\ell^{k-1}\right\rceil}
.

Suppose that
n=m\ell^{k-1}+r
for integers
m\geq 0
and
0\leq r<\ell^{k-1}
.
Consider the set
M_{n}=[M;M;\ldots;M;M_{r}]
of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
constructed by vertically concatenating
m
copies of
M
and one copy of
M_{r}
.
By Lemma
4
, the set
M
is complete and the set
M_{r}
is semi-complete.
So, there are at most
m+1
constant cross sections in
\Phi(M_{n})
for any sequence of permutations
\Phi\in S_{e}^{k}
.
Therefore, we have
d(\langle M_{n}\rangle)=n-(m+1)=n-{\left\lceil n/\ell^{k-1}\right\rceil}
and
D_{n,\ell,k}=n-{\left\lceil n/\ell^{k-1}\right\rceil}
.
∎

3.4
Computing exact Hamming distance

Let
T=\langle Q\rangle=\left[\langle q_{1}\rangle,\langle q_{2}\rangle,\ldots,\langle q_{k}\rangle\right]
be a set of sequence patterns in
\mathcal{T}_{n,\ell}
and
C(Q)=\left[c_{1};c_{2};\ldots;c_{n}\right]
be the sequence of all cross sections in
Q
.
We say that a subset
X(Q)\subset C(Q)
is
maximally connected
if all the cross sections in
X(Q)
are pairwise connected, and any cross section in
C(Q)-X(Q)
is connected to some cross section in
X(Q)
.
We use
\mathcal{X}_{i}(Q)
to denote the set of all maximally connected subsets of
C(Q)
that contain
c_{i}
, and we use
v(\mathcal{X}_{i}(Q))
to denote the maximum cardinality of elements in
\mathcal{X}_{i}(Q)
.
Lemma
2
implies that two cross sections that are not connected can not both be mapped to constant cross sections by any sequence of permutations
\Phi\in S_{\ell}^{k}
.
So, the minimum number of cross sections in
C(Q)-X(Q)
over all maximally connected subset
X(Q)\subset C(Q)
gives the exact distance
d(\langle Q\rangle)
, and we have the following Lemma
5
, where Formula (
20
) can be used to compute the exact distance.

Lemma 5
.

Let
Q=\left[q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{k}\right]
be a set of
k
sequences in
\mathcal{Q}_{n,\ell}
.

d(\langle Q\rangle)=n-\max_{\Phi\in S_{\ell}^{k}}s(\Phi(Q))=n-\max_{1\leq i\leq n}v(\mathcal{X}_{i}(Q))

(20)

Let
T=\langle Q\rangle=\left[\langle q_{1}\rangle,\langle q_{2}\rangle\right]
be a set of two sequence patterns in
\mathcal{T}_{n,\ell}
and
C(Q)=\left[c_{1};c_{2};\ldots;c_{n}\right]
be the sequence of all cross sections in
Q
.
To compute the exact distance of the sequence patterns in
\langle Q\rangle
, we construct an
\ell\times\ell
matrix
A_{Q}
as follows.
For any cross section
c=\left[a,b\right]\in\mathcal{C}_{2,\ell}
where
a,b\in L
, if
c
appears
m
times in
C(Q)
, then the entry of
A_{Q}
at row
a
and column
b
is
A_{Q}(a,b)=m
.
Moreover, any sequence of permutations
\Phi=\left[\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\right]\in S_{\ell}^{2}
can be written as a permutation matrix
P_{\Phi}
, where
\phi_{1}
permutes rows and
\phi_{2}
permutes columns.
Note that the diagonal entries of
A_{Q}
record the number of constant cross sections in
Q
.
Thus Formula (
12
) can be written as Formula (
21
).

d(\langle Q\rangle)=n-\max_{\Phi\in S_{\ell}^{2}}\mathrm{tr}(P_{\Phi}A_{Q})

(21)

This is equivalent to the linear assignment problem, and we can use the Hungarian algorithm or Kuhn-Munkres algorithm to compute the exact distance between two sequence patterns in polynomial time
Kuhn1955
;
Munkres1957
.
In general, computing the exact distance of a set
T=\langle Q\rangle=\left[\langle q_{1}\rangle,\langle q_{2}\rangle,\ldots,\langle q_{k}\rangle\right]
of sequence patterns in
\mathcal{T}_{n,\ell}
is equivalent to the
k
-dimensional assignment problem, and algorithms to solve the assignment problem, for example in
Li2021
, can be used to compute the exact distance of sequence patterns.

4
Discussion

We have formally defined the Hamming distance for
k
sequence patterns of length
n
and level
\ell
.
We have computed the maximal Hamming distance of
k
sequence patterns of length
n
and level
\ell
and demonstrated how to compute the exact distance between two sequence patterns of length
n
and level
\ell
.
There remains considerable scope to modify the definitions and ask various questions.
For example, we can impose restrictions such as all symbols must be present in a sequence or present symbols must appear twice;
inspired by coloring problems, we can require that adjacent elements in a sequence must have distinct symbols.
With these constraints, what is the maximal distance for such sequence patterns?
Lastly, it would also be interesting to check if the distance for
k
sequence patterns is a metric under some generalization of the triangle inequality, which can involve hypergraphs
Friedgut2004
.

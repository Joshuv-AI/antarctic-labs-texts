Fixed points and normal automorphisms of the unit ball of bounded operators on ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n}
Rachna Aggarwal, Krishnendu Gongopadhyay and Mukund Madhav Mishra

Abstract
. We examine the group of isometries of the open unit ball of a complex Banach space of certain bounded linear operators equipped with the Carathéodory metric. Therein we obtain a characterization of the normal isometries in terms of their special type of fixed points.

In this article, we consider certain infinite dimensional extension of these pseudo-unitary groups and investigate one of their significant subclass from the fixed point classification perspective. The subclass under consideration is the class of normal elements. If we consider holomorphic isometry on the open unit ball of the complex Banach space of bounded linear operators between Hilbert spaces then it can be represented by a linear operator on a Hilbert space.
We reserve the phrases ‘normal isometry’   and ‘non-unitary normal isometry’   for an isometry whose linear representation is normal and non-unitary normal as an operator.
A bounded linear operator on a Hilbert space is called normal if it commutes with its Hilbert adjoint and unitary if its Hilbert adjoint coincides with its inverse. An operator is non-unitary normal if it is not unitary and a normal operator. The central result of this article is Theorem
1.4
which characterizes a non-unitary normal isometry on the basis of a special class of its fixed points. Before describing our main result, we make the following set up below.

2.

As mentioned above, let

A biholomorphic map or a holomorphic automorphism of

[

throughout this article, the word isometry would either mean a biholomorphic automorphism of

As Harris
[

]

[

]

Remark 1
.

[

For a contraction

For

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptT_{B}(A)=(I-BB^{*})^{-1/2}(A+B)(I+B^{*}A)^{-1}(I-B^{*}B)^{1/2}

[

As a corollary, we present the form of self-adjoint isometries.

Corollary 3.1.1
.

Let
T=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}BU&CV\\
C^{*}U&EV\\
\end{array}}\right]\in\mathcal{G}
be a normal isometry. Then
T
is self-adjoint if and only if both

and
V
are self-adjoint operators.

Proof.

T
is self-adjoint if and only if
T=T^{*}
, i.e.

\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}BU&CV\\
C^{*}U&EV\\
\end{array}}\right]=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}U^{-1}B&U^{-1}C\\

\end{array}}\right]

which on equating the respective entries gives

and
EV=V^{-1}E
. Now corollary follows using Proposition
1.1
and the fact that a unitary operator is self-adjoint if and only if it is an involution, i.e. order two element.
∎

Proof.

Let us understand the general form of elements of

From the proof of Theorem

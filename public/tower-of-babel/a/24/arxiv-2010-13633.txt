Ramsey upper density of infinite graph factors
József Balogh, Ander Lamaison

Abstract
The study of upper density problems on Ramsey theory was initiated by Erdős and Galvin in 1993. In this paper we are concerned with the following problem: given a fixed finite graph F F , what is the largest value of λ \lambda such that every 2-edge-coloring of the complete graph on ℕ \mathbb{N} contains a monochromatic infinite F F -factor whose vertex set has upper density at least λ \lambda ? Here we prove a new lower bound for this problem. For some choices of F F , including cliques and odd cycles, this new bound is sharp, as it matches an older upper bound. For the particular case where F F is a triangle, we also give an explicit lower bound of 1 − 1 7 = 0.62203 ​ … 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}=0.62203\dots , improving the previous best bound of 3/5.

Introduction

Let
K_{\mathbb{N}}
denote the complete graph on the natural numbers. Given a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, its upper density is defined as
\bar{d}(S)=\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|S\cap[t]|}{t}
. Given a subgraph
G\subseteq K_{\mathbb{N}}
, its upper density is
\bar{d}(G)=\bar{d}(V(G))
.

Definition 1
.

Let
H
be a countably infinite graph. The Ramsey (upper) density of
H
, denoted by
\rho(H)
, is the supremum of the values of
\lambda
such that, in every red-blue-coloring of the edges of
K_{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a monochromatic subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq K_{\mathbb{N}}
isomorphic to
H
with
\bar{d}(H^{\prime})\geq\lambda
.

This concept was first introduced by Erdős and Galvin
[
6
]
, for the particular case of the infinite path
P_{\infty}
. They proved that
2/3\leq\rho(P_{\infty})\leq 8/9
. After some improvements on the lower bound by DeBiasio and McKenney
[
4
]
and by Lo, Sanhueza-Matamala and Wang
[
9
]
, the Ramsey density of the infinite path was determined by Corsten, DeBiasio, Lang and the second author in
[
2
]
, as
\rho(P_{\infty})=(12+\sqrt{8})/17=0.87226\dots
The parameter for general
H
was first defined by DeBiasio and McKenney
[
4
]
. Many results on the Ramsey density of different classes of graphs
H
can be found in
[
8
]
and
[
3
]
.

We focus on the case of infinite factors. Given a finite graph
F
, the infinite
F
-factor, denoted by
\omega\cdot F
, is the graph formed by infinitely many pairwise disjoint copies of
F
. Several results relating to the Ramsey density of infinite factors appear in
[
8
]
.

Some of these are given in terms of a certain fixed function
f
which is defined as follows. The reader should be forgiven for skipping such an unwieldy definition, as it will not be actually used in this paper. Instead, we will rely on the results from
[
8
]
that use
f
. These will be treated as black boxes.

Definition 2
.

Given a continuous function
g:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma,t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\Gamma^{+}_{\gamma}(g,t)=\min\{x:\gamma x+g(x)\geq t\},\hskip 22.76228pt\Gamma^{-}_{\gamma}(g,t)=\min\{x:\gamma x-g(x)\geq t\},

where the minimum of the empty set is taken as
+\infty
. We define the function
h(\gamma)
as the infimum, over all 1-Lipschitz functions
g
with
g(0)=0
, of

\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{+}_{\gamma}(g,t)+\Gamma^{-}_{\gamma}(g,t)}{t}.

We take
f:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

f(\lambda)=1-\frac{1}{\frac{2\lambda}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}h\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda+1}\right)+\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}}.

We extend this definition by continuity to
f(0)=1
and
f(+\infty)=1/2
.

Plot of the bounds on the function
f(x)
. The conjectured value is given in blue.

This function satisfies the following bounds:

\frac{x+1}{2x+1}\leq f(x)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{2x^{2}+3x+7+2\sqrt{x+1}}{4x^{2}+4x+9}&\text{for}&0\leq x<3,\\
&&\\
\frac{x+1}{2x}&\text{for}&x\geq 3,\end{array}\right.

(1)

and the upper bound is known to be sharp for
x\in[0,1]
. A conjecture in
[
8
]
states that the upper bound is the correct value for all
x
. Note that, with this definition,
\rho(P_{\infty})=f(1)
. The three results from
[
8
]
that apply to infinite factors, one upper bound and two lower bounds, are as follows:

Theorem 1

(
[
8
]
)
.

(i)

For every non-empty finite graph
F
, we have

\rho(\omega\cdot F)\leq f\left(\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\text{ indep. in }F\\
I\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{|N(I)|}{|I|}\right).

(ii)

Let
F
be a graph, and let
I\subseteq V(F)
be a non-empty independent set such that
N(I)
is also independent. Then
\rho(\omega\cdot F)\geq f\left(\frac{|N(I)|}{|I|}\right)
.

(iii)

Let
F
be a finite graph. Then
\rho(\omega\cdot F)\geq\frac{|V(F)|}{2|V(F)|-\alpha(F)}
.

For certain choices of
F
, these bounds are sufficient to find the exact value of the Ramsey density. Indeed, if one of the independent sets
I
that minimize the ratio
\frac{|N(I)|}{|I|}
also satisfies that
N(I)
is independent, then
(i)
and
(ii)
combined give that
\rho(\omega\cdot F)=\frac{|N(I)|}{|I|}
. This is the case, for example, for all bipartite graphs.

The bound
(iii)
is derived from a result of Burr, Erdős and Spencer
[
1
]
, and does not depend on the function
f
. There is currently no known graph
F
for which this bound is tight and, in fact, in this paper we will show that, unless the lower bound of (
1
) is tight for some
x
, the bound
(iii)
is never tight.

Two interesting cases in which the exact value does not follow from these bounds are cliques and odd cycles.

•

In the case of cliques, we have
\rho(\omega\cdot K_{n})\leq f(n-1)\leq\frac{n}{2n-2}
from
(i)
and
\rho(\omega\cdot K_{n})\geq\frac{n}{2n-1}
from
(iii)
. We cannot obtain any lower bound from
(ii)
because there is no non-empty independent set
I
such that
N(I)
is independent, if
n\geq 3.

•

In the case of odd cycles, we have
\rho(\omega\cdot C_{2n+1})\leq f\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)
from
(i)
,
f(\omega\cdot C_{2n+1})\geq f\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)
from
(ii)
(if
n\geq 2
), and
\rho(\omega\cdot C_{2n+1})\geq\frac{2n+1}{3n+2}
from
(iii)
. For
n
large enough, the lower bound from
(ii)
is better than that from
(iii)
, although the latter has the advantage of being easier to compute.

In this paper we will prove the following new lower bound for infinite factors:

Theorem 2
.

Let
F
be a finite graph. Then
\rho(\omega\cdot F)\geq f\left(\frac{|V(F)|}{\alpha(F)}-1\right)
.

This bound is not weaker than Theorem
1
(iii)
. That result can be expressed as
\rho(\omega\cdot F)\geq\bar{f}\left(\frac{|V(F)|}{\alpha(F)}-1\right)
, for
\bar{f}(x)=\frac{x+1}{2x+1}
. By (
1
), we have
f(x)\geq\bar{f}(x)
.

We can compare this lower bound to the upper bound in Theorem
1
(i)
to find a new family of graphs for which we can obtain an exact result:

Corollary 1
.

Let
F
be a finite graph. Suppose that, among the independent sets
I
that minimize
\frac{|N(I)|}{|I|}
, there is at least one with size
\alpha(F)
. Then
\rho(\omega\cdot F)=f\left(\frac{|V(F)|}{\alpha(F)}-1\right)
. In particular:

•

\rho(\omega\cdot K_{n})=f(n-1)
for all
n\geq 1
.

•

\rho(\omega\cdot C_{2n+1})=f\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)
for all
n\geq 1
.

Since a triangle is both a clique and an odd cycle, these results show that
\rho(\omega\cdot K_{3})=f(2)
, answering a question from
[
8
]
. Note however that, since we do not know the actual value of
f(2)
, the explicit bounds on
\rho(\omega\cdot K_{3})
have not improved: all we know is that
\frac{3}{5}\leq\rho(\omega\cdot K_{3})\leq(21+\sqrt{12})/33=0.74133\dots
(we suspect that the upper bound is sharp). The next result improves upon this lower bound:

Theorem 3
.

\rho(\omega\cdot K_{3})\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\approx 0.62204
(and therefore
f(2)\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}
).

Unlike Theorem
2
, whose proof is based on the techniques from
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
(which is where the function
f
comes from), Theorem
3
is based on a careful analysis of the technique of Burr, Erdős and Spencer
[
1
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2

The proof of Lemma
2
uses the same overarching outline as Theorem
1
(ii)
: given an edge coloring of
K_{\mathbb{N}}
we color the vertices of the graph while encoding some information, we find a smaller substructure in the resulting coloring and finally use the information in the vertices to extend this substructure into our desired graph.

The main difference comes from the choice of coloring. While the proof from
[
8
]
uses a coloring due to Elekes, Soukup, Soukup and Szentmiklóssy
[
5
]
, here instead we will use a coloring that arises from a straightforward application of Ramsey’s theorem. The reason for this change is that the coloring from
[
5
]
is good at finding “connections at infinity”, allowing one to add infinitely many vertices to a component with little restriction as to how they are joined. Because the components of
\omega\cdot F
all have bounded size, we do not need to worry about connecting more and more vertices into a component. Instead, we will be able to create each component of
\omega\cdot F
in one or two steps.

The intermediate step of finding a smaller substructure is essentially the same. We will use the following lemma:

Lemma 1
.

Let
r
and
s
be positive integers. For every
\epsilon>0
there exist
\tau>0
and
N
with the following property: for every graph
G
on vertex set

, with
n>N
, such that
\delta(G)>(1-\tau)n
, in every coloring
\Psi:V(G)\cup E(G)\rightarrow\{R,B\}
there exists a subgraph
F\subseteq G
, a color
C\in\{R,B\}
and a value
k\in[\delta n,n]
such that
|V(F)\cap[k]|\geq(f(s/r)-\epsilon)k
and every component of
F
is of one of the following types:

•

An isolated vertex of color
C
.

•

A copy of
K_{r,s}
in which the edges and the side of size
s
have color
C
, and the side of size
r
has the opposite color.

This is a finitary version of Lemma 2 in
[
8
]
. The same proof that was used in that paper works here.

Definition 3

Given a set

Lemma 2

For every
\epsilon>0
and

Let
\alpha=\lceil 2\epsilon^{-1}\rceil s

of disjoint monochromatic copies of

Let

or

The proof of Theorem

Definition 4
.

A
b
owtie is an edge-colored graph on five vertices, formed by a red triangle and a blue triangle sharing a vertex.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\Psi:K_{n}\rightarrow\{R,B\}
, let
F
be the largest monochromatic family of disjoint triangles, and let
F^{\prime}
be the largest family of disjoint bowties. Then
3|F|+2|F^{\prime}|\geq n-5
.

Proof.

We start by noting that, if a two-edge-colored
K_{6}
contains a vertex-disjoint red triangle and a blue triangle, then it also contains a bowtie. This is because out of the nine edges between both triangles, there are at least five with the same color, w.l.o.g. red. By the pigeonhole principle, there is a vertex
v
in the blue triangle incident to at least two red edges. Then the original blue triangle plus the two red-neighbors of
v
form a bowtie.

Now consider the largest family
F^{\prime}
of vertex-disjoint bowties. The set of vertices not in
F^{\prime}
cannot contain triangles in both colors, by the argument above. Suppose w.l.o.g. that all remaining triangles are red. Let
F^{\prime\prime}
be a maximal family of vertex-disjoint red triangles in
V(K_{n})\setminus V(F^{\prime})
. We know that
|V(F^{\prime})|+|V(F^{\prime\prime})|\geq n-5
, because the remaining vertices do not contain a monochromatic triangle in either color. Now note that there is a family of disjoint red triangles in
K_{n}
of size
|F^{\prime}|+|F^{\prime\prime}|
, obtained by taking the triangles in
F^{\prime\prime}
and the red triangles in each bowtie of
F^{\prime}
. Thus we get
|F|\geq|F^{\prime}|+|F^{\prime\prime}|
, and

3|F|+2|F^{\prime}|\geq 3(|F^{\prime}|+|F^{\prime\prime}|)+2|F^{\prime}|=5|F^{\prime}|+3|F^{\prime\prime}|=|V(F^{\prime})|+|V(F^{\prime\prime})|\geq n-5,

as we wanted to prove.∎

Lemma 6

Proof.

Let

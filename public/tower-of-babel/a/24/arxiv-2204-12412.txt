Polynomiality of the faithful dimension for nilpotent groups over finite truncated valuation rings
Mohammad Bardestani, Keivan Mallahi-Karai, Dzmitry Rumiantsau, Hadi Salmasian

Abstract
. Given a finite group G \mathrm{G} , the faithful dimension of G \mathrm{G} over ℂ \mathbb{C} , denoted by m faithful ​ ( G ) m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathrm{G}) , is the smallest integer n n such that G \mathrm{G} can be embedded in GL n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}) . Continuing the work initiated in [ BMKS19 ] , we address the problem of determining the faithful dimension of a finite p p -group of the form 𝒢 R := exp ⁡ ( 𝔤 R ) \mathscr{G}_{R}:=\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{R}) associated to 𝔤 R := 𝔤 ⊗ ℤ R \mathfrak{g}_{R}:=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R in the Lazard correspondence, where 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is a nilpotent ℤ \mathbb{Z} -Lie algebra and R R ranges over finite truncated valuation rings. Our first main result is that if R R is a finite field with p f p^{f} elements and p p is sufficiently large, then m faithful ​ ( 𝒢 R ) = f ​ g ​ ( p f ) m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})=fg(p^{f}) where g ⁡ ( T ) g(T) belongs to a finite list of polynomials g 1 , … , g k g_{1},\ldots,g_{k} , with non-negative integer coefficients. The latter list of polynomials is uniquely determined by the Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . Furthermore, for each 1 ≤ i ≤ k 1\leq i\leq k the set of pairs ( p , f ) (p,f) for which g = g i g=g_{i} is a finite union of Cartesian products 𝒫 × ℱ \mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{F} , where 𝒫 \mathscr{P} is a Frobenius set of prime numbers and ℱ \mathscr{F} is a subset of ℕ \mathbb{N} that belongs to the Boolean algebra generated by arithmetic progressions. Previously, existence of such a polynomial-type formula for m faithful ​ ( 𝒢 R ) m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{R}) was only established under the assumption that either f = 1 f=1 or p p is fixed. Next we formulate a conjectural polynomiality property for the value of m faithful ​ ( 𝒢 R ) m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{R}) in the more general setting where R R is a finite truncated valuation ring, and prove special cases of this conjecture. In particular, we show that for a vast class of Lie algebras 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} that are defined by partial orders, m faithful ​ ( 𝒢 R ) m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{R}) is given by a single polynomial-type formula. Finally, we compute m faithful ​ ( 𝒢 R ) m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{R}) precisely in the case where 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is the free metabelian nilpotent Lie algebra of class c c on n n generators and R R is a finite truncated valuation ring.



1.
Introduction

The
faithful dimension
of a finite group
\mathrm{G}
over
\mathbb{C}
is
the smallest possible dimension of a faithful complex representation of
\mathrm{G}
.
Throughout this paper we denote the faithful dimension of
\mathrm{G}
by

m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathrm{G})
.
It is known
that
\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{G})\leq m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathrm{G})
for a finite group
\mathrm{G}
(see
[
BF03
, Proposition 4.15]
), where
\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{G})

denotes the essential dimension of
\mathrm{G}
over a field
\mathbb{K}
as defined by Buhler and
Reichstein
[
BR97
]
. It follows from a theorem of Karpenko and Merkurjev
[
KM08
]

that equality holds for all finite
p
-groups (see also
[
Mer17
]
).

The current work is a continuation of
[
BMKS19
]
in which the question of determining the faithful dimension was systematically studied for finite
p
-groups associated to
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebras via the

Lazard correspondence
. Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a
finite
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra which is nilpotent and has cardinality
p^{k}
for a prime number
p
that is strictly larger than the nilpotency class of
\mathfrak{f}
. Then to
\mathfrak{f}
we can associate a finite
p
-group with underlying set
\mathfrak{f}
and with
group multiplication defined by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, that is

\displaystyle x*y:=\sum_{n>0}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{n},b_{n})\\
a_{j}+b_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}+b_{i})^{-1}}{a_{1}!b_{1}!\cdots a_{n}!b_{n}!}(\mathrm{ad}_{x})^{a_{1}}(\mathrm{ad}_{y})^{b_{1}}\dots(\mathrm{ad}_{x})^{a_{n}}(\mathrm{ad}_{y})^{b_{n}-1}(y),

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{f}
. By a result of Lazard
[
Khu98
, Chapter 9]
every finite
p
-group of nilpotency class strictly smaller than
p
is obtained in this way from a unique Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}
. It is natural to denote this
p
-group by
\exp(\mathfrak{f})
.

A general class of examples of the above construction can be given as follows. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra that is finitely generated as an abelian group, and let
R
be a finite commutative unital ring of cardinality
p^{k}
where
p
is a prime number strictly larger than the nilpotency class of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the above construction yields a finite
p
-group
\mathscr{G}_{R}:=\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{R})
where
\mathfrak{g}_{R}:=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R
.
For example, for
p\geq n\geq 2
the group of
n\times n
unitriangular matrices over
R
is obtained in this fashion from the Lie algebra of
n\times n
strictly upper triangular matrices over
R
(which has nilpotency class
n-1
).

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote a finite field of order
q:=p^{f}
. A central result of our previous work
[
BMKS19
]
is about the dependence of
m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})
on
p
and
f
. More precisely, we proved the following two assertions (see Theorems 2.5 and 2.7 of
[
BMKS19
]
):

(A)

There exist finitely many polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
with non-negative integer coefficients such that for every prime
p
we have
m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})=g(p)
where
g=g_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq k
. The set of
p
for which
g=g_{i}
is definable in terms of existence of solutions of polynomial systems modulo
p
.

(B)

Given any sufficiently large prime
p
, there exist finitely many polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
, depending only on
\mathfrak{g}
and on
p
, such that for every
q:=p^{f}
with
f\in\mathbb{N}
we have
m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})=fg(q)
where
g=g_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq k
. The set of
f\in\mathbb{N}
for which
g=g_{i}
is a finite union of arithmetic progressions.

In statement A only
p
varies, while in statement B only
f
varies and it is not clear how the
g_{i}
depend on
p
. Statements A and B suggest that

m_{\mathrm{faithful}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})
should possess
a stronger polynomiality-type property, where both
p
and
f
are allowed to vary simultaneously. We did not succeed in proving a unifying assertion in
[
BMKS19
]
(see Remark 2.8 of the latter reference).

The first major result of the present paper
(Theorem
1.1
) proves such a unifying assertion.
For a polynomial
g(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
, we denote the set of primes
p
for which the congruence

g(T)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
has a solution
by
V_{g}
. We call a set of prime numbers a
Frobenius set
if it is in the Boolean algebra generated by the sets
V_{g}
. The Frobenius sets are closely related to Serre’s Frobenian sets (see
[
BMKS19
]
or
[
Lag83
]
for a precise statement). Henceforth, by an arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{N}
we mean a subset of
\mathbb{N}
of the form
\left\{a+kb\,:\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}\right\}
where
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra which is finitely generated as an abelian group.
Then there exist

(a)

a constant
M=M(\mathfrak{g})
only depending on
\mathfrak{g}
,

(b)

a partition
\left\{\mathscr{P}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{P}_{r}\right\}
of the set of primes larger than
M
into Frobenius sets,

(c)

a partition
\{\mathscr{F}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{F}_{s}\}
of natural numbers into arithmetic progressions, and

(d)

polynomials
g_{ij}(T)
, for
1\leq i\leq r
and
1\leq j\leq s
, with non-negative integer coefficients,

such that
for all
q:=p^{f}
where
(p,f)\in\mathscr{P}_{i}\times\mathscr{F}_{j}

for some

1\leq i\leq r
and
1\leq j\leq s
, we have

m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})=fg_{ij}(q).

We remark that the groups
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
have exponent
p
, so that by a result of Brauer
\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/p})
is a splitting field of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. It follows from
[
KM08
]
that
\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})=m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})
for every field
\mathbb{K}
that contains
\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/p})
.

A natural extension of the class of
p
-groups
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
is the groups
\mathscr{G}_{R}
with
R\cong\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is the valuation ring of a local field and
\mathfrak{p}
is the prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}
.
Up to isomorphism, every finite local ring with a nilpotent and principal maximal ideal is of the form
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
(see
[
Del84
,
McL73
]
). Such rings are called
finite truncated valuation
rings
.

Let
R
be a finite truncated valuation ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. We use
d=d_{R}
to denote the smallest positive integer satisfying
\mathfrak{m}^{d}=0
. If
p=p_{R}:=\mathrm{char}(R/\mathfrak{m})
, then
pR=\mathfrak{m}^{e}
for some
e=e_{R}\geq 1
and
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
for some
f=f_{R}\geq 1
.
Henceforth we refer to the quadruple
(d,p,e,f)
as the
associated parameters
of
R
. It turns out that we can formulate the problem (and our results) only in terms of
(d,p,e,f)
, even though these parameters do not identify
R
uniquely up to isomorphism.
We conjecture the following extension of Theorem
1.1

for
m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Then the
constant
M(\mathfrak{g})
, the partitions
\{\mathscr{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{r}\}
and

\{\mathscr{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{F}_{s}\}
, and the polynomials
g_{ij}(T)
can be chosen such that
for every finite truncated valuation ring
R
with associated parameters
(d,p,e,f)
we have

m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})=f\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}g_{ij}(p^{f(d-\ell)})\quad\text{ whenever }(p,f)\in\mathscr{P}_{i}\times\mathscr{F}_{j}.

We are unable to prove Conjecture
1.2
in full generality, but
we
establish two results
that support this conjecture. The first result, Theorem
1.3
, shows that
m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})
is not greater than the conjectural value, but grows with the same rate as
d
tends to infinity.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
,
\{\mathscr{P}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
,

\{\mathscr{F}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
, and the
g_{ij}(T)

be as in
Theorem
1.1
.
Fix

1\leq i_{\circ}\leq r
and
1\leq j_{\circ}\leq s
.
Then there exists a constant
M>0
such that for
every finite truncated valuation ring
R
with associated parameters
(d,p,e,f)

satisfying
(p,f)\in\mathscr{P}_{i_{\circ}}\times\mathscr{F}_{j_{\circ}}
and
p>M
, we have

fg_{i_{\circ}j_{\circ}}(p^{fd})\leq m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})\leq f\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}g_{i_{\circ}j_{\circ}}(p^{f(d-\ell)}).

Remark 1.4
.

Note that the above theorem establishes Conjecture
1.2
in the case
e=1
. In this case, the rings
R
are of the form
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
where
\mathcal{O}
is the ring of integers of a finite unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

The second result, Theorem
1.5
, establishes the conjecture for groups associated to
pattern Lie algebras
.
Before stating Theorem
1.5

we recall some definitions.
Let
\prec
be a partial order on the set
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. To this partial order, we assign the
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie
subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\prec}
of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{Z})
that is spanned by the
e_{ij}
satisfying
i\prec j
, where the
e_{ij}
denote the standard basis vectors of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{Z})
.
Many nilpotent Lie algebras of interest (e.g., nilradicals of parabolic subalgebras of semisimple Lie algebras) are pattern Lie algebras.

Without loss of generality we assume that
\prec
is compatible with the usual ordering of
[n]
, so that elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{\prec}
are strictly upper triangular matrices. For any
i\prec j
define

(1.1)

\alpha(i,j):=\#\{k\in[n]:i\prec k\prec j\}.

A pair
(i,j)
with
i\prec j
is called
extreme
if
i
is minimal and
j
is maximal with respect to
\prec
. The set of extreme pairs will be denoted by

I_{\mathrm{ex}}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{g}_{\prec}
with
\mathfrak{g}_{\prec}
as above, and let
R
be a finite truncated valuation ring with associated parameters
(d,p,e,f)
such that

p>\max\{\alpha(i,j):i\prec j\}+1.

Then

(1.2)

m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{\mathrm{ex}}}fp^{f(d-\ell)\alpha(i,j)}.

Special cases of Theorem
1.5
were proved in
[
BMKS19
]
(for
R=\mathbb{F}_{q}
) and
[
BMKS16
]
(for Heisenberg and unitriangular groups over general
R
).
The Heisenberg Lie algebra (which is the nilradical of the parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
with Levi factor
\mathfrak{gl}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{n-2}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
) corresponds to the partial order

1\prec 2,3,\ldots,n-1\prec n,

and the unitriangular Lie algebra corresponds to the total order
1\prec\cdots\prec n
.

We conclude this paper by computing
m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})
when
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
is the free metabelian nilpotent Lie algebra of class
c
on
n
generators.
Recall that

\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}:=\mathfrak{f}_{n,c}/[[\mathfrak{f}_{n,c},\mathfrak{f}_{n,c}],[\mathfrak{f}_{n,c},\mathfrak{f}_{n,c}]],

where
\mathfrak{f}_{n,c}
denotes the free nilpotent
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra of class
c
on
n
generators.
Indeed
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
is the largest metabelian quotient of

\mathfrak{f}_{n,c}
. The following theorem is a generalization of

[
BMKS16
, Theorem 2.15]
, which corresponds to the special case
\mathfrak{m}_{2,c}
. Special cases of
\mathfrak{m}_{3,5},\mathfrak{m}_{3,6}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{n,4}
for all
n\geq 2
over finite fields were computed by Tielker
[
Tie18
]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Set
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
where
n,c\geq 2
. Then for
p
sufficiently large we have

m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})=(c-1){n+c-2\choose c}\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}fp^{f(d-\ell)}.

Let us now explain the key ideas that are used in the present paper.
Similar to our previous work
[
BMKS19
]
, we heavily use the Kirillov orbit method for finite
p
-groups, as well as the notion of the commutator matrix associated to a nilpotent Lie algebra, defined originally by Grunewald and Segal
[
GS84
]
. Another important tool is a theorem from
[
BMKS19
]
which relates the faithful dimension to the question of existence of rational points in general position on certain rank varieties. However, the proof of Theorem
1.1
requires two new ideas: first, a theorem of Ax from his seminal work
[
Ax68
]
(see also
[
vdD91
]
) which roughly states that any elementary statement in the language of finite fields is equivalent to one about single-variable polynomials; second, a theorem of Dedekind about factorization of the reduction modulo
p
of a monic polynomial with integer coefficients.
In addition, in the proof of Theorem
1.3
we need
a (not so well known) variant of the multivariate Hensel’s lemma
that does not require any smoothness or genericity assumption (see Proposition
3.4
).
This variant was first established by Ax and Kochen
[
AK65
]
using ultrafilter methods (for further references, see Section
3
).

2.
Polynomiality along all finite fields

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.1
.
We begin with recalling a result of Ax from model theory of finite fields.
Let
{\mathcal{Q}}:=\{p^{f}\,:\,p\text{ is prime and }f\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the set of all prime powers.
For
n\geq 1
, denote by
{\mathcal{B}_{n}}
the Boolean algebra on
{\mathcal{Q}}
generated by the
subsets

Z_{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}}:=\{q\in\mathcal{Q}:\text{ the equations }h_{i}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})=0,1\leq i\leq k,\text{ have a solution in }\mathbb{F}_{q}\},

where
h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}
range over all polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[T_{1},\dots,T_{n}]
.
Also, let
\mathcal{B}
be the Boolean algebra generated by
finite subsets of
\mathcal{Q}

and
the sets
Z_{h}
where
h\in\mathbb{Z}[T_{1}]
.
In
[
Ax68
, Theorem 11]
, Ax proved that for all
n\geq 1
, we have
{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
. This statement generalizes
[
Ax67
, Theorem 1]
, which proves a similar assertion for primes.
It also follows from a result of van den Dries
in
[
vdD91
, Subsec. (2.3)]
.

In order to prove
Theorem
1.1
we need
a refinement of the aforementioned result of Ax (see Proposition
2.4
). This refinement may be well known to the experts in the field (see e.g.
[
Ax68
, Theorem 11’]
for a somewhat similar statement), but we were unable to locate the required formulation in the literature. For this reason and for convenience of the reader we will supply a proof. We would like to thank J. Derakhshan for communicating the main steps of this proof to us.

Let us recall some basic facts from algebraic number theory.
Let
h(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
be a monic polynomial.
The discriminant of
h(T)
is defined to be

\mathrm{Disc}_{h}:=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{2},

where
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
are the roots of
h(T)
. Note that since
h(T)
is monic,
\mathrm{Disc}_{h}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We denote the splitting field of
h(T)
by
E
, that is,
E:=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
, and the ring of algebraic integers of
E
by
\mathcal{O}_{E}
.

Let
p
be a prime number that does not divide
\mathrm{Disc}_{h}
. Then
p
is unramified in every simple extension
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{i})
(see
[
Mar18
, Theorem 3.24]
), hence by
[
Mar18
, Theorem 4.31]
it is also unramified in the compositum of the
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{i})
, that is, in
E
.
Therefore to every prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
that lies over
p\mathbb{Z}
(i.e.,
\mathfrak{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}=p\mathbb{Z}
) one can associate a Frobenius automorphism

[
Mar18
, Theorem 4.32]
,
which is the unique element
\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})

that satisfies

\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)\equiv\alpha^{p}\mod\mathfrak{q}\quad\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{E}.

The
\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}
form a conjugacy class of
\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
,
denoted by the Artin symbol
\left(\frac{E/\mathbb{Q}}{p}\right)
.

Denote the reduction of
h(T)
modulo
p
by
\bar{h}(T)
. Since
p\nmid\mathrm{Disc}_{h}
,
the roots of
\bar{h}(T)
are also simple.
For the proof of the next theorem,
see
[
Jac85
, Theorems 4.37 and 4.38]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Dedekind)
.

Let
h(T)
and
p
be as above. Suppose

(2.1)

\bar{h}(T)=h_{1}(T)\cdots h_{r}(T)

is the factorization of
\bar{h}(T)
into irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Set
n_{i}:=\deg h_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
that lies over
p\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the cycle decomposition of
\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}
, when viewed as a permutation of the roots of
h(T)
, is of the form
\varsigma_{1}\cdots\varsigma_{r}
with each
\varsigma_{i}
a cycle of length
n_{i}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Theorem
2.1
is not stated in
[
Jac85
]
exactly in the given form.
Jacobson proves (by an argument originally due to Tate)
that for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{h}:=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}]
that lies over
p\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a unique
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
such that for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{h}
one
has
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)\equiv\alpha^{p}\mod\mathfrak{p}
,
and furthermore the restriction of
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
to the roots of
h(T)
has the desired cycle decomposition. Now if
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{E}
is a prime ideal that lies over
p\mathbb{Z}
, then

\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}
also satisfies

\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)\equiv\alpha^{p}\mod\mathfrak{p}
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{h}
, where
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{q}\cap\mathcal{O}_{h}
. Thus by the uniqueness of
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
we obtain
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}=\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
h(T)
,
p
,
\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}
, and
n_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
be as in Theorem
2.1
.
Denote by
S_{p}(h)
the set of integers
f\geq 1
such that
\bar{h}(T)
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
.
Then

S_{p}(h)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\mathbb{N}=\{k\geq 1:\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k})\neq\varnothing\},

where
\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k})
is the set of fixed points of

\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k}
, viewed as
a permutation of the roots of
h(T)
.

Proof.

From the cycle decomposition of
\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}
in Theorem
2.1
it follows immediately that

\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k}
has a fixed point if and only if
n_{i}|k
for some
1\leq i\leq r
. This proves the second equality.
Thus it remains to verify that
S_{p}(h)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\mathbb{N}
.

First observe that for each
1\leq i\leq r
,
the quotient
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(h_{i}(x))
is a field of order
p^{n_{i}}
in which
\bar{h}(x)
has a root.
Recall that
k|l
if and only if

Remark 3.1
.

Any finitely generated abelian group
A
can be expressed as a direct sum
A\cong A_{F}\oplus A_{T}
where
A_{F}
is free abelian and
A_{T}
is torsion.
Tensoring
A
(over
\mathbb{Z}
) with a commutative ring
R
whose characteristic is a prime number larger than the exponent of
A_{T}
annihilates
A_{T}
. Furthermore, if
A
and
B
are finitely generated abelian groups and
A\to B
is an injective group homomorphism, then the induced map
A_{F}\to B/B_{T}\cong B_{F}
is also an injection. In particular a basis of
A_{F}
is mapped to a linearly independent set of vectors. After tensoring with
R
, if the charatecteristic of
R
is a sufficiently large prime, the image of the basis still remains linearly independent (because the determinant of a square minor of maximum size will remain nonzero). It follows that a short exact sequence of finitely generated abelian groups will remain exact upon tensoring with a finite truncated valuation ring
R
as long as
p
is sufficiently large (depending on the exact sequence).

-group, then the minimum number of generators of
A
is equal to
\dim_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, the minimum number of generators of the abelian group
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
is equal to
fe
.
This is because
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\cong(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d})/(p(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}))\cong\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{e}
and

\#\mathfrak{p}^{i}/\mathfrak{p}^{i+1}=p^{f}
for
0\leq i\leq e-1
.

Next we establish a general combinatorial lemma about bases of direct sums.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be two vector spaces of dimensions
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
over an arbitrary field. Let
S=\{(v_{i},v^{\prime}_{i}):1\leq i\leq d_{1}+d_{2}\}
be a basis for
V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
. Then
S
can be partitioned into two sets
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, of sizes
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
respectively, such that the
V_{i}
-components of the vectors in
B_{i}
form a basis of
V_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

The following argument is communicated to us by I. Bogdanov. Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d_{1}}\}
and
\{e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{d_{2}}\}
denote bases of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, respectively. Then for
1\leq j\leq d_{1}+d_{2}
we obtain

v_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}a_{ij}e_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad v^{\prime}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{2}}b_{ij}e^{\prime}_{j}.

Consider the square matrix
A
of size
d_{1}+d_{2}
whose
i
-th column, for
1\leq i\leq d_{1}+d_{2}
, is
the transpose of the row vector

(a_{1i},\cdots,a_{d_{1}i},b_{1i},\ldots,b_{d_{2}i}).

Since
S
is a basis,
A
is invertible.
Set
I:=\{1,\dots,d_{1}\}
and consider the generalized Laplace expansion

\det(A)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq\{1,\dots,d_{1}+d_{2}\}\\
|J|=d_{1}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\sum_{i\in I}i+\sum_{j\in J}j}\det A_{I,J}\det A_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}},

where
I^{\prime}:=\{d_{1}+1,\ldots,d_{1}+d_{2}\}
,
J^{\prime}
is the complement of
J
in
\{1,\ldots,d_{1}+d_{2}\}
, and
A_{I,J}
(respectively,
A_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}
) is the
minor of
A
corresponding to rows and columns indexed
by
I
and
J
(respectively, by
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
). Since
\det(A)\neq 0
, there exists
J
with
\det A_{I,J}\det A_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}\neq 0
.
The desired sets are
B_{1}:=\{(v_{j},v^{\prime}_{j}):j\in J\}
and
B_{2}:=\{(v_{j},v^{\prime}_{j}):j\in J^{\prime}\}
.
∎

Lemma
4.4
and induction yield the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over an arbitrary field and
let

S
be a basis for
V^{\oplus k}
where
k\geq 1
. Then
S
can be partitioned into subsets
B_{1},\dots,B_{k}
such that
each of the sets
\{\pi_{\ell}(w):w\in B_{\ell}\}

is a basis of
V

for
1\leq\ell\leq k
, where

\pi_{\ell}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k}):=v_{\ell}
.

The following simple lemma was also used in
[
BMKS16
]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1}
be non-negative real numbers with
\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}a_{\ell}=m
. Assume that for any
0\leq\ell\leq m-1
we have
a_{\ell}+\cdots+a_{m-1}\leq m-\ell
. Then for any decreasing sequence
x_{0}\geq\cdots\geq x_{m-1}
, we have

\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}a_{\ell}x_{\ell}\geq\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}x_{\ell}
.

Proof.

Define
A_{\ell}:=\sum_{j=\ell}^{m-1}a_{j}

for
0\leq\ell\leq m-1

and set
A_{m}:=0
. Then
A_{\ell}\leq m-\ell
and
A_{0}=m
. Set
\Delta A_{\ell}:=A_{\ell}-A_{\ell+1}
and
\Delta x_{\ell}:=x_{\ell}-x_{\ell-1}
. Since
\Delta x_{\ell}\leq 0
, we have

\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}a_{\ell}x_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\Delta A_{\ell}x_{\ell}=A_{0}x_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}A_{\ell}\Delta x_{\ell}\geq mx_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}(m-\ell)\Delta x_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}x_{\ell}.\qed

Let

Theorem 4.7
.

Assume that
p
is an odd prime that is strictly larger than the nilpotency class of
\mathfrak{f}
.
Then there exists a bijection between the orbits of the coadjoint action on

and characters of irreducible representations of

.
Furthermore, if

denotes the irreducible representation corresponding to the coadjoint orbit

(a)

The character of

is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{\Theta}(x):=|\Theta|^{-1/2}\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta(x)
for

(b)

\displaystyle\dim(\rho_{\Theta})=\left(\frac{\#\mathfrak{f}}{\#\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\theta_{0})}\right)^{1/2}

for
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
, where

\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\theta_{0}):=\left\{x\in\mathfrak{f}\,:\,\theta_{0}([x,\mathfrak{f}])=1\right\}.

(c)

\rho_{\Theta}(g)
is multiplication by the scalar
\theta(g)
for

and
\theta\in\Theta
.

Recall that in this section
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{g}_{\prec}

Remark 4.8

The nilpotency class of

where

By the isomorphism (

), elements of

are of the form
\psi_{\bf b}
for a vector

with entries in

(4.2)

\psi_{{\bf b}}\left(\sum_{i\prec j}x_{ij}e_{ij}\right):=\psi\left(\sum_{i\prec j}b_{ij}x_{ij}\right).

Definition 4.9
.

We define the level of

to be the smallest

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
b_{ij}=\tilde{b}_{ij}+\mathfrak{p}^{d}
for
i\prec j
be elements of
R
. Set
{\bf b}:=(b_{ij})_{i\prec j}
, and let
\rho_{{}_{\bf b}}
be the irreducible representation of
\mathscr{G}_{R}
corresponding to the orbit of
\psi_{\bf b}
under the coadjoint action. Then

\dim\rho_{{}_{\bf b}}\geq p^{f(d-\mathbf{lev}(b_{i_{1}j_{1}}))\alpha(i_{1},j_{1})}

for all
(i_{1},j_{1})\in I_{\mathrm{ex}}
.

Proof.

We use Theorem
4.7
(b).
Let
x=\sum_{i\prec j}x_{ij}e_{ij}\in\mathfrak{g}_{R}
be an element of
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{g}_{R}}(\psi_{{\bf b}})
. Then for any
y=\sum_{i\prec j}y_{ij}e_{ij}\in\mathfrak{g}_{R}
we have

\begin{split}1=\psi_{{\bf b}}([x,y])&=\psi_{{\bf b}}\left(\sum_{i\prec j,\,k\prec l}x_{ij}y_{kl}[e_{ij},e_{kl}]\right)\\
&=\psi\left(\sum_{i\prec j}\sum_{i\prec k\prec j}b_{ij}(x_{ik}y_{kj}-x_{kj}y_{ik})\right)=\psi\left(\sum_{i\prec j}\left(\sum_{k\prec i}b_{kj}x_{ki}-\sum_{j\prec l}b_{il}x_{jl}\right)y_{ij}\right).\end{split}

Since the
y_{ij}\in R
are arbitrary and
\psi
is primitive, the stabilizer of

\psi_{{}_{\bf b}}
is equal to the solution set of the system of linear equations

\mathscr{L}:=\left\{L_{ij}(x_{st})=0\ :\ i,j\in[n]\text{ and }i\prec j\right\}
, where

(4.3)

L_{ij}(x_{st}):=\sum_{k\prec i}b_{kj}x_{ki}-\sum_{j\prec l}b_{il}x_{jl}.

In particular since
(i_{1},j_{1})\in I_{\mathrm{ex}}
, the linear forms
L_{i_{1}i}(x_{st})
and
L_{jj_{1}}(x_{st})
, for
i_{1}\prec i\prec j_{1}
and
i_{1}\prec j\prec j_{1}
, yield
2\alpha(i_{1},j_{1})
linear equations that can be written as

(4.4)

b_{i_{1}j_{1}}x_{ij_{1}}=-\sum_{i\prec k\neq j_{1}}b_{i_{1}k}x_{ik}\quad\text{ and }\quad b_{i_{1}j_{1}}x_{i_{1}j}=-\sum_{i_{1}\neq l\prec j}b_{lj_{1}}x_{lj}.

The cardinality of the kernel of the map
R\to R,\,x\mapsto b_{i_{1}j_{1}}x

p^{f\mathbf{lev}(b_{i_{1}j_{1}})}
.
To see this, note that the ideal generated by
b_{i_{1}j_{1}}
is
\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
, with
\ell=\mathbf{lev}(b_{i_{1}j_{1}})

above fact now follows from
\Ann(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}/\mathfrak{p}^{d})=\mathfrak{p}^{(d-\ell)}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
in combination with
\#(\mathfrak{p}^{i}/\mathfrak{p}^{i+1})=p^{f}
for
0\leq i\leq d-1
.

It follows that for any choice of values for the variables on the right hand side of (
4.4
), there exists at most
p^{f\mathbf{lev}(b_{i_{1}j_{1}})}
choices for each of the

variables

and
x_{jj_{1}}
such that the corresponding equation in (

) is satisfied.
Therefore the number of solutions of the linear system
\mathscr{L}
is
at most

(4.5)

p^{fd(|I|-2\alpha(i_{1},j_{1}))+2f\alpha(i_{1},j_{1})\mathbf{lev}(b_{i_{1}j_{1}})},

where
I:=\{(i,j):i\prec j\}
.
Using Theorem
4.7
(b) it is now straightforward to verify that

\dim\rho_{{}_{\bf b}}\geq p^{f(d-\mathbf{lev}(b_{i_{1}j_{1}}))\alpha(i_{1},j_{1})}
.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
b=\tilde{b}+\mathfrak{p}^{d}\in R

We write the linear system in the proof of Proposition

5.
The faithful dimension in the case
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}

In this section we prove
Theorem
1.6
. We begin by introducing some notation.
Recall that
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
k\geq 1
, the set of sequences

\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k}),\quad i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in[n]

is denoted by
\mathscr{A}(n,k)
.
We say that
\mathbf{i}
is
decreasing
if
i_{1}\geq\dots\geq i_{k}
. The set of decreasing sequences
in
\mathscr{A}(n,k)
is denoted by
\mathscr{D}(n,k)
.
There is an obvious sorting map

\bar{\,}:\mathscr{A}(n,k)\to\mathscr{D}(n,k).

For example
\overline{(5,4,4,2,3,1)}=(5,4,4,3,2,1)
.
We say that
\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{A}(n,k)
is a

Hall sequence
if the initial sequence
(i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1})
is decreasing and
i_{k-1}<i_{k}
(by convention, every element of
\mathscr{A}(n,1)
is a Hall sequence). The
subset of Hall sequences in
\mathscr{A}(n,k)
is denoted by
\mathscr{H}(n,k)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

(a)

For
n,k\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\#\mathscr{D}(n,k)={n+k-1\choose k}.

(b)

For
n\geq 2
and
k\geq 2
we have

\#\mathscr{H}(n,k)=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}m{k+m-2\choose k-2}=(k-1){k+n-2\choose k}.

Proof.

For part (a), note that cardinality of
\mathscr{D}(n,k)
is equal to the number of solutions of the equation
t_{1}+\cdots+t_{n}=k
in non-negative integers, where
t_{j}=\#\{\ell\in[k]:i_{\ell}=j\}
.

For part (b), fix
n,k\geq 2
and for
m\in[n-1]
denote by
A_{m}
the subset of
\mathscr{H}(n,k)
consisting of those sequences

(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\in\mathscr{H}(n,k)
such that
i_{k-1}=m
. Note that since
i_{k-1}<i_{k}\leq n
, we must have
i_{k-1}\in[n-1]
. Once
m
is fixed, there are
n-m
options left for
i_{k}
. Moreover, the sequence
(i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1})
can be any decreasing sequence in
[n]
for which
i_{k-1}=m
. Using the map
(i_{1},\dots,i_{k-2})\mapsto(i_{1}-m+1,\dots,i_{k-2}-m+1)
these sequences stand in one-to-one correspondence with elements of the set
\mathscr{D}(n-m+1,k-2)
, whose cardinality by part (a) is
equal to
{n-m+k-2\choose k-2}
. Since the two choices can be made independently of each other
we have

\#\mathscr{H}(n,k)=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(n-m){n-m+k-2\choose k-2}.

Replacing
m
by
n-m
in this expression yields the first equality in part (b).
For the second equality note that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}m{k+m-2\choose k-2}=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(k-1){k+m-2\choose k-1}=(k-1)\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}{k+m-2\choose k-1},

and
\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}{k+m-2\choose k-1}
counts the number of subsets of size
k
of
[k+n-2]
, where the summand
{k+m-2\choose k-1}
corresponds to subsets
of
[k+n-2]

with maximum equal to
k+m-1
.
∎

Since

\mathfrak{f}_{n,2}=\mathfrak{m}_{n,2}
,
the case
c=2
of Theorem
1.6

follows from
[
BMKS16
, Theorem 2.13]
. Thus from now on we assume that
c\geq 3
.
We denote the standard generators of
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
by
X_{n}:=\{\mathsf{v}_{i}:i\in[n]\}
.
For each
k
-tuple
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\in\mathscr{A}(n,k)
, write

\mathsf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}:=[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{2}},\dots,[\mathsf{v}_{i_{k-1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{k}}]\dots].

Set

\mathscr{H}^{\leq c-1}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{c-1}\mathscr{H}(n,j)
and

\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}:=\bigcup_{j=2}^{c}\mathscr{H}(n,j)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
n
and

be as above. For each

, the set

is a basis for

. In particular,

(a)

forms a basis for

(b)

Elements in

represent a basis of
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}/\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{m}_{n,c})
.

(c)

Elements in
\left\{\mathsf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\,:\,\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}\right\}

.

Proof.

This is well known. See

∎

In the sequel, we will carry out a close study of the commutator matrix of
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
with respect to the bases for
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}/\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{m}_{n,c})
and

[\mathfrak{m}_{n,c},\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}]
described in Lemma
5.2
.
For
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathscr{H}^{\leq c-1}

let
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the structural constants defined by

[\mathsf{v}_{\mathbf{i}},\mathsf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}]=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}}\lambda^{\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}\mathsf{v}_{\mathbf{k}}.

Let
\mathbf{T}:=(T_{\mathbf{i}})_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}}

be a vector of variables, and for
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathscr{H}^{\leq c-1}
, define
the linear forms

\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{T}):=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}}\lambda_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{k}}T_{\mathbf{k}}.

The commutator matrix of
\mathfrak{g}

relative to the chosen bases is the
N\times N
skew-symmetric matrix of linear forms given by

F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}):=[\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{T})]_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathscr{H}^{\leq c-1}},\quad\text{where $N:=\#\mathscr{H}^{\leq c-1}$.}

For any

(i_{1},\ldots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
, set
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1})
and
\mathbf{j}:=(i_{2},\ldots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c-1)
. Then the
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})
entry of

F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})
is equal to
T_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{c})}
.
Further, for any
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})
and
\mathbf{j}:=(j_{1},\dots,j_{l})
, we have
\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}=0
unless
\min(k,l)=1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1})\in\mathscr{H}(n,1)
and
\mathbf{j}:=(i_{2},\dots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c-1)
. Then

{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{T})=\begin{cases}T_{(\overline{i_{1},\dots,i_{c-1}},i_{c})}&\text{ if }\ i_{1}\geq i_{c-1},\\
T_{(i_{2},\dots,i_{c-1},i_{1},i_{c})}-T_{(\overline{i_{2},\dots,i_{c-2},i_{c}},i_{1},i_{c-1})}&\text{ if }\ i_{1}<i_{c-1}.\end{cases}

Proof.

First observe that
for
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
and
a^{\prime\prime}\in[\mathfrak{m}_{n,c},\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}]
we have

(5.1)

[a,[a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}]]=[a^{\prime},[a,a^{\prime\prime}]],

because

[a,[a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}]]=-[a^{\prime},[a^{\prime\prime},a]]-[a^{\prime\prime},[a,a^{\prime}]]=[a^{\prime},[a,a^{\prime\prime}]],

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
is metabelian and
a^{\prime\prime}
is a commutator.

Suppose that
i_{1}\geq i_{c-1}
,
and let
2\leq r\leq c-1
be the smallest integer with
i_{1}\geq i_{r}
. By the assumption such an
r
exists. If
c=3
, then
we must have
r=2
, hence

i_{1}\geq i_{2}
. Thus

(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})\in\mathscr{H}(n,3)
, hence
[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},\mathsf{v}_{(i_{2},i_{3})}]=[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{2}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{3}}]]=\mathsf{v}_{(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})}
, from which it follows that
\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{T})=\Lambda_{(i_{1}),(i_{2},i_{3})}(\mathbf{T})=T_{(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})}
.

For
c>3
, set

\eta_{k}:=[\mathsf{v}_{i_{k}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{k+1}},\dots,[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{c}}]\dots]
. It follows from (
5.1
) that

[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{2}},\dots,[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{k}}]\dots]=[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{2}},\eta_{3}]]=[\mathsf{v}_{i_{2}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},\eta_{3}]].

By repeating this process, we can keep swapping
\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}}
with subsequent terms
\mathsf{v}_{i_{3}},\dots
until
we arrive at
\mathsf{v}_{i_{r}}
where the process is terminated. Note that

(i_{2},\dots,i_{r-1},i_{1},i_{r},\dots,i_{c-1})=\overline{(i_{1},\dots,i_{c})}.

This proves the lemma for the case
i_{1}\geq i_{c-1}
.

Let us now consider the case

i_{1}<i_{c-1}
. In this case, the process described above can be continued all the way until
\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}}
arrives in the innermost commutator, that is,

[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{2}},\dots,[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{k}}]\dots]=[\mathsf{v}_{i_{2}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{3}},\dots,[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-2}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{c}}]]\dots].

We can now use the Jacobi identity to write

[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{c}}]]=[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-1}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{c}}]]-[\mathsf{v}_{i_{c}},[\mathsf{v}_{i_{1}},\mathsf{v}_{i_{c-1}}]]
.
Similar to the previous case, by a repeated application
of (
5.1
) we can move
\mathsf{v}_{i_{c}}
inside
to the location so that the resulting sequence is decreasing. This proves the claim.
∎

From now on we set

\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})
to be equal to the submatrix of
{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})

that lies in the intersection of rows
\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,1)
and columns
\mathbf{j}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c-1)
.
We remark that
the only variables that appear in the entries of
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})
are the
T_{\mathbf{i}}
for
\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
K
be any field and fix scalars
\alpha_{i},\lambda_{j}\in K
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
0\leq j\leq n-1
.
For each
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
set

(5.2)

T_{\mathbf{i}}:=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{k=1}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}\right)\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{i_{c-1}}&\alpha_{i_{c}}\\
\lambda_{i_{c-1}-1}&\lambda_{i_{c}-1}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

Set
\mathbf{T}:=(T_{\mathbf{i}})_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}
.
Then the matrix
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})
has rank at most
1
.

Proof.

First we show that for every
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1})\in\mathscr{H}(n,1)
and
\mathbf{j}:=(i_{2},\dots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c-1)
,

(5.3)

\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}\right)\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{i_{c-1}}&\alpha_{i_{c}}\\
\lambda_{i_{c-1}-1}&\lambda_{i_{c}-1}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

We consider two different cases. If
i_{1}\geq i_{c-1}
, then it follows from Lemma
5.3
that

{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{T})=T_{(\overline{i_{1},\dots,i_{c-1}},i_{c})}.

Since the sequence
(i_{2},\dots,i_{c-1})
is decreasing, and
i_{1}\geq i_{c-1}
, it follows that
i_{c-1}

is also the least element of
(\overline{i_{1},\dots,i_{c-1}},i_{c})
and hence
the first
c-2
terms of
(\overline{i_{1},\dots,i_{c-1}},i_{c})
are precisely
i_{1},\dots,i_{c-2}
, perhaps in a different order.
Thus (
5.3
) follows from the fact that the last two terms of the sequence are
i_{c-1}
and
i_{c}
, in the same order.

If
i_{1}<i_{c-1}
, then it follows from Lemma
5.3
that

{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{T})=T_{(i_{2},\dots,i_{c-1},i_{1},i_{c})}-T_{(\overline{i_{2},\dots,i_{c-2},i_{c}},i_{1},i_{c-1})}.

Since both
(i_{2},\dots,i_{c-1},i_{1},i_{c})
and
(\overline{i_{2},\dots,i_{c-2},i_{c}},i_{1},i_{c-1})

are Hall sequences, we have

(5.4)

\begin{split}{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{T})&=\alpha_{i_{c-1}}\cdot\left(\prod_{k=2}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}\right)\cdot\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{i_{1}}&\alpha_{i_{c}}\\
\lambda_{i_{1}-1}&\lambda_{i_{c}-1}\\
\end{vmatrix}-\alpha_{i_{c}}\cdot\left(\prod_{k=2}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}\right)\cdot\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{i_{1}}&\alpha_{i_{c-1}}\\
\lambda_{i_{1}-1}&\lambda_{i_{c-1}-1}\\
\end{vmatrix}\\
&=\left(\prod_{k=2}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}\right)\cdot\left(\alpha_{i_{c-1}}\cdot\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{i_{1}}&\alpha_{i_{c}}\\
\lambda_{i_{1}-1}&\lambda_{i_{c}-1}\\
\end{vmatrix}-\alpha_{i_{c}}\cdot\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{i_{1}}&\alpha_{i_{c-1}}\\
\lambda_{i_{1}-1}&\lambda_{i_{c-1}-1}\\
\end{vmatrix}\right)\end{split}

After applying the elementary identity

r^{\prime}\begin{vmatrix}q&r\\
s&t\\
\end{vmatrix}-r\begin{vmatrix}q&r^{\prime}\\
s&t^{\prime}\\
\end{vmatrix}=q\begin{vmatrix}r^{\prime}&r\\
t^{\prime}&t\\
\end{vmatrix}

to the expression on the right hand side and merging the prefactor
q:=\alpha_{i_{1}}
with the product

\prod_{k=2}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}
we obtain (
5.3
).
From (
5.3
) it follows that for
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1})\in\mathscr{H}(n,1)
and
\mathbf{j}:=(i_{2},\ldots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c-1)
, we have
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})_{\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}}=\alpha_{i_{1}}\beta_{\mathbf{j}}
for some
\beta_{\mathbf{j}}\in K
. Thus
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})
is
expressible as the product of the column vector
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathscr{H}(n,1)}
and the row vector
(\beta_{\mathbf{j}})_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c-1)}
, so that
\mathrm{rk}(\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}))\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
r,m,\delta\geq 1
. Let
K
be any field.

(a)

Suppose
f\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
is a polynomial in
m
variables over
K
such that the
degree of
f
as a polynomial in
x_{i}
is at most
\delta_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Assume that

\#K\geq 1+\max(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{m})
, and
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=0
for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in K^{m}
. Then
f
is the zero polynomial.

(b)

Suppose
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
, and the degree of each one of
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
in the variable
x_{i}
is at most
\delta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
Let
f:K^{m}\to K^{r}
be the polynomial map defined by
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
. Assume that the
K
-subspace
of
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
spanned by
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
has dimension at least
\delta
. If
\#K\geq 1+\max(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{m})
, then there exist

u_{1},\dots,u_{\delta}\in K^{m}
for which
f(u_{1}),\dots,f(u_{\delta})
are linearly independent vectors
in
K^{r}
.

Proof.

Part (a) follows by induction on
m
. For
m=1
, the statement is clear. Assuming that (a) holds for
m-1
, write
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}):=\sum_{i=0}^{\delta_{m}}c_{i}x_{m}^{i}
where the
c_{i}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1}]
. Fix
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m-1})\in K^{m-1}
and consider the one-variable polynomial

g(x_{m}):=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m-1},x_{m}).

Since
g(x_{m})=0
for all
x_{m}\in K
, and

\#K>\deg g
, it follows that all the coefficients of
g
vanish. The claim follows by the induction hypothesis applied to the
c_{i}
.

For (b)
by choosing a maximal linearly independent subset of
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
, we can assume that
\delta=r
. Thus the goal is to find
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\in K^{m}
for which
f(u_{1}),\dots,f(u_{r})
are linearly independent vectors in
K^{r}
. If this is not the case, then the image
of
f
must lie in a proper subspace of
K^{r}
. This implies that there exists a non-zero vector

(c_{1},\dots,c_{r})\in K^{r}
such that
c_{1}f_{1}(u)+\cdots+c_{r}f_{r}(u)=0
for all
u\in K^{m}
. By part (a),
this implies that the polynomial
c_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}f_{r}
is the zero polynomial, which is a contradiction.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.6
in the case where
R
is a finite field. In this case, we need to show that for
p
sufficiently large we have

(5.5)

m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})=(c-1){n+c-2\choose c}fq\qquad\text{ for all }f\geq 1,

where
q:=p^{f}
.
Recall that
T_{i}
is chosen as in (
5.2
).
We set
\lambda_{0}:=0
and
\lambda_{1}:=1
. Then the entries of
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}
are polynomials in variables
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}

and
\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}
. Thus
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}
takes values
in the vector space of matrices of size
n\times\#\mathscr{H}(n,c-1)
with entries in the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}]
.
First we prove the following claim:

The set
\{T_{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}
is linearly independent in

\mathbb{F}_{q}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}]
.

To prove this claim suppose that

\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}c_{\mathbf{i}}\cdot\left(\prod_{k=1}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}\right)\cdot\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{i_{c-1}}&\alpha_{i_{c}}\\
\lambda_{i_{c-1}-1}&\lambda_{i_{c}-1}\\
\end{vmatrix}=0,

for some coefficients
c_{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We will show that all the coefficients must be zero.
For each such linear dependence, define
r
to be the largest value of
i_{c}
for which there exists
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)

such that
c_{\mathbf{i}}\neq 0
. Note that clearly
r\geq 2
. First suppose that
r=2
. This implies that

i_{c-1}=1
and hence the linear dependence equation simplifies to

\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{c-2})\in\mathscr{D}(n,c-2)}c_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{c-2},1,2)}\prod_{k=1}^{c-1}\alpha_{i_{k}}=0.

Consider a monomial
g:=\alpha_{i_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{c-1}}
that appears in the above linear combination. Then
\deg_{\alpha_{i}}(g)=\#\{j:1\leq j\leq c-1\text{ and }i_{j}=i\}
.
Since the sequence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{c-1})
is decreasing, the sequence
(\deg_{\alpha_{1}}(g),\dots,\deg_{\alpha_{n}}(g))

uniquely determines the values
i_{1},\ldots,i_{c-2}
, hence uniquely determines
g
as well. Thus the monomials in the above linear combination have distinct degree sequences and therefore
there is no possibility of cancellation between them.
Next suppose that
r\geq 3
. This implies that there are some monomials involving
\lambda_{r-1}
and
that there are no terms involving
\lambda_{j}
for
j>r-1
. It is also clear that the only terms involving

\lambda_{r-1}
correspond to those sequences
(i_{1},\dots,i_{c})
for which
i_{c}=r
and hence
these terms are of the form

\prod_{k=1}^{c-2}\alpha_{i_{k}}(\alpha_{i_{c-1}}\lambda_{r-1}-\alpha_{r}\lambda_{i_{c-1}-1}).

By considering only the terms involving
\lambda_{r-1}
we obtain

\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{c-1})\in\mathscr{D}(n-1,c-1),i_{c-1}<r}c_{\mathbf{i}}\prod_{k=1}^{c-1}\alpha_{i_{k}}=0.

As in the previous case the monomials corresponding to different
\mathbf{i}
are distinct, and hence this can
only happen if
c_{\mathbf{i}}=0
for all such terms. This contradicts the choice of
r
.
This completes the proof of linear independence of the
\{T_{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}
.

Next set

f:=\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
in Lemma
5.6
(b), where
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}:=(T_{\mathbf{i}})_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}
. Note that the components of
f
are the entries of

\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
which are
polynomials
in the
\alpha_{i}
and the
\lambda_{i}
.
Furthermore, for every
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{c})\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
if we set
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}:=(i_{1})
and
\mathbf{j}^{\prime}:=(i_{2},\ldots,i_{c})
then by Lemma
5.3

we have
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}=T_{\mathbf{i}}
.
From linear independence of the set
\{T_{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}
and
Lemma
5.6
(b)
it follows that for
q\geq c
one can find
\#\mathscr{H}(n,c)
linearly independent vectors
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}:=(T_{\mathbf{i}})_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}

with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}

for which the values
\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
are linearly independent. By Lemma

5.4
it follows that

\mathrm{rk}(\widetilde{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}))=1
for all such
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
.
We can extend each such vector
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
to a vector
\mathbf{T}:=(T_{\mathbf{i}})_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}}
by setting

T_{\mathbf{i}}=0
for
\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,k)
, where
2\leq k\leq c-1
. For the latter vectors
\mathbf{T}
we have

\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}))=2
. Since
F_{\mathfrak{g}}
is skew symmetric and each variable
T_{\mathbf{i}}

where
\mathbf{i}:=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
appears in the
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j})
-entry of
F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})
, where
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}:=(i_{1})
and
\mathbf{j}:=(i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})
, we have
\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T}))\geq 2
whenever
\mathbf{T}\neq 0
.
In view of Theorem
2.6
this proves the assertion
in the case that
R
is a finite field.

Let us now consider the case where
R=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
.
Using Theorem
1.3
and equality (
5.5
)
which was just proved, the upper bound follows immediately. Let us now turn to establishing the lower bound.
We start with the following lemma.
Recall the notion of level of an element of
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
from Definition
4.9
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
B
be an
r\times r
skew-symmetric matrix with entries in

\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}
. Suppose
B
has an entry of level
\ell
. Then

\#\ker B\leq p^{fd(r-2)+2f\ell}.

Proof.

Write
B=(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}
. If
\ell=d
, there is nothing to prove. Next assume that the entry in question is non-zero, and hence non-diagonal. After possibly permuting rows and columns, we can assume that
\mathbf{lev}(B_{12})=\ell
. Consider a vector
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d})^{\oplus r}
in
\ker B
. Then the following equations hold:

B_{12}x_{2}=-\sum_{j=3}^{r}B_{1j}x_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{21}x_{1}=-\sum_{j=3}^{r}B_{2j}x_{j}.

Once the values of the
x_{j}
for
3\leq j\leq r
are set, the number of choices for
each one of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
is
p^{f\ell}
. Since the number of choices for

(x_{3},\dots,x_{r})
is
p^{fd(r-2)}
, the claim follows immediately.
∎

In order to avoid confusion with parameters
n
and
c
of
\mathfrak{m}_{n,c}
, henceforth we will denote the parameters
l_{1},l_{2},n,m

that were associated to
\mathfrak{g}
in Section

2

by

\underline{l}_{1},\underline{l}_{2},\underline{n},\underline{m}
.
Thus in particular we have

\underline{m}=\#\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}\quad,\quad\underline{l}_{1}=\#\mathscr{H}(n,c)=(c-1){n+c-2\choose c}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{l}_{2}=0.

Moreover, the commutator matrix
F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{T})
is an
\underline{n}\times\underline{n}
matrix with
\underline{n}=\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathfrak{m}_{n,c})-\underline{l}_{1}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathbf{a}:=(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i}))_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}^{\geq 2}}\in(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d})^{\oplus\underline{m}}
. Then

\#\ker_{\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}}F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a})\leq\min_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}p^{fd(\underline{n}-2)+2f\mathbf{lev}(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i}))}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
we have
\underline{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{c-1}\#\mathscr{H}(n,j)
and the rows and columns of
F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a})
are indexed by elements of
\mathscr{H}^{\leq c-1}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{c-1}\mathscr{H}(n,j)
. Now fix any
\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{H}^{\leq c-1}
.
By Lemma
5.7
for
B:=F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a})
,
r:=\underline{n}
, and
\ell:=\mathbf{lev}(F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a})_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{j}^{\prime}})
we have

\#\ker_{\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}}F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a})\leq p^{fd(\underline{n}-2)+2f\mathbf{lev}(F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a})_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{j}^{\prime}})}.

In particular, if for any

\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
we set

\mathbf{i}^{\prime}:=(i_{1})
and

\mathbf{j}^{\prime}:=(i_{2},\dots,i_{c})

then by Lemma
5.3

we obtain

\#\ker_{\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}}F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a})\leq p^{fd(\underline{n}-2)+2f\mathbf{lev}(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i}))}
. Finally, by taking the minimum over all
\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
we obtain the assertion of the lemma.
∎

Let
S=\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{fe\underline{l}_{1}}\}\subseteq(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d})^{\oplus\underline{m}}
be a set of vectors that corresponds to
m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})
in
Proposition
3.2
(thus we assume that
p
is sufficiently large accordingly).
Let
\pi_{\mathbf{i}}
for
\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
denote the natural projection

\bigoplus_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{e})\to\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{e},

that maps
\mathbf{a}
to
\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i})
.
Using
Lemma
4.5
,
we partition
S
into sets
S_{\mathbf{i}}
, each of cardinality
ef
, such that
\pi_{\mathbf{i}}({\mathsf{proj}}(S_{\mathbf{i}}))
is a basis
for the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{e}
. By Lemma

5.8
we have

\displaystyle m_{{}_{\mathrm{faithful}}}(\mathscr{G}_{R})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{fe\underline{l}_{1}}\left(\frac{p^{fd\underline{n}}}{\#\ker_{\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}}(F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a}_{i}))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in S_{\mathbf{i}}}\left(\frac{p^{fd\underline{n}}}{\#\ker_{\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{d}}(F_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbf{a}))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in S_{\mathbf{i}}}p^{f(d-\mathbf{lev}(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i}))}).

Recall that for every
0\leq\ell\leq e-1
, the set of
x\in(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{e})
with
\mathbf{lev}(x)\geq\ell
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
- subspace of dimension
f(e-\ell)
.
Let us call this subspace
\mathcal{W}_{\ell}
.
Fix
\mathbf{i}\in\mathscr{H}(n,c)
, and set
a_{\ell}=\frac{1}{f}\#\{\mathbf{a}\in S_{\mathbf{i}}:\mathbf{lev}(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i}))=\ell\}
.
Since
\{\pi_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathsf{proj}(\mathbf{a})):\mathbf{a}\in S_{\mathbf{i}}\}
is a basis for
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{e}
, it follows that for every
0\leq\ell\leq e-1
, the
set of
\mathbf{a}\in S_{\mathbf{i}}
with

\mathbf{lev}(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i}))\geq\ell
is a linearly independent subset of
\mathcal{W}_{\ell}
. Thus, the cardinality of the latter set of vectors is at most
\dim\mathcal{W}_{\ell}=f(e-\ell)
. Hence

a_{\ell}+\cdots+a_{e-1}\leq e-\ell
for all
0\leq\ell\leq e-1
.
Using Lemma
4.6
for
m=e
and
x_{\ell}=p^{f(d-\ell)}
, where
0\leq\ell\leq e-1
, we deduce

(5.7)

\frac{1}{f}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in S_{\mathbf{i}}}p^{f(d-\mathbf{lev}(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i})))}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}a_{\ell}x_{\ell}\geq\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}x_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}p^{f(d-\ell)}.

Equivalently,
\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in S_{\mathbf{i}}}p^{f(d-\mathbf{lev}(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{i})))}\geq f\sum_{\ell=0}^{e-1}p^{f(d-\ell)}.

The assertion of Theorem
1.6
follows immediately from combining (
5
) and (
5.7
).

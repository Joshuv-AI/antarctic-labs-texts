Symmetries of free massless particles and soft theorems
Shamik Banerjee

Abstract
In an earlier paper we have constructed a basis of massless single particle quantum states which transform in the unitary principal series representation of the four dimensional Lorentz group. The S-matrix written in this basis gives rise to the Mellin transformed amplitude of Pasterski-Shao-Strominger and its generalization. In this basis the particle can be thought of as living on the null-infinity in the Minkowski space. In this paper we take some preliminary steps to see how the connection between soft theorems and symmetries work out in this picture. For simplicity we consider only the leading soft photon and soft graviton theorems which are related to U(1) Kac-Moody and supertranslations.



Soft Theorems
Shamik Banerjee

I
Introduction

In the pioneering works
Strominger:2013lka
;
Strominger:2013jfa
a remarkable connection was found between the asymptotic symmetries of gauge and gravity theories in flat space and the physics in the infrared. In particular, this has established
Strominger:2013lka
;
Strominger:2013jfa
;
He:2014cra
;
Strominger:2014pwa
;
He
that Weinberg’s soft theorems
Weinberg:1965nx
for massless particles can be thought of as the Ward identity for infinite-dimensional global symmetries acting on the null-infinity in Minkowski space-time. Later this was extended to massive particles
Kapec:2015ena
;
Campiglia:2015qka
;
Campiglia:2015kxa
in which case the infinite dimensional symmetries act on timelike infinity rather than null-infinity. In this paper we focus only on the massless case.

In four dimensions the space of null-momentum directions is a two-sphere - the so-called celestial sphere. The four dimensional Lorentz group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
acts on the celestial sphere as the group of global conformal transformations. Now in a related development
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
Pasterski, Shao and Strominger have proposed an integral (Mellin) transform of massless (and massive) scattering amplitude (S-matrix) with the property that the resulting amplitude transforms under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
as the correlation function of products of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
primaries living on the celestial sphere. From the space-time point of view one gets the amplitude on the celestial sphere if, instead of the plane waves, one uses the conformal primary wave-functions
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Cheung:2016iub
;
deBoer:2003vf
to describe the external particles. The study of
Pasterski:2017kqt
also established that the conformal primary wave-functions are closely related to the Unitary Principal Continuous Series Representation of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
(Lorentz group)
bargman
;
Gadde:2017sjg
;
Hogervorst:2017sfd
;
Simmons-Duffin:2017nub
. The Mellin transformed amplitude has been computed for several field theories at both tree and loop level
Pasterski:2017ylz
;
Cardona:2017keg
;
Lam:2017ofc
;
Banerjee:2017jeg
;
Schreiber:2017jsr
.

Now, one of the most attractive features of this approach
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
is that it suggests a way of going
directly
from the momentum space to null-infinity. The connection between soft theorems and asymptotic symmetries also suggests that one should be able to do this. But this particular approach goes beyond the soft sector of the gauge or gravity theory. In an earlier paper
Banerjee:2018gce
we have studied this question from the Hilbert space perspective. Following
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
we have constructed
Banerjee:2018gce
a basis of massless single particle quantum states which transform in the unitary principal series representation of the Lorentz group. It turns out that these states also transform nicely under global space-time translations. From physical point of view a massless particle in this state can be thought of as living on the null-infinity.

An interesting question to ask is that how one can see the connection between soft-theorems and symmetries in this approach. In this paper we take preliminary steps in this direction by rewriting Weinberg’s soft theorems in this basis
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
;
Banerjee:2018gce
and relating it to infinite dimensional global symmetries
Strominger:2013lka
;
Strominger:2013jfa
. Technically this is very similar to
Kapec:2017gsg
except that we are using a different basis.

II
conventions

In this paper we consider only massless particles. It is well known that we can associate a Hermitian matrix with a (null) four-vector
p
in the following way,

p=\begin{pmatrix}p^{0}-p^{3}&p^{1}+ip^{2}\\
p^{1}-ip^{2}&p^{0}+p^{3}\end{pmatrix}

(1)

The determinant of the matrix gives the norm of the four vector, i.e,
\det p=-p^{2}
. Now let
\Lambda
be a
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
matrix which acts on
p
as,

p\rightarrow p^{\prime}=\Lambda p\Lambda^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}p^{\prime 0}-p^{\prime 3}&p^{\prime 1}+ip^{\prime 2}\\
p^{\prime 1}-ip^{\prime 2}&p^{\prime 0}+p^{\prime 3}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})

(2)

Under this action
p^{\prime}
is again an Hermitian matrix and the determinant does not change, i.e,
\det p=\det p^{\prime}
and so the the norm of the four-vectors
p
and
p^{\prime}
are the same. So Eq-
2
represents a Lorentz transformation. In this way we can associate to every
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
matrix
\Lambda
a Lorentz transformation which we also denote by
\Lambda
. To be more precise, the pair of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
matrices
\{\pm\Lambda\}
get mapped to the same Lorentz transformation. In terms of the components of the four vector
p
we can write the Lorentz transformation as,

p^{\prime\mu}={\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}(a,b,c,d)\ p^{\nu}

(3)

where
{\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}(a,b,c,d)
is calculated from Eq-
2
.

Another important quantity is the complex number
z
defined as,

z=\frac{p^{1}+ip^{2}}{p^{0}+p^{3}},\ \ p^{2}=0

(4)

Topologically a space-like cross-section of the null-cone is a two-sphere and
z
is the stereographic coordinate on that. So
z
specifies the direction of the null momentum vector. The space of null directions is also known as the celestial sphere.

Under (Lorentz)
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation
z
transforms as,

z\rightarrow z^{\prime}=\frac{p^{\prime 1}+ip^{\prime 2}}{p^{\prime 0}+p^{\prime 3}}=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}

(5)

So the Lorentz transformations act as global conformal transformations on
S^{2}
.

In terms of
z
a null vector
p
can be parametrized as,

p=E\bigg(1,\frac{z+\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}},\frac{-i(z-\bar{z})}{1+z\bar{z}},\frac{1-z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg),\ \ E=p^{0}

(6)

An alternative parametrization is,

p=\omega\bigg(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z}\bigg)=\frac{E}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z}\bigg)

(7)

We will use both.

Under (Lorentz)
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation,

E\rightarrow E^{\prime}=E\ \frac{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(8)

In terms of
\omega
,

\omega\rightarrow\omega^{\prime}=\omega|cz+d|^{2}

(9)

Let us now say a few things about the unitary operators which implement Lorentz transformation in Hilbert space. Let us consider a Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
which acts on space-time coordinates as,
x^{\mu}\rightarrow x^{\prime\mu}={\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}x^{\nu}
. Let
U(\Lambda)
denote the corresponding unitary operator acting on the Hilbert space. For an infinitesimal transformation we write
Weinberg:1995mt
,

U(\Lambda)=1+\frac{i}{2}\omega_{\mu\nu}J^{\mu\nu}+........,\ \ {\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}={\delta^{\mu}}_{\nu}+{\omega^{\mu}}_{\nu},\ \ \omega_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\alpha}\ {\omega^{\alpha}}_{\nu}=-\omega_{\nu\mu},\ \ J^{\mu\nu\dagger}=J^{\mu\nu}

(10)

Now the six generators
J^{\mu\nu}
can be organised into rotation and boost three-vectors given by
\vec{J}=(J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})
and
\vec{K}=(K_{1},K_{2},K_{3})
.
\vec{J}
and
\vec{K}
are both Hermitian. For our purpose it will be more convenient to write them in terms of the generators
(L_{0},L_{\pm 1},\bar{L}_{0},\bar{L}_{\pm 1})
defined as
Banerjee:2018gce
,

L_{0}=\frac{-J_{3}+iK_{3}}{2},\ \ \bar{L}_{0}=-{L_{0}}^{\dagger}

(11)

L_{1}=\frac{-J_{1}+iK_{1}}{2}+i\frac{-J_{2}+iK_{2}}{2},\ \ \bar{L}_{1}=-{L_{1}}^{\dagger}

(12)

L_{-1}=-\frac{-J_{1}+iK_{1}}{2}+i\frac{-J_{2}+iK_{2}}{2},\ \ \bar{L}_{-1}=-{L_{-1}}^{\dagger}

(13)

The commutation relation between these generators can be written as,

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},\ \ [\bar{L}_{m},\bar{L}_{n}]=(m-n)\bar{L}_{m+n},\ \ m,n=0,\pm 1

(14)

The hermiticity of
\vec{J}
and
\vec{K}
gives the following reality condition,

L_{n}^{\dagger}=-\bar{L}_{n}

(15)

Now consider an infinitesimal Lorentz transformation
U(\Lambda)
generated by
L_{n}
and
\bar{L}_{n}
. It can be written as

U(\Lambda)=1+\alpha L_{n}+\bar{\alpha}\bar{L}_{n}+.......

(16)

where
\alpha
is a complex number and
\bar{\alpha}
is the complex conjugate of
\alpha
. Using the reality condition on
L_{n}
and
\bar{L}_{n}
it is easy to see that
U(\Lambda)
is unitary.

Now the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformations which correspond to
U(\Lambda)
can be written in terms of
z
as
Banerjee:2018gce

z\rightarrow z^{\prime}=z+\alpha z^{n},\ \ n=0,\pm 1

(17)

So as usual
L_{1}
generates translation,
L_{0}
generates dilatation and
L_{-1}
generates special conformal transformations on the two-sphere parametrized by
(z,\bar{z})
.

III
Review

Let us now review the part of
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
;
Banerjee:2018gce
which will be used in this paper.

Let us consider free massless particles in four dimensions. The Hilbert space of these particles carries unitary representation of the four dimensional Poincare group
ISO(3,1)
. For our purpose it will be useful to identify the Lorentz (sub-)group with
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
.

Using this data one can construct an
abstract three dimensional space-time
with coordinates denoted by
(u,z,\bar{z})
on which the Poincare group acts as,

\Lambda(u,z,\bar{z})=\bigg(\frac{u\ (1+z\bar{z})}{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}\ ,\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\ ,\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

(18)

T(l)(u,z,\bar{z})=(u+f(z,\bar{z},l),z,\bar{z})

(19)

where

f(z,\bar{z},l)=\frac{(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(20)

Here
\Lambda
is a
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
(Lorentz) transformation and
T(l)
is a global space-time translation by a four-vector
l^{\mu}
. There is no Poincare invariant metric in this space but rather there is a conformal structure.

This three dimensional space-time hosts free Heisenberg-picture creation and annihilation fields defined as,

A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1+i\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{i\lambda}e^{iEu}\ a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},\ 2\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}

(21)

A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1-i\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{-i\lambda}e^{-iEu}\ a(p,\sigma),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},\ 2\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}

(22)

where
a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma)
and
a(p,\sigma)
are the momentum space creation and annihilation operators for a massless particle with null momentum
p
and helicity
\sigma
.

The equations defining
A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})
and
A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})
on the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space are analogous to the equations

\phi^{-}(x^{\mu})=\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2|\vec{p}|}e^{-ip.x}a^{\dagger}(p)

(23)

\phi^{+}(x^{\mu})=\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2|\vec{p}|}e^{ip.x}a(p)

(24)

which define, for example, the creation and annihilation parts of a free massless scalar (quantum) field on the Minkowski space-time. The only difference is that now we have defined
A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})
and
A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})
also for spinning massless particles.

Let us also define Lorentz tensors in this space as any function
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
which transforms under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
as,

\phi^{\prime}_{h,\bar{h}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})\bigg(\frac{dz^{\prime}}{dz}\bigg)^{h}\bigg(\frac{d\bar{z}^{\prime}}{d\bar{z}}\bigg)^{\bar{h}}=\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(25)

where
(h,\bar{h})
are arbitrary complex numbers subject to the restriction that
h-\bar{h}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
.
(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})
are related to
(u,z,\bar{z})
by Eq-(
18
).

Under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
(Lorentz) transformation creation and annihilation fields transform homogeneously as tensors,

U(\Lambda)\ A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})\ U(\Lambda)^{-1}\\
=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}\ A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}\bigg(\frac{u\ (1+z\bar{z})}{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\ ,\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

(26)

U(\Lambda)\ A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})\ U(\Lambda)^{-1}\\
=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2\bar{h}^{*}}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2h^{*}}}\ A_{\lambda,\sigma}\bigg(\frac{u\ (1+z\bar{z})}{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\ ,\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

(27)

where
(h,\bar{h})
are little-group indices given by,

h=\frac{1+i\lambda-\sigma}{2},\ \bar{h}=\frac{1+i\lambda+\sigma}{2}

(28)

*
denotes complex conjugation.

Now under global space-time translation by a four-vector
l^{\mu}
the fields transform as,

e^{-il.P}A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{il.P}=A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u+f(z,\bar{z},l),z,\bar{z})

(29)

e^{-il.P}A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{il.P}=A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u+f(z,\bar{z},l),z,\bar{z})

(30)

where
P^{\mu}
is the translation operator and
f(z,\bar{z},l)
is given in Eq-
20
.

The (anti-)commutator of the creation and annihilation fields can be computed and is given by,

[A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z}),A^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})]_{\pm}=\frac{\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma\big(i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\big)}{(1+z\bar{z})^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}\frac{\delta^{2}(z^{\prime}-z)}{\big(-i(u^{\prime}-u+i\epsilon)\big)^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}},\ \epsilon\rightarrow 0+

(31)

Here we have used the relation,

[a(p_{1},\sigma_{1}),a^{\dagger}(p_{2},\sigma_{2})]_{\pm}=(2\pi)^{3}2|\vec{p}_{1}|\delta^{3}({\vec{p}_{1}-\vec{p}_{2}})\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}

(32)

This commutator is Lorentz covariant and translation invariant.

This, at least in principle, complete the specification of the free field theory on
(u,z,\bar{z})
space because any other thing can be computed from the basic commutator given in Eq-(
31
).

Now, as far as the action of the Poincare group is concerned, this abstract three dimensional space-time can be
realized
as the null-infinity of the Minkowski space with
(u,z,\bar{z})
as Bondi coordinates. But this does not play any major role in the following discussions.

IV
Symmetries of free massless particles

The following discussion is strongly motivated by the remarkable connection between soft-theorems and asymptotic symmetries. In particular, it seems that if we want to use the approach of
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
;
Banerjee:2018gce
to study the relation between soft-theorems and symmetries, then we cannot think of these symmetries as asymptotic. The simplest reason being that the starting point is not a theory defined in Minkowski space-time.

The following discussion is a very preliminary attempt in this direction. Loosely speaking we pick up the
hard
part of the asymptotic symmetry generator (charge) and then turn off all interactions. In the non-interacting theory the hard-charge by itself is a symmetry generator and so we can study it in its own right in the free Hilbert space. This is also closer to the usual way of doing perturbative quantum field theory where we start with a free theory with certain symmetries and then ask what kind of interactions are allowed if the symmetry remains intact in the interacting theory.

IV.1
(Super)translation

Let us consider free massless particles of helicity
\sigma
. We keep the helicity unspecified because at this stage nothing really depends crucially on helicity and the following discussion is purely algebraic. For simplicity let us just consider bosons. The conclusions remain unchanged for fermions.

The Hamiltonian can be written as,

H=\int d\mu(p)|\vec{p}|\ a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma)a(p,\sigma)

(33)

where
d\mu(p)
is the Lorentz invariant measure given by

d\mu(p)=\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2|\vec{p}|}

(34)

and

[a(p_{1},\sigma),a^{\dagger}(p_{2},\sigma)]=(2\pi)^{3}\ 2|\vec{p}_{1}|\delta^{3}({\vec{p}_{1}-\vec{p}_{2}})

(35)

Now parametrizing
p
in terms of
(E,z,\bar{z})
we can write,

H=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)

(36)

where

d\mu(p)=\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2|\vec{p}|}=\frac{E^{2}dE}{(2\pi)^{3}2E}\ \frac{4d^{2}z}{(1+z\bar{z})^{2}},\ \ d^{2}z=d\real z\ d\imaginary z

(37)

Let us now consider the following charges defined as,

\boxed{T_{f}=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ f(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)=T_{f}^{\dagger}}

(38)

where
f(z,\bar{z})
is an arbitrary real smooth function on the 2-sphere parametrized by
(z,\bar{z})
. The operator
T_{f}
has the following properties :

1)

[H,T_{f}]=0

(39)

So the charges are conserved for any function
f(z,\bar{z})
.

2)

[T_{f},T_{f^{\prime}}]=0

(40)

for arbitrary
f
and
f^{\prime}
.

3)
The last important property is,

[T_{f},a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)]=Ef(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)

(41)

[T_{f},a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)]=-Ef(z,\bar{z})a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)

(42)

Let us now consider the unitary operator
U_{f}=e^{-iH_{f}}
. This unitary operator is a ”Supertranslation - operator” in the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space.

In order to see this let us go back to the basic creation/annihilation fields in the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space given by,

A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1+i\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{i\lambda}e^{iEu}\ a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},\ \sigma\in\mathbb{Z}

(43)

A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1-i\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{-i\lambda}e^{-iEu}\ a(p,\sigma),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},\ \sigma\in\mathbb{Z}

(44)

Now it is easy to see using the commutator of
T_{f}
with the momentum space creation/annihilation operators that ,

\begin{gathered}e^{iT_{f}}A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{-iT_{f}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1+i\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{i\lambda}e^{iE(u+f(z,\bar{z}))}\ a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma)\\
=A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u+f(z,\bar{z}),z,\bar{z})\end{gathered}

(45)

For infinitesimal transformation,

f(z,\bar{z})\frac{\partial A_{\lambda,\sigma}^{\dagger}}{\partial u}=i[T_{f},A_{\lambda,\sigma}^{\dagger}]

(46)

Similarly,

\begin{gathered}e^{iT_{f}}A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{-iT_{f}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1+i\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{i\lambda}e^{iE(u+f(z,\bar{z}))}\ a(p,\sigma)\\
=A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u+f(z,\bar{z}),z,\bar{z})\end{gathered}

(47)

and

f(z,\bar{z})\frac{\partial A_{\lambda,\sigma}}{\partial u}=i[T_{f},A_{\lambda,\sigma}]

(48)

for infinitesimal
f
.

So we can see that the unitary transformation
U_{f}=e^{-iT_{f}}
generates a point transformation in the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space given by,

(u,z,\bar{z})\rightarrow(u+f(z,\bar{z}),z,\bar{z})

(49)

where
f(z,\bar{z})
is an arbitrary real smooth function. Now if we choose
f(z,\bar{z})
to be of the following form

f(z,\bar{z},l)=\frac{(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(50)

where
l^{\mu}
is some four-vector, then we get back the global Minkowski space translations. If we relax this condition then we get ”Supertranslations” in the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space. At this point we would like to emphasize that this has nothing to do with asymptotic symmetry.

Let us now see explicitly what happens to the commutator of
A_{\lambda,\sigma}
and
A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}
. The commutator is given by,

[A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z}),A^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})]=\frac{\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma\big(i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\big)}{(1+z\bar{z})^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}\frac{\delta^{2}(z^{\prime}-z)}{\big(-i(u^{\prime}-u+i\epsilon)\big)^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}},\ \epsilon\rightarrow 0+

(51)

Now using the expressions for
e^{iT_{f}}A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{-iT_{f}}
and
e^{iT_{f}}A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{-iT_{f}}
we get,

\begin{gathered}\left[e^{iT_{f}}A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{-iT_{f}},e^{iT_{f}}A^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})e^{-iT_{f}}\right]\\
\\
=\left[A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u+f(z,\bar{z}),z,\bar{z}),A^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(u+f(z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime}),z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})\right]\\
\\
=\frac{\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma\big(i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\big)}{(1+z\bar{z})^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}\frac{\delta^{2}(z^{\prime}-z)}{\big(-i(u^{\prime}+f(z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})-u-f(z,\bar{z})+i\epsilon)\big)^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}\\
\\
=\frac{\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma\big(i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\big)}{(1+z\bar{z})^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}\frac{\delta^{2}(z^{\prime}-z)}{\big(-i(u^{\prime}-u+i\epsilon)\big)^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}\\
\\
=\left[A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z}),A^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})\right]\end{gathered}

(52)

The
f
’s in the denominator cancel due to the presence of
\delta^{2}(z-z^{\prime})
. Physically this is just the fact that a free particle moves without changing its direction of motion and this makes the free theory on
(u,z,\bar{z})
space ultralocal in the spatial direction.

Let us now write down the commutation relation between the Lorentz generators and the supertranslations and show that the algebra is isomorphic to the BMS algebra without superrotations.

Let us start with the definition of the charge
T_{f}
given by,

T_{f}=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ f(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)

(53)

We will also need the following transformation properties,

U(\Lambda)^{-1}a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma)U(\Lambda)=e^{i\sigma\theta(\Lambda,p)}a^{\dagger}(\Lambda^{-1}p,\sigma)

(54)

U(\Lambda)^{-1}a(p,\sigma)U(\Lambda)=e^{-i\sigma\theta(\Lambda,p)}a(\Lambda^{-1}p,\sigma)

(55)

where the quantities appearing have their standard meaning
Weinberg:1995mt
. Using this we can write,

\begin{gathered}U(\Lambda)^{-1}T_{f}U(\Lambda)=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ f(z,\bar{z})U(\Lambda)^{-1}a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)U(\Lambda)U(\Lambda)^{-1}a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)U(\Lambda)\\
=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ f(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(\Lambda^{-1}E,\Lambda^{-1}z,\Lambda^{-1}\bar{z},\sigma)a(\Lambda^{-1}E,\Lambda^{-1}z,\Lambda^{-1}\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=\int d\mu(\Lambda^{-1}p)\ \Lambda\Lambda^{-1}E\ f(\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}z,\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(\Lambda^{-1}E,\Lambda^{-1}z,\Lambda^{-1}\bar{z},\sigma)a(\Lambda^{-1}E,\Lambda^{-1}z,\Lambda^{-1}\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=\int d\mu(p)\ \Lambda E\ f(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=\int d\mu(p)\ \bigg(E\ \frac{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)\ f(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ \bigg(\frac{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}{1+z\bar{z}}\ f(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})\bigg)a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ f^{\prime}(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=T_{f^{\prime}}\end{gathered}

(56)

So the supertranslation charges transform as,

\boxed{U(\Lambda)^{-1}T_{f}U(\Lambda)=T_{f^{\prime}},\ \ f^{\prime}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}{1+z\bar{z}}\ f(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})}

(57)

Here
\Lambda z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
.

Now since the global translation generators
P^{\mu}
are already part of the supertranslations the above commutation relation already contains
U(\Lambda)^{-1}P^{\mu}U(\Lambda)={\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}P^{\nu}
. It is instructive to write the global translation generators in terms of
T_{f}
. In terms of creation/annihilation operators we can write,

P^{\mu}=\int d\mu(p)\ p^{\mu}\ a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma),

(58)

Now,

p=E\bigg(1,\frac{z+\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}},\frac{-i(z-\bar{z})}{1+z\bar{z}},\frac{1-z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)

(59)

So we get,

P^{0}=H=T_{f},\ \ f=1

(60)

P^{1}=T_{f},\ \ f=\frac{z+\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(61)

P^{2}=T_{f},\ \ f=\frac{-i(z-\bar{z})}{1+z\bar{z}}

(62)

P^{3}=T_{f},\ \ f=\frac{1-z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(63)

Let us now write down the following linear combinations,

P^{0}+P^{3}=T_{00},\ \ f=\frac{2z^{0}\bar{z}^{0}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(64)

P^{0}-P^{3}=T_{11},\ \ f=\frac{2z^{1}\bar{z}^{1}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(65)

P^{1}+iP^{2}=T_{10},\ \ f=\frac{2z^{1}\bar{z}^{0}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(66)

P^{1}-iP^{2}=T_{01},\ \ f=\frac{2z^{0}\bar{z}^{1}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(67)

IV.1.1
Global BMS

Now to write down the generators of supertranslation we have to expand the function
f(z,\bar{z})
in a basis. The natural basis to do this is the spherical harmonics on the sphere
Sachs:1962zza
. So we can define,

T_{lm}=T_{f},\ \ f\sim Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)

(68)

We can use the basic relation Eq-
57
to calculate the commutator between the Lorentz generators and the
T_{lm}
. But for simplicity we will not do so.

IV.1.2
Local BMS

It is simpler to compute the commutator between Lorentz generators and
T_{pq}
defined as
Barnich:2009se
,

T_{f}=T_{pq},\ \ f=\frac{2z^{p}\bar{z}^{q}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(69)

where
(p,q)
are integers. The corresponding generators will be denoted by
T_{pq}
.

Let us also note that the reality condition on the generators is given by,

T_{pq}^{\dagger}=T_{qp}

(70)

So the Hermitian charge can be written as,

T=\sum_{p,q}C_{pq}T_{pq},\ \ C_{pq}^{*}=C_{qp}

(71)

Let us now write down the commutators between the Lorentz generators
(L_{0},L_{\pm 1},\bar{L}_{0},\bar{L}_{\pm 1})
and
T_{pq}
. In order to this we have to use the relation derived earlier,

U(\Lambda)^{-1}T_{f}U(\Lambda)=T_{f^{\prime}},\ \ f^{\prime}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}{1+z\bar{z}}\ f(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})

(72)

Now it is much simpler to work with the function
F(z,\bar{z})
defined as,

F(z,\bar{z})=(1+z\bar{z})f(z,\bar{z})

(73)

In terms of
F
, the charge can be written as,

\begin{gathered}T_{F}=T_{f}=\int d\mu(p)\ E\ f(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=\int d\mu(p)\ \frac{E}{1+z\bar{z}}\ \ (1+z\bar{z})f(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)\\
=\int d\mu(p)\ \frac{E}{1+z\bar{z}}\ F(z,\bar{z})\ a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)\end{gathered}

(74)

Under (Lorentz)
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation
T_{F}(=T_{f})
transforms as,

U(\Lambda)^{-1}T_{F}U(\Lambda)=T_{F^{\prime}},\ \ F^{\prime}(z,\bar{z})=|cz+d|^{2}\ F(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})

(75)

Now consider the infinitesimal
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation generated by
(L_{n},\bar{L}_{n})
,

U(\Lambda)=1+\alpha L_{n}+\bar{\alpha}\bar{L}_{n},\ \ U(\Lambda)^{-1}=1-\alpha L_{n}-\bar{\alpha}\bar{L}_{n}

(76)

where
\bar{\alpha}
is the complex conjugate of
\alpha
. In terms of the
z
coordinate this generates the transformation,

z\rightarrow\Lambda z=z+\alpha z^{n}

(77)

Now the generators
T_{pq}
correspond to
F_{pq}(z,\bar{z})=2z^{p}\bar{z}^{q}
, i.e,

\boxed{T_{pq}=\int d\mu(p)\ \frac{E}{1+z\bar{z}}\ 2z^{p}\bar{z}^{q}\ a^{\dagger}(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)a(E,z,\bar{z},\sigma)=T_{qp}^{\dagger}}

(78)

Now we use the infinitesimal version of the relation given in Eq-
75

(1-\alpha L_{n}-\bar{\alpha}\bar{L}_{n})T_{F}(1+\alpha L_{n}+\bar{\alpha}\bar{L}_{n})=T_{F^{\prime}}

(79)

F^{\prime}(z,\bar{z})=\bigg(1-\frac{n+1}{2}(\alpha z^{n}+\bar{\alpha}\bar{z}^{n})\bigg)F\big(z+\alpha z^{n},\bar{z}+\bar{\alpha}\bar{z}^{n}\big)

(80)

and replace
F(z,\bar{z})
with
F_{pq}(z,\bar{z})=2z^{p}\bar{z}^{q}
and
T_{F}
with
T_{pq}
. Now after simple manipulations we get the following commutators,

\boxed{[L_{n},T_{pq}]=\bigg(\frac{n+1}{2}-p\bigg)T_{p+n,q},\ \ [\bar{L}_{n},T_{pq}]=\bigg(\frac{n+1}{2}-q\bigg)T_{p,q+n},\ \ p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

(81)

The rest of the commutators are,

\boxed{[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},\ \ [\bar{L}_{m},\bar{L}_{n}]=(m-n)\bar{L}_{m+n},\ \ [T_{pq},T_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}]=0,\ \ [L_{m},\bar{L}_{n}]=0,\ \ L_{n}^{\dagger}=-\bar{L}_{n}}

(82)

Here
m,n=0,\pm 1
.

This is a subalgebra of the extended BMS algebra, including superrotations, found in
Barnich:2009se
.

IV.1.3
Space-time relaization

Now since we know how the charges act on creation and annihilation operators in momentum space we can also also write down the action of the unitary operator
e^{-iT_{f}}
on quantum fields defined on Minkowski space. Let us consider a massless scalar field in Minkowski space. The field corresponding to this is,

\phi(x)=\int d\mu(p)\big(e^{ip.x}a(p)+e^{-ip.x}a^{\dagger}(p)\big)

(83)

So the transformed field can be written as,

\phi_{f}(x)=e^{iT_{f}}\phi(x)e^{-iT_{f}}=\int d\mu(p)\big(e^{ip.x}e^{iE_{p}f(z,\bar{z})}a(p)+e^{-ip.x}e^{-iE_{p}f(z,\bar{z})}a^{\dagger}(p)\big),\ \ E_{p}=|\vec{p}|

(84)

Now if we choose
f(z,\bar{z})
to be of the following form,

f(z,\bar{z},l)=\frac{(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(85)

then

\phi_{f}(x^{\mu})=\phi(x^{\mu}+l^{\mu})

(86)

So it is a space-time translation by four-vector
l^{\mu}
. But, there are infinitely many functions
f(z,\bar{z})
for which there is no such simple geometric interpretation in terms of space-time. In fact if we think of
a
and
a^{\dagger}
as complex conjugate c-numbers then
\phi_{f}
is a solution of the massless Klein-Gordon equation if
\phi
is. So it maps classical solutions to classical solutions as is expected for a symmetry transformation.

Now suppose we demand that an interacting theory of massless scalar fields should also have this global symmetry. Arbitrary interaction will generically break this symmetry, but
Strominger:2013jfa
;
He
have shown that if we couple the massless particle to dynamical gravity then this symmetry is preserved. This symmetry is now realized as the group of large diffeomorphisms and becomes the conventional BMS supertranslations. So now, as a part of a much bigger (gauge) symmetry group, the infinite dimensional global symmetry is realized locally geometrically in space-time. What this means is that we can write down actions, as integral over space-time of a local Lagrangian density, which are invariant under diffeomorphism and the infinite dimensional global symmetry is a subset of such diffeomorphisms. This should be contrasted with the situation in
(u,z,\bar{z})
space where supertranslations are realized locally from the very beginning.

IV.2
Global U(1) Kac-Moody

Now consider a charged massless scalar particle. The usual expression for the conserved charge of the particle can be written as,

Q=e\int d\mu(p)\big(a^{\dagger}(p)a(p)-b^{\dagger}(p)b(p)\big)

(87)

where the notation is standard. The commutators are,

[Q,a^{\dagger}(p)]=ea^{\dagger}(p),\ \ [Q,a(p)]=-ea(p)

(88)

[Q,b^{\dagger}(p)]=-eb^{\dagger}(p),\ \ [Q,b(p)]=eb(p)

(89)

Now just like supertranslation we can consider the following charges given by,

Q_{f}=e\int d\mu(p)f(z,\bar{z})\big(a^{\dagger}(p)a(p)-b^{\dagger}(p)b(p)\big)=Q_{f}^{\dagger}

(90)

Q_{f}
is obviously conserved and
[Q_{f},Q_{f^{\prime}}]=0
for all
f
. The commutator of the charge with the cretaion-annihilation operators is given by,

[Q_{f},a^{\dagger}(p)]=ef(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(p),\ \ [Q_{f},a(p)]=-ef(z,\bar{z})a(p)

(91)

[Q_{f},b^{\dagger}(p)]=-ef(z,\bar{z})a^{\dagger}(p),\ \ [Q_{f},b(p)]=ef(z,\bar{z})a(p)

(92)

On the creation and annihilation fields on the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space the unitary operator
e^{-iQ_{f}}
acts like,

e^{-iQ_{f}}A_{\lambda}^{\dagger}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{iQ_{f}}=e^{-ief(z,\bar{z})}A_{\lambda}^{\dagger}(u,z,\bar{z}),\ \ e^{-iQ_{f}}A_{\lambda}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{iQ_{f}}=e^{ief(z,\bar{z})}A_{\lambda}(u,z,\bar{z})

(93)

e^{-iQ_{f}}B_{\lambda}^{\dagger}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{iQ_{f}}=e^{ief(z,\bar{z})}B_{\lambda}^{\dagger}(u,z,\bar{z}),\ \ e^{-iQ_{f}}B_{\lambda}(u,z,\bar{z})e^{iQ_{f}}=e^{-ief(z,\bar{z})}B_{\lambda}(u,z,\bar{z})

(94)

So in the
(u,z,\bar{z})
charges act by local phase multiplication. So we get an infinite dimensional global
U(1)
symmetry. There is one copy of
U(1)
at every point of the sphere.

Let us now find out the Lorentz transformation properties of the charges. By the same method, that we have applied to supertranslation, we get,

U(\Lambda)^{-1}Q_{f}U(\Lambda)=Q_{f^{\prime}},\ \ f^{\prime}(z,\bar{z})=f(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})

(95)

So the total charge given by
Q_{f=1}
is a scalar. Now we define the ”moments”,

Q_{pq}=e\int d\mu(p)z^{p}\bar{z}^{q}\big(a^{\dagger}(p)a(p)-b^{\dagger}(p)b(p)\big)=Q_{qp}^{\dagger},\ \ (p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}

(96)

Now as we have already discussed in the case of supertranslation, to get globally defined charges we should expand
f(z,\bar{z})
in speherical harmonics on the
S^{2}
. But it is easier to work with the above charges. This is also in the spirit of
Strominger:2013lka
.

The commutator of
Q_{pq}
with the Lorentz generators
(L_{0},L_{\pm 1},\bar{L}_{0},\bar{L}_{\pm 1})
, as following from Eq-
95
is given by,

\boxed{[L_{n},Q_{pq}]=-p\ Q_{p+n,q}\ ,\ \ [\bar{L}_{n},Q_{pq}]=-q\ Q_{p,q+n}\ ,\ \ [Q_{pq},Q_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}]=0}

(97)

This is a level zero
U(1)
Kac-Moody algebra except that we have confined ourselves to the global (Lorentz) conformal algebra. To connect it to the more familiar ”holomorphic” and ”antiholomorphic” charges, we can define
Q_{p}=Q_{p,q=0}
and
\bar{Q}_{q}=Q_{p=0,q}
which satisfy,

[L_{n},Q_{p}]=-pQ_{p+n},\ \ [\bar{L}_{n},Q_{p}]=0,\ \ [Q_{p},Q_{q}]=0

(98)

[L_{n},\bar{Q}_{q}]=0\ ,\ \ [\bar{L}_{n},\bar{Q}_{q}]=-qQ_{q+n},\ \ [\bar{Q}_{p},\bar{Q}_{q}]=0

(99)

[Q,\bar{Q}]=0

(100)

One can repeat the same exercise with
f(z,\bar{z})
expanded in spherical harmonics. It will be interesting to do that.

The most important question of course is how constraining the symmetries are once we go to the interacting theory. For example the infinite dimensional global
U(1)
symmetry of a free charged scalar particle is preserved if we couple it to photons
He:2014cra
;
Strominger:2017zoo
. Then it is realized as large gauge transformations at null-infinity. So it is sufficient to have photons in the interacting theory. But is it also necessary ?

IV.3
Discussion

If we confine ourselves to the Hilbert space only then the statement that a free massless particle has infinite symmetries has, strictly speaking, no content. The Hilbert space of any free particle already has an infinite number of conserved quantities given by the number operators
a^{\dagger}(p)a(p)
. We can take any arbitrary linear combination of the number operators and generate another conserved quantity. A priori there is no reason to prefer one linear combination over the other. But we know that some of them are distinguished by the fact that they are related to space-time symmetries and are also symmetries of interacting theories. For example, Global space-time translations belong to this class but there are many more. So how do we decide which symmetries are useful ?

The connection between soft-theorems and symmetries suggest that in the free Hilbert space of massless particles we consider hermitian operators of the form,

Q_{K}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int\int d\mu(p)d\mu(p^{\prime})a(p,\sigma)^{\dagger}K(\sigma p,\sigma^{\prime}p^{\prime})a(p^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})

(101)

where
K
is a hermitian kernel which we assume to be local in momentum space, i.e, it is a sum of derivatives of a finite number of momentum space delta function. Now let us impose the following reasonable conditions :

1) The hermitian operators
Q_{K}
corresponding to different
K
’s should form a closed algebra under commutation. The algebra may be finite or infinite dimensional. In the two examples we have studied they are infinite dimensional.

2) It seems that as far as the free theory is concerned we can think in terms of (quantum) fields either on Minkowski space or on the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space. The action of
Q_{K}
on the fields should be local in
at least
one description. Supertranslations are examples of this. The action of
T_{f}
for a generic
f
is local only in the
(u,z,\bar{z})
space whereas global translations are local in both descriptions.

Q_{K}
’s satisfying at least the above two conditions may reasonably be said to form a symmetry algebra of a free massless particle. Now, heuristically speaking, free particles are maximally symmetric objects. So classifying all possible kernels
K
subject to the constraint that the charges
Q_{K}
satisfy the above two conditions can possibly give us the global symmetries that a putative interacting theory of massless particles can have. This is a mathematically well-posed problem and it will be interesting to know the answer.

V
S-Matrix and Soft Limits

V.1
A Convenient Set Of Variables

In practice it is much simpler to write the creation and annihilation fields in terms of the new variables defined as,

U=u(1+z\bar{z}),\ \omega=\frac{E}{1+z\bar{z}}

(102)

Under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
(Lorentz) transformation

\Lambda(U,z,\bar{z})=\bigg(\frac{U}{|cz+d|^{2}},\ \frac{az+b}{cz+d},\ \frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

(103)

and under space-time translation by a 4-vector
l^{\mu}
,

T(l)(u,z,\bar{z})=(u+g(z,\bar{z},l),z,\bar{z})

(104)

where

g(z,\bar{z},l)=(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z}=(1+z\bar{z})f(z,\bar{z},l)

(105)

In terms of these new variables we can write,

A^{\dagger}_{\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\ \omega^{i\lambda}e^{i\omega U}\ a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma)

(106)

and

A_{\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\ \omega^{-i\lambda}e^{-i\omega U}\ a(p,\sigma)

(107)

The null momentum
p
is now parametrized as,

p=\omega(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z})=\frac{E}{1+z\bar{z}}(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z})

(108)

V.2
S-matrix

Let us now consider a
M\rightarrow N
massless particle scattering. The quantum state of a particle is specified by its null four-momentum
p
, helicity
\sigma
and an extra label
n
which carries information about other internal quantum numbers like charge. The corresponding creation and annihilation operators are denoted by
a^{\dagger}(p,\sigma,n)
and
a(p,\sigma,n)
. Since we are considering a scattering process, there are also the labels
in
and
out
depending on whether the particle is incoming or outgoing.

Let us now introduce the
in
and
out
creation and annihilation free-fields in the
(U,z,\bar{z})
space, defined as

A^{\dagger in/out}_{n,\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\ \omega^{i\lambda}e^{i\omega U}\ a^{\dagger}_{in/out}(p,\sigma,n)

(109)

A^{in/out}_{n,\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\ \omega^{-i\lambda}e^{-i\omega U}\ a_{in/out}(p,\sigma,n)

(110)

Using these we can write,

\begin{gathered}\tilde{A}\big(\{n,\lambda,\sigma,U,z,\bar{z}\}\big)=\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8\pi^{4}})^{M+N}}\bigg(\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\omega_{i}^{-i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}U_{i}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{j}\omega_{j}^{i\lambda_{j}}e^{i\omega_{j}U_{j}}\bigg)\\
S_{N\leftarrow M}\big(\{p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)\end{gathered}

(111)

where
\ket{\Omega}
is the Poincare vacuum and the S-matrix is defined as usual by,

S_{N\leftarrow M}\big(\{p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)=\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}a_{out}(p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}a^{\dagger}_{in}(p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j})\ket{\Omega}

(112)

We will refer to L.H.S of Eq-
111
as the
\tilde{A}
-amplitude. This is essentially the integral transform of the S-matrix and its generalization which appears in
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
;
Banerjee:2018gce
. As we will see this form of the L.H.S is more convenient for discussing the soft limit.

It will be interesting to construct the interpolating fields on this space but this is beyond the scope of the present paper.

V.3
Soft Limit

Let us now consider a
M\rightarrow N+1
scattering process. We have added an extra particle in the final state with momentum
p
and helicity
\sigma
which will be taken to be soft at the end. The corresponding
\tilde{A}
-amplitude can be written as,

\begin{gathered}\bra{\Omega}A^{out}_{\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8\pi^{4}})^{M+N}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\omega^{-i\lambda}e^{-i\omega U}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\omega_{i}^{-i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}U_{i}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{j}\omega_{j}^{i\lambda_{j}}e^{i\omega_{j}U_{j}}\bigg)\\
S_{N+1\leftarrow M}\big(\{p,\sigma\ ;\ p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)\end{gathered}

(113)

In the definition of the
\tilde{A}
-amplitude all the external legs of the S-matrix are integrated over. So in order to take the soft limit we have to make the leg corresponding to the would-be soft particle free. This can be done by inverting the transformation at the soft leg. The resulting formula can be written as,

\begin{gathered}\sqrt{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \omega^{i\lambda-1}e^{i\omega U}\bra{\Omega}A^{out}_{\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8\pi^{4}})^{M+N}}\bigg(\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\omega_{i}^{-i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}U_{i}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{j}\omega_{j}^{i\lambda_{j}}e^{i\omega_{j}U_{j}}\bigg)\\
S_{N+1\leftarrow M}\big(\{p,\sigma\ ;\ p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)\end{gathered}

(114)

We can see that the outgoing leg of the S-matrix with momentum
p
and helicity
\sigma
is now free. Now one can take the soft limit by multiplying both sides with appropriate factors of
\omega
and taking
\omega
to zero. Let us denote this factor by
f(\omega)
.

where

. We have also parametrized an arbitrary null momentum

\begin{gathered}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0+}\sqrt{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \omega^{i\lambda}e^{i\omega U}\bra{\Omega}A^{out}_{\lambda,\sigma=+2}(U,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\

Now in the same way we define the positive helicity soft-graviton operator

Using this operator we can write Eq-

\begin{gathered}\bra{\Omega}O_{+}(z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8\pi^{4}})^{M+N}}\bigg(\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\omega_{i}^{-i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}U_{i}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{j}\omega_{j}^{i\lambda_{j}}e^{i\omega_{j}U_{j}}\bigg)\\
\bigg\{\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{\omega_{j}(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{j})}{z-z_{j}}-\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\frac{\omega_{i}(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{i})}{z-z_{i}}\bigg]S_{N\leftarrow M}\big(\ p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)\bigg\}\end{gathered}

(130)

Now applying
\bar{\partial}_{z}
to both sides we get,

\begin{gathered}\bra{\Omega}\bar{\partial}_{z}O_{+}(z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8\pi^{4}})^{M+N}}\bigg(\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\omega_{i}^{-i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}U_{i}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{j}\omega_{j}^{i\lambda_{j}}e^{i\omega_{j}U_{j}}\bigg)\\
\bigg\{\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{\omega_{j}}{z-z_{j}}-\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\frac{\omega_{i}}{z-z_{i}}\bigg]S_{N\leftarrow M}\big(\{p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)\bigg\}\\
\\
=\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{-i\partial/\partial U_{j}}{z-z_{j}}+\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\frac{-i\partial/\partial U_{i}}{z-z_{i}}\bigg]\\
\bigg[\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8\pi^{4}})^{M+N}}\bigg(\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\omega_{i}^{-i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}U_{i}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{j}\omega_{j}^{i\lambda_{j}}e^{i\omega_{j}U_{j}}\bigg)\\
S_{N\leftarrow M}\big(\ p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)\bigg]\\
\\
=\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{-i\partial/\partial U_{j}}{z-z_{j}}+\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\frac{-i\partial/\partial U_{i}}{z-z_{i}}\bigg]\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\end{gathered}

(131)

Therefore Weinberg’s soft-graviton theorem takes the following form,

\begin{gathered}\bra{\Omega}\bar{\partial}O_{+}(z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{-i\partial/\partial U_{j}}{z-z_{j}}+\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\frac{-i\partial/\partial U_{i}}{z-z_{i}}\bigg]\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\end{gathered}

(132)

Now using the relation

\bar{\partial}_{z}\frac{1}{z-w}=2\pi\delta^{2}(z-w)

(133)

it is easy to check that the insertion of the operator
T(g)
, given by

T(g)=\frac{i}{\pi\kappa}\int d^{2}zg(z,\bar{z})\bar{\partial}^{2}O_{+}(z,\bar{z})

(134)

leads to

\boxed{\begin{gathered}\bra{\Omega}T(g)\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}g(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{j}}+\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}g(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{i}}\bigg]\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\end{gathered}}

(135)

This relation means that the insertion of the operator
T(g)
generates a point transformation in the
(U,z,\bar{z})
space, given by,

\big(U,z,\bar{z}\big)\rightarrow\big(U+g(z,\bar{z}),z,\bar{z}\big)

(136)

where
g(z,\bar{z})
is an
arbitrary
function on the plane. If we think of the
(U,z,\bar{z})
space as the null-infinity in Minkowski space then these transformations can be identified with BMS supertranslations
Strominger:2013jfa
;
Bondi:1962px
;
Barnich:2009se
.

Now as Eq-
135
shows the
\tilde{A}
-amplitudes are not generically invariant under this transformation
Strominger:2013jfa
. But there are four transformations, corresponding to four global space-time translations, under which
\tilde{A}
remains unchanged. To see this let us first note that in terms of the variable,
U=u(1+z\bar{z})
, global space-time translation by a 4-vector
l^{\mu}
is given by,

U\rightarrow U+(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z},\ z\rightarrow z,\ \bar{z}\rightarrow\bar{z}

(137)

Now let
g(z,\bar{z},l)
denote the function
g(z,\bar{z})
corresponding to the global space-time translation by 4-vector
l^{\mu}
. Then,

g(z,\bar{z},l)=(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z}

(138)

It is also easy to see that,

{\bar{\partial}}^{2}g(z,\bar{z},l)={\partial}^{2}g(z,\bar{z},l)=0

(139)

Using
g(z,\bar{z},l)
we can write the identity given in Eq-
135
as,

\begin{gathered}\frac{i}{\pi\kappa}\int d^{2}z\ g(z,\bar{z},l)\bar{\partial}^{2}\bra{\Omega}O_{+}(z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}g(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j},l)\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{j}}+\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}g(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},l)\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{i}}\bigg]\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\end{gathered}

(140)

Now assuming that we are allowed to integrate by parts we get,

\begin{gathered}\frac{i}{\pi\kappa}\int d^{2}z\bar{\partial}^{2}g(z,\bar{z},l)\bra{\Omega}O_{+}(z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}g(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j},l)\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{j}}+\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}g(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},l)\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{i}}\bigg]\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\end{gathered}

(141)

Now using
\bar{\partial}^{2}g(z,\bar{z},l)=0
we get,

\boxed{\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}g(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j},l)\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{j}}+\sum_{i=M+1}^{M+N}g(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},l)\frac{\partial}{\partial U_{i}}\bigg]\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}=0}

(142)

This is essentially the statement of the invariance of the
\tilde{A}
amplitude (or the S-matrix in the Fock basis) under global space-time translations.

VII
A slightly different treatment of the soft-operator

Let us again consider the equation,

\begin{gathered}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0+}\sqrt{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \omega^{i\lambda}e^{i\omega U}\bra{\Omega}A^{out}_{\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}\\
=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{8\pi^{4}})^{M+N}}\bigg(\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\omega_{i}^{-i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}U_{i}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{j}\omega_{j}^{i\lambda_{j}}e^{i\omega_{j}U_{j}}\bigg)\\
\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0+}\omega S_{N+1\leftarrow M}\big(\{p,\sigma\ ;\ p_{i},\sigma_{i},n_{i}\},out\ \big|\ \{p_{j},\sigma_{j},n_{j}\},in\big)\end{gathered}

(143)

and study the following operator.

J_{\sigma,\omega}(U,z,\bar{z})=\sqrt{2\pi^{2}}\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \omega^{i\lambda}e^{i\omega U}A^{out}_{\lambda,\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})

(144)

This is the same as
\omega a^{out}(p,\sigma)
but we do not want to use this fact in this section. This is the reason why we have kept the
U
dependence in
J_{\sigma,\omega}
. The only thing that we want to use is the Lorentz transformation property of the objects involved in Eq-
143
. We will show, using the Lorentz transformation property of
A_{\lambda,\sigma}
, that in the soft limit (
\omega\rightarrow 0+
) the soft operator defined as
J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=J_{\sigma,\omega=0}(U,z,\bar{z})
transforms as a tensor in the
(U,z,\bar{z})
space. Then we invoke the soft theorem to show that the tensor
J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})
does not depend on
U
, i.e,
\partial_{U}J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=0
. This is an interesting phenomenon because no such thing can happen in a Lorentz invariant theory defined on Minkowski space. The condition that a non-trivial tensor function on Minkowski space be independent of time is not Lorentz invariant. But the equation
\partial_{U}J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=0
is in fact Lorentz invariant in
(U,z,\bar{z})
space. So the following demonstration can be thought of as an overall consistency check of the procedure.

Using the transformation property of
A_{\lambda,\sigma}
we get,

U(\Lambda)J_{\sigma,\omega}(U,z,\bar{z})U(\Lambda)^{-1}=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}J_{\sigma,\omega|cz+d|^{2}}\bigg(\frac{U}{|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\ \frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

(145)

where
U(\Lambda)
is a
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation and

h=\frac{1+\sigma}{2},\ \bar{h}=\frac{1-\sigma}{2}

(146)

Now let us take the soft limit
\omega\rightarrow 0+
and define the soft operator
J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})
to be
J_{\sigma,\omega=0}(U,z,\bar{z})
. Under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation,

U(\Lambda)J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})U(\Lambda)^{-1}=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}J_{\sigma}\bigg(\frac{U}{|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\ \frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

(147)

We can also study the transformation of
J_{\sigma,\omega}(U,z,\bar{z})
under global translation by a four vector
l^{\mu}
. The transformation property is given by,

e^{-il.P}J_{\sigma,\omega}(U,z,\bar{z})e^{il.P}=e^{-i\omega g(z,\bar{z},l)}J_{\sigma,\omega}(U+g(z,\bar{z},l),z,\bar{z})

(148)

where

g(z,\bar{z},l)=(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z}

(149)

So in the soft limit
\omega\rightarrow 0+
we get,

e^{-il.P}J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})e^{il.P}=J_{\sigma}(U+g(z,\bar{z},l),z,\bar{z})

(150)

So we can see that the soft-operator
J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})
transforms like an
ISL(2,\mathbb{C})
primary as defined in
Banerjee:2018gce
.

Now soft theorem tells us that the correlator

\bra{\Omega}J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=M+1}^{M+N}A^{out}_{n_{i},\lambda_{i},\sigma_{i}}(U_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{M}A^{\dagger in}_{n_{j},\lambda_{j},\sigma_{j}}(U_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\ket{\Omega}

(151)

does
not
depend on
U
. So we can write
\partial_{U}J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=0
as an operator equation because its matrix elements between arbitrary in and out states is zero. Let us now show that the condition
\partial_{U}J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=0
is in fact Lorentz invariant.

We can prove this more generally for any tensor
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(U,z,\bar{z})
which satisfies
\partial_{U}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(U,z,\bar{z})=0
.

Under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation,

\phi^{\prime}_{h,\bar{h}}(U^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})\bigg(\frac{dz^{\prime}}{dz}\bigg)^{h}\bigg(\frac{d\bar{z}^{\prime}}{d\bar{z}}\bigg)^{\bar{h}}=\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(U,z,\bar{z})

(152)

where

(U^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=\bigg(\frac{U}{|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

(153)

Therefore,

\phi^{\prime}_{h,\bar{h}}(U^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=(cz+d)^{2h}(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(U,z,\bar{z})

(154)

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial U^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial U}{\partial U^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial U}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial U^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial\bar{z}}{\partial U^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}=|cz+d|^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial U}

(155)

Using this we get,

\partial_{U^{\prime}}\phi^{\prime}_{h,\bar{h}}(U^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=|cz+d|^{2}(cz+d)^{2h}(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}\ \partial_{U}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(U,z,\bar{z})

(156)

So
\partial_{U}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}
transforms homogeneously under Lorentz transformation. So in the
(U,z,\bar{z})
space, if it is zero in one frame then it is zero in any other frame. So
J_{\sigma}(U,z,\bar{z})=J_{\sigma}(z,\bar{z})
.

This shows that in the
(U,z,\bar{z})
space we have well-defined Lorentz tensors of the form
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})
.

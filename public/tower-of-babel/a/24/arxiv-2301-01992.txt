Monotonicity of the period and positive periodic solutions of a quasilinear equation Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Jean Dolbeault


Abstract
. We investigate the monotonicity of the minimal period of the periodic solutions of some quasilinear differential equations involving the p p -Laplace operator. The monotonicity is obtained as a function of a Hamiltonian energy in two cases. We first extend to the case p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 classical results for p = 2 p=2 due to Chow and Wang, and Chicone. Then we consider a differential equation associated with a fundamental interpolation inequality in Sobolev spaces. In that case, we generalize to p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 a recent result due to Benguria, Depassier and Loss when p = 2 p=2 .

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study monotonicity properties of the
minimal period
of positive periodic solutions of

\big(\phi_{p}(w^{\prime})\big)^{\prime}+\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)=0\,,

(1)

where
p\geq 2
,
\phi_{p}(s)=|s|^{p-2}s
and
w\mapsto(\phi_{p}(w^{\prime}))^{\prime}
is the
p
-Laplace operator. The energy
E=|w^{\prime}|^{p}/{p^{\prime}}+\mathcal{V}(w)
is conserved if
w
solves (
1
). Here
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
is the classical notation for the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Otherwise
′
denotes the derivative of a function of one real variable. We are interested in the positive periodic solutions with energy less than
E_{*}:=\mathcal{V}(0)
which are enclosed by an homoclinic orbit attached to
(w,w^{\prime})=(0,0)
. We shall further assume that the nonnegative potential
\mathcal{V}
is such that these solutions are uniquely determined, up to translations, by the energy level
E
, with minimal period
T(E)
. Our purpose is indeed to study under which conditions
T
is an increasing function of
E
in the range
0\leq E<E_{*}
. Furthermore we will consider the asymptotic behaviour of
T(E)
as
E\to 0_{+}
and as
E\to(E_{*})_{-}
. Surprisingly enough, the limit of
T(E)
as
E\to 0_{+}
is different in the cases
p=2
and
p>2
. We shall assume that
\mathcal{V}
is a potential of class
C^{2}
defined on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathcal{V}
is a
C^{2}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and there are
A
,
B\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<A<B
such that
\mathcal{V}(0)=\mathcal{V}(B)=E_{*}>0
,
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(0)=\mathcal{V}(A)=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(A)=0
,
\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(A)\neq 0
,

and
0<\mathcal{V}(w)<E_{*}
for all
w\in(0,A)\cup(A,B)
.

(H1)

See Fig.
1
. The point
(w,w^{\prime})=(A,0)
is a stationary solution of (
1
) giving rise to a
center
surrounded by closed periodic orbits with minimal period
T(E)
, such that these periodic orbits are enclosed by the homoclinic orbit attached to
(0,0)
.

Figure 1.

\mathcal{V}
satisfying assumption (

). Our monotonicity results require stronger assumptions on
\mathcal{V}
which, typically, hold in the first case (left) but not in the second case (right).

Our first result is an extension to
p>2
of a result of Chow and Wang
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p>2
. Assume that
\mathcal{V}
satisfies (
H1
) and

(w-A)\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)>0\quad\forall\,w\in(0,A)\cup(A,B)\,.

(H2)

If
w\mapsto|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)|^{2}-p^{\prime}\,\mathcal{V}(w)\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(w)
is positive, then
E\mapsto T(E)
is increasing on
(0,E_{*})
.

Notice that
w\mapsto|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)|^{2}-p^{\prime}\,\mathcal{V}(w)\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(w)
is a positive function if and only if
w\mapsto\mathcal{V}(w)\,|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)|^{-p^{\prime}}
is a monotonically increasing function.

Our second result is an extension to
p>2
of the monotonicity result
[
7
, Theorem A]
under
Chicone’s condition
, which is also a growth condition, but of higher order in the derivatives.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p>2
. Assume that
\mathcal{V}
is a
C^{3}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
which satisfies (
H1
). If
\mathcal{V}/(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{2}
is a convex function, then
E\mapsto T(E)
is increasing on
(0,E_{*})
.

A central motivation for this paper arises from the study of a minimization problem which is exposed in Appendix
A
(with additional references) and can be reduced to the study of all positive periodic solutions on
\mathbb{R}
of

\big(\phi_{p}(w^{\prime})\big)^{\prime}+\phi_{q}(w)-\phi_{p}(w)=0\,.

(2)

Equation (
2
) enters in the framework of (
1
) with

and potential

\mathcal{V}(w)=\tfrac{1}{q}\,|w|^{q}-\,|w|^{p}-\big(\tfrac{1}{q}-\tfrac{1}{p}\big)\,,

so that

. A positive periodic solution exists only if the energy level satisfies the condition

. Again we let

be the minimal period of such a solution. Theorems

Theorem 3
.

Let
p

2

is increasing on
(0,E_{*})
with
\lim_{E\to 0_{+}}T(E)=0

\lim_{E\to(E_{*})_{-}}T(E)=+\infty

If
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
is the Hölder conjugate of the exponent
p

\mathcal{H}(u,v):=\mathcal{V}(u)+\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}\,|v|^{p^{\prime}}\,,

Equation (

with

and

. Although this Hamiltonian structure may superficially look similar to those of
[
22
, Theorem 1]
, we have a definitely different set of assumptions. In
[
21
]
, a very large set of Hamiltonian systems is considered but again our assumptions differ, for instance for the simple reason that the function

is not of class
C^{2}
. Further references on the period function can be found in
[
24
]
. There are various other extensions of Chicone’s result
[
7
]
, see for instance
[
6

The monotonicity of the minimal period as a function of the energy level is a question of interest by itself and particularly in the model case of the potential
\mathcal{V}
given by (

), even in the case

. For this last case we quote from
[
4
]
that: “It is somewhat surprising that, despite its ubiquity, the monotonicity of the period function for [this problem] in full generality was only established recently.” In
[
20
]
, Miyamoto and Yagasaki proved the monotonicity of the period function for

and for

[
24
]
, Yagasaki generalized the result to all values of

. Both papers,
[

,

]
, rely on Chicone’s criterion which is difficult to apply to non-integer values of

[
4

considered the positive periodic solutions of

(
i.e.

[

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the proof of the
p
-Laplacian version of results which are classical when

and are summarized in Theorems
1
and
2
. For

, we are not aware of such statements in the existing literature but they are natural extensions of the case

. The result of Theorem

is by far more difficult. In Section

we start with problem (
1
) by making a change of variables and obtain an expression for the minimal period which goes along some of Chicone’s ideas. We also prove some properties of the minimal period when the energy goes to zero and when it goes to the homoclinic level

[

]
for

-Laplace operator with

2.
A
p
-Laplacian version of some classical results

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorems
1
and
2
. We also provide a slightly more detailed statement of Theorem
1
.

We begin by extending
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
by Chow and Wang to the
p
-Laplacian case
p\geq 2
. We recall that
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
denotes the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Equation (
1
) has a first integral given by

\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}\,|w^{\prime}|^{p}+\mathcal{V}(w)=E

(4)

for any energy level
E\in(0,E_{*})
and the minimal period is given in terms of the energy by

T(E)=\frac{2}{p^{\prime 1/p}}\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{dw}{\big(E-\mathcal{V}(w)\big)^{1/p}}

(5)

where
w_{i}(E)
,
i=1
,
2
, are two roots of
\mathcal{V}(w)=E
such that

0<w_{1}(E)<A<w_{2}(E)<B\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{V}(w)<E\quad\forall\,w\in\big(w_{1}(E),w_{2}(E)\big)\,.

At this point, let us notice that the map
E\mapsto T(E)
is a continuous function if we assume (
H2
), but that it is not the case if
\mathcal{V}
admits another local minimum than
w=A
in the interval
(A,B)
: see for instance Fig.
1
. Let us define

\gamma(w,E):=p^{\prime}\,\big(E-\mathcal{V}(w)\big)\,,\quad\mathcal{R}(w):=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}-p^{\prime}\,\mathcal{V}(w)\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(w)

and notice that

\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial w}=-\,p^{\prime}\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial E}=p^{\prime}\,.

The following result is a detailed version of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
p\geq 2
and consider Equation (
1
) where we assume that
\mathcal{V}
satisfies (
H1
). With the above notations, for any
E\in(0,E_{*})
, it holds that

\frac{dT}{dE}(E)=\frac{2}{p^{\prime}\,E}\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{\mathcal{R}(w)}{\gamma(w,E)^{1/p}\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}}\,dw

(6)

if the integral in the right hand side is finite. Thus if
\mathcal{R}
is positive on
(0,A)\cup(A,B)
, then the minimal period is increasing.

Notice that from Assumption (
H1
), we know that
\mathcal{V}(0)=E_{*}>0
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(0)=0
so that
\lim_{w\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{V}(w)\,|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)|^{-p^{\prime}}=+\infty
and

\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}}{|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}|^{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{\mathcal{R}\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}{|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}|^{{p^{\prime}}+2}}\,.

This is incompatible with
\mathcal{R}
being a negative valued function in a neighbourhood of
w=0_{+}
. Alternatively, if we remove the assumption that
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(0)=0
, then it can make sense to assume that
\mathcal{R}
is a negative function on
(0,A)\cup(A,B)
. In this latter case, the minimal period is decreasing.

Proof.

The proof relies on the same strategy as for
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
. We skip some details and emphasize only the changes needed to cover the case
p>2
. Let us set

I(E):=\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\,\gamma(w,E)^{1/p^{\prime}}\,dw\quad\mbox{and}\quad J(E):=\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\big(\gamma(w,E)-p^{\prime}\,E\big)\,\gamma(w,E)^{1/p^{\prime}}\,dw\,.

By differentiating with respect to
E
, we obtain

\frac{dI}{dE}(E)=\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{dw}{\gamma(w,E)^{1/p}}=\frac{1}{2}\,T(E)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{dJ}{dE}(E)=\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{\gamma(w,E)-p^{\prime}E}{\gamma(w,E)^{1/p}}\,dw\,,

which implies that

\frac{dJ}{dE}(E)=I(E)-p^{\prime}\,E\,\frac{dI}{dE}(E)\,.

Differentiating once more with respect to
E
, we get

\frac{d^{2}\!J}{dE^{2}}(E)=(1-p^{\prime})\,\frac{dI}{dE}(E)-p^{\prime}\,E\,\frac{d^{2}\!I}{dE^{2}}(E)\,.

(7)

On the other hand, by integrating by parts in

\int_{w_{1}}^{w_{2}}\,\gamma^{1+1/p^{\prime}}\,\frac{\mathcal{V}^{\prime 2}-\mathcal{V}\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{V}^{\prime 2}}\,dw=\int_{w_{1}}^{w_{2}}\,\gamma^{1+1/p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}\,dw=-\,\frac{1+p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}\int_{w_{1}}^{w_{2}}\,\gamma^{1/p^{\prime}}\,\frac{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}\,\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial w}\,dw\,,

we obtain

J(E)=-\,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+1}\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\,\gamma(w,E)^{1+1/p^{\prime}}\,\frac{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}-\mathcal{V}(w)\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(w)}{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}}\,dw

by definition of
J
and
\gamma
. See
[
8
]
for further details in the case
p=2
. By differentiating twice this expression of
J(E)
with respect to
E
, we obtain

\frac{d^{2}\!J}{dE^{2}}(E)=-\,p^{\prime}\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}-\mathcal{V}(w)\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(w)}{\gamma(w,E)^{1/p}\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}}\,dw\,.

Since
T(E)=2\frac{dI}{dE}(E)
, we learn from (
7
) that

\displaystyle\frac{p^{\prime}\,E}{2}\,\frac{dT}{dE}(E)=

\displaystyle\,p^{\prime}\,E\,\frac{d^{2}\!I}{dE^{2}}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(1-p^{\prime})\,\frac{dI}{dE}(E)-\frac{d^{2}\!J}{dE^{2}}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(1-p^{\prime})\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{dw}{\gamma(w,E)^{1/p}}+p^{\prime}\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}-\mathcal{V}(w)\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(w)}{\gamma(w,E)^{1/p}\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}}\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{w_{1}(E)}^{w_{2}(E)}\frac{\mathcal{R}(w)}{\gamma(w,E)^{1/p}\,\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)^{2}}\,dw\,.

This concludes the proof of (
6
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let us consider again Equation (
1
) with a potential
\mathcal{V}
which satisfies (
H1
). We adapt the proof of
[
7
, Theorem A]
to the case
p>2
. Let us consider the function

\mathsf{H}(w):=\frac{w-A}{|w-A|}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{V}(w)}

(8)

for any
w\in(0,A)\cup(A,B)
and extend it by
\mathsf{H}(A)=0
at
w=A
. With the notations of (
5
), we have
\mathsf{H}\big(w_{1}(E)\big)=-\,\sqrt{E}
,
\mathsf{H}\big(w_{2}(E)\big)=+\,\sqrt{E}
and we can reparametrize the interval
\big(w_{1}(E),w_{2}(E)\big)
with some
\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
such that

\sqrt{E}\,\sin\theta=\mathsf{H}(w)\,.

With this change of variables, the minimal period can be written as

T(E)=2\,\frac{E^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}{(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{(\cos\theta)^{1-2/p}}{\big(\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\circ\mathsf{H}^{-1}\big)\big(\sqrt{E}\,\sin\theta\big)}\,d\theta\,.

(9)

Its derivative with respect to
E
is given by

\frac{dT}{dE}(E)=\big(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{p}\big)\,\frac{T(E)}{E}-(p^{\prime}\,E)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\mathsf{H}^{\prime\prime}(w)}{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}(w)^{3}}\,(\cos\theta)^{1-2/p}\,\sin\theta\,d\theta

where we use the short-hand notation
w=w(\theta)=\mathsf{H}^{-1}\big(\sqrt{E}\,\sin\theta\big)
. After an integration by parts and using
w^{\prime}(\theta)=\sqrt{E}\,\cos\theta/\mathsf{H}^{\prime}(w)
, this expression becomes

\frac{dT}{dE}(E)=\big(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{p}\big)\,\frac{T(E)}{E}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\,E^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{3\,\mathsf{H}^{\prime\prime}(w)^{2}-\mathsf{H}^{\prime}(w)\,\mathsf{H}^{\prime\prime\prime}(w)}{\mathsf{H}^{\prime}(w)^{5}}\,(\cos\theta)^{3-2/p}\,d\theta

and it results that

3\,(\mathsf{H}^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{H}^{\prime\prime\prime}=\frac{|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}|^{4}}{8\,\mathcal{V}^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}}{|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}|^{2}}\right)^{\prime\prime}

is positive if and only if
\mathcal{V}/(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{2}
is a convex function. This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

3.
Asymptotic results

As in Section
2
, recall that (
1
) has a first integral given by (
4
) where
E\geq 0
is the energy level. In this short section, we shall assume that (
H1
) holds, define

\omega:=\sqrt{\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(A)}>0

(10)

and make the additional hypothesis

\liminf_{w\to 0_{+}}\frac{|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(w)|}{w^{p-1}}>0\,.

(H3)

This assumption is satisfied in case of (

and in that case
\omega=\sqrt{\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(1)}=\sqrt{q-p}
, but the following result holds for a much larger class of potentials.

Lemma 5
.

Let
p>1
. If
\mathcal{V}
is a potential such that (
H1
) holds, then we have

T(E)\sim\frac{2\,\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,\Gamma\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)}{(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\omega\,\Gamma\big(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\big)}\,E^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\quad\mbox{as}\quad E\to 0_{+}

with
\omega
defined by (
10
). As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{E\to 0_{+}}T(E)=

\displaystyle\,0

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad p>2\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{E\to 0_{+}}T(E)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{2\,\pi}{\omega}

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad p=2\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{E\to 0_{+}}T(E)=

\displaystyle\,+\infty

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad p\in(1,2)\,.

Additionally, if (
H3
) holds, then for any
p>1
we have
\lim_{E\to(E_{*})_{-}}T(E)=+\infty
.

Proof.

In a neighbourhood of
w=A
, we can write
\mathcal{V}(w)\sim\frac{1}{2}\,\omega^{2}\,(w-A)^{2}
, use (
5
) and the change of variables
w=A+\sqrt{2\,E}\,y/\omega
to obtain

T(E)\sim\frac{2\,\sqrt{2}}{p^{\prime 1/p}\,\omega}\,E^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dy}{\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{1/p}}\quad\mbox{as}\quad E\to 0_{+}\,.

We obtain the expression of the integral using the formulae
[
1
, 6.2.1 & 6.2.2]
for the Euler Beta function.

Now let us consider the limit as
E\to(E_{*})_{-}
. We learn from (
H3
) that

E_{*}-\mathcal{V}(w)\geq\frac{\ell}{p}\,w^{p}

for some
\ell>0
if
w>0
is taken small enough. We deduce from (
5
) that
T(E)
diverges as
E\to(E_{*})_{-}
.
∎

4.
The monotonicity of the minimal period

In this section, we develop a new sufficient condition so that the monotonicity of the period holds, see Lemma
6
below. This condition will be used in Section
5
to prove the monotonicity statement of Theorem
3

2
to study the monotonicity of the minimal period for periodic solutions of (

), corresponding to the potential defined by (

h(y):=\frac{y-A}{|y-A|}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{V}(y^{1/p})}\quad\forall\,y\in\big(0,A^{p}\big)\cup\big(A^{p},B^{p}\big)

(D1)

extend it by

. Under Assumption (

),

,

,

of
\mathcal{V}(y^{1/p})=E
. As in Theorem

,
\mathcal{V}(y^{1/p})=E
admits no other root in
(0,B)
for any
E\in(0,E_{*})
and the map

is continuous. Assumption (

\mathcal{V}
on either

h^{\prime}(y)>0\quad\forall\,y\in\big(y_{1}(E),A^{p}\big)\cup\big(A^{p},y_{2}(E)\big)\,.

We introduce the

y\mapsto\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
such that

\sqrt{E}\,\sin\theta=h(y)\,.

(11)

As in the proof of Theorem
2
, we use the expression (
9

\mathsf{H}\big(w(y)\big):=h(y)
as in (

), where

and emphasize that

is a function of

is such that

By differentiating

in (

\frac{T^{\prime}(E)}{T(E)}=\frac{p-2}{2\,p}\,\frac{1}{E}+\frac{J^{\prime}(E)}{J(E)}

where

J^{\prime}(E)=-\,\frac{1}{2\,\sqrt{E}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,K(y)\,(\cos\theta)^{1-2/p}\,\sin\theta\,d\theta\,,

y
is given by (

Appendix A
A variational problem

A central motivation for studying (
2
) arises from the minimization problem

\mu(\lambda):=\inf_{f\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\setminus{\{0\}}}\frac{\|{f^{\prime}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}+\lambda\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}{\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}

(18)

where
q>p
is an arbitrary exponent and
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the unit circle. This problem can also be seen as the search for the optimal constant in the
interpolation inequality

\|{f^{\prime}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}+\lambda\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}\geq\mu(\lambda)\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\,.

Testing the inequality with constant functions shows that
\mu(\lambda)\leq\bar{\mu}(\lambda):=\lambda\,|\mathbb{S}^{1}|^{\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}
. If
p=2
, one can consider the interpolation inequality on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
for any integer
d\geq 1
and it is well known from the
carré du champ
method
[
2
,
3
]
that equality holds if and only if
\lambda\leq d/(q-2)
. If
\lambda>d/(q-2)
, we have
\mu(\lambda)<\bar{\mu}(\lambda)
and optimal functions are non-constant, so that
symmetry breaking
occurs. This is a basic mechanism in
phase transition
theory that can be interpreted as a bifurcation of a branch of non-trivial functions from a branch of constant functions. An important question is therefore to find the largest value of
\lambda>0
such that
\mu(\lambda)=\bar{\mu}(\lambda)
. This is an open question for
p>2
, even if
d=1
.

In dimension
d=1
, the bifurcation problem degenerates in the limit case
p=2
, for which
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1/(q-2)
according to
[
2
]
. We refer to
[
4
, Section 1]
for an introduction to the minimization problem (
18
) with
p=2
, the issue of the branches and the monotonicity of the period problem. In that case, proving that
symmetry breaking
occurs if and only if
\lambda>1/(q-2)
can be reduced to a proof of the
monotonicity of the minimal period
using Chicone’s criterion
[
7
, Theorem A]
, which provides an interesting alternative to the
carré du champ
method. The study of bifurcation problems using the period function goes back to
[
23
]
in case of equations with cubic non-linearities and was later extended to various classes of Hamiltonian systems in
[
22
,
21
,
10
,
9
,
19
]
. It is therefore natural to consider the case
p>2
.

The minimization problem with
p>2
was studied in
[
18
]
. There is an optimal function for (
18
) and the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation turns out to be the nonlinear differential equation with nonlocal terms given by

-\,\|{f^{\prime}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2-p}\,\big(\phi_{p}(f^{\prime})\big)^{\prime}+\lambda\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2-p}\,\phi_{p}(f)=\mu(\lambda)\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2-q}\,\phi_{q}(f)\,,

(19)

where we look for positive solutions on
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\setminus{\{0\}}
or equivalently positive
2\pi
-periodic solutions on
\mathbb{R}
. So far, we do not know the precise value of
\lambda
for which there is symmetry breaking but according to
[
18
]

rigidity
holds if

for some
\lambda_{1}>0
, where rigidity means that any positive solution of (
19
) is a constant. In that range, we have
\mu(\lambda)=\bar{\mu}(\lambda)
. On the contrary, one can prove that
symmetry breaking
occurs if
\lambda>\lambda_{2}
for some
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}
, so that
\mu(\lambda)<\bar{\mu}(\lambda)
and (
19
) admits non-constant positive solutions for any
\lambda>\lambda_{2}
. As shown in
[

]
, using homogeneity, scalings and a suitable change of variables, the study of (
19

\mathbb{R}

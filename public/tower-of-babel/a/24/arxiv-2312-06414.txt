Matrix-weighted little BMO spaces in two parameters
S. Kakaroumpas and O. Soler i Gibert

Abstract
. In this paper we set up a theory of two-matrix weighted little BMO in two parameters. We prove that being a member of this class is equivalent to belonging uniformly in each variable to two-matrix weighted (one-parameter) BMO, a class studied extensively by J. Isralowitz, S. Pott, S. Treil and others. Using this equivalence, we deduce lower and upper bounds in terms of the two-matrix weighted little BMO norm of the symbol for the norm of commutators with Journé operators.

1.
Introduction

The study of characterizations of the space
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
a classical topic of interest due to the role of this space as a natural substitute
of the space
L^{\infty}
in many areas of analysis.
Recall that the space
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all locally integrable functions
b
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}:=\sup_{Q}\strokedint_{Q}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}}|{\mathrm{d}}x<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and where
\langle b\rangle_{Q}
denotes the average of
b
over
Q
.
One such result is a theorem of Z. Nehari
[
30
]
,
which states that the norm of the Hankel operator
\mathrm{H}_{b}
with symbol
b

acting on the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is equivalent to the
BMO
norm of
b.

The real variable version of this theorem is the commutator theorem
[
7
]
of
R. R. Coifman, R. Rochberg and G. Weiss, which relates
the norm of commutators between Riesz transforms and multiplication by a function
b

with the
BMO
norm of
b.

Since then, the knowledge on
L^{p}
boundedness of commutators between
general Calderón-Zygmund operators and multiplication by a function
and its relation to different versions of the space
BMO
has been greatly extended.
In this article, we will consider various generalizations of the space
BMO

at the same time.

The main goal of this paper is to develop a theory of two matrix-weighted little BMO. Let
1<p<\infty
. Consider two biparameter
d\times d
-matrix valued
A_{p}
weights
U,V
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C})),
that is a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
that takes values on the complex
d\times d
matrices, and where by locally integrable we mean that the matrix norm
|B(x)|
is a locally integrable real valued function. We define the two-matrix weighted little BMO norm
\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(U,V,p)}}
by

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(U,V,p)}}:=\sup_{R}\left(\strokedint_{R}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}^{-1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},

where the supremum is taken over all rectangles
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
(with sides parallel to the coordinate axes), and all reducing operators are taken with respect to exponent
p
(see Section
2.6
for definitions). We also define

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{bmo}}(U,V,p)}}:=\sup_{R}\strokedint_{R}|\mathcal{V}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}^{-1}|\mathrm{d}x,

where the supremum is taken over all rectangles
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
(with sides parallel to the coordinate axes), and all reducing operators are taken with respect to exponent
p
.
Our first result states that both resulting spaces are equivalent.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p,U,V,B
be as above. Then, there holds

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\emph{bmo}}(U,V,p)}},

where all implied constants depend only on
n,m,d,p,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
.

Using Theorem
1.1
we deduce lower and upper bounds for commutators with Journé operators.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider a function
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C})),
let
1<p<\infty
and let
U
and
V
be biparameter
d\times d
-matrix valued
A_{p}
weights. Let
R_{j}^{i},
be the Riesz transform acting on the
i
-th variable and in the
j
-direction with respect to that variable. Then, there holds

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}}\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|[R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k},B]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}},

where the implied constant depends only on
n,m,d,p,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T
be any Journé operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
1<p<\infty
, let
U,V
be biparameter
d\times d
-matrix valued
A_{p}
weights on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and let
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
. Then

\|[T,B]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}}\lesssim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}},

where the implied constant depends only on
T,n,m,p,d,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
.

1.1.
Historical background

Since we take various generalizations of the classical BMO space at once, we review the definitions and known related results for each of these separately.

Weighted BMO spaces.
B. Muckenhoupt and R. L. Wheeden
[
28
]
considered and studied the weighted BMO norm

(1.1)

\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\nu)}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{\nu(Q)}\int_{Q}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}}|{\mathrm{d}}x,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\nu
is a weight. Such weighted BMO spaces characterize the boundedness of commutators over weighted Lebesgue spaces. Concretely, I. Holmes, M. Lacey and B. D. Wick
[
17
]
showed, extending earlier results
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
for the unweighted case, that if
1<p<\infty
,
\mu,\lambda
are
A_{p}
weights on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\nu=\mu^{1/p}\lambda^{-1/p}
, then for any Calderón–Zygmund operator
T
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
one has the inequality

\|[T,b]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\lambda)}}\leq C\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\nu)}},

where the constant
0<C<\infty
depends on
n,p,T
and
[\mu]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},[\lambda]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
, and if
R^{(1)},\ldots,R^{(n)}
are the Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|[R^{(i)},b]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\lambda)}}\geq c\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\nu)}},

where the constant
0<c<\infty
depends only on
n,p
and
[\mu]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},[\lambda]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
. The commutator
[T,b]
acts here by

[T,b](f):=T(bf)-bT(f).

We refer to Subsection
2.7
for precise definition of
A_{p}
weights. One of the crucial results of
[
17
]
was the following weighted John–Nirenberg type inequality,

(1.2)

c\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\nu)}}\leq\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}}|^{p}\lambda(x){\mathrm{d}}x\right)^{1/p}\leq C\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\nu)}},

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the constants
0<c,C<\infty
, depend on
n,p
and
[\mu]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},[\lambda]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
. In
[
17
]
it was also shown that
\text{BMO}(\nu)
is the dual to a certain weighted Hardy space.

Matrix-weighted BMO spaces.
Due to the lack of commutativity of matrices, one has to formulate all matrix-weighted BMO spaces directly in a two-weight fashion. Namely, let
1<p<\infty
and consider two
d\times d
-matrix valued
A_{p}
weights
U,V
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};M_{d}(\mathbb{C})),
that is a locally integrable complex
d\times d
-matrix valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the sense that the matrix norm
|B(x)|
is a locally integrable real valued function. We define the two matrix-weighted Bloom BMO norm
\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(U,V,p)}}
by

(1.3)

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(U,V,p)}}:=\sup_{Q}\left(\strokedint_{Q}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}})\mathcal{U}^{-1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sides parallel to the coordinate hyperplanes, and where
\mathcal{U}_{Q}
denotes the reducing operator of
U
over the cube
Q
taken with respect to exponent
p
(see Section
2.6
for the corresponding definition and a brief exposition). This is the direct matrix-valued analog of the middle term of (
1.2
). As explained in
[
24
]
, the direct matrix-valued analog of (
1.1
) is

(1.4)

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{BMO}}(U,V,p)}}:=\sup_{Q}\strokedint_{Q}|\mathcal{V}_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}})\mathcal{U}^{-1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle Q}}|\mathrm{d}x,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sides parallel to the coordinate hyperplanes, and all reducing operators are taken with respect to exponent
p
. These matrix-weighted BMO spaces and variants of them were studied extensively in
[
20
]
,
[
22
]
, culminating in
[
24
]
which gave a complete theory of such spaces. In particular,
[
24
]
established a matrix-valued analog of (
1.2
),

(1.5)

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{BMO}}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B^{\ast}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(V^{\prime},U^{\prime},p^{\prime})}},

where all implied constants depend only on
n,d,p,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
. Moreover, work
[
20
]
established in addition an
H^{1}
-BMO duality type result in the matrix-weighted setting for
p=2
.

Isralowitz–Pott–Treil
[
24
]
showed that
\text{BMO}(U,V,p)
is the correct space to characterize boundedness between matrix-weighted spaces of commutators of the form
[T,B]
, where
T
is a Calderón–Zygmund operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see Subsection
2.2
for the precise definition of
[T,B]
in this context). They used a clever tensorization trick that allows one to reduce
d\times d
-matrix weighted estimates for the commutator
[T,B]
to
higher-dimensional

(2d)\times(2d)
-matrix weighted estimates for the operator
T
. We refer to
[
24
]
for details. It is worth noting that the results of
[
24
]
were extended a little later by Cardenas–Isralowitz
[
5
]
to fractional matrix-weighted BMO spaces and commutators of fractional integral operators.

Biparameter BMO spaces.
The study of
biparameter
BMO spaces (i. e. invariant under rescaling each variable separately) was initiated by S. Y. A. Chang
[
6
]
and R. Fefferman
[
13
]
. These works investigated extensively the biparameter product BMO space
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
on the product space
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, which consists of all locally integrable functions
b
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}}:=\sup_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\\
R\subseteq\Omega\end{subarray}}\left|(b,w_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\right|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all (say) bounded Borel subsets
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with nonzero measure,
where
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}
stands for the family of all dyadic rectangles of
\mathbb{R}^{2},

and
(w_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle R\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}}}
is some (mildly regular) wavelet system adapted to
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}
. Here and below we denote

(b,w_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}b(x)w_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}(x){\mathrm{d}}x.

These works by Chang and Fefferman established in particular that
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
is the dual to the biparameter Hardy space.

One can also define a different (nonequivalent) biparameter BMO space
by an oscillation expression analogous to that used in classical BMO.
To this end, let
b
be a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We say that
b
belongs to the little BMO space
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
if

\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}:=\sup_{R}\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}|{\mathrm{d}}x<\infty,

where the supremum ranges over all rectangles
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with sides parallel to the coordinate planes but of arbitrary eccentricity, and where
\langle b\rangle_{R}
denotes the average of
b
over the rectangle
R
. Given now a weight
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a locally integrable function
b
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, I. Holmes, S. Petermichl and B. D. Wick
[
18
]
introduced and studied the weighted little BMO space
\text{bmo}(\nu)
which consists of all locally integrable functions
b
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(\nu)}}:=\sup_{R}\frac{1}{\nu(R)}\int_{R}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}|{\mathrm{d}}x<\infty,

where the supremum ranges over all rectangles
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with sides parallel to the coordinate planes but of arbitrary eccentricity. In
[
18
]
it is shown (extending an earlier result of S. H. Ferguson and C. Sadosky
[
15
]
for the tensor product of two Hilbert transforms in the unweighted case) that if
1<p<\infty
,
\mu,\lambda
are biparameter
A_{p}
weights on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\nu=\mu^{1/p}\lambda^{-1/p}
, then for any Journé operator
T
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
one has the inequality

(1.6)

\|[T,b]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\lambda)}}\leq C\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(\nu)}},

where the constant
0<C<\infty
depends on
n,m,p,T
and
[\mu]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}},[\lambda]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
. Moreover,
[
18
]
establishes that, with the same conditions on
p
,
\mu
and
\lambda
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and if
R^{(1)},\ldots,R^{(n)}
are the Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|[R^{(i)}\otimes R^{(j)},b]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\lambda)}}\geq c\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(\nu)}},

where the constant
0<c<\infty
depends only on
n,p
and
[\mu]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}},[\lambda]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
. Holmes–Petermichl–Wick
[
18
]
provided through an application of the Lebesgue differentiation theorem a characterization of weighted little BMO in terms of usual (one-parameter) BMO, namely that

(1.7)

\|b\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(\nu)}}\sim\max\bigg(\esssup_{x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|b(\,\cdot\,,x_{2})\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\nu(\,\cdot\,,x_{2}))}},~\esssup_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|b(x_{1},\,\cdot\,)\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{BMO}(\nu(x_{1},\,\cdot\,))}}\bigg)

with the comparability constants depending only on
n,m
and
[\nu]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
, allowing them to deduce weighted John–Nirenberg type inequalities for weighted little BMO analogous to estimate (
1.2
).

2.
Background

In this section we fix some important definitions and notation, and we recall known facts about matrix-valued weights that we will need in the sequel.

2.1.
Tensor products of operators

If
f
is any function on a product space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then for all
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
f_{x_{1}}
the function on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
given by

f_{x_{1}}(x_{2}):=f(x_{1},x_{2}).

Moreover, for all
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote by
f_{x_{2}}
the function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

f_{x_{2}}(x_{1}):=f(x_{1},x_{2}).

We will be often denoting
f_{x_{1}},f_{x_{2}}
by
f(x_{1},\,\cdot\,),f(\,\cdot\,,x_{2})
respectively.

Let
T_{1}
be an operator acting on (suitable)
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
T_{1}f
is again a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
T_{2}
be an operator acting on (suitable)
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
T_{2}f
is again a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The tensor product
T_{1}\otimes T_{2}
is defined as the operator acting on (suitable)
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued functions
f
on the product space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

(T_{1}\otimes T_{2})f(x_{1},x_{2}):=T_{2}(g(x_{1},\,\cdot\,))(x_{2}),\qquad g(x_{1},y_{2}):=T_{1}(f(\,\cdot\,,y_{2}))(x_{1}),

for
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

2.2.
Vector-valued extensions of linear operators

Let
T
be a linear operator acting on (suitable) scalar-valued (i.e.
\mathbb{C}
-valued) functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in such a way that
Tf
is also a scalar-valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, we define the (canonical)
d
-vector valued extension of
T
acting on (suitable)
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows. Given any (suitable)
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

Tf:=(Tf_{1},\ldots,Tf_{d}),

where
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d})
, i.e.
f_{i}
is the scalar-valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
obtained as the
i
-th coordinate projection of
f
, for all
i=1,\ldots,d
; observe that
Tf
is itself a

\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Notice that abusing notation, we have denoted
T
and its vector-valued extension by the same symbol; we will always be doing so in the sequel.

Given a linear operator
T
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a
d\times d
-matrix valued function
B
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can consider the commutator
[T,B]
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

[T,B](f):=T(Bf)-BT(f).

2.3.
Dyadic grids

For definiteness, in what follows intervals in
\mathbb{R}
will always be assumed to be left-closed, right-open and bounded. A cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be a set of the form
Q=I_{1}\times\ldots\times I_{n}
, where
I_{k},~k=1,\ldots,n
are intervals in
\mathbb{R}
of the same length; we denote by
\ell(Q):=|I_{1}|
the sidelength of
Q
. A rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
(with sides parallel to the coordinate axes) will be a set of the form
R=R_{1}\times R_{2}
, where
R_{1}
is a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R_{2}
is a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

A collection
\mathcal{D}
of intervals in
\mathbb{R}
will be said to be a
dyadic grid
in
\mathbb{R}
if one can write
\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{k}
, such that the following hold:

•

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{D}_{k}
forms a partition of
\mathbb{R}
, and all intervals in
\mathcal{D}_{k}
have the same length
2^{-k}

•

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, every
J\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
can be written as a union of exactly
2
intervals in
\mathcal{D}_{k+1}
.

We say that
\mathcal{D}
is the
standard dyadic grid
in
\mathbb{R}
if

\mathcal{D}:=\{[m2^{k},(m+1)2^{k}):~k,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

A collection
\mathcal{D}
of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be said to be a
dyadic grid
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if for some dyadic grids
\mathcal{D}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}
one can write
\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{k}
, where

\mathcal{D}_{k}=\{I_{1}\times\ldots\times I_{n}:~I_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{i},~|I_{i}|=2^{-k},~i=1,\ldots,n\}.

We say that
\mathcal{D}
is the
standard dyadic grid
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if

\mathcal{D}:=\{[m_{1}2^{k},(m_{1}+1)2^{k})\times\dots\times[m_{n}2^{k},(m_{n}+1)2^{k}):~k,m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

If
\mathcal{D}
is a dyadic grid in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we denote

\operatorname{ch}_{i}(Q):=\{K\in\mathcal{D}:~K\subseteq Q,~|K|=2^{-in}|Q|\},\qquad Q\in\mathcal{D},~i=0,1,2,\ldots.

A collection
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}
is said to be a
product dyadic grid
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
if for a dyadic grid
\mathcal{D}^{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a dyadic grid
\mathcal{D}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
we have

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}:=\{R_{1}\times R_{2}:~R_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{i},~i=1,2\},

and in this case we write (slightly abusing the notation)
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}^{1}\times\mathcal{D}^{2}
.

If
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}
is a product dyadic grid in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then we denote

\operatorname{ch}_{i}(R):=\{Q_{1}\times Q_{2}:~Q_{i}\in\operatorname{ch}_{i_{j}}(R_{j}),~j=1,2\},\qquad R\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}},~i=(i_{1},i_{2}),~i_{1},i_{2}=0,1,2,\ldots.

We say that
R
is the
i
-th ancestor of
P
in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}
if
P\in\operatorname{ch}_{i}(R)
.

2.4.
Expressing matrix norms in terms of the norms of the columns

In the sequel we denote by
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{d})
the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. We will be often using the fact that

(2.1)

|A|\sim_{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}|Ae_{k}|,\quad\forall A\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}),

without explicitly mentioning it. Note also that for all
0<p<\infty
and for nonnegative numbers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
we have the estimate

\min(1,d^{p-1})\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{p}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\right)^{p}\leq\max(1,d^{p-1})\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{p}.

In particular, if
F_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is a sequence of Lebesgue-measurable functions and
0<p,q,r<\infty
, then we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|F_{k}(x)|^{r}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}\sim_{d,p,q,r}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|F_{k}(x)e_{i}|\right)^{r}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\sim_{d,p,q,r}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|F_{k}(x)e_{i}|^{r}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}\sim_{d,p,q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|F_{k}(x)e_{i}|^{r}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\sim_{d,q}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|F_{k}(x)e_{i}|^{r}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}.

Matrix-valued weighted Lebesgue spaces

A function

on

is said to be a

-matrix valued weight
if it is a locally integrable

-valued function such that

-matrix valued weight

on

2.6.
Reducing operators

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
E
be a bounded measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of nonzero measure. Let
W
be a
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
-valued function on
E
that is integrable over
E
(meaning that
\int_{E}|W(x)|\mathrm{d}x<\infty
) and that takes a.e. values in the set of positive-definite
d\times d
-matrices. It is proved in
[
16
, Proposition 1.2]
(in the generality of abstract norms on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
) as an application of the standard properties of John ellipsoids that there exists a (not necessarily unique) positive-definite matrix
\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, called
reducing operator
of
W
over
E
with respect to the exponent
p
, such that

(2.3)

\left(\strokedint_{E}|W(x)^{1/p}e|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}\leq|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}e|\leq\sqrt{d}\left(\strokedint_{E}|W(x)^{1/p}e|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},\qquad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

If
d=1
, i.e.
W
is scalar-valued, then one can clearly take (and we will always be taking)
\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}:=(W)_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{1/p}
. Moreover, if
p=2
, then

\strokedint_{E}|W(x)^{1/2}e|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\strokedint_{E}\langle W(x)e,e\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\langle(W)_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}e,e\rangle=|(W)_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{1/2}e|^{2},\qquad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

and thus in this special case one can take (and we will always be taking)
\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}:=(W)_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{1/2}
.

Assume in addition now that the function
W^{\prime}\coloneq W^{-1/(p-1)}
is also integrable over
E
. Then we let
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}
be the reducing matrix of
W^{\prime}
over
E
corresponding to the exponent
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
, so that

|\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}e|\sim_{d}\left(\strokedint_{E}|W^{\prime}(x)^{1/p^{\prime}}e|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}=\left(\strokedint_{E}|W(x)^{-1/p}e|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p^{\prime}},\qquad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

Note that one can take (and we will always be taking)
\mathcal{W}^{\prime\prime}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}=\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}
. Observe that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{\prime}|\sim_{p,d}\left(\strokedint_{E}\left(\strokedint_{E}|W(x)^{1/p}W(y)^{-1/p}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}.

For a detailed exposition of reducing operators we refer for example to
[
12
]
. Here we note just the following estimates that we will need in the sequel. A proof of part (1) can be found, for example, in
[
12
]
. A proof of part (2) can be found in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
W
be a
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
-valued function on
E
, such that
W
and
W^{\prime}=W^{-1/(p-1)}
are integrable over
E
for some
1<p<\infty
. Set

\displaystyle C_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}:=\strokedint_{E}\left(\strokedint_{E}|W(x)^{1/p}W(y)^{-1/p}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x.

We consider reducing operators of
W
with respect to exponent
p
and of
W^{\prime}
with respect to exponent
p^{\prime}
.

(1)

There holds

|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{-1}e|\leq|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{\prime}e|\lesssim_{p,d}C_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{1/p}|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{-1}e|,\qquad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

(2)

There holds

|\langle W^{1/p}\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}e|\leq|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}e|\lesssim_{p,d}C_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}^{d/p}|\langle W^{1/p}\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}e|,\qquad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

2.6.1.
Iterating reducing operators

Let
E,F
be measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m}
respectively with
0<|E|,|F|<\infty
. Let
1<p<\infty
. Let

be a

-valued integrable function on

taking a.e. values in the set of positive-definite

-matrices, such that

. For all

,

. For a.e.

over

with respect to the exponent

. If

Set

12
]

]

Of course, analogous facts hold if one “iterates” the operation of taking reducing operators in the reverse order.

3.1.

Let

Lemma 3.2

There holds

Proof.

The proof combines and adapts parts of the proofs of

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
F\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
be arbitrary. Let
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
F(\,\cdot\,,x_{2})\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
, and let
(Q_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1}
be a sequence of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
shrinking to
x_{2}
. Assume that for
some
cube
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(possibly depending on
F
), there holds

\strokedint_{P}|AF(x_{1},x_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|AF(x_{1},y_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2},\qquad\forall A,B\in M_{d}(\mathbb{Q}+i\mathbb{Q}).

Then, there holds

\strokedint_{P}|AF(x_{1},x_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|AF(x_{1},y_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2},\qquad\forall A,B\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}).

Proof.

Let
A,B\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
be arbitrary. Pick sequences
(A_{\ell})^{\infty}_{\ell=1},(B_{\ell})^{\infty}_{\ell=1}
in
M_{d}(\mathbb{Q}+i\mathbb{Q})
such that
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}A_{\ell}=A
,
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}B_{\ell}=B
. Then, since by assumption
F(\,\cdot\,,x_{2})\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
by the Theorem of Dominated Convergence we deduce

\strokedint_{P}|AF(x_{1},x_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\strokedint_{P}|A_{\ell}F(x_{1},x_{2})B_{\ell}|\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

We observe now that for all
k,\ell=1,2,\ldots
we have

\displaystyle\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|A_{\ell}F(x_{1},y_{2})B_{\ell}|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}\leq\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|AF(x_{1},y_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

\displaystyle+\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|AF(x_{1},y_{2})(B_{\ell}-B)|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}+\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|(A_{\ell}-A)F(x_{1},y_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

\displaystyle+\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|(A_{\ell}-A)F(x_{1},y_{2})(B_{\ell}-B)|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|AF(x_{1},y_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}+|A|\cdot|B_{\ell}-B|\cdot\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|F(x_{1},y_{2})|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

\displaystyle+|A_{\ell}-A|\cdot|B|\cdot\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|F(x_{1},y_{2})|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}+|A_{\ell}-A|\cdot|B_{\ell}-B|\cdot\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|F(x_{1},y_{2})|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}.

Taking for every fixed
\ell=1,2,\ldots
liminf as
k\rightarrow\infty
and using the assumption we obtain

\displaystyle\strokedint_{P}|A_{\ell}F(x_{1},x_{2})B_{\ell}|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\strokedint_{P\times Q_{k}}|AF(x_{1},y_{2})B|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

\displaystyle+(|A|\cdot|B_{\ell}-B|+|A_{\ell}-A|\cdot|B|+|A_{\ell}-A|\cdot|B_{\ell}-B|)\cdot\strokedint_{P}|F(x_{1},x_{2})|\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

The desired result follows then after taking the limit as
\ell\rightarrow\infty
in the last display.
∎

Continuous versions

Let

. Let
U,V
be biparameter
d\times d
-matrix valued

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(U,V,p)}}
by

(3.7)

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(U,V,p)}}:=\sup_{R}\left(\strokedint_{R}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}^{-1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},

where the supremum is taken over all rectangles

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

. We also define

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}^{2}(U,V,p)}}:=\sup_{R}\left(\strokedint_{R}\left(\strokedint_{R}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-B(y))U(y)^{-1/p}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p^{\prime}/p}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/p^{\prime}},

where both suprema are taken over all rectangles

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

By the well-known
1/3
trick (see e.g.
[
26
]
) we have that there exist
3^{n}
dyadic grids
\mathcal{D}_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{3^{n}}^{(n)}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that for all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exist
i\in\{1,\ldots,3^{n}\}
and
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{i}
with
Q\subseteq Q^{\prime}
and
\ell(Q^{\prime})\leq 6\ell(Q)
. Thus, we can consider the product dyadic grids

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}^{i,j}:=\mathcal{D}_{i}^{(n)}\times\mathcal{D}_{j}^{(m)},\qquad i=1,\ldots,3^{n},~j=1,\ldots,3^{m}

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. To be able to use covering arguments in the matrix-weighted setting, we need the following trivial observation, which follows immediately from the definition of reducing operators.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
E,F
be measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
E\subseteq F
and
0<|E|,|F|<\infty
. Let
W\in L^{1}(F;M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
. Then, there holds

|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}e|\lesssim_{p,d}\left(\frac{|F|}{|E|}\right)^{1/p}|\mathcal{W}_{{}_{\scriptstyle F}}e|,\qquad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

We can now extend Proposition
3.1
to the continuous setting.

Corollary 3.7
.

There holds

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}}\sim\max(\esssup_{x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|B_{x_{2}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{BMO}(U_{x_{2}},V_{x_{2}},p)}},\esssup_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|B_{x_{1}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{BMO}(U_{x_{1}},V_{x_{1}},p)}}),

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B^{\ast}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(V^{\prime},U^{\prime},p^{\prime})}}\sim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\emph{bmo}}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B^{\ast}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\emph{bmo}}(V^{\prime},U^{\prime},p^{\prime})}},

and

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}^{1}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}^{2}(U,V,p)}},

where all implied constants depend only on
n,m,p,d,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
.

Proof.

Let
R
be any rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. There exist
i\in\{1,\ldots,3^{n}\}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,3^{m}\}
and
S\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}^{i,j}
, such that
R\subseteq S
and
|S|\leq 6^{n+m}|R|
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left(\strokedint_{R}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}^{-1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,m,p,d}\left(\strokedint_{S}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,m,p,d}\left(\strokedint_{S}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,d}[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}^{i,j}}}^{1/p}\left(\strokedint_{S}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}^{-1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}^{1/p}\left(\strokedint_{S}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}^{-1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}^{1/p}\left(\strokedint_{S}|V(x)^{1/p}(\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}^{-1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},

where in the first
\lesssim_{n,m,p,d}
and in the first
\lesssim_{p,d}
we used Lemma
2.1
, and in the second
\lesssim_{n,m,p,d}
we used Lemma
3.6
. By definition we have

\left(\strokedint_{S}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}^{-1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}\leq\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}_{{}_{\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}^{i,j}}}(U,V,p)}}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left(\strokedint_{S}|V(x)^{1/p}(\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}^{-1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}\sim_{p,d}|\mathcal{V}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}(\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}^{-1}|

\displaystyle\leq\strokedint_{R}|\mathcal{V}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}^{-1}|\mathrm{d}x\lesssim_{n,m}\strokedint_{S}|\mathcal{V}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}(B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}})\mathcal{U}_{{}_{\scriptstyle S}}^{-1}|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{bmo}}_{{}_{\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}^{i,j}}}(U,V,p)}}\sim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}_{{}_{\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}^{i,j}}}(U,V,p)}},

where in the last
\sim
all implied constants depend only on
n,m,p,d,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
.

Therefore

(3.11)

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}(U,V,p)}}\sim\max\left\{\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\text{bmo}_{{}_{\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}^{i,j}}}(U,V,p)}}:~i=1,\ldots,3^{n},~j=1,\ldots,3^{m}\right\},

where all implied constants depend only on
n,m,p,d,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
. Similar arguments yield analogs of (
3.11
) for the other variants of matrix-weighted little BMO (as well as for one-parameter matrix-weighted BMO spaces), thus the desired estimates follow from Proposition
3.1
.
∎

4.
Matrix-weighted little BMO lower bounds

Here we show how to adapt the argument from
[
24
, Section 3]
to get a lower bound, in terms of the little BMO matrix norm, for commutators with all possible Riesz transforms.
To be more precise, let
R^{1}_{j}
with
1\leq j\leq n
and
R^{2}_{k}
with
1\leq k\leq m
denote, respectively, the Riesz transforms acting on the first and second variables for scalar functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}.

As before, we use the same notation for the canonical extensions of
R^{1}_{j}
and
R^{2}_{k}
acting componentwise on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
(without risk of ambiguity as we will only use this canonical extension in the rest of this section).
Recall that, taking
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},
these operators have kernels

\frac{x_{j}-x_{j}^{\prime}}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{n+1}}\otimes I_{d}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{y_{k}-y_{k}^{\prime}}{|y-y^{\prime}|^{m+1}}\otimes I_{d}.

Our goal is to show the following

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider a function
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C})),
let
1<p<\infty
and let
U
and
V
be
d\times d
-matrix valued biparameter
A_{p}
weights.
Then, there holds

(4.1)

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}}\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|[R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k},B]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}},

where the implied constant depends only on the
n,m,p,d,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}}}
.

It can look like the proof of this result takes a different approach to the classical one for such lower bounds, due to Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss
[
7
]
and based on a property of spherical harmonics.
However, the main idea of this approach is still at the core of the argument since we will also express the unit as a linear combination of products of Riesz transforms, together with the tensorization trick due to Isralowitz–Pott–Treil
[
24
]
.
For a suitable scalar function
h
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m},
let us denote by
\mathcal{F}\left[{h}\right](t)\coloneq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}h(x)e^{-i\langle t,x\rangle}\mathrm{d}x
its Fourier transform.
Consider now the Wiener algebra
W_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})\coloneq\{\psi=\mathcal{F}\left[{\rho}\right]\colon\rho\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})\},
that is the set of functions that are Fourier transforms of integrable functions.
First we need the following lemma, which follows from an observation in
[
27
, Lemma 2.1]
.
For
x=(x_{1},x_{2}),y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},
let us denote by
K_{jk}(x,y)
the kernel of the product
R^{1}_{j}R^{2}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
W
is a
d\times d
-matrix valued weight and that
R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}
are bounded in
L^{p}(W)
for
1\leq j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq m.

That is, for compactly supported
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}(W)
and
g\in L^{2}\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(W^{-p^{\prime}/p})
such that
\mathrm{dist}(\operatorname{supp}f,\operatorname{supp}g)>0,
if
E
is a measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m},
then

(4.2)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}(x,y)K_{jk}(x,y)\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right|\\
\leq\|\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}}\|f\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)}}\|g\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p^{\prime}}(W^{-p^{\prime}/p})}}.

Then, if
\psi\in W_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}),

\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
and
E
is a measurable set of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m},
it holds that

(4.3)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\psi\left(\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{\varepsilon_{1}},\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}(x,y)K_{jk}(x,y)\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right|\\
\lesssim\|\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle E}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}}\|f\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)}}\|g\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p^{\prime}}(W^{-p^{\prime}/p})}}.

Proof.

Since
K_{jk}(x,y)
satisfies (
4.2
), so does the kernel
K_{jk}(x,y)e^{-i\langle a,x-y\rangle}
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}.

In particular, taking
\rho\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}),
the kernel

\mathcal{F}\left[{\rho}\right](x-y)K_{jk}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\rho(a)K_{jk}(x,y)e^{-i\langle a,x-y\rangle}\mathrm{d}a

also satisfies (
4.2
) (with
\|\rho\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}}
as a multiplicative constant).
Now, consider
(x_{1},x_{2}),(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
and observe that rescaling each variable separately we get

\mathcal{F}\left[{\rho}\right](t_{1}/\varepsilon_{1},t_{2}/\varepsilon_{2})=\varepsilon_{1}^{n}\varepsilon_{2}^{m}\mathcal{F}\left[{\rho(\varepsilon_{1}x_{1},\varepsilon_{2}x_{2})}\right]

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\varepsilon_{1}^{n}\varepsilon_{2}^{m}\rho(\varepsilon_{1}x_{1},\varepsilon_{2}x_{2})|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\|\rho\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}}.

Thus, letting
\rho
to be such that
\psi=\mathcal{F}\left[{\rho}\right]
and taking these observations into account, the kernel
\psi((x_{1}-y_{1})/\varepsilon_{1},(x_{2}-y_{2})/\varepsilon_{2})K_{jk}(x,y)
satisfies equation (
4.2
) (with the multiplicative constant
\|\rho\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}}
), which is precisely (
4.3
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Observe that if
W
is a
d\times d
-matrix valued biparameter
A_{2}
weight defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m},
then the operators
R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}
(which are paraproduct-free) are bounded in
L^{p}(W)
for
1\leq j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq m
(see
[
12
]
, and also
[
29
]
in the case
p=2
and
[
10
]
in the case of general
p
for the one-parameter results).

The next lemma is just a reformulation of
[
24
, Lemma 3.2]
for it to be directly applicable to the biparameter setting.
We include its proof, which is elementary, for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm c}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}),
then the three functions
|x|\varphi(x,y),

|y|\varphi(x,y)
and
|x||y|\varphi(x,y)
are in
W_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}).

Proof.

The same as in
[
24
, Lemma 3.2]
.
We show first the case of
|x||y|\varphi(x,y),
as the other two are treated similarly, and then we indicate the only difference for those.
Let
F(x,y)=|x||y|\varphi(x,y)
and take
1<\delta<\min\{1+1/(n+m-1),2\}.

For
\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and
\beta\in\{0,1\}^{m}
define

P_{\alpha\beta}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon(-1)^{\alpha_{j}}|x_{\alpha_{j}}|\leq(-1)^{\alpha_{j}},(-1)^{\beta_{k}}|y_{\beta_{k}}|\leq(-1)^{\beta_{k}}\}.

Then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}\left[{F}\right]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\int_{P_{\alpha\beta}}|x^{\alpha}|^{-1}|y^{\beta}|^{-1}\left(|x^{\alpha}||y^{\beta}||\mathcal{F}\left[{F}\right](x,y)|\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\int_{P_{\alpha\beta}}|x^{\alpha}|^{-\delta}|y^{\beta}|^{-\delta}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\delta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}\mathcal{F}\left[{F}\right](x,y)|^{\delta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\delta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|\mathcal{F}\left[{D_{1}^{\alpha}D_{2}^{\beta}F}\right](x,y)|^{\delta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\delta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|D_{1}^{\alpha}D_{2}^{\beta}F(x,y)|^{\delta}\right)^{1/\delta},

where we used the Hausdorff–Young Inequality in the last step and the symbols
D_{1}^{\alpha}
and
D_{2}^{\beta}
to denote, respectively, the
\alpha
-partial on the first variable and the
\beta
-partial derivative on the second variable.
The result now follows from the estimate

|D_{1}^{\alpha}D_{2}^{\beta}F(x,y)|\lesssim|(x,y)|^{1-|\alpha|-|\beta|},

the fact that
\varphi
is compactly supported and that
1<\delta<1+1/(n+m-1)
and Fourier inversion.

For the case of
|x|\varphi(x,y)
and
|y|\varphi(x,y),
the computation is exactly the same.
The only difference is that at the end one has the estimates
|D_{1}^{\alpha}D_{2}^{\beta}F(x,y)|\lesssim|x|^{1-|\alpha|}
and
|D_{1}^{\alpha}D_{2}^{\beta}F(x,y)|\lesssim|y|^{1-|\beta|}
respectively.
Thus, in these two cases it would be enough to take
\delta<1+1/(n-1)
and
\delta<1+1/(m-1)
accordingly.
∎

The last ingredient to prove Theorem
4.1
is the following lemma, which gives a local lower bound in terms of reducing operators.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
W
be a
d\times d
-matrix valued weight and assume that
R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}
are bounded in
L^{p}(W)
for
1\leq j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq m.

If
R
is a rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, then

|\mathcal{W}_{R}^{\prime}\mathcal{W}_{R}|\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}}.

Proof.

Write
R=I\times J
and let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
be the side-lengths of
I
and
J
respectively.
Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm c}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})
be such that
\varphi(x,y)=1
if
(x,y)\in[-2,2]^{n+m}.

Fix
1\leq j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq m.

Note that if both
n
and
m
are odd, then
x_{j}y_{k}|x|^{n-1}|y|^{m-1}\varphi^{2}(x,y)\in C^{\infty}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm c}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})\subseteq W_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}).

On the other hand, if both
n
and
m
are even, then
x_{j}y_{k}|x|^{n-2}|y|^{m-2}\varphi(x,y)\in C^{\infty}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm c}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})\subseteq W_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}),
so that Lemma
4.2
implies that
x_{j}y_{k}|x|^{n-1}|y|^{m-1}\varphi^{2}(x,y)\in W_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}).

The case in which
n
is even and
m
is odd (and vice versa) is also treated with Lemma
4.2
to get that
x_{j}y_{k}|x|^{n-1}|y|^{m-1}\varphi^{2}(x,y)\in W_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}).

Next, observe that we can rewrite the averaging operator kernel as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{1}_{R\times R}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))}{|R|}

\displaystyle\sim\varepsilon_{1}^{-n}\varepsilon_{2}^{-m}\varphi\left(\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{1}},\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{R\times R}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j,k}\frac{x_{j}-x_{j}^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\frac{y_{k}-y_{k}^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\left|\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right|^{n-1}\left|\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\right|^{m-1}K_{jk}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle\times\varphi^{2}\left(\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{1}},\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{R\times R}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})),

so that each of the terms in this sum satisfies (
4.3
).
Hence, for any compactly supported
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}(W)
and
g\in L^{2}\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(W^{-p^{\prime}/p}),
the averaging operator satisfies

\left|(A_{R}f,g)\right|\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}}\|f\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)}}\|g\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p^{\prime}}(W^{-p^{\prime}/p})}}.

Using a standard density argument, it follows that

|\mathcal{W}_{R}^{\prime}\mathcal{W}_{R}|\sim\|A_{R}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}}\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}},

where in the
\sim
we applied
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Assume that the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is finite, since otherwise it is trivial.
For a matrix valued weight
W
and a rectangle
R\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},
let us denote

[W]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p},R}}\coloneq\strokedint_{R}\left(\strokedint_{R}|W(x)^{1/p}W(y)^{-1/p}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x,

and for a matrix valued function
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
let us denote

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{bmo}}^{1}(U,V,p),R}}\coloneq\left(\strokedint_{R}\left(\strokedint_{R}|V(x)^{1/p}(B(x)-B(y))U(y)^{-1/p}|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}.

Define the
2d\times 2d
-matrix valued function

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}V^{1/p}&V^{1/p}B\\
0&U^{1/p}\end{pmatrix},

that has a.e. inverse

\Phi^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}V^{-1/p}&-BU^{-1/p}\\
0&U^{-1/p}\end{pmatrix}

and let
W^{1/p}=(\Phi^{\ast}\Phi)^{1/2}.

By the polar decomposition we have that
\Phi=\mathcal{U}W^{1/p},
where
\mathcal{U}
is an a.e. unitary matrix.
Note that a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\strokedint_{R}\bigg(\strokedint_{R}

\displaystyle\|W^{1/p}(x)W^{-1/p}(y)\|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{p/p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\sim[V]_{A_{p},R}+[U]_{A_{p},R}+\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{bmo}}^{1}(U,V,p),R}}^{p}.

Now, use that for a linear operator
T
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued functions it happens that

\Phi(T\otimes I_{2})\Phi^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}V^{1/p}(T\otimes I_{2})V^{-1/p}&V^{1/p}[B,T]U^{-1/p}\\
0&U^{1/p}(T\otimes I_{2})U^{-1/p}\end{pmatrix},

so that by Lemma
4.5
we obtain

\displaystyle\big([V]_{A_{p},R}+[U]_{A_{p},R}

\displaystyle+\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{bmo}}^{1}(U,V,p),R}}^{p}\big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\sim[W]_{A_{p},R}^{1/p}\sim|\mathcal{W}_{R}^{\prime}\mathcal{W}_{R}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{{}_{\scriptstyle R}}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|W^{1/p}R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}W^{-1/p}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}}

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|\Phi R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\Phi^{-1}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\big(\|[B,R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}}

\displaystyle+\|R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(U)}}+\|R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(V)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}}\big).

Since both
U
and
V
are biparameter
A_{p}
weights, all quantities above are finite.
Thus, rescaling
B\mapsto rB,
dividing by
r
and letting
r\to\infty
we get

\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\text{bmo}}^{1}(U,V,p),R}}\lesssim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\|[B,R^{1}_{j}R^{2}_{k}]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}}.

Finally, taking the supremum over all rectangles and using Corollary
3.7
, the result follows.
∎

5.
Matrix-weighted little BMO upper bounds

In this section, we briefly explain how one can get upper bounds in terms of matrix-weighted little BMO norms for commutators with (a wide classes of) Journé operators. The main tool we need is the following lemma. It is the natural biparameter counterpart of
[
24
, Lemma 1.3]
due to Isralowitz–Pott–Treil. Since the proof in the present two-parameter setting is exactly the same as in the one-parameter setting of
[
24
]
(up to replacing the word “cube” with the word “rectangle”), we omit it and instead refer the reader to
[
24
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
T
be a linear operator acting on scalar-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
1<p<\infty
. Assume that there exists an increasing function
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
(possibly depending on
T,n,m,p,d
) such that for all biparameter
(2d)\times(2d)
-matrix valued
A_{p}
weights
W
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the canonical vector-valued extension of
T
(denoted again by
T
) satisfies

\|T\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(W)\rightarrow L^{p}(W)}}\leq\phi([W]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}).

Then, for all biparameter
d\times d
-matrix valued
A_{p}
weights
U,V
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and for all
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
there holds

\|[T,B]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}}\leq\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}^{1}(U,V,p)}}\phi(3^{p-1}([U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}+[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}+1)).

Using Lemma
5.1
coupled with Corollary
3.7
and already known weighted estimates from
[
31
]
, we can deduce the main result.

Theorem
1.3
.

Let
T
be any Journé operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
1<p<\infty
, let
U,V
be biparameter
d\times d
-matrix valued
A_{p}
weights on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and let
B\in L^{1}_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m};M_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
. Then

\|[T,B]\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L^{p}(U)\rightarrow L^{p}(V)}}\lesssim\|B\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle\emph{bmo}(U,V,p)}},

where the implied constant depends only on
T,n,m,p,d,[U]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
and
[V]_{{}_{\scriptstyle A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}}
.

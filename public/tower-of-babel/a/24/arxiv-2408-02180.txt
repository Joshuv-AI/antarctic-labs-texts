1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The spherical maximal operator of (complex) order
\alpha
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
was introduced in 1976 by Stein
[
24
]
, which is defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}_{t}f(x)|,

where
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}_{t}
is the spherical operator given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}_{t}f(x)={1\over\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\{|y|\leq 1\}}\left(1-{|y|^{2}}\right)^{\alpha-1}f(x-ty)\,\mathrm{d}y.

In the case
\alpha=1
,
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}
corresponds to the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and in the case
\alpha=0
, one recovers the spherical maximal operator
{\mathfrak{M}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|{\mathfrak{M}}_{t}f(x)|
in which

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{M}}_{t}f(x)={1\over\omega_{n-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f(x-t\omega)\,\text{d}\omega,\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t>0,

where
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
denotes the standard unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
\omega_{n-1}
is the surface area of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\text{d}\omega
is the induced Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}.
The following brief summary gives an overview of the progress
so far in this direction of research.

(i)  In
[
24
, Theorem 2]
, Stein showed that for
n\geq 2

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

provided that

\alpha>1-n+n/p
when
1<p\leq 2;

or

\alpha>(2-n)/p
when
2\leq p\leq\infty.

The above maximal theorem tells us that when
\alpha=0
and
n\geq 3
, the operator
{\mathfrak{M}}
is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for
p>n/(n-1)
.
This range of
p
is sharp. As is pointed out in
[
24
,
25
]
, no such result can hold for
p\leq n/(n-1)
if
n\geq 2
.

(ii)  In
[
2
]
, Bourgain proved that when
\alpha=0
and
n=2,
the operator
{\mathfrak{M}}
is bounded on
L^{p}{(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
for
p>2
. Bourgain’s theorem indicates that there exists
\varepsilon(p)>0
such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{2}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{2}})},\ \ \ \mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>-\varepsilon(p),\ \ \ 2<p<\infty.

This result cannot hold even for
\alpha=0
when
p=2
, see
[
24
,
25
]
.
Subsequently Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge
[
16
]
gave an alternative proof of Bourgain’s result by using local smoothing estimates for the wave operator in
2+1
dimensions.

(iii)  Using the Bourgain-Demeter decoupling theorem
[
3
]
, Miao, Yang and Zheng
[
15
]
extended certain range of
\alpha
in Stein’s result
[
24
, Theorem 2]
by showing that for
n\geq 2
and
p>2
, (
1.4
) holds
whenever

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>\max\left\{{(1-n)/4}+{(3-n)/(2p)},\,{(1-n)/p}\right\}.

(iv)  Recently, Liu, Song, and the second and fourth named authors
[
14
]
obtained the necessary condition on
(\alpha,p)
for the operator
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}
to be bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by showing that for
n\geq 2
and
p>2
(
1.4
)
holds only if

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha\geq\max\{1/p-(n-1)/2,\ -(n-1)/p\}.

In the two dimensional case
n=2
, they showed that (
1.4
) holds whenever
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>\max\{1/p-1/2,\ -1/p\}
by applying the work of Guth-Wang-Zhang
[
8
]
on local smoothing estimates for the wave operator in
2+1
dimensions.

Note that in
[
17
]
, Nowak, Roncal and Szarek
[
17
]
found
sharp conditions
for the operator
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}
on radial functions to be bounded on

L^{p}_{\rm rad}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. They
proved that for
n\geq 2
and
\alpha>(1-n)/2
,
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{\rm rad}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
if and only if

\alpha>1-n+n/p
if
1<p\leq 2
;
\alpha>1/p+(1-n)/2
if
2\leq p\leq 2n/(n-1)
; and
\alpha\geq(1-n)/p
if
p>2n/(n-1)
.

In the last decades the spherical maximal operators
have attracted a lot of attention and have been studied extensively by many authors.
The maximal theorem for spherical operator has some applications; for example, Stein
[
24
]
used it to derive a Fatou’s theorem for wave equation.
The spherical operator can be extended to a more general hypersurface in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, see
[
2
,
7
,
10
,
12
,
16
,
20
,
21
,

,

,
25
,

]
for the references therein.

1.2.
Main results

The purpose of this article is to investigate
the spherical maximal operators of (complex) order
\alpha

n

\mathbb{H}^{n}

[z,w]=z_{0}w_{0}-z_{1}w_{1}-\cdots-z_{n}w_{n},\ \ \ \ z=(z_{0},\dots,z_{n}),w=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}.

The hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{z=(z_{0},z^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}:[z]:=[z,z]=1,\ z_{0}>0\},

and we equip this space with the Riemannian metric induced by the above Minkowski metric
[\cdot,\cdot]
. This Riemannian metric induces in turn a measure, by which we denote
\mathrm{d}z
. Define the spherical maximal operators by

(1.6)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}^{\alpha}(f)(z)=\sup_{t>0}|M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)(z)|,

where

(1.7)

\displaystyle M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)(z)=2\mathrm{e}^{t}\left({\mathrm{e}^{t}-1\over\sinh t}\right)^{n-2}{1\over(\mathrm{e}^{t}-1)^{2\alpha+n-2}}{1\over\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{B_{t}(z)}[\mathrm{e}^{t}z-w]^{\alpha-1}f(w)\,\mathrm{d}w,

B_{t}(z)
stands for the geodesic ball in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
centered at
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
with radius

and
f
is in the Schwartz space on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. These operators
\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}
and

are initially defined for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>0,
but the definition can be extended to all complex
\alpha
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>(1-n)/2
by analytic continuation, see Lemma
2.3
below. For
\alpha=0,
one recovers the spherical maximal operator

(1.8)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}(f)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|f\ast\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t}(z)|,

where
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t}
is the normalized spherical measure defined on
[
10

\alpha

2.
Fourier analysis on the hyperbolic space

In this section we recall some basic facts about the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as well as its Fourier theory (see for examples,
[
9
,
11
,
18
]
).
The Minkowski metric on
{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}
is given by

[z,w]=z_{0}w_{0}-z_{1}w_{1}-\cdots-z_{n}w_{n},\ \ \ \ \ z=(z_{0},\dots,z_{n}),\ w=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}.

We define
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as the hyperboloid

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{z=(z_{0},z^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}:[z,z]=1,\ z_{0}>0\}

and equip this space with the Riemannian metric induced by the Minkowski metric
[\cdot,\cdot]
. This Riemannian metric induces in turn a measure, by which we denote
\mathrm{d}z
. We will further distinguish the point
{\bf 0}=(1,0,\dots,0)
.

The Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is denoted by
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
. It has spectrum
(-\infty,-(n-1)^{2}/4]
. For
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, let

b(\omega)=(1,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

The analog of plane waves is provided by

h_{\lambda,\omega}(z)=[z,b(\omega)]^{i\lambda-(n-1)/2},\ \ \ z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}

(notice that
[z,b(\omega)]>0
for
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
). They satisfy

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}h_{\lambda,\omega}(z)=-\left(\lambda^{2}+{(n-1)^{2}\over 4}\right)\,h_{\lambda,\omega}(z).

The Helgason Fourier transform on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined as

(2.1)

{\mathscr{F}}(f)(\lambda,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,h_{\lambda,\omega}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,\ \ \ \ (\lambda,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},

while the inverse Fourier transform is then given by

(2.2)

f(z)={1\over\omega_{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{\mathscr{F}}(f)(\lambda,\omega)\,{\overline{h_{\lambda,\omega}(z)}}\,|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{d}\omega,

for the Harish-Chandra function

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)={2^{n-2}\Gamma(n/2)\over\sqrt{\uppi}}{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda)\over\Gamma((n-1)/2+\mathrm{i}\lambda)}.

This time the following Plancherel’s theorem holds: the Fourier transform is an isometry from
L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathrm{d}z)
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}_{+}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}\mathrm{d}\lambda{\mathrm{d}\omega\over\omega_{n-1}})
.

We need the estimates for the Harish-Chandra function
{\mathcal{H}}
, which will be frequently used throughout the paper.
For its proof, we refer the reader to
[
10
, Proposition A1(a)(b)]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
The Harish-Chandra function
{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)
satisfies

|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}=({\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)\,{\mathcal{H}}(-\lambda))^{-1}.

The function
\lambda^{-1}({\mathcal{H}}(-\lambda))^{-1}
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\left|{\mathrm{d}^{k}\over\mathrm{d}\lambda^{k}}(\lambda^{-1}({\mathcal{H}}(\lambda))^{-1})\right|\leq C_{k}\,(1+|\lambda|)^{(n-1)/2-1-k}.

In analogy to
{\mathcal{H}}
, we define another function

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)={2^{n-2}\Gamma(n/2)\over\sqrt{\uppi}}{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda)\over\Gamma((n-1)/2+\alpha+\mathrm{i}\lambda)},\ \ \ {\rm Re}\,\alpha>{(1-n)/2}.

Specifically, when
\alpha=0
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)
is exactly the Harish-Chandra function
{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)
defined in (
2.3
). For the function
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
we have the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\rm Re}\,\alpha>{(1-n)/2}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the function
\lambda\,{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\left|{\mathrm{d}^{k}\over\mathrm{d}\lambda^{k}}(\lambda\,{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda))\right|\leq C_{\alpha,k}\,(1+|\lambda|)^{1-(n-1)/2-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha-k}.

Proof.

This is a consequence of Stirling’s formula (
[
27
, p. 151, Example. (ii)]
).
∎

In the space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we can adopt polar coordinates

\displaystyle z=(\cosh r,\omega\sinh r),\ \ \ \ \ \ (r,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},

where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the unit sphere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The canonical Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is then given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\sinh^{2}r\,\mathrm{d}s_{n-1}^{2},

where
r\geq 0
is the geodesic distance to the orginal
{\bf 0}
and
\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{n-1}
is the canonical Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. In these coordinates, the volume element becomes

\mathrm{d}z=(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\omega.

The spherical function on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(z)={1\over\omega_{n-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}h_{\lambda,\omega}(z)\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

It depends only on the distance
r
of
z
to the origin
\mathbf{0}
and can be written as

(2.5)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(r)={\Gamma(n/2)\over\sqrt{\uppi}\,\Gamma((n-1)/2)\,}\int_{0}^{\uppi}(\cosh r-\cos s\,\cosh r)^{-(n-1)/2+\mathrm{i}\lambda}(\sin s)^{n-2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

See
[
28
]
.

Since radial functions on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
depend on
r
only, the Helgason Fourier transform (
2.1
) of such function
f
can be written as

{\mathscr{F}}(f)(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=\omega_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(r)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}r

and the inverse Fourier transform (
2.2
) is given by

f(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathscr{F}}(f)(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

If
K
is radial, then the convolution of
f
and
K
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
can be written as

f\ast K(z)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(w)\,K(d(z,w))\,\mathrm{d}w,

where
d(z,w)=\mathop{\mathrm{arcosh}}([z,w])
denotes the geodesic distance between
z
and
w
.

In the following, we denote
D=\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}-(n-1)^{2}/4}
.
For any bounded Borel function
m:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we define the
radial Fourier multipliers
m(D)
by the formula

(2.6)

\displaystyle m(D)(f)(z)={\mathscr{F}}^{-1}[m(\lambda)\,{\mathscr{F}}(f)(\lambda,\omega)](z).

See
[
6
, p. 10]
. It follows from (
2.6
) that the
radial Fourier multipliers
m(D)
can be rewritten in the following:

\displaystyle m(D)(f)(z)=f*K(z)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(w)\,K(d(z,w))\,\mathrm{d}w,

where

\displaystyle K(r)=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(m)(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}m(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

As mentioned in Section
1
, the operator
M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)
in (
1.7
) is initially defined for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>0,
but the definition can be extended to all complex
\alpha
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>(1-n)/2
by analytic continuation.
To explain this, we shall use the Legendre function. Recall that
in view of
[
4
, p. 155, (1)]
, for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(-\mu)\geq\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nu>-1
and
\zeta\notin[-1,\infty)
the Legendre function of order
\mu
and degree
\nu
has the following integral representation:

\displaystyle P^{\mu}_{\nu}(\zeta)={2^{-\nu}(\zeta^{2}-1)^{-\mu/2}\over\Gamma(-\mu-\nu)\,\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\zeta+\cosh s)^{\mu-\nu-1}(\sinh s)^{2\nu+1}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then we have the following result.

Lemma 2.3
.

The operator
M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)
in (
1.7
) can be extended to

\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>(1-n)/2
via analytic continuation such that

\displaystyle M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)(z)=m^{\alpha}_{t}(D)(f)(z),

where

(2.7)

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}_{t}(\lambda)=2^{(n-2)/2+\alpha}\Gamma\!\left({n\over 2}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha t}(\mathrm{e}^{t}-1)^{-2\alpha}(\sinh t)^{\alpha-(n-2)/2}P^{-\alpha-(n-2)/2}_{-1/2+\mathrm{i}\lambda}(\cosh t).

Proof.

In
[
13
, Theorem 1]
, El Kohen showed that for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>(1-n)/2
one can write

\displaystyle M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)(z)=m^{\alpha}_{t}(D)(f)(z)

via analytic continuation, where

(2.8)

m^{\alpha}_{t}(\lambda)=2^{{n-2\over 2}+\alpha}\Gamma\!\left({n\over 2}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha t}(\mathrm{e}^{t}-1)^{-2\alpha}(\sinh t)^{-(n-2)}I^{\alpha+{n-2\over 2}}P^{0}_{-{1\over 2}+\mathrm{i}\lambda}(\cosh t)

and
I^{\gamma}
is the fractional integral operator given by

\displaystyle I^{\gamma}(f)(s)={1\over\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{1}^{s}f(t)\,(s-t)^{\gamma-1}\,\mathrm{d}t.

In view of
[
4
, (5) on p. 159]
, we have

\displaystyle I^{\alpha+(n-2)/2}P^{0}_{-1/2+\mathrm{i}\lambda}(\cosh t)=(\sinh t)^{\alpha+(n-2)/2}P^{-\alpha-(n-2)/2}_{-1/2+\mathrm{i}\lambda}(\cosh t).

Substituting this back into (
2.8

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t>10}|M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{p_{n}}

Assume

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}_{T}(f)(z)=\sup_{t\in[T,T+1]}|M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)(z)|.

It follows from (iii) of Lemma

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}_{t}(\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)t/2}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda t}{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)\,a^{\alpha}_{2}(\lambda,t)+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda t}{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(-\lambda)\,a^{\alpha}_{2}(-\lambda,t)\right),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)(z)=\sup_{t\in[T,T+1]}|f*K^{\alpha}_{t,j}(z)|\leq{\mathfrak{A}}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)(z)+\mathfrak{E}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)(z),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{A}}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)(z)

(4.14)

Lemma 4.8

The following lemma is the essential step in proving Proposition

Lemma 4.9

Let

For

,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha,\delta}2^{-(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha+(n-1)/p_{n}-\delta)j}\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)(1-1/p_{n})T}\|f\|_{p_{n}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6

(4.21)

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{A}}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha,\delta}2^{-\varepsilon j}\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)(1-1/p_{n})T}\|f\|_{p_{n}}

We now prove (

Roughly speaking, the favorable factor

We start by localizing the operator

with supports inside balls of radius

\displaystyle\|\psi_{0}\,{\mathfrak{A}}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(\psi_{1}f)\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha,\delta}2^{-\varepsilon j}\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)T/p_{n}}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\psi_{1}}f\|_{p_{n}}.

To see that (
4.22
) implies the desired estimate (
4.21

\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in K}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with each

are mutually disjoint. By

B_{k}

times as
B_{k}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k\in K}\mathbbm{1}_{2B_{k}}\right\|_{\infty}<\infty.

For any

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in K}\#L_{k}\leq C{\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)T}\over c_{0}^{n}}.

Recall also that the Schwartz kernels

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{A}}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)\|^{p_{n}}_{p_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left|\sum_{k\in K}\eta_{k}(z)\,{\mathfrak{A}}^{\alpha}_{T,j}\!\left(\sum_{l\in L_{k}}\eta_{l}f\right)\!(z)\right|^{p_{n}}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle C\,(\#L_{k})^{p_{n}/p_{n}^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{l\in L_{k}}\|\eta_{k}{\mathfrak{A}}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(\eta_{l}f)\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}}

\displaystyle C_{c_{0},\alpha,\delta}2^{-\varepsilon p_{n}j}\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)T}\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)p_{n}T}\|f\|_{p_{n}}^{p_{n}},

which proves (
4.21
). Here we use (
4.23
) in the second and the last inequalities, and the penultimate inequality follows from (
4.22
). Hence to prove (
4.21
) it suffices to show (
4.22

Let us now prove (
4.22
). To do this, we define another cutoff function
\widetilde{\phi}_{s}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
by
\widetilde{\phi}_{s}\equiv\phi_{s}
on
[s,\infty)
and
\widetilde{\phi}_{s}\equiv 0
on
(-\infty,s-1]
. Note that in view of (

(4.24)

\mathscr{A}^{\alpha}_{t,j}(r)=\widetilde{\phi}_{T}(r)\,\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)(r+t)/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\beta_{j}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(t-r)}{{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)\over{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)}a_{2}^{\alpha}(\lambda,t)\,a_{2}^{0}(-\lambda,r)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\mathfrak{R}^{\alpha}_{t,j}(r),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}^{\alpha}_{t,j}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)(r+t)/2}\left((\phi_{T}(r)-\widetilde{\phi}_{T}(r)-\phi_{0}(r))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\beta_{j}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(t-r)}{{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)\over{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)}a_{2}^{\alpha}(\lambda,t)\,a_{2}^{0}(-\lambda,r)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\right.

\displaystyle+\left.(\phi_{T}(r)-\phi_{0}(r))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\beta_{j}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(t+r)}{{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)\over{\mathcal{H}}(-\lambda)}a_{2}^{\alpha}(\lambda,t)\,a_{2}^{0}(\lambda,r)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\right).

2.1
,
2.2
and

(iii) and integration by parts give that

which indicates that, in view of (

-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha
. Hence by (

4.3
, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{t\in[T,T+1]}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(v^{\prime},u^{\prime}-T_{0})\,\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}^{\alpha}_{t,j}((v,u),(v^{\prime},u^{\prime}-T_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\right|^{p_{n}}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{\alpha,\delta}2^{-\varepsilon p_{n}j}\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)p_{n}T}\int_{\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\psi_{1}}|f(v^{\prime},u^{\prime}-T_{0})|^{p_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathrm{d}u^{\prime},

where the term
\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)p_{n}T}
comes from the exponential decay in (
4.25
). Note that under the Iwasawa coordinates, the measure induced by the Riemannian metric reads
\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)u}\,\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}u
. So

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t\in[T,T+1]}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(w)\,\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}^{\alpha}_{t,j}(\cdot,w)\,\mathrm{d}w\right|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha,\delta}2^{-\varepsilon j}\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)T/p_{n}}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\psi_{1}}f\|_{p_{n}},

which is (
4.27
).
∎

Now we apply Lemma
4.7
, Lemma
4.8
and Lemma
4.9
to prove Proposition
4.5
.

Proof of Proposition
4.5
.

Let
\delta
be as in Lemma
4.9
. By Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
and complex interpolation, we have

(4.31)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}2^{-(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha+(n-2)/p_{n}-\delta)j}\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)T/p_{n}}(1+T)^{2/p_{n}}\|f\|_{p_{n}}.

In order to sum over
j
, we choose
j_{0}
with
2^{j_{0}}=\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)p_{n}}(1+T)^{-2}
such that the two norms in (
4.20
) and (
4.31
) are equal. The result is

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}_{T}(f)\|_{p_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)\|_{p_{n}}+\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}_{T,j}(f)\|_{p_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,\delta}\mathrm{e}^{[-(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha+(n-1)/p_{n}-\delta)(n-1)p_{n}+(n-1)(1-1/p_{n})]T}(1+T)^{-2(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha+(n-1)/p_{n}-\delta)}\|f\|_{p_{n}},

which proves Proposition
4.5
.
∎

Next we turn to handle the “local” part of the operator
\mathfrak{m}^{\alpha}
by proving the following result.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
p_{n}
be as in (
1.14
).
If
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>(1-n)/p_{n}
, then

(4.32)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 10}|M^{\alpha}_{t}(f)|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{p_{n}}.

Proof.

Consider a partition of unity
\{\varkappa_{k}\}_{k\in\widetilde{K}}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with each
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\varkappa_{k}
contained in a ball
B_{k}
of radius
10
, where the balls
B_{k}
satisfy that
(1/5)B_{k}
are mutually disjoint. Let
\varsigma_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
such that
\varsigma_{k}\equiv 1
on
2B_{k}
and
\varsigma_{k}\equiv 0
outside
3B_{k}
. By (
1.7
) we have that
M^{\alpha}_{t}(\varkappa_{k}f)=\varsigma_{k}M^{\alpha}_{t}(\varkappa_{k}f)
for
0<t\leq 10
.

To prove Proposition
4.10
, it is enough to show that for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>(1-n)/p_{n}
there holds

(4.33)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t\in[1,10]}|\psi_{0}\,M^{\alpha}_{t}(\psi_{1}f)|\right\|_{p_{n}}+\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 1}|\psi_{0}\,M^{\alpha}_{t}(\psi_{1}f)|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\psi_{1}}f\|_{p_{n}}

where
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
have sufficiently small supports.

Let us prove (
4.33
) for the case
1\leq t\leq 10
. Since the convolution kernel
K^{\alpha}_{t}
of
M^{\alpha}_{t}
satisfies
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}K^{\alpha}_{t}(r)\subset\{r:0\leq r\leq t\}
(see (
1.6
)), we write

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=\phi_{t}(r)\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{\alpha}_{t}(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=:\mathscr{B}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)+\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{t}(r),

where by Lemma
2.1
and the asymptotics of
m^{\alpha}_{t}
and
\varphi_{\lambda}
(see Lemma
2.4
(iii) and Remark
2.5
)

(4.34)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=(\phi_{t}(r)-\phi_{0}(r))\,\mathrm{e}^{-(n-1)(r+t)/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(t-r)}{{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)\over{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)}a^{\alpha}_{2}(\lambda,t)\,a^{0}_{2}(-\lambda,r)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=K^{\alpha}_{t}(r)-\mathscr{B}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)
and
\phi_{s}
is the cutoff function as above.

Observe that in terms of (
4.33
),
\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{t}

is an error term. To see this, by Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.4

together with
an argument in the proof of Lemma
4.6
, we have

(4.35)

\displaystyle|\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)|\leq C_{\alpha}(\phi_{10}(r)+\phi_{0}(r)\,r^{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha-(n+1)/2}),

which implies

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left\|\sup_{t\in[1,10]}|\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{t}(d(z,\cdot))|\right\|_{1}+\sup_{w\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left\|\sup_{t\in[1,10]}|\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{t}(d(\cdot,w))|\right\|_{1}\leq C_{\alpha}.

By Schur’s lemma, we obtain

(4.36)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t\in[1,10]}|\psi_{0}\,((\psi_{1}f)*\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{t})|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{p_{n}}.

Let us turn to the term
\mathscr{B}^{\alpha}_{t}
. In view of
the remark following
[
20
, Corollary 2.2]
, under local coordinates, for
1\leq t\leq 10
, if we set the function
\Phi
in (
4.4
) to be
d(z,w)
and the symbol
a
in (
4.4
) to be

(\phi_{t}(d(z,w))-\phi_{0}(d(z,w))){{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(\lambda)\over{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)}a^{\alpha}_{2}(\lambda,t)\,a^{0}_{2}(-\lambda,d(z,w)),

then the conditions on
\Phi
and
a
in Proposition
4.3
are fulfilled. Therefore we can invoke (
4.8
) in Proposition
4.3
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t\in[1,10]}|\psi_{0}\,((\psi_{1}f)*\mathscr{B}^{\alpha}_{t})|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\psi_{1}}f\|_{p_{n}}\ \ \ \ \mbox{for }\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>{1-n\over p_{n}}.

This, combined with (
4.36
), concludes the proof of (
4.33
) for the case
1\leq t\leq 10
.

Now we consider (
4.33
) for the case
0<t\leq 1
. Let
\gamma
be the cutoff function in the proof of Lemma
4.6
. We define another cutoff function
\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\widetilde{\gamma}\equiv 1
on
[1/2,\infty)
and
\widetilde{\gamma}\equiv 0
on
[0,1/4]
. Let
K^{\alpha}_{t}(r)
be the convolution kernel of
M^{\alpha}_{t}
. Note that in view of (
1.7
),
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}K^{\alpha}_{t}(r)\subset\{r:0\leq r\leq t\}
. So we can write

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=\gamma\!\left({r\over t}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{\alpha}_{t}(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)+\mathscr{W}^{\alpha}_{t}(r),

where by the asymptotics of
m^{\alpha}_{t}
and
\varphi_{\lambda}
(see Lemma
2.4
(ii) and Remark
2.5
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=\widetilde{\gamma}\!\left({r\over t}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(t-r)}a^{\alpha,N}_{1}(\lambda,t)\,a^{0,N}_{1}(-\lambda,r)\,(1-\gamma(\lambda r))\,|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

and
\mathscr{W}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=K^{\alpha}_{t}(r)-\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)
.

Observe that in terms of (
4.33
),
\mathscr{W}^{\alpha}_{t}
is an error term. To see this, by Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.4
, an argument in the proof of Lemma
4.6
shows that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{W}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)|\leq C_{\alpha}t^{-n}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2t]}(r)\left(1+\left({t\over r}\right)^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha+(n+1)/2}\right),

and so

(4.37)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 1}|\psi_{0}\,((\psi_{1}f)*\mathscr{W}^{\alpha}_{t})|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{p_{n}}\ \ \ \mbox{for }\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>{1-n\over p_{n}}.

Now we claim that for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>{(1-n)/p_{n}}
,

(4.38)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 1}|\psi_{0}\,((\psi_{1}f)*\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t})|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{p_{n}}.

To prove (
4.38
), we observe that

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=\widetilde{\widetilde{\gamma}}\!\left({r\over t}\right)t^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(1-r/t)}|\lambda|^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha}\widetilde{a}^{\alpha}_{1}(\lambda,t)\,\widetilde{a}^{0}_{1}\!\left(\lambda{r\over t},r\right)\left(1-\gamma\!\left(\lambda{r\over t}\right)\right)\mathfrak{w}\!\left({\lambda\over t}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where
\widetilde{\widetilde{\gamma}}(s)=s\,\widetilde{\gamma}(s)
,

\mathfrak{w}(\lambda)=|\lambda|^{-(n-1)}|{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)|^{-2}
and
\widetilde{a}^{\alpha}_{1}(\lambda,t)=|\lambda|^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha-(n-1)/2}a^{\alpha,N}_{1}(\lambda/t,t)
. By Lemma
2.2
and (
2.13
), we see that
\mathfrak{w}(\lambda),\widetilde{a}^{\alpha}_{1}(\lambda,t)\in S^{0}_{\lambda}
.
Without losing any generality, we assume the supports of
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
in (
4.38
) are in some neighborhoods of
\mathbf{0}
. Here we adopt the hyperboloid coordinates. Let

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\tau:&\mathbb{R}^{n}&\to&\mathbb{H}^{n},\\
&x&\mapsto&(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},x).\end{array}

Under this coordinates, the geodesic distance reads

\displaystyle d(\tau(x),\tau(y))=\mathop{\mathrm{arcosh}}(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1+|y|^{2}}-x\cdot y).

We set
\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y)=t^{-1}d(t\,\tau(x),t\,\tau(y))
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}^{\alpha}_{t}(r)=t^{n}\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t}(tr)=\widetilde{\widetilde{\gamma}}(r)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(1-r)}|\lambda|^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha}\widetilde{a}^{\alpha}_{1}(\lambda,t)\,\widetilde{a}^{0}_{1}(\lambda r,tr)\,(1-\gamma(\lambda r))\,\mathfrak{w}\!\left({\lambda\over t}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Then (
4.38
) is equivalent to

(4.40)

\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\left|\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(\cdot)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(y)\,g(y)\,\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}^{\alpha}_{t}(\widetilde{d}^{t}(\cdot,y))\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\right\|_{L^{p_{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\alpha}\|g\|_{L^{p_{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha>{(1-n)/p_{n}}.

To prove (
4.40
), we set

\displaystyle b(\lambda,t,x,y)=\widetilde{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y))\,(1-\gamma(\lambda\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y)))\,|\lambda|^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha}\widetilde{a}^{\alpha}_{1}(\lambda,t)\,\widetilde{a}^{0}_{1}(\lambda\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y),t\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y))\,\mathfrak{w}\!\left({\lambda\over t}\right).

In view of
[
21
, Proposition 6.2.4]
, modulo a
C^{\infty}
error, for
0<t\leq 1
,
\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t}(\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y))
can be written as the sum of finite
terms, each of which is of the form

(4.41)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y))\,\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}_{0}(x)\,\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}_{1}(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\widetilde{\varphi}(x,t,\xi)-y\cdot\xi)}\widetilde{b}(x,t,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where

\displaystyle|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}_{x}\partial^{\gamma_{2}}_{\xi}\widetilde{b}(x,t,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(1+|\xi|)^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha-(n-1)/2-|\gamma_{2}|}\qquad\mbox{for }0<t\leq 2^{-j_{0}},

\displaystyle|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}_{x}\partial^{k}_{t}\partial^{\gamma_{2}}_{\xi}\widetilde{b}(x,t,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(1+|\xi|)^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha-(n-1)/2-|\gamma_{2}|}\qquad\mbox{for }t\in[2^{-j_{0}},1]

for an integer
j_{0}

to be
chosen later, and
\widetilde{\varphi}
is homogeneous of degree
1
on
\xi
satisfying
\det({\partial^{2}\widetilde{\varphi}/\partial x\partial\xi})\neq 0
. If we set
T_{t}\ (0<t\leq 1)
to be the operator given by integration against the Schwartz kernel (
4.41
), then by
[
1
, Proposition 3.2]
and
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
,

\displaystyle\|T_{t}(g)(x)\|_{L^{p_{n}}_{x,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[2^{-j_{0}},1])}\leq C_{\alpha}\|g\|_{W^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha-(n-1)/p_{n}-s,p_{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\mbox{for any }s<{1\over p_{n}}.

We then use an argument in
the proof of

[
21
, Theorem 6.3.1]
to obtain

\|\sup_{t\in[2^{-j_{0}},1]}|T_{t}(g)|\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|g\|_{p_{n}}.

Hence, the proof of (
4.40
) reduces to
showing

(4.42)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 2^{-j_{0}}}|T_{t}(g)|\right\|_{p_{n}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|g\|_{p_{n}}.

For this, we need to consider
\mathscr{P}^{\alpha}_{t}(\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y))
when
t\to 0
. Note that
\widetilde{d}^{t}(x,y)\to|x-y|
as

uniformly in
x,y
belonging to a fixed compact set. We also have that
\mathfrak{w}(\lambda)\to|C_{n}^{\prime}|^{-2}
as

, with
C_{n}^{\prime}=2^{n-2}\Gamma(n/2)/\sqrt{\uppi}
being the constant in (
2.3
). It tells us that
\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{0}(\widetilde{d}^{0}(x,y))
is of the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{\widetilde{\gamma}}(|x-y|)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(1-|x-y|)}|\lambda|^{-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\alpha}\widetilde{a}^{\alpha}_{1}(\lambda,0)\,\widetilde{a}^{0}_{1}(\lambda|x-y|,0)\,(1-\gamma(\lambda|x-y|))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

This, together with
[
21
, Proposition 6.2.4]
and the asymptotics of the Fourier transform of the spherical measure on

derived using
[

, Chapter VIII, (15) and (25)]
, indicates that
\widetilde{\varphi}
in (
4.41
) satisfies
\widetilde{\varphi}(x,0,\xi)=x\cdot\xi\pm|\xi|
. Choosing

sufficiently large and noting that

is homogeneous of degree

, we use Taylor’s formula to derive that the phase function in (
4.41
) satisfies

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}(x,t,\xi)=x\cdot\xi\pm|\xi|+O(t|\xi|)\qquad\mbox{for }0<t\leq 2^{-j_{0}}.

Then we rescale the integral representation of
T_{t}
into the form of the operator
\mathcal{F}_{t}
on
[
21
, p. 192]
. Using the argument showing
[
21
, Corollary 6.3.3]
, together with
[
1
, Proposition 3.2]
and
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
, we obtain (
4.42

4.40
) is valid. This proves (
4.38
) and finishes the proof of (
4.33
) for the case
0<t\leq 1
. Hence, the proof of Proposition
4.10
is concluded.
∎

Gödel-type spacetimes in f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity
A. M. Silva, M. J. Rebouças, N. A. Lemos

Abstract
There is ongoing interest in the nonmetricity formulation of gravity. The nonlinear extension of the theory, called f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity, has recently been proposed and offers a promising avenue for addressing some of the long-standing challenges in cosmology and fundamental physics. A number of solutions have already been found that have confirmed the usefulness of the theory for astrophysics and cosmology. Motivated by earlier work on Gödel-type spacetimes we investigate whether f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity admits such cosmological solutions, which entail causality violation. In the coincident gauge, which is shown to be a legitimate gauge because the connection field equations are satisfied, we find that Gödel-type metrics are allowed by this modified gravity theory for any function f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) . Noncausal and causal solutions exist depending on the matter content. For a perfect fluid, out of all Gödel-type metrics only Gödel’s original model is allowed in f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity, implying violation of causality. The requirement that energy conditions be obeyed by the fluid leads to mild restrictions on f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) . For a massless scalar field in the presence of a cosmological constant, there are causal solutions for any reasonable function f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) .

Introduction

General relativity is a well-established theory that has successfully passed many stringent experimental tests
[
2
]
. However, the application of Einstein’s theory of gravity to cosmology requires that the universe contain dark matter and be uniformly filled with dark energy, which together comprise around 95% of the universe’s material content but whose physical nature remains mysterious. Inflation is also an external ingredient that is added to the very early stages of the universe’s evolution in order to solve certain fine tuning problems

It is reasonable to assume that, rather than adding exotic components to the matter content, the challenges of the standard cosmological model can be met through some modification of general relativity that still withstands all solar system and astrophysical tests. The fact that the canonical quantization of general relativity seems to give rise to a nonrenormalizable quantum field theory

Among other possibilities, general relativity can be equivalently formulated in terms of the nonmetricity tensor

However, it is not clear if a certain coordinate system chosen to express the metric is one of the privileged coordinate systems in which the connection vanishes. This raises the question of compatibility between the coincident gauge and a particular coordinate system in which one is working. The compatibility criterion we adopt is straightforward: for the coincident gauge to be compatible with the chosen coordinate system the trivial connection
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\mu\nu}=0

In this paper we look for possible Gödel-type solutions and the associated causality violation in
f(Q)

II
A brief overview of
f(Q)
gravity

One approach to reasonable modifications of Einstein’s theory of gravity involves considering different geometrical objects in the gravitational action, such as the torsion scalar
T
in the theory of teleparallelism or the nonmetricity scalar
Q
in the theory of coincident general relativity
[
10
,
24
]
. A unifying framework to describe these modifications of general relativity was introduced in
[
49
]
, where a triangle was considered with each vertex representing one of the possible fundamental geometrical objects in terms of which the theory can be formulated. This so-called geometric trinity summarizes three equivalent ways to describe general relativity.

Of particular interest to the present article is the nonlinear extension of the theory of coincident general relativity

[
10
,
24
]
, also known as symmetric teleparallel general relativity (STGR). Its fundamental object is the nonmetricity tensor
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
which is defined by

Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\alpha\mu}g_{\lambda\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\alpha\nu}g_{\mu\lambda}

(1)

and measures the variation of the norm of a vector field under parallel transport specified by the symmetric connection
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
. The assumption that
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
does not vanish makes room for a different geometric interpretation of gravitational interactions, focusing on the distortion of lengths rather than angles
[
15
]
. Since it is also postulated that the Riemann tensor as well as torsion are zero, the connection is symmetric and the gravitational effects are encoded in the nonmetricity tensor.

Nonlinear extensions of the previously mentioned theories have long been investigated, first in the form of
f(R)
gravity, which takes into account nonlinear terms in the Ricci scalar
R
, and next in the form of
f(T)
gravity which considers nonlinear terms in the torsion scalar
T
. The recently proposed theory of
f(Q)
gravity containing nonlinear terms in the nonmetricity has been drawing a lot of attention.
Similarly to what is done in
f(R)
and
f(T)
theories, upon introducing a general infinitely differentiable function
f(Q)
of the nonmetricity scalar one is led to an action of the form
[
24
]

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}f(Q)+S_{m},

(2)

where
8\pi G/c^{4}=1
and
S_{m}
is the matter action.
Varying this action with respect to the metric leads to the metric field equations
[
18
]

\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\alpha}\bigl(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\alpha}_{\,\,\,\,\mu\nu}\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}fg_{\mu\nu}+f_{Q}\bigl(P_{\nu\alpha\beta}Q_{\mu}^{\,\,\,\alpha\beta}-2P_{\alpha\beta\mu}Q^{\alpha\beta}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\nu}\bigr)=T_{\mu\nu}

(3)

where the subscript
Q
means derivative:
f_{Q}(Q)=f^{\prime}(Q)
.
Variation of the action (
2
) with respect to the connection yields the connection field equations
[
18
]

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\bigl(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\mu\nu}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\alpha}\bigr)=0,

(4)

which are dynamical equations for the connection.
In the above equations
P^{\alpha\mu\nu}
is the nonmetricity conjugate tensor defined as

P^{\alpha\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{4}Q^{\alpha\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{(\mu\nu)\alpha}+\frac{1}{4}(Q^{\alpha}-{\tilde{Q}}^{\alpha})g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g^{\alpha(\mu}Q^{\nu)}

(5)

where the parentheses surrounding the indices denote symmetrization, as for example in
A_{(\mu\nu)}=(A_{\mu\nu}+A_{\nu\mu})/2
.
In the preceding equation there also appear the independent traces of the nonmetricity tensor given by

Q_{\alpha}=g^{\sigma\lambda}Q_{\alpha\sigma\lambda},\qquad{\tilde{Q}}_{\alpha}=g^{\sigma\lambda}Q_{\sigma\alpha\lambda}.

(6)

Finally, the nonmetricity scalar
Q
is defined by

Q=-Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}P^{\alpha\mu\nu}.

(7)

An equivalent and more illuminating form of the metric field equations (
3
) is
[
20
,
21
]

f_{Q}{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\bigl(Qf_{Q}-f)+2f_{QQ}\bigl(\partial_{\lambda}Q\bigr)P^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\,\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}

(8)

where
{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein tensor associated with the Levi-Civita connection.
In this form, it becomes clear that if
f_{QQ}=0
then
f(Q)=AQ+B
and the theory is equivalent to general relativity (if
B=0
) or general relativity with a cosmological constant (if
B\neq 0
). Furthermore, if the nonmetricity scalar
Q
is constant the metric field equations (
8
) are also equivalent to those of general relativity with a cosmological constant, but in this case for any function
f(Q)
.

Solutions to equations (
8
) have been investigated as well as their implications to cosmology
[
18
,
19
,
50
]
. In particular, it has been found that
f(Q)
gravity can give rise to inflationary solutions that seem to be consistent with the current understanding of the physics of the early universe
[
19
]
.

III
Gödel-type metrics

The famous rotating universe model discovered by Gödel
[
25
]
is the best
known example of a solution to Einstein’s equations
with a physically well-motivated source that explicitly shows
that general relativity allows the existence of closed timelike worldlines, implying violation of causality in certain regions of spacetime. Gödel’s model
is a solution to the Einstein field equations with a cosmological constant and dust, but it can also be seen as a solution without cosmological constant with the matter content described by a perfect fluid with equation of state
p=\rho
. Because of its unforeseen properties, Gödel’s solution has motivated a large amount of research into
rotating Gödel-type models as well as on causal anomalies
both in general relativity

and modified gravity theories

]
.

Gödel’s solution to the general relativity field equations is a particular member of the broad family of geometries whose general form in cylindrical coordinates
(r,\phi,z)
is
[
28
]

ds^{2}=[dt+H(r)d\phi]^{2}-D^{2}(r)d\phi^{2}-dr^{2}-dz^{2}.

(9)

The necessary and sufficient conditions for this metric
to be spacetime homogeneous are
[
28
,
32
]

\frac{H^{\prime}}{D}=2\omega,\qquad\frac{D^{\prime\prime}}{D}=m^{2},

(10)

where the prime denotes derivative, and the parameters
\omega,m

are constants such that
\omega
is real and nonzero while
-\infty<m^{2}<\infty
. As a consequence, the Gödel-type metrics fall into three classes: (i) hyperbolic class (
m^{2}>0
); (ii) trigonometric class (
m^{2}<0
); (iii) linear class (
m^{2}=0
).

With the coordinate numbering
x^{0}=t,x^{1}=r,\,x^{2}=\phi,\,x^{3}=z

the components of the metric tensor are

\bigl(g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&H&0\\
0&-1&0&0\\
H&0&G&0\\
0&0&0&-1\end{array}\right)

(11)

while those of its inverse are

\bigl(g^{\mu\nu}\bigr)=\left(\begin{array}[]{crcr}-G/D^{2}&0&H/D^{2}&0\\
0&-1&0&0\\
H/D^{2}&0&-1/D^{2}&0\\
0&0&0&-1\end{array}\right)

(12)

where

G=H^{2}-D^{2}.

(13)

The metric (
9
) can be put in the form
[
27
]

ds^{2}=\eta_{AB}\theta^{A}\theta^{B}=\bigl(\theta^{0}\bigr)^{2}-\bigl(\theta^{1}\bigr)^{2}-\bigl(\theta^{2}\bigr)^{2}-\bigl(\theta^{3}\bigr)^{2}

(14)

where
\eta_{AB}=\mbox{diag}\,(1,-1,-1,-1)

and
\theta^{A}=e^{A}_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
. The nonvanishing components of the tetrad
e^{A}_{\mu}
are

e^{(0)}_{0}=1,\quad e^{(0)}_{2}=H,\quad e^{(1)}_{1}=1,\quad e^{(2)}_{2}=D,\quad e^{(3)}_{3}=1.

(15)

The inverse tetrad
e^{\mu}_{B}
, defined by
e^{A}_{\mu}e^{\mu}_{B}=\delta^{A}_{B}
, has the following nonzero components:

e^{0}_{(0)}=e^{1}_{(1)}=e^{3}_{(3)}=1,\quad e^{0}_{(2)}=-\frac{H}{D},\quad e^{2}_{(2)}=\frac{1}{D}.

(16)

We shall employ these tetrads for the sole purpose of projecting the metric field equations (
8
), and for the very practical reason that the components
\,{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{AB}\,
of the Einstein tensor for Gödel-type metrics have been previously computed and are easy to find in the literature.

IV
Gödel-type metrics in
f(Q)
gravity

Our main interest lies in finding out whether
f(Q)
gravity admits Gödel-like violations of causality. To this end,
we must investigate whether there are Gödel-type solutions in
f(Q)
gravity. We look for solutions to the field equations in the coincident gauge.

The coincident gauge is defined by

\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\mu\nu}=0,

(17)

implying that

\nabla_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}.

(18)

In this gauge the nonmetricity components are simply

Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu},

(19)

and the only nonvanishing components for the metric (
9
) are

Q_{102}=Q_{120}=H^{\prime}(r),\quad Q_{122}=G^{\prime}(r).

(20)

Taking into account Eq. (
70
) in the Appendix, the nonmetricity scalar (
7
) is immediately found to be

Q=\frac{{H^{\prime}}^{2}}{2D^{2}}=2\omega^{2},

(21)

where Eq. (
10
) has been used.
The fact that
Q
is constant will be of the utmost consequence in what follows. As pointed out in Section
II
, essentially the same solutions as in general relativity will be allowed.

At this moment, we wish to call attention to a very significant point that is sometimes overlooked: the coincident gauge is legitimate only if the trivial connection
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\mu\nu}=0
satisfies the connection field equations (
4
).

In the case under consideration, inasmuch as all geometric quantities depend on
x^{1}=r
alone, the connection field equations (
4
) reduce to

\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}\bigl(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{11}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\alpha}\bigr)=0.

(22)

Equation (
70
) in the Appendix shows that
P^{11}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\alpha}=0
, implying that
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\mu\nu}=0
satisfies the connection field equations.

It is of paramount importance to emphasize that here the coincident gauge is not merely a convenient but questionable choice because the connection field equations are satisfied.
This means that our forthcoming results are not an artifact of an improper gauge choice.

In the tetrad basis defined by (
15
) and (
16
) the metric field equations (
8
) take the form

f_{Q}{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{AB}\bigl(Qf_{Q}-f)+2f_{QQ}U_{C}P^{C}_{\,\,\,\,AB}=T_{AB},

(23)

where

{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{AB}=e^{\mu}_{A}e^{\nu}_{B}{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{\mu\nu},\quad U_{C}=e^{\lambda}_{C}\partial_{\lambda}Q,\quad P^{C}_{\,\,\,\,AB}=e^{C}_{\lambda}e^{\mu}_{A}e^{\nu}_{B}P^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\,\mu\nu},\quad T_{AB}=e^{\mu}_{A}e^{\nu}_{B}T_{\mu\nu}.

(24)

Tetrad indices are raised with
\eta^{AB}
and lowered with
\eta_{AB}
. We choose to write the metric field equations (
8
) in the tetrad basis for a purely pragmatic reason: the tetrad basis components
\,{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{AB}\,
of the Einstein tensor for Gödel-type metrics can be readily found in the literature, sparing us from unnecessary tiresome calculations. Furthermore, the form taken by the Einstein tensor in the tetrad basis is extremely simple:
\,{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{AB}\,
is diagonal and all of its components are constants — see Eq. (
26
) below.

Since
Q
is constant,
U_{C}=e^{\lambda}_{C}\partial_{\lambda}Q=0
and the field equations (
23
) reduce to

f_{Q}{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{AB}\bigl(Qf_{Q}-f)=T_{AB}.

(25)

For the Gödel-type geometry (
9
) with (
10
) and (
13
), the nonzero components of the Einstein tensor in the tetrad basis are
[
27
,
45
]

{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{(0)(0)}=3\omega^{2}-m^{2},\quad{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{(1)(1)}={\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{(2)(2)}=\omega^{2},\quad{\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize o}}{G}}_{(3)(3)}=m^{2}-\omega^{2}.

(26)

V
Perfect fluid and violation of causality

The Gödel-type metric (
9
) is compatible with the perfect fluid energy-momentum tensor

T_{AB}=(\rho+p)u_{A}u_{B}-p\eta_{AB},\qquad u_{A}=(1,0,0,0),

(27)

whose nonzero components are

T_{(0)(0)}=\rho,\qquad T_{(1)(1)}=T_{(2)(2)}=T_{(3)(3)}=p.

(28)

In this case, the nontrivial components of the field equations (
25
) for
f(Q)
gravity take the following form:

\displaystyle f_{Q}(3\omega^{2}-m^{2})+\frac{1}{2}(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho,

(29)

\displaystyle f_{Q}\omega^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p,

(30)

\displaystyle f_{Q}\omega^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p,

(31)

\displaystyle f_{Q}(m^{2}-\omega^{2})-\frac{1}{2}(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p.

(32)

By combining Eqs. (
30
) and (
32
) we get

f_{Q}\omega^{2}=f_{Q}(m^{2}-\omega^{2})

(33)

which implies

m^{2}=2\omega^{2}

(34)

on the assumption that
f_{Q}>0
. The equality
m^{2}=2\omega^{2}
, in turn,
implies that the metric (
9
) reduces to Gödel’s original solution
[
25
,
28
]
.
In general, if
0<m^{2}<4\omega^{2}
there is a radius beyond which causality may be violated
[
25
,
27
,
28
]
. This critical radius is

r_{c}=\frac{2}{|m|}\sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{4\omega^{2}}{m^{2}}-1\right)^{-1}.

(35)

Therefore, taking (
34
) into account, it follows that in the present case the critical radius is

r_{c}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}\,|\omega|}\,\sinh^{-1}(1)=\sqrt{2}\ln(1+\sqrt{2})\,|\omega|^{-1}.

(36)

There is violation of causality for
r>r_{c}
because the circles

r=\mbox{const}>r_{c},\qquad t=\mbox{const},\qquad z=\mbox{const}

(37)

are closed timelike curves
[
25
,
27
,
28
]
.

Note that if
Q=Q_{0}
and
f_{Q}(Q_{0})=0
then Eq. (
33
) does not fix a relation between
m
and
\omega
. This would be the case, for example, if
f(Q)=Q_{0}+a(Q-Q_{0})^{2}
with nonzero constants
Q_{0}
and
a
. Then Eqs. (
29
) to (
32
) would hold true for a fluid with equation of state
p=-\rho
, which is tantamount to a cosmological constant, for any values of
m
and
\omega
. Of course, this still includes all cases such that
0<m^{2}<4\omega^{2}
, which entail violation of causality.

VI
Energy conditions

Energy conditions are essentially statements to the effect that the total energy density is nonnegative everywhere in spacetime and that matter cannot travel faster than light
[
75
,
76
]
. They are expected to be obeyed by “normal” matter and non-gravitational fields in order to rule out “unphysical” solutions of the Einstein field equations.

Let us examine to what extent the function
f(Q)
is restricted by energy conditions on the perfect fluid that acts as the matter source of Gödel’s solution in the context of
f(Q)
gravity.

The weak energy condition requires
\rho\geq 0
and
\rho+p\geq 0
. From Eq. (
29
) we must have

2Qf_{Q}-f\geq 0,

(38)

where Eqs. (
21
) and (
34
) have been used. In addition to that, with the use of Eqs. (
29
) and
(
30
) the condition
\rho+p\geq 0
implies

Qf_{Q}\geq 0.

(39)

Together with
\rho+p\geq 0
, the strong energy condition stipulates that
\rho+3p\geq 0
. From (
29
) and
(
30
) it follows that the latter condition leads to

Qf_{Q}+f\geq 0.

(40)

Since
Q>0
, condition (
39
) is satisfied if
f_{Q}>0
. Therefore, the weak and strong energy conditions are certainly obeyed as long as the function
f(Q)
satisfies the following inequalities:

f_{Q}>0,\qquad 2Qf_{Q}-f\geq 0,\qquad Qf_{Q}+f\geq 0.

(41)

The dominant energy condition stipulates that
\rho-|p|\geq 0
. Taking into account that
\omega^{2}=Q/2
and
m^{2}=2\omega^{2}=Q
, equations (
29
) and (
30
) give

\rho=Qf_{Q}-\frac{f}{2},\qquad p=\frac{f}{2}.

(42)

Therefore,

\rho-|p|=Qf_{Q}-\frac{f}{2}-\frac{|f|}{2}.

(43)

If
f\geq 0
this reduces to
\rho-|p|=Qf_{Q}-f
whereas if
f<0
one has
\rho-|p|=Qf_{Q}
. The latter inequality is satisfied if
f_{Q}>0
, while the former is a new restriction on
f(Q)
in addition to (
41
). But notice that
2Qf_{Q}-f\geq 0
is automatically satisfied if
Qf_{Q}-f\geq 0
because
Q>0
and
f_{Q}>0
.
So, all energy conditions are fulfilled if the following set of inequalities are satisfied:

f_{Q}>0,\qquad Qf_{Q}-f\geq 0,\qquad Qf_{Q}+f\geq 0.

(44)

By way of illustration, let us now see what these inequalities have to say about three models proposed in the literature.

\bullet
Model 1:
f(Q)=Q+aQ^{2}
(considered in
[
20
]
and also in
[
22
]
). Conditions (
44
) require

1+2aQ>0,\qquad aQ^{2}\geq 0,\qquad 2Q+3aQ^{2}\geq 0.

(45)

All conditions are satisfied as long as
a\geq 0
.

\bullet
Model 2:
f(Q)=Q^{n}
(considered in
[
22
]
). For this model conditions (
44
) give

nQ^{n-1}>0,\qquad(n-1)Q^{n}\geq 0,\qquad(n+1)Q^{n}\geq 0.

(46)

All conditions are satisfied if
n\geq 1

VII
Causal solutions with massless scalar field

A source other than a perfect fluid may lead to causal solutions. Following Rebouças and Tiomno
[
28
]
, let us add a massless scalar field
\Phi
to the perfect fluid to obtain the energy momentum tensor

T_{AB}=(\rho+p)u_{A}u_{B}-p\eta_{AB}+\partial_{A}\Phi\partial_{B}\Phi-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{AB}\eta^{CD}\partial_{C}\Phi\partial_{D}\Phi.

(48)

VII.1
Scalar field depending on
r
alone

As our first try, let us suppose
\Phi
depends only on
r
. In this case
T_{AB}
is diagonal and its components are

T_{(0)(0)}=\rho+\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2},\quad T_{(1)(1)}=p+\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2},\quad T_{(2)(2)}=T_{(3)(3)}=p-\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2}.

(49)

Then the field equations (
25
) become

\displaystyle f_{Q}(3\omega^{2}-m^{2})+\frac{1}{2}(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho+\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2},

(50)

\displaystyle f_{Q}\omega^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p+\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2},

(51)

\displaystyle f_{Q}\omega^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p-\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2},

(52)

\displaystyle f_{Q}(m^{2}-\omega^{2})-\frac{1}{2}(Qf_{Q}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p-\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2}.

(53)

From Eqs. (
52
) and (
53
) it follows at once that
m^{2}=2\omega^{2}
and we are back to Gödel’s original model. Moreover, Eqs. (
51
) and (
52
) imply that
\Phi^{\prime}(r)=0
, and one returns to the pure perfect fluid case of Section
V
.

VII.2
Cosmological constant and scalar field depending on
z
alone

Let us take

f(Q)=g(Q)-2\Lambda,

(54)

which is equivalent to adding a cosmological constant
\Lambda
to the field equations, and assume not only that there is no fluid but also that
\Phi
depends only on
z
.
In this case, putting
\rho=p=0
, the total energy momentum tensor (
48
) reduces to the one associated with the massless scalar field, which is diagonal with components

T_{(0)(0)}=-T_{(1)(1)}=-T_{(2)(2)}=T_{(3)(3)}=\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{2}.

(55)

Then the field equations (
25
) become

\displaystyle g_{Q}(3\omega^{2}-m^{2})+\Lambda+\frac{1}{2}(Qg_{Q}-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{2},

(56)

\displaystyle g_{Q}\omega^{2}-\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}(Qg_{Q}-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{2},

(57)

\displaystyle g_{Q}\omega^{2}-\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(Qg_{Q}-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{2},

(58)

\displaystyle g_{Q}(m^{2}-\omega^{2})-\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}(Qg_{Q}-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{2}.

(59)

By adding Eqs. (
56
) and (
57
) one arrives at

g_{Q}(4\omega^{2}-m^{2})=0.

(60)

If
g_{Q}>0
this leads to

m^{2}=4\omega^{2}.

(61)

According to Eq. (
35
),
r_{c}=\infty
if
m^{2}=4\omega^{2}
. In other words, there is no critical radius and the solution is causal. With
m^{2}=4\omega^{2}
the field equations (
58
) and (
59
) become

\displaystyle\omega^{2}g_{Q}-\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}(Qg_{Q}-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{2},

(62)

\displaystyle 3\omega^{2}g_{Q}-\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}(Qg_{Q}-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{2}.

(63)

Upon adding Eqs. (
62
) and (
63
) one gets

\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(Qg_{Q}+g\bigr),

(64)

where we have used
\omega^{2}=Q/2
.
Insertion of the above result into any of equations (
62
) or (
63
) leads to

\Phi^{\prime}(z)^{2}=Qg_{Q}.

(65)

Since
Q>0
and
g_{Q}=f_{Q}>0
by conditions (
44
), the right-hand side of this equation is positive. Thus, Eq. (
65
) is satisfied by a scalar field of the form
\Phi(z)=az
where
a
is a real nonzero constant. Assuming that the function
g(Q)
itself obeys conditions (
44
), the cosmological constant (
64
) is positive. So, for any reasonable function
f(Q)
, we have constructed a causal solution to the field equations with a
positive cosmological constant and a massless scalar field as matter source.

VIII
Conclusion

The content of any new gravity theory should be explored to the fullest extent and depth possible. One relevant question to be asked is whether, as general relativity, the new theory admits Gödel-like noncausal solutions. Aiming to investigate the possible existence of Gödel-type solutions in
f(Q)
gravity, we have studied the conditions for a class of homogeneous Gödel-type metrics to satisfy the field equations of the theory. We have shown that adoption of the coincident gauge is warranted because the connection field equations are obeyed. Working in the coincident gauge we have found that
f(Q)
gravity admits Gödel-type solutions for any function
f(Q)
. Out of all Gödel-type metrics only Gödel’s original model is allowed in
f(Q)
gravity with a perfect fluid as source. This implies that violation of causality in the form of closed timelike curves is a feature common to general relativity and
f(Q)
gravity. We have also found that mild restrictions on the function
f(Q)
arise from the imposition of energy conditions on the fluid. By considering three forms for
f(Q)
that have been put forward in the literature, we have established that they satisfy the weak, strong and dominant energy conditions for a wide range of values of the parameters that enter
f(Q)
.

In the presence of a cosmological constant, with a massless scalar field as matter content, we have constructed causal solutions for any sensible function
f(Q)
. Just like general relativity,
f(Q)
gravity admits causal or noncausal solutions depending on the matter content described by the energy momentum tensor, and on the presence or absence of a cosmological constant.
Future work should further explore this issue, examining whether the noncausal behavior of this modified gravity theory extends to a wider class of solutions under more general physical conditions.

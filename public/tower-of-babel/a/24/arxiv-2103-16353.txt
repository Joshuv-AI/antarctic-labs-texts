On orbital stability of the physical ground states of the NLS equations
Yavdat Ilyasov

Abstract
. We prove orbital stability result for physical ground states of a nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation in the sense that the set of these ground states is contained in the set of prescribed mass solutions which is orbital stable by the Cazenave-Lions theorem. We apply the nonlinear generalized Rayleigh quotients method which allows establishing a one-to-one correspondence between the values of the mass m m , the frequency λ \lambda , and the action level S S of the physical ground states.

1.
Introduction

We consider the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation with combined power-type nonlinearities

(1.1)

i\psi_{t}=\Delta\psi+\mu|\psi|^{p-2}\psi-|\psi|^{q-2}\psi,~~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\psi
is a complex-valued function of
(t,x)
,
p,q\in(2,2^{*})
,
N\geq 3
,
2^{*}:=2N/(N-2)
, and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.
NLS equations of this type have attracted much
attention in the last decades. For the physical background, we refer the reader to
[
4
,
25
,
32
,
35
]
and references therein.
According to
[
10
,
32
]
, the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) with the initial value
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is locally well posed and has the unique local solution
\psi\in C([0,T(\psi_{0})),H^{1})\cap C^{1}([0,T(\psi_{0})),H^{-1})
for some
T(\psi_{0})>0
, which satisfies the
energy conservation law
:

E\equiv H_{\mu}(\psi(t)):=\int\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{p}|\psi|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}|\psi|^{q}\right)dx,

and the
mass (charge, particle
numbers) conservation law
:

\alpha\equiv Q(\psi(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int|\psi|^{2}dx.

In the present paper, we study the existence and stability of the standing waves
\psi_{\lambda}=e^{i\lambda t}u
of (
1.1
), where the amplitude function
u
satisfies

(1.2)

-\Delta u-\lambda u-\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{q-2}u=0,~~x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Here
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the
frequency
of the standing wave.
Notice that the action functional

S_{\lambda,\mu}(u):=H_{\mu}(u)-\lambda Q(u)

associated with problem is also a conserved quantity. For a given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, a solution
\bar{u}
of (
1.2
) is said to be
ground state
if
S_{\lambda,\mu}(\bar{u})\leq S_{\lambda,\mu}(w)
, for any
w\in H^{1}\setminus 0
such that
DS_{\lambda,\mu}(w)=0
.
It is important to note that the set of ground states
G_{\lambda}(\mu)
of (
1.2
) (corresponding to this definition) may contain nonphysical solutions of (
1.2
), for example, solutions that disappear under a small perturbation of the parameter
\lambda
. In the present paper, we will focus on a subset of ground states that are preserved under a small perturbation of parameters of the problem. We call such a subset the set of physical ground states of (
1.2
).

We also deal with the so-called
prescribed action solution
of (
1.2
), i.e., a function
u^{S}\in H^{1}
which for a given action
S\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies

(1.3)

S_{\lambda,\mu}(u^{S})=S~~\mbox{and}~~DS_{\lambda,\mu}(u^{S})=0,

with some
\lambda\leq 0
.
Note that the standard approach for problem (
1.2
) considers solutions with
prescribed frequency

\lambda
, and unknowns action
S
and mass
\alpha
(see, e.g,
[
6
,
10
]
). An alternative formulation which has also been actively investigated over the last decades consists of finding the solution
u
to (
1.2
) having
prescribed mass

\alpha=Q(u)
, while
\lambda
and
S
are unknown (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
11
,
18
,
29
,
28
]
). Note that the frequency
\lambda
can be also considered as a value of the following conserved quantity

(1.4)

\lambda=\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(\psi(t)):=\frac{H_{\mu}(\psi)-S}{Q(\psi)}.

We call the frequency
\lambda
, action
S
and mass
\alpha
the
main parameters of problem
(
1.1
).
Thus, from the mathematical point of view, all of these three approaches, namely, with prescribed frequency, with prescribed action and with prescribed mass are equally acceptable. Moreover, all of these approaches evidently are relevant from the physical point of view. In particular, the approach with prescribed action arises in the study of inverse problems and the so-called spectral and scattering control problems (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
17
,
34
]
and the references therein).

We shall pay a special attention to the standing wave
\psi_{\lambda}
of (
1.1
) with
\lambda=0
which we call the
zero frequency solution
. The corresponding equation (
1.2
) with
\lambda=0
for the amplitude function
u
we shall call the
zero frequency problem
.
In the literature, such an equation is sometimes called the ”zero mass” case problem (cf.
[
6
,
9
]
). Besides the fact that this type of problems is interesting from a purely mathematical point of view (see, e.g.
[
3
,
6
,
9
,
14
,
12
,
31
]
), they often arise in physical problems, such as in the Euclidean Yang-Mills theory, equations of the filtration through porous media, the study of solutions with compact supports of the reaction-diffusion systems, plasma physics, among others (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
15
,
23
,
27
]
and the references therein).

The orbital stability of the ground states of many equations can be often investigated using the Lyapunov function, determined by the action functional
S_{\lambda,\mu}(u)
restricted to the manifold of functions
u
with fixed mass integral
\alpha=Q(u)
. The general theorem on orbital stability of solutions of nonlinear problems based on this idea was proved in the famous work by T. Cazenave & P.L. Lions
[
11
]
in 1983. They showed that if the following
prescribed mass minimization problem

(1.5)

\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{\mu}:=\min\{H_{\mu}(u):Q(u)=\alpha,~u\in H^{1}\setminus 0\},~\alpha>0

has a solution, and all of its minimizing sequences are relatively compact, then the set of prescribed mass solutions:

\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha):=\{u\in H^{1}:~\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{\mu}:=H_{\mu}(u),~Q(u)=\alpha\}

is orbital stable in the sense that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any solution (
1.1
) with initial data
u(0)
such that
\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)}\|u(0)-\phi\|_{H^{1}}<\delta
there holds

\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)}\|u(t)-\phi\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon,~~\forall t\geq 0.

Nevertheless, it should be noted that this result does not always entail the orbital stability of the set of ground states of (
1.2
).
Indeed, for any
u_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)
by the Lagrange multipliers rule there exists
a constant
\nu(u_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{\alpha}
solves (
1.2
) with
\lambda=\nu(u_{\alpha})
. Thus to obtain the orbital stability of the set of ground states we need to know:

1)
Whether the values of Lagrange multipliers
\nu(u_{\alpha})
for all functions
u_{\alpha}
from the set of solutions of (
1.5
) are identical, i.e.,

\nu(u_{\alpha})\equiv\lambda_{\alpha}
,
\forall u_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)
for some
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
?

2)
Whether the set of solutions
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)
of problem (
1.5
) coincides with the set of ground states
G_{\lambda_{\alpha}}
of (
1.2
)
?

Affirmative answers to these questions can be obtained only for some special cases of the NLS equation. In particular, this is true for the NLS equation with a monomial nonlinearity, i.e., for
i\psi_{t}=\Delta\psi+|\psi|^{q-2}\psi
, where the Lagrange multiplier may be eliminated by scale changes (see, e.g,
[
21
]
). This is also true in the case of dimension
N=1
, which is characterized by the uniqueness of positive solution (see, e.g.,
[
19
,
22
,
24
]
).

The purpose of this article is to provide some answers to these questions. In this respect, we present a new type of variational functional associated with problem (
1.1
), which makes it possible to find the ground state
\bar{u}
of the problem, as well as allows to uniquely determine the corresponding values of mass
\alpha
, frequency
\lambda
and action level
S_{\lambda,\mu}
of this state.

Let us state our main results.
Notice that
u^{S}\in H^{1}\setminus 0
is a prescribed action solution of (
1.2
) with action
S>0
, namely it satisfies to (
1.3
), if and only if
u^{S}
is a critical point of
\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)
with a critical value
\lambda
, i.e.,

D\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u^{S})=0~~\mbox{and}~~\lambda=\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u^{S}).

For a given
S>0
, we call a solution
\hat{u}
of (
1.2
) the
fundamental frequency solution
(respectively,
e^{i\hat{\lambda}t}\hat{u}
is said to be
fundamental frequency standing wave
of (
1.1
)) with a
fundamental frequency

\hat{\lambda}^{S}_{\mu}
if

\hat{\lambda}^{S}_{\mu}=\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(\hat{u})\leq\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(w)

for any
w\in H^{1}\setminus 0
such that
D\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(w)=0
. For
S>0
, we denote by

(1.6)

G^{S}(\mu):=\{u\in H^{1}\setminus 0:\Lambda_{\mu}^{S}(u)=\hat{\lambda}_{\mu}^{S},~D\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u^{S})=0\}

the set of fundamental frequency solutions of (
1.2
) with the fundamental frequency
\hat{\lambda}_{\mu}^{S}
. Below we show that the existence of fundamental frequency solution entails the existence of ground state and that the converse is also true (see below Lemma
5.7
). In what follows, we will refer to
G^{S}(\mu)
the set of ground states, as well.

Definition 1.1
.

We call
u^{S}\in{G}^{S}(\mu)
for
S>0
the physical ground state of (
1.2
) with respect to the action value if there exists a sequence
u^{S_{m}}\in G^{S_{m}}(\mu)
,
m=1,\ldots,
such that
\lim_{m\to+\infty}S_{m}=S
and
u^{S_{m}}\to u^{S}
in
H^{1}
as
m\to+\infty
. The set of physical ground states of (
1.2
) with respect to the action value we denote by
\hat{G}^{S}(\mu)
.

It is easily seen that any ground state
v_{\lambda}
of (
1.2
) from the residual set
\hat{G}^{S,c}(\mu):=G^{S}(\mu)\setminus\hat{G}^{S}(\mu)
disappears after a small perturbation of the parameter
S
. It can also makes sense to refer to the physical ground states
\hat{G}^{S}(\mu)
as the
ground states of the branches of solutions
of (
1.2
).

Consider

\alpha_{0}(\mu):=\inf\{\alpha>0:\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{\mu}<0\}.

We need the following result of M. Shibata (see Theorems 1.1, 1.3, Lemma 2.3 in
[
28
]
):

Theorem
(M. Shibata, 2014)
.
Let
\mu>0
and
2<q<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
.
Then
\alpha_{0}(\mu)>0
;
if
\alpha>\alpha_{0}(\mu)
, the set of prescribed mass solutions
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)
is not empty;
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)=\emptyset
if
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}(\mu)]
; the function
\alpha\mapsto\hat{H}^{\alpha}_{\mu}
is nonincreasing;
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)
is an orbital stable set of (
1.1
).

Our main result on the orbital stability of the set of physical ground states of (
1.2
) is as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
2<q<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
,
\mu>0
,
N\geq 3
. Then for any given
\alpha\in(\alpha_{0}(\mu),+\infty)
, there exists
{S_{\alpha}}>0
such that the set of physical ground states
\hat{G}^{S_{\alpha}}(\mu)
is contained in the orbital stable set of prescribed mass solutions
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\alpha)
.
Furthermore, all ground states from
\hat{G}^{S_{\alpha}}(\mu)
have identical mass and frequency, i.e.,
\alpha=Q(u)
,
\lambda_{S_{\alpha}}:=\Lambda^{S_{\alpha}}_{\mu}(u)
,
\forall u\in\hat{G}^{S_{\alpha}}(\mu)
.

The proof of this result is based on the application of the nonlinear generalized Rayleigh quotient method
[
16
]
(
the NG-Rayleigh quotient method
for short) to (
1.2
) and using
\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)
as the Rayleigh quotient. A distinctive feature of the NG-Rayleigh method is that it allows to find the critical values of the problems’ parameters, and at the same time to convert the original variational functionals into functionals with simpler geometry (see
[
8
,
16
]
).

According to the NG-Rayleigh quotient method
[
16
]
, the functional
\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)
corresponds to the following
NG-Rayleigh quotient
:

(1.7)

\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u):=\frac{c_{N}^{S}(\int|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{N}{(N-2)}}-\mu\frac{2}{p}\int|u|^{p}+\frac{2}{q}\int|u|^{q}}{\int|u|^{2}},\,\,u\in H^{1}\setminus\{0\},~~S>0,

where
c_{N}^{S}=(N-2)/(N^{\frac{N}{(N-2)}}S^{\frac{2}{(N-2)}})
, for
p,q\in(2,2^{*})
and
\mu>0
. Below we will see that any critical point of
\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)
in
H^{1}\setminus 0
corresponds, possibly after some scaling, to a critical point of
\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)
, and thus it gives a solution of equation (
1.2
) with prescribed action
S>0
. Moreover, the NG-Rayleigh quotient
\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)
is characterized by properties similar to those that has the usual Rayleigh quotient of linear theory
[
26
]
. In particular, similar to the spectral theory the following critical value

(1.8)

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{S}_{\mu}:=\min_{u\in H^{1}\setminus 0}\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)

plays a principal role in the investigation of problem (
1.2
). In the case
2<q<p<2^{*}
, using the NG-Rayleigh quotient method
[
16
]
as well, we introduce the following principal critical value by
\mu

(1.9)

\hat{\mu}^{S}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}\setminus 0}\mu^{S}(u).

Here,
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(1.10)

\mu^{S}(u):=\left(\frac{c(p,q,N)}{S^{\frac{2(p-q)}{(2^{*}-q)(N-2)}}}\right)\frac{(\int|u|^{q})^{\frac{2^{*}-p}{2^{*}-q}}(\int|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{2^{*}(p-q)}{2(2^{*}-q)}}}{\int|u|^{p}},~~u\in H^{1},

where constant
c(p,q,N)
does not depend on
S
(see below (
2.4
)).

The NG-Rayleigh quotient
\mu^{S}(u)
is characterized by the fact that its critical points correspond to the zero frequency solutions of (
1.2
). Below we show that
\hat{\mu}^{S}>0
.

2.
The nonlinear generalized Rayleigh quotients

We denote by
H^{1}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

the Sobolev space of functions with norm

\|u\|_{1}=(\int(|u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})^{1/2},

and use the space

\mathcal{D}^{1,2}:=\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}

with inner product
(u,v):=\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx
and norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}}:=\int|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx
. For abbreviation, we denote

\int\cdots\,:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\cdots\,dx
. For Gateaux differentiable functional
F:H^{1}(\mathcal{D}^{1,2})\to\mathbb{R}
, its derivative at
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{D}^{1,2})
we denote by
DF(u)
.
For
u\in H^{1}
, we denote
u_{\sigma}:=u(x/\sigma)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\sigma>0
, and

T(u):=\int|\nabla u|^{2},~~Q(u):=\int|u|^{2},~~A(u):=\int|u|^{p},~~B(u):=\int|u|^{q}.

With these notations we have

S_{\lambda,\mu}(u):=\frac{1}{2}T(u)-\lambda\frac{1}{2}Q(u)-\mu\frac{1}{p}A(u)+\frac{1}{q}B(u).

For
S\geq 0
, introduce the so-called
action-level Rayleigh quotient

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u):=\frac{\frac{1}{2}T(u)-\mu\frac{1}{p}A(u)+\frac{1}{q}B(u)-S}{\frac{1}{2}Q(u)}.

Notice that for any
S\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

•

\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)=\lambda~\Leftrightarrow~S_{\lambda,\mu}(u)=S
,

•

D\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)=0
with
\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)=\lambda~\Leftrightarrow~DS_{\lambda,\mu}(u)=0
.

Let
u\in H^{1}\setminus 0
,
S>0
,
\sigma>0
, consider

\Lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u_{\sigma})=\frac{\sigma^{-2}\frac{1}{2}T(u)-\mu\frac{1}{p}A(u)+\frac{1}{q}B(u)-\sigma^{-N}S}{\frac{1}{2}Q(u)}.

Then

\frac{d}{d\sigma}(\Lambda^{S}_{\mu})(u_{\sigma})=0~\Leftrightarrow~\frac{2}{Q(u)}\left(-\sigma^{-3}T(u)+\frac{NS}{\sigma^{N+1}}\right)=0~\Leftrightarrow~\sigma=\sigma^{S}(u):=\left(\frac{NS}{T(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-2}}.

Hence, in accordance with the NG-Rayleigh quotient method
[
16
]
, we are able to introduce the following NG-Rayleigh quotient (cf. (
1.7
))

(2.2)

\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u):=\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u_{\sigma^{S}(u)})=\frac{2}{Q(u)}\left(\frac{c_{N}^{S}}{2}T^{\frac{N}{(N-2)}}(u)-\mu\frac{1}{p}A(u)+\frac{1}{q}B(u)\right),

where

Observe that

\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u_{\sigma})=\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)

Lemma 2.1

For

Proof.

The proof easily follows by direct calculations of the derivative

Remark 2.2

In view of the homogeneity of

Consider

•

\forall u\in H^{1}\setminus 0

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume that

\hat{\lambda}_{\mu}^{S}\equiv\inf_{u\in H^{1}\setminus 0}\lambda^{S}_{\mu}(u)<0

In the case

For every

\mu^{S}(u):=M^{S}(s_{m}(u)u)=\min_{s\geq 0}M^{S}(su)=C_{p,q,N,S}\frac{B^{\frac{2^{*}-p}{2^{*}-q}}(u)T^{\frac{2^{*}(p-q)}{2(2^{*}-q)}}(u)}{A(u)},

0

.

Assume that

Lemma 3.2

Recall the Brezis-Lieb lemma (see
[
21
]

Lemma 3.3

Denote

Lemma 4.1

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{u}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\|\nabla{u_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\lim_{i\to\infty}\|\nabla({u_{i}}-\hat{u}^{*})\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

Proposition 5.2

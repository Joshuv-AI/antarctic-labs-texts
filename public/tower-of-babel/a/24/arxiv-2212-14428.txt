\displaystyle\frac{2(6\pi+1)}{A_{3}(I)}I\left[2[\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}r)-1]+\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}r)}{A_{3}(I)^{1/2}}\right]+\frac{3\pi}{8}I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\left[\frac{2(6\pi+1)}{A_{3}(I)}\left(2[\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}r)-1]+\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}r)}{A_{3}(I)^{1/2}}\right)+\frac{3\pi}{8}\right],

from where one deduces (
3.16
).

To finish this section, we prove (
3.17
). Let us denote by

h(r)
the RHS of (
3.38
). Then, taking
r=D:=\mbox{Diameter}(M)
we have
B_{M}(x,D)=M
for any
x\in M
, and so,

C_{1}(I)(g+1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{1.1}}}{{\leq}}\mbox{Area}(M)=\mbox{Area}\left[B_{M}(x,D)\right]\leq h(D).

(3.39)

Since
\sinh(t)\leq\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(t)
,

\displaystyle h(D)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I\left[\frac{2(6\pi+1)}{A_{3}(I)}\left(2[\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}D)-1]+\frac{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}D)}{A_{3}(I)^{1/2}}\right)+\frac{3\pi}{8}\right]

(3.40)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(A_{3}(I)\geq 6)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle I\left[\frac{6\pi+1}{3}\left(2[\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}D)-1]+\frac{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}D)}{\sqrt{6}}\right)+\frac{3\pi}{8}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\left[\frac{6\pi+1}{3}\left(\left(2+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right)\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}D)-2\right)+\frac{3\pi}{8}\right]

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle I\frac{6\pi+1}{3}\left(2+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right)\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}D).

(
3.39
) and (
3.40
) give

C_{1}(I)(g+1)\leq I\frac{6\pi+1}{3}\left(2+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right)\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits(\sqrt{A_{3}(I)}D),

from where inequality (
3.17
) follows directly.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

3.8
Proof of item
2
of the Theorem
1.1

Consider an element
(F\colon M\looparrowright X)\in\Lambda(I,1,1,1,1)
.
As in previous sections, we may assume that
M
is compact and
connected, and let
g=g(M)
be the genus of
M
.

Claim 3.11
.

If
I=0
, then (
1.2
) holds with
G(0)=0
.

Proof.

Since
M
is stable, we have
|A_{M}|\leq C_{s}
in
M

by (
3.19
). Thus, the Gauss equation implies that
the Gaussian curvature
K
of
M
satisfies
K\geq-1-\frac{1}{2}C_{s}^{2}
.
Using the Gauss-Bonnet theorem,

\textstyle{(1+\frac{1}{2}C_{s}^{2})}\mbox{Area}(M)\geq-\int_{M}K=-2\pi\chi(M)\geq 2\pi(g-1).

Hence,

\mbox{Area}(M)\geq\frac{2\pi}{1+\frac{1}{2}C_{s}^{2}}(g-1),

which is strictly bigger than
\frac{\pi}{3+4C_{s}+4C_{2}^{2}}(g+1)
when

g\geq 2
. Consequently, (
1.2
) holds whenever
g\geq 2
.
To finish the proof of the claim it remains to check
that (
1.2
) holds for
g=0,1
, which we do next.

\mbox{Area}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\footnotesize(item~\ref{It0})}}}{{\geq}}C_{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\footnotesize(a)}}}{{>}}\frac{2\pi}{3+4C_{s}+4C_{2}^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\footnotesize(b)}}}{{\geq}}\frac{2\pi}{3+4C_{s}+4C_{2}^{2}}(g+1)

where in (a) we have used that
C_{s}\geq 2\pi
, and in (b) that
g\leq 1
.
Now the claim is proved.
∎

By Claim
3.11
, it remains to prove item
2
of
Theorem
1.1
assuming
I\geq 1
.
The additional assumption
g\geq 12I-3
guarantees,
by (
3.11
), that

\int_{\widetilde{M}}K=\int_{{M}}K-\int_{\cup_{i=1}^{k}\Delta_{i}}K\leq-\pi(g+1).

(3.41)

By Lemma 7.1 in
[
9
]
,
there exists a positive constant
\widehat{C}_{s}(1)
, which in our setting is

1+2C_{s}
, such that if
\sup|A_{M}|>\widehat{C}_{s}(1)
, then there exists a nonempty finite subset

\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}\subset M

with
1\leq n\leq I
, such that

1.

|A_{M}|
achieves its maximum in
M
at
q_{1}
, and for

i=2,\ldots,n
,
|A_{M}|
achieves its maximum in

M\setminus[B_{M}(q_{1},1)\cup\ldots\cup B_{M}(q_{i-1},1)]
at
q_{i}
.

2.

For each
i=1,\ldots,n
,
|A_{M}|(q_{i})>\widehat{C}_{s}(1)

and the intrinsic
balls
B_{M}(q_{i},1/2)
are pairwise disjoint and unstable.

3.

|A_{M}|\leq\widehat{C}_{s}(1)
in
M\setminus[B_{M}(q_{1},1)\cup\ldots\cup B_{M}(q_{n},1)]
.

We next define a partition of the surface
\widetilde{M}
that appears
in item (
3
) of Theorem
2.2
.

•

If
\sup|A_{M}|\leq\widehat{C}_{s}(1)
, let
\widetilde{M}_{1}=\varnothing
and
\widetilde{M}_{2}=\widetilde{M}
.

•

Otherwise, let

\widetilde{M}_{1}=\widetilde{M}\cap[\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{M}(q_{i},1)]
and

\widetilde{M}_{2}=\widetilde{M}\setminus[\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{M}(q_{i},1)]
.

In particular, the second fundamental form of the surface
\widetilde{M}_{2}

satisfies
|A_{\widetilde{M}_{2}}|\leq\widehat{C}_{s}(1)
.

By the discussion around inequality (
3.6
),
the Gaussian curvature function
of
\widetilde{M}_{1}
satisfies
K_{\widetilde{M}_{1}}\geq K_{1}

(where
K_{1}=K_{1}(I)\leq-3/2
is defined in (
3.4
)),
and the Gaussian curvature function of
\widetilde{M}_{2}

satisfies
K_{\widetilde{M}_{2}}\geq-1-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{C}_{s}(1)^{2}
.
Also, by inequalities (
3.13
) and (
3.16
),
there exists an explicit function
h\colon\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)

such that

\mbox{Area}[\widetilde{M}\cap[\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{M}(q_{i},1)]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mbox{Area}B_{M}(q_{i},1)\leq h(I).

(3.42)

Therefore,

{\textstyle K_{1}(I)h(I)-\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{C}_{s}(1)^{2}\right]\mbox{\rm Area}(\widetilde{M}_{2})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{3.37}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\widetilde{M}_{1}}K_{\widetilde{M}_{1}}+\int_{\widetilde{M}_{2}}K_{\widetilde{M}_{2}}=\int_{\widetilde{M}}K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{3.7a}}}{{\leq}}-\pi(g+1).

(3.43)

Solving for the area of
\widetilde{M}_{2}
, we have

\mbox{\rm Area}(\widetilde{M}_{2})\geq\frac{\pi(g+1)+K_{1}(I)h(I)}{1+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{C}_{s}(1)^{2}}.

(3.44)

After setting
C:=\frac{\pi/2}{1+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{C}_{s}(1)^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{3+4C_{s}+4C_{s}^{2}}
,
we get the estimate

\mbox{\rm Area}(M)\geq\mbox{\rm Area}(\widetilde{M})\geq\mbox{\rm Area}(\widetilde{M}_{2})\geq C(g+1)+\frac{\frac{\pi}{2}(g+1)+K_{1}(I)h(I)}{1+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{C}_{s}(1)^{2}}.

(3.45)

Define

G(I):=\max\left\{12I-3,\left\lceil\frac{-2K_{1}(I)h(I)}{\pi}\right\rceil-1\right\}\in\mathbb{N},

where for a real number
x
, we denote by
\lceil x\rceil
the
smallest integer that is not smaller than
x
(also known as the

ceiling function
at
x
). Then, whenever
g(M)\geq G(I)
we have
that the second term in the RHS of (
3.45
) is non-negative,
which completes the proof of item
2
of Theorem
1.1
.

3.9
Proof of item
3
of Theorem
1.1

Recall that in Section
3.1
we normalized the space

\Lambda
, passing from an
H
-immersion
(F\colon M\looparrowright X)\in\Lambda(I,H_{0},r_{0},1,K_{0})
to the immersion

(F^{\prime}\colon M\looparrowright X^{\prime})\in\Lambda(I,1,1,1,1)
,
where
{\lambda}
is given by (
3.1
) and
X^{\prime}
is the Riemannian manifold
obtained after scaling the original metric of
X
by
\sqrt{{\lambda}}
.
Observe that
F^{\prime}
has mean curvature
H^{\prime}=H/{\lambda}
and
X^{\prime}
has
scalar curvature
\rho^{\prime}=\rho/{\lambda}^{2}
.

Suppose that the scalar curvature
\rho
of

X
satisfies
3H^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho\geq c
in
X
for some
c>0
, where

H
is the mean curvature of an immersion
(F\colon M\looparrowright X)\in\Lambda(I,H_{0},r_{0},1,K_{0})
.
In this setting, Rosenberg
[
13
]
(see also
[
10
, Theorem 2.12]
) proved that
every stable subdomain
\Omega\subset M
satisfies

d_{M}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3c}}:=R_{c},

(3.46)

for all
x\in\Omega
. Since
3(H^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\prime}\geq c^{\prime}:=c/{\lambda}^{2}
,
the estimate (
3.46
) applied to the same stable
subdomain
\Omega
viewed inside the domain of
F^{\prime}
gives
that the intrinsic distance in the metric induced by
F^{\prime}

from any
x\in\Omega
to
\partial\Omega
is at most

\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3c^{\prime}}}=\frac{2\pi{\lambda}}{\sqrt{3c}}
. This linear
scaling on the upper bound for the intrinsic radius of stable subdomains
allows us to reduce item
3
of Theorem
1.1
to the
following statement.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
F\colon M\looparrowright X
be an
H
-immersion in
\Lambda(I,1,1,1,1)
,
where
M
is connected.
If the scalar curvature
\rho
of
X
satisfies
3H^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho\geq c
in
X
for some
c>0
, then
M
is compact, and
there exists

such that

\mbox{\rm Area}(M)\leq A_{2}(I,c)\qquad\mbox{\rm Diameter}(M)\leq 2(I+1)R_{c},\qquad g(M)\leq\frac{A_{2}(I,c)}{C_{1}(I)}-1.

(3.47)

Proof.

We first show that
M
is compact. Arguing by contradiction,
suppose
M
is non-compact. Since
M
is complete,
there is a geodesic ray in
M
, i.e., an embedded,
length-minimizing unit-speed geodesic arc
{\gamma}\colon[0,\infty)\to M
. Consider the infinite collection

\mathcal{C}(n)=\{B_{M}({\gamma}(2jn),n)\ |\ j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}

of pairwise disjoint open intrinsic balls in
M
.
Since the index of
M
is at most
I
, then the subcollection
of unstable balls in
\mathcal{C}(n)
is finite. This
implies that
M
contains stable balls of arbitrarily large
radius, a property which contradicts that for
r>R_{c}
,

B_{M}(x,r)
cannot be stable as follows from (
3.46
).
Therefore,
M
is compact.

We will divide the proof of (
3.47
) into two claims.

Claim 3.13
.

Diameter
(M)\leq 2(I+1)R_{c}
(i.e., the second inequality
in (
3.47
) holds).

Proof of Claim
3.13
.

Arguing by contradiction, suppose that there exist points
p,q\in M

at intrinsic distance
L:=d_{M}(p,q)>2(I+1)R_{c}
. Let
{\Gamma}\colon[0,L]\to M
be a geodesic arc parameterized by arc length, such that
{\Gamma}(0)=p

and
{\Gamma}(L)=q
. Choose
R>R_{c}
such that
2(I+1)R\leq L
.
Consider the following collection of
I+1

pairwise disjoint open intrinsic balls in
M
, see Figure
3

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(n)=\{B_{M}\left({\Gamma}((2j-1)R),R\right)\ |\ j=1,\ldots,I+1\}.

[h]

Figure 3:
The pairwise disjoint collection of metric balls in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(n)
.
Observe that the boundary of the last ball in the chain,

\partial B_{M}({\Gamma}((2I+1)R,R)
, intersects the image of
{\Gamma}

at the points
{\Gamma}(2IR)
,
{\Gamma}(2(I+1)R)
, and that
2(I+1)R\leq L
by construction.

Since the index of
M
is at most
I
and the
I+1
balls in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(n)

are pairwise disjoint, we deduce that at least one of these balls is
stable. This contradicts that

and (
3.46
). This
contradiction proves the claim.
∎

Claim 3.14
.

Let
\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{h}(I,r)\colon(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})\times(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

be the maximum of the right-hand-sides of (

) and
(

Observe that

Remark 3.15

Since the function

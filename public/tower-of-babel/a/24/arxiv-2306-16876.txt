Vieta-Lucas Wavelet based schemes for the numerical solution of the singular models
Shivani Aeri, Rakesh Kumar, Dumitru Baleanu, Kottakkaran Sooppy Nisar

Abstract
In this paper, numerical methods based on Vieta-Lucas wavelets are proposed for solving a class of singular differential equations. The operational matrix of the derivative for Vieta-Lucas wavelets is derived. It is employed to reduce the differential equations into the system of algebraic equations by applying the ideas of the collocation scheme, Tau scheme, and Galerkin scheme respectively. Furthermore, the convergence analysis and error estimates for Vieta-Lucas wavelets are performed. In the numerical section, the comparative analysis is presented among the different versions of the proposed Vieta-Lucas wavelet methods, and the accuracy of the approaches is evaluated by computing the errors and comparing them to the existing findings. Keywords: Vieta-Lucas wavelets, generating function, Rodrigues’ formula, collocation method, Galerkin method, Tau method.

Vieta-Lucas polynomials and properties

In this section, we give brief introduction about Vieta-Lucas polynomials, recurrence relation, orthogonality property, generating function, Vieta-Lucas differential equation and Rodrigues’ formula.

Definition 2.1
.

The Vieta-Lucas polynomials
\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)}
of degree
\rm{m}
(
\rm{m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
) can be defined as
[
40
,
41
]
:

\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)=2\cos(m\delta)},

(4)

where
\delta=\arccos{(\frac{\zeta}{2})}
and
|\zeta|\in[-2,2]
,
\delta\in[0,\pi].

Proposition 2.1
.

The recurrence relation for Vieta-Lucas polynomials
\rm{VL_{m}}(\zeta)
is given by
[
40
]
:

\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)=\zeta VL_{m-1}(\zeta)-VL_{m-2}(\zeta)},\penalty\ \penalty\ m\geq 2,

(5)

with
\rm{VL_{0}(\zeta)=2}
and

\rm{VL_{1}(\zeta)=\zeta}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The Vieta-Lucas polynomials can be represented in terms of power series expansion as
[
40
]
:

\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\frac{m(m-i-1)!\>}{i!(m-2i)!}\zeta^{m-2i}},\penalty\ \penalty\ m\geq 1.

(6)

The first few Vieta-Lucas polynomials are given as:

\begin{split}\rm{VL_{0}(\zeta)}&=2,\\
\rm{VL_{1}(\zeta)}&=\zeta,\\
\rm{VL_{2}(\zeta)}&=\zeta^{2}-2,\\
\rm{VL_{3}(\zeta)}&=\zeta^{3}-3\zeta,\\
\rm{VL_{4}(\zeta)}&=\zeta^{4}-4\zeta^{2}+2,\\
\rm{VL_{5}(\zeta)}&=\zeta^{5}-5\zeta^{3}+5\zeta,\\
\rm{VL_{6}(\zeta)}&=\zeta^{6}-6\zeta^{4}+9\zeta^{2}-2.\end{split}

Proposition 2.3
.

The Vieta-Lucas polynomials
\rm{VL_{n}(\zeta)}
and
\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)}
defined over
[-2,2]
are orthogonal in weighted sense with the weight function
\rm{w(\zeta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4-\zeta^{2}}}}
and satisfy the following condition
[
25
]
:

\rm{\langle VL_{n}(\zeta),VL_{m}(\zeta)\rangle_{w(\zeta)}=\int_{-2}^{2}VL_{n}(\zeta)VL_{m}(\zeta)w(\zeta)\,d\zeta=\begin{cases}4\pi,&n=m=0,\\
2\pi,&n=m\neq 0,\\
0,&n\neq m.\end{cases}}

(7)

Proposition 2.4
.

The generating function for Vieta-Lucas polynomials is defined as:

\rm{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}=\frac{2-\zeta t}{1-\zeta t+t^{2}}}.

(8)

Proof.

Since,

\rm{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}=\rm{VL}_{0}(\zeta)t^{0}+\rm{VL}_{1}(\zeta)t^{1}+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}}&=\rm{2+\zeta t}+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\rm{[\zeta\rm{VL}_{m-1}(\zeta)-\rm{VL}_{m-2}(\zeta)]t^{m}},\\
\implies\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}}&=\rm{2+\zeta t+\zeta}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m+1}}-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m+2}},\\
\implies\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}}&=\rm{2+\zeta t+\zeta t}[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}-\rm{VL}_{0}(\zeta)]-t^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}},\\
\implies\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}}&=\rm{2-\zeta t+(\zeta t-t^{2})}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}},\\
\implies\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)t^{m}}&=\rm{\frac{2-\zeta t}{1-\zeta t+t^{2}}}.\end{split}

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The Vieta-Lucas differential equation is defined as
[
42
]
:

\rm{(4-\zeta^{2}){\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\mskip 0.0mu2}Y}{\mathrm{d}\zeta^{\mskip 1.0mu2}\mskip-1.0mu\mskip 1.0mu}}-\zeta{\frac{\mathrm{d}Y}{\mathrm{d}\zeta}}+m^{2}Y=0},\penalty\ \penalty\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(9)

Theorem 2.1
.

The Rodrigues’ formula for Vieta-Lucas polynomials
\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)
can be obtained as:

\rm{VL_{m}(\zeta)=(-1)^{m}\>2\,\frac{m!}{(2m)!}(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{d^{m}}{d\zeta^{m}}\Big\{(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\Big\}}.

(10)

Proof.

Consider

\rm{f(\zeta)=(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{m-\frac{1}{2}}}.

On differentiating, we obtain

\rm{f^{\prime}(\zeta)=-(2m-1)\zeta(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{m-\frac{3}{2}}},

\Rightarrow\rm{(4-{\zeta}^{2})f^{\prime}(\zeta)+(2m-1)\zeta f(\zeta)=0}.

On differentiating it (m+1) times, we have

\rm{(4-{\zeta}^{2})\>D^{m+2}f(\zeta)-3\,\zeta\>D^{m+1}f(\zeta)+(m^{2}-1)\>D^{m}f(\zeta)=0,}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ where\penalty\ D\equiv\frac{d}{d\zeta}.

(11)

Let
\rm{g(\zeta)=(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\>D^{m}f(\zeta)\>}
, then (
11
) reduces to

\rm{(4-\zeta^{2})\,g^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)-\zeta\,g^{\prime}(\zeta)+m^{2}g(\zeta)=0},

which is equivalent to Vieta-Lucas differential equation. Thus, we can choose
\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)=C_{m}\,g(\zeta)
,  where
\rm C_{m}
is a constant to be determined. To find
\rm C_{m}
, the coefficients of
\rm{\zeta^{m}}
in
\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)
and
\rm{g(\zeta)}
are required to be compared.
The coefficient of
\rm{\zeta^{m}}
in
\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)
is 1.

Since,

\begin{split}\rm{g(\zeta)}&=\rm{(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\>D^{m}\,(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{m-\frac{1}{2}}},\\
&=\rm{(4-{\zeta}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\>\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\>D^{m-j}\,(2+\zeta)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\>D^{j}\,(2-\zeta)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ (by Leibniz's rule)}\\
&=\rm{(-1)^{m}\>m!\>\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m-\frac{1}{2}}{j}\binom{m-\frac{1}{2}}{m-j}(\zeta-2)^{m-j}\>(\zeta+2)^{j}}.\end{split}

Therefore, the coefficient of
\rm{\zeta^{m}}
in
\rm{g(\zeta)}
is

\rm{(-1)^{m}\>m!\>\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m-\frac{1}{2}}{j}\binom{m-\frac{1}{2}}{m-j}}.

The Vandermonde’s convolution modifies the above coefficient of
\rm{\zeta^{m}}
to

\rm{\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2}\frac{(2m)!}{m!}}.

Thus,
\rm{C_{m}=(-1)^{m}\>2\;\frac{m!}{(2m)!}}
.
Hence the Rodrigues’ formula.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The zeroes and extreme points of
\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)
in
[-2,2]
are respectively presented as

\zeta_{j}=2\cos{\left((j-\frac{1}{2})\frac{\pi}{m}\right)}
,
\zeta_{j}=2\cos{\left(j\frac{\pi}{m}\right)}
, where j=1,2,3,…, m
[
40
]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

The function
\zeta^{m}
can be expressed in terms of
\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)
as:

\rm{\zeta^{m}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor^{\star}}{m\choose j}\rm{VL}_{m-2j}(\zeta),

(12)

where ’
{\star}
’ denotes that the last term in the sum is to be halved when m is even.

Proof.

Since,

\zeta^{m}=(2\cos{\delta})^{m}=(e^{i\delta}+e^{-i\delta})^{m}.

(13)

Therefore, the use of binomial expansion gives

\begin{split}\zeta^{m}&=e^{im\delta}+{m\choose 1}e^{i(m-2)\delta}+\dots+{m\choose m-1}e^{-i(m-2)\delta}+e^{-im\delta},\\
&=(e^{im\delta}+e^{-im\delta})+{m\choose 1}(e^{i(m-2)\delta}+e^{-i(m-2)\delta})+\dots,\\
&=2\cos{m\delta}+{m\choose 1}2\cos{(m-2)\delta}+{m\choose 2}2\cos{(m-4)\delta}+\dots,\\
&=\rm{VL}_{m}(\zeta)+{m\choose 1}\rm{VL}_{m-2}(\zeta)+{m\choose 2}\rm{VL}_{m-4}(\zeta)+\dots.\end{split}

Hence the result.
∎

Shifted Vieta-Lucas polynomials

Definition 3.1
.

The shifted Vieta-Lucas polynomials defined over
[0,2]
are denoted by
\rm{VL^{*}_{m}(\zeta)}
of degree
\rm{m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
as
[
29
]
:

\rm{VL^{*}_{m}(\zeta)=VL_{m}(2\zeta-2)}.

(14)

The recurrence relation for shifted Vieta-Lucas polynomials
\rm{VL^{*}_{m}(\zeta)}
is
[
29
]
:

\rm{VL^{*}_{m}(\zeta)=(2\zeta-2)VL^{*}_{m-1}(\zeta)-VL^{*}_{m-2}(\zeta)},

(15)

provided
\rm{VL^{*}_{0}(\zeta)=2}
and

\rm{VL^{*}_{1}(\zeta)=2\zeta-2}.

The shifted Vieta-Lucas polynomials satisfy the following orthogonality property
[
29
]
:

\rm{\langle VL^{*}_{n}(\zeta),VL^{*}_{m}(\zeta)\rangle_{w^{*}(\zeta)}=\int_{0}^{2}VL^{*}_{n}(\zeta)VL^{*}_{m}(\zeta)w^{*}(\zeta)\,d\zeta=\begin{cases}4\pi,&n=m=0,\\
2\pi,&n=m\neq 0,\\
0,&n\neq m,\end{cases}}

(16)

where
\rm{w^{*}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\zeta-\zeta^{2}}}}
is the weight function of shifted Vieta-Lucas polynomials.

Vieta-Lucas wavelets and function approximation

Wavelet is a type of function that is derived through the dilation and translation of the mother wavelet. The continuous wavelet family with dilation parameter
h
and translation parameter
r
is defined as
[
43
]
:

\gamma_{h,r}(\zeta)=|h|^{-1/2}\gamma(\frac{\zeta-r}{h}),\hskip 14.22636pth,r\in\mathbb{R},h\neq 0.

(17)

If
h=h_{0}^{-k},r=sr_{0}h_{0}^{-k},h_{0}>1,r_{0}>0
, then the discrete wavelet family consists of the following members:

\gamma_{k,s}(\zeta)=|h_{0}|^{k/2}\gamma(h_{0}\zeta-sr_{0}),\hskip 14.22636ptk,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

(18)

where
\gamma_{k,s}(x)
constitutes the wavelet basis for
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. For a specific choice of
h_{0}=2
and
r_{0}=1
,
\gamma_{k,s}(x)
constitutes an orthonormal basis.

Definition 4.1
.

The Vieta-Lucas wavelets
\rm{\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)=\Upsilon(k,s,m,\zeta)}
is defined on the interval [0,2) as:

\rm{\Upsilon_{s,m}}(\zeta)=\begin{cases}\rm{2^{\frac{k}{2}}\>\widehat{\rm{VL}}_{m}(\>2^{k}\zeta-\hat{s}\>),}&\rm{\frac{\hat{s}-2}{2^{k}}\leq\zeta<\frac{\hat{s}+2}{2^{k}}},\\
0,&\text{Otherwise},\end{cases}

(19)

where

\widehat{\rm{VL}}_{m}(\zeta)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}},&m=0,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\rm{VL_{m}}(\zeta),&m\geq 1,\end{cases}

m=0,1,2,\dots,M-1;
M be the maximum order of Vieta-Lucas polynomials,
s=1,2,\dots,2^{k-1}
;
k=1,2,3,\dots;

\hat{s}=2(2s-1)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Vieta-Lucas wavelets form an orthogonal set with respect to the weight functions
\rm{w_{s}(\zeta)=w(2^{k}\zeta-\hat{s})}
.

Definition 4.2
.

A function
\rm{Y(\zeta)}
defined over
{L^{2}}_{w_{s}}[0,2]
can be written in terms of Vieta-Lucas wavelets series as:

\rm{Y(\zeta)=\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Lambda_{s,m}\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)},

with

\rm{\Lambda_{s,m}=\langle Y(\zeta),\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)\rangle_{w_{s}(\zeta)}=\int_{0}^{2}Y(\zeta)\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)w_{s}(\zeta)\,d\zeta},

(22)

where
\langle*,*\rangle
denotes the inner product.

The truncated form of Vieta-Lucas wavelet series can be written as:

\rm{\bar{Y}(\zeta)}\cong\rm{\sum_{s=1}^{2^{k-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\Lambda_{s,m}\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)=\rm{\Lambda^{T}\Upsilon(\zeta)}},

where
\Lambda
and
\Upsilon(\zeta)
are

\displaystyle\rm{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\rm{\Upsilon(\zeta)}

\displaystyle=\rm{[\Upsilon_{1,0}(\zeta),\dots,\Upsilon_{1,M-1}(\zeta),\Upsilon_{2,0}(\zeta),\dots,\Upsilon_{2,M-1}(\zeta),\dots,\Upsilon_{2^{k-1},0}(\zeta),\dots,\Upsilon_{2^{k-1},M-1}(\zeta)]^{T}}.

5
Vieta-Lucas wavelet based operational matrix

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Upsilon(\zeta)
be the Vieta-Lucas wavelets vector defined in (
25
). Then the derivative of the vector
\Upsilon(\zeta)
can be expressed as:

\frac{\rm{d\Upsilon(\zeta)}}{\rm{d\zeta}}=\rm{D\Upsilon(\zeta)},

(26)

where
D
is the
\eta\times\eta
matrix given by

\rm{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\rm{F}&0&\dots&0\\
0&\rm{F}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\rm{F}\end{pmatrix}

in which
F
is
M\times M
square matrix whose
(u,v)^{th}
element is defined as:

\rm{F_{u,v}}=\begin{cases}\rm{\frac{2^{k}(u-1)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{u-1}}\sqrt{\alpha_{v-1}}}},&\rm{u=2,\dots,M;}\hskip 2.84544pt\rm{v=1,2,\dots,u-1;}\hskip 2.84544pt\rm{(u+v)}=\text{odd},\\
0,&\text{Otherwise}.\end{cases}

(27)

Proof.

Using shifted Vieta-Lucas polynomials vector,
\Upsilon(\zeta)
can be rewritten as

\rm{\Upsilon_{u}(\zeta)=\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)=2^{\frac{k}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha_{m}}}VL^{*}_{m}(2^{k-1}\zeta-2s+2)\chi_{[\frac{s-1}{2^{k-2}},\frac{s}{2^{k-2}}]}},

(28)

where
\rm{u=(s-1)M+(m+1)}
;
\rm{s=1,2,3,....2^{k-1}}
,
\rm{m=0,1,2,....M-1}
and

\rm{\chi_{[\frac{s-1}{2^{k-2}},\frac{s}{2^{k-2}}]}}=\begin{cases}1,&\rm{\zeta\in[\frac{s-1}{2^{k-2}},\frac{s}{2^{k-2}}]},\\
0,&\text{Otherwise}.\end{cases}

(29)

On differentiating (
28
) with respect to
\rm{\zeta}
, we obtain

\rm{\frac{d\Upsilon_{u}(\zeta)}{d\zeta}=2^{\frac{3k}{2}-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha_{m}}}\frac{d}{d\zeta}VL^{*}_{m}(2^{k-1}\zeta-2s+2)\chi_{[\frac{s-1}{2^{k-2}},\frac{s}{2^{k-2}}]}},

(30)

since (
30
) vanishes outside the interval
\rm{\zeta\in[\frac{s-1}{2^{k-2}},\frac{s}{2^{k-2}}]}
. Thus, the nonzero components of Vieta-Lucas wavelets expansion exist only in the interval
\rm{\zeta\in[\frac{s-1}{2^{k-2}},\frac{s}{2^{k-2}}]}
i.e,
\rm{\Upsilon_{i}(\zeta)}
for
i=(s-1)M+1,(s-1)M+2,\dots,sM.
Vieta-Lucas wavelets expansion can now be written as

\rm{\frac{d\Upsilon_{u}(\zeta)}{d\zeta}=\sum_{i=(s-1)M+1}^{sM}a_{i}\Upsilon_{i}(\zeta)}.

Here,

\rm{\frac{d}{d\zeta}\Upsilon_{u}(\zeta)=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for}\hskip 5.69046ptu=1,M+1,2M+1,\dots,(2^{k-1}-1)M+1},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{because}\penalty\ \rm{\frac{d}{d\zeta}VL_{0}(\zeta)=0}\penalty\ \text{everywhere.}

So, the first row of matrix
F
is zero.

Now, the first derivative of shifted Vieta-Lucas polynomials can be expressed as

\rm{\frac{d}{d\zeta}VL^{*}_{m}(\zeta)=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j+m=odd\end{subarray}}^{M-1}\frac{m}{\alpha_{j}}VL^{*}_{j}(\zeta)}.

(31)

Using (
31
) in (
30
), we obtain

\rm{\frac{d\Upsilon_{u}(\zeta)}{d\zeta}}=\rm{2^{\frac{3k}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha_{m}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j+m=odd\end{subarray}}^{M-1}\rm{\frac{m}{\alpha_{j}}VL^{*}_{m}(2^{k-1}\zeta-2s+2)\chi_{[\frac{s-1}{2^{k-2}},\frac{s}{2^{k-2}}]}},}

(32)

which can be rewritten as

\rm{\frac{d\Upsilon_{u}(\zeta)}{d\zeta}=2^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v=1\\
u+v=odd\end{subarray}}^{u-1}\frac{u-1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{u-1}}\sqrt{\alpha_{v-1}}}\Upsilon_{(s-1)M+v}(\zeta).}

Therefore

\rm{\frac{d\Upsilon_{u}(\zeta)}{d\zeta}=F_{u,v}\Upsilon_{(s-1)M+v}(\zeta),}

with

\rm{F_{u,v}}=\begin{cases}\rm{2^{k}\frac{u-1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{u-1}}\sqrt{\alpha_{v-1}}}},&\rm{u=2,\dots,M;\hskip 2.84544ptv=1,2,\dots,u-1;\hskip 2.84544pt(u+v)=\text{odd}},\\
0,&\text{Otherwise}.\end{cases}

(33)

which leads to the desired expression.
∎

For example, if we select k=2 and M=3, then the discrete members of shifted Vieta-Lucas wavelets can be written as:

\displaystyle\rm{\Upsilon_{1}(\zeta)=\Upsilon_{1,0}(\zeta)}=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rm{\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}},&0\leq\zeta<1,\\
0,&\text{Otherwise}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\rm{\Upsilon_{2}(\zeta)=\Upsilon_{1,1}(\zeta)}=

Numerical Scheme

Consider the most general appearance of second order singular differential equations as

\rm{Y^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)+\frac{\mu}{\zeta}Y^{\prime}(\zeta)+f(\zeta,Y(\zeta))=g(\zeta),\hskip 14.22636pt0\leq\zeta\leq L}.

(35)

\rm{Y(\zeta)|_{\zeta=0}=\alpha_{0},\>Y^{\prime}(\zeta)|_{\zeta=0}=\alpha_{1}},

(36)

\rm{Y(\zeta)|_{\zeta=0}=\beta_{0},\>Y(\zeta)|_{\zeta=L}=\beta_{1}}.

(37)

35

37

\rm{\bar{Y}(\zeta)=\sum_{s=1}^{2^{k-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\Lambda_{s,m}\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)=\Lambda^{T}\Upsilon(\zeta)}.

Now by using (
34

\rm{\bar{Y}^{\prime}(\zeta)=\Lambda^{\rm{T}}D\Upsilon(\zeta)}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \rm{\bar{Y}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)=\Lambda^{\rm{T}}D^{(2)}\Upsilon(\zeta)}.

(39)

can be obtained by using equations (
38

39

35

\rm{\Lambda^{T}\Upsilon(0)=\alpha_{0},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \Lambda^{T}D\Upsilon(0)=\alpha_{1}},

When
\rm{R(\zeta)}
is zero, the exact solution is obtained, although it is quite difficult to get
\rm{R(\zeta)}
identically zero. So our primary focus is to make the residual value as small as possible. Thus, we utilize the weighted residual methods in order to minimize the residual function. Now, the weighted residual equation is written as

\rm{\langle W,R(\zeta)\rangle}=\int_{0}^{2}WR(\zeta)\,d\zeta=0,\penalty\ \penalty\

(43)

\rm{W}

Thus, three weighted residual approaches are presented which are based on the different choices of

Approach - I
: Collocation Approach

In this approach, the weighted function is chosen as Dirac delta(
\delta

\rm{\langle\delta(\zeta-\zeta_{i}),R(\zeta)\rangle=\int_{0}^{2}\delta(\zeta-\zeta_{i})R(\zeta)\,d\zeta,\penalty\ \penalty\ i=1,2,3,\dots,2^{k-1}M-2,}

\rm{R(\zeta_{i})}=0,\hskip 14.22636pti=1,2,3,\dots,2^{k-1}M-2.

(44)

Here, the extrema of Vieta-Lucas polynomials are chosen as the collocation points.
Now, the
\rm{2^{k-1}M}
system of equations (equation (
44

41
) or (
42

\rm{Y_{VLWC}(\zeta)}
solution.

Approach - II
: Tau Approach

In this approach, the weighted function is chosen to be the same as the Vieta-Lucas wavelets
\Upsilon_{i}
, which yields
\rm{(2^{k-1}M-2)}

\rm{\langle\Upsilon_{i},R(\zeta)\rangle=\int_{0}^{2}\Upsilon_{i}\penalty\ R(\zeta)\,d\zeta=0,\hskip 19.91684pti=1,2,3,\dots,2^{k-1}M-2.}

\rm{2^{k-1}M}

45

41

\rm{Y_{VLWT}(\zeta)}

Approach - III

The main idea behind this approach is to expand the solutions not only in terms of usual Vieta-Lucas wavelets expansion, but with some combinations of Vieta-Lucas wavelets which satisfy the boundary requirements. In this approach, the weighted functions are taken as the trial series solution, which gives

\rm{\langle\Psi_{i},R(\zeta)\rangle=\int_{0}^{2}\Psi_{i}\penalty\ {R}(\zeta)\,d\zeta=0,\hskip 19.91684pti=1,2,3,\dots,2^{k-1}M,}

Now, solving
\rm{2^{k-1}M}

Theorem 7.1
.

Let

\rm{Y(\zeta)=\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Lambda_{s,m}\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)}.

Proof.

Suppose
\rm{Y(\zeta)}\in L^{2}_{w_{s}}[0,2]

\rm{Y(\zeta)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\Lambda_{s,m}\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)}

\rm{\Lambda_{s,m}=\langle Y(\zeta),\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)\rangle_{w_{s}(\zeta)}}

\rm{\Upsilon_{s,m}(\zeta)=\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta)}

\rm{\varsigma_{j}=\langle Y(\zeta),\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta)\rangle}

The sequence of partial sum

\rm{\rho_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\varsigma_{j}\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta)}.

For s
>

\rm{\left\lVert\rho_{s}-\rho_{m}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{j=m+1}^{s}|\varsigma_{j}|^{2}}.

Now,

\rm{\left\lVert\sum_{j=m+1}^{s}\varsigma_{j}\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta)\right\rVert^{2}=\langle\sum_{j=m+1}^{s}\varsigma_{j}\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta),\sum_{j=m+1}^{s}\varsigma_{j}\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta)\rangle=\sum_{j=m+1}^{s}|\varsigma_{j}|^{2},\hskip 5.69046pts>m.}

From Bessel’s inequality, we know
\rm{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\varsigma_{j}|^{2}}

\rm{\left\lVert\sum_{j=m+1}^{s}\varsigma_{j}\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta)\right\rVert^{2}}\rightarrow 0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{as}\hskip 5.69046pts\rightarrow\infty.

\end{split}

Which implies

\rm{\langle\rho-Y(\zeta),\Upsilon(\zeta)\rangle=0}.

Therefore,
\rm{Y(\zeta)=\rho}

\rm{\sum_{j=1}^{s}\varsigma_{j}\Upsilon_{j}(\zeta)}

\rm{Y(\zeta)}

Conclusion

In this work, the second-order SDEs are analyzed by using the novel numerical schemes which are based on Vieta-Lucas wavelets. The operational matrix approach combined with three weighted residual approaches is used to solve singular IVPs and singular BVPs. Simultaneously two convergence theorems are proved, and the upper bound for the tolerable error is explored. The numerical examples are taken to demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approaches. The approximate solutions derived through the proposed methods are found to be extremely accurate, which proves the accuracy and applicability.

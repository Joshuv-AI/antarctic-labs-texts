Light-front approach to relativistic electrodynamics
Gaetano Fiore

Abstract
We illustrate how our recent light-front approach simplifies relativistic electrodynamics with an electromagnetic (EM) field F μ ​ ν F^{\mu\nu} that is the sum of a (even very intense) plane travelling wave F t μ ​ ν ​ ( c ​ t − z ) F_{t}^{\mu\nu}(ct\!-\!z) and a static part F s μ ​ ν ​ ( x , y , z ) F_{s}^{\mu\nu}(x,y,z) ; it adopts the light-like coordinate ξ = c ​ t − z \xi=ct\!-\!z instead of time t t as an independent variable. This can be applied to several cases of extreme acceleration, both in vacuum and in a cold diluted plasma hit by a very short and intense laser pulse (slingshot effect, plasma wave-breaking and laser wake-field acceleration, etc.)

3.3
Comparison with the ‘comoving frame’ approach

The Lorentz-Maxwell and continuity equations in a (homogeneous)
plasma admit
[
26
]
travelling wave solutions where the
EM field (plane wave laser pulse) has the form
{\bf E}(t,{\bf x})\!=\!{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(v_{l}t\!-\!z)
,
{\bf B}(t,{\bf x})=\mathbf{k}\wedge{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(v_{l}t\!-\!z)
; i.e. the pulse travels with a (constant) velocity
{\bf v}_{l}=v_{l}\mathbf{k}
,
v_{l}\leq c
.
If
v_{l}\!<\!c
, in studying the electrodynamics (plasma waves, LWFA, …)
induced by such a pulse
it is common and useful to adopt the ‘comoving frame’ approach.
This consists in describing the physics not w.r.t. the inertial reference frame
\cal{R}
of the laboratory, but w.r.t. a one
\cal{R}^{\prime}
comoving with the pulse, i.e. moving
with velocity
{\bf v}_{l}
with respect to (w.r.t.)
\cal{R}
. Setting
\beta_{l}=v_{l}/c
,
\gamma_{l}^{-1}=\sqrt{1\!-\!\beta_{l}^{2}}
, the spacetime coordinates w.r.t.
\cal{R},\cal{R}^{\prime}
are related
by the Lorentz transformation

ct^{\prime}=\gamma_{l}(ct-\beta_{l}z),\qquad{\bf x}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}{}^{\prime}={\bf x}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp},\qquad z^{\prime}=\gamma_{l}(z-\beta_{l}ct),

(70)

and more generally the components of all 4-tensors [in particular the canonically conjugated 4-momentum
(H,c{\bf P})
and the EM tensor
(F^{\mu\nu})
] w.r.t.
\cal{R},\cal{R}^{\prime}
are related by this Lorentz transformation and can be expressed in terms of
{\rm x}^{\prime}
; the pulse part
{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}{}^{\prime}
of the EM field is a function of
t^{\prime}
only.
Therefore adopting
t^{\prime}
as the independent variable has the same advantage described before, i.e.
{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}{}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})
appears in the equations of motion of
all charged particles (including the plasma constituents) as a known forcing term;
correspondingly, the known expression
{\bf A}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}{}^{\prime}({\rm x}^{\prime})={\bm{\alpha}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}{}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})
appears in the Hamiltonian of all charged particles. However, this approach is no more applicable
when
v=c
, for which the transformation (
70
) is ill-defined.

On the contrary, although
\xi=ct\!-\!\beta_{l}z\propto t^{\prime}
can no more be interpreted as the time coordinate w.r.t.
an inertial reference frame, in our approach adopting
\xi
instead of
t^{\prime}
as the independent variable keeps this advantage and allows
to take the limit
v\to c
.

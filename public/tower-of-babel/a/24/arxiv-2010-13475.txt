On the Non-Commuting Graph of the Group U 6 ​ n U_{6n}
Sanhan Khasraw, C.H. Jaf, Nor Haniza Sarmin, Ibrahim Gambo

Abstract
A non-commuting graph of a finite group G G is a graph whose vertices are non-central elements of G G and two vertices are adjacent if they don’t commute in G G . In this paper, we study the non-commuting graph of the group U 6 ​ n U_{6n} and explore some of its properties including the independent number, clique and chromatic numbers. Also, the general formula of the resolving polynomial of the non-commuting graph of the group U 6 ​ n U_{6n} are provided. Furthermore, we find the detour index, eccentric connectivity, total eccentricity and independent polynomials of the graph.



Keywords
: Non-commuting graph, independent number, chromatic number, clique number, resolving polynomial of a graph.

Lemma 2.1
.

For the group
U_{6n}
, and

1.

Z(U_{6n})=\langle a^{2}\rangle

2.

C_{U_{6n}}(a^{2r+1})=\langle a\rangle,

C_{U_{6n}}(a^{2r+1}b)=\langle a^{2}\rangle\cdot\langle\{a^{2s+1}b:0\leq s\leq n-1\}\rangle

C_{U_{6n}}(a^{2r+1}b^{2})=\langle a^{2}\rangle\cdot\langle\{a^{2s+1}b^{2}:0\leq s\leq n-1\}\rangle

5.

C_{U_{6n}}(a^{2r}b)=\langle a^{2}\rangle\cdot\langle\{a^{2s}b,a^{2s}b^{2}:0\leq s\leq n-1\}\rangle

The following useful lemma is used in calculating the degree of vertices in

Lemma 2.2

Let

The above lemmas lead to the following

Corollary 2.3

Theorem 2.4
.

For

\Gamma=\Gamma({U_{6n}})

Theorem 2.5

For

Some polynomials of the non-commuting graph
\Gamma(U_{6n})

In this section some properties of non-commuting graphs of
U_{6n}
are explored, namely the detour index, the eccentric connectivity, the total eccentricity and the independent polynomials.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma({U_{6n}})
be a non-commuting graph of
U_{6n}
. Then,
D(u,v)=5n-1
for any
u,v\in\Gamma({U_{6n}})
.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.5
, one can see that no two vertices in
\Omega_{i}
are adjacent, and each vertex in
\Omega_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex in
\Omega_{j}
for
i\neq j
and
i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. Then for all
u,v\in\Omega
, where
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{3}\cup\Omega_{4}
, there is a
u-v
path of length
5n-1
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma({U_{6n}})
be a non-commuting graph of
U_{6n}
. Then,
D(\Gamma({U_{6n}}),x)=\frac{5n(5n-1)}{2}x^{5n-1}
.

Proof.

We have that
|\Gamma({U_{6n}})|=5n
. Then there are
{{5n}\choose{2}}=\frac{5n(5n-1)}{2}
possibilities of choosing distinct pairs of vertices from
\Gamma({U_{6n}})
. By Lemma
4.1
,
D(u,v)=5n-1
for any distinct pairs of
u,v\in\Gamma({U_{6n}})
. Thus,
D(\Gamma({U_{6n}}),x)=\sum_{\{u,v\}}x^{D(u,v)}={{5n}\choose{2}}x^{5n-1}=\frac{5n(5n-1)}{2}x^{5n-1}
.
∎

Theorem
4.2
leads to the following result.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma({U_{6n}})
be a non-commuting graph of
U_{6n}
. Then,
dd(\Gamma({U_{6n}}))=\frac{5n(5n-1)^{2}}{2}
.

Lemma 4.4

On selection dynamics for a nonlocal phenotype-structured model
Shen Bian, Jiale Bu

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the analysis of the long-time behavior of a phenotypic-structured model where phenotypic changes do not occur. We give a mathematical description of the process in which the best adapted trait is selected in a given environment created by the total population. It is exhibited that the long-time limit of the unique solution to the nonlocal equation is given by a Dirac mass centered at the peak of the fitness within or at the boundary of the region where the initial data is positive. Specially, If the peak of the fitness can’t be in the support of the solution, then the infinite time blow-up of the solution occurs near the boundary of the region where the solution is positive. Moreover, our numerical results facilitate a deeper understanding of identifying the position of the centers.

In this paper, we mainly focus on an extension of a selection principle. We use
u(x,t)
to represent the density of individuals with the trait

\rho(t)

\frac{b(x)}{1+\rho(t)}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t)=\left(\frac{b(x)}{1+c_{0}\rho(t)}-d(x)\rho(t)\right)u(x,t)=R(x,\rho(t))u(x,t),&x\in{\mathbb{R}},~t>0,\\

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}.\end{array}\right.

The term
\frac{b(x)}{1+c_{0}\rho(t)}-d(x)\rho(t)

Within the framework of (

(i)

The problem (

(ii)

(iii)

Resorting to the structure of (

\rho(t).
By exploiting the decreasing of the fitness with respect to
\rho(t)
, we prove our desired results via the monotonicity of the Lyapunov functional with respect to time.

The structure of this paper is organized as follows. We commence with a physiologically structured population where a selection principle can be proved (see Theorem

Theorem 2

(Asymptotic behaviour)
.

Under assumptions (
6

9
), we assume that there exist

such that

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{m}\leq\rho(0)\leq\bar{\rho}_{M},

(11)

Then the solution to (
1

\displaystyle\rho_{m}\leq\rho(t)\leq\rho_{M},\quad\forall~t\geq 0

(12)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{m}=\min\left(r_{m},\rho(0)\right),~~(1+r_{m})r_{m}=\frac{b_{m}}{d_{M}},

(13)

\displaystyle\rho_{M}=\max\left(r_{M},\rho(0)\right),~~(1+r_{M})r_{M}=\frac{b_{M}}{d_{m}}.

(14)

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\rho(t)\to\bar{\rho},~~~u(x,t)\rightharpoonup\overline{\rho}\delta(x-\bar{x}),~~\text{as}~~t\to\infty.

(15)

Proof.

The proof can be divided into 5 steps. In Step 1, we first give a priori estimate for
\rho(t)
which plays very important role in proving the long-time limit of
\rho(t)
. Subsequently, a Lyapunov functional is constructed in Steps 2-3 to deduce that there exists a limit for
\rho(t)
as time goes to infinity. Then, Steps 4-5 identify the limit for
\rho(t)
and the weak limit for
u(x,t)
.

Step 1
(A priori estimate for
\rho(t)
)  In order to prove (
12
), we first integrate the equation (
1
) in
x
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{b(x)}{1+\rho(t)}-d(x)\rho(t)\right)u(x,t)dx.

(16)

Using assumption (
6
) we further estimate

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{b_{M}}{1+\rho(t)}-d_{m}\rho(t)\right)u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{b_{M}}{d_{m}}-\rho(1+\rho)\right)\frac{d_{m}\rho}{1+\rho}

(17)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dt}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{b_{m}}{1+\rho(t)}-d_{M}\rho(t)\right)u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{b_{m}}{d_{M}}-\rho(1+\rho)\right)\frac{d_{M}\rho}{1+\rho}.

(18)

Therefore we find that for all times

\displaystyle\min\left(r_{m},\rho(0)\right)\leq\rho(t)\leq\max\left(r_{M},\rho(0)\right)

(19)

where
r_{m}(1+r_{m})=\frac{b_{m}}{d_{M}}
and
r_{M}(1+r_{M})=\frac{b_{M}}{d_{m}}
.

Step 2
(A Lyapunov functional)  Based on (
19
), a Lyapunov functional is constructed to derive the existence of the limit for
\rho(t).
We first introduce a function

P(\rho)=\frac{1}{3}\rho^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho

\displaystyle\rho P(\rho)^{\prime}+P(\rho)=Q(\rho):=\rho^{2}+\rho

(20)

on the interval

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left[\frac{b(x)}{d(x)}-P(\rho(t))\right]u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{b(x)}{d(x)}-P(\rho(t))-\rho(t)P^{\prime}(\rho(t))\right)\left(\frac{b(x)}{1+\rho(t)}-d(x)\rho(t)\right)u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{1+\rho(t)}{d(x)}\left[\frac{b(x)}{1+\rho(t)}-d(x)\rho(t)\right]^{2}u(x,t)dx\geq 0.

(21)

Consequently,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left[\frac{b(x)}{d(x)}-P(\rho(t))\right]u(x,t)dx
is bounded and non-decreasing in time, and thus converges as

. Namely,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left[\frac{b(x)}{d(x)}-P(\rho(t))\right]u(x,t)dx\to L\in{\mathbb{R}},

(22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left[\frac{b(x)}{1+\rho(t)}-d(x)\rho(t)\right]^{2}u(x,t)dxdt<\infty.

(23)

Weights of mod p p automorphic forms and partial Hasse invariants
Naoki Imai, Jean-Stefan Koskivirta

Abstract
For a connected, reductive group G G over a finite field endowed with a cocharacter μ \mu , we define the zip cone of ( G , μ ) (G,\mu) as the cone of all possible weights of mod p p automorphic forms on the stack of G G -zips. This cone is conjectured to coincide with the cone of weights of characteristic p p automorphic forms for Hodge-type Shimura varieties of good reduction. We prove in full generality that the cone of weights of characteristic 0 0 automorphic forms is contained in the zip cone, which gives further evidence to this conjecture. Furthermore, we determine exactly when the zip cone is generated by the weights of partial Hasse invariants, which is a group-theoretical generalization of a result of Diamond–Kassaei and Goldring–Koskivirta.

Introduction

This paper is aimed at understanding automorphic forms in characteristic
p

The following conjecture is expected, but we could not find a reference for it.

Conjecture 1
.

One has

The inclusion

(
[
GK18

)
.

One has
{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}

In the case of Hilbert–Blumenthal Shimura varieties one has

, hence Conjecture 1 is compatible with the result of Diamond–Kassaei mentioned above. Aside from this case, Goldring and the second-named author showed this conjecture for Picard modular surfaces at a split prime and Siegel threefolds (
[

, Theorem D]

and unitary Shimura varieties of signature

with

[

]
.

We now describe our results more precisely. We defined in
[
GK19a
]
the stack of
G
-zip flags, denoted by
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
, which is a group-theoretical analogue of the flag space of Ekedahl–van der Geer (
[
EvdG09
]
). There is a natural projection

\pi\colon\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}

whose fibers are flag varieties isomorphic to
P/B
. The stack
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
carries a family of line bundles
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{flag}}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
such that
\pi_{*}({\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{flag}}(\lambda))={\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)
. In particular, we can identify
H^{0}(\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))
and
H^{0}(\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{flag}}(\lambda))
. Moreover,
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
admits a stratification
({\mathcal{F}}_{w})_{w\in W}
analogous to the Bruhat decomposition, where
W=W(G,T)
is the Weyl group of
G
. By
[
IK21b
]
, there exists a family of partial Hasse invariants
\{h_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}
(where
\Delta
is the set of simple roots). Specifically,
h_{\alpha}
is a section of
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{flag}}(\lambda_{\alpha})
(for some
\lambda_{\alpha}\in X^{*}(T)
) whose vanishing locus is the closure of a single codimension one stratum in
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
(and each such stratum is cut out by exactly one of the
h_{\alpha}
). The cone generated by the
(\lambda_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Delta}
is called the partial Hasse invariant cone
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}
(Definition
3.6.1
). One has by construction
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset C_{\mathsf{zip}}
. As an analogue of
[
DK23
, Corollary 8.3]
, we ask whether
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
holds in general. Let
w_{0,L}
be the longest element in the Weyl group
W_{L}=W(L,T)
. Let
\sigma
denote the action of Frobenius on the based root datum of
(G,B,T)
. We show:

Theorem 1

(Theorem
4.3.1
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

One has
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
.

(ii)

One has
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{GS}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}
.

(iii)

L
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\sigma
acts on
\Delta_{L}
by
-w_{0,L}
.

Pairs
(G,\mu)
satisfying condition (iii) are called of Hasse-type. For a Shimura variety
S_{K}
as above, we always have
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. We deduce that a necessary condition for
{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
to be generated by partial Hasse invariants is that
(G,\mu)
is of Hasse-type. A classification of Hasse-type cases is given in an appendix by Wushi Goldring (see §
A
). For example, orthogonal Shimura varieties give rise to pairs

of Hasse-type (see §

). Condition (ii) has also an interpretation for Shimura varieties. One can show in general that

(
[

, Proposition 1.8.3]
) and hence
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{C})\subset{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Since it is expected that
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{C})={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{GS}}
, Condition (ii) is necessary for
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}={\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
to hold. From Conjecture 1 and Conjecture 2, we expect that the containment

should hold in general, which is now a purely group-theoretical statement. We confirm this expectation:

Theorem 2

(Theorem
6.4.3
)
.

For general
(G,\mu)
, we have
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{GS}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
.

This theorem gives further evidence for Conjecture 2. In
[

, Theorem 2 was proved only when

. We now explain the proof of Theorem 2. The proof uses a general technique that makes it possible to reduce questions pertaining to

to the case of a split group. In the split case, Theorem 2 is already known by
[
Kos19
, Corollary 3.5.6]
. We explain how we can reduce to the case of a split group. Denote by

the largest algebraic subgroup defined over

. It is a Levi subgroup of

containing

. There is a cocharacter
\mu_{0}
with centralizer

, and we consider the pair

X_{+,L}^{*}(T)\cap\Big(w_{0,L}w_{0,L_{0}}{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}(G_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}},\mu_{0})\Big)\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}.

This theorem is useful in general to reduce questions on

Our final result is the construction of natural mod

is a certain finite (generally non-smooth) subgroup of

. There is an integer

, such that the norm

is a section of
{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(d\lambda)

) over the

of

(see §

Theorem 4

(Proposition

)
.

The section

extends to

if and only if for all

one has

\sum_{w\in W_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\sum_{i=0}^{r_{\alpha}-1}q^{i+\ell(w)}\ \langle w\lambda,\sigma^{i}(\alpha^{\vee})\rangle\leq 0.

(1)

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{hw}}
be the set of

-dominant characters

satisfying the above inequality (
1
). Theorem 4 shows that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{hw}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
, which provides another natural subcone of

. We obtain a family of interesting automorphic forms

in characteristic

of weight

(by pullback via

). There is also an analogue of Theorem 4 for the lowest weight vector (§

), and we define the lowest weight cone

similarly. When

is defined over

, one has

The motivation for introducing the family
(\mathbf{f}_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is the following. As mentioned above, Diamond–Kassaei showed in
[
DK17
]
that the weight of any Hilbert modular form in characteristic

is spanned by the weights of partial Hasse invariants. This is also true for the Siegel-type Shimura variety
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
, but it fails for
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
when

. In the case
n=3
, Goldring and the second-named author showed that the weight of any automorphic form for

is spanned by the weights of partial Hasse invariants and of the forms
(\mathbf{f}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{hw}}}
. Therefore, these forms seem to have some significance for more general groups. Moreover, the vanishing locus of
\mathbf{f}_{\lambda}
is an interesting subvariety stable by Hecke operators, that we plan to investigate in future papers.

We briefly explain the content of each section. In §2 we review the stack of
G
-zips, vector bundles thereon and the connection with Shimura varieties. Section 3 is dedicated to the study of the cone
C_{\zip}
, called the zip cone. We explain the motivation for introducing this set. We define several related subcones which arise naturally. We define automorphic forms on
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
attached to highest weight vectors. In section 4, we consider pairs
(G,\mu)
of Hasse-type and we give a complete characterization in terms of
C_{\zip}
. In section 5, similarly to the highest weight vectors, we show that the lowest weight vectors give rise naturally to certain automorphic forms on
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
. In section 6, we study pairs
(G,\mu)
where
G
is the Weil restriction of a reductive group defined over an extension. This machinery makes it possible to reduce several questions to the case of a split group. Using this, we can check in full generality the expectation that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\GS}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\zip}
. Finally, in the last section, we illustrate the results in the case of a unitary group

and for odd orthogonal groups. In the appendix by Wushi Goldring, we give an exhaustive classification of pairs
(G,\mu)
of Hasse-type.

2.2

We recall some facts about the stack of

2.2.1
Definitions

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In this paper, a zip datum is a tuple
{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(G,P,L,Q,M,\varphi)
consisting of the following objects:

(i)

P\subset G_{k}
and
Q\subset G_{k}
are parabolic subgroups of
G_{k}
.

(ii)

L\subset P
and
M\subset Q
are Levi subgroups such that
L^{(q)}=M
.

For an algebraic group
H
, denote by
R_{\mathrm{u}}(H)
the unipotent radical of
H
. If
P^{\prime}\subset G_{k}
is a parabolic subgroup with Levi subgroup
L^{\prime}\subset P^{\prime}
, any

can be written uniquely as
x=\overline{x}u
with
\overline{x}\in L^{\prime}
and
u\in R_{\mathrm{u}}(P^{\prime})
. We denote by
\theta^{P^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}\colon P^{\prime}\to L^{\prime}
the map
x\mapsto\overline{x}
. Since
M=L^{(q)}
, we have a Frobenius isogeny
\varphi\colon L\to M
. Put

E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(x,y)\in P\times Q\mid\varphi(\theta^{P}_{L}(x))=\theta^{Q}_{M}(y)\}.

(2.2.1)

Equivalently,
E
is the subgroup of
P\times Q
generated by
R_{\mathrm{u}}(P)\times R_{\mathrm{u}}(Q)
and elements of the form
(a,\varphi(a))
with
a\in L
. Let
G\times G
act on
G
by
(a,b)\cdot g\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}agb^{-1}
, and let
E
act on
G
by restricting this action to

. The stack of
G
-zips of type
{\mathcal{Z}}
(
[
PWZ11
]
,
[
PWZ15
]
) can be defined as the quotient stack

\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}=\left[E\backslash G_{k}\right].

There is an equivalent definition of
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}
in terms of torsors. The stack
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}
is the stack over
k
such that for all
k
-scheme
S
, the groupoid
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{{\mathcal{Z}}}(S)

is the category of tuples
\underline{I}=(I,I_{P},I_{Q},\iota)
, where

is a
G
-torsor over

,
I_{P}\subset I
and
I_{Q}\subset I
are respectively a

-subtorsor and a

-subtorsor of

, and

is an isomorphism of

2.2.2
Cocharacter datum

A

is a pair
(G,\mu)
where
G
is a reductive connected group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\mu\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},k}\to G_{k}
is a cocharacter. One can attach to
(G,\mu)
a zip datum
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}
, defined as follows. First, denote by
P_{+}(\mu)
(resp.
P_{-}(\mu)
) the unique parabolic subgroup of
G_{k}
such that
P_{+}(\mu)(k)
(resp.
P_{-}(\mu)(k)
) consists of the elements

satisfying that the map

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},k}\to G_{k};\ t\mapsto\mu(t)g\mu(t)^{-1}\quad(\textrm{resp. }t\mapsto\mu(t)^{-1}g\mu(t))

extends to a morphism of varieties
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}\to G_{k}
. We obtain a pair of parabolics
(P_{+}(\mu),P_{-}(\mu))
in
G_{k}
whose intersection
P_{+}(\mu)\cap P_{-}(\mu)=L(\mu)
is the centralizer of
\mu
(it is a common Levi subgroup of
P_{+}(\mu)
and
P_{-}(\mu)
). Set
P\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}P_{-}(\mu)
,
Q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(P_{+}(\mu))^{(q)}
,
L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L(\mu)
and
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(L(\mu))^{(q)}
. The tuple
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(G,P,L,Q,M,\varphi)
is a zip datum, which we call the zip datum attached to the cocharacter datum
(G,\mu)
. We write simply
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
for
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}}
. We always consider zip data of this form.

Remark 2.2.1
.

A general zip datum
(G,P,L,Q,M,\varphi)
is of the form
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}
for a cocharacter
\mu\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},k}\to G_{k}
if and only if
\sigma(P)
and
Q
are opposite parabolic subgroups with common Levi
M=\sigma(L)
.

Remark 2.2.2
.

If
\mu
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then so are
P
and
Q
. In this case, we have

and
P
,
Q
are opposite parabolic subgroups with common Levi subgroup
L
.

2.2.3
Frames

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}=(G,P,Q,L,M)
be a zip datum. In this paper, a frame for

is a triple

where
(B,T)
is a Borel pair of

defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying

(i)

One has the inclusion

.

(ii)

is an element satisfying the conditions

(2.2.2)

We put

. Other papers (

of

(2.2.3)

(

2.3.1
Representation theory

For an algebraic group
G
over a field
K
, denote by
\Rep(G)
the category of algebraic representations of
G
on finite-dimensional
K
-vector spaces. We denote a representation
\rho\colon G\to\GL_{K}(V)
by
(V,\rho)
, or sometimes simply
\rho
or
V
. For an algebraic group
G
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a
G_{k}
-representation
(V,\rho)
and an integer
m
, we denote by
(V^{[m]},\rho^{[m]})
the representation such that
V^{[m]}=V
and

\rho^{[m]}\colon G_{k}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{m}}G_{k}\xrightarrow{\rho}\GL(V).

(2.3.1)

Let
H
be a split connected reductive
K
-group and choose a Borel pair
(B_{H},T)
defined over
K
. If
K
has characteristic zero,
\Rep(H)
is semisimple. In characteristic
p
however, this is no longer true in general. For
\lambda\in X_{+}^{*}(T)
, let
{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}
be the line bundle attached to
\lambda
on the flag variety
H/B_{H}
by the usual associated sheaf construction (
[
Jan03
, §5.8]
). Define an
H
-representation
V_{H}(\lambda)
by:

V_{H}(\lambda)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}H^{0}(H/B_{H},{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda})

(2.3.2)

In other words, one has
V_{H}(\lambda)=\Ind_{B_{H}}^{H}\lambda
. The representation
V_{H}(\lambda)
is of highest weight
\lambda
. If
\cara(K)=0
, the representation
V_{H}(\lambda)
is irreducible. We view elements of
V_{H}(\lambda)
as regular maps
f\colon H\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
satisfying

f(hb)=\lambda(b^{-1})f(h),\quad\forall h\in H,\ \forall b\in B_{H}.

(2.3.3)

For dominant characters
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}
, there is a natural surjective map

V_{H}(\lambda)\otimes V_{H}(\lambda^{\prime})\to V_{H}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}).

(2.3.4)

In the description given by (
2.3.3
), this map is
f\otimes f^{\prime}\mapsto ff^{\prime}
(for
f\in V_{H}(\lambda)
,
f^{\prime}\in V_{H}(\lambda^{\prime})
). Denote by
W_{H}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W(H,T)
the Weyl group and
w_{0,H}\in W_{H}
the longest element. Then
V_{H}(\lambda)
has a unique
B_{H}
-stable line, which is a weight space for the weight
w_{0,H}\lambda
.

2.3.2
Vector bundles on quotient stacks

For an algebraic stack
{\mathcal{X}}
, write
\mathfrak{VB}({\mathcal{X}})
for the category of vector bundles on

. Let
X
be a
k
-scheme and

an affine

-group scheme acting on

. If

on the stack

(2.3.5)

Similarly to the usual associated sheaf construction

are given by

H^{0}([H\backslash X],{\mathcal{V}}_{H,X}(\rho))=\left\{f\colon X\to V\mid f(h\cdot x)=\rho(h)f(x),\ \forall h\in H,\ \forall x\in X\right\}.

(2.3.6)

Fix a cocharacter datum

(2.3.8)

2.5
The stack of
G
-zip flags

2.5.1
Definition

Let
(G,\mu)
be a cocharacter datum with attached zip datum
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}=(G,P,L,Q,M,\varphi)
(§
2.2.2
). Fix a frame
(B,T,z)
with
z=\sigma(w_{0,I})w_{0}=w_{0}w_{0,J}
(Lemma
2.2.3
). The stack of zip flags (
[
GK19a
, Definition 2.1.1]
) is defined as

\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}=[E\backslash(G_{k}\times P/B)]

(2.5.1)

where the group
E
acts on the variety
G_{k}\times(P/B)
by the rule
(a,b)\cdot(g,hB)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(agb^{-1},ahB)
for all
(a,b)\in E
and all
(g,hB)\in G_{k}\times P/B
. The first projection
G_{k}\times P/B\to G_{k}
is
E
-equivariant, and yields a natural morphism of stacks

\pi\colon\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}.

(2.5.2)

Similarly to the stack
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
, there is an interpretation of this stack in terms of torsors (
[
GK19a
, Definition 2.1.1]
). For any
k
-scheme
S
, the
S
-points of
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
are pairs
(\underline{I},J)
where
\underline{I}=(I,I_{P},I_{Q},\iota)
is a
G
-zip over
S
(see the end of §
2.2.1
) and
J\subset I_{P}
is a
B
-torsor. The map (
2.5.2
) is given by forgetting the
B

-torsor
J
.

Set
E^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E\cap(B\times G_{k})
. Then the injective map
G_{k}\to G_{k}\times P/B;\ g\mapsto(g,B)
yields an isomorphism of stacks
[E^{\prime}\backslash G_{k}]\simeq\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
(see
[
GK19a
, (2.1.5)]
). We recall the stratification of
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
. First, define the Schubert stack as the quotient stack

\Sbt\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[B\backslash G_{k}/B].

(2.5.3)

This stack is finite and smooth. Its topological space is isomorphic to
W
, endowed with the topology induced by the Bruhat order on
W
. This follows easily from the Bruhat decomposition of
G
. One can show that
E^{\prime}\subset B\times{}^{z}B
. In particular, there is a natural projection map
[E^{\prime}\backslash G_{k}]\to[B\backslash G_{k}/{}^{z}\!B]
. Composing with the isomorphism
[B\backslash G_{k}/{}^{z}B]\to[B\backslash G_{k}/B]
induced by
G_{k}\to G_{k};\ g\mapsto gz
, we obtain a smooth, surjective map

\psi\colon\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\to\Sbt.

(2.5.4)

For
w\in W
, put
\Sbt_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[B\backslash BwB/B]
, it is a locally closed substack of
\Sbt
. The

of
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
are defined as fibers of the map
\psi
. They are locally closed substacks (endowed with the reduced structure). Concretely, let
w\in W
and put

F_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}B(wz^{-1}){}^{z}B=BwBz^{-1},

(2.5.5)

which is the
B\times{}^{z}B
-orbit of
wz^{-1}
. The set
F_{w}
is locally closed in
G_{k}
, and one has
\dim(F_{w})=\ell(w)+\dim(B)
. Then, via the isomorphism
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\simeq[E^{\prime}\backslash G_{k}]
, the flag strata of
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
are the locally closed substacks

{\mathcal{F}}_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[E^{\prime}\backslash F_{w}],\quad w\in W.

(2.5.6)

The set
F_{w_{0}}\subset G_{k}
is open in
G_{k}
and similarly the stratum
{\mathcal{F}}_{w_{0}}
is open in
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}

-orbits of codimension

are

for

. The Zariski closure

2.6
Shimura varieties and
G
-zips

We explain the connection between the stack of
G
-zips and Shimura varieties. Let
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
be a Shimura datum
[
Del79
, 2.1.1]
. In particular,
\mathbf{G}
is a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{Q}
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{X}
provides a well-defined
\mathbf{G}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
-conjugacy class of cocharacters
\{\mu\}
of
\mathbf{G}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Write
\mathbf{E}=E(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
for the reflex field of
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
(i.e. the field of definition of
\{\mu\}
) and
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{E}}
for its ring of integers.
Given an open compact subgroup
K\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{A}_{f})
, write
\Sh(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})_{K}
for Deligne’s canonical model at level
K
over
\mathbf{E}
(see
[
Del79
]
). For
K\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
small enough,
\Sh(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})_{K}
is a smooth, quasi-projective scheme over
\mathbf{E}
. Every inclusion
K^{\prime}\subset K
induces a finite étale projection
\pi_{K^{\prime}/K}\colon\Sh(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})_{K^{\prime}}\to\Sh(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})_{K}
.

Let
g\geq 1
and let
(V,\psi)
be a
2g
-dimensional, non-degenerate symplectic space over
\mathbb{Q}
. Write
\GSp(2g)=\GSp(V,\psi)
for the group of symplectic similitudes of
(V,\psi)
. Write
\mathbf{X}_{g}
for the double Siegel half-space
[
Del79
, 1.3.1]
. The pair
(\GSp(2g),\mathbf{X}_{g})
is called the Siegel Shimura datum and has reflex field
\mathbb{Q}
. Recall that
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
is called of Hodge-type if there exists an embedding of Shimura data
\iota\colon(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})\hookrightarrow(\GSp(2g),\mathbf{X}_{g})
for some
g\geq 1
. Henceforth, assume
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
is of Hodge-type.

Fix a prime number
p
, and assume that the level
K
is of the form
K=K_{p}K^{p}
where
K_{p}\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is a hyperspecial subgroup and
K^{p}\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
is an open compact subgroup. Recall that a hyperspecial subgroup of
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
exists if and only if
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified, and one has
K_{p}={\mathscr{G}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
where
{\mathscr{G}}
is a reductive group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
{\mathscr{G}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\simeq\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and

{\mathscr{G}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}

For any place

above

in
\mathbf{E}
, Kisin (
[
Kis10
]
) and Vasiu (
[
Vas99
]
) constructed a smooth, canonical model

over

of
\Sh(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})_{K}
. Let
\kappa(v)
denote the residue field of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{E}_{v}}
and let
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
be an algebraic closure of
\kappa(v)
. Set

\mu\in\{\mu\}

\mathbf{E}_{v}
by
[
Kot84

We can also assume that
\mu

Kim18

equal to the centralizer of

[
IK21a
, §2.5]

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\mathscr{B}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{E}_{v}}}\subset{\mathscr{P}}

is a cocharacter datum, and it yields a zip datum

2.2.2

. For an
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{E}_{v}}
-algebra

H^{0}({\mathscr{S}}_{K}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{E}_{v}}}R,{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))
the space of automorphic forms of level

and weight

with coefficients in

The flag space of the Siegel modular variety

]

(\underline{A},{\mathcal{F}}_{\bullet})
where
\underline{A}=(A,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
is a principally polarized abelian variety of rank

and

is a full symplectic flag refining the Hodge filtration of

. In general, we defined in
[
GK19a
, §9.1]
the flag space
\Flag(S_{K})
of
S_{K}
as the fiber product

\textstyle{\Flag(S_{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{K}}
\scriptstyle{\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}}}
\textstyle{\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{S_{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\zeta}
\textstyle{\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}}

(2.6.3)

The stratification

on
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
induces by pullback via

a stratification

of

3
The zip cone

In this section, we will consider several subsets of
X^{*}(T)
. A
cone
in
X^{*}(T)
will be an additive submonoid containing
0
. If
C\subset X^{*}(T)
is a cone, we define its saturation (or saturated cone) as follows

{\mathcal{C}}=\{\lambda\in X^{*}(T)\mid\exists N\geq 1,\ N\lambda\in C\}.

(3.0.1)

We say that
C
is saturated if
C={\mathcal{C}}
. Define also
C_{\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}}
as follows

C_{\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\lambda_{i}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}N\geq 1,\ a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0},\ \lambda_{i}\in C\right\}.

(3.0.2)

There is a bijection between saturated cones of
X^{*}(T)
and additive submonoids of
X^{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
stable by
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
. The bijection is given by the maps
C\mapsto C_{\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}}
and
C^{\prime}\mapsto C^{\prime}\cap X^{*}(T)
.

3.1
Example: Hilbert–Blumenthal Shimura varieties

We recall some results of Diamond–Kassaei in
[
DK17
]
and extended in
[
DK23
]
that motivate this paper. We give a short explanation of
[
DK17
, Corollary 5.4]
. The authors study Hilbert automorphic forms in characteristic
p
. Specifically, let
\mathbf{F}/\mathbb{Q}
be a totally real extension of degree
d=[\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{Q}]
and let
\mathbf{G}
be the subgroup of
\Res_{\mathbf{F}/\mathbb{Q}}(\GL_{2,\mathbf{F}})
defined by

\mathbf{G}(R)=\{g\in\GL_{2}(R\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbf{F})\ |\ \det(g)\in R^{\times}\}.

(3.1.1)

Let
p
be a prime number unramified in
\mathbf{F}
(in
[
DK23
]
,
p
is allowed to be ramified in
\mathbf{F}
). The lattice
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{F}}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbf{F}
gives rise to a reductive model
{\mathscr{G}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Fix a small enough level
K^{p}\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
outside
p
and set
K_{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathscr{G}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
K=K_{p}K^{p}
. Let
S_{K}
be the (geometric) special fiber of the corresponding Hilbert–Blumenthal Shimura variety of level
K
.
The scheme
S_{K}
is smooth of dimension
d
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. It parametrizes tuples
(A,\lambda,\iota,\overline{\eta})
of abelian schemes over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of dimension
d
endowed with a principal polarization
\lambda
, an action
\iota
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{F}}
on
A
and a
K^{p}
-level structure
\overline{\eta}
.

Let
\Sigma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom(\mathbf{F},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
be the set of field embeddings
\mathbf{F}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Write
(\mathbf{e}_{\tau})_{\tau}
for the canonical basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma}
. Let
\sigma
denote the action of Frobenius on
\Sigma
. For each
\tau\in\Sigma
, there is an associated line bundle
\omega_{\tau}
on
S_{K}
. For
\mathbf{k}=\sum_{\tau}k_{\tau}\mathbf{e}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma}
, define

\omega^{\mathbf{k}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigotimes_{\tau\in\Sigma}\omega_{\tau}^{k_{\tau}}.

(3.1.2)

Elements of
H^{0}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\omega^{\mathbf{k}})
are called mod
p
Hilbert modular forms of weight
\mathbf{k}
. There is an Ekedahl–Oort stratification on
S_{K}
given by the isomorphism class of the
p
-torsion
A[p]
(with its additional structure given by
\lambda
and
\iota
). There is a unique open stratum (on which
A
is an ordinary abelian variety). The codimension one strata can be labeled as
(S_{K,\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma}
. Andreatta–Goren (
[
AG05
]
) constructed partial Hasse invariants
\Ha_{\tau}
for each
\tau\in\Sigma
. The weight of
\Ha_{\tau}
is given by

\mathbf{h}_{\tau}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}e_{\tau}-pe_{\sigma^{-1}\tau}.

(3.1.3)

Note that the sign of
\mathbf{h}_{\tau}
is different in
[
AG05
]
, due to a different convention of positivity. The main property of
\Ha_{\tau}
is that it vanishes exactly on the Zariski closure of the codimension one stratum
S_{K,\tau}
. It is a special case of the sections
\Ha_{\alpha}
defined in Definition
2.5.2
. Define the partial Hasse invariant cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma}
as the cone of
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma}
which are spanned (over
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
) by the weights
(\mathbf{h}_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma}
defined above.

Theorem 3.1.1

(Diamond–Kassaei,
[
DK17
, Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.4]
)
.

(1)

Let
f\in H^{0}(S_{K},\omega^{\mathbf{k}})
and assume that
pk_{\tau}>k_{\sigma^{-1}\tau}
. Then
f
is divisible by
\Ha_{\tau}
.

(2)

If
H^{0}(S_{K},\omega^{\mathbf{k}})\neq 0
, then
\mathbf{k}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}
.

The authors define a minimal cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm min}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}
as follows:

{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm min}=\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma}\ |\ pk_{\tau}\leq k_{\sigma^{-1}\tau}\ \textrm{ for all }\tau\in\Sigma\}.

(3.1.4)

Theorem
3.1.1
(
1
) shows that any Hilbert modular form
f
of weight
\mathbf{k}
can be written as a product
f=f_{\min}h
, where
f_{\min}
has weight
\mathbf{k}_{\min}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\min}
and
h
is a product of partial Hasse invariants. In particular (
2
) is a direct consequence of (
1
). One motivation of this paper is to understand the natural setting in which one might expect a generalization to other Shimura varieties of Theorem
3.1.1
(
2
). In
[
GK22a
]
, Goldring and the second-named author show that (
1
) also admits a similar generalization for several Hodge-type Shimura varieties.

3.2
General setting

We attempt to give an abstract setting in which Theorem
3.1.1
may generalize. First, by observing the example of Hilbert–Blumenthal varieties, we extract the essential properties of the objects we want to study. Specifically, we consider a stack
Y
over
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
endowed with the following structure:

(a)

There is a locally closed stratification
Y=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}Y_{i}
such that the Zariski closure of a stratum is a union of strata.

(b)

There is a free, finite-type
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Lambda
and a family of line bundles
(\omega(\lambda))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
on
Y
, such that
\omega(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})=\omega(\lambda)\otimes\omega(\lambda^{\prime})
for all
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
.

(c)

For each codimension one stratum
Y_{i}\subset Y
, there is
\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda
and
\Ha_{i}\in H^{0}(X,\omega(\lambda_{i}))
such that the support of
{\rm div}(\Ha_{i})
is
\overline{Y}_{i}
. By analogy, we call
\Ha_{i}
a partial Hasse invariant for
Y_{i}
.

Denote by
I_{1}\subset I
the indices such that
Y_{i}
has codimension one. Let
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset\Lambda
denote the cone generated by the elements
\{\lambda_{i}\mid i\in I_{1}\}
, and call it the partial Hasse invariant cone. Put

C_{Y}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid H^{0}(Y,\omega(\lambda))\neq 0\}.

(3.2.1)

By definition, one has
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset C_{Y}
. If
Y
is connected, then
C_{Y}
is a cone (i.e. an additive submonoid) of
\Lambda
. Indeed, if
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
and
f,f^{\prime}
are nonzero sections of
\omega(\lambda)
and
\omega(\lambda^{\prime})
respectively, then
ff^{\prime}
is a section of
\omega(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})
. Since
Y
is connected,
ff^{\prime}
is nonzero. Write
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{Y}
for the saturation of
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}
and
C_{Y}
inside
\Lambda
, respectively.

Definition 3.2.1
.

Let
Y
be a stack satisfying
\bracketa
,
\bracketb
and
\bracketc
. We say that
Y
has the Hasse property if
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}={\mathcal{C}}_{Y}
.

For example, Theorem
3.1.1
(
2
) shows that the geometric special fiber of the Hilbert–Blumenthal Shimura variety at a place of good reduction satisfies the Hasse property. Let
(G,\mu)
be a cocharacter datum and let
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
be the attached stack of
G
-zips. Fix a frame
(B,T,z)
as in §
2.2.3
.
Then, the stack of zip flags
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
(§
2.5
) satisfies all requirements (a), (b) and (c) above. First, we have the flag stratification
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}{\mathcal{F}}_{w}
as in §(
2.5.6
). Setting
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X^{*}(T)
, we have the family of line bundles
({\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{flag}}(\lambda))_{\lambda\in X^{*}(T)}
satisfying (b) by (
2.5.12
). Finally, we have partial Hasse invariants (§
2.5.3
). In this paper, we give a full answer as to whether
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
satisfies the Hasse property.

Similarly, let
Y
be a scheme endowed with a smooth, surjective morphism
\zeta_{Y}\colon Y\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
. Then
Y
inherits naturally by pullback all the structure from
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
, and hence satisfies all required properties (a), (b) and (c) above. In particular, if we start with a scheme
X
and a smooth, surjective morphism
\zeta\colon X\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
, then we can consider the flag space
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Flag(X)
(similarly to the flag space of
S_{K}
defined at the end of §
2.6
). It is defined as the fiber product

Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\times_{\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}}\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}.

(3.2.2)

The induced map
\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}}\colon Y\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
is again smooth and surjective. Hence,
Y
inherits the structure as above and satisfies (a), (b) and (c). Denote by
\pi\colon Y\to X
and
\pi\colon\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
the natural projections. In both cases, we have
\pi_{*}({\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{flag}}(\lambda))={\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)
because
\zeta
is smooth and
\pi
is proper. Therefore, the cones
C_{Y}
and
C_{\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}}
can also be written as follows:

\displaystyle C_{Y}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda\in X^{*}(T)\mid H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))\neq 0\},

(3.2.3)

\displaystyle C_{\mathsf{zip}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}C_{\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda\in X^{*}(T)\mid H^{0}(\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))\neq 0\}.

(3.2.4)

We will use the notation
C_{\mathsf{zip}}
(introduced in
[
Kos19
]
), instead of
C_{\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}}
. When
Y
is given as (
3.2.2
) above, we call
Y

the flag space
of
(X,\zeta)
. Furthermore, we make the slight abuse of saying that
(X,\zeta)
satisfies the Hasse property if
(Y,\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}})
does. In particular, let
X=S_{K}
be the geometric special fiber modulo
p
of a Hodge-type Shimura variety with good reduction at
p
. By Zhang’s result, there is a smooth, surjective morphism
\zeta\colon X\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
, and so we obtain
(Y,\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}})
as above. Our goal is to investigate which Hodge-type Shimura varieties satisfy the Hasse property.

We now return to a general pair
(X,\zeta)
. Since
\zeta
is surjective, pullback by
\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}}
induces an inclusion

H^{0}(\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))\subset H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)).

(3.2.5)

Hence we have inclusions
C_{\mathsf{zip}}\subset C_{Y}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{Y}
. Furthermore, Hasse invariants exist already on
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
by section
2.5.3
, hence the cone
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}
generated by their weights satisfies
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset C_{\mathsf{zip}}
. Therefore, we have in general

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{Y}.

(3.2.6)

In particular, if the pair
(X,\zeta)
satisfies the Hasse property, then all three cones above coincide. In other words, a necessary condition for
X
to satisfy the Hasse property is that
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
itself satisfies this property, which is equivalent to the condition
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}
. This is an obstruction for a potential generalization of Theorem
3.1.1
(
2
) to other Shimura varieties.

Remark 3.2.2
.

When we start with a pair
(X,\zeta)
and construct
(Y,\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}})
by fiber product as in (
3.2.2
), formula (
3.2.3
) shows immediately that

{\mathcal{C}}_{Y}\subset X^{*}_{+,I}(T).

(3.2.7)

Indeed, this follows simply from the fact that if
\lambda
is not
I
-dominant, then
{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)=0
. Thus, in the example of Shimura varieties, we have the inclusion (
3.2.7
).

3.3
Previous results

We review previous results from
[
GK18
]
. Let
(X,\zeta)
be a pair consisting of a
k
-scheme
X
and a smooth, surjective morphism of stacks
\zeta\colon X\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
, and let
(Y,\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}})
be the flag space of
X
. We make the following assumption:

Assumption 3.3.1
.

(A)

For any
w\in W
with
\ell(w)=1
, the closed stratum
\overline{Y}_{w}=\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}}^{-1}(\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}_{w})
is pseudo-complete (i.e. any element of
H^{0}(\overline{Y}_{w},{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{Y}_{w}})
is locally constant on
\overline{Y}_{w}
for the Zariski-topology).

(B)

The restriction
\zeta
to any connected component
X^{\circ}\subset X
is smooth and surjective.

For example, Condition (A) is satisfied if
X
is a proper
k
-scheme. In general, it can happen that the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
is strict. In this case, it is impossible for
Y
to satisfy the Hasse property. However, Goldring and the second-named author conjectured in general:

Conjecture 3.3.2
.

Under Assumption
3.3.1
, we have
{\mathcal{C}}_{Y}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
.

Let
S_{K}
be the special fiber of a Hodge-type Shimura variety at a prime
p
of good reduction. In this case, we write
C_{K}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
for the cone
C_{Y}
, i.e.

C_{K}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\lambda\in X^{*}(T)\mid H^{0}(S_{K},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))\neq 0\}.

(3.3.1)

By
[
Kos19
, Corollary 1.5.3]
, the saturation of
C_{K}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is independent of
K
, so we simply denote it by
{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Let
\zeta\colon S_{K}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
be the map (
2.6.1
). We do not know whether the pair
(X,\zeta)
always satisfies condition (A) of Assumption
3.3.1
. However, by
[
GK19a
, Theorem 6.2.1]
, Zhang’s map
\zeta\colon S_{K}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
admits an extension to a toroidal compactification

\zeta^{\Sigma}\colon S_{K}^{\Sigma}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}

(3.3.2)

where
\Sigma
is a sufficiently fine cone decomposition. By construction, the pullback
{\mathcal{V}}_{I}^{\Sigma}(\lambda)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\zeta^{\Sigma,*}({\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))
is the canonical extension of
{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)
to
S_{K}^{\Sigma}
. Furthermore, by
[
And23
, Theorem 1.2]
, the map
\zeta^{\Sigma}
is smooth. Since
\zeta
is surjective,
\zeta^{\Sigma}
is also surjective. By
[
WZ23
, Proposition 6.20]
, any connected component

intersects the unique zero-dimensional stratum. Since
\zeta^{\Sigma}\colon S^{\circ}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
is smooth, it has an open image, therefore it must be surjective. In particular, the pair
(S_{K}^{\Sigma},\zeta^{\Sigma})
satisfies Conditions (A) and (B). Furthermore, in most cases Koecher’s principle holds by
[
LS18
, Theorem 2.5.11]
, i.e. we have an equality

H^{0}(S^{\Sigma}_{K},{\mathcal{V}}^{\Sigma}_{I}(\lambda))=H^{0}(S_{K},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)).

(3.3.3)

In particular, the cone attached to the pair
(S_{K}^{\Sigma},\zeta^{\Sigma})
is the same as the cone attached to
(S_{K},\zeta)
, namely
C_{K}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Therefore, by the above discussion, we deduce that Conjecture
3.3.2
applies to Shimura varieties and predicts the following:

Conjecture 3.3.3
.

If
S_{K}
is the special fiber of a Hodge-type Shimura variety at a prime
p
of good reduction, we have
{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
.

In
[
GK18
, Theorem D]
, the authors proved that certain Shimura varieties satisfy the Hasse property. Specifically, they showed the following:

Theorem 3.3.4

(
[
GK18
, Theorem D]
)
.

Let
(X,\zeta)
be a pair which satisfies Assumption
3.3.1
and let
(Y,\zeta_{\mathsf{flag}})
be the flag space of
X
. Suppose that
(G,\mu)
is one of the following three pairs:

(1)

G
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-form of
\GL_{2}^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
, and
\mu
is non-trivial on each factor,

(2)

G=\GL_{3,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, and
\mu\colon z\mapsto{\rm diag}(z,z,1)
,

(3)

G=\GSp(4)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, and
\mu\colon z\mapsto{\rm diag}(z,z,1,1)
.

Then
(X,\zeta)
satisfies the Hasse property. In other words, we have
{\mathcal{C}}_{Y}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}

The above theorem also holds if we change the group

to a group with the same adjoint group. By the above discussion, Theorem
3.3.4
applies to Hilbert–Blumenthal Shimura varieties, Picard surfaces at a split prime, Siegel modular threefolds and shows that Conjecture
3.3.2
holds in each case. Goldring and the second-named author proved Conjecture

in
[

]
for certain Shimura varieties for which the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
is strict. Namely, they showed Conjecture

for the Siegel modular variety
{\mathcal{A}}_{3}
as well as unitary Shimura varieties of signature

with

at split or inert primes, except when

and

is inert. With the exception of the case

and

split, the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
is strict in each of these cases.

3.4
First properties of
C_{\mathsf{zip}}

Let
(G,\mu)
be a cocharacter datum over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}=(G,P,L,Q,M,\varphi)
the attached zip datum (§
2.2.2
). Fix a frame
(B,T,z)
with
z=\sigma(w_{0,I})w_{0}
(see §
2.2.2
). Let
C_{\mathsf{zip}}\subset X^{*}(T)
be the zip cone, defined in (

). We start with some elementary properties of
C_{\mathsf{zip}}
. As we already noted, we have
C_{\mathsf{zip}}\subset X^{*}_{+,I}(T)
. Furthermore, the cone
C_{\mathsf{zip}}
has maximal rank in

, in the sense that
\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathsf{zip}})=X^{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}

, Lemma 3.4.2]
(with the notation of
loc. cit.
,
\mathcal{C}_{w_{0}}\subset C_{\mathsf{zip}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{w_{0}}
has maximal rank). Note that the cocharacter datum is assumed to be Hodge-type
in
[
GK19a
, §3.4]
, but this assumption is unnecessary for
[
GK19a
, Lemma 3.4.2]
.

Next, we consider line bundles on
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
. Recall that
{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)
is a line bundle if and only if
\lambda\in X^{*}(L)
(viewed as a subgroup of

). Define the following set:

X^{*}_{-}(L)_{{\rm reg}}=\{\lambda\in X^{*}(L)\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<0,\ \forall\alpha\in\Delta^{P}\}.

(3.4.1)

These characters were termed

-ample in
[
GK19a
, Definition N.5.1]
. The notation used in (

) is more enlightening, since these characters are in particular in

(the cone of anti-dominant characters).
An immediate consequence of

, Theorem 5.1.4]
is the inclusion

. Set
X^{*}_{-}(L)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X^{*}_{-}(T)\cap X^{*}(L)
. The stronger inclusion

is claimed in

, Proposition 1.6.1]
with an incomplete proof, so we give one below:

Proposition 3.4.1
.

We have
X^{*}_{-}(L)\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in X^{*}_{-}(T)\cap X^{*}(L)
. Applying
[
IK21a
, Theorem 3.4.1]
to the one-dimensional
L
-representation
V_{I}(\lambda)
, we obtain:

H^{0}(\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))=V_{I}(\lambda)^{L_{\varphi}}\cap\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Delta^{P}}{\rm Fil}_{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\Xi_{\alpha},\mathbf{a}_{\alpha},\mathbf{r}_{\alpha}}V_{\lambda}.

(3.4.2)

Furthermore,
{\rm Fil}_{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\Xi_{\alpha},\mathbf{a}_{\alpha},\mathbf{r}_{\alpha}}V_{\lambda}=V_{\lambda}=V_{I}(\lambda)
if
\langle\lambda,\delta_{\alpha}\rangle\geq 0
and is
0
otherwise. Let
d_{\alpha}\geq 1
be an integer such that
\alpha
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{\alpha}}}
. We find that
\delta_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{q^{d_{\alpha}}-1}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{\alpha}-1}q^{i}\sigma^{i}(\alpha^{\vee})
. Since
\lambda\in X^{*}_{-}(T)
, we have
\langle\lambda,\sigma^{i}(\alpha^{\vee})\rangle\leq 0
for all

, hence
\langle\lambda,\delta_{\alpha}\rangle\geq 0
. We deduce
H^{0}(\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))=V_{I}(\lambda)^{L_{\varphi}}
. Finally, if we change
\lambda
to
N\lambda
where

divides the order of the finite group scheme
L_{\varphi}
, we obtain

. In particular, this space is nonzero, and this proves the result.
∎

3.5
Norm of the highest weight vector

Recall that we always have
1\in U_{\mu}
, where
U_{\mu}\subset G_{k}
is the unique open
E
-orbit. Recall the definition of the finite subgroup
L_{\varphi}\subset L
given in (
2.4.2
). Put
N_{\varphi}=|L_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|q^{m}
where
L_{0}
is the Levi subgroup defined in (
2.4.3
) and
m\geq 0
is the smallest integer such that the finite unipotent group

is annihilated by
\varphi^{m}
. For
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in X^{*}(T)
and
f\in V_{I}(\lambda)
,
f^{\prime}\in V_{I}(\lambda^{\prime})
, let
ff^{\prime}\in V_{I}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})
be the image of

by the map (
2.3.4
). For

and

define

\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left(\prod_{s\in L_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}s\cdot f\right)^{q^{m}}\in V_{I}(N_{\varphi}\lambda).

(3.5.1)

It is clear that
\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f)
is
L_{\varphi}
-invariant. In particular, it gives rise to an element in

by Lemma

. In general, it is difficult to determine whether

extends to a global section. However, this is possible when

is a highest weight vector, as we now explain.

Let
f_{\lambda}\in V_{I}(\lambda)
be a nonzero element in the highest weight line of
V_{I}(\lambda)
. The following result generalizes
[
Kos19
, Theorem 2]
(where
P
was assumed to be defined over

, here we do not make this assumption). For
\alpha\in\Delta^{P}
, denote by

the smallest integer

such that
\sigma^{r}(\alpha)=\alpha
.

Proposition 3.5.1
.

The section
\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f_{\lambda})
extends to a global section over
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
if and only if for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{P}
, the following holds:

\sum_{w\in W_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\sum_{i=0}^{r_{\alpha}-1}q^{i+\ell(w)}\ \langle w\lambda,\sigma^{i}(\alpha^{\vee})\rangle\leq 0.

(3.5.2)

Before giving the proof, we need to recall some facts from
[
IK21a
, §3.1]
. First, we have

G_{k}\setminus U_{\mu}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Delta^{P}}Z_{\alpha},\quad Z_{\alpha}=\overline{E\cdot s_{\alpha}}

(3.5.3)

where

denotes the
E
-orbit of
s_{\alpha}
and the bar denotes the Zariski closure. This follows easily from Theorem
2.2.4
. For any
\alpha\in\Delta^{P}
, define an open subset

X_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G_{k}\setminus\bigcup_{\beta\in\Delta^{P},\,\beta\neq\alpha}Z_{\beta}.

(3.5.4)

Then
U_{\mu}\subset X_{\alpha}
and
X_{\alpha}\setminus U_{\mu}
is irreducible. Choose a realization

(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Phi}
and let
\phi_{\alpha}\colon\SL_{2}\to G
be the map attached to
\alpha
(see §
2.1
). Set
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
and
Y_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
. For
\alpha\in\Delta^{P}
, define
\psi_{\alpha}\colon Y\to G
by

\psi_{\alpha}\colon((x,y),t)\mapsto x\phi_{\alpha}\left(A(t)\right)y^{-1}\quad\textrm{where}\ A(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}t&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right)\in\SL_{2,k}.

(3.5.5)

It satisfies
\psi_{\alpha}((x,y),t)\in X_{\alpha}
for all

and
\psi_{\alpha}((x,y),t)\in U_{\mu}
if and only if

(see
[
IK21a
, Proposition 3.1.4]
).

We now prove Proposition
3.5.1
. We use a similar argument as in
[
Kos19
, Theorem 3.5.3]
. Set
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{\mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pi^{-1}({\mathcal{U}}_{\mu})
, where
\pi\colon\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\to\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
is the natural projection. One has clearly
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{\mu}\simeq[E^{\prime}\backslash U_{\mu}]
via the isomorphism
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}\simeq[E^{\prime}\backslash G]
explained in §
2.5
. We have an identification

H^{0}({\mathcal{U}}_{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(N_{\varphi}\lambda))=H^{0}({\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{flag}}(N_{\varphi}\lambda))

(3.5.6)

similarly to (
2.5.10
). In particular, we can view
\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f_{\lambda})
as a function
h\colon U_{\mu}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
satisfying the relation
h(axb^{-1})=\lambda(a)h(x)
for all
(a,b)\in E^{\prime}
and
x\in U_{\mu}
(using (
2.3.6
)). Specifically, the function
h
is given by

h(x_{1}x_{2}^{-1})=\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f_{\lambda})(\theta_{L}^{P}(x_{1})^{-1})

(3.5.7)

for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in E
using (
2.5.11
).
The function

is well-defined because
\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f_{\lambda})
is
L_{\varphi}
-invariant. Furthermore,
\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f_{\lambda})
extends to
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
if and only if
h
extends to a function

. By the strategy explained in
[
Kos19
, §3.2]
and in
[
IK21a
, §3.1]
, the function
h
extends to
G
if and only if for each
\alpha\in\Delta^{P}
, the function
h\circ\psi_{\alpha}\colon Y_{0}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
extends to a function
Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
. It remains to compute the
t
-valuation of the function
h\circ\psi_{\alpha}
, viewed as an element of
R[t,\frac{1}{t}]
where
R=k[E]
is the ring of functions of
E
. Put

m_{\alpha}=\min\{m\geq 1\mid\sigma^{-m}(\alpha)\notin I\},\quad\alpha\in\Delta^{P}

(3.5.8)

and
t_{\alpha}=t^{-1}\alpha(\varphi(\delta_{\alpha}(t)))^{-1}=t\alpha(\delta_{\alpha}(t))^{-1}\in t^{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
t
is an indeterminate and
\delta_{\alpha}=\wp_{*}^{-1}(\alpha^{\vee})
as defined in §
2.4
. Set

u_{t,\alpha}=\prod_{i=1}^{m_{\alpha}-1}\phi_{\sigma^{-i}(\alpha)}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&-t_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q^{i}}}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)

where the product is taken in increasing order of indices. By the proof of
[
IK21a
, Proposition 3.1.4]
, for all
(x,y)\in E
and

, we can write
\psi_{\alpha}((x,y),t)=x_{1}x_{2}^{-1}
with

and

-t^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\delta_{\alpha}(t)u_{t,\alpha}.

(3.5.9)

By definition of
m_{\alpha}
, all the roots
\sigma^{-i}(\alpha)
(for

) appearing in the formula of

lie in
I
. Using (

), we deduce:

\displaystyle h\circ\psi_{\alpha}((x,y),t)

\displaystyle=\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f_{\lambda})(u_{t,\alpha}^{-1}\delta_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}\theta_{L}^{P}(x)^{-1})

(3.5.10)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{s\in L_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}f_{\lambda}(su_{t,\alpha}^{-1}\delta_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}\theta_{L}^{P}(x)^{-1})\right)^{q^{m}}.

(3.5.11)

Consider the element

, which lies in

. We can still speak of the
t
-valuation of this element, which is a rational number. Equivalently, to simplify notation, we change
\theta_{L}^{P}(x)^{-1}
to a generic element

and we compute the
t
-valuation of
F_{s}(t,g)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f_{\lambda}(su_{t,\alpha}^{-1}\delta_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}g)
, viewed as an element of
k[L][t^{\mathbb{Q}}]
. Let

be this valuation.
We put
B^{+}_{L}=B^{+}\cap L
.
Define a parabolic subgroup of
L
by
Q_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L_{0}B^{+}_{L}
. It is clear that
u_{t,\alpha}
lies in
R_{\mathrm{u}}(Q_{0})
, thus for all

, we have
su_{t,\alpha}^{-1}s^{-1}\in R_{\mathrm{u}}(Q_{0})
. Since

is invariant by
R_{\mathrm{u}}(B^{+}_{L})
, we obtain

F_{s}(t,g)=f_{\lambda}(s\delta_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}g)
. Now, the rest of the proof is completely similar to
[
Kos19
, Theorem 3.5.3]
. We recall it briefly.

Let
B_{L_{0}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}B\cap L_{0}
and
B^{+}_{L_{0}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}B^{+}\cap L_{0}
. If we change

to

with

, then

. Indeed, this follows from
f_{\lambda}(bs\delta_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}g)=\lambda(b)^{-1}f_{\lambda}(s\delta_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}g)
since
f_{\lambda}
is a
B^{+}_{L}
-eigenfunction. Similarly, we claim that
v_{\alpha}(sb)=v_{\alpha}(s)
for all

. By symmetry, it suffices to show

. We can write

F_{sb}(t,g)=F_{s}(t,\Gamma(t)g)
where

\Gamma(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\delta_{\alpha}(t)b\delta_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}.

(3.5.12)

We view
\Gamma
as a map
\Gamma\colon\spec(k[t^{\mathbb{Q}}])\to L
. Since
\alpha\in\Delta^{P}
, the cocharacter
\alpha^{\vee}
is anti-
L
-dominant. It follows that for all

,
\sigma^{j}(\alpha^{\vee})
is an anti-
L_{0}
-dominant quasi-cocharacter. It is easy to see that
\delta_{\alpha}
is explicitly given by the formula

\delta_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{q^{r_{\alpha}}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{r_{\alpha}-1}q^{j}\sigma^{j}(\alpha^{\vee}).

(3.5.13)

In particular,
\delta_{\alpha}
is
L_{0}
-dominant. We deduce that the function
\Gamma
extends to a map
\spec(k[t^{\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}}])\to L
. This follows simply from the fact that
\delta_{\alpha}(t)
acts on the root space
U_{\beta}
(for

) by

, using (
2.1.1
). Write
F_{s}(t,g)=t^{v_{\alpha}(s)}F_{s,0}(t,g)
where

is an element of

whose

-valuation is
0
. Then
F_{sb}(t,g)=t^{v_{\alpha}(s)}F_{s,0}(t,\Gamma(t)g)
and

F_{s,0}(t,\Gamma(t)g)\in k[L][t^{\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}}]
.
Hence
v_{\alpha}(sb)\geq v_{\alpha}(s)
as claimed.

Now, consider the Bruhat decomposition of
L_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
:

L_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\bigsqcup_{w\in W_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}B^{+}_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})wB^{+}_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

(3.5.14)

as in
[
Kos19
, Lemma 3.4.4]
. By
[
Kos19
, Lemma 3.4.5]
, one has

|B^{+}_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})wB^{+}_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=|T(\mathbb{F}_{q})|q^{\dim(R_{\mathrm{u}}(B_{L_{0}}))+\ell(w)}.

(3.5.15)

Thus, we can determine completely
v_{\alpha}
from the values
v_{\alpha}(w)
for
w\in W_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Similarly to
[
Kos19
, Proposition 3.5.2]
, we have
v_{\alpha}(w)=\langle w\lambda,\delta_{\alpha}\rangle
. We deduce that the
t
-valuation of
h\circ\psi_{\alpha}((x,y),t)
is

q^{m}\sum_{s\in L_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}v_{\alpha}(s)=q^{m}|T(\mathbb{F}_{q})|q^{\dim(R_{\mathrm{u}}(B_{L_{0}}))}\sum_{w\in W_{L_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}q^{\ell(w)}\langle w\lambda,\delta_{\alpha}\rangle.

(3.5.16)

The statement of Proposition
3.5.1
then follows by replacing
\delta_{\alpha}
by the expression in (
3.5.13
).

Definition 3.5.2
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{hw}}\subset X_{+,I}^{*}(T)
the subset of characters
\lambda
satisfying the inequalities (
3.5.2
) and call
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{hw}}
the highest weight cone.

By construction, for all

, the section
\mathbf{f}_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Norm_{L_{\varphi}}(f_{\lambda})
is a nonzero section of

and hence

. We deduce that

. If

is the good reduction special fiber of a Hodge-type Shimura variety and

is the map (

), we obtain a family of mod

3.6
Partial Hasse invariant cone, Griffiths–Schmid cone

We defined in §
3.2
the partial Hasse invariant cone

as the cone generated by the weights of partial Hasse invariants on
\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt ZipFlag}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
(see

). There is an ambiguity in this definition, because if

is a partial Hasse invariant for

(Definition

), then
\chi f^{n}
for
\chi\in X^{*}(G)
and

is also a partial Hasse invariant for

. Therefore, we give below an unambiguous definition of

.

Definition 3.6.1

(
[
Kos19
, Definition 1.7.1]
)
.

Define
C_{\mathsf{pHa}}
as the image of
X^{*}_{+}(T)
by

h_{{\mathcal{Z}}}\colon X^{*}(T)\to X^{*}(T);\quad\lambda\mapsto\lambda-qw_{0,I}(\sigma^{-1}\lambda).

(3.6.1)

We write
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}
for the saturation of

. One has

since

for

. If

is split over

, we have an equivalence

\lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{pHa}}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ qw_{0,I}\lambda+\lambda\in X_{-}^{*}(T).

(3.6.2)

Definition 3.6.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{GS}}
denote the set of characters

satisfying

\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0\ \textrm{ for }\alpha\in I,

(3.6.3)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 0\ \textrm{ for }\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi^{+}_{L}.

(3.6.4)

One sees easily that

if and only if

is dominant. Clearly

is a saturated subcone of

and contains

. We explain the significance of

. Consider a Hodge-type Shimura variety

over the reflex field
\mathbf{E}
, with good reduction at the prime

, as in §
2.6
. Similarly to (

), we define a cone

by

C_{K}(\mathbb{C})=\{\lambda\in X^{*}(T)\mid H^{0}(\Sh(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})_{K}\otimes_{\mathbf{E}}\mathbb{C},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda))\neq 0\}.

(3.6.5)

Again, the saturation of

is independent of

, so we denote it by

. Based on the results of

]
, it is expected that

, but we could not find a reference for this result. The inclusion

is proved for general Hodge-type Shimura varieties in

, Theorem 2.6.4]
.

By reduction modulo

, one can show that

(see

, Proposition 1.8.3]
). Combining the expectation

, one should expect an inclusion

.

Lemma 3.7.1
.

One has
X_{-}^{*}(L)\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{hw}}
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in X_{-}^{*}(L)
, we have

for all

. Hence
\langle w\lambda,\sigma^{i}\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\leq 0
for all

,

∎

We postpone the proof of

in the general case, which is quite involved. The following was proved in
[
Kos19
, Corollary 3.5.6]
:

Lemma 3.7.2
.

Assume that
P
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then one has
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{GS}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{hw}}
.

This shows

in the case when

is defined over

. However, the inclusion

at an inert prime, where the group

is a unitary group of rank

over

4

4.1
Topology of
C_{\mathsf{zip},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}

Let
(G,\mu)
be a cocharacter datum. We showed
X^{*}_{-}(L)\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{zip}}
in Proposition
3.4.1
. For
X_{-}^{*}(L)_{{\rm reg}}
(see (
3.4.1
)), we have a more precise result (
[
KW18
, Theorem 5.1.4]
):

Theorem 4.1.1
.

For all
\lambda\in X^{*}_{-}(L)_{{\rm reg}}
, there is a section
h\in H^{0}(\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu},{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(N_{\varphi}\lambda))
whose non-vanishing locus is exactly
{\mathcal{U}}_{\mu}
.

Here
N_{\varphi}\geq 1
is the integer defined in §
3.5
. Since
\lambda\in X^{*}(L)
, the vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)
is a line bundle, and thus
{\mathcal{V}}_{I}(N_{\varphi}\lambda)={\mathcal{V}}_{I}(\lambda)^{\otimes N_{\varphi}}
. A subset of an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space stable under linear combination with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
will be called an
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-subcone. We endow
X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
with the subspace topology of
X^{*}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 4.1.2
.

Let
C\subset X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
be an
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-subcone and let
\lambda\in C
. Then
C
is a neighborhood of
\lambda
in
X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
if and only if for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
, there exists
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\lambda^{\prime}+r\lambda\in C
.

Proof.

First, assume that
C
is a neighborhood of
\lambda
in
X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
. There is an open subset
V
of
X^{*}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\lambda\in V\cap X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\subset C
. Fix
\lambda^{\prime}\in X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
. For large
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we have
\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{r}\in V
, and this element is also in
X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
. Thus
\lambda^{\prime}+r\lambda\in C
.

We prove the converse. We claim that for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
, there exists
r>1
such that
\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{r}\in C
. Indeed, let
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{r}\in C
. Then for all
\gamma>0
, we have
\gamma\lambda+\frac{\gamma\lambda^{\prime}}{r}=\lambda+\frac{\gamma(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}{r}+(\gamma-1+\frac{\gamma}{r})\lambda\in C
. For
\gamma=\frac{r}{r+1}
, we have
\gamma-1+\frac{\gamma}{r}=0
hence
\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{r+1}\in C
. Hence, by taking
\lambda
as the origin, we are reduced to the following:

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an intersection of closed half-spaces
containing
0
, and
0\in Y\subset X
a convex subset satisfying: for all
x\in X
,
\exists r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\frac{x}{r}\in Y
. Then
Y
is a neighborhood of
0
in
X
.

Taking intersections with a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is a convex polytope, we may assume that
X
is a convex polytope. Since
X
is the convex hull of finitely many points, there exists
r>1
such that
\frac{1}{r}X=\{\frac{x}{r}\mid x\in X\}\subset Y
. Hence, it suffices to show that
\frac{1}{r}X
is a neighborhood of
0
in
X
. There are linear forms
u_{1},\dots,u_{d}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
x\in X
if and only if
u_{i}(x)\leq m_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,d
. Hence
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})
maps
X
to
Z=\prod_{i=1}^{d}]-\infty,m_{i}]
. For
r>1
,
\frac{1}{r}Z
is clearly a neighborhood of
0
in
Z
, hence
\frac{1}{r}X=u^{-1}(\frac{1}{r}Z)
is a neighborhood of
0
in
X
.
∎

The following Lemma was proved in a slightly restricted setting in
[
Kos19
, Proposition 2.2.1]
, so we restate it below.

Lemma 4.1.3
.

The cone
C_{\mathsf{zip},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is a neighborhood of
X^{*}_{-}(L)_{{\rm reg}}
in
X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in X^{*}_{-}(L)_{{\rm reg}}
, we show that
C_{\mathsf{zip},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is a neighborhood of
\lambda
in
X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
.
By Lemma
4.1.2
, it suffices to show that for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in X^{*}_{+,I}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
, there is
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\lambda^{\prime}+r\lambda\in C_{\mathsf{zip},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
.
We may assume
\lambda^{\prime}\in X^{*}_{+,I}(T)
by scaling.
Let

be the section provided by Theorem
4.1.1
. By Lemma

we have

is nonzero; let

be a nonzero element therein. This section may have poles on the complement of
{\mathcal{U}}_{\mu}
. However, since

∎

Lemma 4.1.4
.

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{GS},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
and

We may ask whether

.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The cone
C_{\mathsf{pHa},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is a neighborhood of
X^{*}_{-}(L)_{{\rm reg}}
in
X_{+,I}^{*}(T)_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}

(2)

One has

(3)

P

\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the Frobenius

Since

in

(i). Assume that (i) holds. In particular,

Hence for all
\alpha\in I

\langle\chi,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=q\langle\chi,\sigma(w_{0,I}\alpha^{\vee})\rangle

w_{0,I}\alpha
is a negative root, and so is

to itself, and all elements in the image satisfy

for all

, hence any character
\chi\in X^{*}(L)
is orthogonal to

for all

. Hence we must have

is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Next, for
\alpha\in I
, let

such that

\lambda\in X_{-}^{*}(L)_{{\rm reg}}

As before, we deduce

cannot be orthogonal to all

\Rightarrow
(iii). Finally, the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow

\Rightarrow
(4) in the proof of
[
Kos19

(after changing

to

∎

Definition 4.1.6
.

We say that a cocharacter datum

are satisfied.

The main result of this section is that (i), (ii), (iii) above are also equivalent to the equality

is of Hasse-type.

means by definition that

(

) can only potentially generalize to Hodge-type Shimura varieties

Lemma 6.2.2

A.4
Special cases I: Maximal Dynkin triples

Definition A.4.1
.

We say that a Dynkin pair
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}})
is
maximal
if, for every connected component
{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}
of
{\mathfrak{D}}
, either
{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{I}}={\mathfrak{D}}_{i}
or
{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{I}}
is a proper, maximal sub-diagram of
{\mathfrak{D}}
, i.e.,
\card({\mathfrak{D}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{I}})=\card({\mathfrak{D}}_{i})-1
. We say that a Dynkin triple
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
is maximal if the underlying pair
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}})
is.

Remark A.4.2
.

By definition, a Dynkin pair
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}})
is maximal if and only if this is true of every component
({\mathfrak{D}}_{i},{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{I}})
.

Remark A.4.3
.

If the Dynkin triple
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
arises from
(G,\mu,P,B,T)
as in
A.2
, then
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}})
is maximal if and only if, for every nontrivial, minimal, normal, connected
k
-subgroup
G_{i}
of
G
, the Levi subgroup
L={\rm Cent}(\mu)\cap G_{i}
of
G_{i}
is either all of
G_{i}
or a proper, maximal Levi of
G_{i}
.

A
\sigma
-orbit of a Dynkin triple
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
is a Dynkin triple
({\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
such that
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
is a
\sigma
-orbit of connected components of
{\mathfrak{D}}
,
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime}:={\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathfrak{I}}
and
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma_{|{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}}\in\Aut({\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime})
.

Corollary A.4.4
.

A maximal Dynkin triple
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
satisfies
A.1.1
if and only if every
\sigma

A.5
Special cases II: Dynkin triples of Hodge-type

Let
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
be a Hodge-type Shimura datum, i.e., a Shimura datum which admits a symplectic embedding
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})\hookrightarrow(\GSp(2g),\mathbf{X}_{g})
into a Siegel-type Shimura datum for some
g\geq 1
.
For every prime
p
such that
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified, the process recalled in §
2.6
produces a connected, reductive
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group
G
from
\mathbf{G}
and a cocharacter
\mu\in X_{*}(G)
from
\mathbf{X}
.
Then
A.2
associates a Dynkin triple
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
to
(G,\mu)
(which may depend on
p
).

Definition A.5.1
.

We say that a Dynkin triple
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
is of Hodge-type if it arises from some Hodge-type Shimura datum
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
and some prime
p
by the process described above.

Combining Deligne’s classification of symplectic embeddings
[
Del79
, 1.3.9, 2.3.4-2.3.10]
with
A.4.4
gives:

Corollary A.5.2
.

Assume
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
is of Hodge-type.
Then
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
is maximal and
A.1.1
holds precisely in cases
(
1
), (
2
), (
6
) and the sub-case
B_{n}
of (
3
) of
A.4.4
.

In other words the only maximal triples
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
with
{\mathfrak{D}}
classical and connected which satisfy

but are not of Hodge-type are those where

is of type

Proofs

The proofs are exercises in reading the
Planches
of Bourbaki
[
Bou68
, Chap. 6, Planches I-IX]
. Most importantly, consulting
loc. cit.
, one finds:

Lemma A.6.1
.

The connected Dynkin diagrams
{\mathfrak{D}}
which have trivial opposition involution
-w_{0}=1
in
\Aut({\mathfrak{D}})
are precisely those of type
A_{1},B_{n}
(
n\geq 2
),
C_{n}
(
n\geq 3
),

D_{2n}
(
n\geq 2)
,
G_{2},F_{4},E_{7}
and
E_{8}
.

Proof of
A.3.1
.

Assume
\sigma=1
. Then the problem is to identify the sub-diagrams
{\mathfrak{I}}
which satisfy
-w_{0,{\mathfrak{I}}}=1
. Using
A.6.1
, one sees that
{\mathfrak{I}}
should contain none of the following

(1)

A connected component of type
A_{m}
with
m\geq 2
,

(2)

a connected component of type
D_{2k+1}
with
k\geq 2
,

(3)

a sub-diagram of type
E_{6}
.

The first two restrictions (
1
)–(
2
) establish
A.3.1
when
{\mathfrak{D}}
is not of type
E
. Type
E
is handled the same way, except that in addition one disqualifies the unique sub-diagram of type
E_{6}
. Note that the sub-diagrams of type
D_{5}
in
E_{6}
are excluded by (2).
∎

Proof of
A.3.4
.

Since
\sigma\neq 1
and
{\mathfrak{I}}\neq\emptyset
,
{\mathfrak{D}}
is connected by
A.3.3
. Thus
{\mathfrak{D}}
must be of type
A_{n}
(
n\geq 2
),
D_{n}
(
n\geq 3
) or
E_{6}
.

Consider type
E_{6}
. Since
\sigma\neq 1
, it must be the opposition involution
\sigma=-w_{0}
. There are precisely six
-w_{0}
-stable sub-diagrams without isolated points, of types
A_{2},A_{3},A_{2}\times A_{2}
,
D_{4}
,
A_{5}
and
E_{6}
.
The action of
\sigma
on the unique sub-diagram of type
D_{4}
is nontrivial, while
-w_{0}=1
for
D_{4}
. On the other hand, the action of
\sigma
on the
\sigma
-stable
A_{2}
is trivial, while
-w_{0}\neq 1
for
A_{2}
. Thus both of these sub-diagrams fail to satisfy
A.1.1
. The sub-diagram of type
A_{2}\times A_{2}
also fails to satisfy
A.1.1
because the Weyl group preserves connected components, hence so does the opposition involution
-w_{0}
. The remaining three sub-diagrams
A_{3},A_{5}
and
E_{6}
do satisfy
A.1.1
. This proves
A.3.4
(
4
).

In type
A
, if
\sigma\neq 1
then again
\sigma=-w_{0}
is the opposition involution. We then conclude the same way as was argued for
A_{2}\times A_{2}
in
E_{6}
, that the opposition involution of a diagram preserves its connected components.

In type
D
,
\sigma
will act trivially on
\sigma
-stable sub-diagrams of type
A
with more than one point, while these have
-w_{0}\neq 1
. On the other hand,
\sigma\neq 1
will act non-trivially on a sub-diagram of type
D
, so the latter must be of type
D_{2k+1}
rather than
D_{2k}
.
∎

Proof of
A.4.4
.

As in
A.4.2
,
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
is maximal if and only if all its
\sigma
-orbits are maximal.
The cases where
({\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime})^{\geq 2}\neq\emptyset
follow directly from
A.3.1
and
A.3.4
. Assume that
({\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is maximal and
({\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime})^{\geq 2}=\emptyset
. If
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
is disconnected, then
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
is of type
A_{1}^{m}
by
A.3.3
. If
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
is connected and and
({\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime})^{\geq 2}=\emptyset
, then
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
must have size one or two.
∎

Recall from
[
Del79
, 1.2.5]
that a vertex
v\in{\mathfrak{D}}
is
special
if the corresponding simple root
\alpha_{v}\in\Delta
has multiplicity one in the decomposition of the highest root of the connected component
{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}
of
{\mathfrak{D}}
containing
v
. Equivalently,
v
is special if and only if the corresponding fundamental coweight is minuscule.

Proof of
A.5.2
.

Assume that
({\mathfrak{D}},{\mathfrak{I}},\sigma)
is of Hodge-type. By
[
Del79
, 1.3.9]
, every connected component
{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}
of
{\mathfrak{D}}
is of classical type (
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
or
D_{n})
and for every component satisfying
{\mathfrak{I}}\cap{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}\subsetneqq{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}
, the complement
{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}\setminus({\mathfrak{I}}\cap{\mathfrak{D}}_{i})=\{v_{i}\}
for some special vertex
v_{i}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}
. Let
({\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime},\sigma)
be a
\sigma
-orbit and assume that
{\mathfrak{I}}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Then
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
is connected. The cases (
4
) and (
5
) of type
D_{2m}
with nontrivial
\sigma
in
A.4.4
are excluded as follows: Since
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
is a Shimura datum, the adjoint group
\mathbf{G}^{\ad}_{\mathbb{R}}
over
\mathbb{R}
is an inner form of its compact form.
The compact form of the adjoint split group of type
D_{2m}
is inner to the split form.
1
1

1

This follows from the facts that
\SO(2m,2m)
is
\mathbb{R}
-split of type
D_{2m}
, that

is its compact form, and that, for

Therefore, if

\sigma=1

is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple of type

We have excluded all the cases of

which don’t appear in

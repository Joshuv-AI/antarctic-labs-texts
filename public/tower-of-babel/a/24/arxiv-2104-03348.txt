Ping-pong partitions and locally discrete groups of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms, II: Applications
Sébastien Alvarez and Pablo G. Barrientos and Dmitry Filimonov and Victor Kleptsyn and Dominique Malicet and Carlos Meniño and Michele Triestino

Abstract
. In the first part of this work we have established an efficient method to obtain a topological classification of locally discrete, finitely generated, virtually free subgroups of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms. In this second part we describe several consequences, among which the solution (within this setting) to an old conjecture by P. R. Dippolito [Ann. Math. 107 (1978), 403–453] that actions with invariant Cantor sets must be semi-conjugate to piecewise linear actions. In addition, we exhibit examples of locally discrete, minimal actions which are not of Fuchsian type. MSC2020: Primary 37C85, 20E06. Secondary 20E08, 37B05, 37E10. Keywords: piecewise-linear maps, ping-pong, groups acting on the circle, Bass–Serre theory, virtually free groups, multiconvergence property, rotation number, locally discrete groups.



1.
Introduction and results

1.1.
On the structure of codimension-one foliations

In the recent years, probably under the impulse of the monographs by Ghys
[
16
]
and Navas
[
25
]
there has been an intense activity around the study of groups acting on the circle.
One historical motivation is the
theory of codimension-one foliations
. Indeed, an action of a group
G
on the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
gives rise, by suspension of the action, to a codimension-one foliation of a closed manifold. There is a perfect dictionary between the dynamics of the action on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and the dynamics of the leaves of the foliation. Foliations defined by suspensions represent a particular class (e.g. the manifold
M
must admit a flat circle bundle structure; if the foliation has no compact leaf, then it has a unique minimal set; etc.) however their study is important for developing new techniques, manufacturing examples, and test conjectures.

The main motivation for this work is an old conjecture in foliation theory stated by Dippolito
[
10
]
(see also Question 16 in the recent survey of Navas
[
26
]
for the ICM 2018):

Conjecture 1.1

(Dippolito)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a codimension-one foliation of a closed manifold which is transversely
C^{2}
, with exceptional minimal set
\Lambda
. Then there exists a
transverse measure supported on
\Lambda
for which the Radon-Nikodym
derivative of the action of any holonomy pseudogroup is locally constant.

As Dippolito writes in
[
10
]
, this conjecture is conditioned on the solution of a major open problem:

Conjecture 1.2

(Dippolito)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a codimension-one foliation of a closed manifold which is transversely
C^{2}
, with exceptional minimal set
\Lambda
. Then there exists a semi-exceptional leaf (that is, a boundary leaf of
M\setminus\Lambda
), whose germs of holonomy maps form an infinite cyclic subgroup.

The only result available in this direction goes back to the Ph.D. thesis of Hector (Strasbourg, 1972), which establishes it under the assumption of non-trivial
r
-jets for elements in the holonomy group, for some
r\geq 1
. This holds for instance in the case of transversely real-analytic foliations. This result is the so-called
Hector’s lemma
, see also Navas
[
24
, Appendix]
for a detailed account. For a short discussion around Conjecture
1.2
, see also Question B.1.1 in the list of problems in foliation theory compiled by Langevin
[
20
]
in 1992.

Our principal contribution is to show (or at least give strong evidences) that indeed Conjecture
1.2
is the unique obstacle towards Conjecture
1.1
. As a consequence of a more general result (Theorem
3.10
), we are able to establish Conjecture
1.1
for groups of real-analytic diffeomorphisms of the circle:

Theorem D
.

Let

be a finitely generated group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms acting with a minimal invariant Cantor set. Then the action of

The definition of Thompson’s group

will be given in Definition

. Concisely, it is defined as the group of all dyadic piecewise linear homeomorphisms of the circle

.

The proof of Theorem

relies on an old theorem of Ghys
[

]
and a recent work of Deroin, Kleptsyn and Navas

, after which we have an efficient way to determine a topological classification of groups of real-analytic diffeomorphisms with a minimal invariant Cantor set and we are able to apply Theorem

. Precise statements will be recalled in the next section.
We are confident that our strategy for Theorem

can be extended to deal with transversely

1.2.
Multiconvergence property

The next results give further dynamical information about the actions of virtually free, locally discrete subgroups of
\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, notably giving a description of periodic points.

Before stating Theorem
E
, recall that one of the deepest results on groups acting on the circle is the characterization of Fuchsian groups (i.e. discrete subgroups of
\mathsf{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
), up to
C^{0}
conjugacy, by the so-called convergence property (by works of Tukia
[
28
]
, Gabai
[
13
]
, Casson and Jungreis
[
7
]
), which is the case
K=1
in the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
G\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a subgroup. We say that
G
has the
multiconvergence property
if there exists a uniform
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every infinite sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
of distinct elements in
G
, there exist finite subsets
A
and
R\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
, with
\#A,\#R\leq K
, and a subsequence
\{g_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
such that the sequence of restrictions

\{g_{n_{k}}\restriction_{\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R}\}_{k}
pointwise converges, as
k\to\infty
, to the locally constant map
g_{\infty}
, with image
g_{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R)=A
, with
\#R
discontinuity points at
R
and such that
g_{\infty}(a)=a
for every
a\in A\setminus R
.

Typical examples of groups with the multiconvergence property are discrete subgroups of finite central extensions of
\mathsf{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, but we also have:

Theorem E
.

If
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is a finitely generated, virtually free, locally discrete subgroup, then it has the multiconvergence property.

This immediately implies the following.

Corollary F
.

If
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is a finitely generated, virtually free, locally discrete subgroup, then the number of fixed points of a non-trivial element in
G
is uniformly bounded.

Let us discuss some direct consequences on the possible values of rotation numbers. The following definition is inspired by the monograph by Kim, Koberda and Mj
[
19
]
:

Definition 1.4
.

Let
G\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a subgroup. The
rotation spectrum

\rot(G):=\{\rot(g)\mid g\in G\}
of
G
is the collection of rotation numbers of elements of
G
.

The classical Denjoy theorem states that if a diffeomorphism
f
in
\mathsf{Diff}^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
has irrational rotation number (thus the subgroup
\langle f\rangle\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
has infinite rotation spectrum), then the action of
\langle f\rangle
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is minimal.
The following corollary is an analogue for finitely generated groups of real-analytic diffeomorphisms:

Corollary G
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated subgroup of
\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with infinite rotation spectrum. Then
G
acts minimally on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose
G
does not act minimally, then either it acts with a finite orbit (in which case the proof ends easily), or it acts with a minimal invariant Cantor set. In the latter case, Denjoy theorem implies that every element has rational rotation number. Moreover by a result of Ghys
[
15
]
,
G
must be virtually free. Observe that a finitely generated virtually free group cannot have elements of arbitrary large period; also, by Corollary
F
, we have a uniform bound on periods of periodic orbits and thus on denominators of rotation numbers.
∎

Remark 1.5
.

The regularity assumption
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
in Corollary
G
is necessary: Thompson’s group
T\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
has infinite rotation spectrum (it contains all dyadic rotations, and actually
\rot(T)=\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, see Lemma
3.4
) and is semi-conjugate to a subgroup of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
acting with a minimal invariant Cantor set (Ghys and Sergiescu
[
14
]
).

In
[
23
]
, Matsuda proved that if a subgroup
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is non-locally discrete and acts without finite orbits, then
G
has infinite rotation spectrum. As a direct consequence of Corollary
F
, we obtain that the converse holds for virtually free groups:

Corollary H
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated, virtually free subgroup of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, whose action on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
has no finite orbit. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

G
is locally discrete;

(2)

the rotation spectrum
\rot(G)=\{\rot(g)\mid g\in G\}
is finite.

Remark 1.6
.

After Conjecture
1.11
, we expect that the statement of the corollary remains valid without the virtual-freeness assumption.

Remark 1.7
.

Let us mention an open problem suggested by Matsuda
[
23
]
: if
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
has infinite rotation spectrum, does it contain an element with irrational rotation number?

1.3.
On the classification of locally discrete groups

For the next result, we recall some context, starting from the following definition.

Definition 1.8
.

A subgroup
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is (
C^{1}
-)
locally discrete
if for every interval
I\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
the restriction of the identity to
I
is isolated in the
C^{1}
topology among the set of restrictions

G\restriction_{I}=\{g\restriction_{I}\}_{g\in G}\subset C^{1}(I;\mathbb{S}^{1}).

Remark 1.9
.

Notice that
a priori
the notion of local discreteness is stronger than simple discreteness. However no example of discrete, non-locally discrete group is known. In this direction, Bertrand Deroin informed us that he is able to find many such examples inside the group of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms. However, the question in real-analytic regularity stays open, see also the discussion about work of Eskif and Rebelo
[
11
]
below.

Also, when
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is a group of
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms, we introduce the subset of
non-expandable points

\mathsf{NE}(G)=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\mid g^{\prime}(x)\leq 1\,\text{for every }g\in G\}
.
Recent developments of Rebelo, the authors and collaborators
[
27
,
12
,
8
,
2
,
9
]
have clarified the picture for locally discrete groups:

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a finitely generated subgroup.

(1)

If
\mathsf{NE}(G)\neq\varnothing
, then
G
is locally discrete.

(2)

If
G
is locally discrete and is

•

neither one-ended, non-finitely presentable, nor

•

one-ended, with unbounded torsion,

then
G
must be of the following type:

•

either virtually free and
\mathsf{NE}(G)\neq\varnothing
, or

•

C^{\omega}
conjugate to a finite central extension of a cocompact Fuchsian group, and
\mathsf{NE}(G)=\varnothing
.

Conjecture 1.11

(“Missing Piece” Conjecture from Alvarez
et al.

[
2
]
)
.

The dichotomy in Theorem
1.10
holds for every locally discrete, finitely generated subgroup
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

This suggests that locally discrete subgroups of
\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
should be quite elementary, and that subgroups of
\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
of more complicated type should be non-locally discrete. For instance,
\mathsf{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
contains some closed hyperbolic 3-manifold groups as non-locally discrete subgroups (this is well-known to experts and one early reference is in the book of Maclachlan and Reid
[
21
, Section 13.7]
; see also the discussion in Bonatti, Kim, Koberda and Triestino
[
4
]
).

Natural examples of locally discrete groups are the fundamental groups of hyperbolic surfaces of finite type (they are discrete in
\mathsf{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\subset\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
) and their finite central extensions. For a certain time, this was the only known class of such examples, thus motivating Ghys to ask whether any locally discrete subgroup of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
acting minimally on the circle comes from a surface group (personal communication). We will see in this work (Section
3.4
) that ping-pong partitions give a simple combinatorial way to construct a rich collection of exotic examples of locally discrete subgroups
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, giving negative answer to the question of Ghys. The main technical ingredients are Theorem
3.10
(realization in
\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
), and Theorem
3.13
for realizing
minimal
free group actions.

On the other hand, finitely generated subgroups of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
that are non-locally discrete have been extensively studied by different authors in the last two decades. In particular, they display strong rigidity properties. As a sample statement, let us mention the following result by Eskif and Rebelo
[
11
]
, which is about groups which are non-
C^{2}
-locally discrete (the definition is the same as above, but considering the
C^{2}
closure):

Theorem 1.12

(Eskif and Rebelo)
.

Consider two finitely generated, non-abelian subgroups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Suppose that these groups are non-
C^{2}
-locally discrete. Then every homeomorphism
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
satisfying
G_{2}=hG_{1}h^{-1}
coincides with an element of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

Clearly if
G
is non-
C^{2}
-locally discrete, then it is also non-
C^{1}
-locally discrete, but the converse is currently unknown (cf. Remark
1.9
).
However, we have the following statement, which extends
[
11
, Theorem B]
by Eskif and Rebelo (by a completely different approach, we are able to remove a technical assumption).

Corollary I
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a finitely generated, non-elementary Gromov-hyperbolic group, and consider two topologically conjugate faithful representations
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}:\Gamma\to\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Assume that
\rho_{1}(\Gamma)\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is non-
C^{2}
-locally discrete. Then every orientation-preserving homeomorphism
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
conjugating the representations
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
coincides with an element of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

Proof.

If
G_{1}=\rho_{1}(\Gamma)
(and thus
G_{2}=\rho_{2}(\Gamma)
) admits a finite orbit, then we can repeat the quite standard argument in
[
11
]
. Otherwise, after
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
(which is an intermediate step for Theorem
1.12
), it is enough to prove that
\mathsf{NE}(G_{2})=\varnothing
. Recall that a Gromov-hyperbolic group is always finitely presented and with bounded torsion
[
5
, Chapter III.
\Gamma
]
.
After Theorem
1.10
, if
\mathsf{NE}(G_{2})\neq\varnothing
, the Gromov-hyperbolic group
G_{2}
is virtually free and therefore it admits a ping-pong partition by Theorem
A
. Therefore
G_{1}
, being
C^{0}
conjugate to
G_{2}
, also admits a ping-pong partition. This contradicts that
G_{1}
is non-
C^{2}
-locally discrete.
∎

Organization of the paper

2.
Ping-pong partitions for virtually free groups

As we explained in Section
1.3
, an important aspect of the classification of locally discrete subgroups
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is to understand how
virtually free groups
may act. For this, we have introduced
in
[
1
]
the notion of
ping-pong partition
, which is a semi-conjugacy invariant, depending on a
marking
. We briefly recall the notions appearing in
[
1
]
. It is a classical result that a finitely generated group is virtually free if and only if it admits a proper isometric action (i.e. with finite stabilizers) on a locally finite simplicial tree, with bounded fundamental domain. A
marking
of a virtually free group
G
is the choice of an orientation-preserving isometric action
\alpha:G\to\Isom_{+}(X)
of
G
on a tree
X
and a fundamental domain
T\subset X
. Given such a marking, we denote by
\overline{X}=(V,E)
the quotient graph of the action, and the choice of
T\subset X
determines a spanning tree in
\overline{X}
, with set of oriented edges
E_{T}\subset E
. We write
S:=E\setminus E_{T}
. The marking determines finite vertex groups
G_{v}\subset G
(for
v\in V
) and edge groups
A_{e}
(for
e\in E
) with boundary injections
\alpha_{e}:A_{e}\to G_{o(e)}
,
\omega_{e}:A_{e}\to G_{t(e)}
(here
o(e)
and
t(e)
denote respectively the origin and target of the oriented edge
e\in E
). This gives the following presentation of the group
G
, as the fundamental group of a graph of groups:

(2.1)

G\cong\pi_{1}(\overline{X};G_{v},A_{e})=\mathopen{\resizebox{85197}{1671168}{$\Bigg\langle$}}\hbox{$G_{v},E\,\left|\begin{array}[]{lr}\rel(G_{v})&\text{for }v\in V,\\
\overline{e}=e^{-1}&\text{for }e\in E,\\
e=\mathrm{id}&\text{for }e\in E_{T},\\
e^{-1}\alpha_{e}(g)e=\omega_{e}(g)&\text{ for }e\in E,g\in A_{e}\end{array}\right.$}\mathclose{\resizebox{85197}{1671168}{$\Bigg\rangle$}}.

Remark 2.1
.

Virtually free groups of circle homeomorphisms are of very special form: vertex groups are finite cyclic, and in fact they are always free-by-finite cyclic (see
[
1

Definition 2.4

(Ping-pong partition)

Let

A

(PPP 1)

Letting

defines an interactive family in the sense of Definition

, with three additional requirements: for any
v\in V

the subsets
U_{v}^{e}

3.
Realization

In this section we discuss the problem of realization. That is, given a ping-pong partition for a marked virtually free group
(G,\alpha,T)
of circle homeomorphisms, is it possible to find a new action of
(G,\alpha,T)
with equivalent ping-pong partition, but of a given desired regularity? We will be particularly interested in the case of piecewise linear regularity (Theorem
D
, related to Dippolito’s conjecture) and real-analytic regularity (for the examples in Section
3.4
).

Some of the results are obtained by performing small perturbations of the original action. For this, we recall that the group
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is a topological group when endowed with the topology of uniform convergence. This topology is determined by the uniform distance: given
f
and
g
in
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, define
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}d_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(f(x),g(x))
, where

d_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
denotes the distance on the circle on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
induced by the Euclidean distance on the line (so
d_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(x,y)
is the length of the shortest connected component of
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\{x,y\}
).
This also defines a topology on the space of representations of a group
G
in
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
: two representations
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
in
\mathsf{Hom}(G,\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1}))
are
\varepsilon
-close if there exists a symmetric generating system
\mathcal{G}
of
G
such that
\sup_{s\in\mathcal{G}}d_{C^{0}}(\rho_{1}(s),\rho_{2}(s))\leq\varepsilon
.

When two points
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
are sufficiently close, we can use a Euclidean chart to measure their distance, and we will often write
|x-y|=d_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(x,y)
, where
x-y
denotes the usual operation in any Euclidean chart.

Lemma 3.11

f^{\prime}(x)<1
for every
x\in(x_{j},x_{j+1})+\mathbb{Z}

Proof.

Using a trigonometrical approximation, we shall find a real-analytic function with the required properties. We will first construct the derivative in the form
f^{\prime}(x)=1+q(x)r(x)
where
q
is a
1
-periodic trigonometric polynomial with roots at
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and
r
is everywhere positive. More precisely, consider the subset
S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
formed by all

such that

\frac{y_{j}-y_{j-1}}{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}-1\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{y_{j+1}-y_{j}}{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}-1

\epsilon_{j}=1
if

, and
\epsilon_{j}=2
if
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus S
. For an appropriate choice of sign, the trigonometric polynomial

q(x)=\pm\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sin^{\epsilon_{j}}\left(\pi(x-x_{j})\right)
has the desired properties.

Let us look for a
1
-periodic function
r\in C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
of the form
r(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}S_{j}\,r_{j}(x)
, where
S_{j}>0
and the

will be constructed as approximate characteristic functions of the subsets
(x_{j},x_{j+1})+\mathbb{Z}
.
Given a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, consider the following
1
-periodic function
\varrho_{j}^{\varepsilon}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R})

\varrho_{j}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\varepsilon&\text{for }x\not\in[x_{j},x_{j+1}]+\mathbb{Z},\\[5.0pt]
\varepsilon+\left(\dfrac{1}{|q(x_{j}+\varepsilon)|}-\varepsilon\right)\dfrac{x-x_{j}-m}{\varepsilon}&\text{for }x\in[x_{j},x_{j}+\varepsilon]+m,\\[5.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{|q(x)|}&\text{for }x\in[x_{j}+\varepsilon,x_{j+1}-\varepsilon]+m,\\[5.0pt]
\varepsilon-\left(\dfrac{1}{|q(x_{j+1}-\varepsilon)|}-\varepsilon\right)\dfrac{x-x_{j+1}-m}{\varepsilon}&\text{for }x\in[x_{j+1}-\varepsilon,x_{j+1}]+m,\end{array}\right.(m\in\mathbb{Z}).

Using Weierstrass approximation theorem, we can take a
1
-periodic trigonometric polynomial
r_{j}^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
which is
\varepsilon/2
-close to
\varrho_{j}^{\varepsilon}
(with respect to the uniform distance). Note that after this choice, the function
r_{j}^{\varepsilon}
is everywhere positive for sufficiently small

.
Also, by construction, the function

tends pointwise to
\mathbf{1}_{(x_{j},x_{j+1})+\mathbb{Z}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and therefore the quantity
a_{jk}^{\varepsilon}:=\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}|q(x)|r^{\varepsilon}_{j}(x)\,dx
(where we set

) tends to
(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\delta_{jk}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

In order to ensure property (
1
) we consider the following system of linear equations in the unknowns

:

(3.5)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}S_{j}\,a_{jk}^{\varepsilon}=\left|(y_{k+1}-y_{k})-(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\right|\hskip 10.00002pt(k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}),

where we write
y_{n+1}=y_{1}+1
. By the previous observation, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the matrix of the system (
3.5
) tends to the diagonal matrix
\mathsf{diag}(x_{2}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}-x_{n})
, and therefore for sufficiently small

) admits a unique solution, which we denote by

. Note that for every

S_{j}^{\varepsilon}\to\left|\dfrac{y_{j+1}-y_{j}}{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}-1\right|\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Set then

, and observe that for every

we have

Therefore, we can find
\varepsilon>0
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

•

the system (
3.5
) has a solution (hence the function

is defined),

•

for every

(respectively,

The next statement is the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.12
.

The group of real-analytic diffeomorphisms
\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is combinatorial.

Proof.

As we are considering
D_{0}=\mathbb{S}^{1}
, condition (C
1
) is trivially satisfied. Lemma
3.11
above gives condition (C
2
). Condition (C
3
) is clear (all rigid rotations are in
\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
).
It is well-known that any two real-analytic diffeomorphisms of finite order and same rotation number are
C^{\omega}
conjugate (see e.g.
[

, II.6]
): let

be of finite order and
\rot(a)=p/q
, denote by
A
any lift of
a
to the real line, then
h_{a}(x)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}A^{i}(x)-i\frac{p}{q}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
defines a
C^{\omega}
conjugacy of
a
to the rotation
R_{p/q}
of angle
p/q\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Therefore given any two finite order elements
a
and
b\in\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
with same rotation number
\rot(a)=\rot(b)=p/q
, the composition
h=h_{b}^{-1}\circ h_{a}
conjugates
a
to
b
. We claim that it also satisfies the additional requirements in condition (C
4
). For this, let
E\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a finite subset such that

for every

, and denote by

and

and

h_{a}(x)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}A^{i}(x)-i\frac{p}{q}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}B^{i}(x)-i\frac{p}{q}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}=h_{b}(x)

∎

3.3.
Minimality criterion

Ping-pong partitions are an adapted tool to recognize semi-conjugate actions, but they do not provide full topological information. In particular, it is unclear how to decide whether an action with ping-pong partition is minimal. In this direction we prove the following partial result.

Theorem 3.13
.

Every marked, finitely generated free subgroup of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with a proper ping-pong partition is semi-conjugate to a subgroup of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
whose action is minimal.

We will prove Theorem
3.13
later in the subsection. Before this, we will make some general considerations for recognizing minimal actions of virtually free groups.

Remark 3.14
.

At the present moment we are unable to extend Theorem
3.13
to every
virtually
free group; technical difficulties arise when there are torsion elements. In fact, it could be that not every combinatorial configuration is realizable in real-analytic regularity: an elementary instance of this is Kontsevich’s “métro ticket” theorem, discussed by Ghys in
[
17
]
, and our approach is faced to an obstruction of similar nature. In some particular cases, it is possible to construct a minimal action, for instance when the group is a central extension of a free group, or when the group is of the form
F_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, where the cyclic group acts on
F_{n}
by permutation of a free basis (in which case one has simply to implement the symmetries of the free generating set in their real-analytic realizations as in Lemma
3.11
).

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
(G,\alpha,T)
be a marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup of

.

Let

be the subcollection of intervals

such that there exists a finite sequence

such that for every

. It is clear that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is a finite family of neutral intervals of
\mathcal{I}
. As before, we denote by

.

For

(that is, we iterate

3.3.2.
Strictly Markovian partitions

In order to prove Theorem
3.13
, we need the following definition.

Definition 3.20
.

Let
(G,\alpha,T)
be a marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. We say that a ping-pong partition
\Theta
for
(G,\alpha,T)
is
strictly Markovian
if it is proper, its gaps are degenerated, and it has no neutral interval.

The next two lemmas show how to reduce to the case of group actions with strictly Markovian ping-pong partitions.

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
(G,\alpha,T)
be a marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with proper ping-pong partition
\Theta
and minimal invariant set
\Lambda
. Then for every atom
O\in\Theta
, we have
O\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

As
\Lambda\subset\bigcup_{O\in\Theta}\overline{O}
, there exists an atom
O\in\Theta
intersecting
\Lambda
. Now, by the
G
-invariance of
\Lambda
, we can propagate this to every other atom of
\Theta
using the properties (IF
2
,
7
,
8
) of an interactive family.
∎

Lemma 3.22
.

Every marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with a proper ping-pong partition is semi-conjugate to a marked subgroup of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with a strictly Markovian ping-pong partition.

Proof.

If the action of the subgroup is minimal then the ping-pong partition has no neutral intervals (see Lemma
3.19
) and its gaps are degenerated. We assume next that the minimal invariant set
\Lambda
is a Cantor set. We write
(G,\alpha,T)
for the marked subgroup, and
\Theta
for the ping-pong partition.
Take a minimalization of
G
, that is, a subgroup
G^{\prime}\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, isomorphic to
G
, whose action is minimal and semi-conjugate to that of
G
. Write
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
for the surjective map realizing the semi-conjugacy between
G
and
G^{\prime}
. Note that
h
is finite-to-1 on
\Lambda
.
By Lemma
3.21
, for every atom
O\in\Theta
, the intersection
O\cap\Lambda
is infinite (because
\Lambda
has no isolated points) so the image
h(O)
has non-empty interior.
We define
\Theta^{\prime}=\{\mathsf{Int}(h(O))\}_{O\in\Theta}
. As
h
defines a semi-conjugacy,
\Theta^{\prime}
defines a ping-pong partition for the action of
G^{\prime}
(with respect to the marking induced by the isomorphism with
G
). Since
G^{\prime}
is minimal, we are now reduced to the first case considered in this proof, so the ping-pong partition
\Theta^{\prime}
has no non-degenerated gaps and no neutral intervals.
∎

3.3.3.
Markovian expansion and first returns

We assume that
(G,\alpha,T)
is a marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with a strictly Markovian ping-pong partition
\Theta
.
As the ping-pong partition
\Theta
is strictly Markovian, for any point
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
there exists a unique interval
I_{x}\in\mathcal{I}
such that
x
belongs to
I_{x}
union its leftmost point.
Fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. We define by induction a sequence of generators
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{G}
and intervals
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{I}
, as follows.
Write
I_{0}=I_{x_{0}}
and, for any
n\geq 1
, write
x_{n}=s_{n-1}(x_{n-1})
and
I_{n}=I_{x_{n}}
; for any
n\geq 0
, we take as
s_{n}\in\mathcal{G}
the first generator appearing in a shortest Markovian sequence for
I_{n}
.
The sequence of partial compositions
\{s_{n}\cdots s_{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a
Markovian expansion
of
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Take now
I\in\mathcal{I}
and let
x_{0}\in\Delta_{0}
be its leftmost point. Consider a Markovian expansion
\{s_{n}\cdots s_{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
x_{0}
and assume there exists a least integer
n\geq 1
such that
x_{n}=x_{0}
; then the element
h=(s_{n-1}\cdots s_{0})^{-1}
satisfies
I\supset h(I)
and
h(x_{0})=x_{0}
. In analogy with Definition
4.3
, we say that the element
h
is a
first return to
I
.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
(G,\alpha,T)
be a marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with a strictly Markovian ping-pong partition
\Theta
. Assume that for every
I\in\mathcal{I}
and every first return
h
to
I
, the map
h
has no fixed point on
I
. Then
\Delta_{0}\subset\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\Delta_{0}
. We take a Markovian expansion
\{s_{n}\cdots s_{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
x_{0}
. Since
\Delta_{0}
is finite, there exist two least integers
n<m
such that
x_{n}=x_{m}
. We write
x=x_{n}=x_{m}
for this point and
I=I_{n}=(x,y)
for the corresponding interval in
\mathcal{I}
. Consider the element
h=(s_{m-1}\cdots s_{n})^{-1}\in G
.
By minimality of the choice of
n
and
m
, the map
h
is a first return to
I
. In particular
h
has no fixed point on
h(I)\cap I
by hypothesis.
As
h(I)\subset I
, we deduce that
h^{n}(y)\to x
as
n\to\infty
. Note that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
h^{n}(y)\in\Delta_{\infty}=G(\Delta_{0})
. As
\Delta_{\infty}
accumulates on
\Lambda
(see
[

), we deduce that

, and thus, by

is in
\Lambda
.
∎

3.3.4.
Minimal and real-analytic realization

We keep the assumption that
(G,\alpha,T)
admits a strictly Markovian ping-pong partition
\Theta
.
For
l\geq 1
, we let
\mathcal{I}_{l}
be the collection of intervals of
\mathcal{I}
admitting a shortest Markovian sequence of length

.

Remark 3.24
.

By definition, we have that
I\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
if and only if there exists
s\in\mathcal{G}
such that
s(I)
is
\mathcal{I}
-Markovian. When
l\geq 2
, if

and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}
is a shortest Markovian sequence, then
s_{1}(I)\in\mathcal{I}_{l-1}
.
Since
\mathcal{I}
is finite there exists a largest

such that
\mathcal{I}_{k}
is non-empty, and as we are assuming that
\Theta
is strictly Markovian, we have
\mathcal{I}=\bigcup_{l=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{l}

For practical purposes, it will be better to work with a particular class of strictly Markovian ping-pong partitions:

Definition 3.25
.

Let
\Theta
be a ping-pong partition for a marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup
(G,\alpha,T)
of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.
We say that
\Theta
is

if it is strictly Markovian and for every interval

and shortest Markovian sequence

for

|s_{1}(I)|>|I|
.

Note the definition above is not restrictive:

Lemma 3.26
.

Every marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup
(G,\alpha,T)
of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with a strictly Markovian ping-pong partition is conjugate to a subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, for which the ping-pong partition induced by the conjugacy is geometric.

Moreover, when
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, we can take
G^{\prime}\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

Proof.

Let
\Theta
be the ping-pong partition for
(G,\alpha,T)
, with collection of intervals
\mathcal{I}
.
We first make the choice of a collection of intervals
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
, and then consider an appropriate conjugating map. For this, we take
a partition
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
of the circle into
\#\mathcal{I}
open intervals, and fix a bijection
\theta:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
which preserves the circular order of the intervals. For

, we set
\mathcal{I}_{l}^{\prime}=\theta(\mathcal{I}_{l})
. We choose the partition
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
so that the following conditions are satisfied:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\max_{I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}|I^{\prime}|<2\min_{I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}|I^{\prime}|;

(3.7)

\displaystyle\min_{I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{l-1}}|I^{\prime}|>\max_{I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{l}}|I^{\prime}|\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for any }l\geq 2.

We can now take a real-analytic diffeomorphism
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that for every

, we have

. Then the conjugate subgroup

Indeed, if
I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{1}
, then there exists a generator

is

such that

and by condition (

) we have
|s(I^{\prime})|>|I^{\prime}|
.
∎

.

Let
\Theta
be a geometric ping-pong partition for a marked, finitely generated, virtually free subgroup

\mathcal{I}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(1)

J_{n}\subset I_{n}
;

(2)

either
s_{n}(I_{n})=I_{n+1}
, or
s_{n}(I_{n})
is
\mathcal{I}
-Markovian and
I_{n+1}\subset s_{n}(I_{n})
;

(3)

s_{n}^{\prime}(x)>1
for every
x\in I_{n}
;

(4)

s_{n}(J_{n})=J_{n+1}
.

From these conditions it is clear that the sequence of lengths
|J_{n}|
is strictly increasing, and that the gaps
J_{n}
are pairwise disjoint (if two gaps of
\Lambda
have non-empty intersection they must be equal). This contradicts the fact that there are only finitely many gaps whose length exceeds a given constant.
∎

From this discussion, we deduce that ping-pong partitions for free groups admit minimal real-analytic realizations.

Proof of Theorem
3.13
.

In the case of a free group
G
, a marking corresponds to the choice of a symmetric free generating system
S
(see §
4.1
). We also choose a subset
S_{0}\subset S
such that
S=S_{0}\sqcup S_{0}^{-1}
. Let
\Theta
be a ping-pong partition for
(G,S)\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. After Lemma
3.22
, we can assume that
\Theta
is strictly Markovian, and after Lemma
3.26
we can assume that
\Theta
is geometric.
Using Lemma
3.11
, for every
s\in S_{0}
we can find a real-analytic diffeomorphism
\overline{s}\in\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
such that
s(x)=\overline{s}(x)
for every
x\in\Delta_{0}\cup s^{-1}(\Delta_{0})
, and such that if
I\in\mathcal{I}
is such that
|s(I)|>|I|
then
\overline{s}^{\prime}(x)>1
on
I
, whereas if
|s^{-1}(I)|>|I|
, then
(\overline{s}^{-1})^{\prime}(x)>1
on
I
. The first condition guarantees that the marked subgroup
(\overline{G}=\langle\overline{S}\rangle,\overline{S})
, where
\overline{S}=\{\overline{s},\overline{s}^{-1}:s\in S_{0}\}
, has an equivalent ping-pong partition (it is basically the starting partition
\Theta
), which is moreover geometric. By Theorem
B
, this gives that the action of
\overline{G}
is semi-conjugate to the action of

. The second condition on derivatives guarantees that the partition
\Theta
for
(\overline{G},\overline{S})
is infinitesimally geometric, so that from Lemma
3.28
we deduce that the action of
\overline{G}
is minimal.
∎

4.
Dynamical properties of DKN partitions

4.1.
The DKN partition for free groups

In the case of locally discrete free subgroups
G\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, the ping-pong partitions given by Theorem
A
are a straightforward consequence of the work of Deroin, Kleptsyn, and Navas
[
8
]
. In this case a marking is simply a choice of a free, symmetric generating system
S
for
G
. We will say that
(G,S)
is a
marked free group
. We will denote by
\|\cdot\|
the word-length on
G
with respect to the generating system
S
.
For any
s\in S
, consider the subset
W_{s}
of
G
defined by

(4.1)

W_{s}=\left\{g\in G\mid g=s_{\ell}\cdots s_{1}\text{ in normal form, with }s_{1}=s\right\},

and then consider the subset
U_{s}
of the circle defined by

(4.2)

U_{s}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\,\middle|\,\exists\text{ neighbourhood }I_{x}\ni x\text{ s.t.\penalty\ }\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{g\notin W_{s},\,\|g\|\geq n}|g(I_{x})|=0\right\}.

Remark 4.1
.

The definition (
4.2
) of the subsets
U_{s}
is the one given in
[
1
]
and it slightly differs from the one used in
[
8
]
. We refer to Appendix
A
for a comparison between the two different definitions (see in particular Proposition
A.6
).

The following properties of the collection of subsets
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
have essentially been proved in
[
8
]
, see also
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
and discussions therein. See also Appendix
A
.

Theorem 4.2

(Deroin, Kleptsyn, and Navas)
.

Let
(G,S)\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a finitely generated, locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms, with minimal invariant set
\Lambda
.
Consider the collection
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
defined in (
4.2
). We have:

(1)

every subset
U_{s}
is open;

(2)

every subset
U_{s}
has finitely many connected components;

(3)

any two different subsets
U_{s}
have empty intersection inside the minimal invariant set
\Lambda
;

(4)

the union of the subsets
U_{s}
covers all but finitely many points of
\Lambda
;

(5)

if
s,t\in S
, are such that
t\neq s
then

s(U_{t})\subset U_{s^{-1}}
.

Note that Theorem
4.2
does not imply that the collection of subsets
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
is a ping-pong partition, in the sense of Definition
2.4
, as two distinct elements of
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
may have non-empty intersection outside the minimal invariant compact set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
For this reason, for every
s\in S
we introduce a subset
\hat{U}_{s}\subset U_{s}
defined by the following procedure: if an endpoint of a connected component of
U_{s}
belongs to a gap
J\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\Lambda
(that is, a connected component of the complement
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\Lambda
), we remove
U_{s}\cap\overline{J}
from
U_{s}
. As
U_{s}
has finitely many connected components, we only have to do this operation finitely many times.
Keeping the terminology of
[
1
]
, we refer to the collection of subsets
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
as to the
DKN partition
, whereas
\{\hat{U}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
is the
DKN ping-pong partition
for
(G,S)
(which is a ping-pong partition in the sense of Definition
2.4
).

The following notion plays a crucial rôle in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, and it will also be useful here.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(G,S)\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a finitely generated, locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms, and let
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be the associated DKN partition. Let
s\in S
and let
I
be a connected component of
U_{s}
.
A non-trivial element
g\in G
, written in normal form as
g=s_{\ell}\cdots s_{1}
, is
wandering
(with respect to
I
) if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

s_{1}\neq s
, or equivalently
g\notin W_{s}
;

(2)

the intermediate images
s_{k}\cdots s_{1}(I)
, for
k\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}
, are pairwise disjoint.

Moreover, if
g
also satisfies
g(I)\cap I\neq\varnothing
, then we say that
g
is a
first return
(to
I
).

Remark 4.4
.

Wandering elements are called admissible elements in the terminology in
[
8
]
.

The following facts have been proved in
[
8
, §3.4]
in the case of minimal actions, and in
[
8
, §4]
for actions with minimal invariant Cantor sets. Formally speaking (see Remark
4.1
), the results in
[
8
]
apply to connected components of the partition
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
, but the proofs can be easily adapted to the partition
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
. Otherwise, the reader can see the discussion in Appendix
A
, from which it is possible to deduce all desired results for the partition
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
from those for the partition
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(G,S)\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a finitely generated, locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms, and let
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be the associated DKN partition. For any
s\in S
and connected component
I=(x_{-},x_{+})
of
U_{s}
, the following properties hold:

(1)

two distinct wandering elements
g
and
h
have disjoint images:
g(I)\cap h(I)=\varnothing
;

(2)

there exist two first returns
g_{-}
and
g_{+}
fixing
x_{-}
and
x_{+}
respectively, such that
g^{k}_{\pm}(x)\to x_{\pm}
as
k\to\infty
for every
x\in I
;

(3)

there exists a non-empty closed interval
J\subset I
such that

I=g_{-}(I)\sqcup J\sqcup g_{+}(I)
;
in particular for every first return
g
which is distinct from
g_{\pm}
one has
g(I)\subset J
;

(4)

for every
g\notin W_{s}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g
can be written uniquely as

g=\lambda g_{n}\cdots g_{1},

where for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the element
g_{i}\in G
is a first return and the element
\lambda\in G
is either wandering or trivial.

From this we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(G,S)\subset\mathsf{Diff}_{+}^{\omega}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a finitely generated, locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms, and let
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be the associated DKN partition. For any
s\in S
and connected component
I
of
U_{s}
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{g\notin W_{s},\,\|g\|\geq n}|g(I)|=0.

Proof.

We use Lemma
4.5
to fix some notation and constants.
First, for every element
g\notin W_{s}
take the largest
k=k(g)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g=\tilde{g}g_{0}^{k}
, with
g_{0}\in\{g_{-},g_{+}\}
and
\tilde{g}\notin W_{s}
. If
\widetilde{g}
is not wandering, there exist a first return
g_{1}\neq g_{0}
, and
\overline{g}\notin W_{s}
such that
g=\overline{g}g_{1}g_{0}^{k}
.
Write
I=g_{-}(I)\sqcup J\sqcup g_{+}(I)
as in Lemma
4.5
, then the subset

J^{\prime}:=g_{-}(g_{+}(I))\cup J\cup g_{+}(g_{-}(I))

is a closed subinterval of
I
.
Fix now
\varepsilon>0
. By compactness and the definition (
4.2
) of
U_{s}
, there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.3)

|g(J^{\prime})|\leq\varepsilon\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every $g\notin W_{s}$ with $\|g\|\geq N_{0}$}.

Since for fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there are only finitely many elements
g\in G
with
\|g\|\leq N
, and since they are uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we can fix
\delta(N)\in(0,\varepsilon]
such that

(4.4)

|g(I^{\prime})|\leq\varepsilon\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every $g\in G$ with $\|g\|\leq N$ and every interval $I^{\prime}$ with $|I^{\prime}|\leq\delta(N)$}.

Observe also that from Lemma
4.5
, we have

(4.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{h\text{ wandering},\,\|h\|\geq n}|h(I)|=0.

In particular there exists a positive integer
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.6)

|h(I)|\leq\delta(N_{0})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every wandering element $h$ of length $\|h\|\geq N_{1}$}.

Finally if
g_{0}=g_{\pm}
we have
|g_{0}^{k}(I)|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, so there exists
N_{2}>0
such that

(4.7)

|g_{0}^{k}(I)|\leq\delta(N_{0}+N_{1})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every $k\geq N_{2}$}.

Fix now
N:=N_{0}+N_{1}+N_{2}\max\{\|g_{-}\|,\|g_{+}\|\}
, take an element
g\notin W_{s}
with
\|g\|\geq N
, and write
g=\widetilde{g}g_{0}^{k}
as at the beginning of the proof.

Case 1
.

\widetilde{g}
is wandering.

Proof in Case
1
.

Remark that we must have
\|\widetilde{g}\|\geq N_{0}+N_{1}
or
k\geq N_{2}
. Assume first that
\|\widetilde{g}\|\geq N_{0}+N_{1}
, so that by (
4.6
) we have

|\widetilde{g}g_{0}^{k}(I)|\leq|\widetilde{g}(I)|\leq\delta(N_{0})\leq\varepsilon.

If otherwise
\|\widetilde{g}\|\leq N_{0}+N_{1}
, then
k\geq N_{2}
and we deduce from (
4.7
) that
|g_{0}^{k}(I)|\leq\delta(N_{0}+N_{1})
. As
\|\widetilde{g}\|\leq N_{0}+N_{1}
, then
|\widetilde{g}g_{0}^{k}(I)|\leq\varepsilon
by (
4.4
).
∎

Case 2
.

\widetilde{g}
is not wandering.

Proof in Case
2
.

We write
g=\overline{g}g_{1}g_{0}^{k}
as at the beginning of the proof.
Remark that we must have
\|\overline{g}\|\geq N_{0}
or
\|g_{1}\|\geq N_{1}
or
k\geq N_{2}
.
Assume first
\|\overline{g}\|\geq N_{0}
, and note that as
g_{1}\neq g_{0}
then we have
g_{1}g_{0}^{k}(I)\subset J^{\prime}
. Hence
|\overline{g}g_{1}g_{0}^{k}(I)|\leq|\overline{g}(J)|\leq\varepsilon
by (
4.3
). Next, assume
\|\overline{g}\|<N_{0}
and
\|g_{1}\|\geq N_{1}
, and note that by (
4.6
) we have

|g_{1}g_{0}^{k}(I)|\leq|g_{1}(I)|\leq\delta(N_{0}).

As
\|\overline{g}\|<N_{0}
, we conclude by (
4.4
) that

|\overline{g}g_{1}g_{0}^{k}(I)|\leq\varepsilon
. Finally, the case where
\|\widetilde{g}\|\leq N_{0}+N_{1}
and
k\geq N_{2}
follows from (
4.7
) and (
4.4
) as in Case
1
.
∎

The list of cases being exhaustive, this proves that if
g\notin W_{s}
is such that
\|g\|\geq N
, then
|g(I)|\leq\varepsilon
, as desired.
∎

4.2.
Multiconvergence property

In this subsection we prove Theorem
E
and its consequences.
We first observe that it is enough to consider the case of free groups.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
G\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a group of circle homeomorphisms, and let
H\subset G
be a subgroup of finite index. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the group
G
has the multiconvergence property;

(2)

the subgroup
H
has the multiconvergence property.

Proof.

The only non-trivial implication is (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
). That is, we assume that
H
satisfies the multiconvergence property with a uniform constant
K\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{g_{n}\}_{n}\subset G
be an infinite sequence of distinct elements. As
H\subset G
has finite index, there exists a coset
Ht
to which infinitely many elements of the sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
belong. By finite-index assumption, upon extracting, we can assume that
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
is contained in a single coset
Ht
, and we write
g_{n}=h_{n}t
, with
h_{n}\in H
.
Clearly the sequence
\{h_{n}\}_{n}\subset H
consists of distinct elements, and up to passing to a subsequence, we can assume that there exist finite subsets
A
and
R\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
\#A=\#R\leq K
for which the sequence of restrictions
\{h_{n}\restriction_{\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R}\}_{n}
converges to the locally constant map
h_{\infty}
, with discontinuities at
R
and values in
A
, as in Definition
1.3
. Then the sequence of restrictions
\{g_{n}\restriction_{\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus t^{-1}(R)}\}_{n}
converges to the map
h_{\infty}t
, which is locally constant on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus t^{-1}(R)
, and whose image is
A
. Therefore
G
satisfies the multiconvergence property, with the same uniform bound
K\in\mathbb{N}
as for the subgroup
H
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
E
.

After Lemma
4.7
, it is enough to assume that
G
is a free group, freely generated by a finite symmetric set
S
; we denote by
||\,\cdot\,||
the corresponding word-length function. Consider the corresponding DKN partition, given by the subsets
U_{s}
as in (
4.2
). We will prove that
G
verifies the multiconvergence property with
K=\max_{s\in S}b_{0}(U_{s})
, where
b_{0}(U_{s})
stands for the zeroth Betti number (number of connected components) of the open subset
U_{s}
. For this, let us take an infinite sequence
\{g_{m}\}_{m}
of distinct elements of
G
.
Up to passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that
\{g_{m}\}_{m}
converges monotonically to a point of the boundary
\partial G
of the free group
G
: there exist a monotone sequence of integers
\{n_{m}\}_{m}\subset\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n}\subset S
of generators such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{n}\neq s_{n+1}^{-1}
and for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
one has

g_{m}=s_{n_{m}}s_{{n_{m}}-1}\cdots s_{1}.

First, we find the repelling subset
R
. We start by the following elementary observation.

Claim 1
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(4.8)

s_{1}^{-1}\cdots s_{n}^{-1}(U_{s_{{n+1}}})\subset s_{1}^{-1}\cdots s_{{n-1}}^{-1}(U_{s_{{n}}}).

Proof of claim.

Indeed for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
s_{{n+1}}\neq s_{{n}}^{-1}
so by Theorem
4.2
.(
5
) we have
s_{n}^{-1}(U_{s_{{n+1}}})\subset U_{s_{n}}.
So the claim follows by precomposing by the element
s_{1}^{-1}\cdots s_{{n-1}}^{-1}.

∎

As a consequence of Claim
1
, the subsets
R_{n}:=\overline{s_{1}^{-1}\cdots s_{{n-1}}^{-1}(U_{s_{{n}}})}

form a decreasing sequence of non-empty compact subsets in
\overline{U_{s_{1}}}
. So the intersection

R:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}R_{n}

is a non-empty compact subset.

Claim 2
.

The number of connected components of
R
is bounded above by
K=\max_{s\in S}b_{0}(U_{s})
.

Proof of claim.

Indeed for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the subset
R_{n}
has the same number of connected components as the subset
U_{s_{n}}
, which is less than or equal to
K
by our choice of
K
.
∎

Claim 3
.

The subset
R\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
is finite, and thus by Claim
2
it has at most
K
points.

Proof of claim.

A connected component
I
of
R
is the decreasing intersection of connected components
I_{n}
of
R_{n}
. We must prove that
|I_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

By the pigeonhole principle, there exist
s\in S
, a connected component
J
of
U_{s}
and a sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{m_{k}}=s
and

I_{m_{k}}=\overline{s_{1}^{-1}\cdots s_{m_{k}-1}^{-1}(J)}.

Now since by hypothesis
s_{m_{k}-1}^{-1}\neq s_{m_{k}}
, it follows from Proposition
4.6
that
\lim_{k\to\infty}|I_{m_{k}}|=0
. As the intervals
I_{n}
are nested, this gives the desired conclusion.
∎

We now turn to the construction of the attracting subset
A
. Note that by Claim
2
, there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the number of connected components of
R_{n}
does not depend on
n\geq N_{0}
(this number equals
\#R
). Without loss of generality, we can assume that the sequence of elements
\{g_{m}\}_{m}
is such that
n_{m}\geq N_{0}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

By construction and Theorem
4.2
.(
5
), for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the image
g_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R_{N_{0}})
is contained in
U_{s_{n_{m}}^{-1}}

and has
\#R
connected components. Let
J_{0}
be a connected component of
g_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R_{N_{0}})
, let
I
be the connected component of
U_{s_{n_{0}}^{-1}}
containing it and let
J_{m}
denote
g_{m}g_{0}^{-1}(J_{0})\subset U_{s_{m}^{-1}}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By Proposition
4.6
, we have
|J_{m}|\leq|g_{m}g_{0}^{-1}(I)|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Let
x_{m}
denote the midpoint of
J_{m}
. By compactness of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, upon passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
x_{m}
converges to a point
a\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Running the argument for every connected component of
g_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R_{N_{0}})
, and upon considering a subsequence, we can also assume that there exists a finite subset
A
such that for every
y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R_{N_{0}}
, we have
g_{m}(y)\to a
as
m\to\infty
for some
a\in A
. By construction
\#A\leq\#R
.

Now for
n\geq N_{0}
and
m
such that
n_{m}\geq n
the subset
g_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R_{n})
is contained in
U_{s_{m}^{-1}}
and each of its connected components contains a unique connected component of
g_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R_{N_{0}})
.
Using the argument above, we see that the length of such a component tends to
0
as
m
tends to infinity so for every
y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R_{n}
, we have
g_{m}(y)\to a
as
m\to\infty
for some
a\in A
. Finally, since
n
is arbitrary, this fact holds for every
y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R
. This proves the multiconvergence property.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

We observe that if
G\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
has the multiconvergence property, then it is always possible to choose (with the notation as in Definition
1.3
) the subsets
A
and
R
such that
\#A=\#R
. Indeed, if we have
\#A<\#R
, then there exist a point
r\in R
and a point
a\in A
such that if
J_{-}
and
J_{+}
are the two connected components of
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus R
adjacent to
r
, we have
g_{n_{k}}(y)\to a
as
k\to\infty
for every
y\in J_{-}\cup J_{+}
. In particular we also have
g_{n_{k}}(r)\to a
as
k\to\infty
and we may remove
r
from
R
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
G\subset\mathsf{Homeo}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a subgroup with the multiconvergence property. Then the number of fixed points of a non-trivial element in
G
is uniformly bounded.

Proof.

Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
be the constant from Definition
1.3
of multiconvergence property.
Assume that
g\in G
is an element of infinite order and consider the sequence
\{g^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. As a consequence of the multiconvergence property the cardinality of any finite invariant cannot exceed
\#R=\#A\leq K
. This means in particular that
g
has at most
2K
fixed points.
∎

Proof of Corollary
F
.

Direct consequence of Theorem
E
and Lemma
4.9
. ∎

Proof of Corollary
H
.

As we mentioned in Section
1.2
, Matsuda proved in
[
23
]
that subgroups of
\mathsf{Diff}^{\omega}_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with finite rotation spectrum are locally discrete. The converse implication follows from Corollary
F
and the fact that finitely generated virtually free groups have bounded torsion. We propose an alternative and simpler argument to prove this direction (which does not rely on the multiconvergence property, but on Theorem
4.2
only).

First, it is enough to consider free groups since if
G
is virtually free, it contains a normal free subgroup
H
of finite index
k
(by the so-called Poincaré lemma). Hence for every
g\in G
, we have
g^{k}\in H
and
k\rot(g)=\rot(g^{k})\in\rot(H)
. So if the rotation spectrum
\rot(H)
is finite, then so is
\rot(G)
(in fact, a finite-index normal subgroup
H\lhd G
can be obtained from the function rotation number, see the proof of
[
1
, Theorem C]
).

Assume now that
G
is free and locally discrete, and choose a free, symmetric generating system
S
, with associated DKN partition
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
. Let
g\in G
be a non-trivial element. As rotation number is a conjugacy-invariant, we can assume that
g
can be written in a cyclically reduced normal form
g=s_{n}\cdots s_{1}
, for some
n\geq 1
(that is, we assume
s_{n}\neq s_{1}^{-1}
). By Theorem
4.2
, we have

g(U_{s_{n}^{-1}})=s_{n}\cdots s_{1}(U_{s_{n}^{-1}})\subset U_{s_{n}^{-1}}.

By Theorem
4.2
, the number
q
of connected components of
U_{s_{n}}
is finite. So
g^{q}
preserves all connected components of
U_{s_{n}}
and therefore admits a fixed point:
\rot(g^{q})=0
. Since
q
is uniformly bounded by
K=\max_{s\in S}b_{0}(U_{s})
, we deduce that
\rot(G)
is finite, concluding the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Comparison of partitions

As we mentioned in Remark
4.1
, in
[
8
]
the definition of the sets
U_{s}
(appearing there as
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
) is not exactly the same. Since the present paper relies on the results of
[
8
]
, we discuss here differences and similarities of the two definitions.
As in Section
4.1
, we consider a locally discrete, marked free group
(G,S)
of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms, with minimal invariant set
\Lambda
. The subsets
W_{s}\subset G
and
U_{s}\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
will be the same as those defined at (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively.
We recall now the notation from
[
8
, §3.4]
. For
g\in G
written in normal form as
g=s_{\ell}\cdots s_{1}
, and
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, set

(A.1)

\widehat{S}(g,x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}(s_{\ell}\cdots s_{1})^{\prime}(x),

and, for a generator
s\in S
, set

(A.2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\,\middle|\,\sup_{g\notin W_{s}}\widehat{S}(g,x)<\infty\right\}.

Note that as soon as one
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
is non-empty (which is one of the most difficult results established in
[
8
]
), then every other
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s^{\prime}}
is non-empty, and their union is dense in
\Lambda
(see
[
8
, Lemma 3.20]
). Moreover, for any
s\in S
, only finitely many connected components of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
intersect
\Lambda
(this is
[
8
, Lemma 3.30]
in case of minimal actions, and
[
8
, Lemma 4.7]
for the case of actions with minimal invariant Cantor set). It follows that the union
\bigcup_{s\in S}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
covers the whole minimal invariant set
\Lambda
, but finitely many points.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(G,S)
be a locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms. For every generator
s\in S
, we have the inclusion
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\subset U_{s}
.

Proof.

The first part follows directly from the usual control of distortion (namely Schwarz’s lemma: see
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
). Let
x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
and
C_{x}=\sup_{g\notin W_{s}}\widehat{S}(g,x)<\infty
. Then there exists a neighbourhood
I_{x}
of
x
whose size depends only on the generators of
G
and on
C_{x}
such that for all
g\notin W_{s}
, and
y,z\in I_{x}
, one has
\frac{g^{\prime}(y)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\leq 2.
By definition, if we restrict ourselves to elements
g\notin W_{s}
, we have
\lim_{||g||\to\infty}g^{\prime}(x)=0,

so the uniform distortion control implies that
\lim_{||g||\to\infty}|g(I_{x})|=0
. We deduce that
x\in U_{s}
.
∎

Note that this implies that, for every
s\in S
, only finitely many connected components of
U_{s}
intersect
\Lambda
, and that
\bigcup_{s\in S}U_{s}
covers the whole minimal invariant set
\Lambda
but finitely many points.

We also deduce the following consequence, which is the analogue of Proposition
4.6
. Observe that the proof of Proposition
4.6
relies on Lemma
4.5
, which is stated for the DKN partition
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
but formally only proved in
[
8
]
for the partition
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
(although the proof can be easily adapted to the partition
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
).

Proposition A.2
.

Let
(G,S)
be a locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms. For every generator
s\in S
and connected component
I
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{g\notin W_{s},\,\|g\|\geq n}|g(I)|=0.

Next, we give a more precise description of the inclusion
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\subset U_{s}
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
(G,S)
be a locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms with minimal invariant set
\Lambda
. Fix a generator
s\in S
and two distinct connected components
I
and
I^{\prime}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
.

(1)

If both closures
\overline{I}
and
\overline{I^{\prime}}
intersect the closure of a gap of
\Lambda
, then they are contained in distinct connected components of
U_{s}
.

(2)

If
I
and
I^{\prime}
share a common endpoint, then they are contained in the same connected component of
U_{s}
.

Remark A.4
.

Note that, as a consequence of (
1
), in the situation (
2
) the common endpoint cannot belong to the closure of a gap of
\Lambda

Proof.

We first prove the assertion (
1
). Assume that both closures
\overline{I}
and
\overline{I^{\prime}}
intersect the closure of the same gap
J
of
\Lambda
. Assume that
I
and
I^{\prime}
are contained in the same connected component of
U_{s}
, so the closure
\overline{J}
of the gap is inside this component. By compactness and the definition (
4.2
) of
U_{s}
, this implies that

(A.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{g\notin W_{s},\,\|g\|\geq n}|g(J)|=0.

However, using the analogue of Lemma
4.5
for the partition
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
, applied to
I
, we get a first return
g\notin W_{s}
which fixes the endpoint of
I
contained in
J
(see more specifically
[
8
, Lemma 4.4]
). It follows that
g
fixes the whole gap
J
, contradicting (
A.3
).
Hence, the gap
J
is not contained in a connected component of
U_{s}
, and so the two connected components of
U_{s}
containing
I
and
I^{\prime}
respectively are disjoint.

Assume next that
I
and
I^{\prime}
have a common endpoint
x
, and write
I_{x}=I\cup\{x\}\cup I^{\prime}
, which is a neighbourhood of
x
. Note that for every
g\in G
, we have
|g(I_{x})|=|g(I)|+|g(I^{\prime})|
.
From Proposition
A.2
applied to
I
and
I^{\prime}
individually, we deduce that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{g\notin W_{s},\,\|g\|\geq n}|g(I_{x})|=0

and therefore
x\in U_{s}
. This proves (
2
).
∎

Finally, we recall the following fact and its proof, appearing in
[
1
, Lemma 7.6]
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
(G,S)
be a locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms. For every two distinct generators
s
and
s^{\prime}\in S
, we have
U_{s}\cap U_{s^{\prime}}\cap\Lambda=\varnothing
.

Proof.

Take distinct generators
s
and
s^{\prime}\in S
, and assume there exists
x\in U_{s}\cap U_{s^{\prime}}\cap\Lambda
. Then there exists a neighbourhood
I_{x}
of
x
such that
\lim_{||g||\to\infty}|g(I_{x})|=0
(we use that for all
g\in G
, we have
g\notin W_{s}
or
g\notin W_{s^{\prime}}
since
s\neq s^{\prime}
). We have
\Lambda\cap I_{x}\neq\varnothing
so Sacksteder’s theorem implies the existence of a point
y\in\Lambda\cap I_{x}
and an element
h\in G
such that
h^{\prime}(y)>1
. This contradicts that
\lim_{n\to\infty}|h^{n}(I_{x})|=0
.
∎

It is now quite straightforward to deduce from the previous lemmas that the two partitions
\{U_{s}\}_{s\in S}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
are “equivalent” in the following sense.

Proposition A.6
.

Let
(G,S)
be a locally discrete, marked free group of real-analytic circle diffeomorphisms. For every generator
s\in S
, we have the inclusion
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\subset U_{s}
and the complement
U_{s}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
consists of intervals entirely contained in closures of gaps of
\Lambda
and a finite number of points in
\Lambda
, which are topologically hyperbolic fixed points
x
for some element
g
in the group, but with derivative
g^{\prime}(x)=1
.

Proof.

Fix a generator
s\in S
. Lemma
A.1
gives the inclusion
U_{s}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
. Let
C
be a connected component of
U_{s}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
. Assume first that
C=\{x\}
is a single point, then Lemma
A.3
.(
2
) gives that this does not belong to the closure of a gap of
\Lambda
, and moreover, by the analogue of Lemma
4.5
for
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
(see more specifically
[
8
, Lemma 3.23]
for the case of minimal action, and
[
8
, Lemma 4.4]
for the case of minimal invariant Cantor set) its proof shows that there exists an element
g\notin W_{s}
fixing
x
. As
x\in U_{s}
, the point
x
must be a topologically hyperbolic fixed point for
g
, and moreover
g^{\prime}(x)=1
(this is given by
[
8
, Lemma 3.29]
in the case of minimal action, or by
[
8
, Lemma 4.6]
in the case of minimal invariant Cantor set).

Assume next that
C
is a non-trivial interval, and that
C
contains a point
x
of
\Lambda
in its interior. Then
x
is an accumulation point for
C\cap\Lambda
. As
\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\in S}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s^{\prime}}
covers
\Lambda
with the exception of finitely many points, there must be a different generator
s^{\prime}\neq s
in
S
such that
C\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s^{\prime}}\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing
. Using Lemma
A.1
we deduce that
C\cap U_{s^{\prime}}\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing
, and since
C\subset U_{s}
, we then have
U_{s}\cap U_{s^{\prime}}\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing
, contradicting Lemma
A.5
.
∎

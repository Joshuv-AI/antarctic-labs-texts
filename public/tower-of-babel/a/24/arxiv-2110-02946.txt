Nonlinear convective stability of a critical pulled front undergoing a Turing bifurcation at its back: a case study
Louis Garénaux

Abstract
. We investigate a specific reaction-diffusion system that admits a monostable pulled front propagating at constant critical speed. When a small parameter changes sign, the stable equilibrium behind the front destabilizes, due to essential spectrum crossing the imaginary axis, causing a Turing bifurcation. Despite both equilibrium states are unstable, the front continues to exist, and is shown to be asymptotically stable, against suitably-localized perturbations, with algebraic temporal decay rate t − 3 / 2 t^{-3/2} . To obtain such decay, we rely on point-wise semigroup estimates, and show that the Turing pattern behind the front remains bounded in time, by use of mode-filters.

1.
Introduction

Analysis of evolution problems in PDE often reveals a large variety of nontrivial dynamics. Among parabolic problems, the class of reaction-diffusion equations furnish a wide class of stable – and thus observable – solutions: planar waves such as propagating fronts or periodic patterns, rotating spirals,
etc
.

We are interested here in a system coupling a Kolmogorov-Petrovski-Piskunov (KPP) equation together with a Swift-Hohenberg (SH) equation, We work with
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>0
:

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u=d\partial_{xx}u+\alpha u(1-u^{2})+\beta v,\\[5.0pt]
\partial_{t}v=-(\partial_{xx}+1)^{2}v+v(\mu-\sigma v^{2})-\gamma v(1-u),\end{array}\right.

with parameters
d
,
\alpha
,
\mu
,
\sigma
,
\gamma
positive, and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
nonzero.
The scalar KPP equation is a typical model for front propagation from a stable to an unstable state
[
KPP37
,
Fis37
]
:

(2)

\partial_{t}u=d\partial_{xx}u+f(u).

The diffusion coefficient
d
is positive and the reaction term
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
is of KPP type: it admits two equilibrium states
f(0)=0=f(1)
with distinct stability w.r.t. time
f^{\prime}(0)>0>f^{\prime}(1)
. Generally, a concavity hypothesis is added:
f^{\prime\prime}(u)<0
for
u\in(0,1)
. Although
f(u)=\alpha u(1-u)
is the canonical choice, we will work with
f(u)=\alpha u(1-u^{2})
. All statements and proofs below adapt to the
u(1-u)
case.

Equation (
2
) admits a family of fronts
(q_{c})_{c>0}
– with
q_{c}
propagating at constant speed

q_{c}\to 1
when
x\to-\infty
) to the unstable one (
q_{c}\to 0
when
x\to+\infty
)
[
AW78
]
.
Supercritical fronts
c>c_{*}:=2\sqrt{d\,f^{\prime}(0)}=2\sqrt{d\alpha}
are
convectively stable
with exponential decay in time, against sufficiently localized perturbations. Indeed, when set in a moving frame, conjugating the problem with spatial exponential weights allow to both stabilize the essential spectrum and erase the eigenvalue at
\lambda=0
, creating a spectral gap at linear level
[
Sat76
]
.
Stability of the critical front
q_{*}:=q_{c_{*}}
is more involved, since the linear essential spectrum can only be
marginally stabilized
: in the optimal exponentially weighted space, essential spectrum lies at left of the imaginary axis but touches it, due to the presence of absolute spectrum
\left\{-\xi^{2}:\xi\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset\mathbb{C}
up to the origin. Last advances in this direction
[
Gal94
,
FH18
,
AS21
]
state that
q_{*}
is asymptotically stable with decay rate

, and that this rate is optimal. Gallay used renormalization group method; Faye and Holzer relied on point-wise estimates for the resolvent of the full problem; Avery and Scheel reduced the problem to its asymptotic properties through far-field decomposition, and use resolvent estimates on each side.
For subcritical speeds
0<c<c_{*}
, there still exists non-monotonic, homoclinic trajectories
q_{c}
from
1
to
0
. However, Sturm Liouville theory ensures the existence of unstable point spectrum, preventing stability.

The scalar SH equation is a simple model for the formation of periodic pattern
[
SH77
,
CH93
]
:

(3)

\partial_{t}v=-(\partial_{xx}+1)^{2}v+\mu v-\sigma v^{3}.

When the small parameter

becomes positive, (

\pm 1
. This causes the constant equilibrium state

to bifurcate into periodic profile of amplitude

. It can be obtained through center manifold theory
[

]
.

For all values of
\mu
, the coupled system (
1
) admits

as a front with propagation speed

. The linear coupling term

feeds the front dynamic with oscillatory pattern, while the nonlinear coupling term
\gamma v(1-u)
stabilize at linear level ahead of the front. Thus oscillating profile are expected to appear only behind the front.

Multiple works about bistable front –
i.e.
a front that connects two stable states:
f(-1)=0=f(1)
and
f^{\prime}(-1)<0
,
f^{\prime}(1)<0
– undergoing a Turing bifurcation have been done.
When bifurcation occurs behind the front,
[
SS01
]
showed that no modulated front appears: the Turing pattern travels at slow speed

, hence is left behind the front. However, the front survive after bifurcation, although through a non-modulated form. Its nonlinear stability is showed in
[
BGS09
]
for a general second-order setting.

When the state ahead of a bistable front bifurcates,
[
SS01
]
showed existence and linear stability of modulated fronts –
i.e.
coherent structures that link stable state behind to Turing pattern ahead – in a general second-order setting. For a system that ressembles (
1
),
[
GSU04
]
obtain nonlinear stability of such structure. We also mention
[
GS07
]
for a quadratic coupling
\beta v^{2}
instead of a linear one. The signed term therein allows to obtain

When bifurcation occurs both ahead and behind a bistable front, spectral stability of either traveling or standing pulses is showed for a general system
[

]
and
[
SS04
]
, while nonlinear stability of a traveling pulse for a precise system is showed in
[

]
.

For monostable KPP fronts, previous works rather investigated the Turing bifurcation in a non-local KPP context.
[
BNPR09
]
first investigate the existence of steady states and propagating fronts, together with the monotonicity of this fronts. A precise construction of modulated fronts is made in
[
FH15
]
, while global properties are obtained in
[
HR14
]
: boundedness of solutions when initial condition is non-positive, together with bounds on propagation speeds.
It seems that the question of the stability of such structure is open, as remarked in
[

,

]
. Our conclusion is that Turing bifurcation behave the same when it occurs behind a monostable or a bistable front: see
fig.

On one hand, the essential spectrum is unstable, which require to work in exponentially weighted spaces. Even so, the optimal exponential weight only allows to marginally stabilize the spectrum. On the other hand, the translation eigenvalue

in the essential spectrum does not contribute to a zero of the so-called Evans function, due to the corresponding eigenfunction

having only weak exponential decay:
q_{*}^{\prime}(x)\sim(ax+b)e^{-\frac{c_{*}}{2}x}
when

Let us emphasize that the coupling at linear level

This typically happens at

When no such parameter is available, one has to work with unstable spectrum in any weighted space. This is referred to as

, where this exact situation is investigated. Using a precise decomposition of perturbations, they conclude on nonlinear stability of the front

In the supercritical case

, it is possible to obtain spectral gap for the essential spectrum, and to remove the translation eigenvalue, by use of a strong enough weight
[
Sat76
]
. In such case, the situation is simpler than bistable case: exponential stability without a phase can be obtained by adapting the current proofs or the ones of
[
BGS09
]
. If one rather use the optimal supercritical weight, the situation has to be discussed.

In the monostable critical case, instability ahead of the front is still an open problem, and would be an interesting direction to follow. For example, one can replace the coupling term
\gamma v(1-u)
by
\gamma uv
. We point out that a modulated front for such system is necessarily non-positive, which prevent the use of a KPP reaction term

.

2.2.
Sketch of the proof

We informally present the main ideas of our proof.

First, the dynamic for fully weighted perturbation
U
writes as:

\partial_{t}U=\mathcal{L}U+\mathcal{N}(U).

Where the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
has marginally stable spectrum, similarly to the KPP equation, and the nonlinear part
\mathcal{N}
is unbounded w.r.t. to
x\in\mathbb{R}
. At linear level, we expect an algebraic decay:
t^{-3/2}
. We follow the approach from
[
FH18
]
: we first look at the solution to the linear Cauchy problem

(15)

\partial_{t}p=\mathcal{L}p,\hskip 40.00006ptp(0,\cdot)=p_{0},

and assume that it is expressed through a kernel:
p(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,y)p_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y
. The matrix valued function
\mathcal{G}_{\cdot}(\cdot,y)
has to satisfy the linear problem (
15
), with a Dirac delta initial condition:
\mathcal{G}_{0}(x,y)=\delta_{y}(x)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
.
Remark that
\mathcal{G}
is an upper-triangular matrix, due to the triangular structure of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{G}_{0}
.
We note it

\mathcal{G}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{G}^{\text{kpp}}&\mathcal{G}^{\text{co}}\\
0&\mathcal{G}^{\text{sh}}\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathcal{G}^{i}
solves
\partial_{t}\mathcal{G}_{t}=\mathcal{L}^{i}\mathcal{G}_{t}
when
i\in\left\{\text{kpp},\text{sh}\right\}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\text{co}}
accounts for the coupling terms.
We then apply Laplace transform to obtain the spectral Green kernel
G_{\lambda}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,y)\mathrm{d}t
, that satisfies the
fundamental eigenproblem

(\lambda-\mathcal{L})G_{\lambda}=\delta_{y},

for suitable
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. As above, it is an upper-triangular matrix that writes

G=\begin{pmatrix}G^{\text{kpp}}&G^{\text{co}}\\
0&G^{\text{sh}}\end{pmatrix},

where each
G^{i}
is the Laplace transform of
\mathcal{G}^{i}
for
i\in\left\{\text{kpp},\text{sh},\text{co}\right\}
. Hence, they satisfy

(\lambda-\mathcal{L}^{\text{kpp}})G_{\lambda}^{\text{kpp}}(\cdot,y)=\delta_{y},\hskip 40.00006pt(\lambda-\mathcal{L}^{\text{sh}})G_{\lambda}^{\text{sh}}(\cdot,y)=\delta_{y},

(\lambda-\mathcal{L}^{\text{kpp}})G_{\lambda}^{\text{co}}(\cdot,y)=\beta G_{\lambda}^{\text{sh}}(\cdot,y).

The homogeneous eigenproblems
(\lambda-\mathcal{L}^{i})\phi=0
with
i\in\left\{\text{kpp},\text{sh}\right\}
are ODEs, their solutions admit exponential behaviors at
\pm\infty
:

\phi(x)=e^{\nu(\lambda)x}(1+e^{-r\lvert x\rvert}\kappa(x)),

where
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
is a solution of the dispersion relations
\lambda-\mathcal{L}^{i,\pm}(\nu)=0
; the real
r>0
does not depend on
\nu
nor
\lambda
;
\kappa
is a bounded function on the half-line
\mathbb{R}_{\pm}
.
Such solutions can be concatenated to construct
G_{\lambda}^{i}(\cdot,y)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then, the coupled spectral green function expresses as the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-inner product

G_{\lambda}^{\text{co}}(x,y)=\langle G_{\lambda}^{\text{kpp}}(x,\cdot),\beta G_{\lambda}^{\text{sh}}(\cdot,y)\rangle.

This leads to spatial localization of
G_{\lambda}
, which is converted into temporal decay for
\mathcal{G}_{t}
through the inverse Laplace transform:

\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\Lambda}e^{\lambda t}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where
\Lambda
is a contour in the resolvent set
\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma_{\text{}}(\mathcal{L})
, that can be chosen as a continuous deformation of a sectorial contour, see
[
Dav02
, section 3]

Remark 2.5

3.1.
Essential and point spectrum

We say that a scalar differential operator
\mathcal{T}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}(x)\partial_{x}^{j}
is
exponentially asymptotic
if it converges with uniform exponential rate at
\pm\infty
: there exists
r>0
such that for all
0\leq j\leq n
,

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}e^{r\lvert x\rvert}\lvert a_{j}(x)-a_{j}^{\pm}\rvert=0.

We note
\Sigma_{\text{}}(\mathcal{T})
the spectrum of
\mathcal{T}
,
i.e.
the set of complex numbers
\lambda
such that
\lambda-\mathcal{T}:H^{n}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is not bounded invertible. To study the spectrum, we decompose it into two distinct parts. We say that
\lambda-\mathcal{T}
is Fredholm if its Fredholm index

\fred(\lambda-\mathcal{T}):=\dim\ker(\lambda-\mathcal{T})-\codim\range(\lambda-\mathcal{T})

is defined and finite. We define the point spectrum as

\Sigma_{\text{pt}}(\mathcal{T}):=\left\{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\text{}}(\mathcal{T}):\fred(\lambda-\mathcal{T})=0\right\},

it corresponds to the subset in which the rank-nullity theorem holds.
Then, the essential spectrum is the complementary set:

\Sigma_{\text{ess}}(\mathcal{T}):=\left\{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\text{}}(\mathcal{T}):\fred(\lambda-\mathcal{T})\text{ is non defined or nonzero}\right\}.

With such definitions, the asymptotic operator with piece-wise constant coefficients

\mathcal{T}^{\infty}(x):=\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}^{+}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}^{+}\partial_{x}^{j}&\text{if }x>0,\\
\mathcal{T}^{-}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}^{-}\partial_{x}^{j}&\text{if }x<0,\end{cases}

share the same essential spectrum as
\mathcal{T}
, see
[
KP13
, p 40]
. If both
\mathcal{T}^{+}
and
\mathcal{T}^{-}
are elliptic – in one dimensional space, it comes down to both

and

being positive – then

is located to the left of the asymptotic Fredholm borders

\Sigma_{\text{}}(\mathcal{T}^{+})\cup\Sigma_{\text{}}(\mathcal{T}^{-})=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}^{+}(i\xi)^{j}:\xi\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\cup\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}^{-}(i\xi)^{j}:\xi\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset\mathbb{C},

see again
[
KP13
, Theorem 3.1.13]
.

4

4

4.

4.1.
Mode filters

Since part of the spectrum of

is unstable, the dynamic for

is unstable at linear level. We count on the nonlinear terms to control

for large bounded times

. To do so, we mostly follow the approach of Guido Schneider, see
[

]
. We separate the

from the

modes in our solution: the first ones grow or are bounded at linear level, while the second ones decay exponentially, uniformly in
\mu
. Let us use a smooth, positive cut-off function

(28)

\chi_{\text{c}}(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in I_{\text{c}}:=[-\frac{9}{8},-\frac{7}{8}]\cup[\frac{7}{8},\frac{9}{8}],\\

with

the ball centered at
x
with radius
r
. We then work in Fourier space: the matrix
\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{-}(\xi)
has two eigenvalues

\lambda_{\text{c}}(\xi)=-(1-\xi^{2})+\mu,\hskip 40.00006pt\lambda_{\text{s}}(\xi)=-d\xi^{2}-2\alpha,

with associated eigenvectors
\varrho_{i}
, for
i\in\left\{\text{s},\text{c}\right\}
. We note
\varPi_{i}(\xi)\hat{V}=\langle\hat{V},\varrho_{i}^{*}(\xi)\rangle\varrho_{i}(\xi)
the respective parallel projections onto each of the eigenspaces, and we separate critical from stable frequencies:

\varPi_{\text{c}}V=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto\chi_{\text{c}}(\xi)\left\langle\hat{V}(\xi),\varrho_{\text{c}}^{*}(\xi)\right\rangle\varrho_{\text{c}}(\xi)\right),\hskip 40.00006pt\varPi_{\text{s}}V=V-\varPi_{\text{c}}V

Remark that
\chi_{\text{c}}^{2}\neq\chi_{\text{c}}
, so that neither
\varPi_{\text{c}}
or
\varPi_{\text{s}}
are projections. However, using
I_{\text{c}}^{h}:=[-\frac{5}{4},-\frac{3}{4}]\cup[\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}]
and
I_{s}^{h}:=[-\frac{17}{16},-\frac{15}{16}]\cup[\frac{15}{16},\frac{17}{16}]
, we define
\chi_{\text{c}}^{h}
and
\chi_{s}^{h}
with respective support
I_{\text{c}}^{h}+B(0,\frac{1}{4})
and
I_{s}^{h}+B(0,\frac{1}{16})
, in a similar way as (
28
). Then with

\varPi_{\text{c}}^{h}V:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi_{\text{c}}^{h}\varPi_{1}\hat{V}),\hskip 40.00006pt\varPi_{\text{s}}^{h}V:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1-\chi_{\text{s}}^{h})\varPi_{1}\hat{V})+\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varPi_{2}\hat{V}),

we have
\varPi^{h}_{i}\varPi_{i}=\varPi_{i}
for

. Ultimately, we will use a scalar “projection” onto half of the critical Fourier modes, to select only frequencies close to
\xi=1
:

(29)

\pi_{1}^{h}V=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto\chi_{\text{c}}^{h}(\xi)\mathbf{1}_{\xi>0}\frac{1}{\langle\rho_{\text{c}}(1),\rho_{\text{c}}^{*}(\xi)\rangle}\langle\hat{V}(\xi),\varrho_{\text{c}}^{*}(\xi)\rangle\right).

Such decompositions are well-behaved with Fourier transform. However, we need to measure objects with
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
norms, since the pattern we want to study behave as the solution of a bistable equation. To combine those two constraint, we use the so-called uniformly-localized space, that were introduce by Guido Schneider, see for example
[
Sch94c
]
. We first define a weight

\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}(x):=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}.

Then, we say that
u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})
if

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\cdot-y)u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<+\infty.

Similarly, we define
H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})
and its norm by
\lVert u\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\cdot-y)u\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}
when
s\geq 0
.
Then, we use the following injections to link with Sobolev spaces, that were used to estimate Green’s kernel.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
s\geq 1
. Then the following injections are continuous:
W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subset H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let first recall the definition of
\lVert.\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}
that we use.
Let
\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}(x):=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}
, then

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert u(\cdot-y)\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}.

From one hand the injection
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
leads to

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert u(\cdot-y)\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert u(\cdot-y)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},

It corresponds to the second injection.
From the other hand,
\lVert u\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{u,l}}\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}
leads to the first and third injection.
∎

As announced, we will need to estimate operators in Fourier space. We will use the following Lemma, see
[
Sch94a
, Section 3.1]
and
[

, Lemma 5]

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
d
,
n
be positive integers, and
M
an operator that acts in Fourier space as a point-wise linear application:
\hat{M}(\xi)\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

. Then for

\lVert Mu\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})^{d}}\leq C(s,q)\lVert\xi\mapsto\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{s-q}{2}}\hat{M}(\xi)\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R},\mathfrak{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})^{n}},

with a positive constant
C(s,q)
independent of
u
and
M
. Furthermore if
\alpha
and
\xi_{0}
are reals, then

\lVert x\mapsto e^{i\xi_{0}x}Mu(\alpha x)\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})^{d}}\leq C\lVert x\mapsto e^{i\xi_{0}x}u(\alpha x)\rVert_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})^{n}},

where the above constant satisfies

C\leq C(s,q)\left\lVert\xi\mapsto\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{s-q}{2}}\hat{M}\left(\frac{\xi}{\alpha}-\xi_{0}\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R},\mathfrak{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

4.2.
Linear dynamic

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
T
and
\mu_{0}
be positive constants. Then there exists
C>0
and
\kappa>0
such that for all
0<\mu<\mu_{0}
, the following holds. For
s\geq 0
, and

\lVert e^{t\mathcal{T}^{-}}\varPi_{\text{c}}^{h}V\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\lVert V\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})},

\lVert e^{t\mathcal{T}^{-}}\varPi_{\text{s}}^{h}V\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq Ce^{-\kappa t}\lVert V\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}.

Both estimates still holds when
\varPi_{i}^{h}
are replaced by
\varPi_{i}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T}^{-}
has constant coefficients – see (
12
) – it acts in Fourier space through multiplication. Hence we rely on multiplier theory, see
[
MS95
]
. Since at fixed Fourier parameter
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, the eigenvalues of matrix
\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{-}(\xi)
satisfy
\real{\lambda_{i}(\xi)}\leq\mu
, we obtain using
lemma

4.2
that for

:

\left\lVert e^{t\mathcal{T}^{-}}\varPi_{\text{c}}^{h}V\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\left\lVert\exp(t\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{-})\hat{\varPi_{\text{c}}^{h}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\lVert V\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq Ce^{2t\mu}\lVert V\rVert_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{u,l}}(\mathbb{R})},

with
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
the set of
2\times 2
matrices.
The case of
e^{t\mathcal{T}^{-}}\varPi_{\text{s}}^{h}
adapts easily: for
t\geq 0
and
\xi
in the support of
\chi_{\text{s}}
, the eigenvalues of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{-}(\xi)
satisfy
\real(\lambda_{j}(\xi))\leq-2\kappa
.
To see that
\varPi_{i}^{h}
may be replaced by
\varPi_{i}
, simply use that

to obtain the result.
∎

In the following, we can combine

Following

As above, we will note

Proposition 4.4
.

There exist a positive constant

such that for

\left\lVert\frac{U(t)}{\rho_{*}}\right\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times W^{4,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\frac{\lVert U_{0}\rVert_{X}}{(1+t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Proof.

First write the Duhamel formula for

\partial_{x}^{j}u_{1}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{j}\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\text{kpp}}u_{1}^{0}+\partial_{x}^{j}\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\text{co}}u_{2}^{0}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{j}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{\text{kpp}}\mathcal{N}_{1}+\partial_{x}^{j}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{\text{co}}\mathcal{N}_{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

3.15
– see also

The localized weight
\omega_{\text{kpp}}
in nonlinear terms allows to gain as many powers of

Proposition 4.5
.

There exists positive constants

and

Proof.

From

4.1
, it is enough to bound
\lVert\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\cdot},V(t))\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

For
\tilde{x}\leq-1

\varpi(\tilde{x})=1
, hence

\mathcal{S}(\tilde{x},V)=(1-q_{*}(\tilde{x}))\begin{pmatrix}3\alpha(1+q_{*}(\tilde{x}))v_{1}\\
-\gamma v_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

We show that
\lVert\omega_{\text{sh}}(1-q_{*})\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(-\infty,0)}
is finite. The equilibrium point
(q,q^{\prime})=(1,0)
for the front equation is a saddle, with one positive eigenvalue
\kappa=(-1+\sqrt{3})\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{d}}

\sup_{x\leq 0}\lvert q_{*}(x)-1\rvert e^{-\kappa x}<+\infty,\hskip 40.00006pt\sup_{x\leq 0}\lvert q_{*}^{\prime}(x)\rvert e^{-\kappa x}<+\infty.

For
\tilde{x}\leq-1

\left\lVert\mathcal{S}(\cdot-c_{*}t,V(t,\cdot))\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(-\infty,0)}\leq C\left\lVert\frac{V(t)}{\omega_{\text{sh}}(\cdot-c_{*}t)}\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(-\infty,0)}.

Now for

, recall the expression of the source term (

\varpi^{-1}(\mathcal{A}-c_{*}\partial_{x})\varpi-(\mathcal{A}-c_{*}\partial_{x})=\varpi^{-1}[\mathcal{A}-c_{*}\partial_{x},\varpi]

\lVert\varpi^{-1}\partial_{x}\varpi\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(0,+\infty)}<\infty

\lVert\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\cdot},V)\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(0,+\infty)}\leq C\lVert V\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}\times W^{4,\infty}(0,+\infty)}+C\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(0,+\infty)}^{2}+C\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(0,+\infty)}^{3}.

\omega_{\text{kpp}}(\tilde{x})=1

\frac{V(t,x)}{\omega_{\text{sh}}(\tilde{x})}=\frac{U(t,\tilde{x})}{\rho_{*}(\tilde{x})}

ensures the second claimed estimate. The proof is complete.

Lemma 4.7

Lemma 4.9

(Global attractor for Ginzburg-Landau)
.

Let
T,X\mapsto A(T,X)
be a solution of the Ginzburg-Landau equation (

such that

\lVert A(T)\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{GL}+e^{-T/2}\lVert A(0)\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Since the coefficients of GL are real, the dynamic is close to that of a bistable equation.
We follow
[
Sch94b
, Theorem 7]
. From computations made in
[
CE90
, Theorem 4.1]
, we recover that

\lVert A(T)h\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{GL}+e^{-2T} \lVert A(0)h\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

where

. The same estimate holds for
\partial_{x}A
. It leads to

\lvert A(T,X)\rvert\leq\frac{1}{h(X)^{2}}\left(C_{GL}+e^{-T} \lVert A(0)h\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right),

The bound on

∎

Appendix A
Ginzburg-Landau equation

Here we derive the Ginzburg-Landau amplitude equation from the dynamic at
-\infty
. We study the following system:

(35)

\partial_{t}V=\mathcal{T}_{0}V+\mathcal{T}_{\mu}V+\mathcal{N}_{2}(V)+\mathcal{N}_{3}(V),

\mathcal{T}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}d\partial_{x}^{2}-2\alpha&\beta\\

0&\mu\end{pmatrix},

while the quadratic and cubic terms write as:

\mathcal{N}_{2}(V)=\begin{pmatrix}-3\alpha{v_{1}}^{2}\\
\gamma{v_{1}}{v_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 40.00006pt\mathcal{N}_{3}(V)=-\begin{pmatrix}\alpha{v_{1}}^{3}\\

In the following, we note
M(X)
the symbol of the constant coefficient operator
\mathcal{T}_{0}

where

is the
n\times n
matrix obtained if one vectorise the ODE (
43
). Since the trace is invariant under circular permutation, we recognize

\partial_{x}\mathcal{W}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(A\cdot\Phi\cdot\mathrm{Com}(\Phi)^{\mathrm{T}}\right)=\det(\Phi)\mathrm{Tr}(A).

This scalar ODE on
\mathcal{W}
is easily solved. Noticing that
\mathrm{Tr}(A(\lambda,x))=-a_{n-1}(x)
conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma E.3
.

1\leq q\leq p\leq+\infty
, and

Proof.

This is the well-known Minkowsky’s integral inequality.

Lemma E.4
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>1
and
\gamma>0
be reals. Then there exists
C>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
:

(44)

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{(1+s)^{\alpha}(1+t-s)^{\beta}}\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\min(\alpha,\beta)}},\hskip 40.00006pt\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{-\gamma(t-\tau)}}{(1+\tau)^{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

One can slightly adapt this statement when
\alpha=1
or
\beta=1
, see
[
Xin92
]
. First, remark that

I_{\alpha,\beta}(t):=\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{(1+s)^{\alpha}(1+t-s)^{\beta}}\leq\frac{1}{(1+t/2)^{\beta}}\left[\frac{1-\alpha}{(1+s)^{\alpha-1}}\right]_{0}^{t/2}\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{(1+t/2)^{\beta}}.

Hence, we cut the integral in (
44
) at
t/2
, and the change of variable
z=t-s
leads to

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{(1+s)^{\alpha}(1+t-s)^{\beta}}=I_{\alpha,\beta}(t)+I_{\beta,\alpha}(t)\leq\frac{C}{(1+t/2)^{\min(\alpha,\beta)}}\leq\frac{C\ 2^{\min(\alpha,\beta)}}{(1+t)^{\min(\alpha,\beta)}}.

Which is the first claimed estimate. The second one follows from
e^{-\gamma(t-\tau)}\leq\frac{C}{(1+t-\tau)^{\alpha}}.

∎

Lemma E.5
.

Let
b,\varepsilon
be positive constants, and note
a_{0}(b,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2}(2b)^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}>0
. Then for all
0<a<a_{0}
, the following holds. If
t\mapsto x(t)
is a positive continuous function that satisfy
x(t)\leq a+bx(t)^{1+\varepsilon}
and such that
x(0)
is small enough, then
x(t)\leq 2a
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Introduce
f(x)=a+bx^{1+\varepsilon}-x
. Then
f(2a)=a(2^{1+\varepsilon}ba^{\varepsilon}-1)<0
when
a
is smaller than
a_{0}
, while
f(0)=a>0
. Assume
x(0)\leq 2a
. Then the connected component of
f^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
that contains
x(0)
is include in
(-\infty,2a)
. Since
f(x(t))\geq 0
by construction, the continuity of
x
implies
x(t)\leq 2a
for all
t>0
. This conclude the proof.
∎

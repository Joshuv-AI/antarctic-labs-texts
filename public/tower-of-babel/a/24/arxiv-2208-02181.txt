A NOTE ON MINIMAL RESOLUTIONS OF VECTOR-SPREAD BOREL IDEALS
Marilena Crupi, Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
We consider vector--spread Borel ideals. We show that these ideals have linear quotients and thereby we determine the graded Betti numbers and the bigraded Poincaré series. A characterization of the extremal Betti numbers of such a class of ideals is given. Finally, we classify all Cohen--Macaulay vector--spread Borel ideals. † † Keywords: monomial ideals, vector–spread Borel ideals, Cohen–Macaulay ideals, extremal Betti numbers. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05B35, 05E40, 13B25, 13D02, 68W30.

Introduction

In this note we study the class of vector–spread Borel ideals introduced in
[
15
]
as a generalization of the class of
t
–spread ideals, where
t
is a non negative integer
[
13
]
(see, also,
[
4
,
5
,
12
]
and the reference therein). Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a fixed field
K
and let
{\bf t}=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}
, with
d\geq 2
, a
(d-1)
–tuple whose entries are non negative integers. A monomial
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}
(
1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\cdots\leq j_{\ell}\leq n
) of degree
\ell\leq d
of
S
is called a
vector–spread monomial of type
{\bf t}
or simply a
{\bf t}
–spread monomial
if
j_{i+1}-j_{i}\geq t_{i}
, for
i=0,\dots,\ell-1
.
A
{\bf t}
–spread monomial ideal
is a monomial ideal generated by
{\bf t}
–spread monomials.
For instance,
I=(x_{1}x_{3}^{2}x_{5},x_{1}x_{3}^{2}x_{6},x_{1}x_{4}x_{5})
is a
(2,0,1)
–spread monomial ideal of the polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}]
, but it is not
(2,1,1)
–spread as
x_{1}x_{3}^{2}x_{5}\in G(I)
is not
(2,1,1)
–spread. One can note that any monomial (ideal) is
{\bf 0}
–spread, where
{\bf 0}=(0,0,\dots,0)
. If
t_{i}\geq 1
, for all
i
, a
{\bf t}
–spread monomial (ideal) is a
squarefree
monomial (ideal). A
{\bf t}
–spread monomial ideal
I
of
S
is said
vector–spread Borel ideal of type

{\bf t}
or simply a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal
if for any
{\bf t}
–spread monomial
u\in I
, and all
j<i
such that
x_{i}
divides
u
and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})
is
{\bf t}
–spread, then
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in I
. For
{\bf t}={\bf 0}=(0,0,\dots,0)
(
{\bf t}={\bf 1}=(1,1,\dots,1)
) one obtains the classical notion of strongly stable (squarefree strongly stable) ideal
[
16
]
.

We study the graded Betti numbers of a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal
I
by combinatorial tools and therefore we obtain a formula for their computation. As a consequence we are able to give a characterization of the extremal Betti numbers of such an ideal
I
. Finally, the Cohen–Macaulay
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideals are classified. Our approach is similar to the one of
[
13
]
.

In this note we discuss a unified concept to deal with the classes of monomial ideals which are among the most important in Combinatorics (stable, strongly stable ideals) in order to get more general results. Section
1
contains some preliminaries and notions that will be used in the article. In Section
2
, we review a result on the graded Betti numbers of a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal stated in
[
15
]
. We propose a simpler method for determining a formula for the graded Betti numbers of such a class of monomial ideals (Corollary
2.4
). A key result is Theorem
2.2
which states that a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal has linear quotients. The notion of
vector–spread support
(Subsection 1.2) plays an essential role in this context. Indeed, the linear quotients of vector–spread Borel ideals can be expressed in terms of vector–spread supports (Corollary
2.3
).
Moreover, the bigraded Poincaré series of such ideals is also determined (Corollary
2.5
). In Section
3
, we obtain a characterization of the extremal Betti numbers of
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideals (Proposition
3.3
) by the results in Section
2
. Finally, Section
4
contains one of the main result in the article. We analyze the Cohen–Macauleyness of
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideals via the formula of the graded Betti numbers described in Section
2
. Indeed, we obtain a classification (Theorem
4.3
) by investigating the height and the projective dimension of such ideals.

1. Preliminaries

Throughout the article, we denote by
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
and by
{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
(
{\operatorname{Mon}}_{d}(S)
) the set of all monomials (of degree
d
) in
S
.

1.1. A glimpse to graded Betti numbers.

Given a monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
of
S
,
the
support
of
u
is the set
{\operatorname{supp}}(u)=\{i:a_{i}>0\}=\{i:x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. We denote by
\max(u)
(
\min(u)
) the maximal (minimal) integer
i\in{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
.

For a monomial ideal
I
of
S
,
G(I)
denotes the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
and we set
G(I)_{j}=\{u\in G(I):\deg(u)=j\}
. It is known that a monomial ideal
I
of
S
has a unique minimal graded free
S
–resolution

{\mathbb{F}}:0\rightarrow F_{p}\xrightarrow{\ d_{p}\ }F_{p-1}\xrightarrow{\ d_{p-1}\ }\cdots\xrightarrow{\ d_{1}\ }F_{0}\xrightarrow{\ d_{0}\ }I\rightarrow 0,

where
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}(I)}
. The numbers
\beta_{i,j}(I)=\dim_{K}\operatorname{Tor}(K,I)_{j}
are called the
graded Betti numbers
of
I
. For all
i
,
\beta_{i}(I)=\sum_{j}\beta_{i,j}(I)
is the
i
th total Betti number
of
I
.
One defines the
projective dimension
and the
regularity
of
I
as follows

\displaystyle{\operatorname{pd}}(I)

\displaystyle=\max\{i:\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0,\ \textup{for some}\ j\}=\max\{i:\beta_{i}(I)\neq 0\},

\displaystyle{\operatorname{reg}}(I)

\displaystyle=\max\{j-i:\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}=\max\{j:\beta_{i,i+j}(I)\neq 0\ \textup{for some}\ i\}.

These algebraic invariants have been refined in
[
7
]
by the notion of
extremal Betti number
.
A graded Betti number
\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)\neq 0
of a monomial ideal
I
of
S
is called
extremal
if
\beta_{i,i+j}(I)=0
for all
i\geq k
,
j\geq\ell
such that
(i,j)\neq(k,\ell)
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. It is known that
\textup{depth}(S/I)\leq\dim(S/I)
. If the equality holds, we say that
S/I
is a
Cohen–Macaulay ring
and
I
is a
Cohen–Macaulay ideal
.

1.2. A glimpse to vector–spread monomial ideals.

Let
{\bf t}=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}
,
d\geq 2
, a
(d-1)
–tuple whose entries are non negative integers. Let us write a monomial
u\in S

u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}
,
1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\cdots\leq j_{\ell}\leq n

Let
T=K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},\dots]
be the polynomial ring in infinitely many variables. Denote by
{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
the set of all
{\bf t}
–spread monomials of
T
and by
{\operatorname{Mon}}(S;{\bf t})
the set of all
{\bf t}
–spread monomials of
S
. Furthermore, for all

, we define the following sets

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(T;{\bf t})

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t}):\deg(u)=\ell\big\},

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(S;{\bf t})

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S;{\bf t}):\deg(u)=\ell\big\}.

Let us denote by
M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}
the set of all
{\bf t}
–spread monomials of
S
having degree
\ell
. One may observe that
M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}=\varnothing
for
\ell>d
.
In order to compute the cardinality of the set

, one can consider a special
shifting operator
(see
[
6
,
13
]
). More in detail, one defines the map
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}:{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf 0})\rightarrow{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
, by setting
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}(1)=1
,
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}(x_{i})=x_{i}
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}})=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}x_{j_{k}+\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}t_{s}},

for all monomials
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf 0})
with
2\leq\ell\leq d
.
The map
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}
is bijective and its inverse is the map
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}:{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})\rightarrow{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf 0})
defined as follows:
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}(1)=1
,
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}(x_{i})=x_{i}
, for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}})

=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}x_{j_{k}-\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}t_{s}}
, for all monomials
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
with
2\leq\ell\leq d
.

In particular, the restriction
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}|_{M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}}
is a injective map and its image is equal to the set
M_{n-(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1}),\ell,{\bf 0}}={\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n-(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1})}])
. Thus

|M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}|=\binom{n+(\ell-1)-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}}{\ell},\quad\mbox{for $0\leq\ell\leq d$}.

For more details on this topic see
[
15
]
.

If
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
are
{\bf t}
–spread monomials of
S

B_{\bf t}(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})
the smallest
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of

{\bf t}
–spread Borel generators
of

. If

B_{\bf t}(u_{1})=B_{\bf t}(u)
is called a
principal
{\bf t}
–spread Borel ideal
. It is clear that for each
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable monomial ideal

{\bf t}
–spread monomials
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\in S

Observe that for
u=x_{n-(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1})}x_{n-(t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1})}\cdots x_{n-t_{\ell-1}}x_{n}
, the ideal
B_{\bf t}(u)
contains all
{\bf t}
–spread monomials of degree

.

{\bf t}
–spread Veronese ideal of degree

Now let us recall the pivotal notion of
{\bf t}
–spread support
of a
{\bf t}
–spread monomial

If
n

,
[n]
denotes the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. For
j\leq k

Let

Note that

2. The graded Betti numbers

In
[
15
]
, the graded Betti numbers of vector–spread strongly stable ideals have been determined by means of the Koszul homology. More precisely, if
I
is a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
, then the graded Betti numbers of
I
are given by

\beta_{i,i+j}(I)=\sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{\max(u)-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}t_{\ell}}{i},\quad\mbox{for all $i,j$}.

The purpose of this section is to quickly obtain this formula using a different and easier method than the one used in
[
15
]
. The vector–spread support will be a key tool.

We need to fix some notations and recall some results from
[
17
]
. Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. Let
G(I)=\{u_{1}>u_{2}>\dots>u_{m}\}
be the minimal generating set of
I
ordered with respect to the
pure lexicographic order

[
16
]
. For
k=1,\dots,m
, we set
J_{k}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
and

\textup{set}(u_{k})=\big\{i\in[n]:x_{i}\in(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}):u_{k}\big\}.

Note that
\textup{set}(u_{1})=\varnothing
. Our aim is to prove that
I
has
linear quotients
,
i.e.
,

J_{k-1}:u_{k}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k-1}):u_{k},

is generated by variables, for all
k=2,\dots,m
. In such a case, by
[
17
, Lemma 1.5]
, one has

\beta_{i,i+j}(I)=\sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{|\textup{set}(u)|}{i},\quad\mbox{for all $i,j\geq 0$}.

(2)

We quote the next crucial lemma from
[
15
]
(see also
[
13
, Lemma 1.3]
). It provides the existence of a
standard decomposition
for a
{\bf t}
–spread monomial belonging to a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal.

Lemma 2.1

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
, and
w\in I
a
{\bf t}
–spread monomial. Then, there exist
u\in G(I)
and
v\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
such that
w=uv
and
\max(u)\leq\min(v)
.

Now we are in position to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.2

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. Then
I
has linear quotients, in particular it is componentwise linear.

Proof.
Let
G(I)=\{u_{1}>u_{2}>\dots>u_{m}\}
ordered with respect to the pure lexicographic order. Fix
k\in\{2,\dots,m\}
. A set of generators for
J_{k-1}:u_{k}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}):u_{k}
is
\{u_{s}/\textup{gcd}(u_{k},u_{s}):s=1,\ldots,k-1\}

[
16
, Proposition 1.2.2]
. Hence, it suffices to show that for each
s
, there exists a variable
x_{i}\in J_{k-1}:u_{k}
with
x_{i}
dividing
u_{s}/\textup{gcd}(u_{k},u_{s})
. Let
u_{s}=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{p}},\ u_{k}=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{q}},
with
u_{s}>_{{\operatorname{lex}}}u_{k}
. Hence, there exists an integer
\ell\in[q]
such that

i_{1}=j_{1},\ \ i_{2}=j_{2},\ \ \ldots,\ \ i_{\ell-1}=j_{\ell-1},\ \ i_{\ell}<j_{\ell}.

(3)

Set
v=x_{i_{\ell}}(u_{k}/x_{j_{\ell}})
. Then
v=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell-1}}x_{i_{\ell}}x_{j_{\ell+1}}\cdots x_{j_{q}}
. We need to show that
v
is
{\bf t}
–spread. This is the case, as
i_{\ell}-j_{\ell-1}=i_{\ell}-i_{\ell-1}\geq t_{\ell-1}
and
j_{\ell+1}-i_{\ell}>j_{\ell+1}-j_{\ell}\geq t_{\ell}
.
On the other hand,
I
is a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal. Therefore
v\in I
and, furthermore,
v>_{{\operatorname{lex}}}u_{k}
. Now we show that
v\in J_{k-1}
. By Lemma
2.1
,
v=u_{r}w
with
u_{r}\in G(I)
and
w\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
such that
\max(u_{r})\leq\min(w)
. If
v\notin J_{k-1}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1})
, then
u_{r}\in G(I)\setminus\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}\}
, and consequently
u_{r}\leq_{{\operatorname{lex}}}u_{k}
. As
v=u_{r}w
, then
v\leq_{{\operatorname{lex}}}u_{k}
, a contradiction.
Therefore,
v\in J_{k-1}
and
x_{i_{\ell}}u_{k}=vx_{j_{\ell}}\in J_{k-1}
. Thus
x_{i_{\ell}}\in J_{k-1}:u_{k}
and, by (
3
),
x_{i_{\ell}}
divides
u_{s}/\textup{gcd}(u_{k},u_{s})
. It follows that
I
has linear quotients. Finally, it is well known that ideals with linear quotients are componentwise linear
[
16
, Theorem 8.2.15]
.

\Box

The following corollary highlights the role of the vector–spread supports.

Corollary 2.3

In the previous setting, for all
k=2,\dots,m
, we have

\textup{set}(u_{k})=[\max(u_{k})-1]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u_{k}).

Proof.
Let
u_{k}=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}
. We have
{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u_{k})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{d-1}\big(\bigcup_{q=0}^{t_{i}-1}\{j_{i}+q\}\big)
. Let
\ell\in[n]
. To determine if
\ell\in\textup{set}(u_{k})
, we consider two cases.

Case 1
. Let
\max(u_{k})\leq\ell\leq n
, then
x_{\ell}u_{k}=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}x_{\ell}
. First, observe that the ideal
J_{k-1}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k-1})
is
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable. If for absurd
\ell\in\textup{set}(u_{k})
, then
x_{\ell}u_{k}\in J_{k-1}
. Hence
u_{r}
divides
x_{\ell}u_{k}
, for some
r\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
. Since
\deg(u_{r})\leq\deg(u_{k})
, then
u_{r}
divides
u_{k}
. An absurd, since
u_{k}
is a minimal generator of
I
.

Case 2
. Let
\ell\in{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u_{k})
, then
\ell=j_{r}+s
, for some
r\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
,
0\leq s\leq t_{r}-1
,
t_{r}\geq 1
. If
x_{\ell}u_{k}\in J_{k}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k})
, then
u_{p}
divides
x_{\ell}u_{k}
, for some
p\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
. As
j_{r}+s-j_{r}=s<t_{r}
, and
u_{p}
is
{\bf t}
–spread, necessarily
u_{p}
divides
u_{k}
. An absurd, since
u_{k}
is a minimal generator of
I
.

The above cases imply that if
\ell\in\textup{set}(u_{k})
, then
\ell\notin[\max(u_{k}),n]
and
\ell\notin{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u_{k})
.

Now assume
\ell\in[\max(u_{k})-1]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u_{k})
. Then
\ell=j_{r}+s
, for some
r\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
and
s\geq t_{r}
. Let
j_{q}=\min\big\{j\in{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{k}):j>\ell\big\}
. Then
x_{\ell}(u_{k}/x_{j_{q}})
is a
{\bf t}
–spread monomial that belongs to the ideal
(J_{k-1},u_{k})=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k-1},u_{k})=J_{k}
, as
\ell<j_{q}
and
(J_{k-1},u_{k})
is a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal. By Lemma
2.1
,
x_{\ell}(u_{k}/x_{j_{q}})=u_{p}w
with
p\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and
w\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
such that
\max(u_{p})\leq\min(w)
. Clearly,
p\neq k
and so
x_{\ell}(u_{k}/x_{j_{q}})\in J_{k-1}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k-1})
. Finally
x_{\ell}u_{k}=x_{\ell}(u_{k}/x_{j_{q}})x_{j_{q}}\in J_{k-1}
and
\ell\in\textup{set}(u_{k})
. The assertion follows.

\Box

As a consequence of the above result, one has

J_{k-1}:u_{k}=\big(x_{i}:i\in[\max(u_{k})-1]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u_{k})\big),\quad\mbox{for $k=2,\dots,m$.}

On the other hand, for each

\big|[\max(u_{k})-1]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u_{k})\big|=\max(u_{k})-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\deg(u_{k})-1}t_{\ell}.

Corollary 2.4

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. Then,

\beta_{i,i+j}(I)=\sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{\max(u)-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}t_{\ell}}{i},\quad\mbox{for all $i,j\geq 0$}.

As a consequence, we obtain:

Corollary 2.5

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. Then

P_{S/I}(y,z)=1+\sum_{u\in G(I)}(1+y)^{\max(u)-1-\sum_{s=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{s}}yz^{\deg(u)}.

Proof.
Let
P_{S/I}(y,z)=\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}(I)y^{i}z^{j}
be the bigraded Poincaré series of
S/I
. From
[
17
, Corollary 1.6]
, for an ideal
I
with linear quotients, one has

P_{S/I}(y,z)=1+\sum_{u\in G(I)}(1+y)^{|\textup{set}(u)|}yz^{\deg(u)}.

The assertion follows from Corollary
2.4
.

3. Extremal Betti numbers

In this section we characterize the extremal Betti numbers of the class of
{\bf t}
–spread Borel ideals. Our results generalizes some statements in
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
,
3
,
9
,
10
]
and the reference therein).

First, one can note that Corollary
2.4
implies

\displaystyle{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \max\big\{\deg(u):u\in G(I)\big\},

(4)

\displaystyle{\operatorname{pd}}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \max\big\{\max(u)-1-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}:u\in G(I)\big\}.

(5)

For our aim, the next lemma will play a crucial role.

Lemma 3.1

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. If
\beta_{i,i+j}(I)\neq 0
, then
\beta_{k,k+j}(I)\neq 0
for all
k=0,\dots,i
.

Proof.
Let
\beta_{i,i+j}(I)\neq 0
. From Corollary
2.4
, there exists a monomial
u\in G(I)_{j}
such that
\max(u)-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}t_{\ell}\geq i
. Hence,
\max(u)-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}t_{\ell}\geq k
, for all
k=0,\dots,i
. This implies that
\beta_{k,k+j}(I)\neq 0
, for all
k=0,\dots,i
.

\Box

The previous lemma and the definition of extremal Betti number imply the next result.

Corollary 3.2

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)
is extremal;

(ii)

\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)\neq 0
, and
\beta_{i,i+\ell}(I),\beta_{k,k+j}(I)=0
, for all
i>k
and all
j>\ell
.

The following characterization generalizes a result in
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 3.3

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)
is extremal;

(ii)

\max\big\{\max(u):u\in G(I)_{\ell}\big\}=k+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1
, and
\max(u)<k+\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}t_{r}+1
, for all
u\in G(I)_{j}
and all
j>\ell
.

Proof.
(i)
\implies
(ii). Since
I
is
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable, Corollary
2.4
implies that
\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)
is non zero if and only if there exists a monomial
u_{0}\in G(I)_{\ell}
such that
\max(u_{0})\geq k+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1
. Therefore,

\max\big\{\max(u):u\in G(I)_{\ell}\big\}\ \geq\ \max(u_{0})\ \geq\ k+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1.

(6)

Suppose that for a monomial
u_{1}\in G(I)_{\ell}
,
\max(u_{1})=j+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1>k+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1
, for some
j>k
. Then
\beta_{j,j+\ell}(I)\neq 0
, which contradicts the fact that
\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)
is extremal (Corollary
3.2
). Hence,
k+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1=\max\big\{\max(u):u\in G(I)_{\ell}\big\}
.
Assume there exists a monomial
v\in G(I)_{j}
such that
\max(v)\geq k+\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}t_{r}+1
, for some
j>\ell
. Then
\beta_{k,k+j}(I)\neq 0
. A contradiction, since
\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)
is extremal (Corollary
3.2
). Hence, condition (ii) holds.

(ii)
\implies
(i). Since
\max\big\{\max(u):u\in G(I)_{\ell}\big\}=k+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1
, then
\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)\neq 0
and
\beta_{j,j+\ell}(I)=0
, for all
j>k
. Moreover,
\max(u)<k+\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}t_{r}+1
, for all
u\in G(I)_{j}
and all
j>\ell
, implies that
\beta_{k,k+j}(I)=0
, for all
j>\ell
. Finally, by Corollary
3.2
,
\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)
is extremal.

\Box

The previous result yields the following useful corollary.

Corollary 3.4

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
, and let
\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)
be an extremal Betti number of
I
. Then

\beta_{k,k+\ell}(I)=\big|\big\{u\in G(I)_{\ell}:\max(u)=k+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1\big\}\big|\leq\ \tbinom{k+\ell-1}{\ell-1}.

Proof.
The equality follows immediately from Proposition
3.3
. For the inequality, it sufficies to observe that

\displaystyle\big|\big\{u\in M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}:\max(u)=k+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1\big\}\big|=

\displaystyle=\binom{k+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}+1+(\ell-1)-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}-1}{\ell-1}=\binom{k+\ell-1}{\ell-1}.

\Box

4. Cohen–Macaulay vector–spread Borel ideals

In this section we investigate the Cohen–Macaulayness of vector–spread Borel ideals by tools from
[
11
,
13
]
. To obtain a characterization, we only need to investigate the height of a vector–spread Borel ideal. Indeed, from Corollary
2.4
and the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula, one has that

0pt(I)=n-\max\big\{\max(u)-1-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}:u\in G(I)\big\}.

Proposition 4.1

Let
I_{n,d,{\bf t}}
be the
{\bf t}
–spread Veronese ideal of degree
d
of
S
. Then

\textup{height}(I_{n,d,{\bf t}})=n-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}.

Proof.
First, observe that
I_{n,d,{\bf t}}\neq(0)
if and only if
n\geq 1+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}
.
The monomial prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}=(x_{i}:i=1,2,\dots,n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j})
is a minimal prime of
I_{n,d,{\bf t}}
, as each monomial generator
u\in G(I_{n,d,{\bf t}})=M_{n,d,{\bf t}}
has minimum belonging to the set
\big\{1,2,\dots,n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}\big\}
. Therefore

\textup{height}(I_{n,d,{\bf t}})\leq n-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}.

Suppose
\textup{height}(I_{n,d,{\bf t}})=k<n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}
. Then, there would be a minimal prime
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{i_{j}}:j=1,\dots,k)
of
I_{n,d,{\bf t}}
of height
k\leq n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}-1
, with all
i_{j}
distinct. The set
A=[n]\setminus\{i_{j}:j=1,\dots,k\}
has cardinality

|A|=n-k\geq n-\big(n-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}-1\big)=1+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}.

We set
\ell_{1}=\min(A)
and
\ell_{j}=\min\{\ell\in A:\ell\geq\ell_{j-1}+t_{j}\}
, for all
j\geq 2
. The sequence
\ell_{1}\leq\ell_{2}\leq\dots\leq\ell_{k}
has at least
d
terms, otherwise
|A|<1+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}
. So, the monomial
w=x_{\ell_{1}}x_{\ell_{2}}\cdots x_{\ell_{d}}
is
{\bf t}
–spread of degree
d
, but
w\in G(I_{n,d,{\bf t}})\setminus\mathfrak{q}
, an absurd. Finally, we have
\textup{height}(I_{n,d,{\bf t}})=n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}
, as desired.

\Box

Theorem 4.2

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
. Then

\textup{height}(I)=\max\big\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\big\}.

Proof.
First we prove that
\textup{height}(I)\leq\max\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\}
. Indeed, let
u_{0}\in G(I)
such that
\min(u_{0})=\max\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\}
. In such a case the monomial prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{[\min(u_{0})]}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{\min(u_{0})})
is a minimal prime of
I
, thus proving the inequality.
For the other inequality, write
u_{0}=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}
and consider the monomial
v_{0}=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{1}+t_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{1}+(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{d-1})}
. Then
v_{0}\in B_{\bf t}(u_{0})\subseteq I
. Moreover,
I^{\prime}=B_{\bf t}(v_{0})=I_{j_{1}+t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{d-1},d,{\bf t}}
. Hence, by Proposition
4.1

\textup{height}(I)\geq\textup{height}(I^{\prime})=j_{1}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-1}t_{\ell}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-1}t_{\ell}=j_{1}=\min(v_{0})=\min(u_{0}).

\Box

Using what we have shown thus far, we are able to classify all Cohen–Macaulay vector–spread strongly stable ideals of
S
.

Theorem 4.3

Let
I\subseteq S
be a
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
,
{\bf t}=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d-1})
. Then
S/I
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring if and only if there exists
u\in G(I)
of degree
\ell\leq d
such that

u=x_{n-(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1})}x_{n-(t_{2}+t_{3}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1})}\cdots x_{n-t_{\ell-1}}x_{n}\in G(I).

In particular, if
I
is equigenerated in degree
\ell
then
S/I
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
I=I_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}
is a vector–spread Veronese ideal.

Proof.
Firstly, we may suppose
n\in\bigcup_{u\in G(I)}{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
. On the contrary, setting
\widetilde{n}=

\max\bigcup_{u\in G(I)}{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
, we may replace
S
with
\widetilde{S}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{\widetilde{n}}]
and consider
I\cap\widetilde{S}
, instead. Since
{\operatorname{pd}}(S/I)={\operatorname{pd}}(I)+1
, by (
5
), we have

\displaystyle{\operatorname{pd}}(S/I)\

\displaystyle=\ {\textstyle\max\big\{\max(u)-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}:u\in G(I)\big\}}.

On the other hand, by Theorem
4.2
,

\displaystyle\dim(S/I)\

\displaystyle=\ n-\max\big\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\big\}.

By the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula,
n-{\operatorname{pd}}(S/I)=0pt(S/I)
. Since
S/I
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
0pt(S/I)=\dim(S/I)
, by the previous formulas,
S/I
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if

\max\Big\{\max(u)-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}:u\in G(I)\Big\}=\max\big\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\big\}.

(7)

Let
u_{0}\in G(I)
such that
\min(u_{0})=\max\big\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\big\}
. Clearly, for all
u\in G(I)
,
\min(u)\leq\max(u)-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}
. Hence, equation (
7
) holds if and only if

\min(u_{0})=\max(u_{0})-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}.

Thus,
S/I
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if there exist a monomial
u_{0}\in G(I)
and an integer
\ell\leq d
such that

\min(u_{0})=\max\big\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\big\},\ u_{0}=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{1}+t_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{1}+(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1})}.

It remains to show that
j_{1}=n-\sum_{r=1}^{\ell-1}t_{r}
. Since
n\in\bigcup_{u\in G(I)}{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
, it follows the existence of a monomial
u\in G(I)
with
\max(u)=n
. Moreover,
\min(u)\leq\min(u_{0})
, as
\min(u_{0})=\max\{\min(u):u\in G(I)\}
. Note that

\max(u)-\textstyle\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{r}\leq j_{1}=\min(u_{0}).

Hence, as
\max(u)=n
, we have
j_{1}\geq n-\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{r}
. If
\deg(u)\leq\deg(u_{0})
, then
j_{1}\geq n-\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{r}\geq n-\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u_{0})-1}t_{r}
. On the other hand,
j_{1}\leq n-\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u_{0})-1}t_{r}
. So, in such a case,
j_{1}=n-\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u_{0})-1}t_{r}
, as desired.

Suppose now
\deg(u)>\deg(u_{0})
. If
u=x_{k_{1}}x_{k_{2}}\cdots x_{k_{\ell}}x_{k_{\ell+1}}\cdots x_{k_{\deg(u)}}
, where
\ell=\deg(u_{0})
, we set
u_{1}=x_{k_{1}}x_{k_{2}}\cdots x_{k_{\ell}}
. If
k_{\ell}\leq j_{1}+\sum_{r=1}^{\ell-1}t_{r}
, then
u_{1}
is
{\bf t}
–spread and
k_{1}\leq j_{1},\ k_{2}\leq j_{2},\ \dots,\ k_{\ell}\leq j_{\ell}
, where
j_{i}=j_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}t_{j}
(
i=1,\ldots,\ell
). Since
I
is
{\bf t}
–spread strongly stable, then
u_{1}\in I
. A contradiction, as
u_{1}
divides
u
and
u
is a minimal generator. Therefore, we must have
k_{\ell}>j_{1}+\sum_{r=1}^{\ell-1}t_{r}
and consequently

\max(u)-\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{r}\geq\max(u_{1})-\sum_{r=1}^{\deg(u_{1})-1}t_{r}>j_{1},

which contradicts (
7
).

\Box

Remark 4.4

From Theorem
4.3
, the vector–spread Veronese ideal
I_{n,d,{\bf t}}
is Cohen–Macaulay. Moreover,
{\operatorname{pd}}(S/I_{n,d,{\bf t}})=n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}
. So
I_{n,d,{\bf t}}
is a Cohen–Macaulay ideal with pure resolution of type
(d_{1},\dots,d_{p})
, with
d_{j}=d+j-1
, for
j=1,\dots,p
. Therefore, by
[
8
, Theorem 4.1.15]
, we have for all
i\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\beta_{i}(S/I_{n,d,{\bf t}})\

\displaystyle=\ (-1)^{i+1}\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{j}}{d_{j}-d_{i}}=(-1)^{i+1}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{d+j-1}{j-i}\prod_{j=i+1}^{p}\frac{d+j-1}{j-i}

\displaystyle=\ \binom{d+i-2}{d-1}\binom{n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}+d-1}{d+i-1}.

Note that for
i=1
, we obtain
\mu(I_{n,d,{\bf t}})=\beta_{1}(S/I_{n,d,{\bf t}})=\binom{n-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}+d-1}{d}=|M_{n,d,{\bf t}}|
and we get again formula (
1
).

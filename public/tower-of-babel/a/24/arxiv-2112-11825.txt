Charged rotating black strings
in Einsteinian quartic gravity

Abstract




We find an analytic asymptotically anti-de Sitter solution for charged rotating black strings in Einsteinian quartic gravity. By studying the near-horizon behavior of the solutions, we independently extract their thermodynamic properties, analytically. As a consistency check, we show that the first law of thermodynamics for rotating black strings holds exactly in both charged and uncharged cases.

tends to zero and after resolving the ambiguity in the limits via L’Hospital rule, we recover
k_{g}=3r_{h}/2l^{2}\Xi
and
M=r_{h}^{3}/l^{2}
which are respectively the surface gravity and mass of black strings in Einstein gravity.

2.3
Thermodynamics

When higher-curvature corrections are included, the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is modified by additional terms, which originate from the Wald entropy formula
[
27
,
28
]

{\sf S}=-2\pi\int_{H}d^{2}x\sqrt{\gamma}\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta R_{abcd}}\epsilon_{ab}\epsilon_{cd},

(2.40)

where
\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta R_{abcd}}
is the Euler-Lagrange derivative of gravitational Lagrangian,
\gamma
is the determinant of the induced metric on the horizon, and
\epsilon_{ab}
is the binormal of the horizon, normalized by
\epsilon_{ab}\epsilon^{ab}=-2
. This antisymmetric tensor is defined by the following relation:

\triangledown_{a}\chi_{b}=\kappa_{g}\epsilon_{ab}.

(2.41)

Using the above equation, the nonzero components of the binormal of horizon of metric (
2.8
) are given by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{tr}=-\epsilon_{rt}=-\Xi,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{r\phi}=-\epsilon_{\phi r}=-a.

(2.42)

For the metric (
2.8
) with a cylindrical horizon placed at
r=r_{h}
, one finds the following value for entropy per unit length:

\displaystyle{\cal S}=\frac{\pi\Xi r_{h}^{2}}{10l}\left[5+\frac{\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}\left(\tau^{3/4}-\sqrt{2\sqrt{10\xi}l^{3/2}-\tau^{3/2}}\right)^{3}}{\sqrt{\xi}l^{3/2}\tau^{3/4}}\right].

(2.43)

In writing the above result in terms of horizon radius
r_{h}
, the transformation
K\rightarrow-K
, the relation
f^{\prime}(r_{h})=2\Xi\kappa_{g}
, and Eq. (
2.36
) has been used. Here, in the limit
K\rightarrow 0
, we obtain
{\cal S}=\pi r_{h}^{2}\Xi/2l
which is the entropy of black strings in Einstein gravity. The Hawking temperature can easily be found in terms of the horizon radius from surface gravity as

T=\frac{\kappa_{g}}{2\pi}=\frac{\sqrt[6]{5}r_{h}\left(\tau^{3/4}-\sqrt{2\sqrt{10\xi}l^{3/2}-\tau^{3/2}}\right)}{4\pi\ 2^{5/6}\sqrt[3]{K}\sqrt[6]{\xi}\sqrt[4]{\tau}\sqrt{l}\Xi}.

(2.44)

Here, we are going to find the mass and angular momentum of black strings by using the counterterm method. To this end, we add the Gibbons–Hawking boundary term, which removes the divergences of the action (
2.1
), as

S_{b}=\frac{C(l_{\rm eff}^{2})}{8\pi}\int_{\partial\cal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{-\gamma}\Theta,

(2.45)

where
\gamma
is the determinant of the induced metric on the boundary, and
\Theta
is the trace of the extrinsic curvature
\Theta_{ab}
of the boundary. Here,
C(l_{\rm eff}^{2})
is a constant, which depends on the background curvature, and is given by
[
29
]

\displaystyle C(l_{\rm eff}^{2})=-\frac{l_{\rm eff}^{2}}{6}{\cal L}|_{\rm AdS},

(2.46)

where
{\cal L}|_{\rm AdS}
is the Lagrangian of the corresponding theory evaluated on
{\rm AdS}_{4}
background with curvature scale
l_{\rm eff}
.
We use the counterterm approach
[
30
,
31
]
to eliminate the divergences of action. To do so, we add some local surface integrals to the action to make it finite. The counterterms for the case of EQG, which makes the action finite up to four dimensions, are

S_{ct}=\frac{C(l_{\rm eff}^{2})}{8\pi}\int_{\partial\cal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{-\gamma}\left(\frac{2}{l_{\rm eff}}-\frac{l_{\rm eff}}{2}{\cal R}\right),

(2.47)

where
{\cal R}
is the Ricci scalar for the boundary metric
\gamma
. Notice that,
l_{\rm eff}
is a scale length factor that depends on
l
and
K
, that must reduce to
l
as
K
goes to zero. That is indeed the root of Eq. (
2.17
). The total action can be written as a linear combination of the action of bulk, boundary and the counterterm,

S_{total}=S+S_{b}+S_{ct}.

(2.48)

Having had the total finite action, one can use the Brown-York definition of stress tensor
[
32
]
by varying the action with respect to boundary metric
\gamma_{ab}
, and finding a divergence-free stress tensor

T^{ab}=\frac{1-\frac{32K}{5l_{\rm eff}^{6}}}{8\pi}\left(\Theta^{ab}-\Theta\gamma^{ab}+\frac{2}{l_{\rm eff}}\gamma^{ab}-\frac{l_{\rm eff}}{2}{\cal G}^{ab}\right),

(2.49)

where
{\cal G}_{ab}={\cal R}_{ab}-{\cal R}\gamma_{ab}/2
is the Einstein tensor of boundary metric
\gamma_{ab}
. For asymptotically AdS solutions with flat horizons
{\cal R}_{abcd}(\gamma)=0
, the only nonvanishing counterterm, is the first term in (
2.47
), which yields the stress tensor (
2.49
) up to the third term. Using (
2.49
), one can define the quasilocal conserved quantities for an asymptotically AdS spacetime. The conserved charge associated to a Killing vector
\xi_{a}
is given by

Q_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma}d^{3}x\sqrt{\sigma}u^{a}T_{ab}\xi^{b}.

(2.50)

Here,
u_{a}=-N\delta^{0}_{a}
, while
N
and
\sigma
are the lapse function and the metric of spacelike surface
\Sigma
, which appear in the ADM–like decomposition of the boundary metric,

\gamma_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}=-N^{2}dt^{2}+\sigma_{ab}\left(dx^{a}+V^{a}dt\right)\left(dx^{b}+V^{b}dt\right),

(2.51)

where
V^{a}
is the shift vector. To obtain the total mass (energy), we should set
\xi=\partial_{t}
,
i.e.,
the Killing vector conjugate to time coordinate
t
, and to obtain the angular momentum, we should set
\xi=\partial_{\phi}
,
i.e.,
the Killing vector conjugate to angular coordinate
\phi
. Using the definition (
2.50
) for conserved charges, we find the total mass per unit length of horizon to be given by

{\cal M}=\frac{1}{8l}\left(3\Xi^{2}-1\right)M,

(2.52)

while the angular momentum per unit length of horizon is given by

{\cal J}=\frac{3}{8l}\Xi aM=\frac{3}{8}\Xi\sqrt{\Xi^{2}-1}M,

(2.53)

where Eq. (
2.9
) has been used. Substituting the mass (
2.37
) into the above equations, one arrives at

\displaystyle{\cal M}=\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{8l^{3}}\left(3\Xi^{2}-1\right)\left[1+\frac{3\left(\tau^{3/4}-\sqrt{2\sqrt{10\xi}l^{3/2}-\tau^{3/2}}\right)^{4}}{16\sqrt[3]{10K}\xi^{2/3}\tau}\right],

(2.54)

\displaystyle{\cal J}=\frac{3r_{h}^{3}}{8l^{2}}\sqrt{\Xi^{2}-1}\left[1+\frac{3\left(\tau^{3/4}-\sqrt{2\sqrt{10\xi}l^{3/2}-\tau^{3/2}}\right)^{4}}{16\sqrt[3]{10K}\xi^{2/3}\tau}\right].

(2.55)

To check the first law of thermodynamics for uncharged black strings, we consider
{\cal M}(r_{h})
in Eq. (
2.54
) as a Smarr-type formula. On the other side, from Eqs. (
2.43
) and (
2.55
), one can also obtain
r_{h}=r_{h}({\cal S},{\cal J})
and as a result
{\cal M}={\cal M}({\cal S},{\cal J})
. Therefore, temperature
T
and angular velocity
\Omega
can be defined as intensive quantities conjugate to
{\cal S}
and
{\cal J}
, respectively, and can be obtained from

\displaystyle T\equiv\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal S}}\right)_{{\cal J}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)_{{\cal J}}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal S}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)^{-1}_{{\cal J}},

\displaystyle\Omega\equiv\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal J}}\right)_{{\cal S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)_{{\cal S}}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal J}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)^{-1}_{{\cal S}}.

(2.56)

Our calculations show that
T
and
\Omega
calculated by the above equations coincide with corresponding ones in (
2.44
) and (
2.28
), respectively. So, as a consistency check, one can verify that the first law of thermodynamics for uncharged rotating black strings,

d{\cal M}=Td{\cal S}+\Omega d{\cal J},

(2.57)

holds exactly.

3
Asymptotically AdS charged solutions

Here we add a Maxwell term to the EQG action

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{16\pi}\left(R-2\Lambda-\sum_{i=1}^{6}{\hat{\lambda}}_{(i)}S_{4}^{(i)}\right)-\frac{1}{4}F_{ab}F^{ab}\right],

(3.1)

where
F_{ab}=2\partial_{[a}A_{b]}
. The vector potential and nonzero components of electromagnetic tensor are
[
15
]

\displaystyle A_{a}=A_{0}(r)\left(\Xi\delta_{a}^{t}-a\delta_{a}^{\phi}\right);

\displaystyle F_{tr}=-F_{rt}=-\Xi A^{\prime}_{0}(r),F_{\phi r}=-F_{r\phi}=aA^{\prime}_{0}(r).

(3.2)

The
t
and
\phi
components of the Maxwell equation,
\triangledown_{a}F^{ab}=0
, now read

rA^{\prime\prime}_{0}g-rA^{\prime}_{0}g^{\prime}+2A^{\prime}_{0}g=0.

(3.3)

Here also we find that
g^{\prime}=0
and accordingly set
g=1
, which yields the following value for the gauge potential

A_{0}(r)=\frac{q}{4\pi r},

(3.4)

where
q
is an integration constant, which is indeed the electric charge of the black string. As the uncharged case, the equation for
f
can be found by variation of (
3.1
) with respect to the metric function
g
,

-\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{3}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r}-rf-\frac{1}{10}K\left[\frac{3}{r}f^{\prime 3}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}ff^{\prime 2}\left(f^{\prime}-3rf^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{24}{r^{3}}f^{2}f^{\prime}\left(f^{\prime}-rf^{\prime\prime}\right)\right]=r_{0},

(3.5)

where we have defined
Q^{2}\equiv q^{2}/4\pi
, and as the uncharged case
r_{0}
is an integration constant, which is related to the mass of the black string as
r_{0}=M
.

3.1
Large-r asymptotic region

In this section, we will perform an asymptotic expansion around
r\rightarrow\infty
. Setting
K=0
in Eq. (
3.5
) leads to

f(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}-\frac{M}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}},

(3.6)

which is the usual asymptotically AdS charged solution of rotating black strings
[
15
,
14
]
. To examine the large-
r
behavior of the solution, suppose that the metric function
f
is given by (
2.15
). By inserting this series expansion into Eq. (
3.5
), one observes that the large-
r
expansion of metric function
f
takes the following form

f_{1/r}(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{l_{\rm eff}^{2}}-\frac{M}{\left(1-\frac{32K}{5l_{\rm eff}^{6}}\right)r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{\left(1-\frac{32K}{5l_{\rm eff}^{6}}\right)r^{2}}+{\cal O}(r^{-3}),

(3.7)

where the effective radius of the AdS space
l_{\rm eff}
, is defined by Eq. (
2.17
). The linearized homogeneous equation satisfied by
f_{h}(r)
is given by

f_{h}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{12M}{4Q^{2}-3Mr}f_{h}^{\prime}(r)+\frac{3M\gamma^{2}r^{2}}{4Q^{2}-3Mr}f_{h}(r)=0,

(3.8)

at the large-
r
limit. Notice that when
Q=0
, we recover Eq. (
2.18
). Unfortunately, this equation can not be solved analytically. But due to the existence of
\left(4Q^{2}-3Mr\right)
in the denominator, it is possible to expand the above equation at large
r
one more time and find a solution and discuss the boundary conditions. By doing so, we arrive at

f_{h}^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{\left(4Q^{2}+3Mr\right)\gamma^{2}}{3M}f_{h}(r)=0.

(3.9)

Here,
\gamma^{2}
is given by Eq. (
2.19
). The solution reads,

f_{h}(r)=A^{\prime}\text{Ai}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\left|\gamma^{2}\right|^{1/3}\right)+B^{\prime}\text{Bi}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\left|\gamma^{2}\right|^{1/3}\right),

(3.10)

where
Ai
and
Bi
are the Airy functions of the first and second kinds, and
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are some constants. In the case of
\gamma^{2}>0
, and at large
r
,

\displaystyle f_{h}^{(+)}(r)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{1/4}\times

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\{A^{\prime}\exp\left[-\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]+B^{\prime}\exp\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]\right\},

and so we must set
B^{\prime}=0
to ensure the AdS boundary conditions are satisfied. As a result no ghost excitations can propagate to infinity. If
\gamma^{2}<0
, then the homogeneous solution asymptotically takes the following form at large
r
:

\displaystyle f_{h}^{(-)}(r)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{-1/4}\times

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left\{A^{\prime}\sin\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]+B^{\prime}\cos\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]\right\}.

Here also the solution oscillates rapidly, and therefore, does not approach AdS asymptotically, and so we set
A^{\prime}=B^{\prime}=0
. As the uncharged case, to avoid any oscillating behavior at infinity, we restrict the solutions to the constraint
\gamma^{2}>0
. Observe that all the leading asymptotic corrections to the metric come from the solution (
3.7
), while the contributions from the homogeneous equation are extremely subleading. Hence, the term proportional to
A^{\prime}
above can be discarded from the asymptotic expansion.

3.2
Near-horizon region

Expanding the field equation (
3.5
) near the horizon, via the Taylor series (
2.29
), leads to the following equations at two lowest-order:

\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}-\frac{24K\kappa_{g}^{4}\Xi^{4}}{5r_{h}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{h}}-M=0,

(3.13)

\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{l^{2}}-\frac{8K\kappa_{g}^{4}\Xi^{4}}{5r_{h}^{2}}-\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}-2\kappa_{g}\Xi r_{h}=0.

(3.14)

By converting
K\rightarrow-K
because of the constraint (
2.34
) and solving the above equations, one can easily find the surface gravity and mass as

\kappa_{g}=\frac{\sqrt[6]{5}\left(\sqrt{\frac{6\sqrt{10\tau^{\prime}}r_{h}^{3}l^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}}-\eta^{\prime}}+\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}\right)}{2\ 2^{5/6}\sqrt[3]{3K}\Xi\sqrt{l}\sqrt[6]{\tau^{\prime}}},

(3.15)

M=\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{h}}+\frac{\left(\sqrt{\frac{6\sqrt{10\tau^{\prime}}r_{h}^{3}l^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}}-\eta^{\prime}}+\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}\right)^{4}}{16\sqrt[3]{30K}r_{h}l^{2}\tau^{\prime 2/3}},

(3.16)

where

\xi^{\prime}\equiv 2025r_{h}^{12}l^{6}-1920K\left(3r_{h}^{4}-Q^{2}l^{2}\right)^{3},

(3.17)

\tau^{\prime}\equiv\sqrt{\xi^{\prime}}+45r_{h}^{6}l^{3},

(3.18)

\zeta^{\prime}\equiv 4\sqrt[3]{30}\left(3r_{h}^{4}-Q^{2}l^{2}\right).

(3.19)

\eta^{\prime}\equiv\sqrt[3]{K}\zeta^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime 2/3}.

(3.20)

As mentioned above for the uncharged case, all
a_{n}
for
n>2
are determined in terms of
K
,
Q
,
r_{h}
and
a_{2}
, while
a_{2}
is left undetermined by the field equations.

As a consistency check, when
K\rightarrow 0
the above quantities for surface gravity and mass tend to
k_{g}=\frac{3r_{h}}{2l^{2}\Xi}-\frac{Q^{2}}{2r_{h}^{3}\Xi}
and
M=\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{h}}
, respectively, which are the corresponding ones in RN-AdS solutions of black strings.

3.3
Thermodynamics

In the case of charged solutions, we find that the Wald entropy per unit length reads,

{\cal S}=\frac{\pi\Xi\left[5r_{h}^{4}+\frac{\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}r_{h}\left(\sqrt{\frac{6\sqrt{10\tau^{\prime}}r_{h}^{3}l^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}}-\eta^{\prime}}+\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}\right)^{3}}{3l^{3/2}\sqrt{\tau^{\prime}}}\right]}{10r_{h}^{2}l}.

(3.21)

Here also we observe that when coupling constant goes to zero, we recover the entropy of black strings in Einstein-Maxwell gravity, as expected. The Hawking temperature also can easily be found in terms of total charge and the horizon radius by multiplying the surface gravity (
3.15
) with a factor
1/2\pi
,

T=\frac{\sqrt[6]{5}\left(\sqrt{\frac{6\sqrt{10\tau^{\prime}}r_{h}^{3}l^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}}-\eta^{\prime}}+\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}\right)}{4\pi\ 2^{5/6}\sqrt[3]{3K}\Xi\sqrt{l}\sqrt[6]{\tau^{\prime}}}.

(3.22)

Substituting Eq. (
3.16
) into the first line of Eqs. (
2.52
) and (
2.53
), one finds the total mass (energy) and angular momentum per unit length as

{\cal M}=\frac{1}{8l}\left(3\Xi^{2}-1\right)\left[\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{h}}+\frac{\left(\sqrt{\frac{6\sqrt{10\tau^{\prime}}r_{h}^{3}l^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}}-\eta^{\prime}}+\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}\right)^{4}}{16\sqrt[3]{30K}r_{h}l^{2}\tau^{\prime 2/3}}\right],

(3.23)

{\cal J}=\frac{3}{8}\sqrt{\Xi^{2}-1}\left[\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{h}}+\frac{\left(\sqrt{\frac{6\sqrt{10\tau^{\prime}}r_{h}^{3}l^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}}-\eta^{\prime}}+\sqrt{\eta^{\prime}}\right)^{4}}{16\sqrt[3]{30K}r_{h}l^{2}\tau^{\prime 2/3}}\right].

(3.24)

Now we are going to find the electric potential and total charge of black strings. The electric potential
\Phi
, measured at infinity with respect to the horizon, is defined by
[
18
]

\Phi=A_{a}\chi^{a}\lvert_{r\rightarrow\infty}-A_{a}\chi^{a}\lvert_{r=r_{h}},

(3.25)

Calculating the above expression yields the following value for electric potential

\displaystyle\Phi=\frac{q}{4\pi\Xi r_{h}}=\frac{Q}{2\sqrt{\pi}\Xi r_{h}}.

(3.26)

To calculate the electric charge of the black string, we first determine the electric field by considering the projections of the electromagnetic field tensor on a special hypersurface. The normal vectors to such a hypersurface are

u^{0}=\frac{1}{N},u^{r}=0,u^{i}=-\frac{V^{i}}{N},

(3.27)

where
N
and
V^{i}
are the lapse function and shift vector, respectively. The electric field is also given by
E^{\mu}=g^{\mu\rho}F_{\rho\nu}u^{\nu}
. The electric charge per unit length of black strings can be found by calculating the flux of the electric field at infinity, yielding

\displaystyle{\cal Q}=\frac{q\Xi}{2l}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}Q\Xi}{l}.

(3.28)

Now, we are in a position to verify the validity of the first law of thermodynamics for charged black strings. To do so, we first consider Eq. (
3.16
) as a Smarr-type formula for mass
{\cal M}(r_{h},{\cal Q})
. It can also be seen from Eqs. (
3.21
), (
2.55
) and (
3.28
) that
r_{h}=r_{h}({\cal S},{\cal J},{\cal Q})
and accordingly
{\cal M}={\cal M}({\cal S},{\cal J},{\cal Q})
. Therefore, temperature
T
, angular velocity
\Omega
and electric potential
\Phi
can be defined as conjugate intensive parameters for
{\cal S}
,
{\cal J}
and
{\cal Q}
, and are given by the following expressions

\displaystyle T\equiv\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal S}}\right)_{{\cal J},{\cal Q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)_{{\cal J},{\cal Q}}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal S}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)^{-1}_{{\cal J},{\cal Q}},

\displaystyle\Omega\equiv\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal J}}\right)_{{\cal S},{\cal Q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)_{{\cal S},{\cal Q}}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal J}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)^{-1}_{{\cal S},{\cal Q}},

\displaystyle\Phi\equiv\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal Q}}\right)_{{\cal S},{\cal J}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal Q}}\right)_{r_{h},{\cal J}}+\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)_{{\cal Q},{\cal J}}\left(\frac{\partial r_{h}}{\partial{\cal Q}}\right)_{{\cal S},{\cal J}}

(3.29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal Q}}\right)_{r_{h},{\cal J}}-\frac{\left(\frac{\partial{\cal S}}{\partial{\cal Q}}\right)_{r_{h},{\cal J}}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)_{{\cal Q},{\cal J}}}{\left(\frac{\partial{\cal S}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)_{{\cal Q},{\cal J}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial{\cal Q}}\right)_{r_{h},{\cal J}}-T\left(\frac{\partial{\cal S}}{\partial{\cal Q}}\right)_{r_{h},{\cal J}}.

It is not difficult to show that the above quantities coincide with those obtained in Eqs. (
3.22
), (
2.28
) and (
3.26
). Having had the thermodynamic properties, one can easily check that the first law of thermodynamics for charged rotating black strings,

d{\cal M}=Td{\cal S}+\Omega d{\cal J}+\Phi d{\cal Q},

(3.30)

is fully satisfied in the presence of EQG theory.

4
Discussion

We have done the first study of asymptotically AdS charged rotating black string solutions in the context of EQG, a class of theories for which corrections to the EH action are quartic in curvature. It can be seen that under cylindrical symmetry, just like spherical one, there is a degeneracy that yields the same field equation for all theories in the class. It means that the corrections to Einstein gravity depend only on a single coupling constant. These theories are the next simplest kinds that occur in GQTG after Einsteinian cubic gravity, and are the highest in curvature that allow explicit solutions for the total mass and Hawking temperature in terms of the horizon radius. We have obtained the solutions in both the near-horizon and large-distance approximations.

We have proved that, in our model, the ghost-free branch of solutions will occur when the spacetime is asymptotically AdS, as well. We have found that the solutions have a curvature singularity in
r=0
at which both Ricci and Kretschmann scalars diverge. We have used the definition of conserved charge provided by the quasilocal stress–tensor introduced by Brown and York to calculate the total energy (mass) and angular momentum of these solutions. We have also obtained exact formulas for all the relevant thermodynamical quantities and using them the validity of first law of thermodynamics for rotating black strings have been verified in both charged and uncharged cases. This is an interesting check of our calculations, since the physical quantities appearing in this expression namely, the Abbott-Deser mass
{\cal M}
, the Wald entropy
{\cal S}
, the Hawking temperature
T
, the angular velocity
\Omega
, the angular momentum
{\cal J}
, the electric potential
\Phi
and the charge
{\cal Q}
have been computed independently.

As an extra check of our calculations, it also can be seen that when the EQG coupling constant
K
tends to zero, we recover the corresponding physical quantities in Einstein gravity.

For future works, it also would be interesting to perform this kind of study in higher dimensions,
i.e.,
finding the black brane solutions in the context of EQG. Finding the solutions in the presence of nonlinear Born-Infeld electrodynamics is also an interesting feature. More generally, it would be of interest to study holographic implications of the quartic curvature term.

Subalgebra generated by ad-locally nilpotent elements of Borcherds Generalized Kac-Moody Lie algebras
Shrawan Kumar

Abstract




1.
Abstract

We determine the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}
of a Borcherds symmetrizable generalized Kac-Moody Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
generated by
\ad
-locally nilpotent elements and show that it is ‘essentially’ the same as the Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
with its simple roots precisely the real simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
.

2.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(A)
be the symmetrizable Generalized Kac-Moody
(GKM) algebra associated to a
\ell\times\ell
matrix
A
(cf. Section 3). Let

\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:\,\,\text{$\ad x$ acts locally nilpotently on $\mathfrak{g}$}\},

and let
\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie subalgebra generated by
\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
. Then, we prove the following theorem
(cf. Theorem
4.1
):

Theorem.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{[}g](A)
be as above, where
\ell\geq 2
and
A
is indecomposable, i.e., the corresponding Dynkin diagram is connected. Then,

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)+\mathfrak{h},

where
B\subset A
is the submatrix parameterized by those
i
such that
a_{i,i}=2

and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)
is the derived subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}(B)
.

As shown in Remark
4.2
, the assumption
\ell\geq 2
in the above theorem is necessary in general.

3.
Basic Definition

In this section, we recall the definition of Borcherds Generalized Kac-Moody Lie algebras
\mathfrak{[}g]
(for short GKM algebras). For a more extensive treatment of
\mathfrak{[}g]
and its properties, see Chapters 1, 11 of
[
Kac
]
and the papers
[
Bo1
]
and
[
Bo2
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})
be a
\ell\times\ell
matrix (for
\ell\geq 1
) with real entries, satisfying the following properties;

(P1) either
a_{i,i}=2
or
a_{i,i}\leq 0
,

(P2)
a_{i,j}\leq 0
if
i\neq j
, and
a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
if
a_{i,i}=2
,

(P3)
a_{i,j}=0
if and only if
a_{j,i}=0
.

Fix a
realization
of
A
, which is a triple
(\mathfrak{h},\Pi,\Pi^{\vee})
consisting of a complex vector space
\mathfrak{h}
,
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}\}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
\Pi^{\vee}=\{\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}^{\vee}\}\subset\mathfrak{h}
are indexed subsets, satisfying the following three conditions:

(Q1) both sets
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\vee}
are linearly independent,

(Q2)
\alpha_{j}(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})=a_{i,j}
, for all
i,j
,

(Q3)
\ell-\rank A=\dim\mathfrak{h}-\ell
.

By
[
Kac
]
, Proposition 1.1, such a realization is unique up to an isomorphism of the triple.

Now, the Borcherds Generalized Kac-Moody Lie algebra (for short GKM algebra)
\mathfrak{[}g](A)
is defined as the Lie algebra generated by

\{e_{i},f_{i},\mathfrak{h}\}_{1\leq i\leq\ell}
subject to the following relations:

(R1)
[e_{i},f_{j}]=\delta{ij}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
, for all
i
,

(R2)
[h,h^{\prime}]=0
, for all
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}
,

(R3)
[h,e_{i}]=\alpha_{i}(h)e_{i};\,\,[h,f_{i}]=-\alpha_{i}(h)f_{i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}
,

(R4)
(\ad e_{i})^{1-a_{i,j}}e_{j}=(\ad f_{i})^{1-a_{i,j}}f_{j}=0,\,\,
if
a_{i,i}=2
and
i\neq j
,

(R5)
[e_{i},e_{j}]=[f_{i},f_{j}]=0,\,\,
if
a_{i,j}=0
.

The matrix
A
(or the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(A)
) is called
symmetrizable
if there exists an invertible diagonal matrix

D=\text{diag}(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{\ell})
such that the matrix
DA
is symmetric.

4.
Main theorem and its proof

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{[}g](A)
be the symmetrizable GKM algebra associated to a
\ell\times\ell
matrix
A
as in the last section. Assume further that
\ell\geq 2
and
A
is indecomposable, i.e., the corresponding Dynkin diagram is connected. Let

\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:\,\,\text{$\ad x$ acts locally nilpotently on $\mathfrak{g}$}\},

and let
\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie subalgebra generated by
\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
. Then,

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)+\mathfrak{h},

where
B\subset A
is the submatrix parameterized by those
i
such that
a_{ii}=2
, i.e.,
\alpha_{i}
is a real root
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)
is the derived subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}(B)
.

Proof.

Consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
induced from a homomorphism
\theta:Q:=\oplus_{i}\,\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}\to\mathbb{Z}
. Then, for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil},x_{+}(\theta)\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
, where
x_{+}(\theta)
is the top degree component of
x
in the
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
induced by
\theta
. To prove this, observe that for any
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
(where
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the root space corresponding to the root
\alpha
or
0
),

(\ad x)^{n}(y)=(\ad x_{+}(\theta))^{n}(y)+\,\text{lower degree terms}.

Similarly, for
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil},x_{-}(\theta)\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
, where
x_{-}(\theta)
is the lowest degree component of
x
.

Further, given any nonzero
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we can get a gradation
\theta_{x}:Q\to\mathbb{Z}
as above (depending upon
x
) such that all the homogeneous degree components of
x
(under
\theta_{x}
) belong to root spaces
\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
. To prove this, write
x=\sum_{j}\,x_{\beta_{j}}
, where
\beta_{j}
are distinct roots or zero,
x_{\beta_{j}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\beta_{j}}
and each
x_{\beta_{j}}\neq 0
. Consider the finite collection of weights:
\{\beta_{j}-\beta_{k}\}_{j\neq k}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Now, we can find a vector
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}=Q\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
such that the standard dot product
(\cdot,\cdot)
in
\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}
:

(1)

\theta_{\gamma}(\beta_{j}-\beta_{k}):=(\beta_{j}-\beta_{k},\gamma)\neq 0,\,\,\text{for any $j\neq k$}.

To prove the above equation, consider the
\ell-1
-dimensional subspace
V_{j,k}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}
(for any
j\neq k
) perpendicular to
\beta_{j}-\beta_{k}
. Since the collection
\{\beta_{j}-\beta_{k}\}_{j\neq k}
is finite, we can find a vector
\gamma
such that the equation (
1
) is satisfied. We can further take
\gamma\in Q\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
by clearing the denominators.

So, if
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
, then either
x
belongs to the center
Z(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
(cf.
[
Kac
]
, Proposition 1.6) or the root component
x_{\beta}\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
for some root
\beta
(
\beta\neq 0
). Moreover, if some nonzero root component of
x
belongs to the root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}
such that
\delta
contains an imaginary simple root
\delta_{p}
(i.e., with
a_{p,p}\leq 0
) with nonzero coefficient, we can assume that
x_{\delta}\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
(possibly with a different nonzero root component of
x
corresponding to a root containing an imaginary simple root with nonzero coefficient). This is achieved by taking
\gamma
as above but requiring
\theta_{\gamma}(\alpha_{p})
to be much larger for all the imaginary simple roots
\alpha_{p}
as compared to the values
\theta_{\gamma}(\alpha_{q})
for all the real simple roots
\alpha_{q}
(i.e., those with
a_{q,q}=2
).

By using the Cartan involution
\omega
of
\mathfrak{g}
(i.e.,
\omega(e_{i})=-f_{i},\omega(f_{i})=-e_{i},\omega(h)=-h\,\forall h\in\mathfrak{h}
), if needed, we can further assume that
\delta
is a positive root. Write

\delta=\sum_{p}(m_{p}\alpha_{p})+\sum_{q}(n_{q}\alpha_{q}),\,\,\text{for $m_{p},n_{q}\geq 0$},

where
\alpha_{p}
(resp.
\alpha_{q}
) run over all the imaginary (resp. real) simple roots. In particular, some
m_{p}>0
. By
[
Kac
]
, Exercise 11.21, the support

\supp(\delta)
is connected. Assume first that
\delta
is not an imaginary simple root. Further, taking some
W
-translate (where
W
is the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
, cf.
[
Kac
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
11.13), we can assume that
\delta(\alpha_{q}^{\vee})\leq 0
for all the real simple coroots
\alpha_{q}^{\vee}
(cf.
[
Kac
]
, Identity 11.13.3). Now, with respect to the
W
-invariant symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
(cf.
[
Kac
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.1),

\displaystyle\langle\delta,\delta\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\,m_{p}\langle\delta,\alpha_{p}\rangle+\sum_{q}\,n_{q}\langle\delta,\alpha_{q}\rangle

(2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}\,n_{q}\langle\delta,\alpha_{q}\rangle+\sum_{p,q}\,m_{p}n_{q}\langle\alpha_{q},\alpha_{p}\rangle+\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}\,m_{p}m_{p^{\prime}}\langle\alpha_{p^{\prime}},\alpha_{p}\rangle,

where
\alpha_{p^{\prime}}
also runs over imaginary simple roots.
Now, by assumption,

(3)

\langle\delta,\alpha_{q}\rangle\leq 0,\,\,\text{ for all the real simple roots}.

For any imaginary simple root
\alpha_{p}

and any real simple root
\alpha_{q}
,
we have

(4)

\langle\alpha_{q},\alpha_{p}\rangle\leq 0,\,\,\text{since $a_{p,q}\leq 0$}.

Further, for imaginary simple roots
\alpha_{p},\alpha_{p^{\prime}}
,

(5)

\langle\alpha_{p^{\prime}},\alpha_{p}\rangle\leq 0,\,\,\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Kac}{}{}]}, Identity 2.1.6}.

Observe that we can take the normalizing factor
\epsilon_{i}>0
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
as can be seen from
the identity:

\epsilon_{i}a_{i,j}=\epsilon_{j}a_{j,i},\,\,\text{for all $1\leq i,j\leq\ell$},

where the diagonal matrix
D=\text{diag}(\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{\ell})
is such that DA is a symmetric matrix. Moreover, since there exists
p
with
m_{p}\neq 0
and since
\supp\delta
is connected and
\delta
is not a simple root, by
[
Kac
]
, Identity 2.1.6,

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{p^{\prime}},\alpha_{p}\rangle<0,\,\,\text{for some $p^{\prime}\neq p$ with $m_{p^{\prime}}\neq 0$ and $\alpha_{p^{\prime}}$ an imaginary simple root}

(6)

\displaystyle\text{or $\langle\alpha_{q},\alpha_{p}\rangle<0$ for some $q$ with $n_{q}\neq 0$ and $\alpha_{q}$ a real simple root}.

Thus, combining the equations (
4
) - (
4
), we get:

\langle\delta,\delta\rangle<0.

By
[
Kac
]
, Corollary 9.12,
\oplus_{k>0}\,\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}
is a free Lie algebra on a basis of the form
\oplus_{k>0}\,\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}^{o}
, where

\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}^{o}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}:\langle x,y\rangle=0\forall y\text{ in the Lie subalgebra generated by}\,\mathfrak{g}_{-\delta},\mathfrak{g}_{-2\delta},\dots,\mathfrak{g}_{-(k-1)\delta}\}.

Observe next that
\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}\neq 0
for any
k>0
by
[
Kac
]
, Identity 11.13.3. If
\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}
is one dimensional, then so is
\mathfrak{g}_{-\delta}
and hence
\mathfrak{g}_{2\delta}^{o}\neq 0
. Thus,
\oplus_{k>0}\,\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}
is a free Lie algebra on at least
2
generators. If
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}\geq 2
, then
\oplus_{k>0}\,\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}
is again a free Lie algebra on at least two generators (since
\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}^{o}=\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}
).
Thus,
\ad(x_{\delta})
can not act locally nilpotently on
\oplus_{k>0}\,\mathfrak{g}_{k\delta}

and hence on
\mathfrak{g}
(since the enveloping algebra of a free Lie algebra is the tensor algebra on the same generators and now use
[
Ku
]
, Identity (3) of Definition 1.3.2).

Now, if
\delta=\alpha_{p}
is an imaginary simple root. Then, again
x_{\delta}=e_{p}
can not act nilpotently on any
e_{i}
,
i\neq p
such that
a_{i,p}\neq 0
. (This is where we have used the assumption that
A
is indecomposable and
\ell\geq 2
.) To prove this, use
[
Kac
]
, Identity 11.13.3 by observing that
(n\alpha_{p}+\alpha_{i})\in K
for all
n\geq 2
in the notation of loc cit. Thus, we conclude that any
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
must be of the form
x\in\mathfrak{g}(B)+\mathfrak{h}
. Hence,

\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)+\mathfrak{h}.

Further, by
[
Ku
]
, Lemma 1.3.3(a) and the defining relations of
\mathfrak{g}(A)
,
e_{i},f_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{\nil}
for any real simple root
\alpha_{i}
. Thus,

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}.

This proves the theorem

∎

Remark 4.2
.

(a) Define

{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}^{o}_{\nil}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}:\,\,\text{$\ad x$ acts locally nilpotently on $\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}$}\}

and let
{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}_{\nil}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
be the Lie subalgebra generated by
{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}^{o}_{\nil}
. Then, by the same proof as above,

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{\nil}\subset(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}(B)+\mathfrak{h})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}.

(b) It is easy to see that the above theorem remains true in the case
A
is parameterized by
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
.

(c) For the
1\times 1
-matrix
A=(0)
, following
[
Kac
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.9,
\mathfrak{g}(A)=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}f_{1}
, where
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\alpha_{1}^{\vee}\oplus\mathbb{C}d
and
[e_{1},f_{1}]=\alpha_{1}^{\vee},[\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\mathfrak{g}]=0,[d,e_{1}]=e_{1},[d,f_{1}]=-f_{1}.
Thus, in this case,
\mathfrak{g}_{\nil}=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.
Hence, the assumption
\ell\geq 2
in the above theorem is necessary in general.

(d) One interesting consequence of the above theorem is that the only connected ‘reasonable’ group attached to a GKM algebra
\mathfrak{g}(A)
is the one coming from its subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(B)
(up to an
\mathfrak{h}
-factor).

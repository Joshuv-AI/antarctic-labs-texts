Full satisfaction classes, definability, and automorphisms
Bartosz Wcisło

Abstract
We show that for every countable recursively saturated model M M of Peano Arithmetic and every subset A ⊆ M A\subseteq M , there exists a full satisfaction class S A ⊂ M 2 S_{A}\subset M^{2} such that A A is definable in ( M , S A ) (M,S_{A}) without parametres. It follows that in every such model, there exists a full satisfaction class which makes every element definable and thus the expanded model is minimal and rigid. On the other hand, we show that for every full satisfaction class S S there are two elements which have the same arithmetical type, but exactly one of them is in S S . In particular, the automorphism group of a model expanded with a satisfaction class is never equal to the automorphism group of the original model. The analogue of many of the results proved here for full satisfaction classes were obtained by Roman Kossak for partial inductive satisfaction classes. However, most of the proofs relied heavily on the induction scheme in a crucial way, so recapturing the results in the setting of full satisfaction classes requires quite different arguments.

Introduction

Satisfaction classes are a classical topic in the study of models of Peano Arithmetic. A full satisfaction class is essentially a function which assigns to each formula in the sense of a model a set of elements which "satisfy" that formula. We require that this assignment is compositional in that, for instance, the set of elements satisfying a conjunction
\phi\wedge\psi
is the intersection of the sets of elements satisfying
\phi
and
\psi
. In that way we endow nonstandard arithmetical formulae with certain "semantics." We can also weaken the requirement that the relation of satisfaction is defined for all formulae, thus obtaining a notion of a partial satisfaction class, or strengthen it in a number of interesting ways. For instance, we may require that the expansion of a model
M
with a satisfaction class
S
satisfies full induction, obtaining a notion of an inductive satisfaction class.

A systematic study of satisfaction classes was initiated by Krajewski,
preceded by a study of "nonstandard semantics" by Robinson,
[
15
]
. Probably the most important result of the whole area was obtained by Kotlarski, Krajewski, and Lachlan in
[
13
]
, where it was proved that in every countable recursively saturated model of
PA
there exists a full satisfaction class. The saturation assumption turned out to be essential, since it was subsequently shown by Lachlan in
[
14
]
that indeed every model with a full satisfaction class is recursively saturated. Later, it was shown that the presence of a full satisfaction class in a model is a strictly stronger requirement than recursive saturation. In
[
17
]
it was proved by Smith that every model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
with a full satisfaction class has an undefinable subset whose all bounded initial segments are coded in the model. On the other hand, by the previous work of Kaufmann,
[
8
]
, we know that there are uncountable recursively saturated models in which no such subset can be found (so called rather classless models).

Model-theoretic properties of the satisfaction classes themselves were also a subject of study. In
[
10
]
, a number of results typical for models of
PA
were proved by Kossak for the models expanded with satisfaction classes. For instance, it was shown that in every countable recursively saturated model
M
of
PA
, there exists a class
S\subset M
such that
(M,S)
is minimal, i.e., it does not have a proper elementary submodel. It was also proved that in any countable model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
, there exists a family of continuum many pairwise non-elementary-equivalent satisfaction classes. The work of Kossak was later used by him in a joint paper with Schmerl,
[
11
]
, to obtain new results on models of pure
PA
.

The findings from
[
10
]
mostly applied to inductive satisfaction classes, so most of them do not typically generalise when we require that the constructed class is also full, since the presence of a full inductive satisfaction class has nontrivial implications for the theory of the underlying model (e.g., it can be verified that such a model satisfies
\textnormal{Con}_{\textnormal{PA}}
). The proofs in
[
10
]
mostly relied on the induction scheme in a crucial way, so recapturing the results in the setting of full satisfaction classes requires quite different arguments.

1

1

This paper actually answers some questions of Roman Kossak, who asked during his visit in Warsaw whether certain results from that previous work also hold for full satisfaction classes.

In this article, we investigate such full satisfaction classes with special model-theoretic properties. Among other things, we show that in a countable model of
PA
, every set is definable (without parametres) from a full satisfaction class. This has a number of model-theoretic corollaries. For instance, we can require that every element in the model is definable from
S
without parametres, so that all elements of
M
become distinguishable in the presence of a full satisfaction class. On the other hand, we show that if
S
is a full satisfaction class on
M
, we can always find a pair of sentences
\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
with the same arithmetical types such that
\phi
is rendered true and
\psi
is rendered false, so it cannot happen that all elements indistinguishable by arithmetical formulae are also indistinguishable from the perspective of a given satisfaction class.

The study of satisfaction classes closely parallels the study of truth predicates which is an active research area in philosophical logic.
2
2

2

A classical textbook on axiomatic truth predicates is
[
6
]
. See also
[
1
]
for an overview of some more recent results in that field.
Although the results in both terminological traditions seem to go hand-in-hand, they are typically not formulated in a way that allows for a direct translation. In this article, we try to close this small gap and propose certain additional technical assumptions both on satisfaction and on truth classes that makes the correspondence completely direct. In particular, all our results apply,
mutatis mutandis
, both to satisfaction classes and to truth predicates.

.

3

2.2
Satisfaction classes

In the literature, there are two concurrent terminological traditions. One of them speaks of satisfaction classes and is more widespread in the study of models of arithmetic. The other tradition, which stems from philosophical logic, tends to formulate results in terms of truth predicates. In deference to
[
10
]
, we will follow the former tradition. However, to make our results easily translatable to the language of truth predicates, we will make sure that all satisfaction classes which we construct satisfy certain additional regularity properties.

Definition 8
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and let
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
. Let
S\subset M^{2}
. We say that
S
satisfies
compositional conditions
for
\phi
,
\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
if the disjunction of the following clauses holds in
(M,S)
:

1.

\exists s,t\in\textnormal{Term}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(\phi=(s=t)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\ \Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv s^{\alpha}=t^{\alpha}\Big)\Big).

2.

\exists\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(\phi=(\neg\psi)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\ \Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv\neg S(\psi,\alpha)\Big)\Big).

3.

\exists\psi,\eta\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\ \Big(\phi=(\psi\vee\eta)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv S(\psi,\alpha)\vee S(\eta,\alpha)\Big)\Big).

4.

\exists\psi,\eta\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\ \Big(\phi=(\psi\wedge\eta)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv S(\psi,\alpha)\wedge S(\eta,\alpha)\Big)\Big).

5.

\exists\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\exists v\in\textnormal{Var}\ \Big(\phi=(\exists v\psi)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv\exists\beta\sim_{v}\alpha S(\psi,\beta)\Big)\Big).

6.

\exists\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\exists v\in\textnormal{Var}\ \Big(\phi=(\forall v\psi)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv\forall\beta\sim_{v}\alpha\ S(\psi,\beta)\Big)\Big).

Definition 9
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
. We say that a set
S\subset M^{2}
is a
satisfaction class
if there exists
D\subset\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
such that:

•

If
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
, then
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M),\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)
.

•

If there exists
\alpha
such that
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
, then
(M,S)\models\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
.

•

If there exists
\alpha
such that
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
, then either
\phi\in D
or there exists
\psi
such that
\phi=\neg\psi
and
\psi\in D
.

•

(M,S)
satisfies
\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
for every
\phi\in D
.

•

If
\psi
is a direct subformula of
\phi
and
\phi\in D
, then
\psi\in D
.

•

If
\phi\in D
, then for every
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)
either
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
or
(\neg\phi,\alpha)\in S
.

By a
domain of

S
,
\textnormal{dom}(S)
, we mean the minimal set
D\subset\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
satisfying the above conditions.
5
5

5

Without the minimality requirement the domain is ”almost” unique, since it not determined how to treat the formulae which are negations satisfied under all assignments. If
(\neg\phi,\alpha)\in S
for all
\alpha
and
(\neg\neg\neg\phi,\alpha)\notin S
for any
\alpha
, then we stipulate that
\phi
is in the domain of
S
, but
\neg\phi
is not.

We say that a satisfaction class
S

decides
a formula
\phi
if for every
\alpha\in\textnormal{Val}(\phi)
either
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
or
(\neg\phi,\alpha)\in S
.
We say that
S
is a
full satisfaction class
if
\textnormal{dom}(S)=M
, i.e.,
(M,S)
satisfies
\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
for every
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
. We call a satisfaction class
partial
if it is not full.
A (full or partial) satisfaction class
S
is
inductive
iff
(M,S)
satisfies the full induction scheme.

Remark 10
.

Our definition of a satisfaction class agrees with the usual one for full satisfaction classes and essentially agrees with the usual one for satisfaction classes defined on the formulae in some initial segment of a model.

According to the definition proposed above, a satisfaction class is required to be completely determined about any formula to which it ascribes any assignments. If
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
, then we fully know for any
\beta\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)
whether
(\phi,\beta)
holds or not and the same holds for the direct subformulae of
\phi
.

Notice that our definition is not very restrictive. Let us introduce a notion of a pre-satisfaction class:
S
is a pre-satisfaction class if it satisfies compositional conditions on a set of formulae
D
closed under direct subformulae. Then given such an
S
, we can define a satisfaction class
S^{\prime}
such that
S^{\prime}\cap(D\times M)=S\cap(D\times M)
. Indeed, let
(\phi,\alpha)\in S^{\prime}
if either
\phi\in D
and
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
or
\phi=\neg\psi
,
\psi\in D
and
(\psi,\alpha)\notin S
. One can verify that
S^{\prime}
defined in such a way is a satisfaction class in our sense, so we do not lose very much by imposing the "definiteness" conditions.

Remark 11
.

In Definition
9
, we required that satisfaction classes are defined over the arithmetical language. Of course, nothing prevents us from defining them over an arbitrary (say, primitive recursive) language
\mathscr{L}
. In such a case, we have to add the atomic clauses for the new atomic formulae of
\mathscr{L}
to the condition
\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
and replace every mention of
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
with
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}}
.

As we have already remarked, there are two concurrent terminological traditions which speak of essentially the same objects: satisfaction classes and truth predicates. Unfortunately, these two notions do not translate directly into each other. Indeed, suppose that
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and
S
is a full satisfaction class on
M
. Then we would like to define a truth predicate
T
as follows:

T=\{\phi[\alpha]\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\ \mid\ (\phi,\alpha)\in S\}.

However, defined in such a way,
T
is not necessarily a truth predicate.
6
6

6

We will shortly define what we actually mean by a truth predicate. For the time being, the reader should think of it as a set of sentences in the sense of a model which satisfies compositional conditions similar to that of a satisfaction class, where we require that true quantified statements are witnessed by (certain) terms.
Indeed, take a formula
\phi(v)\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
with only one free variable
v
and suppose that
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
, where
\alpha
sends
v
to
0
. Then, by definition
T(\phi(0))
holds. However, notice that nothing in the definition of the satisfaction class guarantees that
(\phi(0),\emptyset)\in S
. In fact, one can construct full satisfaction classes such that both
(\phi,\alpha)
and
(\neg\phi(0),\emptyset)
belong to
S
. Such a class would yield
T(\neg\phi(0))
, contradicting compositionality.
7
7

7

Such a construction could be performed by an application of the Enayat–Visser technique which we discuss in this article.

A familiar definition which says that a sentence is true iff it is satisfied under any assignment works even worse. Again, we can construct models of
PA
with full satisfaction classes,
(M,S)
, where for some sentence
\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
neither
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
nor
(\neg\phi,\alpha)\in S
holds for all
\alpha
(that is, the satisfaction predicate may depend nontrivially on the assignments, even though a formula in question has no free variables). A truth predicate obtained from such a model with this classical definition would not even satisfy the compositional condition for the negation.

The question of defining a satisfaction class out of a truth class is slightly subtler and depends on what exact compositional conditions for the quantifiers are accepted. One of the common choices requires that quantified sentences are true if they are witnessed by some term. Thus the compositional axiom for the existential quantifier looks as follows:

T\exists v\phi\equiv\exists t\in\textnormal{ClTerm}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ T\phi(t).

If we accept this kind of axioms and set, for instance,

S=\{(\phi,\alpha)\in M^{2}\ \mid\ \phi[\alpha]\in T\}.

then in general we cannot show that quantifier axioms for the satisfaction class will hold, since it could happen for a nonstandard
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
that
\exists v\phi\in T,\phi(0+0)\in T
and for all
x\in M
,
\phi(\underline{x})\notin T
. Then the quantifier axiom for
S
would break down as well. (Recall that in
\phi[\alpha]
we substitute for any free variable
v
the
numeral
whose value is
\alpha(v)
.) Whether a satisfaction class can define a truth predicate with some other, possibly much more complex definition (or vice versa), seems to be a rather subtle question, but we will not pursue it any further in this article.

In order to make the statements about truth predicates and satisfaction classes directly translatable between the two settings, we will impose some additional technical conditions on the constructed classes. This will not be important for any results in this article (i.e., they still hold if we do not make any of these regularity assumptions).

Definition 12
.

Suppose that
\phi,\psi
are arithmetical formulae. Let
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi),\beta\in\textnormal{Asn}(\psi)
. We say that the pairs
(\phi,\alpha),(\psi,\beta)
are
extensionally equivalent
if there exist two sequences of closed terms
\bar{s},\bar{t}
such that for each
i
,
{t_{i}}^{\circ}={s_{i}}^{\circ}
, and a formula
\eta
such that
\phi[\alpha]=\eta(\bar{s}),\psi[\beta]=\eta(\bar{t})
. If
(\phi,\alpha),(\psi,\beta)
are extensionally equivalent, we denote it with
(\phi,\alpha)\simeq(\psi,\beta)
.

Definition 13
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and let
S\subset M^{2}
be a (full or partial) satisfaction class. We say that
M
is
regular
if for any extensionally equivalent pairs
(\phi,\alpha)\simeq(\psi,\beta)
,
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
iff
(\psi,\beta)\in S
.

Regular satisfaction classes are in one-to-one correspondence with compositional truth predicates satisfying regularity assumptions, so our results extend to that setting. Let us state this in a more precise manner.

Definition 14
.

By
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
(compositional truth) we mean an axiomatic theory in the arithmetical language with a fresh predicate
T
extending
PA
with the following axioms:

1.

\forall x\ \Big(T(x)\rightarrow x\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big).

2.

\forall s,t\in\textnormal{ClTerm}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(T(s=t)\equiv{s}^{\circ}={t}^{\circ}\Big).

3.

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(T\neg\phi\equiv\neg T\phi\Big).

4.

\forall\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(T(\phi\vee\psi)\equiv T\phi\vee T\psi)\Big).

5.

\forall\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(T(\phi\wedge\psi)\equiv T\phi\wedge T\psi)\Big).

6.

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall v\in\textnormal{Var}\ \Big(\phi=(\exists v\psi)\rightarrow\Big(T\phi\equiv\exists x\ T\phi(\underline{x})\Big)\Big).

7.

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall v\in\textnormal{Var}\ \Big(\phi=(\forall v\psi)\rightarrow\Big(T\phi\equiv\forall x\ T\phi(\underline{x})\Big)\Big).

8.

\forall\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall\bar{s},\bar{t}\in\textnormal{ClTermSeq}\Big(\bar{{s}^{\circ}}=\bar{{t}^{\circ}}\rightarrow T\phi(\bar{s})\equiv T\phi(\bar{t})\Big).

Essentially, in our definitions we stipulate that both satisfaction classes and truth predicates are not sensitive to specific terms, but only see their values. The following claim can be now easily verified.

Proposition 15
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and let
S\subset M^{2}
be a regular satisfaction class. Let
T:=\{\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\ \mid\ (\phi[\alpha],\emptyset)\in S\}
. Then
(M,T)\models\textnormal{CT}^{-}
. Conversely, let
(M,T)\models\textnormal{CT}^{-}
. Let
S=\{(\phi,\alpha)\in M^{2}\ \mid\ \phi[\alpha]\in T\}.
Then
S
is a regular satisfaction class.

We can define a number of other natural regularity properties of satisfaction or truth classes. Let us introduce one of them which will play a prominent role in our paper.

Definition 16
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
, let
S
be a satisfaction class, and let
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
. We say that the
quantifier correctness
property holds for
\phi
if for all
c\in M
and all
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M)
such that
\exists\bar{v}\phi
is in the domain of
S
,

S(\exists\bar{v}\phi,\alpha)\equiv\exists\beta\sim_{\bar{v}}\alpha\ S(\phi,\beta);

and for all
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M)
such that
\forall\bar{v}\phi
is in the domain of
S
,

S(\forall\bar{v}\phi,\alpha)\equiv\forall\beta\sim_{\bar{v}}\alpha\ S(\phi,\beta).

We say that the quantifier correctness
fails
for
\phi
if one of the above equivalences does not hold for a pair of formulae
\phi,\exists\bar{v}\phi
or
\phi,\forall\bar{v}\phi
which are both in
\textnormal{dom}(S)
.

In other words, quantifier correctness means that
\phi
satisfies compositional clauses with respect to blocks of quantifiers. If
S
is a full satisfaction class, then the quantifier correctness fails for
\phi
iff it does not hold for
\phi
. In the case of partial satisfaction classes the distinction is nontrivial. For instance, suppose that
M\models\textnormal{PA}
is a nonstandard model and
S\subset M^{2}
contains only standard arithmetical formulae (on which all satisfaction classes agree). Then the quantifier correctness will not hold in general, since
S
does not contain e.g.
\exists v_{1}\ldots\exists v_{c}0=0
for nonstandardly long blocs of quantifiers. On the other hand, this is not yet a failure of the quantifier correctness for which we require that for some
\alpha
,
S(\neg\exists v_{1}\ldots\exists v_{c}0=0,\alpha)
holds.

3
Enayat–Visser Lemma

In the article
[
4
]
(see also
[
3
]
), a method for constructing satisfaction classes was introduced which proved to be extremely handy, as it allows us to guarantee that the constructed classes enjoy a number of additional properties.

However, these applications typically require slight modifications of the original proof in a way which prevents us from simply quoting the result. One attempt to present Enayat–Visser construction in a form of a more general statement has been made by James Schmerl in
[
16
]
. Unfortunately, his formulation, while very elegant, seems (at least to us) rather unsuitable for the typical applications of the discussed technique. In this section, we try to present the core of Enayat–Visser argument in a way that allows us to use it as a (simple) black box.

Definition 17
.

By an
Enayat–Visser class
(or EV class) we mean a class
\mathscr{C}
of pairs
(M,S)
, where
M
is a model of some theory
T
in a language extending
\mathscr{L}
and
S\subset M^{2}
is an
\mathscr{L}
-satisfaction class such that the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

For every
(M,S)\in\mathscr{C}
, there exists
(M^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathscr{C}
such that
M^{\prime}\succeq M
,
S^{\prime}\supseteq S
, and the the domain of
S^{\prime}
contains
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}}(M)
. (Unboundedness.)

2.

If
(M_{i},S_{i})\in\mathscr{C}
for
i<\omega
and for each
i
,
M_{i}\preceq M_{i+1},S_{i}\subset S_{i+1}
, then
(M^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathscr{C}
, where
M^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i<\omega}M_{i},S^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i<\omega}S_{i}
. (Closure.)

In the paper, we will only focus on the case where
\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}
is the arithmetical language. Note that in the definition we do not assume that
\mathscr{C}
is nonempty. However, it will be implied by the assumptions of the following theorem, which encapsulates the core of the Enayat–Visser argument.

Theorem 18

(Enayat–Visser Lemma)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an Enayat–Visser class, let
M\models T
, and suppose that there exists
S\subset M^{2}
such that
(M,S)\in\mathscr{C}
. Then there exists a model
M^{\prime}\succeq M
and a full
\mathscr{L}
-satisfaction class
S^{\prime}\supset S
such that
(M^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

Fix a model
M\models T
, and a satisfaction class
S
on
M
such that
(M,S)\in\mathscr{C}
. Using the second condition of the definition of Enayat–Visser classes, we inductively build a chain of models
M=M_{0}\preceq M_{1}\preceq M_{2}\preceq\ldots
of length
\omega
and satisfaction classes
S_{i}\subset M_{i}
such that for each
i<j
,
S_{i}\subseteq S_{j}
, the domain of
S_{i+1}
contains
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}}(S_{i})
, and
(M_{i},S_{i})\in\mathscr{C}
.

Let
M^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i<\omega}M_{i}
and let
S^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i<\omega}S_{i}
. By the third condition in the definition of Enayat–Visser class,
(M^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathscr{C}
, so it is enough to verify that
S^{\prime}
is a full satisfaction class. Since every
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}}(M^{\prime})
is in
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}}(M_{j})
for some
j
, this implies that
\phi
is in the domain of
S_{j+1}
and, consequently, in the domain of
S^{\prime}
.
∎

The essence of Enayat–Visser technique is that the unboundedness condition in the definition of EV is much easier to check than the existence of a full satisfaction class
S^{\prime}
, since the former can be expressed as a scheme which in turn allows us to use a compactness argument. We will use this method in Section
6
. Let us add that it would be nice to incorporate this "local" nature of the argument to the statement of Theorem
18
. Unfortunately, it seems that general enough statements which cover actual applications of the technique would be somewhat awkward to express.

4
Full satisfaction classes and definability

In this section, we will prove that in every countable, recursively saturated model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
, there exists a full satisfaction class
S
such that
(M,S)
is minimal. We will actually demonstrate a more general result.

Theorem 19
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
be a countable recursively saturated model and let
A\subset M
be an arbitrary set. There exists a full satisfaction class
S\subset M^{2}
such that
A
is definable in
(M,S)
without parametres.

Proof.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
be a countable recursively saturated model. Let for
a\in M
,

\eta_{a}=\forall x\underbrace{\Bigl(x=x\wedge x=x\wedge\ldots\wedge x=x\Bigr)}_{a\textnormal{ times}}

and let for
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M)
,
\eta_{a,\bar{v}}
be the following formula:

\exists\bar{v}\ \eta_{a}.

We will construct a satisfaction class such that:

•

If quantifier correctness fails for
\phi
, then
\phi=\eta_{a,\bar{v}}
for some
a\in A
and some
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M)
(where we also allow the trivial block of quantifiers).

•

If
a\in A
, then the quantifier correctness fails for
\eta_{a}
.

Let us fix an enumeration
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of the elements of
M
and a cofinal increasing sequence of elements of
M
,
(b_{n})_{n\in\omega}
.
We inductively construct regular satisfaction classes
S_{0}\subseteq S_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq M
such that:

•

The domain of
S_{n}
contains exactly the formulae of syntactic depth at most
b
for some
b\geq b_{n}
.
8
8

8

Recall that the notion of syntactic depth was introduced in Definition
1
.

•

If
a_{n}\in A
, then the quantifier correctness fails for the formula
\eta_{a_{n}}
.

•

If
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
is not of the form
\eta_{a_{i},\bar{v}}
for some
i\leq n,\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M)
, then the quantifier correctness holds for
\phi
.

•

The model
(M,S_{n})
is recursively saturated.

The inductive steps of the construction are handled via the following lemma:

Lemma 20

(Flexibility of quantifier correctness)
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
. Let
a,b\in S
and let
S\subset M
be a regular satisfaction class whose domain consists exactly of formulae of syntactic depth at most
b
. Then there exists an elementary extension
(M,S)\preceq(M^{\prime},S^{\prime}_{0})
and a full satisfaction class
S^{\prime}\supset S^{\prime}_{0}
such that the quantifier correctness fails for the formula
\eta_{a}
and if it fails for
\phi
according to
S^{\prime}
, then it fails according to
S_{0}^{\prime}
or
\phi=\eta_{a,\bar{v}}
for some
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M^{\prime})
.

The above Lemma can be proved by the Enayat–Visser methods, but the proof itself turns out to be somewhat complicated when we are trying to provide all the relevant details which would be rather disruptive for the whole argument. Therefore we postpone it until Section
6
. Let us now finish the proof under the assumption that the lemma holds. Suppose that we have constructed the set
S_{n}
. Applying Lemma
20
to the model
(M,S_{n})
and using its resplendence, we find a full satisfaction class
S\supseteq S_{n}
such that
(M,S)
is recursively saturated as a structure in the extended language, the quantifier correctness fails for formulae of the form
\eta_{a_{i}}
for
i\leq n+1
such that
a_{i}\in A
and holds for all formulae not of the form
\eta_{a_{i},\bar{v}}
. Finally, we let
S_{n+1}
be
S
restricted so that
\textnormal{dom}(S_{n+1})
contains exactly the formulae of syntactic depth at most
b\geq b_{n+1}
, where
b
is large enough that if the quantifier correctness fails for
\eta_{a_{i}}
,
i\leq n+1
, then this failure is already witnessed by the formulae of depth less than
b
. Since this restriction is definable in
S
, the obtained model
(M,S_{n+1})
is also recursively saturated and clearly satisfies all the other requirements of the inductive construction.

We set
S=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n}
. Since all
S_{n}
are compatible satisfaction classes, and the union of their domains is the whole model,
S
is a full satisfaction class as well. Finally, we claim that
A
is definable in
(M,S)
. Indeed, it is enough to notice that
A
is precisely the set of
x
such that the quantifier correctness fails for the formula
\eta_{x}
. ∎

Corollary 21
.

Let
M
be a countable recursively saturated model of
PA
. Then there exists a full satisfaction class
S\subset M

(M,S)

Proof.

Fix any bijection
f:\omega\to M
. This bijection can be coded by a subset of
M^{2}
. Fix a satisfaction class
S\subset M
such that
f

.

For any countable recursively saturated model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
, there exists a full satisfaction class
S\subset M
such that
(M,S)
has no proper elementary submodels and no nontrivial automorphisms.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the previous corollary.
∎

Corollary 23
.

In every countable recursively saturated model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
, there exist continuum many pairwise non-elementary-equivalent full satisfaction classes.

Proof.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
be countable and recursively saturated. By inspection of the proof of Theorem

.

Corollary 24

If

Let us make one final remark. One could be dissatisfied with the fact that the satisfaction class we have constructed is rather pathological and one could wonder whether the result would still hold if we restricted our attention to full satisfaction classes which satisfy the quantifier correctness property.

It seems that while we used some specific pathology, the way we did it is rather generic. For instance if we require that the constructed class satisfy the quantifier correctness, we could turn our attention to disjunctions of the form
\eta_{a}\vee\eta_{a}\vee\ldots\vee\eta_{a}
and ask for which
a
all such disjunctions are true.

If we additionally require that all sentences true in the sense of some partial arithmetical
\Sigma_{n}

c

\forall v\xi_{a,c}
is true, where the formulae

are defined inductively via the conditions:

\displaystyle\xi_{a,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v=v\wedge\eta_{a}

\displaystyle\xi_{a,b+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\forall x_{b}\exists y_{b}\ (x_{b}=v\wedge y_{b}=v\wedge\xi_{a,b}).

With just a slightly more complicated proof than the one presented above, one could show that the formulae
\xi_{a,c}
are also flexible in the sense that the set of those
a
such that
\xi_{a,c}
is true for all
c
can be an arbitrary set.

On the other hand, we always have to expect that full satisfaction classes display some pathological behaviour, since already such benign principles as "a finite disjunction is satisfied iff one of the disjuncts is," "truth is closed under propositional proofs" or "every first-order tautology is true" already allow us to draw nontrivial conclusions about the arithmetical theory of the underlying model.

5
Satisfaction classes and automorphisms

In the previous section, we have shown that in every countable recursively saturated model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
there exists a rigid satisfaction class, i.e. a satisfaction class
S\subset M
such that
(M,S)
admits no non-trivial automorphisms. A natural question arises whether the opposite result holds, i.e., whether given a countable recursively saturated model
M
, we can find a satisfaction class
S
on
M
such that
\textnormal{Aut}(M,S)=\textnormal{Aut}(M)
.

Clearly, if
f\in\textnormal{Aut}(M)
, then for any
a\in M
, the elements
a
and
f(a)
have the same arithmetical types. On the other hand, if
a,b\in M
have the same arithmetical types and
M
is countable and recursively saturated, then by Theorem
7
, there exists an automorphism
f\in\textnormal{Aut}(M)
such that
f(a)=b
. Therefore, it is enough to check whether there exists a satisfaction class
S
on
M
such that for any two pairs
(\phi,\alpha),(\psi,\beta)
with the same arithmetical types,
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
iff
(\phi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\in S
.
11
11

11

We are grateful for this observation to Roman Kossak who also suggested to study automorphisms and definability properties of satisfaction classes.

One could guess that such a satisfaction class can be easily constructed by an application of the Enayat–Visser techniques. It is rather surprising that this intuition is wrong.

Theorem 27
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and let
S\subset M^{2}
be a full regular satisfaction class. Then there exist two pairs
(\phi,\alpha),(\phi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\in M^{2}

S
.

The proof will use the following fact:

Proposition 28

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and let

(\phi,\emptyset)\in S,(\psi,\emptyset)\notin S.

to an equivalent sentence in that language, so we can indeed assume that for each

(\gamma(\underline{\phi}),\emptyset)\in S,(\gamma(\underline{\psi}),\emptyset)\notin S.

Since
\textnormal{tp}(\phi,\gamma)=\textnormal{tp}(\psi,\gamma)

\textnormal{tp}(\gamma(\underline{\phi}))=\textnormal{tp}(\gamma(\underline{\psi}))
, because substitution is a definable operation. Thus
(\gamma(\underline{\phi}),\emptyset),(\gamma(\underline{\psi}),\emptyset)
are the desired pairs.
∎

Definition 32

Let

Let

Proof.

Fix a model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
, elements
a,b\in M
, and a satisfaction class
T
on

whose domain consists exactly of formulae of depth
\leq b
. We will show that the class
\mathscr{C}
defined with respect to this model is an EV-class containing some pair

\mathscr{C}
is closed under unions of chains. It is enough to observe that if a failure of quantifier correctness is witnessed by a pair of formulae in a satisfaction class

containing

. On the other hand, if quantifier correctness fails in a union of a chain of satisfaction classes, then this failure has to be witnessed in one of the models.

We will check the unboundedness condition and explain how the argument can be modified to show the existence of an elementary extension
(M^{\prime},T^{\prime})\succeq(M,T)
and a satisfaction class
S
in that extension such that the pair
((M^{\prime},T^{\prime}),S)
belongs to
\mathscr{C}
. In what follows, we will identify pairs
((X,Y),Z)
with triples
(X,Y,Z)
.

Fix a tuple
(N,P,S)\in\mathscr{C}
or
(N,P,S)=(M,T,T)
(to find any

witnessing its nonemptiness). We will find
(N^{\prime},P^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathscr{C}
such that
(N,P)\preceq(N^{\prime},P^{\prime})
,
S^{\prime}\supseteq S
is a satisfaction class, and the domain of
S^{\prime}
contains
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(N)
. We will say that a formula
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(N)
is
unproblematic
if
\phi
is not in the domain of
P
and
\phi
is not of the form

for any
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(N)
. The structure
(N^{\prime},P^{\prime},S^{\prime})
will be obtained as a model of the theory
\Theta
with the following axioms:

•

The elementary diagram of
(N,P)
, where the predicate

is replaced with
P^{\prime}
.

•

\textnormal{Comp}(\phi),\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
(The compositionality scheme for
S^{\prime}
.)

•

\forall\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Val}(\phi),\beta\in\textnormal{Val}(\psi)\Big((\phi,\alpha)\simeq(\psi,\beta)\rightarrow S^{\prime}(\phi,\alpha)\equiv S^{\prime}(\psi,\beta)\Big).
(Regularity.)

•

S^{\prime}(\phi,\alpha)
for all
(\phi,\alpha)\in S.
(The compatibility scheme.)

•

\forall x,y\ P^{\prime}(x,y)\rightarrow S^{\prime}(x,y).

•

\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(Q\bar{v}\phi)\ \Big(S^{\prime}(Q\bar{v}\phi,\alpha)\equiv Q\beta\sim_{\bar{v}}\alpha\ S^{\prime}(\phi,\beta)\Big)
, where
Q\in\{\forall,\exists\},\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M),\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
and
\phi
is unproblematic. (The quantifier correctness scheme.)

In the initial step, when
(N,P,S)=(M,T,T)
, we additionally pick two sequences
\bar{w},\bar{w^{\prime}}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M)
such that
\bar{w}\lhd\bar{w^{\prime}}
(

is an initial segment of
w^{\prime}
) and the differences
|\bar{w}|-b
,
|\bar{w^{\prime}}|-|\bar{w}|
are nonstandard, where
|\bar{w}|
,
|\bar{w^{\prime}}|
are the lengths of the respective sequences. We add the following axioms to
\Theta

•

S^{\prime}(\eta_{a,\bar{w}},\emptyset).

•

\neg S^{\prime}(\eta_{a,\bar{w^{\prime}}},\emptyset)
.

This guarantees that the quantifier correctness fails for
\eta_{a}
. The requirement on the length of
\bar{w}
is to guarantee that the syntactic depth of
\eta_{a,\bar{w}}
is high enough that the formula is not in the domain of

.

If we take any model
(N^{\prime},P^{\prime},S^{\prime})\models\Theta
and restrict the predicate
S^{\prime}
to the formulae
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(N^{\prime})
whose syntactic templates

are in
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(N)
(i.e., they essentially come from the original model), we obtain a model in
\mathscr{C}
(compositionality and "non-failure" of quantifier correctness are easy to verify; the closure conditions on the domain follow automatically by elementarity and regularity). Therefore, it is enough to check that
\Theta
is consistent which we will verify by a compactness argument. Let
\Theta_{0}\subset\Theta
be a finite subtheory. We will find a subset

such that
(N,S^{\prime})
satisfies
\Theta_{0}
.

We introduce another technical notion which will be useful in this proof: if
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(N)
begins with a quantifier

\psi
such that
\phi=Q\bar{v}\psi
for some
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(M)
and one of the following holds:

•

\psi
has syntactic depth
b
.

•

There is no formula satisfying the first condition and
\psi
does not begin with a quantifier
Q
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}
be the (finite) set of formulae that appear under the satisfaction predicate
S^{\prime}
in the theory
\Theta_{0}
. Let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}
be an enumeration of formulae from
\Gamma_{0}
and their roots (notice that we do not
close

\Gamma_{0}
under taking roots; we apply the operation only once). Without loss of generality, we can assume that

are among
\phi_{i}
. (This will play any role only in the "initial step.") Consider the equivalence classes
[\phi_{i}]_{\sim}
under the syntactic similarity relation
\sim

by induction on the following relation

[\phi]\lhd[\psi]

•

\phi^{\prime}
is a direct subformula of
\psi^{\prime}

•

\phi^{\prime}

and

\psi^{\prime}=Q\bar{v}\phi^{\prime}

The second condition is somewhat lengthy, but the idea is simple: we are allowed to make jumps over nonstandard blocks of quantifiers, but the relation

We construct

as a union of a sequence of sets

\textnormal{rk}([\phi])=\sup\{\textnormal{rk}([\phi_{i}])\ \mid\ i\leq n\wedge[\phi_{i}]\lhd[\phi]\}.

When showing the unboundedness of

S_{0}=S.

In this step, we are implicitly dealing with both the formulae from the domain of

and the formulae

whose class

is
\lhd

When dealing with
(N,P,S)=(M,T,T)

as the set of

such that one of the following conditions holds:

•

(\phi,\alpha)\in T
.

•

There exists a sequence
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}
of a standard length

) such that

The second condition guarantees that the sentence

which we picked when we were defining the theory

We extend

and the

•

For some

•

For some

•

For some

\phi=\psi\wedge\eta

and
(\eta,\alpha)\in S_{j}
.

•

For some

\phi=\exists v\psi

(\psi,\beta)\in S_{j}

•

For some

\phi=\forall v\psi

(\psi,\beta)\in S_{j}
.

•

There exists
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(N)

There exists
\bar{v}\in\textnormal{VarSeq}(N)

for some

\textnormal{rk}([\psi])=j

(\psi,\beta)\in S_{j}
.

Since there are only finitely many classes

, there exists

. We define

as this last set in our construction. We now have to check that the constructed predicate satisfies the finite subtheory

Obviously,

sastisfies the elementary diagram of

,

satisfies all the instances of the compatibility scheme and it extends

Let us now check that if we consider the case where
(N,P,S)=(M,T,T)
, then indeed
\eta_{a,\bar{w}}
is rendered true and
\eta_{a,\bar{w^{\prime}}}
is false. For either of the formulae, this is clear if its class is
\lhd
-minimal. If
\textnormal{rk}[\eta_{a,\bar{w}}]=j>0
, then since
\eta_{a,\bar{w}}
is problematic, this means that the classes
[\eta_{a,\bar{u_{1}}}],\ldots[\eta_{a,\bar{u_{j}}}]
are among
[\phi_{i}]
, where
\eta_{a,\bar{u_{k}}}
is the formula obtained from
\eta_{a,\bar{w}}
by removing the outermost
k

[\eta_{a,\bar{u_{j}}}]
is a minimal class. By construction, the formula
\eta_{a,\bar{u_{j}}}
is satisfied under all assignments. Then using compositional conditions, we can check by induction that all the other formulae
\eta_{a,\bar{u_{j}}}
are satisfied under all assignments. The proof for
\eta_{a,\bar{w^{\prime}}}
is analogous, but now we consider the formulae
\eta_{a,\bar{u^{\prime}_{k}}}
obtained by removing the outermost
k
quantifiers from
\eta_{a,\bar{w^{\prime}}}
. By construction, the minimal formula
\eta{a,\bar{u^{\prime}_{j}}}
is not satisfied under any assignment and we check that the same holds for all

by induction using the compositional conditions.

The set
S^{\prime}
satisfies all the instances of the compositionality scheme from

by construction and the compositionality of

. Note that there is no conflict between defining

to simply contain

and further extending it by compositional conditions, since compositional clauses determine uniquely the behaviour of

on a given formula given the behaviour on its subformulae.

The quantifier correctness scheme is handled similarly. We have to check that there is no conflict between the definition of
S_{0}
and how it was extended in the further steps. If

is in the domain of
S
and
\phi
is unproblematic, then the root of

is a subformula of
\phi
(possibly not proper) and by assumption it is in the domain of
S
. Therefore, we can check by induction on rank that
S^{\prime}
is defined both on
\phi
and

by applying the quantifier correctness clause to a formula in the domain of
S
. (The assumption that

is defined exactly on formulae of depth

guarantees that the definition of the root makes sense).

Regularity holds for
S_{0}
by the assumption on

in the proof that
\mathscr{C}
is unbounded. In the case where
(N,P)=(M,T)
, we use the assumption that

is regular and the fact that the similarity classes of formulae
\eta_{a,\bar{v}}
are singletons (by definition of these specific formulae). Then we show by induction that the regularity holds for all other sets

. First, observe that for every class
[\phi]
or rank

, the predicate
S_{j+1}
is defined using the same condition for every formula

, which follows by definition of syntactic similarity (all similar formulae have the same syntactic tree) and the fact that either all formulae in

are problematic or none is (which, in turn, depends on the definition of
\eta_{a}
and the assumption that the domain of

includes exactly the formulae of depth

preserves regularity. This concludes the proof.

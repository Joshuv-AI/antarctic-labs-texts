Quantum speed of evolution in a Markovian bosonic environment
Paulina Marian, Tudor A. Marian

Abstract
We present explicit evaluations of quantum speed limit times pertinent to the Markovian dynamics of an open continuous-variable system. Specifically, we consider the standard setting of a cavity mode of the quantum radiation field weakly coupled to a thermal bosonic reservoir. The evolution of the field state is ruled by the quantum optical master equation, which is known to have an exact analytic solution. Starting from a pure input state, we employ two indicators of how different the initial and evolved states are, namely, the fidelity of evolution and the Hilbert-Schmidt distance of evolution. The former was introduced by del Campo et al. [Phys. Rev. Lett. 110 , 050403 (2013)], who derived a time-independent speed limit for the evolution of a Markovian open system. We evaluate it for this field-reservoir setting, with an arbitrary input pure state of the field mode. The resultant formula is then specialized to the coherent and Fock states. On the other hand, we exploit an alternative approach that employs both indicators of evolution mentioned above. Their rates of change have the same upper bound, and consequently provide a unique time-dependent quantum speed limit. It turns out that the associate quantum speed limit time built with the Hilbert-Schmidt metric is tighter than the fidelity-based one. As apposite applications, we investigate the damping of the coherent and Fock states by using the characteristic functions of the corresponding evolved states. General expressions of both the fidelity and the Hilbert-Schmidt distance of evolution are obtained and analyzed for these two classes of input states. In the case of a coherent state, we derive accurate formulas for their common speed limit and the pair of associate limit times. We also find exact expressions of the same quantities in the limiting case of thermalization of the vacuum state, as well as for dissipation of one- and two-photon states.

II
Quantum speed limits for Markovian open systems

The Markovian dynamics of an open quantum system with the unperturbed Hamiltonian

\hat{H}
is described by a Lindblad-type master equation for its density operator

\hat{\rho}(t)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}(t)}{\partial t}={\cal L}\hat{\rho}(t):=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H},\hat{\rho}(t)]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k}\left[\hat{L}_{k}\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{L}_{k}^{{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{L}_{k}^{{\dagger}}\hat{L}_{k},\hat{\rho}(t)\}\right].

(9)

In Eq. (

displaying the semigroup property

being the identity map. It can be shown that any map

describing the state change of the open system over time

completely positive, and trace-preserving quantum operation

A bound to the speed of evolution from the initial state

to an evolved one

of an accepted measure of distinguishability between the states
{\hat{\rho}}(0)
and

by the unperturbed Hamiltonian

and the Lindblad operators in Eq. (

as well as by the input state

) or (

III.1
Quantum optical master equation

Here we choose to employ the master equation for a damped harmonic oscillator

;
BP

as a simplified version of quantum optical master equation

of the radiation field with the angular frequency
\omega
and the amplitude operators

\hat{a}
and
{{\hat{a}}^{{\dagger}}}
is sustained by a cavity which stands for the environment.
The effective interaction between the mode and the cavity consists in photon scattering
by atoms into and out of the mode. These radiative processes produce a damping
of the cavity mode characterized by the field-reservoir coupling constant
\gamma

The mean number of photons
\bar{n}_{\rm R}
in any mode of the reservoir having
the same frequency
\omega
is given by the Bose-Einstein distribution function:

\bar{n}_{\rm R}=\left[\exp{\left(\frac{\hbar\omega}{k_{B}T}\right)}-1\right]^{-1}.

(38)

Recall also the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hbar\omega\,{\hat{a}}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}

for the free evolution of the mode. Under the weak-coupling condition
\gamma\ll\omega

one can describe the damping of the cavity mode by the following quantum optical master
equationin the Schrödinger picture

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}(t)}{\partial t}=-i\omega[\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a},\,\hat{\rho}(t)]

\displaystyle+\gamma\bar{n}_{R}\left\{\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{a}-\frac{1}{2}[\hat{a}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{\rho}(t)+\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{a}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}]\right\}

\displaystyle+\gamma(\bar{n}_{R}+1)\left\{\hat{a}\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}[\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}\hat{\rho}(t)+\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}]\right\}.

(39)

The master equation (
39
) can be derived starting from the von Neumann equation
for the density operator of the field-reservoir system in the interaction picture, by applying
three distinct approximations, namely, the Born, Markov, and rotating wave approximations

.
The Markov approximation is valid provided that a strong inequality between the relaxation times
of the field mode,
{\tau}_{F}
, and the reservoir,
{\tau}_{R}
, is fulfilled:
{\tau}_{F}\gg{\tau}_{R}
.
The former has the order
{\tau}_{F}\approx{\gamma}^{-1}
, where
\gamma
is essentially
a typical value for the rates of the electric dipole transitions in atoms, i. e.,
10^{7}\,{\rm s}^{-1}

to
10^{9}\,{\rm s}^{-1}
. On the other hand, the latter is of same order as the vacuum correlation time
of the bosonic reservoir, which is given by the inverse of a typical transition frequency
in the optical domain:
{\tau}_{R}\approx{\omega}^{-1}\approx 10^{-15}\,{\rm s}
.
To sum up, the condition of validity for the Markov approximation,
{\tau}_{F}\gg{\tau}_{R}
,
coincides with that for the Born approximation, namely, the weak-coupling condition

\gamma\ll\omega
. This is largely fulfilled in the optical domain, where

\gamma/\omega\approx 10^{-6}
. Finally, the rotating wave approximation is justified
as well, since the terms containing factors
\exp[i({\omega}^{\prime}-\omega)t]
with

{\omega}^{\prime}\neq\omega
are rapidly oscillating on the time scale

{\tau}_{F}\approx{\gamma}^{-1}
of the mode damping and can therefore be neglected.

The quantum optical master equation (
39

it preserves the positivity of the density operator. As a consequence, the Robertson-Schrödinger
uncertainty relation holds at any time

39

9
),
we identify two unbounded Lindblad operators:

\hat{L}_{1}=\sqrt{\gamma\bar{n}_{R}}\;\hat{a}^{{\dagger}},\;\;\;\hat{L}_{2}=\sqrt{\gamma(\bar{n}_{R}+1)}\;\hat{a}.

the Gaussian character as well as the classicality of the initial state
PT93b
.
An input coherent state
{\hat{\rho}}(0)=|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|
is Gaussian,
since its NCF is the exponential
\chi^{(N)}(\lambda,{\lambda}^{\ast},0)=\exp{\left({\alpha}^{\ast}\lambda-\alpha{\lambda}^{\ast}\right)},

and lies at the limit of classicality, since its Glauber-Sudarshan
P
representation
is a Dirac
\delta
distribution:
P(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},0)=\delta^{(2)}(\beta-\alpha)
.

The transient NCF (
) reads:

\displaystyle\chi^{(N)}(\lambda,{\lambda}^{\ast},t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)|\lambda|^{2}+{\alpha}^{\ast}(t)\lambda-\alpha(t){\lambda}^{\ast}\right]

(69)

where

\alpha(t):=\alpha\exp{\left[-\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+i\omega\right)t\right]},

(70)

in accordance with Eq. (
63
). It describes an evolving displaced thermal state (DTS):

{\hat{\rho}}_{\,\rm DT}(t)=\hat{D}[\alpha(t),{\alpha}^{\ast}(t)]\,{\hat{\rho}}_{\rm T}[{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)]\,\hat{D}^{{\dagger}}[\alpha(t),{\alpha}^{\ast}(t)].

(71)

The one-mode DTSs are special Gaussian states, whose statistical properties
are systematically studied in Ref.
PT93a
. The expected extremity limits
of the evolved state (
71
),

\lim_{t\to 0}\left[{\hat{\rho}}_{\,\rm DT}(t)\right]=|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\left[{\hat{\rho}}_{\,\rm DT}(t)\right]={\hat{\rho}}_{\rm T}({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}),

are readily checked.

Being the Fourier transform of the Gaussian function

{\pi}^{-1}\chi^{(N)}(\lambda,{\lambda}^{\ast},t)

CG
, the Glauber-Sudarshan
quasiprobability distribution is also a regular Gaussian function for
t>0
:

P(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)=\frac{1}{\pi{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\exp\left[-\frac{|\beta-\alpha(t)|^{2}}{{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\right],\quad(t>0),

with the appropriate limit
\delta^{(2)}(\beta-\alpha)
at
t=0
.

We mention two extreme situations that are insightful:

1.

Dissipation of a coherent state

(\alpha\neq 0,\,{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}=0)
.

The evolving state (
71
) becomes the coherent state
|\alpha(t)\rangle\langle\alpha(t)|

with the attenuated oscillating amplitude (
70
).

2.

Thermalization of the vacuum state

(\alpha=0,\,{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}>0)
.

If
{\hat{\rho}}(0)=|0\rangle\langle 0|
, then the transient state (
71
) reduces
to the evolving TS
\,{\hat{\rho}}_{\rm T}[{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)].

V.2
Main ingredients

At this point, we find it convenient to introduce the time-decreasing variable

\eta:=\exp(-\gamma t),\qquad\eta\in(0,1].

(72)

In order to evaluate the integrals (
65
)-(
67
) for the evolving DTS (
71
),
we make use of Eqs. (
61
) and (
69
), finding the exact explicit formulas:

\displaystyle{\cal F}(t)=\frac{1}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\exp\left[-|\alpha|^{2}\,\frac{1-2\sqrt{\eta}\cos(\omega t)+\eta}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\right],

(73)

{\cal P}(t)=\frac{1}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle\left[{\tilde{v}}(t)\right]^{2}=2\left[{\omega}^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma\right)^{2}\right]|\alpha|^{2}\frac{\eta}{\left[1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)\right]^{2}}

\displaystyle+2\left(\gamma{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\right)^{2}\frac{{\eta}^{2}}{\left[1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)\right]^{3}}\,.

(75)

An analysis of the above functions will suggest us how to exploit them in an efficient way.

V.2.1
Fidelity of evolution

The fidelity of evolution (
73
) is equal, up to a factor
\pi
, to the Husimi
Q
function
of the transient DTS (
71
):

{\cal F}(t)=\langle\alpha|{\hat{\rho}}_{\,\rm DT}(t)|\alpha\rangle=:\pi\,Q_{\,\rm DT}(\alpha,{\alpha}^{\ast},t).

(76)

Its value at thermal equilibrium,

\lim_{t\to\infty}[{\cal F}(t)]=\langle\alpha|{\hat{\rho}}_{\rm T}({\bar{n}}_{\rm R})|\alpha\rangle=:\pi Q_{\rm T}({\bar{n}}_{\rm R};\alpha,{\alpha}^{\ast}),

reads:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left[{\cal F}(t)\right]=\frac{1}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\right).

(77)

If
\alpha\neq 0
, the fidelity of damping (
73
) suffers from the drawback that,
under the weak-coupling condition
\gamma\ll\omega
, and at the scale
of the field relaxation time
{\tau}_{F}\approx{\gamma}^{-1},
it oscillates very rapidly
due to the presence of the function
\cos(\omega t)
at the exponent. In the present case,
this is a major inconvenience concerning the figures of merit occurring in the QSLTs
(
21
), (
29
), and (
34
). In principle, one can remedy it by replacing
the oscillating function
\cos(\omega t)
either with one of its convenient values
or with a significant average connected to it. In Appendix A, we point out the appropriate
approach of this kind. The conclusion is that the fidelity of evolution in the interaction picture
is the only suitable one. This means that instead of the oscillating function (
73
),
we will use exclusively the smooth fidelity

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{(1)}(t)=\frac{1}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\exp\left[-|\alpha|^{2}\,\frac{\left(1-\sqrt{\eta}\right)^{2}}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\right].

(78)

Let us mention the short-time approximation of the function
{\cal F}^{(1)}(t)
, Eq. (
78
):

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{(1)}(t)=1-{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}(\gamma t)+\frac{1}{2}\left[{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}(1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})-\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}\right](\gamma t)^{2}

\displaystyle+{\rm O}\left[(\gamma t)^{3}\right],\qquad(\gamma t\ll 1),

(79)

as well as its asymptotic behavior:

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{(1)}(t)=\frac{1}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\right)\left[1+2\,\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\sqrt{\eta}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+{\rm O}(\eta)\right],\qquad(\gamma t\gg 1).

(80)

From Eq. (
79
) one gets the rate of dechorence of the field mode:

\,{\Gamma}_{\rm d}^{(1)}:=-\dot{\cal F}^{(1)}(0)={\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\gamma
; its inverse
is the decoherence time
{\tau}_{\rm d}^{(1)}=1/({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\gamma).

V.2.2
Purity

The purity (
74
) of the transient DTS (
71
) does not depend on the coherent
amplitude
\alpha
. It is a strictly monotonic and strictly convex function of time,
which decreases from the initial pure-state value
{\cal P}(0)=1
towards the steady-state limit

{\cal P}(\infty)=(1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})^{-1.}

We write down an expansion of the purity (
74
) that is valid at short times:

\displaystyle{\cal P}(t)=1-2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}(\gamma t)+\frac{1}{2}\left[2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}(1+4{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})\right](\gamma t)^{2}+{\rm O}\left[(\gamma t)^{3}\right],

\displaystyle(\gamma t\ll 1),

(81)

and the asymptotic formula:

\displaystyle{\cal P}(t)=\frac{1}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\left[1+\frac{2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\,\eta+{\rm O}\left({\eta}^{2}\right)\right],

\displaystyle(\gamma t\gg 1).

(82)

According to Eq. (
81
), the rate of mixing of the initially coherent state is

\,{\Gamma}_{\rm m}:=-\dot{\cal P}(0)=2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\gamma
; its inverse defines
a conventional mixing time:
{\tau}_{\rm m}=1/(2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\gamma).

On the one hand, from Eqs. (
79
) and (
81
), we get the short-time expression
of the HS evolution distance (
161
):

{\cal G}^{(1)}(t)=\dot{\cal G}^{(1)}(0)\,t+{\rm O}\left[(\gamma t)^{2}\right],\qquad(\gamma t\ll 1),

(83)

with the slope at the origin

\dot{\cal G}^{(1)}(0)=\sqrt{2}\gamma\left({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\alpha|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(84)

On the other hand, Eqs. (
80
) and (
82
) provide the steady-state sign rule:

\displaystyle\text{sgn}\left\{\lim_{t\to\infty}\left[{\cal F}^{(1)}(t)-{\cal P}(t)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\text{sgn}\left[(1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})\ln\left(1+\frac{{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}{1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\right)-|\alpha|^{2}\right].

V.2.3
Upper bound for the speed of evolution

In the sequel, we employ two approximations of the exact formula (
75
)
obtained by keeping only its most important term:

\displaystyle{\tilde{v}}(t)=\sqrt{2}\left[{\omega}^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{|\alpha|\,\sqrt{\eta}}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\,,

\displaystyle(|\alpha|\omega\gg{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\gamma);

(85)

{\tilde{v}}(t)=\sqrt{2}\frac{{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\gamma\eta}{\left[1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}},\qquad(|\alpha|\omega\ll{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\gamma).

(86)

The condition for the validity of Eq. (
85
) is largely fulfilled. For instance,
when choosing
|\alpha|=10^{-3}
and at room temperature, it holds for laser frequencies
ranging from UV to IR. Therefore, Eq. (
86
) may only be used for a low-intensity laser
whose frequency is in the far IR. Note that Eq. (
85
) becomes exact for a dissipating
coherent field mode
(\alpha\neq 0,\,{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}=0),\,
while Eq. (
86
)
is rigorously exact when describing the thermalization of the vacuum

(\alpha=0,\,{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}>0).

VI
Thermalization of a Fock state

VI.1
Transient state

The CF (
61
) of an initial number state
|M\rangle\langle M|
is

\displaystyle{\chi}_{M}(\lambda,{\lambda}^{\ast},0)):=\langle M|{\hat{D}}(\lambda,{\lambda}^{\ast})|M\rangle

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}|\lambda|^{2}\right)L_{M}\left(|\lambda|^{2}\right),

(109)

where
L_{M}(x)
stands for the Laguerre polynomial of degree
M
, Eq. (
164
).
According to Eqs. (
), (
56
), and (
61
), the CF and the NCF
of the thermalized number state
{\hat{\rho}}_{M}(t)
are given by the formula::

\displaystyle\chi_{M}(\lambda,{\lambda}^{\ast},t)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}|\lambda|^{2}\right)\chi_{M}^{(N)}(\lambda,{\lambda}^{\ast},t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\left[\frac{1}{2}+\bar{n}_{\rm T}(t)\right]|\lambda|^{2}\right)L_{M}\left(\eta\,|\lambda|^{2}\right).

(110)

Therefore, the transient state
{\hat{\rho}}_{M}(t)
describes the linear superposition
of an attenuated mode with a definite initial number of photons
M

and a thermal mode whose mean photon number
\bar{n}_{\rm T}(t)
, Eq. (
58
),
is fed by the reservoir..

The Fourier transforms of the CF and the NCF in Eq. (
110
) are respectively
the Wigner function
W_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)
and, up to a factor
\pi,

the Glauber-Sudarshan
P
function
\pi P_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)

CG
.
In Ref.
PIT13
, these functions are evaluated and found to have a similar structure:

\displaystyle W_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)=\frac{2}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\exp\left[-\frac{2|\beta|^{2}}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}-2(1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})\eta}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\right]^{M}

\displaystyle\times L_{M}\left(-\frac{4\eta\,|\beta|^{2}}{\left[1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)\right]\left[1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}-2(1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})\eta\,\right]}\right);

(111)

\displaystyle P_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\exp\left[-\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}-(1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})\eta}{{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)}\right]^{M}

\displaystyle\times L_{M}\left(-\frac{\eta\,|\beta|^{2})}{{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)\left[{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}-(1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})\eta\,\right]}\right).

(112)

In what follows, we leave out the special case
M=0
,

since thermalization of the vacuum
is already treated in the preceding section. For
M>0,
the regular functions (
111
)
and (
112
) are positive on the whole phase space if the arguments of their Laguerre
polynomials are negative:

t\geqq t_{\rm w}:=\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\right)\Longrightarrow W_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)>0;

(113)

t\geqq t_{\rm c}:=\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}}\right)\Longrightarrow P_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)>0.

(114)

As shown in Ref.
PIT13
, the damping of an excited number state involves
three successive stages, each of them with specific features of the evolved state:

•

\;t\in[\,0,t_{\rm w}).

Strong non-classicality:
Both functions

W_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)
and
P_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)
have some negative values;

•

\;t\in[\,t_{\rm w},t_{\rm c}).

Weak non-classicality:

W_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)>0
, while
P_{M}(\beta,{\beta}^{\ast},t)

is negative somewhere;

•

\;t\geqq t_{\rm c}.

Classicality:

VI.2.1
Fidelity of evolution

The fidelity of evolution (
65
) is evaluated making use of the integral (

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{M}(t)=\frac{{\eta}^{M}\,[u(t)]^{-M}}{[1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm T}(t)]^{2M+1}}\,{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(-M,-M;\,1;\,u(t)\right).

(115)

In Eq. (
115
), the Gauss hypergeometric function
{{}_{2}F_{1}}
, Eq. (
171
),
is a monic polynomial of degree
M
with the positive variable

u(t):=\frac{1}{{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}(1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})}\,\frac{\eta}{(1-\eta)^{2}},\qquad({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}>0).

(116)

This variable is a strictly decreasing and strictly convex function of time: it decreases
from
u(0)=\infty
to
u(\infty)=0
. The above-mentioned property of the hypergeometric
polynomial
{{}_{2}F_{1}}
in Eq. (
115

\lim_{u\to\infty}\left[u^{-M}{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(-M,-M;\,1;\,u\right)\right]=1.

(117)

Therefore, in the limit case
{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}=0
, corresponding to the dissipation
of the mode, Eq. (
115
) simplifies to:

{\cal F}_{M}(t)={\eta}^{M},\qquad({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}=0).

(118)

The time derivative of the fidelity (
115
) has a rather complicated expression:

\displaystyle\dot{\cal F}_{M}(t)=-\gamma{\cal F}_{M}(t)\left\{M+(2M+1)\frac{{\bar{n}}_{R}\,\eta}{1+{\bar{n}}_{T}(t)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left[-M+M^{2}u(t)\,\frac{{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(-M+1,-M+1;\,2;\,u(t)\right)}{{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(-M,-M;\,1;\,u(t)\right)}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\frac{1+\eta}{1-\eta}\right\}.

(119)

For that very reason, it is hard to answer the general question whether the function of time
(
115
) is monotonic or not, whatever values of its parameters
M
and
{\bar{n}}_{R}

are chosen. Nevertheless, in the simplest case
M=1
, a thorough analysis of damping
is carried out in Appendix C.

The fidelity of evolution (
115
) has the short-time expansion:

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{M}(t)=1-\left[M+(2M+1){\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\right](\gamma t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[M^{2}+\left(6M^{2}+4M+1\right){\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2\left(3M^{2}+3M+1\right){\bar{n}}_{\rm R}^{2}\right](\gamma t)^{2}

\displaystyle+{\rm O}\left[(\gamma t)^{3}\right],\qquad(\gamma t\ll 1),

(120)

Accordingly, the modulus of the slope of its graph at
t=0
, i. e., the rate of decoherence
of the initial Fock state is
{\Gamma}_{\rm d}(M,{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}):=-\dot{\cal F}_{M}(0)=\gamma\left[M+(2M+1){\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\right]
.

We also write the asymptotic formula:

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{M}(t)=\frac{1}{1+{\bar{n}}_{R}}\left(\frac{{\bar{n}}_{R}}{1+{\bar{n}}_{R}}\right)^{M}\left[1+\frac{(M-{\bar{n}}_{R})^{2}}{{\bar{n}}_{R}(1+{\bar{n}}_{R})}\,\eta\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2\left[{\bar{n}}_{R}(1+{\bar{n}}_{R})\right]^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}[M(M-1)]^{2}+2{\bar{n}}_{R}\left\{M{\bar{n}}_{R}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.+(M-{\bar{n}}_{R})\left[-2M(M-1)+{\bar{n}}_{R}(3M-{\bar{n}}_{R})\right]\right\}\right){\eta}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+{\rm O}\left({\eta}^{3}\right)\right],\qquad({\bar{n}}_{R}>0,\;\;\gamma t\gg 1).

(121)

From Eq. (
121
) one learns that the steady-state limit

\lim_{t\to\infty}[{\cal F}_{M}(t)]=\frac{1}{1+{\bar{n}}_{R}}\left(\frac{{\bar{n}}_{R}}{1+{\bar{n}}_{R}}\right)^{M}

(122)

is always reached from above. In fact, this limit is an input-output fidelity,
i. e., the fidelity between the initial Fock state

|M\rangle\langle M|
and the TS
{\hat{\rho}}_{\rm T}({\bar{n}}_{\rm R})
,
which is eventually imposed by the bosonic reservoir to the field mode at equilibrium:

\lim_{t\to\infty}[{\cal F}_{M}(t)]=\langle M|{\hat{\rho}}_{\rm T}({\bar{n}}_{\rm R})|M\rangle.

At the classicality threshold
t_{\rm c}
, Eq. (
114

116

is equal to one,

u(t)=1\;\iff\;t=t_{\rm c},\qquad({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}>0).

Consequently, the fidelity (
115

119

to a single monomial owing to Gauss’s summation formula (

{\cal F}_{M}(t_{\rm c})=\binom{2M}{M}\,\frac{({\bar{n}}_{\rm R})^{M}(1+{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})^{M+1}}{\left(1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}\right)^{2M+1}},\;\;({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}>0);

(123)

{\dot{\cal F}}_{M}(t_{\rm c})=-\gamma\frac{M+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R}^{2}}{2(1+2{\bar{n}}_{\rm R})}{\cal F}_{M}(t_{\rm c})<0,\;\;({\bar{n}}_{\rm R}>0).

Both fidelities (

VII
Summary and conclusions

relation by Mandelstam and Tamm in 1945, many efforts were orientated towards
the evaluation of upper bounds on the speed of evolution for various types

as well as an extension to the evolution of mixed states
GLM

Appendix B
An infinite integral involving Laguerre polynomials

Our aim here is to evaluate the integral

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(s,\sigma,{\sigma}^{\prime}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\exp(-st)L_{M}(\sigma t)L_{M}({\sigma}^{\prime}t),

\displaystyle(s\geqq\sigma>0,\;\;s\geqq{\sigma}^{\prime}>0).

(163)

Throughout this paper we denote the Laguerre polynomial of degree
M
by
L_{M}(x)
.
To write it explicitly, it is convenient to use its expression as a confluent hypergeometric
function

HTF2a

\displaystyle L_{M}(z)={{}_{1}F_{1}}(-M;1;z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-M)_{m}}{(1)_{m}\,m!}\,z^{m},

(164)

with the Pochhammer symbol defined as follows:

\displaystyle(a)_{0}:=1,(a)_{m}:=\frac{\Gamma(a+m)}{\Gamma(a)}=a(a+1)...(a+m-1),

\displaystyle(m=1,2,3,...).

We exploit a particular case of the Hille-Hardy summation formula

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}L_{m}(x)L_{m}(y)z^{m}

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-1}\exp\left[-\frac{z}{1-z}(x+y)\right]I_{0}\left[2\frac{(xyz)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-z}\right],

\displaystyle(|z|<1),

is the modified Bessel function of the first kind and order zero, Eq. (
160

The function on the r. h. s. of Eq. (
165

of the Laguerre polynomials (
164

One gets the Laplace transform of the function

by integrating its Maclaurin series

We choose

\displaystyle a=s+\frac{z}{1-z}({\sigma}+{\sigma}^{\prime})\text{\;\; and \;\; }b=2\frac{z^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-z}({\sigma}{\sigma}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}

into Eq. (

provided that the condition

is fulfilled. This is equivalent

\displaystyle p(z):=Az^{2}-2Bz+C:\qquad A=(s-{\sigma}-{\sigma}^{\prime})^{2}\geqq 0,

\displaystyle B=(s-\sigma)(s-{\sigma}^{\prime})+{\sigma}{\sigma}^{\prime}>0,\qquad C=s^{2}>0.

(169)

One has the following alternative:

a)

Then,

Owing to its non-negative discriminant,

the quadratic trinomial (

on which

and therefore Eq. (

Taking into account the notations (

)

\displaystyle\left[(1-z)a\right]^{-1}\left(1-\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{s-(\sigma+{\sigma}^{\prime})}{s}\right]^{m}z^{m}

\displaystyle\times{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(-m,m+1;1;-\frac{\sigma{\sigma}^{\prime}}{s[s-(\sigma+{\sigma}^{\prime})]}\right).

(170)

Recall that the Gauss hypergeometric function

is the sum of a hypergeometric

\displaystyle{{}_{2}F_{1}}(a,b;\,c;\,z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{m}(b)_{m}}{(c)_{m}\,m!}\,z^{m},

\displaystyle(c\neq 0,-1,-2,-3,...,\qquad|z|<1).

(171)

Substitution of Eq. (

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(s,\sigma,{\sigma}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{s}\left[\frac{s-(\sigma+{\sigma}^{\prime})}{s}\right]^{M}

\displaystyle\times{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(-M,M+1;\,1;\,-\frac{\sigma{\sigma}^{\prime}}{s[s-(\sigma+{\sigma}^{\prime})]}\right),

(172)

where the Gauss hypergeometric function is a polynomial of degree

\displaystyle{{}_{2}F_{1}}(a,b;\,c;\,z)=(1-z)^{-a}\,{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(a,c-b;\,c;\,\frac{z}{z-1}\right),

\displaystyle\times{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(-M,-M;\,1;\,\frac{\sigma{\sigma}^{\prime}}{(s-\sigma)(s-{\sigma}^{\prime})}\right),

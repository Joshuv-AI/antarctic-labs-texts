The minimal model program for arithmetic surfaces enriched by a Brauer class
Daniel Chan, Colin Ingalls

Abstract
. We examine the noncommutative minimal model program for orders on arithmetic surfaces, or equivalently, arithmetic surfaces enriched by a Brauer class β \beta . When β \beta has prime index p > 5 p>5 , we show the classical theory extends with analogues of existence of terminal resolutions, Castelnuovo contraction and Zariski factorisation. We also classify β \beta -terminal surfaces and Castelnuovo contractions, and discover new unexpected behaviour.



1.
Introduction

Mori’s minimal model program (MMP) is a major organising paradigm, initially introduced to study higher dimensional varieties over a field. Noncommutative versions of this program were initiated in
[
CI05
]
for orders on surfaces over a field (henceforth dubbed geometric surfaces) and
[
CCdV+17
]
in higher dimensions. Much of classical Italian surface theory could be recovered, from resolution of singularities to the birational classification.

In this paper, we study the MMP for orders on arithmetic surfaces
X
. This noncommutative theory can also be viewed purely algebro-geometrically, as algebraic geometry enriched by a Brauer class
\beta\in\text{Br}\,K(X)
and we restrict to this viewpoint here.
For us, (perhaps departing from convention), an
arithmetic surface
will mean a normal separated integral two-dimensional excellent scheme
X
which is quasi-projective over a noetherian affine scheme and has finite residue fields. Reassuringly, we show (in the case of prime index Brauer class), the existence of terminal resolutions and analogues of Castelnuovo contraction and Zariski factorisation. We also classify terminal singularities and Castelnuovo contractions where surprising new phenomena show up. Whereas for both the commutative theory of arithmetic surfaces and for orders over geometric surfaces, these are fairly close to the classical Italian case, now, we find that if
\beta\neq 0
, regularity of
X
is neither a sufficient nor necessary condition for being terminal, and Castelnuovo contractions may or may not correspond to blowing up closed points even when
X
is regular.

The basic idea of the noncommutative MMP can be described as follows. Assume, as we always will, that the order of the Brauer class
\beta
is prime to all the residue characteristics of
X
. We can associate a log surface
(X,\Delta=\Delta_{X,\beta})
to this data as follows. Let
C\subset X
be an irreducible curve. There is a residue map we call the
ramification map

a_{C}\colon\textup{Br}\,K(X)\to H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. The co-efficient of
C
in
\Delta
is given by the standard co-efficient
1-\frac{1}{e_{C}}
where the
ramification index

e_{C}
is the order of
a_{C}(\beta)
. The motivation for this comes from noncommutative algebra. The Brauer class
\beta
gives a central simple
K
-algebra
A
(up to Morita equivalence), and to any maximal order in
A
, there is a natural analogue of the canonical line bundle which is related to
K_{X}
by
\Delta_{X,\beta}
in a manner reminiscent of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula. When
X
is regular with trivial Brauer group e.g. in the Hensel local case, then
\beta
is determined by its ramification data. It is now natural to define the canonical divisor
K_{X,\beta}=K_{X,\Delta}
. One important distinction between the study of the
Brauer log pair

(X,\beta)
and associated log geometry, is that the ramification datum
a_{C}(\beta)
contains a lot more data than the standard co-efficient
1-\frac{1}{e_{C}}
. Indeed,
a_{C}(\beta)
corresponds to a
\mathbb{Z}/e_{C}
-cover
{\widetilde{C}}\to C
(together with the Galois action), which can in turn ramify. We refer to this as
secondary ramification
of
\beta
.

We obtain a natural notion of discrepancy for Brauer log pairs
(X,\beta)
which we call the
b-discrepancy
, as follows. Suppose that
Y\to X
is a proper birational map and that
E\subset Y
is an exceptional curve. Then the b-discrepancy of
\beta
along
E
is the usual log discrepancy plus the co-efficient of
E
in
\Delta_{Y,\beta}
. The whole framework of Mori’s minimal model program now makes sense in this setting, replacing the usual discrepancy with b-discrepancy. In particular, we can talk about terminal Brauer log pairs. Our main goal is to recover classical surface theory in this setting, that is, classify terminal Brauer log pairs and establish the analogue of Castelnuovo contraction.

Perhaps the most important distinction between the study of the Brauer log pair
(X,\beta)
and the log geometry of
(X,\Delta_{X,\beta})
, is that
\beta
determines ramification, and hence standard co-efficients on exceptional curves over
X
in a highly non-trivial way. For the geometric surfaces studied in
[
CI05
]
, the key to addressing this problem is the Artin-Mumford sequence
[
AM72
]
which describes the Brauer group of
K(X)
in terms of ramification data. For rational resolutions, the sequence is exact and gives precise information which allows us to control how ramification data behaves under blowups. The classification of terminal Brauer log pairs
(X,\beta)
is relatively straightforward,
X
is smooth, the associated log boundary
\Delta_{X,\beta}
has normal crossings, and at any node of
\Delta_{X,\beta}
, there is a further condition involving secondary ramification (see
[
CI05
, Definition 2.5]
).

For arithmetic surfaces, we have a generalisation of the Artin-Mumford sequence due to Saltman
[
Sal08
]
which similarly describes the part
\textup{Br}(K(X))^{\prime}
of the Brauer group
\textup{Br}(K(X))
which is prime to all the residue characteristics. Unfortunately, even for rational resolutions, the sequence is only a complex which gives a non-trivial obstruction group. We are only able to compute this obstruction when there is no secondary ramification. Nevertheless, this is enough to prove a slew of interesting results, and in particular, recover classical Italian surface theory when
\beta
has prime order
p>5
. Crucial to our theory is the development of a new “fan calculus” which, in this case, gives the ramification data of sufficiently many exceptional curves so as to pin down the terminal condition.

The following is a watered down version of our classification theorem for terminal Brauer log pairs.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\beta)
be a Brauer log pair where
\beta
has prime index
p>5
. Then étale locally at any closed point
x\in X
either

(1)

X
is regular at
x
, the ramification locus of
\beta
has at worst nodal singularities, and there is non-trivial secondary ramification if
x
is a node, OR

(2)

X
is a Hirzebruch-Jung singularity
x
with determinant
p
(see Definition
6.4
), the ramification locus does not pass through
x
, but
\beta
is non-trivial étale locally there.

The full Theorem
7.1
, gives precise conditions for the terminal condition. It shows the converse is almost true, and in particular, for any Hirzebruch-Jung singularity
X
with determinant
p>5
, there exist non-trivial Brauer classes
\beta
such that
(X,\beta)
is terminal. This is in stark contrast to the case of geometric surfaces where
(X,\beta)
terminal guarantees
X
is smooth.

Our version of Castelnuovo contraction is formulated using the framework of MMP.

Theorem 1.2
.

(see Theorem
8.5
) Let
(Y,\beta)
be a terminal Brauer log pair where
\beta
has prime order
p>5
. Let
E\subset Y
be an irreducible projective curve with
K_{Y,\beta}.E<0,E^{2}<0
. Then there is a contraction map
f\colon Y\to X
and
(X,\beta)
is also terminal.

The Zariski factorisation theorem also follows easily from this (
8.7
).

In the prime index
p>5
we also have a complete classification (see Theorem
8.8
and Remark
8.10
) of the “Castelnuovo contractions” of Theorem
1.2
. We cannot give the full result here in the introduction, but the following corollary gives the gist of the bizarre new phenomena that can occur.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,\beta)
be a terminal Brauer log pair with
\beta
of prime index
p>5
. For any closed point
x\in X
, there is exists a unique (up to isomorphism) proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
which contracts a single irreducible curve and such that
(Y,\beta)
is terminal. Moreover,

(1)

if
x
is a regular point, then
Y
has at most one singular point, and

(2)

if
x
is a Hirzeburch-Jung singularity, then either
Y
is regular and
f
is the contraction of a
(-p)
-curve, or
Y
has exactly two Hirzebruch-Jung singularities.

The theorem suggests that we should think of the contraction map
f
there as the “
\beta
-twisted blowup” of
X
at
x
, and surprisingly, at least to us, this is not necessarily the usual blowup of
X
at
x
but depends critically on
\beta
.

Example 1.4
.

We give a simple example illustrating some of the new phenomena that arise. Let
q
be a prime such that
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
so the
q-
adic integers
\mathfrak{o}=\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
contains
\omega
, a primitive cube root of 1. The arithmetic surface of interest will be
X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{o}[x]
. Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{o}^{\times}
be a non-cube so
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{o}[\sqrt[3]{\alpha}]
defines a degree 3 unramified extension of
\mathfrak{o}
. The central simple algebra
K(X)\langle u,v\rangle/(u^{3}-\alpha,v^{3}-x,vu-\omega uv)
defines an index 3 Brauer class
\beta=(\alpha,x)_{\omega}\in\text{Br}\,K(X)
which has ramification
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{o}
along the curve
C:x=0
and is unramified elsewhere. Now
(X,\beta)
is terminal. We can blowup
X
at closed points four times to obtain a proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
whose exceptional locus
E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup E_{3}\cup E_{4}
has dual intersection graph

(1.1)

\textstyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(-3)}}{{E_{1}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(-1)}}{{E_{2}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(-3)}}{{E_{3}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(-1)}}{{E_{4}}}}

and the strict transform
{\widetilde{C}}
of
C
intersects only
E_{4}
. One shows fairly easily with the results in Section
4
that
\beta
ramifies on
E_{1},E_{3},{\widetilde{C}}
and is unramified elsewhere so in particular is terminal on
Y
. We may thus factorise
f\colon Y\to X
as a composite of Castelnuovo contractions with respect to
\beta
. It turns out that contracting either of the
(-1)
curves
E_{2}
or
E_{4}
will give a surface which is not
\beta
-terminal. Instead, one should contract the two
(-3)
-curves first. Next, one contracts
E_{2}
to a canonical Hirzebruch-Jung singularity of type
A_{2}
which we note has determinant 3. Finally, one contracts
E_{4}
to arrive at
X
. This example, shows that changing the Brauer class from the trivial one to
\beta
above, completely changes how you Zariski factorise
f
.

In the final section, we look at terminal Brauer log pairs on regular surfaces, without the prime index hypothesis on the Brauer class. With our current methods, we cannot classify the possibilities, but we do show that the ramification locus is close to normal crossing, though, to our surprise, it does not have to be.

Notations 1.5
.

Throughout, we let
X
be an integral scheme, usually an arithmetic surface. We will denote the rational function field of
X
as
K(X)
. Given any abelian group
H
, the notation
H^{\prime}
denotes the part of
H
which is prime to all the residue characteristics of
X
.
If there is more than one scheme
X
, involved, the set of their residue characteristics will be the same, so there should be no ambiguity as to what
H^{\prime}
means.

Finally, the notion of intersection products for arithmetic surfaces depends on what ring you wish to compute lengths of cohomology groups over. We will try to spell this out every time there is a possibility of ambiguity rather than fixing a base ring like
\mathbb{Z}
. The one exception is that given an irreducible projective curve
E
, the self-intersection number
E^{2}
will always be computed with respect to
H^{0}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E})
so Castelnuovo contractions will contract precisely the
(-1)
-curves.

2.
The Artin-Mumford-Saltman sequence

Let
X
be an arithmetic surface as in the introduction. Suppose that
X
is furthermore regular and let
K=K(X)
denote its field of fractions.

Recall Grothendieck’s approach
[
Gro68b
]
to studying
Br(K)^{\prime}
. Let
g\colon\operatorname{Spec}K\to X
be the inclusion of the generic point and
i_{C}\colon\operatorname{Spec}K(C)\to X
be the inclusion of the generic point of a curve
C\in X^{(1)}
. There is an exact sequence of étale sheaves on
X
:

(2.1)

0\to\mathbb{G}_{m,X}\to g_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,K}\to\bigoplus_{C\in X^{(1)}}i_{C*}\mathbb{Z}\to 0.

We obtain the following exact sequence in étale cohomology.

(2.2)

0\to\textup{Br}(X)^{\prime}\to H^{2}_{et}(X,g_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m.K})^{\prime}\xrightarrow{a}\bigoplus_{C\in X^{(1)}}H^{2}_{et}(X,i_{C*}\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\partial}H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\theta}H^{3}_{et}(X,g_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,K})^{\prime}

The Leray spectral sequences for
g
and
i_{C}
then embed
H^{2}_{et}(X,g_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m.K})^{\prime}
in
\textup{Br}(K)^{\prime}
and
H^{2}_{et}(X,i_{C*}\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}
into
H^{2}(K(C),\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}\simeq H^{1}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}
. The morphism
a
above lifts to the ramification map

(2.3)

a=(a_{C})\colon\textup{Br}(K)^{\prime}\xrightarrow{a}\bigoplus_{C}H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}.

Now the elements of
H^{1}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
classify cyclic extensions of
K(C)
with chosen generator of the Galois group, so there are
secondary ramification
maps
r\colon H^{1}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to\medoplus_{q\in C}\mu^{-1}
. Given
\beta\in\textup{Br}\,K
such that
a_{C}(\beta)\in H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is non-zero, we say that
C
is a
ramification curve
of
\beta
, and the corresponding (possibly ramified) cyclic cover
{\widetilde{C}}\to C
, is the
ramification cover
. Its ramification
r(a_{C}(\beta))
will be referred to as
secondary ramification
of
\beta
. This will be a key concept for us.

The Leray spectral sequence for
g
also gives a map
H^{3}_{et}(X,g_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,K})\to H^{3}_{et}(K,\mathbb{G}_{m,K})
with kernel the image of
H^{0}(X,R^{2}g_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,K})
so composing with
\theta
in Equation (
2.2
) above gives a map

H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\prime}\to H^{3}_{et}(K,\mathbb{G}_{m,K})^{\prime}.

We can now describe the Artin-Mumford-Saltman sequence below
[
AM72
]
,
[
Sal08
, Theorem 6.12]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

There is a complex

0\to\textup{Br}(X)^{\prime}\to\textup{Br}(K)^{\prime}\xrightarrow{a}\bigoplus_{C}H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}\xrightarrow{r}\bigoplus_{q\in X^{(2)}}\mu^{-1}.

whose only cohomology is at the
H^{1}
term. The cohomology there is given by the kernel of

H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\prime}\to H^{3}_{et}(K,\mathbb{G}_{m,K})^{\prime}

This sequence will be the main tool for determining possible ramification data. Given an element
(z_{C})\in\medoplus_{C}H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
, the
primary obstruction
to this coming from
\textup{Br}\,K
is that
r(z_{C})=0
. We refer to this condition as
secondary ramification cancelling
since a key case is when there are two ramification curves
C_{1},C_{2}
which intersect at some point
q
and their ramifications there,
r(z_{C_{1}})_{q},r(z_{C_{2}})_{q}\in\mu^{-1}
are inverses of each other. Let
\ker:=\ker\left(r\colon\medoplus_{C}H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to\medoplus\mu^{-1}\right)
. There remains a
secondary obstruction map

ob\colon\ker\to H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G})^{\prime}
.

3.
Discrepancy

In this section, we review the notions of log discrepancy and b-discrepancy for Brauer log pairs
(X,\beta)
where
X
is an arithmetic surface with function field
K
and
\beta\in\textup{Br}(K)^{\prime}
is a Brauer class whose index is prime to all the residue characteristics.

For each
C\subset X
, an irreducible curve, there is a ramification map

a_{C}\colon\textup{Br}(K)^{\prime}\to H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}

As in the introduction, we obtain an associated boundary divisor
\Delta_{X,\beta}\in\textup{Div}\,X
defined by

\Delta_{X,\beta}=\sum_{C}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{C}}\right)C,\quad n_{C}:=\text{order of }a_{C}(\beta)

and hence associated log surface
(X,\Delta_{X,\beta})
. We also define

(3.1)

K_{X,\beta}=K_{X}+\Delta_{X,\beta}.

Now consider a proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
and an exceptional curve
E\subset Y
. Since
K(Y)=K(X)
, one can consider the Brauer log pair
(Y,\beta)
. There exist rational numbers
a_{E},b_{E}
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle K_{Y}+f^{-1}_{*}\Delta_{X,\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{*}K_{X,\beta}+\sum_{E}a_{E}E

(3.3)

\displaystyle K_{Y,\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{*}K_{X,\beta}+\sum_{E}b_{E}E

We say
a_{E}
is the
log discrepancy
of
(X,\beta)
along
E
and
b_{E}
is the b-discrepancy. We say
(X,\Delta_{X,\beta})
is
log terminal
if for all
f
and
E
, we have
a_{E}>-1
and we say
(X,\beta)
is
terminal (resp. canonical)
if all the
b_{E}>0
(resp.
b_{E}\geq 0
).

Remark 3.1
.

Evidently, we have the formula

b_{E}=a_{E}+1-\frac{1}{n_{E}}

so
(X,\beta)
terminal guarantees that the associated log surface is log terminal.

One may compute discrepancy étale locally on
X
, but with a bit of care as we elaborate now. Let
x\in X
be a closed point and consider the restricted Brauer class
\beta_{x}\in BrK(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{h})
. Now the ramification map commutes with étale localisation, so
\beta_{x}
does determine all the
n_{E}
for exceptional curves over
x
, However,
\beta_{x}
does not determine the global log boundary
\Delta_{X,\beta}
even locally at
x
since
H^{1}_{et}(K(C),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(K(\mathcal{O}_{C,x}^{h}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is not necessarily injective. This corresponds to the fact that a degree
n_{C}
ramification cover
{\widetilde{C}}\to C
may split into
g_{C}>1
components upon restriction to an étale local neighbourhood of
x\in C
.

This suggests

Definition 3.2
.

A
localised Brauer class

(\beta,g_{C})
on
X
consists of a Brauer class
\beta\in\textup{Br}\,K(X)^{\prime}
and positive integers
g_{C}
, for each irreducible affine curve
C\subset X
, such that all but finitely many
g_{C}
equal 1 and all are coprime to the residue characteristics.

Example 3.3
.

Let
q=p^{m}
be prime power. Let
n\mid q-1
and let
\zeta
be a primitive
n^{\mbox{th}}
root of unity.
Let
R=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,y]
and let
A=R\langle u,v\rangle/(u^{n}-x,v^{n}-y,uv-\zeta vu).

Let
\beta\in\textup{Br}K(R)
be the Brauer class of
A\otimes K(R)
.
When we henselise at a point in
V(xy)\subset\operatorname{Spec}R
that is not the origin,
for example
z=(1,0),
we obtain
\beta|_{R^{h}_{z}}=0
and
g_{C}=0

for all curves except
D=V(y)
where
g_{D}=1
. The
g_{D}
in the localised Brauer class keeps track of global ramification that becomes trivial on henselisation.

Now consider proper birational maps
X^{\prime}\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}
. Then a localised Brauer class
(\beta,g_{C})
, naturally induces the localised Brauer class
(\beta,g_{f^{-1}_{*}C})
on
X^{\prime}
and
(\beta,g_{f_{*}C})
on
X^{\prime\prime}
. The collection of all these localised Brauer classes as
X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}
vary will also be called a
localised Brauer class
on the birational equivalence class of
X
and will be denoted
\tilde{\beta}=(\beta,g)
. It now becomes clear that
we can define b-discrepancy, terminal etc for localised Brauer classes by modifying the definition of the associated boundary divisor with
n_{C}=e_{C}g_{C}
where
e_{C}
is the order of
a_{C}(\beta)
. We call
n_{C}
the
ramification index
of the localised Brauer class
(\beta,g_{C})
at
C
and when
n_{C}>1
, we say that
C
is a
ramification curve
.

4.
The secondary obstruction for rational resolutions

Our goal in this section, is to study how ramification behaves under blowups and, more generally, birational transformations. In the geometric case studied in
[
CI05
]
, this can be deduced from the Artin-Mumford sequence in Theorem
2.1
. This is comparatively easy, because in computing the secondary obstruction map
ob\colon\ker\to H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G})^{\prime}
(notation as in Section
2
) we may restrict to étale neighbourhoods of the exceptional curves where
H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
vanishes. Thus we need only consider the primary obstruction.

For arithmetic surfaces, this is no longer true, and unfortunately, we do not know how to compute the secondary obstruction in general. However, in the notation of Equation (
2.2
), we have
H^{2}_{et}(X,i_{C*}\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}\subseteq\ker
and
ob
restricted to this subgroup is the boundary map
\partial
from
2.2
. In this section,
we compute
\partial
in the case of rational resolutions. This will give the secondary obstruction map in the case where there is no secondary ramification.

Let
R
be a commutative two-dimensional excellent normal noetherian Hensel local domain with finite residue field
\kappa
. Let
f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be a rational resolution, that is, a projective birational morphism where
X
is regular and
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
.

We first compute
H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\prime}
. Let
{\widetilde{R}}
be the strict henselisation of
R
and
{\widetilde{X}}=X\times_{R}{\widetilde{R}}
and
G=\textup{Gal}(\bar{\kappa}/\kappa)\simeq\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
be the Galois group of
{\widetilde{R}}/R
. We use the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence

H^{p}(G,H^{q}_{et}({\widetilde{X}},\mathbb{G}_{m}))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m}).

Lemma 4.1
.

We have
H^{3}_{et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq H^{2}(G,\operatorname{Pic}{\widetilde{X}}).

Proof.

It suffices to show that the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence degenerates appropriately. We first show that
\textup{Br}({\widetilde{X}})^{\prime}=0
. By the Kummer exact sequence, it suffices to show that elements of
H^{2}_{et}({\widetilde{X}},\mu_{d})^{\prime}
lift to
H^{1}({\widetilde{X}},\mathbb{G}_{m})=\operatorname{Pic}{\widetilde{X}}
. Let
{\widetilde{X}}_{0}
be the closed fibre of
{\widetilde{X}}\to\operatorname{Spec}{\widetilde{R}}
. The proper base change theorem
[
Mil80
, Chapter VI, Corollary 2.7]
shows that
H^{2}_{et}({\widetilde{X}},\mu_{d})^{\prime}=H^{2}_{et}({\widetilde{X}}_{0},\mu_{d})^{\prime}
. Now the Brauer group of
{\widetilde{X}}_{0}
is trivial, so every element of
H^{2}_{et}({\widetilde{X}}_{0},\mu_{d})^{\prime}
comes from a line bundle on
{\widetilde{X}}_{0}
. To conclude
\textup{Br}({\widetilde{X}})=0
, it remains now only to note that
\operatorname{Pic}{\widetilde{X}}\to\operatorname{Pic}{\widetilde{X}}_{0}
is surjective by
[
Lip69
, Lemma 14.3]
. Now consider the terms
H^{p}(G,{\widetilde{R}}^{\times})=H^{p}_{et}(\operatorname{Spec}R,\mathbb{G}_{m,R})=H^{p}_{et}(\operatorname{Spec}\kappa,\mathbb{G}_{m,\kappa})=0
for
p>0
by
[
Mil80
, Chapter III, Remark 3.11(a)]
.

It remains now only to show that
H^{3}_{et}({\widetilde{X}},\mathbb{G})^{\prime}=0
. Let
l
be a positive integer relatively prime to
\textup{char}\,\kappa
. From the proper base change theorem, we have
H^{3}_{et}({\widetilde{X}},\mu_{l^{r}})=H^{3}_{et}({\widetilde{X}}_{0},\mu_{l^{r}})
which is zero since
{\widetilde{X}}_{0}
is a curve defined over an algebraically closed field. It follows from the Kummer exact sequence
as per
[
Gro68a
, Corollaire 3.2]
that the
l
-torsion part of
H^{3}_{et}({\widetilde{X}},\mathbb{G})
is also zero.
∎

Recall that
f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
is a rational resolution. Let
C
be a curve on
X
. If
C
is an exceptional curve, then it is rational by assumption. Suppose that
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})=\kappa^{\prime}
is a degree

In this section, we establish the existence of terminal resolutions in our context.

Proof.

This is essentially

∎

We can construct terminal resolutions as per

]

8.
Contraction theorems

In this section, we wish to give sufficient criteria for the contractibility of terminal localised Brauer classes and classify contractions, at least under the additional assumption where the localised Brauer class has index a prime
p>5
. In particular, we show that given any terminal localised Brauer class
(\beta,g)
on X and closed point
x\in X
, there is a unique proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
such that
Y
is still terminal with respect to
(\beta,g)
, and
f
contracts a single irreducible curve to
x
and is an isomorphism away from
x
. We may thus think of
Y
as the “blowup” of
X
at
x
with respect to the localised Brauer class
(\beta,g)
.

Throughout this section, let
X
be an arithmetic surface and
(\beta,g)
be a terminal localised Brauer class on the birational equivalence class of
X
. We let
\Delta_{X,\beta,g}
be the associated boundary divisor and
K_{X,\beta,g}
be the canonical divisor
K_{X}+\Delta_{X,\beta,g}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
(\beta,g_{C})
be a terminal localised Brauer class on
X
. Suppose there exists a projective irreducible curve
E\subset X
and a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
contracting precisely
E
such that
K_{X,\beta,g}.E<0
. Then
(\beta,g_{f_{*}C})
is terminal on
Y
.

Proof.

We write

K_{X,\beta,g}=f^{*}K_{Y,\beta,g}+aE

and observe on taking the intersection product with
E
(with respect to
H^{0}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E})
), that
K_{X,\beta,g}.E<0\implies a>0
.

Now consider any proper birational morphism
g\colon W\to X
. Since
(\beta,g)
is terminal on
X
, we know there are positive discrepancies
a_{i}
giving

(8.1)

K_{W,\beta,g}=g^{*}K_{X,\beta,g}+\sum a_{i}E_{i}=g^{*}(f^{*}K_{Y,\beta,g}+aE)+\sum a_{i}E_{i}.

The proposition follows.
∎

Ideally, we would like to replace the contractibility hypothesis in the lemma above with the numerical criterion
E^{2}<0
. One easy general result is the following.

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
(\beta,g_{C})
be a terminal localised Brauer class on
X
and let
E\subset X
be a projective irreducible curve with
K_{X,\beta,g}.E<0,\ E^{2}<0
. If
\beta
is also unramified on
E
, then
E
is contractible, say via
f\colon X\to Y
and
(\beta,g)
is also terminal on
Y
.

Proof.

In this case we have
K_{X,\beta,g}=K_{X}+\Delta
where
\Delta.E\geq 0
since
\beta
is unramified on
E
. The inequality
0>K_{X,\beta,g}.E=(K_{X}+\Delta).E
ensures that
K_{X}.E<-\Delta.E\leq 0
so we may contract
E
by MMP for surfaces (see
[
KK94
, Lemma 2.3.5]
or
[
BMP+20
]
, which apply in our setting, despite more stringent hypotheses which are not used in the proof of this fact). The Corollary now follows from Lemma
8.1
.
∎

To deal with the ramified case, we need to make use of the classification of terminal localised Brauer classes.

Proposition 8.3
.

Suppose that
(\beta,g)
is a localised Brauer class which is terminal on
X
and of prime index
p>5
. Let
E\subset X
be an irreducible projective curve with
K_{X,\beta,g}.E<0,\ E^{2}<0
and such that
\beta
ramifies on
E
. Then there is a contraction map
f\colon X\to Y
, and
(\beta,g)
is terminal on
Y
too. Furthermore, exactly one of the following occurs.

(1)

the ramification cover
{\widetilde{E}}\to E
of
\beta
along
E
is itself ramified. In this case,
Y
is regular and étale locally at

, we are in case (3) of Theorem
7.1
, that is,
\beta
has secondary ramification at

which is a node of the ramification locus.

(2)

The ramification cover
{\widetilde{E}}\to E
of
\beta
along
E
is unramified and
E
is a (-p)-curve.

Proof.

The classification of terminal localised Brauer classes Theorem
7.1
(4) shows that
X
is regular in some Zariski open neighbourhood of

so we may assume that
X
is regular. If the ramification cover
{\widetilde{E}}\to E
is ramified, then we may apply the argument in
[
CI05
, Theorem 3.10]
for Castelnuovo contraction for terminal orders on geometric surfaces. This gives case (1).

We thus assume that
{\widetilde{E}}\to E
is unramified. All intersection products below will be with respect to

. We write

\Delta_{X,\beta,g}=\Delta^{\prime}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)E,\quad\text{so}\quad\Delta^{\prime}.E\geq 0.

Now

Now at every point of

, so on some étale neighbourhood of

. Thus vanishing of secondary obstruction for

shows that

for some integer

It follows that
m-2<-\Delta^{\prime}.E\leq 0

∎

Remark 8.4
.

Note that Case (2) of Proposition

-curve

We now have the following version of the Castelnuovo contraction theorem.

Definition 8.6

The Zariski factorisation theorem in this context follows formally using the standard argument which we reproduce.

Theorem 8.7

Let
(\beta,g)
be a terminal localised Brauer class of prime index

The next goal is to classify Castelnuovo contractions in the case of prime index

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
(\beta,g)
be a terminal localised Brauer class of prime index

It thus makes sense to define the

Remark 8.10

Proof.

The proof of this theorem will take the rest of the section. We may and will reduce to the étale local case where

is the spectrum of Hensel local ring.

Lemma 8.11

In this case, being terminal with respect to

Suppose now we are in case (2) of Theorem

be the
p
-th blowup so the exceptional curves form an HJ-string with

exceptional curves and weights

. Note that by vanishing of the secondary obstruction,

ramifies on all the

-curves but not on the

-curve. We may thus contract all the

-curves, via say

, to a Hirzebruch-Jung singularity which is still terminal with respect to

being case (4) of Theorem

and the next lemma states that this is the unique such.

Lemma 8.12
.

Suppose that we are in case (2) of Theorem
7.1
, that is,
(\beta,g)
is a terminal localised Brauer class on an arithmetic surface
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
where

is regular Hensel local and
\beta
ramifies on a curve

. Here

is regular except for a single Hirzebruch-Jung singularity whose minimal resolution has

(-2)-curves. Furthermore,
\beta
is unramified on the exceptional curve and the Hirzebruch-Jung singularity is case (4) of Theorem
7.1
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a Castelnuovo contraction and
g\colon{\widetilde{Y}}\to Y
be a minimal resolution. We may factorise
fg\colon{\widetilde{Y}}\to X
into a sequence of blowups, and our approach will be to constrain the possibilities for what this sequence might be. Now minimality of
g
and the fact that
f
has precisely one exceptional curve

means that there is exactly one
(-1)
-curve in
{\widetilde{Y}}
and its image in
Y
is

. Thus the sequence of blowups must involve repeatedly blowing up on the
(-1)
-curve created in the previous blowup. Suppose the ramification of
\beta
along the ramification curve

is
\zeta
. Consider the first blowup
h_{1}\colon X_{1}\to X
. Then
\beta
is also ramified on the exceptional

, so the the next blowup must be at the closed point of the strict transform of

. There can be no further blowups, for

, and so by Theorem

(4), does not correspond to an exceptional of

.
∎

Lemma 8.13
.

Let
\beta
be a terminal Brauer class on a regular arithmetic surface

where
R
is Hensel local such that we are in case (3) of Theorem

, that is,
\beta
has non-trivial secondary ramification.Then up to isomorphism, there exists a unique Castelnuovo contraction

with respect to
\beta
which in this case is the blowup at the closed point.

Proof.

We follow the approach in Lemma

and so consider an arbitrary
\beta
-contraction
f\colon Y\to X
and let
g\colon{\widetilde{Y}}\to Y
be the minimal resolution of the underlying surface
Y
.We factorise

into a sequence of blowups and show that it is in fact single blowup
f_{1}\colon X_{1}\to X
. If this is not the case, then the exceptional

of

must give rise to one of the exceptional curves of

and hence, one of the exceptional curves in Case (4) of Theorem

. However, the ramification cover of

giving the ramification of

.
∎

It remains now only to examine Case (4) of Theorem

to complete the proof of Theorem
8.8
. To this end, we need several preliminary results which will help us control the determinant
\operatorname{det}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})
of an HJ-string with weights

. Below we write

and

for the

Proposition 8.14
.

(1)

If
\vec{2}=(2,2,\ldots,2)\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}

\operatorname{det}(\vec{2})=r+1
.

(2)

\operatorname{det}(\vec{m}+\vec{e}_{i})=\operatorname{det}(\vec{m})+\operatorname{det}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1})\operatorname{det}(m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{r})
.

(3)

\operatorname{det}(\vec{m})=\operatorname{det}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-2},m_{i-1}+1,\,1,\,m_{i}+1,m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{r})

Consider a cone
\sigma=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\vec{a}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\vec{b}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with boundary rays determined by vectors
\vec{a},\vec{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
which we may assume to be primitive. Over
\mathbb{C}
, this determines, via toric geometry, a Hirzebruch-Jung singularity and hence a an HJ-string, say with weights
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
.

Proposition 8.15
.

Let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
be the weights of an HJ-string determined by two non-parallel primitive vectors

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}b_{1}\\
b_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}

\operatorname{det}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})=\left|\operatorname{det}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right|

Proof.

We have seen this in the case where

and the general case follows by change of basis.
∎

Lemma 8.16
.

Let
(\beta,g)
be a terminal localised Brauer class of prime index
p>5
on an arithmetic surface
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
where
R
is a Hensel local ring defining a Hirzebruch-Jung singularity with weights given by the cone
\sigma=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p\\
-k\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
0<k<p
. Then, up to isomorphism, there exists a unique Castelnuovo contraction with respect to
(\beta,g)
of the form
f\colon Y\to X
. If
k=1
, then
Y
is regular and
f
is the contraction of a
(-p)
-curve as in Proposition
8.3
(2). If
k>1
, then the ray
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p\\
-k+1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
divides
\sigma
into two cones, say
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
. Then

contains exactly two Hirzebruch-Jung singularities, and their weights are given by the cones
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a
(\beta,g)
-contraction and
h\colon{\widetilde{X}}\to X,\ h_{Y}\colon{\widetilde{Y}}\to Y
be the minimal resolutions of the underlying surfaces. We consider the induced morphism
\tilde{f}\colon{\widetilde{Y}}\to{\widetilde{X}}
and, as in the proofs of Lemmas
8.12
and
8.13
, factorise it into a sequence of blowups
\tilde{f}=f_{l}\circ f_{l-1}\ldots\circ f_{1}
. Furthermore, as observed in those lemmas, we see that the sequence involves only blowing up a point on the most recently created
(-1)
-curve to ensure there is only one
(-1)
-curve in
{\widetilde{Y}}
. We may assume that
\tilde{f}
is not the identity, for that is the case where
f,h
are just the contraction of a
(-p)
-curve as in the statement of the Lemma. Let
E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup\ldots\cup E_{r}
be the exceptional locus of
h
. We assume the
E_{i}
indexed so
E_{1}-\ldots-E_{r}
is part of an HJ-string
\mathcal{E}
with weights
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
and determinant
\operatorname{det}(\vec{m})=p
. We let
{\widetilde{E}}_{1},\ldots,{\widetilde{E}}_{r}
denote the strict transforms of
E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}
in
{\widetilde{Y}}
, and more generally use the tilde notation to denote the strict transform in
{\widetilde{Y}}
. Note that the exceptional locus of
h_{Y}
will consist of a
(-1)
-curve and a number of HJ-strings corresponding to the Hirzebruch-Jung singularities of
Y
. We will refer to these HJ-strings as the HJ-strings of
{\widetilde{Y}}
. Our method is to analyse how these can arise from portions of
{\widetilde{E}}_{1},\ldots,{\widetilde{E}}_{r}
and exceptionals of the blowups
f_{j}
.

Suppose the first blowup
f_{1}\colon X_{1}\to{\widetilde{X}}
is at some non-nodal point of the exceptional locus of
h
and thus, lies on a unique exceptional, say
E_{i}
. It is clear then that
{\widetilde{E}}_{1},\ldots,{\widetilde{E}}_{r}
must lie in a single HJ-string of
{\widetilde{Y}}
. If furthermore,
1<i<r
, then it must be a complete HJ-string of
{\widetilde{Y}}
with vector of weights
\vec{m}+a\vec{e}_{i}
for some positive integer

. Proposition
8.14
(1) and (2) show that
\operatorname{det}(\vec{m}+a\vec{e}_{i})>p
so
Y
is not
(\beta,g)
-terminal by Theorem
7.1
. We may thus assume that
i=r
. The complete HJ-string of
{\widetilde{Y}}
thus has the form
E_{0}-{\widetilde{E}}_{1}-\ldots-{\widetilde{E}}_{r}-\ldots-{\widetilde{E}}_{s+1}
. Here, we can assume
{\widetilde{E}}_{s+1}
is the final
(-1)
-curve and the HJ-string is obtained from
\mathcal{E}
, by repeatedly blowing up at the right hand end. Proposition
8.14
(1),(2) and (3) now show that the determinant has again strictly increased, so we know this cannot be the case.

We have thus shown that
f_{1}
must be the blowup at a nodal point of the exceptional locus of
g
. We can now argue as in the previous paragraph to see that all the blowups
f_{j}
must be at nodal points. The exceptionals of
\tilde{f}h
, must give rise to an HJ-string
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}={\widetilde{E}}_{0}-\ldots-{\widetilde{E}}_{s+1}
in the HJ-spectrum
\mathcal{E}_{*}
generated by the seed
\mathcal{E}
. Consider the fan representation
\nu\colon\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{E}_{*}\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of Example
6.5
, that is, so
\nu(E_{0})=(0,1),\nu(E_{r+1})=(p,-k)
. Note that

for in that case,

is the contraction of a single
(-p)
-curve. Let
E
be the unique exceptional in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
with weight 1 and
\nu(E)=(a,-b)
. We thus see that

has two Hirzebruch-Jung singularities, and their corresponding HJ-strings are
{\widetilde{E}}_{0}-\ldots-E
and
E-\ldots-{\widetilde{E}}_{s+1}
. The determinants of these two HJ-strings must be
p
by Theorem

(4), so firstly, we see that

. This also shows that
\beta
is unramified on

. For the other Hirzebruch-Jung singularity to have determinant

, we use Proposition
6.7
to see

p=\begin{vmatrix}p&p\\
-k&-b\end{vmatrix}=p(k-b)

so we must have

.

Conversely, we can construct this Castelnuovo contraction by blowing up
{\widetilde{X}}
until the exceptional corresponding to
(p,-k+1)
is achieved, and then applying Lipman’s version of Artin contraction to contract all other exceptionals.
∎

Finally, Lemmas
8.11
,
8.12
,
8.13
and

.
∎

9.
Terminal Brauer classes on regular surfaces

In this section, we examine terminal Brauer log pairs of arbitrary index on a regular surface. Our objectives were much more modest here, namely, to bound the singularities of the ramification locus. We succeed in showing the ramification is close to normal crossing, and we obtain reasonably precise control when it is not normal crossing.

Let
R
denote a regular excellent two-dimensional noetherian Hensel local ring with finite residue field
\kappa
and
(\beta,g_{C})
be a localised Brauer class as in Section
3
, that is,
\beta\in\textup{Br}K(R)^{\prime}
and
g_{C}
are positive integers which are equal to 1 for all but finitely many irreducible curves
C\subset\operatorname{Spec}R
. For each prime divisor
C\in\operatorname{Spec}R
, we have a positive integer
g_{C}
which is 1 for all but finitely many
C
. Below,
x
denotes the closed point of
\operatorname{Spec}R
.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
(\beta,g_{C})
be a terminal localised Brauer class on
\operatorname{Spec}R
and
\Gamma
the union of the ramification curves. Then
\textup{mult}_{x}\Gamma\leq 2
.

Proof.

We know that the associated log surface is log terminal from which it follows that
\textup{mult}_{x}\Gamma\leq 3
and furthermore, if equality occurs, then the ramification indices
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
of
(\beta,g_{C})
written with multiplicity, are a Platonic triple.

Suppose the latter occurs and let
f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be the blowup at
x
and
E
the exceptional curve. The b-discrepancy of
E
is

(9.1)

b\textup{-disc}=\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}-1-\frac{1}{e}>0.

where
e
is the ramification index of
\beta
along
E
. Now
e
is a factor of the order
n
, of the Brauer class
\beta
. Now
\textup{Br}\,R=0
, so the Artin-Mumford-Saltman sequence shows that
n
, and hence
e
divides the lowest common multiple of
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
. The formula (
9.1
) for b-discrepancy now eliminates the possibilities
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})=(2,3,3),(2,3,4),(2,3,5)
.

In the case
(2,2,d)
, Inequality (
9.1
) forces all the
g_{C}
to be 1 and
e=2d
so
d
must be odd. Let
C_{1},C_{2}\subset\operatorname{Spec}R
be the (possibly equal) ramification curves of ramification index 2, and
C_{3}
the ramification curve of ramification index
d
. Now vanishing of the primary obstruction, together with the fact that
d
is odd, shows that the ramification cover
\tilde{C}_{3}\to C_{3}
is étale. Suppose first that the ramification covers of
C_{1},C_{2}
are also étale, so the three ramification data are given by
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
where
G
is the absolute Galois group of the residue field as usual. Now
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}
are the unique non-zero 2-torsion element in
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and Corollary
4.4
then shows that the ramification along
E
is given by
\zeta_{3}
so
e=d
as opposed to
2d
, a contradiction. We may thus suppose that at least one of the ramification covers of
C_{1},C_{2}
is ramified, so vanishing of the primary obstruction ensures they both are.

Let
C^{\prime}_{i}
be the strict transform of
C_{i}
. Suppose first that
C^{\prime}_{3}
intersects
C^{\prime}_{2}
. We wish to derive a contradiction by blowing up the point of intersection to obtain
f_{1}\colon X_{1}\to X
and showing the b-discrepancy of its exceptional curve
E_{1}
is non-positive. Indeed, the coefficient of
E_{1}
in
K_{X_{1}}-(ff_{1})^{*}(\sum_{i}(1-\tfrac{1}{e_{i}})C_{i})
is
-\tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{2}{d}
. Now
\beta
is
2d
-torsion, so the maximum possible b-discrepancy is

-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{2}{d}+1-\frac{1}{2d}=\frac{3}{2d}-\frac{1}{2}\leq 0

since
d\geq 3
.

We may thus suppose that the only ramification curve that
C^{\prime}_{3}
intersects, is
E
. To eliminate the case
(2,2,d)
, we consider discrepancies of exceptional curves over
y=C^{\prime}_{3}\cap E
. Let
\tilde{E}\to E
be the cyclic degree
2d
cover corresponding to the ramification above
E
. We wish to study this locally above
y
, which we now know is étale there.
To do so, consider the Brauer class
2\beta
which is now unramified above
C_{1},C_{2}
so the secondary obstruction along
E
can now be calculated using Corollary
4.4
to show the ramification of
2\beta
along
E
is
2\zeta
where
\zeta\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is the order
d
element giving the ramification above
C_{3}
.
Thus, étale locally above
y
,
{\widetilde{E}}\to E
is given by
\zeta+\zeta_{2}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
, where
\zeta_{2}
is one of the two 2-torsion elements.

We now repeatedly blowup points infinitely near
y
, which are on the strict transform of
E
. More precisely, define
X_{0}=X,E_{0}=E
and
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\to X_{i-1}
to be the blowup of the closed point of the strict transform of
E
which lies above
y
. The ramification along
E_{i}
can be computed by induction using Corollary
4.4
to give
(i+1)\zeta+i\zeta_{2}
. In particular, since
d
is odd,
E_{d-1}
is not a ramification curve, and the b- and log discrepancies coincide there. Similarly, one computes inductively that the log discrepancy of
E_{i}
is
\frac{i+1}{d}-1
so
E_{d-1}
has zero b-discrepancy.

∎

We say that a curve
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Spec}R
with multiplicity 2, has an
A_{3}
-singularity if its strict transform, on blowing up the closed point of
\operatorname{Spec}R
, has normal crossings.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
(\beta,g_{C})
be a terminal localised Brauer class on
\operatorname{Spec}R
and let
\Gamma
be the union of its ramification curves. Then either i)
\Gamma
has normal crossings, or ii)
\Gamma
is an
A_{3}
-singularity, there is non-trivial secondary ramification and the (primary) ramification indices are
n_{1}=2
,
n_{2}=2l
for some odd
l
and all
g_{C}=1
.

Proof.

We know from Lemma
9.1
, that
\textup{mult}\,\Gamma\leq 2
, so we may suppose the multiplicity is 2 and that
\Gamma
is not normal crossing. Let
d
be the number of times we need to blowup the singularity of (the strict transform of)
\Gamma
until the multiplicity of
\Gamma
at any closed point of the blowup is one.

Let
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
be the ramification indices of the localised Brauer class
(\beta,g_{C})
, written with multiplicity and
e_{1},e_{2}
be the corresponding ramification indices of
\beta
. The associated log surface
(\operatorname{Spec}R,\Delta)
is log terminal. From
[
Kol13
, Theorem 3.38]
, we know that
\{d,n_{1},n_{2}\}
must be a Platonic triple.

We first eliminate the possibility that
d\geq 3
, so let us assume this. Since every Platonic triple contains 2, we have
n_{1}=2
. We blow up
\operatorname{Spec}R
three times at the singularity of
\Gamma
to obtain
f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
with three exceptional curves
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
forming an HJ-string
E_{1}-E_{2}-E_{3}
with self-intersections
E_{1}^{2}=E_{2}^{2}=-2,\ E_{3}^{2}=-1
. Since
d\geq 3
, the log discrepancy of
(\operatorname{Spec}R,\Delta)
along
E_{3}
is

3-3(1-\frac{1}{2})-3(1-\frac{1}{n_{2}})=\frac{3}{n_{2}}-\frac{3}{2}.

Now the maximum possible ramification index of
\beta
along
E_{3}
is
l=\textup{lcm}(2,e_{2})\leq 2n_{2}
since
\beta
is
l
-torsion. The co-efficient of
E_{3}
in the log surface associated to
(X,\beta)
is thus
\leq 1-\frac{1}{l}
. We thus have

0<\frac{3}{n_{2}}-\frac{3}{2}+1-\frac{1}{l}\leq\frac{5}{2n_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}

and the second inequality is an equality precisely when
n_{2}=e_{2}
is odd.
This shows that
n_{2}<5
. If
n_{2}=4
, then
l\leq 4
too and the b-discrepancy along
E_{3}
is now bounded above by
\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}=0
. We next rule out
n_{2}=3
. In this case,
n_{1},n_{2}
are relatively prime so there is no secondary ramification. Thus the ramification along the ramification curves
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
are given by
z_{1},z_{2}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
which are respectively 2 and 3-torsion (here
G
is the absolute Galois group of the residue field as usual). Corollary
4.4
, shows that the ramification along
E_{3}
is given by
3(z_{1}+z_{2})=z_{1}
which is 2-torsion. Thus the b-discrepancy along
E_{3}
is
\frac{3}{3}-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=0
.

We finally rule out the case
d\geq 3,n_{2}=2
. If there is no secondary ramification, then as in the
n_{2}=3
case, the ramification along the ramification curves
C_{1},C_{2}
are given by
z_{1},z_{2}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z})
. However, this time,
z_{1},z_{2}
are the unique non-trivial 2-torsion element so
\beta
is unramified along
E_{1}
by Corollary
4.4
. The b- and log discrepancies thus coincide and are thus non-positive. We may thus suppose there is secondary ramification. Since secondary ramification must cancel in
X
, we see that the ramification covers above
E_{1},E_{2}
must be unramified and 2-torsion. Suppose these are given by
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. We compute the secondary obstruction of
\beta
along
E_{1}
using Proposition
4.3
, to be
-2\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}=\zeta_{2}=0
. The b-discrepancy along
E_{2}
thus coincides with the log discrepancy which is 0.

We have thus shown that
d=2
. Suppose first that
n_{1}=3
. To resolve
(\operatorname{Spec}R,\Gamma)
, we need now only blow up twice to obtain
g\colon Y\to\operatorname{Spec}R
with two exceptional curves
F_{1},F_{2}
with
F_{1}^{2}=-2,F_{2}^{2}=-1
. The log discrepancy along
F_{2}
is
\frac{2}{n_{2}}-\frac{4}{3}
. We eliminate the possibilities for
n_{2}
. If
n_{2}=3
too, then the ramification index of
\beta
along
F_{2}
is at most 3 whilst the log discrepancy is
-\frac{2}{3}
. The b-discrepancy is thus at most 0. If
n_{2}=5
, then the log discrepancy of
F_{2}
is
-\frac{14}{15}
whilst the maximum ramification index of
\beta
there is
n_{1}n_{2}=15
. Thus the b-discrepancy is at most 0. Finally, suppose that
n_{2}=4
so the log discrepancy of
F_{2}
is
-\frac{5}{6}
. It suffices to show that the ramification index of
\beta
along
F_{2}
is at most 6. Now
n_{1},n_{2}
are relatively prime so there is no secondary ramification and the ramification along ramification curves is given by
z_{1},z_{2}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Here
z_{1},z_{2}
are 3 and 4-torsion and Corollary
4.4
shows that the ramification along
F_{2}
is given by
2(z_{1}+z_{2})
which is 6-torsion, as desired.

We are finally reduced to the case where
d=n_{1}=2
. The log discrepancy along
F_{2}
is now
\frac{2}{n_{2}}-1
. Suppose first that the ramification covers over
C_{1},C_{2}
are unramified so are given by 2 and
n_{2}
-torsion
z_{1},z_{2}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
respectively. The ramification along
F_{2}
is given by Corollary
4.4
as
2(z_{1}+z_{2})=2z_{2}
. If
n_{2}
is even, then this is
n_{2}/2
-torsion and the b-discrepancy is thus at most 0. If on the other hand
n_{2}
is odd, then we blow up the strict transform of
C_{2}
repeatedly another
n_{2}-2
times. Let
E
be the resulting
(-1)
-curve which has log discrepancy

\frac{n_{2}-2}{n_{2}}+\frac{2}{n_{2}}-1=0.

The ramification along
E
is however
n_{2}z_{2}=0
so again the localised Brauer class is not terminal.

We thus have
d=n_{1}=2
and furthermore, the ramification covers are ramified so the ramification indices
e_{1},e_{2}
of
\beta
satisfy
e_{1}=2\ |\ e_{2}
too. We are done if
C_{1}=C_{2}
, so we suppose this is not the case. Consider the map
g\colon Y\to\operatorname{Spec}R
above. Since secondary ramification must cancel, the ramification cover of
\beta
along
F_{1}
must be unramified, say given by
\zeta_{1}\in H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. We may then construct an unramified cover of
C_{2}
corresponding to this
\zeta_{1}
. By the Artin-Mumford-Saltman sequence on
\operatorname{Spec}R
, this is the ramification data of a unique Brauer class, say
\beta^{\prime}
on
\operatorname{Spec}R
. We consider
\beta-\beta^{\prime}
which will be unramified on
F_{1}
. Let
\zeta
be the ramification of
\beta-\beta^{\prime}
along
F_{2}
, which determines a cyclic ramified cover
{\widetilde{F}}_{2}\to F_{2}
. Let
g_{*}^{-1}C_{1},g_{*}^{-1}C_{2}\subset Y
be the strict transforms of
C_{1},C_{2}
which intersect
F_{2}
at distinct
\kappa
-rational points of
F_{2}
since
d=2
. Since secondary ramification of
\beta-\beta^{\prime}
has to cancel,
{\widetilde{F}}_{2}\to F_{2}
has to ramify at
g_{*}^{-1}C_{i}\cap F_{2}
with ramification index two. We factorise
{\widetilde{F}}_{2}\to F^{et}_{2}\to F_{2}
where
F^{et}_{2}
corresponds to the maximal unramified subextension. To determine
F^{et}_{2}
, we consider the Artin-Mumford-Saltman sequence on an étale neighbourhood of
F_{1}
. The secondary obstruction along
F_{1}
must vanish. But this is given by the étale local behaviour of
\zeta
at
y=F_{1}\cap F_{2}
. We see thus that
{\widetilde{F}}_{2}\to F_{2}
is completely split above
y
so
F^{et}_{2}=F_{2}
. It follows that
{\widetilde{F}}_{2}\to F_{2}
is a double cover. Let
\zeta^{\prime}_{2}
be the corresponding cohomology class of
H^{1}_{et}(K(F_{2}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Returning to
\beta
, we see that its ramification along
F_{2}
is given by
\zeta^{\prime}_{2}+2\zeta_{1}
which is
e_{2}/2
-torsion unless
e_{2}=2l
for some odd integer
l
. Now the log discrepancy of
F_{2}
is
\frac{2}{n_{2}}-1
so we must have
n_{2}=e_{2}
and
\zeta^{\prime}_{2}+2\zeta_{1}
is
e_{2}/2
-torsion. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 9.3
.

The non-normal crossing case ii) in the above theorem actually does give examples of terminal Brauer log pairs, though it is a little difficult to describe succinctly exactly when this occurs. Suppose we are given tangential curves
C_{1},C_{2}\subset\operatorname{Spec}R
of multiplicity 1 at the closed point
x
. Let
z_{i}\in H^{1}_{et}(K(C_{i}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
be 2-torsion elements corresponding to ramified covers of
C_{1},C_{2}
. By the Artin-Mumford-Saltman sequence, there exists a Brauer class
\beta\in\textup{Br}\,K(R)
with this ramification data. Similarly, there is a 2-torsion Brauer class
\beta^{\prime}
which is only ramified on
C_{1}
, and the ramification cover is unramified. Let
g\colon Y\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be the resolution in the proof of the theorem and
F_{1},F_{2}
be the exceptional curves. Now both
\beta
and
\beta+\beta^{\prime}
have ramification as described in case ii) of the theorem, but one is terminal, whilst the other is not. Indeed, we know from Corollary
4.4
, that
\beta^{\prime}
is ramified on
F_{1}
with the ramification cover there unramified of order 2. It follows that, of
\beta
and
\beta+\beta^{\prime}
, exactly one is unramified along
F_{1}
and so is not terminal. Suppose
\beta
is the one which is ramified along
F_{1}
. It is also ramified along
F_{2}
. Now
g
is a good log resolution of
(\operatorname{Spec}R,\frac{1}{2}(C_{1}+C_{2}))
and the log discrepancies of both
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are both 0. Hence
\beta
is terminal.

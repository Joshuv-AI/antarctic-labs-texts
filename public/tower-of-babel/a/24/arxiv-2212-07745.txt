Duality for Landau-Ginzburg models
Claude Sabbah

Abstract
. This article surveys various duality statements attached to a pair consisting of a smooth complex quasi-projective variety and a regular function on it.

1.
Introduction

1.a.
Comparing cohomologies of complexes of differential forms

The origin of this note is a question of Bumsig Kim: given a regular function
f
on a smooth connected complex quasi-projective variety
U
of dimension
n
, to compare two kinds of cohomologies attached to
f
together with their natural duality pairings:

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

the hypercohomology of the twisted de Rham complex
(\Omega^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{U},{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}f)
together with a variant with compact support (to be defined since it is not a complex in
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathscr{O}_{U})
) and the natural pairing between them,

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

the cohomology and cohomology with compact support of the complex
(\Omega^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{U},{\mathrm{d}}f)
, together with the Serre duality pairing between them (see e.g.
[
Har72
]
).

A first observation is that, although the first (hyper)cohomologies are finite dimensional, the second ones are not, unless some assumption on
f
is added, e.g. the critical set, which is the support of the cohomology sheaves
\mathscr{H}^{j}(\Omega^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{U},{\mathrm{d}}f)
, is compact (recall that it is anyway contained in a finite number of fibers of
f
). This leads us to choose a good projectivization of
(U,f)
as a projective morphism
f:Y\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
, so that
Y
is smooth quasi-projective and
H:=Y\smallsetminus U
is a divisor with normal crossings, and to consider suitable cohomologies on
Y
.

Furthermore, one way to compare them is to introduce a parameter
u
and to consider the twisted de Rham complex
(\Omega^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{U}[u],u{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}f)
, where, all along this note, the notation
[u]
means the tensor product
\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[u]
. The main question is then whether the hypercohomology
\boldsymbol{H}^{k}(U,(\Omega^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{U}[u],u{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}f))
of the latter complex is a free
\mathbb{C}[u]
-module of finite rank.

(i)

This property does not hold in general, as shown by the following simple example. Let
U=\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
with coordinate
t
and set
f=0
. Then

H^{1}(U,u{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}f)=\coker\Bigl[\mathbb{C}[t,u]\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{\textstyle\kern 4.0ptu\partial_{t}\kern 3.0pt}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u\partial_{t}}}{{\longrightarrow}}}{}{}\mathbb{C}[t,u]\Bigr]

and this
\mathbb{C}[u]
-module is identified with the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathbb{C}[t]
on which
u
acts by zero, hence is not of finite type.

(ii)

This property holds if
f
is
proper
, but we are mainly interested in cases where
f
is not proper.

(iii)

This property holds if we replace
\mathbb{C}[u]
with the ring of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
, meaning that the problem of comparison only occurs at the origin
u=0
(see (
1.2
)).

(iv)

As indicated above, a way to prevent the infinite dimensionality at
u=0
is to replace the twisted de Rham complex with parameter
u
by a subcomplex that solves the comparison. This can be done by choosing a good projectivization
(Y,f)
as above and by considering the twisted logarithmic complex
(\Omega^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{Y}(\log H)[u],u{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}f)
with parameter
u
(a meromorphic version of this complex, called the de Rham complex of the Brieskorn lattice, is considered in
[
SY15
,
§
8]
). This will be our starting point.

The abutment of the pairing we look for is obtained by means of a smooth projectivization
X
of
Y
such that
f
extends as a morphism
f:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
D=X\smallsetminus U
is a normal crossing divisor. The de Rham hypercohomologies
\boldsymbol{H}^{k}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}},{\mathrm{d}}))
and
\boldsymbol{H}^{k}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}},0))
have the same dimension due to Hodge degeneration. It follows that the hypercohomology of the complex
(\Omega_{X}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}[u],u{\mathrm{d}})
is
\mathbb{C}[u]
-free of finite rank: its has finite type over
\mathbb{C}[u]
and for any
u_{o}\in\mathbb{C}
, the dimension of the hypercohomology of
(\Omega_{X}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}},u_{o}{\mathrm{d}})
is independent of
u_{o}
, so that the assertion follows from Lemma
1.7
below. We can then identify
\boldsymbol{H}^{2n}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}[u],u{\mathrm{d}}))
with

H^{2n}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}},{\mathrm{d}}))\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]\simeq H^{n}(X,\Omega_{X}^{n})\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]\simeq\mathbb{C}[u].

\MakePointrait

The

(v)

One can give a
\mathscr{D}
-module-theoretic interpretation of the previous results (see Section
4

\mathscr{D}_{U}
denote the ring of algebraic differential operators on
U
with its filtration

by the order, we consider the Rees ring

For (

1.b.

If the critical set of

\mathbb{C}[u]

^
C

\mathbb{C}[u]
with the ring
\mathbb{C}[\![u]\!]
of formal power series in a suitable way, under the following condition:

1.c.

Fully-connected bond percolation on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}
David Dereudre

Abstract
We consider the bond percolation model on the lattice ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} ( d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 ) with the constraint to be fully connected. Each edge is open with probability p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) , closed with probability 1 − p 1-p and then the process is conditioned to have a unique open connected component (bounded or unbounded). The model is defined on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} by passing to the limit for a sequence of finite volume models with general boundary conditions. Several questions and problems are investigated: existence, uniqueness, phase transition, DLR equations. Our main result involves the existence of a threshold 0 < p ∗ ​ ( d ) < 1 0<p^{*}(d)<1 such that any infinite volume model is necessary the vacuum state in subcritical regime (no open edges) and is non trivial in the supercritical regime (existence of a stationary unbounded connected cluster). Bounds for p ∗ ​ ( d ) p^{*}(d) are given and show that it is drastically smaller than the standard bond percolation threshold in ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . For instance 0.128 < p ∗ ​ ( 2 ) < 0.202 0.128<p^{*}(2)<0.202 (rigorous bounds) whereas the 2D bond percolation threshold is equal to 1 / 2 1/2 .



key words:
FK-percolation, random cluster model, phase transition, FKG inequalities, DLR equations.

Introduction

In the standard bond percolation model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, all edges are independently open with probability
p\in(0,1)
and closed with probability
1-p
. Then the main questions involves the existence or not, the size, the shape of the unbounded connected component of open edges with respect to the parameter
p
. This model is abundantly studied in the percolation theory literature
[
4
,
10
]
. In the present paper we consider this bond percolation model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with the constraint to be fully connected. Heuristically it means that we condition the Bernoulli bond percolation model by the event claiming that the number of open connected components (bounded and unbounded) is equal to one. Obviously this conditioning is forbidden since that the probability measure of the event is null. To this end two rigorous ways are possible and explored here. The first one is to consider the model in a finite window (with some boundary conditions) and to pass to limit with respect to the size of the window. A second approach is to consider the existence and the description of the model via the so-called DLR (Dobrushin-Lanford-Ruelle) equations which prescribe the local conditional distributions via specifications (see equations (
1
) for details). In the following for
p\in(0,1)
we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
all stationary accumulation points for the limiting procedure and by
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
the set of all stationary Gibbs measures, solutions of DLR equations.

Our main result involves the existence of a non trivial threshold
0<p^{*}(d)<1
which depends only on the dimension
d\geq 2
such that

•

(subcritical regime) for
p<p^{*}(d)
, any thermodynamic limit does not have open edges. All edges are closed. The set
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
is reduced to the probability measure charging the null configuration (the vacuum state). Equivalently the set
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
is empty.

•

(supercritical regime) for
p>p^{*}(d)
, there exists a non trivial stationary distribution
P
belonging to
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
and
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
.

Note that in the supercritical regime the uniqueness of elements in
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
or
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
is not guaranteed in general. A uniqueness result is only given in the case
d=2
and
p\geq 1/2
. Rigorous bounds for
p^{*}(d)
are also provided (see Theorem
3
). In particular in dimension
d=2
we have
0.128<p^{*}(2)<0.202
and it is remarkable to see that it is drastically smaller than the 2D bond percolation threshold equals to
1/2
. In any dimension
d\geq 2
,
p^{*}(d)
is smaller than the standard percolation threshold
p_{c}(d)
and the lower and upper bounds are sharp enough to imply that
p^{*}(d)\sim e^{-1}p_{c}(d)
when
d\mapsto+\infty
.

Further its own interest, there are several motivations to study the fully connected bond percolation model. First this model is related to the so-called random cluster model or FK-percolation; see
[
11
]
for a general presentation on lattice and
[
3
]
for a recent version in the continuum. It is defined via the formal unnormalised density
q^{N_{cc}}
where
N_{cc}
is the number of connected component and
q>0
a positive parameter. Our setting corresponds to the random cluster model associated to the Widom-Rowlinson lattice model with
q\to 0

[
9
]
. The case
q<1
is less studied in the literature since the central and crucial FKG inequalities are lost. Our first motivation was to study the case ”
q=0
” without FKG inequalities and monotonicity and to prove a sharp phase transition phenomena as presented above. Note that phase transition results without FKG inequalities/monotonicity are rare in the literature and it still remains a general and global challenge for several models in statistical physics.

Connexions with the incipient cluster at criticality are also possible. In dimension
d=2
it is well known that the Bernoulli bond model does not percolate at criticality
p=p_{c}(2)=1/2
. The incipient cluster has been introduced to force percolation at criticality by conditioning to the existence of a connected component from
0
to infinity. As in our setting the conditioning is not possible and several strategy has been developed to give a sense to the conditional probability
[
1
,
12
,
13
]
. With our conditioning we force percolation at criticality and
partially at subcriticality as well.

The main original idea developed in the present paper is to merge the fully connected percolation model in a more general model of statistical physics on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with two parameters
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The formal Hamiltonian is given by

H=\lambda N+\mu\partial N+\infty\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}\neq 1\}},

where
N
is the number of open edges,
\partial N
is the number of closed edges sharing at least one vertex with an open edge and
N_{cc}
is the number of open clusters. The fully connected bond percolation model corresponds to the case
\lambda=\log(p/(1-p))
and
\mu=0
. Using tools of statistical physics we study the two-parameter model and prove for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
a phase transition phenomenon with respect to
\lambda
. The tools are related to the pressure function and its properties (convexity, differentiability, non-dependence of boundary conditions, etc). The sharpness of bounds for
p^{*}(d)
are also due to the connexion with this two-parameter model.

The two-parameter model has also its own interest since it is a special case of random connected weighted sub-graphs picked uniformly in a host graph. We find these kind of graphs in several domains of applied science
[
14
]
. Here the weights are simple and encoded by only two quantities (the volume and the perimeter of the connected component) but more complicated weights and other host graphs than
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
could be considered. We believe that our proof of phase transition phenomenon is robust enough to be applied for many different Hamiltonians. Note that the phase transition ”empty configuration/unbounded configuration” is relevant for application since it corresponds to the emergence of a macroscopic object inside a very large host graph.

Let us finish the introduction with a numerical illustration of the phase transition phenomenon. Using a birth-death Metropolis Hastings algorithm, we sample the fully-connected bond percolation model with free boundary condition on a 2D grid
30*30
. The simulation highlights that
p^{*}(2)
is between 0.15 and 0.2. At the middle, a simulation of the process with
p=0.2
. The monitoring control on the left gives the number of open edges during the run of the algorithm and shows that the equilibrium state seems to be attained. The monitoring control on the right is for
p=0.15
and shows that the connected component has a microscopic size with respect to the size of the window. It would disappear at the limit when the size of the window tends to infinity

2
The fully connected bond percolation model

2.1
Description of the model

We denote by
\mathcal{E}
the set of edges in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where an edge is a couple of vertices with distance one for the
L^{1}
norm. The space of configurations
\Omega
is defined by
\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{E}}
and is is equipped with the standard
\sigma
-algebra generated by cylinders. For any
\omega
in
\Omega
, we say that an edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
is open if
\omega(e)=1
; it is closed otherwise (
\omega(e)=0
). For any
A\subset\mathcal{E}
we denote by
\omega_{A}
the restriction of
\omega
to
A
(i.e. an element in
\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{A}}
) and for
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
two configurations in
\Omega
, we denote by
\omega_{A}\omega^{\prime}_{A^{c}}
the concatenation of configurations
\omega_{A}
and
\omega^{\prime}_{A^{c}}
. For any
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the set
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
denotes the edges in
\mathcal{E}
such that both extremities are in
\Lambda
. The space of configuration
\Omega_{\Lambda}
is defined by
\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}
. In the following, without any ambiguity, we denote by
\omega_{\Lambda}
an element in
\Omega_{\Lambda}
or the restriction of an element
\omega\in\Omega
to
\Omega_{\Lambda}
(i.e.
\omega_{\Lambda}=\omega_{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}
).

For any
p\in(0,1)
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{p}
(respectively
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}
) the probability measure
B(p)^{\otimes\mathcal{E}}
(respectively
\mathcal{B}(p)^{\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}
) on
\Omega
(respectively
\Omega_{\Lambda}
) where
\mathcal{B}(p)
is the standard Bernoulli distribution with parameter
p
. The set of allowed configurations is denoted by
\mathcal{A}
;

\mathcal{A}=\Big\{\text{There is only one connected component of open edges }\Big\}.

We have to give a sense to ”
\mathbb{P}_{p}(.|\mathcal{A})
”:

-The thermodynamic approach.
We consider the window
\Lambda_{n}=\{-n,n\}^{d}
and the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\Lambda_{n}}_{p}(.|\mathcal{A})
which is well defined. Since that the state space
\Omega
is compact the sequence
(\mathbb{P}^{\Lambda_{n}}_{p}(.|\mathcal{A}))_{n\geq 1}
admits accumulation points for the weak convergence of measures. Boundary conditions can also be considered in a general matter (wired, free, periodic); for the sake of simplicity the details involving the boundary conditions are given in Definition
1
below. We denote by
\mathcal{L}(p)
all possible accumulation points for all possible boundary conditions. We are mainly interested in probability measures
P\in\mathcal{L}(p)
which are invariant by translations (stationary in space). This set is denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
.

-The DLR equations approach.
We are looking for probability measures
P
on
\Omega
such that
P(\mathcal{A})=1
and such that given the outside configuration
\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}
the configuration
\omega_{\Lambda}
inside
\Lambda
is picked uniformly on
\mathcal{A}
with the distribution
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}
. That leads to the following conditional distribution for
P
-a.e.
\omega_{{\mathcal{E}^{c}_{\Lambda}}}

P(d\omega_{\Lambda}|\omega_{{\mathcal{E}^{c}_{\Lambda}}})=\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda}(\omega_{{\mathcal{E}^{c}_{\Lambda}}})}1_{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}(d\omega_{\Lambda}),

(1)

where
Z_{\Lambda}(\omega_{{\mathcal{E}^{c}_{\Lambda}}})
is the normalizing constant. The collection of equations (
1
) for all bounded
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are called DLR equations (for Dobrushin, Lanford and Ruelle). We denote by
\mathcal{G}(p)
the set of all probability measures
P
such that
P(\mathcal{A})=1
and such that DLR equations hold. The set of probability measures
P\in\mathcal{G}(p)
which are invariant by translations is denoted
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
.

Both approaches are common in statistical physics. For a non-expert reader we advice the general references
[
6
,
8
]
. Note that at this stage only the existence of element
P\in\mathcal{L}(p)
is ensured by a simple compactness argument. The set
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
,
\mathcal{G}(p)
and
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
could be empty. We will introduce a periodic boundary condition which necessary will produce stationary elements in
\mathcal{L}(p)
. So
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
will be not empty as well.

2.2
Results

Our main motivation is to study the elements and the geometry of
\mathcal{L}(p),\mathcal{L}_{s}(p),\mathcal{G}(p)
or
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
with respect to the parameter
p\in(0,1)
. Can we observe phase transition phenomena? i.e. abrupt modifications for some special critical values for
p
? Are the spaces identical and/or reduced to a singleton and/or empty?

A first natural question is to know if
\mathcal{L}(p)\neq\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
or
\mathcal{G}(p)\neq\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
. This phenomenon is well-known and identified by the statistical physics community as the symmetry breaking (here the translations would be broken). It is a general and difficult question mainly open for all models. We do not investigate it here and we will focus mainly (but not only) on
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
and
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
.

Our first result involves the connectivity of elements
P
in
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
or
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
. In ad-equation with our heuristic definition we expect that
P(\mathcal{A})=1
. It is guaranteed by definition for
P\in\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
but for
P\in\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
it is more complicated because during the thermodynamic limit two bad phenomena may occur: the unique connected component can disappear at infinity and it remains no open edge; i.e. the probability measure limit is the measure
\delta_{0^{\mathcal{E}}}
, where

0^{\mathcal{E}}\text{ is the configuration full of }0\text{ (the vacuum configuration)}.

or the unique connected component in finite volume regime splits in severals parts in the infinite volume regime. This second scenario is in fact impossible.

Theorem 1
.

For any
P\in\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
,
P(\mathcal{A}\cup\{0^{\mathcal{E}}\})=1
.

Let us give an heuristic argument explaining why the limit can be
0^{\mathcal{E}}
. If
p
is small the unique connected component produced by
\mathbb{P}^{\Lambda_{n}}_{p}(.|\mathcal{A})
has a microscopic size with respect to the size of the window and disappears at the limit. Actually
p
has to be large enough in order to produce a macroscopic connected component which survives when passing to the limit. It is natural to look for a threshold which separates the existence/non-existence of a macroscopic connected component in the thermodynamic limit. However the existence of such a threshold is not obvious since the model does not inherit any stochastic monotony with respect to
p
(FKG inequality does not hold here).

Theorem 2
.

For any
d\geq 2
, there exists a threshold
0<p^{*}(d)<1
such that

•

if
p>p^{*}(d)
, there exists
P
in
\in\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)\cap\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
with
P(\mathcal{A})=1
.

•

if
p<p^{*}(d)
,
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)=\{\delta_{0^{\mathcal{E}}}\}
.

Moreover we can prove that
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
and
\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
are almost identical excepted the possible existence of the null configuration. Actually
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)\subset\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
and for all
P\in\mathcal{L}_{s}(p)
such that
P(\mathcal{A})>0
then
P(.|\mathcal{A})\in\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
.

Note that for
p>p^{*}(d)
the unique connected component is necessary unbounded (by a stationary argument) and so percolation occurs. An experimented reader in percolation theory should be interested in comparing the threshold
p^{*}(d)
in Theorem
2
with the standard percolation threshold
0<p_{c}(d)<1
defined by

p_{c}(d)=\inf\{p>0,\,\mathbb{P}_{p}(\text{ there exists an unbounded connected component})=1\}.

(2)

We advice
[
10
]
for definition and first properties of such a threshold. In particular in the following we will use the facts that
p_{c}(2)=1/2
and
p_{c}(d)\sim 1/2d
when
d\to\infty
. Our main and original result provides sharp bounds for
p^{*}(d)
and comparison with
p_{c}(d)
.

Theorem 3
.

For any
d\geq 2

\frac{e^{\lambda_{\text{min}}^{*}(d)}}{1+e^{\lambda_{\text{min}}^{*}(d)}}\leq p^{*}(d)\leq\frac{e^{\lambda_{\text{max}}^{*}(d)}}{1+e^{\lambda_{\text{max}}^{*}(d)}},

(3)

with

\lambda_{\text{min}}^{*}(d)=-\log(2d-1)+(2d-2)\log\left(\frac{2d-2}{2d-1}\right),

\lambda_{\text{max}}^{*}(d)=-\log(p_{c}(d))+\frac{1-p_{c}(d)}{p_{c}(d)}\log(1-p_{c}(d)),

In particular, for
d=2
, since
p_{c}(2)=1/2
we obtain that

0.128<p^{*}(2)<0.202.

(4)

In the case
d=3
, the threshold
p_{c}(3)
is unknown but numerical approximation gives
p_{c}(3)\approx 0.25

[
16
]
and therefore

0.075<p^{*}(3)<0.099.

(5)

It is remarkable to note that
p^{*}(d)
is drastically smaller than
p_{c}(d)
. Actually a simple study of bounds in Theorem
3
provides the following asymptotic result.

Corollary 1
.

When
d\to\infty
the following equivalence holds

p^{*}(d)\sim e^{-1}p_{c}(d).

We do not have any reason to believe that
p^{*}(d)
would be equal to
e^{-1}p_{c}(d)
for all
d\geq 2
. However note that identities
e^{-1}p_{c}(2)=0.184
and
e^{-1}p_{c}(3)\approx 0.091
are compatible with bounds (
4
) and (
5
).

Let us now turn to a last theorem which claims that for
p
large enough the four sets
\mathcal{L}(p),\mathcal{L}_{s}(p),\mathcal{G}(p)
and
\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)
are identical and reduced to a singleton (modulo a vacuum part).

Theorem 4
.

For
d=2
and
p\geq 1/2
there exists a stationary probability measure
P
such that

\mathcal{G}(p)=\mathcal{G}_{s}(p)=\{P\}.

Moreover for any
Q\in\mathcal{L}(p)
, there exists
\alpha\in[0,1]
such that
Q=\alpha P+(1-\alpha)\delta_{0^{\mathcal{E}}}.

Note that the theorem holds for
p=1/2
corresponding to the critical setting in dimension
d=2
. So, as for the incipient cluster
[
13
]
, the forced unbounded connected component process is unique in distribution at criticality. In dimension
d\geq 3
the theorem remains true for
p
large enough but our proof would be valid only for
p
strictly larger than
p_{c}(d)
. We omit to give it. We believe that mainly
\alpha=1
in Theorem
4
but we did not succeed to remove the possible vacuum part in general. However the vacuum part could exist for
p=p^{*}(d)
corresponding to a possible liquid-gas phase transition.

2.3
Main tools and ideas

Let us present the main tools and ideas we use to prove theorems presented above. Theorem
1
is based on two steps. First we show that the connected components of any accumulation point
P\in\mathcal{L}(p)
are necessary unbounded. Then a general Burton and Keane strategy ensures that the number of unbounded connected components is equal to zero or one.

The existence of the threshold in Theorem
2
is more delicate. Recall that this model does not exhibit any natural monotonicity with respect to
p
. Actually Theorem
2
is based on a precise analysis of the function
\mathcal{P}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathcal{P}(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#(\mathcal{E}_{n})}\log(\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda_{n}}(\mathcal{A})).

Excepted scaling constants, this function is called pressure by the statistical physics community. It is common to identify phase transition phenomenon by detecting lost of analyticity of
\mathcal{P}
for special critical values of
p\in(0,1)
. We use this strategy here. In fact
\mathcal{P}
is larger or equal to
-\log(2)
and we show that that the expected threshold
p^{*}(d)
is defined by

p^{*}(d)=\inf(p\in(0,1),\mathcal{P}(p)>-\log(2)).

(6)

Combining different tools from statistical physics we show Theorem
2
.

Theorem
4
on the uniqueness of accumulation points or Gibbs measures is obtained by a coupling algorithm. This result is strongly inspired by the disagreement percolation argument to prove uniqueness of Gibbs measures
[
15
]
.

The main original part of our work involves Theorem
3
. First, using simple combinatorial arguments it is possible to obtain trivial bounds for
p^{*}(d)
. However they are not sharp as they are in Theorem
3
. Our strategy is to merge the model with one parameter
p\in(0,1)
inside a model with two parameters
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The finite volume definition on
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is given by the following distribution

Q^{\Lambda}_{\lambda,\mu}(\omega_{\Lambda}):=\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda,\mu)}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\mathcal{A}}(\omega_{\Lambda})e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})}e^{\mu\partial N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})},\quad\omega_{\Lambda}\in\Omega_{\Lambda},

(7)

where
Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda,\mu)
is the normalising constant,
N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})
is the number of open edges in
\omega_{\Lambda}
and
\partial N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})
is the number of closed edges such that at least one of its extremities belongs to an open edge of
\omega_{\Lambda}
. The quantity
N(\omega_{\Lambda})
can be viewed as the size of the open cluster whereas
\partial N(\omega_{\Lambda})
is the perimeter of the open cluster. It is easy to see that
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}(.|\mathcal{A})
corresponds to
Q^{\Lambda}_{\lambda,\mu}
with
\lambda=\log(p/(1-p))
and
\mu=0
(modulo a boundary effect). We show that for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a critical parameter
\lambda^{*}(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}
as in Theorem
2
and therefore

p^{*}(d)=\frac{e^{\lambda^{*}(0)}}{1+e^{\lambda^{*}(0)}},

which explains the special form of bounds in (
3
). The interest of this two-parameter model comes from its partial tractability since for
\mu\leq\log(1-p_{c}(d))
we have explicitly

\lambda^{*}(\mu)=\log(1-e^{\mu}).

It is exactly at this point that the standard percolation threshold
p_{c}(d)
appears. Now exploiting the convexity of the pressure of the two-parameter model and some estimates on its derivatives we obtain sharp bounds for
\lambda^{*}(0)
and the proof of Theorem
3
follows.

3
The two-parameter model

3.1
Connected components and boundary conditions

For a configuration
\omega\in\Omega
, we denote by
N_{cc}(\omega)
the number of connected components of open edges. Before defining the number of connected components for an element
\omega_{\Lambda}\in\Omega_{\Lambda}
, we introduce the notion of boundary condition in a general matter. We denote by
\partial\Lambda
the boundary of
\Lambda
(i.e. any vertex
i\in\Lambda
such that
i
has a neighbour in
\Lambda^{c}
).

Definition 1
.

For any bounded subset
\Lambda
, a boundary condition of
\Lambda
(noted ”
bc(\Lambda)
” or simply ”bc” if no ambiguity holds) is any partition of

\partial\Lambda
where each set of the partition is marked by
0
or
1
; i.e. any collection
((E_{1},\delta_{1}),(E_{2},\delta_{2}),\ldots,(E_{k},\delta_{k}))
such that
(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{k})
is a partition of
\partial\Lambda
and
\delta_{i}\in\{0,1\}
,
1\leq i\leq k
. A marked set
(E_{i},\delta_{i})
corresponds to a collection of vertices which are identified to be a single point (by considering for instance the quotient space) which is closed or open depending on the value of
\delta_{i}
(as usual,
1=
”open” and
0=
”closed”).

The free boundary condition is a partition with closed singletons; i.e.
((\{i\},0),i\in\partial\Lambda)
. The wired boundary condition is a partition with only one open set
\partial\Lambda
; i.e.
((\partial\Lambda,1))
. The left-right crossing boundary condition is a partition with two open sets (the right and left faces of a cube
\Lambda
), the other sets are closed singleton. The periodic condition is a partition with closed pairs of opposite vertices at the boundary. Several other combinations are possible as ”closed wired”, ”open periodic”, etc…

Then for any
\omega_{\Lambda}
in
\Omega_{\Lambda}
, we denote by
N_{cc}^{bc(\Lambda)}(\omega_{\Lambda})
the number of connected components in
\omega_{\Lambda}
taking account the boundary condition ”
bc(\Lambda)
”. To avoid any confusion, it is defined as the number of connected components in the following graph:

•

the vertices are the points in
\Lambda\backslash\partial\Lambda
, which belongs to an open edge in
\omega_{\Lambda}
, and any set
E
of the partition
bc(\Lambda)
which is either open or if at least one vertex of
E
belongs to an open edge in
\omega_{\Lambda}
.

•

the edges are induced by open edges in
\omega_{\Lambda}
.

A natural way for producing a boundary condition of
\Lambda
is to use the open edges of a configuration
\omega
outside
\Lambda
. Precisely, for any
\omega\in\Omega
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we denote by
bc(\Lambda,\omega)
the following boundary condition : the points at the boundary are in the same set
E
of the partition if they are connected by a path of open edges in
\omega
not belonging in
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
(they are connected from outside of
\Lambda
). Each set
E
of the partition is declared open if at least one vertex of
E
belongs to an open edge in
\omega
not belonging in
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
(it is open from outside of
\Lambda
).

Recall that the space of allowed configurations in
\Omega
is
\mathcal{A}=\{N_{cc}=1\}
. Similarly
\mathcal{A}^{bc(\Lambda)}
is the space of configurations
\{N_{cc}^{bc(\Lambda)}=1\}
in
\Omega_{\Lambda}
for the boundary condition
bc(\Lambda)
.

In the following we often use the bounded box
\Lambda_{n}=\{-n,\ldots,n\}^{d}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
n\geq 1
. In this case, we use the notations
\Omega_{n}
,
\mathcal{E}_{n}
,
N_{n}
,
bc(n)
,
N^{bc(n)}_{cc}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})
and
\mathcal{A}^{bc(n)}
in place of
\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}
,
N_{\Lambda_{n}}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{n}}
,
bc(\Lambda_{n})
,

and

3.2
Finite volume models

For any
p\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, recall that
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}
denotes the probability measure
B(p)^{\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}
on
\Omega_{\Lambda}
and so for every
\omega_{\Lambda}

\mathbb{P}^{\Lambda}_{p}(\omega_{\Lambda})=c_{\Lambda}\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)^{N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})}=c_{\Lambda}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})},

(8)

where
c_{\Lambda}:=1/(1-p)^{\#\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}
is the normalization constant,
N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})
is the number of open edges in
\omega_{\Lambda}
and
\lambda
is the parameter
\log(p/(1-p))
.

Before introducing the finite volume model, we define a last quantity providing the number of closed edges with open neighbour. For any
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, any
\omega_{\Lambda}
in
\Omega_{\Lambda}
and any boundary condition
{\text{bc}}(\Lambda)
we denote by
\partial N_{\Lambda}^{{\text{bc}}(\Lambda)}(\omega_{\Lambda})
(or simply
\partial N_{\Lambda}^{{\text{bc}}}(\omega_{\Lambda})
to avoid repetition) the number of closed edges in
\omega_{\Lambda}
such that at least one of its extremities belongs to an open edge of
\omega_{\Lambda}
or an open set
(E,1)
in the boundary condition
{\text{bc}}(\Lambda)
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a connected bounded subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, ”
{\text{bc}}(\Lambda)
” be a boundary condition and
\lambda,\mu
be two real numbers in
\mathbb{R}
. The fully-connected bond measure on
\Lambda
with parameter
(\lambda,\mu)
and boundary condition ”
bc(\Lambda)
” is the probability measure on
\Omega_{\Lambda}
defined by

Q_{\Lambda,\lambda,\mu}^{{\text{bc}}}(\omega_{\Lambda}):=\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda}^{{\text{bc}}}(\lambda,\mu)}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{{\text{bc}}(\Lambda)}(\omega_{\Lambda})=1\right\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})},

(9)

where
Z_{\Lambda}^{{\text{bc}}}(\lambda,\mu)
is the normalization constant. Note that
Z_{\Lambda}^{{\text{bc}}}(\lambda,\mu)\geq e^{\lambda\#\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}>0
since the configuration with all open edges is allowed.

With a good choice of parameters we identify two main models.

-Example 1 (infinite cluster of Bernoulli bond percolation):

For any
p\in(0,1)
, we fix
\lambda=\log(p)
and
\mu=\log(1-p)
. By a simple identification we see that the weight

\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{\text{wired}}(\omega_{\Lambda})=1\right\}}p^{N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})}(1-p)^{\partial N^{\text{wired}}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda})}

of
Q_{\Lambda,\lambda,\mu}^{\text{wired}}
is exactly the probability that the open edges of
\omega_{\Lambda}
corresponds to open clusters hitting the boundary of
\Lambda
for a Bernoulli bond percolation
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}
. In particular
Z_{\Lambda}^{\text{wired}}
is a sum of probability weights and is equal to one. In the thermodynamic limit
(i.e.
\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
),
Q_{\Lambda,\lambda,\mu}^{\text{wired}}
converges to the distribution of the infinite cluster in
\mathbb{P}_{p}
if it exists. If it does not exist
Q_{\Lambda,\lambda,\mu}^{\text{wired}}
converges to the null configuration
0^{\mathcal{E}}
(all edges are closed). See Proposition
1
for details..

-Example 2 (Fully connected bond percolation model):

For any
p\in(0,1)
, we fix
\lambda=\log(p/(1-p))
and
\mu=0
. Then the probability measure
Q_{\Lambda,\lambda,0}^{\text{bc}}
is simply the distribution of a Bernoulli bond model with parameter
p
conditionned to have a unique connected component (with respect to the boundary condition bc);

Q_{\Lambda,\lambda,0}^{\text{bc}}=\mathbb{P}^{\Lambda}_{p}(.|N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}=1).

In particular for
p=1/2
,
Q_{\Lambda,0,0}^{\text{free}}
samples randomly and uniformly a connected graph in
\Lambda
.

3.3
Thermodynamic limits

Let us now turn to the main object of the present paper; any thermodynamic limit of finite volume fully connected bond models. For simplicity, in the following the thermodynamic limits are always along the sequence of boxes
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and the boundary condition ”
bc
” denotes in fact a sequence of boundary conditions ”
({\text{bc}}(n))_{n\geq 1}
”. The probability measures
Q_{\Lambda_{n},\lambda,\mu}^{{\text{bc}}(n)}
is simply denoted by
Q_{n}^{bc}
.

Definition 3
.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{bc}(\lambda,\mu)
the set of all accumulation points of

(Q_{n}^{bc})_{n\geq 1}
(embedded in
\Omega
) for the weak convergence of measures. Any element in
\mathcal{L}^{bc}(\lambda,\mu)
is called a fully-connected bond measure with parameters
(\lambda,\mu)
and boundary condition ”bc”.
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
is the union of all
\mathcal{L}^{bc}(\lambda,\mu)
for any choice of boundary condition ”bc”. We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{bc}(\lambda,\mu)
(or
\mathcal{L}_{s}(\lambda,\mu)
) the elements of
\mathcal{L}^{bc}(\lambda,\mu)
(or
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
) which are stationary in space, meaning invariant in distribution with respect to any translation
\tau_{x}
by vector
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Since
\Omega
is compact the space
\mathcal{L}^{bc}(\lambda,\mu)
is not empty for any
\lambda
,
\mu
and sequence of boundary conditions ”
({\text{bc}}(n))_{n\geq 1}
”. The existence of elements in

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{bc}(\lambda,\mu)
for any ”bc” is more delicate and discussed below. However for the periodic boundary condition ”bc=per” the accumulations points are necessary stationary in space and so
\mathcal{L}^{per}(\lambda,\mu)
and
\mathcal{L}_{s}(\lambda,\mu)
are not empty. The following proposition provides a clear interpretation of the thermodynamic limits in the case of example 1 above.

We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\infty}
the probability on
\Omega
such that the open edges are distributed as the unique infinite open cluster in the Benoulli percolation
\mathbb{P}_{p}
, if percolation occurs. If percolation does not occur,
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\infty}
is not defined. So
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\infty}
is defined for
p>p_{c}(d)
and for
p=p_{c}(d)
if the conjecture ”
\theta(p_{c})=0
” is not true. Note that
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\infty}(\mathcal{A})=1
.

Proposition 1
.

For any
p\in(0,1)
, we fix
\lambda=\log(p)
and
\mu=\log(1-p)
.

•

If
p>p_{c}(d)
, then
\mathcal{L}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu)=\{\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\infty}\}

•

If
p<p_{c}(d)
, then
\mathcal{L}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu)=\{\delta_{0^{\mathcal{E}}}\}
.

•

If
p=p_{c}(d)
, it depends if the infinite cluster exists at criticality (conjecture ”
\theta(p_{c})=0
”). If it exists then
\mathcal{L}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu)=\{\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\infty}\}
otherwise
\mathcal{L}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu)=\{\delta_{0^{\mathcal{E}}}\}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the description of
Q_{\Lambda_{n},\lambda,\mu}^{\text{wired}}
given in example 1 which is identified as the distribution of open clusters hitting the boundary of
\Lambda_{n}
for a Bernoulli bond percolation
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda_{n}}
. So the proof of the proposition is a simple consequence of the following coupling. For any
n\geq 1
and
\omega\in\Omega
we consider the configuration
\omega_{n}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}
whom open edges are the open clusters of
\omega
inside
\Lambda_{n}
hitting the boundary of
\Lambda_{n}
. Therefore if
\omega
is distributed with respect
\mathbb{P}_{p}
then
\omega_{n}
is distributed with respect to
Q_{\Lambda_{n},\lambda,\mu}^{\text{wired}}
. It is now clear that the local limit of
\omega_{n}
, when
n\to\infty
, is the infinite open cluster of
\omega
if it exists or the vacuum configuration
0^{\mathcal{E}}
if it does not exist. We deduce that the weak limit of
Q_{\Lambda_{n},\lambda,\mu}^{\text{wired}}
when
n\to\infty
is the distribution
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\infty}
if percolation occurs or
\delta_{0^{\mathcal{E}}}
if it does not.

∎

3.4
Connectivity properties

In this section we investigate the topological properties of elements
P
in
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
.

Proposition 2
.

For any
P
in
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
,
P
-almost surely the connected components of open edges are unbounded.

Proof.

Let
P
in
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
. First we show that for
P
-almost every
\omega
, if
\omega
contains a bounded connected component then
\omega
is reduced to this bounded component (in a second step we show that it is impossible). We make a proof by contradiction by assuming the opposite: with positive probability there exist a bounded connected component and another connected component (bounded or not). So we can find a bounded set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
large enough such that

P\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\text{-There exists a connected component completly incuded in }\Lambda\\
\text{-There exists another connected component hitting }\Lambda.\end{array}\right)>0.

But this event is local with probability zero for any
Q^{bc}_{n}
. That implies a contradiction by weak convergence of
(Q^{bc}_{n})
to
P
(for a subsequence).

It remains to prove that a unique bounded connected component can not occur with positive probability. We make again a proof by contradiction. Assume that there exists a bounded set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

P\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{There exists a unique bounded connected component}\\
\text{and it is incuded in }\Lambda\end{array}\right)>0.

By the weak convergence of
(Q^{bc}_{n})
to
P
(for a subsequence) and the previous result, we deduce that there exist
\delta>0
and
n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}

Q_{n}^{{\text{bc}}}(\text{There exists a unique bounded connected component and it is incuded in }\Lambda)>\delta.

If the boundary condition has an open vertex, this implies a contradiction for
n
large enough. If the boundary condition is completely closed, we choose an integer
k
large enough such that
k\delta>1
. We choose also
n
large enough such that
\Lambda_{n}
contains
k
disjoint copies
(\Lambda^{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
obtained by translations of
\Lambda
. So by definition of
Q_{n}^{{\text{bc}}}
, we obtain for
1\leq i\leq k
,

Q_{n}^{{\text{bc}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{There exists a unique bounded connected component }\\
\text{ and it is incuded in }\Lambda^{i}\end{array}\right)>\delta.

But these
k
events are disjoint and
k\delta>1
. It is impossible.
∎

Now we investigate the number of unbounded connected components. To this end, a precious tool is the finite energy property and the general Burton and Kean argument. The model here does not satisfy exactly the finite energy property but only the following variant.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a bounded connected set in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
P\in\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
and let
A
be an event in
\Omega
measurable with respect to the sigma-field generated by events
(\{e\text{ is open}\})_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{c}_{\Lambda}}
. We assume that

P(A\cap\{\text{an edge between a vertice in }\Lambda\text{ and }\Lambda^{c}\text{ is open}\})>0.

Then

P(A\cap\{\text{ all edges in }\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}\text{ are open}\})>0.

Proof.

Let
P
be in
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
. For sake a simplicity, we note
P=\lim_{n\to\infty}Q_{n}^{bc}
in omitting the limit under a subsequence.

By the martingale convergence Theorem, the indicator function
\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}
can be approximated by local bounded functions which are measurable with respect to the sigma-field generated on
\mathcal{E}^{c}_{\Lambda}
. Indeed,
P
almost surely
\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}=\lim_{\Delta\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}}E(\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}|\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\Delta})
where
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\Delta}
is the sigma-field generated on
\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
. Then

\displaystyle P(A\cap\{\text{ all edges in }\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}\text{ are open}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}}E_{P}\left(E(\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}|\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\Delta})\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{\text{ all edges in }\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}\text{ are open}\}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{Q_{n}^{bc}}\left(E(\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}|\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\Delta})\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{\text{ all edges in }\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}\text{ are open}\}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{Q_{n}^{bc}}\left(E(\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}|\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\Delta})\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{\text{ all edges in }\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}\text{ are open}\}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{\text{an edge between a vertice in }\Lambda\text{ and }\Lambda^{c}\text{ is open}\}}\right).

Given that
\text{an edge between a vertice in }\Lambda\text{ and }\Lambda^{c}\text{ is open}
, the weight under
Q_{n}^{bc}
of configurations
\{\text{ all edges in }\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}\text{ are open}\}
is positive. Moreover there exists a constant
c>0
such that this weight is larger than
c
times the weight of all other allowed configurations. Therefore

\displaystyle P(A\cap\{\text{ all edges in }\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}\text{ are open}\})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\lim_{\Delta\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{Q_{n}^{bc}}\left(E(\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}|\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\Delta})\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{\text{an edge between a vertice in }\Lambda\text{ and }\Lambda^{c}\text{ is open}\}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\lim_{\Delta\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}}E_{P}\left(E(\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{A}|\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\Delta})\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{\text{an edge between a vertice in }\Lambda\text{ and }\Lambda^{c}\text{ is open}\}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle cP(A\cap\{\text{an edge between a vertice in }\Lambda\text{ and }\Lambda^{c}\text{ is open}\})>0.

∎

Now we obtain the following theorem which claims that in the stationary case, the number of connected components (necessary unbounded) are equal to zero or one. If it is zero then the fully-connected bond measure is the vacuum state (i.e. charging the null configuration
0^{\mathcal{E}}
).

Theorem 5
.

Let
P
be in
\mathcal{L}_{s}(\lambda,\mu)
then

P(N_{cc}=0\text{ or }1)=1.

Proof.

The proof follows the standard Burton and Keane arguments
[
2
]
for which we give only a sketch of the proof. By shift invariance we show that each ergodic phase of
\tilde{P}
(in the extremal decomposition of
P
) has
\tilde{P}
-almost surely a fix number of connected components in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. By local modification (Lemma
1
) this number can not be finite greater than two. We finish the proof by a trifurcation argument showing that this number can not be infinite.

∎

Remark. 1
.

We do not know if Theorem
5
is valid for
P\in\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
without assuming that
P
is stationary. We do not know also if such non-stationary probability measures exist.

3.5
DLR equations

In this section we investigate the DLR equations for the two-parameter model. Let us first define the Gibbs kernels. For any configuration
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{A}
, any bounded

and any

we consider the probability measure on

and moreover this extremity (or the other one) belongs to
\Lambda
. This special form for

allows to take account the full dependence of
\omega_{\Lambda}
in the computation of
\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Delta}(\tilde{\omega}_{\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}}\omega_{\Lambda})
with
\Delta
larger than
\Lambda
. Note that

depends only on
\tilde{\omega}_{L^{\oplus}}
with

Note also the difference between

and
\partial N_{\Lambda}^{\text{bc}(\tilde{\omega})}(\omega_{\Lambda})
defined before Definition
2
. As usual

is the normalization constant

(of Theorem

\Delta:=|E_{P}(f\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}=1\}})-E_{P}(f_{\Lambda}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}=1\}})|=0,

3.6
Pressure

In this section we study the pressure of the model. Let us recall that
Z_{n}^{\text{bc}}(\lambda,\mu)
is the partition function of
Q_{n}^{\text{bc}}
;

Z_{n}^{\text{bc}}(\lambda,\mu):=\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\right\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}.

Proposition 3
.

The following limit exists in
[0,+\infty)
and is called pressure with wired boundary condition

\mathcal{P}(\lambda,\mu)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}.

Moreover for any compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{K}
and any boundary condition ”bc”

\left|\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\text{bc}}(\lambda,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}-\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}

(19)

and for any
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{A}
such that at least one vertex in
\Lambda_{n}
belongs to an open edge of
\tilde{\omega}

\left|\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\tilde{\omega}}(\lambda,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}-\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}.

(20)

In particular, the pressures with boundary condition ”bc” or
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{A}
exist and are equal to the pressure with wired boundary condition.

Proof.

We first prove that the pressure with wired boundary condition exists by following a standard bloc decomposition. For any
2\leq m\leq n
, we consider the Euclidean division n=km+l with
0\leq l<m
and
k\geq 0
. Let
(\Lambda_{m-1}^{i})_{1\leq i\leq k^{d}}
be a family of
k^{d}
disjoint sets inside
\Lambda_{n}
where each
\Lambda_{m-1}^{i}
is a translation of
\Lambda_{m-1}
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}^{\text{out}}_{n,m}
the edges in
\mathcal{E}_{n}
which are not inside the boxes
(\Lambda_{m-2}^{i})_{1\leq i\leq k^{d}}
;

\mathcal{E}^{\text{out}}_{n,m}:=\mathcal{E}_{n}\backslash\left(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k^{d}}\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{m-2}^{i}}\right).

A bloc decomposition of the partition function implies

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{\text{wired}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\right\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{wired}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\\
\text{the edges }\omega_{\mathcal{E}^{\text{out}}_{n,m}}\text{ are open.}\end{subarray}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{\text{wired}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\right\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{wired}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{\lambda\#\mathcal{E}^{\text{out}}_{n,m}}e^{2dk^{d}m^{d-1}\min(\mu,0)}\prod_{1\leq i\leq k^{d}}Z_{\Lambda_{m-2}^{i}}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu),

where the combinatorial term
2dk^{d}m^{d-1}
in the exponential is the maximal number of edges in
\cup_{1\leq i\leq k^{d}}\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{m-1}^{i}}
sharing a vertex with an edge in
\mathcal{E}^{\text{out}}_{n,m}
. We denote by
N_{m}
the following limit
\#\mathcal{E}_{n}/k^{d}
when
n
goes to infinity. It is easy to see that
N_{m}
exists and that it is equivalent to
\#\mathcal{E}_{m}
when
m\to\infty
. Therefore

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}\ln(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))\geq\frac{1}{N_{m}}\left(\ln(Z_{m-2}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))-Cm^{d-1}\right),

where
C\geq 0
is a constant (depending only on
d
) taking account all boundary terms. This inequality holds for each
m\geq 2
. So, letting
m
tends to infinity

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}\ln(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))\geq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{m}}\ln(Z_{m-2}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{E}_{m}}\ln(Z_{m}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu)),

which proves that the limit exists in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
. Simple combinatorial arguments show that this limit is larger than
0
and smaller than
\log(2)+\max(0,\lambda)+\max(0,\mu)
, which excludes the case where
\mathcal{P}(\lambda,\mu)
is plus or minus infinity.

Let us now prove (
19
). The proof of (
20
) is similar excepted a detail which we provide at the end. For any
n\geq 2
we consider the following set
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
of edges in
\mathcal{E}_{n}
(the symbol
g
is related to the grid built by the edges considered in this set). An edge
e=(k,k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
if it is at the boundary (i.e.
e\notin\mathcal{E}_{n-1}
) or if at least one of the
d
coordinates
i
of
k
(or
k^{\prime}
) satisfies
i=[[\sqrt{n}[i/\sqrt{n}]]
where
[.]
denotes the integer part of a real number.

In other words the set of edges
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
fills the boundary of
\Lambda_{n}
and separate
\Lambda_{n}
with large hyperplanes (in all directions) with inter-distance around
\sqrt{n}
. The complement
\mathcal{E}_{n}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
is composed with approximatively
(2n)^{d/2}
disjoints cubes of edges with length side
\sqrt{n}
. The number of edges in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
is of order
n^{d-1/2}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{K}
. For every boundary condition bc and bc’

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{\text{bc}}(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\right\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\text{ and at least one edge of }\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}\text{ is open}\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\text{ and all edges of }\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}\text{ are closed}\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}.

If all edges of
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
are closed it means that the unique open cluster is included in one cube of the grid. The number of cubes is of order
n^{d/2}
, the number of edges in each cube if of order
n^{d/2}
and so the number of configurations in each cube is of order
2^{(n^{d/2})}
. Moreover the weight
e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}
is of order
max(1,e^{\lambda},e^{\mu})^{(n^{d/2})}
. Therefore there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that

\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\text{ and all edges of }\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}\text{ are closed}\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}\leq C_{1}^{(n^{d/2})}.

For the other sum, we compare it with the same sum but for which all edges in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
are open;

\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\text{ and all edges of }\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}\text{ are open}\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}.

For each configuration for which ”at least one edge in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
open” we associate easily a configuration for which all edges are open by opening the edges in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
which are closed. The modification of the weight
e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}
is of order a constant to the power of the cardinal of
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
. That provides a multiplicative factor
C^{(n^{d-1/2})}
for some constant
C
. Moreover the association mentioned above is not a bijection since two different configurations with ”at least one edge in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
open” can produce the same configuration after opening all edges in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
. However any configuration with ”all edges in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
are open” comes from at most

different configurations with ”at least one edge in
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}
open”. We deduce that there exists a constant
C_{2}
such

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\text{ and at least one edge of }\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}\text{ is open}\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}^{(n^{d-1/2})}\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\text{ and all edges of }\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}\text{ are open}\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}.

are open then
\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda})=1\}=\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc'}}(\omega_{\Lambda})=1\}
and
\partial N^{\text{bc}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=\partial N^{\text{bc'}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})
we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{\text{bc}}(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}^{n^{d-1/2}}\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc'}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\text{ and all edges of }\mathcal{E}_{n}^{g}\text{ are open}\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc'}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

(21)

\displaystyle+C_{1}^{n^{d/2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}^{n^{d-1/2}}\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc'}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{bc'}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

\displaystyle+C_{1}^{n^{d/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{2}^{n^{d-1/2}}Z_{n}^{\text{bc'}}(\lambda,\mu)+C_{1}^{n^{d/2}}.

Since a configuration with all edges at the boundary open and all edges in the bulk closed is allowed we deduce that
Z_{n}^{\text{bc'}}\geq C_{3}^{(n^{d-1})}
for some constant
C_{3}>0
. Therefore
C_{1}^{n^{d/2}}\leq C_{1}^{n^{d/2}}(1/C_{3})^{(n^{d-1})}Z_{n}^{\text{bc'}}
. At the end there exists a constant
C>0
such that

Z_{n}^{\text{bc}}(\lambda,\mu)\leq C^{(n^{d-1/2})}Z_{n}^{\text{bc'}}(\lambda,\mu).

(22)

Passing to the logarithm and dividing by
\#\mathcal{E}_{n}
we find that

\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\text{bc}}(\lambda,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}-\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\text{bc'}}(\lambda,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}\leq\log(C)\frac{n^{d-1/2}}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}.

(23)

Noting that

is of order

and applying twice the previous inequality with one time bc=”wired” and a second time with bc’=”wired” the inequality (

Involving the proof of (

). The scheme of the proof is exactly the same excepted we need the assumption that at least one vertex in
\Lambda_{n}
belongs to an edge of
\tilde{\omega}
. Without these assumption the first inequality in (

Indeed if the connected component of
\tilde{\omega}
does not hit

then

if and only if
\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}=0^{\mathcal{E}_{n}}
. Therefore the assertion
\{N_{cc}^{\text{bc}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\}=\{N_{cc}^{\tilde{\omega}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\}
if all edges of

are open is not true in general. This equivalence is crucial to prove (

). The rest of the proof is the same.

Let us finish this section with standard properties of the pressure function
\mathcal{P}
.

Proposition 4
.

The function (
\lambda,\mu)\to\mathcal{P}(\lambda,\mu)
is convex and non-decreasing with respect to each variable
\lambda
or
\mu
.

Proof.

Let us recall that

Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu)=\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{\text{wired}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})=1\right\}}e^{\lambda N_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{wired}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}.

So by simple calculus we have

\frac{\partial\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}})}{\partial\lambda}=E_{Q_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}}(N_{\Lambda_{n}}),\quad\frac{\partial\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}})}{\partial\mu}=E_{Q_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}}(\partial N_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}),

(24)

\frac{\partial^{2}\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}})}{\partial^{2}\lambda}=\text{Var}_{Q_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}}(N_{\Lambda_{n}}),\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}})}{\partial^{2}\mu}=\text{Var}_{Q_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}}(\partial N_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}})

and

\frac{\partial^{2}\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}})}{\partial\lambda\partial\mu}=\text{Cov}_{Q_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}}(N_{\Lambda_{n}},\partial N_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}),

which ensures that the function
(\lambda,\mu)\mapsto\log(Z_{\Lambda_{n}}^{\text{wired}}(\lambda,\mu))
is non-decreasing (with respect to each variable
\lambda
or
\mu
) and convex. Dividing by
\#\mathcal{E}_{n}
and passing to the limit, the function
(\lambda,\mu)\mapsto\mathcal{P}(\lambda,\mu))
is non-decreasing and convex as well.

∎

3.7
Existence of the threshold

In this section we show that for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}

Explicit value for the threshold

Based on the representation of the infinite cluster for a Bernoulli bond percolation model (see Proposotion

Theorem 8

For any

and

. We deduce that for all

(28)

\mu<\log(1-p_{c}(d))
and

1
and Corollary

\lambda>\log(1-e^{\mu})
small enough the pressure
\mathcal{P}(\lambda,\mu)=0
. Let
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\log(1-e^{\mu})+\alpha,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda_{n}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}}\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{\left\{N_{cc}^{\text{wired}}(\omega_{\Lambda})=1\right\}}e^{\alpha N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\log(1-e^{\mu})N_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}e^{\mu\partial N^{\text{wired}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\omega_{\Lambda_{n}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}_{p}}\left(e^{\alpha N_{n}^{\text{boundary}}}\right),

where, as usual

is the random variates counting the number of edges inside open clusters in
\Lambda_{n}
hitting the boundary
\partial\Lambda_{n}
. For every

\Lambda_{n}

and the number of edges in the clusters hitting

N_{n}^{\text{boundary}}

(N^{i})_{i\in\partial\Lambda_{n}}

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\log(1-e^{\mu})+\alpha,\mu)

\displaystyle E(e^{\alpha N^{0}})^{\#\partial\Lambda_{n}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\log(1-e^{\mu})+\alpha,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(Z_{n}^{\text{wired}}(\log(1-e^{\mu})+\alpha,\mu))}{\#\mathcal{E}_{n}}

Let us give an interpretation of the lemma. For any process
P\in\mathcal{G}(p)\cup\mathcal{L}(p)
, during an exploration procedure declaring the states of edges, if we want to explore an new edge closed to an open edge, then this new edge is open with probability at least
p
.

Let us now prove the theorem. Let
P
and
Q
be two elements in
\mathcal{G}(p)\cup\mathcal{L}(p)
. In the following, for all bounded cubes
\Lambda\subset\Delta\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we build a coupling with three processes
X
,

,

on
\Omega
such that
X\sim P
,
Y\sim Q
and
Z\sim\mathcal{B}(p)^{\otimes\mathcal{E}}
having the following coupling property:

[Coupling property:]
if
Z
has an open connected path in
\Delta
surrounding
\Lambda
then
X
and
Y
are identical in
\Lambda
or
X
(or
Y
) is null in
\Lambda
.

Before proving that this coupling exists, let us show how it allows to prove that for
p
larger than the bond percolation threshold on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(i.e.
p\geq\frac{1}{2}
) then
P(.|\{0^{\mathcal{E}}\}^{c})
and
Q(.|\{0^{\mathcal{E}}\}^{c})
have the same distribution. The proof of the theorem follows. Indeed, for a fixed bounded set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the probability, that
Z
has an open connected path in
\Delta
surrounding
\Lambda
, is going to one when
\Delta
is going to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(note that this fact is true also at the critical point

). Then realizations of

and

are either equal in
\Lambda
or one of them (or both) charges the null configuration in
\Lambda
. But given the event
\{0^{\mathcal{E}}\}^{c}
the probability under
P
or

that a realization charges the null configuration in
\Lambda
, is going to zero when
\Lambda
is going to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. So the probability that realizations under

and

are locally equal is going to one when
\Lambda
is going to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. It is enough to ensure that the probability measures

and

are the same. Recall that for

in
\mathcal{G}(p)
, by definition

, and so the set

. Now for any

Let us now give the construction of the coupling with the good properties mentioned above. We fix a collection of independent random variates
(U_{e})_{e\in\mathcal{E}}
with uniform distribution on
[0,1]
and, as usual, the process
Z
is simply defined by
(\mbox{\rm 1\kern-2.29996ptI}_{U_{e}<p})_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\Delta}}
. Now we build
X
and
Y
using the following revealment algorithm. First we realize arbitrary
X
and
Y
outside the window
\Delta
(i.e. for any edge having at least one vertex in
\Delta^{c}
) with distributions
P
and
Q
. The boundary conditions in
\Delta
are now observable.

By Proposition
2
any connected component produced under
P
or
Q
is almost surely unbounded. So if the realization of
X
(or
Y
) outside
\Delta
is null we extend
X
(or
Y
) inside
\Delta
with the null configuration as well. If the realization of
X
(or
Y
) outside
\Delta
is not null but satisfies the ”free” boundary condition (no open edge at the boundary) then the realization of
P
(or
Q
) inside
\Delta
is necessary the null configuration. So we set
X
(or
Y
) equals to the null configuration in
\Delta
as well. In any case the coupling property occurs.

Let us now build the coupling in the case where the boundary conditions for
X
and/or
Y
are not ”free”. We sample
X
(or
Y
) inside
\Delta
with the following revealment algorithm. We reveal the open edges one by one exploring the neighbourhood of open edges revealed before by the algorithm. We start from the boundary of
\Delta
where open edges are present. When we explore a new edge
e
(hitting necessary an open edge explored before), by lemma
4
, the probability that this edge
e
is open is larger than
p
. So we declare that this edge is open in
X
if the uniform variate
U_{e}
is smaller than the expected probability. We note directly that this edge
e
is open in
Z
if it is open in
X
. That is the way to couple
X
with
Z
. The order that the Algorithm uses to choose the edges during the exploration is crucial and explained now. First we choose an a priori order on edges in
\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}
to organize the exploration. At each step of the algorithm we choose a new edge in the list of edges hitting an open edge explored before. We choose it following the a priori order but we force some extra rules. The main aim is the following.

Main property of the Algorithm:
If the algorithm reveals a cluster of
Z
in
\Delta
, then it is explored only on its exterior boundary. The interior is not revealed.

Let us recall what is the exterior boundary of a finite cluster of edges
C
. The topological cluster (i.e. the union of edge-segments) splits the space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in different connected components where only one is unbounded. The exterior boundary of
C
is the set of the edges at the boundary of this unbounded connected component.

Let us describe now the extra rules:

•

Rule 1 (Clock-wise direction rule):
The algorithm always chooses the edges in the clockwise direction with respect to the edges discovered before. It means that a new explored edge does not have a non-explored edge at the anticlockwise position. This rule prevents that the new explored edge is inside a cluster of
Z
. If a cluster of
Z
is discovered, necessary the edge belongs to its exterior boundary.

•

Rule 2 (Boundary cluster of
Z
priority rule):
If a cluster of
Z
is discovered, then the algorithm explores in priority the full exterior boundary of this cluster. Precisely, after discovering the first edge of the cluster, the algorithm explores the edges around the new vertex at the boundary (in the clockwise direction as mentioned in the Rule 1). When a new edge at the boundary is discovered, it starts again a new exploration around the new vertex and so on. Actually the Algorithm explores the exterior boundary of the cluster in the clockwise direction without revealing any edges inside the cluster. Let us note that the algorithm can visit twice an exterior boundary edge if the cluster is very thinned at this edge.

•

Rule 3 (No exploration inside cluster of
Z
rule):
The Algorithm never explores inside clusters of
Z
. Thanks to Rules 1 and 2, the exterior of the boundary of a cluster of
Z
is explored first during the run of the algorithm. We add the constraint that the algorithm will never explore latter the rest of the cluster.

The Algorithm stops when all edges allowed to be explored have been explored. It is not difficult to see that the process
X
(or
Y
) is build completely excepted inside the clusters of
Z
met during the exploration. We sample arbitrary inside these clusters following the DLR description. This procedure does not depend on the distribution
P
or
Q
. So if
X
and
Y
discover the same cluster of
Z
, we use the same sampling inside the cluster. That is the way to couple
X
and
Y
. It is now not difficult to see that the ”coupling property” holds with this construction. Indeed, if
Z
has an open connected path in
\Delta
surrounding
\Lambda
then this path is included in an open cluster with exterior boundary surrounding
\Lambda
as well. So if the explorations of
X
and
Y
meet this cluster of
Z
then
X
and
Y
are identical by construction. If the exploration of
X
(or
Y
) does not meet this cluster then
X
(or
Y
) is null on
\Lambda
.

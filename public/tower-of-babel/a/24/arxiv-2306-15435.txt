Perturbation of an α \alpha -stable type stochastic process by a pseudo-gradient
Mykola Boiko, Mykhailo Osypchuk

Abstract
We consider a Markov process defined by some pseudo-differential operator of an order 1 < α < 2 1<\alpha<2 as the process generator. Using a pseudo-gradient operator, that is, the operator defined by the symbol i ​ λ ​ | λ | β − 1 i\lambda|\lambda|^{\beta-1} with some 0 < β < 1 0<\beta<1 , the perturbation of the Markov process under consideration by the pseudo-gradient with a multiplier, which is integrable at some great enough power, is constructed. Such perturbation defines a family of evolution operators, properties of which are investigated. Let the Theorem
1
assumptions hold. Then the following statements are true:

•

the operators
\mathds{T}_{st}
,
0\leq s<t
are linear and bounded on
C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

•

if
\varphi(x)\equiv 1
then
\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)\equiv 1
;

•

the family of operators
\{\mathds{T}_{st}:0\leq s<t\}
has an evolution property, that is,
\mathds{T}_{s\tau}\mathds{T}_{\tau t}=\mathds{T}_{st}
for all
0\leq s<\tau<t
;

•

\wlim_{s\uparrow t}\mathds{T}_{st}=I
, where
I
is the identical operator, i.e.,
\lim_{s\uparrow t}\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)=\varphi(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The linearity of operator
\mathds{T}_{st}
is evident. Let us prove its boundedness. If
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then using inequality (
1
) we can write (
\|\varphi\|=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(x)|
)

\displaystyle|\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)|\leq\|\varphi\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|G(s,x,t,y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\|\varphi\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{(t-s)^{\beta/\alpha}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}+\frac{(t-s)^{1-\gamma/\alpha}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|)^{d+\alpha-\gamma}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq

\displaystyle{C}_{T}\|\varphi\|

for all
0\leq s<t\leq T
and each
T>0
. Therefore the operators
\mathds{T}_{st}
are bounded.

Next, if
\varphi(x)\equiv 1
then

\displaystyle\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y+

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(\tau,z,t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y)\,\mathrm{d}z.

The function
w(s,x,t,1)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(\tau,z,t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y
,
0\leq s<t
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a solution to equation (
21
) with
w_{0}(s,x,t,1)\equiv 0
.
So,
w(s,x,t,1)\equiv 0
and
\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\equiv 1
.

Although the evolution property can be proved in a standard way (see, for example
[
10
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
16
]
), we will provide it here. For this, let us choose arbitrary
0\leq s<u<t
,
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider

\displaystyle\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)=\mathds{T}_{st}^{0}\varphi(x)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z=

\displaystyle\mathds{T}_{su}^{0}(\mathds{T}_{ut}^{0}\varphi)(x)+\int_{s}^{u}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,u,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{u}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(u,y,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z=

\displaystyle\mathds{T}_{su}^{0}(\mathds{T}_{ut}\varphi)(x)+\int_{s}^{u}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z,

where

\mathds{T}_{st}^{0}\varphi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,t,y)\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

(22)

Using (
21
), one can obtain (by changing of integration order)

w(s,x,t,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{\beta}g(s,\cdot,u,z)(x)g(u,z,t,y)\,\mathrm{d}z+

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v_{0}(s,x,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z+

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v_{0}(s,x,u,y)g(u,y,\tau,z)\,\mathrm{d}y(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z=

\displaystyle w_{0}(s,x,u,\mathds{T}_{ut}\varphi)+\int_{s}^{u}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v_{0}(s,x,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z.

Here we used the well-known relation (the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation)

g(s,x,t,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,u,z)g(u,z,t,y)\,\mathrm{d}z,

which is true for all
0\leq s<u<t
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Since equation (
21
) has a unique solution, we state that

w(s,x,t,\varphi)=w(s,x,u,\mathds{T}_{ut}\varphi)

for all
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
0\leq s<u<t
. Therefore
\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)=\mathds{T}_{su}(\mathds{T}_{ut}\varphi)(x)
and the evolution property is proved.

The last statement of this theorem follows from the following two facts: first, using (
22
) we have the equality
\wlim_{s\uparrow t}\mathds{T}_{st}^{0}=I
, and second, (note that
1\leq q<\frac{d+\alpha}{d+1}
)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(s,x,\tau,z)(b(\tau,z),w(\tau,z,t,\varphi))\,\mathrm{d}z\right|\leq

\displaystyle\|b\|_{p}^{T}N_{0,T}C_{T}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(\tau-s)^{(1-\gamma/\alpha)q}}{((\tau-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|)^{(d+\alpha-\gamma)q}}(t-\tau)^{-q\beta/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/q}\leq

\displaystyle{C}_{T}\left(\int_{s}^{t}(\tau-s)^{-(q-1)d/\alpha}(t-\tau)^{-q\beta/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{1/q}=

\displaystyle{C}_{T}(t-s)^{1-q\beta/\alpha-(q-1)d/\alpha}\to 0,\mbox{ as }s\uparrow t.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 2
.

We cannot state that the operators
\mathds{T}_{st}
keep the cone of non-negative functions. We have no proof that the function
\mathds{T}_{st}\varphi(x)
can have negative values if
\varphi(x)\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. But the example of
\alpha
-stable process and
b(t,x)\equiv b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
confirms this fact.
Exactly analogous to how it is done in
[
1
]
for the case
\beta=\alpha-1
, we can obtain the following equality

G(s,x,t,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\{i(x-y-(t-s)b|\lambda|^{\beta-1},\lambda)-c(t-s)|\lambda|^{\alpha}\}\mathrm{d}\lambda,

in our case. The function
G
here has negative values as the Fourier transform of a non-positive definite function.

Thus, the evolution operators family
\{\mathds{T}_{st}:0\leq s<t\}
does not define any of the Markov processes but only a pseudo-process possessing a ‘‘Markov property’’.

(26)

for all

, where

, in which
\hat{v}
and
\tilde{v}
are solutions to equations obtained from (
11
) replacing the function

, respectively. Relation (
11
) leads us to the equation

\displaystyle W(s,x,t,y)=W_{0}(s,x,t,y)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{0}(s,x,\tau,z),\hat{b}(s,x))W(\tau,z,t,y))\,\mathrm{d}z+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W_{0}(s,x,\tau,z)(\tilde{b}(\tau,z),\tilde{v}(\tau,z,t,y))\,\mathrm{d}z,

\displaystyle|W_{0}(s,x,t,y)\leq|\hat{b}(s,x)-\tilde{b}(s,x)|N_{\beta,T}\left(\frac{1}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\beta}}\right.+

Since

for each

I_{1}\leq\|\tilde{b}\|_{p}^{T}(2CB(1,\theta q))^{1/q}\frac{(t-s)^{\theta}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\beta}},

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\|\tilde{b}\|_{p}^{T}\left(2C\max\left(B\left(1,\theta q+q\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right),B\left(1+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)q,\theta q\right)\right)\right)^{1/q}\times

where, as it was above,

,

and

is maximum of positive constants derived from inequality (
2
) in considered four cases. Therefore

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W_{0}(s,x,\tau,z)(\tilde{b}(\tau,z),\tilde{v}(\tau,z,t,y))\,\mathrm{d}z\right|\leq C_{T}\|\tilde{b}\|_{p}^{T}|\hat{b}(s,x)-\tilde{b}(s,x)|\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\beta}}+\frac{(t-s)^{1-\beta/\alpha}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\alpha-\gamma}}\right).

Then we can write down the following

W(s,x,t,y)=W_{0}^{*}(s,x,t,y)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{0}(s,x,\tau,z),\hat{b}(s,x))W(\tau,z,t,y))\,\mathrm{d}z.

(27)

W_{k}^{*}(s,x,t,y)=\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{0}(s,x,\tau,z),\hat{b}(s,x))W_{k-1}^{*}(\tau,z,t,y))\,\mathrm{d}z\quad k=1,2,\dots.

\displaystyle|W_{0}^{*}(s,x,t,y)|\leq C_{T}(1+\|\tilde{b}\|_{p}^{T})|\hat{b}(s,x)-\tilde{b}(s,x)|\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\beta}}+\frac{(t-s)^{1-\beta/\alpha}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\alpha-\gamma}}\right).

\displaystyle|W_{k}^{*}(s,x,t,y)|\leq R_{k}|\hat{b}(s,x)|\|\hat{b}-\tilde{b}\|_{p}^{T}\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(t-s)^{k\theta}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\beta}}+\frac{(t-s)^{k\theta+1-\beta/\alpha}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\alpha-\gamma}}\right).

valid for all

, where

and

is some positive constant depended on
T
, and, for

\displaystyle(8C\max(B(1,k\theta q),B(\theta q,(k-1)\theta q+1)))^{1/q}.

Therefore the series

converges uniformly with respect to

and locally uniformly with respect to

is a solution to equation (

). In addition we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle|W(s,x,t,y)|\leq{C}_{T}\left((1+\|\tilde{b}\|_{p}^{T})|\hat{b}(s,x)-\tilde{b}(s,x)|+|\hat{b}(s,x)|\|\hat{b}-\tilde{b}\|_{p}^{T}\right)\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\beta}}+\frac{(t-s)^{1-\beta/\alpha}}{((t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|y-x|)^{d+\alpha-\gamma}}\right),

(28)

valid for all
0\leq s<t\leq T
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and each
T>0
, where
{C}_{T}
is some positive constant depended on
T
.

Using (

),(

2

∎

A black lens in bubble of nothing Preprint: TTI-MATHPHYS-15
Shinya Tomizawa, Ryotaku Suzuki

Abstract
Applying the inverse scattering method to static and bi-axisymmetric Einstein equations, we construct a non-rotating black lens inside bubble of nothing whose horizon is topologically lens space L ⁡ ( n , 1 ) = S 3 / ℤ n L(n,1)=S^{3}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n} . Using this solution, we discuss whether a static black lens can be in equilibrium by the force balance between the expansion and gravitational attraction.

I
Introduction

The studies on higher-dimensional black hole solutions to Einstein’s equations have played important roles in the microscopic derivation of black hole entropy
Strominger:1996sh
and in the fundamental research on the scenario of large extra dimensions
Argyres:1998qn
through black hole production in an accelerator.
The recent developments in solution-generation techniques enable us to find various exact solutions of higher-dimensional black holes, our understanding of them is still not enough.
For example, according to the topology theorem for five-dimensional black holes
Galloway:2005mf
;
Cai:2001su
;
Hollands:2007aj
;
Hollands:2010qy
, the topology of the spatial cross section of the event horizon must be either sphere
S^{3}
, ring
S^{1}\times S^{2}
or lens spaces
L(p,q)
, if the spacetime with commuting two rotational Killing vector fields and a timelike Killing vector field
is asymptotically flat.
As for the first two topologies, the exact solutions to vacuum Einstein’s equations
Tangherlini:1963bw
;
Myers:1986un
;
Emparan:2001wn
;
Pomeransky:2006bd
have already been found.
For lens space topology, however, it has been difficult to find a regular vacuum solution since the resultant solutions have naked singularities.

The inverse scattering method (ISM) is perhaps one of the most powerful tools to obtain exact solutions of Einstein equations with
(D-2)
Killing isometries, where
D
is a spacetime dimension.
In particular, combined with the concept of the rod structure
Harmark:2004rm
, this method has succeeded to derive five-dimensional vacuum black hole solutions.
The first example of the construction of black hole solutions by the ISM is the re-derivation of the five-dimensional Myers-Perry black hole solution
Pomeransky:2005sj
.
Next, the black ring with
S^{2}
-rotation was re-derived
Tomizawa:2005wv
by the ISM (this was first derived in Refs.
Mishima:2005id
;
Figueras:2005zp
),
but it turned out that the generation of the black ring with
S^{1}
rotation has a certain problem on how to choose the seed, since an easy choice of the seed always results in the generation of a singular solution.
The suitable seed to derive the black ring with
S^{1}
-rotation was first considered in
Iguchi:2006rd
;
Tomizawa:2006vp
.
Subsequently, the more general black ring solution with both
S^{1}
and
S^{2}
rotations was constructed by Pomerasnky and Sen’kov
Pomeransky:2006bd
.

Using the ISM, some authors attempted to construct asymptotically flat black lens solutions to the five-dimensional vacuum Einstein equations.
First, Evslin
Evslin:2008gx
attempted to construct a static black lens with the lens space topology of
L(n^{2}+1,1)
but found that curvature singularities cannot be eliminated, whereas both conical and orbifold singularities can be removed.
Moreover, by using the ISM, Chen and Teo
Chen:2008fa
constructed black hole solutions with the horizon topology of
L(n,1)=S^{3}/{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
but these solutions must have either conical singularities or naked curvature singularities.
Thus, the major obstacle in constructing a black lens solution is always the existence of naked singularities.
However, the sudden breakthrough has come from supersymmetric solutions.
Kunduri and Lucietti
Kunduri:2014kja
succeeded in the derivation of the first regular supersymmetric solution of an asymptotically flat black lens with the horizon topology of
L(2,1)=S^{3}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
This solution was further generalized to the more general supersymmetric black lens with the horizon topology
L(n,1)=S^{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\ (n\geq 3)

Tomizawa:2016kjh
;
Breunholder:2017ubu
.
Getting a useful clue from the work of Kundhuri and Lucietti, Ref
Tomizawa:2019acu
attempted to construct the vacuum solution of the black lens with
L(2,1)
without singularities but the solution has unavoidable closed timelike curves (CTCs).
Thereafter, Ref.
Lucietti:2020phh
discuss nonexistence of vacuum black lenses.

Supersymmetric black lenses carry the mass, electric charge (saturating BPS bound), two angular momenta and magnetic fluxes
Kunduri:2014kja
;
Tomizawa:2006vp
.
As is discussed in Ref.
Tomizawa:2006vp
, there exists no limit such that all the magnetic fluxes vanish.
Therefore, as for the supersymmetric solutions, the existence of the magnetic fluxes seem to play an essential role in supporting the horizon of the black lens.
In general, however, it is not clear whether such magnetic fluxes necessarily need to construct a black lens.
Recently, a different type of solutions within a class of generalized Weyl solutions, static black hole binaries and black rings in expanding bubbles of nothing, was studied in Ref.
Astorino:2022fge
, although so far equilibrium configuration of black holes in bubble had been studied in the context of Kaluza-Klein theory
Elvang:2002br
;
Tomizawa:2007mz
;
Iguchi:2007xs
.
As is well-known, an asymptotically flat, static black ring cannot be in equilibrium since the horizon collapse due to the self-gravitational force.
However, the black ring in
Astorino:2022fge
is allowed to be in static equilibrium by the balance between the expanding force of a bubble and the gravitational force, so it has no conical singularities.
This solution leads a simple, interesting question to us.
Is a non-rotating black lens in bubble of nothing allowed to be in equilibrium?
To study such a solution may enable us to know what (except for magnetic fluxes) is needed to obtain a regular black lens.
Thus, the end of this paper is to investigate whether expanding bubble of nothing admits the existence of a black lens in equilibrium.
In this paper, to derive such a solution, we apply the ISM to the five-dimensional vacuum Einstein equations with staticity and bi-axisymmetry, and construct a
1
-soliton solution by considering a static black ring inside of bubble in
Astorino:2022fge
as a seed solution.
Note that our procedure in the ISM is entirely the same as the work of Chen-Teo where the seed solution is chosen as a static black ring, namely, the only different point is the seed solution.

We organize the remaining part of this paper as follows.
In the following section
II
, under the assumptions of staticity and bi-axisymmetry, we present a vacuum solution of a non-rotating black lens with the horizon topology
L(n,1)
in bubble of nothing as a
1
-soliton solution in five dimensions by using the ISM.
In section
III
, we impose the boundary conditions under such that the spacetime has
none of curvature, conical, and orbifold singularities on the axis and horizon.
In section
IV
, we further impose no closed timelike curves (CTCs) in the domain of communication.
In section
V
, we discuss whether the non-rotating black lens in bubble of nothing indeed exist.
In section
VI
, we confirm the limit of our solution to the Chen-Teo static solution.
In the final section
VII
, we devote ourselves to the summary and discussion on our results.

II
Static black ring in bubble of nothing as a seed solution

In general, the metric for a stationary and bi-axisymmetric spacetime can be written in the canonical coordinates as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=g_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}+f(d\rho^{2}+dz^{2}),\quad(a,b=t,\phi,\psi),

(1)

where
g_{ab}
and
f
depend on only
(\rho,z)
.
The following constraint condition must be satisfied

\displaystyle{\rm det}(g_{ab})=-\rho^{2}.

(2)

According to the procedure of Chen and Teo
Chen:2008fa
, we construct the static black lens in bubble of nothing by the ISM, where the static black ring as the seed solution is replaced with the black ring in bubble of nothing
Astorino:2022fge
(see Fig.
1
on the rod structure.).

Figure 1:
Rod structure of the black ring inside bubble of nothing.

Therefore, let us start with the exact solution of the black ring in bubble of nothing to the five-dimensional vacuum Einstein equations, whose metric is given by

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm diag\ }\left(-\rho^{2}\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{0}\mu_{2}},\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\mu_{3}},\mu_{3}\right),

(3)

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{f}\frac{\mu_{3}W_{01}^{2}W_{03}W_{12}^{2}W_{23}}{W_{02}^{2}W_{13}W_{00}W_{11}W_{22}W_{33}},

(4)

where for
i,j=0,1,2,3
,

\displaystyle\mu_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{i})^{2}}-(z-z_{i}),

(5)

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\mu_{i}},

(6)

\displaystyle W_{ij}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}+\mu_{i}\mu_{j}.

(7)

First, let us remove a trivial anti-soliton with the BZ vector
(0,0,1)
at
z=z_{3}
:

\displaystyle g_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm diag\ }\left(1,1,-\frac{\bar{\mu}_{3}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)G_{0}={\rm diag\ }\left(1,1,-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\mu_{3}^{2}}\right)G_{0},

(8)

In turn, let us add back a non-trivial anti-soliton with
(0,-a,1)
to this, and then we can obtain a new
1
-soliton solution, a solution of
a static black lens in bubble of nothing
,

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho^{2}\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{0}\mu_{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{2}(\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}+4a^{2}z_{3}^{2}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}^{2}W_{13}^{2})}{\mu_{1}\mu_{3}(\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}-4a^{2}z_{3}^{2}\rho^{2}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{13}^{2})}d\phi^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle-2az_{3}\frac{2\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{03}W_{13}W_{23}W_{33}}{\mu_{3}(\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}-4a^{2}z_{3}^{2}\rho^{2}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{13}^{2})}d\phi d\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu_{1}\mu_{3}^{2}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}+4a^{2}z_{3}^{2}\rho^{4}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{13}^{2}}{\mu_{3}(\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}-4a^{2}z_{3}^{2}\rho^{2}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{13}^{2})}d\psi^{2},

and

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}\frac{\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}-4a^{2}z_{3}^{2}\rho^{2}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{13}^{2}}{\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}}.

(10)

It is easy to confirm that in the limit of
a\to 0
, this solution coincides with the static black ring in bubble of nothing
Astorino:2022fge
.
One should note that
t,\psi
is dimensionless and
\phi
has the dimension of length.
In the following section, after an appropriate coordinate transformation, we will impose the periodicity of
\phi,\psi
so that conical singularities do not exist on symmetry of axises.

Boundary conditions on the rods

2
and neither conical singularities nor orbifold singularities,
let us introduce new parameters
b:=z_{3}a
and new coordinates
(\phi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
defined by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\frac{z_{30}}{z_{30}-2b^{2}}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}+\frac{b}{z_{30}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}\right),\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\frac{z_{30}}{z_{30}-2b^{2}}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}+2b\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right),

(11)

and then the angular components are written as

\displaystyle g_{\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z_{30}}{z_{30}-2b^{2}}\left(g_{\phi\phi}+\frac{b^{2}}{z_{30}^{2}}g_{\psi\psi}+\frac{2b}{z_{30}}g_{\phi\psi}\right),

(12)

\displaystyle g_{\psi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z_{30}}{z_{30}-2b^{2}}(g_{\psi\psi}+4b^{2}g_{\phi\phi}+4bg_{\phi\psi}),

(13)

\displaystyle g_{\phi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\frac{z_{30}}{z_{30}-2b^{2}}\left[\left(1+\frac{2b^{2}}{z_{30}}\right)g_{\phi\psi}+2bg_{\phi\phi}+\frac{b}{z_{30}}g_{\psi\psi}\right],

Focusing on the two-dimensional space
\Sigma=\{(\rho,z)|\ \rho>0,-\infty<z<\infty\}

The rod structure

;
Harmark:2004rm

(D-2)

The
z
-axis (
\rho=0
) of the metric, which corresponds to a fixed point set of a certain Killing isometry, is decomposed into five parts,
I_{-}=\{(\rho,z)\ |\ \rho=0,\ z<z_{0}\}
,
I_{0}=\{(\rho,z)\ |\ \rho=0,\ z_{0}<z<z_{1}\}

I_{1}=\{(\rho,z)\ |\ \rho=0,\ z_{1}<z<z_{2}\}

Thus, the boundary
\partial\Sigma
of
\Sigma

I_{\pm},I_{i}\ (i=0,\ldots,2)
and the asymptotic region
I_{\infty}=\{(\rho,z)|\ \sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}\to\infty\
with
z/\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}
finite
\}
.

2

I_{3}=\{(\rho,z)\ |\ \rho=0,\ z>z_{3}\}

\displaystyle C_{f}=\frac{z_{30}}{z_{30}-2b^{2}}.

(16)

(2)

I_{0}=\{(\rho,z)\ |\ z_{0}<z<z_{1},\rho=0\}

The Killing vector

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\sqrt{\frac{\rho^{2}f_{1}}{g_{\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}}}}=\frac{\Delta\phi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\Longleftrightarrow\frac{2C_{f}z_{10}^{2}(z_{30}-2b^{2})}{z_{20}^{2}}=\left(\frac{\Delta\phi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}.

(17)

(3)

I_{2}=\{(\rho,z)\ |\ z_{2}<z<z_{3},\rho=0\}

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\sqrt{\frac{\rho^{2}f_{1}}{g_{1ab}v_{23}^{a}v_{23}^{b}}}=\frac{\Delta\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\Longleftrightarrow\frac{2C_{f}(z_{30}z_{32}-2b^{2}z_{31})^{2}}{z_{32}z_{31}(z_{30}-2b^{2})}=\left(\frac{\Delta\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}.

(18)

\phi^{\prime}
and
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}

\varphi
and
\tilde{\varphi}
with

periodicity by

and
\tilde{\varphi}:=L\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}

16
), the conditions (
17
) and (
18
) can be written as,

\displaystyle(\ref{eq:conical_01})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{2z_{10}^{2}z_{30}}{z_{20}^{2}}=L^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta\varphi}{2\pi}\right)^{2}

(19)

\displaystyle(\ref{eq:conical_23})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{2z_{30}(z_{30}z_{32}-2b^{2}z_{31})^{2}}{z_{32}z_{31}(z_{30}-2b^{2})^{2}}=L^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta\tilde{\varphi}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}.

(20)

Moreover, we impose the boundary condition on the parameters
(z_{i},b)
so that the spatial topology of the horizon is lens space
L(n;1)=S^{3}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
(

.

IV
CTCs

We require absence of CTCs on
\Sigma\cup\partial\Sigma
.
The necessary and sufficient conditions to ensure that CTCs do not exist on
\Sigma\cup\partial\Sigma
is such that
g_{\phi\phi}
and
g_{\psi\psi}
(or
g_{\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}}
and
g_{\psi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}}
) become nonnegative in the region:

The condition for the absence of CTCs is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}-4b^{2}\rho^{2}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{13}^{2}>0,

(22)

which imposes an upper bound for
b^{2}
at each point

\displaystyle b^{2}<U(\rho,z):=\frac{\mu_{1}W_{03}^{2}W_{23}^{2}}{4\rho^{2}\mu_{0}\mu_{2}W_{13}^{2}}.

(23)

Therefore, if the minimum
U_{min}
of
U(\rho,z)
exists on
\Sigma\cup\partial\Sigma
and
b^{2}<U_{min}
holds, CTCs do not exist in the region.
To prove this, we show that the function
U(\rho,z)
has a minimum at
(\rho,z)=(0,z_{3})
on the rod
I_{\pm},I_{i}\ (i=0,\ldots,2)
.

It is not difficult to show that the function
U(\rho,z)
has a minimum not on
\Sigma
but on
\partial\Sigma
.
To see this, one should note that the norm of the gradient can be written as

\displaystyle(\partial_{\rho}U)^{2}+(\partial_{z}U)^{2}=\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}W_{03}^{4}W_{23}^{4}}{4\rho^{6}\mu_{0}^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}W_{00}W_{11}W_{22}W_{13}^{4}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left((\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})^{2}\rho^{4}+2\mu_{0}\mu_{2}(2\mu_{0}\mu_{2}-(\mu_{0}+\mu_{2})\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}^{2})\rho^{2}+\mu_{0}^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}\right),

(24)

where the first line is always positive for
\rho>0
, and the second line is also positive since

\displaystyle(\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})^{2}\rho^{4}+2\mu_{0}\mu_{2}(\mu_{1}^{2}-(\mu_{0}+\mu_{2})\mu_{1}+2\mu_{0}\mu_{2})\rho^{2}+\mu_{0}^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\right)\rho^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{2}\left(\mu_{1}^{2}-(\mu_{0}+\mu_{2})\mu_{1}+2\mu_{0}\mu_{2}\right)}{\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{4\mu_{0}^{3}\mu_{2}^{3}\left(\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}\right)\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)}{\left(\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\right){}^{2}},

(25)

where the positivity of the last term can be shown from

\displaystyle(\mu_{0}-\mu_{1})(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{z_{10}z_{21}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{0})(\mu_{2}+\mu_{1})}{(\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{1})^{2}}+\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{0})^{2}})(\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{2})^{2}}+\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{1})^{2}})}>0.

(26)

Therefore, the gradient of a smooth function
U(\rho,z)
cannot be zero on
\Sigma
, which means that
U(\rho,z)

must have a minimum not on
\Sigma
but on
\partial\Sigma
.
Hence, in what follows, let us consider a minimum of
U(\rho,z)
on
\partial\Sigma
, which corresponds to
I_{i}\ (i=\pm,0,\ldots,2),I_{\infty}
.

First, let us consider the minimum of
U(0,z)
on the rod
\rho=0
, i.e., on
I_{i}\ (i=\pm,0,\ldots,2)
.
On
I_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle U(0,z)=\frac{(z-z_{0})(z-z_{2})}{2(z-z_{1})},\quad U_{,z}(0,z)=\frac{(z-z_{1})^{2}+z_{10}z_{21}}{2(z-z_{1})^{2}}>0,

(27)

and hence the monotonically increasing function
U(0,z)
on
I_{+}
has a minimum at
z=z_{3}
, which is written as

\displaystyle U(0,z_{3})=\frac{z_{30}z_{32}}{2z_{31}}.

(28)

On the other hand, since on
I_{2}

\displaystyle U(0,z)=\frac{(z-z_{1})z_{30}^{2}z_{32}^{2}}{2(z-z_{0})(z-z_{2})z_{31}^{2}},\quad U_{,z}(0,z)=-\frac{[(z-z_{1})^{2}+z_{10}z_{21}]z_{30}^{2}z_{32}^{2}}{2(z-z_{0})^{2}(z-z_{2})^{2}z_{31}^{2}}<0,

(29)

the monotonically decreasing function
U(0,z)
on
I_{2}
has a minimum at
z=z_{3}
, and hence

\displaystyle U(0,z)\geq U(0,z_{3}).

(30)

Moreover, observing on
I_{0}
,

\displaystyle U(0,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(z-z_{2})z_{30}^{2}}{2(z-z_{0})(z-z_{1})},

(31)

\displaystyle U_{,z}(0,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{[(z-z_{2})^{2}-z_{20}z_{21}]z_{30}^{2}}{2(z-z_{0})^{2}(z-z_{1})^{2}}\begin{cases}<0&(z_{0}<z<z_{2}-\sqrt{z_{20}z_{21}})\\
>0&(z_{2}-\sqrt{z_{20}z_{21}}<z<z_{1}),\end{cases}

(32)

we find that the function
U(0,z)
on
I_{0}
has a local minimum at
z=z_{*}:=z_{2}-\sqrt{z_{20}z_{21}}
, and hence

\displaystyle U(0,z)\geq U(0,z_{*})=\frac{z_{30}^{2}}{2(z_{20}+z_{21}-2\sqrt{z_{20}z_{21}})},

(33)

where we note that the ratio of these minima on
I_{0},I_{2},I_{+}
is computed as

\displaystyle\frac{U(0,z_{*})}{U(0,z_{3})}=\left(1+\frac{z_{21}}{z_{32}}\right)\left(1+\frac{z_{32}}{z_{20}}\right)\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{z_{21}}{z_{20}}}\right)^{-2}>1.

(34)

Furthermore, near
I_{-}
and
I_{1}
, the function
U(\rho,z)
behaves as, respectively,

\displaystyle U(\rho,z)\simeq\frac{2(z_{0}-z)(z_{2}-z)(z_{3}-z)^{2}}{(z_{1}-z)\rho^{2}},\quad U(\rho,z)\simeq\frac{2(z-z_{1})(z_{2}-z)(z_{3}-z)^{2}z_{30}^{2}}{(z-z_{0})z_{31}^{2}\rho^{2}},

(35)

which implies
U(0,z)=\infty
on
I_{-}
and
I_{1}
.
To summarize, we have shown that the minimum of
U(0,z)
on
I_{i}\ (i=\pm,0,\ldots,2)
is given by Eq. (
28
).

Next, let us consider the function
U(\rho,z)
in the asymptotic region, namely, on
I_{\infty}
.
We can show that in the asymptotic region,
U(\rho,z)
behaves as

\displaystyle U(\rho,z)\simeq\frac{1}{1+\frac{z}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}}\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}.

(36)

Hence, the function
U(\rho,z)
diverges on
I_{\infty}
, so it cannot have a minimum on
I_{\infty}
.

Thus, we conclude that Eq. (
28
) is also a minimum on
\Sigma\cup\partial\Sigma
as well as on
\partial\Sigma
, and hence
the necessary and sufficient condition for the absence of CTCs is given by

\displaystyle b^{2}<\frac{z_{30}z_{32}}{2z_{31}}.

(37)

V
On existence of solutions

From the discussion in Sec
III
and
IV
, we have shown that the absence of conical/orbifold singularities, the black lens condition require

\displaystyle\frac{2z_{10}^{2}z_{30}}{z_{20}^{2}}=L^{2},

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{2z_{30}(z_{32}z_{30}-2b^{2}z_{31})^{2}}{z_{32}z_{31}(z_{30}-2b^{2})^{2}}=L^{2},

(39)

\displaystyle\frac{Lbz_{21}}{z_{30}z_{32}-2b^{2}z_{31}}=n,

(40)

Now, to confirm whether there is really a parameter range such that all these conditions can be satisfied, we reparameterize the rod interval
z_{i,i-1}:=z_{i}-z_{i-1}\ (i=1,2,3)
and the redefined BZ parameter
b
as follows

\displaystyle z_{10}=\ell,\quad z_{21}:=x\,\ell,\quad z_{32}=y\,\ell,\quad L=\sqrt{\ell}\,\hat{L},\quad b=\sqrt{\ell}\,\hat{b},

(41)

where
\ell
fixes the size of the bubble on
I_{0}
,

x
and
y
are the size of the horizon and the distance between the horizon and the center which is often called nut.
All the dimensionless parameters but
n
,
x,y,\ell,\hat{L},\hat{b}
, are assumed to be positive.
The condition for avoiding CTCs is now given by

\displaystyle b^{2}-\frac{y(1+x+y)}{2(x+y)}<0.

(42)

From Eq. (
40
), we have

\displaystyle\hat{L}=\frac{n(y^{2}-2\tilde{b}^{2}(x+y)+y(1+x))}{\hat{b}\,x}.

(43)

Eliminating
\hat{L}
from Eqs. (
38
) and (
39
) in terms of Eq.(
43
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0=2\hat{b}^{2}(x+y+1)\left\{2n^{2}(x+1)^{2}y^{2}+2n^{2}(x+1)^{2}xy+x^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-4\hat{b}^{4}n^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x+y)^{2}-n^{2}(x+1)^{2}y^{2}(x+y+1)^{2},

(44)

and

\displaystyle 0=\left\{y(x+y+1)-2\hat{b}^{2}(x+y)\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left[4\hat{b}^{4}n^{2}y(x+y)-2\hat{b}^{2}(x+y+1)\left\{2n^{2}y(x+y)+x^{2}\right\}+n^{2}y(x+y)(x+y+1)^{2}\right].

(45)

First, we consider the conical singularity free condition on
I_{2}
in Eq. (
45
), which admits three branches for
\hat{b}^{2}

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\pm}^{2}:=\frac{(x+y+1)\left(x^{2}+2n^{2}(x+y)y\pm x\sqrt{x^{2}+4n^{2}xy+4n^{2}y^{2}}\right)}{4n^{2}(x+y)y},

(46)

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{0}^{2}:=\frac{y(x+y+1)}{2(x+y)}.

(47)

From Eq. (
46
), we can show

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\pm}^{2}-\frac{y(1+x+y)}{2(x+y)}=\frac{x(x+y+1)\left(x+2n^{2}y\pm\sqrt{x^{2}+4n^{2}xy+4n^{2}y^{2}}\right)}{4n^{2}(x+y)y}>0,

(48)

where we note

\displaystyle(x+2n^{2}y)^{2}-(x^{2}+4n^{2}xy+4n^{2}y^{2})=4n^{2}(n^{2}-1)y^{2}>0.

(49)

Therefore, this shows that the nonexistence condition of CTCs, (
42
), cannot be satisfied for any
x>0
and
y>0
.
On the other hand, substituting Eq. (
47
) into Eq. (
43
), we can show

\displaystyle\hat{L}=0,

(50)

which cannot satisfy
\hat{L}>0
.
Hence, the solution without the conical singularity on
I_{2}
cannot avoid CTCs.

Next, we consider the conical singularity free condition on
I_{0}
in Eq. (
44
), from which we can obtain two branches for
\hat{b}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{2}=\tilde{b}^{2}_{\pm}:=\frac{(x+y+1)\left\{x^{2}+2n^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x+y)y\pm x\sqrt{x^{2}+4n^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x+y)y}\right\}}{4n^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x+y)^{2}},

(51)

which leads to

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{\pm}^{2}-\frac{y(1+x+y)}{2(x+y)}=\frac{x(x+y+1)\left(x\pm\sqrt{x^{2}+4n^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x+y)y}\right)}{4n^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x+y)^{2}}.

(52)

From these, we find that only the branch

can satisfy the nonexistence condition of CTCs (

).

In summary, if one imposes the absence of conical singularities on the whole symmetry of axis

, the presence of CTCs cannot be avoided around the center

.
However, if one imposes it only on

and

for

Here, we confirm that our solution coincides with the asymptotically flat, static black lens solution by Chen-Teo

in a certain scaling limit, for which the following variables are used,

\displaystyle z_{0}=-\lambda^{2},\quad z_{1}=-\lambda c\kappa^{2},\quad z_{2}=\lambda c\kappa^{2},\quad z_{3}=\lambda\kappa^{2},\quad L=\sqrt{2}\lambda\bar{L},\quad a=\frac{\bar{a}}{\sqrt{2}\kappa^{2}}.

(53)

With the rescaled coordinates,

\displaystyle\rho\to\lambda\bar{\rho},\quad z\to\lambda\bar{z},

(54)

and the rescaled parameters,

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{0}:=z_{1}/\lambda=-c\kappa^{2},\quad\bar{z}_{1}:=z_{2}/\lambda=c\kappa^{2},\quad\bar{z}_{1}:=z_{3}/\lambda=\kappa^{2}

(55)

the limit
\lambda\to\infty
pushes
z_{0}
away to
-\infty
, and one can see that the rod structure in
(\bar{\rho},\bar{z})
recovers that of the static black lens in Ref.
Chen:2008fa
.
In the limit
\lambda\to\infty
, Eq. (
38
) requires

\displaystyle\bar{L}=1.

(56)

and then, Eqs. (
39
) and (
40
) leads, respectively, to the nonexistence condition of the conical singularities on
\bar{z}\in(\bar{z}_{2},\bar{z}_{3})
and the condition of horizon topology

in Ref.

\displaystyle\frac{(1-c-\bar{a}^{2}(1+c))^{2}}{(1-\bar{a}^{2})^{2}(1-c^{2})}=1,\quad\frac{2\bar{a}c}{1-c-\bar{a}^{2}(1+c)}=n,

where
\bar{a}
corresponds to

in Ref.

Moreover, the limit of the nonexistence condition for CTCs (

\displaystyle\bar{a}^{2}<\frac{c-1}{c+1},

VII
Summary and Discussions

In this paper, using the ISM for static and bi-axisymmetric Einstein equations, we have constructed the non-rotating black lens inside a bubble of nothing whose horizon is topologically lens space
L(n,1)=S^{3}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.
Our work is entirely the parallel to the work of Chen-Teo
Chen:2008fa
, where the static black ring as a seed solution is replaced with a static black ring in bubble of nothing
Astorino:2022fge
.
Using this solution, we have investigated whether a static black lens can be in equilibrium by the force balance between the expansion and gravitational attraction.
If we require the absence of CTCs in the domain of outer communication,
the non-rotating black lens must have conical singularities between the horizon and the center.
It has been shown, however, that for black lens, the existence of expanding bubble does not exclude conical singularities and hence the two forces, the force of the bubble expansion and gravitational attraction, cannot be in static equilibrium unlike for the black ring.

Refs.
Chen:2008fa
;
Tomizawa:2019acu
have argued whether a rotating black lens can be in equilibrium by the balance between
the gravitational force (attraction) and the centrifugal force (repulsive force), and concluded that it cannot be in equilibrium.
The generalization of this rotating black lens solution to one in expanding bubble may be an interesting issue,
since whether such a black lens without conical singularities exists depends on the balance among the gravitational force, the centrifugal force and the expansion of the bubble.
Such a rotating black lens solution is expected to be two or more than soliton solution.
This deserves our future work.

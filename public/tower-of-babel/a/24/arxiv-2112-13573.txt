The generalized uncertainty principle effect in acoustic black holes
M. A. Anacleto, F. A. Brito, G. C. Luna, E. Passos

Abstract
We obtain an effective acoustic metric with quantum corrections that are provided by a minimum length implemented by the generalized Heisenberg uncertainty principle (GUP) in the Abelian Higgs model. The effective acoustic metric now depends on the contribution of scalar and vector potentials. We also explore the Hawking radiation and entropy by considering the effective canonical acoustic black hole and find that the modified Hawking temperature leads to logarithm corrections to the entropy. Finally, we investigate the dispersion relations of the model to establish the relationships among the deviations of the group velocity, frequency and temperature due to the GUP.

II
GUP-Corrected Acoustic Metric

In this section, we consider the Abelian Higgs model that describes high energy physics in order to derive a GUP-corrected acoustic black hole metric.
This study is motivated by the fact that acoustic black holes could be formed in high energy physical processes, such as quark matters, neutron stars and quark gluon plasma.
Thus, it seems to be natural to look for acoustic black holes in a quark gluon plasma fluid with GUP correction in this regime.
In addition acoustic phenomena in quark gluon plasma
matter was investigated in
Casalderrey-Solana:2004fdk
and acoustic black holes
in a plasma fluid was explored in
GarciadeAndrade:2008kz
.
Thus, we start with the following Lagrangian of the Abelian Higgs model:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\left|\left(\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}\right)\phi\right|^{2}+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}-b|\phi|^{4},

(2)

where
A_{\mu}
is the gauge field
and
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the field strength tensor. The model deals with a charged fluid since the Lagrangian is invariant under
U(1)
gauge symmetry
which allows a minimal coupling between the complex scalar field
\phi
and the gauge field
A_{\mu}
via Noether current and coupling constant
e
.

Let us first consider only the free scalar field sector given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\left|\partial_{\mu}\phi\right|^{2}+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}.

(3)

In order to obtain an effective acoustic metric,
we introduce the contribution of the minimum length in the above Lagrangian by means of the following prescription
Quesne:2006fs
;
Dias:2016lkg
;
HoffDaSilva:2020uov
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\rightarrow\left(1+\beta\square\right)\partial_{\mu},

(4)

where
\beta
is the deformation parameter.
By substituting (
4
) into (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\left|\partial_{\mu}\phi\right|^{2}+2\beta\square\left|\partial_{\mu}\phi\right|^{2}+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}.

(5)

Here we keep the terms up to first order in
\beta
.
Thus, we obtain the modified Klein-Gordon equation given by

\displaystyle\square\phi+2\beta\square^{2}\phi-m^{2}\phi=0.

(6)

Now isolating the term
\square\phi
in (
6
) and replacing it in (
4
), we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\left|\left(1+\beta m^{2}\right)\partial_{\mu}\phi\right|^{2}+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}.

(7)

So the new contribution of the derivative is

\partial_{\mu}\rightarrow\left(1+\beta m^{2}\right)\partial_{\mu}
.
Now we replace this new contribution of the derivative in the Lagrangian (
2
) and so we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}(1+2\beta m^{2})+\left|\left[\left(1+\beta m^{2}\right)\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}\right]\phi\right|^{2}+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}-b|\phi|^{4}.

(8)

Using the Madelung representation for the scalar field

\phi=\sqrt{\rho\left(\vec{x},t\right)}e^{iS\left(\vec{x},t\right)}
,
the Lagrangian becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}(1+2\beta m^{2})+\frac{1}{4\rho}\partial_{\mu}\rho\partial^{\mu}\rho\left(1+2\beta m^{2}\right)+\left(1+2\beta m^{2}\right)\rho\partial_{\mu}S\partial^{\mu}S

(9)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\left(1+\beta m^{2}\right)e\rho A^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}S+e^{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}\rho+m^{2}\rho-b\rho^{2},

and the equations of motion for
S
and
\rho
are

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left[\rho(\partial^{\mu}S-eA^{\mu})\left(1+2\beta m^{2}\right)+\beta m^{2}e\rho A^{\mu}\right]=0,

(10)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\sqrt{\rho}\,(1+2\beta m^{2})-\left(\partial_{\mu}S-eA_{\mu}\right)^{2}(1+2\beta m^{2})-2\beta m^{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}S-eA_{\mu}\right)eA^{\mu}-m^{2}+2b\rho=0.

(11)

The Eq. (
10
) is the continuity equation and Eq. (
11
) is an equation describing a hydrodynamical fluid
with a quantum potential term
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\sqrt{\rho}\,(1+2\beta m^{2})
which can be negligible in the hydrodynamic region.
However, in this regime the model still describes relativistic fluids.
On the other hand, one can also apply the Madelung representation of the condensate wave function in Abelian Higgs model and in relativistic Bose-Einstein condensate as has been argued in
Ge:2010wx
and
Fagnocchi:2010sn
;
Giacomelli:2017eze
, respectively.
Thus, just as in the Schroedinger equation, this approach yields the continuity and the Euler equation applied to an irrotational and inviscid fluid in the absence of the quantum potential.

For the gauge field
A_{\mu}
, we find

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}(1+2\beta m^{2})=2e\rho(1+\beta m^{2})u^{\nu}+2\beta m^{2}e^{2}\rho A^{\nu},

(12)

or

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=2e\rho(1-\beta m^{2})u^{\nu}+2\beta m^{2}e^{2}\rho A^{\nu},

(13)

where
u^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}S-eA^{\mu}=(-\omega,-v^{i})
.

Next we make the following perturbation in the equations of motion (
10
) and (
11
):

\displaystyle S=S_{0}+\epsilon S_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(14)

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{0}+\epsilon\rho_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(15)

so that we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left\{\rho_{1}(1+2\alpha)u_{0}^{\mu}+\rho_{1}\alpha eA^{\mu}+\rho_{0}\left(1+2\alpha\right)\partial^{\mu}S_{1}\right\}=0,

(16)

and

\displaystyle(1+2\alpha)u_{0}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}S_{1}+\alpha eA^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}S_{1}-b\rho_{1}=0.

(17)

For simplicity we have defined
u_{0}^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}S_{0}-eA^{\mu}
and

\alpha=\beta m^{2}
is the dimensionless GUP parameter.
Now, solving (
17
) for
\rho_{1}
and substituting into (
16
), we find

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left[u^{\mu}_{0}u^{\nu}_{0}(1+4\alpha)+\alpha eu^{\mu}_{0}A^{\nu}+\alpha eA^{\mu}u^{\nu}_{0}+b\rho_{0}\left(1+2\alpha\right)g^{\mu\nu}\right]\partial_{\nu}S_{1}=0.

(18)

We can also write the above equation as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\{\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{0}\left[-1-4\alpha+\frac{2\alpha eA_{t}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}-\frac{b\rho_{0}}{\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{0}}\left(1+2\alpha\right)\right]\partial_{t}S_{1}+\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{0}\left[-\frac{v^{i}_{0}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\left(1+4\alpha-\frac{eA_{t}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\right)+\alpha\frac{eA^{i}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\right]\partial_{i}S_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle+\partial_{i}\left\{\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{0}\left[-\frac{v^{i}_{0}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\left(1+4\alpha-\frac{eA_{t}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\right)+\alpha\frac{eA^{i}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\right]\partial_{t}S_{1}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{0}\left[-(1+4\alpha)\frac{v^{i}_{0}v^{j}_{0}}{\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{0}}+\alpha\left(v^{i}\frac{eA^{j}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}+v^{j}\frac{eA^{i}}{\tilde{\omega}_{0}}\right)+\frac{b\rho_{0}}{\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{0}}\left(1+2\alpha\right)\delta^{ij}\right]\partial_{j}S_{1}\right\}=0,

(19)

where
\tilde{\omega}_{0}=eA_{t}+\omega_{0}
,
\omega_{0}=-\partial^{t}S_{0}
and
v_{0}^{i}=\partial_{i}S_{0}+eA^{i}
(the local velocity field).
In addition, at the weak field limit, we define

c^{2}_{s}=b\rho_{0}/\omega^{2}_{0}
,

v^{i}=v^{i}_{0}/\omega_{0}
,

\Phi=A_{t}/\omega_{0}
and
\Lambda^{i}=A^{i}/\omega_{0}
. In order to identify an acoustic metric, the last three quantities should be recognized as the vector velocity, electric scalar potential and magnetic vector potential, respectively.
So the equation (
II
) becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\{\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}^{2}}\left[-1-4\alpha+2\alpha e\Phi-c_{s}^{2}\left(1+2\alpha\right)\right]\partial_{t}S_{1}+\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}^{2}}\left[-(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)v^{i}+\alpha e\Lambda^{i}\right]\partial_{i}S_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle+\,\partial_{i}\left\{\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}^{2}}\left[-(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)v^{i}+\alpha e\Lambda^{i}\right]\partial_{t}S_{1}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}^{2}}\left[-(1+4\alpha)v^{i}v^{j}+\alpha e\Lambda^{j}v^{i}+\alpha v^{j}e\Lambda^{i}+c_{s}^{2}\left(1+2\alpha\right)\delta^{ij}\right]\partial_{j}S_{1}\right\}=0.

(20)

We can now write the above equation as the equation of the fluctuations in a (3+1) dimensional curved space as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\right)\psi=0,

(21)

where

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c^{2}_{s}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1-c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)-2\alpha(2-e\Phi)&\vdots&-v^{i}(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)+\alpha e\Lambda^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)+\alpha e\Lambda^{j}&\vdots&c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)\delta^{ij}-v^{i}v^{j}(1+4\alpha)+\alpha v^{i}e\Lambda^{j}+\alpha e\Lambda^{i}v^{j}\end{array}\right).

Therefore, the effective relativistic acoustic metric is given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)+v^{2}(1+4\alpha)-2\alpha e\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\Lambda}&\vdots&-v^{i}(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)+\alpha e\Lambda^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)+\alpha e\Lambda^{j}&\vdots&[1+c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)-2\alpha(e\Phi-2)]\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=(1+c^{2}_{s}-v^{2})+2\alpha(e\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\Lambda}-3v^{2}-e\Phi+3)+4\alpha c^{2}_{s}.

(30)

Note that we have obtained a modified relativistic acoustic metric with new terms emerging. Especially the terms generated due to the scalar potential
\Phi
and the vector potential
\vec{\Lambda}
.
Furthermore, in the absence of potentials, i.e., by taking
e=0
, we find the relativistic acoustic metric with GUP corrections, given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)+v^{2}(1+4\alpha)&\vdots&-v^{i}(1+4\alpha)\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}(1+4\alpha)&\vdots&[1+4\alpha+c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)]\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=(1+c^{2}_{s}-v^{2})+2\alpha(3+2c^{2}_{s}-3v^{2}).

(35)

In addition, for
\alpha=0
, we have

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}\sqrt{1+c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c^{2}_{s}+v^{2}&\vdots&-v^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}&\vdots&(1+c^{2}_{s})\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right),

such that the relativistic acoustic metric obtained in
Ge:2010wx
is recovered.
However, in the non-relativistic limit the metric (
II
) becomes

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}\sqrt{f}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)+v^{2}(1+4\alpha)-2\alpha e\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\Lambda}&\vdots&-v^{i}(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)+\alpha e\Lambda^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)+\alpha e\Lambda^{j}&\vdots&[1-2\alpha(e\Phi-2)]\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle f=1+6\alpha+2\alpha(e\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\Lambda}-e\Phi).

(44)

So for
e=0
in the metric above, we find

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}\sqrt{1+6\alpha}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)+v^{2}(1+4\alpha)&\vdots&-v^{i}(1+4\alpha)\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}(1+4\alpha)&\vdots&\left(1+4\alpha\right)\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right),

which is the metric of the acoustic black hole with GUP corrections without the effect of potentials.

Now, for
\alpha=0
, we obtain the metric found by Unruh
Unruh:1980cg
up to an overall factor

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-(c^{2}_{s}-v^{2})&\vdots&-v^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}&\vdots&\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right).

We can also write the effective acoustic line element for the metric (
II
) as follows:

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}\sqrt{f}}\left[-F(v)\,dt^{2}-2\vec{V}\cdot d\vec{x}dt+[1-2\alpha(e\Phi-2)]dx^{2}\right],

(53)

where

\displaystyle F(v)=c^{2}_{s}(1+2\alpha)-v^{2}(1+4\alpha)+2\alpha e\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\Lambda},

(54)

\displaystyle\vec{V}=(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)\vec{v}-e\alpha\vec{\Lambda}.

(55)

Now, by performing a coordinate change

\displaystyle d\tau=dt+\frac{\vec{V}\cdot d\vec{x}}{F(v)},

(56)

the line element can be written in stationary form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{c_{s}\sqrt{f}}\left[-F(v)\,d\tau^{2}+\left(\frac{V^{i}V^{j}}{F(v)}+\left[1-2\alpha(e\Phi-2)\right]\delta^{ij}\right)dx^{i}dx^{j}\right].

(57)

At this point, to address the Hawking temperature issue, we will consider the configuration of an incompressible fluid with spherical symmetry.
For this situation the density
\rho
is a position independent quantity, the sound speed is also a constant and the continuity equation implies that
v\sim 1/r^{2}
.
Thus, disregarding position-independent factors, the line element (
57
) written in the Schwarzschild-type form is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\mathcal{F}(v_{r})d\tau^{2}+\frac{c^{2}_{s}\left(1+6\alpha-2\alpha e\Phi\right)}{f(v_{r})\mathcal{F}(v_{r})}dr^{2}+\frac{\left[1-2\alpha(e\Phi-2)\right]}{\sqrt{f(v_{r})}}r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(58)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v_{r})=\frac{F(v_{r})}{\sqrt{f(v_{r})}}\approx c^{2}_{s}[1-\alpha(1-e\Phi+ev_{r}\Lambda_{r})]-v^{2}_{r}[1-\alpha(ev_{r}\Lambda_{r}-e\Phi-1)]+2e\alpha v_{r}\Lambda_{r}.

(59)

Next, by continuity equation
v_{r}=c_{s}r^{2}_{h}/r^{2}
, where
r_{h}
is the radius of the event horizon,
we have that the modified canonical acoustic black hole reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(r)=c^{2}_{s}\left[1-\alpha\left(1-e\Phi(r)+\frac{ec_{s}r^{2}_{h}\Lambda_{r}(r)}{r^{2}}\right)\right]-\frac{c^{2}_{s}r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}\left[1-\alpha\left(\frac{ec_{s}r^{2}_{h}\Lambda_{r}(r)}{r^{2}}-e\Phi(r)-1\right)\right]+\frac{2e\alpha c_{s}r^{2}_{h}\Lambda_{r}(r)}{r^{2}}.

(60)

Notice that when
e=0
and
c_{s}=1
the metric function above is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(r)=1-\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}},

(61)

which is the canonical acoustic black hole metric found by Visser
Visser:1997ux
.
This metric is different from the well-known four-dimensional black hole geometries normally found in general relativity.
To obtain a result conforming to the Schwarzschild geometry, but not identical to it, we can consider from the continuity equation that
\rho v_{r}\propto 1/r^{2}
with
v_{r}=\sqrt{2GM/r}
and
\rho\propto r^{-3/2}
, such a way for the metric function, we find that
\mathcal{F}(r)=1-2GM/r
— see
Visser:1997ux
for further details.

Therefore, the Hawking temperature in this case is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(r_{h})}{4\pi}

(62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}_{s}(1+\alpha)+e\alpha c^{2}_{s}\left[\Phi(r_{h})-c_{s}\Lambda_{r}(r_{h})\right]}{\pi r_{h}}+\frac{2e\alpha c_{s}}{4\pi}\left(\Lambda^{\prime}_{r}(r_{h})-\frac{2\Lambda_{r}(r_{h})}{r_{h}}\right).

(63)

Note that the Hawking temperature of the modified canonical acoustic black hole is affected by the GUP deformation parameter
\alpha
and also by the contribution of the potentials
\Phi
and
\Lambda_{r}
.
In particular, for
\Lambda_{r}=0
the Hawking temperature in this case is reduced to

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=\frac{c^{2}_{s}[1+\alpha+e\alpha\Phi(r_{h})]}{\pi r_{h}}.

(64)

Furthermore, assuming
c_{s}=1
and a scalar potential of the type
\Phi=r_{0}/r
, the temperature becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=T_{H}\left(1+\alpha+\frac{\alpha er_{0}}{r_{h}}\right),

(65)

where
T_{H}=1/\pi r_{h}
is the Hawking temperature of the canonical acoustic black hole for
\alpha=0
and
r_{0}
is a parameter with length dimension.
Therefore, in the absence of the scalar potential (
r_{0}=0
or
e=0
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=T_{H}\left(1+\alpha\right),

(66)

and so the Hawking temperature has its value increased when we vary the parameter
\alpha
. On the other hand, when the scalar potential is considered the temperature is modified and a new correction term appears.

Furthermore, with the result obtained for the temperature in (
65
) we can determine
the entropy (entanglement entropy
Anacleto:2019rfn
) of the modified canonical acoustic black hole
by applying the first law of thermodynamics such that

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{dE}{dT}=\int\frac{\kappa\,dA}{8\pi\tilde{T}_{H}}=\int\frac{dA}{4}\left(1-\alpha^{2}-\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}\alpha^{2}er_{0}}{\sqrt{A}}-\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}e^{2}r^{2}_{0}}{A}\right),

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})A}{4}-\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\alpha^{2}er_{0}\sqrt{A}}{4}-\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}e^{2}r^{2}_{0}}{4}\ln\left(\frac{A}{4r^{2}_{0}}\right),

(68)

where
A=4\pi r^{2}_{h}
is the horizon area of the canonical acoustic black hole.
Note that we have found a logarithmic correction term for entropy. This correction term is generated due to the GUP and the scalar potential.
The presence of the logarithmic correction term is related to the appearance of black hole remnants in gravitation
Nozari:2005ah
.

The metric (
58
) can also be written, for
\Lambda_{r}=0
, as follows

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\mathcal{F}(v_{r})d\tau^{2}+\frac{c^{2}_{s}}{\mathcal{F}(v_{r})}dr^{2}+\left(1+\alpha-\alpha e\Phi\right)r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(69)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v_{r})=c^{2}_{s}[1-\alpha(1-e\Phi)]-v^{2}_{r}[1+\alpha(1+e\Phi)].

(70)

Now, recalling that
v_{r}=c_{s}r^{2}_{h}/r^{2}
and
\Phi=r_{0}/r
and making
c_{s}=1
, we find

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\mathcal{F}(r)d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\mathcal{F}(r)}+\left(1+\alpha-\frac{\alpha er_{0}}{r}\right)r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(71)

being

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(r)=1-\alpha+\frac{\alpha er_{0}}{r}-\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}\left[1+\alpha\left(1+\frac{er_{0}}{r}\right)\right].

(72)

At the limit of
r\gg r_{h}\equiv\alpha er_{0}
the metric becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\mathcal{F}(r)d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\mathcal{F}(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(73)

that in this case the metric function
\mathcal{F}(r)
is now given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(r)=1-\alpha^{2}+\frac{r_{h}}{r},

(74)

which is an asymptotically global monopole metric.
So this result is a consequence of the scalar potential effect on the effective acoustic metric.

Now, for the case
\Phi=0
and
\Lambda=\lambda/r
, being
\lambda
a parameter with a length dimension.
In this case the Hawking temperature in (
63
) becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=T_{H}\left(1+\alpha-\frac{5\alpha e\lambda}{2r_{h}}\right),

(75)

and for entropy we find

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})A}{4}+\frac{20\sqrt{\pi}\alpha^{2}e\lambda\sqrt{A}}{4}-\frac{25\pi\alpha^{2}e^{2}\lambda^{2}}{4}\ln\left(\frac{A}{4\lambda^{2}}\right).

(76)

Again a logarithmic correction is also generated for the pure vector potential contribution whose pre-factor with respect to the pure scalar potential case is
(5\lambda/2r_{0})^{2}
, which mimics logarithm corrections due to species with different spins.

Here it is worth mentioning that considering the contributions of
\Phi=r_{0}/r
and
\Lambda_{r}=\lambda/r

the metric function (
60
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(r)=1-\alpha\left(1-\frac{er_{0}}{r}-\frac{e\lambda r^{2}_{h}}{r^{3}}\right)-\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}\left[1+\alpha\left(1+\frac{er_{0}}{r}-\frac{e\lambda r^{2}_{h}}{r^{3}}\right)\right].

(77)

From (
63
), by making
c_{s}=1
,
\Phi=r_{0}/r
and
\Lambda_{r}=\lambda/r
, we obtain the Hawking temperature which is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=T_{H}\left(1+\alpha+\frac{e\alpha r_{0}}{r_{h}}-\frac{5e\alpha\lambda}{2r_{h}}\right).

(78)

On the other hand, if
\Lambda_{r}=\lambda_{0}
, where
\lambda_{0}
is a dimensionless constant, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(r)=1-\alpha\left(1-\frac{er_{0}}{r}-\frac{e\lambda_{0}r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}\right)-\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}\left[1+\alpha\left(1+\frac{er_{0}}{r}-\frac{e\lambda_{0}r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}\right)\right].

(79)

Our result shows similarity with the metric obtained in
Ling:2021vgk

with the identification of effective charge and mass as
Q^{2}=\alpha e\lambda_{0}r^{2}_{h}
and
2M=\alpha er_{0}
for
r_{0}<0
, respectively.
Therefore, we highlight another new result that arises due to the GUP effect, that is, the contributions of
\Phi
and
\Lambda
can generate Reissner-Nordström-like contributions for the metric.
In this case the Hawking temperature is given by equation (
63
) making
c_{s}=1
,
\Phi=r_{0}/r
and

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=T_{H}\left(1+\alpha-2e\alpha\lambda_{0}+\frac{e\alpha r_{0}}{r_{h}}\right).

Hence, when

III
The Dispersion Relation

In order to study further effects from the GUP we shall now investigate the dispersion relation.
For this we will use the following notation:

\displaystyle S_{1}\sim\mbox{Re}\left[e^{i\tilde{\omega}t-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\right],\qquad\tilde{\omega}=\frac{\partial S_{1}}{\partial t},\qquad\vec{k}=\nabla S_{1}.

(81)

In this way the Klein-Gordon equation (
II
) in terms of momenta and frequency becomes

\displaystyle a\,\tilde{\omega}^{2}+b\,\tilde{\omega}+c=0,

(82)

where

\displaystyle a=1-2\alpha(e\Phi-2)+c_{s}^{2}\left(1+2\alpha\right),

(83)

\displaystyle b=2(1+4\alpha-\alpha e\Phi)(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{k})-2\alpha(\vec{\Lambda}\cdot\vec{k}),

(84)

\displaystyle c=-\left[-(1+4\alpha)v^{2}k^{2}+2\alpha e(\vec{\Lambda}\cdot\vec{k})(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{k})+c_{s}^{2}\left(1+2\alpha\right)k^{2}\right].

(85)

Now, by considering
k^{i}=\delta^{i1}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}=\frac{-2\left[(1+4\alpha+\alpha e\Phi)v_{1}-\alpha e\Lambda_{1}\right]k\pm\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a},

(86)

with

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Delta}=2c_{s}k\sqrt{1+2\alpha-2\alpha(e\Phi-2)+4\alpha e\Phi\frac{v_{1}^{2}}{c^{2}_{s}}-{2\alpha\frac{e\Lambda_{1}v_{1}}{c^{2}_{s}}}+\left[(1+4\alpha)c^{2}_{s}-(1+6\alpha)v^{2}_{1}+{2\alpha e\Lambda_{1}v_{1}}\right]}.

(87)

In particular for
\Lambda_{1}=0
and in the non-relativistic limit, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}\approx\pm\frac{c_{s}\sqrt{1+6\alpha-2\alpha e\Phi}}{1+4\alpha-2\alpha e\Phi}k=\eta\,\omega\left(1-\alpha+\alpha e\Phi\right),

(88)

being
\omega=c_{s}k
(the dispersion relation for
\alpha=0
) and

\eta=\pm 1
the polarizations.
Now, to explore the scalar potential effect recall that

\Phi=r_{0}/r
. Hence, focusing on the dispersion relation in the vicinity of the event horizon,

r\rightarrow r_{h}
, becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}=\eta\,\omega\left(1-\alpha+\frac{er_{0}\alpha}{r_{h}}\right)=\eta(1-\alpha)\omega\left(1+\frac{er_{0}\alpha}{r_{h}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}).

(89)

Next, we redefine
\bar{\omega}=\tilde{\omega}/(1-\alpha)

and apply
k\sim\Delta k\geq 1/\Delta x=1/r_{h}
.
Thus, the dispersion relation above is rewritten in the form

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}=\eta\,\omega\left(1+\alpha er_{0}k\right).

(90)

So we note that the scalar potential contribution generated by the GUP is associated with a viscosity effect in the fluid. Similar behavior has been analyzed in
Anacleto:2019rfn
in the background with Lorentz violation.

We can also write an expression in terms of the frequency difference given by

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\omega}{\omega}=\frac{\bar{\omega}-\eta\omega}{\omega}=\eta\alpha er_{0}k.

(91)

Now we can get from the dispersion relation (
90
) the phase velocity (
v_{p}
) and the group velocity (
v_{g}
) as follows:

\displaystyle v_{p}=\frac{\bar{\omega}}{k}=\eta c_{s}(1+\alpha er_{0}k),

(92)

and

\displaystyle v_{g}=\frac{d\bar{\omega}}{dk}=\eta c_{s}(1+2\alpha er_{0}k).

(93)

Using the Rayleigh’s formula

\displaystyle v_{g}=v_{p}+k\frac{dv_{p}}{dk},

(94)

we obtain an expression for the velocity difference given by

\displaystyle\frac{v_{g}-v_{p}}{v_{p}}=\alpha er_{0}k.

(95)

In this case, we have a supersonic behavior (
v_{g}>v_{p}
) as in BEC systems whose excitations follow ‘superluminal’ dispersion relations
Robertson:2012ku
.

Furthermore, from equation (
65
), for the Hawking temperature we can also obtain a relationship for the temperature difference. So we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{H}=T_{H}(1+\alpha er_{0}k).

(96)

Here we have redefined
\mathcal{T}_{H}=\tilde{T}_{H}/(1+\alpha)
.
Hence, we find the following relationship for the Hawking temperature variation due to the potential effect

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta T_{H}}{T_{H}}=\frac{\mathcal{T}_{H}-T_{H}}{T_{H}}=\alpha er_{0}k.

(97)

Therefore, we find the following relationship:

\displaystyle\frac{v_{g}-v_{p}}{v_{p}}=\eta\frac{\Delta\omega}{\omega}=\frac{\Delta T_{H}}{T_{H}}.

(98)

Moreover, we emphasize that the modification of the dispersion relation associated with
\delta=\alpha er_{0}k
is analogous to Lorentz-violating parameter in BEC physics whose deviation is
\delta\sim 10^{-5}
— see
Anacleto:2019rfn
and references therein. As a result, by recalling
k\sim 1/r_{h}
, such that
\delta\sim\alpha e\Phi(r_{h})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta T_{H}}{T_{H}}\sim\Phi(r_{h})\sim 10^{-5}.

(99)

Interestingly enough, this result is also analogous to the small temperature fluctuations in the anisotropy of the cosmic microwave
background (CMB), with the scalar field evaluated at horizon corresponding to fluctuations of the gravitational potential in the cosmological scenario.

IV
Conclusions

We obtained an effective acoustic metric for acoustic black holes with quantum corrections implemented by the generalized Heisenberg uncertainty principle (GUP) in the Abelian Higgs model. Thus, contrary to the usual acoustic metrics, the obtained effective acoustic metric now depends on the contribution of scalar and vector potentials that emerge as an effect of the GUP.
We have examined the Hawking temperature and the entropy for the modified canonical acoustic black hole. As a result, the Hawking temperature that changes due to the effect of the GUP provides logarithmic corrections to the entropy. We address the modification of the Hawking temperature by considering different effects by turning on scalar and vector potential contributions separately and also turning on them together.
Moreover, by analyzing the dispersion relation of the model we have obtained a relationship between the group and phase velocity difference due to the GUP effect, as well as expressions for the frequency and temperature deviations. Finally, we have made a few comments on how they can be related to BEC physics with supersonic dispersion relations and small temperature fluctuations in the anisotropy of the cosmic microwave background (CMB).

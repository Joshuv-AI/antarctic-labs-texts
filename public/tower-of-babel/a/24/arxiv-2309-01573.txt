Abstract
. For a submodule N N of an R R -module M M , a unique product of prime ideals in R R is assigned, which is called the generalized prime ideal factorization of N N in M M , and denoted as 𝒫 M ​ ( N ) {\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N) . But for a product of prime ideals 𝔭 1 ⋯ 𝔭 n {{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}} in R R and an R R -module M M , there may not exist a submodule N N in M M with 𝒫 M ( N ) = 𝔭 1 ⋯ 𝔭 n {\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}} . In this article, for an arbitrary product of prime ideals 𝔭 1 ⋯ 𝔭 n {{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}} and a module M M , we find conditions for the existence of submodules in M M having 𝔭 1 ⋯ 𝔭 n {{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}} as their generalized prime ideal factorization.

2.
Ideals as Generalized Prime Ideal Factorization of Submodules

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N
be a submodule of
M
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{r}
be some minimal prime ideals in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
. Then there exists a submodule
K
of
M
containing
N
such that
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(K)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{r}}
.

Proof.

Let
N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{q}}_{n}}{\subset}M_{n}=M
be an RPE filtration of
M
over
N
. Since
\{{\mathfrak{q}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{q}}_{n}\}=\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
, for each
1\leq i\leq r
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}={\mathfrak{q}}_{j}
for some
j
. Since
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}},\dots,{{\mathfrak{p}}_{r}}
are minimal, we can reorder
{\mathfrak{q}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{q}}_{n}
such that
{\mathfrak{q}}_{j}\not\subset{\mathfrak{q}}_{k}
for
1\leq j<k\leq n
and
{\mathfrak{q}}_{n-r+i}={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
. So using Lemma
1.1
sufficient times we can have an RPE filtration

N=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\cdots\subset K_{n-r}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}K_{n-r+1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}\cdots\subset K_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{r}}{\subset}K_{n}=M

of
M
over
N
. Then

K_{n-r}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}K_{n-r+1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}\cdots\subset K_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{r}}{\subset}K_{n}=M

is an RPE filtration. So if
K=K_{n-r}
, then
K
is a submodule of
M
containing
N
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(K)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{r}}
.
∎

Now we show that for a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in

,

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

be an
R
-module and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}

\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(M)

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\in\mathrm{Supp}(M/{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}M})

(iii)

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}

\mathrm{Ass}(M/{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}M})

(iv)

There exists a submodule

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow

for some

{({M}/{{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}M}})}_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}=0

. Therefore

.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): If
{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathrm{Supp}(M/{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}M})
, then
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, and therefore
\mathfrak{q}
contains some
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
. So the set of minimal elements of
\mathrm{Supp}(M/{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}M})
is contained in

. Since

are minimal in

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv): Since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
are minimal in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}M})
, by Lemma

, there exists a submodule
N
of

{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(i): Since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
are the prime ideals which occur in an RPE filtration of

\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}=\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(M)
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal in
R
. Then
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)
if and only if there exists a submodule
N
in
M
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)=\mathfrak{p}
.

In Proposition
2.2
, the prime ideals are distinct. Now we find conditions for the product of prime ideals that need not be distinct to be a generalized prime ideal factorization of some submodule.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal in
R
and

be a positive integer. If
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Ass}({{\mathfrak{p}}^{r-1}M}/{{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}M})

Proof.

Let
N=\{x\in M\mid({\mathfrak{p}}^{r}M:x)\not\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}

and

. Then there exists

, which implies that

. Hence

is a submodule of
M
. Since
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Ass}({{\mathfrak{p}}^{r-1}M}/{{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}M})

is a proper submodule of

. Also,
N\supseteq{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}M
.

We claim that
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
. Then
{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
since
{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}M\subseteq N
. Therefore
{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Now
\mathfrak{q}=(N:z)
for some
z\in M
,

, that is,
({\mathfrak{p}}^{r}M:z)\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Let
a\in\mathfrak{q}
. Then

, which gives
({\mathfrak{p}}^{r}M:az)\not\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Let

. This implies

. Therefore
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Hence
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
.

If
N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{k-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}{\subset}M_{k}=M
is an RPE filtration of

over

, then

. So

. Suppose

. Then
{\mathfrak{p}}^{r-1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}
, which implies

. But

, and this implies

. By Lemma

M_{r}=\{x\in M\mid{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}x\subseteq N\}
. For any

. So,

∎

. Since
L^{(1)}_{j-1}\subset L
, from (
2.1
) we get that
p_{i}x\in L^{(i)}_{k}
for every
2\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq k\leq r_{i}
.

Since
p_{n}x\in L^{(n)}_{r_{n}-1}
,
L^{(n)}_{r_{n}-1}\subset M
is a
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
-prime extension, and
p_{n}\notin{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
, we have
x\in L^{(n)}_{r_{n}-1}
. Then
p_{n}x\in L^{(n)}_{r_{n}-2}
and
L^{(n)}_{r_{n}-2}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}L^{(n)}_{r_{n}-1}
is a prime extension implies
x\in L^{(n)}_{r_{n}-2}
. Repeating this argument
r_{n}-3
times, we get
x\in L^{(n-1)}_{r_{n-1}}
.

Replacing
M
by
L^{(n-1)}_{r_{n-1}}
and
p_{n}
by
p_{n-1}
in the previous paragraph, we get
x\in L^{(n-2)}_{r_{n-2}}
. Continuing this process, finally we get
x\in L^{(1)}_{r_{1}}=L
, a contradiction. Therefore,
L^{(1)}_{j-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}L^{(1)}_{j}
is a maximal prime extension in
M
for every
1\leq j\leq r_{1}
, and hence (
2.3
) is an MPE filtration of
M
over
N
.

So, for
1\leq j\leq r_{1}
, we get
\mathrm{Ass}(M/L^{(1)}_{j-1})=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}
and since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\not\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
for every
i>1
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
is maximal in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/L^{(1)}_{j-1})
. Therefore (
2.3
) is an RPE filtration of
M
over
N
. Hence
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}
.
∎

The converse of Theorem
2.5
does not hold. For example, if
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
are prime ideals in a ring
R
and
M
is the
R
-module
\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}\oplus\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}
, then for its submodule
N=\frac{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}\oplus 0
, we have the RPE filtration

N=\dfrac{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}\oplus 0\quad\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}\quad\dfrac{R}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}\oplus 0\quad\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}\quad\dfrac{R}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}\oplus\dfrac{R}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}=M

of
M
over
N
. So we have
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
. But
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}M=0
. Therefore
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\notin\mathrm{Supp}({\mathfrak{p}}_{2}M)
.

Next, we show that if we assume further that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for
i\neq j
, the converse of Theorem
2.5
holds. We need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
2
, Lemma 2.8]

If
N\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}K
is a regular
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension in
M
, then for any submodule
L
of
M
,
N\cap L\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}K\cap L
is a regular
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension in
L
when
N\cap L\neq K\cap L
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
N
be a submodule of
M
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
are distinct prime ideals in
R
and
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
are positive integers. If all the prime ideals in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
are minimal, then
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\in\mathrm{Supp}({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}^{r_{i-1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{r_{i}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}^{r_{i+1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M)
for
i=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
are minimal, for every
i
we can reorder
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
such that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
and by Lemma
1.1
, we have an RPE filtration

N\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}L^{(1)}_{1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}L^{(1)}_{2}\subset\cdots\subset L^{(1)}_{r_{1}-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}L^{(1)}_{r_{1}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}L^{(2)}_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}L^{(n)}_{r_{n}}=M

of
M
over
N
. So it is enough to show that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\in\mathrm{Supp}({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}

{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}

\cdots

{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M)
. Clearly
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M\subseteq N
and
{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M}\subseteq L^{(1)}_{1}
.

We claim that
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M\not\subseteq N
. Let
x\in L^{(1)}_{r_{1}}\setminus L^{(1)}_{r_{1}-1}
. Then
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}x\subseteq L^{(1)}_{1}
and
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}x\not\subset N
. So there exists
b\in{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}
such that
bx\in L^{(1)}_{1}\setminus N
. Choose
a_{j}\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
for every
2\leq j\leq n
and let
a=\prod_{2\leq j\leq n}a_{j}^{r_{j}}
. Then
bax\in{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M}
. Suppose
bax\in N
. Then, since
bx\in L^{(1)}_{1}\setminus N
and
N\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}L^{(1)}_{1}
is a
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
-prime extension, we get
a\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
, a contradiction. So
bax\notin N
. Therefore
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M\not\subseteq N
. So we have

\displaystyle N\,\cap\;({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M)\;

\displaystyle\subsetneq\;{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M

\displaystyle=\;L^{(1)}_{1}\,\cap\;({{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M}).

Then by Lemma
2.6
,

N\,\cap\;({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M)\quad\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}\quad{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M}

is a regular
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
-prime extension in
{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M}
. Then by Corollary
2.3
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\in\mathrm{Supp}({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M)
.
∎

From Theorems
2.5
and
2.7
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
be distinct prime ideals in
R
with
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for
i\neq j
, and
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
be positive integers. Then
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}
is the generalized prime ideal factorization of some submodule of
M
if and only if
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\in\mathrm{Supp}({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}^{r_{i-1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{r_{i}-1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}^{r_{i+1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}^{r_{n}}M)
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

In
[
5
]
we have found conditions for
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}M)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}

[
5
, Theorem 2.14]
and showed that this need not always be true
[
5
, Example 2.5]
. Now for an
R
-module
M
and a product of prime ideals
{\mathfrak{a}}={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
(
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
’s not necessarily distinct), we give a necessary and sufficient condition for
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}({\mathfrak{a}}M)={\mathfrak{a}}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
be prime ideals in
R
, not necessarily distinct, with
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
maximal among
\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}=R
, and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. Then
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}({\mathfrak{a}}M)={\mathfrak{a}}
if and only if
\mathrm{Ass}\big(\frac{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})}{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})}\big)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{Ass}\big(\frac{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})}{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})}\big)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, we show that

{\mathfrak{a}}M\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{1})\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{2})\subset\cdots\subset({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{n-1})\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}})=M

(2.4)

is an RPE filtration.

\mathrm{Ass}\big(\frac{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})}{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})}\big)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\}
implies that there exists a regular
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-prime extension
K
of
({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})
in
({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
. Then
K=\{x\in({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})\mid

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}x\subseteq({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})\}
by Lemma
1.3
. For every
x\in({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}x={\mathfrak{a}}_{i}x\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}M
, that is,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}x\subseteq({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})
. Therefore,
K=({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
, and hence
({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
is the unique regular
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-prime extension of
({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})
in
({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
. Suppose it is not maximal in
M
. Then there exists
x\in M\setminus({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
such that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}x\subseteq({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})
, i.e.,
x\in({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})=({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
, a contradiction. So
({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
is a maximal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-prime extension of
({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})
in
M
for every
i
. Therefore (
2.4
) is an MPE filtration of
M
over
{\mathfrak{a}}M
. This implies that
\mathrm{Ass}\big(\frac{M}{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})}\big)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
. Since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is maximal among
\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is maximal in
\mathrm{Ass}\big(\frac{M}{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})}\big)
. Therefore (
2.4
) is an RPE filtration. Hence
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}({\mathfrak{a}}M)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}={\mathfrak{a}}
.

Conversely, suppose that
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}({\mathfrak{a}}M)={\mathfrak{a}}={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
. Since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is maximal among
\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, we can construct an RPE filtration

{\mathfrak{a}}M=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}M_{2}\subset\cdots M_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}M_{n}=M

of
M
over
{\mathfrak{a}}M
. Then by Lemma
1.3
,
M_{i}=\{x\in M\mid{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}x\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}M\}
, i.e.,
M_{i}=({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})
for every
1\leq i\leq n
. Then clearly
\mathrm{Ass}\big(\frac{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i})}{({\mathfrak{a}}M:{\mathfrak{a}}_{i-1})}\big)=\mathrm{Ass}\big(\frac{M_{i}}{M_{i-1}}\big)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.
∎

Manifolds with non-positive second Chern-Ricci curvature
Xiaokui Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a Kählerian or projectivity criterion for a class of compact Hermitian surfaces with non-positive second Chern-Ricci curvature.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold. When
\omega
is not Kähler, there are many different connections on
T^{1,0}M
and their curvature tensors are different. Recently, there are many works on curvature relations for various connections, for instances,
[
7
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
36
]
and
[
42
]
.
It is still a challenge to investigate the geometry and topology determined by those connections and curvature tensors. The key difficulty is that the metric differential has no Kähler symmetry. Hermitian metrics with special symmetric are investigated extensively in recent years. For instances, certain balanced metrics are constructed in
[
14
]
, and the Kählerian of various Kähler-like metrics are obtained in
[
6
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
46
]
. For more discussions along this line, one can see
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
45
]
.

On the other hand, the geometry and topology of compact Hermitian surfaces are well studied by using the Weyl structure and conformal transformations. In particular, when
(M,\omega)
is Hermitian-Einstein and
\omega
is a Gauduchon metric, Gauduchon and Ivanov observed in
[
19
]
(see also
[
34
]
) that the Lee form is parallel and such manifolds are classified in
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
. For more results along this line, we refer to

[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
31
]
and the references therein.

It is well-known that the first Chern-Ricci curvature
\Theta^{(1)}
of the Chern connection represents the first Chern class of
M
. In particular, if
\omega
has positive first Chern-Ricci curvature
\Theta^{(1)}
, then
M
must be a Kähler manifold with
c_{1}(M)>0
. Moreover, it has a Kähler metric with positive Ricci curvature (
[
41
]
) and so
M
is simply connected. However, the geometry and topology of the second Chern-Ricci curvature
\Theta^{(2)}
is more mysterious. For instance,
it is well-known that (e.g.
[
27
,
28
]
) the non-Kähler manifold
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
has positive
\Theta^{(2)}
and
\pi_{1}(M)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
. On the other hand, we proved in
[
37
]
that if a compact Kähler manifold admits a Hermitian metric with positive second Chern-Ricci curvature
\Theta^{(2)}
, then
M
is projective and simply connected.

In this paper, we establish a Kählerian or projectivity criterion for a class of compact Hermitian manifolds with non-positive second Chern-Ricci curvature. We begin with the classical result of Gauduchon-Ivanov
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface. Suppose
\omega
is Gauduchon and weakly Hermitian-Einstein, i.e.
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and

(1.1)

\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)=u\omega

for some
u\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. If
u\leq 0
, then
\omega
is a Kähler metric.

The original proof of this theorem uses the geometry of Weyl-Einstein structure.
We shall give a simplified proof of this result and derive some applications. For higher dimensional Hermitian manifolds, we obtain

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a compact complex manifold with
\dim M=n
. If it admits a Hermtian metric
\omega
with
\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and

\mathrm{\Theta^{(2)}}(\omega)
is quasi-negative, then
the signed top intersection number

(-1)^{n}c_{1}^{n}(M)>0.

Moreover, the cohomology groups
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
,
H^{1,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
and
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
are all non-zero.

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is a combinatoric version of the Chern-Weil theory (Theorem
4.1
). As an application of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain an extension of Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface. If
\omega
is Gauduchon and

\mathrm{\Theta^{(2)}}(\omega)\leq 0,

then either
(M,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold with
c_{1}^{2}(M)=0
, or
M
is a projective manifold with
c_{1}^{2}(M)>0
.

It worths to point out that similar results as in Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
can not hold for compact Hermitian manifolds with
\mathrm{\Theta^{(2)}}>0
. Indeed, it is shown in
[
27
, Proposition 6.1]
and
[
28
, Section 6]
that the canonically induced metric
\omega
on the diagonal Hopf surface
M={\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
is Gauduchon and
\mathrm{\Theta^{(2)}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{4}\omega>0
. However, the top intersection number
c_{1}^{2}(M)=0
and
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,{\mathbb{R}})=H^{1,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})=0
.

On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
is related to a question raised by Angella, Calamai and Spotti in
[
2
, Remark 13]
. Actually, all weakly Hermitian-Einstein surfaces are conformal Kähler except Hopf surfaces (
[
19
, Theorem 2]
). One can see that Hopf surfaces can not support weakly Hermitian-Einstein mertic with Hermitian-Einstein function
u\leq 0
(Theorem
3.6
). More generally,

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
M
be a Hopf surface. Then it can not support a smooth Hermitian metric
\omega
with non-positive second Chern-Ricci curvature
\mathrm{\Theta^{(2)}}(\omega)
.

For more discussions on Chern scalar curvature and curvature relations, we refer to
[

]
,

Moreover, by using the techniques in

[
40

, we shall discuss the complex geometry of various Riemannian curvatur tensors in details.

2.
Background materials

In this section, we fix the conventions briefly for readers’ convenience and also refer to
[
28
]
and
[
39
]
for more details.
Let
(M,g,\nabla)
be a
2n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
. The tangent bundle of
M
is denoted by
T_{\mathbb{R}}M
. The Riemannian curvature tensor of

(M,g,\nabla)
is defined as

R(X,Y,Z,W)=g\left(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,W\right)

for tangent vectors
X,Y,Z,W\in\Gamma(M,T_{\mathbb{R}}M)
. Let
T_{\mathbb{C}}M=T_{\mathbb{R}}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
. One can extend the metric
g
and the
Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
to
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M
in the
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear way.
Let
(M,g,J)
be a Hermitian manifold and
z^{i}=x^{i}+\sqrt{-1}x^{I}
be local holomorphic coordinates on
M
.
There is a Hermitian form
h:T_{\mathbb{C}}M\times T_{\mathbb{C}}M\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
given
by
h(X,Y):=g(X,Y)
. The
fundamental
2
-form associated to the
J
-invariant metric
g
is
\omega=\sqrt{-1}h_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}
. The complexified Christoffel symbols are given by

(2.1)

\Gamma_{AB}^{C}=\sum_{E}\frac{1}{2}g^{CE}\big(\frac{\partial g_{AE}}{\partial z^{B}}+\frac{\partial g_{BE}}{\partial z^{A}}-\frac{\partial g_{AB}}{\partial z^{E}}\big)=\sum_{E}\frac{1}{2}h^{CE}\big(\frac{\partial h_{AE}}{\partial z^{B}}+\frac{\partial h_{BE}}{\partial z^{A}}-\frac{\partial h_{AB}}{\partial z^{E}}\big)

where
A,B,C,E\in\{1,\cdots,n,\overline{1},\cdots,\overline{n}\}
and

z^{A}=z^{i}
if
A=i
,
z^{A}=\overline{z}^{i}
if
A=\overline{i}
. For
example

(2.2)

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}h^{k\overline{\ell}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{j\overline{\ell}}}{\partial z^{i}}+\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{\ell}}}{\partial z^{j}}\right),\ \Gamma_{\overline{i}j}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}h^{k\overline{\ell}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{j\overline{\ell}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{i}}-\frac{\partial h_{j\overline{i}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{\ell}}\right).

Since
\Gamma_{AB}^{C}=\Gamma_{BA}^{C}
, we have
\Gamma_{\overline{i}j}^{k}=\Gamma_{j\overline{i}}^{k}
. We also have
\Gamma_{\overline{i}\overline{j}}^{k}=\Gamma_{ij}^{\overline{k}}=0
since

h_{pq}=h_{\overline{i}\overline{j}}=0
.

2.A.
The induced Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}
on
(T^{1,0}M,h)

There is an induced connection

\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}
on
T^{1,0}M

given by

(2.3)

\nabla^{\text{LC}}=\pi\circ\nabla:\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\rightarrow}}\Gamma(M,T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M\otimes T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}\Gamma(M,T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M\otimes T^{1,0}M).

It is easy to see that

\nabla^{\text{LC}}
is
determined by the following relations

(2.4)

\nabla^{\text{LC}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}:=\Gamma_{ik}^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{p}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}:=\Gamma_{\overline{j}k}^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{p}}

where the Christoffel symbols are defined in (
2.2
). As usual, the curvature tensor
\mathfrak{R}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{2}T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M\otimes T^{*1,0}M\otimes T^{1,0}M)
of

\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}
is defined as

(2.5)

\mathfrak{R}(X,Y)s=\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}_{X}\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}_{Y}s-\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}_{Y}\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}_{X}s-\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}_{[X,Y]}s

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(M,T_{{\mathbb{C}}}M)
and
s\in\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M)
. A straightforward
computation shows that the curvature tensor
\mathfrak{R}
has

(1,1)
components

(2.6)

\mathfrak{R}_{i\overline{j}k}^{\ell}=-\left(\frac{\partial\Gamma^{\ell}_{ik}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}-\frac{\partial\Gamma^{\ell}_{\overline{j}k}}{\partial z^{i}}+\Gamma_{ik}^{s}\Gamma^{\ell}_{\overline{j}s}-\Gamma_{\overline{j}k}^{s}\Gamma^{\ell}_{is}\right).

2.B.
The Chern connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{ch}}
on
(T^{1,0}M,h)

The Chern
connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{ch}}
on
(T^{1,0}M,h)
is determined by the rules

(2.7)

\nabla^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}:={{}^{c}\Gamma}_{ik}^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{p}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nabla^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}:=0

where
{{}^{c}\Gamma}_{ik}^{p}=h^{p\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{\ell}}}{\partial z^{i}}
.
The curvature components of
\nabla^{\mathrm{ch}}
are

(2.8)

\Theta_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}=-\frac{\partial^{2}h_{k\overline{\ell}}}{\partial z^{i}\partial\overline{z}^{j}}+h^{p\overline{q}}\frac{\partial h_{p\overline{\ell}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{q}}}{\partial z^{i}}.

It is
well-known that the
first Chern-Ricci curvature
is

(2.9)

\Theta^{(1)}:=\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j},\ \ \ \Theta^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}=h^{k\overline{\ell}}\Theta_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}=-\frac{\partial^{2}\log\det(h_{k\overline{\ell}})}{\partial z^{i}\partial\overline{z}^{j}}.

The
second Chern-Ricci curvature

\Theta^{(2)}=\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{(2)}_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}
has
components

\Theta^{(2)}_{i\overline{j}}=h^{k\overline{\ell}}\Theta_{k\overline{\ell}i\overline{j}}.

3.
Weakly Hermitian-Einstein surfaces

In this section, we refine the classical result of Gauduchon-Ivanov
[
19
]
. On a compact Hermitian surface
\left(M,\omega=\sqrt{-1}h_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\right)
, the torsion tensor
T
of
(M,h)
has components

(3.1)

T_{ij}^{k}=h^{k\overline{\ell}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{j\overline{\ell}}}{\partial z^{i}}-\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{\ell}}}{\partial z^{j}}\right).

We aslo write

(3.2)

T_{i}:=\sum\limits_{k}T_{ik}^{k},\ \ \ T_{\overline{i}}:=\overline{T_{i}}.

It is easy to see that

(3.3)

\Gamma_{\overline{i}j}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\overline{T_{ip}^{q}}h_{q\overline{k}}h^{j\overline{p}},\ \ \ 2\sum_{k}\Gamma_{\overline{i}k}^{k}=T_{\overline{i}}.

By the
well-known Bochner formula (e.g.
[
27
, Lemma 4.3]
),

(3.4)

[\overline{\partial}^{*},L]=\sqrt{-1}\left(\partial+\tau\right)

where
L\varphi=\omega\wedge\varphi
and
\tau=[\Lambda,\partial\omega]
, one deduces that

(3.5)

\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega=\sqrt{-1}T_{i}dz^{i},\ \ \ \partial^{*}\omega=-\sqrt{-1}T_{\overline{j}}d\overline{z}^{j}.

Let
\theta:=T_{i}dz^{i}+T_{\overline{j}}d\overline{z}^{j}
be the Lee form. By using
[
34
, Proposition 2.2]
, Gauduchon and Ivanov obtained the following result in
[
19
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface. Suppose
\omega
is Gauduchon and weakly Hermitian-Einstein, i.e.
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and

(3.6)

\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)=u\omega

for some
u\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Then
\nabla\theta
is skew-symmetric.

Actually, it is proved in
[
19
, Theorem 1]
that when
\omega
is Gauduchon and weakly Hermitian-Einstein, then the background Riemannian metric is Weyl-Einstein. By
[
34
, Proposition 2.2]
, they deduced that
\nabla\theta
is skew-symmetric.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a Hermitian surface. If
\nabla\theta
is skew-symmetric, then

(3.7)

\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega=-2\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\wedge\partial^{*}\omega+2|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}\omega.

In particular,
\omega
is Gauduchon.

Proof.

A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle\left(\nabla\theta\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}\right)+\left(\nabla\theta\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}T_{\overline{j}}+\nabla_{\overline{j}}T_{i}-\Gamma_{i\overline{j}}^{s}T_{s}-\Gamma_{\overline{j}i}^{\overline{s}}T_{\overline{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial T_{\overline{j}}}{\partial z^{i}}+\frac{\partial T_{i}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}-2\Gamma_{i\overline{j}}^{s}T_{s}-2\Gamma_{\overline{j}i}^{\overline{s}}T_{\overline{s}}.

Moreover, on a Hermitian surface, we have

(3.8)

2\Gamma_{i\overline{j}}^{s}T_{s}=2\Gamma_{\overline{j}i}^{\overline{s}}T_{\overline{s}}=T_{i}T_{\overline{j}}-|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}h_{i\overline{j}}.

Hence, we obtain (
3.7
). By taking trace, we deduce that

\Lambda(\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega)=2|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}.

Note also that

\Lambda\partial\partial^{*}\omega-|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}=\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega.

Hence,
\omega
is Gauduchon.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface. Suppose
\omega
is Gauduchon and weakly Hermitian-Einstein. Then

(3.9)

\|\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=\|\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=(|\partial^{*}\omega|^{4},1).

Proof.

A straightforward calculation shows that

(3.10)

\left(\partial\partial^{*}\omega,\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right)=\|\Lambda\partial\partial^{*}\omega\|^{2}.

Indeed, for any
(1,1)
form
\eta
on a Hermitian surface, we have

(3.11)

*\eta=-\eta+(\Lambda\eta)\omega

and so

\displaystyle\left(\partial\partial^{*}\omega,\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{*}\omega,\partial^{*}\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega\right)=\left(\partial^{*}\omega,-*\overline{\partial}*\left(\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{*}\omega,*\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right)+\left(\partial^{*}\omega,-*\overline{\partial}\left(\left(\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right)\omega\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\partial}^{*}*\partial^{*}\omega,\left(\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right)\omega\right)=\left(*\partial\partial^{*}\omega,\left(\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right)\omega\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda\partial\partial^{*}\omega,\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right).

Since
\omega
is Gauduchon,

(3.12)

\Lambda\partial\partial^{*}\omega=\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega=|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

(3.13)

\left(\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega,\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\right)=2\|\partial\partial^{*}\omega\|^{2}+2\|\Lambda\partial\partial^{*}\omega\|^{2}.

On the other hand, by (
3.7
),
\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega=2|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}\omega-2\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\wedge\partial^{*}\omega
, one has

\|\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=\|2|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}\omega-2\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\wedge\partial^{*}\omega\|^{2}=4(|\partial^{*}\omega|^{4},1)

and so
\|\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=\|\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=(|\partial^{*}\omega|^{4},1)
.
∎

The following result is proved in
[
19
, Theorem 2]
. We give a simplified proof here.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface. Suppose
\omega
is Gauduchon and weakly Hermitian-Einstein, i.e.

(3.14)

\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)=u\omega

for some
u\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Then
u-|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}
is a constant. Moreover, either
\omega
is a Kähler metric, or
u=|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}
.

Proof.

In this case, we have

(3.15)

\Theta^{(1)}-\left(\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega\right)=\left(u-|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}\right)\omega.

Let
f=u-|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}
. By applying
\partial\overline{\partial}
to both sides, we have

(3.16)

0=\partial\overline{\partial}(f\omega)=\partial\overline{\partial}f\wedge\omega+\partial f\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega-\overline{\partial}f\wedge\partial\omega.

By taking trace, we get an elliptic equation. The maximum principle tells that
f
is a constant. ( One can also deduce that by multiplying
f
and doing integration by parts.) We denote this constant by
\lambda
and so

\Theta^{(1)}-\left(\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega\right)=\lambda\omega.

By taking
\partial
on both sides, we deduce that
-\partial\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega=\lambda\partial\omega
.
Therefore

-\left(\partial\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega,\partial\omega\right)=\lambda(\partial\omega,\partial\omega)=\lambda\left\|\partial^{*}\omega\right\|^{2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(\partial\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega,\partial\omega\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega,\partial^{*}\partial\omega\right)=\left(\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega,-*\overline{\partial}*\partial*\omega\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega,*\overline{\partial\partial^{*}}\omega\right)=-\|\overline{\partial\partial^{*}}\omega\|^{2}+\|\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\|\overline{\partial\partial^{*}}\omega\|^{2}+\left(|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{4},1\right)=0

since
\|\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=\|\Lambda\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=\left(|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{4},1\right)
. Therefore, we obtain

(3.17)

\lambda\|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega\|^{2}=0.

If
\lambda\neq 0
, then
\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega=0
and so
\omega
is Kähler. If
\lambda=0
, then
u-|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}=0
.
∎

Actually, it is proved in
[
18
]
that if
u-|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}=0
, then
M
is a Hopf surface which is non-Kähler. In this case

(3.18)

\Theta^{(1)}-\left(\partial\partial^{*}\omega+\overline{\partial\partial}^{*}\omega\right)=0.

We refine Theorem
3.4
and obtain a generalization of Theorem
1.1
:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface. Suppose
\omega
is Gauduchon and weakly Hermitian-Einstein,

(3.19)

\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)=u\omega

for some
u\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. If there exists some Hermitian metric
\omega_{0}
such that
\int_{M}u\omega_{0}^{2}\leq 0
, then
\omega
is Kähler.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.4
,

u=\lambda+|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}

for some constant
\lambda
. If there exists some Hermitian metric
\omega_{0}
such that
\int_{M}u\omega_{0}^{2}\leq 0
, we deduce that either
\lambda=0
or
\lambda<0
. If
\lambda=0
, we have
|\partial^{*}\omega|^{2}=0
and so
\omega
is Kähler. If
\lambda<0
, by Theorem
3.4
,
\omega
is Kähler.
∎

The following results are esstential known to experts along this line.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface. Suppose
\omega
is weakly Hermitian-Einstein, i.e.

(3.20)

\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)=u\omega

for some
u\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. If
u\leq 0
, then
M
is a Kähler surface and
\omega
is conformal to a Kähler metric.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{f}=e^{f}\omega
be the Gauduchon metric in the conformal class of
\omega
. By using the curvature formula of the Chern connection, one has

\displaystyle\Theta_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}(\omega_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}(e^{f}h_{k\overline{\ell}})}{\partial z^{i}\partial\overline{z}^{j}}+e^{-f}h^{p\overline{q}}\frac{\partial(e^{f}h_{p\overline{\ell}})}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}\frac{\partial(e^{f}h_{k\overline{q}})}{\partial z^{i}}=e^{f}\Theta_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}-e^{f}h_{k\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{i}\partial\overline{z}^{j}}.

In particular,

(3.21)

\Theta^{(2)}(\omega_{f})=\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)-\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\overline{\partial}f\right)\omega=e^{-f}\left(u-\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\overline{\partial}f\right)\right)\omega_{f}.

For simplicity, we set

(3.22)

u_{f}:=e^{-f}u-\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f).

It is obvious that

\int_{M}u_{f}\omega_{f}^{2}=\int_{M}e^{-f}u\omega_{f}^{2}-\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f)\omega_{f}^{2}=\int_{M}e^{-f}u\omega_{f}^{2}\leq 0.

By Proposition
3.5
,
\omega_{f}=e^{f}\omega
is Kähler.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian surface with
\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)=-c^{2}\omega
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then
(M,\omega)
is Kähler-Einstein.

Proof.

Suppose that
\omega_{f}=e^{f}\omega
is a Gauduchon metric in the conformal class of
\omega
with
\inf_{M}f=0
. By Theorem
3.6
, we deduce that

\Theta^{(2)}(\omega_{f})=u_{f}\omega_{f}

and
\omega_{f}
is Kähler. Since
\Theta^{(1)}(\omega_{f})=\Theta^{(2)}(\omega_{f})
is
\partial\overline{\partial}
-closed, we have

\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u_{f}=0.

The strong maximum principle asserts that
u_{f}
is a constant. Since
f\geq 0
and

u_{f}=-e^{-f}c^{2}-\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f),\ \ \int_{M}u_{f}\omega_{f}^{2}=-\int_{M}e^{-f}c^{2}\omega_{f}^{2}\leq 0,

we deduce that

-c^{2}\leq u_{f}\leq 0.

On the other hand, at a minimum point
p
of
f
,
f(p)=0
, and we have

u_{f}=-c^{2}-\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f)(p)\leq-c^{2}.

Hence,
u_{f}=-c^{2}
. Therefore,
f=0
and
\omega_{f}=\omega
is Kähler.
∎

4.
Manifolds with non-positive second Chern-Ricci curvature

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
, Corollary
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
. The key ingredient is the following theorem, which is obtained by
using the Chern-Weil theory for a combination of metric connections on Hermitian line bundles.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be a compact complex manifold with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=n
and
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over
M
. For
i=1,\cdots,m
, let
h_{i}
be a smooth Hermitian metric on
L
and
\nabla_{i}
be a metric connection on
(L,h_{i})
. Let
R_{i}
be the
(1,1)
-component of the curvature tensor of
(L,h_{i},\nabla_{i})
. Suppose that there exist real numbers
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}R_{i}

is a quasi-positive
(1,1)
-form over
M
.

(1)

If
\sum_{i}a_{i}>0
, then the top interesection number
c^{n}_{1}(L)>0
;

(2)

If
\sum_{i}a_{i}<0
, then the signed top interesection number
(-1)^{n}c^{n}_{1}(L)>0
.

Proof.

Let
\nabla
be an arbitrary metric compatible connection on the Hermitian holomorphic line bundle
(L,h)
and
R\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M)
be its curvature tensor. There is a natural decomposition according to the degrees

(4.1)

R=\eta^{2,0}+\eta^{0,2}+\eta^{1,1}.

Since
\nabla
is metric compatible, we know

\eta^{0,2}=\overline{\eta^{2,0}}.

Let
\nabla^{\mathrm{ch}}
be the Chern connection on
(L,h)
and
\Theta\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M)
be its Chern curvature. By the Chern-Weil theory (e.g.
[
44
]
), one has

(4.2)

R-\Theta=d\beta

for some
1
-form
\beta
on
M
. We apply this to the family
\{(L,h_{i},\nabla_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
and deduce that

\sum a_{i}R_{i}-\sum a_{i}\Theta=d\beta_{0}+\eta_{0}^{2,0}+\overline{\eta_{0}^{2,0}}

for some
1
-form
\beta_{0}
and
(2,0)
-form
\eta^{2,0}_{0}
. Note that both of them depend on
a_{i}
.

Suppose
a=\sum_{i}a_{i}\neq 0
. We consider a
2
-form

(4.3)

W:=\frac{1}{a}\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}R_{i}-\eta_{0}^{2,0}-\overline{\eta_{0}^{2,0}}\right).

It is clear that

(4.4)

W-\Theta=a^{-1}d\beta_{0},

and

\displaystyle c_{1}^{n}(L)=\int_{M}\Theta^{n}=\int_{M}W^{n}=a^{-n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}{n\choose{2\ell}}{{2\ell}\choose\ell}\int_{M}(\eta_{0}^{2,0}\wedge\overline{\eta_{0}^{2,0}})^{\ell}\wedge\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}R_{i}\right)^{n-2\ell}.

If
\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}R_{i}
is quasi-positive, then

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}{n\choose{2\ell}}{{2\ell}\choose\ell}\int_{M}(\eta_{0}^{2,0}\wedge\overline{\eta_{0}^{2,0}})^{\ell}\wedge\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}R_{i}\right)^{n-2\ell}\geq 0

for
1\leq\ell\leq\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]
. Moreover, one has

\int_{M}\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}R_{i}\right)^{n}>0.

Hence, we obtain
c_{1}^{n}(L)>0
if
a>0
and
(-1)^{n}c_{1}^{n}(L)>0
if
a<0
.∎

We need the following curvature estimate in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a Hermitian manifold with
\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
. Then

(4.5)

2{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}-\Theta^{(1)}\leq\Theta^{(2)}

as Hermitian
(1,1)
-forms on
M
, where

\mathfrak{R}^{(1)}=\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{R}_{i\overline{j}k}^{k}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}.

Moreover, the identity holds if and only if
\omega
is Kähler.

Proof.

By using the curvature formula, we have

\Theta^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}=-\frac{\partial{{}^{c}\Gamma_{ik}^{k}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}},\ \ \Theta^{(2)}_{i\overline{j}}=-h_{p\overline{j}}h^{k\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial{{}^{c}\Gamma_{ki}^{p}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{\ell}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{levicivitacurvatureformula})}}{{=}}-\left(\frac{\partial\Gamma_{ik}^{k}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}-\frac{\partial\Gamma_{\overline{j}k}^{k}}{\partial z^{i}}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\Theta^{(1)}+\Theta^{(2)}-2{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}=\frac{\partial{{}^{c}\Gamma}_{ki}^{k}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}-2\frac{\partial\Gamma_{\overline{j}k}^{k}}{\partial z^{i}}-h_{p\overline{j}}h^{k\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial{{}^{c}\Gamma_{ki}^{p}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}\left(h^{k\overline{m}}\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{m}}}{\partial z^{k}}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\left(h^{k\overline{m}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}-\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{j}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}}\right)\right)-h_{p\overline{j}}h^{k\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{\ell}}\left(h^{p\overline{m}}\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{m}}}{\partial z^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h^{k\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}\cdot\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{m}}}{\partial z^{k}}-\frac{\partial h^{k\overline{m}}}{\partial z^{i}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}-\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{j}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}}\right)-h_{p\overline{j}}h^{k\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial h^{p\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{\ell}}\cdot\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{m}}}{\partial z^{k}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle h^{p\overline{q}}h_{k\overline{\ell}}\cdot T_{ip}^{k}\cdot\overline{T_{jq}^{\ell}},

where we use the condition
\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
in the identity
(*)
. Indeed,

\displaystyle h^{k\overline{m}}\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}\partial z^{k}}-h^{k\overline{m}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}-\frac{\partial h_{k\overline{j}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}}\right)-h_{p\overline{j}}h^{k\overline{\ell}}h^{p\overline{m}}\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{\ell}\partial z^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{k\overline{m}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}\partial z^{k}}+\frac{\partial^{2}h_{k\overline{j}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}\partial z^{i}}-\frac{\partial^{2}h_{k\overline{m}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}\partial z^{i}}-\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i\overline{j}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}\partial z^{k}}\right)=(\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega)_{i\overline{j}}.

The verification of
(**)
is straightforward. Hence, we have

(4.6)

\Theta^{(1)}+\Theta^{(2)}=2{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}+Q

where
Q:=\sqrt{-1}Q_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}=\sqrt{-1}h^{p\overline{q}}h_{k\overline{\ell}}\cdot T_{ip}^{k}\cdot\overline{T_{jq}^{\ell}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}.
It is obvious that
Q
is a Hermitian semi-positive
(1,1)
-form on
M
. Moreover, if
\Theta^{(1)}+\Theta^{(2)}=2{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}
, then
Q\equiv 0
and
T\equiv 0
. Hence,
\omega
is Kähler.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
We apply the Chern-Weil theory to the Hermitian manifold

(M,\omega)
. Let
E=T^{1,0}M
and
h
be the Hermitian metric induced by
\omega
. With respect to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}

on
(E,h)
, we have a decomposition

R^{\det E}=\eta^{2,0}+\eta^{0,2}+\eta^{1,1}.

It is obvious that

(4.7)

\eta^{1,1}=\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{R}_{i\overline{j}k}^{k}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}=\mathfrak{R}^{(1)},\ \ \ \quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta^{0,2}=\overline{\eta^{2,0}}.

If
\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and
\Theta^{(2)}
is quasi-positive, by Lemma
4.2
,

-2{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}+\Theta^{(1)}\geq-\Theta^{(2)}

is quasi-positive. By Theorem
4.1
, we obtain the conclusion that
(-1)^{n}c_{1}^{n}(M)>0
. This implies the non-vanishing of the cohomology groups
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
,
H^{1,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
and
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
. ∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.
By Lemma
4.2
,

-2{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}+\Theta^{(1)}=-\Theta^{(2)}+Q.

On a Hermitian surface, we have

(4.8)

Q=\sqrt{-1}h^{p\overline{q}}h_{k\overline{\ell}}\cdot T_{ip}^{k}\cdot\overline{T_{jq}^{\ell}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}=\left|\partial^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\omega

Indeed, we can verify it by using normal coordinates (e.g.
[
28
, Lemma 3.4]
) at a point
p\in M
. That is, there exist local holomorphic “normal
coordinates”
\{z^{i}\}
centered at
p
such that

(4.9)

h_{i\overline{j}}(p)=\delta_{ij},\ \ \ \Gamma_{ij}^{k}(p)=0,\ \ \ \Gamma_{\overline{j}i}^{k}(p)=\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{k}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}(p)=-\frac{\partial h_{i\overline{j}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{k}}(p).

If we set

(4.10)

a_{1\overline{1}}=\frac{\partial h_{2\overline{2}}}{\partial z^{1}}\frac{\partial h_{2\overline{2}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{1}},\ \ \ \ \ a_{1\overline{2}}=\frac{\partial h_{2\overline{2}}}{\partial z^{1}}\frac{\partial h_{1\overline{1}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{2}},\ \ \ \ a_{2\overline{1}}=\frac{\partial h_{1\overline{1}}}{\partial z^{2}}\frac{\partial h_{2\overline{2}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{1}},\ \ \ \ a_{2\overline{2}}=\frac{\partial h_{1\overline{1}}}{\partial z^{2}}\frac{\partial h_{1\overline{1}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{2}},

one can compute easily that

(4.11)

|\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega|^{2}=4\left(\frac{\partial h_{2\overline{2}}}{\partial z^{1}}\frac{\partial h_{2\overline{2}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{1}}+\frac{\partial h_{1\overline{1}}}{\partial z^{2}}\frac{\partial h_{1\overline{1}}}{\partial\overline{z}^{2}}\right)=4(a_{1\overline{1}}+a_{2\overline{2}})

and

(4.12)

Q=\sum_{i,j,p,q}\sqrt{-1}T_{ip}^{q}\overline{T_{jp}^{q}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}=4\sqrt{-1}(a_{1\overline{1}}+a_{2\overline{2}})(dz^{1}\wedge d\overline{z}^{1}+dz^{2}\wedge d\overline{z}^{2})

and so
Q=\left|\partial^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\omega
.

From the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we know

(4.13)

c^{2}_{1}(M)\geq\int_{M}\left(-\Theta^{(2)}+Q\right)^{2}\geq\int_{M}Q^{2}=\int_{M}|\partial^{*}\omega|^{4}\omega^{2}\geq 0.

Hence, if
c_{1}^{2}(M)=0
, then
\partial^{*}\omega=0
and
\omega
is Kähler. Otherwise,
c^{2}_{1}(M)>0
and by
[
22
, Theorem 9]
, we know
M
is projective. ∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.
Suppose
M
has a Hermitian metric with
\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)\leq 0
. Then the Chern scalar curvature
s_{\mathrm{{\mathbb{C}}}}\leq 0
. If
s_{\mathrm{{\mathbb{C}}}}=0
, then
\Theta^{(2)}(\omega)=0
. By Theorem
3.6
,
M
is Kähler and it is impossible. If
s_{\mathrm{{\mathbb{C}}}}
is quasi-negative, then by
[
38
, Theorem 1]
,
K_{M}^{-1}
is not pseudo-effective. But it is well-known that
K_{M}^{-1}
is semi-positive. This is a contradiction. ∎

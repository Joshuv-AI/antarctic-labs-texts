Parameter Dependent Chen–Fliess Series and Their Nonrecursive Interconnections
W. Steven Gray, Natalie Pham

Abstract
. A class of parameter dependent Chen–Fliess series is introduced where the series coefficients are taken from a noncommutative ring of multivariable differential operators. Such series are shown in the linear case to represent formal solutions to Cauchy initial value problems for nonhomogeneous PDEs and thus are useful for characterizing the input-output maps of distributed control systems. It is also shown that this class of functional series is almost closed under the set of nonrecursive interconnections, that is, any finite combination of parallel and series interconnections without a closed-loop. Some sufficient conditions are needed for the series interconnection. Specific examples are given involving the transport equation and the wave equation.

Introduction

Chen–Fliess functional series, which are infinite series of scaled iterated integrals, constitute a particularly convenient class
of nonlinear input-output systems due to their representations
in terms of noncommutative formal power series and all the various algebraic structures available in this
setting to support computations
[
8
,
9
,
11
,
18
]
.
In addition, their convergence properties are well
understood
[
8
,
13
,
25
,
26
,
30
]
,
they have a well developed realization theory
[
9
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
23
,
19
,
24
]
, and their relationship to input-output differential equations
has been extensively characterized
[
22
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
. As a class, they are closed under all the usual types of system interconnections encountered in
applications
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

In this paper,
a class of
parameter dependent
Chen–Fliess series is introduced where the series coefficients are taken from a noncommutative ring of multivariable differential operators
rather than from a field as done in the classical case (usually either
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
).
Such series have the potential to represent formal solutions to Cauchy initial value problems for nonlinear nonhomogeneous PDEs.
The concept is being employed implicitly in most inductive schemes for solving such problems (see, for example,
[
20
]
).
In this initial development of the
concept, the focus will be primarily on the linear case where the required algebraic machinery for doing computations is already available.
Of course, such PDE’s are often used to characterize the input-output maps of a distributed control systems. So its applications are immediate.
It should also be noted that the generalization present here is distinct from that given in
[
6
]
, where the coefficients
are from a field but instead the iterated integrals are matrix-valued and thus not commutative.

It is next shown that this class of functional series is
almost
closed under the set of nonrecursive interconnections, that is, any finite combination of parallel and series interconnections without a closed-loop.
Some sufficient conditions are needed for the series interconnection. This represents the first significant departure from the classical case.
Specific examples are then given involving the transport equation and the wave equation. Finally, feedback connections are
not
considered here as they
will likely require a major effort to determine under what conditions such interconnections are even well defined.
This is a direct consequence of the fact that the algebraic nature of the series interconnection is more complex in this new setting, and feedback interconnections are ultimately comprised of an infinite sequence of series interconnections.
Thus, that topic is deferred to future work.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, the general definition of a parameter dependent Chen–Fliess series is presented. It is then specialized to
the linear case in order to provide some sufficient conditions for convergence. In Section
3
,
the parallel and series connections are then considered. The conclusions of the paper are summarized in the final section.

2.
Parameter Dependent Chen–Fliess Series

The general case is first presented. Then the single-input, single-output (SISO) linear case is further developed.

2.1.
General Case

Fix an alphabet
X=\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
. Let
X^{\ast}
denote the set of all words over
X
including the
empty word
\emptyset
.
For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
let
u_{i}
be a real-valued function defined on
\Theta\times[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
, where
\Theta\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{\rm d}
with

{\rm d}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
T\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
fixed.
It will often be assumed that
t_{0}=0
. In addition,
u
is presumed to be a smooth function of the
parameters
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\rm d})\in\Theta
, and
for every fixed
\theta
assume that
u_{i}(\theta,\cdot)\in L_{1}[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
.
A
Chen series
in this setting is defined to be the formal power series

P(\theta,t)=\sum_{\eta\in X^{\ast}}E_{\eta}[u](\theta,t)\eta,

where
E_{\eta}
is defined inductively by

E_{x_{i}\bar{\eta}}[u](\theta,t)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}u_{i}(\theta,\tau)E_{\bar{\eta}}[u](\theta,\tau)\,d\tau

with
E_{\emptyset}:=1
,

x_{i}\in X
,
\bar{\eta}\in X^{\ast}
, and
u_{0}:=1
. Perfectly
analogous to the standard case
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, it follows that

\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}u_{i}x_{i}\right)P,\;\;P(\theta,t_{0})=\hbox{$1$},

where
\hbox{$1$}:=1\emptyset
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
be a multiindex with
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. For each
\alpha
there corresponds
a differential operator

D_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}\theta_{1}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{\rm d}}}{\partial^{\alpha_{\rm d}}\theta_{\rm d}}.

Define
\left|\alpha\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{\rm d}\alpha_{i}
.
Consider the ring
R
of smooth
{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell}
vector-valued functions on
\Theta
with multiplication defined componentwise.
Then the noncommutative ring of multivariate polynomial differential operators in
{\rm d}
variables over
R
is
denoted by

{\mathscr{D}}_{\rm d}:=R[D_{1},\ldots,D_{\rm d}]
, where
D_{i}:=\partial/\partial\theta_{i}
.
If
R
is replaced with
{\mathbb{R}}[\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d}]
in the definition, then
{\mathscr{D}}_{\rm d}
becomes the
d
-th Weyl algebra
[
5
]
.
For any word
\eta\in X^{\ast}
,
let
(c,\eta)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{\rm d}
so that

(c_{i},\eta)=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq n_{\eta}}a_{\alpha}D_{\alpha},\;\;a_{\alpha,i}\in C^{\infty}(\Theta),\;\;i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\},\;\;n_{\eta}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}_{\rm d}\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle
denote the linear space of formal power series over
X
with coefficients in
{\mathscr{D}}_{\rm d}
.
The following definition presents the central object of study in this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

An
m
-input,
\ell
-output parameter dependent Chen–Fliess series with generating series

c\in{\mathscr{D}}_{\rm d}\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle
is defined by the scalar product

\displaystyle y_{i}(\theta,t)

\displaystyle=F_{c_{i}}[u](\theta,t)=\sum_{\eta\in X^{\ast}}(c_{i},\eta)(\theta)(P[u](\theta,t),\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in X^{\ast}}(c_{i},\eta)(\theta)E_{\eta}[u](\theta,t),\;\;i=1,\ldots,\ell.

Of course, the summation above is only formal unless convergence can be proved. This issue is addressed
next in the linear setting.

2.2.
Single-Parameter SISO Linear Case

The single-parameter single-input, single-output case corresponds to setting
{\rm d}=1
,
m=1
, and
\ell=1
in the
construction above. In which case,
X=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
,
R=C^{\infty}(\Theta)
, and
D_{1}
is equivalent to the commutative
Weyl algebra when
R
is replaced with
{\mathbb{R}}[\theta]
.
To linearize the setup,
define the following two subsets of
X^{\ast}
:

X_{0}^{\ast}=\{\eta=x_{0}^{k}:k\geq 0\},\;\;X_{L}=\{\eta\in X^{\ast}:\left|\eta\right|_{x_{1}}=1\},

where
\left|\eta\right|_{x_{1}}
denotes the number of times the letter
x_{1}
appears in
\eta
.
The linearized Chen series is then

P_{L}[u]:=P_{L}^{0}[u]+P_{L}^{1}[u],

where

P_{L}^{0}[u]=\sum_{\eta\in X_{0}^{\ast}}E_{\eta}[u]\eta,\;\;P_{L}^{1}[u]=\sum_{\eta\in X_{L}}E_{\eta}[u]\eta.

Lemma 2.1
.

The series
P_{L}^{0}
,
P_{L}^{1}
, and
P_{L}
satisfy the following linear equations, respectively:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{L}^{0}[u]

\displaystyle=x_{0}P_{L}^{0}[u]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{L}^{1}[u]

\displaystyle=x_{0}P_{L}^{1}[u]+u_{1}x_{1}P_{L}^{0}[u]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{L}[u]

\displaystyle=x_{0}P_{L}[u]+u_{1}x_{1}P_{L}^{0}[u].

Proof:
All the identities follow directly from the following calculation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{L}[u]

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{L}^{0}[u]+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{L}^{1}[u]

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{\eta\in X_{0}^{\ast}}\eta E_{\eta}[u]+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{\eta\in X_{L}}\eta E_{\eta}[u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in X_{0}^{\ast}}x_{0}\eta E_{\eta}[u]+\sum_{\eta\in X_{L}}x_{0}\eta E_{\eta}[u]+u_{1}\sum_{\eta\in X_{0}^{\ast}}x_{1}\eta E_{\eta}[u]

\displaystyle=x_{0}P_{L}[u]+u_{1}x_{1}P_{L}^{0}[u].

Definition 2.2
.

A SISO parameter dependent linear Chen–Fliess series with generating series

c\in{\mathscr{D}}_{1}\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle
is defined by the scalar product

(1)

y(\theta,t)=F_{c,L}[u](\theta,t)=\sum_{\eta\in X_{0}^{\ast}\cup X_{L}}(c,\eta)(\theta)(P_{L}[u](\theta,t),\eta).

The following lemma will be useful for providing sufficient conditions under which the series (
1
) converges.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
a,b,T\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
a<b
.
Let
u
be smooth in
\theta\in\Theta=[a,b]
and assume that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}

\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right\|_{1}=\int_{a}^{b}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\theta^{k}}u(\theta,\tau)\right|\,d\tau\,d\theta

is finite.
Then given any
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, it follows that

\left|E_{x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j}}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right](\theta,t)\right|\leq\frac{i^{i}j^{j}T^{i+j}}{i!j!(i+j)^{i+j}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right\|_{1}

for all
(\theta,t)\in[a,b]\times[0,T]
.

Proof:
From Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\left|E_{x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j}}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right](\theta,t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-\tau)^{i}}{i!}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\theta^{k}}u(\theta,\tau)\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}\,d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\frac{(T-\tau)^{i}}{i!}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\theta^{k}}u(\theta,\tau)\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}\right|\,d\tau\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{(T-\tau)^{i}}{i!}\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{i^{i}j^{j}T^{i+j}}{i!j!(i+j)^{i+j}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right\|_{1}.

Corollary 2.1
.

There exists a constant
K_{E}>0
such that

\left|E_{x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j}}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right](\theta,t)\right|\leq K_{E}\frac{T^{i+j}}{(i+j)!}\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right\|_{1}

for all
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
(\theta,t)\in[a,b]\times[0,T]
.

Proof:
The claim is a direct result of Lemma
2.2
and
Stirling’s formula
i^{i}/i!\sim{\rm e}^{i}/\sqrt{2\pi i}
,
i\gg 1
.

The next theorem provides sufficient conditions under which the zero state
portion of (
1
) converges when

(c,x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j})=\alpha_{i+j}\frac{\partial^{i+j}}{\partial\theta^{i+j}}.

In short, if certain growth rates on
\|\alpha_{k}\|_{\infty}
and
\|\partial^{k}u/\partial\theta^{k}\|_{1}
apply
as a function of
k
, then the linear Chen–Fliess series converges provided that
[a,b]
and
[0,T]
are sufficiently small in length.
That is, these conditions ensures convergence in a local sense.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\Theta=[a,b]
is a finite interval so that each
\alpha_{i+j}\in L_{\infty}[a,b]
.
Assume there exist real numbers
K_{\alpha},M>0
satisfying

\|\alpha_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq K_{\alpha}M^{k}k!,\;\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Let
u
be smooth in
\theta
with
\partial^{k}u/\partial\theta^{k}\in L_{1}([a,b]\times[0,T])
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Assume there exist
real numbers
K_{u},R>0
so that

\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial\theta^{k}}\right\|_{1}\leq K_{u}R^{k},\;\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Then the parameter dependent linear Chen–Fliess series

F[u](\theta,t)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{i+j}(\theta)\frac{\partial^{i+j}}{\partial\theta^{i+j}}E_{x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j}}[u](\theta,t)

converges absolutely and uniformly on
[a,b]\times[0,T]
when
MRT<1
.

Proof:
It follows from the stated assumptions and Corollary
2.1
that

\displaystyle|F[u](\theta,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\|\alpha_{i+j}\|_{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial^{i+j}}{\partial\theta^{i+j}}E_{x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j}}[u](\theta,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}K_{\alpha}M^{i+j}(i+j)!\;K_{E}\frac{T^{i+j}}{(i+j)!}\left\|\frac{\partial^{i+j}u}{\partial\theta^{i+j}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=K_{\alpha}K_{E}K_{u}\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(MRT)^{i+j}

\displaystyle=K_{\alpha}K_{E}K_{u}\left(\frac{1}{1-MRT}\right)^{2}

provided that
MRT<1
.

The following theorem introduces a more restrictive growth condition on the series coefficients so that the condition
MRT<1
above is no longer required.
That is,
[a,b]
and
[0,T]
need only be finite. This is viewed as a type of global convergence.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
\Theta=[a,b]
is a finite interval so that each
\alpha_{i+j}\in L_{\infty}[a,b]
.
Assume there exist real numbers
K_{\alpha},M>0
satisfying

\|\alpha_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq K_{\alpha}M^{k}(k!)^{s},\;\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

where
0\leq s<1
.
Let
u
be smooth in
\theta
with
\partial^{k}u/\partial\theta^{k}\in L_{1}([a,b]\times[0,T])
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Assume there exist
real numbers
K_{u},R>0
so that

\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\theta^{k}}u\right\|_{1}\leq K_{u}R^{k},\;\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Then the parameter dependent linear Chen–Fliess series

F[u](\theta,t)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{i+j}(\theta)\frac{\partial^{i+j}}{\partial\theta^{i+j}}E_{x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j}}[u](\theta,t)

converges absolutely and uniformly on
[a,b]\times[0,T]
.

Proof:
Similar to the proof of the previous theorem, observe

\displaystyle|F[u](\theta,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\|\alpha_{i+j}\|_{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial^{i+j}}{\partial\theta^{i+j}}E_{x_{0}^{i}x_{1}x_{0}^{j}}[u](\theta,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}K_{\alpha}M^{i+j}((i+j)!)^{s}\;K_{E}\frac{T^{i+j}}{(i+j)!}\left\|\frac{\partial^{i+j}u}{\partial\theta^{i+j}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=K_{\alpha}K_{E}K_{u}\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(MRT)^{i+j}\frac{((i+j)!)^{s}}{(i+j)!}

\displaystyle=K_{\alpha}K_{E}K_{u}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)(MRT)^{k}\frac{(k!)^{s}}{k!}.

The final step above follows from the standard double summation formula
\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}b_{i,j}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{k}b_{l,k-l}
, where now
b_{l,k-l}

is invariant with respect to
l
.
Define the sequence
a_{k}=(k+1)(MRT)^{k}(k!)^{s}/(k!)
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and observe that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}\right|=MRT\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k+2}{k+1}\cdot\frac{1}{(k+1)^{1-s}}=0

since
0\leq s<1
.
Thus, from the ratio test, the series converges absolutely and uniformly on
[a,b]\times[0,T]
.

Example 2.1
.

Consider a single-parameter SISO system
u\mapsto y
defined in terms of the transport equation

(2)

\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}+V\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}=u,

where
(\theta,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
V\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Assume the smooth initial condition

y(\theta,0)=y_{0}(\theta)
.
The assertion is that the formal solution to this linear PDE
can be written in the form
y=F_{c,L}[u]
for
some suitable generating series
c\in{\mathscr{D}}_{1}\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle
.
To determine this series, first observe from Lemma
2.1

that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\left(c,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{L}[u]\right)

\displaystyle=(c,x_{0}P_{L}[u])+u(c,x_{1}P_{L}^{0}[u]),

or equivalently,

\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}-(x_{0}^{-1}(c),P_{L}[u])=u(x_{1}^{-1}(c),P_{L}^{0}[u]).

(For brevity
u_{1}
is written here as
u
.)
Comparing this identity against the transport equation yields the system of equations:

(3)

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{-1}(c),P_{L}^{0}[u])

\displaystyle=1

(4)

\displaystyle-(x_{0}^{-1}(c),P_{L}[u])

\displaystyle=V\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}.

Since
y=(c,P_{L}[u])
, it also follows that

y(\theta,0)=(c,P_{L}[u](\theta,0))=(c,\emptyset)(\theta)=y_{0}(\theta).

Equation (
3
) has the explicit form

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(c,x_{1}x_{0}^{k})\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=1,\;\;\forall t\geq 0.

Thus,

c=y_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(c,x_{0}^{k})x_{0}^{k}+x_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(c,x_{0}^{k}x_{1})x_{0}^{k}x_{1}.

It follows from (
4
) that

-(x_{0}^{-1}(c),P_{L}^{0}[u])-(x_{0}^{-1}(c),P_{L}^{1}[u])=V\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta},

where

V\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}=V\left(\frac{\partial c}{\partial\theta},P_{L}^{0}[u]\right)+V\left(\frac{\partial c}{\partial\theta},P_{L}^{1}[u]\right)+V\left(c,\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}P_{L}^{1}[u]\right).

Thus, one must conclude that

\displaystyle(c,x_{0}^{k+1})

\displaystyle=-V\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(c,x_{0}^{k})

\displaystyle(c,x_{0}^{k+1}x_{1})

\displaystyle=-V\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(c,x_{0}^{k}x_{1})-V(c,x_{0}^{k}x_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}.

Therefore,

c=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-V\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)^{k}y_{0}\,x_{0}^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-V\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)^{k}x_{0}^{k}x_{1},

which yields the Chen–Fliess series solution to the transport equation

(5)

y=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-V\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)^{k}y_{0}E_{x_{0}^{k}}[u]+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-V\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)^{k}E_{x_{0}^{k}x_{1}}[u].

This series solution of the transport equation was computed in
[
21
]
via state space methods. A somewhat simpler approach is
given in Section
3.2
.
If, for example,
V
is constant and
u(\theta,t)=t\sin(\omega\theta)
, then it follows that

\displaystyle y(\theta,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left.\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}(y_{0}(\theta-Vt))\right|_{t=0}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-V)^{k}\frac{t^{k+2}}{(k+2)!}\frac{d^{k}}{d\theta^{k}}\sin(\omega\theta)

\displaystyle=y_{0}(\theta-Vt)+\frac{\sin(\omega(Vt-\theta))+\sin(\omega\theta)-V\omega t\cos(\omega\theta)}{(V\omega)^{2}}.

From Theorem 2.3, the series converges globally with growth constants

K_{\alpha}=1
,
M=|V|
,
K_{u}=T^{2}(b-a)/2
,
R=|\omega|
, and
s=0
.

3.
Nonrecursive System Interconnections

In this section, the nonrecursive interconnections of parameter dependent Chen–Fliess series are characterized.
The most frequently encountered of such interconnections in applications are the parallel and
series interconnections. The focus here will primarily be on the SISO case. The main two questions to be addressed are: Under what conditions is this
class of systems closed under a given interconnection topology, and, when appropriate, what is the generating series of the composite system?
As will be seen, these are purely algebraic problems when the operators are viewed as formal series.
In addition, there are also important analytical problems that arise. For example, are these interconnections
well-posed
in the
sense that all the inputs and outputs in a given network are well defined functions when the operators are convergent in some sense?
Furthermore, is the composite system convergent given that its subsystems
are convergent? Is the sense of convergence preserved? For example, if the subsystems are all globally convergent, is the
composite system also globally convergent or perhaps only locally convergent?
In the classical case, these issues were addressed comprehensively in
[
25
,
26
,
30
]
.
Such problems tend to be quite technical and outside the scope of the present paper.

3.1.
Parallel Connections

Parallel interconnection of
F_{c}
and
F_{d}
.

Consider two systems
F_{c}
and
F_{d}
interconnected in parallel as shown in Figure

.

and the outputs are combined by some binary operation

.

Theorem 3.1
.

Define the alphabets
X_{c}=\{x_{0},x_{c}\}
,
X_{d}=\{x_{0},x_{d}\}
, and
X=X_{c}\cup X_{d}
.
Let

and
d\in{\mathscr{D}}_{{\rm d}_{d}}\langle\langle X_{d}\rangle\rangle
and define the
associated systems
y_{c}=F_{c}[u_{c}]
and
y_{d}=F_{d}[u_{d}]
.
The parallel sum interconnection

y(\theta,t)=F_{c}[u](\theta_{c},t)+F_{d}[u](\theta_{d},t)

with
\theta:=(\theta_{c},\theta_{d})\in\Theta_{c}\times\Theta_{d}
has the representation
y=F_{c+d}[u]

with
c+d\in{\mathscr{D}}_{{\rm d}_{c}+{\rm d}_{d}}\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle
.
Likewise,
the parallel product interconnection

y(\theta,t)=F_{c}[u](\theta_{c},t)F_{d}[u](\theta_{d},t)

has the representation
y=F_{c{\,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}}\,}d}[u]
with
c{\,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}}\,}d\in{\mathscr{D}}_{{\rm d}_{c}+{\rm d}_{d}}\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle
.

Example 3.2
.

Consider two single-parameter SISO transport systems

\displaystyle y_{c}(\theta_{c},t)=F_{c}[u](\theta_{c},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-V_{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{c}}\right)^{k}E_{x_{0}^{k}x_{c}}[u](\theta_{c},t)

\displaystyle y_{d}(\theta_{d},t)=F_{d}[u](\theta_{d},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(-V_{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{d}}\right)^{l}E_{x_{0}^{l}x_{d}}[u](\theta_{d},t).

The parallel sum connection is characterized by the linear generating series

c+d=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-V_{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{c}}\right)^{k}x_{0}^{k}x_{c}+\left(-V_{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{d}}\right)^{k}{x_{0}^{k}x_{d}}.

It constitutes a transport system in the sense that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial y_{c}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial y_{d}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\left(-V_{c}\frac{\partial y_{c}}{\partial\theta_{c}}+u\right)+\left(-V_{d}\frac{\partial y_{d}}{\partial\theta_{d}}+u\right)

\displaystyle=-[V_{c}\;\;V_{d}]\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}+2u.

The parallel product connection has the generating series

c{\,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}}\,}d=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(-V_{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{c}}\right)^{k}\left(-V_{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{d}}\right)^{l}x_{0}^{k}x_{c}{\,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}}\,}{x_{0}^{l}x_{d}},

which is no longer linear, and thus, does not represent a linear transport system.

Theorem 3.2
.

Define the alphabets
X_{c}=\{x_{0},x_{c}\}
and
X_{d}=\{x_{0},x_{d}\}
. Let

c\in{\mathscr{D}}_{{\rm d}}\langle\langle X_{c}\rangle\rangle
and
d\in{\mathscr{D}}_{{\rm d}}\langle\langle X_{d}\rangle\rangle
be the generating series for

y_{c}=F_{c}[u_{c}]
and
y_{d}=F_{d}[u_{d}]
, respectively. If
F_{c}
is linear, then
the series interconnection

y(\theta,t)=(F_{c}\circ F_{d})[u](\theta,t)

has the representation
F_{c\circ d}
, where the composition product
c\circ d\in{\mathscr{D}}_{{\rm d}}\langle\langle X_{d}\rangle\rangle
.

Proof:
It is first shown by induction on the length of the word

\eta\in X_{c}^{\ast}
that
E_{\eta}\circ F_{d}=F_{\eta\circ d}

for any
d\in{\mathscr{D}}_{{\rm d}}\langle\langle X_{d}\rangle\rangle
.
Trivially,

(E_{\emptyset}\circ F_{d})[u]=E_{\emptyset}[F_{d}[u]]=E_{\emptyset}[u]=F_{\psi_{d}(\emptyset)(\hbox{\scriptsize$1$})}[u].

Next, assume that the claim holds for all words
\eta
up to length
k\geq 0
. Then
for any
x_{i}\in X_{c}
observe that

\displaystyle E_{x_{i}\eta}[F_{d}[u]](t,t_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}F_{d_{i}}[u](\tau)\,E_{\eta}[F_{d}[u]](\tau,t_{0})\,d\tau

\displaystyle=F_{x_{0}(d_{i}{\,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 3.5pt\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}{\mathbin{\hbox{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt}\hrule}\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\hskip 2.6pt\vrule height=3.0pt,width=0.0pt\vrule}\hrule}}}}}\,}\psi_{d}(\eta)(\hbox{\scriptsize$1$}))}[u](t)

\displaystyle=F_{\psi_{d}(x_{i}\eta)(\hbox{\scriptsize$1$})}[u](t),

where
d_{0}=\hbox{$1$}
and
d_{c}=d
.
Thus, the identity in question holds for every
\eta\in X_{c}^{\ast}
,
and all the shuffle products are well defined from the linearity assumption.
Finally, observe that

\displaystyle(F_{c}\circ F_{d})[u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in X_{c}^{\ast}}(c,\eta)E_{\eta}[F_{d}[u]]=\sum_{\eta\in X_{c}^{\ast}}(c,\eta)F_{\psi_{d}(\eta)(\hbox{\scriptsize$1$})}[u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in X_{c}^{\ast}}(c,\eta)\left[\sum_{\nu\in X_{d}^{\ast}}(\psi_{d}(\eta)(\hbox{$1$}),\nu)E_{\nu}[u]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in X_{d}^{\ast}}\left[\sum_{\eta\in X_{c}^{\ast}}(c,\eta)(\psi_{d}(\eta)(\hbox{$1$}),\nu)\right]E_{\nu}[u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in X_{d}^{\ast}}(c\circ d,\nu)\>E_{\nu}[u]

\displaystyle=F_{c\circ d}[u].

y=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)^{k+l}E_{x_{0}^{k+l+1}x_{1}}[u].

As a parallel sum connection it has the representation

y=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)^{k}E_{x_{0}^{k+1}x_{1}}[u]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)^{k}E_{x_{0}^{k+1}x_{1}}[u].

4.
Conclusions

A class of parameter dependent Chen–Fliess series was introduced where the series coefficients are taken from a noncommutative ring of multivariable differential operators.
Sufficient conditions for convergence were given for a class of such series in the linear case.
It was also shown that these functional series are closed under the set of nonrecursive interconnections modulo some sufficient conditions for the series interconnection.
Specific examples are given involving the transport equation and the wave equation.

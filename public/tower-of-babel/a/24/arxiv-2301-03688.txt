Sign-changing solutions for the sinh–Poisson equation with Robin Boundary condition
Pablo Figueroa, Leonelo Iturriaga, Erwin Topp

Abstract
. Given ϵ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \epsilon\in(0,1) and λ > 1 \lambda>1 , we address the existence of solutions for the Sinh-Poisson equation with Robin boundary value condition { Δ ​ u + ϵ 2 ​ ( e u − e − u ) = 0 in ​ Ω ∂ u ∂ ν + λ ​ u = 0 on ​ ∂ Ω , \begin{cases}\Delta u+\epsilon^{2}(e^{u}-e^{-u})=0&\mbox{in }\Omega\\ \frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\lambda u=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases} where Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} is a bounded smooth domain. We prove two existence results under a suitable relation between ϵ \epsilon small and λ \lambda large. When Ω \Omega is symmetric with respect to an axis, we prove the existence of a family of solutions u ϵ , λ u_{\epsilon,\lambda} concentrating at two points with different spin, both located on the symmetry line and close to the boundary. In the second result, we assume Ω \Omega is not simply connected and we construct sign-changing solutions concentrating at points located close to the boundary, each of them on a different connected component of the boundary.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
a bounded domain with smooth boundary.
In this paper we are interested in study of the singular perturbation problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta u+\epsilon^{2}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}u:=\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\lambda u&=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\nu(x)
denotes the exterior unit normal on
x\in\partial\Omega
, and
\epsilon,\lambda
are positive parameters. Our main concern is the construction of sign-changing solutions when
\epsilon
is small, and simultaneously,
\lambda
is large.

Robin boundary condition (also known as boundary condition of the third type) can be seen as a combination of Neumann and Dirichlet boundary condition, in a proportion cast by
\lambda
. In
[
4
]
, Berestycki and Wei study concentration phenomena for the least energy solution of equations of Ni-Takagi type with Robin boundary condition. In fact, they prove the existence of
\lambda^{*}
such that, for
\lambda\geq\lambda^{*}
, the problem resembles the behavior of the Dirichlet problem studied (c.f.
[
31
]
), meanwhile for
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
problem is closer to the one with Neumann boundary condition (c.f.
[
30
]
). In terms of applications, Robin boundary condition is considered in biological models
[
16
]
, and thermal conductivity
[
10
]
.

If we formally take the limit
\lambda\to\infty
in (
1.1
), the problem becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta u+\epsilon^{2}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&=0,&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u&=0,&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

This equation is tipically referred as
\sinh
-Poisson equation
with Dirichlet boundary condition, and it has connection with the description of two-dimensional turbulent Euler flows, see Onsager
[
32
,
23
,
28
,
6
,
27
]
for a physical discussion of this problem.
Roughly speaking, the location of vortices in the flow can be described through concentration points of the solution to (
1
).

In this respect, Bartolucci and Pistoia
[
2
]
prove that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a solution to problem (
1
) that concentrates around stable critical points of the Hamiltonian associated to the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega
, given by

(1.5)

\varphi_{m}(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H(\xi_{i},\xi_{i})+\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}a_{j}G(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}),\quad(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k})\in\Omega^{k},

as
\epsilon\to 0
. Here,
a_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
for all
i=1,...,m
determine the spin configuration of the concentrating points;
G
is the Green function with Dirichlet boundary condition

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}-\Delta_{x}G(x,y)&=8\pi\delta_{y}(x)&\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
G(x,y)&=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and
H
is the regular part of the Green function, also referred as the associated Robin function. Moreover, it is proven in
[
2
]
that

u^{\epsilon}(x)\to 8\pi\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}G(x,\xi_{i})\quad\mbox{in}\ C^{1,\sigma}(\bar{\Omega}\setminus\{\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m}\}),\ \mbox{as}\ \epsilon\to 0,

and the solution concentrates each
\xi_{i}
with a sign determined by
a_{i}
. Concentration points are away to the boundary in consonance with the Dirichlet condition.

Positive concentrating solutions, concentrating around critical points of a Hamiltonian function draws back to the early nineties in the seminal work of Nagasaki and Suzuki
[
29
]
for the Liouville equation (namely, when
e^{u}-e^{-u}
is replaced by
e^{u}
in (
1
)) with Dirichlet boundary condition, and it is shown the effect of the domain determines the existence of concentration, and also the location of the blow-up points. In the case of the
\sinh
-Poisson equation, negative concentration points are allowed . In
[
2
]
, the authors provide the existence of solutions with two concentration points with different sign, provided
\Omega
is axially symmetric. This result was later extended by Bartsch, Pistoia and Weth
in
[
3
]
, where the authors prove the existence of solutions with an arbitrary number of concentrating points, located in the symmetry axis and alternating sign. For general domains, they prove the result for
3
and
4
concentrating points, under suitable configuration of the spins. We also would like to mention the contributions in
[
14
,
15
]
concerning construction of solutions to equations with nonlinearities of exponential type in dimension two, in
[
18
,
20
]
for compact Riemann surfaces, and in
[
13
]
for fractional equations.

Concerning Robin boundary condition, in
[
12
]
the authors address the Liouville equation with Robin boundary condition

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta u+\epsilon^{2}e^{u}&=0&\mbox{in}\Omega,\\
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}u&=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and prove the existence of positive solutions with arbitrary number of concentration points, located at critical points of the Hamiltonian function

(1.8)

\varphi_{m}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H_{\lambda}(\xi_{i},\xi_{i})+\sum_{i\neq j}^{m}G_{\lambda}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}),\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},...,m)\in\Omega^{m},

where, this time,
G_{\lambda}
is the Green function

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}-\Delta G_{\lambda}(x,y)&=8\pi\delta_{y}(x)&x\in\Omega\\
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}(G_{\lambda}(\cdot,y))&=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and in this case,
H_{\lambda}
is the associated Robin function, defined as

(1.10)

H_{\lambda}(x,y)=G_{\lambda}(x,y)-\Gamma(x-y),\quad x,y\in\Omega,

where
\Gamma(x-y)=-4\log|x-y|
is the fundamental solution for the Laplacian in the plane. The analysis in
[
12
]
(see also
[
7
,
33
]
) uses in a crucial way the asymptotic behavior
H_{\lambda}
when
\lambda
is large. Accurate asymptotics can be found in Dávila, Kowalczyk and Montenegro in
[
11
]
, showing that the behavior of the map
x\mapsto H_{\lambda}(x,x)
in the normal direction to the boundary develops a strict minima at distance of order
O(\lambda^{-1})
to
\partial\Omega
. Thus, critical points for
\varphi_{m}
in (
1.8
) can be found for tuples
(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m})
such that each
\xi_{i}
is sufficiently close to the boundary, and away each other. In fact, two types of different solutions can be found, one associated to minima of
\varphi_{m}
, and the other associated to a critical point of linking-type.

Our approach follows several of the concepts discussed above. In the first main result, we rely on axial symmetry just as in
[
2
,
3
]
to conclude the existence of sign-changing, two-point concentrating solution in a certain regime of
\epsilon
small and
\lambda
large.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is bounded domain with smooth boundary, and symmetric with respect to the axis
x
. Denote
(a,b)=\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. Then, for each
\alpha>16
, there exist
\lambda_{0}>1,\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that, for every
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and
\epsilon
satisfying
\epsilon\lambda^{\alpha}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, problem (
1.1
) has a solution
u_{\epsilon,\lambda}
that concentrates with different sign at two points
\xi_{i}=(t_{i},0),i=1,2
, with

|t_{1}-a|,|t_{2}-b|=O(\lambda^{-1}).

Here, by
concentration at a point

\xi\in\Omega
we mean that for all
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\sup_{B_{\delta}(\xi)}|u_{\epsilon,\lambda}|\to+\infty
as
\epsilon\to 0,\lambda\to\infty
. Notice that by the invariance of the problem, if
u_{\epsilon,\lambda}
is solution,
-u_{\epsilon,\lambda}
is also a solution.

The proof of the previous theorem rely on Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, and most of the technical arguments are performed over the equation (
2.17
), equivalent to (
1.1
). Our approach in the construction of approximate solution are rather close to those present in
[
12
,
17
,
15
]
. We use a two-parameter family of entire solutions of the Liouville equation
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as the first ansatz, in junction with smooth correctors that allows us to satisfy the boundary condition. The reduced problem corresponds to that of adjusting variationally the location of the concentrating points, as critical points of an energy functional associated to the weak formulation of the problem. In a saturation regime of the parameters, that functional is close to the Hamiltonian
\varphi_{m}
, which is given by

(1.11)

\varphi_{m}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H_{\lambda}(\xi_{i},\xi_{i})+\sum_{i\neq j}^{m}a_{i}a_{j}G_{\lambda}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}),\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},...,m)\in\Omega^{m},

with
a_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
for
i=1,..,m
(
m=2
in the setting of Theorem
1.1
). Notice that in contrast with (
1.8
), this time the contribution of the Green function maybe unbounded from below for tuples
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\Omega\times\Omega
close to the diagonal if
a_{1}a_{2}=-1
, and this makes more difficult to locate concentrating points as minima. The relation among
\epsilon
and
\lambda
in the theorem allows us to control the error terms to get the criticality from the minima of the Robin function showed in
[
11
]
, which occurs close to boundary. In particular, the method leads to a relation among
\epsilon
and
\lambda
involving logarithmic corrections that explains the corresponding hypothesis in Theorem
1.1
, see Proposition (
5.2
). For simplicity, we state our main result in a simpler form, at the expense of a non sharp estimate.

Concentration near the boundary is in big contrast with previous results regarding interior concentration, see for instance
[
29
,
5
,
24
,
26
,
1
,
19
,
17
]
. We believe that by methods similar to the ones presented here, solutions with interior concentration can be constructed for (
1.1
), since we expect to have
H_{\lambda}(\cdot,\xi)\to H(\cdot,\xi)
as
\lambda\to\infty
, locally uniform in
\Omega
, where
H
is the regular part of the Green function with Dirichlet boundary condition. These solution are associated to maxima for the Hamiltonian function. We do not pursue in this direction and focus on concentration near the boundary.

In our second main result, we are able to replace the symmetry assumption of the previous theorem by a topological one.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\Omega
is not simply connected, and let
\Gamma_{1},..,\Gamma_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be the connected components of
\partial\Omega
, and let
k\in\{1,...,m\}
. Then, for each
\alpha>16
there exist
\lambda_{0}>1
and
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for each
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and each
\epsilon
satisfying
\epsilon\lambda^{\alpha}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, problem (
1.1
) has a sign-changing solution
u_{\epsilon,\lambda}
that concentrates at points
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k}\in\Omega
, with
\mathrm{dist}(\xi_{i},\Gamma_{i})=O(\lambda^{-1})
for all
i=1,...,k
, and where
\Gamma_{i}\neq\Gamma_{j}
if
i\neq j
.

This theorem follows the same strategy of the previous result, where as before the concentration points are also minima to the Hamiltonian function. In this case, the uniform distance among connected components of the boundary of the domain allows us to control the contribution of the Green function in
\varphi_{m}
, no matter the sign of the interacting points is. In fact, in the notation of the theorem, we have
\sum_{k=1}^{n}2^{k}\binom{n}{k}
different solutions (including the ones concentrating at one point, and the solutions due to the invariance of the equation).

It is well-known that the use of index/linking arguments have been successful for the study of criticality of smooth functionals, and it has been employed in problems similar to ours, see for example
[
17
,
1
,
12
]
and its references. It would be interesting to know if solutions of saddle-point type could be obtained for this problem, but we did not pursue in this direction.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, describing a first approximation solution to problem (
1.1
) and estimating the error. Section
3
is
devoted to perform the finite dimensional reduction. In Section
4
we study the associated nonlinear problem. Section
5
contains the asymptotic expansion of
the reduced energy. Finally, in Section
6
we will prove our main results.

Preliminaries and ansatz for solutions

In this section, we denote

2.1.
Preliminaries about Green and Robin function.

We start providing some estimates for the Green function with Robin boundary condition
G_{\lambda}
in (
1.9
), and its regular part
H_{\lambda}
in (
1.10
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\xi\in\Omega
.

•

For each
\delta>0
small, there exists
C
depending on
\delta
and
\Omega
such that if
d(\xi)\geq\delta
we have

\|H_{\lambda}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq C.

•

There exists
\delta\in(0,1)
small and
C_{\Omega}>0
large depending on the smoothness of
\Omega
, such that, for all
\lambda
large in terms of
\delta
, if
(\lambda\log\lambda)^{-1}\leq d(\xi)\leq\delta
, then

(2.1)

-C_{\Omega}+2\log|x-\xi^{*}|\leq H_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\leq C_{\Omega},

where
\xi^{*}\in\Omega^{c}
is the reflection of
\xi
with respect to the tangent to
\partial\Omega
supported at the projection of
\xi
.

•

In particular, for any
0<\delta
there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that

(2.2)

\|G_{\lambda}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\setminus\overline{B_{\delta}(\xi)}\right)}\leq C_{\delta}.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\Omega
close to the boundary.
Up to rotation and translation, we can assume that the projection of
\xi
onto the boundary is the origin, and
\nu(0)=-e_{2}
. We denote
\xi^{*}=(0,-d(\xi))
.

We divide the analysis depending on the distance of
\xi
to the boundary. Let
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,1)
to be specified later on.

By definition of
H_{\lambda}
, for each
x\in\partial\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}(x)=\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}(-\Gamma)=

\displaystyle|x-\xi|^{-2}(x-\xi)\cdot\nu(x)+\lambda\log|x-\xi|.

If
d(\xi)\geq\tilde{\delta}
, then it is easy to see using maximum principle for Robin boundary condition (see Lemma 2.1 in
[
12
]
) that

\|H_{\lambda}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\tilde{\delta}}.

From here, we assume
(\lambda\log\lambda)^{-1}\leq d(\xi)\leq\tilde{\delta}
.

For
x\in\partial\Omega
such that
|x-\xi|\geq\tilde{\delta}
we have

|\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}(x)|\leq\lambda C_{\Omega},

where
C_{\Omega}=\log\max\{1,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\}+\tilde{\delta}^{-1}
.

From here, we concentrate on the points
x
on the boundary such that
|x-\xi|\leq\tilde{\delta}
.

We start with upper bounds for
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}
. If
|x-\xi|\leq\lambda^{-1}
, then

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}(x)\leq\lambda\log\lambda+\lambda\log|x-\xi|\leq 0,

meanwhile, for
\lambda^{-1}\leq|x-\xi|\leq\tilde{\delta}
we have

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}(x)\leq\lambda+\lambda\log(\tilde{\delta})\leq 0,

provided
\tilde{\delta}\leq e^{-1}
.

From here, using maximum principle for Robin boundary condition, using
C_{\Omega}
as supersolution, we conclude the upper bound in (
2.1
).

For the lower bound, we start recalling that for all
|x-\xi|\leq\tilde{\delta}
on the boundary, we have
|x-\xi|
and
|x-\xi^{*}|
are comparable, in the sense that there exists
c_{\Omega}\in(0,1)
such that

c_{\Omega}\leq\frac{|x-\xi|}{|x-\xi^{*}|}\leq c_{\Omega}^{-1},\quad x\in\partial\Omega\cap B(\xi,3\tilde{\delta}).

This is a consequence of the smoothness of the domain.

If
|x-\xi|\geq\lambda^{-\theta}
for
\theta\in(1/2,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}\geq

\displaystyle-|x-\xi|^{-1}+\lambda\log|x-\xi|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\lambda^{\theta}+\lambda\log|x-\xi^{*}|+\lambda\log c_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\lambda(\log|x-\xi^{*}|+\log c_{\Omega}).

Tomamos
S=\log|x-\xi^{*}|
y

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}S=\widehat{x-\xi^{*}}\cdot\nu(x)|x-\xi^{*}|^{-1}+\lambda\log|x-\xi^{*}|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\Omega}^{-\theta}\lambda^{\theta}+\lambda\log|x-\xi^{*}|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(c_{\Omega}^{-\theta}\lambda^{\theta-1}+\log|x-\xi^{*}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lambda\log|x-\xi^{*}|

If
|x-\xi|\leq\lambda^{-\theta}
, we use that
x
can be writen as
x=(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))
with
\psi(0)=0,\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
,
\psi^{\prime\prime}
bounded, from which

\widehat{(x-\xi)}\cdot\nu(x)=\frac{x^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}-\psi+d(\xi)}{\sqrt{1+(\psi^{\prime})^{2}}\sqrt{|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(\psi-d(\xi))^{2}}}\geq\frac{1}{2},

for all
\lambda
large enough. This implies that

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}\geq\frac{c_{\Omega}}{2|x-\xi^{*}|}+\lambda\log|x-\xi^{*}|+\lambda\log c_{\Omega}.

Thus, we use the function
S(x)=2\log|x-\xi^{*}|-2\log c_{\Omega}
, which, in view of the estimates above, satisfies
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}H_{\lambda}\geq\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}S
on
\partial\Omega
. Hence, we use maximum principle again, to conclude that
H_{\lambda}\geq S
in
\Omega
. This leads to the lower bound in (
2.1
). The remaining estimates can be easily obtained from (
2.1
) and the definition of
H,G
and
\Gamma
. This completes the proof.

2.2.
Ansatz for the solution. Reduced problem.

In this section we will construct an approximation of the solution to problem (
1.1
). Then we estimate the error of such approximation in a suitable norm. The basic idea is to consider a parameter
\mu>0
and the functions

(2.3)

w_{\mu}(x)=\log\frac{8\mu^{2}}{(\mu^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

which are solutions to the Liouville equation in the whole plane

(2.4)

\Delta u+e^{u}=0\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

An actual solution will have an asymptotic profile as
\epsilon\to 0
and
\lambda\to+\infty
which resembles these solutions, properly translated and rescaled in terms of these parameters. Specifically, we choose our scaling parameter as

(2.5)

\rho:=\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda^{2}}.

Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset(0,+\infty)
,
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
, and for each
j=1,...,m
, we denote

(2.6)

\begin{split}w_{j}(x)&=w_{\mu_{j}\rho}(x-\xi_{j})+2\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}=\log\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}+|x-\xi_{j}|^{2})^{2}}+2\log\frac{\rho}{\epsilon},\end{split}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is easy to see that for each
j
the function
w_{j}
satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Delta w_{j}+\epsilon^{2}e^{w_{j}}=0\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

In order to satisfy the Robin boundary condition we introduce harmonic functions
H_{j}
satisfying

(2.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}-\Delta H_{j}&=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}(H_{j})&=-\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}(w_{j})&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Our single-point ansatz takes the form

(2.9)

U_{j}(x)=w_{j}(x)+H_{j}(x),\quad x\in\Omega.

Using the explicit form of
w_{j}
in (
2.6
) together with (
2.8
), we can use maximum principle in Lemma 2.1 in
[
12
]
over
x\mapsto H_{\lambda}(x,\xi_{j})-H_{j}(x)
to conclude that

H_{j}(x)+2\log\frac{\rho}{\epsilon}+\log(8\mu_{j}^{2})-H_{\lambda}(x,\xi_{j})=O\bigg(\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}}{d_{j}^{2}}\bigg)+O\left(\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}}{\lambda d_{j}^{3}}\right),

uniformly in
\bar{\Omega}
. Here and in what follows, we have adopted the notation
d(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
for
x\in\Omega
, and
d_{j}=d(\xi_{j})
.

Thus, in view of the definition of
w_{j}
and the estimate above, we have the expansion

U_{j}(x)=G_{\lambda}(x,\xi_{j})-2\log\Big(1+\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}}{|x-\xi_{j}|^{2}}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}}{d_{j}^{2}}\Big).

Now we provide the first estimates concerning the ansantz when we consider the expected location of the concentration points
\{\xi_{j}\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega,\{\mu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset(0,+\infty)
satisfying

(2.10)

\displaystyle d_{j}\in(\delta\lambda^{-1},\delta^{-1}\lambda^{-1}),

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\in(\delta,\delta^{-1}),

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then, for each

(2.12)

\begin{split}H_{j}(x)=H_{\lambda}(x,\xi_{j})-\log(8\mu_{j}^{2})+4\log\lambda+O\big(\rho^{2}\lambda^{2}\big),\end{split}

uniformly in
\bar{\Omega}
; and for each

.

Consider
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
with

) as

(2.14)

\displaystyle U(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}U_{j}(x).

Following the directions of
[
17
]
, we study the problem in expanded variables depending on
\rho

). For this, we consider the function

for

, so that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta v+\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}(e^{v}-e^{-v})&=0&\mbox{in }\Omega_{\rho},\\
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda\rho}v&=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega_{\rho}.\end{array}\right.

We also adopt the notation
\xi_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\rho}\xi_{j}
,
V_{j}(y)=U_{j}(\rho y)
and

Thus, considering

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}L\phi&=-[R+\Lambda\phi+N(\phi)]&\mbox{in }\Omega_{\rho},\\
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda\rho}\phi&=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega_{\rho},\end{array}\right.

where

(2.17)

\displaystyle L\phi=\Delta\phi+W\phi,\quad\mbox{ with }\quad W=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2})^{2}},

(2.18)

\displaystyle\Lambda\phi=\Big[(\epsilon\rho)^{2}(e^{V}+e^{-V})-W\Big]\phi,

(2.19)

\displaystyle N(\phi)=(\epsilon\rho)^{2}\left[e^{V}(e^{\phi}-\phi-1)-e^{-V}(e^{-\phi}+\phi-1)\right],

(2.20)

\displaystyle R(y)=\Delta V+(\epsilon\rho)^{2}(e^{V}-e^{-V}).

2.3.
First estimates for problem (

In what follows, we recall relevant estimates concerning the Robin function

\displaystyle v(\theta)=-2\theta-4\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2\theta s}}{(1+s)^{2}}ds.

It is possible to see that

The Robin function obeys the expansion

for each

For reasons that will be made clear later in the next section, for measurable functions

\|h\|_{*}=\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\rho}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}(1+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|)^{-2-\sigma}+\rho^{2}\right)^{-1}|h(y)|,

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega

\delta>0

Set

(2.26)

\displaystyle\log(8\mu_{j}^{2})=H_{\lambda}(\xi_{j},\xi_{j})+\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}G_{\lambda}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})+4\log\lambda.

Then, there exists

just depending on

for all

Moreover, we have

and

Proof:
The uniform estimates for
\mu_{j}
are a consequence of the expansion of the Robin function (

), together with the estimates for the Green function in Lemma
2.2
.

We estimate

by dividing the analysis in different regions. Note that by definition of

and (

Case 1:
Assume that

and for

where the

and

) we have

\max_{a=\pm 1}\{e^{aV_{j}(y)}\}\leq C\max_{a=\pm 1}\{e^{aG_{\lambda}(\rho y,\xi_{j})}\}.

From Lemma
2.1
we deduce that

is uniformly bounded away

. This together with (

|R(y)|=|\Delta V+\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}g(V)|\leq C(\rho^{4}+\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2})\quad\mbox{ in }\ \Omega_{\rho}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\overline{B_{\delta/\rho}(\xi_{j}^{\prime})}.

Case 2:
Now, assume that
\delta/(\rho\lambda\log\lambda)\leq|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|\leq\delta/\rho
for some

. As in (
2.28
) in the previous case, we see that

\displaystyle|\Delta V_{j}(y)|=\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{4}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}+\delta^{2}[\lambda\log\lambda]^{-2})^{2}}=O(\rho^{4}\lambda^{4}\log^{4}\lambda)=O(\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}\log\lambda).

Using again estimates (
2.13
), (
2.1

, we arrive at

If

Taking into account that

becomes

and

in

\displaystyle|\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}g(V(y))|\leq C\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}\left[\frac{\lambda^{4}}{\rho^{4}|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{4}}+\rho^{4}\lambda^{4}|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{4}\right],

for

. This estimate allows us to conclude that

|R(y)|\leq C\epsilon\lambda^{7}\log\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{3}},\quad y\in\Omega_{\rho}\cap(B_{\delta/\rho}(\xi_{j}^{\prime})\setminus B_{\delta/(\lambda\rho\log\lambda)}(\xi_{j}^{\prime})),

in view of

\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}\left[\log\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{\rho^{4}|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{4}}+\rho^{4}\lambda^{4}|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{4}\right][1+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{3}]\leq C\epsilon\lambda^{7}\log\lambda

for
y\in\Omega_{\rho}\cap(B_{\delta/\rho}(\xi_{j}^{\prime})\setminus B_{\delta/(\lambda\rho\log\lambda)}(\xi_{j}^{\prime}))
.

Case 3:
Finally, assume that

for some

. In this case, by the condition over the points

(we can choose

Note that we get that for

in the considered region where the

\delta
and
\mu_{j}
.

Now, we deal with the exponential term. We first assume that
a_{j}=1
. Notice that

\begin{split}\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}g(V)&=\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}(e^{w_{j}(\rho y)}e^{H_{j}(\rho y)+\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}V_{i}}-e^{-V}).\end{split}

By estimates (
2.12

\displaystyle e^{V(y)}

\displaystyle=e^{w_{j}(\rho y)}e^{H_{j}(\rho y)+\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}V_{i}}

\displaystyle=e^{w_{j}(\rho y)}\exp\Big(H_{\lambda}(\rho y,\xi_{j})+\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}G_{\lambda}(\rho y,\xi_{i})-\log(8\mu_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+4\log\lambda+O\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}_{j}\rho^{2}}{d_{j}^{2}}\Big)\Big)

At this point, we notice that by a first-order Taylor expansion, for

in this region we have

(2.32)

H_{\lambda}(x,\xi_{j})=H_{\lambda}(\xi_{j},\xi_{j})+O(\lambda\log\lambda\,|x-\xi_{j}|),

and for

and
\lambda

Using this and assumption (

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}e^{V(y)}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}e^{w_{j}(\rho y)}\cdot\exp\bigg(O(\lambda\log\lambda\,|\rho y-\xi_{j}|)+O\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}_{j}\rho^{2}}{d_{j}^{2}}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2})^{2}}\left[1+O(\rho\lambda\log\lambda|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|)+O(\rho^{2}\lambda^{2})\right]

Using the same computation, it is possible to see that

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}e^{-V(y)}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}\frac{(\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}+|\rho y-\xi_{j}|^{2})^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{8\mu_{j}^{2}\rho^{2}}\;O(1)

\displaystyle=O\left(\rho^{4}\epsilon^{4}[1+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|]^{4}\right)=O\left(\rho^{4}\epsilon^{4}\Big[\frac{\delta}{\rho\lambda}\Big]^{4}\right)=O(\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2})

From here, we conclude that

(2.34)

\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}g(V)=\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2})^{2}}\left[1+O(\rho\lambda\log\lambda|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|)+O(\rho^{2}\lambda^{2})\right]+O(\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2})

We finish the proof by assuming
a_{j}=-1
. Similarly as above, we have the following estimates

\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}g(V)=\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}(e^{V}-e^{w_{j}(\rho y)}e^{H_{j}(\rho y)+\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}V_{i}}).

\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}e^{-V(y)}=\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2})^{2}}\left(1+O(\rho\lambda\log\lambda|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|)+O(\rho^{2}\lambda^{2})\right).

and

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}e^{V(y)}

\displaystyle=O\left(\rho^{4}\epsilon^{4}[1+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|]^{4}\right)=O(\rho^{2}\epsilon^{2}).

Thus, we get (
2.34

2.31

2.35

and estimates (
2.29

2.27

3.
Linear problem

In this section we shall reduce the solvability of (
2.16
) by using the so-called Lyapunov-Schmidt finite dimensional variational reduction and prove the main result. In this procedure an important step is the solvability theory for the linear operator, obtained as the linearization of (
2.16
) at the approximating solution
V
, namely, (
2.17
). Observe that, as
\epsilon\to 0
and
\lambda\to+\infty
, formally the operator
L
, around a point
\xi_{j}^{\prime}
, approaches
L_{j}
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as

L_{j}(\phi)=\Delta\phi+\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2})^{2}}\phi,\quad j=1,\dots,m.

We start this section with some notation. Given
v_{j}=v_{\mu_{j}}
the entire functions solving the equation

\Delta v+\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{(\mu_{j}^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}v=0\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

as a consequence of the invariance under dilations and traslations of the problem (
2.4
), it is possible to find nontrivial solutions of this equations and, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, are denoted by

(3.1)

\begin{split}z_{ij}(x)=&\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{i}}v_{j}(|x+\zeta|)\Big|_{\zeta=0}=\frac{4\mu_{j}x_{i}}{\mu_{j}^{2}+|x|^{2}},\quad i=1,2,\ j=1,...,m,\\
z_{0j}(x)=&\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(v_{j}(|sx|)+2\log(s))\Big|_{s=1}=\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}-|x|^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}+|x|^{2}},\quad j=1,...,m.\end{split}

The kernel of
L_{j}
in

is non-empty and is spanned by the functions
Z_{ij}
,
i=0,1,2
, due to the intrinsic invariances of the problem (
2.16
), where

(3.2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ Z_{0j}(y)=\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}-|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}},\quad\ \ \ Z_{ij}(y)=\frac{4\mu_{j}(y-\xi_{j}^{\prime})_{i}}{\mu_{j}^{2}+|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}},\ \ i=1,2,

see
[
1
]
for a proof. Consider a large but fixed number
R_{0}>0
and a radial
smooth cut-off function
\chi
with
\chi(r)=1
if
r<R_{0}
and

\chi(r)=0
if
r>R_{0}+1
. Write

(3.3)

\displaystyle\chi_{j}(y)=\chi\left(|y-\xi^{\prime}_{j}|\right).

Recalling
L
in (
2.17
), the main result of this section is the following

Proposition 3.1
.

There exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and
\epsilon>0
satisfying
0<\rho\lambda<\epsilon_{0}
, for any points
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})
satisfying (
2.10
) and for any

with

, there is a unique solution
\phi:=T_{\lambda}(h)
and coefficients

,

,

of problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle L(\phi)=h+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{ij}\chi_{j}Z_{ij}&{\rm in}\ \Omega_{\rho}\\[11.38092pt]

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\rho}}\chi_{j}Z_{ij}\phi=0&{\rm for}\ i=1,2,\ j=1,\dots,m.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the map

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho})}\leq C|\log(\rho\lambda)|\ \|h\|_{*},\quad|c_{ij}|\leq C\|h\|_{*},

\|\partial_{(\xi_{l})_{k}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho})}\leq C|\log(\rho\lambda)|^{2}\ \|h\|_{*},\quad\text{for }\ \ l=1\dots,m,\ k=1,2.

In order to prove the above result, we require some a priori estimates. We start with the following result which can be found in Lemma 3.2 in
[
12

.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for

\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and

\phi
of the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta\phi+W\phi=h&{\rm in}\ \Omega_{\rho};\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}+\rho\lambda\phi=g&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega_{\rho};\\[8.53581pt]

Proof of main results

Recall

Concerning

\begin{split}h(\theta)&=4\log\theta+O(1)\quad\text{as }\ \theta\to+\infty,\\

Furthermore, from (

6.1.
Symmetric case.

Here we follow closely the arguments in
[
3
]
. For this, we assume the domain
\Omega
is such that
\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}\neq\emptyset
, and that it is symmetric with respect to the reflection at
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. In this setting, we have
G_{\lambda}
in (
1.9
) is also symmetric with respect to this reflection in the following sense: for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega
, let
\tilde{x}=(x_{1},-x_{2})
, then

(6.1)

G(x,y)=G(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\quad\mbox{for all}\ x\neq y.

In fact, defining
\tilde{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)=G_{\lambda}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, we have

-\Delta_{x}\tilde{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)=-\Delta_{x}G_{\lambda}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=8\pi\delta_{\tilde{y}}(\tilde{x})=8\pi\delta_{y}(x),

meanwhile, for each
x\in\partial\Omega
, using that
\nu(\tilde{x})=(\nu(x)_{1},-\nu(x)_{2})
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\tilde{G}_{\lambda}(x,y)=\nabla_{x}G_{\lambda}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\cdot(\nu(x)_{1},-\nu(x)_{2})+\lambda G_{\lambda}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}G(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=0,

from which, using the uniqueness of the Green function, we conclude the symmetry property.
The symmetry condition is inherited by
H_{\lambda}
as
H_{\lambda}(\tilde{x})=H_{\lambda}(x)
. For a
m
-tupe
\xi=(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m})
, we denote
\tilde{\xi}=(\tilde{\xi}_{1},...,\tilde{\xi}_{m})
, and

\mu_{\xi,j}=\mu_{\tilde{\xi},j}
for
\mu
as in (
2.26
), and

\varphi_{m}(\xi)=\varphi_{m}(\tilde{\xi})
, where
\varphi
is defined in (
5.4
).

We stress the notation by writing
w_{\xi,j}(x),U_{\xi,j}(x)
as the functions introduced in (
2.6
), (
2.9
) with the particular choice of
\xi
.
Then, by related invariance of the equation, it is possible to see that
w_{\tilde{\xi},j}(\tilde{x})=w_{\xi,j}(x)
, and that
x\mapsto U_{\tilde{\xi},j}(\tilde{x})
solves the same equation than
U_{\xi,j}(x)
, from which

U_{\xi,j}(x)=U_{\tilde{\xi},j}(\tilde{x})
and therefore
R_{\xi}(x)=R_{\tilde{\xi}}(\tilde{x})
.

Now, if we denote
c(\xi)=(c_{ij}(\xi))_{ij}
and
(c(\xi),\phi_{\xi})
as the unique solution for (
4.1
) given in Proposition
4.1
,
we consider the function
\tilde{\phi}(x)=\phi_{\tilde{\xi}}(\tilde{x})
, it is possible to prove that
\tilde{\phi}
satisfies the same equation than
\phi_{\xi}
, from which they are equal by uniqueness.

Then, by definition of
F
and using the previous symmetry properties, we have

F(\tilde{\xi})=F(\xi)

from which we have
\theta(\xi)=\theta(\tilde{\xi})
. Thus, we have that
F
is a
C^{1}
and symmetric with respect to
x
perturbation of the function
\varphi

has critical points whenever the function

has stable critical points.

Now we are ready to provide the

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Assume
\Omega
is simply connected, contains the origin and it is symmetric with respect to the
x
-axis.
By symmetry and the expansion of the energy given in Proposition
5.2
, it suffices to prove that there is a (nondegenerate) critical point to the function

(t_{1},t_{2})\mapsto\varphi_{2}((t_{1},0),(t_{2},0)),\quad t_{1},t_{2}\in(a,b),

where we identify the set
\{\xi\in\Omega:\xi_{2}=0\}
with the interval
(a,b)
. With a slight abuse of notation, we denote this function by
\varphi_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})
. We are interested in sign-changing solutions, from which
\varphi_{2}
takes the form

\varphi_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})=H_{\lambda}((t_{1},0),(t_{1},0))+H_{\lambda}((t_{2},0),(t_{2},0))-2G_{\lambda}((t_{1},0),(t_{2},0)).

Using the expansion (
2.23
), we have

\varphi_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})=-8\log\lambda+h(\lambda d(t_{1},0))+h(\lambda d(t_{2},0))-2G_{\lambda}((t_{1},0),(t_{2},0))+O(\lambda^{-1}),

where
O(\lambda^{-1})
does not depend on
(t_{1},t_{2})
.

Fix
\delta=(b-a)/4
and for
K>1
to be determined, consider the set

\Omega_{0}=\{(t_{1},t_{2})\in(a,b)^{2}:\lambda d((t_{i},0))\in(K^{-1},K),i=1,2;\ |t_{1}-t_{2}|>\delta\}.

Let us stress that our symmetry assumption implies that
(t_{1},t_{2})\in\partial\Omega_{0}
if and only if
\lambda d((t_{i},0))\in\{K^{-1},K\}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
.
First, we choose
(\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*})\in\Omega_{0}
with
\xi_{i}^{*}\in S^{*}
, namely,
\lambda d(\xi_{i}^{*})=\theta_{0}
, for
i=1,2
so that, by the positivity of the Green’s function and (
2.23
)

\begin{split}\varphi_{2}(\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*})&=H_{\lambda}(\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{1}^{*})+H_{\lambda}(\xi_{2}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*})-2G_{\lambda}(\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*})\\
&\leq H_{\lambda}(\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{1}^{*})+H_{\lambda}(\xi_{2}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*})\leq-8\log\lambda+2h(\theta_{0})+\frac{C^{\prime}}{\lambda}\end{split}

Let
C_{\delta}>0
such that
|G(x,y)|\leq C_{\delta}
for all
x,y\in\Omega
with
|x-y|\geq\delta
and
d(x),d(y)\geq(\lambda\log\lambda)^{-1}
. If
\lambda d((t_{i},0))=K^{-1}
for some

i=1
), we have that
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\partial\Omega_{0}
and by (
2.2
), we can write

\begin{split}\varphi_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})&\geq-8\log\lambda+h(K^{-1})+h(\theta_{0})-C_{\delta}-\frac{C^{\prime}}{\lambda}.\end{split}

Now, we fix
K
large just depending on
h(\theta_{0})
and
C_{\delta}(\Omega)
such that

h(K^{-1})>C_{\delta}+h(\theta_{0})+2,

which is valid for all

large enough such that
(\lambda\log\lambda)^{-1}\leq K^{-1}\lambda^{-1}
. Hence, choosing
\lambda
larger, if necessary, we also get that
\frac{2C^{\prime}}{\lambda}<1
.
The same estimate can be found if
\lambda d(t_{i},0)=K
. From here, we deduce that for any

\begin{split}\varphi_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})&\geq-8\log\lambda+2h(\theta_{0})-h(\theta_{0})+h(K)-C_{\delta}(\Omega)-\frac{C^{\prime}}{\lambda}\\
&\geq-8\log\lambda+2h(\theta_{0})+\frac{C^{\prime}}{\lambda}+1\\
&>\varphi_{2}(\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*}).\end{split}

This implies that

, from which there is an interior minima of

, which is stable under symmetric approximations. Therefore, by using Proposition

in

for

6.2.
Not simply connected case.

Taking advantage of previous estimates we are now ready to

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

Assume that

Let us stress that our assumption

In other words, we will find a sign-changing solution

to (

) having a positive bubble centered at

with

.

(\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{m}^{*})\in\Omega_{K}
with
\xi_{i}\in S^{*}
for all
i
, namely,
\lambda d(\xi_{i}^{*})=\theta_{0}
, for all

so that, by the positivity of the Green’s function we obtain that

\begin{split}\varphi_{m}(\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{m}^{*})&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H_{\lambda}(\xi_{i}^{*},\xi_{i}^{*})+\sum_{i,j\in I_{1}\atop i\neq j}G_{\lambda}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})+\sum_{i,j\in I_{2}\atop i\neq j}G_{\lambda}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})\\

, namely,

(say

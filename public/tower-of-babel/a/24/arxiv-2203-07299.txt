Embedding between Lebesgue and weak Lebesgue sequence spaces is strictly singular Thanks: The second author was supported by the grant P201-18-00580S of the Grant Agency of the Czech Republic
J. Lang, A. Nekvinda

Abstract
. Given 1 < p < q < ∞ 1<p<q<\infty it is well know that the natural embedding of Lebesgue sequence spaces ℓ p ↪ ℓ q \ell_{p}\hookrightarrow\ell_{q} is strictly singular. In this paper we extend this classical results and show that even the natural non-compact embedding between Lebesgue and weak Lebesgue sequence spaces ℓ p ↪ ℓ p , ∞ \ell_{p}\hookrightarrow\ell_{p,\infty} is strictly singular.

Primary 47G10, Secondary 47B10

1.
Introduction

It is true generally acknowledge that among all bounded operators acting on Banach spaces the compact operators hold a quite unique position since they play an essential role in many different areas of mathematics. Then also operators, which are in some “sense” close to compact operators, deserve detailed study. Among all classes of non-compact operators which are “close” to compact maps the special position is occupied by strictly singular and by finitely strictly singular operators (for definition see Sec. 2).

Let us mention a couple of examples highlighting importance of strictly singular operators.
It is well know that Fredholm operators are invariant when perturbed by strictly singular operators
(i.e. if
T
is Fredholm and
S
is strictly singular then
T+S
is Fredholm, see
[
1
, Therem 4.63]
). And that the limiting case of Fourier transformation
\mathcal{F}
considered as an operator from
L^{1}
into
L^{\infty}
, which is obviously non-compact, is strictly singular (see
[
6
]
).
Also when we consider limiting Sobolev embedding
E_{d}
(here by limiting Sobolev embedding we understand Sobolev embedding for which is impossible to ”significantly” increase the source space or decrease the target space without loosing the boundedness):

(1.1)

\displaystyle E_{d}:W^{1}_{0}L^{d,1}((0,1)^{d})\hookrightarrow C((0,1)^{d}),

where
W^{1}_{0}L^{d,1}((0,1)^{d})
denote a space of all functions
u
for which
|\nabla u|
belongs to Lorentz space
L^{d,1}
and
u
has a zero trace. It was observed, from the behavior of strict s-numbers for
E_{d}
, in
[
3
]
that
E_{d}
, which is non-compact, is finitely strictly singular and then strictly singular map.

From the above examples arises a natural question: Are all “limiting” Sobolev embeddings on bounded domain strictly singular?

In many cases, as in (
1.1
), the limiting Sobolev embeddings have the optimal source or the optimal target space equal to Lorentz space. In order to be able study the above question we need to have more accurate information about relation between Lorentz spaces, for instance if the natural embedding between Lebesgue sequence and Lorentz sequence spaces

(1.2)

I:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{p,\infty},

is strictly singular. This question, which can be also considered as a natural generalization of of well known fact that for
1<p<q<\infty
the non-compact embedding
\ell_{p}\hookrightarrow\ell_{q}
is strictly singular (see
[
1
, Therem 4.58]
), is the focus of our paper and we will prove that the non-compact embedding (
1.2
) is strictly singular.

The paper is structured as follows. In Sect. 2, we recall definitions
and we collect all necessary later-needed material and technical lemmas. In Sect. 3 is proved that embedding
\ell_{p}\hookrightarrow\ell_{p,\infty}
is strictly singular.

2.
preliminaries

In this section we recall definitions, notations and some technical lemmas needed in Section 3.
We start by recalling the definition of strictly singular operators.

Definition 2.1
.

A bounded operator
T:X\to Y
between Banach spaces is said to be strictly singular if there is no infinite dimensional closed subspace
Z
of
X
such that
T:Z\to T(Z)
, the restriction of
T
to
Z
, is an isomorphism.

See
[
1
, section 4.5]
for more information about strictly singular operators. Note that it is not difficult to see that each compact operator is strictly singular. Also it is worthy to mention that there exists a class of operator called
finitely strictly singular operators
which lays between compact and strictly singular operators. These are operators for which Bernstein numbers are vanishing,
b_{n}(T)\to 0
.

By
\#(F)
we denote the number of elements of a finite set
F
. For a sequence
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)
we define the corresponding modulus sequence
|u|=(|u_{1}|,|u_{2}|,\dots)
. We say that
|u|\leq|v|
if
|u_{i}|\leq|v_{i}|
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we introduce Banach function spaces which we will need later.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a a set of all sequences of real numbers and
\|.\|:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
. Assume that
\|.\|
satisfies for all
u,v\in\mathcal{S}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(i)

\|u+v\|\leq\|u\|+\|v\|
,

(ii)

\|\alpha u\|=|\alpha|\ \|u\|
,

(iii)

\|u\|\geq 0
and
\|u\|=0
if and only if
u=0
,

(iv)

\|u\|=\|\ |u|\ \|
,

(v)

if
|u|\leq|v|
then
\|u\|\leq\|v\|
,

(vi)

if
0\leq u_{n}\nearrow u
then
\|u_{n}\|\nearrow\|u\|
,

(vii)

if
\#\{i;u_{i}\neq 0\}<\infty
then
\|u\|<\infty
.

Define
X:=\{u;\|u\|<\infty\}
. Then we call
X
a sequence Banach function space.

Remark that each Banach function space is complete (for details see
[
2
]
, Theorem 1.6).

We repeated the definition of strictly singular operators on Banach function spaces by the following alternative definition:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces and assume that
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a linear bounded operator. We say that
T
is strictly singular operator if

\displaystyle\inf\{\|Tx\|_{Y};\|x\|_{X}=1,x\in Z\}=0

for each infinite dimensional subspace
Z\subset X
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a sequence
a=(a(1),a(2),\dots)\in c_{0}
we set for
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\mu_{a}(\lambda)=\#\{i;|a(i)|>\lambda\}

and

\displaystyle a^{*}(j)=\min\{\lambda>0;\mu_{a}(\lambda)\leq j\}.

We will call
a^{*}=(a^{*}(1),a^{*}(2),\dots)
as a non-increasing rearrangement of
a
.

For a sequence
a=(a(1),a(2),\dots)\in c_{0}
we define

\displaystyle\textup{supp }a=\{j\in\mathbb{N};a(j)\neq 0\}.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a sequence
u=(u(1),u(2),\dots)\in c_{0}
with
\textup{supp }u=\{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots n_{k}
. Define a non-increasing rearrangement
u^{\diamond}
of
u
with respect to
\textup{supp }u
by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\diamond}(n_{j})=u^{*}(j)&\ j\in\{1,2,\dots,k\},\\
u^{\diamond}(i)=0&\ i\notin\{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{k}\}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.6
.

If
\textup{supp }u:=\{n+1,n+2,\dots,m\}
then

(2.1)

\displaystyle u^{\diamond}(j)=u^{*}(j-n).

In the next we recall the definition of sequence spaces.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. We set for a sequence
u
norms:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}=\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(u^{*}(j))^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p,\infty}=\sup\ j^{1/p}\ u^{*}(j),

and we define Lebesgue space
\ell_{p}
and weak Lebesgue space
\ell_{p,\infty}
as collections of all sequences
u
for which the norms
\|u\|_{p}
and
\|u\|_{p,\infty}
are finite, respectively.

Unfortunately,
\|.\|_{p,\infty}
do not satisfy the triangle inequality (i). But it is well-known (see
[
2
]
, Lemma 4.5) that for
1<p<\infty
there is a norm
\|.\|_{(p,\infty)}
equivalent to
\|u\|_{p,\infty}
which satisfies (i), …, (vii). So, we can assume that
\|.\|_{p,\infty}
satisfies (i) and we will mentioned the next well known lemma without proof.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then both spaces
\ell_{p}
and
\ell_{p,\infty}
are Banach function spaces and
\ell^{p}\hookrightarrow\ell^{p,\infty}

Given
u\in\ell_{p}\ (u\in\ell_{p,\infty})
we will write
u(i)
for the value of
u
at the index
i
.

We denote by
D_{p}
the norm of this embedding, i.e.

(2.2)

\displaystyle D_{p}=\sup\{\|a\|_{p,\infty};\|a\|_{p}\leq 1\}.

For a sequence
b=(b(1),b(2),\dots)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}

we define

\displaystyle P_{m}(b)=(b(1),b(2),\dots,b(m),0,0,\dots)

\displaystyle R_{m}(b)=b-P_{m}b=(0,0,\dots,0,b({m+1}),b({m+2}),\dots).

Definition 2.9
.

Let
X
be a Banach function space of sequences. We say that
u\in X
has an absolutely continuous norm in
X
, written
u\in X_{a}
, if

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|R_{m}(u)\|_{X}=0.

We say that
X
has an absolutely continuous norm if
X_{a}=X
.

We left proofs for the next two lemmas to the reader.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then
\ell_{p}
has an absolutely continuous norm, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|R_{m}(u)\|_{p}=0\ \text{ for each }\ u\in\ell_{p}.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Assume that
X\subset\ell_{p}
be a subspace,
\dim X=\infty
.
Set

\displaystyle X_{n}=\{a\in X;a(1)=a(2)=\dots=a(n)=0\}.

Then
\dim X_{n}=\infty
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
X\subset\ell_{p}
be a closed subspace with
\dim X=\infty
.
Assume
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0,1>\delta>0
. Then there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
u\in X_{n}
such that denoting
v:=P_{m}u
,
w:=R_{m}u
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}=1,

(2.4)

\displaystyle m>2n,\ m\geq N

(2.5)

\displaystyle\textup{supp }v\subset\{n+1,n+2,\dots,m\},

(2.6)

\displaystyle|v(j)|\leq\varepsilon\text{ for all }j,

(2.7)

\displaystyle 1-\delta\leq\|v\|_{p}\leq 1,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{p}\leq\delta.

Proof.

Set
n_{0}:=n
and
construct, by induction, sequences
n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots
and
u_{i}\in X
such that for
v_{i}:=P_{n_{i}}u_{i}
,
w_{i}:=R_{n_{i}}u_{i}
we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\textup{supp }v_{i}\subset\{n_{i-1}+1,n_{i-1}+2,\dots,n_{i}\},

(2.10)

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\delta}{2^{i}}\leq\|v_{i}\|_{p}\leq 1.

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|w_{i}\|_{p}\leq\frac{\delta}{2^{i}}.

Since
\dim X_{n}=\infty
we can find
u_{1}\in X_{n}
with
\|u_{1}\|_{p}=1
. Take
n_{1}>n
such that
\|R_{n_{1}}u_{1}\|_{p}\leq\delta/2
. Denote
v_{1}:=P_{n_{1}}u_{1}
,
w_{1}:=R_{n_{1}}u_{1}
. Clearly,
\textup{supp }v_{1}\subset\{n+1,n+2,\dots,n_{1}\}
and

\displaystyle 1\geq\|v_{1}\|_{p}\geq\|u_{1}\|_{p}-\|w_{1}\|_{p}\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{2}.

Suppose that we have constructed
n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{k}
,
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\in X
and appropriate functions
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}
satisfying (
2.9
) and (
2.10
).
Since
\dim X_{n_{k}}=\infty
we are able to find
u_{k+1}\in X_{n_{k}}
with
\|u_{k+1}\|_{p}=1
. It is easy to see that we can take an index
n_{k+1}>n_{k}
such that

\|R_{n_{k+1}}u_{k+1}\|_{p}\leq\frac{\delta}{2^{k+1}}
. Set
w_{k+1}=R_{n_{k+1}}u_{k+1}
,
v_{k+1}=P_{n_{k+1}}u_{k+1}
. Consequently

\displaystyle 1\geq\|v_{k+1}\|_{p}\geq\|u_{k+1}\|_{p}-\|w_{k+1}\|_{p}\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{2^{k+1}}.

Moreover
\textup{supp }v_{k+1}\subset\{n_{k}+1,n_{k}+2,\dots,n_{k+1}\}
.

Now, consider sequences

\displaystyle y_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{j},\ \ \ s_{k}:=\|y_{k}\|_{p}.

We can write

\displaystyle s_{k}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{j}\Big\|_{p}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}\Big\|_{p}\geq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|w_{j}\|_{p}\geq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\delta}{2^{j}}\geq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\delta.

Since by (
2.10
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{i}\|_{p}\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{2^{i}}\geq 1-\delta

and
v_{j}
have pairwise disjoint supports by (
2.5
), then

\displaystyle s_{k}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|v_{j}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\geq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}(1-\delta)^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\geq(1-\delta)k^{1/p}

and consequently
s_{k}\nearrow\infty
.

Then we can choose
m
large enough such that

(2.12)

\displaystyle m>2n,\ m\geq N,\ \frac{1}{s_{m}}\leq\varepsilon

and set

\displaystyle u=\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}.

It is seen from the definition of
s_{m}
that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}=1,

which proves (
2.3
).

Clearly, condition (
2.4
) is satisfied. By the definition of
v=P_{m}u
we obtain directly

\displaystyle\textup{supp }v\subset\{n_{0}+1,n_{0}+2,\dots,m\}=\{n+1,n+2,\dots,m\}

which proves (
2.5
).

Since
\|v_{k}\|_{p}\leq 1
we have for each
s\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle|u_{k}(s)|\leq 1.

Clearly, using that
u_{j}
have pairwise disjoint supports, we have for each

\displaystyle|v(s)|\leq|u(s)|\leq\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|u_{j}(s)|\leq\frac{1}{s_{m}}\overset{\eqref{wdvfhnrogronhi}}{\leq}\varepsilon

which proves (
2.6
).

At last,

\displaystyle w=R_{m}u=R_{m}\Big(\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\Big)=R_{m}\Big(\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}+\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{m}(v_{j})+\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{m}(w_{j})=\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{m}(w_{j}).

Thus

\displaystyle\|w\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|R_{m}(w_{j})\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{j}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{dlkvdnvkdjvdkv}}{\leq}\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\delta}{2^{i}}\leq\frac{1}{s_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\delta}{2^{j}}\leq\frac{\delta}{s_{m}}\leq\delta

which proves (
2.8
).

Finally, (
2.7
) follows directly from

\displaystyle 1\geq\|v\|_{p}\geq\|u\|_{p}-\|w\|_{p}\geq 1-\delta

which concludes the proof.

3.
Main theorem

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then the embedding
\ell_{p}\hookrightarrow\ell_{p,\infty}
is strictly singular.

Proof.

Having a sequence
0=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots
and
u_{k}\in X_{n_{k-1}}
,
k\geq 1
, we denote

\displaystyle v_{k}=P_{n_{k}}u_{k},\ w_{k}=R_{n_{k}}u_{k},

\displaystyle I_{k}=\{n_{k-1}+1,n_{k-1}+2,\dots,n_{k}\},

\displaystyle b_{k}=\min\{|v_{k}(j)|;v_{k}(j)\neq 0,j\in I_{k}\}.

Choose
0<\delta<1
.
We will construct by mathematical induction a sequence
0=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots
and
u_{k}\in X_{n_{k-1}}
,
k\geq 1
,
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|u_{k}\|_{p}=1,

(3.2)

\displaystyle 2n_{k-1}<n_{k}.

(3.3)

\displaystyle\textup{supp }v_{k}\subset I_{k},

(3.4)

\displaystyle|v_{k+1}(j)|\leq\min\Big\{b_{k},\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}\Big\},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n_{k+1}-n_{k})^{1/p}}\leq b_{k},

(3.6)

\displaystyle 1-\delta\leq\|v_{k}\|_{p}\leq 1,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|w_{k}\|_{p}\leq\frac{\delta}{2^{k}}.

Consider first
k=1
. Find
u_{1}\in X_{0}=X
with
\|u_{1}\|_{p}=1
. Then we can choose
n_{1}>n_{0}
such that
\|w_{1}\|_{p}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
. Consequently

\displaystyle\|v_{1}\|_{p}\overset{}{\geq}\|u_{1}\|_{p}-\|w_{1}\|_{p}\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{2}\geq 1-\delta.

It is easy now to verify conditions (
3.1
) – (
3.7
).

Assume that we have constructed
0=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{k}
and
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}
,
u_{i}\in X_{n_{i-1}}
satisfying (
3.1
) – (
3.7
). Consider a space
X_{n_{k}}
. Choose
\frac{\delta}{2^{k+1}}
instead of
\delta
in Lemma
2.12
and set

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\min\Big\{b_{k},\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}\Big\}.

Find
N>n_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(N-n_{k})^{1/p}}\leq b_{k}.

By Lemma
2.12
there exist
u\in X_{n_{k}}
,
\|u\|_{p}=1
and
m\geq N
,
m\geq 2n_{k}
such that for
v=P_{m}u
,
w=R_{m}u
we have

\displaystyle\textup{supp }v\subset\{n_{k}+1,n_{k}+2,\dots,m\},

\displaystyle|v(j)|\leq\varepsilon,

\displaystyle 1-\delta\leq\|v\|_{p}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\|w\|_{p}\leq\frac{\delta}{2^{k+1}}.

Now, it suffices to choose
n_{k+1}=m
and
u_{k+1}=u
.

Set now

\displaystyle z_{N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}\in X.

Then we can write

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|z_{N}\|_{p}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}\Big\|_{p}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\overset{}{\geq}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}\Big\|_{p}\overset{}{\geq}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|w_{j}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{klvjnfovn}}{\geq}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\delta}{2^{j}}\geq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}-\delta.

By (
3.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{i}\|_{p}\geq 1-\delta,

and due (
3.3
) we have that
v_{j}
have pairwise disjoint supports and then

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p}=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|v_{j}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\geq(1-\delta)N^{1/p}

which gives with (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\|z_{N}\|_{p}\rightarrow\infty.

Now we try to estimate
\|z_{N}\|_{p,\infty}
.
Clearly

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|z_{N}\|_{p,\infty}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}+\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}\leq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|w_{j}\|_{p,\infty}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{klvjnfovn}}{\leq}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\delta}{2^{j}}\leq\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}+\delta.

It remains to estimate
\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}
.
Denote

\displaystyle A_{k}=\{j\in I_{k},v_{k}(j)=0\}.

Set

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}_{k}(j)=|v_{k}(j)|+\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}\chi_{A_{k}}(j)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{z}_{N}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\widetilde{v}_{k}.

Since
|{v}_{k}(j)|\leq\widetilde{v}_{k}(j)
we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|z_{N}\|_{p,\infty}\overset{\eqref{sdoicvhdiovnh}}{\leq}2^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}\Big\|_{p,\infty}+\delta\Big)\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}|v_{j}|\Big\|_{p,\infty}+\delta\Big)

and also

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)\geq b_{k}\overset{\eqref{efklbvkpbnmkb}}{\geq}\frac{1}{(n_{k+1}-n_{k})^{1/p}}

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)\geq|v_{k+1}(j)|+\frac{1}{(n_{k+1}-n_{k})^{1/p}}\chi_{A_{k+1}}(j)=\widetilde{v}_{k+1}(j).

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)=\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}\overset{\eqref{efklbvkpbnmkb}}{\geq}|v_{k+1}(j)|.

Further by (

) we have

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}\geq\frac{1}{(n_{k+1}-n_{k})^{1/p}}

and so

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)=\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}{\geq}\frac{1}{(n_{k+1}-n_{k})^{1/p}}.

Consequently

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)\geq|v_{k+1}(j)|+\frac{1}{(n_{k+1}-n_{k})^{1/p}}\chi_{A_{k+1}}(j)=\widetilde{v}_{k+1}(j).

We have proved

(3.11)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{v}_{k+1}(j)|\leq|\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)|\ \ i\in I_{k},j\in I_{k+1}.

Fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
I_{k}
have pairwise disjoint supports and
\mathbb{N}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}
there exists a unique
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
j\in I_{k}
.

If
k>N
then
\widetilde{z}_{N}(j)=0
and since
|\widetilde{z}_{N}(i)|>0
for
i\leq n_{N}
we obtain
(\widetilde{z}_{N})^{*}(j)=0
and so

(3.12)

\displaystyle j^{1/p}(\widetilde{z}_{N})^{*}(j)=0,\ \ j\geq n_{N}+1.

Let
k\leq N
. Then
n_{k-1}+1\leq j\leq n_{k}
and by (
3.11
) there is
n_{k-1}+1\leq i\leq n_{k}
such that
(\widetilde{z}_{N})^{*}(j)=(\widetilde{v}_{k})^{*}(j-n_{k-1})=\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)
.

We have two possibilities. Either
(a)

n_{k-1}+1\leq j\leq 2n_{k-1}
or
(b)

2n_{k-1}<j\leq n_{k}
.

(a)
If
n_{k-1}+1\leq j\leq 2n_{k-1}
we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle j^{1/p}(\widetilde{z}_{N})^{*}(j)=j^{1/p}(\widetilde{v}_{k})^{*}(j-n_{k-1})\leq(2n_{k-1})^{1/p}\widetilde{v}_{k}(i)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{efklbvkpbnmkb}}{\leq}\frac{(2n_{k-1})^{1/p}}{(n_{k-1}-n_{k-2})^{1/p}}\overset{\eqref{dklvhdvhjvk}}{\leq}4^{1/p}\leq 2^{1+1/p}.

(b)
If
2n_{k-1}<j\leq n_{k}
we obtain by Proposition
2.8

\displaystyle j^{1/p}(\widetilde{z}_{N})^{*}(j)=j^{1/p}(\widetilde{v}_{k})^{*}(j-n_{k-1})\leq\Big(\frac{j}{j-n_{k-1}}\Big)^{1/p}(j-n_{k-1})^{1/p}(\widetilde{v}_{k})^{*}(j-n_{k-1})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/p}\|(\widetilde{v}_{k})^{*}\|_{p,\infty}\leq 2^{1/p}D_{p}\|(\widetilde{v}_{k})^{*}\|_{p}=2^{1/p}D_{p}\|\widetilde{v}_{k}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/p}D_{p}\Big(\|v_{k}\|_{p}+\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}\|\chi_{A_{k}}\|_{p}\Big)

\displaystyle=2^{1/p}D_{p}\Big(\|v_{k}\|_{p}+\frac{1}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}(\#A_{k})^{1/p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/p}D_{p}\Big(1+\ \frac{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}{(n_{k}-n_{k-1})^{1/p}}\Big)=2^{1/p+1}D_{p}.

This gives us with (
3.13
) and (
3.12
)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{z}_{N}\|_{p,\infty}\lesssim 2^{1+1/p}(1+D_{p}).

Using (
3.10
) we conclude that
\|z_{N}\|_{p,\infty}
is bounded which proves that the embedding cannot be an isomorphism on
X
and finishes the proof.
∎

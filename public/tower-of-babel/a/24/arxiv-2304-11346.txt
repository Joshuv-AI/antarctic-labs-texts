The Yang-Mills-Higgs functional on complex line bundles: asymptotics for critical points
Giacomo Canevari, Federico Luigi Dipasquale, Giandomenico Orlandi

Abstract
We consider a gauge-invariant Ginzburg-Landau functional (also known as Abelian Yang-Mills-Higgs model), on Hermitian line bundles over closed Riemannian manifolds of dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . Assuming a logarithmic energy bound in the coupling parameter, we study the asymptotic behaviour of critical points in the London limit. After a convenient choice of the gauge, we show compactness of finite-energy critical points in Sobolev norms. Moreover, thanks to a suitable monotonicity formula, we prove that the energy densities of critical points, rescaled by the logarithm of the coupling parameter, converge to the weight measure of a stationary, rectifiable varifold of codimension 2.



Introduction

Let
(M,\,g)
be a smooth, compact, connected, oriented Riemannian manifold
without boundary, of dimension
n\geq 3
.
Let
E\to M
be a Hermitian line bundle over
M
, equipped with a
(smooth) reference metric connection
\D_{0}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the Ginzburg-Landau-type functional

G_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A):=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|u\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\vol_{g}.

(1)

Here
u\colon M\to E
is a section of the bundle,
A
is a real-valued

1
-form on
M
,

\D_{A}:=\D_{0}-iA
and
F_{A}
is the curvature
2
-form of
\D_{A}
.
We denote the integrand of
G_{\varepsilon}
as

e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon}):=\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|u\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\!.

(2)

The functional
G_{\varepsilon}
is also known as the

Abelian Yang-Mills-Higgs energy
.
One prominent feature of
G_{\varepsilon}

is
gauge-invariance
: for any
\Phi\in W^{2,2}(M,\,\mathbb{S}^{1})
and any
pair
(u,\,A)\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
with finite energy,
each term of the energy density
e_{\varepsilon}

is invariant under the
gauge transformation

(u,\,A)\mapsto\left(\Phi u,\,\Phi\cdot A\right):=\left(\Phi u,\,A-i\Phi^{-1}\d\Phi\right)\!,

(3)

where
\Phi u
is defined by the fibre-wise action
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}\simeq\mathrm{U}(1)
on
E
.

For any
u
and
A
, we define the forms
(see e.g.
[
31
]
)

j(u,\,A):=\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,iu\right\rangle,\qquad J(u,\,A):=\frac{1}{2}\d j(u,\,A)+\frac{1}{2}F_{A},

(4)

called, respectively, the
gauge-invariant pre-Jacobian
and the

gauge-invariant Jacobian
of the pair
(u,\,A)
.
If
(u,\,A)
is a pair with finite energy, both
j(u,\,A)

and
J(u,\,A)
are well-defined, both in the sense of distributions and
pointwise, and invariant under gauge transformation.

In the present paper, we consider

finite-energy critical points
of
G_{\varepsilon}
.
More precisely, we notice that the functional
G_{\varepsilon}

is well-defined and finite for
(u,\,A)\in\mathcal{E}
, where

\mathcal{E}:=(W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty})(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

and critical points
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})
of
G_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{E}
satisfy
the Euler-Lagrange equations

\displaystyle\D_{A}^{*}\D_{A}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-1\right)u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0

(5)

\displaystyle\d^{*}F_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})

(6)

where
\D^{*}_{A}
is the
L^{2}
-adjoint of
\D_{A}
,

F_{\varepsilon}:=F_{A_{\varepsilon}}

and
\d^{*}
denotes the codifferential.
By taking the differential in both sides of (
6
),
we obtain the
London equation
(at level
\varepsilon
)

-\Delta F_{\varepsilon}+F_{\varepsilon}=2J(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})

(7)

Throughout the paper, we
assume

that
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset\mathcal{E}

is a sequence of critical points of
G_{\varepsilon}
satisfying
the logarithmic energy bound

G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\leq\Lambda\left|\log\varepsilon\right|

(8)

for some constant
\Lambda>0
that does not depend on
\varepsilon
.
This assumption is natural, as the energy of minimisers
of
G_{\varepsilon}
is precisely of order
\left|\log\varepsilon\right|
,
whenever the bundle
E\to M
is non-trivial

[
13
, Theorem A and Remark 2]
.
Under this assumption, we have this (preliminary) convergence result:

Theorem 0

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset\mathcal{E}
be a sequence of
critical points of
G_{\varepsilon}
satisfying the logarithmic energy
bound (
8
). Then, there exist a (non-relabelled)
subsequence,
2
-forms
J_{*}
,
F_{*}
and a
1
-form
j_{*}

such that the following properties hold:

(i)

J(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\to J_{*}
strongly
in
W^{-1,p}(M,\ \Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

for any
p\in\left[1,\,\frac{n}{n-1}\right)
;

(ii)

F_{A_{\varepsilon}}\to F_{*}
strongly
in
W^{1,p}(M,\ \Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

for any
p\in\left[1,\,\frac{n}{n-1}\right)
;

(iii)

j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\to j_{*}
strongly
in
L^{p}(M,\ \Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

for any
p\in\left[1,\,\frac{n}{n-1}\right)
;

(iv)

the Hodge dual
\star J_{*}
is a boundary-less,
integer-multiplicity rectifiable
(n-2)
-current in
M
,
whose integral homology class
\mathcal{C}\subseteq H_{n-2}(M;\,\mathbb{Z})

is Poincaré dual to the first Chern class of
E\to M
;

(v)

the limit curvature
F_{*}
satisfies

\d^{*}F_{*}=j_{*},\qquad-\Delta F_{*}+F_{*}=2\pi J_{*},

(9)

in the sense of distributions.

The proof of Theorem
0
is contained in
[
13
]

(see, in particular, Theorem A and Remark 3.12).
Property (iii) is not mentioned explicitly in
[
13
]
,
but it is an immediate consequence of (
6
) and (ii).
Note that Property (i) is completely independent of
the Euler-Lagrange equations (
5
)–(
6
);
it holds true for any sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}

that satisfies (
8
).

Main results.

In this paper, we will address two more questions about the asymptotic behaviour, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, of critical points
satisfying (
8
).
Our first main result describes the concentration of the
rescaled energy densities
.
We denote by
\spt\mu
the support of a Radon measure
\mu

and by
\mathcal{H}^{n-2}
the
(n-2)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset\mathcal{E}
be any sequence of
critical points of
G_{\varepsilon}
satisfying the logarithmic energy
bound (
8
).
Then, up to extraction of a subsequence, we have

\frac{e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\,\vol_{g}\rightharpoonup^{*}\mu_{*}\qquad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0

in the sense of Radon measures in
M
.
The limit measure
\mu_{*}
satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(\spt\mu_{*})<+\infty

and, moreover, it is the weight measure of a stationary,
rectifiable
(n-2)
-varifold in
M
.

Recall that
varifolds
are measure-theoretic generalisations of
smooth submanifolds (see e.g.
[
34
]
and Section
5

below for details). In particular, stationary rectifiable varifolds are generalisations of
minimal surfaces. Constructing non-trivial stationary, rectifiable varifolds in
Riemannian manifolds is a fundamental problem in Geometric Measure
Theory
[
3
,
29
]
.

Remark 1
.

By
\Gamma
-convergence
[
13
, Theorem A and Remark 3.13]
,
the limit measure
\mu_{*}
satisfies
\mu_{*}\geq\pi\left|J_{*}\right|

where
J_{*}
is the limit Jacobian, given by Theorem
0
.
In particular,
\mu_{*}
it is always nonzero,
so long as the bundle
E\to M
is nontrivial.
Moreover, if
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}
is a sequence of minimisers,
then
\mu_{*}=\pi\left|J_{*}\right|
and the Hodge dual
\star J_{*}

is area-minimising in its integral homology class
\mathcal{C}

[
13
, Corollary B]
.
However, we do not know whether the inequality

\mu_{*}\geq\pi\left|J_{*}\right|
may be strict, in general.
A related question, which we do not address here,
is whether or not
\mu_{*}
is an integral varifold
(see the paragraph on open questions below).

Remark 2
.

A more detailed description of
\mu_{*}
can be found
in Theorem
5.3
in Section
5
,
of which Theorem
1
is an abridged version.
Contrary to what is observed in related problems
(e.g.
[
9
,
37
]
),
the measure
\mu_{*}
contains no diffuse part;
see Remark
5.4
.

The proof of Theorem
1
relies
on a suitable monotonicity formula
(see Theorem
3
below).

In our second main result, we prove compactness for the sequence

\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}
in

W^{1,p}(M,\,E)\times W^{2,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, for any
p
with

1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
,
so long as
A_{\varepsilon}
is in Coulomb gauge, that is,

\begin{split}A_{\varepsilon}=\d^{*}\psi_{\varepsilon}+\zeta_{\varepsilon}\end{split}

(10)

where
\psi_{\varepsilon}
is an exact
2
-form,
\zeta_{\varepsilon}

is a harmonic
1
-form and
\left\|\zeta_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C_{M}

for some constant
C_{M}
that depends on
M
only
(see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 2.10]
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset\mathcal{E}

be a sequence of critical points of
G_{\varepsilon}
.
Assume that
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}
satisfies the logarithmic energy bound
(
8
) and that each
A_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
10
).
Then, up to extraction of a (non-relabelled) subsequence, there holds

u_{\varepsilon}\to u_{*}\quad\textrm{strongly in }W^{1,p}(M,\,E),\qquad A_{\varepsilon}\to A_{*}\quad\textrm{strongly in }W^{2,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for any
p
with
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
.
Moreover,
u_{*}
and
A_{*}
are smooth in
M\setminus\spt\mu_{*}
,
they satisfy
\left|u_{*}\right|=1
in
M\setminus\spt\mu_{*}
and

j(u_{*},\,A_{*})=\d^{*}F_{A_{*}}

(11)

in the sense of distributions in
M
.

Remark 3
.

By continuity of the Jacobian and the differential operator,
the limits
u_{*}
and
A_{*}
are compatible with
J_{*}
,
F_{*}
,
j_{*}

given by Theorem
0

— that is,
F_{*}=F_{A_{*}}
,
J_{*}=J(u_{*},\,A_{*})
,
and
j_{*}=j(u_{*},\,A_{*})
.
Moreover, Equation (
11
) implies that
u_{*}

is an
A_{*}
-harmonic unit section away from the support of
\mu_{*}
,
where both
u_{*}
and
A_{*}
are smooth.
In other words, there holds

\D_{A_{*}}^{*}\D_{A_{*}}u_{*}=\left|\D_{A_{*}}u_{*}\right|^{2}u_{*}\qquad\textrm{pointwise in }M\setminus\spt\mu_{*}

(12)

(see Remark
2.3
for details).
Finally, by passing to the limit in (
10
),
it follows that
A_{*}=\d^{*}\psi_{*}+\zeta_{*}
, where
\psi_{*}

is an exact
2
-form and
\zeta_{*}
is a harmonic
1
-form.

Remark 4
.

Theorem
2
applies, in particular,
to any sequence of
minimisers
of
G_{\varepsilon}

in the class

W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.
Indeed, by
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
, minimisers belong
to
\mathcal{E}
and, by
[
13
, Remark 2]
,
they satisfy the assumption (
8
).

Before illustrating the ideas of the proof, let us motivate the interest towards our results.

Background and motivation.

Ginzburg-Landau functionals of the form (
1
)
were originally proposed as a model of
superconductivity. This theory gained
much popularity, as it accounts for most commonly observed
effects in superconductors,
and it gradually became relevant to other areas of physics,
such as particle physics and gauge theories.
The asymptotic regime as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which is known as
the
London limit
in superconductivity theory or the
strongly repulsive limit

in particle physics, is characterised by the emergence of

topological singularities
:
due to topological obstructions set by the structure of the bundle
E\to M
,
critical points develop singularities, in the asymptotic limit,
which are supported on a set of dimension
n-2
.
The topologically-driven energy concentration phenomenon
can be detected by the distributional Jacobian,
which both enjoys remarkable compactness properties and
identifies the topological singularities that emerge in the limit
as
\varepsilon\to 0
(see e.g.
[
22
,
1
,
2
]
).

The asymptotic analysis of (
1
) in
the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
relies on analogous
results for the “non-magnetic” version of the functional,
in which the variable
A
is set to zero and the energy
is considered as a functional of
u
only.
The analysis of critical points of Ginzburg-Landau-type functionals without magnetic field was
initiated by Bethuel, Brezis, and Hélein
[
7
]
in
the 2-dimensional, Euclidean setting. The analysis was then extended to critical points on
2
-dimensional Riemann surfaces
[
5
]
,
higher-dimensional Euclidean domains
[
23
,
8
]

and, more recently, higher-dimensional manifolds
[
14
,
37
,
15
,
16
]
.
In particular, our Theorems
2
and
1

generalise results that were first obtained in
[
8
]

in the Euclidean, non-magnetic case.
In all these works, a main difficulty lies in the
lack of uniform energy bounds,
as the natural energy scaling considered is
the logarithmic one (
8
).

As for the gauge-invariant, “magnetic” version of the functional,
the first results addressed the asymptotic
behaviour of minimisers in two-dimensional Euclidean domains
[
10
]
and on Riemann surfaces
[
26
,
30
]
.
A detailed analysis of the asymptotics
of critical points for the gauge-invariant
functional in 2 dimensions, in the Euclidean setting and
in the logarithmic energy regime, has been
carried out by Sandier and Serfaty
— see e.g.
[
32
, Chapter 13]

and the references therein.
Apparently, less effort has been put in studying the asymptotic behaviour of critical
points in higher dimensions. Recently, this problem has been
addressed in
[
27
]
,
in the context of Hermitian line bundles, for the

self-dual
version
G_{\varepsilon}^{\rm self}
of
G_{\varepsilon}
, in which the
curvature term
\int_{M}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}
is replaced by

\varepsilon^{2}\int_{M}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}
.
The main outcome of
[
27
]
is that,
for a sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}
of critical points with

bounded

G_{\varepsilon}^{\rm self}
-energy,
the energy densities
e_{\varepsilon}^{\rm self}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})

converge, up to subsequences, to the weight measure of a
limiting stationary, rectifiable,
integral

varifold of codimension 2 in
M
.
Conversely, in
[
16
]
, it is shown that any
non-degenerate minimal submanifold of codimension 2 in
M
can be obtained as
energy concentration set of a sequences of bounded energy critical points of

G_{\varepsilon}^{\rm self}
.

Proofs of the main results: a sketch.

In the paper, we first address the proof of Theorem
2
,
which ultimately relies
on the
L^{p}
-compactness for the prejacobians
j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})

given by Theorem
0
.
Instead, the proof of Theorem
1
combines ideas from
[
8
]

in order to deal with the logarithmic energy regime and from
[
26
,
27
]

to deal with the Hermitian line bundle setting.
The most difficult part is to identify
\mu_{*}
as the weight measure of a
stationary, rectifiable
(n-2)
-varifold.
The key point is showing that, if
\mu_{*}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))
is smaller
than
\eta_{0}\,R^{n-2}
, where
\eta_{0}
is suitable
uniform “small” constant,
then
\mu_{*}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2}(x_{0}))=0
.
This is shown in Lemma
5.6
. The proof goes
along the lines of that of
[
8
, Proposition VIII.1]

and it is based on a
clearing-out
result
(see the analysis in Section
4
,
in particular Proposition
4.1

and Proposition
4.6
).

A crucial rôle is played by the following (almost)

(n-2)
-
monotonicity inequality
.
Let
\inj(M)
be the injectivity radius of
M
.
For
x_{0}\in M
and
r\in(0,\inj(M))
, we denote
by
\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})
the geodesic ball of radius
r

centered at
x_{0}
. Given
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{E}
,
we define

E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho):=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\,\vol_{g}.

(13)

and

X_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho):=\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon},\nu}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\left|{\rm i}_{\nu}F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{\hat{g}}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}\,\rho}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\vol_{g}

(14)

where
\nu
is the exterior unit normal
to
\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0})
,
{\rm i}_{\nu}

denotes the contraction against
\nu
(see (
17
) below),

F_{\varepsilon}:=F_{A_{\varepsilon}}
and
\hat{g}
is the metric induced by
g

on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
.

Theorem 3
.

There exist
\alpha\in(0,\,2)
,
R_{0}\in(0,\,\inj(M))
, and
C>0
,
all depending only on
M
and
g
, such that the following holds.
If
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{E}

is any critical point of
G_{\varepsilon}
and
x_{0}
is any point of
M
,
then the inequality

\begin{split}\frac{e^{Cr^{2}}}{r^{n-2}}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,r)&+\int_{r}^{R}\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}}{\rho^{n-2}}X_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)\,\d\rho\\
&\leq\frac{e^{CR^{2}}}{R^{n-2}}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,R)+\frac{Ce^{CR^{2}}(R^{\alpha}-r^{\alpha})}{R^{n-2+\alpha}}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,R)\end{split}

(15)

holds for any
0<r<R<R_{0}
.

The proof of Theorem
3
is delicate.
A classical argument
(see e.g. Proposition
3.3
below),
based on the Pohozaev identity, proves that

\frac{\d}{\d\rho}\left(\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)}{\rho^{n-4}}\right)\geq 0

(16)

for any
x_{0}\in M
,
\rho\in(0,\,\inj(M))
and some uniform constant
C>0
.
The exponent of
n-4
at the denominator
appears for scaling reasons
(heuristically, the curvature
F_{\varepsilon}
scales as
a second derivative, so the integral of
\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}

scales as a length to the power of
n-4
).
The inequality (
16
) is known
to be sharp in the context of non-Abelian Yang-Mills-Higgs
theories
[
35
]
.
However, in our Abelian setting, Theorem
3

gives an (almost) monotonicity formula
for the energy rescaled by
\rho^{n-2}
.
A similar result is obtained in
[
27
, Section 4]
,
for the Ginzburg-Landau functional in the self-dual scaling.
However, the arguments of
[
27
]
fall apart
in our setting, because we work with a different scaling
of the energy. Instead, we exploit the properties of
the Euler-Lagrange equations. In particular, our arguments
rely on Equation (
6
), which can be reformulated as a

uniformly elliptic
equation for the variable
A_{\varepsilon}
.
By elliptic regularity estimates, we can control the
curvature terms and improve on the monotonicity,
obtaining in the end Theorem
3
.
As a byproduct of our arguments, we obtain
a bound for the Morrey-Campanato
L^{q,2}
-norm
of
F_{\varepsilon}
, in terms of the rescaled
energy (
13
), for any
q<n

(see Proposition
3.7
below).

Back on the proof of Theorem
1
,
the clearing-out property combined with the
energy bound (
8
) implies
a two-sided estimate for the
(n-2)
-density of
\mu_{*}
, see
Lemma
5.7
.
Following ideas in
[
8
, pp. 498–499]
(extended first
in the gauge-invariant setting in
[
32
]
and
to the context of Hermitian line bundles in
[
27
, Section 6]
),
the conclusion is achieved once we characterise
\mu_{*}

in terms of the
stress-energy tensor fields

T_{\varepsilon}
, defined
in (
5.3
) below.
Indeed, by criticality of
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})
, each tensor field
T_{\varepsilon}

is stationary (i.e., divergence-free).
Since stationarity is a linear condition,
their weak limit
T_{*}
is still stationary.
On the other hand,
T_{*}
is naturally
identified with a
(n-2)
-generalised varifold in
M

whose weight measure
\left|T_{*}\right|
is absolutely continuous
with respect to
\mu_{*}
.
Then, by Lemma
5.7
, we can apply a suitable version of
the Ambrosio-Soner rectifiability criterion
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]

to deduce that
T_{*}
is also rectifiable and that
\mu_{*}=\left|T_{*}\right|
,
whence the conclusion of Theorem
1
follows.

Further comments and open questions.

We did not address specifically the problem
of the existence, in full generality, of non-minimising
critical points of
G_{\varepsilon}
.
We believe that they could be constructed by adapting the general framework
developed in
[
21
]
.

Another interesting question that we have not addressed in this paper
is whether the varifold
\frac{1}{\pi}\mu_{*}
is
integral
or not.
For the magnetic Ginzburg-Landau functional in the self-dual scaling,
it is known that the measure
\frac{1}{\pi}\mu_{*}
is integral
[
27
]
.
By contrast, for critical points of the non-magnetic
Ginzburg-Landau functional (
5.4
) in Euclidean domains,
the situation is more involved
[
28
]

— that is, the density of the limiting varifold
\frac{1}{\pi}\mu_{*}
at a generic point
is either
1
or possibly a non integer number larger or equal than 2.
Unfortunately, the arguments
in
[
27
]
are really tailored to the
bounded energy regime and do not carry over to our setting.

Finally, it would be interesting to
study, along the lines of
[
16
]
,
whether any non-degenerate minimal codimension 2
submanifold of
M
can be obtained as energy
concentration set of critical points of
G_{\varepsilon}
,
for an appropriate choice of the total space
E
of the bundle
E\to M
.
We leave all these questions to future investigation.

Organisation of the paper.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
1
we derive
several
a priori
estimates for gauge-invariant quantities, such as

\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|
,
\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|
, and
\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|
,
that are used throughout the whole paper.
In Section
2
we prove Theorem
2
.
In Section
3
we prove the almost

Lemma 1.4

For any section

(1.4)

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{2}(M,\,E)
be a section and

A
be
1

\D_{A}u\in L^{2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)

For any smooth function

\begin{split}\int_{M}\left\langle\D^{*}_{A}\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle\,\varphi\,\vol_{g}-\int_{M}\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}\,\varphi\,\vol_{g}&=\int_{M}\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,\D_{A}(u\varphi)\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}-\int_{M}\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}\,\varphi\,\vol_{g}\\
&=\int_{M}\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,\d\varphi\otimes u\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}\end{split}

Let
x\in M
, and let
\{\tau_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{n}\}

be an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{T}_{x}M
. The scalar product
on
\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E
is the one induced by the Hermitian form on
E

and the Riemannian metric on

; therefore, at the given point

we have

\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,\d\varphi\otimes u\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle\D_{A\,\tau_{j}}u,\,u\right\rangle\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\tau_{j}}

(1.5)

However, the right-hand side of (

is also the scalar product between the (real-valued)

-forms

\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle

, evaluated at

.
As a consequence, we have

\begin{split}\int_{M}\left(\left\langle\D^{*}_{A}\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle-\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}\right)\varphi\,\vol_{g}=\int_{M}\left\langle\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle\!,\,\d\varphi\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}&=\int_{M}\varphi\,\d^{*}\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}\end{split}

Since

is arbitrary, we deduce
\left\langle\D^{*}_{A}\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle-\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}=\d^{*}\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle

\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle\in L^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

and the connection

\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle=\d(\left|u\right|^{2}/2)
in the sense of distributions
and hence,

\left\langle\D^{*}_{A}\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle-\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\d^{*}\d\left(\left|u\right|^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\left(\left|u\right|^{2}\right)

in the sense of distributions on

Lemma 1.5

Let

\left\|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1}.

(1.6)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary and
let
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})
be any critical

G_{\varepsilon}
. In particular,

\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)

\int_{M}\left\langle\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon},\,\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}v\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{M}\left\langle v,\,u_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2})\,\vol_{g}.

(1.7)

Let
x_{0}\in M
be a Lebesgue point for
\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})
be the geodesic ball of center

and radius

, with

smaller than the injectivity radius of
M

For any
s>0
such that

is still smaller than the injectivity

\varphi_{s}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

\spt(\varphi_{s})\subset\mathcal{B}_{r+s}(x_{0}),\quad 0\leq\varphi_{s}\leq 1,\quad\varphi_{s}\equiv 1\mbox{ on }\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0}),\quad\left|\d\varphi_{s}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{s}.

(1.8)

Since
v:=u_{\varepsilon}\varphi_{s}
belongs to

, it
is admissible to test (
5
). By (

), we get

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}(x_{0})}\varphi_{s}\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2})\,\vol_{g}\\
=-\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r+s}(x_{0})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon},\,\d\varphi_{s}\otimes u_{\varepsilon}+\varphi_{s}\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}.

Since

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}&\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2})\,\vol_{g}\\
&\leq\liminf_{s\to 0}\frac{C}{s}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r+s}(x_{0})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|\,\vol_{g}\\
&=\liminf_{s\to 0}\frac{C}{s}\int_{r}^{r+s}\left(\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|\,\d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\,\d t\\

Using once again

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\leq Cr^{n}\varepsilon^{-2}+Cr^{n/2-1/2}\left(\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u\right|^{2}\,\d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}

Now, we define

y(r)\equiv y(x_{0},r):=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}.

Recasting (

and rearranging,

C_{*}y(r)\leq r^{n}\varepsilon^{-2}+r^{n/2-1/2}(y^{\prime}(r))^{1/2},

y^{\prime}(r)\geq\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\left(\left(C_{*}y(r)-r^{n}\varepsilon^{-2}\right)^{+}\right)^{2}.

(1.11)

Suppose now

C_{*}y(r)-r^{n}\varepsilon^{-2}\geq\frac{C_{*}}{2}y(r)\quad\mbox{for any }r>0\mbox{ small enough}.

(1.12)

Then, for

y^{\prime}(r)\geq\frac{C}{r^{n-1}}y(r)^{2},

which implies

\frac{1}{y(r)}\geq\frac{1}{y(R)}+C\left(\frac{1}{r^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{R^{n-2}}\right)\geq\frac{C}{r^{n-2}}

small enough (and, perhaps, a different

y(r)\leq C_{1}r^{n-2}\quad\mbox{for any }r>0\mbox{ small enough}

for some constant

) for

C_{*}y(r_{k})-r^{n}_{k}\varepsilon^{-2}<\frac{C_{*}}{2}y(r_{k}).

Since almost every

Proof.

Remark 2.3

Let

be a test function. From (

\begin{split}0=\int_{M}\left\langle j(u_{*},\,A_{*}),\,\d\varphi\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}&=\int_{M}\left\langle\D_{A_{*}}u_{*},\,\d\varphi\otimes iu_{*}\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}\\

As

that

In other words,

is an

-harmonic
unit section away from the singular set

\rho\in(0,\,\bar{\rho})

\frac{\d}{\d\rho}\left(\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}\,E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)}{\rho^{n-4+\alpha}}\right)>0

is defined by (

).

Proof.

Assume, for the moment being,
\alpha
is any (fixed) nonnegative number.
Using (

), we compute

\begin{split}&\frac{\d}{\d\rho}\left(\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}\,E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)}{\rho^{n-4+\alpha}}\right)\\
&=\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}}{\rho^{n-4+\alpha}}\left[\left(2C\rho-\frac{n-4+\alpha}{\rho}\right)E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\right]\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:PS-ineq}}}{{\geq}}\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}}{\rho^{n-4+\alpha}}\left\{\vphantom{\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{\left(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\,\vol_{g}}\left(2C-C(M)\right)\rho E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)+X_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{2-\alpha}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{\left(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\,\vol_{g}-\frac{\alpha}{2\rho}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\right\},\end{split}

for any
\rho\in(0,\,\inj(M))
. Then, by (

) and Young’s

\begin{split}&\frac{\d}{\d\rho}\left(\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}}{\rho^{n-4+\alpha}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\,\vol_{g}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{e^{C\rho^{2}}}{\rho^{n-4+\alpha}}\left\{\vphantom{\int_{\mathcal{\partial B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|{\rm i}_{\nu}F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{\hat{g}}}\left(2C-C(M)\right)\rho E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)+X_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\,x_{0})\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{2-\alpha}{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{\left(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\,\vol_{g}\right.\\
&\left.-2\alpha K^{2}\rho\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}-2\alpha\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathcal{\partial B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|{\rm i}_{\nu}F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{\hat{g}}\right\},\end{split}

for any
\rho\in(0,\,\bar{\rho})
, with
\bar{\rho}
as in
Lemma
3.2
, and
where
K
and
\lambda
are the same constants as in
Lemma

(both of which depend only on
M
and
g
).
Thus, recalling the definition (

and taking
\alpha=\min\left\{2,\,\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}},\,\frac{C(M)}{2K^{2}}\right\}

∎

With Proposition

decay estimate for the

-norm of the curvatures

Lemma 3.4
.

There exist a number
\alpha\in(0,\,2)
and a number
R_{0}\in(0,\,\inj(M))
,
both depending only on
M
and
g
, such that the following holds. If

(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{E}
is any
critical point of
G_{\varepsilon}
,
then for any
x_{0}\in M
and any
0<r<R<R_{0}
, there holds

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\frac{r^{n-2+\alpha}}{R^{n-2+\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\,\vol_{g},

(3.18)

where the implicit constant in of the right-hand-side depends only on

M
and
g
.

Proof.

We start by fixing
x_{0}\in M
and considering two geodesic balls

\mathcal{B}_{r}:=\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})
and

\mathcal{B}_{s}:=\mathcal{B}_{s}(x_{0})
, with
0<r<s<R
and

R\in(0,\,\inj(M))
small enough (to be further specified later). Up to
subtracting a (smooth,
\varepsilon
-independent) 1-form to
A_{\varepsilon}
, we can
write

\d A_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}\qquad\mbox{in }\mathcal{B}_{R}.

Moreover, we can fix Coulomb gauge (without changing notation), i.e.,
we can assume that

\d^{*}A_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\mbox{in }\mathcal{B}_{R},\quad{\rm i}_{\nu}A_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\mathcal{B}_{R},

where
\nu
is the unit normal field to
\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}
. Then,
the Euler-Lagrange equation (
6
) becomes

-\Delta A_{\varepsilon}=j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\qquad\mbox{in }\mathcal{B}_{R}.

Standard decay estimates for elliptic equations
with a right-hand side (see e.g. Proposition
B.4

for more details) imply that there exists
R_{*}>0
(depending only on

M
and
g
) so that, for
0<r<s<R<R_{*}
, there holds

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left(\left|\d A_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}A_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{s^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left(\left|\d A_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}A_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}+s^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left|j(u_{\varepsilon},\,\D_{A_{\varepsilon}})\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g},

up to a constant depending only on
M
and
g
.
Keeping in mind that
\d^{*}A_{\varepsilon}=0
and

\d A_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{B}_{R}
,
and recalling the pointwise estimate

\left|j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\right|\leq\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|
, it follows

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{s^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}+s^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g},

(3.19)

up to a constant depending only on
M
and
g
.

Taking now
R_{0}=\min\{R_{*},\,\bar{\rho}\}
,
where
\bar{\rho}
is the distinguished radius (depending only on
M

and
g
) given by Proposition
3.3
and shrinking

so that

, by (

)

) we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}&\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{s^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}+s^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\,\vol_{g}\\
&\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{s^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}+\frac{s^{n-2+\alpha}}{R^{n-4+\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\,\vol_{g},\end{split}

(3.20)

for
\alpha\in(0,2]
as given by Proposition
3.3
.

Assume

. Then, by a classical iteration lemma
(e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma III.2.1]

or
[
6
, Lemma B.3]
), it follows

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}&\lesssim\frac{r^{n-2+\alpha}}{s^{n-2+\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\vol_{g}+\frac{r^{n-2+\alpha}}{R^{n-4+\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\,\vol_{g}\\
&\lesssim\frac{r^{n-2+\alpha}}{s^{n-2+\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{s}}\left|F_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}+\frac{r^{n-2+\alpha}}{R^{n-2+\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\,\vol_{g}\end{split}

for any
0<r<s<R<R_{0}
.
(The second inequality holds because

is
certainly bounded from above.) In particular, by taking

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\subset\mathcal{E}
be any critical point of

. Moreover, let

\alpha\in(0,\,2)
,

.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.2
,

1.5

, the energy
density is a bounded function (and its

-norm is of order
\mathrm{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
).

By (
3.24
) and (
3.2
), we clearly obtain

\frac{\d}{\d\rho}\left\{e^{C\rho^{2}/2}\rho^{2-n}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)\right\}\geq\rho^{1-n}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{\left(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}\right)^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\vol_{g}(y)-\frac{Ce^{C\rho^{2}/2}\rho^{\alpha-1}}{R^{n-2+\alpha}}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,R).

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}

(centered at

\begin{split}\frac{\d}{\d\rho}\Big\{e^{C\rho^{2}/2}\rho^{2-n}E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)&+\frac{1}{n-2}\rho^{2-n}W_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,\rho)\Big\}\geq\\

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\tau\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, any

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\,\d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\lesssim\int_{M}\left(\left|\d\tau\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\tau\right|^{2}+\left|\tau\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}

,
x_{0}

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, we write
\mathcal{B}_{r}

instead of
\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})
.
We claim that, for any scalar function

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{r}}f^{2}\,\d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left|\d f\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}f^{2}\,\vol_{g}

The inequality (
4.20

if
\mathcal{B}_{r}
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
equipped with the Euclidean metric. In case
\mathcal{B}_{r}

is a geodesic ball in
M
(with

),
the estimate (
4.20
) follows
by composition with
(normal geodesic) coordinate charts.
The implicit constant in front of the right-hand side of (
4.20
)
depends only on the metric and is bounded uniformly
with respect to
x_{0}
,
r
, because
M
is compact and smooth.

Now, take a form
\tau\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.
Let
(x^{1},\,\ldots,x^{n})
be normal geodesic
charts in
\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})

,
where the sum is taken over all multi-indices
\alpha

By applying (

) to each component

we deduce

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\,\d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\lesssim\sum_{\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left|\d\tau_{\alpha}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\left\|\tau\right\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(M)}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left\|\tau\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r})}

The last term in the right-hand side can be estimated
by applying the Hölder inequality and Sobolev embeddings.
Indeed, if

then

and hence,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left\|\tau\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r})}\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left\|\tau\right\|^{2}_{L^{p}(M)}\left|\vol(\mathcal{B}_{r})\right|^{2/n}\lesssim\left\|\tau\right\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(M)}\end{split}

(where the implicit constants are uniform with respect to

,
x_{0}
).
The lemma now follows by applying Gaffney’s inequality
(see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 4.8]
).

\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{B}_{7R/12})
is a cut-off function, such that

\zeta=1
in
\mathcal{B}_{R/2}
and
\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\lesssim R^{-1}
.
We obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{7R/12}}\left(\left|\nabla\rho_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}(1+\rho_{\varepsilon})(1-\rho_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right)\zeta\,\vol_{g}&=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{7R/12}}(\rho_{\varepsilon}-1)\left\langle\nabla\rho_{\varepsilon},\,\nabla\zeta\right\rangle\vol_{g}\\
&\qquad+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{7R/12}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(1-\rho_{\varepsilon})\,\zeta\left|\d\theta_{\varepsilon}-A_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\vol_{g}\end{split}

The second term in the right-hand side is bounded
uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon
,
due to (
4.52
),
(
4.55
) and
the bound
\left|\rho_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1
. Therefore,
upon applying the Young inequality at the right-hand side,
we deduce that

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R/2}}\left(\left|\nabla\rho_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}\right)\vol_{g}\leq C_{R}

(4.57)

for some
\varepsilon
-independent constant

.
From (
4.52
), (
4.55
) and (
4.57
),
we obtain the energy bound
E_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},\,R)\leq C_{R}
,
which contradicts (
4.45
).
This completes the proof.
∎

5
Convergence to the limiting varifold

In this section we consider the
rescaled
energy measures, defined as

\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})}{\pi\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\,\vol_{g}

(5.1)

where we identify
\vol_{g}
with a Radon measure on
M
, in a canonical way.
We prove that the measures
\mu_{\varepsilon}
converge
weakly
∗
in the sense of Radon measures in
M
to a limiting Radon measure

\mu_{*}
and that
\mu_{*}
is the weight measure of a stationary, rectifiable

(n-2)
-varifold in
M
. For a precise formulation of this result,
cf. Theorem
5.3
below.

A
k
-varifold

V
in a Riemannian
n
-manifold
M

is defined as a nonnegative
Radon measure on
G_{k}(\mathrm{T}M)
, where
k\leq n
is an integer and
G_{k}(\mathrm{T}M)
is
the (total space of the) Grassmanian bundle on
M
, a bundle whose typical
fibre is the Grassmanian manifold of
k
-planes in the tangent space to
M
.
(Details on the construction of
G_{k}(\mathrm{T}M)

can be found, for instance, in
[
24
, Section 6.5.7]
.)
We will denote the set of
k
-varifolds in
M
by
\mathcal{V}_{k}(M)
.

Remark 5.1
.

Varifolds lack a boundary operator and
there is no natural notion of orientation on them.
Thus, varifolds are less regular objects than currents (indeed, any current
induces a varifold but the converse does not hold), although they are both
generalisations of the concept of smooth submanifold.

As in
[
27
]
, one wishes to prove the rectifiability of the
limiting varifold by applying (a suitable variant of) the
Ambrosio-Soner rectifiability criterion for generalised varifolds,
i.e. Theorem 3.8 of
[
4
]
. Since in
[
4
]
the
authors work in the Euclidean setting, an extension of the Ambrosio-Soner
theorem to the present setting is needed but, as explained in

[
27
, p. 1058]
, easy to obtain. For the reader’s convenience, we
recall the main points of the argument below.

Following
[
27
, Section 6]
, we use the metric
g
to canonically
identify tensors of rank
(2,\,0)
,
(1,\,1)
and
(0,\,2)
. We denote by

\EndSym(\mathrm{T}M)
the space of symmetric endomorphisms of
\mathrm{T}M
and we define a
subbundle
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)
of
\EndSym(\mathrm{T}M)
as follows:

\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M):=\left\{S\in\EndSym(\mathrm{T}M):-ng\leq S\leq g\mbox{ and }{\rm Tr}(S)\geq k\right\}.

The typical fibre of
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)
at
x\in M
is the set

\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(\mathrm{T}_{x}M)
of symmetric endomorphisms

S_{x}\colon\mathrm{T}_{x}M\to\mathrm{T}_{x}M
with trace
\geq k
and such that

-ng_{x}\leq S_{x}\leq g_{x}
as bilinear forms on
\mathrm{T}_{x}M
.

We define a
generalised
k
-varifold in
M
as a nonnegative Radon
measure on
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)
, thus extending in the most natural way the
definition in
[
4
, Section 3]

(cf.
[
27
, Section 6]
). We denote
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{k}(M)
the set of
generalised
k
-varifolds on
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}^{*}_{k}(M)

and let
\pi
be the canonical projection

\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)\to M
. We call
\|V\|:=\pi_{*}V
the

weight measure
of
V
, where

\pi
denotes the canonical projection
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)\to M
and

\pi_{*}V
denotes the pushforward measure of
V
through
\pi
.
We say that
V

has first variation
if and only if there exists a
vector Radon measure
\delta V\in C(M,\,\mathrm{T}M)^{\prime}

such that, for any
X\in C^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}M)
,

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)}\left\langle S_{x},\,\nabla X(x)\right\rangle\,\d V(x,S_{x})=-\int_{M}\left\langle X,\,\nu\right\rangle\,\d{\left\|\delta V\right\|},

(5.2)

where
\nabla
denotes the Levi Civita connection of

M
,
S_{x}\in\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(\mathrm{T}_{x}M)
,
\left\langle S_{x},\,\nabla X(x)\right\rangle

denotes the scalar product of the matrices representing
S_{x}
and

\nabla X(x)
, regarded as endomorphisms of
\mathrm{T}_{x}M
, and
\nu
is a

\left\|\delta V\right\|
-measurable vector field on
M
with
\left|\nu\right|=1

\left\|\delta V\right\|
-a.e. in
M
.
When (
5.2
) holds, we say that
\delta V
is the
first variation
of
V
.
We call
V

stationary
if
\delta V(X)=0
for all

X\in C^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}M)
.
We say that a sequence
\{V_{h}\}\subset\mathcal{V}^{*}_{k}(M)

converges weakly
∗
to
V\in\mathcal{V}^{*}_{k}(M)
if and only if
\{V_{h}\}

converges weakly
∗
in the sense of measures on
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(M)

to
V
. Next, we define the
upper
k
-dimensional density
of
V

at
x\in M
as

\Theta^{*}_{k}(\|V\|,\,x):=\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\|V\|(\mathcal{B}_{r}(x))}{\mu(\mathcal{B}_{r}(x))}.

Keeping in mind the construction of
G_{k}(\mathrm{T}M)
, a standard localisation
argument shows that the
following version of
[
4
, Theorem 3.8(c)]
holds
(cf.
[
36
, Proposition 5.1]
)

Theorem 5.2

(Rectifiability criterion)
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}^{*}_{k}(M)
be a generalised
k
-varifold with first
variation and assume that

\Theta^{*}_{k}(\|V\|,\,x)>0\qquad\mbox{for }\|V\|{\mbox{-}a.e.}\,x\in M.

Then, there exists a (classical)
k
-rectifiable varifold
\widetilde{V}

such that

\left\|V\right\|=\|\widetilde{V}\|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\delta V=\delta\widetilde{V}.

We follow the strategy of
[
27
, Section 6]
, applying the
modifications made necessary by the logarithmic energy regime.

For any given

u\in(W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty})(M,E)
and
A\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
we define the

(0,2)
tensors
\D_{A}u^{*}\D_{A}u
and
F_{A}^{*}F_{A}
by

\D_{A}u^{*}\D_{A}u(E_{i},E_{j}):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\D_{A,E_{i}}u,\,\D_{A,E_{j}}u\right\rangle,

and, respectively,

F_{A}^{*}F_{A}(E_{i},E_{j}):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle F_{A}(E_{i},\,E_{j}),\,F_{A}(E_{i},\,E_{j})\right\rangle,

where
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\}
is any local orthonormal frame for
E\to M
.
It is easily checked that
\D_{A}u^{*}\D_{A}u
and
F_{A}^{*}F_{A}
are
gauge-invariant and smooth.

Next, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the

rescaled
stress-energy tensor field

T_{\varepsilon}\equiv T_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon}):=\frac{1}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\left(e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})g-\D_{A}u^{*}\D_{A}u-F_{A}^{*}F_{A}\right),

(5.3)

where
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})
is any critical point of
G_{\varepsilon}
.
By the same
computations as in
[
27
, Section 4]
, we see that
T_{\varepsilon}
is
divergence-free, for any
\varepsilon>0
.
By means of the metric, we can identify
T_{\varepsilon}
with the induced

\EndSym(\mathrm{T}M)
-valued Radon measure in
M
. If we can show that
the sequence
\{T_{\varepsilon}\}

converges to a limiting
\EndSym(\mathrm{T}M)
-valued measure

and that

\left|T_{*}\right|
is absolutely continuous with respect to

, then we can
represent
T_{*}
by a generalised
(n-2)
-varifold

. The necessary estimates
to this purpose are provided by Lemma
5.8
. These estimates ensure,
in addition, that we can apply Theorem

to obtain
the rectifiability of

.

The main result of this section is
Theorem
5.3
below (cf.
[

, Proposition 6.4]

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}
, where
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is defined
by (

sequence

Then, there exist a bounded Radon measure

on

and a (not
relabelled) subsequence such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\mu_{*}
weakly
∗

in the sense of measures. Moreover, the
(n-2)
-density

of
\mu_{*}

at

is defined at every
x_{0}\in M
and

\mu_{*}
is the weight measure of an associated stationary, rectifiable

(n-2)
-varifold
V=v(\Sigma,\theta)
, which in turn is such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{M}\left\langle T_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon}),\,S\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}=\int_{\Sigma}\theta(x)\left\langle\mathrm{T}_{x}\Sigma,\,S(x)\right\rangle\,\d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(x)

for all

where

,

\mu_{*}=\theta\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Sigma
.

Remark 5.4
.

The limiting measure
\mu_{*}
given by Theorem
5.3

is purely concentrated on a codimension-two varifold
and it does not
contain any “diffuse part” (i.e., a contibution absolutely continuous with respect to
\vol_{g}
).
The latter is instead typical of the “non-magnetic” Ginzburg-Landau
functional

I_{\varepsilon}(v)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left|\d v\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|v\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\,\vol_{g}\qquad\textrm{for }v\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathbb{C})

(5.4)

without further constraints. For instance, it is proven in
[
37
]

that the rescaled energy measures of critical points

v_{\varepsilon}
of
I_{\varepsilon}
(satisfying

I_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\sim\On(\left|\log\varepsilon\right|)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
)
concentrate towards a measure of the form

\widetilde{\mu}:=\left\|V\right\|+\left|\psi\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}
,
where
\psi
is a possibly non-trivial harmonic one-form on
M
.
A similar phenomenon occurs also in the evolutionary case, even in
the Euclidean setting (i.e., in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
).
Indeed, it is proven in

[
9
, Theorem A]

that for solutions to the Ginzburg-Landau heat flow,
in the logarithmic energy regime,
the rescaled parabolic energy measures converge to measures of the form

\widehat{\mu}(x,t)=\|\widehat{V}\|+|\nabla\widehat{\Phi}|^{2}\,\d x
,
where
\widehat{\Phi}
is a solution of the heat equation in

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
In rough terms, the diffuse part in the limiting measure arises because of wild
oscillations in the phase made possible by the high energy at disposal.
In the situation considered in
[
8
]
, this phenomenon is
ruled out by topological arguments.
The same would occur
if the manifold
M
is simply connected.
In Theorem
5.3
, there
is no diffuse part in
\mu_{*}
because of
the structure of the gauge-invariant functional,
which allows us to remove the non-topological contributions
to the energy, to leading order, by a suitable choice of
the connection
\D_{A}
.
This is independent of the topology of
M
; it is
a feature of the energy functional
G_{\varepsilon}
.

The first step in the proof of Theorem
5.3
is the
characterisation of the limiting measure
\mu_{*}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}

Up to small modifications, the proof is as in
[

Proof of Theorem

.

The existence of

Appendix A
Poincaré- and trace-type inequalities for differential forms

In the appendix, we collect a few technical results
on differential forms that are certainly part of the folklore.
However, since these results are crucial to our analysis,
we provide detailed proofs, for the convenience of the reader.
The following proposition provides a Poincaré-type inequality and a
trace-type inequality for co-closed
k
-forms normal to the boundary of
geodesic balls.
A crucial point is that in both inequalities we make explicit the dependence
on the radius of the ball.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
M
be closed Riemannian manifold,
x_{0}\in M
,

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
the geodesic ball of radius
\rho
centered at

x_{0}
, and let
\omega\in W^{1,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
with
p\in(1,\,+\infty)
be a
1
-form such that

\begin{cases}\d^{*}\omega=0&\mbox{in }\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}),\\
{\rm i}_{\nu}\omega=0&\mbox{on }\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

(A.1)

Then,

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim\rho\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))},

(A.2)

up to a constant depending only on
M
,
p
, and
g
. Moreover,

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim\rho^{1-1/p}\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0}))},

(A.3)

up to a constant depending only on
M
,
p
, and
g
.

Proof.

To prove (
A.2
), we notice that, if we were to work in a
Euclidean ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the Euclidean metric,
from the Gaffney-type inequality

[
19
, Theorem 4.11]
for any
1
-form

\omega
satisfying
{\rm i}_{\nu}\omega=0
we would get

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\leq C_{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))\left(\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}+\left\|\d^{*}_{0}\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\right),

(A.4)

where
\d^{*}_{0}
denotes the codifferential operator with respect to the
Euclidean metric and
C_{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))
is a constant depending only on

p
and

B_{\rho}(x_{0})
(in a way we are now going to make precise).
By (
A.4
) with
\rho=1
and
x_{0}=0

and a classical scaling argument, we easily obtain

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim\rho\left(\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}+\left\|\d^{*}_{0}\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\right),

(A.5)

up to constant depending only on
n
, which in particular
establishes (
A.4
) in the Euclidean case. We remark
explicitly that the absolute values and the integrals involved in the

L^{p}
-norms in (
A.5
) are computed with respect to the
Euclidean metric and the Euclidean volume form.

For the general case, we can work in normal coordinates centered at
x_{0}
.
Recall that the image of the geodesic ball

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
through the normal coordinates chart is the
Euclidean ball
B_{\rho}
(centered at the origin). Thus, it is enough
to consider the case in which
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})=B_{\rho}
is a
Euclidean ball centered at the origin endowed with the metric
g
of

M
.

Assume
\omega\in W^{1,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
is a
1
-form on
M

satisfying (
A.1
).
Since Gauss’ Lemma implies that the unit normal field is the same both
when evaluated with respect to
g
and with respect to the Euclidean
metric, (
A.4
) holds for
\omega
. By hypothesis, we have

\d^{*}\omega=0
. Writing
\d^{*}\omega
in coordinates (employing Einstein
notation), we see that

\begin{split}\d^{*}\omega&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(g^{ij})}}\partial_{i}\left(\sqrt{\det g}g^{ij}\omega_{j}\right)\\
&=g^{ij}\partial_{i}\omega_{j}+\frac{\partial_{i}(\sqrt{\det g}g^{ij})}{\sqrt{\det g}}\omega_{j}\\
&=\d^{*}_{0}\omega+(g^{ij}-\delta^{ij})\partial_{i}\omega_{j}+\frac{\partial_{i}(\sqrt{\det g}g^{ij})}{\sqrt{\det g}}\omega_{j}.\end{split}

By the standard properties of normal coordinates, we have

(g^{ij}-\delta^{ij})=\mathrm{O}(\rho^{2})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{\partial_{i}(\sqrt{\det g}g^{ij})}{\sqrt{\det g}}=\mathrm{O}(\rho)

(A.6)

as
\rho\to 0
. Thus, the assumption
\d^{*}\omega=0
along
with (
A.6
) leads to

\left\|\d^{*}_{0}\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}\lesssim\rho\left(\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}+\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}\right),

(A.7)

for any
\rho
sufficiently small and up to constant depending only on
n
,
p
and
g
.
In (
A.7
), the norms are again computed with respect to
the Euclidean metric and volume form. Choosing
\rho
sufficiently small,
by (
A.4
) and (
A.7
), we obtain

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}\lesssim\rho\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})},

(A.8)

for any
\rho
sufficiently small and up to constant depending only on
n
,
p
and
g
.
Once more, the norms in (
A.8
) are computed with
respect to
the Euclidean metric and volume form. However, since
M
is compact,

g
is controlled from below and from above by the Euclidean metric.
Similarly,
\vol_{g}
is controlled from below and from above by the
Euclidean volume form, and hence (
A.2
) follows from
(
A.8
) (up to replacing the implicit constant
in (
A.2
) with a larger one, which however depends still
only on
p
,
M
and
g
.

The proof of (
A.3
) follows a similar pattern. We begin again
with a Euclidean ball endowed with the Euclidean metric. Then the
inequality

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(\partial B_{\rho})}\leq C_{p}(B_{\rho})\left(\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}+\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}+\left\|\d^{*}_{0}\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}\right)

(A.9)

is proven by following the usual argument for the trace inequality for
Sobolev functions and using the Gaffney-type inequality

[
19
, Theorem 4.8]
. Then, by scaling, we derive

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(\partial B_{\rho})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho^{1/p}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}+\rho^{1-1/p}\left(\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}+\left\|\d^{*}_{0}\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}\right),

(A.10)

up to a constant depending only on
n
and
p
.
In view of (
A.1
) and (
A.5
), this establishes (
A.3
)
in the Euclidean case. For the general case, we obtain (
A.3
)
from (
A.10
) arguing as in the proof
of (
A.2
).
Since the argument is very similar, we leave the details to the reader.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

There exists a number
R_{*}>0

such that, for any
R\in(0,\,R_{*})
,
any
x_{0}\in M
and any
k
-form
\omega\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}),\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
with
\omega=0
on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
,
there holds

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\omega\right|^{2}\vol_{g}\lesssim R^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\d\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}

(A.11)

where the implicit constant in front of the right-hand side
is independent of
R
,
x_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{\omega}
be the
k
-form on
M

defined by
\bar{\omega}(x):=\omega(x)

if
x\in\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
and
\bar{\omega}(x):=0

otherwise. As
\omega=0
on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
,
we have
\bar{\omega}\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.
Let
p:=2^{*}=2n/(n-2)
. By applying Sobolev embeddings
and the Gaffney inequality (in
M
,
[
33
, Proposition 4.10]
), we have

\left\|\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\left\|\bar{\omega}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\lesssim\left\|\d\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\left\|\d^{*}\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\left\|\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}

where the implicit constants depend only on
M

(not on
R
or
x_{0}
). On the other hand,
the Hölder inequality gives

\begin{split}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}&\lesssim\left(\vol(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))\right)^{1/2-1/p}\left\|\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\\
&\lesssim R\left\|\d\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}+R\left\|\d^{*}\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}+R\left\|\bar{\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\end{split}

If
R<R_{*}
and
R_{*}
is chosen small enough,
then the last term in the right-hand side
can be absorbed in the left-hand side,
and the lemma follows.
∎

Appendix B
Elliptic estimates for the Hodge Laplacian

In this section, we gather a few regularity estimates
for elliptic problems involving differential forms.
These results, as the ones contained in Appendix
A
,
are classical, but we include detailed proofs for the reader’s convenience.
We denote by
\Delta
the Hodge-Laplace operator
on differential forms, defined by
-\Delta:=\d\d^{*}+\d^{*}\d
,
where
\d
is the exterior differential and
\d^{*}
the codifferential.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
p>n/2
, let
f\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

and let
\tau\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
be such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tau=f-H(f)&\textrm{in the sense of distributions in }M\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\langle\tau,\,\xi\right\rangle\vol_{g}=0&\textrm{for any harmonic~$k$-form }\xi.\end{cases}

(B.1)

Then,
\tau
is continuous and, for any
x\in M
,
there holds

\left|\tau(x)\right|\lesssim\int_{M}\frac{\left|f(y)\right|}{\dist(x,\,y)^{n-2}}\vol_{g}(y)

(B.2)

where
\dist
denotes the geodesic distance on
M
.

The proof of Proposition (
B.1
)
is based on the existence of a Green form
for the Hodge-Laplace operator
-\Delta
on
k
-forms.
The Green form is a differential form
\mathfrak{g}
on
M\times M

of degree
(k,\,k)
— that is, at each point of
M\times M
,

\mathfrak{g}
can locally be written as

\mathfrak{g}(x,\,y)=\sum_{I,J}\mathfrak{g}_{IJ}(x,\,y)\d x^{I}\wedge\d y^{J},

where
(x^{1},\,\ldots,\,x^{n})
,
(y^{1},\,\ldots,\,y^{n})

are local coordinate systems on
M
, the
\mathfrak{g}_{IJ}(x,\,y)
’s
are scalar coefficients, and the sum is taken over all
multi-indices
I
,
J
of order
k
.
The Green form
\mathfrak{g}
is uniquely characterised by the
following property: for any smooth
k
-form
f
on
M
,
the unique solution
\tau
of (
B.1
)
can be written as

\tau(x)=\int_{M}\mathfrak{g}(x,\,y)\wedge\star f(y)\qquad\textrm{for any }x\in M.

(B.3)

The existence of a Green form
g
was proved by de Rham
(see
[
17
, Chapter III, Section 21]
),
based on previous work by Bidal and de Rham
who constructed a parametrix (i.e., an “approximate inverse”)
for the Hodge-Laplace operator. The Green form is smooth in

\{(x,\,y)\in M\times M\colon x\neq y\}

and satisfies

\left|\mathfrak{g}(x,\,y)\right|\lesssim\dist(x,\,y)^{n-2}

(B.4)

for any
x\in M
,
y\in M
with
x\neq y

(see
[
17
]
).

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.

If
f
is a smooth
k
-form, the estimate (
B.2
)
is an immediate consequence of (
B.3
)
and (
B.4
). The estimate (
B.2
)
implies, via the Hölder inequality,
an estimate for the
L^{\infty}(M)
-norm of
\tau

in terms of the
L^{p}(M)
-norm of
f
, for any
p>n/2
.
Indeed, if
p>n/2
and
q:=p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
,
then
q<n/(n-2)
and the function
\dist(x,\,\cdot)^{n-2}

belongs to
L^{q}(M)
(in fact, the
L^{q}(M)
-norm
of
\dist(x,\,\cdot)^{n-2}
is uniformly bounded
with respect to
x
). Then, the proposition
follows by a density argument.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
,
0<R<\inj(M)

and let
\omega\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}),\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

be such that
-\Delta\omega=0
in
\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
. Then,

\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2})}\lesssim R^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}

(B.5)

(where the implicit constant in front of the
right-hand side is independent of
x_{0}
,
R
).

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{B}_{R})
be a cut-off
function such that
\zeta=1
in
\mathcal{B}_{R/2}

and
\left|\d\zeta\right|\lesssim R^{-1}
in
\mathcal{B}_{R}
.
There holds

\begin{split}\left\langle\d\omega,\,\d(\zeta^{2}\omega)\right\rangle=\left\langle\d\omega,\,\d\zeta\wedge\zeta\omega+\zeta\d(\zeta\omega)\right\rangle&=\left\langle\zeta\d\omega,\,\d\zeta\wedge\omega+\d(\zeta\omega)\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle\d(\zeta\omega)-\d\zeta\wedge\omega,\,\d\zeta\wedge\omega+\d(\zeta\omega)\right\rangle\\
&=\left|\d(\zeta\omega)\right|^{2}-\left|\d\zeta\wedge\omega\right|^{2}\end{split}

(B.6)

and similarly,

\begin{split}\left\langle\d^{*}\omega,\,\d^{*}(\zeta^{2}\omega)\right\rangle=\left\langle\d(\star\omega),\,\d(\zeta^{2}\star\omega)\right\rangle&=\left|\d(\zeta\star\omega)\right|^{2}-\left|\d\zeta\wedge\star\omega\right|^{2}\\
&=\left|\d^{*}(\zeta\omega)\right|^{2}-\left|\d\zeta\wedge\star\omega\right|^{2}\end{split}

(B.7)

Therefore, by testing the equation
-\Delta\omega=0

against
\zeta^{2}\omega
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\d(\zeta\omega)\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}(\zeta\omega)\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}&=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\d\zeta\wedge\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d\zeta\wedge\star\omega\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}\\
&\lesssim R^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\omega\right|^{2}\vol_{g}\end{split}

By applying the Gaffney inequality (in
M
,
[
33
, Proposition 4.10]
),
we obtain

\left\|\zeta\omega\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\lesssim R^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}+\left\|\zeta\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim R^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}

and the lemma follows.
∎

Thus, harmonic forms in geodesic balls satisfy a Caccioppoli-type inequality,
exactly as in the Euclidean case. Moreover, exactly as in the case of harmonic
functions on balls of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see, e.g.,
[
6
, Lemma 4.11]
),
Caccioppoli inequality allows to prove a decay property of

L^{2}
-norm of harmonic forms in geodesic balls.

Theorem B.3
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
,
0<R<\inj(M)

and let
\omega\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}),\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

be such that
-\Delta\omega=0
in
\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
. Then,

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\omega\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{R^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\omega\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g},

(B.8)

where the implicit constant in front of the
right-hand side is independent of
x_{0}
,
R
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2})
be a cut-off function,
such that
\zeta=1
in
\mathcal{B}_{R/4}

and
\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\lesssim R^{-1}
,
\left|\nabla^{2}\zeta\right|\lesssim R^{-2}
.
Let
\tau
be the unique solution of

-\Delta\tau=-\Delta(\zeta\omega)\quad\textrm{in }M,\qquad H(\tau)=0.

(
\tau
exists, because
\Delta(\zeta\omega)
is orthogonal
to all harmonic forms.) Then,
\zeta\omega=\tau+H(\zeta\omega)
.
Let
x\in\mathcal{B}_{R/8}
. By Proposition
B.1
,
we have

\left|\tau(x)\right|\lesssim\int_{M}\frac{\left|\Delta(\zeta\omega)(y)\right|}{\dist(x,\,y)^{n-2}}\vol_{g}(y)\lesssim R^{2-n}\left\|\Delta(\zeta\omega)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{R/4})}

(B.9)

because
\Delta(\zeta\omega)=\Delta\omega=0
in
\mathcal{B}_{R/4}

and
\zeta\omega=0
out of
\mathcal{B}_{R/2}

(and
\dist(x,\,y)\geq R/8
for any
x\in\mathcal{B}_{R/8}
,

y\in\mathcal{B}_{R/2}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{R/4}
).
By the Hölder inequality,

\left|\tau(x)\right|\lesssim R^{2-n/2}\left\|\Delta(\zeta\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{R/4})}

(B.10)

Now, we have

\Delta(\zeta\omega)=\d^{*}(\d\zeta\wedge\omega+\zeta\d\omega)+\d\left(\pm\star(\d\zeta\wedge\star\omega)+\zeta\d^{*}\omega)\right)=\ldots=\zeta\Delta\omega+\mathrm{l.o.t.},

where l.o.t. is a sum of terms that do not
contain second derivatives of
\omega

(but do contain derivatives of
\zeta
). In particular,
as
\Delta\omega=0
,

\left\|\Delta(\zeta\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2})}\lesssim R^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2})}+R^{-2}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R/2})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{sketch-decay1}}}{{\lesssim}}R^{-2}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}

(B.11)

(the factors of
R^{-1}
,
R^{-2}
come from the derivatives of
\zeta
).
Combining (
B.10
) with (
B.11
),
we deduce

\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{R/8})}\lesssim R^{-n/2}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}

(B.12)

The estimate of the
L^{\infty}
-norm
of
H(\zeta\omega)
is immediate
because
H(\zeta\omega)
is harmonic —
and all norms are equivalent on
\Harm^{k}(M)
.
In particular,

\left\|H(\zeta\omega)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\lesssim\left\|H(\zeta\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim R^{-n/2}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}

(because
R
is bounded from above).
Overall, we have proved

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{R/8})}\lesssim R^{-n/2}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}

(B.13)

which immediately implies decay (cf., for instance,

[
6
, Lemma 4.11]
.)
∎

In Proposition
B.4
below, we extend the decay estimate in
Theorem
B.3
to forms solving Poisson-type equations with

L^{2}
-integrable sources.

Proposition B.4
.

There exists a number
R_{*}>0
, depending only on
M
,
such that the following statement holds.
If
x_{0}\in M
,
0<R<R_{*}
and
\omega
,
f

are
k
-form on
\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
such that

-\Delta\omega=f
in
\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
, then

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\d\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{R^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\d\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}+R^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left|f\right|^{2}\vol_{g}

(B.14)

for every
r\in(0,\,R)
.

Proof.

The proof follows a very well-known pattern, typical for analogous
estimates for elliptic systems in the Euclidean setting (see, e.g.,

[
6
, Lemma 4.13]
).
We provide details for completeness, as for differential forms on manifolds
they are apparently lacking in the literature (to the best to our
knowledge, at least).

Step 1.
Fix arbitrarily
x_{0}\in M
. For the time being, let

R_{*}=\inj(M)
, and pick any
R\in(0,\,R_{*})
. During the whole proof,
we omit to indicate the center of the balls, as it will be always

x_{0}
.

As a preliminary step, we write

\omega=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
, where
\omega_{1}
satisfies

\begin{cases}\Delta\omega_{1}=0&\mbox{in }\mathcal{B}_{R},\\
\omega_{1}=\omega&\mbox{on }\partial\mathcal{B}_{R},\end{cases}

(B.15)

while
\omega_{2}
is the solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta\omega_{2}=f&\mbox{in }\mathcal{B}_{R},\\
\omega_{2}=0&\mbox{on }\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}.\end{cases}

(B.16)

In the next steps, we first obtain suitable estimates for
\omega_{1}
and

\omega_{2}
, and then we combine them to yield (
B.14
).

Step 2: Estimate for
\omega_{1}
.
Since
\Delta\omega_{1}=0
in

\mathcal{B}_{R}
, we have
\Delta(\d\omega_{1})=0
and

\Delta(\d^{*}\omega_{1})=0
in
\mathcal{B}_{R}
as well. Thus, the decay
estimate (
B.8
) holds for both
\d\omega_{1}
and

\d^{*}\omega_{1}
and yields

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left(\left|\d\omega_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{1}\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{R^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left(\left|\d\omega_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{1}\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}

(B.17)

for any
r\in(0,\,R)
, where the implicit constant at right-hand-side
depends only on
M
and
g
. On the other hand,
\omega_{1}
minimises the
functional

\omega\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left(\left|\d\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}

(B.18)

among all
k
-forms
\omega
such that
\omega=\omega_{1}
on

\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}
. This fact, along
with (
B.17
), yields

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left(\left|\d\omega_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{1}\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{R^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left(\left|\d\omega_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{1}\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}.

(B.19)

Step 3: Estimate for
\omega_{2}
.
The estimate for
\omega_{2}
follows
by testing (
B.16
) against
\omega_{2}
. We obtain

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left(\left|\d\omega_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{2}\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\left\|\omega_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}.

(B.20)

Notice that there are no boundary terms in (
B.20
)
because
\omega_{2}=0
on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}
. By
Lemma
A.2
, up to shrink
R_{*}
if necessary (but still
according only to
M
and
g
), we get

\left\|\omega_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\lesssim R\left(\left\|\d\omega_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}+\left\|\d^{*}\omega_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\right),

(B.21)

and therefore

\left\|\d\omega_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}+\left\|\d^{*}\omega_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\lesssim R\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})},

(B.22)

up to a constant depending only on
M
and
g
.

Step 4: Conclusion.
We can now estimate

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left(\left|\d\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}\leq\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}}\left(\left|\d\omega_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\d\omega_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{2}\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\eqref{eq:decay-RHS-compu4},\eqref{eq:decay-RHS-compu7}}}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{r^{n}}{R^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left(\left|\d\omega_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega_{1}\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}+R^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left|f\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\\
\lesssim\frac{r^{n}}{R^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left(\left|\d\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}+R^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}\left|f\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g},

where the last line (which provides the desired inequality) follows because

\omega_{1}
minimises the functional (
B.18
) among

k
-forms with the same value on the boundary of
\mathcal{B}_{R}
.
Then, the conclusion follows by the arbitrariness of
x_{0}
and the
compactness of
M
, which allows to choose a uniform critical radius
R_{*}
.
∎

We conclude this section by giving the proof
of a few Caccioppoli-type inequalities, which appeared in
the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Lemma B.5
.

There exists a number
R_{*}>0
, depending only on
M
,
such that the following statement holds.
Let
x_{0}\in M
,
0<r<R<R_{*}
,
a\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))

and
a_{0}>0
be such that
a_{0}^{-1}\leq a(x)\leq a_{0}

for a.e.
x\in\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
. Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}),\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

and let
\theta\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))
be a weak solution
of the equation

\d^{*}\left(a\,\d\theta\right)=\d^{*}f\qquad\textrm{in }\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}).

(B.23)

Then,

\left\|\d\theta\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0}))}\lesssim(R-r)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}

(B.24)

The estimate (
B.24
) is a Caccioppoli inequality,
except that the right-hand side contains the
L^{1}
-norm of
\theta

instead of the usual
L^{2}
norm. Therefore, we include a proof of
Lemma
B.5
, for the reader’s convenience.

Proof of Lemma
B.5
.

In the proof, we will apply the following inequality:
for any
x_{0}\in M
,
0<\sigma<\inj(M)

and
\theta\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(x_{0}))
,
there holds

\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}\lesssim\left\|\d\theta\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}+\sigma^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}

(B.25)

where the implicit constant in front of the right-hand side
does not depend on
\sigma
,
x_{0}
.
In case
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(x_{0})
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
equipped with the Euclidean metric, the
estimate (
B.25
)
is a special case of Gagliardo and Nirenberg’s
interpolation inequality, also known as Nash’s inequality
(see
[
25
]
).
A scaling argument shows that the implicit
constant in front of the right-hand side is independent of
\sigma
.
When
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(x_{0})
is a geodesic ball on
M
,
of radius
\sigma
smaller than the injectivity radius of
M
,
(
B.25
) follows from
its Euclidean counterpart, up to composition with
local (e.g., geodesic) coordinates.

Let
\rho
,
\sigma
be positive numbers
such that
r<\rho<\sigma<R
.
We consider the Caccioppoli inequality

\begin{split}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|\d\theta\right|^{2}\vol_{g}\lesssim(\sigma-\rho)^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}}\theta^{2}\vol_{g}+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}}\left|f\right|^{2}\vol_{g}\end{split}

(B.26)

For simplicity of notation, we have dropped the dependence
on
x_{0}
in all balls.
The estimate (
B.26
) is obtained
by considering a suitable cut-off
function
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})

and testing Equation (
B.23
) against
\zeta^{2}\theta
.
The estimate (
B.26
), combined
with (
B.25
) and the Young inequality, implies

\begin{split}\left\|\d\theta\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}&\lesssim(\sigma-\rho)^{-1}\left\|\d\theta\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}+(\sigma-\rho)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\\
&\lesssim\delta\left\|\d\theta\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}+C_{\delta}(\sigma-\rho)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\end{split}

(B.27)

for an arbitrary
\delta\in(0,\,1)
and a constant
C_{\delta}

that depends only on
\delta
,
a_{0}
and
(M,\,g)
.
As the inequality (
B.27
) is valid for
arbitrary values of
\rho
,
\sigma
with
r<\rho<\sigma<R
,
it can be applied iteratively. The lemma follows by
an iteration argument (see
[
18
, Lemma V.3.1]

or
[
6
, Lemma B.1]
).
∎

There is another Caccioppoli-type inequality we need,
which follows along the same lines of Lemma
B.5

but applies to
k
-forms instead of scalar functions.
As a preliminary result, we first provide a version
of the Nash inequality (
B.25
) for differential forms.

Lemma B.6
.

For any
x_{0}\in M
, any
0<r<R<\inj(M)/2

and any
\omega\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}),\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
there holds

\begin{split}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0}))}&\lesssim\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\d^{*}\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}+(R-r)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}\end{split}

where the implicit constant in front of the right-hand side
does not depend on
r
,
R
,
x_{0}
.

Proof.

All the balls we consider are centered at
x_{0}
,
so we write
\mathcal{B}_{r}
,
\mathcal{B}_{R}

instead of
\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0})
,
\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0})
and so on.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
r\geq R/2
.
Let
\rho
,
\sigma
be positive numbers,
such that
r<\rho<\sigma<R
.
Let
\omega\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R},\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.
Using geodesic normal coordinates
(x^{1},\,\ldots,\,x^{n})

centered at
x_{0}
, we can write
\omega
component-wise
as
\omega=\sum_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}\,\d x^{\alpha}
,
where the sum is taken over all multi-indices
\alpha

of order
k
.
By applying the inequality (
B.25
)
(on the ball
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
) to each component
of
\omega
, we obtain

\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}\lesssim\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}+\rho^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}

(B.28)

The implicit constant in front of the right-hand side
depend on the metric of
g
, but it can be estimated uniformly
with respect to
x_{0}
and
\rho
. Now, let
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})
be a cut-off function, such that
\zeta=1

in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
and
\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\lesssim(\sigma-\rho)^{-1}
.
By applying the Gaffney inequality
[
33
, Proposition 4.10]

to
\zeta\omega\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}&\lesssim\left\|\d(\zeta\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\left\|\d^{*}(\zeta\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\left\|\zeta\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}+\left\|\d^{*}\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}+(\sigma-\rho)^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}\end{split}

(B.29)

By combining (
B.28
) with (
B.29
)
(and observing that
\rho\geq\sigma-\rho
because we
have assumed that
r\geq R/2
), we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}&\lesssim\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}+\left\|\d^{*}\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}\\
&\qquad+(\sigma-\rho)^{-\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}+(\sigma-\rho)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\end{split}

By applying the Young inequality at the right-hand side,
for each
\delta>0
we find a constant
C_{\delta}
such that

\begin{split}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}&\lesssim\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}+\left\|\d^{*}\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}\\
&\qquad+C_{\delta}(\sigma-\rho)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}+\delta\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}\end{split}

(B.30)

If we choose
\delta
small enough (uniformly with respect to
r
,

R
,
x_{0}
), the term
\delta\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}

at the right-hand side can be ‘absorbed into the left-hand side’,
in a manner of speaking, by means of an iteration argument
(see
[
18
, Lemma V.3.1]

or
[
6
, Lemma B.1]
). The lemma follows.
∎

Lemma B.7
.

There exists a number
R_{*}>0
, depending only on
M
,
such that the following statement holds.
Let
x_{0}\in M
,
0<r<R<R_{*}

and
c\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))

be such that
c\geq 0
.
Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}),\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

and let
\omega\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))
be a weak solution
of the equation

-\Delta\omega+c\,\omega=f\qquad\textrm{in }\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}).

(B.31)

Then,

\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{0}))}\lesssim(R-r)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}+R\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R}(x_{0}))}

Proof.

Once again, we write
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}

for a generic (geodesic) ball of radius
\rho
and center
x_{0}
.
Let
r^{\prime}:=(r+R)/2
,
let
\rho
,
\sigma
be positive numbers
such that
r^{\prime}<\rho<\sigma<R
,
and let
\tau:=(\rho+\sigma)/2
.
Let
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{B}_{\tau})

be a cut-off function such that
\zeta=1

in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
and

\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-1}
.
By testing (
B.31
) against
\zeta^{2}\,\omega
,
and keeping (
B.6
), (
B.7
) into account, we deduce

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}}\left(\left|\d\omega\right|^{2}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tau}}\left|\omega\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tau}}\left|f\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}

By applying Lemma
B.6
and the Young inequality,
we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}+\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\rho})}&\lesssim(\sigma-\rho)^{-1}\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}\\
&\qquad+(\sigma-\rho)^{-1}\left\|\d^{*}\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}\\
&\qquad+(\sigma-\rho)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}+R\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\\
&\lesssim\delta\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}+\delta\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}\\
&\qquad+C_{\delta}(\sigma-\rho)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{\sigma})}+R\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\end{split}

for an arbitrary
\delta>0
and some constant
C_{\delta}

depending on
\delta
, but not on
\rho
,
\sigma
.
An iteration argument (see
[
18
, Lemma V.3.1]

or
[
6
, Lemma B.1]
) now gives

\begin{split}\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime}})}+\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime}})}&\lesssim(R-r^{\prime})^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}+R\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}\end{split}

(B.32)

where, we recall,
r^{\prime}=(r+R)/2
.
On the other hand, setting
r^{\prime\prime}:=(r+r^{\prime})/2
,
Lemma
B.6
and the (weighted) Young inequality imply

\begin{split}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime\prime}})}\lesssim(r^{\prime}-r^{\prime\prime})\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime}})}+(r^{\prime}-r^{\prime\prime})\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime}})}+(r^{\prime}-r^{\prime\prime})^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime}})}^{2}\end{split}

(B.33)

Finally, the Gaffney-type inequality (
B.29
) gives

\begin{split}\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{r})}\lesssim\left\|\d\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime\prime}})}+\left\|\d^{*}\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime\prime}})}+({r^{\prime\prime}}-r)^{-1}\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{r^{\prime\prime}})}\end{split}

(B.34)

Combining (
B.32
) with (
B.33
)
and (
B.34
), the lemma follows.

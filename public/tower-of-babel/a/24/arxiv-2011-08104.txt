1.
Introduction

In the theory of classical eigenfunctions of singular Sturm-Liouville equations such as the
Jacobi, Laguerre and Hermite polynomials or the Bessel, Whittaker and Jacobi functions,
integral representations of products are very deep formulas which prove to be useful in various
branches of mathematics. One can see
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
16
]
for a comprehensive list of such identities.
Such product formulas are used, for instance, to introduce a translation operator and a convolution structure.

The purpose of this paper is to build a translation operator and a convolution structure for the one-dimensional generalized Fourier transform

{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa,n}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and
\kappa>\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{2n}
, arising from the Dunkl’s theory
[
6
,
9
]
. Indeed, it is well known that the Euclidean Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\,K(x,\xi)dx,

where the kernel
K(x,\xi)
is the unique solution of the partial differential equation system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial x_{j}\,K(x,\xi)=-i\xi_{j}K(x,\xi)&\;\;for\;j=1,\ldots,d\\
K(0,\xi)=1&\;\;for\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

An other description was discovered by R. Howe
[
14
]
. It states that
\mathcal{F}
can also be defined by

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}=exp\left(\frac{i\pi d}{4}\right)\,exp\left(\frac{i\pi}{4}(\Delta-\|x\|^{2})\right),

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. S. Ben Said, T. Kobayashi and B.Orsted
[
6
]

gave an extension of (
1.1
) by replacing the Euclidean Laplace operator
\Delta
by the sum of squares

\Delta_{\kappa}
of Dunkl operators associated with a given finite reflection group in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. They defined a generalized Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,a}
, where
a
is a positive real parameter coming from the interpolation of the minimal
unitary representations of two different reductive Lie groups and
\kappa
is a real parameter coming from Dunkl’s theory (see
[
9
]
). Many other researchs deal with this operator. One can cite for example
[
5
,
8
,
12
]
.

In this paper, we consider the case
a=\frac{2}{n}
, where

n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and
\kappa>\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{2n}
.

Lets define the measure

(1.2)

d\mu_{\kappa,n}(x)=\left(M_{\kappa,n}\right)^{-1}|x|^{2\kappa+\frac{2}{n}-2}dx,

where

(1.3)

M_{\kappa,n}=2\left(\frac{2}{n}\right)^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(\kappa n+1-\frac{n}{2}\right).

We denote
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,\frac{2}{n}}
, the generalized Hankel transform, defined for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n})
, by

(1.4)

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}f(\lambda)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(x)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(x),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

where

B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}
is the generalized Hankel function, defined by

(1.5)

B_{\lambda}^{{\kappa,n}}(x)=j_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}\left(n|\lambda x|^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)+(-i)^{n}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{\Gamma(\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\kappa n+\frac{n}{2}+1)}\lambda xj_{\kappa n+\frac{n}{2}}\left(n|\lambda x|^{\frac{1}{n}}\right),

where
j_{\alpha}
is the normalized Bessel function of first kind and order
\alpha
, given by

(1.6)

j_{\alpha}(z)=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(z).

Note that
J_{\alpha}
is the Bessel function of first kind and index
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
.

Our aim in this paper is to establish the following product formula

(1.7)

B^{\kappa,n}_{\lambda}(x)\,B^{\kappa,n}_{\lambda}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}B^{\kappa,n}_{\lambda}(z)\,d\nu_{x,y}^{\kappa,n}(z),\quad\lambda,\>x,\>y\in\mathbb{R}.

Here the measure
d\nu_{x,y}^{\kappa,n}
is finded explicitly by relation (
4.5
). It is real valued measure, compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}
. It may not be positive and it is uniformly bounded on
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.

Formula (
1.7
) recover the cases
n=1
for Dunkl kernel on
\mathbb{R}
in
[
15
]
and
n=2
for a modified Hankel kernel in
[
4
]
.

Analougous results are obtained by Flensted-Jensen and Koornwinder
[
13
]
for Jacobi functions, and of Ben Salem
and Ould Ahmed Salem
[
7
]
for Jacobi-Dunkl functions, and Anker, Ayadi and the third author
[
3
]
for Opdam’s hypergeometric functions.

The key tool in the proof of (
1.7
) is the relation (
3.6
) of Theorem
3.1
which gives an integral representation of the product
u^{n}j_{\alpha+n}(u)j_{\alpha}(v)
, for
u,v\in[0,+\infty[
,

n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and
\alpha>-1/2
, invoking Gegenbauer polynomials. This last formula seems to be new.

The product formula (
1.7
) allows us to define and
study the translation operators

\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)d\nu_{x,y}^{\kappa,n}(z).

In particular, we find that
\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}
is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n})

Next, we introduce the convolution product for suitable functions

, by

f\star_{\kappa,n}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}g(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z).

We show in particular that
f\star_{\kappa,n}g=g\star_{\kappa,n}f
and
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f\star_{\kappa,n}g)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(g)
.

Preliminaries

In this section we recall some properties of the Gegenbauer polynomials and of the Bessel functions. See (
[
1
,

,

For
\alpha>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the Gegenbauer polynomials
C_{m}^{\alpha}
, are defined by

C_{m}^{\alpha}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(m+2\alpha)}{\Gamma(2\alpha)\Gamma(m+1)}\;_{2}F_{1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-m\;\;m+2\alpha\\
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\end{array};\,\frac{1-t}{2}\right],

where
{}_{2}F_{1}
is the Gauss hypergeometric function.
Explicitly, it takes the form

(2.1)

C_{m}^{\alpha}(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{[m/2]}(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(m-k+\alpha)}{k!(m-2k)!}(2t)^{m-2k}.

Furthermore, we have

(2.2)

|C^{\alpha}_{m}(\cos\phi)|\leq C^{\alpha}_{m}(1).

Gegenbauer polynomials could be defined by the recurrence formula:

\displaystyle C_{0}^{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha t,

(2.3)

\displaystyle mC_{m}^{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(m+\alpha-1)tC_{m-1}^{\alpha}(t)-(m+2\alpha-2)C_{m-2}^{\alpha}(t),\quad m\geq 2.

In particular, we get

C_{2}^{\alpha}(t)=2\alpha(\alpha+1)t^{2}-\alpha,

(2.4)

C_{3}^{\alpha}(t)=\frac{2^{2}}{3}\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)t^{3}-2\alpha(\alpha+1)t,

and

(2.5)

C_{4}^{\alpha}(t)=\frac{4}{6}\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)t^{4}-12\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)t^{2}+3\alpha(\alpha+1).

Gegenbauer polynomials can also be defined by the generating function

(1-2tr+r^{2})^{-2\alpha}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}C_{m}^{\alpha}(t)r^{m},

from which it follows that

(2.6)

C_{m}^{\alpha}(1)=\displaystyle\frac{(2\alpha)_{m}}{m!};\quad C_{m}^{\alpha}(-1)=(-1)^{m}\displaystyle\frac{(2\alpha)_{m}}{m!},

where
(\alpha)_{m}=\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+m)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
is the Pochhammer symbol.

Gegenbauer polynomials form an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}((-1,1),(1-t^{2})^{\alpha-1/2}dt)
and we have

(2.7)

\int_{-1}^{1}C_{m}^{\alpha}(t)C_{n}^{\alpha}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\alpha-1/2}\,dt=\frac{\pi\Gamma(2\alpha+m)}{2^{2\alpha-1}\Gamma(m+1)(m+\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)^{2}}\delta_{nm}.

The Bessel function
J_{\alpha}
of first kind and index
\alpha>-1/2
, is given by

J_{\alpha}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\,\Gamma(\alpha+n+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\alpha+2n}.

There is a relationship between Gegenbauer polynomials and the Bessel functions which is known as Gegenbauer’s addition theorem and it states, for
\alpha=0
,

(2.8)

J_{0}(\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi})=J_{0}(u)J_{0}(v)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}J_{m}(u)J_{m}(v)\cos m\phi,\quad 0\leq\phi\leq\pi,

and for general
\alpha
,

(2.9)

\frac{J_{\alpha}(\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos(\phi)})}{\{\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos(\phi)}\}^{\alpha}}=2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\alpha+m)\frac{J_{\alpha+m}(u)}{u^{\alpha}}\frac{J_{\alpha+m}(v)}{v^{\alpha}}C_{m}^{\alpha}(\cos\phi).

A consequence of (
2.7
) and Gegenbauer’s addition formulas (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) is the Sonine product formula given, for
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
, by

j_{\alpha}(u)j_{\alpha}(v)=C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\pi}j_{\alpha}\{\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}\}(\sin\phi)^{2\alpha}d\phi,

where
C_{\alpha}=\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha+1/2)}
.

Making the substitution

w=\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi},\quad 0\leq\phi\leq\pi,

we get the Bessel product formula

(2.10)

j_{\alpha}(\lambda u)j_{\alpha}(\lambda v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\alpha}(\lambda w)K_{B}^{\alpha}(u,v,w)\,w^{2\alpha+1}dw,

where

(2.11)

K_{B}^{\alpha}(u,v,w)=2^{-2\alpha+1}C_{\alpha}\frac{\{[(u+v)^{2}-w^{2}][w^{2}-(u-v)^{2}]\}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}}{(uvw)^{2\alpha}}

for
|u-v|\leq w\leq u+v
and
K^{\alpha}_{B}(u,v,w)=0
elsewhere.

K^{\alpha}_{B}
is homogeneous of degree
-2\alpha-2,
i.e.

(2.12)

K^{\alpha}_{B}(\lambda u,\lambda v,\lambda w)=\lambda^{-2\alpha-2}K^{\alpha}_{B}(u,v,w),\ \ \lambda>0.

Recall that

(2.13)

\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{B}^{\alpha}(u,v,w)\,w^{2\alpha+1}dw=1.

We close this section by recalling the Sonine’s integral formula

(2.14)

j_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{2\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(\alpha-\beta+1)}\int_{0}^{1}j_{\beta}(xt)(1-t^{2})^{\alpha-\beta-1}t^{2\beta+1}dt,\quad\alpha>\beta>-\frac{1}{2},

the derivative formula

(2.15)

\frac{d}{dx}j_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{-1}{2(\alpha+1)}xj_{\alpha+1}(x)

and the three-term recurrence relation

(2.16)

j_{\alpha+n-1}(u)=j_{\alpha+n-2}(u)+\frac{u^{2}}{4(\alpha+n-1)(\alpha+n)}j_{\alpha+n}(u),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}.

\displaystyle nu^{n-1}j_{\alpha+n-1}(u)j_{\alpha}(v)-\frac{2\alpha+n}{4(\alpha+n)(\alpha+n+1)}u^{n+1}j_{\alpha+n+1}(u)j_{\alpha}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\,\int_{0}^{\pi}n\psi_{n-1}(u,v,\phi)j_{\alpha+n-1}\left(\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}\right)(\sin\phi)^{2\alpha}d\phi

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{(u-v\cos\phi)\psi_{n}(u,v,\phi)-\frac{n}{2(\alpha+n)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi\right)\psi_{n-1}(u,v,\phi)\right\}d\phi.

Using the recurrence hypothesis (

\displaystyle u^{n+1}j_{\alpha+n+1}(u)j_{\alpha}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\alpha}}{2\alpha+n}\int_{0}^{\pi}(\sin\phi)^{2\alpha}\,j_{\alpha+n+1}\left(\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{2(\alpha+n)(u-v\cos\phi)\psi_{n}(u,v,\phi)-n\psi_{n-1}(u,v,\phi)\left(u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi\right)\right\}d\phi.

Having in mind (
3.7
) we obtain by direct calculations

\displaystyle 2(\alpha+n)(u-v\cos\phi)\psi_{n}(u,v,\phi)-n\psi_{n-1}(u,v,\phi)\left(u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(2\alpha)_{n}}\left\{2(\alpha+n)\frac{u-v\cos\phi}{\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}}C_{n}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{u-v\cos\phi}{\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.(2\alpha+n-1)C_{n-1}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{u-v\cos\phi}{\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}}\right)\right\}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}.

), we rewrite the equality (

) in the following form

\displaystyle u^{n+1}j_{\alpha+n+1}(u)j_{\alpha}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{C_{\alpha}(n+1)!}{(2\alpha)_{n+1}}\int_{0}^{\pi}C_{n+1}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{u-v\cos\phi}{\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}j_{\alpha+n+1}\left(\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}-2uv\cos\phi}\right)(\sin\phi)^{2\alpha}d\phi.

From which we obtain the recursion relation (

Making the substitution

with the help of the following expression

5.
Convolution structure

Let us denote by

\bullet\quad\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
the space of bounded continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
.

\bullet\quad\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support.

Recall that for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and
\kappa>\frac{n-1}{2n}
, the generalized Hankel transform
\mathcal{F}_{{\kappa,n}}
is defined by (
1.4
). Its inverse is given
by

\mathcal{F}_{{\kappa,n}}^{-1}(g)(x)=\mathcal{F}_{{\kappa,n}}(g)((-1)^{n}x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

The generalized Hankel transform
\mathcal{F}_{{\kappa,n}}
can be expressed in terms of Hankel transform

(5.1)

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}(f)(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2^{\alpha-1}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(t)j_{\alpha}(t\lambda)t^{2\alpha+1}dt,\quad\lambda\in]0,+\infty[.

More precisely:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
,
\kappa>\frac{n-1}{n}
, and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f)(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2n^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(g_{n})(|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}})+\frac{(-i)^{n}\lambda}{n!2^{n}n^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(J_{n}(f_{o}))(|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}}),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

where
g_{n}
and
J_{n}
are the functions defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

g_{n}(t)=f_{e}((\frac{t}{n})^{n});\quad J_{n}(f_{o})(t)=\int_{s}^{\infty}f_{o}((\frac{t}{n})^{n})(t^{2}-s^{2})^{n-1}t^{-n+1}dt,

and
f_{e}
and
f_{o}
are the even and odd parts of the function
f
.

Proof.

By making a change of variable and using (
5.1
), we get

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f_{e})(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{n}\right)^{-\left(\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}\right)}\Gamma\left(\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{-1}\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}f_{e}(x)j_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(n(|\lambda|x)^{\frac{1}{n}})x^{2\kappa+\frac{2}{n}-2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}n^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1}\Gamma(\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{e}((\frac{t}{n})^{n})j_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(t|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}})t^{2\kappa n-n+1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(g_{n})(|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}}).

Proceeding in similar way, we get

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f_{o})(\lambda)=\frac{(-i)^{n}(\frac{n}{2})^{\kappa n+\frac{n}{2}}\lambda}{\Gamma\left(\kappa n+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{o}(x)j_{\kappa n+\frac{n}{2}}(n|\lambda x|^{\frac{1}{n}})x^{2\kappa+\frac{2}{n}-1}dx.

Sonine’s integral formula (
2.14
) and Fubini’s theorem lead to

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f_{o})(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\kappa,n}\lambda\;\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{o}((\frac{t}{n})^{n})\left[\int_{0}^{1}(1-u^{2})^{n-1}j_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(tu|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}})u^{2\kappa n-n+1}du\right]t^{2\kappa n-n+1}dt.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\kappa,n}\lambda\;\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{o}((\frac{t}{n})^{n})\left[\int_{0}^{t}(t^{2}-s^{2})^{n-1}j_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(s|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}})s^{2\kappa n-n+1}ds\right]t^{-n+1}dt.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\kappa,n}\lambda\;\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(s|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}})\left[\int_{s}^{\infty}f_{o}((\frac{t}{n})^{n})(t^{2}-s^{2})^{n-1}t^{-n+1}dt\right]s^{2\kappa n-n+1}ds,

where

\displaystyle C_{\kappa,n}=\frac{(-i)^{n}}{n!2^{\kappa n+\frac{n}{2}-1}n^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1}\Gamma\left(\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}
.

Using (
5.1
, it
derives

(5.3)

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f_{o})(\lambda)=\frac{(-i)^{n}\lambda}{2^{n}n!n^{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}+1}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa n-\frac{n}{2}}(J_{n}(f_{o}))(|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{n}}).

Combining (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we obtain the result.
∎

is bounded by
1
then we get easily that
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}
is well defined for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n})
and we have

\|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}f\|_{\kappa,\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\kappa,1}.

By Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem we extend the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}
for functions
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n})
, where
1\leq p\leq 2
and we have the Hausdorff-Young inequality

\|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}f\|_{\kappa,p^{\prime}}\leq\|f\|_{\kappa,p}.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and
\kappa>\frac{n-1}{2n}
, we define the translation operator
\tau_{y}^{\kappa,n}
by

\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f(y)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)d\nu_{x,y}^{\kappa,n}(z),\quad y\in\mathbb{R},

where
d\nu_{x,y}^{\kappa,n}
is given by (
4.5
).

The following properties hold.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
,
\kappa>\frac{n-1}{n}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

(i)

\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f(y)=\tau_{y}^{\kappa,n}f(x)
.

(ii)

\tau_{0}^{\kappa,n}f=f
.

(iii)

\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}\tau_{y}^{\kappa,n}=\tau_{y}^{\kappa,n}\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}.

If we suppose also that
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, then

(iv)

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}\left(\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f\right)(\lambda)=B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f)(\lambda)
.

(v)

T^{\kappa,n}\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}=\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}T^{\kappa,n}
,

where

T^{\kappa,n}f(x)=|x|^{2(1-\frac{1}{n})}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}f(x)+\displaystyle\frac{2\kappa}{x}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}f(x)-\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{x^{2}}(1-s)f(x)\right\},

here
sf(x)=f(-x),
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

i) follows from the property
\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(y,x,z)
.

ii) is a consequence of the fact that
B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(0)=1
.

iii) follows from i).

iv) Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, then from Definition
5.2
and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f(y)B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)\right]B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)\right]d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z).

The property
\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,y)
, gives

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f)(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,y)B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)\right]d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z).

Using Theorem
4.2
, we see that

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f)(\lambda)=B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f)(\lambda).

v) First, we note that

T^{\kappa,n}B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(y)=-|\lambda|^{\frac{2}{n}}B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(y)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}T^{\kappa,n}f(y)g(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)T^{\kappa,n}g(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y).

Thus, from iv), we see that

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}\left(T^{\kappa,n}\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f\right)(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}\left(\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}T^{\kappa,n}f\right)(\lambda)=-|\lambda|^{\frac{2}{n}}B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f)(\lambda).

The assertion follows from the injectivity of the generalized Hankel transform.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and
\kappa>\frac{n-1}{2n}
,

1\leq p\leq\infty
,
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(5.4)

\|\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}(f)\|_{\kappa,p}\leq 4\,\|f\|_{\kappa,p},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We distinguish the cases:

Case 1:

p=\infty
is obvious.

Case 2:
If
p=1
, the assertion follows from Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem, the property
\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,y)
and iii) of Theorem
4.4
.

Case 3:
Let
1<p<+\infty
and
p^{\prime}
denotes the Hölder conjugate exponent of
p
. Then by Hölder inequality, we have

|\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f(y)|^{p}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(z)|^{p}|\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)|d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)|d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}

Therefore

\|\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f\|^{p}_{\kappa,p}\leq 4^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(z)|^{p}\,|\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)|\,d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)\,d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y).

Using again Fubini’s theorem and the property
\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,y)
, we get

\displaystyle\|\tau_{y}^{\kappa,n}f\|^{p}_{\kappa,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(z)|^{p}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,y)|\,d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)\,d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{p}\|f\|_{\kappa,p}^{p}.

Thus, by taking the
\left(1/p\right)^{th}
power in both sides, we obtain (
5.4
).
∎

Definition 5.5
.

The convolution product of two suitable functions
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{R}
is defined by

f\star_{\kappa,n}g(x)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\,\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}g((-1)^{n}y)\;d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y).

It shares the following immediate properties.

Properties 5.6
.

i)

f\star_{\kappa,n}g=g\star_{\kappa,n}f
.

ii)

\left(f\star_{\kappa,n}g\right)\star_{\kappa,n}h=f\star_{\kappa,n}\left(g\star_{\kappa,n}h\right)
.

iii)

(Young inequality)
For
p,q,r
such that
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-1=\frac{1}{r},
and
for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n}),
the convolution product

f\star_{\kappa}g
is a well defined element in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R},d\mu_{\kappa,n})
and

\|f\star_{\kappa,n}g\|_{\kappa,r}\leq 4\,\|f\|_{\kappa,p}\|g\|_{\kappa,q}.

Proof.

i) By using Fubini’s theorem and the property
\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,y)
, we obtain

\displaystyle f\star_{\kappa,n}g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(z)\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,(-1)^{n}y,z)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)\right]d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(z)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,(-1)^{n}y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)\right]d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z).

So using the property
\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}((-1)^{n}x,z,(-1)^{n}y)=\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,(-1)^{n}z,y)
, we get

\displaystyle f\star_{\kappa,n}g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(z)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,(-1)^{n}z,y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)\right]d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(z)\tau_{x}^{\kappa,n}f((-1)^{n}z)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\star_{\kappa,n}f(x).

ii) is obvious.

iii) follows by standard arguments.
∎

For every
R>0
, let us denote by
\mathcal{C}_{R}(\mathbb{R})
the space of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}

which are supported in
[-R,R]
. Then

Proposition 5.7
.

For
f\in\mathcal{D}_{R_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{R_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f\star_{\kappa,n}g\in\mathcal{D}_{R_{1}+R_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
and we have

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f\star_{\kappa,n}g)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(g).

Proof.

Using Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}\left(f\star_{\kappa,n}g\right)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(x)f\star_{\kappa,n}g(x)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(x)\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,(-1)^{n}y,z)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(x)\right)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)\right]d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z).

Invoking the property
\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(x,(-1)^{n}y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\kappa,n}(y,z,x)
and Theorem
4.2
, lead to

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}\left(f\star_{\kappa,n}g\right)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(z)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(z)\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)B_{\lambda}^{\kappa,n}(y)d\mu_{\kappa,n}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(f)(\lambda)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa,n}(g)(\lambda).

Which proves the proposition.

∎

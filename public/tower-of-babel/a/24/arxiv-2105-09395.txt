Moduli spaces of slope-semistable pure sheaves
Mihai Pavel

Abstract
We construct a moduli space of slope-semistable pure sheaves, building upon previous work of Le Potier and Jun Li on torsion-free sheaves over smooth surfaces. In particular, our construction provides a compactification of the Simpson moduli space of slope-stable reflexive sheaves. We also prove an effective restriction theorem for slope-(semi)stable pure sheaves following an approach due to Langer.



1
Introduction

Moduli spaces of sheaves have been extensively researched throughout the last decades, especially over low-dimensional base schemes. Their study spans across a large variety of fields, with important applications in hyperkähler geometry, gauge theory, enumerative geometry, etc. The primary point of departure concerns the construction of a moduli space of sheaves in the category of schemes. In this regard, one has to restrict to a bounded family of sheaves, in order to ensure that the moduli space will be at least of finite type over the base field. This is usually done by imposing certain stability conditions on sheaves, such as slope-stability or Gieseker-stability. The former condition was first introduced by Mumford
[
25
]
while studying the moduli of sheaves over curves, and later generalized to higher dimensions by Takemoto
[
29
]
. This notion is naturally defined for torsion-free sheaves over smooth varieties, although it has a version, called
\hat{\mu}
-stability, well defined also for pure sheaves that might be supported in positive codimension. We shall introduce
\hat{\mu}
-semistability in Section
2
, but for a more detailed account of these concepts and their basic properties we refer the reader to
[
11
, Section 1]
.

The precise formulation of the moduli problem is usually done using the categorical language of (co)representable functors. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
together with a fixed polarization
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
. Choose a numerical Grothendieck class
c\in K(X)_{\text{num}}
, that will fix the topological type of sheaves, and consider the moduli functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:(\text{Sch}^{ft}/\mathbb{C})^{\text{op}}\to(\text{Sets})

that associates to any scheme
S
of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
the set of all equivalence classes of
S
-flat families of slope-semistable sheaves of class
c
on
X
. In this language, the existence of a fine moduli space of sheaves is equivalent to the respresentability of the functor
\mathcal{M}
. In general, as semistable sheaves have many automorphisms, we should not expect that
\mathcal{M}
is represented. However, one may still investigate if the functor
\mathcal{M}
is corepresented. For this to happen, one has to work instead with the notion of Gieseker-semistability, a stronger condition than slope-semistability, that was used by Gieseker
[
5
]
to construct projective moduli spaces of torsion-free sheaves over smooth surfaces. His methods were further generalized by Maruyama
[
21
]
in higher dimensions. In addition, with a slightly different approach, Simpson
[
27
]
was later able to work-out the construction in the case of pure sheaves. It is noteworthy that all these constructions are based on the well-known GIT technique developed by Mumford
[
26
]
.

From an algebraic point of view, working with Gieseker-stability gives the right moduli space of sheaves with good functorial properties. On the other hand, a strong motivation for studying slope-(semi)stability spurs from the so-called Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence, which asserts that holomorphic vector bundles over compact complex manifolds admit Hermitian Yang–Mills (HYM) connections if only if they are slope-polystable. It is not suprising that this significant result, also known as the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau Theorem
[
2
,
30
]
, has found many applications in the literature, as it draws a deep connection between algebraic geometry and gauge theory. In what follows, we present only the algebraic side of the story, but we refer the reader to
[
4
]
for the analytical aspects of the theory.

In contrast to the case of Gieseker-stability, there are still many open problems concerning the construction of a right moduli space of slope-semistable sheaves. In this respect, Langer
[
15
]
has already pointed out several drawbacks of working with slope-semistability, such as the fact that the moduli stack of torsion-free slope-semistable sheaves is neither
S
-complete nor
\Theta
-reductive (in the sense of
[
1
]
). This indicates that the existence of a moduli space corepresenting the moduli functor
\mathcal{M}
in the category of schemes is very unlikely. Building upon work of Le Potier and Jun Li, Huybrechts and Lehn constructed in
[
11
, Ch. 8]
a projective scheme
M^{\mu ss}
parametrizing slope-semistable sheaves with fixed determinant on a smooth surface. Even though the scheme
M^{\mu ss}
does not corepresent
\mathcal{M}
, it turns out that
M^{\mu ss}
is closely related to the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck compactification of HYM connections (see
[
20
]
), a reminder of the close ties with gauge theory. Greb and Toma
[
7
]
later generalized their construction over higher dimensional base schemes endowed with a multipolarization, but only within the category of weakly normal varieties. Also their moduli spaces over higher-dimensional bases found applications in gauge theory (see
[
6
]
). We wish to emphasize that the work mentioned above treats only the case of torsion-free sheaves of fixed determinant on smooth varieties.

Main results
.

In this paper we aim to construct a moduli space of
\hat{\mu}
-semistable pure sheaves on
X
. As before, the general strategy follows ideas of Le Potier
[
18
]
and Jun Li
[
20
]
, even though our approach differs in several relevant aspects that we carefully point out below:

1.

The key ingredient in all these constructions is the Mehta–Ramanathan restriction theorem, which asserts that slope-(semi)stability is preserved by restriction to a general divisor of sufficiently large degree. This was previously known for torsion-free sheaves over smooth varieties (see
[
23
,
24
,
3
,
13
]
). We prove here
a restriction theorem for pure sheaves
, whose support schemes might be lower dimensional and singular.

Theorem
.

Let
E
be a
\hat{\mu}
-(semi)stable sheaf on a polarized smooth variety
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))
over
\mathbb{C}
. Then the restriction
E|_{D}
to a general divisor
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)|
remains
\hat{\mu}
-(semi)stable for
a\gg 0
.

In fact, in Section
3
we prove something more, by also giving an effective bound on the degree of the divisor from which the theorem holds. The methods employed in the proof are based on
[
13
]
. Due to the geometric importance of slope semistability, our restriction theorem is of independent interest and might have applications in the study of moduli spaces of pure sheaves supported in positive codimension. Our result also generalizes a theorem of Höring and Peternell
[
10
, Lem. 2.11]
, which they apply to the study of the Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition for minimal models. Their result is the only restriction theorem we are aware of over singular spaces.

2.

To ease up the presentation, we write down the construction when
X
is endowed with a single polarization
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
, but the same techniques could be adapted for a multipolarization. Since the
\hat{\mu}
-semistable sheaves of class
c
on
X
form a bounded family, cf.
[
11
, Thm. 3.3.7]
, they all fit inside a locally closed subscheme
R^{ss}
of an appropriate Quot scheme (see Section
6
). However, any quotient of
R^{ss}
is uniquely determined only up to the action of a linear group
G
. We would like to quotient
R^{ss}
by
G
, but unfortunately this problem does not admit a good GIT quotient. Nevertheless,
R^{ss}
is endowed with a natural
G
-equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}
(see Section
6
), and we will show in Theorem
4.1
that a power of
\mathcal{L}
is globally generated by
G
-invariant sections, after replacing
R^{ss}
by its weak normalization if necessary. To achieve this, we will restrict the universal family of quotients over
R^{ss}
to a smooth complete intersection
C\subset X
such that its support has fiber dimension one, and where slope-(semi)stability coincides with Gieseker-(semi)stability. Through this process, for a fixed quotient
[E]\in R^{ss}
, our restriction theorem ensures that its
\hat{\mu}
-semistability is preserved if we choose
C
sufficiently general of large enough degree. Hence
E|_{C}
lies inside the Simpson moduli space
M^{Gss}_{C}
of
1
-dimensional (Gieseker-)semistable sheaves on
C

[
27
]
. This will be enough to construct a
G
-invariant rational map
\psi_{E}:R^{ss}\dashrightarrow M^{Gss}_{C}
, well-defined around
[E]
, such that some power
\mathcal{L}^{\nu}
is the pull-back via
\psi_{E}
of an ample line bundle on
M^{Gss}_{C}
. This way we will obtain a local
G
-invariant section of
\mathcal{L}^{\nu}
non-vanishing at
[E]
, which moreover will be defined outside a closed subset of codimension
\geq 2
in
R^{ss}
(see Section
4.2
for the details). Then, by using the weak normality assumption as in
[
7
]
, we will extend this section over the whole
R^{ss}
. Repeating this construction around each point of
R^{ss}
will show that a power of
\mathcal{L}
is globally generated by
G
-invariant sections.

In
[
11
]
and
[
7
]
, the authors follow the same lines as above, except that they use the Gieseker–Maruyama construction of
M^{Gss}_{C}
, which endows
M^{Gss}_{C}
with a relative ample line bundle
\mathcal{A}
over
\Pic(C)
. In order to separate points of
R^{ss}
, they pull back sections (of some power) of
\mathcal{A}
from
M^{Gss}_{C}
to
R^{ss}
, and for this reason they need to fix the determinant of sheaves. Our approach overcomes this restriction, we
do not fix the determinant
since we use an (absolute) ample line bundle over
M^{Gss}_{C}
in the corresponding case, as given by Simpson’s construction. Also, one can immediately derive from our methods a relative moduli space over
\Pic(X)
.

3.

To end the construction, one then considers the Proj-scheme of a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(R^{ss},\mathcal{L}^{k\nu})^{G}

for some power
\nu>0
such that
\mathcal{L}^{\nu}
is globally generated, and declares it the moduli space of slope-semistable sheaves. However, as
R^{ss}
is not necessarily a proper scheme, some care has to be taken since this algebra is not a priori of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. In contrast to
[
11
,
7
]
, we follow a different path for proving the projectivity of the moduli space, since we are under the impression that there is a missing step in
[
11
, Prop. 8.2.6]
(see Remark
5.2
). In doing so, we first introduce the notion of
G
-properness (see Definition
5.1
) for
G
-invariants morphisms of schemes, which generalizes the classical notion of properness when
G
acts trivially. This holds in particular for
R^{ss}
(see Lemma
6.1
), as a consequence of the valuative criterion of properness due to Langton
[
16
]
. We further define what we call the relative
G
-normalization, an equivariant version of the well-known Stein factorization, and then show its existence when
G
is reductive (see Proposition
5.2
). Using this ingredient, we construct
the Iitaka
G
-fibration
corresponding to a
G
-proper scheme endowed with a
G
-equivariant semiample line bundle (see Theorem
5.3
). Our work generalizes the classical Iitaka fibration to the equivariant case, and may find applications in other places as well.

Finally, we employ the Iitaka
G
-fibration to construct the moduli space. As a culmination of our work, we obtain the following main result proving the existence and uniqueness of a moduli space parametrizing
\hat{\mu}
-semistable sheaves of class
c
on
X
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}^{wn}
the restriction of
\mathcal{M}
to the full subcategory
(\text{Sch}^{wn}/\mathbb{C})
of weakly normal varieties.

Main Theorem
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
together with a fixed polarization
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
, and choose a numerical Grothendieck class
c\in K(X)_{\text{num}}
of dimension
d>1
. Then there exists a unique triple
(M^{\mu ss},\mathcal{A},e)
formed of a weakly normal projective variety
M^{\mu ss}
endowed with an ample line bundle
\mathcal{A}
and a natural number
e>0
such that there is a natural transformation
\Psi:\mathcal{M}^{wn}\to\Hom_{(\text{Sch}^{wn}/\mathbb{C})}(-,M^{\mu ss})
, that associates to any weakly normal variety
S
and any
S
-flat family
\mathcal{E}
of
\hat{\mu}
-semistable sheaves of class
c
on
X
a classifying morphism
\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}:S\to M^{\mu ss}
, satisfying the following properties:

(a)

For any
S
-flat family
\mathcal{E}
of
\hat{\mu}
-semistable sheaves of class
c
on
X
, the classifying morphism
\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(\mathcal{A})\cong\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}(w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\cdot h^{d-1})^{e},

where
\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}(w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\cdot h^{d-1})
is the determinant line bundle on
S
defined in Section
4
.

(b)

For any other triple
(M^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime},e^{\prime})
, with
M^{\prime}
a projective scheme over
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
an ample line bundle on
M^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime}
a natural number satisfying property (1), we have
e|e^{\prime}
and there exists a unique morphism
\phi:M^{\mu ss}\to M^{\prime}
such that
\phi^{*}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{A}^{(e^{\prime}/e)}
.

Moreover, if the class
c
has dimension
2
, then the same result as above holds true over the category
(\text{Sch}^{ft}/\mathbb{C})
of schemes of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

4.

In the last section we describe the geometric points of the moduli space
M^{\mu ss}
. Consequently, we generalize several known results in the literature to the case of pure sheaves (see
[
18
,
20
,
11
,
7
]
). For a detailed exposition of the state-of-the-art in the torsion-free case see also
[
6
, Sect. 2.8]
. In particular, we show in Proposition
6.1
that our moduli space
M^{\mu ss}
provides a compactification of the Simpson moduli space of slope-stable reflexive (pure) sheaves. We summarize the separation results in Corollary
6.1
.

Outline of the paper
.

In Preliminaries, we introduce the notion of
\hat{\mu}
-stability and recall some of its basic properties. Here we also define the reflexive hull of a pure sheaf, that will be needed later for describing the geometric points of the moduli space. In Section
3
, we prove our restriction theorems for slope-semistability in the pure case, by following a strategy developed by Langer. In Section
4
, we introduce a class of determinant line bundles which correspond functorially to any
S
-flat family of slope-semistable sheaves and show their semiampleness, as stated by Theorem
4.1
. Similarly to the case studied in
[
7
]
, in order to prove the semiampleness result in higher dimensions we need to work under weak normality assumptions on schemes. In Section
5
we generalize the Stein factorization and the Iitaka fibration to the equivariant case. For this, we introduce the notion of
G
-properness for
G
-invariant morphisms and describe some of its basic properties. Among other things, here we also prove a devissage lemma for equivariant sheaves on
G
-proper schemes. Finally, in Section
6
we give the construction of the moduli space of sheaves. We end by studying the geometric points of
M^{\mu ss}
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we set up notation and terminology. We first introduce the slope-semistability conditions and then recall some known facts about the reflexive hull of a pure sheaf. The main reference here is
[
11
, Sect. 1]
.

Throughout this paper, let
X
be a connected smooth projective variety of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{C}
, and fix
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
a very ample line bundle on
X
.

Let
E
be a coherent sheaf of pure dimension
d
on
X
, i.e.
\dim(F)=d
for all non-zero subsheaves
F\subset E
. The Hilbert polynomial of
E
is given by
P(E,m)=h^{0}(X,E(m))
for
m\gg 0
, and has the form

\displaystyle P(E,m)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}(E)\frac{m^{i}}{i!},

with rational coefficients
\alpha_{i}(E)
. We call
r(E)\coloneqq\alpha_{d}(E)
the
multiplicity
of
E
.

If
E
is pure of dimension
d=\dim(X)
, also called
torsion-free
, define its
rank
by
\rk(E)\coloneqq\alpha_{d}(E)/\alpha_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
and its
\mu
-
slope
by
\mu(E)\coloneqq c_{1}(E)H^{d-1}/\rk(E)
for some divisor
H\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
. Recall that
E
is called
\mu
-
semistable
(resp.
\mu
-
stable
) if
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)
(resp.
<
) for all subsheaves
F\subset E
of rank
0<\rk(F)<\rk(E)
.

A simple computation using the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch formula yields

\displaystyle c_{1}(E)H^{d-1}=\alpha_{d-1}(E)-\rk(E)\alpha_{d-1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}).

Hence one may also use the ratio
\hat{\mu}(E)\coloneqq\alpha_{d-1}(E)/\alpha_{d}(E)
, called the
\hat{\mu}
-
slope
of
E
, to check the slope-semistability of
E
. Indeed, as we have

\displaystyle\mu(E)=\alpha_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\hat{\mu}(E)-\alpha_{d-1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}).

This motivates the following definition of slope-semistability for pure sheaves:

Definition 2.1
.

A coherent sheaf
E
of dimension
d
is called
\hat{\mu}
-
semistable
(resp.
\hat{\mu}
-
stable
) if
E
is pure and
\hat{\mu}(F)\leq\hat{\mu}(E)
(resp.
<
) for all subsheaves
F\subset E
of multiplicity
0<\alpha_{d}(F)<\alpha_{d}(E)
.

If
E
is pure, then
E
has a unique Harder-Narasimhan filtration

\displaystyle 0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{l}=E

such that its factors
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
are
\hat{\mu}
-semistable and

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(E_{1})>\hat{\mu}(E_{2}/E_{1})>\ldots>\hat{\mu}(E/E_{l-1}).

We denote
\hat{\mu}_{\max}(E)\coloneqq\hat{\mu}(E_{1})
and
\hat{\mu}_{\min}(E)\coloneqq\hat{\mu}(E/E_{l-1})
. If
E
is already
\hat{\mu}
-semistable, then
E
has a Jordan-Hölder filtration

\displaystyle 0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{l}=E

such that its factors
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
satisfy
\hat{\mu}(E_{i}/E_{i-1})=\hat{\mu}(E)
. In this case the filtration is not unique, but nevertheless its graded sheaf
\gr_{JH}(E)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i}E_{i}/E_{i-1}
is so in codimension one, i.e. outside a closed subset of codimension
\geq 2
in
\Supp(E)
.

2.1
Reflexive hull of a sheaf

Let
E
be a pure sheaf on
X
. Recall that
E
satisfies property
S_{2}
if

\displaystyle\depth_{x}(E)\geq\min\{2,\codim(x,\Supp(E))\}\text{ for all }x\in\Supp(E).

If
E
is torsion-free, then
E
has a unique reflexive hull
E^{\vee\vee}\coloneqq\sheafHom(\sheafHom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}),\mathcal{O}_{X})
that is also torsion-free and satisfies
S_{2}
. If
E
is pure of dimension
d
, we set

\displaystyle E^{H}\coloneqq\Exts^{n-d}(\Exts^{n-d}(E,\omega_{X}),\omega_{X}),

where
\omega_{X}
is the dualizing line bundle on
X
. We call
E^{H}
the
reflexive hull
of
E
. Then there is a natural inclusion
E\to E^{H}
, cf.
[
11
, Prop. 1.1.10]
, such that
E^{H}/E
has codimension
\geq 2
in
\Supp(E)
. Moreover
E^{H}
is pure and satisfies
S_{2}
.

Notice that we recover the classical notion for
d=n
. Also, this definition agrees with the more general one given by Kollár in
[
12
, Sect. 9]
. For a throughout study of reflexive hulls we refer the reader to his book.

3
Restriction theorems

In this section we prove restriction theorems for
\hat{\mu}
-semistable pure sheaves. When the base scheme is smooth, it is already known that slope-semistability or slope-stability is preserved by restriction to a general divisor of sufficiently large degree. The first general results of this kind were proven by Mehta and Ramanathan in
[
23
,
24
]
. However their restriction theorems are not effective, as they provide no bound on the degree of the divisor. Such an effective restriction theorem, but only for slope-semistability, was later given by Flenner
[
3
]
in zero characteristic. Finally, Langer
[
13
]
was able to prove stronger effective restriction theorems that also work in positive characteristic. Here we generalize his method to the case of pure sheaves supported in positive codimension.

We end this section by studying the preservation of flatness for families of sheaves under restriction to divisors.

3.1
Restriction theorems for
\hat{\mu}
-semistability

Let
E
be a purely
d
-dimensional sheaf on
X
. Denote its support by
Y
and let
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
be the pullback of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
to
Y
. One can find a linear subspace
W\subset H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1))
of dimension
d+1
that gives a surjective finite morphism
\pi:Y\to\mathbb{P}(W)
with
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{O}(1))=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
. Here
\mathcal{O}(1)
is the canonical line bundle corresponding to a hyperplane
H\in\mathbb{P}(W^{\vee})
. Since
\pi_{*}
is exact,
E
and
\pi_{*}E
have the same Hilbert polynomial. Thus,
\alpha_{d}(E)=\rk(\pi_{*}E)
and

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(E)=\mu(\pi_{*}E)+\hat{\mu}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(W)})=\mu(\pi_{*}E)+(d+1)/2,

where the slope
\mu(\pi_{*}E)
is computed with respect to
H
. Note that semistability is not necessarily preserved by pushforward, but we have the following inequalities due to Langer
[
14
, Lem. 6.2.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the above notation, we have

1.

\mu_{\max}(\pi_{*}E)-\mu(\pi_{*}E)\leq\hat{\mu}_{\max}(E)-\hat{\mu}(E)+\rk(\pi_{*}E)^{2}
,

2.

\mu(\pi_{*}E)-\mu_{\min}(\pi_{*}E)\leq\hat{\mu}(E)-\hat{\mu}_{\min}(E)+\rk(\pi_{*}E)^{2}
.

For
a>0
, denote by
\mathbb{H}_{a}=\mathbb{P}(S^{a}(W^{\vee}))
the linear system of hypersurfaces of degree
a
in
\mathbb{P}(W)
. For
D\in\mathbb{H}_{a}
, the scheme-theoretic inverse image
\pi^{-1}(D)
is an effective divisor on
Y
, which is
E
-regular and moreover satisfies
\pi_{*}(E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)})=\pi_{*}(E)|_{D}
. If the restriction
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
is pure, one can easily check that

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)})=\hat{\mu}(\pi_{*}(E)|_{D})=\mu(\pi_{*}(E)|_{D})/a+\hat{\mu}(\mathcal{O}_{D}).

(3.1)

Note that since
\pi
is finite,
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
is pure if and only if
\pi_{*}(E)|_{D}
is pure.

Lemma 3.2
.

The restriction
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
is pure for a general divisor
D\in\mathbb{H}_{a}
.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Cor. 1.1.14 ii)]
, if
\pi^{-1}(D)
contains none of the associated points of
E
and
\Exts_{X}^{q}(E,\omega_{X})
for all
q\geq 0
, then
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
is pure. Obviously we can choose such a divisor since
W
is base-point-free.
∎

In what follows, for any torsion-free sheaf
F
on
\mathbb{P}(W)
the
discriminant
of
F
is defined as usual by

\displaystyle\Delta(F)=2\rk(F)c_{2}(F)-(\rk(F)-1)c_{1}(F)^{2}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a pure sheaf of dimension
d
and multiplicity
r\coloneqq\rk(\pi_{*}G)
on
Y
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}G)H^{d-2}+r^{2}(\hat{\mu}_{\max}(G)-\hat{\mu}(G))(\hat{\mu}(G)-\hat{\mu}_{\min}(G))\geq-r^{6}.

Proof.

We first introduce some notation. Let
0=G_{0}\subset G_{1}\subset\ldots\subset G_{l}=G
be the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
G
. Since
\pi_{*}
is exact,
0=\pi_{*}G_{0}\subset\pi_{*}G_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\pi_{*}G_{l}=\pi_{*}G
remains a filtration. Set
F_{i}\coloneqq\pi_{*}(G_{i}/G_{i-1})
,
r_{i}\coloneqq\rk(F_{i})
,
\mu_{i}\coloneqq\mu(F_{i})
,
\mu\coloneqq\mu(\pi_{*}G)
.

A direct computation using the Hodge Index Theorem gives

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta(\pi_{*}G)H^{d-2}}{r}=

\displaystyle\sum\frac{\Delta(F_{i})H^{d-2}}{r_{i}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i<j}r_{i}r_{j}\left(\frac{c_{1}(F_{i})}{r_{i}}-\frac{c_{1}(F_{j})}{r_{j}}\right)^{2}H^{d-2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\frac{\Delta(F_{i})H^{d-2}}{r_{i}}-\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i<j}r_{i}r_{j}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j})^{2}.

By using
[
13
, Lem. 1.4]
, we get

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}G)H^{d-2}+r^{2}(\mu_{1}-\mu)(\mu-\mu_{l})\geq\sum\frac{r}{r_{i}}\Delta(F_{i})H^{d-2},

or equivalently

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}G)H^{d-2}+r^{2}(\hat{\mu}_{\max}(G)-\hat{\mu}(G))(\hat{\mu}(G)-\hat{\mu}_{\min}(G))\geq\sum\frac{r}{r_{i}}\Delta(F_{i})H^{d-2}.

Now the result follows from the lemma bellow.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Under the above assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\sum\frac{r}{r_{i}}\Delta(F_{i})H^{d-2}\geq-r^{6}.

Proof.

Applying
[
13
, Thm. 5.1]
to each
F_{i}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(F_{i})H^{d-2}+r_{i}^{2}(\mu_{\max}(F_{i})-\mu(F_{i}))(\mu(F_{i})-\mu_{\min}(F_{i}))\geq 0.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\max}(F_{i})-\mu(F_{i})\leq r_{i}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\mu(F_{i})-\mu_{\min}(F_{i})\leq r_{i}^{2}.

Putting together the above inequalities yields

\displaystyle\sum\frac{r}{r_{i}}\Delta(F_{i})H^{d-2}\geq-r\sum r_{i}^{5}\geq-r\left(\sum r_{i}\right)^{5}\geq-r^{6}.

∎

We are now ready to prove the announced restriction theorem. We give a proof by contradiction following the idea of Langer
[
13
, Thm. 5.2]
. In this regard, for the reader’s convenience, we adopt his notation.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
E
is
\hat{\mu}
-stable, and let
D\in\mathbb{H}_{a}
be a normal divisor on
\mathbb{P}(W)
such that
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
is pure. If

\displaystyle a>\frac{r-1}{r}\Delta(\pi_{*}E)H^{d-2}+\frac{1}{r(r-1)}+r^{5}(r-1),

then the restriction
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
is
\hat{\mu}
-stable.

Proof.

Suppose, on contrary, that
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
is not stable. Let
S
be a saturated destabilizing subsheaf of
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
, and denote
T\coloneqq(E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}/S)
. Let
G
be the kernel of the composition
E\to E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}\to T
. Then there are two short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to G\to E\to T\to 0

and

\displaystyle 0\to E(-\pi^{-1}(D))\to G\to S\to 0.

Set
r\coloneqq\rk(\pi_{*}G)
and
\rho\coloneqq\rk(\pi_{*}S)
. A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}G)H^{d-2}=\penalty\

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}E)H^{d-2}-\rho(r-\rho)a^{2}

\displaystyle+2(rc_{1}(\pi_{*}T)-(r-\rho)Dc_{1}(\pi_{*}E))H^{d-2}.

Since
T
is a destabilizing quotient of
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
, we have
\hat{\mu}(T)\leq\hat{\mu}(E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)})
, or equivalently
\mu(\pi_{*}T)\leq\mu(\pi_{*}(E)|_{D})
by (
3.1
). Then
2(rc_{1}(\pi_{*}T)-(r-\rho)Dc_{1}(\pi_{*}E))H^{d-2}\leq 0
, and so

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}G)H^{d-2}\leq\Delta(\pi_{*}E)H^{d-2}-\rho(r-\rho)a^{2}.

By using Lemma
3.3
, we deduce

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}E)H^{d-2}-\rho(r-\rho)a^{2}+r^{2}(\hat{\mu}_{\max}(G)-\hat{\mu}(G))(\hat{\mu}(G)-\hat{\mu}_{\min}(G))\geq-r^{6}.

(3.2)

Since
E
and
E(-\pi^{-1}(D))
are stable, we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\max}(G)-\hat{\mu}(G)=\hat{\mu}_{\max}(G)-\hat{\mu}(E)+\frac{r-\rho}{r}a\leq\frac{r-\rho}{r}a-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}

(3.3)

and

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(G)-\hat{\mu}_{\min}(G)=\hat{\mu}(E)-\hat{\mu}_{\min}(G)+\frac{\rho}{r}a\leq\frac{\rho}{r}a-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}.

(3.4)

Putting together the inequalities (
3.2
)–(
3.4
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}E)H^{d-2}-\rho(r-\rho)a^{2}+r^{2}\left(\frac{r-\rho}{r}a-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\right)\left(\frac{\rho}{r}a-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\right)\geq-r^{6}.

Thus

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi_{*}E)H^{d-2}-\frac{r}{r-1}a+\frac{1}{(r-1)^{2}}\geq-r^{6},

which gives a contradiction.
∎

Note that the theorem above is effective, i.e. we can choose
a>0
large enough to work for all stable sheaves. Furthermore, essentially the same proof gives also an effective restriction theorem for
\hat{\mu}
-semistable sheaves.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
E
is
\hat{\mu}
-semistable. For
a\gg 0
the restriction
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D)}
remains
\hat{\mu}
-semistable for a general divisor
D\in\mathbb{H}_{a}
.

Proof.

See the proof in
[
13
, Cor. 5.4]
.
∎

For
a>0
, let
\Pi_{a}\coloneqq|\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)|
be the linear system of hypersurfaces of degree
a
on
X
. By using the openness property of slope-semistable sheaves in flat families, one can further show:

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose that
E
is
\hat{\mu}
-(semi)stable. The restriction
E|_{D}
to a general divisor
D\in\Pi_{a}
remains
\hat{\mu}
-(semi)stable for
a\gg 0
.

Proof.

We prove the result only when
E
is
\hat{\mu}
-semistable, as the stable case is completely similar. By Theorem
3.2
, for
a\gg 0
there is a divisor
D^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}_{a}
such that the restriction
E|_{\pi^{-1}(D^{\prime})}
remains
\hat{\mu}
-semistable. Note that the divisor
\pi^{-1}(D^{\prime})
must avoid the associated points of
E
.
If we choose
a
large enough, then
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(a))\to H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a))
is surjective, and so
\pi^{-1}(D^{\prime})
is the restriction of some divisor

contains none of the associated points of

.

Consider the incidence variety
Z=\{(D,x)\in\Pi_{a}\times X\mid x\in D\}
with its natural projections
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
. If

is the open subset of divisors avoiding the associated points of

, then for each

we have a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to E(-D)\to E\to E|_{D}\to 0.

Hence the Hilbert polynomial of

remains constant when

varies through

, which means that
p_{2}^{*}E
is flat over

. But
D_{0}\in U
and since the semistability property is open in flat families (cf.

, Prop. 2.3.1]
), we conclude that

remains
\hat{\mu}
-semistable for a general divisor

.

∎

3.2
Restriction of a flat family to a divisor

Next we turn our attention to the flatness property for families of sheaves.
In what follows, let
S
be a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme of finite type and
\mathcal{E}
a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{S\times X}
-module such that each geometric fiber
\mathcal{E}_{s}
has no zero-dimensional associated points.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
a\gg 0
, there exists a general divisor
D\in\Pi_{a}
that avoids the associated points of every geometric fiber
\mathcal{E}_{s}
for
s\in S
.

Proof.

Consider the family
\Sigma
of all irreducible closed subschemes of
X
corresponding to associated points of
\mathcal{E}_{s}
for
s\in S
closed. By
[
8
, Prop. 1.3]
, the family
\Sigma
is bounded, i.e. the set of Hilbert polynomials
\{P_{Y}\}_{Y\in\Sigma}
is finite, and for
a\gg 0
every element of
\Sigma
is
a
-regular. Without loss of generality, we may assume that all elements of
\Sigma
have the same Hilbert polynomial, say
P
. In this case,
\Sigma
is a subset of the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb_{X}(P)
.

Consider the incidence variety

\displaystyle I=\{(Y,D)\in\Hilb_{X}(P)\times\Pi_{a}\mid Y\in D\}

with its natural projections
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
. Since each
Y\in\Sigma
is
a
-regular, its Hilbert polynomial satisfies
P(a)=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a))
. Thus,
p_{1}
is a projective bundle with fibers of dimension
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(a))-P(a)
. Therefore,
\dim(I)=\dim(\Hilb_{X}(P))+h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(a))-P(a)
. On the other hand, the general fiber of
p_{2}
has dimension

\displaystyle\dim(I)-\dim(\Pi_{a})=\dim(\Hilb_{X}(P))-P(a)<0.

The last inequality is satisfied for
a\gg 0
, since
P
has degree
>0
by assumption. In other words, for
a\gg 0
there is a general divisor
D\in\Pi_{a}
which contains no point of
\Hilb_{X}(P)
. In particular,
D
contains no element of
\Sigma
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is
S
-flat and each geometric fiber
\mathcal{E}_{s}
has no zero-dimensional associated points. Then the restriction
\mathcal{E}|_{S\times D}
to a general divisor
D\in\Pi_{a}
remains
S
-flat for
a\gg 0
.

Proof.

Let
D\in\Pi_{a}
be a general divisor satisfying Lemma
3.5
. Then
D
is
\mathcal{E}_{s}
-regular, i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{s}(-D)\to\mathcal{E}_{s}
is injective, for every
s\in S
. By
[
22
, Thm. 22.5]
it follows that
\mathcal{E}|_{S\times D}
is
S
-flat.
∎

Even though the family
\mathcal{E}|_{S\times D}
remains
S
-flat, some of its fibers
\mathcal{E}_{s}|_{D}
may have zero-dimensional associated points. In this case the hypotheses of Lemma
3.6
are not fulfilled anymore for
\mathcal{E}|_{S\times D}
, and so we may lose the flatness if we restrict again our family. Nevertheless, we next show that in some cases there exists an open locus
U\subset S
, whose complement has codimension
\geq 2
in
S
, over which the flatness is preserved.

In what follows, we assume that
S
is endowed with the action of a connected algebraic group
G
. The following results are a direct generalization to the case of pure sheaves of
[
7
, Lem. 3.1]
and
[
7
, Cor. 3.2]
. For the reader’s convenience, we include their proofs.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a
G
-equivariant
S
-flat family of sheaves on
X
, and let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
be
G
-stable irreducible closed subschemes of

. For each index

, suppose there exists a point

\mathcal{E}_{s_{i}}

. Then, for

, there exists an open subset

, there exists a
G
-stable closed subscheme

1.

the restriction

,

2.

D

\mathcal{E}_{s}
-regular for all

,

3.

each
\mathcal{E}_{s_{i}}|_{D}
is pure of dimension
d-1
on

,

4.

for each
i
,

has codimension
\geq 2
in
S_{i}
.

Proof.

Since the property of being a pure sheaf is open, there exists a largest
G
-stable open subset
V\subset S
, containing all the
s_{i}
, such that
\mathcal{E}
has pure fibers of dimension
d
over
V
. By Lemma
3.6
, for
a\gg 0
there exists an open subset

. Moreover, by shrinking

Next pick an index

. If

Consider the open subset

of

. Then, for each

\displaystyle T=S\setminus(V\cup\bigcup_{S_{i}\cap V\neq\emptyset}N(s^{\prime}_{i},D))

∎

.

Let

,

for each

.
∎

.

.

4
Semiampleness of determinant line bundles

In this section, we introduce a class of determinant line bundles which correspond functorially to any
S
-flat family of slope-semistable sheaves and then show their semiampleness. We will work under the following set-up:

Set-up
.

Fix a class
c\in K(X)_{\text{num}}
of dimension
d>0
and multiplicity
r
. Let
G
be a connected algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
acting on a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
S
of finite type and
\mathcal{E}
a
G
-equivariant
S
-flat family of sheaves of class

on
X
.

Consider the map
\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}:K(X)\to\Pic^{G}(S)
given by the composition:

\displaystyle K(X)\xrightarrow{p_{2}^{*}}K^{0}(S\times X)\xrightarrow{\cdot[\mathcal{E}]}K^{0}(S\times X)\xrightarrow{{p_{1}}_{!}}K^{0}(S)\xrightarrow{\det}\Pic^{G}(S).

Note that if

is a class satisfying

, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathcal{E}\otimes p_{1}^{*}(L)}(u)\cong\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}(u)^{\rk(L)}\otimes L^{\chi(c\cdot u)}\cong\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}(u).

for any line bundle
L
on

. Also, one can easily see that

behaves well under base-change. We refer the reader to
[
19
]
or
[
18
]
for a detailed account of the properties of

.

4.1
Grothendieck classes of sheaves

Pick an index
1\leq j\leq d-1
and choose
d-j

D_{1},\ldots,D_{d-j}\in\Pi_{a}
such that

. For

\displaystyle w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\coloneqq\chi(c(m^{\prime})\cdot h^{d-j})[\mathcal{O}_{X}(l^{\prime})]-\chi(c(l^{\prime})\cdot h^{d-j})[\mathcal{O}_{X}(m^{\prime})],

. The restriction of

\displaystyle w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|_{X^{(d-j)}}=\chi(c|_{X^{(d-j)}}(m^{\prime}))[\mathcal{O}_{X^{(d-j)}}(l^{\prime})]-\chi(c|_{X^{(d-j)}}(l^{\prime}))[\mathcal{O}_{X^{(d-j)}}(m^{\prime})]

in

Remark 4.1
.

1.

If

, then we recover the classes defined in the torsion-free case (up to a multiplication by

) by Huybrechts–Lehn
[

2.

Clearly

of Gieseker-semistable sheaves of class on

below. Before we state the result, some preparation is in place.

Denote by

the Hilbert polynomial of

\displaystyle V^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}^{\oplus P^{\prime}(m^{\prime})},\quad\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\coloneqq V^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{(d-j)}}(-m^{\prime}).

Consider the Quot scheme

, endowed with the natural action of

. For

, Grothendieck
[

]
showed that there is a (

-equivariant) closed immersion

\displaystyle\varphi_{l^{\prime}}:\Quot_{X^{(d-j)}}\to\Grass_{l^{\prime}}\coloneqq\Grass(H^{0}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(l^{\prime})),P^{\prime}(l^{\prime}))

that associates to a quotient
[q:\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\to F]
the corresponding quotient space

. Denote by

the pullback via

of the canonical line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\Grass_{l^{\prime}}}(1)
on
\Grass_{l^{\prime}}
given by the Plücker embedding. Let

be the open subscheme consisting of quotients
[q:\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\to F]
such that

for all

,

2.

the induced map

is an isomorphism.

Clearly

is invariant under the
G^{\prime}
-action. This induces also a

-linearization of

over

, cf.
[
11
, p. 101]
.

We are now ready to prove the announced lemma, which is a generalization of
[
19
, Prop. 1.3]
and relates the

-equivariant line bundles

and
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(w_{l,m}|_{X^{(d-j)}})
over

. Here and in what follows,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
denotes the universal quotient over
\Quot_{X^{(d-j)}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\chi(c|_{X^{(d-j)}}(m^{\prime}))H_{l^{\prime}}=\lambda_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|_{X^{(d-j)}})

in
\Pic^{G^{\prime}}(Q)
with additive notation.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Prop. 2.2.5]
,
H_{l^{\prime}}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle H_{l^{\prime}}=\lambda_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}([\mathcal{O}_{X^{(d-j)}}(l^{\prime})]).

On the other side, we have
{R^{i}p_{1}}_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(m^{\prime}))=0
for all
i>0
and
{p_{1}}_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(m^{\prime}))\cong V^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q}
over

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}([\mathcal{O}_{X^{(d-j)}}(m^{\prime})])\cong\det({p_1}_*(\sheaf{F}'(m')))\cong\det(V')\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q}

is trivial in
\Pic^{G^{\prime}}(Q)
. We obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|_{X^{(d-j)}})

\displaystyle=\chi(c|_{X^{(d-j)}}(m^{\prime}))\lambda_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}([\mathcal{O}_{X^{(d-j)}}(l^{\prime})])-\chi(c|_{X^{(d-j)}}(l^{\prime}))\lambda_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}([\mathcal{O}_{X^{(d-j)}}(m^{\prime})])

\displaystyle=\chi(c|_{X^{(d-j)}}(m^{\prime}))H_{l^{\prime}}

in

(with additive notation).
∎

The lemma below will be used implicitly in the next section to assume that the morphism
\varphi_{l^{\prime}}
embeds the Quot scheme into the Grassmannian for any complete intersection

.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
l^{\prime}\gg 0
, the morphism
\varphi_{l^{\prime}}
is a closed immersion for every smooth complete intersection
X^{(d-j)}\subset X
of

divisors in
\Pi_{a}
.

Proof.

Any such complete intersection

may be seen as a closed point in the Hilbert scheme

, where

is the Hilbert polynomial of

. Indeed,

does not depend on

, as one easily checks by using the Koszul resolution of

to compute its Hilbert polynomial. Denote by

the universal closed subscheme. Then

is a closed point of the relative Quot scheme

\displaystyle\Quot_{Z}(V^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}(-m^{\prime}),P^{\prime})\to\Hilb_{X}(P).

Since this construction is functorial, it is enough to choose
l^{\prime}
sufficiently large such that

\displaystyle\varphi:\Quot_{Z}(V^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}(-m^{\prime}),P^{\prime})\to\Grass(V^{\prime}\otimes H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(l^{\prime}-m^{\prime})),P^{\prime}(l^{\prime})),

5.1

Lemma 5.2

For any short exact sequence of

\displaystyle 0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0,

Proof.

The proof is completely analogue to the one in the non-equivariant case, we omit it.

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If

-stable closed subscheme of

Proof.

Let

Theorem 5.2
.

Any
G
-equivariant sheaf on

.

Proof.

We argue by noetherian induction as follows. Let

-stable closed subscheme of

and

still have distinct support, so they are not
S
-equivalent. By Theorem
3.1
, for
a\gg 0
we may assume that
E_{1}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
are
\hat{\mu}
-semistable. Then there exists a
G^{\prime}
-invariant section
\sigma\in H^{0}(\Quot_{X^{(d-1)}},H_{l^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}})^{G^{\prime}}
that separates
E_{1}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
. As in Section 4.2, this yields a section
\Gamma_{\mathcal{F}}(\sigma)
that separates
[E_{1}]
and
[E_{2}]
, which is exactly what we need.

Now, suppose that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have same support. Let
D\in\Pi_{a}
be a divisor avoiding the associated points of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, and consider the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to E_{2}^{H}(-D)\to E_{2}^{H}\to E_{2}^{H}|_{D}\to 0

Applying the functor
\Hom(E_{1}^{H},-)
we obtain a long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\Hom(E_{1}^{H},E_{2}^{H})\to\Hom(E_{1}^{H}|_{D},E_{2}^{H}|_{D})\to\Ext^{1}(E_{1}^{H},E_{2}^{H}(-D))\to\ldots,

where the first morphism is injective since the sheaves are pure. By using Serre duality and the local-to-global Ext spectral sequence, one obtains

\displaystyle\Ext^{1}(E_{1}^{H},E_{2}^{H}(-D))\cong H^{0}(X,\Exts^{n-1}(E_{1}^{H},E_{2}^{H})\otimes\omega_{X}(D))

for
a\gg 0
. As
E_{2}^{H}
is pure and
S_{2}
, we have
\Exts^{n-1}(E_{1}^{H},E_{2}^{H})=0
, and so

\displaystyle\Hom(E_{1}^{H},E_{2}^{H})\to\Hom(E_{1}^{H}|_{D},E_{2}^{H}|_{D})

is an isomorphism. One can choose a general
D\in\Pi_{a}
such that
E_{1}^{H}|_{D}
and
E_{2}^{H}|_{D}
remain reflexive, cf.
[
11
, Cor. 1.1.14]
. Thus, repeating the argument above, we obtain an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Hom(E_{1}^{H},E_{2}^{H})\to\Hom(E_{1}^{H}|_{X^{(d-1)}},E_{2}^{H}|_{X^{(d-1)}})

for a general smooth complete intersection
X^{(d-1)}
. By assumption
E_{1}^{H}\ncong E_{2}^{H}
, and so
E_{1}^{H}|_{X^{(d-1)}}\ncong E_{2}^{H}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
. For
a\gg 0
, one can choose
X^{(d-1)}
such that

1.

the restricted sheaves
E_{1}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
remain polystable,

2.

E_{1}|_{X^{(d-1)}}\cong E_{1}^{H}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-1)}}\cong E_{2}^{H}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
.

Indeed, the first condition is a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, and the second one is satisfied by a sufficiently general complete intersection. Therefore, for such
X^{(d-1)}
,
E_{1}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-1)}}
are not
S
-equivalent. Now the result follows as in the first part of the proof.
∎

By using the result above one can show that
M^{\mu ss}
provides a compactification of the moduli space of
\hat{\mu}
-stable reflexive sheaves of class
c
on
X
. To be precise, let
M^{Gss}
be the Simpson moduli space of semistable sheaves of class
c
on
X
, and denote by
M_{refl}^{s}\subset M^{Gss}
the open locus of
\hat{\mu}
-stable reflexive sheaves. By the universal property of
M^{Gss}
, there is a natural morphism
\rho:M_{refl}^{s}\to M^{\mu ss}
. We show below that
\rho
is an open embedding.

Proposition 6.1
.

The natural morphism
\rho:M_{refl}^{s}\to M^{\mu ss}
is an open immersion.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.2
, we know that
\rho
is injective. It remains to show that
\rho
is also unramified at each point of
M_{refl}^{s}
. So choose a closed point
[E]\in M_{refl}^{s}
corresponding to a
\hat{\mu}
-stable reflexive sheaf
E
. As shown in Lemma
6.2
, for general
D\in\Pi_{a}
, we have
\Ext^{1}(E,E(-D))=0
and an injection

\displaystyle\Ext^{1}(E,E)\to\Ext^{1}(E|_{D},E|_{D}).

Thus, for a general complete intersection
X^{(d-1)}\subset X
of
d-1
divisors in
\Pi_{a}
, we obtain an injection

\displaystyle\Ext^{1}(E,E)\to\Ext^{1}(E|_{X^{(d-1)}},E|_{X^{(d-1)}}).

(6.1)

Now, suppose that the restriction
E|_{X^{(d-1)}}
remains
\hat{\mu}
-stable, which holds for a general divisor of large degree by Theorem
3.1
. Then we have a rational morphism
\theta:M_{refl}^{s}\dashrightarrow M_{X^{(d-1)}}
, where
M_{X^{(d-1)}}
denotes the Simpson moduli space of semistable sheaves on
{X^{(d-1)}}
. By the injectivity of (
6.1
), it follows that
\theta
is an open immersion locally around
[E]\in M_{refl}^{s}
. By using the following commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{refl}^{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\mu ss}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{X^{(d-1)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}

one deduces that
\rho
is also an open immersion locally around
[E]\in M_{refl}^{s}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two polystable quotient sheaves of
R^{ss}
having the same reflexive hull
F
such that
C_{d-2}(E_{1})\neq C_{d-2}(E_{2})
. Then
\varphi
separates
[E_{1}]
and
[E_{2}]
in
M^{\mu ss}
.

Proof.

Choose
d-1
divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{d-1}\in\Pi_{a}
such that
X^{(d-1)}=D_{1}\cap\ldots\cap D_{d-1}
and
X^{(d-2)}\coloneqq D_{1}\cap\ldots\cap D_{d-2}
are complete intersections in
X
. If these divisors are chosen general enough, then

1.

E_{1}|_{X^{(d-2)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-2)}}
remain polystable and have the same reflexive hull
F|_{X^{(d-2)}}
,

2.

C_{0}(E_{1}|_{X^{(d-2)}})\neq C_{0}(E_{1}|_{X^{(d-2)}})
.

The first condition is satisfied by Theorem
3.1
and
[
11
, Cor. 1.1.14]
, and the second one clearly holds for a sufficiently general complete intersection. Moreover, by Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we have the following
G
-isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\cong\lambda_{\mathcal{F}|_{R^{ss}\times X^{(d-2)}}}(w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|_{X^{(d-2)}}\cdot h|_{X^{(d-2)}})\cong\lambda_{\mathcal{F}|_{R^{ss}\times X^{(d-1)}}}(w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|_{X^{(d-1)}})

over a
G
-stable open subset
V\subset R^{ss}
containing
E_{1}|_{X^{(d-2)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-2)}}
, whose complement has codimension
\geq 2
in
R^{ss}
. Thus, it is enough to find sections of
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}|_{R^{ss}\times X^{(d-2)}}}(w_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|_{X^{(d-2)}}\cdot h|_{X^{(d-2)}})
over
V
that separate
E_{1}|_{X^{(d-2)}}
and
E_{2}|_{X^{(d-2)}}
, since then we can extend them over the whole weakly normal variety
R^{ss}
, exactly as we did in the proof of the Semiampleness Theorem
4.1
. In conclusion, we can restrict ourselves to the case
d=2
, which we treat in detail below.

In the two-dimensional case, the proof follows closely the one given by Jun Li in
[
20
, Lem. 3.6]
. We may assume that there is a point
y_{0}\in\Supp(F)
such that
\length_{y_{0}}(C_{0}(E_{1}))>\length_{y_{0}}(C_{0}(E_{2}))\geq 0
. Choose
D_{0}\in\Pi_{a}
a smooth divisor passing through
y_{0}
such that
E_{1}|_{D_{0}}
and
E_{2}|_{D_{0}}
are reflexive at
D_{0}\setminus\{y_{0}\}
, and
F|_{D_{0}}
is
\hat{\mu}
-semistable. Now take another smooth divisor
D_{1}\in\Pi_{a}
such that
D_{0}\cap D_{1}
is a smooth complete intersection that avoids
y_{0}
. By blowing up
X
along
D_{0}\cap D_{1}
we get a smooth projective scheme
\widetilde{X}
with a natural map
q:\widetilde{X}\to X
. Moreover, the linear system
D_{t}
generated by
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
extends to a base-point-free pencil
\widetilde{D}_{t}
on
\widetilde{X}
, which induces a morphism
p:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Note that
\widetilde{D}_{t}
is canonically isomorphic with
D_{t}
via
q
for all
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For this reason, in what follows we freely identify
q^{*}E_{1}|_{\widetilde{D}_{t}}
,
q^{*}E_{2}|_{\widetilde{D}_{t}}
and
q^{*}F|_{\widetilde{D}_{t}}
with
E_{1}|_{D_{t}}
,
E_{2}|_{D_{t}}
and
F|_{D_{t}}
respectively.

By the choice of the linear system, locally around
0\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we can find an open neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that any fiber
\widetilde{D}_{t}
is smooth for
t\in U
, and
E_{1}|_{D_{t}}
and
E_{2}|_{D_{t}}
are
\hat{\mu}
-semistable reflexive sheaves for
0\neq t\in U
. We denote by
p_{U}:\widetilde{X}_{U}\to U
the pullback of
p
along the inclusion
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

For all
t\in U
, the
F|_{D_{t}}
have the same Hilbert polynomial, say
\widetilde{P}
. According to
[
11
, Thm. 4.3.7]
, there exists a relative moduli space
M_{\widetilde{X}_{U}/U}(\widetilde{P})\to U
endowed with an ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{M_{\widetilde{X}_{U}/U}}(1)
, such that each fiber
M_{D_{t}}
is the Simpson moduli space of semistable sheaves on
D_{t}
with Hilbert polynomial
\widetilde{P}
. Since
F|_{D_{0}}
is
\hat{\mu}
-semistable, there is a section
v_{0}\in H^{0}(M_{D_{t}},\mathcal{O}_{M_{D_{t}}}(k))
for
k\gg 0
, nonvanishing at
F|_{D_{0}}
. By using the base change theorem and shrinking
U
if necessary, we can extend
v_{0}
to a section
v\in H^{0}(M_{\widetilde{X}_{U}/U},\mathcal{O}_{M_{\widetilde{X}_{U}/U}}(k))
.

As in Section 4.2, for each
t\in U
, there exists a rational morphism
\psi_{t}:R^{ss}\dashrightarrow M_{D_{t}}
corresponding to the universal family
\mathcal{F}
on
R^{ss}\times X
. Moreover, we can pullback the restricted section
v|_{M_{D_{t}}}
via
\psi_{t}
to obtain a canonical section
\widetilde{v}_{t}\in H^{0}(R^{ss},\mathcal{L}^{\nu})^{G}
for
\nu\gg 0
. Since this construction is canonical, we can view the
\widetilde{v}_{t}
as restrictions of a section
\widetilde{v}\in H^{0}(R^{ss}\times U,p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\nu})^{G}
, where
p_{1}:R^{ss}\times U\to R^{ss}
is the first projection.

Recall that
\mathcal{L}=\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}(u)
on
R^{ss}
, for an appropriate Grothendieck class
u\in K(X)
. Denote by
\widetilde{u}
the pullback of the class
u
to
K(\widetilde{X})
and by
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq(1_{R^{ss}}\times q)^{*}\mathcal{F}
. Consider the determinant line bundle

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{R^{ss}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}\coloneqq\det((1_{\Rss} \times p)_{!}( \widetilde{\sheaf{F}}\otimes p_2^*\widetilde{u}))

on
R^{ss}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For
i=1,2
, the fiber of
\mathcal{L}_{R^{ss}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}
over
[E_{i}]\in R^{ss}
gives a line bundle

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{i,\mathbb{P}^{1}}\coloneqq\det(p_{!}(q^*E_i \otimes p_2^*\widetilde{u}))

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. By using the base change theorem, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{R^{ss}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong p_{1}^{*}A\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\alpha),

where
A
is a line bundle
R^{ss}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\alpha)
a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Thus, we have canonical isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{i,\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\alpha).

On the other hand, consider the line bundle
p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{L}
on
R^{ss}\times U
. As above, the fiber of
p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{L}
over
[E_{i}]\in R^{ss}
gives a trivial line bundle
L_{i}
on
U
. Thus, if we fix a trivialization of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\alpha)
over
U
, then we have the following canonical isomorphisms

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}:\mathcal{L}_{i,\mathbb{P}^{1}}|_{U}\to L_{i}.

For
i=1,2
, we have the following short exact sequences on
\widetilde{X}_{U}
:

\displaystyle 0\to q^{*}E_{i}\to q^{*}F\to T_{i}\to 0,

where
q:\widetilde{X}_{U}\to X
is the natural map, and
T_{i}
are torsion sheaves on
\widetilde{X}_{U}
. This gives us isomorphisms

\displaystyle\phi_{i}:\det(p_{!}(q^*F \otimes p_2^*\widetilde{u}))\otimes\det(p_{!}(T_i \otimes p_2^*\widetilde{u}))^{-1}\to\mathcal{L}_{i,\mathbb{P}^{1}}.

(6.2)

over
U
. Since
E_{1}|_{D_{t}}\cong E_{2}|_{D_{t}}\cong F|_{D_{t}}
for
0\neq t\in U
,
T_{i}
is supported on
D_{0}
and
\det(p_{!}(T_i \otimes p_2^*\widetilde{u}))^{-1}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(l_{i}0)|_{U}
, where
l_{i}=\length(T_{i}|_{D_{0}})=\length_{y_{0}}(C_{0}(E_{i}))
. Note that
l_{1}>l_{2}
by assumption.

Denote by
\widetilde{v}^{i}\in H^{0}(U,L_{i}^{\nu})
the restriction of
\widetilde{v}
to
[E_{i}]\times U
. Since
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are canonical, we get that
\phi_{1}^{*}(\gamma_{1}^{*}(\widetilde{v}^{1}))
and
\phi_{2}^{*}(\gamma_{2}^{*}(\widetilde{v}^{2}))
agree over
U\setminus\{0\}
as sections of the line bundle
\det(p_{!}(q^*F \otimes p_2^*\widetilde{u}))
. Note that the extension of
\phi_{1}^{*}(\gamma_{1}^{*}(\widetilde{v}^{1}))
over
U
cannot be trivial, since we assumed that
v_{0}(F|_{D_{0}})\neq 0
. It follows by (
6.2
) that
\widetilde{v}^{1}
and
\widetilde{v}^{2}
have different vanishing order at
0\in U
. Thus, for some
t_{1},t_{2}\in U
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{v}^{1}(t_{1})}{\widetilde{v}^{2}(t_{1})}\neq\frac{\widetilde{v}^{1}(t_{2})}{\widetilde{v}^{2}(t_{2})},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{v}_{t_{1}}([E_{1}])}{\widetilde{v}_{t_{2}}([E_{1}])}\neq\frac{\widetilde{v}_{t_{1}}([E_{2}])}{\widetilde{v}_{t_{2}}([E_{2}])}.

Taking
\lambda=\widetilde{v}_{t_{1}}([E_{1}])/\widetilde{v}_{t_{2}}([E_{1}])
, then
\sigma=\widetilde{v}_{t_{1}}-\lambda\widetilde{v}_{t_{2}}
is a
G
-invariant section of
\mathcal{L}^{\nu}
such that
\sigma([E_{1}])=0
and
\sigma([E_{2}])\neq 0
. Therefore
\varphi
separates
[E_{1}]
and
[E_{2}]
in
M^{\mu ss}
.
∎

It remains to treat the case when two polystable quotiens

and

of

have the same reflexive hull

and

. Here one can prove the following:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
S
be connected (weakly normal)
\mathbb{C}
-scheme of finite type and
\mathcal{E}
an
S
-flat family of sheaves of class
c
on
X
such that each geometric fiber satisfies
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{H}=F
and
C_{d-2}(\mathcal{E}_{s})=C
, where
F
is a fixed reflexive hull and
C
a fixed
(d-2)
-dimensional cycle on

. Then the classifying morphism
\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}:S\to M^{\mu ss}
, given by the Main Theorem, is constant.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
7
, Lem. 5.7]
and we omit it.
∎

We summarize the results of this section in the following corollary:

Corollary 6.1
.

If
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are two polystable quotient sheaves of
R^{ss}
such that either

1.

E_{1}^{H}\ncong E_{2}^{H}
, or

,

\varphi

and

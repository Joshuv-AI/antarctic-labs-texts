Orbital-Free Quasi-Density Functional Theory
Carlos L. Benavides-Riveros

Abstract
Wigner functions are broadly used to probe non-classical effects in the macroscopic world. Here we develop an orbital-free functional framework to compute the 1-body Wigner quasi-probability for both fermionic and bosonic systems. Since the key variable is a quasi-density, this theory is particularly well suited to circumvent the problem of finding the Pauli potential or approximating the kinetic energy in orbital-free density functional theory. As proof of principle, we find that the universal functional for the building block of optical lattices results from a translation, a contraction, and a rotation of the corresponding functional of the 1-body reduced density matrix, indicating a strong connection between these functional theories. Furthermore, we relate the concepts of Wigner negativity and v v -representability , and find a manifold of ground states with negative Wigner functions.



Introduction.—
Detecting and understanding quantum features at the macroscopic level is one of the main theoretical and technological challenges of modern quantum sciences. Nowadays, state-of-the-art experiments can directly observe non-classical behavior (as quantum superposition) in systems with a truly macroscopic number of particles, with as many as
10^{16}
atoms
Friedman et al. 2000
;
Fein et al. 2019
;
Wollack et al. 2022
;
Schrinski et al. 2023
. A powerful theoretical and computational strategy to detect that
quantumness
is by directly measuring the system’s corresponding Wigner function. Although normalized to unity, Wigner functions are quasi-probability distributions that can take negative values, a phenomenon that has no classical counterpart. Hence, negativity in the Wigner functions has been linked to non-classical features of quantum states and is considered a distinctive signature of quantum entanglement
Walschaers et al. 2017
;
Dahl et al. 2006
;
Kenfack and Życzkowski 2004
, contextuality
Spekkens 2008
;
Raussendorf et al. 2017
;
Howard et al. 2014
, quantum computation
Veitch et al. 2012
, quantum steering
Walschaers et al. 2020
;
Walschaers 2022
, or even quantum gravity
Howl et al. 2021
.

Due to the exponentially large Hilbert spaces of quantum many-body systems, finding the corresponding Wigner function is, in general, a computationally prohibitive task. Yet for identical particles it is possible to circumvent the Hilbert space’s exponential growth by means of a universal functional of certain reduced, more manageable, quantities, like, e.g., the density. Based on the important observation that electronic systems are fully determined by the ground-state density
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
, density functional theory (DFT) is a prominent methodology in electronic structure calculations, with applications ranging from quantum chemistry and material science
Jones 2015
;
Teale et al. 2022
to self-driving labs
Huang et al. 2023
. Quite remarkably, orbital-free DFT achieves a computational linear scaling with the system size
Ryczko et al. 2022
. But, unfortunately, from the density alone it is not possible to reconstruct the Wigner function, and therefore standard DFT is, in general, not suitable for describing non-classical features of quantum many-body systems.

A recent phase-space formulation of DFT employs, as the central variable, the 1-particle Wigner quasi-density, which is in a one-to-one correspondence with the respective ground state for interacting many-fermion/boson systems
Blanchard et al. 2012
. Its main feature is that the 1-body Wigner function can be accessed directly, without pre-computing the full wave function. This Wigner quasi-density functional theory (quasi-DFT) is a promising theoretical tool to model many-body problems while accounting for non-classical features, strong interactions, and quantum correlations, with the same computational cost as standard DFT. As we will show below, the theory has also the potential of bypassing well-known problems of orbital-free DFT. To date, however, there are neither orbital-free nor orbital-dependent functionals for quasi-DFT.

Here, we will obtain equations for the fermionic/bosonic 1-particle quasi-density. This is, we will argue, the initial step to developing a full
orbital-free
framework for Wigner quasi-DFT. As one of our main results, we will show that
\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
, the 1-particle Wigner quasi-density, satisfies the following, effective, eigen-equation:

\displaystyle h_{\rm eff}\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star h_{\rm eff}=\mu\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,,

(1)

where
h_{\rm eff}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{p}^{2}+v_{\rm ext}({\mathbf{r}})+v_{\rm eff}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
,
v_{\rm ext}({\mathbf{r}})
is the external potential,
v_{\rm eff}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
is certain effective potential that we introduce below, and the symbol
\star
is the so-called star product of phase-space quantum mechanics.

The letter is organized as follows: First, we review both the orbital-free functional theories and the Wigner formulation of DFT. Second, we derive an Euler-Lagrange equation for the 1-body Wigner quasi-density. Next, we derive an equation using the Moyal product. We then employ the Hubbard model to present for the first time a functional realization of quasi-DFT. We conclude with a summary and discuss some implications of our results. In the Appendixes, we provide additional technical details.

Functional theories.—
The enormous success of DFT in electronic structure calculations is mainly due to the existence of a set of self-consistent 1-particle equations that allow for the computation of the density from 1-particle orbitals
Kohn and Sham 1965
. Although it is much cheaper than wave-function methods, this Kohn-Sham DFT still has an unfavorably computational scaling with the cube of the number of electrons
Engel and Dreizler 2011
. In turn, orbital-free DFT allows a much more favorable, linear scaling with the system size
Teale et al. 2022
;
Ryczko et al. 2022
, but this computational advantage is counterbalanced by the fact that the quantum mechanical kinetic energy functional is unknown, and it is written as a classical, approximate function of the electron density. A parallel intellectual effort is the 1-particle reduced density matrix functional theory (1-RDMFT) that exploits the full 1-particle picture of the many-body problem by seeking a universal functional of the 1-body reduced density matrix (1-RDM),
Gilbert 1975
;
Donnelly and Parr 1978
;
Pernal and Giesbertz 2016
, for fermionic
Schilling 2018
;
Piris 2017
;
Schmidt et al. 2019
, bosonic
Benavides-Riveros et al. 2020
;
Liebert and Schilling 2021
;
Maciazek 2021
;
Liebert and Schilling 2023
, or relativistic
Rodríguez-Mayorga et al. 2022
interacting particles. Similar to DFT, 1-RDMFT is based on a one-to-one correspondence between the ground state and its corresponding 1-RDM. Although this theory is in a better position than DFT to tackle strong correlations
Sharma et al. 2013
, its broad use has been hampered by the absence of Kohn-Sham-like equations for the natural orbitals (i.e., the eigenvectors of the 1-RDM)
Pernal and Giesbertz 2016
;
Piris and Pernal 2017
. It, therefore, comes as no surprise that fermionic 1-RDMFT is computationally much more demanding than DFT
Gebauer et al. 2016
. Unfortunately, there are quite a few orbital-free formulations of 1-RDMFT (the most notable being the exchange part of the Hartree-Fock functional). The development of an orbital-free perspective of 1-RDMFT could boost its broad applicability.

Wigner quasi-DFT.—
A generalization of the Hohenberg-Kohn
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
and Gilbert
Gilbert 1975
theorems to Hamiltonians of the form
H=h+V
, with a
fixed
two-particle interaction
V
, proves the existence of a universal Wigner functional
\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]
of the 1-body quasi-density
\omega

Blanchard et al. 2012
. Indeed, for any choice of the 1-particle phase-space Hamiltonian
h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{p}^{2}+v_{\rm ext}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
the energy functional:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[\omega]\equiv\int h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})d\Omega+\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]\geq E_{\rm gs}\,,

(3)

is bounded from below by the exact ground-state energy. The equality in Eq. (
3
) holds exactly when
\mathcal{E}[\omega]
is evaluated using the ground-state 1-body quasi-density
\omega_{\rm gs}
. As in standard DFT, the functional
\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]
is completely independent of any external (phase-space) potential
v({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
. As in 1-RDMFT, it is also completely independent of the kinetic energy and depends only on the fixed two-particle interaction
V
. The (universal) functional
\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]
obeys a constrained-search formulation, by considering only many-body wave-functions that integrate to the same
\omega
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]=\min_{\Psi\rightarrow\omega}\mbox{$\langle\Psi|$}V\mbox{$|\Psi\rangle$}\,.

(4)

While the functional is unknown, it is known that it has some better scaling properties than the functionals in DFT. For instance, by defining,
\omega_{\lambda}=\omega(\lambda{\mathbf{r}},\lambda^{-1}\mathbf{p})
, one can show that
\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega_{\lambda}]=\lambda\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]

Blanchard et al. 2012
, a fact that lies in the exact knowledge of the kinetic energy functional.

Representability condition of
\omega
.—
In a quite natural way, 1-body quasi-densities inherit the representability conditions of the 1-RDM,
\gamma
. Due to unitary invariance, those can be expressed as conditions on the eigenvalues of
\gamma

Coleman 1963
. Therefore, it is convenient to use the spectral representation of
\omega
(i.e.,
\omega=\sum_{i}n_{i}f_{i}
) to find its representability conditions. In general,
n_{i}\geq 0
. In addition, in the case of fermions:

\displaystyle\omega\star\omega\leq\omega\,,

(5)

which is just a consequence of the Pauli exclusion principle
4
4

4

There are in the literature other representability conditions for the spin coordinates
Benavides-Riveros and Gracia-Bondía 2013
.

Equation for the 1-body quasi-density.—
We now exhibit an exact equation for the phase-space 1-body quasi-density. Let
\mathcal{E}[\omega]
be the energy functional of the Wigner function (
3
), subject to the constraint
\int d\Omega\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=N
. The
N
-particle phase-space density which minimizes such a functional is found by applying a functional derivative of the Lagrangian
\mathcal{E}[\omega]-\mu N
with respect to
\omega
, yielding the Euler-Lagrange equation of Wigner quasi-DFT:

\displaystyle h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}=\mu\,.

(6)

There is an important consequence of this result. As is well known, one of the central problems in orbital-free DFT is approximating the kinetic energy functional in terms of the density
Chai and Weeks 2007
;
Iyengar et al. 2001
or, alternatively, the Pauli potential
Finzel 2016
;
Kraisler and Schild 2020
. It is, indeed, particularly crucial that the Pauli principle be captured precisely in the kinetic energy. As we can see in Eq. (
6
), this important problem is completely absent in the phase-space formalism. First, the kinetic energy and the external potential are exact, rather simple phase-space functions, and no approximation is needed. Second, the representability condition of the Wigner function (
5
) guarantees that the Pauli principle is fulfilled. As a consequence, our orbital-free quasi-DFT needs only to approximate the universal functional
\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]
.

\star
-eigenequation for
\omega
.—
Inspired by the work of Levy, Perdew and Sahni
Levy et al. 1984
we exhibit now an exact
\star
-eigenequation for the 1-particle quasi-density. As explained in Appendix
C
, by computing the directional functional derivative of
\mathcal{E}[\omega]
at the point
\omega
in the direction of
\omega
one can show that
\omega_{\rm gs}
fulfills the following equation:

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm gs}\star h_{\rm eff}=h_{\rm eff}\star\omega_{\rm gs}=\mu\,\omega_{\rm gs}\,,

(7)

where
h_{\rm eff}=h+\delta\mathcal{F}_{V}[\omega]/\delta\omega|_{\omega=\omega_{\rm gs}}
. The simplicity of this formula can be compared with the one from orbital-free DFT for
\sqrt{n({\mathbf{r}})}

March 1986
. Noticeably, the formula (
7
) allows for a Wigner-Moyal expansion of the equation for the quasi-density:

\sum_{n}\frac{i^{n}\hbar^{n}}{2^{n}n!}h_{\rm eff}(\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{r}\vec{\partial}_{p}-\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{p}\vec{\partial}_{r})^{n}\omega=\mu\omega
.

Conclusion.—
Unveiling the role of quantum effects at the classical level is a crucial problem for developing quantum technologies. The Wigner quasi-probability is usually employed as a probe of such quantumness. This letter showed that
\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
, the (fermionic or bosonic) 1-body Wigner quasi-density, can be inserted into a functional-theoretical framework in an orbital-free manner. By providing an Euler-Lagrange equation and a Wigner-Moyal eigen-equation, we showed that
\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
can be computed as a stationary point without referring to orbital equations, circumventing some known problems of orbital-free DFT (e.g., approximating the kinetic energy functional or finding the Pauli potential). In this framework, the concepts of Wigner negativity and
v
-representability can be related. We would like to emphasize that one of the most important aspects of our results is that the
\star
-product gives a very rich structure for extracting the corresponding 1-particle Wigner function. In that sense, quasi-DFT is a functional theory that can connect directly with DFT and with semiclassical expansions of the many-body problem. There are several potential research directions from our results: First, one could develop machine learning quasi-DFT functionals which is now current practice for standard DFT
Grisafi et al. 2022
;
Kirkpatrick et al. 2021
;
Ma et al. 2022
;
Gedeon et al. 2021
. Second, since Wigner negativities carry important quantum information it will be interesting to see what information they can unveil for electronic correlations
Izsák et al. 2023
;
Zhang and Truhlar 2021
or fermionic entanglement
Ptaszyński and Esposito 2023
. Finally, it could be quite promising to tackle —within this orbital-free framework— quantum excitations in the same spirit of state-average calculations
Benavides-Riveros et al. 2022
or the recently formulated
\mathbf{w}
-1-RDMFT
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
.

Appendix A
Discrete Wigner formalism for the Hubbard model

Here we apply the discrete Wigner formalism to the Hubbard model of
L
sites. This is defined in the
L
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{L}
whose position basis is
\mathcal{S}=\{\mbox{$|1\rangle$},...,\mbox{$|L\rangle$}\}
. Another orthonormal basis for the same Hilbert space is
\{\mbox{$|\phi_{0}\rangle$},...,\mbox{$|\phi_{L-1}\rangle$}\}
, defined by the Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi_{m}\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum^{L}_{n=1}e^{in\phi_{m}}\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\,,

(12)

with
\phi_{m}=\frac{2\pi}{L}m
. The set of pairs
\{n,\phi_{m}\}_{n,m}
constitutes a
L\times L
grid. This is the phase space
\Gamma^{L}
associated with the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{L}

Wootters 1987
.

The operators
\hat{n}=\sum_{n}n\mbox{$|n\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle n|$}
and
\hat{\phi}=\sum_{m}\phi_{m}\mbox{$|\phi_{m}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\phi_{m}|$}
can be used to construct the following unitary operators:

\displaystyle\hat{V}=\exp\left(i\frac{2\pi}{L}\hat{n}\right)\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\hat{U}=\exp(i\hat{\phi})\,,

(13)

which satisfy the Weyl relation
Przanowski and Tosiek 2017
:

\displaystyle\hat{D}(k,l)\equiv\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi kl}{L}\right)\hat{U}^{k}\hat{V}^{l}=\exp\left(i\frac{\pi kl}{L}\right)\hat{U}^{l}\hat{V}^{k}\,,\qquad k,l\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(14)

With this operator, the authors of Ref.
Przanowski and Tosiek 2017
define the phase-space point operator:

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}_{\kappa}(n,\phi_{m})=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k,l}\kappa(k,l)\hat{D}(k,l)\exp\left[-i\left(k\phi_{m}+\frac{2\pi}{L}ln\right)\right]\,,

(15)

with a kernel
\kappa(k,l)
, whose properties are determined by the properties of
\hat{\Omega}_{\kappa}
. In particular, the operator’s hermiticity condition:
\hat{\Omega}_{\kappa}(n,\phi_{m})=\hat{\Omega}^{\dagger}_{\kappa}(n,\phi_{m})
,
\forall(n,\phi_{m})\in\Gamma^{L}
, which is needed to map phase-space functions to hermitian operators, results in the condition
\kappa^{*}(k,l)=(-1)^{L+k+l}\kappa(L-k,L-l)
. For odd
L=2N+1
, for instance, a kernel can be chosen to be
Przanowski and Tosiek 2017
:
\kappa(k,l)=\cos(\pi kl/L)
.

The map between
f(n,\phi_{m})
, a real function in
\Gamma^{L}
, and
\hat{f}
, an operator in
\mathcal{H}^{L}
, is realized by means of the following relation:

\displaystyle\hat{f}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{m,n}f(n,\phi_{m})\hat{\Omega}_{\kappa}(n,\phi_{m})\,.

(16)

Eq. (
16
) can now be used to find the Wigner quasi-distribution. Since the average value of the
observable represented by the operator
\hat{f}
in a state defined by the density operator
\hat{\gamma}
reads

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}[\hat{\gamma}\hat{f}]=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{m,n}f(n,\phi_{m})\mbox{Tr}\left[\hat{\gamma}\hat{\Omega}_{\kappa}(n,\phi_{m})\right]\,.

(17)

a natural definition for the Wigner quasi-probability (for the kernel
\kappa
) arises:

\omega(n,\phi_{m})=\mbox{Tr}\left[\hat{\gamma}\hat{\Omega}_{\kappa}(n,\phi_{m})\right]
. From this definition, one can write:

\displaystyle\omega(n,\phi_{m})=\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}(n,\phi_{m};n^{\prime},m^{\prime})\mbox{$\langle n^{\prime}|$}\hat{\gamma}\mbox{$|m^{\prime}\rangle$}\,,

(18)

where

\mathcal{D}(n,\phi_{m};n^{\prime},m^{\prime})=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k,l,s}\kappa(k,l)\exp\left[i\left(\frac{\pi kl}{L}+k\phi_{s}+n^{\prime}\phi_{s+l}-m^{\prime}\phi_{s}-k\phi_{m}-\frac{2\pi}{L}ln\right)\right]
.
If one vectorizes both
\omega
and
\gamma
, to wit,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\left|{\omega}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega(1,\phi_{0})\\
\omega(1,\phi_{1})\\
\omega(1,\phi_{2})\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\mathinner{\left|{\gamma}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}=\begin{pmatrix}\mbox{$\langle 1|$}\gamma\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\\
\mbox{$\langle 1|$}\gamma\mbox{$|2\rangle$}\\
\mbox{$\langle 1|$}\gamma\mbox{$|3\rangle$}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}\,,

(19)

one can formally write (
18
) as
\mathinner{\left|{\omega}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}=\hat{\mathcal{D}}\mathinner{\left|{\gamma}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}
.

Appendix B
The Generalized Bose-Hubbard dimer

In this section, we focus on the generalized Bose-Hubbard dimer, whose Hamiltonian reads

H=-t(b_{l}^{\dagger}b_{r}+b_{r}^{\dagger}b_{l})+\!\sum_{j=l/r}\!v_{j}\hat{n}_{j}+V\,,

(20)

with

V=u_{1}\left[\hat{n}_{l}(\hat{n}_{l}-1)+\hat{n}_{r}(\hat{n}_{r}-1)\right]+u_{2}\hat{n}_{l}\hat{n}_{r}+u_{3}\left[(b^{\dagger}_{l})^{2}(b_{r})^{2}+(b^{\dagger}_{r})^{2}(b_{l})^{2}\right]\,.

(21)

The operators
b^{\dagger}_{j}
and
b_{j}
create and annihilate a particle on the sites
j=l/r
, and
\hat{n}_{j}
is the corresponding particle-number operator. Any
N
-body ground state of the Hamiltonian (
20
) can be expressed as a linear combination of the configuration states
|n,N-n\rangle
. Assuming real wave functions, we represent the 1-RDM
\gamma\equiv\mbox{Tr}_{N-1}[\Gamma]
of any pure or ensemble state with respect to the lattice site states
\mbox{$|l\rangle$},\mbox{$|r\rangle$}
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}\equiv\frac{1}{N}\mbox{$\langle\Psi|$}b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{j}\mbox{$|\Psi\rangle$}\,,\qquad i,j=l,r\,.

(22)

Since
\gamma_{ll}+\gamma_{rr}=1
(by normalization) and

\gamma_{lr}=\gamma_{rl}
the 1RDM is fully determined by two free parameters. We represent the 1-RDM as:

\displaystyle\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{ll}&\gamma_{lr}\\
\gamma_{lr}&1-\gamma_{ll}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(23)

The only two degrees of freedom of this matrix can be represented in a vector form:

\mathinner{\left|{\gamma}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{ll}\\
\gamma_{lr}\end{pmatrix}
.

B.1
1-body Wigner function

Our goal is to find the Wigner function associated with this matrix in the grid
\{(l,0),(l,1),(r,0),(r,1)\}
, with the momentum basis:
\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mbox{$|L\rangle$}+\mbox{$|R\rangle$})
and
\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mbox{$|L\rangle$}-\mbox{$|R\rangle$})
. This grid can be seen as a two-dimensional vector space over a finite field, in which the Wigner function is defined:

\begin{array}[]{c@{}c@{}c}\begin{matrix}&&|&&\\
&\omega(l,1)&|&\omega(r,1)&\\
&&|&&\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}&&|&&\\
&\omega(l,0)&|&\omega(r,0)&\\
&&|&&\end{matrix}\end{array}

Notice that
\gamma
can be written as

\displaystyle\gamma=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\vec{\gamma}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)\,,

(24)

where
\vec{\gamma}=(\gamma_{lr},0,\gamma_{ll}-\tfrac{1}{2})

and
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
are the Pauli matrices. For a qubit a phase-space point operator is known to be
Przanowski and Tosiek 2017
:

\displaystyle\Omega(n,\phi_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+(-1)^{m}(\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$})-\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 1|$})+(-1)^{n}(\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 1|$})+\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$})+i(-1)^{n+m}(\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 1|$})-\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$})\right]\,.

(25)

Therefore, by computing
\omega(n,\phi_{m})=\frac{1}{4}\mbox{Tr}[\gamma\Omega(n,\phi_{m})]
one finds in vectorized form the following equation:

\displaystyle\mathinner{\left|{\omega(\gamma)}\right\rangle\kern-3.75pt\rangle}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}\mathinner{\left|{\gamma}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}\,,

(26)

where

\displaystyle\mathinner{\left|{\omega}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega(l,0)\\
\omega(l,1)\end{pmatrix}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\mathcal{D}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix}

(27)

with
\omega(r,1)=\tfrac{1}{2}-\omega(l,0)
and
\omega(r,0)=\tfrac{1}{2}-\omega(l,1)
. Notice that
\mathcal{D}
is an orthogonal matrix. Inverting (
26
) one gets
\mathinner{\left|{\gamma(\omega)}\right\rangle\kern-3.75pt\rangle}=\mathcal{D}\mathinner{\left|{\omega}\right\rangle\kern-2.15277pt\rangle}
.

B.2
Representability

Since
\gamma^{2}\leq\gamma
, the domain of pure/ensemble
N
-representable 1RDMs takes the form of a disc of radius
\tfrac{1}{2}
given by

\left(\gamma_{ll}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\gamma_{lr}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

(28)

The area of this disc is
A_{N}=\pi/4
and its boundary
\partial\mathcal{P}_{p}
(i.e.,

\gamma_{LR}^{2}+(\gamma_{LL}-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}=\tfrac{1}{4}
) corresponds to complete Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC)
Benavides-Riveros et al. 2020
. Plugging (
26
) in (
28
) one gets:

\left(\omega_{l,0}-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}+\left(\omega_{l,1}-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}.

(29)

Proof of Eq. (7)

We take the the functional derivative of
\mathcal{E}[\omega]-\mu N[\omega]
at the point
\omega
in the direction of
\omega
:

\displaystyle 0=\delta\big(\mathcal{E}[\omega]-\mu N[\omega]\big)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\delta\big(\mathcal{E}[\omega]-\mu N[\omega]\big)}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int\left[h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}[\omega]}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}-\mu\right]\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int\left[h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}[\omega]}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\mu\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\right]d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int\left[h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}[\omega]}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\mu\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\right]d\Omega\,,

(30)

where
d\Omega=d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}d^{3}\mathbf{p}
. In the last line we have used the fact that
\int fgd\Omega=\int f\star gd\Omega
. Therefore, we conclude that at each phase-space point:

\displaystyle h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}[\omega]}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\mu\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=0\,.

(31)

Since
\int fgd\Omega=\int g\star fd\Omega
also holds, we also conclude that:

\displaystyle\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}[\omega]}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}-\mu\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=0\,.

(32)

Appendix D
Hartree-Fock in phase space

In this last section, we investigate the form of the orbital-free Hartree-Fock equations in phase space for a system of
N
electrons. As the respective wave function is a single Slater determinant, the 1-body reduced-density matrix is a projector:

\gamma({\mathbf{r}},{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})=\sum^{N}_{n=1}\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{r}})\varphi^{*}_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\,,

with
\int\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{r}})\varphi_{m}^{*}({\mathbf{r}})d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}=\delta_{nm}
. The first result we will prove is that the corresponding Wigner function satisfies
\omega\star\omega=\omega
.

Proof.

Let us first define the Wigner phase-space orbitals
\chi_{n}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=\int\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{z}})\varphi_{n}^{*}({\mathbf{r}}+{\mathbf{z}})e^{2i\mathbf{p}\cdot{\mathbf{z}}}d^{3}{\mathbf{z}}
. They satisfy the following equation:

\displaystyle\chi_{n}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\chi_{m}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\int\,\chi_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\chi_{m}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})e^{2i({\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}-{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})}\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\int\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime})\,\varphi^{*}_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime})\,\varphi_{m}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime})\,\varphi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}+{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime})\,e^{2i({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{p}}e^{2i({\mathbf{z}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime})\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}e^{2i({\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}})\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}}d\Omega^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime\prime}\,d^{3}{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime}\,d^{3}{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\int\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime})\,\varphi^{*}_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime})\,\varphi_{m}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime})\,\varphi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}+{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime})e^{2i({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{p}}\,\delta({\mathbf{z}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime})\,\delta({\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}})\,d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\,d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\,d^{3}{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime}\,d^{3}{\mathbf{z}}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\int\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime})\,\varphi^{*}_{n}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime})\,\varphi_{m}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}})\,\varphi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}})e^{2i({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{p}}\,d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}.

Letting
\mathbf{u}={\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{v}={\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}
, we have:

\displaystyle\chi_{n}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\chi_{m}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\int\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{r}}-\mathbf{u})\,\varphi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{r}}+\mathbf{u})\,e^{2i\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{p}}d^{3}\mathbf{u}\int\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{v})\,\varphi^{*}_{n}(\mathbf{v})\,d^{3}\mathbf{v}=\delta_{nm}\chi_{n}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=\sum_{nm}\chi_{n}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\chi_{m}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=\sum_{nm}\chi_{n}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\delta_{nm}=\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,.

(33)

∎

This result indicates that we have to solve the Hartree-Fock functional subject to the condition
\omega\star\omega=\omega
and the normalization
\int\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})d\Omega=N
. Using the Lagrange multipliers
\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
and
\beta
, the variational problem reads:

\displaystyle\delta\left\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm HF}[\omega]-\int\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\left[\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\right]\,d\Omega-\beta\left[\int\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,d\Omega-N\right]\right\}=0\,.

(34)

Before performing the variation note that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}\int\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,d\Omega

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\delta}{\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})}\int\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,\omega({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\omega({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})e^{2i({\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}-{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})}\,d\Omega\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int\alpha({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\omega({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})e^{2i({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}-{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})}\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\alpha({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})\,\omega({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})e^{2i({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}-{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p})}\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,.

(35)

Using this result in Eq. (
34
) we obtain

\displaystyle L({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\equiv f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\alpha({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\beta=0\,,

(36)

where
f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=\delta\mathcal{E}_{\rm HF}[\omega]/\delta\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
. Multiplying (with the
\star
-product) this equation on the left by
\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
(i.e,
\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star L({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
) and on the right (i.e,
L({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
), and then subtracting both equations we obtain that
\omega

\star
-anticommutes with
f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
:

\displaystyle[f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p}),\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})]_{\star}\equiv f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})-\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\star f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=0\,.

(37)

This is the equation of
\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
within Hartree-Fock theory. Recall that it admits an expansion in
\hbar
. For this reason, this equation allows a semiclassical expansion that does not exist in the double-coordinate representation.

To finish the calculation we give now the explicit form of
f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})
. Let us define the 1-particle Hamiltonian
\mathbf{p}^{2}/2m+v({\mathbf{r}})
, with
v({\mathbf{r}})
being the external potential. Using the inverse of the Wigner transformation, the Hartree-Fock energy reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm HF}[\omega]=\int h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,d\Omega+\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})\,\omega({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})}{|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|}d\Omega\,d\Omega^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\int e^{i(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\cdot({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}\frac{\omega(({\mathbf{r}}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})/2,\mathbf{p})\,\omega(({\mathbf{r}}+{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})/2,\mathbf{p}^{\prime})}{|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|}d\Omega\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,.

A straightforward calculation finally gives:

\displaystyle f_{\rm HF}({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})=h({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p})+\int\frac{\omega({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})}{|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|}\,d\Omega^{\prime}-\int\frac{e^{i(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}}}{|\mathbf{{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}|}\omega({\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,.

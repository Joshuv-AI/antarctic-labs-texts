On the Functions Which are CCZ-equivalent but not EA-equivalent to Quadratic Functions over 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
Jaeseong Jeong, Namhun Koo, Soonhak Kwon

Abstract
For a given function F F from 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} to itself, determining whether there exists a function which is CCZ-equivalent but EA-inequivalent to F F is a very important and interesting problem. For example, Kölsch [ 33 ] showed that there is no function which is CCZ-equivalent but EA-inequivalent to the inverse function. On the other hand, for the cases of Gold function F ⁡ ( x ) = x 2 i + 1 F(x)=x^{2^{i}+1} and F ⁡ ( x ) = x 3 + Tr ⁡ ( x 9 ) F(x)=x^{3}+{\rm Tr}(x^{9}) over 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} , Budaghyan, Carlet and Pott (respectively, Budaghyan, Carlet and Leander) [ 13 , 11 ] found functions which are CCZ-equivalent but EA-inequivalent to F F . In this paper, when a given function F F has a component function which has a linear structure, we present functions which are CCZ-equivalent to F F , and if suitable conditions are satisfied, the constructed functions are shown to be EA-inequivalent to F F . As a consequence, for every quadratic function F F on 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} ( n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 ) with nonlinearity > 0 >0 and differential uniformity ≤ 2 n − 3 \leq 2^{n-3} , we explicitly construct functions which are CCZ-equivalent but EA-inequivalent to F F . Also for every non-planar quadratic function on 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} ( p > 2 , n ≥ 4 ) (p>2,n\geq 4) with | 𝒲 F | ≤ p n − 1 |{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|\leq p^{n-1} and differential uniformity ≤ p n − 3 \leq p^{n-3} , we explicitly construct functions which are CCZ-equivalent but EA-inequivalent to F F . As an application, for a proper divisor m m of n n , we present many examples of ( n , m ) (n,m) -functions F F on 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} such that the CCZ-equivalence class of F F is strictly larger that the EA-equivalence class of F F .

Introduction

Vectorial Boolean functions are widely applied in cryptography and coding theory. Many researchers have been interested
in constructing vectorial Boolean functions with good cryptographic properties. For example, related to S-Box in AES,

The big APN problem
, finding an APN permutation on even dimension, is one of the most prominent problems that
many researchers are interested in. Moreover, functions with low differential uniformity, low boomerang uniformity, and
high nonlinearity have also garnered significant interest among researchers. Please see

When new cryptographic functions are proposed, researchers consider whether they are cryptographically equivalent to
other known functions or not. One of such equivalence relations is proposed by Carlet, Charpin and Zinoviev
[
22
]
,
and it is referred to as CCZ-equivalence named after the proposers. Several cryptographic properties such as
differential spectrum and extended Walsh spectrum are invariant under CCZ-equivalence. To show that the given two
functions are not CCZ-equivalent, one may compare their differential spectrum or extended Walsh spectrum. However,
proving CCZ-equivalence of two functions with the same differential spectrum and extended Walsh spectrum is not easy in
general. The EA-equivalence (extended affine equivalence) is another cryptographic equivalence relation, and it is well
known that if two functions are EA-equivalent then they are CCZ-equivalent. It is also known that EA-equivalence
preserves the algebraic degree, while two CCZ-equivalent functions may have different algebraic degrees. Two very
important topics related with CCZ and EA equivalences are

1.

determining whether the two equivalences are the same notion for some class of functions.

2.

finding CCZ-equivalent but EA-inequivalent functions if CCZ-equivalence is strictly more general than
EA-equivalence for some class of functions.

For the first topic, it is known that CCZ-equivalence and EA-equivalence are the same notion

2
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
the finite field of
p^{n}
elements where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p
is a prime number. Let

{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
be the multiplicative group consisting of all invertible elements in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
.
To measure the resistance of the block cipher (especially a
S
-box inside of the block cipher) against
the differential cryptanalysis, for every
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
and
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
, we define the difference
distribution table
\Delta_{F}(a,b)
and the differential uniformity
\Delta_{F}
for a vectorial
(n,n)
-function

F:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{F}(a,b)=\#\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}:F(x+a)-F(x)=b\},

\displaystyle\Delta_{F}=\max\{\Delta_{F}(a,b):a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}^{\times},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\}.

We also
define the
differential
of
F
to the direction of
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
by

D_{a}F(x)=F(x+a)-F(x)\mbox{ for all }x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}

so that
\Delta_{F}(a,b)=\#\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}:D_{a}F(x)=b\}
. Then the lower the value of
\Delta_{F}
is, the better
F

resists a differential attack. We say that
F
is planar or perfect nonlinear (PN) if
\Delta_{F}=1
, and almost perfect
nonlinear (APN) if
\Delta_{F}=2
.

On the other hand, there is one important equivalence notion of vectorial Boolean functions called
Carlet-Charpin-Zinoviev (CCZ) equivalence. This notion was introduced in
[
22
]
, and the term
CCZ-equivalence was first used in
[
13
]
. This equivalence is broader than other known equivalences
such as affine equivalence and extended affine equivalence (EA-equivalence).

Definition 2.1

Two functions
F,F^{\prime}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
are said to be

–

affine equivalent if
F^{\prime}=A_{1}\circ F\circ A_{2}
,
where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are affine permutations on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
.

–

extended affine equivalent (EA-equivalent) if

F^{\prime}=A_{1}\circ F\circ A_{2}+A_{3}
, where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are affine
permutations on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
and
A_{3}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
is an affine function.

–

CCZ-equivalent if there
exists an affine permutation
\mathscr{L}
on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that
\mathscr{L}({\mathcal{G}}_{F})={\mathcal{G}}_{F^{\prime}}
where

{\mathcal{G}}_{F}
is the graph of the function
F
, that is
{\mathcal{G}}_{F}=\{(x,F(x)):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
.

Remark 2.2

We briefly write
F\overset{\textrm{\tiny affine}}{\sim}F^{\prime}
if

F
and
F^{\prime}
are affine equivalent. In a similar way, we write
F\overset{\textrm{\tiny EA}}{\sim}F^{\prime}
(resp.
F\overset{\textrm{\tiny CCZ}}{\sim}F^{\prime}
) if
F
and
F^{\prime}
are EA-equivalent (resp. CCZ-equivalent). Then, we have the
following.

F\overset{\textrm{\tiny affine}}{\sim}F^{\prime}\Rightarrow F\overset{\textrm{\tiny EA}}{\sim}F^{\prime}\Rightarrow F\overset{\textrm{\tiny CCZ}}{\sim}F^{\prime}

Remark 2.3

Since the above mentioned three relations are equivalence relations, we may naturally think of the equivalence classes
for each equivalence relations. Namely, we denote
\mathscr{C}_{\textrm{affine}}(F)
the set consisting of all
F^{\prime}
such
that
F^{\prime}\overset{\textrm{\tiny affine}}{\sim}F
. In a similar manner, we denote
\mathscr{C}_{\textrm{EA}}(F)
(resp.

\mathscr{C}_{\textrm{CCZ}}(F)
) the set consisting of all
F^{\prime}
such that
F^{\prime}\overset{\textrm{\tiny EA}}{\sim}F
(resp.

F^{\prime}\overset{\textrm{\tiny CCZ}}{\sim}F
).

It is well-known that the differential uniformity is invariant under CCZ-equivalence, and consequently
under EA-equivalence and affine equivalence. Another important notion invariant under CCZ-equivalence is
the linearity, which is the maximum absolute value of all Walsh coefficients of a given function.
To explain Walsh coefficient and Walsh transform, we need a notion of trace function
(a very special form of linear function).
Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
m
be a positive divisor of
n
such that
n=ms
. We denote
{\rm Tr}^{n}_{m}(x)
the trace function from

{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
, that is

{\rm Tr}^{n}_{m}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}x^{p^{jm}}=x+x^{p^{m}}+x^{p^{2m}}+\cdots+x^{p^{(s-1)m}}.

When
m=1
, we write
{\rm Tr}(x)
instead of
{\rm Tr}^{n}_{1}(x)
if there is no confusion of related fields. For
d|m
and
m|n
,
it is trivial to check

{\rm Tr}_{d}^{n}(x)={\rm Tr}_{d}^{m}{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

Let
\xi=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p}}\in\mathbb{C}
be a complex primitive
p^{th}
root of unity. For
(n,1)
-function

f:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, its
Walsh-Hadamard transform

{\mathcal{W}}_{f}
is the Fourier transform of the function

\xi^{f(x)}
, that is,

{\mathcal{W}}_{f}(a)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{f(x)-{\rm Tr}(ax)}\mbox{ for every }a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}.

We define
|{\mathcal{W}}_{f}|
be the maximum absolute value of all
{\mathcal{W}}_{f}(a)
for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. That is,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}}_{f}|=\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}|{\mathcal{W}}_{f}(a)|,

(2.1)

where
|{\mathcal{W}}_{f}(a)|^{2}={\mathcal{W}}_{f}(a)\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{f}}(a)
and
\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{f}}(a)
is the complex conjugate of
{\mathcal{W}}_{f}(a)
.
Due to Parseval’s identity, one always have
|{\mathcal{W}}_{f}|^{2}\geq p^{n}
, and
a
(n,1)
-function
f
achieving the lower bound
|{\mathcal{W}}_{f}|=p^{\frac{n}{2}}
is called a (generalized or
p
-ary) bent function.
Please refer the book of Mesnager
[
38
]
for more about bent functions.

For
(n,n)
-function
F:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
, we define its
Walsh transform

{\mathcal{W}}_{F}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

by

{\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b)={\mathcal{W}}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)}(a)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(bF(x)-ax)}\mbox{ for every }a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}},

where value
{\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b)
is called the Walsh coefficient of
F
, and
{\rm Tr}(bF)
is called a
component function of
F
. In a similar way, we
define

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}},b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}}|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b)|=\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}},b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}}|{\mathcal{W}}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)}(a)|=\max_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}}|{\mathcal{W}}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)}|.

(2.2)

The value
|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|
is called the linearity of
F
and is written as
\textup{{L}}(F)=|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|
. For a binary case (i.e.,

p=2
), we also define
\textup{{NL}}(F)
(the nonlinearity of
F
) as

\textup{{NL}}(F)=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\textup{{L}}(F)
. For arbitrary characteristic
p
,
it is well-known that
|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|
is invariant under CCZ-equivalence.

There is one more notion (which we will discuss very often) so called
algebraic degree
which is invariant under
EA-equivalence but not under CCZ-equivalence.
Any
(n,n)
-function
F
on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is uniquely represented by a univariate polynomial
F(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}[x]

of degree
<p^{n}
. That is,

F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{p^{n}-1}a_{i}x^{i},\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}.

For any integer
0\leq k<p^{n}
with its
p
-ary expansion
k=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}k_{j}p^{j}
, we define
p
-weight of
k
,

w_{p}(k)
, as
w_{p}(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}k_{j}
. An algebraic degree of
F
,
d^{\circ}(F)
, is the maximum value among all

w_{p}(i)
with
a_{i}\neq 0
;

d^{\circ}(F)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{0\leq i<p^{n}}\\
{a_{i}\neq 0}\end{subarray}}w_{p}(i)\quad\text{ where }F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{p^{n}-1}a_{i}x^{i}.

An
(n,n)
-function
F
with its algebraic degree
two
is called a quadratic function, and a quadratic
function with no affine term,

F(x)=\sum_{i\leq j}a_{i,j}x^{p^{i}+p^{j}},\quad a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}},

is called a DO-polynomial (Dembowski-Ostrom polynomial). It is understood that the summation indices
i\leq j

becomes strict (i.e.,
i<j
) if
p=2
.

Linear Structure, Linear Translator and Linear Kernel

Let
q=p^{n}
be a power of a prime
p
and let
f:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a
(n,1)
-function on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Definition 3.1

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We say
\gamma
is a
b
-linear structure of
f
if

\displaystyle f(x+\gamma)-f(x)=b\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}},

and
\gamma
is called a linear structure of
f
if
\gamma
is a
b
-linear structure of
f
for some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We also say
f
has a (nontrivial) linear structure if there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
such that
\gamma
is a linear
structure of
f
.

Definition 3.2

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We say
\gamma
is a
b
-linear translator of
f
if

\displaystyle f(x+u\gamma)-f(x)=ub\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\text{ and }\text{ for all }u\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

and
\gamma
is called a linear translator of
f
if
\gamma
is a
b
-linear translator of
f
for some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We also
say
f
has a (nontrivial) linear translator if there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
such that
\gamma
is a linear
translator of
f
.

If
\gamma
is a
b
-linear translator, then
\gamma
is also a
b
-linear structure. However,
it turns out that, over a prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, ‘linear
translator’ and ‘linear structure’ are the same notion as is shown below.

Lemma 3.3

Let
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
. Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a
(n,1)
-function. Then
\gamma
is a

b
-linear structure of
f
if and only if
\gamma
is a
b
-linear translator of
f
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
is clear by taking
u=1
.

(\Rightarrow)
For any
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
,

\displaystyle f(x+u\gamma)-f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{u}f(x+k\gamma)-f(x+(k-1)\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{u}f(x_{k}+\gamma)-f(x_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{u}b=ub,

where
x_{k}=x+(k-1)\gamma
.

Remark 3.4

It is well-known (e.g. see
[
34
]
) that the set

{\mathcal{LK}}_{f}\overset{\text{\rm def}}{=}\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}:\gamma\text{ is a linear structure of }f\}

is a vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which is briefly sketched below for
the case of component functions. The subspace
{\mathcal{LK}}_{f}
is called the linear kernel of
f
.

Let
F(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}[x]
be an
(n,n)
-function and let

\displaystyle L_{\gamma}(x)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}L_{\gamma,F}(x)=F(x+\gamma)-F(x)-(F(\gamma)-F(0))=D_{\gamma}F(x)-D_{\gamma}F(0)

be the differential
D_{\gamma}F(x)
with its constant term
killed. Let
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
and let

\begin{split}\mathcal{LK}&\overset{\text{def}}{=}\mathcal{LK}_{{\rm Tr}(\beta F)}\\
&=\{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}:{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(x))=\,\text{constant}\,={\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(0))\textrm{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\}\\
&=\{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}:{\rm Tr}(\beta L_{\gamma}(x))=0\textrm{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\}\\
&=\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}:{\rm Im}L_{\gamma}\subset\langle\beta\rangle^{\perp}\}=\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}:\beta\in({\rm Im}L_{\gamma})^{\perp}\}\end{split}

(3.1)

be the linear kernel of the component function
{\rm Tr}(\beta F)
,
where
W^{\perp}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}:{\rm Tr}(xw)=0\text{ for all }w\in W\}
for a subset
W
of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

Remark 3.5

From the definition of the linear kernel, one has

\gamma
is a linear structure of

\Leftrightarrow

\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle=F(x+\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})-F(x)-(F(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})-F(0))

\displaystyle=F(x+\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})-F(x+\gamma)-(F(\gamma^{\prime})-F(0))+F(x+\gamma)-F(x)-(F(\gamma)-F(0))

\displaystyle\qquad+F(\gamma^{\prime})-F(0)+F(\gamma)-F(0)-(F(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})-F(0))

\displaystyle=L_{\gamma^{\prime}}(x+\gamma)+L_{\gamma}(x)+F(\gamma^{\prime})+F(\gamma)-F(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})-F(0)

\displaystyle=L_{\gamma^{\prime}}(x+\gamma)+L_{\gamma}(x)-L_{\gamma}(\gamma^{\prime}),

(3.2)

one has

.
(Please refer Remark

Note that
L_{\gamma}(\gamma^{\prime})=L_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\gamma)

4
Walsh Transform of (
p
-ary) Strongly Plateaued Functions over Arbitrary Characteristic

The subsequent survey also applies to quadratic functions as a special case. Moreover, we will give concrete examples
of
F^{\prime}
satisfying
F\overset{\textrm{\tiny CCZ}}{\sim}F^{\prime}
but
F\overset{\textrm{\tiny EA}}{\not\sim}F^{\prime}

only for quadratic
F
in Section
7
.
For a binary case, Walsh transform of strongly plateaued functions
is well-documented (See for example
[
18
]
). In fact, The case
p\neq 2
is not so much
different from the case
p=2
. However, for the clarity of the exposition for general
p
,
we briefly survey Walsh transform of strongly plateaued functions on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
with arbitrary
characteristic
p
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
be a strongly plateaued function (or a quadratic function). Let
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
and let

\mathcal{LK}=\mathcal{LK}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)}=\{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}:{\rm Tr}(bL_{\gamma}(x))=0\textrm{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\}
be the linear kernel of
{\rm Tr}(bF)
with

L_{\gamma}(x)=F(x+\gamma)-F(x)-(F(\gamma)-F(0))

(See Equation (
3.1
)). Noting that
\bar{\xi}=\xi^{-1}
,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(bF(y)-ay)}\cdot\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}(\xi^{-1})^{{\rm Tr}(bF(x)-ax)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}(a(y-x))}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(b[F(y)-F(x)])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}(a\gamma)}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(b[F(x+\gamma)-F(x)])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}(a\gamma)}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(b[F(x+\gamma)-F(x)])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}(a\gamma)}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(bL_{\gamma}(x))+\epsilon_{\gamma}}\qquad(\epsilon_{\gamma}={\rm Tr}(b[F(\gamma)-F(0)]))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{LK}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}(a\gamma)}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{\epsilon_{\gamma}}+\sum_{\gamma\notin\mathcal{LK}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}(a\gamma)}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(bL_{\gamma}(x))+\epsilon_{\gamma}}.

For
\gamma\notin\mathcal{LK}
, since
{\rm Tr}(bL_{\gamma}(x))
is a balanced function if
F
is strongly plateaued (or
since
{\rm Tr}(bL_{\gamma}(x))
is nonzero linear function if
f
is quadratic),
{\rm Tr}(bL_{\gamma}(x))
takes every
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
equally many times. Therefore since
\sum_{u=0}^{p-1}\xi^{u}=0
, one gets

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b)|^{2}=p^{n}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{LK}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}(a\gamma)+\epsilon_{\gamma}}.

Now let
\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\cdots,\gamma_{k}\}
be a basis for
\mathcal{LK}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and let

\epsilon_{i}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\epsilon_{\gamma_{i}}={\rm Tr}(b[F(\gamma_{i})-F(0)])
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then using the
vector space property of
\mathcal{LK}
, for any
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{LK}
with
e_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, one can express
\epsilon_{\gamma}
as

\epsilon_{\gamma}=\epsilon_{\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\gamma_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\epsilon_{\gamma_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\epsilon_{i}\,\,\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Extend
\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\cdots,\gamma_{k}\}
to a basis

\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\cdots,\gamma_{k},\gamma_{k+1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}
for
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and let
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}
be a unique dual basis of
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}
, i.e.,
{\rm Tr}(\alpha_{i}\gamma_{j})=1
if
i=j
and

{\rm Tr}(\alpha_{i}\gamma_{j})=0

if
i\neq j
.

Let
a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
(
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
). Then

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b)|^{2}

\displaystyle=p^{n}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{LK}}\xi^{-{\rm Tr}\left((\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\alpha_{i})(\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{j}\gamma_{j})\right)+\epsilon_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=p^{n}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{LK}}\xi^{-(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i})+\epsilon_{\gamma}}=p^{n}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{LK}}\xi^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\epsilon_{i}-a_{i})e_{i}}.

As
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\gamma_{i}
runs through all elements of
\mathcal{LK}
, the vectors

\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}\rangle
runs through all vectors of

\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
. Therefore, if
\langle\epsilon_{1}-a_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{k}-a_{k}\rangle
is a nonzero vector,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\epsilon_{i}-a_{i})e_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
is balanced
for all inputs
\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}\rangle
. With these observations, one has the following well-known
result of Walsh transform of strongly plateaued (or quadratic) functions over arbitrary characteristic
p
.

Proposition 4.1

(Walsh transform of strongly plateaued functions)
Let
F(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}[x]
be an
(n,n)
-function such that
F
is strongly plateaued (or quadratic).
Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. let
{\mathcal{LK}}={\mathcal{LK}}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)}
be the linear kernel of
{\rm Tr}(bF)
of dimension
0\leq k\leq n

such that
{\mathcal{LK}}
has a basis
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k}\}
, and
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k},\gamma_{k+1},\cdots\gamma_{n}\}

is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Let
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}

be a unique dual basis of
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}
.
Let
a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
\epsilon_{i}={\rm Tr}(b[F(\gamma_{i})-F(0)])

(1\leq i\leq k)
. Then one has

|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b)|^{2}=p^{n}\sum_{\gamma(=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{j}\gamma_{j})\in\mathcal{LK}}\xi^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\epsilon_{i}-a_{i})e_{i}}=\begin{cases}p^{n+k}&\text{ if }\epsilon_{i}=a_{i}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq k\\
0&\text{ if not}.\end{cases}

Consequently one has,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)}|

\displaystyle=p^{\frac{n+k}{2}}\quad\text{ where }k=\dim{\mathcal{LK}}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)},

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|

\displaystyle=p^{\frac{n+k_{m}}{2}}\quad\text{ where }k_{m}=\max_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}}\dim{\mathcal{LK}}_{{\rm Tr}(bF)}.

5
CCZ-Equivalence of Some Functions Having Linear Structure

Lemma 5.1

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
. Let
{\mathscr{L}}:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

be defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\mapsto(x+\gamma{\rm Tr}(\alpha x+\beta y),y).

Then,
{\mathscr{L}}
is a linear permutation on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
if and only if
{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)\neq-1
.

Proof.
Since the linearity of
{\mathscr{L}}
is clear, it is enough to show

{\mathscr{L}}\text{ is one to one }\Longleftrightarrow{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)\neq-1

Now,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(x,y)={\mathscr{L}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(x+\gamma{\rm Tr}(\alpha x+\beta y),y)=(x^{\prime}+\gamma{\rm Tr}(\alpha x^{\prime}+\beta y^{\prime}),y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y^{\prime}=y\text{ and }x+\gamma{\rm Tr}(\alpha x)=x^{\prime}+\gamma{\rm Tr}(\alpha x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y^{\prime}=y\text{ and }x-x^{\prime}=-\gamma{\rm Tr}(\alpha(x-x^{\prime}))=-\gamma u\quad(u={\rm Tr}(\alpha(x-x^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{F}_{p})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y^{\prime}=y\text{ and }-\gamma u=\gamma u{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y^{\prime}=y\text{ and }u({\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)+1)=0\quad(\because\gamma\neq 0)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y^{\prime}=y\text{ and }x^{\prime}=x\quad\text{or}\quad y^{\prime}=y\text{ and }{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)=-1\quad(x-x^{\prime}=-\gamma u)

Proposition 5.2

Let
p
be a prime. Suppose that
F_{0}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}[x]
is an
(n,n)
-function such
that
\gamma
is a linear structure of
{\rm Tr}(\beta F_{0})

for some
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
. Then, choosing
\alpha,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
satisfying

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)=-2,\qquad{\rm Tr}(c\beta\gamma)=-{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(0)),

and letting
F(x)=F_{0}(x)+cx
, the function

\displaystyle H(x)=x+\gamma{\rm Tr}\left(\alpha x+\beta F(x)\right)

is an involution, i.e.
H\circ H(x)=x
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

Proof.
Since

\displaystyle L_{\gamma,F}(x)

\displaystyle=F(x+\gamma)-F(x)-(F(\gamma)-F(0))

\displaystyle=F_{0}(x+\gamma)-F_{0}(x)+c\gamma-(F_{0}(\gamma)+c\gamma-F_{0}(0))=L_{\gamma,F_{0}}(x),

L_{\gamma}(x)=L_{\gamma,F}(x)=L_{\gamma,F_{0}}(x)
is independent of choices of
F(x)=F_{0}(x)+cx
for any

c\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.
On the other hand,
D_{\gamma}F(x)=D_{\gamma}F_{0}(x)+c\gamma
so that

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(x))={\rm Tr}\left(\beta(D_{\gamma}F_{0}(x)+c\gamma)\right)={\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(x))+{\rm Tr}(c\beta\gamma).

Since
\gamma
is a linear structure of
{\rm Tr}(\beta F_{0}(x))

(i.e.,
{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(x))={\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(0))
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
),
choosing any
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
satisfying

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(c\beta\gamma)=-{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(0))=-{\rm Tr}(\beta[F_{0}(\gamma)-F_{0}(0)]),

one finds that
\gamma
is a
0
-linear structure of
{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x))
with
F(x)=F_{0}(x)+cx
. That is,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(x))=0\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}.

(5.1)

Define
\varepsilon
as an
(n,1)
-function

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\varepsilon(x)={\rm Tr}\left(\alpha x+\beta F(x)\right)

so that
H(x)=x+\gamma\varepsilon
. Then

\displaystyle F\circ H(x)=F(x+\gamma\varepsilon)=F(x)+F(x+\gamma\varepsilon)-F(x)=F(x)+D_{\gamma\varepsilon}F(x),

(5.2)

where
D_{\gamma\varepsilon}F(x)
is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\gamma\varepsilon}F(x)=F(x+\gamma\varepsilon)-F(x)=F\left(x+\gamma{\rm Tr}(\alpha x+\beta F(x))\right)-F(x).

Then

\displaystyle H\circ H(x)

\displaystyle=H(x)+\gamma{\rm Tr}\left(\alpha H(x)+\beta F(H(x))\right)

\displaystyle=x+\gamma\varepsilon+\gamma{\rm Tr}\left(\alpha(x+\gamma\varepsilon)+\beta[F(x)+D_{\gamma\varepsilon}F(x)]\right)

(5.3)

\displaystyle=x+\gamma\varepsilon+\gamma\varepsilon+\gamma{\rm Tr}\left(\alpha\gamma\varepsilon+\beta D_{\gamma\varepsilon}F(x)\right)

\displaystyle=x+\gamma\varepsilon(2+{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma))+\gamma{\rm Tr}\left(\beta D_{\gamma\varepsilon}F(x)\right)

\displaystyle=x+\gamma{\rm Tr}\left(\beta D_{\gamma\varepsilon}F(x)\right).\qquad(\because{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)=-2)

(5.4)

Also, from Equation (
5.1
), one has
{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(x))=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.
Moreover, from Lemma
3.3
by taking
f(x)={\rm Tr}(\beta F(x))

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left(\beta F(x+\gamma u)-\beta F(x)\right)={\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma u}F(x))=0

for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Therefore, from Equation (
5.4
), one has

H\circ H(x)=x
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

\displaystyle=(y,F\circ H(y))\qquad(y=H(x))

Let

1.

If

\displaystyle F\circ H(x)

\displaystyle=F(x)+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F(x).

(5.5)

2.

If

\displaystyle F\circ H(x)

\displaystyle=F(x)+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F(x)+F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(\gamma)(\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon).

(5.6)

Proof.

H(x)=x+\gamma\varepsilon

\displaystyle F\circ H(x)

\displaystyle=F(x+\gamma\varepsilon)=F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x+\gamma\varepsilon)+F_{\ell}(x+\gamma\varepsilon)+F(0).

(5.7)

Since

\displaystyle F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x+\gamma\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\leq j}a_{i,j}(x+\gamma\varepsilon)^{p^{i}+p^{j}}=\sum_{i\leq j}a_{i,j}(x^{p^{i}}+\gamma^{p^{i}}\varepsilon)(x^{p^{j}}+\gamma^{p^{j}}\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\leq j}a_{i,j}\left(x^{p^{i}+p^{j}}+(\gamma^{p^{j}}x^{p^{i}}+\gamma^{p^{i}}x^{p^{j}}+\gamma^{p^{i}+p^{j}})\varepsilon+\gamma^{p^{i}+p^{j}}(\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x)+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x)+F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(\gamma)(\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon),

(5.8)

Combining Equation (

) and (

\displaystyle F\circ H(x)

\displaystyle=F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x+\gamma\varepsilon)+F_{\ell}(x+\gamma\varepsilon)+F(0)

\displaystyle=F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x)+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x)+F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(\gamma)(\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon)+F_{\ell}(x)+F_{\ell}(\gamma)\varepsilon+F(0)

\displaystyle=\{F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x)+F_{\ell}(x)+F(0)\}+\varepsilon\{D_{\gamma}F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(x)+F_{\ell}(\gamma)\}+F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(\gamma)(\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=F(x)+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F(x)+F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(\gamma)(\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon).

From the above Proposition, to show

, it is enough to show that the algebraic degrees of

\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F(x)
and
\varepsilon^{2}
are greater than two. In the following section, we will show that

\displaystyle d^{\circ}(\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F(x))

\displaystyle=3\quad\text{ for any }p,\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\quad d^{\circ}(\varepsilon^{2})

\displaystyle=4\quad\text{ for odd }p,

are satisfied under certain mild restrictions on

.

6
Algebraic Degrees of Products of Quadratic Functions

For quadratic
F(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}[x]
, recall that
L_{\gamma}(x)=F(x+\gamma)-F(x)-(F(\gamma)-F(0))
is bilinear with respect
to
\gamma,x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}.
That is,
L_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}}(x)=L_{\gamma_{1}}(x)+L_{\gamma_{2}}(x)
and

L_{\gamma}(x+y)=L_{\gamma}(x)+L_{\gamma}(y)
. Moreover
L_{\gamma}(x)
is symmetric in the sense that

L_{\gamma}(\beta)=L_{\beta}(\gamma)
.

Lemma 6.1

Let
F(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}[x]
be a quadratic
(n,n)
-function and let

Proof.
From

because

. Now

\displaystyle D_{\gamma_{1}}\left({\rm Tr}(\beta F(x))L_{\gamma}(x)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}))L_{\gamma}(x+\gamma_{1})-{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x))L_{\gamma}(x)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1})-\beta F(x)\right)L_{\gamma}(x)+{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(x)+\beta(F(\gamma_{1})-F(0))\right)L_{\gamma}(x)+{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(x)\right)L_{\gamma}(x)+{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1})+{\rm Tr}(\beta(F(\gamma_{1})-F(0)))L_{\gamma}(x).

Since
d^{\circ}(L_{\gamma}(x))\leq 1

D_{\gamma_{3}}D_{\gamma_{2}}D_{\gamma_{1}}\left({\rm Tr}(\beta F(x))L_{\gamma}(x)\right)=D_{\gamma_{3}}D_{\gamma_{2}}\left[{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(x)\right)L_{\gamma}(x)+{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1})\right],

where

\displaystyle D_{\gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle\left[{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(x)\right)L_{\gamma}(x)+{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1})\right]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(x+\gamma_{2})\right)L_{\gamma}(x+\gamma_{2})+{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle-{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(x)\right)L_{\gamma}(x)-{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x+\gamma_{1}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(x)\right)L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{2})+{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{2})\right)L_{\gamma}(x)+{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{2})\right)L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle+{\rm Tr}\left(\beta L_{\gamma_{2}}(x+\gamma_{1})+\beta(F(\gamma_{2})-F(0))\right)L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1}).

Since
D_{\gamma_{3}}
kills all the constant terms, we get

D_{\gamma_{3}}D_{\gamma_{2}}D_{\gamma_{1}}\left({\rm Tr}(\beta F(x))L_{\gamma}(x)\right)={\rm Tr}(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{3}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{2})+{\rm Tr}(\beta L_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{2}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{3})+{\rm Tr}(\beta L_{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{3}))L_{\gamma}(\gamma_{1}).

Remark 6.3

Please be aware that if any of the two conditions
|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|<p^{n}
and
\Delta_{F}\leq p^{n-3}
is not satisfied, then
there are some functions
F
such that
d^{\circ}(\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F)<3
. Let us give such examples for a binary case.
Let
F(x)=x{\rm Tr}(x)
be a quadratic
(n,n)
-function on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. It is clear that
\textup{{NL}}(F)=0
since
{\rm Tr}(F(x))={\rm Tr}(x)
.
Let
\gamma=1=\beta
and
\alpha=0
. Then, since
d^{\circ}({\rm Tr}(F))=1
, one gets
d^{\circ}\left({\rm Tr}(F(x))D_{1}F(x)\right)<3
.
Also, for the same
F(x)=x{\rm Tr}(x)
, by choosing
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{\times}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
satisfying

{\rm Tr}(\gamma)=0
and
{\rm Tr}(\beta\gamma)=0
, one gets
{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(x))=0
for all

where

{\rm Tr}(\beta F(x))={\rm Tr}(x){\rm Tr}(\beta x)
and
D_{\gamma}F(x)=\gamma{\rm Tr}(x)
. Therefore one has

d^{\circ}\left({\rm Tr}(\beta F)D_{\gamma}F\right)=2
even if
d^{\circ}({\rm Tr}(\beta F))=2
and
d^{\circ}(D_{\gamma}F)=1
.
For another example, let

be a Gold function on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{4}}
. Note that

\Delta_{F}=4>2=2^{n-3}
. Then one has
{\rm Tr}(F(x))=x^{5}+x^{10}+x^{5}+x^{10}=0
for all

, which implies that

d^{\circ}\left({\rm Tr}(F(x))D_{\gamma}F(x)\right)=0
for any
\gamma
. For another extreme case, let
F(x)=x^{2^{i}+1}+x^{2^{j}+1}+x^{2^{i}+2^{j}}

(with

) be a quadratic
(n,n)
-function on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. It is clear that
\Delta_{F}=2^{n}
since

D_{1}F(x)=x^{2^{i}}+x+1+x^{2^{j}}+x+1+x^{2^{i}}+x^{2^{j}}+1=1
. Therefore one has

d^{\circ}\left({\rm Tr}(\beta F)D_{1}F\right)<3
for any
\beta
.

Let

(n,m+k)
-function
F^{\prime}
satisfying
\mathscr{C}_{\textrm{EA}}(F^{\prime})\subsetneqq\mathscr{C}_{\textrm{CCZ}}(F^{\prime})
. See also
[
41
]
for a generalization of
these methods over finite abelian groups.

Please note that, when
m=n
, Theorem
8.1
is exactly same to Theorem
6.7
. When
m
is
a proper divisor of
m
(i.e.,
m<n
), then one needs a stronger assumption of the lower bound of
\Delta_{F}
in Theorem

8.1
,
that is,
p^{n-m}<\Delta_{F}
. Moreover, the case
\Delta_{F}=p^{n-m}
happens if and only if
F
is a bent (or perfect non-linear)

(n,m)
-function.
Recall that, in Section
2
, we defined that
(n,1)
-function
f
on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is bent if

|{\mathcal{W}}_{f}|=p^{\frac{n}{2}}
. In a similar manner, we define that
(n,m)
-function
F
on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is bent if
every component function
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(bF)\,\,(b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{\times})
is bent. Then the following equivalent conditions are well-known
(See for example
[
40
]
.):

–

F:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
is bent.

–

D_{\gamma}{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\beta F)
is balanced for every
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{\times}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
.

–

D_{\gamma}F
is balanced for every
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}
.

–

\Delta_{F}=p^{n-m}
.

Therefore,
explicit examples
of
(n,m)
-function
F
satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
8.1

can be constructed by finding non-bent
(n,m)
-function
F
satisfying
\Delta_{F}\leq p^{n-3}
. Since every

(n,m)
-function is of the form
{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(G)
for some
(n,n)
-function
G
, we may construct explicit examples of

(n,m)
-functions satisfying Theorem
8.1
using our previous results in Section
6
as follows.

Theorem 8.4

Let
F_{0}(x)
be a quadratic
(n,n)
-function on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
6.7
with
corresponding
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c
in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
F(x)=F_{0}(x)+cx
. Letting
\Delta_{F}=p^{s}
, assume further that

0<s\leq m-3
(i.e.,
p^{n-m}<p^{n-m+s}\leq p^{n-3}
) where
m|n
with
m\geq 4
. Then the
(n,m)
-function

F_{0}^{\prime}(x)={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F(x))

satisfies the
conditions of Theorem
8.1
with
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha,\beta^{\prime}=1,\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma
and
c^{\prime}=0
. Therefore, using

\varepsilon^{\prime}={\rm Tr}(\alpha^{\prime}x)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\beta^{\prime}F^{\prime}(x))={\rm Tr}(\alpha x)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}({\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F(x)))={\rm Tr}(\alpha x+\beta F(x))=\varepsilon,

one has the followings.

1.

When
p=2
:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\circ H^{\prime}

\displaystyle=F^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{\prime}D_{\gamma^{\prime}}F^{\prime}=F^{\prime}+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F^{\prime}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F)+\varepsilon{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta D_{\gamma}F)

is
CCZ-equivalent to
F^{\prime}={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F)
but not EA-equivalent to each other.

2.

When
p>2
:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\circ H^{\prime}

\displaystyle=F^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{\prime}D_{\gamma^{\prime}}F^{\prime}+{F}_{\text{\tiny DO}}^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime})({\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}-\varepsilon^{\prime})

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F)+\varepsilon{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta D_{\gamma}F)+{{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F_{\text{\tiny DO}})}(\gamma)({\varepsilon}^{2}-\varepsilon)

is
CCZ-equivalent to
F^{\prime}={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F)
but not EA-equivalent to each other.

Proof.
Since
c^{\prime}=0
, one has
F^{\prime}(x)=F_{0}^{\prime}(x)={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F(x))
and

{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\beta^{\prime}D_{\gamma^{\prime}}F^{\prime}(x))={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(D_{\gamma}{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F(x)))={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}({\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(x)))={\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F(x))=0,

which says that
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma
is a
0
-linear structure of
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(F^{\prime})
.
Also, for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
, one has

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{F^{\prime}}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(bF^{\prime}(x))-{\rm Tr}(ax)}=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(b{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F(x)))-{\rm Tr}(ax)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}({\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(b\beta F(x)))-{\rm Tr}(ax)}=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\xi^{{\rm Tr}(b\beta F(x))-{\rm Tr}(ax)}={\mathcal{W}}_{F}(a,b\beta).

Therefore one gets
|{\mathcal{W}}_{F^{\prime}}|=|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|<2^{n}
for a binary case and
|{\mathcal{W}}_{F^{\prime}}|=|{\mathcal{W}}_{F}|\leq p^{n-1}
for odd
characteristic case. Note that
F^{\prime}={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(\beta F)
is not a bent function because
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(F^{\prime})={\rm Tr}(\beta F)
is not
a bent function (since
\gamma
is a
0
-linear structure of
{\rm Tr}(\beta F)
). Thus one has the lower bound

p^{n-m}<\Delta_{F^{\prime}}
. For the upper bound, one gets
\Delta_{F^{\prime}}=\underset{\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{\times}}{\max}|{\rm Ker}({\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}\circ\beta L_{\delta})|\leq p^{n-m+s}\leq p^{n-3}
where
L_{\delta}(x)=F(x+\delta)-F(x)-(F(\delta)-F(0))
.

\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}

9
Conclusions and Future Works

For almost all quadratic
(n,n)
-functions
F_{0}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
with
n\geq 4
, we constructed
F^{\prime}
satisfying
F^{\prime}\overset{\textrm{\tiny CCZ}}{\sim}F_{0}
but
F^{\prime}\overset{\textrm{\tiny EA}}{\not\sim}F_{0}
. For a binary case, assuming
\textup{{NL}}(F_{0})\neq 0
and
\Delta_{F_{0}}\leq 2^{n-3}
, the function

F^{\prime}=F+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F\quad(\text{with }F(x)=F_{0}(x)+cx\text{ and }\varepsilon={\rm Tr}(\alpha x+\beta F(x)))

satisfies
F_{0}\overset{\textrm{\tiny EA}}{\not\sim}F^{\prime}\overset{\textrm{\tiny CCZ}}{\sim}F_{0}
for many suitably chosen
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and
c
in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, where the
number of possible choices of
\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c\}
is at least
2^{2n-2}(2^{n}-1)
and exactly
2^{2n-2}(2^{n}-1)

if
F_{0}
is APN. For a field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
with
p>2
, assuming
|{\mathcal{W}}_{F_{0}}|\leq p^{n-1}
and
1<\Delta_{F_{0}}\leq p^{n-3}
,
the function

F^{\prime}=F+\varepsilon D_{\gamma}F+F_{\text{\tiny DO}}(\gamma)(\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon)\quad(\text{with }F(x)=F_{0}(x)+cx\text{ and }\varepsilon={\rm Tr}(\alpha x+\beta F(x)))

satisfies
F_{0}\overset{\textrm{\tiny EA}}{\not\sim}F^{\prime}\overset{\textrm{\tiny CCZ}}{\sim}F_{0}
for many suitably chosen
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and
c
in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, where the
number of possible choices of
\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c\}
is at least
p^{2n-2}(p-1)
.

For arbitrary characteristic
p
, one can choose
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and
c
(in the order of
\gamma\mapsto\{\beta,\alpha\}\mapsto c
) satisfying

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(x))

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(0))\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}},\,\,{\rm Tr}(\alpha\gamma)=-2,

\displaystyle\text{\emph{and} }\,\,{\rm Tr}(c\beta\gamma)

\displaystyle=-{\rm Tr}(\beta D_{\gamma}F_{0}(0)).

For a fixed
F_{0}
, such construction generates many
F^{\prime}
because
F^{\prime}
is depending on the choices of

\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and
c
. Though all of such
F^{\prime}
are CCZ-equivalent to each other, EA-equivalence or
(EA-inequivalence) among them are unclear at this moment. Moreover such
F^{\prime}
are no longer plateaued, and the
corresponding the Walsh spectrum needs to be investigated further. Also, it may be possible to apply our technique to
general
(n,m)
-functions, where
m
is not necessarily a divisor of
n
. Our technique (especially in Proposition

5.2
) heavily relies on the equivalence between the linear structure and the linear translator over the prime
field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, where the linear map
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{n}
has its image in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. That is the reason why we could not extended the
ideas in
[
13
,
11
]
, where they also used the trace maps over the intermediate fields such as
{\rm Tr}_{3}^{n}
or

{\rm Tr}_{d}^{n}
with
d|n
. Therefore, extending Proposition
5.2
to the trace map over the intermediate fields is
also desirable.

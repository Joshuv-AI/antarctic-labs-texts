Introduction

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
p
be an odd prime of good reduction for
E
. In the early 1970s, motivated by Iwasawa’s theory for the
p
-part of class groups of number fields in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions, Mazur
initiated a parallel study for the arithmetic of
E
over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
. In particular, in
[
Maz72
]
he proved a foundational “control theorem” for the
p
-primary Selmer group
{\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
, viewed as a module over the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket{{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rrbracket}
,
and formulated an analogue of Iwasawa’s main conjecture, expressing the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual of
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})={\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\vee}
in terms of the
p
-adic
L
-function attached to
E
by the work Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer
[
MSD74
]
:

(MC)

\ch_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\bigr)\overset{}{=}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})\bigr)

(see [
op. cit.
, Conj. 3]). By the Weierstrass preparation theorem, conjecture (
MC
) can be viewed as the equality between two integral
p
-adic polynomials attached to
E
, one by means of the arithmetic of
E
(i.e., its Mordell–Weil and Tate–Shafarevich groups)
over
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
and the other by
means of the modular symbols associated with
E
(available thanks to its modularity
[
Wil95
,
TW95
,
BCDT01
]
), encoding the special values of the Hasse–Weil
L
-function of
E
twisted by finite order characters of
{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
.

One of our main results in this paper is the proof (under a mild hypothesis) of Mazur’s main conjecture (
MC
) in the case where
p
is an

Eisenstein prime
for
E
, meaning that
E
admits a rational
p
-isogeny. Equivalently, we consider the case where
E[p]
is
reducible
as a
{\rm Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-module. (In the non-Eisenstein case, conjecture (
MC
) was proved under mild hypotheses in
[
Kat04
,
SU14
,
Wan15
]
.)

For Eisenstein primes
p
, Kato proved
[
Kat04
]
that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
is
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}
-torsion and that its characteristic ideal contains
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})
after inverting
p
. This ambiguity of powers of
p
was subsequently removed by Wüthrich
[
Wut14
]
.
On the other hand, in their foundational paper
[
GV00
]
, Greenberg–Vatsal proved conjecture (
MC
) under the assumption that the
isogeny character

\phi:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times},

i.e., the character describing the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}={\rm Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
on the kernel
C\subset E[p]
of the underlying rational
p
-isogeny, satisfies:

(GV)

\phi
is either unramified at
p
and odd, or ramified at
p
and even.

Under this hypothesis, they could show that both
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})
have vanishing
\mu
-invariant (building on the work of Ferrero–Washington
[
FW79
]
and the proof of Iwasawa’s main conjecture by Mazur–Wiles
[
MW84
]
), reducing their result on (
MC
) to a delicate comparison of Iwasawa
\lambda
-invariants.

Without hypothesis (
GV
), the situation is known to be more complicated. Indeed, Greenberg showed that if
E[p^{\infty}]
contains a
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-invariant cyclic subgroup
\Phi
of order
p^{m}
which is ramified at
p
and odd, then
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
has
\mu
-invariant
\geq m
(see
[
Gre99
, Prop. 5.7]
). On the analytic side, a conjecture by Stevens
[
Ste89
]
predicts a similar phenomenon for
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})
.
The methods in this paper allow us to prove Mazur’s main conjecture (
MC
) for Eisenstein primes regardless of the value of the
\mu
-invariant. In particular, our results include giving a new proof for the case previously handled by Greenberg–Vatsal.

Let
G_{p}\subset G_{\mathbb{Q}}
be a decomposition group at
p
and let
\omega:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
be the Teichmüller character. Our main result towards Mazur’s main conjecture is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
p>2
be an Eisenstein prime for
E
of good reduction. Suppose the isogeny character
\phi
satisfies
\phi|_{G_{p}}\neq 1,\omega
. Then
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
is
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}
-torsion with

\ch_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\bigr)\overset{}{=}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})\bigr),

and hence Mazur’s main conjecture holds.

Two key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
A
are the proof of an Iwasawa main conjecture for
E
over the
anticyclotomic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field
K/\mathbb{Q}
, and a special case of the cyclotomic Euler system of Beilinson–Flach classes originally constructed by Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes
[
KLZ17
]
, serving as a bridge between the cyclotomic and anticyclotomic settings. In the rest of this introduction we describe these ingredients in more detail and outline some of the ideas in our proofs.

0.1.
Anticyclotomic main conjectures

Denote by
N
the conductor of
E
, and let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field such that

(disc)

the discriminant
D_{K}
is odd and
D_{K}\neq-3
,

and such that the following
Heegner hypothesis
holds:

(Heeg)

every prime
\ell|N
splits in
K
.

Let
\Gamma_{K}^{-}={\rm Gal}(K^{-}_{\infty}/K)
be the Galois group of the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
, and
for each
n
denote by
K_{n}^{-}
the subfield of
K^{-}_{\infty}
with
[K_{n}^{-}:K]=p^{n}
. In sharp contrast with the case of
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
, one can show that
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(K_{n}^{-})
is unbounded as
n\to\infty
, and therefore the Pontryagin dual
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})

of the Selmer group
{\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})
has positive rank as a module over the anticyclotomic Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{K}^{-}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket{\Gamma_{K}^{-}}\rrbracket
. This unbounded growth is accounted for by the existence of Heegner points on
E
associated with a given modular parametrization

\pi:X_{0}(N)\rightarrow E.

These points give rise to a
\Lambda_{K}^{-}
-adic class
\kappa_{1}^{\rm Hg}
which was first shown to be non-torsion (as had been conjectured by Mazur) by Cornut–Vatsal
[
Cor02
,
Vat03
]
.
A formulation of the Iwasawa main conjecture in this context was given by Perrin-Riou
[
PR87a
]
. Write

\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{-}):=\mathop{\mathchoice{\varlim@\displaystyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\textstyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptstyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptscriptstyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}}\displaylimits_{n}\mathop{\mathchoice{\varlim@\displaystyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\textstyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptstyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptscriptstyle{\leftarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}}\displaylimits_{m}{\rm Sel}_{p^{m}}(E/K_{n}^{-}),

which is a compact
\Lambda_{K}^{-}
-module containing
\kappa_{1}^{\rm Hg}
.

Conjecture B
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve,
p>2
a prime of good ordinary reduction for
E
, and let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field satisfying
(
Heeg
)
and
(
disc
)
. Then
\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})
and
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})
both
have
\Lambda^{-}_{K}
-rank one, and

{\rm char}_{\Lambda^{-}_{K}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})_{\rm tors}\bigr)={\rm char}_{\Lambda^{-}_{K}}\bigl(\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})/(\kappa_{1}^{\rm Hg})\bigr)^{2}

where the subscript
tors
denotes the
\Lambda^{-}_{K}
-torsion submodule.

The first general result towards Conjecture
B
for Eisenstein primes
p
was obtained in
[
CGLS22
]
. Namely, it was shown (see [
op. cit.
, Cor. D]) that Perrin-Riou’s main conjecture holds for Eisenstein primes
p
under the additional hypotheses that

(spl)

(p)=v\bar{v}
splits in
K
,

that
\phi|_{G_{p}}\neq 1,\omega
, and that

(Sel)

\textrm{the $\mathbb{Z}_{p}$-corank of ${\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)$ is $1$}.

In particular, hypothesis (
Sel
)—which was harmless for the arithmetic applications in rank
\leq 1
studied in
[
CGLS22
]
, but becomes a serious restriction in an attempt to prove Mazur’s main conjecture building on a proof of Conjecture
B
—was imposed to account for the inability of the methods in [
op. cit.
, §3] to control certain error terms appearing at height one primes of
\Lambda_{K}^{-}
approaching the augmentation ideal
\mathfrak{P}_{0}=(\gamma^{-}-1)\subset\Lambda_{K}^{-}
.

A key technical innovation in this paper is the development of a Kolyvagin system argument allowing us to prove the “upper bound” divisibility predicted by Conjecture
B
also at height one primes of
\Lambda_{K}^{-}
near
\mathfrak{P}_{0}
, including
\mathfrak{P}_{0}
itself. The new arguments (contained in Sect.
5
) complement those developed in
[
CGLS22
, §3]
, and put together they yield the following result.

Theorem C
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, let
p>2
be an Eisenstein prime for
E
of good reduction, and let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field satisfying
(
Heeg
)
,
(
disc
)
, and
(
spl
)
. Suppose the isogeny character
\phi
satisfies

\phi|_{G_{p}}\neq 1,\omega
. Then Conjecture
B
holds.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\rm ur}
be the completion of the ring of integers of the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and put

\Lambda^{-,\rm ur}_{K}:=\Lambda_{K}^{-}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\rm ur}.

By the well-known equivalence between Conjecture
B
and the Iwasawa–Greenberg main conjecture for the
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm BDP}(f/K)
introduced in
[
BDP13
]
(see e.g.
[
BCK21
, Thm. 5.2]
), under the same hypotheses of Theorem
C
we deduce that the Greenberg Selmer group
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})
(with the relaxed and the strict local condition at the primes above
v
and
\bar{v}
, respectively) is
\Lambda_{K}^{-}
-torsion, with

{\rm char}_{\Lambda_{K}^{-}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(E/K_{\infty}^{-})\bigr)\Lambda_{K}^{-,\rm ur}=\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm BDP}(f/K)\bigr)

as ideals in
\Lambda_{K}^{-,\rm ur}
. This equality of characterisitc ideals is the first key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
A
.

0.2.
Congruences and Beilinson–Flach classes

In
[
KLZ17
]
,
Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes constructed a cyclotomic Euler system (over
\mathbb{Q}
) for the Rankin–Selberg convolution of two modular forms moving in Hida families. To be able to use these classes as a bridge between the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}^{-}/K
and the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
(or rather its translate by
K
), here we are led to consider a variant of their construction attached to the pair
(f,\bm{g})
, where
f
is the weight
2
newform attached to
E
, and
\bm{g}
is a suitable CM Hida family. Because our
\bm{g}
specializes in weight
1
to the
p
-irregular Eisenstein series
{\rm Eis}_{1,\eta}
,
where
\eta=\eta_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
is the quadratic character associated to
K/\mathbb{Q}
, in fact we use a refinement of the construction in
[
KLZ17
]
studied in
[
BST21
]
.

Let
\Lambda_{K}
(resp.
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
) be the Iwasawa algebra for the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
K
(resp. the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}^{+}/K
). In particular, from these works we obtain a two-variable Iwasawa cohomology class

BF\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\rm Iw}(K_{\infty},T_{p}E_{\bullet}),

where
E_{\bullet}
is the distinguished elliptic curve in the isogeny class of
E
constructed by Wüthrich
[
Wut14
]
. We also deduce two explicit reciprocity laws:

(1)

One relating
{\rm loc}_{\bar{v}}(BF)
to a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)\in\Lambda_{K}
whose image
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+}
under the natural projection
\Lambda_{K}\rightarrow\Lambda_{K}^{+}
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+}=\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E_{\bullet}/\mathbb{Q})\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E_{\bullet}^{K}/\mathbb{Q})

up to a unit, where
E_{\bullet}^{K}
is the twist of
E_{\bullet}
by the quadratic field
K
.

(2)

Another relating
{\rm loc}_{v}(BF)
to a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f/K)\in\Lambda_{K}^{\rm ur}=\Lambda_{K}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\rm ur}
whose image
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f/K)^{-}
under the natural projection
\Lambda_{K}^{\rm ur}\rightarrow\Lambda_{K}^{-,\rm ur}
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f/K)^{-}=\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm BDP}(f/K)

up to a unit.

With these results at hand,
one can give a simplified proof of Theorem
A
under some additional hypotheses, including hypothesis (
Sel
) for a suitably chosen
K
(of course, this condition cannot be arranged as long as
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(\mathbb{Q})\geq 2
).
As it helps clarify our overall strategy and motivates the more technical arguments that are needed in general, in Sect.
4
we explain the proof of Theorem
A
in this rank one case.

To treat the general case, we take a character
\alpha
of
\Gamma_{K}^{-}
with

\alpha\equiv 1\;({\rm mod}\,p^{m})

for some
m\gg 0
such that
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm BDP}(f/K)(\alpha)\neq 0
, and consider
\alpha
-twisted versions of the above Selmer groups and
p
-adic
L
-functions over
K
. With the aid of a cyclotomic Euler system extending the
\alpha
-twist of the class
BF
and the full strength of Theorem
C
, we prove that the twisted Selmer group
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

(0.1)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)=\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+}\bigr)

as ideals in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
. On the other hand, Kato’s divisibility
[
Kat04
]
(as refined by Wüthrich
[
Wut14
]
) applied to
E_{\bullet}
and
E_{\bullet}^{K}
implies that the untwisted Selmer group
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with the divisibility

(0.2)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+}\bigr)

as ideals in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
. By a congruence argument building on the earlier results in this paper,
we deduce from (
0.1
) that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+}
have
the same
Iwasawa invariant, and so equality holds in (
0.2
). The proof of Theorem
A
for
E
then follows easily from Kato’s work and the invariance of Mazur’s main conjecture under isogenies.

0.3.
Applications to the Birch and Swinnerton Dyer conjecture, and Kolyvagin’s conjecture

As a consequence of Theorem
A
, we deduce most cases of the
p
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton Dyer formula for elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
of analytic rank
\leq 1
at Eisenstein primes
p
.

Theorem D
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
and
p>2
be as in Theorem
A
, and let
r\in\{0,1\}
. If
{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=r
, then

\ord_{p}\biggl(\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{\operatorname{Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})\cdot\Omega_{E}}\biggr)=\ord_{p}\biggl(\#\text{\wncyr{W}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\prod_{\ell\nmid\infty}c_{\ell}(E/\mathbb{Q})\biggr),

where

•

{\rm Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is the regulator of
E(\mathbb{Q})
,

•

\Omega_{E}=\int_{E(\mathbb{R})}|\omega_{E}|
is the Néron period associated to the Néron differential
\omega_{E}
, and

•

c_{\ell}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is the Tamagawa number of
E
at the prime
\ell
.

In other words, the
p
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula for
E
holds.

Proof.

In the case
r=0
, the argument is the same as in
[
CGLS22
, Thm. 5.1.4]
, replacing the appeal to
[
GV00
, Thm. 1.3]
by an appeal to our Theorem
A
. In the case
r=1
, we argue as in
[
CGLS22
, Thm. 5.3.1]
, choosing a suitable
K
with
L(E^{K},1)\neq 0
and applying our result in the rank
0
case to
E^{K}
.
∎

Note that the previously known cases of Theorem
D
for analytic rank
r=0
(resp.
r=1
) required
E
(resp. an odd quadratic twist of
E
) to satisfy (
GV
), accounting for roughly only “half” of the possible cases.

It is easy to produce examples of elliptic curves to which Theorem
D
can be applied. Let
p=5
and
J
be any of the three non-isomorphic elliptic curves of conductor 11; one could take for example
the elliptic curve of Weierstrass equation

y^{2}+y=x^{3}-x^{2}-10x-20,

which is the strong Weil curve of conductor
11
.
It is known that
J[5]
is reducible and its semi-simplification is isomorphic to
\mu_{5}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, the rank of
J
is equal to zero. We can then consider
\psi
any quadratic character such that
\psi(5)=-1
and take
E=J_{\psi}
the quadratic twist of
J
by
\psi
. Since the root number of
J
is 1, applying
[
FH95
, Thm. B.1]
, we can produce infinitely many
\psi
such that
E=J_{\psi}
has analytic (and hence algebraic) rank zero and for which the hypothesis of Theorem
D
are satisfied for
p=5
. Note that prior to this paper, the only elliptic curves in this family for which the theorem was known are of the form
E=J_{\psi}
where
\psi
is odd.

Similarly, take
p=5
and consider the elliptic curve

J:y^{2}+y=x^{3}+x^{2}-10x+10.

The curve
J
has conductor
123
and analytic rank
1
, and satisfies
J[5]^{ss}=\mu_{5}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}
as
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-modules. Let
\psi
any quadratic character such that
\psi(5)=-1
and take
E=J_{\psi}
the quadratic twist of
J
by
\psi
. Since the root number of
J
is
-1
(being of analytic rank one), by
[
FH95
, Thm. B.2]
we can find infinitely many
\psi
as above for which
E=J_{\psi}
also has analytic rank one and hence to which Theorem
D
can be applied for
p=5
.
Earlier result in this direction would only apply to the elliptic curve in this family of the form
E=J_{\psi}
with
\psi
even.

One can proceed similarly for
p=3,7,13,37
, taking quadratic twists of elliptic curves of rank zero or one and with a rational
p
-isogeny. If the character describing the kernel of the isogeny is not trivial (which has to be the case for
p=13
or
p=37
), one might have to impose further conditions to the quadratic character at some bad primes in order to apply
[
FH95
, Thm. B]
.

As further application of Theorem
C
(and Theorem
A
respectively), we also point out that in
[
CGS23
]
we prove Kolyvagin’s conjecture about the non-vanishing of the Kolyvagin system of Heegner points (and the analogous statement for the Kolyvagin system obtained from Kato’s classes respectively) at Eisenstein primes. Crucial to the proof are the equalities in the main conjectures proved in this paper alongside the upper bound on the size of the Selmer group of certain twists of the Tate module of the elliptic curve in terms of the index of a Kolyvagin system class, which in the anticyclotomic setting we prove in Sect.
5
(Theorem
5.1.1
).

0.4.
Outline of the paper

We begin in Sect.
1
by introducing the different one- and two-variable
p
-adic
L
-functions, and various relations between them, that will be needed for our arguments. Then in Sect.
2
we introduce corresponding Selmer groups, both primitive and imprimitive, and prove some ancillary results for the latter ones. In Sect.
3
we discuss Beilinson–Flach classes and some applications, including an equivalence between three different Iwasawa main conjectures. In Sect.
4
we illustrate our general strategy by outlining a proof of Theorem
A
under some additional hypotheses (including (
Sel
) for a suitably chosen
K
), for which the earlier results in this paper and those in
[
CGLS22
]
are sufficient. In Sect.
5
we prove the key Theorem
5.1.1
for the
p
-adic Tate module of
E
twisted by characters
\alpha
of
\Gamma_{K}^{-}
that are close to
1
. The result is an upper bound on the size of the Selmer group in terms of the index of a Kolyvagin system class with an ‘error term’ that remains bounded as
\alpha
approaches
1
. The proof of Theorem
C
then follows easily. Finally, in Sect.
6
we put everything together to conclude the proof of Theorem
A
.

Let

be an elliptic curve of conductor

be an odd prime of good ordinary reduction for

We denote by

(resp.
\Gamma_{K}^{-}
) is identified with the Galois group of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}^{+}/K
(resp. the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K^{-}_{\infty}/K
), and
hence
\Gamma^{+}_{K}

\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}

\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket{\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}}\rrbracket,\quad\Lambda_{K}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket{\Gamma_{K}}\rrbracket,\quad\Lambda_{K}^{\pm}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma_{K}^{\pm}\rrbracket

be the corresponding Iwasawa algebras, so in particular

1.1.
Cyclotomic
p
-adic
L
-function

Given a modular parametrization
\pi_{E}:X_{0}(N)\rightarrow E
, we denote by
c_{E}\in\mathbb{Z}
the corresponding Manin constant, so that

\pi_{E}^{*}(\omega_{E})=c_{E}\cdot 2\pi if(\tau)d\tau,

where
\omega_{E}
is a minimal differential on
E
and
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is the newform associated with
E
. Pick generators
\delta^{\pm}
of
H_{1}(E,\mathbb{Z})^{\pm}
, and define the Néron periods
\Omega_{E}^{\pm}
by

\Omega_{E}^{\pm}=\int_{\delta^{\pm}}\omega_{E}.

We normalize the
\delta^{\pm}

so that
\Omega_{E}^{+}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\Omega_{E}^{-}\in i\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

The Fourier coefficient
a_{p}
is a
p
-adic unit by hypothesis, and we let
\alpha_{p}
be the
p
-adic unit root of
x^{2}-a_{p}x+p
.

Theorem 1.1.1
.

There exists an element
\mathcal{L}^{\rm MSD}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that for any finite order character
\chi
of
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
of conductor
p^{r}
with
r>0
, we have

\mathcal{L}^{\rm MSD}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})(\chi)=\frac{p^{r}}{\tau(\overline{\chi})\alpha_{p}^{r}}\cdot\frac{L(E,\overline{\chi},1)}{\Omega_{E}^{+}},

where
\tau(\overline{\chi})=\sum_{a\;{\rm mod}\;p^{r}}\overline{\chi}(a)e^{2\pi ia/p^{r}}
is the Gauss sum, and

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})(1)=\bigl(1-\alpha_{p}^{-1}\bigr)^{2}\cdot\frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_{E}^{+}}.

Proof.

The construction of
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})
as an element in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with the stated interpolation property is given in
[
MSD74
, §9]
.
The integrality of
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is shown in
[
GV00
, Prop. 3.7]
in the case where
E[p]
is irreducible as a
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-module, and in
[
Wut14
, Cor. 18]
in the reducible case. Since the latter integrality result and some of the ingredients in the proof will be important later, we briefly outline the argument. First, Wüthrich shows the existence of an elliptic curve
E_{\bullet}/\mathbb{Q}
in the isogeny class of
E
with
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E_{\bullet}/\mathbb{Q})\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}
and whose
p
-adic Tate module satisfies

T_{p}E_{\bullet}\simeq V_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(f)(1),

where
V_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(f)
is the geometric lattice in the
p
-adic Galois representation
V_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(f)
associated to
f
considered in
[
Kat04
, §8.3]
. Building on the theorem of Ferrero–Washington
[
FW79
]
, Kato’s divisibility in the Iwasawa main conjecture for
E_{\bullet}
“without zeta functions”
[
Kat04
, Thm. 12.5(4)]
is shown to be integral, from which the integrality of
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E_{\bullet}/\mathbb{Q})
and of
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})
itself follows by global duality and the invariance of Mazur’s main conjecture under isogenies.
∎

1.2.
Two-variable
p
-adic
L
-function, I

In this section we recall the
p
-adic
L
-function constructed in
[
PR88
]
following Hida’s
p
-adic Rankin method
[
Hid85
]
, and explain the relation with the
p
-adic
L
-function of Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer.

Let
\Sigma
be the set of infinity types of Hecke characters
\psi
of
K
for which
s=1
is a critical value of the Rankin
L
-function
L(f/K,\psi,s)
. Following the notations and conventions in
[
LLZ15
, §6.1]
, the set
\Sigma
decomposes as

\Sigma=\Sigma^{(1)}\cup\Sigma^{(2)}\cup\Sigma^{(2^{\prime})},

where
\Sigma^{(1)}=\{(0,0)\}
(i.e., corresponding to characters
\psi
of finite order),
\Sigma^{(2)}=\{(a,b)\colon a\leq-1,b\geq 1\}
, and
\Sigma^{(2^{\prime})}=\{(b,a)\colon a\leq-1,b\geq 1\}
. Note that the regions
\Sigma^{(2)}
and
\Sigma^{(2^{\prime})}
are interchanged by the involution
\psi\mapsto\psi^{\tau}
, where
\psi^{\tau}
denotes the composition of
\psi
with the non-trivial automorphism
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
.

For
\psi
a finite order Hecke character of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{c}
, denote by
\theta(\psi)
the associated theta series, which is an eigenform of weight one and level
M=D_{K}\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{c})
. As in
[
PR87b
, p. 457]
, define the “Artin root number” of
\psi
to be the complex number
W(\psi)
with
|W(\psi)|=1
given by

\theta(\psi)|_{1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&-1\\
M&\end{smallmatrix}\right)=-iW(\psi)\cdot\theta(\psi);

then the completed
L
-function
\Lambda(\psi,s)=M^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\psi(\mathfrak{a})\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{a})^{-s}
satisfies the functional equation
\Lambda(\psi,s)=W(\psi)\Lambda(\overline{\psi},1-s)
. As before, let
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be the newform associated with
E
, and put

H_{p}(f)=\Bigl(1-\frac{p}{\alpha_{p}^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{p}^{2}}\Bigr).

Denote by
c_{f}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
the (cuspidal)
congruence number
of
f
, as defined in e.g.
[
Hid81
, §7]
or
[
Rib83
]
.

Theorem 1.2.1
.

There exists an element
\mathcal{L}_{p}(f/K,\Sigma^{(1)})\in c_{f}^{-1}\Lambda_{K}
such that for every finite order character
\psi
of
\Gamma_{K}
of conductor
v^{m}\bar{v}^{n}
with
m+n>0
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{p}(f/K,\Sigma^{(1)})(\psi)=\frac{W(\psi)p^{(m+n)/2}}{\alpha_{p}^{m+n}H_{p}(f)}\cdot\frac{L(f/K,\overline{\psi},1)}{8\pi^{2}\langle f,f\rangle_{N}},

where
W(\psi)
is the Artin root number of
\psi
, and

\mathcal{L}_{p}(f/K,\Sigma^{(1)})(1)=\frac{(1-\alpha_{p}^{-1})^{4}}{H_{p}(f)}\cdot\frac{L(f/K,1)}{8\pi^{2}\langle f,f\rangle_{N}},

where
\langle f,g\rangle_{N}=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathcal{H}}\overline{f(\tau)}g(\tau)dxdy
is the Petersson inner product on
S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
.

Proof.

This is a reformulation of
[
PR87b
, Thm. 1.1]
. For our later use, we note that this is the same as the two-variable
p
-adic
L
-function

L_{p}(f,\bm{g})\in c_{f}^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\widehat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\bm{g}}\llbracket\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\rrbracket

obtained by specializing to
f
the three-variable
p
-adic Rankin
L
-series in
[
KLZ17
, Thm. 7.7.2]
, where
\bm{g}
is the CM Hida family introduced in the proof of Lemma
1.4.4
below.
∎

Definition 1.2.2

(“Perrin-Riou’s
p
-adic
L
-function”)
.

Let
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be the newform associated to
E
, and let
\pi_{E}:X_{0}(N)\rightarrow E
be a modular parametrization. Then we
put

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E/K):=\frac{\deg(\pi_{E})}{c_{E}^{2}}\cdot H_{p}(f)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p}(f/K,\Sigma^{(1)}),

where
c_{E}
is the Manin constant associated to
\pi_{E}
.

Remark 1.2.3
.

Note that the factor
H_{p}(f)
can be interpreted as an Euler factor coming from the adjoint
L
-function of
f
(see
[
KLZ20
, Rem. 6.5.10]
). Moreover, setting

\Omega_{E/K}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|D_{K}|}}\int_{E(\mathbb{C})}\omega_{E}\wedge i\,\overline{\omega_{E}}

we find

8\pi^{2}\langle f,f\rangle_{N}=\int_{X_{0}(N)}\omega_{f}\wedge i\,\overline{\omega_{f}}=\frac{\deg(\pi_{E})}{c_{E}^{2}}\cdot\Omega_{E/K},

and so by Theorem
1.2.1
the
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E/K)
interpolates the finite order twists of
L(f/K,1)
normalized by the complex period
\Omega_{E/K}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{L}^{\rm PR}_{p}(E/K)^{+}\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}
the image of
\mathcal{L}^{\rm PR}_{p}(E/K)
under the map induced by the projection
\Gamma_{K}\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma_{K}^{+}\simeq\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proposition 1.2.4
.

Up to a unit in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E/K)^{+}=\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})\cdot\mathcal{L}^{\rm MSD}_{p}(E^{K}/\mathbb{Q}),

where
E^{K}
is the twist of
E
by the quadratic character corresponding to
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character of conductor
p^{r}
. Using the standard formula

W(\chi\circ\mathbf{N})=\chi(|D_{K}|)\frac{\tau(\chi)^{2}}{p^{r}}=\chi(|D_{K}|)\frac{p^{r}}{\tau(\overline{\chi})^{2}}

(see e.g.
[
Miy06
, Lem. 4.8.1]
) and the factorization

L(f/K,\overline{\chi}\circ\mathbf{N},1)=L(E,\overline{\chi},1)\cdot L(E^{K},\overline{\chi},1),

from Theorem
1.1.1
and Theorem
1.2.1
we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p}(f/K,\Sigma^{(1)})(\chi\circ\mathbf{N})\cdot H_{p}(f)

\displaystyle=W(\chi\circ\mathbf{N})\cdot\frac{p^{r}}{\alpha_{p}^{2r}}\cdot L(f/K,\overline{\chi}\circ\mathbf{N},1)

\displaystyle=\chi(|D_{K}|)\cdot\frac{p^{2r}}{\tau(\overline{\chi})\alpha_{p}^{2r}}\cdot L(E,\overline{\chi},1)\cdot L(E^{K},\overline{\chi},1)

\displaystyle=\chi(|D_{K}|)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})(\chi)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E^{K}/\mathbb{Q})(\chi)\cdot\frac{\Omega_{E}^{+}\cdot\Omega_{E^{K}}^{+}}{8\pi^{2}\langle f,f\rangle_{N}}.

Since the complex period
\Omega_{E/K}
in Remark
1.3.2
satisfies

\Omega_{E/K}=[E(\mathbb{R}):E^{0}(\mathbb{R})]\cdot\Omega_{E}^{+}\cdot\Omega_{E^{K}}^{+}

(see
[
GZ86
, §V.2]
), the result in now clear from the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E/K)
.
∎

Definition 1.4.3

(“Greenberg’s

Lemma 1.4.4
.

The

-adic

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f/K)\in\Lambda_{K}^{\rm ur}

Denote by

, and let

Since

Proposition 1.4.5
.

We have the equality

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f/K)^{-}\cdot\Lambda_{K}^{-,\rm{ur}}=\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm BDP}(f/K)\cdot\Lambda_{K}^{-,\rm{ur}}.

Proof.

This follows from a direct comparison of the interpolation formulas in Theorem
1.4.1
, Theorem
1.3.1
and Theorem

, together with an application of Dirichlet’s class number formula (cf.
[

Indeed, let
\xi

(n,-n)
,

. Then the character

, and noting that

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\bar{v}}(K)(\xi^{1-\tau}\mathbf{N}^{-1})=\frac{\Omega_{p}^{4n}}{\Omega_{\infty}^{4n}}\cdot\Gamma(2n+1)\cdot\biggl(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{D_{K}}}\biggr)^{2n-1}\cdot(1-\xi^{1-\tau}(\bar{v}))(1-\xi^{1-\tau}({v})p^{-1})\cdot L(\xi^{1-\tau},1).

2.2.1.

As observed by Greenberg, imprimitive Selmer groups as above tend to have better properties (e.g. with respect to congruences) than their primitive counterparts. Here we shall also find it convenient to work first with imprimitive Selmer group, hence our interest in the next two results.

Proposition 2.2.2
.

Assume that
E(K)[p]=0
and that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion. Then for any

consisting of primes away from

, the Selmer group

\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

where
\mathcal{P}_{w}^{+}(1)\in\Lambda_{K}^{+}
is as in Definition
1.5.2
, with
\alpha=1
.

Proof.

From the assumption that
E(K)[p]=0
, we see that the

-invariants of
{\rm Hom}(T_{p}E,\mu_{p^{\infty}})
are trivial, and so by
[

the global-to-local map defining
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord}}(K,T_{p}E\otimes(\Lambda_{K}^{+})^{\vee})
as in (

(2.3)

the module

\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathrm{H}^{1}(K_{w},T_{p}E\otimes(\Lambda_{K}^{+})^{\vee})^{\vee}\bigr)=\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{w}^{+}(1)\bigr).

The result now follows by taking characteristic ideals in (
2.3
).
∎

Corollary 2.2.3
.

Assume that
E(K)[p]=0
and let

be any subset consisting of primes away from

Proof.

Since for any prime

and Proposition
2.2.2
.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Assume
E(K)[p]=0

(iii)

Since the

Put

Proposition 2.3.4

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

K_{\infty}^{+}/K

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(E(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+}):=\mathfrak{X}_{\rm rel,\rm str}(E(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+}),\quad\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}(E(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}}(K,T_{p}E(\alpha)\otimes\Lambda_{K}^{+}),

etc.. Write
BF_{\alpha}^{+}
for the image of the class
BF_{\alpha}
of Theorem
3.1.1
under the natural projection

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}}(K,(T_{p}E_{\bullet})(\alpha)\widehat{\otimes}\Lambda_{K})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}}(K,(T_{p}E_{\bullet})(\alpha)\widehat{\otimes}\Lambda_{K}^{+}),

so we have
BF_{\alpha}^{+}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
.

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Suppose
E_{\bullet}(K)[p]=0
and that
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+}
are both nonzero. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
has
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-rank one,
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord,\rm str}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha^{-1})/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, and

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord,\rm str}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha^{-1})/K_{\infty}^{+}\bigr)\supset\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})/\Lambda_{K}^{+}BF_{\alpha}^{+}\bigr).

(ii)

\mathfrak{S}_{\rm str,\rm rel}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
and
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
are both
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, and

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\Lambda_{K}^{+,\rm ur}\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+}\bigr).

(iii)

\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
and
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
are both
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, and

\ch_{\Lambda^{+}_{K}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+}\bigr).

The same result holds for the opposite divisibilities.

Proof.

This is a well-known consequence of Poitou–Tate duality and the reciprocity laws of Theorem
3.1.1
, but we provide the details for the convenience of the reader. Below we set
\mathfrak{S}_{\rm str,\rm rel}=\mathfrak{S}_{\rm str,\rm rel}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
,
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
, etc. for the ease of notation; and similarly,
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(\alpha^{-1})=\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha^{-1})/K_{\infty}^{+})
,
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(\alpha^{-1})=\mathfrak{X}_{\Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha^{-1})/K_{\infty}^{+})
, etc.. Note that, since
\alpha^{\tau}=\alpha^{-1}
and
(T_{p}E_{0}\otimes(\Lambda_{K}^{+})^{\vee})^{\tau}\simeq T_{p}E_{0}\otimes(\Lambda_{K}^{+})^{\vee}
, the action of complex conjugation gives rise to isomorphisms of
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-modules:

(3.1)

\mathfrak{X}_{\Gr}(\alpha^{-1})\simeq\mathfrak{X}_{\Gr},\ \ \ \mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(\alpha^{-1})\simeq\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}\ \ \ \mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord,str}(\alpha^{-1})\simeq\mathfrak{X}_{\rm str,ord}.

For the equivalence
{\rm(i)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(ii)}
consider the exact sequence

(3.2)

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{\rm str,\rm rel}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\rm ord}(K_{v},T_{p}E_{\bullet}(\alpha)\otimes\Lambda_{K}^{+})\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(\alpha^{-1})\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord,\rm str}(\alpha^{-1})\rightarrow 0.

In both cases, we see that
\mathfrak{S}_{\rm str,\rm rel}
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, hence trivial (by the assumption
E_{\bullet}(K)[p]=0
), and (
3.2
) yields the exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}/\Lambda_{K}^{+}BF^{+}_{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\rm ord}(K_{v},T_{p}E_{\bullet}(\alpha)\otimes\Lambda_{K}^{+})/\Lambda_{K}^{+}\loc_{v}(BF_{\alpha}^{+})\to\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(\alpha^{-1})\to\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord,\rm str}(\alpha^{-1})\to 0.

Since by Corollary
3.1.3
the second term in this exact sequence is pseudo-isomorphic—via the map
{\rm Col}_{\bm{g}}
—to
\Lambda_{K}^{+,\rm ur}/(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+})
, the equivalence
{\rm(i)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(ii)}
follows from this, the multiplicativity of characteristic ideals, and the isomorphisms (
3.1
). On the other hand, for the equivalence
{\rm(i)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(iii)}
consider the exact sequence

(3.3)

Remark 5.3.2
.

The primary difference between Proposition
5.3.1
and
[
CGLS22
, Prop. 3.3.6]
is that here we have restricted ourselves to the
\mathfrak{m}^{m}
-torsion of the Selmer groups of

(see (
5.3
)) and so we can directly work with the eigenspaces of complex conjugation.
For the proof of
loc. cit.
we worked over an extension trivialising the character
\alpha\mod\varpi^{k}
and then used “some quadratic forms” to
estimate the linear independence of the images of the localisations of the classes. The upshot is that our error term no longer involves
the

of
[

]
. This is crucial for removing the corank one assumption in the proof of the anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture in
op. cit.
. However it causes some complications in the proof of Theorem
5.1.1
: we use the
\mathfrak{m}^{m}
-Selmer groups to control the
image of the localisation at Kolyvagin primes of classes in the
\mathfrak{m}^{k}
-Selmer groups, and the resulting control is not as tight as in
[
CGLS22
]
.

The strategy to produce the

we can show this easily.

Lemma 6.1.1
.

With
\alpha
chosen as above, the
p
-adic
L
-functions
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+}
are both nonzero.

Proof.

For
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+}
, this is clear from (
6.1
) and the congruence of Lemma
1.5.1
; and for
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+}
it follows from the relations

(6.3)

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+}(0)=\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{-}(0)\,\sim_{p}\,\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm BDP}(f(\alpha)/K)(0),

using Proposition
1.4.5
for the last equality up to a
p
-adic unit.
∎

In light of this nonvanishing, by Corollary
3.1.3
the class
BF_{\alpha}^{+}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
is nonzero, and so by Theorem
3.3.1
the Selmer group
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord,\rm str}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha^{-1})/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

(6.4)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord,\rm str}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha^{-1})/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{S}_{\rm ord,\rm rel}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})/\Lambda_{K}^{+}BF_{\alpha}^{+}\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Note the need to invert
p
in this divisibility, an ambiguity that we shall remove in the next result.

Lemma 6.1.2
.

The module
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

(6.5)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+}\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
.

Proof.

Directly from the combination of Proposition
3.2.1
, Lemma
6.1.1
, and (
6.4
)
we get that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with the claimed divisibility holding in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
S=\Sigma\smallsetminus\{p,\infty\}
. By Corollary
2.2.3
, it follows that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
is also
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

(6.6)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+,S}\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\in\Lambda_{K}^{+}
a characteristic power series for
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
, so from the above we have

(6.7)

\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\cdot h=\varpi^{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+,S}

for some
h\in\Lambda_{K}^{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
k<0
there is nothing to show, so assume
k\geq 0
. From Kato’s divisibility
[
Kat04
, Thm. 17.4]
(refined to an integral statement as in
[
Wut14
, Thm. 16]
), Proposition
1.2.4
, and Proposition
2.2.1
we have that the untwisted Selmer group
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with the integral divisibility

(6.8)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+}\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
, and so from Proposition
2.2.2
we get that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
is also
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, and we have the integral divisibility

(6.9)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+,S}\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
. By the congruences of Proposition
2.3.4
and Lemma
1.5.1
, it follows from (
6.12
) that for
\alpha
sufficiently close to
1
(i.e. taking
m\gg 0
above) the
\mu
-invariant of
\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
is at most that of
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+,S}
. Thus from (
6.7
) we see that
h

\varpi^{k}
, and therefore the divisibility (
6.6
) holds in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
. Together with Corollary
2.2.3
, this yields the result.
∎

From the preceding two lemmas and Proposition
3.2.1
, we deduce that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

(6.10)

\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{\rm Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+}\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{K}^{+,\rm ur}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\rm Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\in\Lambda_{K}^{+}
is any characteristic power series for
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})

\mathcal{F}_{\Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})(0)\,\sim_{p}\,\mathcal{F}_{\Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{-})(0)\,\sim_{p}\,\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm BDP}(f(\alpha)/K)(0)\,\sim_{p}\,\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{-}(0)\neq 0

following from Proposition

, relation (

), and Proposition

by easy commutative algebra (see

\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{\rm Gr}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)=\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm Gr}(f(\alpha)/K)^{+}\bigr).

(Note that conditions (a) and (b) in Lemma

needed for the above application of Proposition

with

In particular, for
\alpha
as above sufficiently close to

that

is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)=\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+}\bigr).

Step 3
.
We are now in a position to prove Conjecture
2.1.3
for
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{+})
.

Theorem 6.1.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and
p>2
a prime of good reduction for
E
such that
E[p]^{ss}=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\phi)\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi)

with
\phi|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\neq 1,\omega
.
Then module
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, with

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/K_{\infty}^{+}))=(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E/K)^{+}).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1.4
, it suffices to prove the result of
E_{\bullet}
. Let
S=\Sigma\smallsetminus\{p,\infty\}
. As shown above, from Kato’s work we can deduce that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
is
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, and we have the integral divisibility

(6.12)

\ch_{\Lambda_{K}^{+}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+,S}\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
. Take a character
\alpha:\Gamma_{K}^{-}\rightarrow R^{\times}
with

\alpha\equiv 1\;({\rm mod}\,\varpi^{m})

for some
m\gg 0
so that the equality (
6.11
) holds. (Note that the argument in
Step 1
and
Step 2
leading to that equality only excludes finitely many
\alpha
.) By Corollary
2.2.3
it follows that
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
is also
\Lambda_{K}^{+}
-torsion, and denoting by
\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
and
\mathcal{F}^{S}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\in\Lambda_{K}^{+}
characteristic power series for
\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
and
\mathfrak{X}^{S}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})
, respectively, we have

(6.13)

\mathcal{F}^{S}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})\equiv\mathcal{F}^{S}_{\rm ord}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K_{\infty}^{+})\equiv\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}(\alpha)/K)^{+,S}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+,S}\;({\rm mod}\,\varpi^{m}),

as a consequence of Proposition
2.3.4
, the combination of (
6.11
) and Corollary
2.2.3
, and Lemma
1.5.1
, respectively. Taking
m\gg 0
, it follows from the congruence (
6.13
) that
\mathcal{F}_{\rm ord}^{S}(E_{\bullet}/K_{\infty}^{+})
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm PR}(E_{\bullet}/K)^{+,S}
have
the same
Iwasawa invariants
\lambda
and
\mu
, and so equality holds in (
6.12
). By Corollary
2.2.3
, this yields the proof of the theorem.
∎

The proof of Mazur’s main conjeture for
E
now follows easily.

Proof of Theorem
6.0.1
.

Choose an imaginary quadratic field
K
satisfying hypotheses (
disc
), (
Heeg
), and (
spl
). As before, from
[
Kat04
]
and
[
Wut14
]
we have the divisibilities

\ch_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q})\bigr),\quad\ch_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E^{K}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\bigr)\supset\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E^{K}/\mathbb{Q})\bigr)

in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}
. If
\ch_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}))\neq(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q}))

\ch_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\rm ord}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}))=(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\rm MSD}(E/\mathbb{Q}))

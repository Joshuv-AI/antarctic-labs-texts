Convergence Rates of Two-Time-Scale Gradient Descent-Ascent Dynamics for Solving Nonconvex Min-Max Problems
Thinh T. Doan

Abstract
There are much recent interests in solving noncovnex min-max optimization problems due to its broad applications in many areas including machine learning, networked resource allocations, and distributed optimization. Perhaps, the most popular first-order method in solving min-max optimization is the so-called simultaneous (or single-loop) gradient descent-ascent algorithm due to its simplicity in implementation. However, theoretical guarantees on the convergence of this algorithm is very sparse since it can diverge even in a simple bilinear problem. In this paper, our focus is to characterize the finite-time performance (or convergence rates) of the continuous-time variant of simultaneous gradient descent-ascent algorithm. In particular, we derive the rates of convergence of this method under a number of different conditions on the underlying objective function, namely, two-sided Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PŁ), one-sided PŁ, nonconvex-strongly concave, and strongly convex-nonconcave conditions. Our convergence results improve the ones in prior works under the same conditions of objective functions. The key idea in our analysis is to use the classic singular perturbation theory and coupling Lyapunov functions to address the time-scale difference and interactions between the gradient descent and ascent dynamics. Our results on the behavior of continuous-time algorithm may be used to enhance the convergence properties of its discrete-time counterpart.

In the existing literature, there are two types of iterative first-order methods for solving problem (

On the other hand, single-loop algorithm simultaneously updates the iterates

Our focus in this paper is to study the continuous-time variant of the single-loop gradient descent-ascent method for solving problem (

Considering the continuous-time variant will help us to have a better understanding about the behavior of this method through studying the convergence of the corresponding differential equations using Lyapunov theory. Main Contributions.
The focus of this paper is to study the performance of the continuous-time gradient descent-ascent dynamics in solving nonconvex min-max optimization problems. In particular, we derive the rates of convergence of this method under a number of different conditions on the underlying objective function, namely, two-sided Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PŁ), one-sided PŁ, nonconvex-strongly concave, and strongly convex-nonconcave conditions. 2
Two-Time-Scale Gradient Descent-Ascent Dynamics

For solving problem (
1
), we are interested in studying two-time-scale gradient descent-ascent dynamics (GDAD), where we implement simultaneously the following two differential equations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{x}(t)&=\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=-\alpha\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t)),\\
\dot{y}(t)&=\frac{d}{dt}y(t)=\beta\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t)),\end{aligned}

(2)

Here,
\alpha,\beta
are two step sizes, whose values will be specified later. In the convex-concave setting, one can choose
\alpha=\beta
. However, as observed in
[
68
]
, choosing different step sizes achieves a better convergence in the context of nonconvex problem. Indeed, we will choose
\alpha\ll\beta
since in our settings studied in the following sections, the maximization problem is often easier to solve than the minimization problem. In this case, the dynamic of
y(t)
is implemented at a faster time scale (using larger step sizes) than
x(t)
(using smaller step sizes). The time-scale difference is loosely defined as the ratio
\alpha/\beta\ll 1
. Thus, one has to design these two step sizes properly so that the method converges as fast as possible.

Technical Approach.
The convergence analysis of (
2
) studied in this paper is mainly motivated by the classic singular perturbation theory
[
69
]
. The main idea of our approach can be explained as follows. Since
y
is implemented at a faster time scale than
x
, one can consider
x(t)=x
being fixed in
\dot{y}
and separately study the stability of the system
\dot{y}
using Lyapunov theory. Let
V_{2}
be the Lyapunov function corresponding to
\dot{y}
. When
\dot{y}
converges to an equilibrium
y
(e.g.,
\nabla_{y}f(x,y)=0
), one can fix
y(t)=y
and study the stability of
\dot{x}
. Let
V_{1}
be the corresponding Lyapunov function of
\dot{x}
. We note that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
both depend on
x
and
y
, as a result, their time derivatives are coupled through the dynamics in (
2
). Addressing this coupling and the time-scale difference between the two dynamics is the key idea in our approach. To do that, we will consider the following Lyapunov function

\displaystyle V(x,y)=V_{1}(x,y)+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{\beta}V_{2}(x,y),

(3)

where
\alpha/\beta
represents the time-scale difference, while the constant
\gamma
will be properly chosen to eliminate the impact of
x
on the convergence of
y
and vice versa. Proper choices of these constants will also help us to derive the convergence rates of (
2
). Similar approach has been used in different settings of two-time-scale methods, see for example
[
53
,
66
]
.

We conclude this section by introducing two assumptions for our analysis studied later.

Assumption 1
.

The function
f(\cdot,\cdot)
has Lipschitz continuous gradients for each variable, i.e., there exist positive constants

,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\nabla_{x}f(x_{1},y_{1})-\nabla_{x}f(x_{2},y_{2})\|&\leq L_{x}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|+L_{xy}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|,\\
\|\nabla_{y}f(x_{1},y_{1})-\nabla_{y}f(x_{2},y_{2})\|&\leq L_{xy}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|+L_{y}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|.\end{aligned}

(4)

Assumption 2
.

Given any
x
the problem
\max_{y}f(x,y)
has a nonempty solution set
{\cal Y}^{\star}(x)
, i.e., there exists

such that

\displaystyle y^{\star}(x)=\arg\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x,y),\quad\text{where }f(x,y^{\star}(x))\text{ is finite}.

3.1
Two-Sided Polyak–Łojasiewicz Conditions

We first study the convergence rates of GDAD when
f
satisfies a two-sided Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PŁ) condition, which is considered in
[
36
]
and stated here for convenience.

Definition 1

(Two-Sided PŁ Conditions)
.

A continuously differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called to satisfy two-sided PŁ conditions if there exist two positive constants
\mu_{x}
and
\mu_{y}
such that
\mu_{x},\mu_{y}\leq\min\{L_{x},L_{y},L_{xy}\}
the following conditions hold for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 2\mu_{x}[f(x,y)-\min_{x}f(x,y)]&\leq\|\nabla_{x}f(x,y)\|^{2},\\
2\mu_{y}[\max_{y}f(x,y)-f(x,y)]&\leq\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\|^{2}.\end{aligned}

(5)

The two-sided PŁ condition, which we will assume to hold in this subsection, is a generalized variant of the popular PŁ condition, proposed by
[
70
]
as a sufficient condition to guarantee that the classic gradient descent method converges exponentially to the optimal value of an unconstrained minimization problem. As shown in
[
71
]
, the PŁ condition also implies the quadratic growth condition, i.e., given any
x
we have

\displaystyle\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x,z)-f(x,y)\geq\frac{\mu_{y}}{2}\|{\cal P}_{{\cal Y}^{\star}(x)}[y]-y\|^{2},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(6)

where we assume that
{\cal Y}^{\star}(x)
is a nonempty solution set of
\max_{y}f(x,y)
and
{\cal P}_{{\cal Y}^{\star}(x)}[y]
is the projection of
y
to this set. More discussions on PŁ condition can be found in
[
71
]
, while some examples of functions satisfying the two-sided PŁ condition are given in
[
36
]
.

Our focus in this section is to show that GDAD converges exponentially to the global min-max solution
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
of
f
under the two-sided PŁ condition. To do that, we consider the following assumption and lemmas, which are useful for our analysis considered later. We first consider an assumption on the existence of
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
, a global min-max solution of
f
.

Assumption 3
.

There exists a global min-max solution
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
of
f
, i.e.,

\displaystyle x^{\star}=\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x,y^{\star})\quad\text{and}\quad y^{\star}=\arg\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x^{\star},y).

Next, we consider the following lemma about the Lipschitz continuity of the gradient of
f(x,y^{\star}(x))
, which is a variant of the well-known Danskin lemma
[
72
]
[Proposition B.25] and studied in
[
33
]
[Lemma A.5].

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
–
3
hold. Then, the function
\max_{y}f(x,y)
is differentiable and its gradient
\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{\star}(x))
is Lipschitz continuous with a constant
L_{x}+\frac{L_{xy}}{\mu_{y}}
.

Finally, for our analysis we consider the following two Lyapunov functions

\displaystyle V_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x,y)-\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x,y),

(7)

\displaystyle V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\max_{y}f(x,y)-f(x,y),

(8)

where it is obvious to see that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are nonnegative. The time derivatives of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
over the trajectories
\dot{x}
and
\dot{y}
are given in the following lemma, whose proof can be found in Section
4.1
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
–
3
hold. Then we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{\star}(x))\|^{2}+\frac{L_{xy}^{2}\alpha}{\mu_{y}}V_{2}(x,y).

(9)

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq-\beta\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\|^{2}+\frac{3\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{\star}(x))\|^{2}+\frac{5L_{xy}^{2}\alpha}{\mu_{y}}V_{2}(x,y).

(10)

As mentioned, the dynamics of
\dot{x}
and
\dot{y}
are implemented at different time scales, where this difference is often loosely defined as the ratio
\beta/\alpha>1
. To capture such time-scale difference in our analysis, we will utilize the coupling Lyapunov function defined in (
3
). We denote by
\mu=\min\{\mu_{x},\mu_{y}\}
and the condition number

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{L_{xy}}{\mu}\geq 1.

(11)

representing the condition number of
f(x,y)
. The convergence rate of GDAD under the two-sided PŁ condition is formally stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
–
3
hold. Let
\gamma,\alpha,\beta
be chosen as

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{L_{xy}^{2}}{\mu_{y}^{2}},\quad\alpha=\frac{\mu^{2}}{10\mu_{x}L_{xy}^{2}},\quad\beta=\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu_{x}\mu_{y}^{2}}\cdot

(12)

Then we have for all
t\geq 0

\displaystyle V(x(t),y(t))\leq e^{-\frac{t}{20\kappa^{2}}}V(x(0),y(0)).

(13)

Proof.

By (
5
) we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu_{y}[\max_{y}f(x,y)-f(x,y)]=2\mu_{y}V_{2}(x,y).

Thus, by using (
9
), (
10
), (
3
), and the preceding relation we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=\dot{V}_{1}(x(t))+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{\beta}\dot{V}_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y^{\star}(x(t)))\|^{2}+\frac{L_{xy}^{2}\alpha}{\mu_{y}}V_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\quad-2\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha V_{2}(x(t),y(t))+\frac{3\gamma\alpha^{2}}{2\beta}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y^{\star}(x(t)))\|^{2}+\frac{5L_{xy}^{2}\gamma\alpha^{2}}{\mu_{y}\beta}V_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{4}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y^{\star}(x(t)))\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}V_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3\gamma\alpha}{\beta}\right)\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y^{\star}(x(t)))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{3\mu_{y}\gamma}{2}-\frac{L_{xy}^{2}}{\mu_{y}}-\frac{5L_{xy}^{2}\gamma\alpha}{\mu_{y}\beta}\right)\alpha V_{2}(x(t),y(t)).

(14)

Using (
12
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3\gamma\alpha}{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3L_{xy}^{2}}{\mu_{y}^{2}}\frac{\mu^{2}}{10\mu_{x}L^{2}_{xy}}\frac{\mu_{x}\mu_{y}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=\frac{1}{5},

\displaystyle\frac{3\mu_{y}\gamma}{2}-\frac{L_{xy}^{2}}{\mu_{y}}-\frac{5L_{xy}^{2}\gamma\alpha}{\mu_{y}\beta}=\frac{L_{xy}^{2}}{2\mu_{y}}-\frac{L_{xy}^{2}}{2\mu_{y}}=0,

which when substituting into (
14
) and using (
5
) and
y^{\star}(x)=\arg\max_{y}f(x,y)
we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\alpha}{4}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y^{\star}(x(t)))\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}V_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\mu_{x}\alpha}{2}\Big[\max_{y}f(x(t),y)-\min_{x}f(x,y^{\star}(x(t)))\Big]-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\mu_{x}\alpha}{2}(\max_{y}f(x(t),y)-\min_{x}\max_{y}f(x,y))-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mu_{x}\alpha}{2}V_{1}(x(t))-\frac{\mu_{y}\beta}{2}\frac{\gamma\alpha}{\beta}V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\mu_{x}\alpha}{2}(V_{1}(x(t))+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{\beta}V_{2}(x(t),y(t)))=-\frac{\mu_{x}\alpha}{2}V(x(t),y(t)),

where the last inequality is due to

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\alpha=\frac{\mu^{2}}{10L_{xy}^{2}}\leq\mu_{y}\beta=\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu_{x}\mu_{y}}\cdot

Taking the integral on both sides of the equation above immediately gives (

), i.e.,

\displaystyle V(x(t),y(t))\leq e^{-\frac{\mu_{x}\alpha t}{2}}V(x(0),y(0))=e^{-\frac{t}{20\kappa^{2}}}V(x(0),y(0)).

3.2
Nonconvex–Polyak-Łojasiewicz Conditions

In this subsection, we consider an extension of the result studied in the previous section, where we assume that the objective function
f(x,\cdot)
satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition given any
x

is nonconvex given any

.

Assumption 4

(One-Sided PŁ Conditions)
.

We assume that
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is nonconvex in
x

y
and satisfies the PŁ condition in
y
for any fixed

\mu_{y}
such that the following condition hold for any

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 2\mu_{y}[\max_{y}f(x,y)-f(x,y)]\leq\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\|^{2}.\end{aligned}

\displaystyle V_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x,y)-\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x,y),

\displaystyle V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\max_{y}f(x,y)-f(x,y),

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{32L_{xy}^{2}}{\mu_{y}^{2}},\quad\alpha=\frac{1}{8L_{xy}^{2}},\quad\beta=\frac{1}{\mu_{y}^{2}}\cdot

Then we have for all
T\geq 0

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\\
\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\end{array}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L_{xy}\sqrt{V_{1}(x(0),y(0))+4V_{2}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{T}}\cdot

Proof.

By using (
18
), (
19
), and (
20
) we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=\dot{V}_{1}(x(t))+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{\beta}\dot{V}_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\beta\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\alpha\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma\alpha^{2}}{8\beta}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\frac{4L_{xy}^{2}\gamma\alpha^{2}}{\mu_{y}\beta}V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{4}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\beta\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{4}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\frac{4L_{xy}^{2}\gamma\alpha^{2}}{\mu_{y}\beta}V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma\alpha^{2}}{8\beta}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{4}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\beta\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}\Big(1-\frac{4L_{xy}^{2}\alpha}{\mu_{y}^{2}\beta}\Big)V_{2}(x,y)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{4\beta}\Big)\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2},

(24)

where in the last inequality we use (
15
) to have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu_{y}[\max_{y}f(x,y)-f(x,y)]=2\mu_{y}V_{2}(x,y).

Using (
21
) and the preceding relation we have

\displaystyle\frac{-\gamma\alpha}{4}+\beta=-\frac{1}{\mu_{y}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{y}^{2}}=0,\quad 1-\frac{4L_{xy}^{2}\alpha}{\mu_{y}^{2}\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad 1-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{4\beta}=0,

which when substituting into (
24
) gives

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}.

Taking the integral on both sides over
t\in[0,T]
for some
T\geq 0
and rearranging we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{t=0}^{T}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}dt+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}\int_{t=0}^{T}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq V(x(0),y(0)),

which since
\gamma\geq 1
and by using (
21
) gives

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\\
\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\end{array}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2V(x(0),y(0))}{\alpha T}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L_{xy}\sqrt{V_{1}(x(0),y(0))+4V_{2}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{T}},

which concludes our proof.
∎

3.3
Nonconvex–Strongly Concave Conditions

In this subsection, we study the rate of GDAD when the function
f(x,y)
is nonconvex given any
y
and strongly concave given any
x
. In particular, we consider the following assumption.

Assumption 5
.

The objective function
f(\cdot,y)
is nonconvex for any given
y

f(x,\cdot)
is strongly concave with constant
\mu_{y}>0
for any given
x

\displaystyle f(x,y_{1})-f(x,y_{2})-\langle\nabla f(x,y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\leq-\frac{\mu_{y}}{2}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2},\quad\forall y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(25)

For our analysis of in this section, we introduce the following two Lyapunov functions

\displaystyle V_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x,y)-\min_{(x,y)}f(x,y)

\displaystyle V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{y}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|\beta\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\|^{2}.

(27)

The time derivatives of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
over the trajectories
\dot{x}
and
\dot{y}
are given in the following lemma, whose proof is presented in Section

.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
5

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{1}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}.

(28)

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq L_{xy}\beta\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\mu_{y}\beta\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}.

(29)

We next derive the convergence rate of GDAD under Assumption
5
in the following theorem, where we show that GDAD converges sublinear to a stationary point of
f
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
5
hold. Let
\gamma,\alpha,\beta
be chosen as

\displaystyle\gamma=\mu_{y}L_{xy}^{2},\quad\alpha=\frac{1}{L_{xy}^{2}},\quad\beta=\frac{4}{\mu_{y}^{2}}.

(30)

Then we have for all
T\geq 0

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\\
\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\end{array}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{xy}\sqrt{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{2L_{xy}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(0),y(0))\|}{\sqrt{\mu_{y}T}}\cdot

Proof.

By using (
28
) and (
29
) we consider

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=\dot{V}_{1}(x(t),y(t))+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{\beta}\dot{V}_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\alpha\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{4}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}-(\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{\beta})\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\alpha\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{4}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\alpha\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2},

where in the last equality we use (

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{\mu_{y}^{2}}{4}-\frac{\mu_{y}^{2}}{4}=0.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\alpha\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{2}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\mu_{y}L_{xy}\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{y}^{2}L_{xy}^{2}\alpha}{2}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0,

which when using into (
33
) we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha}{4}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{y}\gamma\alpha\beta^{2}}{4}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}-\frac{4L_{xy}^{2}\alpha}{\mu_{y}^{2}}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-\alpha}{2}\big(\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}\big),

which when taking the integral on both sides over
t
from
0
to

and rearrange we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{t=0}^{T}\big(\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}\big)dt\leq V(x(0),y(0)).

Thus, the preceding relation gives (

), i.e., for all
T>0

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\\
\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\end{array}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2V(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{\alpha T}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{\alpha T}}+\frac{\sqrt{2V_{2}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{\alpha T}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}{\alpha T}}+\sqrt{\frac{\gamma\alpha\beta\|\nabla_{y}f(x(0),y(0))\|^{2}}{\alpha T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{xy}\sqrt{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{2L_{xy}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(0),y(0))\|}{\sqrt{\mu_{y}T}}\cdot

3.4
Strongly Convex–Nonconcave Conditions

As mentioned, the single-loop GDA method is applicable to the convex-nonconcave min-max problem, while the nested-loop GDA method is not. In this section, we complete our analysis by studying the rate of GDAD when the function
f(x,y)
is strongly convex given any
y
and nonconcave given any
x
. In particular, we consider the following assumption.

Assumption 6
.

The objective function
f(x,\cdot)
is nonconcave for any given
x
and
f(\cdot,y)
is strongly convex with constant
\mu_{x}>0
for any given
y
. The latter is equivalent to

\displaystyle f(x_{1},y)-f(x_{2},y)-\langle\nabla f(x_{2},y),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\frac{\mu_{x}}{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2},\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(34)

For our analysis of in this section, we introduce the following two Lyapunov functions

\displaystyle V_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|\alpha\nabla_{x}f(x,y)\|^{2}

(35)

\displaystyle V_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\max_{x,y}f(x,y)-f(x,y).

(36)

The time derivatives of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
over the trajectories
\dot{x}
and
\dot{y}
are given in the following lemma, whose proof is presented in Section
4.4
.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
6
hold. Then we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{1}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\mu_{x}\alpha\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\alpha\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|.

(37)

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{2}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}.

(38)

Since in the strongly convex-nonconcave setting the minimization problem is easier to solve than the maximization problem, we consider the following Lyapunov function

\displaystyle V(x,y)=V_{2}(x,y)+\frac{\gamma\beta}{\alpha}V_{1}(x,y),

(39)

where
\beta\ll\alpha
. In this case,
\dot{x}
is updated at a faster time scale than
\dot{y}
. Using this Lyapunov function, we now derive the convergence rate of GDAD under Assumption
6
in the following theorem, which basically is similar to the one in Theorem
3
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
6
hold. Let
\gamma,\alpha,\beta
be chosen as

\displaystyle\gamma=\mu_{x}L_{xy}^{2},\quad\alpha=\frac{4}{\mu_{x}^{2}},\quad\beta=\frac{1}{L_{xy}^{2}}.

(40)

Then we have for all
T\geq 0

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\\
\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\end{array}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{xy}\sqrt{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{2L_{xy}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(0),y(0))\|}{\sqrt{\mu_{x}T}}\cdot

Proof.

By using (
37
) and (
38
) we consider

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=\dot{V}_{2}(x(t),y(t))+\frac{\gamma\beta}{\alpha}\dot{V}_{1}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\beta\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\mu_{x}\gamma\beta\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta}{4}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-(\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta}{4}-\frac{1}{\alpha})\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\beta\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta}{4}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\beta\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2},

(43)

where in the last equality we use (

Using (

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}+L_{xy}\gamma\beta\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}+\mu_{x}L_{xy}\|\dot{y}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|-\frac{\mu_{x}^{2}L_{xy}^{2}\beta}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0,

which when using into (
43
) we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\dot{y}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta}{4}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta}{2}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{x}\gamma\beta\alpha^{2}}{4}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta}{2}\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}-\frac{4L_{xy}^{2}\beta}{\mu_{x}^{2}}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-\beta}{2}\big(\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}\big),

which when taking the integral on both sides over
t
from
0
to
T
and rearrange we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{t=0}^{T}\big(\|\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|^{2}\big)dt\leq V(x(0),y(0)).

Thus, the preceding relation gives (
4
), i.e., for all
T>0

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{x}f(x(t),y(t))\\
\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\end{array}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2V(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{\beta T}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{\beta T}}+\frac{\sqrt{2V_{2}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{\beta T}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}{\beta T}}+\sqrt{\frac{\gamma\beta\alpha\|\nabla_{x}f(x(0),y(0))\|^{2}}{\beta T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{xy}\sqrt{2V_{1}(x(0),y(0))}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{2L_{xy}\|\nabla_{x}f(x(0),y(0))\|}{\sqrt{\mu_{x}T}}\cdot

∎

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we consider two-time-scale gradient descent-ascent dynamics for solving nonconvex min-max optimization problems. Our main focus is to derive the convergence rates of this method for different settings of the underlying objective functions. Our techniques are mainly motivated by the classic singular perturbation, where we show that our analysis improves the existing results under the same conditions. A natural extension from this work is to provide a better analysis for the discrete-time variant of GDAD. Another interesting future direction is to consider the stochastic setting and its accelerated counterpart.

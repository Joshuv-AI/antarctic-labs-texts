Characterisation for Exponential Stability of port-Hamiltonian Systems
Sascha Trostorff, Marcus Waurick

Abstract
Given an energy-dissipating port-Hamiltonian system, we characterise the exponential decay of the energy via the model ingredients under mild conditions on the Hamiltonian density ℋ \mathcal{H} . In passing, we obtain generalisations for sufficient criteria in the literature by making regularity requirements for the Hamiltonian density largely obsolete. The key assumption for the characterisation (and thus the sufficient criteria) to work is a uniform bound for a family of fundamental solutions for some non-autonomous, finite-dimensional ODEs. Regularity conditions on ℋ \mathcal{H} for previously known criteria such as bounded variation are shown to imply the key assumption. Exponentially stable port-Hamiltonian systems with densities in L ∞ L_{\infty} only are also provided.



Keywords
port-Hamiltonian systems, Exponential stability,

C_{0}
-semi-groups, Infinite-dimensional systems theory

In this section, we recall the functional analytic setting of port-Hamiltonian
systems and detail some results from the literature characterising
the generation property of
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, we slightly reformulate

To start out with we fix

\displaystyle\|M^{\ast}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\left(\iota_{+}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}+\iota_{-}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}\right)C(Q_{+}v+Q_{-}v)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\left(\iota_{+}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}+\iota_{-}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}\right)CSu\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\left(\iota_{+}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}+\iota_{-}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}\right)(P_{-}x+P_{+}y)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}x\|_{E_{-}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}x\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}x+\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}Q_{+}v+\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}Q_{-}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|P_{+}v+P_{-}v\|^{2}=\|v\|^{2},

which yields
\|M\|=\|M^{\ast}\|\leq 1.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Assume

W=K\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{+}-MQ_{-}&Q_{-}-MQ_{+}\end{array}\right)

for
K,M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with
\|M\|\leq 1
and
K
invertible.
We show that
W
has rank
d
. Since
K
is invertible, it suffices
to show that
\widetilde{W}\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{+}-MQ_{-}&Q_{-}-MQ_{+}\end{array}\right)
has rank
d
, which in turn is equivalent to
\ker\left(\widetilde{W}\right)^{\ast}=\{0\}.

So, let
u\in\ker(\widetilde{W})^{\ast}
; that is,

Q_{+}u=Q_{-}M^{\ast}u,\quad Q_{-}u=Q_{+}M^{\ast}u.

Since
Q_{-}
and
Q_{+}
attain values in
E_{-}
and
E_{+}
,
respectively, we infer
Q_{-}u=Q_{+}u=0
and hence
P_{-}u=P_{+}u=0,

which imply
u=0.
It remains to show (
4
). As
shown above, this is equivalent to (
5
).
So let
(x,y)=W^{\ast}u
for some
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; that is

x=\left(Q_{+}-Q_{-}M^{\ast}\right)K^{\ast}u,\quad y=\left(Q_{-}-Q_{+}M^{\ast}\right)K^{\ast}u.

Then we compute

\displaystyle\|\iota_{+}^{\text{$\ast$}}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}x\|_{E_{-}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}x\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|P_{+}M^{\ast}K^{\ast}u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}+\|P_{-}M^{\ast}K^{\ast}u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|M^{\ast}K^{\ast}u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|K^{\ast}u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|P_{+}K^{\ast}u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}+\|P_{-}K^{\ast}u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}x\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}x\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}.

For the final claim, we note that the strict positive definiteness
in (

) is equivalent to

\|\iota_{+}^{\text{$\ast$}}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}x\|_{E_{-}}^{2}<\|\iota_{+}^{\text{$\ast$}}x\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}\quad((x,y)\in\operatorname{ran}W^{\ast}\setminus\{0\}),

which by the computations above is equivalent to

∎

Using the latter lemma, we obtain the following characterisation result

.

(ii)

\displaystyle=2\langle P_{1}\partial_{1}\mathcal{H}u,\mathcal{H}u\rangle

3
Characterisation of Exponential Stability

In this section we prove the main theorem of the present manuscript.
We start off with an elementary observation, see also
[
13
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{H}\in L_{\infty}(a,b;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
with

0<m\leq\mathcal{H}(x)=\mathcal{H}(x)^{\top}
for almost every
x\in(a,b)

and let
H
be the Hilbert space
L_{2}(a,b;\mathbb{R}^{d})
endowed with
the inner product
\langle u,v\rangle_{H}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{H}u,v\rangle_{L_{2}}.

Define

S:L_{2}(a,b)^{d}\to H,\quad u\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{-1}u.

Then
S
is a Banach space isomorphism with
S^{\ast}v=v
for each

v\in L_{2}(a,b)^{d}.

Proof.

S
clearly is bounded, one-to-one and onto; i.e., a Banach space
isomorphism. Moreover, for
u\in L_{2}(a,b)^{d},v\in H
we compute

\langle Su,v\rangle_{H}=\langle\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}^{-1}u,v\rangle_{L_{2}(a,b)^{d}}=\langle u,v\rangle_{L_{2}(a,b)^{d}},

which shows
S^{\ast}v=v.

∎

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
we consider the following ordinary differential equation

v^{\prime}(x)=P_{1}^{-1}(\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}(x)^{-1}-P_{0})v(x)

and denote by
\Phi_{t}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
the fundamental
matrix satisfying
\Phi_{t}(a)=1_{d}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in[a,b]

we have
\Phi_{t}(x)^{-1}=P_{1}^{-1}\Phi_{t}(x)^{\ast}P_{1}.
In
particular
\|\Phi_{t}(x)^{-1}\|\leq\|P_{1}^{-1}\|\|P_{1}\|\|\Phi_{t}(x)\|.

Proof.

We compute the derivative of
\Psi\colon x\mapsto\Phi_{t}(x)^{-1}
.
We have

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\Psi(x)\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(x)\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=-\Psi(x)P_{1}^{-1}(\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}(x)^{-1}-P_{0})\Phi_{t}(x)\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=-\Psi(x)P_{1}^{-1}(\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}(x)^{-1}-P_{0}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{\ast}\right)^{\prime}(x)=\Psi^{\prime}(x)^{\ast}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}(x)^{-1}-P_{0})P_{1}^{-1}\Psi(x)^{\ast}

and

\left(P_{1}^{-1}\Psi^{\ast}\right)^{\prime}(x)=P_{1}^{-1}(\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}(x)^{-1}-P_{0})P_{1}^{-1}\Psi(x)^{\ast}.

Thus,
P_{1}^{-1}\Psi^{*}
solves the same ODE as
\Phi_{t}
does;
taking into account the initial values it follows that

P_{1}^{-1}\Psi^{\ast}(x)=\Phi_{t}(x)P_{1}^{-1}

and hence,

\Phi_{t}(x)^{-1}=P_{1}^{-1}\Phi_{t}(x)^{\ast}P_{1}.

The theorem underlying the proof of our characterisation of exponential
stability is the celebrated result by Gearhart–Prüß.

Theorem 3.3

(Gearhart–Prüß, see
[
14
]
)
.

\mathcal{A}
generates an exponentially stable

C_{0}
-semi-group on a Hilbert space
H
, if, and only if,

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0}}\|(z-\mathcal{A})^{-1}\|_{L(H)}<\infty.

(7)

The decisive step to reach our goal now becomes the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be as in Theorem

(iii) and assume that

.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=T_{t}v_{0}-W\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Phi_{t}(b)\int_{a}^{b}\Phi_{t}(s)^{-1}P_{1}^{-1}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
0\end{array}\right).

(i)
\Rightarrow

is invertible.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): By our considerations at the beginning of

for

(ii)

Example 4.3
.

Consider the matrices
M\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\

for all

0&-1\end{array}\right)
. By Theorem

(iv)

W=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-M&1_{2}\end{array}\right)

leads to
\mathcal{A}
generate a contraction semi-group. We consider

\displaystyle T_{t}

\displaystyle=W\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tP_{1}^{-1}\mathcal{H}_{0}^{-1}}\\
1_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-M&1_{2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{0}^{-1}}\\
1_{2}\end{array}\right)=-M\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{0}^{-1}}+1_{2}

and analyse the respective inverses for all

\displaystyle\det(T_{t})

\displaystyle=\det\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

1&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t}&0\\

It is the aim of this section to put the characterisation into perspective

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\colon\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq H

\displaystyle\to H

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto P_{1}(\mathcal{H}u)^{\prime}+P_{0}\mathcal{H}u,

be a port-Hamiltonian operator as in (

and that

(8)

then

-semi-group.

Remark 5.2
.

In Subsection

implies Theorem

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be as in Theorem
2.4
.
Then the mapping

\operatorname{tr}\colon\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad u\mapsto Q_{-}(\mathcal{H}u)(b)+Q_{+}(\mathcal{H}u)(a)

is onto.

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
M
as in Theorem
2.4
(iv).
We define a function
v_{+}\colon[a,b]\to E_{+}
by

v_{+}(t)\coloneqq\frac{t-a}{b-a}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}My+\frac{t-b}{a-b}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}y

and similarly
v_{-}\colon[a,b]\to E_{-}
by

v_{-}(t)\coloneqq\frac{t-a}{b-a}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}y+\frac{t-b}{a-b}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}My.

Then clearly,
v\coloneqq\iota_{+}v_{+}+\iota_{-}v_{-}\in H^{1}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})

and it satisfies

\displaystyle Q_{-}v(b)+Q_{+}v(a)

\displaystyle=\iota_{-}\iota_{-}^{\ast}y+\iota_{+}\iota_{+}^{\ast}y=y,

\displaystyle Q_{-}v(a)+Q_{+}v(b)

\displaystyle=\iota_{-}\iota_{-}^{\ast}My+\iota_{+}\iota_{+}^{\ast}My=My,

which shows on the one hand that
u\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{-1}v\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{A})

and on the other hand that
\operatorname{tr}u=y
.
∎

It is possible to recast the condition in Theorem
5.1

as an inequality merely containing finite-dimensional spaces. This

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be as in Theorem
2.4

with
M
as in Theorem
2.4
(iv). Moreover let

and
\eta\in\{a,b\}.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

For all

\langle\mathcal{A}u,u\rangle_{H}\leq-c\|\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(\eta)\|^{2}.

(b)

For all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
either

\|y\|^{2}-\|My\|^{2}\geq 2c\left(\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}My\|_{E_{-}}^{2}+\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}\right)

if
\eta=a
or

\|y\|^{2}-\|My\|^{2}\geq 2c\left(\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}+\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}My\|_{E_{+}}^{2}\right)

if
\eta=b.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}u,u\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|M\left(Q_{-}(\mathcal{H}u)(b)+Q_{+}(\mathcal{H}u)(a)\right)\|^{2}-\|Q_{-}\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(b)+Q_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(a)\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|M\operatorname{tr}u\|^{2}-\|\operatorname{tr}u\|^{2}\right)

for all
u\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{A}).
Moreover,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(a)

\displaystyle=\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}Q_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(a)+\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}Q_{-}\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(a)

\displaystyle=\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}\operatorname{tr}u+\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}M\operatorname{tr}u,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(b)

\displaystyle=\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}Q_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(b)+\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}Q_{-}\left(\mathcal{H}u\right)(b)

\displaystyle=\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}M\operatorname{tr}u+\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}\operatorname{tr}u.

Now the assertion follows from Lemma

.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\in L_{\infty}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})

such that
\mathcal{O}(x)
is self-adjoint for a.e.
x\in[a,b].

Moreover, let
\Pi\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be the fundamental
solution to the differential equation

v^{\prime}(x)=P_{1}^{-1}(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{O}(x)-P_{0})v(x)

with
\Pi(a)=1_{d}.
Then the following statements hold:

(a)

The matrix

V\coloneqq Q_{-}+Q_{+}\Pi(b)

is invertible with
\|V^{-1}\|\leq C_{1}\|\Pi(b)\|+C_{2},
where

C_{1},C_{2}
are just depending on the values of
P_{1}
. Moreover,
the matrix

U\coloneqq\left(Q_{-}\Pi(b)+Q_{+}\right)V^{-1}

is unitary.

(b)

For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have the following estimates

\|y\|^{2}\leq\|V\|^{2}\left(\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}Uy\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}\right)

and

\|y\|^{2}\leq\|\Pi(b)^{-1}\|^{2}\|V\|^{2}\left(\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}Uy\|_{E_{-}}^{2}\right).

Proof.

(a) We recall from Lemma
3.2
that

\Pi(b)^{-1}=P_{1}^{-1}\Pi(b)^{\ast}P_{1}
or equivalently
P_{1}=\Pi(b)^{\ast}P_{1}\Pi(b)
.
We set
W\coloneqq Q_{-}\Pi(b)+Q_{+}
and compute

\displaystyle V^{\ast}V

\displaystyle=(Q_{-}+\Pi(b)^{\ast}Q_{+})\left(Q_{-}+Q_{+}\Pi(b)\right)

\displaystyle=Q_{-}^{2}+\Pi(b)^{\ast}Q_{+}^{2}\Pi(b)

\displaystyle=Q_{+}^{2}-P_{1}+\Pi(b)^{\ast}\left(P_{1}+Q_{-}^{2}\right)\Pi(b)

\displaystyle=Q_{+}^{2}+\Pi(b)^{\ast}Q_{-}^{2}\Pi(b)

\displaystyle=W^{\ast}W.

In particular, we have
\|Vx\|=\|Wx\|
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus,
if
Vx=0,
then
Wx=0
and hence,
Q_{-}x=Q_{-}Vx=0
as well as

Q_{+}x=Q_{+}Wx=0.
The latter gives
P_{1}x=(Q_{+}^{2}-Q_{-}^{2})x=0

and thus,
x=0
showing the invertibility of
V.
Moreover, for

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we compute

\displaystyle\|Ux\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle WV^{-1}x,WV^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle V^{-1}x,W^{\ast}WV^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle V^{-1}x,V^{\ast}x\rangle=\|x\|^{2},

showing that
U
is unitary. It remains to prove the estimate for
the norm of the inverse of
V
. We set
D\coloneqq\iota_{+}^{\ast}\Pi(b)\iota_{+}

and
C\coloneqq\iota_{-}^{\ast}\Pi(b)\iota_{+}
compute for
x\in E_{-}

\displaystyle\|\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{1}^{+}x,x\rangle=\langle\iota_{+}^{\ast}P_{1}\iota_{+}x,x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\iota_{+}^{\ast}\left(\Pi(b)^{\ast}P_{1}\Pi(b)\right)\iota_{+}x,x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{1}\Pi(b)\iota_{+}x,\Pi(b)\iota_{+}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{1}(\iota_{+}\iota_{+}^{\ast}+\iota_{-}\iota_{-}^{\ast})\Pi(b)\iota_{+}x,\Pi(b)\iota_{+}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{1}^{+}Dx,Dx\rangle-\langle P_{1}^{-}Cx,Cx\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}Dx\|^{2}\leq\|\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\|Dx\|^{2}.

Hence,
D
is invertible with
\|D^{-1}\|\leq\frac{\|\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|}{\|\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|}
.
Since
V
is unitarily equivalent (via the decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{d}=E_{+}\oplus E_{-})

to the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}D&\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}B\\
0&\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{array}\right)

with
B\coloneqq\iota_{+}^{\ast}\Pi(b)\iota_{-}
, its inverse is
unitarily equivalent to

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D^{-1}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}&-D^{-1}B\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{array}\right)

and thus, the desired estimate for
\|V^{-1}\|
follows.

(b) Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We compute

\displaystyle\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}Uy\|_{E_{+}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}\left(P_{1}^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}Uy\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}\iota_{+}^{\ast}V^{-1}y\|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{-}(P_{1}^{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}VV^{-1}y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{-}\iota_{-}^{\ast}V^{-1}y\|.

Consequently, we obtain

\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}Uy\|_{E_{+}}^{2}+\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}=\|V^{-1}y\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\|V\|^{2}}\|y\|^{2},

which proves the first estimate. Similarly, we compute

\displaystyle\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}Uy\|_{E_{-}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{-}\left(P_{1}^{-}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{-}^{\ast}Uy\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{-}\iota_{-}^{\ast}\Pi(b)V^{-1}y\|^{2}

as well as

\displaystyle\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}(P_{1}^{+})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\iota_{+}^{\ast}VV^{-1}y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\iota_{+}\iota_{+}^{\ast}\Pi(b)V^{-1}y\|^{2}.

Hence, we obtain

\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}Uy\|_{E_{-}}^{2}+\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}=\|\Pi(b)V^{-1}y\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\|\Pi(b)^{-1}\|^{2}\|V\|^{2}}\|y\|^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

By Theorem
3.5
we need to prove that

T_{t}\coloneqq W\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Phi_{t}(b)\\
1\end{array}\right)

is invertible for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
with
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|T_{t}^{-1}\|<\infty.

By Theorem
2.4
the matrix
W
can be expressed

with

and

has the form

\displaystyle T_{t}

\displaystyle=K\left(\left(Q_{+}-MQ_{-}\right)\Phi_{t}(b)+\left(Q_{-}-MQ_{+}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=K\left(Q_{-}+Q_{+}\Phi_{t}(b)-M(Q_{-}\Phi_{t}(b)+Q_{+})\right).

As in Proposition
5.5
(a) we set

V_{t}\coloneqq Q_{-}+Q_{+}\Phi_{t}(b)

which is an invertible matrix by Proposition
5.5

(a) and

U_{t}\coloneqq(Q_{-}\Phi_{t}(b)+Q_{+})V_{t}^{-1}

is unitary by Proposition
5.5
(a). Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We prove the assertion by showing that there exists some
\kappa>0

independently of

\|y\|\leq\kappa\|T_{t}y\|\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

Using the representation above, we have

\displaystyle T_{t}y

\displaystyle=K\left(V_{t}y-M(Q_{+}\Phi_{t}(b)+Q_{-})y\right)

\displaystyle=K(1-MU_{t})V_{t}y.

Since

and

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|V_{t}^{-1}\|<\infty

(a), it suffices to prove

\|V_{t}y\|\leq\kappa\|\widetilde{T}_{t}y\|\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}),

where
\widetilde{T}_{t}=K^{-1}T_{t}
. We employ Lemma

to obtain

\|y\|^{2}-\|My\|^{2}\geq 2c\left(\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}My\|_{E_{-}}^{2}+\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{+}}^{2}\right)

if
\eta=a
or

\|y\|^{2}-\|My\|^{2}\geq 2c\left(\|\iota_{-}^{\ast}y\|_{E_{-}}^{2}+\|\iota_{+}^{\ast}My\|_{E_{+}}^{2}\right)

if
\eta=b.
Let us start with the case

Applying this

and using that

as well as that

is isometric, we get

\displaystyle\|V_{t}y\|^{2}

Definition 6.6

Let

be an open interval,
\alpha\in L_{1}(I)

Then
\alpha

, if

\sup\{|\int_{I}\alpha(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x|;\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(I),\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}<\infty.

By the Riesz–Markov representation theorem, there exists a unique

-\int_{I}\alpha\phi^{\prime}=\int_{I}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\eqqcolon\langle\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha,\phi\rangle,

\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|\coloneqq|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha|(I)=\sup\{|\int_{I}\alpha(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x|;\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(I),\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1\},

|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha|
denotes the total variation of the measure

\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha.
The space

BV(I)\coloneqq\{\alpha\in L_{1}(I);\alpha\text{ of bounded variation}\}

becomes a Banach space, if endowed with the norm given by

\|\alpha\|_{BV}\coloneqq\|\alpha\|_{L_{1}}+\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|.

A matrix-valued function

if all its components are of
bounded variation. In this case we set

\mathrm{D}_{x}G\coloneqq\left(\mathrm{D}_{x}G_{ij}\right)_{i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}

as a matrix-valued measure.

\mathcal{H}^{-1}
.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\colon(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\in L_{\infty}(a,b;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})

be of bounded variation and assume that
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{O}(x)\geq m

for some
m>0
and a.e.
x\in(a,b).
Then
x\mapsto\mathcal{O}(x)^{-1}

is of bounded variation.

Proof.

Since every matrix entry of
\mathcal{O}
is in
L_{1}
, we may
choose a common Lebesgue point
t_{0}
for all matrix entries. By
Theorem
6.8
, the function given by

\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x)\coloneqq\mathcal{O}(t_{0})+\left(\begin{cases}\mathrm{D}_{x}\mathcal{O}_{i,j}([t_{0},x)),&x>t_{0},\\
-\mathrm{D}_{x}\mathcal{O}_{i.j}([x,t_{0})),&x\leq t_{0}\end{cases}\right)_{i,j}

defines a left-continuous representative of
\mathcal{O}
. By composition,

x\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x)^{-1}
is, too, left-continuous
and evidently it is a representative of
x\mapsto\mathcal{O}(x)^{-1}
.
Since
\|\mathcal{O}(x)^{-1}\|\leq 1/m
by
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{O}(x)\geq m

and the boundedness of
(a,b)
it follows that
x\mapsto\mathcal{O}(x)^{-1}\in L_{1}(a,b;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Next, let
a<x_{0}<\cdots<x_{n}<b
and compute

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j})^{-1}-\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j-1})^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j})^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j-1})-\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j})\right)\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j-1})^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j})^{-1}\|\|\left(\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j-1})-\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j})\right)\|\|\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j-1})^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\left(\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j-1})-\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(x_{j})\right)\|.

Thus, if
\kappa>0
is such that
\|A\|\leq\kappa\sum_{i,j}|A_{i,j}|
,
for every
k,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

\operatorname{var}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(\cdot)^{-1}\right)_{k,l}\leq\frac{\kappa}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{var}\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{lc}}(\cdot)_{i,j}<\infty.

Hence, by Theorem
6.8
, the assertion follows.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open
and bounded interval,
\alpha\in BV(I)\cap L_{\infty}(I).
If
u\in H^{1}(I)
,
then
\alpha u\in BV(I)\cap L_{\infty}(I)
and

\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha u)=u\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha+u^{\prime}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where
\,\mathrm{d}\lambda
denotes the Lebesgue measure and
u\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha

is the measure
\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha
with density
u
. Moreover, we have

\|\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha u)\|\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\sqrt{b-a}.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(I)
. Assume that
u
is continuously differentiable.
Then we compute

\displaystyle-\int_{I}\alpha u\phi^{\prime}d\lambda

\displaystyle=-\int_{I}\alpha((u\phi)^{\prime}-u^{\prime}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha,u\phi\rangle+\int_{I}u^{\prime}\alpha\phi\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\langle u\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha+u^{\prime}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\phi\rangle

We estimate

\displaystyle|\int_{I}\alpha u\phi^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|\|u\phi\|_{\infty}+\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|u^{\prime}\phi\|_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|\|u\|_{\infty}\|\phi\|_{\infty}+\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}}\|\phi\|_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|\phi\|_{\infty}+\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\sqrt{b-a}\|\phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left(\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\sqrt{b-a}\right)\|\phi\|_{\infty}.

Hence,
\alpha u\in BV(I)
and

\|\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha u)\|\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha\|\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\sqrt{b-a}.

Moreover, we estimate

\|\alpha u\|_{L_{1}}\leq\|u\|_{\infty}\|\alpha\|_{L_{1}}\leq c\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|\alpha\|_{L_{1}},

by the Sobolev embedding theorem. In particular, let
(u_{n})_{n}

be a sequence of continuously differentiable functions converging
to
u
in
H^{1}
. Then, by the estimates above,
\alpha u\in BV(I)
.
Moreover, using the product formula from the beginning of the proof
for
u_{n}
and letting
n\to\infty
, we infer

\mathrm{D}_{x}(\alpha u)=u\mathrm{D}_{x}\alpha+u^{\prime}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Next, we recall Gronwall’s inequality for locally finite measures.

Definition 6.11
.

I\subseteq\mathbb{R}

7
Conclusion

We presented a new characterisation of exponential stability for port-Hamiltonian
systems. The characterisation works only if a certain family of fundamental
solutions of a non-autonomous ODE-system is uniformly bounded. Whether
or not this boundedness is needed lies beyond the scope of this article
and can be considered an
open problem
for the time-being.
We emphasise that as the strategy above uses existence and boundedness
of the resolvent of the generator on the imaginary axis it might be
possible, invoking results such as
[
1
,
4
]
,
to show explicit algebraic decay for certain set-ups. Whether at all
these set-ups exists and how they maybe characterised will be addressed
in future work.

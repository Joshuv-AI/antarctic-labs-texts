Abstract
. We prove sharp near-diagonal pointwise bounds for the Green function G Ω ​ ( x , y ) G_{\Omega}(x,y) for nonlocal operators of fractional order α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \alpha\in(0,2) . The novelty of our results is two-fold: the estimates are robust as α → 2 − \alpha\to 2- and we prove the bounds without making use of the Dirichlet heat kernel p Ω ​ ( t , x , y ) p_{\Omega}(t;x,y) . In this way we can cover cases, in which the Green function satisfies isotropic bounds but the heat kernel does not.

1.
Introduction

An important object in Potential Analysis is the Green function. Explicit formulas for the Green function of the fractional Laplace operator for
0<\alpha<2
are complicated, even for domains like a ball in the Euclidean space, see
[
Rie38
,
BGR61
]
or more recently
[
Buc16
]
. Let
G^{(\alpha)}
be the Green function for the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and assume
d\geq 3
. A careful study of the constants shows for
x_{0}\in B

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 2}\,\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to x_{0}\\
y\to x_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{G^{(\alpha)}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha-d}}=\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 2}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-\alpha}{2})}{2^{\alpha}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}=\frac{1}{d(d-2)|B_{1}|}.

As a consequence, one can expect near-diagonal pointwise bounds for the Green function of a given nonlocal operator of fractional order
\alpha\in(0,2)
with constants, which are independent of
\alpha
away from zero. Establishing such results is one aim of the current paper.

There exist several very detailed works on pointwise bounds for the Green function for nonlocal operators of fractional order such as
[
CKS10
,
BGR14
]
. The authors establish fine near-diagonal and boundary estimates, which are sharp as long as
\alpha\in(0,2)
is fixed but these results contain constants, which degenerate as
\alpha\to 2-
. The reason for this phenomenon is that the main tool inside the proofs is the formula
G^{(\alpha)}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{(\alpha)}_{\Omega}(t;x,y)\mathrm{d}t
, where
p^{(\alpha)}_{\Omega}
is the corresponding Dirichlet heat kernel. Since the decay property of
p^{(\alpha)}_{\Omega}
for large values of
|x-y|
changes from polynomial to exponential at
\alpha=2
, this approach would need very careful tracking of the constants in order to lead to robust estimates. Our results are much weaker in many respects but they provide near-diagonal bounds which are robust in the sense that the constants in the bounds stay uniform as
\alpha\to 2-
. In this way, the corresponding near-diagonal bounds for second order operators can be recovered by taking the limit as
\alpha\to 2-
. Since the proofs do not rely on any estimate of the heat kernel
p^{(\alpha)}_{\Omega}
we have a second advantage. We can establish rotational bounds for the Green function in situations where the heat kernel does not satisfy rotational bounds, see
Example

1.8
below.

Let us explain our set-up and the main results. Throughout the paper, we always assume
k
to be a measurable symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{diag}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1\land|y-x|^{2}\right)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y<+\infty.

Example 1.1
.

An important example satisfying (
1.1
) is given by

\displaystyle k^{(\alpha)}(x,y)=\tfrac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}{\pi^{d/2}|\Gamma(\frac{-\alpha}{2})|}\,|y-x|^{-d-\alpha}\qquad(0<\alpha<2).

Note that the ratio
\tfrac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}{\pi^{d/2}|\Gamma(\frac{-\alpha}{2})|}/(2-\alpha)
remains bounded as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
. Since we do not care about the behavior of this constant for
\alpha\to 0
, we will often just use
2-\alpha
as a multiplicative constant.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. For measurable functions
u,v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we define the expression

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x))\,k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

provided that is it finite. If
k=k_{\alpha}
from above, then we write
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}^{\alpha}
instead of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
. Moreover, we set
\mathcal{E}(u,v)=\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u,v)
resp.
\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(u,v)=\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{\alpha}(u,v)
. Note that (
1.1
) implies that the double-integral (
1.2
) converges absolutely for given
u,v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
(see
[
FOT11
, Example 1.2.1]
). We define an operator

L\varphi(x)=2\,p.v.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

We refer the reader to
Section

2
for definition of function spaces associated to
k
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A measurable function
G:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
is called a
Green function
of
L
on
\Omega
if
G(\cdot,y)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
for each
y\in\Omega
,
G=0

\mathrm{a.e.}
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus(\Omega\times\Omega)
, and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(x,y)\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\varphi(y)

for every
y\in\Omega
,
\varphi\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)\cap C(\Omega)
, and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with

(1.4)

\mathcal{E}(\varphi,v)=\langle\psi,v\rangle\quad\text{for all }v\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)\,.

Remark
.

(1)

Definition

1.2
is analogous to the one in
[
LSW63
, Section 6]
.

(2)

The above definition corresponds to the usual definition
G(x,y)

=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{\Omega}(t;x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t
, where
p_{\Omega}
is the corresponding heat kernel. This can be easily seen in case of a Lévy process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with an infinitesimal generator
L
, where the Green function
G
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle G(\cdot,y),\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle-LG(\cdot,y),\varphi\rangle=\left\langle-L\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{t}(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}t,\varphi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle-\int_{0}^{\infty}Lp_{t}(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}t,\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}p_{t}(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}t,\varphi\right\rangle=\varphi(y)

for every
y\in\Omega
,
\varphi\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)\cap C(\Omega)
, and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with (
1.4
). In other words, a Green function solves the equation
-LG(\cdot,y)=\delta_{y}
, where
\delta_{y}
is the Dirac measure at the point

. In case of

from
Example

, the function

is nothing but the Green function of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
in the domain
\Omega
.

Note that (

Let us review some related results. With the help of techniques from classical potential theory
[
LSW63
,
GW82
,
Zha86
]
establish sharp Green function estimates for second order operators on bounded
C^{1,1}
domains.
[
Kul97
,
CS98
]
extend these results to operators of fractional order by studying symmetric
\alpha
-stable processes (
0<\alpha<2
) on bounded
C^{1,1}
domains. Their proofs rely on explicit formulas for the Green functions on balls.
[
CKS10
]
uses fine estimates of the two-sided Dirichlet heat kernel in order to prove a similar result. Dirichlet heat kernel estimates and Green function estimates have been extended to a wide class of operators of fractional order and related Markov processes. See
[
CKSV12
,
CKS14
,
BGR14
,
KM14
,
GKK20
]
for a selection of recent results in this direction. As mentioned in the beginning of the introduction, these estimates are sharp for fixed
\alpha
but the constants in the estimates may degenerate as
\alpha\to 2-
.
In the case of symmetric
\alpha
-stable processes, i.e., for translation invariant operators with appropriate symmetric measures,
[
Che99
,
CS04
]
do establish Green function estimates that are robust as
\alpha\to 2-
. Both works use explicit formulas for the Green function on balls, thus the restricted scope. However, the results of
[
Che99
,
CS04
]
include global estimates up to the boundary of the considered domain. It is an interesting task to prove such global bounds in the framework of our work.

Using a different approach based on variational techniques as in
[
LSW63
,
GW82
]
, the Green function for nonlocal operators of fractional order with rough coefficients is studied in
[
KS02
,
KMS15
,
CS18
]
. The authors of
[
CS18
]
obtain the near-diagonal estimates for Green functions and the boundary Harnack principle in the framework of nonlinear nonlocal operators modeled after the fractional
p
-Laplacian. While these results are obtained for kernels comparable to rotationally symmetric ones, our results allow for kernels that may not have rotationally symmetric bounds.

.

.

(ii)

H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)=\{u\in H(\mathbb{R}^{d};k):u=0~\mathrm{a.e.}~\text{on}~\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega\}
.

If

as in

, then we write
H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k^{(\alpha)})=H^{\alpha/2}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In a similar manner, we introduce the space
W^{\alpha/2,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p\geq 1
:

(iii)

W^{\alpha/2,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{u\in W^{\alpha/2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):u=0~\mathrm{a.e.}~\text{on}~\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega\}
.

We will also make use of the weak
L^{p}
spaces:

(iv)

L^{p}_{\mathrm{weak}}(\Omega)=\{u:[u]_{L^{p}_{\mathrm{weak}}(\Omega)}<+\infty\}
, where

\displaystyle[u]_{L^{p}_{\mathrm{weak}}(\Omega)}=\sup_{t>0}t|\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|>t\}|^{1/p}.

It is known
[

]
that the spaces

and
H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
, endowed with the norm

, are Hilbert spaces even when

is not symmetric. Similarly,
W^{\alpha/2,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space with the fractional Sobolev norm

\begin{split}\|u\|_{W^{\alpha/2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&=(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}+[u]_{W^{\alpha/2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p})^{1/p}\\
&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+(2-\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+\frac{\alpha}{2}p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}.\end{split}

Note that, for simplicity, we choose
(2-\alpha)
as a constant in the seminorm because, in our results, we only trace the behavior of constants for
\alpha\to 2-
. Let us collect some embedding theorems for the spaces introduced above.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded. If
0<\beta<\alpha<2
and
p>q\geq 1
, then
W^{\alpha/2,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset W^{\beta/2,q}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{\alpha/2,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

(2.1)

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty.

To estimate
[u]_{W^{\beta/2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, let us fix a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
and consider a large Ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
\Omega\subset B_{R}(x_{0})
and
\mathrm{dist}(\Omega,\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\geq 2\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
. Then, by
[
Coz17
, Lemma 4.6]
we have

(2.2)

I_{1}:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{d+\frac{\beta}{2}q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+\frac{\alpha}{2}p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q/p}\leq C[u]_{W^{\alpha/2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}

for some
C=C(d,\alpha,\beta,p,q,R,|\Omega|)>0
. Recalling
u=0
outside
B_{R}(x_{0})
, we have

(2.3)

I_{2}:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{d+\frac{\beta}{2}q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+\frac{\beta}{2}q}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

If
x\in\Omega
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
, then
|x-y|\geq|y-x_{0}|-|x-x_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|y-x_{0}|
. Thus, it follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) that

(2.4)

I_{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+\frac{\beta}{2}q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q},

where
C
is a constant depending on
d
,
\beta
,
p
,
q
,
R
, and
|\Omega|
. Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) yields

(2.5)

[u]_{W^{\beta/2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\leq 2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{d+\frac{\beta}{2}q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2(I_{1}+I_{2})\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha/2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}<\infty.

Therefore, the desired result follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.5
).
∎

Lemma 2.6

W^{\beta/2,1}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
, and we may assume
G_{\rho_{k}}\to G
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We now prove that
G
is a Green function of
L
on
\Omega
. It is enough to check (
1.3
). Let
\varphi\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)\cap C(\Omega)
and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy (
1.4
). Since
G_{\rho_{k}}\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
, we have from (
1.4
) and (
3.1
)

(3.11)

\langle G_{\rho_{k}}(\cdot,y_{0}),\psi\rangle=\mathcal{E}(G_{\rho_{k}}(\cdot,y_{0}),\varphi)=\fint_{B_{\rho_{k}}(y_{0})}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by (
3.10
) the left-hand side of (
3.11
) converges to
\langle G(\cdot,y_{0}),\psi\rangle
as
k\to\infty
. Clearly, the right-hand side of (
3.11
) converges to
\varphi(y_{0})
as
k\to\infty
. Thus, the equality (
1.3
) follows.

To prove (
1.5
), we set
\Omega_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:G(x)>t\}\subset\Omega
for
t>0
. By (
2.6
) and (
3.9
) we have

\|G_{\rho_{k}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\frac{d}{\alpha}|\Omega_{t}|^{\alpha/d}[G_{\rho_{k}}]_{L^{d/(d-\alpha)}_{\mathrm{weak}}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C\,|\Omega_{t}|^{\alpha/d}.

Since the
L^{1}
-norm is weakly lower semicontinuous, we obtain

t|\Omega_{t}|\leq\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|G_{\rho_{k}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C|\Omega_{t}|^{\alpha/d},

which implies

[G]_{L^{d/(d-\alpha)}_{\mathrm{weak}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sup_{t>0}t|\Omega_{t}|^{(d-\alpha)/d}\leq C,

where
C
depends only on
d
,
c_{\ast}
, and
\alpha_{0}
.

We next prove the uniqueness under the assumptions (
(

E
≥

)
) and (
(U1)
). Assume that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are Green functions of
L
on
\Omega
. Let
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By the unique solvability of the Dirichlet problem
[
FKV15
, Proposition 3.4]
, there is a unique weak solution
\varphi\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
of
-L\varphi=\psi
in
\Omega
, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}(\varphi,v)=\langle\psi,v\rangle
for all
v\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
. The weak solution
\varphi
is locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
, see
[
KW22
, Theorem 1.1 (ii)]
, which covers more general nonsymmetric operators. Note that the assumption (
(

E
≥

)
) implies (Poinc) and (Sob), and (
(U1)
) implies (Cutoff) and (
\infty
-Tail). Since
\varphi
is continuous, we conclude from the definition of the Green function

(3.12)

\langle G_{1}(\cdot,y),\psi\rangle=\varphi(y)=\langle G_{2}(\cdot,y),\psi\rangle

for every
y\in\Omega
. Since (
3.12
) holds for arbitrarily
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we deduce
G_{1}(\cdot,y)=G_{2}(\cdot,y)
a.e. in
\Omega
. Furthermore, since
G_{1}(\cdot,y)
and
G_{2}(\cdot,y)
are continuous on
\Omega\setminus\{y\}
, we conclude
G_{1}(x,y)=G_{2}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\Omega
,
x\neq y
.
∎

4.
Pointwise upper bounds

This section is devoted to pointwise upper bounds for Green functions as stated in
Theorem

1.4
. The main idea is to prove a local boundedness result in
Theorem

4.2
involving a nonlocal tail. We control the tail by means of the uniform estimate (
1.5
). The local boundedness with tail was first established in
[
DCKP16
]
for weak solutions to nonlocal operators and made robust in
[
Coz17
]
. It was further extended in
[
Sch20
]
to more general operators with kernels satisfying (
(

E
≥

)
) and (
(U2)
). The following uniform estimate is a simplified version of the result in
[
Sch20
, Theorem 11.10]
:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{+}^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}+r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

However, this is not perfectly fit to Green functions because
L^{2}
-integrability for Green functions is not guaranteed. We need
L^{q}
-average with
q\in(1,d/(d-\alpha))
instead of
L^{2}
-average in
(
4.1
). The local boundedness with
L^{q}
-average can be proved in the standard way as in
[
DCKP16
,
Coz17
,
Sch20
]
by using Moser’s iteration, but let us include the whole proof since it is nowhere written.

Let us first prove the following Caccioppoli-type estimates
which will be used in the proof of the local boundedness.

Lemma 4.1

(Caccioppoli estimates)
.

Let
u\in H(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
satisfy

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)\leq 0\quad\text{for every nonnegative}~\varphi\in H_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k).

For any
q>1
there exists a constant
C
, depending only on
q
,
such that for any nonnegative function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle\left(\eta(y)w^{q/2}(y)-\eta(x)w^{q/2}(x)\right)^{2}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}(w^{q}(y)+w^{q}(x))|\eta(y)-\eta(x)|^{2}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+C\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{\rho}(x_{0})}w(y)\eta^{2}(x)w^{q-1}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
w:=(u-k)_{+}
with
k\geq 0
.

In
Lemma

4.1
and
Theorem

4.2
we assume
u\in H(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
, which is sufficient for our purposes. Of course, one could weaken the assumption by assuming regularity of
u
only around the point
x_{0}
.

Proof.

In this proof, let
B=B_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]

be a smooth function with
\mathrm{supp}\,\eta\subset B
. By putting
\varphi=\eta^{2}w^{q-1}\in H_{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
into the equation (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}0\geq&~\int_{B}\int_{B}(u(y)-u(x))(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&+2\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B}(u(y)-u(x))(-\varphi(x))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

For
I_{1}
, we use an inequality

\displaystyle(u(y)-u(x))(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\geq(w(y)-w(x))\left(\eta^{2}(y)w^{q-1}(y)-\eta^{2}(x)w^{q-1}(x)\right)

and
Lemma

2.8
to obtain

(4.4)

\begin{split}I_{1}\geq&~\frac{q-1}{32q^{2}}\int_{B}\int_{B}\left(\eta(y)w^{q/2}(y)-\eta(x)w^{q/2}(x)\right)^{2}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&-2\left(1\lor\frac{1}{q-1}\right)\int_{B}\int_{B}(w^{q}(y)+w^{q}(x))|\eta(y)-\eta(x)|^{2}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,.\end{split}

For
I_{2}
, we observe that

\displaystyle(u(y)-u(x))(-\eta^{2}(x)w^{q-1}(x))\geq(u(y)-k)(-\eta^{2}(x)w^{q-1}(x))\geq-w(y)\eta^{2}(x)w^{q-1}(x),

from which we estimate

(4.5)

I_{2}\geq-2\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B}w(y)\eta^{2}(x)w^{q-1}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Combining (
4.3
), (
4.4
), and
(
4.5
), we conclude the lemma.
∎

We next prove the local boundedness with
L^{q}
-average and tail by using Moser’s iteration technique. See also
[
DCKP16
,
Coz17
,
Sch20
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
0<\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha<2
,
c_{\ast},c_{K}>0
, and assume that
k
satisfies (
(

E
≥

)
) and (
(U2)
). For any
q>1
, there exists a constant
C
, depending only on
d
,
q
,
c_{\ast}
,
c_{K}
, and
\alpha_{0}
, such that if
u\in H(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
satisfies (
4.2
), then

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{+}^{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/q}+r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

Proof.

For any
j=0,1,\cdots
, we define

\displaystyle r_{j}=(1+2^{-j})\frac{r}{2},\quad\tilde{r}_{j}=\frac{r_{j}+r_{j+1}}{2},\quad B_{j}=B_{r_{j}}(x_{0}),\quad\tilde{B}_{j}=B_{\tilde{r}_{j}}(x_{0}),

\displaystyle\eta_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{B}_{j}),\quad 0\leq\eta_{j}\leq 1,\quad\eta_{j}\equiv 1~\text{on}~B_{j+1},\quad|\nabla\eta_{j}|\leq 2^{j+3}/r,

\displaystyle k_{j}=(1-2^{-j})K,\quad\tilde{k}_{j}=\frac{k_{j+1}+k_{j}}{2}\quad\text{for some}~K\geq 0,~\text{which will be chosen later},

\displaystyle w_{j}=(u-k_{j})_{+},\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{w}_{j}=(u-\tilde{k}_{j})_{+}.

By
Lemma

4.1
with
\rho=r_{j},\eta=\eta_{j}
, and
k=\tilde{k}_{j}
, we obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}&\left(\eta_{j}(y)\tilde{w}_{j}^{q/2}(y)-\eta_{j}(x)\tilde{w}_{j}^{q/2}(x)\right)^{2}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq&~C\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}\left(\tilde{w}_{j}^{q}(y)+\tilde{w}_{j}^{q}(x)\right)|\eta_{j}(y)-\eta_{j}(x)|^{2}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&+C\int_{B_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{j}}\tilde{w}_{j}(y)\eta_{j}^{2}(x)\tilde{w}_{j}^{q-1}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Since
|\eta_{j}(y)-\eta_{j}(x)|^{2}\leq 2^{2j+6}r^{-2}|y-x|^{2}
, the symmetry of
k
and the assumption (
(U2)
) yield

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C2^{2j}r^{-2}(2-\alpha)\int_{B_{j}}\tilde{w}_{j}^{q}(x)\int_{B_{j}}|y-x|^{2-d-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C2^{2j}r^{d-2}(2-\alpha)\fint_{B_{j}}w_{j}^{q}(x)\int_{B_{j}}|y-x|^{2-d-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C2^{2j}r^{d-\alpha}\fint_{B_{j}}w_{j}^{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

where
C
depends on
d
,
q
,
c_{K}
, and
\alpha_{0}
. Note that the assumption (
(U1)
) is sufficient at this point for the estimate of
I_{1}
.

For
I_{2}
we use the inequalities
\tilde{w}_{j}\leq w_{0}=u_{+}
and

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{j}^{q-1}=(u-\tilde{k}_{j})_{+}^{q-1}\leq\frac{(u-k_{j})_{+}^{q}}{\tilde{k}_{j}-k_{j}}=\frac{w_{j}^{q}}{\tilde{k}_{j}-k_{j}}\leq 4K^{-1}2^{j}w_{j}^{q},

and the assumption (
(U2)
) to obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\frac{2^{j}}{K}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{j}}\frac{u_{+}(y)w_{j}^{q}(x)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
C=C(q,c_{K})>0
. If
x\in\tilde{B}_{j}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{j}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{|y-x|}\leq 1+\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|y-x|}\leq 1+\frac{\tilde{r}_{j}}{r_{j}-\tilde{r}_{j}}\leq C2^{j}.

Thus, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\frac{2^{j(d+\alpha+1)}}{K}r^{d-\alpha}\left(r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\fint_{B_{j}}w_{j}^{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

On the other hand, applying the
Theorem

2.3
(ii)
to
\eta_{j}\tilde{w}_{j}^{q/2}
leads us to

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{j+1}}\tilde{w}_{j}^{q\chi}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\chi}\leq C\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{B_{j}}\left(\eta_{j}\tilde{w}_{j}^{q/2},\eta_{j}\tilde{w}_{j}^{q/2}\right)+Cr_{j}^{-\alpha}\int_{B_{j}}\tilde{w}_{j}^{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\chi=d/(d-\alpha)
. Therefore, using (
(

E
≥

)
) we
combine inequalities (
4.6
)–(
4.9
) as

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{j+1}}\tilde{w}_{j}^{q\chi}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\chi}\leq C\left(2^{2j}+\frac{2^{j(d+\alpha+1)}}{K}r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\fint_{B_{j}}w_{j}^{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We set
A_{j}:=(\fint_{B_{j}}w_{j}^{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x)^{1/q}
and assume

(4.10)

\displaystyle K\geq r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Then, since

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{j}^{q\chi}=(u-\tilde{k}_{j})_{+}^{q\chi}\geq(k_{j+1}-\tilde{k}_{j})^{q(\chi-1)}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{q}=\left(\frac{K}{2^{j+2}}\right)^{q(\chi-1)}w_{j+1}^{q},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{A_{j+1}}{K}\leq\tilde{C}C_{0}^{j}\left(\frac{A_{j}}{K}\right)^{\chi},

where
\tilde{C}=2^{2(\chi-1)}C^{\chi/q}

and
C_{0}=2^{\frac{\chi(d+\alpha+1)}{q}-1+\chi}>1
. It will follow that

A_{j}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
, provided that
A_{0}\leq\tilde{C}^{-\frac{1}{\chi-1}}C_{0}^{-\frac{1}{(\chi-1)^{2}}}K
. Thus, we choose

\displaystyle K=\tilde{C}^{\frac{1}{\chi-1}}C_{0}^{\frac{1}{(\chi-1)^{2}}}A_{0}+r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

which is in accordance with (
4.10
). Note that since
(\chi-1)^{-1}\leq 1

and
\chi/(\chi-1)=d/\alpha\leq d/\alpha_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{C}^{\frac{1}{\chi-1}}C_{0}^{\frac{1}{(\chi-1)^{2}}}=4C^{\frac{\chi}{q(\chi-1)}}2^{\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}\frac{d+\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{1}{\chi-1}}\leq 4C^{\frac{d}{q\alpha_{0}}}2^{\frac{d(d+3)}{q\alpha_{0}}+1}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u\leq K\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{+}^{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/q}+r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
,
c_{\ast}
,
c_{K}
and
\alpha_{0}
.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the upper bound of Green functions
using
Theorem

4.2
and the uniform estimates (
1.5
).

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Let
G
be the Green function of
L
on
\Omega
, which is the limit of regularized Green functions
G_{\rho}
. Let
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
with
x_{0}\neq y_{0}
and assume
r:=|x_{0}-y_{0}|/2\geq\rho
. We first consider the case
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. Since
\mathcal{E}(G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0}),\varphi)=0
for all
\varphi\in H_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
,
Theorem

4.2
shows that for any
q>1

(4.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})\leq Cr^{-d/q}\|G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})\|_{L^{q}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}+r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{G_{\rho}(x,y_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
,
c_{\ast}
,
c_{K}
, and
\alpha_{0}
. Note that the essential supremum in the left-hand side of (
4.11
) is
realized as the supremum by the interior regularity results
[
KW22
,
DK20
]
. In particular, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle G_{\rho}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0}).

Let us choose
q=(1+d/(d-\alpha_{0}))/2\in(1,d/(d-\alpha))

so that the constant
C
in (
4.11
) depends on
d
,
c_{\ast}
,
c_{K}
, and
\alpha_{0}
.
Moreover, this choice of
q
also makes the constant in the
following estimates depend only on
d
,
c_{\ast}
,
c_{K}
, and
\alpha_{0}
: by the inequality (
2.6
) with
p=d/(d-\alpha)
we have

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C\left(1+\frac{q}{p-q}\right)^{1/q}r^{\alpha-d}[G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})]_{L^{d/(d-\alpha)}_{\mathrm{weak}}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}.

Using (
3.9
), we obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq Cr^{\alpha-d},

where we used

\displaystyle p-q\geq\frac{d}{d-\alpha_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{d}{d-\alpha_{0}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\alpha_{0}}{d-\alpha_{0}}.

To estimate
I_{2}
, we split it into two integrals:

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&=r^{\alpha}\int_{B_{r/2}^{c}(x_{0})\cap\{G\leq r^{\alpha-d}\}}\frac{G_{\rho}(x,y_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x+r^{\alpha}\int_{B_{r/2}^{c}(x_{0})\cap\{G_{\rho}>r^{\alpha-d}\}}\frac{G_{\rho}(x,y_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=:I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}.\end{split}

The term
I_{2,1}
can be easily computed as

(4.15)

\displaystyle I_{2,1}\leq r^{2\alpha-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{d+\alpha}}=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{\alpha}r^{2\alpha-d}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{-\alpha}\leq\frac{4|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{\alpha_{0}}r^{\alpha-d}.

For
I_{2,2}
we have

\displaystyle I_{2,2}\leq 2^{d+\alpha}r^{-d}\int_{\{G_{\rho}>r^{\alpha-d}\}}G_{\rho}(x,y_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{d+2}r^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{\{G_{\rho}>r^{\alpha-d}\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\{G_{\rho}>t\}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x.

By utilizing the Fubini Theorem we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2,2}\leq Cr^{-d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\{G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})>(r^{\alpha-d}\lor t)\}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t.

We now make use of the estimate (
3.9
) and deduce

(4.16)

I_{2,2}\leq Cr^{-d}[G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})]_{L^{d/(d-\alpha)}_{\mathrm{weak}}(\Omega)}^{d/(d-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(r^{\alpha-d}\lor t)^{-\frac{d}{d-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq Cr^{\alpha-d},

where
C=C(d,c_{\ast},\alpha_{0})>0
. By combining (
4.11
)–(
4.16
), we conclude that

\displaystyle G_{\rho}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq C|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{\alpha-d},

where
C=C(d,c_{\ast},c_{K},\alpha_{0})>0
, from which (
1.6
) follows by taking
\rho\to 0
.

Finally, let us consider the case
B_{r}(x_{0})\not\subset\Omega
. In this case we
consider a bounded open set
\tilde{\Omega}\supset\Omega
such that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\tilde{\Omega}
and let
\tilde{G}
be the Green function
of
L
on
\tilde{\Omega}
, which is defined as the limit of regularized Green functions
\tilde{G}_{\rho}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left(G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})-\tilde{G}_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0}),\varphi\right)=0\quad\text{for all}~\varphi\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k).

Since
G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})=0

\mathrm{a.e.}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
, we have
G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})\leq\tilde{G}_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})

\mathrm{a.e.}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
.
Thus, using
\varphi:=(G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})-\tilde{G}_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0}))_{+}\in H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d};k)
as a test function we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{E}\left(G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})-\tilde{G}_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0}),\varphi\right)\geq\mathcal{E}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq c_{\ast}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq 0.

Here we used the assumption (
(

E
≥

)
). Therefore, we have
\varphi=0

\mathrm{a.e.}
in

\Omega
, which in turn implies
G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})\leq\tilde{G}_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})

\mathrm{a.e.}
in
\Omega
. Since
G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})-\tilde{G}_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})
is Hölder
continuous in
\Omega\setminus\{y_{0}\}
, we have
G_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})\leq\tilde{G}_{\rho}(\cdot,y_{0})
in
\Omega\setminus\{y_{0}\}
.
The upper bound of
G_{\rho}
follows from the upper bound of
\tilde{G}_{\rho}
.
∎

5.
Pointwise lower bounds

The aim of this section is to prove the pointwise lower bounds for the Green function by modifying the classical proof for second order differential operators in
[
GW82
]
. The main tool for this proof (
[
GW82

The Harnack inequality (
(H)
) for Green function on the annulus
B_{2r}(y)\setminus B_{r}(y)
implies the same inequalities on larger annuli by standard covering argument.

Lemma 5.5
.

Condition (
(H)
) implies the following condition: For every
M>2
there is
c=c(M,c_{H})>0
such that for every ball
B_{Mr}(y)\Subset\Omega

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{Mr}(y)\setminus B_{r}(y)}G(\cdot,y)\leq c\inf_{B_{Mr}(y)\setminus B_{r}(y)}G(\cdot,y).

Proof.

We write
B_{R}=B_{R}(y)
. Note that the infimum of
G(\cdot,y)
over

B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}
is attained in some smaller annulus, say

(5.18)

\inf_{B_{R+r/4}\setminus B_{R}}G(\cdot,y)=\inf_{B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}}G(\cdot,y)

for some
B_{R+r/4}\setminus B_{R}\subset B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}
.

Let us now cover the annulus
B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}
with
overlapping annuli to use the chaining argument.
There exist an integer
N=N(M)\in\mathbb{N}
and
a sequence of radii
r=r_{0}<\cdots<r_{N}=Mr/2
such that

2r_{j-1}-r_{j}>r/2
for all
j=1,\dots,N
.
We define
A_{j}=B_{2r_{j}}\setminus B_{r_{j}}
, then the family of
annuli
\{A_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
satisfies the following properties:

\begin{split}&B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}=\cup_{j=0}^{N}A_{j}\quad\text{and}\\
&B_{r_{j}+3r/8}\setminus B_{r_{j}+r/8}\subset A_{j-1}\cap A_{j}\quad\text{for}~j=1,\dots,N.\end{split}

In other words, the annulus
B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}
is covered by
\{A_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
and each intersection
A_{j-1}\cap A_{j}
between adjacent annuli has enough space to contain an annulus whose radius difference is
r/4
. Therefore,
B_{R}\setminus B_{R+r/4}\subset A_{j_{0}}
for some

j_{0}\in\{0,\dots,N\}
.

For any
j=0,\dots,N-1
, we observe that

\sup_{A_{j}}G\leq c_{H}\inf_{A_{j}}G\leq c_{H}\inf_{A_{j}\cap A_{j+1}}G\leq c_{H}\sup_{A_{j}\cap A_{j+1}}G\leq c_{H}\sup_{A_{j+1}}G

by (
(H)
). Similarly, we also have
\sup_{A_{j+1}}G\leq c_{H}\sup_{A_{j}}G
.
Therefore, we have by (
5.18
)

\sup_{A_{j}}G\leq c_{H}^{|j-j_{0}|}\sup_{A_{j_{0}}}G\leq c_{H}^{N+1}\inf_{A_{j_{0}}}G\leq c_{H}^{N+1}\inf_{B_{R+r/4}\setminus B_{R}}G=c_{H}^{N+1}\inf_{B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}}G

for any
j=0,\dots,N
. We conclude the lemma by observing that

\sup_{B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}}G\leq\max_{j}\bigg\{\sup_{A_{j}}G\bigg\}\leq c_{H}^{N+1}\inf_{B_{Mr}\setminus B_{r}}G.

∎

Since
x_{0}\in A^{3r/2}_{\varepsilon r/2}(y_{0})
, we conclude the theorem.
∎

6.
Symmetry

This section is devoted to the proof of the symmetry of the Green function.

Proof of
Theorem

1.7
.

Let
x,y\in\Omega
with
x\neq y
. Recall that the Green function
G
of
L
on
\Omega
is constructed by a sequence of regularized Green functions. Thus, we have a sequence
\rho_{i}<|x-y|/3
converging to zero and a sequence of regularized Green functions
G_{\rho_{i}}(\cdot,y)
converging to
G(\cdot,y)
. Let
\sigma_{j}<|x-y|/3
be another sequence converging to zero and let
G_{\sigma_{j}}(\cdot,x)
be the corresponding regularized Green functions converging to
G(\cdot,x)
. Then we have

\displaystyle a_{ij}:=\fint_{B_{\rho_{i}}(y)}G_{\sigma_{j}}(z,x)\,\mathrm{d}z=\mathcal{E}(G_{\rho_{i}}(\cdot,y),G_{\sigma_{j}}(\cdot,x))=\fint_{B_{\sigma_{j}}(x)}G_{\rho_{i}}(z,y)\,\mathrm{d}z.

Since
G_{\sigma_{j}}(\cdot,x)
weakly converges to
G(\cdot,x)
in
W^{\beta/2,q}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
\beta\in(0,\alpha)
and
q\in[1,d/(d-\alpha/2))
, and
G(\cdot,y)
is continuous on
B_{\sigma_{j}}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}a_{ij}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\fint_{B_{\rho_{i}}(y)}G(z,x)\,\mathrm{d}z=G(y,x).

In the same way we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}a_{ij}=G(x,y).

By
[
KW22
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have the uniform estimate on the Hölder norm of
G_{\rho_{i}}(\cdot,y)
on
B_{\sigma_{j}}(x)
which is independent of
i
. Therefore, the double sequence
a_{ij}
converges uniformly in
j
with respect to
i
. The proof is complete.
∎

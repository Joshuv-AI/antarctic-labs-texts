Quantum thermodynamics of holographic quenches and bounds on the growth of entanglement from the QNEC
Tanay Kibe, Ayan Mukhopadhyay and Pratik Roy

Abstract
The quantum null energy condition (QNEC) is a lower bound on the energy-momentum tensor in terms of the variation of the entanglement entropy of a sub-region along a null direction. To gain insights into quantum thermodynamics of many-body systems, we study if the QNEC restricts irreversible entropy production in quenches driven by energy-momentum inflow from an infinite memoryless bath in two-dimensional holographic theories. We find that an increase in both entropy and temperature, as implied by the Clausius inequality of classical thermodynamics, are necessary but not sufficient to not violate QNEC in quenches leading to transitions between thermal states with momentum which are dual to Banados-Teitelboim-Zanelli geometries. For an arbitrary initial state, we can determine the lower and upper bounds on the increase of entropy (temperature) for a fixed increase in temperature (entropy). Our results provide explicit instances of quantum lower and upper bounds on irreversible entropy production whose existence has been established in literature. We also find monotonic behavior of the non-saturation of the QNEC with time after a quench, and analytically determine their asymptotic values. Our study shows that the entanglement entropy of an interval of length l l always thermalizes in time l / 2 l/2 with an exponent 3 / 2 3/2 . Furthermore, we determine the coefficient of initial quadratic growth of entanglement analytically for any l l , and show that the slope of the asymptotic ballistic growth of entanglement for a semi-infinite interval is twice the difference of the entropy densities of the final and initial states. We determine explicit upper and lower bounds on these rates of growth of entanglement.

When any system with finite energy interacts with a memoryless infinitely large energy bath, its entropy can only increase monotonically. In holographic QFTs, this feature is reproduced via the monotonic growth of the area of the apparent and event horizons, and eventual thermalization following a quench

In this work, we consider fast quenches that lead to transitions between thermal states carrying momentum in 2D holographic systems, and establish that the QNEC implies more than the mere rise of both the temperature and the thermodynamic entropy. For a fixed increase in the entropy (temperature), the increase in temperature (entropy) has to be bounded from both above and below so that the QNEC is unviolated after quench. Our results thus provide explicit instances of the upper and lower bounds on irreversible entropy production in quantum many-body systems whose existence has been established using tools of quantum information theory

Our holographic results apply when the central charge

IV
Discussion

Our result establishing lower and upper bounds on irreversible entropy production in holographic CFTs after quenches via the application of QNEC offers a novel perspective on the quantum thermodynamics of many-body systems.

As detailed in the Supplemental Material, our methods allow study of fast quenches between arbitrary
quantum equilibrium states

Ecker et al. 2019
which saturate the QNEC inequalities (
1
). These states can be essentially described as Virasoro hair on top of vacuum and thermal states, and are dual to Banados geometries
Banados 1999
. Following our methods, an erasure protocol for quantum information encoded in Virasoro hair has been implemented
Banerjee et al. 2022
. The QNEC inequalities reproduce the Landauer principle
Sagawa 2017
;
Esposito et al. 2010
;
Reeb and Wolf 2014
and also demonstrate that certain types of encoding are tolerant against erasures faster than any microscopic timescale
Banerjee et al. 2022
. A more general study of transitions between quantum equilibrium states should lead to novel consequences for various quantum channels.

IV.1
Quenched Bañados geometries and the uniformization map

In the main text we describe transitions between rotating BTZ black branes. These are a special case of transitions between the more general Bañados geometries
Banados 1999
defined below.
Quenches leading to transitions between quantum equilibrium states (dual to Bañados geometries) can be described by dual metrics of the form (see also
Sfetsos 1995
)

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\rm d}r{\rm d}t-\left(\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}-2m(t,x)L^{2}\right){\rm d}t^{2}+2j(t,x)L^{2}{\rm d}t{\rm d}x

(15)

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}{\rm d}x^{2}

in the ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein gauge. This geometry is supported by a bulk stress tensor
T_{MN}
that is conserved and traceless in the background metric (
15
) and whose non-vanishing components are

\displaystyle T_{tt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q(t,x)L^{2}}{r}+\frac{\partial_{x}p(t,x)L^{4}}{r^{2}}+\frac{p(t,x)j(t,x)L^{6}}{r^{3}},

\displaystyle\,\,T_{tx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p(t,x)L^{2}}{r}.

(16)

The gravitational equations (with
\Lambda=-1/L^{2}
)

R_{MN}-\frac{1}{2}RG_{MN}-\frac{1}{L^{2}}G_{MN}=8\pi G\,T_{MN}

(17)

are satisfied simply by requiring

\displaystyle\partial_{t}m(t,x)-\partial_{x}j(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi Gq(t,x),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j(t,x)-\partial_{x}m(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi Gp(t,x).

(18)

In absence of bulk matter, we therefore obtain

\displaystyle m(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{+}(x^{+})+\mathcal{L}_{-}(x^{-}),

\displaystyle j(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{+}(x^{+})-\mathcal{L}_{-}(x^{-}).

(19)

with
x^{\pm}=t\pm x
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
being arbitrary chiral functions. These solutions are known as Banados geometries
Banados 1999
and are related to the vacuum solution (for which
m(t,x)=j(t,x)=0
) locally by a diffeomorphism that is non-vanishing at the boundary and is dual to a conformal transformation as will be shown later. See
Compère et al. 2016
;
Sheikh-Jabbari and Yavartanoo 2016
for detailed CFT interpretation. The dual CFT states have been called quantum equilibrium states since they saturate the QNEC inequalities (
1
)
Ecker et al. 2019
.

The gravitational constraints (
IV.1
) imply that the metric (
2
) can describe instantaneous transitions between arbitrary quantum equilibrium states at
t=0
with the initial (
i
) and final (
f
) chiral functions being
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}^{i,f}
so that

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(-t)(\mathcal{L}^{i}_{+}(x^{+})+\mathcal{L}^{i}_{-}(x^{-}))+\theta(t)(\mathcal{L}^{f}_{+}(x^{+})+\mathcal{L}^{f}_{-}(x^{-})),

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(-t)(\mathcal{L}^{i}_{+}(x^{+})-\mathcal{L}^{i}_{-}(x^{-}))+\theta(t)(\mathcal{L}^{f}_{+}(x^{+})-\mathcal{L}^{f}_{-}(x^{-})),

(20)

if we set

\displaystyle 8\pi Gq

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(t)(\mathcal{L}^{f}_{+}(x)-\mathcal{L}^{i}_{+}(x)+\mathcal{L}^{f}_{-}(-x)-\mathcal{L}^{i}_{-}(-x)),

\displaystyle 8\pi Gp

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(t)(\mathcal{L}^{f}_{+}(x)-\mathcal{L}^{i}_{+}(x)-\mathcal{L}^{f}_{-}(-x)+\mathcal{L}^{i}_{-}(-x)).

(21)

Holographic renormalization
Henningson and Skenderis 1998
;
Balasubramanian and Kraus 1999
of the on-shell gravitational action for the metric (
2
) can be used to extract the expectation value of the energy-momentum tensor of the dual state (living in flat Minkowski metric) and it is (with
c=3L/2G
):

\langle t_{\pm\pm}\rangle=\frac{c}{24\pi}(m(t,x)\pm j(t,x)),\quad\langle t_{+-}\rangle=0.

(22)

Any Bañados geometry (
IV.1
) can be uniformized to the Poincaré patch metric. The uniformization map is defined in terms of boundary lightcone coordinates
X_{b}^{\pm}(x^{\pm})
that satisfy

{\rm Sch}(X_{b}^{\pm}(x^{\pm}),x^{\pm})=-2\mathcal{L}_{\pm}(x^{\pm})

(23)

where
{\rm Sch}
denotes the Schwarzian derivative. The uniformization map, which takes the metric (
2
) to the Poincaré patch metric with coordinates
T
,
X
and
R
is

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(X_{b}^{+}(x^{+})+X_{b}^{-}(x^{-})

\displaystyle+\frac{{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime}(x^{+})+{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime}(x^{-})-2\sqrt{{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime}(x^{+}){X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime}(x^{-})}}{\frac{r}{L^{2}}-\frac{{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime\prime}(x^{+})}{2{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime}(x^{+})}-\frac{{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime\prime}(x^{-})}{2{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime}(x^{-})}}\Bigg),

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(X_{b}^{+}(x^{+})-X_{b}^{-}(x^{-})+\frac{{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime}(x^{+})-{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime}(x^{-})}{\frac{r}{L^{2}}-\frac{{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime\prime}(x^{+})}{2{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime}(x^{+})}-\frac{{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime\prime}(x^{-})}{2{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime}(x^{-})}}\Bigg),

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{2}\frac{{\frac{r}{L^{2}}-\frac{{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime\prime}(x^{+})}{2{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime}(x^{+})}-\frac{{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime\prime}(x^{-})}{2{X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime}(x^{-})}}}{\sqrt{{X_{b}^{+}}^{\prime}(x^{+}){X_{b}^{-}}^{\prime}(x^{-})}}.

(24)

It is easy to see that at the boundary
r=\infty
,
X^{\pm}=T\pm X
reduces to
X_{b}^{\pm}(x^{\pm})
respectively – so the boundary lightcones are reparametrized following (
23
). The boundary metric, which is identified with the physical metric on which the CFT lives, still remains the flat Minkowski space. The uniformization map transforms
\langle t_{\pm\pm}\rangle
of the dual quantum equilibrium state to zero in agreement with the conformal anomaly of the dual CFT, provided the central charge is indeed
c=3L/(2G)
. For BTZ black brane solutions we choose

X_{b}^{\pm}(x^{\pm})=L\exp(2\mu_{\pm}x^{\pm}).

(25)

The uniformization map is valid only outside the outer horizon
r_{out}=L^{2}(\mu^{+}+\mu^{-})
with the latter mapping to the Poincaré horizon
R=0
. Note that (
23
) determines
X_{b}^{\pm}(x^{\pm})
only up to an overall
SL(2,R)
(fractional linear) transformation, which accounts for the local
SL(2,R)\times SL(2,R)
isometries of Banados metrics. It is convenient to fix these redundancies by choosing
X_{b}^{\pm}
such that
\infty
is a fixed point,
X_{b}^{\pm}(-\infty)=0
and
X_{b}^{\pm}(0)=L
.

IV.2
Computing the entanglement entropy and QNEC

by the cut and glue method

Let
x_{1,2}^{\pm}
denote the lightcone coordinates of the endpoints of the entangling interval whose entanglement entropy is of interest. In the dual Banados spacetime we place these points at the boundary
r=\infty
. When we map this spacetime to the Poincaré patch metric via the uniformization map (
IV.1
), the endpoints get mapped to
(X_{b}^{+}(x_{1}^{+}),X_{b}^{-}(x_{1}^{-}))
and
(X_{b}^{+}(x_{2}^{+}),X_{b}^{-}(x_{2}^{-}))
at the boundary
R=\infty
. These endpoints are denoted as
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
in Fig.
1
. In what follows, it is useful to redefine the Poincaré patch radial coordinate as
Z=L^{2}/R
so that the boundary is at
Z=0
. Explicitly, the endpoints of the entangling interval at the boundary are at

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{b}^{+}(x_{1}^{+})+X_{b}^{-}(x_{1}^{-})),X_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{b}^{+}(x_{1}^{+})-X_{b}^{-}(x_{1}^{-})),

\displaystyle Z_{1}=0,

\displaystyle T_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{b}^{+}(x_{2}^{+})+X_{b}^{-}(x_{2}^{-})),X_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{b}^{+}(x_{2}^{+})-X_{b}^{-}(x_{2}^{-})),

\displaystyle Z_{2}=0.

(26)

The equations for the geodesic in the Poincaré patch metric connecting any two endpoints
(T_{1},X_{1},Z_{1})
and
(T_{2},X_{2},Z_{2})
are given by the two equations

\displaystyle Z+T-\frac{T_{1}+T_{2}}{2}-\frac{T_{2}-T_{1}}{X_{2}-X_{1}}\left(X-\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}}{2}\right)=0,

\displaystyle\frac{Z^{2}}{(X_{2}-X_{1})^{2}-(T_{2}-T_{1})^{2}}-\frac{(2X-X_{1}-X_{2})^{2}}{4(X_{2}-X_{1})^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}.

(27)

As shown in Fig.
1
, when the endpoints lie in the post-quench geometry, the above geodesic is cut into three segments by the cut and glue prescription described in the main text.
The two gluing hypersurfaces, which are simply the respective images of the hypersurface
t=0
are:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{i,f}=(T^{i,f}(0,x,r),X^{i,f}(0,x,r),R^{i,f}(0,x,r)).

(28)

The intersection points of the geodesic and the gluing hypersurface
\Sigma^{f}
parametrized by the physical coordinates
x
and
r
can be found by substituting (
IV.2
) and (
28
) into the above two equations (
IV.2
) for the geodesic, and solving for
x
and
r
. We obtain at most two pairs of solutions giving the two intersection points
q_{1}(x_{1}^{*},r_{1}^{*})
and
q_{2}(x_{2}^{*},r_{2}^{*})
shown in Fig.
1
. The image of the intersection points on
\Sigma^{i}
is obtained simply by substituting the values of
x_{1,2}^{*}
and
r_{1,2}^{*}
for the equation describing
\Sigma^{i}
in (
28
). The geodesic with these endpoints in the pre-quench Poincaré patch is then completed by the arc following the equations (
IV.2
) with endpoints chosen to be these intersection points (see Fig.
1
). To compute the length of the arcs we simply need the endpoints explicitly and employ the invariant distance formula mentioned in the main text.

For BTZ geometries, the explicit form of
X_{b}^{\pm}
given by (
25
) allows for explicit analytic solutions of the intersection points at early time and near thermalization time (
l/2
) generically, and for all times when the final state is non-rotating. When the boundary interval is at
t=l/2
the geodesic intersects
\Sigma^{f}
tangentially and lies entirely in the post-quench Poincaré patch otherwise and the two intersection points merge into one as given by

r_{*}=L^{2}(\mu_{+}^{f}\coth(\mu_{+}^{f}l)+\mu_{-}^{f}\coth(\mu_{-}^{f}l)),\,x_{*}=l/2.

(29)

For
t>l/2
, the geodesic does not intersect the gluing hypersurface. When
t\sim 0
, the intersection points can be computed in power series in

.

It is obvious that the above computation can be repeated after displacing one of the two boundary endpoints to obtain
\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}
.

We should be able to use this method also to study retarded correlation functions in quenched geometries following
Banerjee et al. 2016
– in this case one needs to analytically continue the bulk fields across the gluing surface.

IV.3
Explicit results for
S_{\rm ent}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}
for quenches leading to a non-rotating BTZ state

Since the intersection points

\mu_{\pm}^{i}

\mu_{\pm}^{f}=\mu

\displaystyle S_{\rm ent}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{6}\ln\Big(\frac{4\mu^{2}\cosh^{2}(2\mu t)-(\mu_{+}^{i}-\mu_{-}^{i})^{2}\sinh^{2}(2\mu t)}{4\mu^{2}\cosh(2\mu t)^{\frac{\mu_{+}^{i}+\mu_{-}^{i}}{\mu}}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{c}{6}\ln\left(\frac{\sinh(\mu_{+}^{i}l)\sinh(\mu_{-}^{i}l)}{\epsilon^{2}\mu_{+}^{i}\mu_{-}^{i}}\right)+\cdots,

denoting terms which vanish as

10

;

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{24}\,\frac{e^{4\mu t}(\mu_{+}^{i}+\mu_{-}^{i}){\rm sech}^{2}(2\mu t)}{2\mu-(\mu_{+}^{i}-\mu_{-}^{i})\tanh(2\mu t)}\Big(2\mu(2\mu+\mu_{-}^{i}-3\mu_{+}^{i})

\displaystyle+(\mu_{+}^{i}-\mu_{-}^{i})(2\mu+\mu_{+}^{i}+\mu_{-}^{i})\tanh(2\mu t)\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{24}\,\frac{e^{-4\mu t}(\mu_{+}^{i}+\mu_{-}^{i}){\rm sech}^{2}(2\mu t)}{2\mu-(\mu_{+}^{i}-\mu_{-}^{i})\tanh(2\mu t))}\Big(2\mu(2\mu+\mu_{+}^{i}-3\mu_{-}^{i})

(31)

\displaystyle+(\mu_{+}^{i}-\mu_{-}^{i})(2\mu+\mu_{+}^{i}+\mu_{-}^{i})\tanh(2\mu t)\Big).

Requiring

IV.4
Computing
\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}
for the semi-infinite interval and determining allowed quenches

For arbitrary quenches between rotating thermal states, the QNEC inequality (
1

\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}
for this semi-infinite interval analytically just after the quench i.e. when

and is small, and also when
t
is large, as in both cases we can obtain analytic expressions for the intersection points between the geodesic and the gluing hypersurface. At
t=0

\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{+}(t=0^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{24}\big(3({\mu_{+}^{f}}^{2}-{\mu_{+}^{i}}^{2})

(32)

\displaystyle+(\mu_{-}^{f}-\mu_{-}^{i})(2\mu_{+}^{i}-2\mu_{+}^{f}-\mu_{-}^{f}-\mu_{-}^{i})\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{-}(t=0^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{24}\big(3({\mu_{-}^{f}}^{2}-{\mu_{-}^{i}}^{2})

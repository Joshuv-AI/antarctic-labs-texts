Abstract




N(k)
-contact metric as
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton

Abstract:
The aim of this paper is characterize a class of contact metric manifolds admitting
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton. It is shown that if a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
N(k)
-contact metric manifold
M
admits
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton or
\ast
-conformal gradient Ricci soliton, then the manifold
M
is
\ast
-Ricci flat and locally isometric to the Riemannian of a flat
(n+1)
-dimensional manifold and an
n
-dimensional manifold of constant curvature
4
for
n>1
and flat for
n=1
. Further, for the first case, the soliton vector field is conformal and for the
\ast
-gradient case, the potential function
f
is either harmonic or satisfy a Poisson equation. Finally, an example is presented to support the results.

N(k)
-contact metric manifolds,
\ast
-Ricci tensor, Conformal Ricci soliton,
\ast

1.
Introduction

In 2004, Fischer
[
10
]
introduced the notion of conformal Ricci flow as a variation of the classical Ricci flow equation. Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed, connected, oriented differentiable manifold. Then the conformal Ricci flow on
M
is defined by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}+2(S+\frac{g}{n})=-pg\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;r=-1,

where
p
is a time dependent non-dynamical scalar field,
S
is the
(0,2)
symmetric Ricci tensor and
r
is the scalar curvature of the manifold.

The concept of conformal Ricci soliton was introduced by Basu and Bhattacharyya
[
1
]
on a
(2n+1)
-dimensional Kenmotsu manifold as

\displaystyle\pounds_{V}g+2S=[2\lambda-(p+\frac{2}{2n+1})]g,

where
\lambda
is a constant and
\pounds_{V}
is the Lie derivative along the vector field V. This notion was studied by Dey and Majhi
[
7
]
, Nagaraja and Venu
[
13
]
and many others on several contact metric manifolds.

In 2002, Hamada
[
11
]
defined the
\ast
-Ricci tensor on real hypersurfaces of complex space forms by

\displaystyle S^{\ast}(X,Y)=g(Q^{\ast}X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}(trace(\phi\circ R(X,\phi Y)))

for any vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
, where
Q^{\ast}
is the
(1,1)

\ast
-Ricci operator. The
\ast
-scalar curvature
r^{\ast}
is defined by
r^{\ast}=trace(Q^{\ast})
. A Riemannian manifold
M
is called
\ast
-Ricci flat if
S^{\ast}
vanishes identically.

Recently, several notions related to the
\ast
-Ricci tensor were introduced. In 2014, the notion of
\ast
-Ricci soliton
[
12
]
was introduced and further widely studied by several authors. In 2019, the notion of
\ast
-critical point equation
[
8
]
was introduced and further studied by the authors in
[
9
]
. In this paper, we study the notion of
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton defined as

Definition 1.1
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
(2n+1)\geq 3
is said to admit
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton
(g,V,\lambda)
if

\displaystyle\pounds_{V}g+2S^{\ast}=[2\lambda-(p+\frac{2}{2n+1})]g,

(1.1)

where
\lambda
is a constant, provided
S^{\ast}
is symmetric.

A
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton is said to be
\ast
-conformal gradient Ricci soliton if the vector field
V
is gradient of some smooth function
f
on
M
. In this case, the
\ast
-conformal gradient Ricci soliton is given by

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f+S^{\ast}=[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})]g,

(1.2)

where
(\nabla^{2}f)(X,Y)=Hessf(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}Df,Y)
is the Hessian of
f
and
D
is the gradient operator.

Note that, the
\ast
-Ricci tensor is not symmetric in general. Hence, for a non-symmetric
\ast
-Ricci tensor of a manifold, the above notion is incosistant. In a
N(k)
-contact metric manifold,
S^{\ast}
is symmetric (given later) and hence, the above definition is well defined on
N(k)
-contact metric manifolds.

The present paper is organized as follows: In section 2, we recall some preliminary results from the literature of
N(k)
-contact metric manifolds. Section 3 deals with
N(k)
-contact metric manifolds admitting
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton and
\ast
-conformal gradient Ricci soliton. In the final section, we present an example to verify our results.

2.
Preliminaries

A
(2n+1)
-dimensional almost contact metric manifold
M
is a smooth manifold together with a structure
(\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
satisfying

\displaystyle\phi^{2}X=-X+\eta(X)\xi,\;\;\eta(\xi)=1,\;\;\phi\xi=0,\;\;\eta\circ\phi=0,

(2.1)

\displaystyle g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)

(2.2)

for any vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
, where
\phi
is a
(1,1)
tensor field,
\xi
is a unit vector field,
\eta
is a one form defined by
\eta(X)=g(X,\xi)
and
g
is the Riemannian metric. Using (
2.2
), we can easily see that
\phi
is skew-symmetric, that is,

\displaystyle g(\phi X,Y)=-g(X,\phi Y).

(2.3)

An almost contact metric structure becomes a contact metric structure if
g(\phi X,Y)=d\eta(X,Y)
for all vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
. On a contact metric manifold, the
(1,1)
-tensor field
h
is defined as
h=\frac{1}{2}\pounds_{\xi}\phi
. The tensor field
h
is symmetric and satisfies

\displaystyle h\phi=-\phi h,\;\;trace(h)=trace(\phi h)=0,\;\;h\xi=0.

(2.4)

Also on a contact metric manifold, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\xi=-\phi X-\phi hX.

(2.5)

In
[
16
]
, Tanno introduced the notion of
k
-nullity distribution on a Riemannian manifold as

\displaystyle N(k)=\{Z\in T(M):R(X,Y)Z=k[g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y]\},

k
being a real number and
T(M)
is the Lie algebra of all vector fields on
M
. If the characteristic vector field
\xi\in N(k)
, then we call a contact metric manifold as
N(k)
-contact metric manifold
[
16
]
. However, for a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
N(k)
-contact metric manifold, we have ( see
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
)

\displaystyle h^{2}=(k-1)\phi^{2},

(2.6)

\displaystyle R(X,Y)\xi=k[\eta(Y)X-\eta(X)Y],

(2.7)

\displaystyle R(\xi,X)Y=k[g(X,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)X],

(2.8)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\eta)Y=g(X+hX,\phi Y),

(2.9)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\phi)Y=g(X+hX,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)(X+hX),

(2.10)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\phi h)Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[g(X,hY)+(k-1)g(X,-Y+\eta(Y)\xi)]\xi

(2.11)

\displaystyle+\eta(Y)[hX+(k-1)(-X+\eta(X)\xi)]

for any vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
, where
R
is the Riemann curvature tensor. For further details on
N(k)
-contact metric manifolds, we refer the reader to go through thereferences (
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
) and references therein.

3.
\ast
-Conformal Ricci soliton

In this section, we study the notion of
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton in the framework of
N(k)
-contact metric manifolds. To prove the main theorems, we need the following lemmas:

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
3
]
)
A contact metric manifold
M^{2n+1}
satisfying the condition
R(X,Y)\xi=0
for all
X
,
Y
is locally isometric to the Riemannian product of a flat
(n+1)
-dimensional manifold and an
n
-dimensional manifold of positive curvature
4
, i.e.,
E^{n+1}(0)\times S^{n}(4)
for
n>1
and flat for
n=1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
8
]
)
A
(2n+1)
-dimensional
N(k)
-contact metric manifold is
\ast
-
\eta
-Einstein and the
\ast
-Ricci tensor is given by

\displaystyle S^{\ast}(X,Y)=-k[g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)].

(3.1)

Note 3.3
.

We observe from lemma
3.2
that the
\ast
-Ricci tensor
S^{\ast}
of a
N(k)
-contact metric manifold is symmetric, i.e.,
S^{\ast}(X,Y)=S^{\ast}(Y,X)
. Hence, the notion of
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton is consistent in this setting.

Lemma 3.4
.

On a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
N(k)
-contact metric manifold
M
, the
\ast
-Ricci tensor
S^{\ast}
satisfies the following relation:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast})(X,Y)-(\nabla_{X}S^{\ast})(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}S^{\ast})(X,Z)

\displaystyle=-2k[\eta(Y)g(\phi X,Z)+\eta(Y)g(\phi X,hZ)+\eta(X)g(\phi Y,Z)+\eta(X)g(\phi Y,hZ)]

for any vector fields
X
,
Y
and
Z
on
M
.

Proof.

Differentiating (
3.1
) covariantly along any vector field
Z
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast}(X,Y)=-k[\nabla_{Z}g(X,Y)-(\nabla_{Z}\eta(X))\eta(Y)-(\nabla_{Z}\eta(Y))\eta(X)].

(3.2)

Now,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast})(X,Y)=\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast}(X,Y)-S^{\ast}(\nabla_{Z}X,Y)-S^{\ast}(X,\nabla_{Z}Y).

Using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) in the foregoing equation, we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast})(X,Y)=k[((\nabla_{Z}\eta)X)\eta(Y)+((\nabla_{Z}\eta)Y)\eta(X)].

(3.3)

Using (
2.9
) in (
3.3
), we infer that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast})(X,Y)=k[\eta(Y)g(Z+hZ,\phi X)+\eta(X)g(Z+hZ,\phi Y)].

(3.4)

In a similar manner, we get

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}S^{\ast})(Y,Z)=k[\eta(Z)g(X+hX,\phi Y)+\eta(Y)g(X+hX,\phi Z)].

(3.5)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}S^{\ast})(X,Z)=k[\eta(Z)g(Y+hY,\phi X)+\eta(X)g(Y+hY,\phi Z)].

(3.6)

With the help of (
3.4
)-(
3.6
), we complete the proof by using (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
M
be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional

N(k)
-contact metric manifold admitting
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton
(g,V,\lambda)
, then

(1)

The manifold
M
is locally isometric to
E^{n+1}(0)\times S^{n}(4)
for
n>1
and flat for
n=1
.

(2)

The manifold
M
is
\ast
-Ricci flat.

(3)

The vector field V is conformal,

provided
\lambda\neq\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1}
.

Proof.

From (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}g)(X,Y)+2S^{\ast}(X,Y)=[2\lambda-(p+\frac{2}{2n+1})]g(X,Y).

(3.7)

Differentiating the above equation covariantly along any vector field
Z
, we get

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}\pounds_{V}g)(X,Y)=-2(\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast})(X,Y).

(3.8)

It is well known that (see
[
17
]
)

(\pounds_{V}\nabla_{X}g-\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}g-\nabla_{[V,X]}g)(Y,Z)=-g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)-g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Z),Y).

Since
\nabla g=0
, then the above relation becomes

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}g)(Y,Z)=g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)+g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Z),Y).

(3.9)

Since
\pounds_{V}\nabla
is symmetric, then it follows from (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}g)(Y,Z)+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}g)(X,Z)

(3.10)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{Z}\pounds_{V}g)(X,Y).

Using (
3.8
) in (
3.10
) we have

g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)=(\nabla_{Z}S^{\ast})(X,Y)-(\nabla_{X}S^{\ast})(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}S^{\ast})(X,Z).

Now, using Lemma
3.4
in the foregoing equation yields

\displaystyle g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k[\eta(Y)g(\phi X,Z)+\eta(Y)g(\phi X,hZ)

\displaystyle+\eta(X)g(\phi Y,Z)+\eta(X)g(\phi Y,hZ)],

which implies

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y)=2k[\eta(Y)\phi X+\eta(Y)h\phi X+\eta(X)\phi Y+\eta(X)h\phi Y].

(3.11)

Substituting
Y=\xi
in (
3.11
), we get

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=2k[\phi X+h\phi X].

(3.12)

Differentiating (
3.12
) along any vector field
Y
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=2k[\nabla_{Y}\phi X+\nabla_{Y}h\phi X].

(3.13)

Now,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=\nabla_{Y}(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)-(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(\nabla_{Y}X,\xi)-(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\nabla_{Y}\xi).

Using (
2.1
)-(
2.5
) and (
3.11
)-(
3.13
) in the foregoing equation, we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k[(\nabla_{Y}\phi)X+(\nabla_{Y}h\phi)X+(k-2)\eta(X)\xi

(3.14)

\displaystyle-(k-2)\eta(X)\eta(Y)\xi-2\eta(X)hY].

Now, using (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) in (
3.14
), we get

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k[kg(X,Y)\xi+(k-2)\eta(Y)X

(3.15)

\displaystyle+(k-2)\eta(X)Y-2\eta(X)hY

\displaystyle-2\eta(Y)hX-(3k-4)\eta(X)\eta(Y)\xi].

Due to Yano
[
17
]
, it is known that

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}R)(X,Y)Z=(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Z),

Using the equation (
3.15
) in the above formula, we obtain

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}R)(X,\xi)\xi=(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(\xi,\xi)-(\nabla_{\xi}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=0.

(3.16)

Now, substituting
Y=\xi
in (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}g)(X,\xi)=[2\lambda-(p+\frac{2}{2n+1})]\eta(X),

(3.17)

which implies

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\eta)X-g(X,\pounds_{V}\xi)=[2\lambda-(p+\frac{2}{2n+1})]\eta(X).

(3.18)

From (
3.18
), after putting
X=\xi
, we can easily obtain that

\displaystyle\eta(\pounds_{V}\xi)=-[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})].

(3.19)

Now, from (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle R(X,\xi)\xi=k(X-\eta(X)\xi).

(3.20)

With the help of (
3.18
)-(
3.20
) and (
2.7
)-(
2.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}R)(X,\xi)\xi=k[2\lambda-(p+\frac{2}{2n+1})](X-\eta(X)\xi).

(3.21)

Equating (
3.16
) and (
3.21
) and then taking inner product with
Y
yields

\displaystyle k[2\lambda-(p+\frac{2}{2n+1})](g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y))=0,

which implies
k=0
, since by hypothesis,
\lambda\neq\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1}
. Therefore, from (
3.1
), we have
S^{\ast}=0
, i.e., the manifold is
\ast
-Ricci flat. Again from (
2.7
), we have
R(X,Y)\xi=0
and hence, from lemma
3.1
, it follows that the manifold
M
is locally isometric to
E^{n+1}(0)\times S^{n}(4)
for
n>1
and flat for
n=1
.

Now, we know that a vector field
X
on a Riemannian manifold
M
is said to be conformal if there is a smooth function
\sigma
on
M
such that
\pounds_{X}g=2\sigma g
. Using
S^{\ast}=0
in (
3.7
), we get
\pounds_{V}g=2[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})]g
and hence
V
is a conformal vector field.
∎

Note 3.6
.

If
\lambda=(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})
, then from (
1.1
), we can say that the
\ast
-conformal Ricci soliton reduces to a steady
\ast
-Ricci soliton. We need the following well known definition to discuss about this further.

Definition 3.7
.

On an almost contact metric manifold
M
, a vector field
V
is said to be Killing if
\pounds_{V}g=0
and an infinitesimal contact transformation if
\pounds_{V}\eta=f\eta
for some smooth function
f
on
M
. In particular, if
f=0
, then
V
is said to be strict infinitesimal contact transformation.

Remark 3.8
.

If
k\neq 0
and
\lambda=(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})
, then from (
3.18
), we have
(\pounds_{V}\eta)X=g(X,\pounds_{V}\xi)
. Thus
V
will be an infinitesimal contact transformation if
\pounds_{V}\xi=f\xi
for some smooth function
f
on
M
. But in view of (
3.19
), we have
\eta(\pounds_{V}\xi)=0
, which implies
\pounds_{V}\xi\bot\xi
. Hence
\pounds_{V}\xi\not=f\xi
for any smooth function
f
on
M
, unless
f=0
identically. Hence,
V
cannot be an infinitesimal contact transformation on
M
but it can be a strict infinitesimal contact transformation if
\pounds_{V}\xi=0
.

Remark 3.9
.

If
k=0
and
\lambda=(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})
, then from (
3.7
), we have
\pounds_{V}g=0
. Hence
V
is a Killing vector field.

To prove our next theorem regarding
\ast
-conformal gradient Ricci soliton, we first state and prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M
be
(2n+1)
-dimensional
N(k)
-contact metric manifold admitting
\ast
-conformal gradient Ricci soliton
(g,V,\lambda)
. Then the curvature tensor

R(X,Y)Df=k[2g(\phi X,Y)\xi-\eta(X)(\phi Y+\phi hY)+\eta(Y)(\phi X+\phi hX)]

for any vector fields
X
and
Y

, where

.

Proof.

Equation (
1.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}Df=[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})]X-Q^{\ast}X.

(3.23)

Differentiating (
3.23
) along any vector field
Y
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Df=[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})]\nabla_{Y}X-\nabla_{Y}Q^{\ast}X.

(3.24)

Interchanging
X
and
Y
in the above equation, we get

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Df=[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})]\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{X}Q^{\ast}Y.

(3.25)

Again from (

), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{[X,Y]}Df=[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})](\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X)-Q^{\ast}(\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X).

(3.26)

It is well known that

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Df=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Df-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Df-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Df.

Substituting (
3.24
)-(
3.26
) in the foregoing equation, we obtain

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Df=(\nabla_{Y}Q^{\ast})X-(\nabla_{X}Q^{\ast})Y.

(3.27)

Now, from (

) and (

), we can write

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}Q^{\ast})Y=k[g(X+hX,\phi Y)\xi-\eta(Y)(\phi X+\phi hX)].

(3.28)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}Q^{\ast})X=k[g(Y+hY,\phi X)\xi-\eta(X)(\phi Y+\phi hY)].

(3.29)

We now complete the proof by substituting (
3.28
) and (
3.29
) in (
3.27
).
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
M
be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
N(k)
-contact metric manifold admitting
\ast
-conformal gradient Ricci soliton
(g,V,\lambda)
, where
V=Df
for some smooth function
f
on
M
, then

(1)

The Manifold
M
is locally isometric to
E^{n+1}(0)\times S^{n}(4)
for
n>1
and flat for
n=1
.

(2)

The manifold
M
is
\ast
-Ricci flat.

(3)

The potential function
f
is either harmonic or satisfy a physical Poisson equation.

Proof.

Putting
X=\xi
in (
3.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle R(\xi,Y)Df=-k[\phi Y+\phi hY].

Taking inner product of the foregoing equation yields

\displaystyle g(R(\xi,Y)Df,X)=-k[g(\phi X,Y)+g(\phi hX,Y)].

(3.30)

Since
g(R(\xi,Y)Df,X)=-g(R(\xi,Y)X,Df)
, then using (
2.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle g(R(\xi,Y)Df,X)=-kg(X,Y)(\xi f)+k\eta(X)(Yf).

(3.31)

Equating (
3.30
) and (
3.31
) and then antisymmetrizing yields

\displaystyle k\eta(X)(Yf)-k\eta(Y)(Xf)-2kg(\phi X,Y)=0.

Putting
X=\xi
in the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle k[(Yf)-(\xi f)\eta(Y)]=0,

\displaystyle k[Df-(\xi f)\xi]=0.

Hence, either

or
Df=(\xi f)\xi
.

Case 1: If

, then (
3.1
) implies
S^{\ast}=0
. Also from (
2.7
),

. Using lemma

, we can say that

is locally isometric to

. Again using

) and then tracing yields

Case 2: If
Df=(\xi f)\xi
, then differentiating this along any vector field

, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}Df=(X(\xi f))\xi+(\xi f)(-\phi X-\phi hX).

(3.32)

Equating (

) and (

), we get

\displaystyle Q^{\ast}X=[\lambda-(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2n+1})]X-(X(\xi f))\xi+(\xi f)(\phi X+\phi hX).

(3.33)

Comparing the coefficients of
X

Total Stiefel Whitney classes for real representations of GL n \GL_{n} over 𝔽 q , ℝ \mathbb{F}_{q},\mathbb{R} and ℂ \mathbb{C}
Jyotirmoy Ganguly, Rohit Joshi

Abstract
. We compute the total Stiefel Whitney class for a real representation π \pi of GL n ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) \GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) , where q q is odd in terms of character values of π \pi on order 2 2 diagonal elements. We also compute the total Stiefel Whitney classes of real representations of GL n ⁡ ( ℝ ) \GL_{n}(\mathbb{R}) and GL n ⁡ ( ℂ ) \GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}) .



1.
Introduction

Let
\pi
be a real representation of a finite group
G
. One can define Stiefel Whitney classes (SWC)
w_{i}(\pi)
for
0\leq i\leq\deg\pi
as members of the cohomology groups
H^{i}(G)=H^{i}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. The total Stiefel Whitney class of
\pi
in the cohomology ring
H^{*}(G)
is defined as

w(\pi)=w_{0}(\pi)+w_{1}(\pi)+\cdots+w_{d}(\pi),

where
d=\deg\pi
. (see
[
Ben91
, Section 2.6, page no. 50]
for details).

In this article, we compute the total SWC for a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q
is odd, in terms of the character values on diagonal elements of order
2
. The computations for the particular case of
n=2
can be found in
[
GJ22
]
.
We also compute the total SWC of a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. The computation of second SWC for representations of
S_{n}
and related groups can be found in
[
GS20
]
. Explicit expressions for the first and second SWC of the representations of
\mathrm{O}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
were found in
[
GJ21
]
in terms of highest weights.

We will now describe our results in some more detail.
Let
\mathfrak{X}
denote the set of binary strings of length
n
. For
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathfrak{X}
, define
\nu(a)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Put
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=\{a\in\mathfrak{X}\mid\nu(a)=i\}
.
Let
C_{m}
denote the additive cyclic group of order
m
. We fix a generator
x
for
C_{m}
.

Consider the linear character
\chi^{j}
of
C_{m}
given by
\chi^{j}(x)=\zeta_{m}^{j}
, where
\zeta_{m}
is the primitive
m
th root of unity. For
m
even, write ‘
\sgn
’ for
\chi^{m/2}
.
For a binary string
a\in\mathfrak{X}
and
m
even, consider the representation
\sgn_{a}
of
C_{m}^{n}
, defined as
\sgn_{a}=\boxtimes_{j=1}^{n}(\sgn)^{a_{j}}
(external tensor product), where

(\sgn)^{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{1},\quad\text{if $\epsilon=0$},\\
\sgn,\quad\text{if $\epsilon=1$}.\end{cases}

Here we use some terminologies from supercharacter theory. For more details we refer the reader to
[
DI08
]
. Consider the action of
S_{n}
on
C_{2}^{n}
by permuting the copies of
C_{2}
. If we identify
C_{2}^{n}
with set of binary strings
\mathfrak{X}
, then the superclasses are
\mathfrak{X}_{i}
which are orbits of
S_{n}
action.

We will identify
C_{2}^{n}
with the subgroup

\text{diag}(\pm 1,\ldots,\pm 1)\subset\GL_{n}(F),

where
F=\mathbb{F}_{q},\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
depending upon the context. We will write

h_{i}=\diag(\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{i\,\text{times}},1,\ldots,1)\in\GL_{n}(F).

Then
h_{i}
represents the superclass
\mathfrak{X}_{i}
.
Consider the representations

\sigma_{i}=\bigoplus\limits_{l\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}}\sgn_{l}

(1)

of
C_{m}^{n}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. The supercharacters are the direct sum of the representations in a single orbit for the action of
S_{n}
on characters of
C_{2}^{m}
. Then clearly the supercharacters are
\sigma_{i}
.

For
l\in\mathfrak{X}
, consider the linear character
\chi(l)
of
C_{m}^{n}
given by
\chi(l)=\bigotimes\limits_{k=1}^{n}\chi^{l_{k}}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, take
e_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}
such that
e_{ij}=1
if
i=j
and
e_{ij}=0
otherwise. If
4\mid m
then

H^{*}(C_{m}^{n})=\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}{(s_{1}^{2},\ldots,s_{n}^{2})},

where
s_{i}=w_{1}(\sgn_{e_{i}})
and
t_{i}=w_{2}(\chi(e_{i})_{\mathbb{R}})
. On the other hand

H^{*}(C_{2}^{n})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}],

where
v_{i}=w_{1}(\sgn_{e_{i}})
.
Let
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
and
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
. For
l\in\mathfrak{X}
we write
l\cdot v
(resp.
l\cdot t
) for
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}v_{i}
(resp.
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}t_{i}
).

For a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{n}(F)
where
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
or
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, consider the term

c_{k}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(h_{k})\chi_{\pi}(h_{i}),

where
M=(\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(h_{k}))
is the matrix which is exactly the supercharacter table. Thus we explicitly compute the table by proving that
\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(h_{k})
is the coefficient of
y^{i}
in the expression
(1-y)^{k}(1+y)^{n-k}
(see Proposition
2
).

For a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we define

\delta=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $\det\pi=1$},\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For a subgroup
H
of
G
we denote by
w^{H}(\pi)
(resp.
w_{i}^{H}(\pi)
) the total Stiefel Whitney class
w(\pi\mid_{H})=\res^{G}_{H}(w(\pi))
(resp.
w_{i}(\pi\mid_{H})
).
Let
D
denote the diagonal torus of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
We have
\mathrm{res}:H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\hookrightarrow H^{*}(D)
for
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
\mathrm{res}:H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\hookrightarrow H^{*}(C_{2}^{n})
for
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
are injections (see
[
AM13
, Theorem
4.4
, page
227
]
). Our main theorem, proved in Section
4
, says that

Theorem 1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
where
q
is odd.

(1)

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
then

w^{D}(\pi)=\left(1+\delta b_{1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{p\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}}(1+p\cdot t)\right)^{c_{i}/2},

where
p\cdot t=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}t_{i}
and
b_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}
.

(2)

If
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
then

w^{C_{2}^{n}}(\pi)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{l\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}}(1+l\cdot v)\right)^{c_{i}},

where
l\cdot v=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}v_{i}
.

We use the same approach to compute the total Stiefel Whitney class of real representations of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
We have
\res:H^{*}(\GL_{n}(k))\hookrightarrow H^{*}(C_{2}^{n})
is injective when
k=\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
or
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the total Stiefel Whitney class of
\pi
is given by

w^{C_{2}^{n}}(\pi)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{l\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}}(1+l\cdot v)\right)^{c_{i}}.

(2)

In
[
Nak60
, Corollary
6.7
]
Nakaoka proved that the cohomology of the symmetric group stabilizes. In particular, the result says that if
i<n/2
, then
the map
\res:H^{i}(S_{n})\to H^{i}(S_{2i})
is an isomorphism.
We prove a similar result for
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Theorem 3
.

The map
\res:H^{i}(\GL_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to H^{i}(\GL_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
, where
m\geq n
and
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, is injective for
i<2n-1
.

We have a similar result for the case when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
(see Theorem
8
). We also provide criteria for spinoriality of real representations of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
where
q
is odd (see Theorem
14
). This recovers the main result in
[
JS21
, Theorem
1
]

Theorem
3
allows further simplification to calculate the second and fourth Stiefel Whitney classes. Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q
is odd. Then we have

\begin{split}w_{2}^{D}(\pi)&=\dfrac{m_{\pi}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\right),\quad\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
\\
w_{2}^{C_{2}^{n}}(\pi)&=\dbinom{m_{\pi}}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\right),\quad\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 3\pmod{4},\end{split}

where
m_{\pi}=\frac{\dim\pi-\chi_{\pi}(h_{1})}{2}
. Moreover, if
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then

w_{4}^{D}(\pi)=\binom{m_{\pi}/2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{2}+\frac{\dim\pi-\chi_{\pi}(h_{2})}{8}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}t_{i}t_{j}.

As an application of the result we obtain the following property for the real principal series representations (see Section
9.1
).

Theorem 4
.

Let
\pi
be a real principal series representation for
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
where
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then
w_{4}(\pi)=0
for
n\geq 5
.

We also compute the second SWC for
q
odd and the fourth SWC for
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

A complex representation
(\pi,V)
of a finite group
G
is called orthogonal if it preserves a non degenerate symmetric bilinear form. We call
\pi
real if the representation space
V
is real. For a complex representation
\pi
one can consider its realisation
(\pi_{\mathbb{R}},V_{\mathbb{R}})
by forgetting the complex structure of
V
and regarding it as a real vector space.

2.1.
Irreducible Representations of
C_{m}^{n}

Take the set of ordered pairs

\mathcal{P}=\{\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})\mid\tau_{j}\in C_{m},1\leq j\leq n\}.

Consider the subset
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{P}
given by

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})\mid\tau_{j}\in\{0,m/2\},1\leq j\leq n\}.

Consider the linear character

\chi_{\tau}=(\chi^{\tau_{1}}\otimes\chi^{\tau_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\chi^{\tau_{n}})
of
C_{m}^{n}
(See Section
2
)). We have
\chi_{-\tau}=\chi_{\tau}^{\vee}
.
Note that
\chi_{\tau}
is real if
\tau\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Define an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{P}
as

\tau\sim(-\tau_{1},-\tau_{2},\ldots,-\tau_{n}).

Put
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}=(\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}^{\prime})/\sim
. Therefore a real irreducible representations of
C_{m}^{n}
is either of the form
(\chi_{\tau})_{\mathbb{R}}
for
\tau\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
or of the form
\chi_{\tau}
where
\tau\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.

2.2.
Cohomology of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, for
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}

The cohomology ring of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
is given by

H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[a_{2},a_{4},\ldots,a_{2n}]\otimes E[b_{1},b_{3},\ldots,b_{2n-1}],

(3)

where

a_{2l}=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{l}}t_{i_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{l}},\quad b_{2l-1}=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{l},1\leq j\leq l}t_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{t}_{i_{j}}\cdots t_{i_{l}}s_{i_{j}},\quad 1\leq l\leq n,

and
E[b_{1},\ldots,b_{2n-1}]
denotes the alternating algebra.
For details we refer the reader to
[
FP06
, Theorem
8.1
, page
288
]
.

We know that the symmetric group
S_{n}
is the Weyl group of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with respect to the diagonal torus
D
. It acts on
D
by permuting the diagonal entries.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\pi
be a representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then
\pi\mid_{D}
is
S_{n}
invariant.

Proof.

We leave the proof to the reader.
∎

This enables us to work with
S_{n}
invariant representations of
D
.

Definition 1
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a subgroup of a finite group
G
.
We say that
G^{\prime}

detects
the (mod
2
) cohomology of
G
, provided that the restriction map

\res:H^{*}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to H^{*}(G^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})

(4)

is injective.

The action of
S_{n}
on
D
induces an action of
S_{n}
on
H^{*}(D)
. We write
H^{*}(D)^{S_{n}}
to denote the subalgebra fixed under the action.

Theorem 5

(Corollary of Theorem
3
, page
571
Quillen)
.

The diagonal subgroup
D
detects the cohomology of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In fact, the homomorphism

H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to H^{*}(D)

induced from the restriction of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
to
D
is injective. Moreover, the image lies inside
H^{*}(D)^{S_{n}}
.

3.
SWC of
S_{n}
Invariant Representations of
C_{2}^{n}

An
S_{n}
invariant representation of
C_{2}^{n}
will be of the form

\pi=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\sigma_{i},

(5)

where
\sigma_{i}
is as in (
1
).

Proposition 2
.

The character value
\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(h_{k})
is equal to the coefficient of
y^{i}
in the expression
(1-y)^{k}(1+y)^{n-k}
.

Proof.

Fix two integers
i,k
such that
0\leq i,k\leq n
.
Let
R_{l}
denote the set of elements
a\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}
such that the first
k
coordinates of
a
contains
l
number of
1
’s. Then for
a\in R_{l}
we have
\chi_{\sgn_{a}}(h_{k})=(-1)^{l}.
Put
\tau_{l}=\bigoplus\limits_{a\in R_{l}}\sgn_{a}
. Then

\chi_{\tau_{l}}(h_{k})=\binom{k}{l}\binom{n-k}{i-l}(-1)^{l}.

Observe that
\bigoplus\limits_{l=0}^{k}\tau_{l}=\sigma_{i}
.
Thus

\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(h_{k})=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\cdot\binom{n-k}{i-l}(-1)^{l}.

(6)

Note that this gives the coefficient of
y^{i}
in
(1-y)^{k}(1+y)^{n-k}
.

∎

From (
5
) we have

\chi_{\pi}(h_{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\cdot\chi_{\sigma_{j}}(h_{i})

(7)

Define matrix
M=(m_{ij})
where
0\leq i,j\leq n
such that

m_{ij}=\chi_{\sigma_{j}}(h_{i}).

(8)

Take

a={}^{t}\left(\chi_{\pi}(h_{0}),\ldots,\chi_{\pi}(h_{n})\right)\quad\text{and}\quad m={}^{t}\left(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n}\right),
where
{}^{t}A
denotes the transpose of the matrix
A
. Observe that

a=M\cdot m.

(9)

Theorem 6
.

We have

M^{2}=2^{n}I_{n+1},

where
I_{n+1}
denotes the identity matrix of rank
n+1
.

Proof.

Let

R_{i}=(m_{i0},\ldots,m_{in}),

be the
i
-th row of
M
. Note that from Proposition
2
the element
m_{il}
,
0\leq l\leq n
, is equal to the coefficient of
y^{l}
in
(1-y)^{i}(1+y)^{n-i}
.

We calculate the
x
-th row in the matrix
M^{2}
.

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}(M^{2})_{xl}y^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}m_{xi}m_{il}y^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}m_{xi}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{n}m_{il}y^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(h_{x})(1-y)^{i}(1+y)^{n-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{x}\binom{x}{l}\cdot\binom{n-x}{i-l}(-1)^{l}\right)(1-y)^{i}(1+y)^{n-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{x}\binom{x}{l}(-1)^{l}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n-x}{i-l}(1-y)^{i}(1+y)^{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{x}\binom{x}{l}(-1)^{l}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{n-x}\binom{n-x}{t}(1-y)^{t+l}(1+y)^{n-t-l}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{x}\binom{x}{l}(-1)^{l}(1-y)^{l}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{n-x}\binom{n-x}{t}(1-y)^{t}(1+y)^{n-t-l-x+x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{x}\binom{x}{l}(-1)^{l}(1-y)^{l}(1+y)^{x-l}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{n-x}\binom{n-x}{t}(1-y)^{t}(1+y)^{n-x-t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{x}\binom{x}{l}(y-1)^{l}(y+1)^{x-l}(1-y+1+y)^{n-x}

\displaystyle=(2y)^{x}2^{n-x}

\displaystyle=2^{n}y^{x}.

∎

Since
M
is invertible, we have

m=M^{-1}\cdot a.

(10)

Proposition 3
.

Let
\pi
be the representation of
C_{2}^{n}
as in equation (
5
). Then we have

c_{k}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(h_{k})\chi_{\pi}(h_{i}).

Proof.

The proof follows from Equations (
8
) (
10
) and Theorem
6
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\pi
be an
S_{n}
invariant representation of
C_{2}^{n}
of the form
\pi=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\sigma_{i}
.Then we have

w(\pi)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{l\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}}(1+l\cdot v)\right)^{c_{i}}

(11)

where
c_{i}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(h_{i})\chi_{\pi}(h_{k})
.

Proof.

We have

w(\sigma_{i})=\prod_{l\in\mathfrak{X}_{i}}(1+l\cdot v)

and

Now from Proposition

Hence the result follows.

4.
proof of main theorem

For a subset
\text{I}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{l}\}
of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and for a multiset
\text{J}=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{k}\}
where
j_{i}\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
we define

s^{\text{I}}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}\text{ and }t^{\text{J}}=t_{j_{1}}t_{j_{2}}\cdots t_{j_{k}}.

Observe that
H^{*}(C_{4m}^{n})=\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}{(s_{1}^{2},\ldots,s_{n}^{2})}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
vector space with basis
s^{\text{I}}t^{\text{J}}
, where
\text{I}\,\,(\text{ resp. }\text{J})
varies over all subsets ( resp. multi-sets) over
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. If
\pi
is a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and if
w(\pi)=\sum\limits_{\text{I},\text{J}}a_{\text{I},\text{J}}s^{\text{I}}t^{\text{J}}
, then elements of the form
s^{\text{I}}
have coefficient
0
i.e.
a_{\text{I},\emptyset}=0
if
|\text{I}|\geq 2
.

Proof.

Consider the projection map

\mathrm{pr}:\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}{(s_{1}^{2},\ldots,s_{n}^{2})}\to\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}]}{(s_{1}^{2},\ldots,s_{n}^{2})}

defined by
\mathrm{pr}(s_{i})=s_{i}
and
\mathrm{pr}(t_{i})=0
for all
i
. This gives
\pr(a_{2i})=0
for all
i
and
\pr(b_{2i-1})=0
for
i>1
. Then from Equation (
3
) we have

\mathrm{pr}\left(H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\right)=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[b_{1}].

(12)

Now if
s^{I}
appears as a term in
w(\pi)
, where
|I|\geq 2
, then it survives in
\pr(w(\pi))
. But this contradicts Equation (
12
).

∎

Proof of Main Theorem

1
.

Let
\widehat{P}
be as defined in Section
2
. Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Recall that for a subgroup
H
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we write
w_{i}^{H}(\pi)
and
w^{H}(\pi)
to denote
w_{i}(\pi\mid_{H})
and
w(\pi\mid_{H})
respectively.

Since
\pi\mid_{D}
is an
S_{n}
invariant representation of
D
we write

\pi\mid_{D}=\mathbbm{1}^{\oplus m_{0}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}^{\oplus m_{i}}\bigoplus_{\tau\in\widehat{P}}((\chi_{\tau})_{\mathbb{R}})^{\oplus m_{\tau}}.

(13)

i_{2}^{*}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2})=v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}
.

For a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, Theorem
1
gives

w(\pi)=\begin{cases}(1+\delta s_{1})(1+t_{1})^{c_{1}/2},&\text{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
(1+v_{1})^{c_{1}},&\text{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4},\end{cases}

For a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, Theorem
1
says

w(\pi)=\begin{cases}(1+\delta(s_{1}+s_{2}))((1+t_{1})(1+t_{2}))^{c_{1}^{\prime}/2}(1+t_{1}+t_{2})^{c_{2}^{\prime}/2},&\text{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
(1+v_{1})^{c_{1}^{\prime}}(1+v_{2})^{c_{1}^{\prime}}(1+v_{1}+v_{2})^{c_{2}^{\prime}},&\text{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

For the case of
\GL_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we have

\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}\\
c_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix}=(1/2)\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{\pi}(h_{0})\\
\chi_{\pi}(h_{1})\\
\end{pmatrix},

while for the case of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we have

\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}^{\prime}\\
c_{1}^{\prime}\\
c_{2}^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}=(1/4)\begin{pmatrix}1&2&1\\
1&0&-1\\
1&-2&1\\
\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{\pi}(h_{0})\\
\chi_{\pi}(h_{1})\\
\chi_{\pi}(h_{2})\\
\end{pmatrix}.

For
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
we compute
w_{1}(\pi)
in terms of character values.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. Then we have

w_{1}(\pi)=m_{\pi}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\right).

Proof.

Since
i=1
in Theorem
8
, we have
n=2
. We calculate for
\GL_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. So for the representation
\pi
we have

w_{1}(\pi)=c_{1}v_{1}.

Since
c_{1}=m_{\pi}
, we have the result.

∎

Next we compute the second SWC.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q
is odd. Then we have

w_{2}(\pi)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{m_{\pi}}{2}a_{2},&\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
\\
\dbinom{m_{\pi}}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\right),&\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, we have

w_{2}(\pi)=\frac{c_{1}}{2}t_{1}.

We have
c_{1}=(1/2)(\chi_{\pi}(h_{0})-\chi_{\pi}(h_{1}))=m_{\pi}.

Thus by Theorem
3
we have

w_{2}(\pi)=\frac{m_{\pi}}{2}a_{2}.

For a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
we have

w_{2}(\pi)=\binom{c_{1}^{\prime}+c_{2}^{\prime}}{2}(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2})+c_{1}^{\prime 2}v_{1}v_{2}.

We know from
[
GJ22
, Lemma
4.4
]
) that
c_{1}^{\prime}
is even.
Moreover we have

c_{1}^{\prime}+c_{2}^{\prime}=(1/2)(\chi_{\pi}(h_{0})-\chi_{\pi}(h_{1}))=m_{\pi}
.
Hence the Theorem follows from Theorem
8
. ∎

Using the detection result we compute the fourth SWC in terms of character values when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

Theorem 13
.

Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then

w_{4}(\pi)=\binom{m_{\pi}/2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{2}+\frac{\dim\pi-\chi_{\pi}(h_{2})}{8}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}t_{i}t_{j}.

Proof.

From Theorem
3

we know that the map
\res:H^{4}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to H^{4}(\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
is injective. We have

w^{\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\pi)=(1+\delta(s_{1}+s_{2}))\left((1+t_{1})(1+t_{2})\right)^{c_{1}^{\prime}/2}(1+t_{1}+t_{2})^{c_{2}^{\prime}/2}.

Then we calculate

\displaystyle\res(w_{4}(\pi))

\displaystyle=\left(\binom{c_{1}^{\prime}/2}{2}+\binom{c_{2}^{\prime}/2}{2}+\frac{c_{1}^{\prime}c_{2}^{\prime}}{4}\right)(t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2})+\frac{c_{1}^{\prime}}{2}t_{1}t_{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{\frac{c_{1}^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{c_{2}^{\prime}}{2}}{2}(t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2})+\frac{c_{1}^{\prime}}{2}t_{1}t_{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{m_{\pi}/2}{2}(t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2})+\frac{\dim\pi-\chi_{\pi}(h_{2})}{8}t_{1}t_{2}.

So we have

w_{4}(\pi)=\binom{m_{\pi}/2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{2}+\frac{\dim\pi-\chi_{\pi}(h_{2})}{8}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}t_{i}t_{j}.

∎

7.1.
Lifting Criteria for Real Representations of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

A real representation
\pi
of a finite group
G
is called spinorial provided it lifts to the
\mathrm{Pin}
group, which is a double cover of the orthogonal group. One knows that
\pi
is spinorial iff
w_{2}(\pi)+w_{1}^{2}(\pi)=0
(see
[
GKT89
, page
328
]
).

The main result in
[
JS21
, Theorem
1
]
provides a criterion for spinoriality of a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in terms of character values except for some particular pairs
(n,q)
. We recover the result here for
q
odd.

Theorem 14
.

Let
\pi
be a real representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q
is odd. Then

(1)

for
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, we have
\pi
is spinorial iff
m_{\pi}\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,

(2)

for
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, we have
\pi
is spinorial iff
m_{\pi}\equiv 0
or
3\pmod{4}
.

Proof.

We know that
\pi
is spinorial iff
w_{2}(\pi)+w_{1}^{2}(\pi)=0
. From Theorems
3
and
8
we deduce that
\pi
is spinorial iff
res(w_{2}(\pi)+w_{1}^{2}(\pi))=0
, where
\res:H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to H^{*}(\GL_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
denotes the restriction map, where
k=1
for
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
k=2
for
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.

From Theorems
11
and
12
, it follows that for a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we have

\res(w_{2}(\pi)+w_{1}^{2}(\pi))=\begin{cases}\dfrac{m_{\pi}}{2}t_{1},&\text{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
\dfrac{m_{\pi}(m_{\pi}+1)}{2}(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}),&\text{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

∎

7.2.
Non-Detection by An-isotropic Torus

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
and
G=\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
q
is odd. We fix a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the map
x\to a\cdot x
gives a linear map
M_{a}\in G
. Then
M=\{M_{a},a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}\}
is a cyclic group of order
q^{n}-1
and is called an anisotropic torus.

Theorem 15
.

The anisotropic torus
M
does not detect the mod-2 cohomology of
G
.

Proof.

Consider the representation
\pi=(\chi)_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\chi=\chi^{j}\circ\det
and
j
is odd.
One has

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(h_{1})

\displaystyle=2\chi_{j}(\det(h_{1}))

\displaystyle=2\chi_{j}(-1)

\displaystyle=2(-1)^{j}.

Since
j
is odd we have
\chi_{\pi}(h_{1})=-2
. This gives

m_{\pi}=\frac{1}{2}(2+2)=2.

Then from Theorem
12
one obtains

w_{2}(\pi)\neq 0.

Let
\hat{g}
be a generator of
M
. Now we have

w_{2}(\pi\mid_{M})=\kappa(c_{1}(\chi^{j}\circ\det\mid_{M})).

We have

\chi(\hat{g})=\chi^{j}\circ\det(\hat{g})=\zeta_{q-1}^{j}=\zeta_{q^{2}-1}^{(q+1)j}.

Take
u=c_{1}(\dot{\chi})\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
where
\dot{\chi}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
such that
\dot{\chi}(\hat{g})=\zeta_{q^{2}-1}
. As a result we get

\displaystyle\kappa(c_{1}(\chi^{j}\circ\det\mid_{M}))

\displaystyle=\kappa((q+1)ju)

\displaystyle=(q+1)j\kappa(u)

\displaystyle=0.

This shows that
w_{2}(\pi\mid_{M})=\res^{G}_{M}(w_{2}(\pi))=0
whereas
w_{2}(\pi)\neq 0
. ∎

8.
The Case of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})

For a Lie group
G
we write
H^{i}(G)
to denote the (singular) cohomology
H^{i}(BG)
of a classifying space
BG
of
G
. Consider the subgroup

\Gamma=\diag(\pm 1,\pm 1,\ldots,\pm 1)\cong C_{2}^{n}

of
\GL_{n}(k)
, where
k=\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{C}
.
Take
T=(S^{1})^{n}
to be the diagonal torus of the unitary group
U_{n}
. Now we establish some useful detection results.

Proposition 7
.

The mod
2
cohomology of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is detected by the group
\Gamma
.

Proof.

From
[
T+87
, Theorem
2.1
]
we obtain that the maps
i_{1}^{*}:H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\to H^{*}(U_{n})
and
i_{2}^{*}:H^{*}(U_{n})\to H^{*}(T)
are injective. As a result we conclude that the mod
2
cohomology of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is detected by the diagonal torus
T
.
First we prove that the mod
2
cohomology of
S^{1}
is detected by
C_{2}
. Note that the circle group
S^{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. We know that
H^{*}(\mathrm{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[w_{2}]
, where
w_{2}
denotes the second Stiefel Whitney class of standard representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The standard representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be thought of as the representation
\pi=\rho\oplus\rho^{\vee}
of
S^{1}
where
\rho(z)=z
for
z\in S^{1}
. So
w_{2}=w_{2}(\pi)
. One can consider
C_{2}=\{\pm 1\}
as a subgroup of
S^{1}
to obtain a map
\res:H^{*}(\mathrm{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\to H^{*}(C_{2},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. We have
H^{*}(C_{2},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[v]
, where
\deg v=1
. Note that
\pi\mid_{C_{2}}=\sgn\oplus\sgn
. Thus we have

\res w_{2}(\pi)=w_{2}(\sgn\oplus\sgn)=w_{1}^{2}(\sgn)\neq 0.

So
\res(w_{2})=v^{2}
. This implies that the map ‘
\res
’ is injective. Now the result follows from Kunneth formula.

∎

Proposition 8
.

The mod
2
cohomology of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is detected by
\Gamma
.

Proof.

From
[
Mit01
, Theorem
7.7
]
we know that
B\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is homotopy equivalent to
BO_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus the restriction map
\res_{O}:H^{*}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R}))\to H^{*}(O_{n}(\mathbb{R}))
is an isomorphism. From
[
T+87
, Theorem 2.2]
we obtain that
\res_{\Gamma}:H^{*}(O_{n}(\mathbb{R}))\to H^{*}(\Gamma)
is an injection. Thus the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
cohomology of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is detected by the subgroup
\Gamma\cong C_{2}^{n}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Given a real representation
\pi
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})(\text{ or }\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
, we calculate the total Stiefel Whitney class of
\psi=\pi\mid_{\Gamma}
. Moreover
\psi
is a
S_{n}
invariant representation as it is a restriction of representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})(\text{ or }\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
. Now the Theorem
2
follows from Theorem
7
.
∎

Remark 3
.

Let

9.1.
Principal Series Representation

Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\ldots,\chi_{n}
are linear characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and let
B
be the Borel subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Put

\pi=\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}(\chi_{1}\boxtimes\chi_{2}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\chi_{n}).

Such representations are called principal series representations of
G
. Such a representation
\pi
is orthogonal iff

\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n}\}=\{\chi_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\chi_{n}^{-1}\},

as multisets. We introduce the notation

\displaystyle[n]_{q}!

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{q^{i}-1}{q-1}

\displaystyle=(q+1)(q^{2}+q+1)\cdots(q^{n-1}+q^{n-2}+\cdots+q+1).

From
[
Car85
, Proposition
7.5.3
, page no.
233
]
we obtain the following result.

\chi_{\pi}(h_{k})=[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\chi_{i_{1}}(-1)\chi_{i_{2}}(-1)\cdots\chi_{i_{k}}(-1).

(20)

Put

T_{i}=(1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{i}).

So we calculate

\displaystyle m_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\dim\pi-\chi_{\pi}(h_{1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left([n]_{q}!-[n-1]_{q}!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(-1)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[n-1]_{q}!\left(T_{n-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(-1)\right).

Similarly one has

\displaystyle n_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\dim\pi-\chi_{\pi}(h_{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[n-2]_{q}!\left(T_{n-2}T_{n-1}-(1+q)\sum_{i<j}\chi_{i}(-1)\chi_{j}(-1)\right).

Theorem 16
.

Let
\pi
be a real principal series representation for
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
where

w_{2}(\pi)=0
for

.

Proof.

From
[

5
]
we know that if
n\geq 3
, then all the real principal series representations of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are spinorial. Now
\pi
is spinorial iff
w_{2}(\pi)=w_{1}^{2}(\pi)
(see
[

, Proposition
6.1
]
). If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,then
w_{1}^{2}(\pi)=0
. Therefore
w_{2}(\pi)=0
.

Now consider the case for
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.
We have

m_{\pi}=\frac{1}{2}[n-1]_{q}!\left(T_{n-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(-1)\right).

Also

T_{n-1}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(-1)\equiv n\pmod{2}.

This gives
T_{n-1}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(-1)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.
We have
[m]_{q}!\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for
m\geq 2
. For
n\geq 3
, we have
\upsilon_{2}(m_{\pi})\geq 2
. Therefore

\dbinom{m_{\pi}}{2}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.
Therefore

for

.

∎

9.3.1.
Computation of
w_{2}(\pi)

We have
\chi_{\pi}(h_{1})=p^{k}
where
p^{k}
is the highest power of
p
dividing
|Z_{G}(h_{1})|
(see
[
Car85
, Theorem
6.4.7
, page
195
]
). We have
Z_{G}(h_{1})\cong\GL_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\GL_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Thus
\chi_{\pi}(h_{1})=q^{(1/2)(n-1)(n-2)}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle m_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{q^{(1/2)(n(n-1))}-q^{(1/2)(n-1)(n-2)}}{2}

\displaystyle=(1/2)\cdot q^{(1/2)(n-1)(n-2)}(q^{n-1}-1)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{q-1}{2}\cdot q^{(1/2)(n-1)(n-2)}\cdot(1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-2}).

For
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
we have

w_{2}(\pi)=\begin{cases}a_{2},&\text{ if }q\equiv 5\mod 8\text{ and for }n\text{ even },\\
0,&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

For
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
we have

w_{2}(\pi)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2},&\text{ if }q\equiv 3\mod 8\text{ and for }n\equiv 0\pmod{4},\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2},&\text{ if }q\equiv 7\mod 8\text{ and for }n\equiv 2\pmod{4},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

Existence and Stability of Strong Solutions to the Abels-Garcke-Grün model in Three Dimensions
Andrea Giorgini

Abstract
. This work is devoted to the analysis of strong solutions to the Abels-Garcke-Grün (AGG) model in three dimensions. First, we prove the existence of local-in-time strong solutions originating from an initial datum ( u 0 , ϕ 0 ) ∈ 𝐇 σ 1 × H 2 ​ ( Ω ) (\textbf{{u}}_{0},\phi_{0})\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\sigma}\times H^{2}(\Omega) such that μ 0 ∈ H 1 ​ ( Ω ) \mu_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega) and | ϕ 0 ¯ | ≤ 1 |\overline{\phi_{0}}|\leq 1 . For the subclass of initial data that are strictly separated from the pure phases, the corresponding strong solutions are locally unique. Finally, we show a stability estimate between the solutions to the AGG model and the model H. These results extend the analysis achieved by the author in Calc. Var. (2021) 60:100 to three dimensional bounded domains.

Introduction

Given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we study the Abels-Garcke-Grün (AGG) model in
\Omega\times(0,T)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}(\rho(\phi)\textbf{{u}})+\mathrm{div}\,\big(\textbf{{u}}\otimes(\rho(\phi)\textbf{{u}}+\widetilde{\mathbf{J}})\big)-\mathrm{div}\,(\nu(\phi){\mathbb{D}}\textbf{{u}})+\nabla P=-\mathrm{div}\,(\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi)\\
\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{u}}=0\\
\partial_{t}\phi+\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla\phi=\Delta\mu\\
\mu=-\Delta\phi+\Psi^{\prime}(\phi),\end{cases}

(1.1)

completed with the following boundary and initial conditions

\begin{cases}\textbf{{u}}=\mathbf{0},\quad\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\phi=\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\mu=0\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\textbf{{u}}(\cdot,0)=\textbf{{u}}_{0},\quad\phi(\cdot,0)=\phi_{0}\quad&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Here,

In the system,

The homogeneous free energy density

(i)

The solution

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\textbf{{u}}\in C([0,T_{0}];\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};\mathbf{H}^{2}_{\sigma})\cap W^{1,2}(0,T_{0};{\mathbf{L}}^{2}_{\sigma}),\quad P\in L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\Omega)),\\
&\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};W^{2,6}(\Omega)),\ \partial_{t}\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\Omega)),\\
&\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T_{0})):|\phi(x,t)|<1\ \text{a.e. in }\ \Omega\times(0,T_{0}),\\
&\mu\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{3}(\Omega)),\quad F^{\prime}(\phi)\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{6}(\Omega)).\end{split}

(1.7)

Before proceeding with our second result, it is worth mentioning that the proof of Theorem
1.1
, although still based on a semi-Galerkin approximation, differs from the one of
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
for several aspects. First, the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
exploited the continuity of the chemical potential and the regularity of its time derivative, which are properties available for the strong solutions of the convective Cahn-Hilliard equation in two dimensions. Since these are still an open question in three dimensions, we overcome this issue by employing an approximation procedure involving the convective viscous Cahn-Hilliard equation (see Appendix
A
), together with an appropriate regularization of the initial datum. Such approximations are crucial to rigorously justify the higher-order Sobolev estimates obtained for the approximate solutions. Secondly, due to the lack of global-in-time separation property in three dimensions, we show local uniqueness of solutions departing from a subclass of initial data such that
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
. For such class of solutions, the separation property holds on a (possible short) time interval by embedding in
Hölder spaces. Notice that the argument proposed in
[

based on estimates in dual spaces cannot be used due to the non-constant density. Moreover, the separation property (or, at least,

[

Exploiting this estimates, we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\phi\right)\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leq\left|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2}+C\left|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right|^{2}\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{2}-\overline{\rho}\right)\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}_{H}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|\leq C\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2}+C\left|\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{2}-\overline{\rho}\right|^{2}\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}_{H}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

By Sobolev embedding, we find

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}\big(\rho(\phi)(\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{{u}}-\overline{\rho}(\textbf{{u}}_{H}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{{u}}_{H}\big)\cdot\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho(\phi)(\textbf{{v}}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{{u}}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho(\phi)(\textbf{{u}}_{H}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{{v}}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\big(\rho(\phi)-\overline{\rho}\big)(\textbf{{u}}_{H}\cdot\nabla)\textbf{{u}}_{H}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|\\

&\leq\frac{\nu_{\ast}}{8}\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(K_{0})\left(1+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}_{H}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2}+C(K_{0})\left(\left|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{2}-\overline{\rho}\right|^{2}\right),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right)\big((\nabla\mu\cdot\nabla)\textbf{{u}}\big)\cdot\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leq\left|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right|\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{3}}\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\leq C\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2}+C(K_{0})\left|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right|^{2}\|\nabla\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}.\end{split}

In a similar way as in

, we obtain

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nu(\phi)-\nu(\phi_{H})){\mathbb{D}}\textbf{{u}}_{H}:\nabla\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{6}}\|{\mathbb{D}}\textbf{{u}}_{H}\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\textbf{{u}}_{H}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi-\nabla\phi_{H}\otimes\nabla\phi_{H}\right):\nabla\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leq\left(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\phi_{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(K_{0})\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\nu^{\prime}(\phi){\mathbb{D}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\nabla\phi\cdot\textbf{{v}}\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leq C\|{\mathbb{D}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\nu_{\ast}}{8}\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(K_{0})\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nu^{\prime}(\phi)\left(\nabla\phi\cdot\textbf{{v}}\right)\Pi\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leq C\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Pi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\nu_{\ast}}{8}\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(K_{0})\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla A^{-1}\varphi\,{\rm d}x\right|\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\varphi\|_{\ast}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\phi_{H}\,\textbf{{v}}\cdot\nabla A^{-1}\varphi\,{\rm d}x\right|\leq\frac{\nu_{\ast}}{8}\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\varphi\|_{\ast}^{2}.\end{split}

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg(\left(\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{4}\right)\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\varphi\|_{\ast}^{2}\bigg)\leq f_{1}(t)\big(\|\textbf{{v}}\|_{\sharp}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{\ast}^{2}\big)+f_{2}(t)\Big(\Big|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{2}-\overline{\rho}\Big|^{2}\Big),

where

\displaystyle f_{1}(t)=C(K_{0})\left(1+\|\textbf{{u}}_{H}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle f_{2}(t)=C(K_{0})\left(1+\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}_{H}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}_{H}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right).

\|\textbf{{v}}(t)\|_{\sharp}^{2}+\|\varphi(t)\|_{\ast}^{2}\leq\frac{\left(\left|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{2}-\overline{\rho}\right|^{2}\right)}{\min\{\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{4},\frac{1}{2}\}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\int_{s}^{t}f_{1}(r)\,{\rm d}r}f_{2}(s)\,{\rm d}s,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T_{0}].

\|\textbf{{u}}(t)-\textbf{{u}}_{H}(t)\|_{(\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\sigma})^{\prime}}+\|\phi(t)-\phi_{H}(t)\|_{(H^{1})^{\prime}}\leq\frac{C(K_{0})}{\min\{\sqrt{\rho_{\ast}},1\}}\left(\left|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{2}\right|+\left|\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}{2}-\overline{\rho}\right|\right),\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T_{0}],

where the positive constant

Appendix A
On the convective Viscous Cahn-Hilliard system

Given
\alpha>0
and an incompressible velocity field

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(\phi\,\textbf{{u}},\nabla A^{-1}(\phi-\phi_{0})).

Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg(\|\nabla A^{-1}(\phi-\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\phi-\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg)\leq C\big(1+\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|\phi-\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Thanks to
[
30
, Lemma 4.1, Chap. IV]
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\nabla A^{-1}(\phi(t)-\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\phi(t)-\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\bigg(C_{\alpha}\big(1+\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\big)t+C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\textbf{{u}}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\bigg)^{2}

for all
t\in[0,T]
. By choosing
t=h
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\|\nabla A^{-1}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&+\alpha\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}\left(1+\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(A.15)

Since
\|\partial_{t}^{h}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}\leq\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}
, by combining (
A.14
) and (
A.15
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\alpha\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\\
&\leq C_{\alpha}\left(1+\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2})+\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{G(T)},\end{split}

(A.16)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
G(T)=\int_{0}^{T}C_{\alpha}\left(1+\|\textbf{{u}}(\tau)\|_{L^{3}}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau+C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\,{\rm d}\tau
.
In light of the convergence
\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\rightarrow\partial_{t}\phi
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
as
h\rightarrow 0
, we infer that

\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C(\alpha,T,\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{X_{T}}),

(A.17)

where
X_{T}=L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
. Next, we derive further regularity properties on
\phi
and
\mu
. By the incompressibility constraint, we recall that
\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}}\right)
. Then, thanks to (
A.10
) and (
A.17
), we easily have

\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\alpha,T,\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{X_{T}}\right).

(A.18)

As a consequence, by
[
22
, Theorem A.1]
we get

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|F^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\alpha,T,\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{X_{T}}\right).

(A.19)

Finally, since
\textbf{{u}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))
and
\nabla\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))
, by comparison in (
A.1
)
1
, we also find

\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\alpha,T,\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{X_{T}}\right).

(A.20)

Regularity 2.

Let us now write (
A.1
)
2
as follows

\alpha\partial_{t}\phi-\Delta\phi+F^{\prime}(\phi)=h\quad\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(A.21)

where
h=\mu+\theta_{0}\phi
. Thanks to (
A.20
),

h\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
. Next, we consider the ODEs problems

\begin{cases}\alpha\partial_{t}U+F^{\prime}(U)=\overline{H},\\
U(0)=1-\delta_{0}\end{cases}\quad\begin{cases}\alpha\partial_{t}V+F^{\prime}(V)=\underline{H},\\
V(0)=-1+\delta_{0}\end{cases}\quad\text{in }(0,T),

(A.22)

where
\overline{H}=\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\underline{H}=-\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
It is not difficult to show that there exist two unique solutions
U,V\in C([0,T])
with
U_{t},V_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
. In particular, since
\lim_{s\rightarrow\pm 1}F^{\prime}(s)=\pm\infty
and
\overline{H},\underline{H}\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, a simple comparison argument entails that there exists
\delta>0
such that

-1+\delta\leq V(t)\leq U(t)\leq 1-\delta,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T].

More precisely, it can be checked that
1-\delta\leq\max\{1-\delta_{0},(F^{\prime})^{-1}(\|\overline{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)})\}
. We are left to show that
V(t)\leq\phi(x,t)\leq U(t)
in
\Omega\times[0,T]
. To this aim, we use the Stampacchia method. We define
w=\phi-U
and we consider the problem

\begin{cases}\alpha\partial_{t}w+\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla\phi-\Delta\phi+F^{\prime}(\phi)-F^{\prime}(U)=h-\overline{H}\quad&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
w(0)=\phi_{0}-1+\delta_{0}\quad&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(A.23)

Multiplying the equation by
w^{+}=\max\{\phi-U,0\}
and integrating over
\Omega
, and using that
\nabla\phi=\nabla w^{+}
on the set
\{x\in\Omega:\phi\leq U\}
, we find

\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|w^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}(\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla w^{+})w^{+}\,{\rm d}x+\|\nabla w^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}(F^{\prime}(\phi)-F^{\prime}(U))w^{+}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}(h-\overline{H})w^{+}\,{\rm d}x.

By the monotonicity of
F^{\prime}
, it follows that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|w^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|w^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|w^{+}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T],

which, in turn, gives the desired result, namely
\phi(x,t)\leq U(t)
in
\Omega\times[0,T]
. A similar argument entails that
V(t)\leq\phi(x,t)
in
\Omega\times[0,T]
.
Therefore, we obtain by continuity the separation property

\max_{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]}|\phi(x,t)|\leq 1-\delta.

(A.24)

As a consequence, it follows from (
A.19
) that
\Psi^{\prime}(\phi)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
. Then, we deduce by comparison in (
A.1
)
2
and by elliptic regularity that

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\alpha,T,\delta,\|\Delta\phi_{0}-F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{X_{T}}\right).

Regularity 3.
Thanks to the above regularity, we rewrite (
A.13
) as follows

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\,v+\alpha\nabla\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\cdot\nabla v\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{h}(\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla\phi)v\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi-\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\Psi^{\prime}(\phi))\cdot\nabla v\,{\rm d}x

(A.25)

for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Taking
v=\partial_{t}^{h}\phi
and exploiting the boundary conditions of
\phi
and
u
, we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg(\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg)+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{h}(\textbf{{u}}\phi)\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{h}F^{\prime}(\phi)\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\,{\rm d}x+\theta_{0}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\partial_{t}^{h}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{6}}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{3}}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\theta_{0}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\partial_{t}^{h}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}+C\left(1+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{3}}\right)\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Here we have used the separation property (
A.24
) and the inequality

\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}
. Then, we infer from the Gronwall lemma that

\begin{split}&\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\\
&\quad\leq\left(\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\textbf{{u}}(\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\right)\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{G}(T)}\end{split}

(A.26)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\widetilde{G}(T)=C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}(1+\|\textbf{{u}}(\tau)\|_{L^{3}})\,{\rm d}\tau
.
Since
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\phi_{0}=0
on
\partial\Omega
by assumption, we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg(\|\phi-\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\Delta\phi-F^{\prime}(\phi)+\theta_{0}\phi)\cdot\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\,{\rm d}x-\|\Delta(\phi-\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Delta\phi_{0}\cdot\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-F^{\prime}(\phi)+\theta_{0}\phi)\cdot\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\,{\rm d}x.

Thus, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg(\|\phi-\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg)\leq C\big(1+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}}\big)\|\nabla(\phi-\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}.

By using
[
30
, Lemma 4.1, Chap. IV]
and taking
t=h
, we arrive at

\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}\left(1+\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\right).

(A.27)

Combining the above inequality with (
A.26
), we are led to

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{\alpha}\left(1+\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T+1;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega))}^{2}\right)\mathrm{e}^{C\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|\textbf{{u}}(\tau)\|_{L^{3}})\,{\rm d}\tau}

for all
t\in[0,T]
, which, in turn, implies

\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\alpha,T,\delta,\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{Y_{T}}),

(A.28)

where
Y_{T}=L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega))
.
As an immediate consequence, in light of (
A.19
), (
A.20
) and (
A.24
), we infer by comparison in (
A.1
)
2
that

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega))}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(\Omega))}\leq C(\alpha,T,\delta,\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{Y_{T}}),

(A.29)

Next, we take
v=A^{-1}\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi
in (
A.25
).
Exploiting (
A.24
) and (
A.28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla A^{-1}\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{h}(\phi\textbf{{u}})\cdot\nabla A^{-1}\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{h}\Psi^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{3}}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla A^{-1}\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{6}}+C\|\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(1+\|\partial_{t}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\right).

By recalling (
A.27
), the Gronwall lemma entails

\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\leq C(\alpha,T,\delta,\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{Y_{T}}),

(A.30)

which, in turn, gives that there exists
\partial_{t}^{2}\phi\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
such that

\|\partial_{t}^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\alpha,T,\delta,\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{Y_{T}}).

Thus, by comparison in (
A.1
), we conclude that there exists
\partial_{t}\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
such that

\|\partial_{t}\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\alpha,T,\delta,\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)},\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{Y_{T}}).

The proof is complete.

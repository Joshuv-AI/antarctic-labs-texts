N N -photon amplitudes in a plane-wave background
James P. Edwards, Christian Schubert

Abstract
We use the worldline formalism to derive master formulas for the one-loop N N - photon amplitudes in a plane-wave background, for both scalar and spinor QED. This generalises previous work by Ilderton and Torgrimsson for the vacuum polarisation case, although with some change in methodology since, instead of evaluating the path integral on the semi-classical trajectory, we use the special kinematics of the plane-wave background to uncover the crypto-gaussian character of this type of worldline path integral.

N
-photon amplitudes in the worldline formalism

The starting point for the calculation of the scalar QED
N
-photon amplitudes in the worldline formalism
is Feynman’s
[
11
]
worldline representation of the
one-loop effective action
\Gamma_{\rm scal}[A]

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}[A]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\ \,{\rm e}^{-m^{2}T}\int_{x(T)=x(0)}Dx\,\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl(\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{4}+ie\,\dot{x}\cdot A(x)\bigr)}

(1)

The path integral runs over all closed trajectories in spacetime obeying the
periodicity condition
x(T)=x(0)
in proper time.
The
N
-photon amplitudes are obtained from this by expanding the “interaction exponential”
and Fourier transformation, which leads to the “vertex operator representation” of the
N
-photon amplitude:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}(k_{1},\varepsilon_{1};\ldots;k_{N},\varepsilon_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-ie)^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\,\,{\rm e}^{-m^{2}T}\int_{x(0)=x(T)}Dx\,\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{4}}

(2)

\displaystyle\times V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{1},\varepsilon_{1}]\cdots V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{N},\varepsilon_{N}]

Here each photon is represented by the following
photon vertex operator
, integrated along the trajectory:

\displaystyle V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k,\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\varepsilon\cdot\dot{x}(\tau)\,{\,{\rm e}}^{ik\cdot x(\tau)}

(3)

After a formal exponentiation

\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\dot{x}_{i}\,\,{\rm e}^{ik_{i}\cdot x_{i}}=\,{\rm e}^{\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\dot{x}_{i}+ik_{i}\cdot x_{i}}\mid_{\varepsilon_{i}}

the path integral can be done by gaussian integration using the basic correlator

\displaystyle\langle x^{\mu}(\tau)x^{\nu}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle=-G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\delta^{\mu\nu},\quad G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\equiv\mid\tau-\tau^{\prime}\mid-\frac{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}}{T}\,.

(4)

This results in the following “Bern-Kosower representation” of the
N
-photon amplitude
[
27
,
13
,
14
]

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}(k_{1},\varepsilon_{1};\ldots;k_{N},\varepsilon_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(-ie)}^{N}{(2\pi)}^{D}\delta(\sum k_{i}){\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}}\frac{dT}{T}{(4\pi T)}^{-\frac{D}{2}}e^{-m^{2}T}

(5)

\displaystyle\hskip-150.0pt\times\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}\exp\biggl\{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}G_{ij}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}-i\dot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{j}\Bigr]\biggr\}\Bigl|_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\ldots\varepsilon_{N}}

Here a ‘dot’ denotes a derivative acting on the first variable,

\displaystyle\dot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sign}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-2\frac{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}{T},\quad\ddot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=2{\delta}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-\frac{2}{T}

(6)

and we abbreviate
G_{ij}\equiv G(\tau_{i},\tau_{j})
etc.
The factor
{(4\pi T)}^{-\frac{D}{2}}

represents the free Gaussian path integral
determinant factor, and the

{(2\pi)}^{D}\delta(\sum k_{i})
factor is produced by the integration over the zero mode
x_{0}^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,x^{\mu}(\tau)

of the path integral.
The exponential must still be expanded and only the terms be retained that contain
each polarisation vector
\varepsilon_{i}
linearly:

\displaystyle\exp\bigl\{\cdot\bigr\}\bigl|_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\ldots\varepsilon_{N}}\quad\equiv{(-i)}^{N}P_{N}(\dot{G}_{ij},\ddot{G}_{ij})\exp\biggl[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}G_{ij}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}\biggr]

(7)

with certain polynomials

is a Lorentz vector whose components are Grassmann functions,

Applying the same procedure as for the scalar case above, one obtains the following generalization

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm spin}(k_{1},\varepsilon_{1};\ldots;k_{N},\varepsilon_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(-ie)^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\,\,{\rm e}^{-m^{2}T}\int_{x(0)=x(T)}Dx\,e^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{NS}^{\{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{S}\}}

N
-photon amplitude in a plane-wave background (scalar QED)

In general, a plane-wave field can be defined by a vector potential
A(x)
of the form

\displaystyle eA_{\mu}(x)=a_{\mu}(n\cdot x)

(20)

where
n^{\mu}
is a null vector,

\displaystyle n^{2}=0

(21)

and, as is usual, we will further impose the
light-front gauge condition

\displaystyle n\cdot a=0

(22)

Note that we absorb the charge
e
in the definition of
a_{\mu}
.
Repeating the procedure of the previous section with the addition of the potential
a_{\mu}
to
the worldline Lagrangian, we straightforwardly get a representation of the
N
-photon amplitude in
the plane-wave background that generalizes the vacuum formula (
2
),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}(\{{k_{i},\varepsilon_{i}\}};a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-ie)^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\,\,{\rm e}^{-m^{2}T}\int Dx\,\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl[\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{4}+i\dot{x}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(n\cdot x)\bigr]}

(23)

\displaystyle\times V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{1},\varepsilon_{1}]\cdots V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{N},\varepsilon_{N}]

Fixing the zero-mode problem as usual by
separating off the average position
x_{0}^{\mu}
of the trajectory,
x^{\mu}(\tau)=x_{0}^{\mu}+q^{\mu}(\tau)
,
we note that, differently from the vacuum case, it now appears not only in the exponents of the vertex operators,
but also in the argument of
a_{\mu}(n\cdot x)
. Thus it will now be convenient to introduce
(euclidean) light-cone coordinates adapted to the null vector
n^{\mu}
.
Thus we set

n^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,0,1,i)
, and define
x^{+}\equiv n\cdot x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x^{3}+ix^{4})
(“light-front time”)
and
x^{-}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-x^{3}+ix^{4})
.
We will further denote
x^{\perp}\equiv(x^{1},x^{2})
.
Defining also
k^{\pm}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\pm k^{3}+ik^{4})
,
and using the decomposition

\displaystyle k\cdot x=-k^{+}x^{-}-k^{-}x^{+}+k^{1}x^{1}+k^{2}x^{2}

(24)

allows us to integrate out
x_{0}^{\mu}
but for its
x_{0}^{+}
component:

\displaystyle\int Dx\,\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl[\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{4}+i\dot{x}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(n\cdot x)\bigr]}V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{1},\varepsilon_{1}]\cdots V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{N},\varepsilon_{N}]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{1}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{2}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{+}\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{0}^{+}\,{\rm e}^{-ix_{0}^{+}\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{-}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}

\displaystyle\times\int Dq\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl[\frac{\dot{q}^{2}}{4}+i\dot{q}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q(\tau))\bigr]}\,\,{\rm e}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(ik_{i}\cdot q_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\dot{q}_{i})}\Big|_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\cdots\varepsilon_{N}}\,.

(25)

The calculation of the functional integral at first sight looks like an intractable problem, since the integration variable
q(\tau)

appears in the argument of the unknown function
a_{\mu}
. In
[
26
]
this problem was solved for the two-point case
using the fact that the plane-wave path integral possesses the gaussian property that its semiclassical approximation
is exact. For the
N

using the relations (
21
) and (
22

\displaystyle\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau i\dot{q}\cdot a(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q(\tau))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-i\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\dot{q}\cdot a(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q(\tau))

\displaystyle\hskip-140.0pt+\frac{(-i)^{2}}{2!}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\dot{q}_{1}\cdot a(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q_{1})\int_{0}^{T}d\tau^{\prime}\,\dot{q}_{2}\cdot a(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q_{2})+\cdots

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q_{m})=a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+})+a^{\prime}_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+})(n\cdot q_{m})+\frac{1}{2!}a^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+})(n\cdot q_{m})^{2}+\cdots

6
Summary and Outlook

We have used the worldline formalism to construct a master formula for the
N
-photon amplitudes in a general
plane-wave background field, for both scalar and spinor QED.
We believe that it compares favourably with other available techniques for calculations in plane-wave backgrounds.
As usual in applications of the worldline formalism to QED, it unifies the scalar and spinor cases in the sense that
any spinor QED calculation yields the corresponding scalar QED quantity as a side result.
In particular, with the formalism developed here a calculation of the photon-photon scattering amplitudes in
a plane-wave background should be quite feasible.
In a more extensive publication, we will give a more detailed derivation, including an alternative approach using the
worldline super formalism for the spinor QED case, and explore the
N=3
and
N=4
cases.
It should also be interesting to generalize the mapping of worldline averages to spacetime averages, introduced in
[
26
]
, to the

N
-point case.
Also under consideration is the extension of the formalism to the open-line case, i.e. the photon-dressed scalar and spinor propagators
in a plane-wave background.

Global W 1 , p W^{1,p} regularity for elliptic problem with measure source and Leray-Hardy potential California State University, Los Angeles, 5151, USA. E-mail: hichem.hajaiej@gmail.com
Huyuan Chen

Abstract
In this paper, we develop the Littman-Stampacchia-Weinberger duality approach to obtain global W 1 , p W^{1,p} estimates for a class of elliptic problems involving Leray-Hardy operators and measure sources in a distributional framework associated to a dual formulation with a specific weight function.



Key Words
: Leray-Hardy potential; Duality approach; Radon measure.

MSC2010
: 35J75, 35B99, 35D99.

Introduction

Our concern in this paper is to derive global
W^{1,p}
estimates for weak solutions of non-homogeneous Hardy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=\nu&{\rm in}\ \ {\Omega},\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{L_{\mu}\,}\displaystyle u=0&{\rm on}\ \,\partial{\Omega},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
N\geq 3
,
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain containing the origin,

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}

is the Leray-Hardy operator defined by
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}=-\Delta+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}
,

\mu\geq\mu_{0}:=-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
and
\nu
is a non-homogeneous source.

Note that the inverse square potential in
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
makes this operator degenerate at the origin.
Due to this fact, challenging problems appear in the related Hardy semilinear elliptic equations.
The numerous underlying problems were a source of motivation for many researchers. In order to solve
(
1.1
) in the standard case,
\nu
is a function, many aspects have been developed : Hardy inequality, see the references
[
5
,
15
,
19
,
21
,
28
,
29
]
, variational methods
[
16
,
20
]
for example, the singularities of semilinear Hardy elliptic equation by
[
7
,
9
,
14
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
26
,
30
]
and the references therein. When
N\geq 3
,
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
, Dupaigne in
[
15
]
(also see
[
4
,
5
]
) studied the weak solutions for the equation
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=u^{p}+tf\ \ {\rm in}\ \,\Omega
,
subject to the zero Dirichlet boundary condition, with
t>0,\,p>1
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, in the distributional sense that
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
u^{p}\in L^{1}(\Omega,\rho dx)
and

\int_{\Omega}u\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\xi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\xi\,dx+t\int_{\Omega}f\xi\,dx,\quad\forall\,\xi\in C^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega),

(1.2)

where
\rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

However, the normal distributional identity (
1.2
) does not seem to extend to address other types of isolated singular solutions, such as the solutions of

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

(1.3)

Indeed, when
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, problem (
1.3
)
has two radially symmetric solutions with the explicit formulas given by

\Phi_{\mu}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|x|^{\tau_{-}(\mu)}&{\rm if}\quad\mu>\mu_{0}\\[2.84526pt]
\phantom{}-|x|^{\tau_{-}(\mu)}\ln|x|&{\rm if}\quad\mu=\mu_{0}\end{array}\right.\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\Gamma_{\mu}(x)=|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)},

(1.4)

where

\tau_{-}(\mu)=-\frac{N-2}{2}-\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\tau_{+}(\mu)=-\frac{N-2}{2}+\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}},

\tau_{-}(\mu)
and
\tau_{+}(\mu)
are the zero points of
\mu-\tau(\tau+N-2)=0
. To simplify the notations, we write
\tau_{+}
and
\tau_{-}
in what follows.
Obviously,
\Phi_{\mu}
has a too strong singularity at the origin to hope that
\Phi_{\mu}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
when
\mu\geq\frac{N^{2}+4}{2}
.
Due to its singularity,
\Phi_{\mu}
can’t be viewed as a fundamental solution expressed by Dirac mass at origin in the normal distributional identity for
\mu\not=0
.

To overcome this difficulty,
[
11
]
introduces a new distributional identity for
\Phi_{\mu}
as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{\mu}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}\xi\,d\gamma_{\mu}=c_{\mu}\xi(0),\quad\forall\,\xi\in C_{c}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.5)

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac mass at the origin, here and in what follows,

d\gamma_{\mu}(x)=\Gamma_{\mu}(x)dx,\quad\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mu}=-\Delta-\frac{2\tau_{+}}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla,\quad c_{\mu}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}2\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}}\,|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|&{\rm if}\quad\mu>\mu_{0},\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|&{\rm if}\quad\mu=\mu_{0}\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

and
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
is the sphere of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
The distributional identity (
1.5
) could be seen as

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\Phi_{\mu}=c_{\mu}\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.7)

From above distributional identity,
\Phi_{\mu}
is a fundamental solution of Hardy operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
.

Inspired by the new dual identity (
1.5
), semilinear Hardy problems involving Radon measure

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u+g(u)=\nu&{\rm in}\ \ {\Omega},\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{L_{\mu}+g(u)\,}\displaystyle u=0&{\rm on}\ \,\partial{\Omega}\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

have been considered in
[
12
]
(also
[
13
]
when the origin is at boundary) in a measure framework for
\nu=\nu\lfloor_{\Omega^{*}}+k\delta_{0}
,
where
\Omega^{*}=\Omega\setminus\{0\}
,
\nu\lfloor_{\Omega^{*}}\in\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})
, here
\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})
is denoted by the set of Radon measures
\nu
in
\Omega^{*}
such that

\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})}={\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{*}}}\Gamma_{\mu}d|\nu|:=\sup\left\{{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{*}}}\zeta d|\nu|\,:\zeta\in C_{c}(\Omega^{*}),\,0\leq\zeta\leq\Gamma_{\mu}\right\}<\infty.

(1.9)

We denote by
\overline{\mathfrak{M}}(\Omega;\Gamma_{\mu})
the set of measures which can be written under the form

\nu=\nu\lfloor_{\Omega^{*}}+k\delta_{0},

(1.10)

where
\nu\lfloor_{\Omega^{*}}\in\mathfrak{M}(\Omega;\Gamma_{\mu})
and
k\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\overline{\Omega}^{*}\!\!:=\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\}
and

\mathbb{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)=\left\{\xi\in C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}^{*}):|x|{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}^{*}\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\right\}.

(1.11)

We note that
C^{1,1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\subset\mathbb{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
.

Definition 1.1

We say that
u
is a weak solution (or a very weak solution) of (
1.8
) with
\nu=\nu\lfloor_{\Omega^{*}}+k\delta_{0}\in\overline{\mathfrak{M}}(\Omega;\Gamma_{\mu})
, if
u\in L^{1}(\Omega,\,|x|^{-1}d{\gamma_{\mu}})
,
g(u)\in L^{1}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})
and

\int_{\Omega}u\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mu}\xi\,d{\gamma_{\mu}}+\int_{\Omega}g(u)\xi\,d\gamma_{\mu}={\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}}\xi d(\Gamma_{\mu}\nu)+c_{\mu}k\xi(0)\quad\text{for all }\;\xi\in\mathbb{X}_{\mu}(\Omega).

(1.12)

Similarly, a function
u
is called a weak solution (or a very weak solution) of (
1.1
) with
\nu=\nu\lfloor_{\Omega^{*}}+k\delta_{0}
,
if
u
is in

L^{1}(\Omega,\,|x|^{-1}d{\gamma_{\mu}})
and satisfies (
1.12
) with
g\equiv 0
.

Away from the origin, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
is uniformly elliptic, therefore local regularity properties for second
elliptic equation could be applied directly locally in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. The main goal of this paper is
to study the global regularity of solutions of (
1.1
) and to apply the global regularity to
semilinear elliptic equation (
1.8
). Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\mu>\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}
,
\nu\in\overline{\mathfrak{M}}(\Omega;\Gamma_{\mu})
with the form
\nu=\nu\lfloor_{\Omega^{*}}+k\delta_{0}

for
k\in\mathbb{R}
, and
u
is a very weak solution of (
1.1
).
Let

p^{*}_{\mu}=\frac{N+\tau_{+}}{N-1+\tau_{+}}.

(1.13)

Then

(i)

u\Gamma_{\mu}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
p\in[1,\frac{N}{N-1})
and there exists
C_{1}>0
depending on
N,\ p,\,\mu
and
\Omega
such that

\|u\Gamma_{\mu}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}(\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})}+k).

(1.14)

(ii)

u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})
with
p\in[1,\min\{p^{*}_{\mu},\frac{N}{N-1}\})
and there exists
C_{2}>0
depending on
N,\ p,\,\mu
and
\Omega
such that

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})}\leq C_{2}(\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})}+k).

(1.15)

Remark 1.1

(i)

p^{*}_{\mu}\leq\frac{N}{N-1}
for
\mu\geq 0
and
p^{*}_{\mu}>\frac{N}{N-1}

for
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
;

(ii)
An equivalent form of
p^{*}_{\mu}
is
p^{*}_{\mu}=1-\frac{2}{\tau_{-}}
by definition of
\tau_{-}
, and

\min\{p^{*}_{\mu},\frac{N}{N-1}\}
is the critical exponent for the existence of (
1.8
)
for
\nu\in\overline{\mathfrak{M}}(\Omega;\Gamma_{\mu})
in
[
12
]
, is the critical exponents for
isolated singular solutions of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}u=u^{p}
in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
,
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
in
[
10
]
.

It is well-known that the classical method for global
W^{1,p}
estimates for second order elliptic equations named as Littman-Stampacchia-Weinberger duality approach ( LSW approach for short) in
[
25
,
27
]
, with the coefficients being in
L^{\infty}
. LSW approach has been used to deal with various elliptic equations, see references
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
22
,
28
]
. Global
W^{1,p}
estimates in Theorem
1.1
are derived by LSW approach. Due to the inverse-square potential, the Hardy operator is degenerated. Our dual operator for
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
is no longer
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
, but is
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\mu}
in our distributional sense, see Definition
1.1
. Observe that the coefficient of dual operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}
in gradient term is singular.
That is the main reason for assumptions that
N\geq 3
and
\mu>\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}
, and under these assumptions,
the solutions for nonhomogeneous Dirichlet problem

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}u=f_{n}+{\rm div}F_{n}\ \ {\rm in}\ \,\Omega,\qquad u=0\ \ {\rm on}\ \,\partial\Omega

(1.16)

keep bounded
in the approximation of
f
and
F
in
L^{r}
nonhomogeneous terms with
r>N
by smooth functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{F_{n}\}_{n}
. These uniform estimates
enable us to make use of the LSW duality approach.

While if
\mu>0
for
N=2
or if
\mu\in[\mu_{0},\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}]
for
N\geq 3
, dual problem (
1.16
) has a unique classical solution by constructing super and sub solutions
when
f
and
F
are regular. However, it is very challenging to obtain weak solutions and
L^{\infty}
estimates in the approximation of
f
and
F
in
L^{2}
spaces.
It seems that one has to come up with a new and self-contained
approach to solve this problem.

For our semilinear elliptic problem (
1.8
), a direct corollary for a global regularity could be obtained as follows.

Corollary 1.1

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\mu>\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}
and
\nu\in\overline{\mathfrak{M}}(\Omega;\Gamma_{\mu})
.
If problem (
1.8
) admits a weak solution
u_{g}
, then
u_{g}\Gamma_{\mu}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for
p\in[1,\frac{N}{N-1})
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})
with
p\in[1,\min\{p^{*}_{\mu},\frac{N}{N-1}\})
.

The global regularity
u_{g}\Gamma_{\mu}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
obtained in Corollary
1.1
indicates that
the zero Dirichlet boundary restriction of (
1.8
) could be understood in the trace sense.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we approximate the solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}v=f\Gamma_{\mu}+{\rm div}(F\Gamma_{\mu})
, subject to zero Dirichlet boundary condition and
build
L^{\infty}
estimate for functions
f,F
in suitable spaces. Section 3 is devoted to prove Theorem
1.1
by developing LSW duality approach.

2
Weak solution of Dual Hardy problem

2.1
Existence

The essential point to obtain
W^{1,p}
estimates by applying the Littman-Stampacchia-Weinberger duality method
is to get
L^{\infty}
estimates of the related dual nonhomogeneous problem. In what follows, we denote by
c_{i}
a generic positive constant.

Let us consider the solution
w
of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}w=f+{\rm div}F&{\rm in}\quad{\Omega},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{L_{\mu}}\displaystyle w=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial{\Omega},\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

where
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and
F:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Let
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the Hilbert space defined as the closure of functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)

under the norm

\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\sqrt{<u,u>_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}

with the inner product

<u,v>_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

A function
w
is called a weak solution of (
2.1
) if
w\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

and

\int_{\Omega}\Big(\nabla w\cdot\nabla v-\frac{2\tau_{+}}{|x|^{2}}\,(x\cdot\nabla w)v\Big)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(fv-F\cdot\nabla v)\,dx\quad\text{ for any }v\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

A function
w
is called a classical solution of (
2.1
) if
w\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{2}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})

and
w
verifies equation (
2.1
) pointwisely in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
.

Our existence results state as follows.

Proposition 2.1

Assume that
N\geq 3
and
\mu>\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}.
Then

(i)
for
\theta\in(0,1)
, for any
f\in C^{\theta}(\bar{\Omega})
and
F\in C^{1,\theta}(\bar{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
problem (
2.1
) has a bounded, unique classical solution.

(ii)
for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
F\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
problem (
2.1
) has a unique weak solution
w\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying that

\|w\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(2.2)

Due to the singularity of the coefficient of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\mu}
, we use the following sequence of operator
to approximate it.
Given
\epsilon\in(0,\,1)
, we denote

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mu,\epsilon}=-\Delta-2\tau_{+}\frac{1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}}}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla

and we consider the approximated problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu,\epsilon}^{*}w=f+{\rm div}F&{\rm in}\quad{\Omega},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{L_{\mu}}\displaystyle w=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial{\Omega},\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

where
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
F:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}}
is the characteristic function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(0)
.

Since
2\tau_{+}1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}}|x|^{-2}\,x
is bounded, smooth in
\Omega\setminus\partial B_{\epsilon}(0)
and

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mu,\epsilon}
is a uniformly elliptic operator, then for any
f\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})

and
F\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, problem (
2.3
) admits a unique classical solution
u_{\epsilon}
by the
Perron’s super and sub solutions’ method and comparison principle.
On the other hand, by the fact that
0\in\Omega
, there exist
\epsilon_{0}\in(0.\,1)
and
R_{0}>\epsilon_{0}
such that

B_{\epsilon_{0}}(0)\subset\Omega\subset B_{R_{0}}(0).

Lemma 2.1

Assume that
N\geq 2
,
\epsilon\in[0,\,\epsilon_{0})
,
\mu>\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}
,
f\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
,

F\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{\epsilon}
is the unique classical solution of problem (
2.3
).
Then there exists
c_{2}>0
independent of
f,F
and
\epsilon
such that

\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c_{2}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(2.4)

Proof.
It is obvious that
u_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for
\epsilon\in(0,\,1)

and we observe that

(N-2)\tau_{+}>-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\ \text{ if }\ \mu>-\frac{3}{4}\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4},

(2.5)

that is,

\frac{4\tau_{+}}{N-2}>-1.

Multiplying
u_{\epsilon}
in (
2.3
) and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain that

\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}-\frac{\tau_{+}1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}}}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{2})\Big)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(fu_{\epsilon}-F\cdot\nabla u_{\epsilon})\,dx.

By Hölder inequality and Poincaré inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(fu_{\epsilon}-F\cdot\nabla u_{\epsilon})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})})(\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})})\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
c_{3}>0
is independent of
f,F
and
\epsilon
.
Observe that
\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}u_{\epsilon}^{2}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega\setminus B_{\epsilon}(0):\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the divergence theorem, (see e.g.
[
31
, Theorem 6.3.4]
), imply that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\epsilon}(0)}\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{2})\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}dx+\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\epsilon}(0)}u_{\epsilon}^{2}{\rm div}(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}u)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\epsilon}(0)}{\rm div}(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}u_{\epsilon}^{2})dx

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\epsilon}(0)}u_{\epsilon}^{2}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\frac{-x}{|x|}d\omega(x)\leq 0,

thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}-\frac{\tau_{+}1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}}}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{2})\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dx+\tau_{+}\int_{\Omega}u_{\epsilon}^{2}\,{\rm div}(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(N-2)\tau_{+}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\epsilon}(0)}\frac{u_{\epsilon}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx.

When
\tau_{+}\geq 0
i.e.
\mu\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}-\frac{\tau_{+}1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}}}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{2})\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

When
N\geq 3
and
0>\mu>-\frac{3}{4}\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
, by Hardy inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}-\frac{\tau_{+}1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}}}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{2})\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(N-2)\tau_{+}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\epsilon}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{4\tau_{+}}{N-2})\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Therefore, there exists
c_{4}>0
independent of
\epsilon,f
and
F
such that

\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c_{4}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}),

which completes the proof.
\Box

Lemma 2.2

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
0<\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon_{2}<\epsilon_{0}
,
\mu>\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}
and
f\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
and
F\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{\epsilon_{1}},\,u_{\epsilon_{2}}
are the unique classical solutions of problem (
2.3
) with
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon=\epsilon_{2}
respecitvely.

Then there exists
c_{5}>0
independent of
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
such that

\|\nabla(u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c_{5}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}^{N-2}-\epsilon_{1}^{N-2}}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Due to the boundedness of
\Omega
, there exists
R_{0}\geq\epsilon_{0}
such that

\Omega\subset B_{R_{0}}(0)
. Let
a_{0}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|{\rm div}F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and

W_{0}(x)=\frac{a_{0}}{N+2}(R_{0}^{2}-|x|^{2}),\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega.

(2.8)

For
\mu>\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0}
, we have that
2N+2\tau_{+}>N+2
and

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mu,\epsilon}W_{0}=\frac{a_{0}}{N+2}(2N+2\tau_{+}1_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}})\geq a_{0}.

In addition, Comparison Principle implies that for any
\epsilon\in(0,\,\epsilon_{0})
,

|u_{\epsilon}|\leq W_{0}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \Omega.

Direct computation shows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu,\epsilon_{1}}^{*}u_{\epsilon_{1}}-\mathcal{L}_{\mu,\epsilon}^{*}u_{\epsilon_{2}})(u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}})\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}})|^{2}dx+\tau_{+}\int_{\Omega}(u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}})^{2}\,{\rm div}(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}1_{B_{\epsilon_{1}}^{c}})dx

\displaystyle-2\tau_{+}\int_{B_{\epsilon_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{\epsilon_{1}}(0)}(u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}})\frac{x\cdot\nabla u_{\epsilon_{2}}}{|x|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{4\tau_{+}}{N-2})\|\nabla(u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-2|\tau_{+}|(\int_{B_{\epsilon_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{\epsilon_{1}}(0)}\frac{|u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\epsilon_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{4\tau_{+}}{N-2})\|\nabla(u_{\epsilon_{1}}-u_{\epsilon_{2}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-4a_{0}|\tau_{+}|(\int_{B_{\epsilon_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{\epsilon_{1}}(0)}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}dx)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\epsilon_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

thus, by Lemma

\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{17}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})}.

Henceforth, we have that

\left|\int_{\Omega}u\Gamma_{\mu}{\rm div}F\,dx\right|\leq c_{18}\|\nu\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\,\Gamma_{\mu})}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})}.

Consequently, the functional

F\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega},d\gamma_{\mu};\mathbb{R}^{N})\mapsto\int_{\Omega}u\Gamma_{\mu}\,{\rm div}F\,dx

admits a unique continuous linear extension in
L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists a unique function
\tilde{\bf u}\in L^{q}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu};\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that for any
F\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\bar{\Omega},d\gamma_{\mu};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\int_{\Omega}u\Gamma_{\mu}\,{\rm div}F\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\bf u}\cdot F\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(\gamma_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\tilde{\bf u})\cdot(\gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}F)\,dx,

which implies that
\Gamma_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\tilde{\bf u}=\Gamma_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\nabla(u\Gamma_{\mu})=\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}\nabla u+\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}u\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\|(\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}\nabla u+\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}u\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq c_{23}\|\nu\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\,\Gamma_{\mu})}.

Claim 1:
for
1\leq q<p^{*}_{\mu}
, we have that

\|\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}u\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq c_{24}\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})}.

Thus,

\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}\nabla u=[(\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}\nabla u+\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}u\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})-\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}u\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}]\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
i.e.
|\nabla u|\in L^{q}(\Omega,\,d\gamma_{\mu})
.

We end the proof by showing
Claim 1
. In fact, by Lemma
3.1
, we have that
u\Gamma_{\mu}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for any
p\in[1,\,\frac{N}{N-1})
, then by Sobolev Embedding theorem, for any
\sigma\in[1,\frac{N}{N-2})
, there holds that

\|u\Gamma_{\mu}\|_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}\leq c_{25}\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})}.

We observe that for
1\leq q<p^{*}_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big|\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{q}}u\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\Big|^{q}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\tau_{+}(1-q)-q}||x|^{\tau_{+}}u|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\int_{\Omega}||x|^{\tau_{+}}u|^{q_{1}}dx)^{\frac{q}{q_{1}}}(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{[\tau_{+}(1-q)-q]q_{2}}dx)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}},

where
q_{2}=\frac{q_{1}}{q_{1}-q}
and we require that

q_{1}<\frac{N}{N-2}

(3.11)

and

[\tau_{+}(1-q)-q]q_{2}>-N.

(3.12)

(
3.12
) could be equivalent to

Nq<[N-q-\tau_{+}(q-1)]q_{1}
, then by (
3.11

(N-2)q<N-q-\tau_{+}(q-1),

which holds for any

.

For the case
\mu\in(\frac{3}{4}\mu_{0},0)
,
we note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}d\gamma_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Gamma_{\mu}|\nabla(\Gamma_{\mu}^{-1}(\Gamma_{\mu}u))|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p}\Big(|\tau_{+}|^{p}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{(1-p)\tau_{+}-p}|\Gamma_{\mu}u|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|x|^{(1-p)\tau_{+}}|\nabla(\Gamma_{\mu}u)|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{26}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\Gamma_{\mu}u|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\Gamma_{\mu}u)|^{p}dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{27}\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega^{*};\Gamma_{\mu})}^{p},

where we used
p\geq 1
and

for

4
More global regularity: the Marcinkiewicz estimates

In this section, we append a bit discussion of global regularity for non-homogeneous
Hardy problem (
1.1
). To this end, we recall the definition and basic properties of the Marcinkiewicz
spaces.

Definition 4.1

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a domain and
\nu
be a positive
Borel measure in
\Omega
. For
\kappa>1
,

\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa/(\kappa-1)
and
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega,d\nu)
, we
set

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|u\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Omega,d\nu)}=\inf\{c\in[0,\infty]:\int_{E}|u|d\nu\leq c\left(\int_{E}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}},\ \forall E\subset\Omega\ \rm{Borel\ set}\}\end{array}

and

M^{\kappa}(\Omega,d\nu)=\{u\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega,d\nu):\|u\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Omega,\,d\nu)}<+\infty\}.

(4.13)

M^{\kappa}(\Omega,d\nu)
is called the Marcinkiewicz space with
exponent
\kappa
or weak
L^{\kappa}
space and

\|.\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Omega,d\nu)}
is a quasi-norm. The following
property holds.

Proposition 4.1

[
1
,
2
]

Assume that
1\leq q<\kappa<+\infty
and
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega,d\nu)
.
Then there exists
C(q,\kappa)>0
such that

\int_{E}|u|^{q}d\nu\leq C(q,\kappa)\|u\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Omega,d\nu)}\left(\int_{E}d\nu\right)^{1-q/\kappa}

for any Borel set
E
of
\Omega
.

Proposition 4.2

Let
\mu>0
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\ (N\geq 2)
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain
and
\mathbb{G}_{\mu}[\nu](\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\mu}(\cdot,y)d\sigma(y)
for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{M}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\mu})
. Then there exists
c_{28}>0
such that

\|\mathbb{G}_{\mu}[\sigma]\|_{M^{q}(\Omega,\,d\gamma_{\mu})}\leq c_{28}\|\sigma\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega,\,\Gamma_{\mu})},

(4.14)

where
1<q\leq\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}
.

Proof.
For
\lambda>0
and
y\in\Omega
, we denote

A_{\lambda}(y)=\{x\in\Omega\setminus\{y\}:G_{\mu}(x,y)>\lambda\}\ \ {\rm{and}}\quad m_{\lambda}(y)=\int_{A_{\lambda}(y)}d\gamma_{\mu}.

From
[
11
, Lemma 4.1]
if follows that for any
(x,y)\in(\Omega\setminus\{0\})\times(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
,
x\neq y
,

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{29}\frac{\Gamma_{\mu}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}.

Observe that

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}(y)\subset\left\{x\in\Omega\setminus\{y\}:\frac{c_{29}\Gamma_{\mu}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}>\lambda\right\}=D_{\lambda}(y),

where

D_{\lambda}(y)=\left\{x\in\Omega:|x-y|<(\frac{c_{29}\Gamma_{\mu}(y)}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}\right\}
.
Then we have that

m_{\lambda}(y)\leq\int_{D_{\lambda}(y)}d\gamma_{\mu}\leq\int_{D_{\lambda}(0)}d\gamma_{\mu}=c_{29}(\frac{c_{29}\Gamma_{\mu}(y)}{\lambda})^{\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}.

(4.15)

For any Borel set
E
of
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}G_{\mu}(x,y)\Gamma_{\mu}(x)dx\leq\int_{A_{\lambda}(y)}G_{\mu}(x,y)\Gamma_{\mu}(x)dx+\lambda\int_{E}d\gamma_{\mu}.

Using Fubini’s theorem, integration by parts formula and estimate (
4.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\lambda}(y)}G_{\mu}(x,y)d\gamma_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}sdm_{s}(y)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda m_{\lambda}(y)+\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}m_{s}(y)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{30}\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}(y)\lambda^{1-\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{E}G_{\mu}(x,y)d\gamma_{\mu}\leq c_{30}\Gamma_{\mu}^{\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}(y)\lambda^{1-\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}+\lambda\int_{E}d\gamma_{\mu}.

By choosing
\lambda=\Gamma_{\mu}(y)(\int_{E}d\gamma_{\mu})^{-\frac{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}
and
c_{31}=c_{30}+1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}G_{\mu}(x,y)d\gamma_{\mu}\leq c_{31}\Gamma_{\mu}(y)(\int_{E}d\gamma_{\mu})^{\frac{2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{E}\mathbb{G}_{\mu}[|\sigma|](x)\Gamma_{\mu}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{E}G_{\mu}(x,y)\Gamma_{\mu}(x)dxd|\sigma(y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{31}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma_{\mu}(y)d|\sigma(y)|\left(\int_{E}d\gamma_{\mu}\right)^{\frac{2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{31}\|\sigma\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\mu})}\left(\int_{E}d\gamma_{\mu}\right)^{\frac{2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{G}_{\mu}[\sigma]\|_{M^{\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)}{N-2+\tau_{+}(\mu)}}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})}\leq c_{31}\|\sigma\|_{\mathfrak{M}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\mu})}.

We complete the proof.
\Box

We remark that the biggest defect of this Marcinkiewicz estimate in Proposition
4.2
is the restriction
that
\mu>0
. For
N\geq 3
and
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
, a much weaker type of Marcinkiewicz estimate could
be derived due to the Green kernel’s estimates, see
[
11
, Lemma 4.1]
.

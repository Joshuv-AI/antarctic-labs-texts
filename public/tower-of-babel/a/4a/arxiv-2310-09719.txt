Klingen Vectors for Depth Zero Supercuspidals of
GSp

Abstract




1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with a dimension counting problem in
p
-adic representation theory for the group
{\rm GSp}(4)
. Part of the motivation comes from the following problem in the classical theory of Siegel modular forms. If
\Gamma\subset{\rm Sp}(4,{\mathbb{Q}})
is a congruence subgroup, then the dimension of the space of cusp forms of level
\Gamma
(and integer weight
k
, say) is not known in general. For example, if

\Gamma=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm Sp}(4,{\mathbb{Z}})

is the “Klingen congruence subgroup of level
4
” then the associated dimensions were only recently computed in
[
RSY2022
]
. The method by which this was achieved required, as one of its several inputs, the dimensions of spaces of fixed vectors in all irreducible smooth representations of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Q}}_{2})
for the subgroups

{\rm Kl}(4)=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

These dimensions, and more, had been computed in
[
Yi2021
]
. We remark that the different “shape” of the subgroups in question is an artifact of different conventions for alternating forms in the classical and representation-theoretic contexts.

If one hopes to use the approach taken in
[
RSY2022
]
for other congruence subgroups, then a necessary component is the determination of dimensions of spaces of fixed vectors in all irreducible smooth representations of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
for appropriate local subgroups. In this paper we are concerned with the subgroups

{\rm Kl}(p^{n})=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).

At this time, computing the associated dimensions for all representations appears overly ambitious, so we restrict ourselves to the very special case of depth zero supercuspidals (constructed in the next section). While this is a limitation, some interesting phenomena already arise that had not been observed for
n\leq 2
. The most significant of these is that the dimensions now depend on
p
(once
n\geq 4
), and on finer internal structure of these supercuspidals (once
n\geq 3
). This internal structure can also be expressed in terms of Langlands parameters or of
L
-packets; see Remark
5.3
. We note in particular that dependence on
p
never occurs when one instead considers the sequence paramodular subgroups or stable Klingen subgroups; see
[
Ro2007
]
and
[
J2022
]
. This difference has implications for how one might hope a local newform and oldform theory would work for the Klingen congruence subgroups. For example, it suggests that one would need a plethora of raising operators to obtain all oldforms.

We give a brief outline of the contents of this paper. After setting up notation and definitions in section 2, we show in section 3 that one class of depth zero supercuspidal representations (all of which are nongeneric) will not have any fixed vectors for
{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
; see Theorem
3.1
. In section 4 we consider the remaining depth zero supercuspidals, and reduce to the determination and enumeration of a certain set of double cosets, together with a collection of character table computations for
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Along the way, we can conclude that depth zero supercuspidals will have no fixed vectors for
{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
(for all
n\geq 0
) if they are nongeneric. We conjecture that this remains true for all nongeneric supercuspidals. The main result, our dimension formula, is Theorem
5.1
.

Setup

Let
F
be a finite extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, with ring of integers
{\mathfrak{o}}
, maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, uniformizer
\varpi
, and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&&&1\\
&&1&\\
&-1&&\\
-1&&&\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and let
G={\rm GSp}(4,F)=\{g\in{\rm GL}(4,F):{{}^{t}}gJg=\mu(g)J\}
,
Z=Z(G)
,
T\subset G
the set of diagonal matrices, and
G^{0}=\{g\in G:\mu(g)\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}\}
. We will need the two maximal compact subgroups
K={\rm GSp}(4,{\mathfrak{o}})
and

{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\\
\mathfrak{p}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}&\mathfrak{p}&\mathfrak{p}&{\mathfrak{o}}\end{bmatrix}\cap G^{0}

as well as the standard Iwahori subgroup

I=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}&{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}&\mathfrak{p}&{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}&\mathfrak{p}&\mathfrak{p}&{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}\end{bmatrix}\cap G

and the sequence of Klingen congruence subgroups

{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}^{n}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}^{n}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}&{\mathfrak{o}}\\
\mathfrak{p}^{n}&\mathfrak{p}^{n}&\mathfrak{p}^{n}&{\mathfrak{o}}\end{bmatrix}\cap G^{0}

where
n
is a positive integer. Let
K^{+}
and
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
be the prounipotent radicals of
K
and
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
, respectively. Define the two matrices

s_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}&1&&\\
1&&&\\
&&&1\\
&&1&\end{bmatrix},\qquad s_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&&1&\\
&-1&&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}.

The images of
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
generate the Weyl group
W:=N_{G}(T)/T
.

If
H
is a subgroup of
G
and
\rho:H\to{\rm GL}(W)
is a representation of
H
, then
\text{\rm c-Ind}_{H}^{G}(\rho)
is the space of functions
f:G\to W
such that
f(hg)=\rho(h)f(g)
for all
h\in H
,
g\in G
, with
f
being smooth and having compact support modulo
H
. This space is a smooth representation of
G
under right translation.

If
\pi
is a depth zero supercuspidal representation of
G
then
\pi
arises in one of two ways. Either
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{ZK}^{G}(\sigma)
where
\sigma|_{K}
is an inflation of an irreducible cuspidal representation of
K/K^{+}
, or
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))}^{G}(\tau)
where
\tau
is an irreducible representation of
N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))
and
\tau|_{{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})}
is an inflation of a cuspidal representation of
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})/{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
. We will compute
\dim\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}
for all such
\pi
. Since
\pi
is supercuspidal, it has no fixed vectors for an Iwahori subgroup, and in particular
\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p})}=0
. We therefore need consider only fixed vectors for
{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
with
n\geq 2
.

3
Klingen vectors for
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))}^{G}(\tau)

We will first consider those depth zero supercuspidals coming from
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})
. It turns out that these do not admit any Klingen vectors of any level.

3.1 Theorem.

Let
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))}^{G}(\tau)
as above. Then
\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}=0
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

For
g\in G
and
n\geq 1
define

R_{g}:=\left(g{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})g^{-1}\cap{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})\right){\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}=\left(g{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})g^{-1}\cap N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))\right){\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}.

If
f\in\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}
then
f(g)\in\tau^{R_{g}}
. It suffices to show
\tau^{R_{g}}=0
for
g
ranging over a set of representatives for
N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))\backslash G/{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
. We have
G=IN_{G}(T)I
by a well-known decomposition (see
[
Iw1965
]
). Since
N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))
contains
I
along with a set of representatives for
N_{G}(T)/T
, and using the Iwahori factorization of
I
, we may take

g=\begin{bmatrix}\varpi^{2i+j}&&&\\
&\varpi^{i+j}&&\\
&&\varpi^{i}&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
x&1&&\\
y&&1&\\
z&y&-x&1\end{bmatrix}

where
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{p}
. Using
s_{2}\in{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})\cap{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})
we may assume that
i+j\geq i
, so
j\geq 0
.

If
x=0
then let
d\in{\mathfrak{o}}
and compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&\varpi^{j}d&1&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&d&1&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}.

Thus
R_{g}\supset\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&{\mathfrak{o}}&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right].
The image of
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&{\mathfrak{o}}&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
in
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})/{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
is the unipotent radical of a parabolic subgroup. So
\tau^{R_{g}}=0
since
\tau|_{{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})}
is cuspidal.

If
x\neq 0
then we may assume
1\leq{\rm val}(x)\leq i
, since
g=S(\varpi^{-i}x,0,0)t_{i,j}S(0,y,z+xy)
, and
S(\varpi^{-i}x,0,0)\in{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})
if
{\rm val}(x)>i
. Then let
d\in\mathfrak{p}^{2i+j+1-2{\rm val}(x)}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{j+1}
, so
dx\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+j+1}
, and compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&d&1&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
-\varpi^{-i-j}dx&\varpi^{-j}d&1&\\
-\varpi^{-2i-j}dx^{2}&-\varpi^{-i-j}dx&&1\end{bmatrix}.

Thus
R_{g}\supset\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
\mathfrak{p}&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right].
The image of
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
\mathfrak{p}&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
in
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})/{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
is the unipotent radical of a parabolic subgroup. So
\tau^{R_{g}}=0
since
\tau|_{{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})}
is cuspidal. ∎

Klingen vectors for
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{ZK}^{G}(\sigma)

For
g\in G
and fixed
n\geq 1
define the objects

\displaystyle[g]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ZKg{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})

\displaystyle R_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})g^{-1}\cap K)K^{+}/K^{+}

\displaystyle{\rm Supp}(\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{[g]:\sigma^{R_{g}}\neq 0\}=\{[g]:\exists f\in\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})},\ f(g)\neq 0\}.

Then

\dim\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}=\sum\limits_{[g]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)}\dim\sigma^{R_{g}}.

We therefore require a determination of

4.1
Determination of
{\rm Supp}(\pi)

In this section we obtain a parametrization of
{\rm Supp}(\pi)
; in a subsequent section we will compute the values of
\dim\sigma^{R_{g}}
via the character table of
K/K^{+}={\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. We have
G=IN_{G}(T)I
by a well-known decomposition; see
[
Iw1965
]
. Since
K\supset I
and
K\supset\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle
, using the Iwahori factorization for
I
we may take double coset representatives for
ZK\backslash G/{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
to have the form

t_{i,j}S(x,y,z):=\begin{bmatrix}\varpi^{2i+j}&&&\\
&\varpi^{i+j}&&\\
&&\varpi^{i}&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
x&1&&\\
y&&1&\\
z&y&-x&1\end{bmatrix}

where
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{p}
. Using
s_{2}\in{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})\cap K
we may assume that
i+j\geq i
, so
j\geq 0
.

4.1 Lemma.

Consider
[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]
with
j\geq 0
.

a) If
x\neq 0
we may assume
1\leq{\rm val}(x)\leq i-1
. If
y\neq 0
we may assume
1\leq{\rm val}(y)\leq i+j-1
. If
z\neq 0
we may assume
1\leq{\rm val}(z)\leq\min(2i+j,n)-1
.

b) If
xy\neq 0
we may assume
1\leq{\rm val}(y/x)\leq j-1
.

c) If
xz\neq 0
, we may assume
1\leq{\rm val}(z/x)\leq i+j-1
.

d) If
yz\neq 0
we may assume
\min(1,1+i)\leq{\rm val}(z/y)\leq\max(i,{\rm val}(y))-1
.

Proof.

a) These conditions follow from the equalities

\displaystyle t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\varpi^{-i}x,0,0)t_{i,j}S(0,y,z+xy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(0,\varpi^{-i-j}y,0)t_{i,j}S(x,0,z-xy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(0,0,\varpi^{-2i-j}z)t_{i,j}S(x,y,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{i,j}S(x,y,0)S(0,0,z).

b) Let
c\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}
and
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&b&\\
&c&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in K
. Then

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(t_{i,j}At_{i,j}^{-1}\right)t_{i,j}A^{-1}S(x,y,z)A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x-by,y-cx,z)].

If
{\rm val}(x)\geq{\rm val}(y)
then we may take
c=0
and
b=x/y
to eliminate
x
. If
{\rm val}(y)-{\rm val}(x)\geq j
then we may take
b=0
and
c=y/x
to eliminate
y
.

c) If
z/x\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+j}
then
z/x^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{j+1}
since
x\neq 0
and using the first part. For
d\in{\mathfrak{o}}
let
A(d)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&d&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)A(-z/x^{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[A(-\varpi^{-j}z/x^{2})t_{i,j}S(x,y+z/x,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(0,z/x,0)S(x,y,0)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[S(0,\varpi^{-i-j}z/x,0)t_{i,j}S(x,y,0)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,0)].

If
{\rm val}(z/x)\leq 0
then we may assume
1\leq{\rm val}(x)<i
and
i+j>0
. For
d\in{\mathfrak{o}}
, let
C(d)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&d&\\
&1&&d\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}C(x/z)C(-x/z)S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[C(\varpi^{i+j}x/z)t_{i,j}C(-x/z)S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}C(-x/z)S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(0,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})h]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(0,y,z)].

where
y^{\prime}=y/(1-xy/z)
,
z^{\prime}=z/(1-xy/z)
,

h=\begin{bmatrix}1-xy/z&&-x/z&\\
&1-xy/z&x^{2}/z&-x/z\\
&&1+xy^{\prime}/z&\\
&&&1+xy^{\prime}/z\end{bmatrix}\in{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})

and we used integral diagonal matrices for the last equality.

d) Suppose
{\rm val}(z/y)\geq\max(i,{\rm val}(y))
. For
d\in{\mathfrak{o}}
let
B(d)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&d&\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)B(z/y^{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B(\varpi^{j}z/y^{2})t_{i,j}S(x-z/y,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(-z/y,0,0)S(x,y,0)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[S(-\varpi^{-i}z/y,0,0)t_{i,j}S(x,y,0)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,0)].

Finally, suppose
{\rm val}(z/y)\leq\min(0,i)
. Let
D(d)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&d&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&-d\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}D(-y/z)D(y/z)S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[D(-\varpi^{i}y/z)t_{i,j}D(y/z)S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}D(y/z)S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x^{\prime},0,z^{\prime})h]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,0,z)].

where
x^{\prime}=x/(1+xy/z)
,
z^{\prime}=z/(1+xy/z)
,

h=\begin{bmatrix}1+xy/z&y/z&&\\
&1-x^{\prime}y/z&&\\
&&1+xy/z&-y/z\\
&&&1-x^{\prime}y/z\end{bmatrix}\in{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})

and we used integral diagonal matrices for the last equality.
∎

Define the two unipotent radicals
U_{S},U_{K}\subset{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
by

U_{S}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
x&y&1&\\
z&x&&1\end{bmatrix}:x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\},\qquad U_{K}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\

Since

is cuspidal,
\sigma^{R_{g}}=0
if
R_{g}
contains a conjugate of

or

.

4.2 Lemma.

Let
j\geq 0
and
[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
. Then
2-n\leq i\leq n-1
and
2i+j\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
g=t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)
. We compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
\varpi^{n}c_{1}&1&&\\
\varpi^{n}c_{2}&&1&\\
\varpi^{n}c_{3}&\varpi^{n}c_{2}&-\varpi^{n}c_{1}&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
\varpi^{n-i}c_{1}&1&&\\
\varpi^{n-i-j}c_{2}&&1&\\
\varpi^{n-2i-j}(c_{3}-2c_{2}x+2c_{1}y)&\varpi^{n}c_{2}&-\varpi^{n}c_{1}&1\end{bmatrix}.

If
i\geq n
then
i+j\geq n
and
2i+j\geq n
. Taking
c_{1}\in\mathfrak{p}^{i-n}
,
c_{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{j-j-n}
, and
c_{3}\in 2c_{2}x-2c_{1}y+\mathfrak{p}^{2i+j-n}
shows
R_{g}\supset U_{K}
. Thus
i\leq n-1
. If
2i+j\leq 0
then
i\leq 0
and we may assume
x=z=0
. We compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&b_{1}&&b_{3}\\
&1&&\\
&d&1&-b_{1}\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\varpi^{i}(b_{1}-yb_{3})&&\varpi^{2i+j}b_{3}\\
&1&&\\
&\varpi^{-j}(d+y(2b_{1}-yb_{3})&1&-\varpi^{i}(b_{1}-yb_{3})\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}.

Taking
b_{1}\in yb_{3}+\mathfrak{p}^{-i}
,
b_{3}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-2i-j}
, and
d\in-y(2b_{1}-yb_{3})+\mathfrak{p}^{j}
shows that
R_{g}
contains a conjugate of
U_{S}
. Thus
2i+j\geq 1
. Finally, if
i\leq 1-n\leq-1
then we may assume
x=0
. Note
i+j\geq-i+1\geq n
. If
z\neq 0
, then we compute

g\begin{bmatrix}a&&&\\
&1&&\\
\varpi^{n}c&d&a&\\
&\varpi^{n}ca^{-1}&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}a&&&\\
&1&&\\
\varpi^{n-i-j}c&\varpi^{-j}d&a&\\
\varpi^{-2i-j}z(a-1)&\varpi^{n-i-j}ca^{-1}&&1\end{bmatrix}.

We may assume
{\rm val}(z)<2i+j
, and let

,

, and
a\in 1+\mathfrak{p}^{2i+j-{\rm val}(z)}
. Then

. If
z=0
, then we compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&b&&\\
&1&&\\
\varpi^{n}c&d&1&-b\\
&\varpi^{n}c&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\varpi^{i}b&&\\
&1&&\\
\varpi^{n-i-j}c&\varpi^{-j}(d+2yb)&1&-\varpi^{i}b\\

,

, and

contains a conjugate of

.

4.3 Proposition.

Suppose
j\geq 0
and
[g]:=[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
.

must hold in all cases. Suppose next that
i\geq{\rm val}(xz/y)
. Then
j\geq{\rm val}(y+z/x)
. The proof so far makes clear that

R_{g}\supset\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&&\\
&a&&\\
b&&a&\\
c&b&&a\end{bmatrix}:a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}.

Let

,

,
d_{1}=1-bx
and
d_{3}=d_{1}^{-1}(m\left(1-\frac{bz}{y}\right)+b^{2}z+by(bx-2))
. We compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&b&&\\
&d_{1}&&\\
&d_{3}&1&-b\\
&&&d_{1}\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}d_{1}&\varpi^{i}b&&\\
&1&&\\
&\varpi^{-j}m&d_{1}&-\varpi^{i}b\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}

.

Finally, suppose

. Then taking

,

as above, we compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&d_{3}&d_{4}&\\
\frac{-d_{3}xz}{y}&&&d_{4}\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&\varpi^{-j}d_{3}&d_{4}&\\
&&&d_{4}\end{bmatrix}

and conclude that
R_{g}\supset U_{S}
.

Proof of ii): Suppose
{\rm val}(y^{2}/x^{2})\leq j
. Take

and compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1+dx/y&-dx^{2}/y^{2}&\\
&d&1-dx/y&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1+dx/y&-\varpi^{j}dx^{2}/y^{2}&\\
&\varpi^{-j}d&1-dx/y&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}.

Together with the above, this yields

.

Conversely, suppose that i) and ii) hold. Take
m\in\mathfrak{p}^{i}
,
d_{1}=1-\frac{m}{x}\in 1+\mathfrak{p}
,
d_{3}=-m(y+\frac{z}{x})\frac{y}{d_{1}xz}\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}
, and

. Then

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&d_{1}&&\\
&d_{3}&d_{4}&\\
&&&d_{1}d_{4}\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
\varpi^{-i}m&d_{1}&&\\
0&\varpi^{-j}d_{3}&d_{4}&\\
0&-\varpi^{-i-j}md_{3}&-\varpi^{-i}d_{4}m&d_{1}d_{4}\end{bmatrix}

which shows that
R_{g}
contains a conjugate of the claimed subgroup. We will show that equality holds. Then Lemma
4.20
below shows that
\dim\sigma^{R_{g}}>0
.

Observe that
{\rm val}(z)\geq i+1+{\rm val}(y/x)\geq i+1+\frac{j+1}{2}
and
{\rm val}(y)\geq\frac{j+1}{2}+{\rm val}(x)
so
{\rm val}(yz)>i+j
,
{\rm val}(y^{2})>j
,
{\rm val}(z^{2})>2i+j
and
{\rm val}(xz)>i
. It follows that

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&b\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}\in K^{+}

for all

. Since
i+j<n
, we also have

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
\varpi^{n}c_{1}&1&&\\
\varpi^{n}c_{2}&&1&\\
&\varpi^{n}c_{2}&-\varpi^{n}c_{1}&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}\in\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
\mathfrak{p}^{n-2i-j}&&&1\end{bmatrix}K^{+}

for all

. So to determine

we need to consider

&d_{1}&d_{2}&\\
&d_{3}&d_{4}&\\
\varpi^{n}c&&&d_{1}d_{4}-d_{2}d_{3}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&b_{1}&b_{2}&\\
&1&&b_{2}\\
&&1&-b_{1}\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}.

Suppose first that
{\rm val}(y)>j
. The condition

forces

, as well as the existence of

and
m_{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+j}
such that

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-b_{1}x-b_{2}y-\frac{d_{2}y}{x}-\frac{m_{1}}{x}

\displaystyle d_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{b_{1}z}{y}\right)^{-1}(1-b_{1}x-b_{2}y-\frac{d_{3}x}{y}-\frac{m_{2}}{y}).

One then only needs the
(4,1)
entry in

to lie in
{\mathfrak{o}}
. It can be checked that this forces
{\rm val}(\varpi^{-i}m_{1})={\rm val}(\varpi^{-j}d_{3})
, and the form of

follows.

Now suppose that
{\rm val}(y)\leq j
. This forces
{\rm val}(z)={\rm val}(xy)
and

, which also implies

and
{\rm val}(y^{2})>i+j

then forces the existence of
m_{6}\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}
and
m_{1},m_{2}
as above with

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{d_{2}y}{x}-\frac{m_{1}}{x}

\displaystyle d_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{b_{1}z}{y}\right)^{-1}(1-b_{1}x-\frac{d_{3}x}{y}-\frac{m_{2}}{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-b_{2}y\right)^{-1}(m_{6}-b_{1}y(1+d_{4})).

One then only needs the

{\mathfrak{o}}
. It can be checked that this forces

∎

We now make the following observation.

4.8 Theorem.

Suppose
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{ZK}^{G}(\sigma)
is a nongeneric depth zero supercuspidal irreducible representation of

. Then
\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}=0

.

Proof.

The condition on
\pi
is equivalent to
\sigma
being a nongeneric cuspidal irreducible representation of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. For all the subgroups
R_{g}
associated to potential

, we have seen that
R_{g}
contains a conjugate of the subgroup
\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&x\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]:x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}
. So
\sigma^{R_{g}}=0
by Lemma
4.16
.
∎

4.9 Corollary.

Suppose
\pi
is a nongeneric depth zero supercuspidal irreducible representation of

. Then
\pi^{{\rm Kl}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}=0
for all

This follows from Theorems

and

.
∎

4.2

We have determined the necessary and sufficient conditions on

{\rm Supp}(\pi)
. It remains to consider when two such double cosets are equal, and then to enumerate
{\rm Supp}(\pi)

The first column of Table 1 below lists the families of double coset representatives. The conjugacy class of

in
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})

[t_{i,j}X_{k}]
and
[t_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}Y_{k^{\prime}}]

determines

and

and

[t_{i,j}Y_{k}]

4.10 Remark.

The functions supported on the double cosets
ZKt_{i,j}{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
, where
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
is the paramodular group of level
\mathfrak{p}^{n}

-fixed vectors in

. Since

determines these
i
and
j
.

This just leaves the last family of double cosets
[g]=[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]
considered in Lemma
4.7
. It is easy to show by direct computation that
[g]
determines
{\rm val}(x)
,
{\rm val}(y)
, and
{\rm val}(z)
. Using integral diagonal matrices, we may assume that

and

are powers of
\varpi
. So it remains only to consider what conditions on a unit

are necessary and sufficient to allow

4.11 Lemma.

Let
[g]:=[t_{i,j}S(x,y,uz)]
and
[g_{1}]:=[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]
lie in
{\rm Supp}(\pi)
, where
[g_{1}]
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
4.7
and
u\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}
. Then
[g]=[g_{1}]
if and only if
u\in 1+\mathfrak{p}^{j-{\rm val}(y/x)}
.

Proof.

If
{\rm val}(u-1)\geq j-{\rm val}(y/x)
then
[g_{1}]=[g]
since

g_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&\frac{y}{x}(1-u)&u&\\
&&&u\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&\varpi^{-j}\frac{y}{x}(1-u)&u&\\
&&&u\end{bmatrix}\in K.

If
{\rm val}(u-1)<j-{\rm val}(y/x)
then one can show by a tedious matrix computation that
[g_{1}]\neq[g]
; we omit the details.∎

For
\vec{k}=(i,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},u)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{4}\times{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}
let
j=i+{\rm val}(\varpi^{k_{y}}+u\varpi^{k_{z}-k_{x}})-(k_{x}+k_{z}-k_{y})
; we assume that
\varpi^{k_{y}}+u\varpi^{k_{z}-k_{x}}\neq 0
. Then define

We have determined the conditions on
\vec{k}
so that
[S(\vec{k})]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
, as well as necessary and sufficient conditions for two such double cosets to be distinct. It remains to compute the number of such double cosets. First, we do so for the others.

4.12 Proposition.

The numbers of distinct double cosets of each type, except those of the form
[S(\vec{k})]
, are given in the last column of Table 1.

Proof.

The numbers of double cosets
[t_{i,j}]
for the first four rows of Table 1 are easy to compute.

Consider the double cosets
[t_{i,j}X_{k}]
. The conditions on
(i,j,k)
can be written as
k+1\leq i\leq n-1-j-k
,
1\leq j\leq n-2-2k
,
1\leq k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\right\rfloor
. The reader can check that

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\right\rfloor}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-2-2k}(n-1-j-2k)=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)(n-3)(2n-7)}{24}\right\rfloor.

It is not hard to check that the map
(i,j,k)\mapsto(i,j,i+j+k)
induces a bijection between the sets of
[t_{i,j}X_{k}]
and
[t_{i,j}Z_{k}]
lying in
{\rm Supp}(\pi)
. Equality with the number of
[t_{i,j}X_{k}]
follows.

For
[t_{i,j}Y_{k}]
, the conditions
1\leq j
,
1\leq i
,
k\leq i+j-1
, and
2i+j\leq\min(n,2k)-1
force
i+\left\lceil\frac{j+1}{2}\right\rceil\leq k

1\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-j-1}{2}\right\rfloor
, and

\sum\limits_{j=3}^{n-3}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-j-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\sum\limits_{k=i+\left\lceil\frac{j+1}{2}\right\rceil}^{i+j-1}(1)=\frac{n-3}{6}\left\lfloor\frac{(n-2)(n-4)}{4}\right\rfloor.

This completes the proof.
∎

We finally count the last family of double cosets, those of the form
[g]=[S(\vec{k})]
. For ease of notation, let
x=\varpi^{k_{x}}
,
y=\varpi^{k_{y}}
and
z=u\varpi^{k_{z}}
. For a fixed
(i,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})

[S(\vec{k})]
correspond to orbits of

under multiplication on the sets

\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}&\text{ if }{\rm val}(z)\neq{\rm val}(xy)\\
{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}\setminus(-1+\mathfrak{p})&\text{ if }{\rm val}(z)={\rm val}(xy)\text{ and }{\rm val}(1+u)=0\\
(-1+\mathfrak{p}^{{\rm val}(1+u)})\setminus(-1+\mathfrak{p}^{{\rm val}(1+u)+1})&\text{ if }{\rm val}(z)={\rm val}(xy)\text{ and }{\rm val}(1+u)>0\end{cases}.

This follows from Lemma
4.11
. So the number of choices of

yielding distinct
[S(\vec{k})]
are

\begin{cases}(q-1)q^{j-{\rm val}(y/x)-1}&\text{ if }{\rm val}(z)\neq{\rm val}(xy)\\
(q-2)q^{j-{\rm val}(y/x)-1}&\text{ if }{\rm val}(z)={\rm val}(xy)\text{ and }{\rm val}(1+u)=0\\
(q-1)q^{j-{\rm val}(y/x)-{\rm val}(1+u)-1}&\text{ if }{\rm val}(z)={\rm val}(xy)\text{ and }{\rm val}(1+u)>0\end{cases}.

Note
j-{\rm val}(y/x)-{\rm val}(1+u)=i-{\rm val}(x)>0
when

. Let

.

4.13 Lemma.

a) The number of double cosets
[S(\vec{k})]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
with
{\rm val}(z)<{\rm val}(xy)
is

q^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-5}{4}\right\rfloor}a_{q}\left(n-4\left\lfloor\frac{n-5}{4}\right\rfloor\right)-a_{q}(n)

b) The number of double cosets
[S(\vec{k})]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
with
{\rm val}(z)>{\rm val}(xy)
is the same as the number with
{\rm val}(z)<{\rm val}(xy)
.

Proof.

a) Let
a={\rm val}(x)-k
,
b={\rm val}(y)-2k
and
c={\rm val}(z)-3k
. The system of inequalities becomes

c-b+1\leq a\leq b
,
\left\lfloor\frac{c+2}{2}\right\rfloor\leq b\leq c
,

1\leq c\leq n-4k
,

\leq k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{4}\right\rfloor=:M
. Let
A=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-5)(n-7)(2n-15)}{24}\right\rfloor
. The number of double cosets is
q-1
times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\sum\limits_{c=1}^{n-4k}\sum\limits_{b=\left\lfloor\frac{c+2}{2}\right\rfloor}^{c}(2b-c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\sum\limits_{c=1}^{n-4k}\left\lfloor\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{c+2}{2}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\left\lfloor\frac{(n-4k+1)(n-4k+3)(2n-8k+1)}{24}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{M-2}q^{m}\left\lfloor\frac{(n-7-4m)(n-5-4m)(2n-15-8m)}{24}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{M-2}q^{m}\left(A-(n^{2}-17n+73)m+(4n-42)m(m-1)-\frac{16}{3}m(m-1)(m-2)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(A-(n^{2}-17n+73)q\frac{d}{dq}+(4n-42)q^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dq^{2}}-\frac{16}{3}q^{3}\frac{d^{3}}{dq^{3}}\right)\left(\frac{q^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-5}{4}\right\rfloor}-1}{q-1}\right).

The remainder of the computation is tedious but elementary.

b) Let
a={\rm val}(x)+k
,
b={\rm val}(y)
and
c={\rm val}(z)+k
.
The system of inequalities becomes

,

,

times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\sum\limits_{a=2k}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\sum\limits_{b=a}^{n-1-a}(n-a-b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\sum\limits_{a=2k}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\sum\limits_{b=1}^{n-2a}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\sum\limits_{a=2k}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(n-2a)(n-2a+1)}{2}.

The internal sum evaluates to the same quantity as appeared in the case

∎

4.14 Lemma.

The number of double cosets
[S(\vec{k})]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
with
{\rm val}(z)={\rm val}(xy)
and
{\rm val}(1+u)=0
is

q^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-4}{4}\right\rfloor}b_{q}\left(n-4\left\lfloor\frac{n-4}{4}\right\rfloor\right)-b_{q}(n)

Let
a={\rm val}(x)+k
and
b={\rm val}(y)
. Then the inequalities become

,
2k\leq a\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
,
2\leq k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\right\rfloor=:M
, and the number of double cosets is
q-2
times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\sum\limits_{a=2k}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}(n-2a+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{M}q^{k-2}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}+4n+4}{4}\right\rfloor-2(n+2)k+4k^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-8}{4}\rfloor}q^{m}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}-12n+36}{4}\right\rfloor+(16-2n)m+4m(m-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}-12n+36}{4}\right\rfloor+(16-2n)q\frac{d}{dq}+4q^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dq^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{q^{\lfloor\frac{n-4}{4}\rfloor}-1}{q-1}\right).

The remainder of the computation is tedious but elementary. ∎

4.15 Lemma.

The number of double cosets
[S(\vec{k})]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
with
{\rm val}(1+u)>0
is

q^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-6}{4}\right\rfloor}c_{q}\left(n-4\left\lfloor\frac{n-6}{4}\right\rfloor\right)-c_{q}(n)

where

c_{q}(n)=\frac{n-3}{6}\left\lfloor\frac{(n-2)(n-4)}{4}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{q-1}\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}-2n+2}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{4n+4}{(q-1)^{2}}+\frac{16}{(q-1)^{3}}.

Proof.

Let
m=k-2-{\rm val}(1+u)
,
a={\rm val}(x)+k
, and
b={\rm val}(y)
. The inequalities become

,

,

,

0\leq m\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-10}{4}\right\rfloor=:N
. Let

. The number of double cosets is

times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{N}q^{m}\sum\limits_{a=2m+5}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}(n-2a+1)(a-2m-4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{N}q^{m}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-8-4m}{2}\right\rfloor}a(n-5-4m-2(a+1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{N}q^{m}\left(\frac{n-7-4m}{6}\right)\left\lfloor\frac{(n-8-4m)(n-6-4m)}{4}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{N}q^{m}\left(B-\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}-18n+82}{2}\right\rfloor m+(2n-22)m(m-1)-\frac{8m(m-1)(m-2)}{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(B-\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}-18n+82}{2}\right\rfloor q\frac{d}{dq}+(2n-22)q^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dq^{2}}-\frac{8}{3}q^{3}\frac{d^{3}}{dq^{3}}\right)\left(\frac{q^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-6}{10}\right\rfloor}-1}{q-1}\right).

The rest of the computation is tedious but elementary.
∎

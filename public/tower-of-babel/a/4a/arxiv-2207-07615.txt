PLSS: A Projected Linear Systems Solver Thanks: Version of August 10, 2026.
Johannes J. Brust, Michael A. Saunders

Abstract
We propose iterative projection method s for solving square or rectangular consistent linear systems 𝐀𝐱 = 𝐛 \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b} . Existing projection methods use sketching matrices (possibly randomized) to generate a sequence of small projected subproblems, but even the smaller systems can be costly. We develop a process that appends one column to the sketching matrix each iteration and converges in a finite number of iterations whether the sketch is random or deterministic. In general, our process generates orthogonal updates to the approximate solution 𝐱 k \mathbf{x}_{k} . By choosing the sketch to be the set of all previous residuals, we obtain a simple recursive update and convergence in at most rank ​ ( 𝐀 ) \text{rank}(\mathbf{A}) iterations (in exact arithmetic). By choosing a sequence of identity columns for the sketch, we develop a generalization of the Kaczmarz method. In experiments on large sparse systems, our method (PLSS) with residual sketches is competitive with LSQR and LSMR, and with residual and identity sketches compares favorably with state-of-the-art randomized methods.

Introduction

Consider a general linear system

\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b},\qquad\mathbf{b}\in\textrm{range}(\mathbf{A}),

(1)

where
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
For computations with large matrices, randomized methods
[
6
,
7
,
16
]

aim to generate
inexpensive
(possibly low-accuracy) estimates of

\mathbf{x}
by solving smaller projected systems.
When the elements of
\mathbf{A}
are contaminated by errors or noise,
which
may be the
case in data-driven problems, highly
accurate solutions are not always requested. For large systems, randomized solvers are growing in popularity, although it is not uncommon
to observe slow convergence for naive implementations.
Reasons
for the widespread interest
may be the arguably intuitive approach of solving a sequence of
small projected systems instead of (
1
),
and the fact that randomization has emerged as an
enabling technology in data science
.
This article develops
a family of
projection methods that solve a sequence of smaller systems

and can have
significant advantages in terms of computation, accuracy, and convergence.

1.1
Notation

Integer
k\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}1}
represents the iteration index,
and vector
\mathbf{e}_{k}
denotes the
k^{\text{th}}
column of the identity matrix, with dimension
depending on the context. The
k^{\textnormal{th}}
row of
\mathbf{A}
is
\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\top}=\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
. For the
k^{\text{th}}
solution estimate
\mathbf{x}_{k}
, the residual vector is
\mathbf{r}_{k}:=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k}
, with associated vector
\mathbf{y}_{k}:=\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k}
.
Lower-case Greek letters represent scalars, and
the range of integers from
1
to
k
is written
1\!:\!k
.
To prove convergence we make use of the economy SVD,
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
.

1.2
Related work

To handle large problems, there has been a growing interest in sketching techniques. A
straightforward
approach is
random sketching
, which consists of selecting a random matrix

\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
with
r\ll m
and solving the reduced linear system

\mathbf{S}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{b}.

(2)

By the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma
[
8
]
, a solution to the reduced system (
2
) is related to a solution
of (
1
). However, unless
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{S})=\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{A})
, the solutions will not be the
same
.

Since (
2
) involves the product
\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
, previous work in
[
1
,
Cartis2021
,
10
]
has focused on the choice of
\mathbf{S}
to reduce the computational cost of this product.
In particular, with certain sketching matrices
\mathbf{S}
, the randomized Kaczmarcz
[
17
]
, randomized coordinate descent
[
11
]
, and stochastic
Newton
[
15
]
methods can be defined by (
2
).
An overview of randomized iterative methods for solving
linear systems is in
[
6
,
16
]
, which we consider to be state-of-the-art for the purpose of this
article.

1.3
Motivation

Given
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
let estimates of the solution to (
1
) be defined by the iterative process

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k}=\mathbf{x}_{k-1}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}},\quad\quad k=1,2,\ldots,}

(3)

where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called the
update
. It is important to compute the update
efficiently. Because sketching techniques aim to generate iterates with relatively low
computational complexity, we choose to solve a sequence of sketched systems. In particular,
we use a sequence of full-rank matrices

\mathbf{S}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}},\ k={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}1,2,}\dots,\qquad\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{S}_{k})={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}.

(4)

The matrices can be arbitrary as long as they have the specified dimensions and rank.
We assume that
\mathbf{S}_{k}
is in the range of
\mathbf{A}
, though this is not strictly necessary.
We show later that the
iterates
\mathbf{x}_{k}
for solving (
1
) converge in a finite number of steps.
We also demonstrate that a particular choice for
\mathbf{S}_{k}
results in very efficient
updates.

Part of our scheme is an additional

full-rank
parameter matrix
\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
that can
possibly improve the numerical behavior of the methods. (This matrix is not essential,
and all results hold when
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{I}_{n\times n}
.) Computations with
\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B}

are intended to be inexpensive, as they would be if it were a diagonal matrix. The sequence of
systems that define each update are of the form

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B}&\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k}\\
\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{0}_{k\times k}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\\
\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k-1})

(5)

with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times 1}
.
Since only
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}
is used to define the next iterates,
we do not compute
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}
.
When
\mathbf{S}_{k}
is in the range of
\mathbf{A}
, solving (
5
) is equivalent to the constrained least-squares problem

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\text{ arg min }}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}

(6)

subject to

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{A}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}+\mathbf{p})={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{b}.

(7)

Details of formulating problem (
6
)–(
7
) from (
5
) are in Appendix
A
.

Here we summarize that the solution
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}}
to (
6
)–(
7
) is defined by

\mathbf{W}:=(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}

and

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{k}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{k}})^{-1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}).

(8)

If
\mathbf{S}_{k}
is not in the range of
\mathbf{A}
, the inverse in (
8
)
is replaced by the pseudo-inverse.
For ease of notation we set
\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{I}
in the next sections (and hence
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
). Later we lift this assumption and describe updates with nontrivial
\mathbf{W}
.

Once
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}}
is obtained from (
8
), it can define the next iterate via (
3
).
Note that popular methods in
[
6
,
16
]
use an update like
(
8
); however, the matrix
\mathbf{S}\equiv\mathbf{S}_{k}
is then typically randomly generated, and

\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}
,
\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}
and
(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S})^{-1}

are typically recomputed each iteration. Thus, a small parameter
r>0
must be selected
such that a random
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
maintains low computational cost. Convergence is characterized by a rate
\rho\in[0,1)
that depends on the smallest singular value of a matrix defined by
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{A}
. Convergence implicitly depends on the choice of
r
and may result in prohibitively many iterations when
the rate
\rho
is close to unity.

1.4
Contributions

We prove that iteration (
3
)–(
8
) converges in
k_{\max}

steps (
1\leq k_{\max}\leq\min(m,n)
) when the sketch is in the range of
\mathbf{A}

or the system is underdetermined. Each sketch
\mathbf{S}_{k}
can be random
or deterministic, and can be augmented by one column each
iteration or recomputed from scratch. We show that the
finite-termination property holds for
m\geq n
and also
m<n
. By
selecting previous residuals to form each
\mathbf{S}_{k}
, we
develop an iteration with orthogonal updates and residuals. This
process is simple to implement and only stores and updates five
vectors. By selecting columns of the identity matrix for each
\mathbf{S}_{k}
, we
develop an update that generalizes the Kaczmarz method
[
9
]
. These
choices are two instances in our general class of methods
characterized by the choice of
\mathbf{S}_{k}
.

2
Method

Our method solves a sequence of sketched systems

\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k}=\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{b}
that exploit information generated at previous iterations. Suppose that one sketching column
\mathbf{s}_{k}
is generated each iteration and stored in the matrix

\mathbf{S}_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{s}_{1}&\mathbf{s}_{2}&\dots&\mathbf{s}_{k}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}.

(9)

Throughout,
we assume that
\mathbf{S}_{k}
has full column rank.

2.1
Orthogonality

When constraints (
7
) are satisfied, each update
has the property

\mathbf{0}=\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}})-\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{b}=-\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k}}.

(10)

Therefore from (
9
), by construction, previous sketching columns are orthogonal to the next residual (and hence linearly independent):

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{j}}\perp{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{i}}\textnormal{ for }{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}1}\leq j\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}i},1\leq i\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}
. In section
4
we show that the updates
\mathbf{p}_{k}
are also orthogonal.

2.2
Practical computations

We develop general techniques to compute
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}
(
8
)
efficiently.
First, we describe a method based on updating a QR factorization.

Second
, we deduce an
alternative
method
(based on updating a triangular factorization) to avoid recomputing
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{A}
and
({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{k}})^{-1}
.
However, since both of these methods, for general sketches, have memory requirements
that grow with
k
, we
additionally
show
in Section
4
that
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
satisfies
a short recursion defined by
\mathbf{p}_{k-1}
and another vector
when the sketch is chosen judiciously
.

In order to avoid recomputing the potentially expensive product
\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
,
let
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{y}_{k}}}:=\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}}
and define

{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}:=[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\>\mathbf{y}_{1}\>\mathbf{y}_{2}\>\dots\>\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}]
to collect the previous
\mathbf{y}
’s. Then

(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{\top}=\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{S}}_{k-1}&\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{y}_{k}}}\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf{Y}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}.

(11)

2.3
QR factorization

In general it is numerically safer to update factors of a matrix rather than its inverse (because factors exist even when the matrix is singular). QR
factors of
\mathbf{Y}_{k}
can be used to this effect. Specifically, let the QR factorization be

\mathbf{Y}_{k}=\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{T}_{k},

(12)

where
\mathbf{Q}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
is orthonormal and
\mathbf{T}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
is upper triangular.
Note that the update
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
from (
8
) with
\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\equiv{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\top}}\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}

simplifies to

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\mathbf{Y}_{k}(\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{k})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}=\frac{\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}r_{kk}}}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}=\frac{\rho_{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}r_{kk}}}}\mathbf{q}_{k},

(13)

where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}r_{kk}}}
is the final diagonal element in
\mathbf{T}_{k}
. Householder reflectors
\mathbf{H}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
can be used to represent

\mathbf{Q}_{k}={\mathbf{H}}_{1}~{\mathbf{H}}_{2}~\cdots~{\mathbf{H}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
in factored form with essentially the same storage as
\mathbf{Y}_{k}

[
5
]
.
Computing

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
in (
13
) requires one product with the factored form of
\mathbf{Q}_{k}
. In particular,
\mathbf{q}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
and
r_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}

are obtained by

\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathbf{q}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}(\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}})=\mathbf{q}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=r_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}.

We note that another option to develop the QR factorization (
12
) is to append
{\mathbf{y}}_{k}
to

{\mathbf{T}}_{k-1}
and apply a sequence of plane rotations to eliminate the nonzeros in
({\mathbf{y}}_{k})_{k+1:m}

to define
{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}
and
{\mathbf{T}}_{k}
.
Both QR strategies use storage that grow with
k
.

2.4
Triangular factorization

In another approach, we update the inverse of
\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{k}
in
eq.

8
.
This is based on storing and updating the previous inverse in order to obtain the next.
Concretely, suppose we store

{\mathbf{N}}_{k-1}=({\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1})^{-1}=(\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k-1})^{-1}

Theorem 1
.

Assume

\mathbf{N}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{N}}_{k-1}+\frac{1}{\delta_{k}}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}^{\top}&\frac{-1}{\delta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}\\

where

\mathbf{D}_{k}:=\textrm{diag}(1/\delta_{j})_{j=1,\dots,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}-\mathbf{Y}_{k-1}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}-\mathbf{Y}_{k-1}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-1}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-1}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-1}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\right).

Proof.

Substituting (
16
) into (
8
) with the definition
from (
11
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{Y}_{k-1}\mathbf{N}_{k-1}+\frac{1}{\delta_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}}\mathbf{Y}_{k-1}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}^{\top}-\frac{1}{\delta_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}}\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{S}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}

(18)

\displaystyle+\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}-\mathbf{Y}_{k-1}\widehat{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}}_{k}\right),

where
\mathbf{S}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}=\mathbf{0}
by the orthogonality
property.

\square

Formula (
17
) implies that we do not have to compute
\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{k}

explicitly, nor do solves with it. Instead, the factors

\mathbf{R}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}
and
\mathbf{D}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}
can be updated
one column per iteration
by products with triangular
matrices only.

For instance,
\mathbf{R}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}
is obtained by computing
{\mathbf{t}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
( suppressing superscripts) and appending:
\mathbf{R}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}=[\>{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}\quad{\mathbf{t}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\>]
.
(Since
{\mathbf{t}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}=[\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k-1}^{\top}\>{-1}\>\mathbf{0}^{\top}]^{\top}
, it is obtained from
2 multiplications with triangular matrices only:
\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k-1}={\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}({\mathbf{D}}_{k-2}({\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}})))
.)

However, (
17
) still needs
{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}
,
{\mathbf{R}}_{k-1}
and
{\mathbf{D}}_{k-1}
, which all grow with
k
.

2.5
Orthogonality of
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}

It is valuable to note from (
17
) that

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\left(\mathbf{I}-{\mathbf{Y}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}({\mathbf{Y}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}})^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}},

so that
{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\mathbf{0}
.
Defining

{\mathbf{P}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}:=\begin{bmatrix}~{\mathbf{p}}_{1}&\dots&{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}~\end{bmatrix}

and noting that

{\mathbf{p}}_{i}\in\textnormal{span}({\mathbf{Y}}_{i})
for

i=1\!:\!k-1
, we see that

{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\mathbf{0}.

(19)

That is, the updates generated by our
class of methods
are orthogonal. We also define the
squared lengths
\theta_{i}=\|{\mathbf{p}}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}

(
i=1\!:\!{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}
) and the diagonal matrix

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{k-1}:={\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\text{diag}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k-1}).

2.6
Linear combination of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}

The methods in Sections
2.4
and
2.3
use memory that grows with
k
.
By further unwinding recursive relations in (
17
),
described in Appendix
B
, we can represent the update as a linear combination
of previous updates.
This enables us to derive in Section
4

a short recursion defined in terms of
{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}
and
{\mathbf{y}}_{k}
only
. For some scalars

\alpha_{j}
, the dependencies become

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\alpha_{j}{\mathbf{p}}_{j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\big).

(20)

Representation (
20
) implies that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}=\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\mathbf{g}_{k-1}+\gamma_{k-1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{y}_{k}}
for
some vector
\mathbf{g}_{k-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
and scalar
\gamma_{k-1}
. This leads to a computationally
efficient formula for
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
.

3
Convergence

We first show finite termination for iterates generated by (
3
) and (
8
) using a sequence
\{\mathbf{S}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}i}}\}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}i=1}}^{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}
in the range of

\mathbf{A}
.
This ensures that update (
8
) is well defined in terms of the inverse.
Denote by
(\cdot)^{-1}
the inverse for square matrices
and by
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
the pseudo-inverse for rectangular matrices.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
m\geq n
and
\mathbf{A}
has full column rank.
Given
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider the sequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}\}
computed by (
3
) and
(
8
),
where the inverse in (
8
) is well defined
. Also assume that after
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}n}
iterations,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{n}}
has full rank and is in the range of
\mathbf{A}
.
Then,
\mathbf{x}_{n}
solves (
1
).

Proof.

After
k={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}n}
iterations,
\mathbf{S}_{k}
is by assumption a rank-
n
matrix in the range of
\mathbf{A}
, so that
\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
is a nonsingular square matrix.
Hence,
\left(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k}\right)^{-1}=(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k})^{-1}\mathbf{W}^{-1}(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{-1}
. Let
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}:=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}
, so that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k}\left(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}=(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}.

Let
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
be the economy SVD of
\mathbf{A}
.
As
\mathbf{S}_{k}
is in the range of
\mathbf{A}
, it can be represented by
\mathbf{S}_{k}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{T}_{k}
for some nonsingular
\mathbf{T}_{k}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{T}_{k}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}=\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{n}=\mathbf{x}_{n-1}+\mathbf{p}_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}_{n-1}+\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{n-1}})=\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}.

We conclude that
\mathbf{x}_{n}
is the least squares solution of (
1
)

when
m>n
, and the unique solution when
m=n
.

\square

When not every member of
\{\mathbf{S}_{k}\}
is in the range of
\mathbf{A}
, Corollary
4
shows convergence in at most
m
iterations.

Corollary 4
.

Suppose we use the same iterative process
from
Theorem
3
except that
each matrix in the sequence
\{\mathbf{S}_{k}\}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k=1}}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m-1}}
has full rank only
(and is not necessarily in the range of
\mathbf{A}
),
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{m}}

is a square nonsingular matrix,
and (
8
) is defined by the pseudo-inverse
. Then
either
\mathbf{x}_{m}
or
\mathbf{x}_{l}
,
n\leq l<m
, is a
solution of (
1
).

Proof.

At iteration
k={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m}
, the matrix
\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
does not have full rank. Thus the update
\mathbf{p}_{k}
in (
8
) is defined by the pseudo-inverse

(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k})^{\dagger}
.
Since

\mathbf{S}_{k}
is square and
nonsingular,

\mathbf{S}_{k}(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k})^{\dagger}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}=(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top})^{\dagger}.

Let
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}:=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}}
so that

\mathbf{p}_{k}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top})^{\dagger}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}=\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}
.
Then

\mathbf{x}_{m}=\mathbf{x}_{m-1}+\mathbf{p}_{m}=\mathbf{x}_{m-1}+\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{m-1}})=\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}.

At an earlier iteration

n\leq k=l<{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m}
, if
{\mathbf{S}}_{k}
can be
partitioned by a square nonsingular matrix
{\mathbf{T}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

and a matrix
\mathbf{U}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}-n)}
in the nullspace of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{A}^{\top}}
(i.e.,
\mathbf{U}^{\top}_{\perp}\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{0}
where

\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
) so that

{\mathbf{S}}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{U}{\mathbf{T}}_{k}&\mathbf{U}_{\perp}\end{bmatrix},

then
{\mathbf{x}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}l}}
is a solution to (
1
). In particular,

({\mathbf{S}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k})^{\dagger}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{T}_{k}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{V}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{T}_{k}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}.

The update is then

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{m}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}},

and hence
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}}
will be a least-squares solution of (
1
).

\square

Corollary
4
further implies that the process in (
3
) and
(
8
), with appropriate sketching matrices,
also finds a solution when
\mathbf{A}
is underdetermined.

Corollary 5
.

If
m<n
so that
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is underdetermined,
and
\{\mathbf{S}_{k}\}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k=1}}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m-1}}
is a sequence of full-rank matrices with

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{m}}
nonsingular, then
\mathbf{x}_{m}
solves (
1
).

Proof.

At iteration
k={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m}
, matrix
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{m}}
is square and nonsingular so that
the update is given by ( as in Corollary
4
)

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top})^{\dagger}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}},\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{m}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}_{m-1}+\mathbf{p}_{m})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{m-1}+\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top})^{\dagger}\mathbf{r}_{m-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{m-1}+(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{m-1}),

and we conclude that
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{m}=\mathbf{b}
and that
\mathbf{x}_{m}
solves (
1
).

\square

4
PLSS residuals

By storing a history of residuals in the sketching matrix, we construct an iteration
with orthogonal residuals and updates
. Recall that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k}:=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k}}

is the residual at iteration
k
(where since
\mathbf{b}
is in the range of
\mathbf{A}
,
all residuals are, too). Suppose the previous residuals have been stored, i.e.,
\mathbf{s}_{i}=\mathbf{r}_{i-1}
(
1\leq i\leq k
) so that the history of all residuals is in the matrix

\mathbf{S}_{k}:=\left[\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{0}\quad\mathbf{r}_{1}\quad\cdots\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}\ \right]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}.

(21)

Since
\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k}=\mathbf{0}
, all residuals are orthogonal with this choice of sketch. Moreover, since all residuals are in the
range of
\mathbf{A}
, by Theorem
3
, iteration (
3
) converges in at most
\text{min}(m,n)
iterations
in exact arithmetic. Defining the scalar
\rho_{i}=\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}_{2}
(
0\leq i\leq k-1
) we develop a 1-step
recursive update.

Theorem 6
.

The update
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
from (
8
) with
\mathbf{S}_{k}
from (
21
)
can be computed by the 1-step recursive formula

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}{\mathbf{p}}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}+\gamma_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}},

(22)

\displaystyle\beta_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\big(\frac{\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|\|\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\|}{\|{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}\|\|{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}\|}-1\big)\big(\frac{\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|\|\mathbf{y}_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\|}{\|{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}\|\|{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}\|}+1\big)},

(23)

\displaystyle\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\|{\mathbf{y}}_{k}\|^{2}\big(1-\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|}{\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|\|{\mathbf{y}}_{k}\|}\big)\big(1+\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|}{\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|\|{\mathbf{y}}_{k}\|}\big)}.

(24)

Proof.

From (
20
),
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
can be represented as a linear combination
of the columns in
{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}
and
\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
. Thus for a vector

{\mathbf{g}}_{k-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{k-1}

and scalar
\gamma_{k-1}
we have

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}={\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}{\mathbf{g}}_{k-1}+\gamma_{k-1}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}.

(25)

Since
{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\mathbf{0}
, by multiplying
(
25
) left and right by
{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}

and solving with the diagonal
{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
we obtain

\mathbf{g}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}=-\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}^{-1}{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
.
To simplify
{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
, recall that
\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}r}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
and
\mathbf{x}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}={\mathbf{x}}_{k-2}+{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}
, so that

\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}={\mathbf{r}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-2}}-\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{p}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}.

(26)

Because residuals are orthogonal, multiplying (
26
) on the left by

\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}
gives

\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}=\rho_{k-2}{\mathbf{e}}_{k-1}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\rho_{k-1}=-\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}.

From (
26
) we also have

\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}={\mathbf{r}}_{k-3}-\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-2}-\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1},\quad\rho_{k-1}{\mathbf{e}}_{k}=\rho_{k-3}{\mathbf{e}}_{k-2}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-2}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1},

and
\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}=-\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
implies that

\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-2}=0
. Similarly,
\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-i}=0
for

i=3\!:\!k-1
. Therefore,
{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=-\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\mathbf{e}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
and

{\mathbf{g}}_{k-1}=(\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}/\theta_{k-1})\mathbf{e}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
.
From (
20
),

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\frac{\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}{\theta_{k-1}}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}+\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}},

(27)

and from (
8
) we have
\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}=\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
. Thus, multiplying
(

) by
\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}
gives

\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}=-\frac{\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}^{2}}{\theta_{k-1}}+\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\|^{2}.

Solving the previous expression for
\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}
we find

\displaystyle\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{k-1}\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}{\theta_{k-1}\|\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\|^{2}-\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|^{2}\big(\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\|}{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|}-\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|}{\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|}\big)\big(\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\|}{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|}+\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|}{\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|}\big)},

\displaystyle\beta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}}{\theta_{k-1}}\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}=\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|^{4}}{(\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|\|{\mathbf{y}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\|-\|{\mathbf{r}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|^{2})(\|{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}\|\|{\mathbf{y}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\|+\|{\mathbf{r}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\|^{2})}.

Therefore we specify
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\beta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}+\gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
.

\square

4.1
\mathbf{W}
not the identity

Lifting the assumption from Section

, suppose that

\mathbf{W}=(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}
. In this case, we may represent it in triangular factorized form as
\mathbf{W}={\mathbf{R}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{\textnormal{W}}

(because
\mathbf{W}
is symmetric and nonsingular). Further, define the transformed quantities

{{\mathbf{x}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}:={\mathbf{R}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{-\top}\mathbf{x}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}},\quad{{\mathbf{p}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k}}:={\mathbf{R}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{-\top}\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}},\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathbf{A}}_{\textnormal{W}}:=\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{R}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{\top}.

Then update (
8
) becomes

{{\mathbf{p}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k}}={\mathbf{A}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k}(\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}_{\textnormal{W}}{\mathbf{A}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}.

As for
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
, there is a recursion

{{\mathbf{p}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k}}={{\beta}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}{{\mathbf{p}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}+{{\gamma}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}{{\mathbf{y}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k}}

(28)

with certain scalars
{{\beta}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}
and
{{\gamma}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}
, where

{{\mathbf{y}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k}}:={\mathbf{A}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}
.
Because
{{\mathbf{p}}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}={\mathbf{R}}_{\textnormal{W}}^{-\top}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}
, we find from (
28
),
by rewriting variables in terms of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}
and
\mathbf{y}_{k}
, that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}={{\beta}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}+{{\gamma}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{y}_{k},

(29)

where

\displaystyle{{\beta}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{({\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{-1}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1})({\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{W}{\mathbf{y}}_{k})}{\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}}}-1\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{({\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{-1}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1})({\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{W}{\mathbf{y}}_{k})}{\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}}}+1\bigg)},

\displaystyle{{\gamma}^{\textnormal{W}}_{k-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{({\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{W}{\mathbf{y}}_{k})^{-1}}{\bigg(1-\sqrt{\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}}{({\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{-1}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1})({\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{W}{\mathbf{y}}_{k})}}\bigg)\bigg(1+\sqrt{\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}}{({\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{-1}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1})({\mathbf{y}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{W}{\mathbf{y}}_{k})}}\bigg)}.

Note that (
29
) can be evaluated efficiently as long as products
and solves with
\mathbf{W}
are inexpensive. Therefore, we typically use the identity or a diagonal matrix that
normalizes the columns of
\mathbf{A}
, namely

\mathbf{W}=\text{diag}\big(\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{A}_{:,1}\|},\dots,\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{A}_{:,n}\|}\big)
.

5
Algorithm

Because of the short recursive update formula, our method can be implemented efficiently as in Algorithm
1
.
The parameter matrix
\mathbf{B}
, where
\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{W}^{-1}
and
(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}=\mathbf{W}
, is optional.

Algorithm 1

PLSS
(Projected Linear Systems Solver)

0:

\mathtt{A\in(m\times n),~x_{0},~b}
,
\mathtt{0<maxIt}
, (
\mathtt{Optional:B}^{\top}\mathtt{B\in(n\times n))}

1:

if

\mathtt{B}^{\top}\mathtt{B}\neq\mathtt{Empty}

then

2:

\mathtt{WI=\mathtt{B}^{\top}\mathtt{B}};

3:

else

4:

\mathtt{WI=I;}~\mathtt{\%~Identity~size~(n\times n)}

5:

end

if

6:

\mathtt{\%~Initialization}

7:

\mathtt{x_{k}=x_{0};}

\phantom{\mathtt{WI}}\mathtt{~r_{k}=b-A*x_{k};}

\mathtt{~y_{k}=A^{\top}*r_{k};}

\mathtt{\rho_{k}=r_{k}^{\top}*r_{k};}

\mathtt{~WIy_{k}=WI\backslash y_{k};}

\mathtt{~\phi_{k}=y_{k}^{\top}*WIy_{k};}

8:

\mathtt{p_{k}=(\rho_{k}/\phi_{k}).*WIy_{k};}

9:

\mathtt{\theta_{k}=p_{k}^{\top}(WI*p_{k});}

10:

\mathtt{x_{k}=x_{k}+p_{k};}

11:

while

\mathtt{k<maxIt}

do

12:

\mathtt{k=k+1;}

13:

\mathtt{r_{k}=r_{k}-A*p_{k};}

14:

\mathtt{y_{k}=A^{\top}*r_{k};}

15:

\mathtt{\rho_{k}=r_{k}^{\top}*r_{k};}

16:

\mathtt{WIy_{k}=WI\backslash y_{k};}

17:

\mathtt{\phi_{k}=y_{k}^{\top}*Wiy_{k};}

18:

\mathtt{\beta_{k}=1/((sqrt(\theta_{k}*\phi_{k})/\rho_{k}-1)*(sqrt(\theta_{k}*\phi_{k})/\rho_{k}+1));}

19:

\mathtt{\gamma_{k}=(\theta_{k}/\rho_{k})*\beta_{k};}

20:

\mathtt{p_{k}=\beta_{k}*p_{k}+\gamma_{k}*WIy_{k};}

21:

\mathtt{\theta_{k}=p_{k}^{\top}(WI*p_{k});}

22:

\mathtt{x_{k}=x_{k}+p_{k};}

23:

end

while

We emphasize that Algorithm
1
updates only four vectors

\mathtt{r_{k},~y_{k}}
,
\mathtt{p_{k}}
and
\mathtt{x_{k}}
(and uses one intermediate vector
\mathtt{WIy_{k}}
).
When the matrix
\mathtt{WI}
is diagonal (which is typically the case), we always apply
it element-wise to a vector (i.e.,
\mathtt{WI~.*~p_{k}}
or
\mathtt{WI~.\backslash~y_{k}}
).
The algorithm uses one multiplication with
\mathtt{A}
and one with
\mathtt{A}^{\top}
per iteration.
For reference, we note that LSQR’s
[
13
, Table 1]
dominant
number of multiplications for large sparse
\mathbf{A}
at each iteration is
O(3m+5n)

(assuming
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{A}^{{}^{\top}}\mathbf{u}
cost
O(m)
and
O(n)
multiplications).
When
WI
is the identity then Algorithm
1
incurs
O(2m+5n)
multiplications.
For practical implementation one may wish to change the stopping condition in the loop.
For instance, with consistent linear systems (where
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
can be solved exactly),
the condition
\mathtt{sqrt(\rho_{k})/norm(b)<\tau}
for a tolerance
\mathtt{\tau>0}
may be used
to stop the iterations.

6
Relation to Craig’s method

The orthogonal residuals in our method are reminiscent of Craig’s method
[
12
,
13
]
.
Indeed when
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
, the updates
{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}
from (
8
) or (
22
)
with
\mathbf{S}_{k}
from (
21
) correspond to the updates in Craig’s method. For background, Craig’s method can be developed using the Golub-Kahan
bidiagonalization procedure as described in
[
13
, Secs. 3 & 7.2]
.
With
\beta_{1}\mathbf{u}_{1}=\mathbf{b}
,
\beta_{1}\equiv\|\mathbf{b}\|
, the bidiagonalization
generates orthonormal
matrices
\mathbf{U}_{k}
and
\mathbf{V}_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
and a lower bidiagonal
matrix
\mathbf{L}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
with diagonals
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}

and subdiagonals
\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{k}
.
After
k
iterations, the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{V}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{k}\mathbf{L}_{k}+\beta_{k+1}\mathbf{u}_{k+1}\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top},

(30)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{L}_{k}^{\top}.

(31)

With
\mathbf{z}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
defined by

\mathbf{L}_{k}\mathbf{z}_{k}=\beta_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1}
and
\zeta_{k}
denoting the
k^{\text{th}}
element of
\mathbf{z}_{k}
, the iterates in Craig’s method are computed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{z}_{k}={\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}+\zeta_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k},

(32)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k}=-\zeta_{k}\beta_{k+1}\mathbf{u}_{k+1}.

(33)

We prove that iterates generated by Craig’s method are equivalent to (
8
) with
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}

and using residuals in a sketch.

Theorem 7
.

The updates
\zeta_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}\equiv{\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}
in Craig’s method are equal to the
updates
{\mathbf{p}}_{k}
in (
8
) or (
22
) with
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{S}_{k}
in (
21
).

Proof.

First note that the residual in Craig’s method is

\mathbf{r}_{k}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}({\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}+\zeta_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k})={\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}-\zeta_{k}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_{k},

and also
\mathbf{r}_{k}=-\zeta_{k}\beta_{k+1}\mathbf{u}_{k+1}
from (
33
). By orthogonality of
\mathbf{U}_{k}
it holds that

\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k}=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}(\zeta_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k})=\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}.

(34)

We define the update as
\zeta_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}\equiv{\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}
. The scalar
\zeta_{k}

is defined recursively as
\zeta_{k}=-\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\zeta_{k-1}
(with
\zeta_{0}\equiv-1
, cf.
[
13
, Sec. 7.2]
). From (
30
)–(
31
)
we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}^{\top}(-\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}^{\top}(-\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k})

\displaystyle=-\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{L}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
\beta_{k}\\
\alpha_{k}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=-\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}\alpha_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}-\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
\beta_{k}\\
0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}^{2}{\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}-\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}(\mathbf{L}_{k}^{\top}-\mathbf{L}_{k})\mathbf{e}_{k}.

Using

\phi_{k}\equiv-\alpha_{k}^{2}/(\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k})
and a vector
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we have

\frac{-\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}}{\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}}=\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{k}=\frac{-\alpha_{k}^{2}}{\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}}{\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}+\mathbf{V}_{k}(\mathbf{L}_{k}^{\top}-\mathbf{L}_{k})\mathbf{e}_{k}\equiv\phi_{k}{\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}+\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{k}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}.

(35)

Combining the second equality in (
34
) with (
35
) gives the system

\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{k}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{k}\\
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}\\
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{k}\\
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}\end{bmatrix},

which leads to

\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{k})^{-1}\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}={\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}.

(36)

With
{\mathbf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
as defined in (
21
), matrices
\mathbf{U}_{k}
and
{\mathbf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}

are closely related:

\mathbf{U}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{r}}_{0}&{\mathbf{r}}_{1}&\cdots&{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{D}_{k}={\mathbf{S}}_{k}\mathbf{D}_{k},\quad\mathbf{D}_{k}\equiv-\text{diag}(\zeta_{0}\beta_{1},\zeta_{1}\beta_{2},\ldots,\zeta_{k-1}\beta_{k}).

Substituting
\mathbf{U}_{k}={\mathbf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}\mathbf{D}_{k}
into (
36
), we obtain

\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}({\mathbf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}{\mathbf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}})^{-1}{\mathbf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}^{\top}{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}={\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\textnormal{C}}.

(37)

Comparing (
37
) with (
8
) we see that the updates are the same
when
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
.

\square

7
PLSS Kaczmarz

Another way to construct the sketching matrix is to concatenate columns of the identity matrix
\mathbf{e}_{k}
in the sketch
\mathbf{S}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{e}}_{1}&{\mathbf{e}}_{2}&\cdots&{\mathbf{e}}_{k}\end{bmatrix}
. When the columns are assembled in a random
order, this process generalizes the randomized Kaczmarz iteration
[
9
,
17
]
. We denote
a random identity column by
\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}
. From
\mathbf{S}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}=\mathbf{0}
we have
\mathbf{S}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}=r_{i_{k}}^{(k-1)}\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}^{\top}
, where
r_{i_{k}}^{(k-1)}
is the

{i_{k}}^{\text{th}}
element of
{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}
. Thus the updates with this sketch are (cf. (
8
))

{\mathbf{p}}_{k}=r_{i_{k}}^{(k-1)}\mathbf{Y}_{k}(\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{k})^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}.

In the notation of Sections
2.5
and
2.6
, the updates are orthogonal and can be
represented by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}=\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\mathbf{g}_{k-1}+\gamma_{k-1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{y}_{k}}.

Since
{\mathbf{y}}_{k}=\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}={\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}
(the
{i_{k}}^{\textnormal{th}}
row of
\mathbf{A}
) and
from
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{y}_{k}^{\top}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{k}}=r_{i_{k}}^{(k-1)}=\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}^{\top}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k-1})
, we develop the update

\displaystyle{\mathbf{p}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{k-1}({\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}-{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}^{-1}_{k-1}{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}),

(38)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}^{\top}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k-1})}{(\|{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}\|_{2}-\|{\mathbf{d}}_{k-1}\|_{2})(\|{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}\|_{2}+\|{\mathbf{d}}_{k-1}\|_{2})},

(39)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{d}}_{k-1}

\displaystyle\equiv{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}^{-1/2}_{k-1}{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}^{\top}{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}.

(40)

If the history of previous updates is not used, i.e.,
{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}=\mathbf{0}
,
then
{\mathbf{p}}_{k}
is equal to the update in the Kaczmarz method. The Kaczmarz method is useful,
especially in situations where the entire matrix is not accessible (possibly because of its size), because it enables versions that access only one row of
\mathbf{A}
each iteration. Note that update (
38
) can be implemented with one multiplication of

{\mathbf{P}}_{k-1}
and one
\mathbf{A}
. To compute the next update using

{\mathbf{r}}_{k}={\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}-\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{p}}_{k}
and

\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}^{\top}={\mathbf{p}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{i_{k}}
,
we compute
\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{p}}_{k}

and append it to an array that stores

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{p}}_{1}&\cdots\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{p}}_{k}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}={\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}
.
With this,
{\mathbf{p}}_{k+1}
can be computed by selecting the
(k+1)^{\textnormal{th}}
column of
{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}
to obtain
{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\top}
, then
multiplying
{\mathbf{P}}_{k}({\boldsymbol{\Theta}}^{-1/2}_{k}\mathbf{d}_{k})
and forming

\mathbf{p}_{k+1}=\gamma_{k}({\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\top}-{\mathbf{P}}_{k}({\boldsymbol{\Theta}}^{-1/2}_{k}\mathbf{d}_{k}))
.

9
Conclusions

We develop an iterative projection method for solving consistent rectangular or square systems. Our method is based on appending one column each iteration to the sketching matrix. For full-rank sketches (common in practice) we prove that the underlying process terminates, with exact arithmetic, in a
finite number of iterations. When the sketching matrix
stores the history of all previous residuals, we develop a method with
orthogonal residuals and updates. We include a parameter matrix that can be used to improve computations. Importantly, we derive a short recursive formula that is simple to implement, and an algorithm that updates only four vectors. In numerical experiments, including large sparse systems, our methods compare favorably to widely known methods (
LSQR
,
LSMR
or
CRAIG
) and to some existing randomized methods.

Appendix A
Optimality

Solving linear system (
5
) is equivalent to the constrained optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}-\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}

(41)

subject to

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{A}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}+\mathbf{p})={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{b}.

(42)

When
\mathbf{S}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
is in the range of
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
, it can be represented as
\mathbf{S}_{k}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{T}_{k}
using a
nonsingular matrix
\mathbf{T}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
. Thus constraint (
42
) implies

{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{k-1}+\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{b}\quad\text{ or }\quad\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1,}

with
\mathbf{r}_{k-1}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}
. Since
\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}=\|\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}
is constant with respect to
\mathbf{p}
, problem
(
41
)–(
42
) is equivalently represented by

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}

subject to

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{A}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}+\mathbf{p})={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k}}\mathbf{b}.

Appendix B
Linear combination of updates

We derive a representation of
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
as a linear combination
of previous updates
{\mathbf{p}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}},\ldots,{\mathbf{p}}_{1}
. Recall from (
17
)
that

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}-\mathbf{Y}_{k-1}\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k}\right),

where

\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k}={\mathbf{R}}_{k-1}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-1}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-1}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}^{\top}&\\
&0\end{bmatrix}+\frac{{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}{\mathbf{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k-1}}^{\top}}{\delta_{k-1}}\bigg){\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}

(with superscript indices suppressed).
From this we deduce

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}+\frac{{\mathbf{t}}_{k-1}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}})}{\delta_{k-2}}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}{\mathbf{t}}_{k-1}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}+\frac{{\mathbf{t}}_{k-1}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}})}{\delta_{k-1}}({\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k-1}-{\mathbf{y}}_{k-1})

\displaystyle={\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-2}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}-\frac{({\mathbf{t}}_{k-1}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}))}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-2}}}{\mathbf{p}}_{k-1}.

Now for
j=1\!:\!k-1
, define scalars

\alpha_{j}={\mathbf{t}}_{j}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}})/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\mathbf{s}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{j-1})}
,
so that writing
{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-2}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-2}

in terms of
{\mathbf{R}}_{k-3}{\mathbf{D}}_{k-3}{\mathbf{R}}_{k-3}
and
{\mathbf{t}}_{k-2}
(and recursively backwards) gives

{\mathbf{Y}}_{k-1}\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k}=-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\alpha_{j}{\mathbf{p}}_{j}.

Hence the update
\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
can be represented by previous updates and the vector
\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}
:

\mathbf{p}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k}}-\mathbf{Y}_{k-1}\widehat{{\mathbf{t}}}_{k}\right)=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{s}_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}}}{\delta_{k}}\big({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{y}_{k}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\alpha_{j}{\mathbf{p}}_{j}\big).

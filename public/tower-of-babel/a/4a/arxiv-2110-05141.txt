Manin triples and non-degenerate anti-symmetric bilinear forms on Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 2 Thanks: The second author was supported by the grant NYUAD-065.
Saïd Benayadi, Sofiane Bouarroudj

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce and develop the notion of a Manin triple for a Lie superalgebra 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} defined over a field of characteristic p = 2 p=2 . We find cohomological necessary conditions for the pair ( 𝔤 , 𝔤 ∗ ) ({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}) to form a Manin triple. We introduce the concept of Lie bi-superalgebras for p = 2 p=2 and establish a link between Manin triples and Lie bi-superalgebras. In particular, we study Manin triples defined by a classical r r -matrix with an extra condition (called an admissible classical r r -matrix). A particular case is examined where 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} has an even invariant non-degenerate bilinear form. In this case, admissible r r -matrices can be obtained inductively through the process of double extensions. In addition, we introduce the notion of double extensions of Manin triples, and show how to get a new Manin triple from an existing one. Keywords. Manin triple; admissible classical r r -matrices; modular Lie superalgebra; characteristic 2 2 ; left-alternating; left-symmetric



1.
Introduction

Hereafter,
\mathbb{K}
is an arbitrary field of characteristic
p
. We will specify when
\mathbb{K}
is required to be algebraically closed. For a list of simple Lie superalgebras and their “relatives” we use in this paper, see
[
BGL
,
BGLLS

.

1.1.
Manin triples for Lie (super)algebras

Drinfeld
[
D
]
proved that a complex Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is also a Lie bialgebra if and only if it constitutes, together with its dual
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, a Manin triple
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
, i.e.,
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
as vector spaces,
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Lie algebra having a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\mathscr{B}
(NIS for short) such that
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
are isotropic with respect to
\mathscr{B}
. A superization of Drinfeld’s result is due to Olshansky
[
O
]
. Further, Leites and Shapovalov
[
LSh
]
listed, à la Olshlansky, important examples of simple (and close to simple) Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{h}}
of finite dimension and of polynomial growth such that
{\mathfrak{h}}
splits into Manin-Olshansky triple.

In this paper we construct matched pairs as well as Manin triples for Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
. Our construction is different from the case where
p\not=2
because of the squaring, which was equivalent to the bracket for
p\not=2
but plays an important role now, and introduce several completely new concepts.

1.2.
r
-Matrices on (super)algebras

Belavin and Drinfeld
[
BD1
,
BD2
]
classified
r
-matrices (that describe quasi- triangular Lie bialgebra structures). Their classification uses the notion of admissible triples that satisfy certain relations. A superization of
[
BD1
,
BD2
]
is due to Leites and Serganova
[
LS
]
with corrections in
[
Lyb
]
and by Karaali
[
Ka1
,
Ka2
]
who used similar constructions using ideas of Belavin and Drinfeld for Lie superalgebras with a NIS. Karaali constructed several examples of
r
-matrices on simple Lie superalgebras; however, the classification is not reached yet since certain non-degenerate
r
-matrices do not fit such a description.

For Lie algebras, Michaelis
[
M
]
found a condition under which the structure of a triangular, coboundary Lie
bialgebra
exists on any Lie algebra containing linearly independent elements
a
and
b
satisfying
[a,b]=kb
for some non-zero
k\in\mathbb{K}
. This method gives the structure of a Lie bialgebra on the Virasoro algebra (with or without a central element) and on many other Lie algebras.

Feldvoss in
[
F1
]
showed that for every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over
an algebraically closed
field of characteristic
p\not=2
, the following are equivalent:

(i) the algebra is either abelian or the three dimensional Heisenberg algebra;

(ii) it does not admits any non-trivial triangular Lie bialgebra structure;

(iii) it does not admit any non-trivial quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra structure.

However, if the characteristic is equal to 2, then any non-abelian Lie algebra admits a non-trivial quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra structure.

A further generalization to an arbitrary field was carried out by Feldvoss himself in
[
F2
]
.

De Smedt (
[
Sm
]
) in 1994 (rediscovered in
[
F2
]
) showed that every finite-dimensional non-abelian Lie algebra over the real or complex numbers admits a non-trivial coboundary Lie bialgebra structure.

For Lie superalgebras over a field of characteristic
2
, we define the notion of Lie bi-superalgebra and show that this notion is equivalent to the notion of a Manin triple (see §
3.4
). In the case of coboundary Lie bi-superalgebras, the associated classical
r
-matrices must have an extra condition, see Eq. (
29
) in Theorem
3.4.8
. We call such classical
r
-matrices
admissible
. In the case where the Lie superalgebra has an even NIS, we show that the existence of an admissible
r
-matrix is equivalent to the existence of an admissible Yang-Baxter operator, see Prop.
3.4.19
. This operator is used to study the structure of Lie bi-superalgebra associated with the admissible classical
r
-matrix. Due to the distinguish role of the squaring on the Lie superalgebra in characteristic
2
, all these notions require special approaches as compared to their analogs in other characteristics.

1.3.
Double extensions (DEs) of Lie (super)algebras with a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form (NIS)

Medina and Revoy in
[
MR1
]
introduced the notion of
double extensions
for Lie algebras with NIS in characteristic zero by means of a 1-dimensional space on a simple Lie algebra. They showed that if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is such an irreducible non-simple Lie algebra (of
\dim({\mathfrak{g}})>1
), then it can be described inductively in terms of another Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{a}}
with NIS; in particular, if the centre of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is not trivial, then
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a double extension of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{a}}
with NIS by means of a 1-dimensional space (see also
[
FS
]
). Solvable NIS-Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}
can be embraced by this method since the centre of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is non-trivial. Passage to the ground field of characteristic
p>0
brings new examples (see
[
BBH
]
), but nothing conceptually new when it comes to the construction of double extensions, except, perhaps, for inductive descriptions à la Medina and Revoy, since Lie’s theorem and the Levi decomposition do not hold true any more if
p>0
.

The first superization of results by Medina and Revoy was initiated in
[
BeB
]
provided
\mathrm{dim}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})=2
. They showed that every non-simple NIS-Lie superalgebra can be described by successive double extensions. The construction has been improved in a series of papers (see
[
ABBQ
,
ABB
,
BBB
,
B
,
Bor
]
). Superization of the definition of a double extension introduces completely new possibilities:
the bilinear form
{\mathscr{B}}
, the derivation
{\mathscr{D}}
, the central element
x
may be odd
provided
p({\mathscr{B}})=p(x)+p(x^{*})
. The first of these possibilities is related with Manin-Olshansky triples of the type described in
[
LSh
]
, and loops with values in
{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{q}}(n)
and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{(1)}(0|2n+1)
, see
[
BLS
]
.

The
case of characteristic
2
is completely different and requires new definitions and methods as shown in
[
BeBou
]
.

In the last section of this paper, the techniques of double extensions will be used to describe a class of Lie superalgebras with admissible
r
-matrices. Such Lie superalgebras carry a NIS as well as a closed
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric ortho-orthogonal form – see §
2.2
for the terminology. An adapted process of double extension is used to describe these
r
-matrices inductively, see §
4.1
. Additionally, we introduce the notion of double extension for Manin triples in §
4.2
and establish its converse.

Notation.
Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra, and let
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
be its dual also endowed with a Lie superalgebra structure. By identifying
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{**}
we view the map
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}(-)(x)\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{**}
, for every
f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, as an element of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and denote it by
x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}
for short.

2.1.
Symmetric and anti-symmetric bilinear forms on superspaces for
p\not=2
see
[
LSoS
, Ch.1]

Let
V
and
W
be two superspaces defined over an arbitrary field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic
p\not=2
.

Let
{\mathscr{B}}\in\text{Bil}(V,W)
be a homogenous bilinear form. The Gram matrix
B=(B_{ij})
associated to
{\mathscr{B}}
is given by the formula,

(1)

B_{ij}=(-1)^{p({\mathscr{B}})p(v_{i})}{\mathscr{B}}(v_{i},v_{j})\text{~~for the basis vectors $v_{i}\in V$.}

This definition can be extended by linearity to non-homogenous forms. Moreover, it allows us to identify a bilinear form
B(V,W)
with an element of
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(V,W^{*})
.
Consider the
upsetting
of bilinear forms

u\colon\mathop{\mathrm{Bil}}\nolimits(V,W)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Bil}}\nolimits(W,V)
given by the formula

(2)

u({\mathscr{B}})(w,v)=(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}{\mathscr{B}}(v,w)\text{~~for any $v\in V$ and $w\in W$.}

In terms of the Gram matrix
B
of
{\mathscr{B}}
: the form

{\mathscr{B}}
is
symmetric
if and only if

(3)

u(B)=B,\;\text{ where $u(B)=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}R^{t}&(-1)^{p({\mathscr{B}})}T^{t}\\
(-1)^{p({\mathscr{B}})}S^{t}&-U^{t}\end{pmatrix}$}$ for $B=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}R&S\\
T&U\end{pmatrix}$}$.}

Similarly,
anti-symmetry
of
{\mathscr{B}}
means that
u(B)=-B
.

Since
p\not=2
, observe that if
{\mathscr{B}}
is symmetric, then
U
is zero-diagonal, and if
{\mathscr{B}}
is anti-symmetric, then
R
is zero-diagonal.

A non-degenerate even (resp. odd) bilinear form is called
orthosymplectic
(resp.
periplectic
).

Most popular normal shapes of the even non-degenerate (anti)-symmetric forms are the following matrices in the standard format:

(4)

\begin{array}[]{l}B^{\prime}_{ev}(m|2n)=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}1_{m}&0\\
0&J_{2n}\end{pmatrix}$},\quad\text{where
$J_{2n}=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}0&1_{n}\\
-1_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}$}$}\\
B_{ev}(2k|2n)=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{2k}&0\\
0&J_{2n}\end{pmatrix}$},\quad\text{where
$\Pi_{2k}=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}0&1_{k}\\
1_{k}&0\end{pmatrix}$}$},\\
B_{ev}(2k+1|2n)=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{2k+1}&0\\
0&J_{2n}\end{pmatrix}$},\quad\text{where
$\Pi_{2k+1}=\text{\footnotesize$\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1_{k}\\
0&1&0\\
1_{k}&0&0\end{pmatrix}$}$}.\end{array}

The normal shapes of the odd non-degenerate bilinear form are the following matrices in the standard format (NB: we did not make a mistake below selecting (anti-)symmetric form)

(5)

B_{odd}(n|n)=\begin{cases}J_{2n}&\text{if the form is symmetric},\\
\Pi_{2n}&\text{if the form is anti-symmetric}.\\
\end{cases}

Let
{\mathscr{B}}\in\text{Bil}(V,W)
be a bilinear form. The Gram matrix
B=(B_{ij})
associated to
{\mathscr{B}}
is given by the formula

(6)

B_{ij}={\mathscr{B}}(v_{i},v_{j})\text{~~for the basis vectors $v_{i}\in V$.}

The
upsetting
of bilinear forms

u\colon\mathop{\mathrm{Bil}}\nolimits(V,W)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Bil}}\nolimits(W,V)
is given by the formula

(7)

u({\mathscr{B}})(w,v)={\mathscr{B}}(v,w)\text{~~for any $v\in V$ and $w\in W$.}

In terms of the Gram matrix
B
of
{\mathscr{B}}
, we have

u(B)=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}R^{t}&T^{t}\\
S^{t}&U^{t}\end{pmatrix}$}\text{ for }B=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}R&S\\
T&U\end{pmatrix}$}.

Our main definition is the following. We say that the bilinear form
{\mathscr{B}}
is

•

symmetric

;

•

{\bar{0}}
-

(called supersymmetric in
[

]
) if
u(B)=B
and
U
is zero-diagonal;

•

{\bar{1}}
-
antisymmetric
if
u(B)=B
and
R
is zero-diagonal.

In the particular case of a purely even space, we say that
B
is
symmetric
if
u(B)=B
and antisymmetric if
u(B)=B
and
R
is zero-diagonal (recall that in this case
S=0,T=0,
and
U=0
.)

A non-degenerate
even
(resp.
odd
) bilinear form is called
ortho-orthogonal
(resp.
periplectic
).

A. Lebedev proved (see
[
LeD
]
) that over algebraically closed fields of characteristic
2
the equivalence classes of non-degenerate symmetric bilinear forms

on the
n
-dimensional space

are as follows:

(i) for
n

{\mathscr{B}}
one can take
1_{n}
.

(ii) for

{\mathscr{B}}
of one class one can take
1_{n}
, of another class one can take
\Pi_{n}
.

Following the custom in differential geometry, we will denote a
{\bar{1}}

.

Let us reformulate the condition of
{\bar{1}}

in terms of the bilinear form

2.3.
Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
.

Following
[
BGL
,
LeD
,
BGLLS
,
BLLSq
]
, a
Lie superalgebra
in characteristic
2
is a
superspace
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
over
\mathbb{K}
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is a Lie algebra,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is a

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-module made two-sided by symmetry, and on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
a
squaring
, denoted by
s_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
, is
given. The bracket on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
, as well as the action of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
, is denoted by the same symbol
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
. For any
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
, their bracket is then defined by

[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x+y)-s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-s_{\mathfrak{g}}(y).

The bracket is extended to non-homogenous elements by bilinearity.

The Jacobi identity involving the squaring reads as follows:

[s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[x,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\;\text{ for any $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Let
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
be a bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}:=({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},s_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Following
[
BeBou
]
, we say that
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
invariant
if
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z)={\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})
for any
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Observe that the squaring is not involved here.

We call the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}:=({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},s_{\mathfrak{g}})
a
NIS-superalgebra
(sometimes called quadratics, see
[
BeB
]
) if it has a non-degenerate invariant
{\bar{0}}
-antisymmetric bilinear form
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. A NIS-Lie superalgebra is called
irreducible
if it cannot be decomposed into a direct sum of mutually orthogonal ideals.

For any Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
in characteristic
2
, its
derived algebras
are
defined to be (for
i\geq 0
)

{\mathfrak{g}}^{(0)}:={\mathfrak{g}},\quad{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i+1)}=[{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i)},{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i)}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\mid x\in({\mathfrak{g}}^{(i)})_{\bar{1}}\}.

A linear map
{\mathscr{D}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
derivation
of the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
if, in addition to

(10)

{\mathscr{D}}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[{\mathscr{D}}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,{\mathscr{D}}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}\quad\text{for any $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}$ and $y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

we have

(11)

{\mathscr{D}}(s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))=[{\mathscr{D}}(x),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}\quad\text{for any $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$}.

It is worth noticing that condition (
11
) implies condition (
10
) if
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
.

We denote the space of all derivations of
{\mathfrak{g}}

by
{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

2.4.
Non-degenerate
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric bilinear forms on Lie superalgebras for
p=2

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
be a Lie superalgebra in characteristic
2
.

Let
\omega:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
be a bilinear form. We say that
\omega
is
closed
if the following cocycle conditions are satisfied:

(12)

\displaystyle\omega([x,y],z)+\omega([z,x],y)+\omega([y,z],x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{ for all $x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}$};

(13)

\displaystyle\omega(s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(x,[x,z])\quad\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $z\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Observe that the squaring has to be taken into account in defining the cocycle condition. For more details, see
[
BGL2
]
.

Proposition 2.4.1

(Link NIS’s with
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric forms)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\mathscr{B})
be a NIS Lie super-algebra in characteristic 2 such that
\mathscr{B}
is homogeneous. Then
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a homogeneous, non-degenerate, closed,
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric form
\omega
if and only if there exists an invertible
\Delta\in{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, where
p(\omega)=p(\mathscr{B})+p(\Delta)
, such that
{\mathscr{B}}
is
\Delta
-invariant and

\omega(x,y)=\mathscr{B}(\Delta(x),y)\quad\text{for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Proof.

Let us first prove the “only if” part. Suppose that
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a non-degenerate, homogeneous, closed,
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric form
\omega
. Since
\omega
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are non-degenerate, there exists
\Delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}})
such that

\omega(x,y)={\mathscr{B}}(\Delta(x),y)\quad\text{for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Let us prove that
\Delta
is an invertible derivation. Indeed, the fact that
\Delta
is invertible follows from the fact that
\omega
is non-degenerate. Moreover, the fact that
{\mathscr{B}}
is
\Delta
-invariant follows from condition (
8
) or (
9
) above. For all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathscr{B}}(\Delta([x,y]),z)=\omega([x,y],z)=\omega([z,x],y)+\omega([y,z],x)={\mathscr{B}}(\Delta([z,x]),y)+{\mathscr{B}}(\Delta([y,z]),x)

\displaystyle={\mathscr{B}}([z,x],\Delta(y))+{\mathscr{B}}([y,z],\Delta(x))={\mathscr{B}}(z,[x,\Delta(y)])+{\mathscr{B}}(z,[y,\Delta(x)])

\displaystyle={\mathscr{B}}(z,[x,\Delta(y)]+[y,\Delta(x)]).

Therefore,
\Delta([x,y])=[\Delta(x),y]+[x,\Delta(y)]
since
{\mathscr{B}}
is non-degenerate and
{\bar{0}}
-antisymmetric. On the other hand, (for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
and
z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
):

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathscr{B}}(\Delta(s(x)),z)=\omega(s(x),z)=\omega(x,[x,z])={\mathscr{B}}(\Delta(x),[x,z])={\mathscr{B}}([\Delta(x),x],z).\end{array}

It follows that
\Delta(s(x))=[x,\Delta(x)]
since
{\mathscr{B}}
is non-degenerate. Therefore,
\Delta
is indeed a derivation.

For the “if” part, the proof results from the fact that
\Delta
is a derivation and the bilinear form
{\mathscr{B}}
is
\Delta
-invariant. ∎

2.5.
Left-symmetric and left-alternative algebras in characteristic
2

In this subsection, we will show that every homogeneous, non-degenerate, closed,
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric form on a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
in characteristic 2 naturally induces a new algebra structure, denoted by
\star
, on the underlying vector space such that its commutator is nothing but the bracket on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Moreover, this structure is left-alternative, and therefore left-symmetric (see, Prop.
2.5.1
). These structures are also known as
Vinberg algebras
as they were used in
[
V
]
(for a thorough study of these structures, see
[
N
]
.) In addition, if
{\mathfrak{g}}
has a NIS
\mathscr{B}
then
{\mathfrak{g}}
has an invertible derivation, relating
\omega
and
\mathscr{B}
, such that the derivation will define the structure
\star
. In Section
4
, we will show that this derivation yields a Lie bisuperalgebra structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and hence a Manin-triple
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a Lie superalgebra in characteristic 2 equipped with a homogeneous, non-degenerate, closed,
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric form
\omega
.

For all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the map
z\mapsto\omega(y,[x,z])
is a linear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Moreover, the map
z\mapsto\omega(z,\cdot)
identifies
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. It follows that there exists
x\star y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\omega(x\star y,z)=\omega(y,[x,z]).

Observe that if both
x
and
y
are homogeneous, then
x\star y
is homogeneous as well. Moreover,
p(x\star y)=p(x)+p(y)
.

For all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

0=\omega(x,[y,z])+\omega(y,[z,x])+\omega(z,[x,y])=\omega(y\star x,z)+\omega(x\star y,z)+\omega(z,[x,y]).

Therefore,

(14)

[x,y]=x\star y+y\star x\;\text{ for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Aditionnally, for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

0=\omega(s(x),y)+\omega(x,[x,y])=\omega(x\star x,y).

Therefore,

(15)

s(x)=x\star x\;\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$}.

Now, for all
x,y,z,t\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&=&\omega(t,[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]])\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega(t,[x,[y,z]])+\omega(t,[y,[z,x]])+\omega(t,[z,[x,y]])\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega(y\star(x\star t),z)+\omega(x\star(y\star t)t,z)+\omega([x,y]\star t,z).\end{array}

Now Eq. (
14
) and the fact that
\omega
is non-degenerate imply that

\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,t)=\mathrm{Asso}(y,x,t)\;\text{ for all $x,y,t\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

where

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,t)&:=&(x\star y)\star t+x\star(y\star t)\;\text{ for all $x,y,t\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

On the other hand,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&=&\omega([s(x),y]+[x,[x,y]],t)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega(t,[s(x),y])+\omega(t,[x,[x,y]])\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega(s(x)\star t,y)+\omega(x\star(x\star t),y).\end{array}

Now Eq. (
15
) and the fact that
\omega
is non-degenerate imply that

\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,t)=0\;\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $t\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Suppose further that
{\mathfrak{g}}
has a NIS
\mathscr{B}
that is homogeneous, and let us describe
\star
in terms of the derivation
\Delta
. For all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, and using Proposition
2.4.1
we have

0=\omega(x\star y,z)-\omega(y,[x,z])={\mathscr{B}}(\Delta(x\star y),z)-{\mathscr{B}}(\Delta(y),[x,z])={\mathscr{B}}(\Delta(x\star y)-[\Delta(y),x],z).

Since
{\mathscr{B}}
is non-degenerate and
\Delta
is invertible, it follows that

x\star y=\Delta^{-1}([x,\Delta(y)])\;\text{ for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

In particular,
s(x)=x\star x=\Delta^{-1}([x,\Delta(x)])
. We then arrive at the following definition.

An
algebra

{\mathfrak{g}}
in characteristic
2
is called
left-symmetric
(resp.
left-alternative
) if it is endowed with a bilinear operation
\star
satisfying (for all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
):

\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,z)=\mathrm{Asso}(y,x,z)\quad\text{(resp. $\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,z)=0$)},

where

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,z)&:=&(x\star y)\star z+x\star(y\star z)\text{ for all $x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},\end{array}

is called the
associator
.

Proposition 2.5.1

(left-alternative implies left-symmetric)
.

In characteristic
2
, every left-alternative algebra is left-symmetric.

Proof.

Indeed, for all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{Asso}(x+y,x+y,z)=\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,z)+\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,z)+\mathrm{Asso}(y,x,z)+\mathrm{Asso}(y,y,z).\qed\end{array}

Remark 2.5.2
.

(i) This proposition shows that left-alternatives algebras constitute a subclass of left-symmetric algebras, which is not true if
p\not=2
. Whether these classes are the same or not is an open question.

(ii) Note that the structure
\star
associated with a closed
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric ortho-orthogonal (or periplectic) form is left-alternative.

Theorem 2.5.3

(Constructing Lie superalgebras from Lie algebras)
.

Let
p=2
.

(
i
)
Every Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with an invertible derivation
\Delta\in{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{g}})
admits a left-symmetric structure. Explicitly,

(16)

x\star y:=\Delta^{-1}([x,\Delta(y)])\;\text{ for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

(
ii
)
Every
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
with an invertible homogeneous derivation
\Delta
can be turned into a Lie superalgebra provided that

\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,y)=0\;\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

where the assciator
\mathrm{Asso}
is associated with the operation (
16
). Explicitly, the map

s:{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\quad x\mapsto x\star x

is a squaring on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

(i) Let us first compute the associator. For all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,z)=(x\star y)\star z+x\star(y\star z)=\Delta^{-1}\left([x\star y,\Delta(z)]+[x,\Delta(y\star z)]\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta^{-1}\left([\Delta^{-1}[x,\Delta(y)],\Delta(z)]+[x,[y,\Delta(z)]\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta^{-1}\left([[x,y],\Delta(z)]+[\Delta^{-1}[\Delta(x),y],\Delta(z)]+[x,[y,\Delta(z)]]\right).

Now, using the Jacobi identity and the fact that
\Delta
is a derivation we see that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,z)+\mathrm{Asso}(y,x,z)&=&\Delta^{-1}\left([\Delta^{-1}[\Delta(x),y],\Delta(z)]+[x,[y,\Delta(z)]]\right)\\[5.69054pt]
&&+\Delta^{-1}\left([\Delta^{-1}[\Delta(y),x],\Delta(z)]+[y,[x,\Delta(z)]]\right)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\Delta^{-1}\left([[x,y],\Delta(z)]+[\Delta(z),[x,y]]\right)=0.\end{array}

Therefore, the operation (
16
) defines a left-symmetric structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

(ii) Suppose now that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded and that
\Delta
is homogeneous. Let us check that the map

s:{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\quad x\mapsto x\star x

is indeed a squaring. For all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
, we have

s(\lambda x)=(\lambda x)\star(\lambda x)=\lambda^{2}(x\star x)=\lambda^{2}s(x).

Additionally, for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
:

\displaystyle s(x+y)-s(x)-s(y)=(x+y)\star(x+y)-x\star x-y\star y=x\star y+y\star x

\displaystyle=\Delta^{-1}[x,\Delta(y)]+\Delta^{-1}[y,\Delta(x)]=\Delta^{-1}(\Delta([x,y]))=[x,y].

Moreover, for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[s(x),y]+[x,[x,y]]&=&s(x)\star y+y\star s(x)+x\star[x,y]+[x,y]\star x\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(x\star x)\star y+y\star(x\star x)+x\star(x\star y+y\star x)+(x\star y+y\star x)\star x\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(x\star x)\star y+x\star(x\star y)+y\star(x\star x)+(y\star x)\star x+x\star(y\star x)\\[5.69054pt]
&&+(x\star y)\star x\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,y)+\mathrm{Asso}(y,x,x)+\mathrm{Asso}(x,y,x)=\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,y).\end{array}

It follows that the Jacobi identity involving the squaring holds if and only if

\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,y)=0\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.\qed

Corollary 2.5.4

(The case of
2
-step nilpotent algebras)
.

Every
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebra in characteristic
2
with an invertible homogeneous derivation can naturally be turned into a Lie superalgebra.

Proof.

Let us first observe that since
\Delta
is invertible, then
\Delta([{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}])=[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
. Now, for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asso}(x,x,y)=x\star(x\star y)+(x\star x)\star y=x\star\Delta^{-1}[x,\Delta(y)]+\Delta^{-1}[x,\Delta(x)]\star y

\displaystyle=\Delta^{-1}\left([x,[x,\Delta(y)]]+[\Delta^{-1}[x,\Delta(x)],\Delta(y)]\right)=\Delta^{-1}(0)=0.\qed

3.
The Manin triples and matched pairs for Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2

Throughout this section
\mathbb{K}
is an arbitray field of characteristic
2
.

3.1.
Manin triples

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a Lie superalgebra and
{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}
be two Lie sub-superalgebras of
{\mathfrak{h}}
. The triple
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}})
is called a
Manin triple
if it satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{k}}
, the direct sum of vector subspaces
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}
;

(2)

there exists an even NIS
\mathscr{B}
on
{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}
are isotropic with respect to
\mathscr{B}
.

For a comprehensive construction of Manin triples for complex Lie algebras, see
[
CP
]
. For Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2, the first example of a Manin triple was given in
[
BeBou
]
. Here we generalize the construction developed in
[
BeBou
]
.

Theorem 3.1.1

(On Manin Triples)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},s_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie superalgebra in characteristic
2
, and suppose that on the dual space
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
a Lie superalgebra structure
({\mathfrak{g}}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})
is also given. On the space
{\mathfrak{h}}:={\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, we consider a natural even bilinear form defined by

{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x+f,y+g)=g(x)+f(y)\quad\text{ for every $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $f,g\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}$.}

There exists a unique Lie NIS-superalgebra structure on
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}})
provided the conditions (
17
), (
18
), and (
19
) are fulfilled:

(17)

\displaystyle s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{h}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}}\;\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $h\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}$};

(18)

\displaystyle s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f)\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f,f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y}]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}+f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}}\;\text{ for all $y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{\bar{1}}$};

(19)

\displaystyle[x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f},y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}}+[x,y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f},

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}$, and $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}$};\\
\text{or for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}$, $y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{\bar{0}}$};\\
\text{or for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$, $y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{\bar{1}}$}.\end{array}

Explicitly,

•

The bracket is given by
(
for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and for all
f,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
)

[x+f,y+h]_{\mathfrak{h}}:=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}+x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{h}+[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}+f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y}+h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}.

•

The squaring is given by

s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x+f)=s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)+s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f)+f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}+x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}\;\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{\bar{1}}$.}

The bilinear form
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}
is
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}
-invariant and even.

Clearly,

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}|_{\mathfrak{g}}=[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}|_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}=[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},\quad s_{\mathfrak{h}}|_{\mathfrak{g}}=s_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad s_{\mathfrak{h}}|_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}=s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},

and therefore
({\mathfrak{h}}:={\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
is a Manin triple.

For the proof, see Section
3.5
.

The reader may wonder why there is no analogue of Eq. (
19
) on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. The answer is yes indeed it should be, but Lemma
3.1.2
shows that it is equivalent to Eq. (
19
).

Lemma 3.1.2

(Technical)
.

(
i
)
For all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
f,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, we have

h([x,y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[f,h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(y).

(
ii
)
For all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
f,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, the following two conditions are equivalent

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f},y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}}&=&[x,y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f},\\[5.69054pt]
[f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x},h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}+h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}}&=&[f,h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}+f\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{h}}+[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}.\end{array}

Proof.

For Part (i), we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}h([x,y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}]_{\mathfrak{g}})&=&h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}(y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f})=y\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f}(h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x})\quad\text{(we identify ${\mathfrak{g}}$ with ${\mathfrak{g}}^{**}$)}\\[5.69054pt]
&=&y([f,h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})=[f,h\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(y).\end{array}

Part (ii) follows by evaluating the first equation at an arbitrary
h\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, evaluating the second equation at an arbitrary
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, and then using Part (i).∎

3.2.
A few examples of Manin triples in characteristic
2

Here we give three examples of Manin triples of Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
.

Example 1:
A Manin triple constructed from the Heisenberg superalgebra. Consider the Heisenberg superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{hei}}(0|2)
spanned by
p,q
(odd) and
z
(even), with
s_{\mathfrak{g}}(p)=s_{\mathfrak{g}}(q)=0
and the only nonzero bracket:
[p,q]=z
. We endow
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
with a Lie superalgebra structure given by:

[p^{*},z^{*}]=sq^{*}+tp^{*},\quad[q^{*},z^{*}]=uq^{*}+vp^{*}\text{ for any fixed $s,t,u,v\in\mathbb{K}$}.

A NIS-Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}:={\mathfrak{hei}}(0|2)\oplus{\mathfrak{hei}}(0|2)^{*}
exists. A direct computation shows the following:

•

The bracket on
{\mathfrak{h}}
is given by

\begin{array}[]{l}[p,p^{*}]=tz,\quad[p,q^{*}]=vz,\quad[p,z^{*}]=tp+vq+q^{*},\\[5.69054pt]
[q,p^{*}]=sz,\quad[q,q^{*}]=uz,\quad[q,z^{*}]=sp+uq+p^{*}.\end{array}

•

The squaring on
{\mathfrak{h}}
is given by (where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\in\mathbb{K}
):

\begin{array}[]{lcl}s_{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha_{1}p+\alpha_{2}q+\alpha_{3}p^{*}+\alpha_{4}q^{*})&=&(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}t+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{4}v+\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}s+\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}u)z.\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

We give an example of an outer derivation
{\mathscr{D}}
on
{\mathfrak{h}}
that is invertible. This example will justify the studies in Section
4
. The example we provide here was borrowed from
[
BeBou
]
in the case where
{\mathfrak{hei}}(0|2)^{*}
has an abelian structure; namely,
s=t=u=v=0
. The ground field
{\mathbb{K}}
is assumed to be algebraically closed, hence infinite. The invertible derivation is as follows (for
\alpha_{9},\alpha_{10}\not=0,1
):

{\mathscr{D}}=\alpha_{2}D_{2}+\alpha_{4}D_{4}+\alpha_{9}D_{9}+\alpha_{10}D_{10}+(\alpha_{9}+\alpha_{10})D_{11},

where

\begin{array}[]{lcllcllcllcl}D_{2}&=&q^{*}\otimes\widehat{q},&D_{4}&=&p^{*}\otimes\widehat{p},\\[5.69054pt]
D_{9}&=&p\otimes\widehat{p}+q^{*}\otimes\widehat{q^{*}}+z\otimes\widehat{z},&D_{10}&=&q\otimes\widehat{q}+p^{*}\otimes\widehat{p^{*}}+z\otimes\widehat{z},\\[5.69054pt]
D_{11}&=&q^{*}\otimes\widehat{q^{*}}+p^{*}\otimes\widehat{p^{*}}+z^{*}\otimes\widehat{z^{*}}.\end{array}

Example 2:
The Poisson superalgebras
[
LSh
]
. Consider
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{po}}(0|2n-1)
. Let
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{po}}(0|2n)
be generated by
\mathbb{K}[\theta_{1},...,\theta_{2n}]
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
by
\mathbb{K}[\theta_{1},...,\theta_{2n-1}]

{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}
on

is given by the Berezin integral
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x,y)=\int xy\;\mathrm{vol}(\theta)

{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}={\mathfrak{g}}\cdot\zeta
, where
\zeta=\theta_{2n}+\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1}k_{i}\theta_{i}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1}{k_{i}}^{2}=1.

Example 3:
An example of a Lie superalgebra endowed with both NIS and a closed
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric ortho-orthogonal form. Let
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},s_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a Lie superalgebra with an invertible derivation
\mathscr{D}
(take for instance
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{hei}}(0|2)\oplus{\mathfrak{hei}}(0|2)^{*}

{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
. There exists a NIS
\mathscr{B}
on the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}

Moreover,

is
\mathscr{B}
-symmetric. Therefore, the form

is closed,
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric and ortho-orthogonal on

3.3.
Review on cohomology of Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2

Recall basic facts concerning the cohomology of Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
; for more details, see
[
BGL2
]
. 1-cochains are just linear functions on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
. In our context, the
differential
of a 1-cochain
c
is defined as follows:

(20)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\mathfrak{d}}c(x,y)&=&c([x,y])+x\cdot c(y)+y\cdot c(x)\quad\text{for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$};\\[2.84526pt]
{\mathfrak{d}}c(x,x)&=&c(s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))+x\cdot c(x)\quad\text{for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$}.\end{array}

A 1-cocycle
c
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
must satisfy the following conditions:

(21)

\displaystyle x\cdot c(y)+y\cdot c(x)+c([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

(22)

\displaystyle x\cdot c(x)+c(s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$}.

The space of all 1-cocycles is denoted by
Z^{1}({\mathfrak{g}};M)
.

To every Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
in characteristic
2
, we can assign its
desuperization
denoted by
F({\mathfrak{g}})
, where
F
is the functor of forgetting the super structure. The space
F({\mathfrak{g}})
turns
{\mathfrak{g}}
into an ordinary
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded Lie algebra; for more details, see
[
BLLSq
]
. Now, any 1-cocycle
c
on the Lie algebra
F({\mathfrak{g}})
is captured only by the condition (
21
), and therefore the cohomology of
F({\mathfrak{g}})
viewed as a Lie algebra and that of
{\mathfrak{g}}
viewed as a Lie superalgebra are not necessarily isomorphic. We refer to
[
BGL2
]
for illustrative examples.

Let us define a bilinear form
\langle-,-\rangle:{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
by
\langle f,x\rangle=f(x)
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and for all
f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
is finite-dimensional, we have
({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}={\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
so we also denote by
\langle-,-\rangle
the bilinear form on
({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}\times({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})
defined by

\langle f\otimes h,x\otimes y\rangle=f(x)h(y)\text{ for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $f,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

then extend the definition by linearity. Given the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}:{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, its dual is the map
c_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
. Similarly, given the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
, its dual is the map
c_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}:{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. More precisely,

(23)

\displaystyle\langle[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},x\rangle

\displaystyle=

3.4.
r
-matrices and Manin triples

This section is devoted to the study of triangular Lie bi-superalgebras in characteristic
2
. We refer to
[
CP
,
D
,
GZB
,
M2
]
in the case of Lie (super)algebras defined over a field of characteristic zero, and to
[
F1
,
F2
]
in the case of modular Lie algebras.

A
Lie coalgebra
over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
p\not=2
is a vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}
over
\mathbb{K}
together with a linear mapping
c:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
, such that

(i)

\mathop{\text{\rm Im}}\nolimits(c)\subseteq\mathop{\text{\rm Im}}\nolimits(1-\tau)
, and

(ii)

(1+\xi+\xi^{2})\circ(1\otimes c)\otimes c=0,

where
\tau:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the switch mapping sending
x\otimes y
to
y\otimes x
for all

x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
while
\xi:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the cycle mapping sending
x\otimes y\otimes z
to

y\otimes z\otimes x
for all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

The mapping
c
is called the cobracket of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, (i) is called

co-anticommutativity
, and (ii) is called the
co-Jacobi identity
.

For
p=2
, the co-anticommutativity condition (i) has to be changed to the following condition:

c^{t}(f\otimes f)=0\text{ for all $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}$},

where
c^{t}:{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is the dual of
c
. Our choice is based on the fact that a coalgebra structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
will define a Lie algebra structure on the dual space
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. Recall that for
p=2
, the anti-commutativity of the bracket is expressed as
[a,a]=0.

A
\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2
-graded Lie coalgebra is a
\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2
-graded vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
over
\mathbb{K}
endowed with a cobracket
c
such that
c({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{i}})\subset({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{i}}
, where
\bar{i}={\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}
(namely,
c
is even).

A
Lie bialgebra
over
\mathbb{K}
is a vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}
over
\mathbb{K}
together with
\mathbb{K}
-linear mappings

[\cdot,\cdot]:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
and
c:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra,
({\mathfrak{g}},c)
is a Lie
coalgebra and
c
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
to the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
. Namely,

c([x,y])=x\cdot c(y)-y\cdot c(x)\text{ for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

A
coboundary
Lie bialgebra is a Lie bialgebra such that the cobracket
c

is a cboundary, i.e., there exists an element
r\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

c(x)=x\cdot r
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra and
r\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
be defined by
r=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}
, with
a_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}},
and
b_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Consider
{\mathfrak{g}}
embedded in its universal
enveloping superalgebra
U({\mathfrak{g}})
. We put

r^{12}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\otimes 1,\quad r^{13}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}\otimes 1\otimes b_{i},\quad\text{ and }\quad r^{23}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}1\otimes a_{i}\otimes b_{i}.

The mapping
\mathrm{CBY}:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
given by

r\rightarrow[r^{12},r^{13}]+[r^{12},r^{23}]+[r^{13},r^{23}]

is called the
classical Yang-Baxter map
, the equation
\mathrm{CYB}(r)=0
is called the
classical Yang-Baxter equation
(CYBE), and a solution of the CYBE is called a
classical

r
-matrix.

If
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Lie algebra and
V
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module, then the set of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant elements of
V
is defined as

V^{\mathfrak{g}}:=\{v\in V\,|\,x\cdot v=0\text{ for all }x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}.

In the case of Lie algebras defined over a field of characteristic zero, Drinfeld proved the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4.1

(Lie bialgebra structure, see
[
D
]
)
.

Let
r\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
and define
\delta_{r}(x):=x\cdot r
for every

(

(

\mathrm{CYB}(r)\in({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}

Therefore, every solution

{\mathfrak{g}}

Following

For

(\delta_{r})^{t}(f\otimes f)=0\text{ for all $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}$},

Proposition 3.4.2

(Eq. (

\Rightarrow r+\tau(r)\in({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}

(

We have: Eq. (

(

Let us just prove part (ii). Observe that if Eq. (

(\delta_{r})^{t}(f\otimes g+g\otimes f)=0\text{ for all $f,g\in{\mathfrak{g}}.$}

Now, for all

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\langle f\otimes g+g\otimes f,\delta_{r}(x)\rangle=\langle f\otimes g,\delta_{r}(x)+\tau\circ\delta_{r}(x)\rangle.

It remains to show that the condition

\delta_{r}(x)+\tau\circ\delta_{r}(x)=x\cdot r+\tau\circ x\cdot r=x\cdot(r+\tau(r)).\qed

s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(\lambda f)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda f([-,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\lambda f(b_{i})=\lambda^{2}\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}f([-,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})f(b_{i})=\lambda^{2}s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f).

Second, the map (for all

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(f,g)&\mapsto&s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f+g)+s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f)+s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(g)\\[2.84526pt]
&&\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(f([-,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})g(b_{i})+g([-,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})f(b_{i})\right).\end{array}

is obviously bilinear.
∎

Theorem 3.4.8

(Condition on the squaring to satisfy the Jacobi identity)

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}f(b_{j})\left(f([[x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})h(b_{i})+f([a_{i},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})h([x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}f([x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})(f([b_{i},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}h(b_{j})+f(a_{j})h([b_{i},b_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+f(a_{i})f([[x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})h(b_{j})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j}f(a_{i})f(a_{j})h([[x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},b_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

Proof.

We have already a Lie algebra structure on

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},x\rangle&=&\langle f\otimes h,c_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\rangle\quad\text{ for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $f,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}$}.\end{array}

\displaystyle\langle[s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f),h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},x\rangle=\langle s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f)\otimes h,c_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\rangle=\langle s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f)\otimes h,\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\left([x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes b_{i}+a_{i}\otimes[x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f)([x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})h(b_{i})+s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(f)(a_{i})h([x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(f([[x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})f(b_{j})h(b_{i})+f([a_{i},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})h([x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\langle[f,[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},x\rangle=\langle f\otimes[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},c_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\rangle=\langle f\otimes[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}},\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\left([x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes b_{i}+a_{i}\otimes[x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(f([x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}(b_{i})+f(a_{i})[f,h]_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}([x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(f([x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\langle f\otimes h,c_{\mathfrak{g}}(b_{i})\rangle+f(a_{i})\langle f\otimes h,c_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}f([x,a_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})(f([b_{i},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})h(b_{j})+f(a_{j})h([b_{i},b_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}f(a_{i})\left(f([[x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},a_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})h(b_{j})+f(a_{j})h([[x,b_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},b_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\right).\qed

We have just proved that if the condition (

.

Moreover, the squaring reads as follows

s_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(f)=s_{{\mathfrak{c}}}(f)+\alpha(f)x\text{ for all $f\in{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bar{1}}$,}

where
s_{\mathfrak{c}}(f)\in{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bar{0}}
.

Now, let
f\in{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{1}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{1}}
. Since
s_{\mathfrak{h}}(f)\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}}
, then
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(f),x^{*})=0
as
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{k}},{\mathfrak{k}})=0
. It follows that

0={\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(f),x^{*})={\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(s_{\mathfrak{c}}(f)+\alpha(f)x,x^{*})=\alpha(f).

Therefore,
\alpha({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{1}})=\{0\}
.

Following the same proof as in
[
BeBou
, Prop. 3.1.3]
, we can show that
({\mathfrak{c}},\beta,s_{\mathfrak{c}},{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{c}})
is a double extension of
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},s_{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}})
by means of the derivation
{\mathscr{D}}
and the quadratic form
\alpha
. The proof also implies that
\psi=0
and that

\phi(f,g)={\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{c}}({\mathscr{D}}(f),g)\text{ for all $f,g\in{\mathfrak{c}}$}.

Let us show that
{\mathscr{D}}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}
. Since
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{k}}\cap{\mathscr{K}}^{\perp}
, but
{\mathscr{K}}^{\perp}
is an ideal in
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}
is a sub-superalgebra of
{\mathfrak{h}}
, it follows that for all
f\in{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}
, we have

{\mathscr{D}}(f)=[x^{*},f]\in({\mathfrak{k}}\cap{\mathscr{K}}^{\perp})_{\bar{0}}={\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}.

It remains to show that
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{a}})={\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{b}},{\mathfrak{b}})=0
, but this is obvious since
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
.∎

Hereafter, we will carry out the study in the case of
\mathscr{D}_{\bar{1}}
-extensions.

Theorem 4.2.3

(
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{1}}
-extension for Manin triples)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}})
be a Manin triple in charac-teristic
2
with a NIS
{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}}
. Let
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{h}})_{\bar{1}}
satisfy Eqns. (
43
), (
44
), (
45
) and
{\mathscr{D}}({\mathfrak{k}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{k}}
. Let
(\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathscr{K}}^{*},\tilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}})
be the double extension of
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathscr{B}})

by the one-dimensional Lie superalgebra
{\mathscr{K}}
(by means of
{\mathscr{D}}
). If
a_{\bar{0}}\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}}
, then
(\tilde{\mathfrak{h}},\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{\mathfrak{k}})
is a Manin triple with a NIS
\tilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}}
, where
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathscr{K}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathfrak{k}}
.

Proof.

Similar to that of Theorem
4.2.1
.
∎

Now, let us give the converse of Theorem
4.2.3
.

Theorem 4.2.4

4.2.3
)
.

Let

be a Manin triple such that

({\mathfrak{z}}({\mathfrak{h}})\cap{\mathscr{C}}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{h}})\cap{\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{1}}\not=0

as in Theorem

.

5.
Conclusion and outlook

(1) Manin triples for Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
are studied and two examples are given in §
3.2
. The notion of Lie bi-superalgebra has also been studied and a link has been established between the two. Because of the squaring, the conditions for the existence of a Lie bi-superalgebra structure are more complex than for characteristic not 2.

(2) The construction has been generalized to any pair of Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}
satisfying certain conditions. Such a pair satisfying these conditions is called a
matched pair
.

(3) It has been showed that if there is a classical
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
F({\mathfrak{g}})
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Lie superalgebra and
F
is the functor of forgetting the super structure, then a squaring on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
can be defined. It has been showed that this squaring satisfies the Jacobi identity if and only if a certain condition (
29
) is satisfied. Such classical
r
-matrices are called
admissible
. Surprisingly, having a classical admissible
r
-matrix guarantees the existence of Lie bi-superalgebra on
{\mathfrak{g}}
in characteristic 2.

Open problem:
Following
[
F1
,
F2
]
, which Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 possess at least one admissible non-trivial classical matrix
r
? At least describe such
r
-matrices in the case of known Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 (see
[
BGL
]
,
[
BGLLS
]
).

(4) In particular, admissible classical
r
-matrices on Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 with an even NIS have been studied at the end of §
3.4
. It has been showed that every such Lie superalgebras endowed additionally with a closed
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric ortho-orthogonal form posses an admissible classical
r
-matrix. More precisely, these
r
-matrices are obtained by the inverse of the derivations that links NIS and the ortho-orthogonal form. We have developed a process of double extensions to describe these
r
-matrices, see §
4.1
.

Open problems:
(i) Following
[
BBM
]
, is it possible to obtain every Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
in characteristic 2 with an even NIS and a closed
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric ortho-orthogonal form as a double extension of a smaller Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{a}}
(with the same property) such that
\dim({\mathfrak{g}})=\dim({\mathfrak{a}})+2
? (ii) Study this problem in the case where NIS is odd. We believe that this can be done by studying Manin triples of the form
({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}),{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
, where
\Pi
is the functor of changing the parity.

(5) It has been proved in
[
BaB
]
that every even NIS-Lie superalgebra equipped with a closed antisymmetric ortho-symplectic form over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero is actually a Manin triple. The proof makes use of the weight space decomposition of a Lie superalgebra that is only valid over a field of characteristic zero. We do not know whether the result of
[
BaB
]
is true or not true in our context, perhaps ideas from
[
BLLS
]
could help.

(6) The importance of invertible derivations on Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 (not necessarily NIS) is crucial to construct admissible Yang-Baxter operators, as presented in Example 3 of §
3.2
and Example 4 in §
4.1
. It is a well-known theorem by Jacobson
[
J
]
that every Lie algebra in characteristic 0 with an invertible derivation has to be nilpotent.

Open problem:
Is Jacobson’s Theorem still valid for Lie (super)algebras in characteristic 2? If the theorem doesn’t hold describe the structure of such Lie (super)algebras.

(7) It has been shown that every Lie algebra with an invertible derivation gives rise to a left-symmetric structure on it. Further, it has been shown in Theorem
2.5.3
that every
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Lie algebra in characteristic
2
with an invertible derivation can be turned into a Lie superalgebra provided that the left-symmetric structure is left-alternative.

Open problems:
(i) It has been proved that every left-alternative structure is left-symmetric, see Prop.
2.5.1
. What about the converse? (ii) If the converse doe not hold, describe invertible derivations on
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Lie algebras in characteristic 2 for which the left-symmetric structure is left-alternative. (iii) Compare the method 2 in
[
BLLSq
]
with our method in constructing Lie superalgebras from
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Lie algebras in characteristic 2.

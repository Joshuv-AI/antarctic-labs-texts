Extensions, deformation and categorification of AssDer pairs
Apurba Das, Ashis Mandal

Abstract
In this paper, we consider associative algebras equipped with derivations. Such a pair of an associative algebra with a derivation is called an AssDer pair. Using the Hochschild cohomology for associative algebras, we define cohomology for an AssDer pair with coefficients in a representation. We study central extensions and abelian extensions of AssDer pairs. Moreover, we consider extensions of a pair of derivations in central extensions of associative algebras. Next, we study formal one-parameter deformations of AssDer pair by deforming both the associative product and the derivation. They are governed by the cohomology of the AssDer pair with representation in itself. In the next part, we study 2 2 -term A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras with homotopy derivations considered by Loday and Doubek-Lada. Finally, we introduce 2 2 -derivations on associative 2 2 -algebras and show that the category of associative 2 2 -algebras with 2 2 -derivations are equivalent to the category of 2 2 -term A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras with homotopy derivations.



Keywords:
AssDer pair, Hochschild cohomology, Crossed extensions, Abelian extensions, Deformations, Homotopy derivations, Categorifications.

16E40, 16S80, 16W25, 18N25, 18N40.

2
AssDer pairs

In this section, we introduce representations and cohomology of AssDer pairs. We also relate this cohomology with the cohomology of LieDer pairs as introduced in
[
27
]
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. A bimodule over it consists of a vector space
M
together with two linear maps
l:A\otimes M\rightarrow M
and
r:M\otimes A\rightarrow M
satisfying

\displaystyle l(ab,m)=l(a,l(b,m)),~r(l(a,m),b)=l(a,r(m,b))~\text{ and }~r(r(m,a),b)=r(m,ab),

for all
a,b\in A
and
m\in M
. It follows that
A
is a bimodule over the associative algebra
A
itself with the left and right actions given by the algebra multiplication. This is called the adjoint bimodule structure.

A derivation on
A
with values in the bimodule
M
is given by a linear map
\phi:A\rightarrow M
that satisfies

\phi(ab)=r(\phi(a),b)+l(a,\phi(b)),~~~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Derivations are
1
-cocycles in the Hochschild complex of the associative algebra
A
with coefficients in
M

[
16
]
. See also
[
19
]
for more details.
Our main objective in this paper is a pair
(A,\phi_{A})
in which
A
is an associative algebra and
\phi_{A}:A\rightarrow A
is a derivation on
A
with values in the adjoint representation. Thus,
\phi_{A}
satisfies

\displaystyle\phi_{A}(ab)=\phi_{A}(a)b+a\phi_{A}(b),~~~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Such a pair
(A,\phi_{A})
is called an AssDer pair. Here we give few examples of AssDer pairs.

Example 1
.

(i)

The notion of derivations are generalization of the usual derivative of functions. For instance, if
A=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial algebra in
n
variables, then for
1\leq i\leq n
, the partial derivatives
\phi_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
are derivations on
A
. In fact, the space of derivations on
A
is linearly spanned by
\{\phi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}.

(ii)

Any derivation on the space
C^{\infty}(M)
of smooth functions on a manifold
M
is given by a vector field . Therefore
(C^{\infty}(M),X)
is an AssDer pair, for any
X\in\mathcal{X}(M)
- denotes the space of vector fields on
M
.

(iii)

A (non-commutative) Poisson algebra is an associative algebra
P
together with a Lie bracket
\{~,~\}
on it which is a derivation on each entry with respect to the associative product. It follows that if
(P,\{~,~\})
is a (non-commutative) Poisson algebra, then for any
a\in P
, the linear map
\phi_{a}=\{a,~\}
is a derivation for associative product. Hence
(P,\phi_{a})
is an AssDer pair.

(iv)

Let
V
be a vector space and
d:V\rightarrow V
be a linear map. Consider the tensor algebra
{T}(V)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}V^{\otimes n}
(resp. reduced tensor algebra
\overline{T}(V)=\oplus_{n\geq 1}V^{\otimes n}
) with the concatenation product. The linear map
d
induces a linear map

\overline{d}:{T}(V)\rightarrow{T}(V)

(resp.
\overline{d}:\overline{T}(V)\rightarrow\overline{T}(V)
) as follows

\displaystyle\overline{d}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes dv_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}.

It is easy to verify the
\overline{d}
is a derivation on
T(V)
(resp.
\overline{T}(V))
. Hence
({T}(V),{d})
(resp.
(\overline{T}(V),\overline{d})
) is an unital (resp. non-unital) AssDer pair. In fact, any derivation on
T(V)
(resp.
\overline{T}(V))
arises in this way.

Remark 1
.

Let
A
be an unital, commutative associative algebra and
\phi_{A}
be a derivation on
A
. Then
\phi_{A}
induces a Lie bracket on
A
given by

\displaystyle[a,b]_{\phi_{A}}:=a\phi_{A}(b)-\phi_{A}(a)b,~~~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

We denote this Lie algebra by
A_{\phi_{A}}.
This Lie bracket additionally satisfies the following Leibniz rule

\displaystyle[a,fb]_{\phi_{A}}=f[a,b]_{\phi_{A}}+\rho(a)(f)b,~~~\text{ for }a,b,f\in A,

(1)

where
\rho:(A,[~,~]_{\phi_{A}})\rightarrow(\mathrm{Der}(A),[~,~])
is the
A
-linear Lie algebra morphism given by
\rho(a)=a\phi_{A}
. In other words, the triple
(A,A_{\phi_{A}},\rho)
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra
[
17
]
.
†
†

Lie-Rinehart algebras are algebraic analouge of Lie algebroids
[
21
]
. More precisely, a Lie-Rinehart algebra consists of a triple
(A,L,\rho)
where
A
is an associative algebra,
L
a Lie algebra also an
A
-module and an
A
-module map
\rho:L\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(A)
which is a morphism of Lie algebras and the Leibniz identity (

) holds. Thus a Lie algebra

is a Lie-Rinehart algebra in which

Conversely, any Lie-Rinehart algebra structure on
(A,A)
arises by a derivation in the above way. Thus, to better understand Lie-Rinehart algebra structures on
(A,A)
one needs to know derivations on

.
In Remark
4
, we observe that this association is compatible with deformation theories of AssDer pairs and Lie-Rinehart algebras.

Definition 1
.

Let
(A,\phi_{A})
and
(B,\phi_{B})
be two AssDer pairs. A morphism between them consists of an algebra map
f:A\rightarrow B
that commute with derivations, i.e.

f\circ\phi_{A}=\phi_{B}\circ f.

We denote the category of AssDer pairs together with morphisms between them by
AssDer
.

Let
(V,d)
be a vector space together with a linear map. The free AssDer pair over
(V,d)
is an AssDer pair
(\mathcal{F}(V),\phi_{\mathcal{F}(V)})
equipped with a linear map

that satisfies
\phi_{\mathcal{F}(V)}\circ i=i\circ d
and the following universal condition holds:

For any AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})
and a linear map
f:V\rightarrow A
satisfying
\phi_{A}\circ f=f\circ d
, there exists an unique AssDer pair morphism
\widetilde{f}:(\mathcal{F}(V),\phi_{\mathcal{F}(V)})\rightarrow(A,\phi_{A})
such that
\widetilde{f}\circ i=f.

It follows that the free AssDer pair over
(V,d)
is well-defined up to a unique isomorphism.

Definition 2

Let
(A,\phi_{A})
be an AssDer pair. A left module over it consists of a pair
(M,\phi_{M})
in which

is a left

\displaystyle\phi_{M}(am)=\phi_{A}(a)m+a\phi_{M}(m).

(2)

Similarly, a right module over

is a pair
(M,\phi_{M})
in which

\displaystyle\phi_{M}(ma)=\phi_{M}(m)a+m\phi_{A}(a).

A bimodule (representation) over

which is both a left module and a right module over

and

for all

It follows that the AssDer pair

is a representation of itself for the adjoint bimodule structure on

Proposition 2
.

Let
(M,\phi_{M})
be a representation of the AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})
. Then
(M^{*},-\phi_{M}^{*})
is also a representation of

where the

is given by

\displaystyle A\otimes M^{*}\rightarrow M^{*}\qquad\qquad\qquad M^{*}\otimes A\rightarrow M^{*}

\displaystyle(af)(m)=f(ma)~~~\qquad\qquad\quad(fa)(m)=f(am),

for
a\in A,f\in M^{*}
and

Proof.

The fact that
M^{*}
is an
A
-bimodule is standard
[
19
]
. To verify that
(M^{*},-\phi_{M}^{*})
is a representation of the AssDer pair, we observe that

\displaystyle\langle-\phi_{M}^{*}(af),m\rangle=\langle af,-\phi_{M}(m)\rangle=~

\displaystyle f(-\phi_{M}(m)a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle f(m\phi_{A}(a))-f(\phi_{M}(ma))\qquad(\text{by }~~(\ref{rep-2-eqn}))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{A}(a)f,m\rangle-\langle a\phi_{M}^{*}(f),m\rangle.

This shows that
-\phi_{M}^{*}(af)=\phi_{A}(a)f+a(-\phi_{M}^{*}(f)).
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\langle-\phi_{M}^{*}(fa),m\rangle=\langle fa,-\phi_{M}(m)\rangle=~

\displaystyle f(-a\phi_{M}(m))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle f(\phi_{A}(a)m)-f(\phi_{M}(am))\qquad(\text{by }~~(\ref{rep-1-eqn}))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\langle f\phi_{A}(a),m\rangle-\langle\phi_{M}^{*}(f)a,m\rangle.

This shows that
-\phi_{M}^{*}(fa)=-\phi_{M}^{*}(f)a+f\phi_{A}(a)
. Hence the result follows.
∎

Thus by above Proposition,

is a representation of the AssDer pair

. It is called the coadjoint representation of the AssDer pair

Given an associative algebra and a bimodule over it, one can construct a semi-direct product associative algebra
[
19
]
. This result can be extended to AssDer pairs.

Proposition 3
.

Let
(A,\phi_{A})
be an
{AssDer}
pair and
(M,\phi_{M})
be a representation of it. Then
(A\oplus M,\phi_{A}\oplus\phi_{M})
is an
{AssDer}
pair where the associative structure on
A\oplus M
is given by the semi-direct product

\displaystyle(a,m)\cdot(b,n)=(ab,an+mb).

Proof.

It is known that
A\oplus M
with the above product is an associative algebra. Thus, it is enough to show that
\phi_{A}\oplus\phi_{M}:A\oplus M\rightarrow A\oplus M
is a derivation. Observe that

\displaystyle(\phi_{A}\oplus\phi_{M})((a,m)\cdot(b,n))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\phi_{A}(ab),\phi_{M}(an)+\phi_{M}(mb))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\phi_{A}(a)b+a\phi_{A}(b),\phi_{A}(a)n+a\phi_{M}(n)+\phi_{M}(m)b+m\phi_{A}(b))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\phi_{A}(a)b,\phi_{A}(a)n+\phi_{M}(m)b)~+~(a\phi_{A}(b),a\phi_{M}(n)+m\phi_{A}(b))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\phi_{A}(a),\phi_{M}(m))\cdot(b,n)~+~(a,m)\cdot(\phi_{A}(b)\oplus\phi_{M}(n))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\phi_{A}\oplus\phi_{M})(a,m)\cdot(b,n)~+~(a,m)\cdot(\phi_{A}\oplus\phi_{M})(b,n).

Hence the proof.
∎

Let
A
be an associative algebra and

be an

\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}:C^{n}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}(A,M)
is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})

(4)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle a_{1}f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}~f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},a_{i}a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})a_{n+1}.

(5)

The corresponding cohomology groups are denoted by

.

\displaystyle[f,g]:=f\circ g-(-1)^{(m-1)(n-1)}g\circ f,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{(i-1)(n-1)}f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g(a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),\ldots,a_{m+n-1}),

for

and

\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f=(-1)^{n-1}[\mu,f],~~~~\text{ for }f\in C^{n}(A,A).

Before we introduce the coboundary operator, we define a new map

\delta f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_{A}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{id})-\phi_{M}\circ f.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial f=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f,-\delta f),~~~~\text{ for }f\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,M)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,M),\\

(7)

To prove that

Lemma 1
.

\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\circ\delta=\delta\circ\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}.

Proposition 4
.

The map

.

Proof.

For

\displaystyle\partial^{2}f=\partial(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f,-\delta f)=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{2}f,~-\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\delta f+\delta\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)=0.

For

\displaystyle\partial^{2}(f_{n},\overline{f}_{n})=~

\displaystyle\partial(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f_{n},~\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\overline{f}_{n}+(-1)^{n}\delta f_{n})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{2}f_{n},~\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{2}\overline{f}_{n}+(-1)^{n}\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\delta f_{n}+(-1)^{n+1}\delta\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f_{n})=0.

Hence the result follows.

Proof.

First note that, since

Remark 4
.

Let
(A,\phi_{A})
be an AssDer pair in which

Next, we discuss an equivalence between two formal deformations of an AssDer pair.

Definition 8
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\phi_{t})
and
(\mu_{t}^{\prime},\phi_{t}^{\prime})
be two formal deformations of an AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})

\Phi_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\Phi_{i}:A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]]

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\mu_{t}=~\mu_{t}^{\prime}\circ(\Phi_{t}\otimes\Phi_{t})\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi_{t}\circ\phi_{t}=~\phi_{t}^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{t}.

It follows that the following identities must hold (by equating coefficients of

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\Phi_{i}\circ\mu_{j}=~\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}^{\prime}\circ(\Phi_{j}\otimes\Phi_{k})\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i+j=n}\Phi_{i}\circ\phi_{j}=~\sum_{i+j=n}\phi_{i}^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{j}.

For

\displaystyle\mu_{1}+\Phi_{1}\circ\mu=~

\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{\prime}+\mu\circ(\Phi_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})+\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi_{1}),

\displaystyle\phi_{1}+\Phi_{1}\circ\phi_{A}=~

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{\prime}+\phi_{A}\circ\Phi.

This implies that
(\mu_{1},\phi_{1})-(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\phi_{1}^{\prime})=\partial(\Phi_{1}).

Thus we have the following.

Theorem 5
.

The infinitesimals corresponding to equivalent deformations of an AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})
are cohomologous. Therefore, they correspond to the same cohomology class.

To obtain a one-to-one correspondence between the second cohomology group

Definition 9

An infinitesimal deformation of an AssDer pair

(A,\phi_{A})
over
\mathbb{K}[[t]]/(t^{2})~~

(\mu_{1},\phi_{1})
is a
2

Theorem 6
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the space of equivalence classes of infinitesimal deformations of the AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})
and the second cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A).

Proof.

It is already shown that the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{infinitesimal~deformations}/\sim~\rightarrow~H^{2}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)~\text{ given by }~[(\mu_{t},\phi_{t})]\mapsto[(\mu_{1},\phi_{1})]

is well defined. This map is bijective with the inverse map given as follows. For any
2

(\mu_{1},\phi_{1})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)

(\mu_{1},\phi_{1})-(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\phi_{1}^{\prime})=\partial h,

Definition 10

A formal deformation

\mathrm{AssDer}

Theorem 7
.

If

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t},\phi_{t})
be any formal
1

that the linear term
(\mu_{1},\phi_{1})
is a

such that

(\mu_{1},\phi_{1})=\partial\Phi_{1}.

Setting

and define

\displaystyle\mu_{t}^{\prime}=\Phi_{t}^{-1}\circ\mu_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\otimes\Phi_{t}),\qquad\phi_{t}^{\prime}=\Phi_{t}^{-1}\circ\phi_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}.

(34)

Then

Remark 5
.

An AssDer pair

5.1
Extensions of finite order deformation

In this subsection, we consider deformations of order
n
of an AssDer pair.
To any deformation of order
n
, we
associate a
3
-cocycle in the cohomology of the AssDer pair with coefficients in itself. We show that such a deformation extends to deformation of order
(n+1)
if and only if the corresponding cohomology class is trivial.

Let
(A,\phi_{A})
be an AssDer pair. Consider the
\mathbb{K}[[t]]/(t^{n+1})
-module
A[[t]]/(t^{n+1})
. A deformation of order
n
of the AssDer
(A,\phi_{A})
consists of a pair
(\mu_{t},\phi_{t})
where
\mu_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\mu_{i}
and
\phi_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\phi_{i}
such that
\mu_{t}
defines an associative product on
A[[t]]/(t^{n+1})
and
\phi_{t}
defines a derivation on it.

Thus, in a deformation of order
n
, the following identities must hold

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\mu_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c)),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\phi_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\mu_{i}(\phi_{j}(a),b)+\mu_{i}(a,\phi_{j}(b)),

for
k=0,1,\ldots,n.
In other words,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=k,i,j>0}[\mu_{i},\mu_{j}]=~

\displaystyle-[\mu,\mu_{k}]~~\mbox{and}

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k,i,j>0}[\phi_{i},\mu_{j}]=~

\displaystyle-[\phi_{A},\mu_{k}]+[\mu,\phi_{k}].

(36)

Let
(\mu_{n+1},\phi_{n+1})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)
be such that
(\mu_{t}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\mu_{i}+t^{n+1}\mu_{n+1},~\phi_{t}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\phi_{i}+t^{n+1}\phi_{n+1})
defines a deformation of order
n+1
.
Then the deformation
(\mu_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\mu_{i},\phi_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\phi_{i})
is said to be extensible.
In such a case, two more deformation equations need to be satisfied, namely,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\mu_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c))~\mbox{and}

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\phi_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\mu_{i}(\phi_{j}(a),b)+\mu_{i}(a,\phi_{j}(b)).

(38)

The identities (
37
) and (
38
) can be written as

\begin{split}\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mu_{n+1})(a,b,c)=~&\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c)-\mu_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c))\\
&(=\mathrm{Ob}^{3}(a,b,c)~\mathrm{say})\\
(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\phi_{n+1}+\delta\mu_{n+1})(a,b)=~&\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}\phi_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b))-\mu_{i}(\phi_{j}(a),b)-\mu_{i}(a,\phi_{j}(b))\\
&(=\mathrm{Ob}^{2}(a,b)~\mathrm{say})\end{split}

Note that, in terms of the Gerstenhaber bracket (
6
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ob}^{3}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}[\mu_{i},\mu_{j}]~~~\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\mathrm{Ob}^{2}=\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}[\phi_{i},\mu_{j}].

Proposition 13
.

The pair
(\mathrm{Ob}^{3},\mathrm{Ob}^{2})
is a
3
-cocycle in the cohomology of the AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})
with coefficients in itself.

Proof.

It is known from the finite order deformations of associative algebras
[
15
]
, the obstruction
\mathrm{Ob}^{3}
is a Hochschild
3
-cocycle in the cohomology of
A
, i.e.
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mathrm{Ob}^{3})=0
. Moreover, we have

\begin{split}&\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mathrm{Ob}^{2})+(-1)^{3}~\delta(\mathrm{Ob}^{3})\\
&=-[\mu,\mathrm{Ob}^{2}]+[\phi_{A},\mathrm{Ob}^{3}]\\
&=-\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}\big([[\mu,\phi_{i}],\mu_{j}]+[\phi_{i},[\mu,\mu_{j}]]\big)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}\big([[\phi_{A},\mu_{i}],\mu_{j}]+[\mu_{i},[\phi_{A},\mu_{j}]]\big)\\
&=-\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}\big([[\mu,\phi_{i}],\mu_{j}]+[\phi_{i},[\mu,\mu_{j}]]\big)+\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}[[\phi_{A},\mu_{i}],\mu_{j}]\\
&=-\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}{i+j=n+1},\\
{i,j>0}\end{array}}\big([[\mu,\phi_{i}],\mu_{j}]-[[\phi_{A},\mu_{i}],\mu_{j}])+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}{i+j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}=n+1},\\
{i,j^{\prime},j^{\prime\prime}>0}\end{array}}[\phi_{i},[\mu_{j}^{\prime},\mu_{j}^{\prime\prime}]](\mathrm{by}~~(\ref{n-def-eqn-1}))\\
&=-\sum_{i^{\prime}+i^{\prime\prime}+j=n+1,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime},j>0}[[\phi_{i^{\prime}},\mu_{i^{\prime\prime}}],\mu_{j}]-\sum_{i+j=n+1,i,j>0}([[\phi_{A},\mu_{i}],\mu_{j}]-[[\phi_{A},\mu_{i}],\mu_{j}])\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}=n+1,i,j^{\prime},j^{\prime\prime}>0}([[\phi_{i},\mu_{j^{\prime}}],\mu_{j^{\prime\prime}}]+[\mu_{j^{\prime}},[\phi_{i},\mu_{j^{\prime\prime}}]])(\mathrm{by}~~(\ref{n-def-eqn-2}))\\
&=-\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}{i^{\prime}+i^{\prime\prime}+j=n+1},\\
{i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime},j>0}\end{array}}[[\phi_{i^{\prime}},\mu_{i^{\prime\prime}}],\mu_{j}]+\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}{i+j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}=n+1},\\
{i,j^{\prime},j^{\prime\prime}\geq 0}\end{array}}[[\phi_{i},\mu_{j^{\prime}}],\mu_{j^{\prime\prime}}]=0.\end{split}

Thus,

\partial(\mathrm{Ob}^{3},\mathrm{Ob}^{2})=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mathrm{Ob}^{3}),\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mathrm{Ob}^{2})+(-1)^{3}~\delta(\mathrm{Ob}^{3}))=0.

∎

Therefore,
(\mathrm{Ob}^{3},\mathrm{Ob}^{2})
defines a cohomology class in
H^{3}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)
. If this cohomology class vanishes, i.e
(\mathrm{Ob}^{3},\mathrm{Ob}^{2})
is a coboundary, then we have

\displaystyle\partial(\mu_{n+1},\phi_{n+1})=(\mathrm{Ob}^{3},\mathrm{Ob}^{2}),

for some
(\mu_{n+1},\phi_{n+1})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)
. In such a case
(\mu_{t}^{\prime}=\mu_{t}+t^{n+1}\mu_{n+1},\phi_{t}^{\prime}=\phi_{t}+t^{n+1}\phi_{n+1})
defines a deformation of order
n+1
. Therefore, the deformation
(\mu_{t},\phi_{t})
becomes extensible. On the other hand, if
(\mu_{t},\phi_{t})
is extensible, there exists
(\mu_{n+1},\phi_{n+1})\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)
such that
(\mu_{t}^{\prime}=\mu_{t}+t^{n+1}\mu_{n+1},\phi_{t}^{\prime}=\phi_{t}+t^{n+1}\phi_{n+1})
is a deformation of order
n+1
. Hence the obstruction
(\mathrm{Ob}^{3},\mathrm{Ob}^{2})
is given by the coboundary
\partial(\mu_{n+1},\phi_{n+1})
. Thus the corresponding cohomology class is null. Therefore, we obtain the following.

Theorem 8
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\phi_{t})
be a deformation of order
n
of the AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})
. It is extensible if and only if the obstruction class
[(\mathrm{Ob}^{3},\mathrm{Ob}^{2})]
vanishes.

Theorem 9
.

If
H^{3}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)=0
then every finite order deformation of the AssDer pair
(A,\phi_{A})
extends to a deformation of next order.

Corollary 2
.

If
H^{3}_{\mathrm{AssDer}}(A,A)=0
then every
2
-cocycle is the infinitesimal of a formal deformation of
(A,\phi_{A})
.

6
Homotopy derivations on
A_{\infty}
-algebras

In this section, we are interested in homotopy associative algebras (
A_{\infty}
-algebras
[

) with homotopy derivations. Note that homotopy derivation on

-algebras were studied by Loday
[
20

and further developed by Doubek-Lada
[

. However, we will be most interested in
A_{\infty}
-algebras whose underlying graded vector space is concentrated in degrees
0
and

Definition 11
.

A
2
-term
A_{\infty}
-algebra consists of a chain complex
A:=(A_{1}\xrightarrow{d}A_{0})
together with maps
\mu_{2}:A_{i}\otimes A_{j}\rightarrow A_{i+j}

and a map
\mu_{3}:A_{0}\otimes A_{0}\otimes A_{0}\rightarrow A_{1}
satisfying the followings: for any

and

,

(a)

d\mu_{2}(a,m)=\mu_{2}(a,dm)
,

(b)

d\mu_{2}(m,a)=\mu_{2}(dm,a)
,

(c)

\mu_{2}(dm,n)=\mu_{2}(m,dn)
,

(d)

d\mu_{3}(a,b,c)=\mu_{2}\big(\mu_{2}(a,b),c\big)-\mu_{2}\big(a,\mu_{2}(b,c)\big)
,

(e1)

\mu_{3}(a,b,dm)=\mu_{2}\big(\mu_{2}(a,b),m\big)-\mu_{2}\big(a,\mu_{2}(b,m)\big),

(e2)

\mu_{3}(a,dm,c)=\mu_{2}\big(\mu_{2}(a,m),c\big)-\mu_{2}\big(a,\mu_{2}(m,c)\big)
,

(e3)

\mu_{3}(dm,b,c)=\mu_{2}\big(\mu_{2}(m,b),c\big)-\mu_{2}\big(m,\mu_{2}(b,c)\big)

(f)

\mu_{3}\big(\mu_{2}(a,b),c,e\big)-\mu_{3}\big(a,\mu_{2}(b,c),e\big)+\mu_{3}\big(a,b,\mu_{2}(c,e)\big)=\mu_{2}\big(\mu_{3}(a,b,c),e\big)+\mu_{2}\big(a,\mu_{3}(b,c,e)\big).

A
2
-term
A_{\infty}
-algebra as above may be denoted by
(A_{1}\xrightarrow{d}A_{0},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
. When
A_{1}=0
, one simply get an associative algebra structure on

with the multiplication given by
\mu_{2}:A_{0}\otimes A_{0}\rightarrow A_{0}.

A
2
-term
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(A_{1}\xrightarrow{d}A_{0},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})

-cocycles of associative algebras. There is a one-to-one correspondence between skeletal algebras and triples
(A,M,\theta),
where
A
is an associative algebra,
M
is an
A
-bimodule and
\theta
is a Hochschild
3
-cocycle of
A
with coefficients in

[
9
]
. More precisely, let
(A_{1}\xrightarrow{0}A_{0},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
be a skeletal algebra. Then
(A_{0},\mu_{2})
is an associative algebra,
A_{1}
is an
A_{0}
-bimodule by

and

A_{0}

Definition 12
.

Let
(A_{1}\xrightarrow{d}A_{0},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})

(A_{1}^{\prime},\xrightarrow{d^{\prime}}A_{0}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime},\mu_{3}^{\prime})
be

(which consists of linear maps

such that for any

and

, the following conditions hold

(a)

(b)

f_{2}(a,dm)=f_{1}(\mu_{2}(a,m))-\mu_{2}^{\prime}(f_{0}(a),f_{1}(m)),

(c)

f_{2}(dm,a)=f_{1}(\mu_{2}(m,a))-\mu_{2}^{\prime}(f_{1}(m),f_{0}(a))

Definition 13

Let

Proposition 14
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between skeletal

Let
(A_{1}\xrightarrow{0}A_{0},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})

Definition 14

A

Example 2

Let
(A,\phi_{A})

Conclusions.

In this paper, we mainly concentrate on a pair of an associative algebra and a derivation on it. We call such a pair an AssDer pair. Among other things, we study extensions and deformations of an AssDer pair by extending the classical extensions and deformations of associative algebras. For this, we define a cohomology theory for AssDer pairs. This cohomology is based on the Hochschild cohomology of associative algebras.

In
[
10
]
the author study deformation of multiplications in an operad which generalizes the deformation of associative algebras. As applications, the author formulates deformation of various Loday-type (e.g. dendriform, tridendriform, dialgebra, quadri) algebras. See
[
10
,
29
]
for cohomology of Loday-type algebras. Given an operad
\mathcal{O}
with a multiplication
m\in\mathcal{O}(2)
(i.e.
m
satisfies
m\circ_{1}m=m\circ_{2}m
), an element
\phi\in\mathcal{O}(1)
is called a derivation for
m
if
\phi
satisfies

\phi\circ m=m\circ_{1}\phi+m\circ_{2}\phi.

By the method of the present paper, one may study deformations of a pair
(m,\phi)
where
m
is a multiplication on
\mathcal{O}
and
\phi
is a derivation for
m
. Therefore, one may deduce deformations of Loday-type algebras equipped with derivations.

In
[
5
]
Balavoine studied deformations of algebras over quadratic operads. Derivations on an algebra over a quadratic operad are studied in
[
20
,
11
]
. Given a quadratic operad
\mathcal{P}
, they construct a new operad
\mathcal{DP}
whose algebras are
\mathcal{P}
-algebras equipped with derivations. Therefore, deformations of algebras over
\mathcal{DP}
are deformations of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras with derivations. Motivated from this, in a subsequent paper, we aim to construct an explicit cohomology and deformation theory for
\mathcal{P}
-algebras equipped with derivations.

The results of the present paper can be dualized to study deformations of coalgebras with coderivations. Since an
A_{\infty}
-algebra can be described by a square-zero coderivation on the tensor coalgebra of a graded vector space, one can explore formal deformations of
A_{\infty}
-algebras and compare with the results of
[
12
]
.

In
[
8
]
the authors consider the deformation problem of a Lie algebroid
A
. Such deformation is governed by a (shifted) graded Lie algebra on the space of multiderivations on
A
. An
1
-cocycle of the corresponding complex is given by a Lie algebroid derivation on
A
. A derivation of the underlying vector bundle
A
is a Lie algebroid derivation if it is also a derivation for the Lie bracket on
A
. For any

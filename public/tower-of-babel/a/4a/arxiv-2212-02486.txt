Critical Fubini-Study metrics over non-archimedean field
Yanbo Fang

Abstract
. Over a non-archimedean local place, the height of a projective variety with respect to a very ample line bundle equipped with a Fubini-Study metric is related to the naive height of its Chow form. Using a non-Archimedean Kempf-Ness criteria, we characterize Fubini-Study metrics that minimize the height under the special linear action in terms of their Monge-Ampère polytopes. This polytope can be constructed either as its non-Archimedean Bergman functional or as the weight polytope for the residual action on its Chow form; it is associated with a polymatroid.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be a rank one torus compatible with
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert

\underline{e}
and corresponding action weight
\lambda
. Then for

and

it holds that

\phi_{\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{t}}(x)-\phi_{\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{0}}(x)=-\log~(\lVert\hat{x}\rVert^{\vee}_{0}/\lVert\hat{x}\rVert^{\vee}_{t})=t\cdot b_{\underline{e}}(x)[\lambda].

Proof.

Choose an affine lift
\hat{x}
for
x
and any
s\in E
with

, one has

\phi_{\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert^{\vee}_{t}}(x)-\phi_{\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert^{\vee}_{0}}(x)=\log(\lvert s(\hat{x})\rvert/\lVert\hat{x}\rVert^{\vee}_{t})-\log(\lvert s(\hat{x})\rvert/\lVert\hat{x}\rVert^{\vee}_{0})=-\log~(\lVert\hat{x}_{t}\rVert^{\vee}/\lVert\hat{x}\rVert^{\vee}_{0}).

Write

, the choice of orthonormal coordinates implies that

\begin{split}&\log~(\lVert\hat{x}\rVert^{\vee}_{t}/\lVert\hat{x}\rVert^{\vee}_{0})=\log~(\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\lvert a_{i}\rvert\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{i}t}\}/\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\lvert a_{i}\rvert\})\\

∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume that
(X,O(1))
is Chow stable. A diagonalizable Fubini-Study metric
\phi_{\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert}
is critical if and only if
\boldsymbol{0}\in P_{MA}(X,\lVert\cdot\rVert)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be a rank one torus of
\mathbb{S}\mathbb{L}(n+1)
compatible with
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert
, choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
for
\lVert\cdot\rVert
that diagonalizes
\mathfrak{t}
, by the continuity of metric on the intersection line bundle in each of its argument, the variation of Chow height is (write
\phi^{((k))}
for the
k
-fold repetition of
\phi
in
\langle\dots\rangle
)

\begin{split}&\langle\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{t}}\rangle^{\langle d+1\rangle}-\langle\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}}\rangle^{\langle d+1\rangle}\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{d}\langle\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{t}}-\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}},\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{t}}^{((d-k))},\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}}^{((k))}\rangle\\
=&(d+1)\langle\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{t}}-\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}},\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}}^{((d))}\rangle+o(t^{2})\\
=&t\cdot(d+1)\langle b_{\underline{e}}(x)[\lambda],\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}}^{((d))}\rangle+o(t^{2})\end{split}

By properties of intersection pairing metric and of Monge-Ampère measure,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}|_{0^{+}}\langle\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{t}}\rangle^{\langle d+1\rangle}&=(d+1)\int_{X^{\an}}b_{\underline{e}}(x)[\lambda]\cdot c_{1}(\phi_{\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}})^{\wedge d}\\
&=(d+1)\sum_{a\in\Sh}\mu_{a}\cdot b_{\underline{e}}(\gamma_{a})[\lambda].\end{split}

By convex analysis, the last term can be expressed as

\sum_{a\in\Sh}\mu_{a}\cdot b_{\underline{e}}(\gamma_{a})[\lambda]=\sum_{a\in\Sh}\max_{\mu_{a}\Delta_{I(a)}}\{-\lambda(\mathord{\cdot})\}=\max_{\sum_{a\in\Sh}\mu_{a}\Delta_{I(a)}}\{-\underline{\lambda}(\mathord{\cdot})\}=\max_{P_{\MA}(\lVert\cdot\rVert)}\{-\lambda(\mathord{\cdot})\}.

That
\phi_{\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert}
is critical, namely the above variation being non-negative for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\sum\lambda_{i}=0
, is equivalent to the containment
\boldsymbol{0}\in P_{MA}(X,\lVert\cdot\rVert,\underline{e})
.
∎

3.1.

(see

We shall make use of tropical forms and currents on
\mathbb{R}
-affine spaces, more precisely the calculus of
\delta
-forms; in this article only the constant-weighted polyhedral complexes and piecewise-linear functions appear, as all our metric objects actually come from integral models.

A tropical cycle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a locally finite polyhedral complex
\mathcal{C}
(of pure dimension
d
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a collection of (constant-)weights
\{\mu_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}_{(d)}}
satisfying the balancing condition at each facet. There is an intersection product
\cdot
for tropical cycles using the stable tropical intersection which can be calculated using the fan displacement rule. Pull-back satisfies the graph construction and direct image satisfies projection formula: for an affine map
F:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and tropical cycles
C
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
D
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, write
\pi_{i}
the projections from
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it holds that

F^{*}(C)=(\pi_{1})_{*}\{(\pi_{2})^{*}(C)\cdot\mathbf{\Gamma}_{F}\},\quad F_{*}\{F^{*}C\cdot D\}=F_{*}(D)\cdot C.

A
\delta
-form
B
is a combination
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}\alpha_{\sigma}\wedge\delta_{[\sigma,\mu_{\sigma}]}
for a collection of (Lagerberg) (super-)forms
\{\alpha_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}
on weighted polyhedra
\{[\sigma,\mu_{\sigma}]\}_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}
that satisfy the balancing condition at each facet. Viewed as a (super-)current
[B]
by integration, they can be characterized by the condition that both
[B]
and its differentials
d^{\prime}[B]
and
d^{\prime\prime}[B]
are polyhedral
[
Mih
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Pull-back and push-forward operations exists. There is a graded-commutative
\wedge
-product on
\delta
-forms that extends the
\wedge
-product of smooth forms and the stable intersection product of tropical cycles. Pull-back satisfies the graph construction and push-forwards satisfies the projection formula: for an affine map
F:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta
-forms
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
T
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}

F^{*}(S)=(\pi_{1})_{*}\{(\pi_{2})^{*}(S)\wedge\delta_{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{F}}\},\quad S\wedge F_{*}(T)=F_{*}\{F^{*}(S)\wedge T\}.

There are differential operators
d
with components
d^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime\prime}
on
\delta
-forms viewed as (super-)currents acting by integration on (super-)forms. In particular, for a piecewise linear function
\mathbf{f}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote by
[\mathbf{f}]
the
(n,n)
-current
\mathbf{f}\wedge[\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu_{\mathrm{st}}]

-current is given by

\delta
-form.

Proposition 3.2

The tropical Chow hypersurfaces are given by

\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbf{X})=(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{2})_{*}\{(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{1})^{*}\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{I}_{i,\iota}\}=\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}(\mathbf{X}_{\iota})\boxminus\prod_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{H}_{i},

Proof.

First calculate the incidence intersection using graph formula for pull-back:

\begin{split}(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{1})^{*}\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{I}_{i,\iota}&=(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{1})^{*}\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\cdot(+)_{\mathbf{12}}^{*}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbf{H}_{i}\\

&=(\mathbf{p}_{12})_{*}\{(\mathbf{p}_{12})^{*}(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{1})^{*}\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\cdot(\mathbf{p}_{3})^{*}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbf{H}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{\Gamma}_{(+)_{\mathbf{12}}}\}\\

Then calculate the correspondence transform using projection formula for push-forward:

\begin{split}\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbf{X})&=(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{2})_{*}\{(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{1})^{*}\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{I}_{i,\iota}\}\\
&=(\mathbf{p}^{12}_{2})_{*}\{(\mathbf{p}_{12})_{*}((\mathbf{p}_{1})^{*}\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\cdot(\mathbf{p}_{3})^{*}\prod_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{H}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{\Gamma}_{(-)_{\mathbf{13}}})\}\\

&=\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\boxminus\prod_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{H}_{i},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\mathbf{\Delta}^{*}\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbf{X})&=\mathbf{\Delta}^{*}(\mathbf{\Delta}_{*}\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\boxminus\prod_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{H}_{i})=\mathbf{X}_{\iota}\boxminus\mathbf{H}^{\cdot(d+1)}.\end{split}

Lemma 3.7
.

For the
\delta
-form
\mathbf{u}\wedge\delta_{\mathbf{H}^{\cdot(d)}}
, it holds that

\partial_{\tau}(\mathbf{u}\wedge\delta_{\mathbf{H}^{\cdot(d)}})=(d+1)(\partial_{\tau}\mathbf{u})\wedge\delta_{\mathbf{H}^{\cdot(d)}}.

Proof.

By the continuity of wedge product for
\delta
-forms and the integration by part formula, one has

\begin{split}&\tau^{*}(\mathbf{u}(\dd\mathbf{u})^{\wedge d})=\mathbf{u}_{\tau}(\dd\mathbf{u}_{\tau})^{\wedge d}\\
&=(\mathbf{u}_{\tau}-\mathbf{u})(\dd\mathbf{u}_{\tau})^{\wedge d}+\mathbf{u}\dd(\mathbf{u}_{\tau}-\mathbf{u})\wedge\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(\dd\mathbf{u}_{\tau})^{\wedge d-1-l}\wedge(\dd\mathbf{u})^{l}\\
&=(\mathbf{u}_{\tau}-\mathbf{u})(\dd\mathbf{u}_{\tau})^{\wedge d}+(\mathbf{u}_{\tau}-\mathbf{u})\dd\mathbf{u}\wedge\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(\dd\mathbf{u}_{\tau})^{\wedge d-1-l}\wedge(\dd\mathbf{u})^{l}\\
&=\tau(d+1)\mathbf{u}(\dd\mathbf{u})^{\wedge d}+o(|\tau|),\end{split}

hence the desired identity holds as
\dd\mathbf{u}=\delta_{\mathbf{H}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.8

The following equalities hold

\partial_{\tau}\mathbf{\Delta}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{X}}=\delta_{\mathbf{X}_{\iota}}\boxminus(-\partial_{\tau}\mathbf{u})\delta_{\mathbf{H}^{\cdot(d)}},\quad\partial\mathbf{\Delta}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{X}}(\boldsymbol{0})=(-)^{*}(d+1)\sum_{a\in\Sh}\lambda_{a}\triangle_{I(a)}.

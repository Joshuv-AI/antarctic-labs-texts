Markov Processes and Stochastic Extrinsic Derivative Flows on the Space of Absolutely Continuous Measures
Panpan Ren, Feng-Yu Wang, Simon Wittmann

Abstract
Let 𝕄 ac {\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}} (resp. 𝒫 ac {\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}} ) be the class of finite (resp. probability) measures absolutely continuous with respect to a σ \sigma -finite Radon measure on a Polish space. We present a criterion on the quasi-regularity of Dirichlet forms on 𝕄 ac {\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}} and 𝒫 ac {\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}} respectively, in terms of upper bound conditions given by the uniform ( L 1 + L ∞ ) (L^{1}+L^{\infty}) -norm of the extrinsic derivative, see Theorem 2.2 . As applications, we construct a class of general type Markov processes on 𝕄 ac {\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}} and 𝒫 ac {\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}} via quasi-regular Dirichlet forms containing the diffusion, jump and killing terms, see Corollary 3.7 . Moreover, stochastic extrinsic derivative flows on 𝕄 ac {\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}} and 𝒫 ac {\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}} are studied by using quasi-regular Dirichlet forms, which in particular provide martingale solutions to SDEs on these two spaces, with drifts given by the extrinsic derivative of entropy functionals, see Example 4.7 and Example 4.9 .

Introduction

To develop stochastic analysis on the space of measures, it is crucial to construct reasonable measure-valued processes which may play the role of Brownian motion to drive SDEs.
By constructing (quasi-)regular local Dirichlet forms in the
L^{2}
-space of reference probability measures supported on singular distributions,
several type measure-valued diffusion processes have been constructed in the literature, see
[
15
,
18
,
19
,
10
,
25
,
23
,
24
]
and references therein.

Recently, O-U type Dirichlet forms was introduced in
[
21
]
by using the integral of intrinsic derivative with respect to Gaussian type measures on
2
-Wasserstein space,
and in our previous joint work
[
22
]
we proved the quasi-regularity with extensions to
p
-Wasserstein spaces over Banach spaces, for
p\geq 1
.
From analysis point of view, it is natural to ask for diffusion processes on the space of absolutely continuous distributions, and to solve SDEs
for density functions driven by these processes as noises.

In this paper, we fix a reference measure
\lambda
on the base space, and link the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\lambda)
to the space of finite (respectively, probability) measures by using the map

(1.1)

L^{2}(\lambda)\ni f\mapsto f^{2}\lambda\,\Big(\text{respectively, }\ \frac{f^{2}\lambda}{\lambda(f^{2})}\ \text{for}\ f\neq 0\Big),

where and in the following,
f^{2}\lambda
is the measure with
\frac{\,\text{\rm{d}}(f^{2}\lambda)}{\,\text{\rm{d}}\lambda}=f^{2}.

It turns out that under this map the Fréchet derivative in
L^{2}(\lambda)
corresponds to the extrinsic derivative of measures introduced in
[
18
]
. This observation indicates us to study quasi-regular Dirichlet forms and SDEs
on the space of absolutely continuous measures, by using the extrinsic derivative, as a counterpart of
[
22
]
where the intrinsic derivative is adopted to construct quasi-regular Dirichlet forms on Wasserstein spaces.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a Polish space,
let
\mathbb{M}
be the set of finite Borel measures on
M
, and let

\mathscr{P}
be the class of Borel probability measures on
M
. Both
\mathbb{M}
and
\mathscr{P}
are complete under the total variation distance

\rho_{var}(\mu,\nu):=|\mu-\nu|(M),

where
|\mu-\nu|:=(\mu-\nu)^{+}+(\mu-\nu)^{-}
is the total variation of
\mu-\nu.

For a fixed reference measure
0\neq\lambda
on
M
, which is assumed to be
\sigma
-finite and Radon,
we denote

h_{\mu}:=\frac{\,\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\,\text{\rm{d}}\lambda},\ \ \mu\in\mathbb{M},\ \mu\ll\lambda.

We aim to develop stochastic analysis on the space of absolutely continuous finite measures

{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}:=\big\{\mu\in\mathbb{M}:\ \mu\ll\lambda\big\},

as well as the space of absolutely continuous probability measures

{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}:=\big\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}:\ \mu\ll\lambda\big\}.

Both
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
and
{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
are Polish under the total variation distance, which is reformulated as

(1.2)

\rho_{var}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\lambda(|h_{\mu_{1}}-h_{\mu_{2}}|),\ \ \mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}{\rm\ or\ }{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}},

and
{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
is a closed subspace of
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
. Here and throughout the paper, we simply denote

\lambda(f)=\int_{M}f\,\text{\rm{d}}\lambda

for a measurable function
f
such that the integral exists.

We now recall the (convexity) extrinsic derivative for functions of measures, which goes back to
[
18
]
where the space of discrete measures is concerned.

•

A continuous function
u:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called extrinsically differentiable with extrinsic derivative
D^{\textit{E}}u
, if

D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{u(\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})-u(\mu)}{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}

exists for all
(\mu,x)\in\mathbb{M}\times M
, where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x
. We write
u\in C^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})
, if additionally
D^{\textit{E}}u
is continuous on
\mathbb{M}\times M
. The
set
{C}^{\textit{E},1}_{b}(\mathbb{M})
comprises all
u\in C^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})
with

\sup_{(\mu,x)\in\mathbb{M}\times M}\big\{|u(\mu)|+|D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)(x)|\big\}<\infty.

•

A continuous function
u:\mathscr{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called extrinsically differentiable on
\mathscr{P}
with convexity extrinsic derivative
{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u
, if

{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{u((1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})-u(\mu)}{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}

exists for all
(\mu,x)\in\mathscr{P}\times M
. We write
u\in{C}^{\textit{E},1}_{b}(\mathscr{P})
, if additionally
{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u
is continuous on
\mathscr{P}\times M
with

\sup_{(\mu,x)\in\mathscr{P}\times M}\big\{|u(\mu)|+|{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)(x)|\big\}<\infty.

•

D^{\textit{E}}
and
{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}
are called, respectively, the extrinsic derivative operator and the convexity extrinsic derivative operator.

Regarding a finite measure as the distribution of a particle system, the (convexity) extrinsic derivative describes the rate giving birth a small weighted particle at point
x
(meanwhile killing the same weight uniformly from the system to keep the total mass), while the intrinsic derivative introduced in
[
1
]
refers to the rate of particles moving along vector fields.
The reader shall be referred to
[
5
]
,
[
20
]
and the references therein for applications of the intrinsic, respectively extrinsic, derivative and links of different derivatives for functions of measures.

(respectively,

Of particular interest is the entropy extrinsic derivative flows addressed in Example

and Example

,

Noting that this function

2
A criterion for quasi-regularity

We first recall the definition of quasi-regular Dirichlet form, which can be found in e.g.
[
16
]
, then establish a quasi-regularity criterion for Dirichlet forms on
L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda)
or
L^{2}({\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda)
, for a reference probability measure
\Lambda
on
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
or
{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
.

2.1
Quasi-regular Dirichlet form

Let
(E,\rho_{E})
be a Polish (or slightly more general, Lusin) space, and let
\Lambda
be a probability measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{B}(E)
.

A Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
on
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
is a densely defined bilinear form having the following properties (see
[
16
, Definition I.4.5]
):

\bullet

Positivity:
\mathscr{E}(u,u)\geq 0,u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),

\bullet

Coercivity: there exists a constant
K>0
such that

|\mathscr{E}_{1}(u,v)|:=\big|\mathscr{E}(u,v)+\Lambda(u,v)|\leq K\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{1}(u,u)\mathscr{E}_{1}(v,v)},\ \ u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),

\bullet

Markov property: if
u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),
then
1\land u^{+}\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
and

(2.1)

\mathscr{E}(u+u^{+}\land 1,u-u^{+}\land 1)\geq 0,\ \ \mathscr{E}(u-u^{+}\land 1,u+u^{+}\land 1)\geq 0.

In this paper, we only consider symmetric Dirichlet forms, i.e.

\mathscr{E}(u,v)=\mathscr{E}(v,u),\ \ u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}).

In this case, (
2.1
) is equivalent to
\mathscr{E}(u^{+}\land 1)\leq\mathscr{E}(u),
where we simply denote
\mathscr{E}(u)=\mathscr{E}(u,u),

and
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
is a separable Hilbert space with the inner product
\mathscr{E}_{1}
, so that the
\mathscr{E}_{1}
-norm

\mathscr{E}_{1}(u)^{\frac{1}{2}}:=\big(\Lambda(u^{2})+\mathscr{E}(u,u)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})

is complete.

We repeat some potential theoretic concepts for Dirichlet forms, found in
[
16
, Chapt. III]
, and we always assume
\boldsymbol{1}_{E}\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
.
For further reading on the terminology and statements regarding the local property of Dirichlet forms we refer to
[
16
, Chapt. V]
and
[
7
, Chapt. I]
.
For an open set
O\subseteq E
the
1
-capacity associated to
\mathscr{E}
is defined as

{\rm Cap}_{1}(O):=\inf\big\{\mathscr{E}_{1}(u,u)\,:\,u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),\,u(z)\geq 1\text{ for }\Lambda\text{-a.e.~}z\in O\big\}

with the convention of
\inf(\emptyset):=\infty
. For an arbitrary set
A\subseteq E
, let

{\rm Cap}_{1}(A):=\inf\big\{{\rm Cap}_{1}(O)\,:\,A\subseteq O,\,O\ \text{is\ an open set in }E\big\}.

An
\mathscr{E}
-nest (or nest for short) is a sequence of closed subsets
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
E
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rm Cap}_{1}(E\setminus K_{n})=0.

A measurable function
u:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called quasi-continuous, if there exists a nest
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the restriction
u|_{K_{n}}
is continuous for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

A sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of measurable functions is said to converge quasi-uniformly to a function
u:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, if there exists a nest
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the sequence of restricted functions
u_{k}|_{K_{n}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, converge to
u|_{K_{n}}
uniformly on
K_{n}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

If a property, which an element
z\in E
either has or doesn’t, holds for all
z
in the complement of a set
N\subseteq E
with
{\rm Cap}_{1}(N)=0
, then this property is said to hold quasi-everywhere (q.-e.) on
E
.

For any measurable function
u
on
E
, the smallest closed subset

{\rm supp}[u]:={\rm supp}[|u|\Lambda]

of
E
such that
(|u|\Lambda)(E\setminus{\rm supp}[u])=0,

where
|u|\Lambda
is the measure on
E
with density
|u|
with respect to
\Lambda
,
is well-defined since
E
is Polish and in particular strongly Lindelöf.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
in
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
is called local, if

(2.2)

\mathscr{E}(u,v)=0\quad\text{for }u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\ \text{with}\ {\rm supp}[u]\cap{\rm supp}[v]=\emptyset.

(2)

A continuous bilinear map
\Gamma:\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\times\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\rightarrow L^{1}(E,\Lambda)
is called the square-field operator of
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
, if

\mathscr{E}(u,v)=\int_{E}\Gamma(u,v)\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda,\ \ u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),

(2.3)

\mathscr{E}(uv,u)-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{E}(v,u^{2})=\int_{E}v\Gamma(u,u)\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda,\quad u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\cap L^{\infty}(E,\Lambda).

To verify the local property or the existence of a square-field operator, it is enough to confirm (
2.2
) respectively (
2.3
) on a dense subset of
(\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),\mathscr{E}_{1}^{1/2})
.

Definition 2.2

(Definition IV.3.1 in
[
16
]
)
.

The Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
is called quasi-regular, if the following three conditions hold.

Q1)

There exists an
\mathscr{E}
-nest of compact sets (i.e. the
1
-capacity is tight).

Q2)

(\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),\mathscr{E}_{1}^{1/2})
has a dense subspace consisting of quasi-continuous functions.

Q3)

There exists
N\subseteq M
with
{\rm Cap}_{1}(N)=0
and a
sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
of quasi-continuous functions which
separate points in
E\setminus N
, i.e. for any two different points
z_{1},z_{2}\in E\setminus N
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
u_{n}(z_{1})\neq u_{n}(z_{2})
.

Note that
\mathscr{E}(u):=\mathscr{E}(u,u)
for
u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
can be extended to all
u\in L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
by letting

\mathscr{E}(u):=\infty,\quad\ \ u\in L^{2}(E,\Lambda)\setminus(\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}).

In
[
12
]
a correspondence between regular Dirichlet forms and strong Markov processes is built,
see
[
13
]
for a complete theory and more references. This is extended in
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
to the quasi-regular setting on a Lusin space
(E,\mathscr{B}(E))
. It was observed in
[
8
]
that a quasi-regular Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
becomes regular under one-point compactification.

According to
[
16
, Definitions IV.1.8, IV.1.13, V.1.10]
, a standard Markov process

\mathbf{M}=(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(X_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathbb{P}_{z})_{z\in E})

with natural filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is called a non-terminating diffusion process on
E
if

\mathbb{P}_{z}\big(X_{\cdot}\in C([0,\infty),E)\big)=1\quad\text{for }z\in E.

It is called
\Lambda
-tight for a
\sigma
-finite measure
\Lambda
if there exists a sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n}
of compact sets in
E
such that stopping times

\tau_{n}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\notin K_{n}\},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}

satisfy

\mathbb{P}_{z}\big(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{n}=\infty\big)=1\quad\text{for }\Lambda\text{-a.e.~}z\in E.

The diffusion process is called properly associated with a Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
in
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
, if for any bounded measurable function
u:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
t>0
,

(2.4)

E\ni z\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{z}[u(X_{t})]:=\int_{\Omega}u(X_{t})\,\,\text{\rm{d}}\mathbb{P}_{z}

is a quasi-continuous
\Lambda
-version of
P_{t}u
, where
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the associated Markov semigroup in
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
associated with
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
.

Definition 2.3

(Extension of the generator domain)
.

Let
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
be a Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
.
The generator
(L,\mathscr{D}(L))
of
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
extends to all

(2.5)

u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}):\quad\exists f\in L^{1}(E,\Lambda):\quad-\mathscr{E}(v,u)=\Lambda(vf)\quad\forall v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\cap L^{\infty}(E,\Lambda)

by
Lu:=f
.
The linear space defined by (
2.5
) is called the extended generator domain
\mathscr{D}_{e}(L)
of
\mathscr{E}
.

Every
u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
admits a quasi-continuous modification
\tilde{u}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and defines a continuous additive functional of the process
{(X_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
via

A^{[u]}_{t}:=\tilde{u}(X_{t})-\tilde{u}(X_{0})
,
t\geq 0
.
This and the existence of a decomposition

(2.6)

A^{[u]}_{t}=M^{[u]}_{t}+N^{[u]}_{t},\quad t\geq 0,

into a martingale additive functional
{(M^{[u]}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
of finite energy and an additive functional of zero energy
{(N^{[u]}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
is a consequence of

[
13
, Thm. 5.2.2]
and the method of regularization (see
[
16
, Chapt. VI, in part. Thm. VI.2.5]
,
[
8
]
and also
[
11
, Sect. 4]
).

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
be a local quasi-regular Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
and
(L,\mathscr{D}(L))
be its generator.
We assume conservativity, i.e.
\mathscr{E}(\boldsymbol{1}_{E},\boldsymbol{1}_{E})=0
.
It holds:

(1)

There exists a non-terminating diffusion process
\mathbf{M}=(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(X_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathbb{P}_{z})_{z\in E})
on
E
which is properly associated with
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
. In particular,
\Lambda
is an invariant probability measure of
\mathbf{M}
.

(2)

\mathbf{M}
solves the martingale problem for
(L,\mathscr{D}_{e}(L))
, i.e. for
u\in\mathscr{D}_{e}(L)
, the additive functional

\tilde{u}(X_{t})-\tilde{u}(X_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}Lu(X_{s})\,\text{\rm{d}}s,\quad t\geq 0,

is a
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t}
-martingale under
\mathbb{P}_{z}
for q.-e.
z\in E
.

Proof.

(1)
The first statement follows from
[
16
, Thm. V.1.11]
.

(2)
The map in (
2.4
) uniquely determines a strongly continuous contraction semigroup with parameter
t\geq 0
in
L^{1}(E,\Lambda)
.
Furthermore, the generator
(L^{(1)},\mathscr{D}(L^{(1)}))
in
L^{1}(E,\Lambda)
of the extended semigroup is an extension of
(L,\mathscr{D}_{e}(L))
, more precisely

\mathscr{D}(L^{(1)})\cap\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})=\mathscr{D}_{e}(\mathscr{E}).

Now, we assume
u\in\mathscr{D}_{e}(L)
. Then
Lu\in\mathscr{D}_{e}(\mathscr{E})
and
the additive functional
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}{Lu}(X_{s})\,\text{\rm{d}}s

coincides with
{(N^{[u]}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
because of
[
13
, Thm. 5.2.4]
and

\mathbb{E}_{z}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}{Lu}(X_{s})\,\text{\rm{d}}s\Big]=\mathbb{E}_{z}[\tilde{u}(X_{t})]-\tilde{u}(z),\quad\Lambda\text{-a.e.~}z\in E.

∎

2.2
A quasi-regularity criterion with extrinsic derivative

We now consider
(E,\rho_{E})=({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\rho_{var})
with
\rho_{var}
as in (
1.2
), and let
\Lambda
be a Borel probability measure on
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
.
Given a measurable function
u
on
E
with
\Lambda(u^{2})<\infty
, we simply denote by
u
the corresponding element in
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
, which is the class of functions
\Lambda
-a.e. equal to
u
.

We will present a handy criterion for the quasi-regularity of a Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda)
, which
is formulated in terms of upper bounds for
\mathscr{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
by uniform norms of extrinsic derivatives, for the class of cylinder functions

(2.7)

\mathscr{X}:=\big\{\mathbb{M}\ni\mu\mapsto g(\mu(f_{1}),\cdots,\mu(f_{n})):n\in\mathbb{N},\,f_{i}\in C_{b}(M),\,g\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big\}.

Clearly, for such a cylindrical function
u
, we have
u\in C^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})
with

(2.8)

D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}g)(\mu(f_{1}),\cdots,\mu(f_{n}))f_{i}(x),\quad(\mu,x)\in\mathbb{M}\times M,

and
u\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P})
with

(2.9)

{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}g)(\mu(f_{1}),\cdots,\mu(f_{n}))(f_{i}(x)-\mu(f_{i})),\quad(\mu,x)\in\mathscr{P}\times M.

Theorem 2.2
.

A Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda)
is quasi-regular, if
(\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),\mathscr{E}^{1/2}_{1})
has a dense subspace consisting of quasi-continuous functions and additionally one of the following holds true:

(1)

There exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\mathscr{E}(u,u)\leq C\Big(\sup_{\mu\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\|D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\mu)+L^{\infty}(\mu)}+\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}\Big),\quad u\in\mathscr{X}.

(2)

\Lambda({\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}})=1
and there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

It is easy to see for any

To prove Theorem

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathscr{X}_{1}
separating points in
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\ (
hence in
{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}})

Proof.

Let

be dense in

Then the family

separates points in

. On the other hand,
it is easy to see that the set

\mathbb{I}:=\Big\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}:\ N_{m}:=\sup\{i\in\mathbb{N}:m_{i}>0\}<\infty\Big\}

and

if

for all

. For any

under the convention that

. So,
\{f_{m}:m\in\mathbb{I}\}
is closed under multiplication, and separates points in
E
since it contains
\{\frac{1}{2}+\rho_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
. By
[
6
, Theorem 11(b)]

the countable family

To approximate

by functions in
\bigcap_{1\leq p<\infty}\mathscr{X}_{p}
, for each

, let
\chi_{l}\in C^{1}_{0}([0,\infty))

(2.10)

\chi|_{[0,2l]^{c}}=0,\ \ \ \chi_{l}(s)=s\ \text{for}\ s\in[0,l].

(a) By

\begin{split}&\big|u(\gamma)-u(\eta)\big|\\

(b) Let
{\{u_{n}\}}_{n\geq 1}\subset{C}^{\textit{E},1}_{b}(\mathbb{M})

u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}})
, we find a subsequence
{\{u_{n_{k}}\}}_{k\geq 1}
such that

u_{n_{k}}(\mu)\rightarrow 0,\ \ \Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}\text{-a.e.~}\mu\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\,k\rightarrow\infty.

Hence, (
3.3
) and
J\ll\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}\times\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}
imply that
w(\gamma,\eta)=0
for
J
-a.e.
(\gamma,\eta)\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\times{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
, and moreover

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{J}(u_{n},u_{n})=0,

so that
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{J},C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}))
is closable.
The Markovian property of
\mathscr{E}^{J}_{\mathbb{M}}
follows from a standard argument (see e.g.
[
13
, Ex.I.1.2.1]
), while the quasi-regularity is a consequence of Theorem
2.2
and (
3.1
).
Therefore, assertion (1) is proved. The second assertion can be proved similarly, by using (
3.2
) in place of (
3.1
).
∎

3.2
Diffusion type quasi-regular Dirichlet forms

Let

H:=L^{2}(M,\lambda),\ \ H_{0}:=H\setminus\{0\}.

We formulate the map
\Psi
as follows with different image spaces:

(3.4)

\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}:H\ni h\mapsto h^{2}\lambda\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},

(3.5)

\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}:H_{0}\ni h\mapsto\frac{h^{2}\lambda}{\lambda(h^{2})}\in{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}.

Then, fixing a Borel probability measure
\Lambda_{0}
on
H
with
\Lambda_{0}(\{0\})=0
, we obtain the following reference probability measures on
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
and
{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
respectively:

\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}:=\Lambda_{0}\circ\Psi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}},\qquad\Lambda_{\mathscr{P}}:=\Lambda_{0}\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}^{-1},

where the latter can also be regarded as a probability on
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
with
\Lambda_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}})=1
, or as a probability on
{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
. Topics related to the closability and quasi-regularity of bilinear forms are not affected by which point of view we choose, since
{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
is a closed subset of
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
.

Let

C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}):=\Big\{u\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}):\ \sup_{\mu\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\|D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}<\infty\Big\}.

Obviously,
C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})
contains the class of functions

\mathbb{M}\ni\mu\mapsto\varphi(\mu(M))u(\mu),\ \ u\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}),\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}([0,\infty)),

which is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}})
for any probability measure
\Lambda
on
{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}
. Here,
C_{0}^{1}
is the class of
C^{1}
functions with compact support.

We will present conditions on
\Lambda_{0}
such that the bilinear form

(3.6)

\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\mu\big((D^{E}u)(D^{E}v)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu),\ \ u,v\in C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})

is closable in
L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}})
and its closure is a quasi-regular Dirichlet form, and correspondingly,

(3.7)

\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}(u,v):=\int_{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\mu\big((\widetilde{D}^{E}u)(\widetilde{D}^{E}v)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathscr{P}}(\mu),\ \ u,v\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P})

is closable in
L^{2}({\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}})
and its closure is a quasi-regular Dirichlet form.

More generally, we consider weighted bilinear forms with a
``
diffusion coefficient”
A
defined as the following two families.

(A_{\mathbb{M}})

Family
\{A(\mu)\}_{\mu\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}
. Each
A(\mu)
is a positively definite bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(M,\mu)
, such that
for any
\eta\in C_{b}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\times M),

(3.8)

(A\eta)(\mu,x):=A(\mu)\eta(\mu,\cdot)(x)

is measurable in
(\mu,x)\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\times M,
and

\int_{{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\|A(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu)<\infty.

(A_{\mathscr{P}})

Family
\{A(\mu)\}_{\mu\in{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}
. Each
A(\mu)
is a positively definite bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(M,\mu)

such that
for any
\eta\in C_{b}({\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\times M),

(A\eta)(\mu,x)
defined in (
3.8
)
is measurable in
(\mu,x)\in{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}\times M,
and

\int_{{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\|A(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathscr{P}}(\mu)<\infty.

We now consider the bilinear forms

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}(u,v):=\int_{{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\big\langle A(\mu)D^{E}u(\mu),D^{E}v(\mu)\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu),\ \ u,v\in C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A}(u,v):=\int_{{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\big\langle A(\mu)\widetilde{D}^{E}u(\mu),\widetilde{D}^{E}v(\mu)\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathscr{P}}(\mu),\ \ u,v\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}).

It is easy to see that for any
h,h^{\prime}\in H
with
h\neq 0
, we have
h^{\prime}h^{-1}\in H:=L^{2}(M,\mu)
for
\mu=h^{2}\lambda
, and

(3.9)

\begin{split}&h^{\prime}\mapsto A^{\mathbb{M}}_{h}h^{\prime}:=hA\big(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h)\big)(h^{\prime}h^{-1}),\\
&h^{\prime}\mapsto A_{h}^{\mathscr{P}}h^{\prime}:=hA\big(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h)\big)(h^{\prime}h^{-1})\end{split}

give two families
\{A_{h}^{\mathbb{M}}\}_{h\in H_{0}}
and
\{A_{h}^{\mathscr{P}}\}_{h\in H_{0}}

of positive definite bounded linear operators on
H.

Let
C^{1}(H)
be the space of Fréchet differentiable functions
f
on
H
with continuous derivative
\nabla f:H\rightarrow H
, and let

\displaystyle C_{b}^{1}(H):=\big\{f\in C^{1}(H):\ \|f\|_{\infty}+\big\|\|\nabla f(\cdot)\|_{H}\big\|_{\infty}<\infty\big\},

\displaystyle C_{b,0}^{1}(H)=\Big\{f\in C^{1}(H):\ \|f\|_{\infty}+\big\|\|\cdot\|_{H}\|\nabla f(\cdot)\|_{H}\big\|_{\infty}<\infty\Big\}.

Then in situations
(A_{\mathbb{M}})
and
(A_{\mathscr{P}})
we may define bilinear forms

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{E}^{0,A}_{\mathbb{M}}(f,g):=\int_{H}\langle A_{h}^{\mathbb{M}}\nabla f(h),\nabla g(h)\rangle_{H}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(h),\ \ f,g\in C_{b}^{1}(H),\\
&\mathscr{E}^{0,A}_{\mathscr{P}}(f,g):=\int_{H}\langle A_{h}^{\mathscr{P}}\nabla f(h),\nabla g(h)\rangle_{H}\|h\|^{2}_{H}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(h),\ \ f,g\in C_{b,0}^{1}(H).\end{split}

The following result links
\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}
and
\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A}
to these two bilinear forms respectively.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
H,H_{0},\Lambda_{0},\mathscr{E}^{0,A}_{\mathbb{M}},\mathscr{E}^{0,A}_{\mathscr{P}},\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}
and
\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A}
be introduced above.

(1)

In situation
(A_{\mathbb{M}})
, for any
u\in C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}),
we have
u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}\in C_{b}^{1}(H)
and

(3.11)

\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}(u,v)=\frac{1}{4}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{0,A}(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}},v\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}),\ \ \ \ u,v\in C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}).

Consequently, if
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{0,A},C_{b}^{1}(H))
is closable in
L^{2}(H,\Lambda_{0})
, so is
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A},C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}))
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}),
and its closure
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}))
is a local quasi-regular Dirichlet form.

(2)

In situation
(A_{\mathscr{P}})
, for any
u\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}),
we have
u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}\in C_{b,0}^{1}(H)
and

(3.12)

\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A}(u,v)=\frac{1}{4}\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{0,A}(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}},v\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}),\ \ \ \ u,v\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}).

Consequently, if
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{0,A},C_{b,0}^{1}(H))
is closable in
L^{2}(H,\Lambda_{0})
, so is
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A},C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}))
in
L^{2}({\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathscr{P}}),

and its closure
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A}))
is a local quasi-regular Dirichlet form.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we present the following lemma on chain rules of the Fréchet derivative for composed functions with
\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}
and
\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}
, which lead to (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) respectively.

Lemma 3.3
.

(1)

If
u\in{C}^{\textit{E},1}_{b,2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}})
, then
u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}\in C^{1}(H)
with

(3.13)

\nabla(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})(h)=2h(D^{\textit{E}}u)(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h)),\,\quad h\in H.

In particular,

\big\|\nabla(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})(h)\big\|_{H}=2\big\|D^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h))\big\|_{L^{2}(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h))},\quad h\in H.

(2)

If
u\in{C}^{\textit{E},1}_{b}({\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}})
, then

u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}\in C_{b,0}^{1}(H)
with

(3.14)

\nabla(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}})(h)=\frac{2h}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h)),\,\quad h\in H_{0}.

In particular,

\big\|\nabla(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}})(h)\big\|_{H}=\frac{2\big\|{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h))\big\|_{L^{2}(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h))}}{\|h\|_{H}},\quad h\in H_{0}.

Proof.

(1)
Let
u\in{C}^{\textit{E},1}_{b,2}(\mathbb{M})
and
h\in H
. For
\phi\in H_{0}
, we set

\mu_{\phi,r}:=(1-r)\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h)+r\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h+\phi),\quad r\in(0,1).

Then, by
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
, it holds

(3.15)

\begin{split}&(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})(h+\phi)-(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})(h)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\,\text{\rm{d}}u(\mu_{\phi,s})}{\,\text{\rm{d}}s}\Big|_{s=r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\,\text{\rm{d}}r\int_{M}D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})\,\text{\rm{d}}\big(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h+\phi)-\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h)\big)\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\,\text{\rm{d}}r\int_{M}D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})\cdot\big((h+\phi)^{2}-h^{2}\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}\lambda.\end{split}

Since
u\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})
,
D^{\textit{E}}u
is bounded and

\lim_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})=D^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h)).

By combining this with (
3.15
) and

\lim_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\frac{\|(h+\phi)^{2}-h^{2}-2h\phi\|_{L^{1}(\lambda)}}{\|\phi\|_{H}}=0,

we may apply the triangular inequality and the dominated convergence theorem to get

\displaystyle\limsup_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})(h+\phi)-(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})(h)-2{\big\langle}D^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h))h,\phi{\big\rangle}_{H}}{\|\phi\|_{H}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\bigg(|D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})|\cdot\bigg|\frac{(h+\phi)^{2}-h^{2}-2h\phi}{\|\phi\|_{H}}\bigg|\bigg)\,\text{\rm{d}}r

\displaystyle\quad+\limsup_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg|2\bigg\langle hD^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})-hD^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h)),\frac{\phi}{\|\phi\|_{H}}{\bigg\rangle}_{H}\bigg|\,\text{\rm{d}}r=0.

So,
u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}
is Frechét differentiable on
H
and (
3.13
) holds. This together with the continuity of
\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}
and
u\in C_{b,2}^{1}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}})
implies that

u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}\in C_{b}^{1}(H).

(2)
Let
u\in{C}^{\textit{E},1}_{b}(\mathscr{P})
and
h\in H_{0}
. For
\phi\in H_{0}\setminus\{-h\}
, we set

\mu_{\phi,r}:=(1-r)\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h)+r\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h+\phi),\quad r\in(0,1),

and

\nu_{\phi,r}:=(1+r)\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h+\phi)-r\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h),\quad r\in(0,1).

Then, by
[
20
, Lemma 3.3]
, it holds

(3.16)

\begin{split}&(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}})(h+\phi)-(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}})(h)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\,\text{\rm{d}}u(\mu_{s,\phi})}{\,\text{\rm{d}}s}\Big|_{s=r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\,\text{\rm{d}}u(\mu_{r,\phi}+s(\nu_{r,\phi}-\mu_{r,\phi}))}{\,\text{\rm{d}}s}{\Big|}_{s=0}\,\text{\rm{d}}r=\int_{0}^{1}\,\text{\rm{d}}r\int_{M}{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{r,\phi}))\,\text{\rm{d}}(\nu_{r,\phi}-\mu_{r,\phi})\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\,\text{\rm{d}}r\int_{M}{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{r,\phi})\bigg(\frac{(h+\phi)^{2}}{\|h+\phi\|_{H}^{2}}-\frac{h^{2}}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}\bigg)\,\text{\rm{d}}\lambda.\end{split}

It is easy to see that

(3.17)

\lim_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{H}}\bigg\|\frac{(h+\phi)^{2}}{\|h+\phi\|_{H}^{2}}-\frac{h^{2}}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}-\Big(\frac{2h\phi}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}-\frac{2h^{2}\langle h,\phi\rangle_{H}}{\|h\|_{H}^{4}}\Big)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\lambda)}=0

Besides,
u\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P})
implies that
{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u
is bounded and

\lim_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})={\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h)).

Combining this with (
3.16
), (
3.17
) and the fact that

(3.18)

\Big\langle h{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h)),\frac{h\langle h,\phi\rangle_{H}}{\|h\|_{H}^{4}}\Big\rangle_{H}=\frac{\langle h,\phi\rangle_{H}}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}\int_{M}{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u\Big(\frac{h^{2}}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}\lambda\Big)\frac{h^{2}}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}\,\text{\rm{d}}\lambda=0,

we may apply the triangular inequality and the dominated convergence theorem to derive

\displaystyle\limsup_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{H}}\bigg|(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}})(h+\phi)-(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}})(h)-\frac{2{\big\langle}{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h))h,\phi\rangle_{H}}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\bigg(|{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})|\cdot\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{H}}\bigg|\frac{(h+\phi)^{2}}{\|h+\phi\|_{H}^{2}}-\frac{h^{2}}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}-\Big(\frac{2h\phi}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}}-\frac{2h^{2}\langle h,\phi\rangle_{H}}{\|h\|_{H}^{4}}\Big)\bigg|\bigg)\,\text{\rm{d}}r

\displaystyle\quad+\limsup_{\|\phi\|_{H}\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg|2\bigg\langle h{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\mu_{\phi,r})-h{\tilde{D}}^{\textit{E}}u(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h)),\frac{\phi}{\|h\|_{H}^{2}\|\phi\|_{H}}-\frac{h\langle h,\phi\rangle_{H}}{\|h\|_{H}^{4}\|\phi\|_{H}}{\bigg\rangle}_{H}\bigg|\,\text{\rm{d}}r=0.

The remainder of the proof is similar to case (1).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

(1) Let
u,v\in C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})
. By Lemma
3.3
(1),
(
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{0,A}(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}},v\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})=4\int_{H}\big\langle A(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h))D^{E}u(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h)),D^{E}v(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h))\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}(h))}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(h)

\displaystyle=4\int_{{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\big\langle A(\mu)D^{E}u(\mu),D^{E}v(\mu)\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu)=4\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}(u,v).

So, (
3.11
) holds.

If
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{0,A},C_{b}^{1}(H))
is closable in

, then the closability of
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A},C_{b,2}^{1}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}))
in

is an immediate consequence of the equalities (

) and

(3.19)

\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}})}=\|u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}\|_{L^{2}(H,\Lambda_{0})},\quad u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}),

by (

) as a consequence of
[
16

. So,
the closure
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}))

(3.20)

\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}(u,v)=\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{0,A}(u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}},v\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}),\ \ u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A})\subseteq\big\{u:u\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}}\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{0,A})\big\}.

to
\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}
as well.

Moreover, the quasi-regularity follows since the condition in Theorem

(1) holds for

(2) Let

(2), (

) and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{0,A}(u\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}},v\circ\Psi_{\mathscr{P}})=4\int_{H}\frac{1}{\lambda(h^{2})}\big\langle hA(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h))\widetilde{D}^{E}u(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h)),h\widetilde{D}^{E}v(\Psi_{\mathscr{P}}(h))\big\rangle_{H}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(h)

\displaystyle=4\int_{{\mathscr{P}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\big\langle A(\mu)\widetilde{D}^{E}u(\mu),\widetilde{D}^{E}v(\mu)\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathscr{P}}(\mu)=4\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}^{A}(u,v).

3.12
) holds.

Remark 3.4
.

When
\int_{{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\mu(M)\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu)<\infty
, in situation
(A_{\mathbb{M}})
we have
C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})\subset\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A})

and

(3.21)

\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A}(u,v):=\int_{{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\big\langle A(\mu)D^{E}u(\mu),D^{E}v(\mu)\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu),\ \ u,v\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}).

To see this, let
u\in C_{b}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M})
and choose
\varphi_{n}\in C^{1}_{0}([0,\infty))
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\boldsymbol{1}_{[0,n]}\leq\varphi_{n}\leq\boldsymbol{1}_{[0,n+1]},\ \ \varphi^{\prime}_{n}\leq\boldsymbol{1}_{[n,n+1]}.

Then for each
n\geq 1
the function

\mu\mapsto u_{n}(\mu):=\varphi_{n}(\mu(M))u(\mu)

is in the class
C_{b,2}^{E,1}(\mathbb{M}),
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\Big(|u(\mu)-u_{n}(\mu)|^{2}+\big\|D^{\textit{E}}u(\mu,\cdot)-D^{\textit{E}}u_{n}(\mu,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\Big)\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\big\|D^{\textit{E}}u(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|u(\cdot)\|^{2}_{\infty}\Big)\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}\big(\mu(M)\boldsymbol{1}_{[n,\infty)}(\mu(M))\big)=0.

Therefore,

u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{M}}^{A})
and (
3.21
) holds.

Example 3.5
.

Let
K
be a bounded, positively definite linear operator on
H
with

(3.22)

k_{1}\|f\|_{H}^{2}\leq\langle Kf,f\rangle_{H}\leq k_{2}\|f\|_{H}^{2},\ \ f\in H

for constants
k_{1},k_{2}\in(0,\infty),
and let
\Lambda_{0}
be a non-degenerate Gaussian measure on
H
, i.e. having full topological support.
If we set

A(\mu)g:=h_{\mu}^{-\frac{1}{2}}K\big(g{h_{\mu}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big),\ \ g\in L^{2}(\mu),\,\mu\in{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}},

then the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
are satisfied. Indeed, by (
3.9
) we have

\displaystyle\langle K(\textnormal{sign}(h)h^{\prime}),\textnormal{sign}(h)h^{\prime}\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{h}^{\mathbb{M}}h^{\prime},h^{\prime}\rangle_{H},

\displaystyle\langle K(\textnormal{sign}(h)h^{\prime}),\textnormal{sign}(h)h^{\prime}\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{h}^{\mathscr{P}}h^{\prime},h^{\prime}\rangle_{H},

so that
it suffices to apply (
3.22
) to
f:=\textnormal{sign}(h)h^{\prime}
and use the closability in
L^{2}(H,\Lambda_{0})

-

On the Schwartz space of rapidly decaying functions we define the operator

T_{\alpha}f(x):=-\Delta f(x)+(|x|^{2}+\alpha)f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

with parameter
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, which corresponds to the Hamiltonian of the harmonic oscillator plus a multiple of the identity operator.
Then,
T_{\alpha}
extends (uniquely) to a positive, self-adjoint operator
(T_{\alpha},\mathscr{D}(T_{\alpha}))
on

with eigenvalues

(4.6)

2(n_{1}+\dots+n_{d})+d+\alpha,\quad n_{1},\dots,n_{d}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

The corresponding eigenfunctions

for

are given by

f_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}(x)=Z_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}^{-1}\,e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathscr{H}_{n_{i}}(x_{i}),

where

This is a consequence of the fact that the Hermite functions

\xi_{n}:\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto{\big(\pi^{1/2}2^{n}n!\big)}^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{|s|^{2}}{2}}\mathscr{H}_{n}(s),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},

satisfy

-\xi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(s)+s^{2}\xi_{n}(s)=(2n+1)\xi_{n}(s),\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

and form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\,\text{\rm{d}}s)
, see e.g.
[
14
, Appendicies A.1 & A.5]
.

For

(T_{\alpha}^{k/2},\mathscr{D}(T_{\alpha}^{k/2}))
is a positive, self-adjoint operator on
H
.
The Gaussian measure
\Lambda_{0}=G_{k,\alpha}

.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\alpha,k\in(0,\infty)
.

(1)

The linear space

is a Hilbert space with inner product

\langle g,f\rangle_{H_{k,\alpha}}:=\big\langle T^{k/2}_{\alpha}g,T^{k/2}_{\alpha}f\big\rangle_{H}\quad\text{for }f,g\in H_{k,\alpha}.

(2)

Let

be the centered Gaussian measure on

with covariance operator

for some constant

(4.7)

\int_{H_{k,\alpha}}\langle f,h\rangle_{H_{k,\alpha}}\langle g,h\rangle_{H_{k,\alpha}}\,\text{\rm{d}}G_{k,\alpha}(h)=\langle f,T_{\alpha}^{-d^{\prime}}g\rangle_{H_{k,\alpha}},\quad f,g\in H_{k,\alpha}.

This is well-defined since

so that

is a symmetric trace class operator on

Remark 4.3

Obviously,

(4.9)

Proposition 4.4

Let a measurable function

,

\alpha\in[1,\infty)

Proof.

Noting that

Next, by (
4.8

Combining this with (
4.8

Next, we prepare to show

,

By (

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{M}^{\textnormal{ac}}}}\text{\rm{e}}^{\delta\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})-\beta(\mu)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\mathbb{M}}(\mu)=\int_{H}\text{\rm{e}}^{\delta\|f\|_{H}^{2}-(\beta\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{M}})(f)}\,\text{\rm{d}}G_{k,\alpha}(f)

(c) Let

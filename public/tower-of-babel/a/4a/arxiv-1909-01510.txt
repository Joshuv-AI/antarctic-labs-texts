Abstract
. Following the same method of [ BM17 ] and [ Zha19 ] , we calculate the ( 𝔤 , K ) (\mathfrak{g},K) module structure for the principal series representation of S ​ p ​ ( 4 , ℝ ) Sp(4,\mathbb{R}) . which is consistent with the Iwasawa decomposition. Namely, for any

and

such that

w_{\alpha_{i}}^{-1}nw_{\alpha_{i}}=\kappa_{\alpha}(t)h_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\right)\chi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)n^{\prime}.

4.
Parabolic Subgroups of
Sp(4,\mathbb{R})

4.1.
Minimal Parabolic Subgroup and Jacobi Parabolic Subgroup

Denote
P_{0}
as the
minimal parabolic
subgroup of
Sp(4,\mathbb{R})
, with the Lie algebra having a Levi decomposition:

\mathfrak{p}_{0}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0},\text{ where }\mathfrak{n}_{0}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

For any real root
\alpha
of
\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
, consider the homomorphism
\phi_{\alpha}:\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, which exponentiates to a homomorphism
\Phi_{\alpha}:\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow G_{\mathbb{C}}
. Letting
\gamma_{\alpha}
be the image of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, then

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha}=\exp\frac{2\pi iH_{\alpha}}{|\alpha|^{2}}=\exp\pi(X_{\alpha}-\theta X_{\alpha})

Recalling the statements in
[
Kna13
, Section VII.4]
, every nonidentity element of the centralizer
M
of
\mathfrak{a}
in
K
has order 2, and is generated by
\gamma_{\alpha}
for all real roots
\alpha
. In the case of
Sp(4,\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle M=\{\gamma_{\alpha_{2}}^{\epsilon_{1}}\gamma_{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}^{\epsilon_{2}}|\epsilon_{i}=0\text{ or }1\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},

The generators
\gamma_{\alpha_{2}},\gamma_{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}
of
M
can also be expressed in terms of
U_{i}
:

\gamma_{\alpha_{2}}=\exp(\pi(U_{0}-U_{3})),\gamma_{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}=\exp(\pi(U_{0}+U_{3})).

The minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{0}
has a Levi decomposition
P_{0}=MA_{0}N_{0}

M
is the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}
in
K
, and
A_{0}
and
N_{0}
are the analytic subgroups formed by exponentiating
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{0}
, respectively.

There are two proper standard parabolic subgroups that contain
P_{0}
. The parabolic subgroup
P_{1}
with an abelian nilpotent radical is called a
Siegel
subgroup. The Levi decomposition
P_{1}=M_{1}N_{1}
has the form:

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\\
0&(A^{t})^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),A\in GL(2,\mathbb{R})\}\cong GL(2,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle N_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&x_{4}&x_{3}\\
0&1&x_{3}&x_{2}\\
0&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Their Lie algebras have restricted root space decompositions:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\{\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

There is another class of parabolic subgroup called the
Jacobi
(or
Heisenberg
and in some literature also called
Klingen
) parabolic subgroup
P_{2}=M_{2}N_{2}
, having a Levi decomposition:

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}h_{1}&0&0&0\\
0&a&0&b\\
0&0&1/h_{1}&0\\
0&c&0&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in SL(2,\mathbb{R}),h_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}\}\cong SL(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}

\displaystyle N_{2}

\displaystyle=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x_{1}&x_{4}&x_{3}\\
0&1&x_{3}&0\\
0&0&1&0\\
0&0&-x_{1}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

The Lie algebras of each subgroup have restricted root space decompositions:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{2}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{2}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Thus
\mathfrak{n}_{2}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\}}\mathbb{R}X_{\alpha}
. Since the root vectors
X_{\alpha}
satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[X_{\alpha},X_{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}]

\displaystyle=0\text{ for }\alpha\in\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\}

\displaystyle[X_{\alpha_{1}},X_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}]

\displaystyle=2X_{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}},

the group
N_{2}
is isomorphic to the Heisenberg group
\mathcal{H}_{3}
.

4.2.
Induction from the Minimal Parabolic Subgroup

In this section consider the minimal principal series obtained by induction from the minimal parabolic subgroup

. Consider the Cartan involution

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\varpi_{i}

If all of the

(2)

where

such that
\delta(\gamma_{\alpha_{2}}^{\epsilon_{1}}\gamma_{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}^{\epsilon_{2}})=(-1)^{\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1}+\delta_{2}\epsilon_{2}}
.

If the numbers

)-(

on the complex Cartan subalgebra

We define the

(4.3)

\displaystyle I_{P}(\chi_{\delta,\lambda})=\{f:G\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}|f(gman)=(\chi_{\delta,\lambda+\rho})^{-1}(ma)f(g)\}.

The meromorphic function

\eta(w_{0},\chi_{\delta,\lambda})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\text{ reduced}\\

(6.73)

\displaystyle\eta_{\delta,\alpha}(z)=\gamma_{\delta,\alpha}(z)\overline{\gamma_{\delta,\alpha}(-\bar{z})}.

\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha\rangle>0\text{ for positive }\alpha_{i}\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\delta,\alpha}\left(\frac{\langle\lambda,\rho^{(\alpha)}\rangle}{\langle\rho^{(\alpha)},\rho^{(\alpha)}\rangle}\right).

We denote the new normalized intertwining operator by

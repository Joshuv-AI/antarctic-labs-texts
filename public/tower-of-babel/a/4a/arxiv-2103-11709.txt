A Superposition-Based Calculus for Quantum Diagrammatic Reasoning and Beyond
Rachid Echahed, Mnacho Echenim

Abstract
We introduce a class of rooted graphs which allows one to encode various kinds of classical or quantum circuits. We then follow a set-theoretic approach to define rewrite systems over the considered graphs and propose a new complete Superposition calculus which handles sets of formulas consisting of equations or disequations over these graphs.



1
Introduction

Graph-based languages are
a popular specification and verification tool, both in mathematics and computer science. They have become particularly useful in quantum computing, for the formal verification of quantum algorithms and protocols, and several such languages have been developed over the past years for such an analysis, including the ZW calculus
[
8
]
, the ZH calculus
[
3
]
, the ZX calculus
[
7
]
, the SZX
[
4
]
calculus, or more recently the PBS-calculus
[
5
]
. The reason these languages are so useful is that standard approaches to verifying the correctness of quantum algorithms and protocols involve computations on complex matrices, and such approaches are non-intuitive and error-prone. On the other hand, graph-based languages permit to represent the same algorithms and protocols in a more intuitive way, by abstracting the numerical values and matrices, and replacing computations on these matrices by rewrite rules. Using graph-based languages to represent quantum algorithms thus permits to reduce many verification tasks to testing the equivalence of two graphs modulo a set of equations, which represent properties of the considered computations. It is also possible to test the equivalence between graphs in a user-friendly way using software such as, e.g., quantomatic
1
1

1

https://quantomatic.github.io/
. These tools permit to create graphs, define graph rewrite rules and apply them automatically, and even to define simplification tactics.
However, these tactics have to be specified by the user and are not necessarily complete.

A natural question is whether it is possible to find strategies that can be applied to graph rewrite rules in a purely automated way. Such rewrite strategies must fulfill several requirements:

(i)

they
must be efficient (i.e., as restrictive as possible with regards to the number of rule applications);

(ii)

they must be complete, in the sense that any two equivalent graphs must always be reducible to the same representative; and

(iii)

they must be generic, because graph languages and the equations that can be applied are very diverse and constantly evolving.

The Superposition

calculus
[
2
]
is a proof procedure that meets these three requirements. This is a highly efficient calculus that permits to reason automatically on first-order theories involving equalities.
It may be viewed as an extension of the Knuth-Bendix algorithm
[
9
]
to a wider class of equational formulæ, involving disjunctions (e.g., conditional rules) and quantifiers.
It may also be viewed as an extension of the Resolution calculus
[
14
,
11
]
, which is the most efficient proof procedure for predicate logic,
to sets of clauses containing the equality predicate.
The calculus is presented as a set of inference rules, deducing new assertions from axioms or previously generated assertions, together with a generic redundancy criterion that permits to prune the search space and discard many inferences.
This calculus is complete: if a formula
\phi
is deducible from a set of axioms
E
then this is automatically detected by the calculus. It is also generic, and can be uniformly applied to any set of axioms that can be expressed in first-order logic.
Most efficient theorem provers, such as Vampire
[
13
]
, E
[
15
]
or Spass
[
16
]
, are based on the Superposition calculus.

In this paper, we present an adaptation of the Superposition calculus to so-called
graph rewrite systems
, i.e., to sets of equations involving graphs rather than first-order terms.
The Superposition calculus cannot be extended to the entire class of graphs in a straightforward manner.
This is due to the fact that one of the fundamental properties that is required to prove completeness in the first-order case, namely confluence, does not hold for graphs. In fact, confluence on the class of graphs is not even decidable
[
12
]
. We thus exploit the fact that the graphs considered in the context of quantum computing are not arbitrary, and define a class that is general enough to encode the graph-based languages that are used for quantum diagrammatic reasoning.

The paper is decomposed as follows. After a recap on notions and notations, we introduce the class of graphs on which the extended Superposition calculus will be applied in Section
3
, along with a replacement operation that controls the way graphs can be rewritten. Formulas involving graphs are introduced in Section
4
, as well as the way such formulas are interpreted. The extension of the Superposition calculus is defined in Section
5
, and this calculus is proved to be refutationally complete in Section
6
. Section
7
illustrates the generality of our approach by considering the so-called subclass of
circuits
, which is an interesting candidate to encode the graphs used in the ZX calculus.

,

With a slight abuse of notations, we sometimes use set notations on tuples, i.e., we write

3
Graphs

3.1
Definitions

We define the class of graphs on which the adaptation of the Superposition calculus will be applied. Intuitively, this class consists of labeled graphs with a distinguished sequence of nodes which can be viewed as an interface that constrains the operations of replacing a subgraph by another graph that can be performed.

Let
{\cal N}
be a fixed countable set of
nodes
,
disjoint from the set of terms,
and let
{\tt S}
be a set of
sorts
.
We consider a function
\mathit{sort}
mapping every node
\alpha\in{\cal N}

to a sort in
{\tt S}
and a pre-order
\trianglelefteq
on
{\cal N}
.
We write
\alpha\triangleq\alpha^{\prime}
if
\alpha\trianglelefteq\alpha^{\prime}

and
\alpha^{\prime}\trianglelefteq\alpha
, and
\alpha\vartriangleleft\alpha^{\prime}

for
\alpha\trianglelefteq\alpha^{\prime}

and
\alpha^{\prime}\not\trianglelefteq\alpha
.

Definition 2
.

A graph
G
is a
tuple
\langle{\cal N}_{G},{\cal R}_{G},{\cal E}_{G},l_{G}\rangle

where:

•

{\cal N}_{G}
is a finite subset of
{\cal N}
.

•

{\cal R}_{G}
is a finite sequence of nodes in
{\cal N}_{G}
, with no repetition, called the roots of
G
. We denote by
{\cal N}^{*}_{G}
the set of nodes occurring in
{\cal N}_{G}
but not in
{\cal R}_{G}
.

•

{\cal E}_{G}
is a finite set of ordered pairs (written
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)
) of nodes in
{\cal N}_{G}
, called edges. We write
(\alpha\rightleftarrows\beta)
to denote any edge
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)
or
\left(\beta\rightarrow\alpha\right)

•

l_{G}
is a function mapping each node
\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G}
to a term (called the label of
\alpha
).

Two graphs
G
and
H
are disjoint
if
{\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{H}=\emptyset
.
We denote by
{\cal V}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\bigcup_{\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G}}{\cal V}(l_{G}(\alpha))
the set of variables occurring in the label of some node in
G
.
A graph
G
is ground if
{\cal V}(G)=\emptyset
.

Note that the function
l_{G}
is not defined over the
nodes in
{\cal R}_{G}
.

Definition 3
.

For any substitution
\sigma
and graph
G
,
\sigma(G)
denotes the graph
\langle{\cal N}_{G},{\cal R}_{G},{\cal E}_{G},l^{\prime}\rangle
, where
l^{\prime}(\alpha)=\sigma(l_{G}(\alpha))
, for all
\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G}
.

Definition 4
.

A graph
H
is a subgraph
of
G
(written
H\preceq G
) if the following conditions hold:

1.

{\cal N}_{H}\subseteq{\cal N}_{G}
;

2.

If
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{H}
then

\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{G}
.

3.

If
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{G}
and
\alpha,\beta\in{\cal N}_{H}

then
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{H}
.

4.

If
(\alpha\rightleftarrows\beta)\in{\cal E}_{G}
,
\beta\not\in{\cal N}_{H}

and
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H}
, then
\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H}
.

5.

If
\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{G}
and
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H}
, then

\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H}
.

6.

l_{H}
is the restriction of
l_{G}
to the nodes in
{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
.

Proposition 5
.

The relation
\preceq
is transitive and reflexive.

Proof.

It is immediate to check that
\preceq
is reflexive.
Assume that
I\preceq H\preceq G
.
Then:

1.

{\cal N}_{I}\subseteq{\cal N}_{H}\subseteq{\cal N}_{G}
hence

{\cal N}_{I}\subseteq{\cal N}_{G}
.

2.

We have
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{I}\Rightarrow\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{H}\Rightarrow\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{G}
.

3.

If
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{G}
and
\alpha,\beta\in{\cal N}^{*}_{I}
, then by Condition
5
of Definition
4

\alpha,\beta\in{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
, thus
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{H}
and

\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{I}
.

4.

Assume that
(\alpha\rightleftarrows\beta)\in{\cal E}_{G}
,
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{I}
and
\beta\not\in{\cal N}_{I}
. If

\beta\in{\cal N}_{H}
, then
(\alpha\rightleftarrows\beta)\in{\cal E}_{H}
and we deduce that
\alpha
must occur in
{\cal R}_{I}
because
I\preceq H
.
If

\beta\not\in{\cal N}_{H}
then

\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H}
since
H\preceq G
.
Thus
\alpha\in{\cal R}_{I}
, since
I\preceq H
.

5.

l_{I}=l_{H}|_{{\cal N}^{*}_{I}}=(l_{G}|_{{\cal N}^{*}_{H}})|_{{\cal N}^{*}_{I}}=l_{G}|_{{\cal N}^{*}_{I}}
(because

{\cal N}^{*}_{I}\subseteq{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
).

∎

Proposition 6
.

If

then
{\cal R}_{G}\subseteq{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that
\alpha\in{\cal R}_{G}\cap{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
. Then by definition,
\alpha
is a node in
{\cal N}_{H}
that does not occur in

of Definition

.
∎

In what follows, we define an equivalence relation on graphs to formalize the fact that the properties we prove do not depend on the actual nodes that occur in the graph.

Definition 7
.

An
{\cal N}
-mapping

\mathit{sort}(\alpha)=\mathit{sort}(\mu(\alpha))

and
\alpha\triangleq\mu(\alpha)
.
For technical convenience we assume that

-mapping is extended to the identity on terms.

Proposition 8
.

If

-mapping

is a graph. Moreover,
{\cal N}_{\mu(G)}=\mu({\cal N}_{G})
,

{\cal E}_{\mu(G)}=\mu({\cal E}_{G})
,

{\cal R}_{\mu(G)}=\mu({\cal R}_{G})

and

l_{\mu(G)}=\mu(l_{G})=\mu\circ l_{G}\circ\mu^{-1}=l_{G}\circ\mu^{-1}

Proof.

The result follows immediately from the definition of
\mu(G)
(see Section
2
) and from the fact that

is injective. ∎

Similarly, because
{\cal N}
-mappings are injective, we have the following result:

Proposition 9
.

If

and
{\cal N}_{G}\subseteq\mathit{dom}(\mu)
for an

-mapping

Definition 10
.

We write

if there exists
\mu
such that
\mathit{dom}(\mu)\supseteq{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}
and

.

Proposition 11
.

The relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

•

Reflexivity.

The identity on
{\cal N}_{G}
is an
{\cal N}
-mapping, thus
G\sim G
.

•

Symmetry.

If
G\sim G^{\prime}
, then

G=\mu(G^{\prime})
, where
\mu
is an
{\cal N}
-mapping such that
\mathit{dom}(\mu)\supseteq{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}
.
Since
\mu
is injective,
\mu^{-1}
is well-defined,
\mu^{-1}(G)=(\mu^{-1}\circ\mu)(G^{\prime})=G^{\prime}
and
\mathit{dom}(\mu^{-1})=\mathit{img}(\mu)\supseteq\mu({\cal N}_{G^{\prime}})={\cal N}_{G}
.
Hence
G^{\prime}\sim G
.

•

Transivity.

If
G\sim G^{\prime}\sim G^{\prime\prime}
then there exist
{\cal N}
-mappings
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
such that

G=\mu(G^{\prime})
,
G^{\prime}=\mu^{\prime}(G^{\prime\prime})

\mathit{dom}(\mu)\supseteq{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}

and
\mathit{dom}(\mu^{\prime})\supseteq{\cal N}_{G^{\prime\prime}}

Then since the composition of two injective functions is also injective,

is an
{\cal N}
-mapping,
and
G=(\mu\circ\mu^{\prime})(\mu^{\prime}(G^{\prime\prime}))

with
\mathit{dom}(\mu\circ\mu^{\prime})\supseteq{\cal N}_{G^{\prime\prime}}
.
Hence
G\sim G^{\prime\prime}
.

∎

{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}\setminus{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}=\left[({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}\right]\setminus{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}=\left[{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}\right]\setminus{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}={\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
by Proposition
14
. Consider a node
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}
; we show that
\alpha\in{\cal R}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
if and only if
\alpha\in{\cal R}_{G^{\prime}}\setminus{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
;
together with the previous result this will prove the second item of the proposition. First assume that
\alpha\in{\cal R}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
and let
\mu
denote the mapping from
H
to
H^{\prime}
in
G
. Then by definition, we have
\mu(\alpha)=\alpha\in{\cal R}_{G^{\prime}}
and by Proposition
14
,
\alpha\notin{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
; hence the result. Conversely, if
\alpha\in{\cal R}_{G^{\prime}}\setminus{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
then
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}\setminus{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}={\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
by the first item of the proposition, and by definition,
\alpha=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal R}_{G}
. Since
\alpha\notin{\cal N}_{H}
, necessarily
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
, hence the result.
∎

Observe that, with the notations of Definition
15
, if
\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G^{\prime}}\setminus{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}={\cal N}^{*}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
then necessarily
\mu(\alpha)=\alpha
hence

\mu^{-1}(\alpha)=\alpha
, thus

l_{G^{\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{G}(\mu^{-1}(\alpha))
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
H
be a subgraph of
G

and let
H^{\prime}
be a graph substitutable for
H
in
G
.
Then
G[H^{\prime}/H]
is a graph and
H^{\prime}\preceq G[H^{\prime}/H]
.

Proof.

Observe that the function
\mu
in Definition
15
is injective, since
{\cal R}_{H^{\prime}}
is repetition-free and
no node in
{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
may occur in
{\cal R}_{H^{\prime}}
, because
{\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal N}_{H}
by hypothesis.
We show that the domain of
\mu
contains all nodes occurring either in

{\cal R}_{G}
or in an edge in
{\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H}
.
By Condition
5

of Definition
4
, the nodes in
{\cal R}_{G}

occur in either
{\cal R}_{H}

or
{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
.

The function
l_{G^{\prime}}
is well-defined: if
\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{H^{\prime}}

and
\alpha=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
for

\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
, then
we have
\alpha^{\prime}\not\in{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
(because
{\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal N}_{G}
),
hence

\alpha^{\prime}\not=\alpha
, and
in this case, by definition of
\mu
,
\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H^{\prime}}
, hence may not occur in
{\cal N}^{*}_{H^{\prime}}
, a contradiction.
Thus it is straightforward to check that
G[H^{\prime}/H]
is a graph.

We prove that

H^{\prime}\preceq G^{\prime}
, with
G^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}G[H^{\prime}/H]
:

1.

{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}=({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
.

2.

If
\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}
then

(\alpha\rightleftarrows\beta)\in{\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}=\mu({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H})\cup{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}
.

3.

If
\alpha,\beta\in{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
and

\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}
, then by definition of
{\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}
, either

\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}
and the proof is completed,
or

\left(\alpha\rightarrow\beta\right)\in\mu({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H})
.
In the latter case, we have

\alpha=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
,
\beta=\mu(\beta^{\prime})
with

\left(\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow\beta^{\prime}\right)\in{\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H}
.
We show that
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{H}
.
If
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
, then we have
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}\cap{\cal N}_{G}
,
thus
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{H}
(since
H^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
).
Otherwise,
\alpha\not=\alpha^{\prime}
, hence
by definition of
\mu
,
\alpha^{\prime}
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H}
, thus
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{H}
.
Similarly,
\beta^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{H}
,
and since
H\preceq G
we deduce that

\left(\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow\beta^{\prime}\right)\in{\cal E}_{H}
, contradicting the fact that
\left(\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow\beta^{\prime}\right)\in{\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H}
.

4.

Assume that
(\alpha\rightleftarrows\beta)\in{\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}
,
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
and

\beta\not\in{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
. By definition of
{\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}
this entails
that

(\alpha\rightleftarrows\beta)\in\mu({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H})

and
\alpha=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
,
\beta=\mu(\beta^{\prime})
, for some
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{G}
.
If
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
then
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}\cup{\cal N}_{G}
, and
since
H^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
, we have

\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H}
, thus, by definition of
\mu
,

\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H}
(since
\alpha\in\mathit{dom}(\mu)
).
Otherwise,

\alpha\not=\alpha^{\prime}
, and
\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H^{\prime}}
by definition of
\mu
.

5.

Let
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
be a node occurring in
{\cal R}_{G^{\prime}}
.
We have
\alpha=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
for some node occurring in
{\cal R}_{G}
.
By definition of
\mu
, either
\alpha
occurs in
{\cal R}_{H^{\prime}}
and the proof is completed,
or
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
with
\alpha\not\in{\cal N}_{H}
, which contradicts the hypothesis
that
H^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
.

6.

By definition of
l_{G^{\prime}}
, we have

l_{H^{\prime}}=l_{G^{\prime}}|_{{\cal N}^{*}_{H^{\prime}}}
.

∎

3.2
Properties of the Replacement Operation

We establish some basic properties of the replacement operation, all of which are similar to their counterparts for the replacement operation on first-order terms.

Proposition 19
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
H\preceq G
.
Then
H
is substitutable for
H
in
G
and
G[H/H]=G
.

Proof.

By definition,
{\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{H}\subseteq{\cal N}_{H}

thus
H
is substitutable for
H
in
G
.
The conditions of Definition
15
(with
H=H^{\prime}
) entail that
\mu
is the identity on
{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}^{*}_{H}

and that
{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}=({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{H}={\cal N}_{G}
,

{\cal R}_{G^{\prime}}={\cal R}_{G}
,

{\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}=({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H})\cup{\cal E}_{H}={\cal E}_{G}

and
l_{G^{\prime}}=l_{G}
(since
l_{H}=l_{G}|_{{\cal N}^{*}_{H}}
).
∎

Proposition 20
.

Let
G,H
and
H^{\prime}
be graphs, with
H\preceq G

and
H^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
.
Let
\mu

be an
{\cal N}
-mapping with domain
{\cal N}_{G}\cup{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}
.
Then
\mu(H^{\prime})
is substitutable for
\mu(H)
in
\mu(G)
, and:

\mu(G[H^{\prime}/H])=\mu(G)[\mu(H^{\prime})/\mu(H)]

Proof.

We have
{\cal N}_{\mu(G)}\cap{\cal N}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}=\mu({\cal N}_{G})\cap\mu({\cal N}_{H^{\prime}})=\mu({\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}})
by injectivity of
\mu
,
thus

{\cal N}_{\mu(G)}\cap{\cal N}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}\subseteq\mu({\cal N}_{H})={\cal N}_{\mu(H)}
(since

{\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal N}_{H}
, as
H^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
). Consequently,

\mu(H^{\prime})
is substitutable for
\mu(H)
in
\mu(G)
.

Let
G^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}G[H^{\prime}/H]

and
G^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\mu(G)[\mu(H^{\prime})/\mu(H)]
.
We show that
\mu(G^{\prime})=G^{\prime\prime}
.
Let
{\cal R}_{H}=\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
,

{\cal R}_{H^{\prime}}=\langle\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\rangle
, and:

\mu^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{i}\mapsto\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{\alpha\mapsto\alpha\mid\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}\}

\mu^{\prime\prime}=\{\mu(\alpha_{i})\mapsto\mu(\alpha_{i}^{\prime})\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{\alpha\mapsto\alpha\mid\alpha\in{\cal N}_{\mu(G)}\setminus{\cal N}_{\mu(H)}\}

Observe that
\mu^{\prime\prime}=\mu\circ\mu^{\prime}\circ\mu^{-1}
. We have:

•

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\cal N}_{\mu(G^{\prime})}&=&\mu({\cal N}_{G^{\prime}})=\mu(({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}})=\mu({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup\mu({\cal N}_{H^{\prime}})\\
&=&(\mu({\cal N}_{G})\setminus\mu({\cal N}_{H}))\cup\mu({\cal N}_{H^{\prime}})\quad\text{by injectivity of $\mu$}\\
&=&{\cal N}_{G^{\prime\prime}}\\
\end{array}

•

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\cal E}_{\mu(G^{\prime})}&=&\mu({\cal E}_{G^{\prime}})=\mu(\mu^{\prime}({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H})\cup{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}})\\
&=&\mu(\mu^{\prime}({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H}))\cup\mu({\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}))=\mu(\mu^{\prime}({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H}))\cup{\cal E}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}\\
&=&(\mu(\mu^{\prime}({\cal E}_{G}))\setminus\mu(\mu^{\prime}({\cal E}_{H})))\cup{\cal E}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}\quad\text{by injectivity of $\mu,\mu^{\prime}$}\\
&=&({\cal E}_{\mu(\mu^{\prime}(G))}))\setminus{\cal E}_{\mu(\mu^{\prime}(H))})\cup{\cal E}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}\\
\end{array}

Further,

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\cal E}_{G^{\prime\prime}}&=&\mu^{\prime\prime}({\cal E}_{\mu(G)}\setminus{\cal E}_{\mu(H)})\cup{\cal E}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}=({\cal E}_{\mu^{\prime\prime}(\mu(G))}\setminus{\cal E}_{\mu^{\prime\prime}(\mu(H))})\cup{\cal E}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}\\
&=&({\cal E}_{\mu(\mu^{\prime}(G))}\setminus{\cal E}_{\mu(\mu^{\prime}(H))})\cup{\cal E}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}\\
\end{array}

Hence
{\cal E}_{\mu(G^{\prime})}={\cal E}_{G^{\prime\prime}}
.

•

{\cal R}_{\mu(G^{\prime})}=\mu({\cal R}_{G^{\prime}})=\mu(\mu^{\prime}({\cal R}_{G}))=\mu^{\prime\prime}(\mu({\cal R}_{G}))=\mu^{\prime\prime}({\cal R}_{\mu(G)}))={\cal R}_{G^{\prime\prime}}
.

•

Assume that
\alpha=\mu^{\prime\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})
, with
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}^{*}_{\mu(G)}\setminus{\cal N}^{*}_{\mu(H)}
.
Then
\alpha^{\prime}=\mu(\alpha^{\prime\prime})
, where
\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
,
and
\mu(\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime\prime}))=\mu^{\prime\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})=\alpha
.
Furthermore,
l_{G^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{\mu(G)}(\alpha^{\prime})=l_{G}(\alpha^{\prime\prime})=l_{G^{\prime}}(\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime\prime}))=l_{\mu(G^{\prime})}(\alpha)
, because
\mu^{-1}(\alpha)=\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime\prime})
. If
\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{\mu(H^{\prime})}
, then
\alpha=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
, with
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
. Moreover, we have

l_{G^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{\mu(H^{\prime})}(\alpha)=l_{H^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})=l_{G^{\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{\mu(G^{\prime})}(\alpha)
.
Finally, if

\alpha=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
is not of one of the two forms above, then
it is easy to check that

\alpha^{\prime}\not\in{\cal N}^{*}_{H^{\prime}}
,
and that
there is no
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}=\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime\prime})
, with
\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
.
Thus
l_{G^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha)
and

l_{G^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})
are both undefined.

∎

The next lemma shows that renaming the nodes of some subgraph does not affect the graphs obtained
by replacing this subgraph.

Lemma 21
.

Let
G,H
and
H^{\prime}
be graphs, with
H\preceq G

and
H^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
.
Let
\mu
be an
{\cal N}
-mapping of domain
{\cal N}_{G}
such that

\mu(\alpha)=\alpha
if
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
.
Then:

\mu(G)[H^{\prime}/\mu(H)]=G[H^{\prime}/H]

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}G[H^{\prime}/H]
,

G^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\mu(G)[H^{\prime}/\mu(H)]
,

{\cal R}_{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
,

{\cal R}_{H^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\langle\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\rangle

and consider the following
{\cal N}
-mappings:

\mu_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\{\alpha_{i}\mapsto\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{\alpha\mapsto\alpha\mid\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}\}

and

\mu_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\{\mu(\alpha_{i})\mapsto\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{\alpha\mapsto\alpha\mid\alpha\in{\cal N}_{\mu(G)}\setminus{\cal N}_{\mu(H)}\}.

By injectivity of
\mu
, we have
{\cal N}_{\mu(G)}\setminus{\cal N}_{\mu(H)}=\mu({\cal N}_{G})\setminus\mu({\cal N}_{H})=\mu({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})={\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
, since
\mu(\alpha)=\alpha
if
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
.
Thus
\mu_{2}\circ\mu=\mu_{1}
.
We show that
G^{\prime}=G^{\prime\prime}
:

•

By Definition
15
(
1
),

{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}=({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}

and

{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}=({\cal N}_{\mu(G)}\setminus{\cal N}_{\mu(H)})\cup{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}=({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}}={\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}
.

•

By Definition
15
(
2
),

{\cal R}_{G^{\prime}}=\mu_{1}({\cal R}_{G})

and

{\cal R}_{G^{\prime\prime}}=\mu_{2}({\cal R}_{\mu(G)})=\mu_{2}(\mu({\cal R}_{G}))=\mu_{1}({\cal R}_{G})={\cal R}_{G^{\prime}}
.

•

By Definition
15
(
3
),

{\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}=\mu_{1}({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H})\cup{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}
and

{\cal E}_{G^{\prime\prime}}=\mu_{2}({\cal E}_{\mu(G)}\setminus{\cal E}_{\mu(H)})\cup{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}=\mu_{2}(\mu({\cal E}_{G})\setminus\mu({\cal E}_{H}))\cup{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}
.
Since
\mu
is injective, we deduce that

{\cal E}_{G^{\prime\prime}}=\mu_{2}(\mu({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H}))\cup{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}=\mu_{1}({\cal E}_{G}\setminus{\cal E}_{H})\cup{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}={\cal E}_{G^{\prime}}
.

•

Let
\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}^{*}_{H}
, with
\alpha=\mu_{1}(\alpha^{\prime})
, hence
\alpha=\mu_{2}(\mu(\alpha))
.
By Definition
15
(
4
),
we have
l_{G^{\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{G}(\alpha^{\prime})

and

l_{G^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{\mu(G)}(\mu(\alpha^{\prime}))=l_{G}(\alpha^{\prime})=l_{G^{\prime}}(\alpha)
.
If
\alpha\in{\cal N}^{*}_{H^{\prime}}
, then, again by
Definition
15
(
4
),
we have
l_{G^{\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{G^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha)=l_{H^{\prime}}(\alpha)
.

∎

This entails that the replacement of a subgraph by an isomorphic subgraph preserves isomorphism:

Corollary 22
.

Let
G,H,H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}
be graphs, with

H\preceq G
,
H^{\prime}_{1}
and
H^{\prime}_{2}
are substitutable for
H
in
G

and
H^{\prime}_{1}\sim H^{\prime}_{2}
. Then:

G[H_{1}^{\prime}/H]\sim G[H_{2}^{\prime}/H]

Proof.

Since
H^{\prime}_{1}\sim H^{\prime}_{2}
, there exists an
{\cal N}
-mapping
\mu
of domain
{\cal N}_{H^{\prime}_{2}}
such that

H^{\prime}_{1}=\mu(H^{\prime}_{2})
.
Consider the extension
\mu^{\prime}
of
\mu
to
{\cal N}_{G}
, such that:

\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)=\alpha
if
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}

and all the nodes in
{\cal N}_{H}\setminus{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}

are mapped to pairwise distinct nodes not occurring in
G
or
H_{1}^{\prime}
.
Observe that
\mu
is well-defined, since

{\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal N}_{H}
, as
H_{2}^{\prime}
is substitutable for

H
in
G
.
We show that
\mu^{\prime}
is injective.
Let
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
such that
\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)=\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})
.
If
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}
then
\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)=\mu(\alpha)

and
\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})=\mu(\alpha^{\prime})
hence
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
as
\mu
is injective.
If
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
then

\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)=\alpha

and
\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})=\alpha^{\prime}
hence
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
.
If
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{H}\setminus{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}
then
we have
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
by definition of
\mu^{\prime}
.
If
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H}\setminus{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}
and

\alpha^{\prime}\not\in{\cal N}_{H}\setminus{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}
, then by definition of
\mu^{\prime}
,

\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})\in{\cal N}_{G}\cup\mu^{\prime}({\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}})={\cal N}_{G}\cup{\cal N}_{H_{1}^{\prime}}

and

\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)\not\in{\cal N}_{G}\cup{\cal N}_{H_{1}^{\prime}}
, which contradicts the fact that

\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)=\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})
.
The only remaining case is (by symmetry):

\alpha\in{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}

and
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
.
Then
\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)=\mu(\alpha)\in{\cal N}_{H_{1}^{\prime}}

and
\mu^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})=\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{G}
, hence, since
H_{1}^{\prime}
is substitutable for

H
in
G
, we must have
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{H}
, contradicting the fact that
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
.

We get, by Proposition
20
, since
\mu^{\prime}
coincides with
\mu
on
{\cal N}_{H_{2}^{\prime}}
:

\mu^{\prime}(G[H_{2}^{\prime}/H])=\mu^{\prime}(G)[\mu^{\prime}(H_{2}^{\prime})/\mu^{\prime}(H)]=\mu^{\prime}(G)[\mu(H_{2}^{\prime})/\mu^{\prime}(H)]=\mu^{\prime}(G)[H_{1}^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}(H)]

By definition of
\mu^{\prime}
,
\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)=\alpha
holds for any node
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
, hence,
by Lemma
21
, we deduce:

\mu^{\prime}(G[H_{2}^{\prime}/H])=G[H_{1}^{\prime}/H]

Therefore,
G[H_{1}^{\prime}/H]\sim G[H_{2}^{\prime}/H]
.
∎

Lemma 23
.

Let
G
,
H
,
I
,
I^{\prime}
, with

H\preceq G
,
I
is substitutable for
H
in
G

and
I^{\prime}
is substitutable for
I
in
G[I/H]
.
Then
I^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
and
G[I/H][I^{\prime}/I]=G[I^{\prime}/H]
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}G[I/H]
.
We show that
I^{\prime}
is substitutable for
H
in
G
, i.e., that
{\cal N}_{G}\cap{\cal N}_{I^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal N}_{H}
. We have
{\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}=({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{I}
, and show that
({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cap{\cal N}_{I^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.
We have

({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cap{\cal N}_{I^{\prime}}\subseteq\left[({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cup{\cal N}_{I}\right]\cap{\cal N}_{I^{\prime}}={\cal N}_{G^{\prime}}\cap{\cal N}_{I^{\prime}}\subseteq{\cal N}_{I},

because
I^{\prime}
is substitutable for
I
in
G^{\prime}
by hypothesis. We deduce that
({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cap{\cal N}_{I^{\prime}}\subseteq({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cap{\cal N}_{I}
. But
({\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H})\cap{\cal N}_{I}=\emptyset
by Proposition
14
, hence the result.

We show that
G^{\prime}[I^{\prime}/I]=G[I^{\prime}/H]
. We define
\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\mu^{H\rightarrow I}_{G}
,
\mu^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\mu^{I\rightarrow I^{\prime}}_{G^{\prime}}
and
\mu^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\mu^{H\rightarrow I^{\prime}}_{G}
.
Note that
\mu^{\prime\prime}=\mu^{\prime}\circ\mu
Indeed, by definition we have
\mathit{dom}(\mu)=\mathit{dom}(\mu^{\prime})
. Further, for every
\alpha\in\mathit{dom}(\mu)
, if
\alpha\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
then

\mu(\alpha)=\mu^{\prime}(\alpha)
,
and
\alpha\not\in{\cal N}_{I}
(since
I
is substitutable for
H

in
G
), hence

\mu^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)=\alpha
.
If
\alpha\in\mathit{dom}(\mu)
and
\alpha\not\in{\cal N}_{G}\setminus{\cal N}_{H}
, then
\alpha=\alpha_{i}
, for some
i=1,\dots,n
, with

{\cal R}_{H}=\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
. By definition,

4
Graph Literals and Graph Congruences

In this section we introduce
graph literals
, which are either equations or disequations between graphs, along with
graph congruences
, which permit to define a semantics on a set of graph literals.

Definition 40
.

A graph equation is an unordered pair written
G\approx H
, where

G,H
are root-compatible graphs.
A graph disequation is the negation of a graph equation, written
G\not\approx H
.
A graph literal is either a graph equation or a graph disequation or
\bot
.
The set of variables occurring in a graph literal
{\cal L}
is defined as follows:

{\cal V}(G\approx H)={\cal V}(G\not\approx H)={\cal V}(G)\cup{\cal V}(H)

and
{\cal V}(\bot)=\emptyset
.
All substitutions
and
{\cal N}
-mappings
\mu
are extended to graph literals as follows:

\mu(G\bowtie H)=\mu(G)\bowtie\mu(H)
, for
\bowtie\in\{\approx,\not\approx\}
,
and
\mu(\bot)=\bot
.

If
{\cal L},{\cal L}^{\prime}
are graph literals, we write

7
An Application to Circuits

In this section we consider a class of graphs, denoted by
Circuits
, which intuitively consists of graphs that represent gates. Informally, a gate with
n
entries and
m

•

one node that contains the information about the number of entries and outputs;

•

n

m
nodes that represent the outputs;

•

n+m
root nodes that are used for rewriting operations.

We also define the notion of a

and show that it is a
{\cal C}
-relation, thus guaranteeing the completeness of the proof procedure defined in Section
5
.

n

n

7.1

It is natural to construct a circuit by composing simpler components. We introduce two standard composition operations: parallel and sequential composition. The empty graph is the neutral element for parallel composition, and we introduce the class of so-called

8
Discussion

We have defined an extension of the Superposition calculus to a class of graphs that is general enough to encode the graph-based languages that are used to perform diagrammatic reasoning on quantum algorithms and protocols. This calculus is complete, meaning that if a graph formula is unsatisfiable, then it is guaranteed that the calculus will generate
\bot
. We now intend to adapt an implementation of the Saturation calculus to design a tool on which we will be able to evaluate the efficiency of the calculus and investigate how it can be improved for specific subclasses of graphs – for example, by defining suitable orderings for such subclasses in order to reduce the search space as much as possible.

There are several lines of future work that would be interesting to
explore. We plan to investigate how variables representing graphs can
be introduced into the calculus. During the construction of a
refutation using a calculus that can handle such variables, these
variables would be instantiated by graphs with specific properties
that guarantee correctness, and would for example permit to
automatically synthesize circuits.
Another promising topic is to extend graph literals with constraints. This would permit to represent algorithms in a more concise or natural way, while still being able to automatically verify these algorithms.

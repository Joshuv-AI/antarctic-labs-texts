A noncommutative maximal inequality for ergodic averages along arithmetic sets
Cheng Chen, Guixiang Hong, Liang Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a noncommutative maximal inequality for ergodic averages with respect to the set { k t | k = 1 , 2 , 3 , … } \{k^{t}|k=1,2,3,...\} acting on noncommutative L p L_{p} spaces for p > 5 + 1 2 p>\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2} .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46L51; Secondary 42B20

1.
Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mu,T)
be a dynamical system, where
\mu
denotes a probability measure,
T
stands for an invertible measure-preserving transformation.
The Birkhoff ergodic theorem indicates that for any
f\in L_{1}(\Omega)
, the ergodic average

A_{N}f:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}T^{k}f

converges almost everywhere as
N\rightarrow\infty
. It is commonly recognized that in numerous instances, establishing a maximal ergodic inequality suffices to derive a pointwise ergodic theorem. For instance, Wiener’s weak
(1,1)
type estimate of the maximal operator associated to the time averages, as detailed in
[
29
]
, could deduce the Birkhoff ergodic theorem. In classical ergodic theory, the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, together with this strategy, was surrounded by numerous significant advancements. For instance, Dunford and Schwartz
[
9
]
established the strong
(p,p)
maximal inequalities for all
1<p<\infty
in the context of the ergodic average associated with a positive
L_{1}
-
L_{\infty}
contraction
T
. Subsequently, Akcoglu
[
1
]
demonstrated that the validity of these strong
(p,p)
maximal inequalities can be assured even when
T
is merely a positive
L_{p}
contraction. These results, viewed as ergodic theorems associated to the action of the group
\mathbb{Z}
, have been extended to a considerably large class of groups. For instance, after a series of progress made by Calderón, Wiener, Gromov among others, Lindenstrauss
[
23
]
further generalized the Birkhoff ergodic theorem to arbitrary amenable groups, broadening its scope and applicability. See the survey
[
27
]
by Nevo for ergodic theory associated to group actions. Motivated partially by the generalized uniform distribution problem, in his celebrated works
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, Bourgain established the pointwise ergodic theorem for certain subset of integers by demonstrating the
L_{p}
-boundedness of maximal function for
p>1
. Specifically, Bourgain’s results indicate that for any
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
with
p>1
, the ergodic average

A_{N}f:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}T^{k^{t}}f

converges almost everywhere as
N\rightarrow\infty
, and the maximal function
\sup_{N\geq 1}|A_{N}f|
is
L_{p}
-bounded. Here
t\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Bourgain’s work has been playing a central role in the development of the so-called discrete Fourier analysis, and one can find more information in the survey
[
10
]
[Sections II.1 and II. 3] and a recent work
[
19
]
as well as the references therein.

On the other hand, motivated by the quantum mechanics, operator algebra, noncommutative geometry, and noncommutative martingale theory, there appear more and more research papers on ergodic theory in the noncommutative setting. In the nascent stages of noncommutative ergodic theory, the focus was primarily on mean ergodic theorems. It was Lance’s groundbreaking work
[
21
]
that ignited the study of individual ergodic theorems, establishing noncommutative generalizations of the Birkhoff ergodic theorem for bounded functions. This topic then gained significant momentum, leading to a series of influential studies (see e.g.
[
8
,
20
]
). Notably, after Yeadon’s weak
(1,1)
type maximal ergodic inequality
[
30
]
, Junge and Xu
[
18
]
obtained the full version of noncommutative analogue of the Dunford-Schwartz ergodic theorem. Their groundbreaking work has since inspired numerous subsequent studies on noncommutative ergodic theorems, see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
15
,
17
,
24
,
16
,
22
]
and references therein. Beyond the class of Dunford-Schwartz operators explored by Junge and Xu, Hong
[
12
]
studied the noncommutative ergodic averages of balls and spheres over Euclidean spaces. Furthermore, Hong, Liao and Wang
[
14
]
obtained noncommutative extensions of the Calderón pointwise ergodic theorem for groups of polynomial growth. Recently, Cadilhac and Wang
[
7
]
established the maximal ergodic inequality for ergodic averages of amenable groups, marking a noncommutative analogue of the Lindenstrauss ergodic theorem. Despite these advancements, to the best of our knowledge, there remains a gap in the literature regarding a noncommutative analogue of Bourgain’s ergodic theorem presented in
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. In this paper, we delve into this unexplored territory, aiming to bridge this gap and contribute to the understanding of noncommutative ergodic theory by exploring the noncommutative analogue of Bourgain’s seminal results.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau
, and
\gamma
be an automorphism on
\mathcal{N}
satisfying
\tau=\tau\circ\gamma
. The noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces associated to
(\mathcal{N},\tau)
is denoted by
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
, then it is well-known that
\gamma
extends to an isometry on
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We introduce the noncommutative
ergodic averages associated with
\gamma
on
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
as follows,

A_{N}x:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma^{k^{t}}(x),\ x\in\mathcal{N},

(1.1)

where
t\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then we have the following noncommutative maximal ergodic inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
p>\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
, the following inequality

\Big\|{\sup_{N}}^{+}A_{N}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}\leq C_{p}\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}

(1.2)

holds for all
x\in{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}
.

We refer the reader to the next section for the precise definition of the noncommutative maximal norm. Theorem
1.1
extends Bourgain’s result in
[
5
]
to the noncommutative setting. Extending these results to the noncommutative setting has been a challenging task, due to the inherent complexities and subtleties associated with noncommutative algebras.
Our approach to overcoming these difficulties was to establish a noncommutative analogue of the sampling principle, as outlined in
[
25
]
. This principle allows us to adapt Bourgain’s method to the noncommutative case, the successful application of this method not only validates the strength and generality of Bourgain’s approach but also opens up new avenues for exploring ergodic theory in noncommutative settings.

Remark 1.2
.

Up to now, due to the noncommutativity, it remains an outstanding challenge to obtain Theorem
1.1
for all
p>1
as in Bourgain’s work
[
6
]
. The noncommutative framework introduces complexities that have hindered generalizations to the full range of
p
values.

On the other hand, Bourgain’s groundbreaking approach in
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
successfully utilized the quantitative maximal ergodic inequality to derive the pointwise ergodic theorem. This method circumvented the critical obstacle posed by the absence of a natural dense set of functions for which pointwise convergence holds. However, in the noncommutative setting, Bourgain’s method faces a new hurdle: the need to tackle a distribution inequality. In the commutative case, distribution inequalities are well-understood and can be effectively employed. But in the noncommutative realm, dealing with such inequalities appears to be an extremely daunting task. The noncommutative structure introduces additional complexities that make it challenging to adapt Bourgain’s method directly.

Consequently, obtaining a noncommutative pointwise ergodic theorem through the noncommutative maximal ergodic inequality presented in our paper remains an open problem. Despite the progress made in Theorem
1.1
, further research is needed to bridge this gap and extend Bourgain’s pointwise ergodic result to the noncommutative setting.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we provide a review of noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces, the noncommutative
\ell_{\infty}
-valued
L_{p}
spaces, and some auxiliary results from
[
5
]
. This section serves as a foundation for the subsequent discussions, establishing notation and terminology used throughout the paper.

In Section
3
, we delve into the noncommutative realm, proving a noncommutative sampling principle (see Lemma
3.5
). This principle is a crucial component in our analysis, enabling us to extend Bourgain’s ideas to the noncommutative setting.

Finally, in Section
4
, we present the complete proof of Theorem
1.1
. This theorem establishes the noncommutative maximal ergodic inequality, which is the main result of this paper. By leveraging the noncommutative sampling principle and other techniques, we demonstrate the validity of this inequality within the specified range of
p
.

Notation:
In the subsequent discussion,
L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}}
represents the tensor von Neumann algebra associated with a measurable space
(\Omega,\mu)
and a semifinite von Neumann algebra
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. The notation
A\lesssim B
is used to indicate that there exists a positive constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
, and
A\sim B
means that both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold.
Throughout the whole article, we denote by
C
a positive constant which is independent of the main parameters, but it may
vary from line to line. For a given function
f
,
\Re f
denotes the real part of
f
while
\Im f
represents the imaginary part.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra endowed with a normal semifinite faithful trace
\tau
.

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})_{+}
denotes the set of all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
such that
\tau(\mathrm{supp}(x))<\infty
, where
\mathrm{supp}(x)
is the support of
x
. The linear span of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})_{+}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
and constitutes a weak*- dense *-subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is defined as

\|x\|_{p}=(\tau(|x|^{p}))^{1/p},\quad x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M}),

where
|x|=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
is the modulus of
x
. This norm induces a completion of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
known as the noncommutative
L_{p}
space associated to
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, denoted by
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.
As usual, we set
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{M}
, and
\|x\|_{\infty}:=\|x\|_{\mathcal{M}}
.
For more information on noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces, we refer to
[
28
]
.

Frequently, we will make use of the operator-valued version of the Hölder inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
f
is a
\mathcal{M}_{+}
-valued function on a measurable space
(\Sigma,\mu)
and
g
is a positive integrable function. Then for
1<p<\infty
, we have the following operator inequality,

\int_{\Sigma}f(x)g(x)d\mu(x)\leq\Big(\int_{\Sigma}|g(x)|^{p^{\prime}}d\mu(x)\Big)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\cdot\Big(\int_{\Sigma}|f(x)|^{p}d\mu(x)\Big)^{1/p},

whenever all the terms above make sense. Here,
p^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate index of
p
, i.e.
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

This inequality is well-known in noncommutative analysis and can be found, for instance, in
[
13
, Lemma 2.4]
.

2.2.
Noncommutative
\ell_{\infty}
-valued
L_{p}
spaces

Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
and an index set
\Lambda
, the space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
is the space of all
x=(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
that can be factorized as:

x_{\lambda}=ay_{\lambda}b,\ \lambda\in\Lambda,

where
a,\ b\in L_{2p}(\mathcal{M}),\ y_{\lambda}\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
\ \sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|y_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
The norm in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
is defined by

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))}:=\inf_{x_{\lambda}=ay_{\lambda}b}\Big\{\|a\|_{2p}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|y_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{2p}\Big\}.

and this norm is commonly denoted by
\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}x_{\lambda}\|_{p}
.
It was shown in
[
18
]
that
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
if and only if

\sup\Big\{\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda\in I}}^{+}\ x_{\lambda}\Big\|_{p}\ :\ I\ \text{ is a finite subset of}\ \Lambda\Big\}<\infty.

In fact,
\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}x_{\lambda}\|_{p}
is equal to this supremum. Moreover, when
x=(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a sequence of self-adjoint operators, then
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
if and only if there exists a positive element
a\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
such that for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
-a\leq x_{\lambda}\leq a
, and

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}\ x_{\lambda}\Big\|_{p}=\inf\Big\{\|a\|_{p}:\ a\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})_{+},-a\leq x_{\lambda}\leq a,\ \forall\lambda\in\Lambda\Big\}.

For brevity, when no confusion arises, we denote the space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
simply by
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
.

Additionally, the following useful interpolation result can be found in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
. Let
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
, then we have

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})=\big(L_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}),L_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})\big)_{\theta}.

2.3.
Exponential sums and approximate kernels

Let
\mathbb{T}
be the dual group of
\mathbb{Z}
isomorphic to
[0,1)
and let
K_{N}
be the discrete measure defined as
K_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{\{k^{t}\}}
. The Fourier transform of
K_{N}
is given by the Guass-Weyl sum, expressed as

\hat{K}_{N}(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}e^{-2\pi ik^{t}\theta},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}.

(2.1)

Now, fix a constant
0<\rho<2
and let
\{p_{j}\}
be the sequence of consecutive primes. For any
1\leq m\leq 2^{\rho s},s\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, one can define

Q_{s,m}=\prod_{(m-1)2^{s}<j\leq m2^{s}}p_{j}^{st},

and the set

\mathfrak{R}_{s}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}|\ \theta\cdot Q_{s,1}\cdot Q_{s,m}\in\mathbb{Z},\ \text{for some}\ 2\leq m\leq 2^{\rho s}\}.

Observe that
\mathfrak{R}_{s}
can be rewritten as

\mathfrak{R}_{s}=\mathbb{Z}_{Q{s,1}}\cup\Big(\bigcup_{2\leq m\leq 2^{\rho s}}(\mathbb{Z}_{Q{s,1}\cdot Q{s,m}}\backslash\mathbb{Z}_{Q{s,1}})\Big),

(2.2)

where

\mathbb{Z}_{Q}:=\{a/Q:0\leq a<Q\}.

Then
\{\mathfrak{R}_{s}\}
is an increasing sequence whose union is
\mathbb{T}\cap\mathbb{Q}
and each
\mathfrak{R}_{s}
is the disjoint union of
2^{\rho s}
differences of cyclic subgroups of
\mathbb{T}
. For
\xi\in\mathbb{T}\cap\mathbb{Q}
, expressed as
\xi=a/q
with
(a,q)=1
, one can define

S(\xi):=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{0\leq r<q}e^{-2\pi ir^{t}a/q}.

There exists an estimate for
S(\xi)
(see e.g.
[
5
, Lemma 4]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

If
a/q\in\mathbb{T}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s}
, then
|S(a/q)|\leq C2^{-(1+\rho)s/2}.

One can define an approximate kernel
L_{N}
whose Fourier transform is expressed as

\hat{L}_{N}(\theta)=\hat{k}(N^{t}\theta)\phi(\theta)+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi)).

(2.3)

Here,
k(x):=t^{-1}x^{1/t-1}\chi_{[0,1]}(x)
is the kernel function, and
D_{s}
is an integer depending on
s
that satisfies the conditions

\log Q_{s,m}<\frac{1}{100}\log D_{s}\ (1\leq m\leq 2^{s\rho})\quad\text{and}\ \log D_{s}\leq Cs^{2}2^{s}.

The function
\phi
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
that equals
1
on
[\frac{-1}{10},\frac{1}{10}]
and vanishes outside
[\frac{-1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
.

Two crucial estimates for the approximate kernels are provided in the literature (see
[
5
, Lemma 5, Lemma 6]
for details).

Lemma 2.4
.

For
0<\rho^{\prime}<\rho
, one has

\|\hat{K}_{N}-\hat{L}_{N}\|_{\infty}\leq C(\log N)^{-(1+\rho^{\prime})/2}.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
q<D_{s}
, there is a uniform estimate

\Big\|\sum_{0\leq a<q}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(a/q)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-a/q))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-a/q))e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C.

Moreover, one has

\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C2^{\rho s}

and

\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C2^{\rho s}.

Remark 2.6
.

It is worth pointing out that the reference
[
5
]
assumes that
0<\rho<1
, however, we find that we need some
\rho>1
to make sure Theorem
1.1
holds for all
p>\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
, see the equality (
4.27
) for more details. Therefore, we assume that
0<\rho<2
, which is sufficient to guarantee the validity of all the estimates required in this subsection. Moreover, we believe that even the reference
[
5
]
should assume that
0<\rho<2
.

3.
A noncommutative sampling principle

In this section, we aim to establish a noncommutative analogue of the sampling principle stated in
[
25
]
.
Given the function
\Phi(x):=\Big(\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}\Big)^{2}
defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for a suitable operator-valued function
f
on
\mathbb{Z}
, we define its extension
f_{ext}
on
\mathbb{R}
by

f_{ext}(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\Phi(x-n).

(3.1)

Note that the series above converges for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
whenever
f
belongs to
L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty.
Furthermore, we have the following equivalence.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, if
f\in L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
, then we have

\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\sim\|f_{ext}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f
is positive. Given that
1\leq p<\infty
. First, recognizing that both
f
and
\Phi
are positive, we fix
x\in\mathbb{R}
, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

f_{ext}(x)\leq\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{p}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{(p-1)/p}.

Then, combining with the facts (see
[
25
, page 193]
)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)dx\lesssim 1,\qquad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(x-n)\lesssim 1,

we can further deduce that

\displaystyle\|f_{ext}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{p}\Phi(x-n)\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{p-1}dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\tau\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{p}\Big)\times\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)dx\Big)\times\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}^{p}.

On the other hand, let
\Psi
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
such that its Fourier transform
\widehat{\Psi}
equals
1
on
[-1,1]
and has compact support. One can see e.g.
[
25
, page 194]
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(x)|dx\lesssim 1,\qquad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\Psi(n-x)|\lesssim 1

and

f(n)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{ext}(x)\Psi(n-x)dx,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that
\Psi
can be expressed as a linear combination of four positive functions
\Psi_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 4
that satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{i}dx\lesssim 1,\qquad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi_{i}(n-x)\lesssim 1.

Precisely, we define
\Psi_{1}=\big(\Re(\Psi)\big)_{+},\Psi_{2}=\big(\Re(\Psi)\big)_{-},\Psi_{3}=\big(\Im(\Psi)\big)_{+},\Psi_{4}=\big(\Im(\Psi)\big)_{-}.

Let
f_{i}(n):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{ext}(x)\Psi_{i}(n-x)dx
, by invoking Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

f_{i}(n)\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f_{ext}(x)|^{p}\Psi_{i}(n-x)dx\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{i}(n-x)dx\Big)^{(p-1)/p}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|f_{i}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}^{p}=\tau\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{i}(n)|^{p}\Big)\lesssim\tau\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f_{ext}(x)|^{p}\Psi_{i}(n-x)dx\Big)\lesssim\|f_{ext}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}^{p}.

Now,
\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|f_{i}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|f_{ext}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}
,
this completes the proof for
1\leq p<\infty
. For
p=\infty
, the proof is indeed simpler as we only need to consider the supremum norms directly.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, if
(f_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}\in L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
, then we have

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\sim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(f_{\lambda})_{ext}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

Proof.

Firstly, we assume that
f_{\lambda}
is self-adjoint for
\lambda>0
.
Since
f_{\lambda}
is self-adjoint for
\lambda>0
, then
for
\epsilon>0
, there exists
0<a_{\epsilon}\in L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
such that
-a_{\epsilon}\leq f_{\lambda}\leq a_{\epsilon}
for
\lambda>0
, and

\|a_{\epsilon}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\epsilon.

As in Lemma
3.1
, we have

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{\epsilon}(n)\Phi(x-n)\leq\Big(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{\epsilon}(n)|^{p}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{(p-1)/p}.

and

-\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{\epsilon}(n)\Phi(x-n)\leq(f_{\lambda})_{ext}(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{\lambda}(n)\Phi(x-n)\leq\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{\epsilon}(n)\Phi(x-n).

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(f_{\lambda})_{ext}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{\epsilon}(n)|^{p}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(x-n)\Big)^{(p-1)/p}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a_{\epsilon}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\epsilon.

Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we deduce

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(f_{\lambda})_{ext}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

(3.2)

For a general
f_{\lambda}
, we consider its real and imaginary parts,
\Re(f_{\lambda})=\frac{f_{\lambda}+f^{*}_{\lambda}}{2}
and
\Im(f_{\lambda})=\frac{f_{\lambda}-f^{*}_{\lambda}}{2i}
respectively, by applying the triangle inequality along with inequality (
3.2
), we obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(f_{\lambda})_{ext}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(\Re(f_{\lambda}))_{ext}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(\Im(f_{\lambda}))_{ext}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\Re(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\Im(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}^{*}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

Moreover, by combining the arguments presented above with the same technique employed in Lemma
3.1
, we can derive the reverse inequality as well.
∎

Utilizing the lemmas previously established, we arrive at the following noncommutative sampling principle.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{\phi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
be a sequence of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}
and
supp(\phi_{\lambda})\subset(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
,
T_{\phi_{\lambda}}
is the Fourier multiplier operator associated with symbol
\phi_{\lambda}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, if the estimate

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}T_{\phi_{\lambda}}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

(3.3)

holds for all
f\in{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}
, then the following estimate

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

(3.4)

holds for all
f\in{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}
, where
(T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(f)(n):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n-m)\big(\phi_{\lambda}\big)^{\vee}(m)
.

Proof.

Define a function
\widetilde{\phi_{\lambda}}
on
\mathbb{R}
by

\widetilde{\phi_{\lambda}}(\xi):=\sum_{|\ell|\leq 1}\phi_{\lambda}(\xi+\ell),

and let
T_{\widetilde{\phi_{\lambda}}}
be the Fourier multiplier operator associated with
\widetilde{\phi_{\lambda}}
. By applying the estimate (
3.3
) and the triangle inequality, we obtain

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}T_{\widetilde{\phi_{\lambda}}}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\sum_{|\ell|\leq 1}\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}T_{\phi_{\lambda}}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

(3.5)

Furthermore, one can verify that (see
[
25
, pages 194-195]
)

T_{\widetilde{\phi_{\lambda}}}(f_{ext})=\big((T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(f)\big)_{ext}.

(3.6)

Employing Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
along with the inequality (
3.5
) and equality (
3.6
), we deduce

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\big((T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(f)\big)_{ext}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}T_{\widetilde{\phi_{\lambda}}}(f_{ext})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{ext}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

∎

To effectively apply the sampling principle in our context, we require a modified version of the noncommutative sampling principle.
Let
q
be a positive integer, and define
\alpha
as the isomorphism from
q\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}
, where

\alpha(n_{1}q,n_{2}):=n_{1}q+n_{2},\quad n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z},\ 0\leq n_{2}<q.

Now, given an operator-valued function
f
mapping from
\mathbb{Z}
to a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, we introduce the associated function
\alpha(f)
which maps from

q\mathbb{Z}
to
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
as follows,

\big((\alpha(f))(n_{1}q)\big)(n_{2}):=f(n_{1}q+n_{2}),\quad n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z},\ 0\leq n_{2}<q.

Furthermore, we define a function
\sigma(f)
mapping from
q\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathcal{M}
according to

(\sigma(f))(nq):=f(n),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that

\tau\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\sigma(f)(nq)\big|^{p}\Big)=\tau\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|f(n)\big|^{p}\Big)=\tau\Big(\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{q-1}\big|f(n_{1}q+n_{2})\big|^{p}\Big),

we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma(f)\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}^{p}=\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}^{p}=\|\alpha(f)\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}^{p}.

(3.7)

Furthermore, we establish the following crucial equivalence of the maximal norms.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}\in L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
, then, the following holds,

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\sim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\alpha(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\sim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sigma(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary. Since
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}\in L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
, there exists
a,b,F_{\lambda}
, such that

f_{\lambda}=aF_{\lambda}b
, and

\|a\|_{L_{2p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}{\sup_{\lambda>0}}\|F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}}\|b\|_{L_{2p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\epsilon

Hence
\alpha(f_{\lambda})=\alpha(a)\alpha(F_{\lambda})\alpha(b)
, then we have

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\alpha(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha(a)\|_{2p}{\sup_{\lambda>0}}\|\alpha(F_{\lambda})\|_{\infty}\|\alpha(b)\|_{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{L_{2p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}{\sup_{\lambda>0}}\|F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}}\|b\|_{L_{2p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\epsilon.

Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\alpha(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

Via a similar argument, we can deduce the inverse inequality

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\alpha(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}.

Therefore

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\sim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\alpha(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}.

Moreover, we also have

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}f_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\sim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sigma(f_{\lambda})\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}.

∎

Now, consider a smooth function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}
that is supported on
(-\frac{1}{2q},\frac{1}{2q})
. One can define
\phi_{per}^{q}
as

\phi_{per}^{q}(\xi):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(\xi-n/q).

Let
(T_{\phi}^{q})_{dis}
be the Fourier multiplier operator associated with symbol
\phi_{per}^{q}
, which means that for suitable
f
, we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}((T_{\phi}^{q})_{dis}(f))(n)e^{-2\pi in\xi}=\phi_{per}^{q}(\xi)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)e^{-2\pi in\xi}.

(3.8)

To understand the operator better, let us determine its kernel
K_{dis}^{q}
. We compute

\displaystyle K_{dis}^{q}(m)

\displaystyle=\int_{(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})}\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(\xi-n/q)\Big)e^{2\pi im\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{q},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{q})}\phi(\xi)e^{2\pi im\xi}d\xi e^{2\pi i\frac{mn}{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{q},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{q})\cap(-\frac{1}{2q}.\frac{1}{2q})}\phi(\xi)e^{2\pi im\xi}d\xi e^{2\pi i\frac{mn}{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|<\frac{q+1}{2}}\int_{(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{q},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{q})\cap(-\frac{1}{2q}.\frac{1}{2q})}\phi(\xi)e^{2\pi im\xi}d\xi e^{2\pi i\frac{mn}{q}}

Observing the above expression, one can find that
K_{dis}^{q}(m)=0
if
m
is not divisible by
q
, and
K_{dis}^{q}(m)=q\check{\phi}(m)
if
q
divides
m
.
Hence

((T_{\phi}^{q})_{dis}(f))(n)=q\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n-mq)(\phi)^{\vee}(mq).

(3.9)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\{\phi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
be a sequence of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}
and
supp(\phi_{\lambda})\subset(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
,
T_{\phi_{\lambda}}
is the Fourier multiplier operator with the multiplier function
\phi_{\lambda}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, if the estimate

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}T_{\phi_{\lambda}}(g)\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

(3.10)

holds for all
g\in{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}
and arbitrary semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
. Then the following estimate

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(T_{\phi_{\lambda,q}}^{q})_{dis}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

(3.11)

holds for all
f\in L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)
and arbitrary semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, where
\phi_{\lambda,q}(\xi):=\phi_{\lambda}(q\xi)
.

Proof.

Fix a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, given
f\in L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)
.
Utilizing equality (
3.9
), we derive the following equations

\displaystyle\big(\alpha((T_{\phi_{\lambda,q}}^{q})_{dis}(f))(n_{1}q)\big)(n_{2})

\displaystyle=\big((T_{\phi_{\lambda,q}}^{q})_{dis}(f)\big)(n_{1}q+n_{2})

\displaystyle=q\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n_{1}q+n_{2}-mq)(\phi_{\lambda,q})^{\vee}(mq)

\displaystyle=q\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\big((\alpha(f))((n_{1}-m)q)\big)(n_{2})(\phi_{\lambda,q})^{\vee}(mq)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\big((\sigma^{-1}(\alpha(f)))(n_{1}-m)\big)(n_{2})(\phi_{\lambda})^{\vee}(m).

This implies that

\sigma^{-1}(\alpha((T_{\phi_{\lambda}}^{q})_{dis}(f)))(n_{1})=\alpha((T_{\phi_{\lambda}}^{q})_{dis}(f))(n_{1}q)=(T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(\sigma^{-1}(\alpha(f)))(n_{1}).

(3.12)

Here,
\sigma^{-1}
denotes the map such that
\sigma^{-1}\circ\sigma=id
.
By invoking inequality (
3.10
), Lemma
3.3
implies the following inequality

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(g)\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

(3.13)

holds for all
g\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
.
Finally, by combining the inequalities (
3.7
), (
3.12
), (
3.13
), along with Lemma
3.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(T_{\phi_{\lambda,q}}^{q})_{dis}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\sim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\big(\alpha((T_{\phi_{\lambda,q}}^{q})_{dis}(f))\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\sim\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sigma^{-1}(\alpha((T_{\phi_{\lambda}}^{q})_{dis}(f)))\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(T_{\phi_{\lambda}})_{dis}(\sigma^{-1}(\alpha(f)))\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sigma^{-1}(\alpha(f))\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\alpha(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|f\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}.

∎

4.
Proof of the main result

In this section,
\mathcal{N}
denotes a semifinite von Neumann algebra, fixed as per the notation employed in Theorem
1.1
.

4.1.
Some lemmas

The lemmas presented in this subsection are crucial to our ultimate goal of deriving the noncommutative maximal ergodic inequality. These lemmas provide key estimates and properties that will be used in subsequent derivations.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k(x):=t^{-1}x^{1/t-1}\chi_{[0,1]}(x)
, where
t>0
. For
1<p<\infty
, we have the following inequality,

\Big\|{\sup_{\eta>0}}^{+}f*k_{\eta}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}})},

where
k_{\eta}(x)=\frac{1}{\eta}k\big(\frac{x}{\eta}\big)
and
{\mathcal{M}}
is an arbitrary semifinite von Neumann algebra. The constant
C
only depends on
p
and
t
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})_{+}\otimes\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{M}})_{+}
. Then

\displaystyle(f*k_{\eta})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\eta}t^{-1}f(x-y)(\frac{y}{\eta})^{1/t-1}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{2^{-(k+1)}\eta}^{2^{-k}\eta}t^{-1}f(x-y)(\frac{y}{\eta})^{1/t-1}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{-k})^{1/t}}{2^{-k}\eta}\int_{2^{-(k+1)}\eta}^{2^{-k}\eta}t^{-1}f(x-y)dy.

Hence by the triangle inequality and the noncommutative Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequality (see e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 3.3]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\eta>0}}^{+}f*k_{\eta}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t^{-1}2^{-k/t}\Big\|{\sup_{\eta>0}}^{+}\frac{1}{2^{-k}\eta}\int_{2^{-(k+1)}\eta}^{2^{-k}\eta}f(x-y)dy\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t^{-1}2^{-k/t}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}})}.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a smooth function supported on
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
in
\mathbb{R}
and let
1\leq q<D
. Then

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{0\leq a<q}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\varphi(D\beta)\hat{f}(a/q+\beta)e^{2\pi im(a/q+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.1)

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda}(\xi):=\hat{k}(\frac{\lambda\xi}{q})\varphi(\frac{D\xi}{q})
, then
supp(\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda})\subset(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
and we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq a<q}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{k}(\lambda(\beta-a/q))\varphi(D(\beta-a/q))\hat{f}(\beta)e^{2\pi im\beta}d\beta=\big((T^{q}_{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,q}})_{dis}(f)\big)(m),

(4.2)

where
(T^{q}_{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,q}})_{dis}
is defined as in equality (
3.8
).
By applying Lemma
4.1
and the Young inequality, for all
g\in{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}
and arbitrary semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}T_{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda}}(g)\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}g*k_{\lambda/q}*(\check{\varphi})_{D/q}\Big\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g*(\check{\varphi})_{D/q}\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{p}\big(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)}.

(4.3)

Then combining equality (
4.2
) with Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{0\leq a<q}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\varphi(D\beta)\hat{f}(a/q+\beta)e^{2\pi im(a/q+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{0\leq a<q}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{k}(\lambda(\beta-a/q))\varphi(D(\beta-a/q))\hat{f}(\beta)e^{2\pi im\beta}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}(T^{q}_{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,q}})_{dis}(f)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
p\in(\frac{3\rho+2}{2\rho+1},2]
, we have the following estimate,

\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

Proof.

It suffices to show for any
N>0
, there exists
c(p)>\frac{1}{p}
, such that

\Big\|f*(K_{N}-L_{N})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C(\log(N))^{-c(p)}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.4)

On one hand, by applying Lemma
2.5
and the Young inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\hat{f}(\theta)e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\hat{f}(\theta)e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle+\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\hat{f}(\theta)e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{s\rho}\|f\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.5)

On the other hand,we observe that for any distinct
\xi
and
\eta
in
\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}
, we have
D_{s}|\xi-\eta|>2
. This implies that for a fixed
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
, there can be at most one
\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}
such that
\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\neq 0
. Utilizing Lemma
2.3
, one has

\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\Big|\leq C2^{-\frac{(1+\rho)s}{2}}.

(4.6)

Then by combining the Parseval identity with inequality (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\hat{f}(\theta)e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\Big|^{2}|\hat{f}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-\frac{(1+\rho)s}{2}}\Big\{\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\hat{f}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}=C2^{-\frac{(1+\rho)s}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.7)

By interpolation between (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), for
r\in(1,2)
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\hat{f}(\theta)e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-s(2\rho+1-\frac{3\rho+1}{r})}\|f\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.8)

Hence if
r>\frac{3\rho+1}{2\rho+1}
, by combining the Young inequality with (
4.8
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\Big\|f*L_{N}\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta))\phi(\theta)\hat{f}(\theta)e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\backslash\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{k}(N^{t}(\theta-\xi))\phi(D_{s}(\theta-\xi))\hat{f}(\theta)e^{2\pi i\theta m}d\theta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}2^{-s(2\rho+1-\frac{3\rho+1}{r})}\|f\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.9)

Since it is straightforward to show that
\Big\|f*K_{N}\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}
, we can derive the following inequality,

\Big\|f*(K_{N}-L_{N})\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.10)

Applying Lemma
2.4
and the Parseval identity, for
0<\rho^{\prime}<\rho
, we have

\Big\|f*(K_{N}-L_{N})\Big\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C(\log(N))^{-\frac{1+\rho^{\prime}}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.11)

By interpolation again, fix
p>\frac{3\rho+2}{2\rho+1}
, we arrive at

\Big\|f*(K_{N}-L_{N})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C(\log(N))^{-(1+\rho^{\prime})\frac{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}}{\frac{2}{r}-1}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.12)

Then we can choose
\rho^{\prime}<\rho,r>\frac{3\rho+1}{2\rho+1}
such that
(1+\rho^{\prime})\frac{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}}{\frac{2}{r}-1}>\frac{1}{p}
, thereby completing the proof of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For
p\in(\frac{2\rho+1}{\rho+1},2]
, we have the following estimate,

\Big\|{\sup_{N}}^{+}f*L_{N}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

Proof.

For a fixed value of
s
,
\Lambda_{p,s}
denotes the best constant that satisfies the following inequality,

\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq\Lambda_{p,s}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.13)

Utilizing this inequality in conjunction with Lemma
4.1
, we can draw parallels to the proof of inequality (
4.9
) and arrive at the following deduction,

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{N}}^{+}f*L_{N}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{p,s}\Big)\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.14)

Hence it suffices to show that the series
\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{p,s}<\infty
. Observe that the set
\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}
can be expressed as the disjoint union of at most
2^{s\rho}
subset
\Gamma_{i}
of
\mathbb{T}
, where each
\Gamma_{i}
is a difference of cyclic subgroups. Let
\psi
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
that equals
1
on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
and vanishes outside
[-1,1]
.
One can define the function

F_{i}(m):=\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))\hat{f}(\alpha)e^{2\pi im\alpha}d\alpha.

Observing that for distinct
\xi
and
\eta
in
\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}
, we have
D_{s}|\xi-\eta|>2
. As a result, the support properties of
\phi
and
\psi
ensure that
\psi(D_{s}(\xi+\beta-\eta))\phi(D_{s}\beta)
vanishes for all
\beta\in\mathbb{T}
when
\xi\neq\eta
. Therefore, we can rewrite the expression as follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{\eta\in\Gamma_{i}}\hat{f}(\xi+\beta)S(\eta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\psi(D_{s}(\xi+\beta-\eta))\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{F_{i}}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta.

(4.15)

For fixed
\Gamma_{i}
, we may assume that
\Gamma_{i}=\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}\backslash\mathbb{Z}_{q_{2}}
for some
q_{2}<q_{1}<D_{s}
. Utilizing Lemma
4.2
for
1<r\leq 2
and the triangle inequality, we can derive the following inequality,

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{F_{i}}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{\ell}}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{F_{i}}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\|F_{i}\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}=C_{r}\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))\hat{f}(\alpha)e^{2\pi im\alpha}d\alpha\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.16)

Furthermore, as demonstrated in
[
5
, page 84]
, the inequality

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))\Big)^{\vee}\Big\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}<C

holds true for a constant C that is independent of
i
. By the equality (
4.15
), the triangle inequality, inequality (
4.16
) and the Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{F_{i}}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{F_{i}}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\sum_{i}\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))\hat{f}(\alpha)e^{2\pi im\alpha}d\alpha\Big\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}2^{s\rho}\|f\|_{L_{r}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.17)

When
r=2
, we can employ the triangle inequality, inequality (
4.16
), the Parseval identity, Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Lemma
2.3
to derive the following,

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{\lambda>0}}^{+}\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{F_{i}}(\xi+\beta)\hat{k}(\lambda\beta)\phi(D_{s}\beta)e^{2\pi im(\xi+\beta)}d\beta\Big\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}\Big\|\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))\hat{f}(\alpha)e^{2\pi im\alpha}d\alpha\Big\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}\Big\{\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))|^{2}|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}d\alpha\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{i}\Big\{\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}|S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))|^{2}|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}d\alpha\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\frac{s\rho}{2}}\Big\{\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma_{i}}|S(\xi)\psi(D_{s}(\alpha-\xi))|^{2}|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}d\alpha\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\frac{s\rho}{2}}\cdot 2^{\frac{-s(1+\rho)}{2}}\Big\{\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}d\alpha\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}=C2^{\frac{-s}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.18)

The final inequality use the fact that
\psi(D_{s}(\cdot-\xi))
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}\setminus\mathfrak{R}_{s-1}
have disjoint supports. Now, for
1<r<2
and
p\in(r,2)
, let
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{\theta}{r}
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Interpolating between inequalities (
4.17
) and (
4.18
), we obtain

\Lambda_{p,s}\leq C_{r}2^{\frac{-s(1-\theta)}{2}+s\rho\theta}=C_{r}2^{\frac{-s(1-(2\rho+1)\theta)}{2}}=C_{r}2^{\frac{-s\big(1-(\frac{2}{p}-1)\frac{r(2\rho+1)}{2-r}\big)}{2}}.

If
p>\frac{2\rho+1}{\rho+1}
, we can find
r\in(1,2)
such that

p>\frac{2}{1+\frac{2-r}{r(2\rho+1)}},

which ensures that
\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{p,s}<\infty.

∎

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

As in the commutative setting
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, Theorem
1.1
can be simplified to the case of the shift operation on
\mathbb{Z}
. More precisely,
for any
\mathcal{N}
-valued function
f
defined on
\mathbb{Z}
with finite support, we introduce the notation

\mathcal{A}_{N}f:=f*K_{N},\quad K_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{\{k^{t}\}}.

(4.19)

We claim that it suffices to show for
p>\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
, the following maximal inequality

\Big\|{\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{N}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

(4.20)

holds for all
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})
-valued function
f
defined on
\mathbb{Z}
with finite support.

Now, let us demonstrate how inequality (
4.20
) can be leveraged to establish Theorem
1.1
. Fix
p>\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
, and without loss of generality, assume that
x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})_{+}
. We then have

\Big\|{\sup_{N}}^{+}A_{N}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}=\sup_{N}\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}A_{k}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to establish the inequality

\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}A_{k}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}\leq C_{p}\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}.

(4.21)

holds for any positive integer
N
.
Fix a positive integer
N
and define a
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})
-valued function on
\mathbb{Z}
as

\phi(j):=\gamma^{j}(x),\qquad\text{for}\ j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then one can see

(\mathcal{A}_{N}\phi)(j)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi(j+k^{t})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma^{j+k^{t}}(x)=A_{N}(\gamma^{j}(x)).

For a large positive integer
J
, we set
\psi_{J}(j):=\phi(j)\chi_{1\leq j\leq J}
, then for all
j\leq J-N^{t}
, we have

(\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi_{J})(j)=A_{N}(\gamma^{j}(x)).

(4.22)

Applying inequality (
4.20
) to
\psi_{J}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{k}\psi_{J}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}\lesssim\|\psi_{J}\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\psi_{J}(j)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}=J\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p},

(4.23)

where the final equality follows from the fact that
\gamma
is an isometry in
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
. Furthermore, as shown in the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
, we have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}(\mathcal{A}_{k}\psi_{J})(j)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{k}\psi_{J}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}.

(4.24)

Using
[
11
, Proposition 7.3]
, we obtain the following crucial inequality

\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}A_{k}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}A_{k}(\gamma^{j}x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}.

(4.25)

By combining the previously established inequalities (
4.22
), (
4.23
), (
4.24
), and (
4.25
), we can derive the following estimate

\displaystyle(J-N^{t})\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}A_{k}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{J-N^{t}}\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}A_{k}(\gamma^{j}x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J-N^{t}}\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}(\mathcal{A}_{k}\psi_{J})(j)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim J\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}.

From this, we can deduce that

\frac{J-N^{t}}{J}\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N_{0}}}^{+}{A}_{k}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}\lesssim\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}.

Since
J
is an arbitrary positive integer, taking the limit as
J\rightarrow\infty
yields

\Big\|{\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}}^{+}A_{k}(x)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}.

This completes the proof of inequality (
4.21
), thereby establishing Theorem
1.1
.

In the following, we concentrate on establishing the proof of inequality (
4.20
).
Assuming, without loss of generality, that
f
is positive, we note that for
N_{2}>N_{1}
, one has

\mathcal{A}_{N_{1}}f=f*K_{N_{1}}\leq\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}}(f*K_{N_{2}})=\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}}\mathcal{A}_{N_{2}}f.

Consequently, it suffices to demonstrate

\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{2^{k}}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

(4.26)

Applying the triangle inequality and noting the facts that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Big|\Re\Big(f*(K_{2^{m}}-L_{2^{m}})\Big)\Big|\leq\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big|\Re\Big(f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}}\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

and

\Big|\Im\Big(f*(K_{2^{m}}-L_{2^{m}})\Big)\Big|\leq\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big|\Im\Big(f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}}\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}f*K_{2^{k}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}-\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}f*L_{2^{k}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}\Re\Big(f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}+\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}\Im\Big(f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\Re\Big(f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\Im\Big(f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

Therefore, we deduce

\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{2^{k}}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|f*(K_{2^{k}}-L_{2^{k}})\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+\Big\|{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}}^{+}f*L_{2^{k}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

Observing that

\min_{\rho\in(0,2)}\max\Big\{\frac{2\rho+1}{\rho+1},\frac{3\rho+2}{2\rho+1}\Big\}=\frac{2\rho+1}{\rho+1}\Big|_{\rho=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}}=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},

(4.27)

we note that for
p\in(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},2]
, there exists a suitable
\rho\in(0,2)
such that
p>\max\{\frac{2\rho+1}{\rho+1},\frac{3\rho+2}{2\rho+1}\}
.
Finally, by invoking Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
, we arrive at the desired inequality (
4.26
). Additionally, inequality (
4.26
) holds for
p=\infty
trivially. Therefore, through interpolation, we deduce that inequality (
4.26
) holds for all
p>\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

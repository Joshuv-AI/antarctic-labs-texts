Dynamics of non-autonomous systems with nested invariant cone structure and its applications
Dun Zhou

Abstract
The current paper is devoted to the investigation of the influence of nested invariant cone structure on the dynamics, in the context of non-autonomous (time almost-periodic)cases. We first prove that the nested invariant cone structure can persistent under C 1 C^{1} -perturbations; and the dynamics of the omega-limit set of any precompact orbit can be reduced to the dynamics of a compact invariant of a suitable finite dimensional system(see Theorem 2.1). In some special cases, the dynamics of any omega-limit set generated by the skew-product semiflow of such a system is similar to a one-dimensional system, that is, the omega-limit set contains at most two minimal sets, and any minimal set is an almost automorphic extension of its base flow(a universal phenomenon in multi-frequency driven systems, introduced by S. Bochner), these results are also correct for such systems under C 1 C^{1} -small perturbations(see Theorems 2.2, 2.3). To our best knowledge, it is the first paper to touch the global dynamics of abstract non-autonomous systems with invariant nested cones; the setting is general, since it contains an autonomous system plus an almost-periodic perturbation term, and a periodic system with another periodic perturbation term (these two periods are irrationally dependent)as special cases. The results can be viewed as a generalization of important works of W. Shen and Y. Yi(1995 J. Differential Equations 122 114-136) for scalar parabolic equations with separated boundary conditions, Y. Wang(2007 Nonlinearity 20 831-843) for tridiagonal competitive–cooperative systems(see Section 4).

Introduction

The invariant cone plays an essential role in characterizing the dynamic behavior of systems, for instance, the well known theory of monotone dynamical systems which founded by M. W. Hirsch, H. Matano et al, focused on the influence of the invariant closed convex cones on the systems. Another important application of invariant cone is to ensure the existence of hyperbolic structures, see
[
22

49

there is a family of cones

•

the linearized operator corresponding to the solution operator of the system satisfies exponential separability on each

(see Assumptions (H1)-(H5) in section 2.2).

Such systems are widely exist in mathematical models established in biology and physics. Its discovery can be traced back to the number of vector coordinate sign changes introduced by Gantmacher in analysing the oscillatory matrix(see
[

[

becomes an important index of the existence of NIC. As a matter of fact, systems with such structures include: semilinear parabolic equations on one-dimensional bounded fixed regions or with free boundary conditions, tridiagonal competitive cooperative systems, monotone (time-delay) cyclic feedback systems, strong

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we introduce some conceptions and properties of skew product semiflows, almost-periodic (almost-automorphic) functions (flows), cone and exponential separations; and we then present our main results in the framework of skew-product semiflows of non-autonomous systems. In section 3, proofs of our main results are given based on Proposition

Competitive-cooperative tridiagonal systems

In this subsection, we consider the perturbations of
n

\dot{x}=f(t,x)+\epsilon g(t,x)

where

satisfies the tridiagonal structure:

\begin{split}\dot{x}_{1}&=f_{1}(t,x_{1},x_{2}),\\
\dot{x}_{i}&=f_{i}(t,x_{i-1},x_{i},x_{i+1}),\quad 2\leq i\leq n-1,\\
\dot{x}_{n}&=f_{n}(t,x_{n-1},x_{n}),\\
\end{split}

(4.35)

The standard assumption on the tridiagonal system (
4.35
) is that the variable
x_{i+1}
affects

\dot{x}_{i}
and
x_{i}
affects

\dot{x}_{i+1}
monotonically in the same fashion. More precisely, there are
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
\delta_{i}\in\{-1,+1\}
,
i=1,\cdots,n-1
, such that

\delta_{i}\dfrac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{i+1}}(t,x)\geq\epsilon_{0},\quad\delta_{i}\dfrac{\partial f_{i+1}}{\partial x_{i}}(t,x)\geq\epsilon_{0},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,

for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This is one of the most commonly studied tridiagonal systems, called
competitive-cooperative systems
, in which individuals either compete or cooperate with their neighboring species. In particular, if
\delta_{i}=-1
for
all
i
, then (
4.35
) is called
competitive
.
If
\delta_{i}=1
for all
i
, then (
4.35
) is called

cooperative
. Of course, when
n=2
, system (
4.35
) naturally reduces to a two-dimensional competitive or cooperative system.

The equation (
4.35
) is often used in modelling ecosystems of
n

species
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}
with a certain hierarchical structure. In such
hierarchy,
x_{1}
interacts only with
x_{2}
,
x_{n}
only with
x_{n-1}
, and for

, species
x_{i}
interacts with
x_{i-1}
and
x_{i+1}
.

The asymptotic behavior of bounded solutions of (
4.35
) are thoroughly investigated by many others.
When
f
is independent of
t
, then all bounded trajectories of system converge to equilibria (see Smillie
[
43
]
). In the case that
f
of
t
is
T
-periodic, then every bounded solution is asymptotic to a
T
-periodic solution (Smith
[
44
]
). For
f
of
t
is almost-periodic, the
\omega
-limit set of any bounded orbit contains at most two minimal sets, and each minimal set is an almost automorphic extension of the base flow; particularly, any hyperbolic
\omega
-limit set is a 1-1 extension of the base flow(see Wang
[
46
]
, Fang etc.
[
8
]
).

Similar as in
[
44
]
, we now let
\hat{x}_{i}=\mu_{i}x_{i},\mu_{i}\in\{+1,-1\}

with
\mu_{1}=1,\mu_{i}=\delta_{i-1}\mu_{i-1}
. Then (
4.35

Therefore, we can always assume, without loss of generality, that the tridiagonal

4.35

\dfrac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{i+1}}(t,x)\geq\epsilon_{0},\quad\dfrac{\partial f_{i+1}}{\partial x_{i}}(t,x)\geq\epsilon_{0},\qquad 1\leq i\leq n-1,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

\end{split}

5
Some Remarks

1) Remark on invariant cone systems. As we have known, monotone dynamic systems study the effect of invariant convex cones on dynamics. With the help of the invariant convex cone structure, the famous generic convergence for autonomous system “most bounded trajectories approach a set of equilibria” is established by (see
[
20
]
). Since then, the theory and application of monotone dynamic systems have been developed vigorously. For instance, for a smooth periodic strong monotone system, most orbits converge to linearly stable cycles, see
[
16
,
36
]
. Meanwhile, chaos can happen for that “minority” orbits of monotone system(see Smale
[
42
]
), which means it is impossible to obtain the global convergence of general monotone dynamical system.

2) Remark on the assumption of
C^{1}
-perturbations. Note that even for a finite dimension system arbitrary small
C^{0}
-perturbations could have a chaos (see an example in
[
15
]
for negative cyclic feedback systems), that is, the structure of
\omega
-limit sets may not keep under small
C^{0}
-perturbations. Therefore,
C^{0}
-perturbations are not sufficient to assure the stability of the structure of
\omega
-limit sets.

3) Remark on the advantage of invariant cones method. Back to equation (
1.1
), similar functions to the zero number function
Z
, called the discrete Lyapunov functionals (DL functionals), are widely exist in many other systems (
[
1
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
43
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
31
,
29
]
). However, the forms of DL functionals are usually different for different systems, and not easy to find; hence, the DL functional may not work for its’ perturbed systems. To this extend, the method of NIC has a high degree of unity and universality, and extend the range of systems we’re considering.

4) Remark on main results. The results generalized the conclusions in Tereščák
[
45
]
for periodic systems to time almost-periodic systems. Nevertheless, it does not mean that these generalizations can be obtained directly from the approaches in Tereščák
[
45
]
, since there are some essential difficulties in dealing with non-autonomous systems, for example, by using the Poincaré map, Tereščák
[
45
]
proved that the DL functional induced by nest invariant cones is constant on any
\omega
-limit sets that generated by Poincaré map. This property plays a very important role in characterizing the shape of
\omega
-limit sets. While for non-autonomous systems, due to the lacking of Poincaré map, even for some specific systems, like (
1.2
) (see
[
40
]
, the constancy of DL functional on a minimal set is under the assumption that the minimal is unique ergodic) or tridigonal competitive and cooperative systems, one does not get the constant properties on a minimal set not to mention that for
\omega
-limit set. Therefore, to investigate the dynamics for non-autonomous systems, we first need to make some of the proofs in
[

]
rigorous (although the conclusions in
[

]
are correct, some details of the proof are not strictly given), and then generalized some of these results to non-autonomous cases in the framework of skew-product semiflow. Second, considering that the constant properties of DL functional are difficult to obtain for non-autonomous systems, we establish the relationship between the index of invariant cones and the solution of the linear systems, and then develop some tools for the non-autonomous systems. For instance, we defined a partial order on a compact invariant set

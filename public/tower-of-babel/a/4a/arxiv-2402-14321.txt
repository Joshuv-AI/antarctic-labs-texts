Non-relativistic spin splitting in compensated magnets that are not altermagnets
Lin-Ding Yuan, Alexandru B. Georgescu, James M. Rondinelli

Abstract
The non-relativistic spin-splitting (NRSS) of electronic bands in “altermagnets” has sparked renewed interest in antiferromagnets (AFMs) that have no net magnetization. However, altermagnets with collinear and compensated magnetism are not the only type of NRSS AFMs. In this study, we identify the symmetry conditions and characteristic signatures of a distinct group of NRSS AFMs that go beyond the description of altermagnets. These compounds exhibit a broken spin-degeneracy among the spin-polarized bands at the Γ \Gamma point in the absence of spin-orbit coupling (SOC). We use density functional theory calculations to validate these models in ternary magnetic nitrides, specifically MnXN 2 (X = Si, Ge, Sn), and their cation ordered variants. By removing the previous NRSS constraint on Γ \Gamma , these compounds may facilitate the generation of spin currents without cancellation arising from the alternating spin polarizations. Our findings expand the scope of NRSS eligible materials.

In this Letter, we examine the overlooked class of NRSS antiferromagnets that are not altermagnets using symmetry principles.
We show they are characterized by degeneracy removal of the spin polarized bands at
\Gamma
in the absence of spin-orbit coupling (SOC).
This feature contrasts with the behavior found in altermagnets, where electronic states at
\Gamma
are always spin degenerate in the absence of SOC. They are further distinct from the
\Gamma
-spin splitting induced by SOC
Milivojević et al. 2024
.
We then validate these models using density functional theory calculations on ternary manganese nitride MnXN
2

We conclude by discussing possible implications of this previously missing class of NRSS AFMs.
As the degeneracy at
\Gamma
is not enforced, these materials may present stronger spin currents without compensation.
Our work expands the palette of materials for NRSS beyond the conventional altermagnetism paradigm.

To summarize, we identified a subclass of NRSS material that cannot be categorized by altermagnets. We formulated the necessary symmetry conditions and its fingerprints, spin splitting at
\Gamma
. We demonstrate the special type of NRSS materials in a series of magnetic ternary MnXN
2
(X = Si, Ge, Sn) nitrides and their cation ordered derivatives.
We envision these materials will be useful for novel spin-based applications. Specifically, materials belonging to the “altermagnet” category feature alternating spin splitting and possible compensation of majority and minority spin manifolds that may be less suitable for generating highly spin polarized current. The NRSS
\Gamma
-point spin-split identified in this subclass of AFMs have no such constraints. Therefore, we conjecture that a search for more examples or their

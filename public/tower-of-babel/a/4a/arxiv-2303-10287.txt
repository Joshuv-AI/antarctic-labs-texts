Non-Steepness and Maximum Likelihood Estimation Properties of the Truncated Multivariate Normal Distributions
Michael Levine, Donald Richards, Jianxi Su

Abstract
This article considers exponential families of truncated multivariate normal distributions with one-sided truncation for some or all coordinates. We observe that if all components are one-sided truncated then this family is not full. The family of truncated multivariate normal distributions is extended to a full family, and the extended family is investigated in detail. We identify the canonical parameter space of the extended family, establish that the family is not regular, and deduce as a consequence that the family is not steep. We also consider maximum likelihood estimation for the location vector parameter, and the positive definite (symmetric) matrix dispersion parameter of a truncated non-singular multivariate normal distribution. It is shown that if the sample size is sufficiently large then, almost surely, the likelihood function has a unique maximizer on the canonical parameter space if any such maximizer exists. It is also shown that each solution to the score equations for the location and dispersion parameters satisfies the method-of-moments equations. The family of truncated multivariate normal distributions with a single one-sided truncated component is also considered; the corresponding canonical parameter space is identified, and it is shown that the family is not steep. Finally, it is observed that similar results arise in the case of an arbitrary number of truncated components.

\bm{V}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{d\times d}
,

\mathbb{E}\exp[\mathrm{i}(\bm{v}^{\prime}\bm{T}_{1}+\mathop{\rm{tr}}\bm{V}\bm{T}_{2})]\\
=\left[\frac{C\big(({\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}-2\mathrm{i}{\thinspace}n^{-1}\bm{V})^{-1}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}{\bm{\mu}}+\mathrm{i}\,n^{-1}\bm{v}),({\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}-2\mathrm{i}{\thinspace}n^{-1}\bm{V})^{-1}\big)}{C({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})}\right]^{n}.

Although the characteristic function can be used, in principle, to obtain the probability density function, the lack of an explicit expression for the normalizing constant
C({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})
precludes a straightforward characterization of the support of the density function of
(\bm{T}_{1},\bm{T}_{2})
.

(ii) Concerning Theorem

(iii), the proof will follow from the fact that the family

is minimal. Note also that the non-steepness of

We write
\bm{X}\sim N_{d}({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}};\bm{0})
whenever

has the density function (

). Also, we now assume that

is positive definite.

Unlike the untruncated normal distribution,

and

are not the mean and covariance matrix, respectively, of

, so we define

{\bm{\nu}}=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{d}):=\mathbb{E}(\bm{X})

and

{\bm{\Lambda}}=(\lambda_{i,j}):={\mathrm{Cov}}(\bm{X})\equiv\mathbb{E}[(\bm{X}-{\bm{\nu}})(\bm{X}-{\bm{\nu}})^{\prime}].

We note that research on the moments of the truncated multivariate normal distributions was initiated in

and continued later in

;

;

Let us introduce the notation

(\nabla C)({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}}):=\nabla_{\bm{t}}\,C(\bm{t},{\bm{\Sigma}})\Big|_{\bm{t}={\bm{\mu}}}

for the gradient of
C({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})
with respect to
{\bm{\mu}}
and

(\nabla\nabla^{\prime}C)({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}}):=\nabla_{\bm{t}}\phantom{{}^{\prime}}\nabla_{\bm{t}}^{\prime}C(\bm{t},{\bm{\Sigma}})\Big|_{\bm{t}={\bm{\mu}}}

for the corresponding Hessian matrix. The following auxiliary result is useful as it leads to expressions for the mean and covariance matrix of the multivariate truncated normal distribution in terms of the corresponding parameters of the underlying multivariate normal distribution. These results enable us, in turn, to obtain convenient representations of the maximum likelihood estimators of parameters of the underlying multivariate normal distributions in terms of observations generated by a multivariate truncated normal distribution, provided that these estimators exist and are in the interior of the parameter space.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
\bm{X}\sim N_{d}({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}};\bm{0})
. Then

{\bm{\nu}}={\bm{\mu}}+[C({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})]^{-1}\ {\bm{\Sigma}}\,(\nabla C)({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})

(4.1)

and

{\bm{\Lambda}}={\bm{\Sigma}}+[C({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})]^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}(\nabla\nabla^{\prime}C)({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}}){\bm{\Sigma}}-({\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{\mu}})({\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{\mu}})^{\prime}.

(4.2)

Suppose that
(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})
exists. By (
4.1
), (
4.2
), and the invariance principle of maximum likelihood estimation
(
Anderson
, pp. 70–71)
, we find that the pair
(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})
is related to
(\widehat{\bm{\nu}},\widehat{\bm{\Lambda}})
, the maximum likelihood estimator of
({\bm{\nu}},{\bm{\Lambda}})
, through the equations

\widehat{\bm{\nu}}=\widehat{\bm{\mu}}+[C(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})]^{-1}\ \widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}\,(\nabla C)(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})

(4.3)

and

\widehat{\bm{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}+[C(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})]^{-1}\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}\,(\nabla\nabla^{\prime}C)(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})\,\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}-(\widehat{\bm{\nu}}-\widehat{\bm{\mu}})(\widehat{\bm{\nu}}-\widehat{\bm{\mu}})^{\prime}.

(4.4)

Our next goal is to obtain the score equations for
(\widehat{\bm{\nu}},\widehat{\bm{\Lambda}})
.

In the sequel, for any vector
{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\bm{S}({\bm{\alpha}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bm{x}_{j}-{\bm{\alpha}})(\bm{x}_{j}-{\bm{\alpha}})^{\prime}.

(4.5)

In particular,

\bm{S}(\overline{\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bm{x}_{j}-\overline{\bm{x}})(\bm{x}_{j}-\overline{\bm{x}})^{\prime}

is the maximum likelihood estimator of the covariance matrix in the classical untruncated setting.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}
be a random sample from
\bm{X}
and suppose that
(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})
, the maximum likelihood estimator of
({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})
, exists and is in the interior of the parameter space. Then
(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})
satisfies the equations

\widehat{\bm{\mu}}+[C(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})]^{-1}\ \widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}\,(\nabla C)(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})=\overline{\bm{x}},

(4.6)

and

\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}+[C(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})]^{-1}\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}\,(\nabla\nabla^{\prime}C)(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})\,\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}-(\overline{\bm{x}}-\widehat{\bm{\mu}})(\overline{\bm{x}}-\widehat{\bm{\mu}})^{\prime}=\bm{S}(\overline{\bm{x}}).

(4.7)

Moreover,
\widehat{\bm{\nu}}=\overline{\bm{x}}
and
\widehat{\bm{\Lambda}}=\bm{S}(\overline{\bm{x}})
.

Remark 4.5
.

(i) In the untruncated case, the normalizing constant
C({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})
does not depend on
{\bm{\mu}}
and therefore
\nabla_{\bm{\mu}}C({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\Sigma}})\equiv\bm{0}
. Then (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) reduce to
\widehat{{\bm{\nu}}}=\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}=\overline{\bm{x}}
and
\widehat{\hbox{Cov}}(\bm{X})=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bm{x}_{j}-\overline{\bm{x}})(\bm{x}_{j}-\overline{\bm{x}})^{\prime}
, which are well-known results for the untruncated normal distributions.

(ii) It also follows from (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) that the method-of-moments estimator of
({\bm{\nu}},{\bm{\Lambda}})
for the truncated multivariate normal distribution is the same as in the untruncated case. This result was noted by Cohen
(
Cohen
, Sections 2.3 and 12.2)
for the univariate truncated normal distributions and for multivariate normal distributions with a
single truncated component variable
. Therefore, Theorem
4.4
extends Cohen’s observation to the general multivariate setting in which all components are truncated.

We also remark that Cohen’s result for the univariate truncated normal distributions or the multivariate normal distributions with one truncated component cannot be extended to the general truncated multivariate setting by using conditional distributions, for in the case of the truncated normal distributions, the conditional distributions generally are not truncated normal.

(iii) For the case in which only one component is truncated, Cohen derived explicit formulas for the maximum likelihood estimator. However, it appears to be infeasible to obtain similar formulas if two or more components are truncated; moreover, Cohen did not consider such cases.

Due to the implicit nature of the score equations (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), it is a challenging problem to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of solutions to those equations. The following result provides a necessary condition for the existence of solutions to the score equations, and we also note that this condition was proved by del Castillo
(
delCastillo
, Theorem 4.2)
to be necessary and sufficient in the one-dimensional case.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
n>d
and that there exists a solution
(\widehat{\bm{\mu}},\widehat{\bm{S}})
to the score equations (
4.6
) and (
4.7
). Then

0\leq(\overline{\bm{x}}-\widehat{\bm{\mu}})^{\prime}\widehat{\bm{S}}^{-1}(\overline{\bm{x}}-\widehat{\bm{\mu}})<1.

(4.8)

In the one-dimensional case in which
X
has a normal distribution that is truncated to the left at
0
, it is shown in
delCastillo
;
Hegde
that the domain of means,
\{(\mathbb{E}(X),\mathbb{E}(X^{2}))\}
,
equals
the region
\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}:x^{2}\leq y\leq 2x^{2}\}
; equivalently,

[\mathbb{E}(X)]^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(X^{2})\leq 2[\mathbb{E}(X)]^{2}.

(4.9)

Moreover, the sufficiency of (
4.8
) in the one-dimensional case stems from an upper bound on the domain of means.

In the multivariate case, it was shown in Theorem
2.1
that
\mathbb{E}(\bm{X})\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}^{\prime})\preccurlyeq\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}\bm{X}^{\prime})
, and this reduces in the one-dimensional case to the lower bound in (

\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}\bm{X}^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}(\bm{X})\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}^{\prime})
in the multivariate case, and we do so in the following result. Using the notation
\bm{I}_{d}
for the identity matrix of order
d
, we have

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose that
\bm{X}
has the truncated multivariate normal distribution (
2.1
) with mean
{\bm{\nu}}
and covariance matrix
{\bm{\Lambda}}
. Then

\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}\bm{X}^{\prime})\preccurlyeq\mathbb{E}(\bm{X})\mathbb{E}(\bm{X})^{\prime}+(\mathbb{E}(\|\bm{X}\|^{2})+\|\mathbb{E}(\bm{X})\|^{2})\bm{I}_{d},

(4.10)

and the domain of means
\{(\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}),\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}\bm{X}^{\prime}))\}
is contained in the set

\{({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}}):{\bm{\theta}}{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\preccurlyeq{\bm{\Theta}}\preccurlyeq{\bm{\theta}}{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}+(\mathop{\rm{tr}}{\bm{\Theta}}+\|{\bm{\theta}}\|^{2})\bm{I}_{d}\}.

(4.11)

We remark that although (

to the score equations (

5
The case of several untruncated components

A reviewer raised an interesting question: Is the exponential family corresponding to a multivariate normal distribution with some, but not all, components truncated also not steep? To demonstrate that the answer is affirmative, we consider in this section a multivariate normal distribution with one truncated component. We also note that our proof of non-steepness in this case can be extended,
mutatis mutandis
, to any number of truncated components.

Moreover, it is worthwhile to emphasize that the single-component truncated normal distribution is a multivariate distribution, which is in contrast to the single-component truncated univariate distribution studied in
delCastillo
. As mentioned earlier, the truncated multivariate normal family is not closed under marginalization
Levine
, so the results established in this section do not follow from the results in
delCastillo
;
Hegde
.

In this section, we suppose that
X_{1}
is truncated to the left at zero and
X_{2},\ldots,X_{d}
are untruncated. Similar to Section
2
, we parametrize the exponential family using the parameter
({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}})
. We partition
\bm{t}
,
{\bm{\theta}}
, and
{\bm{\Theta}}
as follows: Let
\bm{t}_{2}=(t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})^{\prime}
and
{\bm{\theta}}_{2}=(\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{d})^{\prime}
, so that

\bm{t}=\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}\\
\bm{t}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bm{\theta}}=\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{1}\\
{\bm{\theta}}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bm{\Theta}}=(\Theta_{jk})_{d\times d}=\begin{pmatrix}\Theta_{11}&{\bm{\Theta}}_{12}\\
{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}&{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}\end{pmatrix},

where
\Theta_{11}
is
1\times 1
,
{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}={\bm{\Theta}}_{12}^{\prime}
is
(d-1)\times 1
, and
{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}
is
(d-1)\times(d-1)
. Further, we define

\theta_{1\cdot 2}:=\theta_{1}-{\bm{\Theta}}_{12}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \Theta_{11\cdot 2}:=\Theta_{11}-{\bm{\Theta}}_{12}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}.

(5.1)

Define the Laplace transform of the corresponding exponential family,

L_{T}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}}\exp({\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\bm{t}-\bm{t}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{t})\,{{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}\bm{t}_{2}{{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}t_{1},

(5.2)

and let
{\mathcal{D}}=\{({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{d\times d}:L_{T}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}})<\infty\}
be the canonical parameter space associated with (
5.2

in detail, is essential for identifying points on

where the gradient of the corresponding cumulant-generating function remains finite, which in turn implies the non-steepness of the exponential family.

Proposition 5.1
.

The single-component truncated multivariate normal distributions constitute a full exponential family with canonical parameter space

{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{0}\cup{\mathcal{D}}_{1},

where

{\mathcal{D}}_{0}=\{({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{d\times d}:\theta_{1\cdot 2}<0,{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}\ \hbox{is positive definite},\Theta_{11\cdot 2}=0\}.

and
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}_{d}^{d\times d}
.

We now present the main result of this section, which establishes the non-steepness property of the truncated multivariate normal distributions when one component is truncated.

Theorem 5.2
.

The exponential family corresponding to the single-component truncated multivariate normal distributions is not steep.

and Theorem

are given in subsection

6
Concluding remarks

In this article, we have realized as an exponential family the collection of multivariate normal distributions in which each component is one-sided truncated. We derived the canonical parameter space for this family and showed that the family is full and minimal, but neither regular nor steep. We also derived the score equations arising from maximum likelihood estimation for the parameters of the truncated multivariate normal distributions and we obtained a necessary condition for the existence of solutions to the score equations.

We note that the articles
Cohen
;
Manjunath
provided numerical algorithms for solving the score equations; however, those algorithms are based on the tacit assumption that such solutions exist. In future work, we will address the issues of both existence and uniqueness of solutions to the score equations, taking as our guiding light the article by Bedbur,
et al.

Bedbur_etal
. We remark that such research necessarily requires new approaches that (1) investigate the convexity properties of the likelihood as a simultaneous function of
{\bm{\mu}}
and
{\bm{\Sigma}}
, and (2) are able to resolve the situation in which the set of solutions to the score equations contain continuous curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{d\times d}
.

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{H}_{1}\bm{w}_{1}+\bm{H}_{2}\bm{w}_{2}>\bm{0}}\exp({\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\bm{H}_{1}\bm{w}_{1}+\bm{w}_{1}^{\prime}{\bm{\Xi}}_{1}\bm{w}_{1}+{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\bm{H}_{2}\bm{w}_{2}-\bm{w}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Xi}}_{2}\bm{w}_{2}){{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}\bm{w}_{1}{{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}\bm{w}_{2}.

\bigg\{({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{d\times d}:{\bm{\Theta}}\not\in\bigcup_{r=0}^{d}{\mathcal{S}}_{r}^{d\times d}\bigg\}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{c},

\displaystyle=C_{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta_{1}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}){{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}t_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22})\exp(\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}t_{1}^{2}\exp(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}){{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}t_{1}.

For
j=2,\ldots,d
, a similar calculation shows that

m_{j,j}=C_{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta_{j}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}){{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}t_{1}.

Noting that
\theta_{1\cdot 2}=\theta_{1}-{\bm{\Theta}}_{12}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}\equiv\theta_{1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}
, we have

\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}_{2}}(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})=-{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2},

equivalently, for
j=2,\ldots,d
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})

\displaystyle=(-{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})_{j}

\displaystyle\equiv-({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21})_{j}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=2}^{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})_{j,k}\theta_{k}.

Therefore, for
j=2,\ldots,d
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta_{j}^{2}}(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})_{j,j}.

Next, it follows by direct calculation that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta_{j}^{2}}\exp(-

\displaystyle\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\big[\big((-{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})_{j}\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})_{j,j}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\exp(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}),

hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\exp(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{j=2}^{d}\big[\big((-{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})_{j}\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})_{j,j}\big]\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\exp(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}).

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{d}

\displaystyle\big[\big((-{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})_{j}\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})_{j,j}\big]

\displaystyle=(-{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})^{\prime}(-{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2})+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm{tr}}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})

then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{d}m_{j,j}

\displaystyle=C_{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22})\sum_{j=2}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta_{j}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\big(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}\big){{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}t_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22})

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[{\bm{\Theta}}_{12}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-2}{\bm{\Theta}}_{21}t_{1}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-2}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}-{\bm{\Theta}}_{12}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-2}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm{tr}}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times\exp(-\Theta_{11\cdot 2}t_{1}^{2}+\theta_{1\cdot 2}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{4}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{2}){{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}t_{1}.

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm{tr}}

\displaystyle(\bm{M})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}m_{j,j}

therefore

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm{tr}}(\bm{M})\big|_{({\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{\Theta}}_{0})}

\displaystyle=C_{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22})\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[t_{1}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm{tr}}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})\big]\exp(\theta_{0,1}\,t_{1}){{\hskip 1.0pt}\rm{d}}t_{1}

\displaystyle=-\big(2\theta_{0,1}^{-3}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm{tr}}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}^{-1})\theta_{0,1}\big)C_{d}({\bm{\Theta}}_{22}).

(7.30)

Denote by
\eta_{1}(\bm{M}),\ldots,\eta_{d}(\bm{M})
the eigenvalues of
\bm{M}
. Also, let
\|\bm{M}\|_{1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\eta_{j}(\bm{M})|
denote the

\bm{M}
. The Frobenius norm,

7.29

\bm{M}

j=1,\ldots,d

7.28

\|\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}K_{T}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\Theta}})\|_{F}\Big|_{({\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{\Theta}}_{0})}\\

(7.31)

Combining (
7.26

Abstract
. In this paper, we extend Gang Liu’s three circle theorem for Kähler manifolds to almost Hermitian manifolds. As applications of the three circle theorem, we obtain sharp dimension estimates for spaces of holomorphic functions of polynomial growth and rigidity for the estimates, Liouville theorems for pluri-subharmonic functions of sub-logarithmic growth and Liouville theorems for holomorphic functions of Cheng-type, and fundamental theorem of algebra on almost Hermitian manifolds with slightly negative holomorphic sectional curvature. The results are generalization of Liu’s results and some of them are new even for the Kähler case. We also discuss the converse of the three circle theorem on Hermitian manifolds which turns out to be rather different with the Kähler case. In order to obtain the three circle theorem on almost Hermitian manifolds, we also establish a general maximum principle in the spirit of Calabi’s trick so that a general three circle theorem is a straight forward consequence of the general maximum principle. The general maximum principle and three circle theorem established may be of independent interests.

Introduction

One of the recent breakthroughs in the study of the structures of nonnegatively curved complete noncompact Kähler manifolds is a series of works
[
16
,
15
,
14
,
13
]
by Liu which finally sovled Yau’s finite generation conjecture and Yau’s uniformization conjecture under the assumption of nonnegative holomorphic bisectional curvature and maximal volume growth. The later conjecture was also solved by Tam and his collaborators in a series of works
[
4
,
11
,
12
]
using Kähler-Ricci flow following the pioneer work of Shi
[
24
]
. Mok
[
17
]
and Chen-Tang-Zhu
[
6
]
also solved the later conjecture on complete noncompact Kähler surfaces under certain geometric asumptions. It is an interesting problem to extend the aforementioned results to complete noncompact Hermitian manifolds. There is a similar problem in the compact case. It was shown by Siu-Yau
[
25
]
and Mori
[
18
]
independently that compact Kähler manifolds with positive holomorphic bisectional curvature must be biholomorphic to the complex projective space. In fact, Mori’s result is more general. He solved the Hartshorne conjecture in algebraic geometry which at least implies that a compact Hermitian manifold with positive curvature in the sense of Griffiths must be also biholomorphic to the complex projective space. It is a folklore problem in geometric analysis to give an analytic proof of the aforementioned fact.

For analytic methods dealing with the Hermitian case, similar to Kähler-Ricci flow, there are Hermitian curvature flow introduced by Streets-Tian
[
23
]
, and Chern-Ricci flow introduced by Gill
[
9
]
and Tosatti-Weinkove
[
26
]
. It seems that the Hermitian curvature flow is more like the Kähler-Ricci flow because it preserves the signs of certain interested curvatures (See
[
28
,
29
]
) while the Chern-Ricci flow does not (See
[
30
]
). Analogous to the Kähler case, there must be a similar approach to Liu’s for the Hermitian case. The starting point of Liu’s approach is an elegant and clever generalization of Hadamard’s three circle theorem to complete noncompact Kähler manifolds with nonnegative holomorphic sectional curvature in
[
16
]
. So, the first step to extend Liu’s approach in the Hermitian case is to extend the three circle theorem to Hermitian manifolds. This is the motivation of our work. In fact, we are able to extend the three circle theorem to the more general almost Hermitian case.

Let’s recall Liu’s (
[
16
]
) three circle theorem first.

Theorem 1.1

(Liu
[
16
]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete Kähler manifold. Then
M
satisfies the three circle theorem if and only if the holomorphic sectional curvature is nonnegative. Here, a complete Kähler manifold is said to satisfy the three circle theorem if for any
p\in M
,
R>0
and nonzero holomorphic function

on
B_{p}(R)
,
\log M(f,r)
is a convex function with respect to
\log r
for
r\in(0,R)
where
M(f,r)=\max_{x\in B_{p}(r)}|f(x)|
.

In
[
16
]
, Liu also extend the ”if” part of the three circle theorem above to much more general Kähler manifolds (See
[
16
, Theorem 8]
). The purpose of this paper is to extend Liu’s results in
[
16
]
on Kähler manifolds to Hermitian manifolds or almost Hermitian manifolds and give some applications. We would also like to mention that an analogue of Liu’s three circle theorem in the case of CR manifolds was obtained in
[
3
]
.

Recall that an almost Hermitian manifold
(M^{2n},J,g)
is a manifold
M
of real dimension
2n
equipped with an almost complex structure
J
and a compatible Riemannian metric
g
:

g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y)

for any tangent vectors
X
and
Y
. When
J
is integrable,
(M^{2n},J,g)
is called a Hermitian manifold. The Levi-Civita connection on
(M,J,g)
denoted as
\nabla
is torsion free and compatible with
g
but may not be compatible with
J
. In fact,
\nabla J\not\equiv 0
unless
(M,J,g)
is Kähler. The Chern connection on
(M,J,g)
denoted as
D
is the unique connection on
M
compatible with
J
and
g
, and with vanishing
(1,1)
part of the torsion. More precisely, let

(1.1)

\tau(X,Y)=D_{X}Y-D_{Y}X-[X,Y]

be the torsion of
D
. Then,

(1.2)

\tau(\xi,\bar{\eta})=0

for any
(1,0)
-vectors
\xi
and
\eta
. Or equivalently,

(1.3)

\tau(X,JY)=\tau(JX,Y)

for any tangent vectors
X
and
Y
. All the linear operations on real vector fields are extended to complex vector fields by complex linear extension. Moreover, for any
\omega\in A^{p,q}(M)
, define
\partial\omega:=(d\omega)^{p+1,q}
and
\overline{\partial}\omega:=(d\omega)^{p,q+1}
. The same as on complex manifolds, a complex-valued function
f
on
M
is said to be holomorphic if
\overline{\partial}f=0
, and a real-valued function
u
on
M
is said to be pluri-subharmonic if
{\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}}u
is a nonnegative
(1,1)
-form.

By examining Liu’s work
[
16
]
, we know that a key observation in Liu’s argument is a comparison result for
{\rm Hess}(r)(J\nabla r,J\nabla r)
where
{\rm Hess}
means the Hessian operator with respect to Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
and
r
is the distance function with respect to some fixed point. On almost Hermitian manifolds, such a comparison was essentially already known in Gray’s work
[
10
]
using Levi-Civita connection and in the first named author’s work
[
32
]
using Chern connection. Such a comparison was also obtained for Hermitian manifolds in the work
[
7
]
of Chen-Yang. More precisely, all of these works contain the following conclusion in an implicit form:

(1.4)

L[\log r]\leq 0

within the cut-locus of the fixed point under the curvature assumption

(1.5)

R^{L}(X,JX,JX,X)-\|(\nabla_{X}J)X\|^{2}\geq 0

for any tangent vector
X
where
R^{L}
is the curvature tensor for the Levi-Civita connection (see
[
10
]
), or equivalently

(1.6)

R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}+\tau_{i1}^{\bar{1}}|^{2}\geq 0

for any unitary
(1,0)
-frame
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
where
R
is the curvature tensor for the Chern connection (see
[
7
]
for the Hermitian case and
[
32
]
for the almost Hermitian case). Here,

(1.7)

L[u]:={\rm Hess}(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u)+{\rm Hess}(u)(J\nabla u,J\nabla u).

The equivalence of (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) can be seen by using the curvature identities derived by the first named author in
[
31
]
(See Lemma
2.3
for details). According to these results, we introduce the following notion.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold and
\xi
be a unit
(1,0)
-vector. Define the holomorphic sectional curvature
H(\xi)
along the direction
\xi
as

(1.8)

H(\xi):=R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}+\tau_{i1}^{\bar{1}}|^{2}

where we have fixed a unitary frame
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
with
e_{1}=\xi
. Let
\xi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X-{\sqrt{-1}}JX)
with
X
a unit real tangent vector. Then, we also write
H(\xi)
as
H(X)
.

By following the argument in
[
32
]
, we are able to obtain a general comparison result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a complete almost Hermitian manifold and
o\in M
. Let
h\in C^{1}((0,R))
be such that

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}th(t)=1
, and

(2)

for any normal minimal geodesic
\gamma:[0,l]\to B_{o}(R)
starting at
o
,

(1.9)

h^{\prime}(t)+h^{2}(t)+H(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\geq 0

for
t\in(0,l]
.

Let
v\in C^{2}((0,R))
be such that

(1)

v^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t\in(0,R)
;

(2)

v^{\prime\prime}(t)+hv^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
for
t\in(0,R)
.

Then

L[v(r_{o})]\leq 0

in
B^{\prime}_{o}(R):=B_{o}(R)\setminus\{o\}
, in the sense of barrier, where
r_{o}(x)=r(o,x)
. In particular, if the holomorphic sectional curvature of
(M,J,g)
in
B_{o}(R)
is not less than a constant

, then

(1.10)

L[\log({\mathbf{tn}}_{K}(r_{o}/2))]\leq 0

in
B^{\prime}_{o}(R)
, in the sense of barrier, where

(1.11)

{\mathbf{tn}}_{K}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\tan(\sqrt{K}t)&K>0\\
t&K=0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{-K}}\tanh(\sqrt{-K}t)&K<0.\end{array}\right.

Here, because the distance function may not be smooth and motivated by Calabi’s trick
[
2
]
, we say that a continuous function
v
on a open subset
\Omega
of
M
satisfies

L[v]\leq 0

in the sense of barrier. If for any
\epsilon>0
and
x\in\Omega
, there is a smooth function
v_{x,\epsilon}
defined on an open neighborhood
U_{x,\epsilon}\subset\Omega
of
x
such that
v\leq v_{x,\epsilon}
on
U_{x,\epsilon}
,
v(x)=v_{x,\epsilon}(x)
and
L[v_{x,\epsilon}](x)\leq\epsilon
.

The three circle theorem we obtained for almost Hermitian manifolds is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a complete noncompact almost Hermitian manifold and
o\in M
. Let
h\in C^{1}((0,R))
be such that

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}th(t)=1
, and

(2)

for any normal minimal geodesic
\gamma:[0,l]\to B_{o}(R)
starting at
o
,

(1.12)

h^{\prime}(t)+h^{2}(t)+H(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\geq 0

for
t\in(0,l]
.

Let
v\in C^{2}((0,R))
be such that

(1)

v^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t\in(0,R)
;

(2)

v^{\prime\prime}(t)+hv^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
for
t\in(0,R)
.

Then, for any
u\in C^{2}(B_{o}(R))
with
L[u]\geq 0
,
M_{o}(u,r)
is a convex function of
v(r)
for
r\in(0,R)
. Moreover, if the holomorphic sectional curvature of
(M,J,g)
in
B_{o}(R)
is not less than a constant
K
, then
M_{o}(u,r)
is a convex function of
\log{\mathbf{tn}}_{K}(r/2)
for
r\in(0,R)
. In particular,

(1)

if
u\in C^{2}(B_{o}(R))
is a pluri-subharmonic function, then
M_{o}(u,r)
is a convex function of
v(r)
for
r\in(0,R)
;

(2)

for any holomorphic functions
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{k}
on
B_{o}(R)
,
\log M_{o}(|f_{1}|^{2}+|f_{2}|^{2}+\cdots+|f_{k}|^{2},r)
is a convex function of
v(r)
for
r\in(0,R)
;

(3)

if the holomorphic sectional curvature of
(M,J,g)
in
B_{o}(R)
is not less than a constant
K
and
u\in C^{2}(B_{o}(R))
is a pluri-subharmonic function, then
M_{o}(u,r)
is a convex function of
\log{\mathbf{tn}}_{k}(r/2)
for
r\in(0,R)
;

(4)

if the holomorphic sectional curvature of
(M,J,g)
in
B_{o}(R)
is not less than a constant
K
, then
\log M_{o}(|f_{1}|^{2}+|f_{2}|^{2}+\cdots+|f_{k}|^{2},r)
is a convex function of
\log{\mathbf{tn}}_{k}(r/2)
for
r\in(0,R)
and any holomorphic functions
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{k}
on
B_{o}(R)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For convenience of later application of Theorem
1.3

Definition 1.2
.

Let

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a complete noncompact almost Hermitian manifold and

. For

, define

H_{o}(r)=\inf\{H(\gamma^{\prime}(r))\ |\ \gamma:[0,r]\to M{\rm\ is\ a\ normal\ minimal\ geodesic\ with\ }\gamma(0)=o\}.

H_{o}(0)=\min\{H(X)\ |\ X\in T_{o}M\ {\rm with}\ \|X\|=1\}.

The first application of Theorem
1.3
is the following Liouville theorem for functions satisfying
L[u]\geq 0
on almost Hermitian manifolds with slightly negative holomorphic sectional curvature. Such a Liouville theorem for pluri-subharmonic function on complete Kähler manifolds with nonnegative holomorphic bisectional curvature was first obtained by Ni-Tam
[
21
]
. Liu
[
16
]
relaxed the curvature assumption to nonnegative holomorphic sectional curvature and extend it to functions satisfying

. Recently, under the different curvature assumption of nonnegative orthogonal holomorphic bisectional curvature and nonnegative Ricci curvature, Ni-Niu
[
20

also obtained the Liouville theorem for pluri-subharmonic functions on complete noncompact Kähler manifolds.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a complete noncompact almost Hermitian manifold. Suppose for each
p\in M
, there is a nonnegative continuous function
q_{p}
on
[0,+\infty)
such that

(1)

H_{p}(r)\geq-q_{p}(r)
for any

, and

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}rq_{p}(r)dr<+\infty

Let

and

(1.14)

\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M_{o}(u,r)}{\log r}\leq 0.

where

is a fixed point on

. Then,

must be a constant function. In particular, any pluri-subharmonic function

satisfying (

) must be a constant function.

Similarly as in the Kähler case, we denote the ring of holomorphic functions on
M
as
\mathcal{O}(M)
. A function

on

is said to be of polynomial growth if there are constants

such that

(1.15)

|f(x)|\leq C(1+r(x)^{d}),\ \forall x\in M.

Denote the ring of holomorphic functions on

of polynomial growth as
\mathcal{P}(M)
. Denote
\mathcal{O}_{d}(M)
the space of holomorphic functions such that (

) holds for some

. For each nonzero

, define

(1.16)

\deg f=\inf\{d>0\ |\ f\in\mathcal{O}_{d}(M)\}

which is called the degree of

. Then, similar to
[
16
]
, we have the following consequence of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a complete noncompact almost Hermitian manifold and
o\in M
. Suppose there is a nonegative continuous function
q(t)
on
[0,+\infty)
such that

(1)

H_{o}(r)\geq-q(r)
for any

, and

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}rq(r)dr<+\infty
.

Then,

(1)

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{P}(M)
if and only if
\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}<+\infty
;

(2)

for any

\deg f=\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}=\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r};

(3)

for any

is decreasing on

on complete noncompact Kähler manifolds was first obtained by Ni

(M^{2n},J,g)
be a complete noncompact almost Hermitian manifold and

. Suppose there is a nonnegative continuous function
q(t)
on

such that

(1)

H_{o}(r)\geq-q(r)
for any

, and

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}rq(r)dr<+\infty

(1.17)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}r^{-\frac{1}{I(q)}}{M_{o}(|f|,r)}=0.

We would like to emphasize that the results we obtained for complete noncompact almost Hermitian manifolds with slightly negative holomorphic sectional curvature can be viewed as an extension of Theorem 11 in

The second part of this paper is to consider the converse of the three circle theorem for Hermitian manifolds. We don’t consider this for almost Hermitian manifolds because when the almost complex structure is not integrable, the almost Hermitian manifold may support no local holomorphic function. By following the local arguments in
[
16
]
, we find that the situation for Hermitian manifolds is quite different with the Kähler case. We don’t get necessary and sufficient conditions.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold. If for any
o\in M
,
R>0
, and any nonzero holomorphic function
f
on
B_{o}(R)
,
\log M_{o}(|f|,r)
is a convex function with respect to
\log r
for

, we say that
(M,J,g)
satisfies the three circle theorem.

By following the idea in
[
16
]
, we have the following converse of the three circle theorem for Hermitian manifolds.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a Hermitian manifold satisfying the three circle theorem. Then

R^{L}(X,JX,JX,X)+3\|(\nabla_{X}J)X\|^{2}\geq 0

for any real tangent vector
X
, or equivalently

R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}|^{2}\geq 0

for any unitary
(1,0)
-frame
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some preliminaries on almost Hermitian geometry. In Section 3, we give a general maximum principle and a general three circle theorem which set up the framework for latter applications. In Section 4, we obtain the main results for almost Hermitian manifolds by applying the framework set up in Section 3. Finally, in Section 5, we consider converse of three circle theorem on Hermitian manifolds.

2.
Preliminaries on almost Hermitian geometry

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold of real dimension
2n
.
The difference of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
and the Chern connection
D
is given by the following identity:

(2.1)

\left\langle\nabla_{Y}X,Z\right\rangle=\left\langle D_{Y}X,Z\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}[\left\langle\tau(X,Y),Z\right\rangle+\left\langle\tau(Y,Z),X\right\rangle-\left\langle\tau(Z,X),Y\right\rangle]

for any tangent vector fields
X,Y,Z
(For a proof of this identity, see
[
8
]
). Recall that the Nijenhuis tensor for an almost complex manifold is a vector-valued two-form defined as

(2.2)

N_{J}(X,Y)=[JX,JY]-J[JX,Y]-J[X,JY]-[X,Y]

for any tangent vectors
X
and
Y
. A direct computation gives us that
N_{J}=0
if and only if
\tau(\xi,\eta)^{0,1}=0
for any
(1,0)
-vectors
\xi
and
\eta
(See
[
31
]
for example). For any smooth function
f
on
M
, we denote

(2.3)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)=Ddf

the Hessian of
f
with respect to the connection
D
. Note that
{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)
may not be a symmetric tensor because
D
may have non-vanishing torsion. In fact,

(2.4)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(X,Y)-{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(Y,X)=\tau(X,Y)(f).

for any tangent vectors
X,Y
. Then, by (
1.2
) and (
1.3
),

(2.5)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(\xi,\bar{\eta})={\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(\bar{\eta},\xi)

for any
(1,0)
-vectors
\xi
and
\eta
, and

(2.6)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(X,JX)={\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(JX,X)

for any tangent vector
X
. So, for any
\xi=X-{\sqrt{-1}}JX
with
X
a real tangent vector,

(2.7)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(\xi,\bar{\xi})={\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(X,X)+{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(JX,JX).

Moreover, by direct computation, we have the following identities between
\partial\overline{\partial}f
and
{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold. Then,

(2.8)

\partial\overline{\partial}f(\xi,\bar{\eta})={\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(\xi,\bar{\eta})=-\overline{\partial}\partial f(\xi,\bar{\eta})

for any smooth function
f
and
(1,0)
-vectors
\xi
and
\eta
.

Proof.

By definitions and (
2.5
),

(2.9)

\begin{split}\partial\overline{\partial}f(\xi,\bar{\eta})=&d\overline{\partial}f(\xi,\bar{\eta})\\
=&\xi(\overline{\partial}f(\bar{\eta}))-\bar{\eta}(\overline{\partial}f(\xi))-\overline{\partial}f([\xi,\bar{\eta}])\\
=&\xi\bar{\eta}(f)-[\xi,\bar{\eta}]^{0,1}(f)\\
=&\xi\bar{\eta}(f)-D_{\xi}\bar{\eta}(f)\\
=&{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(\xi,\bar{\eta})\end{split}

The other identity can be shown similarly.
∎

By Lemma
2.1
, a real-valued function
u
is pluri-subharmonic if and only if

(2.10)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(u)(\xi,\bar{\xi})\geq 0

for any
(1,0)
-vector
\xi
. By (
2.7
), this is also equivalent to

(2.11)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(u)(X,X)+{\rm Hess}_{D}(u)(JX,JX)\geq 0

for any real tangent vector
X
. Moreover, the same as in the case of complex manifolds, one can construct pluri-subharmonic functions out of holomorphic functions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M^{2n},J)
be an almost complex manifold with real dimension
2n
and
\Omega\subset M
be an open subset.
Then,
\log(|f_{1}|^{2}+|f_{2}|^{2}+\cdots+|f_{k}|^{2}+\epsilon)
is pluri-subharmonic on
\Omega
for any holomorphic functions
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{k}
on
\Omega
and
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
2.1
, we know that

(2.12)

\begin{split}&{\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}}\log(|f_{1}|^{2}+|f_{2}|^{2}+\cdots+|f_{k}|^{2}+\epsilon)\\
=&\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|f_{i}|^{2}+\epsilon\right)^{-2}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|f_{i}|^{2}+\epsilon\right)\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\sqrt{-1}}\partial f_{j}\wedge\overline{\partial}\overline{f_{j}}-{\sqrt{-1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\overline{f_{i}}\partial f_{i}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}\overline{\partial}\overline{f_{j}}\right)\right).\end{split}

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we know that

(2.13)

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|f_{i}|^{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\sqrt{-1}}\partial f_{j}\wedge\overline{\partial}\overline{f_{j}}-{\sqrt{-1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\overline{f_{i}}\partial f_{i}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}\overline{\partial}\overline{f_{j}}\right)\geq 0.

Then, the conclusion of the lemma follows directly.
∎

The Hessian operator with respect to the Levi-Civita connection is denoted as

(2.14)

{\rm Hess}(f)=\nabla df.

By direct computation using (
2.1
),

(2.15)

{\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(X,Y)={\rm Hess}(f)(X,Y)+\frac{1}{2}[\left\langle\tau(X,Y),\nabla f\right\rangle+\left\langle\tau(Y,\nabla f),X\right\rangle-\left\langle\tau(\nabla f,X),Y\right\rangle]

for any tangent vector fields
X
and
Y
. By (
2.7
), (
2.15
) and (
1.3
),

(2.16)

L[f]={\rm Hess}(f)(\xi_{f},\bar{\xi}_{f})={\rm Hess}_{D}(f)(\xi_{f},\bar{\xi}_{f})

where
\xi_{f}=\nabla f-{\sqrt{-1}}J\nabla f
. For each nonzero real tangent vector
X
, we denote the normalization of the
(1,0)
-part of
X
as
\mathbf{U}(X)
. More precisely,

(2.17)

\mathbf{U}(X):=\frac{X-{\sqrt{-1}}JX}{\|X-{\sqrt{-1}}JX\|}.

Next, we come to show the equivalence of the curvature notions formulated in terms of Levi-Civita connection in Gray’s work
[
10
]
and in Definition
1.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold and
\xi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X-{\sqrt{-1}}JX)
with
X
a unit real tangent vector. Then,

(2.18)

H(\xi)=R^{L}(X,JX,JX,X)-\|(\nabla_{X}J)(X)\|^{2}.

Proof.

Note that for any tangent vector fields
X
and
Y
, by (
2.1
) and (
1.3
),

(2.19)

\begin{split}&\left\langle(\nabla_{X}J)(X),Y\right\rangle\\
=&\left\langle(\nabla_{X}J)(X),Y\right\rangle-\left\langle(D_{X}J)(X),Y\right\rangle\\
=&\left\langle\nabla_{X}(JX)-D_{X}(JX),Y\right\rangle-\left\langle J(\nabla_{X}X-D_{X}X),Y\right\rangle\\
=&\frac{1}{2}(\left\langle\tau(X,Y),JX\right\rangle-\left\langle\tau(Y,JX),X\right\rangle)+\left\langle\nabla_{X}X-D_{X}X,JY\right\rangle\\
=&\frac{1}{2}(\left\langle\tau(X,Y),JX\right\rangle+\left\langle\tau(JX,Y),X\right\rangle)+\left\langle\tau(X,JY),X\right\rangle\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\tau(X,Y),JX\right\rangle+\frac{3}{2}\left\langle\tau(JX,Y),X\right\rangle.\end{split}

Let
e_{1}=\xi,e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
be a local unitary frame. Then,
X=\frac{e_{1}+\overline{e_{1}}}{\sqrt{2}}
and
JX=\frac{{\sqrt{-1}}(e_{1}-\overline{e_{1}})}{\sqrt{2}}
. By (
2.19
),

(2.20)

(\nabla_{X}J)X=-{\sqrt{-1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{1}}+\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{i}}}^{1}\right)e_{i}+{\sqrt{-1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tau_{1i}^{1}+\tau_{1i}^{\bar{1}}\right)\overline{e_{i}}.

Hence

(2.21)

\|(\nabla_{X}J)X\|^{2}=2\sum_{i=2}^{n}\left|\frac{1}{2}\tau_{1i}^{1}+\tau_{1i}^{\bar{1}}\right|^{2}.

Combining this with Corollary 3.1 in
[
31
]
, we have

(2.22)

\begin{split}&R^{L}(X,JX,JX,X)-\|(\nabla_{X}J)X\|^{2}\\
=&R^{L}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-2\sum_{i=2}^{n}\left|\frac{1}{2}\tau_{1i}^{1}+\tau_{1i}^{\bar{1}}\right|^{2}\\
=&R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\left(|\tau_{1i}^{\bar{1}}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\tau_{1i}^{1}|^{2}\right)-2\sum_{i=2}^{n}\left|\frac{1}{2}\tau_{1i}^{1}+\tau_{1i}^{\bar{1}}\right|^{2}\\
=&R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-\sum_{i=2}^{n}\left|\tau_{1i}^{1}+\tau_{1i}^{\bar{1}}\right|^{2}\\
=&H(\xi).\end{split}

∎

3.
Maximum principle and three circle theorem

In this section, motivated by Calabi’s work
[
2
]
, we obtain a general maximum principle and three circle theorem, and apply them to prove Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
T_{1},T_{2},\cdots,T_{m}
be
(1,1)
-tensors on
M
, and
Q
be a
(0,3)
tensor on
M
. For any smooth function
f
on
M
, define

(3.1)

\mathscr{L}[f]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\rm Hess}(f)(T_{i}\nabla f,T_{i}\nabla f)+Q(\nabla f,\nabla f,\nabla f).

Let
\Omega
be a precompact open subset in
M
, and
u
and
v
be two continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}
such that for any
\epsilon>0
and
x\in\Omega
, there are two smooth functions
u_{x,\epsilon}
and
v_{x,\epsilon}
defined on some neighborhood
U_{x,\epsilon}
of
x
, and a positive constant
c_{x}
independent of
\epsilon
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

u\geq u_{x,\epsilon}
on
U_{x,\epsilon}
and
u(x)=u_{x,\epsilon}(x)
;

(2)

\mathscr{L}[u_{x,\epsilon}](x)\geq-\epsilon
;

(3)

v\leq v_{x,\epsilon}
on
U_{x,\epsilon}
and
v(x)=v_{x,\epsilon}(x)
;

(4)

\mathscr{L}[v_{x,\epsilon}](x)\leq\epsilon
;

(5)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla u_{x,\epsilon},\nabla u_{x,\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla v_{x,\epsilon},\nabla v_{x,\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x)\geq c_{x}
;

(6)

u|_{\partial\Omega}\leq v|_{\partial\Omega}
.

Then,
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

We will proceed by contradiction. Suppose
u>v
for some point in
\Omega
. For each
\delta>0
small enough, let
u_{\delta}=a_{\delta}\ln(e^{u}+\delta)-b_{\delta}
where
a_{\delta}=\frac{1}{1+\delta}
and

b_{\delta}=\frac{\ln(e^{-\delta\min_{\partial\Omega}u}+\delta e^{-(1+\delta)\min_{\partial\Omega}u})}{1+\delta}.

Then,
u_{\delta}|_{\partial\Omega}\leq u|_{\partial\Omega}
. Similarly, let
v_{\delta}=A_{\delta}\ln(e^{v}-\delta)+B_{\delta}
where
A_{\delta}=\frac{1}{1-\delta}
and

B_{\delta}=-\frac{\ln(e^{\delta\min_{\partial\Omega}v}-\delta e^{-(1-\delta)\min_{\partial\Omega}v})}{1-\delta}.

Then,
v_{\delta}|_{\partial\Omega}\geq v|_{\partial\Omega}
. Note that
u_{\delta}\to u
and
v_{\delta}\to v
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
. So, we can fix
\delta>0
small enough, such that
\max_{\overline{\Omega}}(u_{\delta}-v_{\delta})>0
. Let
x_{\delta}\in\Omega
be a maximum point of
u_{\delta}-v_{\delta}
. For each
\epsilon>0
, let

(3.2)

u_{\delta,\epsilon}=a_{\delta}\ln(e^{u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}}+\delta)-b_{\delta}

and

(3.3)

v_{\delta,\epsilon}=A_{\delta}\ln(e^{v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}}-\delta)+B_{\delta}.

Then, it is clear that
x_{\delta}
is a maximum point of
u_{\delta,\epsilon}-v_{\delta,\epsilon}
in the neighborhood
U_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}
of
x_{\delta}
by the assumptions (1) and (3). So,

(3.4)

\nabla u_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{\delta})=\nabla v_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{\delta})

and

(3.5)

{\rm Hess}(u_{\delta,\epsilon})(x_{\delta})\leq{\rm Hess}(v_{\delta,\epsilon})(x_{\delta}).

Thus,

(3.6)

\mathscr{L}[u_{\delta,\epsilon}](x_{\delta})\leq\mathscr{L}[v_{\delta,\epsilon}](x_{\delta}).

Moreover, by direct computation and the assumptions (2) and (4),

(3.7)

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}[u_{\delta,\epsilon}](x_{\delta})=&\left(\frac{a_{\delta}e^{u(x_{\delta})}}{e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta}\right)^{3}\left(\mathscr{L}[u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}](x_{\delta})+\frac{\delta}{e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon},\nabla u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x_{\delta})\right)\\
\geq&\left(\frac{a_{\delta}e^{u(x_{\delta})}}{e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta}\right)^{3}\left(-\epsilon+\frac{\delta}{e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon},\nabla u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x_{\delta})\right)\\
\end{split}

and

(3.8)

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}[v_{\delta,\epsilon}](x_{\delta})=&\left(\frac{A_{\delta}e^{v(x_{\delta})}}{e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta}\right)^{3}\left(\mathscr{L}[v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}](x_{\delta})-\frac{\delta}{e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon},\nabla v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x_{\delta})\right)\\
\leq&\left(\frac{A_{\delta}e^{v(x_{\delta})}}{e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta}\right)^{3}\left(\epsilon-\frac{\delta}{e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon},\nabla v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}\right).\\
\end{split}

Then, by assumption (5),

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{L}[u_{\delta,\epsilon}](x_{\delta})-\mathscr{L}[v_{\delta,\epsilon}](x_{\delta})\\
\geq&-\left[\left(\frac{a_{\delta}e^{u(x_{\delta})}}{e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta}\right)^{3}+\left(\frac{A_{\delta}e^{v(x_{\delta})}}{e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta}\right)^{3}\right]\epsilon+\min\left\{\frac{\delta a_{\delta}^{3}e^{3u(x_{\delta})}}{(e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta)^{4}},\frac{\delta A_{\delta}^{3}e^{3v(x_{\delta})}}{(e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta)^{4}}\right\}\\
&\times\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon},\nabla u_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x_{\delta})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon},\nabla v_{x_{\delta},\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x_{\delta})\right)\\
\geq&-\left[\left(\frac{a_{\delta}e^{u(x_{\delta})}}{e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta}\right)^{3}+\left(\frac{A_{\delta}e^{v(x_{\delta})}}{e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta}\right)^{3}\right]\epsilon+\min\left\{\frac{\delta a_{\delta}^{3}e^{3u(x_{\delta})}}{(e^{u(x_{\delta})}+\delta)^{4}},\frac{\delta A_{\delta}^{3}e^{3v(x_{\delta})}}{(e^{v(x_{\delta})}-\delta)^{4}}\right\}c_{x_{\delta}}\\
>&0\end{split}

when
\epsilon>0
is small enough. This contradicts (
3.6
) and completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 1
.

Assumption (5) in Theorem
3.1
can be viewed as a nondegenerate condition on
T_{1},T_{2},\cdots T_{m}
. It is necessary for the maximum principle. Otherwise, consider the case
T_{1}=T_{2}=\cdots=T_{m}=0
. It is clear that the maximum principle is not true in this case because the crucial second order terms in
\mathscr{L}
vanishes. In our application,
\mathscr{L}=L
with
T_{1}=I
and
T_{2}=J
and
v
is a strictly increasing function of
r
. So, assumption (5) in Theorem
3.1
will be automatically satisfied.

By the maximum principle above, we have the following generalized three circle theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
T_{1},T_{2},\cdots,T_{m}
be
(1,1)
-tensors on
M
, and
Q
be a
(0,3)
tensor on
M
. For any smooth function
f
on
M
, define

(3.10)

\mathscr{L}[f]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\rm Hess}(f)(T_{i}\nabla f,T_{i}\nabla f)+Q(\nabla f,\nabla f,\nabla f).

Let
\Omega
be an open subset in
M
, and
u
and
v
be two continuous function on
\Omega
such that for any
\epsilon>0
and
x\in\Omega
, there are two smooth functions
u_{x,\epsilon}
and
v_{x,\epsilon}
defined on some neighborhood
U_{x,\epsilon}
of
x
, and a positive constant
c_{x}
independent of
\epsilon
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

u\geq u_{x,\epsilon}
on
U_{x,\epsilon}
and
u(x)=u_{x,\epsilon}(x)
;

(2)

\mathscr{L}[u_{x,\epsilon}](x)\geq-\epsilon
;

(3)

v\leq v_{x,\epsilon}
on
U_{x,\epsilon}
and
v(x)=v_{x,\epsilon}(x)
;

(4)

\mathscr{L}[v_{x,\epsilon}](x)\leq\epsilon
;

(5)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla u_{x,\epsilon},\nabla u_{x,\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle T_{i}\nabla v_{x,\epsilon},\nabla v_{x,\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}(x)\geq c_{x}
;

(6)

v:\Omega\to(\inf_{\Omega}v,\sup_{\Omega}v)
is proper;

(7)

M_{v}(u,t):=\max_{x\in S_{v}(t)}u(x)
is increasing on
t\in(\inf_{\Omega}v,\sup_{\Omega}v)
where
S_{v}(t)=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ v(x)=t\}.

Then,
M_{v}(u,t)
is a convex function of
t
in
(\inf_{\Omega}v,\sup_{\Omega}v)
.

Proof.

For any
\inf_{\Omega}u<t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}<\sup_{\Omega}u
.
If
M_{v}(u,t_{3})=M_{v}(u,t_{1})
, by that
M_{v}(u,t)
is increasing on
t
, we know that
M_{v}(u,t)
is a constant when
t\in[t_{1},t_{3}]
. So,

(3.11)

M_{v}(u,t_{2})=\frac{t_{3}-t_{2}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{1})+\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{3}).

When
M_{v}(u,t_{3})>M_{v}(u,t_{1})
. Let

(3.12)

\tilde{v}=\frac{M_{v}(u,t_{3})-M_{v}(u,t_{1})}{t_{3}-t_{1}}v+\frac{M_{v}(u,t_{1})t_{3}-M_{v}(u,t_{3})t_{1}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}.

Then
\tilde{v}\geq u
on
S_{v}(t_{1})\cup S_{v}(t_{3})
. So, by Theorem
3.1
,

(3.13)

\tilde{v}\geq u

in
A_{v}(t_{1},t_{3}):=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ t_{1}<v(x)<t_{3}\}
. Then, for any
x\in S_{v}(t_{2})
,

(3.14)

u(x)\leq\frac{M_{v}(u,t_{3})-M_{v}(u,t_{1})}{t_{3}-t_{1}}t_{2}+\frac{M_{v}(u,t_{1})t_{3}-M_{v}(u,t_{3})t_{1}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}.

and hence

(3.15)

M_{v}(u,t_{2})\leq\frac{t_{3}-t_{2}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{1})+\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{3}).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

As an application of the general maximum principle and three circle theorem. We come to prove Theorem

and Theorem

, a maximum principle and a three circle theorem for functions satisfying

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

-K
with

be a lower bound for the holomorphic sectional curvature of

in

4.
Almost Hermitian manifolds with slightly negative holomorphic sectional curvature

In this section, we prove the main results for almost Hermitian manifolds by applying the framework that set up in the last section. We first need the following lemmas on asymptotic behaviors of solutions for the ordinary differential equations related to the equations appeared in Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
q(t)
be a nonnegative continuous function on
[0,+\infty)
and
u\in C^{2}([0,+\infty))
be the solution of

u^{\prime\prime}-qu=0

with
u(0)=0
and
u^{\prime}(0)=1
. Then,

(1)

u^{\prime}
is increasing;

(2)

u^{\prime}(+\infty):=\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}u^{\prime}(t)<+\infty
if and only if
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}tq(t)dt<+\infty
;

(3)

when
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}tq(t)dt<+\infty
,
u^{\prime}(+\infty)\leq I(q).

Proof.

It is clear that
u>0
when
t>0
and hence
u^{\prime\prime}=qu\geq 0
on
[0,+\infty)
. So,
u^{\prime}(t)
is increasing and

u^{\prime}(t)\geq u^{\prime}(0)=1.

Thus
u(t)\geq t
. If
u^{\prime}(+\infty)<+\infty
, by that

(4.1)

u^{\prime}(+\infty)-1=\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{\prime\prime}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{+\infty}q(s)u(s)ds\geq\int_{0}^{+\infty}sq(s)ds,

one has

(4.2)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}tq(t)dt<+\infty.

On the other hand, when
\int_{0}^{+\infty}tq(t)dt<+\infty
, note that

(4.3)

\begin{split}u(t)=&\int_{0}^{t}u^{\prime}(s)ds\\
=&t+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}u^{\prime\prime}(\tau)d\tau ds\\
=&t+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}q(\tau)u(\tau)d\tau ds\\
=&t+\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)q(\tau)u(\tau)d\tau\\
\leq&t\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}q(\tau)u(\tau)d\tau\right).\\
\end{split}

Let
F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(\tau)u(\tau)d\tau
. Then, by the last inequality,

(4.4)

\begin{split}F^{\prime}(t)=q(t)u(t)\leq tq(t)\left(1+F(t)\right).\end{split}

So,

(4.5)

\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}sq(s)ds}F(t)\right]^{\prime}=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}sq(s)ds}[F^{\prime}(t)-tq(t)F(t)]\leq e^{-\int_{0}^{t}sq(s)ds}tq(t).

Thus,

(4.6)

F(t)\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}sq(s)ds}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{\tau}sq(s)ds}\tau q(\tau)d\tau=e^{\int_{0}^{t}sq(s)ds}-1.

for
t\geq 0
. Then,

(4.7)

u^{\prime}(t)=1+\int_{0}^{t}u^{\prime\prime}(s)ds=1+\int_{0}^{t}q(s)u(s)ds=1+F(t)\leq I(q).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
q(t)
be a nonnegative continuous function on
[0,+\infty)
such that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}tq(t)dt<+\infty
, and
h
be the solution of

(4.8)

h^{\prime}+h^{2}-q(t)=0

with
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}th(t)=1
. Let
v\in C^{2}((0,+\infty))
the solution of

(4.9)

v^{\prime\prime}+hv^{\prime}=0

with
v^{\prime}>0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{v(t)}{\log t}=1
. Then,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{v(t)}{\log t}
exists and

\frac{1}{I(q)}\leq\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{v(t)}{\log t}\leq 1.

Moreover,

(4.10)

\log t+v(1)\geq v(t)\geq\frac{1}{I(q)}\log t+v(1),\ \forall t>1.

Proof.

Let
u
be the function in Lemma
4.1
. It is not hard to check that

h=\frac{u^{\prime}}{u}.

Substituting this into the equation of
v
, we have

(4.11)

(uv^{\prime})^{\prime}=0.

Thus
v^{\prime}=\frac{c}{u}
for some positive constant
c
. By L’Hospital’s rule,

1=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{v(t)}{\log t}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}tv^{\prime}(t)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{ct}{u(t)}=c.

By L’Hospital’s rule again,

(4.12)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{v(t)}{\log t}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}{tv^{\prime}(t)}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{t}{u(t)}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{u^{\prime}(t)}=\frac{1}{u^{\prime}(+\infty)}\geq\frac{1}{I(q)}.

Moreover, because
u^{\prime}
is increasing,
u^{\prime}(+\infty)\geq u^{\prime}(0)=1
. Thus

(4.13)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{v(t)}{\log t}=\frac{1}{u^{\prime}(+\infty)}\leq 1.

Finally, for any
t>1
,

(4.14)

\begin{split}\log t=\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{s}ds\geq v(t)-v(1)=\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{u}ds\geq\frac{1}{I(q)}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{s}ds=\frac{1}{I(q)}\log t.\end{split}

This proves (
4.10
) and completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to prove the Liouville-type result in Corollary
1.1
by using Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Corollary
1.1
.

Note that for any
p\in M
, by Theorem
1.4
,

(4.15)

M_{p}(u,r)\leq M_{o}(u,r+r(o,p)).

So,

(4.16)

\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M_{p}(u,r)}{\log r}\leq\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M_{o}(u,r+r(o,p))}{\log r}\leq 0.

Moreover, by Theorem
1.3
,
M_{p}(u,r)
is convex with respect to
v_{p}(r)
where
v_{p}
is the function associated to
q_{p}
. So, for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}
,

(4.17)

M_{p}(u,r_{2})\leq\frac{v_{p}(r_{3})-v_{p}(r_{2})}{v_{p}(r_{3})-v_{p}(r_{1})}M_{p}(u,r_{1})+\frac{v_{p}(r_{2})-v_{p}(r_{1})}{v_{p}(r_{3})-v_{p}(r_{1})}M_{p}(u,r_{3})

Taking
\liminf_{r_{3}\to+\infty}
in the last inequality, by (
4.16
) and Lemma
4.2
, we get

(4.18)

M_{p}(u,r_{2})\leq M_{p}(u,r_{1}).

Then, by Theorem
1.4
,

M_{p}(u,r_{1})=M_{p}(u,r_{2})

for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
. Letting
r_{1}\to 0^{+}
in the last equation, we get

(4.19)

u(p)=M_{p}(u,r_{2})

for any
r_{2}>0
. So
u(p)=\max_{M}u
. Note that
p
is arbitrary. Thus
u
is a constant function.

By Lemma
2.1
and (
2.16
), we know that a pluri-subharmonic function

will automatically satisfy

. So, we have the same Liouville property for pluri-subharmonic functions.
∎

We next come to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

(1) It is clear by (
1.15
) that if
f\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, then

(4.20)

\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}\leq\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}<+\infty.

Conversely, suppose that
\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}=\lambda
. Then, by Theorem
1.3
,
\log M_{o}(|f|,r)-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}v(r)
is a convex function of
v(r)
, where
\alpha=\displaystyle\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{v(r)}{\log r}
. Then,

(4.21)

\begin{split}&\log M_{o}(|f|,r_{2})-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}v(r_{2})\\
\leq&\frac{v(r_{2})-v(r_{1})}{v(r_{3})-v(r_{1})}\left(\log M_{o}(|f|,r_{3})-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}v(r_{3})\right)+\frac{v(r_{3})-v(r_{2})}{v(r_{3})-v(r_{1})}\left(\log M_{o}(|f|,r_{1})-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}v(r_{1})\right)\end{split}

for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}
. Taking
\liminf_{r_{3}\to+\infty}
in the last inequality, we know that
\log M_{o}(|f|,r)-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}v(r)
is decreasing. So,

(4.22)

\log M_{o}(|f|,r)\leq\log M_{o}(|f|,1)-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}v(1)+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}v(r)

for any
r>1
, and hence

(4.23)

\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}\leq\lambda.

Thus
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(M)\subset\mathcal{P}(M)
.

(2) and (3) is clear from the proof of (1) by noting that
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{I(q)}
.

(4) By taking
\lambda={\rm ord}_{o}(f)
and
\alpha=1
in (
4.21
), letting
r_{1}\to 0^{+}
, and noting that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{v(r)}=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}={\rm ord}_{o}(f),

one gets the conclusion.

(5) is a direct corollary of (3) and (4).
∎

Remark 2
.

For a holomorphic function

By (4) of Theorem

, a Liouville theorem of Cheng-type for holomorphic functions.

.

Suppose

is nonconstant. Let

and

. By (4) of Theorem

,

\frac{|f(o)|+M_{o}(|f|,r_{2})}{e^{kv(r_{2})}}\geq\frac{M_{o}(|h|,r_{2})}{e^{kv(r_{2})}}\geq\frac{M_{o}(|h|,r_{1})}{e^{kv(r_{1})}}

By Corollary
1.2
, we can prove Corollary
1.3
, an analogue of fundamental theorem for algebra on almost Hermitian manifolds with slightly negative holomorphic sectional curvature.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant holomorphic function of polynomial growth on

such that

everywhere. Consider the universal covering
\pi:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
with

. Because

has a lift

along

which is clearly still holomorphic. That is,

for some holomorphic function

on

. Let

\zeta=e^{\frac{h}{N}}
. Then,

. Thus,

At the end of this section, we come to prove Theorem
1.6
. Before proving Theorem
1.6
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},J)
be an almost complex manifold of real dimension
2n
and
o\in M
. Let
(U,z)
a local complex coordinate at
o
such that
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\big|_{o}\in T^{1,0}_{o}M
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, and
f
be a holomorphic function on
U
such that

\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}f}{\partial z^{\alpha}}(o)=0

for any multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq m
. Then,
{\rm ord}_{o}(f)\geq m+1
.

Proof.

Suppose that

J\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}=J_{i}^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}+J_{i}^{\bar{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\bar{j}}}

and

J\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{{\bar{i}}}}=J_{{\bar{i}}}^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}+J_{{\bar{i}}}^{{\bar{j}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{{\bar{j}}}}.

It is clear that
\overline{J_{i}^{j}}=J_{\bar{i}}^{\bar{j}}
and
\overline{J_{i}^{\bar{j}}}=J_{\bar{i}}^{j}
and moreover
J_{i}^{j}(o)={\sqrt{-1}}\delta_{i}^{j}
and
J_{i}^{\bar{j}}(o)=0
since
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\big|_{o}
is a
(1,0)
-vector. Note that

(4.25)

\begin{split}\overline{\partial}f=&(df)^{(0,1)}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}(df+{\sqrt{-1}}Jdf)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{i}}dz^{i}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{i}}}dz^{\bar{i}}+{\sqrt{-1}}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{j}}Jdz^{j}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{j}}}Jdz^{\bar{j}}\right)\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{i}}+{\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{j}}J_{i}^{j}+{\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{j}}}J_{i}^{\bar{j}}\right)dz^{i}+\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{i}}}+{\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{j}}J_{\bar{i}}^{j}+{\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{j}}}J_{\bar{i}}^{\bar{j}}\right)dz^{\bar{i}}\right).\end{split}

So,

(4.26)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{i}}}+{\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{j}}J_{\bar{i}}^{j}+{\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{j}}}J_{\bar{i}}^{\bar{j}}=0.

This implies that, in a neighborhood of
o
,

(4.27)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}=A(z)\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}

where
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}=(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{1}}},\cdots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{n}}})^{T}
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}=(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{n}})^{T}.
Here
A
is the product of the inverse matrix of
(\delta_{i}^{j}+{\sqrt{-1}}J_{\bar{i}}^{\bar{j}})_{i,j=1,2,\cdots,n}
and
-({\sqrt{-1}}J_{\bar{i}}^{j})_{i,j=1,2,\cdots,n}
.

We next come to prove that

(4.28)

\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}f}{\partial z^{\alpha}\partial\bar{z}^{\beta}}(o)=0

for any multi-index
\alpha
and
\beta
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq m
by induction on
|\beta|
. The claim is clearly true when
|\beta|=0
by assumption. Suppose the claim is true for
|\beta|\leq k<m
. When
|\beta|=k+1
, let
\gamma
be a multi-index with
|\gamma|=k
such that
\beta_{i}=\gamma_{i}+1
for some fixed
i
. Then, by (
4.27
),

(4.29)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{\bar{i}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(z)\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{j}}.

So,

(4.30)

\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}f}{\partial z^{\alpha}\partial\bar{z}^{\beta}}(o)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|+|\gamma|}}{\partial z^{\alpha}\partial\bar{z}^{\gamma}}\left(A_{ij}(z)\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{j}}\right)(o)=0

by the induction hypothesis.

By (
4.28
), we know that the partial derivatives of
f
vanishes up to order
m
. Thus
{\rm ord}_{o}(f)\geq m+1
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
(z^{1},z^{2},\cdots,z^{n})
be a local complex coordinate at
o
such that
z^{i}(o)=0
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\big|_{o}
is a
(1,0)
-vector for

.
Let

\Phi:\mathcal{O}_{d}(M)\to\mathcal{O}_{[I(q)d]}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})

\Phi(f)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq[I(q)d]}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}f}{\partial z^{\alpha}}(o)z^{\alpha}.

If
\Phi
is not injective, then there is a nonzero

\Phi(f)=0
. By Lemma
4.3
,

{\rm ord}_{o}(f)\geq[I(q)d]+1>I(q)d\geq I(q)\deg f.

This contradicts the vanishing order estimate in (5) of Theorem
1.5

1.18
) and its special case (
1.19
).

When the holomorphic sectional curvature of

is nonnegative and the equality of (
1.19
) holds for some positive integer

, the map
\Phi:\mathcal{O}_{d}(M)\to\mathcal{O}_{d}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
defined above is an isomorphism. So, there are

such that
\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial z^{i}}(o)=\delta_{ij}
. Then
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}
form a local holomorphic coordinate at
o
. Because the point
o
can be arbitrarily chosen, the complex structure
J
is integrable. Thus
\tau_{ij}^{\bar{k}}=0
for any
i,j,k=1,2,\cdots,n
.

For each
\xi\in T^{1,0}_{o}M
with
\|\xi\|=1
, let
(z^{1},z^{2},\cdots,z^{n})
be a holomorphic coordinate at
o
with
z(o)=0
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{1}}\big|_{o}=\xi
and
g_{i\bar{j}}(o)=\delta_{ij}
. By that
\Phi
is an isomorphism, there is a holomorphic function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{d}(M)
such that

(4.31)

f(z)=(z^{1})^{d}+O(r(z)^{d+1})

Because
{\rm ord}_{o}(f)=d\geq\deg f
, by Theorem
1.5
,
{\rm ord}_{o}(f)=\deg f=d
and

(4.32)

\log M_{o}(|f|,r)=d\log r+c

for some constant
c
. For each
\epsilon>0
, let
z_{\epsilon}\in S_{o}(\epsilon)
attain the maximal modulus of
f
on
S_{o}(\epsilon)
. Let

F(x)=\log|f|(x)-d\log r(x)-c

where
r(x)=r_{o}(x)
. Then,
F\leq 0
and
F(z_{\epsilon})=0
. Noting that
f(z_{\epsilon})\neq 0
and
L[\log|f|](z_{\epsilon})=0
since
f
is holomorphic. We have

(4.33)

\nabla\log|f|(z_{\epsilon})=d\nabla\log r(z_{\epsilon})

and

(4.34)

L[\log r](z_{\epsilon})\geq 0

because
z_{\epsilon}
is a maximum point for
F
. Thus, by Theorem
1.2
,

(4.35)

L[\log r](z_{\epsilon})=0

and hence by (
2.24
) in the proof of Theorem
1.2
and (
2.15
),

(4.36)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tau_{{\bar{i}}\bar{1}}^{\bar{1}}(z_{\epsilon})=r_{1{\bar{i}}}(z_{\epsilon})={\rm Hess}(r)(z_{\epsilon})(e_{1}^{\epsilon},\overline{e_{i}^{\epsilon}})+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\tau_{\bar{i}\bar{1}}^{\bar{1}}(z_{\epsilon})

for
i=2,\cdots,n
. Here, at
z_{\epsilon}
, the unitary frame
e_{1}^{\epsilon},e_{2}^{\epsilon},\cdots,e_{n}^{\epsilon}
is chosen to be such that

e_{1}^{\epsilon}=\mathbf{U}(\nabla r)(z_{\epsilon})=\mathbf{U}(\nabla|f|^{2})(z_{\epsilon}).

Note that
\mathbf{U}(\nabla|f|^{2})(z_{\epsilon})
sub-converges to
\lambda\xi
with
|\lambda|=1
by (
4.31
). Then, by letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in (
4.36
), since

(4.37)

{\rm Hess}(r)(z_{\epsilon})(e_{1}^{\epsilon},\overline{e_{i}^{\epsilon}})\to 0

as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
for
i=2,3,\cdots,n
(See
[
27
, Lemma 2.2]
), we know that

(4.38)

\tau_{i1}^{1}(o)=0

for
i=2,3,\cdots,n
, where the unitary frame at
o
is a sub-convergent limit of
(e_{1}^{\epsilon},e_{2}^{\epsilon},\cdots,e_{n}^{\epsilon})
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
. This implies that

(4.39)

\left\langle\tau(\eta,\xi),\bar{\xi}\right\rangle=0

for any
\eta\in T_{o}^{1,0}M
with
\eta\perp\xi
. Because
\xi
is arbitrary chosen and
\tau
is skew symmetric, we know that

(4.40)

\left\langle\tau(\eta,\xi),\bar{\xi}\right\rangle=0

for any
\eta,\xi\in T_{o}^{1,0}M
. Then

(4.41)

\left\langle\tau(\eta,\xi+\zeta),\overline{\xi+\zeta}\right\rangle=0

and

(4.42)

\left\langle\tau(\eta,\xi+{\sqrt{-1}}\zeta),\overline{\xi+{\sqrt{-1}}\zeta}\right\rangle=0

for any
\eta,\xi,\zeta\in T_{o}^{1,0}M
which implies that

\left\langle\tau(\eta,\xi),\bar{\zeta}\right\rangle=0

for any
\eta,\xi,\zeta\in T_{o}^{1,0}M
. Thus
\tau_{ij}^{k}(o)=0
for any
i,j,k=1,2,\cdots,n
. Because
o
is arbitrary chosen, we know that
\tau_{ij}^{k}=0
all over
M
. Hence
\tau
vanishes and
(M,J,g)
is Kähler. Finally, by the rigidity part of
[
16
, Theorem 4]
, we know that
(M,J,g)
must be biholomorphically isometric to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
∎

5.
Converse of Three Circle Theorem on Hermitian manifolds

In this section, we will discuss the converse of the three circle theorem on Hermitian manifolds and prove Theorem
1.7
.

For completeness, we first compute the equation for geodesics in a local holomorphic coordinate. Here, for geodesics we always mean geodesics with respect to the Levi-Civita connection.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a Hermitian manifold and
o\in M
. Let
\gamma
a be geodesic starting at
o
and
z=(z^{1},z^{2},\cdots,z^{n})
be a local holomorphic coordinate at
o
. Suppose that
z(\gamma(t))=(z^{1}(t),z^{2}(t),\cdots,z^{n}(t))
. Then,

(5.1)

\frac{d^{2}z^{i}}{dt^{2}}+\Gamma_{jk}^{i}\frac{dz^{j}}{dt}\frac{dz^{k}}{dt}+g^{\bar{\lambda}i}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}}\frac{dz^{\bar{j}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{l}}{dt}=0

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
is the Christofel symbol for the Chern connection
D
.

Proof.

Let
X=\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}-D_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=-D_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}+X^{\bar{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\bar{i}}}
. Then, by (
2.1
),

(5.2)

\left\langle X,\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\bar{j}}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tau\left(\gamma^{\prime},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\bar{j}}}\right),\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle.

Thus, by noting that
\tau_{ij}^{\bar{k}}=0
because of the integrability of complex structure, we have

(5.3)

X^{i}g_{i{\bar{j}}}=\tau_{{\bar{i}}{\bar{j}}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}}\frac{dz^{\bar{i}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{l}}{dt}.

Therefore,

(5.4)

X^{i}=g^{\bar{\lambda}i}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}}\frac{dz^{\bar{j}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{l}}{dt}.

Moreover, note that

(5.5)

D_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\left(\frac{d^{2}z^{i}}{dt^{2}}+\Gamma_{jk}^{i}\frac{dz^{j}}{dt}\frac{dz^{k}}{dt}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}+\left(\frac{d^{2}z^{\bar{i}}}{dt^{2}}+\overline{\Gamma_{jk}^{i}}\frac{dz^{\bar{j}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{\bar{k}}}{dt}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\bar{i}}}.

So, the equation for geodesics in local holomorphic coordinates is (
5.1
).
∎

We next need the following lemma for the existence of good local holomorphic coordinates for Hermitian manifolds.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a Herimitian manifold. Then, for any
o\in M
and
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
of
\in T^{1,0}_{o}M
, there is a local holomorphic coordinate
(z^{1},z^{2},\cdots,z^{n})
at
o
such that
z(o)=0
,
e_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\big|_{o}
,

\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}(o)=\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(o)+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{ij}^{k}(o)=\frac{1}{2}\left({\Gamma_{ij}^{k}+\Gamma_{ji}^{k}}\right)(o)=0

and

\partial_{i}\tilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^{l}(o)X^{i}X^{j}X^{k}=\partial_{i}\Gamma_{jk}^{l}(o)X^{i}X^{j}X^{k}=0

for any
X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. We call this coordinate a normal local holomorphic coordinate at
o
for the basis
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
of
T^{1,0}_{o}M
on the Hermitian manifold.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{D}
be the connection defined by
\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}
. Then
\tilde{D}
is a torsion free connection compatible with the complex structure. So, by standard argument, we know the existence of local holomorphic coordinate
w
at
o
such that
w(o)=0
,
e_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{i}}\big|_{o}
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{i}w^{j}}^{w^{k}}(o)=0
. Let
z
be another local holomorphic coordinate at
o
to be determined such that
z(o)=0
,
\frac{\partial w^{i}}{\partial z^{j}}(o)=\delta_{j}^{i}
and
\frac{\partial^{2}w^{k}}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}}(o)=0
. Then, it is clear that
e_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\big|_{o}
and by the transformation of Christofel symbols

(5.6)

\tilde{\Gamma}_{z^{i}z^{j}}^{z^{k}}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{\lambda}w^{\mu}}^{w^{\nu}}\frac{\partial w^{\lambda}}{\partial z^{i}}\frac{\partial w^{\mu}}{\partial z^{j}}\frac{\partial z^{k}}{\partial w^{\nu}}+\frac{\partial^{2}w^{\nu}}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}}\frac{\partial z^{k}}{\partial w^{\nu}}

we know that
\tilde{\Gamma}_{z^{i}z^{j}}^{z^{k}}(o)=0
. Moreover, note that

(5.7)

\begin{split}\partial_{z^{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{z^{j}z^{k}}^{z^{l}}(o)=&\partial_{z^{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{\lambda}w^{\mu}}^{w^{\nu}}\frac{\partial w^{\lambda}}{\partial z^{j}}\frac{\partial w^{\mu}}{\partial z^{k}}\frac{\partial z^{l}}{\partial w^{\nu}}+\frac{\partial^{3}w^{\nu}}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}\partial z^{k}}\frac{\partial z^{l}}{\partial w^{\nu}}\\
=&\partial_{w^{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{j}w^{k}}^{w^{l}}(o)+\frac{\partial^{3}w^{l}}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}\partial z^{k}}(0)\end{split}

So, we require that

(5.8)

\frac{\partial^{3}w^{l}}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}\partial z^{k}}(0)X^{i}X^{j}X^{k}=-\partial_{w^{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{j}w^{k}}^{w^{l}}(o)X^{i}X^{j}X^{k}

for any
X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. This requirement can be satisfied by chosen
z
such that

(5.9)

\frac{\partial^{3}w^{l}}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}\partial z^{k}}(0)=-\frac{1}{3}\left(\partial_{w^{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{j}w^{k}}^{w^{l}}+\partial_{w^{k}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{i}w^{j}}^{w^{l}}+\partial_{w^{j}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{k}w^{i}}^{w^{l}}\right)(o)

by noting that
\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{i}w^{j}}^{w_{k}}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{w^{j}w^{i}}^{w_{k}}
. This completes the proof of lemma.
∎

Remark 3
.

The requirement that
\partial_{i}\tilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^{l}(o)X^{i}X^{j}X^{k}=0
for any
X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is the same as requiring that

(5.10)

\partial_{i}\tilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^{l}+\partial_{j}\tilde{\Gamma}_{ki}^{l}+\partial_{k}\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{l}(o)=0.

It is also equivalent to

(5.11)

\partial_{i}\Gamma_{jk}^{l}+\partial_{i}\Gamma_{kj}^{l}+\partial_{j}\Gamma_{ki}^{l}+\partial_{j}\Gamma_{ik}^{l}+\partial_{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{l}+\partial_{k}\Gamma_{ji}^{l}(o)=0.

Next, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g)
be a Hermitian manifold,
o\in M
,
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
be a unitary basis of
T_{o}^{1,0}M
, and
z=(z^{1},z^{2},\cdots,z^{n})
be a normal local holomorphic coordinate for
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
. Let
\gamma
be a normal geodesic with
\gamma(0)=o
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=X=X^{i}e_{i}+X^{{\bar{i}}}\overline{e_{i}}\in T_{o}M
. Suppose
z(\gamma(t))=(z^{1}(t),z^{2}(t),\cdots,z^{n}(t))
. Then,

\begin{split}&z^{i}(t)\\
=&X^{i}t+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}t^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\bigg(\left(R_{j{\bar{i}}k{\bar{l}}}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\lambda j}^{i}\tau_{\bar{\lambda}\bar{l}}^{\bar{k}}-\tau_{\bar{\lambda}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\lambda j}^{l}\right)(o)X^{j}X^{k}X^{\bar{l}}\\
&-\left(\partial_{\mu}(\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\mu}X^{l}+\partial_{\bar{\mu}}(\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\bar{\mu}}X^{l}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\bar{k}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{\mu}}(o)X^{\bar{\lambda}}X^{\mu}\right)X^{\bar{j}}\bigg)t^{3}+O(t^{4}).\end{split}

Proof.

Substituting the initial data
z^{i}(0)=0
and
\frac{dz^{i}}{dt}(0)=X^{i}
into (
5.1
), we have

(5.12)

\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}z^{i}}{dt^{2}}(0)=&-\Gamma_{jk}^{i}(o)X^{j}X^{k}-g^{\bar{\lambda}i}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}}(o)X^{\bar{j}}X^{l}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\tau_{jk}^{i}(o)X^{j}X^{k}-\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{i}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}\\
=&\tau_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}\\
\end{split}

by that
\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}(o)=0
and
g_{i{\bar{j}}}(o)=\delta_{ij}
. Moreover,

(5.13)

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{3}z^{i}}{dt^{3}}(0)\\
=&-\partial_{l}\Gamma_{jk}^{i}(o)\frac{dz^{l}}{dt}\frac{dz^{j}}{dt}\frac{dz^{k}}{dt}(0)-\partial_{\bar{l}}\Gamma_{jk}^{i}(o)\frac{dz^{\bar{l}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{j}}{dt}\frac{dz^{k}}{dt}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{jk}^{i}(o)\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dz^{j}}{dt}\frac{dz^{k}}{dt}\right)(0)\\
&-\partial_{\mu}(g^{\bar{\lambda}i}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)\frac{dz^{\mu}}{dt}\frac{dz^{\bar{j}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{l}}{dt}(0)-\partial_{\bar{\mu}}(g^{\bar{\lambda}i}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)\frac{dz^{\bar{\mu}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{\bar{j}}}{dt}\frac{dz^{l}}{dt}(0)\\
&-g^{\bar{\lambda}i}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}}(o)\frac{d^{2}z^{\bar{j}}}{dt^{2}}\frac{dz^{l}}{dt}(0)-g^{\bar{\lambda}i}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}}(o)\frac{dz^{\bar{j}}}{dt}\frac{d^{2}z^{l}}{dt^{2}}(0)\\
=&R_{j{\bar{i}}k{\bar{l}}}(o)X^{j}X^{k}X^{\bar{l}}-\partial_{\mu}(g^{\bar{\lambda}i})\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\mu}X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}-\partial_{\mu}(\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\mu}X^{\bar{j}}X^{l}\\
&-\partial_{\bar{\mu}}(g^{\bar{\lambda}i})\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\bar{\mu}}X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}-\partial_{\bar{\mu}}(\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\bar{\mu}}X^{\bar{j}}X^{l}-\tau_{\bar{\lambda}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\lambda j}^{l}(o)X^{j}X^{k}X^{\bar{l}}\\
&-\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\bar{k}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{\mu}}(o)X^{\bar{\lambda}}X^{\mu}X^{\bar{j}}\end{split}

by noting that
\partial_{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{l}(o)X^{i}X^{j}X^{k}=0
,
R_{j{\bar{i}}k{\bar{l}}}(o)=-\partial_{\bar{l}}\Gamma_{jk}^{i}(o)
and using (
5.12
). Furthermore, note that

(5.14)

\partial_{\mu}g^{\bar{\lambda}i}(o)=-\partial_{\mu}g_{\lambda{\bar{i}}}(o)=-\Gamma_{\lambda\mu}^{i}(o)=\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\lambda\mu}^{i}(o)

and

(5.15)

\partial_{\bar{\mu}}g^{\bar{\lambda}i}(o)=-\partial_{\bar{\mu}}g_{\lambda{\bar{i}}}(o)=-\Gamma_{{\bar{i}}\bar{\mu}}^{\bar{\lambda}}(o)=\frac{1}{2}\tau_{{\bar{i}}\bar{\mu}}^{\bar{\lambda}}(o).

Substituting these into (
5.13
), we get

(5.16)

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{3}z^{i}}{dt^{3}}(0)\\
=&\left(R_{j{\bar{i}}k{\bar{l}}}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\lambda j}^{i}\tau_{\bar{\lambda}\bar{l}}^{\bar{k}}-\tau_{\bar{\lambda}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\lambda j}^{l}\right)(o)X^{j}X^{k}X^{\bar{l}}\\
&-\left(\partial_{\mu}(\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\mu}X^{l}+\partial_{\bar{\mu}}(\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\bar{\mu}}X^{l}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{\bar{j}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\bar{k}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{\mu}}(o)X^{\bar{\lambda}}X^{\mu}\right)X^{\bar{j}}.\end{split}

Then, by Taylor expansion, we get the conclusion.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Suppose the conclusion is not true. Then, there is a point
o\in M
and a unitary basis
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
at
o
such that

(5.17)

R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}|(o)<0.

Let
(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n})
be a normal local holomorphic coordinate for
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
at
o
.

In the following, let the notations be the same as in Lemma
5.3
. Let

f(z)=z^{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{1i}^{1}(o)z^{i}\right)

and

p(z)=z^{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{i}}}^{\bar{1}}(o)z^{\bar{i}}\right).

Then
f
is a holomorphic function near
o
and
|f|=|p|
. Because
\log M(|f|,r)
is a convex function of
\log r
, by (4) of Theorem
1.5
, we know that
\frac{M_{o}(|f|,r)}{r}
is increasing. On the other hand, by Lemma
5.3
,

\begin{split}&p(z(t))\\
=&X^{1}t+\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{j,k=2}^{n}\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{j}}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}\right)t^{2}+\\
&\frac{1}{6}\bigg(\left(R_{j\bar{1}k{\bar{l}}}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\lambda j}^{1}\tau_{\bar{\lambda}\bar{l}}^{\bar{k}}-\tau_{\bar{\lambda}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\lambda j}^{l}\right)(o)X^{j}X^{k}X^{\bar{l}}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{{\bar{i}}1}^{\bar{1}}\tau_{ij}^{k}(o)X^{j}X^{\bar{k}}X^{1}\\
&-\left(\partial_{\mu}(\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\mu}X^{l}+\partial_{\bar{\mu}}(\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\bar{\mu}}X^{l}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\bar{k}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{\mu}}(o)X^{\bar{\lambda}}X^{\mu}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{\bar{1}\bar{i}}^{\bar{1}}\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{j}}}^{\bar{k}}X^{k}X^{\bar{i}}\right)X^{\bar{j}}\bigg)t^{3}\\
&+O(t^{4}).\end{split}

Thus,

\begin{split}&|f(z(t))|^{2}\\
=&|p(z(t))|^{2}\\
=&|X^{1}|^{2}t^{2}+\Re\left\{\left(\sum_{j,k=2}^{n}\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{j}}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}\right)X^{\bar{1}}\right\}t^{3}\\
&+\frac{1}{3}\Re\bigg\{\left(R_{j\bar{1}k{\bar{l}}}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\lambda j}^{1}\tau_{\bar{\lambda}\bar{l}}^{\bar{k}}-\tau_{\bar{\lambda}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\lambda j}^{l}\right)(o)X^{j}X^{k}X^{\bar{l}}X^{\bar{1}}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{{\bar{i}}1}^{\bar{1}}\tau_{ij}^{k}(o)X^{j}X^{\bar{k}}|X^{1}|^{2}\\
&-\left(\partial_{\mu}(\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\mu}X^{l}+\partial_{\bar{\mu}}(\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}g_{l{\bar{k}}})(o)X^{\bar{\mu}}X^{l}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{\bar{j}\bar{1}}^{\bar{k}}\tau_{\bar{k}\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{\mu}}(o)X^{\bar{\lambda}}X^{\mu}+\frac{3}{2}\tau_{\bar{1}\bar{i}}^{\bar{1}}\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{j}}}^{\bar{k}}X^{k}X^{\bar{i}}\right)X^{\bar{j}}X^{\bar{1}}\bigg\}t^{4}\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\left|\sum_{j,k=2}^{n}\tau_{\bar{1}{\bar{j}}}^{\bar{k}}(o)X^{\bar{j}}X^{k}\right|^{2}t^{4}+O(t^{5})\\
\leq&|X^{1}|^{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{3}(R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}|^{2})(o)|X^{1}|^{4}t^{4}+C\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-|X^{1}|^{2}\right)t^{3}+t^{4}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}-|X^{1}|^{2}}+t^{5}\right)\end{split}

for some positive constant
C
and when
t
is small enough, by noting that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{i}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}

since
\|X\|=1
and
g_{i\bar{j}}(o)=\delta_{ij}
. Note that

(5.18)

\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}-|X^{1}|^{2}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{2}-|X^{1}|^{2}\right)

where
\epsilon>0
is chosen to satisfy

(5.19)

\frac{C\epsilon}{4}=-\frac{1}{24}(R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}|^{2})(o).

So, when
t
is small enough,

(5.20)

\begin{split}|f(z(t))|^{2}\leq&|X^{1}|^{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{3}(R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}|^{2})(o)|X^{1}|^{4}t^{4}\\
&+C\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-|X^{1}|^{2}\right)t^{3}+\left(\frac{\epsilon}{4}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{2}-|X^{1}|^{2}\right)\right)t^{4}+t^{5}\right)\end{split}

Note that, when
t
is small enough, the function

(5.21)

F_{t}(x)=x+\frac{1}{3}(R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}|^{2})(o)x^{2}t^{2}+C\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-x\right)t+\left(\frac{\epsilon}{4}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{2}-x\right)\right)t^{2}+t^{3}\right)

is increasing for
x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. Thus
F_{t}(x)\leq F_{t}(\frac{1}{2})
. So,

(5.22)

|f(z(t))|^{2}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{24}(R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\tau_{i1}^{1}|^{2})(o)t^{4}+Ct^{5}<\frac{t^{2}}{2}

when
t>0
is small enough. This implies that

(5.23)

\frac{M_{o}(|f|,r)}{r}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}

when
r>0
is small enough. On the other hand, it is clear that

(5.24)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{M_{o}(|f|,r)}{r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

This contradicts the fact that
\frac{M_{o}(|f|,r)}{r}
is increasing.

The equivalence of the two curvature conditions can be seen in the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

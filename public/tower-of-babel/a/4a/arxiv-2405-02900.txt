Weighted Ehrhart theory via equivariant toric geometry
Laurenţiu Maxim, Jörg Schürmann

Abstract
. We give a K K -theoretic and geometric interpretation for a generalized weighted Ehrhart theory of a full-dimensional lattice polytope P P , depending on a given homogeneous polynomial function φ \varphi on P P , and with Laurent polynomial weights f Q ​ ( y ) ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ y ± 1 ] f_{Q}(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}] associated to the faces Q ⪯ P Q\preceq P of the polytope. For this purpose, we calculate equivariant K K -theoretic Hodge–Chern classes of a torus-equivariant mixed Hodge module ℳ {\mathcal{M}} on the toric variety X P X_{P} associated to P P (defined via an equivariant embedding of X P X_{P} into an ambient smooth variety). For any integer ℓ \ell , we introduce a corresponding equivariant Hodge χ y \chi_{y} -polynomial χ y ​ ( X P , ℓ ​ D P , [ ℳ ] ) \chi_{y}(X_{P},\ell D_{P};[{\mathcal{M}}]) , with D P D_{P} the corresponding ample Cartier divisor on X P X_{P} (defined by the facet presentation of P P ). Motivic properties of the Hodge–Chern classes are used to express this equivariant Hodge polynomial in terms of weighted character sums fitting with a generalized weighted Ehrhart theory. The equivariant Hodge polynomials are shown to satisfy a reciprocity and purity formula fitting with the duality for equivariant mixed Hodge modules, and implying the corresponding properties for the generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomials. In the special case of the equivariant intersection cohomology mixed Hodge module, with the weight function corresponding to Stanley’s g g -function of the polar polytope of P P , we recover in geometric terms a recent combinatorial formula of Beck–Gunnells–Materov. More generally, motivated by the analogy to the Kazhdan–Lusztig theory, we introduce a duality involution on the free ℤ ⁡ [ y ± 1 ] {\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}] -module of weight functions corresponding to the duality of equivariant mixed Hodge modules, and prove a new reciprocity formula in terms of this duality. This unifies and generalizes the classical reciprocity formula of Brion–Vergne in Ehrhart theory as well as the above-mentioned more recent combinatorial formula of Beck–Gunnells–Materov.

1.
Introduction

We provide a geometric (equivariant) perspective for the generalized weighted Ehrhart theory developed in
[
5
]
for a full-dimensional lattice polytope
P
with a homogeneous polynomial function
\varphi
defined on it. This is achieved by attaching Laurent polynomial weights
f_{Q}(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
of geometric origin to the faces
\emptyset\neq Q\preceq P
of the polytope (with the partial order given by face inclusion). The Brion–Vergne
[
10
]
combinatorial approach to reciprocity can be linearly extended to this generalized weighted Ehrhart theory. Motivation for using such weights comes from our prior work
[
11
]
on equivariant Hodge–Chern and Hirzebruch characteristic classes of toric varieties (associated to such polytopes), as well as from the use of Stanley’s
g
-functions in the generalized Ehrhart reciprocity theorem of Beck–Gunnells–Materov
[
5
]
.

1.1.
Combinatorial and geometrical posets and their identification

We review here some basic notions explaining the relation between lattice polytopes and toric geometry; for more details see
[
13
,
20
]
.

Let
M\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be a lattice and
P\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}:=M\otimes{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a full-dimensional lattice polytope with vertices in
M
.
To
P
one associates a fan
\Sigma=\Sigma_{P}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
, with
N
the dual lattice of
M
, called the inner normal fan of
P
. To the fan
\Sigma_{P}
one further associates a projective toric variety
X=X_{P}
with torus
{\mathbb{T}}\;\simeq({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
(whose character lattice is
M
) and an ample Cartier divisor
D=D_{P}
. There is an order-reversing one-to-one correspondence between the faces
\emptyset\neq Q\preceq P
of
P
and the cones
\sigma_{Q}
of
\Sigma_{P}
, with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)=n-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma_{Q})
. There is an order-reversing one-to-one correspondence between the cones
\sigma_{Q}
of
\Sigma_{P}
and the
{\mathbb{T}}
-orbits
O_{Q}
of the toric variety
X_{P}
, with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(O_{Q})=n-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma_{Q})
. Then the identification between the faces
\emptyset\neq Q\preceq P
of
P
and the torus orbits
O_{Q}
of the toric variety
X_{P}
is order-preserving, with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(O_{Q})
.
The closure
V_{Q}
of the orbit
O_{Q}
is itself a toric varity for a quotient torus of
{\mathbb{T}}
. For
F=Q
a facet of
P
(i.e.
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)=n-1
), this gives a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant prime divisor
D_{F}:=V_{F}
.
Since
P
is full-dimensional, it has a unique facet presentation

P=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}|\>\langle m,u_{F}\rangle\geq-a_{F}\>\text{for all facets $F$ of $P$}\}\>,

with
a_{F}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
u_{F}\in N
the inward pointing facet normal and
\langle-,-\rangle
the (real extension of the) duality pairing

M\times N\to{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then by definition
D=D_{P}:=\sum_{F}a_{F}\cdot D_{F}
is the ample Cartier divisor associated to
P
.
Note that the fan
\Sigma_{P}
and the toric variety
X_{P}
are not changed, if we multiply
P
by a positive integer
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
or translate it by a lattice point
m\in M
:

\Sigma_{P}=\Sigma_{kP-m}\quad\text{and}\quad X_{P}=X_{kP-m}\>.

But the ample Cartier divisor
D_{P}
is changed via

D_{kP-m}=k\cdot D_{P}+div(\chi^{m})=k\cdot D_{P}+\sum_{F}\langle m,u_{F}\rangle\cdot D_{F}\>,

with
div(\chi^{m})
the divisor of the character
\chi^{m}
of
m\in M
viewed as a rational function on
X_{P}\supset{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
For a given face
Q
of
P
, choose
m\in Q\cap M
so that
Q-m
is a full dimensional lattice polytope in its linear hull

Span(Q-m)
relative to the lattice
M^{\prime}=M\cap Span(Q-m)
. Then the orbit closure
V_{Q}
is the toric variety associated to
Q-m
.

If, in addition,
P
contains the origin in its interior, then its
polar polytope

P^{\circ}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}

is defined as in
[
20
, Section 1.5]
, as a full-dimensional rational polytope with respect to the rational points
N_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
, and containing the origin in the interior, given by

P^{\circ}:=\{u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}|\>\langle m,u\rangle\geq-1\>\text{for all $m\in P$}\}\>,\quad\text{with $(P^{\circ})^{\circ}=P$.}

By taking cones at the origin of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
over the proper faces of
P^{\circ}
, with the empty set
\emptyset
corresponding to the origin, one gets the same lattice fan
\Sigma_{P}
(hence the same toric variety
X_{P}
). This correspondence between the proper faces
\emptyset\preceq Q^{\circ}\prec P^{\circ}
of
P^{\circ}
and the cones of
\Sigma_{P}
is order-preserving, increasing real dimension by
1
. (Here
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\emptyset=-1
by convention.) This induces an order-reversing one-to-one correspondence between the faces
Q
of
P
, and the faces
Q^{\circ}
of the polar polytope
P^{\circ}
, switching the roles of polytopes and emptysets (seen as faces). Moreover, for a proper face
\emptyset\neq Q\prec P
of
P
, one has
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q^{\circ})=n-1
.
In particular, the facets
F
of
P
correspond to the vertices
a_{F}^{-1}\cdot u_{F}\in N_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
P^{\circ}

(with
a_{F}>0
, since the origin is an interior point of
P
).

The original polytope
P\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
is classically used for counting lattice points (and the corresponding Ehrhart theory), fitting with sections of the ample line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P})
on
X_{P}

[
14
,
20
,
13
]
. The torus orbits of
X_{P}
give a natural
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Whitney stratification, which is particularly useful for studying the topology of
X_{P}
as well as
in our geometric interpretation of weighted lattice points counting via the theory of Hirzebruch homology classes
T_{y*}
of
[
8
]
and the corresponding Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem; see
[
26
,
27
]
. The polar polytope
P^{\circ}
appears in Stanley’s work
[
35
]
for recursively defining his
g
-polynomials

g_{Q^{\circ}}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
(with
g(\emptyset)=1
), for the Eulerian graded poset given by the faces
\emptyset\preceq Q^{\circ}\preceq P^{\circ}
,
with rank function
\rho(Q^{\circ})=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q^{\circ})+1
.
It is well-known
[
35
,
15
,
18
,
33
]
(see also
[
7
]
) that these
g
-polynomials are related to the intersection cohomology complex of
X_{P}
. In the use of these
g
-polynomials, we will (implicitly) switch from
P
to
P^{\prime}=kP-m
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and

m\in M
, so that
P^{\prime}
contains the origin in its interior. Then
g_{Q^{\circ}}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
doesn’t depend on the choice of
k
and
m
.

1.2.
Generalized weighted Ehrhart theory

Let
\varphi\colon M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a homogeneous polynomial function.
We assign to the posets mentioned in the previous subsection Laurent polynomial weights via a
weight vector

f=\{f_{Q}\}
, with
f_{Q}(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
indexed here by the non-empty faces
\emptyset\neq Q\preceq P
of
P
. Motivated by
[
5
]
, we assemble these weights, the combinatorics of
P
, and the polynomial function
\varphi
into the
generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomial

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
, resp.,
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
, as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

For
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, define the generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomial of
P
,
f
and
\varphi
by

(1.1)

\begin{split}E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)&:=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\varphi(m)\\
&=:(1+y)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y),\end{split}

with

{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)
denoting the relative interior of the face
{\ell}Q
of the dilated polytope
\ell P
.

The renormalization
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
is more natural to work with from the geometric perspective considered in this paper.
The Brion–Vergne
[
10
, Prop.4.1]
combinatorial approach to reciprocity can be linearly extended over
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]

to this generalized weighted Ehrhart theory, so that

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
, resp.,
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
, have the following properties:

(1)

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
is obtained by evaluating a polynomial
E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(z,y)
at
z=\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Similarly for
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
. In particular, these expressions can be evaluated at any
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(2)

(Reciprocity formula) For
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, using
[
10
, Prop.4.1]
for each face
Q
of
P
, one gets

(1.2)

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{{\ell}}Q\cap M}\varphi(m).

Similarly,

(1.3)

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot(-1)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{{\ell}}Q\cap M}\varphi(m).

(3)

(Constant term) For
\ell=0
,

(1.4)

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(0,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\varphi(0),

that is, evaluating
\sum_{m\in{{\ell}}Q\cap M}\varphi(m)
at
\ell=0
as
\varphi(0)
(see
[
10
, Prop.4.1]
). Similarly,

(1.5)

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(0,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot(-1)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\varphi(0).

Remark 1.2
.

If we do not assume that the polynomial function
\varphi
is homogeneous, then one has for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
the reciprocity formula

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{{\ell}}Q\cap M}\varphi(-m)\>,

with constant term
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(0,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\varphi(0)
.

In the following, we formulate all our results only for
\varphi
homogeneous.

Remark 1.3
.

In case
f_{Q}(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y]
for all
Q
, we are allowed to evaluate these formulae at
y=0
.
For instance, if
f_{Q}=\delta_{Q,^{\prime}Q}
is a Kronecker function, i.e.,
f_{Q}=1
for
Q=Q^{\prime}
and
f_{Q}=0
for
Q\neq Q^{\prime}
, with
\emptyset\neq Q^{\prime}
a given face of
P
, one gets

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,0)=\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,0)=\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q^{\prime})\cap M}\varphi(m)\>,

with the reciprocity formula (for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
)

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,0)=\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,0)=(-1)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{{\ell}}Q^{\prime}\cap M}\varphi(m)\>,

and with constant term
E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(0,0)=\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(0,0)=(-1)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\varphi(0)
. For
Q^{\prime}=P
this is exactly the result of Brion–Vergne

[
10
, Prop.4.1]
. But their proof (based on
[
10
, Prop.3.8]
)
can easily be adapted to the case of a general face
Q^{\prime}
of
P
(using a translation
Q^{\prime}-m
as a full-dimensional lattice polytope in
Span(Q^{\prime}-m)
, together with the projection dual to the inclusion
Span(Q^{\prime}-m)_{\mathbb{C}}\hookrightarrow M_{\mathbb{C}}
). Note that
\{\delta_{Q,Q^{\prime}}|\>\emptyset\neq Q^{\prime}\preceq P\}
is a basis of the free
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-module of weight functions.
Finally for
\varphi=1
and
f_{Q}=\delta_{Q,Q^{\prime}}
we just get the classical Ehrhart polynomial of
{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q^{\prime})
:

E^{1}_{P,f}(\ell,0)=\widetilde{E}^{1}_{P,f}(\ell,0)=|{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q^{\prime})\cap M|\>,

with
|-|
denoting the cardinality of a finite set, together with the reciprocity formula (for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
)

E^{1}_{P,f}(-\ell,0)=\widetilde{E}^{1}_{P,f}(-\ell,0)=(-1)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})}\cdot|{{\ell}}Q^{\prime}\cap M|\>,

and constant term
E^{1}_{P,f}(0,0)=\widetilde{E}^{1}_{P,f}(0,0)=(-1)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})}
. In our weighted context it is more natural to start with

{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q^{\prime})
, whereas classically one starts with the Ehrhart polynomial of
Q^{\prime}
(obtained from these formulae by multiplication with

(-1)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})}
).

Consider now the weight vector given by Stanley’s
g
-polynomials

(1.6)

f_{Q}(y)=g_{Q^{\circ}}(-y)=:\widetilde{g}_{Q}(-y)

for the polar polytope of
a suitable
P^{\prime}=kP-m
(as before), with
g_{\emptyset}(-y)=\widetilde{g}_{P}(-y)=1
. In this setup, it was shown in
[
5
, Thm.1.3, Thm.2.6]
that the following
purity
property holds:

(1.7)

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,y)=(-y)^{\dim(P)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,1/y).

This property can be reformulated as

(1.8)

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,y)=(-y)^{\dim(P)}\cdot(-1)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,{1}/{y}).

Let us note here that our definition (
1.1
) for
E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
equals
G_{\varphi}(\ell,y)
from
[
5
, Formula (14)]
.
The constant term
h_{P^{\prime\circ}}(y):=E^{1}_{P,f}(0,-y)=\widetilde{E}^{1}_{P,f}(0,-y)
for
\varphi=1
is by definition just Stanley’s
h
-polynomial of
P^{\prime\circ}
, so that one recovers Stanley’s “master duality” theorem
[
36
, Thm.3.16.9]

h_{P^{\prime\circ}}(y)=y^{\dim(P^{\prime\circ})}\cdot h_{P^{\prime\circ}}(1/y)\>.

In the geometric terms of the next section,
h_{P^{\prime\circ}}(t^{2})
will be the intersection cohomology Poincaré polynomial of
X_{P}

(as already discussed in
[
27
]
),
so that this is boiling down to Poincaré duality for the intersection cohomology
IH^{*}(X_{P})
of
X_{P}
.
In the case when
P
is a simple polytope, the polar polytope
P^{\prime\circ}
is simplicial, so that
g_{Q^{\circ}}(-y)=1
, for all faces
Q
of
P
. In this case, as explained in
[
5
]
, the equality (
1.7
) implies the Dehn–Sommerville relations for
P^{\prime}
, boiling down to Poincaré duality for the usual cohomology
H^{*}(X_{P})=IH^{*}(X_{P})
of
X_{P}
, which is then a rational homology manifold.

Our main new result unifies the
reciprocity formulae
of Brion–Vergne

[
10
, Prop.4.1]
, extended to all faces
Q^{\prime}
of
P
as in Remark
1.3

(which implies (
1.2
) and (
1.3
)), as well as the
purity
results
(
1.7
) and (
1.8
), extending the latter also to all faces
Q
of
P
(related to the intersection cohomology of the orbit closures

V_{Q}
). This will be done in terms of the following duality group homomorphism
{\mathbb{D}}
on the free
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-module of weight functions.

Definition 1.4

(Duality for weight functions)
.

The
dual

{\mathbb{D}}(f)=\{{\mathbb{D}}(f)_{Q}\}
of the weight function
f=\{f_{Q}\}
is defined as

(1.9)

{\mathbb{D}}(f)_{Q}(y):=\sum_{Q\preceq E\preceq P}(1+y)^{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(E)-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)}\cdot(-y)^{-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(E)}\cdot f_{E}(1/y)\>.

The geometric meaning of this duality
{\mathbb{D}}
will be explained later on (see formula (
5.8
)),
and it is motivated by the analogy to the
Kazhdan–Lusztig theory
(see, e.g.,
[
29
, Sect.4.4]
,
[
2
, Thm.7.3.8, Thm.9.3.3]
, and also
[
22
, Ch.13]
,
[
38
]
).
If we define the standard duality
{\mathbb{D}}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]

via
{\mathbb{D}}p(y):=p(1/y)
for
p(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
, then the duality for weight functions has by its definition the
module property

(1.10)

{\mathbb{D}}(p\cdot f)={\mathbb{D}}(p)\cdot{\mathbb{D}}(f)

for
p=p(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
and
f=\{f_{Q}\}
a weight function.

Example 1.5

(
g
-functions of faces)
.

Let
Q^{\prime}
be a fixed face of
P
, and choose
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and
m\in M
so that
P^{\prime}=kQ^{\prime}-m
is a full-dimensional lattice polytope in its linear hull
Span(P^{\prime})
relative to the lattice
M^{\prime}:=Span(P^{\prime})\cap M
, containing zero in its relative interior. Let
P^{\prime\circ}
be the polar polytope of
P^{\prime}\subset M^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
and define

f_{Q,Q^{\prime}}(y)=\begin{cases}g_{Q^{\circ}}(-y)=:\widetilde{g}_{Q}(-y)\quad\text{ for $Q\preceq Q^{\prime}$,}\\
\quad\quad 0\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This is independent of the choice of
k
and
m
, with
f_{Q^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}(y)=1
, so that the weight functions
\{f_{Q,Q^{\prime}}\}
for all

\emptyset\neq Q^{\prime}\preceq P
are another basis of the free
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-module of weight functions. Moreover

(1.11)

{\mathbb{D}}(f_{Q,Q^{\prime}})=(-y)^{-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q^{\prime})}\cdot f_{Q,Q^{\prime}}\>,

which is equivalent to
[
5
, Equation (13)]
for the polytope
P^{\prime}
(as used for
Q^{\prime}=P
in the proof of their
purity
results).
In particular
{\mathbb{D}}
is an involution.

Theorem 1.6

(Reciprocity for duality)
.

Let
P
be a full-dimensional lattice polytope in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, with
\varphi:M_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
a homogenous polynomial
and
f=\{f_{Q}\}
a polynomial weight
f_{Q}(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
indexed by the non-empty faces
\emptyset\neq Q\preceq P
of
P
.
Then one has for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
:

(1.12)

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,y)=(-y)^{deg(\varphi)}\cdot E^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathbb{D}}(f)}(\ell,1/y)\>,

and similarly

(1.13)

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(-\ell,y)=(-1)^{deg(\varphi)}\cdot\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathbb{D}}(f)}(\ell,1/y)\>.

For a geometric statement and proof of the above theorem, see Theorem
6.13
. For
f=\{f_{Q,Q^{\prime}}\}
as in Example
1.5
we get the corresponding
purity
results extending
(
1.7
) and (
1.8
) from the case
Q^{\prime}=P
to a general face
Q^{\prime}
of
P
.
On the other hand, for
f=\{\delta_{Q,Q^{\prime}}\}
one gets

{\mathbb{D}}f_{Q}(y)=(1+y)^{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q^{\prime})-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)}\cdot(-y)^{-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q^{\prime})}

for
Q\preceq Q^{\prime}
and
{\mathbb{D}}f_{Q}(y)=0
otherwise, with

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)=(1+y)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q^{\prime})\cap M}\varphi(m)

and

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathbb{D}}(f)}(\ell,\frac{1}{y})=(1+y)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})}\cdot\left((-1)^{\dim(Q^{\prime})}\cdot\sum_{m\in{{\ell}}Q^{\prime}\cap M}\varphi(m)\right).

So in this case (
1.13
) is equivalent to the reciprocity formula of Brion–Vergne

[
10
, Prop.4.1]
, extended to all faces
Q^{\prime}
of
P
as in Remark
1.3
.

Remark 1.7
.

Note that formulae (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) are linear over
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
, so it is enough to prove them for a basis of the free
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-module of weight functions. The reciprocity theorem of Brion–Vergne is then equivalent to Theorem
1.6
. Similarly, Theorem
1.6
is also equivalent to the purity results (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) for the case of a general face of
P
.

As is well known (and recalled in Section
6
), the classical geometric origin of Ehrhart theory and the reciprocity formula of Brion–Vergne

[
10
, Prop.4.1]
is the torus-equivariant cohomology of the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P})
associated to the ample Cartier divisor
D_{P}

on the projective toric variety
X_{P}
, namely,

(1.14)

\Gamma(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))=\bigoplus_{m\in P\cap M}{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\chi^{m}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[M],

where
\chi^{m}
denotes the character defined by
m\in M
, and
{\mathbb{C}}[M]
is the coordinate ring of the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
,
with all higher cohomology trivial by the Demazure vanishing theorem (e.g., see
[
13
, Prop.4.3.3, Thm.9.2.3]
).
Moreover, one has by the Batyrev-Borisov vanishing theorem (e.g., see
[
13
, Prop.9.2.7]
) that

(1.15)

H^{n}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-D_{P}))=\bigoplus_{m\in{\rm Int}(P)\cap M}{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\chi^{-m}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[M],

with
{\rm Int}({P})
the interior of
P
, and all other cohomology groups vanishing.
Since
X_{\ell P}=X_{P}
and
D_{\ell P}=\ell D_{P}
for a positive integer
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, one gets for the corresponding
torus-equivariant Euler characteristics
:

(1.16)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\ell D_{P}))&=\sum_{m\in\ell P\cap M}\chi^{-m}\quad\text{and}\\
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-\ell D_{P}))&=(-1)^{n}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Int}(\ell P)\cap M}\chi^{m}\>.\end{split}

Equivariant Serre duality then also gives

(1.17)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-\ell D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}})&=(-1)^{n}\cdot\sum_{m\in\ell P\cap M}\chi^{m}\quad\text{and}\\
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\ell D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}})&=\sum_{m\in{\rm Int}(\ell P)\cap M}\chi^{-m}\>,\end{split}

with
\omega_{X_{P}}=\widehat{\Omega}^{\dim(P)}_{X_{P}}
the equivariant dualizing and (Zariski) canonical sheaf of
X_{P}
.
Here we choose the convention that the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}[M]
as follows: if
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
and
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[M]
, then
t\cdot f\in{\mathbb{C}}[M]
is given by
p\mapsto f(t^{-1}\cdot p)
, for
p\in{\mathbb{T}}
(see
[
13
, pag.18]
). In particular,
t\cdot\chi^{m}=\chi^{m}(t^{-1})\chi^{m}

(explaing in (
1.16
) the switch from
\chi^{m}
to its dual character
\chi^{-m}
).
If we translate
P
to
P^{\prime}=P-m_{0}
for a lattice point
m_{0}\in M
, with
X_{P^{\prime}}=X_{P}
, then

D_{P^{\prime}}=D_{P}+div(\chi^{m_{0}}),\quad\text{with}\quad{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\ell D_{P^{\prime}})=\chi^{\ell m_{0}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\ell D_{P})\>.

The multiplication by
\chi^{\ell m_{0}}
of the equivariant Euler characteristics on the left hand side above therefore fits on the right hand side with the translation from
P
to
P^{\prime}
via
\chi^{m_{0}}\cdot\chi^{m}=\chi^{m_{0}+m}
.
For a face
Q
of
P
and the orbit closure
V_{Q}\subset X_{P}
this implies then similar formulae as above for

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(V_{Q},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\pm\ell D_{P})|_{V_{Q}})\quad\text{ and}\quad\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(V_{Q},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\pm\ell D_{P})|_{V_{Q}})\otimes\omega_{V_{Q}})\>,

by just changing
P
to
Q
,
{\rm Int}(-)
to
{\rm Relint}(-)
, and
n
to
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)
.

These
equivariant Euler characteristics
can then be used for the study of the Ehrhart polynomials of

\sum_{m\in{{\ell}}Q\cap M}\varphi(m)\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\varphi(m)\>,

with the reciprocity related to
equivariant Serre duality
. Forgetting the additional equivariant structure corresponds to the use of

\varphi=1
, giving back the classical Ehrhart polynomials for the number of lattice points

|{{\ell}}Q\cap M|\quad\text{and}\quad|{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M|\>.

The geometric meaning of the weight functions
f_{Q}(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
in our weighted Ehrhart polynomials is of a different nature,
related to the topology of the toric variety
X_{P}
, e.g., the intersection cohomology
IH^{*}(X_{P})
of
X_{P}
,
with the reciprocity related to Poincaré duality for the intersection cohomology. The theory of
(torus-equivariant) mixed Hodge modules
on the toric variety
X_{P}
gives a very natural connection between these two points of view. Their underlying perverse sheaves are related to intersection cohomology and the topology of the toric variety
X_{P}
, whereas the graded pieces of the filtered de Rham complex of their
underlying filtered D-modules give interesting complexes of coherent
{\mathcal{O}}
-sheaves on
X_{P}
.
Moreover the
duality
of mixed Hodge modules fits with the Verdier duality of perverse sheaves (like intersection cohomology complexes), as well with the Grothendieck- and (in the toric context also) Serre-duality of coherent
{\mathcal{O}}
-sheaves.
Finally, the stalk or global cohomology (with compact support) of a mixed Hodge module carry a mixed Hodge structure.
Here we only take into account their Hodge filtrations, with the corresponding
\chi_{y}
-genus (or Hodge-filtered Euler characteristic) of the stalks giving
our weight functions
f_{Q}(y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
. In
[
27
]
we already studied the weighted Ehrhart polynomials for the special case

\varphi=1
via the theory of mixed Hodge modules on the toric variety
X_{P}
in the non-equivariant context,
using their
Hirzebruch classes
in the homology of
X_{P}
. Here we now develop the corresponding
much reacher torus-equivariant context, which allows the study of the more general weighted Ehrhart polynomials with respect to any homogenous polynomial
\varphi
. Here it is more natural to work with the K-theoretic
equivariant Chern classes
of the Hodge modules. Finally, also the explicit
duality for weight functions
as discussed before, is new.
As we explain next, it naturally fits with the duality of mixed Hodge modules on the toric variety
X_{P}
.

1.3.
Geometric viewpoint on generalized weighted Ehrhart theory

We give a geometric interpretation and proofs of the results mentioned before for any homogeneous polynomial function
\varphi
and any weight vector
f
via the theory of
equivariant
mixed Hodge modules on a projective toric variety and their
K
-theoretic equivariant characteristic classes. Moreover, using the equivariant intersection Hodge module
{IC}^{H}_{X_{P}}
and its purity, we give a geometric proof of formula (
1.7
) and its counterpart for orbit closures
V_{Q}\subset X:=X_{P}
.

1.3.1.
Geometric realization of weight vectors and duality

As already indicated in
[
27
]
, we identify the weight vectors
\{f_{Q}\}
, seen as
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-valued functions on the set of faces
Q
of
P
, with the
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-valued constructible functions on
X_{P}
, via

\{f_{Q}\}\mapsto\sum_{Q\preceq P}1_{O_{Q}}\cdot f_{Q}\in F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}].

Note that
F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}]
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-module freely generated by the
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant constructible functions
1_{O_{Q}}\in F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\subset F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}]
, with
1_{O_{Q^{\prime}}}
corresponding to the weight function
\{\delta_{Q,Q^{\prime}}\}
. In what follows, we adapt the formulations from
[
27
]
in motivic and Hodge-theoretic terms to the equivariant context.

There is a tautological (injective) homomorphism

\tau^{\mathbb{T}}:F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}]\longrightarrow K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(var/X)[{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}]

to the equivariant Grothendieck group of
{\mathbb{T}}
-varieties over
X
, localized at the Lefschetz motive
{\mathbb{L}}=[{\mathbb{C}}\to pt]
(with the trivial
{\mathbb{T}}
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}
), given by

1_{O_{Q}}\cdot f_{Q}(y)\mapsto[O_{Q}\hookrightarrow X]\cdot f_{Q}(-{\mathbb{L}}).

Here,
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(var/X)
is a
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(var/pt)
-module via the identification
X\times pt\simeq X
.

In order to identify the equivariant intersection cohomology complex and duality we need to go one step further, and work with
the abelian category
{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
of
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant mixed Hodge modules on
X=X_{P}
. For technical reasons, here we need to choose a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant closed embedding
i:X\hookrightarrow Z
into an ambient smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety
Z
,
and work with the Grothendieck group
K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(X))=\,K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
of equivariant mixed Hodge modules on
Z
with support on
X
(so that the corresponding Grothendieck calculus is equivariantly available, see
[
1
]
. The main results will be independent of the choice of this embedding
X\hookrightarrow Z
). There is a canonical homomorphism

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(var/X)[{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}]\to K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(X))=\,K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))

such that

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}([O_{Q}\hookrightarrow X]\cdot f_{Q}(-{\mathbb{L}}))=[(j_{Q})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}}]\cdot f_{Q}(-u),

with
j_{Q}:O_{Q}\hookrightarrow X\hookrightarrow Z
the orbit inclusion in the ambient smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety
Z
, and
{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}}
denoting the equivariant constant Hodge module on the orbit
O_{Q}
.
Here,

u=[{\mathbb{Q}}(-1)]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt))\simeq K_{0}({\rm MHM}(pt))\simeq K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p}),

and the module structure on
K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
is induced from the exterior product for the identification
Z\simeq pt\times Z
. Also,
{\rm MHS}^{p}
is the abelian category of graded polarizable
{\mathbb{Q}}
-mixed Hodge structures, with
{\mathbb{Q}}(-1)
the corresponding Tate Hodge structure of weight two.

The ambient Grothendieck groups
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(var/X)[{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}]
and
K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
are only used formally in the background. For our applications to the toric context, we only need to work with the (sub)groups

(1.18)

\{f_{Q}\}\longleftrightarrow F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}]\longleftrightarrow\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}])\longleftrightarrow\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}\left(\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}])\right)

realizing our weight vectors in the language of constructible functions, motivic, and mixed Hodge module contexts, respectively. These identifications are linear over the following ring coefficient isomorphisms, with
-y={\mathbb{L}}=u
:

{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]\longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]\longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{L}}^{\pm 1}]\longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[u^{\pm 1}],

identifying the coordinate basis elements as follows:

e_{Q^{\prime}}:=\{\delta_{Q,Q^{\prime}}\}\longleftrightarrow 1_{O_{Q^{\prime}}}\longleftrightarrow[O_{Q^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow X]\longleftrightarrow[(j_{Q^{\prime}})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q^{\prime}}}]\>.

By their definitions, all groups homomorphisms in (
1.18
) are surjective. In our geometric applications, we work in the group

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}\left(\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\right)
, recovering
the corresponding weight vector
\{f_{Q}\}
via

f_{Q}(y):=\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])

as the Hodge
\chi_{y}
-polynomial (see (
2.3
) for a definition) of the stalk class
i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}]
, with
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
, and
i_{x_{Q}}:\{x_{Q}\}\hookrightarrow Z
the inclusion (in the ambient smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety) of a point
x_{Q}
chosen in the orbit
O_{Q}
. Then our aim is to show that many invariants of
[{\mathcal{M}}]
depend only of this choice of a weight function.

Specific to the toric context is the fact that the stabilizers of the torus action are connected, so that, using
[
38
, Lem.1.2]
, any class
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
can be decomposed as (see Corollary
3.3
)

(1.19)

\begin{split}[{\mathcal{M}}]&=\sum_{Q\preceq P}\,\big[(j_{Q})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}_{O_{Q}}^{H}\big]\cdot i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}],\end{split}

and the stalk classes do not see the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action, i.e.,

i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt))\simeq K_{0}({\rm MHM}(pt))\simeq K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p}).

Since the composed map

{\mathbb{Z}}[u^{\pm 1}]\longrightarrow K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})\overset{\chi_{y}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]

is the isomorphism given by
u\mapsto-y
, we get the injectivity of the composition

F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}]\overset{\tau^{\mathbb{T}}}{\longrightarrow}K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(var/X)[{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}]\overset{\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}}{\longrightarrow}K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z)).

In this way we also see that any weight vector
f
is realized by the stalk class of a certain equivariant mixed Hodge module complex via
f_{Q}(y):=\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])
.

Even more specific to the toric context is that the submodule
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}\left(\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}])\right)\subset K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
is preserved under the duality
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}
of torus-equivariant mixed Hodge modules on
Z
(with support in
X
), since each torus orbit has a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant affine neighborhood of product type.
Note that this duality has the following module property:
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}(u\cdot-)=u^{-1}\cdot{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}(-)
.
As we will see, under the identifications above, this corresponds to our duality involution
{\mathbb{D}}
on the
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
-module of weight functions.

Let
IC^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}\in{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z)
be the equivariant intersection cohomology Hodge module of the orbit closure

V_{Q^{\prime}}\subset X
corresponding to the face
Q^{\prime}
of
P
, and
{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}:=IC^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}[-n^{\prime}]
with
n^{\prime}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{Q^{\prime}})=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q^{\prime})
.
Since
{IC}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}
is pure of weight
n^{\prime}
, we get that

{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}\simeq{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}[2n^{\prime}](n^{\prime})\>.

This implies the following key purity property:

(1.20)

{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}[{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}]=u^{-n^{\prime}}\cdot[{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}]\in\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}\left(\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}])\right)\subset K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))\>.

As already explained in the non-equivariant context of
[
27
]
(for
Q^{\prime}=P
), the weight vector associated to
[{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}]
is the collection
f_{Q,Q^{\prime}}
of Stanley’s
g
-functions as in Example
1.5
, i.e.,
\widetilde{g}_{Q}(-y)=\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q^{\prime}}}])

for all
Q\preceq Q^{\prime}
(cf. also
[
7
,
18
,
15
,
33
]
). So if we use the combinatorial proof of the duality property (
1.11
)
of these
f_{Q,Q^{\prime}}
, then this already implies the identification of the dualites
{\mathbb{D}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}
. We will give later on (see (
5.8
)) another direct geometric proof for the identification of these dualities, so that (
1.20
) will imply the key duality property (
1.11
).

1.3.2.
Twisted equivariant Hodge–Chern classes and generalized weighted Ehrhart theory

We can now explain our geometric interpretation of the generalized weighted Ehrhart theory introduced in Subsection
1.2
, in terms of suitable twists (by the line bundle of the ample Cartier divisor
D_{P}
of
P
) of the equivariant Hodge–Chern class transformation

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}:K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))\longrightarrow K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)[y^{\pm 1}]\simeq K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)[y^{\pm 1}],

generalizing the corresponding non-equivariant Hodge–Chern classes of
[
8
,
34
]
.
This equivariant Hodge–Chern transformation is defined in terms of the equivariant filtered De Rham complex of
{\mathcal{M}}\in{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z)
, which equivariantly can only be defined on the ambient smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety
Z
. Here,
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)\simeq K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)
is the Grothendieck group of
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant coherent sheaves on (
Z
with set-theoretic support in)
X
.

The equivariant Hodge–Chern class transformation
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
commutes with cross product
\boxtimes
for the identification
Z\times pt\simeq Z
, fitting with the module structure over
K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})
and the ring homomorphism

\chi_{y}:K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})\to{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
.
Moreover, it is functorial for
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant morphisms
f\colon(Z,X)\to(Z^{\prime},X^{\prime})
of pairs, with
Z,Z^{\prime}
smooth complex algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-varieties, and
f:Z\to Z^{\prime}
restricting to a
proper
morphism
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
of closed
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant subvarieties.
Especially for
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
an isomorphism, this shows that
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
doesn’t depend on the choice of the embedding

X\hookrightarrow Z
.
In this paper, the functoriality is used for the closed inclusions
k_{Q}:V_{Q}\hookrightarrow Z
of the closure of the
{\mathbb{T}}
-orbits
O_{Q}
into the ambient smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety
Z
, and their compositions with toric resolutions of these orbit closures, as well as for taking degrees of these classes (i.e., pushing down to a point space) in case
X
is compact.
These properties are then used to prove the following basic identity (see (
5.9
)):

(1.21)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{Q})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}}\big])=(1+y)^{\dim(O_{Q})}\cdot(k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{V_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}},

with
\omega_{V_{Q}}
the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant canonical (and dualizing) sheaf of the orbit closure
V_{Q}
.

As explained in
[
16
, Sect.3]
, the equivariant Hodge–Chern class transformation
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
commutes with equivariant duality for Grothendieck classes of equivariant mixed Hodge modules on
Z
with support in
X
, which are in the image of
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}

(or, most important for us, contained in
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}\left(\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}])\right)
), i.e., on such elements one has

(1.22)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}\circ{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}={\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}\circ\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y},

Here,
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}
is induced from the exact duality functor of equivariant mixed Hodge modules (on
Z
, with support on
X
).
For
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}
, one can use either the equivariant Grothendieck duality on the Grothendieck group
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)
induced from Grothendieck duality on
Z
(remembering the support in
X
), or the equivariant Grothendieck duality on the Grothendieck group
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)
induced from Grothendieck duality on
X
(see
[
25
]
for the foundations of equivariant Grothendieck duality). As in the nonequivariant context,
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}

is extended to
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)[y^{\pm 1}]
by
y\mapsto 1/y
.
With the above notations, this duality property of
\hbox{\rm DR}_{y}^{\mathbb{T}}
implies our second basic formula (cf. (
5.15
)):

(1.23)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{Q})_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}}\big])=(1+y)^{\dim(O_{Q})}\cdot(k_{Q})_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\>.

Equivalently,

(1.24)

\begin{split}\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}\left({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}\big[(j_{Q})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}}\big]\right)&=(-y)^{-\dim(O_{Q})}\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(O_{Q})}\cdot(k_{Q})_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\\
&=(-1)^{\dim(O_{Q})}\cdot(1+1/y)^{\dim(O_{Q})}\cdot(k_{Q})_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}},\end{split}

since

{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}\left((j_{Q})_{!}({\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}})\right)\simeq(j_{Q})_{*}({\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}})[2\dim(O_{Q})](\dim(O_{Q}))\>.

This allows us to calculate
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}\left({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}(-)\right)
without knowing the stalk information or weight function
of
\big[(j_{Q})_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{Q}}\big]
.

Let now
D^{\prime}
be a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Cartier divisor on
X=X_{P}
, e.g.,
D^{\prime}=\ell D=\ell D_{P}
for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
be fixed.
Then

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D^{\prime}):=\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D^{\prime})]\in K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}],

where we extend the
K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
-module structure of
K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
induced from the tensor product linearly over
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
.
Here
K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
is the Grothendieck group of
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant vector bundles (or their coherent locally free sheaves of sections) on
X
. For
X
compact and

a_{X}:X\to pt
the constant map as above, we define the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant Hodge polynomial of
(X,D^{\prime},[{\mathcal{M}}])
as:

(1.25)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,D^{\prime};[{\mathcal{M}}])&:=(a_{X})_{*}\left(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D^{\prime})\right)\in K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt)[y^{\pm 1}]=K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)[y^{\pm 1}]\\
&=:\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X;\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D^{\prime}))\in{\mathbb{Z}}[M][y^{\pm 1}],\end{split}

which only depends on the Grothendieck class
[{\mathcal{M}}]
, as well as on
D^{\prime}
.
Only for
D^{\prime}=0
it just depends on
[{\mathcal{M}}]
.
In the second equality of the above definition, we use the isomorphism
K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M]
induced by the eigenspace decomposition of a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{T}}
-representation
by characters
\chi^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[M]
(for
m\in M
an element in the character lattice of
{\mathbb{T}}
), see (
4.2
).

With these notations, we can now formulate the second main result of this paper (see Theorems
6.4
,
6.8
,
5.2
):

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X=X_{P}
be the projective toric variety with ample Cartier divisor
D=D_{P}
associated to a full-dimensional lattice polytope
P\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and let
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Let
Z
be an ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold containing
X
as a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant subvariety.
For any face
Q
of
P
, let
x_{Q}\in O_{Q}
be a point in the orbit
O_{Q}
of
X
, with inclusion map
i_{x_{Q}}:\{x_{Q}\}\hookrightarrow Z
, and set
f_{Q}(y)=\chi_{y}(i^{*}_{x_{Q}}[{\mathcal{M}}])
, with
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))

the (virtual difference) Grothendieck class
of a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant mixed Hodge module on
Z
with support in
X
. Then we have:

(a)

(1.26)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])&=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\chi^{-m},\end{split}

with
{\rm Relint}
denoting the relative interior of a face.

(b)

(1.27)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,-\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])&=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\ell}Q\cap M}\chi^{m},\end{split}

(c)

(1.28)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,0\cdot D;[{\mathcal{M}}])&=\sum_{Q\preceq P}\left(f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\right)\cdot\chi^{0}.\end{split}

Here, for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
\ell D_{P}=D_{\ell P}
is the ample Cartier divisor corresponding to the dilated polytope
\ell P
, with the same toric variety
X_{\ell P}=X_{P}
.

All the formulae of the above theorem rely on the decomposition formula (
1.19
), together with well-known character decompositions of

H^{*}(X;{\mathcal{O}}(\pm\ell D))
and
H^{*}(X;{\mathcal{O}}(\pm\ell D)\otimes\omega_{X})
, for any integer
\ell
, as in the classical work of Danilov
[
14
]

mentioned in (
1.14
) and (
1.15
). More precisely, we use the correspondinging
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant Euler characteristics
\chi^{{\mathbb{T}}}
as given in (
1.16
) and (
1.17
), and also the mentioned versions for

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(V_{Q},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\pm\ell D_{P})|_{V_{Q}})\quad\text{ and}\quad\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(V_{Q},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\pm\ell D_{P})|_{V_{Q}})\otimes\omega_{V_{Q}})

for
Q
a face of
P
.
In addition, we also use the two basic identities (
1.21
) and (
1.23
).

Furthermore, using the duality property (
1.22
) for
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
(as in Lemma
6.14
), together with equivariant Serre duality, and combining this with
(b)
, we obtain for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

the following
main duality formula
for the equivariant Hodge polynomial:

(1.29)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}[{\mathcal{M}}])=\chi^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{1/y}(X,-\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])|_{m\mapsto-m}.

Here, the involution
m\mapsto-m
of
{\mathbb{Z}}[M]
corresponds to the involution
(-)^{\vee}
on
K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
induced from duality of equivariant vector bundles on a point space, i.e., finite dimensional representation spaces of
{\mathbb{T}}
.
Together with the purity property (
1.20
), the duality formula (
1.29
) applied to
{\mathcal{M}}={IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q}}
for

Q
an
n^{\prime}
-dimensional face of

yields

(1.30)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,-\ell D;[{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q}}])=(-y)^{n^{\prime}}\cdot\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{1/y}(X,\ell D;[{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q}}])|_{m\mapsto-m}\>.

Let us finally explain the role of the chosen homogeneous polynomial function
\varphi:M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
for proving the properties of the generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomials
E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
and, resp.,
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
, stated in Subsection
1.2
. The homogeneous polynomial
\varphi
defines group homomorphisms

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}:K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\ \ \ E^{\varphi}:K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}[y]

via
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}(\chi^{m}):=\varphi(-m)
, resp.,

{E}^{\varphi}(\chi^{m}):=\varphi(-(1+y)\cdot m)
.
Since
\varphi
is homogeneous, the duality involution
m\mapsto-m
on the left-hand side corresponds to the multiplication by
(-1)^{\deg(\varphi)}
on the right-hand side (with
y\mapsto y
).
Linearly extending
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi},E^{\varphi}
over Laurent polynomials in

, we then have that

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)=\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}\big(\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])\big)

and

{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)={E}^{\varphi}\big(\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])\big)

with
f_{Q}(y)=\chi_{y}(i^{*}_{x_{Q}}[{\mathcal{M}}])
.
The duality involution
m\mapsto-m
and
y\mapsto 1/y
on the left-hand side of the group homomorphisms

(1.31)

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}:K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)[y^{\pm 1}]\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M][y^{\pm 1}]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm 1}],\ \ \ E^{\varphi}:K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm 1}]

corresponds to first switching

and then multiplying by
(-1)^{\deg(\varphi)}
or, resp., by
(-y)^{\deg(\varphi)}
on the right-hand side.

Then the formulae for
\chi_{y}^{\mathbb{T}}(X,\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])
stated in this subsection translate via
E^{\varphi}
and
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}
into the corresponding properties for the generalized
weighted Ehrhart polynomials
E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
and, resp.,
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
, as stated in Subsection
1.2
(see also Section
6
).

To explain geometrically the polynomial behavior in
\ell
of
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
, we have to go from
K
-theory to equivariant homology via the equivariant Todd class transformation, using in addition equivariant localization at the torus fixed points (as explained in the Appendix).

{\mathbb{T}}=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
-action (e.g., the toric variety

associated to a full-dimensional lattice polytope

{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant embedding of

.

6

see
[

.
Then the abelian category

of
{\mathbb{T}}

[

Let

which is even a

In
[
1

, Achar introduced a triangulated category

{\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
. The actual definition of

is not needed in this paper, we refer to
[

]
for more details on its construction. However, we note that

is only defined for a smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety

, and it is

the derived category of
{\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
. So one has a canonical isomorphism of Grothendieck groups

K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(Z))\simeq K_{0}(D_{\rm MHM}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z))\>.

The category
D_{\rm MHM}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
has the same
6
-functor formalism as developed for

in
[
31
]
, e.g., for a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant morphism

of smooth

{\mathbb{T}}
-varieties on has

(2.2)

f_{*}\circ{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}\simeq{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z^{\prime}}\circ f_{!}\quad\text{and}\quad f^{*}\circ{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z^{\prime}}\simeq{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}\circ f^{!}

for the corresponding equivariant duality functors
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}
and

All of these functors, as well as the t-structure, commute with a corresponding t-exact forgetful functor
{\rm For}:D_{\rm MHM}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)\to D^{b}{\rm MHM}(Z)
.

Let
D_{\rm MHM,X}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)\subset D_{\rm MHM}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
be the full triangulated subcategory
of equivariant elements, whose cohomology objects are supported on

(after forgetting the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action), i.e., they belong to

{\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{X}(Z)
. By this definition, the t-structure above restricts to a t-structure on

,
with heart
{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z)
as well as
commuting with

{\rm For}
, and inducing a canonical isomorphism of Grothendieck groups
(and this is all what we need)

K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(X))\simeq K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))\simeq K_{0}(D_{\rm MHM,X}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z))\>.

We next discuss a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant version of the Hodge–Chern
class of
X
, see also
[
16
]
. In the non-equivariant case, this was initially introduced in
[
8
]
, but see also
[
34
]
for a definition and study via mixed Hodge modules.

Let
X
be as before a closed
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant subvariety of a complex algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
, and let
{\mathcal{M}}\in{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z)
be a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant mixed Hodge module on
Z
with support in
X
, e.g.,
{\mathcal{M}}=IC_{X}^{H}
the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant intersection cohomology module (defined only for
X
pure-dimensional, already as an element in
{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
, by the equivariant intermediate extension of the constant Hodge module from the regular part of
X
, first to
X
and then to the ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety
Z
).
For
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
consider

\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}({\mathcal{M}})
of the underlying algebraic left
{\mathcal{D}}_{Z}
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
with its integrable connection

\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}({\mathcal{M}})=[{\mathcal{M}}\overset{\nabla}{\longrightarrow}{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}}\Omega^{1}_{Z}\overset{\nabla}{\longrightarrow}\cdots\overset{\nabla}{\longrightarrow}{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}}\Omega^{\dim(Z)}_{Z}]

with
{\mathcal{M}}
in degree

, filtered by

F_{p}\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}({\mathcal{M}})=[F_{p}{\mathcal{M}}\overset{\nabla}{\longrightarrow}F_{p+1}{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}}\Omega^{1}_{Z}\overset{\nabla}{\longrightarrow}\cdots\overset{\nabla}{\longrightarrow}F_{p+\dim(Z)}{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}}\Omega^{\dim(Z)}_{Z}].

The complex
Gr^{F}_{p}\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}({\mathcal{M}})
associated to the above filtered de Rham complex
\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}({\mathcal{M}})
is a bounded complex of
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant coherent sheaves on

with support in
X
. (We use the same symbol for both the mixed Hodge module and the underlying filtered (left)

-module.) Moreover,

for all but finitely many integers

and
{\mathcal{M}}

Remark 2.1
.

It follows from
[
30
, Lem.3.2.6]
that the complex
Gr^{F}_{p}\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}({\mathcal{M}})
is a bounded complex of (equivariant) coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules (not just set-theoretically supported on
X
), i.e., these (equivariant) coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules are annihilated by the ideal sheaf
{\mathcal{I}}_{X}
of
X
in
Z
. ∎

The transformation
Gr^{F}_{p}\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}
induces a corresponding map on Grothendieck groups since morphisms of (equivariant) mixed Hodge modules are strict with respect to the Hodge filtration. Let (as before)
D_{{\rm MHM},X}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
be the full subcategory of
D_{{\rm MHM}}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
consisting of equivariant elements with support in
X
.
For such an equivariant object
{\mathcal{M}}\in D_{{\rm MHM},X}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
on
Z
with support in
X
, we perform the above construction for each cohomology object of
{\mathcal{M}}
. So one gets induced Grothendieck group transformations

Gr^{F}_{p}\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}:K_{0}(D_{{\rm MHM},X}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z))\simeq K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))\longrightarrow K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)\simeq K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X),

where
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)\simeq K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)
is the Grothendieck group of
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant coherent sheaves on (
Z
with set-theoretical support in)
X
. The fact that the transformation
Gr^{F}_{p}\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}
takes values in
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)
follows directly from Remark
2.1
. Alternatively, using the ambient variety
Z
, this can also be deduced from
the identification

i_{*}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)\simeq K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)

induced by the pushforward
i_{*}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)\to K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)
for the closed embedding
i:X\hookrightarrow Z
. Indeed, the right inverse of

is
i^{*}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)\to K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)
, which is induced from the derived pullback (i.e., alternating sum of equivariant tor-sheaves), since

is of finite tor-dimension (as

is smooth). So

is injective. Moreover, if
{\mathcal{F}}
is an equivariant coherent sheaf on
Z
supported on
X
, then
{\mathcal{F}}
is annihilated by a finite power of the ideal sheaf
{\mathcal{I}}_{X}
of
X
, and

[{\mathcal{F}}]=\sum_{i\geq 0}[{\mathcal{I}}_{X}^{i}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}/{{\mathcal{I}}_{X}^{i+1}}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}]\in i_{*}K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)\subset K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z),

proving the surjectivity of

.

We can now make the following definition.

Definition 2.2

(Equivariant Hodge–Chern transformation)
.

The
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant Hodge–Chern class transformation
of a complex algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety
X
equivariantly embedded in an ambient complex algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
is defined as:

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}:K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))\longrightarrow K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)[y^{\pm 1}]

\begin{split}\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])&:=\sum_{p}\,\big[Gr^{F}_{-p}\hbox{\rm DR}_{Z}([{\mathcal{M}}])\big]_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot(-y)^{p}.\end{split}

Remark 2.3
.

By definition,
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])
depends only on the Grothendieck class
[{\mathcal{M}}]
, and not on the choice of a representative
{\mathcal{M}}\in D^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}_{{\rm MHM},X}(Z)
for this class. Forgetting the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action, one recovers by definition the Hodge–Chern class transformation
\hbox{\rm DR}_{y}
from
[
8
,
34
]
. ∎

Proposition 2.4

(Functoriality of
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
)
.

Let
f\colon(Z,X)\to(Z^{\prime},X^{\prime})
be a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant morphism of smooth complex algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-varieties
f:Z\to Z^{\prime}
restricting to a proper morphism
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
of closed
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant subvarieties. Then

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}\circ f_{*}=f_{*}\circ\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}:K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))\to K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})[y^{\pm 1}].

Proof.

This can be done exactly as explained in
[
16
, Section 3]
. First, one reduces by induction on the dimension of
X
to the case when
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is projective,
by using for
X
the equivariant Chow-Lemma of
[
37
, Thm.2]
, together with a corresponding (graph-) embedding into a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant smooth quasi-projective variety, which then exists by
[
37
, Thm.1]
. For a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant morphism

f\colon(Z,X)\to(Z^{\prime},X^{\prime})
with
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
projective one uses then in addition a corresponding
strictness
result of
M. Saito
[
31
, Thm.2.14]
for the underlying (non-equivariant) higher direct image mixed Hodge modules,
proved in
[
31
, Thm.2.14]
directly for a projective morphism
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
of possible singular varieties.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Under the identifications
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)\simeq K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)
and
K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})\simeq K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X^{\prime})
, the pushforward
f_{*}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X}(Z)\to K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0,X^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})
corresponds to the usual pushforward
f_{*}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)\to K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X^{\prime})
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The transformation
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
is (in the following sense) independent of the choice of an
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant embedding of
X
in a smooth ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
. In the context of Proposition
2.4
, with

an isomorphism, the pushforward
f_{*}\colon K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))\to K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime}))
is an isomorphism
fitting with the identification (

). In particular, the transformation
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
commutes with this isomorphism and the isomorphism
f_{*}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)\to K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X^{\prime})
, as in the above remark. If we now consider two equivariant embeddings

and

, the above observation can be applied to the diagonal embedding

(with the diagonal
{\mathbb{T}}
-action) and the projections on the two factors to show that

is (up to isomorphism) independent of the choice of an equivariant embedding of
X
in a smooth ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold. ∎

Example 2.7

(Degree of
\hbox{\rm DR}_{y}^{\mathbb{T}}
)
.

Let us illustrate the definition of the equivariant Hodge–Chern transformation when
X=Z=pt
is a point space. In this case, since
{\mathbb{T}}
is connected, we get by
[

{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt)={\rm MHM}(pt)={\rm MHS}^{p},

where
{\rm MHS}^{p}
is the category of graded-polarizable mixed
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Hodge

-module filtration to a decreasing Hodge filtration so that on a point space this identification gives

By definition, for
{\mathcal{M}}\in D_{\rm MHM}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(pt)
we have that

[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}(D_{\rm MHM}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(pt))=K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt))=K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p}),

so
DR^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])

does not see the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action, i.e.,

(2.3)

DR^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])=\sum_{j,p}\,(-1)^{j}\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\text{\rm Gr}_{F}^{p}(H^{j}({\rm For}({\mathcal{M}}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}})\,(-y)^{p}=:\chi_{y}([{\rm For}({\mathcal{M}})])

is the corresponding Hodge
\chi_{y}
-polynomial, defining a ring homomorphism

\chi_{y}:K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})\longrightarrow K_{0}(pt)[y^{\pm 1}]={\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]\hookrightarrow K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(pt)[y^{\pm 1}].

E.g., for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\chi_{y}({\mathbb{Q}}(n))=(-y)^{-n}
, where
{\mathbb{Q}}(n)
denotes the Tate Hodge structure of weight
-2n
on the vector space
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Similarly,

\chi_{y}(X):=\chi_{y}([H^{\bullet}_{c}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})])

is the (compactly supported) Hodge polynomial of
X
.
E.g.,
\chi_{y}(({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n})=(-y-1)^{n}
.

By functoriality, if
X
is compact, and
a_{Z}:Z\to pt
is the constant map to a point, we have for

{\mathcal{M}}\in D^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}_{{\rm MHM},X}(Z)
and
(a_{Z})_{*}{\mathcal{M}}\in D_{\rm MHM}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(pt)
:

(2.4)

\chi_{y}(X;[{\mathcal{M}}]):=\chi_{y}([(a_{Z})_{*}{\mathcal{M}}])=\chi_{y}([H^{\bullet}(X;{\rm For}({\mathcal{M}}))])

is the degree of the (equivariant) Hodge–Chern class of
[{\rm For}({\mathcal{M}})]
(resp.
[{\mathcal{M}}]
) depending only on
[{\rm For}({\mathcal{M}})]
. ∎

Definition 2.8
.

Let

be the subring of

Remark 2.10

Note that for

in the ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold

(up to non-canonical isomorphism) as the cone of the adjunction map

(2.7)

{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{X}:=Cone(j_{!}j^{*}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{Z}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{Z})\in D^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}_{{\rm{\rm MHM}},X}(Z).

Let us now relate the transformation
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}
to its motivic counterpart, as used e.g., in

{\mathbb{T}}

{\mathbb{T}}

where

is a smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety. For singular

(with proper support) for morphisms of pairs

3.
Computations via stratifications

Let us now indicate how to compute the equivariant Hodge–Chern classes in terms of a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant stratification given by finitely many
{\mathbb{T}}
-orbits.

Let as before
X
be a closed
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant algebraic subvariety of an algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
, with inclusion map
i:X\hookrightarrow Z
. Assume that
X
has only finitely many
{\mathbb{T}}
-orbits, with the strata
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
given by these orbits.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}\in D^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}_{{\rm MHM},X}(Z)
be an equivariant element on
Z
with support in
X
.
Then the underlying bounded constructible complex

K:={\it{rat}}({\rm For}({\mathcal{M}}))\in D^{b}_{c}(Z;{\mathbb{Q}})

is supported on
X
and constructible with respect to this orbit stratification, i.e.,
the sheaves
L_{S,\ell}:={\mathcal{H}}^{\ell}K|_{S}
are local systems on
S
for any
S
and
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
by the equivariant structure
(see, e.g.,
[
2
, Proof of Prop.6.2.13]
).
Let
U=Z\setminus X
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}={\mathcal{S}}_{X}\cup\{U\}
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}
is an algebraic stratification of
Z
satisfying the above properties for each
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
, together with
L_{U,\ell}:={\mathcal{H}}^{\ell}K|_{U}\simeq 0
.
For each
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
, consider the inclusions

j_{S,Z}:S\overset{i_{S,{\bar{S}}}}{\hookrightarrow}{\bar{S}}\overset{i_{{\bar{S}},X}}{\hookrightarrow}X\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}Z

together with

i_{{\bar{S}},Z}=i\circ i_{{\bar{S}},X},

so that

j_{S,Z}=i_{{\bar{S}},Z}\circ i_{S,{\bar{S}}}.

Under the above notations and assumptions, and using the
6
-functor formalism from
[
1
]
we get as in
[
28
, Prop.5.1.2]
the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

(3.1)

[{\mathcal{M}}]=\sum_{S\neq U,\ell}\,(-1)^{\ell}\cdot\big[(j_{S,Z})_{!}L_{S,\ell}^{H}\big]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z)),

where
L_{S,\ell}^{H}=H^{\ell+\dim(S)}(j_{S,Z})^{*}{\mathcal{M}}[-\dim(S)]
is the shifted equivariant smooth mixed Hodge module on
S
with
L_{S,\ell}
the underlying local system of
{\rm For}(L_{S,\ell}^{H})
, and the summation is over strata
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that in this case an orbit-stratum
S
is
affine
, since it is another torus of maybe smaller dimension, so that
(j_{S,Z})_{!}
is t-exact
for (equivariant) mixed Hodge modules and their underlying (equivariant) perverse sheaves. So in this context one has for all strata
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
:

(j_{S,Z})_{!}L_{S,\ell}^{H}[\dim(S)]\in{\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{X}(Z)\simeq{\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\>.

Especially
(j_{S,Z})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}_{S}^{H}[\dim(S)]\in{\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{X}(Z)\simeq{\rm MHM}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
for all strata
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
.

In the notations of Proposition
3.1
, we get the following.

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume, moreover, that for any orbit
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
, the stabilizer
{\mathbb{T}}_{s}\subset{\mathbb{T}}
at a point
s\in S
is connected. Then each
L_{S,\ell}^{H}\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{S}\boxtimes L_{S,s,\ell}^{H}
is a constant variation of mixed Hodge structures on
S
with stalk the mixed Hodge structure
L_{S,s,\ell}^{H}
, and we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}[{\mathcal{M}}]&=\sum_{S,\ell}\,(-1)^{\ell}\cdot\big[(j_{S,Z})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}_{S}^{H}\big]\cdot[L^{H}_{S,s,\ell}]\\
&=\sum_{S}\,\big[(j_{S,Z})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}_{S}^{H}\big]\cdot i_{s}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}],\end{split}

with
i_{s}:\{s\}\hookrightarrow Z
the inclusion, for
s\in S
chosen, and the summation is over the orbit-strata
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
.

In particular,

(3.3)

\begin{split}\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])&=\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}}\,\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}\big[(j_{S,Z})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}_{S}^{H}\big]\cdot\chi_{y}(i_{s}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\\
&=\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}}\,mC_{y}^{\mathbb{T}}([j_{S,Z}:S\hookrightarrow Z])\cdot\chi_{y}(i_{s}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\\
&=\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}}\,(i_{{\bar{S}},Z})_{*}mC^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([i_{S,{\bar{S}}}:S\hookrightarrow{\bar{S}}])\cdot\chi_{y}(i_{s}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}]).\end{split}

Proof.

The fact that each
L^{H}_{S,\ell}
is a constant variation of mixed Hodge structures on
S
follows by
[
38
, Lem.1.2]
using the assumption that the stabilizer
{\mathbb{T}}_{s}
of
s\in S
is connected. This proves (
3.2
). To show (
3.3
), we apply the equivariant Hodge–Chern class transformation
\hbox{\rm DR}_{y}^{\mathbb{T}}
to (
3.2
), using the fact that
K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
is a
K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt))\simeq K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})
-module, together with the functoriality of the equivariant motivic Chern class transformation
mC_{y}^{\mathbb{T}}
for the closed embedding
i_{{\bar{S}},Z}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Formula (
3.2
) shows that under the connected
{\mathbb{T}}
-stabilizers assumption, the class
[{\mathcal{M}}]
belongs to the (free)
K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt))=K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})
-submodule (respectively,
K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})^{\rm Tate}
-submodule, if the stalks
i_{s}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}]
are of Tate type) of
K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
generated by classes
[(j_{S,Z})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}_{S}^{H}\big]=\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}([j_{S,Z}:S\hookrightarrow Z])
,
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
. In what follows, we regard

\chi_{y}(i_{s}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]

as a weight (depending only on
[{\mathcal{M}}]
) attached to the stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
containing
s
. In the case of a projective toric variety
X=X_{P}
associated to a full dimensional lattice polytope
P
(as in the Introduction),
the subgroup

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}\left(\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}])\right)\subset K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}({\rm MHM}_{X}(Z))

is the
K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})^{\rm Tate}
-submodule generated by classes
[{\mathcal{M}}]
whose stalks are of Tate type:

i_{s}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})^{\rm Tate}\subset K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p})\quad\text{for all strata $S$.}

In particular, the intersection cohomology Hodge modules of the orbit closures
V_{Q}
of

are of Tate type, so that this property

of Tate type
is stable under duality
{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}
. This is more generally true for

a quasi-projective toric variety
(as discussed later on). ∎

Remark 3.5
.

Under the assumption of connected
{\mathbb{T}}
-stabilizers, formula (

) reduces the calculation of equivariant Hodge–Chern classes to the motivic calculus of

]
, up to assigning the above mentioned Laurent polynomial weights

By functoriality, the terms

appearing in formula (

, Thm.3.3]
, adapting ideas from

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

-equivariant compactification of a stratum

.

Under the notations and assumption of Proposition

4.
Twisted equivariant Hodge–Chern classes and their degrees

Assume now that a torus
{\mathbb{T}}
with character lattice
M
acts linearly on a finite dimensional complex vector space
W
, with eigenspace

(4.1)

W_{\chi^{m}}:=\{w\in W\mid t\cdot w=\chi^{m}(t)w\ \text{ for all}\ t\in{\mathbb{T}}\},

for
\chi^{m}
a character of
{\mathbb{T}}
. Then, as in
[
13
, Prop.1.1.2]
, one has an eigenspace decomposition

W=\bigoplus_{m\in M}W_{\chi^{m}}.

This induces an isomorphism

(4.2)

K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[M],\ \ [W]_{\mathbb{T}}\mapsto\sum_{m\in M}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W_{\chi^{m}}\cdot\chi^{m},

identifying the duality involution
(-)^{\vee}
on the left hand side with the involution
m\mapsto-m
on characters.
If
X
is a compact algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety, with a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
, then the cohomology spaces
H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{F}})
are finite dimensional
{\mathbb{T}}
-representations, vanishing for
i
large enough. The
K
-theoretic Euler characteristic
of
{\mathcal{F}}
is then defined as:

(4.3)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,{\mathcal{F}})&:=(a_{X})_{*}[{\mathcal{F}}]_{\mathbb{T}}=[H^{\bullet}(X;{\mathcal{F}})]_{\mathbb{T}}:=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{F}})]_{\mathbb{T}}\in K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\\
&=\sum_{m\in M}\sum_{i=0}^{\dim(X)}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{F}})_{\chi^{m}}\cdot\chi^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[M],\end{split}

with
a_{X}:X\to pt
the constant map to a point. Note that
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,{\mathcal{F}})
depends only on the class
[{\mathcal{F}}]\in K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
.

Let now
X
be a closed
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant compact algebraic subvariety of an algebraic
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
,
and let
D
be a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Cartier divisor on
X
. Let
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
be fixed.
Then

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D):=\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)]\in K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}],

where we extend the
K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
-module structure of
K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
induced from the tensor product linearly over
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
. For
a_{X}:X\to pt
the constant map as above, we can then define the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant Hodge polynomial of
(X,D,[{\mathcal{M}}])
as:

(4.4)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,D;[{\mathcal{M}}])&:=(a_{X})_{*}\left(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\right)\in K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(pt)[y^{\pm 1}]=K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)[y^{\pm 1}]\\
&=:\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X;\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))\in{\mathbb{Z}}[M][y^{\pm 1}],\end{split}

which only depends on the Grothendieck class
[{\mathcal{M}}]
, as well as on
D
.
Only for
D=0
it just depends on
[{\mathcal{M}}]
.
In other words,
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,D;[{\mathcal{M}}])
is an incarnation (in terms of the characters of
{\mathbb{T}}
) of the degree of the Hodge–Chern class of
[{\mathcal{M}}]
twisted by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)
.

Remark 4.1
.

If
D=0
,

(4.5)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,0;[{\mathcal{M}}])&=(a_{X})_{*}\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])=\chi_{y}(X;[{\mathcal{M}}])\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]\\
&=\chi_{y}(X;[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot\chi^{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[M][y^{\pm 1}],\end{split}

is the usual Hodge polynomial of
[{\mathcal{M}}]
as in (
2.4
). ∎

Example 4.2
.

If
X
is smooth and
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathbb{Q}}_{X}^{H}
is the constant Hodge module complex (viewed as a Hodge module complex on
Z
), then

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,D;[{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}^{H}])=\sum_{p=0}^{\dim(X)}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}\left(X,\Omega^{p}_{X}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\right)\cdot y^{p},

which is exactly
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))
, the equivariant Hirzebruch polynomial of
D
, e.g., see
[
11
, Sect.3.2]
.∎

Example 4.3
.

Assume
X
is a toric variety or a complex algebraic
V
-manifold. Then

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,D;[{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}^{H}])=\sum_{p=0}^{\dim(X)}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}\left(X,\widehat{\Omega}^{p}_{X}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\right)\cdot y^{p},

where
\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
denotes the corresponding sheaf of Zariski differential
p
-forms, is the Hodge polynomial of
D
considered in
[
11
, Sect.3.2]
in the toric context.
∎

5.
Characteristic class formulae in toric geometry

In this section, we specialize the formulae of previous sections to the case of toric varieties (e.g., associated to a full-dimensional lattice polytope), which are known to be Whitney stratified by the orbits of the torus action.

Let us first introduce some notation. Let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be a toric variety associated to the fan
\Sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}\cong N\otimes{\mathbb{R}}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, with torus
{\mathbb{T}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{C}}^{*}=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
having
M
, the dual of the lattice
N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, as the character lattice. The action of the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
on itself extends to an algebraic action of
{\mathbb{T}}
on
X

with finitely many orbits
O_{\sigma}
, one for each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
. In fact, to a
k
-dimensional cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
there corresponds an
(n-k)
-dimensional torus-orbit
O_{\sigma}
\ \cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n-k}
, and the orbit closure
V_{\sigma}
of
O_{\sigma}
is itself a toric variety and a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant subvariety of
X
.
Let
N(\sigma)=N/N_{\sigma},

with
N_{\sigma}
denoting the sublattice of
N
spanned by the points in
\sigma\cap N
. Let
{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}=N(\sigma)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
be the torus associated to
N(\sigma)
. For each cone
\nu\in\Sigma
containing
\sigma
(in short,
\sigma\preceq\nu
), let
\overline{\nu}
be the image cone in
N(\sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}
under the quotient map
N_{\mathbb{R}}\to N(\sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

(5.1)

Star(\sigma)=\{\overline{\nu}\subseteq N(\sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\sigma\preceq\nu\}

is a fan in
N(\sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}
, with associated toric variety isomorphic to
V_{\sigma}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Prop.3.2.7]
), and
{\mathbb{T}}
acts on
V_{\sigma}
via the morphism

(5.2)

{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}

induced by the quotient map
N\to N(\sigma)
.

Before specializing formula (
3.3
) to this toric context, we need the following key additional input derived in
[
11
]
:

Proposition 5.1
.

[
11
, Prop.3.8]

Let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be a toric variety defined by the fan
\Sigma
. For any cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, with orbit
O_{\sigma}
and inclusion
i_{\sigma}:O_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow V_{\sigma}
in the orbit closure, we have:

(5.3)

mC^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([i_{\sigma}])=(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}},

where
\omega_{V_{\sigma}}=\widehat{\Omega}^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}_{V_{\sigma}}
is the canonical sheaf on the toric variety
V_{\sigma}
, viewed as a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant sheaf via the quotient map
{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}
given by (
5.2
).

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
3.6
by using a toric resolution
V
of
V_{\sigma}
, together with the fact that the locally free sheaf
\Omega_{V}^{1}(\log(D))
is
{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}
-equivariantly, hence also
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariantly, a trivial sheaf of rank
\dim(O_{\sigma})
, and there is a
{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}
-equivariant, hence also
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant isomorphism
R(\pi_{V})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{V}(-D)\simeq\omega_{V_{\sigma}}
.
∎

In the following we assume that
X=X_{\Sigma}
is a quasi-projective toric variety. Then
[
37
]
guarantees the existence of some smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant ambient variety
Z
with a closed
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant embedding
i:X\hookrightarrow Z
. Note also that the torus stabilizer at any point in a torus orbit of
X
is itself a torus, hence
connected
.

Together with formula (
3.3
), Proposition
5.1
then gives the following:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be a quasi-projective toric variety defined by the fan
\Sigma
, with torus
{\mathbb{T}}
.
For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
with orbit
O_{\sigma}
, let
k_{\sigma}:V_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow Z
be the inclusion of the orbit closure in the smooth ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
mentioned above, and fix a point
x_{\sigma}\in O_{\sigma}
with inclusion
i_{x_{\sigma}}:\{x_{\sigma}\}\hookrightarrow Z
. Let
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
be a (virtual difference) Grothendieck class
of a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant mixed Hodge module on
Z
with support in
X
.
Then:

(5.4)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\,\chi_{y}(i_{x_{\sigma}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}.

In particular, when
X
is compact, by taking degrees we get

(5.5)

\chi_{y}(X;[{\mathcal{M}}])=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\,\chi_{y}(i_{x_{\sigma}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}.

Theorem
5.2
yields the following:

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be the quasi-projective toric variety defined by a fan
\Sigma
, with a chosen closed
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant embedding into a smooth ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety
Z
. For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
with orbit
O_{\sigma}
,
fix a point
x_{\sigma}\in O_{\sigma}
.
Then we have the following formula:

(5.6)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{IC^{\prime}}_{X}^{H}])=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\,\chi_{y}({IC^{\prime}}_{X}^{H}|_{x_{\sigma}})\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}.

Here,
{IC}_{X}^{H}\in{\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z)
is regarded as a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant Hodge module on the ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
, and each class
[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}
is pushed into
K_{0,X}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z)\simeq K_{0}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
.

Remark 5.4
.

In particular, if
X_{\Sigma}
is a simplicial toric variety then
\chi_{y}({IC^{\prime}}_{X}^{H}|_{x_{\sigma}})=\chi_{y}({\mathbb{Q}}_{X}^{H}|_{x_{\sigma}})=1
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
, and one recovers in this case a result from
[
11
]
. Also note that formula (
5.6
) for
X
an affine toric variety also fits with recent results of the paper
[
24
]
. ∎

Let us next recall the identification of
\chi_{y}({IC^{\prime}}_{X}^{H}|_{x_{\sigma}})
with the corresponding Stanley
g
-function, as mentioned in the Introduction, see
[
15
, Thm.6.2]
,
[
18
, Thm.1.2]
,
[
33
, Thm.1]
. Here we follow the treatment from
[
33
]
, formulated in terms of mixed Hodge modules, as used in this paper.
Recall that to a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
one associates a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant open affine toric subvariety
U_{\sigma}\subset X_{\Sigma}
, which is of global product type

U_{\sigma}=Z_{\sigma}\times O_{\sigma},

as follows. Choose a splitting
N=N_{\sigma}\oplus{N^{\prime}}
of
N
, where
N_{\sigma}=N\cap\langle\sigma\rangle
is the lattice spanned by
\sigma
, with a corresponding splitting
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathbb{T}}
. Here
Z_{\sigma}
is the
{\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}
-toric variety of
\sigma
in
N_{\sigma}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
, and
{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}\simeq O_{\sigma}
. Let
s_{\sigma}:Z_{\sigma}\to U_{\sigma}
be a trivial section (given by crossing with a point in
{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}
) of the projection
p_{\sigma}:U_{\sigma}\to Z_{\sigma}
, so that
s_{\sigma}^{*}{IC^{\prime}}_{X}^{H}\simeq{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{Z_{\sigma}}
. Then the stalk cohomology
of
{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{Z_{\sigma}}
at the unique
{\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}
-fixed point
x_{\sigma}
in
Z_{\sigma}
is calculated in
[
33
, (1.7.5)]
as the primitive intersection cohomology of a projective toric variety
Y
defined as follows: an interior point of
N_{\sigma}
gives a
1
-parameter group homomorphism
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\to{\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}
, so that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
Z_{\sigma}
has
x_{\sigma}
as the only attracting fixed point, and
Y
is the quotient of
Z_{\sigma}\setminus\{x_{\sigma}\}
by
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. This implies that the stalk cohomologies of
{IC^{\prime}}_{X}^{H}
have pure Hodge structures, which inductively are seen to be of Tate type, so that the odd degree stalk cohomology is vanishing.
It follows that

\chi_{y}({IC^{\prime}}_{X}^{H}|_{x_{\sigma}})=g(-y)=g(t^{2}),

with
-y=t^{2}
, and
g
the Poincaré polynomial of the primitive intersection cohomology of

.
This

-polynomial agrees via the recursive formula
[
33
, (1.7.9)]
inductively with Stanley’s
g
-function of the corresponding polar polytope (as in the Introduction).
Finally, the above-mentioned Tate property of the stalk cohomologies of intersection cohomology complexes implies that, for a toric variety
X_{P}
associated to a full-dimensional lattice polytope

, one has

[{IC^{\prime}}^{H}_{V_{Q}}]\in\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}\left(\tau^{\mathbb{T}}(F^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)[y^{\pm 1}])\right)\subset K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))

for any face
Q
of
P
.

With the above notations, we next explain the
geometric
origin of the duality transformation (
1.9
) for weight functions. For the use of the equivariant
6
-functor formalism, we need to consider a
{\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}
-equivariant embedding
Z_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow Z^{\prime}
into a smooth
{\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}
-variety. Taking products with
{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}
, this gives a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant embedding
U_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow Z^{\prime}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}=:Z
together with a trivial section
s^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\to Z
of the projection
p^{\prime}:Z\to Z^{\prime}
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}\in D_{\rm MHM,U_{\sigma}}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)
be a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant element on
Z
with support on
U_{\sigma}
. By induction equivalence (e.g., see
[
1
, Ex.8.8, Ex.8.9]
), we get that
{\mathcal{M}}\simeq(p^{\prime})^{*}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
for some
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}\in D_{\rm MHM,Z_{\sigma}}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})
, with
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}\simeq(s^{\prime})^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
. Since

is smooth, we have that

{\mathcal{M}}\simeq(p^{\prime})^{!}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}[-2d^{\prime}](-d^{\prime})\in D_{\rm MHM,U_{\sigma}}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}}(Z),

for
d^{\prime}:=\dim({\mathbb{T}}^{\prime})
. Hence

(s^{\prime})^{!}{\mathcal{M}}\simeq{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}[-2d^{\prime}](-d^{\prime})\simeq(s^{\prime})^{*}{\mathcal{M}}[-2d^{\prime}](-d^{\prime})\in D_{\rm MHM,Z_{\sigma}}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime}).

Let
i_{x_{\sigma}}:\{x_{\sigma}\}\hookrightarrow Z_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow Z^{\prime}
be the point inclusion of the attracting fixed point in
Z_{\sigma}
. Then

(i_{x_{\sigma}})^{!}(s^{\prime})^{!}{\mathcal{M}}\simeq\left((i_{x_{\sigma}})^{!}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}\right)[-2d^{\prime}](-d^{\prime}).

Let
a:Z^{\prime}\to\{x_{\sigma}\}
be the constant map. Applying
a_{!}
to the adjunction
(i_{x_{\sigma}})_{!}(i_{x_{\sigma}})^{!}\to id
yields a morphism
(i_{x_{\sigma}})^{!}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}\to a_{!}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
in

. The underlying nonequivariant morphism is even an isomorphism by
[
2
, Thm.2.10.3]
, because the underlying constructible sheaf complex is
{\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}
-equivariant, and therefore
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant for the
1
-parameter subgroup
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\to{\mathbb{T}}_{\sigma}
as above (with

the unique attracting fixed point). This implies that

(i_{x_{\sigma}})^{!}(s^{\prime})^{!}[{\mathcal{M}}]=a_{!}[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(-d^{\prime})]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}(x_{\sigma}))=K_{0}({\rm MHS}^{p}),

for any
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}(D_{\rm MHM,U_{\sigma}}^{b,{\mathbb{T}}}(Z))=K_{0}({\rm MHM}_{U_{\sigma}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)).

In the above notations, for any cone
0\preceq\tau\preceq\sigma
(i.e., for all
{\mathbb{T}}
-orbits
O_{\tau}
contained in
U_{\sigma}
, or equivalently, with
O_{\sigma}
contained in the orbit closure
V_{\tau}
),
let

f_{\tau}(y):=\chi_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}|_{x_{\tau}}]).

Applying
\chi_{y}
and using the additivity as in (

), we get

(5.7)

\chi_{y}\left((i_{x_{\sigma}})^{!}(s^{\prime})^{!}[{\mathcal{M}}]\right)=\sum_{0\preceq\tau\preceq\sigma}(-1-y)^{\dim(O_{\tau})-\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(-y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot f_{\tau}(y).

Dualizing this formula, we get

(5.8)

\begin{split}f_{\sigma}({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}[{\mathcal{M}}])&:=\chi_{y}\left((i_{x_{\sigma}})^{*}(s^{\prime})^{*}[{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}{\mathcal{M}}]\right)\\
&=\sum_{0\preceq\tau\preceq\sigma}(-1-1/y)^{\dim(O_{\tau})-\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(-y)^{-\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot f_{\tau}(1/y)\\
&=\sum_{0\preceq\tau\preceq\sigma}(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\tau})-\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(-y)^{-\dim(O_{\tau})}\cdot f_{\tau}(1/y).\end{split}

Formula (
5.8
) matches the definition of the weight function from (

), since the calculation of (co)stalks in a given point

can be done on the
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant open affine neighborhood

.

For future reference, we also reformulate here (

) in the language of equivariant mixed Hodge modules. Let
k_{\sigma}:V_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow Z
be the orbit closure embedding into the smooth ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold

constructed in
[

]
. Let

.

Corollary 5.5
.

In the above notations,

(5.9)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])=(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}.

Proof.

This follows immediately by applying the proper pushforward
(k_{\sigma})_{*}
to both sides of (
5.3
), using the functoriality of

and the definition of
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg}
. Indeed,

(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}=(k_{\sigma})_{*}mC^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([i_{\sigma}])=mC^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([j_{\sigma}])=\hbox{\rm DR}_{y}^{\mathbb{T}}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}]).

∎

For our applications to generalized weighted Ehrhart theory (or Euler-Maclaurin sums) in the next section, it is also important to compute
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])
, which, as we will see below, is related to
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])
via duality.
Before computing
\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

For
\sigma\in\Sigma
with orbit
O_{\sigma}
and orbit closure
V_{\sigma}
, we have for the equivariant dualizing complex that

(5.10)

{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}({\mathcal{O}}_{V_{\sigma}})\simeq\omega_{V_{\sigma}}[\dim(O_{\sigma})].

In particular, by equivariant biduality, we get

(5.11)

{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}=(-1)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot[{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}\overset{f_{\sigma}}{\to}V_{\sigma}
be a toric resolution of singularities of
V_{\sigma}
. Then
\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}
is a toric variety obtained by refining the fan of
V_{\sigma}
. Let
O_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}
be the natural open inclusion, with complement the simple normal crossing divisor
D_{\sigma}
whose irreducible components correspond to the rays in the fan of
\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}
. Note that
D_{\sigma}
is a
{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}
-invariant (hence a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant) divisor.

Furthermore, note that the canonical sheaf
\omega_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}
on the toric variety
\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}
is precisely given by
{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}(-D_{\sigma})\simeq\omega_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}
as
{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}
-equivariant, hence also
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant sheaves, e.g., see
[
13
, Thm.8.2.3]
. And since the toric morphism
f_{\sigma}:\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}\to V_{\sigma}
is induced by a refinement of the fan of
V_{\sigma}
, it follows from
[
13
, Thm.8.2.15]
that there is a
{\mathbb{T}}_{N(\sigma)}
-equivariant, hence also
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant, isomorphism:

(5.12)

{f_{\sigma}}_{*}\omega_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}\simeq\omega_{V_{\sigma}}.

Altogether, we obtain the following sequence of isomorphisms:

(5.13)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}({\mathcal{O}}_{V_{\sigma}})&\simeq{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}\left((Rf_{\sigma})_{*}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}\big)\right)\\
&\simeq(Rf_{\sigma})_{*}\big({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}})\big)\\
&\simeq(Rf_{\sigma})_{*}\big(\omega_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}[\dim(O_{\sigma})]\big)\\
&\simeq\omega_{V_{\sigma}}[\dim(O_{\sigma})],\end{split}

where the first isomorphism follows since
{V}_{\sigma}
has only rational singularities (as in the proof of
[
11
, Prop.3.5]
); the second isomorphism follows since equivariant Grothendieck duality commutes with proper pushforward (cf.
[
25
, Thm.25.2]
); the third isomorphism follows
by

(5.14)

{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}})\simeq\omega_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}[\dim(O_{\sigma})],

for the smooth variety
\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}
,
cf.
[
25
, Thm.28.11]
; finally, the last isomorphism is a consequence of (
5.12
) and the vanishing of the higher derived image sheaves of
\omega_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}
, i.e.,

R^{i}(f_{\sigma})_{*}\omega_{\widetilde{V}_{\sigma}}=0
, for all
i>0
,
see
[
13
, Thm.9.3.12]
.

By using equivariant biduality, cf.
[
25
, Prop.31.9, Lem.31.11]
, we get from (
5.10
) that

{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{\sigma}}\simeq({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}\omega_{V_{\sigma}})[-\dim(O_{\sigma})],

which yields (
5.11
).
∎

In the notations of the above Corollary
5.5
, we then have the following

Proposition 5.7
.

For
j_{\sigma}:O_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow Z
the orbit inclusion into the ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
,

(5.15)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])=(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}.

Proof.

Since
O_{\sigma}
is smooth,
{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}
is a (shifted)
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant pure Hodge module of weight
\dim(O_{\sigma})
, so

{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{O_{\sigma}}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}[2\dim(O_{\sigma}](\dim(O_{\sigma})).

Using (
2.5
), the fact that the even shift does not affect the Grothendieck class, together with (
2.2
) for the inclusion
j_{\sigma}
,
we get

(5.16)

\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])=(-y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}\big[(j_{\sigma})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big]).

Since
(j_{\sigma})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\in{\rm Image}(\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{Hdg})
, Remark
2.12
gives

(5.17)

\begin{split}\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}_{Z}\big[(j_{\sigma})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])&=\left({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}\left(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(\big[(j_{\sigma})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma}}\big])\right)\right)_{|_{y\mapsto 1/y}}\\
&\overset{\eqref{drtc}}{=}\left({\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}\left((1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\right)\right)_{|_{y\mapsto 1/y}}\\
&=(1+\frac{1}{y})^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{T}}[\omega_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\\
&\overset{\eqref{Sed}}{=}y^{-\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(-1)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\\
&=(-y)^{-\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(O_{\sigma})}\cdot(k_{\sigma})_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{\sigma}}]_{\mathbb{T}}.\end{split}

Here, in the first two equalities we use a linear extension of equivariant Grothedieck duality over
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
.
Formula (
5.15
) is now a consequence of (
5.16
) and (
5.17
).
∎

6.
Applications to weighted Ehrhart theory

Let now
M\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be a lattice and
P\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a full-dimensional lattice polytope with associated projective toric variety
X=X_{P}
, inner normal fan
\Sigma=\Sigma_{P}
and ample Cartier divisor
D=D_{P}
.
One has (e.g., see
[
13
, Prop.4.3.3]
)

(6.1)

\Gamma(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))=\bigoplus_{m\in P\cap M}{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\chi^{m}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[M],

where
\chi^{m}
denotes the character defined by
m\in M
, and
{\mathbb{C}}[M]
is the coordinate ring of the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
.
Also, since
D_{P}
is ample, Demazure vanishing (e.g., see
[
13
, Thm.9.2.3]
) yields that

(6.2)

H^{i}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))=0,\ {\rm for\ all}\ i>0.

Moreover, one has by the Batyrev-Borisov vanishing theorem (e.g., see
[
13
, Prop.9.2.7]
) that

(6.3)

H^{i}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-D_{P}))=0,\ {\rm for\ all}\ i\neq n,

and

(6.4)

H^{n}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-D_{P}))=\bigoplus_{m\in{\rm Int}(P)\cap M}{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\chi^{-m},

with
{\rm Int}({P})
the interior of
P
. Also, by equivariant toric Serre duality
[
13
, Thm.9.2.10]
(fitting with our description of the equivariant dualizing complex in (
5.10
)),

(6.5)

\bigoplus_{m\in{\rm Int}(P)\cap M}{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\chi^{m}=\left(H^{n}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-D_{P}))\right)^{\vee}\simeq H^{0}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}}),

with
\omega_{X_{P}}=\widehat{\Omega}^{\dim(P)}_{X_{P}}
the equivariant dualizing and canonical sheaf of
X_{P}
, together with
H^{i}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}})=0
for all
i\neq 0
.

Remark 6.1
.

For any
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Cartier divisor
D
on an
n
-dimensional complete toric variety
X
with torus
{\mathbb{T}}
, one has the nondegenerate equivariant pairing

H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D))\times H^{n-i}(X;{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\otimes\omega_{X})\longrightarrow H^{n}(X;\omega_{X})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

induced by the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant sheaf isomorphism

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\otimes\omega_{X}\longrightarrow\omega_{X}

and the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant trace map
\omega_{X}[n]\to\omega_{pt}={\mathbb{C}}
. This induces the
equivariant Serre duality

\left(H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D))\right)^{\vee}\simeq H^{n-i}(X;{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\otimes\omega_{X}).

∎

Remark 6.2
.

For a cone
\sigma_{Q}\in\Sigma_{P}
associated to a face
Q
of
P
, the corresponding orbit closure
V_{\sigma_{Q}}
can be identified with the toric variety
X_{Q}
, with corresponding lattice polytope defined as follows (cf.
[
13
, Prop.3.2.9]
): translate
P
by a vertex
m_{0}
of
Q
so that the origin is a vertex of
Q_{0}:=Q-m_{0}
; while this translation by
m_{0}
does not change
\Sigma_{P}
or
X_{P}
,
Q_{0}
is now a full-dimensional polytope in
Span(Q_{0})
relative to the lattice
Span(Q_{0})\cap M
, and
X_{Q}
is the associated toric variety.
However, if one wishes to apply the above formulas to the toric variety
X_{Q}
associated to a face
Q
of
P
, one needs to work with the corresponding divisor

(6.6)

D_{Q_{0}}={D_{P-m_{0}}}|_{X_{Q}}=D_{P}|_{X_{Q}}+div(\chi^{m_{0}}).

∎

Formulae (
6.1
)-(
6.4
) correspond to the weight decomposition of the respective cohomology groups, e.g.,

(6.7)

H^{i}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))=\bigoplus_{m\in M}H^{i}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))_{m},

as in
[
13
, Sect.9.1]
.
To relate this weight decomposition with the eigenspace decomposition of (
4.1
), we recall that the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}[M]
as follows: if
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
and
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[M]
, then
t\cdot f\in{\mathbb{C}}[M]
is given by
p\mapsto f(t^{-1}\cdot p)
, for
p\in{\mathbb{T}}
(see
[
13
, pag.18]
). In particular,
t\cdot\chi^{m}=\chi^{m}(t^{-1})\chi^{m}
, so that

H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))_{m}=H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))_{\chi^{-m}}.

Let us note that if for a positive integer
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
we consider the dilated polytope

\ell P:=\{\ell\cdot u\ |\ u\in P\},

then the toric varieties corresponding to
P
and, resp.,
\ell P
, are the same, but
D_{\ell P}=\ell D_{P}
. Together with (
6.1
)-(
6.5
), this gives for such
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
:

(6.8)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\ell D_{P}))=\sum_{m\in\ell P\cap M}\chi^{-m},

(6.9)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-\ell D_{P}))=(-1)^{n}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Int}(\ell P)\cap M}\chi^{m}

and

(6.10)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\ell D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}})=\sum_{m\in{\rm Int}(\ell P)\cap M}\chi^{-m}.

More generally, we have by
[
11
, Rem.4.9]
, applied to the polytope
\ell P
, the following identity:

(6.11)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},mC^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X_{P})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(\ell D_{P}))=\sum_{Q\preceq P}(1+y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}(\ell Q)\cap M}\chi^{-m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}}[y].

where
{\rm Relint}
denotes the relative interior, and the first summation is over the faces
Q
of the lattice polytope
P
.

For further reference, let us also include here the following.

Lemma 6.3
.

In the above notations, with
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,

(6.12)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{P},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-\ell D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}})=(-1)^{n}\cdot\sum_{m\in\ell P\cap M}\chi^{m}

Proof.

By toric equivariant Serre duality
[
13
, Thm.9.2.10]
and Remark
6.1
, together with (
6.1
) and (
6.2
),

(6.13)

\bigoplus_{m\in P\cap M}{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\chi^{-m}=\left(H^{0}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))\right)^{\vee}\simeq H^{n}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}}),

and
H^{i}(X_{P};{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{P}}(-D_{P})\otimes\omega_{X_{P}})=0
for all
i\neq n
.
The assertion follows by reverting to the weight decomposition of these cohomology groups, as indicated above.
∎

Making use of Theorem
5.2
, we can now generalize formula (
6.11
) as follows.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
X=X_{P}
be the projective toric variety with ample Cartier divisor
D=D_{P}
associated to a full-dimensional lattice polytope
P\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and let
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Let
Z
be an ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold containing
X
as a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant subvariety.
Then for any (virtual-difference) Grothendieck class

[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
of a mixed Hodge module on
Z
with support in
X
, we have

(6.14)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])&=\sum_{Q\preceq P}\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\chi^{-m},\end{split}

where the first summation on the right is over the faces
Q
of
P
, and
x_{Q}\in O_{\sigma_{Q}}
is a point in the orbit of
X
associated to (the cone
\sigma_{Q}
in the inner normal fan of
P
corresponding to) the face
Q
, with inclusion map
i_{x_{Q}}:\{x_{Q}\}\hookrightarrow Z
.

Proof.

For a face
Q
of
P
, denote by
k_{Q}:V_{\sigma_{Q}}:=X_{Q}\hookrightarrow X\hookrightarrow Z
the inclusion in the ambient
{\mathbb{T}}
-manifold
Z
of the orbit closure associated to the (cone
\sigma_{Q}
of the) face
Q
. Note that we have
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Q)=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(O_{\sigma_{Q}}).
Let
{\mathbb{T}}_{Q}
be the quotient torus of
{\mathbb{T}}
corresponding to
X_{Q}
, as in (
5.2
).

In what follows, we will denote all constant maps to a point by
a
, regardless of their domains (which should be understood from the context).
Then by (
4.4
) and (
5.4
) the following equality holds:

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;{\mathcal{M}})&=a_{*}\left(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D)\right)\\
&=\sum_{Q\preceq P}\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot a_{*}\left((k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D)\right).\end{split}

So it remains to prove that, for any face
Q
of
P
, we have that:

(6.15)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,(k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D))=a_{*}\left((k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D)\right)=\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}(\ell Q)\cap M}\chi^{-m}.

This follows from the projection formula and (
6.10
), by making use of formula (
6.6
). Indeed,

\begin{split}a_{*}\left((k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D)\right)&=a_{*}(k_{Q})_{*}\left([\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes k_{Q}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D)\right)\\
&\overset{(\ref{tran})}{=}a_{*}\left([\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{Q}}(\ell D_{Q_{0}}-div(\chi^{\ell m_{0}}))\right)\\
&=\chi^{-\ell m_{0}}\cdot a_{*}\left([\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{Q}}(\ell D_{Q_{0}})\right)\\
&\overset{({\ast})}{=}\chi^{-\ell m_{0}}\cdot a_{*}\left([\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{{\mathbb{T}}_{Q}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{Q}}(\ell D_{Q_{0}})\right)\\
&\overset{(\ref{am6})}{=}\chi^{-\ell m_{0}}\cdot\left(\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}\ \ell(Q-m_{0})\cap M}\chi^{-m}\right)\\
&=\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}(\ell Q)\cap M}\chi^{-m},\end{split}

where
(\ast)
uses
[
11
, Prop.3.11]
, showing the equality of the equivariant classes

(6.16)

[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}=[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{{\mathbb{T}}_{Q}}

under the natural inclusion of Grothendieck groups

K_{0}^{{\mathbb{T}}_{Q}}(X_{Q})\hookrightarrow K_{0}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X_{Q})

induced by the surjection
{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{T}}_{Q}
. Here
m_{0}
is a vertex of
Q
as in (
6.6
), so that
Q_{0}:=Q-m_{0}
is a full-dimensional lattice polytope in
Span(Q_{0})
relative to the lattice
Span(Q_{0})\cap M
, with
X_{Q}
the associated toric variety.
∎

Definition 6.6

(Generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomials)
.

Let
\varphi:M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a homogeneous polynomial function, and
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
With the above notations, the expression

(6.18)

E^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathcal{M}}}(\ell,y):=\sum_{Q\preceq P}\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\varphi(m)

is obtained from
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;{\mathcal{M}})
by substituting
\chi^{-m}
by
\varphi\left((1+y)m\right)=(1+y)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\varphi(m)
, for each
m
.
As we will show later on in the Appendix (but see also
[
10
, Prop.4.1]
),
E^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathcal{M}}}(\ell,y)
is obtained by evaluating a polynomial
E^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathcal{M}}}(z,y)
at
z=\ell
. We call this polynomial the
generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomial
of
P
and
\varphi
with coefficients induced from
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
. Similarly, one can define

(6.19)

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathcal{M}}}(\ell,y):=\sum_{Q\preceq P}\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\varphi(m)

which obtained from
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;{\mathcal{M}})
by substituting
\chi^{-m}
by
\varphi\left(m\right)
, for each
m
. Since
\varphi
is homogeneous, this substitution fits with the duality involution
\chi^{m}\mapsto\chi^{-m}
for
m\in M
, via
\varphi(-m)=(-1)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\varphi(m)
.

More generally, for a Laurent polynomial
weight function

f:\{\text{faces of }P\}\to{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}],\ \ \ Q\mapsto f_{Q}(y)

defined on the set of faces of
P
, we define as in the Introduction an associated generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomial by

(6.20)

\begin{split}E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)&:=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\varphi(m)\\
&=:(1+y)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y).\end{split}

.

.

Theorem 6.8
.

In the notations of Theorem
6.4
, we have for

(6.21)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,-\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])&=\sum_{Q\preceq P}\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\ell}Q\cap M}\chi^{m}.\end{split}

Proof.

The proof is very similar to that of Theorem
6.4
, so we only sketch it below. In view of (
5.4
), this amounts to showing that for any face
Q
of
P
, and using notations from loc.cit., we have the following formula:

(6.22)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,(k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-\ell D))&=a_{*}\left((k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-\ell D)\right)\\
&=(-1)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in\ell Q\cap M}\chi^{m}.\end{split}

Indeed,

\begin{split}a_{*}\left((k_{Q})_{*}[\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-\ell D)\right)&=a_{*}(k_{Q})_{*}\left([\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes k_{Q}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-\ell D)\right)\\
&\overset{(\ref{tran})}{=}a_{*}\left([\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{Q}}(-\ell D_{Q_{0}}+div(\chi^{\ell m_{0}}))\right)\\
&=\chi^{\ell m_{0}}\cdot a_{*}\left([\omega_{X_{Q}}]_{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{Q}}(-\ell D_{Q_{0}})\right)\\

&\overset{(\ref{am56})}{=}\chi^{\ell m_{0}}\cdot\left((-1)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in\ell(Q-m_{0})\cap M}\chi^{m}\right)\\
&=(-1)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in\ell Q\cap M}\chi^{m},\end{split}

where
(\ast)
uses again the identification (
6.16
).
∎

Corollary 6.9

Let

In particular,

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(0,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\varphi(0)

(6.26)

\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(0,y)=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(-1-y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot(-1)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\varphi(0).

Proof.

As in Remark
6.7

Appendix A

In this final section we give a geometric proof of the following result (without relying on the combinatorial work of Brion–Vergne
[
10
]
).

Theorem A.1
.

Let
P\subset M_{R}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a full-dimensional lattice polytope. Let
\varphi:M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a homogeneous polynomial function, and
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Then for any Laurent polynomial weight function

f:\{\text{faces of }P\}\to{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}],\ \ \ Q\mapsto f_{Q}(y)

defined on the set of faces of
P
, the expression

(A.1)

E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y):=\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)+\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}\varphi(m)=(1+y)^{\deg(\varphi)}\cdot\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)

is obtained by evaluating a polynomial
E^{\varphi}_{P,f}(z,y)
at
z=\ell
.

Let
X=X_{P}
be the toric variety with torus
{\mathbb{T}}
and ample Cartier divisor
D=D_{P}
associated to the lattice polytope
P
. Let
Z
be an ambient smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-variety.
In view of Remark
6.7
, it suffices to prove the assertion in the above theorem for
E^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathcal{M}}}(\ell,y)
, as defined in (
6.18
), i.e., assuming that
f_{Q}(y)=\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])
, for a certain class
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in K_{0}({\rm MHM}^{\mathbb{T}}_{X}(Z))
of a mixed Hodge module (complex) on
Z
with support in
X=X_{P}
, with
x_{Q}\in O_{\sigma_{Q}}
a point in the orbit of
X
associated to (the cone
\sigma_{Q}
in the inner normal fan of
P
corresponding to) the face
Q
, and
i_{x_{Q}}:\{x_{Q}\}\to Z
the inclusion map.
Our proof in this case is based on localization techniques developed in
[
11
]
. We first introduce some terminology.

Recall that the (Borel-type) rational equivariant cohomology of
X
is defined as

(A.2)

H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}):=H^{*}(E{\mathbb{T}}\times_{\mathbb{T}}X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}),

where
{\mathbb{T}}\hookrightarrow E{\mathbb{T}}\to B{\mathbb{T}}
is the universal principal
{\mathbb{T}}
-bundle, i.e.,
B{\mathbb{T}}=(B\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{*})^{n}=({\mathbb{C}}P^{\infty})^{n}
and
E{\mathbb{T}}
is contractible. In particular, if
X=pt
is a point space,

(A.3)

H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt;{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{*}(B{\mathbb{T}};{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]=:(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}.

Similarly, one can define equivariant homology groups
H_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})
as in
[
9
]
,
[
4
, Sect.17]
and
[
11
, Sect.2.5]
, using suitable finite-dimensional approximations of
B{\mathbb{T}}
. Note that these equivariant homology groups can be non-zero also in negative degrees. As in the non-equivariant context, there is a cap product operation

\cap:H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})\times H_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})\longrightarrow H_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}),

together with an equivariant Poincaré duality if
X
is smooth, see
[
4
, Sect.17.1]
. In particular,
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})
, resp.,
H_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})
, is a
(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-algebra, resp.,
(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-module.

For
m\in M
, an element in the character lattice of the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
, one can view the corresponding character
\chi^{m}:{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
as a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant line bundle
{\mathbb{C}}_{\chi^{m}}
over a point space
pt
, where the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}
is induced via
\chi^{m}
.
This gives rise to an abelian group isomorphism

(M,+)\simeq(Pic_{\mathbb{T}}(pt),\otimes)\>.

Recall that
m\mapsto-m
corresponds to the duality involution
(-)^{\vee}
. Taking the first equivariant Chern class

c^{1}_{\mathbb{T}}
(or the dual
-c^{1}_{\mathbb{T}}=c^{1}_{\mathbb{T}}\circ(-)^{\vee}
) of
{\mathbb{C}}_{\chi^{m}}
gives an isomorphism

(A.4)

c=c^{1}_{\mathbb{T}},\>\text{resp.},\>s=-c^{1}_{\mathbb{T}}:\>M\simeq H_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}(pt;\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$})

and

(A.5)

c,\>\text{resp.},\>s:Sym_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}(M)\simeq H_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(pt;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})=(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}\>,

with
Sym_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}(M)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}Sym^{k}_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}(M)
the (rational) symmetric algebra of
M
.
So if
m_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,n
) is a basis of
M\simeq\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}^{n}
, then
H_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(pt;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})=(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}\simeq\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}],
with

t_{i}=\pm c^{1}_{\mathbb{T}}({\mathbb{C}}_{\chi^{m_{i}}})
for
i=1,\dots,n
.

Let

\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}\simeq(H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}))^{an}=:(\Lambda^{an}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}\subset(\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}[[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]]

be the subring of convergent power series (around zero) with rational coefficients, i.e., after pairing with
z\in N_{\mbox{$\mathbb{K}$}}=N\otimes_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}}\mbox{$\mathbb{K}$}
(for
\mbox{$\mathbb{K}$}=\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$},\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}
) one gets a convergent power series
function
in
z
around zero, whose corresponding Taylor polynomials have rational coefficients. Here, the degree completion
(\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}
of
(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}
corresponds to formal power series with rational coefficients.

Recall that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant coherent sheaf on
X
, then the
K
-theoretic Euler characteristic of
{\mathcal{F}}
is defined as in (
4.3
) by:

(A.6)

\begin{split}\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,{\mathcal{F}})&=\sum_{m\in M}\sum_{i=0}^{\dim(X)}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{F}})_{\chi^{m}}\cdot\chi^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[M],\end{split}

By applying the equivariant Chern character ring homomorphism

{\rm ch}^{\mathbb{T}}:{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\simeq K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\hookrightarrow(\Lambda^{an}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}\subset(\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}

given by
m\mapsto e^{c(m)}
,
we further get the
cohomological Euler characteristic
of
{\mathcal{F}}
:

(A.7)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,{\mathcal{F}})=\sum_{m\in M}\sum_{i=0}^{\dim(X)}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i}(X;{\mathcal{F}})_{\chi^{m}}\cdot e^{c(m)}\in(\Lambda^{an}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}\subset(\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}\>.

Consider now the fixed-point set
X^{\mathbb{T}}
of the torus action; this can be identified with the set of vertices of the lattice polytope
P
.
By
[
9
, Lem.8.4, Lem.8.5]
and
[
4
, Thm.17.3.1]
, the inclusion
\alpha:X^{\mathbb{T}}\hookrightarrow X
induces an injective homomorphism of
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt;{\mathbb{Q}})
-modules

\alpha_{*}:\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}};{\mathbb{Q}})\hookrightarrow\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})

which becomes an isomorphism

\alpha_{*}:\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}};{\mathbb{Q}})_{L}\simeq\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})_{L}

upon localization at the multiplicative subset
L\subset(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}}=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt;{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by the elements
\pm c(m)
, for
0\neq m\in M
(cf.
[
9
, Cor.8.9]
). Here, we use the completion of

{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}):=\bigoplus_{i\leq 2\dim(X)}{H}_{i}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}),

defined as

\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}):=\prod_{i\leq 2\dim(X)}{H}_{i}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}).

Since

\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}};{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{x\in X^{\mathbb{T}}}\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(x;{\mathbb{Q}}),

one gets via the isomorphism
\alpha_{*}
a projection map of
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt;{\mathbb{Q}})
-modules, called the homological localization map at
x
,

pr_{x}:\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})_{L}\simeq\bigoplus_{x\in X^{\mathbb{T}}}\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(x;{\mathbb{Q}})_{L}\longrightarrow\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(x;{\mathbb{Q}})_{L}=L^{-1}(\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Moreover, as in
[
11
, Sect.4.2, formula (4.22)]
, if
i_{x}:\{x\}\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion map, then for any
a\in\widehat{H}^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})_{L}
and
b\in\widehat{H}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})_{L}
, we have:

(A.8)

pr_{x}(a\cap b)=i_{x}^{*}(a)\cap pr_{x}(b).

We then have the following result from
[
11
, Sect.4.2, Prop.4.19]
, adapted to the notations of our setting.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
X=X_{P}
be the projective toric variety associated to the full-dimensional lattice polytope
P\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, with ample Cartier divisor
D=D_{P}
. Then for
{\mathcal{F}}
a
{\mathbb{T}}
-coherent sheaf on
X
, the cohomological Euler characteristic
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\otimes{\mathcal{F}})
can be calculated via localization at the
{\mathbb{T}}
-fixed points as:

(A.9)

\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\otimes{\mathcal{F}})=\sum_{v\ {\rm vertex\ of}\ P}pr_{x_{v}}({\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}([{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\otimes{\mathcal{F}}]),

with each summand

(A.10)

pr_{x_{v}}({\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}([{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\otimes{\mathcal{F}}])\in L^{-1}(\Lambda^{an}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}},

where
x_{v}
is the
{\mathbb{T}}
-fixed point of
X
corresponding to the vertex
v
of
P
, and
{\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}:K^{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(X)\longrightarrow\widehat{H}^{\mathbb{T}}_{2*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})
is the equivariant Riemann–Roch map of Edidin–Graham
[
17
]
(see also
[
9
]
).

Remark A.3
.

The above homological localization formula is in fact a consequence of a similar localization formula in equivariant
K
-theory, using the fact that the equivariant Chern character induces a ring homomorphism of localized rings

{\rm ch}^{\mathbb{T}}:S^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\simeq S^{-1}K^{0}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\longrightarrow L^{-1}(\Lambda^{an}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}},

with
S
the multiplicative system generated by elements
1-\chi^{m}
for
0\neq m\in M
. Note that for a homogeneous polynomial function
\varphi:M_{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, this property does not hold for the corresponding group homomorphisms
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}
and
E^{\varphi}
as defined in the Introduction. For this reason, we use exponential sums and their Taylor expansions (as in
[
10
]
), as we shall explain below.
∎

Recall the identification

s:Sym_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}(M)\simeq H_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(pt;\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$})=:(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mbox{$\mathbb{Q}$}}
.
Let us fix
z\in N_{\mathbb{C}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{C}}={\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{R}}(M_{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}})
. By the universal property of
Sym
,
z
induces an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra homomorphism

\langle-,z\rangle:Sym_{\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}}(M)\to{\mathbb{C}}\>,

by which we can view
\langle p,z\rangle
for
z
now variable and
p\in Sym_{\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}}(M)
, resp.,
p\in(Sym_{\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}}(M))^{an}
fixed,
as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued polynomial on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, resp., as a convergent power series function around zero in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
A.1
.

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

From the geometric viewpoint, it is more natural to prove the assertion for
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,f}(\ell,y)
.

By applying the Chern character
{\rm ch}^{\mathbb{T}}
to formula (
6.14
), we obtain its cohomological counterpart as follows:

(A.11)

\begin{split}\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}e^{s(m)}&=\chi^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}(X,\ell D;[{\mathcal{M}}])\\
&=\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(X;\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D))\\
&\overset{\eqref{eqec}}{=}\sum_{v\ {\rm vertex}}pr_{x_{v}}({\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell D)))\\
&\overset{(*)}{=}\sum_{v\ {\rm vertex}}pr_{x_{v}}(e^{\ell[D]}\cap{\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])))\\
&\overset{\eqref{proj}}{=}\sum_{v\ {\rm vertex}}e^{\ell\cdot i_{{x_{v}}}^{*}[D]}\cap pr_{x_{v}}({\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}]))),\end{split}

where
i_{{x_{v}}}:\{x_{v}\}\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion of the
{\mathbb{T}}
-fixed point of
X
corresponding to the vertex
v
of
P
,
with
f_{Q}(y)=\chi_{y}(i_{x_{Q}}^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}])
, and
(*)
makes use of the module property for
{\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}
.

Applying
\langle-,z\rangle
to both sides of (
A.11
), we obtain

(A.12)

\begin{split}\sum_{Q\preceq P}f_{Q}(y)\cdot(1+y)^{\dim(Q)}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\rm Relint}({{\ell}}Q)\cap M}e^{\langle m,z\rangle}&=\sum_{v\ {\rm vertex}}e^{\langle\ell\cdot i_{{x_{v}}}^{*}[D],z\rangle}\cdot\langle pr_{x_{v}}({\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}]))),z\rangle.\end{split}

Let us now fix a vertex
v
of
P
. By (
A.10
), one also has that

pr_{x_{v}}({\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}])))\in L^{-1}(\Lambda^{an}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}}[y^{\pm 1}],

since
e^{\ell\cdot i_{{x_{v}}}^{*}[D]}\in(\Lambda^{an}_{\mathbb{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
is invertible. Then
\langle pr_{x_{v}}({\rm td}_{*}^{\mathbb{T}}(\hbox{\rm DR}^{\mathbb{T}}_{y}([{\mathcal{M}}]))),z\rangle
is a finite linear combination over
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
of terms of the form
\frac{h(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\langle m_{i},z\rangle}
, with
0\neq m_{i}\in M
and
h
analytic in
z
near
0
.

As in
[
10
, Prop.4.1]
, we next replace
z
by
tz
in (
A.12
), with
0\neq t\in{\mathbb{R}}
small and
z
generic (in the sense that
\langle m_{i},z\rangle\neq 0
,
i=1,\ldots k
), and consider the expansion into Laurent series in
t
on both sides of the equality.
We then obtain exactly as in
[
10
, Prop.4.1]
the polynomial behavior in
\ell
of
\widetilde{E}^{\varphi}_{P,{\mathcal{M}}}(\ell,y)
for the polynomial function
\varphi(m)=\langle m,z\rangle^{r}
, for any given non-negative integer
r
and
z
outside a finite number of hyperplanes. This then implies the statement for any polynomial
\varphi(m)
.
∎

Remark A.4
.

If we want to estimate the degree in
\ell
of the generalized weighted Ehrhart polynomial in Theorem
A.1
to be at most
deg(\varphi)+d
for some
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, we need to control uniformly the number
k\leq d
of linear factors appearing in the above localization. In fact, we can choose
d=n=\dim(P)
, as we will now explain. By linearity over
{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}]
and using the previous notations, it suffices to work with
{\mathcal{M}}=(j_{Q})_{!}{\mathbb{Q}}^{H}_{O_{\sigma_{Q}}}
, for all faces
Q
of
P
. By using a resolution of singularities of the orbit closure and functoriality, one can further reduce to the case of a smooth projective toric variety
X^{\prime}
of dimension at most
n^{\prime}\leq n
. In this smooth context, a more precise formula for the homological localization
pr_{x}
to a torus fixed point
x
is available via equivariant Poincaré duality as (e.g., see
[
11
, Sect.4.2]
):

pr_{x}=\frac{i_{x}^{*}}{c_{n^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{T}}(N_{x})},

with
i_{x}:\{x\}\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
the inclusion map and
c_{n^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{T}}(N_{x})
the top equivariant Chern class of the normal bundle
N_{x}
of
x
in
X^{\prime}
. Taking a character decomposition of
N_{x}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}{\mathbb{C}}_{\chi^{m_{i}}}
, we get
c_{n^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{T}}(N_{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}c(m_{i})
. ∎

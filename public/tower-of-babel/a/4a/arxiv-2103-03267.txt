Currents, charges and algebras in exceptional generalised geometry


Abstract
A classical E d ⁡ ( d ) E_{d(d)} -invariant Hamiltonian formulation of world-volume theories of half-BPS p p -branes in type IIb and eleven-dimensional supergravity is proposed, extending known results to d ≤ 6 d\leq 6 . It consists of a Hamiltonian, characterised by a generalised metric, and a current algebra constructed s.t. it reproduces the E d ⁡ ( d ) E_{d(d)} generalised Lie derivative. E d ⁡ ( d ) E_{d(d)} -covariance necessitates the introduction of so-called charges, specifying the type of p p -brane and the choice of section. For p > 2 p>2 , currents of p p -branes are generically non-geometric due to the imposition of U U -duality, e.g. the M5-currents contain coordinates associated to the M2-momentum. A derivation of the E d ⁡ ( d ) E_{d(d)} -invariant current algebra from a canonical Poisson structure is in general not possible. At most, one can derive a current algebra associated to para-Hermitian exceptional geometry. The membrane in the SL ( 5 ) (5) -theory is studied in detail. It is shown that in a generalised frame the current algebra is twisted by the generalised fluxes. As a consistency check, the double dimensional reduction from membranes in M-theory to strings in type IIa string theory is performed. Many features generalise to p p -branes in SL ( p + 3 ) (p+3) generalised geometries that form building blocks for the E d ⁡ ( d ) E_{d(d)} -invariant currents.

1.1
The string case

The classical dynamics of a string coupling to the NS-NS part of an arbitrary supergravity background can be described by the Hamiltonian

H=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathcal{H}^{IJ}\big(G(x(\sigma)),B(x(\sigma))\big)\mathbf{E}_{I}(\sigma)\mathbf{E}_{J}(\sigma),

(1.1)

where the embedding of the string into target space
x(\sigma)
and the canonical momentum
p(\sigma)
are collected in
\mathbf{E}_{M}(\sigma)=(p_{\mu}(\sigma),\partial x^{\mu}(\sigma))
, from now called
current
.
\sigma
is the spatial world-volume coordinate,
\partial=\partial_{\sigma}
.
\mathcal{H}
is the generalised metric, encoding the
\sigma
-model couplings, metric
G
and the
B
-field, in the Hamiltonian and the indices
I,J,K,...
are raised and lowered by the O
(d,d)
-metric
\eta
:

\mathcal{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}G-BG^{-1}B&BG^{-1}\\
-G^{-1}B&G^{-1}\end{array}\right),\qquad\eta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mathbb{1}\\
\mathbb{1}&0\end{array}\right).

(1.2)

The Virasoro constraints take the form
\mathcal{H}^{IJ}\mathbf{E}_{I}\mathbf{E}_{J}=0=\eta^{IJ}\mathbf{E}_{I}\mathbf{E}_{J}.

Current algebra and generalised Lie brackets.

For a Hamiltonian description we still need to specify the Poisson brackets of the currents
\mathbf{E}_{I}
– the
current algebra
. In this article, we often discuss two types of current algebra brackets, the
Dorfman current algebra

\left\{\mathbf{E}_{I}(\sigma),\mathbf{E}_{J}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}_{D}=\eta_{IJ}\partial\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

(1.3)

and the
Lie current algebra

\left\{\mathbf{E}_{I}(\sigma),\mathbf{E}_{J}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}_{L}=\eta_{IJ}\frac{1}{2}(\partial-\partial^{\prime})\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})+\omega_{IJ}\frac{1}{2}(\partial+\partial^{\prime})\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

(1.4)

where
\omega_{IJ}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\mathbb{1}\\
\mathbb{1}&0\end{array}\right)
. The difference between these current algebra brackets is a total world-sheet derivative term
\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\ \partial(...)
. Depending on the boundary conditions on
x(\sigma)
such total derivative terms could be neglected, they correspond to boundary contributions for the open string and winding contributions for the closed string. They have been shown to be important on the quantum level
[
67
]
and, on the level of action, the difference between the O
(d,d)
-invariant version and the standard non-linear
\sigma
-model is a topological term
[
2
,
68
,
69
]
.

As functions on the phase space we consider functionals

\phi[x]=-\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\phi^{I}\big(x(\sigma)\big)\mathbf{E}_{I}(\sigma)\quad\in(T\oplus T^{\star})LM.

(1.5)

The Poisson brackets of these reproduce the generalised Lie derivative of O
(d,d)
generalised geometry for (
1.3
)
respectively the canonical Lie bracket for (
1.4

\left\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\right\}_{D}=[v_{1},v_{2}]+\mathcal{L}_{v_{1}}\xi_{2}-\mathrm{d}(\iota_{v_{2}}\xi_{1}),\qquad\left\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\right\}_{L}=[v_{1},v_{2}]+\mathcal{L}_{v_{1}}\xi_{2}-\mathcal{L}_{v_{2}}\xi_{1}

(1.6)

with

Deriving the current algebra.

In a manifestly O
(d,d)
-covariant way, the current is defined as
\mathbf{E}_{I}=\eta_{IJ}\partial X^{J}
for extended coordinates
X^{M}=(x^{\mu},\tilde{x}_{\mu})
. The
current algebra
can be obtained
non-covariantly
by the identification
p_{\mu}=\partial\tilde{x}_{\mu}
, as above, and applying the canonical Poisson brackets

\displaystyle\left\{p_{\mu}(\sigma),x^{\nu}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}=-\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}),\quad\left\{p_{\mu}(\sigma),p_{\nu}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}=\left\{x^{\mu}(\sigma),x^{\nu}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}=0.

(1.7)

The O
(d,d)
-covariant way to derive the current algebra is as Dirac brackets
[
70
,
52
]
for the constraints

\bm{\Phi}_{I}=\mathbf{P}_{I}-\mathbf{E}_{I}=\mathbf{P}_{I}-\eta_{IJ}\partial X^{J}

(1.8)

on an
extended

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{P}_{M}(\sigma),X^{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}=-c\delta_{M}^{N}\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}),\quad\left\{\mathbf{P}_{M}(\sigma),\mathbf{P}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}=\left\{X^{M}(\sigma),X^{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}=0.

(1.9)

The Dirac procedure is necessary, because the constraints
\bm{\Phi}_{M}
are second class, i.e.

\left\{\bm{\Phi}_{M}(\sigma),\bm{\Phi}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}=C_{MN}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=-2c\eta_{MN}\partial\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

(1.10)

is invertible. The resulting Dirac brackets for the currents
\mathbf{E}
are

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{E}_{M}(\sigma),\mathbf{E}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}_{D.B.}

\displaystyle=-\int\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\prime\prime}\int\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\prime\prime\prime}\left\{\mathbf{E}_{M}(\sigma),\bm{\Phi}_{K}(\sigma^{\prime\prime})\right\}(C^{-1})^{KL}(\sigma^{\prime\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime\prime})\left\{\bm{\Phi}_{L}(\sigma^{\prime\prime\prime}),\mathbf{E}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2}\eta_{MN}\delta\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}).

(1.11)

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{E}_{M}(\sigma),X^{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}_{D.B}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c}{2}\delta^{N}_{M}\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}).

(1.12)

\left\{\mathbf{E}_{M}(\sigma),\mathbf{E}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}_{D.B.}
coincides with (
1.3
) for
c=2
. As will be important later, the Dirac procedure naturally constructs a Lie bracket (skewsymmetric and satisfying Jacobi identity) but the calculation seems to work only
when neglecting total spatial world-sheet derivative terms
. Hence, for the string one can derive the Dorfman current algebra (
1.3
) as a Dirac bracket up to such terms.

Generalised flux frame.

A different choice of basis for the currents
\mathbf{E}_{A}={E_{A}}^{I}\mathbf{E}_{I}
with

O
(d,d)

, leads to a diagonalisation of the Hamiltonian

and a twist of the current algebra

\left\{\mathbf{E}_{A}(\sigma),\mathbf{E}_{B}(\sigma^{\prime})\right\}_{D}=\eta_{AB}\partial\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})-{\mathbf{F}^{C}}_{AB}(\sigma)\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

(1.13)

by the generalised fluxes
\mathbf{F}_{ABC}=\left(\partial_{[A}{E_{B}}^{I}\right)E_{C]I}
with
\partial_{A}={E_{A}}^{M}\partial_{M}={E_{A}}^{M}(\partial_{\mu},0)
. In case of a generalised parallelisable background the

can be chosen to be constant. Then, this Hamiltonian formulation takes the form of an
\mathcal{E}
-model.

The non-geometry of the currents.

As already noted previously
[

,

]

but also
\mathcal{Q}^{\mu}

E_{d(d)}
-covariance.

In
[
46
,
49
]
and, from a current algebra approach for the M5-brane,
[

]
this was done by introducing the gauge fields, that typical for M5-brane actions
[

non-geometric coordinates
, that are associated to the momenta of the lower dimensional branes. In the example of the NS/D5-brane (
3.50

\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}^{m}_{\underline{\mu}_{4}}
and so on. Hence, one interpretation of the extended coordinates could be to account for these d.o.f.s.

Consequentially, from the point of view of such a higher brane, whose phase space coordinate normally just would be
x^{\mu}
and
p_{\mu}=Z_{\mu}
, the currents itself already are non-geometric as also the additional non-geometric coordinates
\tilde{x}
appear inevitably. Finally, such terms are logical to appear if one strives for an
E_{d(d)}
-covariant setup: As for example the M2- and M5-brane or the D-branes are connected by duality, in general M5 currents can’t appear alone in such a setup. Another way to understand that fact is, that in the generalised Lie derivative for
d>4
(in the M-theory picture) a ’coupling’ of two-forms appears:
[\omega_{1},\omega_{2}]_{D}=\omega_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}\omega_{2}
for two-forms
\omega_{i}
. Hence, the current algebra bracket of two M2-currents forms an M5-current, necessitating that in presence of an M5-current also M2-currents have to be present. Interestingly, the converse does not seem to be true: one can formulate an M2-brane current algebra, by consistently setting the (canonical) M5-current to zero.

Deriving the current algebra?

As the generalised Lie derivative for
E_{d(d)}

is not equivalent to the standard Lie derivative up to total derivatives,
one cannot hope to derive this current algebra

p

-invariant Dirac bracket approach lead to a non-Lie bracket. The only reason, why one could make it work in the previous cases, is that there the difference is always only a total-derivative term on the spatial world-volume, that would not contribute classically.

It is possible to derive a para-Hermitian

Outlook

There are several conceptual questions left open in this article. The main result, the

Appendix A
Conventions and identities

We define the generalised Kronecker symbols as follows:

\epsilon^{i_{1}...i_{k}j_{1}...j_{n-k}}\epsilon_{i_{1}...i_{k}k_{1}...k_{n-k}}=k!\ \delta^{j_{1}...j_{n-k}}_{k_{1}...k_{n-k}}=k!\ (n-k)!\ \delta^{j_{1}}_{[k_{1}}...\delta^{j_{n-k}}_{k_{n-k}]}

The standard
\delta
-distribution
f(\sigma)=\int\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\prime}f(\sigma^{\prime})\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

behaves in a maybe unexpected way, when applied to functions on compact spaces. In particular
(\partial_{1}+\partial_{2})\delta(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})\neq 0
,
but instead

\int\mathrm{d}\sigma_{1}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{2}\ \phi(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})(\partial_{1}+\partial_{2})\delta(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})=\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\ \partial\left(\phi(\sigma,\sigma)\right).

(A.1)

As discussed in the main text, this term is not vanishing in general. Strings and membranes can have non-trivial winding around a non-trivial cycle in target space. In that case, the coordinate fields
x(\sigma)
are not smooth, such that in particular
\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\ \partial x(\sigma)\neq 0
. For open world-volumes there are boundary contributions, as well.

In many calculations it is helpful to write

\delta(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})=\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2}),

(A.2)

to see for example that
(\partial_{1}+\partial_{2})\delta(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})=\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\partial\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2}).
The following distributional identities follow:

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\sigma f(\sigma)(\partial_{1}+...+\partial_{n})\big(\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\cdot...\cdot\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{n})\big)

\displaystyle{}\qquad=\int\mathrm{d}\sigma\ \big((\partial f(\sigma))+(n-1)f(\sigma)\partial\big)\big(\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\cdot...\cdot\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{n})\big)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e(\sigma_{1})\cdot e^{-1}(\sigma_{2})(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})\big(\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2})\big)

(A.3)

\displaystyle{}\qquad=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})\big(\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2})\big)\mathbbm{1}-\big((\partial e)\cdot e^{-1}\big)(\sigma)\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle\big(f(\sigma_{2})\partial_{1}+f(\sigma_{1})\partial_{2}\big)\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle{}\qquad=\big(\partial f(\sigma)\big)\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2})+\partial\big(f(\sigma)\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{2})\big)

for arbitrary (matrix-valued) functions
e
and
f
which hold without any additional boundary terms.

The higher world-volume currents are written directly as forms (on the spatial world-volume). Typical important identities are

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}(\sigma)\wedge\mathrm{d}\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}(\sigma^{\prime})\wedge\mathrm{d}\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\omega(\sigma^{\prime})\wedge\mathrm{d}\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\omega(\sigma)\wedge\mathrm{d}\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})+(\mathrm{d}\omega)(\sigma)\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\quad\text{for $(p-1)$-forms }\omega.

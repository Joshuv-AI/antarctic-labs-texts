Asymptotic analysis of k k -hop connectivity in the 1D unit disk random graph model
Nicolas Privault

Abstract
We propose an algorithm for the closed-form recursive computation of joint moments and cumulants of all orders for k k -hop counts in the 1D unit disk random graph model with Poisson distributed vertices. Our approach uses decompositions of k k -hop counts into multiple Poisson stochastic integrals. As a consequence, using the Stein method we derive Berry-Esseen bounds for the asymptotic convergence of renormalized k k -hop path counts to the normal distribution as the density of Poisson vertices tends to infinity. Computer codes for the recursive symbolic computation of moments and cumulants are provided in appendix.

Set partitions, moments, cumulants, and Möbius inversion

This section gathers some preliminary facts
on the relationships between joint moments, cumulants, and sums over partitions
that will be useful in the sequel.
We let
\Pi[n]
denote the set of partitions of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
and given a symmetric function
f(\rho)=f(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{l})
where

\pi=\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{l}\}\in\Pi[n]
is a partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}

of size
n\geq 1
we will use the notation

\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[n]}f(\pi)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}}f(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{l}).

We will also use the Möbius transform
\widehat{G}
of a function
G
on
partitions
\pi
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, defined as

\widehat{G}(\sigma):=\sum_{\pi\preceq\sigma}G(\pi),\hskip 20.00003pt\sigma\in\Pi[n],

(1.2)

where the sum (
1.2
) runs over all partitions
\pi
of

\{1,\ldots,n\}
that are finer than
\sigma
.
The Möbius inversion formula,
see e.g.
[
Rot75
]
or § 2.5 of
[
PT11
]
,
states that the function
G
in (
1.2
)
can be recovered from its Möbius transform

\widehat{G}
as

G(\pi)=\sum_{\sigma\preceq\pi}\mu(\sigma,\pi)\widehat{G}(\sigma),

(1.3)

where
\mu(\sigma,\pi)

is the Möbius function, with

\mu(\sigma,\widehat{\bf 1})=(|\sigma|-1)!(-1)^{|\sigma|}
,
where
|\sigma|
denotes the cardinality of the
block
\sigma\in\Pi[n]
and

\widehat{\bf 1}:=\{\{1,\ldots,n\}\}
is
the one-block partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
By (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) we also have the relation

G(\pi)=\sum_{\sigma\preceq\pi}\mu(\sigma,\pi)\sum_{\eta\preceq\sigma}G(\eta)=\sum_{\eta\preceq\sigma\preceq\pi}\mu(\sigma,\pi)G(\eta),\hskip 20.00003pt\pi\in\Pi[n].

(1.4)

Given
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
a random vector,
the joint cumulants of order
(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})

are the coefficients

\kappa\big(X_{1}^{l_{1}};\ldots;X_{n}^{l_{n}}\big)

appearing in the log-moment generating (MGF) expansion

\log\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits\big[\mathrm{e}^{t_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+t_{n}X_{n}}\big]=\sum_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\geq 1}\frac{t^{l_{1}}_{1}\cdots t^{l_{n}}_{n}}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{n}!}\kappa\big(X_{1}^{l_{1}};\ldots;X_{n}^{l_{n}}\big),

for
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
in a neighborhood of zero in
{\mathord{{\rm I\kern-3.0ptR}}}^{n}
.
The joint moments of
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})

are given from its cumulants by the joint moment-cumulant relation

\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits[X_{1}\cdots X_{n}]=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[n]}\prod_{A\in\pi}\kappa\big((X_{i})_{i\in A}\big)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\kappa\big((X_{i})_{i\in\pi_{j}}\big),

see Theorem 1 in
[
Luk55
]
or Relation (2.9) in
[
McC87
]
.
The Möbius inversion relation (
1.3
) also allows us to
recover joint cumulants from joint moments
as

\displaystyle\kappa(X_{1};\ldots;X_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Pi[n]}\mu(\sigma,\widehat{\bf 1})\prod_{A\in\sigma}\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits\Bigg[\prod_{i\in A}X_{i}\Bigg]

(1.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits\Bigg[\prod_{i\in\pi_{j}}X_{i}\Bigg],

see Theorem 1 of
[
Luk55
]

or Corollary 5.1.6 in
[
Sta99
]
.
In particular,
the cumulant of order
n\geq 1
of a Poisson distributed random
variable
X
is its intensity is
\lambda>0
for all
n\geq 1
, with

\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits[X^{n}]=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\lambda^{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}S(n,l)\lambda^{l}

where

S(n,l)
the Stirling number of the second kind,
1\leq l\leq n
.
For
\lambda=1
this yields the Bell number

B_{n}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}S(n,l)

which is the number of partitions of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, i.e. the
cardinality of
\Pi[n]
.

Multiple stochastic integral representation of
k
-hop counts

In the sequel we use the notations

u\wedge v:=\min(u,v)
and
u\vee v:=\max(u,v)
,
u,v\geq 0
.
Our approach to the recursive computation of moments and cumulants
relies on the following stochastic integral representation of

k
-hop counts with respect to the Poisson process
(N_{t})_{t\in{\mathord{{\rm I\kern-3.0ptR}}}_{+}}

with intensity (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.1

Let
k\geq 2
.
The number of
k
-hops joining
0
to
t\in[0,kr]
can be written as
the (non-compensated) multiple Poisson stochastic integral

\sigma_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}f_{k}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k-1})dN_{s_{1}}\cdots dN_{s_{k-1}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,kr],

(2.1)

where
f_{k}
is the function of
k-1
variables defined as

f_{k}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k-1}):=\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}{\bf 1}_{\{s_{l+1}<s_{l}+r\}},

(2.2)

s_{1},\ldots,s_{k-1}\in[0,t]
, with
s_{0}:=0
and
s_{k}:=t
.

Proof.

When
k=2
we note that if a node is present at
s\in[0,r]
it
connects to every node inside
[0,s)
, and
therefore it generates
\sigma_{1}(s^{-})
new nodes,
where
\sigma_{1}(s^{-})
denotes the almost sure left limit

\sigma_{1}(s^{-}):=\lim_{u\nearrow s}\sigma_{1}(u)
.
When
s\in(r,2r]
, if a node is present at
s\in[0,r]

then
the count of
1
-hops up linking
0
to
s-r

has to be deducted, which yields the evolution

d\sigma_{2}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\bf 1}_{[0,r]}(s)\sigma_{1}(s^{-})dN_{s},\quad s\in[0,r],\\
\\
-{\bf 1}_{[r,2r]}(s)\sigma_{1}(s^{-}-r)dN_{s-r},\quad s\in(r,2r],\end{array}\right.

where
(T_{n})_{n\geq 1}

denotes the jump times sequence of
(N_{t})_{t\in[0,2r]}
.
More generally, applying this argument by iterations to any
k\geq 3

leads to the system of jump stochastic differential equations

d\sigma_{k}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\bf 1}_{[0,(k-1)r]}(s)\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s},\quad s\in[0,(k-1)r],\\
\\
-{\bf 1}_{[r,kr]}(s)\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-}-r)dN_{s-r},\quad s\in((k-1)r,kr],\end{array}\right.

or

d\sigma_{k}(s)={\bf 1}_{[0,(k-1)r]}(s)\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s}-{\bf 1}_{[r,kr]}(s)\sigma^{(k-1)}_{s^{-}-r}dN_{s-r},\hskip 10.00002pts\in[0,kr],

hence the recurrence relation

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{((k-1)r)\wedge t}\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s}-\int_{r}^{r\vee t}\sigma^{(k-1)}_{s^{-}-r}dN_{s-r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{((k-1)r)\wedge t}\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s}-\int_{0}^{0\vee(t-r)}\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0\vee(t-r)}^{((k-1)r)\wedge t}\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{t-r}^{(k-1)r}\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s},\quad t\in[(k-1)r,kr],\\
\\
\displaystyle\int_{t-r}^{t}\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s},\qquad t\in[r,(k-1)r],\\
\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k-1}(s^{-})dN_{s},\qquad t\in[0,r],\end{array}\right.

for
k\geq 2
.
Finally, by induction we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0\vee(t-r)}^{((k-1)r)\wedge t}\int_{0\vee(s_{k-1}^{-}-r)}^{((k-2)r)\wedge s_{k-1}^{-}}\cdots\int_{0\vee(s_{2}^{-}-r)}^{r\wedge s_{2}^{-}}dN_{s_{1}}\cdots dN_{s_{k-1}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0\vee(t-r)}^{((k-1)r)\wedge t}\int_{0\vee(s_{k-1}^{-}-r)}^{((k-2)r)\wedge t}\cdots\int_{0\vee(s_{2}^{-}-r)}^{r\wedge t}dN_{s_{1}}\cdots dN_{s_{k-1}},

and we conclude by letting

f_{k}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k-1}):={\bf 1}_{\{t-r<s_{k-1}<(k-1)r\}}{\bf 1}_{\{s_{k-1}-r<s_{k-2}<(k-2)r\}}\cdots{\bf 1}_{\{s_{2}-r<s_{1}<r\}},

s_{1},\ldots,s_{k-1}\in[0,t]
.

\square

In particular, the
2
-hop count is given by

\sigma_{2}(t)=\int_{0\vee(t-r)}^{r\wedge t}dN_{s}=N_{t}{\bf 1}_{[0,r]}(t)+(N_{r}-N_{t-r}){\bf 1}_{[r,2r]}(t).

(2.5)

In the case of
3
-hops, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}(t)=\int_{0\vee(t-r)}^{(2r)\wedge t}\sigma_{2}(s^{-})dN_{s}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{t-r}^{2r}\sigma_{2}(s^{-})dN_{s}=\int_{t-r}^{2r}\int_{s^{-}-r}^{s^{-}}dN_{u}dN_{s}=\int_{t-r}^{2r}(N_{s^{-}}-N_{s^{-}-r})dN_{s},\quad t\in[2r,3r],\\
\\
\displaystyle\int_{t-r}^{t}\sigma_{2}(s^{-})dN_{s}=\int_{t-r}^{t}N_{s^{-}}dN_{s}-\int_{r}^{t}N_{s^{-}-r}dN_{s}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ =\frac{1}{2}(N_{t}-1)N_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(N_{t-r}-1)N_{t-r}-\sum_{l=1+N_{r}}^{N_{t}}N_{T_{l}^{-}-r},\quad t\in[r,2r],\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}N_{s^{-}}dN_{s}=\frac{1}{2}(N_{t}-1)N_{t},\qquad t\in[0,r].\end{array}\right.

More generally, when
t\in[(l-1)r,lr]
for some
l\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
,
by (
2.4
) we have

\sigma_{k}(t)=\int_{0\vee(t-r)}^{t}\int_{0\vee(s_{k-1}^{-}-r)}^{t}\cdots\int_{0\vee(s_{l+1}^{-}-r)}^{t}\int_{s_{l}^{-}-r}^{(l-1)r}\cdots\int_{s_{2}^{-}-r}^{r}dN_{s_{1}}\cdots dN_{s_{k-1}},

hence the identity in distribution

\sigma_{k}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\simeq}}\int_{r\vee t}^{t+r}\int_{0\vee(s_{k-1}^{-}-r)}^{t+r}\cdots\int_{0\vee(s_{l+1}^{-}-r)}^{t+r}\int_{s_{l}^{-}-r}^{lr}\int_{s_{l-1}^{-}-r}^{(l-1)r}\cdots\int_{s_{2}^{-}-r}^{2r}dN_{s_{1}}\cdots dN_{s_{k-1}},

where we let
s_{k}:=t
.
The multiple compensated Poisson stochastic integral of order

n\geq 1
of a deterministic symmetric function

f_{n}\in L^{2}({\mathord{{\rm I\kern-3.0ptR}}}_{+}^{n},\lambda^{\otimes n})

is defined
by

I_{n}(f_{n}):=n!\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{2}}{f}_{n}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})d(N_{t_{1}}-\lambda(dt_{1}))\cdots d(N_{t_{n}}-\lambda(dt_{n})),

with the isometry property

\mathbb{E}[(I_{n}(f_{n}))^{2}]=(n!)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{2}}f_{n}^{2}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\lambda(dt_{1})\cdots\lambda(dt_{n}),\hskip 10.00002ptn\geq 1,

(2.6)

see e.g. Propositions 2.7.1 and 6.2.4 in
[
Pri09
]
,
and references therein.
The next corollary of Proposition
2.1

gives the chaos decomposition of
k
-hop counts
in terms of multiple Poisson stochastic integrals.

Corollary 2.2

For any
t\in[0,kr]
, the number
\sigma_{k}(t)
of
k
-hops linking
0
to
t
can be
represented as the sum of multiple
compensated Poisson stochastic integrals

\sigma_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}{k-1\choose l}I_{l}\left({\bf 1}_{\{*\in[0,t]^{l}\}}\int_{0}^{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{f}_{k}(*,s_{l+1},\ldots,s_{k-1})ds_{l+1}\cdots ds_{k-1}\right),

(2.7)

where
\widetilde{f}_{k}
is the symmetrization in
k-1
variables of the function

f_{k}
defined in (
2.2
).

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
Proposition
2.1
and the binomial theorem applied
to
(dN_{t_{1}}-\lambda(dt_{1}))\cdots(dN_{t_{k-1}}-\lambda(dt_{k-1}))
.

\square

Single node per cell

In the case where

[(l-1)r,lr]

Multiple Poisson stochastic integral expression

In particular, when

Proposition 3.1

Let

For

\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\simeq}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}^{-}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{2}^{-}}dN^{(1)}_{s_{1}}\cdots dN^{(k-1)}_{s_{k-1}},

Proof.

When
t\in[(k-1)r,kr]
, by (

) we have the identity in distribution

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\simeq}}

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{kr}\int_{s_{k-1}^{-}-r}^{(k-1)r}\cdots\int_{s_{2}^{-}-r}^{2r}dN_{s_{1}}\cdots dN_{s_{k-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\int_{0}^{kr-t}\int_{r}^{r+u_{k-1}^{-}}\cdots\int_{(k-2)r}^{r+u_{2}^{-}}dN_{kr-u_{1}}\cdots dN_{kr-u_{k-1}}

\sigma_{k}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\simeq}}\int_{0}^{kr-t}\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}^{-}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{2}^{-}}dN^{(1)}_{s_{1}}\cdots dN^{(k-1)}_{s_{k-1}},\hskip 20.00003pt(k-1)r\leq t\leq kr.

\square

-Statistics formulation

As noted in e.g.

(5.4)

\square

Joint cumulants recursion

In the sequel, given

\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{l}\}
a partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}

and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}\in[0,r]
we denote by

c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}(\widebar{\tau}_{\pi_{1}};\ldots;\widebar{\tau}_{\pi_{l}}):=\kappa_{\lambda}\big((\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau_{1}))^{|\pi_{1}|},\ldots,(\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau_{l}))^{|\pi_{l}|}\big)

the joint cumulant
of
\big((\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau_{1}))^{|\pi_{1}|},\ldots,(\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau_{l}))^{|\pi_{l}|}\big)
, and we let

c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}(\tau_{1};\ldots;\tau_{n})

denote the joint cumulant

\kappa_{\lambda}(\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau_{1}),\ldots,\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau_{n}))
.
The following proposition is the counterpart of the moment recursion
of Proposition
5.1
obtained by the Möbius inversion relation
(
1.3
).
Particular cases are considered with explicit computations for
n=2,3,4
in
Appendix
D
.

Proposition 6.1

Let
\tau\in[0,r]
and
k,n\geq 1
.
The cumulant of order
n
of
\sigma_{k+1}(kr-\tau)

satisfies the recursion

c_{k+1,n}^{(\lambda)}(\tau_{1};\ldots;\tau_{n})=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\pi_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\pi_{l}}}c_{k,l}^{(\lambda)}(\widebar{s}_{\pi_{1}};\ldots;\widebar{s}_{\pi_{l}})\lambda_{k}(ds_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(ds_{l}),

(6.1)

where we let

\widehat{\tau}_{\pi}:=\min_{i\in\pi}\tau_{i}
for
\pi\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

When
k=1
we have
\sigma_{1}(\tau)=1
,
\tau\in[0,r]
,
hence
c_{1,n}^{(\lambda)}(\tau_{1};\ldots;\tau_{n})={\bf 1}_{\{n=1\}}
, and
by (
2.5
) the cumulants of the two-hop count

\sigma_{2}(2r-\tau)=N_{r}-N_{r-\tau}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\simeq}}N^{(1)}_{\tau}
,

\tau\in[0,r]
,
are the joint Poisson cumulants

c_{2,n}^{(\lambda)}(\tau_{1};\ldots;\tau_{n})=\min(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}),\hskip 20.00003ptn\geq 1,

(6.2)

which is consistent with the joint Poisson moments formula
(
5.1
) and shows that (
6.1
) holds at the rank
k=1
.
Next,
assuming that (
6.1
) holds at the rank
k\geq 1
,
by the joint cumulant-moment inversion relation (
1.5
)
we have

\displaystyle c_{k+1,n}^{(\lambda)}(\tau_{1};\ldots;\tau_{n})=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{q=1}^{l}m^{(\lambda)}_{k+1,|\pi_{q}|}(\tau_{\pi_{q}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{q=1}^{l}\sum_{\eta^{q}\preceq\pi_{q}}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{|\eta^{q}|}}}m^{(\lambda)}_{k,|\pi_{q}|}\big(\widebar{u}_{\eta^{q}_{1}};\ldots;\widebar{u}_{\eta^{q}_{|\eta^{q}|}}\big)\lambda_{k}(du_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(du_{|\eta^{q}|}),

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\sum_{\eta^{i}\preceq\pi_{i}\atop{1\leq i\leq l}}\prod_{q=1}^{l}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{|\eta^{q}|}}}m^{(\lambda)}_{k,|\eta^{q}|}\big(\widebar{u}_{\eta^{q}_{1}};\ldots;\widebar{u}_{\eta^{q}_{|\eta^{q}|}}\big)\lambda_{k}(du_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(du_{|\eta^{q}|}),

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\sum_{\eta^{i}\preceq\pi_{i}\atop{1\leq i\leq l}}\prod_{q=1}^{l}\sum_{\psi^{q}\preceq\eta^{q}}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{|\eta^{q}|}}}\prod_{m=1}^{|\psi^{q}|}c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}\big(\big(\widebar{u}_{\eta^{q}_{i}}\big)_{i\in\psi^{q}_{m}}\big)\lambda_{k}(du_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(du_{|\eta^{q}|})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\sum_{\psi^{i}\preceq\eta^{i}\preceq\pi_{i}\atop{1\leq i\leq l}}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta^{q}_{|\eta^{q}|}}}\prod_{m=1}^{|\psi^{q}|}\prod_{q=1}^{l}c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}\big(\big(\widebar{u}_{\eta^{q}_{i}}\big)_{i\in\psi^{q}_{m}}\big)\lambda_{k}(du_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(du_{|\psi^{q}|})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\mu(\sigma,\widehat{\bf 1})\sum_{\eta\preceq\sigma\preceq\widehat{\bf 1}}\sum_{\psi\preceq\eta}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\psi_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\psi_{|\psi|}}}\prod_{A\in\eta}c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}\big(\big(\widebar{u}_{A\cap B}\big)_{B\in\psi}\big)\lambda_{k}(du_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(du_{|\psi|})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\mu(\sigma,\widehat{\bf 1})\sum_{\eta\preceq\sigma\preceq\widehat{\bf 1}}G(\eta)

\displaystyle=G(\widehat{\bf 1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\preceq\widehat{\bf 1}}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\eta_{|\eta|}}}c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}(\widebar{u}_{\eta_{1}};\ldots;\widebar{u}_{\eta_{|\eta|}})\lambda_{k}(du_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(du_{|\eta|}),

where we applied Relation (
1.4
)
with
\pi:=\widehat{\bf 1}
and

G(\eta):=\sum_{\psi\preceq\eta}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\psi_{1}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}_{\psi_{|\psi|}}}\prod_{A\in\eta}c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}\big(\big(\widebar{u}_{A\cap B}\big)_{B\in\psi}\big)\lambda_{k}(du_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}(du_{|\psi|})

which shows (
6.1
) by induction on
k\geq 1
.

\square

Proposition 6.2

For any sequence
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n+1})
of random variables we have

\kappa(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}X_{n+1})=\kappa(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{n+1})+\sum_{\eta_{1}\cup\eta_{2}=\{1,\ldots,n+1\}\atop\eta_{1}\ni n,\ \!\eta_{2}\ni n+1,\ \!\eta_{1}\cap\eta_{2}=\emptyset}\kappa(X_{\eta_{1}},X_{n})\kappa(X_{\eta_{2}},X_{n+1}).

Proof.

This relation is a particular case of the cumulant-moment relationship

\kappa\big(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\big)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits\Bigg[\prod_{i\in\pi_{j}}Z_{i}\Bigg]

which yields, taking

,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\kappa(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}X_{n+1})=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits\Bigg[\prod_{i\in\pi_{j}}X_{i}\prod_{\pi_{j}\ni n}(X_{n}X_{n+1})\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(l-1)!(-1)^{l-1}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{p=1}^{|\pi_{j}|+1}\sum_{\eta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\eta_{p}=\pi_{j}\cup\{n,n+1\}}\prod_{v=1}^{p}\kappa\big((X_{u})_{u\in\eta_{p}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{n+1})+\sum_{\eta_{1}\cup\eta_{2}=\{1,\ldots,n+1\}\atop\eta_{1}\ni n,\ \!\eta_{2}\ni n+1,\ \!\eta_{1}\cap\eta_{2}=\emptyset}\kappa(X_{\eta_{1}},X_{n})\kappa(X_{\eta_{2}},X_{n+1}),

\square

7
Moment and cumulant bounds

In this section we take

\lambda_{l}(ds):=\lambda ds
,
l=1,\ldots,k
,

\lambda>0
,
and we write
f(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{n})
if there exist
C_{n}>0
and

\lambda_{n}>0
such that

|f(\lambda)|\leq C_{n}\lambda^{n}
for any
\lambda>\lambda_{n}
.

Proposition 7.1

Moment bound. For any
\tau\in[0,r]
and
n\geq 0
, we have

\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits_{\lambda}[(\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau))^{n}]\leq(\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits[(N_{\lambda\tau})^{n}])^{k-1}=O\big((\lambda\tau)^{(k-1)n}\big)

as
\lambda
tends to infinity.

Proof.

We show by induction on
k\geq 1
that

m^{(\lambda)}_{k,n}(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})\leq(\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits[(N_{\lambda\tau})^{n}])^{k-1},\hskip 20.00003pt0\leq\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}\leq\tau.

where,
denoting by

S(n,l)
the Stirling number of the second
kind,

\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits[(N_{\lambda\tau})^{n}]=\sum_{l=1}^{n}S(n,l)(\lambda\tau)^{l}=O(\lambda^{n}).

The case
k=1
is covered by the fact that

\sigma_{1}(r-\tau)=1
and
m^{(\lambda)}_{1,n}(\tau)=1
,
n\geq 0
.
Next, by the recurrence relation (
5.2
) we have

\displaystyle m^{(\lambda)}_{k+1,n}(\tau,\ldots,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(\lambda\tau)^{l}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\sup_{0\leq\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}\leq\tau}m^{(\lambda)}_{k,n}(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}\leq\tau}m^{(\lambda)}_{k,n}(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l})\sum_{l=1}^{n}S(n,l)(\lambda\tau)^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits[(N_{\lambda\tau})^{n}]m^{(\lambda)}_{k,n}(\tau,\ldots,\tau),\hskip 20.00003ptk\geq 1.

\square

The following bound on joint cumulants is also obtained by induction.

Proposition 7.2

For any
\tau\in[0,r]
,

k\geq 2
and
l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}\geq 0
,
p\geq 1
,
we have the joint cumulant bound

\kappa_{\lambda}\big((\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau))^{l_{1}},\ldots,(\sigma_{k}(kr-\tau))^{l_{p}}\big)\leq(2(\lambda+1)\tau)^{(k-1)(l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p})+1-p}(B_{n})^{k-2},

(7.1)

where
B_{n}
denotes the Bell number of order
n\geq 1
.
In particular, we have

c_{k,n}^{(\lambda)}(\tau;\ldots;\tau)\leq(2(\lambda+1)\tau)^{1+(k-2)n}(B_{n})^{k-2},\hskip 10.00002pt\tau\in[0,r].

(7.2)

Proof.

We note that for
n=p
, as in (
6.2
) we have

c_{2,n}^{(\lambda)}(\tau_{1};\ldots;\tau_{n})=\min(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})
,
hence by induction from Proposition
6.2
we obtain

\kappa_{\lambda}\big((\sigma_{2}(2r-\tau_{1}))^{l_{1}},\ldots,(\sigma_{2}(2r-\tau_{p}))^{l_{p}}\big)\leq(2(\lambda+1)\tau)^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}+1-p},

0\leq\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{p}\leq\tau
,
which is (
7.1
) for
k=2
.
Next, using Proposition
6.1
,
assuming that (
7.1
) holds at the rank
k\geq 2
,
by induction for
\lambda\geq 1

we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\big|\kappa_{\lambda}\big(\sigma_{k+1}((k+1)r-\tau),\ldots,\sigma_{k+1}((k+1)r-\tau)\big)\big|

(7.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}\lambda^{l}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\int_{[0,\tau]^{l}}|c_{k,l}^{(\lambda)}(\widebar{\tau}_{\pi_{1}};\ldots;\widebar{\tau}_{\pi_{l}})|d\tau_{1}\cdots d\tau_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B_{n})^{k-2}\sum_{l=1}^{n}(\lambda\tau)^{l}\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}(2(\lambda+1)\tau)^{(k-1)n+1-l}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(B_{n})^{k-2}(2(\lambda+1)\tau)^{(k-1)n+1}\sum_{l=1}^{n}S(n,l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B_{n})^{k-1}(2(\lambda+1)\tau)^{(k-1)n+1},

which yields

\kappa_{\lambda}\big((\sigma_{k+1}((k+1)r-\tau_{1}))^{l_{1}},\ldots,(\sigma_{k+1}((k+1)r-\tau_{p}))^{l_{p}}\big)\leq(B_{n})^{k-1}(2(\lambda+1)\tau)^{k(l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p})+1-p},

0\leq\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{p}\leq\tau
,
by induction from Proposition
6.2
.

\square

8

In this section we will use the Wasserstein and Kolmogorov distances

d_{W}(X,Y)
and
d_{K}(X,Y)

between the distributions of random variables
X,Y
, defined as

d_{W}(X,Y):=\sup_{h\in\mathrm{Lip}(1)}|\mathrm{E}[h(X)]-\mathrm{E}[h(Y)]|,

where
\mathrm{Lip}(1)
denotes the class of real-valued
Lipschitz functions with
Lipschitz constant less than or equal to
1
,
and

d_{K}(X,Y):=\sup_{x\in{\mathord{{\rm I\kern-3.0ptR}}}}|{\mathord{\mathbb{P}}}(X\leq x)-{\mathord{\mathbb{P}}}(Y\leq x)|.

For
\lambda>0
and
t\in{\mathord{{\rm I\kern-3.0ptR}}}_{+}
we let

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{(\lambda)}(t):=\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\cdots\int_{0}^{\lambda t}f_{k}(s_{1}/\lambda,\ldots,s_{k-1}/\lambda)dN_{s_{1}}\cdots dN_{s_{k-1}}

(8.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\lambda^{k-1-l}{k-1\choose l}I_{l}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}f_{k-1}(*,s_{l+1},\ldots,s_{k-1})ds_{l+1}\cdots ds_{k-1}\right)

according to (
2.1
) and (
2.7
),
so that the distribution of
\sigma_{k}^{(\lambda)}(t)

under
{\mathord{\mathbb{P}}}
is the distribution of
\sigma_{k}(t)
under
the distribution
{\mathord{\mathbb{P}}}_{\lambda}
of the 1D unit disk graph
with constant Poisson intensity
\lambda>0
.

In addition, given
k\geq 2
and
t\in[(k-1)r,kr)
,
we consider the renormalized
k
-hop count

\widetilde{\sigma}_{k}^{(\lambda)}(t):=\frac{\sigma_{k}^{(\lambda)}(t)-\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits_{\lambda}[\sigma_{k}(t)]}{\sqrt{{\mathrm{{\rm Var}}}_{\lambda}[\sigma_{k}(t)]}}.

From Proposition
7.2
, for any
t\in[(k-1)r,kr)
, the skewness
of

tends to infinity.

Proof.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits_{\lambda}\big[\big(\widetilde{\sigma}_{k}^{(\lambda)}(t)\big)^{4}\big]-3

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{k,4}^{(\lambda)}(kr-t;kr-t;kr-t;kr-t)}{({\mathrm{{\rm Var}}}_{\lambda}[\sigma_{k}(t)])^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B_{4})^{k-2}O(\lambda^{-1}),

as
\lambda

)

k-1

of

Appendix B
Moments of Poisson stochastic integrals

In this section we review the background results on the
moments of Poisson stochastic integrals that are used in this paper.
Let
\omega(dx)
denote a Poisson point process
with intensity measure
\mu(dx)
on a measure space
X
.
The next proposition, see Proposition 3.1 in
[
Pri12
]

or Theorem 1 and Proposition 7 in
[
Pri16
]
,
provides a moment identity for Poisson stochastic integrals
with random integrands using sums over partitions.

Proposition B.1

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}:X\times\Omega\longrightarrow{\mathord{{\rm I\kern-3.0ptR}}}

be deterministic functions,
n\geq 1
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\hbox{\rm I\kern-1.99997ptE}}\nolimits\left[\int_{X}f_{1}(x)\omega(dx)\cdots\int_{X}f_{n}(x)\omega(dx)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}~\sum_{\pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{l}=\{1,\ldots,n\}}\int_{X^{l}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{l}\prod_{i\in\pi_{j}}f_{i}(x_{j},\xi)\right)\mu(dx_{1})\cdots\mu(dx_{l}),

where the sum runs over all partitions

\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{l}
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
of sizes
and
|\pi_{l}|
denotes the cardinality of each block
\pi_{l}
,

l=1,\ldots,n
.

Proposition
B.1
extends in the next Proposition
B.2

as a moment identity for the stochastic integral of functions

f(z_{1},\ldots z_{p})
of
p
variables

z_{1},\ldots z_{p}\in X^{p}
, see in Theorem 3.1 of
[
BRSW17
]
.
For this, let
\Pi[n\times p]
denote the set of partitions
of

[n\times p]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{1,\ldots,p\}=\big\{(i,j)\ :\ i=1,\ldots,n,\ j=1,\ldots,p\big\},

identified to
\{1,\ldots,np\}
, and let

\rho:=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{n})\in\Pi[n\times p]
denote the
partition made of the
n
blocks

\rho_{i}:=\{(i,1),\ldots,(i,p)\}
of size
p
, for

i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proposition B.2

For
f:X^{p}\times\Omega\longrightarrow{\mathord{{\rm I\kern-3.0ptR}}}
be
a sufficiently integrable function of

Sparse regularization of inverse problems by operator-adapted frame thresholding
Jürgen Frikel, Markus Haltmeier

Abstract
We analyze sparse frame based regularization of inverse problems by means of a diagonal frame decomposition (DFD) for the forward operator, which generalizes the SVD. The DFD allows to define a non-iterative (direct) operator-adapted frame thresholding approach which we show to provide a convergent regularization method with linear convergence rates. These results will be compared to the well-known analysis and synthesis variants of sparse ℓ 1 \ell^{1} -regularization which are usually implemented thorough iterative schemes. If the frame is a basis (non-redundant case), the three versions of sparse regularization, namely synthesis and analysis variants of ℓ 1 \ell^{1} -regularization as well as the DFD thresholding are equivalent. However, in the redundant case, those three approaches are pairwise different.



1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with inverse problems of the form

\bm{y}^{\delta}=\mathbf{A}\bm{x}+z\,,

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{A}\colon\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{A})\subseteq\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
is a linear operator between Hilbert spaces, and
z
denotes the data distortion (noise).
We allow unbounded operators and assume that
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{A})
is dense. Moreover, we assume that the unknown object
\bm{x}
is an element of a closed subspace space
\mathbb{X}_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{X}
on which
\mathbf{A}
is bounded.
We are particularly interested in problems, where (
1.1
)
is ill-posed in which case the solution of
(
1.1
) (if existent) is either not unique or the solution operator is not continuous (hence, the solution process is unstable with respect to data perturbations). In order to stabilize the inversion of (
1.1
) one has to apply regularization methods, cf.
[
11
,
23
]
. The basic idea of regularization is to include a-priori information about the unknown object into the solution process.

In this paper, we use sparsity based regularization, where the a-priori assumption on the unknown object is sparsity of
\bm{x}
with respect to a frame
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of
\mathbb{X}
, cf.
[
23
,
14
,
7
,
17
,
21
,
3
]
. That is, we regularize the recovery of
\bm{x}
from measurements (
1.1
) by enforcing sparsity of
\bm{x}
with respect to a suitably chosen frame of
\mathbb{X}
. Sparse regularization is well investigated and has been applied to many different imaging problems, and by now there are many algorithms available that implement sparse regularization. However, when dealing with frames, there are at least two fundamentally different concepts implementing sparsity, namely the synthesis and the analysis variant. The reason for this lies in the fact that expansions of
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{X}
with respect to frames are not unique (which is in contrast to basis expansions). In the synthesis variant, it is assumed that the unknown
is a sparse linear combination of frame elements, whereas, in the analysis variant, it is required that the inner products
\left\langle u_{\lambda},\bm{x}\right\rangle
with respect to a given frame are sparse.
The difference between these approaches has been pointed out clearly in
[
10
]
.

Sparse regularization is widely used in inverse problems as it provides good regularization results and is able to preserve or emphasize features (e.g., edges) in the reconstruction. However, this often comes at the price of speed, since most of the algorithms implementing sparse regularization are based on variational formulations that are solved by iterative schemes.

In the present paper, we investigate a third variant of sparse regularization that is based on an operator-adapted diagonal frame decomposition (DFD) of the unknown object, cf.
[
9
,
4
,
6
]
and which allows to define a direct (non-iterative) sparse regularization method. In the noise-free case (
z=0
), explicit reproducing formulas for the unknown object can be derived from the DFD, where the frame coefficients of
\bm{x}
are calculated directly from the data
\bm{y}=\mathbf{A}\bm{x}
. In the presence of noise (
z\neq 0
), regularized versions of those formulas are obtained by applying component-wise soft-thresholding to the calculated coefficients, where the soft-thresholding operator is defined as follows:

Definition 1.1

(Soft-thresholding)
.

Let
\Lambda
be some index set.

\blacksquare

For

\eta,d\in\mathbb{K}
let
\operatorname{soft}(\eta,d)\coloneqq\operatorname{sign}(\eta)\max{\left\{0,\left|\eta\right|-d\right\}}
.

\blacksquare

For
\bm{\eta},\bm{d}\in\mathbb{K}^{\Lambda}
we define
the component-wise soft-thresholding by

\mathbb{S}_{\bm{d}}(\bm{\eta})\coloneqq\left(\operatorname{soft}\left(\eta_{\lambda},d_{\lambda}\right)\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\,.

(1.2)

Here and below we define
\operatorname{sign}(\eta)\coloneqq\eta/\left|\eta\right|
for
\eta\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
and
\operatorname{sign}(0)\coloneqq 0
. The advantage of the DFD-variant of sparse regularization lies in the fact that it admits a non-iterative (direct) and fast implementation of sparse regularization that can be easily implemented for several inverse problems.

We point out, that the three variants of sparse regularization (mentioned above) are equivalent if orthonormal bases are used instead of frames, but they are fundamentally different in the redundant case.

As the main theoretical results in this paper we show that the third variant of sparse regularization, which we call

DFD-thresholding
, defines a convergent regularization method and we derive linear convergence rates for sparse solutions.
For the basis case, the same results follow from existing results of
\ell^{1}
-regularization
[
7
,
14
,
15
]
. In the redundant case, the results follow from
[
15
]
for the synthesis approach and from
[
16
]
for the analysis approach. In case of DFD-thresholding, we are not aware of any results concerning convergence analysis or convergence rates.

2
Diagonal frame decomposition

In this section, we introduce the concept of operator adapted diagonal frame decompositions (DFD) and discuss some classical examples of such DFDs in the case of the classical 2D Radon transform and the forward operator of photoacoustic tomography with a flat observation surface.

2.1
Formal definition

We define the operator adapted diagonal frame decomposition as a generalization of the wavelet vaguelette decomposition and the biorthogonal curvelet or shearlet decompositions to general frames, cf.
[
9
,
4
,
6
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Diagonal frame decomposition (DFD))
.

Let
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\mathbb{X}^{\Lambda}
,
(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\mathbb{Y}^{\Lambda}
and let
(\kappa_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a family of positive numbers. For a linear operator
\mathbf{A}\colon\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{A})\subseteq\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
, we call
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda},\kappa_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
a
diagonal frame decomposition (DFD) for
\mathbf{A}
, if the following conditions hold:

(D1)

(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is a frame of
\mathbb{X}
,

(D2)

(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is a frame of
\overline{\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A})}=\overline{\mathbf{A}(\mathbb{X})}
,

(D3)

\forall\lambda\in\Lambda\colon\kappa_{\lambda}\neq 0\wedge\mathbf{A}^{*}v_{\lambda}=\kappa_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The DFD generalizes the singular value decomposition (SVD) and the wavelet-vaguelette decomposition (WVD) (cf.
[
9
]
) as it allows the systems
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
and
(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
to be non-orthogonal and redundant.
Note that by
(D2)
and
(D3)
, the frame
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
satisfies
u_{\lambda}\in\mathbf{A}^{*}(\overline{\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A})})=\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A}^{*})
, where we have made use of the identity
\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A})^{\bot}=\ker(\mathbf{A}^{*})
. For typical inverse problems this yields a notable smoothness assumption on the involved elements of the frame
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
.

Although the SVD has proven itself to be a useful tool for analyzing and solving inverse problems it has the following drawbacks: First, ONBs that are provided by the SVD (though optimally adapted to the operator in consideration), in many cases, don’t provide sparse representations of signals of interest and, hence, are not suitable for the use in sparse regularization. In particular, frames that provide sparse representation of signals (such as wavelets or wavelet-like systems) are often not part of the SVD. Second, SVD is often very hard to compute and not known analytically for many practical applications.

To overcome some of those difficulties, wavelet-vaguelette decompositions were introduced. Nevertheless, this concept builds upon expansions of signals with respect to orthogonal wavelet-systems, which may not provide an optimal sparse representation of signals of interest, e.g., signals with sharp edges. Thus, by allowing general frames, the BCD offers great flexibility in the choice of a suitable function system for sparse regularization while retaining the advantages.

Definition 2.3
.

For a frame
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of
\mathbb{X}
, the
synthesis operator
is defined as

\mathbf{U}\colon\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\to\mathbb{Y}\colon\bm{\xi}\mapsto\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\xi_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}

and the corresponding
analysis operator
is defined as its adjoint,
\mathbf{U}^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{X}\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\colon\bm{x}\mapsto(\left\langle\bm{x},u_{\lambda}\right\rangle)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.

In what follows, the synthesis operator of a frame will be always denoted with the corresponding upper case letter, e.g., if
(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a frame, then
\mathbf{V}
denotes the corresponding synthesis and
\mathbf{V}^{\ast}
the analysis operator.

In order to simplify the notation, we will also refer to
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\bm{\kappa})
the as DFD instead of using the full notation
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda},\kappa_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.

If a DFD exists for an operator
\mathbf{A}
, it immediately gives rise to a
reproducing formula

\bm{x}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\langle\bm{x},u_{\lambda}\right\rangle\bar{u}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\kappa_{\lambda}^{-1}\left\langle\mathbf{A}\bm{x},v_{\lambda}\right\rangle\bar{u}_{\lambda}=\bar{\mathbf{U}}\circ\mathbf{M}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}_{\bm{\kappa}}\circ\mathbf{V}^{\ast}(\mathbf{A}\bm{x}),

(2.1)

where
(\bar{u}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is the dual frame to
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
(cf.
[
5
]
) and
\bar{\mathbf{U}}
the corresponding synthesis operator.
Moreover,
\mathbf{M}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}_{\bm{\kappa}}
denotes the Moore-Penrose inverse of
\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}
and performs point-wise division with
\bm{\kappa}
, i.e.

(\mathbf{M}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}_{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\eta}))_{\lambda}\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\eta_{\lambda}/\kappa_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}&\text{ if }\kappa_{\lambda}\neq 0\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\,.\end{cases}

(2.2)

Hence, from given (clean) data
\bm{y}
, one can calculate the frame coefficient of
\bm{x}
and obtain a reconstruction via (
2.1
). The key to the practical use of this reproducing formulas is the efficient implementation of the analysis and synthesis operators
\mathbf{V}^{\ast}
and
\bar{\mathbf{U}}
, respectively. For particular cases, we will provide efficient and easy to implement algorithms for the evaluation of
\mathbf{V}^{\ast}
and
\bar{\mathbf{U}}
.

Note that, the reproducing formula (
2.1
) (similarly to the SVD) reveals the ill-posedness of the operator equation through the decay of the
quasi-singular values

\kappa_{\lambda}
. A regularized version of the reproducing formula (
2.1
) can be obtained by incorporating soft-thresholding of the frame coefficients. In section
4
, we present a complete analysis of this approach as we are not aware of any results for the general DFD-thresholding in the context of regularization theory.

We now provide several examples of DFDs, including the wavelet vaguelette decomposition and the biorthogonal curvelet decomposition
[
4
]
for the Radon transform as well as a DFD for the forward operator of photoacoustic tomography with flat observation surface.

2.2
Radon transform

Definition 2.4

(Radon transform)
.

The Radon transform
\mathbf{R}\colon L^{2}(B_{1}(0))\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
is defined
by

\forall(\theta,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\colon\quad\mathbf{R}f(\theta,s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s\theta+t\theta^{\bot})\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(2.3)

It is well known that the Radon transform is bounded
on
L^{2}(B_{1}(0))
, see
[
20
]
.
Let
\mathbf{F}g(\theta,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\theta,s)e^{-i\omega s}
be the Fourier transform with respect to the first component and consider the Riesz potential
[
20
]

(\mathbf{I}^{-\alpha}g)(\theta,\omega)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\omega\right|^{\alpha}(\mathbf{F}g)(\theta,\omega)e^{i\omega s}\,\mathrm{d}s

(2.4)

for
\alpha>-1
. The following hold:

(R1)

The commutation relation
(\mathbf{I}^{-\alpha}\circ\mathbf{R})f=(\mathbf{R}\circ(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2})f
.

(R2)

The filtered backprojection formula

f=(4\pi)^{-1}\mathbf{R}^{*}(\mathbf{I}^{-1}\circ\mathbf{R})f=:\mathbf{R}^{\sharp}\mathbf{R}f
.

(R3)

Isometry property

4\pi\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\mathbf{I}^{-1}\circ\mathbf{R}f_{1},\mathbf{R}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}
.

Using these ingredients, one can obtain a
DFD for the Radon transform as follows:

Example 2.5
.

DFD for the Radon transform
Let
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be either an orthonormal basis of wavelets with compact support, a (band-limited) curvelet or a shearlet tight frame with

\lambda=(j,k,\beta)\in\Lambda
where

j\geq 0
is the scale index.
Then
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\bm{\kappa})
is a DFD with

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2^{-j/2}\,(4\pi)^{-1}(\mathbf{I}^{-1}\circ\mathbf{R})u_{\lambda}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2^{-j/2}\,.

(2.6)

These results have been obtained in
[
9
]

for wavelet bases, in
[
4
]
for curvelet systems and in
[
6
]
for the shearlet frame.
All cases are shown in similar manner and basically follow
from
(R1)
,
(R2)
and the fact that
that
2^{-j/2}(-\Delta)^{1/4}(4\pi)^{-1}u_{\lambda}\simeq u_{\lambda}
for any of the considered systems.
The limited data case has been studied in
[
12
]
.

Equation (
2.5
) implies

\left\langle g,v_{\lambda}\right\rangle=2^{-j/2}(4\pi)^{-1}\left\langle g,\mathbf{I}^{-1}\circ\mathbf{R}u_{\lambda}\right\rangle=2^{-j/2}(4\pi)^{-1}\left\langle\mathbf{R}^{*}\circ\mathbf{I}^{-1}g,u_{\lambda}\right\rangle\,.

(2.7)

This gives an efficient numerical algorithm for the evaluation of
\mathbf{V}^{\ast}
provided that
\mathbf{U}^{\ast}
is associated with an efficient
algorithm. This is in particular the case for the
wavelet, shearlet and curvelet frames as above.

Remark 2.6
.

We would like to note that in order to define a DFD for the case of curvelets or shearlets one needs to consider the Radon transform on subspaces of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
consisting of functions that are defined on unbounded domains (since band-limited curvelets or shearlets have non-compact support). However, since the Radon transform is an unbounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the reproducing formula (
2.1
) will not hold any more in general. The reproducing formulas are at least available for the case that the object
\bm{x}
can be represented as a finite linear combination of curvelets or shearlets (cf.
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
).

Another possibility would be to consider projections of curvelet or shearlet frames onto the space
L^{2}(B_{1}(0))
, which would yield a frame for this space (cf.
[
5
]
) and then define the DFD in the same way as above. Because the Radon transform is continuous on
L^{2}(B_{1}(0))
, the reproducing formula (
2.1
) will hold for general linear combinations.

.

Inversion of the wave equation

We consider a planar geometry, which has been considered in our previous work

]

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta u(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

(2.8)

\displaystyle u(x,y,0)

\displaystyle=y^{1/2}f(x,y)

\displaystyle(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,y,0)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,.

The trace map

for

is known to be an isometry from

In particular, the operator

Definition 2.8

(Forward operator for the wave equation)
.

We define
\mathbf{A}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{H}_{+})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{H}_{+})

\mathbf{A}f\coloneqq t^{-1/2}u
, for

where

is the solution of (

), and extending it by continuity

The isometry property implies that any frame

This, in particular, yields a wavelet vaguelette decomposition and a biorthogonal curvelet decomposition for the wave equation.

.

Let
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

3
Sparse
\ell^{1}
-regularization

There are two fundamentally different and well-studied instances of sparse frame based regularization, namely
\ell^{1}
-analysis regularization and
\ell^{1}
-synthesis regularization. They are defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm ANA}(\bm{y}_{\delta})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\argmin_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{X}}{\left\{\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\mathbf{A}\bm{x}-\bm{y}_{\delta}\right\|}^{2}+\alpha\sum_{\lambda}d_{\lambda}\left|\left\langle u_{\lambda},\bm{x}\right\rangle\right|\right\}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm SYN}(\bm{y}_{\delta})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{W}\left(\argmin_{\bm{\xi}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}{\left\{\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\bm{\xi}-\bm{y}_{\delta}\right\|}^{2}+\alpha\sum_{\lambda}d_{\lambda}\left|\xi_{\lambda}\right|\right\}}\right)\,,

(3.2)

respectively, with weights
d_{\lambda}>0
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call
\bm{\xi}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
sparse if the set

{\left\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\xi_{\lambda}\neq 0\right\}}
is finite.

If
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
is sparse,
we write
\operatorname{Sign}(\bm{\xi})\coloneqq\{\bm{z}=(z_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\mid z_{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Sign}(\xi_{\lambda})\}

where
\operatorname{Sign}(\,\cdot\,)\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the multi-valued signum function defined by
\operatorname{Sign}(0)=[-1,1]
and

\operatorname{Sign}(x)={\left\{x/\left|x\right|\right\}}
for
x\neq 0
.
We will use the notation

\left\|\;\cdot\;\right\|_{\bm{d},1}\colon\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\to\mathbb{R}\cup{\left\{\infty\right\}}\colon\bm{\xi}\mapsto\begin{cases}\sum_{\lambda}d_{\lambda}\left|\xi_{\lambda}\right|&\text{ if }(d_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{1}(\Lambda)\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise }\,.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Any element in the set
\argmin\{{\left\|\mathbf{U}^{*}(\bm{x})\right\|}_{\bm{d},1}\mid\mathbf{A}\bm{x}=\bm{y}\}
is called
{\left\|\mathbf{U}^{*}(\,\cdot\,)\right\|}_{\bm{d},1}
-minimizing solution of
\mathbf{A}(\bm{x})=\bm{y}
.
Note that
{\left\|\mathbf{U}^{*}(\,\cdot\,)\right\|}_{\bm{d},1}
-minimizing solutions exists whenever
there is any solution
\bm{x}
with
{\left\|\mathbf{U}^{*}(\bm{x})\right\|}_{\bm{d},1}<\infty
, as follows from
[
23

(A2)

,

of
\mathbb{X}

(A3)

\inf\{d_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}>0
.

For certain sparse elements we will state linear error estimates which have been derived in

]
.
See

3.1
\ell^{1}
-Analysis regularization

Let us define the
\ell^{1}
-analysis Tikhonov functional
by

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\bm{y}}\colon\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup{\left\{\infty\right\}}\colon\bm{x}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\mathbf{A}\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\|}^{2}+\alpha{\left\|\mathbf{U}^{*}\bm{x}\right\|}_{\bm{d},1}\,.

(3.4)

Then we have
\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm ANA}(\bm{y})=\argmin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\bm{y}}
.

Proposition 3.2

(Convergence of analysis regularization)
.

Let
(A1)
–
(A3)
be satisfied, suppose

\bm{y}\in\mathbb{Y}
,
\alpha>0
,

(\bm{y}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{Y}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
\bm{y}^{k}\to\bm{y}
,
and choose
\bm{x}^{k}\in\argmin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\bm{y}^{k}}
.

\blacksquare

Existence:

The functional
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\bm{y}}
has at least one minimizer.

\blacksquare

Stability:

There exists a subsequence
(\bm{x}^{k(\ell)})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
of

(\bm{x}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a minimizer
\bm{x}_{\alpha}\in\argmin\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\bm{y}}

3.3
Sparse regularization using an SVD

In the special case that
\mathbf{U}
is part of an SVD, then analysis and
synthesis regularization are equivalent
and can be computed explicitly by soft-thresholding
of the expansion coefficients.

Theorem 3.6

(Equivalence in the SVD case)
.

Let
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\bm{\kappa})
be an SVD for
\mathbf{A}
, let
\bm{y}_{\delta}\in\mathbb{Y}
and
consider (
3.1
), (
3.2
) with
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{U}
.
Then

\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm ANA}(\bm{y}_{\delta})=\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm SYN}(\bm{y}_{\delta})=\{(\mathbf{U}\circ\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}\circ\mathbf{V}^{\ast})(\bm{y}_{\delta})\}\,,

equals the soft-thresholding estimator in the SVD system.

Proof.

Because
\mathbf{U}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{X}
, we have
\bm{x}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\xi}\Leftrightarrow\xi=\mathbf{U}^{*}\bm{x}
which implies that

\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm ANA}(\bm{y}_{\delta})=\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm SYN}(\bm{y}_{\delta})
.
Now let
\bm{x}_{\alpha}\in\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm SYN}(\bm{y}_{\delta})
be any minimizer of
the
\ell^{1}
-analysis Tikhonov functional
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\bm{y}}
.
Let
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A})^{\bot}}
denote the orthogonal projection on
\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A})^{\bot}
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\bm{y}}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\mathbf{A}\bm{x}-\bm{y}\right\|}^{2}+\alpha{\left\|\mathbf{U}^{*}\bm{x}\right\|}_{\bm{d},1}

\displaystyle={\left\|\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A})^{\bot}}(\bm{y})\right\|}^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{1}{2}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{A}\bm{x}-\bm{y},v_{\lambda}\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha d_{\lambda}\left|\left\langle\bm{x},u_{\lambda}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle={\left\|\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{ran}(\mathbf{A})^{\bot}}(\bm{y})\right\|}^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{1}{2}\left|\kappa_{\lambda}\left\langle\bm{x},u_{\lambda}\right\rangle-\left\langle\bm{y},v_{\lambda}\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\alpha d_{\lambda}\left|\left\langle\bm{x},u_{\lambda}\right\rangle\right|\,.

The latter sum is minimized by componentwise soft-thresholding.
This shows
\bm{x}_{\alpha,\delta}=(\mathbf{U}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}^{2}}\circ\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbf{V}^{\ast})(\bm{y}_{\delta})=(\mathbf{U}\circ\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}\circ\mathbf{V}^{\ast})(\bm{y}_{\delta})
and
concludes the proof.
∎

In the case that
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\bm{\kappa})
is a redundant
DFD expansion and not an SVD, then (
3.1
), (
3.2
), and the soft-thresholding estimator

(\mathbf{U}\circ\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}\circ\mathbf{V}^{\ast})(\bm{y}_{\delta})

(3.6)

are all non-equivalent. Further, in this case, (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) have to be computed by iterative minimization algorithms. This requires repeated application of the forward and adjoint problem and therefore is time consuming. In the following section, we study
DFD thresholding which is the analog of (
3.6
)
for redundant systems. Despite the non-equivalence to
\ell^{1}
-regularization, we are able to derive
the same type of convergence results and linear convergence rates as for the analysis and synthesis variants of
\ell^{1}
-regularization.

4
Regularization via DFD thresholding

Throughout this section we fix the following assumptions:

(B1)

\mathbf{A}\colon\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
is bounded linear.

(B2)

(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\bm{\kappa})
is s
DFD for

4.1
DFD soft-thresholding

Any DFD gives an explicit inversion formula

\bm{x}=(\bar{\mathbf{U}}\circ\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbf{V}^{*})(\mathbf{A}\bm{x})
where

\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}
is defined by (
2.2

For ill-posed problems,
\kappa_{\lambda}\to 0

and therefore the above reproducing formula
is unstable when applied to noisy data
\bm{y}_{\delta}

instead of
\mathbf{A}\bm{x}
. Below we stabilize the inversion
by including the soft-thresholding operation.

Definition 4.1

(DFD soft-thresholding)
.

Let
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\bm{\kappa})
be a DFD for
\mathbf{A}
. We define the nonlinear
DFD soft-thresholding estimator by

\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm DFD}\colon\mathbb{Y}\to\mathbb{X}\colon\bm{y}\mapsto(\bar{\mathbf{U}}\circ\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}\circ\mathbf{V}^{*})(\bm{y})\,.

(4.1)

If
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
,
(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
are ONBs,
then Theorem
3.6
shows that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are equivalent to (
4.1
).
In the case of general frames,
\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm ANA}
,

\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm SYN}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm DFD}
are all different.

As the main result in this paper we show that DFD soft-thresholding yields the same theoretical results as
\ell^{1}
-regularization.
Assuming efficient implementations for
\bar{\mathbf{U}}
and
\mathbf{V}^{*}
,
the DFD estimator has the advantage that it can be calculated non-iteratively
and is therefore much faster than
\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm SYN}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm DFD}
.

Consider the
\ell^{1}
-Tikhonov functional for the multiplication
operator
\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}
,

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\bm{\eta}}\colon\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\to\mathbb{R}\cup{\left\{\infty\right\}}\colon\bm{\xi}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}\right\|}^{2}+\alpha{\left\|\bm{\xi}\right\|}_{\bm{d},1}\,.

(4.2)

The proof strategy used in this paper is based on the
following Lemma.

Lemma 4.2

(
\ell^{1}
-minimization for multiplication operators)
.

(a)

\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\forall\bm{\eta}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\colon

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\bm{\eta}}
has a unique minimizer.

(b)

\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\forall\bm{\eta}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\colon

(\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}})(\bm{\eta})=\argmin\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\bm{\eta}}
.

(c)

\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}\colon\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
is continuous.

(d)

\mathbf{V}^{*}\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}\mathbf{U}^{*}

Proof.

Because
(\operatorname{Id},\operatorname{Id},\bm{\kappa})
is an SVD for
\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}
,
Items
(a)
,
(b)
follow from Theorem
3.6
, the
equivalence of
\ell^{1}
-regularization and soft-thresholding in the SVD case.
Item
(c)
follows from Proposition
3.4
.
Moreover, the equality
(\mathbf{V}^{*}\mathbf{A}\bm{x})_{\lambda}=\left\langle v_{\lambda},\mathbf{A}\bm{x}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathbf{A}^{*}v_{\lambda},\bm{x}\right\rangle=\kappa_{\lambda}\left\langle u_{\lambda},\bm{x}\right\rangle=(\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}\mathbf{U}^{*}\bm{x})_{\lambda}
shows Item
(d)
.
∎

Note that the continuity of
\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}
(see Item
(b)
in the above lemma) is not obvious as it is the composition of the soft thresholding
\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}
with the discontinuous operator in
\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}

(b)
of
\mathbf{M}_{\bm{\kappa}}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\alpha\bm{d}/\bm{\kappa}}

\ell^{1}
-Tikhonov functional

4.3
where we use the convergence of

to show convergence the DFD soft-thresholding estimator
\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm DFD}

\|\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}^{\rm DFD}(\bm{y}_{\delta})-\bm{x}^{\bm{\texttt{+}}}\|\leq c{\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|}\delta

5
Conclusion

To overcome the inherent ill-posedness of inverse problems, regularization methods incorporate available prior information about the unknowns to be reconstructed. In this context, a useful prior is sparsity with respect to a certain frame. There are at least two different regularization strategies implementing sparsity with respect to a frame, namely
\ell^{1}
-analysis
regularization and
\ell^{1}
-synthesis regularization.
In this paper, we analyzed DFD-thresholding as a third variant
of sparse regularization. One advantage of DFD-thresholding compared to other sparse regularization methods is its non-iterative nature leading to fast algorithms.
Besides having a DFD, actually computing the DFD soft-thresholding estimator (
4.1
) requires the dual frame
\bar{\mathbf{U}}
. While in the general situation, the DFD and the dual frame have to be computed numerically, we have shown that for many practical examples (see Section
2
) they are known explicitly and efficient algorithms are available for its numerical evaluation.

The DFD-approach presented in this paper is well studied in the context of statistical estimating using certain multi-scale systems. However, its analysis in the context of regularization theory has not been given so far. In this paper we closed this gap and presented a complete convergence analysis of
DFD-thresholding as regularization method.

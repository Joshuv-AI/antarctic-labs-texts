Asymptotic stability near the soliton for quartic Klein-Gordon in 1D
Adilbek Kairzhan, Fabio Pusateri

Abstract
. We consider the nonlinear focusing Klein-Gordon equation in 1 + 1 1+1 dimensions and the global space-time dynamics of solutions near the unstable soliton. Our main result is a proof of optimal decay, and local decay, for even perturbations of the static soliton originating from well-prepared initial data belonging to a subset of the center-stable manifold constructed in [ 3 , 27 ] . Our results complement those of Kowalczyk-Martel-Muñoz [ 27 ] and confirm numerical results of Bizon-Chmaj-Szpak [ 4 ] when considering nonlinearities u p u^{p} with p ≥ 4 p\geq 4 . In particular, we provide new information both local and global in space about asymptotically stable perturbations of the soliton under localization assumptions on the data.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The problem

We consider the focusing quartic Klein-Gordon (NLKG) equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u+u=u^{4},

where
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
are time and space variables, and
u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}
.
Our results apply to more general power nonlinearities as well but we restrict
our discussion and proof to the case
p=4
for concreteness; see Remark
1.3
.
The Hamiltonian associated with (
1.1
) is

(1.2)

\mathcal{H}(u,u_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[(\partial_{t}u)^{2}+(\partial_{x}u)^{2}+u^{2}\right]dx-\frac{1}{5}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{5}dx,

and for initial data in the energy space,
(u(0),u_{t}(0))\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
the equation is locally well-posed, and globally well-posed for small solutions.
Global existence for small energy initial data follows from standard energy estimates, see for example
[
5
]
. It is also known that finite time blow-up occurs for solutions with negative energy initial data
[
31
]
and for large positive initial data
[
43
,
45
]
, which follows from a convexity argument.

(
1.1
) admits an explicit time-independent static soliton solution

(1.3)

Q(x)=(\alpha+1)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\text{sech}^{1/\alpha}(\alpha x),\quad\alpha:=\frac{3}{2}.

In this paper we study the dynamics of certain classes
of global-in-time solutions (
t\geq 0
) in the vicinity of
Q

and, in particular, their behavior on the whole real line and their decay towards the soliton.
Letting
u=Q+v
denote the perturbed soliton for a real-valued and sufficiently small
v
,
we see that

(1.4)

v_{tt}+\mathcal{L}v=N(v),

where

(1.5)

\mathcal{L}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-4Q^{3},\qquad N(v):=6Q^{2}v^{2}+4Qv^{3}+v^{4}.

The spectral properties of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
were described in details in
[
6
]
,
also in the case of general powers
u^{p}
in (
1.1
).
In particular, it is known that
\mathcal{L}
has continuous spectrum
[1,\infty)
and exactly two isolated eigenvalues:

-

The first eigenvalue is
\lambda_{0}=-\alpha(\alpha+2)=-\Omega^{2}

(
\Omega>0
) with corresponding even normalized eigenfunction

(1.6)

\rho(x):=c_{0}(\text{sech}(\alpha x))^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}},\qquad\mathcal{L}\rho=-\Omega^{2}\rho,

where the positive
c_{0}
is chosen to satisfy
\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}=1
;

-

The other eigenvalue is
\lambda_{1}=0
with corresponding odd eigenfunction
Q^{\prime}(x)
;
in particular,
\mathcal{L}
has no gap eigenvalues (internal mode) in
(0,1)
.

-

Moreover, the operator
\mathcal{L}
has no resonance at the edge of the continuous spectrum,
i.e., there does not exist a bounded solution
\varphi
to
\mathcal{L}\varphi=\varphi
.
Equivalently, the potential
V=-4Q^{3}
is ‘generic’.

To avoid the translation mode and having to modulate the soliton,
we restrict our analysis to even perturbations, and decompose
v
as

(1.7)

v(t,x)=a(t)\rho(x)+\chi(t,x),\quad\langle\chi,\rho\rangle_{L_{x}^{2}}=0,\quad\chi=P_{c}v,

where
P_{c}:L_{x}^{2}\to L_{x}^{2}
is the projection onto the continuous spectrum of
\mathcal{L}
.

Note that, in view of the decomposition

Remark 1.6

(About local decay and rates of convergence)

Algebraic rates of decay towards the soliton on the center-stable manifold had been predicted numerically already in

In particular, Fig. 2 in

Remark 1.7

(About the parity assumption)
.

Our main result in Theorem
3
applies to even solutions.
Since the Klein-Gordon equation is invariant under translations and Lorentz transformations,
in the general case of data without parity,
one needs to consider the whole family of soliton solutions

(1.31)

\displaystyle Q(\gamma(x-\beta t-x_{0})),\qquad\gamma:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}},\quad|\beta|<1.

The proof of an asymptotic stability result in this case would be substantially more involved;
it would require the linearization around a moving soliton of the form
Q(\gamma(t)(x-q(t)))
and

(1) A precise study of a more complicated linear operator in matrix form
(this will have additional off-diagonal first order terms compared to the operator appearing in (
1.8
));

(2) An extension of the analysis of the distorted Fourier transform and of the nonlinear spectral distribution
adapted to this new linearized operator;

(3) A modulation analysis with refined estimates for the time-dependent parameters of the moving soliton.

Our expectation is that all these issues could be tackled using a combination of
(a) classical tools, for example those concerning the set-up of the modulation analysis
and the leveraging of local decay, see
[
29
]

and (b) our techniques based on the distorted Fourier transform
which can give a more precise description of the linear flow and of the nonlinear oscillations,
and sharper estimates on the evolution of the modulation parameters.

We refer to the recent work
[
9
]
where
some of these difficulties have been addressed for some Schrödinger type equations.

The expected asymptotic stability result for general initial data sufficiently close to a soliton (
1.31
)
would then look as follows:
for well prepared initial data (on the center-stable manifold)
of the form
u_{0}(x)=Q(\gamma_{0}(x-q_{0}))+v_{0}(x)
, with
v_{0}
small in a weighted Sobolev space,
the solution will satisfy

u(t,x)=Q\big(\gamma(t)(x-q(t))\big)+v(t,x)

where the modulation parameters
\gamma(t),q(t)
converge as

and the radiation

{\|v(t)\|}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1/2}
.

1.4.
Strategy of the proof

We begin by following the standard strategy
of substituting the decomposition (
1.7
) into (
1.4
)-(
1.5
),
and separating the discrete and continuous modes as follows:

(1.32)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a_{tt}-\Omega^{2}a=\langle N(v),\rho\rangle_{L_{x}^{2}}:=F[v],\\
\chi_{tt}+\mathcal{L}\chi=P_{c}N(v).\end{array}\right.

Following the Duhamel’s principle, (
1.32
) is equivalent to the system of integral equations

(1.33)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a(t)=\cosh(\Omega t)a(0)+\dfrac{1}{\Omega}\sinh(\Omega t)\dot{a}(0)+\dfrac{1}{\Omega}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}}\sinh(\Omega(t-s))F[v](s)ds,\\
\chi(t)=\cos(\sqrt{H+1}t)\chi(0)+\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{H+1}\,t)}{\sqrt{H+1}}\dot{\chi}(0)+\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}}\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{H+1}(t-s))}{\sqrt{H+1}}P_{c}N(v(s))ds.\end{array}\right.

Imposing the
stability condition

(1.34)

\dot{a}(0)=-\Omega a(0)-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\Omega s}F[v](s)ds,

which ensures that the contribution of the exponentially
increasing term
e^{\Omega t}
of
a(t)
vanishes as
t\to\infty
,
(
1.33
) reduces to

(1.35)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a(t)=e^{-\Omega t}\left[a(0)+\dfrac{1}{2\Omega}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}}e^{-\Omega s}F[v](s)ds\right]-\dfrac{1}{2\Omega}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}}e^{-\Omega|t-s|}F[v](s)ds,\\
\chi(t)=\cos(\sqrt{H+1}t)\chi(0)+\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{H+1}\,t)}{\sqrt{H+1}}\dot{\chi}(0)+\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}}\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{H+1}(t-s))}{\sqrt{H+1}}P_{c}N(v(s))ds.\end{array}\right.

Note that (
1.34
) holds for solutions on the center-stable manifold,
in view of (
1.13
).

To rewrite the evolution equation for
\chi(t,x)
in (
1.32
)
in a convenient way for our analysis, we introduce the profile function
g(t,x)
as

(1.36)

g(t,x):=e^{it\sqrt{H+1}}(\partial_{t}-i\sqrt{H+1})\chi(t,x).

Then the real-valued function
\chi
is given in terms of
g
and
\bar{g}
by

(1.37)

\chi(t,x)=\frac{1}{2i\sqrt{H+1}}(e^{it\sqrt{H+1}}\bar{g}(t,x)-e^{-it\sqrt{H+1}}g(t,x)).

We then want to look at Duhamel’s formula in terms of
g
on the distorted Fourier side.
We refer the reader to Section
2
for the definition of the distorted Fourier
Transform (dFT) and its properties.
Applying the distorted Fourier transform to (
1.36
)
gives
\widetilde{g}(t,k)=e^{it\langle k\rangle}(\partial_{t}-i\langle k\rangle)\widetilde{\chi}(t,k)
,
and the second equation in (
1.35
) becomes

(1.38)

\partial_{t}\widetilde{g}(t,k)=e^{it\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}N(Q,v),\qquad N(Q,v):=6Q^{2}v^{2}+4Qv^{3}+v^{4}.

Integrating (
1.38
) over time gives

(1.39)

\widetilde{g}(t,k)=\widetilde{g}(0,k)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}N(s,k)ds.

Equation (
1.39
) is the starting point for our analysis of the
projection on the continuous spectrum of the solution.

To obtain the desired nonlinear estimates that will imply our main result,
we introduce the following norm

(1.40)

\|v\|_{X}:=\|\langle t\rangle^{2}a(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(0,\infty)}+\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}((0,\infty):L_{k}^{2})}+\|\langle k\rangle^{2}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}((0,\infty):L_{k}^{2})},

The first norm above is used to control the evolution of the solution in the direction of the
even eigenfunction, while the other two control the evolution of the continuous spectral component.
The proof of our result will follow from a priori estimates for this norm:

Proposition 1.1
.

For
\|v\|_{X}
sufficiently small we have

\|v\|_{X}\lesssim|a(0)|+\left\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\left\|\langle k\rangle^{2}\widetilde{g}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\|v\|_{X}^{2}.

Proposition
1.1
follows from the three Propositions
3.1
,

3.2
and
3.3
.
With Proposition
1.1
we can prove the main theorem.

2.1.
Jost functions

We define
f_{+}(x,k)
and
f_{-}(x,k)
as solutions to the differential equation

(2.2)

Hf_{\pm}(x,k)=k^{2}f_{\pm}(x,k)

which satisfy the asymptotic boundary conditions

(2.3)

\lim_{x\to\infty}|e^{-ikx}f_{+}(x,k)-1|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{x\to-\infty}|e^{ikx}f_{-}(x,k)-1|=0

We further define
m_{\pm}(x,k):=e^{\mp ikx}f_{\pm}(x,k)
. For fixed
x
, the functions
m_{\pm}(x,k)
are analytic in
\text{Im }k>0
and continuous in
\text{Im }k\geq 0
. The following lemma provides with the primary estimates on
m_{\pm}
, which will be used later.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
s=0,1,2
and every positive integer
N
, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\partial_{k}^{s}(m_{\pm}(x,k)-1)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-12}\langle k\rangle^{-1},\quad\qquad\pm x\geq-1,

\displaystyle|\partial_{k}^{s}(m_{\pm}(x,k)-1)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{s+1}\langle k\rangle^{-1},\quad\qquad\pm x\leq 1.

Moreover, in the presence of the
x
derivatives, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{N}\partial_{k}^{s}m_{\pm}(x,k)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-12}\langle k\rangle^{-1+N},\quad\qquad\pm x\geq-1,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{N}\partial_{k}^{s}m_{\pm}(x,k)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{s+1}\langle k\rangle^{-1+N},\qquad\qquad\pm x\leq 1.

Proof.

The estimates follow by iterating the Volterra integral equations for
m_{\pm}
given by

m_{\pm}(x,k)=1\pm\int_{x}^{\pm\infty}D_{k}(\pm(y-x))V(y)m_{\pm}(y,k)dy,\quad\text{where }D_{k}(y)=\frac{e^{2iky}-1}{2ik}

as in Deift-Trubowitz
[
16
]
or Appendix A in
[
23
]
under the assumption (
2.1
) on
V
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Results similar to those in Lemma
2.1
can be derived for any
s\geq 0
,
and in such case some of the bounds should be modified,
see for example
[
7
,
22
]
. However, we will be working only with up to second derivatives
of
m_{\pm}
, and so are the choices of
s
.

The Jost functions
f_{\pm}
are related through so-called transmission and reflection coefficients,
T(k)
and
R_{\pm}(k)
, respectively
[
16
]
. That is,

(2.6)

\displaystyle T(k)f_{+}(x,k)

\displaystyle=R_{-}(k)f_{-}(x,k)+f_{-}(x,-k),

\displaystyle T(k)f_{-}(x,k)

\displaystyle=R_{+}(k)f_{+}(x,k)+f_{+}(x,-k),

and using the boundary conditions (
2.3
), we obtain

(2.7)

\displaystyle T(k)f_{+}(x,k)

\displaystyle\sim R_{-}(k)e^{-ikx}+e^{ikx}\quad\text{as }x\to-\infty

\displaystyle T(k)f_{-}(x,k)

\displaystyle\sim R_{+}(k)e^{ikx}+e^{-ikx}\quad\text{as }x\to\infty.

The coefficients
T(k)
and
R_{\pm}(k)
can be written explicitly through

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T(k)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2ik}\int V(x)m_{\pm}(x,k)dx,

\displaystyle\frac{R_{\pm}(k)}{T(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2ik}\int e^{\mp 2ikx}V(x)m_{\mp}(x,k)dx,

and satisfy the following relations at every
k
:

\displaystyle T(-k)=\overline{T(k)},\quad R_{\pm}(-k)=\overline{R_{\pm}(k)},

\displaystyle|T(k)|^{2}+|R_{\pm}(k)|^{2}=1,\quad T(k)\overline{R_{-}(k)}+\overline{T(k)}R_{+}(k)=0.

One distinguishes
two possible asymptotics of
T(k)
for
k\approx 0
,
depending on the value of the integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V(x)m_{+}(x,0)dx
,
which is zero whenever the potential
V
is
generic
.
Here are some equivalent definitions of generic potentials.

Definition 2.1
.

The potential
V
is generic if one of the following, equivalent, conditions hold:

•

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V(x)m_{+}(x,0)dx=0
;

•

T(0)=0
and
R_{\pm}(0)=-1
;

•

H=-\partial_{x}^{2}+V
has no zero resonance.

For generic potentials,
T(k)
and
R_{\pm}(k)
have the following asymptotics around
k\approx 0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
V
satisfying Assumption
2.1
,
the coefficients
T(k)
and
R_{\pm}(k)
have the following properties:

•

as
k\to 0
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle T(k)

\displaystyle=\alpha k+\mathcal{O}(k^{2})\quad\text{for some }\alpha\neq 0,

\displaystyle 1+R_{\pm}(k)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\pm}k+o(k).

In particular,
T(0)=0
and
R_{\pm}(0)=-1
.

•

For every
k\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle|T(k)|+|R(k)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle|\partial_{k}T(k)|+|\partial_{k}R_{\pm}(k)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-1}.

•

For every
k\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(2.11)

\left|\frac{T(k)}{k}\right|+\langle k\rangle\left|\partial_{k}\left(\frac{T(k)}{k}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle k\rangle},

and

(2.12)

\left|\frac{1+R_{\pm}(k)}{k}\right|+\left|\partial_{k}\left(\frac{1+R_{\pm}(k)}{k}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle k\rangle}.

Proof.

The detailed proof of the estimates (
2.9
)–(
2.10
)
can be found in Deift-Trubowitz
[
16
]
, so we only prove the
estimate (
2.11
) and the proof of (
2.12
) is similar.
It is worth pointing out that Assumption
2.1
on
V

is stronger than needed for this lemma to be true, and it suffices to have
\langle x\rangle^{2}V\in L_{x}^{1}
.

The asymptotic of
T(k)
around
k\approx 0
imply that there exists a positive constant
k^{*}
such that

\left|\frac{T(k)}{k}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle k\rangle}\quad\text{for every }|k|\leq k^{*}.

On the other hand, for
|k|>k^{*}
, we use uniform boundedness of
|T(k)|
to get

\left|\frac{T(k)}{k}\right|\lesssim\frac{|T(k)|}{\langle k\rangle}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle k\rangle}.

Combining these estimates together we obtain the first part of (
2.11
). To get the bound on the derivative part, we note that

\partial_{k}\left(\frac{T(k)}{k}\right)=\left(\frac{T(k)}{k}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2i}\int V(x)\partial_{k}m_{\pm}(x,k)dx-1\right),

which leads to a desired estimate in the view of (
2.4
) and assumptions on
V
.
∎

Pseudo-differential bounds

We provide some

Under milder assumptions than ours, which in particular do not require any regularity for

Lemma 2.3
.

For every function

2.3.
Distorted Fourier transform

In this subsection, we present the generalization of the standard Fourier transform
which we apply later to the perturbed Schrödinger operator
H=-\partial_{x}^{2}+V
.
Throughout the section and further in the paper,
we use the notation

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}:=\sqrt{-\partial_{x}^{2}+V},\qquad D:=\sqrt{-\partial_{x}^{2}}=|\partial_{x}|.

The standard Fourier transform on the line is defined for every
\phi\in L^{2}
as

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\phi(k)=\widehat{\phi}(k):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{-ikx}\phi(x)\,dx,

with

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{ikx}\phi(k)\,dx.

When dealing with the operator
H
, the function
e^{-ikx}

inside the standard Fourier transform can be replaced by a generalized
eigenfunction
\psi(x,k)
given in terms of the
Jost functions
f_{\pm}
from (
2.2
) in the following way:

(2.15)

\psi(x,k):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} &T(k)f_{+}(x,k)\quad&\text{for }k\geq 0,\\
&T(-k)f_{-}(x,-k)\quad&\text{for }k<0\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

and we now introduce the
distorted
Fourier transform for
h\in\mathcal{S}
by

(2.16)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}h(k)=\widetilde{h}(k):=\int\overline{\psi(x,k)}h(x)\,dx.

Lemma 2.4
.

In above settings,
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}:L^{2}_{x}\to L^{2}_{k}
is an isometry,

(2.17)

\|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}h\|_{L^{2}_{k}}=\|P_{c}h\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\quad\text{for all }h\in L^{2}.

The inverse of the distorted Fourier transform is given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g(x)=\int\psi(x,k)g(k)dk.

Setting
\widetilde{D}:=\sqrt{-\partial_{xx}+V}
, we have

m(\widetilde{D})P_{c}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}m(k)\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}.

Moreover, the distorted Fourier transform satisfies the following properties:

•

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}=\text{\rm Id}_{L_{k}^{2}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=P_{c}
;

•

if
h\in L^{1}_{x}
, then
\widetilde{h}
is a continuous, bounded function;

•

since
V
is generic,
\widetilde{h}(0)=0
;

•

we have

(2.18)

\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\|\langle x\rangle h\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

and, for
s\geq 0

(2.19)

{\big\|\langle k\rangle^{s}\widetilde{h}\big\|}_{L^{2}_{k}}=\|P_{c}h\|_{H_{x}^{s}}

Proof.

The proofs can be found in Agmon
[
2
]
, Dunford-Schwartz
[
18
]
, Germain-Pusateri-Rousset
[
23
]
and Yafaev
[
44
]
.
∎

2.4.
Decomposition of the generalized eigenfunctions

By the definition of the generalized eigenfunction we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\text{for }k\geq 0,\qquad\sqrt{2\pi}\psi(x,k)=T(k)f_{+}(x,k)=T(k)m_{+}(x,k)e^{ikx}

\displaystyle\text{for }k<0,\qquad\sqrt{2\pi}\psi(x,k)=T(-k)f_{-}(x,-k)=T(-k)m_{-}(x,-k)e^{ikx}

We introduce smooth versions of the indicator functions,
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}
,

(2.21)

\sigma_{+}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\varphi(y)dy,\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma_{+}(x)+\sigma_{-}(x)=1,

where
\varphi
is a smooth, nonnegative function, which is equal to
1
in a neighborhood of
0
, vanishes outside of
[-2,2]
and
\int\varphi~dx=1
. By construction,
\sigma_{+}(x)
vanishes for large negative values of
x
while
\sigma_{-}(x)
vanishes for large positive values of
x
. These functions allow to decompose expressions in (
2.20
) as

\displaystyle\text{for }k\geq 0,\quad\sqrt{2\pi}\psi(x,k)=\sigma_{+}(x)T(k)f_{+}(x,k)+\sigma_{-}(x)\left[f_{-}(x,-k)+R_{-}(k)f_{-}(x,k)\right],

\displaystyle\text{for }k<0,\quad\sqrt{2\pi}\psi(x,k)=\sigma_{-}(x)T(-k)f_{-}(x,-k)+\sigma_{+}(x)\left[f_{+}(x,k)+R_{+}(-k)f_{+}(x,-k)\right],

where we used the relations (
2.6
).
We further rewrite the generalized eigenfunction above using
m_{\pm}(x,k)=f_{\pm}(x,k)e^{\mp ikx}
,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\text{for }k\geq 0,\qquad\begin{aligned} \sqrt{2\pi}\psi(x,k)=&~\sigma_{+}(x)T(k)m_{+}(x,k)e^{ikx}\\
&+\sigma_{-}(x)\left[m_{-}(x,-k)e^{ikx}+R_{-}(k)m_{-}(x,k)e^{-ikx}\right]\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\text{for }k<0,\qquad\begin{aligned} \sqrt{2\pi}\psi(x,k)=&~\sigma_{-}(x)T(-k)m_{-}(x,-k)e^{ikx}\\
&+\sigma_{+}(x)\left[m_{+}(x,k)e^{ikx}+R_{+}(-k)m_{+}(x,-k)e^{-ikx}\right],\end{aligned}

Remark 2.3
.

Based on the boundedness of
\sigma_{\pm}m_{\pm}
in (
2.4
),
T(k)
and
R_{\pm}(k)
in (

(2.23)

|\psi(x,k)|\lesssim 1.

In the view of (
2.4
), we even further decompose
\psi
into a singular and regular parts as follows

(2.24)

\sqrt{2\pi}\psi(x,k)=\psi_{S}(x,k)+\psi_{R}(x,k).

The singular part
\psi_{S}
is given by

(2.25)

\psi_{S}(x,k):=\sigma_{+}(x)\psi_{S,+}(x,k)+\sigma_{-}(x)\psi_{S,-}(x,k),

where, setting the indicator functions
\mathbf{1}_{+}:=\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}
and
\mathbf{1}_{-}:=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}

(2.26)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\psi_{S,+}(x,k):=\mathbf{1}_{+}(k)T(k)e^{ikx}+\mathbf{1}_{-}(k)\left[e^{ikx}+R_{+}(-k)e^{-ikx}\right],\\[4.0pt]
\psi_{S,-}(x,k):=\mathbf{1}_{+}(k)\left[e^{ikx}+R_{-}(k)e^{-ikx}\right]+\mathbf{1}_{-}(k)T(-k)e^{ikx}.\end{array}\right.

The rest is a regular part
\psi_{R}(x,k)
given by

(2.27)

\psi_{R}(x,k):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} &\sigma_{+}(x)T(k)(m_{+}(x,k)-1)e^{ikx}\\[-2.0pt]
&+\sigma_{-}(x)[(m_{-}(x,-k)-1)e^{ikx}+R_{-}(k)(m_{-}(x,k)-1)e^{-ikx}],\qquad k\geq 0\end{aligned}\\
\\
\begin{aligned} &\sigma_{-}(x)T(-k)(m_{-}(x,-k)-1)e^{ikx}\\[-2.0pt]
&+\sigma_{+}(x)[(m_{+}(x,k)-1)e^{ikx}+R_{+}(-k)(m_{+}(x,-k)-1)e^{-ikx}],\qquad k<0.\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

Note that the regular part is localized around
x=0
due to the estimates (
2.4
).
On the other hand, the singular part
\psi_{S}

is a combination of oscillatory terms given by exponential functions with bounded coefficients,
see (
2.26
).
From (
2.26
), we can further rewrite the elements of the singular part as

(2.28)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\psi_{S,+}(x,k):=a_{+,+}(k)e^{ikx}+a_{+,-}(k)e^{-ikx},\\[4.0pt]
\psi_{S,-}(x,k):=a_{-,+}(k)e^{ikx}+a_{-,-}(k)e^{-ikx},\end{array}\right.

where

(2.29)

\displaystyle a_{+,+}(k)=\mathbf{1}_{+}(k)T(k)+\mathbf{1}_{-}(k),\qquad a_{+,-}(k)=\mathbf{1}_{-}(k)R_{+}(-k),

\displaystyle a_{-,+}(k)=\mathbf{1}_{+}(k)+\mathbf{1}_{-}(k)T(-k),\qquad a_{-,-}(k)=\mathbf{1}_{+}(k)R_{-}(k).

As it would be useful later, we also point out that, given
h\in P_{c}L^{2}_{x}
,
the decomposition (
2.24
) allows to rewrite

(2.30)

h=h_{S}+h_{R},\quad\text{where }h_{*}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\psi_{*}(x,k)\widetilde{h}(k)dk\quad\text{ for }*\in\{S,R\}.

Remark 2.4
.

The outcomes of Lemma
2.3

also apply to
\psi_{R}
. In other words, based on the construction of
\psi_{R}
in (
2.27
), we can verify that

(2.31)

\left\|\int\overline{\psi_{R}(x,k)}h(x)dx\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\left\|\langle x\rangle^{-11}h\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

and

(2.32)

\left\|\int\partial_{k}\overline{\psi_{R}(x,k)}h(x)dx\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\left\|\langle x\rangle^{-10}h\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Moreover, the function
\psi_{R}
decays as

(2.33)

|\psi_{R}(x,k)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-12}\langle k\rangle^{-1}.

In this section we provide linear and local decay estimates for the term

\langle\widetilde{D}\rangle^{-1}e^{\pm it\langle\widetilde{D}\rangle}P_{c}f,

Lemma 2.5

In particular, using

Remark 2.5

3.
Set-up and bootstrap

In this section we set-up the upcoming analysis of the evolution equations
for
a(t)
and
\chi(t,x)
in (
1.32
), and state our main bootstrap propositions
that will imply Proposition
1.1
.

We let
H:=P_{c}(-\partial_{xx}+V(x))
with
V(x):=-4Q^{3}
,
where
Q(x)
is the soliton (
1.3
).
Then the operator (
1.5
) is
\mathcal{L}=H+1
.
Recall the definition of

g(t,x):=e^{it\sqrt{H+1}}(\partial_{t}-i\sqrt{H+1})\chi(t,x)

in (
1.36
), so that

\chi
is given as in (
1.37
)
Recall also the notation for our main norm (
1.40
),

(3.1)

\|v\|_{X}:=\|\langle t\rangle^{2}a(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(0,\infty)}+\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}((0,\infty):L_{k}^{2})}+\|\langle k\rangle^{2}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}((0,\infty):L_{k}^{2})}.

Remark 3.1
.

From Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.7

the following estimates for
\chi
follow

(3.2)

\|\partial_{x}^{N}\chi(t)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{N}{5}}\left(\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\|\langle k\rangle^{2}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\right)\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{N}{5}}{\|v\|}_{X},\qquad N=0,1,2,

and

(3.3)

\left\|\langle x\rangle^{-2}\chi(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{H^{1}_{k}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}{\|v\|}_{X}.

Applying the distorted Fourier transform to the relation (
1.36
),
we obtain
\widetilde{g}(t,k)=e^{it\langle k\rangle}(\partial_{t}-i\langle k\rangle)\widetilde{\chi}(t,k)

and, after substitution into the second equation in (
1.32
),

(3.4)

\partial_{t}\widetilde{g}(t,k)=e^{it\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}N(Q,v),

where
N(Q,v)=6Q^{2}v^{2}+4Qv^{3}+v^{4}
and we recall that the term
v

is decomposed as
v(t,x)=a(t)\rho(x)+\chi(t,x)
.
Integrating the equation (
3.4
) over time give

(3.5)

\widetilde{g}(t,k)=\widetilde{g}(0,k)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}N(s,k)ds,

and applying the partial derivative with respect to
k
, we obtain

(3.6)

\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}(t,k)=\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}(0,k)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{k}\left[e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}N(s,k)\right]ds.

In the course of our estimates we will need to deal with
\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{g}(t)\|_{L_{k}^{2}}
,
which can be bounded using the following

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
\|v\|_{X}\ll 1
, we have

(3.7)

\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{g}\|_{L_{k}^{2}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-3/2}\|v\|_{X}^{2}

Proof.

From the evolution equation (
3.4
), since
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is an
L_{x}^{2}\to L_{k}^{2}
isometry, we have

\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{g}\|_{L_{k}^{2}}\lesssim\|Q^{2}v^{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|Qv^{3}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|v^{4}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

We substitute the decomposition
v(t,x)=a(t)\rho(x)+\chi(t,x)
,
and bound the first two terms using the local decay (
3.3
) as

\|Q^{2}v^{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim|a(t)|^{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-2}\chi\|^{2}_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-2}\|v\|_{X}^{2},

with a similar estimate for
\|Qv^{3}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
.
For the remaining term we have

\|v^{4}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim|a(t)|^{4}+\|\chi^{4}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-8}\|v\|_{X}^{4}+\|\chi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\chi\|^{3}_{L_{x}^{\infty}}.

The term
\|\chi\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}
is bounded by (
3.2
),
while
\|\chi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L_{k}^{2}}\leq\|v\|_{X}
, using (
2.17
).
Then, we have
\|v^{4}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-3/2}\|v\|^{4}_{X},

and combining all the estimates we obtain the desired bound (
3.7
).
∎

In the following subsections, we give estimates on the three norms in (
3.1
).

3.1.
Estimates for the first norm in (
3.1
)

Proposition 3.1
.

Given
\|v\|_{X}\ll 1
, for every
t\geq 0
we have

(3.8)

\langle t\rangle^{2}|a(t)|\lesssim|a(0)|+\|v\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof.

Using the integral equation for
a(t)
in (
1.35
), with
F[v]
as in (
1.32
), we have

\displaystyle\langle t\rangle^{2}a(t)

\displaystyle=\langle t\rangle^{2}e^{-\Omega t}\left[a(0)+\frac{1}{2\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\Omega s}F[v](s)ds\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\langle t\rangle^{2}e^{-\Omega t}}{2\Omega}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Omega s}F[v](s)ds-\frac{\langle t\rangle^{2}}{2\Omega}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\Omega(s-t)}F[v](s)ds

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{3}(t).

To bound
F[v](s)
we use the definition in (
1.5
) and the decomposition of
v
in (
1.7
)
to get

(3.9)

|F[v](s)|\lesssim|\langle v^{2}(s),\rho\rangle|+|\langle v^{3}(s),\rho\rangle|+|\langle v^{4}(s),\rho\rangle|\lesssim\left\|\langle x\rangle^{-2}v(s)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{2}\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-2}\|v\|_{X}^{2},

where for the last inequality we used (
3.3
).
Then, using (
3.9
), direct computations show that

|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)|\lesssim|a(0)|+\|v\|_{X}^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)|,|\mathcal{I}_{3}(t)|\lesssim\|v\|_{X}^{2}.

Combining these estimates, we obtain the desired result.
∎

3.2.
Estimates for the second norm in (
3.1
)

Here, we give the
L_{k}^{2}
bound for
\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}

Proposition 3.2
.

Given
\|v\|_{X}\ll 1

(3.10)

\left\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\left\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\|v\|_{X}^{2}.

The proof is based on the estimates for the terms in the Duhamel expression
for
\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}
in (
3.6

(3.11)

Then the
L_{k}^{2}
bound for
\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}
is given by

(3.12)

\left\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\left\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{g}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\left\|A_{1}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\left\|A_{2}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\left\|A_{3}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}},

where

(3.13)

\displaystyle A_{1}(t,k):=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{k}\left[e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}(a(s)\rho+\chi(s))^{2}(k)\right]ds,

\displaystyle A_{2}(t,k):=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{k}\left[e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q(a(s)\rho+\chi(s))^{3}(k)\right]ds,

\displaystyle A_{3}(t,k):=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{k}\left[e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}\left(a(s)\rho+\chi(s)\right)^{4}(k)\right]ds.

To prove Proposition
3.2

\left\|A_{j}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X}^{2}
for every
j=1,2,3
. We will verify these estimates in the following lemmas as well as in Section
4
and Section

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
\|v\|_{X}\ll 1
, for every
t\geq 0
we have

(3.14)

\left\|A_{1}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\left\|\int_{0}^{t}s\frac{k}{\langle k\rangle}e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}\chi^{2}(s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\|v\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof.

Distributing
\partial_{k}
and using Minkowski inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|A_{1}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{k}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}(a(s)\rho+\chi(s))^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}ds

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}s\frac{k}{\langle k\rangle}e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}(a(s)\rho+\chi(s))^{2}ds\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle=:A_{11}+A_{12}.

The first term
A_{11}
is easily bounded using (
2.18
),
followed by
the local decay estimates (
3.3

\displaystyle A_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle x\rangle Q^{2}(a(s)\rho+\chi(s))^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}a^{2}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle x\rangle^{-2}\chi(s)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{2}ds\lesssim\|v\|_{X}^{2}.

For the second term

\displaystyle A_{12}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}s\;a^{2}(s)\left\|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}\rho^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}ds+\int_{0}^{t}s\;|a(s)|\left\|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}\rho\chi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}ds

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}s\frac{k}{\langle k\rangle}e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}\chi^{2}(s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle:=B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}.

and

\displaystyle\left\|\langle k\rangle^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}v^{4}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim{\big\|v^{4}(s)\big\|}_{H_{2}^{x}}\lesssim{\big\|v(s)\big\|}_{H_{x}^{2}}{\big\|v(s)\big\|}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{3}\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\|v\|_{X}^{4}.

4.
Quadratic term analysis

In this section we prove the following bound for the quadratic
terms on the right-hand side of (

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
\|v\|_{X}\ll 1
, for every
t\geq 0
we have

(4.1)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}s\frac{k}{\langle k\rangle}e^{is\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}P_{c}Q^{2}\chi^{2}(s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X}^{2}.

To get a bound in (
4.1
), we use the following smoothing-type estimate:

Lemma 4.2
.

Given a function
\mathcal{Q}:\mathbb{R}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{k}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
such that, for some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\langle x\rangle^{\beta}\mathcal{Q}(x,k)\big|<\infty,

and
\phi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
such that
|\phi(k)|\lesssim|k|/\langle k\rangle
,
then, for all
t\geq 0
,

(4.2)

{\Big\|\langle x\rangle^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}(x,k)\phi(k)e^{i\langle k\rangle s}h(k)\,dk\Big\|}_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{s}^{2}([0,t])}\lesssim{\|h\|}_{L^{2}}.

Under the same assumptions, the following inhomogeneous estimate holds:

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\langle k\rangle s}\overline{\phi}(k)\overline{\mathcal{Q}}(x,k)F(s,x)\,dx\right]\,ds\Big\|}_{L_{k}^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle{\Big\|\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}F\Big\|}_{L_{x}^{1}L_{s}^{2}([0,\infty])}.

We now complete the proof of Lemma
4.1
using Lemma
4.2
above.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Applying (
4.3
)
to the left hand side of (
4.1
), that is,
letting
\beta=0
,
\mathcal{Q}(x,k)=\psi(x,k)
,
\phi(k)=k/\langle k\rangle
,
and
F(s,x)=s\,Q^{2}(x)\chi^{2}(s,x)
, we get

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\langle k\rangle s}\frac{k}{\langle k\rangle}\overline{\psi(x,k)}F(s,x)\,dx\right]\,ds\Big\|}_{L_{k}^{2}}&\lesssim{\|s\,Q^{2}(\cdot)\chi^{2}(s,\cdot)\|}_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R})L_{s}^{2}([0,\infty])}\\
&\lesssim{\|s\,Q(\cdot)\chi^{2}(s,\cdot)\|}_{L_{s}^{2}([0,\infty])L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\lesssim{\|v\|}_{X}^{2}{\|s\langle s\rangle^{-2}\|}_{L_{s}^{2}([0,\infty])}\lesssim{\|v\|}_{X}^{2},\end{split}

having used the local decay estimate (
3.3
).
∎

Observe that Lemma
4.2
does not use the genericity of the potential.
The proof is similar to the one for the analogous
case of the Schrödinger flow
e^{itH}
given in
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
.
We provide the details here for completeness.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Without loss of generality we may assume
\beta=0

and restrict the integral in (
4.2
) to
k\in[0,\infty)
.
Making a change of variable
\langle k\rangle=\lambda
,
so that
k\langle k\rangle^{-1}dk=d\lambda
,
dk=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1}}d\lambda
one has

\displaystyle I(s,x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{Q}(x,k)\phi(k)e^{i\langle k\rangle s}h(k)\,dk

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathcal{Q}(x,\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})\phi(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})e^{is\lambda}h(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1}}\,d\lambda.

Taking the
L^{2}_{s}
norm and applying Plancherel’s theorem we obtain

\displaystyle{\|I(\cdot,x)\|}_{L^{2}_{s}([0,t])}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}(x,\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})\,\mathbf{1}_{[1,\infty)}(\lambda)\phi(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})e^{is\lambda}h(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1}}\,d\lambda\right\|_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\mathcal{Q}(x,\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})\frac{\lambda\,\phi(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1}}h(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})\right|^{2}\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}\big|h(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})\big|^{2}\,d\lambda\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(k)|^{2}\frac{k}{\langle k\rangle}\,dk\lesssim{\|h\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}.

To obtain the inhomogeneous estimate (
4.3
)
we test the expression on the left-hand side against an arbitrary function
h\in L^{2}
:

\displaystyle\left\langle h,\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\langle k\rangle s}\overline{\phi}(k)\overline{\mathcal{Q}}(y,k)F(s,y)\,dy\right]ds\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\langle k\rangle s}\phi(k)\mathcal{Q}(y,k)h(k)\,dk\Big)\overline{F}(s,y)\,dyds

Applying Hölder’s inequality followed by the homogeneous estimate (
4.2
):

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int e^{i\langle k\rangle s}\phi(k)\mathcal{Q}(y,k)h(k)\,dk\Big)\overline{F}(s,y)\,dyds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\Big\|\int\mathcal{Q}(x,k)\phi(k)e^{i\langle k\rangle s}h(k)\,dk\Big\|}_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{s}^{2}([0,t])}\big\|F\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}L_{s}^{2}([0,t])}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\|h\|}_{L^{2}}{\big\|F\big\|}_{L_{x}^{1}L_{s}^{2}([0,t])}

which implies (
4.3
) by duality.
∎

5.1.

Using the decomposition (

To bound the

norm of

we use the simple relation
\left\|u_{1}(s)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|\langle l\rangle\widehat{u}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{l}}
,
which implies

(5.38)

\left\|u_{1}(s)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|\partial_{s}\widetilde{g}\right\|_{L^{2}_{l}}\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\|v\|_{X}^{2}

according to the bound in (

).
From the dispersive decay estimates for the case of the standard Fourier transform
(
2.37
),
(
2.10
), and (
5.26
),
we have

(5.39)

\left\|u_{2}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{r}(s)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-1/2}\|v\|_{X}.

Using (
5.38
) and (
5.39
) into (
5.37
), we obtain

(5.40)

\left\|J_{1}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X}^{5}\int_{0}^{t}\langle s\rangle^{-5/2}ds\lesssim\|v\|_{X}^{4}.

The remaining integrals
J_{2}
and
J_{3}
can be treated in the same way with appropriate
modifications for
u_{2}(s,x)
. In both cases, the
L_{x}^{2}
norm of

is bounded by
\|v\|_{X}
,
and this lead to a
\|v\|_{X}^{4}
bound for both
\left\|J_{2}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}
and
\left\|J_{3}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{k}}

Combining these estimates, we obtain
\|\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X}^{4}
.
Together with the previously obtained result for
\mathcal{I}_{1}
, we get the desired estimate (

∎

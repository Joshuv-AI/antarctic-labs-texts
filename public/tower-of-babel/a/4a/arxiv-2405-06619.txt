Interfacial spin-orbit coupling driven enhancement of superconductivity by parallel magnetic field
Zh. Devizorova, A.I. Buzdin

Abstract
It is commonly believed that time reversal symmetry breaking perturbations such as magnetic field or scattering on magnetic impurities destruct superconductivity and suppress the critical temperature of the superconducting phase transition. However, in the recent experimental studies significant enhancement of superconducting critical temperature by parallel magnetic field was found for several thin superconducting systems [H.J. Gardner, et al., Nature Physics 7 , 895 (2011); T. Asaba, et al., Scientific Reports 8 , 1 (2018) ]. Here we present a possible explanation of the observed phenomenon showing that combination of interfacial spin-orbit interaction and external magnetic field can cause the increase of the superconducting critical temperature of thin superconducting film in wide range of magnetic fields. We use the Ginzburg-Landau formalism taking into account Lifshitz invariant originated from Rashba spin-orbit interaction, which is also responsible for the superconducting diode effect. We also calculate the modification of the Little-Parks oscillations of the critical temperature of hollow superconducting cylinder at the presence of Rashba interfacial spin-orbit interaction.



Since Cooper pairs responsible for superconductivity are formed from time-reversal invariant electronic state, mechanisms that break this symmetry, including magnetic or exchange fields and scattering on paramagnetic impurities, are expected to destroy superconducting state
Maki 1966
;
Ginzburg 1956
;
Abrikosov and Gor’kov 1960
. There are two mechanism of this destruction: orbital
Abrikosov
and paramagnetic
Bulaevskii et al. 1985
;
Flouquet and Buzdin 2002
. The orbital effect is connected with the generation of screening Meissner currents in response to the applied magnetic field, which raise the kinetic energy of superconducting condensate. When this extra energy reaches the superconducting condensation energy, the superconducting state become less favourable, than normal one, and superconductivity destroys. On the other hand, the magnetic field tends to align spins of electron, which prevent superconducting singlet pairing with Cooper pairs formed from two electrons having opposite spin projections (paramagnetic mechanism). As a result, it is expected that the critical temperature of superconducting phase transition will be suppressed by applied magnetic field.

In the recent experimental studies the notable
enhancement
of critical temperature (
T_{c}
) by external magnetic field was found for several layered superconducting systems
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
;
Asaba et al. 2018
, including ultrathin Pb films
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
, two dimensional electron gas at LaAlO
3
/SrTiO
3
interface
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
, superconducting epitaxial thin film tungsten telluride
Asaba et al. 2018
(WTe
2
). For Pb films the enhancement of
T_{c}
demonstrates systematic dependence on film thickness. Note that the reduction of pair-breaking effect due to polarization of paramagnetic impurities by applied magnetic field can causes similar enhancement of
T_{c}

Kharitonov and Feigelman 2005
. However, on experiment
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
intentional addition of paramagnetic impurities (Cr) into Pb sample diminishes the effect. Thus, the effect is not due to polarization of paramagnetic impurities. At the same time, in all these systems considerable intrinsic spin-orbit coupling (SOC) is present. Moreover, the enhancement of
T_{c}
by applied magnetic field is more pronounced in Pb films, where SOC is larger, in comparison with LaAlO
3
/SrTiO
3
sample
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
. In addition all samples are lack of inversion symmetry. Thus, one can expect that Rashba-type SOC
Bychkov and Rashba 1984
;
Rashba and Sheka 1991
, which results from strong intrinsic spin-orbit interaction and lack of inversion symmetry in the sample, can play important role in the observed phenomenon.

In the present paper we show that external magnetic field can cause the
enhancement
of
T_{c}
(see Fig.
3
) of thin superconducting film with interfacial Rashba SOC , schematically shown in Fig.
1
. We use phenomenological Ginzburg-Landau (GL) formalism with additional term named Lifshitz invariant, which is proportional to the SOC parameter and magnetic field. We show that, this extra term leads to the nonzero derivative of the order parameter on the interface with broken inversion symmetry. In semi-infinite superconductor such non-zero derivative causes the local enhancement of the order parameter near the interface
Mironov and Buzdin 2017
. If the thickness or the sample of the order of superconducting coherence length, one can expect the enhancement of
T_{c}
resulting from Lifshitz invariant. Here we show, that such enhancement is possible for reasonable choice of parameters (see Fig.
3
). To describe the bend in
T_{c}(H)
dependence in higher magnetic field, we take into account the third over gradients term in Lifshitz invariant, which is also responsible for the superconducting diode effect widely discussed in literature
Yuan and Fu 2022
;
Ando et al. 2020
;
Daido et al. 2022
;
He et al. 2022
;
Baumgartner et al. 2022
. We also calculate
T_{c}
for a thin-walled superconducting cylinder with interfacial Rashba SOC, schematically shown in Fig.
4
. It is well-known, that without SOC
T_{c}
of such a system is suppressed by external magnetic field and exhibits Little-Parks oscillations as a function of external magnetic flux
Parks and Little 1964
. Here we show that Rashba SOC at the surface of the cylinder can give rise to the total enhancement, but not suppression of its
T_{c}
superimposed by Little-Parks like oscillations, see Fig.
5
.

The GL functional of superconductor with interfacial Rashba-type spin-orbit interaction placed into external magnetic field reads as
Mironov and Buzdin 2017
;
Samokhin 2004
;
Kaur et al. 2005
;
Yuan and Fu 2022
:

G=\int dV\Biggl(\alpha|\psi|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}|\psi|^{4}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m}|\hat{\bm{D}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{(\bm{B}-\bm{H_{0}})^{2}}{8\pi}+\\
+\epsilon_{so}({\bf r})\bm{n}\times\bm{B}\biggl[\psi^{*}\hat{\bm{D}}(1-b\xi^{2}\hat{\bm{D}}^{2})\psi+\\
+\psi\left(\hat{\bm{D}}(1-b\xi^{2}\hat{\bm{D}}^{2})\psi\right)^{*}\biggr]\Biggr).

(1)

Here
\psi
is the superconducting order parameter,
\alpha=a(T-T_{c0})
and
\beta
are the standard GL coefficients,
T_{c0}
is the critical temperature of bulk superconductor,
\hat{\bm{D}}=-i\bm{\nabla}+\frac{2e}{c\hbar}\bm{A}
,
{\bf A}
is the vector-potential,
\bf{B}=\bm{\nabla}\times\bf{A}
is the magnetic field induction,
\bf{H}_{0}
is the external magnetic field,
\bm{n}
is the unit vector directed as the external normal to the superconductor’s interface with Rashba SOC,
\epsilon_{so}({\bf r})
is the spin-orbit constant, which is nonzero only in the atomically thin area of the width
l_{so}
near the interface with Rashba SOC,
\xi
is superconducting coherence length [
\xi=\xi_{0}=0.18v_{F}/T_{c0}
in pure limit and
\xi=\sqrt{D_{s}/(2\pi T_{co})}
in dirty limit, where
D_{s}
is diffusion coefficient],
b\approx 1
is numerical constant (
b=1.13
in pure limit
Yuan and Fu 2022
).

We calculate superconducting critical temperature
T_{c}
of superconducting film with the thickness
L
lying on insulating substrate placed into external magnetic field
{\bf H}_{0}
(see Fig.
1
). Due to lack of inversion symmetry in the structure, interfacial Rashba SOC appears at superconductor/substrate interface. Thus, the structure is described by free energy functional
1
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
x
-axis coincides with
{\bf n}
,
z
-axis is directed along external magnetic field
\bm{H}_{0}=H_{0}\bm{e}_{z}
and the vector potential is along
y
-axis
\bm{A}=A_{y}\bm{e}_{y}
. Superconducting film occupies the region
-L/2<x<L/2
.

Since near the superconducting phase transition the order parameter is small, we can write down the Ginzburg-Landau equations in the lowest order over
\psi
. In such the case in the equations for
\psi
we can take
\bm{B}\approx\bm{H}_{0}
and
A_{y}=H_{0}x
. Since the vector-potential is directed along the
y
axis, we can choose the ansatz for the order parameter in the form:
\psi=e^{ik_{y}y}\psi(x)
.

The Ginzburg-Landau equation for the order parameter and the boundary condition on the top surface have the standard form and read as:
\partial_{x}\psi(x)|_{x=-L/2}=0
,

\alpha\psi(x)-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m}\partial_{xx}\psi(x)+\frac{\left(\hbar k_{y}+2eH_{0}x/c\right)^{2}}{4m}\psi(x)=0.

(2)

At the same time, the boundary condition at the bottom interface must be modified by Rashba interfacial spin-orbit interaction. We derive this boundary condition minimizing the free energy functional (
1
) and show, that the derivative of the order parameter on the bottom surface is nonzero due to the presence of both magnetic field and Rashba SOC:

\partial_{x}\psi(x)|_{x=L/2}=s_{soc}\frac{H_{0}}{H_{c2}}\left(k_{y}+\frac{eH_{0}L}{c\hbar}\right)\times\\
\times\biggl[1-b\xi^{2}\left(k_{y}+\frac{eH_{0}L}{c\hbar}\right)^{2}\biggr]\psi(L/2).

(3)

Here the parameters
s_{soc}=8k_{soc}l_{so}
,
k_{soc}=m\epsilon_{so}H_{c2}/\hbar^{2}
are determined by the strength of spin-orbit interaction; the upper critical field is
H_{c2}=\Phi_{0}/[2\pi\xi^{2}(0)]
, where
\Phi_{0}=\pi\hbar c/e
is superconducting flux quantum and
\xi(T)=\sqrt{\hbar^{2}/[4ma(T_{c0}-T)]}
is temperature dependent superconducting coherence length.

Following the procedure described in Ref. [
Saint-James et al. 1969
] we introduce the dimensionless coordinate
X=2x/L
, the modulation vector
K_{y}=k_{y}L/2
, the parameters
\tilde{H}_{0}=eH_{0}L^{2}/(2\hbar c)
,
\epsilon_{0}=-m\alpha L^{2}/\hbar^{2}
and rewrite the GL equation in the following form:

\partial_{XX}\psi(X)+(K_{y}+\tilde{H}_{0}X)^{2}\psi(X)=\epsilon_{0}\psi(X).

(4)

At the same time, the boundary conditions read:
\partial_{X}\psi(-1)=0
,

\left(\frac{\partial_{X}\psi}{\psi}\right)|_{X=1}=p_{soc}\tilde{H}_{0}(K_{y}+\tilde{H}_{0})\times\\
\times\left[1-b\left(\frac{2\xi}{L}\right)^{2}(K_{y}+\tilde{H}_{0})^{2}\right],

(5)

where
p_{soc}=8k_{soc}l_{so}\left[2\xi(0)/L\right]^{2}
.

Next it is useful to introduce a new variable
t=\sqrt{2|\tilde{H}_{0}|}(X+K_{y}/\tilde{H}_{0})
. The resulting GL equation and the boundary conditions are the following:

-\partial_{tt}\psi(t)+\frac{1}{4}t^{2}\psi(t)=\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2|\tilde{H}_{0}|}\psi(t),

(6)

\partial_{t}\psi|_{\sqrt{2|\tilde{H}_{0}|}(-1+K_{y}/\tilde{H}_{0})}=0,

(7)

\left.\left(\frac{\partial_{t}\psi}{\psi}\right)\right|_{\sqrt{2|\tilde{H}_{0}|}(1+K_{y}/\tilde{H}_{0})}=\frac{p_{soc}\tilde{H}_{0}(K_{y}+\tilde{H}_{0})}{\sqrt{2|\tilde{H}_{0}|}}\times\\
\times\biggl[1-b\left(\frac{2\xi}{L}\right)^{2}(K_{y}+\tilde{H}_{0})^{2}\biggr].

(8)

The solution of Eq.(
6
) has the form of the linear combination of the Weber functions
\psi(t)=A_{\nu}D_{\nu}(t)+B_{\nu}D_{\nu}(-t)
, where
2\nu+1=\epsilon_{0}/|\tilde{H}_{0}|
. Substituting it into the boundary conditions we obtain the following equation, which implicitly defines the function
T_{c}(H_{0})
:

D^{\prime}_{\nu}(t_{+})D^{\prime}_{\nu}(-t_{-})-D^{\prime}_{\nu}(-t_{+})D^{\prime}_{\nu}(t_{-})=\\
=\frac{p_{soc}\tilde{H}_{0}(K_{y}+\tilde{H}_{0})}{\sqrt{2|\tilde{H}_{0}|}}\biggl[1-b\left(\frac{2\xi}{L}\right)^{2}(K_{y}+\tilde{H}_{0})^{2}\biggr]\times\\
\times\biggl[D_{\nu}(t_{+})D^{\prime}_{\nu}(-t_{-})+D_{\nu}(-t_{+})D^{\prime}_{\nu}(t_{-})\biggr],

(9)

where
t_{\pm}=\sqrt{2|\tilde{H}_{0}|}(\pm 1+K_{y}/\tilde{H}_{0})
.

The maximal temperature, at which the film is in superconducting state at fixed magnetic field, corresponds to the minimal value of
\nu
, satisfying Eq.(
9
). At fixed
K_{y}
and
\tilde{H}_{0}
this equation has infinite but discrete number of solutions for
\nu
, and we find the minimal one. Then we minimize it with respect to
K_{y}
and find the minimal value
\nu_{0}
for fixed
\tilde{H}_{0}
. As a result, we obtain the dependence
\epsilon_{0}(\tilde{H}_{0})
in the form
\epsilon_{0}=(2\nu_{0}+1)|\tilde{H}_{0}|
. Since
\epsilon=-[L/2\xi(0)]^{2}(T-T_{c0})/T_{c0}
and
\tilde{H}_{0}=[L/2\xi(0)]^{2}(H_{0}/H_{c2})
, we can plot the maximal temperature when the film is in superconducting state (i.e. the superconducting critical temperature
T_{c}
) at fixed external magnetic field vs. this external magnetic field
H_{0}
, see. Fig.
2
.

Our calculations shows, that interfacial Rashba SOC increases the superconducting critical temperature
T_{c}
of a superconducting film in comparison with the film without SOC, see. Fig.
2
. However, for reasonable chose of SOC parameter in thick superconducting film with
L\gtrsim\xi
, for which three-dimensional GL equation is applicable, total enhancement of
T_{c}
is hardly realised. Indeed, at small magnetic fields the increase in
T_{c}
due to Lifshitz invariant is of the order of
(\Delta T_{c}/T_{c0})\approx(s_{soc}/2)^{2}[\xi(0)/L]^{2}[H_{0}/H_{c2}]^{2}
. At the same time, the decrease of
T_{c}
caused by orbital effect is proportional to
(\Delta T_{c}/T_{c0})\approx-(1/6)[L/\xi(0)]^{2}[H_{0}/H_{c2}]^{2}
. As a result, to have total enhancement of
T_{c}
at small magnetic field we need
L\lesssim\sqrt{s_{soc}}\xi(0)
, which is equivalent to
p_{soc}\gtrsim 1
, since
p_{soc}=s_{soc}\left[2\xi(0)/L\right]^{2}
. Thus, for
L=2\sqrt{b}\xi
the threshold value of SOC parameter to have total enhancement of
T_{c}
is of the order of
s_{soc}=8k_{soc}l_{so}\sim 1
. This estimates is confirmed by more careful analysis, see. Fig.
2
, where for film with the thickness
L=2\sqrt{b}\xi
the
T_{c}
is enhanced at
p_{soc}=1
. For thicker films we need
s_{soc}\gg 1
, which is hardly realised in real materials. Thus, the enhancement of
T_{c}
can be possible only in thin superconducting films with the thickness
L\ll\xi
. Below we analyse such the case.

The superconducting critical temperature

In thin superconducting films with the thickness
L\ll\xi
the superconducting order parameter does not depend on
x
-coordinate and can be represented as
\psi=\psi_{0}e^{ik_{y}y}
. In such the case the effective two-dimensional GL functional near the phase transition has the following form:

\frac{G}{S}=\frac{\hbar^{2}\psi_{0}^{2}}{mL}\biggl[-\epsilon_{0}+K_{y}^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}\tilde{H}_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}p_{soc}\tilde{H}_{0}K_{y}\times\\
\times\left(1-(2\sqrt{b}\xi/L)^{2}K_{y}^{2}\right)\biggr].

(10)

Here
\eta=\{2/3+[2\xi(0)/L]^{2}\alpha_{p}\}
, where parameter
\alpha_{p}\propto[2\mu_{B}H_{c2}/(\pi T_{c0})]^{2}
is responsible for paramagnetic effect. Note that strong spin-orbit scattering can suppress paramagnetic mechanism of superconductivity destruction
Werthamer et al. 1966
;
Bulaevskii et al. 1986
. In dirty samples with normal scattering is much more frequent, than spin-orbit one, the paramagnetic parameter can be estimated as
\alpha_{p}\approx[2\mu_{B}H_{c2}/(\pi T_{c0})]^{2}(1+\lambda_{scat})^{-1}
, where
\lambda_{scat}=2/(3\pi T_{c0}\tau_{so})
and
\tau_{so}
is spin-orbit scattering time
Werthamer et al. 1966
. If spin-orbit scattering is strong, i.e.
\pi T_{c0}\tau_{so}\lesssim 1
, the paramagnetic parameter
\alpha_{p}
can be significantly reduced.

The optimal modulation vector minimizing the GL functional Eq.(
10
) has the form:

K_{y}=\frac{2}{3p_{soc}\tilde{H}_{0}A^{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{3}{4}p_{soc}^{2}\tilde{H}_{0}^{2}A^{2}}-1\right),

(11)

where
A=2\sqrt{b}\xi/L
. Since at the superconducting phase transition
G=0
, the change in the critical temperature
\Delta T_{c}=(T_{c}-T_{c0})
caused by magnetic field and spin-orbit coupling is the following:

\frac{\Delta T_{c}}{T_{c0}}=\frac{4\xi(0)^{2}}{L^{2}}\biggl[-K_{y}^{2}-\frac{\eta}{2}\tilde{H}_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}p_{soc}\tilde{H}_{0}K_{y}(1-A^{2}K_{y}^{2})\biggr],

(12)

where
K_{y}
is determined by Eq.(
11
).

The typical dependencies
\Delta T_{c}(H_{0})
for different SOC parameters are shown in Fig.
3
. Note that for
L\sim 0.2\xi
the parameter
p_{soc}=12
correspond to SOC constant
s_{soc}\sim 0.12
, which is reasonable value. We also assume strong spin-orbit scattering in the system, which decrease paramagnetic contribution to
\Delta T_{c}
.

Our calculations show, that for reasonable choice of parameters the superconducting critical temperature
T_{c}
of a thin (
L\ll\xi
) superconducting film with interfacial Rashba SOC can be enhanced by external magnetic field in wide range of the fields, see. Fig.
3
. This enhancement is caused by Lifshitz invariant in the free energy functional Eq.
1
. The results (see. Fig.
3
) are in qualitative agreement with the experimental data obtained in Refs.[
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
;
Asaba et al. 2018
]. Note that we expect, that in all systems experimentally studied in Refs.[
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
;
Asaba et al. 2018
] spin-orbit scattering is strong.

Interfacial Rashba SOC can also modify Little-Parks oscillations of
T_{c}
[see Fig.
5
]. To show this, let us consider a hollow superconducting cylinder having internal (external) radius
R_{1}
(
R_{2}
) with broken inversion symmetry at the internal surface, resulting in Rashba spin-orbit interaction, placed into external magnetic field
{\bf H}_{0}=H_{0}{\bf e}_{z}
, see Fig.
4
. We assume the cylinder to be thin-walled, i.e.
\Delta R=(R_{2}-R_{1})\ll\xi(0)
. In such the case the order parameter in cylindrical coordinate system
(r,\varphi,z)
read as
\psi({\bf r})=\psi_{0}\exp{(il\varphi)}
, where
l
is integer angular momentum. As a result, the relative change of the critical temperature
\tau=(T_{c}-T_{c0})/T_{c0}
is the following:

\tau=-\frac{(l+\mu)^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2\alpha_{p}}{\rho^{4}}\mu^{2}-\frac{2s_{soc}}{\rho^{4}\delta}\mu(l+\mu)\times\\
\times\left[1-\tilde{A}^{2}(l+\mu)^{2}\right],

(13)

Here
\mu=\Phi/\Phi_{0}
, where
\Phi=\pi H_{0}R_{1}^{2}
is external magnetic flux through the cylinder,
\rho=R_{1}/\xi(0)
,
\delta=\Delta R/R_{1}
and
\tilde{A}=\sqrt{b}\xi/R_{1}
.

Figure 5:
The superconducting critical temperature
T_{c}
of a hollow thin-walled cylinder as a function of external magnetic flux
\Phi
. Here Rashba SOC parameter
s_{soc}=0.12
for red curve, while SOC is absent for blue and green ones. The value of paramagnetic effect (PME) is
\tilde{\chi}=0.24
for red and green curve. The blue curve demonstrates Little-Parks (LP) oscillations without both PME and SOC. For all curves
R_{1}/\xi(0)=4
,
\Delta R/\xi(0)=0.2
and
b\sim 1
.

The first term in Eq.(
13
) describes usual Little-Parks oscillations
Parks and Little 1964
. The second one is responsible for suppression of
T_{c}
due to paramagnetic effect. In contrary, the last term in Eq.(
13
), caused by the combination of interfacial Rashba SOC and external magnetic field, lead to the enhancement of
T_{c}
.
As a result, the envelope function of
T_{c}(\Phi)
dependence demonstrates nonmonotonic behaviour: it grows initially, reaches the maximum and then declines. This nonmonotonic envelope is superimposed by Little-Parks like oscillations [see Fig.
5
]. As a result, for reasonable choice of parameters the
T_{c}
of hollow thin-walled cylinder could be enhanced, but not suppressed, by external magnetic flux.

In conclusion, we show that the critical temperature of thin superconducting film with broken inversion symmetry and strong SOC in the bulk can be enhanced by external magnetic field, see Fig.
3
. This effect is caused by Lifshitz invariant in GL functional, which results from combination of external magnetic field and interfacial Rashba SOC, appearing, on its tern, due to lack of structural inversion symmetry combined with bulk SOC. The results are in qualitative agreement with the experimental data
Jeffrey Gardner et al. 2011
;
Asaba et al. 2018
. The same mechanism can cause the modification of Little-Parks oscillations in cylindrical geometry, see Fig.
5
.

On diagonal digraphs, Koszul algebras and triangulations of homology spheres
Sergei O. Ivanov, Lev Mukoseev

Abstract
. We study magnitude homology of digraphs, with a particular focus on diagonal digraphs, i.e., digraphs whose magnitude homology is concentrated on the diagonal. For any digraph G G , we provide a complete description of the second magnitude homology MH 2 , k ​ ( G ) \mathrm{MH}_{2,k}(G) . This allows us to define a combinatorial condition, denoted by ( 𝒱 ℓ ) (\mathcal{V}_{\ell}) , which is equivalent to the vanishing of MH 2 , k ​ ( G , ℤ ) {\rm MH}_{2,k}(G,\mathbb{Z}) for all k > ℓ k>\ell . In particular, diagonal digraphs satisfy ( 𝒱 2 ) (\mathcal{V}_{2}) . As a corollary, we obtain that the 2-dimensional CW-complex obtained from a diagonal undirected graph by attaching 2-cells to all squares and triangles of the graph is simply connected. We also give an interpretation of diagonality in terms of Koszul algebras: a digraph G G is diagonal if and only if the distance algebra σ ​ G \sigma G is Koszul over any field, and if and only if G G satisfies ( 𝒱 2 ) (\mathcal{V}_{2}) and the path cochain algebra Ω ∙ ​ ( G ) \Omega^{\bullet}(G) is Koszul over any field. To provide a source of examples of digraphs, we study the extended Hasse diagram G ^ K \hat{G}_{K} of a pure simplicial complex K K . For a triangulation K K of a topological manifold M M , we express the non-diagonal part of the magnitude homology of G ^ K \hat{G}_{K} in terms of the homology of M M . As a corollary, we obtain that if K K is a triangulation of a closed manifold M M , then G ^ K \hat{G}_{K} is diagonal if and only if M M is a homology sphere.



1.
Introduction

Hepworth and Willerton in
[
28
]
introduced magnitude homology of a graph as a categorification of the magnitude of a graph studied by Leinster
[
32
]
. This concept was later generalized by Leinster and Shulman to arbitrary generalized metric spaces and to enriched categories with some additional data
[
33
]
. Hepworth
[
25
]
developed the theory of magnitude cohomology. Kaneta and Yoshinaga pointed out the complexity of magnitude homology of graphs by showing that the homology of any pure simplicial complex can be embedded into the magnitude homology of some graph
[
30
]
. Asao studied this theory in the setting of digraphs and showed that it is related to path homology theory
[
1
]
. Asao together with the first-named author proved that the magnitude homology and cohomology of a digraph
G
can be presented in terms of the Tor and Ext functors over a graded algebra
\sigma G
, which we call the distance algebra of
G

[
3
]
.

Hepworth and Willerton call a graph diagonal if its integral magnitude homology groups are concentrated in the diagonal degrees. They provide many examples: trees, complete graphs, the icosahedral graph (see also
[
23
]
), and the join of two arbitrary graphs. The box product of two diagonal graphs is also diagonal; in particular, hypercubes are diagonal. Median graphs, which are retracts of hypercubes (see
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
), are also diagonal. Consequently, square graphs are diagonal
[
36
]
. Diagonal digraphs can be defined similarly.

This article can be roughly divided into four parts. In the first part, we show that the magnitude homology of a digraph is isomorphic to a subquotient of its path algebra. This description, reminiscent of Gruenberg’s description of group homology, is referred to as the Gruenberg formula. In the second part, we use the Gruenberg formula to provide a complete description of the second magnitude homology
\mathrm{MH}_{2,\ell}(G)
for any
\ell
and any finite digraph
G
. From this, we derive a necessary condition for a digraph to be diagonal.

The third part connects the diagonality of a digraph to representation theory through the theory of Koszul algebras. Finally, in the fourth part, inspired by the work of Kaneta and Yoshinaga, we study digraphs associated with simplicial complexes, which we call extended Hasse diagrams. We examine conditions under which a digraph corresponding to a triangulation of a manifold is diagonal.

1.1.
The Gruenberg formula

In
[
22
]
, Gruenberg shows that any presentation of a group
\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{R}
defines an explicit projective resolution of the trivial module, described in terms of the ideals
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{r}
of the free group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{F}]
. Here,
\mathbf{f}
denotes the augmentation ideal, and
\mathbf{r}
is the kernel of the map
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{F}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]
. Since the homology of
\mathcal{G}
can be presented as Tor functors,

(1.1)

H_{n}(\mathcal{G})={\rm Tor}_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}),

this resolution was used to obtain the following isomorphisms for group homology
[
21
, §3.7]
:

(1.2)

H_{2n}(\mathcal{G})\cong\frac{\mathbf{r}^{n}\cap\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n-1}\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}+\mathbf{r}^{n}\mathbf{f}},\hskip 28.45274ptH_{2n+1}(\mathcal{G})\cong\frac{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}\cap\mathbf{r}^{n}\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{r}^{n+1}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}\mathbf{f}}.

Apparently, the idea of constructing such a resolution first appeared in the article by Eilenberg, Nagao, and Nakayama
[
14
]
in 1956. However, it was Gruenberg who popularized this resolution in group homology theory in 1960. This idea was also used by Bachmann
[
5
]
for augmented algebras in 1972 and by Govorov
[
17
, Lemma 1]
for graded algebras in 1973. For the case of finite-dimensional algebras, the same idea appeared in the work of Bongartz
[
9
]
and Butler and King
[
11
]
. We establish a general version of the Gruenberg formula that covers all of these statements (Theorem
2.9
). In this general version of the theorem, we replace the algebra
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{F}]
with an arbitrary quasi-free algebra.

For a finite digraph
G
and a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
, we consider the distance algebra
\sigma G
, which is a graded algebra defined as a quotient of the path algebra:

(1.3)

\sigma G=\mathbb{K}G/R,

where
R
is a homogeneous ideal generated by differences of shortest paths having the same initial and terminal vertices, and by all long paths (i.e., non-shortest paths). In
[
3
, Th. 6.2]
, Asao and the first-named author proved that magnitude homology with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
can be regarded as the Tor functor over the distance algebra:

(1.4)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)\cong\mathrm{Tor}^{\sigma G}_{n,\ell}(S,S),

where
S=(\sigma G)_{0}
. We also denote by
J
the ideal of
\mathbb{K}G
generated by all arrows. Using the Tor description of magnitude homology, the fact that
\mathbb{K}G
is a quasi-free algebra, and our general version of the Gruenberg formula, we prove the following formulas for magnitude homology (Theorem
3.5
):

(1.5)

\mathrm{MH}_{2n,*}(G)\cong\frac{R^{n}\cap JR^{n-1}J}{JR^{n}+R^{n}J},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathrm{MH}_{2n+1,*}(G)\cong\frac{JR^{n}\cap R^{n}J}{R^{n+1}+JR^{n}J}.

1.2.
The second magnitude homology group

For the second magnitude homology group, the Gruenberg formula takes the form:

(1.6)

\mathrm{MH}_{2,*}(G)\cong\frac{R}{JR+RJ}.

Asao proves that
\mathrm{MH}_{2,\ell}(G)
is the zeroth homology group of a pair of simplicial complexes
[
4
]
. In particular,
\mathrm{MH}_{2,\ell}(G)
is a free
\mathbb{K}
-module. The formula (
1.6
) allows us to describe its basis. This provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the vanishing of
{\rm MH}_{2,k}(G)
for
k>\ell
. We denote this condition by
(\mathcal{V}_{\ell})
and describe it below.

In order to describe the property
(\mathcal{V}_{\ell})
, we need to introduce additional terminology. We say that two paths
p=(p_{0},\dots,p_{n})
and
q=(q_{0},\dots,q_{m})
in
G
connect the same vertices if
p_{0}=q_{0}
and
p_{n}=q_{m}
. An equivalence relation
\sim
on the set of paths is called a
congruence
if
p\sim\tilde{p}
implies that
p
and
\tilde{p}
connect the same vertices and
qpq^{\prime}\sim q\tilde{p}q^{\prime}
for any
q
and
q^{\prime}
such that the concatenation is defined. The
\ell
-short congruence
is the minimal congruence on the set of paths such that any two shortest paths connecting the same two vertices at distance at most
\ell
are equivalent. A path that is not shortest is called long.

A long path is called
\ell
-reducible
if it contains a long subpath of length at most
\ell
. A long path is called
\ell
-quasi-reducible
if it is
\ell
-shortly congruent to an
\ell
-reducible path. Then
\mathrm{MH}_{2,k}(G)=0
for any
k>\ell
if and only if the following condition is satisfied (Theorem
4.10
):

(\mathcal{V}_{\ell})

Any two shortest paths connecting the same vertices are
\ell
-shortly congruent, and any long path is
\ell
-quasi-reducible.

A long path is called minimal if all of its proper subpaths are shortest. To verify the condition
(\mathcal{V}_{\ell})
for a given digraph, it is sufficient to consider the shortest paths and minimal long paths.

Note that any diagonal digraph satisfies the condition
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
, but this condition is not equivalent to diagonality. For instance, the digraphs (an undirected edge means two arrows in opposite directions)

(1.7)

satisfy
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
, but they are not diagonal, because
\mathrm{MH}_{3,4}(G,\mathbb{Z})\neq 0

(see computations in Examples
3.6
and
3.7
). Below, we construct a more conceptual example of a non-diagonal digraph satisfying
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
, which is defined by a combinatorial triangulation of
S^{1}\times I
.

Di and Zhang, together with the authors, studied a filtered simplicial set
\mathscr{N}^{*}(G)
, called the filtered nerve of
G
, in
[
13
, §1]
(see also
[
27
, §3]
). They considered the
\ell
-fundamental groupoid of a digraph
G
, defined as the fundamental groupoid of the simplicial set
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G)
. Similarly, we define the
\ell
-fundamental category
\tau^{\ell}(G)
of a digraph
G
as the fundamental category of the simplicial set
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G)
. We prove that for a digraph
G
and an integer
\ell\geq 2
,

(1.8)

\mathrm{MH}_{2,k}(G)=0\text{ for }k>\ell\hskip 14.22636pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.22636pt\tau^{\ell}(G)\text{ is a thin category.}

As a corollary, we obtain that the GLMY-fundamental group of a diagonal undirected graph is trivial (Corollary
4.21
).

It is known
[
20
, Corollary 4.5]
that
\pi^{\sf GLMY}_{1}(G)
is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}({\sf CW}^{2}(G))
, where
{\sf CW}^{2}(G)
is the two-dimensional CW-complex obtained from the geometric realization of
G
by attaching 2-cells to all its squares and triangles. Therefore, we conclude that

(1.9)

G\text{ is a diagonal undirected graph}\hskip 14.22636pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.22636pt{\sf CW}^{2}(G)\text{ is simply connected.}

It is worth noting that the path homology of a diagonal undirected graph is also trivial
[
1
, Prop. 8.6]
, and the bigraded path homology is also trivial
[
26
]
. Thus, diagonal undirected graphs, in some sense, behave like contractible spaces from the viewpoint of GLMY theory.

1.3.
Diagonal digraphs and Koszul algebras

For a digraph
G
, Grigor’yan–Lin–Muranov–Yau consider a dg-algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)=\Omega^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{K})
, whose cohomology is the path cohomology
{\rm PH}^{n}(G)=H^{n}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G))
, and whose elements are called “
d
-invariant forms”
[
18
, §3.4]
. The algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
will be referred to as the path cochain algebra. We prove that
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
is isomorphic to a quotient of the path algebra:

(1.10)

\Omega^{\bullet}(G)\cong\mathbb{K}G/T,

where
T
is an ideal generated by quadratic relations
t_{x,y}
indexed by pairs of vertices at distance two (Theorem
5.4
). By a result of Hepworth
[
25
, Th. 6.2]
, the latter quotient algebra is isomorphic to the diagonal part of the magnitude cohomology algebra:

(1.11)

\Omega^{\bullet}(G)\cong\mathrm{MH}^{\sf diag}(G).

This is a dual version of the result of Asao
[
1
, Lemma 6.8]
.

We further show that, if
G
satisfies
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
and
\mathbb{K}
is a field, then the distance algebra
\sigma G
is quadratic and quadratic dual to the path cochain algebra:

(1.12)

(\sigma G)^{!}\cong\Omega^{\bullet}(G^{\text{op}}).

Using this, we obtain a characterization of diagonal digraphs in terms of Koszul algebras (Theorem
6.5
). Namely, we prove that the following statements about a finite digraph
G
are equivalent:

(1)

G
is diagonal;

(2)

\sigma G
is Koszul for any field
\mathbb{K}
;

(3)

G
satisfies
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
and
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
is Koszul for any field
\mathbb{K}
.

For a digraph
G
satisfying
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
, we also give an explicit description of the Koszul complex of the quadratic algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
(Proposition
6.6
). This provides another equivalent description of diagonal digraphs from a completely different viewpoint (Corollary
6.7
).

1.4.
Extended Hasse diagrams

Here we develop the ideas of Kaneta–Yoshinaga
[
30
, §5.3]
in the setting of digraphs. For a pure simplicial complex
K
, we consider a digraph
\hat{G}_{K}
, whose vertices are the simplices of
K
together with two additional vertices
\hat{0}
and
\hat{1}
. There are three types of arrows in
\hat{G}_{K}
: pairs of simplices
(\sigma,\tau)
, where
\sigma
is a face of
\tau
such that
\dim(\tau)=\dim(\sigma)+1
; pairs of the form
(\hat{0},\sigma)
, where
\sigma
is a
0
-simplex; and pairs of the form
(\sigma,\hat{1})
, where
\sigma
is a maximal simplex. The digraph
\hat{G}_{K}
will be referred to as the extended Hasse diagram of
K
.

We describe the non-diagonal part of the magnitude homology
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})
for a triangulation
K
of a topological manifold (Theorem
7.8
). Namely, we prove that, for a triangulation
K
of a topological manifold with boundary
M
and
n\neq\ell
, we have

(1.13)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})\cong\begin{cases}\bar{H}_{n-2}(M),&\ell-2=\dim(M),\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

As a corollary, we obtain that for a triangulation
K
of a closed manifold
M
of dimension at least one, the digraph
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal if and only if
M
is a homology sphere. Therefore, the Koszul property for
\hat{G}_{K}
is a topological invariant of
M
. Note that the Koszul property has already appeared in the literature as a topological invariant in a similar context
[
35
]
.

Since any smooth manifold with boundary has a triangulation, this theorem allows us to construct interesting examples of (non-)diagonal digraphs. For example, take
2\leq n_{0}<\ell_{0}
and consider a triangulation
K
of the product
M=S^{n_{0}-2}\times I^{\ell_{0}-n_{0}}
. Then the non-diagonal part of the magnitude homology groups of
\hat{G}_{K}
is concentrated in degree
(n_{0},\ell_{0})
.

2.
A general version of the Gruenberg formula

In this section we present some background related to quasi-free algebras and prove a general version of the Gruenberg formula for a quotient algebra of a quasi-free algebra
A=F/I.
In the following sections we use this formula for the path algebra associated with a digraph
F=\mathbb{K}G.

2.1.
Quasi-free algebras

We fix a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
and denote by
\mathbb{G}
an abelian group. For future references, we will work in the setting of
\mathbb{G}
-graded algebras over
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb{G}
-graded modules over them in this section, but in the next sections we will only use
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}.
In this section the term ‘graded’ means ‘
\mathbb{G}
-graded’. All tensor products and hom-sets in this subsection will be
\mathbb{G}
-graded. Some background about
\mathbb{G}
-graded algebras and modules can be found in
[
3
, Appendix]
. For a graded algebra
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\mathbb{G}}A_{g}
, we consider the graded algebra
A^{e}=A^{op}\otimes A.
Then any graded
A
-bimodule can be identified with a right graded
A^{e}
-module.

A graded algebra
F
is called
quasi-free
if the projective dimension of
F
in the category of graded
F
-bimodules is at most one. If we set

\Omega^{1}_{F}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mu:F\otimes F\to F),
where
\mu(a\otimes b)=ab,
then we obtain a short exact sequence of graded
F
-bimodules

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{F}\longrightarrow F\otimes F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow 0,

where
F\otimes F
is a free graded bimodule of rank one. Therefore
F
is quasi-free if and only if
\Omega^{1}_{F}
is a projective graded
F
-bimodule (see
[
12
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
8
]
).

We also prove that under some conditions right homogeneous ideals of a quasi-free graded algebra
F
are projective graded
F
-modules.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F
be a quasi-free graded algebra and
M
be a right graded
F
-module, which is projective as a
\mathbb{K}
-module. Then the projective dimension of
M
in the category of graded
F
-modules is at most one.

Proof.

It is well known that for any three rings
A,B,C
and any
(A,B)
-bimodule
X
there is a pair of adjoint functors

(2.2)

X\otimes_{B}-:{\rm Bimod}(B,C)\leftrightarrows{\rm Bimod}(A,C):\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(X,-).

In other words, there is a natural isomorphism (see
[
15
, §III.11.4]
)

(2.3)

\mathrm{Hom}_{(A,C)}(X\otimes_{B}Y,Z)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{(B,C)}(Y,\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(X,Z)).

If we take
X=M,

Y=P,

A=\mathbb{K},

B=C=F,
we obtain an isomorphism

(2.4)

\mathrm{Hom}_{F}(M\otimes_{F}P,-)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{F^{e}}(P,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(M,-)).

Since
M
is projective over
\mathbb{K}
and
P
is projective over
F^{e},
we obtain that the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{F^{e}}(P,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(M,-))
is exact. Therefore
M\otimes_{F}P
is a projective right graded
F
-module. Now consider a projective resolution in the category of graded bimodules
P_{1}\rightarrowtail P_{0}\twoheadrightarrow F.
Since
F
is projective as a left
F
-module, the sequence splits as a short exact sequence of left graded modules. Therefore, tensoring by
M
over
F
we obtain a short exact sequence
M\otimes_{F}P_{1}\rightarrowtail M\otimes_{F}P_{0}\twoheadrightarrow M.
Therefore,
M\otimes_{F}P_{1}\rightarrowtail M\otimes_{F}P_{0}
is a projective resolution of
M
of length
1.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
F
be a quasi-free graded algebra and
I
be a right homogeneous ideal of
F
such that
F/I
is projective over
\mathbb{K}.
Then
I
is a projective graded right
F
-module.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
we know that
F/I
has projective dimension over
F
at most one. Therefore, the short exact sequence
I\rightarrowtail F\twoheadrightarrow F/I
implies that the projective dimension of
I
over
F
is
0.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F
be a quasi-free graded algebra,
J
be a right homogeneous ideal of
F,
and
I
be a two-sided homogeneous ideal of
F.
Assume that
F/I
and
F/J
are projective over
\mathbb{K}.
Then
F/JI
is also projective over
\mathbb{K}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2

J
is projective as a right
F
-module. Then
J/JI\cong J\otimes_{F}F/I
is projective as a right
F/I
-module. Since
F/I
is projective over
\mathbb{K},
we obtain that
J/JI
is also projective over
\mathbb{K}.
Then the short exact sequence
J/JI\rightarrowtail F/JI\twoheadrightarrow F/J
implies that
F/JI
is also projective over
\mathbb{K}.

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
F
be a quasi-free graded algebra,
J
be a right homogeneous ideal of
F,
and
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
be two-sided homogeneous ideals of
F.
Assume that
F/J
and
F/I_{1},\dots,F/I_{n}
are projective over
\mathbb{K}.
Then
F/(JI_{1}\dots I_{n})
is also projective over
\mathbb{K}
and
JI_{1}\dots I_{n}
is a projective graded right
F
-module.

Proof.

It follows by induction from Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Next, we give two examples of quasi-free algebras:
the path algebra
\mathbb{K}Q
of a quiver
Q
and the group algebra
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{F}]
of a free group
\mathcal{F}.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
be a quiver with a finite set of vertices
Q_{0}
equipped with a function
|\cdot|:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{G}.
Then the graded path algebra
\mathbb{K}Q
is quasi-free, where the grading is defined so that the degree of a path
\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{n}
is the sum
|\alpha_{1}|+\dots+|\alpha_{n}|
and the degree of an idempotent
e_{x},x\in Q_{0}
is zero.

Proof.

Set
F=\mathbb{K}Q
and consider the following sequence of bimodules

(2.5)

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}Fe_{s(\alpha)}\otimes\mathbb{K}\alpha\otimes e_{t(\alpha)}F\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{x\in Q_{0}}Fe_{x}\otimes e_{x}F\overset{\mu}{\longrightarrow}F\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mu(a\otimes b)=ab
and
\partial(a\otimes\alpha\otimes b)=a\alpha\otimes b-a\otimes\alpha b.
We claim that it is a projective resolution of
F
in the category of bimodules. Since
Fe_{x}\otimes e_{y}F
is a direct summand of
F\otimes F,
it is a projective
F
-bimodule. So we only need to prove that (
2.5
) is a short exact sequence. In order to do this we construct homomorphisms of right
F
-modules
\sigma:F\to\bigoplus_{x\in Q_{0}}Fe_{x}\otimes e_{x}F
and
\tau:\bigoplus_{x\in Q_{0}}Fe_{x}\otimes e_{x}F\to\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}Fe_{s(\alpha)}\otimes\mathbb{K}\alpha\otimes e_{t(\alpha)}F
such that

(2.6)

\sigma\mu+\partial\tau={\rm id},\hskip 14.22636pt\mu\sigma={\rm id},\hskip 14.22636pt\tau\partial={\rm id}.

The morphisms are defined by formulas
\sigma(a)=e_{x}\otimes a,a\in e_{x}F
and

(2.7)

\tau(\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{n}\otimes a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{i-1}\otimes\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha_{i+1}\dots\alpha_{n}a,

where
\alpha_{i}\in Q_{1}
and
\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{n}
is a path to a vertex
x
and
a\in e_{x}F.
Now the equations (
2.6
) can be verified by a direct computation.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

A free graded algebra
\mathbb{K}\langle X\rangle
with a grading defined by any map
|\cdot|:X\to\mathbb{G}
is quasi-free.

Proof.

A free algebra can be presented as a path algebra of a quiver with one vertex.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(X)
be a free group generated by a set
X.
Then the (non-graded) group algebra
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{F}]
is quasi-free.

Proof.

Set
F=\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{F}]
and
consider the following sequence of
F
-bimodules

(2.8)

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in X}F\otimes\mathbb{K}x\otimes F\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}F\otimes F\overset{\mu}{\longrightarrow}F\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mu(a\otimes b)=ab
and
\partial(a\otimes x\otimes b)=ax\otimes b-a\otimes xb.
It is easy to see that
F\otimes F
and
F\otimes\mathbb{K}x\otimes F
are free bimodules. So we only need to show that the sequence
2.8
is exact. In order to do this we construct homomorphisms of right
F
-modules
\sigma:F\to F\otimes F
and
\tau:F\otimes F\to\bigoplus_{x\in X}F\otimes\mathbb{K}x\otimes F
such that the equations (
2.6
) are satisfied. The map
\sigma
is defined by the formula
\sigma(a)=1\otimes a.
In order to define
\tau
we first define it for an element of the form
x^{\varepsilon}\otimes 1,
where
x\in X
and
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}.
It is defined by the formula

(2.9)

\tau(x^{\varepsilon}\otimes 1)=\begin{cases}1\otimes x\otimes 1,&\varepsilon=1\\
-x^{-1}\otimes x\otimes x^{-1},&\varepsilon=-1.\end{cases}

note that we have

(2.10)

\partial(\tau(x^{\varepsilon}\otimes 1))=x^{\varepsilon}\otimes 1-1\otimes x^{\varepsilon}.

In general,
\tau
is defined so that

(2.11)

\tau(x_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\otimes a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots x_{i-1}^{\varepsilon_{i-1}}\tau(x_{i}^{\varepsilon_{i}}\otimes 1)x_{i+1}^{\varepsilon_{i+1}}\dots x_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}a.

Now, using the formula (
2.10
) and the fact that

is a bimodule homomorphism, it is easy to check the equations (
2.6
).
∎

2.2.
The Gruenberg formula

The following theorem is a generalization of the Gruenberg resolution
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2.8

(The Gruenberg resolution)
.

Let
F
be a quasi-free graded algebra over a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
,
I
be a two-sided homogeneous ideal of
F
and
J
be a right homogeneous ideal of
F
such that
I\subseteq J.
Assume that
F/I
and
F/J
are projective
\mathbb{K}
-modules and set
A=F/I
and
S=F/J.
Then there is a projective resolution
P_{\bullet}
of the graded right
A
-module
S

(2.12)

\dots\to\frac{JI}{JI^{2}}\to\frac{I}{I^{2}}\to\frac{J}{JI}\to\frac{F}{I}\to S

such that

(2.13)

P_{2n}=\frac{I^{n}}{I^{n+1}},\hskip 28.45274ptP_{2n+1}=\frac{JI^{n}}{JI^{n+1}}

and the differential and the augmentation are induced by the embeddings
JI^{n}\hookrightarrow I^{n}\hookrightarrow JI^{n-1}.

Proof.

The fact that the chain complex
\dots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to S\to 0
is exact is obvious. Proposition
2.4
implies that
I^{n},JI^{n}
are projective
F
-modules. Therefore
I^{n}/I^{n+1},JI^{n}/JI^{n+1}
are projective
A
-modules.
∎

If
A
is a graded algebra, and
M,M^{\prime}
are a right and left graded
A
-modules, then we can consider a bigraded Tor functor

(2.14)

\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{*,*}(M,M^{\prime})

which is defined as
\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{n,g}(M,M^{\prime})=(H_{n}(P_{\bullet}\otimes_{A}M^{\prime}))_{g}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
g\in\mathbb{G},
where
P_{\bullet}
is a graded projective resolution of
M
and
\otimes_{A}
denoted the graded tensor product over
A
(see
[
3
, §7.2]
).

The following theorem is a generalization of the classical Gruenberg formula
[
21
, §3.7]
and a generalization of a similar statement of Govorov in the case of graded algebras
[
17
, Lemma 1]
.

Theorem 2.9

(The Gruenberg formula)
.

Let
F
be a quasi-free graded algebra over a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
,
I
be a two-sided homogeneous ideal of
F
,
J
be a right homogeneous ideal of
F,
and
J^{\prime}
be a left homogeneous ideal of
F
such that
I\subseteq J
and
I\subseteq J^{\prime}.
Assume that
F/I
and
F/J
are projective
\mathbb{K}
-modules and set

(2.15)

A=F/I,\hskip 14.22636ptS=F/J,\hskip 14.22636ptS^{\prime}=F/J^{\prime}.

Then for
n\geq 0
there are isomorphisms of graded modules

(2.16)

\mathrm{Tor}_{2n,*}^{A}(S,S^{\prime})\cong\frac{I^{n}\cap JI^{n-1}J^{\prime}}{JI^{n}+I^{n}J^{\prime}},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{2n+1,*}(S,S^{\prime})\cong\frac{JI^{n}\cap I^{n}J^{\prime}}{I^{n+1}+JI^{n}J^{\prime}}.

Proof.

For any right graded
A
-module
M
we have
M\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}=M/MJ^{\prime}.
Let
P_{\bullet}
be the Gruenberg resolution whose components are
P_{2n}=I^{n}/I^{n+1}
and
P_{2n+1}=JI^{n}/JI^{n+1}.
Then
P_{2n}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}\cong I^{n}/I^{n}J^{\prime}
and
P_{2n+1}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}\cong JI^{n}/JI^{n}J^{\prime}.
Therefore we have

(2.17)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Ker}(P_{2n}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}\to P_{2n-1}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime})&\cong(I^{n}\cap JI^{n}J^{\prime})/I^{n}J^{\prime},\\
\mathrm{Im}(P_{2n+1}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}\to P_{2n}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime})&\cong(JI^{n}+I^{n}J^{\prime})/I^{n}J^{\prime},\\
\mathrm{Ker}(P_{2n+1}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}\to P_{2n}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime})&\cong(JI^{n}\cap I^{n}J^{\prime})/JI^{n}J^{\prime},\\
\mathrm{Im}(P_{2n+2}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}\to P_{2n+1}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime})&\cong(I^{n+1}+JI^{n}J^{\prime})/JI^{n}J^{\prime}.\end{split}

This proves the claim.
∎

Remark 2.10

(Naturality of the Gruenberg formulas)
.

The Gruenberg formulas from Theorem
2.9
are natural in
S
and
S^{\prime}
in the following sense. Assume we have another two ideals
\tilde{J},\tilde{J}^{\prime}
satisfying the same assumptions as
J,J^{\prime}
such that
J\subseteq\tilde{J}
and
J^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{J}^{\prime}.
If we set
\tilde{S}=F/\tilde{J}
and
\tilde{S}^{\prime}=F/\tilde{J}^{\prime},
we have epimorphisms of
A
-modules
S\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{S}
and
S^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{S}^{\prime},
that induce a morphism

(2.18)

\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{*,*}(S,S^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{*,*}(\tilde{S},\tilde{S}^{\prime}).

On the other hand, we have morphisms

(2.19)

\frac{I^{n}\cap JI^{n-1}J^{\prime}}{JI^{n}+I^{n}J^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\frac{I^{n}\cap\tilde{J}I^{n-1}\tilde{J}^{\prime}}{\tilde{J}I^{n}+I^{n}\tilde{J}^{\prime}},\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{JI^{n}\cap I^{n}J^{\prime}}{I^{n+1}+JI^{n}J^{\prime}}\to\frac{\tilde{J}I^{n}\cap I^{n}\tilde{J}^{\prime}}{I^{n+1}+\tilde{J}I^{n}\tilde{J}^{\prime}}

induced by inclusions. Then the isomorphisms (

) are consistent with morphisms (
2.18
) and (

As a corollary we obtain the classical result of Gruenberg.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a free group and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{R}
be its quotient group. Denote by
\bf f
the augmentation ideal of the group algebra

and set

Then there are isomorphisms

(2.20)

3.
The Gruenberg formula for the magnitude homology

In this section we recall the basic information about the magnitude homology groups of digraphs
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)
and about the decomposition into a direct sum of directed components
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)=\bigoplus_{x,y}\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}
. We will recall the theorem about the presentation of the magnitude homology groups as a Tor functor

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)=\mathrm{Tor}^{\sigma G}_{n,\ell}(S,S)

and prove a version of it for the directed components

(3.1)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}=\mathrm{Tor}^{\sigma G}_{n,\ell}(S_{x},S_{y}).

Using this we will prove the Gruenberg formula for the magnitude homology groups (Theorem
3.5
).
We will also demonstrate the computational convenience of this formula with examples (Example
3.6
, Example
3.7
).

3.1.
Magnitude homology

Let
G
be a digraph. For any vertices
x,y
of
G
we denote by
d(x,y)
the infimum of lengths of paths from
x
to
y.
For a tuple of vertices
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
we say that
n
is the length of the tuple and

(3.2)

|x_{0},\dots,x_{n}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d(x_{i},x_{i+1})

is the norm of the tuple. For any nonnegative integer
\ell
the chain complex of
\mathbb{K}
-modules
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
is defined so that

(3.3)

\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(G)=\mathbb{K}\cdot\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mid|x_{0},\dots,x_{n}|=\ell,x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}\}

and the differential is defined by the formula
\partial_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\partial_{n,i},
where

(3.4)

\partial_{n,i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\begin{cases}(x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n}),&|x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n}|=\ell\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that
\partial_{n,0}=\partial_{n,n}=0.
The magnitude homology with coefficients in a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
is defined as the homology of this complex
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)=H_{n}(\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)).
If we need to specify the commutative ring
\mathbb{K},
we use the notations
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})
and
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K}).

Following
[
4
, Prop.2.9]

[
30
, Th.5.11]
,
[
2
]
,
[
26
, Lemma 9.10]
for any two vertices
x,y
of
G
we consider a free submodule
\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(x,y)\subseteq\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(G)
generated by tuples of the form
(x,x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},y).
It is easy to see that these submodules form a chain subcomplex
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(x,y)\subseteq\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
and there is a decomposition

(3.5)

\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)=\bigoplus_{x,y}\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}.

This decomposition induces a decomposition of homology groups

(3.6)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{x,y}\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}.

The group
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}
will be referred to as the directed component of the magnitude homology (in the direction from
x
to
y
).

The magnitude cohomology is defined by the formula
\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G)=H^{n}(\mathrm{MC}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)),
where

\mathrm{MC}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G),\mathbb{K}).

So, we can also consider the complexes
\mathrm{MC}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y},\mathbb{K}),
define the directed magnitude cohomology
\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}
as their cohomology and obtain

(3.7)

\mathrm{MC}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)\cong\prod_{x,y}\mathrm{MC}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G)=\prod_{x,y}\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}.

Recall
[
32
]
that the magnitude
{\rm Mag}(G)
of a finite digraph
G
with
m
vertices can be defined as an integral formal power series in
\mathbb{Z}[\![q]\!],

(3.8)

{\rm Mag}(G)=\sum_{x,y}(Z^{-1}_{G})_{x,y},

where
Z_{G}
is an
m\times m
matrix over
\mathbb{Z}[\![q]\!]
, indexed by vertices, defined by
(Z_{G})_{x,y}=q^{d(x,y)},
assuming
q^{\infty}=0.

The motivation for introducing the magnitude homology in
[
28
]
was that for a field
\mathbb{K}
we have

(3.9)

\mathrm{Mag}(G)=\sum_{n,\ell}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G))\cdot q^{\ell}.

Let us prove a component-wise version of this formula.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite digraph and
\mathbb{K}
be a field. Then for any
\ell\geq 0
and vertices
x,y
we have

(3.10)

(Z_{G}^{-1})_{x,y}=\sum_{n,\ell}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y})\cdot q^{\ell}.

Proof.

First we note that
\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(G)=0
for
n>\ell,
and the sum in the formula (
3.10
) is finite. Since
\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y})=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}),
it is sufficient to prove that
\mathrm{Mag}_{\ell}(G)_{x,y}=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}).
The matrix
Z_{G}
can be presented as
Z_{G}=I+\tilde{Z}_{G},
where
I
is the identity matrix and
(\tilde{Z}_{G})_{x,y}=q^{d(x,y)}
for
x\neq y
and
(\tilde{Z}_{G})_{x,x}=0.

Since all components of
\tilde{Z}_{G}
are divisible by
q,
the components of
\tilde{Z}_{G}^{n}
are divisible by
q^{n},
and we obtain
Z_{G}^{-1}=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\tilde{Z}_{G}^{n}.
It is easy to check by induction that
(\tilde{Z}_{G}^{n})_{x,y}=\sum_{x=x_{0}\neq\dots\neq x_{n}=y}q^{|x_{0},\dots,x_{n}|},
where the sum runs over all tuples
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}
for any
i
and
x_{0}=x,x_{n}=y.
Therefore,
(\tilde{Z}_{G}^{n})_{x,y}=\sum_{\ell}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y})\cdot q^{\ell}.
The result follows.
∎

3.2.
Non-normalized magnitude chain complex

For a general simplicial
\mathbb{K}
-module
X
, one can associate two chain complexes. The non-normalized chain complex
C(X)
whose components are
C_{n}(X)=X_{n}
and the differentials are defined by
\partial_{n}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\partial_{n,i},
where
\partial_{n,i}:X_{n}\to X_{n-1}
are the face maps. And the normalized chain complex
N(X),
which is a quotient of
C(X)
and it has components
N_{n}(X)=X_{n}/(\sum_{i}\sigma_{n-1,i}(X_{n-1})),
where
\sigma_{n-1,i}:X_{n-1}\to X_{n}
are the degeneracy maps. It is well known that the map
C(X)\twoheadrightarrow N(X)
is a chain homotopy equivalence. In particular
H_{*}(X):=H_{*}(C(X))\cong H_{*}(N(X)).

The chain complex
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
is a normalized chain complex of a simplicial
\mathbb{K}
-module
\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
freely generated by the magnitude simplicial set
\mathscr{M}^{\ell}(G)
defined in
[
13
, (1.9)]
(see also
[
33
, Definition 5.7]
). The components of
\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
are generated by tuples with possible consecutive repetitions

(3.11)

\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)=\mathbb{K}\cdot\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mid|x_{0},\dots,x_{n}|=\ell\}.

The face maps
\partial_{n,i}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
are defined by (
3.4
) and the degeneracy maps
\sigma_{n,i}
are defined by
\sigma_{n,i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{0},\dots,x_{i},x_{i},\dots,x_{n}).
In this case
\partial_{n,0}
and
\partial_{n,n}
are nontrivial maps:
\partial_{n,0}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
is nontrivial if and only if
x_{0}=x_{1},
and
\partial_{n,n}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
is nontrivial if and only if
x_{n-1}=x_{n}
. Therefore
\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
is chain homotopy equivalent to
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)
and

(3.12)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)\cong H_{n}(\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)).

Applying
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(-,\mathbb{K})
to chain homotopy equivalent complexes we obtain homotopy equivalent complexes. Therefore

(3.13)

\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G)\cong H^{n}(\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)),

where
\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G),\mathbb{K}).

Note that we can similarly define simplicial submodules

(3.14)

\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)

and obtain that the chain complex
\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}
and

(3.15)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}\cong H_{n}(\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}).

We also have

(3.16)

\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G)_{x,y}\cong H^{n}(\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}),

where
\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}^{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet,\ell}(G)_{x,y},\mathbb{K}).

3.3.
Magnitude homology is a derived functor

For a finite digraph
G
we consider the graded path algebra
\mathbb{K}G.
A path in
G
is a tuple of vertices

such that
(p_{i},p_{i+1})
is an arrow in
G

The number

A path

of
\mathbb{K}G

3.4.
The Gruenberg formula

Note that
\mathbb{K}G=\bigoplus_{x,y}e_{x}(\mathbb{K}G)e_{y}.
For any ideal

of

\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{b}}\cong\bigoplus_{x,y}\frac{e_{x}\mathfrak{a}e_{y}}{e_{x}\mathfrak{b}e_{y}}.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any finite digraph
G,

\mathbb{K}

).

Proof.

The isomorphisms (

, Proposition

and the fact that

and

Let us prove the isomorphism (

3.31

and Theorem

imply the isomorphism

\mathrm{MH}_{2n,*}(G)_{x,y}\cong\frac{R^{n}\cap J_{x}R^{n-1}J_{y}}{J_{x}R^{n}+R^{n}J_{y}}.

Naturality of the Gruenberg formulas

4.
The second magnitude homology

In this section, we use the Gruenberg formula to show that, for any finite digraph
G
, the second magnitude homology

is a free
\mathbb{K}
-module with an explicit basis (Theorem
4.5
). Furthermore, we use this basis to obtain, for a given
\ell

such that

for

(Theorem

). Using this description, we show that if

\ell
-fundamental category of

If there is a path

p_{0}

Definition 4.1

(Congruences and ideals for paths)
.

Consider the category of paths in a digraph

whose objects are vertices, morphisms are paths and composition is defined by the concatenation. We say that some equivalence relation on the set of paths is a congruence if it is a congruence on the category of paths i.e. if
p\sim\tilde{p},
then the
p
and
\tilde{p}
connect the same vertices and
qpq^{\prime}\sim q\tilde{p}q^{\prime}

p\in I
implies
qpq^{\prime}\in I
whenever the concatenation is defined. For example, the set of long paths is an ideal.

Definition 4.2

(

-short congruence) The

-short congruence is the minimal congruence on the set of paths such that any two shortest paths connecting the same two vertices at distance at most
\ell

Proposition 4.9

-shortly congruent;

(2)

any minimal long path of length

(\ell-1)
-shortly congruent to a non-minimal long path.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary

.
∎

A long path is called
\ell

if it contains a long subpath of length at most
\ell.
Then a non-minimal long path of length

-reducible path of length

\ell

\ell
-shortly congruent to an
\ell
-reducible path. Note that the set of all
\ell

\ell

(\mathcal{V}_{\ell})

any two shortest paths connecting the same vertices are
\ell
-shortly congruent; and any long path is
\ell
-quasi-reducible.

First we prove that two shortest paths connecting the same vertices are

s
and
\tilde{s}
are two shortest paths of length

Since they are
(\ell+n-1)
-shortly congruent, there is a sequence of shortest paths
s=s_{0},\dots,s_{m}=\tilde{s}
such that for any
0\leq i\leq m-1
we have
s_{i}=p_{i}t_{i}p^{\prime}_{i}
and
s_{i+1}=p_{i}\tilde{t}_{i}p_{i}^{\prime}
such that
t_{i}
and
\tilde{t}_{i}
are shortest paths of length
\leq\ell+k-1.
By the inductive assumption,
t_{i}
and
\tilde{t}_{i}
are
\ell
-shortly congruent. Therefore,
s_{i}
and
s_{i+1}
are
\ell
-shortly congruent. Hence
s
and
\tilde{s}
are
\ell
-shortly congruent.

Let us prove that any long path is
\ell
-quasi-reducible. Any long path of length at most
\ell
is
\ell
-reducible. So we can assume that the long path has length
\ell+n
for some
n\geq 1.
Since we already know that any two shortest paths connecting the same vertices are
\ell
-shortly congruent, the
(\ell+n-1)
-short congruence coincides with the
\ell
-short congruence for any
n\geq 1.
Then Proposition
4.9
implies that for any
n\geq 1
any long path
p
of length
\ell+n
is
\ell
-shortly congruent to an
(\ell+n-1)
-reducible long path
\tilde{p}
. Choose a long subpath
\hat{p}
of length
(\ell+n-1)
in
\tilde{p}.
By inductive assumption
\hat{p}
is
\ell
-quasi-reducible. Since the set of
\ell
-quasi-reducible paths is an ideal, we obtain that
\tilde{p}
and

are also
\ell
-quasi-reducible.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Any diagonal digraph satisfies
(\mathcal{V}_{2}).

4.3.
Thinness of the fundamental category

In this subsection, for a digraph
G
and a positive integer
\ell,
we define a category
\tau^{\ell}(G)
that we call
\ell
-fundamental category of
G
.
It is closely related with the
\ell
-fundamental groupoid
\Pi^{\ell}(G)
and
\ell
-fundamental group
\pi^{\ell}_{1}(G)
studied in
[
13
]
. We will show that if there is an integer
\ell
such that
\mathrm{MH}_{2,k}(G)=0
for any
k>\ell,
then
\tau^{\ell}(G)
is a thin category.

We denote by
\tau
the functor of the fundamental category of a simplicial set
\tau:{\sf sSets}\to{\sf Cat},
which is the left adjoint to the nerve functor
{\sf Nrv}:{\sf Cat}\to{\sf sSets}
. It can be explicitly described as follows (see
[
16
, §II.4.1]
,
[
29
, §1.3]
).
Let
X
be a simplicial set with face maps denoted by
\partial_{i}
and degeneracy maps denoted by
\sigma_{i}.
Then
\tau(X)
is the quotient of the free path category of its
1
-skeleton (treated as a quiver) by relations
\partial_{0}t\circ\partial_{2}t=\partial_{1}t,
where
t
is a
2
-simplex, and relations
\sigma_{0}x=1_{x},
where
\alpha
is a
1
-simplex. The composition is defined by the concatenation, and the identity morphisms are degenerate
1
-simplices
1_{x}=\sigma_{0}x
. The groupoid associated with
\tau(X)
is the fundamental groupoid of
X

[
16
, §II.7.1]

(4.13)

\Pi(X)=\tau(X)^{\sf gpd}.

Following
[
13
]
, we denote by
\mathscr{N}(G)
the nerve of a digraph
G,
which is a simplicial set, whose
n
-simplices are tuples of vertices
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that for any
0\leq i\leq n-1
there is a path from
x_{i}
to
x_{i+1}.
The face maps are defined by deleting a vertex, and the degeneracy maps are defined by doubling of vertices. This simplicial set has a natural filtration by simplicial subsets

(4.14)

\mathscr{N}^{1}(G)\subseteq\mathscr{N}^{2}(G)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathscr{N}(G),

where
n
-simplices of
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(X)
are tuples of vertices
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that
|x_{0},\dots,x_{n}|\leq\ell.
The
\ell
-fundamental category of
G
is defined by

(4.15)

\tau^{\ell}(G)=\tau(\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G)).

Then the
\ell
-fundamental groupoid
\Pi^{\ell}(G)=\Pi(\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G))
studied in
[
13
]
is the groupoid associated with the
\ell
-fundamental category

(4.16)

\Pi^{\ell}(G)=\tau^{\ell}(G)^{\sf gpd}

and the
\ell
-fundamental group is defined by
\pi^{\ell}_{1}(G,x)=\Pi^{\ell}(G)(x,x).

The
\ell
-fundamental category can be described in terms of equivalence classes of paths.

Definition 4.12

(
\tau^{\ell}
-congruence)
.

\tau^{\ell}
-congruence is the minimal congruence on the set of paths such that any two paths connecting the same vertices of (possibly different) lengths at most
\ell
are equivalent.

Note that
\ell
-shortly congruent paths are
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent. However,
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent paths can have different lengths.

Example 4.13
.

In the following graph

(4.17)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 3}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 5}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 4}

all the paths
(0,1,3,5),

(0,1,3,4,5),
and
(0,2,4,5)
are
\tau^{2}
-congruent, but all of them are not
2
-shortly congruent to each other.

Example 4.14
.

Another example is given by the following graph.

(4.18)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 3}

The paths
(0,1,3)
and
(0,2,3)
are

-congruent but not

-shortly congruent, because they are not shortest.

The following proposition is an analogue of the description of the fundamental groupoid
\Pi^{\ell}(G)
given in
[
13
, Prop.2.2]
.

Proposition 4.15
.

The fundamental category
\tau^{\ell}(G)
of a digraph
G
is naturally isomorphic to the category, whose objects are vertices of
G
, whose morphisms from a vertex
x
to a vertex
y
are
\tau^{\ell}
-congruence classes of paths from
x
to
y,
and the composition is defined by concatenation.

Proof.

In the proof, denote by
\mathcal{C}
the category, whose objects are vertices of
G
and morphisms are
\tau^{\ell}
-congruence classes of paths. The
\tau^{\ell}
-congruence class of a path
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
will be denoted by
[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}].
We need to prove that
\tau^{\ell}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}.
The plan of the proof is the following: we construct two functors
\Psi:\tau^{\ell}(G)\to\mathcal{C}
and
\Phi:\mathcal{C}\to\tau^{\ell}(G)
and prove that
\Psi\Phi={\sf Id}
and
\Phi\Psi={\sf Id}.

Before we start constructing the functors, we need to note that, if
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
is a path of length
n\leq\ell
in
G,
then there is the following equation in
\tau^{\ell}(G)

(4.19)

((x_{0},x_{1}),(x_{1},x_{2}),\dots,(x_{n-1},x_{n}))=((x_{0},x_{n})).

Indeed, for any
0\leq i\leq n-2
we have
d(x_{i},x_{i+1})+d(x_{i+1},x_{n})\leq\ell,
and hence
(x_{i},x_{i+1},x_{n})
is a
2
-simplex in
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G).
Therefore
((x_{i},x_{i+1}),(x_{i+1},x_{n}))=((x_{i},x_{n})),
and we obtain (
4.19
) by induction.

The functor
\Phi:\mathcal{C}\to\tau^{\ell}(G)
is defined identically on objects and sends
[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
in
G
to the class of the path
((x_{0},x_{1}),\dots,(x_{n-1},x_{n}))
of
1
-simplices of
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(X).
In order to show that
\Phi
is well defined, we need to check that
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent paths have the same images.
It is sufficient to show that two different paths
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
and
(y_{0},\dots,y_{m})
of lengths
n,m\leq\ell
connecting the same vertices
x_{0}=y_{0}
and
x_{n}=y_{m}
have the same images in
\tau^{\ell}(G)
. It follows from (
4.19
). Hence the functor
\Phi
is well defined.

The functor
\Psi:\tau^{\ell}(G)\to\mathcal{C}
is defined identically on objects. If
(x,y)
is a
1
-simplex of
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G),
then
d(x,y)\leq n
and we define
\Psi((x,y))=[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}],
where
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
is a path from
x
to
y
of any length
n
such that
d(x,y)\leq n\leq\ell.
Since
n\leq\ell,
any two such paths are
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent, and the definition does not depend on the choice. If we have a
2
-simplex
(x,y,z)
of
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G),
then
d(x,y)+d(y,z)\leq\ell
and the concatenation of the shortest path from
x
to
y
and the shortest path from
y
to
z
has length at most
\ell.
Therefore
\Psi((y,z))\circ\Psi((x,y))=\Psi((x,z)).
Therefore, the definition on objects and
1
-simplices gives a well defined functor
\Psi:\tau^{\ell}(G)\to\mathcal{C}.

The fact that
\Psi\Phi={\sf Id}
is straightforward. Let us prove that
\Phi\Psi={\sf Id}.
For objects it is obvious. Let us prove it for morphisms. It is sufficient to check it for
1
-simplices. If we have a
1
-simplex
(x,y)
in
\mathscr{N}^{\ell}(G),
then
\Phi\Psi((x,y))=((x_{0},x_{1}),(x_{1},x_{2}),\dots,(x_{n-1},x_{n})),
where
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
is a path of length
n
from
x
to
y
such that
d(x,y)\leq n\leq\ell.
Then (
4.19
) implies
\Phi\Psi((x,y))=(x,y).

∎

A category is called thin if all its hom-sets have cardinality at most one. Thin categories are in one-to-one correspondence with preorders.

Proposition 4.16
.

If
G
is a finite digraph and
\ell\geq 1
is an integer such that
\mathrm{MH}_{2,k}(G)=0
for any
k>\ell,
then
\tau^{\ell}(G)
is thin.

Proof.

Proposition
4.15
implies that we need to prove that any two paths connecting the same vertices are
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent.
By Theorem
4.10
any two shortest paths are
\ell
-shortly congruent and any long path is
\ell
-shortly congruent to an
\ell
-reducible long path.
Since
\ell
-short congruence implies
\tau^{\ell}
-congruence, any two shortest paths connecting the same vertices are
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent. Note that any
\ell
-reducible path is
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent to a path of shorter length. Then any long path is
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent to a path of shorter length. Therefore by induction we obtain that any long path is
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent to a shortest path. Hence all paths between two vertices are
\tau^{\ell}
-congruent.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

The condition that

is thin is not equivalent to the fact that

for any

For example,
for the graph (

) the category

is thin, but

because paths
(0,1,3,5)
and
(0,1,3,5)
are two shortest paths of length

which are not
2
-shortly congruent.

By an undirected graph we mean a directed graph such that for any arrow

the pair

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
G
be an undirected graph and

be its vertices. Then

(1)

a minimal long path from
x
to

has length either
d(x,y)+1
or
d(x,y)+2;

(2)

any minimal long path of length

is
(d(x,y)+1)
-shortly congruent to a
2
-reducible path.

Proof.

Set

Assume that
p=(p_{0},\dots,p_{m})
is a path from

to

of length

The triangle inequality implies that

Then
(p_{0},\dots,p_{m-1})
is a long path. Hence
p
is not a minimal long path.

Assume that
p=(p_{0},\dots,p_{n+2})
is a minimal long path from
x
to

Choose some shortest path

from
x
to

Then
(p_{0},\dots,p_{n+1})
is a shortest path from

to

Hence
(s_{0},\dots,s_{n},p_{n+1})
is also a shortest path from

to

Therefore,

(p_{0},\dots,p_{n},p_{n+1},p_{n+2})
is
(n+1)
-shortly congruent to

Since

we obtain that

-reducible.
∎

.

Let

4.5.
Application: girth

In order to illustrate our methods, we give an alternative proof of a theorem proved by Asao–Hiraoka–Kanazawa
[
2
]
.
By a cycle in an undirected graph we mean a path
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that
n\geq 3,

x_{0}=x_{n}
and all the vertices
x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}
are distinct. If
e
is an edge of
G
we denote by
{\sf gir}_{e}(G)
the length of the shortest cycle containing
e.
If such a cycle does not exist,
{\sf gir}_{e}(G)=\infty.
By the definition we have
{\sf gir}_{e}(G)\geq 3
for any
G
and
e.

Proposition 4.23

(cf.
[
2
, Th.1.5]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite undirected graph and
e
be its edge. If
{\sf gir}_{e}(G)<\infty,
then
\mathrm{MH}_{2,\ell}(G)\neq 0,
where
\ell=\lfloor({\sf gir}_{e}(G)+1)/2\rfloor.

Proof.

Set
g={\sf gir}_{e}(G).

In this proof, for a path
q
we denote by
q_{i}
its
i
-th vertex
q=(q_{0},\dots,q_{n})
and denote by
q^{-1}
the opposite path
(q_{n},\dots,q_{0})
. We will also introduce some terminology that we will use only in this proof. We say that a path
q
of length
n
is a quasi-cycle if
q_{n}=q_{0}
and
q_{1}\neq q_{n-1}.
It is easy to see that from any quasi-cycle
q
by deleting some of its vertices we can obtain a cycle
c
of length
m\leq n
such that
c_{0}=q_{0}
and
c_{1}=q_{1},c_{m-1}=q_{n-1}.
We say that a quasi-cycle
q
begins from the edge
(q_{0},q_{1}).
Therefore, the condition
g={\sf gir}_{e}(G)
implies that any quasi-cycle
q
that begins from
e
has length at least
g.

Denote by
c
some shortest cycle that begins from
e.
Consider the path
p:=(c_{0},\dots,c_{\ell}),
where
\ell=\lfloor(g+1)/2\rfloor.

We claim that for any path
p^{\prime}
which is
(\ell-1)
-shortly congruent to
p
we have
p^{\prime}_{1}=c_{1}.

Let us prove this. Assume the contrary: let
p^{\prime}
be a path which is
(\ell-1)
-shortly congruent to
p
and
p^{\prime}_{1}\neq c_{1}.
Then there exists a path
\tilde{p}
which differs from
p^{\prime}
by an
(\ell-1)
-short move such that
\tilde{p}_{1}=c_{1}
. Since
p^{\prime}_{1}\neq\tilde{p}_{1}
and the paths differ by an
(\ell-1)
-short move, we obtain
p^{\prime}_{\ell-1}=\tilde{p}_{\ell-1}.
Therefore
(\tilde{p}_{0},\dots,\tilde{p}_{\ell-1},p^{\prime}_{\ell-1},\dots,p^{\prime}_{0})
is a quasi-cycle of length
2(\ell-1)<g
that begins from
e,
which is a contradiction.

Assume that
g=2\ell.
Then
\ell\geq 2
and
d(c_{0},c_{\ell})=\ell
because otherwise there would be a shorter quasi-cycle beginning by
e.
Therefore we obtain that there are two shortest paths
p
and
(c_{g},c_{g-1},\dots,c_{\ell})
which are not
(\ell-1)
-shortly congruent. Then Proposition
4.9
implies that
\mathrm{MH}_{2,\ell}(G)\neq 0.

Assume that
g=2\ell-1.
Then
\ell\geq 2
and
d(c_{0},c_{\ell})=d(c_{0},c_{\ell-1})=d(c_{1},c_{\ell})=\ell-1
because otherwise there would be a shorter cycle containing
e.

Then
p
is a minimal long path of length
\ell.
We claim that
p
is not
(\ell-1)
-shortly congruent to a non-minimal long path. Assume the contrary, that there is a non-minimal long path
p^{\prime}
which is
(\ell-1)
-shortly congruent to
p.
The fact that it is non-minimal implies that either
d(p^{\prime}_{0},p^{\prime}_{\ell-1})\leq\ell-2
or
d(p^{\prime}_{1},p^{\prime}_{\ell})\leq\ell-2.
As we proved above,
p^{\prime}_{1}=c_{1},
and hence
d(p^{\prime}_{1},p^{\prime}_{\ell})=d(c_{1},c_{\ell})=\ell-1.
This implies that
d(c_{0},p^{\prime}_{\ell-1})\leq\ell-2
and
d(c_{1},p^{\prime}_{\ell-1})=\ell-2.
Take a shortest path
s
from
c_{0}
to
p^{\prime}_{\ell-1}.
Since
d(c_{1},p^{\prime}_{\ell-1})=\ell-2,
we obtain
s_{1}\neq c_{1}.
Therefore
(p^{\prime}_{0},\dots,p^{\prime}_{\ell-1})s^{-1}
is a quasi-cycle beginning from
e
of length
\ell-1+d(c_{0},p^{\prime}_{\ell-1})\leq 2\ell-3<g.
This gives a contradiction. Therefore,
p
is a minimal long path which is not
(\ell-1)
-shortly congruent to a non-minimal long path. Hence Proposition
4.9
implies that
\mathrm{MH}_{2,\ell}(G)\neq 0.

∎

5.
Path cochain algebra

In this section we consider the dg-algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
studied in GLMY-theory
[
18
, §3.4]
, whose elements are called “
d
-invariant forms”. We call this dg-algebra path cochain algebra. In
[
18
]
this dg-algebra was considered over a field, but we will consider it over a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
, so as to make it more consistent with magnitude homology, which is typically considered over
\mathbb{Z}.

The goal of this section is to prove that
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
is isomorphic to a quotient of the path algebra
\mathbb{K}G
by some quadratic relations (similar to the description done of the diagonal part of the magnitude cohomology
[
25
, Th.6.2]
).

Let us recall the definition of
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
following
[
18
]
. For a finite set
X,
we consider the free module generated by
(n+1)
-tuples
\mathbb{K}\cdot X^{n+1}
and its dual
\Lambda^{n}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}\cdot X^{n+1},\mathbb{K}).
Then the basis of
\Lambda^{n}(X)
dual to the basis of tuples in
\mathbb{K}\cdot X^{n+1}
is denoted by

(5.1)

e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}\in\Lambda^{n}(X).

These modules form a cochain complex
\Lambda^{\bullet}(X)
with the differential
\partial:\Lambda^{n}(X)\to\Lambda^{n+1}(X)
defined by

(5.2)

\partial(e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}})=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\sum_{v\in X}(-1)^{i}e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{i-1},v,x_{i},\dots,x_{n}}

(see
[
18
, (2.15)]
). We consider a chain subcomplex
\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(X)\subseteq\Lambda^{\bullet}(X),
where
\mathcal{R}^{n}(X)
is generated by the elements of the dual basis
e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}
for any
i.
Then there is a structure of a dg-algebra on
\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(X)
with the product defined by

(5.3)

6.
Diagonal digraphs and Koszul algebras

In this section we show that a finite digraph
G
is diagonal if and only if the distance algebra
\sigma G
is Koszul for any field, and if and only if
G
satisfies
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
and the path cochain algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
is Koszul for any field. Moreover, for a digraph
G
satisfying
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
, we give an explicit description of the Koszul complex of the quadratic algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
(Proposition
6.6
). This provides another equivalent description of diagonal digraphs from a completely different viewpoint (Corollary
6.7
).

6.1.
Background on quadratic and Koszul algebras

Here we will recall the basic information about quadratic and Koszul algebras in the setting of graded quiver algebras over fields
[
34
]
,
[
7
]
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
be a non-negatively graded ring and
S=A_{0}
. If
M,N
are graded
A
-modules we denote by
\mathrm{Ext}^{n,\ell}_{A}(M,N)
the bigraded Ext-functor in the category of graded modules. If
M,N
are ungraded
A
-modules, we set
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{A}(M,N)
the ordinary Ext functor. The graded ring
A
is called Koszul if
S
is a semisimple ring and there exists a graded projective resolution of
S
over
A

(6.1)

\dots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to S\to 0

such that
P_{n}
is a graded projective
A
-module generated in degree
n.
It is known
[
7
, Prop.2.1.3]
that

(6.2)

A\text{ is Koszul}\hskip 14.22636pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{Ext}^{n,\ell}_{A}(S,S)=0\text{ for }\ell\neq n.

We denote by

(6.3)

E(A)=\mathrm{Ext}^{*}_{A}(S,S)

the Ext algebra, where the product is given by the Yoneda product. We will restrict ourselves to graded quiver algebras over a field.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field,
Q
be a finite quiver, and
\mathbb{K}Q=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\mathbb{K}Q)_{n}
be the graded path algebra, where the grading is defined by the lengths of paths. For a homogeneous ideal
I
of
\mathbb{K}Q
we denote by
I_{n}
its homogeneous components. Then
I
is called admissible if
I_{0}=I_{1}=0.
By a
graded quiver algebra
we mean an algebra of the form
A=\mathbb{K}Q/I,
where
I
is a graded admissible ideal.

A graded quiver algebra
A=\mathbb{K}Q/I
is called
quadratic
if
I
is generated by
I_{2}.
It is known
[
7
, Theorem 2.3.2]
, for a graded quiver algebra
A,
we have

(6.4)

A\text{ is quadratic}\hskip 14.22636pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{Ext}^{2,\ell}_{A}(S,S)=0\text{ for }\ell\neq 2.

Assume that
A=\mathbb{K}Q/I
is a quadratic algebra. Denote by
Q^{op}
the quiver opposite to
Q
and, for any path
p
in
Q,
denote by
p^{op}
the opposite path in
Q^{op}.
Then there is a non-degenerate bilinear form

(6.5)

\langle-,=\rangle:(\mathbb{K}Q)_{2}\times(\mathbb{K}Q^{op})_{2}\to\mathbb{K}

which is defined on paths via Kronecker delta
\langle p,q\rangle=\delta_{p^{op},q}.
For a quadratic algebra
A=\mathbb{K}Q/I
we
denote by
I_{2}^{\bot}
the vector subspace of
(\mathbb{K}Q^{op})_{2}
orthogonal to
I_{2}
with respect to this bilinear form. We also denote by
I^{\bot}
the ideal of
\mathbb{K}Q^{op}
generated by
I_{2}^{\bot}.
Then the dual quadratic algebra to
A
is defined by

(6.6)

A^{!}=(\mathbb{K}Q^{op})/I^{\bot}.

If a graded quiver algebra is Koszul, it is quadratic, and a quadratic algebra
A
is Koszul if and only if
A^{!}
is Koszul. Moreover, for any Koszul graded quiver algebra
A
there is an isomorphism

(6.7)

E(A)\cong A^{!}.

6.2.
Diagonal digraphs

Lemma 6.1
.

For any digraph
G
and any
n
the group
\mathrm{MH}_{n,n}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is free abelian.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{MC}_{n+1,n}(G,\mathbb{Z})=0,
we have
\mathrm{MH}_{n,n}(G,\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathrm{MC}_{n,n}(G,\mathbb{Z}),
and we use the fact that a subgroup of a free abelian group is free abelian.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

For any digraph
G
and any
n,\ell
there are short exact sequences

(6.8)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}\rightarrowtail\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}(\mathrm{MH}_{n-1,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{K}),

(6.9)

{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}(\mathrm{MH}_{n-1,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{K})\rightarrowtail\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{K}).

Proof.

It follows from the universal coefficient theorem for chain complexes
[
39
, Th.3.6.1, Th.3.6.5]
, the isomorphisms

(6.10)

\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})=\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{MC}^{\bullet,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{K})

and the fact that the components of
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})
are free abelian groups.
∎

A digraph
G
is called diagonal if
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})=0
for any
\ell\neq n

[
28
]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

For a finite digraph
G
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

is diagonal;

(2)

is diagonal;

(3)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})=0

\mathbb{K};

(4)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})=0

\ell\neq n

\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})=0
for
\ell\neq n;

(6)

\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})=0
for
\ell\neq n
and any commutative ring
\mathbb{K};

\mathrm{MH}^{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})=0
for
\ell\neq n
and any prime field
\mathbb{K}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})\cong\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G^{op},\mathbb{K})
sending

Hence

If

is diagonal, then by Lemma (

implies
(3),

(4),

(5),

(6),

Obviously

and
(6)\Rightarrow(7).
Since, for any field

Therefore, it is sufficient to prove that

(4)\Rightarrow(1).
In general, if

is a finitely generated abelian group such that

, then
A=0.
Since
G
is finite, the abelian groups
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})
are finitely generated. Hence the equation
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})=0
for any

and a prime field

combined with the short exact sequence (

Therefore,

∎

Proposition 6.4
.

The following statements about a finite digraph
G
are equivalent.

(1)

\mathrm{MH}_{2,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})=0
for
\ell\neq 2;

(2)

G
satisfies
(\mathcal{V}_{2});

(3)

\sigma G
is quadratic for any field
\mathbb{K}.

Moreover, if these properties are satisfied, then

(6.11)

(\sigma G)^{!}\cong\Omega^{\bullet}(G^{op})

for any field
\mathbb{K}.

Proof.

The equivalence of (1) and (2) follows from Theorem
4.10
.
Using Lemma
6.2
and the fact that
\mathrm{MH}_{1,\ell}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is free abelian, we obtain that
(1)
is equivalent to the fact that
\mathrm{MH}^{2,\ell}(G,\mathbb{K})=0
for
\ell\neq 2
and any field
\mathbb{K}.
Then by Theorem
3.2
and (
6.4
) we obtain that (1) is equivalent to (3).

Let us assume that
\sigma G
is quadratic and prove (
6.11
). Since
\sigma G=\mathbb{K}G/R
is quadratic,
R
is generated by
R_{2}.
Denote by
R_{2}^{\mathtt{S}}
the vector space generated by differences of shortest paths of length
2
connecting the same vertices, and by
R_{2}^{\mathtt{L}}
the vector space generated by long paths of length two. Then
R_{2}=R_{2}^{\mathtt{S}}\oplus R_{2}^{\mathtt{L}}.
Then
\dim R_{2}^{\mathtt{L}}
is equal to the number of long paths of length

and
\dim R_{2}^{\mathtt{S}}
is equal to the number of short paths of length two minus the number of pairs at distance two. Therefore
\dim(\mathbb{K}G)_{2}-\dim R_{2}
is equal to the number of pairs at distance two. It follows that
\dim R_{2}^{\bot}
is equal to the number of pairs at distance two. On the other hand, it is easy to check that any element of the form

(see (

of length

connecting the same vertices, and to any long path of length

It follows that

is generated by the elements of the form

∎

.

The following statements about a finite digraph

(2)

is Koszul for any field

The equivalence

(Theorem

), and Lemma

.
The implications

and

6.3.
Koszul complex of a digraph

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field,
A=\mathbb{K}Q/I
be a quadratic algebra,
A^{!}=\mathbb{K}Q^{op}/I^{\bot}
be its dual and
S=(\mathbb{K}Q)_{0}=(\mathbb{K}Q^{op})_{0}
. We treat the
n
-th homogeneous components
A^{!}_{n}
as a right
S
-module, and
A
as a
(A,S)
-bimodule, and consider a chain complex
{\sf K}_{\bullet}
of graded left
A
-modules, whose components are described as graded hom-modules of right
S
-modules

(6.12)

{\sf K}_{n}=\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(A^{!}_{n},A)\cong A\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(A^{!}_{n},S)

and the differential
\partial:{\sf K}_{n}\to{\sf K}_{n-1}
is defined by

\partial(\varphi)(a)=\sum_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\varphi(a\alpha^{op})\cdot\alpha.
Here we assume that
{\sf K}_{n}
is a graded module, whose homogeneous components are

(6.13)

({\sf K}_{n})_{n+i}=\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(A^{!}_{n},A_{i})\cong A_{i}\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(A^{!}_{n},S).

The chain complex
{\sf K}_{\bullet}
is called

the Koszul complex
of
A.

Consider a map
\varepsilon:{\sf K}_{0}\to S

defined by
\varepsilon(\varphi)=\varphi(1).
Then it is known that
[
7
, §2.6,§2.8]

(6.14)

A\text{ is Koszul }\hskip 14.22636pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 14.22636pt{\sf K}_{\bullet}\text{ is a resolution of }S.

Now let
G
be a digraph and
\mathbb{K}
be a field. Consider a graded chain complex
{\sf K}^{G}_{\bullet}
of left
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
-modules whose components are defined by

(6.15)

{\sf K}^{G}_{n}=\bigoplus_{d(x,y)=n}\Omega^{\bullet}(G)\cdot e_{x},

where the sum is taken over all pairs of vertices
(x,y)
such that
d(x,y)=n.
Denote by
\kappa_{x,y}\in{\sf K}^{G}_{n}
the image of
e_{x}
in the summand indexed by
(x,y).
Then the grading is defined so that
|\kappa_{x,y}|=d(x,y)
and the differential
\partial:{\sf K}^{G}_{n}\to{\sf K}^{G}_{n-1}
is defined on the generators by

(6.16)

\partial(\kappa_{x,y})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v:d(x,v)=1\\
d(v,y)=n-1\end{subarray}}(x,v)\cdot\kappa_{v,y},

where the sum is taken over all vertices
v
such that
d(x,v)=1
and
d(v,y)=n-1.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
G
be a finite digraph satisfying
(\mathcal{V}_{2}).
Then the chain complex
{\sf K}^{G}_{\bullet}
is well defined and it is isomorphic to the Koszul complex of the path cochain algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G).

Proof.

Denote by
{\sf K}_{\bullet}
the Koszul complex of
\Omega^{\bullet}(G).

By Proposition
6.4
we have isomorphisms

(\Omega^{\bullet}(G))^{!}\cong\sigma G^{op},\hskip 28.45274pt(\sigma G^{op})_{n}=\bigoplus_{d(x,y)=n}\mathbb{K}\cdot(y,x)

and

\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(\mathbb{K}\cdot(y,x),\Omega^{\bullet}(G))\cong\Omega^{\bullet}(G)\cdot e_{x}.

Therefore, we obtain

{\sf K}_{n}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{S}((\sigma G^{op})_{n},\Omega^{\bullet}(G))\cong{\sf K}^{G}_{n}.
For each pair of vertices
x,y
such that
d(x,y)=n,
we consider the elements
\theta_{x,y}\in{\sf K}_{n}
defined by

\theta_{x,y}(b,a)=\delta_{(x,y),(a,b)}e_{x}
and
\delta
is the Kronecker delta. Then the isomorphism
\theta:{\sf K}^{G}_{n}\to C_{n}
is defined by
\theta(\kappa_{x,y})=\theta_{x,y}.
For any pair of vertices
(a,b)
such that
d(a,b)=n-1
a computation shows that

(6.17)

\begin{split}\partial(\theta_{x,y})(b,a)&=\sum_{d(s,t)=1}\theta_{x,y}((b,a)(t,s))\cdot(s,t)\\
&=\delta_{y,b}\cdot\delta_{d(x,a),1}\cdot(x,a)\end{split}

and

(6.18)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v:d(x,v)=1\\
d(v,y)=n-1\end{subarray}}(x,v)\cdot\theta_{v,y}(b,a)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v:d(x,v)=1\\
d(v,y)=n-1\end{subarray}}(x,v)\cdot\delta_{(v,y),(a,b)}\\
&=\delta_{y,b}\cdot\delta_{d(x,a),1}\cdot(x,a).\end{split}

Therefore we obtain

(6.19)

\partial(\theta_{x,y})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v:d(x,v)=1\\
d(v,y)=n-1\end{subarray}}(x,v)\cdot\theta_{v,y}.

This implies that the differential on
{\sf K}^{G}_{\bullet}
is well defined and
\theta
is an isomorphism of chain complexes.
∎

.

A finite digraph
G
is diagonal if and only if it satisfies
(\mathcal{V}_{2})
and
{\sf K}^{G}_{\bullet}
is a resolution of
S
for any field
\mathbb{K}.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
6.5
, Proposition
6.6
and (
6.14
).

. We describe the magnitude homology of
G_{P}
in terms of the homology of posets. Next, we apply this to describe the homology of a digraph
\hat{G}_{K}
associated with a pure simplicial complex
K
, which we call the extended Hasse diagram. Finally, we show that if
K
is a triangulation of a manifold, then the non-diagonal part of the magnitude homology of
\hat{G}_{K}
can be expressed in terms of the homology of the manifold. As a corollary, we deduce that
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal if and only if the manifold is a homology sphere.

7.1.
Ranked posets

For a poset
P
and a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
we denote by
C_{\bullet}(P)
the chain complex whose components are
C_{n}(P)=\mathbb{K}\cdot\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mid x_{0}<\dots<x_{n}\}
and the differential is defined as the alternating sum of deleting maps
\partial(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n})
. We denote by
\bar{C}_{\bullet}(P)
a chain complex, whose non-negative part coincides with
C_{\bullet}(P),
and that has one more non-zero component
\bar{C}_{-1}(P)=\mathbb{K}.
The differential
\bar{C}_{0}(P)\to\bar{C}_{-1}(P)
sends
(x_{0})
to
1.
Then the reduced homology of
P
with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
is defined by the formula

(7.1)

\bar{H}_{n}(P)=H_{n}(\bar{C}_{\bullet}(P)).

If we need to specify
\mathbb{K},
we will denote
\bar{H}_{*}(P)
by
\bar{H}_{*}(P,\mathbb{K}).
Note that

(7.2)

\bar{H}_{-1}(\emptyset)=\mathbb{K}.

A
chain
in a poset
P
is a totally ordered subset. An element
y\in P

covers
an element
x\in P,
if
x<y
and there is no
z
such that
x<z<y.
A
ranked poset
is a poset
P
where every maximal chain is finite and all maximal chains have the same number of elements. Then there is a uniquely defined rank function
r:P\to\mathbb{N}
such that
r(x)=0
if and only if
x
is minimal, and if
y
covers
x,
then
r(y)=r(x)+1.
In this case all maximal elements of
P
have the same rank, which we call the dimension of
P,
and denote by
\dim P.
The dimension of the empty set is defined as
-1.
Note that for any elements
x\leq y
of
P
the open interval

(7.3)

]x,y[\>=\{z\in P\mid x<z<y\}

is also a ranked poset of dimension
r(y)-r(x)-1.

If
P
is a ranked poset, we denote by
G_{P}
the digraph, whose vertices are elements of
P
and there is an arrow
x\to y
if and only if
y
covers
x.
Then the distance
d(x,y)
is finite if and only if
x\leq y
and in this case
d(x,y)=r(y)-r(x).

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
P
be a ranked poset and
x,y
be its elements. If
x<y,
then there is an isomorphism of chain complexes

(7.4)

\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,r(x)-r(y)}(G_{P})_{x,y}\cong\bar{C}_{\bullet-2}(\>]x,y[\>).

Moreover,
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G(P))_{x,y}=0
if
\ell\neq r(x)-r(y)
or
x\not\leq y.

Proof.

If
x\not\leq y,
then
d(x,y)=\infty,
and hence
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G_{P})_{x,y}=0.
Since
|x_{0},\dots,x_{n}|
is equal to either
r(x_{n})-r(x_{0})
or to infinity, if
\ell\neq r(x)-r(y),
then
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet,\ell}(G_{P})_{x,y}=0.

Now assume that
x<y
and
\ell=r(y)-r(x).

If
n\neq 1,
then the map
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
defines a bijection from the set

(7.5)

\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mid x_{i}\neq x_{i+1},x_{0}=x,x_{n}=y,|x_{0},\dots,x_{n}|=\ell\}

to the set

(7.6)

\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\mid x<x_{1}<\dots<x_{n-1}<y\}.

This bijection defines an isomorphism
f_{n}:\mathrm{MC}_{n,\ell}(G_{P})_{x,y}\overset{\cong}{\to}\bar{C}_{n-2}(\>]x,y[\>)
for
n\neq 1.
If
n=1,
we have
\mathrm{MC}_{1,\ell}(G_{P})_{x,y}\cong\langle(x,y)\rangle\cong\mathbb{K}
and denote by
f_{1}:\mathrm{MC}_{1,\ell}(G_{P})_{x,y}\to\mathbb{K}
this isomorphism. It is easy to see that
f_{n}
is compatible with the differential up to sign. It follows that the maps
(-1)^{n}f_{n}
define an isomorphism of chain complexes.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
P
be a ranked poset,
x,y
be its elements and
\ell\geq 1
. Then

(7.7)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G_{P})_{x,y}\cong\begin{cases}\bar{H}_{n-2}(\>]x,y[\>),&\ell=r(x)-r(y),\ x<y\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular, for
\ell\geq 1,
we obtain

(7.8)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(G_{P})\cong\bigoplus_{x<y,r(y)-r(x)=\ell}\bar{H}_{n-2}(\>]x,y[\>).

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
7.1
.
∎

7.2.
Pure simplicial complexes

Let
K
be a pure (abstract) simplicial complex of dimension
d
. The link of a simplex
\sigma\in K
is a simplicial subcomplex of
K
defined by

(7.9)

\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)=\{\tau\in K\mid\tau\cap\sigma=\emptyset,\tau\cup\sigma\in K\}.

The link
\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)
is also a pure simplicial complex of dimension
d-\dim(\sigma)-1.

We denote by
P(K)
the face poset of
K,
defined by
P(K)=(K,\subseteq).
It is well known that the homology of
K
coincides with
P(K)

(7.10)

H_{*}(K)\cong H_{*}(P(K)),

because the order complex of
P(K)
is the barycentric subdivision of
K
, and the homology of a poset
P
is the homology of its order complex
[
38
, §1.1]
. We denote by
L(K)
the face lattice of
K
, defined by

(7.11)

L(K):=K\sqcup\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\},

where the simplices are ordered by inclusion and
\hat{0}<\sigma<\hat{1}
for any
\sigma\in K

[
38
, §1.1]
. Then
L(K)
is a ranked poset of dimension
d+2.
The
extended Hasse diagram
of
K
is defined by

(7.12)

\hat{G}_{K}=G_{L(K)}.

We also denote by
K(n)
the set of
n
-simplices of
K.

Theorem 7.3
.

For any pure simplicial complex
K
of dimension
d
there are isomorphisms

(7.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MH}_{n,d+2}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\hat{0},\hat{1}}

\displaystyle\cong\bar{H}_{n-2}(K),

(7.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\sigma,\hat{1}}

\displaystyle\cong\bar{H}_{n-2}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)),

\displaystyle\ell=d+1-\dim(\sigma)

(7.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MH}_{n,n}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\hat{0},\sigma}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{K},

\displaystyle n=\dim(\sigma)+1,

(7.16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MH}_{n,n}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\sigma,\tau}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{K},

\displaystyle n=\dim(\tau)-\dim(\sigma),\sigma\subsetneq\tau

and all other modules
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{x,y}
for
\ell\geq 1
are trivial. Moreover, for
\ell\geq 1,
we obtain

(7.17)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})\cong\begin{cases}\bar{H}_{n-2}(K),&\ell=d+2\\
\bigoplus_{\sigma\in K(d+1-\ell)}\bar{H}_{n-2}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)),&1\leq\ell\neq d+2,\ n\neq\ell\\
\mathbb{K}^{D(n)}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{\sigma\in K(d+1-n)}\bar{H}_{n-2}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma))\right),&1\leq n=\ell\neq d+2,\\
\end{cases}

where

D(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-n+1}\binom{n+i}{i}\cdot|K(n+i-1)|.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.2
, we know that
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{x,y}
is isomorphic to
H_{n-2}(\>]x,y[\>),
if
x<y
and
\ell=r(x)-r(y),
and equal to zero otherwise. Here we denote by
r
the rank function for
L(K),
which is defined by
r(\hat{0})=0,r(\hat{1})=d+2
and
r(\sigma)=\dim(\sigma)+1.

Assume that
(x,y)=(\hat{0},\hat{1}).
Then we obtain that
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\hat{0},\hat{1}}
is trivial for
\ell\neq d+2,
and
\mathrm{MH}_{n,d+2}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\hat{0},\hat{1}}=\bar{H}_{n-2}(\>]\hat{0},\hat{1}[\>)\cong\bar{H}_{n-2}(P(K))\cong\bar{H}_{n-2}(K).

Assume that
(x,y)=(\sigma,\hat{1}).
Then
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\sigma,\hat{1}}
is trivial for
\ell\neq d+1-\dim(\sigma).
Note that there is an isomorphism of posets
\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)\cong\>]\sigma,\hat{1}[\>
defined by
\tau\mapsto\sigma\cup\tau.
Then
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\sigma,\hat{1}}\cong\bar{H}_{n-2}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma))
for
\ell=d+1-\dim(\sigma).

Assume that
(x,y)=(\hat{0},\sigma).
Then
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G})_{\hat{0},\sigma}
is trivial for
\ell\neq\dim(\sigma)+1.
Note that there is an isomorphism
\>]\hat{0},\sigma[\>=\{\tau\in K\mid\tau\subsetneq\sigma\}\cong\partial\Delta^{\dim(\sigma)}.
Hence
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\hat{0},\sigma}\cong\bar{H}_{n-2}(\partial\Delta^{\dim(\sigma)})
for
\ell=\dim(\sigma)+1.
Hence
\mathrm{MH}_{n,n}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\hat{0},\sigma}\cong\mathbb{K}
for
n=\dim(\sigma)+1,
and
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\hat{0},\sigma}
is trivial otherwise.

Assume that
(x,y)=(\sigma,\tau),
where
\sigma\subsetneq\tau.
Then
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\sigma,\tau}=0
for
\ell\neq\dim(\tau)-\dim(\sigma).

The isomorphism
\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)\cong\>]\sigma,1[\>,

\theta\mapsto\theta\cup\sigma
restricts to an isomorphism
\{\theta\mid\emptyset\neq\theta\subsetneq\tau\setminus\sigma\}\cong\>]\sigma,\tau[\>.
Therefore
\>]\sigma,\tau[\>\cong\partial\Delta^{\dim(\tau)-\dim(\sigma)-1}.
It follows that
\mathrm{MH}_{n,n}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\sigma,\tau}\cong\mathbb{K}
for
n=\dim(\tau)-\dim(\sigma),
and
\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})_{\sigma,\tau}
is trivial otherwise.

The formula for
D(n)
follows from the facts that there are
|K(n-1)|
simplices of dimension
n-1
and for
i\geq 1
we have
\binom{n+i}{i}
simplices
\sigma\subseteq\tau
of dimension
i-1
for each simplex
\sigma\in K(n+i-1).

∎

Let
K
be a pure simplicial complex of dimension
d.
We say that the reduced homology of
K
is
concentrated in the top dimension
if
\bar{H}_{i}(K,\mathbb{Z})=0
for
i\neq d.
In this case
H_{d}(K,\mathbb{Z})
is a free abelian group and the universal coefficient theorem implies that
\bar{H}_{i}(K,\mathbb{K})=0
for
i\neq d
for any commutative ring
\mathbb{K}.
Note that we do not assume that
H_{d}(K,\mathbb{Z})\neq 0.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
K
be a pure simplicial complex such that, for any
\sigma\in K,
the reduced homology of
\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)
is concentrated in the top dimension. Then for
n\neq\ell
we have

(7.18)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K})\cong\begin{cases}\bar{H}_{n-2}(K),&\ell-2=\dim(K),\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By Theorem
7.3
we obtain that it is sufficient to prove that
\bar{H}_{n-2}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma))=0
for any
\sigma
such that
\ell\neq n,
where
\ell=\dim(K)+1-\dim(\sigma).
Since
\dim(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma))=\dim(K)-\dim(\sigma)-1,
then

\ell\neq n
is equivalent to
\dim(K)\neq n-2.
This proves the claim.
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
K
be a pure simplicial complex. Then
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal if and only if

(1)

the reduced homology of
K
is concentrated in the top dimension;

(2)

the reduced homology of
\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)
is concentrated in the top dimension for any
\sigma\in K.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
and set
d=\dim(K).
Theorem
7.3
implies that
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal if and only if the following two properties are satisfied:
(1)
\bar{H}_{n-2}(K)=0
for
n\neq d+2;
(2)
\bar{H}_{n-2}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma))=0
for
n\neq d+1-\dim(\sigma).
The first property is equivalent to
\bar{H}_{m}(K)=0
for
m\neq d.
The second property is equivalent to
\bar{H}_{m}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma))=0
for
m\neq d-1-\dim(\sigma)=\dim(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)).

∎

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
K
be a connected pure simplicial complex of dimension at least two and
\ell\geq 2
. Then
\hat{G}_{K}
satisfies property
(\mathcal{V}_{\ell})
if and only if
\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)
is connected for any
\sigma\in K
of dimension
\dim(\sigma)\leq\dim(K)-\ell.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
and set
d=\dim(K)\geq 2.
Theorem
7.3
implies that
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal if and only if
\bar{H}_{0}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma))=0
for
k\geq\ell+1,
where
k=d+1-\dim(\sigma).
The assertion follows.
∎

7.3.
Triangulations of manifolds

A triangulation of a topological manifold
M
is a simplicial complex
K
together with a homeomorphism
|K|\cong M,
where
|K|
is the geometric realization.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
M
be a topological manifold with boundary,
K
be a triangulation of
M
and
\mathbb{K}
be a commutative ring.
Then for
n\neq\ell
we have

(7.21)

\mathrm{MH}_{n,\ell}(\hat{G}_{K},\mathbb{K})\cong\begin{cases}\bar{H}_{n-2}(M,\mathbb{K}),&\ell-2=\dim(M),\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For any simplicial complex
K,
any simplex
\sigma\in K
and any point from the interior of the geometric realisation of the simplex
x\in|\sigma|^{\circ}\subseteq|K|,
the local homology of
|K|
at the point
x
is isomorphic to the shifted homology of the link of
\sigma

[
37
, Lemma 5.2.2]

(7.22)

H_{i}(|K|,|K|\setminus\{x\})\cong\bar{H}_{i-\dim(\sigma)-1}(\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)).

Without loss of generality, we assume that
|K|
is a topological manifold of dimension
d.
Then by excision theorem for any
x\in|K|
the group
H_{i}(|K|,|K|\setminus\{x\})
is either isomorphic to
H_{i}(D^{d},D^{d}\setminus\{0\})
or to
H_{i}(D^{d}_{\geq 0},D^{d}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{0\}),
where
D^{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the standard closed disk, and
D^{d}_{\geq 0}=D^{d}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}).
Combining this with the formula (
7.22
) we obtain that the reduced homology of
\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)
is concentrated in the top dimension, for any
\sigma\in K
(compare with
[
37
, Th.5.2.4(1)]
). Then the statement follows from Proposition
7.4
.
∎

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
K
be a triangulation of a closed topological manifold
M
of dimension at least one. Then
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal if and only if
M
is a homology sphere.

Proof.

Set
d=\dim(M)\geq 1.
If
M
is a homology sphere, then by Theorem
7.8
we obtain that
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal. Now assume that
\hat{G}_{K}
is diagonal and prove that
M
is a homology sphere. By Theorem
7.8
we obtain that
\bar{H}_{n-2}(M,\mathbb{Z})=0
for
n\neq\ell,
where
\ell=d+2.
Substituting
m=n-2,
we obtain
\bar{H}_{m}(M,\mathbb{Z})=0
for
m\neq d.
Since
d\geq 1,
we have
\bar{H}_{0}(M,\mathbb{Z})=0.
Therefore,
M
is connected. If
d=1,
then in this case we obtain
M=S^{1}.
Further we assume that
d\geq 2.

Assume that
M
is not orientable. Then there is a subgroup of index two in
\pi_{1}(M)

[
24
, Prop. 3.25]
. A subgroup of index two is normal. Therefore
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})=\pi_{1}(M)_{ab}
is nontrivial, and this gives a contradiction, because
d\geq 2
.

Assume that
M
is orientable. For a connected orientable closed manifold
M
of dimension
d
we have
H_{0}(M,\mathbb{Z})=H_{d}(M,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}

[
24
, §3.3]
. Hence
M
is a homology sphere.
∎

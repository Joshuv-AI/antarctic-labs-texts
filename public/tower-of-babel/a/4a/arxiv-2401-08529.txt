On the determinant in Bray-Moore’s TAP complexity formula
David Belius, Francesco Concetti, Giuseppe Genovese

Abstract
. In the computation of the TAP complexity, originally carried out by Bray and Moore, a fundamental step is to calculate the determinant of a random Hessian. As the replica method does not give a clear prescription, physicists debated how to perform this computation and its consequences on the TAP complexity for a long time. In this paper we prove the original Bray and Moore formula for the behaviour of the determinant at exponential scale to be correct, and compute an important prefactor coming from a small outlier in the spectrum. MSC: 60K40, 82B44, 82D30.

Introduction

The problem of determining asymptotics of absolute values of random determinants at exponential scale is receiving increasing attention in the last years, especially in connection to the computation of the complexity of random landscapes

Bray and Moore
[
14
]
computed the number of metastable states of the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model using the Kac-Rice formula
[
6
]
. Metastable states are understood as the critical point of the TAP free energy (after Thouless, Palmer, Anderson
[
28
]
), that is

(1.1)

F_{\TAP}(m):=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i,j\in[N]}J_{ij}m_{i}m_{j}+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}+\ent(m)+\frac{N\beta^{2}}{2}(1-Q(m))^{2}\,,\qquad m\in[-1,1]^{N}\,.

Here
\beta>0,h\in\mathbb{R}
,
J=\{J_{ij}\}_{i,j\in[N]}
denotes a symmetric matrix with centred Gaussian entries: we take i.i.d. diagonal entries with
\mathbb{E}[J^{2}_{ii}]=2
,
i\in[N]
and i.i.d. upper triangular entries with
\mathbb{E}[J_{ij}^{2}]=1
,
i,j\in[N]
; moreover
\ent(m)
is the sum of the coin tossing entropy computed in the coordinates of
m\in[-1,1]^{N}
and

(1.2)

Q(m):=\frac{\|m\|_{2}^{2}}{N}\,.

The Kac-Rice formula then gives for any measurable

(1.3)

\mathbb{E}[\card\{m\in B\,:\,\nabla F_{\rm{TAP}}(m)=0\}]=\int\!\!f(m)\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\det(\nabla^{2}F_{\TAP})(m)\right|\,|\,\,\,\nabla F_{\TAP}(m)=0\right]dm\,,

where
f(m)
is the density of the vector
\nabla F_{\rm{TAP}}
computed in zero (whose explicit form is not important here).

One crucial point in
[
14
]
is the computation of the expected random determinant of the Hessian appearing in (
1.3
). This is the Bray-Moore determinant.
The computation is rather delicate, even for
h=0
. Indeed in general computing the expected value of the modulus of the determinant by the replica method is a genuine technical challenge
[
19
]
. Therefore different methods have been proposed to compute the determinant without the modulus and to obtain from this an estimate for the quantity with modulus. This question generated a debate in the theoretical physics community that lasted for over twenty years and which we briefly summarize below, referring to e.g.
[

,
24
,
21
,
23
,
15

The gradient of the TAP free energy appearing in (

(1.4)

\nabla F_{\TAP}(m)=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{N}}Jm+h-\atanh(m)-2\beta^{2}m(1-Q(m))\,,

and the Hessian is

(1.5)

\nabla^{2}F_{\TAP}(m)=\frac{\beta J}{\sqrt{N}}-D_{N}(m)+4\frac{\beta^{2}}{N}mm^{T}\,,

where

(1.6)

D_{N}(m):=\diag(\{(1-m_{i}^{2})^{-1}+2\beta^{2}(1-Q(m))\}_{i\in[N]})\,.

We study the determinant of (
1.5
) conditioned on the TAP equations
\nabla F_{\TAP}(m)=0
being satisfied.

In
[
14

Bray and Moore computed (for
h=0
) the r.h.s. of (
1.3

1.5
) and arguing that the remaining term of the Hessian matrix is positive definite, which justifies dropping the absolute value. Identifying so
|\det|
with

[
14

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\det(\nabla^{2}F_{\TAP})\right|\,|\,\,\,\nabla F_{\TAP}(m)=0\right]

(1.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{g\>\leqlant\>1-Q(m)}\left(\beta^{2}g^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\log((1-m_{i}^{2})^{-1}+2\beta^{2}(1-Q(m))-2\beta^{2}g)\right)=:\Upsilon(\beta,m)\,\,.

They then assumed
g=1-Q(m)
for the minimiser above and moved on with the complexity computation.

In this paper we extend recent mathematically rigorous techniques for computing such expectations of determinants
with
the absolute value
[
5

18
,
27
,
10
,
17
]
to derive a rigorous proof of the formula of Bray-Moore.
We furthermore compute a correction term, which is subleading for most

but becomes large when
m
is close to zero. In a future work we will use the estimates proved here to compute the complexity of TAP solutions from (
1.3

close to zero when

In the physics literature Plefka
[
24

and later Kurchan
[

pointed out that the rank-one projector term in (

[

noted an apparent contradiction arising from Morse theory (see for instance
[

(1.9)

\sum_{m^{*}\text{ critical points of $F_{\TAP}$}}(-1)^{\#\text{ positive eigenvalues of }\nabla^{2}F_{\TAP}(m^{*})}

is a topological invariant of the space

(called the Euler-Poincaré characteristic). Its value for

\beta

1.9

[
4

interpreted this as the appearance of critical points in pairs so as to cause cancellations in in (

2.
Stieltjes transform and additive convolution

In this section we collect a number of results about Stieltjes transforms and additive convolutions of probability measures that are used in the sequel. We start by recalling the definitions.
Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Its Stieltjes transform always indicated here by
\widehat{\nu}
is defined by

(2.1)

\widehat{\nu}(z):=\int\frac{\nu(dx)}{z-x},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}\,.

The additive convolution of
\nu
with the semicircle law
\mu:=\nu\boxplus\mu_{\rm{sc},\sigma}
is the unique probability measure whose Stieltjes transform satisfies

(2.2)

\widehat{\mu}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\nu(dx)}{x-\sqrt{2}z+2\beta^{2}\widehat{\mu}(z)},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

We list several useful properties of the Stieltjes transform in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure on

and denote by

•

Thus, from (

Lemma 2.3

The proof of Proposition
4.1
needs some preparation. We start by two deterministic lemmas. The first one compares the quantity

(see (

)) between matrices which differ by a lower rank matrix. Then in the second lemma we deduce a similar bound for certain minors of a given matrix.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
N>2k
. Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
be symmetric, invertible matrices such that
\textup{rank}(A-B)\>\leqlant\>k
.
Then

(4.10)

\displaystyle N|L(A)-L(B)|<2k|\log(\|A\|_{\textup{op}})|+2k|\log(\|B\|_{\textup{op}})|-2k\log(\lambda_{\textup{min}}(B))-2k\log(\lambda_{\textup{min}}(A)).

Proof.

Let
p\in[N]\cup\{0\}
denote the number of positive eigenvalues of
A
, that is

(4.11)

\lambda_{1}(A)\>\geqslant\>\ldots\>\geqslant\>\lambda_{p}(A)\>\geqslant\>0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad 0\>\geqslant\>\lambda_{p+1}(A)\>\geqslant\>\ldots\>\geqslant\>\lambda_{N}(A)\,.

Since the matrix
A-B
has rank less or equal to
k
, then
\lambda_{j}(A-B)=0
for all
j\in\{k+1,\cdots,N-k\}
. Hence the Weyl inequality for the eigenvalues yields

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(A)\>\geqslant\>\lambda_{j+k}(B)\,,

\displaystyle 1\>\leqlant\>j\>\leqlant\>N-k\,,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(A)\>\leqlant\>\lambda_{j-k}(B)\,,

\displaystyle k+1\>\leqlant\>j\>\leqlant\>N\,.

Then by (
4.11
)

(4.14)

\displaystyle 0\>\leqlant\>\lambda_{j}(A)\>\leqlant\>\lambda_{j-k}(B)\,,

\displaystyle\textup{if $p>k$ and }k+1\>\leqlant\>j\>\leqlant\>p\,,

(4.15)

\displaystyle 0\>\geqslant\>\lambda_{j}(A)\>\geqslant\>\lambda_{j+k}(B)\,,

\displaystyle\textup{if $p<N-k$ and }p+1\>\leqlant\>j\>\leqlant\>N-k\,.

For the remaining eigenvalues, we consider the upper bound

(4.16)

|\lambda_{j}(A)|\>\leqlant\>\|A\|_{\textup{op}},\quad\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,k\}\cup\{N-k+1,\cdots,N\}

and the lower bound

(4.17)

|\lambda_{j}(B)|\>\geqslant\>\lambda_{\textup{min}}(B),\qquad j\in[N].

We distinguish few cases.

I.

p=0
. We have

(4.18)

\displaystyle NL(A)

\displaystyle\leqlant\>\sum_{j=1}^{N-k}\log\left(\left|\lambda_{j+4}\left(B\right)\right|\right)+\sum^{N}_{i=N-k+1}\log\left(|\lambda_{i}\left(A\right)|\right)

\displaystyle\leqlant\>NL(B)-k\log\left(\lambda_{\textup{min}}\left(B\right)\right)+k\log\left(\|A\|_{\textup{op}}\right).

II.

p\in[k]
. We have

(4.19)

\displaystyle NL(A)

\displaystyle\leqlant\>\sum_{j=p+1}^{N-k}\log\left(\left|\lambda_{j+k}\left(B\right)\right|\right)+\sum^{p}_{j=1}\log\left(|\lambda_{j}\left(A\right)|\right)+\sum^{N}_{j=N-k+1}\log\left(|\lambda_{j}\left(A\right)|\right)

\displaystyle\leqlant\>NL(B)-(p+k)\log\left(\lambda_{\textup{min}}\left(B\right)\right)+(p+k)\log\left(\|A\|_{\textup{op}}\right)

and
p+k\>\leqlant\>2k
.

III.

p\in\{k+1,\cdots,N-k\}
. We have

(4.20)

\displaystyle NL(A)

\displaystyle\leqlant\>\sum_{j=k+1}^{p}\log\left(\left|\lambda_{j-k}\left(B\right)\right|\right)+\sum_{j=p+1}^{N-k}\log\left(\left|\lambda_{j+k}\left(B\right)\right|\right)+\sum^{k}_{i=1}\log\left(|\lambda_{i}\left(A\right)|\right)+\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}\log\left(|\lambda_{N-i}\left(A\right)|\right)

\displaystyle=NL(B)-\sum^{p+k}_{j=p-k+1}\log(|\lambda_{j}\left(B\right)|)+2k\log\left(\|A\|_{\textup{op}}\right)

\displaystyle=NL(B)-2k\log(\lambda_{\textup{min}}\left(B\right))+2k\log\left(\|A\|_{\textup{op}}\right).

IV.

p\in\{N-k+1,\cdots,N\}
. We have

(4.21)

\displaystyle NL(A)

\displaystyle\leqlant\>\sum_{j=k+1}^{p}\log\left(\left|\lambda_{j-k}\left(B\right)\right|\right)+\sum^{k}_{i=1}\log\left(|\lambda_{i}\left(A\right)|\right)+\sum^{N}_{j=p+1}\log\left(|\lambda_{j}\left(A\right)|\right)

\displaystyle\leqlant\>NL(B)-(N-p+k)\log\left(\lambda_{\textup{min}}\left(B\right)\right)+(N-p+k)\log\left(\|A\|_{\textup{op}}\right)

and
N-p+k\>\leqlant\>2k
.

From the above cases, we deduce

(4.22)

N(L(A)-L(B))\>\leqlant\>2k\log(\|A\|_{\textup{op}})-2k\log(\lambda_{\min}(B)).

Reverting the role of
A
and
B
we obtain also

(4.23)

N(L(B)-L(A))\>\leqlant\>2k\log(\|B\|_{\textup{op}})-2k\log(\lambda_{\min}(A)).

Combining (
4.22
) and (
4.23
) we get

(4.24)

N|L(A)-L(B)|\>\leqlant\>2k\max\left(\log(\|A\|_{\textup{op}})-\log(\lambda_{\min}(B)),\,\,\log(\|B\|_{\textup{op}})-\log(\lambda_{\min}(A))\right),

whence (
4.10
) follows.
∎

Recall that

Let

(4.27)

\hline\cr\\[-10.0pt]

We have

(4.30)

\widetilde{A}-A=P_{[k]}+P_{[k]}AP_{[k]}-P_{[k]}A-AP_{[k]}=P_{[k]}(I_{N}+A)P_{[k]}-(P_{[k]}A+AP_{[k]}).

The first summand on the r.h.s. of (

\textup{rank}(\widetilde{A}-A)\>\leqlant\>2k

(4.31)

N|L(\widetilde{A})-L(A)|\>\leqlant\>4k|\log(\|A\|_{\textup{op}})|+4k|\log(\|\widetilde{A}\|_{\textup{op}})|-4k\log(\lambda_{\min}(A))-4k\log(\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde{A})).

We have

(4.32)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{A}\|_{\textup{op}}\>\leqlant\>\|P_{[k]}\|_{\textup{op}}+\|P^{\perp}_{[k]}AP^{\perp}_{[k]}\|_{\textup{op}}\>\leqlant\>1+\|A\|_{\textup{op}}

and
\|P^{\perp}_{[k]}AP^{\perp}_{[k]}\|_{\textup{op}}\>\leqlant\>\|A\|_{\textup{op}}
. Moreover

(4.33)

\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde{A})=\min\left(1,\lambda_{\min}(A^{([k],[k])})\right).

Thus

(4.34)

|\log(\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde{A}))|\>\leqlant\>|\log(\lambda_{\textup{min}}(A^{([k],[k])}))|

Hence, combining (

), (
4.32
) and (

, we end the proof.

Recall that

(4.35)

Z=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{N}}J-D=Z_{*}-A.

5.
Preliminaries on the Bray-Moore formula

In this section we begin the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
First of all, let us recall some definitions from Section
1
. The Hessian of the TAP free energy is given by

(5.1)

\nabla^{2}F_{\TAP}(m)=\frac{\beta J}{\sqrt{N}}-D_{N}(m)+4\frac{\beta^{2}}{N}mm^{T}\,,

where

(5.2)

D_{N}(m):=\diag(\{(1-m_{i}^{2})^{-1}+2\beta^{2}(1-Q(m))\}_{i\in[N]})\,.

From now on we denote

(5.3)

Z:=\frac{\beta J}{\sqrt{N}}-D_{N}(m)\,.

Consider the event

\Omega(m):=\{J\,:\,\nabla F_{\TAP}(m)=0\}
. We analyse the law of the Hessian conditionally on
\Omega(m)
, which we denote by

(5.4)

\nabla^{2}F_{\TAP}(m)\big|_{\Omega(m)}\,.

The determinant of the matrix (
5.4
) is what we call the Bray-Moore determinant.

In what follows we consider
m\neq 0
. Moreover we need to impose a cutoff which keeps
m
away from the boundary of
[-1,1]^{N}
, where the TAP free energy is singular. For
\alpha>0
we set

(5.5)

L_{\alpha,N}=[\,-1+e^{-\alpha\sqrt{N}},1-e^{-\alpha\sqrt{N}}\,]^{N}\setminus\{0\}

and we convey that

from now on.

Since the matrix
D_{N}(m)
depends on
m\in L_{\alpha,N}
the additive convolution of the empirical law of the eigenvalues of

with the semicircle law and its Stieltjes transform will also depend on
m
. They are given by

(5.6)

\mu_{m}:=\nu_{D_{N}}\boxplus\mu_{\rm{sc},\sqrt{2}\beta}

and

(5.7)

\widehat{\mu}_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{1}{(1-m_{i}^{2})^{-1}+\beta^{2}(1-Q(m))-\sqrt{2}z-2\beta^{2}\widehat{\mu}(z)}\,.

We also remark that, even though this is not made explicit in our notation, the measure
\mu_{m}
depends on
N
through the choice of the point

.

The connection between the additive convolution and the Bray-Moore formula (see (
1.7
), (
1.8
), (
1.11
)) is provided by the following statement.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
, and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough. For any

it holds

(5.8)

\int\log|x|\mu_{m}(dx)=\min_{g\>\leqlant\>1-Q(m)}\left(\beta^{2}g^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\log((1-m_{i}^{2})^{-1}+2\beta^{2}(1-Q(m))-2\beta^{2}g)\right)\,

and the minimum is attained at a unique point in
[0,1-Q(m)]
.
Moreover the
\min

(

)
is attained at

if and only if the Plefka condition

(5.9)

2\beta^{2}\sum^{N}_{i=1}(1-m_{i}^{2})^{2}\>\leqlant\>1

holds.

Proof.

Let us set for brevity

(5.10)

\displaystyle R_{N}(g)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\beta^{2}g^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\log((1-m_{i}^{2})^{-1}+2\beta^{2}(1-Q(m))-2\beta^{2}g)\,,

(5.11)

\displaystyle F_{N}(g)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(1-m_{i}^{2})^{-1}+2\beta^{2}(1-Q(m))-2\beta^{2}g}\,.

The function
R_{N}
is piece-wise differentiable and it diverges for

. Therefore it attains its minima in the interior of its support.
Moreover
F_{N}
is a rational function with
K
poles

all lying on the positive semi-axis, which we order increasingly.
We have

(5.12)

It is easy to see that there is no solutions of

on the negative semi-axis. Moreover in
[

, Lemma 4.8, Lemma 4.9]
it is proven that

1.

In
(0,p_{1})
there are at most two solutions of the equation

, namely

and

with

;

;

3.

It holds that

.

We conclude

\int\log|x|\mu_{m}(dx)=\min_{g\>\leqlant\>1-Q(m)}R_{N}(g)\,.

Now assume that (
5.9
) holds. Computing the first derivative of the function

we get

(5.13)

and clearly for any

Since

, it must be

, hence if

.
∎

Therefore Theorem

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
\beta>0,h\in\mathbb{R}
. The set
\{m\in(-1,1)^{N}\,:\,v=0\}
is discrete with

(6.88)

\card\{m\in(-1,1)^{N}\,:\,v=0\}\lesssim 3^{N}.

Proof.

Recalling (
5.2
) and (
6.1
),

is equivalent to

(6.89)

\frac{m}{1-m^{2}}+h-\atanh m=4\beta^{2}mQ(m)

where we use the notation for which the vector
f(m)
has
i

. Note that if

then

for our next considerations.

For

we set

Thus

\displaystyle\{m\in(-1,1)^{N}\setminus\{0\}\mid v=0\}

\displaystyle=\{m\in(-1,1)^{N}\setminus\{0\}\mid U(m_{i})=Q(m)\,\forall i\in[N]\}

(6.90)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{q\in(0,1]}\left\{m\in(-1,1)^{N}\setminus\{0\}\mid Q(m)=q,\,\,U(m_{i})=q\,\forall i\in[N]\right\}\,.

We first argue that for a fixed

(6.91)

U(m)=q,\quad m\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\},

has finitely many solutions,
and that these solutions are real analytic as functions of

has a single root

only for

). The inverses

,

,
thus exist and are analytic functions. Setting

When solving the vector equation for a fixed

Let now

Recurrence of the plane Elephant random walk
Nicolas Curien, Lucile Laulin

Abstract
We give a short proof of the recurrence of the two-dimensional elephant random walk in the diffusive regime. This was recently established by Qin [ 5 ] , but our proof mainly uses very rough comparison with the standard plane random walk. We hope that the method can be useful for other applications.

Introduction

The elephant random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
has been introduced in dimension 1 by Schütz and Trimper
[
6
]
and is a well-studied discrete process with reinforcement, see
[
3
]
for background and references. Its definition (see (
2.1
)) depends on a memory parameter
1
1

1

The usual definition uses a memory parameter
p\in[0,1]
which is the probability to reproduce a (uniform) former step of the walk, or to move in one of the
3
remaining directions with the same probability
(1-p)/3
so that
\alpha=(4p-1)/3
, see
[
3
, Eq. (1.4)]
.

\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2d-1},1)
and it exhibits a phase transition going from a diffusive when
\alpha<\alpha_{c}=\frac{1}{2}
to a superdiffusive behavior when
\alpha>\alpha_{c}
. We focus here on the two-dimensional case and establish recurrence of the process in the diffusive regime.

Theorem 1.1
.

In the diffusive regime
\alpha<\alpha_{c}=\frac{1}{2}
, the plane elephant random walk is recurrent.

This has been recently proved by Qin
[
5
]
but our approach is different and much shorter, however it gives less quantitive information and does not directly apply in the critical regime
\alpha=\alpha_{c}
.
We use a comparaison to the simple random walk which could apply in dimension 1 as well since the simple random walk is also recurrent in that case.

It is worth pointing out that
[
5
]
established that the elephant random walk is always transient when
d\geq 3
, similar to the simple random walk.

Notation.

We write
\mathbf{e}_{i}
the four directions of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for
1\leq i\leq 4
. We shall write
(X_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}k\geq 0)
for the canonical underlying process starting from
\mathbf{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(0,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we denote its steps by
\Delta X_{k}=X_{k+1}-X_{k}\in\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{3},\mathbf{e}_{4}\}
and we introduce for
1\leq i\leq 4
the centered counting direction processes
D^{[X]}_{k}(\mathbf{e}_{i})
defined by

\displaystyle D^{[X]}_{k}(\mathbf{e}_{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{1}\{X_{j+1}-X_{j}=\mathbf{e}_{i}\}-\frac{k}{4},\qquad\mbox{ in particular notice that }\quad\sum_{i=1}^{4}D^{[X]}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})=0.

(1.1)

For any stopping time
\theta
, we denote by
X^{(\theta)}
the shifted process
X^{(\theta)}_{k}=X_{\theta+k}-X_{\theta}
for
k\geq 0
. Finally
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is the canonical filtration generated by the first
n
steps of the walk and we use
X_{[0,n]}
as a shorthand for
(X_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\leq k\leq n)
.

Comparison between elephant and simple random walk

Under the law
\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}
the underlying process
(X)
evolves as the standard simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, whereas under
\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-1.99168pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}
, it evolves as the
\alpha
-elephant random walk i.e. satisfying for
n\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-1.99168pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}(\Delta X_{n}=\mathbf{e}_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{1}{4}+\alpha\frac{D^{[X]}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})}{n},

(2.1)

(where we interpret
0/0=0
for
n=0
). In particular, under
\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-1.99168pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}
, the process
(D_{k}^{[X]}(\mathbf{e}_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}1\leq i\leq 4,k\geq 0)
is Markov and evolves as an urn process with four colors, which was crucially used in
[
1
]
to establish the phase transition diffusive/superdiffusive. The local evolution of the elephant random walk (for large times) ressembles that of the simple random walk and this is quantified in the following propsition:

Proposition 2.1

(Markov contiguity)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and any
A>0
, there exist
c_{\varepsilon,A}>0
and a sequence of events
E_{n}
satisfying
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}(X_{[0,n]}\in E_{n})\geq 1-\varepsilon
such that for any measurable function
f
,

\mathbb{E}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}\left[f\big(X^{(n)}_{[0,n]}\big)\mathbf{1}_{X^{(n)}_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{n}\mbox{ and }\frac{|D^{[X]}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq A\mbox{ for all }1\leq i\leq 4\right]\right.\geq c_{\varepsilon,A}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[f\big(X_{[0,n]})\mathbf{1}_{X_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}}\right].

Proof.

In the event considered in the conditioning, we have
|D_{n}^{[X]}(\mathbf{e}_{i})|\leq A\sqrt{n}
for all
1\leq i\leq 4
. By (
2.1
), the Radon–Nikodym derivative of
(X_{n+k}-X_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\leq k\leq n)
under
\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-1.99168pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{RND}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\prod_{k=n}^{2n-1}\left(1+4\alpha\frac{D_{k}^{[X]}(\Delta X_{k})}{k}\right)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1+4\alpha\frac{D_{n}^{[X]}(\Delta X^{(n)}_{k})+D_{k}^{[X^{(n)}]}(\Delta X^{(n)}_{k})}{n+k}\right).

(2.2)

By Donsker’s invariance principle, we can find a constant
A_{\varepsilon}
such that the event

G_{n}=\left\{\max_{i}\sup_{0\leq k\leq n}|D_{k}^{[X^{(n)}]}(\mathbf{e}_{i})|\leq A_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{n}\right\}

has probability at least
1-\varepsilon
under
\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}
. On this event (and conditionally on the event of the statement of the proposition), the counting directions processes
D_{[n,2n]}^{[X]}(\mathbf{e}_{i})
are in absolute value bounded by
(A+A_{\varepsilon})\sqrt{n}
. In particular, using
\log(1+\alpha x)\geq\alpha x-(\alpha x)^{2}
for small
|x|
, we deduce that on this event, for
n
large enough, the Radon-Nikodym derivative in (
2.2
) is lower bounded by

\mathrm{RND}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}}\geq\exp\left(4\alpha M_{n}-(4\alpha)^{2}(A+A_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}}\quad\mbox{where}\quad M_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{D_{n+k}^{[X]}(\Delta X_{n+k})}{n+k}.

Using (
1.1
), it is trivial to check that
(M_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\leq j\leq n)
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{n+\cdot})
- martingale with quadratic variation

\mathbb{E}[M_{j+1}^{2}-M_{j}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n+j}]=\mathbb{E}[(M_{j+1}-M_{j})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n+j}]=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{4}\left(D_{n+j}^{[X]}(\mathbf{e}_{i})\right)^{2}}{(n+j)^{2}}\underset{\mbox{on }G_{n}}{\leq}\frac{(A+A_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{n}.

Consider then the
(\mathcal{F}_{n+\cdot})
-stopping time

\vartheta=\inf\left\{j\geq 0\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\left\{\max_{i}\sup_{0\leq k\leq j}|D_{k}^{[X^{(n)}]}(\mathbf{e}_{i})|\leq A_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{n}\right\}\right\}.

By the penultimate display, the stopped martingale
(M_{k\wedge\vartheta}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\leq j\leq n)
has quadratic variation bounded above by
n\times\frac{(A+A_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{n}
and it follows that
\mathbb{E}[M_{n}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[M_{n\wedge\vartheta}^{2}]\leq(A+A_{\varepsilon})^{2}
.

In particular, thanks to Markov inequality, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the event
H_{n}=\{|M_{n}|\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}}<\frac{(A+A_{\varepsilon})}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\}
is of probability at least
1-\varepsilon
. Gathering up the pieces, on the event
E_{n}=G_{n}\cap H_{n}
which is of
\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}
measure at least
1-2\varepsilon
, the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the elephant w.r.t. the simple random walk is at least
\mathrm{e}^{-4\alpha\frac{(A+A_{\varepsilon})}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}-(4\alpha)^{2}(A+A_{\varepsilon})^{2}}=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}c_{\varepsilon,A}
. ∎

. Conditionally on

and on the fact that the counting directions processes are controlled at time

, the blue and red parts are independent on events of large probability. This is sufficient to imply a lower bound on the probability of return to
\mathbf{0}
.

It is classical that in the plane, the simple random walk started from
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\|x\|\approx\sqrt{n}
has a probability of order
\log^{-1}n
to visit
(0,0)
within
n
steps. Our weak bound (Proposition
2.1
) is sufficient to imply the same kind of estimate for the elephant random walk:

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
A>0
there exists
c_{A}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\hskip-1.99168pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}\left[\exists\ \frac{5}{2}n\leq k\leq 3n\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X_{k}=0\left|\mathcal{F}_{n}\mbox{ and }\frac{|D^{[X]}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq A\mbox{ for all }1\leq i\leq 4\right]\right.\geq\frac{c_{A}}{\log n}.

Proof.

Let us denote
\mathbf{x}_{n}=X_{n}
which is fixed conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Using Proposition
2.1
twice, for any positive functions
f
and
g
and any
A,A^{\prime}>0
and any
\varepsilon>0
, we can find two sequences of events
E_{n}
and
E_{n}^{\prime}
and constants
c_{\varepsilon,A}
and
c_{\varepsilon,A^{\prime}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}\left[f(X^{(2n)}_{[0,n]})g(X^{(n)}_{[0,n]})\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}\left[f(X^{(2n)}_{[0,n]})g(X^{(n)}_{[0,n]})\mathbf{1}_{\frac{\|D^{[X]}_{2n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})\|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq A^{\prime},\ \forall 1\leq i\leq 4}\Bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,A^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[f(X_{[0,n]})\mathbf{1}_{X_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}^{\prime}}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}\left.\left[\mathbf{1}_{\frac{\|D^{[X]}_{2n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})\|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq A^{\prime},\ \forall 1\leq i\leq 4}g(X^{(n)}_{[0,n]})\right|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,A^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[f(X_{[0,n]})\mathbf{1}_{X_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}^{\prime}}\right]\cdot c_{\varepsilon,A}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[g(X_{[0,n]})\mathbf{1}_{X_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{\frac{\|D^{[X]}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})\|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq A^{\prime}-A,\ \forall 1\leq i\leq 4}\right]\mathbf{1}_{\frac{\|D^{[X]}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})\|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq A,\ \forall 1\leq i\leq 4}.

Up to increasing
A^{\prime}
we may suppose that the event
H_{n}=E_{n}\cap E^{\prime}_{n}\cap\{\frac{\|D^{[X]}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{i})\|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq A^{\prime}-A,\ \forall 1\leq i\leq 4\}
has probability at least
1-3\varepsilon
and particularizing the inequality above, we deduce that for some constant
\tilde{c}_{\varepsilon,A}>0
the probability in the proposition is lower bounded by

\tilde{c}_{\varepsilon,A}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left(\exists\ \frac{3}{2}n\leq k\leq 2n\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X_{k}=-\mathbf{x}_{n}\mbox{ and }\left.\begin{array}[]{l}X^{(0)}_{[0,n]}\in H_{n}\\
X^{(n)}_{[0,n]}\in H_{n}\end{array}\right.\right),

so that we can apply the following lemma to conclude.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
A>0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta_{A}>0
so that if
\mathbf{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is such that
\|\mathbf{x}_{n}\|\leq A\sqrt{n}
and if
E_{n}
is a sequence of events such that
\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}(X_{[0,n]}\in E_{n})\geq 1-\varepsilon
then we have

\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left(\exists\frac{3}{2}n\leq k\leq 2n\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X_{k}=-\mathbf{x}_{n}\mbox{ and }\left.\begin{array}[]{l}X^{(0)}_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}\\
X^{(n)}_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}\end{array}\right.\right)\geq\frac{\delta_{A}}{\log n}.

Proof.

We use a second-moment method on the random variable

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}^{E_{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\#\left\{\frac{3}{2}n\leq k\leq 2n\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X_{k}=-\mathbf{x}_{n}\right\}\mathbf{1}_{X^{(0)}_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{X^{(n)}_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}}.

We denote by
p_{k}^{E_{n}}(y)=\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}[\mathbf{1}_{X_{k}=y}\mathbf{1}_{X_{[0,n]}\in E_{n}}]
and
p_{k}(y)=\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}(X_{k}=y)
for the heat kernels. By the standard local limit theorem (or just Stirling approximation on the binomial coefficients) there exists
C>0
such that
p_{k}(y)\leq\frac{C}{k}
for all
k\geq 1
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. First, by lifting the restrictions on
E_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[\left(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}^{E_{n}}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[\left(\sum_{k=3/2n}^{2n}\mathbf{1}_{X_{k}=-\mathbf{x}_{n}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\frac{3}{2}n\leq k\leq k^{\prime}\leq 2n}p_{k}(-\mathbf{x}_{n})p_{k^{\prime}-k}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\frac{3}{2}n\leq k\leq k^{\prime}\leq 2n}\frac{C}{n}\frac{C}{k^{\prime}-k}\leq{C^{\prime}}\log(n),

for some
C^{\prime}>0
(independent of
n
). To evaluate the first moment, introduce the (truncated) Green functions
g^{E_{n}}(y)=\sum_{k=n/2}^{n}p_{k}^{E_{n}}(y)
and similarly
g(y)=\sum_{k=n/2}^{n}p_{k}(y)
. In particular, since
\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}(E_{n})\geq 1-\varepsilon
we have
\|p-p^{E_{n}}\|_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{y}p(y)-p^{E_{n}}(y)\leq\varepsilon
and similarly and
\|g-g^{E_{n}}\|_{1}=\sum_{y}g(y)-g^{E_{n}}(y)\leq\varepsilon n
. Recalling that
\frac{C}{n}\geq p^{E_{n}}_{n}(y)\geq p_{n}(y)
and
2C\geq g^{E_{n}}(y)\geq g(y)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}[\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}^{E_{n}}]=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{n}^{E_{n}}(y)g^{E_{n}}(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}p_{n}^{E_{n}}(y)g^{E_{n}}(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})-p^{E_{n}}_{n}(y)g(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})\\
-p_{n}(y)g(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})+p_{n}^{E_{n}}(y)g(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})\\
+p_{n}(y)g(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{y}p_{n}(y)g(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})-\|p_{n}^{E_{n}}\|_{\infty}\|g-g^{E_{n}}\|_{1}-\|g\|_{\infty}\|p_{n}-p_{n}^{E_{n}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{y}p_{n}(y)g(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})-3C^{2}\varepsilon.

However, since
\|\mathbf{x}_{n}\|\leq A\sqrt{n}
, the local limit theorem implies that
\sum_{y}p_{n}(y)g(-y-\mathbf{x}_{n})>c_{A}
for some
c_{A}>0
independently of
n
and so one can choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that if
\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}(E_{n})\geq 1-\varepsilon
then we have
\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}^{E_{n}}\right]>c_{A}/2
. We conclude by the second moment method that

\mathbb{P}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}^{E_{n}}>0)\geq\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}^{E_{n}}\right]/\mathbb{E}_{\includegraphics[width]{srwbis}}\left[\left(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}^{E_{n}}\right)^{2}\right]\geq\frac{c_{A}}{2C^{\prime}\ \log n}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us denote
\mathcal{P}_{3^{j}}=\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-1.99168pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}(\exists\ 3^{j}\leq k\leq 3^{j+1},X_{k}=\mathbf{0}\mid\mathcal{F}_{3^{j}})
. When
\alpha<\alpha_{c}
, i.e. the diffusive regime, Bertenghi
[
1
, Theorem 4.2]
showed that under
\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}
we have

\left(\frac{D_{n}^{X}(\mathbf{e}_{i})}{\sqrt{n}}\right)_{1\leq i\leq 4}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{(d)}(\mathcal{X}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4},

for some random variable
\mathcal{X}
(whose distribution is irrelevant for our purposes). Together with our Proposition
2.2
, this shows that in the diffusive regime, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for large
j
’s we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}(j\cdot\mathcal{P}_{3^{j}}>\delta)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

(3.1)

Notice that the variables
\mathcal{P}_{3^{j}}
are not independent, but Jeulin’s lemma
[
4
, Proposition 3.2]
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{3^{k}}=\infty,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}-a.s.

(3.2)

To be honest we rather use the proof that the lemma itself, and since the argument is short let us reproduce it here: Suppose by contradiction that there exists
\varepsilon,M>0
so that the event
A=\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{3^{k}}<M\}
has probability at least
\varepsilon>0
. Using (
3.1
) we take
\delta>0
so that
\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-0.56905pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}(j\cdot\mathcal{P}_{3^{j}}>\delta)\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and write

M\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{A}\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{P}_{3^{k}}\right]\geq\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\delta}{j}\cdot\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{\hskip-1.99168pt\includegraphics[width]{elpter}}\left(A\cap\left\{\mathcal{P}_{3^{j}}>\frac{\delta}{j}\right\}\right)}_{\geq\varepsilon-(1-(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}))=\varepsilon/2}=\infty,

which is a contradiction. Given (
3.2
), the conditional Borel-Cantelli lemma (
[
2
, Theorem 4.3.4]
) then implies that the events
\{\exists\ 3^{j}\leq k\leq 3^{j+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X_{k}=\mathbf{0}\}
happen for infinitely many
j
’s with probability one, implying recurrence of the process.

Two geometric proofs of the classification of algebraic supergroups with semisimple representation theory
Alexander Sherman

Abstract
. We present two novel proofs of the known classification of connected affine algebraic supergroups G G such that Rep ⁡ G \operatorname{Rep}G is semisimple. The proofs are geometrically motivated, although both rely on an algebraic lemma that characterizes 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ n ) \mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n) amongst simple Lie superalgebras.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected affine algebraic supergroup over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. When is the category
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
semisimple?

If
G=G_{0}
is an algebraic group, the answer to this question is elegant:
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple if and only if
G
is reductive, meaning that
R_{u}(G)
, the unipotent radical of
G
, is trivial. Equivalently,
G
has a faithful semisimple representation. This theorem leads to the study of reductive algebraic groups, which has a well-known and beautiful classification in terms of root datum.

For supergroups, semisimplicity is a rare phenomenon. Perhaps the most natural supergroup to study is the general linear supergroup,
G=GL(V)
, for a super vector space
V
. However its representation category is not semisimple unless
V
is purely even or purely odd, i.e. unless
G=G_{0}
.

In
[
Hoc76
]
and
[
DH76
]
, it was proven that the only simple Lie superalgebra which has semisimple finite-dimensional representation theory and is not purely even is
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
. If
\mathfrak{g}
is an arbitrary Lie superalgebra with semisimple finite-dimensional representation theory, then it must be a direct product of simple ones with the same property, so this effectively answers our question on the level of Lie superalgebras. We write the formal statement on the level of supergroups in
1.1
.

However the original proof of Djokovic and Hochschild came before Kac’s seminal paper on Lie superalgebras (
[
Kac77
]
) and before much of the existing technology in the study of Lie superalgebras and supergroups had been developed. We seek to supplement their original result with new proofs which also provide insight into the lack of semisimplicity for the representation theory of Lie superalgebras and supergroups.

Therefore we present two geometric proofs of the following classification theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a connected algebraic supergroup. Then
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple if and only if

G\cong K\times OSp(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k})

for a reductive algebraic group
K
and positive integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
.

We follow the convention of
[
EAS20
]
to write
OSp(1|2n)
for what is more precisely written
SOSp(1|2n)
, i.e. the form-preserving automorphisms of
(k^{1|2n},(-,-))
with Berezinian 1.

The statement of the theorem is reduced, via an algebraic argument, to the following statement. Define

\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}=\{u\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}:u^{2}(=[u,u]/2)\text{ is semisimple in }\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\}.

Note that this space was considered previously by Serganova (in private communication).

Theorem 1.2
.

\operatorname{Rep}G
is semisimple if and ony if
G_{0}
is reductive and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}=\{0\}
.

We note the following nice interpretation of
1.2
. If
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}
, write
h:=u^{2}
. We define
DS_{u}
to be the functor
V\mapsto(V^{h})_{u}
on the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Here,
V^{h}
denotes the
h
-invariants of
V
, and since
u^{2}=h
we have that
u
acts by a square-zero operator on
V^{h}
, and
(V^{h})_{u}
denotes its cohomology. We call
DS_{u}
a Duflo-Serganova functor.

Then
DS_{u}
is a tensor functor and enjoys many of the same properties as the usual Duflo-Serganova functor defined in
[
DS05
]
, where
u^{2}=0
by assumption. We may consider
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}
as the parameter space for Duflo-Serganova functors on the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Then
1.2
tells us that the existence of Duflo-Serganova functors is only a feature of algebraic supergroups with non-semisimple representation theory.

We also call attention to recent work of Entova and Serganova in
[
EAS20
]
where a different equivalent condition for semisimplicity of
\operatorname{Rep}G
was given. There, the authors define a subset
\mathfrak{g}_{neat}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
, which consists of all
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
with the property that
u^{2}
is nilpotent, and such that the action of
u
on any finite-dimensional representation of
G
may be extended to an action of
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2)
. They prove that
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple if and only if
\mathfrak{g}_{neat}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
. One consequence of this is that if
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple, then
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{1|1},G)=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
, where
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{1|1}
is the supergroup with Lie superalgebra generated freely by one odd element and
(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{1|1})_{0}=\mathbb{G}_{a}
.

On the other hand,
1.2
may be stated by saying that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{G}_{a},G)=\{0\}
, where
\mathbb{M}
is the minuscule supergroup defined in section 5 of
[
EAS20
]
. The supergroup
\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{G}_{a}
has a one-dimensional odd part and an infinite-dimensional pro-reductive central even part, i
.
e
.
an infinite-dimensional torus. We may view
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{G}_{a})
as the category of representations of the two-dimensional Lie superalgebra freely generated by an odd element
u
such that
[u,u]
acts semisimply. In general we have an identification
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{G}_{a},G)=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The author has declined to use a term for the property of a supergroup that
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
be semisimple. This is due to fear of overuse of particular words, e.g. semisimple or reductive. To avoid confusion, we don’t give this property a name.

2.
Preliminary arguments

We refer to
[
CCF11
]
for background on algebraic supergroups. We write
G
for a connected affine algebraic supergroup, and
\mathfrak{g}
for its Lie superalgebra. If
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
we will write
u^{2}
for
[u,u]/2
. The even part of
G
is written
G_{0}
. Let
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
denote the category of rational
G
-modules. Recall that
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is equivalent to the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules that also integrate to an action of
G_{0}
.

We write
k
for the trivial representation of
G
. The following is well-known.

Lemma 2.1
.

The category
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple if and only if
k
is projective.

2.1.
Coordinate ring of
G

Write
k[G]
for the coordinate ring of functions on
G
, a supercommutative Hopf superalgebra. We will view
k[G]
as a
G
-module according the action by left translation, or as a
G\times G
-module according to the actions by left and right translation. Recall that as a
G
-module we have
k[G]\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{0}}^{G}k[G_{0}]
, i
.
e
.
we have an identification of superalgebras (see
[
Kos82
]
)

k[G]=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g},k[G_{0}]).

Further, this identification is
G_{0}
-equivariant, in the senses that it realizes
k[G]
as
S(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{*})\otimes k[G_{0}]
as a
G_{0}
-module. In particular,
k[G_{0}]
is a
G_{0}
-stable subalgebra of
k[G]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The
G
-submodule
k\cdot 1
splits off of
k[G]
.

(2)

The
G\times G
submodule
k\cdot 1
splits off of
k[G]
.

(3)

k
is projective as a
G
-module.

Proof.

Recall that a
G
-module is the same as a
k[G]
-comodule. In the category of
k[G]
-comodules,
k[G]
is injective, and thus if
k\cdot 1
splits off it must be injective as well. By duality,
k
will also be projective. Now
(1)\iff(3)
follow and from this it is easy to prove
(2)\iff(3)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple, then
G_{0}
is reductive.

Proof.

By
lemma

2.2
, if
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple then
k\cdot 1
splits off
k[G]
as a
G
-module. However
k[G_{0}]
may be realized as a
G_{0}
-subalgebra of
k[G]
, and thus
k\cdot 1
splits off of
k[G_{0}]
. By
lemma

2.2
we have
k
is a projective
G_{0}
-module and thus
\operatorname{Rep}(G_{0})
is semisimple, and therefore
G_{0}
is reductive.
∎

A supergroup
G
is called quasireductive if
G_{0}
is reductive. We have thus shown that if
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple, then
G
is quasireductive. Quasireductive supergroups and in particular their representation theory were studied in
[
Ser11
]
amongst other places.

2.2.
Properties of
\mathfrak{g}

Recall that a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is quasireductive if
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is reductive and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
is a semisimple
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-module. In particular the Lie superalgebra of a quasireductive Lie supergroup is itself quasireductive.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple, then
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
is a product of its center and simple quasireductive Lie superalgebras. Further, its center is purely even.

Proof.

The first statement follows immediately from semisimplicity and the reductivity of
G_{0}
. For the second statement, if the center had a non-trivial odd component then
G
would admit
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{0|1}
as a quotient, whose representation theory is not semisimple.
∎

2.3.
The Lie supergroup
OSp(1|2n)

We define the Lie supergroup
OSp(1|2n)
in terms of a corresponding super Harish-Chandra pair. First, let
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
be the Lie superalgebra with
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
and
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)_{\overline{1}}=k^{2n}
, the standard representation of
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
. Write
(-,-)
for an
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
-invariant symplectic form on
k^{2n}
. Then the odd Lie bracket
[-,-]:S^{2}k^{2n}\to\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
is given by

[u,v](w)=(u,w)v+(v,w)u

where we view
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
as a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(k^{2n})
. Notice that in this way
[-,-]:S^{2}k^{2n}\to\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
defines an isomorphism, and that any other
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
-equivariant map between these spaces differs from it by a scalar.

Now consider the super Harish-Chandra pair
(Sp(2n),\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n))
, where
Sp(2n)
has the natural action on
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
. This forms a super Harish-Chandra pair, and thus defines a (quasireductive) Lie supergroup, which we call
OSp(1|2n)
. As stated in the introduction, this supergroup is perhaps better denoted
SOSP(1|2n)
, but we drop the first
S
for aesthetic purposes.

Lemma 2.5
.

OSp(1|2n)
is the unique connected supergroup
G
with
\operatorname{Lie}G\cong\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
. Thus, for positive integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
,
OSp(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k})
is the unique connected supergroup with Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n_{k})
.

Proof.

Suppose
G
is a connected supergroup with
LieG\cong\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
. Then since
Sp(2n)
is simply connected,
G_{0}
must be a quotient of
Sp(2n)
by a finite central subgroup. However, the standard representation
k^{2n}
of
Sp(2n)
is faithful, and must descend to a representation of
G_{0}
. Thus we are forced to have
G_{0}\cong Sp(2n)
. The statement now follows.
∎

2.4.
A sufficient criteria

Define

\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}=\{u\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}:u^{2}\text{ is semisimple in }\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\}.

The following proof uses the ideas of Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 4.1 of
[
DH76
]
. Note that one could easily use the classification of simple Lie superalgebras, but we use a simpler, more direct proof.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple quasireductive Lie superalgebra with
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}\neq 0
, such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}=\{0\}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
.

Proof.

Choose a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
, which gives rise to a root decomposition on
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
be a non-zero root vector of weight
\alpha
. Note that
\alpha\neq 0
, else
u^{2}\in\mathfrak{t}
, a contradiction. Then
u^{2}
is of weight
2\alpha
, and must be non-zero of course, else
u^{2}
is semisimple. It follows that
2\alpha
is a root of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
and is divisible by
2
as a root. By the classification of irreducible root systems,
2\alpha
must belong to a root system of type
C_{n}
, and
2\alpha
is a long root. Further, if
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}>1
then the map
[-,-]:S^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
must give rise to some non-zero
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
such that
v^{2}=0
, since
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}=1
. Thus by our assumption,
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=1
.

So far we have shown that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
is a multiplicity-free sum of the standard module of different copies of
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be distinct irreducible summands in
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
. Then since
[-,-]:V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-equivariant, it must be 0, and thus
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
commute. Let
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
be an irreducible summand, and let
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(V)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
be the Lie superalgebra acting on it non-trivially. Let
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(V)\oplus V
. Then from what we have shown, it follows that
\mathfrak{k}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{g}
.

Now
S^{2}V\cong\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(V)
, and thus either
[-,-]:S^{2}V\to\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(V)
is an isomorphism or is the zero map. If it is the zero map, then
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}=V
defines an abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, a contradiction, so instead this map must be an isomorphism. It is now easy to prove that
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Suppose that
G
is a quasireductive Lie supergroup such that
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}=\{0\}
. Then there exists a reductive even subgroup
K
of
G
, and positive integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
such that

G\cong K\times OSp(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k}).

Proof.

By
lemma

2.4
, we may write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{g}_{k}
for a reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
and simple quasireductive Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{g}_{k}
with nonzero odd part. By
2.6
,
\mathfrak{g}_{i}\cong\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n_{i})
for some positive integer
n_{i}
. Let
\mathfrak{k}
integrate to the connected subgroup
K
of
G
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{g}_{k}
integrate to the connected subgroup
OSp(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k})
of
G
, where we use
lemma

2.5
. Thus we have a map

K\times OSp(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k})\to G

which induces an isomorphism of Lie superalgebras. Further,
K_{0}\cap(OSp(1|2n_{1})_{0}\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k})_{0})
is normal in
OSp(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k})
, and so must be trivial by
lemma

2.5
. Hence we find that

K\times OSp(1|2n_{1})\times\cdots\times OSp(1|2n_{k})\cong G.

∎

With
2.7
proven, it remains to prove the following.

Theorem 2.8
.

If
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is semisimple, then
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}=\{0\}
.

For this we offer two proofs, along with a proof that
\operatorname{Rep}(OSp(1|2n))
is indeed semisimple.

3.
Proof I: vector fields on
G

In light of
lemma

2.2
, in order to prove
2.8
, we show the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}\neq\{0\}
. Then
k\cdot 1
does not split off
k[G]
.

For the proof, we need an important lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be an affine supervariety, and suppose that
u
is an odd vector field on
X
such that
u^{2}
acts semisimply on
k[X]
. Suppose further that
u
is everywhere non-vanishing as a vector field. Then there exists a function
f\in A
such that
uf=1
. In particular,
k\cdot 1
does not split off
k[X]
as a module over the Lie superalgebra generated by
u
.

Proof.

Since
u
is everywhere non-vanishing and
X
is affine, for each closed point
x\in X(k)
there exist an odd function
\xi_{x}\in k[X]
such that
(u\xi_{x})(x)\neq 0
. Thus the ideal generated by
\{u\xi_{x}\}_{x\in X(k)}
is equal to
k[X]
, so we may choose finitely many functions in this set, say
u\xi_{1},\dots,u\xi_{k}
, and even functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
such that

g_{1}u\xi_{1}+\dots+g_{k}u\xi_{k}=1.

Set
p=g_{1}\xi_{1}+\dots+g_{k}\xi_{k}
. We compute that

up=g_{1}u\xi_{1}+\dots+g_{k}u\xi_{k}+u(g_{1})\xi_{1}+\dots+u(g_{k})\xi_{k}=1+\eta

where
\eta=u(g_{1})\xi_{1}+\dots+u(g_{k})\xi_{k}
. Since
u(g_{i})
is odd,
\eta\in(k[X]_{\overline{1}})^{2}
, and this nilpotent. Write
h=u^{2}
and recall that
[h,u]=0
by the Jacobi identity. We may then write
p=\sum\limits_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}
and
\eta=\sum\limits_{\lambda}\eta_{\lambda}
according the decomposition of
k[X]
into eigenspaces of
h
. Since
h
preserves
(k[X]_{\overline{1}})^{2}
,
\eta_{\lambda}\in(k[X]_{\overline{1}})^{2}
for all
\lambda
. Now we see that

up=\sum\limits_{\lambda}u(p_{\lambda})=1+\sum\limits_{\lambda}\eta_{\lambda}.

Since
h(1)=0
, we find that
\alpha:=u(p_{0})=1+\eta_{0}
is a unit in
k[X]
. Further,
u\alpha=u^{2}(fp_{0})=h(p_{0})=0
, so if we set
f:=p_{0}/\alpha
, we find that

u(f)=u(p_{0}/\alpha)=\alpha/\alpha=1,

and we are done.
∎

We are now ready to prove
3.1
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a non-zero element
u
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}
. Consider the right-invariant vector field
u_{R}
on
G
determined by
u
. By construction
u_{R}
is non-vanishing and
(u_{R})^{2}=(u^{2})_{R}
acts semisimply on
k[G]
. Then by
lemma

3.2
there exists a function
f\in k[G]
such that
uf=1
. Hence
k\cdot 1
does not split off
k[G]
, so
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
is not semisimple.
∎

4.
Proof II: a differential operator on
G/G_{0}

Consider the homogeneous
G
-space
G/G_{0}
. As a
G_{0}
-module, and as an algebra, we have an isomorphism

k[G/G_{0}]\cong\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{*}.

In particular
G/G_{0}
has one point as a space, and a finite-dimensional algebra of functions. As a
G
-module we have an identification

k[G/G_{0}]=\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{0}}^{G}k=\operatorname{Coind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}^{\mathfrak{g}}k.

Since
G_{0}
is reductive,
k[G/G_{0}]
is injective, and explicitly we may write

k[G/G_{0}]\cong\bigoplus\limits_{L}I(L)^{\oplus[\operatorname{Res}_{G_{0}}^{G}L:k]},

where
I(L)
denotes the indecomposable injective
G
-module with socle
L
, an irreducible
G
-module. In particular,
I(k)
appears with multiplicity one, so we may write

k[G/G_{0}]=I(k)\oplus M

for some
G
-module
M
. Now
\operatorname{Rep}G
is semisimple if and only if
I(k)=k=P(k)
. Thus assume from now on that
I(k)=P(k)
. In this case, there exists a operator
\phi\in\operatorname{End}(I(k))
which takes the head to the tail. Define
\Phi:=\phi\oplus\mathbf{0}_{M}\in\operatorname{End}(k[G/G_{0}])
. The following are straightforward to prove.

Lemma 4.1
.

(1)

\Phi\circ u=u\circ\Phi=0
for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
;

(2)

\Phi^{2}\neq 0
if and only if
I(k)=k=P(k)
if and only if
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
.

Since
k[G/G_{0}]
is finite-dimensional, we have that
\operatorname{End}(k[G/G_{0}])=D(G/G_{0})
, where
D(G/G_{0})
is the algebra of differential operators on
G/G_{0}
. Thus
\Phi
is a differential operator, and in fact is a
G
-equivariant differential operator by construction. Write
D^{G}(G/G_{0})
for the space of
G
-equivariant differential operators on
G/G_{0}
. Then we have the well known isomorphism

\operatorname{res}:D^{G}(G/G_{0})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Dist}(G/G_{0},eG_{0})^{G_{0}}\cong(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g})^{G_{0}}.

Here
\operatorname{Dist}(G/G_{0},eG_{0})
denotes the distributions on
G/G_{0}
supported at the only point, so in fact this is nothing but
k[G/G_{0}]^{*}
as a
G
-module.

Lemma 4.2
.

k\cdot\operatorname{res}(\Phi)=(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g})^{G}
.

Proof.

We have an identification

\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}^{\mathfrak{g}}k,

so
\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g})^{G}\leq 1
. On the other hand
lemma

4.1
implies that
\operatorname{res}(\Phi)\in(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})^{G}
, so we are done.
∎

We write
v:=\operatorname{res}(\Phi)
.

Remark 4.3
.

The element
v
is an example of a ghost distribution on the symmetric space
G/G_{0}
. Ghost distributions were defined by the author in
[
She21
]
for any supersymmetric space.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that
v
agrees (up to scalar) with the element
v_{\emptyset}
defined in section 3 of
[
Gor00
]
. This is not difficult to show given what we have proven thus far, and we refer
[
She21
]
for a full proof.

The next lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

\varepsilon(v)=\Phi(1)(eG_{0}),

where
\varepsilon
is the counit on
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
. Thus
\operatorname{Rep}G
is semisimple if and only if
\varepsilon(v)\neq 0
.

We now may prove
2.8
.

Proof.

Suppose that
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{ss}
is nonzero. Then
DS_{u}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}=0
because
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
is projective, being induced from
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. However
u\cdot v=0
by definition of
v
, so there exists
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
such that
v=u\cdot v^{\prime}=v^{\prime}u
. But then

\varepsilon(v)=\varepsilon(v^{\prime}u)=\varepsilon(v^{\prime})\varepsilon(u)=0.

∎

4.1.
Reductivity of
OSp(1|2n)

All that remains is to prove that
\operatorname{Rep}OSp(1|2n)
is semisimple. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
. By our work above, it suffices to prove that for a non-zero element
v\in(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
we have
\varepsilon(v)\neq 0
. To show this we only need to construct
v
and check that
\varepsilon(v)\neq 0
.

However this has already been done in
[
DH76
]
. There, they prove that the element

v=(1+t_{1})(3+t_{2})\cdots((2n-1)+t_{n})

is
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant, where
t_{i}=a_{i}b_{i}
, and
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
are a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
such that
(a_{i},b_{j})=\delta_{ij}
, where
(-,-)
is
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2n)
-invariant form on
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
as discussed in
section

2.3
. Note that in
[
DH76
]
they actually consider the
\mathfrak{g}
-invariants of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. To go between their case and ours we only need apply the antipode on
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
.

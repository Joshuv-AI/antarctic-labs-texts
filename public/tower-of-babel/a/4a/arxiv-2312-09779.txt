Convex ordering of solutions to one-dimensional SDEs
Benjamin Jourdain, Gilles Pagès

Abstract
In this paper, we are interested in the propagation of convexity by the strong solution to a one-dimensional Brownian stochastic differential equation with coefficients Lipschitz in the spatial variable uniformly in the time variable and in the convex ordering between the solutions of two such equations. We prove that while these properties hold without further assumptions for convex functions of the processes at one instant only, an assumption almost amounting to spatial convexity of the diffusion coefficient is needed for the extension to convex functions at two instants. Under this spatial convexity of the diffusion coefficients, the two properties even hold for convex functionals of the whole path. For directionally convex functionals, the spatial convexity of the diffusion coefficient is no longer needed. Our method of proof consists in first establishing the results for time discretization schemes of Euler type and then transferring them to their limiting Brownian diffusions. We thus exhibit approximations which avoid convexity arbitrages by preserving convexity propagation and comparison and can be computed by Monte Carlo simulation.

Introduction

In Section

1

This statement assumes convexity of at least one of the two diffusion coefficients in the spatial variable. The objective of this paper is to generalize this theorem essentially in two directions. The first one is to analyze the role played in dimension one by the convexity assumption made on the diffusion coefficient in the propagation of convexity and subsequently in the comparison property. The second one is to extend the class of functionals for which such type of propagation/comparison can be established (see further on).

Our first objective is motivated by a result from

To elucidate this paradox about the spatial convexity assumption needed on the diffusion coefficient, we were led to introduce the notion of

On our way through the paper, we always provide results for both “regular” and increasing convex propagation and ordering,

Our method of proof for all these results always relies on the same paradigm: we first establish our results for time discretization schemes of Euler type and then transfer them using appropriate convergence theorems of these schemes to their respective Brownian diffusions. Like in

convexity arbitrages

Also note that the results obtained in discrete time have their own interest for applications.

2
Functional convex ordering of one-dimensional diffusions

Before being more specific on the state of the art functional convex ordering for one dimensional Brownian diffusions, let us briefly recall some definitions about convex ordering of random vectors (in view of the proofs) and stochastic processes (which are main objects of interest in our results).

Definition 2.1
(Convex orderings)

Let
U
,
V\!\in L^{1}({\mathbb{P}})
be two
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
-valued random vectors and let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be pathwise continuous real-valued stochastic processes such that
{\mathbb{E}}\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|
and
{\mathbb{E}}\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}|<+\infty
.

(a)
Convex ordering.
We say that
U
is dominated for the convex ordering by
V
, denoted

U\preceq_{cvx}V,

if, for every convex function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\,f(U)\leq{\mathbb{E}}\,f(V)\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}.

(2.1)

(b)
Monotone convex ordering. We say that
U
is dominated for the non-decreasing convex ordering by
V
, denoted

U\preceq_{icv}V,

if (
2.1
) holds for every convex function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
non-decreasing in each of its variables.

(c)
Functional convex ordering. We say that
X
is dominated by
Y
for the functional convex ordering, denoted

X\preceq_{cvx}Y

if for every lower semi-continuous (l.s.c.) convex functional
F:{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}

{\mathbb{E}}\,F(X)\leq{\mathbb{E}}\,F(Y)\!\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}.

(2.2)

(d)
Functional monotone convex ordering.We say that
X
is dominated by
Y
(defined as above) for the increasing functional convex ordering, denoted

X\preceq_{icvx}Y,

if (
2.2
) holds true for every l.s.c. convex functional
F:{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
, non-decreasing for the natural partial order on
{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})
(i.e. for every
(x,y)\!\in{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\times{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,
F(x)\leq F(x+y)
).

Obviously,
U\preceq_{cvx}V\Longrightarrow U\preceq_{icvx}V
and
X\preceq_{cvx}Y\Longrightarrow X\preceq_{icvx}Y
.

These definitions only involve the distributions of
U
,
V
and
X
,
Y
respectively. So we could have defined equivalently these orderings on the set of
{\cal P}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and
{\cal P}_{1}\big(C([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\big)
of distributions with finite first moment.

Note that the expectations involved in the definitions make sense in
{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}
since any convex function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
(resp. l.s.c. convex functional
F:{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
) is bounded from below by an affine function (resp. by a continuous affine functional according to Lemma 7.5
[
1
]
). The next proposition which can be proved by inf-convolution arguments (see the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 1.9]
) states a characterization of convex ordering(s) based on the sole
Lipschitz
convex functions/functionals.

Proposition 2.2
(Convex ordering characterization)

(a)
For every (non-decreasing in each of its variables) convex function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists a non-decreasing sequence of (non-decreasing in each of their variables) Lipschitz continuous convex functions having
f
as a pointwise limit.

(b)
To establish (increasing) convex ordering it suffices to establish (
2.1
) for (non-decreasing in each of their variables) Lipschitz continuous convex functions
f
.

(c)

For every (non-decreasing) l.s.c. convex functional
F:{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
the exists a non-decreasing sequence of (non-decreasing) Lipschitz continuous convex functionals having
F
as a pointwise limit.

(d)
To establish (increasing) functional convex ordering it suffices to establish (
2.2
) for (non-decreasing) Lipschitz continuous convex functionals
F
.

(2.3)

and

,

3
Main results

In order to state our main results, let us first introduce the
m
-marginal convex ordering between two stochastic processes.

Definition 3.1
(
m
-marginal convex ordering)

Let
m\!\in{\mathbb{N}}
. A real-valued stochastic process
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that
\forall t\in[0,T],\;{\mathbb{E}}\,|X_{t}|<\infty
is dominated by a real-valued process
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that
\forall t\in[0,T],\;{\mathbb{E}}\,|Y_{t}|<\infty
for the
m
-marginal convex ordering if, for every
t_{1},\,t_{2},\cdots,\,t_{m}\!\in[0,T]
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m}
,

(X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{m}})\preceq_{cvx}(Y_{t_{1}},\ldots,Y_{t_{m}}).

3.1
1
-marginal convex ordering and convexity propagation without convexity of the diffusion coefficient

For
1
-marginal convex ordering and convexity propagation, no spatial convexity property of the diffusion coefficients is needed.

Theorem 3.2

[1-marginal convex orderings without convexity of the diffusion coefficients] Assume that the functions
b
,
\beta
,
\sigma
,
\vartheta:{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy (

) and that
X_{0},Y_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{P}})
.

(a)
Non-decreasing convex ordering. Assume that either
\forall\,t\!\in[0,T]
,

is convex or
\forall\,t\!\in[0,T]
,

is convex. We then have

\Big(b\leq\beta\mbox{ and }0\leq\sigma\leq\vartheta\mbox{ on }[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\mbox{ and }X_{0}\preceq_{icv}Y_{0}\Big)\Longrightarrow X_{{}_{T}}\preceq_{icv}Y_{{}_{T}}.

Moreover, when

,

is convex (resp.
\forall\,t\!\in[0,T]
,
\beta(t,\cdot)
is convex), then for every non-decreasing convex function

(b)
Convex ordering. Let
b(t,x)=\beta(t,x)=\lambda(t)+\mu(t)x
,
(t,x)\!\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}
be a time dependent affine function in space where
\lambda
and
\mu
are bounded Borel functions.
Then,

\Big(0\leq\sigma\leq\vartheta\mbox{ on }[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\mbox{ and }X_{0}\preceq_{cvx}Y_{0}\Big)\Longrightarrow X_{{}_{T}}\preceq_{cvx}Y_{{}_{T}}.

Moreover, for every convex function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

x\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{x}_{T})\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}\left(\mbox{resp. }y\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}\,f(Y^{y}_{T})\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}\;\mbox{ is convex.}

Remarks and comments.

\bullet

From convex ordering to propagation of convexity and back.
The (non-decreasing) convexity of
x\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{x}_{T})
for
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
(non-decreasing) convex and the (increasing) convex ordering are closely intertwined. For the choice
(\beta,\vartheta)=(b,\sigma)
and
X_{0}\sim\delta_{\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y}\leq_{cvx}Y_{0}=\varepsilon\delta_{x}+(1-\varepsilon)\delta_{y}
where

,

and

{\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y}_{T})\leq\alpha{\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{x}_{T})+(1-\alpha){\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{y}_{T})
for
f:{\mathbb{E}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
(non-decreasing) convex while for the choice
X_{0}=x\leq y=Y_{0}
so that
X_{0}\leq_{icv}Y_{0}
, the increasing convex ordering implies that
{\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{x}_{T})\leq{\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{y}_{T})
for
f:{\mathbb{E}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
non-decreasing and convex.
Conversely, we will rely on Euler discretizations of the SDEs to prove Theorem

and at the level of these Euler schemes, the propagation of convexity turns out to be a key step to prove the convex ordering (see the proof of Proposition

below).

\bullet

\bullet

]
a direct proof of the convexity of

for

Lipschitz convex using stochastic calculus when

3.2
A necessary condition for

Proposition 3.3
(

(a)
Assume that

3.3
Ordering and propagation for directionally convex functionals

Prior to the main result of this section (Theorem
3.10
), we first recall the definitions of directional convexity in both vector and functional settings and their first properties illustrated by some examples. In particular, we stress the fact that directional convexity is not a restriction of the notion of convexity but a different class of functions/functionals (except in one dimension) with an overlap but none being more general than the other. We refer to Section 3.12 of
[
17
]
where this notion is introduced and investigated for more details. As a second step, we define the resulting stochastic order, first for random vectors and then for stochastic processes. Finally we state our main result.

Definition 3.4
(Directional convexity)

(a)
A function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be directionally convex if it is Borel measurable and

\forall(x,y,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m},\quad f(x+y+z)-f(x+y)-f(x+z)+f(x)\geq 0.

(3.11)

(b)
A functional
F:{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be directionally convex if it is measurable and

\forall(x,y,z)\in{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\times{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}}_{+})^{2},\quad F(x+y+z)-F(x+y)-F(x+z)+F(x)\geq 0.

(3.12)

Remark 3.5
(Characterisations)

\mathbf{P1.}
A directionally convex function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is measurable and mid-point convex in each of its variables (the other variables being fixed) whence, according to
[
24
]
, convex in each of its variables. This implies that for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, the right-hand derivative
\partial_{x_{i}+}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{m})
is non-decreasing in each of its variables and therefore locally bounded. As a consequence, the function
f
is continuous.

\mathbf{P2.}
A twice continuously differentiable function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is directionally convex if and only if for all
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
,
\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}f\geq 0
.

\mathbf{P3.}
When
d=1
, directional convexity amounts to regular convexity.

\mathbf{P4.}
When
g:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is convex, then for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
,
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\ni(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\mapsto g(x_{i})
is directionally convex.

\mathbf{P5.}
A twice continuously Fréchet differentiable functional
F:{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is directionally convex if and only if, for every
x\!\in{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})
and every
u,\,v\!\in{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

D^{2}F(x).(u,v)\geq 0.

The following examples illustrate that the notions of convexity and directional convexity have overlap but that none of the two is more general than the other.

Example 3.6

(a)
The funtion

defined by

is (clearly) convex but not directionally convex. Indeed, if we set

(b)
Let us consider the quadratic function

One checks that

—

(c)

(d)

The following lemma is the counterpart for directionally convex functions of the properties of monotone approximation of convex functions by Lipschitz convex ones stated in Proposition

Lemma 3.7

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be directionally convex (non-decreasing in each of its variables). Then there exists a non-decreasing sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of Lipschitz continuous directionally convex (non-decreasing in each variable) functions having

m

is integrable and
{\mathbb{E}}\,f(U)
makes sense in

Proof.

The directionally convex function

\partial_{i-}f(u_{1},\cdots,u_{i},\cdots,u_{m})
and a right-hand derivative
\partial_{i+}f(u_{1},\cdots,u_{i},\cdots,u_{m})
with respect to its
i

i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
. The directional convexity implies that
\partial_{i-}f
and
\partial_{i+}f
are non-decreasing in each of their variables and therefore locally bounded. As a consequence

is locally Lipschitz continuous. The construction in
[
17

then provides a non-decreasing sequence of Lipschitz continuous directionally convex functions

that converge pointwise to

\displaystyle f_{n}(u_{1},\cdots

\displaystyle,u_{m})=f(\psi_{n}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{i}\leq-n\}}\partial_{i-}f(\psi_{n}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m}))(u_{i}+n)+\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{i}\geq n\}}\partial_{i+}f(\psi_{n}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m}))(u_{i}-n)\right),

. When

When

is integrable. Since

\Box

4
Convergence and ordering of Euler schemes with truncated noise

Our proofs rely on the same “transfer” paradigm: prove the results in discrete time on discretization schemes of Euler type and then transfer them by a strong (or functional weak ) convergence theorem toward the diffusion. We will extensively use for the questions under consideration in this paper some specific truncated Euler schemes, especially when dealing with monotone convex ordering and propagation. We always consider a taylored Euler scheme for which the lightest assumptions are needed but, when it turns out not to be simulable, we also give a result for a simulable Euler scheme under slightly more stringent assumptions.

It turns out that we will need to truncate the Brownian increments in the Euler scheme to ensure propagation of convexity by the transitions associated with this scheme. That is why, in the next section, we address the convergence of such Euler schemes with truncated noise.

4.1

are bounded on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
On the other hand, condition
(iii)
implies that
\sigma^{2}(t,\cdot)
has a Lipschitz derivative and hence is semi-convex.

Proof.
Since directional and regular convexity coincide for a function from
{\mathbb{R}}
to
{\mathbb{R}}
, it is sufficient to prove
(a)
. By Lemma
3.7
, it is enough to deal with functions
f
which are also Lipschitz continuous. By spatial convolution with
n^{m}\rho(nx)
where
\rho
is a compactly supported
C^{\infty}
probability density on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, such a function may be approximated uniformly by functions which are moreover
C^{\infty}
and inherit its directional convexity and monotonicity properties. Therefore, we may even suppose that
f
is
C^{\infty}
with bounded first order derivatives.

By
[
12
, Theorem 4.6.5]
and its proof, there exists a modification of the solution to the stochastic differential equation (
2.3
) such that for
t\in[0,T]
,
x\mapsto X^{x}_{t}
is a diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}
with derivative
\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t}
solving the equation obtained by spatial derivation of (
2.3
) :

d\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t}=\partial_{2}b(t,X^{x}_{t})\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t}+\partial_{2}\sigma(t,X^{x}_{t})\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t}dW_{t},\;\partial_{x}X^{x}_{0}=1,

so that

\forall t\in[0,T],\;\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{2}b(s,X^{x}_{s})ds}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{2}\sigma(s,X^{x})dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{2}\sigma(s,X^{x}_{s}))^{2}ds}.

As a consequence
\partial_{x}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\partial_{k}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t_{k}}
. The boundedness of the first order derivatives of
f
and of
\partial_{2}b
,
\partial_{2}\sigma
implies that
\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\partial_{k}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t_{k}}\right)^{2}\right]<+\infty
so that we may interchange the derivative and the expectation and obtain

\partial_{x}{\mathbb{E}}\,f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\partial_{k}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})\partial_{x}X^{x}_{t_{k}}\Big).

By boundedness of
\partial_{2}\sigma
,
{\mathbb{E}}\,e^{\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{2}\sigma(s,X^{x})dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{2}\sigma(s,X^{x}_{s}))^{2}ds}=1
for each
t\in[0,T]
. By Girsanov’s theorem, under the probability measure
{\mathbb{Q}}_{x,k}=e^{\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\partial_{2}\sigma(s,X^{x})dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{k}}(\partial_{2}\sigma(s,X^{x}_{s}))^{2}ds}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}
,
\left(B^{x,k}_{t}=W_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{s\leq t_{k}\}}\partial_{2}\sigma(s,X^{x}_{s})ds\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a Brownian motion and
X^{x,k}
is solution to the SDE

X^{x,k}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}(b+{\mathbf{1}}_{\{s\leq t_{k}\}}\sigma\partial_{2}\sigma)(s,X^{x,k}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X^{x,k}_{s})dB^{x,k}_{s}.

Since
b,\sigma
and
b+\sigma\partial_{2}\sigma
are Lipschitz continuous in space uniformly in time,

{\cal L}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{x,k}}(X^{x,k})={\cal L}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(\tilde{X}^{x,k})
where
\tilde{X}^{x,k}
follows the same SDE as
X^{x,k}
but with the Brownian motion
B^{x,k}
replaced by
W
:

\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}(b+{\mathbf{1}}_{\{s\leq t_{k}\}}\sigma\partial_{2}\sigma)(s,\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{s})dW_{s}.

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{x}{\mathbb{E}}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{x,k}}\Big(\partial_{k}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\partial_{2}b(s,X^{x}_{s})ds}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\partial_{k}f(\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{t_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{t_{m}})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\partial_{2}b(s,\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{s})ds}\Big).

At this stage, since
b,\sigma
and
b+\sigma\partial_{2}\sigma
are Lipschitz continuous in space uniformly in time, by
[
21
, Theorem 3.7 p.394]
,
x\mapsto\tilde{X}^{x}
is (a.s.) pathwise non-decreasing. For each
k\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, since
\partial_{k\ell}f\geq 0
for all
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
by Property
\mathbf{P2}
in Remark
3.5
,
x\mapsto\partial_{k}f(\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{t_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{X}^{x,k}_{t_{m}})
is non-decreasing as well. When
b(t,\cdot)
is affine and therefore
\partial_{2}b(t,\cdot)
constant for each
t\in[0,T]
, we deduce that
x\mapsto\partial_{x}{\mathbb{E}}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})
is non-decreasing and therefore
x\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})
is convex. The conclusion remains valid when
b(t,\cdot)
is convex and therefore
\partial_{2}b(t,\cdot)
non-decreasing for each
t\!\in[0,T]
and
f
is non-decreasing in each of its variables so that
\partial_{k}f\geq 0
for each
k\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
. Finally, the pathwise non-decreasing property of
x\mapsto X^{x}
deduced from
[
21
, Theorem 3.7 p.394]
implies that
x\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}f(X^{x}_{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{x}_{t_{m}})
is non-decreasing when
f
is non decreasing in each of its variables.
\Box

Appendix B
Proof of Example
3.6
(d)

The convexity of
\varphi
implies that
{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})\ni x\mapsto\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s))ds
is convex and the first statement follows from the convexity of the composition of a non-decreasing convex function with a convex function.
To prove the second statement, let
x\in{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})
and
y,z\in{\cal C}([0,T],{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
. For
s\in[0,T]
, we have
x(s)+y(s),x(s)+z(s)\in[x(s),x(s)+y(s)+z(s)]
so that
\varphi(x(s)+y(s)),\varphi(x(s)+z(s))\in[\varphi(x(s)),\varphi(x(s)+y(s)+z(s))]
by monotonicity of
\varphi
and

\varphi(x(s)+y(s))+\varphi(x(s)+z(s))\leq\varphi(x(s))+\varphi(x(s)+y(s)+z(s))
by convexity of this function. As a consequence,
\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s)+y(s))ds,\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s)+z(s))ds\in\left[\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s))ds,\int_{0}^{T}(\varphi(x(s)+y(s))+\varphi(x(s)+z(s))-\varphi(x(s)))\right]
and

\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s)+y(s))ds+\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s)+z(s))ds-\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s))ds\leq\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x(s)+y(s)+z(s))ds.

With the convexity of
\Psi
for the first inequality then its monotonicity for the second, we conclude that

\displaystyle F(x+y)+F(x+z)

\displaystyle\leq F(x)+\Psi\left(\int_{0}^{T}(\varphi(x(s)+y(s))+\varphi(x(s)+z(s))-\varphi(x(s)))ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq F(x)+F(x+y+z).

Owen’s
T

\Abstract
The Owen’s
T
function is presented in four new ways, one of them as a series similar to the Euler’s arctangent series divided by
2\pi
, which is its majorant series. All possibilities enable numerically stable and fast convergent computation of the bivariate normal integral with simple recursion. When tested
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
computation on a random sample of one million parameter triplets with uniformly distributed components and using double precision arithmetic, the maximum absolute error was
3.45\cdot 10^{-16}
. In additional testing, focusing on cases with correlation coefficients close to one in absolute value, when the computation may be very sensitive to small rounding errors, the accuracy was retained. In rare potentially critical cases, a simple adjustment to the computation procedure was performed – one potentially critical computation was replaced with two equivalent non-critical ones. All new series are suitable for vector and high-precision computation, assuming they are supplemented with appropriate efficient and accurate computation of the arctangent and standard normal cumulative distribution functions. They are implemented by the
\proglang
R package
\pkg
Phi2rho, available on CRAN. Its functions allow vector arguments and are ready to work with the
\pkg
Rmpfr package, which enables the use of arbitrary precision instead of double precision numbers. A special test with up to
1024
-bit precision computation is also presented.

Introduction

Let
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t
be the standard normal cumulative distribution function (cdf) and
\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\;e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}
the standard normal probability density function (pdf). The bivariate standard normal cdf

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}\;\int_{-\infty}^{x}\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{-\frac{s^{2}-2\varrho st+t^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho^{2})}}\,\mathrm{d}s\;\mathrm{d}t

(1)

with a correlation coefficient
\varrho\in(-1,1)
has the bivariate standard normal pdf

\varphi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\tfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)}{\partial x\,\partial y}=\tfrac{1}{2\pi\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}\;e^{-\frac{x^{2}-2\varrho xy+y^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho^{2})}}.

(2)

If
\varrho=0
, then
\Phi_{0}^{2}(x,y)=\Phi(x)\,\Phi(y)
, while for
\lvert\varrho\rvert=1
we have the degenerated cases

\Phi_{\pm 1}^{2}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\min\{\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\},&\mbox{if }\varrho=+1,\\
\max\{\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)-1,0\},&\mbox{if }\varrho=-1.\end{cases}

(3)

Additionally, for
\varrho\in(-1,1)
define

L(x,y,\varrho)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}\,\int_{x}^{\infty}\int_{y}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{s^{2}-2\varrho st+t^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho^{2})}}\,\mathrm{d}s\;\mathrm{d}t,

L(x,y,-1)=\max\{1-\Phi(x)-\Phi(y),0\}
and
L(x,y,1)=1-\max\{\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\}
. The functions
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
and
L(x,y,\varrho)
are symmetric regarding
x
and
y
, and because they are complementary, it suffices to analyze only one of them. In this respect,
L
is often preferred, but not in this paper where some equations for
L
are also rewritten using
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=L(-x,-y,\varrho)
and some other auxiliary equations.

Noting that (
3
) implies
\Phi_{1}^{2}(x,x)=\Phi(x)=2\,\Phi_{0}^{2}(x,0)
and
\Phi_{-1}^{2}(x,-x)=0=\Phi_{0}^{2}(x,0)+\Phi_{0}^{2}(-x,0)-\frac{1}{2}
, the equations from
(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1972
, p. 937, 26.3.19 and 26.3.20)
, which do not cover these cases if
x\neq 0
, as we get an indefinite expression
\frac{0}{0}
for the auxiliary correlation coefficients
\varrho_{x}
and
\varrho_{y}
, can be transformed into

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\arcsin\varrho,&\mbox{if }x=y=0,\\
\Phi_{\varrho_{x}}^{2}(x,0)+\Phi_{\varrho_{y}}^{2}(y,0)-\beta,&\mbox{if }x\neq 0\mbox{ or }y\neq 0,\end{cases}

(4)

where
\beta=0
if
xy>0
or
xy=0
and
x+y\geq 0
, and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
otherwise,

\varrho_{x}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}0&\mbox{if }\lvert\varrho\rvert=1\mbox{ and }x\neq 0\mbox{ and }x-y\,\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)=0&0\\
\tfrac{(\varrho x-y)\,\mathrm{sgn}(x)}{\sqrt{x^{2}-2\varrho xy+y^{2}}}&\mbox{otherwise}&\tfrac{(\varrho y-x)\,\mathrm{sgn}(y)}{\sqrt{x^{2}-2\varrho xy+y^{2}}}\end{array}\right\}=\varrho_{y},

(5)

where
\mathrm{sgn}(x)=1
, if
x\geq 0
and
\mathrm{sgn}(x)=-1
, if
x<0
. Note that although
\lvert\varrho\rvert
is not close to
1
,
\lvert\varrho_{x}\rvert
or
\lvert\varrho_{y}\rvert
can be, and that
\lvert\varrho\rvert=1
and the lower equations (
5
) imply
\lvert\varrho_{x}\rvert=1
and
\lvert\varrho_{y}\rvert=1
.

Since
(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1972
, p. 936, 26.3.9 and 26.3.8)
imply

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(-x,0)=\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,0)-\Phi(x)+\tfrac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{-\varrho}^{2}(x,0)=\Phi(x)-\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,0),

(6)

and
\Phi_{-\varrho}^{2}(-x,0)=\frac{1}{2}-\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,0)
, the problem how to compute
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
is reduced to the computation of
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,0)
for
x\geq 0
and
\varrho\in[0,1)
.

Extensive older bibliography exists about how
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
can be computed
(
Gupta 1963
, see)
. Many old methods, e.g.
Donnelly 1973
’s (
Donnelly 1973
) method based on
Owen 1956
’s (
Owen 1956
) results,
Divgi 1979
’s (
Divgi 1979
) method and the method of
Drezner and Wesolowsky 1990
, are sufficiently effective and accurate for the majority of present needs, but new methods still appear, e.g.
(
Patefield and Tandy 2000
;
Genz 2004
;
Meyer 2013
;
Fayed and Atiya 2014b
)
. More details and comparisons of some methods can be found in
(
Terza and Welland 1991
;
Gai 2001
;
Aǧca and Chance 2003
)
.

The Owen’s
T
function is defined by
(
Owen 1956
, pp. 1078–1079)

\begin{split}T(h,a)&=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{a}\tfrac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}(1+x^{2})}}{1+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\tfrac{\arctan a}{2\pi}-\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{(-1)^{k}a^{2k+1}}{2k+1}\left(1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\tfrac{h^{2i}}{2^{i}i!}\right)\end{split}

(7)

for
a,h\in\mathbb{R}
. It appears frequently in the extensive Owen’s table of integrals involving Gaussian functions
(
Owen 1980
, pp. 393–413)
and can be used for computing
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
, as will be explained in the next section. The series (
7
) is used in
Donnelly 1973
’s (
Donnelly 1973
) algorithm and, based on it, is also realized in
\code
pbinorm() function from the
\pkg
VGAM package
(
Yee 2023
)
. Since it alternates, there are problems with numerical stability, e.g. using double precision arithmetic, it may provide only single precision results. More about Owen’s
T
function and older ways of calculating it are given by
Patefield and Tandy 2000
, who developed a hybrid method to compute it. To achieve double precision accuracy, they use the series (
7
) or one of five other methods. Which method is chosen and which its parameters are used depends on which of the
18
regions
(h,a)
belongs to
(see
Patefield and Tandy 2000
, p. 7, Fig. 2)
. Their method is also implemented in
\code
OwenT() function from the
\pkg
OwenQ package
(
Laurent 2023
)
, hereafter
\code
OwenQ::OwenT().

The method presented in this paper extends the theoretical knowledge and can be easily implemented. Its great advantage over the already mentioned methods, which enable
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
computation via Owen’s
T
function, is its simplicity, faster convergence and ensured numerical stability. Regarding other methods that have proven to be good or even excellent in terms of
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
computation speed and accuracy, e.g. the one implemented in the
\code
pmvnorm() function from the
\pkg
mvtnorm package
(
Genz and Bretz 2009
)
, the main advantages are simplicity and independence from various constants. Both enable easy portability between different environments and using high-precision arithmetic. There is no need to adjust the number and precision of various constants, such as weights and knots in quadrature formulas, which are used in many
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
computational methods.

In Section
2
, the background results are presented, which are the starting point for the development of a new method, as well as for the acceleration of the tetrachoric series, which was used for the benchmark values computation. In Section
3
, auxiliary results are derived. In core Section
4
, new series for
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,0)
are derived in Theorems
3
and
4
, and in Corollary
2
they are combined in a unified series for
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
. However, in all cases, the series must be supplemented by the standard normal cdf and some of them also by the arctangent function computation. Four series for computing the Owen’s
T
function are presented in Theorem
5
. Among them (
40b
) can be viewed as a modified Euler’s arctangent series. In Sections
5
and
6
, algorithmic and numeric issues are discussed, and testing results are presented.

Using double precision arithmetic and testing on a random sample of one million triplets
(x,y,\varrho)
with uniformly distributed components, the maximum absolute error was
3.45\cdot 10^{-16}
, which is approximately
3.11
-times the machine epsilon
2^{-53}
. On the same random sample, but with
\varrho
transformed so that more than half of
\lvert\varrho\rvert
were greater than
0.9999
, and by equivalent computing of two function values instead of
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
in rare cases with
\varphi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)>1
, the maximum absolute error was even slightly smaller.

All new series are suitable for vector and high-precision computation. The fastest one asymptotically converges as
\sum_{k}\frac{q^{k+1}e^{-q}}{(k+1)!}\,\frac{\min\{a^{2k},1\}}{\sqrt{k+1}\;(1+a^{2})^{k}}
, where
q=\frac{1}{2}(1+a^{2})h^{2}
. Using the
\pkg
Rmpfr package
(
Maechler 2023
)
and
1024
-bit precision computation,
199
iterations were needed to compute
\Phi_{\sqrt{2}/2}^{2}(2.1,0)
, achieving an absolute error smaller than
2^{-1024}\approx 5.56\cdot 10^{-309}
.

2
Background results

The bivariate standard normal cdf can be expanded into tetrachoric series
Kendall 1941
, p. 196
,
Abramowitz and Stegun 1972
, p. 940, 26.3.29

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\Phi(x)\,\Phi(y)+\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H\!e_{k}(x)\,H\!e_{k}(y)\,\tfrac{\varrho^{k+1}}{(k+1)!},

(8)

where
H\!e_{k}(x)
are the “probabilist’s” Hermite polynomials defined by

H\!e_{k}(x)=(-1)^{k}\,e^{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}\,\tfrac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}x^{k}}\;e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

The tetrachoric series (
8
) converges a bit faster than a geometric series with a quotient
\varrho
and slowly converges for
\lvert\varrho\rvert
close to or equal to
1
.

Let
\mathbb{N}_{-1}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{-1,0\}
. Extending the usual double factorial function definition for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
to
k\in\mathbb{N}_{-1}
with
(-1)!!=1
, we have

H\!e_{k}(0)=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if $k$ is odd},\\
(-1)^{\frac{k}{2}}(k-1)!!,&\mbox{if $k$ is even}.\end{cases}

Setting
y=0
in (
8
), transforming the summation index
\frac{k}{2}\mapsto k
, and using
(2k-1)!!=\frac{(2k)!}{2^{k}k!}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we get a slightly faster converging series

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,0)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Phi(x)+\tfrac{\varphi(x)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H\!e_{2k}(x)\,\tfrac{(-1)^{k}\varrho^{2k+1}}{2^{k}k!\,(2k+1)}.

(9)

Vasicek 1998
found an alternative series for the computation of
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,0)
, which is a good alternative to the series (
9
) if
\varrho^{2}>\frac{1}{2}
.

Integrating the well known equation
(
Plackett 1954
, p. 352)

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\,\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\tfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)}{\partial x\,\partial y}=\varphi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)

(10)

with respect to
\varrho
, using equations
\Phi_{0}^{2}(x,y)=\Phi(x)\,\Phi(y)
and (
3
), we get

\begin{split}\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)&=\Phi(x)\,\Phi(y)+\int_{0}^{\varrho}\varphi_{s}^{2}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\min\{\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\}-\int_{\varrho}^{1}\varphi_{s}^{2}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\max\{\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)-1,0\}+\int_{-1}^{\varrho}\varphi_{s}^{2}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(11)

Due to historical reasons, the variates
h
and
k
are often used instead of
x
and
y
. We will use
h
instead of
x
but will not need
k
in the role of
y
.

In the sequel, let
\varrho\in(-1,1)
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
be permanently connected by the one-to-one correspondence
r=\frac{\varrho}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}
and
\varrho=\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}
. Notice that (
4
) and
\arcsin\varrho=\arctan r
imply
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(0,0)=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\arctan r
. Setting
x=h
,
y=0
, and writing
e^{-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}}\int_{a}^{b}e^{\frac{1}{2}h^{2}}\varphi_{s}^{2}(h,0)\,\mathrm{d}s
instead of
\int_{a}^{b}\varphi_{s}^{2}(h,0)\,\mathrm{d}s
, the equations (
11
) can be rewritten to

\begin{split}\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)&=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Phi(h)+\varphi(h)\,T_{r}(-\tfrac{1}{2}h^{2})\\
&=\min\{\Phi(h),\tfrac{1}{2}\}-\varphi(h)\,\widetilde{T}_{r}(-\tfrac{1}{2}h^{2})\\
&=\max\{\Phi(h)-\tfrac{1}{2},0\}+\varphi(h)\,\overline{T}_{r}(-\tfrac{1}{2}h^{2}),\end{split}

(12)

where, using a substitution
s=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}
,

T_{r}(w)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\varrho}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\;e^{\frac{ws^{2}}{1-s^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{r}\tfrac{e^{wx^{2}}}{1+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad(w\in\mathbb{R}).

Analogously,
\widetilde{T}_{r}(w)
and
\overline{T}_{r}(w)
are defined for
w\leq 0
by integrals with limits
(\varrho,1)
and
(-1,\varrho)
, and
(r,\infty)
and
(-\infty,r)
respectively, which do not exist for
w>0
. The computation of
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)
is reduced to the computation of one of the integrals
T_{r}(-\frac{1}{2}h^{2})
,
\widetilde{T}_{r}(-\frac{1}{2}h^{2})
and
\overline{T}_{r}(-\frac{1}{2}h^{2})
.

The special cases (
12
) of the equations (
11
) in combination with (
4
) are a starting point for some computation methods for
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
which are based on the integration. Owen’s is one of them – note that
\varphi(h)\,T_{a}(-\frac{1}{2}h^{2})=T(h,a)
. Using
r
instead of
a
in the sequel and resolving the problem with
\frac{0}{0}
as before, Owen’s equation
(
Owen 1980
, p. 416, (3.1))
can be rewritten as

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\arcsin\varrho,&\mbox{if }x=y=0,\\
\tfrac{1}{2}(\Phi(x)+\Phi(y))-T(x,r_{x})-T(y,r_{y})-\beta,&\mbox{if }x\neq 0\mbox{ or }y\neq 0,\end{cases}

(13)

where
\beta=0
if
xy>0
or
xy=0
and
x+y\geq 0
, and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
otherwise, and

r_{x}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}0&\mbox{if }\lvert\varrho\rvert=1\mbox{ and }x\neq 0\mbox{ and }x-y\,\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)=0&0\\
\tfrac{y-\varrho x}{x\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}&\mbox{otherwise}&\frac{x-\varrho y}{y\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}\end{array}\right\}=r_{y}.

(14)

Another Owen’s important equation is
(
Owen 1980
, p. 414, 2.7)

T(h,r)+T(rh,\tfrac{1}{r})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\Phi(h)+\Phi(rh))-\Phi(h)\,\Phi(rh)-\beta,

(15)

where
\beta=0
if
r\geq 0
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
if
r<0
. It implies
\varphi(h)\,T_{1}(-\frac{1}{2}h^{2})=T(h,1)=\frac{1}{2}\Phi(h)\,(1-\Phi(h))
.
If
\varrho=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
, then
r=1
. Using (
12
) and the right equation (
6
), we get

\Phi_{\sqrt{2}/2}^{2}(h,0)=\Phi(h)(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Phi(h))\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{-\sqrt{2}/2}^{2}(h,0)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\Phi(h))^{2}.

(16)

Let
\varrho\in(-1,1)
,
\overline{\varrho}=\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}
,

r=\frac{\varrho}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}
,
\overline{r}=\frac{\overline{\varrho}}{\sqrt{1-\overline{\varrho}^{2}}}=\frac{1}{r}
and
h\in\mathbb{R}
. By adding
\frac{1}{2}(\Phi(h)+\Phi(rh))
on both sides of (
15
) and using (
7
) and (
12
), it follows

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)+\,\Phi_{\overline{\varrho}}^{2}(rh,0)=\Phi(h)+\Phi(rh)-\Phi(h)\,\Phi(rh)-\beta,

(17)

where
\beta=0
if
r\geq 0
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
if
r<0
.

If
\lvert\varrho\rvert>\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
, then
\lvert r\rvert>1
,
\lvert\overline{r}\rvert<1
and
\lvert\overline{\varrho}\rvert<\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
.
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)
can be computed by computing
\Phi_{\overline{\varrho}}^{2}(rh,0)
and using (
17
). Consequently, the right equation (
6
) and the transformation of the parameters
rh\mapsto\overline{h}
and
\frac{1}{r}\mapsto\overline{r}
, if needed, reduce the condition
\varrho\in(-1,1)
to
\varrho\in[0,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}]
with a corresponding
r\in[0,1]
. The problem how to compute
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
is reduced to the computation of
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)
for
h\geq 0
and
\varrho\in[0,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}]
.

The theorems in the sequel do not depend on the restriction to
\varrho^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
with a corresponding
\lvert r\rvert\leq 1
found in
(
Patefield and Tandy 2000
)
. However, if
\lvert r\rvert>1
, the transformation of
rh\mapsto\overline{h}
and
\frac{1}{r}\mapsto\overline{r}
speeds up convergence significantly. This is, for example, also the case for the Vasicek’s series and the tetrachoric series (
9
), which transforms into

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)=\Phi(h)+\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi(rh)-\Phi(h)\Phi(rh)-\tfrac{\varphi(rh)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H\!e_{2k}(rh)\,\tfrac{(-1)^{k}\overline{\varrho}^{2k+1}}{2^{k}k!\,(2k+1)}-\beta,

(18)

where
\overline{\varrho}=\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}
,
\beta=0
if
r\geq 0
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
if
r<0
. Since the results calculated with the Vasicek’s series in Section
6
only supplement benchmark values calculated with the tetrachoric series, details about it are not given here.

3
Auxiliary results

This paper pays attention to the estimation of the method error due to truncation of infinite series, while the analysis of rounding errors is out of its scope. The following lemma is intended for this. In it and all the theorems below, the phrase “truncated after the
n^{th}
term” also means omitting the entire series. In these cases, set
n=-1
and assume the empty sum is
0
.

Lemma 1
.

If the Euler’s series

\arctan r=\tfrac{r}{1+r^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{(2k)!!}{(2k+1)!!}\left(\tfrac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}\quad(r\in\mathbb{R})

(19)

is truncated after the
n^{th}
term, then the remainder

R_{n}=\tfrac{r}{1+r^{2}}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\tfrac{(2k)!!}{(2k+1)!!}\left(\tfrac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{-1})

has the same sign as
r
and is bounded in absolute value by

B_{n}\leq\left\lvert R_{n}\right\rvert\leq(1+r^{2})\,B_{n},\quad\text{where}\quad B_{n}=\tfrac{(2n+2)!!}{(2n+3)!!}\,\tfrac{\lvert r\rvert}{1+r^{2}}\left(\tfrac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{n+1}.

(20)

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Noting that
k\geq n+1\geq 0
and
\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{k}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos^{2k+1}t\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(2k)!!}{(2k+1)!!}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2015
, p. 397, 3.621, 4.)
, and using Tonelli’s theorem, it follows

\begin{split}S(r,\alpha)&=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\tfrac{(2k)!!}{(2k+1)!!}\left(\tfrac{\alpha\,r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{\alpha\,r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{\alpha\,r^{2}\,(1-x^{2})}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}\mathrm{d}x=(1+r^{2})\left(\tfrac{\alpha\,r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{n+1}\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{(1-x^{2})^{n+1}\,\mathrm{d}x}{1+(1-\alpha)\,r^{2}+\alpha\,r^{2}x^{2}}.\end{split}

(21)

The denominator in the last integrand is an increasing function of
x
. Inserting
1
and
0
instead of
x
implies

Theorem 2
.

Let
\hat{f}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{c}_{k}\,x^{2k}
and

. Then

Proof.

Let

&=\tfrac{(2k-1)!!\,\hat{c}_{k}}{2\,\lvert\beta\rvert^{2k+1}}\,\tfrac{\gamma(k+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}x^{2})}{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})}=\tfrac{(2k-1)!!\,\hat{c}_{k}}{2\,\lvert\beta\rvert^{2k+1}}\,P(k+\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\beta^{2}x^{2}).\end{split}

Noting that

Let

and

,

\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\lvert F_{k}(x)\rvert\leq\tfrac{\lvert x\rvert\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}x^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\tfrac{\lvert\tilde{c}_{k}\rvert\,\lvert x\rvert^{2k+1}}{k+1}<\infty

∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varrho\in(-1,1)
,
r=\frac{\varrho}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}
and
q=\frac{1}{2}(1+r^{2})h^{2}=\frac{h^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho^{2})}
. Then

\begin{split}\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)&=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Phi(h)+\tfrac{\arctan r}{2\pi}-\tfrac{r}{2\pi\,(1+r^{2})}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\left(\tfrac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}\\
&=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Phi(h)+\tfrac{\arcsin\varrho}{2\pi}-\tfrac{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}{2\pi}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\,\varrho^{2k+1},\end{split}

(29)

where
c_{k}=\frac{(2k)!!}{(2k+1)!!}\,P(k+1,q)
. The series

converges for every
r,h\in\mathbb{R}
. If it is truncated after the
n^{th}
term, then

(30)

where
S_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}

is bounded by

(31)

where

.

Proof.

From (
7
) and
\Phi(x)-\frac{1}{2}=\varphi(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!!}

\tfrac{\partial T(h,r)}{\partial h}=-\,\tfrac{h}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}(1+x^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\varphi(h)\left(\Phi(rh)-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)=\varphi(\beta h)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{c}_{k}\,h^{2k+1},

(32)

r,h\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\beta=\sqrt{1+r^{2}}
and
\tilde{c}_{k}=-\,\frac{r^{2k+1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\;(2k+1)!!}
. Since

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{c}_{k}\,h^{2k+1}=-\,\tfrac{\varphi(h)}{\varphi(\beta h)}\left(\Phi(rh)-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)=-\,e^{\frac{1}{2}r^{2}h^{2}}\left(\Phi(rh)-\tfrac{1}{2}\right),

the series on the left side converges for every

and is an odd function of
rh
. If

, then all its terms are positive, implying that it converges absolutely.

All requirements of Theorem
2
are fulfilled. Assuming

and using

, (
32
) and (

), it follows

T(h,r)=\tfrac{\arctan r}{2\pi}-\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{(2k)!!}{(2k+1)!!}\,\tfrac{r^{2k+1}}{\beta^{2k+2}}\,P(k+1,\tfrac{1}{2}\beta^{2}h^{2}),

which, together with (

), (
7
),
\frac{r^{2}}{\beta^{2}}=\frac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}=\varrho^{2}
,
\frac{r}{1+r^{2}}=\varrho\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}

. Since (

. Using (

Note that if

(33)

Proof.

6
Numeric issues and testing results

A common cause of numerical instability, where two absolutely large values give an absolutely small result when added or subtracted, cannot occur in Recursions
1
and
2
. The calculation by them is stable, but something may happen that needs to be pointed out. The side effect of transforming
rh\mapsto\overline{h}
and
\frac{1}{r}\mapsto\overline{r}
is that
\lvert\overline{h}\rvert
can become unusually large. When computing
\Phi(h)
, according to
Marsaglia 2004
, “[…] the region of interest may not be the statistician’s
0<x<5
, say, but rather
10<x<14
[…]”. Assume the same needs when computing
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)
, and using transformed parameters if
\varrho^{2}>\frac{1}{2}
. If
h=14
and
\varrho=\frac{10}{\sqrt{1+10^{2}}}\approx 0.995
, then
r\approx 10
,
\overline{r}\approx 0.1
and
\overline{h}\approx 140
, implying that the region of interest could be
\lvert h\rvert<140
. Even the usual
\lvert h\rvert<5
transformed to
\lvert\overline{h}\rvert<50
is well beyond the established limits.

The “problem” of too large
\lvert h\rvert
is mentioned because we have to be aware of it if we are going to perform high-precision computation. In any case, the recursion must be complemented by a suitable computation of
\Phi(h)
and
\arctan r
if needed. As an example that not all are like that, the computation by the well known series
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\,x^{2k+1}}{2^{k}k!\,(2k+1)}

(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1972
, p. 932, 26.2.10)
and the standard double precision arithmetic is numerically unstable even for moderately large
\lvert h\rvert
, e.g. using the programming language
\proglang
R
(
R Core Team 2023
)
, for
h=10
we get a completely wrong result, approximately
-1683
. A similar problem can lead to a significant decrease in accuracy if
T(h,r)
is calculated with (
7
), as will be seen in Subsection
6.1
.

In double precision arithmetic, the minimal positive non-zero number is
\eta\approx 2.23\cdot 10^{-308}
. Assuming
b_{0}=e^{-q}\geq\eta
, it follows
q_{max}=-\log(\eta)\approx 708.40
and
h_{max}(r)=\sqrt{-\,\frac{2\log(\eta)}{1+r^{2}}}
. If
\lvert r\rvert\leq 1
, then
h_{max}(r)
is between
h_{max}(1)\approx 26.62
and
h_{max}(0)\approx 37.64
. If
e^{-q}<\eta
, then all elements of the sequence
\{b_{k}\}
are zero. Consequently, all elements of
\{d_{k}\}
are one and all elements of
\{S_{k}\}
in Recursion
2
are zero.

From the integral in (
7
), it follows
\lvert T(h,r)\rvert\leq\varphi(h)\,\frac{\Phi(\lvert r\rvert\lvert h\rvert)-\frac{1}{2}}{\lvert h\rvert}\leq\frac{\varphi(h)}{2\,\lvert h\rvert}
if
h\neq 0
, where the upper bound is a decreasing function of
\lvert h\rvert
. If
e^{-q}<\eta
and
\lvert r\rvert\leq 1
, then
\lvert h\rvert>h_{max}(1)
and
\lvert T(h,r)\rvert\leq\frac{\varphi(h_{max}(1))}{2\,h_{max}(1)}\approx 1.12\cdot 10^{-156}
. Analogously to the case when
r=0
, the recursion terminates with
S_{1}=0
, which is practically a correct result for
T(h,r)
, as well as
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)=\frac{1}{2}
if
h>0
, and
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(h,0)=0
if
h<0
.

Numeric issues and results when computing

In all
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
computational methods based on (
4
) and (
5
), or (
13
) and (
14
), exceptional cases with
\lvert\varrho\rvert=1
,
x\neq 0
and
x-y\,\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)=0
are not problematic because (
3
) can be used, however, nearby cases are their weak point. Recalling that
q_{x}=q_{y}=\frac{x^{2}-2\varrho xy+y^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho^{2})}=q
and using (
10
) and (
2
), it follows
\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\,\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\frac{e^{-q}}{2\pi\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}
and
\lim_{\lvert\varrho\rvert\to 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\,\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=0
, except if
x-y\,\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)=0
. If
\lvert\varrho\rvert
is close to
1
and
x-y\,\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)\simeq 0
, then
x^{2}-2\varrho xy+y^{2}\simeq 0
and
q
may be small enough that
\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\,\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)
is moderate or even large. As a result, even a small rounding error in the calculation of key variables that depend on
\varrho
can cause a much larger error in the result. The calculation of
r_{x}
and
r_{y}
with (
14
) is particularly problematic. In a similar situation,
Meyer 2013
recommends an equivalent but less sensitive calculation by

if
\varrho\approx 1
and
r_{x}=\frac{x+y}{x\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}-\sqrt{\frac{1+\varrho}{1-\varrho}}
if
\varrho\approx-1
, and analogously for
r_{y}
. In our case, his proposal improves accuracy, but does not solve the problem sufficiently.

The problem could be solved by a hybrid computation along the lines of some other methods, e.g. the series from
(
Fayed and Atiya 2014a
, pp. 242–245)
could be used if
\lvert\varrho\rvert\approx 1
and

. Using the initial idea to develop these series, i.e. axis rotation in a way that removes the cross product term

in (

and

the equation (
1
) transforms to

\begin{split}\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)&=\tfrac{1}{2\pi\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}}}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho)}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{u+\sqrt{2}\,y}e^{-\frac{v^{2}}{2\,(1+\varrho)}}\,\mathrm{d}v\right)\mathrm{d}u\\
&+\tfrac{1}{2\pi\,\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}\int_{\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}}}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho)}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-u+\sqrt{2}\,x}e^{-\frac{v^{2}}{2\,(1+\varrho)}}\,\mathrm{d}v\right)\mathrm{d}u\end{split}

and, using
\frac{u}{\sqrt{1-\varrho}}=\tilde{u}
and
\frac{v}{\sqrt{1+\varrho}}=\tilde{v}
, to

\begin{split}\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)&=\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2\,(1-\varrho)}}}\varphi(\tilde{u})\,\Phi\left(\tilde{u}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\varrho}{1+\varrho}}+y\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{1+\varrho}}\,\right)\mathrm{d}\tilde{u}\\
&+\int_{\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2\,(1-\varrho)}}}^{\infty}\varphi(\tilde{u})\,\Phi\left(-\tilde{u}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\varrho}{1+\varrho}}+x\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{1+\varrho}}\,\right)\mathrm{d}\tilde{u}.\end{split}

By transforming
\tilde{u}\mapsto-\tilde{u}
in the second integral and using
(
Owen 1980
, p. 402, 10,010.1)
, it follows
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\Phi_{\varrho^{*}}^{2}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2\,(1-\varrho)}},y\Big)+\Phi_{\varrho^{*}}^{2}\Big(-\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2\,(1-\varrho)}},x\Big)
, where
\varrho^{*}=-\,\sqrt{\frac{1-\varrho}{2}}
. This equation is suitable for
\varrho\approx 1
. From it and
\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\Phi(x)-\Phi_{-\varrho}^{2}(x,-y)
we obtain the equation

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\Phi(x)-\Phi_{\hat{\varrho}}^{2}\left(\tfrac{x+y}{\sqrt{2\,(1+\varrho)}},-y\right)-\Phi_{\hat{\varrho}}^{2}\left(-\tfrac{x+y}{\sqrt{2\,(1+\varrho)}},x\right),

where
\hat{\varrho}=-\,\sqrt{\frac{1+\varrho}{2}}
, that is suitable for
\varrho\approx-1
. Both are combined in

\Phi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)=\tfrac{1-\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)}{2}\,\Phi(x)+\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)\left(\Phi_{\tilde{\varrho}}^{2}(z,\tilde{y})+\Phi_{\tilde{\varrho}}^{2}(-z,x)\right),

(41)

where
\tilde{\varrho}=-\,\sqrt{\frac{1-\lvert\varrho\rvert}{2}}
,
\tilde{y}=y\,\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)
and
z=\frac{x-\tilde{y}}{\sqrt{2\,(1-\lvert\varrho\rvert)}}
. Noting that

\tilde{q}=\tfrac{\tilde{y}^{2}-2\tilde{\varrho}\tilde{y}z+z^{2}}{2\,(1-\tilde{\varrho}^{2})}=\tfrac{x^{2}+2\tilde{\varrho}xz+z^{2}}{2\,(1-\tilde{\varrho}^{2})}=\tfrac{x^{2}-2\varrho xy+y^{2}}{2\,(1-\varrho^{2})}=q,

hence
\varphi_{\tilde{\varrho}}^{2}(z,\tilde{y})=\varphi_{\tilde{\varrho}}^{2}(-z,x)=\frac{e^{-q}}{\pi\,\sqrt{2\,(1+\lvert\varrho\rvert})}
, we stop here because one potentially critical case is replaced with two non-critical ones.

If we continued to simplify the right side of (
41
) by (
13
), we would get (
13
). This actually happens when calculating with the functions used in tests, but it turns out that the results are more accurate than if we start with (
13
) if
\lvert\varrho\rvert\approx 1
and
x-y\,\mathrm{sgn}(\varrho)\simeq 0
. Of course, the equation (
41
) could be used as a starting point instead of (
13
) in all cases, but this is not rational because it requires
4
calculations of the
T
function instead of
2
, two of which cancel each other out, at least in theory, and as a result, in non-critical cases the error may be larger than when starting with (
13
). When testing, (
41
) was used as a starting point instead of (
13
) if
\varphi_{\varrho}^{2}(x,y)>1
. However, since
\min\left\{\lvert\varrho\rvert,\sqrt{\frac{1-\lvert\varrho\rvert}{2}}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, using (
41
) may also make sense in some other circumstances, e.g. when computing directly with (
8
).

7
Conclusion

If the generally applicable Theorem
2
is not considered, Theorems
3
and
4
, and Corollary
2
are interesting mainly theoretically, because Theorem
5
, together with (
13
) and (
14
), replaces and supplements them in practice. The series (
40a
)–(
40d
) enable fast and numerically stable computation, which is often more important than the speed of convergence. From Tables
1
and
4
can be concluded that the computation with (
40a
) or (
40c
) needs significantly fewer iterations than the computation with the accelerated tetrachoric and Vasicek’s series, and from Tables
5
and
6
can be assumed that the advantage increases with increasing precision. For
53
-bit precision (double precision), it required a half, and for
1024
-bit precision only a fifth, of the iterations demanded by the competing series. However, the cost of calculating
\arctan r
is not taken into account. In connection with this, let us just mention two more questions.

Recalling that
e^{-x}I_{n}(x)=P(n+1,x)
, the Taylor series of the arctangent function and (
7
) imply

T(h,r)=\tfrac{\arctan r}{2\pi}-\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{(-1)^{k}r^{2k+1}}{2k+1}\;P(k+1,\tfrac{1}{2}h^{2})=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{(-1)^{k}r^{2k+1}}{2k+1}\;Q(k+1,\tfrac{1}{2}h^{2}),

which
Fayed and Atiya 2014a
have already noticed. If the above series, (
29
) and (
34
) are viewed as functions of the variable
h
, they have a similar structure, but the coefficients, depending on
r
, belong to different arctangent series. Whether the similarity could be explained by a similar Euler transform as the one which transforms the Taylor arctangent series to the Euler’s arctangent series seems interesting question, but was not deeply explored.

In Recursion
1
, the external calculation of the arctangent function is avoided, assuming that the Euler’s series (
19
) is used for it, and the expectation that there would be no difference in the number of iterations for Recursions
1
and
2
if those for calculating the arctangent were also taken into account. However, the use of the external arctangent calculation allows the use of already known and possible future faster converging series. Such is the case with

\arctan r=\tfrac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{(2k-1)!!}{(2k)!!\,(2k+1)}\left(\tfrac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}\quad(r\in\mathbb{R}),

(42)

which is based on the Taylor series of
\arcsin r
and
\arctan r=\arcsin\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}
. Indeed, the number of iterations is not significantly smaller if
\lvert r\rvert\leq 1
and the accuracy obtained by double precision computation is sufficient, and even the square root must be calculated, but it could be significantly smaller in high-precision computation. The Euler’s series (
19
) is included in
(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1972
)
and
(
Olver et al. 2010
)
, but (
42
) is not, even though it converges faster. It can be found in
(
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2015
, p. 61, 1.644 1.)
as

\arctan r=\tfrac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}(2k+1)}\left(\tfrac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{k}

with a reference to the 1922 source. Due to equations
(2k-1)!!=\frac{(2k)!}{2^{k}k!}
and
(2k)!!=2^{k}k!
, the coefficients are the same as those in (
42
). Whether we could also find a corresponding series for the Owen’s
T
function, which would converge faster than (
40b
), remains an open challenge.

ChatGPT Meets Iris Biometrics
Parisa Farmanifard, Arun Ross

Abstract
This study utilizes the advanced capabilities of the GPT-4 multimodal Large Language Model (LLM) to explore its potential in iris recognition — a field less common and more specialized than face recognition. By focusing on this niche yet crucial area, we investigate how well AI tools like ChatGPT can understand and analyze iris images. Through a series of meticulously designed experiments employing a zero-shot learning approach, the capabilities of ChatGPT-4 was assessed across various challenging conditions including diverse datasets, presentation attacks, occlusions such as glasses, and other real-world variations. The findings convey ChatGPT-4’s remarkable adaptability and precision, revealing its proficiency in identifying distinctive iris features, while also detecting subtle effects like makeup on iris recognition. A comparative analysis with Gemini Advanced – Google’s AI model – highlighted ChatGPT-4’s better performance and user experience in complex iris analysis tasks. This research not only validates the use of LLMs for specialized biometric applications but also emphasizes the importance of nuanced query framing and interaction design in extracting significant insights from biometric data. Our findings suggest a promising path for future research and the development of more adaptable, efficient, robust and interactive biometric security solutions.

Introduction

Iris recognition, which is based on the unique patterns of the human iris
[
9
]
, is now being used in a number of access control and border security applications
[
10
,
15
]
. Over the years, a number of different techniques have been developed for processing iris images, which are typically acquired in the near-infrared (NIR) spectrum using specialized hardware
[
16
]
.

The introduction of sophisticated Large Language Models (LLMs) such as OpenAI’s Generative Pre-trained Transformer, GPT-4
[
2
]
, marks a significant paradigm shift in the realm of computer vision. GPT-4 not only embodies advancements in natural language processing but also extends its capabilities to interpreting complex visual data, thereby broadening the scope of LLM applications beyond traditional text-centric tasks. This expansion enhances the potential for integrating LLMs into traditional biometric tasks (such as face or iris analysis). Apart from GPT-4, other LLMs, such as PaLM
[
3
,
8
]
and LLaMA
[
30
]
, have also been introduced but they primarily process text data. These models currently exhibit limited capabilities in handling multimodal inputs, which restricts their utility in diverse real-world applications.

In this paper, we investigate the capabilities of ChatGPT-4 in iris analysis, subjecting the model to a variety of tasks that range from distinguishing between distinctly different iris images to interpreting those with subtle variations and real-world anomalies. Our methodology evaluates GPT-4’s analytical ability and provides insights into effective query formulation to maximize the performance of LLMs in specialized tasks such as iris recognition. Supported by literature that highlights the adaptability of LLMs to manage diverse and intricate tasks, this research investigates the possibility of employing technologies like ChatGPT-4 to enhance biometric systems
[
6
,
17
]
. The results of this exploration affirm the potential of LLMs in assisting both humans and machines in the task of iris analysis.

This paper is motivated by the pivotal question: Can ChatGPT-4, known for its advanced linguistic and emerging visual data processing capabilities, effectively analyze iris images for biometric purposes? We conduct an extensive evaluation of GPT-4’s ability to compare and analyze iris patterns, and contrast its performance against established iris matchers thereby assessing the potential of LLMs to enhance biometric recognition.

Background

Large Language Models (LLMs), which are expansive deep neural networks often with billions of parameters that are designed to predict subsequent words in text using the transformer architecture
[
17
,
32
]
, have become a pivotal area of exploration
[
36
,
37
]
. The utility of LLMs like ChatGPT-4 extends beyond traditional uses, having been successfully employed in diverse fields such as education, where they assist in personalized learning experiences
[
18
,
28
]
, and in programming, where they help in code generation and debugging
[
26
]
. These applications demonstrate the model’s adaptability and capacity for zero-shot learning, where it performs tasks for which it was not explicitly trained.

Recent advancements have shown a notable shift towards leveraging LLMs to perform biometric analysis. Face biometrics, in particular, has been at the forefront of this transition. A recent study by DeAndres-Tame et al.
[
11
]
illustrates the application of ChatGPT in face recognition, revealing its potential to not only enhance accuracy but also provide explainable outcomes. Further research has expanded on these capabilities, with studies evaluating ChatGPT’s performance in face recognition, gender classification, and age estimation. Researchers developed tailored prompting strategies to navigate ChatGPT’s privacy safeguards, uncovering the model’s potential in estimating demographic details with notable accuracy
[
14
]
. These findings highlight the versatility and effectiveness of LLMs in performing biometric tasks.

Parallel to advancements in biometric recognition, the field of medical imaging has also experienced transformative changes with the introduction of LLMs
[
20
,
22
,
33
]
. These models have significantly impacted medical diagnostics, with their ability to accurately annotate and describe complex medical images. Studies have shown improved annotation quality, which supports more precise diagnosis and treatment planning
[
24
]
. Yang et al.
[
35
]
provide a comprehensive analysis of the transformative potential of LLMs across the medical imaging ecosystem. Their research introduced the BIGR-H framework, analyzing the interactions between LLMs and key stakeholders such as businesses, insurers, governments, research institutions, and hospitals. This analysis underscores the significant role of LLMs in enhancing radiologists’ interpretive abilities, improving patient-physician communication, and optimizing clinical workflows.

Collectively, these studies not only demonstrate the capabilities of LLMs in traditional and novel applications but also inspire our exploration into their potential for advancing iris recognition technologies. As we delve deeper into the capabilities of GPT-4 for iris analysis, the insights from these other studies provide a crucial framework for understanding the impact of LLMs in biometric and medical applications.

Our Approach

3.1
ChatGPT-4

Given the limitations of GPT-
3.5
in handling visual data, our study leveraged GPT-
4
’s advanced features, which include the capability to upload and analyze images – a significant leap forward in LLM-assisted iris recognition, traditionally reliant on specialized systems. Our approach was designed using straightforward Python code to effectively engage with the GPT-4 Application Programming Interface (API), using the GPT4-Turbo model, streamlining our experimental processes. We started with the chatbot interface to fine-tune our settings before moving to the API for in-depth experiments. We carefully set key API parameters, crafted clear prompts, and strictly followed the system and user roles to manage query processing and responses effectively, deliberately avoiding the assistant role. We also capped the token count at 1,000 to balance detail with cost-efficiency
[
11
]
.

The strategic use of GPT-4 ensured that our interactions with the model were consistent, replicable, and could be easily modified to suit the needs of each specific experiment. Importantly, our experience confirmed that whether using the GPT-4 API or engaging directly with GPT-4 through other interfaces, the responses and capabilities demonstrated were consistent, underscoring the reliability and stability of GPT-4’s performance across different modes of interaction.

This consistency was crucial for our investigation, allowing us to focus on the experimental design and the rationale behind each test without concern for variability in the responses due to the method of access. The specific outcomes of these experiments, revealing insights into ChatGPT-4’s potential in iris analysis, are detailed in the results section, reflecting the efficacy and adaptability of GPT-4 in handling complex analytical tasks.

[

and IIT-Delhi-Iris
[
19
]
datasets. The objective was to discern whether GPT-4 could accurately determine if the images did
not
belong to the same individual. Upon encountering a refusal from the system, citing an incapability to perform biometric analyses -- likely a safeguard for ethical considerations -- we refined our inquiry. By adjusting our prompt to clarify the non-biometric nature

•

Soft Biometrics:
GPT-4 demonstrated its capability to accurately describe iris features and estimate gender, showcasing its adeptness at handling soft biometric data.

•

Presentation Attack Detection:
We evaluated GPT-4’s ability to detect presentation attacks (PAs) using postmortem iris images and irides with contact lenses, testing its effectiveness in distinguishing between bonafide and PA.

•

By partially covering the iris in some images, we assessed GPT-4’s adaptability to incomplete biometric data.

•

Color Coding:
We tested GPT-4’s sensitivity to color-coded images, by matching probe irides with gallery irides bordered by various colored boxes. The model struggled, incorrectly identifying box locations. Notably, we discovered that using the term
“eye”

This approach, while seemingly simple, required a deep understanding of natural language processing principles and the LLM’s interpretative framework. By iteratively refining our questions and presenting a variety of image-based challenges, we aimed to uncover the extent of GPT-4’s utility in a field as specialized and critical as iris recognition.

In essence, our methodology reflects a blend of technical precision in prompt engineering and psychological acuity in communication, setting the stage for a comprehensive examination of LLM’s potential to contribute to the evolving landscape of biometrics. The outcomes of these experiments, detailed in the following sections, offer intriguing insights into the capabilities and limitations of leveraging a chat-based system for tasks traditionally dominated by highly specialized biometric technologies.

4.3
PA Detection

Our study further explored the domain of Iris Presentation Attack (PA) detection (Figure
5
), a critical component in ensuring the security of iris recognition systems. This part of our research aimed to assess whether ChatGPT-4 could detect attempts to bypass biometric systems through two specific PA instruments: postmortem iris images and textured contact lenses (Figure
5
).
In the first experiment, we used images from the Warsaw-v1 postmortem iris dataset
[
31
]
. We presented these images to ChatGPT-4 with an unclear prompt, “How about these two images?” to test the model’s innate ability to recognize irregularities. Remarkably, ChatGPT-4 suspected that one of the images was an
“artificial representation”
due to its unnatural clarity. This response indicates ChatGPT-4’s inherent ability to detect iris PAs and its skill in spotting anomalies that may suggest tampering or artificial alterations, even without specific instructions related to PA detection. Continuing with this line of investigation, the second experiment involved images from the Clarkson dataset (LiveDet2017
[
34
]
), which includes both genuine iris scans and those modified by contact lenses. Without revealing the particular focus of our test, we asked ChatGPT-4 to assess these images. The model impressively identified signs of
“pattern overlaying”
in the second image, correctly deducing that the anomaly was due to a cosmetic contact lens altering the natural iris pattern. This accurate identification highlights ChatGPT-4’s ability to detect subtle modifications intended to mimic real iris features, affirming its valuable role in strengthening the security of iris recognition systems.

4.4
Multiple Images

In Figure
6
, we expanded our examination of ChatGPT-4’s capabilities in iris recognition by conducting a series of tests designed to determine its ability to differentiate between multiple iris images, some of which belonged to the same iris and others that did not. These experiments were aimed at assessing performance across various datasets, focusing on CASIA-Iris-Interval-v3, ND-Iris-0405, and IIT-Delhi-Iris, to explore how dataset characteristics impact recognition accuracy.
Initially, we presented ChatGPT-4 with three iris images from the CASIA-Iris-Interval-v3 dataset – two from the same iris and one from another. The prompt was deliberately nondescriptive to test ChatGPT’s natural recognition capabilities. The model incorrectly identified the images as belonging to the same individual, suggesting that the complexity and variability in iris patterns of the CASIA dataset pose significant challenges for LLM-based recognition without explicit contextual cues. To further examine the influence of dataset characteristics on LLM performance, we used the same unclear prompt with three iris images from the IIT-Delhi-Iris dataset, maintaining a mix of two images of the same iris and one from a different iris. This time, ChatGPT-4 correctly identified the images, indicating that the traits of the IIT-Delhi-Iris dataset might be more conducive to LLM-based recognition, thereby highlighting the relative complexity of the CASIA dataset.
Additional experiments analyzed sets of four and six iris images, mixing images from the CASIA dataset with those from other datasets to test performance in more complex scenarios. When only one CASIA image was included in a set of four, ChatGPT-4 correctly identified the relationships among the images, showing it could manage the dataset’s complexity in a constrained context. However, increasing the number of CASIA images to two in a set of six resulted in an incorrect response, as the model mistakenly identified the two CASIA images as depicting the same individual. This result reaffirms that the CASIA dataset presents a unique challenge for ChatGPT-4, likely due to its diverse and complex iris patterns.

Adapting our language to use

This underscores the critical role of precise language and conceptual framing in enhancing interactions with LLMs. Further experiments in a probe-and-gallery setup with distinct box colors initially failed, revealing the model’s difficulty with color cues. Adjusting to a uniform color scheme labeled by numbers markedly improved performance, although the inclusion of multiple CASIA images still posed challenges – increasing the likelihood of errors and illustrating the dataset’s inherent complexity as a factor impacting ChatGPT. Upon increasing our experiment’s image count to 12 and 20, ChatGPT-4 consistently identified correct matches, confirming its robustness in this task.

4.6
Gemini Results

In Figure
8
, alongside our investigations into ChatGPT-4, we conducted experiments with Gemini, Google’s AI model, to compare its performance and user experience with that of ChatGPT-4. A notable challenge with Gemini was its limitation in processing only one image at a time, contrasting with ChatGPT-4’s ability to handle multiple images simultaneously. This, therefore, required combining images into a single file using external tools before analysis, adding complexity and reducing the efficiency of the experimental setup. Using a methodology similar to that used with ChatGPT-4, we presented Gemini with composite images of iris sets and employed similar prompts to test its recognition capabilities. This experiment with Gemini provides critical insights: Gemini’s single-image processing is a notable limitation for conducting sophisticated or multi-image analyses. While it can adapt to specific prompts, its lack of contextual continuity limits its effectiveness in scenarios requiring nuanced understanding and sequential analysis. Summary and Future Work

This research highlights the impressive capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT-4 in biometric recognition, particularly iris. Through a series of experiments, ChatGPT-4 demonstrated significant adaptability and accuracy in analyzing iris images under diverse conditions, compared to Gemini. The success of the model in these tasks underscores the critical role of prompt engineering in enhancing the effectiveness of LLMs for biometrics. This insight challenges existing paradigms in biometrics and broadens the scope for LLM’s applicability in this field. The results encourage further investigation into optimizing LLMs for biometrics, which includes refining training methodologies and enhancing model responsiveness to complex datasets. Future research should also aim to compare different LLMs to pinpoint design and usability improvements. The integration of LLM technologies like ChatGPT-4 into biometric analysis signals a promising direction towards developing more sophisticated, adaptable, and interactive biometric systems.

All interactions and conversations conducted with ChatGPT during this research are saved and available for review, ensuring transparency and reproducibility of our findings.

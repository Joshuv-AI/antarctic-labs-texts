Centrally endo-AIP modules
Shiv Kumar, Ashok Ji Gupta \comma \corrauth

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the concept of centrally endo-AIP modules. We call a module M M centrally endo-AIP, if the left annihilator of any fully invariant submodule N N of M M in the endomorphism ring S = E ​ n ​ d R ​ ( M ) S=End_{R}(M) is a centrally s-unital ideal of S S . We discuss some properties of centrally endo-AIP modules. We also study the endomorphism ring of centrally endo-AIP modules and characterize quasi-Baer modules in terms of centrally endo-AIP modules.

and
\phi_{2}\in T_{2}
,
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}&&0\\
0&&\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in l_{S}(N)
and
\alpha\phi=\alpha
. Therefore,
l_{S}(N)
is a centrally s-unital ideal of
S
. Hence,
M
is a centrally endo-AIP module.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}M_{\lambda}
, where
M_{\lambda}
is a centrally endo-AIP module for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
M_{\lambda}\cong M_{\nu}
for every
\lambda,\nu\in\Lambda
. Then
M
is a centrally endo-AIP module.

Proof 2.20
.

First, we prove the theorem for
\Lambda=\{1,2,...,n\}
. Now, we assume
{(M_{\lambda})}_{R}\cong X_{R}
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Let
N
be a fully invariant submodule of
M
, then by (Lemma 1.10,
[
15
]
),
N=\bigoplus_{\lambda=1}^{n}N_{\lambda}
, where
N_{\lambda}=N\cap M_{\lambda}\subseteq X
and each
N_{\lambda}
is fully invariant in
X
. It is observe that, if
{S^{\prime}}=End_{R}(X)
then
S\cong Mat_{n}({S^{\prime}})
and

l_{S}(N)=\begin{pmatrix}l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{1})&&l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{2})&&...&&l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{n})\\
l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{1})&&l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{2})&&...&&l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{n})\\
...&&\>...&&...&&...\\
l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{1})&&l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{2})&&...&&l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{n})\end{pmatrix}
. So, if
\phi\in l_{S}(N)
then
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\\
\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\\
...&&...&&...&&\\
\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\end{pmatrix}
, where
\phi_{\lambda}\in l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{\lambda})
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Since
X
is a centrally endo-AIP module, so for each
\phi_{\lambda}\in l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{\lambda})
, there are some central elements
\psi_{\lambda}\in l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{\lambda})
such that
\phi_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}=\phi_{\lambda}
. Hence,
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\\
\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\\
...&&...&&...&&...\\
\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\psi_{1}&&\phi_{2}\psi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\psi_{n}\\
\phi_{1}\psi_{1}&&\phi_{2}\psi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\psi_{n}\\
...&&...&&...&&...\\
\phi_{1}\psi_{1}&&\phi_{2}\psi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\psi_{n}\end{pmatrix}
which implies that
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\\
\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\\
...&&...&&...&&...\\
\phi_{1}&&\phi_{2}&&...&&\phi_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}&&0&&...&&0\\
0&&\psi_{2}&&...&&0\\
...&&...&&...&&...\\
0&&0&&...&&\psi_{n}\end{pmatrix}
. Since, for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
\psi_{\lambda}\in l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{\lambda})
is a central element, therefore
\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}&&0&&...&&0\\
0&&\psi_{2}&&...&&0\\
...&&...&&...&&...\\
0&&0&&...&&\psi_{n}\end{pmatrix}
is a central element of
l_{S}(N)
.
Now
\psi(N)=0
because
\psi_{\lambda}\in l_{S^{\prime}}(N_{\lambda})
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Therefore,
l_{S}(N)
is a centrally s-unital ideal of
S
. By assuming
\Lambda
an infinite set, the proof can be extended to a column finite matrix ring. Hence,
M
is a centrally endo-AIP module.

Theorem 2.21
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

Every projective right
R
-module is centrally endo-AIP module;

(ii)

Every free
R
-module is centrally endo-AIP module;

(iii)

R
is a centrally AIP ring.

Proof 2.22
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
It is clear.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
It is well known that a projective module is a direct summand of a free module. Since, from (ii) every free module is centrally endo-AIP module. Therefore, from Proposition
2.9
every projective module is centrally endo-AIP module.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
It is clear that
R_{R}
is a free right
R
-module. So, by (ii)
R_{R}
is a centrally endo-AIP
R
-module. Therefore,
R
is a centrally AIP ring.

(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
Let
M=R^{(\Lambda)}
be a free
R
-module and
\Lambda
be an arbitrary index set. Since
R
is a centrally AIP ring, so from Theorem
2.19

M
is a centrally endo-AIP module.

Remark 2.23
.

From Theorem
2.21
it is clear that, if

and matrix ring

are centrally AIP rings, see also (Lemma 3.4,

]

[

]

3
Endomorphism rings of centrally endo-AIP modules

In this section we study the endomorphism ring of centrally endo-AIP modules and discuss the equivalency among quasi-Baer modules, centrally endo-AIP modules and endo-AIP modules.

Proposition 3.1
.

The endomorphism ring of centrally endo-AIP module is centrally AIP ring.

Proof 3.2
.

Let
S=End_{R}(M)
be an endomorphism ring of
M
,
T
be an ideal of
S
and
f\in l_{S}(T)
. Then
f(T(M))=0
which implies that
f\in l_{S}(T(M))
. It is clear that
T(M)
is a fully invariant submodule of
M
. Since
M
is a centrally endo-AIP module, there exists a central element
g\in l_{S}(T(M))
such that
fg=f
. Thus,
gT(M)=0\Rightarrow gT=0
which implies that
g\in l_{S}(T)
. Hence,
S
is a centrally AIP ring.

Corollary 3.3
.

The endomorphism ring of a centrally endo-AIP module is a semiprime ring.

Proof 3.4
.

Since, from (Proposition 2.9,
[
13
]
) every centrally AIP ring is semiprime ring. Therefore, the proof follows from Proposition
3.1
.

Remark 3.5
.

We observe that when we take the class of finitely generated projective module
M
over a centrally AIP ring
R
, then the endomorphism ring of
M
is a centrally AIP. In particular, the centrally AIP property is Morita invariant (Theorem 3.5,
[
13
]
).

The following example shows that the converse of proposition
3.1
need not be true in general.

Example 3.6
.

Let
M=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-module, where
p
is prime . It is well known that
End_{R}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{(\bar{p})}
(ring of p-adic integers) (see Example 3, page 216
[
7
]
), which is a commutative domain and endo-AIP ring (see Example 3.2,
[
6
]
). Therefore, it is a centrally AIP ring. Also,
M
is not an endo-AIP module (see Example 3.2,
[
6
]
). Hence, by Proposition
2.2

M
is not a centrally endo-AIP module.

Recall that a module
M
is locally principally quasi-retractable module, if for every principal ideal
I
of
S=End_{R}(M)
such that
r_{M}(I)\neq 0
, then there exists a non-zero endomorphism
\psi\in S
such that
r_{M}(I)=\psi(M)
(see Definition 3.3,
[
6
]
).

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
M
be a locally principally quasi-retractable module. If
S=End_{R}(M)
is a left centrally AIP ring, then
M
is a centrally endo-AIP module.

Proof 3.8
.

Let
N
be a fully invariant submodule of
M
. Then for every
f\in l_{S}(N)
,
SfS\subseteq l_{S}(N)
. Thus,
0\neq N\subseteq r_{M}(SfS)
. Since
M
is a locally principally quasi-retractable module and
r_{M}(SfS)\neq 0
, so there exists
0\neq g\in S
such that
N\subseteq r_{M}(SfS)=g(M)
and
f\in l_{S}(SgS)
. Since
S
is a left centrally AIP ring, there is a central element
h\in l_{S}(SgS)
such that
fh=f=hf
. Now as
N\subseteq g(M)
so
h(N)\subseteq h(g(M))=0
. Therefore,
h\in l_{S}(N)
. Hence,
M
is centrally endo-AIP module.

Recall that a right
R
-module
M
has uniform dimension
n
(written as
u.dim(M_{R})=n
) if there is an essential submodule
N
of
M
, which is a direct sum of
n
uniform submodules. If no such integer exists, then
u.dim(M)=\infty
. Further, a ring
R
has finite right (left) uniform dimesion, if
u.dim(R_{R})=n
(
u.dim(_{R}R)=n
) for a positive integer
n
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
M
be a centrally endo-AIP module and
S=End_{R}(M)
. If
S
has finite right uniform dimension, then
S
is a quasi-Baer ring.

Proof 3.10
.

Let
M
be a centrally endo-AIP module. Then, from Proposition
3.1

S
is centrally AIP ring. So, by assumption
S
is a centrally AIP ring with finite right uniform dimension. Hence, from (Theorem 5.1,
[
13
]
)
S
is a quasi-Baer ring.

A right
R
-module
M
is called semi-projective
[
3
]
, if
T=Hom_{R}(M,TM)
for any cyclic right ideal
T
of
S=End_{R}(M)
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Endomorphism ring of a centrally endo-AIP semi-projective retractable module with finite uniform dimension is quasi-Baer.

Proof 3.12
.

Let
M
be a semi-projective retractable module with finite uniform dimension. Then, from (Theorem 2.6,
[
3
]
)
S=End_{R}(M)
has finite right uniform dimension. Therefore, from Proposition
3.9
,

is a quasi-Baer ring.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module with endomorphism ring
S=End_{R}(M)
. If
M
is a centrally endo-AIP module and
u.dim(S_{S})=1
, then
S
is a prime ring.

Proof 3.14
.

Let
M
be a centrally endo-AIP module and
u.dim(S_{S})=1
. Then from Corollary
3.3
and Proposition
3.9
,
S
is a semiprime quasi-Baer ring. If
S
is not a prime ring, then
l_{S}(S\phi)=Se
for some

and

. Since

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
M
be an endo-AIP module and
S=End_{R}(M)
. If
S
is local ring then

is prime.

Proof 3.16
.

It is clear from (Theorem 3.1,
[
6
]
) that the endomorphism ring of an endo-AIP module is AIP ring. Thus,

is a local AIP ring. Since, every local AIP ring is a prime ring (Proposition 5.3,
[

]

Recall from
[
16

, a fully invariant submodule

. Further, a fully invariant submodule

Proposition 3.17
.

Let

be a semiprime (prime) module and

satisfies ascending chain condition on its principal left ideals. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

is a quasi-Baer module;

(ii)

M
is an endo-AIP module;

(iii)

M
is a centrally endo-AIP module.

Proof 3.18
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
It is clear.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
Let
M
be an endo-AIP module,

be a fully invariant submodule of

and

satisfies ascending chain condition on its principal left ideals. Then, from (Proposition 3.9,
[
6

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
Since,

[

Polynomial bounds for the solutions of parametric transmission problems on smooth, bounded domains
Simon Labrunie, Hassan Mohsen, Victor Nistor

Abstract
. We consider a family ( P ω ) ω ∈ Ω (P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega} of elliptic second order differential operators on a domain U 0 ⊂ ℝ m U_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m} whose coefficients depend on the space variable x ∈ U 0 x\in U_{0} and on ω ∈ Ω , \omega\in\Omega, a probability space. We allow the coefficients a i ​ j a_{ij} of P ω P_{\omega} to have jumps over a fixed interface Γ ⊂ U 0 \Gamma\subset U_{0} (independent of ω ∈ Ω \omega\in\Omega ). We obtain polynomial in the norms of the coefficients estimates on the norm of the solution u ω u_{\omega} to the equation P ω ​ u ω = f P_{\omega}u_{\omega}=f with transmission and mixed boundary conditions (we consider “sign-changing” problems as well). In particular, we show that, if f f and the coefficients a i ​ j a_{ij} are smooth enough and follow a log-normal-type distribution, then the map Ω ∋ ω → ‖ u ω ‖ H k + 1 ​ ( U 0 ) \Omega\ni\omega\to\|u_{\omega}\|_{H^{k+1}(U_{0})} is in L p ​ ( Ω ) L^{p}(\Omega) , for all 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty . The same is true for the norms of the inverses of the resulting operators. We expect our estimates to be useful in Uncertainty Quantification.

1.2.
The probabilistic setting

Let us assume that the coefficients

depend on both the space variable
x\in U_{0}
and on an
additional variable
\omega\in\Omega
in a probability space. This is a setting that appears in
practice, because the coefficients

represent properties of materials that are not always
known exactly. Our results then translate into estimates for the family

and the associated
transmission/mixed boundary value problem. One of the reasons Theorem

is important is
that it can be used to treat log-normal-type distributions for the coefficients.
Indeed, we show that, if

and the coefficients

are smooth enough and follow a log-normal-type distribution, then the map
\Omega\ni\omega\to\|u_{\omega}\|_{H^{k+1}(U_{0})}
is in

, for all
0\leq p<\infty
.
The same is true for the norm of
P_{\omega}^{-1}

(acting between suitable spaces that take into account the boundary and transmission conditions),
see Theorems
5.5
and
5.8
.
We apply our results to obtain Finite Element estimates for Gaussian families and to prove that the
error is integrable, Theorem
5.10
, thus yielding, in a certain sense, optimal rates
of convergence. We expect these estimates to be useful in Uncertainty
Quantification
[
16
,
18
,
26
,
40
]
.

1.3.
Motivation: sign changing problems and connection with boundary triples

The initial motivation for this paper (which is largely based on the Ph. D. Thesis of the
second named author
[
32
]
) is to study the regularity of problems with “sign-changing”
coefficients for
polygonal domains
. Here “sign changing” means that the matrix
A
can be
definite of different signs on different
subdomains
U_{j}\subset U_{0}
.
(For instance, our operator could be of the form
P^{A}=c_{j}\Delta
with,
c_{j}
constant on

U_{j}
, with these constants
c_{j}
possibly of different signs.) An approach to the study of these
problems is via “T-coercivity,” as in the paper by Ciarlet, Chesnel, and Bonnet-Ben Dia

[
6
]
. See also
[
7
,
9
,
13
,
15
,
37
]
for further
motivation and results on this type of problems. When studying regularity on polygonal domains, we are
facing several issues, among which is the development in singular functions and regularity
in weighted (i.e. Kondratiev) spaces. As is well known, see, for instance
[
17
,
24
,
25
,
34
]
, it is convenient to study a certain induced operator on the circle. More precisely,
we are interested in whether it is self-adjoint and in its spectrum, since the spectrum of
this self-adjoint operator gives the singular values and singular functions of the operator.
A numerical study of this spectrum is contained in
[
32
]
. The self-adjointness
problem also arises in the study of the regularity–even for smooth domains. These problems
are conveniently approached in our setting using the techniques of boundary triples, as
in the paper by Brüning, Geyler, and Pankrashkin
[
11
]
. Results in this
direction were obtained in
[
32
]
. Using self-adjointness results, we then prove
a parametric regularity result for problems with sign changing coefficients, Theorem
6.2
.

Contents

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

we introduce our

We also formulate Nirenberg’s trick, which is used in “raising the regularity” in the induction

. In Section

2.5.
Nirenberg’s trick

We shall need a version of Nirenberg’s lemma (or “trick”), as it is formalized in

[
12
,
33
]
. Let us first recall some classical definitions.
We let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
denote the set of continuous, linear
maps
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
between two normed spaces.

Definition 2.22
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be two normed spaces and

T_{t}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
,
t\geq 0
. We say that
T_{t}

converges strongly

to
T
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
for
t\searrow 0
(i.e.
t\to 0
,
t>0
), if

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\,,\quad\lim_{t\searrow 0}\|T_{t}x-Tx\|_{\mathcal{Y}}=0\,.

We shall need also the following well-known concept
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.23
.

A family of operators
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{L}(X):=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{X})

is a
strongly continuous semigroup
on
\mathcal{X}
if:

(i)

S(0)=id_{\mathcal{X}}
(the identity map).

(ii)

For all
t,t^{\prime}\geq 0
, we have
S(t+t^{\prime})=S(t)S(t^{\prime})
.

(iii)

S(t)
converges strongly to
S(0)=id_{\mathcal{X}}
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
for
t\searrow 0
.

Definition 2.24
.

Let
S(t)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
be a strongly continuous semigroup on a Banach space.
Its
infinitesimal generator
is the (possibly unbounded) operator

L_{S}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\,t^{-1}(S(t)x-x)

with domain
\mathcal{D}(L_{S})\subset\mathcal{X}

the set of
x\in\mathcal{X}
for which this limit exists.

We shall make essential use of the following lemma. (It is a form of “Nirenberg’s trick”,
see also
[
12
,
30
]
for further references.)

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
S_{\mathcal{X}}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
and
S_{\mathcal{Y}}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Y})

be two strongly continuous semi-groups of operators on Banach spaces
with infinitesimal generators
L_{\mathcal{X}}
and
L_{\mathcal{Y}}
, respectively. Let us assume that:

(i)

T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
is invertible,

(ii)

for all
t\geq 0
, there exists
T_{t}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
such that

T_{t}S_{\mathcal{X}}(t)=S_{\mathcal{Y}}(t)T
, and

(iii)

t^{-1}(T_{t}-T)
converges strongly to
Q\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
for
t\searrow 0
.

Then
T^{-1}(\mathcal{D}(L_{\mathcal{Y}}))\subset\mathcal{D}(L_{\mathcal{X}})
, and, most importantly,
for all
v\in\mathcal{D}(L_{\mathcal{Y}})
, we have

L_{\mathcal{X}}(T^{-1}v)\,=\,T^{-1}L_{\mathcal{Y}}v-T^{-1}QT^{-1}v\,.

See
[
12
,
33
]
for a proof.
It also follows from the proof that the induced operator
\tilde{T}:=T|_{\mathcal{D}(L_{\mathcal{X}})}:\mathcal{D}(L_{\mathcal{X}})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(L_{\mathcal{Y}})
is well-defined, continuous, and bijective, but this
fact will not be used in this paper.

(ii)

\big(\mathcal{P}_{n+k}^{A}\big)^{-1}

(iii)

k,n\geq 0
,
\mathcal{P}_{k}:=\mathcal{P}_{k}^{A}
,
and suppose that the operator
\mathcal{P}_{n}:V_{n}\to V_{n}^{-}
is invertible.
Then
\mathcal{P}_{n+k+1}:V_{n+k+1}\to V_{n+k+1}^{-}
is also invertible and its inverse has norm

|||\mathcal{P}_{n+k+1}^{-1}|||_{n+k+1}\,\lesssim\,|||\mathcal{P}_{n}^{-1}|||_{n}^{k+2}\,|||a_{mm}^{-1}|||_{\mathcal{V},n+k}^{(k+1)^{2}}\|A\|_{\check{W}^{n+k+1,\infty}}^{(k+2)(k+1)}\,.

Proof.

Lemma
3.11

implies that
|||\mathcal{P}_{n}^{-1}|||_{n}^{q+1}\,|||a_{mm}^{-1}|||_{\mathcal{V},n+k}^{(q+1)k+1}\|A\|_{\check{W}^{n+k+1,\infty}}^{(q+1)(k+1)}
is increasing in
q
. Since
\|F\|_{V_{k+2-q}^{-}}

is decreasing in
q
, the result follows.
∎

The above result is obviously true also for
k=-1
, as long as one defines
|||a_{mm}^{-1}|||_{\mathcal{V},n+k}

for
n=0
(actually, one can simply ignore that term for
n=0
and
k=-1
). In order to avoid
this discussion, it was convenient to state the above result (as well as Theorem
4.1
)
in the given range of
k
(i.e.
k\geq 0
). We now shift back and replace
k+1
with
k
.
We obtain the following consequence.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Suppose that

(i)

A:\Theta\to M_{(m+1)}(\check{W}^{n+k,\infty}(U_{0}))

is bounded;

(ii)

\mathcal{P}_{n}^{A(\theta)}:V_{n}\to V_{n}^{-}
is invertible for
all
\theta\in\Theta
; and

(iii)

the function
\theta\to\big(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{A(\theta)}\big)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(V_{n}^{-};V_{n})

is bounded.

Then the function
\big(\mathcal{P}_{n+k}^{A}\big)^{-1}(\theta):=\big(\mathcal{P}_{n+k}^{A(\theta)}\big)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(V_{n+k}^{-};V_{n+k})

is also bounded. Consequently, if
\Theta
is a probability space and
A
is
measurable, then the functions
\big(\mathcal{P}_{n+k}^{A}\big)^{-1}
and
\|\big(\mathcal{P}_{n+k}^{A}\big)^{-1}\|

are integrable on
\Theta
.

Proof.

The first part follows right away from Corollary
4.6
.
The second part follows by combining the first part with Corollary
4.5
(i), since
bounded, measurable functions on probability spaces are integrable.
∎

The main applications in this paper will be to extend the above integrability result to
the case when the norms of the operators
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{A(\theta)}\in\mathcal{L}(V_{n};V_{n}^{-})

are not uniformly bounded.

5.1.
General case

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

(see Definition

. Then
b^{-1}\in\check{W}^{k,\infty}(U_{0})
and

\displaystyle\|b^{-1}\|_{\check{W}^{k,\infty}}\leq c_{3}\|b^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{k+1}\|b\|_{\check{W}^{k,\infty}}^{k}\,.

We will denote
\operatorname{Re}A:=\frac{1}{2}\big(A+A^{*}\big)
, for any
matrix
A\in M_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})
(with
A^{*}
the adjoint of
A
, i.e. its
transposed conjugate). We write
A\geq\gamma I_{m+1}
if, for
any complex vector
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
on which
A
acts,
we have
(A\xi,\xi)\geq\gamma\|\xi\|^{2}
. (Thus,
I_{m+1}
is the identity
matrix of
M_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})
.) We write
A>0
if

A\geq 0
and
A
is invertible. If
A\in M_{m+1}(L^{\infty}(U_{0}))

the inequality
A\geq\gamma I_{m+1}
means that

for almost all

. In the following, we will also omit
A
in the notation
of the operators
P^{A}
and
\mathcal{P}_{k}^{A}
when there is no risk of confusion.

Lemma 5.3
.

See Notation
5.1
for a review of the notation. Let
A\in M_{m+1}(L^{\infty}(U_{0}))
.
If
\operatorname{Re}A\geq\gamma I_{m+1}
, then
\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{0}^{A}:V_{0}\to V_{0}^{-}
is invertible
with
|||\mathcal{P}_{0}^{-1}|||_{0}\leq\gamma^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}:=\mathcal{P}_{0}^{A}
. As
\gamma>0
, we obtain for
u\in V_{0}=H^{1}(U_{0})
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}(\mathcal{P}_{0}u,u){\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\operatorname{Re}B^{A}(u,u)

\displaystyle\,=\,\operatorname{Re}\int_{U_{0}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{m}a_{ij}\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}\overline{u}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\int_{U_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}|\partial_{i}u|^{2}\,dx\,=:\,\gamma\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,.

So
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is invertible and
|||\mathcal{P}_{0}^{-1}|||_{0}\leq\gamma^{-1}
,
by Lax–Milgram’s lemma.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
U_{0}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}U_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is bounded with
\partial U_{k}

smooth, as usual. Let
A\in M_{m+1}(\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}(U_{0}))
be such that

A\geq\gamma I_{m+1}
, with
\gamma>0
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{j}:=\mathcal{P}_{j}^{A}:V_{j}\to V_{j}^{-}

for all

and
k\geq-1
. (See Notation
5.1
for a review of the notation.)
Then, given
F\in V_{k+1}^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{0}^{-1}F\|_{V_{k+1}}\lesssim\sum_{q=0}^{k+1}\,\gamma^{-q-1}\,|||a_{mm}^{-1}|||_{\mathcal{V},k}^{(q+1)k+1}\|A\|_{\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}}^{(q+1)(k+1)}\|F\|_{V_{k+1-q}^{-}}\,.

In particular,
\mathcal{P}_{k+1}
is invertible. Let
K:=(k+2)(k^{2}+k+1)+k
. Then

|||\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{-1}|||_{k+1}\leq\gamma^{-K-1}\,\|A\|_{\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}}^{K}\,.

Proof.

The hypothesis of the theorem imply that
\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{0}^{A}:V_{0}:=H_{D}^{1}(U_{0})\to V_{0}^{-}=:H_{D}^{1}(U_{0})^{*}
is invertible and

.
Moreover, we also have
\gamma\lesssim a_{mm}
, so

.
It follows from Lemma

that

|||a_{mm}^{-1}|||_{\mathcal{V},k}\leq\gamma^{-k-1}|||a_{mm}^{-1}|||_{\mathcal{V},k}^{k}\leq\gamma^{-k-1}\|A\|_{\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}}^{k}\,.

The rest is a consequence of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

We obtain the following consequence for the case where the coefficients are
random Gaussian variables defined on a measured space
\Omega
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
X=(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{q}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a Gaussian vector random variable
with covariance
\sigma=(\sigma_{ij})>0
and let
\gamma>0
. Let
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{q}\in M_{m+1}(\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}(U_{0}))
,
\operatorname{Re}A_{\ell}\geq 0
for all
1\leq\ell\leq q
, such that,
for all
x\in U_{0}
, there is
\ell
with
\operatorname{Re}A_{\ell}(x)\geq\gamma I_{m+1}
. We let
A(\omega):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}A_{j}
and
\gamma(\omega)
be the largest constant
such that
\gamma(\omega)I\leq\operatorname{Re}A(\omega)
. Then, for all
0<p,r,s<\infty
, the function

\Omega\ni\omega\to\gamma(\omega)^{-s}\|A(\omega)\|_{\check{W}^{k+1}}^{r}|||\big(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)}\big)^{-1}|||_{k+1}^{p}\in(0,\infty)

is integrable.

Proof.

The given function is measurable by Corollary
4.5
(i).
We have

(32)

\operatorname{Re}A(\omega){\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}A_{j}\geq\gamma\,\min_{\ell=1}^{q}\{e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}\}\,I_{m+1}\,,

where
I_{m+1}
is the identity matrix of
M_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})
, as before.
Hence
\gamma(\omega)\geq\gamma\,\min_{\ell=1}^{q}\{e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}\}
.
Similary, we have the estimate

(33)

\|A(\omega)\|_{\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}}\,\leq\,\sum_{j=1}^{q}e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}\|A_{j}\|_{\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}}\,.

By Theorem
5.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma(\omega)^{-s/p}\|A(\omega)\|_{\check{W}^{k+1}}^{r/p}\,|||(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}|||_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\gamma\min_{\ell=1}^{q}\{e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}\}\big)^{-K-s/p-1}\,\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}\|A_{\ell}\|_{\check{W}^{k,\infty}}\Big)^{K+r/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{|X_{\ell}(\omega)|}\Big)^{2K+r/p+s/p+1}\,.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\gamma(\omega)^{-s/p}\|A(\omega)\|_{\check{W}^{k+1}}^{r}\,|||(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}|||_{k+1}^{p}d\omega

\displaystyle\leq\ C\,\int_{\Omega}\,\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{|X_{\ell}(\omega)|}\Big)^{2K+r/p+s/p+1}d\omega

\displaystyle\,=\,\ C\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\,\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{|x_{\ell}|}\Big)^{a}e^{-(\sigma^{-1}x,x)}dx\,<\,\infty

since, for all
a>0
,

\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{|x_{\ell}|}\big)^{a}<(q+e^{|x_{1}|+\ldots+|x_{q}|})^{a},
which is integrable with respect to the measure of density

e^{-(\sigma^{-1}x,x)}\leq e^{-\epsilon\|x\|^{2}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
.
∎

5.2.
Poincaré inequality

We shall say that the Poincaré inequality is satisfied by the functions in

H_{D}^{1}(U_{0}):=\{u\in H^{1}(U_{0})\mid u|_{\partial_{D}U_{0}}=0\}
with
constant
\eta_{U}
if, for all
u\in H_{D}^{1}(U_{0})
, we have

(34)

|u|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}^{2}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\int_{U_{0}}|\nabla u(x)|dx\,\geq\,\eta_{U}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}^{2}\,.

If this condition is satisfied, then we get stronger results as follows.
Let
A=[a_{ij}]\in M_{m+1}(L^{\infty}(U_{0}))
and
P^{A}
and
\mathcal{P}_{k}^{A}
be the associated
operators, as before. Recall then that
A
(or
P^{A}
, or, yet,
\mathcal{P}_{k}^{A}
)
is
\gamma
–strongly elliptic
if, for all
x\in U_{0}
and all
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, we have

(35)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\overline{\xi}_{j}\geq\gamma|\xi|^{2}\,.

The largest
\gamma
with this property will be denoted
\gamma(A)
and is called the

coercivity constant
of
A
. Of course
\gamma(A)=\|(A+A^{*})^{-1}\|^{-1}/2
if
A

is coercive.
Let
I^{\prime}:=0\oplus I_{m}\in M_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})
be the projection with entries
(I^{\prime})_{ij}
given by

(36)

\begin{cases}(I^{\prime})_{ii}=1&\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq m\\
(I^{\prime})_{ij}=0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(Thus
I^{\prime}
differs from the identity matrix
I_{m+1}\in M_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})
in only one entry.)
The strong ellipticity condition then becomes
I^{\prime}AI^{\prime}\geq\gamma I^{\prime}
.
If
a_{0j}=a_{i0}=0
for all
0\leq i,j\leq m
,
then
I^{\prime}AI^{\prime}=A
and the strong ellipticity condition becomes
A\geq\gamma I^{\prime}
.
The interest of the concept of strong ellipticity is that operators satisfying this
condition have the following properties.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
A=[a_{ij}]\in M_{m+1}(L^{\infty}(U_{0}))
be such that

a_{i0}=a_{0j}=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
and
\operatorname{Re}A\geq 0
. Suppose
that
A\geq\gamma I^{\prime}
(i.e.
A
is
\gamma
–strongly elliptic) and that
the Poincaré inequality is satisfied on
H_{D}^{1}(U_{0})
with constant
\eta_{U}>0
.
Then

\mathcal{P}_{0}:=\mathcal{P}_{0}^{A}:V_{0}\to V_{0}^{-}
is invertible with norm

|||\mathcal{P}_{0}^{-1}|||_{0}\leq(\eta_{U}\gamma)^{-1}
.

Proof.

We have

\operatorname{Re}(\mathcal{P}_{0}^{A}u,u)\,=\,B^{A}(u,u)\geq\gamma^{2}|u|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}^{2}+a_{00}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\geq(\eta_{U}\gamma)^{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}^{2}\,,

then the Lax–Milgram’s Lemma gives the results in view of the definitions of

V_{0}:=H_{D}^{1}(U_{0})
and
V_{0}^{-}:=V_{0}^{*}
.
∎

We immediately obtain the following consequence, with essentially the same proof
as that of Theorem
5.4
. See Notation
5.1
for
a review of the notation.

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose that
A\in\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}(U_{0})

satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
5.6
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{j}:=\mathcal{P}_{j}^{A}:V_{j}\to V_{j}^{-}
for all

and

. Then, given

we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{0}^{-1}F\|_{V_{k+1}}\lesssim\sum_{q=0}^{k+1}\,\gamma^{-q-1}\,|||a_{mm}^{-1}|||_{\mathcal{V},k}^{(q+1)k+1}\|A\|_{\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}}^{(q+1)(k+1)}\|F\|_{V_{k+1-q}^{-}}\,.

In particular,
\mathcal{P}_{k+1}
is invertible. Let
K:=(k+2)(k^{2}+k+1)+k
. Then

|||\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{-1}|||_{k+1}\lesssim\gamma^{-K-1}\,\|A\|_{\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}}^{K}\,.

We obtain the following consequence (with the same proof as Theorem
5.5
).
We use the hypotheses of the Theorems
5.5
and
5.7

(listed below):

Theorem 5.8
.

We continue to use Notation
5.1
. Let
\gamma>0
and

A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{q}\in M_{m+1}(\check{W}^{k+1,\infty}(U_{0}))
be matrices such that

(i)

(A_{\ell})_{i0}=(A_{\ell})_{0j}=0
, for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
and all
1\leq\ell\leq q
,

(ii)

\operatorname{Re}A_{\ell}\geq 0
for all
1\leq\ell\leq q
, and

(iii)

for each
x\in U_{0}
, there is
1\leq\ell\leq q
such that
\operatorname{Re}A_{\ell}(x)\geq\gamma I^{\prime}
,
where
I^{\prime}:=0\oplus I_{m}
(see Equation (
36
)).

We assume that the Poincaré inequality is satisfied on
H_{D}^{1}(U_{0})
. Let
X:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{q}

be Gaussian as in Theorem
5.5
and
A(\omega):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}e^{X_{\ell}(\omega)}A_{\ell}

Then

is coercive with coercivity constant
\gamma(\omega):=\gamma(A(\omega))
such that,
for all

the function

The conditions

, for all

and all

model homogeneous second order operators (such as “sign-changing Laplacians”). Our operators

.

Proof.

∎

5.3.
Application to the Finite Element Method

Let
S_{N}\subset H_{D}^{1}
be a sequence of finite dimensional vector subspaces.
We let
Q_{N}
denote the projection of
u\in H_{D}^{1}
on
S_{N}
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}}
.
We suppose that there exist
k>1
,
C_{rate}>0
, and
\mu>0
such that,
for all
u\in\check{H}^{k+1}(U_{0})
, we have

(37)

\|u-Q_{N}u\|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}\leq C_{rate}\dim(S_{N})^{-\mu}\|u\|_{\check{H}^{k+1}(U_{0})}\,.

See
[
31
]
for an example of such subspaces using the Generalized Finite
Element spaces and
[
4
]
for an example of such subspaces using anisotropically
graded meshes on 3D polyhedral domains. Let us denote by
Q_{N}^{A}
the projection onto

S_{N}
in the inner product
B^{A}
whenever
A=A^{*}
and
A
satisfies the hypotheses of
Lemma
5.6
(this guarantees then that
B^{A}
defines an inner product
on
H_{D}^{1}(U_{0})
).

Proposition 5.9
.

We keep the notation and
assumptions of Lemma
5.6
and of Equation (
37
).
Let
A=A^{*}
and
Q_{N}^{A}
be the projection onto
S_{N}
in the inner product
B^{A}
. Then

\|u-Q_{N}^{A}u\|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}\leq C_{rate}(\gamma\eta_{U})^{-1/2}\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\dim(S_{N})^{-\mu}\|u\|_{\check{H}^{k+1}(U_{0})}\,.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{dist}_{(\,,\,)}
be the distance in some inner product
(\,,\,)

(or in some norm induced from an inner product).
We have

\gamma\eta_{U}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\gamma\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq B^{A}(u,u)\leq\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,.

Hence we have successively

\displaystyle\|u-Q_{N}^{A}u\|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma\eta_{U})^{-1/2}B^{A}(u-Q_{N}^{A}u,u-Q_{N}^{A}u)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\,=\,(\gamma\eta_{U})^{-1/2}\operatorname{dist}_{B^{A}}(u,S_{N})

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma\eta_{U})^{-1/2}\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\operatorname{dist}_{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{H^{1}}}(u,S_{N})

\displaystyle\,=\,(\gamma\eta_{U})^{-1/2}\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\|u-Q_{N}u\|_{H^{1}(U_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{rate}(\gamma\eta_{U})^{-1/2}\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\dim(S_{N})^{-\mu}\|u\|_{\check{H}^{k+1}(U_{0})}\,,

where the last inequality is by the assumption (
37
).
∎

We then obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.10
.

We use the assumptions and notation of Theorem
5.8
and the
condition (
37
). Then, there exists
C_{X,p}
such that, for all
F\in V_{k}^{-}
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\|(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F-Q_{N}(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F\|_{H^{1}}^{p}d\omega\ \leq\ C_{p,X}\dim(S_{N})^{-p\mu}\|F\|_{V_{k}^{-}}^{p}\,.

If
A_{\ell}=A_{\ell}^{*}
for all
\ell
, then there exists also

C_{X,p}^{FEM}
such that, for all
F\in V_{k}^{-}
,

\int_{\Omega}\|(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F-Q_{N}^{A(\omega)}(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F\|_{H^{1}}^{p}d\omega\ \leq\ C_{p,X}^{FEM}\dim(S_{N})^{-p\mu}\|F\|_{V_{k}^{-}}^{p}\,.

Proof.

Equation (
37
) for
u:=(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F

immediately gives:

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F-Q_{N}(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\dim(S_{N})^{-\mu}\|(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F\|_{\check{H}^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\dim(S_{N})^{-\mu}|||(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}|||_{k}\|F\|_{V_{k}^{-}}\,.

So we may just choose
C_{p,X}:=C^{p}\int_{\Omega}|||(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}|||_{k}^{p}\,d\omega
,
which is finite, by Theorem
5.8
. For the second estimate, we
proceed similarly, but using Proposition
5.9
instead of Equation (
37
)
and

C_{p,X}^{FEM}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}C^{p}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(\omega)^{-p/2}\|A(\omega)\|_{\check{W}^{k+1}}^{p/2}|||(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{A(\omega)})^{-1}F|||_{k}^{p}\,d\omega\,,

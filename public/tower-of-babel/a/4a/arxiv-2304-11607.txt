1.
Introduction

Let
{P_{n}}
denote the Pell sequence defined by the recurrence

P_{0}=0,\quad P_{1}=1,\quad P_{n}=2P_{n-1}+P_{n-2},\ n\geq 2.

Also, let
{Q_{n}}
denote the Pell-Lucas sequence defined by the recurrence

Q_{0}=2,\quad Q_{1}=2,\quad Q_{n}=2Q_{n-1}+Q_{n-2},\ n\geq 2.

Many properties of these sequences are well known. Let
\alpha=1+\sqrt{2}
and
\beta=1-\sqrt{2}
. Obviously,
2<\alpha<3
and
-1<\beta<0
. The explicit Binet’s formulas for
{P_{n}}
and

{Q_{n}}
are as follows

(1.1)

P_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{2\sqrt{2}},\quad Q_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n},\quad\textrm{for all }n\geq 0.

For
n\geq 1
, the inequalities

(1.2)

\alpha^{n-2}\leq P_{n}\leq\alpha^{n-1}\quad\textrm{and }\quad\alpha^{n-2}\leq Q_{n}<\alpha^{n+1}

hold.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
b\geq 2
be an integer. Let
N
be a positive integer and suppose
N
can be written as

N=a_{1}\cdot b^{d}+a_{2},

where
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are positive integers and
d
is the number of digits of
a_{2}
in base
b
. Then we will call the number
N
a
b
-concatenation of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
.

In this paper we will consider two Diophantine equations

(1.3)

\displaystyle P_{n}

\displaystyle=b^{d}P_{m}+Q_{k}

and

(1.4)

\displaystyle P_{n}

\displaystyle=b^{d}Q_{m}+P_{k},

and search for integer solutions
(b,n,m,k)
, such that
b\geq 2
,
n,m,k\geq 0
, where
d
is defined as in Definition
1.1
. The motivation for this work arises from the recent work of the authors of
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
. The main results are the following two theorems:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
b\geq 2
be an integer. Then, the Diophantine equation

P_{n}=b^{d}P_{m}+Q_{k}

has only finitely many solutions in nonegative integers
n,m,k
and
d
is the number of digits of
Q_{k}
in base
b
.
Namely, we have
n<5.746\cdot 10^{27}\log^{3}b.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
b\geq 2
be an integer. Then, the Diophantine equation

P_{n}=b^{d}Q_{m}+P_{k}

has only finitely many solutions in nonegative integers
n,m,k
and
d
is the number of digits in
P_{k}
in base
b
.
Namely, we have
n<4.7\cdot 10^{30}\log^{3}b.

For the proofs of the Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
we will follow the ideas of
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
. In fact, the proofs use Baker’s theory of lower bounds for nonzero linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers and the reduction method due to Baker and
Davenport, the version of Bravo, Gómez and Luca.

Some supporting results are as follows. We start with the height of an algebraic number. Let
\eta
be an algebraic number of degree
t,
let
a_{0}\neq 0
be the leading coefficient of its minimal polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}
and let
\eta=\eta^{(1)},\ldots,\eta^{(t)}
denote its conjugates. The logarithmic height of
\eta
is defined by

h(\eta)=\frac{1}{t}\left(\log|a_{0}|+\sum_{j=1}^{t}\log\max\left(1,\left|\eta^{(j)}\right|\right)\right).

\bullet
If
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are algebraic numbers and
j\in\mathbb{Z},
then we have

\displaystyle h(\eta_{1}\pm\eta_{2})

\displaystyle\leq h(\eta_{1})+h(\eta_{2})+\log 2,

\displaystyle h(\eta_{1}\eta_{2}^{\pm 1})

\displaystyle\leq h(\eta_{1})+h(\eta_{2}),

\displaystyle h(\eta_{1}^{j})

\displaystyle=|j|h(\eta_{1}).

\bullet
If
p
and
q
are integers such that
q>0
and
\gcd(p,q)=1,
then taking
\eta=p/q
the above definition reduces to
h(\eta)=\log(\max\{|p|,q\}).

.

(Theorem 9.4 of
[

\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}

Assume that

B\geq\max\{|b_{1}|,\dots,|b_{s}|\}.

If
\Lambda:=\gamma_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{s}^{b_{s}}-1\neq 0
, then

(1.5)

|\Lambda|\geq\exp(-1.4\cdot 30^{s+3}\cdot s^{4.5}\cdot D^{2}(1+\log D)(1+\log B)A_{1}\cdots A_{s}).

2.
The first equation
P_{n}=b^{d}P_{m}+Q_{k}

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

First, observe that for
m=0
we have
P_{m}=0
, so the equation (
1.3
) has the form

P_{n}=Q_{k}

which has only two trivial solutions
P_{2}=Q_{0}
and
P_{2}=Q_{1}
(it is proved in Section 4.2 in
[
1
]
). Hence, for any base
b\geq 2
, we only have two solutions when
m=0
. From now on we assume that
m\geq 1
.
As in
[
1
]
, from inequalities (
1.2
) we have,

(2.1)

(k-2)\frac{\log\alpha}{\log b}<d<1+(k+1)\frac{\log\alpha}{\log b}.

Also,

(2.2)

Q_{k}<b^{d}\leq b\cdot Q_{k}.

Now, by observing these inequalities and inserting them in (
1.3
) we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-2}\leq P_{n}=b^{d}P_{m}+Q_{k}

\displaystyle\leq bQ_{k}P_{m}+Q_{k}\leq(b+1)Q_{k}P_{m}

\displaystyle<(b+1)\alpha^{k+1}\alpha^{m-1}=(b+1)\alpha^{k+m}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\log_{\alpha}(b+1)+k+m}.

Hence, we have an upper bound for
n
in the terms of
m
,
k
and
b
:

(2.3)

n<m+k+2+\log_{\alpha}(b+1).

On the other hand, from the inequality

\alpha^{n-1}\geq P_{n}>Q_{k}P_{m}+Q_{k}>Q_{k}P_{m}\geq\alpha^{k+m-4}

we obtain a lower bound for
n
, namely

(2.4)

n>m+k-3.

Note that
Q_{k}=P_{k+1}+P_{k-1}
for
k\geq 1
. Assuming that
m\geq 1
, we can derive the following using
P_{m}\geq 1
and the inequality (
2.2
):

\displaystyle P_{n}

\displaystyle=b^{d}P_{m}+Q_{k}\geq b^{d}+Q_{k}>2Q_{k}

\displaystyle=2(P_{k+1}+P_{k-1})>P_{k+1}.

This implies
n>k+1
, i.e.

(2.5)

n-k\geq 2.

Now, let’s rewrite equation (
1.3
) by using (
1.1
). We have

\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{2\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\alpha^{m}}{2\sqrt{2}}b^{d}-\frac{\beta^{m}}{2\sqrt{2}}b^{d}+Q_{k}.

After rearranging and multiplying with
2\sqrt{2}/\alpha^{n}
we get

1-\frac{b^{d}}{\alpha^{n-m}}=\frac{\beta^{n}}{\alpha^{n}}-\frac{b^{d}\beta^{m}}{\alpha^{n}}+\frac{Q_{k}2\sqrt{2}}{\alpha^{n}}.

Since
|\beta|=1/\alpha
we have

\left|1-\frac{b^{d}}{\alpha^{n-m}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2n}}+\frac{b^{d}}{\alpha^{n+m}}+\frac{Q_{k}2\sqrt{2}}{\alpha^{n}}.

Now we use (
1.2
) and (
2.2
) and get

\displaystyle\left|1-\frac{b^{d}}{\alpha^{n-m}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2n}}+\frac{b\cdot Q_{k}}{\alpha^{n+m}}+\frac{Q_{k}2\sqrt{2}}{\alpha^{n}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\alpha^{2n}}+\frac{b\cdot\alpha^{k+1}}{\alpha^{n+m}}+\frac{\alpha^{k+1}2\sqrt{2}}{\alpha^{n}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\alpha^{2n}}+\frac{b\cdot\alpha}{\alpha^{n-k}}+\frac{\alpha 2\sqrt{2}}{\alpha^{n-k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+b\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{2}\alpha}{\alpha^{n-k}}.

For
b\geq 3
we have
1+b\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{2}\alpha<b\alpha^{2}
, and for
b=2
we have
1+b\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{2}\alpha=1+b\alpha^{2}
, so for
b\geq 2
we take

1+b\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{2}\alpha<1.1b\cdot\alpha^{2}.

This implies

(2.6)

\left|1-\frac{b^{d}}{\alpha^{n-m}}\right|<\frac{1.1b}{\alpha^{n-k-2}}.

In order to apply Theorem
1.4
, we define

(2.7)

|\Lambda_{1}|=|1-b^{d}\cdot\alpha^{-(n-m)}|.

Then

\gamma_{1}=b,\quad\gamma_{2}=\alpha,\quad b_{1}=d,\quad b_{2}=-(n-m).

Notice that we have
\Lambda_{1}\neq 0
. If we assume the contrary, that
\Lambda_{1}=0
, we would have
\alpha^{n-m}=b^{d}
. By taking norm in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
, we get
b^{2d}=\pm 1
which is impossible since
b\geq 2
.
Moreover, it is easy to show that
D=2
,
h(\gamma_{1})=\log b
and
h(\gamma_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\log\alpha
. So we take

A_{1}=2\log b,\quad A_{2}=\log\alpha.

After dividing equation (
1.3
) with
P_{m}
we get

b^{d}<b^{d}+\frac{Q_{k}}{P_{m}}=\frac{P_{n}}{P_{m}}\leq\frac{\alpha^{n-1}}{\alpha^{m-2}}=\alpha^{n-m+1}.

This implies

(2.8)

d<(n-m+1)\frac{\log\alpha}{\log b}\leq(n-m+1)\frac{\log\alpha}{\log 2}<1.3(n-m+1).

We now have

B=\max\{|b_{1}|,|b_{2}|\}=\max\{d,n-m\}<1.3(n-m+1).

Hence, we can take
B=1.3(n-m+1)
. Also,
s=2
, so Theorem
1.4
implies

|\Lambda_{1}|>\exp(-1.0427\cdot 10^{10}(1+\log(1.3(n-m+1)))\log b\cdot\log\alpha).

Combining this with (
2.6
) we get

(2.9)

n-k-2<1.043\cdot 10^{10}\log b\cdot(1+\log(1.3(n-m+1))).

Then, we can rewrite equation (
1.3
) in the form

\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{2\sqrt{2}}=b^{d}P_{m}+\alpha^{k}+\beta^{k}.

After rearranging we have

\alpha^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}-\alpha^{k-n}\right)-b^{d}P_{m}=\frac{\beta^{n}}{2\sqrt{2}}+\beta^{k},

and again we can get an upper bound for the absolute value of the right-hand side:

\left|\alpha^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}-\alpha^{k-n}\right)-b^{d}P_{m}\right|<\frac{|\beta^{n}|}{2\sqrt{2}}+|\beta^{k}|=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\alpha^{n}}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}}.

After dividing and some manipulations we get

(2.10)

\left|1-\frac{b^{d}P_{m}}{\alpha^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}-\alpha^{k-n}\right)}\right|<\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\alpha^{n}}+\dfrac{1}{\alpha^{k}}}{\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}-\alpha^{k-n}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\alpha^{n}}<\frac{5.885}{\alpha^{n}},

since
n-k\geq 2
,
n>m\geq 1
and
k\geq 0
.
We define

|\Lambda_{2}|=\left|1-\frac{b^{d}P_{m}}{\alpha^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}-\alpha^{k-n}\right)}\right|,

and apply Theorem
1.4
with parameters

\gamma_{1}=b,\quad\gamma_{2}=\alpha,\quad\gamma_{3}=\frac{P_{m}}{1/2\sqrt{2}-\alpha^{k-n}},

b_{1}=d,\quad b_{2}=-n,\quad b_{3}=1.

Note that if
|\Lambda_{2}|=0,
then

(2.11)

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}-2\sqrt{2}\alpha^{k}=2\sqrt{2}b^{d}P_{m}.

Moreover, the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}
is given by

\{id,\sigma\},

where we identify the automorphism
\sigma
to the permutation
(\alpha\beta).
Conjugating both sides of expression (
2.11
) with
\sigma
we obtain

\beta^{n}+2\sqrt{2}\beta^{k}=-2\sqrt{2}b^{d}P_{m}.

From this equality and using the fact that
n>k
and
\beta=-\alpha^{-1},
we get

2\sqrt{2}b^{d}\leq|\beta^{n}+2\sqrt{2}\beta^{k}|\leq\dfrac{\alpha^{k}+2\sqrt{2}\alpha^{n}}{\alpha^{n+k}}<\dfrac{1+2\sqrt{2}}{\alpha^{k}},

which is a contradiction because
k\geq 1
and
b\geq 2
. It follows that
\Lambda_{2}\neq 0.
Again, it is easy to show that all conditions of Theorem
1.4
are satisfied and
D=2
,
h(\gamma_{1})=\log b
,
h(\gamma_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\log\alpha.
Observe that

\displaystyle h(\gamma_{3})

\displaystyle\leq h(P_{m})+h\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}-\alpha^{k-n}\right)

\displaystyle<(m-1)\log\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\log 8+\frac{n-k}{2}\log\alpha+\log 2.

From (
2.4
) we have
m-1<n-k+2
, hence,

h(\gamma_{3})<\frac{3(n-k)+4}{2}\log\alpha+\frac{5}{2}\log 2.

We take

\displaystyle A_{1}=2\log b,\quad A_{2}=\log\alpha,

\displaystyle A_{3}=(3(n-k)+4)\log\alpha+5\log 2.

Also,
d<1.3(n-m+1)<1.3(n+1)
so

B\geq\max\{|b_{1}|,|b_{2}|,|b_{3}|\}=\max\{d,1,n\}<1.3(n+1)

and we can take
B=1.3(n+1)
. We also have
s=3
.
With these parameters, Theorem
1.4
together with (
2.10
) yields

(2.12)

n<1.94\cdot 10^{12}(1+\log(1.3(n+1)))[(3(n-k)+4)\log\alpha+5\log 2]\log b.

From (

) we have an estimate

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=(3(n-k)+4)\log\alpha+5\log 2=(3(n-k-2)+10)\log\alpha+5\log 2

\displaystyle<3.129\cdot 10^{10}(1+\log(1.3(n+1)))\log b\log\alpha+10\log\alpha+5\log 2

\displaystyle<2.8\cdot 10^{10}(1+\log(1.3(n+1)))\log b.

Substituting this expression into (

For

Since

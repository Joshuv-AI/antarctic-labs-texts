Section conjectures over ℂ \mathbb{C} and Kodaira fibrations
Simon Shuofeng Xu with an appendix by Seraphina Eun Bi Lee and Carlos A. Serván

Abstract
. In this paper we propose and study topological and Hodge theoretic analogues of Grothendieck’s section conjecture over the complex numbers. We study these questions in the context of family of curves, in particular Kodaira fibrations, and in the context of the family of Jacobians associated to a Kodaira fibration. We showed that in the case of family of curves, both the topological and Hodge-theoretic analogues of the injectivity part of the section conjecture holds, and that the topological analogue of the surjectivity part of the section conjecture does not hold in general for families of curves (proven in the appendix written by Lee and Serván) and families of Jacobians.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we would like to propose and study some analogues of Grothendieck’s section conjecture over the complex numbers. To explain these analogues, we first recall the anabelian philosophy which motivates the present work. In
[
Gro97
]
, Grothendieck conjectured that there exists a special class of schemes, called the anabelian schemes, defined over a field
k

\mathbb{Q}

(1.1)

1\to\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X_{\overline{k}})\to\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\to\Gal(\overline{k}/k)\to 1

Loosely speaking, this means that maps between anabelian schemes

Remark 1.0.1

Recall that to define the étale fundamental group of

Furthermore, Grothendieck conjectured that the class of anabelian schemes should satisfy the following properties (see
[
Fal98
]

(1)

it should contain all hyperbolic curves

(2)

it should contain the moduli stacks
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
of smooth projective curves of genus
g
with
n

(3)

it should be closed under taking fibrations, i.e., if
f:X\to Y
is a smooth proper map such that both
Y
and all the fibers
X_{y}
are also in this class, then so is
X
.

Now if one also believes that a point
\Spec k
is anabelian, then one arrives at the Grothendieck’s section conjecture for smooth proper curves of genus
g\geq 2

Conjecture 1.0.2

(Grothendieck’s section conjecture)
.

Let
X/k
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
over a field
k
that is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the section map

\text{sec}:\{k\text{-rational points of }X\}\to\{\text{splittings of }(\ref{etale SES})\}/\text{conjugation}

is a bijection.

Remark 1.0.3
.

One can see that the notion of anabelian schemes is supposed to be an algebraic analogue of
k(\pi,1)
-spaces in topology. Indeed, hyperbolic curves and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
are all
k(\pi,1)
-spaces, and if we have a Serre fibration
f:X\to Y
, where both
Y
and the fiber
F
are
k(\pi,1)
-spaces, then so is
X
. However, it’s worth pointing out that the analogy is not perfect and there are varieties which are
k(\pi,1)
spaces that are not anabelian (for example, elliptic curves over a field of characteristics
0
are not anabelian but they are still
k(\pi,1)
spaces).

The question we would like to explore in this paper is that if we now work over the complex numbers, is there a reasonable class of schemes, and some functorial invariant
F
like the étale fundamental group, so that the functor which sends such a scheme
X
to this functorial invariant
F(X)
is fully faithful. A naïve approach to this question is that we may simply take the class of anabelian schemes proposed by Grothendieck and see if one can find any interesting functorial invariant that can replace the étale fundamental group when we work over the complex numbers.

In this paper, we study two invariants that naturally occur in complex algebraic geometry: the first one is the topological fundamental group and the other one is the category of graded-polarizable admissible
\mathbb{Z}
-variation of mixed Hodge structures. Furthermore, we study them in the context of families of curves over another curve, in particular Kodaira fibrations, and the family of Jacobians associated to a Kodaira fibration. We first recall what a Kodaira fibration is.

Definition 1.0.4

(Kodaira fibration)
.

A
Kodaira fibration

f:X\to B
is a non-isotrivial fibration from a smooth projective surface
X
onto a smooth projective curve
B
such that all of the fibers are smooth projective of genus
g
.

Examples of such a fibration are first constructed by Kodaira in
[
Kod67
]
; see also the work of Parshin
[
Par68
]
and Atiyah
[
Ati69
]
. For a recent survey on Kodaira fibration, see
[
Cat17
]
. By an theorem in a paper of Kas which he attributed to Kodaira and Bers
[
Kas68
, Theorem 1.1]
, we know that given a Kodaira fibration
f:X\to B
, the genus of the base
B
is at least
2
and the genus of the fiber
X_{b}
is at least
3
. In particular, they should be examples of anabelian schemes, making them ideal testing grounds for anabelian conjectures. We can now formulate the questions we study in this paper:

Topological version of the question
. Given a Kodaira fibration
f:X\to B
, we have a short exact sequence of topological fundamental groups

(1.2)

1\to\pi_{1}(X_{b})\to\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(B)\to 1.

Now any algebraic section induces a splitting of this short exact sequence up to conjugation by
\pi_{1}(X_{b})
, so we get a section map

\sec_{\text{top}}:\{\text{algebraic sections of }f:X\to B\}\to\{\text{sections of (\ref{top. SES})}\}/\text{conjugation},

where the conjugation action is the natural action of
\pi_{1}(S_{b})
on the set of sections of (
1.2
) defined by

(g\cdot f)(x):=gf(x)g^{-1}\quad\quad\text{ for all }g\in\pi_{1}(X_{b}),x\in\pi_{1}(B),f\text{ splittings of (\ref{top. SES})}.

The following question is then a topological analogue of Grothendieck’s section conjecture (Conj.
1.0.2
).

Question 1.0.5
.

Is the section map
\sec_{\text{top}}
a bijection?

Now it’s worth pausing and asking why one should think that Question
1.0.5
may have a positive answer. Indeed, if we just consider the naïve topological analogue of Grothendieck’s section conjecture for smooth projective curves
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, we will not get an one-to-one correspondence: there are infinitely many maps from
\Spec\mathbb{C}
to
X
, but only one group theoretic map from
\{*\}=\pi_{1}(\Spec\mathbb{C})\to\pi_{1}(X)
. Therefore, the section map in this case is surjective but never injective.

1

One may repair this issue by working with
\pi_{0}(X(\mathbb{C}))
, and this approach is used in the formulation of the real section conjecture. See
[
Sti13
, Section 16.1]
for an overview.

However, notice that in this case,
\pi_{1}(\Spec\mathbb{C})
is trivial, and therefore
X\to\Spec\mathbb{C}
has trivial monodromy. On the other hand, in the case of a curve over a number field
k
, since
\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(k)=\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
, we do have non-trivial monodromy. Therefore, in some sense, Kodaira fibrations are closer analogues to curves over number fields than Riemann surfaces. Indeed, the analogue of Faltings’ theorem, known as the geometric Mordell conjecture holds for Kodaira fibrations
[
Man63
]
, i.e. any Kodaira fibration
f:X\to B
has only finitely many algebraic sections. Furthermore, one can give a proof of geometric Mordell conjecture using a strategy similar to Faltings’ original proof
[
McM00
]
. In particular, it proves a geometric Shafarevich conjecture
[
McM00
, Theorem 3.1]
, which states that for a given base
B
, there are only finitely many Kodaira fibrations
f:X\to B
with fiber genus
g(S_{b})=g
.

Perhaps the most relevant evidence is that the topological analogue of Grothendieck’s section conjecture holds for the universal curve
\mathcal{C}_{g}
over the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{g}
of genus
g
curves when
g\geq 2
: for
\mathcal{C}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
, the associated short exact sequence of topological fundamental group is the Birman short exact sequence

1\to\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\to\text{MCG}_{g,1}\to\text{MCG}_{g}\to 1,

where
\Sigma_{g}
is a smooth compact orientable Riemann surface of genus
g
, and
\text{MCG}_{g,n}
is the mapping class group of a genus
g
surface with
n
marked points. This short exact sequence is known to be a non-split sequence whenever
g\geq 2

[
FM12
, Corollary 5.11]
(as we will use later, it does not even virtually split, see
[
CS21
]
). Analogous results hold for the universal
n
-pointed curve as well
[
Che19
, Corollary 1.2]
. Therefore, it’s interesting to ask if Question
1.0.5
has a positive answer at least when the monodromy representation

\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})

associated to a Kodaira fibration
f:X\to B
has large image. One of the main results of this paper is that the non-injectivity phenomenon observed in the case of Riemann surfaces does not occur for Kodaira fibrations with large monodromy:

Theorem 1.0.6

(See Cor.
3.1.5
in Section
3
)
.

If
f:X\to B
is a Kodaira fibration whose monodromy representation
\rho
has no invariants, then
\sec_{\text{top}}
is injective.

The main strategy to prove this theorem is to study the associated family of Jacobians
\pi:\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
attached to a Kodaira fibration
f:X\to B
. One could similarly define an abelianized section map in this context and ask Question
1.0.5
in the case of family of Jacobians. Then the theorem above follows from the following one:

Theorem 1.0.7
.

Let
\pi:\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
be the family of Jacobians associated to a Kodaira fibration. Then if the monodromy representation associated to
f:X\to B
has no invariants, the abelianized section map is injective (see Cor.
3.1.4
).

In fact, this theorem is true for any abelian schemes over a curve, and we work in that generality at the beginning of section
3
.

On the other hand, it turns out that the surjectivity part of the topological section question is not true in general.

Proposition 1.0.8
.

(1)

Let
f:X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration with an algebraic section and
\pi:\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
is the associated family of Jacobians. Then the abelianized section map is never surjective (see Corollary
3.2.5
);

(2)

Let
f:X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration with an algebraic section. Then there exists a double cover
B^{\prime}\to B
such that the base change
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}:=X\times_{B}B^{\prime}\to B
is a Kodaira fibration for which the topological section map is not surjective (see Appendix
A
).

The second part of the proposition is due to Seraphina Lee and Carlos Serván who adapted their construction in
[
LS24
]
of holomorphic Lefschetz fibrations with infinitely many sections to this setting. We are very grateful to them for writing up a proof of the second part of this proposition as an appendix to this paper.

We also show that Lee and Serván’s construction in fact implies the existence of counterexamples to the surjectivity of the topological section map which have no algebraic sections. To explain this, we first introduce the weak topological section conjecture:

Conjecture 1.0.9

(Weak topological section conjecture)
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration. Then it admits an algebraic section if and only if the short exact sequence (
1.2
) of fundamental groups splits.

Next we relate the surjectivity of topological section maps for Kodaira fibrations to this weak topological section conjecture. More precisely, we prove the following:

Proposition 1.0.10

(see Cor.
4.0.9
)
.

The surjectivity of the section map
\sec_{\text{top}}
is equivalent to the weak topological section conjecture for all connected finite étale covers
X^{\prime}
of
X
such that
X^{\prime}\to B
has connected fibers (i.e.
X^{\prime}\to B
is also a Kodaira fibration).

Remark 1.0.11
.

This result also has an analogue in the arithmetic setting
[
Sti10
, Theorem 31]
, which states that the surjectivity part of Grothendieck’s section conjecture may be deduced from the weak section conjecture (i.e. existence of a rational points is the same as the existence of a splitting of the sequence
1.1
) for geometrically connected finite étale covers of the curve. In fact, the proof is almost the same as the proof of this analogous result in
[
Sti10
]
.

Then combining these two results, we see that

Corollary 1.0.12
.

There exists a Kodaira fibration with no algebraic section but whose associated short exact sequence of topological fundamental group splits.

Hodge theoretic version of the question
. Another possible candidate to replace the étale fundamental group is the category of graded-polarizable admissible
\mathbb{Z}
-variation of mixed Hodge structures
\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}
, as Hodge theory has always been an extremely useful tool in the study of complex algebraic geometry. The downside of this category is that it’s not a Tannakian category, and therefore, we cannot use it to define a Tannakian fundamental group. This means that we will lose the group theoretic aspect of anabelian geometry if we work with
\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Nevertheless, it still makes sense to ask if functors between these categories are in one-to-one correspondence with algebraic sections. In other words, given a map
f:X\to B
, we get a section map

\text{sec}:\{\text{algebraic sections to }f\}\to\{\text{section functors from }\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)\to\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(B)\},

where section functors are defined to be functors that becomes isomorphic to the identity functor after composing with the pullback
f^{*}
. Then the Hodge theoretic section question asks if this Hodge theoretic section map is a bijection. See section (
5
) for more detailed backgrounds and a more precise formulation of the question.

For this Hodge theoretic analogue, we prove the following injectivity result:

Theorem 1.0.13

(see Prop.
5.2.1
)
.

For any family of curves
f:X\to B
. The injectivity part of the Hodge theoretic section question holds, i.e. distinct algebraic sections produce non-isomorphic section functors.

It’s worth pointing out that this result does not make any assumption on the monodromy of the family. Some special cases of this theorem can be readily deduced from known results. When
B
is a point, this theorem follows from the Hain and Pulte’s pointed Torelli theorem (see
[
Pul88
, Theorem 5.5]
and
[
Hai87b
, Theorem 7.5]
). Hain, through private communication, also informed us that his proof of the main result in
[
Hai11
]
can be adapted to prove that this Hodge theoretic section map is a bijection in the case where
B=\mathcal{M}_{g}
for any
g>4
.

2.
Some construction of Kodaira fibrations

In this paper, we will prove theorems about Kodaira fibrations satisfying some extra hypotheses. In this section we would like to demonstrate that those theorems are non-empty, i.e., we give some standard constructions of Kodaira fibrations, which produce Kodaira fibrations satisifying those extra hypotheses. We also take this opportunity to set up some notations.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration and let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli stack of smooth projective curves of genus
g
. By the universal property of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, such a Kodaira fibration
f\colon X\to B
corresponds to some non-constant map
\varphi\colon B\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Therefore, to construct a Kodaira fibration, it is enough to construct complete curves inside
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. To avoid issues with stacks, we will work instead with the fine moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]
of genus
g
curves with fixed level
n\geq 3
structure. The following construction, which we called the moduli construction, is fairly well-known (see for example
[
Fla22
, Prop. 2.1]
).

Moduli construction
: Suppose
g\geq 4
and consider the Satake compactification
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]^{*}
, i.e., the closure of
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]
inside the Satake compactification
\mathcal{A}_{g}[n]^{*}
of
\mathcal{A}_{g}[n]
via the Torelli map
J\colon\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]\to\mathcal{A}_{g}[n]
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]^{*}
is projective, we may embed it into some large projective space and cut it with hyperplane sections and produce a curve. Now that when
g\geq 4
, the boundary component
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]^{*}-J(\mathcal{M}_{g}[n])
is of codimension at least
2
and the hyperelliptic locus
\mathcal{H}_{g}[n]
, where the Torelli map fails to be an immersion, is of codimension at least
2
. Hence, a curve obtained by cutting
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]^{*}
with hyperplane sections can avoid the boundary component as well as the hyperelliptic locus, and corresponds to a Kodaira fibration via the universal property of
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]
.

Lemma 2.0.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration constructed via the moduli construction explained above. Then the image of the monodromy representation

\rho\colon\pi_{1}(B)\to\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})

is of finite index inside
\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
and hence the monodromy action on
H^{1}(X_{b},\mathbb{Z})
has no invariants (i.e.
H^{0}(C,R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z})=0
).

Proof.

By Lefschetz’s hyerplane theorem for quasi-projective varieties
[
GM88
, page 153]
, the monodromy representation
\rho
factors through
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}[n])
via a surjection:

\pi_{1}(B)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}[n])\to\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}),

and by definition, the last map, which corresponds to the monodromy representation for the universal family over
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]
, surjects onto the kernel of
\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})\to\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, which certainly acts on
H^{1}(X_{b},\mathbb{Z})
with no invariants.
∎

Remark 2.0.2
.

For any Kodaira fibration
f:X\to B
, a standard argument using Torelli’s theorem (see
[
Fla22
, page 2]
) shows that the monodromy representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
always has infinite image.

Since we are interested in studying algebraic sections of Kodaira fibrations, it would be quite pointless if there are no Kodaira fibrations with algebraic sections. The following theorem of Bregman proves that that’s not the case, and that monodromy cannot obstruct the existence of an algebraic section.

Proposition 2.0.3
.

[
Bre21
, Prop. 4.2]

For every Kodaira fibration
f\colon X\to B
, there exists a Kodaira fibration
\tilde{f}\colon\tilde{X}\to\tilde{B}
with an algebraic section such that

(1)

The fibers of
f\colon X\to B
and
\tilde{f}\colon\tilde{X}\to\tilde{B}
are of same genus
g

(2)

The monodromy homomorphisms
\pi_{1}(B)\to\Mod_{g}
and
\pi_{1}(\tilde{B})\to\Mod_{g}
have the same image.

Sketch of a proof.

The idea is similar to that of the moduli construction. First embed
X
into some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Then a general hyperplane section
\tilde{B}
will be smooth and projective and admits a non-constant and hence finite map onto
C
. Then
\tilde{X}
can be constructed as the fiber product of
\tilde{B}
and
X
over
B
and the algebraic section comes from the identity map
\tilde{B}\to\tilde{B}
and the natural inclusion map
\tilde{B}\to S
. By the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, we know that
\pi_{1}(\tilde{B})
surjects onto
\pi_{1}(X)
and hence onto
\pi_{1}(B)
and the monodromy representation factors through this surjection. This proves the statement on the monodromy homomorphism.
∎

3.
Algebraic sections to family of Jacobians

3.1.
Family of Jacobians and injectivity

Results in this section holds more generally for any abelian schemes over a curve, so we first work in that generality. Let
p\colon\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme over a smooth projective curve
B
and let
q\colon\mathcal{A}^{\vee}=\Pic^{0}_{\mathcal{A}/B}\to B
be the associated dual abelian scheme. Associated to the map

1\to H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{A})\to\pi_{1}(B)\to 1.

Given an algebraic section
s\colon B\to\mathcal{A}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{0}(B,(\mathcal{A})^{\text{cont}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(B,(R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})^{\text{cont}})}=0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{\text{cont}}}

This shows that
\psi
factors through
\iota
. Therefore, it’s enough to construct an isomorphism
F
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{0}(B,(\mathcal{A})^{\text{cont}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(\pi_{1}(B),H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\sec_{\text{ab}})_{\text{cont}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{\text{cont}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

Furthermore, since
(R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})^{\text{cont}}
is a fine sheaf, we know
H^{1}(B,(R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}^{\text{cont}}))=0
and therefore the map
\psi_{\text{cont}}
is surjective. On the other hand, because
p\colon\mathcal{A}\to B
is a map of
K(\pi,1)
-spaces, we know that the map
(\sec_{\text{ab}})_{\text{cont}}
is also surjective. This suggests the following definition of the map
F
: for any cohomology class
[s]\in H^{1}(\pi_{1}(B),H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z}))
, represent it by a continuous section
s\colon B\to\mathcal{A}
, and define
F([s])=\psi_{\text{cont}}(s)
.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

The map
F
constructed as above is well-defined and is an isomorphism which makes the desired diagram commute.

Proof.

Note that by construction, if
F
is well-defined, it automatically makes the desired diagram commute, and is automatically a surjective group homomorphism. Since any surjection between free abelian groups of the same rank is automatically an isomorphism, it’s enough to show that
F
is well-defined.

To show that
F
is well-defined, we need to show that given any pair of continuous section
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
, if they are homotopic, then
\psi_{\text{cont}}(s_{1})=\psi_{\text{cont}}(s_{2})
, and if they induce conjugate group theoretic splittings, then we also have
\psi_{\text{cont}}(s_{1})=\psi_{\text{cont}}(s_{2})
. To this end, we need to use an explicit description of the map
\psi_{\text{cont}}
. It’s well-known that
H^{1}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))
parametrizes
R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)
-torsors on
B

[
Sta18
, Lemma 21.4.3]
, and by unwinding the proof of this fact (e.g. the one given in
[
Sta18
]
), we see that
\psi_{\text{cont}}
admits the following description: let
s\colon B\to\mathcal{A}
be a continuous section. Then
\psi_{\text{cont}}(s)
is the isomorphism class of
R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)
-torsor
\mathcal{F}\subset(R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})^{\text{cont}}
defined as the subsheaf of sections in
(R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})^{\text{cont}}
that maps to
s
via the surjection
R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{A}^{\vee}
.

Now suppose
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are homotopic continuous sections and let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
be the
R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)
-torsors they induce. Explicitly, over some trivializing open subset
U
, we know that
\mathcal{F}_{i}(U)
is the set of sections
s\colon U\to U\times H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b}^{\vee},\mathcal{O})
which after composing with the map
U\times H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b}^{\vee},\mathcal{O})\to U\times\mathcal{A}_{b}
becomes
s_{i}|_{U}
,
i=1,2
. Note that the map
U\times H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b}^{\vee},\mathcal{O})\to U\times\mathcal{A}_{b}
is a covering map and hence we may lift the homotopy between
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
to a unique homotopy between sections of
\mathcal{F}_{1}(U)
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}(U)
. In particular,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(U)\cong\mathcal{F}_{2}(U)
. Since the isomorphisms over each open is obtained by lifting the same homotopy between
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
, they must glue to an isomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cong\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

Finally, suppose that
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
induce conjugate group theoretic splittings. This means that they are conjugate via an element of
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})=H^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\vee},\mathbb{Z})
, and so they are related via the Deck transformation coming from
R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)
, and so by definition, the two torsors are isomorphic, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.1.2
.

The map
\sec_{\text{ab}}
is injective and/or surjective if and only if the map
\psi
is injective and/or surjective.

Using this corollary, we prove the following proposition:

Proposition 3.1.3
.

Let
p\colon\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme. Then the section map
\sec_{\text{ab}}\colon H^{0}(B,\mathcal{A})\to H^{1}(\pi_{1}(B),H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z}))
is injective if and only if the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(B)
on
H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})
has no invariant factors.

Proof.

First observe that since
\mathcal{A}_{b}
and
\mathcal{A}_{b}^{\vee}
are isogenous, the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(B)
on
H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})
having no invariants is the same as the monodromy action on
H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b}^{\vee},\mathbb{Z})
having no invariants.

Suppose the monodromy action has no invariants. Then by Corollary
3.1.2
, it’s enough to show that
H^{0}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})=0
. Consider the Higgs bundle associated to variation of Hodge structure
R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}
:

\mathcal{E}:=q_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}/B}\oplus R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{\theta}q_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}/B}\oplus R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}\oplus\omega_{B},

where the Higgs field
\theta
is defined by the following two maps

\displaystyle q_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}/B}\xrightarrow{\nabla}R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}\otimes\omega_{B}

\displaystyle R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{\text{zero map}}q_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}/B}\oplus R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}\otimes\omega_{B}

Here
\nabla
is induced by the Gauss-Manin connection on the flat bundle associated to
R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}
.

Now if
H^{0}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})\neq 0
, then
\mathcal{O}_{B}
maps into
R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O}
and hence
(\mathcal{O}_{B},0)
will be a sub-Higgs bundle of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
. On the other hand, by a theorem of Simpson
[
Sim91
, Theorem 1]
, we know that the
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
is polystable, and so
(\mathcal{O},0)
must be one of the irreducible factors of
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
and hence the trivial representation should appear as a sub-representation of the monodromy representation, contradicting the assumption that the monodromy has no invariants.

On the other hand, suppose that the map
\psi
is injective. Then we see that we must have an isomorphism between
H^{0}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))
and
H^{0}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathcal{O})
. However, the former is a discrete group and the latter is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space. Hence they are isomorphic if and only if both are
0
. In particular,
H^{0}(B,R^{1}q_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))=0
and the monodromy representation has no invariants.
∎

We can immediately deduce the following corollary for family of Jacobians associated to a Kodaira fibration:

Corollary 3.1.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration and
p\colon\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
be the corresponding family of Jacobians. Then

\sec_{\text{ab}}\colon H^{0}(X,\Pic^{0}_{X/B})\to H^{1}(\pi_{1}(B),H_{1}(X_{b},\mathbb{Z}))

is injective if and only if the associated monodromy action on
H^{1}(X_{b},\mathbb{Z})
has no invariants.

Furthermore, the abelian version of the section question is also related to the original topological section question for Kodaira fibrations:

Corollary 3.1.5
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration whose monodromy action on
H^{1}(X_{b},\mathbb{Z})
has no invariants, then the corresponding map

\{\text{algebraic sections to }f\colon X\to B\}\to\{\text{sections of (\ref{top. SES})}\}/\text{conjugation}

is injective.

Proof.

If
f\colon X\to B
has no sections, then the statement is trivially true so let’s assume that we have a fixed section
s_{0}\colon B\to X
. Then we may define a
B
-morphism
h\colon C\to\Pic^{0}_{X/B}
which maps
x\in X_{b}
to the divisor class
[s_{0}(f(x))-x]
. Note that
h
is injective, as it’s just the Abel-Jacobi map on each fiber.

This section also allows us to identify
\Pic^{0}_{X/B}
and
\Pic^{1}_{X/B}
and so we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(X_{b})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(B)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{1}(X_{b},\mathbb{Z})=\pi_{1}(X_{b})^{\text{ab}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(\Pic^{0}_{X/B})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(B)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

Let
s
and
s^{\prime}
be two distinct algebraic sections of
f\colon X\to B
. By post-composing with
h
and using the injectivity of
h
, we get two distinct sections
\tilde{s}
and
\tilde{s}^{\prime}
of
\pi\colon\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
. If
s
and
s^{\prime}
are conjugate via some element
g\in\pi_{1}(X_{b})
, then
\tilde{s}
and
\tilde{s}^{\prime}
must be conjugate via the image of
g
in
H_{1}(X_{b},\mathbb{Z})
, contradicting Corollary
3.1.4
. Hence, the map from algebraic sections to group theoretic splittings modulo conjugation is injective as desired.
∎

3.2.
Family of Jacobians and non-surjectivity

In this subsection, we come back to the case of family of Jacobians
p\colon\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
associated to a Kodaira fibration
f\colon X\to B
. Note that since Jacobians of curves are principally polarized, they are self-dual and therefore we will henceforth identify
p\colon\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
and
q\colon(\Pic^{0}_{X/B})^{\vee}\to B
. The goal of this subsection is to show that if
f\colon X\to B
admits an algebraic section, then the map
\sec_{\text{ab}}\colon H^{0}(B,\Pic^{0}_{X/B})\to H^{1}(\pi_{1}(B),H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z}))
is never surjective.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Suppose that
f\colon X\to B
is a Kodaira fibration with an algebraic section. Then the map
\sec_{\text{ab}}
is not surjective if and only if the map
\phi\colon H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
induced by the inclusion
R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)\to R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is non-zero.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.1.2
, we see that the map
\sec_{\text{ab}}
is surjective if and only if the map
H^{1}(B,R^{1}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{1}(B,R^{1}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Pic^{0}_{X/B}})
is non-zero. Now just like before, any algebraic section of
f\colon X\to B
induces an inclusion
h\colon X\to\Pic^{0}_{X/B}
and hence morphisms of sheaves
R^{1}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)\to R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)
and
R^{1}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Pic^{0}_{X/B}}\to R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. By checking locally, one can verify that these two morphisms of sheaves are in fact isomorphisms and therefore we can identify the map
H^{1}(B,R^{1}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{1}(B,R^{1}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Pic^{0}_{X/B}})
with the map
H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
as desired.
∎

Then it’s enough to understand the map
\phi\colon H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
. The first step is to compute the degree of the vector bundle
R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. This computation is probably known to experts but we could not find a reference:

Lemma 3.2.2
.

The vector bundle
R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is of negative degree.

Proof.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration such that the fiber
X_{b}
has genus
g
and the base
B
has genus
h
. By Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch, we know that

\ch(f_{!}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))=f_{*}(\ch(\mathcal{O}_{X})\cdot\td_{X/B}),

where
\ch
denotes the Chern character, and
\td_{X/B}
is the Todd class of the relative tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{X/B}
. Since we are in the relative curve setting, we know that the higher derived pushforward
R^{i}f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
vanishes for all
i\geq 2
, so we can rewrite the the left hand side of the equation to get

\displaystyle\ch(f_{!}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))

\displaystyle=\ch(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})-\ch(R^{1}f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))

\displaystyle=\ch(\mathcal{O}_{B})-\ch(R^{1}f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))

\displaystyle=1-(\rk(R^{1}f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))+c_{1}(R^{1}f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))+\dots).

On the other hand, since
\ch(\mathcal{O}_{X})=1
, we see that the right hand side is simply

\displaystyle f_{*}\td_{X/B}

\displaystyle=f_{*}(\td(\mathcal{T}_{X/B}))

\displaystyle=f_{*}\left(1+\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X/B})}{2}+\frac{c_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X/B})+c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X/B})}{12}+\dots\right).

As
\mathcal{T}_{X/B}
is a line bundle, we know that it has no higher Chern classes. It follows then

\deg R^{1}f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=c_{1}(R^{1}f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))=-f_{*}\left(\frac{c_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X/B})}{12}\right).

Thus, it’s enough to compute
f_{*}(c_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X/B}))
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{X/B}=\Omega_{X/B}^{\vee}
, we know that
c_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X/B})=c_{1}^{2}(\Omega_{X/B})
so we can work with the relative differential. Consider the following short exact sequence

0\to f^{*}\Omega_{B}\to\Omega_{X}\to\Omega_{X/B}\to 0.

By taking the wedge power, we get the following isomorphism

\wedge^{2}\Omega_{X}\cong f_{*}\Omega_{B}\otimes\Omega_{X/B}.

Since
c_{1}
is a group homomorphism, we know that
c_{1}(\wedge^{2}\Omega_{X})=c_{1}(f_{*}\Omega_{B})+c_{1}(\Omega_{X/B})
. Then

\displaystyle c_{1}(\Omega_{X/B})^{2}

\displaystyle=(c_{1}(\wedge^{2}\Omega_{X}))^{2}-2c_{1}(\wedge^{2}\Omega_{X})\cdot c_{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{B})+(c_{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{B}))^{2}

\displaystyle=K_{X}^{2}-2K_{X}\cdot c_{1}(f^{*}(K_{B}))+(c_{1}(f^{*}(K_{B}))^{2})

where
K_{X}
is a canonical divisor on
X
, and
K_{B}
is a canonical divisor on
B
. Now since
c_{1}
is functorial and
f^{*}
is a ring homomorphism, we know that

(c_{1}(f^{*}(K_{B})))^{2}=f^{*}(c_{1}(K_{B})^{2}).

Because
B
is a curve, this has to vanish. It follows then

(c_{1}(\Omega_{X/B}))^{2}=K_{X}^{2}-2K_{X}\cdot f^{*}K_{B}

and hence

f_{*}((c_{1}(\Omega_{X/B}))^{2})=f_{*}(K_{X}^{2})-2f_{*}(K_{X}\cdot f^{*}K_{B}).

Since we may compute degrees both before and after pushing-forward, we know that
f_{*}(K_{B}^{2})=K_{B}^{2}
. To understand the last term, we first use projection formula to write it as

2f_{*}(K_{X}\cdot f^{*}K_{B})=2f_{*}(K_{X})\cdot K_{B}.

Now
\deg f_{*}(K_{X})=K_{X}\cdot X_{b}
, and because
K_{X}\cdot X_{b}=\deg K_{X}|_{X_{b}}=\deg K_{X/B}|_{X_{b}}
for any generic fiber
X_{b}
, we see that
K_{X}\cdot X_{b}=2g-2
,. Now since
K_{B}
has degree
2h-2
, we see that

f_{*}((c_{1}(\Omega_{X/B}))^{2})=K_{X}^{2}-8(g-1)(h-1).

Hence, we have

\deg R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=c_{1}(R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})=\frac{-K^{2}_{X}+8(g-1)(h-1)}{12}=\frac{-K^{2}_{X}+2\chi_{X}}{12},

where
\chi_{X}
is the Euler characteristics of
X
. Finally, by the signature formula of Hirzebruch, Atiyah and Singer, we know that the signature
\sigma(X)
of
X
is precisely given by

\sigma(X)=\frac{1}{3}(K^{2}_{X}-2\chi_{X}).

Since Kodaira fibrations necessarily have positive signatures
[
Cat17
, Corollary 42]
, we may conclude that
\deg R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}<0
.
∎

Corollary 3.2.3
.

\dim H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})>3
.

Proof.

By Riemann-Roch, we know that

\dim H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})=-\left(\deg R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}+\rk(R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})(1-h)-\dim H^{0}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})\right).

Lemma
3.2.2
says that
-\deg R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}>0
. Furthermore, it’s well known (e.g.
[
Kas68
, Theorem 1.1]
) that the base curve of a Kodaira fibration has genus at least
2
and the fiber has genus at least
3
, so we know that

\dim H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})>\rk(R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})(h-1)\geq 3\cdot 1=3,

as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.2.4
.

The map
\phi\colon H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
is non-zero.

Proof.

Let
\Phi\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be the map induced by the exponential map
\mathbb{Z}(1)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
. We know that both
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}(1))
and
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
are equipped with compatible Leray filtrations and the map
\phi\colon H^{1}(X,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))\to H^{1}(X,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
is induced by
\Phi
between the associated graded pieces of the Leray filtrations. Because the map
f\colon X\to B
admits a section, the Leray filtration splits, and so we may view
H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))
(resp.
H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
) as a subgroup of
H^{2}(B,\mathbb{Z}(1))
(as a subspace of
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{X})
). As the map
\Phi
factors through
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
, we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}(1))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

Note that the map
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is the projection map induced by the Hodge decomposition on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
and in particular this map is surjective. Now as
H^{0}(B,R^{2}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})=H^{2}(B,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
, we know that
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
. It follows from Corollary
3.2.3
that
\dim H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})>3
. On the other hand, we also have
\dim H^{0}(B,R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))=\dim H^{2}(B,f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))=1
, so we see that
H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))
is a lattice inside
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
which generates a subspace of codimension
2
.Then for the projection map from
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
to be surjective, its restriction to
H^{1}(B,R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1))
must be non-zero and hence the map
\phi
must be non-zero.
∎

This proves the main claim of this subsection:

Corollary 3.2.5
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a Kodaira fibration with an algebraic section, and
p\colon\Pic^{0}_{X/B}\to B
. The map
\sec_{\text{ab}}
is never surjective for these families of Jacobians.

Remark 3.2.6
.

In the case where
f\colon X\to B
has an algebraic section, one may identify
\Pic^{0}_{X/B}
with
\Pic^{1}_{X/B}
, and hence our results shows that the topological section question has a negative answer for
\Pic^{1}_{X/B}\to B
in the case where the associated Kodaira fibration has a section. This in fact differs from the universal case: the universal family of moduli space of degree
1
line bundles
p^{\prime}\colon\Pic^{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}/\mathcal{M}_{g}}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
does satisfies the topological section question when
g\geq 3
in a trivial way, i.e., there is no topological section to
p^{\prime}
(this is first proven by Morita when
g\geq 9
; see
[
Mor86
, Corollary 3, Theorem 4]
. The strengthened result is proven in
[
LLSS23
, Theorem 5.1.11]
).

4.
Weak section conjecture and counterexample to surjectivity

In this section, we study the surjectivity of the topological section map
\sec_{\text{top}}
for Kodaira fibrations
f\colon X\to B
. We first adapt an argument of Stix
[
Sti10
, Theorem 31]
to show that just like in the arithmetic case, the surjectivity problem can be reduced to the case of Kodaira fibrations with no algebraic sections. We’ve chosen to highlight the main ingredients that make this strategy work so we will first work in a fairly general setting.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective map with connected fibers between smooth projective varieties. Assume that we have a short exact sequence of topological fundamental groups

(4.1)

1\to\pi_{1}(X_{b})\to\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(B)\to 1

where
X_{b}
is the fiber over
b\in B
. In this general setting, we have the following two claims:

Statement 4.0.1

(Weak topological section conjecture)
.

The map
X\to B
admits an algebraic section if and only if the associated short exact sequence of topological fundamental groups split.

Statement 4.0.2

(Surjectivity of top. section question)
.

The section map

\sec_{\text{top}}:\{\text{algebraic sections to }f:X\to B\}\to\{\text{splittings of (\ref{top. SES 2})}\}/\text{conjugation}

is surjective.

We prove the following proposition:

Proposition 4.0.3
.

Assume that

5.
Hodge theoretic section question and family of curves

In this section, we turn to the Hodge theoretic section question by replacing the topological fundamental group to a Hodge theoretic invarient, i.e. the category
\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}
, which is sensitive to the underlying algebraic structure of a given variety. First, we record some preliminary facts about this category and give a precise formulation of the Hodge theoretic section question. Next, we prove an injectivity for smooth projective families of curves of genus at least
1
.

5.1.
Preliminary and formulation of the question

Let
X
be a smooth connected variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)
be the category of admissible, graded-polarizable,
\mathbb{Z}
-variation of mixed Hodge structures (VMHS) over
X
. For a precise definition of such an object, see
[
Kas86
]
. Note that this is an abelian tensor category. The unit object in this category is the constant variation of mixed Hodge structure
\underline{\mathbb{Z}(0)}
and by trivial objects, we mean direct sums of the unit objects.

Suppose
X
and
Y
are two smooth complex varieties. Any functor
F\colon\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)\to\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(Y)
is assumed to be exact, additive
\otimes
-functor.

Remark 5.1.1
.

Admissibility is a technical condition on the behavior of a variation of mixed Hodge structure at infinity that will not play a role in the proof of our main injectivity result (Prop.
5.2.1
). The main point is that it ensures the variation of mixed Hodge structure to have some nice properties, which all variations of mixed Hodge structure of geometric origin have. Any VMHS that comes from geometry is admissible. If one wants to ignore this technical point, one may assume that all the spaces in this section are compact, in which case all graded-polarizable variation of
\mathbb{Z}
-mixed Hodge structures are automatically admissible.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective morphism with connected fibers between two smooth connected varieties, and let
f^{*}\colon\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(B)\to\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)
be the pullback functor induced by
f
. Note that it satisfies the conditions we listed above. Furthermore, if
s\colon B\to X
is an algebraic section to
f
, we get a functor
s^{*}\colon\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)\to\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(B)
such that
s^{*}\circ f^{*}=\id_{\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(B)}
. We can make a formal definition:

Definition 5.1.2
.

A functor
F\colon\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)\to\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(Y)
is a section to
f^{*}
if
F\circ f^{*}
is isomorphic to the identity functor on
\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(Y)
.

Now we formulate the Hodge theoretic section question:

Question 5.1.3

(Hodge theoretic section question)
.

(1)

(injectivity) If
s_{1},s_{2}
are two distinct algebraic sections to
f\colon X\to B
, can the functors
s_{1}^{*}
and
s_{2}^{*}
be isomorphic?

(2)

(surjectivity) Suppose that
F\colon\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)\to\VMHS_{\mathbb{Z}}(B)
is a functor which is a section to
f^{*}
. Then can we find an algebraic section
s\colon B\to X
such that
F
is isomorphic to
s^{*}
?

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{1}(B_{1})*_{\pi_{1}(S^{1})}\pi_{1}(B_{2}),

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{1}(X_{1})*_{\pi_{1}(S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g})}\pi_{1}(X_{2}).

Throughout this appendix, we identify
\pi_{1}(B_{i})
and
\pi_{1}(X_{i})
with their images in
\pi_{1}(B^{\prime})
and
\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
respectively induced by the inclusions
B_{i}\hookrightarrow B^{\prime}
and
X_{i}\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
for both
i=1,2
. These identifications are well-defined because the maps
\pi_{1}(B_{i})\to\pi_{1}(B^{\prime})
and
\pi_{1}(X_{i})\to\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
induced by these inclusions are injective for both
i=1,2
.

Let
s_{i}:B_{i}\to X_{i}
denote the restriction of
s:B\to X
to
B_{i}
for both
i=1,2
.

Definition A.0.1
.

For any
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
, let
s_{\gamma}:\pi_{1}(B^{\prime})\to\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
be defined

s_{\gamma}(\ell)=\begin{cases}(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)\in\pi_{1}(X_{1})&\text{ if }\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{1})\\
\gamma\cdot(s_{2})_{*}(\ell)\cdot\gamma^{-1}\in\pi_{1}(X_{2})&\text{ if }\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{2}).\end{cases}

Lemma A.0.2
.

For any
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
, the map
s_{\gamma}
is well-defined. Furthermore,
s_{\gamma}
is a section of
(f^{\prime})_{*}:\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})\to\pi_{1}(B^{\prime})
.

Proof.

To check that
s_{\gamma}
is well-defined, it suffices to show that

(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)=\gamma\cdot(s_{2})_{*}(\ell)\cdot\gamma^{-1}

for
\ell
a generator of
\pi_{1}(S^{1})=\pi_{1}(\partial D^{2})
. For both
i=1,2
, the image
s_{i}(\partial D^{2})
in
f^{-1}(\partial D^{2})\cong\Sigma_{g}\times S^{1}
bounds the disk
s(D^{2})
in
f^{-1}(D^{2})\cong\Sigma_{g}\times D^{2}
, and so

(s_{i})_{*}(\ell)=(1,\ell)\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\times\pi_{1}(S^{1})\cong\pi_{1}(f^{-1}(\partial D^{2})).

Therefore,
(s_{i})_{*}(\ell)
commutes with
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\leq\pi_{1}(f^{-1}(\partial D^{2}))
, and

(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)=(s_{2})_{*}(\ell)=\gamma\cdot(s_{2})_{*}(\ell)\cdot\gamma^{-1}.

For any
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{1})
or for any
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{2})
, compute that

(f^{\prime})_{*}\circ s_{\gamma}(\ell)=\begin{cases}f_{*}\circ(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)\in\pi_{1}(B_{1})&\text{ if }\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{1})\\
f_{*}(\gamma)\cdot f_{*}\circ(s_{2})_{*}(\ell)\cdot f_{*}(\gamma^{-1})\in\pi_{1}(B_{2})&\text{ if }\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{2}).\end{cases}

Note that
f_{*}\circ(s_{i})_{*}=\id_{\pi_{1}(B_{i})}
for both
i=1,2
, and that
f_{*}(\gamma)=1\in\pi_{1}(B_{i})
for any
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
. In particular,
(f^{\prime})_{*}\circ s_{\gamma}(\ell)=\ell
for any
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B^{\prime})
, and hence
s_{\gamma}
is a section of
(f^{\prime})_{*}
.
∎

The following lemma provides a criterion to determine whether two group-theoretic sections
s_{\gamma}
and
s_{\delta}
differ by conjugation by an element of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
.

Lemma A.0.3
.

If
s_{\gamma}
is conjugate to
s_{\delta}
by an element of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
then
[\delta]-[\gamma]
is contained in
H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})^{\pi_{1}(B)}
, the subgroup fixed by the monodromy action of the Kodaira fibration
f:X\to B
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists some
\beta\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
such that

s_{\gamma}(\ell)=\beta\cdot s_{\delta}(\ell)\cdot\beta^{-1}\in\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})

for all
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B^{\prime})
. Because the maps
\pi_{1}(X_{i})\to\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
for
i=1,2
induced by inclusions
X_{i}\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
are injective group homomorphisms, the following equalities also hold in
\pi_{1}(X_{1})
and
\pi_{1}(X_{2})
:

(A.1)

\displaystyle(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\beta\cdot(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)\cdot\beta^{-1}\in\pi_{1}(X_{1})

\displaystyle\text{ if }\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{1}),

(A.2)

\displaystyle\gamma\cdot(s_{2})_{*}(\ell)\cdot\gamma^{-1}

\displaystyle=\beta\cdot(\delta\cdot(s_{2})_{*}(\ell)\cdot\delta^{-1})\cdot\beta^{-1}\in\pi_{1}(X_{2})

\displaystyle\text{ if }\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{2}).

Recall that under the identifications
X_{1}=X_{2}
and
B_{1}=B_{2}
, the sections
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
agree. Therefore, (
A.2
) implies that

\displaystyle(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{-1}\cdot\beta\cdot(\delta\cdot(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)\cdot\delta^{-1})\cdot\beta^{-1}\cdot\gamma\in\pi_{1}(X_{1})

\displaystyle\text{ if }\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{1}).

Combining with (
A.1
), we obtain for all
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{1})

(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)=(\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta)\cdot(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)\cdot(\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta)^{-1}\in\pi_{1}(X_{1}),

i.e.
(\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta)\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
commutes with
(s_{1})_{*}(\ell)\in\pi_{1}(X_{1})
for all
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B_{1})
. Because the map
\pi_{1}(B_{1})\to\pi_{1}(B)
induced by the inclusion
B_{1}\hookrightarrow B
is surjective and because
s_{1}:B_{1}\to X_{1}
is the restriction of
s:B\to X
, it also holds that
(\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta)\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
commutes with
s_{*}(\ell)\in\pi_{1}(X)
for all
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B)
.

Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Aut(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z}))
denote the monodromy representation of
f:X\to B
. Then
\rho
can be described using the section
s:B\to X
in the following way. For any
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
and any
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B)
, the action of
\ell
on the homology class
[\alpha]\in H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})
is

\rho(\ell)([\alpha])=[\,\underbrace{s_{*}(\ell)^{-1}\cdot\alpha\cdot s_{*}(\ell)}_{\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(X)}\,]\in H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z}).

Therefore, for any
\ell\in\pi_{1}(B)
,

\rho(\ell)([\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta])=[\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta]

because
s_{*}(\ell)
commutes with
\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta
. In other words,
[\beta^{-1}\delta^{-1}\beta\gamma]\in H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})
is fixed under the action of
\pi_{1}(B)
, i.e.
[\beta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\beta\delta]=-[\gamma]+[\delta]
is contained in
H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})^{\pi_{1}(B)}
.
∎

We apply this criterion to finish the proof of Proposition
1.0.8
part (2).

Proof of Proposition
1.0.8
part (2).

For any
\gamma,\delta\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
, the associated sections
s_{\gamma}
and
s_{\delta}
are not conjugate by an
element of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
if
[\gamma]\neq[\delta]
as elements of the quotient
H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})/H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})^{\pi_{1}(B)}
by Lemma
A.0.3
. By (a dual version of) Remark
2.0.2
, the monodromy action
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Aut(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z}))
of
f:X\to B
is nontrivial. In other words, the quotient

H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})/H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})^{\pi_{1}(B)}

is an infinite free abelian group. Therefore, there exist infinitely many pairwise nonconjugate sections of the short exact sequence (
1.2
) of the form
s_{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
, and the map
\textrm{sec}_{\textrm{top}}
has infinite codomain. On the other hand, the geometric Mordell conjecture for Kodaira fibrations
[
Man63
]
says that any Kodaira fibration has finitely many algebraic sections, i.e. the map
\textrm{sec}_{\textrm{top}}
has finite domain. Therefore,
\textrm{sec}_{\textrm{top}}
is not surjective.
∎

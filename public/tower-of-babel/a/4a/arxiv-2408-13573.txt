Theory of perturbation of the magnetostatic field by an anisotropic magnetic toroid
Hamad M. Alkhoori

Abstract
The perturbation of a magnetostatic field by a toroid made of a homogeneous anisotropic magnetic material was formulated using the solutions of the Laplace equation in the toroidal coordinate system. That was straightforward in the region outside the toroid, but an affine coordinate transformation had to be employed inside the toroid. The coefficients of the series expansion of the perturbation potential in terms of appropriate toroidal basis functions were related to the coefficients of the series expansion of the source potential in terms of appropriate toroidal basis functions by a transition matrix. As a result of the solution of this novel problem, the consequences of material anisotropy on perturbing the magnetostatic field are clearly evident in the region near the toroid.



1
Introduction

In a source-free region, the magnetostatic problem requires solution of the
Gauss equation
\nabla\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})=0
and the Ampere equation

\nabla\times\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r})=\mathbf{0}
, where
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r})
is the magnetostatic field and
\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})
is the magnetostatic flux density. The Ampere equation allows the definition of a scalar magnetic potential
\Phi({\bf r})
through

\mathbf{H}({\bf r})=-\nabla\Phi({\bf r})\,.

(1)

Suppose that
\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})=\mu\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r})
in a certain region filled with a homogeneous isotropic medium
with permeability scalar
\mu
. Then, the scalar magnetic potential satisfies the Laplace equation

\nabla^{2}\Phi({\bf r})=0

(2)

in that region.

The Laplace equation can be solved by the method of separation of variables in thirteen three-dimensional coordinate systems: Cartesian, confocal paraboloidal, bispherical, toroidal, and
ten specializations of the confocal ellipsoidal system
[
1
,
2
]
. Accordingly, magnetostatic boundary-value problems can be solved, at least in principle, so long as the permeability scalar is piecewise uniform with discontinuities only across boundaries on which one of the three coordinates is invariant.

What if the permeability is not the scalar
\mu
in a certain region filled with a homogeneous medium, but the dyadic
\underline{\underline{\mu}}
? In other words, the medium is anisotropic
[
4
,
3
]
. Then we get

\nabla\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\left[\underline{\underline{\mu}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla\Phi({\bf r})\right]=0

(3)

in that region, so that
\Phi({\bf r})
is not a solution of the Laplace equation in that region.
The solutions of Eq. (
2
) can still be applied though an affine coordinate transformation.

For the sake of illustration, in this paper we have focused on the perturbation of a
magnetostatic field
\mathbf{H}_{\text{source}}

by a toroid made of a homogeneous anisotropic material. Perturbation by anisotropic magnetic toroids
does not appear to have been treated heretofore, despite the fact that magnetic
anisotropy is exhibited
by both crystalline materials
[
6
,
5
,
4
]

and effectively homogeneous composite materials
[
7
,
8
]
. Toroids made of isotropic magnetic materials
are commonplace as cores in transformers and recording heads
[
5
]
, and anisotropic magnetic toroids are expected
to perform better by delivering optimal spatial distributions of
\Phi({\bf r})
.

The plan of this paper is as follows. Section
2
presents the formulation of the boundary-value problem and the solution procedure. Section
3
provides and discusses illustrative numerical results. The paper concludes with
some remarks in Sec.
4
.
Throughout the paper, vectors are in boldface, unit vectors are decorated by carets, dyadics are double underlined, and column vectors as well as matrices are enclosed in square brackets.

Boundary-Value Problem

2.1
Toroidal coordinates

Figure

shows a toroid with mean radius
R_{\rm o}
and cross-sectional radius
r_{\rm o}
, with the constraint
R_{\rm o}>r_{\rm o}
. These two dimensions lead to the definition of the two constants

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}c=+\sqrt{R_{\rm o}^{2}-r_{\rm o}^{2}}\\[5.0pt]
a=\text{csch}^{-1}\left(r_{\rm o}/c\right)\end{array}\right\}

(4)

that are needed to define an appropriate toroidal coordinate system

Figure 1
:
Toroid with mean radius
R_{\rm o}
and cross-sectional radius
r_{\rm o}
.

Cartesian coordinates
\left\{x,y,z\right\}
are given in terms of toroidal coordinates
\left\{\xi,\eta,\phi\right\}
by
[
9
]

x=c\frac{\sinh\xi\cos\phi}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta},

(5a)

y=c\frac{\sinh\xi\sin\phi}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta},

(5b)

and

z=c\frac{\sin\eta}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}.

(5c)

Toroidal coordinates are given in terms of Cartesian coordinates as

\xi=\log\left[\frac{\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}+c\right)^{2}+z^{2}}{\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}-c\right)^{2}+z^{2}}\right]^{1/2},

(6a)

\eta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2cz}{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-c^{2}}\right),

(6b)

and

\phi=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right).

(6c)

The coordinate
\xi=a
is constant on the surface of the toroid, the interior of the toroid being denoted
by

V^{-}:\left\{\left(\xi,\eta,\phi\right)\,|\,\xi>a,\eta\in[0,2\pi),\phi\in[0,2\pi)\right\}

(7a)

and the exterior by

V^{+}:\left\{\left(\xi,\eta,\phi\right)\,|\,\xi<a,\eta\in[0,2\pi),\phi\in[0,2\pi)\right\}\,.

(7b)

2.2
Formulation

Let the toroid be made of an anisotropic magnetic material described by the constitutive relation

{\bf B}({\bf r})=\underline{\underline{\mu}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }{\bf H}({\bf r})=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\,\mu_{\rm r}\,\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }{\bf H}({\bf r})\,,\quad\xi>a\,,

(8a)

where
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
denotes the permeability of free space; the scalar
\mu_{\rm r}>0
; and
the diagonal dyadic
[
3
,
4
]

\underline{\underline{A}}=\alpha_{x}^{-1}\,\hat{{\bf x}}\hat{{\bf x}}+\alpha_{y}^{-1}\,\hat{{\bf y}}\hat{{\bf y}}+\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}}

(8b)

contains the scalars

\alpha_{x}>0
and
\alpha_{y}>0
.
The exterior region
V^{+}
is taken to be vacuous with constitutive relation

{\bf B}({\bf r})=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}{\bf H}({\bf r})\,,\quad\xi<a\,.

(9)

2.3
Potential in region
V^{+}

Equation (
2
) applies in
V^{+}
. Its solution in the toroidal coordinate system
yields
[
9
,
2
]

\Phi({\bf r})=\sum_{j\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{k\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r})+{\cal B}_{jk{\ell}n}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({\bf r})\right]\,,\quad\xi<a\,,

(10)

where the toroidal basis functions

\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r})=\sqrt{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\,Q_{n-\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell}(\cosh\xi)f_{kn}(\eta)f_{j\ell}(\phi)

(11a)

and

\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({\bf r})=\sqrt{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\,P_{n-\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell}(\cosh\xi)f_{kn}(\eta)f_{j\ell}(\phi)

(11b)

involve
the trigonometric functions

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}f_{e\bar{n}}(\psi)=\cos\left(\bar{n}\psi\right)\\[5.0pt]
f_{o\bar{n}}(\psi)=\sin\left(\bar{n}\psi\right)\end{array}\right\}\,.

(12)

The associated Legendre function of the first kind

P_{n-\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell}(\cosh\xi)=\frac{i^{\ell}\Gamma\left(n+{\ell}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\right)\tanh^{\ell}\xi}{2^{\ell}{\ell}!\Gamma\left(n-{\ell}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\right)\cosh^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\xi}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{{\ell}+n+\frac{1}{2}}{2},\frac{{\ell}+n+\frac{3}{2}}{2},{\ell}+1;\tanh^{2}\xi\right)

(13a)

and the associated Legendre function of the second kind

Q_{n-\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell}(\cosh\xi)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(n+{\ell}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\right)\tanh^{\ell}\xi}{\Gamma\left(n+1\right)2^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\cosh^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\xi}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{{\ell}+n+\frac{1}{2}}{2},\frac{{\ell}+n+\frac{3}{2}}{2},n+1;\text{sech}^{2}\xi\right)

(13b)

contain
i=\sqrt{-1}
,
\Gamma(\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} })
as the Gamma function, and
{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} },\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} },\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} };\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} })
as the hypergeometric function
[
9
,
10
]
.
The coefficients
{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}
are associated
with terms that are regular as
\xi\to 0

whereas the coefficients
{\cal B}_{jk{\ell}n}
are associated with terms
that are regular as
\xi\to\infty
.

On the right side of Eq. (
10
),

\Phi_{\rm source}({\bf r})=\sum_{j\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{k\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r})

(14)

is the source potential whereas

\Phi_{\rm pert}({\bf r})=\sum_{j\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{k\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\cal B}_{jk{\ell}n}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({\bf r})\,,\quad\xi<a\,,

(15)

is the perturbation potential. If the toroid
were to be absent, Eq. (
10
) would hold for all
{\bf r}
with
{\cal B}_{jk{\ell}n}\equiv 0

\forall\left\{{j,k,\ell,n}\right\}
.
We proceed with the assumption that the coefficients
{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}
are
known but the coefficients
{\cal B}_{jk{\ell}n}
are not. Furthermore, Eq. (
14
) is required to hold
in some sufficiently large region that contains the toroidal region
V^{-}
but not the region containing the source
of
\Phi_{\rm source}({\bf r})
.

2.4
Source potential

Sources of two types are considered for illustrative numerical results provided
in Sec.
3
: (i) a uniform-magnetostatic-field source and (ii) a point-magnetic-dipole source.

For the uniform-magnetostatic-field source, we have
\mathbf{H}_{\text{source}}=-U_{sx}\hat{{\bf x}}-U_{sy}\hat{{\bf y}}-U_{sz}\hat{{\bf z}}
, with
U_{sx}
,
U_{sy}
, and
U_{sz}
as constants, and hence the source potential is written as

\Phi_{\rm source}({\bf r})=U_{sx}x+U_{sy}y+U_{sz}z.

(16)

On transforming Eq. (
16
) to toroidal coordinates, we obtain

\Phi_{\rm source}({\bf r})=\frac{c}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\left(U_{sx}\sinh\xi\cos\phi+U_{sy}\sinh\xi\sin\phi+U_{sz}\sin\eta\right).

(17)

To find the coefficients
{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}
in Eq. (
14
) for the uniform-magnetostatic-field source, we equate Eqs. (
14
) and (
17
), multiply both sides by
\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r})\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right)
, set
\xi=a
, and integrate over
\eta\in[0,2\pi)
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
. This gives

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{\pi^{2}(1+\delta_{{\ell}0})(1+\delta_{n0})\left[Q_{n-1/2}^{\ell}(\cosh a)\right]^{2}}\times

(18)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\phi=0}^{2\pi}\int_{\eta=0}^{2\pi}\left(U_{sx}\sinh\xi\cos\phi+U_{sy}\sinh\xi\sin\phi+U_{sz}\sin\eta\right)\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r})\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right)^{2}\,\Biggr|_{\xi=a}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\phi\,,

where
\delta_{{\ell}{\ell}^{\prime}}
is the Kronecker delta.

For the point-magnetic-dipole source of magnetic moment
{\bf m}={m_{\rm sx}}\hat{{\bf x}}+{m_{\rm sy}}\hat{{\bf y}}+m_{\rm sz}\hat{{\bf z}}
, where
{m_{\rm sx}}
,
m_{\rm sy}
, and
{m_{\rm sz}}
are constants, located at
{{\bf r}}_{\rm o}\equiv(\xi_{\rm o},\eta_{\rm o},\phi_{\rm o})
, where
\xi_{\rm o}<a
,
\eta_{\rm o}\in[0,2\pi)
, and
\phi_{\rm o}\in[0,2\pi)
, the source coefficients can be obtained with the use of the bilinear expansion of the Green function in toroidal coordinates as
[
11
]

{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}=\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}{|\mathbf{m}|}}{4\pi^{2}c}\frac{\left(2-\delta_{{\ell}0}\right)\left(2-\delta_{n0}\right)(-1)^{m}\Gamma\left(n-{\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(n+{\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,\hat{{\bf m}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla_{\rm o}\left[\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({{\bf r}}_{\rm o})\right],

(19)

where
{|\mathbf{m}|}>0
and the unit vector
\hat{{\bf m}}={\bf m}/|\mathbf{m}|
defines the orientation of the point magnetic dipole.
See the Appendix for additional details.

2.5
Potential in region
V^{-}

Inside the magnetic toroid, the potential
\Phi({\bf r})
does not obey the Laplace equation;
instead,

\nabla\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\left[\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla\Phi({\bf r})\right]=0\,,\quad\xi>a\,.

(20)

In order to solve this equation, let us make an affine coordinate transformation from
{\bf r}

to
{\bf r}_{1}\equiv(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\phi_{1})
as follows
[
12
]
:

{\bf r}_{1}=\underline{\underline{A}}^{-1}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }{\bf r}\,.

(21)

Since
\alpha_{x}>0
and
\alpha_{y}>0
,
the bijective transformation (
21
) maps a sphere into an ellipsoid.
That ellipsoid’s principal axes are the same as
the Cartesian unit vectors affixed to the sphere.
In such a situation, the variables
(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\phi_{1})
are given by

\left.\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned} \xi_{1}({\bf r})&=\log\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{(\cosh\xi-\cos\eta)^{2}}+\left[1+\frac{\sinh\xi\sqrt{\alpha_{x}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\alpha_{y}^{2}\sin^{2}\phi}}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right]}{\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{(\cosh\xi-\cos\eta)^{2}}+\left[1-\frac{\sinh\xi\sqrt{\alpha_{x}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\alpha_{y}^{2}\sin^{2}\phi}}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right]}\right\}^{1/2}\\
\eta_{1}({\bf r})&=\tan^{-1}\left[\frac{2\sin\eta\,(\cosh\xi-\cos\eta)}{\sin^{2}\eta+\sinh^{2}\xi\,(\alpha_{x}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\alpha_{y}^{2}\sin^{2}\phi)-(\cosh\xi-\cos\eta)^{2}}\right]\\
\phi_{1}({\bf r})&=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha_{y}}{\alpha_{x}}\tan\phi\right)\end{aligned}\end{aligned}\right\}.

(22)

In consequence of Eq.(
21
), Eq. (
20
) is transformed to

\nabla_{1}^{2}\,\Phi({\bf r}_{1})=0\,,

(23)

which is the Laplace equation in the transformed space.
Its solution is given by
[
9
,
2
]

\Phi({\bf r}_{1})=\sum_{j\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{k\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[{\cal C}_{jk{\ell}n}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r}_{1})+{\cal D}_{jk{\ell}n}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({\bf r}_{1})\right]\,.

(24)

We must set
{\cal D}_{jk{\ell}n}\equiv 0
in order
to exclude terms on the right side
of Eq. (
24
) that are not regular as
\xi\to\infty
.

Accordingly, we define the potential inside the toroid as

\Phi_{\text{int}}({\bf r})=\sum_{j\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{k\in\left\{e,o\right\}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\cal C}_{jk{\ell}n}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r}_{1})\,,\quad\xi>a\,.

(25)

The unknown coefficients
{\cal C}_{jk{\ell}n}
have to be determined.

2.6
Boundary conditions

With the assumption of the interface
\xi=a
being current-free, the tangential component of the magnetostatic field must be continuous across the interface
\xi=a

[
3
]
. Therefore, the
potential must be continuous across both interfaces; hence,

\Phi_{\rm source}(\xi,\eta,\phi)+\Phi_{\rm pert}(\xi,\eta,\phi)=\Phi_{\text{int}}(\xi,\eta,\phi)\,,\quad\xi=a\,,\quad\eta\in[0,2\pi)\,,\quad\phi\in[0,2\pi)\,.

(26)

As the normal component of the
magnetostatic flux density must be continuous across the interface
\xi=a

[
3
]
, we get

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\upxi}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla\left[\Phi_{\rm source}(\xi,\eta,\phi)+\Phi_{\rm pert}(\xi,\eta,\phi)\right]=\mu_{\rm r}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\upxi}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla\Phi_{\text{int}}(\xi,\eta,\phi)\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\xi=a\,,\quad\eta\in[0,2\pi)\,,\quad\phi\in[0,2\pi)\,,

(27)

where the unit vector
\hat{\boldsymbol{\upxi}}=\displaystyle\frac{1-\cosh\xi\cos\eta}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}(\hat{{\bf x}}\cos\phi+\hat{{\bf y}}\sin\phi)-\hat{{\bf z}}\frac{\sinh\xi\sin\eta}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}
.

2.7
Transition Matrix

On multiplying both sides of Eqs. (
26
) and (
27
) by
\Psi_{\ell^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{1j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}({\bf r})\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right)
, setting
\xi=a
, and integrating over
\eta\in[0,2\pi)

and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
, the following sets of simultaneous algebraic equations are obtained:

\sum_{jk{\ell}n}\left({\cal I}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}+{\cal J}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}{\cal B}_{jk{\ell}n}\right)=\sum_{jk{\ell}n}{\cal K}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}{\cal C}_{jk{\ell}n},

(28)

and

\sum_{jk{\ell}n}\left({\cal L}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}{\cal A}_{jk{\ell}n}+{\cal M}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}{\cal B}_{jk{\ell}n}\right)=\sum_{jk{\ell}n}{\cal N}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}{\cal C}_{jk{\ell}n},

(29)

where

{\cal I}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}=\pi^{2}(1+\delta_{{\ell}0})(1+\delta_{n0})\left[Q_{n-1/2}^{\ell}(\cosh a)\right]^{2}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{{\ell}{\ell}^{\prime}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}},

(30)

{\cal J}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}=\pi^{2}(1+\delta_{{\ell}0})(1+\delta_{n0})P_{n-1/2}^{\ell}(\cosh a)Q_{n-1/2}^{\ell}(\cosh a)\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{{\ell}{\ell}^{\prime}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}},

(31)

{\cal K}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}=\int_{\phi=0}^{2\pi}\int_{\eta=0}^{2\pi}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r}_{1})\Psi_{\ell^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{1j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}({\bf r})\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right)\,\Biggr|_{\xi=a}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\phi\,,

(32)

{\cal L}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}=\int_{\phi=0}^{2\pi}\int_{\eta=0}^{2\pi}\hat{\boldsymbol{\upxi}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla\left[\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r})\right]\Psi_{\ell^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{1j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}({\bf r})\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right)\,\Biggr|_{\xi=a}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\phi\,,

(33)

{\cal M}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}=\int_{\phi=0}^{2\pi}\int_{\eta=0}^{2\pi}\hat{\boldsymbol{\upxi}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla\left[\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({\bf r})\right]\Psi_{\ell^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{1j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}({\bf r})\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right)\,\Biggr|_{\xi=a}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\phi\,,

(34)

and

{\cal N}_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}\ell\ell^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}=\mu_{\rm r}\int_{\phi=0}^{2\pi}\int_{\eta=0}^{2\pi}\hat{\boldsymbol{\upxi}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\underline{\underline{A}}\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\nabla\left[\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r}_{1})\right]\Psi_{\ell^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{1j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}({\bf r})\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\xi-\cos\eta}\right)\,\Biggr|_{\xi=a}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\phi\,.

(35)

After truncating the indexes
{\ell}
,
{\ell}^{\prime}
,
n
, and
n^{\prime}
so that only
{\ell}\in[0,N]
,
{\ell}^{\prime}\in[0,N]
,
n\in[0,N]
, and
n^{\prime}\in[0,N]
are considered with
N>0
,
Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
) can be put together in matrix form symbolically as

\left[{\cal I}\right]\,\left[{\cal A}\right]+\left[{\cal J}\right]\,\left[{\cal B}\right]=\left[{\cal K}\right]\,\left[{\cal C}\right]

(36)

and

\left[{\cal L}\right]\,\left[{\cal A}\right]+\left[{\cal M}\right]\,\left[{\cal B}\right]=\left[{\cal N}\right]\,\left[{\cal C}\right]\,.

(37)

The system of Eqs. (
36
) and (
37
) can be written as

\begin{bmatrix}\left[{\cal A}\right]\\
\left[{\cal B}\right]\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\left[{\cal S}\right]\\
\left[{\cal U}\right]\end{bmatrix}\left[{\cal C}\right],

(38)

where

\begin{bmatrix}\left[{\cal S}\right]\\
\left[{\cal U}\right]\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\left[{\cal I}\right]&&\left[{\cal J}\right]\\
\left[{\cal L}\right]&&\left[{\cal M}\right]\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\left[{\cal K}\right]\\
\left[{\cal N}\right]\end{bmatrix}.

(39)

Then, the column vector
\left[{\cal B}\right]
containing the coefficients in the truncated
series representing the perturbation potential is related to the column vector
\left[{\cal A}\right]

containing the coefficients in the truncated
series representing the source potential by

\left[{\cal B}\right]=\left[{\cal T}\right]\,\left[{\cal A}\right]\,.

(40)

In this equation,
the transition matrix
\left[{\cal T}\right]
, capturing the perturbational characteristics of the anisotropic magnetic toroid, is given by

\left[{\cal T}\right]=\left[{\cal U}\right]\left[{\cal S}\right]^{-1}

(41)

3
Illustrative Numerical Results and Discussion

3.1
Preliminaries

A Mathematica™ program was written to calculate the transition matrix
\left[{\cal T}\right]
of the anisotropic magnetic toroid
for a chosen truncation order
N
. The column vector
\left[{\cal B}\right]
containing
the perturbation-potential coefficients was then calculated using Eq. (
40
). Also, the column vector
\left[{\cal C}\right]
containing the
internal-potential coefficients was calculated using Eq. (
38
). Thereafter, the perturbation potential and the internal potential were calculated using Eqs. (
15
) and (
25
), respectively. Finally, the magnetic field everywhere was calculated using

\mathbf{H}(\xi,\eta,\phi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\nabla\left[\Phi_{\rm source}(\xi,\eta,\phi)+\Phi_{\rm pert}(\xi,\eta,\phi)\right],&\xi<a,\\[5.0pt]
-\nabla\Phi_{\text{int}}(\xi,\eta,\phi),&\xi>a.\end{array}\right.

(42)

Validation of the program was performed by solving independently the problem of a toroid composed of an isotropic magnetic material and implementing this solution in a separate Mathematica™ program. Comparison between the original program (for the anisotropic magnetic toroid) against the program for the isotropic magnetic toroid was done by setting in the original program
\alpha_{x}=\alpha_{y}=1
, with
a
being arbitrary. Excellent agreement in the results of the two programs was obtained for several different values of
\mu_{\rm r}
,
R_{\rm o}
, and
r_{\rm o}
. Furthermore, on the toroidal surface, the source-potential expansions for the cases of the uniform-magnetostatic-field source and the point-magnetic-dipole source were compared against their closed-form expressions and excellent agreements were obtained.

In general, the perturbation and internal potentials depend on the

•

mean radius
R_{\rm o}
and cross-sectional radius
r_{\rm o}
;

•

permeability
\mu_{\rm r}
;

•

anisotropy parameters
\alpha_{x}
and
\alpha_{y}
; and

•

source potential characteristics.

We chose

\mu_{\rm r}=\frac{3\,\mu_{\text{ave}}}{\alpha_{x}^{-2}+\alpha_{y}^{-2}+1},

4
Concluding Remarks

We have formulated a boundary-value problem to determine the perturbation of a magnetostatic field by a toroid made of a homogeneous anisotropic magnetic material. Whereas the solutions of the Laplace equation in the toroidal coordinate system were employed to express the total potential (and, therefore, the total magnetostatic field) as a series outside the toroid, a series of the toroidal solutions of the Laplace equation in a transformed space had to be used to express the potential inside the toroid. Standard boundary conditions were then enforced on the surface of the toroid, and their orthogonality properties on the toroidal surface were used to determine a transition matrix. This transition matrix relates
the perturbation-potential coefficients to the source-potential coefficients.
Illustrative numerical results show that anisotropy of the toroid material has significant effects on the magnetostatic field in the region near the toroid.

The closed-form free-space Green function

G({\bf r},{{\bf r}}_{\rm o})=\frac{1}{|{\bf r}-{{\bf r}}_{\rm o}|},

where

(46)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({\bf r})\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({{\bf r}}_{\rm o}),&\xi>\xi_{\rm o},\\[5.0pt]
\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{1jk}({{\bf r}}_{\rm o})\Psi_{{\ell}n}^{2jk}({\bf r}),&\xi<\xi_{\rm o}.\end{array}\right.

Real topological Hochschild homology of schemes
Jens Hornbostel, Doosung Park

Abstract
. We prove that real topological Hochschild homology THR \mathrm{THR} for schemes with involution satisfies base change and descent for the ℤ / 2 {\mathbb{Z}/2} -isovariant étale topology. As an application, we provide computations for the projective line (with and without involution) and the higher dimensional projective spaces.

1.
Introduction

Hochschild and cyclic homology and their refinements
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
have been extensively studied for many decades, both for their own sake and for their deep connections with algebraic
K
-theory via traces, see e.g.
[
11
]
. Standard textbook references include
[
40
]
and
[
19
]
. More recently, Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze
[
7
]
have introduced a filtration on
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
that is strongly related to integral
p
-adic Hodge theory. Hahn-Raksit-Wilson
[
25
]
have provided an alternative construction of the filtration that applies to commutative ring spectra satisfying certain assumptions.

Both topological and algebraic
K
-theory have real or hermitian refinements. This is also true for
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
, but the serious study of their real variants
\mathrm{THR}
and
\mathrm{TCR}
has just started, see e.g.
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
48
]
. These theories apply to very different branches in algebra and geometry: commutative rings, group rings, schemes, ring spectra, … all with or without a non-trivial involution. Concerning the
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-equivariant cyclotomic trace, we understand that there is work in progress by Harpaz, Nikolaus, and Shah studying this map in the very general setting of stable Poincaré
\infty
-categories. (When studying traces, beware of the difference between rings, which is what most algebraic
K
-theorists and algebraic geometers look at, and ring spectra, which are the input of
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
.)

The current article contributes to a better understanding of
\mathrm{THR}
in algebraic geometry, although some of our results also apply to other settings. Our first main result is the base change result in Theorem

3.2.3
for the isovariant étale topology. This then is one of the main ingredients in the proof of the following isovariant étale descent theorem for
\mathrm{THR}
.

Theorem 3.4.3
.

The presheaf

\mathrm{THR}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{sh}(\mathbf{Aff}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}},\mathrm{Sp}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}})

satisfies isovariant étale descent, where
\mathbf{Aff}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
denotes the category of affine schemes with involutions, and
\mathrm{Sp}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
denotes the
\infty
-category of
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-spectra.

In the non-equivariant case, similar results have been established for
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
by Weibel-Geller
[
60
]
and Geisser-Hesselholt
[
21
]
.

This descent theorem implies in particular that
\mathrm{THR}
satisfies the equivariant Zariski-Mayer-Vietoris
property for affine schemes with involutions. This can be used to extend
\mathrm{THR}
to non-affine schemes with involutions, see
Definition
3.4.6
, in a way that is compatible with existing definitions of
\mathrm{THH}
, see Proposition
3.4.7
.

Using this Zariski-Mayer-Vietoris theorem and various explicit computations of
\mathrm{THR}
of products of monoid rings and maps between them, we are able to compute
\mathrm{THR}(X)
for
X=\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{\sigma}
and more generally
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with trivial involution, see Theorems
5.1.2
,
5.1.4
and
5.2.6
.
Here
\mathbb{P}^{\sigma}
denotes the projective line with the involution switching the homogeneous coordinates.
We recall that Blumberg and Mandell
[
10
]
compute
\mathrm{THH}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
. For
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with the trivial involution, we obtain the following:

Theorem 5.2.6
.

For any separated scheme with involution
X
and integer
n\geq 0
, there is an equivalence of
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-spectra

\mathrm{THR}(X\times\mathbb{P}^{n})\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{THR}(X)\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}i_{*}\mathrm{THH}(X)&\text{if $n$ is even,}\\
\mathrm{THR}(X)\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}i_{*}\mathrm{THH}(X)\oplus\Sigma^{n(\sigma-1)}\mathrm{THR}(X)&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

For a
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-spectra
E
,
i_{*}E:=E\oplus E
with the obvious involution, see (
A.15
).
We refer to Remarks
2.0.1
,
5.1.1
, and
5.2.7
for a comparison with hermitian and real algebraic
K
-theory.

Recall that unlike algebraic and hermitian
K
-theory,
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{THR}
are not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant even on regular schemes. On the other hand,
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
do extend to log schemes, and using descent, trivial
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle formula, and computations on the log schemes
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
, can be shown to be representable in the log-variant of the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stable motivic Morel-Voevodsky homotopy category. This is done in the very recent joint work
[
8
]
of the second author with Federico Binda and Paul Arne Østvær.
We refer to
[
9
]
for an overview of their work.
The results of this article are expected to provide most of the necessary ingredients for showing
stable representability of
\mathrm{THR}
or at least its fixed points
\mathrm{THO}
in the corresponding equivariant log-homotopy categories. Furthermore, the work of Quigley-Shah
[
48
]
allows us to extend many results of this article including Theorems
3.4.3
and
5.2.6
to
\mathrm{TCR}
. The second author hopes to carry out motivic representability of
\mathrm{THR}
and
\mathrm{TCR}
in forthcoming work.

We conclude with a short overview of the different sections. Section
2
discusses some generalities about commutative ring spectra with involutions and their
\mathrm{THR}
.
This includes the study of several functors between
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-equivariant and non-equivariant categories, including the norm and equivariant notion of flatness.
The necessary background on
G
-stable equivariant homotopy theory for finite groups
G
, both
{\infty}
- and model categorical, is provided in Appendix
A
.
Section
3
reviews and extends several definitions for Grothendieck topologies on schemes with involutions, notably Thomason’s isovariant étale topology. Base change and descent for
\mathrm{THR}
are established in subsections
3.2
and
3.4
.
Note that the proof of étale base change in subsection
3.2
heavily relies on various results about Green functors established in subsections
A.4
and
A.5
.
Section
4
recollects and extends material on real and dihedral nerves, which is crucial when computing
\mathrm{THR}
of spherical monoid rings and of monoid rings over Eilenberg MacLane spectra, and where the monoids may have involutions. These monoid ring computations together with isovariant Zariski descent are then used in the computations for projective spaces in section

2.
Real topological Hochschild homology of rings with involutions

Remark 2.0.1
.

\mathrm{THR}
is to real algebraic
K
-theory
\mathbf{KR}
what
\mathrm{THH}
is to algebraic
K
-theory.
Hence we recall some recent results on
real algebraic
K
-theory

\mathbf{KR}
. This is a
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-equivariant motivic spectrum constructed in
[
34
]
and
[
12
]
. We recover hermitian
K
-theory when restricting
\mathbf{KR}
to schemes with trivial
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-action. (This is one incarnation of the philosophy “the fixed points of
\mathbf{KR}
are hermitian
K
-theory” in the world of presheaves on
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-schemes). Forgetting the action, we recover Voevodsky’s algebraic
K
-theory spectrum
KGL
. As observed in
[
61
, section 7.3]
, see also
[
12
]
, Schlichting’s techniques generalize to show that
\mathbf{KR}
is representable in
\mathcal{SH}^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}(k)
.
In particular,
\mathbf{KR}
satisfies equivariant Nisnevich descent. Here and below, following
[
12
]
and
[
34
]
, we consider
\mathbf{KR}
as a motivic spectrum with respect to the circle
T^{\rho}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\wedge\mathbb{P}^{\sigma}
, where
\rho
is the regular representation of
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
, and
\mathbb{P}^{\sigma}
denotes
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with involution switching the homogeneous coordinates. It might be more consistent with the notations for
\mathrm{THH}
to denote the (motivic) hermitian
K
-theory spectrum on schemes with involution by
\mathbf{KO}
, and to reserve the notation
\mathbf{KR}
for a (motivic) spectrum with involution whose fixed points are
\mathbf{KO}
. For further results on
\mathbf{KR}
and projective spaces we refer to Remarks
5.1.1
and
5.2.7
below.

Throughout this section, we fix morphisms of finite groupoids

(2.1)

\mathrm{pt}\xrightarrow{i}\mathrm{B}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\xrightarrow{p}\mathrm{pt},

where
\mathrm{B}G
denotes the finite groupoid consisting of a single set
*
with
\Hom_{\mathrm{B}G}(*,*):=G
.
According to section
A.2
, we often use the alternative notation

N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}=i_{\otimes},\;\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}=p_{\otimes},\;\iota=p^{*},\text{ and }(-)^{G}=p_{*}

instead of the notation in section
A.1
.
In particular, all functors in the sequel are
\infty
-functors, which admit lifts to Quillen functors between model categories.

We then have adjoint pairs

(2.2)

i^{*}:\mathrm{Sp}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Sp}:i_{*}\text{ and }\iota:\mathrm{Sp}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Sp}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:(-)^{G}

and functors

N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\text{ and }\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:\mathrm{Sp}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\to\mathrm{Sp}.

By
[
53
, Theorem 7.12]
, the pair of functors
(i^{*},\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}})
is conservative.
We will use this fact frequently.
We also have adjoint pairs

\displaystyle N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:\mathrm{CAlg}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:i^{*},\text{ }i^{*}:\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{CAlg}:i_{*},

\displaystyle\text{ and }\iota:\mathrm{CAlg}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{CAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:(-)^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}

and a colimit preserving functor

\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:\mathrm{CAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}.

We refer sections
A.1
and
A.2
for further properties of all these functors.

We now define real topological Hochschild homology for commutative ring spectra with involution.

2.1.
Definition of
\mathrm{THR}

Definition 2.1.1
.

Suppose
A\in\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
. For abbreviation, we set

A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}:=N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}i^{*}A.

Since
N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
is left adjoint to
i^{*}
, we have the counit map
A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\to A
.
We use this map to define

\mathrm{THR}(A):=A\wedge_{A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}A\in\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}},

which is called the
real topological Hochschild homology of
A
.
The first
\wedge
in the formulation of
\mathrm{THR}
is the pushout in
\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
.
Under a certain flatness condition, this is equivalent to the Bökstedt model of the real topological Hochschild homology, see
[
16
, Theorem, p. 65]
.
Note also that it is possible to define
\mathrm{THR}
for
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-spectra with slightly less structure, e.g. for
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}
-algebras as in
[
1
]
.

The map to the second smash factor in
A\wedge_{A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}A
gives a canonical map

(2.3)

A\to\mathrm{THR}(A).

In analogy with hermitian
K
-theory
\mathbf{KO}
and real algebraic
K
-theory
\mathbf{KR}
, we define

\mathrm{THO}(A)=(\mathrm{THR}(A))^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\simeq(A\wedge_{N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}i^{*}A}A)^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\in\mathrm{CAlg}

for
A\in\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
.
For
B\in\mathrm{NAlg}=\mathrm{CAlg}
, recall that the
topological Hochschild homology of
B
is defined to be

\mathrm{THH}(B):=B\wedge_{B\wedge B}B\in\mathrm{CAlg}.

which in turn generalizes the classical
[
40
, Proposition 1.1.13]
from rings to ring spectra.

Definition 2.1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category.
An object
X
of
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{B}({\mathbb{Z}/2}),\mathcal{C})
is called an
object of
\mathcal{C}
with involution
.
Explicitly,
X
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
equipped with an automorphism
w\colon X\to X
such that
w\circ w=\mathrm{id}
.

In particular, we have the notions of commutative rings with involutions, commutative monoids with involutions, etc.

We do not discuss definitions of
\mathrm{HR}(A)
refining
\mathrm{HH}(A)
and comparison results between
\mathrm{THR}(A)
and
\mathrm{HR}(A)
, similarly to e.g.
[
46
, Proposition IV.4.2]
, but see Remark
4.2.3
below.
The adjoint functors
\underline{\pi}_{0}
and
\mathrm{H}
, which are studied in the appendix, preserve many of the adjunctions we study, see e.g. Definition
2.3.2
.

Proposition 2.1.3
.

For
A\in\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
and
B\in\mathrm{CAlg}
, there exist canonical equivalences

\mathrm{THH}(i^{*}A)\simeq i^{*}\mathrm{THR}(A)\text{ and }\mathrm{THR}(N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}B)\simeq N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\mathrm{THH}(B).

Hence there exists a canonical equivalence

\mathrm{THR}(A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}})\simeq\mathrm{THR}(A)^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}.

In particular, the first equivalence implies
i^{*}\mathrm{THR}(\iota B)\simeq\mathrm{THH}(B)
, using (2) of Proposition
A.2.7
.

Proof.

Since
N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
and
i^{*}
preserve colimits, we have equivalences

\mathrm{THH}(i^{*}A)\simeq i^{*}A\wedge_{i^{*}A\wedge i^{*}A}i^{*}A\simeq i^{*}A\wedge_{i^{*}N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}i^{*}A}i^{*}A\simeq i^{*}\mathrm{THR}(A)

and

\mathrm{THR}(N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}B)\simeq N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}B\wedge_{N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}i^{*}N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}B}N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}B\simeq N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}(B\wedge_{B\wedge B}B)\simeq N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\mathrm{THH}(B)

by Proposition
A.2.7
(6).
∎

Proposition 2.1.4
.

For
B\in\mathrm{CAlg}
, there exists a canonical equivalence

(2.4)

\mathrm{THR}(i_{*}B)\xrightarrow{\simeq}i_{*}\mathrm{THH}(B).

Proof.

The composite

(2.5)

\mathrm{THR}(i_{*}B)\to i_{*}i^{*}\mathrm{THR}(i_{*}B)\xrightarrow{\simeq}i_{*}\mathrm{THH}(i^{*}i_{*}B)\to i_{*}\mathrm{THH}(B)

defines (
2.4
), where the first (resp. third) map is induced by the unit (resp. counit).
Proposition
2.1.3
shows that the induced map

i^{*}\mathrm{THR}(i_{*}B)\to i^{*}i_{*}\mathrm{THH}(B)

is an equivalence.
By
[
53
, Theorem 7.12]
(note that
i^{*}=\Phi^{e}
), it remains to show that the induced map

\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\mathrm{THR}(i_{*}B)\to\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}i_{*}\mathrm{THH}(B)

is an equivalence.
The right hand side is equivalent to
0
by Proposition
A.2.7
(3),(5).
On the other hand, we have equivalences

\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\mathrm{THR}(i_{*}B)\simeq\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}(i_{*}B)\wedge_{i^{*}i_{*}B}\Phi^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}(i_{*}B)\simeq 0\wedge_{i^{*}i_{*}B}0,

which is equivalent to
0
too.
∎

Applying
i^{*}
and Proposition
A.2.7
(2), we obtain
\mathrm{THO}(i_{*}B)\simeq\mathrm{THH}(B)
,
which compares nicely with the well-known
\mathbf{KO}(X\amalg X)\simeq K(X)
for schemes
X
, where the involution on
X\amalg X
switches the components.

Proposition 2.1.5
.

Let
R\to A,B
be maps in
\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
.
Then there exists a canonical equivalence

\mathrm{THR}(A)\wedge_{\mathrm{THR}(R)}\mathrm{THR}(B)\simeq\mathrm{THR}(A\wedge_{R}B).

Proof.

Both
N^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
and
i^{*}
preserve colimits.
Hence we obtain a canonical equivalence

A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\wedge_{R^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}B^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\simeq(A\wedge_{R}B)^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}.

On the other hand, there are canonical equivalences

\displaystyle\mathrm{THR}(A)\wedge_{\mathrm{THR}(R)}\mathrm{THR}(B)

\displaystyle\simeq(A\wedge_{A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}A)\wedge_{R\wedge_{R^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}R}(B\wedge_{B^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}B)

\displaystyle\simeq(A\wedge_{R}B)\wedge_{A^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\wedge_{R^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}B^{\wedge{\mathbb{Z}/2}}}(A\wedge_{R}B).

Combine the two equations to obtain the desired equivalence.
∎

2.2.
Mackey functors for
{\mathbb{Z}/2}

We refer to the appendix for a general discussion of Mackey and Green functors for finite groups
G
. We now restrict to the case
G={\mathbb{Z}/2}
.

Example 2.2.1
.

According to
[
53
, Example 4.38]
, a Mackey functor
C
for
G:={\mathbb{Z}/2}
can be described as a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(G/e)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(G/G),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tran}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}

where
C(G/e)
is an abelian group with an involution
w
,
C(G/G)
is an abelian group with the trivial involution,
\mathrm{res}
and
\mathrm{tran}
are homomorphisms of abelian groups with involutions, and the equality

\mathrm{res}\circ\mathrm{tran}=\mathrm{id}+w

is satisfied (i.e. the
double coset formula
holds).
A
morphism of Mackey functors
C\to D
is a diagram of abelian groups with involutions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(G/G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(G/G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(G/e)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(G/e)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tran}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tran}}

such that the horizontal homomorphisms commute with
\mathrm{tran}
and
\mathrm{res}
.

Example 2.2.2
.

If
M
is an abelian group with involution, then we can associate the Mackey functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tran}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}

where
\mathrm{tran}
maps
x\in M
to
x+w(x)
, and
\mathrm{res}
is the inclusion.
In this way, we obtain a fully faithful functor from the category of abelian groups with involutions
to the category of Mackey functors
\mathrm{Mack}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
.
We often regard an abelian group with involution as a Mackey functor if no confusion seems likely to arise.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Let
A
be a Green functor for
G:={\mathbb{Z}/2}
, and let
M
and
L
are
A
-modules.
If
L
is associated
(in the sense of the previous example)
with an abelian group with involution, then the induced map

(2.6)

\Hom_{\mathrm{Mod}_{A}}(M,L)\to\Hom_{\mathrm{Mod}_{A(G/e)}}(M(G/e),L(G/e))

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
f\colon M(G/e)\to L(G/e)
be a homomorphism of
A(G/e)
-modules.
Then the image of
f\circ\mathrm{res}
is in
L(G/e)^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
.
Hence there exists a unique homomorphism of
A(G/G)
-modules
g\colon M(G/G)\to L(G/G)
such that in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(G/G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L(G/G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(G/e)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L(G/e)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tran}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tran}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

the pair
(f,g)
commutes with
\mathrm{res}
.
We have

\mathrm{res}\circ g\circ\mathrm{tran}=f\circ\mathrm{res}\circ\mathrm{tran}=f\circ(\mathrm{id}+w)=(\mathrm{id}+w)\circ f=\mathrm{res}\circ\mathrm{tran}\circ f.

Since
\mathrm{res}
for
L
is injective, we deduce that the pair
(f,g)
commutes with
\mathrm{tran}
.
This constructs an inverse of (
2.6
).
∎

2.3.
Equivariant Eilenberg-MacLane spectra

In this subsection, we explain basic properties of equivariant Eilenberg-MacLane spectra.
We also explain how to define
\mathrm{THR}
of commutative rings.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category (not an
\infty
-category).
We have the functors

\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{B}({\mathbb{Z}/2}),\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}\mathcal{C}

induced by (
2.1
).
Let
(-)^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
denote the right adjoint of
\iota
if it exists.

Here, we give some examples.
For a commutative ring
A
,
\iota A
is the commutative ring
A
with the trivial involution.
For an
A
-module
M
,
\iota M
is the
\iota A
-module
M
with the trivial involution.
By abuse of notation we sometimes denote the constant Mackey functors by
\iota A
and
\iota M
as well.

For a commutative ring
B
with involution,
i^{*}B
is the commutative ring obtained by forgetting the involution, and
B^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
is the
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-fixed point ring. For a
B
-module
L
,
i^{*}L
is the
i^{*}B
-module obtained by forgetting the involution.

Definition 2.3.2
.

For an abelian group
M
with involution, we regard
M
as a Mackey functor, and take the functor (
A.25
) to obtain the equivariant Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum
\mathrm{H}M
.
There are canonical equivalences

(2.7)

i^{*}\mathrm{H}M\simeq\mathrm{H}i^{*}M\text{ and }(\mathrm{H}M)^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\simeq\mathrm{H}(M^{\mathbb{Z}/2}).

For a commutative ring
A
with involution, we can regard
\mathrm{H}A
as an object of
\mathrm{NAlg}_{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}
as explained in
[
53
, Example 11.12]
.

Note that for a given commutative ring
B
the commutative ring spectra
\mathrm{H}\iota B
and
\iota\mathrm{H}B
are quite different.
E.g., applying
\underline{\pi}_{0}
to the first one yields the constant Mackey functor associated with
B
whereas for the second one a tensor product over
\mathbb{Z}
with the Burnside ring Mackey functor of

We refer to

Throughout this section, we assume that

We are mostly interested in the case

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
x
be a point of a
G
-scheme

The

The

is defined to be

G_{x}:=\ker(S_{x}\to\mathrm{Aut}(k(x))).

Let

be an equivariant morphism of
G

We say that

(i)

is
(equivariant) étale
if its underlying morphism of schemes is étale,

(ii)

is an

is étale and surjective,

(iii)

is

(iv)

f
is an

is isovariant and an equivariant étale cover,

(v)

is a

if it is an étale cover and for every point

, there exists a point

such that

.

(vi)

is a

if

is an étale cover and for every point

such that

and

.

These covers define
equivariant étale, isovariant étale, fixed point étale, and equivariant Nisnevich topologies
on the category of
G
-schemes.

The isovariant étale topology
was first studied by Thomason
[

]
, see e.g.
[
26
, section 6.1]
. By

[
13
]
.

The discussion in
[
26
, p. 1223]
shows that the equivariant Nisnevich topology is coarser than the fixed point étale topology.
As observed in the proof of
[
26
, Corollary 6.6]
. the fixed point étale topology is equivalent to the isovariant étale topology.
Hence we have the following inclusions of topologies:

\displaystyle\text{(equivariant Nisnevich)}\subset

(fixed point étale)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\text{(isovariant \'{e}tale)}\subset\text{(equivariant \'{e}tale).}

3.4.
Isovariant étale descent

For an abelian group
M
, (
2.7
) gives a canonical equivalence

\mathrm{H}M\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\mathrm{H}\iota M)^{{\mathbb{Z}/2}}.

The last adjunction in (
2.2
) then yields a maps
\iota\mathrm{H}M\to\mathrm{H}\iota M
.
We have a similar map for a commutative ring
A
.

Lemma 3.4.1
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
M
a flat
A
-module.
Then there is an equivalence

\mathrm{H}\iota M\simeq\mathrm{H}\iota A\wedge_{\iota\mathrm{H}A}\iota\mathrm{H}M.

Proof.

By Proposition
A.3.6
,
\mathrm{H}
preserves filtered colimits.
The two functors
\iota
also preserve filtered colimits since they are left adjoints.
Every flat
A
-module is a filtered colimit of finitely generated free
A
-modules by Lazard’s theorem,
so we reduce to the case when
M
is a finitely generated free
A
-module.
In this case, the claim is clear.
∎

For a scheme
X
, let
\mathbf{\acute{E}t}\mathbf{Aff}/X
denote the category of affine schemes étale over
X
. We start by considering the affine case
X=\Spec(A)
.

Lemma 3.4.2
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and let
M
be an
\mathrm{H}A
-module.
The presheaf
\mathcal{F}
of spectra on
\mathbf{\acute{E}t}\mathbf{Aff}/\Spec(A)
given by

\mathcal{F}(\Spec(B)):=M\wedge_{\mathrm{H}A}\mathrm{H}B

for étale homomorphisms
A\to B
satisfies étale descent.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{H}A\to\mathrm{H}B
is flat in the sense of
[
42
, Definition 7.2.2.10]
,
we have an isomorphism

\pi_{q}\mathcal{F}(\Spec(B)))\simeq\pi_{q}(M)\otimes_{A}B

for all integers
q
.
In particular,
\pi_{q}\mathcal{F}
is a quasi-coherent sheaf on
\mathbf{\acute{E}t}\mathbf{Aff}/\Spec(A)
.

According to the paragraph preceding
[
21
, Proposition 3.1.2]
, there is a conditionally convergent spectral sequence

E_{2}^{st}:=H_{{\acute{e}t}}^{s}(X,a_{{\acute{e}t}}\pi_{-t}\mathcal{F})\Rightarrow\pi_{-s-t}L_{{\acute{e}t}}\mathcal{F}(X),

where
a_{{\acute{e}t}}
denotes the étale sheafification functor.
Since
\pi_{-t}\mathcal{F}
is a quasi-coherent sheaf for every integer
t
, the cohomology
H^{s}(X,a_{{\acute{e}t}}\pi_{-t}\mathcal{F})
vanishes for every integer
s\neq 0
.
It follows that
\pi_{-t}\mathcal{F}\to\pi_{-t}L_{{\acute{e}t}}\mathcal{F}
is an isomorphism for every integer
t
, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}
satisfies étale descent.
∎

Let
A
be a commutative ring with involution.
Let
\mathbf{iso\acute{E}tAff}/\Spec(A)
denote the category of affine schemes with involutions isovariant étale over

We only need to show that the restriction of

The functor

is an equivalence of sites by Proposition

Let

If we show that

The dihedral bar construction

4.1.
Crossed simplicial groups

Hochschild and cyclic homology and their real refinements are closely related to cyclic, real and dihedral nerves. We now present a uniform treatment of these constructions. The original references are
[
20
]
and
[
40
]
, parts of this are also explained e.g. in
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
.

Definition 4.1.1
.

(see
[
20
, Definition 1.1]
,
[
40
, chapter 6.3]
)
A
crossed simplicial group

G
is a sequence of groups
\{G_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
together with a category
\Delta G
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\Delta G
contains
\Delta
as a subcategory with the same objects.

(ii)

\mathrm{Aut}([n])
is the opposite group
G_{n}^{op}
.

(iii)

A morphism in
\Delta G
can be uniquely written as the composite
\alpha\circ g
for some
\alpha\in\Hom_{\Delta}([m],[n])
and
g\in G_{m}^{op}
.

Observe that every crossed simplicial group is a (or rather has an underlying) simplicial set, see
[
20
, Lemma 1.3]
.
A
G
-set
is a functor
\Delta G^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}
, which by restriction has an underlying simplicial set.

Construction 4.1.2
.

Let
X
be a simplicial set.
For a crossed simplicial group
G
, the
G
-set

F_{G}(X)
is defined in
[
20
, Definition 4.3]
.
In simplicial degree
q
, we have

F_{G}(X)_{q}:=G_{q}\times X_{q}.

The
G_{q}
action on
F_{G}(X)_{q}
is the left multiplication on
G_{q}
.
The faces and degeneracy maps are given by

(4.1)

d_{i}(g,x):=(d_{i}(g),d_{g^{-1}(i)}(x))\text{ and }s_{i}(g,x):=(s_{i}(g),s_{g^{-1}(i)}(x)).

According to
[
20
, Proposition 5.1]
, we have the projection
p_{1}\colon F_{G}(X)\to G
given by
p_{1}(g,x):=g
.
We also have the projection
p_{2}\colon\lvert F_{G}(X)\rvert\to\lvert X\rvert
given by

p_{2}[(g,x),u]:=[x,gu]

for
u\in\Delta_{top}^{q}
.
Furthermore, the two projections define a homeomorphism

\lvert F_{G}(X)\rvert\cong\lvert G\rvert\times\lvert X\rvert.

If
X
is a
G
-set, then we have the evaluation map
ev\colon F_{G}(X)\to X
given by

ev(g,x):=gx.

According to
[
20
, Theorem 5.3]
, the composite map

\lvert G\rvert\times\lvert X\rvert\xrightarrow{\cong}\lvert F_{G}(X)\rvert\xrightarrow{ev}\lvert X\rvert

defines a
\lvert G\rvert
-action on
\lvert X\rvert
.
The
G
-geometric realization of
X
is
\lvert X\rvert
with this
\lvert G\rvert
-action.

Example 4.1.3
.

We have the following three fundamental examples of crossed simplicial groups.

(1)
[
20
, Example 2, section 1.5]
introduces a crossed simplicial group
G
with
G_{n}:={\mathbb{Z}/2}
.
For this
G
, a
G
-set is called a
real simplicial set
.
The
G
-geometric realization is called the
real geometric realization
.

Explicitly, a real simplicial set
X
is a simplicial set equipped with isomorphisms
w_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n}
with
w_{n}^{2}=\mathrm{id}
for all integers
n\geq 1
satisfying the relations

d_{i}w_{n}=w_{n-1}d_{n-i}\text{ and }s_{i}w_{n}=w_{n+1}s_{n-i}

for
0\leq i\leq n
.

(2)
[
20
, Example 4, section 1.5]
introduces a crossed simplicial group
G
with
G_{n}:=C_{n+1}
.
For this
G
, a
G
-set is called a
cyclic set
, which was defined by Connes.
The
G
-geometric realization is called the
cyclic geometric realization
, and comes with an action of
S^{1}=SO(2)
(see also
[
40
, Theorem 7.1.4]
).

Explicitly, a cyclic set
X
is a simplicial set equipped with isomorphisms
t_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n}
with
(t_{n})^{n+1}=\mathrm{id}
for all integers
n\geq 1
satisfying the relations

d_{i}t_{n}=t_{n-1}d_{i-1}\text{ and }s_{i}t_{n}=t_{n+1}s_{i-1}

for
1\leq i\leq n
.

(3)
[
20
, Example 5, section 1.5]
introduces a crossed simplicial group
G
with
G_{n}:=D_{n+1}
, where
D_{n}
denotes the dihedral group of order
2n
.
For this
G
, a
G
-set is called a
dihedral set
.
The
G
-geometric realization is called the
dihedral geometric realization
.

Explicitly, a dihedral set
X
is a simplicial set equipped with
t_{n}
and
w_{n}
for all integers
n\geq 1
satisfying
w_{n}t_{n}=t_{n}^{-1}w_{n}
and all the above conditions for
t_{n}
and
w_{n}
.

There are obvious forgetful functors from dihedral to real and to cyclic sets.

For
\lvert G\rvert
, we obtain
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
,
S^{1}=U(1)=SO(2)
, and
O(2)
in the three examples above.

Example 4.1.4
.

Let
G
be the crossed simplicial set in Example
4.1.3
(1).
For a simplicial set
X
, the bijection
G\times X_{q}=X_{q}\amalg X_{q}
induces a canonical isomorphism of
G
-sets

F_{G}(X)\cong X\amalg X^{op},

where
w_{n}\colon X_{n}\amalg X_{n}^{op}\to X_{n}\amalg X_{n}^{op}
is the switching map.
If
X
is a real simplicial set, then the evaluation map

ev\colon F_{G}(X)\to X
sends
x\in X_{n}^{op}
to
w_{n}(x)
.
It follows that the
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-action on
\lvert X\rvert
is given by the composite map

\lvert X\rvert\xrightarrow{\cong}\lvert X^{op}\rvert\xrightarrow{w}\lvert X\rvert,

where the first map is the canonical identity.

Example 4.1.5
.

Let
G
be the crossed simplicial set in Example
4.1.3
(2).
For every integer
n\geq 0
, we set

\Lambda^{n}:=F_{G}(\Delta^{n}).

This is Connes’ cyclic
n
-complex.
We warn the reader that
F_{G}(X)
for a simplicial set is different from the cyclic bar construction.

4.2.
Real and dihedral nerves

We now recall how commutative monoids with involution give rise to real and dihedral simplicial sets, refining nerves and cyclic nerves for commutative monoids without involutions.

Definition 4.2.1
.

Let
M
be a commutative monoid with involution
\sigma
.
The
real nerve of
M
, denoted
\mathrm{N}^{\sigma}M
, is the real simplicial set whose underlying simplicial set is the nerve of
M
with
(\mathrm{N}^{\sigma}M)_{q}:=N^{\times q}
and
w
is given by

w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{q}):=(\sigma(x_{q}),\ldots,\sigma(x_{1}))

in simplicial degree
q
.
The
real bar construction
, denoted
\mathrm{B}^{\sigma}M
, is the real geometric realization of
\mathrm{N}^{\sigma}M
.
We refer to
[
17
, Example 2.1.1]
for a similar account.

Definition 4.2.2
.

Let
M
be a commutative monoid with involution
\sigma
.
The
dihedral nerve of
M
, denoted
\mathrm{N}^{\mathrm{di}}M
, is the dihedral set defined as follows.
In simplicial degree
q
, we have
(\mathrm{N}^{\mathrm{di}}M)_{q}:=M^{\times q+1}
.

A.5.

Definition A.5.1

.

If

is a morphism of Green functors and

is a flat

-module, then

-module.

Definition A.5.2
.

Recall from
[

, section 2]
that the

is defined to be the additive category whose objects are the finite

-sets and whose hom groups are given by

-sets
X
and

.

For a finite
G
-set

, let

denote the Mackey functor
\Hom_{\mathfrak{B}_{G}}(-,X)
.
As explained in
[
38
, p. 519]
, there is an isomorphism

M\Box B^{X}(Y)\cong M(Y\times X)

for all finite
G
-sets
X
and
Y
.

Proposition A.5.3
.

Let

be a Green functor.
Then an

-module

is projective if and only if

is a direct summand of a direct sum of

-modules of the form

.

Definition A.5.4
.

Suppose

.

-module

if the following two conditions are satisfied:

is a flat

-module,

(ii)

the induced map

.

.

Let

be a map in
\mathrm{CAlg}_{G}
, and let

The conditions (i) and (ii) in Definition

\underline{\pi}_{*}(A)
-module.

∎

.

Let

Immediate from the condition (ii) in Definition

.
∎

Proposition A.5.7
.

Let
A
be a Green functor, and let

and

.

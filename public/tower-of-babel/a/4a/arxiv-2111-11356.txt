Deformed 𝒘 𝟏 + ∞ \bm{w_{1+\infty}} Algebras in the Celestial CFT
Jorge MAGO, Lecheng REN, Akshay YELLESHPUR SRIKANT and Anastasia VOLOVICH

Abstract
We compute the modification of the w 1 + ∞ w_{1+\infty} algebra of soft graviton, gluon and scalar currents in the celestial CFT due to non-minimal couplings. We find that the Jacobi identity is satisfied only when the spectrum and couplings of the theory obey certain constraints. We comment on the similarities and essential differences of this algebra to W 1 + ∞ W_{1+\infty} .

Introduction

Symmetries have played a central role in shaping our understanding of fundamental physics. They often hint towards simpler or alternate formulations of theories. The Lorentz group
{\rm SO}(3,1)
of 4D flat spacetime acts as the conformal group
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
on the 2D celestial sphere at null infinity. Any theory living on the celestial sphere naturally inherits this conformal symmetry. S-matrix elements, typically evaluated in a basis of momentum eigenstates, can be rewritten in a basis of boost eigenstates via a Mellin transform (for massless particles)
[
36
,
37
,
38
]

[
4
,
35
,
46
]
for reviews).

Celestial CFTs have additional symmetries beyond the usual 2D conformal symmetry
which follow from leading and subleading soft gluon theorems and leading, subleading and subsubleading soft graviton theorems
(see
[
47
]
for a review).
Remarkably, these symmetries completely fix the leading celestial
operator product expansion (OPE) of conformal primary gluons and gravitons on the celestial sphere in terms of the Euler beta function
[
40
]
.
These OPEs have been also derived in
[
22
]

by Mellin transforming the collinear limits of amplitudes.
The Euler beta function has an infinite sequence of poles at integer conformal weights,
associated with conformally soft currents:
the first pole corresponds to the conformally soft current, the second
to the subleading soft current, and
so on.

3
Algebras

The OPEs obtained in the previous sections can be used to compute the commutators of the modes of soft currents. Following the suggestion in Appendix
A
, we have

\displaystyle H^{k,s}(z,\bar{z})=\overset{-\bar{h}}{\underset{\bar{m}=-\infty}{\sum}}\,\frac{1}{\bar{z}^{\bar{m}+\bar{h}}}H_{\bar{m}}^{k,s}(z).

(3.1)

The modes can also be extracted from the current by the contour integral

\displaystyle H^{k,s}_{\bar{m}}(z)=\underset{|\bar{z}|=\epsilon}{\oint}\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{z}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\bar{z}^{\bar{m}+\bar{h}-1}H^{k,s}(z,\bar{z}).

We define a 2D commutator as

\displaystyle\big[H_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{k_{1},s_{1}},H_{\bar{m}_{2}}^{k_{2},s_{2}}\big](z_{2})=\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\oint}\frac{{\rm d}z_{1}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\frac{{\rm d}\bar{z}_{1}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\bar{z}_{1}^{\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{h}_{1}-1}\frac{{\rm d}\bar{z}_{2}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\bar{z}_{2}^{\bar{m}_{2}+\bar{h}_{2}-1}H^{k_{1},s_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})H^{k_{2},s_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2}),

(3.2)

where the contour
\mathcal{C}
is defined by the conditions
z_{1}=z_{2}
,
|\bar{z}_{1}|=\epsilon
,
|\bar{z}_{2}|=\epsilon
with
\epsilon
infinitesimal. The OPE of soft currents with opposite spin involves both holomorphic and anti-holomorphic poles. The commutator defined above captures the residue on the holomorphic poles only. We can alternatively define a commutator which is sensitive to both sets of poles. However, this definition would lead to a violation of
the Jacobi identity as we will see
in Appendix
D
. A particularly striking consequence of the definition (
3.2
) is

\displaystyle\big[H_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{k_{1},s_{1}},H_{\bar{m}_{2}}^{k_{2},s_{2}}\big](z_{2})=0\qquad\text{if}\quad s_{1}<0,\ s_{2}<0,

since the OPE of two negative helicity soft currents only involves anti-holomorphic poles. The algebra of the modes of the soft currents is determined by the OPE (
2.3
) to be

\displaystyle\big[H_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{k_{1},s_{1}},H_{\bar{m}_{2}}^{k_{2},s_{2}}\big]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{p}\frac{\kappa_{s_{1},s_{2},-s_{I}}}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}-2\bar{h}_{1}-2\bar{h}_{2}-2p-\alpha\\
-2\bar{h}_{2}-p\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\times\oint\frac{{\rm d}\bar{z}_{1}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\frac{{\rm d}\bar{z}_{2}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\bar{z}_{1}^{\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{h}_{1}-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{\bar{m}_{2}+\bar{h}_{2}-1}\frac{(\bar{z}_{12})^{\alpha+p}}{\alpha!}\bar{\partial}_{2}^{\alpha}H^{k_{1}+k_{2}+p-1,s_{1}+s_{2}-p-1}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2}).

3.4
Simplest loop corrections and connection with the self-dual sector

One-loop corrections to gluon amplitudes are generically logarithmic and IR divergent. This poses a hurdle to defining a mode expansion of the soft factor. However, one-loop amplitudes involving only positive helicity gluons or only positive helicity gravitons
9
9

9

These amplitudes vanish at tree-level.
are purely algebraic and their effect on the algebra is easily determined. The one-loop all plus gluon amplitude
is
[
8
,
32
]

A_{n;1}^{[1]}\big({1}^{+},{2}^{+},\ldots,{n}^{+}\big)=-\frac{{\rm i}}{48\pi^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j<k<l\leq n}\frac{[ij]\langle jk\rangle[kl]\langle li\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle\langle 23\rangle{\cdots}\langle n1\rangle}.

A similar expression can be obtained for the corresponding one-loop graviton amplitude

12

w_{1+\infty}

This one-loop amplitude also happens to be the only non-zero amplitude in the self-dual sector of Yang–Mills theory. More precisely, the only non-zero amplitudes in self-dual Yang–Mills and self-dual gravity are the three-point tree-level amplitudes with two-positive and one-negative helicities, and the one-loop amplitudes with all positive helicities

16
]
. Consequently, we can only define collinear limits of positive helicity particles in the self-dual sector and the OPEs obtained from them agree with the OPEs of positive helicity particles obtained from the full theory. It would be interesting to analyze the effect of non-minimal couplings (obeying the self-duality condition) on the self-dual sector. Such a theory will naturally be free of anti-holomorphic poles and provides an alternate, and arguably more natural way to deform the

Processing as in the previous sections, we obtain results similar to (

Equations (

\displaystyle H^{k,s,a}=\sum_{\bar{m}=-\infty}^{-\bar{h}}H_{\bar{m}}^{k,s,a}(z)\frac{1}{\bar{z}^{\bar{m}+\bar{h}}}.

Appendix B
Gluon OPEs from double soft limits

In this section, we derive the soft current OPEs directly from
simultaneous
double soft limits of scattering amplitudes. We are mainly interested in the singular terms in the OPE and these correspond to
taking holomorphic and anti-holomorphic collinear limits on the simultaneous double soft limit factor of
n
-gluon amplitudes. We can expand this amplitude in a basis of color-ordered amplitudes as in equation (
A.1
). Collinear poles
\frac{1}{z_{12}}
or
\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{12}}
can only occur in the color-ordered amplitudes when particles 1 and 2 are adjacent. We will restrict our attention to the color-ordered amplitude with the canonical ordering. A similar result holds for the remaining relevant orderings.

The simultaneous double soft limit of two positive helicity gluons has been derived in
[
30
,
49
]
. Keeping only the singular term as
z_{1}\to z_{2}
, we have

\mathcal{A}_{n}\big(1^{+},2^{+},3,\dots,n\big)\xrightarrow{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}\frac{\langle n2\rangle}{\langle n1\rangle\langle 12\rangle}\exp\left(\frac{\langle n1\rangle}{\langle n2\rangle}\tilde{\lambda}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{2}}+\frac{\langle 12\rangle}{\langle n2\rangle}\tilde{\lambda}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{n}}\right)\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\big(2^{+},\dots,n\big).

We parametrize the spinors as in (
A.2
).
In the collinear limit,

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=\tau\omega_{p},\qquad\omega_{2}=(1-\tau)\,\omega_{p},\qquad z_{1}=z_{2}=z_{p}\qquad\bar{z}_{p}=\bar{z}_{2}+\tau\bar{z}_{12}.

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{2}+\frac{\langle n1\rangle}{\langle n2\rangle}\tilde{\lambda}_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{p}}{1-\tau}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\bar{z}_{p}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tau}}\tilde{\lambda}_{p},

(B.1)

which then gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}(1^{+},2^{+},3,\dots,n)\xrightarrow{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{12}}\frac{1}{\tau\,\omega_{p}}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\left(\left\{\sqrt{1-\tau}\lambda_{p},\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tau}}\tilde{\lambda}_{p}\right\},\dots,\big\{\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\big\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{12}}\frac{1}{\tau(1-\tau)\,\omega_{p}}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\big(\big\{\lambda_{p},\tilde{\lambda}_{p}\big\},\dots,\big\{\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\big\}\big).

Recalling that particle labelled by
p
is soft, we can perform a soft expansion of the amplitude to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}\big(1^{+},2^{+},3,\dots,n\big)\xrightarrow{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{z_{12}}\frac{1}{\tau(1-\tau)}\sum_{m=-1}^{\infty}\omega^{m}_{p}H^{m,1}\left(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2}+\tau\bar{z}_{12}\right)\mathcal{A}^{(m)},

(B.2)

where
\mathcal{A}^{(-1)}=\mathcal{A}^{(0)}=\mathcal{A}_{n-2}\left(3,\dots,n\right)
are universal and the rest are specific to the amplitude. We can get the OPE by performing a Mellin transform. Recall that the celestial amplitude, which is the correlation function of primary gluon operators is

\displaystyle\tilde{A_{n}}\big(1^{+},2^{+},3,\dots,n\big)

\displaystyle=\big\langle{\cal O}^{+,a_{1}}_{\Delta_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1}){\cal O}^{+,a_{2}}_{\Delta_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\cdots{\cal O}^{s_{n},a_{n}}_{\Delta_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\omega_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}\omega_{n}\,\omega_{1}^{\Delta_{1}-1}\cdots\omega_{n}^{\Delta_{n}-1}\mathcal{A}_{n}(1^{+},2^{+},3,\dots,n).

The double soft limit is

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon^{2}\big\langle{\cal O}^{+,a_{1}}_{k+\epsilon}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1}){\cal O}^{+,a_{2}}_{l+\epsilon}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\cdots{\cal O}^{s_{n},a_{n}}_{\Delta_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\big\rangle,\qquad k,l=1,0,-1,\dots.

Plugging in the double soft expansion from (
B.2
) and using the identity (
A.4
),
we get

\displaystyle H^{k_{1},+1,a_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})H^{k_{2},+1,a_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\sim-\frac{{\rm i}f^{a_{1}a_{2}b}}{z_{12}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\begin{pmatrix}2-k_{1}-k_{2}-n\\
1-k_{2}\end{pmatrix}\frac{\bar{z}_{12}^{n}}{n!}\bar{\partial}^{n}H^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1,+1,b}.

The simultaneous double soft limit in the mixed helicity case was also derived in
[
30
,
49
]
. The singular terms in the collinear limit are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\big(1^{+},2^{-},3,\dots,n\big)\xrightarrow{1\rightarrow 2}{}

\displaystyle\frac{\langle n2\rangle}{\langle n1\rangle\langle 12\rangle}\exp\left(\frac{\langle n1\rangle}{\langle n2\rangle}\tilde{\lambda}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{2}}\right)\mathcal{A}\big(2^{-},\dots,n\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{[13]}{[12][23]}\exp\left(\frac{[23]}{[13]}\lambda^{\alpha}_{2}\partial_{\lambda^{\alpha}_{1}}\right)\mathcal{A}\big(1^{+},\dots,n\big).

We treat the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic collinear limits separately. In the
z_{12}\rightarrow 0
case, we use (
B.1
) and find

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}\big(1^{+},2^{-},3,\dots,n\big)\xrightarrow{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{12}}\frac{1}{\tau\,\omega_{p}}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\left(\left\{\sqrt{1-\tau}\lambda_{p},\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tau}}\tilde{\lambda}_{p}\right\},\dots,\big\{\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\big\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{12}}\frac{1-\tau}{\tau\,\omega_{p}}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\big(\big\{\lambda_{p},\tilde{\lambda}_{p}\big\},\dots,\big\{\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\big\}\big).

The procedure for performing the Mellin transform is the same as in the
++
case, we just need to account for the difference in powers of
1-\tau
and that the soft particle
p
is now of negative helicity. We then find

\displaystyle\big\langle H^{k_{1},+1,a_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})H^{k_{2},-1,a_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\cdots{\cal O}^{s_{n},a_{n}}_{\Delta_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\big\rangle\xrightarrow{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle-{\rm i}f^{a_{1}a_{2}b}\frac{1}{z_{12}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\begin{pmatrix}-k_{1}-k_{2}-n\\
-1-k_{2}\end{pmatrix}\frac{\bar{z}_{12}^{n}}{n!}\bar{\partial}^{n}H^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1,-1,b}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)}.

To take the anti-holomorphic collinear limit we take the following parametrization

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=\tau\omega_{p},\qquad\omega_{2}=(1-\tau)\omega_{p},\qquad\bar{z}_{1}=\bar{z}_{2}=\bar{z}_{p},\qquad z_{p}=z_{2}+\tau z_{12},

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\frac{[23]}{[13]}\lambda_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{p}}{\tau}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z_{p}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\lambda_{p},

which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}\big(1^{+},2^{-},3,\dots,n\big)\xrightarrow{\bar{z}_{12}\rightarrow 0}{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{12}}\frac{1}{(1-\tau)\,\omega_{p}}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\lambda_{p},\sqrt{\tau}\tilde{\lambda}_{p}\right\},\dots,\{\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{12}}\frac{\tau}{(1-\tau)\omega_{p}}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\left(\{\lambda_{p},\tilde{\lambda}_{p}\},\dots,\{\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\}\right).

Taking the Mellin transform, we find

\displaystyle\big\langle H^{k,+1,a_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})H^{l,-1,a_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\cdots{\cal O}^{s_{n},a_{n}}_{\Delta_{n}}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\big\rangle\xrightarrow{\bar{z}_{12}\rightarrow 0}

\displaystyle-{\rm i}f^{a_{1}a_{2}b}\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{12}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\begin{pmatrix}-k-l-n\\
1-l\end{pmatrix}\frac{z_{12}^{n}}{n!}\partial^{n}H^{k+l-1,+1,b}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{(k+l-1)}.

The full OPE is then

\displaystyle H^{k,+1,a_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})H^{l,-1,a_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{{\rm i}f^{a_{1}a_{2}b}}{z_{12}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\begin{pmatrix}-k-l-n\\
-1-l\end{pmatrix}\frac{\bar{z}_{12}^{n}}{n!}\bar{\partial}^{n}H^{k+l-1,-1,b}

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm i}f^{a_{1}a_{2}b}}{\bar{z}_{12}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\begin{pmatrix}-k-l-n\\
1-l\end{pmatrix}\frac{z^{n}_{12}}{n!}\partial^{n}H^{k+l-1,+1,b}.

Appendix C
Examples for deriving constraints of coupling constants

In this appendix we show how we derive the constraint

3\kappa_{0,2,2}^{2}=10\kappa_{-2,2,2}\,\kappa_{2,2,2}

(C.1)

in (
3.8
). The other constraints can be derived similarly. Consider the double commutator (
3.7
). Take
s_{1}=s_{2}=s_{3}=2
. We then have

\displaystyle\big[\big[W_{\bar{m}_{1}}^{q_{1},2},W_{\bar{m}_{2}}^{q_{2},2}\big],W_{\bar{m}_{3}}^{q_{3},2}\big]+\text{cyclic}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{l=2,4,6}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=0\\
r\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{l}\frac{\kappa_{2,2,l-r-3}}{2}\frac{\kappa_{3-l+r,2,l-4}}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},l-r)N(q_{1}+q_{2}-l-1+r,q_{3},\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},r)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times W^{q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}-l-2,4-l}_{\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2}+\bar{m}_{3}}+\text{cyclic}.

Here we are interested in the coefficients of the generator
W^{q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}-8,-2}
on the right-hand side. These terms correspond to
l=6
, therefore
r
can only be 1, 3, 5 in the summation. In the end the total coefficient is

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1,3,5}\!\!\!\frac{\kappa_{2,2,3-r}}{2}\frac{\kappa_{r-3,2,2}}{2}N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},6-r)N(q_{1}+q_{2}-7+r,q_{3},\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},r)\!+\!\text{cyclic}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\kappa_{-2,2,2}\kappa_{2,2,2}}{4}\big(N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},1)N(q_{1}+q_{2}-2,\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},5)

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{\frac{\kappa_{-2,2,2}\kappa_{2,2,2}}{4}\Big(}{}+N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},5)N(q_{1}+q_{2}-6,q_{3},\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},1)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\frac{\kappa_{0,2,2}^{2}}{4}N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},3)\,N(q_{1}+q_{2}-4,q_{3},\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},3)+\text{cyclic}.

(C.2)

The
N

\displaystyle\frac{1}{5!}N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},1)\,N(q_{1}+q_{2}-2,\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},5)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{5!}N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},5)\,N(q_{1}+q_{2}-6,q_{3},\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},1)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3!^{2}}N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},3)\,N(q_{1}+q_{2}-4,q_{3},\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},3)+\text{cyclic}\ =\ 0.

Therefore, (

) becomes

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\kappa_{-2,2,2}\kappa_{2,2,2}}{4}-3!^{2}\,\frac{\kappa_{0,2,2}^{2}}{4\times 5!}\bigg)\big(N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},1)N(q_{1}+q_{2}-2,\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},5)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad{}+N(q_{1},q_{2},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{2},5)N(q_{1}+q_{2}-6,q_{3},\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2},\bar{m}_{3},1)\big)+\text{cyclic}=0.

For arbitrary

,

,
\bar{m}_{1}
,
\bar{m}_{2}
, the quadratics of

\kappa_{-2,2,2}\kappa_{2,2,2}=\frac{3!^{2}}{5!}\kappa_{0,2,2}^{2}=\frac{3\kappa_{0,2,2}^{2}}{10},

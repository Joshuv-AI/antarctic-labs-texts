h h -vectors of edge rings of odd-cycle compositions
Kieran Bhaskara, Akihiro Higashitani, Nayana Shibu Deepthi

Abstract
. Let 𝕂 ⁡ [ G ] \mathbb{K}[G] be the edge ring of a finite simple graph G G . Investigating properties of the h h -vector of 𝕂 ⁡ [ G ] \mathbb{K}[G] is of great interest in combinatorial commutative algebra. However, there are few families of graphs for which the h h -vector has been explicitly determined. In this paper, we compute the h h -vectors of a certain family of graphs that satisfy the odd-cycle condition, generalizing a result of the second and third named authors. As a corollary, we obtain a characterization of the graphs in this family whose edge rings are almost Gorenstein.

2.1.
Graph theory, toric ideals, edge rings, and simplicial complexes

A
finite simple graph
(or a
graph
)
G=(V(G),E(G))
consists
of a non-empty finite set

V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
, called the
vertices, and a finite set

E(G)=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{q}\}\subseteq\{\{u,v\}\mid u,v\in V(G),u\neq v\}
of distinct unordered pairs
of distinct elements of
V(G)
, called
the edges. We sometimes write
V
(resp.
E
) for
V(G)
(resp.
E(G)
) if
G
is clear from the context.
A graph
H=(V(H),E(H))
is said to be a
subgraph
of a graph
G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. In this case, we say that
G
contains
H
and write
H\subseteq G
.

A
walk
of
G
is a sequence of edges
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{m})
, where each
e_{i}=\{v_{i},w_{i}\}\in E
and
w_{i}=v_{i+1}
for each
i=1,\dots,m-1
.
Equivalently, a walk is a sequence of vertices
(u_{1},\dots,u_{m},u_{m+1})
such that

\{u_{i},u_{i+1}\}\in E
for all

i=1,\dots,m
. Here,
m
is referred to as the

length
of the walk. A walk is

even
if
m
is even, and it is
closed
if
u_{m+1}=u_{1}
. Two vertices
u
and
v
are said to be
connected
if there is a walk between them. A graph
G
is said to be
connected
if every two distinct vertices of
G
are connected.

A
cycle
of a graph
G
is a closed walk
(u_{1},\dots,u_{m},u_{m+1}=u_{1})
of vertices of
G
(with
m\geq 3
) such that the only vertices in the walk that are not pairwise distinct are
u_{1}
and
u_{m+1}
. A cycle of length
m
is called an

m
-cycle
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite simple graph with edge set
E=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{q}\}
(
q\geq 1
) and vertex set
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. Define a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:R=\mathbb{K}[e_{1},\ldots,e_{q}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]

. The

. The

of

Let

be a closed even walk of a graph

. We define a binomial

f_{\Gamma}^{(+)}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}x_{i_{2k-1}}\;\text{ and }\;f_{\Gamma}^{(-)}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}x_{i_{2k}}.

We say that

of

such that

divides
f_{\Gamma}^{(+)}
and
f_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{(-)}

. The following result shows how we may obtain a generating set for

Theorem 2.1

(
[
10
]
*Lemma 5.10)
.

The toric ideal
I_{G}
of a graph

Moreover, this generating set is a Gröbner basis for

with respect to any monomial order (e.g., see

A

4.

\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}}]

Let
R
be a normal Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous domain and
h(R)=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
be its
h
-vector. From
[
34
]
*Theorem 4.4, we know that
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
h(R)
is symmetric, i.e.,

h_{i}=h_{s-i}
for
i=0,1,\dots,s
.

The notion of almost Gorenstein homogeneous rings was given by Goto–Takahashi–Taniguchi in
[
8
]
, as a new class of graded rings between Cohen–Macaulay rings and Gorenstein rings.
For further studies on almost Gorenstein homogeneous rings, see, e.g.,
[
22
,
20
,
30
]
. Note that if
R
is Gorenstein, then it is almost Gorenstein.
We now characterize the almost Gorensteinness of our edge ring
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}}]
.

From
[
22
]
, let us recall a necessary and sufficient condition for a homogeneous domain to be almost Gorenstein.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
22
]
*Corollary 2.7)
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous domain of dimension
d
over a field
\mathbb{K}
and let
h(R)=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
be its
h
-vector.
Then
R
is almost Gorenstein if and only if the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\operatorname{type}{(R)}-1=\sum_{i=0}^{s}((h_{s}+\cdots+h_{s-i})-(h_{0}+\cdots+h_{i}))=:\tilde{e}(R),

where
\operatorname{type}{(R)}
denotes the Cohen–Macaulay type of
R
.

It is well known that
\operatorname{type}{(R)}
coincides with the number of elements in the minimal system of generators of the canonical module of
R
. In the case of
\mathfrak{g}_{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}}
, we have the following.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
37
]
*Theorem 5.7)
.

For any graph
\mathfrak{g}_{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}}
consisting of
n=\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}
cycles,
\operatorname{type}{(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}}])}=n-1.

Now we are in the position to provide a proof for the second main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

As before, let
\mathfrak{g}_{n}\coloneqq\mathfrak{g}_{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}}
.
We know that for
n=1
, the edge ring
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}]
is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in
2N+1
variables.
For the case
n=2
, the toric ideal
I_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}
has only one generator and therefore the corresponding edge ring is a hypersurface.
Thus, the edge ring
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}]
is Gorenstein for both
n=1,2
; which implies that
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}]
is almost Gorenstein.

Now, let
n\geq 3
.
From Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
, we have that
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}]
is almost Gorenstein if and only if
\tilde{e}(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}])=n-2
.
Let
h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}])=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
be the
h
-vector of
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}]
and let
h_{i}^{\prime}=(h_{s}+\cdots+h_{s-i})-(h_{0}+\cdots+h_{i})
, for
i=0,1,\dots,s
.
From the proof of
[
22
]
*Proposition 2.4, we have

(4.1)

\sum_{i=0}^{s}h_{i}^{\prime}t^{i}=\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{i=0}^{s}(h_{s-i}-h_{i})t^{i}.

From Theorem
1.1
, we have
s=\deg h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}];t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}jr_{j}=N
.
For
f(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}t^{i}
, we have
t^{s}f(t^{-1})=\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{s-i}t^{i}
.
Applying this to (
4.1
), we can express

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{s}h_{i}^{\prime}t^{i}=\frac{t^{N}h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}];t^{-1})-h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}];t)}{1-t}.

By (
1.1
), we have

t^{N}h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}];t^{-1})=t^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{-j})^{r_{j}}-t^{N-1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{-(j-1)})^{r_{j}}.

For
N=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}jr_{j}
, we can express
N-1
as
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}(j-1)r_{j}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}-1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle t^{N}h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}];t^{-1})

\displaystyle=t^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{-j})^{r_{j}}-t^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}t^{(j-1)r_{j}}\big(1+\dots+t^{-(j-1)}\big)^{r_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j})^{r_{j}}-t^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j-1})^{r_{j}}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle t^{N}h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}];t^{-1})-h(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}];t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j})^{r_{j}}-t^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j-1})^{r_{j}}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt-\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j})^{r_{j}}+t\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j-1})^{r_{j}}

\displaystyle=t(1-t^{n-2})\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j-1})^{r_{j}}.

Consequently, (
4.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{s}h_{i}^{\prime}t^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{t(1-t^{n-2})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j-1})^{r_{j}}}{1-t}

\displaystyle=t(1+t+\cdots+t^{n-3})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}(1+\dots+t^{j-1})^{r_{j}}.

We see that (
4.1
) evaluated at
t=1
gives
\tilde{e}(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}])
.
Thus,
\tilde{e}(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}])=(n-2)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}j^{r_{j}}
.
Recall that for
n\geq 3
, the edge ring
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}]
is almost Gorenstein if and only if
\tilde{e}(\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}])=n-2
.
That is when
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}j^{r_{j}}=1
. This occurs exclusively when
r_{j}=0
for all
j>1
.
In other words, every cycle in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is a
3
-cycle (
N=n
).
∎

Effects of nonmagnetic impurities and subgap states on the kinetic inductance, complex conductivity, quality factor and depairing current density
Takayuki Kubo

Abstract
We investigate how a combination of a nonmagnetic-impurity scattering rate γ \gamma and finite subgap states parametrized by Dynes Γ \Gamma affects various physical quantities relevant to to superconducting devices: kinetic inductance L k L_{k} , complex conductivity σ \sigma , surface resistance R s R_{s} , quality factor Q Q , and depairing current density J d J_{d} . All the calculations are based on the Eilenberger formalism of the BCS theory. We assume the device materials are extreme type-II s s -wave superconductors. It is well known that the optimum impurity concentration ( γ / Δ 0 ∼ 1 \gamma/\Delta_{0}\sim 1 ) minimizes R s R_{s} . Here, Δ 0 \Delta_{0} is the pair potential for the idealized ( Γ → 0 \Gamma\to 0 ) superconductor for the temperature T → 0 T\to 0 . We find the optimum Γ \Gamma can also reduce R s R_{s} by one order of magnitude for a clean superconductor ( γ / Δ 0 < 1 \gamma/\Delta_{0}<1 ) and a few tens % \% for a dirty superconductor ( γ / Δ 0 > 1 \gamma/\Delta_{0}>1 ). Also, we find a nearly-ideal ( Γ / Δ 0 ≪ 1 \Gamma/\Delta_{0}\ll 1 ) clean-limit superconductor exhibits a frequency-independent R s R_{s} for a broad range of frequency ω \omega , which can significantly improve Q Q of a very compact cavity with a few tens of GHz frequency. As Γ \Gamma or γ \gamma increases, the plateau disappears, and R s R_{s} obeys the ω 2 \omega^{2} dependence. The subgap-state-induced residual surface resistance R res R_{\rm res} is also studied, which can be detected by an SRF-grade high- Q Q 3D resonator. We calculate L k ​ ( γ , Γ , T ) L_{k}(\gamma,\Gamma,T) and J d ​ ( γ , Γ , T ) J_{d}(\gamma,\Gamma,T) , which are monotonic increasing and decreasing functions of ( γ , Γ , T ) (\gamma,\Gamma,T) , respectively. Measurements of ( γ , Γ ) (\gamma,\Gamma) of device materials can give helpful information on engineering ( γ , Γ ) (\gamma,\Gamma) via materials processing, by which it would be possible to improve Q Q , engineer L k L_{k} , and ameliorate J d J_{d} .

II.1
Eilenberger equation

The normal (
g_{m}
) and anomalous (
f_{m}
) quasiclassical Matsubara Green’s functions obey the Eilenberger equation
1968_Eilenberger
;
Kopnin
.
Since we consider the case
\lambda\gg\xi
,
the current distribution varies slowly over the coherence length,
and then the spatial-differentiation terms are negligible.
The Eilenberger equation for the current-carrying state reduces to

\displaystyle\biggl[i\frac{\pi s}{2}\cos\theta+(\hbar\omega_{m}+\Gamma)\biggr]f_{m}-\Delta g_{m}

\displaystyle=\gamma\bigl\{\langle f_{m}\rangle g_{m}-\langle g_{m}\rangle f_{m}\bigr\}.

(1)

Here,

g_{m}^{2}+f_{m}^{2}=1
,

\hbar\omega_{m}=2\pi k_{B}T(m+1/2)
is the Matsubara frequency,

\Gamma
is the Dynes parameter,

\gamma=\hbar/2\tau_{\rm imp}
is the nonmagnetic-impurity scattering rate,

\tau_{\rm imp}
is the electron scattering time on nonmagnetic impurities,

s=\hbar qv_{f}/\pi=(q/q_{0})\Delta_{0}
,

\hbar q
is the superfluid momentum,

v_{f}
is the Fermi velocity,

q_{0}=1/\xi_{0}=\pi\Delta_{0}/\hbar v_{f}
is the inverse of the BCS coherence length,

\Delta
is the pair potential,

\Delta_{0}
is the pair potential of the idealized (
\Gamma\to 0
) BCS superconductor in the zero-current state (
q=0
) at
T\to 0
,

\theta
is the angle between the Fermi velocity and the current,
and the bracket
\langle X\rangle
is the angular averaging of a quantity
X
over the Fermi surface.
We assume a spherical Fermi surface:
\langle X\rangle=(1/2)\int_{0}^{\pi}X\sin\theta d\theta
.
Note here that considering the average distance the electron travels between electron-nonmagnetic impurity collisions is given by
\ell_{\rm imp}=v_{f}\tau_{\rm imp}
, we obtain the relation
\ell_{\rm imp}/\xi_{0}=\pi\Delta_{0}/2\gamma
.

In the zero-current state (
q=0
), the
\theta
dependences of
f_{m}
and
g_{m}
drop off:

\langle f_{m}\rangle=f_{m}
and
\langle g_{m}\rangle=g_{m}
.
Then, Eq. (
1
) reduces to

\displaystyle(\hbar\omega_{m}+\Gamma)f_{m}-\Delta g_{m}=0,

(2)

which no longer includes the nonmagnetic-impurity scattering rate
\gamma
.
Hence, in the zero-current state,
\Delta
does not depend on a concentration of nonmagnetic impurities.
This robustness of
s
-wave superconductor to nonmagnetic impurity is known as Anderson’s theorem

The Eilenberger equation is compensated by the self-consistency equation

\displaystyle\ln\frac{T_{c0}}{T}=2\pi k_{B}T\sum_{\omega_{m}>0}\biggl(\frac{1}{\hbar\omega_{m}}-\frac{\langle f_{m}\rangle}{\Delta}\biggr).

(3)

Here,

is the critical temperature of the idealized (
\Gamma\to 0
) BCS superconductor, and
\gamma_{E}=0.577
is the Euler’s constant.
Solving Eqs. (
1
) and (
3
) for the current-carrying state or Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
) for the zero-current state,
we can calculate various physical quantities.

IV.1
Complex-conductivity formulas

The general formula for the complex conductivity
\sigma
of a large-
\lambda/\xi
superconductor,
which can include an arbitrary concentration of nonmagnetic impurities,
is given by

;
1991_Zimmermann
;
1995_Sauls
;
1991_Marsiglio
;
2003_Marsiglio

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma,\Gamma,T,\omega)=\sigma_{1}+i\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-i\sigma_{n}}{4\omega\tau}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!d\epsilon\bigl[I_{1}\tanh\frac{\epsilon_{-}}{2kT}-I_{2}\tanh\frac{\epsilon_{+}}{2kT}\bigr],

(17)

\displaystyle I_{1}=\frac{g_{+}g_{-}+f_{+}f_{-}-1}{d_{+}+d_{-}}+\frac{g_{+}g_{-}^{*}+f_{+}f_{-}^{*}+1}{d_{+}-d^{*}_{-}},

(18)

\displaystyle I_{2}=\frac{g_{+}^{*}g_{-}^{*}+f_{+}^{*}f_{-}^{*}-1}{-d_{+}^{*}-d_{-}^{*}}+\frac{g_{+}g_{-}^{*}+f_{+}f_{-}^{*}+1}{d_{+}-d_{-}^{*}}.

is a function of the impurity scattering rate
\gamma/\Delta_{0}=\pi\xi_{0}/2\ell_{\rm imp}
, the Dynes parameter
\Gamma
, temperature
T
, and the photon frequency
\omega
;

is the dc conductivity in the normal state,

\tau

\epsilon_{\pm}=\epsilon\pm\hbar\omega/2
,

,
f_{\pm}=f(\epsilon_{\pm})
,
d_{\pm}=d(\epsilon_{\pm})
, and

.

In the normal state (

Then, Eq. (

\sigma_{n1}=\sigma_{n}/[1+(\omega\tau)^{2}]
and
\sigma_{n2}=\sigma_{n}\omega\tau/[1+(\omega\tau)^{2}]

\tau

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm imp}}+\frac{1}{\tau_{\Gamma}}.

(20)

Here
\tau_{\rm imp}=\hbar/2\gamma
and
\tau_{\Gamma}=\hbar/2\Gamma

It is also possible to derive Eqs. (

In the low-frequency regime (

) reduces to

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\gamma,\Gamma,T,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{n}\hbar/\tau}{8k_{B}T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!d\epsilon\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(\epsilon/2k_{B}T)}\biggl[\frac{({\rm Re}\,g)^{2}}{{\rm Im}\,d}

(21)

\displaystyle+\frac{({\rm Re}\,d\,{\rm Im}\,f-{\rm Im}\,d\,{\rm Re}\,f)^{2}}{\{({\rm Re}\,d)^{2}+({\rm Im}\,d)^{2}\}{\rm Im}\,d}\biggr],

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{n}}{2\tau\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!d\epsilon\tanh\frac{\epsilon}{2k_{B}T}{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{f^{2}}{d}\biggr]

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{2}N_{0}v_{f}^{2}}{3\mu_{0}\omega}2\pi k_{B}T\sum_{\omega_{m}>0}\frac{f_{m}^{2}}{d_{m}}=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}\omega\lambda^{2}},

which are used in Ref.

to study the effects of

In the dirty limit (

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\gamma\gg\Delta_{0},\Gamma,T,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\hbar\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!d\epsilon[f_{\rm FD}(\epsilon)-f_{\rm FD}(\epsilon_{++})]M(\epsilon,\omega),

\displaystyle M(\epsilon,\omega)={\rm Re}g(\epsilon){\rm Re}g(\epsilon_{++})+{\rm Re}f(\epsilon){\rm Re}f(\epsilon_{++}),

(24)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(\gamma\gg\Delta_{0},\Gamma,T,\omega)=\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\hbar\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!d\epsilon\tanh\frac{\epsilon}{2k_{B}T}L(\epsilon,\omega),

(25)

\displaystyle L(\epsilon,\omega)={\rm Re}g(\epsilon){\rm Im}g(\epsilon_{++})+{\rm Re}f(\epsilon){\rm Im}f(\epsilon_{++}).

(26)

Here,

The electromagnetic response of a superconductor is generally nonlocal

Remind

,
which simplifies to

\displaystyle Q=\frac{G}{R_{s}},

(35)

\displaystyle G=\frac{\mu_{0}\omega\int|{\bf H}|^{2}dV}{\int|{\bf H}|^{2}dS},

(36)

when
R_{s}
is uniform on the inner surface of the cavity.

R_{s}
using the results of Sec.

-
IV.4
and Eq. (

).

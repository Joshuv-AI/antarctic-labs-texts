On the Complexity of p p -adic continued fractions of rational number
Rafik BELHADEF, Henri-Alex ESBELIN

Abstract
In this paper, we study the complexity of p p -adic continued fractions of a rational number, which is the p-adic analogue of the theorem of Lamé. We calculate the length of Browkin expansion, and the length of Schneider expansion. Also, some numerical examples have been given.

Introduction

It is well known that, in the real case, the sequence of partial quotients of continued fraction expansion of rational number is finite. where the length of this sequence is calculated by Lame’s method. In the p-adic case, tow definitions of the continued fractions are given: the first one is the definition of Schneider [10], and the second is the definition of Ruban [9] modified by Brwokin [4]. For the expansion of rational number, Browkin [5] asked the following question: can every rational number
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
be written as a finite continued fraction? If the answer is ”not”, determine the infinite continued fractions which correspond to rational numbers.

Based on Schneider definition, Bundschuh [6] proved that every rational number has a stationary continued fraction expansion. For Ruban definition, Laohakosol [8] and de Weger [7] proved that every rational number has a finite or periodic continued fraction expansion. Finally, Browkin based on his definition, proved in [4] that every rational number has a finite continued fraction expansion.

In [2] we have studied the complexity of the
p
-adic expansion of a rational number. In the present paper, we give the
p
-adic version of Lamé’s theorem, i.e. a boundary of the length of finit expansion of Browkin (see theorem 3.1), we will also give the length of the non-stationary part of the expansion of Schneider (see theorem 3.2).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let a linear recurrent sequence
\left(\theta_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

\theta_{i+1}=\frac{1}{2}\theta_{i}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\theta_{i-1},\text{ \ \ for all \ \\
}\ i\geq 2,\text{ \ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }\ \theta_{0}=\left|\beta_{0}\right|,\ \theta_{1}=\left|\beta_{1}\right|.\newline

the following two assertions are verified:

1)
\forall i\geq 2:

\left|\beta_{i}\right|\leq\theta_{i}.

2)
\underset{i\rightarrow+\infty}{lim}\theta_{i}=0.

Proof.

1)
For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\left|x_{n}\right|=\left|u^{\prime}_{0}+u_{1}p+u^{\prime}_{2}p^{2}+...+u^{\prime}_{k_{n}}p^{k_{n}}\right|\leq\dfrac{p-1}{2}(1+p+...+p^{k_{n}})\leq\dfrac{p^{1+k_{n}}-1}{2}<\dfrac{p^{1+k_{n}}}{2}

then

\left|\beta_{n+1}\right|\leq\frac{1}{p^{k_{n}}p^{k_{n+1}}}\left(\left|\beta_{n-1}\right|+\left|x_{n}\right|\left|\beta_{n}\right|\right)<\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left|\beta_{n-1}\right|+\dfrac{1}{2}\left|\beta_{n}\right|\text{ }

(2.3)

Now, we prove by induction that
\forall i\geq 2:

\left|\beta_{i}\right|\leq\theta_{i}
.

For
i=2
we have
\left|\beta_{2}\right|\leq\dfrac{1}{p^{2}}\left|\beta_{0}\right|+\dfrac{1}{2}\left|\beta_{1}\right|<\theta_{2}
. Suppose that
\left|\beta_{i}\right|\leq\theta_{i}
, and we show that
\left|\beta_{i+1}\right|\leq\theta_{i+1}
. Indeed, we have

\left|\beta_{i+1}\right|<\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left|\beta_{i-1}\right|+\dfrac{1}{2}\left|\beta_{i}\right|<\frac{1}{p^{2}}\theta_{i-1}+\dfrac{1}{2}\theta_{i}=\theta_{i+1}

then the assertion is true for
i+1
, so is true for all
\forall i\geq 2.

2)
The characteristic polynomial of
\left(\theta_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is given by
2p^{2}X^{2}-p^{2}X-2=0
. this polynomial admits two real roots

\lambda_{1}=\frac{p+\sqrt{p^{2}+16}}{4p}\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ and \ \ \ \ }\lambda_{2}=\frac{p-\sqrt{p^{2}+16}}{4p},

therefore, the general term of the sequence is given by

\theta_{i}=\left(\frac{\left|\beta_{1}\right|-\lambda_{2}\left|\beta_{0}\right|}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\right)\lambda_{1}^{i}+\left(\frac{\left|\beta_{1}\right|+\lambda_{1}\left|\beta_{0}\right|}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\right)\lambda_{2}^{i}

(2.4)

so,
\underset{i\longrightarrow+\infty}{lim}\theta_{i}=0
, because
0<\lambda_{1}<1
and
\ \dfrac{-1}{2}<\lambda_{2}<0.

∎

Proof.

(Main theorem 3.2)

In the stationnay case of Schneider continued fractions of rational number, we have
\forall m\geq k:

\left|y_{m+1}\right|=1
,
b_{m+1}=p-1
,
\alpha_{m+1}=1
. If we have
(a_{i},\alpha_{i})=(p-1,1)
, for
0\leq i\leq k
, then
\dfrac{a}{b}=-1
.

In the following, we suppose
(a_{i},\alpha_{i})=(\lambda,\alpha)\neq(p-1,1)
, for
0\leq i\leq k
, the matrix formula becomes

\displaystyle\binom{a}{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&p^{\alpha}\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&p^{\alpha}\\
1&0\end{array}\right)..\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&p^{\alpha}\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\binom{+1}{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}U_{k}&V_{k}\\
W_{k}&Z_{k}\end{array}\right)\binom{+1}{-1}

So, we find

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a=U_{k}-V_{k}=U_{k}-p^{\alpha}U_{k-1}\\
\\
b=W_{k}-Z_{k}=W_{k}-p^{\alpha}W_{k-1}\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

Thus

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}U_{n}=\lambda U_{n-1}+p^{\alpha}U_{n-2}\\
\\
W_{n}=\lambda W_{n-1}+p^{\alpha}W_{n-2}\end{array}\right.

which means
\left(U_{n}\right)_{n}
and
\left(W_{n}\right)_{n}
are two linear recurrence sequences. Their general forms are as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}U_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4p^{\alpha}+\lambda^{2}}}\left(T_{2}^{n+1}-T_{1}^{n+1}\right)\\
\\
W_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4p^{\alpha}+\lambda^{2}}}\left(T_{2}^{n}-T_{1}^{n}\right)\end{array}\right.

with the first terms
\ U_{0}=1
,
U_{1}=\lambda
,
W_{0}=0
,
W_{1}=1
. Moreover,
T_{2}
and
T_{1}
are the roots of the characteristic equation:
T^{2}-\lambda T-p^{\alpha}=0
, they are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}T_{1}=\dfrac{\lambda-\sqrt{4p^{\alpha}+\lambda^{2}}}{2}\\
\\
T_{2}=\dfrac{\lambda+\sqrt{4p^{\alpha}+\lambda^{2}}}{2}\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

Back to the formula (
4.5
), we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4p^{\alpha}+\lambda^{2}}}\left(T_{2}^{k}\left(T_{2}-p^{\alpha}\right)-T_{1}^{k}\left(T_{1}-p^{\alpha}\right)\right)\\
\\
b=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4p^{\alpha}+\lambda^{2}}}\left(T_{2}^{k-1}\left(T_{2}-p^{\alpha}\right)-T_{1}^{k-1}\left(T_{1}-p^{\alpha}\right)\right)\end{array}\right.

We divide the two numbers

\frac{a}{b}=\frac{T_{2}^{k}\left(T_{2}-p^{\alpha}\right)-T_{1}^{k}\left(T_{1}-p^{\alpha}\right)}{T_{2}^{k-1}\left(T_{2}-p^{\alpha}\right)-T_{1}^{k-1}\left(T_{1}-p^{\alpha}\right)}

then

\frac{T_{2}^{k-1}}{T_{1}^{k-1}}=\frac{\left(T_{1}-p^{\alpha}\right)\left(a-bT_{1}\right)}{\left(T_{2}-p^{\alpha}\right)\left(a-bT_{2}\right)}

(4.7)

hence

\left(\left|\frac{T_{2}}{T_{1}}\right|\right)^{k-1}=\left|\frac{\left(T_{1}-p^{\alpha}\right)\left(a-bT_{1}\right)}{\left(T_{2}-p^{\alpha}\right)\left(a-bT_{2}\right)}\right|

finally

k=\left[\dfrac{\ln\left|\theta\right|}{\ln\left|\frac{T_{2}}{T_{1}}\right|}\right]+1

(4.8)

with

\theta=\frac{\left(T_{1}-p^{\alpha}\right)\left(a-bT_{1}\right)}{\left(T_{2}-p^{\alpha}\right)\left(a-bT_{2}\right)}

it’s clear that
\left|T_{2}\right|>\left|T_{1}\right|
and
T_{1}\neq 0
, so it is necessary that
\left|\theta\right|>1
in order that
k
either well defined.
However, we have the following cases:

*) If
b>1
and
a>\ bT_{2}
or
bT_{1}<\ a<\ bT_{2}
, then
\left|\theta\right|>1
.

*) If
b<-1
and
a<\ bT_{2}
or
bT_{2}<\ a<\ bT_{1}
, then
\left|\theta\right|>1
.

∎

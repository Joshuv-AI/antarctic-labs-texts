Uniform Asymptotic Expansion for the Incomplete Beta Function
Gergő NEMES and Adri B. OLDE DAALHUIS

Abstract
In [Temme N.M., Special functions. An introduction to the classical functions of mathematical physics, A Wiley-Interscience Publication , John Wiley & Sons, Inc., New York, 1996, Section 11.3.3.1] a uniform asymptotic expansion for the incomplete beta function was derived. It was not obvious from those results that the expansion is actually an asymptotic expansion. We derive a remainder estimate that clearly shows that the result indeed has an asymptotic property, and we also give a recurrence relation for the coefficients.

Introduction

For positive real numbers
a
,
b
and
x\in[0,1]
, the (normalised) incomplete beta function
I_{x}(a,b)
is defined by

\displaystyle I_{x}(a,b)=\frac{1}{B(a,b)}\int_{0}^{x}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
B(a,b)
denotes the ordinary beta function:

\displaystyle B(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}

(see, e.g.,
[
2
,
Section 8.17(i)
]
). In this paper, we will use the notation of
[
2
,
Section 8.18(ii)
]
.

The incomplete beta function plays an important role in statistics in connection with the beta distribution (see, for instance,
[
1
, pp. 210–275]
). Large parameter asymptotic approximations are useful in these applications. For fixed
x
and
b
, one could use the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle I_{x}(a,b)=\frac{x^{a}(1-x)^{b-1}}{aB(a,b)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}{1,1-b}\\
{a+1}\end{matrix};\frac{x}{x-1}\right)\sim\frac{x^{a}(1-x)^{b-1}}{aB(a,b)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(1-b\right)_{n}}{\left(a+1\right)_{n}}\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\right)^{n},

(1)

as
a\to+\infty
. The right-hand side of (
1
) converges only for
x\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, but for any fixed
x\in[0,1)
it is still useful when used as an asymptotic expansion as
a\to+\infty
. For more details, see
[
3
,
Section 11.3.3
]
. However, it is readily seen that (
1
) breaks down as
x\to 1
. Since this limit has significant importance in applications, Temme derived in
[
3
,
Section 11.3.3.1
]
an asymptotic expansion as
a\to+\infty
that holds uniformly for
x\in(0,1]
. His result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\xi=-\ln x
. Then for any fixed positive integer
N
and fixed positive real
b
,

\displaystyle I_{x}(a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}d_{n}F_{n}+\mathcal{O}\big(a^{-N}\big)F_{0}\right),

(2)

as
a\to+\infty
, uniformly for
x\in(0,1]
. The functions
F_{n}=F_{n}(\xi,a,b)
are defined by the recurrence relation

\displaystyle aF_{n+1}=(n+b-a\xi)F_{n}+n\xi F_{n-1},

(3)

with

\displaystyle F_{0}=a^{-b}Q(b,a\xi),\qquad F_{1}=\frac{b-a\xi}{a}F_{0}+\frac{\xi^{b}\mathrm{e}^{-a\xi}}{a\Gamma(b)},

and
Q(a,z)=\Gamma(a,z)/\Gamma(a)
is the normalised incomplete gamma function
(
see
[
2
,
Section 8.2(i)
]
)
. The coefficients
d_{n}=d_{n}(\xi,b)
are defined by the generating function

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}}{t}\right)^{b-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(t-\xi)^{n}.

(4)

In particular,

\displaystyle d_{0}=\left(\frac{1-x}{\xi}\right)^{b-1},\qquad d_{1}=\frac{x\xi+x-1}{(1-x)\xi}(b-1)d_{0}.

They satisfy the recurrence relation

\displaystyle\xi(n+1)(n+2)d_{0}d_{n+2}=\xi\sum_{m=0}^{n}(m+1)\left(n-2m+1+\frac{m-n-1}{b-1}\right)d_{m+1}d_{n-m+1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\xi(n+1)(n+2)d_0d_{n+2} =}{}+\sum_{m=0}^{n}(m+1)\left(n-2m-2-\xi+\frac{m-n}{b-1}\right)d_{m+1}d_{n-m}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\xi(n+1)(n+2)d_0d_{n+2} =}{}+\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1-m-b)d_{m}d_{n-m}.

(5)

In the case that
b=1
, we have
d_{0}=1
and
d_{n}=0
for
n\geq 1
.

Our contribution is the remainder estimate in (
2
) and the recurrence relation (
5
). In fact, it is not at all obvious from (
3
) that the sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
has an asymptotic property as
a\to+\infty
. We will show that for any non-negative integer
n
,

\displaystyle 0<F_{n+1}\leq\frac{n+\beta}{a}F_{n},

(6)

where
\beta=\max(1,b)
.

In
[
4
,
Section 38.2.8
]
the function
F_{n}
is identified as a Kummer
U
-function:

\displaystyle F_{n}=\frac{\xi^{n+b}\mathrm{e}^{-a\xi}n!}{\Gamma(b)}U(n+1,n+b+1,a\xi).

2
Proof of the main results

We proceed similarly as in
[
3
,
Section 11.3.3.1
]
and start with the integral representation

\displaystyle I_{x}(a,b)=\frac{1}{B(a,b)}\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}t^{b-1}\mathrm{e}^{-at}\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}}{t}\right)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(7)

We substitute the truncated Taylor series expansion

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}}{t}\right)^{b-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}d_{n}(t-\xi)^{n}+r_{N}(t)

into (
7
) and obtain

\displaystyle I_{x}(a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}d_{n}F_{n}+R_{N}(a,b,x)\right),

where
F_{n}
is given by the integral representation

\displaystyle F_{n}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(b)}\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}t^{b-1}\mathrm{e}^{-at}(t-\xi)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-a\xi}}{\Gamma(b)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\tau+\xi)^{b-1}\tau^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-a\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(8)

and the remainder term
R_{N}(a,b,x)
is defined by

\displaystyle R_{N}(a,b,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(b)}\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}t^{b-1}\mathrm{e}^{-at}r_{N}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(9)

The recurrence relation (
3
) can be obtained from (
8
) via a simple integration by parts.

Let, for a moment,

\displaystyle c_{n}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\tau+\xi)^{b-1}\tau^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-a\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Then via integration by parts we find

\displaystyle ac_{n+1}(a,b)=(n+b)c_{n}(a,b)+\xi(1-b)c_{n}(a,b-1).

(10)

We make the observation that

\displaystyle 0\leq\xi c_{n}(a,b-1)=\xi\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\tau+\xi)^{b-2}\tau^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-a\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq c_{n}(a,b).

(11)

It follows from (
10
) and (
11
) that

\displaystyle ac_{n+1}(a,b)\leq\begin{cases}(n+1)c_{n}(a,b)&\text{if}\ 0<b\leq 1,\\
(n+b)c_{n}(a,b)&\text{if}\ b\geq 1.\end{cases}

Since
F_{n}=\mathrm{e}^{-a\xi}c_{n}(a,b)/\Gamma(b)
, this inequality implies (
6
).

To obtain the remainder estimate in (
2
), we use the Cauchy integral representation

\displaystyle r_{N}(t)=\frac{(t-\xi)^{N}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{\{\xi,t\}}\frac{\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}}{\tau}\right)^{b-1}}{(\tau-t)(\tau-\xi)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(12)

where the contour encircles the points
\xi
and
t
once in the positive sense. From the integral representation (
9
), we have that
0\leq\xi\leq t
. Thus, in the case that
N\geq 1
, we can deform the contour in (
12
) to the path

\displaystyle[1+\infty\mathrm{i},1+\pi\mathrm{i}]\cup[1+\pi\mathrm{i},-1+\pi\mathrm{i}]\cup[-1+\pi\mathrm{i},-1-\pi\mathrm{i}]

\displaystyle\hphantom{[1+\infty\mathrm{i},1+\pi\mathrm{i}]}{}\cup[-1-\pi\mathrm{i},1-\pi\mathrm{i}]\cup[1-\pi\mathrm{i},1-\infty\mathrm{i}].

For the integrals along the final three portions of the path, we have the estimates

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{-1+\pi\mathrm{i}}^{-1-\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}}{\tau}\right)^{b-1}}{(\tau-t)(\tau-\xi)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\leq\frac{\max\left((\mathrm{e}-1)^{b-1},\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}+1}{\sqrt{\pi^{2}+1}}\right)^{b-1}\right)}{(1+\xi)^{N+1}},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{-1-\pi\mathrm{i}}^{1-\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}}{\tau}\right)^{b-1}}{(\tau-t)(\tau-\xi)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\leq\frac{\max\left(\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\pm 1}+1}{\sqrt{\pi^{2}+1}}\right)^{b-1}\right)}{\pi^{N+2}},

(13)

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{1-\pi\mathrm{i}}^{1-\infty\mathrm{i}}\frac{\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}}{\tau}\right)^{b-1}}{(\tau-t)(\tau-\xi)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\pi}^{+\infty}\frac{\max\big(\big(1\pm\mathrm{e}^{-1}\big)^{b-1}\big)\big(s^{2}+1\big)^{(1-b)/2}}{\sqrt{s^{2}+(1-t)^{2}}\big(s^{2}+(1-\xi)^{2}\big)^{N/2}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hphantom{\left|\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{1-\pi\mathrm{i}}^{1-\infty\mathrm{i}} \frac{\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}}{\tau}\right)^{b-1}}{(\tau-t)(\tau-\xi)^{N}}\, \mathrm{d}\tau\right| }{}\leq\frac{\max\big(\big(1\pm\mathrm{e}^{-1}\big)^{b-1}\big)}{2\pi}\int_{\pi}^{+\infty}\frac{\big(s^{2}+1\big)^{(1-b)/2}}{s^{N+1}}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(14)

respectively. The integrals along the first two portions can be estimated similarly to (
13
) and (
14
). Hence, for
0\leq\xi\leq t
and
N\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|r_{N}(t)|\leq C_{N}(b)(t-\xi)^{N},

where the constant
C_{N}(b)
does not depend on
\xi
. Using this result in the integral representation (
9
), we can infer that

\displaystyle|R_{N}(a,b,x)|\leq C_{N}(b)F_{N}.

Finally, combining this result with the inequalities (
6
), we obtain the required remainder estimate in (
2
).

The reader can check that the function
f(t)=\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}}{t}\right)^{b-1}
is a solution of the nonlinear differential equation

\displaystyle tf(t)f^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{b-2}{b-1}tf^{\prime 2}(t)+(t+2)f(t)f^{\prime}(t)+(b-1)f^{2}(t)=0.

If we substitute the Taylor series (
4
) into this differential equation and rearrange the result, we obtain the recurrence relation (
5
).

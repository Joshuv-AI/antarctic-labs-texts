Bernstein von-Mises Theorem for g-prior and nonlocal prior
Xiao Fang xiaofang@ufl.edu

Abstract
The paper develops Bernstein von Mises Theorem under hierarchical g -priors for linear regression models. The results are obtained both when the error variance is known, and also when it is unknown. An inverse gamma prior is attached to the error variance in the later case. The paper also demonstrates some connection between the total variation and α \alpha -divergence measures.

Introduction

Bernstein von-Mises(BVM) Theorem occupies a central place in the statistics literature. In its original version, the theorem establishes asymptotic normality of the posterior distribution of the parameter vector of interest for a regular finite dimensional model.The asymptotic distribution is centered at the posterior mean or posterior mode with inverse of the observed information matrix as its variance-covariance matrix.

There exists a huge literature establishing BVM theorems under different scenarios, but still under the framework of a regular finite dimensional model. It is only recently that attention has bean shifted to establishing BVM theorems where the number of covariates can even depend on the sample size. This has been demonstrated by
(
Song and Liang 2017
)
for a linear regression model with a scale-mixed normal prior with dominating peak around zero. The theorem has been proved with spike and slab prior by
(
Castillo et al. 2015
)
, and
(
Martin and Ning 2020
)
under a linear regression model.

The objective of our paper is to establish BVM theorems both under the g-prior
(
Zellner 1986
;
F.Liang et al. 2012
)
, and non-local priors
(
Johnson and Rossell 2012
)
once again in the set up of linear regression model. For g-priors we have proved the result both when the error variance is known or unknown. For non-local prior, we have established the result when the error is known.

The BVM theorems are usually established by either showing that the total variation between the normalized posterior density and the normal density converges to zero as the sample sizes tend to infinity or the Hellinger distance between the two densities converges to zero as the sample size tend to infinity. Indeed the two criteria are equivalent as we will demonstrate in the next section. We have adopted in this paper the Hellinger distance criterion for establishing the BVM Theorem.

The outline of the remaining sections is as follows. In Section 2, we have proved the equivalence of density convergence under the Renyi divergence and the total variation divergence criteria. As we shall see, the Hellinger divergence is a special case of the Renyi divergence. We have also stated a few lemmas related to tail tail bounds of chi-squared distribution, used repeatedly in the proofs of our main results for g-priors in Section 3 and for non-local priors in Section 4. Some final remarks are made in Section 5. The proofs all the results are given in Section 6.

2
Some Preliminary Result

2.1
\alpha
-divergence Criterion

We first introduce the
(
Renyi 1961
)
divergence criterion, more popularly known as the
\alpha
-divergence criterion between two densities
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, each with respect to some
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
as

D_{\alpha}(p_{1},p_{2})=[1-\int p_{1}^{\alpha}p_{2}^{1-\alpha}d\mu]/[\alpha(1-\alpha)],0<\alpha<1.

It maybe noted that when
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
,

D_{1/2}(p_{1},p_{2})=4[1-\int p_{1}^{1/2}p_{2}^{1/2}d\mu]=2\int(p_{1}^{1/2}-p_{2}^{1/2})^{2}d\mu=2H^{2}(p_{1},p_{2}),

where
H(p_{1},p_{2})=[\int(p_{1}^{1/2}-p_{2}^{1/2})^{2}d\mu]^{1/2}
is the Hellinger distance between two densities
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
. As an aside, the two limitting case
\alpha\to 0
and
\alpha\to 1
yield respectively the Kullback-Leibler(KL) divergence
KL(p_{2},p_{1})=\int\log(p_{2}/p_{1})p_{2}d\mu
and
KL(p_{1},p_{2})=\int\log(p_{1}/p_{2})p_{1}d\mu
. We also recall that the total-variation between two densities
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
is given by

TV(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\int|p_{1}-p_{2}|d\mu
.

Lemma 1
.

Consider a sequence of densities
p_{n}(n\geq 1))
and a density
p
all with respect to some
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
. Then
TV(p_{n},p)\to 0\Rightarrow D_{\alpha}(p_{n},p)\to 0
for all
0<\alpha<1
.

Proof: For
0<\alpha<1
,

\begin{split}D_{\alpha}(p_{n},p)&=[[1-\int p_{n}^{\alpha}p^{1-\alpha}d\mu]/[\alpha(1-\alpha)]]\\
&\leq[1-\int\{\min(p_{n},p)\}^{\alpha}\{\min(p_{n},p)\}^{1-\alpha}d\mu]/[\alpha(1-\alpha)]\\
&=[1-\int\min(p_{n},p)d\mu]/[\alpha(1-\alpha)]\\
&=[2-2\int\min(p_{n},p)d\mu]/[2\alpha(1-\alpha)]\\
&=[p_{n}+p-2\int\min(p_{n},p)d\mu]/[2\alpha(1-\alpha)]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}[\int|p_{n}-p|d\mu]/[\alpha(1-\alpha)]=TV(p_{n},p)/[\alpha(1-\alpha)].\end{split}

Hence,
TV(p_{n},p)\to 0
implies
D_{\alpha}(p_{n},p)\to 0
for all
0<\alpha<1
.

Remark 2
.

The result is not necessarily true when
\alpha\to 0
or
\alpha\to 1
.

In view of the above result,
D_{1/2}(p_{n},p)=2H^{2}(p_{n},p)\to 0
when
TV(p_{n},p)\to 0
. But we also know that
TV(p_{n},p)\leq H(p_{n},p)
. Thus
H(p_{n},p)\to 0\Leftrightarrow TV(p_{n},p)\Leftrightarrow D_{\alpha}(p_{n},p)
for all
0<\alpha<1
.

2.2
Tail bounds for the Chisquared distribution

In this subsection, we state a few lemmas related to tail behavior of Chisquared distribution.

Lemma 3
.

For any
a>0
, we have

P(|\chi^{2}_{p}-p|>a)\leq 2\exp(-\frac{a^{2}}{4p}).

Proof: See Lemma 4.1 of
(
Cao et al. 2020
)
.

Lemma 4
.

(i) For any
c>0
, we have

P(\chi^{2}_{p}(\lambda)-(p+\lambda)>c)\leq\exp(-\frac{p}{2}\{\frac{c}{p+\lambda}-log(1+\frac{c}{p+\lambda})\}).

(ii) for
\omega<1
then

P(\chi^{2}_{p}(\lambda)\leq\omega\lambda)\leq c_{1}\lambda^{-1}\exp\{-\lambda(1-\omega)^{2}/8\},

where
c_{1}>0
is a constant.

(iii)
For any
c>0
,
P(\chi^{2}_{p}(\lambda)-p\leq-c)\leq\exp(-\frac{c^{2}}{4p})
.

Proof: See Lemma 4.2 of
(
Cao et al. 2020
)
for the proof of (i). (ii) is proved in
(
Shin et al. 2019
)
. Finally, (iii) follows from the fact if
U\sim\chi^{2}_{p}(\lambda)
, then
P(U>c)
is strictly increasing in
\lambda
for fixed
p
and
c>0
. Hence

P(\chi^{2}_{p}(\lambda)-p\leq-c)\leq P(\chi^{2}_{p}-p\leq-c)\leq\exp(-\frac{c^{2}}{4p}).

3
Bernstein von-Mises theorem for g-prior

Consider the standard linear regression model

Y=X\beta+\sigma z,

(1)

where
Y
is a
(n\times 1)
vector of response variables,
X
is
n\times p
design matrix,
\beta
is a
(p\times 1)
regression parameter,
\sigma>0
is an scale parameter and
z\sim N_{n}(0,I_{n})
,
I_{n}
being the identity matrix. We will allow
p\equiv p_{n}
to depend on
n

Following
(
F.Liang et al. 2012
)
, we consider the following hierarchical model

\begin{split}Y|\beta&\sim N(X\beta,\sigma^{2}I_{n})\\
\beta|g&\sim N(0,g\sigma^{2}(X^{T}X)^{-1})\\
\pi(g)&\propto g^{b-1}(1+g)^{-(a+b)},g>0.\end{split}

(2)

Writing
\omega=({1+g})^{-1}
,
\omega\sim Beta(a,b)
. Then we have

\pi(\omega|\sigma^{2},Y)\sim\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}\exp(-\omega\frac{Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{\sigma^{2}}),

(3)

\pi(\beta|\omega,\sigma^{2},Y)\sim\exp(-\frac{(\beta-(1-\omega)\hat{\beta})^{T}(X^{T}X)(\beta-(1-\omega)\hat{\beta})}{2(1-\omega)\sigma^{2}}).

(4)

We will establish BVM theorem both when
\sigma^{2}
is fixed and when it is unknown .

3.1
Fixed
\sigma^{2}
case

We can show that for fixed
\sigma^{2}
case, the posterior distribution of
\omega
will concentrate near
0
, so that the posterior distribution of
\beta
will tend to a normal distribution centered at
\hat{\beta}
with variance matrix
\sigma^{2}(X^{T}X)^{-1}
.

Lemma 5
.

Consider the hierarchical prior given before and assume
\sigma^{2}
is fixed. Also we denote the true model as
Y\sim N(X\beta_{0},\sigma^{2}I_{n})
. Let
{\beta_{0}}^{T}{X^{T}X}\beta_{0}\asymp n
and
p=o(\frac{n}{\log n})
,
p\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. For any diverging sequence
\xi_{n}
, let
t_{n}=\xi_{n}p\log n
. Then we have

E_{0}[P(\omega>\frac{t_{n}}{n}|Y)]=o(1),

where
E_{0}
denotes expectation under
\beta=\beta_{0}
.

By applying Lemma
5
, we get BVM theorem for g-prior with fixed
\sigma^{2}
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
p=O(n^{\frac{1}{4}}
) and
\xi_{n}=\log n
in Lemma
5
. Then under conditions of Lemma
5
,

E_{0}H^{2}(\pi(\beta|\sigma^{2},Y),N(\hat{\beta},\sigma^{2}(X^{T}X)^{-1}))\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
.

3.2
Unknown
\sigma^{2}
case

Now, we assume inverse gamma prior
\pi(\sigma^{2})\sim IG(\frac{c}{2},\frac{d}{2})
.

Then we have

\beta|\omega,Y\sim t_{n+c}((1-\omega)\hat{\beta},\frac{d+Y^{T}(I-P_{X})Y+\omega Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{(1-\omega)(n+c)}(X^{T}X)^{-1}),

(5)

where
t_{n+c}
is a student’s t-distribution with
n+c
degrees of freedom,

\pi(\omega|Y)\propto(d+Y^{T}(I-P_{X})Y+\omega Y^{T}P_{X}Y)^{-\frac{n+c}{2}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}.

(6)

Similar to Lemma
5
, we can also show the posterior distribution of
\omega
will concentrate near
0
.

Lemma 7
.

Consider the hierarchical prior given before and assume
\pi(\sigma^{2})\sim IG(\frac{c}{2},\frac{d}{2})
. Let
{\beta_{0}}^{T}{X^{T}X}\beta_{0}\asymp n
and
p=o(\frac{n}{\log n})
,
p\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Then for any diverging sequence
\xi_{n}
, let
t_{n}=\xi_{n}p\log n
, we have

E_{0}[P(\omega>\frac{t_{n}}{n}|Y)]=o(1).

By applying Lemma
7
, we get BVM theorem for the g-prior with unknown
\sigma^{2}
.

Theorem 8
.

Under the same conditions of Lemma
7
, let
p=O(n^{\frac{1}{4}}
) and
\xi_{n}=\log n
. Then we have

E_{0}H^{2}(\pi(\beta|\sigma^{2},Y),N(\hat{\beta},\sigma_{0}^{2}(X^{T}X)^{-1}))\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
.

4
Bernstein von-Mises theorem for nonlocal priors

Now we consider nonlocal prior in
(
Johnson and Rossell 2012
)
, the prior on
\beta
is a pMoM density, as considered in their paper specifically,

\pi(\beta|\tau,\sigma^{2},r)=d_{p}(2\pi)^{-\frac{p}{2}}(\tau\sigma^{2})^{-rp-\frac{p}{2}}|A_{p}|^{1/2}\exp[-\frac{1}{2\tau\sigma^{2}}\beta^{T}A_{p}\beta]\prod_{i=1}^{p}\beta_{i}^{2r},

for
\tau>0
,
A_{p}
a
p\times p
nonsingular scale matrix,
r=1,2,\cdots
, and
d_{p}
is a normalizing constant.

Then we have

\pi(\beta_{k}|k,Y,\tau,r,\sigma^{2})=E_{k}^{-1}(\Pi_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}^{2r})(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-\frac{k}{2}}|C_{k}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Pi_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}^{2r})\exp[\frac{(\beta_{k}-\tilde{\beta}_{k})^{T}C_{k}(\beta_{k}-\tilde{\beta}_{k})}{2\sigma^{2}}],

where
C_{k}=X_{k}^{T}X_{k}+\frac{1}{\tau}A_{k}
,
\tilde{\beta}_{k}=C_{k}^{-1}X_{k}^{T}Y
, for
k=\{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{j}\}(1\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{j}\leq p)
, we let
X_{k}
denote the design matrix formed from
the columns of
X_{n}
corresponding to model
k
,
E_{k}(\cdot)
denotes expectation with respect to multinormal distribution with mean
\tilde{\beta}_{k}
and covariance matrix
\sigma^{2}C_{k}^{-1}
.

We have the following BVM theorem for
\sigma^{2}
is fixed case.

Theorem 9
.

Under the conditions of Theorem 1 in
(
Johnson and Rossell 2012
)
, we have

E_{\beta_{t}^{0}}H^{2}(\pi(\beta|\tau,r,\sigma^{2},Y),N(\hat{\beta}_{t},\sigma^{2}(X_{t}^{T}X_{t})^{-1}))\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
.

5
Discussion

In this paper, we establish BVM theorems both under g-prior
(
Zellner 1986
;
F.Liang et al. 2012
)
and non-local priors(
(
Johnson and Rossell 2012
)
) once again in the set up of linear regression model. For the g-prior we have proved the result both when the error variance is known or unknown. For non-local prior, we have established the result when the error is known. However for non-local priors, BVM theorem for unknown error variance remains to be established.

With BVM theorem, we are also able to obtain many interesting results like valid uncertainty quantification as in
(
Martin and Ning 2020
)
, and we will discuss it in future paper.

When
\beta=\beta_{0}

\frac{Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{\sigma^{2}}\sim{\chi}^{2}_{p}(\frac{{\beta_{0}}^{T}{X^{T}X}\beta_{0}}{2\sigma^{2}}).

Since
{\beta_{0}}^{T}{X^{T}X}\beta_{0}\asymp n
,
p=o(n)
,
p\to\infty
as
n\to\infty

P(\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\beta_{0}}^{T}{X^{T}X}\beta_{0}}{2\sigma^{2}}\leq\frac{Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{\sigma^{2}}\leq p+2\frac{{\beta_{0}}^{T}{X^{T}X}\beta_{0}}{2\sigma^{2}})\to 1

Define

P(S_{n})\to 1
, as

Proof of Lemma
5
:

Throughout the proof, we will use the notation
C(>0)
as a generic constant.

We have
P(\omega>\frac{t_{n}}{n}|Y)\leq\textbf{1}_{S_{n}}P(\omega>\frac{t_{n}}{n}|Y)+\textbf{1}_{S^{c}_{n}}
and
E_{0}\textbf{1}_{S^{c}_{n}}=P(S_{n}^{c})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. So it suffices to show that

E_{0}\{\textbf{1}_{S_{n}}P(\omega>\frac{t_{n}}{n}|Y)\}\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. In view of
\int_{0}^{1}\pi(\omega|Y)d\omega\geq\int^{1}_{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\pi(\omega|Y)d\omega
, all we need to show is

\frac{\int_{0}^{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\pi(\omega|Y)d\omega}{\int^{1}_{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\pi(\omega|Y)d\omega}\textbf{1}_{S_{n}}\to\infty,

as
n\to\infty
.

We have

\pi(\omega|Y)=\frac{1}{m(Y)}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}\exp(-\omega\frac{Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{\sigma^{2}}),

where
m(Y)=\int_{0}^{1}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}\exp(-\omega\frac{Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{\sigma^{2}})d\omega
.

\begin{split}&\textbf{1}_{S_{n}}{\int_{0}^{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}\exp(-\omega\frac{Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{\sigma^{2}})d\omega}\\
\geq&{\int_{0}^{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}\exp(-l_{2}n\omega)d\omega}\\
\geq&C{\int_{0}^{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}\exp(-l_{2}n\omega)d\omega}\\
\geq&C{\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}\exp(-l_{2}n\omega)d\omega}\\
\geq&C{\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}d\omega}=\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{a+\frac{p}{2}}n^{-(a+\frac{p}{2})}.\end{split}

(7)

We also have

\begin{split}&\textbf{1}_{S_{n}}{\int^{1}_{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}\exp(-\omega\frac{Y^{T}P_{X}Y}{\sigma^{2}})d\omega}\\
\leq&{\int^{1}_{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}\exp(-\omega l_{1}n)d\omega}\\
\leq&\exp(-l_{1}t_{n}){\int^{1}_{0}\omega^{a+\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-\omega)^{b-1}d\omega}\\
\leq&\frac{\Gamma(a+\frac{p}{2})\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+\frac{p}{2}+b)}\exp(-l_{1}t_{n})\leq\exp(-l_{1}t_{n}).\end{split}

(8)

Combining (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain

\frac{\int_{0}^{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\pi(\omega|Y)d\omega}{\int^{1}_{\frac{t_{n}}{n}}\pi(\omega|Y)d\omega}\textbf{1}_{S_{n}}\geq\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{a+\frac{p}{2}}\frac{\exp(-l_{1}t_{n})}{n^{-(a+\frac{p}{2})}}\to\infty.

This proves Lemma 3.

Proof of Theorem

\pi(\beta|\sigma^{2},Y)=\int_{0}^{1}\pi(\beta|\omega,\sigma^{2},Y)\pi(\omega|\sigma^{2},Y)d\omega
.

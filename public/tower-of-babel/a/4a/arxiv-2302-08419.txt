On Reconstructing Finite Gauge Group from Fusion Rules
Rajath Radhakrishnan

Abstract
Abstract: Gauging a finite group 0-form symmetry G G of a quantum field theory (QFT) results in a QFT with a Rep ( G ) (G) symmetry implemented by Wilson lines. The group G G determines the fusion of Wilson lines. However, in general, the fusion rules of Wilson lines do not determine G G . In this paper, we study the properties of G G that can be determined from the fusion rules of Wilson lines and surface operators obtained from higher-gauging Wilson lines. This is in the spirit of Richard Brauer who asked what information in addition to the character table of a finite group needs to be known to determine the group. We show that fusion rules of surface operators obtained from higher-gauging Wilson lines can be used to distinguish infinite pairs of groups which cannot be distinguished using the fusion of Wilson lines. We derive necessary conditions for two non-isomorphic groups to have the same surface operator fusion and find a pair of such groups.

S_{(K,[c])}\times S_{(H,[d])}=\sum_{g\in K\backslash G/H}S_{(K\cap\,{}^{g}H,[c\cdot\,{}^{g}d])}~.

(4.1)

From the expression for the fusion coefficient (

), we find
N_{(K,[c]),(H,[d])}^{(L,[e])}\leq|G|
. When

the 2-cocycle

. Therefore, the fusion rule reduces to

S_{(\mathds{Z}_{1},[1])}\times S_{(\mathds{Z}_{1},[1])}=|G|~S_{(\mathds{Z}_{1},[1])}~.

(4.2)

Moreover,
|H\backslash G/K|=|G|
if and only if

. Therefore,
S_{(\mathds{Z}_{1},[1])}
is the unique surface operator with the above fusion rule. The order of subgroups of
G
can be obtained from the fusion rule

S_{(K,[c])}\times S_{(\mathds{Z}_{1},[1])}=\frac{|G|}{|K|}S_{(\mathds{Z}_{1},[1])}~.

(4.3)

Given a 2-representation
(K,[c])
, if the fusion coefficient

N_{(K,[c]),(K,[c])}^{(K,[c])}\neq 0

(4.4)

then
[c]=[1]
. Indeed, the non-trivial fusion coefficient above implies that there exists
g\in K\backslash G/K
such that
[c\cdot\,{}^{g}c]=[c]
which implies that
[c]=[1]
. More generally, given a 2-representation
(K,[c])
, if the fusion coefficient

N_{(H,[d]),(K,[c])}^{(H,[d])}\neq 0

for some

such that

, then

and

belong to the same conjugacy class of subgroups, and
[c]=[1]
. Indeed, the non-trivial fusion coefficient above implies that there exists

H\cap\,{}^{g}K=H,~[d\cdot\,{}^{g}c]=[d]~.

(4.6)

Since
|K|=|H|
,
H\cap\,{}^{g}K=H
implies that
{}^{g}K=H
. Also,
[d\cdot\,{}^{g}c]=[d]
implies that
[c]=[1]
.

Let us use the properties of the fusion rules of surface operators discussed above to obtain properties of the group
G
from

S_{\rho_{1}}\times S_{\rho_{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}N_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}^{\rho_{i}}~S_{\rho_{i}}~.

A 2-representation
\rho

\mathds{1}

K_{\mathds{1}}=G,~[c_{\mathds{1}}]=[1]~.

(4.8)

Also, there is a unique surface operator, say

|G|=N_{\rho_{\text{max}},\rho_{\text{max}}}^{\rho_{\text{max}}}~.

(4.9)

We know that

K_{\rho_{\text{max}}}=\mathds{Z}_{1},~[c_{\rho_{\text{max}}}]=[1]~.

(4.10)

By identifying the surface operator

K_{\rho}
for any 2-representation
\rho

|K_{\rho}|=\frac{|G|}{N_{\rho,\rho_{\text{max}}}^{\rho_{\text{max}}}}~.

Also, from the discussion around (

(4.12)

Since we can identify the subset of 2-representations with trivial

and

(4.13)

This criterion allows us to find all 2-representations corresponding to the same

up to conjugation.

4.1

We can infer the existence or lack of normal subgroups from the fusion rules of surface operators.

.

To see this, consider the fusion rule

S_{(K_{\rho},[c_{\rho}])}\times S_{(K_{\rho},[c_{\rho}])}=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{|G|}{|K_{\rho}|}}S_{(K_{\rho},[c_{\rho_{i}}])}~.

(4.15)

From the expression for the fusion rules (
3.7
), we get

{}^{g}K_{\rho}=K_{\rho}~\forall~g\in K_{\rho}\backslash G/K_{\rho}\implies{}^{g}K_{\rho}=K_{\rho}~\forall~g\in G~,

(4.16)

which implies that
K_{\rho}
is a normal subgroup. Conversely, let us consider a normal subgroup
K_{\rho}\subset G
. Using (
3.7
), we get the fusion rules

S_{(K_{\rho},[c_{\rho}])}\times S_{(K_{\rho},[c_{\rho}])}=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{|G|}{|K_{\rho}|}}S_{(K_{\rho},[c_{\rho_{i}}])}~.

(4.17)

If
N_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}^{\rho^{\prime\prime}}\neq 0
where the 2-representations
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
corresponds to normal subgroups
K_{\rho},K_{\rho^{\prime}}\subseteq G
, then the expression for the fusion rule (
3.7
) implies

K_{\rho^{\prime\prime}}=K_{\rho}\cap K_{\rho^{\prime}}~.

(4.18)

This shows that the modular lattice of normal subgroups of
G
can be determined from the fusion rules of surface operators. Recall that the modular lattice of normal subgroups of
G
can also be determined from the character table (see section
2
). More generally, if
K_{\rho}
is not normal and
K_{\rho^{\prime}}
is normal, then we get

K_{\rho^{\prime\prime}}=K_{\rho}\cap K_{\rho^{\prime}}~,

(4.19)

and then

|K_{\rho}\cap K_{\rho^{\prime}}|=\frac{|G|}{N_{\rho^{\prime\prime},\rho_{\text{max}}}^{\rho_{\text{max}}}}

(4.20)

for some
\rho
. Therefore, the order of intersection of a normal subgroup with a non-normal subgroup can be determined from the fusion rules of surface operators. Note that this information cannot be obtained from the character table (see the
D_{8}
v/s
Q_{8}
discussion in section
2
).

4.2
2-representations of
G/N

In the previous subsection, we learned that given the fusion rules surface operators
S_{\rho}
we can identify the normal subgroups of
G
. In this section, we will show that given a normal subgroup
N
of
G
, the irreducible 2-representations of the quotient group
G/N
are also determined by the 2-representations of
G
. Irreducible 2-representations of
G/N
are labelled by
(M,[d])
where
M
is a subgroup of
G
and
d\in H^{2}(M,U(1))
. The correspondence theorem states that there is a bijection between the subgroups of
G
containing
N
and subgroups of
G/N
(for example see
[
31
]
). For every subgroup
M\subseteq G/N
we have some
K\subseteq G
containing
N
such that

K/N\cong M~.

(4.21)

This is a bijection. Therefore, the irreducible 2-representations of
G/N
can be equivalently labelled as
(K/N,[r])
where
[r]\in H^{2}(K/N,U(1))
. A 2-representation
(K/N,[r])
of
G/N
corresponds to a 2-representation
(K,[c])
of
G
where
[c]\in H^{2}(K,U(1))
is the cohomology class of the inflation of the 2-cocycle
r
given by

c=r\circ\pi~,

(4.22)

where
\pi:G\to G/N
is the projection
\pi(g)=Ng
. If there exists
\pi(l)\in G/N
such that

(K/N,[r])=({}^{\pi(l)}(H/N),[{}^{\pi(l)}p])~.

(4.23)

then we have the relation

\pi(l)\pi(h)\pi(l)^{-1}=\pi(lhl^{-1})=\pi(k)

(4.24)

for
h\in H
and
k\in K
. This implies that
lhl^{-1}=nk
for some
n\in N
. Since
N\subseteq K
,
k^{\prime}:=nk\in K
. Therefore,
lhl^{-1}=k^{\prime}
which shows that the subgroups
H
and
K
of
G
are conjugate. Also, the inflation of 2-cocycles
r
and
p
to
H^{2}(K,U(1))
and
H^{2}(H,U(1))
, respectively are mapped to each other under the conjugation
{}^{l}H=K
. This shows that the equivalent 2-representations
(K/N,[r])
and
(H/N,[p])
of
G/N
correspond to equivalent 2-representations of
(K,[c])
and
(H,[d])
of
G
, respectively, where
c:=r\circ\pi
and
d:=p\circ\pi
.

Consider the fusion coefficient

N_{(K/N,[r]),(H/N,[s])}^{(L/N,[p])}=|\{l\in(K/N)\backslash(G/N)/(H/N)~|~(K/N)\cap\,{}^{l}(H/N)=(L/N),[r\cdot\,{}^{l}s]=[r]\}|

(4.25)

for three 2-representations
(K/N,[r]),(H/N,[s])
and
(L/N,[r])
of
G/N
. Let
[c],[d],[e]
be the inflation of the 2-cocycles
[r],[s],[p]
, respectively. We have

N_{(K,[c]),(H,[d])}^{(L,[e])}=|\{g\in K\backslash G/H~|~K\cap\,{}^{g}H=L,[c\cdot\,{}^{g}d]=[e]|~.

(4.26)

The double coset
K\backslash G/H
is the set of equivalence classes under the equivalence relation
g_{1}\sim g_{2}
if
g_{1}=kg_{2}h
for some
k\in K,h\in H
. If
N
is a normal subgroup of
K,H
and
G
, then

kg_{1}h=g_{2}\implies\pi(kg_{1}h)=\pi(g_{2})\implies\pi(k)\pi(g_{1})\pi(h)=\pi(g_{2})

(4.27)

Note that
\pi(k)\in K/N,\pi(h)\in H/N
and
\pi(g_{1}),\pi(g_{2})\in G/N
. Therefore, if
g_{1}\sim g_{2}
then
\pi(g_{1})
and
\pi(g_{2})
are in the same equivalence class in
(K/N)\backslash(G/N)/(H/N)
. Similarly, if
g_{1}\not\sim g_{2}
then
\pi(g_{1})\not\sim\pi(g_{2})
. The projection
\pi
defines a bijective map between the sets
K\backslash G/H
and
(K/N)\backslash(G/N)/(H/N)
.
Now, if
g\in K\backslash G/H
satisfies

K\cap H^{g}=L,[c\cdot d^{g}]=[e]~,

(4.28)

then

\displaystyle K\cap\,{}^{g}H=L\Leftrightarrow(K\cap\,{}^{g}H)/N=L/N

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(K/N)\cap({}^{g}H)/N=L/N

(4.29)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(K/N)\cap\,{}^{\pi(g)}(H/N)=L/N

(4.30)

and

\displaystyle[c\cdot\,{}^{g}d]=[e]\Leftrightarrow[r\circ\pi\cdot\,{}^{g}(s\circ\pi)]=[p\circ\pi]\Leftrightarrow[r\cdot\,{}^{\pi(g)}s]=[p]~.

(4.31)

where
\pi(g)\in(K/N)\backslash(G/N)/(H/N)
. Therefore,

N_{(K/N,[r]),(H/N,[s])}^{(L/N,[p])}=N_{(K,[c]),(H,[d])}^{(L,[e])}~.

(4.32)

This shows that the subring 2Rep
(G/N)
is contained in 2Rep
(G)
for any normal subgroup
N\vartriangleleft G
. Given a 2-representation
\rho
, how do we determine if
\rho\in\text{2Rep}(G/N)
? First of all, if
\rho\in\text{2Rep}(G/N)
then
K_{\rho}
should contain the normal subgroup
N
. This can be checked using the fusion rules of surface operators and equation (
4.20
). Finally, we also require
[c_{\rho}]
to be an inflation of a 2-cocycle in
H^{2}(K_{\rho}/N,U(1))
. A necessary condition for this is that the restriction of the 2-cocycle
c_{\rho}
to the normal subgroup
N
should be trivial. This can be checked using the fusion rule

S_{\rho}\times S_{\rho_{N}}=S_{\rho^{\prime}}~,

(4.33)

where
\rho_{N}
is the 2-representation satisfying
K_{\rho_{N}}=N
and
[c_{\rho_{N}}]=[1]
. Then, using (
3.7
) we find that
[c_{\rho^{\prime}}]=[1]
if and only if
[c_{\rho}|_{N}]=[1]
.

Note that the 2-representations of
N
itself cannot be determined in general. This is because the 2-representations of
G
only “knows” about subgroups of
N
up to conjugation by
G
. However, the 2-representations of
N
are determined by subgroups of
N
up to conjugation by elements of
N
. In general, the latter set is larger than the former.

4.3
Group Extensions and Fusion Rules

If a group
G
can be obtained from smaller groups through group extensions (including direct and semi-direct products), then
G
contains normal subgroups. Now that we know how to determine the existence or lack of normal subgroups from fusion rules, we can consider the following cases. Suppose there are no fusion rules of the form (
4.14
) except for
K_{\rho}=G,\mathds{Z}_{1}
. This implies that the group
G
does not contain any proper normal subgroups. Therefore,
G
is a simple group and cannot be written as a group extension of smaller groups. In the following subsections, we will consider the case where one or more fusions of the form (
4.14
) do exist for a proper non-trivial subgroup
N\subset G
. Then, we will show that similar fusion rules can be used to determine whether the group
G
can be written as a direct product, semi-direct product or a non-split group extension.

4.3.1
Direct Product

Suppose the group
G
is a direct product of groups
N,K
. Then we have two normal subgroups in
G
isomorphic to
N
and
K
. We will abuse notation and label these normal subgroups by
N,K
itself. Moreover, these normal subgroups should satisfy

N\cap K=\mathds{Z}_{1},~G=NK~.

(4.34)

The second condition is equivalent to requiring
|G|=|N||K|
(Recall that
|NH|=\frac{|N||H|}{|N\cap H|}
). Therefore, the group
G
is a direct product of
N,K
if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied

1.

N\triangleleft G,K\triangleleft G
.

2.

N\cap K=\mathds{Z}_{1}
.

3.

|G|=|N||K|
.

The direct product structure of a group can be determined from the fusion of surface operators.

Two 2-representations
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
satisfy the fusion rules

\displaystyle S_{\rho}\times S_{\rho}=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{|G|}{|K_{\rho}|}}S_{\rho_{i}}~,

(4.35)

\displaystyle S_{\rho^{\prime}}\times S_{\rho^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{|G|}{|K_{\rho^{\prime}}|}}S_{\rho^{\prime}_{j}}~,

(4.36)

\displaystyle S_{\rho}\times S_{\rho^{\prime}}=S_{\rho_{\text{max}}}~.

where
K_{\rho_{i}}
and
K_{\rho}
belong to the same conjugacy class of subgroups for all
i
, and
K_{\rho^{\prime}_{j}}
and
K_{\rho^{\prime}}
belong to the same conjugacy class of subgroups for all
j
if and only if
G=K_{\rho}\times K_{\rho^{\prime}}
.

To understand this statement, assume the three types of fusions above. From section
4.1
we know that first and second fusion implies that
K_{\rho}
and
K_{\rho^{\prime}}
are normal subgroups of
G
. The third fusion above implies that (using (
3.7
))

|K_{\rho}\backslash G/K_{\rho^{\prime}}|=1\text{ and }K_{\rho}\cap K_{\rho^{\prime}}=\mathds{Z}_{1}~.

(4.37)

Since
K_{\rho}
and
K_{\rho^{\prime}}
are normal subgroups, we have

|K_{\rho}\backslash G/K_{\rho^{\prime}}|=\frac{|G|}{|K_{\rho}K_{\rho^{\prime}}|}~.

(4.38)

The fusion rules above imply that
|K_{\rho}K_{\rho^{\prime}}|=|G|
which using
K_{\rho}\cap K_{\rho^{\prime}}=\mathds{Z}_{1}
implies
|K_{\rho}||K_{\rho^{\prime}}|=|G|
. Therefore, the normal subgroups
K_{\rho}
and
K_{\rho^{\prime}}
satisfies the three conditions for
G
to be a direct product
K_{\rho}\times K_{\rho^{\prime}}
.

Conversely, if
G=K_{\rho}\times K_{\rho^{\prime}}
, then
K_{\rho}
and
K_{\rho^{\prime}}
are normal subgroups satisfying
K_{\rho}\cap K_{\rho^{\prime}}=\mathds{Z}_{1}
and
|K_{\rho}||K_{\rho^{\prime}}|=|G|
. Then, using (
3.7
) it is easily verified that the corresponding 2-representations
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
satisfy the fusion rules (
4.35
).

Reconstructing abelian
G

From section
4.1
, we know that the order of all normal subgroups of
G
and their intersections can be determined from the fusion rules of surface operators. In particular, we can check whether all subgroups are normal. If not, then the group
G
is not abelian. If all subgroups are normal, then
G
is of the form
Q_{8}\times A
where
A
is an abelian group

(G)

3.37

is the unique group of order

3.37
). Indeed, the groups of order 8 up to isomorphism are
\mathds{Z}_{8},\mathds{Z}_{2}\times\mathds{Z}_{4},\mathds{Z}_{2}\times\mathds{Z}_{2}\times\mathds{Z}_{2},D_{8}

If

Derived Subgroup

Now that we know how to determine whether a group is abelian or not using its 2-representations, we can also determine the derived subgroup of

from 2-representations. The derived subgroup
[G,G]
is the smallest normal subgroup such that

G

4.4
Distinguishing groups with isomorphic Wilson line fusion

4.4.1
D_{n}
v/s
Q_{n}

As we saw in section
3.3
, the
D_{8}
and
Q_{8}
groups can be distinguished using the number of 2-representations. To relate this to our discussion in section
4.3
note that
D_{8}\simeq\mathds{Z}_{4}\rtimes\mathds{Z}_{2}
while
Q_{8}
is not a semi-direct product. Since fusion rules of surface operators can be used to distinguish a group admitting a semi-direct product presentation from a group that is not a semi-direct product, it follows that
D_{8}
and
Q_{8}
can be distinguished.

More generally, let us consider the Dihedral groups
D_{n}
and generalized quaternion group
Q_{m}
. These groups have the presentations

\displaystyle D_{n}:=\langle r,s|r^{\frac{n}{2}}=s^{2}=(sr)^{2}=1\rangle~,

(4.43)

\displaystyle Q_{2^{m}}:=\langle a,b|a^{2^{n-1}}=b^{4}=1,a^{2^{n-2}}=b^{2},bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle~,

(4.44)

where
n
is an even integer and
m
is an arbitrary integer. It is known that
D_{2^{r}}
and
Q_{2^{r}}
for all
r\geq 3
are non-isomorphic and have the same character table (see page 64 of
[
34
]
).
However, we can use the fusion of surface operators to distinguish this infinite family of pairs of groups. This is because the dihedral group
D_{n}
is a semi-direct product
\mathds{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathds{Z}_{2}
while the generalized quaternion group cannot be written as a semi-direct product. To understand this statement, note that any
x\in Q_{2^{r}}
has order
2^{l}
for some
1\leq l\leq 2^{r}
. Then the order of
x^{2^{l-1}}
is
2
. It follows that any subgroup of
Q_{2^{r}}
contains order two elements. If
Q_{2^{r}}=N\rtimes K
for some subgroups
N
and
K
and
N\cap K=1
, then
Q_{2^{r}}
should have at least two order two elements. However,
Q_{2^{r}}
has only one element of order two. Therefore,
Q_{2^{r}}
is not a semi-direct product.

4.4.2
Groups of order
p^{3}

Note that both
D_{8}
and
Q_{8}
are groups of order
2^{3}
. We can generalize this example to study groups of order
p^{3}
for any prime
p
. It is known that there are only two non-abelian groups of order
p^{3}

[
35
]
. Both of them have the same character table (see page 562, 16.14 in
[
36
]
) and hence cannot be distinguished using the fusion of Wilson lines. Both are semi-direct products as well. One is the Heisenberg group
H_{p}:=(\mathds{Z}_{p}\times\mathds{Z}_{p})\rtimes\mathds{Z}_{p}
while the other one is
G_{p}:=\mathds{Z}_{p^{2}}\rtimes\mathds{Z}_{p}
.
H_{p}
and
G_{p}
can be distinguished using the fact that all non-trivial elements of
H_{p}
have order
p
while
G_{p}
has a normal subgroup isomorphic to
\mathds{Z}_{p^{2}}
.

Suppose we are given a set of fusion rules for surface operators
S_{\rho}
labelled by 2-representations in 2Rep
(G)
for some non-abelian group of order
p^{3}
. Using (
4.11
) we can determine the surface operators for which
K_{\rho}
has order
p^{2}
.
K_{\rho}
is isomorphic to either
\mathds{Z}_{p^{2}}
or
\mathds{Z}_{p}\times\mathds{Z}_{p}
. Also,
H^{2}(\mathds{Z}_{p^{2}},U(1))=\mathds{Z}_{1}
while
H^{2}(\mathds{Z}_{p}\times\mathds{Z}_{p},U(1))=\mathds{Z}_{p}
. Using (
4.12
), we can determine distinct 2-representations with the same
K_{\rho}
but different
c_{\rho}
. If we find only one 2-representation with the same
K_{\rho}
, then this implies that
H^{2}(K_{\rho},U(1))=\mathds{Z}_{1}
and therefore
K_{\rho}=\mathds{Z}_{p^{2}}
which in turn implies that the group
G=G_{p}
. Otherwise,
G=H_{p}
.

4.4.3
Isocategorical Groups

Two groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
are called isocategorical if they have the same tensor category of representations. Such groups were defined and characterised in
[
10
]
. They find an infinite family of non-isomorphic isocategorical groups constructed as follows. Let
V=Y\oplus Y^{*}
be a symplectic vector space constructed from a vector space
Y
of dimension
\geq 3
over a field of two elements. Consider the group
G=V\rtimes\text{Sp}(V)
where Sp
(V)
is the group of symplectic linear transformations on
V
.
G
is isocategorical but not isomorphic to a group
G_{b}
which is an extension of Sp
(V)
by
V
using a cohomology class
b
.
V
is the unique normal subgroup of
G
and
G_{b}
of order
|V|

[
10
]
. Therefore, the subgroup
V
can be identified using the fusion of surface operators. Since
G
is a semi-direct product while
G_{b}
is a non-split group extension, using the results in section
4.3
it is clear that we can distinguish these two groups using surface operator fusion.

4.5
Groups with isomorphic surface operator fusion

In this section, we will derive necessary conditions for two groups to have isomorphic surface operator fusion and find a pair of such groups.
Consider two groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
with the set of 2-representations 2Rep
(G_{1})
and 2Rep
(G_{2})

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we explored the fusion rules of Wilson lines and condensation surface operators obtained from higher-gauging Wilson lines in G-gauge theory, and the properties of the gauge group that can be derived from these fusion rules. We saw that the fusion of Wilson lines do not determine the group
G
up to isomorphism. However, we can determine several properties of
G
from them including the lattice of normal subgroups of
G
. We then looked at the fusion of surface operators obtained from condensing the Wilson lines on surfaces. We showed that the fusion of surface operators also determines the lattice of normal subgroups. Moreover, we can determine the order of the intersection of normal subgroups with all other subgroups. This allowed us to determine whether
G
admits a presentation as a direct product, semi-direct product or a non-split group extension using the fusion rules. We used this fact to show that infinite families of pairs of groups which cannot be distinguished using the fusion of Wilson loops can be distinguished using the fusion of surface operators.

We defined isomorphism of fusion rules of surface operators for two groups. We derived several necessary conditions for such an isomorphism to exist. These necessary conditions pointed us to the pair of groups SmallGroup(5887,6) and SmallGroup(5887,7) which have isomorphic fusion rules for surface operators. This pair of groups can be distinguished using fusion of Wilson lines which shows that the character table of a group cannot be determined from the fusion of surface operators. We learned that, in general, the fusion of Wilson lines contain information about the gauge group that is not accessible from the fusion of surface operators and vice-versa. Finally, we looked at the fusion of non-genuine line operators which live on surface operators and showed that their fusion rules determine the gauge group up to isomorphism.

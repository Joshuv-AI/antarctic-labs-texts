An oscillation free local discontinuous Galerkin method for nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations
Qi Tao, Yong Liu, Yan Jiang, Jianfang Lu

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we develop an oscillation free local discontinuous Galerkin (OFLDG) method for solving nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations. Following the idea of our recent work
[
31
]
, we add the damping terms to the LDG scheme to control the spurious oscillations when solutions have a large gradient. The
L^{2}
-stability and optimal priori error estimates for the semi-discrete scheme are established. The numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed method maintains the high-order accuracy and controls the spurious oscillations well.

Keywords.
degenerate parabolic equations; oscillation free; discontinuous Galerkin methods;
optimal error estimates.

AMS classification.
65M12, 65M60

1
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in designing an oscillation free local discontinuous
Galerkin (OFLDG) method for solving the nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations in the following form:

\displaystyle u_{t}+\nabla\cdot\big(\bm{f}(u)-\bm{a}(u)\,\nabla u\big)=0,\quad\bm{a}(u)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},

(1.1)

where
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})^{T}\in\Omega
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open
and bounded. The flux functions
\bm{f}(u)=\big(f_{1}(u),\ldots,f_{d}(u)\big)^{T}
and

\bm{a}(u)=[a_{ij}(u)]_{d\times d}
is a positive semidefinite matrix.
In particular, when
\bm{a}(u)=0
, the scalar hyperbolic conservation laws are served as
the special cases of (
1.1
). It also includes the heat equation and the porous
medium type equations which are often termed as

degenerate parabolic equations
(DPEs),
that is,
\bm{a}(u)
vanishes for some certain values of
u
.
Consequently, the partial differential equations of type (
1.1
) model
a wide range of phenomena, such as porous media flow
[
4
]
,
glacier movement and growth
[
25
]
and sedimentation processes

[
9
]
, etc.
For the non-degenerate (
\bm{a}(u)\neq 0
for all
u
) problem (
1.1
), it is widely known
that it admits a unique classic solution. However, when
\bm{a}(u)=0
for some
u
, the solution
may not be smooth anymore due to the hyperbolic nature of (
1.1
). Some theoretical results on
the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
1.1
) can be found in
e.g.
[
1
,
19
,
26
]
and the references therein.
In the degenerate case, the solution is often non-smooth and we have to seek
a weak solution. The low regularity of the solution also
brings difficulties to the numerical simulation, especially for the high order methods.
In fact, the spurious oscillations may occur near the interfaces and wave fronts
that are harmful to the robustness of the numerical algorithm.
To overcome this difficulty, various schemes and approaches have been developed in
the literature, such as interface tracking algorithms
[
20
]
,
diffusive kinetic schemes
[
3
]
, relaxation
schemes
[
10
]
, finite difference/volume weighted essentially non-oscillatory (WENO) methods
[
2
,
24
,
27
]
,
entropy stable schemes with artificial viscosity
[
23
]
,
method of lines transpose (
{\rm MOL}^{T}
) approach with nonlinear filters
[
12
]
,
discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods with maximum-principle-satisfying limiters
[
36
,
41
]
,
local DG finite element methods
[
40
]
, direct DG methods
[
30
]
, etc.

In this paper, we focus on the DG method and extend our previous
work
[
31
]
to the nonlinear convection-diffusion problem (
1.1
).
Compared with the continuous finite element method, the DG method has its own
advantages such as the allowance of the hanging nodes, easy
h
-
p
adaptivity,
and high parallel efficiency because of the extremely local data structure.
The first DG method was introduced by
Reed and Hill to solve a steady linear transport problem
[
35
]
in 1973.
Later on, Cockburn et al.
combined the DG discretization in space with the Runge-Kutta time discretization method to
solve the hyperbolic conservation laws successfully in a series of papers

[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
Enlightened by
[
5
,
6
]
,
Cockburn and Shu developed the local
discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) method to solve the convection-diffusion equations in
[
18
]
.
Since the solution of (
1.1
) may not have enough regularity, the DG method
becomes a natural choice for its ability to deal with non-smooth solutions.
Conventionally, there are two approaches to deal with the
spurious oscillations in the DG method. One is to apply the slope limiters to the numerical
solutions at each time level to make them meet specific needs.
There exist many effective and efficient limiters such as the
minmod
type total variation
diminishing (TVD) limiter, total variation bounded (TVB) limiter, weighted essentially
non-oscillatory (WENO) limiter and moment-based limiter

[
7
,
15
,
34
,
42
]
, etc.
Another is to add an artificial diffusion term in the weak formulation, while the artificial
diffusion coefficient should be chosen adequately, see e.g.
[
21
,
22
]
. Recently, we proposed a different approach in controlling the spurious
oscillations for computing the hyperbolic conservation laws in

[
31
,
28
]
and shallow water equations in
[
29
]
.
The key ingredient is to add a numerical damping term in the existing
DG scheme, and the added term is a high order term if the solution stays smooth and
takes effect whenever the solution is non-smooth.
Fortunately, this approach inherits many good properties such as conservation,

L^{2}
-boundedness, optimal error estimates and superconvergence results
from the conventional DG scheme, which makes it quite attractive.
Besides, this approach is so local that it is efficient and friendly for parallel computation.

We proceed to extend this approach to the
convection-diffusion problems (
1.1
) with possibly degenerate diffusion terms.
We adopt the LDG scheme in
[
11
]
, in which it considered the
generalized alternating numerical fluxes, which are more general and complex in the
numerical analysis.
Similar to
[
11
]
, we can also obtain the
L^{2}
-boundedness and optimal
error estimates, while the added damping term can be estimated separately.
It should be noted that the optimal error estimates are based on the so-called

generalized Gauss-Radau
(GGR) projection technique developed in
[
32
]
.
In the numerical simulation, we test several commonly used equations such as
the porous medium equations, Buckley-Leverett
equations, as well as other degenerate parabolic problems. The numerical results show
that our scheme not only possesses the high order accuracy for the smooth
solutions but also can compress the spurious oscillations effectively. This also verifies
the theoretical results and demonstrates the good performance of the proposed algorithm.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we consider the one-dimensional
degenerate parabolic equation (DPE) and propose an oscillation free
local discontinuous Galerkin (OFLDG) scheme. The theoretical analysis
on
L^{2}
-boundedness and optimal
error estimates are derived in the semi-discrete framework.
In Section
3
, we extend the 1D case to multidimensional
problems and obtain similar theoretical results. In Section
4
, we
conduct some numerical experiments by computing different kinds of DPEs, including porous
medium equations, Buckley-Leverett equations in both one and two dimensions.
Concluding remarks are given in Section
5
.

Throughout this paper, we adopt the standard notations in Sobolev
space.
W^{m,p}(D)
on the subdomain
D\subset\Omega
is equipped
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,p}(D)}
.
If
p=2
,
W^{m,2}(D)=H^{m}(D)
,

\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,2}(D)}=\|\cdot\|_{H^{m}(D)}
. We use
\|\cdot\|_{D}
to
denote the
L^{2}
norm in
D
, if
D=\Omega
then we omit the subscript
D
.
For all positive integer
N
, we define
Z_{N}=\{1,\cdots,N\}
.

2
The OFLDG scheme in one dimension

In this section, we present the OFLDG scheme for the following one-dimensional nonlinear
degenerate parabolic equations,

\displaystyle u_{t}+\big(f(u)-a(u)u_{x}\big)_{x}=0,\quad x\in\Omega=[a,b],~~t\in(0,T],

(2.1)

with the initial condition
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
,
x\in\Omega
and periodic or compactly supported
boundary conditions. Here,
f(u)
is the flux function and
a(u)\geq 0
is the viscous coefficient.

2.1
Basic notations

Firstly, we give some notations that will be used later. Let
I_{h}
be a partition of the domain

\Omega
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle a=x_{\frac{1}{2}}<x_{\frac{3}{2}}<\cdots<x_{N+\frac{1}{2}}=b.

For
j\in Z_{N}
, we denote
I_{j}=\big(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)
,
x_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\big(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)
,
h_{j}=x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}
. Furthermore, we assume that the mesh is quasi-uniform,
i.e. there exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle 0<\frac{h}{\rho}<\gamma,\quad\text{ where }~h=\max_{j}=h_{j},\quad\rho=\min_{j}{h_{j}}.

(2.2)

We define the finite element space as follows,

\displaystyle V_{h}^{k}=\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):v_{h}\big|_{I_{j}}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(I_{j}),~~j\in Z_{N}\},

(2.3)

where
\mathcal{P}^{k}(I_{j})
is the polynomial of degree at most
k
in
I_{j}
. We denote the right
and left limits of
v_{h}
at
x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
as
(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}
and
(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}
, respectively.
The jump and average of
v_{h}
at
x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are denoted as:

\displaystyle[\mspace{-2.5mu}[v_{h}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}-(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\quad\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{h}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\Big((v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}+(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}\Big).

We also define
\displaystyle\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{h}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}^{\theta}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\theta(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}+(1-\theta)(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}
,
for an arbitrary parameter
\theta
. We use
\|\cdot\|_{\Gamma_{h}}
to denote the semi-norm
on the boundary, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}=\sum_{j}\Big(\big((v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)^{2}+\big((v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}\big)^{2}\Big)\,.

2.2
The OFLDG scheme

To derive the OFLDG method for (
2.1
), we introduce a new auxiliary variable
q=b(u)u_{x}
,
with
b(u)=\sqrt{a(u)}
. Then, the resulting system is of the form

\displaystyle u_{t}+\big(f(u)-b(u)q\big)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t\in(0,T],

(2.4)

\displaystyle q-g(u)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,\quad x\in\Omega,

(2.5)

where
g(u)=\displaystyle\int^{u}b(u)du
is the diffusion flux for the auxiliary variable
q
.
We define the unknown
\bm{w}=(u,q)^{T}
and the flux function

\bm{h}(\bm{w})=\big(h_{u}(\bm{w}),h_{q}(\bm{w})\big)^{T}=\big(f(u)-b(u)q,\,-g(u)\big)^{T}.

The semi-discrete OFLDG scheme is defined as follows:
seek
\bm{w}_{h}=(u_{h},q_{h})^{T}\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{2}
such that
for any test functions
v_{h}
,
r_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
and
j\in Z_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle((u_{h})_{t},v_{h})_{j}

\displaystyle=H_{j}\big(h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)+D_{j}(u_{h},v_{h}),

(2.6)

\displaystyle(q_{h},r_{h})_{j}

\displaystyle=G_{j}\big(h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),r_{h}\big),

(2.7)

where,
H_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
G_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
D_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle H_{j}\big(h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)=\big(h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),(v_{h})_{x}\big)_{j}-\widehat{h_{u}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(v_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}+\widehat{h_{u}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(v_{h})_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{+},

\displaystyle G_{j}\big(h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),r_{h}\big)=\big(h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),(r_{h})_{x}\big)_{j}-\widehat{h_{q}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(r_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}+\widehat{h_{q}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(r_{h})_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{+},

\displaystyle D_{j}(u_{h},v_{h})=-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}(u_{h}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u_{h})v_{h}\,dx.

Here, we use the notation
\displaystyle(r,v)_{j}=\int_{I_{j}}rv\,dx
, for all
r,v\in L^{2}(I_{j})
.
The “hat” terms are numerical fluxes, defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{h_{u}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\hat{f}\big((u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},(u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)-\frac{[\![g(u_{h})]\!]_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{[\![u_{h}]\!]_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{q_{h}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\gamma[\![q_{h}]\!]_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\widehat{h_{q}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=-\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{g(u_{h})\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\gamma[\![u_{h}]\!]_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\gamma=\Big(\theta-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\frac{[\mspace{-2.5mu}[g(u_{h})]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{[\![u_{h}]\!]_{j+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R},

(2.9)

where,
\hat{f}\big((u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},(u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)
is a monotone flux for
f(u)
, such as the Lax-Friedrichs flux
[
15
]
.

D_{j}(u_{h},v_{h})
in (
2.6
) is the damping term to control spurious oscillations.
In particular,
P_{h}^{\ell-1}
in the damping term is the standard local
L^{2}
projection into
V_{h}^{\ell-1}
,

\ell=1,\cdots,k
, and we define
P_{h}^{-1}=P_{h}^{\,0}
. Parameter
\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})
is the damping coefficient
taken as following:

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})=\frac{2(2\ell+1)h^{\ell}}{(2k-1)\ell!}\Big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}u_{h}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{2}+[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}u_{h}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq k,\,k\geq 1.

(2.10)

Next, we will present the
L^{2}
stability and optimal error estimates results for the OFLDG scheme
(
2.6
)-(
2.7
).

Theorem 2.1
.

For periodic or compactly supported boundary conditions, the solution
\bm{w}_{h}=(u_{h},q_{h})^{T}

of the semi-discrete OFLDG scheme (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) satisfies the following
L^{2}
stability, i.e

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{h}\|^{2}+\|q_{h}\|^{2}\leq 0.

(2.11)

Proof.

We take
v_{h}=u_{h}
,
r_{h}=q_{h}
in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) respectively. After summing it over
j
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{h}\|^{2}+\|q_{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\Big(H_{j}\big(h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),u_{h}\big)+G_{j}\big(h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),q_{h}\big)\Big)+\sum_{j}D_{j}(u_{h},u_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j}\Theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{j}D_{j}(u_{h},u_{h}).

Then, (
2.11
) follows from

\displaystyle\Theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{(u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}^{(u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\Big(f(y)-\hat{f}\big((u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},(u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)\Big)\,dy\geq 0,

\displaystyle D_{j}(u_{h},u_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}(u_{h}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u_{h})u_{h}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}(u_{h}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u_{h})^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

∎

2.3
Error estimates

In this subsection, we give the optimal error estimates of the OFLDG scheme for smooth solutions
of (
2.1
) with periodic boundary conditions and smooth initial conditions.
We follow the similar approach in
[
11
]
. Since the additional damping term is used in the
OFLDG scheme to control the spurious oscillations, we need to prove that the damping term would
not destroy the accuracy. Due to the nonlinear nature of the flux function
\bm{h}(\bm{w})
, we treat it
by Taylor expansion as in
[
11
,
39
]
.
Therefore, we need
a priori assumption
that for sufficiently small
h
, there holds

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Ch.

(2.12)

This assumption is frequently used in the analysis of nonlinear problems. For the linear flux functions,
i.e.
f(u)=cu
, the assumption is not necessary. In fact, this assumption can be justified for
k\geq 1
,
see
[
33
,
39
]
. To utilize the Taylor expansion, we need to ensure that
the
f(u)
and
b(u)
and their derivatives are bounded. Hence, we assume
f(u)
and
b(u)\in C^{2}
.

2.3.1
One-dimensional projection

First of all, we present the projection that will be used in the error estimates.
For a given vector function
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\,v_{2})^{T}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}
, we define the projection
\Pi\bm{v}
:

\displaystyle\Pi\bm{v}=(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,v_{1},\,\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta}\,v_{2})^{T}\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{2}\,,

where
\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,v_{1}
is the generalized Gauss-Radau (GGR) projection of
v_{1}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}v_{1})v_{h}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{j}}v_{1}v_{h}\,dx,\quad\forall\,v_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(I_{j}),~j\in Z_{N},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{\mathbb{G}_{\theta}v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}^{\theta}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}^{\theta}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\quad\forall\,j\in Z_{N}.

(2.14)

\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta}\,v_{2}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta}v_{2})v_{h}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{j}}v_{2}v_{h}\,dx,\quad\quad\forall\,v_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(I_{j}),~j\in Z_{N},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta}v_{2}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}^{\tilde{\theta}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{2}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}^{\tilde{\theta}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\theta-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\big(b(v_{1})_{x}[\mspace{-2.5mu}[v_{1}-\mathbb{G}_{\theta}v_{1}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]\big)_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\,j\in Z_{N}.

(2.16)

Throughout this paper, we denote
\tilde{\theta}=1-\theta
for convenience. For the projection
\Pi
,
there exists the following approximation results which were shown in
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})^{T}\in[H^{s+1}(\Omega)]^{2}
,
s\geq 0
,
\theta>1/2
. The projection

\Pi:[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}\rightarrow[V_{h}^{k}]^{2}
is well defined by (
2.13
)-(
2.16
). Moreover, there holds the approximation property

\displaystyle\|\eta_{\bm{v}}^{i}\|+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\bm{v}}^{i}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\leq Ch^{\min(k,s)+1}\Big(\|v_{1}\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}+\|v_{2}\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}\Big),

(2.17)

where
i=1,2
,
\eta_{\bm{v}}^{1}=v_{1}-\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,v_{1},\eta_{\bm{v}}^{2}=v_{2}-\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta}v_{2}
, and
C
is a positive constant independent of
h
.

2.3.2
An optimal error estimate

In this section, we present an optimal error estimate for the semi-discrete OFLDG method (
2.6
)-(
2.7
). To this end,
we assume
f^{\prime}(u)\geq 0
and adopt the upwind-biased numerical flux for
f(u)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\bm{w}=(u,q)^{T}
be the exact solution of the equation (
2.4
)-(
2.5
).
Suppose
u(x,t)\in L^{\infty}\big((0,T);H^{k+1}(\Omega)\big)
,
u_{t}(x,t)\in L^{2}\big((0,T);H^{k+1}(\Omega)\big)
,

b(u),\,f(u)\in C^{2}
and
f^{\prime}(u)\geq 0
.
Let
\bm{w}_{h}=(u_{h},q_{h})^{T}
be the solution of the semi-discrete OFLDG scheme (
2.6
)-(
2.7
)
with the numerical fluxes (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) and

\displaystyle\hat{f}\big((u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},(u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)=\theta f\big((u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}\big)+(1-\theta)f\big((u_{h})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big),\quad\theta>\frac{1}{2}.

(2.18)

The initial approximation is taken as
u_{h}(\cdot,0)=P_{h}^{k}u(\cdot,0)
,
P_{h}^{k}
is the standard local
L^{2}
projection.
Then we have the following optimal error estimate

\displaystyle\|u(T)-u_{h}(T)\|\leq Ch^{k+1},\quad k\geq 1,

(2.19)

where
C>0
is a constant depending on
u
and its derivatives but independent of
h
.

Proof.

Firstly, we rewrite the error
e_{\bm{w}}=\bm{w}-\bm{w_{h}}=(u-u_{h},p-p_{h})^{T}
in two parts:

e_{u}=u-u_{h}=\eta_{u}-\xi_{u},~\eta_{u}=u-\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u,~\xi_{u}=u_{h}-\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u;

e_{q}=q-q_{h}=\eta_{q}-\xi_{q},~~\eta_{q}=q-\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta}\,q,~~~\xi_{q}=q_{h}-\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta}\,q.

Since the exact solution
\bm{w}=(u,q)^{T}
also satisfies the OFLDG scheme
(
2.6
)-(
2.7
), we have the following error equations:
\forall\,v_{h},r_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
,

\displaystyle((e_{u})_{t},v_{h})_{j}

\displaystyle=H_{j}\big(h_{u}(\bm{w})-h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)-D_{j}(u_{h},v_{h}),

(2.20)

\displaystyle(e_{q},r_{h})_{j}

\displaystyle=G_{j}\big(h_{q}(\bm{w})-h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),r_{h}\big).

(2.21)

Taking
v_{h}=\xi_{u}
,
r_{h}=\xi_{q}
and adding up (
2.20
)-(
2.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle((\xi_{u})_{t},\xi_{u})_{j}+(\xi_{q},\xi_{q})_{j}=

\displaystyle((\eta_{u})_{t},\xi_{u})_{j}+(\eta_{q},\xi_{q})_{j}-H_{j}\big(h_{u}(\bm{w})-h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)

\displaystyle-G_{j}\big(h_{q}(\bm{w})-h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q}\big)+D_{j}(u_{h},\xi_{u}).

Summing over
j
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{q}\|^{2}=&\sum_{j=1}^{N}((\eta_{u})_{t},\xi_{u})_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\eta_{q},\xi_{q})_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}D_{j}(u_{h},\xi_{u})\\
&\!-\!\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(H_{j}\big(h_{u}(\bm{w})\!-\!h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)+G_{j}\big(h_{q}(\bm{w})-h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q}\big)\Big).\end{split}

(2.22)

Now we proceed to estimate the terms in the right hand side of (
2.22
). First we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}((\eta_{u})_{t},\xi_{u})_{j}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\xi_{u}\|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2},

(2.23)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\eta_{q},\xi_{q})_{j}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\xi_{q}\|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{q}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2}.

(2.24)

With the help of the a priori assumption (
2.12
), we could get the estimates
for the last term in (
2.22
) as in
[
11
, Lemma 3.2, Lemma 3.3]
,

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(H_{j}\big(h_{u}(\bm{w})-h_{u}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)+G_{j}\big(h_{q}(\bm{w})-h_{q}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q}\big)\Big)\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{q}\|^{2}+C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2}.

(2.25)

For the damping term
D_{j}(u_{h},\xi_{u})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}D_{j}(u_{h},\xi_{u})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}(u_{h}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u_{h})\xi_{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}\Big(\xi_{u}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}\xi_{u}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u)\Big)\xi_{u}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}\Big(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u)\Big)\xi_{u}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{j}}\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u)\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\|\xi_{u}\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}.

Thanks to the properties of projections
\mathbb{G}_{\theta}
and
P_{h}^{\ell-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u)\|_{L^{2}({I_{j}})}\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u-u\|_{L^{2}({I_{j}})}+\|u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u\|_{L^{2}({I_{j}})}+\|P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u-u)\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle 2\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta}\,u-u\|_{L^{2}({I_{j}})}+\|u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u\|_{L^{2}({I_{j}})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle Ch^{k+1}\|u\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+Ch^{\max(\ell,1)+\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{W^{\max(\ell,1),\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle Ch^{k+1}\|u\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+Ch^{\max(\ell,1)+\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{\max(\ell,1)+1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle Ch^{\max(\ell,1)+\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}.

For the coefficient
\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4(2\ell+1)^{2}h^{2\ell}}{(2k-1)^{2}(\ell!)^{2}}\Big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}(u_{h}-u)]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}+[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}(u_{h}-u)]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2\ell}\Big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\xi_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\xi_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)+Ch^{2\ell}\Big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\eta_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\eta_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}D_{j}(u_{h},\xi_{u})\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}Ch^{\ell+\max(\ell,1)-\frac{1}{2}}\Big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\xi_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\xi_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi_{u}\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}Ch^{\ell+\max(\ell,1)-\frac{1}{2}}\Big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\eta_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\eta_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi_{u}\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}h^{2\ell+1}[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\xi_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}h^{2\ell+1}[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial_{x}^{\ell}\eta_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|\xi_{u}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+C\|\eta_{u}\|\|\xi_{u}\|.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
2.17
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}D_{j}(u_{h},\xi_{u})\leq C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2}.

(2.26)

Therefore, combining equations (
2.23
)-(
2.26
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{q}\|^{2}\leq Ch^{2k+2}+C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi_{q}\|^{2}.

With the Grönwall’s inequality and initial discretization, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\xi_{u}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}.

(2.27)

Finally, combining with the triangle inequality, we obtain the optimal error estimate
(
2.19
).
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the upwind biased flux (
2.18
) is chosen only to obtain the optimal error estimates. One can also obtain the
(k+\frac{1}{2})
-th order error estimate for the monotone numerical flux by the analogous arguments in
[
38
]
.

3
The OFLDG scheme in multidimensions

In this section, we extend the OFLDG method to the multidimensional case.
For simplicity, we only consider the two-dimensional space, and the higher dimensional cases can be
obtained directly by the same line as the two-dimensional one.
We now consider the two-dimensional nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations:

\displaystyle u_{t}+\big(f_{1}(u)-a_{11}(u)u_{x}-a_{12}(u)u_{y}\big)_{x}+\big(f_{2}(u)-a_{21}(u)u_{x}-a_{22}(u)u_{y}\big)_{y}=0,

(3.1)

with the periodic boundary conditions or compactly supported boundary conditions.
(x,y)\in\Omega=[a_{x},b_{x}]\times[a_{y},b_{y}]
,
t\in(0,T]
, and

f_{1}(u)
,
f_{2}(u)
are convective flux functions. The diffusion tensor
\bm{a}(u)

is positive semidefinite and given as

\displaystyle\bm{a}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}(u)&a_{12}(u)\\
a_{21}(u)&a_{22}(u)\end{pmatrix}.

Without loss of generality, we take
a_{11}(u)=a_{1}(u)\geq 0
,
a_{22}(u)=a_{2}(u)\geq 0

and
a_{12}(u)=a_{21}(u)=0.

3.1
Basic notations

Firstly, we assume that a shape regular tessellation of
\Omega
is given as
\Omega_{h}
, with rectangular elements

K_{i,j}=I_{i}\times J_{j}=\big[x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\big]\times\big[y_{j-\frac{1}{2}},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big],~~i\in Z_{N_{x}},~~j\in Z_{N_{y}}.

The union of all element boundaries in
\Omega_{h}
is denoted as
\Gamma_{h}
.
We define the finite element space with the partition
\Omega_{h}
,

\displaystyle W_{h}^{k}=\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):v_{h}\big|_{K_{i,j}}\in\mathcal{Q}^{k}(K_{i,j}),~~i\in Z_{N_{x}},j\in Z_{N_{y}}\},

(3.2)

where
\mathcal{Q}^{k}(K_{i,j})=\mathcal{P}^{k}(I_{i})\otimes\mathcal{P}^{k}(J_{j})
is the tensor
product of two polynomial spaces in which the polynomial degree is at most
k
for each variable.
Now we define

h_{i}^{x}=x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},~h_{j}^{y}=y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-y_{j-\frac{1}{2}},~h_{K_{i,j}}=\max\{h_{i}^{x},h_{j}^{y}\},~h=\max_{i,j}\{h_{K_{i,j}}\}.

For
i\in Z_{N_{x}},j\in Z_{N_{y}}
, we denote
(v_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{\pm}=v_{h}(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm},y)
,
(v_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}=v_{h}(x,y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm})
,
(v_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm,\pm}=v_{h}(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm})
.
Then, we define the average and jump of
v_{h}
at
(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y)
and
(x,y_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
as follows,

\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{h}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}=\frac{1}{2}\big(v_{h}(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{+},y)+v_{h}(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},y)\big),\quad[\mspace{-2.5mu}[v_{h}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}=v_{h}(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{+},y)-v_{h}(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},y).

\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{h}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\big(v_{h}(x,y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+})+v_{h}(x,y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-})\big),\quad[\mspace{-2.5mu}[v_{h}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}=v_{h}(x,y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+})-v_{h}(x,y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}).

The semi-norm on element boundaries in two-dimensional space is defined as follows

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\int_{I_{i}}\big((v_{h})^{+}_{x,j-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}+\big((v_{h})^{-}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}\,dx+\sum_{i,j}\int_{J_{j}}\big((v_{h})^{+}_{i-\frac{1}{2},y}\big)^{2}+\big((v_{h})^{-}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}\big)^{2}\,dy.

3.2
The OFLDG scheme

In this section, we present the OFLDG scheme for the two-dimensional nonlinear parabolic equation
(
3.1
). Similar to the one-dimensional case, we introduce auxiliary variables

q_{1}=b_{1}(u)u_{x}
,
q_{2}=b_{2}(u)u_{y}
, with
b_{1}(u)=\sqrt{a_{1}(u)}
and
b_{2}(u)=\sqrt{a_{2}(u)}

to rewrite (
3.1
) into a first order system,

\displaystyle u_{t}+\big(f_{1}(u)-b_{1}(u)q_{1}\big)_{x}+\big(f_{2}(u)-b_{2}(u)q_{2}\big)_{y}=0,

(3.3)

\displaystyle q_{1}-g_{1}(u)_{x}=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle q_{2}-g_{2}(u)_{y}=0,

(3.5)

where
\displaystyle g_{1}(u)=\int^{u}b_{1}(u)\,du
,
\displaystyle g_{2}(u)=\int^{u}b_{2}(u)\,du
.
We define the unknown variable
\bm{w}=(u,q_{1},q_{2})^{T}
and the flux function

\displaystyle\bm{h}(\bm{w})=

\displaystyle\big(h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}),h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}),h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}),h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w})\big)^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f_{1}(u)-b_{1}(u)q_{1},\,f_{2}(u)-b_{2}(u)q_{2},\,-g_{1}(u),\,-g_{2}(u)\big)^{T}.

The semi-discrete OFLDG scheme is defined as follows:
seek
\bm{w}_{h}=(u_{h},q_{1h},q_{2h})^{T}\in[W_{h}^{k}]^{3}
, such that for all test functions

v_{h},r_{h},p_{h}\in W_{h}^{k}
and
i\in Z_{N_{x}},j\in Z_{N_{y}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}(u_{h})_{t}v_{h}dxdy=

\displaystyle H_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)+H_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)+D_{ij}(u_{h},v_{h}),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}q_{1h}\,r_{h}dxdy=

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),r_{h}\big),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}q_{2h}\,p_{h}dxdy=

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),p_{h}\big),

(3.8)

where
H_{ij}^{1}(\cdot,\cdot),~H_{ij}^{2}(\cdot,\cdot),~G_{ij}^{1}(\cdot,\cdot),~G_{ij}^{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
D_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot)
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle H_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h})(v_{h})_{x}\,dxdy-\int_{J_{j}}\big(\widehat{h_{u}^{1}}(\bm{w}_{h})v_{h}^{-}\big)_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}-\big(\widehat{h_{u}^{1}}(\bm{w}_{h})v_{h}^{+}\big)_{i-\frac{1}{2},y}\,dy,

\displaystyle H_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h})(v_{h})_{y}\,dxdy-\int_{I_{i}}\big(\widehat{h_{u}^{2}}(\bm{w}_{h})v_{h}^{-}\big)_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\big(\widehat{h_{u}^{2}}(\bm{w}_{h})v_{h}^{+}\big)_{x,j-\frac{1}{2}}\,dx,

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),r_{h}\big)=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h})(r_{h})_{x}\,dxdy-\int_{J_{j}}\big(\widehat{h_{q}^{1}}(\bm{w}_{h})r_{h}^{-}\big)_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}-\big(\widehat{h_{q}^{1}}(\bm{w}_{h})r_{h}^{+}\big)_{i-\frac{1}{2},y}\,dy,

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),p_{h}\big)=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h})(p_{h})_{y}\,dxdy-\int_{I_{i}}\big(\widehat{h_{q}^{2}}(\bm{w}_{h})p_{h}^{-}\big)_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\big(\widehat{h_{q}^{2}}(\bm{w}_{h})p_{h}^{+}\big)_{x,j-\frac{1}{2}}\,dx,

\displaystyle D_{ij}(u_{h},v_{h})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma^{\ell}_{K_{i,j}}(u_{h})}{h_{K_{i,j}}}\int_{K_{i,j}}(u_{h}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u_{h})v_{h}\,dxdy.

The numerical fluxes are taken as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{h_{u}^{1}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}

\displaystyle\,=\hat{f_{1}}\big((u_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{-},(u_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{+}\big)-\frac{[\![g_{1}(u_{h})]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}}{[\![u_{h}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{q_{1h}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}-\gamma_{1}[\![q_{1h}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},y},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\widehat{h_{u}^{2}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\hat{f_{2}}\big((u_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},(u_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)\!-\!\frac{[\![g_{2}(u_{h})]\!]_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}}{[\![u_{h}]\!]_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{q_{2h}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\gamma_{2}[\![q_{2h}]\!]_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\widehat{h_{q}^{1}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}

\displaystyle=-\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{g_{1}(u_{h})\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}+\gamma_{1}[\![u_{h}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},y},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\widehat{h_{q}^{2}}(\bm{w}_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=-\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{g_{2}(u_{h})\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}+\gamma_{2}[\![u_{h}]\!]_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}},

(3.12)

where

\gamma_{1}=\Big(\theta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\frac{[\![g_{1}(u_{h})]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}}{[\![u_{h}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}},\quad\gamma_{2}=\Big(\theta_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\frac{[\![g_{2}(u_{h})]\!]_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}}{[\![u_{h}]\!]_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad\theta_{1},~\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

The
P_{h}^{\ell-1}
in the damping term is the standard local
L^{2}
projection into
W_{h}^{\ell-1},\ell=1,\cdots,k
,
and we define
P_{h}^{-1}=P_{h}^{0}
.
The damping coefficient
\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})=\frac{2(2\ell+1)}{(2k-1)}\frac{h^{\ell}}{\ell!}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=\ell}\Big(\frac{1}{N_{e}}\sum_{\bm{v}\in K_{i,j}}\big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}u_{h}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]\Big|_{\bm{v}}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.13)

Here we only consider the jump of
u_{h}
on the vertex
\bm{v}
of two adjacent cells which are shared with edge.
N_{e}
is number of vertexes of
K_{i,j}
.
For more details, see
[
31
]
.
For
L^{2}
-stability of the scheme (
3.6
)-(
3.8
), we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

We assume that simulation over
K_{i,j}\in\Omega_{h}
with the periodic or compactly supported boundary conditions,
then the solution
\bm{w}_{h}=(u_{h},q_{1h},q_{2h})^{T}
of the semi-discrete OFLDG scheme
(
3.6
)-(
3.8
) with the numerical fluxes (
3.9
)-(
3.12
) is stable in the
L^{2}
norm, i.e

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{h}\|^{2}+\|q_{1h}\|^{2}+\|q_{2h}\|^{2}\leq 0.

(3.14)

The proof of this theorem is similar to the one-dimensional case and omit it here.

3.3
Error estimates

In this subsection, we consider the error estimate of the OFLDG scheme (
3.6
)-(
3.8
) with the periodic boundary condition.
Actually, comparing to the LDG method in
[
11
]
, the proposed OFLDG scheme has an additional
damping term to control the spurious oscillation.
Hence, we only need to prove that the damping term does not destroy the accuracy.
Due to the nonlinear nature of the fluxes,
a priori assumption
(
2.12
) is needed in our proof,
In fact, this assumption can be justified for
k\geq 2
in two-dimensional case,
see
[
39
]
.
For the linear flux functions, the assumption is not necessary. Similar to one-dimensional case, we
assume
f_{i}(u)
and
b_{i}(u)\in C^{2},~i=1,2
.

3.3.1
Two-dimensional projections

For a given vector function

\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})^{T}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
,
we define the projection
\bm{\Pi}
:

\displaystyle\bm{\Pi}\bm{v}=\big(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1},\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta_{1},\frac{1}{2}}v_{2},\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\frac{1}{2},\theta_{2}}v_{3}\big)^{T}\in[W_{h}^{k}]^{3}\,.

(3.15)

\bullet

\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1}
is the two-dimensional GGR projection of
v_{1}
,
defined as follows: for all
i\in Z_{N_{x}}
,
j\in Z_{N_{y}}

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1})\,r_{h}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{i,j}}v_{1}r_{h}\,dxdy,\qquad\forall\,r_{h}\in\mathcal{Q}^{k-1}(K_{i,j}),

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{j}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{\theta_{1},y}\,r_{h}\,dy

\displaystyle\,=\int_{J_{j}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{\theta_{1},y}r_{h}dy,\quad\forall\,r_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(J_{j}),

(3.17)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{i}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{x,\theta_{2}}\,r_{h}\,dx

\displaystyle\,=\int_{I_{i}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{x,\theta_{2}}r_{h}\,dx,\quad\forall\,r_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(I_{i}),

(3.18)

\displaystyle\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle=\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{1}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}.

(3.19)

Here and below, we use the following notations:

\displaystyle\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{\theta_{1},y}=\theta_{1}v_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{-}+\widetilde{\theta_{1}}v_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{+},\qquad\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{x,\theta_{2}}=\theta_{2}v_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}+\widetilde{\theta_{2}}v_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+},

\displaystyle\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}=\theta_{1}\theta_{2}v_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-,-}+\theta_{1}\widetilde{\theta_{2}}v_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-,+}+\widetilde{\theta_{1}}\theta_{2}v_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+,-}+\widetilde{\theta_{1}}\widetilde{\theta_{2}}v_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+,+}.

\bullet

\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta_{1},\frac{1}{2}}v_{2}
is defined in the following:
for all
i\in Z_{N_{x}}
,
j\in Z_{N_{y}}

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta_{1},\frac{1}{2}}v_{2})\,r_{h}\,dxdy=\int_{K_{i,j}}v_{2}r_{h}\,dxdy,\quad\forall\,r_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(I_{i})\otimes\mathcal{P}^{k}(J_{j}),

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{J_{j}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta_{1},\frac{1}{2}}v_{2}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{\tilde{\theta}_{1},y}r_{h}\,dy&\,=\Big(\theta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\int_{J_{j}}\big(b_{1}(v_{1})_{x}[\mspace{-2.5mu}[v_{1}-\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]\big)_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}r_{h}\,dy\\
&\quad+\int_{J_{j}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{2}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{\tilde{\theta}_{1},y}r_{h}\,dy,\quad\forall\,r_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(J_{j}).\end{aligned}

(3.21)

\bullet

is defined in the following:
for all

,

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\frac{1}{2},\theta_{2}}v_{3})\,r_{h}\,dxdy=\int_{K_{i,j}}v_{3}r_{h}\,dxdy,\quad\forall\,r_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(I_{i})\otimes\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(J_{j}),

(3.22)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{I_{i}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\frac{1}{2},\theta_{2}}v_{3}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{x,\tilde{\theta}_{2}}r_{h}\,dx&\,=\Big(\theta_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\int_{I_{i}}\big(b_{2}(v_{1})_{y}[\mspace{-2.5mu}[v_{1}-\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}v_{1}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]\big)_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}r_{h}\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{I_{i}}\{\mspace{-6.0mu}\{v_{3}\}\mspace{-6.0mu}\}_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{x,\tilde{\theta}_{2}}r_{h}\,dx,\quad\forall\,r_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(I_{i}).\end{aligned}

(3.23)

It has been proved that the GGR projection
\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}
and projections

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in(H^{s+1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))\times H^{s+1}(\Omega)\times H^{s+1}(\Omega)
,

. For any

\bm{\Pi}\bm{v}
is well defined, and

\displaystyle\|\eta_{\bm{v}}^{i}\|+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\bm{v}}^{i}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\leq Ch^{\min(s,k)+1}(\|v_{1}\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}+\|v_{2}\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}+\|v_{3}\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}),

(3.24)

where
i=1,2,3
,

.

3.3.2
An optimal error estimate

In this subsection, we prove the additional damping term would not destroy the accuracy of the scheme.
Therefore, we only consider a simple case which
f_{i}(u)\geq 0,i=1,2
in the governing equation (
3.1
), and also take the upwind-biased numerical flux for
f_{1}(u)
and
f_{2}(u)
to get the optimal error estimates. Other cases are similar and omit here. Now we present the main result in this subsection as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\bm{w}=(u,q_{1},q_{2})^{T}
be the exact solution of the equation (
3.3
)-(
3.5
).
Suppose
u(x,t)\in L^{\infty}\big((0,T);H^{k+2}(\Omega)\big)
,
u_{t}(x,t)\in L^{2}\big((0,T);H^{k+1}(\Omega)\big)
,

b_{i}(u),\,f_{i}(u)\in C^{2}
and
f^{\prime}_{i}(u)\geq 0,i=1,2
. Let
\bm{w}_{h}=(u_{h},q_{1h},q_{2h})^{T}

be the solution of the semi-discrete OFLDG scheme (
3.6
)-(
3.8
) with
numerical flux (
3.9
)-(
3.12
) and

\hat{f_{1}}\big((u_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{-},(u_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{+}\big)=\theta_{1}f_{1}\big((u_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{-}\big)+(1-\theta_{1})f_{1}\big((u_{h})_{i+\frac{1}{2},y}^{+}\big),\quad\theta_{1}>\frac{1}{2},

\hat{f_{2}}\big((u_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},(u_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)=\theta_{2}f_{2}\big((u_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)+(1-\theta_{2})f_{2}\big((u_{h})_{x,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big),\quad\theta_{2}>\frac{1}{2}.

The initial approximation is taken as
u_{h}(\cdot,\cdot,0)=P_{h}^{k}u(\cdot,\cdot,0)
,

P_{h}^{k}
is the standard local
L^{2}
projection. Then we have the optimal error estimate

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+1},\quad k\geq 2,

(3.25)

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
h
.

Proof.

Similar to one-dimensional case, we also rewrite the error into two parts with the help of the projection
\bm{\Pi}
,

\displaystyle e_{u}=u-u_{h}=\eta_{u}-\xi_{u},\quad\quad\eta_{u}=u-\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u,\quad\xi_{u}=u_{h}-\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u;

\displaystyle e_{q_{1}}=q_{1}-q_{1h}=\eta_{q_{1}}-\xi_{q_{1}},~~\eta_{q_{1}}=q_{1}-\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta_{1},\frac{1}{2}}\,q_{1},~~\xi_{q_{1}}=q_{1h}-\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\theta_{1},\frac{1}{2}}\,q_{1};

\displaystyle e_{q_{2}}=q_{2}-q_{2h}=\eta_{q_{2}}-\xi_{q_{2}},~~\eta_{q_{2}}=q_{2}-\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\frac{1}{2},\theta_{2}}\,q_{2},~~\xi_{q_{2}}=q_{2h}-\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\frac{1}{2},\theta_{2}}\,q_{2}.

Since the exact solution
\bm{w}=(u,q_{1},q_{2})^{T}
also satisfies the OFLDG scheme(
3.6
)-(
3.8
).
Then, for all
v_{h},r_{h},p_{h}\in W_{h}^{k}
we have the following error equations

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}(e_{u})_{t}v_{h}\,dxdy=

\displaystyle~H_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)+H_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),v_{h}\big)

(3.26)

\displaystyle-D_{ij}(u_{h},v_{h}),

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}e_{q_{1}}r_{h}\,dxdy=

\displaystyle~G_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),r_{h}\big),

(3.27)

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}e_{q_{2}}p_{h}\,dxdy=

\displaystyle~G_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),p_{h}\big).

(3.28)

Taking
v_{h}=\xi_{u}
,
r_{h}=\xi_{q_{1}}
,
p_{h}=\xi_{q_{2}}
in (
3.26
), (
3.27
)
and (
3.28
) respectively, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}(\xi_{u})_{t}\xi_{u}\,dxdy=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}(\eta_{u})_{t}\xi_{u}dxdy-H_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)

\displaystyle-H_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)+D_{ij}(u_{h},\xi_{u}),

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}\xi_{q_{1}}\xi_{q_{1}}\,dxdy=

\displaystyle~\int_{K_{i,j}}\eta_{q_{1}}\,\xi_{q_{1}}dxdy-G_{ij}^{1}\big(h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q_{1}}\big),

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i,j}}\xi_{q_{2}}\xi_{q_{2}}\,dxdy=

\displaystyle~\int_{K_{i,j}}\eta_{q_{2}}\,\xi_{q_{2}}dxdy-G_{ij}^{2}\big(h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q_{2}}\big).

Summing it over
i,j
, we can obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{q_{1}}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{q_{2}}\|^{2}\\
=&((\eta_{u})_{t},\xi_{u})+(\eta_{q_{1}},\xi_{q_{1}})+(\eta_{q_{2}},\xi_{q_{2}})+D(u_{h},\xi_{u})\\
&-\Big(H^{1}\big(h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)+H^{2}\big(h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)\Big)\\
&-\Big(G^{1}\big(h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q_{1}}\big)+G^{2}\big(h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q_{2}}\big)\Big),\end{split}

(3.29)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
in two dimensions denotes
\displaystyle(r,v)=\sum_{i,j}\int_{K_{i,j}}rv\,dxdy,

for all
r,v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and

D(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{i,j}D_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot),\,H^{m}(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{i,j}H_{ij}^{m}(\cdot,\cdot),\,G^{m}(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{i,j}G_{ij}^{m}(\cdot,\cdot),\,m=1,2.

Firstly, we have

\displaystyle((\eta_{u})_{t},\xi_{u})+(\eta_{q_{1}},\xi_{q_{1}})+(\eta_{q_{2}},\xi_{q_{2}})\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{q_{1}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{q_{2}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2}.

(3.30)

With the help of the a priori assumption (
2.12
), we could get the following estimates
for the last two terms in (
3.29
) as in
[
11
, Lemma 4.3, Lemma 4.4]
.

\displaystyle-\Big(H^{1}\big(h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)+H^{2}\big(h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{u}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{u}\big)\Big)\leq C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2},

(3.31)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-\Big(G^{1}\big(h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{1}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q_{1}}\big)+G^{2}\big(h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w})-h_{q}^{2}(\bm{w}_{h}),\xi_{q_{2}}\big)\Big)\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{q_{1}}\|^{2}\!+\frac{1}{4}\|\xi_{q_{2}}\|^{2}\\
&\hskip 298.75394pt+C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2}.\end{aligned}

(3.32)

Then, we estimate the damping term
D_{ij}(u_{h},\xi_{u})
.

\displaystyle D_{ij}(u_{h},\xi_{u})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{K_{i,j}}}\int_{K_{i,j}}\big(u_{h}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u_{h}\big)\xi_{u}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{K_{i,j}}}\int_{K_{i,j}}\big(\xi_{u}-P_{h}^{\ell-1}\xi_{u}\big)^{2}+\big(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u)\big)\xi_{u}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{K_{i,j}}}\int_{K_{i,j}}\big(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u)\big)\xi_{u}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})}{h_{K_{i,j}}}\big\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u)\big\|_{L^{2}({K_{i,j}})}\|\xi_{u}\|_{L^{2}({K_{i,j}})}.

Similar to the one-dimensional case, we need to estimate
\big\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u)\big\|_{L^{2}({K_{i,j}})}
and
\sigma_{j}^{\ell}(u_{h})
.
Thanks to the property of projections
P_{h}^{\ell-1}
and
\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u)\|_{L^{2}({K_{i,j}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~2\|\mathbb{G}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\,u-u\|_{L^{2}({K_{i,j}})}+\|u-P_{h}^{\ell-1}u\|_{L^{2}({K_{i,j}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~Ch^{k+1}\|u\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+h^{\max(1,\ell)+1}\|u\|_{W^{\max(1,\ell),\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~Ch^{\max(1,\ell)+1}\|u\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}.

For the coefficients
\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\big(\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\frac{4(2\ell+1)^{2}}{(2k-1)^{2}}\frac{h^{2\ell}}{(\ell!)^{2}}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=\ell}\Big(\frac{1}{N_{e}}\sum_{\bm{v}\in K_{i,j}}\big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}u_{h}-\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}u]\mspace{-2.5mu}]\Big|_{\bm{v}}\big)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=\ell}\frac{h^{2\ell}}{N_{e}}\sum_{\bm{v}\in K_{i,j}}\Big([\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}\xi_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}\Big|_{\bm{v}}+[\mspace{-2.5mu}[\partial^{\bm{\alpha}}\eta_{u}]\mspace{-2.5mu}]^{2}\Big|_{\bm{v}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k}\,.

Then, by the Cauchy-Schwarze inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D(u_{h},\xi_{u})=\sum_{i,j}D_{ij}(u_{h},\xi_{u})&\leq Ch\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\big(\sigma_{K_{i,j}}^{\ell}(u_{h})\big)^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi_{u}\|\\
&\leq C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+Ch^{2k+2}.\end{aligned}

(3.33)

Thus, combining (
3.30
)-(
3.33
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{q_{1}}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{q_{2}}\|^{2}\leq Ch^{2k+2}+C\|\xi_{u}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi_{q_{1}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi_{q_{2}}\|^{2}.

After applying the Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\xi_{u}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}
. Finally, combining with the triangle inequality,
we obtain the optimal error estimate (
3.25
).
∎

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we propose a novel oscillation free local discontinuous Galerkin (OFLDG) method
to solve the nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations. This work is an extension of our recent work
[
31
]
.
The key idea of the OFLDG method is to add some damping to the high order coefficients (
k\geq 1
).
The added damping terms not only preserve the high-order accuracy in smooth regions,
but also control the spurious oscillation well when the solution is of low regularity.
The
L^{2}
-stability and the optimal error estimates of semi-discrete schemes are rigorously established
for both one- and multidimensional nonlinear problems.
Several numerical examples are shown to demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed scheme. Our next work is to extend the current framework to systems such as Navier-Stokes equations.

The Hoyle and associated excited states from the viewpoint of pocket resonances in α \alpha + 8 Be reactions
Teck-Ghee Lee, Orhan Bayrak, Ian J. Thompson, Cheuk-Yin Wong

Abstract
We examine the production of the Hoyle and associated excited states from the viewpoint of pocket resonances in the reaction of an α \alpha -particle on a ground state prolate 8 Be nucleus within the optical model coupled-channel framework. The predicted reaction cross sections, as a function of the center-of-mass energy E cm E_{\rm cm} , show prominent resonances, including the Hoyle resonance. The positions and widths of these resonances are sensitive to the target deformation ( β 2 \beta_{2} parameter) and the parity of the nuclear surface potential − - deeper for the even-parity L L partial waves relative to those for the odd-parity L L partial waves at the surface region because of the Bose-Einstein exchange of the α \alpha -bosons. Decomposing the reaction cross sections to different partial waves, we find that the resonance energies and widths reasonably agree with the available experimental data and previous hyperspherical calculations for the 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} (Hoyle state), 0 3 + 0_{3}^{+} , 1 1 − 1_{1}^{-} and 3 1 − 3_{1}^{-} states of 12 C, except for the narrow theoretical width of the 2 2 + 2_{2}^{+} state. Analyzing the wavefunctions and the resonance widths, we identify the narrow and sharp 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} , 3 1 − 3_{1}^{-} and 2 2 + 2_{2}^{+} resonances as pocket resonances – resonances which occur below the potential barrier, while the broad 0 3 + 0_{3}^{+} and 1 1 − 1_{1}^{-} resonances as above-the-barrier resonances. For astrophysical applications, we also evaluate the astrophysical S ⁡ ( E cm ) S(E_{\rm cm}) -factor for E cm E_{\rm cm} < < 1.0 MeV, for the fusion of α \alpha + 8 Be into the 12 C ( 2 + ) (2^{+}) state based on our estimated s s -wave α \alpha + 8 Be reaction cross section and the associated γ \gamma - and α \alpha -decay widths for the decay of 12 C excited states in the potential pocket.

The reaction cross sections of
\alpha+^{8}
Be obtained from the coupled-channel calculation in Fig.
1
in the last section
arise from the numerical solution of the quantum mechanical problem in the scattering of an
\alpha

8

E_{\rm cm}
of
\alpha
+
8

of
12
C
∗

12

12
C resonances. The CC calculation also reaches its lowest energy limit, below which no numerical CC calculation can be performed because of the finiteness of the spatial integration region and the finite integration grid size. This is, however, an important energy region in astrophysical studies, for which we would like to evaluate the
12
C fusion cross section. It is desirable to represent numerical CC cross sections in analytical phenomenological forms (down to the lowest energy region) for their
theoretical interpretation as well as for their subsequent applications.

We therefore carry out a phenomenological analysis of the CC reaction cross sections for many purposes.
Firstly, from the complex structure of the
\alpha
+
8
Be reaction cross sections,
we wish to extract valuable information on those
12
C states, which have
large overlaps with the
\alpha
+
8
Be channels. Some of these states may be identified as a pocket resonance beneath the potential barrier while others may be an above-the-barrier (Ramsauer-type) resonance

;
Pet62
;
Mar68
.
The extracted information on the positions and widths of the resonances in the
\alpha
+
8
Be reaction can be compared directly with the corresponding experimental data and previous hyperspherical calculations
AR08
. Secondly, we wish to extend our previous analysis of the reaction cross section of Ref.
Won73
on barrier penetration without resonances to new cases of barrier penetration in the presence of resonances. In particular, we can study the resonance profile to examine the mutual interference between the resonance and its background barrier penetration amid a potential barrier, similar to the interference between potential pocket resonances
and the barrier penetration over a double-hump barrier
Won69
.
Thirdly, in addition to extracting reaction information at resonances and their vicinities, we can also extract reaction information on the off-resonance continuum states. Such extraction of the off-resonance CC data provides the necessary tool to determine the barrier penetration for the calculations of the alpha decay width off-resonance continuum states, which may be difficult to obtain by other means. Finally, the analytical description of the reaction cross sections in the lowest energy region allows an analytical extrapolation to extend the reaction cross sections to the lowest energy region which cannot be reached by coupled-channel or other methods.

In the resonance energy region,

which we label with the subscript “contin”, the compound nucleus

The reaction cross section therefore depends on the barrier and the pocket shapes of the potential as a function of the internuclear separation

\cal R_{\rm res}

VI
Summary and Conclusions

The quest for understanding the structure of Hoyle state in
12
C began with Fred Hoyle, in 1954, who solved Salpeter’s puzzle
of
12
C production via the 2-stage 3
\alpha
reaction process in stars, by the prediction of the must-exist resonance 0
+
state in the
12
C
near the 3
\alpha
breakup threshold. The Hoyle and associated states of
12
C are fundamentally interesting
because these states possess exotic triangular-molecular structures with extended size. Moreover, knowing the structure
of the Hoyle state could help reveal how it gives rise to carbon, oxygen, nitrogen, and other light chemical elements that compose
the complex molecules of living things
-
the synthesis of these elements drives the stellar evolutions which ultimately enables
the genesis of life in the Universe. Having said that, the extensive experimental and theoretical investigations
on the properties of the Hoyle and family of states stem from the spectroscopic viewpoints.

In this work, we introduced an alternative founded on the viewpoint of pocket resonances, within a optical model coupled-channel framework, to examine the production of the Hoyle and associated excited states in the nuclear reaction of
\alpha
-particle on a prolate
8
Be nucleus. To do so, we adopted an optical model potential suited for our scattering problem, permitting us to describe and identify the origins of, the narrow and broad resonances present in the reaction cross section in the Hoyle state energy region. While developing the model potential, we discovered that the energies and widths of the resonances, particularly for
L

>
0 partial waves, are extremely sensitive to (i) the target deformation
\beta_{2}
and (ii) the parity of the nuclear surface potential, that is, more attractive for the even
L
partial waves than those of the odd ones owing to the Bose-Einstein exchange of the
\alpha
bosons. Partial-wave decomposition analysis of the reaction cross sections and in comparison with the results from experiments and the 3-body hyperspherical calculations, we found that although the comparison between our theoretical results and these data is not perfect, our optical model coupled-channel calculation has captured the essential elements of the physics in connection to the reaction of the
\alpha
particle on a
8
Be nucleus. Further improvement upon our calculations can be carried, of course, by introducing a
\beta_{4}
deformation parameter that will improve the description of the 2
\alpha
structure of the
8
Be nucleus. Nonetheless, the reasonable agreement between our results and the experimental data, as well as the hyperspherical calculations
-
in terms of the energies and the widths, provides us the confidence that allows for a reasonable estimate of the fusion cross section for
\alpha
+
8
Be reaction, not only at the resonances but also away from the resonances, for which reaction theory can serve well. The important new element here is that at energies away from the resonances and substantially below the barrier, the penetrability of
\alpha
on
8
Be depends on the shape of the interaction potential under which the
\alpha
particle needs to tunneling through. Such detailed structure can only be obtained by an optical model analysis, as carried out here. Consequently, guided by such principles, we parametrize the reaction cross section
\sigma_{R}
for the
\alpha
+
8
Be reaction up to
E
= 4.0 MeV. Furthermore, given the future astrophysical interest, we further examine the fundamental relationship between the reaction and fusion cross section by calculating the
\gamma
- and
\alpha
-decay widths for
\alpha

s
-wave cross-section for
\alpha
+
8
Be fusion into
12
C for
E

<
1.0 MeV.

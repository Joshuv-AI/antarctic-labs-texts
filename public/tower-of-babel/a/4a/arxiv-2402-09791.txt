Strong Hamel functions and symmetries
Ioan Bucataru, Georgeta Creţu

Abstract
. A strong Hamel function is a Hamel function that is the geodesic derivative of some 0 0 -homogeneous function. We prove that strong Hamel functions induce dual symmetries and dynamical symmetries and provide the conditions such that these symmetries are induced by strong Hamel functions. We show that projective deformations by strong Hamel functions preserve the χ \chi -curvature and analyse the relationship with some other functions (Funk and weak Funk functions) preserving curvature tensors under projective deformations. In the flat case, any Hamel function is a strong Hamel function.

53C60, 53B40, 37C79, 37K06, 70H33

1.
Introduction

Hamel functions were introduced by Hamel as solutions to Hilbert’s fourth problem in
[
12
]
, which means they were Finsler functions projectively related to the flat spray. Therefore, Hamel functions originally emerged as solutions to the Euler-Lagrange equations associated to the flat spray. Nowadays, Hamel functions represent a more general class, they are
1
-homogeneous functions that are solutions to the Euler-Lagrange equations for some arbitrary spray.

Hamel functions, as well as strong Hamel functions, frequently appear in Finsler geometry. A Finsler manifold has vanishing
\chi
-curvature if and only if the
S
-function is a strong Hamel function,
[
16
]
. Hamel functions were also used in
[
2
,
3
]
, to extended Beltrami’s Theorem to the Finslerian context. In this two papers, we demonstrated that for two projectively related Finsler functions, if one has constant flag curvature, then the other also has constant flag curvature if and only if the projective deformation is a strong Hamel function.

The connection between a particular strong Hamel function and a geodesic invariant
1
-form (a dual symmetry) has been exploited in
[
4
]
to provide first integrals for a class of Finsler metric.

In this paper we show, in our main Theorem
4.2
, that any strong Hamel function for a geodesic spray induces a dual symmetry and therefore a dynamical symmetry. We also provide the conditions for a dual symmetry, and hence a dynamical symmetry, to be induced by a strong Hamel function.

We analyse various situations where strong Hamel functions appear. For example, in Theorem
5.1
, we show that the
\chi
-curvature of a Finsler metric (and in particular a Finsler metric of constant flag curvature) is preserved by projective deformations if and only if the projective factor is a strong Hamel function. We provide the relationship between strong Hamel functions and other functions: Funk functions preserving the curvature tensor and weak Funk functions preserving the Ricci scalar, by projective deformations. We prove that a
1
-homogeneous function is a Funk function if and only if it is a strong Hamel function and a weak Funk function.

In a recent paper by Crampin
[
8
]
, various relationships between symmetries (invariant vector fields for the canonical symplectic form and the energy function) and geodesic invariants (first integrals) of Finsler spaces are studied. In our work, we focus on obtaining geodesic invariant
1
-forms (dual symmetries, which are not exact) from some special Hamel function. The corresponding vector field, via the symplectic structure, is a symmetry of the geodesic spray but not a Cartan, nor an exact symmetry.

2.
Preliminaries

In this work, we consider
M
a connected, smooth, real and n-dimensional manifold, and assume that all geometric structures are smooth. We denote by
C^{\infty}(M)
the set of smooth functions on
M
, by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the set of vector fields on
M
, and by
\bigwedge^{k}(M)
the set of k-forms on
M
.
Local coordinates on
M
are denoted by
(x^{i})
, while induced local coordinates on the tangent bundle
TM
are denoted by
(x^{i},y^{i})
, for
i\in\{1,...,n\}
.

We denote by
T_{0}M
, the tangent bundle with the zero section removed.
On
TM
there are two canonical structures that we will use further: the Liouville vector field and the tangent endomorphism, given in local coordinates by
\mathcal{C}=y^{i}{\partial}/{\partial y^{i}}
and
J=dx^{i}\otimes{\partial}/{\partial y^{i}}
, respectively.

In this work, inspired by Crampin
[
8
]
, we will use the Liouville vector field as the basic test for positive homogeneity. A geometric object
\mathcal{G}
on
T_{0}M
is positively homogeneous of degree
p
if
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{G}=p\mathcal{G}
. For example,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}J=-J
, hence the vertical endomorphism
J
is
-1
homogeneous.

A system of second order ordinary differential equations on
M
,

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}x^{i}}{dt^{2}}+2G^{i}\left(x,\frac{dx}{dt}\right)=0,

can be identified with a special vector field on
TM
,

\displaystyle G=y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-2G^{i}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}},

that satisfies
JG=\mathcal{C}
. This vector field is called a semispray. If additionally,
G\in\mathfrak{X}(T_{0}M)
and satisfies
[\mathcal{C},G]=G
, we say that
G
is a spray. For a spray
G
, the functions
G^{i}
are
2
-homogeneous in the fibre coordinates.

If we reparameterize the second-order system (
1
), preserving the orientation of the parameter, we obtain a new system and hence a new spray
\widetilde{G}=G-2P\mathcal{C}
.
The function
P\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
is 1-homogeneous, meaning it satisfies
\mathcal{C}(P)=P
. The two sprays
G
and
\widetilde{G}
are called projectively related, while the function P is called
the projective deformation of the spray
G
.

Every spray induces a canonical nonlinear connection, determined by the corresponding horizontal and vertical projectors,
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle h=\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{Id}-[G,J]),\ v=\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{Id}+[G,J]),\ [G,J]=h-v.

In this paper we will use the Frölicher-Nijenhuis formalism,
[
11
]
. Here,
[G,J]
denotes the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket between spray
G
and the tangent structure
J
. The vector valued
1
-form
[G,J]
is
0
-homogeneous. Indeed, using the graded-Jacobi identity for the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket of vector valued forms,

\displaystyle[\mathcal{C},[J,G]]+[G,[\mathcal{C},J]]+[J,[G,\mathcal{C}]]=0,

and considering the homogeneity of
J
and
G
, along with
[G,J]=-[J,G]
, we obtain
[\mathcal{C},[J,G]]=0
. Therefore, the two projectors
h
and
v
are also
0
-homogeneous. Locally, the two projectors
h
and
v
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}\otimes dx^{i},\ v=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}\otimes\delta y^{i},\quad\textrm{with},

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-N_{i}^{j}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}},\ \delta y^{i}=dy^{i}+N_{j}^{i}(x,y)dx^{j},\ N_{j}^{i}(x,y)=\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial y^{j}}(x,y).

An important geometric structure induced by a spray
G
is the curvature tensor
R
associated to the nonlinear connection. It is the Nijenhuis tensor of the horizontal projector, which is the semi-basic vector valued
2
-form:

(3)

R=\frac{1}{2}[h,h]=\frac{1}{2}R_{jk}^{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}\otimes dx^{j}\wedge dx^{k},\ R^{i}_{kj}=\frac{\delta N^{i}_{j}}{\delta x^{k}}-\frac{\delta N^{i}_{k}}{\delta x^{j}}.

Since the horizontal projector
h
is
0
-homogeneous,
[\mathcal{C},h]=0
, then
2[\mathcal{C},R]=[\mathcal{C},[h,h]]=0
, and hence, the curvature tensor
R
is also
0
-homogeneous.

For the geometric setting induced by a spray, we also utilize the induced dynamical covariant derivative, which is a tensor derivation defined by its action on functions and vector fields:

\displaystyle\nabla(f)=G(f),\ \forall f\in C^{\infty}(TM),\quad\nabla X=h[G,hX]+v[G,vX],\ \forall X\in{\mathfrak{X}}(TM).

For a spray
G
and a function
L
on
T_{0}M
, we consider the following semi-basic 1-form, called the Euler-Lagrange 1-form,

(4)

\displaystyle\delta_{G}L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}L-dL=d_{J}\mathcal{L}_{G}L-2d_{h}L=\nabla d_{J}L-d_{h}L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{G\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{i}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}dx^{i}=\left\{\nabla\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{i}}\right)-\frac{\delta L}{\delta x^{i}}\right\}dx^{i}.

If the function
L
is
p
-homogeneous, then the Poincaré-Cartan
1
-form
d_{J}L
is
(p-1)
-homogeneous, and the Euler-Lagrange
1
-form is
p
-homogeneous.

Indeed,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}d_{J}L=d_{J}\mathcal{C}L+d_{[\mathcal{C},J]}L=pd_{J}L-d_{J}L=(p-1)d_{J}L
and, therefore:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}\delta_{G}L=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}L-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}L=\mathcal{L}_{G}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}d_{J}L+\mathcal{L}_{[\mathcal{C},G]}d_{J}L-pdL=p\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}L-pdL=p\delta_{G}L.

Definition 2.1
.

By a
Finsler metric
we mean a continuous function
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}TM\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
, satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

F
is smooth and strictly positive on
T_{0}M
.

(2)

F
is positively homogeneous of order 1, meaning that
F(x,\lambda y)=\lambda F(x,y)
, for all
\lambda>0
and
(x,y)\in TM.

(3)

The metric tensor with components

g_{ij}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}F^{2}}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}\text{ has rank $n$ on $T_{0}M$}.

Remark 2.2
.

The regularity condition 3) of the previous definition is equivalent to the fact that the Poincaré-Cartan 2-form of the regular energy Lagrangian
L=\frac{1}{2}F^{2}
,
\omega_{L}=-dd_{J}L=g_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}
, is non-degenerate and hence it is a
symplectic structure. Therefore, the equation

(5)

i_{G}dd_{J}L=-dL

uniquely determines a vector field
G
on
T_{0}M
, which is called the
geodesic spray
of the Finsler metric.

In Finsler geometry, there are several important non-Riemannian quantities, such as the
S-
function and the
\chi-
curvature
[
16
]
.

For a fixed volume form,
\omega=\sigma(x)dx\wedge dy
, on
T_{0}M
, we define the
S
-
function

S
in terms of the distortion
\tau
:

(6)

\displaystyle S=G\left(\tau\right),\quad\tau=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\det g}{\sigma}.

The
\chi
-
curvature
has several expression, one of them is given in terms of the
S
-function:

(7)

\displaystyle\chi=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{G}S=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\nabla\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial y^{i}}\right)-\frac{\delta S}{\delta x^{i}}\right\}dx^{i}.

Alternative expressions for the
\chi
-curvature were provided by Shen in
[
16
]
.

3.
Strong Hamel functions

Hamel functions were originally introduced as solutions to Hilbert’s fourth problem in
[
12
]
, specifically for the flat spray. Nowadays, these functions represent a larger class, being the solutions for the Euler-Lagrange equations associated to an arbitrary spray.

Definition 3.1
.

A
1
-homogeneous function
f\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
is called a
Hamel function
for the spray
G
if it satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation
\delta_{G}f=0
.

A Hamel function
f\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
is called a
strong Hamel function
(for the spray
G
) if there exists a
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
such that
f=G(f^{\prime})
.

The Euler-Lagrange equations for a Lagrangian function that represents the geodesic derivative of some other function and the relation with the Jacobi equations were studied in
[
6
]
.

From formulae (
7
) and (
6
), we can see that the
S
-function is a strong Hamel function if and only if the
\chi
-curvature vanishes.

Lemma 3.2
.

The Euler-Lagrange
1
-form (
4
) is a projective invariant, when restricted to
1
-homogeneous functions. The class of Hamel functions and strong Hamel functions are preserved by projective deformations.

Proof.

Consider
f\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
a
1
-homogeneous function and
\widetilde{G}=G-2P\mathcal{C}
the projective deformation of a given spray
G
. Using the fact that
f
is
1
-homogeneous, it follows that
d_{J}f
is a
0
-homogeneous
1
-form, which means that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}d_{J}f=0
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\widetilde{G}}f=\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}d_{J}f-df=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}f-df-2\mathcal{L}_{P\mathcal{C}}d_{J}f=\delta_{G}f-2P\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}d_{J}f-d_{J}f(\mathcal{C})dP=\delta_{G}f,

and hence
f
is a Hamel function for the spray
G
if and only if it is a Hamel function for the spray
\widetilde{G}=G-2P\mathcal{C}
.

If there exists a
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}
on
T_{0}M
such that
f=G(f^{\prime})
, then
f=G(f^{\prime})-2P\mathcal{C}(f^{\prime})=\widetilde{G}(f^{\prime})
. Therefore,
f
is a strong Hamel function for the spray
G
if and only if it is a strong Hamel function for any projectively related spray
\widetilde{G}=G-2P\mathcal{C}
.
∎

We provide now a characterisation for Hamel functions in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

We consider
f\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
a
1
-homogeneous function. Then
f
is a Hamel function for a spray
G
if and only if
d_{h}d_{J}f=0
.

Proof.

From Definition
3.1
and one of the equivalent expressions of the Euler-Lagrange
1
-form (
4
), we have that
f
is a Hamel function for the geodesic spray
G
if and only if
d_{J}G(f)-2d_{h}f=0
. If we apply the derivative with respect to the tangent structure in the previous relation we will get that
d_{J}d_{h}f=d_{h}d_{J}f=0
, which is the direct implication of the result.

For the converse we will assume that
d_{h}d_{J}f=0
. We have that

\displaystyle i_{G}d_{h}d_{J}f

\displaystyle=-d_{h}i_{G}d_{J}f+\mathcal{L}_{hG}d_{J}f+i_{[h,G]}d_{J}f

\displaystyle=-d_{h}f+\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}f-d_{v}f=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}f-df=\delta_{G}f,

hence the conclusion is obtained.
∎

In the specific case where the spray is flat, we show that any Hamel function is a strong Hamel function, as we prove next.

Lemma 3.4
.

Consider
G
the flat spray.

i)

A
1
-homogeneous function
f\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
is a Hamel function for the spray
G
if and only if
d_{J}f
is a
d_{h}
-exact
1
-form.

ii)

Any Hamel function is a strong Hamel function.

Proof.

i) From Lemma
3.3
, we have that the
1
-homogeneous function
f
is a Hamel function for the spray
G
if and only if
d_{h}d_{J}f=0
, which means that the
1
-form
d_{J}f
is
d_{h}
-closed. Since
2R=[h,h]=0
, then using a Poincaré-type lemma for the derivation
d_{h}
we have that
d_{J}f
is
d_{h}
-closed if and only if it is
d_{h}
-exact, meaning that there exists a
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}
such that
d_{J}f=d_{h}f^{\prime}
and hence
d_{h}d_{J}f=d_{h}^{2}f^{\prime}=d_{R}f^{\prime}=0
.

ii) For the flat spray
G
and the Hamel function
f
, there exists a
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}
such that
d_{J}f=d_{h}f^{\prime}.
Since
i_{G}d_{J}f=i_{G}d_{h}f^{\prime}
implies
f=G(f^{\prime})
, it follows that the function
f
is a strong Hamel function.
∎

4.
Strong Hamel functions and symmetries

We will see now that strong Hamel functions are uniquely induced by special geodesic invariant
1
-forms, which we will call strong dual symmetries. These correspond, by the Poincaré-Cartan
2
-form, to some special dynamical symmetries of the geodesic spray, which we will call strong dynamical symmetries.

For a
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
, we consider the following
1
-form:

(8)

\alpha=i_{[J,G]}\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}f^{\prime}=\nabla\left(\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}\right)dx^{i}-\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}\delta y^{i}=\left(G\left(\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}\right)-2N^{j}_{i}\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{j}}\right)dx^{i}-\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}dy^{i}.

The
1
-form
\alpha
, introduced in (
8
), is
0
-homogeneous, which means
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}\alpha=0
. A
1
-form (
8
), associated to the distortion function
\tau
, was used in
[
5
]
to characterize first integrals for Finsler functions with vanishing
\chi
-curvature.

For a Finsler function
F
, its energy
L=F^{2}/2
is a regular Lagrangian. Therefore, for the
1
-form
\alpha
, there exists a unique vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(T_{0}M)
such that

(9)

\displaystyle i_{X}dd_{J}L=\alpha.

Using the canonical expression of the symplectic
2
-form,
\omega_{L}=g_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}
, and the last two expressions (
8
) of the
1
-form
\alpha
, we obtain the following expressions of the vector field
X
:

(10)

X=g^{ij}\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{j}}\dfrac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}+g^{ij}\nabla\left(\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{j}}\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}=g^{ij}\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{j}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+g^{ij}G\left(\dfrac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{j}}\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}.

The second equality in (
10
) is due to the fact that the horizontal distribution is a Lagrangian distribution for the symplectic
2
-form
\omega_{L}
, which is equivalent to the fact that the coefficients
N^{i}_{j}
given by (
2
) satisfy
g^{ij}N_{j}^{k}=g^{kj}N_{j}^{i}
,
[
1
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
0
-homogeneous
1
-form
\alpha\in\bigwedge^{1}(T_{0}M)
is called:

(1)

a dual symmetry if it is geodesically invariant, namely it satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=0
,

(2)

a strong dual symmetry if it is a dual symmetry and the semi-basic
1
-form
i_{J}\alpha
is
d_{J}
-closed.

A
(-1)
-homogeneous vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(T_{0}M)
is called:

(3)

a dynamical symmetry if it a symmetry of the geodesic spray, meaning
[G,X]=0
,

(4)

a strong dynamical symmetry if it is a dynamical symmetry and the vector field
JX
is a vertical gradient.

(5)

an invariant vector field if
X(L)=0
.

A comprehensive discussion on dual symmetries, dynamical symmetries, and their interplay in non-autonomous dynamical systems can be found in
[
14
]
.

We present now the main theorem of this work that connects strong Hamel functions, strong dual symmetries and strong dynamical symmetries.

Theorem 4.2
.

We consider
G
the geodesic spray of a Finsler metric
F
. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

i)

There exists a strong Hamel function for the geodesic spray
G
.

ii)

There exists a strong dual symmetry for
G
.

iii)

There exists a strong dynamical symmetry for
G
.

Proof.

We will prove the equivalence between
i)
and
ii)
. If
f
is a strong Hamel function of the geodesic spray
G
, then it follows that there exists the
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}
such that
f=G(f^{\prime})=\nabla f^{\prime}
and
\delta_{G}f=0
.

Using the commutation rule
d_{J}\nabla=\nabla d_{J}+d_{h}+2i_{R}
, we obtain the following equivalent expression of the
1
-form
\alpha
introduced in (
8
):

(11)

\displaystyle\alpha=\nabla d_{J}f^{\prime}-d_{v}f^{\prime}=d_{J}\nabla f^{\prime}-d_{h}f^{\prime}-d_{v}f^{\prime}=d_{J}f-df^{\prime}.

Now, it is easy to check the geodesic invariance of the
1
-form
\alpha
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}f-\mathcal{L}_{G}df^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}f-dG(f^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}f-df=\delta_{G}f=0.

From the last expression (
11
) of the
1
-form
\alpha
, we obtain
i_{J}\alpha=-d_{J}f^{\prime}
, which means that
i_{J}\alpha
is
d_{J}
-exact and hence
d_{J}
-closed.

For the converse, we will assume that
\alpha
is a
0
-homogeneous
1
-form on
T_{0}M
, which can be written as

\alpha=\alpha_{i}dx^{i}+\beta_{i}dy^{i}
. Since
i_{J}\alpha=\beta_{i}dx^{i}
is a
(-1)
-homogeneous and
d_{J}
-closed
1
-form, it follows that it is
d_{J}
-exact. Therefore, there exists a
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}(x,y)=\beta_{i}(x,y)y^{i}=i_{G}i_{J}\alpha
such that
i_{J}\alpha=-d_{J}f^{\prime}
.
Hence, the
1
-form
\alpha
is given by

(12)

\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha_{i}dx^{i}-\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}dy^{i}.

The geodesic derivative of this
1
-form is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=G(\alpha_{i})dx^{i}+\alpha_{i}dy^{i}-G\left(\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}\right)dy^{i}+\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}2\left(\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}dx^{j}+\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial y^{j}}dy^{j}\right).

If the
1
-form
\alpha
is geodesically invariant, then collecting the vertical components, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=G\left(\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}\right)-2\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{j}}\frac{\partial G^{j}}{\partial y^{i}}=\frac{\partial G(f^{\prime})}{\partial y^{i}}-\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial x^{i}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial y^{i}}-\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial x^{i}}.

It follows that the
1
-form
\alpha
is given by:

\displaystyle\alpha=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y^{i}}-\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial x^{i}}\right)dx^{i}-\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}dy^{i}=d_{J}f-df^{\prime},

which represents formula (
11
). Therefore, we have
0=\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=\delta_{G}f
and hence
f=G(f^{\prime})
is a strong Hamel function.

Now, we prove the equivalence between the second and the third condition. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{G}i_{X}dd_{J}L=i_{X}\mathcal{L}_{G}dd_{J}L+i_{[G,X]}dd_{J}L=i_{[G,X]}dd_{J}L.

Since
dd_{J}L
is a symplectic form, we have that
\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=0
if and only if
[G,X]=0
.

For a vector field,
X=X^{i}{\partial}/{\partial x^{i}}+Y^{i}{\partial}/{\partial y^{i}}\in\mathfrak{X}(T_{0}M)
, homogeneous of order
-1
,
JX=X^{i}{\partial}/{\partial y^{i}}
is a vertical gradient if there exists a
0
-homogeneous function
f^{\prime}
on
T_{0}M
such that
g_{ij}X^{j}=\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial y^{i}}
. This condition is equivalent to the fact that
i_{JX}d_{J}F^{2}=i_{J}\alpha
is a
d_{J}
-closed
1
-form, and this completes the proof of the equivalence of ii) and iii).
∎

As a consequence of the previous calculations and theorem, we can establish further connections between strong Hamel functions (dual symmetries, dynamical symmetries), invariant vector fields and first integrals.

Proposition 4.3
.

Consider
X\in\mathfrak{X}(T_{0}M)
a strong dynamical symmetry. Then
X
is an invariant vector field and
JX(L)
is a first integral for the geodesic spray
G
.

Proof.

Consider
X
a strong dynamical symmetry. Using the defining formulae (
5
) and (
9
), for the vector fields
G
and
X
, it follows:

\displaystyle X(L)=i_{X}dL=-i_{X}i_{G}dd_{J}L=i_{G}i_{X}dd_{J}L=i_{G}\alpha=i_{G}(d_{J}f-df^{\prime})=\mathcal{C}(f)-G(f^{\prime})=0.

Using the first expression of the Euler-Lagrange equation (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle X(L)=i_{X}dL=i_{X}\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}L=\mathcal{L}_{G}i_{X}d_{J}L+i_{[X,G]}d_{J}L=G(JX(L)),

which shows that for a dynamical symmetry
X
,
JX(L)
is a first integral for the geodesic spray
G
if and only if
X
is an invariant vector field.
∎

As we mention already, the
S
-function (
6
) is a strong Hamel function if and only if the
\chi
-curvature vanishes. Utilizing formula (
8
) for the distortion
\tau
and Theorem
4.2
, we obtain that the
S
-function is a strong Hamel function if and only if the following
1
-form is geodesically invariant:

(13)

\displaystyle\alpha=\nabla I_{k}dx^{k}-I_{k}\delta y^{k},\quad I_{k}=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial y^{k}}=\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial y^{k}}.

The
1
-form (
13
) has also been considered in
[
8
, Section 8]
, where it has been shown that the corresponding vector field (
9
) satisfies
X(L)=0,
hence it is an invariant vector field.

The geodesic invariance of the
1
-form (
13
), has been exploited in
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
to provide a set of
n-1
first integrals for a Finsler manifold with vanishing
\chi
-curvature (and consequently, with strong Hamel
S
-function), as discussed in
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
.

In the subsequent section, we will delve into additional connections between strong Hamel functions and
\chi
-curvature.

5.
\chi
-curvature and strong Hamel functions

It is established that projective deformations preserve the curvature tensor (
3
) if and only if the projective factor is a Funk function. Similarly, the Ricci scalar is preserved under projective deformations if and only if the projective factor is a weak Funk function,
[
15
, Proposition 12.1.3]
.

We will prove now that the
\chi
-curvature is preserved under projective deformation if and only if the projective factor is a strong Hamel function. Furthermore, we will establish the relationship between Funk functions, weak Funk functions and strong Hamel functions.

We now analyse the behaviour of the
S
-function and
\chi
-curvature under projective deformations.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
and
\widetilde{G}=G-2P\mathcal{C}
be two projectively related geodesic sprays. Then, their
S
-functions and
\chi
-curvatures are related as follows:

(14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}=S+(n+1)P,\quad\widetilde{\chi}=\chi+\dfrac{n+1}{2}\delta_{G}P.

The
\chi
-curvature of a Finsler metric is preserved under projective deformations if and only if the projective factor is a strong Hamel function.

Proof.

For a Finsler metric
F
, the dynamical covariant derivative of its
metric tensor vanishes,
\nabla g_{ij}=0
. Hence
G(g_{ij})-g_{im}N_{j}^{m}-g_{mj}N_{i}^{m}=0
. Contracting with
g^{ij}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle g^{ij}G(g_{ij})=g^{ij}(g_{im}N_{j}^{m}+g_{mj}N_{i}^{j})=2N_{i}^{i},\quad\textrm{and \ hence \ }N_{i}^{i}=\frac{1}{2}G(\ln(\det g)).

The two Finsler metrics
F
and
\widetilde{F}
being projectively
related, their geodesic sprays and nonlinear connections are connected through:

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}=G-2P\mathcal{C},\quad\widetilde{G}^{i}=G^{i}+Py^{i},\quad\widetilde{N}^{i}_{j}=N^{i}_{j}+\frac{\partial P}{\partial y^{j}}y^{i}+P\delta^{i}_{j}.

It follows that the projective factor
P
is given by:

\displaystyle P=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\widetilde{N}^{i}_{i}-N_{i}^{i}\right)=\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\left(\widetilde{G}(\ln\det\widetilde{g})-G(\ln\det g)\right)

Using the definition (
6
) of the
S
-function and distortion
\tau
, for the two projectively related Finsler functions
F
and
\widetilde{F}
, we have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}-S=\widetilde{G}(\widetilde{\tau})-G(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{G}(\ln\det\widetilde{g})-G(\ln\det g)\right)=(n+1)P,

which shows that the first formula (
14
) is true.

Since the
S
-function is
1
-homogeneous, according to the first part of Lemma
3.2
, we have that

\displaystyle 2\widetilde{\chi}=\delta_{\widetilde{G}}\widetilde{S}=\delta_{G}\widetilde{S}=\delta_{G}(S+(n+1)P)=2\chi+(n+1)\delta_{G}P,

which gives the second formula (
14
).

From the second formula (
14
) we have that
\widetilde{\chi}=\chi
if and only if
\delta_{G}P=0
, hence
P
is a Hamel function. Moreover,
2(n+1)P=G(\ln(\det\widetilde{g}/\det g))
, which shows that
P
is a strong Hamel function.
∎

We can use the result of Theorem
5.1
to recover, as a corollary, the Finslerian version of Beltrami’s Theorem, which was proven with different techniques in
[
2
,
3
]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

On a manifold of dimension
n>2
, consider
F
and
\widetilde{F}
two projectively related Finsler metrics and assume that
F
has constant flag curvature. Then,
\widetilde{F}
also has constant flag curvature if and only if the projective factor
P
is a strong Hamel function.

Proof.

A Finsler metric has constant curvature if and only if the geodesic spray is isotropic and the
\chi
-curvature vanishes,
[
13
]
. Since the isotropy condition is projectively invariant, then the second formula (
14
) assures that if one Finsler metric has constant flag curvature then the other also has constant curvature if and only if the projective factor is a strong Hamel function.
∎

An alternative expression for the second formula (
14
), along with its utility in proving a particular Finslerian version of Beltrami’s Theorem in the flat case, has been derived in
[
7
, Proposition 1.1, Theorem 1.2]
.

We will provide now the relation between Funk functions, weak Funk function and strong Hamel function. First, we recall the definition of these functions.

Definition 5.3
.

For a given spray
G
, a
1
-homogeneous function
f
on
T_{0}M
is called a:

i)

Funk function, if it satisfies
d_{h}f=fd_{J}f
;

ii)

weak Funk function, if it satisfies
G(f)=f^{2}
.

From the above definition, one can easily see that Funk functions are weak Funk function. From the expression (
4
) of the Euler-Lagrange operator and Lemma
3.3
, we can see that Funk functions are strong Hamel functions. Next, we present the relation between these functions.

Proposition 5.4
.

A
1
-homogeneous function
f
on
T_{0}M
is a Funk function if and only if it is both a strong Hamel function and a weak Funk function.

Proof.

Consider
f\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
a
1
-homogeneous function. Using the expression (
4
) of the Euler-Lagrange operator, we find that
f
is a Hamel function if and only if it satisfies:

(15)

\displaystyle d_{h}f=\nabla d_{J}f.

By definition, a weak Funk function satisfies
\nabla f=f^{2}
. If we apply the derivation
d_{J}
and use the commutation rule of
\nabla
and
d_{J}
, we obtain

(16)

\displaystyle d_{h}f=2fd_{J}f-\nabla d_{J}f.

Conversely, if we contract both sides of (
16
), which are semi-basic
1
-forms, with the spray
G
, we obtain
G(f)=2f^{2}-\nabla\mathcal{C}(f)
, equivalent to
G(f)=f^{2}
. Therefore,
f
is a weak Funk function if and only if it satisfies (
16
).

It is easy now to see that the two conditions (
15
) and (
16
) are equivalent to
d_{h}f=fd_{J}f
. Hence,
f
is a Funk function if and only if it is a strong Hamel function and a weak Funk function.
∎

Next, we will show how one can obtain examples of strong Hamel functions using projectively related Finsler metrics and some information about the projective factor, following some ideas from
[
9
]
. Consider
\widetilde{F}
a Finsler metric projectively related to a spray
G
, such that the projective factor is a weak Funk function. It follows that
\widetilde{F}
is a strong Hamel function.

Indeed, if
\widetilde{G}
is the geodesic spray of the Finsler metric
\widetilde{F}
, then it is projectively related to the spray
G
,
\widetilde{G}=G-2P\mathcal{C}
. Since
P
is a weak Funk function for the spray
G
, it follows that
G(P)=P^{2}
. Moreover, the projective factor satisfies
G(\widetilde{F})=2P\widetilde{F}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle G\left(\dfrac{\widetilde{F}}{P}\right)=\dfrac{G(\widetilde{F})P-\widetilde{F}G(P)}{P^{2}}=\dfrac{2P\widetilde{F}P-\widetilde{F}P^{2}}{P^{2}}=\widetilde{F}.

Since
\widetilde{F}
is a Hamel function for the spray
G
, and using the above relation, we obtain that
\widetilde{F}
is a strong Hamel function.

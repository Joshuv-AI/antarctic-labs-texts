Abstract
. Topological indices are parameters associated with graphs that have many applications in different areas such as mathematical chemistry. Among various topological indices, the Wiener index is classical [ 25 ] . In this paper, we prove a formula for the Wiener index and more general m m -Steiner Wiener index of an arbitrary graph G G in terms of the cardinalities of its twin classes. In particular, we will show that calculating these parameters for the graph G G can be reduced to calculating the same for a much smaller graph (in general) called the reduced graph of G G . As applications of our main result, the m m -Steiner Wiener index is explicitly calculated for various important classes of graphs from the literature including (a) Power graphs associated with finite groups, (b) Zero divisor graphs and the ideal-based zero divisor graphs associated to commutative rings with unity, and (c) Comaximal ideal graphs associated with commutative rings with unity. We have also found an upper bound on the m m -Steiner Wiener index of an infinite class of graphs called the completely joined graphs. As a corollary of this result, we explicitly calculate the m m -Steiner Wiener index of the complete multipartite graphs.

\small SW_{m}(G)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\

where

Remark 2

The Wiener index is one of the important examples of topological indices that we have calculated in this paper. Using our approach in this paper, we can find a formula for any topological indices in terms of cardinalities of twin classes. Since finding such a formula for other topological indices requires the same work as in this paper, we deal only with the Wiener index and Steiner Wiener index in this paper.

2.2.
Representing arbitrary graphs as generalized composition using twin classes

The results given in this subsection are well-known
[

Let

Let

. For a vertex

if

In general, the vertices

We define an equivalence relation
\sim^{G}
on

as follows. For
u,v\in V(G)
, define

if, and only if,
N_{G}(u)\backslash\{v\}=N_{G}(v)\backslash\{u\}
. In other words, two vertices of

are related by

if they are twin vertices in

. Let

be the equivalence classes of this relation (with respective representatives

) which we call the twin classes of the graph

.

The following lemma is proved in
[
24
, Lemma 3.1]
which illustrates the structure of the graph

in terms of the twin classes.

Lemma 1
.

(1)

The induced subgraph
G(C_{i})
of the graph

is either complete or totally disconnected.

(2)

If

, a vertex

of

is adjacent to either all or none of the vertices of
C_{j}
.

Let
H
be the subgraph induced by the vertices
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}
in

.

is called the reduced graph of the graph

. This smaller graph

reflects many structural properties of the bigger graph

. In particular, we have the following result.

Lemma 2
.

Let

be a graph with at least two twin classes. Then the graph

is connected if, and only if, the graph

Proof.

The proof follows from the more general statement given below.
∎

The following proposition says that

Let

2.3.
The Steiner Wiener index of generalized composition of graphs

In
[
23
, Section 2.4]
, a formula for the Wiener index of the generalized composition of graphs is proved. In this subsection, we extend this formula to
m
-Steiner Wiener index using twin classes.

Definition 2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with vertex set
V
and let
S\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(V)
the set of all
m
-element subsets of the set
V
. The
m
-Steiner distance of the set
S
, denoted by
d_{G}^{m}(S)
, is defined to be the number of edges in the smallest subtree of
G
containing
S
. The
m-Steiner Wiener index
of the graph
G
, denoted by
SW_{m}(G)
, is defined to be

SW_{m}(G)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V\\
|S|=m\end{subarray}}d_{G}^{m}(S).

Let
G
be a connected graph with twin classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
with respective representatives
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}
and
G=H[G_{1},\dots,G_{k}]
where
H=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}
and the factor graphs
G_{i}=G[C_{i}]
’s are either complete or empty (graph with vertices but no edges). Let
V(G_{i})=\{v_{i}^{1},\dots,v_{i}^{n_{i}}\}
be the vertex set of the graph
G_{i}
and
V(G)=\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}V(G_{i})
. Given these, we define the following notions.

Definition 3
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(V(G))
. The intersection number of
S
is defined to be the cardinality of the set
I(S):=\{1\leq i\leq k:S\cap C_{i}\neq\phi\}
. We also define the set
S_{r}=\{c_{i}:S\cap C_{i}\neq\phi\}
which consists of one representative of each twin class which intersects
S
. We also define
t_{i}:=|S\cap C_{i}|
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

The following lemma explains the Steiner distance of certain sets lying in both the graphs
G
and
H
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
S
be a set of
l
vertices of
G
such that the set
S\cap C_{i}
has at most one element for each
1\leq i\leq k
. Then
S=S_{r}
(by changing the representatives

if necessary) and
d_{H}^{l}(S)=d_{G}^{l}(S)
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the definition of the graph
G
.
∎

The following lemma is an extension of
[
23
, Lemma 3]
which explains the
m
-Steiner distance of any set
S
of
m
vertices of
G
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(V(G))
. Then the
m
-Steiner distance of the set
S
in the graph

is given by

d_{G}^{m}(S)=\begin{cases}m-1&\text{ if }I(S)=\{i\}\text{ and }G_{i}\text{ is complete}\\
m&\text{ if }I(S)=\{i\}\text{ and }G_{i}\text{ is totally disconnected}\\
d_{H}^{l}(S_{r})+\sum\limits_{c_{i}\in S_{r}}(t_{i}-1)&\text{ if }l:=|I(S)|>1.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Proof.

Fix
S\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(V(G))
and let
S_{r}=\{c_{i_{1}},\dots,c_{i_{l}}\}
. Without loss of generality, by changing the representatives of the classes
C_{i}
if required, we can assume that
S_{r}\subseteq S
.

Suppose
S_{r}=\{c_{i}\}
and
G_{i}
is complete then the proof is immediate.

Suppose
S_{r}=\{c_{i}\}
and
G_{i}
is totally disconnected. Let
c_{j}\in V(H)
be such that
c_{j}
is adjacent to
c_{i}
. Now, every element of
S
(
=S\cap C_{i}
) is adjacent to
v_{j}^{q}
where
1\leq q\leq n_{j}
. Therefore, the vertices
S\cup\{v_{j}^{q}\}
form a star graph on
m+1
vertices and the result follows.

Suppose
|S_{r}|=l>1
. Let
T
be the smallest subtree of
G
containing
S_{r}
. Then, by the above lemma,
d_{H}^{l}(S_{r})=d_{G}^{l}(S_{r})=h
(say) where
h
is the number of edges in
T
. Let
c_{i}\in S_{r}
. Then there are
t_{i}=|S\cap C_{i}|
many vertices of
C_{i}
are in
S
. If
G_{i}
is a complete graph then we can consider the star subgraph (not necessarily induced) of

formed by these

many vertices whose middle vertex is

. i.e., they can be joined by

edges. Also, we can clue this star graph to the tree

at the common vertex

. If

is totally disconnected then consider the vertex

which is adjacent to
c_{i}
. Since

is adjacent to

, every vertex in

is adjacent to

. This way we get a star subgraph of

formed by the
t_{i}
many vertices
((S\cap C_{i})\backslash\{c_{i}\}\cup\{c\})
whose middle vertex is

(t_{i}-1)
edges. Also, we can clue this star graph to the tree

at the common vertex

are already adjacent in

.

By doing this process for each

, the tree

of

which by construction the smallest subtree containing the set

∎

\small SW_{m}(G)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\

where
|C_{i}|=n_{i}

Proof.

The proof follows from the above discussions.

The Steiner Wiener index of

Definition 4
.

Any graph

Example 1

(1)

(2)

Any graph

(3)

Let

Let

need not be the smallest one. Therefore

in this case. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In the following theorem, we have given an upper bound on the

Theorem 2

Let

where
G_{i}
s are arbitrary graphs be a completely joined graph. Then

SW_{m}(G)\leq\binom{n}{m}(m)

where

.

Remark 4
.

We observe that the above bound on the

-Steiner Wiener index of

depends only on the number of vertices in

and the value of

. In particular, it is independent of the structure of the graphs

for

.

Example 2
.

Consider the wheel graph

In the following corollary, we will explicitly find the

-Steiner Wiener index of the complete multipartite graph

Lemma 6

Consider the complete multipartite graph

. Let

Proof.

Let
G=K_{m_{1},\dots,m_{p}}
be the complete multipartite graph and

be a size

. Note that

where
G_{i}
is the graph
\overline{K_{n_{i}}}
. We assume that

and hence

. Assume that
|I(S)|=1

for some

. Fix a vertex

in the graph

where

. Then

is adjacent to every element of

and hence we can form a subtree

of

on

G_{i}
is totally disconnected and

with

in this case.

Assume

s\in V(G_{i})
for some

and this

is adjacent to

for all

for

of

of size

Therefore the vertices in

∎

Corollary 2
.

From the above lemma, the

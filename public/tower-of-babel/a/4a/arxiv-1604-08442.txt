Jiayu Shao, Lihua You

Abstract
We define three types of upper (and lower) triangular blocked tensors, which are all generalizations of the triangular blocked matrices. We study some basic properties and characterizations of these three types of triangular blocked tensors. We obtain the formulas for the determinants, characteristic polynomials and spectra of the first and second type triangular blocked tensors, and give an example to show that these formulas no longer hold for the third type triangular blocked tensors. We prove that the product of any two ( n 1 , ⋯ , n r ) (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}) -upper (or lower) triangular blocked tensors of the first or second or third type is still an ( n 1 , ⋯ , n r ) (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}) -upper (or lower) triangular blocked tensor of the same type. We also prove that, if an ( n 1 , ⋯ , n r ) (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}) -upper triangular blocked tensor of the first or second or third type has a left k k -inverse, then its unique left k k -inverse is still an ( n 1 , ⋯ , n r ) (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}) -upper triangular blocked tensor of the same type. Also if it has a right k k -inverse, then all of its right k k -inverses are still ( n 1 , ⋯ , n r ) (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}) -upper triangular blocked tensors of the same type. By showing that the left k k -inverse (if any) of a weakly irreducible nonsingular M M -tensor is a positive tensor, we show that the left k k -inverse (if any) of a first or second or third type canonical ( n 1 , ⋯ , n r ) (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}) -upper triangular blocked nonsingular M M -tensor is an ( n 1 , ⋯ , n r ) (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}) -upper triangular blocked tensor of the same type all of whose diagonal blocks are positive tensors. We also show that every order m m dimension n n tensor is permutation similar to some third type normal upper triangular blocked tensor (all of whose diagonal blocks are irreducible). We give an example to show that this is not true for the first type canonical upper triangular blocked tensor. AMS classification: 15A42, 15A18, 15A69 Keywords: tensor, triangular blocked tensor, product, inverse, determinant, M M -tensor

The definitions of the three types of triangular blocked tensors and their basic properties

We first recall the definition of the triangular blocked matrices.

Let
A
be a matrix of order
n
. Let
n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\ (r\geq 2)
be positive integers with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
. Write
S_{0}=0
and

S_{j}=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j},\qquad I_{j}=\{S_{j-1}+1,\cdots,S_{j}\}\quad(j=1,\cdots,r).

If for any
j=2,\cdots,r
, we have

a_{ii_{2}}=0\quad(\forall i\in I_{j},\ \mbox{and}\ i_{2}\leq S_{j-1}),

then we say that
A
is an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-upper triangular blocked matrix.

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}A_{1}&\cdots&\ast&\cdots&\ast\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\cdots&\ast\\
O&\cdots&A_{j}&\cdots&\ast\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
O&\cdots&O&\cdots&A_{r}\\
\end{array}\right)

In the following, we want to generalize this definition from matrices to tensors.

2.1
The definitions of the three types of triangular blocked tensors

Definition 2.1

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an order
m
and dimension
n

n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\ (r\geq 2)

S_{j}=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j},\qquad I_{j}=\{S_{j-1}+1,\cdots,S_{j}\}\quad(j=1,\cdots,r).

(1)
If for any

, we have

a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0\quad(\forall i\in I_{j},\ \mbox{and}\ \min\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S_{j-1}),

(2.1)

then we say that
\mathbb{A}
is a first type
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-upper triangular blocked tensor, or
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-1stUTB tensor (or simply
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor) with diagonal

\mathbb{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{r}

\mathbb{A}_{j}=\mathbb{A}[I_{j}]

(Here the condition

belongs to
I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{j-1}

(2)
If for any

a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0\quad(\forall i\in I_{j},\ \mbox{and}\ \max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\geq S_{j}+1),

(2.2)

then we say that
\mathbb{A}
is a first type
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-lower triangular blocked tensor (or simply
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-LTB tensor) with diagonal blocks

(3)
If for any

a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0\quad(\forall i\in I_{j},\ \mbox{and at least one of }\ i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\notin I_{j}),

then we say that
\mathbb{A}

(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-diagonal blocked tensor with diagonal blocks

b_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=a_{(i+k)(i_{2}+k)\cdots(i_{m}+k)}\quad(i,i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\in[n-k]).

For
j=1,\cdots,r-t
, we further write
n^{\prime}_{j}=n_{t+j}
, and consequently write

S^{\prime}_{j}=n^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+n^{\prime}_{j},\quad I^{\prime}_{j}=\{S^{\prime}_{j-1}+1,\cdots,S^{\prime}_{j}\}\quad(j=1,\cdots,r-t).

Then we have
S^{\prime}_{j}=S_{t+j}-S_{t}=S_{t+j}-k
.

Necessity.

(1). By Definition
2.3
we only need to verify the following

a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0\quad(\forall i>k,\ \mbox{and}\ \max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq k).

Now assume that
i\in I_{j}
. Then
i>k=S_{t}\Longrightarrow j>t
, or
j-1\geq t
. So
\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq k=S_{t}\leq S_{j-1}
. By the hypothesis that
\mathbb{A}
is an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-3rdUTB tensor we have
a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0
. So

\mathbb{A}
is an
(k,n-k)
-3rdUTB tensor with diagonal blocks
\mathbb{A}[I]
and
\mathbb{A}[\overline{I}]
.

(2). Since the condition in Eq.(
2.6
) holds for all
j=2,\cdots,r
for the tensor
\mathbb{A}
,
it also holds for all
j=2,\cdots,t
for the tensor
\mathbb{A}[I]
. So
\mathbb{A}[I]
is an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{t})
-3rdUTB tensor with
diagonal blocks
\mathbb{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{t}
.

(3). By Definition
2.3
we need to show that for any
j=2,\cdots,n-t
, we have

b_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0\quad(\forall i\in I^{\prime}_{j},\ \mbox{and}\ \max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S^{\prime}_{j-1}).

(2.9)

Now

i\in I^{\prime}_{j}\Longrightarrow S_{t+j-1}-k+1=S^{\prime}_{j-1}+1\leq i\leq S_{j}^{\prime}=S_{t+j}-k

\Longrightarrow S_{t+j-1}+1\leq i+k\leq S_{t+j}

\Longrightarrow i+k\in I_{t+j}
.

And

\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S^{\prime}_{j-1}\Longrightarrow\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S_{t+j-1}-k

\Longrightarrow\max\{i_{2}+k,\cdots,i_{m}+k\}\leq S_{t+j-1}
.

So by the hypothesis that
\mathbb{A}
is an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-3rdUTB tensor we have

b_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=a_{(i+k)(i_{2}+k)\cdots(i_{m}+k)}=0.

So
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{A}[\overline{I}]
is an
(n_{t+1},\cdots,n_{r})
-3rdUTB tensor with diagonal blocks
\mathbb{A}_{t+1},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{r}
(where
(n_{t+1},\cdots,n_{r})=(n_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,n^{\prime}_{r-t})
).

Sufficiency.

By Definition
2.3
we need to show that (
2.7
) holds for
j=2,\cdots,r
.
Suppose that
2\leq j\leq r
,
i\in I_{j}
,
\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S_{j-1}
. We consider the following two cases.

Case 1:

j\leq t
.

Then by
\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S_{j-1}\leq S_{t}
we see that
a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}
is an entry of
\mathbb{A}[I]
. So by condition (2) we have
a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0
.

Case 2:

j\geq t+1
.

Then we consider the following two subcases.

Subcase 2.1:

\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S_{t}=k
.

Then by
i\in I_{j}\Longrightarrow i>S_{j-1}\geq S_{t}=k
, we have
a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0
by condition (1).

Subcase 2.2:

\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}>k
.

Now we still have

\max\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq S_{j-1}=S^{\prime}_{j-t-1}+k\Longrightarrow\max\{i_{2}-k,\cdots,i_{m}-k\}\leq S^{\prime}_{j-t-1}.

Also

i\in I_{j}\Longrightarrow S_{j-1}<i\leq S_{j}\Longrightarrow S^{\prime}_{j-t-1}<i-k\leq S^{\prime}_{j-t}\Longrightarrow i-k\in I^{\prime}_{j-t}.

So by condition (3) and using Definition
2.3
for
\mathbb{B}
, we have
b_{(i-k)(i_{2}-k)\cdots(i_{m}-k)}=0
, which is equivalent to
a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0
.

\square

3
The determinants, characteristic polynomials and spectra of the triangular blocked tensors

In this section, we will prove some formulas for the determinants and characteristic polynomials of the first and second type
UTB tensors, and give an example to show that these formulas do not hold for the third type UTB tensors.

The following result for the special case

was proved by Hu et al in
[
8
]
. For the convenience and self-containedness,
here we give an outline of a simplified proof.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
8
]
)

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an order
m
dimension
n
and
(k,n-k)
-UTB (also 2ndUTB by Remark
2.1
) tensor with diagonal blocks

\mathbb{A}_{1}
and
\mathbb{A}_{2}
. Then

\det\mathbb{A}=(\det\mathbb{A}_{1})^{(m-1)^{n-k}}(\det\mathbb{A}_{2})^{(m-1)^{k}}

(3.1)

Proof.

Step 1:
To show that
\det\mathbb{A}=0\Longrightarrow(\det\mathbb{A}_{1})(\det\mathbb{A}_{2})=0
. Suppose not, then

(\det\mathbb{A}_{1})(\det\mathbb{A}_{2})\neq 0\Longrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\det\mathbb{A}_{2}\neq 0\Longrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{2}z=0\ \ \mbox{has only zero solution}\\
\det\mathbb{A}_{1}\neq 0\Longrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{1}y=0\ \ \mbox{has only zero solution}\\
\end{array}\right.

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ast)}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\mathbb{A}x=\mathbb{A}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
z\\
\end{array}\right)=0
has only zero solution

\Longrightarrow\det\mathbb{A}\neq 0
.

where the implication
(\ast)
holds because:
\mathbb{A}
is an
(k,n-k)
-UTB tensor with diagonal blocks
\mathbb{A}_{1}
and
\mathbb{A}_{2}
implies that

\mathbb{A}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
z\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}*\\
\mathbb{A}_{2}z\\
\end{array}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{A}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
0\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbb{A}_{1}y\\
0\\
\end{array}\right).

Step 2:
By using Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz (
[
5
]
) and the irreducibility of the polynomials

\det\mathbb{A}_{1}
and
\det\mathbb{A}_{2}
(
[
8
]
), we have£º
Every irreducible factor of
\det\mathbb{A}
is an irreducible factor of
(\det\mathbb{A}_{1})(\det\mathbb{A}_{2})
.

Thus there exist constants
c,r_{1},r_{2}
(independent of the tensor
\mathbb{A}
) such that

\det\mathbb{A}=c\cdot(\det\mathbb{A}_{1})^{r_{1}}(\det\mathbb{A}_{2})^{r_{2}}.

(3.2)

Step 3:
Take
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{I}
to be the identity tensor, then both
\mathbb{A}_{1}
and

We first prove the case

(for the third type).

The first result (but not the second result about the two diagonal blocks of
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{B}
) of the following lemma
can be found in
[
16
]
(Proposition 4.1). Recall that in Remark
2.1
, we have mentioned that in the case

-UTB tensors and

Lemma 4.1

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be order
m
and order
k
, dimension
n
and
(p,n-p)
-UTB
(also 2ndUTB by Remark
2.1
) tensor with diagonal blocks
\mathbb{A}_{1}
,
\mathbb{A}_{2}
and
\mathbb{B}_{1}
,

\mathbb{B}_{2}
, respectively. Then their product
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{B}
is also a
(p,n-p)
-UTB tensor with the two diagonal blocks

\mathbb{A}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{A}_{2}\mathbb{B}_{2}
.

Proof.
Write
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{A}\mathbb{B}
. Then by the definition of the tensor product we have (see (
1.3
)):

c_{i\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}b_{i_{2}\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{m}\alpha_{m-1}}\qquad(i\in[n],\ \alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m-1}\in[n]^{k-1}).

(4.1)

The proof of the first result that
\mathbb{C}
is a
(p,n-p)
-UTB tensor can be found in
[
16
]
(Proposition 4.1).

Now we show that the diagonal blocks
\mathbb{C}_{1}
and
\mathbb{C}_{2}
of
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{B}
are
\mathbb{A}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{1}

and
\mathbb{A}_{2}\mathbb{B}_{2}

For the first diagonal block
\mathbb{C}_{1}
, let
i
and all the subscripts in
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m-1}
are all
\leq p
.
In this case, if all

, then we must have all

since
\mathbb{B}
is
(p,n-p)
-UTB.
Thus we have

c_{i\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}b_{i_{2}\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{m}\alpha_{m-1}}=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1}^{p}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}b_{i_{2}\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{m}\alpha_{m-1}}=(\mathbb{A}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{1})_{i\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}},

so we obtain
\mathbb{C}_{1}=\mathbb{A}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{1}
.

Now we consider the second diagonal block
\mathbb{C}_{2}
. Let
i
and all the subscripts in
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m-1}
are all
>p
.
In this case, if
\min\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\leq p
, then we will have
a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0
since
\mathbb{A}
is
(p,n-p)
-UTB. Thus we have

c_{i\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}b_{i_{2}\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{m}\alpha_{m-1}}=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=p+1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}b_{i_{2}\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{m}\alpha_{m-1}}.

Let
j=i-p
,
j_{t}=i_{t}-p
, and
\beta_{j}
be the sequence of the subscripts obtained by subtracting

from all the subscripts in
\alpha_{j}

Then we have

\displaystyle(\mathbb{A}_{2}\mathbb{B}_{2})_{j\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{2},\cdots,j_{m}=1}^{n-p}(\mathbb{A}_{2})_{jj_{2}\cdots j_{m}}(\mathbb{B}_{2})_{j_{2}\beta_{1}}\cdots(\mathbb{B}_{2})_{j_{m}\beta_{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=p+1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}b_{i_{2}\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{m}\alpha_{m-1}}=c_{i\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}.

So we obtain

.

\square

In

Definition 5.1

\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be tensors of dimension

with order

and

, respectively.
If
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{I}

In

In

They proved that a tensor

has a left (or right)

-inverse.
They also proved the uniqueness of the left

-inverse, and obtained the expressions of the left and right

-inverses of
\mathbb{A}
.

In this section, we study the left and right

-inverses of an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensors of all the three types.
We will show that, if a first or second or third type
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor
\mathbb{A}
has a left (or right)

-inverse,
then its unique left

-inverse is still an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor of all the three types, and all of its right

-inverses are still

(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensors of all the three types. Furthermore, by showing (in Theorem
) that the left
k
-inverse (if any)
of a weakly irreducible nonsingular

-tensor is a positive tensor, we show that the left
k
-inverse (if any) of a first or second or third
type normal
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB nonsingular

-tensor is an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor of all the three types all of whose diagonal blocks

and

of §6.)

5.1
The left inverses of the triangular blocked tensors

The
i
-th row of a tensor
\mathbb{A}
of order
m
and dimension
n

R_{i}(\mathbb{A})
,
is the row-subtensor of order
m-1
and dimension
n
of
\mathbb{A}
with the entries

(R_{i}(\mathbb{A}))_{i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}.

Definition 5.2

A tensor
\mathbb{A}
of order
m
and dimension
n
is called row-subtensor diagonal, or simply row diagonal,
if all of its row-subtensors
R_{1}(\mathbb{A}),\cdots,R_{n}(\mathbb{A})
are diagonal tensors. Namely, if we have

a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0\quad(\mbox{if}\ i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\ \mbox{are not all equal}).

(
[
13
]
)
Let
\mathbb{A}
be an order
m
and dimension
n
tensor.
Then the majorization matrix
M(\mathbb{A})
of
\mathbb{A}
is the matrix of order
n
with the entries

(M(\mathbb{A}))_{ij}=a_{ij\cdots j}\quad(\forall i,j=1,\cdots,n).

Proposition 5.1

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an order
m
and dimension
n
tensor.
Then
\mathbb{A}
is row diagonal if and only if there exists a matrix
P
of order
n
such that
\mathbb{A}=P\mathbb{I}_{m}
,

P=M(\mathbb{A})
.

Proof.
Sufficiency. If
\mathbb{A}=P\mathbb{I}_{m}
, then by the definition of tensor product we have

a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n}p_{ii_{1}}\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0\quad(\mbox{if}\ i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\ \mbox{are not all equal}),

and

a_{ij\cdots j}=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n}p_{ii_{1}}\delta_{i_{1}j\cdots j}=p_{ij}\quad(\forall i,j=1,\cdots,n).

(5.1)

Thus
\mathbb{A}
is row diagonal, and
P=M(\mathbb{A})
.

Necessity. If
\mathbb{A}
is row diagonal. Write
P=M(\mathbb{A})
and
\mathbb{B}=P\mathbb{I}_{m}
.
Then by the sufficiency part we know that
\mathbb{B}
is row diagonal with

.
Thus we have
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{B}=P\mathbb{I}_{m}=M(\mathbb{A})\mathbb{I}_{m}
.

\square

5.3
The left
k
-inverses of the normal
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB nonsingular
M
-tensors

In
[
6
]
, Ding, Qi and Wei generalized the concept of
M
-matrices to
M
-tensors.

A real tensor
\mathbb{A}
is called a
Z
-tensor, if all of its off-diagonal entries are non-positive.
Equivalently,
\mathbb{A}
is a
Z
-tensor if and only if
\mathbb{A}
can be expressed as
\mathbb{A}=s\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{B}
,
where
\mathbb{B}
is a nonnegative tensor, and
\mathbb{I}
is the unit tensor.

A
Z
-tensor
\mathbb{A}=s\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{B}
is called an
M
-tensor, if
s\geq\rho(\mathbb{B})
; And is called an nonsingular
M
-tensor,
if
s>\rho(\mathbb{B})
.

It is well-known in matrix theory (
[
1
]
) that if
A
is an irreducible nonsingular
M
-matrix, then
A^{-1}
is a positive matrix.

An
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor
\mathbb{A}
of the first or second type is called the first or second type normal,
if all its diagonal blocks
\mathbb{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{r}
are weakly irreducible. An
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor
\mathbb{A}
of
the third type is called the third type normal, if all its diagonal blocks
\mathbb{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{r}
are irreducible
(also see Definitions
6.1
and
6.2
in §6).

In this subsection, we will mainly prove the following Theorem 5.4.

Theorem 5.4

If
\mathbb{A}
is an order
m

n
and the first or second or third type normal
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB
nonsingular
M
-tensor, and
\mathbb{A}
has a left
k
-inverse
\mathbb{B}
. Then
\mathbb{B}
is an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor of all the three types all of whose diagonal blocks
\mathbb{B}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{B}_{r}
are positive tensors.

In order to prove Theorem 5.4, we first need to prove some preliminary results (The following Lemma
5.5
and Theorem
5.3
).

Lemma 5.5

If an order
m
dimension
n
row diagonal tensor
\mathbb{A}=P\mathbb{I}_{m}
, where
P
is a matrix of order
n
.
Then we have:

(1)

\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{m}-\mathbb{A})=(\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{2}-P))^{(m-1)^{n-1}}
.

(2)

\mathbb{A}
is a
Z
-tensor if and only if
P
is a
Z
-matrix.

(3)

\mathbb{A}
is weakly irreducible if and only if
P
is irreducible.

(4)

\mathbb{A}
is an
M
-tensor if and only if
P
is an
M
-matrix.

(5)

\mathbb{A}
is a nonsingular
M
-tensor if and only if
P
is a nonsingular
M
-matrix.

Proof.
(1). We have

\lambda\mathbb{I}_{m}-\mathbb{A}=\lambda\mathbb{I}_{2}\mathbb{I}_{m}-P\mathbb{I}_{m}=(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{2}-P)\mathbb{I}_{m}.

Thus by the formula of the determinants of the product of tensors (
[
16
]
) we have

\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{m}-\mathbb{A})=(\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{2}-P))^{(m-1)^{n-1}}(\det\mathbb{I}_{m})=(\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{2}-P))^{(m-1)^{n-1}}.

(2). Since
\mathbb{A}
is row diagonal, we have
a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}=0
if
i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}
are not all equal. Thus we have:

\mathbb{A}\ \mbox{is a $Z$-tensor}\Longleftrightarrow a_{ij\cdots j}\leq 0\ (\forall i\neq j)\Longleftrightarrow p_{ij}\leq 0\ (\forall i\neq j)\Longleftrightarrow P\ \mbox{ is a $Z$-matrix}.

(3). By Proposition
5.1
we know that
P=M(\mathbb{A})
is the majorization matrix of
\mathbb{A}
.

On the other hand, let
G(|\mathbb{A}|)
be the “representation matrix” of
|\mathbb{A}|
defined as (
[
17
]
):

(G(|\mathbb{A}|))_{ij}=\sum_{\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\ni j}|a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}|.

(5.2)

Then by the hypothesis that
\mathbb{A}
is row diagonal we can verify that

(G(|\mathbb{A}|))_{ij}=\sum_{\{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}\ni j}|a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}|=|a_{ij\cdots j}|=(|M(\mathbb{A})|)_{ij}.

(5.3)

Thus we have
G(|\mathbb{A}|)=|M(\mathbb{A})|
. So by
[
17
]
(Definitions 2.1 and 2.5) we have

\mathbb{A}\ \mbox{is weakly irreducible}\Longleftrightarrow|\mathbb{A}|\ \mbox{is weakly irreducible}\Longleftrightarrow G(|\mathbb{A}|)\ \mbox{is irreducible}

\Longleftrightarrow|M(\mathbb{A})|
is irreducible
\Longleftrightarrow M(\mathbb{A})
is irreducible

\Longleftrightarrow P
is irreducible.

(4). By (1) we have that

\mbox{The real parts of all eigenvalues of $\mathbb{A}$ are}\ \geq 0\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{The real parts of all eigenvalues of $P$ are}\ \geq 0.

So we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}\ \mbox{is an $M$-tensor}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}\ \mbox{is a $Z$-tensor and the real parts of all eigenvalues of $\mathbb{A}$ are}\ \geq 0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle P\ \mbox{is a $Z$-matrix and the real parts of all eigenvalues of $P$ are}\ \geq 0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle P\ \mbox{is an $M$-matrix}.

(5). Replace all “
\geq 0
” by “
>0
”, and “
M
-tensor” by “nonsingular
M
-tensor” in the proof of (4).

\square

From Lemma
5.5
we can obtain the following result which is a generalization of the corresponding result for irreducible nonsingular
M
-matrices.

Theorem 5.3

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an order

n

M
-tensor,
and
\mathbb{A}
has a left

-inverse
\mathbb{B}
. Then
\mathbb{B}>0
.

Proof.
Since
\mathbb{A}
has a left

\mathbb{A}=P\mathbb{I}_{m}
(for some matrix

.
Thus by (3) and (5) of Lemma

,

is an irreducible nonsingular

.

On the other hand, we know that the left

\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{I}_{k}P^{-1}
. Write

, then by the definition of the tensor product

b_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{k}}=\sum_{j_{2},\cdots,j_{k}=1}^{n}\delta_{ij_{2}\cdots j_{k}}q_{j_{2}i_{2}}\cdots q_{j_{k}i_{k}}=q_{ii_{2}}\cdots q_{ii_{k}}>0.

.

\square

Now we are ready to prove Theorem 5.4.

Proof of Theorem 5.4:
Let the diagonal blocks of
\mathbb{A}
be
\mathbb{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{A}_{r}

(all of which are weakly irreducible by hypothesis).

Firstly, by Theorem
5.1
we know that
\mathbb{A}
has a left
k
-inverse implying that each
\mathbb{A}_{i}
also has a left
k
-inverse.

Secondly, we show that
\mathbb{A}
is a nonsingular
M

\mathbb{A}_{i}
is also a nonsingular

-tensor:

Write
\mathbb{A}=s\mathbb{I}_{m}-\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{C}\geq 0

and

. Then

is an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB tensor with the diagonal blocks,
say
\mathbb{C}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{C}_{r}
. Thus we have

\mathbb{A}_{i}=s\mathbb{I}_{m}(n_{i})-\mathbb{C}_{i}\qquad(i=1,\cdots,r),

where
\mathbb{I}_{m}(n_{i})
is the unit tensor of order

and dimension

.

Now

implying that each

is also a nonsingular

Thus we see that this weakly irreducible

satisfies all the conditions of Theorem

we conclude that its left

-inverse
\mathbb{B}_{i}>0\ (i=1,\cdots,r)
.

6
The reducible and weakly reducible normal form of tensors

In this section, we study the reducible and weakly reducible normal forms of tensors.
Since all the problems and results considered in this section depend only on the zero-nonzero pattern of the tensors,
we may assume without loss of generality that all the tensors considered in this section are nonnegative tensors.

First we recall some definitions and results in the matrix case.

An
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB matrix
A
is called normal (or canonical), if all of whose diagonal blocks are irreducible.
This concept can be generalized to tensors as follows.

Definition 6.1

An
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-UTB (or 2ndUTB) tensor
\mathbb{A}
is called a first type (or second type)
(weakly reducible) normal tensor, if all of whose diagonal blocks are weakly irreducible.

Definition 6.2

An
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
-3rdUTB tensor
\mathbb{A}
is called a third type (reducible) normal tensor,
if all of whose diagonal blocks are irreducible.

In the matrix case, we have the following two well-known results about the existence and uniqueness of the normal form of a matrix of order
n
.

Theorem 6.1

(
[
2
]
)
Every matrix
A
of order
n
is permutational similar to some normal upper (or lower)
triangular blocked matrix.

Theorem 6.2

(
[
2
]
)
Let
A
be an
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
normal upper triangular blocked matrix of order
n
with
diagonal blocks
A_{1},\cdots,A_{r}
, and
B
be an
(m_{1},\cdots,m_{t})
normal upper triangular blocked matrix of order
n
with diagonal
blocks
B_{1},\cdots,B_{t}
, respectively. If

and

are permutational similar, then
r=t
, and there exist some permutation
\sigma
:

[r]\rightarrow[r]
, such that
A_{i}
and

are permutational similar for

.

Shao et al in
[
16
]
and Hu et al in
[
9
]
obtained that every order
m

n
tensor is permutation similar to some normal 2ndUTB tensor (also see Proposition 1 of
[
10
]
), thus generalized Theorem
6.1
from matrices to the second type normal upper triangular blocked tensors. In this section, we will show in Theorem
6.3
that Theorem

can also be generalized to the third type normal upper triangular blocked tensors. We also give an example to show that not every tensor can be permutational similar to some first type normal upper triangular blocked tensor.

First we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1

(1)
If an order
m

n

\mathbb{A}
is

-weakly reducible with
|I|=n-k\ (1\leq k\leq n-1)

Then
\mathbb{A}
is permutation similar to some
(k,n-k)
-UTB (and thus 2ndUTB by Remark

) tensor.

(2)
If
\mathbb{A}
is
I
-reducible with
|I|=n-k\ (1\leq k\leq n-1)
. Then
\mathbb{A}
is permutation similar to some

(k,n-k)
-3rdUTB tensor.

Proof.
Take the permutation
\sigma:[n]\rightarrow[n]
, such that
\sigma(I)=\{k+1,\cdots,n\}
.
Take the permutation matrix

. Then the tensor
P\mathbb{A}P^{T}
is a
(k,n-k)
-UTB (or 3rdUTB) tensor which
is permutation similar to
\mathbb{A}
.
\square

Theorem 6.3

Every order
m
dimension
n
tensor
\mathbb{A}
is permutation similar to some normal 3rdUTB tensor.

Proof.
We use induction on
n
. If
\mathbb{A}
is irreducible, then
\mathbb{A}
itself is already in the required form.
If
\mathbb{A}
is reducible, then by Lemma
6.1
there exists some

with

such that
\mathbb{A}
is permutation
similar to some
(k,n-k)
-3rdUTB tensor
\mathbb{B}
with two diagonal blocks
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
.

(The following blocked forms for the matrix cases illustrate the ideas of the proof.)

\mathbb{A}\sim\mathbb{B}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{1}&\ast\\
O&B_{2}\\
\end{array}\right)

By induction we know that
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}

are respectively permutation similar to some normal 3rdUTB tensors
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{D}
all of whose diagonal blocks
\mathbb{C}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{C}_{p}
and
\mathbb{D}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{D}_{q}
are irreducible.

\mathbb{B}_{1}\sim\mathbb{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{C}_{1}&\ldots&\ast\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
O&\ldots&\mathbb{C}_{p}\\
\end{array}\right),\ \ \mathbb{B}_{2}\sim\mathbb{D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{D}_{1}&\ldots&\ast\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
O&\ldots&\mathbb{D}_{q}\\
\end{array}\right)\Longrightarrow\mathbb{B}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{C}&\ast\\
O&\mathbb{D}\\
\end{array}\right):=\mathbb{A}^{*},

where
\mathbb{A}^{*}
defined above is a

-3rdUTB tensor with two diagonal blocks
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{D}
.
By Theorem

we know that the tensor
\mathbb{A}^{*}

and
\mathbb{D}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{D}_{q}
(they are all irreducible), so

is a normal 3rdUTB tensor. But we also have

, thus we obtained the desired result.

\square

Using the proof similar to that of Theorem

]

Theorem 6.4

Every order

\mathbb{A}
be a
(0,1)

Proof.
Firstly,
\mathbb{A}

\mathbb{A}
is

\sigma

-upper triangular blocked tensor

\mathbb{B}_{1},\cdots,\mathbb{B}_{r}

Now take

Abstract
. The existence of decompositions of the form f = P ⋅ q + r f=P\cdot q+r with P k ∗ ​ ( D ) ​ r = 0 P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)r=0 , where f f is entire, P P a polynomial and P k ∗ P^{\ast}_{k} the principal part of P P with its coefficients conjugated, was achieved in [ 1 ] under certain restrictions on the order of f f . Here we prove uniqueness, thereby obtaining Fischer decompositions, under conditions that sometimes match those required for existence, and sometimes are more restrictive, depending on the parameters involved.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)=\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{d}]
be the set of all polynomials in
z=\left(z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, and let
P\in\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
. By
P^{\ast}\left(z\right)
we denote the polynomial obtained from

P\left(z\right)
by conjugating its coefficients, and by
P\left(D\right)
, the linear differential operator obtained by replacing each
appearance of the variable
z_{i}
with
\partial/\partial{z_{i}}
, for

i=1,\dots,d
. Fischer’s decomposition theorem states that for every

homogeneous
polynomial
P
and every polynomial
f
there exist

unique

polynomials

q
and
r
such that

f=P\cdot q+r\text{ and }P^{\ast}\left(D\right)r=0.

According to D. J. Newman and H. S. Shapiro, cf.
[
8
, pg. 971]
,
Fischer’s result “underlies various formal schemes for exhibiting a basic set
of polynomial solutions of a partial differential equation”. H. S. Shapiro and
several of his collaborators studied Fischer decompositions in a wider setting
(cf. for instance
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
)
going beyond the case of polynomials to more general function spaces, in
particular, to the space
E\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
of all entire
functions
f:\mathbb{C}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. We recall a notion
introduced in
[
9
, p. 522]
: given a vector space
E
of infinitely
differentiable functions
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
(defined on an open
subset
G
of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
) that is a module over
\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)
, a polynomial
P
and a differential operator

Q\left(D\right)
are said to form a
Fischer pair for the space
E
, if for each
f\in E
there exist
unique
elements
q\in E
and

r\in E
such that

(1)

f=P\cdot q+r\text{ and }Q\left(D\right)r=0.

The question of uniqueness can be rephrased as follows: suppose that
f=P\cdot q_{j}+r_{j}
and
P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)r_{j}=0
for
j=1,2.
Then
with
q:=q_{2}-q_{1}
and
r=r_{2}-r_{1}
we obtain the equation
0=Pq+r.

Applying the operator
P^{\ast}\left(D\right)
we see that

(2)

P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)\left(Pq\right)=0.

Thus it is sufficient for uniqueness to show from (
2
) that

q=0.

For simplicity in the exposition we indicate next a special case
(corresponding to the parameter
\tau=0
) of our main theorem, which will be
stated only after we recall the definition of the apolar inner product.

Theorem 1
.

Let
0\leq\beta\leq k-1
, let
P
be a non-homogeneous polynomial of degree
k\geq 2
with homogenous expansion
P=P_{0}+\cdots+P_{\beta}+P_{k}
, and let

\varphi:\mathbb{C}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an entire function which
satisfies
P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)\left(P\varphi\right)=0
and has
order
\rho<2k^{-1}\left(k-\beta\right)
. Then
\varphi=0.

In terms of the Fischer operator
F_{QP},
defined via
F_{QP}(\varphi):=Q(D)(P\varphi),
the conclusion of Theorem
1
states that

F_{P_{k}^{*}P}
is injective on the entire functions of sufficiently low order.

Note that we only deal with the case of a non-homogeneous polynomial
P
. The bijectivity of the Fischer operator on the space of
all entire functions
when
P
is homogeneous, was proved by H. S. Shapiro in
[
9
, Theorem 1]
.

We speak of
weak Fischer pairs
when the decomposition
f=P\cdot q+r
is
not required to be unique. In reference
[
1
, Theorem 2]
, the authors
proved the existence of weak Fischer decompositions, under the bound

\rho(k-\tau)<2(k-\beta)
on the order
\rho
of
f
. Our main result, Theorem
3

3

below, partially complements this existence
result, yielding
the injectivity of the Fischer operator under
sometimes more strict assumptions on
\rho
, depending on the parameters.

2.
A lemma on sequences of nonnegative numbers

Lemma 2
.

Let
\left\{a_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence
of non-negative numbers. Suppose there is a finite non-empty subset

E\subset\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(i) There exist constants
A\geq 1
,
D\geq 0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that for
all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

a_{m}\leq A\left(m+D\right)^{\alpha}\max_{j\in E}a_{m+j}.

(ii) There exist constants
A_{0},b_{0}>0
and
D_{0},\alpha_{0}\geq 0
and

\sigma\neq 0
such that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
,

a_{m}\leq A_{0}\ \frac{\left(m+D_{0}\right)^{\alpha_{0}}}{b_{0}^{m}}\ m^{-\frac{m}{\sigma}}\text{ }.

Let
\beta_{\ast}:=\min E
and
\beta^{\ast}=\max E.
If

0\leq\alpha<\frac{\beta_{\ast}}{\sigma},\text{ \hskip 10.22217ptor }\quad\alpha<0\text{
and }\alpha<\frac{\beta^{\ast}}{\sigma},

then for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have
a_{m}=0
. In the case
\alpha=\frac{\beta_{\ast}}{\sigma}>0
then the same conclusion holds under the
additional hypothesis that
A<b_{0}^{\beta_{\ast}}.

Proof.

Assume that
a_{m}\neq 0.
We derive a contradiction. Since

(3)

a_{m}\leq A\left(m+D\right)^{\alpha}\max_{j\in E}a_{m+j}

there exists an
l_{1}\in E
with

a_{m}\leq A\left(m+D\right)^{\alpha}a_{m+l_{1}}.

Applying inequality (
3
) to
a_{m+l_{1}}
instead of
a_{m}
, we
see that there exists an
l_{2}\in E
such that

a_{m}\leq A^{2}\left(m+D\right)^{\alpha}\left(m+l_{1}+D\right)^{\alpha}a_{m+l_{1}+l_{2}}.

Now proceed inductively, to conclude that there exist
l_{1},\dots,l_{j-1}\in E

satisfying

a_{m}\leq A^{j-1}\left(m+D\right)^{\alpha}\left(m+l_{1}+D\right)^{\alpha}\cdots\left(m+D+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j-1}\right)^{\alpha}a_{m+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}}.

Define for
j\geq 1
,

p_{m,j}:=\left(m+D\right)\left(m+l_{1}+D\right)\cdots\left(m+D+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j-1}\right),

and
k_{j}:=m+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}.
Our assumption
a_{m}\leq A_{0}\frac{\left(m+D_{0}\right)^{\alpha_{0}}}{b_{0}^{m}}\frac{1}{m^{\frac{m}{\sigma}}}

implies that

a_{k_{j}}\leq A_{0}\frac{\left(k_{j}+D_{0}\right)^{\alpha_{0}}}{b_{0}^{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{k_{j}}{\sigma}},\

so we have

a_{m}\leq A_{0}\frac{\left(k_{j}+D_{0}\right)^{\alpha_{0}}}{b_{0}^{k_{j}}}A^{j-1}p_{m,j}^{\alpha}k_{j}^{-\frac{k_{j}}{\sigma}}.

Taking
k_{j}
-th roots in the last inequality shows that

(4)

a_{m}^{\frac{1}{k_{j}}}\leq A_{0}^{\frac{1}{k_{j}}}\frac{\left(k_{j}+D_{0}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{k_{j}}}}{b_{0}}A^{\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}p_{m,j}^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}.

Since
k_{j}=m+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}\geq m+\beta_{\ast}j\geq\beta_{\ast}j
we see
that as
j\to\infty
,
k_{j}\rightarrow\infty
, so

a_{m}^{\frac{1}{k_{j}}}\rightarrow 1
,
A_{0}^{\frac{1}{k_{j}}}\rightarrow 1
,
\left(k_{j}+D_{0}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{k_{j}}}\rightarrow 1
, and
A^{\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}\leq A^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\ast}}}
. Thus, it suffices to prove
that
p_{m,j}^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}\rightarrow 0
in order to
obtain a contradiction, since then the left hand side of inequality (
4
) converges to

1
and the right hand side to
0.

If
0\leq\alpha<\beta_{\ast}/\sigma
, then
m+D+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{s}\leq D+k_{j}
for

s=0,\dots,j-1
and
p_{m,j}\leq\left(D+k_{j}\right)^{j},
so

p^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}\leq\left(D+k_{j}\right)^{\alpha\frac{j}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}\leq\left(D+k_{j}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}=\left(\frac{D+k_{j}}{k_{j}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}}k_{j}^{\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)}.

Since
\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}-\frac{1}{\sigma}<0
the term on the right
hand side converges to
0
. If
0<\alpha=\beta_{\ast}/\sigma
, then

1=\lim\sup_{j\rightarrow\infty}a_{m}^{\frac{1}{k_{j}}}\leq\lim\sup_{j\rightarrow\infty}A_{0}^{\frac{1}{k_{j}}}\frac{\left(k_{j}+D_{0}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{k_{j}}}}{b_{0}}A^{\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}\left(\frac{D+k_{j}}{k_{j}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}}\leq\frac{1}{b_{0}}A^{\frac{1}{\beta_{\ast}}}<1.

This finishes the proof when
\alpha\geq 0.

Let us discuss next the case
\alpha<0.
Since
k_{j}\geq\beta_{\ast}j\
for

j\geq 1
, we have

p_{m,j}=\left(m+D\right)\left(m+l_{1}+D\right)\cdots\left(m+D+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j-1}\right)\geq\beta_{\ast}^{j-1}\left(j-1\right)!.

From
\alpha<0
we conclude that

p_{m,j}^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}\leq\left(\beta_{\ast}\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}\left[\left(j-1\right)!\right]^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}.

Define
\beta^{\ast}=\max\left\{l:l\in E\right\}.
Since

k_{j}=m+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}
, it follows that

\beta_{\ast}j\leq k_{j}\leq m+\beta^{\ast}j.

Thus
\left(\beta_{\ast}\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}
is bounded above.
Furthermore, a version of Stirling’s formula
with explicit bounds for
m\geq 0
tells us that

m!\geq\left(2\pi m\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{m}e^{\frac{1}{12m+1}}.

Using
\alpha<0
and the preceding formula with
m=j-1\geq 1
we obtain

\left[\left(j-1\right)!\right]^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}\leq\left(2\pi\left(j-1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}\left(\frac{j-1}{e}\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}e^{\frac{\alpha}{12\left(j-1\right)+1}}.

Note that

\left(\frac{1}{e}\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}=e^{-\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}\leq e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}},

so

\lim\sup_{j\rightarrow\infty}p^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}\leq\lim\sup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left(\beta_{\ast}\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}}\left(j-1\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}

Since
k_{j}\leq m+\beta^{\ast}j
we have

\frac{k_{j}}{j-1}\leq\frac{m}{j-1}+\beta^{\ast}+\frac{1}{j-1}\beta^{\ast}.

By assumption
\alpha<\beta^{\ast}/\sigma
, where
\alpha<0
(and we can have either
\sigma<0
or
\sigma>0
). Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
\alpha<(1+\varepsilon)\beta^{\ast}/\sigma
. Now there exists a
j_{\varepsilon}
such that for all

j\geq j_{\varepsilon}
,

\frac{k_{j}}{j-1}\leq\left(1+\varepsilon\right)\beta^{\ast}.

Then whenever
j\geq j_{\varepsilon}
,

j-1\geq k_{j}\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{-1}\left(\beta^{\ast}\right)^{-1}.

Using again that
\alpha<0
we get

\displaystyle\left(j-1\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq k_{j}^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{-\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}\left(\beta^{\ast}\right)^{-\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq k_{j}^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{-\alpha\frac{1}{\beta_{\ast}}}\left(\beta^{\ast}\right)^{-\alpha\frac{1}{\beta_{\ast}}}.

Now

\lim\sup_{j\rightarrow\infty}p^{\frac{\alpha}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}\leq e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{\ast}}}\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{-\alpha\frac{1}{\beta_{\ast}}}\left(\beta^{\ast}\right)^{-\alpha\frac{1}{\beta_{\ast}}}\lim\sup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left(\beta_{\ast}\right)^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}}k_{j}^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}},

and this limit equals
0
since

\alpha\frac{j-1}{k_{j}}\leq\frac{\alpha}{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)\beta^{\ast}}<\frac{1}{\sigma}.

∎

3.
The apolar inner product, and statement of the main result

The
apolar inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{a}
on

\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{d}]
is defined by

(5)

\left\langle P,Q\right\rangle_{a}:=\left(Q^{*}\left(D\right)P\right)\;(0)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\alpha!c_{\alpha}\overline{d_{\alpha}}.

Note that for monomials
z^{\alpha}
and
z^{\beta}
, definition
(
5
) means that
\left\langle z^{\alpha},z^{\beta}\right\rangle_{a}=0
if
\alpha\neq\beta
, and
\left\langle z^{\alpha},z^{\alpha}\right\rangle_{a}=\alpha!
. Furthermore, from these relations definition
(
5
) is recovered by sesquilinearity.

A fundamental property of the apolar inner product, easily obtained from the
definition (alternatively, see
[
1
, Proposition 3]
) is that given
polynomials
Q,f
and
g
, we have

(6)

\left\langle Q^{\ast}\left(D\right)f,g\right\rangle_{a}=\left\langle f,Q\cdot g\right\rangle_{a}.

Freom the apolar inner product we obtain the associated
apolar norm
in
the usual way:
\left\|f\right\|_{a}:=\sqrt{\left\langle f,f\right\rangle_{a}}.

We are now ready to state our main result. Regarding the notation used below on the non-homogeneous polynomial

P=P_{\beta_{1}}+\cdots+P_{\beta_{2}}+P_{k}
,
we suppose not only that
P_{k}\neq 0
, but also that
P_{\beta_{1}}\neq 0
and

P_{\beta_{2}}\neq 0
; however, it may happen
that
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
0\leq\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}\leq k-1
and let
P
be a
non-homogeneous polynomial of degree
k\geq 2
with homogenous expansion
P=P_{\beta_{1}}+\cdots+P_{\beta_{2}}+P_{k}
, where
P_{\beta_{1}}\neq 0
and

P_{\beta_{2}}\neq 0
.
Suppose there exist
\tau\geq 0
and
C>0
such
that for all natural numbers
m\geq 0
and all homogeneous polynomials
g_{m}

of degree
m
, we have

(7)

\left\|P_{k}g_{m}\right\|_{a}\geq C\left(m+1\right)^{\tau/2}\left\|g_{m}\right\|_{a}.

Let
\rho<2\left(k-\beta_{2}\right)/(k-\tau)
if
\beta_{2}-\tau\geq 0
, and let
\rho<2(k-\beta_{1})/(k+\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}-\tau)
when
\beta_{2}-\tau<0
.
If
\varphi:\mathbb{C}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an entire function of
order
\rho
satisfying
P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)\left(P\varphi\right)=0
, then
\varphi=0.

Note that (
7
) always holds when
\tau=0
, as a consequence of
Bombieri’s inequality, cf.
[
3
]
. When
\tau\geq 1
, we call the
inequalities (
7
)
Khavinson-Shapiro bounds
, cf.
[
1
]
.
A more detailed discussion of condition (
7
) can be found in

[
2
]
. If
k=1
, the Fischer operator
F_{P_{k}^{*}P}
is always bijective on the space of entire functions, by
[
1
, Theorem 8]
. Regarding the bounds on the order, we mention that if
\beta_{2}-\tau\geq 0
, we have

\frac{2}{k}\leq\frac{2(k-\beta_{2})}{k-\tau}\leq 2,

while if
\beta_{2}-\tau<0
, then

2<\frac{2(k-\beta_{1})}{k+\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}-\tau}\leq 2k.

With respect to the existence result of
[
1
, Theorem 2]
, there the assumption on the order is

\rho<\frac{2(k-\beta_{2})}{k-\tau},

which coincides with the uniqueness bound for
\rho
when

\beta_{2}\geq\tau
. But if
\beta_{2}<\tau
, then

\frac{2(k-\beta_{1})}{k+\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}-\tau}\leq\frac{2(k-\beta_{2})}{k-\tau},

so the restriction on the order is at least as strong for the proof of uniqueness as for existence.

4.
Entire functions of finite order

For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
we write
\left|z\right|=\sqrt{\left|z_{1}\right|^{2}+\cdots+\left|z_{d}\right|^{2}}.
The

Suppose that

5.
Proof of Uniqueness

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{C}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an entire function of
order
\rho<\frac{2\left(k-\beta_{2}\right)}{k-\tau}
, and let
P
be a
polynomial of degree
k\geq 1
with homogenous expansion
P=P_{\beta_{1}}+\cdots+P_{\beta_{2}}+P_{k}
, where
0\leq\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}\leq k-1.

Let us write
\varphi=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{m}
, with each
\varphi_{m}

a homogeneous polynomial of degree
m
. Then
u:=P\varphi
can be expressed as

u=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}u_{m},
where the homogeneous polynomials
u_{m}
of

(10)

u_{m+k}=P_{k}\varphi_{m}+....+P_{0}\varphi_{m+k}.

Note that
P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)u=0
implies that
P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)u_{m+k}=0
for every
m\geq 0
, by uniqueness of the representation of
u
as a
series of homogeneous polynomials. Taking the inner product with
P_{k}\varphi_{m}
on both sides of (
10
) and using
P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)u_{m+k}=0
, we obtain

\left\|P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\|_{a}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\langle P_{k-j}\varphi_{m+j},P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\rangle_{a}=\left\langle u_{m+k},P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\rangle_{a}=\left\langle P_{k}^{\ast}\left(D\right)u_{m+k},\varphi_{m}\right\rangle_{a}=0,

so

\left\|P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\|_{a}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left|\left\langle P_{k-j}\varphi_{m+j},P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\rangle_{a}\right|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|P_{k-j}\varphi_{m+j}\right\|_{a}\left\|P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\|_{a}

by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Thus
\left\|P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\|_{a}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|P_{k-j}\varphi_{m+j}\right\|_{a}.
By
Lemma
6
, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
there exists a constant

D_{k-j}
, which depends only on
P_{k-j},
and satisfies

\left\|P_{k-j}\varphi_{m+j}\right\|_{a}\leq D_{k-j}\left\|\varphi_{m+j}\right\|_{a}\left(1+m\right)^{\left(k-j\right)/2}.

Defining

we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|P_{k}\varphi_{m}\right\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in E}D_{k-j}\left\|\varphi_{m+j}\right\|_{a}\left(1+m\right)^{\left(k-j\right)/2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in E}\left\|\varphi_{m+s}\right\|_{a}\sum_{j\in E}D_{k-j}\left(1+m\right)^{\left(k-j\right)/2}.

Note also that from the assumptions

P_{\beta_{1}}\neq 0
and

P_{\beta_{2}}\neq 0
we obtain
\beta_{*}=k-\beta_{2}
and
\beta^{*}=k-\beta_{1}
.
The change of indices

leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in E}D_{k-j}\left(1+m\right)^{\left(k-j\right)/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}D_{n}\left(1+m\right)^{n/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+m\right)^{\beta_{2}/2}\left(\sum_{n=\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{D_{n}}{\left(1+m\right)^{\left(\beta_{2}-n\right)/2}}\right).

Next we fix

so that

\sum_{n=\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{D_{n}}{\left(1+m_{0}\right)^{\left(\beta_{2}-n\right)/2}}\leq D_{\beta_{2}}+\delta.

By hypothesis there exist

\left\|\varphi_{m}\right\|_{a}\leq C^{-1}\left(D_{\beta_{2}}+\delta\right)\frac{\left(1+m\right)^{\beta_{2}/2}}{\left(1+m\right)^{\tau/2}}\max_{j\in E}\left\|\varphi_{m+j}\right\|_{a}\ .

Let us write

, and

, so the first condition in Lemma
2
is
satisfied. To check the second condition we use Proposition
5
, which

so it holds for

Assume that

\alpha\geq 0

,

Abstract
. We study the modular class of Q Q -manifolds, and in particular of negatively graded Lie ∞ \infty -algebroid. We show the equivalence of several descriptions of those classes, that it matches the classes introduced by various authors and that the notion is homotopy invariant. In the process, the adjoint and coadjoint actions up to homotopy of a Lie ∞ \infty -algebroid are spelled out. We also wrote down explicitly some dualities, e.g. between representations up to homotopies of Lie ∞ \infty -algebroids and their Q Q -manifold equivalent, which we hope to be of use for future reference.



Introduction

The modular class can be broadly defined as the obstruction to the existence of an invariant volume form. For Lie algebras, it is a class in the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology obstructing the existence of a bi-invariant volume form on the corresponding Lie group. For a regular foliation, it is the obstruction to the existence of a transverse volume form invariant under all monodromies; It is valued in tangent cohomology. For Poisson structures, it is the obstruction to the existence of a volume form preserved under all Hamiltonian vector fields, and is valued in Poisson cohomology
[

1.1.
Conventions on graded vector spaces

We will work with
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces with finite dimension over a field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
E=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}
be a
finite dimensional graded vector space
(i.e. all vector spaces
E_{i}
are of finite dimension, and this dimension is zero except for finitely many of them). We call
E_{i}
the homogeneous component of
E
of degree
i
. An element
x
of
E_{i}
is said to be homogeneous with degree
|x|=i
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, one may shift all the degrees by
k
and obtain a new grading on
E
. This new graded vector space is denoted by
E[k]
and is defined by
E[k]_{i}=E_{i+k}
.

A morphism
\Phi:E\to V
between two graded vector spaces is a degree preserving linear map, i.e. a collection of linear maps
\Phi_{i}:E_{i}\to V_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We call
\Phi:E\to V
a morphism of degree
k
, for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, if it is a morphism between
E
and
V[k]
.

The dual
E^{*}
of
E
is naturally a finite dimensional graded vector space whose component of degree
i
is the dual
(E_{-i})^{*}
of
E_{-i}
, for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, in equations:
(E^{*})_{i}=(E_{-i})^{*}
.

Given two finite dimensional graded vector spaces
E
and
V
, their direct sum
E\oplus V
[resp. tensor product
E\otimes V
] is a finite dimensional graded vector space with grading

(E\oplus V)_{i}=E_{i}\oplus V_{i}\hskip 14.22636pt\hbox{ [resp. $(E\otimes V)_{i}=\oplus_{j+k=i}E_{j}\otimes V_{k}$ ]}.

We adopt the Koszul sign convention: for homogeneous morphisms
f:E\to V
and
g:F\to W
, the tensor product
f\otimes g:E\otimes F\to V\otimes W
is the morphism of degree
|f|+|g|
given by

(f\otimes g)(x\otimes y)=(-1)^{|x||g|}f(x)\otimes g(y),\quad x\in E,y\in F.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
T^{k}(E)=\otimes^{k}E
and let
T(E)=\oplus_{k}T^{k}(E)
be the tensor algebra over
E
. The
graded symmetric algebra over
E
is the quotient

S(E)=T(E)/\left\langle x\otimes y-(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x\right\rangle.

This quotient is a graded commutative algebra, whose product we denote by
\odot
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
S_{n}
be the permutation group of order
n
. For any
n
-tuple of homogeneous elements
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
in
E
and any
\sigma\in S_{n}
, the
Koszul sign
is the element in
\epsilon(\sigma,x)\in\{-1,1\}
defined by

x_{\sigma(1)}\odot\ldots\odot x_{\sigma(n)}=\epsilon(\sigma,x)\,x_{1}\odot\ldots\odot x_{n}.

For the sake of simplicity, we will simply denote the Koszul sign by
\epsilon(\sigma)
instead of
\epsilon(\sigma,x)
.

An element
\sigma
of
S_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-unshuffle if
\sigma(1)<\ldots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\ldots<\sigma(n)
.
The set of
(i,n-i)
-unshuffles is denoted by
Sh(i,n-i)
.

Since we consider
E
a finite dimensional graded vector space, we identify
S(E^{*})
with
(SE)^{*}
.

Koszul sign conventions and degree reasons yield, for each homogeneous elements
f,g\in E^{*}
,

\displaystyle(f\odot g)(x\odot y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)(x\odot y+(-1)^{|x||y|}y\odot x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x||g|}f(x)g(y)+(-1)^{|x||y|+|y||g|}f(y)g(x),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x||g|}f(x)g(y)+f(y)g(x)\quad x,y\in E.

Lie

Definition 1.1

A

Example 1.2

(Symmetric graded Lie algebra)
.

A symmetric graded Lie algebra is a symmetric Lie
\infty

such that

Example 1.3

(Symmetric DGL algebra)
.

A symmetric differential graded Lie algebra (DGLA) is a symmetric Lie
\infty
-algebra
E=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}
such that
l_{n}=0

and

Then

is a degree

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d\left\{x,y\right\}+\left\{d(x),y\right\}+(-1)^{|x|}\left\{x,d(y)\right\}=0,\\

Example 1.4
.

Let

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial\phi=-\mathrm{d}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\phi+(-1)^{|\phi|+1}\phi\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\mathrm{d},\\

for
\phi,\psi

.

Recall that for
(E,\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1})
, a symmetric Lie
\infty
-algebra, equations (

(i)

(ii)

Let

The graded vector space

1.3.
Symmetric Lie
{{\infty}}

Let
(E,\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1})

{\infty}
-algebra.
Consider the

(S^{\geq 1}(E^{*}),\odot)

Let

Elements of
{\mathcal{E}}
shall be referred to as

and are, by definition, formal sums

with

an element of arity

.
By

, with

, we mean the element of

obtained by pairing
F_{k}\in S^{k}(E^{*})\simeq(S^{k}(E))^{*}

We denote by

, the space of functions of arity
k
and degree

, i.e.

(4)

\mathcal{E}_{i}^{k}=\oplus_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{k}=i}(E_{-i_{1}})^{*}\odot\ldots\odot(E_{-i_{k}})^{*}.

When only the degree or the arity is specified, we shall denote the corresponding vector space by
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{k}
, respectively.

Since it is the completion of
(S^{\geq 1}(E^{*}),\odot)

has no unit.
When a unit is added (i.e., if we consider the completion of

instead), we obtain a unital algebra that we shall denote by

.

We say that a map
\Psi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
has degree

\Psi(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})\subset\mathcal{E}_{\bullet+i}
and arity

Any map of degree

(Notice that the sum runs on

We call the graded derivations of

For each homogeneous

Let us spell out the meaning of (

To start with, recall that an element in

As a consequence, an element of degree

With these conventions in mind, for any algebra morphism

Adjoint and coadjoint representation

An important example of a representation is given by a Lie

2.
The modular class of a Lie
\infty
-algebra

Let
\left(E,Q_{E}\equiv\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}\right)

\infty

The restriction of the adjoint representation

{\ad}_{|S(E)_{-1}}:S(E)_{-1}\subset S(E)\to\End_{0}(E).

In other words, for all homogeneous

whose degree add up to
-1

is a linear endomorphism of
E
of degree

,
i.e. a family indexed by

of linear endomophisms of
E_{i}
.
For each

, let
\omega^{(k)}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}^{k}
be defined, for all homogeneous

whose degree add up to

\omega^{(k)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\str\ad^{(k)}_{x_{1}\odot\ldots\odot x_{k}},

where
\str
denotes the super trace operator on
E
, i.e. the alternate sum of the traces
of the restriction to
E_{i}
of
\ad^{(k)}_{x_{1}\odot\ldots\odot x_{k}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
modular function
of a Lie
\infty
-algebra
\left(E,Q_{E}\equiv\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}\right)
of finite dimension, the function
\omega_{E}\in\mathcal{E}_{+1}=S(E^{*})_{+1}
defined by

\omega_{E}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\omega^{(k)},

with
\omega^{(k)}\in S^{k}(E^{*})_{+1}
.

Remark 2.2
.

It deserves to be noticed that we not really need
E
to be of finite dimension. To define the modular function, it is enough to assume it is of finite rank in every degree. It even suffices that the super-trace is defined.

Let
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{n}
be a basis of
E^{*}
, made of homogeneous elements. Using conventions of Remark
1.7
, any derivation

of
{\mathcal{E}}
, of degree

Q=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{{\tiny{\begin{array}[]{c}1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq n\\

|\xi^{i_{1}}|+\dots+|\xi^{i_{k}}|-|\xi^{j}|=i\end{array}}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{k!}\,\,\,Q^{j}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\xi^{i_{k}}\odot\dots\odot\xi^{i_{1}}\,\,\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{j}}.

We define its

to be the element in
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
given by:

\displaystyle{\rm div}(Q)

\displaystyle:=(-1)^{i+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{j}}Q(\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+1}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{{\tiny{\begin{array}[]{c}1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq n\\
j=1,\dots,n\\
|\xi^{i_{1}}|+\dots+|\xi^{i_{k}}|-|\xi^{j}|=i\end{array}}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{k!}Q^{j}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{j}}\left(\xi^{i_{k}}\odot\dots\odot\xi^{i_{1}}\right).

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+1}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{{\tiny{\begin{array}[]{c}1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}\leq n\\
j=1,\dots,n\\
|\xi^{i_{1}}|+\dots+|\xi^{i_{k-1}}|=i\end{array}}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{(k-1)!}Q^{j}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1},j}\xi^{i_{k-1}}\odot\dots\odot\xi^{i_{1}}

The next proposition implies that our definition of the modular class coincides with the ones of
[
7
]
and
[

]

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\left(E,Q_{E}\equiv\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}\right)
be a Lie
\infty
-algebra of finite dimension.

(1)

The modular function
\omega_{E}
is
Q_{E}
-closed, i.e.
Q_{E}(\omega_{E})=0
.

(2)

The modular function coincides with the divergence of the vector field
Q_{E}
:

\omega_{E}=\mathrm{div}(Q_{E}).

Proof.

Let us prove the first item. By construction,
Q_{E}(\omega_{E})
is a function of degree
2
.
Let us compute its component of arity
k
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in E
be homogeneous elements whose degrees add up to
{-2}
, then:

\displaystyle Q_{E}(\omega_{E})(x_{1}\odot\ldots\odot x_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{h\leq k}\\
\scriptstyle{\sigma\in Sh(h,k-h)}\end{array}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(-1)^{|\omega_{E}|}\varepsilon(\sigma)\,\omega^{(k)}\left(l_{h}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(h)})\odot x_{\sigma(h+1)}\odot\ldots\odot x_{\sigma(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{h\leq k}\\
\scriptstyle{\sigma\in Sh(h,k-h)}\end{array}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\varepsilon(\sigma)\str\,\ad^{(k)}_{l_{h}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(h)})\odot x_{\sigma(h+1)}\odot\ldots\odot x_{\sigma(k)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\str\left(l_{1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\ad_{x_{1}\odot\ldots\odot x_{k}}+\ad_{x_{1}\odot\ldots\odot x_{k}}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}l_{1}\right)+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{h\leq l}\\
\scriptstyle{\sigma\in Sh(h,l-h)}\end{array}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(-1)^{|x_{\sigma(1)}|+\ldots+|x_{\sigma(h)}|}\str\left[\ad^{(h)}_{x_{\sigma(1)}\odot\ldots\odot x_{\sigma(h)}},\ad^{(k-h)}_{x_{\sigma(h+1)}\odot\ldots\odot x_{\sigma(k)}}\right]=0.

Above, we used in the second line, Definition
2.1
of the modular function and in the third line the description given in Equation (
1.16

Let us prove the second item.
Let

Now, for each

Proposition 2.6

The modular class of a Lie
\infty

Proof.

This proof is surprisingly complicated, and will make use of co-derivations, and Sweedler’s notation, see

Consider the reduced symmetric algebra
S^{\geq 1}(E)
equipped with its usual coalgebra structure given by:

\Delta(x_{1}\odot\ldots\odot x_{n})=\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{j=1}\\
\scriptstyle{\sigma\in Sh(j,n-j)}\end{array}}^{\scriptstyle{n-1}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\varepsilon(\sigma)({x_{\sigma(1)}}\odot\ldots\odot{x_{\sigma(j)}})\otimes({x_{\sigma(j+1)}}\odot\ldots\odot{x_{\sigma(n)}}),

for

We will use Sweedler’s notation:
given

\Delta^{(1)}(x)=\Delta(x)=x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)},

and the coassociativity yields

\Delta^{(n)}(x)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{(n-1)})\Delta(x)=x_{(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes x_{(n+1)},\quad n\geq 2.

Notice that
\Delta^{(n)}(x)=0
, for all

Let
\Phi:\left(E,Q_{E}\equiv\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}\right)\to\left(F,Q_{F}\equiv\left\{m_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}\right)
be Lie
\infty

the coderivation of
S^{\geq 1}(E)
defined by the Lie
\infty
-structure in

:

M_{E}(x)=l(x_{(1)})\odot x_{(2)}+l(x),\quad x\in S^{\geq 1}(E).

For each
x\in S^{k}(E)
,
k\geq 1
, let us consider the linear map

\Phi_{x}:E\to F
given by

\Phi_{x}(y)=\Phi_{k+1}(x\odot y).

The map

\Phi_{1}:(E,l_{1})\to(F,m_{1})
is an isomorphism:

l_{1}=\Phi_{1}^{-1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}m_{1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{1}

and, taking into account Definition

\displaystyle\ad^{E}_{x}

\displaystyle+\Phi_{1}^{-1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{M_{E}(x)}+(-1)^{|x|}\Phi_{1}^{-1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{x}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}l_{1}+(-1)^{|x_{(1)}|}\Phi_{1}^{-1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{x_{(1)}}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\ad^{E}_{x_{(2)}}

(12)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{1}^{-1}\ad^{F}_{\Phi(x)}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{1}+\Phi^{-1}_{1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}m_{1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{x}+\Phi_{1}^{-1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\ad^{F}_{\Phi(x_{(1)})}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{x_{(2)}}.

Then, for each

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}\omega_{F}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\str(-1)^{|x_{(1)}|}\Phi_{1}^{-1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{x_{(1)}}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\ad^{E}_{x_{(2)}}-\str\Phi_{1}^{-1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\ad^{F}_{\Phi(x_{(1)})}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}\Phi_{x_{(2)}}.

Proposition 2.8

Proof.

For every graded Lie algebra, the modular function is the supertrace of the adjoint action on elements of degree

Hence, for all

\str\left(\ad_{\left[x\right]}^{(2)}\right)=\str\left(\ad_{x}^{(2)}\right).

4.
The modular class of a Lie
{\infty}
-algebroid

4.1.
Some definitions and computations

The Berezinian bundle of a representation of a Lie
\infty
-algebroid
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
has two characteristics:
(i)
it is concentrated in some degree, and
(ii)
it has rank
1
in this degree. This implies that only its first Taylor coefficient may be non-zero, and only when applied to a section of
E_{-1}
. Given now a
rank-
1
representation

B\to M
, i.e. a representation that admits these two characteristics, denote by
(e,b)\mapsto\nabla^{B}_{e}b
its Taylor coefficient.

From now on, we work in smooth differential geometry, i.e.
\mathcal{O}=C^{\infty}(M)
. Assume there exists a nowhere vanishing section
b
of
B
, then the section
\omega_{b}\in\Gamma(E_{-1}^{*})=\mathcal{E}_{+1}
defined by:

\nabla^{B}_{e}b=\omega_{b}(e)\,b

Q
-closed function on
E
i.e.:

\omega_{b}(l_{2}(e_{1},e_{2}))-\rho(e_{1})[\omega_{b}(e_{2})]+\rho(e_{2})[\omega_{b}(e_{1})]=0\hbox{ and }\omega_{b}(l_{1}(\tilde{e}))=0,

for any
e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E_{-1})
,
\tilde{e}\in\Gamma(E_{-2})
.
Also, it is easily checked that for every nowhere vanishing smooth function
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}
:

\omega_{\lambda b}(e)=\omega_{b}(e)+\frac{\rho(e)[\lambda]}{\lambda}=\omega_{b}(e)+\rho(e)[\mathrm{ln}(|\lambda|)]

or, equivalently,
\omega_{\lambda b}=\omega_{b}+Q_{E}[\mathrm{ln}(|\lambda|)]
. As a consequence, the class of
\omega_{b}\in H^{1}(E,Q)
does not depend on the choice of
b
. If there is no non-vanishing section because the rank
1
bundle
B
is not trivial, then we can consider the representation
B\otimes B
, which is now trivial as a rank
1
bundle, and consider one-half of the class defined above. We call this class the
characteristic class of the rank-
1
representation
B
.

Definition 4.1
.

We call
characteristic class of a representation
of a Lie
\infty
-algebroid the characteristic class of its Berezinian bundle (defined in Theorem
3.30
).

Let
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
be a Lie
\infty
-algebroid and
\nabla=(\nabla^{i})_{i\geq 1}
a connection on
E
. The adjoint representation
\ad^{\nabla}
(see Section
3.3
) has a characteristic class.
Since different connections define isomorphic representations (see Remark
3.26
), we have:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
be a Lie
\infty
-algebroid.
The characteristic class of the adjoint representation with respect to a connection
\nabla
is independent of the chosen connection.

By Proposition
4.2
, the following definition makes sense.

Definition 4.3
.

The
modular class of a Lie
\infty
-algebroid

(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
is the characteristic class of any of its adjoint representations.
When the modular class is zero, we say that the Lie
{\infty}
-algebroid is
unimodular
.

Let us give a concrete description of the modular class.
Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
and
e_{1}^{(i)},\dots,e_{a_{i}}^{(i)}
be local trivializations of
TM

and each one of the vector bundles
E_{-i}
, over some open subset of
M
.

For every
Y\in{\mathfrak{X}}(M)
, we define
{\rm div}(Y)
to be the unique function that satisfies:

[Y,X_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge X_{n}]={\rm div}(Y)X_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge X_{n}

and we define
{\rm{div}^{(i)}(e)}
to be, for all
i\geq 1
and all
e\in\Gamma(E_{-1})
,
the unique function that satisfies:

\{e,e_{1}^{(i)}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{a_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{a_{i}}e_{1}^{(i)}\wedge\dots\wedge\{e,e_{k}^{(i)}\}_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{a_{i}}^{(i)}={\rm{div}^{(i)}(e)}\,\,e_{1}^{(i)}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{a_{i}}^{(i)}

then

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
be a Lie
\infty
-algebroid.
The
modular cocycle
with respect to the local trivializations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
and
e_{1}^{(i)},\dots,e_{a_{i}}^{(i)}
as above is the section
\omega_{\nabla}
of
E_{-1}^{*}
given for all
e\in\Gamma(E_{-1})
by:

\omega_{\nabla}(e)={\rm div}(\rho(e))+\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i}{\rm{div}^{(i)}(e)}.

In the expression of the adjoint action of a Lie
\infty
-algebroid, as described in Proposition
3.27
, the Taylor coefficients (even the one that will appear when computing the action on the Berezinian bundle) have terms involving the
TM
-connection on
E
that do not appear in the expression of
\omega_{\nabla}
. The next statement is therefore not obvious, and deserves a careful proof.

Proposition 4.5
.

Any modular cocycle is a representative of the modular class.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be local coordinate in an coordinate neighborhood
U\subset M
, and
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
be local trivialization of
E
over
U
(by homogeneous sections). We denote the dual variables by
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{r}
and by
\mu
the section of the Berezinian bundle of
E\oplus TM
constructed out the local trivializations
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
and
e_{1}\ldots,e_{r}
.

From Theorem
3.30
we know that
\nabla^{Ber}_{e}\mu=\str\omega(e)\,\mu
,
e\in\Gamma(E_{-1})
where

\displaystyle\str\omega(e)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle dx_{k},\ad^{(1)}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}}(e)\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\langle\xi^{k},\ad^{(1)}_{e_{k}}(e)\right\rangle.

Taking into account the Taylor coefficients of the adjoint representation given in Proposition
3.27
we have

(27)

\displaystyle\str\omega(e)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle dx_{i},\left[\rho(e),\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right]\right\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle dx_{i},\rho(\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}(e))\right\rangle

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|e_{k}|}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\langle\xi^{k},\left\{e_{k},e\right\}_{2}\right\rangle+(-1)^{|e_{k}|}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\langle\xi^{k},\nabla_{\rho(e_{k})}(e)\right\rangle.

Now, consider the functions
\rho_{k}^{i}=\left\langle\rho(e_{k}),dx_{i}\right\rangle=\left\langle\xi^{k},\rho^{*}(dx_{i})\right\rangle
and notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\langle\xi^{k},\nabla_{\rho(e_{k})}(e)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\xi^{k},\nabla_{\rho_{k}^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}(e)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\rho_{k}^{i}\,\xi^{k},\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}(e)\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle(-1)^{|\xi_{k}|}\rho^{*}(dx_{i}),\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}(e)\right\rangle

As a consequence, the second and fourth terms in (
27
) add up to zero. Therefore

(28)

\displaystyle\str\omega(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle dx_{i},\left[\rho(e),\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right]\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{r}(-1)^{|\xi_{k}|}\left\langle\xi^{k},\left\{e_{k},e\right\}_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm div}(\rho(e))+\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i}{\rm{div}^{(i)}(e)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\nabla}(e).

This completes the proof

∎

4.2.
The modular class, leaf by leaf description, and homotopy invariance

For any negatively graded Lie
\infty
-algebroid
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
, the image
\rho(\Gamma(E_{-1}))
of the anchor map is a singular foliation in the sense of
[
2
,
3
]
. In particular, the base manifold
M
is a disjoint union of leaves. More precisely, any equivalence class
L
of the equivalence relation on
M
defined by:
“
m_{1}\sim m_{2}
if and only there exists a finite family of vector fields in
\rho(\Gamma(E_{-1}))
whose successive time-
1
flows maps
m_{1}
to
m_{2}
”
is a submanifold such that
T_{m}L=\rho_{m}(E_{-1}|_{m})
, at every
m\in L
. We call these submanifolds
leaves of
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
.

Let
L
be such a leaf.

(1)

The restriction of the linear part
(E\oplus TM,l_{1},-\rho)
to
L
is a complex of vector bundles

(31)

\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathfrak{i}}_{L}E_{-2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathfrak{i}}_{L}E_{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathfrak{i}}_{L}TM

whose differential has constant rank.

This can be proven as follows. The kernel and image of
\rho
have constant rank along
L
since
\rho({\mathfrak{i}}_{L}E_{-1})=TL
. Let
\sigma\colon TL\to{\mathfrak{i}}_{L}E_{-1}
be a section of the anchor map
\rho\colon{\mathfrak{i}}_{L}E_{-1}\to TL
. Consider the connections on the vector bundles
({\mathfrak{i}}_{L}E_{-i})_{i\geq 2}

and the vector bundle
{\mathrm{Ker}}(\rho)\subset{\mathfrak{i}}_{L}E_{-1}
,
defined by:

\nabla^{\sigma}_{u}e:=l_{2}(\sigma(u),e)\hbox{ for all $u\in\mathfrak{X}(L),e\in\Gamma(E_{-k})$ or $e\in\Gamma({\mathrm{Ker}}(\rho))$}.

The graded Jacobi identity implies that for any
e\in\Gamma(E_{-1}),f\in\Gamma(E_{-k})
(for
k\geq 2
) or
f\in\Gamma({\mathrm{Ker}}(\rho))
:

l_{1}(l_{2}(e,f))=l_{2}(e,l_{1}(f)).

When applied to
e=\sigma(u)
, this reads
[\nabla^{\sigma}_{u},l_{1}]=0
. The differential
l_{1}
is therefore preserved under parallel transportation along
L
, and, in particular, is of constant rank along the leaf
L
.

(2)

The first item implies that the cohomology of the complex (
31
) is a graded bundle over
L
.

We denote by
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
this cohomology, and call it the
graded cohomology of
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
over the leaf
L
.

Notice that
H_{0}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
is the normal bundle
{\mathfrak{i}}_{L}TM/TL
of
L
in
M
.

(3)

Also,
A_{L}(E,Q):=\mathfrak{i}_{L}E_{-1}/l_{1}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E_{-2})
is a (transitive) Lie algebroid over
L
, when equipped with the anchor and Lie bracket:

\rho\colon\overline{e}\mapsto\rho(e)\hbox{ and }\left\{\overline{e_{1}},\overline{e_{2}}\right\}=\overline{l_{2}(e_{1},e_{2})}\hbox{ for all $e_{1},e_{2},e\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E_{-1})$}.

The horizontal bar stands for the natural map
\Gamma(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E_{-1})\mapsto\Gamma(A_{L}(E,Q))
.

We call
A_{L}(E,Q)
the
holonomy Lie algebroid of the leaf
L
for
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
. Its isotropy Lie algebra bundle is

H_{-1}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
by construction.

(4)

Each one of the spaces
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
comes equipped with a canonical
A_{L}(E,Q)
-connection defined by:

\nabla_{\overline{e}}\overline{f}=\overline{l_{2}(e,f)}

where
e\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E_{-1})
and
f\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E_{-k})
, for
k\geq 2
, or
f\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{i}_{L}{\mathrm{Ker}}(\rho))
and the horizontal bars are as before.

As an exception to the previous rule, the
A_{L}(E,Q)
-connection on

H_{0}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)=\mathfrak{i}_{L}TM/TL
is defined by:

\nabla_{\overline{e}}\overline{X}=\overline{[\rho(\tilde{e}),X]}

for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, and every section
\tilde{e}
of
E_{-1}
whose restriction to
L
is
e
. Also,
X\mapsto\overline{X}
stands for the natural map
\mathfrak{X}(M)\mapsto\Gamma(\mathfrak{i}_{L}TM/TL)
.

(5)

The higher Jacobi identities imply that the above
A_{L}(E,Q)
-connections are flat.

The flatness of the
A_{L}(E,Q)
-connection on
H_{-i}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
for
i\geq 1
follows from the graded Jacobi identity, for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E_{-1})
,
f\in\Gamma(E_{-k})
with
k\geq 2
or
f\in{\mathrm{Ker}}(\rho)
:

l_{2}(l_{2}(e_{1},e_{2}),f)=l_{2}(e_{1},l_{2}(e_{2},f))-l_{2}(e_{2},l_{2}(e_{1},f))-l_{3}(e_{1},e_{2},l_{1}(f))-l_{1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}l_{3}(e_{1},e_{2},f)

since
f\mapsto l_{3}(e_{1},e_{2},l_{1}(f))+l_{1}\mbox{\,\tiny{$\circ$}\,}l_{3}(e_{1},e_{2},f)
induce the zero map in cohomology.
The flatness of the
A_{L}(E,Q)
-connection on the normal bundle is well-known and is easy to check: it uses the fact that the anchor map
\rho
is a morphism (see, for instance,
[
3
]
).

Let us conclude this discussion with a Lemma:

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
L
be a leaf of a Lie
\infty
-algebroid
(E,Q\equiv(\left\{l_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1},\rho))
.
The graded cohomology
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
over the leaf
L
is a module over the holonomy Lie algebroid
A_{L}(E,Q)
of
L
.

Let us consider now the Berezinian bundle of the graded vector bundle
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
:

{\mathrm{Ber}}(H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)):=\cdots\wedge^{\text{\rm top}}H_{-3}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)\otimes\wedge^{\text{\rm top}}(H_{-2}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E))^{*}\otimes\wedge^{\text{\rm top}}H_{-1}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)\otimes\wedge^{\text{\rm top}}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}TM/TL)^{*}.

By Lemma
3.29
, there is a canonical isomorphism between the Berezinian of a complex and the Berezinian of its cohomology.
In particular, there is a canonical isomorphism:

\mathfrak{i}_{L}:{\mathrm{Ber}}(E\oplus TM,l_{1},-\rho)\simeq{\mathrm{Ber}}(H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)).

Recall that
(E\oplus TM,l_{1},-\rho)
stands for its linear part. Let us compute the
modular class of the leaf
L
, i.e. the characteristic class of this Lie algebroid representation. This Proposition shall be helpful.

Proposition 4.10
.

For every section
e\in\Gamma(E_{-1})
and every section
\mu
of the Berezinian bundle
{\mathrm{Ber}}(E\oplus TM,l_{1},-\rho)

\nabla_{\overline{e}}^{Ber}\mathfrak{i}_{L}\mu=\mathfrak{i}_{L}\ad_{e}^{Ber}\mu.

In the previous equation, on the left hand side,
\nabla^{Ber}_{\overline{e}}
is computed with the help of the
A_{L}(E,Q)
-connections on
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}E)
.
